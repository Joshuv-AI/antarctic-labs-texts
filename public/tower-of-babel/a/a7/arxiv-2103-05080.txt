No dimension reduction for doubling subsets of ℓ q \ell_{q} when q > 2 q>2 revisited Thanks: F. Baudier was partially supported by the National Science Foundation under Grant Number DMS-1800322.
F. Baudier, K. Swieçicki, A. Swift

Abstract
. We revisit the main results from [ 4 , 5 ] and [ 20 ] about the impossibility of dimension reduction for doubling subsets of ℓ q \ell_{q} for q > 2 q>2 . We provide an alternative elementary proof of this impossibility result that combines the simplicity of the construction in [ 4 , 5 ] with the generality of the approach in [ 20 ] (except for L 1 L_{1} targets). One advantage of this different approach is that it can be naturally generalized to obtain embeddability obstructions into non-positively curved spaces or asymptotically uniformly convex Banach spaces.

is

-uniformly convex (or is uniformly convex of power type
q
) if
\delta_{\mathfrak{X}}(t)\geqslant ct^{q}
for some universal constant
c>0
. A classical result of Pisier
[
29
]
states that a uniformly convex Banach space admits a renorming that is
q
-uniformly convex for some
q\geqslant 2
. The following key lemma is similar to a contraction result for Laakso graphs from
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,p)
is a
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
k
-Laakso substructure in
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
and that
f\colon\mathsf{X}\to(\mathfrak{Y},\|\cdot\|)
is a bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion
D
. Then for every
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k
, if
\{s,a,m_{1},m_{2},b,t\}\subset\mathsf{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,p)
is indexed by a copy of one of the Laakso graphs
\mathsf{L}_{1}
created at step
\ell
, we have:

(3)

\|f(s)-f(t)\|\leqslant D\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}\left(1-\delta_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{2\varepsilon}{D(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}}\right)\right).

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}

(4)

\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\leqslant\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leqslant D\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y).

Let
\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)}{2}(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}
, and

x_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(m_{1})-f(s),\quad x_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(m_{2})-f(s),\quad y_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(t)-f(m_{1}),\quad y_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(t)-f(m_{2}).

For all
i\in\{1,2\}
, it follows from the upper bound in (
4
) and
(c_{4})
that
\frac{\|x_{i}\|}{D\alpha}\leqslant 1
and
\frac{\|y_{i}\|}{D\alpha}\leqslant 1
. On the other hand, it follows from the lower bound in (
4
) and
(c_{5})
that

\frac{\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|}{D\alpha}\geqslant\frac{2\varepsilon}{D(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\frac{\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|}{D\alpha}\geqslant\frac{2\varepsilon}{D(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}}.

Therefore

\Big\|\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2D\alpha}\Big\|\leqslant 1-\delta_{\mathfrak{Y}}\Big(\frac{2\varepsilon}{D(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}}\Big)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\Big\|\frac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2D\alpha}\Big\|\leqslant 1-\delta_{\mathfrak{Y}}\Big(\frac{2\varepsilon}{D(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}}\Big).

Since

\displaystyle f(t)-f(s)

\displaystyle=(f(t)-f(m_{1})+f(m_{1})-f(s)+f(t)-f(m_{2})+f(m_{2})-f(s))/2

\displaystyle=(y_{1}+y_{2}+x_{1}+x_{2})/2,

it follows from the triangle inequality that

\|\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{D\alpha}\|\leqslant 2\Big(1-\delta_{\mathfrak{Y}}\Big(\frac{2\varepsilon}{D(1+\varepsilon^{p})^{1/p}}\Big)\Big)
and the conclusion follows.
∎

By using the tension between the thinness parameter of a thin Laakso substructure and the power type of the modulus of uniform convexity of the host space we can prove the following distortion lower bound.

Theorem 4
.

Let
2\leqslant p<q
and assume that
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion
D
into a
p
-uniformly convex Banach space
\mathfrak{Y}
. There exists
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(p,q,D,\mathfrak{Y})>0
such that if
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
admits a
(\varepsilon,q)
-thin
k
-Laakso substructure then
D=\Omega(k^{1/p-1/q})

Proof.

Assume that for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}

(5)

\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\leqslant\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leqslant D\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y),

and let
\mathcal{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,q)
be a
(\varepsilon,q)
-thin
k
-Laakso substructure with
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\leqslant 2
. The self-improvement argument uses the self-similar structure of the Laakso graphs. For
1\leqslant j\leqslant k
consider the decomposition
\mathsf{L}_{k-j}\oslash\mathsf{L}_{j}
of
\mathsf{L}_{k}
, i.e.
\mathsf{L}_{k}
is formed by replacing each of the
6^{k-j}
edges of
\mathsf{L}_{k-j}
by a copy of
\mathsf{L}_{j}
. We define
D_{j}
to be the smallest constant such that

(6)

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leqslant D_{j}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y),

for all
4\times 6^{k-j}
pairs of points
\{x,y\}
in
\mathcal{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,p)
that are indexed by vertices of a copy of
\mathsf{L}_{j}
in
\mathsf{L}_{k}
of the form
\{s,m_{i}\}
or
\{m_{i},t\}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
, where
s
and
t
are the farther apart vertices in
\mathsf{L}_{j}
whose two distinct midpoints are
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
.

It is clear that for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the inequalities
1\leqslant D_{j}\leqslant D
hold. Assume that
\delta_{\mathfrak{Y}}(t)\geqslant ct^{p}
for some constant
c>0
(that depends on
\mathfrak{Y}
only). Fix
\mathsf{L}^{0}_{j}
as one of the
6^{k-j}
copies of
\mathsf{L}_{j}
in the decomposition
\mathsf{L}_{k-j}\oslash\mathsf{L}_{j}
of
\mathsf{L}_{k}
. Observe that
\mathsf{L}^{0}_{j}=\mathsf{L}_{1}\oslash\mathsf{L}_{j-1}
and let
\{s,a,m_{1},m_{2},b,t\}
denote the vertices of
\mathsf{L}_{1}
in this decomposition of
\mathsf{L}_{j}^{0}
. Consider the pair
\{s,m_{1}\}
as defined above (the 3 other pairs can be treated similarly) and the two copies of
\mathsf{L}_{j-1}
which contain either
s
or
m_{1}
and have the vertex
a
in common. In the proof of Lemma
2
we only used the upper bound in
(
4
)
for pairs of points of the form described in the definition of
D_{j-1}
, and because we assumed that
\delta_{\mathfrak{Y}}(t)\geqslant ct^{p}
and
(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\leqslant 2
we have

(7)

\|f(s)-f(a)\|\leqslant D_{j-1}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,a)(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\Big(1-\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D_{j-1}^{p}}\Big),

and

(8)

\|f(a)-f(m_{1})\|\leqslant D_{j-1}\mathsf{d_{X}}(a,m_{1})(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\Big(1-\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D_{j-1}^{p}}\Big).

Then, it follows from the triangle inequality that

(9)

\|f(s)-f(m_{1})\|\leqslant D_{j-1}(\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,a)+\mathsf{d_{X}}(a,m_{1}))(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\Big(1-\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D_{j-1}^{p}}\Big).

By
(c_{1})
and
(c_{3})
in the construction of the thin Laakso substructures we have

\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,a)+\mathsf{d_{X}}(a,m_{1})=\frac{1}{4}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)+\frac{1}{4}(1+(2\varepsilon)^{q})^{1/q}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t).

Since
q\geqslant 1
we have
(1+(2\varepsilon)^{q})^{1/q}\leqslant 1+(2\varepsilon)^{q}
, and thus

\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,a)+\mathsf{d_{X}}(a,m_{1})\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(2+(2\varepsilon)^{q})=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c_{4})}}{{=}}\frac{1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q}}{(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,m_{1}).

Substituting this last inequality in (
9
) we obtain

\|f(s)-f(m_{1})\|\leqslant D_{j-1}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,m_{1})(1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q})\Big(1-\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D_{j-1}^{p}}\Big).

By symmetry of the
(\varepsilon,q)
-thin Laakso substructures, the other pairs of points in the definition of
D_{j}
can be treated similarly and hence we have proved that

D_{j}\leqslant D_{j-1}(1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q})\Big(1-\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D_{j-1}^{p}}\Big).

Then,

\displaystyle D_{j}

\displaystyle\leqslant D_{j-1}(1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q})-\frac{(1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q})c\varepsilon^{p}}{D_{j-1}^{p-1}}

(10)

\displaystyle\leqslant D_{j-1}+D(2\varepsilon)^{q}-\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D^{p-1}},

where in (
10
) we used the fact that
D_{j-1}\leqslant D
and
1+2^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q}\geqslant 1
.
Rearranging we have

(11)

D_{j-1}-D_{j}\geqslant D\left(\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{D^{p}}-(2\varepsilon)^{q}\right),

If we let
\varepsilon=\gamma D^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}
for some small enough
\gamma
to be chosen later (and that depends only on
p,q
, and
c
), then

(12)

\displaystyle D_{j-1}-D_{j}

\displaystyle\geqslant D\left(c\gamma^{p}\frac{D^{-\frac{p^{2}}{q-p}}}{D^{p}}-(2\gamma)^{q}D^{-\frac{pq}{q-p}}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle\geqslant D\cdot D^{-\frac{pq}{q-p}}(c\gamma^{p}-2^{q}\gamma^{q}).

If we choose
\gamma\in\Big(0,\Big(\frac{c}{2^{q+1}}\Big)^{1/(q-p)}\Big)
, and since
p<q
, we have
c\gamma^{p}-2^{q}\gamma^{q}\geqslant\frac{c}{2}\gamma^{p}>0
. Hence
D_{j-1}-D_{j}\geqslant\frac{c\gamma^{p}}{2}D^{1-\frac{pq}{q-p}}
and summing over
j=2,\dots,k
we get

(14)

D\geqslant D_{1}-D_{k}\geqslant\sum_{j=2}^{k}\frac{c\gamma^{p}}{2}D^{1-\frac{pq}{q-p}}\geqslant\frac{c\gamma^{p}}{2}(k-1)D^{1-\frac{pq}{q-p}},

and hence

D\gtrsim k^{1/p-1/q}
.
∎

Corollary
2
below improves Theorem
3
in several ways. The dependence in the dimension for the thinness parameter is removed. Assertion
1
extends to all
p
-uniformly convex Banach spaces the bound in assertion
2
of Theorem
3
while improving the bound. Indeed if
\mathfrak{Y}=\ell_{p}^{d}
then
D=\Omega\Big(\big(\frac{\log n}{d}\big)^{\min\big\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p}\big\}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\mathfrak{Y}
be a Banach space, and fix
D\geqslant 1
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an
n
-point subset
\mathcal{L}_{n}(q,D,\mathfrak{Y})
of
\ell_{q}
that is
2^{32}
-doubling and such that any bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion
D
into
\mathfrak{Y}
must incur

(1)

D=\Omega\Big((\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)
if
p\in[2,q)
and
\mathfrak{Y}
is a
p
-uniformly convex Banach space

and

(2)

D=\Omega\Big(\Big(\frac{\log n}{d}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)
if
\mathfrak{Y}=\ell_{q}^{d}

Proof.

Assertion
(1)
follows immediately from Theorem
4
and Lemma
1
. The second assertion follows from the fact that
n=\Theta(6^{k})
and that the Banach-Mazur distance between the
2
-uniformly convex spaces
\ell_{2}^{d}
and
\ell_{q}^{d}
is at most
d^{1/2-1/q}
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Very recently, Naor and Young
[
27
]
gave the first partial counter-example to the metric Kadec-Pełczyński problem, which asks whether for
1\leqslant p<r<q<\infty
, a metric space that admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into
L_{p}
and into
L_{q}
necessarily admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into
L_{r}
. Naor and Young produced a Heisenberg-type space that does embed into
\ell_{1}
and into
\ell_{q}
but does not embed into
\ell_{r}
for any
1<r<4\leqslant q
. The fact that what happens for
\ell_{r}
in the range
4\leqslant r<q
is not understood seems inherent of the Heisenberg approach. If we could show that the thin Laakso substructures do embed into
\ell_{1}
then we would have a second counter-example to the metric Kadec-Pełczyński problem which resolves this issue.

It remains to show how Theorem
1
can be derived from Corollary
2
. First observe that for all
q>2
,
D\geqslant 1
, and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the
n
-point doubling subsets
\mathcal{L}_{n}(q,D,\ell_{2})
of
\ell_{q}
belong to the unit ball of
\ell_{q}
. Now consider the subset
Z_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\bigcup_{(k,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(q,k,\ell_{2})\times\{(4^{k},4^{n})\}\subset\ell_{q}\oplus_{q}\mathbb{R}^{2}\equiv\ell_{q}
. Clearly,
Z_{q}
contains an isometric copy of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(q,k,\ell_{2})
and it can be verified that
Z_{q}
is doubling. If
Z_{q}\subset\ell_{q}
admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion
D
into
\ell_{q}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
, then
the proof of assertion (
2
) in Corollary
2
shows that
D=\Omega\Big(\Big(\frac{\log n}{d}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)

since
Z_{q}
contains an isometric copy of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(q,k,\ell_{2})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
k\leqslant D<k+1
, and hence
D
cannot be finite.

2.3.
Quantitative embeddability of
\ell_{q}^{k}
into uniformly convex Banach spaces

The alternative proof of Theorem
1
that we proposed has several noteworthy applications. One application concerns the non-embeddability of
\ell_{q}
into
L_{p}
and more generally lower bounding the quantitative parameter
\mathsf{c}_{\mathfrak{Y}}(\ell_{q}^{k})
whenever
\mathfrak{Y}
is a
p
-uniformly convex Banach space. It is well known that when
2\leqslant p<q
, if
\mathfrak{Y}
has cotype
p
, and in particular if
\mathfrak{Y}
is
p
-uniformly convex, then
\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\mathsf{c}_{\mathfrak{Y}}(\ell_{q}^{k})=\infty
. Quantitatively,

(15)

\mathsf{c}_{\mathfrak{Y}}(\ell_{q}^{k})=\Omega\Big(k^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)\text{ for all }2\leqslant p<q.

and

(16)

\mathsf{c}_{\mathfrak{Y}}(\ell_{q}^{k})=\Omega\Big(k^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)\text{ for all }1\leqslant p\leqslant 2<q.

The fact that these lower bounds are tight follows from simple estimates of the norm of the formal identity (and its inverse) (e.g.
\|I_{\ell_{q}^{k}\to\ell_{p}^{k}}\|\cdot\|I^{-1}_{\ell_{q}^{k}\to\ell_{p}^{k}}\|\leqslant k^{1/p-1/q}
is a consequence of Hölder’s inequality and the monotonicity of the
\ell_{r}
-norms). Since the thin
k
-Laakso substructure lives in
\ell_{q}^{k+1}
and has
\Theta(6^{k})
points these lower bounds follow directly from the first assertion of Corollary
2
. As we point out below the approaches in
[
20
]
and
[
5
]
seem to only give suboptimal results.

The
n
-point doubling subset
\mathcal{H}_{n}(q)
of Lafforgue and Naor lies in some
L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
-space and hence in
\ell_{q}^{n(n-1)/2}
by a result of
[
3
]
. Therefore, if one uses the Heisenberg-type
\sqrt{n}
-point doubling subset of
\ell_{q}^{n}
one can derive that, for example,
\mathsf{c}_{\ell_{2}}(\ell_{q}^{n})=\Omega\Big(\log(n)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)
which is suboptimal. To get the optimal lower bound one would need to be able to show that the doubling subset can actually be embedded into
\ell_{q}^{\Theta(\log n)}
, which is the best we can hope for due to assertion
1
in Theorem
2
. This does not seem to be known and we do not know if this is true.

Following Bartal-Gottlieb-Neiman’s approach, one could obtain partial and suboptimal results as follows. Assume that
\ell_{q}^{k}
admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding into
\ell_{p}^{k}
. Then one can construct a subset in
\ell_{q}^{k}
, namely
\mathcal{L}_{n}(q,D,k-1)
, having
n=\Theta(6^{k})
points and witnessing the fact that
D
must be large. The estimates in Theorem
3
yield
\mathsf{c}_{\ell_{p}^{k}}(\ell_{q}^{k})=\Omega\Big(k^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)
for all
2\leqslant p<q
. Therefore, the right order of magnitude is captured in the range
2\leqslant p<q
but in the (very) restricted case of a finite-dimensional
\ell_{p}
target that has the same dimension as the source space. In the range
1<p<2<q
one gets
\mathsf{c}_{\ell_{p}^{k}}(\ell_{q}^{k})=\Omega\Big(k^{1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big)
which does not capture the right order of magnitude and is clearly suboptimal.

2.4.
Quantitative embeddability of
\ell_{q}^{k}
into non-positively curved spaces

Another advantage of the proof via self-improvement is that it can be extended, with a little bit more care, to cover maps taking values into non-positively curved spaces, and more generally to the context of rounded ball metric spaces.

Recall that the
\eta
-approximate midpoint set of
x,y\in(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
is defined as

\displaystyle\text{Mid}(x,y,\eta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{z\in\mathsf{X}\colon\max\big\{\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,z),\mathsf{d_{X}}(y,z)\big\}\leqslant\frac{1+\eta}{2}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\right\}

\displaystyle=B_{\mathsf{X}}\left(x,\frac{1+\eta}{2}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\right)\cap B_{\mathsf{X}}\left(y,\frac{1+\eta}{2}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\right)

As usual for an arbitrary set
A\subset\mathsf{X}
,
\mathrm{diam}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup\big\{\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\colon x,y\in A\big\}.
The following definition is due to T. J. Laakso
[
19
]
.

A metric space
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
is a
rounded ball space
if for all
t>0
there exists
\eta(t)>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}

(17)

\mathrm{diam}\big(\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta(t))\big)<t\cdot\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y).

Remark 3
.

Note that for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}
and
\eta>0
,
\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta))\leqslant(1+\eta)\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)
always holds. Therefore the rounded ball property is non-trivial only for
t\in(0,1]
and in this case
\eta\in(0,1)
necessarily.

Note that a Banach space is a rounded ball space if and only if it is uniformly convex
[
19
, Lemma 5.2]
. We can define a
rounded ball modulus

\eta_{\mathsf{X}}
as follows

(18)

\eta_{\mathsf{X}}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup\{\eta(t)\colon\eqref{eq:rbinequality}\text{ holds for all }x,y\in\mathsf{X}\}.

We will say that
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
is a rounded space with power type
p
if
\eta_{\mathsf{X}}(t)\geqslant ct^{p}
.

The following contraction lemma is an extension, to the purely metric context of rounded ball spaces, of the contraction phenomenon in Lemma
2
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d_{Y}})
be a metric space and
\varepsilon>0
such that
(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\leqslant 2
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,q)
is a
(\varepsilon,q)
-thin
k
-Laakso substructure in
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
and that
f\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
satisfies

(19)

\frac{1}{A}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\leqslant\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(x),f(y))\leqslant B\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y),

for some constants
A,B>0
. Then for every
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k
, if
\{s,a,m_{1},m_{2},b,t\}\subset\mathsf{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,q)
is indexed by a copy of one of the Laakso graphs
\mathsf{L}_{1}
created at step
\ell
we have:

(20)

\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(s),f(t))\leqslant B\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mathsf{Y}}(\varepsilon/2AB)\Big).

Proof.

Let
r>0
be the smallest radius such that
B_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(s),r)\cap B_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(t),r)\supseteq\{f(m_{1}),f(m_{2})\}
.
Then

r\leqslant\max\{d_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(s),f(m_{1})),d_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(s),f(m_{2})),d_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(t),f(m_{1})),d_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(t),f(m_{2}))\}

and it follows from (
19
) and
(c_{4})
that
r\leqslant\frac{B}{2}(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)
. On the other hand,

(21)

\mathrm{diam}\left(B_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(s),r)\cap B_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(t),r)\right)\geqslant d_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(m_{1}),f(m_{2})),

and thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}\left(B_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(s),r)\cap B_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(t),r)\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{A}\mathsf{d_{X}}(m_{1},m_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c_{5})}}{{=}}\frac{1}{A}\varepsilon\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c_{4})}}{{=}}\frac{1}{A}\varepsilon\Big(\frac{\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,m_{1})}{(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}}+\frac{\mathsf{d_{X}}(t,m_{1})}{(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{AB(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}}\Big(\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(s),f(m_{1}))+\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(t),f(m_{1}))\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2AB}\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(s),f(t)),

where in the last inequality we used our assumption on
\varepsilon
and the triangle inequality.
Therefore,
r\geqslant\frac{1+\eta_{\mathsf{Y}}(\varepsilon/(2AB))}{2}\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(s),f(t))
by definition of the rounded ball modulus, and

\displaystyle d_{\mathsf{Y}}(f(s),f(t))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2r}{1+\eta_{\mathsf{Y}}(\varepsilon/2AB)}\leqslant\frac{B(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)}{1+\eta_{\mathsf{Y}}(\varepsilon/2AB)}

\displaystyle\leqslant B\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+\varepsilon^{q})^{1/q}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mathsf{Y}}(\varepsilon/2AB)\Big).

where in the last inequality we used Remark
3
.
∎

A slightly different implementation of the self-improvement argument gives the following extension of Theorem
4
to metric spaces with rounded ball modulus with power type. We only emphasize the few points in the proof that are different.

Theorem 5
.

Let
1<p<q
and let
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d_{Y}})
be a rounded ball metric space with power type
p
. For all
D\geqslant 1
there exists
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(D,p,q,\mathsf{Y})>0
such that if
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
admits a
(\varepsilon,q)
-thin
k
-Laakso substructure and embeds bi-Lipschitzly with distortion at most
D\geqslant 1
into
\mathsf{Y}
then
D=\Omega(k^{1/p-1/q})
.

Proof.

Assume that for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}

(22)

\frac{1}{A}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\leqslant\mathsf{d_{Y}}(f(x),f(y))\leqslant B\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y),

with
AB\leqslant D
.

This time we define
B_{j}
to be the smallest constant such that

(23)

\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leqslant B_{j}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y),

for all
4\times 6^{k-j}
pairs of points
\{x,y\}
in
\mathcal{L}_{k}(\varepsilon,q)
that are indexed by vertices of a copy of
\mathsf{L}_{j}
in
\mathsf{L}_{k}
of the form
\{s,m_{i}\}
or
\{m_{i},t\}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
, where
s
and
t
are the farther apart vertices in
\mathsf{L}_{j}
whose two distinct midpoints are
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
.

It is clear that for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the inequalities
1\leqslant B_{j}\leqslant B
hold. Since in the proof of Lemma
3
we have only used the upper bound in
(
22
)
for pairs of points of the form described in the definition of
B_{j-1}
, proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
4
we show that

AB_{j-1}-AB_{j}\geqslant AB\left(\frac{c\varepsilon^{p}}{2^{p+1}(AB)^{p}}-(2\varepsilon)^{q}\right).

If we let
\varepsilon=\gamma(AB)^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}
for some small enough
\gamma
to be chosen later (and that depends only on
p,q
, and
c
), then

AB_{j-1}-AB_{j}\geqslant(AB)^{1-\frac{pq}{q-p}}(\frac{c}{2^{p+1}}\gamma^{p}-2^{q}\gamma^{q}).

If we choose
0<\gamma<\Big(\frac{c}{2^{p+q+2}}\Big)^{1/(q-p)}
we have
\frac{c}{2^{p+1}}\gamma^{p}-2^{q}\gamma^{q}\geqslant\frac{c}{2^{p+2}}\gamma^{p}>0
. Hence
AB_{j-1}-AB_{j}\geqslant\frac{c}{2^{p+2}}\gamma^{p}(AB)^{1-\frac{pq}{q-p}}
and summing over
j=2,\dots,k
we conclude that

AB\gtrsim k^{1/p-1/q}
.
∎

We now identify a
4
-point inequality that implies the rounded ball property with power type
p
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
be a metric space and
p\in(0,\infty)
. If there exists
C\in(0,2^{p}]
such that for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathsf{X}
we have

(24)

\mathsf{d_{X}}(x_{1},x_{3})^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(x_{2},x_{4})^{p}\leqslant\frac{C}{4}\Big(\mathsf{d_{X}}(x_{1},x_{2})^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(x_{2},x_{3})^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(x_{3},x_{4})^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(x_{4},x_{1})^{p}\Big),

then
\mathsf{X}
is a rounded ball space with
\eta_{\mathsf{X}}(t)\geqslant t^{p}/(2^{p}-1)
if
p\geqslant 1
and with
\eta_{\mathsf{X}}(t)\geqslant t
if
p\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Fix
t>0
and let
x,y\in\mathsf{X}
and
\eta\in(0,1)
. If
\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta)
is empty or reduced to a single point there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, let
w\neq z\in\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta)
. It follows from (
24
) that

\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(w,z)^{p}\leqslant\frac{C}{4}(\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,w)^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(w,y)^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(y,z)^{p}+\mathsf{d_{X}}(z,x)^{p}),

and by the definition of
\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta)
, we have

\mathsf{d_{X}}(w,z)^{p}\leqslant\Big(C\frac{(1+\eta)^{p}}{2^{p}}-1\Big)\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)^{p}.

And since
C\leqslant 2^{p}
,

\mathsf{d_{X}}(w,z)\leqslant((1+\eta)^{p}-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y).

If
p\geqslant 1
then
((1+\eta)^{p}-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant(2^{p}-1)^{1/p}\eta^{1/p}
, and if
\eta=t^{p}/(2^{p}-1)
, then

\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta))<t\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y).

If
p\in(0,1)
then
((1+\eta)^{p}-1)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\eta
, and
\eta=t
implies that

\mathrm{diam}(\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\eta))<t\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y).\qed

Inequality (
24
) when
p=2
and
C=4
is well known under various names: quadrilateral inequality, roundness
2
, Enflo type
2
with constant
1
. It was proved by Berg and Nikolaev
[
9
]
(see also
[
10
]
or
[
30
]
) that the quadrilateral inequality characterizes CAT(0)-spaces amongst geodesic metric spaces and that CAT(0)-spaces coincide with Alexandrov spaces of non-positive curvatures; and this provides a rather large class of metric spaces which are rounded ball with power type
2
. It is not difficult to show that ultrametric spaces satisfy inequality (
24
) with
p=1
and
C=2
. We give one example of an application of Theorem
6
.

Corollary 3
.

If
q>2
and
(\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{d_{Y}})
is a metric space with roundness 2, in particular an Alexandrov space of non-positive curvature, then

\mathsf{c}_{\mathsf{Y}}(\ell_{q}^{k})=\Omega\Big(k^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\Big).

Remark 4
.

To the best of our knowledge, the only known proof of Corollary
3
can be found in the work of Eskenazis, Mendel, and Naor in
[
14
]
where it was shown that Alexandrov spaces of non-positive curvature have metric cotype
2
. This is a particular case of a much deeper result which says that
q
-barycentric metric spaces have
sharp
metric cotype
q
, and whose proof partly relies on a version of Pisier’s martingale inequality in the context of nonlinear martingales.

3.
Embeddability obstruction via thin
\aleph_{0}
-branching diamond substructures

Using the self-improvement argument together with the smallness of approximate midpoint sets to prove Theorem
1
has the other advantage of being easily generalizable to the asymptotic setting. It is well-known that the size of a
t
-approximate metric midpoint set in an asymptotically uniformly convex Banach spaces is “small”. By “small” we mean that the set is included in the (Banach space) sum of a compact set and a ball of small radius. Therefore the techniques from the previous sections can be adequately modified to show that the presence of countably branching versions of the Laakso-type substructure is a bi-Lipschitz embeddability obstruction. A similar fact for countably branching diamond and Laakso graphs was first proved in
[
6
]
and generalized in
[
31
]
.

The only reason to work with Laakso-type substructures in the previous sections was to produce spaces with the doubling property. In the asymptotic setting, we need to work with substructures whose underlying graphs have vertices with countably many neighbors and fail the doubling property altogether. Therefore, we will only consider simpler diamond-type substructures.

As noted in
[
6
]
it is more convenient to work with the notion of asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a Banach space and
t\in(0,1)
. Define

\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{X}}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\inf_{x\in S_{\mathfrak{X}}}\sup_{Z\in\mathrm{cof}(\mathfrak{X})}\inf_{z\in S_{Z}}\max\{\|x+tz\|,\|x-tz\|\}-1.

The norm of
\mathfrak{X}
is said to be asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex if
\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{X}}(t)>0
for every
t\in(0,1)
. Being asymptotically midpoint uniformly convexifiable is formally weaker than being asymptotically uniformly convexifiable. However, it is still open whether asymptotic uniform convexity and asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity are equivalent notions up to renorming. We now recall some facts that we will need which can be found in
[
6
]
. A characterization of asymptotic midpoint uniformly convex norms was given in
[
13
]
in terms of the Kuratowski measure of noncompactness of approximate midpoint sets. Recall that the Kuratowski measure of noncompactness of a subset
S
of a metric space, denoted by
\alpha(S)
, is defined as the infimum of all
\varepsilon>0
such that
S
can be covered by a finite number of sets of diameter less than
\varepsilon
. Note that it is a property of the metric.

In
[
13
]
it was shown that a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\sup_{x\in S_{\mathfrak{X}}}\alpha(\mathrm{Mid}(-x,x,t))=0.

To prove the main result of this section we need the following lemma which is a particular case of Lemma 4.3 in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 5
.

If the norm of a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex, then for every
t\in(0,1)
and every
x,y\in\mathfrak{X}
there exists a finite subset
S
of
\mathfrak{X}
such that

(25)

\mathrm{Mid}(x,y,\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{X}}(t)/4)\subset S+2t\|x-y\|B_{\mathfrak{X}}.

We define thin diamond substructures that can be used to prove non-embeddability results.

Definition 2

(Thin
\kappa
-branching diamond substructure)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\varepsilon>0
,
\kappa
be a cardinal number, and
I
a set of cardinality
\kappa
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that a metric space
\mathsf{X}
admits a
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
\kappa
-branching
k
-diamond substructure
if there exists a collection
\mathcal{D}^{\kappa}_{k}(\varepsilon,p)
of points indexed by
\mathsf{D}^{\kappa}_{k}
such that for every
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k
if
\{s,\{m_{i}\}_{i\in I},t\}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\kappa}_{k}
is indexed by a copy of one of the diamond created at step
\ell
then:

(
d_{1}
)

\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,m_{i})=\mathsf{d_{X}}(m_{i},t)=\frac{1}{2}(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/p}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)
,  for all
i\in I

(
d_{2}
)

\mathsf{d_{X}}(m_{i},m_{j})=2^{1-1/p}\varepsilon\cdot\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)
for all
i\neq j
.

In Lemma
6
below, we provide a construction of a
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
\aleph_{0}
-branching
k
-diamond substructure in
L_{p}
-spaces, which in turns implies for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\varepsilon>0
the existence of an
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
\aleph_{0}
-branching
k
-diamond substructure.

Lemma 6
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty)
, every
\varepsilon>0
, and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
;
L_{p}
admits a
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
\aleph_{0}
-branching
k
-diamond substructure.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{i,j,k}
stand in for the characteristic function
\chi_{\left[k+\frac{i-1}{2^{j}},k+\frac{i}{2^{j}}\right]}
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
. The
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
\aleph_{0}
-branching
k
-diamond substructure in
L_{p}
with parameter
\varepsilon>0
is defined recursively as follows. For simplicity, we start the induction with the
0
-diamond graph
\mathsf{D}^{\omega}_{0}
to be a single edge with endpoint
s
and
t
, and (again identifying the points in
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}_{k}(\varepsilon,p)
with the vertices of
\mathsf{D}^{\omega}_{k}
) we define
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\omega}(\varepsilon,p):=\{s,t\}
by
s\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\chi_{[0,1]}
and
t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}-\chi_{[0,1]}
and the conditions are vacuously satisfied.
Suppose now that
\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\omega}
has already been defined such that
\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\omega}\subseteq L_{p}[0,k+1]
. To construct
\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\omega}
we introduce for every edge
\{s,t\}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\omega}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
a “midpoint” as follows:

(26)

m_{i}=\frac{s+t}{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}(-1)^{r}\varepsilon\|s-t\|_{p}\chi_{r,i,k+1}.

Then,

\displaystyle\|s-m_{i}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{s-t}{2}-\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}(-1)^{r}\varepsilon\|s-t\|_{p}\chi_{r,i,k+1}\Big\|^{p}_{p}=\Big\|\frac{s-t}{2}\Big\|_{p}^{p}+\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}(-1)^{r}\varepsilon\|s-t\|_{p}\chi_{r,i,k+1}\Big\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{s-t}{2}\Big\|_{p}^{p}+\varepsilon^{p}\|s-t\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})}{2^{p}}\|s-t\|_{p}^{p},

wherein the second equality we used the fact that the vectors have disjoint supports (in
[0,k+1]
and
[k+1,k+2]
, respectively).

For
i<j
, observe that
\chi_{r,i,k+1}=\sum_{\ell=(r-1)2^{j-i}+1}^{r2^{j-i}}\chi_{\ell,j,k+1}
, and so

\displaystyle\|m_{i}-m_{j}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}(-1)^{r}\varepsilon\|s-t\|_{p}\chi_{r,i,k+1}-\sum_{r=1}^{2^{j}}(-1)^{r}\varepsilon\|s-t\|_{p}\chi_{r,j,k+1}\Big\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{p}\|s-t\|^{p}_{p}\left\|\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}\sum_{\ell=(r-1)2^{j-i}+1}^{r2^{j-i}}\Big((-1)^{r}-(-1)^{\ell}\Big)\chi_{\ell,j,k+1}\right\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{p}\|s-t\|^{p}_{p}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}\sum_{\ell=(r-1)2^{j-i}+1}^{r2^{j-i}}\int_{k+1+\frac{\ell-1}{2^{j}}}^{k+1+\frac{\ell}{2^{j}}}\left|(-1)^{r}-(-1)^{\ell}\right|^{p}\mathop{dx}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{p}\|s-t\|^{p}_{p}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{2^{i}}\frac{2^{j-i}}{2}\cdot 2^{-j}\cdot 2^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{p}\|s-t\|^{p}_{p}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{p}

\displaystyle=2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}\|s-t\|^{p}_{p}\qed

Next, we prove the contraction principle that is needed in the asymptotic setting.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
such that
(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/p}\leqslant 2
and let
\kappa
be an infinite cardinality. Assume that
\mathcal{D}^{\kappa}_{k}(\varepsilon,p)
is a
(\varepsilon,p)
-thin
\kappa
-branching
k
-diamond substructure in
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
and that
f\colon\mathsf{X}\to(\mathfrak{Y},\|\cdot\|)
is a bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion
D
. Then for every
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k
, if
\{s,\{m_{i}\}_{i\in I},t\}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\kappa}_{k}
is indexed by a copy of one of the diamond graph
\mathsf{D}_{1}^{\kappa}
created at step
\ell
, we have:

(27)

\|f(s)-f(t)\|\leqslant D\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/p}\left(1-\frac{1}{5}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right).

Proof.

Assume that for all
x,y\in\mathsf{X}

(28)

\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y)\leqslant\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leqslant D\mathsf{d_{X}}(x,y).

We claim that there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(29)

f(m_{j})\notin\text{Mid}\left(f(s),f(t),\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right).

Assuming for a moment that (
29
) holds, then we have either

\|f(m_{j})-f(t)\|>\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right)\|f(s)-f(t)\|

or

\|f(m_{j})-f(s)\|>\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right)\|f(s)-f(t)\|.

In both cases it follows from (
28
) and condition
(d_{1})
above that

\displaystyle\|f(s)-f(t)\|

\displaystyle<D\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/p}\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\delta}_{X}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant D\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/p}\left(1-\frac{1}{5}\tilde{\delta}_{X}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right).

It remains to prove (
29
). By Lemma
5
there exists a finite subset
S:=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{Y}
such that

\text{Mid}\left(f(s),f(t),\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right)\subset S+\frac{\varepsilon}{8D}\|f(s)-f(t)\|B_{\mathfrak{Y}}.

If for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

f(m_{i})\in\text{Mid}\left(f(s),f(t),\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\delta}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16D}\right)\right),

then
f(m_{i})=z_{n_{i}}+y_{i}
with
z_{n_{i}}\in S
and
y_{i}\in\mathfrak{Y}
so that

\|y_{i}\|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{8D}\|f(s)-f(t)\|.

Therefore, for all
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle\|z_{n_{i}}-z_{n_{j}}\|

\displaystyle\geqslant\|f(m_{i})-f(m_{j})\|-\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathsf{d_{X}}(m_{i},m_{j})-\frac{\varepsilon}{4D}\|f(s)-f(t)\|

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathsf{d_{X}}(m_{i},m_{j})-\frac{\varepsilon}{4D}(\|f(s)-f(m_{i})\|+\|f(m_{i})-f(t)\|)

\displaystyle\geqslant 2^{1-1/p}\varepsilon\cdot\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}(1+(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/p}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\geqslant 2^{1-1/p}\varepsilon\cdot\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\cdot\mathsf{d_{X}}(s,t)>0,

which contradicts the fact that
S
is finite.
∎

Since in the proof of Lemma
7
we were careful to only use the upper bound in (
28
) for pairs of points of the form
\{s,m_{i}\}
or
\{t,m_{i}\}
, the derivation of Theorem
6
below from Lemma
7
is by now standard and thus omitted.

Theorem 6
.

Let
1\leqslant p<q
and assume that
(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{d_{X}})
admits a bi-Lipschitz embedding with distortion
D
into a
p
-asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex Banach space
\mathfrak{Y}
. There exists
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(p,q,D,\mathfrak{Y})>0
such that if
\mathsf{X}
admits a
(\varepsilon,q)
-thin
\aleph_{0}
-branching
k
-diamond substructure then
D=\Omega(k^{1/p-1/q})
.

The following consequence is immediate.

Corollary 4
.

L_{q}[0,1]
does not bi-Lipschitzly embed into any
p
-asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex Banach space if
q>p\geqslant 1
. In particular,
L_{q}[0,1]
does not bi-Lipschitzly embed into
\ell_{p}
if
q>p\geqslant 1
.

Remark 5
.

Corollary
4
is not new since it can be shown using classical approximate midpoint techniques (see
[
8
, Chapter 10, Section 2]
or
[
17
]
for instance). The classical approximate midpoint technique provides an obstruction of qualitative nature and relies on some linear arguments but it can handle weaker notions of embeddings. Our proof of Theorem
6
, and in turn of Corollary
4
, identifies concrete and purely metric structures that provide quantitative obstructions to bi-Lipschitz embeddings.

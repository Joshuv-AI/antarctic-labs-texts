Sequential parametrized topological complexity of sphere bundles Thanks: Both authors were partially supported by an EPSRC research grant
Michael Farber, Amit Kumar Paul

Abstract
. Autonomous motion of a system (robot) is controlled by a motion planning algorithm. A sequential parametrized motion planning algorithm [ 10 ] works under variable external conditions and generates continuous motions of the system to attain the prescribed sequence of states at prescribed moments of time. Topological complexity of such algorithms characterises their structure and discontinuities. Information about states of the system consistent with states of the external conditions is described by a fibration p : E → B p:E\to B where the base B B parametrises the external conditions and each fibre p − 1 ​ ( b ) p^{-1}(b) is the configuration space of the system constrained by external conditions b ∈ B b\in B ; more detail on this approach is given below. Our main goal in this paper is to study the sequential topological complexity of sphere bundles ξ ˙ : E ˙ → B \dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B ; in other words we study “parametrized families of spheres” and sequential parametrized motion planning algorithms for such bundles. We use the Euler and Stiefel - Whitney characteristic classes to obtain lower bounds on the topological complexity. We illustrate our results by many explicit examples. Some related results for the special case r = 2 r=2 were described earlier in [ 13 ] .

2.
Sequential parametrized topological complexity

In this section we review the notion of sequential parametrized topological complexity introduced in
[
10
]
, see also
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
. This notion is motivated by the motion planning problem of robotics. We consider an autonomous robot moving in varying external conditions which are parametrized by a topological space
B
.
An important special case is when the external conditions are the positions of the obstacles; in this case the space
B
is the appropriate configuration space. Mathematically, it is convenient to consider a general situation when
B
is an arbitrary topological space. We formalise the problem by considering a Hurewicz fibration
p:E\to B
where for a choice of external conditions
b\in B
the fibre
X_{b}=p^{-1}(b)

is the space of all configurations of the system consistent with external conditions
b
. The total space
E
is the union of the fibres

E=\sqcup_{b\in B}X_{b}
and its topology reflects natural connectivity of the situation. We refer the reader to
[
12
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
where the motion planning problem of many autonomously moving objects in the presence of multiple obstacles was studied.

Let
p:E\to B
be a Hurewicz fibration. As usual, the symbol
E^{I}
stands for the space of all continuous paths
\alpha:I=[0,1]\to E
. We denote by
E^{I}_{B}\subset E^{I}

the space of all paths
\alpha:I\to E
such that
p\circ\alpha:I\to B
is constant.
Such paths
\alpha:I\to E
represent motions of the system under fixed external conditions.
Fix
r\geq 2
points

0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{r}\leq 1
in
I
(“the time schedule”)
and consider the evaluation map

(2)

\displaystyle\Pi_{r}:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{r}_{B},\quad\Pi_{r}(\alpha)=(\alpha(t_{1}),\alpha(t_{2}),\dots,\alpha(t_{r})).

where for an integer
r\geq 2
we denote

E^{r}_{B}=\{(e_{1},\cdots,e_{r})\in E^{r};\,p(e_{1})=\cdots=p(e_{r})\}.

A section
s:E^{r}_{B}\to E^{I}
of the fibration
\Pi_{r}
can be interpreted as
a parametrized sequential motion planning algorithm
, i.e. it is
a function which assigns to every sequence of points
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{r})\in E^{r}_{B}
a continuous path
\alpha:I\to E
(“the motion of the system”) satisfying
\alpha(t_{i})=e_{i}
for every
i=1,2,\dots,r
and such that the path

p\circ\alpha:I\to B
is constant. The latter condition means that the system moves under the constant external conditions
(such as positions of the obstacles).

\Pi_{r}
is a Hurewicz fibration, see
[
3
, Appendix]
; the fibre of
\Pi_{r}
is
(\Omega X)^{r-1}
where
X
is the fibre of
p:E\to B
.
Typically
\Pi_{r}
does not admit continuous sections and hence the
motion planning algorithms are generally discontinuous.

The following definition gives a measure of the complexity of sequential parametrized motion planning algorithms.

Definition 1
.

The
r
-th sequential parametrized topological complexity
of the fibration
p:E\to B
, denoted
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]
, is defined as the sectional category of the fibration
\Pi_{r}
, i.e.

(3)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]:={\sf{secat}}(\Pi_{r}).

In more detail,
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]
is defined as the minimal integer
k
such that there is a open cover

\{U_{0},U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\}
of
E^{r}_{B}
with the property that each open set
U_{i}

admits a continuous section
s_{i}:U_{i}\to E^{I}_{B}
of
\Pi_{r}
.
The following Lemma allows using arbitrary partitions instead of open covers:

Lemma 2

(see Proposition 3.6 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial fibration where
E
and
B
are metrisable separable ANRs. Then the
r
-th sequential parametrized topological complexity
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]
equals the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that the space
E_{B}^{r}
admits a partition

E_{B}^{r}=F_{0}\sqcup F_{1}\sqcup...\sqcup F_{k},\quad F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset\text{ \ for }i\neq j,

and on each set
F_{i}
there exists a continuous section
s_{i}:F_{i}\to E_{B}^{I}
of the fibration
\Pi_{r}
.

Alternative descriptions of sequential parametrized topological complexity

In certain situations it is convenient to generalise the above definition of
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]
as follows.
Let
K
be a path-connected finite CW-complex and let
k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}\in K
be a collection of
r
pairwise
distinct points of
K
, where
r\geq 2
. For a Hurewicz fibration
p:E\to B
, consider the space
E^{K}_{B}
of all continuous maps

\alpha:K\to E
such that the composition
p\circ\alpha:K\to B
is a constant map.
We equip
E^{K}_{B}
with the compact-open topology induced from the function space
E^{K}
.
Consider the evaluation map

\Pi_{r}^{K}:E^{K}_{B}\to E^{r}_{B},\quad\Pi^{K}_{r}(\alpha)=(\alpha(k_{1}),\alpha(k_{2}),\cdots,\alpha(k_{r}))\quad\mbox{for}\quad\alpha\in E^{K}_{B}.

\Pi^{K}_{r}
is a Hurewicz fibration by the result of the Appendix to
[
3
]
.

Lemma 3
.

(See
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.5)
For any path-connected finite CW-complex
K

and a set of pairwise distinct points
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\in K
one has

{\sf{secat}}(\Pi^{K}_{r})={\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B].

Proof.

Let
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{r}\leq 1
be a given time schedule used in the definition of the map
\Pi_{r}=\Pi_{r}^{I}
given by (
2
).
Since
K
is path-connected we may find a continuous map
\gamma:I\to K
with
\gamma(t_{i})=k_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
. We obtain a continuous map
F_{\gamma}:E^{K}_{B}\to E^{I}_{B}
acting by the formula
F_{\gamma}(\alpha)=\alpha\circ\gamma
. It is easy to see that the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{E_{B}^{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\scriptstyle{\Pi_{r}^{K}}
\textstyle{E_{B}^{I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Pi_{r}^{I}}
\textstyle{E_{B}^{r}}

Thus, any partial section
s:U\to E^{K}_{r}
of
\Pi_{r}^{K}
defines a partial section
F\circ s
of
\Pi_{r}^{I}
implying

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]={\sf{secat}}(\Pi^{I}_{r})\leq{\sf{secat}}(\Pi_{r}^{K}).

To obtain the inverse inequality note that any locally finite CW-complex is metrisable. Applying Tietze extension
theorem we can find continuous functions
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{r}:K\to[0,1]
such that
\psi_{i}(k_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
i.e.
\psi_{i}(k_{j})
equals 1 for
j=i
and it equals
0
for
j\not=i
. The function
f=\min\{1,\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\cdot\psi_{i}\}:K\to[0,1]

has the property that
f(k_{i})=t_{i}
for every
i=1,2,\dots,r
. We obtain a continuous map
F^{\prime}:E^{I}_{B}\to E^{K}_{B}
, where
F^{\prime}(\beta)=\beta\circ f
,
\beta\in E^{I}_{B}
, which appears in the commutative diagram

\textstyle{E_{B}^{I}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\Pi_{r}^{I}}
\textstyle{E_{B}^{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Pi_{r}^{K}}
\textstyle{E_{B}^{r}}

As above, this implies the opposite inequality

{\sf{secat}}(\Pi_{r}^{K})\leq{\sf{secat}}(\Pi^{I}_{r})

and completes the proof.
∎

Example 4
.

As an example consider the case when
p:E\to B
is a principal bundle with a connected topological group
G
as fibre. Then

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]={\sf{cat}}(G^{r-1})={\sf{TC}}_{r}(G),

see
[
10
]
, Proposition 3.3. In particular, for the Hopf bundle
p:S^{3}\to S^{2}
one has

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:S^{3}\to S^{2}]={\sf{cat}}((S^{1})^{r-1})=r-1.

The following result (see Proposition 6.3 from
[
10
]
) provides a lower bound for
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]
:

Proposition 5
.

For a fibration
p:E\to B
, consider the diagonal map
\Delta:E\to E^{r}_{B}
where
\Delta(e)=(e,e,\cdots,e)
, and the induced by
\Delta
homomorphism in cohomology

\Delta^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(E^{r}_{B};R)\to H^{\ast}(E;R)

with coefficients in a ring
R
.
If there exist cohomology classes

u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}\in\ker[\Delta^{*}:H^{*}(E_{B}^{r};R)\to H^{*}(E;R)]

such that

u_{1}\cup\cdots\cup u_{k}\neq 0\in H^{*}(E_{B}^{r};R),

then
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]\geq k
.

The upper bound is given by Proposition 6.1 from
[
10
]
which states:

Proposition 6
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial fibration with fiber
X
, where
E,B,X
are CW-complexes. Assume that the fiber
X
is
k
-connected, where
k\geq 0
. Then

(4)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]\leq\left\lceil\frac{r\cdot\dim X+\dim B-k}{k+1}\right\rceil.

Example 7
.

Let
\xi:E\to B
be a rank
q\geq 2
vector bundle over a CW-complex
B
. Consider the unit sphere bundle
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B
.
Its fibre is the sphere
S^{q-1}
and the upper bound (
4
) gives

(5)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\leq\left\lceil\frac{r(q-1)+\dim B-q+2}{q-1}\right\rceil=r-1+\left\lceil\frac{\dim B+1}{q-1}\right\rceil.

3
below we give statements allowing sharper upper bounds in some cases.

We finish this section with a well-known statement which will be used later in this paper.

Lemma 8
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a fibration where the base
B
is metrizable. Then there exists a partition

B=A_{0}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{k}

with
k={\sf{secat}}[p:E\to B]

such that each set
A_{i}
admits a continuous section of
p
and, additionally,

(6)

\displaystyle\bar{A_{i}}\subset\bigcup_{j\geq i}\ A_{j}

for every
i=0,1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

For
k={\sf{secat}}[p:E\to B]
, let
U_{0}\cup U_{1}\cup\dots\cup U_{k}=B
be an open cover
with continuous sections
s_{i}:U_{i}\to E
of
p
where
i=0,1,\dots,k
.

For
b\in B
we denote by
\mu(b)
the cardinality of the set

\{i\ ;\ b\in U_{i}\},
i.e.
\mu(b)\in\{1,2,\dots,k+1\}
is the number of sets
U_{i}
containing the point
b
.
If a sequence of points
b_{n}\in B
with
\mu(b_{n})=i
converges to a point
b_{0}\in B
then clearly

\mu(b_{0})\leq i
.

Denote

A_{i}=\{b\in B\ ;\mu(b)=k+1-i\},\quad 0\leq i\leq k.

Clearly the sets
\{A_{i}\}
are pairwise disjoint and
B=A_{0}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{k}.

To prove (
6
) assume that
b_{0}\in\bar{A_{i}}
. This means that
b_{0}=\lim b_{n}
where
b_{n}\in A_{i}
, i.e.
\mu(b_{n})=k+1-i
. By the remark above
\mu(b_{0})\leq k+1-i
, i.e.
b_{0}\in A_{j}
where
j\geq i
.

For a sequence
\alpha=(i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{p})
, where
0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{p}\leq k
, we denote

U_{\alpha}=U_{i_{1}}\cap U_{2}\cap\dots\cap U_{i_{p}}
and
|\alpha|=p
. For two such sequences we shall write

\alpha>\beta
if
\beta
is a subsequence of
\alpha
. We shall also introduce the notation

U^{\prime}_{\alpha}=U_{\alpha}-\bigcup_{|\beta|>|\alpha|}U_{\beta}.

The set
A_{i}
can be represented as the disjoint union

A_{i}=\bigcup_{|\alpha|=k+1-i}U^{\prime}_{\alpha}.

Similar to the argument given above, one has

\overline{U^{\prime}_{\alpha}}\subset\cup_{\beta<\alpha}U^{\prime}_{\beta}

and therefore

\overline{U^{\prime}_{\alpha}}\cap U^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for every pair
\alpha\not=\alpha^{\prime}
satisfying

|\alpha|=|\alpha^{\prime}|
. Thus, the section
s_{i_{1}}
is defined and is continuous on
U^{\prime}_{\alpha}
where

\alpha=(i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{p})
. Moreover, the sections
s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
jointly define as explained above continuous sections over the sets
A_{i}
.
∎

3.
Sharp upper bound for
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]
.

The results of this section (Theorem
9
and Corollary
10
) sometimes allow to sharpen the upper bound given by Proposition
6
; this will be illustrated by examples in §
8
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial bundle with fibre
X
where the spaces
X,E,B
are finite CW-complexes. Assume that
X
is

k
-connected, where
k\geq 1
, and the fraction

\frac{\dim E^{r}_{B}}{k+1}=\frac{r\cdot\dim X+\dim B}{k+1}=m

is an integer, where
r\geq 2
.
Then:

(A) One has
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]\leq m

and the equality

(7)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]=m

holds if and only if there exists
a cohomology class

\theta\in H^{k+1}(E^{r}_{B};\mathcal{A}),

where
\mathcal{A}
is a local coefficient system over the space
E^{r}_{B}
,
such that
\Delta^{\ast}\theta=0\in H^{k+1}(E;\mathcal{A}|_{E})
and the
m
-th power

\theta^{m}=\theta\cup\theta\cup\dots\cup\theta\not=0\in H^{(k+1)m}(E^{r}_{B};\mathcal{A}^{\otimes m})

is nonzero. Here
\Delta:E\to E^{r}_{B}
denotes the diagonal inclusion.

(B) If, additionally to the above assumptions, the base
B
is simply connected and the homology group
H_{k+1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is
torsion free then
the equality (
7
) holds if and only if there exist integral cohomology classes

v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}\in H^{k+1}(E^{r}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

\Delta^{\ast}v_{i}=0
for
i=1,2,\dots,m

and the cup-product

v_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{m}\not=0\in H^{(k+1)m}(E^{r}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}})

is nonzero.

Proof.

To prove statement (A) we note that the inequality
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]\leq m
follows from Proposition
6
. The existence of a class
\theta\in H^{k+1}(E^{r}_{B};\mathcal{A})
satisfying
\Delta^{\ast}(\theta)=0
and
\theta^{m}\not=0
implies
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]\geq m
, by Proposition
5
. We only need to prove the “only if” part of the statement.

Indeed, assuming that
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]=m
we may apply Theorem 3 of A. Schwarz
[
23
]
which implies that the
fibrewise join
\Pi_{r}^{\ast m}

of
m
copies of the fibration (
2
) admits no continuous sections.
This fibrewise join
\Pi_{r}^{\ast m}
has as its fibre the
m
-fold join

(8)

\displaystyle F_{m,r}=F_{r}\ast F_{r}\ast\dots\ast F_{r}

where
F_{r}=(\Omega X)^{r-1}

is the fibre of (
2
).

Since
X
is
k
-connected, the loop space
\Omega X
is
(k-1)
-connected and hence the fibre
F_{m,r}
is
c
-connected where

c=m(k-1)+2(m-1)=m(k+1)-2=\dim E^{r}_{B}-2.

Here we use the well-known fact that the join of a
p
-connected space and a
q
-connected space is
(p+q+2)
-connected.

The first obstruction
\Theta
for a section of
\Pi_{r}^{\ast m}
lies in the cohomology group

\Theta\in H^{m(k+1)}(E^{r}_{B};\{\pi_{c+1}(F_{m,r})\}).

Here
\{\pi_{c+1}(F_{m,r})\}
is the local system on
E^{r}_{B}
formed by the homotopy groups of the fibres. By
Hurewicz theorem one has an isomorphism of local systems

\{\pi_{c+1}(F_{m,r})\}\simeq{\mathcal{H}}_{c+1}(F_{m,r}).

Here
{\mathcal{H}}_{c+1}(F_{m,r})
is the local system of homology groups
H_{c+1}(F_{m,r})
of the fibres of the fibration

\Pi_{r}^{\ast m}
.

Note that
\Theta\not=0
is nonzero since otherwise the fibration
\Pi_{r}^{\ast m}
would admit a continuous section as all higher order obstruction clearly vanish by dimensional reasons.

Using the Künneth formula (see Chapter 1,
[
23
]
) we can write
an isomorphism of local systems

{\mathcal{H}}_{c+1}(F_{m,r})\simeq{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(F_{r})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(F_{r})\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(F_{r})

where
\mathcal{A}={\mathcal{H}}_{k}(F_{r})

is the local system of the homology groups of the fibres of the original fibration (
2
).
By Theorem 1 from (
[
23
]
) one has

\Theta=\theta\cup\theta\cup\dots\cup\theta=\theta^{m}\not=0

where

\theta\in H^{k+1}(E^{r}_{B};{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(F_{r}))

is the first obstruction for a section of the fibration (
2
).

Note that
\Delta^{\ast}(\theta)=0
as the fibration (
2
) has an obvious section over the diagonal (the constant paths). This completes the proof of (A).

In the case (B) the base
B
is simply connected and hence the local coefficient system

\mathcal{A}={\mathcal{H}}_{k}(F_{r})
is trivial and the abelian group
H_{k}(F_{r})
is free abelian, i.e.

H_{k}(F_{r})=\oplus_{i\in I}A_{i}
where each
A_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore the tensor power
H_{k}(F_{r})^{\otimes m}
is the direct sum

H_{k}(F_{r})^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in I}A_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes A_{i_{m}}

where each tensor product
A_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes A_{i_{m}}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Since the class

\Theta=\theta^{m}\in H^{m(k+1)}(E^{r}_{B};H_{k}(F_{r})^{\otimes m})

is nonzero at least one of its images under the coefficient homomorphisms

H_{k}(F_{r})^{\otimes m}\to A_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes A_{i_{m}}

is nonzero. In that case, the product of the classes

v_{i_{s}}\,\in\,H^{k+1}(E^{r}_{B};A_{i_{s}}),\quad s=1,2,\dots,m

is nonzero and clearly
\Delta^{\ast}(v_{i_{s}})=0.

∎

Corollary 10
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a locally trivial bundle with fibre
X
where the spaces
X,E,B
are finite CW-complexes.
Assume that
X
is
k
-connected, where
k\geq 1
, and
H_{k+1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion free. Additionally, assume that the base
B
is simply connected and

r\cdot\dim X+\dim B=(k+1)m

for some integers
r\geq 2
and
m\geq 2
. If the cup-length of the kernel

(9)

\displaystyle\ker[\Delta^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(E^{r}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{\ast}(E;{\mathbb{Z}})]

is less than
m
then
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[p:E\to B]<m
.

Proof.

This Corollary is a reformulation of the part (B) of Theorem
9
. We only need to add that the kernel (
9
) contains no classes of degree
\leq k
. This can be easily seen from the spectral sequence of the fibration

\pi_{r}:E^{r}_{B}\to E,\quad\pi_{r}(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})=e_{1},

for which the diagonal map
\Delta:E\to E^{r}_{B}
is a section.
∎

4.
Upper bounds for the sequential parametrized topological complexity of sphere bundles

Let
\xi:E\to B
be a locally trivial vector bundle of rank
q={\sf{rk}}(\xi)
equipped with a metric structure, i.e. with a continuous scalar product of each fibre. We shall denote
E=E(\xi)
when dealing with several bundles at once.

Let
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B
denote the unit sphere bundle of
\xi
; the fibre of
\dot{\xi}
is the sphere
S^{q-1}
.
Our goal in this paper is to estimate the sequential parametrized topological complexity

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B].

To state our first result we need to introduce some notations. We shall denote by
\ddot{E}
the space of pairs
(e,e^{\prime})
of unit vectors
e,e^{\prime}\in\dot{E}
such that
\xi(e)=\xi(e^{\prime})
and
e\perp e^{\prime}
. In other words,
\ddot{E}
is the total space of the bundle of Stiefel manifolds associated with
\xi
. Let
\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}
denote the projection
\ddot{\xi}(e,e^{\prime})=e
. Clearly

\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}
is a bundle with fibre
S^{q-2}
.

Proposition 11
.

One has

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\leq{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}]+r-1.

Proof.

Let
Y=Y_{r}
denote the graph with a central vertex
k_{1}
and the vertexes
k_{2},\dots,k_{r}
, each connected to
k_{1}
by an edge as shown on Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
Graph
Y_{r}

Consider the map

(10)

\displaystyle\Pi^{Y}_{r}:\dot{E}_{B}^{Y}\to\dot{E}_{B}^{r}

which acts as the evaluation of a map
\psi:Y\to X
at the points
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
, i.e.
\Pi^{Y}_{r}(\psi)=(\psi(k_{1}),\psi(k_{2}),\dots,\psi(k_{r})).
By Lemma
3
, the sectional category of this map equals
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]
.
A partial section
s
of
\Pi
maps a configuration of points
(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
lying in a single fibre (i.e. such that
\dot{\xi}(e_{1})=\dot{\xi}(e_{2})=\dots=\dot{\xi}(e_{r}))
to a map
\psi:Y\to\dot{E}
with

\dot{\xi}\circ\psi={\rm const}
and

\Pi^{Y}_{r}(\psi)=(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
, i.e.
\psi(k_{i})=e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r
. In other words, to specify a section we need to specify
r-1
paths

\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\dots,\gamma_{r}\in(\dot{E})_{B}^{I}

leading from

e_{1}
to each of the points
e_{2},\dots,e_{r}
correspondingly, i.e.
\gamma_{i}(0)=e_{1}
and
\gamma_{i}(1)=e_{i}
for
i=2,3,\dots,r
.

4.1.
Sets
A_{i}

Applying Lemma
8
to the bundle
\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}
we obtain a partition

(11)

\displaystyle\dot{E}=A_{0}\sqcup A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{k},\quad\mbox{where}\quad k={\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}],

such that
\overline{A}_{i}\subset\cup_{j\geq i}A_{j}
and there exist continuous sections
s_{i}:A_{i}\to\ddot{E}
of the fibration
\ddot{\xi}
for every
i=0,1,\dots,k
.

4.2.
The set
F_{0}

The set
F_{0}\subset\dot{E}^{r}_{B}
is defined as follows:

F_{0}=\{(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})\in\dot{E}^{r}_{B};e_{2}\not=-e_{1},\,e_{3}\not=-e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\not=-e_{1}\}.

We can specify a continuous section of
\Pi^{Y}_{r}
over
F_{0}
by defining the paths
\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{r}
, where

(12)

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}(t)=\frac{(1-t)e_{1}+te_{j}}{||(1-t)e_{1}+te_{j}||},\quad j=2,3,\dots,r

as explained above.

4.3.
The sets
F^{i}_{J}

For a nonempty subset
J\subset\{2,3,\dots,r\}
and for an index
i=0,1,\dots k
, we denote by

F^{i}_{J}\subset\dot{E}^{r}_{B}
the set of all configurations
(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})\in\dot{E}^{r}_{B}
such that

(1)

e_{1}\in A_{i}
;

(2)

e_{j}=-e_{1}
for all
j\in J
;

(3)

e_{j}\not=-e_{1}
for all
j\notin J
.

Each set
F_{J}^{i}
admits a continuous section of the fibration (
10
). Indeed, for
j\in J
we can define

\gamma_{j}(t)=\cos(\pi t)\cdot e_{1}+\sin(\pi t)\cdot s_{i}(e_{1}),\quad t\in[0,1].

For
j\notin J
we define
\gamma_{j}(t)
by formula (
12
).

The closure of the set
F_{J}^{i}
satisfies

(13)

\displaystyle\overline{F^{i}_{J}}\subset\bigcup_{J^{\prime}\supset J,\,i^{\prime}\geq i}F^{i^{\prime}}_{J^{\prime}}.

In particular we see that

\overline{F^{i}_{J}}\cap F^{i^{\prime}}_{J^{\prime}}=\emptyset\quad\mbox{if}\quad|J|+i=|J^{\prime}|+i^{\prime}.

4.4.
The sets
G_{s}

Define

G_{s}=\bigsqcup_{|J|+i=s}F^{i}_{J},\quad s=1,2,\dots,k+r-1.

The sections described above define jointly a continuous section of (
10
) over each
G_{s}
. Together with
the set
F_{0}=G_{0}
defined earlier we obtain a partition

G_{0}\sqcup G_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup G_{k+r-1}

implying that

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}]\leq k+r-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Corollaries of Proposition
11

Corollary 12
.

Let
\xi:E\to B
be a vector bundle admitting a complex structure. Then

(14)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]=r-1\quad\mbox{for all}\quad r=2,3,\dots

Proof.

If the bundle
\xi
admits a complex structure then

{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}]=0.

Indeed, the multiplication by
\sqrt{-1}
defines a continuous section of the bundle
\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}
which can be explicitly given by

s(e)=(e,e^{\prime})
where
e^{\prime}=\sqrt{-1}\cdot e
. Applying Proposition
11
gives
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\leq r-1
.
On the other hand,

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\geq{\sf{TC}}_{r}(S^{q-1})=r-1,

where
q={\sf{rk}}(\xi)
. Here we first used the well-known inequality relating the sequential parametrized topological complexity with the sequential topological complexity of the fibre; on the second step we used the known fact that the sequential topological complexity of an odd dimensional sphere is
r-1
.
∎

Remark 13
.

Clearly the value
r-1
is the lower bound for
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]
for any vector bundle
\xi
. Moreover,
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\geq r
if the rank
q={\sf{rk}}(\xi)
is odd. One may ask if there exist vector bundles
\xi
admitting no complex structure and such that

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]=r-1
for all
r
? In other words we ask whether equation (
14
) characterises the class of vector bundles admitting a complex structure.

Next we mention the following inequality which can be compared with the inequality of Lemma 5.9 from
[
13
]
.

Proposition 14
.

Let the vector bundle
\xi:E(\xi)\to B
be the Whitney sum
\xi=\xi_{1}\oplus\xi_{2}
.
Then

{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}(\xi)\to\dot{E}(\xi)]\leq{\sf{secat}}[\dot{\xi}_{1}:\dot{E}(\xi_{1})\to B]+{\sf{secat}}[\dot{\xi}_{2}:\dot{E}(\xi_{2})\to B]+1.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\xi=\xi_{1}\oplus\xi_{2}
. We want to estimate above the sectional category

{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}(\xi)\to\dot{E}(\xi)]
. A vector
e\in\dot{E}(\xi)
can be represented as a sum

e=e_{1}+e_{2},\quad|e_{1}|^{2}+|e_{2}|^{2}=1,

where
e_{1}\in E(\xi_{1})
and
e_{2}\in E(\xi_{2})
.

Denote by
G_{0}\subset\dot{E}(\xi)
the set of unit vectors
e=e_{1}+e_{2}
such that
e_{1}\not=0
and
e_{2}\not=0
. We define the section
s_{0}:G_{0}\to\ddot{E}(\xi)
by

s_{0}(e)=((e_{1},e_{2}),(e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2}))\in\ddot{E}(\xi)
where

e^{\prime}_{1}=-\frac{e_{1}}{|e_{1}|}\cdot|e_{2}|,\quad e^{\prime}_{2}=\frac{e_{2}}{|e_{2}|}\cdot|e_{1}|.

For
\alpha=1,2
, denote
k_{\alpha}={\sf{secat}}[\dot{\xi}_{\alpha}:\dot{E}(\xi_{\alpha})\to B]
and let

B=U^{\alpha}_{0}\cup U_{1}^{\alpha}\cup\dots\cup U_{k_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}
be an open cover with each set
U_{j}^{\alpha}
admitting a continuous section of the bundle
\dot{\xi}_{\alpha}:\dot{E}(\xi_{\alpha})\to B
. Using Lemma 3.5 from
[
9
]
we may find an open cover

B=W_{0}\cup W_{1}\cup\dots W_{k},\quad\mbox{where}\quad k=k_{1}+k_{2},

such that each open set
W_{j}
admits a continuous section
\sigma_{j}^{\alpha}:W_{j}\to\dot{E}(\xi_{\alpha})

for both bundles
\alpha=1
and
\alpha=2
.

For
j=0,1,\dots,k
define the set
F_{j}=F_{j}^{1}\sqcup F_{j}^{2}\subset\dot{E}(\xi)
as follows

F_{j}^{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha}^{-1}(W_{j})\,\subset\dot{E}(\xi_{\alpha})\subset\dot{E}(\xi),\quad\alpha=1,2.

We may define a continuous section
s_{j}^{\alpha}:F_{j}^{\alpha}\to\ddot{E}(\xi)
by the formula

(15)

\displaystyle s_{j}^{\alpha}(e)=(e,\sigma_{j}^{\beta}(\xi_{\alpha}(e))),\quad\beta\not=\alpha,\quad\beta\in\{1,2\}.

Two sections
s_{j}^{1}
and
s_{j}^{2}
jointly define a continuous section
s_{j}:F_{j}\to\ddot{E}(\xi)
since obviously

\overline{F_{j}^{1}}\cap\overline{F_{j}^{2}}=\emptyset
.

Now we see that the sets

G_{0},F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{k}

cover
\dot{E}(\xi)
and each of these sets admits a continuous section of
\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}(\xi)\to\dot{E}(\xi)
.
This gives the inequality

{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}(\xi)\to\dot{E}(\xi)]\leq k+1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 15
.

If
\xi=\eta\oplus\epsilon
where
\epsilon
is the trivial line bundle then

{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}(\xi)\to\dot{E}(\xi)]\leq{\sf{secat}}[\dot{\eta}:\dot{E}(\eta)\to B]+1

and

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\leq{\sf{secat}}[\dot{\eta}:\dot{E}(\eta)\to B]+r.

Corollary 16
.

If a vector bundle
\xi:E\to B
admits two non-vanishing linearly independent sections then

{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}\to\dot{E}]\leq 1
and
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\leq r.
If additionally the rank
q={\sf{rk}}(\xi)
is odd then

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]=r.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
14
with both bundles
\dot{\xi}_{1}:\dot{E}(\xi_{1})\to B
and
\dot{\xi}_{2}:\dot{E}(\xi_{2})\to B
having globally defined continuous sections. Proposition
14
gives
{\sf{secat}}[\ddot{\xi}:\ddot{E}(\xi)\to\dot{E}(\xi)]\leq 1
and finally by Proposition
11
,
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to B]\leq r
. If

and hence

.
∎

6.
Cohomology algebra of a sphere bundle

In this section we describe the cohomology algebra of a sphere bundle admitting a section.
The result of this section repeats a part of the arguments of the proof of Theorem 4.1 from
[
13
]
.
Cohomology algebras of more general sphere bundles were studied by W. Massey in
[
20
]
.
All cohomology groups in this section are with integer coefficients unless the coefficients are indicated explicitly.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\eta:E(\eta)\to B
be an oriented rank
q-1
real vector bundle and let
\xi=\eta\oplus\epsilon
be the Whitney sum of
\eta
and the trivial line bundle
\epsilon
. The integral cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))
of the unit sphere bundle
\dot{E}(\xi)
is the quotient of the polynomial ring
H^{\ast}(B)[\mathfrak{u}]
, where
\deg{\mathfrak{u}}=q-1
, with respect to the principal ideal generated by the class

(16)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}-\mathfrak{e}(\eta)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}},

where
{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\in H^{q-1}(B)
denotes the Euler class of
\eta
.

Proof.

Fix an orientation of
\epsilon
, i.e. select a section
s:B\to\dot{E}(\epsilon)\subset\dot{E}(\xi)
. Together with the orientation of
\eta
this determines an orientation of
\xi
.

Since the sphere bundle
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to B
has a section its Euler class vanishes,
{\mathfrak{e}}(\xi)=0\in H^{q}(B)
. It
admits a cohomological extension of the fibre, i.e. a cohomology class
{\mathfrak{u}}\in H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi))
such that its restriction onto every fibre is the fundamental class of the fibre, determined by the orientation of
\xi
.
The Leray - Hirsch theorem (see
[
24
]
, chapter 5, §7) gives a
graded group isomorphism

H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))\simeq H^{\ast}(B)\otimes H^{\ast}(S^{q-1}).

In other words, every cohomology class
a\in H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))
has a unique representation in the form

(17)

\displaystyle a=u+v\cdot{\mathfrak{u}},

where
u,v\in H^{\ast}(B)
. Here we identify
H^{\ast}(B)
with its image
\dot{\xi}^{\ast}(H^{\ast}(B))\subset H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))
under the ring homomorphism
\dot{\xi}^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(B)\to H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))
, which is a monomorphism; the symbol
v\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}
in (
17
) stands for the cup-product
\dot{\xi}^{\ast}(v)\cup{\mathfrak{u}}
.
The class
{\mathfrak{u}}
is not unique, it can be replaced by any class of the form
{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{u}}+u
where
u\in H^{q-1}(B)
.
We show below that there exists a specific choice of the fundamental class
{\mathfrak{u}}
such that

(18)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}\,\in\,H^{2(q-1)}(\dot{E}(\xi)).

Note that in this formula one has
{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}={\mathfrak{u}}\cdot{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
: indeed, if
q
is odd the classes commute but for
q
even the Euler class
{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
has order 2.

Denote by
W\subset\dot{E}(\xi)
the set of vectors
e
such that their scalar product with the section
s(\xi(e))
is non-negative,

W=\{e\in\dot{E}(\xi);\,\,\langle e,s(\xi(e))\rangle\,\geq 0\}.

Similarly, we denote

W^{\prime}=\{e\in\dot{E}(\xi);\,\,\langle e,s(\xi(e))\rangle\,\leq 0\}.

Each of the sets
W,W^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to the total space of the unit disc bundle of
\eta
; the intersection

W\cap W^{\prime}=\partial W=\dot{E}(\eta)

is the total space of the unit sphere bundle of
\eta
.

Let
{\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}\in H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi))
be an arbitrary cohomological extension of the fibre. Denote by
\sigma:B\to\dot{E}(\xi)
the reflected section, i.e.
\sigma(b)=-s(b)
for
b\in B
. Then the class
{\mathfrak{u}}={\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime}-a
, where

a=\sigma^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}}^{\prime})\in H^{q-1}(B)
, is a cohomological extension of the fibre satisfying
\sigma^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}})=0
.
We show below that such
{\mathfrak{u}}
satisfies (
18
).

The equation
\sigma^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}})=0
implies
{\mathfrak{u}}|_{W^{\prime}}=0
and hence
{\mathfrak{u}}
can be lifted to a relative cohomology class

\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}\in H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi),W^{\prime})\simeq H^{q-1}(W,\partial W)\simeq H^{q-1}(W/\partial W).

The long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{q-2}(\dot{E}(\xi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}H^{q-2}(W^{\prime})\to H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi),W^{\prime})\to H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi))

shows that the lift
\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}
is unique. Clearly,
W/\partial W
is the Thom space of the bundle
\eta
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}\in H^{q-1}(W/\partial W)
is the Thom class, see §9 of
[
21
]
. By the definition the class

(19)

\displaystyle s^{\ast}(\tilde{\mathfrak{u}})={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\in H^{q-1}(B)

is the Euler class of the bundle
\eta
. Then in the group
H^{2(q-1)}(W,\partial W)
we have

\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}\cup\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}=(\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}|_{W})\cup\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}=(\xi|_{W})^{\ast}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))\cup\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}.

Viewing this as an equality in
H^{2(q-1)}(\dot{E}(\xi),W^{\prime})
and applying the restriction homomorphism

H^{2(q-1)}(\dot{E}(\xi),W^{\prime})\to H^{2(q-1)}(\dot{E}(\xi))
gives (
18
).
∎

For future reference we state the following Corollary which is proven by the arguments above.

Corollary 18
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
17
:

(A)

There exists a unique choice of the cohomological extension of the fibre
{\mathfrak{u}}\in H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi))
such that

(20)

\displaystyle s^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}})={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}},

where
s:B\to\dot{E}(\xi)
is the section determined by the orientation of the bundle
\epsilon
.

(B)

The homomorphism
s^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))\to H^{\ast}(B)
is surjective and its kernel is the principal ideal generated by the class
{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
.

(C)

The length of the longest nontrivial product of classes in the ideal

\ker[s^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))\to H^{\ast}(B)]

equals
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))+1
where
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))
denotes the largest integer
k
such that the power

{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)^{k}\in H^{\ast}(B)
is nonzero.

Proof.

(A) The left formula in (
20
) follows from (
19
). The uniqueness of such
{\mathfrak{u}}
is obvious; it follows from the arguments of the proof of Theorem
17
. The right formula in (
20
) is a part of the statement of Theorem
17
.

To prove (B) consider a class
a=u+v\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}
satisfying
s^{\ast}(a)=0
. Since
s^{\ast}(u)=u
,
s^{\ast}(v)=v
and

s^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}})={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
the equality
s^{\ast}(a)=0
gives
u=-v\cdot{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
and therefore
a=v\cdot({\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)).

Finally to prove (C) we note that the powers of the class
{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
are given by the formula

(21)

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))^{k}=(-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))^{k-1}\cdot({\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))

as follows by induction from (A).
We see that
k
-th power is nonzero for
k={\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))+1
and it vanishes for
k={\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))+2
. The remaining statements are obvious consequences of statements (A) and (B).
∎

Here is a similar statement with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
coefficients; note that we do not require the bundle
\xi
to be orientable:

Theorem 19
.

Let
\xi:E(\xi)\to B
be a rank
q-1
vector bundle such that the unit sphere bundle
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to B
admits a continuous section
s:B\to\dot{E}(\xi)
. Then:

(a)

there exists a unique cohomological extension of the fibre
{\mathfrak{u}}\in H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi);{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
such that

(22)

\displaystyle s^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}})=w_{q-1}(\xi)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}=w_{q-1}(\xi)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}.

Here
w_{q-1}(\xi)\in H^{q-1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
denotes the Stiefel - Whitney class of
\xi
; we identify
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
with its image under the injective homomorphism
\dot{\xi}^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi);{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

(b)

The cohomology algebra
H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi);{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the quotient of the polynomial extension
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})[{\mathfrak{u}}]
with respect to the ideal generated by
{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}+w_{q-1}(\xi)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}
.

(c)

The homomorphism
s^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi);{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is surjective and its kernel is the principal ideal generated by the class
{\mathfrak{u}}+w_{q-1}(\xi)

.

On the other hand, since
\dim\dot{E}=\dim B+q-1\leq 2(q-1)
we see that

1\leq{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))\leq 2
and therefore (
46
) implies that
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]
equals either
r
or
r+1
. The proof of the last statement is based on the fact that under the indicated assumptions
\dim\dot{E}<2(q-1)
and hence
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))=1
.
∎

Next we give an application of the sharp upper bound of Theorem
9
combined with Theorem
20
:

Theorem 30
.

Let
\xi:E\to B
be an oriented rank
q\geq 3
vector bundle.
Suppose that
B
is a simply connected finite CW-complex and its dimension
\dim B
is divisible by
q-1
and satisfies

(47)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))\leq\frac{\dim B}{q-1}.

Then

(48)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\leq r-1+\frac{\dim B}{q-1}.

Note that the upper bound (
48
) is by one better than (
5
).

Proof.

Theorem
9
part (B) is applicable. In our case
k=q-2
and
m=r+\frac{\dim B}{q-1}
. The assumption (
47
) can be written in the form
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))+r-1<m
,
which, by Theorem
20
, means that the cup-length of the kernel (
9
) is smaller than
m
.
Now Corollary
10
applies and gives

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]<m
, which is equivalent to (
48
).
∎

Next we consider the special case when the original bundle
\xi
admits a nonzero section:

Proposition 31
.

Assume that
\xi=\eta\oplus\epsilon
where
\eta:E(\eta)\to B
is an oriented rank
q-1
vector bundle and
\epsilon
is the
trivial oriented line bundle.
Then:
(a) if
q\geq 2
is even then

(49)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))={\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta));

(b) if
q\geq 3
is odd and
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}})
has no 2-torsion then

(50)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))=2\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

In particular, we see that for
q\geq 3
odd the height
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))
is always odd.

Proof.

Below all cohomology groups are understood with integer coefficients. We apply Theorem
17

to the bundle
\xi:E(\xi)\to B
to conclude that
H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))
is the quotient of the polynomial extension
H^{\ast}(B)[{\mathfrak{u}}]
with respect to the principal ideal generated by the class
{\mathfrak{u}}-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
. We shall identify the cohomology of the base
H^{\ast}(B)
with its image in
H^{\ast}(\dot{E}(\xi))
under the monomorphism
\dot{\xi}^{\ast}
.
The class
{\mathfrak{u}}
is a cohomological extension of the fibre, i.e. its restriction to each fibre is the fundamental class of the fibre.
By Leray - Hirsch theorem, the Euler class
{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})\in H^{q-1}(\dot{E}(\xi))
has a unique representation in the form

(51)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})=a+b\cdot{\mathfrak{u}},

where
a\in H^{q-1}(B)
and
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The restriction of the class
{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})
to each fibre of the bundle

\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B
equals
\chi(S^{q-1})
times the fundamental class of the fibre. Hence, we obtain that in (
51
)

b=0
, if
q
is even, and
b=2
if
q
is odd.

Let
s:B\to\dot{E}(\xi)
be the section determined by the trivial summand
\epsilon
. Geometrically it is obvious that

s^{\ast}(\ddot{\xi})=\dot{\eta}.

Therefore, using functoriality of the Euler class, we have
s^{\ast}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta).
On the other hand,
we know that
s^{\ast}({\mathfrak{u}})={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)
, see Corollary
18
. Applying
s^{\ast}
to both sides of equation (
51
) and noting that
s^{\ast}(a)=a
we find that

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta),&\mbox{if $q$ is even},\\
-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)+2\cdot{\mathfrak{u}},&\mbox{if $q$ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

From this, clearly (
49
) follows for
q
even.

Consider now the case when
q\geq 3
is odd. Using (
8
) and the equality
{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}
(see Corollary
18
), we find that

{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})^{2}={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)^{2}
and therefore we get

{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})^{2n}={\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)^{2n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})^{2n+1}=-{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)^{2n+1}+2{\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)^{2n}\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}

for any integer
n\geq 0
. Using our assumption about the absence of 2-torsion in integral cohomology of
B
,
we obtain from these equations that

{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta)),&\mbox{if}&{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))&\mbox{is odd},\\
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))+1,&\mbox{if}&{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))&\mbox{is even.}\end{array}\right.

This is equivalent to our claim (
50
).
∎

Example 32
.

Consider the canonical complex line bundle
\eta:E(\eta)\to{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}
over the complex projective space. Viewing
\eta
as a rank 2 real vector bundle we may apply Proposition
31
to the bundle

\xi=\eta\oplus\epsilon.

Here
q=3
is odd and
{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\eta))=n
. By Proposition
31
we obtain

{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi}))=2\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor+1.

Applying Corollary
27
we obtain

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}]\geq 2\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor+r.

On the other hand the inequality (
5
) gives

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}]\leq n+r.

We see that the lower and upper bounds agree if
n
is even.

If
n
is odd inequality (
47
) is satisfied and we can apply Theorem
30
. Inequality (
48
) then gives

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}]\leq n+r-1.

Thus, we obtain:

Corollary 33
.

For the vector bundle
\xi
of Example
32
one has

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}(\xi)\to{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n+r,&\mbox{if $n$ is even},\\
n+r-1,&\mbox{if $n$ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

Example 34
.

For comparison with the previous example consider the vector bundle

\xi_{\ell}=\eta\oplus\ell\cdot\epsilon=\eta\oplus\epsilon\oplus\epsilon\oplus\dots\oplus\epsilon

over
{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}
, where
\ell=0,1,2,\dots
. Here

{\mathbf{CP}}^{n}

The previous example corresponds to the case

q=2+\ell
.
If

.

9.
Lower bounds using Stiefel - Whitney characteristic classes

In this section we state an analogue of Theorem
20
in which instead of the Euler class feature the Stiefel - Whitney classes. Compared to Theorem
20
this result has two advantages: firstly, it involves characteristic classes of the original vector bundle
\xi:E\to B
and, secondly, it does not require the bundle
\xi
to be orientable. The case
r=2
appears in the paper
[
12
]
.

Theorem 35
.

Let
\xi:E\to B
be a locally trivial vector bundle of rank
q\geq 2
. Consider the associated sphere bundle
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B
, the space
\dot{E}^{r}_{B}
and the diagonal map
\Delta:\dot{E}\to\dot{E}^{r}_{B}
.
Then the cup-length of the kernel

(58)

\displaystyle\ker[\Delta^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(\dot{E}^{r}_{B};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to H^{\ast}(\dot{E};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})]

equals

{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\xi)|w_{q}(\xi))+r-1.

Here
w_{q-1}(\xi)\in H^{q-1}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
and
w_{q}(\xi)\in H^{q}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
denote the Stiefel - Whitney classes of
\xi
and the symbol

{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\xi)|w_{q}(\xi))
denotes the smallest integer
k\geq 0
such that the power
w_{q-1}(\xi)^{k+1}
lies in the ideal of the algebra
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
generated by the class
w_{q}(\xi)
. In particular, using Proposition
5
, one obtains the inequality

(59)

\displaystyle{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B]\geq{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\xi)|w_{q}(\xi))+r-1.

Proof.

Repeating the arguments of the proof of Theorem
20
with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
coefficients and noting that
the reduction mod 2 of the Euler class
{\mathfrak{e}}(\ddot{\xi})
equals the Stiefel - Whitney (SW) class
w_{q-1}(\ddot{\xi})
, one obtains that the
cup-length of the kernel (
58
) equals
{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\ddot{\xi}))+r-1
. Finally we show that

(60)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\ddot{\xi}))={\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\xi)|w_{q}(\xi)).

To prove (
60
) we note that the bundle
\dot{\xi}^{\ast}(\xi)
over
\dot{E}
induced by the map
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B
from
\xi
has the form

\alpha\oplus\epsilon
where
\epsilon
is the trivial line bundle and the fibre of
\alpha
over a point
e\in\dot{E}
is
e^{\perp}
, i.e. the space of vectors of
E
orthogonal to
e
. Clearly, one has
\ddot{\xi}=\dot{\alpha}
for the unit sphere bundles and using the standard properties of the SW-classes we obtain

(61)

\displaystyle w_{q-1}(\ddot{\xi})=\dot{\xi}^{\ast}(w_{q-1}(\xi)).

From the spectral sequence of fibration
\dot{\xi}:\dot{E}\to B
we see that the kernel of the homomorphism

\dot{\xi}^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to H^{\ast}(\dot{E};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the principal ideal generated by the class
w_{q}(\xi)\in H^{q}(\dot{E};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.
Thus, taking into account (
61
) we obtain that a power
w_{q-1}(\ddot{\xi})^{k}
vanishes if and only if the power

w_{q-1}(\xi)^{k}
lies in the ideal of
H^{\ast}(B;{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
generated by
w_{q}(\xi)
. This proves (
60
).
∎

Example 36
.

For an integer
\ell=1,2,\dots
consider the bundle
\xi_{\ell}=\ell\eta\oplus\epsilon
over
{{\mathbf{RP}}}^{n}
, where

\eta
is the canonical line bundle over
{\mathbf{RP}}^{n}
and
\epsilon
is the trivial line bundle. The rank of this bundle equals
q=\ell+1
and the total Stiefel - Whitney class is
(1+\alpha)^{\ell}
where
\alpha\in H^{1}({\mathbf{RP}}^{n};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is the generator. Thus we have

w_{q-1}(\xi_{\ell})=\alpha^{\ell}
and
w_{q}(\xi_{\ell})=0
. The relative height
{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\xi_{\ell})|w_{q}(\xi_{\ell}))
is the smallest
k\geq 0
such that
\ell(k+1)\geq n+1
, i.e.

{\mathfrak{h}}(w_{q-1}(\xi_{\ell})|w_{q}(\xi_{\ell}))=\left\lceil\frac{n+1-\ell}{\ell}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{\ell}\right\rceil-1.

Using (
59
) we obtain

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}_{\ell}:\dot{E}(\xi_{\ell})\to{\mathbf{RP}}^{n}]\geq\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{\ell}\right\rceil+r-2.

On the other hand, using (
5
) we get

{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}_{\ell}:\dot{E}(\xi_{\ell})\to{\mathbf{RP}}^{n}]\leq\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{\ell}\right\rceil+r-1.

These two inequalities determine the value
{\sf{TC}}_{r}[\dot{\xi}_{\ell}:\dot{E}(\xi_{\ell})\to{\mathbf{RP}}^{n}]
with indeterminacy 1.

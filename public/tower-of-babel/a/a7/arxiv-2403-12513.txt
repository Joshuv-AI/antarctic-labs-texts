Classifying Triebel–Lizorkin capacities in metric spaces
Juha Lehrbäck, Kaushik Mohanta, Antti V. Vähäkangas

Abstract
. We study non-local or fractional capacities in metric measure spaces. Our main goal is to clarify the relations between relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacities, potentional Triebel–Lizorkin capacities, and metric space variants of Riesz capacities. As an application of our results, we obtain a characterization of a Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity density condition, which is based on an earlier characterization of a Riesz capacity density condition in terms of Hausdorff contents.

1.
Introduction

In mathematical analysis, there are several possible approaches to the concept of

capacity of a set
, depending, for instance, on the
general setting and the intended applications. Nevertheless, it often turns out that
seemingly different definitions still lead to comparable or
otherwise closely related concepts.

In this work, we examine and compare four different
approaches to nonlocal or fractional capacities in a
metric space
X
equipped with a doubling measure
\mu
.
More precisely, when

B\subset X
is an open ball and
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
is a closed set,
we consider the following,
for parameters
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
0<\beta<1
, and
\Lambda\geq 2
:

•

the relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)
,

•

the potentional Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
,

•

the dual characterization of
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
in terms of measures supported on
E
,

•

the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity
R_{\beta,p}(E)
.

The main purpose of this paper is to establish and clarify the relations between these
concepts. For instance, we examine when these capacities are comparable and
show that often the parameter
q
plays no essential role.
The last part of the paper, where
we characterize a capacity density condition associated to Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacities,
can be viewed as a continuation of
[
6
,
7
]
.

The Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin function spaces
\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and the corresponding relative capacities
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}

are defined using fractional Hajłasz gradients.
In Section
3
we introduce these gradients, following
[
24
]
,
and recall in Lemma
3.2
a Poincaré type inequality for them.
Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and relative capacities
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
are
defined in Section
4
, where we also
prove a lower bound estimate for these capacities in terms of suitable Hausdorff contents;
see Theorem
4.6
.

On the other hand,
following
[
2
,
3
]
,
the Triebel–Lizorkin potentional capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
is
defined in Section
6
using the potential operator
\Hop_{\beta}
,

\Hop_{\beta}f(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,

for a sequence
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
of nonnegative Borel functions in
X
.
Here
n_{0}
is the smallest integer
such that
2^{-n_{0}}\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
, with the interpretation that
n_{0}=-\infty
if
X
is unbounded.

We show that, under suitable assumptions on the space
X

and the parameters

p
,
q
,
\beta
and
\Lambda
,
the capacities

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)
and
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
are comparable
for all compact sets
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
.
In particular, besides the doubling condition (see (
3
)),
we need to assume that the measure
\mu
is also reverse
doubling (see (
4
) and (
5
)),
as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
,
0<\beta<1
and
\Lambda\geq 41
.
Assume that the measure
\mu
is doubling and reverse doubling,
and satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
Let
B=B(x,r)\subset X
be a ball
with
x\in X
and
0<r<\frac{1}{80}\operatorname{diam}(X)

and let
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}

Then

(1)

C^{-1}\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)\leq\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)\leq C\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)\,,

where the constant
C\geq 1
is independent of
B
and
E
.

The two inequalities in (
1
)
hold separately under slightly different assumptions,
see Theorems
6.5
and
6.6
for
precise formulations.
Also recall, as a basic example, that in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(equipped with the
Euclidean distance) the Lebesgue measure is both doubling and reverse doubling and
satisfies condition (
5
) with
\sigma=n
.

As a technical tool for the proof of the second inequality in (
1
),
we introduce a new discrete potential operator
\Lop_{\beta}
,
which majorizes the operator
\Hop_{\beta}
; see Lemma
6.3
.
The definition of
\Lop_{\beta}
(Definition

)
involves a partition of unity and
is inspired by the so-called discrete convolutions, see
e.g.
[

,
23
]
.
Since the functions in the partition of unity are
Lipschitz-continuous, the operator
\Lop_{\beta}
can be seen as a
smooth version of
\Hop_{\beta}
, whose definition
uses noncontinuous characteristic functions of balls.
Boundedness properties of
\Lop_{\beta}

are studied in Section
5
,
and in particular we obtain for
\Lop_{\beta}
a mixed norm estimate in
Lemma
5.2
and a
Sobolev type inequality in Lemma
5.3
.

In Section
7
, we prove a dual characterization of the
Triebel–Lizorkin potentional capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)

for compact sets
E\subset X
.
As in the Euclidean case in
[
3
]
,
this is based on the use of
(nonnegative) Radon measures
\nu
supported on
E
.
In our setting, the sequence

\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu(x)=\left(2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\right)_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\,,\qquad x\in X\,,

plays an important role.
The main tool in the proof of the dual characterization (Theorem
7.3
) is a Minimax theorem, see
Lemma
7.4
.

In Section
8
we prove a version of the
Muckenhoupt–Wheeden theorem (Theorem
8.3
), which gives an estimate for the
norm of
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu

in terms of the fractional maximal function

M_{\beta}\nu(x)=\sup_{0<r\leq 2^{-n_{0}}}r^{\beta}\frac{\nu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

Such results originate in
[
31
]
, and a very general metric space variant
of the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden theorem can be found in
[
33
]
.
Nevertheless, instead of relying on the more abstract machinery from
[
33
]
, we present an alternative
self-contained proof that is suited for our purposes.

The dual characterization of
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}

and the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden theorem are crucial ingredients in the proof of Theorem
9.3
,
which shows that for compact sets the Triebel–Lizorkin capacities
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
are in fact
independent of the
q
-parameter, again under a suitable reverse doubling assumption.
Consequently, by Theorem
1.1
the same independence holds for the relative capacity

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
, as well.

Finally, in Sections
10
and
11

we compare the Triebel–Lizorkin capacities and the Riesz capacities
R_{\beta,p}(E)
,
which are defined in terms of a metric space variant of the classical Riesz potential,

(2)

I_{\beta}f(x)=\int_{X\setminus\{x\}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,

for Borel functions
f\geq 0
in
X
.
See Definitions
10.1
and
10.2
for more details.

In Theorem
10.4
we show that

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\infty}
and
R_{\beta,p}
are comparable.
The proof of this theorem follows by rather straightforward
applications of the doubling and
quantitative reverse doubling conditions, see Lemma
10.3
.
The
q
-independence of the
potentional Triebel–Lizorkin capacities yields
for compact sets the same comparability
also for
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
,
for every
1<q\leq\infty
,
and thus for the relative capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
as well, by
Theorem
1.1
.
In Theorem
10.5
we present an alternative direct proof,
again based on the dual characterization, for the fact that
for compact sets

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
is bounded from above by
R_{\beta,p}
, for all

1<q\leq\infty
.

The main application of the above results is given in Section
11
, where we
characterize, for
0<\beta<1
, a Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity density condition
in complete geodesic metric spaces.
The proof of the characterization is based on the
following facts:

(1)

The Riesz capacity
R_{\beta,p}
dominates the corresponding
Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
, for every
1<q\leq\infty
;
see Corollary
10.6
.

(2)

The Riesz capacity density condition is equivalent to
a Hausdorff content density condition. This is a deep and highly nontrivial result
from
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
and
[
7
, Theorem 9.5]
,
and in particular implies a self-improvement result for the Riesz capacity density condition.
Here the additional assumptions that the space
X
is complete and geodesic are needed.

(3)

The Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
can be estimated from below by suitable
Hausdorff contents, by Theorem
4.6
.

In addition to these main ingredients, we also need estimates for the
capacities and contents of balls. For Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacities
such estimates are obtained in Theorem
4.3
.

The above characterization leads to a self-improvement result (Corollary
11.5
) for the
Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity density condition, which is,
to the best of our knowledge, new even in the context of
Euclidean spaces.
Self-improvement of the capacity density condition for the Riesz capacity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

was originally proven by Lewis
[
26
]
. See also
[
6
,
7
]

for corresponding results for other capacities in metric spaces.

3.
Fractional Hajłasz gradient

We define fractional Hajłasz gradients in open sets as in
[
13
]
, by adapting
[
24
, Definition 1.1]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
0<\beta\leq 1
and let
u\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable function on an open set
\Omega\subset X
. A sequence
g=(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of nonnegative measurable functions is called a fractional
\beta
-Hajłasz gradient of
u
, if there exists
a set
E\subset\Omega
such that
\mu(E)=0
and for all

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x,y\in\Omega\setminus E
satisfying
2^{-k-1}\leq d(x,y)<2^{-k}
, we have

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y))\,.

We denote by
\mathbb{D}^{\beta}_{\Omega}(u)
the collection of all fractional

\beta
-Hajłasz gradients of
u
.

We need the following
metric space variant of the Poincaré type inequality
[
24
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<\beta<1
and assume that
\mu
satisfies the
reverse doubling condition (
4
) with a constant
0<c_{R}<1
.
Let
x_{0}\in X
, let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
2^{-n+3}<\operatorname{diam}(X)
, and assume
that
u\in L^{1}(B(x_{0},2^{-n+2}))
and

(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\mathbb{D}_{B(x_{0},2^{-n+2})}^{\beta}(u)
.
Then

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x_{0},2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{0},2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{0},2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{0},2^{-n})}}|u(x)-u_{B(x_{0},2^{-n})}|\,d\mu(x)\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{R})2^{-n\beta}\sum_{k=n-3}^{n}\;\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x_{0},2^{-n+2})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{0},2^{-n+2})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{0},2^{-n+2})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{0},2^{-n+2})}}g_{k}(x)\,d\mu(x)\,.

Proof.

4.
Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and relative capacity

Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces were introduced in
[
24
]
. The associated global capacities
first appeared in
[
15
]
and
they have been studied, for instance, in
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
and
[

.
See also
[
3
]
and the references therein for
the theory of Triebel–Lizorkin capacities in the Euclidean case.
In this section, we first recall Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
on open sets and then we define the corresponding relative capacities that seem to be novel in this generality.

Fix
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and an open set
\Omega\subset X
. For a sequence
g=(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of measurable
functions in
\Omega
, we define the mixed norm

\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;\,l^{q})}=\bigl\|\|g\|_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}\bigr\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,,

where

\|g\|_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}=\begin{cases}\Bigl(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|g_{k}|^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q},&\quad\text{ for }1\leq q<\infty,\\
\;\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|g_{k}|,&\quad\text{ for }q=\infty.\end{cases}

We use the following
Fefferman–Stein vector-valued maximal function inequality; we refer to
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
for a proof.
If
1<p<\infty
and
1<q\leq\infty
, then there
exists a positive constant
C>0
such that for all sequences of measurable
functions
(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
in
X
we have

(11)

\big\lVert(M^{*}g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\leq C\lVert(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\,,

where
M^{*}g_{k}
is the noncentered maximal function of
g_{k}
for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
0<\beta<1
,
and let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set.
The homogeneous Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin space
\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)

is the seminormed space of all measurable functions
u\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}=\inf_{g\in\mathbb{D}^{\beta}_{\Omega}(u)}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;l^{q})}<\infty\,.

Notice that
\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,\tau}(\Omega)}

whenever
1\leq\tau\leq q\leq\infty
.
We remark that
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}
is a seminorm
but not a norm since
\lvert u\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Omega)}=0

whenever
u
is a constant function.

The following definition of a relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}

is motivated by
[
9

; see also
[
28
, Definition 7.1]
,
[
10
]
and

[
29
, §11]
.

.

Let

\displaystyle<1-c_{R}\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{n}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{n}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{n}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{n}}}\lvert\varphi(y)-\varphi_{B_{n}}\rvert\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{R})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(m+n)\beta}\sum_{k=n+m-3}^{n+m}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,2^{-(m+n)+2})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-(m+n)+2})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-(m+n)+2})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-(m+n)+2})}}g_{k}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{R})\sum_{n=m-2}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta}\;\sum_{k=n-1}^{n+2}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}g_{k}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{R})\sum_{n=m-2}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta}\;\sum_{k=n-1}^{n+2}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}g_{k}(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{R})\sum_{n=m-2}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta}\;\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(y)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/p}\,.

Let
\delta=\beta(p-\eta)/p>0
. Then

\displaystyle 2^{(m-2)\delta}\sum_{n=m-2}^{\infty}2^{-n\delta}=C(\beta,p,\eta,c_{R})(1-c_{R})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\beta,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{\mu})\sum_{n=m-2}^{\infty}2^{-n\beta}\;\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,2^{-n})}}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(y)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/p}\,.

In particular, by comparing the above sums it follows that there exists
n\geq m-2
,
depending on
x
, such that

\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))(2^{-n})^{-\beta\eta}\leq C(\beta,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{\mu})2^{-\beta m(p-\eta)}\;\int_{B(x,2^{-n})}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(y)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y).

Write
r_{x}=2^{-n}
and

B_{x}=B(x,r_{x})=B(x,2^{-n})
. Then the previous estimate gives

\mu(B_{x})r_{x}^{-\beta\eta}\leq C(\beta,p,\eta,c_{R},c_{\mu})r^{\beta(p-\eta)}\;\int_{B_{x}}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(y)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y).

By the
5r
-covering lemma
[
4
, Lemma 1.7]
, we obtain points
x_{\ell}\in E
,

\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that the balls
B_{x_{\ell}}\subset\Lambda B

with radii
r_{x_{\ell}}\leq\Lambda r
are
pairwise disjoint and

E\subset\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\infty}5B_{x_{\ell}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\mu,\beta\eta}_{5\Lambda r}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mu(5B_{x_{\ell}})(5r_{x_{\ell}})^{-\beta\eta}\leq Cr^{\beta(p-\eta)}\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{x_{\ell}}}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(y)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\beta(p-\eta)}\;\int_{\Lambda B}\left(\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(y)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

The desired inequality follows by taking infimum over all functions

g
and
\varphi
as above.
∎

5.
Discrete potential operator

In this section we introduce and study a discrete potential operator
\Lop_{\beta}
.
The main importance of this operator is that it majorizes the potential
\Hop_{\beta}

that is used in the next section to define the Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
;
see Definitions
6.1
and
6.2
.
Moreover, due to the Lipschitz-continuity of the bump functions
\psi_{n,i}

that are used in the definition of
\Lop_{\beta}
, this operator has better
smoothing properties than
\Hop_{\beta}
whose definition is based on
characteristic functions of balls. In particular,
Lemma
5.2
seem not to be available for
\Hop_{\beta}
.

We begin with a construction of a partition of unity, following the
ideas from
[
23
]
and
[
1
]
.
Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
n\geq n_{0}
;
recall that
2^{-n_{0}}
is essentially
\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
By a maximal packing argument
[
16
, p. 101]

and using the separability of
X
, we may select a countable number of points
x_{n,i}\in X
,
i\in P_{n}
,
such that the balls
B(x_{n,i},2^{-n-1})
,
i\in P_{n}
, are pairwise disjoint, while

(17)

X=\bigcup_{i\in P_{n}}B(x_{n,i},2^{-n}).

Since
X
is equipped with a doubling measure
\mu
, the
balls
B(x_{n,i},6\cdot 2^{-n})
have a finite overlapping property,
that is, there exists a constant
N=C(c_{\mu})
such that

(18)

\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{n,i},6\cdot 2^{-n})}\leq N.

In addition, there are constants
\kappa=C(c_{\mu})
and
L=C(c_{\mu})
and
a family of functions
\psi_{n,i}
,
i\in P_{n}
,
with the following properties:

0\leq\psi_{n,i}\leq 1
,

\psi_{n,i}=0
in
X\setminus B(x_{n,i},6\cdot 2^{-n})
,

\psi_{n,i}\geq\kappa
in
B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})
,
\psi_{n,i}
is
2^{n}L
-Lipschitz in
X
, and

\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)=1

for every
x\in X
.

The family
\psi_{n,i}
,
i\in P_{n}
, is called a partition of unity subordinate to the cover
B(x_{n,i},2^{-n})
.
We keep these families, for
n\geq n_{0}
, fixed throughout the rest of the paper, starting from the
following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\beta>0
and let
f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative Borel
functions in
X
.
The discrete potential
\Lop_{\beta}f
is defined by

\Lop_{\beta}f(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.18884ptB(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

The closely related discrete maximal operator is studied for instance in
[
1
,
14
]
.
We also mention that a median type approach to discrete potential operators appears in
[
15
]
,
where approximation properties of
Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin functions are studied in the full range
0<p,q<\infty
.

The following lemma allows us to control the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin seminorm
of the discrete potential
\Lop_{\beta}f
in terms of a mixed norm of
f
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
, and
0<\beta<1
,
and assume that
f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
is a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in
X
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\mu},\beta,p,q)
such that

\lvert\Lop_{\beta}f\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(X)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\,.

Proof.

We may clearly assume that
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}<\infty
.
If
X
is bounded, then we extend
f
as a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
by
setting
f_{n}=0
if
n<n_{0}
.
Define
g=(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where

g_{k}(x)=Nc_{\mu}2^{\beta}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{\beta(k-n)}M^{*}f_{n}(x)+LNc_{\mu}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{k-1}2^{(\beta-1)(k-n)}M^{*}f_{n}(x)

for every
x\in X
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and
N=C(c_{\mu})
and
L=C(c_{\mu})
are the constants
from the definition of the partitions of unity
\psi_{n,i}
.
We aim to show
that
g\in\mathbb{D}_{X}^{\beta}(\Lop_{\beta}f)
.
For this purpose we fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x,y\in X
such that
2^{-k-1}\leq d(x,y)<2^{-k}
.
Observe that
k\geq n_{0}
.

Write
B_{n,i}=B(x_{n,i},2^{-n})
. From the definition of
\Lop_{\beta}
we obtain

(19)

\lvert\Lop_{\beta}f(x)-\Lop_{\beta}f(y)\rvert\leq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\lvert\psi_{n,i}(x)-\psi_{n,i}(y)\rvert\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.18884pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)\,.

We split the outer summation in two parts. First,

(20)

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\lvert\psi_{n,i}(x)-\psi_{n,i}(y)\rvert\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)\\
&\qquad\leq 2^{-k\beta}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{\beta(k-n)}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}(\psi_{n,i}(x)+\psi_{n,i}(y))\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)\\
&\qquad\leq c_{\mu}2^{\beta}d(x,y)^{\beta}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{\beta(k-n)}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(x)+\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(y)\bigr)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt6B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{6B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{6B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{6B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)\\
&\qquad\leq c_{\mu}2^{\beta}d(x,y)^{\beta}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{\beta(k-n)}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(x)M^{*}f_{n}(x)+\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(y)M^{*}f_{n}(y)\bigr)\\
&\qquad\leq Nc_{\mu}2^{\beta}d(x,y)^{\beta}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{\beta(k-n)}(M^{*}f_{n}(x)+M^{*}f_{n}(y))\,.\end{split}

Next we assume that
n_{0}\leq n\leq k-1
and
i\in P_{n}
.
If
\psi_{n,i}(x)\not=0
, then we have

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{n,i}(x)-\psi_{n,i}(y)\rvert\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{n}Ld(x,y)\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(x)c_{\mu}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt6B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{6B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{6B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{6B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{n}Lc_{\mu}d(x,y)^{\beta}2^{-k(1-\beta)}\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(x)M^{*}f_{n}(x)\,.

On the other hand, if
\psi_{n,i}(y)\not=0
, then a similar computation shows that

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{n,i}(x)-\psi_{n,i}(y)\rvert\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)\leq 2^{n}Lc_{\mu}d(x,y)^{\beta}2^{-k(1-\beta)}\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(y)M^{*}f_{n}(y)\,.

Thus in any case we have

(21)

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k-1}2^{-\beta n}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\lvert\psi_{n,i}(x)-\psi_{n,i}(y)\rvert\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(z)\,d\mu(z)\\
&\quad\leq Lc_{\mu}d(x,y)^{\beta}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k-1}2^{-\beta n+n-k(1-\beta)}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}(\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(x)M^{*}f_{n}(x)+\mathbf{1}_{6B_{n,i}}(y)M^{*}f_{n}(y))\\
&\quad\leq LNc_{\mu}d(x,y)^{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{k-1}2^{(\beta-1)(k-n)}(M^{*}f_{n}(x)+M^{*}f_{n}(y))\,.\end{split}

By combining inequalities (
19
), (
20
) and (
21
), it follows that

\lvert\Lop_{\beta}f(x)-\Lop_{\beta}f(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y))\,,

and therefore

g\in\mathbb{D}_{X}^{\beta}(\Lop_{\beta}f)
.

Next, define
a=(a_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where

a_{k}=Nc_{\mu}2^{\beta}\cdot 2^{\beta k}
if
k\leq 0
and

a_{k}=LNc_{\mu}2^{(\beta-1)k}
if
k>0
.
Observe that
(g_{k}(x))_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}=a\star(M^{*}f_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where

\star
designates the discrete convolution

\bigl(a\star(M^{*}f_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigr)_{k}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k-n}M^{*}f_{n}(x)\,,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

Young’s convolution inequality
[
18
, Corollary 20.14]
implies for every
x\in X
that

\displaystyle\big\lVert(g_{k}(x))_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}

\displaystyle=\big\lVert a\star(M^{*}f_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert a\rVert_{l^{1}({\mathbb{Z}})}\big\lVert(M^{*}f_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}

\displaystyle\leq C(L,N,c_{\mu},\beta)\big\lVert(M^{*}f_{n}(x))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}})}\,.

Hence, we see that

\lvert\Lop_{\beta}f\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(X)}\leq\lVert(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}))}\leq C(L,N,c_{\mu},\beta)\big\lVert(M^{*}f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}))}\,.

By the Fefferman–Stein vector-valued maximal function inequality (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big\lVert(M^{*}f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}))}

\displaystyle=\Biggl(\int_{X}\Biggl(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(M^{*}f_{n}(x)\right)^{q}\Biggr)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\Biggl)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},p,q)\Biggl(\int_{X}\Biggl(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{n}(x)^{q}\Biggl)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\Biggl)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=C(c_{\mu},p,q)\lVert(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}))}

(with the usual modification if
q=\infty
).
This concludes the proof, since the constants
L
and
N
only depend on
c_{\mu}
.
∎

The following lemma gives for the discrete potential operator
\Lop_{\beta}
a local embedding,
which can be viewed as a Sobolev type inequality.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

,
1<q\leq\infty
, and
\beta>0
.
Assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (

)

, and

be a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in

.
Then there exists

such that

\left(\int_{B(x_{0},r)}(\Lop_{\beta}f(x))^{p}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}\leq Cr^{\beta}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}

for every

and

.

Proof.

Fix

and

.
If

for every

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=2^{-\beta k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{-\beta(n-k)}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\begin{split}\int_{B(x_{0},r)}(F(x))^{p}\,d\mu(x)&\leq C(p,q,\beta,c_{\mu},N)2^{-\beta pk}\int_{X}\left(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\left(M^{*}f_{n}(x)\right)^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\\

On the other hand, from the quantitative reverse doubling condition (

.

\displaystyle\leq c_{\sigma}^{1/p}2^{-k\beta}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k-1}2^{(\sigma/p-\beta)(n-k)}\left(\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x_{0},2^{-k}))}\right)^{1/p}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

and using

\begin{split}G(x)&\leq C(c_{\sigma},p)2^{-k\beta}\left(\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k-1}2^{(\sigma/p-\beta)(n-k)}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\\

\begin{split}&\left(\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{3B_{n,i}}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)^{p}\\

6.
Capacity with respect to a potential

Next we define the potential
\Hop_{\beta}
and the
associated Triebel–Lizorkin capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
.
The main goal in this section is to show that the
capacities
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
are equivalent,
as was stated in Theorem
1.1
in the introduction.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows by combining
Theorems
6.5
and
6.6
that are
presented at the end of this section.

The discrete potential operator
\Lop_{\beta}
serves as an important technical tool in the proof of
Theorem
6.5
. More precisely, in Lemmas
6.3
and
6.4
we connect

\Lop_{\beta}
with the potential
\Hop_{\beta}
and the capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
, respectively,
and these two elements are then brought together in the proof of Theorem
6.5
.
On the other hand, Theorem
6.6
follows by a much more
direct argument.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\beta>0
and let
f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in
X
. The potential
\Hop_{\beta}f
is defined by

\Hop_{\beta}f(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

We use potentials
\Hop_{\beta}
to define the capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
,
by adapting the treatment in
[
3
, Section 4.4]
to the metric space setting.
This approach is a Triebel–Lizorkin variant of the so-called Meyer’s theory of
L^{p}
-capacities,
that was initially developed in
[
30
]
.
See also
[
35
, Section 3.2]
for a clear presentation of the
L^{p}
-theory
in (weighted) Euclidean spaces.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
\beta>0
. The
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity of a set
E\subset X
is defined by

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)=\inf\bigl\{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}^{p}\,:\,f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\geq 0\text{ and }\Hop_{\beta}f(x)\geq 1\text{ for all }x\in E\bigr\}\,,

where
(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\geq 0
in the infimum means that

(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}

is a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in
X
.

Observe that if
F\subset E\subset X
and
1\leq\tau\leq q\leq\infty
,
then

(26)

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(F)\leq\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)\leq\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\tau}(E).

We have the following pointwise comparison between
the potentials
\Hop_{\beta}f
and
\Lop_{\beta}f
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\beta>0
and let
f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in
X
. Then there

for every

.

, let

be such that

, and let

\psi_{n,i}
,

, be as in the definition
of
\Lop_{\beta}f
, see Definition
5.1
.
By the covering property (
17
),
there exists
j\in P_{n}
such that
x\in B(x_{n,j},2^{-n})
.
Then

.

\displaystyle 2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{3}2^{-\beta n}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-1}c_{\mu}^{3}2^{-\beta n}\psi_{n,j}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,j},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-1}c_{\mu}^{3}2^{-\beta n}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,

where

is as in the definition of the functions

.
By summing over all

, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-1}c_{\mu}^{3}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\sum_{i\in P_{n}}\psi_{n,i}(x)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x_{n,i},3\cdot 2^{-n})}}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)=\kappa^{-1}c_{\mu}^{3}\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\,,

and this concludes the proof.
∎

Next we connect the relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity to
the
\Lop_{\beta}
potentials.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
,
0<\beta<1
, and
\Lambda\geq 2
.
Assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
. Let
B\subset X
be a ball, let
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
be a compact set, and let

f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
is sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in
X
such
that
\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in E
. Then
there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\sigma},p,q,\sigma,\beta,c_{\mu},\Lambda)>0
such that

(27)

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}^{p}\,.

Proof.

Clearly, we may assume that
the right-hand side of (
27
) is finite.
First we consider the case where
\Lop_{\beta}f
is continuous.
We truncate
\Lop_{\beta}f
and then use the fractional type Leibniz rule, as follows.
Write
B=B(x_{0},r)\subset X
, with
x_{0}\in X
and
r>0
, and
let

\eta(x)=\max\biggl\{0,1-\frac{\operatorname{dist}(x,B)}{r}\biggr\}

for every
x\in X
.
Then

\lvert\eta(x)-\eta(y)\rvert\leq\frac{d(x,y)}{r},\qquad\text{for every }x,y\in X\,,

0\leq\eta\leq 1
in
X
,
\eta=1
in the closure
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
, and
\eta=0
in
X\setminus 2B
. The function

\varphi=\eta\Lop_{\beta}f
is continuous in
X
. Since
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
and
\Lop_{\beta}f\geq 1
in
E
, we have

\varphi\geq 1
in
E
, and clearly
\varphi=0
in
X\setminus 2B
.
Let
g=(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\mathbb{D}^{\beta}_{X}(\Lop_{\beta}f)
.
Lemma
3.3
implies that
(\rho_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\mathbb{D}_{X}^{\beta}(\varphi)
, where

\rho_{k}=\big(g_{k}\|\eta\|_{\infty}+2^{k(\beta-1)}r^{-1}\Lop_{\beta}f\big)\mathbf{1}_{\{x\in X\,:\,\eta(x)\not=0\}}\,,\quad\text{ for every }k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

Let
m
be the smallest integer
such that
2^{-m-1}\leq 2\Lambda r
.
Observe that
(\mathbf{1}_{k\geq m}\rho_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\mathbb{D}_{\Lambda B}^{\beta}(\varphi)
.

By Definition
4.2
of the capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}
,
we have

\begin{split}&\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)\leq\lvert\varphi\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(\Lambda B)}^{p}\leq\int_{{\Lambda B}}\left(\sum_{k\geq m}\rho_{k}(x))^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\quad\leq C(p)\int_{2B}\left(\sum_{k\geq m}g_{k}(x))^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)+C(p)\int_{2B}\left(\sum_{k\geq m}\left(2^{k(\beta-1)}r^{-1}\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\right)^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\quad\leq C(p)\int_{X}\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(x))^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)+C(p)r^{-p}\left(\sum_{k\geq m}2^{kq(\beta-1)}\right)^{p/q}\int_{2B}\left(\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\quad\leq C(p)\int_{X}\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(x))^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)+C(p,q,\beta)r^{-p}2^{mp(\beta-1)}\int_{2B}\left(\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\\
&\quad\leq C(p)\int_{X}\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{k}(x))^{q}\right)^{p/q}\,d\mu(x)+C(p,q,\beta,\Lambda)r^{-\beta p}\int_{2B}\left(\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.\end{split}

By taking infimum over all
g
as above, we obtain

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)\leq C(p)\lvert\Lop_{\beta}f\rvert_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}(X)}^{p}+C(p,q,\beta,\Lambda)r^{-\beta p}\int_{2B}\left(\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\right)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,,

and the claim in the present case follows from Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
.

Next we consider the case where the function
\Lop_{\beta}f
is not continuous.
For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we let
h_{k}=(h_{k,n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
, where

h_{k,n}=\mathbf{1}_{\lvert n\rvert\leq k}f_{n}
for every
n\geq n_{0}
.
Then
(\Lop_{\beta}h_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is an increasing sequence
of continuous real-valued functions in
X
that
converges to
\Lop_{\beta}f
pointwise in
X
.
We consider the open sets

E_{k}=\left\{x\in X\,:\,\Lop_{\beta}h_{k}(x)>\frac{1}{2}\right\}\,,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,.

Since
\Lop_{\beta}f(x)\geq 1
for every
x\in E
, we see that

E\subset\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{k}
. By the compactness of
E
, there
exists a finite subcover, and since
E_{n}\subset E_{n+1}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
E\subset E_{k}
.
Thus we have
\Lop_{\beta}(2h_{k})>1
in
E
, and
by the first part of the proof

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)\leq C\lVert 2h_{k}\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}^{p}\leq 2^{p}C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}^{p}\,.

This shows that inequality (
27
) holds also in the non-continuous case,
and the proof is complete.
∎

).

Let

,

7.
Dual characterization of capacity

We now turn to the dual characterization of the capacity
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
given in terms of measures,
see Theorem
7.3
below.
In Euclidean spaces, this type of results have a long history, see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
30
]

and the references therein. We prove the dual characterization
by adapting the proof of equality (4.4.7) in
[
3
]
to the setting of metric measure spaces.
A fundamental tool in the proof is the Minimax theorem
[
3
, Theorem 2.4.1]
.
The somewhat technical task of verifying the assumptions of the Minimax theorem
in our setting is carried out separately in Lemma
7.4
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\beta>0
and let

\nu
be a nonnegative Borel measure in
X
. We define

(29)

\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu(x)=\left(2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\right)_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\,,\qquad x\in X.

Of particular interest to us are the nonnegative Radon measures
on a compact set
E\subset X
.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
E\subset X
be a compact set.
We say that
\nu
is a nonnegative Radon measure on
E
, if

\nu
is a nonnegative Borel measure on
E
that satisfies the following properties:

•

\nu(E)<\infty
.

•

For every Borel set
F
in
E
, we have

\nu(F)=\inf\{\nu(U)\,:\,F\subset U\text{ and }U\text{ is open in }E\}\,.

•

For every Borel set
F
in
E
, we have

\nu(F)=\sup\{\nu(K)\,:\,K\subset F\text{ and }K\text{ is compact in }E\}\,.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
the set of all nonnegative Radon measures on
E
.
If
f
is a Borel function on
X
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
, we
define

\int_{X}f\,d\nu=\int_{E}f|_{E}\,d\nu\,.

We also define
\nu(A)=\nu(A\cap E)
if
A\subset X
is a Borel set, and this
extends
\nu
as a nonnegative Borel measure in
X
.

We are ready to state and prove the dual characterization of the capacity.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
\beta>0
,
and let
E\subset X
be a compact set.
Then

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{1/p}=\sup\Biggl\{\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\Biggr\}\,.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
, let
f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions,
and write

\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)=\int_{X}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu(x)\,.

Using Definition
6.1
and Fubini’s theorem, we have

(30)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)&=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\nu(x)\\
&=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{f_{n}(y)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)\,d\nu(x)\,d\mu(y)\\
&=\int_{X}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(y,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}\,f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\\
&=\int_{X}\langle\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu(y),f(y)\rangle\,d\mu(y)\,.\end{split}

Now, assume that

\Hop_{\beta}f(x)\geq 1
for all
x\in E
.
Then, by using also
Hölder’s inequality twice, we get

\nu(E)\leq\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)\leq\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\,.

By taking infimum over all sequences
f
with
\Hop_{\beta}f\geq 1
in
E
, we thus obtain

\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\leq\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{1/p}\,.

Taking supremum over all
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
gives

\sup\Biggl\{\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\Biggr\}\leq\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{1/p}\,.

For the converse inequality, we will use the Minimax theorem
[
3
, Theorem 2.4.1]
; we refer to Lemma
7.4
for further details. Define

Z=\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\,:\,\nu(E)=1\}

and

W=\big\{f\in L^{p}(X;l^{q})\,:\,f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\geq 0\text{ and }\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\leq 1\big\}\,.

Observe from
[
11
, Lemma 2.3]
and (
30
) that for
\nu\in Z
we have

\frac{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}{\nu(E)}=\sup_{f\in W}\int_{X}\langle\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu(y),f(y)\rangle\,d\mu(y)=\sup_{f\in W}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)\,.

By taking infimum over all
\nu\in Z
, we get

(31)

\inf_{\nu\in Z}\frac{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}{\nu(E)}=\inf_{\nu\in Z}\sup_{f\in W}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)\,.

On the other hand, since the Dirac delta measure
\delta_{x}
belongs to
Z
for any
x\in E
, we have

\inf_{\nu\in Z}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)=\inf_{\nu\in Z}\int_{X}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu(x)\leq\inf_{x\in E}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)\leq\frac{\inf_{x\in E}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}}

for every
f\in W
.
By scaling and Definition
6.2
, it follows that

(32)

\begin{split}\sup_{f\in W}\inf_{\nu\in Z}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)&\leq\sup_{f\in W}\frac{\inf_{x\in E}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}}=\sup_{0\leq f\in L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\frac{\inf_{x\in E}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)}{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}}\\
&=\sup_{0\leq f\in L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\Biggl(\left\lVert\frac{f}{\inf_{x\in E}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}^{p}\Biggr)^{-1/p}\\
&=\Biggl(\inf_{0\leq f\in L^{p}(X;l^{q})}\left\lVert\frac{f}{\inf_{x\in E}\Hop_{\beta}f(x)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{q})}^{p}\Biggr)^{-1/p}\leq\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{-1/p}\,.\end{split}

By (
31
), the Minimax theorem in the following Lemma
7.4
, and (
32
) we obtain

\displaystyle\inf_{\nu\in Z}\frac{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}{\nu(E)}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\nu\in Z}\sup_{f\in W}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)=\sup_{f\in W}\inf_{\nu\in Z}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in W}\inf_{\nu\in Z}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)\leq\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{-1/p}\,.

Hence

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{1/p}\leq\sup_{\nu\in Z}\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\leq\sup\Biggl\{\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\Biggr\}\,,

as claimed.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
7.3
,
we still need to verify that the Minimax theorem is valid in our setting.
The underlying ideas are well known, but we present the details for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 7.4
.

Under the notation of the proof of Theorem
7.3
, we have

\inf_{\nu\in Z}\sup_{f\in W}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)=\sup_{f\in W}\inf_{\nu\in Z}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)\,.

Proof.

Since
E
is compact, the Riesz representation
theorem
[
34
, Theorem 6.19]
implies that every
T\in(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}
is represented by a unique regular Borel measure

\nu
, in the sense that

T(g)=\int_{E}g\,d\nu
for every
f\in C(E)
.
The operator norm
\lVert\nu\rVert
of
\nu
is the total variation of
\nu
.
Hence, we can regard
Z
as a subset of the dual
(C(E),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})^{*}
, and endow
Z
with the relative weak* topology.

We will apply the Minimax theorem
[
3
, Theorem 2.4.1]
to the
functional
\mathcal{E}_{\beta}
defined in
Z\times W
.
The sets
Z
and
W
are convex and
\mathcal{E}_{\beta}

is convex in
Z
and concave in
W
, see
[
3
, p. 30]
.
Hence, we need to show that

(a)

Z
is a Hausdorff space,

(b)

Z
is compact, and

(c)

for any
f\in W
, the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\cdot,f)
is lower semicontinuous in
Z
.

We first prove (a). Let
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in Z=\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E):\nu(E)=1\}
be two distinct measures.
By the uniqueness part of the Riesz representation theorem, there exist a real-valued
g\in C(E)

and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\int_{E}g\,d\nu_{1}<\alpha<\int_{E}g\,d\nu_{2}
. Then

U=\left\{\nu\in Z:\int_{E}g\,d\nu<\alpha\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad V=\left\{\nu\in Z:\int_{E}g\,d\nu>\alpha\right\}

are open and disjoint subsets of
Z
, and

\nu_{1}\in U
and
\nu_{2}\in V
.
Thus
Z
is a Hausdorff space.

Next we prove (b). Given
\nu\in Z
, the operator norm of
\nu
on
(C(E),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is

\lVert\nu\rVert=\nu(E)=1
, and therefore

Z\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(0,1)=\{\nu\in(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}\,:\,\lVert\nu\rVert\leq 1\}\,.

Recall that
Z
is equipped with the relative
weak* topology.
By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, the closed unit ball
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(0,1)
is
compact in the weak* topology. Hence, in order to show that

Z
is compact, it suffices to verify that
Z
is closed in the weak* topology of
(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}
.
Write

G=\{g\in C(E)\,:\,g(E)\subset[0,\infty)\}\,.

For every
g\in G
we define
\Lambda_{g}\colon(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\Lambda_{g}(\nu)=\int_{E}g\,d\nu
.
Then the functionals

\Lambda_{g}
are continuous for every
g\in G
and

Z=\Lambda_{1}^{-1}(\{1\})\cap\bigcap_{g\in G}\Lambda_{g}^{-1}([0,\infty))\,.

Indeed, above the inclusion
from left to right is immediate. Conversely, if
\nu\in(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}

belongs to the set on the right-hand side, then

\nu(E)=1
and
0\leq\int_{E}g\,d\nu<\infty
for all
g\in C(E)

satisfying
0\leq g<\infty
in
E
. Hence
\nu
is a nonnegative linear functional on
C(E)
, and

[
34
, Theorem 2.14]
implies that
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
.
Thus
\nu\in Z
, and it follows that

Z
is closed in
(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}
since it
is an intersection of closed sets.

It remains to prove condition (c).
Since
E\subset X
is compact, by Stone–Weierstrass theorem and Urysohn’s lemma, the
Banach space
(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})
is separable.
Since
Z\subset(C(E),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\infty})^{*}
is weak* compact,
the weak* topology relative to
Z
is metrizable.
Hence, it suffices to show sequential lower semicontinuity.
To this end, we fix
f\in W
. Recall that

\Hop_{\beta}f(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,\qquad x\in X.

For each fixed
n\geq n_{0}
, we define a function
g_{n}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
,

g_{n}(x)=2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,,\qquad x\in E\,.

Let
x\in E
and let
(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence
in
E
such that
x_{j}\to x
in
E
. Then

\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x_{j})

for all
y\in X
.
Thus, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left(\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x_{j})}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\right)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x_{j})}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)=\liminf_{j\to\infty}g_{n}(x_{j})\,.

Hence, the function
g_{n}
is lower semicontinuous in
E
,
and therefore also
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}g_{n}
is lower semicontinuous in
E
.
By
[
17
, Proposition 4.2.2]
, there exists a sequence
(h_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of continuous functions in
E
such that
0\leq h_{1}(x)\leq h_{2}(x)\leq\dotsb\leq\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)

and
\lim_{k\to\infty}h_{k}(x)=\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)
for every
x\in E
.
Now, let
\nu\in Z
and let
(\nu_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
Z
such that
\nu_{i}\to\nu
in
Z
. Then, for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{E}h_{k}(x)\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{E}h_{k}(x)\,d\nu_{i}(x)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{E}\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu_{i}(x)\,.

By using also the monotone convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu,f)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu(x)=\int_{E}\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{E}h_{k}(x)\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathcal{H}_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu_{i}(x)=\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\nu_{i},f)\,.

This shows that
\mathcal{E}_{\beta}(\,\cdot\,,f)
is
sequentially lower semicontinuous, and the proof is complete.
∎

8.
Muckenhoupt–Wheeden theorem

The original formulation of the celebrated Muckenhoupt–Wheeden Theorem in
[
31
]

bounds the
L^{p}
-norm of the Riesz potential by the
L^{p}
-norm of a fractional maximal function
in the (suitably weighted) Euclidean space.
In Theorem
8.3
we present a variant of this result for the
mixed
L^{p}(X,l^{1})
-norm of the operator
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}
.
Theorem
8.3
could actually be obtained as a consequence of a very general
metric space formulation of the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden Theorem in
[
33
]
,
but for the sake of completeness and transparency we give below a more
organic and self-contained proof. In fact, the application of the general result from
[
33
]

would still require rather tedious verification of the various assumptions;
compare for instance to
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
.
Our proof of Theorem
8.3
follows the general approach in
[
3
, Theorem 3.6.1]
,
based on a good
\lambda
-inequality, but the present setting requires several modifications.

For the formulation of Theorem
8.3
, we need fractional maximal functions.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\beta\geq 0
and let
\nu
be a nonnegative Borel
measure in
X
.
The fractional maximal function
M_{\beta}\nu
is defined by

(33)

M_{\beta}\nu(x)=\sup_{0<r\leq 2^{-n_{0}}}r^{\beta}\frac{\nu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

When
X
is unbounded, the supremum is taken over all radii
0<r<\infty
.
When
\beta=0
we get the centered maximal function
which is denoted by
M\nu=M_{0}\nu
.

We need the following technical lemma concerning lower semicontinuity.
Recall that here
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n})
is the closed
ball centered at
x
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\beta>0
and let
\nu
be a nonnegative Borel
measure in
X
. Then the function

\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\,,\qquad x\in X\,,

is lower semicontinuous in
X
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
n\geq n_{0}
.
By the properties of semicontinuous functions, see
[
5
, p. 362]
, it suffices to show that the nonnegative functions

f(x)=\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))
and
g(x)=\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))^{-1}
are lower semicontinuous in
X
.
Let
x\in X
and let
(x_{j})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
X
such that
x_{j}\to x
in
X
.
By Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{B(x,2^{-n})}(y)\,d\nu(y)\leq\int_{X}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{j},2^{-n})}(y)\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{j},2^{-n})}(y)\,d\nu(y)=\liminf_{j\to\infty}f(x_{j})\,,

showing that
f
is lower semicontinuous in
X
.

Define
h(x)=\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))
,
x\in X
. Then,
if
(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence converging to
x\in X
as above, we have

\displaystyle h(x)=\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n})}(y)\,d\mu(y)\geq\int_{X}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x_{j},2^{-n})}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

Write
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x_{j},2^{-n})
and

B=B(x,M)
, where

is chosen so that
{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}\subset B

. Then

\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}}\leq\mathbf{1}_{B}\in L^{1}(d\mu)

.
By applying Fatou’s lemma to the nonnegative functions
\mathbf{1}_{B}-\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}}
,

\displaystyle\mu(B)-\int_{X}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{B}-\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}}\bigr)(y)\,d\mu(y)

Consequently, we see that

h(x)\geq\int_{X}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}}(y)\,d\mu(y)\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}_{j}}(y)\,d\mu(y)=\limsup_{j\to\infty}h(x_{j})\,.

Therefore

g(x)=\frac{1}{h(x)}\leq\frac{1}{\limsup_{j\to\infty}h(x_{j})}=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{h(x_{j})}=\liminf_{j\to\infty}g(x_{j})\,,

and thus also

.

The following proof is an adaptation of the proof of
[

, Theorem 3.6.1]
.
As usual, it is possible to generalize Theorem
8.3
to Muckenhoupt
A_{\infty}

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and

.

) for some exponent

,

Observe that we have, for every

,

\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu(x)\big\rVert_{l^{1}}=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\,.

Throughout the proof, we use the function

h(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

The advantage of

is
the lower semicontinuity in

, given by Lemma

.

If
X
is bounded, we can make a further reduction concerning the measure

such that

\nu\bigl(B(z,\tfrac{1}{3}\operatorname{diam}(X)\bigr)=0\,.

Indeed,
if
X

. Let

and

, whence

.
Define Borel measures

and
\nu_{y}
in
X
by setting

\nu_{x}(A)=\nu(A\setminus B_{x})
and
\nu_{y}(A)=\nu(A\setminus B_{y})

for all Borel sets
A\subset X
.
Then

\nu(A)=\nu((A\setminus B_{x})\cup(A\setminus B_{y}))\leq\nu_{x}(A)+\nu_{y}(A)\,,

for all Borel sets
A\subset X
, and it follows that

\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{1})}\leq\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu_{x}\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{1})}+\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu_{y}\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{1})}\,.

Observe also that
M_{\beta}\nu_{x}\leq M_{\beta}\nu
and

M_{\beta}\nu_{y}\leq M_{\beta}\nu
.
Hence, if
X
is bounded, then
by considering the measures
\nu_{x}
and
\nu_{y}
instead of
\nu

we may assume that (
35
) holds for some
z\in X
.

By arguing as in
[
3
, p. 73]
,
for the claim (
34
)
it is sufficient to establish the following good
\lambda
-inequality:
There exists constants
a=2\cdot c_{\mu}^{5}(1+4\cdot 2^{4\beta})>1
,
C_{1}=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)>0
and

C_{2}=C(c_{\mu})\geq 1
such that for
all
\lambda>0
and all
0<\varepsilon\leq(3c_{\mu}^{2})^{-1}
we have

(36)

\begin{split}&\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\})\\
&\quad\leq C_{1}\varepsilon\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>\lambda\})+C_{2}\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,M_{\beta}\nu(x)>\varepsilon\lambda\})\,.\end{split}

To this end, fix
\lambda>0
and
0<\varepsilon\leq(3c_{\mu}^{2})^{-1}
.
Since the function
h
is lower semicontinuous in
X
, the level set

\Omega=\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>\lambda\}
is open in
X
.
Since
a>1
, we may assume
that
\Omega\not=\emptyset
.

We first consider the case
\Omega=X
.
If
X
is unbounded, then (
36
) holds whenever
C_{1}>0

since
\mu(X)=\infty
by the assumed reverse doubling condition
and the assumption
\operatorname{diam}(X)=\infty
.
If
X
is bounded, there exists
z\in X
such that
(
35
) holds. Since
z\in X=\Omega
, we see that

h(z)>\lambda
. Recall that
by the definition of
n_{0}
we have
X=B(x,2^{-n_{0}})
for every
x\in X
.
If
x\in X
, we obtain from (
35
)
that

\displaystyle\lambda<h(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{0}+2}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(z,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(z,2^{-n}))}\leq 3c_{\mu}^{2}\cdot 2^{-\beta n_{0}}\frac{\nu(X)}{\mu(X)}

\displaystyle=3c_{\mu}^{2}\cdot 2^{-\beta n_{0}}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n_{0}}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n_{0}}))}\leq 3c_{\mu}^{2}M_{\beta}\nu(x)\,.

It follows that
M_{\beta}\nu(x)>(3c_{\mu}^{2})^{-1}\lambda
for every
x\in X
,
and consequently (
36
) holds whenever
C_{2}\geq 1

since
\varepsilon\leq(3c_{\mu}^{2})^{-1}
.
This concludes the proof in the case
\Omega=X
.

In the sequel, we assume that
\Omega\not=\emptyset
and
X\setminus\Omega\neq\emptyset
.
By
[
17
, Proposition 4.1.15]
, there exists
a countable collection
\mathcal{W}=\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}
of the so-called Whitney balls
in
\Omega
such that

\Omega=\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i},r_{i})
and

\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\leq N=C(c_{\mu})
, where

r_{i}=\tfrac{1}{8}\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},X\setminus\Omega)\,.

We write
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\cup\mathcal{W}_{2}
, where
the collection
\mathcal{W}_{1}
consists of all Whitney balls
B_{i}
such that

M_{\beta}\nu(x)>\varepsilon\lambda
for all
x\in B_{i}
,
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}=\mathcal{W}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{1}
.
Thus

(37)

\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,M_{\beta}\nu(x)\leq\varepsilon\lambda\}\neq\emptyset

for all
B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
.
Since
a>1
and
\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\leq\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\})=\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\}}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}}\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}(y)\,d\mu(y)+\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}}\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,.

For the first sum above we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}}\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}}\mu(B_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}}\mu(\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,M_{\beta}\nu(x)>\varepsilon\lambda\})\leq\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,M_{\beta}\nu(x)>\varepsilon\lambda\}}\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq N\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,M_{\beta}\nu(x)>\varepsilon\lambda\})\,.

To estimate the second sum, we claim that

(38)

\mu(\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\})\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)\varepsilon\mu(B_{i})

holds for all
B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
. Using (
38
), we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}}\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)\varepsilon\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}}\mu(B_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)\varepsilon\int_{\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>\lambda\}}\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)N\varepsilon\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,h(x)>\lambda\})\,.

By combining the estimates above, we conclude that the
good
\lambda
-inequality (
36
) holds with

C_{1}=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)N

and
C_{2}=N
, where
N=C(c_{\mu})
.

Thus, to complete the proof, we need to show that (
38
)
holds for all
B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
.
We fix
B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
and
define Borel measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
by setting
\nu_{1}(A)=\nu(A\cap 10B_{i})
and

\nu_{2}(A)=\nu(A\setminus 10B_{i})
for all Borel sets
A\subset X
.
We also write, for all
x\in X
, that

h_{1}(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu_{1}(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad h_{2}(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu_{2}(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\,.

Then
\nu=\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
and
h=h_{1}+h_{2}
.
We prove
inequality (
38
) by showing that

(39)

\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,h(x)>a\lambda\}\subset\left\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,h_{1}(x)>a\lambda/2\right\}

and

(40)

\mu\left(\left\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,h_{1}(x)>a\lambda/2\right\}\right)\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)\varepsilon\mu(B_{i})\,.

For the inclusion in (
39
), we assume that

x\in B_{i}
is such that
h(x)>a\lambda
, and we first aim to show that
h_{2}(x)\leq\frac{a}{2}\lambda
. If
10B_{i}=X
, then

h_{2}(x)=0
. Hence, in the sequel we may assume that
X\setminus 10B_{i}\not=\emptyset
.
Let
k\geq n_{0}
be the largest integer such that

B(x,2^{-k})\setminus 10B_{i}\not=\emptyset
. Then

2^{-k}\geq 9r_{i}>r_{i}
, and by the definition of
\nu_{2}
,

h_{2}(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu_{2}(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\,.

Since
9r_{i}=\frac{9}{8}\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},X\setminus\Omega)
,
there exists
w\in 9B_{i}\setminus\Omega
. Then

h(w)\leq\lambda
by the definition of the set
\Omega
.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
n_{0}\leq n\leq k
. Then

\nu_{2}(B(x,2^{-n}))\leq\nu_{2}(B(w,2^{-n+4}))\leq\nu(B(w,2^{-n+4}))

and

\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(w,2^{-n+4}))\leq\mu(B(x,2^{-n+5}))\leq c_{\mu}^{5}\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))\,.

From these estimates we obtain

2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu_{2}(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\leq c_{\mu}^{5}2^{-\beta(n-4)}\frac{\nu(B(w,2^{-(n-4)}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(w,2^{-(n-4)}))}\,,

and so

h_{2}(x)\leq c_{\mu}^{5}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k}2^{-\beta(n-4)}\frac{\nu(B(w,2^{-(n-4)}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(w,2^{-(n-4)}))}\leq c_{\mu}^{5}(1+4\cdot 2^{4\beta})h(w)\leq\frac{a}{2}\lambda\,.

It follows that

a\lambda<h(x)=h_{1}(x)+h_{2}(x)\leq h_{1}(x)+\frac{a}{2}\lambda\,,

and thus

h_{1}(x)>a\lambda/2
, proving the inclusion in (
39
).

Next we show that (
40
) holds.
Let
x\in B_{i}
be such that
h_{1}(x)>a\lambda/2
and choose

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying
2^{-k-1}<r_{i}\leq 2^{-k}
. Then

\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu_{1}(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}\leq M\nu_{1}(x)\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\leq C(\beta)2^{-\beta k}M\nu_{1}(x)\leq C(\beta)r_{i}^{\beta}M\nu_{1}(x)\,,

where
M
is the centered maximal operator.
On the other hand, the assumed quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
),
with
\sigma>\beta
, gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu_{1}(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}(x,2^{-n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\nu_{1}(X)\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

\displaystyle=\nu_{1}(X)\frac{2^{-\sigma k}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{k}2^{-n(\beta-\sigma)}\left(\frac{2^{-n}}{2^{-k}}\right)^{\sigma}\frac{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\sigma}\nu_{1}(X)\frac{2^{-\sigma k}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{k}2^{n(\sigma-\beta)}

\displaystyle=C(c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma)\nu_{1}(X)\frac{2^{-\beta k}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma)\nu_{1}(X)\frac{r_{i}^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}\,.

Since
\nu_{1}(X)=\nu(10B_{i})
, we obtain

\nu_{1}(X)\frac{r_{i}^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}=r_{i}^{\beta}\frac{\nu_{1}(B(x_{i},10r_{i}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}\leq c_{\mu}^{4}r_{i}^{\beta}\frac{\nu_{1}(B(x,11r_{i}))}{\mu(B(x,11r_{i}))}\leq c_{\mu}^{4}r_{i}^{\beta}M\nu_{1}(x)\,.

By combining the above estimates, we get

a\lambda/2<h_{1}(x)\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)r_{i}^{\beta}M\nu_{1}(x)=\kappa M\nu_{1}(x)\,,

where we have written
\kappa=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)r_{i}^{\beta}
.
We conclude that

(41)

\mu\left(\left\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,h_{1}(x)>a\lambda/2\right\}\right)\leq\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,M\nu_{1}(x)>a\lambda/(2\kappa)\})\,.

The proof of
[
4
, Lemma 3.12]
shows that

(42)

\begin{split}\mu(\{x\in X\,:\,M\nu_{1}(x)>a\lambda/(2\kappa)\})&\leq C(c_{\mu})\frac{2\kappa}{a\lambda}\nu_{1}(X)\\
&\leq\frac{C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)}{a\lambda}r_{i}^{\beta}\frac{\nu_{1}(X)}{\mu(B_{i})}\mu(B_{i})\,.\end{split}

Since
B_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{2}
, by (
37
) there exists
z\in B_{i}
such that
M_{\beta}\nu(z)\leq\varepsilon\lambda
.
Taking also into account that
r_{i}\leq(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
in the bounded case, we obtain

(43)

\begin{split}r_{i}^{\beta}\frac{\nu_{1}(X)}{\mu(B_{i})}&=r_{i}^{\beta}\frac{\nu(B(x_{i},10r_{i}))}{\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i}))}\leq c_{\mu}^{4}(11r_{i})^{\beta}\frac{\nu(B(z,11r_{i}))}{\mu(B(z,11r_{i}))}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{11}{8}\right)^{\beta}c_{\mu}^{4}M_{\beta}\nu(z)\leq\left(\frac{11}{8}\right)^{\beta}c_{\mu}^{4}\varepsilon\lambda\,.\end{split}

By combining the above estimates (
41
), (
42
) and (
43
), we
conclude that

\mu\left(\left\{x\in B_{i}\,:\,h_{1}(x)>a\lambda/2\right\}\right)\leq\frac{C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma)}{a}\varepsilon\mu(B_{i})\,.

Since
a=2\cdot c_{\mu}^{5}(1+4\cdot 2^{4\beta})
, this
shows that (
40
) holds, and the proof is complete.
∎

9.
q
-independence of capacities

In the previous section, we obtained an upper bound for the

L^{p}(X,l^{1})
-norm of
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu

in terms of the
L^{p}
-norm of
M_{\beta}\nu
.
On the other hand, due to the doubling property of
\mu
,
the
L^{p}(X,l^{\infty})
-norm of
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu

gives an easy upper bound for the
L^{p}
-norm of
M_{\beta}\nu
;
cf. Lemma
9.1
and the proof of Lemma
9.2
.
Together, these estimates yield the
main result of this section, Theorem
9.3
,
which gives the comparability of
the
(\beta,p,q)
-capacities
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
for all values
of the parameter
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Observe that the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden Theorem
8.3
and the dual
characterization of
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
in terms of

\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu
are both crucial
for this approach, which in Euclidean spaces originally appeared in
[
2
]
;
see also
[
3
, Sections 3.6 and 4.4]

and
[
20
]
.

We begin with a simple two-sided estimate for the fractional maximal function.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\beta>0
and
let
\nu
be a nonnegative Borel measure in
X
. Then

\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq M_{\beta}\nu(x)\leq c_{\mu}\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

for every
x\in X
, where the supremums are taken over all integers
n
such that
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from definition (
33
). For the converse inequality, we let
x\in X
and
0<r\leq 2^{-n_{0}}
, and let
k\geq n_{0}
be an integer such that
2^{-k-1}<r\leq 2^{-k}
. Then
the doubling property (
3
) of
\mu
implies that

r^{\beta}\frac{\nu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\leq 2^{-\beta k}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-k}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k-1}))}\leq c_{\mu}2^{-\beta k}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-k}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-k}))}\leq c_{\mu}\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n}))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}.

The claim follows by taking supremum over all
0<r\leq 2^{-n_{0}}
.
∎

From Lemma
9.1
and the
Muckenhoupt–Wheeden Theorem
8.3
,
we obtain a
q
-independence result for the
operator
\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}
.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\beta>0
.
Assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta
,
and let
\nu
be a nonnegative Borel measure in
X
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)>0
such that

\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{1})}\leq C\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\lVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{\infty})}\,.

Proof.

The Muckenhoupt–Wheeden Theorem
8.3
gives

\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{1})}\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)\lVert M_{\beta}\nu\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}\,.

On the other hand, Lemma
9.1
implies

\lVert M_{\beta}\nu\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}\leq c_{\mu}\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{\infty})}\,,

and the claim follows by
combining these two estimates.
∎

Under the assumptions of the following theorem, the capacities
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)
and
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\tau}(E)
are
comparable for all
1\leq q,\tau\leq\infty
;
cf.
[
3
, p. 107]
.
We emphasize that the constant of comparison is independent of
q
and
\tau
.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
\beta>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q,\tau\leq\infty
, and assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)>0
such that

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)\leq C\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\tau}(E)

for all compact sets
E\subset X
.

Proof.

The dual characterization of the capacity in
Theorem
7.3
implies that

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{1/p}=\sup\Biggl\{\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\Biggr\}

and

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\tau}(E)^{1/p}=\sup\Biggl\{\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{\tau^{\prime}})}}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\Biggr\}\,.

By Lemma
9.2
, we have

\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{\tau^{\prime}})}\leq\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{1})}\leq C\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{\infty})}\leq C\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}

for every
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
,
where
C=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)
. The desired conclusion follows.
∎

10.
Comparison to Riesz capacity

In this section we clarify the relations between Riesz capacities and Triebel–Lizorkin capacities.
Riesz capacities are defined in terms of Riesz potentials,
which appear frequently in potential analysis,
see for instance
[
3
,
25
]
.
In metric spaces there are several slightly different
ways to define Riesz potentials.
The most natural for our purposes is the following variant,
which appears for instance in
[
12
,
16
,
19
]
.

Definition 10.1
.

Let
\beta>0
and let
\nu
be a nonnegative Borel measure in
X
. The
Riesz potential
I_{\beta}\nu
is defined by

I_{\beta}\nu(x)=\int_{X\setminus\{x\}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\nu(y)\,,\qquad x\in X\,.

If
f\geq 0
is a Borel function in
X
, we write

I_{\beta}f=I_{\beta}\nu
, where
d\nu=f\,d\mu
.

Assuming that
f\geq 0
is a Borel function in
X
and

\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some
\sigma>0
,
we have

(44)

I_{\beta}f(x)=\int_{X\setminus\{x\}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}f(y)\,d\mu(y)=\int_{X}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

for all
x\in X
since
\mu(\{x\})=0
for every
x\in X
.

Riesz capacities are well known in Euclidean spaces;
we refer to
[
3
,
26
]
and the references therein. In metric spaces, variants of Riesz
capacities have been studied for instance in
[
6
,
32
]
.
If
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some
\sigma>0
,
then the following definition coincides with that of
[
6
, Section 2.3]
, by (
44
).

Definition 10.2
.

Let
\beta>0
and
1<p<\infty
. The Riesz

(\beta,p)
-capacity of a subset
E\subset X
is

R_{\beta,p}(E)=\inf\bigl\{\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}\,:\,f\geq 0\text{ and }I_{\beta}f\geq 1\text{ on }E\bigr\}\,,

where
f\geq 0
in the infimum means that
f
is a nonnegative Borel function in
X
.

The pointwise inequalities for Riesz potentials in Lemma
10.3

will be useful when comparing Riesz capacities and Triebel–Lizorkin capacities.
The proofs of these estimates are rather straightforward computations based on
the doubling and reverse doubling conditions.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
\beta>0
and
let
\nu
be a nonnegative Borel measure in
X
. Then

(45)

I_{\beta}\nu(x)\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

for every
x\in X
.

Conversely, if
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta
, then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta)>0
such that

(46)

\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq CI_{\beta}\nu(x)

for every
x\in X
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
and write
A_{n}=B(x,2^{-n})\setminus B(x,2^{-n-1})
for every
n\geq n_{0}
. We have

(47)

I_{\beta}\nu(x)=\int_{X\setminus\{x\}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\nu(y)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{A_{n}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\nu(y)\,.

The doubling property (
3
) of
\mu
implies that

\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\leq\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n-1}))}\leq c_{\mu}\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

for every
y\in A_{n}
and
n\geq n_{0}
, and thus

\displaystyle I_{\beta}\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-\beta n}\nu(A_{n})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}.

This proves inequality (
45
).

For the proof of (
46
), we assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta
.
We estimate

(48)

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\}}\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}d\nu(y)\\
&\qquad=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\int_{A_{m}}\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}d\nu(y)=\sum_{m=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{A_{m}}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{m}\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}d\nu(y).\end{split}

Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
n_{0}\leq n\leq m
and let
y\in A_{m}
. Then the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) implies that

\frac{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq c_{\sigma}\biggl(\frac{d(x,y)}{2^{-n}}\biggr)^{\sigma}.

Hence,

\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}=2^{n(\sigma-\beta)}\frac{2^{-\sigma n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq c_{\sigma}2^{n(\sigma-\beta)}\frac{d(x,y)^{\sigma}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))},

and therefore

(49)

\begin{split}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{m}\frac{2^{-\beta n}}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}&\leq c_{\sigma}\frac{d(x,y)^{\sigma}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{m}2^{n(\sigma-\beta)}\\
&\leq C(c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta)\frac{d(x,y)^{\sigma}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}2^{m(\sigma-\beta)}\\
&\leq C(c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta)\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\end{split}

for every
m\geq n_{0}
and
y\in A_{m}
.
Combining the estimates (
48
) and (
49
)
and the identity in (
47
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta)\sum_{m=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{A_{m}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=C(c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta)I_{\beta}\nu(x)\,,

as was claimed.
∎

The following theorem explains the relation between the
Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacities and the Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacities.
Namely, in the case
q=\infty
these capacities are comparable
for all sets
E\subset X
, under our standard assumptions.
The proof is quite simple, relying on the pointwise
estimates given by Lemma
10.3
.

Theorem 10.4
.

Let
\beta>0
and
1<p<\infty
, and assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta
. Then

c_{\mu}^{-2p}\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\infty}(E)\leq R_{\beta,p}(E)\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta,p)\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\infty}(E)\,,

for all sets
E\subset X
.

Proof.

To show the first inequality, we fix a nonnegative Borel function
f
such that
I_{\beta}f(x)\geq 1

for every
x\in E
. We set
h_{n}=f
for every
n\geq n_{0}
and define
h=(h_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\geq 0
.
Assuming that
x\in E
, Lemma
10.3
with the measure
d\nu=f\,d\mu
gives

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq I_{\beta}f(x)=I_{\beta}\nu(x)\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(x,2^{-n})}(y)}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}h_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)=\Hop_{\beta}(c_{\mu}^{2}h)(x)\,.

Thus

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,\infty}(E)\leq\lVert c_{\mu}^{2}h\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{\infty})}^{p}=c_{\mu}^{2p}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{\infty})}^{p}=c_{\mu}^{2p}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}\,,

and the first inequality of the claim follows by taking infimum over all
f
as above.

Let then
f=(f_{n})_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative Borel functions in
X

such that
\Hop_{\beta}f(x)\geq 1
for all
x\in E
. We define

h=\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}f_{n}\geq 0
and fix
x\in E
.
Using Lemma
10.3
, with the measure
d\nu=h\,d\mu
,
and the fact that
\mu(\{x\})=0
for every
x\in X
,
we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\Hop_{\beta}f(x)=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(y,2^{-n})}(x)}{\mu(B(y,2^{-n}))}f_{n}(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\int_{X\setminus\{x\}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{B(x,2^{-n})}(y)}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}h(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=c_{\mu}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-\beta n}\frac{\nu(B(x,2^{-n})\setminus\{x\})}{\mu(B(x,2^{-n}))}\leq CI_{\beta}\nu(x)=I_{\beta}(Ch)(x)\,,

where
C=C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta)
. This implies that

R_{\beta,p}(E)\leq\lVert Ch\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}=C^{p}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}=C^{p}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X;l^{\infty})}^{p}\,,

and the second inequality of the claim follows by taking infimum over all
f
as above.
∎

By the
q
-independence of the
(\beta,p,q)
-capacities for compact sets, given by
Theorem
9.3
, we obtain from Theorem
10.4

the comparability of
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and
R_{\beta,p}
for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
Thus the following result is an immediate consequence of those two theorems.
Nevertheless, we give below also an alternative direct proof,
which is based on the dual characterization of
\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}
and
uses some arguments from
[
32
]
.

Theorem 10.5
.

Let
\beta>0
,
1<p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)>0
such that

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)\leq CR_{\beta,p}(E)

for all compact sets
E\subset X
.

Proof.

Theorem
7.3
implies that

(50)

\operatorname{Cap}^{\beta}_{p,q}(E)^{1/p}=\sup\Biggl\{\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\,:\,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)\Biggr\}\,.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
and let
f\geq 0
be such that
I_{\beta}f\geq 1
on
E
.
Then

\displaystyle\nu(E)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}I_{\beta}f(x)\,d\nu(x)=\int_{X}\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{X\setminus\{x\}}(y)\frac{d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}f(y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\int_{X}\int_{X}\mathbf{1}_{X\setminus\{y\}}(x)\frac{d(y,x)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,x)))}\,d\nu(x)f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=c_{\mu}\int_{X}I_{\beta}\nu(y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)\leq c_{\mu}\lVert I_{\beta}\nu\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}\,.

Lemma
10.3
and Lemma
9.2
imply that

\displaystyle\lVert I_{\beta}\nu\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{1})}\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}\,.

Hence,

\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}\,,

and by taking infimum over all
f
as above, we get

\frac{\nu(E)}{\big\lVert\widecheck{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}\nu\big\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X;l^{q^{\prime}})}}\leq C(c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},\beta,\sigma,p)R_{\beta,p}(E)^{1/p}\,.

The claim follows by taking supremum over all

\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(E)
and using (
50
).
∎

We also record explicitly the following corollary concerning the comparability
of the Riesz capacity and the
relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity.
This result is needed below in our applications concerning capacity density conditions.

Corollary 10.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
,
0<\beta<1
, and
\Lambda\geq 2
,
and assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
Let
B\subset X
be a ball and let
E\subset{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B}}}
be a compact set.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\sigma},p,q,\sigma,\beta,c_{\mu},\Lambda)>0
such that

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E,2B,\Lambda B)\leq CR_{\beta,p}(E)\,.

Proof.

This follows by combining Theorems
6.5

and
10.5
.
∎

11.
Capacity density

We conclude the paper by applying the results from the previous sections
to obtain a characterization of the capacity density condition
for the relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity.
This extends the results in
[
26
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]

to our present setting
and in particular implies that also this density condition
is open-ended (self-improving) in complete geodesic metric spaces;
see Corollary
11.5
.

In the proof of the characterization in Theorem
11.4
we will
need most of the theory that has been established in the paper. In particular, we
use Corollary
10.6
, which follows from the comparability results
between the relative Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity,
the Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity, and the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity.
In the proofs of the comparability results we applied the discrete
\Lop_{\beta}
-potentials
and the
q
-independence of the Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacities. The proof of the
latter was, in turn, based on the dual characterization of the Triebel–Lizorkin capacities
and the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden Theorem. Finally, we utilize also the
estimates for the relative
(\beta,p,q)
-capacities from Section
4
.

Besides these results from the present work, one essential ingredient in the proof of
Theorem
11.4
is
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
, which
however requires some additional assumptions on the space
X
.
The proof of
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
utilizes a non-trivial characterization of
a Hajłasz capacity density condition in terms of Hausdorff content density conditions
from
[
7
, Theorem 9.5]
.

Before the statement of Theorem
11.4
,
we introduce the relevant density conditions.

Definition 11.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
0<\beta<1
.
A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition,
if there are constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
and
\Lambda>2
such that

(51)

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\geq c_{0}\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<c_{1}\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

By inequality (
44
), the following Riesz capacity density condition
coincides with the one in
[
6
, Definition 6.3]
if
\mu
satisfies the
quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some
\sigma>0
.

Definition 11.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\beta>0
.
A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition,
if there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(52)

R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{0}\>R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

We also need a suitable version of the Hausdorff content density condition;
recall Definition
4.5
. The following definition coincides with
[
6
, Definition 5.1]
.

Definition 11.3
.

A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition of codimension
d\geq 0
, if
there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(53)

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,d}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{0}\>{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,d}_{r}(\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

We are now ready to characterize the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity density condition.
The implication from (ii) to (iii) in the following theorem relies (via
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
)
on
[
7
, Theorem 9.5]
.
This is the most difficult of the implications in the proof.
The bounds
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
in (i) and (iv) are convenient due to the use of
Theorem
4.6
, but they have not been optimized.
Nevertheless, part (iv) shows that the actual value of these constants is not essential.
Moreover, a straightforward modification of Example
4.4
shows that
all closed sets
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfy condition (i) of Theorem
11.4
with
\Lambda=2

when
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
are such that
\beta p<1
.
Not all closed sets satisfy, say, condition (iii) and therefore
the requirement
\Lambda\geq 41
cannot be replaced with
\Lambda\geq 2
in part (i) of Theorem
11.4
.

Theorem 11.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<q\leq\infty
, and
0<\beta<1
.
Assume that
X
is a complete geodesic metric space and that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
. Then the following
conditions are equivalent for all closed sets
E\subset X
:

(i)

E
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
51
),
with some constants
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
.

(ii)

E
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition (
52
).

(iii)

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
53
) for some
0<d<\beta p
.

(iv)

E
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
51
),
for all constants
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
.

Proof.

Assume first that condition (i) holds, with constants
c_{0}>0
,
\Lambda\geq 41
, and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
in (
51
).
Fix
x\in E
and
0<R<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
, and write
r=8c_{1}R<c_{1}\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
The geodesic space
X
is in particular connected, and
therefore, by
[
6
, Lemma 5.5]
, there is a constant
C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta,p)>0
such that

(54)

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,R)})\leq C_{1}R^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,R))\leq C(C_{1},c_{1},c_{\mu})r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.

We use Theorem
4.3
(b)
and inequality (
12
) to get

(55)

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(\beta,p,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

By combining (
54
) and (
55
), we obtain

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,R)})\leq C(\beta,p,\sigma,c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},c_{1},\Lambda)\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

Since
E
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
51
),
with the constant
c_{0}>0
, we see that

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,R)})\leq C(\beta,p,\sigma,c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},c_{0},c_{1},\Lambda)\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

The closed
and bounded set
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
is compact by
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
,
and so Corollary
10.6
implies that

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,R)})\leq C(\beta,p,q,\sigma,c_{\sigma},c_{\mu},c_{0},c_{1},\Lambda)R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

Clearly
R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\leq R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,R)})
, and therefore (ii) holds.

The implication from (ii) to (iii) follows from
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]

and the implication from (iv) to (i) is a triviality.
Hence, it suffices to show that (iii) implies (iv).
To this end, assume that (iii) holds, with a constant
c_{0}>0
in (
53
).
Let
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
, and fix
x\in E
and
0<r<c_{1}\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Theorem
4.3
(a) and inequality (
12
) imply that

(56)

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,p,\Lambda)r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.

On the other hand, by the simple estimate in
[
6
, Remark 5.2]

and the assumed condition (iii),
we obtain

(57)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,d}_{r}(\overline{B(x,r)})\leq C(c_{0})\mathcal{H}^{\mu,d}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

Let
\{B(x_{k},r_{k})\}_{k}
be a countable cover of
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
, with
0<r_{k}\leq 5\Lambda r

for all
k
. If
0<r_{k}\leq r
for all
k
, then by Definition
4.5
of the Hausdorff content we have

\mathcal{H}^{\mu,d}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\leq\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-d}\,,

and thus, by (
57
),

(58)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{0})\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-d}\,.

On the other hand, if
r<r_{k}
for some
k
, then we may assume that
r<r_{1}\leq 5\Lambda r

and that
B(x_{1},r_{1})\cap\overline{B(x,r)}\neq\emptyset
.
In this case we obtain, using also the doubling condition (
3
), that

(59)

\begin{split}r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))&\leq C(d,\Lambda)r_{1}^{-d}\mu(B(x_{1},3r_{1}))\\
&\leq C(d,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\mu(B(x_{1},r_{1}))\,r_{1}^{-d}\leq C(d,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-d}\,.\end{split}

By taking infimum over all such covers of
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
, we conclude from
(
58
) and (
59
) that

(60)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{0},d,c_{\mu},\Lambda)\mathcal{H}^{\mu,d}_{5\Lambda r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

Since
X
is connected, the measure
\mu
satisfies the reverse doubling condition (
4
) for
a constant
0<c_{R}=C(c_{\mu})<1
.
By using (
60
), Theorem
4.6
with
\eta=d/\beta
,
and inequality (
12
), we get

(61)

r^{-d}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(\beta,p,d,c_{\mu},c_{0},\Lambda)r^{\beta p-d}\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,.

Combining (
56
) and (
61
) gives

\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\leq C\operatorname{cap}_{\dot{M}^{\beta}_{p,q}}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r),B(x,\Lambda r))\,,

where
C=C(\beta,p,d,c_{\mu},c_{0},\Lambda)
.
This proves condition (iv), and the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
11.4
,
we obtain for the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin capacity density condition
the following self-improvement property,
which is new even in the Euclidean spaces. Similar self-improvement results for
other fractional capacities have been obtained in
[
26
,
6
,
7
]
.

Corollary 11.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<q,\tau\leq\infty
, and
0<\beta<1
.
Assume that
X
is a complete geodesic metric space and
that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
5
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
Assume that a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\beta,p,q)
-capacity density condition (
51
),
with some constants
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
.
Then there exists
0<\delta<\min\{\beta,p-1\}
such that

E
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\gamma,s,\tau)
-capacity density condition (
51
),
for any
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
, whenever

\beta-\delta<\gamma<1\,,\quad p-\delta<s<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma>\gamma s\,.

Proof.

Theorem
11.4
implies
that
E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
53
)
for some
0<d<\beta p
. Notice that this condition is open-ended, so it allows
us to choose
0<\delta<\min\{\beta,p-1\}
such that
d<(\beta-\delta)(p-\delta)
.
Let
\gamma
and
s
be such that

0<\beta-\delta<\gamma<1\,,\quad 1<p-\delta<s<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sigma>\gamma s\,.

Then
0<d<\gamma s
, and since

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
53
)
of codimension
d
,
Theorem
11.4
implies
that
E
satisfies the Hajłasz–Triebel–Lizorkin
(\gamma,s,\tau)
-capacity
density condition (
51
),
for all constants
\Lambda\geq 41
and
c_{1}\leq 1/80
.
∎

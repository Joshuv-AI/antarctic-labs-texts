Oscillator strengths in the framework of equation of motion multilevel CC3
Alexander C. Paul, Sarai D. Folkestad, Rolf H. Myhre, Henrik Koch

Abstract
We present an efficient implementation of the equation of motion oscillator strengths for the closed-shell multilevel coupled cluster singles and doubles with perturbative triples method (MLCC3) in the electronic structure program e T e^{T} . The orbital space is split into an active part treated with CC3 and an inactive part computed at the coupled cluster singles and doubles (CCSD) level of theory. Asymptotically, the CC3 contribution scales as 𝒪 ⁡ ( n V ​ n v 3 ​ n o 3 ) \mathcal{O}(n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}) floating-point operations (FLOP), where n V n_{\mathrm{V}} is the total number of virtual orbitals while n v n_{\mathrm{v}} and n o n_{\mathrm{o}} are the number of active virtual and occupied orbitals, respectively. The CC3 contribution, thus, only scales linearly with the full system size and can become negligible compared to the cost of CCSD. We demonstrate the capabilities of our implementation by calculating the UV-VIS spectrum of azobenzene and a core excited state of betaine 30 with more than 1000 molecular orbitals.

Introduction

Coupled cluster theory is one of the most accurate models
when spectroscopic properties of small and medium sized molecules
are investigated.
Helgaker et al. 2012
;
Koch and Jørgensen 1990
;
Pedersen and Koch 1997

Due to its high accuracy and relatively feasible computational scaling
as
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{2}_{\mathrm{O}})
,
CCSD is the most widely used variant of coupled cluster.
Despite its accuracy for valence excited states,
larger errors occur when considering core excited states or
double excitation dominated states.

Norman and Dreuw 2018
;
Myhre et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Oosterbaan et al. 2019
;
Myhre et al. 2019
;
Christiansen et al. 1996

Including triple excitations in the parametrization of the wave function
improves the description of such states.
However, the computational cost and the memory requirement
increase to
\mathcal{O}(n^{5}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{O}})
and
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{O}})
,
respectively for CCSDT.
Noga and Bartlett 1987
;
Helgaker et al. 2014

Approximating triples amplitudes with perturbation theory
can reduce the computational cost to
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{O}})

and the required memory to
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{2}_{\mathrm{O}})
.

Triples corrections can be classified as iterative and noniterative models.
In noniterative models, corrections to the CCSD excitation energy are obtained
by expanding the excitation energy using many-body perturbation theory (MBPT).
The advantage of a noniterative approach
is that the triples correction is only computed once.
The disadvantage,
however,
is that transition moments cannot be easily defined.
Christiansen et al. 1996
;
Koch et al. 1994

The noniterative models include CCSDR(1a), CCSDR(1b) and CCSDR(3)
which are derived from the iterative methods CCSDT-1a, CCSDT-1b and CC3,
respectively.
Christiansen et al. 1996
;
Noga et al. 1987
;
Christiansen et al. 1995
;
Hald et al. 2002
;
Paul et al. 2021

Other noteworthy examples are CREOM-CCSD(T), EOMIP-CCSD
∗

—developed specifically for ionized states—
and EOM-CCSD(T)(a)* which introduces
corrections to both the CCSD ground and the excited states.

Kowalski and Piecuch 2004
;
Stanton and Gauss 1996
;
Saeh and Stanton 1999
;
Matthews and Stanton 2016

The best-known methods for including triples excitations iteratively are
CC3 and CCSDT-n.
Christiansen et al. 1995
;
Noga et al. 1987

Both CCSDT-1 and CC3 scale asymptotically as
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{O}})
,
but CC3 includes single excitations to infinite order leading to an improved
description of ground and excited states.
Christiansen et al. 1995

The advantage of iterative models is that they are more robust
tri 2001

and provide a consistent definition of other properties than the energy.

Sauer et al. 2009

However, that comes at the cost of iteratively converging equations scaling
as
\mathcal{O}(n^{4}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{O}})
.
Nevertheless, with current implementations systems of around 400 basis functions
can be routinely treated at the CC3 level.
Paul et al. 2021

Due to the success of coupled cluster theory,
schemes have been developed to reduce the scaling while keeping the accuracy.
Pulay and Sæbø advocated the use of localized molecular orbitals (LMOs),
for a compact description of electronic correlation
in Møller-Plesset (MP) perturbation theory and configuration interaction
singles and doubles(CISD).

Pulay 1983
;
Saebø and Pulay 1993
;
Sæbø and Pulay 1985
;
Saebø and Pulay 1986
;
Saebo and Pulay 1988

They used Boys localization for the occupied molecular orbitals
and projected atomic orbitals (PAOs) for the virtual space,
and reduced the scaling by neglecting the correlation between distant pairs of localized orbitals.

Saebø and Pulay 1993

Werner and Schütz then extended this model to coupled cluster theory
with and without a noniterative triples correction.

Hampel and Werner 1996
;
Schütz and Werner 2000
;
Schütz 2000

Domain based local pair-natural orbital coupled cluster (DLPNO-CC)
methods are also related to this approach.
Neese et al. 2009
;
Neese et al. 2009

The DLPNO-CC approach has recently been extended to
CCSD(T) and also CC3 which was used to calculate the first electronic excited state
of a system with more than 1300 basis functions.
Riplinger et al. 2013
;
Frank et al. 2020

Reducing the size of the active space based on a distance criterium is certainly
successful for ground state properties.
For the description of excitation energies and other excited state properties,
however,
distance measures do not work as well
as more diffuse orbitals become more important.

Korona and Werner 2003
;
Kats et al. 2006
;
Crawford et al. 2019
;
Kats and Schütz 2009
;
Helmich and Hättig 2013
;
Dutta et al. 2016
;
Dutta et al. 2018

Therefore,
larger active spaces have to be employed in these calculations
and different orbital spaces are used for the ground and excited states.

Korona and Werner 2003
;
Kats et al. 2006

Multilevel and embedding methods treat different regions of a system
with different levels of theory.
The idea of obtaining an accurate description of a large molecular system
by coupling the contributions of its subsystems is exploited in
QM/MM approaches,
Warshel and Karplus 1972
;
Warshel and Levitt 1976
;
Field et al. 1990
;
Lin and Truhlar 2007
;
Senn and Thiel 2009
;
Scheurer et al. 2018

frozen density embedding,
Wesolowski and Warshel 1993
;
Neugebauer et al. 2005

subsystem DFT,
Cortona 1991
;
Jacob and Neugebauer 2014

and the ONIOM, IMOMO and LMOMO methods.
Humbel et al. 1996
;
Svensson et al. 1996
;
Mata et al. 2008

Another method related to multilevel coupled cluster (MLCC)
was developed by Oliphant and Adamowicz using CCSD for multireference
systems by including selected triple and quadruple substitutions.

Oliphant and Adamowicz 1991
;
Piecuch et al. 1993
;
Kállay et al. 2002

This scheme was adapted by Köhn and Olsen to include higher order substitutions
at reduced cost.
Olsen 2000
;
Köhn and Olsen 2006

In multilevel coupled cluster (MLCC) one CC wave function
is used for the full system but different parts of the system
are described with different level of truncation.
Myhre et al. 2013
;
Myhre et al. 2014

Considerable savings are achieved by applying the higher order excitation operators
in a smaller (active) subset of the orbitals.
Myhre and Koch 2016

The active orbital space can be selected using localized orbitals
— such as Cholesky orbitals
Aquilante et al. 2006
and projected atomic orbitals (PAOs)

Saebø and Pulay 1993

— or state-selective approaches —
such as the correlated natural transition orbitals (CNTOs).
Høyvik et al. 2017

As MLCC is designed for intensive properties, excitation energies
or oscillator strengths are accurately reproduced
if an appropriate active space is chosen.
Høyvik et al. 2017
;
Myhre and Koch 2016
;
Myhre et al. 2016
;
Folkestad and Koch 2020

While state-selective approaches are preferred to keep
the active space as compact as possible,
they are less suited for transition properties
especially between excited states,
as a consistent active space is needed for all excited states.
Frank et al. 2020

Localized orbitals are only suitable in the cases where the target property
is localized in a smaller region of the molecule.

In this paper we report the extension of the MLCC3 method to compute
oscillator strengths with CC3 quality but at significantly reduced cost.
Employing core-valence separation (CVS),
oscillator strengths are also available for core excited states.
Cederbaum 1987
;
Wenzel et al. 2014
;
Coriani and Koch 2015

This allows us to tackle excited states and oscillator strengths of systems
with more than 1000 basis functions.

Theory

In this section,
we will introduce the closed shell MLCC3 model within the equation of motion (EOM) formalism.
For a more detailed derivation we refer to Refs
14
;
16
.
Consider the general cluster operator

T=\sum_{\mu}\tau_{\mu}X_{\mu},

(1)

where
X_{\mu}
is an excitation operator that converts the reference
determinant,
\ket*{\R}
,
into the excited determinant,
\ket*{\mu}
,
and
\tau_{\mu}
is the corresponding amplitude.
In MLCC3 with two levels,
namely CCSD and CC3,
the cluster operator assumes the form

T=T_{1}+T_{2}+T^{a}_{3}

(2)

with

\begin{split}T_{1}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\\
I\end{subarray}}\tau^{A}_{I}E_{AI}\\
T_{2}&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}AB\\
IJ\end{subarray}}\tau^{AB}_{IJ}E_{AI}E_{BJ}\\
T^{a}_{3}&=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}abc\\
ijk\end{subarray}}\tau^{abc}_{ijk}E_{ai}E_{bj}E_{ck}\end{split}

(3)

where
E_{AI}
and
E_{ai}
are singlet excitation operators.
While the operators
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
excite on the full orbital space
indicated by capitalized indices,
the triples cluster operator
T^{a}_{3}
only excites in the active orbital space
denoted by lower case indices.
We use the standard notation
where the indices
i
,
j
,
k\dots
refer to occupied,

a
,
b
,
c\dots
to virtual,
and
p
,
q
,
r\dots
to general active orbitals.
The CC wave function is defined as

\ket*{\CC}=\exp(T)\ket*{\R}

(4)

and we introduce the similarity transformed Hamiltonian

\bar{H}=\exp(-T)\hat{H}\exp(T)

(5)

where

\hat{H}=\sum_{pq}h_{pq}E_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}(pq|rs)(E_{pq}E_{rs}-E_{ps}\delta_{qr})+h_{nuc}

(6)

is the electronic Hamiltonian.
To obtain the cluster amplitudes a set of biorthogonal determinants

\{\bra*{\mu}\}=\{\bra*{\mu_1}\}\oplus\{\bra*{\mu_2}\}\oplus\{\bra*{\mu_3^a}\}

(7)

is defined,
where the triply excited determinants,
\mu_{3}^{a}
,
are restricted to the active space.
These determinants are generated using the contravariant excitation operator,

\tilde{X}_{\mu}
, such that,

\innerproduct{\mu}{\nu}=\bra*{\R}\tilde{X}_{\mu}X_{\nu}\ket*{\R}=\delta_{\mu\nu}.

(8)

The coupled cluster energy,
\mathrm{E_{CC}}
,
and the cluster amplitudes are then obtained by projection
onto the reference determinant and the set of excited determinants,
respectively,
Helgaker et al. 2014

\displaystyle\mathrm{E_{CC}}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\R}\bar{H}\ket*{\R}

(9)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\mu}\bar{H}\ket*{\R}=0.

(10)

To obtain compact equations we incorporate the effect of the singles
cluster operator into the Hamiltonian and obtain the so-called

T_{1}
-transformed Hamiltonian,

H=\exp(-T_1)\hat{H}\exp(T_1).

(11)

In analogy to MBPT,
the
T_{1}
-transformed Hamiltonian is split into an
effective one-particle operator and a fluctuation potential.

H=F+U

(12)

In CC3 the double excitation amplitudes and the fluctuation potential are
treated as first order in the perturbation while the triples amplitudes
are considered second order.
The single excitation amplitudes are included as zeroth order parameters,
as they have a special role as relaxation parameters.
Christiansen et al. 1995
;
Hald et al. 2002

Inserting eq (
3
) and eq (
7
) into eq (
8
) and neglecting
all terms of third and higher order in the perturbation,
we obtain the MLCC3 ground state equations,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu_{1}}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\mus}H+[H,T_{2}]+[H,T^{a}_{3}]\ket*{\R}

(13)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu_{2}}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\mud}H+[H,T_{2}]+[[H,T_{2}],T_{2}]+[H,T^{a}_{3}]\ket*{\R}

(14)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu^{a}_{3}}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\mut}[H,T_{2}]+[F,T^{a}_{3}]\ket*{\R}.

(15)

The Fock matrix is not necessarily diagonal in the local orbital basis,
but it can be block-diagonalized within the active orbital space,
such that the off-diagonal elements do not contribute to the triples amplitudes.
Therefore,
the triples amplitudes can be expressed in terms of the doubles amplitudes

\tau^{abc}_{ijk}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{abc}_{ijk}}\bra*{\mut}[H,T_{2}]\ket*{\R},

(16)

where
\varepsilon^{abc}_{ijk}
are the orbital energy differences

\varepsilon^{abc}_{ijk}=\varepsilon_{a}+\varepsilon_{b}+\varepsilon_{c}-\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}-\varepsilon_{k}.

(17)

In equation of motion coupled cluster (EOM-CC)
start out from the matrix representation of the similarity transformed Hamiltonian,

\bm{\bar{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bra*{\R}\bar{H}\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\R}\bar{H}\ket*{\nu}\\
\bra*{\mu}\bar{H}\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mu}\bar{H}\ket*{\nu}\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

If the CC ground state equations, eq (
8
),
are converged,
the similarity transformed Hamiltonian can be written as,

\bm{\bar{H}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\bm{\eta}^{T}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{J}\end{pmatrix}+\mathrm{E_{CC}}\bm{I},

(19)

where
\eta_{\nu}=\bra*{\R}[\bar{H},X_{\nu}]\ket*{\R}

and
\bm{J}
is the so-called Jacobian with matrix elements

\bra*{\mu}[\bar{H},X_{\nu}]\ket*{\R}
.
The eigenvectors of
\bar{H}
are the EOM states and the corresponding
eigenvalues the energies of these states.
As the similarity transformed Hamiltonian is non-symmetric,
the left and right eigenvectors are not hermitian conjugates,
but they are biorthonormal.
Helgaker et al. 2014

\bm{\bar{H}}\bm{\bar{R}}_{m}=E_{m}\bm{\bar{R}}_{m}\quad\bm{\bar{L}}^{T}_{m}\bm{\bar{H}}=E_{m}\bm{\bar{L}}^{T}_{m}\quad\bm{\bar{L}}^{T}_{m}\bm{\bar{R}}_{n}=\delta_{mn}

(20)

From the biorthogonality of the EOM states and the structure of the Hamiltonian matrix,
we obtain the left and the right ground state,

\bm{\bar{L}}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\bm{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\quad\bm{\bar{R}}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix},

(21)

and the left and right excited states,
Paul et al. 2021

\bm{\bar{L}}_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\bm{L}_{m}\end{pmatrix}\quad\bm{\bar{R}}_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{\lambda}\bm{R}_{m}\\
\bm{R}_{m}\end{pmatrix}.

(22)

The parameters
\bm{\lambda}

are determined from

\bm{\lambda}^{T}\bm{J}=-\bm{\eta},

(23)

while the parameters of the excited states are determined as eigenvectors of the Jacobian,

\bm{J}
.
The MLCC3 Jacobian is given by
Myhre and Koch 2016

\footnotesize\bm{J}^{MLCC3}=\begin{pmatrix}\bra*{\mus}[H+[H,T_{2}],X_{\nu_{1}}]\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mus}[H,X_{\nu_{2}}]\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mus}[H,X_{\nu^{a}_{3}}]\ket*{\R}\\
\bra*{\mud}[H+[H,T_{2}+T^{a}_{3}],X_{\nu_{1}}]\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mud}[H+[H,T_{2}],X_{\nu_{2}}]\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mud}[H,X_{\nu^{a}_{3}}]\ket*{\R}\\
\bra*{\mut}[H+[H,T_{2}],X_{\nu_{1}}]\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mut}[H,X_{\nu_{2}}]\ket*{\R}&\bra*{\mut}[F,X_{\nu^{a}_{3}}]\ket*{\R}\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

The vectors in eq (
21
) and eq (
22
) correspond to operators
which generate the EOM states from the Hartree-Fock determinant.

\displaystyle\bra*{\tCC}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\R}\Big(1+\sum_{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}\tilde{X}_{\mu}\Big)\exp(-T)

(25)

\displaystyle\ket*{\CC}

\displaystyle=\exp(T)\ket*{\R}

(26)

\displaystyle\bra*{m}

\displaystyle=\bra*{\R}\sum_{\mu}L_{\mu}\tilde{X}_{\mu}\exp(-T)

(27)

\displaystyle\ket*{m}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\mu}R_{\mu}X_{\mu}-\sum_{\mu}R_{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}\Big)\exp(T)\ket*{\R}

(28)

Once the ground and excited states are determined,
left and right transition moments can be obtained in terms of
left (
D^{m\text{-}0}
) and right (
\tilde{D}^{0\text{-}m}
) transition densities.

Stanton and Bartlett 1993
;
Stanton 1994
;
Levchenko et al. 2005

\displaystyle\bra*{\tCC}A\ket*{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq}\tilde{D}^{0\text{-}m}_{pq}A_{pq}

(29)

\displaystyle\bra*{m}A\ket*{\CC}

\displaystyle=\sum_{pq}D^{m\text{-}0}_{pq}A_{pq}

(30)

Here,

A
is a general one-electron operator
A=\sum_{pq}A_{pq}E_{pq}
.

To obtain accurate excitation energies and transition dipole moments,
the selection of the active orbital space is crucial.
In this paper two approaches are chosen to partition the orbital space.
For the cheaper strategy Cholesky orbitals are used for the occupied space.
To obtain these orbitals the Hartree-Fock density is Cholesky decomposed
using the AOs of the active atoms as pivoting elements.

Aquilante et al. 2006
;
Sánchez De Merás et al. 2010

D_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{J}C^{a}_{\alpha J}C^{a}_{\beta J}+\Delta D_{\alpha\beta}

(31)

The decomposition is stopped when the size of all active diagonal elements
is below a given threshold and the coefficients are simply the elements
of the Cholesky vectors
C_{\alpha J}
.
The inactive orbitals are then obtained by decomposing the remaining part of the density,

\bm{\Delta D}
.
Projected atomic orbitals have been shown to give a good description of the
virtual space for solvated systems,
but also adenosine.
Folkestad and Koch 2020
;
Folkestad et al. 2021
;
Goletto et al. 2021

The construction of correlated natural transition orbitals is more costly
as they are obtained from excitation vectors of a coupled cluster calculation.
In MLCC3 we use CNTOs constructed from CCSD excited states
to get a compact description of the excited states.
The CNTOs are generated by diagonalizing two matrices,
denoted by
\bm{M}
and
\bm{N}
,
defined as

\displaystyle M_{ij}=\sum_{a}R^{a}_{i}R^{a}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}ab\\
k\end{subarray}}(1+\delta_{ai,bk}\delta_{ij})R^{ab}_{ik}R^{ab}_{jk}

(32)

\displaystyle N_{ab}=\sum_{i}R^{a}_{i}R^{b}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\\
ij\end{subarray}}(1+\delta_{ai,cj}\delta_{ab})R^{ac}_{ij}R^{bc}_{ij}.

(33)

The eigenvectors of
\bm{M}
and
\bm{N}
correspond to the CNTO transformation
matrices for the occupied and virtual CNTOs, respectively.
The CNTOs whose eigenvalues sum up to a certain cutoff are chosen as active space

\displaystyle 1-\xi_{M}

\displaystyle<\sum_{o}\lambda^{M}_{o}

(34)

\displaystyle 1-\xi_{N}

\displaystyle<\sum_{v}\lambda^{N}_{v}

(35)

where
\lambda^{M}_{o}
and
\lambda^{N}_{v}
are the eigenvalues of
\bm{M}
and
\bm{N}
.
To obtain the most compact basis, separate CNTO bases for each excited state
would be preferable.
However,
due to the non-orthogonality of the orbitals,
subsequent calculation of transition moments between excited states would be complicated.
Therefore,
we choose a state averaged approach,

\bm{M}=\frac{1}{n_{ES}}\sum^{n_{ES}}_{i}\bm{M}_{i},\quad\bm{N}=\frac{1}{n_{ES}}\sum^{n_{ES}}_{i}\bm{N}_{i},

(36)

where
\bm{M}_{i}
and
\bm{N}_{i}
are constructed according to eq (
32
)
and (
33
) for the
i
-th excited state
and

is the number of excited states included in the matrices.

3
Implementation

The closed shell MLCC3 ground and excited states as well as
EOM transition properties have been implemented in the
e^{T}
program package.
Folkestad et al. 2020

One of the advantages of MLCC3 compared to other reduced cost methods is
that only the space,
in which the triples amplitudes are defined,
is restricted.
Therefore,
we can split the occupied and virtual orbitals into active
and inactive subsets,
and use almost identical code for MLCC3 as for full CC3.
The algorithms employed to calculate closed shell CC3 properties
in
e^{T}
have been detailed in Ref.
16

and only a short summary will be given in this paper.
The ground state residual,
\bm{\Omega}
,
and the transformations of a trial vector with the Jacobian
are computed in a restricted loop over the occupied indices
i\geq j\geq k
.
An
n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}
-block of triples amplitudes is constructed for a given
set of indices
\{i,j,k\}
.
Using this structure, the permutational symmetry of the triples amplitudes
can be exploited,
while utilizing efficient matrix multiplication routines
for the contractions of the block of virtual orbitals.

Rendell et al. 1991
;
Matthews et al. 2013
;
Matthews and Stanton 2015

By reformulating the equations in terms of contravariant triples amplitudes

\tilde{\tau}^{abc}_{ijk}=4\tau^{abc}_{ijk}-2\tau^{bac}_{jik}-2\tau^{cba}_{kji}-2\tau^{acb}_{ikj}+\tau^{cab}_{kij}+\tau^{bca}_{jki},

(37)

and residuals,

\tilde{\Omega}
,
the number of memory-bound reordering operations is reduced.
After all contributions to the contravariant residual are collected
it is converted back to the covariant form,
using the relations

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}^{A}_{I}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{A}_{I}

(38)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}^{AB}_{IJ}

\displaystyle=2\Omega^{AB}_{IJ}-\Omega^{BA}_{IJ},\quad\Omega^{AB}_{IJ}=\frac{1}{3}(2\tilde{\Omega}^{AB}_{IJ}+\tilde{\Omega}^{BA}_{IJ}).

(39)

As in CC3,
the
\tau_{3}
amplitudes are defined in terms of the
\tau_{2}
amplitudes

\tau^{abc}_{ijk}=-(\varepsilon^{abc}_{ijk})^{-1}P^{abc}_{ijk}\Big(\sum_{D}\tau^{aD}_{ij}g_{bDck}-\sum_{L}\tau^{ab}_{iL}g_{Ljck}\Big).

(40)

However,
because the triples determinants are restricted to the active space
only the summation indices in the expression for
\tau_{3}
are over the full space.
Here,

P^{abc}_{ijk}
is a permutation operator creating a sum of all unique permutations
of the index pairs
ai,bj,ck
,
and
g_{pqrs}
are two-electron integrals in the
T_{1}
-trasformed basis.
Helgaker et al. 2014

From eq (
40
) it is evident that the most memory efficient implementation
will make use of two separate arrays for
\tau^{ab}_{iL}
and
\tau^{aD}_{ij}
.
Similarly,
two vectors are needed for the doubles part of the ground state residual
because one index originates from a
T_{1}
-transformed two-electron integral,

g_{pqrs}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}^{ab}_{iL}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\\
jk\end{subarray}}\tilde{\tau}^{abc}_{ijk}g_{jLkc}

(41)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}^{aD}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}bc\\
j\end{subarray}}\tilde{\tau}^{abc}_{ijk}g_{Dbkc},

(42)

Therefore,
the memory requirement and the computational cost of the triples contributions
scale linearly with the full size of the system,
and the overall asymptotic scaling for constructing the ground state residual is

4n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
floating point operations (FLOP).

The triples amplitudes of the right excitation vector can be expressed as

R^{abc}_{ijk}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{abc}_{ijk}-\omega}P^{abc}_{ijk}\Big(\sum_{D}\bar{R}^{aD}_{ij}g_{bDck}-\sum_{L}\bar{R}^{ab}_{iL}g_{Ljck}+\sum_{D}\tau^{aD}_{ij}\Upsilon_{bDck}-\sum_{L}\tau^{ab}_{iL}\Upsilon_{Ljck}\Big)

(43)

where
\Upsilon_{bDck}
and
\Upsilon_{Ljck}
are treated as one-index transformed integrals

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{bDck}=\sum_{E}R^{E}_{k}g_{bDcE}-\sum_{M}\big(R^{b}_{M}g_{MDck}+R^{c}_{m}g_{bDMk}\big)

(44)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{Ljck}=\sum_{E}\big(R^{E}_{j}g_{LEck}+R^{E}_{k}g_{LjcE}\big)-\sum_{M}R^{c}_{M}g_{LjMk}.

(45)

and
\bar{R}^{ab}_{ij}=(1+\delta_{ai,bj})R^{ab}_{ij}
.
Helgaker et al. 2014

From eq (
43
) can be seen that the construction of
R_{3}
is
twice as expensive as the construction of
\tau_{3}
.
For the Jacobian transformation the same terms have to be computed
as for the ground state residual,
but
R_{3}
is contracted instead of
\tau_{3}
.
Additionally,
the
\tau_{3}
amplitudes are required for a single term
leading to an overall asymptotic scaling of
8n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP.
It should be noted that the construction of
\Upsilon_{bDck}

scales quadratically with the full system size.
However,
this term will not be significant compared to the
other terms in the Jacobian transformation.

The transpose Jacobian transformation also scales with
8n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP,
as the
L_{3}
and
\tau_{3}
amplitudes need to be constructed and two contractions,
each scaling as
2n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP, are needed.
The final contractions contributing to the singles part of the transformed vector
contains terms that scale quadratically with the full size of the system.
However,
these terms scale at most as
2n_{\mathrm{V}}n_{\mathrm{O}}n^{2}_{\mathrm{v}}n_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP and are therefore
negligible compared to full CCSD.

To obtain core excited states core-valence separation is employed,
where all non-zero elements of both the trial vector and the transformed vector
need to contain at least one index corresponding to a core orbital.

Coriani and Koch 2015
;
Myhre et al. 2016
;
Paul et al. 2021

Therefore,
in this implementation of the Jacobian transformations,
we skip iterations in the loop over
i,j,k

if all indices correspond to valence orbitals.
This reduces the scaling for both Jacobian transformations to
8n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{2}_{\mathrm{o}}
.

As in the full CC3 code the EOM transition densities are constructed in
a loop over the occupied indices and another loop over the virtual indices.
We calculate all contributions to the density in a loop over the occupied indices,
except for one contribution to the occupied-occupied block of the density
which cannot be efficiently calculated in a loop over
i,j,k
.

D^{m\text{-}0}_{kl}\mathrel{-}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}abc\\
ij\end{subarray}}\tilde{L}^{abc}_{ijl}\tau^{abc}_{ijk}

(46)

As shown in eq (
46
) for the occupied-occupied block of the left transition density,
the triples amplitudes that are contracted differ in the occupied indices.
Therefore,
a triples loop over the virtual indices has to be used,
in order to exploit the permutational symmetry of the triples amplitudes.
This leads to an increase in contractions scaling as
2n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP.
However,
the triples amplitudes have to be reconstructed for the loop over
a,b,c

which also leads to a larger prefactor in the scaling.
While the contractions inside the triple loops scale linearly with
the full system size,
there exists one term in the right transition density,
\tilde{D}^{0\text{-}m}
,
that requires storing a subblock of
\tau_{2}
scaling as
n_{\mathrm{V}}n_{\mathrm{O}}n_{\mathrm{v}}n_{\mathrm{o}}

in memory.
This is, however, not an issue as CCSD is used as lower level method
where the full
\tau_{2}
array scaling as
n^{2}_{\mathrm{V}}n^{2}_{\mathrm{O}}
needs to be kept in memory.

Because the triples amplitudes have to be calculated twice
the overall scaling to construct a single
D^{m\text{-}0}
amounts to
10n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP.
The construction of a single
\tilde{D}^{0\text{-}m}
totals
16n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}}
FLOP,
as the
R_{3}
amplitudes are twice as expensive as the
L_{3}
,
and also the
\tau_{3}
and
\lambda_{3}
amplitudes are required.
For transition moments from the ground state,
these densities only need to be computed once per state,
compared to the iterative cost (per state) for the Jacobian transformations.

4
Results and Discussion

With the MLCC3 method, we can obtain
excitation energies and oscillator strengths
of CC3 quality at significantly reduced cost.
We compare the MLCC3 results for oxygen core excitations of guanine to the CC3
results.
The scaling with the size of the inactive space is shown for formaldehyde
with up to six explicit water molecules.
To show the capabilities of the method,
the UV/VIS spectrum of azobenzene
and a core excited state of betaine 30 with more than 1000 molecular orbitals
are reported.

In Table
2
we report speed up compared to CC3.
For terms scaling as
\mathcal{O}(n_{\mathrm{V}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}n^{3}_{\mathrm{o}})
the speed up is calculated as,

S^{GS}=\frac{t^{it}_{CC3}}{t^{it}_{MLCC3}}\qquad S^{GS}_{theo}=\frac{(n_{\mathrm{O}}\times n_{\mathrm{V}})^{3}}{(n_{\mathrm{o}}\times n_{\mathrm{v}})^{3}},

(47)

while for core excited states the reduction in the scaling is given by,

S^{ES}=\frac{t^{it}_{CC3}}{t^{it}_{MLCC3}}\qquad S^{ES}_{theo}=\frac{n^{2}_{\mathrm{O}}\times n^{3}_{\mathrm{V}}}{n^{2}_{\mathrm{o}}\times n^{3}_{\mathrm{v}}}.

(48)

It should be noted that only the dominating terms are included in this estimate,
but terms with a lower scaling can be significant,
especially for small active spaces.
With an active space of 15 occupied orbitals a speed up of about 90 can be reached,
while the deviation from the CC3 results is below 0.2 eV.

As we pursue a state-averaged approach in the determination of the active space,
the performance is expected to deteriorate somewhat when more states are considered.

Azobenzene

In the aug-cc-pVDZ basis, azobenzene has 48 occupied and 364 virtual orbitals.

a CC3 calculation of 10 excited states is costly.

By using an active space containing 34 occupied and 238 virtual orbitals,
the time per iteration of the ground state equations reduced to 36 minutes.

5
Conclusion

The multilevel CC3 method provides a framework,
with which intensive molecular properties can be calculated
at an accuracy approaching that of the CC3 method.
For sufficiently large inactive spaces the computational cost
will tend towards that of CCSD.
Compared to Cholesky PAOs,
CNTOs provide smaller orbital spaces without sacrificing accuracy.
However,
the cost of constructing CNTOs is significant,
as the CCSD ground and excited state equations need to be solved.

There is some ambiguity regarding the selection of the active space using CNTOs.
We can either specify the number of occupied and virtual orbitals explicitly
or use a cutoff,
\xi
,
and include the orbitals whose eigenvalues sum up to
1-\xi
.
The first approach gives great flexibility,
but several calculations are typically needed to confirm that the excitation
energies actually converged.
Using a cutoff on the other hand is a more blackbox approach,
as
\xi=10^{-4}
gives accurate results,
but the active spaces can become larger than required.
Further benchmarking, especially on larger systems,
is needed to obtain a rule of thumb for the selection of an active space.

Two bottlenecks were identified that limit the size of the systems we can treat:
First, the convergence behaviour of the solvers is diminished,
due to the change of the orbital basis.
The start guess could be improved by transforming the CCSD amplitudes
from canonical MOs to the CNTO basis.

For large systems with several hundered to a thousand MOs,
CCSD becomes a bottleneck and
another layer could be introduced at the CCS level of theory.
For the multilevel CC3 model with CC3 in CCSD in CCS,
it has to be investigated how the orbital space is set up effectively,
as NTOs obtained from CCS will not provide a suitable active space.
One possibility could be the approximated CNTOs introduced by Baudin and Kristensen,
or CNTOs obtained from a MLCCSD calculation.

OD-Characterization of Some Simple Unitary Groups
Majid Akbari, Xiaoyou Chen, Faisal Hassani, Ali Reza Moghaddamfar

Abstract
The degree pattern of a finite group is the degree sequence of its prime graph in ascending order of vertices. We say that the problem of OD-characterization is solved for a finite group if we determine the number of pairwise nonisomorphic finite groups with the same order and degree pattern as the group under consideration. In this article the problem of OD-characterization is solved for some simple unitary groups. It was shown, in particular, that the simple unitary groups U 3 ​ ( q ) U_{3}(q) and U 4 ​ ( q ) U_{4}(q) are OD-characterizable, where q q is a prime power < 10 2 <10^{2} .

The concept of the prime graph was first introduced by Gruenberg and Kegel. During their study on prime graph, they showed that a finite group with disconnected prime graph is either a Frobenius group or a
2

Proposition 2.1
(Gruenberg and Kegel)

(
[

]
, Theorem A)

If

, then one of the following occurs:

(1)

s(G)=2
,
G
is a Frobenius group.

(2)

s(G)=2
,
G=ABC
, where

and
AB
are normal subgroups of
G

B
is a normal subgroup of

and

(3)

s(G)\geqslant 2
, there exists a simple group

for some nilpotent normal
\pi_{1}(G)
-subgroup

is a
\pi_{1}(G)
-group. Moreover,
{\rm GK}(P)
is disconnected,

, and for every

A

In

(Suzuki)

(

]
, Theorem B)
Let

are cliques.

Now, suppose

{\rm GK}(G)={\rm GK}[\pi_{1}]\oplus K_{n_{2}}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{n_{s}},

(1)

where

{\rm GK}(G)[\pi_{1}(G)]

The Structure of
U_{n}(q)

We collect here some information about the unitary groups over a finite field.
The general unitary group
{\rm GU}_{n}(q)
is defined as

{\rm GU}_{n}(q)=\{A\in{\rm GL}_{n}(q^{2})\ |\ A\bar{A}^{T}=I_{n}\},

which is a subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{n}(q^{2})
. Here
\bar{A}
denotes the matrix obtained from
A
by raising each entry to the
q
th power.
The special unitary group
{\rm SU}_{n}(q)
is the subgroup
of all matrices in
{\rm GU}_{n}(q)
with determinant
1
.
The orders of
{\rm GU}_{n}(q)
and
{\rm SU}_{n}(q)
are, respectively,

|{\rm GU}_{n}(q)|=q^{n\choose 2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(q^{i}-(-1)^{i})\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ |{\rm SU}_{n}(q)|=q^{n\choose 2}\prod_{i=2}^{n}(q^{i}-(-1)^{i}).

The center
Z
of
{\rm SU}_{n}(q)
is the set of scalar matrices
\lambda I
for which both
\lambda^{n}=1
and
\lambda^{q+1}=1
.
There are exactly
d=(n,q+1)
such
\lambda
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and so the subgroup
Z
has
order
d
. The quotient
{\rm PSU}_{n}(q)={\rm SU}_{n}(q)/Z

is called the
projective special unitary group
, sometimes written by
U_{n}(q)
, and its order is

|U_{n}(q)|=\frac{|{\rm SU}_{n}(q)|}{d}=\frac{1}{d}q^{n\choose 2}\prod_{i=2}^{n}(q^{i}-(-1)^{i}).

(2)

The projective special unitary group
U_{n}(q)
is usually a simple group,
however the exceptions are
U_{2}(2)
,
U_{2}(3)
and
U_{3}(2)
.
It is a standard result that the groups

and

are isomorphic.

B n − 1 B_{n-1} -orbits on the flag variety and the Bruhat graph of the symmetric group
Mark Colarusso, Sam Evens

Abstract
. Let G = G n = G ​ L ​ ( n ) G=G_{n}=GL(n) be the n × n n\times n complex general linear group and embed G n − 1 = G ​ L ​ ( n − 1 ) G_{n-1}=GL(n-1) in the top left hand corner of G G . The standard Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices B n − 1 B_{n-1} of G n − 1 G_{n-1} acts on the flag variety ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} of G G with finitely many orbits. In this paper, we show that each B n − 1 B_{n-1} -orbit is the intersection of orbits of two Borel subgroups of G G acting on ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} . This allows us to give a new combinatorial description of the B n − 1 B_{n-1} -orbits on ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} by associating to each orbit a pair of Weyl group elements. The closure relations for the B n − 1 B_{n-1} -orbits can then be understood in terms of the Bruhat order on the symmetric group, and the Richardson-Springer monoid action on the orbits can be understood in terms of a well-understood monoid action on the symmetric group. This approach makes the closure relation more transparent than in [ Mag05 ] and the monoid action significantly more computable than in our papers [ CE22 ] and [ CE23 ] , and also allows us to obtain new information about the orbits including a simple formula for the dimension of an orbit.

1.
Introduction

Let
G=G_{n}=GL(n)
be the
n\times n
complex general linear group, and let
B_{n-1}
be the standard Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices of

G_{n-1}
embedded in the upper left corner of
G
. In this paper, we give a new combinatorial description of the

B_{n-1}
-orbits on the flag variety
\mathcal{B}_{n}
of
G
by associating to each orbit a pair of elements
in the Weyl group
W
of
G
, which is identified with the symmetric group. By the Bruhat graph for the symmetric group, we mean the graph whose nodes are given by elements of
W
, and where there is an edge between two nodes if one node corresponds to a Schubert cell that is codimension one in the closure of the Schubert cell corresponding to the other node. As a consequence, the closure relations studied in
[
Mag05
]
and the monoid action studied in our previous papers (
[
CE22
]
,
[
CE23
]
) can be studied using the well-known structure of the Bruhat graph. In particular, they can be deduced from the corresponding Bruhat order relation and monoid actions in the Weyl group.

In more detail, let
B=B_{n}\subset G
be the standard Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices stabilizing
the standard flag
\mathcal{E}_{+}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Let
B^{*}
be the Borel subgroup stabilizing the flag
\mathcal{E}^{*}=(V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\dots\subset V_{n-1}\subset V_{n})
, where
V_{i}=\mbox{span}\{e_{n},\,e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1}\}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. For
Q
a
B_{n-1}
-orbit, the Bruhat decomposition implies
B\cdot Q=B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})
and
B^{*}\cdot Q=B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
for unique elements
w,\,u^{*}\in W
. The first result of this paper proves that the
B_{n-1}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
are exactly the nonempty intersections of
B
and
B^{*}
-orbits.
More precisely,

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
2.4
, Corollary
2.5
, and Corollary
2.12
)
The map
\mathcal{S}h:B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\to W\times W
given by

(1.1)

\mathcal{S}h(Q):=(w,u^{*})\mbox{ where }B\cdot Q=B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})\mbox{ and }B^{*}\cdot Q=B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})

is injective. Further, if
\mathcal{S}h(Q)=(w,u^{*})
, then

Q=B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})\cap B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
, and the image of
\mathcal{S}h
consists of all pairs
(w,u^{*})
such that

B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})\cap B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
is nonempty.

This result was suggested to us by John Shareshian. We call a pair of Weyl group elements
(w,u^{*})\in W\times W
with the property that
B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})\cap B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})\neq\emptyset
a
Shareshian pair
. We denote the set of all Shareshian pairs by
\mathcal{S}p\subset W\times W
. Theorem
1.1
implies that the map
\mathcal{S}h:B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}p
in (
1.1
) is bijective. We refer to the map
\mathcal{S}h
as the
Shareshian map
. In Proposition
2.14
, we characterize
\mathcal{S}p
, and verify that the classification of orbits by Shareshian pairs is equivalent to a classification described by Magyar
[
Mag05
]
. It is also equivalent to an earlier description of orbits given by Hashimoto
[
Has04
]
.

The set of all Shareshian pairs comes equipped with a natural partial order
\leq
which is the restriction of the product of Bruhat orders on
W\times W
, where in the second factor the Bruhat order is the one defined by the simple reflections

(1.2)

S^{*}=\{s_{1}^{*},\dots,s_{n-1}^{*}\}\mbox{ with }s_{1}^{*}=(1,n),\mbox{ and }s_{i}^{*}=(i-1,i)\mbox{ for }i=2,\dots,n-1.

The second basic result of this paper asserts that
we can describe the closure relations on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
using the Bruhat order on Shareshian pairs.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem
3.4
)
Let
Q,\,Q^{\prime}\in B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
and let
\mathcal{S}h(Q)
and
\mathcal{S}h(Q^{\prime})
denote the corresponding Shareshian Pairs. Then

Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q}\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{S}h(Q^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{S}h(Q).

This description of the closure relations seems to us considerably more transparent than the description of the closure relations on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
previously given by Magyar in his paper
[
Mag05
]
. See Example
4.15
to see the utility of this perspective. However, our proof relies heavily on the main theorem from
[
Mag05
]
.

In
[
CE22
]
and
[
CE23
]
, we studied an

action on

by simple roots of

and
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which extended a previous action by roots of

discussed in Hashimoto’s work
[

]
.
For a simple root

{\mathfrak{k}}
or

and

-orbit

, we let

denote the monoid action of

the left action and the action by roots of

The Weyl group

has well-studied left and right monoid actions by simple roots of

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorem

The Shareshian map in Equation (

-orbits on

Moreover, each simple root of

]

]
.
See Example

The description of the extended monoid action on

Theorem 1.4
.

(Theorem

)
Let

\mathcal{S}h(Q)=(w,u^{*})
and let

be the

-cycle

\sigma=(n,n-1,\dots,1)
. Then

where
|u^{*}\sigma w^{-1}|
denotes the order of the element

in the group
W
, and
\ell(u^{*})
is the length of
u^{*}
with respect to the set of simple reflections
S^{*}
given in (

).

We note that we proved in
[
CE23
]
that the closure ordering on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
is the so-called standard order of Richardson and Springer discussed in
[
RS90
]
and this result is used in order to prove the above Theorem. This paper is part of a larger project. Indeed, the orbits in

are characterized as the
B
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
satisfying a genericity condition for the second factor. In later work, we plan to give a similar description for all
B
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, and to establish further results on the geometry of orbit closures. In particular, we would like to establish links between our results and the very interesting paper of Travkin
[
Tra09
]
. It would also be interesting to understand the connection between this project and the work of Gandini and Pezzini
[
GP18
]
.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we prove that each
B_{n-1}
-orbit on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is the intersection of a
B
-orbit and a

B^{*}
-orbit, and show that our classification is equivalent to the classification using decorated permutations in
[

]
. In Section

, we prove that if

are two orbits in

and

in the Bruhat order on

. In

from the type of the corresponding

-orbits. We then use this result to compute our formula for the dimension of an orbit

in

2.
Parameterizations of

-orbits on

by Proposition

(1).

On the other hand, suppose

2.3
). Let
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=(\tilde{v}_{1}\subset\tilde{v}_{2}\subset\dots\subset\tilde{v}_{n})
, and let
\mathcal{F}^{*}=(v_{1}^{*}\subset v_{2}^{*}\subset\dots\subset v_{n}^{*})
. Suppose that

is the first index such that
\tilde{v}_{i_{k}}\neq v_{i_{k}}^{*}
. By Equations (
2.3
)-(
2.5
),
\tilde{v}_{i_{k}}=e_{n}
and
v_{i_{k}}^{*}=e_{j_{k}}
, and the vector
v_{i_{k}}
for
\mathcal{F}
is
{\hat{e}}_{j_{k}}

\tilde{v}_{i_{k-1}}=e_{j_{k}}
, then
v_{i_{k-1}}={\hat{e}}_{j_{k-1}}
where

\ell

)-(

) imply that

\mathcal{G}
is another flag in standard form
with

and
\mathcal{G}^{*}=\mathcal{F}^{*}
, then
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}
. Thus, the map

\Phi
in (

Remark 2.9
.

The map
\mathcal{S}h
from

to geometric Shareshian pairs given by

We call the map

, and we can describe it combinatorially as follows. By the Bruhat decomposition, we can write any geometric Shareshian pair as
(B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+}),B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*}))

.

Definition 2.10
.

The Shareshian map is given by:

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{S}h:B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\to W\times W;\;\mathcal{S}h(B_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F})=(w,u^{*})\mbox{ where }\mathcal{F}\mbox{ is in standard form and }\\
&\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=w(\mathcal{E}_{+})\mbox{ and }\mathcal{F}^{*}=u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})\mbox{ are the flags given in Notation \ref{n:tildeandstarflags}.}\end{split}

Notation 2.11
.

We refer to a pair of Weyl group
elements
(w,y)\in W\times W
such that

for some

, as a

. We denote the subset of

.

The following statement is immediate from Remark

.

Corollary 2.12
.

The Shareshian map
\mathcal{S}h:B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{S}p
is bijective.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
Q=\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F}\in B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
with
\mathcal{F}
a flag in standard form and with
\mathcal{S}h(Q)=(w,u^{*})
. Then it follows from definitions that
B\cdot Q=B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})
and
B^{*}\cdot Q=B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
. Thus,
(w,u^{*})
is a Shareshian pair if and only if
B\cdot w(\mathcal{E}_{+})\cap B^{*}\cdot u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})\neq\emptyset
.

We now describe necessary and sufficient conditions for a pair of Weyl group
elements
(w,y)\in W\times W
to be a Shareshian pair, after first introducing some notation. Let
\Delta=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}<n\}
be a subsequence of
\{1,\dots,n\}

containing
n
and let
\tau_{\Delta}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
be the
k+1
-cycle

\tau_{\Delta}:=(n,j_{k},\dots,j_{2},j_{1})
. Recall the
n
-cycle
\sigma=(n,n-1,\dots,2,1)\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
from Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 2.14
.

A pair of Weyl group elements

(w,y)\in W\times W
is a Shareshian pair if and only if there exists a subsequence
\Delta=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}<n\}
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
containing
n
such that:

(1)

y=\tau_{\Delta}w\sigma^{-1}
and

(2)

\Delta
is a decreasing sequence for
w^{-1}
, i.e.,
w^{-1}(n)<w^{-1}(j_{k})<\dots<w^{-1}(j_{1}).

Proof.

Suppose first that
Q\in B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
. We claim that
\mathcal{S}h(Q)=(w,u^{*})
satisfies conditions (1) and (2) of the
Proposition. Let
\mathcal{F}\in Q
be the unique flag in standard form in the orbit
Q
. First, suppose that

\mathcal{F}
contains no hat vectors, so that
\mathcal{F}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}^{*}
by (1) of Proposition
2.7
. Let
\Delta=\{n\}
, so
\tau_{\Delta}=id
. Since
\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{E}^{*})=\mathcal{E_{+}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=w(\mathcal{E}_{+})
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}=u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})
, it follows that
u^{*}=w\sigma^{-1}
, and condition (2) is automatic.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{F}
contains hat vectors. Since
\mathcal{F}
is in standard form, it is given by (
2.3
), so that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}
are given by Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
)
respectively. Let
\Delta:=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}<n\}
, so that
\Delta
consists of the indices of the hat
vectors in
\mathcal{F}
along with
n
.
From Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we see that

\mathcal{F}^{*}=u^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})=\tau_{\Delta}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\tau_{\Delta}w(\mathcal{E}_{+}).

Since
\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{E}^{*})=\mathcal{E}_{+}
, it follows that
u^{*}=\tau_{\Delta}w\sigma^{-1}
. From (
2.4
), we see
w(i_{k})=n,\,w(i_{s})=j_{s+1}
for
s=1,\dots,k-1
, and
w(p)=j_{1}
with
i_{k}<i_{k-1}<\dots<i_{1}<p
, so condition (2) follows.

Conversely, suppose that
(w,y)\in W\times W
satisfies the conditions of (1) and (2) in the statement of the Proposition.
Let
\Delta=\{j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}<n\}
. By condition (2), we have
w^{-1}(n)<w^{-1}(j_{k})<\dots<w^{-1}(j_{1})
.
Let
w^{-1}(n)=i_{k}
,
w^{-1}(j_{k})=i_{k-1},\dots w^{-1}(j_{2})=i_{1}
, and
w^{-1}(j_{1})=p
. Let
\mathcal{G}:=w(\mathcal{E}_{+})
.
Then

\begin{split}&\mathcal{G}=(e_{w(1)}\subset\dots\subset e_{w(i_{k}-1)}\subset\underbrace{e_{n}}_{i_{k}}\subset e_{w(i_{k}+1)}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{j_{k}}}_{i_{k-1}}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{j_{2}}}_{i_{1}}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{j_{1}}}_{p}\subset\\
&\subset e_{w(p+1)}\subset\dots\subset e_{w(n)}),\end{split}

and it follows that

\begin{split}&\tau_{\Delta}(\mathcal{G})=(e_{w(1)}\subset\dots\subset e_{w(i_{k-1})}\subset\underbrace{e_{j_{k}}}_{i_{k}}\subset e_{w(i_{k}+1)}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{j_{k-1}}}_{i_{k-1}}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{j_{1}}}_{i_{1}}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{n}}_{p}\subset\\
&\subset e_{w(p+1)}\subset\dots\subset e_{w(n)}).\end{split}

Now let
\mathcal{F}
be the flag in standard form:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}=(e_{w(1)}\subset\dots\subset e_{w(i_{k}-1)}\subset\underbrace{\hat{e}_{j_{k}}}_{i_{k}}\subset e_{w(i_{k}+1)}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{\hat{e}_{j_{k-1}}}_{i_{k-1}}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{\hat{e}_{j_{1}}}_{i_{1}}\subset\dots\subset\underbrace{e_{n}}_{p}\subset\\
&\subset e_{w(p+1)}\subset\dots\subset e_{w(n)}),\end{split}

Then it follows from Proposition
2.7
that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}^{*}=\tau_{\Delta}(\mathcal{G})
.
But
\mathcal{G}=w(\mathcal{E}_{+})
and

\tau_{\Delta}(\mathcal{G})=\tau_{\Delta}w(\mathcal{E}_{+})=\tau_{\Delta}w\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal{E}^{*}).

Thus,
\mathcal{S}h(B_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F})=(w,\tau_{\Delta}w\sigma^{-1})=(w,y)
by condition (1).
Thus,
(w,y)\in\mathcal{S}p
as asserted.

Q.E.D.

3.
Closure Relations on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
and the Bruhat ordering on Shareshian Pairs

In this section, we prove the second main result of the paper, Theorem
1.2
, which states that the closure ordering on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
is given by the Bruhat ordering on Shareshian pairs by using a related result of Magyar.
Consider a
B_{n-1}
-orbit
Q=B_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, where

\mathcal{F}=(V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\dots\subset V_{n-1}\subset V_{n})

with
\dim(V_{i})=i.

Recall the standard flag

\mathcal{E}_{+}=(E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\dots\subset E_{n-1}\subset E_{n})

with
E_{i}=\mbox{span}\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and

\mathcal{E}^{*}=(E_{1}^{*}\subset E_{2}^{*}\subset\dots\subset E_{n-1}^{*}\subset E_{n}^{*})

with
E_{i}^{*}=\mbox{span}\{e_{n},e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{i-1}\}
from the Introduction. Let
L_{n}=\mbox{${\mathbb{C}}$}e_{n}.

We associate to
Q
the following invariants:

(3.1)

r_{p,q}(Q)=\dim(V_{p}\cap E_{q}),\ \ r^{*}_{p,q}(Q)=\dim(V_{p}\cap E_{q}^{*}),\ \ p,q=1,\dots,n

and

(3.2)

\delta_{p,q}(Q)=\dim(L_{n}\cap(V_{p}+E_{q})),\ \ \overline{r}_{p,q}(Q)=r_{p,q}(Q)+\delta_{p,q}(Q),\ \ p,q=0,\dots,n.

Since
B_{n-1}
fixes the flags
\mathcal{E}_{+}
and
\mathcal{E}^{*}
and the line
L_{n}
, it follows that these invariants depend only on
Q
and not on the flag

\mathcal{F}\in Q.

Remark 3.1
.

Magyar associates invariants
r_{p,q}
and
\overline{r}_{p,q}
to a
G
-orbit on

\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
as follows. For a line
A
and flags
B_{\bullet}
and
C_{\bullet}
with
B_{i}
and
C_{i}
the
i
-dimensional subspaces of
B_{\bullet}
and
C_{\bullet}
respectively, Magyar defines
r_{p,q}(B_{\bullet},C_{\bullet},A)=\dim(B_{p}\cap C_{q})
and
{\overline{r}}_{p,q}(B_{\bullet},C_{\bullet},A)=r_{p,q}(B_{\bullet},C_{\bullet},A)+\dim(A\cap(B_{p}+C_{q})).
Applying the equivalences
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\leftrightarrow B\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{O}_{n})\hookrightarrow G\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathcal{B}_{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
is equivalent to requiring
B_{\bullet}=\mathcal{E}_{+}
and
A=L_{n}.
It follows that Magyar’s invariants equal the invariants
r_{p,q}(Q)
and
\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q)
given in Equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) respectively. Since the equivalences preserve closure relations, we can apply Magyar’s resullts in our setting.

Let
S^{\prime}=B\cdot\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(V_{1}^{\prime}\subset V_{2}^{\prime}\subset\dots\subset V_{n-1}^{\prime}\subset V_{n}^{\prime}),

and let
S=B\cdot\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{F}
as above. Recall the standard fact

(3.3)

S^{\prime}\subset\overline{S}\iff\dim(V_{p}^{\prime}\cap E_{q})\geq\dim(V_{p}\cap E_{q}),\,p,q=1,\dots,n-1

from Proposition 7 in Section 10.5 of
[
Ful97
]
. By changing the standard basis, it follows that if
S^{\prime,*}=B^{*}\cdot\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
S^{*}=B^{*}\cdot\mathcal{F}

(3.4)

S^{\prime,*}\subset\overline{S^{*}}\iff\dim(V_{p}^{\prime}\cap E_{q}^{*})\geq\dim(V_{p}\cap E_{q}^{*}),\,p,q=1,\dots,n-1.

Theorem 3.2
.

(
[
Mag05
]
, Theorem 2.2)
Let
Q^{\prime}=B_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and let
Q=B_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F}
. Then

Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q}
if and only if

(i)
r_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq r_{p,q}(Q)
for
p,q=1,\dots,n-1
, and

(ii)
\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q)
for
p,q=0,\dots,n-1
.

The preceding result is a restatement of a criterion due to Magyar using Remark
3.1
. The next result is used to relate Magyar’s criterion to closure relations for
B^{*}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
Q
be a
B_{n-1}
-orbit on
\mathcal{B}_{n}.
Then
r^{*}_{p,q+1}(Q)=\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q)
for
p,q=0,\dots,n-1.

Proof.

Since
E_{q}^{*}=\mbox{span}\{e_{n},e_{1},\dots,e_{q-1}\},
it follows that

(3.5)

E_{q}^{*}=E_{q-1}+L_{n}.

Let
Q=B_{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{F}
and let
V_{p}
be the
p
-th subspace in the flag
\mathcal{F}
.
By the second isomorphism theorem for modules,

(3.6)

L_{n}/(L_{n}\cap(V_{p}+E_{q}))\cong(L_{n}+V_{p}+E_{q})/(V_{p}+E_{q}).

It follows that

(3.7)

\dim(L_{n})-\dim(L_{n}\cap(V_{p}+E_{q}))=\dim(L_{n}+V_{p}+E_{q})-\dim(V_{p}+E_{q})

so that by Equation (
3.5
)

(3.8)

1-\delta_{p,q}(Q)=\dim(V_{p}+E_{q+1}^{*})-\dim(V_{p}+E_{q}).

Note that

(3.9)

\dim(V_{p}+E_{q})=p+q-\dim(V_{p}\cap E_{q})=p+q-r_{p,q}(Q)

and similarly

(3.10)

\dim(V_{p}+E_{q+1}^{*})=p+q+1-r^{*}_{p,q+1}(Q).

By Equations (
3.8
), (
3.9
), and (
3.10
), we conclude that

r^{*}_{p,q+1}(Q)=\delta_{p,q}(Q)+r_{p,q}(Q)=\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q),

which verifies the Proposition.

Q.E.D.

Recall the map
\mathcal{S}h:B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\to W\times W
from Equation (
2.8
) and the Bruhat order on Shareshian pairs given by the restriction of the product of Bruhat orders on

, where in the second factor the Bruhat order is the one defined with respect to the simple reflections

) corresponding to the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{*}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
be
B_{n-1}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Then

Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q}\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{S}h(Q^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{S}h(Q).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q}
if and only if
r_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq r_{p,q}(Q)
for
p,q=1,\dots,n-1
and

\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q)
for
p,q=0,\dots,n-1
. By Proposition
3.3
,

\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq\overline{r}_{p,q}(Q)
if and only if

r^{*}_{p,q+1}(Q^{\prime})\geq r^{*}_{p,q+1}(Q)
for
p,q=0,\dots,n-1.
By
Equations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
),
r_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq r_{p,q}(Q)
and
r^{*}_{p,q}(Q^{\prime})\geq r^{*}_{p,q}(Q)
for
p,\,q=1,\dots,n
if and only if
B\cdot Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{B\cdot Q}
and
B^{*}\cdot Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{B^{*}\cdot Q},

and this last condition is equivalent to
\mathcal{S}h(Q^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{S}h(Q).

Q.E.D.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
Q\in B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
with
Q_{B}=B\cdot Q
and
Q_{B^{*}}=B^{*}\cdot Q
, so
that
Q=Q_{B}\cap Q_{B^{*}}
by Remark
2.9
. Then

\overline{Q}=\overline{Q_{B}}\cap\overline{Q_{B^{*}}}
.

Proof.

In the notation of Definition
2.8
,
\overline{Q}=\overline{Q_{B}\cap Q_{B^{*}}}\subset\overline{Q_{B}}\cap\overline{Q_{B^{*}}}.
Let
Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q_{B}}\cap\overline{Q_{B^{*}}}.
Then
B\cdot Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q_{B}}
and
B^{*}\cdot Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q_{B^{*}}}.
Hence,

\mathcal{S}h(Q^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{S}h(Q),
so that
Q^{\prime}\subset\overline{Q}
by Theorem
3.4
, and the Corollary follows.

Q.E.D.

4.
The Monoid Action and Shareshian Pairs

We now show the extended monoid action by simple roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}:=\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}_{n-1}
on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}
studied in
[
CE22
]
and
[
CE23
]
can be computed using a monoid action on the set
\mathcal{S}p
of all Shareshian pairs. We give a formula for the dimension of a
B_{n-1}
-orbit in terms of its Shareshian pair. We begin by recalling the construction of a monoid action on the set of orbits of an algebraic subgroup
M
of an algebraic group
R
acting on the flag variety
\mathcal{B}_{R}
of
R
with finitely many orbits.

4.1.
Background on Monoid actions

For more details on the subsequent material, we refer the reader to
[
RS90
]
,
[
Vog83
]
,
[
CE14
]
,
[
CE22
]
and other sources.
Let
R
be a connected reductive algebraic group, let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{R}
be the flag
variety of
R
, and let
M
be an algebraic subgroup of
R
acting on
\mathcal{B}
with finitely many orbits. Identify
\mathcal{B}\cong R/B_{R}
, for a Borel subgroup
B_{R}\subset R
and let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}=\mbox{Lie}(B_{R})\subset\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}
.
Let
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
be the set of simple roots defined by the Borel subalgebra
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, and let
S_{R}
be the simple reflections of the Weyl group
W
of
R
corresponding to
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
. For
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
be the variety of all parabolic subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{r}}
of type
\alpha
and consider the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi_{\alpha}:\mathcal{B}\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}
. For
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
with corresponding reflection
s=s_{\alpha}\in W
, we define an operator
m(s)
on the set of orbits
M\backslash\mathcal{B}
following the above sources. For
Q_{M}\in M\backslash\mathcal{B}
, let
m(s)*Q_{M}
be the unique
M
-orbit which is open and dense in
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})).
Note that
\pi_{\alpha}:\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))\to\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})
is an
M
-equivariant

\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle. Thus,
\dim(\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M})))=\dim(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))+1,
and since
Q_{M}\subset\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q_{M}))
, it follows that the orbit
Q_{M}=m(s)*Q_{M}
if and only if
\dim(m(s)*Q_{M})=\dim(Q_{M}).

Remark 4.1
.

It follows that
Q_{M}\not=m(s)*Q_{M}
if and only if
\dim(m(s)*Q_{M})=\dim(Q_{M})+1.

Computation of
m(s)*Q_{M}
depends on the
the type of the root

\alpha
for the orbit
Q_{M}
, which is determined as follows. For
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}\in Q_{M}
, let
\mbox{${\mathfrak{p}}$}_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\pi_{\alpha}(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime})
and let
B^{\prime}
and
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be the corresponding parabolic subgroups of
R
, and let
V_{\alpha}^{\prime}
be the the solvable radical of
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.
Consider the group

S_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/V_{\alpha}^{\prime}
isogenous to
SL(2)
and its subgroup

M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}:=(M\cap P_{\alpha}^{\prime})/(M\cap V_{\alpha}^{\prime})
.
An easy argument using base change for the inclusion
Q\to\mathcal{B}_{n}
with respect to the morphism
\mathcal{B}_{n}\to G/P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
gives an identification

(4.1)

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))\cong M\times_{M\cap P_{\alpha}^{\prime}}P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/B^{\prime}.

It follows that
M
-orbits in

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
correspond to
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
-orbits in

P^{\prime}_{\alpha}/B^{\prime}\cong{\mbox{${\mathbb{P}}$}}^{1}.
Thus,
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
has only finitely many orbits on
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}/B^{\prime},
and thus matches one of the 4 cases from Section 4.1 of
[
RS90
]
, which we list below in slightly reorganized form.

Definition 4.2
.

(1) If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is solvable and contains the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of
S_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a complex root for
Q_{M}
. If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q_{M}
and otherwise
\alpha
is complex unstable for
Q_{M}
.

(2) If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=S_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a compact root for
Q_{M}.

(3) Suppose
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is one-dimensional and reductive.
If
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}
, then
\alpha
is called a noncompact root for
Q_{M}
, while if
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}\cdot\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}\not=\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime},
then
\alpha
is called a real root for
Q_{M}.

In
[
RS90
]
, the monoid action is only discussed when
M
is locally the fixed points of an involution of
R
, and the terminology in this definition comes from the action of the involution on root spaces, or more precisely the action of an associated real form of
R.
However, as many authors have observed, including
[
Kno95
]
, the construction works in the same way when
M
has finitely many orbits on the flag variety. It follows easily from arguments in
[
RS94
]
that the monoid action depends only the nature of the groups
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}.
This is the perspective we use in this paper.
It is well-known and easy to prove that the type of the root depends only on the orbit
Q_{M}
and not on the point
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}.
The following Proposition gives more detail in the cases when
m(s)*Q\not=Q.

Proposition 4.3
.

(Lemmas 2.1.4 and 2.4.3 of
[
RS94
]
)
Let
Q\in M\backslash\mathcal{B}
with
Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
and
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
.

(1)

If
\alpha
is complex stable for
Q
, then
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
consists of two
M
-orbits:

\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))=Q\cup M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}},

and
m(s)*Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
.

(2)

If
\alpha
is non-compact for
Q
then
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\pi_{\alpha}(Q))
consists of two (resp. three)
M
-orbits, depending on whether
M_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
is the normalizer of a torus (resp. a torus). The open orbit

m(s)*Q=M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vu_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}}
, where
u_{\alpha}\in R
is the Cayley transform with respect to the root
\alpha
as defined in Equation (41) of
[
CE14
]
and
\dim Q=\dim(M\cdot{\rm Ad}(vs)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{r}}$}})
.

4.2.
Extended Monoid Action on
B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}

Recall that we set
K:=G_{n-1}
. In this subsection, we apply the above construction in the case where
R=K\times G
and
M=K_{\Delta}=\{(x,x):\,x\in K\}
is the diagonal copy of
K
in the product
K\times G
. The discussion above yields a monoid action by the standard simple roots of
\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}\oplus\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}

\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}\oplus\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}=\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}}\sqcup\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}

There is a one-to-one correspondence

(4.2)

B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}\leftrightarrow K_{\Delta}\backslash(\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\times\mathcal{B})\mbox{ given by }Q\leftrightarrow K_{\Delta}\cdot(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{n-1},Q).

For
Q\in B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}

K_{\Delta}
-orbit. Note that

The correspondence in (

on

, which we refer to as the extended monoid action. In more detail, for
v\in G
, let
Q=B_{n-1}\cdot{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}\in B_{n-1}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n}.
Let
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}}.
By Equation (4.1) of
[

]

(4.3)

m(s_{\alpha})*Q\mbox{ is the unique open $B_{n-1}$-orbit in }P^{K}_{\alpha}\cdot{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},

. We refer to this monoid action as “the left monoid action”. Let
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}.
By Equation (4.2) of
[
CE22
]
,

(4.4)

m(s_{\alpha})*Q\mbox{ is the unique open $B_{n-1}$-orbit in }B_{n-1}\cdot{\rm Ad}(vP_{\alpha})\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$},

where
P_{\alpha}\supset B
is the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
determined by the root
\alpha
. We refer to this monoid action as “the right monoid action”.

We describe the groups
(K_{\Delta})_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
for the left and right monoid actions in more detail. First, let
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}},

and as in Subsection
4.1
, let
Q=B_{n-1}\cdot{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}
so that

. Then with
\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}=(\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}_{n-1},{\rm Ad}(v)\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}),
the group

(4.5)

(K_{\Delta})_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=(K_{\Delta}\cap(P^{K}_{\alpha},{\rm Ad}(v)B))/(K_{\Delta}\cap(V_{\alpha}^{K},{\rm Ad}(v)B))\cong(P^{K}_{\alpha}\cap{\rm Ad}(v)B)/(V^{K}_{\alpha}\cap{\rm Ad}(v)B),

-orbit on

Now suppose
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
and for

as above, the corresponding group is

(4.6)

(K_{\Delta})_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}=(K_{\Delta}\cap(B_{n-1},{\rm Ad}(v)P_{\alpha}))/(K_{\Delta}\cap(B_{n-1},{\rm Ad}(v)V_{\alpha}))\cong(B_{n-1}\cap{\rm Ad}(v)P_{\alpha})/(B_{n-1}\cap{\rm Ad}(v)V_{\alpha}),

V_{\alpha}
is the solvable radical of

Further,

corresponds to the open
(B_{n-1}\cap{\rm Ad}(v)P_{\alpha})/(B_{n-1}\cap{\rm Ad}(v)V_{\alpha})
-orbit on
({\rm Ad}(v)P_{\alpha})/({\rm Ad}(v)B).

.

Note that for
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}}
or
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},
the group

is solvable. It follows that

is never a compact root for

(see Definition

(2)) and there must be

orbits in the non-compact case by Proposition

(2).

Remark 4.5
.

The right monoid action also arises by applying the construction from the last subsection to the case where

and

The left monoid action arises from a variant of the construction of the last subsection by using the fibre bundle

4.3.
Monoid action on Shareshian Pairs

As in the last subsection, the general theory in Subsection
4.1
can also be applied in the case where

and
M=G_{\Delta}
is the diagonal copy of
G
in
G\times G
. In this case, by realizing

(4.7)

B\backslash G/B\cong G_{\Delta}\backslash(G/B\times G/B),\ BwB/B\mapsto G_{\Delta}\cdot(B,wB),

and the orbits are indexed by

By applying the orbit correspondence from (

on

), and the right monoid action is given as in (

in both equations replaced by
B
and the role of

in (

that appears in (

m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}BwB/B
is the unique open

-orbit in

BwB/B.

Similar results apply to the right monoid action, except in the conclusion
ws_{\alpha}
replaces

For
\alpha
in the left copy of
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},
and the orbit
Q_{\Delta}
through

(4.8)

\alpha

and the orbit

as above,

(4.9)

corresponds to the open
(G_{\Delta})_{\alpha,\mbox{${\mathfrak{b}}$}^{\prime}}
-orbit in

Using the fact that we can assume

\begin{split}\mbox{(Left Action) }&m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w=w\mbox{ if }\ell(s_{\alpha}w)<\ell(w)\mbox{ and }m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w=s_{\alpha}w\mbox{ if }\ell(s_{\alpha}w)>\ell(w).\\
\mbox{(Right Action) }&m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w=w\mbox{ if }\ell(ws_{\alpha})<\ell(w)\mbox{ and }m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w=ws_{\alpha}\mbox{ if }\ell(ws_{\alpha})>\ell(w).\\
\end{split}

By considering the simple roots

and the simple roots

described in Notation

on the product

as follows. Recall the

be the corresponding simple root of
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}^{*}
. The standard simple roots of

\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}}=\{\alpha_{2}^{*},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}^{*}\}

Definition 4.7
.

Define the
restricted diagonal monoid action
on
(W,S)\times(W,S^{*})
via simple roots
\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}\oplus\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
as follows.

For
(w,u^{*})\in(W,S)\times(W,S^{*})
, define

(4.11)

\begin{split}\mbox{ (Left action)}&\mbox{ For }\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}},\,m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}(w,u^{*})=(m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}w,m(s_{\alpha})*_{L}u^{*}).\\
\mbox{ (Right action)}&\mbox{ For }\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}},m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}(w,u^{*})=(m(s_{\alpha})*_{R}w,m(s_{\alpha^{*}})*_{R}u^{*}),\end{split}

where the monoid actions

and

are given in Equation (
4.10

Henceforth, we will drop the subscripts

and

on the monoid actions defined in Equation (

\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}\oplus\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
acts on the left whenever
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{k}}$}}
and on the right if
\alpha\in\Pi_{\mbox{${\mathfrak{g}}$}}
. We now arrive at the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

The Shareshian map

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{j+1}
with

{\mathfrak{g}}

Since

and

\alpha
is complex unstable for

(W,S^{*})
, we
introduce a new total order
\prec
on the set

by declaring

The simple reflection
s_{\alpha^{*}}=\sigma s_{\alpha}\sigma^{-1}=\epsilon_{\sigma(j)}-\epsilon_{\sigma(j+1)}

Proof.

Let

]

is also

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}_{i}}=\mathcal{F}_{i}^{*}

. Further, Remark

, and

Now suppose that
\dim Q=k>0

]

Definition 4.13
.

Let

and let

(W,S)\times(W,S)
.

\widetilde{Sh}(Q^{\prime})\leq\widetilde{Sh}(Q)

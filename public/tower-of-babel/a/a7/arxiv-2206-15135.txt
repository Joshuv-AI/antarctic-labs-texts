Diameter estimation of ( m , ρ ) (m,\rho) -quasi Einstein manifolds
Absos Ali Shaikh 1∗ , Prosenjit Mandal 2 , Chandan Kumar Mondal 3

Abstract
. This paper aims to study the ( m , ρ ) (m,\rho) -quasi Einstein manifold. This article shows that a complete and connected Riemannian manifold under certain conditions becomes compact. Also, we have determined an upper bound of the diameter for such a manifold. It is also exhibited that the potential function acquiesces to the Hodge-de Rham potential up to a real constant in an ( m , ρ ) (m,\rho) -quasi Einstein manifold. Later, some triviality and integral conditions are established for a non-compact complete ( m , ρ ) (m,\rho) -quasi Einstein manifold having finite volume. Finally, it is proved that with some certain constraints, a complete Riemannian manifold admits finite fundamental group. Furthermore, some conditions for compactness criteria have also been deduced.

\alpha g+\frac{1}{m}W^{\flat}\otimes W^{\flat}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{W}g+Ric,

, it’s dual 1-form

-quasi Einstein manifold is said to be a (gradient)

-quasi Einstein manifold (for details see
[

If

For a fixed

López and Rio

Theorem 1.1

Let

diam(N)\leq\pi\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)+K\sqrt{2}}{\lambda+K_{1}}}.

Assume that

Theorem 1.3

If
(N,g)
is a compact and oriented
(m,\rho)
-quasi Einstein manifold, then its potential function is given by

Let

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(N,g)
be a complete
(m,\rho)
-quasi Einstein manifold which is non-compact and of finite volume. If

Let
(N,g)

-quasi Einstein manifold which is non-compact and of finite volume. If

2.
Proof of the results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
p,q\in N
and let
\Gamma
be a minimizing unit speed geodesic segment
from
p
to
q
of length
d
. Considering a parallel orthonormal frame
\{\xi_{1}=\Gamma^{\prime},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n}\}

along
\Gamma
and a smooth function
h\in C^{\infty}([0,d])
such that
h(0)=h(d)=0
, we get (see,
[
11
]
),

(8)

\sum_{i=2}^{n}\eta(h\xi_{i},h\xi_{i})=\int_{0}^{d}\{(n-1)h^{\prime 2}-h^{2}Ric(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})\}dt,

where
\eta
denotes the index form of
\Gamma
.

With our assumption the relation
(\ref{d1})
, yields

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}\eta(h\xi_{i},h\xi_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\left[(n-1)h^{\prime 2}+h^{2}\{\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df-\lambda g-\rho Rg\}(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})\right]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}\left[(n-1)h^{\prime 2}+h^{2}\nabla^{2}f(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})-\frac{h^{2}}{m}\left(df(\Gamma^{\prime})\right)^{2}-h^{2}(\lambda+\rho R)\right]dt.

Again, since
|f|\leq K
, we obtain (see,
[
11
]
),

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}h^{2}\nabla^{2}f(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}h^{2}g(\nabla_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\nabla f,\Gamma^{\prime})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}h^{2}\Gamma^{\prime}(g(\nabla f,\Gamma^{\prime}))dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2K\sqrt{d}\left(\int_{0}^{d}\left(\frac{d}{dt}(hh^{\prime})\right)^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The equations
(\ref{d2})
and
(\ref{d3})
together implies

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}\eta(h\xi_{i},h\xi_{i})\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{d}(n-1)h^{\prime 2}dt+2K\sqrt{d}\left(\int_{0}^{d}\left(\frac{d}{dt}(hh^{\prime})\right)^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{d}\frac{h^{2}}{m}\left(df(\Gamma^{\prime})\right)^{2}dt-\int_{0}^{d}h^{2}(\lambda+\rho R)dt.

If we take
h(t)=sin(\frac{\pi}{d}t)
, then
(\ref{d4})
, yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}\eta(h\xi_{i},h\xi_{i})\leq

\displaystyle(n-1)\int_{0}^{d}\frac{\pi^{2}}{d^{2}}cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{d}t\right)dt+2K\sqrt{d}\left(\int_{0}^{d}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{d^{2}}cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{d}t\right)\right)^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{0}^{d}sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{d}t\right)dt-\int_{0}^{d}sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{d}t\right)\rho Rdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n-1)\frac{\pi^{2}}{2d}+\frac{K\pi^{2}\sqrt{2}}{d}-\frac{\lambda}{2}d-\frac{K_{1}}{2}d.

Since
\Gamma
is a minimizing geodesic, hence
\eta(h\xi_{i},h\xi_{i})\geq 0
. Consequently,

0\leq(n-1)\frac{\pi^{2}}{2d}+\frac{K\pi^{2}\sqrt{2}}{d}-\frac{\lambda}{2}d-\frac{K_{1}}{2}d.

The above relation entails

d\leq\pi\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)+K\sqrt{2}}{\lambda+K_{1}}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Set
\omega=\log u
, then
F=|\nabla\omega|^{2}
and
\omega
satisfies the equation:

\Delta_{f}\omega+|\nabla\omega|^{2}=0.

Thus,

\Delta_{f}\omega=-F.

Therefore,

(12)

|\nabla^{2}\omega|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}|\Delta\omega|^{2}=\frac{1}{n}|F-\langle\nabla f,\nabla\omega\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}F^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\omega\rangle F,

and

(13)

\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla\Delta_{f}\omega\rangle=-\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla F\rangle.

Now, the weighted Bochner formula:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}|\nabla\omega|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}\omega|^{2}+\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla\Delta_{f}\omega\rangle+Ric_{f}(\nabla\omega,\nabla\omega),

together with
(\ref{b1})
,
(\ref{b2})
and our assumption, yields

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}F\geq\frac{1}{n}F^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\omega\rangle F-\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla F\rangle+(\lambda+\rho R)|\nabla\omega|^{2}+\frac{1}{m}df\otimes df(\nabla\omega,\nabla\omega).

This implies

(14)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta F-\langle\nabla f,\nabla F\rangle\geq\frac{1}{n}F^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\omega\rangle F-\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla F\rangle+(\lambda+\rho R)F+\frac{1}{m}\{df(\nabla\omega)\}^{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\langle\nabla\omega,\nabla f\rangle\leq|\nabla\omega||\nabla f|=|\nabla f|F^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the last inequality in
(\ref{b3})
, we get the estimation.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

If
W\in\chi(N)
, then as a consequence of Hodge-de Rham decomposition theorem, (see e.g.
[
21
]
),
W
can be written as

(15)

W-\nabla\sigma=Y,

where
\sigma\in\chi(N)
is the Hodge-de Rham potential and
div\ Y=0
. By considering an
(m,\rho)
-quasi Einstein manifold
(N,g)
so that the equation (
2
) holds, then we obtain

(16)

R+divW=\lambda n+\frac{1}{m}|W|^{2}+\rho Rn.

Therefore (
15
) concludes that
divW=\Delta\sigma
and hence
(\ref{qe1})
entails

(17)

\lambda n+\frac{1}{m}|W|^{2}+\rho Rn=R+\Delta\sigma.

Again
(\ref{e0})
indicates that

(18)

\lambda n+\frac{1}{m}|\nabla f|^{2}+\rho Rn=R+\Delta f.

From
(\ref{qe2})
and
(\ref{qe3})
, we have

\Delta(f-\sigma)=0,

which follows the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We have, for
l>0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l}\int_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)}|W|dV

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)}\Big(\frac{1}{l}\Big)^{2}dV\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)}\langle W,W\rangle dV\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l}\Big(Vol(N)\Big)^{1/2}\|W\|_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)},

where
\mathscr{B}(p,l)
is the open ball of radius

and center at

. Thus

\liminf_{\ l\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{l}\int_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)}|W|dV=0.

Again, a Lipschitz continuous function
\omega_{l}
exists, (see
[
23
]
) such that for some real constant
K_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\omega_{l}(x)\leq 1\quad\forall x\in N,

\displaystyle\text{ supp }\omega_{l}\subset\mathscr{B}(p,2l),

\displaystyle|d\omega_{l}|\leq\frac{K_{2}}{l}\qquad\text{ almost everywhere on }N\text{ and}

\displaystyle\omega_{l}(x)=1\quad\forall x\in\mathscr{B}(p,l).

As
\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}\omega_{l}=1
, using the function
\omega_{l}
, we get

\frac{C}{l}\int_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)}|W|dV\geq\Big|\int_{\mathscr{B}(p,2l)}\omega_{l}divWdV\Big|.

From the defining relation of
(m,\rho)
-quasi Einstein manifold, we obtain

(19)

\int_{N}\{\lambda n+\frac{1}{m}|W|^{2}+(\rho n-1)R\}dV=0.

By virtue of equation (
19
) and our assumption, we get the desired results.
∎

To prove Theorem

Lemma 2.1

(

)

Let
(N,g)

If

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
p\in N
and
\Gamma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow N
be any geodesic starting from
p
and parametrized by the arc length
s
.
Hence (

) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality together imply that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}Ric(\Gamma^{\prime}(s),\Gamma^{\prime}(s))ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}(W^{\flat}(\Gamma^{\prime}(s)))^{2}+\lambda r+\int_{0}^{r}\rho Rg+2g(W_{p},\Gamma^{\prime}(0))-2g(W_{\Gamma(r)},\Gamma^{\prime}(r))ds

\displaystyle 2g(W_{p},\Gamma^{\prime}(0))-2\|W_{\Gamma(r)}\|+\lambda r+K_{3}r.

The boundedness of

\int_{0}^{+\infty}Ric(\Gamma^{\prime}(s),\Gamma^{\prime}(s))=+\infty,

and hence Ambrose’s compactness criteria

Abstract
. We provide bounds on the sizes of the gaps — defined broadly — in the set { k 1 ​ 𝜷 1 + … + k n ​ 𝜷 n ​ (mod 1) : k i ∈ ℤ ∩ ( 0 , Q 1 n ] } \{k_{1}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{n}\mbox{ (mod 1)}:k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\cap(0,Q^{\frac{1}{n}}]\} for generic 𝜷 1 , … , 𝜷 n ∈ ℝ m \boldsymbol{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\beta}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m} and all sufficiently large Q Q . We also introduce a related problem in Diophantine approximation, which we believe is of independent interest.



1.
Introduction and the statements of results

1.1.
A brief introduction

The three-gap theorem states that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q>0
, the set

K(\alpha;Q)=\{k\alpha\mbox{ (mod 1)}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap(0,Q]\}

partitions
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
into intervals of at most three distinct lengths. This simple and attractive statement has caught the attention of a number of mathematicians for several decades, and continues to stimulate new research to this day (see e.g.
[
9
]
and the references therein; see also
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
).

The purpose of the present paper is to propose and explore the following simple, natural curiosity: what can we say about those lengths? More precisely, can we prove any asymptotics or estimates as
Q\rightarrow\infty
?

The part of the literature most closely related to this question seems to be that of the discrepancy function of a Kronecker sequence. Given
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the associated Kronecker sequence

K(\boldsymbol{\beta};Q)=\{k\boldsymbol{\beta}\mbox{ (mod 1)}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap(0,Q]\},

the
discrepancy function

\Delta
is defined to be

\Delta(\boldsymbol{\beta};Q)=\max_{1\leq q\leq Q\atop 0\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\leq 1}\Big|qx_{1}\cdots x_{m}-\big|K(\boldsymbol{\beta};q)\cap\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}[0,x_{i})\big|\Big|.

Regarding the size of
\Delta
, there is a beautiful theorem of Beck
[
2
, Theorem 1]
stating that, for any positive increasing function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

(1.1)

\Delta(\boldsymbol{\beta};Q)\ll(\log Q)^{m}\cdot\varphi(\log\log Q)\Leftrightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi(j)}<\infty

for almost every
\boldsymbol{\beta}
.
As a crude application of this result, it is possible to prove, for example, that the maximal size of the “gap” — let us provisionally define it as the volume of a hypercube in
\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
not intersecting
K(\boldsymbol{\beta};Q)
— is at most of order

Q^{-1}\cdot(\log Q)^{m}\cdot\varphi(\log\log Q),

for any positive increasing
\varphi
satisfying the right-hand side of (
1.1
). It seems this is also the best bound obtainable this way, since (
1.1
) does not yield meaningful information when
Qx_{1}\cdots x_{m}=o\left((\log Q)^{m}\cdot\varphi(\log\log Q)\right)
.

In this paper, we show, among other things, that the above bound can be improved to

Q^{-1}\cdot\log Q\cdot\varphi(\log\log Q).

In fact, we consider our topic in a much broader generality, in terms of both the sequences
K
and the notion of the gap. We provide the details in the next section.

1.2.
A bit of setup, and the main results

Much of our setup closely follows that of the recent works related to the three-gap theorem, such as Marklof-Strömbergsson
[
9
]
and Haynes-Marklof
[
4
,
5
]
. The definitions below are adapted from these works, and then extended to accommodate general linear forms and various notions of gaps.

Fix integers
m,n>0
. For an
m\times n
matrix

B=\begin{pmatrix}|&&|\\
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{\beta}_{n}\\
|&&|\end{pmatrix}

and a positive real number
Q
, write

K(B;Q)=\{B\mathbf{k}\mbox{ (mod 1)}:\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap(0,Q^{\frac{1}{n}}]^{n}\}.

Choose also
p\in[1,\infty]
and an open subset
S
of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The variables
m,n,p,S
may be considered fixed throughout this paper.

Let
d=m+n
and
\mathrm{cone}(S)=\{c\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{u}\in S,c>0\}
. For
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, write

{\inf}_{p}^{S}T:=\inf\{\|\mathbf{y}\|_{p}:\mathbf{y}\in T\cap\mathrm{cone}(S)\},

the infimum of the
L^{p}
-norm of the vectors in
T
in the direction of
S
.
In addition, for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, define

(1.2)

\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})={\inf}_{p}^{S}\{B\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbf{n}\in(0,Q^{\frac{1}{n}}]^{n}\},

the distance from
\mathbf{v}
(mod
1
) to the nearest point in
K(B;Q)
in the direction of
S
with respect to the
L^{p}
-norm.

Our question is then whether we can give any size estimate on
\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})
as
Q\rightarrow\infty
. Of particular interest may be when
\mathbf{v}
is restricted to the elements of
K(B;Q)
. We are able to prove the following bounds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is an increasing function such that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(k)}<\infty.

Then the following statements hold.

(a)

For almost all
m\times n
matrix
B
, and all sufficiently large
Q>0
,

\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in K(B;Q)}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})\leq\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})<Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}\left(f(\log Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}.

(b)

If we further assume that
f(x)
grows at most polynomially in
x
, then also

Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}\left(f(\log Q)\right)^{-\frac{1}{m^{2}}}<\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v}).

(c)

Under the assumptions of (a) and (b) above,

Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}\left(f(\log Q)\right)^{-\frac{1}{m}}<\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in K(B;Q)}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v}).

If in addition
S\cup(-S)
covers the entire unit sphere, then

\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in K(B;Q)}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})\ll_{d,p}Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}.

Our proof of the above bounds starts by translating the problem to the space of lattices à la Marklof-Strömbergsson
[
9
]
. Then methods in the geometry of numbers show that the size of
\gamma_{(B;Q)}
are controlled by the successive minima of some
d
-dimensional lattices. The growth and decay of the successive minima are then in turn controlled by the zero-one law due to Kleinbock-Margulis
[
7
]
(see also
[
6
, Proposition 1.11]
).

One may reasonably be curious about other bounds of the above kind. Following a suggestion of an anonymous referee, we attempted to give a sharper lower bound on
\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in K(B;Q)}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})
and an upper bound on
\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in K(B;Q)}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})
, but the only success we had was with the latter case in which
S
is rather large, as stated in Theorem
1.1
(c) above; see the footnotes in Section 3 for brief comments on the difficulties involved. On the other hand, note that
\inf_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})=0
trivially.

Theorem
1.1
has an application to the following problem in Diophantine approximation, which we believe is of independent interest. In fact, this was the problem we started investigating at first, but we subsequently found that it is closely related to the gap sizes in
K(B;Q)
, which led us to Theorems
1.1
.

Corollary
.

Let
B
be an
m\times n
real-valued matrix, and
N>0
be an integer. Consider the inequalities

(1.3)

\|B\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}\|_{\infty}^{m}<N^{m}\varphi(Q)\mbox{ and }\|\mathbf{n}\|_{\infty}^{n}<(N/2)^{n}Q,

where
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a decreasing function.

Then for almost every
B
, and for all
N
and all
Q\gg_{B,N}0
, (
1.3
) has a solution
(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
every
congruence class of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
modulo
N
, if

\varphi(Q)\geq Q^{-1}f(\log Q)

for
f
as in the statement of Theorem
1.1
.

On the other hand, if

\varphi(Q)\leq Q^{-1}\left(f(\log Q)\right)^{-\frac{1}{m}}

for some
f
as in Theorem
1.1
(b), then for
Q\gg_{B,f}0
and
N\gg_{Q}0
, (
1.3
) fails to have a solution
(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in some congruence class of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
modulo
N
.

It may be of interest to compare this statement with other recent works on Diophantine approximation with congruence conditions. Nesharim-Rühr-Shi
[
10
]
proved a refined version of the classical Khintchine-Groshev theorem in which the solutions are restricted to a fixed congruence class; Alam-Ghosh-Yu
[
1
]
improved it further, by providing an asymptotic formula on the number of such solutions. Both are results in what is sometimes referred to as
asymptotic approximation
, whereas ours is a result in
uniform approximation
. Of course, its main drawback is that it does not provide a strict zero-one law, reflecting the gap in our estimates of
\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})
.

1.1
and its corollary.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Setup

We continue to use the notations defined in Section
1.2
throughout this paper. In this section, we adopt the idea of Marklof-Strömbergsson
[
9
]
to phrase our gap problem in terms of a geodesic flow on the space of lattices. The same framework also appears in Haynes-Marklof
[
4
,
5
]
as well.

Write

U_{B}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Id}_{n}&\\
B&\mathrm{Id}_{m}\end{pmatrix},A_{Q}=\begin{pmatrix}Q^{-\frac{1}{n}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}&\\
&Q^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathrm{Id}_{m}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\gamma_{(B;Q)}
defined in (
1.2
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle={\inf}_{p}^{S}\{B\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbf{n}\in(0,Q^{\frac{1}{n}}]^{n}\}

\displaystyle={\inf}_{p}^{S}\{\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in U_{B}\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbf{x}\in(0,Q^{\frac{1}{n}}]^{n}\}

\displaystyle=Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}{\inf}_{p}^{S}\{\mathbf{y}-Q^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in A_{Q}U_{B}\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbf{x}\in(0,1]^{n}\}

\displaystyle=Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}\Phi(A_{Q}U_{B},Q^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathbf{v}),

where

\Phi(M,\mathbf{w})={\inf}_{p}^{S}\{\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in M\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbf{x}\in(0,1]^{n}\}

for
M\in\mathrm{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We leave open the possibility that
\Phi(M,\mathbf{w})=\infty
.

2.2.
Results from the geometry of numbers

By the word
lattice
we mean a finitely generated discrete
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of a Euclidean space. Unless we mention otherwise, we assume the lattice is of the full rank, that is, its rank is the same as the dimension of its ambient space.
For a lattice
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
i=1,2,\ldots,d
, recall that its
i
-th successive minimum

\lambda_{i}(L)
is the smallest real number such that the elements of
L
of length (with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm) less than or equal to
\lambda_{i}(L)
span a subspace of dimension at least
i
. For a
d\times d
matrix
M
, let us write
\lambda_{i}(M)
for the
i
-th minimum of the lattice generated by the columns of
M
.

The following theorem plays a crucial role in bounding
\gamma_{(B;Q)}
from above.
It and the two lemmas that follow are widely known: in fact, it can be seen as a rephrasing of Babai’s nearest plane algorithm (see e.g.,
[
11
, Chapter 2.2]
and the references therein); also,
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
gives the identical statement, except that it differs from ours by an exponential factor in
d
.
Here we provide a formulation that is convenient for our usage, and supply a proof for completeness.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a (full-rank) lattice. Then any set containing a closed ball of diameter (with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm)
\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(L)
contains a point of
L
.

This is an immediate consequence of the two lemmas below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Every lattice
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits a covering by rectangles of diameter
\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(L)
.

Proof.

Choose linearly independent
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots\mathbf{v}_{d}\in L
such that
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|_{2}=\lambda_{i}(L)
. The
\mathbf{v}_{i}
’s span a sublattice
L^{\prime}
of
L
of finite index. We will demonstrate a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the
L^{\prime}
-translates of the rectangles of diameter at most
\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(L)
, which will prove the lemma.

It is a well-known fact that the lattice generated by the column vectors of a
d\times d
upper-triangular unipotent matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&*&*&\cdots&*\\
&1&*&\cdots&*\\
&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
&&&1&*\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}

admits a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the
d
-dimensional unit cube
[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
. One quick way to see this is as follows: denote by
\mathbf{c}_{i}
the
i
-th column vector of the above matrix, by
G
the lattice generated by
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{d}\}
, and by
G^{\prime}
the (corank
1
) sublattice of
G
generated by
\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{d-1}\}
. Then since

G=\coprod_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(G^{\prime}+k\mathbf{c}_{d}),

and the last coordinate of
\mathbf{c}_{d}
is
1
, the problem reduces to tiling
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
by the
G^{\prime}
-translates of the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit cube. One can now invoke induction to prove the claim.

Back to the lattice
L^{\prime}
generated by the
\mathbf{v}_{i}
’s, the matrix whose
i
-th column is
\mathbf{v}_{i}
has the Iwasawa decomposition
KAN
, where
K\in\mathrm{O}(d,\mathbb{R})
,
A=\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
with
0<a_{i}\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(L)
, and
N
is upper-triangular unipotent. By the above discussion, the lattice generated by the columns of
N
admits a tiling by the unit cube. Applying
KA
to it, we see that
L^{\prime}
admits a tiling by a rectangle of diameter
\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+\ldots+a_{d}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(L)
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a lattice, and
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex, centrally symmetric closed set such that its
L
-translates cover
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then any
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing a closed ball of diameter
\diam(C)
contains a point of
L
.

Proof.

It suffices to assume
D
is a closed ball of diameter
\diam(C)
. Suppose
L\cap D
is empty. Then there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that every point of
L
is at least
\frac{1}{2}\diam(C)+\varepsilon
units of distance away from the center of
D
. But this contradicts the assumption that the translates of
C
by the points of
L
cover
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

By Lemma
2.2
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is covered by the
L
-translates of a rectangle
C
of diameter
\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(L)
. Applying Lemma
2.3
with this
C
completes the proof.
∎

The two lemmas below will be used in Section 4 to give a lower bound on
\sup_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a lattice. Then there exists a subspace
S_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension
m-1
such that
L
is contained in a disjoint union of the translates of
S_{m}
. Furthermore, the (
L^{2}
-)distance between any two of those translates is at least
c_{1}(d)\lambda_{m}(L)
, for a constant
c_{1}(d)>0
depending only on
d
.

Proof.

We need the reduction theory on the space of lattices, for which we adopt the language and the results of
[
8
]
; it is also an excellent reference for readers seeking more details on the subject.

Take a reduced basis
\{\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}\}
of
L
, whose Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization is given by
\{\mathbf{b}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}^{*}\}
(see
[
8
, pp.516]
for both notions). We let
S_{m}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{m-1}\}
. It is clear that
L
is contained in the union of the translates of
S_{m}
by the integer linear combinations of
\mathbf{b}_{m},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}
, and that any two translates are at least
\min_{m\leq i\leq d}\|\mathbf{b}_{i}^{*}\|_{2}
apart. But by
[
8
, (1.7)]
and the remark following
[
8
, Proposition 1.12]
,
\min_{m\leq i\leq d}\|\mathbf{b}_{i}^{*}\|_{2}
is bounded from below by
c_{1}(d)\lambda_{m}(L)
for some
c_{1}(d)>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Continue with the settings of Lemma
2.4
. Then there exists
c_{2}(d)>0
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
[0,c_{2}(d)\lambda_{m}(L)]^{d}+(\mathbf{0}_{n},\mathbf{y})
is disjoint from any translate of
S_{m}
by a point of
L
.

Proof.

Let us take
c_{2}(d)=(1-(2n)^{-1})\sqrt{d}^{-1}c_{1}(d)
, say. Since
S_{m}
is
(m-1)
-dimensional, it is possible to choose
\mathbf{y}
so that
[0,c_{2}(d)\lambda_{m}(L)]^{d}+(\mathbf{0}_{n},\mathbf{y})
is disjoint from any fixed translate of
S_{m}
by a point of
L
. If it is impossible to choose
\mathbf{y}
such that it is disjoint from any translate of
S_{m}
by
L
, then for some
\mathbf{y}
it must intersect with two distinct such translates. But this contradicts Lemma
2.4
, which states that they are at least
c_{1}(d)\lambda_{m}(L)
apart in distance.
∎

2.3.
Zero-one laws on diagonal flows

Theorems 1.7 and 8.7, and Proposition 7.1 of Kleinbock and Margulis
[
7
]
imply the following Borel-Cantelli type statement, paraphrased in the form convenient for our later use.

Theorem 2.6

(Kleinbock-Margulis
[
7
]
, see also Kim-Skenderi
[
6
, Proposition 1.11]
)
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is an increasing function, and
U\in\mathrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
. Then for
1\leq i\leq d-1
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(k)}<\infty\Leftrightarrow\mbox{ for almost every $U$, }\lambda_{i}(A_{R}U)>(f(\log R))^{-\frac{1}{di}}\mbox{ for $R\gg_{U}0$}.

Also, for
2\leq j\leq d
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(k)}<\infty\Leftrightarrow\mbox{ for almost every $U$, }\lambda_{j}(A_{R}U)<(f(\log R))^{\frac{1}{d(d-j+1)}}\mbox{ for $R\gg_{U}0$}.

The same statements continue to hold, if
U
is replaced by
U_{B}
for an
m\times n
matrix
B
, and “almost every
U
” is replaced by “almost every
B
.”

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and its corollary

3.1.
Proof of (a)

Take
U\in\mathrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and let

P=\left(\left(1+n^{-1}\right)\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(A_{R}U)\right)^{n}

(any choice slightly greater than
(\sqrt{d}\lambda_{d}(A_{R}U))^{n}
will do). We determine
Q
by
Q=PR
.

By Theorem
2.1
, an arbitrary translate of
H=(0,P^{\frac{1}{n}}]^{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
contains a point of
A_{R}U\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. This implies that an arbitrary translate of
A_{P}H=(0,1]^{n}\times(0,P^{\frac{d}{mn}}]^{m}
contains a point of
A_{P}A_{R}U\mathbb{Z}^{d}=A_{Q}U\mathbb{Z}^{d}
; equivalently, any translate of
A_{P}H
contains a point of any translate of
A_{Q}U\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. With this in mind, consider the set

(0,1]^{n}\times\left(B_{p}^{m}(rP^{\frac{d}{mn}})\cap\mathrm{cone}(S)\right)

for some
r>0
, where
B_{p}^{m}(t)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
here denotes the
L^{p}
-ball of radius
t
at origin. If
r
is sufficiently large, depending only on
p
and
S
(for instance, if
p=\infty
and
S
is the entire unit sphere, then we can take
r=1/2
) then this set contains a translate of
A_{P}H
, and thus contains a point
A_{Q}U\mathbb{Z}^{d}-(\mathbf{0}_{n},\mathbf{w})
for any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Therefore

(3.1)

\Phi(A_{Q}U,\mathbf{w})<rP^{\frac{d}{mn}}

for any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

We specialize to the case
U=U_{B}
and
\mathbf{w}=Q^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathbf{v}
. By Theorem
2.6
, for every increasing function
f
satisfying

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(k)}<\infty,

it follows that, for almost every
B
and all sufficiently large
R
,

\Phi(A_{Q}U_{B},Q^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathbf{v})<rP^{\frac{d}{mn}}<f(\log R)^{\frac{1}{m}},

by applying Theorem
2.6
with an appropriate constant multiple of
f
if necessary.

At this point, observe that
P\geq 1
, since
\lambda_{d}(L)\geq 1
for any lattice
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
of covolume
1
. It follows that
R\rightarrow\infty
if and only if
Q\rightarrow\infty
, since
Q
is bounded from both sides by functions increasing in
R
, and that

f(\log R)=f(\log Q-\log P)\leq f(\log Q).

Summarizing the discussion so far, we conclude that

\Phi(A_{Q}U_{B},Q^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathbf{v})<f(\log Q)^{\frac{1}{m}},

or equivalently

\gamma_{(B;Q)}(\mathbf{v})<Q^{-\frac{1}{m}}\left(f(\log Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{m}},

for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
all sufficiently large
Q
, depending on
p,S
, and
B
.

3.2.
Proof of (b)

As earlier, set
R=QP^{-1}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, but this time define

P=\left(c_{2}(d)\lambda_{m}(A_{R}U_{B})\right)^{n},

where
c_{2}(d)
is as in Lemma
2.5
. Lemma
2.5
implies that

Dramatic enhancement of visible-light absorption in TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} by adding Bi
Fernando P. Sabino, Anderson Janotti

Abstract
TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} is a wide band-gap semiconductor that has been intensively investigated for photocatalysis and water-spiting. However, weak light absorption in the visible region of the spectrum poses stringent limitation to its practical application. Doping of TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} with N or transition-metal impurities has been explored to shift the onset of optical absorption to the visible region, yet with limited success. Based on hybrid density functional calculations, we propose adding Bi to TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} , in the form of dilute Ti 1-x ​ Bi x ​ O 2 \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} alloys, to efficiently shift the optical absorption to the visible region. Compared to N , Bi introduces an intermediate valence band that is significantly higher in the band gap, and leaves the conduction band almost unchanged, leading to a remarkable redshift in the absorption coefficient to cover almost all the visible-light spectrum. Comparing formation enthalpies, our results show that adding Bi costs significantly less energy than N in oxidizing conditions, and that Ti 1-x ​ Bi x ​ O 2 \text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} might make a much more efficient photocatalyst than TiO 2-y ​ N y \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}} for water splitting.



I
Introduction

Solar energy is an abundant, clean, and renewable energy resource, and our ability to efficiently harness and use it is key for an energy sustainable future
Braff et al. 2016
. Artificial photosynthesis, and photocatalysis in general, is a promising technology to harvest and store solar energy, producing hydrogen or hydrocarbons via water splitting or
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
transformation using a photoelectrochemical device
Ye et al. 2018
;
Jafari et al. 2016
. It remains a significant challenge to fabricate an efficient and stable solar-energy conversion device. Controlling the semiconductor properties in these devices is of primary concern in the development of new materials for solar-energy conversion. Among the semiconductor materials that have been explored as photoelectrodes,
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
stands out as being composed of earth-abundant and non toxic elements, and being photochemically stable under acidic and basic conditions
Fujishima and Honda 1972
;
Ni et al. 2007
;
Jiang et al. 2017
;
Cheng et al. 2021
. However, the large band gap of 3.0 eV for rutile and 3.2 eV for anatase severely limit the performance of
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
since only 5% of the solar spectrum can in principle be utilized
Pascual et al. 1978
;
Tang et al. 1993
;
Jiang et al. 2017
.

Attempts have been made to extend light absorption in
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
to the visible region by adding impurities (doping) or forming dilute alloys, while maintaining the photochemical stability and low cost. Adding
N
has been considered one of the most effective way to bring light absorption in
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
to the visible range
Sato 1986
;
Asahi et al. 2001
;
Chambers et al. 2007
;
Cheung et al. 2007
;
Varley et al. 2011
;
Piskunov et al. 2015
;
Cheng et al. 2021
. The 2
p
orbitals of the
N
substituting for O couple with the O 2
p
orbitals and lead to N-related bands above the original valence band, resulting in photoabsorption in the upper part of the visible spectrum. Similar effects have also been proposed to occur for
C
and
S
additions
Piskunov et al. 2015
;
Umezawa et al. 2008
.
Transition-metal and noble-metal additions, such as
Cr, Co, V, Fe, Au, Ag, Cu, Pt and Pd
Li and Li 2001
;
Fuerte et al. 2001
;
Dvoranová et al. 2002
;
Pelaez et al. 2012
;
Cheng et al. 2021
, have also been proposed to bring the optical absorption in
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
into the visible region. In the cases that have been tried in the laboratory
Sato 1986
;
Asahi et al. 2001
;
Chambers et al. 2007
;
Cheung et al. 2007
;
Li and Li 2001
;
Pelaez et al. 2012
;
Li and Li 2001
;
Pelaez et al. 2012
, the reported enhancements in the visible-light absorption are not substantial, and limited to wavelengths longer than 450 nm (i.e., photon energies higher than 2.75 eV).
Such slight improvements were attributed in part to the low solubility and insufficient redshift of the band gap in the case of
N

Sato 1986
;
Asahi et al. 2001
;
Chambers et al. 2007
;
Cheung et al. 2007
, or to introducing localized in-gap states close to the conduction band and incorporating on the surface and blocking the catalytic sites in the case of transition metals
Li and Li 2001
;
Pelaez et al. 2012
;
visible-light responsiveness of
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
with noble-metal additions was attributed to noble-metal related surface plasmons
Pelaez et al. 2012
instead of absorption in the
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
.

More recently, addition of post transition metals to
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, such as
Bi
, has also been considered, aiming at enhancing visible-light absorption and improving its photocatalytic efficiency

Wu et al. 2009
;
Sajjad et al. 2010
;
Kang et al. 2011
;
Wu et al. 2017
;
Xu et al. 2017
. Earlier work on
the effects of adding Bi to
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
reported an increase of a factor of 10 in the hydrogen photo-generation rate and a remarkable photocurrent enhancement, with optimum results obtained for 1% mol Bi content
Wu et al. 2009
. However, it remains unclear if these improvements came from Bi at the surface or Bi incorporated in the bulk. Subsequent experiments of Bi-added
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
thin films and nanoparticles also reported improvements in photocatalytic efficiency, yet the proposed mechanisms either involved the assumption of a Bi-related band near the
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
conduction band
Sajjad et al. 2010
;
Kang et al. 2011
or Bi-metal/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
formation at the surface
Xu et al. 2017
. The microscopic mechanism, local structure, and effects of Bi on the electronic structure of
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
are yet to be resolved.

Inspired by these earlier promising results, we performed density functional theory (DFT) and hybrid functional calculations
to study the structural, electronic, and optical properties of dilute
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
alloys, in rutile and anatase phases. For comparison, we also performed calculations for dilute
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}}
alloys, focusing on the formation enthalpy of these two systems as function of
Bi
and
N
concentrations, and their electronic and optical properties. We find that
Bi
introduces a partially occupied intermediate valence band, detached from the original O 2
p
valence band, lying almost in the middle of the band gap, leaving the conduction band unchanged. This intermediate valence band leads to high absorption coefficients in the visible range, making
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
much more robust for visible-light water splitting than
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}}
alloys.

II
Computational Approach

The calculations are based on density functional theory
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
within the Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof
exchange and correlation functional revised for solids (PBEsol)
Perdew et al. 2008
and the
hybrid functional of Heyd, Scuseria, and Ernzerhof (HSE06)
Heyd and Scuseria 2004
;
Heyd et al. 2006
,
implemented with projected augmented wave (PAW) potentials
Blöchl 1994
in the VASP code
Kresse and Hafner 1993
;
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
.
The stress tensor and the atomic forces were relaxed using PBEsol with
a cutoff energy of
620\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
for the plane-wave basis set. We employed
a
k
-point mesh of
5{\times}5{\times}9
for integrations over the Brillouin
zone of the 6-atom primitive cell of rutile
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, and maintain the same
k
-mesh density for anatase and the supercells containing
Bi
or
N
.

Since PBEsol severely underestimates band gaps, we employ the
HSE06 hybrid functional to describe the electronic properties of rutile and anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
,
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and

\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}}
. Under this approximation, the exchange potential
is divided in short and long-range parts by a screening parameter
\omega=0.206
Å
-1

Heyd and Scuseria 2004
;
Heyd et al. 2006
.
In the short-range part, non-local Hartree-Fock exchange is mixed with semi-local PBE exchange
Perdew et al. 1996
in a ratio of 25%/75%; the long-range part is described by the PBE functional. For the electronic
structure calculations, with the HSE06 hybrid functional, the cutoff energy was reduced to
470\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
. The density of states (DOS) for the primitive cells and the supercells representing the alloys were calculated using
\Gamma
-centered
k
-point meshes that are equivalent to a
11{\times}11{\times}17
mesh for the rutile
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

The dilute alloys in the rutile and anatase phases,
r
-
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
,

-
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
,
r
-
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}}
, and
a
-
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}}
, were simulated using special quasi-random structures
Zunger et al. 1990
;
van de Walle et al. 2013
to represent random arrangements of
Bi
or
N
. For the rutile phase we use a supercell of 192 atoms where we replaced 1, 2, and 3
Ti
with
Bi
atoms to simulate concentrations of 1.6%, 3.1%, and 4.7%; equivalently we replaced 2, 4, and 6
O
with
N
atoms. For the anatase phase, we used a supercell of 108 atoms where we replaced 1 and 2
Ti

Bi

Since
Bi
and
N
are aliovalent species in
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, the calculations for the density of states and optical properties of
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2-y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2-y}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{y}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{y}}\text{}}
alloys were performed for charge compensated closed shell systems, i.e., with the intermediate valence bands completely filled, assuming that, in practice, donor defects such as oxygen vacancies will be present as compensation centers.
This avoids the difficulties of having to calculate dielectric matrices for metallic systems.
The optical properties were computed with the tetrahedral smearing method, phonon assisted transitions and exciton effects were neglected, as these effects will not affect our conclusions.

Finally, due to the high computation cost to calculate the dielectric matrix with required high-density
k
-point mesh for the alloy supercells using HSE06, we performed the calculations with PBEsol and
used a scissors operator to the PBEsol results and shifted the optical absorption coefficient based on the difference between the band gap obtained in HSE06 and PBEsol. This approach is expected to not affect the intensity of the absorption coefficient near the band gap or elsewhere.

IV
Conclusions

In summary, using DFT and hybrid functional calculations we show that adding
Bi
leads to significantly more efficient visible-light absorption than adding N to
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
. Our results for

a
-
\text{Ti}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{1-x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{1-x}}\text{}}\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{x}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{x}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
alloy with 5.6% shown onset of optical absorption at
\sim
1.7 eV, which is near the upper limit of the IR spectrum, thus efficiently covering almost the whole visible region. As with
N
, adding
Bi
does not affect the position of the conduction band, offering an optimum straddling of the redox potentials for water splitting. The incorporation of
Bi
is predicted to be most favorable in oxidizing conditions in contrast to
N
incorporation. Our results not only explain the available experimental data on
Bi
-doped
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, but also provide the microscopic mechanisms for the observed enhancement of visible-light absorption,
calling for further experimental efforts to study the stability and performance of
Bi
-added
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
in photocatalysis and water splitting.

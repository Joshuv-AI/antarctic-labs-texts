𝐂 𝟏𝟐 + 𝐂 𝟏𝟐 \mathbf{{}^{12}{C}+{}^{12}{C}} Fusion 𝑺 ∗ \bm{S^{*}} -factor from a Full-microscopic Nuclear Model
Yasutaka Taniguchi, Masaaki Kimura

Abstract
The C 12 + C 12 {}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C} fusion reaction plays a vital role in the explosive phenomena of the universe. The resonances in the Gamow window rule its reaction rate and products. Hence, the determination of the resonance parameters by nuclear models is indispensable as the direct measurement is not feasible. Here, for the first time, we report the resonances in the C 12 + C 12 {}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C} fusion reaction described by a full-microscopic nuclear model. The model plausibly reproduces the measured low-energy astrophysical S S -factors and predicts the resonances in the Gamow window. Contradictory to the hindrance model, we conclude that there is no low-energy suppression of the S S -factor.



Introduction.
—
The
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
fusion reaction is a trigger and driving force of the carbon burning in massive
stars
Rolfs and Rodney 1988
;
Woosley et al. 2002
and the X-ray
superburst
Cumming and Bildsten 2001
;
Strohmayer and Brown 2002
. Hence, its reaction rate is a key for
understanding these explosive phenomena. In general, the reaction rate of charged
particles is represented by a product of the exponentially damping factor and the astrophysical

S
-factor
Rolfs and Rodney 1988
. While the former represents the Coulomb penetrability, the
latter carries the nuclear structural information consists of the resonant and non-resonant
contributions. Because the direct measurement is quite difficult, the
S
-factor of the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}

reaction at low-energy has been extrapolated from the measurement at higher energy. As a result, it has been
the source of considerable uncertainty despite decades of studies
Back et al. 2014
;
Beck et al. 2020
.
An estimate by Caughlan and Fowler (CF88)
Caughlan and Fowler 1988
, a de facto standard for astrophysics
simulations, assumes constant
S
-factor. On the contrary, Jiang et
al. proposed the hindrance model
Jiang et al. 2007
, which asserts reducing the
S
-factor at
low energy. It is still controversial which one is reasonable.

In addition to the uncertainty in the global behavior, the fine structure of the
S
-factor
originating in the resonant contribution is even less understood. It is well known that low-energy resonances can significantly affect the evolution of astrophysical
phenomena by increasing the reaction rate in orders of magnitude at specific
temperatures
Rolfs and Rodney 1988
. For example, Cooper et al. introduced an ad-hoc
resonance at 1.5 MeV inside the Gamow window of X-ray superburst
Cooper et al. 2009
. In nuclear physics, such low-energy resonances in
the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
system have long been
discussed
Almqvist et al. 1960
;
Stokstad et al. 1976
;
Korotky et al. 1979
;
Freer 2007
. Recently a couple of direct
measurements have identified the resonances just above the Gamow
window
Spillane et al. 2007
;
Bucher et al. 2015
;
Tan et al. 2020
;
Fruet et al. 2020
. Furthermore, from the indirect measurement,
Tumino et al. reported numerous narrow resonances in the Gamow window
Tumino et al. 2018
. Contrary to
the hindrance model, they proposed the reaction rate enhanced at low energy, albeit its absolute
magnitude is still under debate
Mukhamedzhanov et al. 2019
.

Thus, the resonances in the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
system are of particular interest. Since they are unlikely be
measurable by the direct reactions, the study by nuclear models is indispensable. However, the
description of deep sub-barrier resonances is challenging and demanding. The primary reaction
channels,

{}^{12}\mathrm{C}(^{12}\mathrm{C},\alpha){}^{20}\mathrm{Ne}
and

{}^{12}\mathrm{C}(^{12}\mathrm{C},p){}^{23}\mathrm{Na}
, involve the rearrangement of many nucleons
and the strong channel coupling. To mimic such complex reaction dynamics, nuclear models usually
adopt phenomenological potentials
Gasques et al. 2007
;
Esbensen et al. 2011
;
Diaz-Torres and Wiescher 2018
;
Chien et al. 2018
, undermining their predictability at low energy. In principle, full-microscopic nuclear models
without phenomenologically adjustable parameters can overcome this problem
Godbey et al. 2019
, but
the resonant contributions have never been taken into account. Here, for the first time, we report a
plausible description of the resonances by a full-microscopic nuclear model and provide an
evaluation of the
S
-factor.

Nuclear model and resonance parameters.
—
To describe the low-energy resonances, we employ antisymmetrized molecular dynamics
(AMD)
Kanada-En’yo et al. 2012
, which handles the channel coupling by configuration mixing. Using the Gogny D1S nuclear density functional
Berger et al. 1991
, it
accurately describes the low-energy resonances of astrophysical interests such as
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{16}\mathrm{O}
and
\alpha+{}^{28}\mathrm{Si}
and their compound
systems
Kimura and Horiuchi 2004
;
Taniguchi et al. 2009
;
Taniguchi and Kimura 2020
;
Kimura and Taniguchi 2020
. The model wave function of AMD is
a parity-projected Slater determinant of the nucleon wave packets
Kimura 2004
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\pi P_{r}}{2}{\mathcal{A}}\{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{A}\},\quad(\pi=\pm),

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\sigma=x,y,z}e^{-\nu_{\sigma}\left(r_{\sigma}-Z_{i\sigma}\right)^{2}}\left(a_{i}\ket{\uparrow}+b_{i}\ket{\downarrow}\right)\eta_{i},

(2)

where each wave packet
\varphi_{i}
has the parameters; Gaussian centroid
\bm{Z}_{i}
,
width
\bm{\nu}
and spin
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
. The isospin
\eta_{i}
is fixed to proton or neutron.
Using the constraint on the inter-nuclear distance
Taniguchi et al. 2004
, all parameters are determined for
each channel and inter-nuclear distance by the energy variation. In this study, we have calculated
the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
and
\alpha+{}^{20}\mathrm{Ne}
channels by using this method.

inter-nuclear distances and orientations of nuclei. Note that the rotation and polarization
of nuclei depending on the inter-nuclear distance are described naturally. As explained
later, these are essential in describing the low-energy resonances. In addition, we also include
the wave functions of the compound nucleus
{}^{24}{\rm Mg}

Kimura et al. 2012
. All these channel wave
functions (
\Phi^{\pi}_{i}
) are superposed to describe the resonances,

\displaystyle\Psi_{M}^{J\pi}=\sum_{iK}c_{iK}P^{J}_{MK}\Phi^{\pi}_{i},

(3)

where
P^{J}_{MK}
denotes the angular momentum projector. The resonance energy and the coefficients

c_{iK}
are determined by the diagonalization of the Hamiltonian. The superposition of various
channel wave functions significantly improves the model accuracy. First, it describes the rotational
excitation of nuclei during the reaction process. Therefore, the model can explain the decays to
the excited states such as
\alpha+{}^{20}\mathrm{Ne}(2^{+}_{1})
. It also accounts for the dynamical change
of the Coulomb barrier height due to the nuclear deformation
Hagino and Takigawa 2012
. Second, the channel
coupling is described by a full-microscopic Hamiltonian in a unified manner. Hence, the model is
free from adjustable parameters. These improvements realize an accurate description of the deep
sub-barrier resonances.

To evaluate the decay widths, we calculate the reduced width amplitude (RWA)
Balashov et al. 1959
;
Chiba and Kimura 2017
which
is the overlap between the decay channel and resonance wave functions,

\displaystyle y_{l}^{A_{1}+A_{2}}(a)=\sqrt{\tbinom{24}{A_{1}}}\Braket{\frac{\delta(r-a)}{a^{2}}[\Phi_{A_{1}}\Phi_{A_{2}}Y_{l}(\hat{r})]^{J}_{M}|\Psi^{J\pi}_{M}},

(4)

where the ket is the resonance wave function, while the bra is the channel wave function decaying
into two nuclei with masses
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
separated by the distance
a
with the orbital angular
momentum
l
. We consider four decay channels,
p+{}^{23}\mathrm{Na}(3/2^{+}_{1})
,

p+{}^{23}\mathrm{Na^{*}}(5/2^{+}_{1})
,
\alpha+{}^{20}\mathrm{Ne}(0^{+}_{1})
and
\alpha+{}^{20}\mathrm{Ne^{*}}(2^{+}_{1})
which
are denoted by
p_{0}
,
p_{1}
,
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
, respectively. The wave functions of

\alpha
,
{}^{20}\mathrm{Ne}
, and
{}^{23}\mathrm{Na}
are also calculated by AMD. For the later use, the
RWAs are given as the ratio to the Wigner limit,

\theta^{2}_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(a)=\frac{a}{3}\left|ay_{l}^{A_{1}+A_{2}}(a)\right|^{2},

(5)

where the channel radii for the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
,
\alpha_{0,1}
, and
p_{0,1}
channels are chosen as 6, 6 and
4 fm so that RWAs are smoothly connected to the Coulomb wave function.

The RWAs of the resonances demonstrate the importance of the microscopic treatment of channel
coupling and rotational excitation. The
0^{+}
resonance at 0.94 MeV has a large RWA in the
\alpha_{1}

channel showing the rotational excitation of
{}^{20}{\rm Ne}
. Moreover, the
2^{+}
resonance at 2.18

\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}
, and
p_{0}

{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}

\alpha
and
p

perform a single-channel calculation, for example, with only the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
channel, we do not obtain any
resonance in the Gamow window. It demonstrates the necessity of the full-microscopic
calculation for quantitative discussion of the sub-barrier resonances.

Astrophysical S-factor.
—
From the resonance parameters, we evaluate the modified astrophysical
S
-factors (
S^{\ast}
-factors)
Patterson et al. 1969
. According to the
R
-matrix
theory
Lane and Thomas 1958
;
Descouvemont and Baye 2010
, the partial width for the
A_{1}+A_{2}
decay is given as,

\Gamma_{A_{1}+A_{2}}=\frac{2ka}{F_{l}(ka)^{2}+G_{l}(ka)^{2}}\frac{3\hbar^{2}}{2\mu a^{2}}\theta_{A_{1}+A_{2}}^{2},

(6)

where the wave number
k
is related with the decay
Q
-value as
Q=\hbar^{2}k^{2}/2\mu
with the
reduced mass
\mu
.
F_{l}
and
G_{l}
are the regular and irregular Coulomb wave functions, respectively.
The partial cross section at the center-of-mass energy
E
is given as the Breit-Wigner form
Lane and Thomas 1958
,

\sigma(E)=\frac{\pi\hbar^{2}(2J+1)}{2\mu E}\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{I}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{A_{1}+A_{2}}}}{\left(E-E_{\mathrm{R}}\right)^{2}+\Gamma^{2}/4},

(7)

where
J
and
E_{\mathrm{R}}
denote the spin and energy of a resonance and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{I}}
is the
partial width in the entrance (
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
) channel. The total width
\Gamma
is
estimated in the same manner as in the latest experimental analysis
Tan et al. 2020
;
Fruet et al. 2020
,
i.e., it is estimated from the
p_{1}
and
\alpha_{1}
widths and the linear extrapolation
of the observed branching ratio
Becker et al. 1981
. The
S^{\ast}
-factor is calculated
as,

S^{\ast}(E)=E\sigma(E)\exp(2\pi\eta+0.46~\mathrm{MeV}^{-1}E),

(8)

with the Sommerfeld parameter
\eta=36/137\sqrt{\mu c^{2}/2E_{\text{R}}}

Patterson et al. 1969
.

A novel probe for the resonances.
—
Finally, we propose the isoscalar transitions as a novel experimental probe to identify the resonances
predicted in this study. The transition matrix element from the ground state to a resonance is
defined as,

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{IS}}=\braket{J^{\pi},J_{z}=0|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{IS}}|\mathrm{{}^{24}{Mg}(g.s.)}},

(9)

where the bra is the resonance wave function with the spin-parity
J^{\pi}
, while the ket is the
ground state of
{}^{24}{\rm Mg}
.
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{IS}}
is either of the isoscalar monopole or
quadrupole operators depending on the spin-parity of the resonance,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{IS}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{i=1}^{A}r_{i}^{2},\quad J^{\pi}=0^{+}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{A}r_{i}^{2}Y_{20}(\hat{r}_{i}),\quad J^{\pi}=2^{+}.\end{array}\right.

In recent years, it has been extensively discussed that the isoscalar transitions from the ground
state to the cluster resonances are considerably
enhanced
Kawabata et al. 2007
;
Kanada-En’yo 2007
;
Yamada et al. 2008
;
Chiba et al. 2016
.
As listed in Table
1
, the present calculation predicts that all the resonances have
large transition strengths comparable with the Weisskopf unit. Therefore, the reactions that
induce isoscalar transitions should strongly
populate these resonances. The most promising and feasible reaction may be the
\alpha
-inelastic
scattering,
{}^{24}{\rm Mg}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})^{24}{\rm Mg}^{*}
. Note that this reaction bypasses the
Coulomb barrier, and hence, can easily access the deep sub-barrier resonances. It is encouraging
that a couple of experiments have already been
conducted
Youngblood et al. 2009
;
Kawabata et al. 2013
;
Gupta et al. 2015
;
Adsley et al. 2021
, and several candidates of the
resonances are observed in the energy range of our interest, although the spin-parity and decay
branches have not been confirmed firmly. More detailed analysis and the comparison with the
calculations must uncover the resonances in the Gamow window.

Summary.
—
We have investigated the low-energy resonances in the
{}^{12}\mathrm{C}+{}^{12}\mathrm{C}
fusion reaction, which is of
astrophysical interest. The AMD, a microscopic nuclear model, firstly realized a quantitative
description of the low-energy resonances by handling the channel coupling and nuclear rotation
without adjustable parameters. It successfully described observed resonances and behavior of the

S^{*}
-factors above the Gamow window. Furthermore, it predicted several resonances inside the Gamow
window, which create low-energy
S^{*}
-factor peaks. Consequently, the calculation suggested no
low-energy suppression of the
S^{*}
-factor supporting the result of the indirect
measurement
Tumino et al. 2018
. Finally, we propose the

{}^{24}{\rm Mg}(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})^{24}{\rm Mg}^{*}
reaction experiment as the most promising bypass to
access the deep sub-barrier resonances.

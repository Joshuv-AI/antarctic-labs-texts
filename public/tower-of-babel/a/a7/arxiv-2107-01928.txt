Oscillation numbers for continuous Lagrangian paths and Maslov index Thanks: This research was supported by the Czech Science Foundation under grant GA19–01246S Thanks: Version: August 8, 2026. Submitted to Journal of Dynamics and Differential Equations on 18.02.2021.
Julia Elyseeva, Peter Šepitka, Roman Šimon Hilscher

Abstract
. In this paper we present the theory of oscillation numbers and dual oscillation numbers for continuous Lagrangian paths in ℝ 2 ​ n \mathbb{R}^{2n} . Our main results include a connection of the oscillation numbers of the given Lagrangian path with the Lidskii angles of a special symplectic orthogonal matrix. We also present Sturmian type comparison and separation theorems for the difference of the oscillation numbers of two continuous Lagrangian paths. These results, as well as the definition of the oscillation number itself, are based on the comparative index theory (Elyseeva, 2009). The applications of these results are directed to the theory of Maslov index of two continuous Lagrangian paths. We derive a formula for the Maslov index via the Lidskii angles of a special symplectic orthogonal matrix, and hence we express the Maslov index as the oscillation number of a certain transformed Lagrangian path. The results and methods are based on a generalization of the recently introduced oscillation numbers and dual oscillation numbers for conjoined bases of linear Hamiltonian systems (Elyseeva, 2019 and 2020) and on the connection between the comparative index and Lidskii angles of symplectic matrices (Šepitka and Šimon Hilscher, 2020).

Introduction

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a given dimension and
[a,b]\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
a given interval. In this
paper we develop the theory of oscillation numbers for arbitrary continuous Lagrangian
paths on
[a,b]
. A continuous matrix-valued function
Y:[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times n}

Y^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0ptY(t)=0,\quad\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}Y(t)=n,\quad t\in[a,b],

a Lagrangian path is constant on
[a,b]

Lagrangian paths arise, among others, as particular solutions (called conjoined or
isotropic bases) of the linear Hamiltonian differential system

y^{\prime}={\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0pt{\mathcal{H}}(t)\,y,\quad t\in[a,b],

(H)

where the coefficient matrix
{\mathcal{H}}:[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times 2n}
is symmetric and piecewise
continuous. In this case the function

is piecewise continuously differentiable on

[a,b]
and satisfies (
1.1

{\mathcal{J}}
,

, and
{\mathcal{H}}(t)

n\times n
blocks as

{\mathcal{J}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&I\\
-I&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad Y(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}X(t)\\
U(t)\end{matrix}\right),\quad{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}-C(t)&A^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\\
A(t)&B(t)\end{matrix}\right).

(1.2)

,

,

,

,

The main purpose of this paper is to extend the concepts of an oscillation number

{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])
and a dual oscillation number
{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])
, following the definitions

[

45

and a new connection of the Lidskii angles with the comparative index obtained

we associate the special continuous symplectic and orthogonal

Z_{Y}(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0ptY(t)\,K_{Y}(t)&Y(t)\,K_{Y}(t)\end{matrix}\right),\quad K_{Y}(t):=[Y^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\,Y(t)]^{-1/2},\quad t\in[a,b],

(2\pi q_{j}^{*}(t),2\pi\hskip 1.0pt(q_{j}^{*}(t)+1)]

) relating the

[a,b]
.
We also prove a special invariance property of the oscillation numbers under

We also present (under some monotonicity assumptions) formulas for the oscillation and
dual oscillation numbers of the Lagrangian path

Using the Lidskii angles in our analysis of the oscillation numbers leads in a natural
way to their connection with the Maslov index of two continuous Lagrangian paths

\hat{Y}
on
[a,b]

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])

[

[

,
26

27
]

can be calculated from the changes

[a,b]
of the Lidskii angles of the symplectic matrix

Z_{Y}^{-1}(t)\hskip 1.0ptZ_{\hat{Y}}(t)

[

and
[
6

Consequently, the Maslov index of
Y
and
\hat{Y}

[a,b]
is equal to the
oscillation number of the transformed Lagrangian path

on
[a,b]

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])={\mathcal{N}}(Z_{Y}^{-1}\hskip 1.0pt\hat{Y},[a,b]),

(1.5)

[a,b]
can be expressed
via the Maslov index as

{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])=\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(E,Y,[a,b]),

(1.6)

where the matrix
E=\left(\begin{matrix}0&I\end{matrix}\right)^{T}
represents the vertical Lagrangian plane. We also
discuss (Remark
4.5
) a notion of the dual Maslov index, which is
related in analogous way to the dual oscillation number and to the dual comparative index,
and a new monotonicity result for calculating the Maslov index (Theorem
4.8
).
Thus, we contribute in the spirit of
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
to
a detailed investigation of the Maslov index for Lagrangian paths in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
.

The main properties of the oscillation numbers obtained via the Lidskii angles allow to
derive Sturmian type comparison theorems for the oscillation numbers
(Theorem
5.1
) of two continuous Lagrangian paths on
[a,b]
,
essentially extending the results in
[

, Theorem 4.4]

[

The above mentioned comparison theorem for the oscillation numbers also yields
(Corollary

) the expression of the Maslov index

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])
in terms of the two reference Maslov indices
\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(E,\hat{Y},[a,b])
and

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(E,Y,[a,b])
and in terms of the comparative index of

and

evaluated at
the endpoints of the interval
[a,b]
.

We are convinced that the results in this paper contribute to the understanding of the
role of the comparative index in the oscillation theory of continuous Lagrangian paths.
We believe that further investigations in this direction will lead to substantial
advancements in several areas of theoretical mathematics, such as in the spectral
theory of linear Hamiltonian systems, where the monotonicity assumption on the spectral
parameter is dropped, in the oscillation theory on discrete time domains, or in the
theory of continuous symplectic matrices in general. In particular, the presented
methods are instrumental for future development of the oscillation numbers and the
Maslov index on unbounded intervals (including the properties of the rotation number).

2.
Comparative index and Lidskii angles

In this section we recall the definitions of the comparative index, the dual
comparative index, and the Lidskii angles for a symplectic matrix. We also present
formulas for the calculation of the comparative index and the dual comparative index
by means of the Lidskii angles of suitable symplectic matrices. Following

[
11
,
12
]
or
[
10
, Section 3]
, for two constant
matrices
Y,\hat{Y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times n}
satisfying the properties in
(
1.1
) we define the
comparative index

\mu(Y,\hat{Y})

and the

\mu^{*}(Y,\hat{Y})
by the formulas

\mu(Y,\hat{Y}):=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}{\mathcal{M}}+\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}{\mathcal{P}},\quad\mu^{*}(Y,\hat{Y}):=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}{\mathcal{M}}+\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\,(-{\mathcal{P}}),

(2.1)

where the
n\times n
matrices
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}
are given by

{\mathcal{M}}:=(I-X^{\dagger}X)\,W(Y,\hat{Y}),\quad{\mathcal{P}}:=V\hskip 1.0pt[W(Y,\hat{Y})]^{T}X^{\dagger}{\hat{X}}V,\quad V:=I-{\mathcal{M}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{M}},

(2.2)

and where

of

and

[

,
7
]
. The notation
\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}{\mathcal{P}}
means the number of negative

{\mathcal{P}}

\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}{\mathcal{P}}+\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\,(-{\mathcal{P}})=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}{\mathcal{P}}
.
For convenience we also define the constant
2n\times n
matrix
E
representing
the vertical Lagrangian plane, i.e.,

E:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&I\end{matrix}\right)^{T}.

(2.3)

We will use the following basic invariant properties of the comparative indices defined in
(

[

\displaystyle\mu(YC_{1},\hat{Y}C_{2})=\mu(Y,\hat{Y}),\quad\mu^{*}(YC_{1},\hat{Y}C_{2})=\mu^{*}(Y,\hat{Y}),\quad\det C_{1}\neq 0,\ \det C_{2}\neq 0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mu(LY,L\hat{Y})=\mu(Y,\hat{Y}),\quad\mu^{*}(LY,L\hat{Y})=\mu^{*}(Y,\hat{Y}),\quad L=\left(\begin{matrix}P&0\\
K&P^{T-1}\end{matrix}\right),\ L^{T}\!\hskip-1.0pt{\mathcal{J}}L={\mathcal{J}},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mu(Y,\hat{Y})=\mu^{*}(Z^{-1}E,Z^{-1}\hat{Y}),\quad\mu^{*}(Y,\hat{Y})=\mu(Z^{-1}E,Z^{-1}\hat{Y}),\quad Z^{T}\!\hskip-1.0pt{\mathcal{J}}\hskip-1.0ptZ={\mathcal{J}},\ Z\hskip-1.0ptE=Y.

(2.6)

Next we recall the definition of Lidskii angles. Consider a real symplectic matrix

S=\left(\begin{matrix}S_{11}&S_{12}\\
S_{21}&S_{22}\end{matrix}\right),\quad S_{ij}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n},\quad S^{T}\!{\mathcal{J}}S={\mathcal{J}}.

(2.7)

We define, according to
[

,

,
3
]
or

[

W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}:=(S_{11}-iS_{12})^{-1}\hskip 1.0pt(S_{11}+iS_{12}),

where

well-defined, symmetric, and unitary, and in particular its eigenvalues

In view of (

iI\end{matrix}\right)\!\!\bigg).

where we used the facts that the rank of the upper block of

The following result presents the invariance of the oscillation number and the dual oscillation number under

4.
Oscillation numbers and Maslov index

In this section we make a connection of the oscillation number and the dual oscillation
number of two continuous Lagrangian paths
Y
and
\hat{Y}
on
[a,b]
with the Maslov index.
Here we use the definition of the Maslov index
\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])
from

[
5
, Definition 1.5]
, which we recall.

Let
Y
and
\hat{Y}
be continuous Lagrangian paths on
[a,b]
with their partitions into

n\times n
blocks as in (
1.2
). According to the latter reference,
see also
[
26
, Section 1]
, we consider the complex
n\times n
matrix

\Gamma(t):=-[X(t)+i\hskip 1.0ptU(t)]\,[X(t)-i\hskip 1.0ptU(t)]^{-1}\,[{\hat{X}}(t)-i\hskip 1.0pt{\hat{U}}(t)]\,[{\hat{X}}(t)+i\hskip 1.0pt{\hat{U}}(t)]^{-1},\quad t\in[a,b].

(4.1)

Then the matrix
\Gamma(t)
is well-defined, continuous, and unitary on
[a,b]
. The
last property also follows from the proof of Lemma
4.1
below, see
equation (
4.7
). Let
\gamma_{j}(t)

for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
be the eigenvalues of the matrix
\Gamma(t)
, which are
continuous on
[a,b]
and lie on the unit circle
{\mathbb{U}}
in the complex plane. Let us
fix a point
\tau_{0}\in[a,b]
. Since the matrix
\Gamma(\tau_{0})
has
n
(i.e., finitely
many) eigenvalues
\gamma_{j}(\tau_{0})
, there exists a neighborhood
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{0})
of

\tau_{0}
and a number
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi)
such that the matrix

\Gamma(t)-\exp\hskip 1.0pt\{i\hskip 1.0pt(\pi\pm\varepsilon)\}\,I
is invertible for all
t\in{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{0})
.
This implies by the compactness of
[a,b]
that there exists a finite partition

D=\{a=t_{0}<t_{1}\dots<t_{p}=b\}
of
[a,b]
along with numbers
\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,\pi)
such that
for every
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
the matrix

\Gamma(t)-\exp\hskip 1.0pt\{i\hskip 1.0pt(\pi\pm\varepsilon_{k})\}\,I
is invertible for all

t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k})
, i.e.,
\exp\hskip 1.0pt\{i\hskip 1.0pt(\pi\pm\varepsilon_{k})\}
is not an eigenvalue
of
\Gamma(t)
for
t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k})
. Moreover, for each
t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
there
are at most
n
angles
\theta\in[0,\varepsilon_{k}]\subseteq[0,\pi)
such that

\exp\hskip 1.0pt\{i\hskip 1.0pt(\pi+\theta)\}
is an eigenvalue of
\Gamma(t)
.
This allows to define the number

\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k}):=\sum_{\theta\in[0,\varepsilon_{k})}\!\!\operatorname{\text{\rm def}\hskip 1.0pt}\hskip-1.0pt\Big(\Gamma(t)-\exp\hskip 1.0pt\{i\hskip 1.0pt(\pi+\theta)\}\,I\Big),\quad t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}].

(4.2)

Hence,
\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
is equal to the number of the eigenvalues of
\Gamma(t)
, which
lie on the arc

A_{k}:=\{\exp\hskip 1.0pt(i\hskip 1.0pt\theta),\ \theta\in[\pi,\pi+\varepsilon_{k})\}

of the unit circle
{\mathbb{U}}
. By
[
5
, Definition 1.5]
or

[
26
, Definition 1.4]
the
Maslov index
of the continuous
Lagrangian paths
Y
and
\hat{Y}
is defined as the integer number

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b]):=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\Big(\ell(t_{k},\varepsilon_{k})-\ell(t_{k-1},\varepsilon_{k})\Big).

(4.3)

Note that this definition does not depend on the choice of the partition

D=\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{p}
of
[a,b]
and on the choice of the numbers
\varepsilon_{k}
, as long as
they satisfy the above properties, see e.g.
[
5
, pg. 10]
. This property
will also follow from our main result below (see
Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
Y
and
\hat{Y}
be continuous Lagrangian paths on
[a,b]
and define the matrix

\Gamma(t)
by (
4.1
). Consider the symplectic matrix

(4.4)

where
Z_{Y}(t)
and
Z_{\hat{Y}}(t)
are the symplectic and orthogonal matrices associated
with
Y(t)
and
\hat{Y}(t)
through (
1.4
). Moreover, consider the unitary
and symmetric matrix
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t):=W_{\hskip-1.0ptS(t)}
on
[a,b]
defined in
(
2.8
) which is associated with the above matrix
S(t)
, i.e.,

W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)=K_{\hat{Y}}^{-1}(t)\,[\hskip 1.0ptY^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\hskip 1.0pt\hat{Y}(t)+i\hskip 1.0ptW(Y(t),\hat{Y}(t))]^{-1}\,[\hskip 1.0ptY^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\hskip 1.0pt\hat{Y}(t)-i\hskip 1.0ptW(Y(t),\hat{Y}(t))]\,K_{\hat{Y}}(t).

(4.5)

Then for each
t\in[a,b]
the matrices
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)
and
-\Gamma(t)
are similar.

Proof.

Let us fix any
t\in[a,b]
. For brevity we will suppress the argument
t
in the
following calculations. Consider the auxiliary Lagrangian paths

Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*}:=Y\hskip-1.0ptK_{Y}=\left(\begin{matrix}X_{*}\\
U_{\hskip-1.0pt*}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}:=\hat{Y}\hskip-1.0ptK_{\hat{Y}}=\left(\begin{matrix}\hat{X}_{*}\\
\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}\end{matrix}\right),

(4.6)

which form the second block columns of the matrices
Z_{Y}
and
Z_{\hat{Y}}
, i.e.,

Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*}=Z_{Y}E
and
\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}=Z_{\hat{Y}}E

X_{*}=XK_{Y}

U_{\hskip-1.0pt*}=UK_{Y}
and

\hat{X}_{*}={\hat{X}}K_{\hat{Y}}
,
\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}={\hat{U}}K_{\hat{Y}}
, where the matrices
K_{Y}
and
K_{\hat{Y}}

are invertible, it follows from the definition of
\Gamma
in (

)
that

\Gamma=-(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,(X_{*}-i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}\,(\hat{X}_{*}-i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,(\hat{X}_{*}+i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}.

(4.7)

Note that the orthogonality of the matrices
Z_{Y}
and
Z_{\hat{Y}}
yields that

(X_{*}\pm i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}=X_{*}^{T}\mp i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T},\quad(\hat{X}_{*}\pm i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}=\hat{X}_{*}^{T}\mp i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}.

(4.8)

We express the matrix

in the form

S\overset{\eqref{E:St.ZY.ZhY.def}}{=}\left(\begin{matrix}K_{Y}\hskip 1.0ptY^{T}\hat{Y}K_{\hat{Y}}&-K_{Y}\hskip 1.0ptW(Y,\hat{Y})\hskip 1.0ptK_{\hat{Y}}\\[2.84526pt]
K_{Y}\hskip 1.0ptW(Y,\hat{Y})\hskip 1.0ptK_{\hat{Y}}&K_{Y}\hskip 1.0ptY^{T}\hat{Y}K_{\hat{Y}}\end{matrix}\right)\overset{\eqref{E:GGamma.WS.hlp1}}{=}\left(\begin{matrix}Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}&-W(Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*},\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\\[2.84526pt]
W(Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*},\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})&Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}\end{matrix}\right).

(4.9)

Then according to the form of the matrix

in (
4.5
)

W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}\overset{\eqref{E:GGamma.WS.hlp4}}{=}[\hskip 1.0ptY_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}+i\hskip 1.0ptW(Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*},\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})]^{-1}\,[\hskip 1.0ptY_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}-i\hskip 1.0ptW(Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*},\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})]=L^{T-1}\bar{L}^{T},

(4.10)

where the
n\times n
matrix

L:=\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*}-i\hskip 1.0ptW(\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*},Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*})=[\hskip 1.0ptY_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}+i\hskip 1.0ptW(Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*},\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})]^{T},\quad L^{-1}=\bar{L}^{T}.

(4.11)

This means that
L
is a unitary matrix. Then we obtain that

\displaystyle-\Gamma

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:GGamma.WS.hlp2}}{=}(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,(X_{*}-i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}\,(\hat{X}_{*}-i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,(\hat{X}_{*}+i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:GGamma.WS.hlp3}}{=}(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,\underbrace{(X_{*}^{T}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T})\,(\hat{X}_{*}-i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})}\,\underbrace{(\hat{X}_{*}^{T}-i\hskip 1.0pt\hat{U}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T})\,(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})}\,(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 3.69885pt=(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,[\hskip 1.0ptY_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}-i\hskip 1.0ptW(Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*},\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*})]\,[\hskip 1.0pt\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*}^{T}Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*}+i\hskip 1.0ptW(\hat{Y}_{\hskip-1.0pt*},Y_{\hskip-1.0pt*})](X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84526pt\overset{\eqref{E:GGamma.WS.hlp6}}{=}(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,L^{-1}\bar{L}\,(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1}\overset{\eqref{E:GGamma.WS.hlp5}}{=}(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})\,L^{-1}\,W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}\,L\,(X_{*}+i\hskip 1.0ptU_{\hskip-1.0pt*})^{-1},

where in the last step we used the symmetry of the matrix
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}
. Therefore,
we showed that the matrices
-\Gamma
and
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}
are similar, which
completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

Based on the above preliminary considerations we can now prove the main result of this
section, which connects the Maslov index with the Lidskii angles and hence with the
oscillation number through equation (
1.5
). More precisely, the
Maslov index of
Y
and
\hat{Y}
over the interval
[a,b]
can be calculated as the
total change in the interval
[a,b]
of the integers
q_{j}(t)
, which are associated
through (
3.12
) or (
3.14
) with the continuous Lidskii
angles
\varphi_{j}(t)
of the symplectic matrix
S(t)
defined in (
4.4
),
compare with
[
46
, Definition 2.2]
and
[
6
, Eqs. (2.4)–(2.5)]
.
Consequently, the Maslov index is equal to the oscillation number of the transformed
Lagrangian path
Z_{Y}^{-1}\hskip 1.0pt\hat{Y}
on
[a,b]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
Y
and
\hat{Y}
be continuous Lagrangian paths on
[a,b]
and let the symplectic
and orthogonal matrices
Z_{Y}(t)
,
Z_{\hat{Y}}(t)
together with the invertible matrices

K_{Y}(t)
,
K_{\hat{Y}}(t)
be defined according to
(
1.4
) on
[a,b]
. Then

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\hskip 1.0ptq_{j}(b)-q_{j}(a)\big),

(4.12)

where
q_{j}(t)
are the integers associated through (
3.12
) with the continuous
Lidskii angles
\varphi_{j}(t)
of the symplectic matrix
S(t)
defined in (
4.4
).
Consequently, for any continuous symplectic matrix
Z(t)
such that
Y(t)=Z(t)\hskip 1.0ptE
on
[a,b]

we have

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])={\mathcal{N}}(Z^{-1}\hat{Y},[a,b])={\mathcal{N}}(\tilde{Y},[a,b]),\quad\tilde{Y}:=Z_{Y}^{-1}\hskip 1.0pt\hat{Y}=\left(\begin{matrix}-K_{Y}\hskip 1.0ptW(Y,\hat{Y})\\
K_{Y}\hskip 1.0ptY^{T}\hat{Y}\end{matrix}\right).

(4.13)

Proof.

Let the matrices
Z_{Y}(t)
,
Z_{\hat{Y}}(t)
and
K_{Y}(t)
,
K_{\hat{Y}}(t)
be as in the
theorem. Since the matrix
Z_{Y}(t)
is orthogonal, the form of the transformed
Lagrangian path
\tilde{Y}
defined in (
4.13
) follows from the same
calculation as in (
4.9
). Consider the matrix
\Gamma(t)
defined
in (
4.1
) on
[a,b]
and a partition
D=\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{p}
of
[a,b]
with
the numbers
\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,\pi)
, which are used in (
4.2
) in order to define
the numbers
\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
. With the symplectic matrix
S(t)
in
(
4.4
) we consider the corresponding matrix
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)

in (
4.5
) on
[a,b]
. Denote by
\varphi_{j}(t)
for

j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
the continuous Lidskii angles of the symplectic matrix
S(t)

on
[a,b]
with the corresponding integers
q_{j}(t)
satisfying (
3.12
).
Since by Lemma
4.1
the matrices
\Gamma(t)
and
-W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)

are similar for all
t\in[a,b]
, then these matrices have the same eigenvalues. And
since the arguments of the eigenvalues of the matrices
-W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)
and

W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)
differ by
\pi
, we obtain from (
4.2
) that

\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})=\sum_{\theta\in[0,\varepsilon_{k})}\!\!\operatorname{\text{\rm def}\hskip 1.0pt}\hskip-1.0pt\Big(W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)-\exp\hskip 1.0pt(i\hskip 1.0pt\theta)\,I\Big),\quad t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}].

(4.14)

Equation (
4.14
) shows that
\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
is equal to the
number of the eigenvalues of the matrix
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)
, which lie on the arc

B_{k}:=\{\exp\hskip 1.0pt(i\hskip 1.0pt\theta),\ \theta\in[0,\varepsilon_{k})\}
of the unit circle
{\mathbb{U}}
.
Hence, the changes (i.e., incrementing or decrementing) of the integers

\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
when the eigenvalues of
\Gamma(t)
pass through
-1
, as it is
commented in
[
26
, pp. 796–797]
, can be calculated by the same
changes when the eigenvalues of
W_{\hskip-1.0ptS}(t)
pass through
1
. More precisely,
the number
\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
increases by one if and only if there is a corresponding
Lidskii angle
\varphi_{j}(t)
of the matrix
S(t)
, which arrives from below
to an integer multiple of
2\pi
. And the number
\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
decreases by one
if and only if there is a corresponding Lidskii angle
\varphi_{j}(t)
of
S(t)
,
which leaves an integer multiple of
2\pi
in the downward direction. Consequently,
the sum of the numbers
q_{j}(t)
increases or decreases in the interval
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]

by the same amount as
\ell(t,\varepsilon_{k})
, and thus

\ell(t_{k},\varepsilon_{k})-\ell(t_{k-1},\varepsilon_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\hskip 1.0ptq_{j}(t_{k})-q_{j}(t_{k-1})\big).

(4.15)

By the definition of the Maslov index of
Y
and
\hat{Y}
on
[a,b]
in
(
4.3
) we then obtain

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])\overset{\eqref{E:osc.Maslov.hlp4}}{=}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\hskip 1.0ptq_{j}(t_{k})-q_{j}(t_{k-1})\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\hskip 1.0ptq_{j}(b)-q_{j}(a)\big),

(4.16)

which shows the result in (
4.12
). By combining equality
(
4.16
) and (
3.26
) in
Theorem
3.8
(with the continuous Lagrangian path

Y:=S\hskip-1.0ptE=\tilde{Y}\hskip-1.0ptK_{\hat{Y}}
) we conclude that

\operatorname{\text{\rm Mas}}\hskip 1.0pt(Y,\hat{Y},[a,b])\overset{\eqref{E:osc.Maslov.hlp5}}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\hskip 1.0ptq_{j}(b)-q_{j}(a)\big)\overset{\eqref{E:osc.number.q}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}(\tilde{Y}\hskip-1.0ptK_{\hat{Y}},[a,b])\overset{\eqref{E:osc.number.multiple}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}(\tilde{Y},[a,b]),

where in the last step we used the invariance of the oscillation number with respect
to the multiplication of
\tilde{Y}
by a continuous invertible
n\times n
matrix function
from the right (see Remark
3.3
(i)). Therefore, the second
equality in (
4.13
) is proved. Next we apply Theorem
3.12

with
S(t):=Z(t)
to prove the first equality in (
4.13
) for an arbitrary continuous
symplectic matrix
Z(t)
such that
Z(t)\hskip 1.0ptE=Y(t)
. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.4

Let

Remark 4.5

\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}A(t_{0}^{\pm})

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
Y
be a piecewise continuously differentiable Lagrangian path
[a,b]
with the partition
as in (
1.2
) and assume that

[Y^{\prime}(t)]^{T}\!{\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0ptY(t)=-Y^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\hskip 1.0pt{\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0ptY^{\prime}(t)\geq 0,\quad t\in[a,b].

(4.24)

Then the subspace
\operatorname{\text{\rm Im}}X(t)
is piecewise constant on
[a,b]
and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0}\in(a,b]}\!\!\big(\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(t_{0}^{-})-\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(t_{0})\big)\geq 0,

(4.25)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0}\in[a,b)}\!\!\big(\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(t_{0}^{+})-\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(t_{0})\big)\geq 0,

(4.26)

where the sums in equations (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) are finite.

Proof.

The main idea of the proof is to construct a linear Hamiltonian system in form (
H
) with the
condition
{\mathcal{H}}(t)\geq 0
on
[a,b]
for its coefficient matrix
{\mathcal{H}}(t)
and such that
Y\hskip-1.0ptQ
is
a conjoined basis of this system for a suitably chosen nonsingular matrix-valued function

Q:[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
. Then by a classical result for such systems, see e.g.

[
32
, Theorem 3]
,
[
23
, Proof of Lemma 3.6(a)]
, or
[
16
, Theorem 2.4]
,

[
10
, Theorems 1.79 and 1.81]
, the condition
{\mathcal{H}}(t)\geq 0
will imply that the sets

\operatorname{\text{\rm Ker}}X(t)\hskip 1.0ptQ(t)
and
\operatorname{\text{\rm Im}}X(t)
are piecewise constant on
[a,b]
. Hence, the quantity

\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(t)
is also piecewise constant on
[a,b]
, giving a correct meaning to the right-hand sides
of equations (
4.25
) and (
4.26
).

A construction of such Hamiltonian system is given in
[
16
]
, see also

[
10
, Section 5.2.1]
, where the authors considered symplectic spectral
problems with self-adjoint boundary conditions depending on spectral parameter
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In
the notation of this paper and according to the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
, we consider the
solution
Q(t)
of the linear differential system

Q^{\prime}=-K_{Y}^{2}(t)\hskip 1.0ptY^{T}\hskip-1.0pt(t)\hskip 1.0ptY^{\prime}(t)\,Q,\quad t\in[a,b],\quad Q(a)=I.

(4.27)

Since the coefficient matrix in (
4.27
) is piecewise continuous on
[a,b]
, it follows
that the matrix
Q(t)
is piecewise continuously differentiable and invertible on
[a,b]
. Consider
the invertible matrix
M(t):=K_{Y}(t)\hskip 1.0ptQ^{T-1}(t)
and modify the symplectic matrix
Z_{Y}(t)
in
(
1.4
) to become

\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t):=Z_{Y}(t)\operatorname{\text{\rm diag}}\{M(t),M^{T-1}(t)\}=\left(\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0ptY(t)\hskip 1.0ptK_{Y}^{2}(t)\hskip 1.0ptQ^{T-1}(t)&Y(t)\hskip 1.0ptQ(t)\end{matrix}\right)

(4.28)

on
[a,b]
. Then the matrix
\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t)
is piecewise continuously differentiable on
[a,b]

and symplectic, as a product of two symplectic matrices. Therefore, the piecewise continuous matrix

{\mathcal{H}}(t):=-{\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0pt[\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t)]\hskip 1.0pt^{\prime}\hskip 1.0pt\tilde{Z}_{Y}^{-1}(t)

(4.29)

is symmetric and satisfies with the above defined
\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t)
the linear Hamiltonian system

[\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t)]\hskip 1.0pt^{\prime}={\mathcal{J}}\hskip 1.0pt{\mathcal{H}}(t)\,\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t),\quad t\in[a,b].

(4.30)

Note that
{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\Psi(\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t))
with the notation from
[
16
, Propositions 2.2 and 2.3]
.
Upon calculating the derivative of the matrix
\tilde{Z}_{Y}(t)
in (
4.28
), we get
(suppressing the argument

. Consider the set

{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi):=\big\{\Phi(\cdot)\hskip 1.0ptC,\text{ where $C$ is a~Lagrangian plane}\big\}.

(5.15)

Then the elements
Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
are continuous Lagrangian paths on
[a,b]
, which are
constant multiples of the given symplectic matrix
\Phi(t)
on
[a,b]
, that is,

Y(t)=\Phi(t)\,C
on
[a,b]
for some matrix
C\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n\times n}
with
C^{T}\!{\mathcal{J}}C=0

and
\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}C=n
. In the context of system (
H
) the set
{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
corresponds to the
set of all conjoined bases of (
H
). With the notation in (
5.15
) we can
formulate the following.

Theorem 5.6

(Separation theorem)
.

Let
\Phi(t)
be a continuous symplectic matrix on
[a,b]
. For any continuous
Lagrangian paths
Y
and
\hat{Y}
belonging to the set
{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
defined in
(
5.15
) we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])-{\mathcal{N}}(\hat{Y},[a,b])

\displaystyle=\mu(Y(b),\hat{Y}(b))-\mu(Y(a),\hat{Y}(a)),

(5.16)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])-{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(\hat{Y},[a,b])

\displaystyle=\mu^{*}(Y(a),\hat{Y}(a))-\mu^{*}(Y(b),\hat{Y}(b)).

(5.17)

Proof.

Formulas (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) follow from the comparison theorem
(Theorem
5.1
) and from Corollary
3.10
. Indeed,
considering the continuous Lagrangian path
\tilde{Y}:=\hat{Z}^{-1}Y
on
[a,b]
, then its upper block

{\tilde{X}}
has the form
{\tilde{X}}(t)=-W(\hat{Y}(t),Y(t))
, which is a constant matrix on
[a,b]
in the setting
of this theorem. Hence, we have
{\mathcal{N}}(\tilde{Y},[a,b])=0
and
{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(\tilde{Y},[a,b])=0
by
Corollary
3.10
and then equations (
5.1
) and
(
5.2
) yield the results in (
5.16
) and
(
5.17
).
∎

The results in Theorem
5.6
show that for a given continuous
Lagrangian path
Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
it is possible to calculate the value
{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])

from the oscillation number of a suitable reference Lagrangian path from the set

{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
in (
5.15
).

Remark 5.7
.

Consider the continuous Lagrangian paths
Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},Y_{b}\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
, which are associated in
(
5.15
) with the matrices
C_{a}:=\Phi^{-1}(a)\hskip 1.0ptE
and

C_{b}:=\Phi^{-1}(b)\hskip 1.0ptE
, i.e.,

Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(t)=\Phi(t)\,C_{a},\quad Y_{b}(t)=\Phi(t)\,C_{b},\quad t\in[a,b],\quad Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(a)=E=Y_{b}(b).

(5.18)

Since for any Lagrangian path
Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
we have
\mu(Y(a),E)=0
and

\mu^{*}(Y(b),E)=0
by (
2.1
), it follows from
(
5.16
) with
\hat{Y}:=Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}
and from (
5.17
)
with
\hat{Y}:=Y_{b}
that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle={\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])+\mu(Y(b),Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(b)),

(5.19)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle={\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])+\mu^{*}(Y(a),Y_{b}(a)).

(5.20)

In addition, in the same spirit as in
[
21
, Theorem 4.5]
we obtain from
(
5.19
) and (
5.20
) for any Lagrangian
path
Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
the estimates

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])\leq{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{b},[a,b]),

(5.21)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])\leq{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b]).

(5.22)

Moreover, with the special choices of
Y:=Y_{b}
in (
5.19
) and

Y:=Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}
in (
5.20
) we deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])={\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b]),\quad{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{b},[a,b])={\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b]),

(5.23)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{b},[a,b])-{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}W(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},Y_{b})={\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])-{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b]),

(5.24)

compare with
[
36
, Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 5.6]
. Note that in order
to derive (
5.23
) and (
5.24
)
from (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) we used that

\mu(E,Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(b))
and
\mu^{*}(E,Y_{b}(a))
have the same value, since the Wronskian

W(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(t),Y_{b}(t))\equiv W(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},Y_{b})
is in this case constant on
[a,b]
.

Based on the above separation theorem we are able to answer the question about the
existence of a continuous Lagrangian path
Y
in the given set
{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
, whose
oscillation number and dual oscillation number attain prescribed values satisfying
estimates (
5.21
) and (
5.22
). This
result generalizes
[
40
, Theorem 1.1]
to arbitrary continuous Lagrangian
paths or even to conjoined bases of system (
H
) without assuming the Legendre
condition (
1.3
).

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\Phi(t)
be a continuous symplectic matrix on
[a,b]
and let
Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},Y_{b}\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)

be the unique continuous Lagrangian paths determined by formula (
5.18
)
in Remark
5.7
. Then for any integers
\ell
and
r
satisfying

{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])\leq\ell\leq{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{b},[a,b])\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])\leq r\leq{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])

(5.25)

there exists a continuous Lagrangian path
Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
such that

{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])=\ell\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])=r.

(5.26)

Moreover, if
\ell\geq r
, then the Lagrangian path
Y
can be chosen with
X(a)=I
,
and if
\ell\leq r
, then the Lagrangian path
Y
can be chosen with
X(b)=I
. In
particular, when
\ell=r
the Lagrangian path
Y
may be chosen with both
X(a)

and
X(b)
invertible.

Proof.

Let
\ell
and
r
be given integers satisfying (
5.25
).
Define the integers

p:=\ell-{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b]),\quad q:=r-{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b]).

(5.27)

Then from (
5.25
) and (
5.24
)
it follows that
\max\{p,q\}\leq w:=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}W(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},Y_{b})
holds, where the Wronskian is
constant on
[a,b]
by the last part of Remark
5.7
. We will
apply the ideas of
[
40
, Theorem 2.1]
, which we adopt to the setting
of continuous Lagrangian paths in the set
{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
. We partition the
Lagrangian paths
Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}=(X_{a}^{T},U_{a}^{T})^{T}
and
Y_{b}=(X_{b}^{T},U_{b}^{T})^{T}
according to
notation (
1.2
). First we assume that
\ell\geq r
holds. In view of
(
5.23
) we then obtain that
p\geq q
. In the spirit of the
proof of
[
40
, Theorem 2.1]
we construct the Lagrangian path

Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
by

Y(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}X(t)\\
U(t)\end{matrix}\right):=\Phi(t)\,C,\quad t\in[a,b],\quad C:=\Phi^{-1}(a)\left(\begin{matrix}I\\
D+R_{b}(a)\hskip 1.0ptU_{b}(a)\hskip 1.0ptX_{b}^{\dagger}(a)\end{matrix}\right),

(5.28)

where
R_{b}(a):=X_{b}(a)\hskip 1.0ptX_{b}^{\dagger}(a)
is the orthogonal projector onto

\operatorname{\text{\rm Im}}X_{b}(a)
and where the symmetric
n\times n
matrix
D
has
q
negative
eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}=-1
and
w-p
positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}=1
. More precisely,
see
[
40
, Eqs. (2.29)–(2.31)]
, we take

D:=L\operatorname{\text{\rm diag}}\{-I_{q},I_{w-p},0_{n-w+p-q}\}\hskip 1.0ptL^{T},

(5.29)

where
L
is an orthogonal matrix satisfying

R_{b}(a)=L\operatorname{\text{\rm diag}}\{I_{w},0_{n-w}\}\hskip 1.0ptL^{T},\quad R_{b}(a)\hskip 1.0ptDR_{b}(a)=D.

(5.30)

Equations (
5.29
) and (
5.30
) imply,
compare with
[
40
, Lemma 2.2]
, that

\displaystyle\mu(Y(b),Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(b))

\displaystyle=w-\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\hskip 1.0pt[\hskip 1.0pt-R_{b}(a)\hskip 1.0ptDR_{b}(a)]\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp2}}{=}w-\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\hskip 1.0pt(-D)\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp4}}{=}w-(w-p)=p,

(5.31)

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(Y(a),Y_{b}(a))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\hskip 1.0pt[\hskip 1.0ptR_{b}(a)\hskip 1.0ptDR_{b}(a)]\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp2}}{=}\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}D\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp4}}{=}q.

(5.32)

Then by (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:separ.osc.number.Ya}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])+\mu(Y(b),Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(b))\overset{\eqref{E:distrib.osc.number.hlp3}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])+p\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.lr.hlp1}}{=}\ell,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:separ.osc.number*.Yb}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])+\mu^{*}(Y(a),Y_{b}(a))\overset{\eqref{E:distrib.osc.number.hlp4}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])+q\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.lr.hlp1}}{=}r.

This completes the proof of (
5.26
) for the case of
\ell\geq r
. Moreover,
from (
5.28
) with
t=a
we can see that
X(a)=I
holds.
Next we suppose that
\ell\leq r
, so that
p\leq q
in view of
(
5.23
). We follow the second part of the proof of

[
40
, Theorem 2.1]
. Hence, we construct the Lagrangian path

Y\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi)
by the formula

Y(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}X(t)\\
U(t)\end{matrix}\right):=\Phi(t)\,C,\quad t\in[a,b],\quad C:=\Phi^{-1}(b)\left(\begin{matrix}I\\
D+R_{a}(b)\hskip 1.0ptU_{a}(b)\hskip 1.0ptX_{a}^{\dagger}(b)\end{matrix}\right),

(5.33)

where
R_{a}(b):=X_{a}(b)\hskip 1.0ptX_{a}^{\dagger}(b)
is the orthogonal projector onto

\operatorname{\text{\rm Im}}X_{a}(b)
and where as in
[
40
, Eq. (2.33)]
the symmetric
n\times n

matrix
D
has the form

D:=L\operatorname{\text{\rm diag}}\{-I_{w-q},I_{p},0_{n-w+q-p}\}\hskip 1.0ptL^{T}

(5.34)

with an orthogonal matrix
L
satisfying

R_{a}(b)=L\operatorname{\text{\rm diag}}\{I_{w},0_{n-w}\}\hskip 1.0ptL^{T},\quad R_{a}(b)\hskip 1.0ptDR_{a}(b)=D.

(5.35)

Equations (
5.34
) and (
5.35
) imply,
compare with
[
40
, Lemma 2.3]
, that

\displaystyle\mu(Y(b),Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(b))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\hskip 1.0pt[\hskip 1.0pt-R_{a}(b)\hskip 1.0ptDR_{a}(b)]\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp7}}{=}\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\hskip 1.0pt(-D)\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp6}}{=}p,

(5.36)

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(Y(a),Y_{b}(a))

\displaystyle=w-\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}\hskip 1.0pt[\hskip 1.0ptR_{a}(b)\hskip 1.0ptDR_{a}(b)]\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp7}}{=}w-\operatorname{\text{\rm ind}}D\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.pq.hlp6}}{=}w-(w-q)=q.

(5.37)

Then by (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:separ.osc.number.Ya}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])+\mu(Y(b),Y_{\hskip-1.0pta}(b))\overset{\eqref{E:distrib.osc.number.hlp8}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}(Y_{\hskip-1.0pta},[a,b])+p\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.lr.hlp1}}{=}\ell,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{E:separ.osc.number*.Yb}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])+\mu^{*}(Y(a),Y_{b}(a))\overset{\eqref{E:distrib.osc.number.hlp9}}{=}{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y_{b},[a,b])+q\overset{\eqref{E:main.number.lr.hlp1}}{=}r.

This completes the proof of (
5.26
) for the case of
\ell\leq r
. Moreover,
from (
5.33
) with
t=b
we can see that
X(b)=I
holds.
Finally, if
\ell=r
, then from (
5.26
) we have
{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])={\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])
.
By formula (
3.36
) in Theorem
3.13
it
follows that
\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(a)=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}X(b)
. This means that if
X(a)=I
, then
X(b)
is
nonsingular, while if
X(b)=I
, then
X(a)
is nonsingular. In conclusion, if
\ell=r
,
then the Lagrangian path
Y
can be chosen with both
X(a)
and
X(b)
invertible. The
proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

5.8

) is constructed on

as

\Phi(t)

at

, or at

in
(

.

6.
Conclusions

In this section we make comments about the main results of this paper and their
relationship with some related mathematical problems. The main purpose of this paper
was to study the oscillation number
{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])
and the dual oscillation number

{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])
for a continuous Lagrangian path
Y
on
[a,b]
. These are integer
quantities defined in an algebraic way through the comparative index and the dual
comparative index by using a certain partition of the interval
[a,b]
. Here we use
a more general definition than in
[
18
,
21
]
in the sense that we use
nonconstant symplectic matrices in the partition.
As the main results (Theorem
3.8
and the subsequent results in
Section
5
) we express the quantities
{\mathcal{N}}(Y,[a,b])
and
{\mathcal{N}}^{*}(Y,[a,b])
in
terms of the total changes in the interval
[a,b]
of the integers
q_{j}(t)
and

q_{j}^{*}(t)
, which are associated through (
3.12
) and (
3.13
)
with the continuous Lidskii angles
\varphi_{j}(t)
of the symplectic and orthogonal
matrix
Z_{Y}(t)
on
[a,b]
defined in (
1.4
). The methods, which were used
for the above analysis, are based on the Lidskii angles of symplectic matrices and
their relationship with the comparative index obtained recently in
[
42
]
.
This approach allowed us to connect the oscillation numbers with the Maslov index
(Theorems
4.2
and
4.6
, Corollary
4.4
, and
Remarks
4.5
and
5.5
). In addition, we
derive a general comparison theorem (Theorem
5.1
) for the
oscillation numbers and the dual oscillation
numbers of two arbitrary continuous Lagrangian paths
Y
and
\hat{Y}
on
[a,b]
, as well
as general separation theorems for the case when
Y
and
\hat{Y}
are constant multiples
of a given continuous symplectic matrix (Theorems
5.6

and
5.8
).

The theory presented in this paper is based on detailed matrix analysis with motivations
coming from the theory of differential equations, resp. from the oscillation theory of
linear Hamiltonian systems (
H
). It can be further developed in the direction of
the oscillation theory on discrete time domains
[
10
,
37
]
or
in the direction of singular comparison theorems for the oscillation numbers, as we
recently presented in
[
38
,
39
]
for conjoined bases of nonoscillatory
linear Hamiltonian systems. Such results may have fundamental applications in the theory
of Maslov index on unbounded intervals, or in the study of the rotation number of
a family of linear Hamiltonian systems, such as in
[
28
,
29
]
.
We also expect related research activity in the theory of matrices in general, for example
in the limit theorems for symmetric matrix valued functions generalizing the results in

[
30
,
34
]
or
[
31
, Theorem 3.3.7]
.

Abstract
. Within the context of rough path analysis via fractional calculus, we show how variability can be used to prove the existence of integrals with respect to Hölder continuous multiplicative functionals in the case of Lipschitz coefficients with first order partial derivatives of bounded variation. We discuss applications to certain Gaussian processes, in particular, fractional Brownian motions with Hurst index 1 3 < H ≤ 1 2 \frac{1}{3}<H\leq\frac{1}{2} .

1.
Introduction

It is well-known that under certain complementary regularity conditions on the integrand
X
and the integrator
Y
, formulated in terms of Hölder or Sobolev regularity, or in terms of
p
-variation, the Stieltjes type integral
\int XdY
of a path
X
with respect to a path
Y
exists and admits a typical estimate, see
[
20
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
. These regularity conditions are sometimes referred to by saying that the pair
(X,Y)
is in the so-called
Young regime
. For a coefficient function
\varphi
of sufficient Hölder regularity, even the pair
(\varphi(X),Y)
is in the Young regime and, as a consequence, the integral
\int\varphi(X)\,dY
exists, see Corollary
2.3
below. By this fact, one can study differential equations driven by paths of fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
, see
[
25
,
30
]
.

In
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
, we studied the situation where
\varphi
lacks this sufficient regularity. We considered coefficient functions
\varphi
with components
\varphi_{j}
of bounded variation and proved that under a mutual diffusivity condition on the gradient measures
D\varphi_{j}
and the occupation measure of
X
the compositions
\varphi_{j}(X)
are well-defined and sufficiently regular to ensure that
(\varphi(X),Y)
is in the Young regime and
\int\varphi(X)\,dY
exists. We call this condition the
variability
of
X
with respect to
\varphi
. See Corollary
3.2
below.

Rough path analysis, introduced in
[
20
,
21
,
19
]
, and, in a different formulation, in
[
9
]
, provides a vast toolkit for pathwise integrals beyond the Young regime. See
[
19
,
7
,
8
,
22
]
for background. An approach to rough path analysis via fractional calculus,
[
27
]
, was proposed and investigated in
[
12
]
. Comprehensive further studies in this direction were provided in
[
14
,
16
,
15
]
. The results in
[
12
]
suffice to study differential equations driven by paths of fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
\frac{1}{3}<H\leq\frac{1}{2}
. The hypotheses of
[
12
]
ensure the existence of
\int\varphi(X)\,dY
for
\varphi
with Lipschitz components having partial derivatives of sufficiently high Hölder regularity. We recall this in Theorem
2.6
below.

Here we show that in the setting of
[
12
]
, a pragmatic variant of variability ensures the existence of
\int\varphi(X)\,dY
for
\varphi
with Lipschitz components having first order partial derivatives of bounded variation. This is proved in Theorem
3.4
below, which is a novel observation: Existing results in rough path analysis assume at least some Hölder regularity of first order partials, while in Theorem
3.4
these partials may be discontinuous. For integrals of fractional Brownian motion
B^{H}
with Hurst index
\frac{1}{3}<H\leq\frac{1}{2}
the result applies in two ways. In dimension one we can find an event
\Omega_{1}
of full probability such that for any Lipschitz
\varphi
with
BV
derivative
\varphi^{\prime}
the integral
\int\varphi(B^{H}(\omega))dB^{H}(\omega)
exists for any
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. In dimension
m>1
we can, for any finite nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, find an event
\Omega_{\mu}
of full probability on which the integral exists for any Lipschitz
\varphi
with partials in
BV
and having gradient measures dominated by
\mu
. This is weaker, but still for each
\omega\in\Omega_{\mu}
the integral
\int\varphi(B^{H}(\omega))dB^{H}(\omega)
converges in the deterministic sense. Note that approaches where also the convergence of integrals is stochastic give much stronger results, a low Hölder regularity of
\varphi
suffices, see
[
24
, Proposition 3.5]
and
[
23
, Section 4]
. For
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
even bounded
\varphi
is enough,
[
24
, Proposition 3.3]
.

In Section
2
, we review known existence results for pathwise integrals in the Young regime and in the rough setting of
[
12
]
. In Section
3
, we discuss our former results on compositions of paths and functions of bounded variation and on the existence of pathwise integrals in the Young regime. We prove a new result on the existence of pathwise integrals involving Lipschitz coefficients with partial derivatives of bounded variation under a weighted variability condition, Theorem
3.4
. In Section
4
, we discuss straightforward sufficient conditions
ensuring the a.s. validity of the weighted variability condition for paths of certain Gaussian processes, including fractional Brownian motion.

2.
Pathwise integrals via fractional calculus

2.1.
Function spaces and fractional derivatives

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open or the closure of an open set and
0<\beta<1
. Given a Borel function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
we write

[[f]]_{\beta}:=\sup_{x,y\in U,\ s\neq t}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}

for its
\beta
-Hölder seminorm
. If
1\leq p<\infty
, we write

(2.1)

[f]_{\beta,p}:=\left(\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\beta p}}\>dx\>dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for its
(\beta,p)
-Gagliardo seminorm
, complemented by

[f]_{\beta,\infty}:=\esssup_{x\in U}\int_{U}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\beta}}\>dy

for the case
p=\infty
. As usual, we write
C^{\beta}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
for the space of
f
such that

\|f\|_{C^{\beta}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})}:=\|f\|_{\sup}+[[f]]_{\beta}
is finite and
W^{\beta,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, for the space
of
f
such that
\|f\|_{W^{\beta,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})}+[f]_{\beta,p}
is finite. For bounded and open
U
and
\beta^{\prime}>\beta
the space
C^{\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{U};\mathbb{R}^{m})
is continuously embedded into
W^{\beta,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\overline{U}
denotes the closure of
U
.

Let
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, let
0<\alpha<1
and let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a Borel function. If
f
is sufficiently regular, then the (left and right sided) Weyl-Marchaud fractional derivatives of
f
of order
0<\alpha<1
,
[
27
]
, are defined by

D_{a+}^{\alpha}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{f(t)}{(t-a)^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{a}^{t}\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha+1}}\,ds\right),\quad a\leq t\leq b,

and

D_{b-}^{\alpha}f(t):=\frac{(-1)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\frac{f(t)}{(b-t)^{\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{t}^{b}\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{(s-t)^{\alpha+1}}\,ds\right),\quad a\leq t\leq b,

seen as elements of
L^{1}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

If
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\beta>\alpha
and
f\in W^{\beta,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
is continuous at
a
respectively
b
, then
D_{a+}^{\alpha}(f-f(a))
respectively
D_{b-}^{\alpha}(f-f(b))
is defined and in
L^{p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
with norm bounded by a universal constant times
\|f-f(a)\|_{W^{\beta,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})}
, respectively
\|f-f(b)\|_{W^{\beta,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})}
.

2.2.
Pathwise integrals via fractional calculus

Let
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, let
X,Y:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be Borel functions,
X
continuous at
a
and
Y
continuous at
a
and
b
. Suppose that
1\leq u,v\leq\infty
are such that
\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}\leq 1
with the convention
\frac{1}{\infty}:=0
,
0<\alpha<1
,
D_{a+}^{\alpha}(X-X(a))\in L^{u}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
D_{b-}^{\alpha}(Y-Y(b))\in L^{v}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
We write

(2.2)

\int_{a}^{b}X\,dY:=(-1)^{\alpha}\int_{a}^{b}D_{a+}^{\alpha}(X(t)-X(a))D_{b-}^{1-\alpha}(Y(t)-Y(b))\,dt+X(a)(Y(b)-Y(a)).

This definition was introduced in
[
29
]
, where it was also shown to be independent of the particular choice of
\alpha
,
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
. To quote a variant of this well-known result,
[
25
,
29
,
30
]
, we will say that
\int_{a}^{b}X\,dY
in (
2.2
)
exists
if for some range of
\alpha
the integrands on the right-hand side in (
2.2
) are well-defined, the integral exists, and its value does not depend on the particular choice of
\alpha
within that range. A proof of this particular variant can be found in
[
10
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
0<\gamma,\delta<1
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
be such that

(2.3)

\gamma+\delta>1\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\gamma+\delta.

Suppose that
X,Y:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are Borel functions,
X
is continuous at
a
and
Y
is continuous at
a
and
b
. If

X\in W^{\gamma,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
Y\in W^{\delta,q}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then
\int_{a}^{b}X\,dY
in (
2.2
) exists and satisfies

(2.4)

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}X\,dY-X(a)(Y(b)-Y(a))\Big|\leq c\>\|X-X(a)\|_{W^{\gamma,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})}\|Y-Y(b)\|_{W^{\delta,q}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})}

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on
a
,
b
,
\gamma
,
\delta
,
p
and
q
.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

It is well-known that if
X\in C^{\gamma}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
Y\in C^{\delta}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
\gamma+\delta>1
, then
\int_{a}^{b}X\,dY
exists and agrees with the Young, Lebesgue-Stieltjes and Riemann-Stieltjes integral of
X
with respect to
Y
. In this case one can choose any
1-\delta<\alpha<\gamma
in the right-hand side of (
2.2
). Details may be found in
[
9
,
20
,
28
,
29
]
.

(ii)

If in the situation of Proposition
2.1
we have
\gamma p<1
, then

\int_{a}^{b}X\,dY=(-1)^{\alpha}\int_{a}^{b}D_{a+}^{\alpha}X(t)D_{b-}^{1-\alpha}(Y-Y(b))(t)\,dt,

which is true in particular for the situation in (i), see
[
29
, Remark on p. 340]
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
0<\beta,\delta<1
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Suppose that
X:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
Y:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

are Borel functions,
Y
is continuous at
b
. If
X\in W^{\beta,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
Y\in W^{\delta,q}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
0<\lambda<1
,
\varphi\in C^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, (
2.3
) is satisfied with
\gamma

replaced by
\lambda\beta
and we have
\lambda\beta p<1
, then
\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY
in (
2.2
) exists and satisfies

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY\Big|\leq c\>\Big\{\|\varphi(X)\|_{L^{p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}+[[\varphi]]_{\lambda}[X]_{\beta,p}\Big\}\|Y-Y(b)\|_{W^{\delta,q}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})},

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on
a
,
b
,
\beta
,
\delta
,
p
,
q
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq 1
and choose
\alpha\in(1-\delta,\lambda\beta)
.
The
L^{p}
-norm of the first summand in
D_{a+}^{\alpha}\varphi(X)
is bounded by a constant times
\|\varphi(X)\|_{W^{\alpha,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, see
[
11
, Lemma 4.33]
. This norm, and also the
L^{p}
-norm of the second summand, does not exceed a constant times
\|\varphi(X)\|_{W^{\lambda\beta,p}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Using the Hölder property of
\varphi
on the Gagliardo seminorm part we find the first factor in the stated estimate. The case
1<\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<\lambda\beta+\delta
can be reduced to the first case, see the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 6.2]
.
∎

2.3.
Rough path analysis via fractional calculus

Approaches to pathwise integrals via fractional calculus beyond the Young regime were studied in
[
12
,
4
,
14
,
16
,
15
]
. We follow
[
12
, Section 3]
.

Fix
\frac{1}{3}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
. Given a function
X:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
we use the notation

X_{s,t}=X(t)-X(s),\quad a\leq s<t\leq b.

Set
\Delta:=\{(s,t)\subset[a,b]\times[a,b]\;\colon\;a\leq s\leq t\leq b\}
. A triple
(X,Y,X\otimes Y)
is called an
(m,d)
-dimensional
\beta
-Hölder continuous multiplicative functional
on
[a,b]
if
X=(X^{1},...,X^{m}):[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
Y=(Y^{1},...,Y^{d}):[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions and
X\otimes Y:\Delta\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a continuous function such that

(2.5)

(X\otimes Y)_{s,u}+(X\otimes Y)_{u,t}+X_{s,u}\otimes Y_{u,t}=(X\otimes Y)_{s,t},\quad a\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq b,

and

(2.6)

\left|(X\otimes Y)_{s,t}\right|\leq c\>|t-s|^{2\beta},\quad(s,t)\in\Delta

with a constant
c>0
depending only on
a
,
b
,
\beta
,
X
and
Y
. See for instance
[
21
]
or
[
12
, Definition 3.1]
. The space of all
(m,d)
-dimensional
\beta
-Hölder continuous multiplicative functionals on
[a,b]
is denoted by
M_{m,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
.

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

We will only use the
\beta
-Hölder continuity of
X
and
Y
and condition (
2.6
), Chen’s relation (
2.5
) will not be used.

(ii)

Here the symbol
X\otimes Y
is only a shortcut notation for an abstract two-parameter function. Given
X
and
Y
, the validity of (
2.5
) does not specify a unique tensor product of
X
and
Y
. At the level of
X
or
Y
, formula (
2.5
) is invariant under perturbation by constant paths, whereas at the level of
X\otimes Y
it is invariant under perturbation by increments of paths, see
[
7
,
22
,
9
,
19
]
. We refer to
[
5
]
for a general discussion of the second order object
X\otimes Y
in the context of rough sheets.

Example 2.5
.

(i)

It is well-known that if
X
and
Y
are continuously differentiable, then

(X\otimes Y)_{s,t}^{i,j}=\int_{s<\xi<\eta<t}dX^{i}(\xi)\>dY^{j}(\eta)
,
i=1,\ldots,m
,
j=1,\ldots,d
,
gives a multiplicative functional
(X,Y,X\otimes Y)\in M_{m,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
, see
[
12
, p. 2693]
or
[
8
, Examples 7.1]
.

(ii)

If
X=Y=B
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion and
\frac{1}{3}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
, then one can choose a version of the Stratonovich integral
(B\otimes B)_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}B_{r,s}d^{\circ}B_{r}
so that
\mathbb{P}
-a.a. realizations of
(B,B,B\otimes B)
are in
M_{d,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
, see
[
12
, Section 5]
.

(iii)

If
X=Y=B^{H}
is a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
\frac{1}{3}<H<\frac{1}{2}
, then one can use dyadic approximations or convolutions to define
B^{H}\otimes B^{H}
as an
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. limit, see
[
6
, Theorem 2]
or
[
8
, Theorem 15.35]
. A different definition for
B^{H}\otimes B^{H}
using kernel representations was given in
[
26
, Section 3.1]
. For this definition
[
26
, Propositions 3.2 and 3.3]
ensure that for
\frac{1}{3}<\beta<H

\mathbb{P}
-a.a. realizations of
(B^{H},B^{H},B^{H}\otimes B^{H})
are in
M_{d,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
. A comparison of these definitions is provided in
[
26
, Section 5.1]
.

For
(X,Y,X\otimes Y)\in M_{m,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
and
0<\gamma<1\wedge 2\beta
we define the function

D_{b-}^{\gamma}(X\otimes Y)(r):=\frac{(-1)^{\gamma}}{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}\left(\frac{(X\otimes Y)_{r,b}}{(b-r)^{\gamma}}+\gamma\int_{r}^{b}\frac{(X\otimes Y)_{r,s}}{(s-r)^{\gamma+1}}\,ds\right),\quad a\leq r<b.

Given a
\beta
-Hölder continuous function
X=(X^{1},...,X^{m}):[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, a differentiable function
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},...,\varphi_{d}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\lambda
-Hölder continuous first order partial derivatives
\partial_{i}\varphi=(\partial_{i}\varphi_{1},...,\partial_{i}\varphi_{d})
,
i=1,...,m
, and
0<\gamma<1\wedge\beta(1+\lambda)
,
the
compensated fractional derivative

\hat{D}^{\gamma}_{a+}\varphi(X)
of
\varphi(X)
is defined by

(2.7)

\hat{D}^{\gamma}_{a+}\varphi(X)(r)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\Bigg(\frac{\varphi(X)(r)}{(r-a)^{\gamma}}\\
+\gamma\int_{a}^{r}\frac{(\varphi(X))_{\theta,r}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{i}\varphi(X)(\theta)X^{i}_{\theta,r}}{(r-\theta)^{\gamma+1}}\,d\theta\Bigg),\quad a\leq r\leq b.

We recall
[
12
, Definition 3.2]
: Given
(X,Y,X\otimes Y)\in M_{m,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
, a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<\alpha<1
, we write

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY:=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{a}^{b}\hat{D}^{\alpha}_{a+}\varphi_{j}(X)(r)D_{b-}^{1-\alpha}(Y-Y(b))^{j}(r)\,dr

(2.8)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{2\alpha-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{a}^{b}D_{a+}^{2\alpha-1}(\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X))(r)D_{b-}^{2-2\alpha}(X\otimes Y)^{i,j}_{r,b}\,dr

and agree to say that
\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY
in (
2.8
)
exists
if for a certain range of
\alpha
the integrands on the right-hand side in (
2.8
) are well-defined, the integrals exist and their sum does not depend on the particular choice of
\alpha
within that range.

Up to details, the following was shown in
[
12
, proof of Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose that
\frac{1}{3}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
,
(X,Y,X\otimes Y)\in M_{m,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
and
\varphi
is differentiable with
\lambda
-Hölder continuous first order partial derivatives. If

(2.9)

\frac{1}{\beta}-2<\lambda<1,\

then
\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY
in (
2.8
) exists and we have

(2.10)

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY\Big|\leq c\>\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big\{\|\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+\lip(\varphi_{j})[[X]]_{\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}[[\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}]]_{\lambda}[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+\lambda}\Big\}[[Y^{j}]]_{\beta}\\
+c\>\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big\{\|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+[[\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}]]_{\lambda}[[X]]_{\beta}\Big\}[[(X\otimes Y)_{\cdot,b}^{i,j}]]_{2\beta}.

We state a proof for the convenience of the reader and since parts of this proof will later be recycled to prove our main result Theorem
3.4
below.

Proof.

By (
2.9
) we can find

(2.11)

1-\beta<\alpha<\frac{\lambda\beta+1}{2}.

For any
r,\theta\in[a,b]
, let
\gamma^{\theta,r}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the constant speed parametrization

(2.12)

\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)=tX(r)+(1-t)X(\theta)

of the straight line from
X_{\theta}
to
X_{r}
. For fixed
j
we have

(2.13)

\varphi_{j}(X)_{\theta,r}=\int_{\gamma^{\theta,r}}d\varphi_{j}=\int_{0}^{1}D\varphi_{j}(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))(\dot{\gamma}^{\theta,r})\>dt=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))\>dt\>X_{\theta,r}^{i}

and consequently

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Big|\varphi_{j}(X)_{\theta,r}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X(\theta))X_{\theta,r}^{i}\Big|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))-\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X(\theta)))\>dt\>X_{\theta,r}^{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\>|X_{\theta,r}|\>\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))-\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X(\theta))|\>dt

\displaystyle\leq\>\frac{1}{1+\lambda}|X_{\theta,r}|^{1+\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{m}[[\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}]]_{\lambda},

since
|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-X(\theta)|\leq t|X(r)-X(\theta)|
for any
t\in[0,1]
. Using this on the second summand of
\hat{D}^{\alpha}_{a+}\varphi(X)
and again
[
11
, Lemma 4.33]
on the first, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}|\hat{D}^{\alpha}_{a+}\varphi_{j}(X)(r)|\>dr

\displaystyle\leq c\>\Big\{\|\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{W^{\alpha,1}([a,b])}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}[[\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}]]_{\lambda}[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+\lambda}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq c\>\Big\{\|\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+\lip(\varphi_{j})[[X]]_{\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}[[\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}]]_{\lambda}[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+\lambda}\Big\}.

Here we have used that
\alpha<\beta
by (
2.11
) and since
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda<1
, and we have used that
\frac{\lambda\beta-1}{2}+\beta>0
by (
2.9
) and by (
2.11
) therefore
\alpha<\frac{\lambda\beta+1}{2}<(1+\lambda)\beta
.
Similarly as in the proof of Corollary
2.3
we find that

\int_{a}^{b}|D_{a+}^{2\alpha-1}(\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X))(r)|\>dr\leq c\>\Big\{\|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+[[\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}]]_{\lambda}[[X]]_{\beta}\Big\},

here we use the fact that
2\alpha<\lambda\beta+1
. Since
\beta>1-\alpha
, we have

\|D_{a+}^{1-\alpha}(Y-Y(b))^{j}\|_{\sup}\leq c\>[[Y^{j}]]_{\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad\|D_{b-}^{2-2\alpha}(X\otimes Y)^{i,j}_{\cdot,b}\|_{\sup}\leq c\>[[(X\otimes Y)_{\cdot,b}]]_{2\beta}.

This shows the existence of the integral and the stated estimate. The independence of the integral of the particular choice of
\alpha
is seen similarly as in
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
.
∎

3.
Variability and composition with
BV
-functions

3.1.
Existence of integrals in the elementary case

Recall that
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is of
locally bounded variation
(denoted by
\varphi\in BV_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
) if its distributional partial derivatives
D_{i}\varphi
,
i=1,...,m
, are signed Radon measures. We write
D\varphi=(D_{1}\varphi,...,D_{m}\varphi)
for its
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued gradient measure and
\left\|D\varphi\right\|
for the total variation measure of
D\varphi
. The measure
\left\|D\varphi\right\|
is a nonnegative Radon measure. If
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\left\|D\varphi\right\|(\mathbb{R}^{m})<\infty
, then
\varphi
is said to be of
bounded variation
,
\varphi\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Details and background can for instance be found in
[
3
]
or
[
31
]
.

Given
0<\gamma<m
and a nonnegative Radon measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we write

(3.1)

U^{\gamma}\nu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|x-z|^{\gamma-m}\,\nu(dz),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Up to a constant which depends only on
m
and
\gamma
, the
[0,\infty]
-valued function
U^{\gamma}\nu
is the
Riesz potential of order
\gamma
of
\nu
, see
[
2
]
or
[
17
]
.

Given
\varphi\in BV_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<s<1
, we say that a Borel function
X:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
(s,p)
-variable w.r.t.
\varphi
if the function
t\mapsto U^{1-s}\|D\varphi\|(X(t))
is in
L^{p}([a,b])
. We write
V(\varphi,s,p)
for the set of all
X
integrable over
[a,b]
and
(s,p)
-variable w.r.t.
\varphi
. See
[
11
, Definition 2.2]
or
[
10
, Definition 5.1]
. The following composition results were shown in
[
11
, Theorem 2.12 (iii)]
and
[
10
, Theorem 5.11 (i)]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a Borel function,
0<s,\beta<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
.

(i)

If
\varphi\in BV_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
\gamma<s\beta
and
X\in C^{\beta}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap V(\varphi,s,p)
,
then
\varphi\circ X\in W^{\gamma,p}([a,b])
and in particular,

[\varphi\circ X]_{\gamma,p}\leq c\>[[X]]_{\beta}^{s}\>\big\|U^{1-s}\|D\varphi\|(X)\big\|_{L^{p}([a,b])}

with a constant
c>0
depending only on
a
,
b
,
m
,
s
,
p
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.

(ii)

If
\varphi\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
r\geq 1
is such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{s}{q}\leq\frac{1}{r}
,
\gamma<s\beta
and
X\in W^{\beta,q}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap V(\varphi,s,p)
,
then
\varphi\circ X\in W^{\gamma,r}([a,b])
and in particular,

[\varphi\circ X]_{\gamma,r}\leq c\>[X]_{\beta,q}^{s}\>\big\|U^{1-s}\|D\varphi\|(X)\big\|_{L^{p}([a,b])}

with a constant
c>0
depending only on
a
,
b
,
m
,
s
,
p
,
q
,
r
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.

This gives existence results and estimates for integrals involving
BV
-coefficients in the elementary case, which we quote from
[
11
, Theorem 2.12 and Corollary 4.36]
and
[
10
, Corollary 6.4]
. They follow from Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
and may be seen as counterparts to Corollary
2.3
for
BV
-coefficients.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose that
0<s,\beta,\delta<1
,
Y\in C^{\delta}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
X:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a Borel function.

(i)

If
\varphi\in BV_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
s\beta+\delta>1
and
X\in C^{\beta}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap V(\varphi,s,1)
, then
\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY
in (
2.2
) exists and

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY\Big|\leq c\>\Big\{\|\varphi(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}+[[X]]_{\beta}^{s}\|U^{1-s}\|D\varphi\|(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}\Big\}[[Y]]_{\delta}.

(ii)

If
\varphi\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
s\beta+\delta>1
,
1\leq p,q<\infty
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{s}{q}\leq 1
and

X\in W^{\beta,q}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap V(\varphi,s,p)
, then

\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY
in (
2.2
) exists and

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY\Big|\leq c\>\Big\{\|\varphi(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}+[X]_{\beta,q}^{s}\big\|U^{1-s}\|D\varphi\|(X)\big\|_{L^{p}([a,b])}\Big\}[[Y]]_{\delta}.

Remark 3.3
.

Variants of Theorem
3.1
(ii) and Corollary
3.2
(ii) under the less restrictive hypothesis that

can easily be stated, but come with notationally more involved conditions.

3.2.
Existence of integrals in the rough case

Our main result is the following, which is a counterpart of Theorem
2.6
for Lipschitz coefficients with first order partials in
BV
and a rough counterpart of Corollary
3.2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
\frac{1}{3}<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
,
(X,Y,X\otimes Y)\in M_{m,d}^{\beta}([a,b])
,
\varphi\in\lip(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\in BV_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
for all
i,j
and

(3.2)

\frac{1}{\beta}-2<s<1.

If
X
is
(s,1)
-variable w.r.t. all
\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}
and for some
\frac{1}{2}(\beta(1+s)-(1-\beta))<\varepsilon<\beta(1+s)-(1-\beta)
we have

(3.3)

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}\int_{0}^{1}U^{1-s}\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\|(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))t^{s}\>dt\,d\theta\,dr<\infty

for all
i
and
j
, then
\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY
in (
2.8
) exists and we have

(3.4)

\Big|\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X)\,dY\Big|\leq c\>\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big\{\|\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+\lip(\varphi_{j})[[X]]_{\beta}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}\int_{0}^{1}U^{1-s}\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\|(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))t^{s}\>dt\,d\theta\,dr\>[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+s}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|U^{1-s}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}\>[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+s}\Big\}[[Y^{j}]]_{\beta}\\
+c\>\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big\{\|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+\|U^{1-s}\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\|(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}[[X]]_{\beta}^{s}\Big\}[[(X\otimes Y)_{\cdot,b}^{i,j}]]_{2\beta}.

The segment
\gamma^{\theta,r}
in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) is defined as in (
2.12
).

Note that
1-\beta<\beta(1+s)
by (
3.2
). Hypothesis (
3.3
) may be seen as a
weighted variability condition on
X
w.r.t. the partial derivatives
\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}
along line segments
\gamma^{\theta,r}
.

We turn to a proof of Theorem
3.4
. Given
0<\gamma<m
,
R>0
and a nonnegative Radon measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we define the
truncated fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
\nu
of order
\gamma
by
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma,R}\nu(x):=\sup_{0<r<R}r^{\gamma-m}\nu(B(x,r))
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Obviously

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma,R}\nu(x)\leq U^{\gamma}\nu(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof.

We can mimic (
2.14
) and estimate

(3.5)

\Big|\varphi_{j}(X)_{\theta,r}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X(\theta))X_{\theta,r}\Big|\leq\>|X_{\theta,r}|\>\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))-\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X(\theta))|\>dt.

Using
[
11
, Proposition C.1]
, which is based on
[
1
, Lemma 4.1]
, we find that

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))-\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X(\theta))|

\displaystyle\leq c|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-X(\theta)|^{s}\big\{\mathcal{M}_{1-s,|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-X(\theta)|}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))

\displaystyle\hskip 180.0pt+\mathcal{M}_{1-s,|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-X(\theta)|}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(X(\theta))\big\}

\displaystyle\leq c\>t^{s}|X_{\theta,r}|^{s}\big\{U^{1-s}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))+U^{1-s}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(X(\theta))\big\}.

Consequently (
3.5
) is bounded by

c\>|X_{\theta,r}|^{1+s}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}U^{1-s}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))t^{s}\,dt+\frac{1}{1+s}U^{1-s}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(X(\theta))\right\}.

Now set
\alpha:=\beta(1+s)-\varepsilon
. Then
1-\beta<\alpha<\frac{s\beta+1}{2}<\beta(1+s)
. Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
2.6
, we therefore have

(3.6)

\int_{a}^{b}|\hat{D}^{\alpha}_{a+}\varphi_{j}(X)(r)|\>dr\leq c\>\Big\{\|\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+\lip(\varphi_{j})[[X]]_{\beta}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\beta(1+s)-\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{1}U^{1-s}\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\|(\gamma^{\theta,r}(t))t^{s}\>dt\,d\theta\,dr\>[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+s}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|U^{1-s}\left\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\right\|(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}\>[[X]]_{\beta}^{1+s}\Big\}

and

\int_{a}^{b}|D_{a+}^{2\alpha-1}(\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X))(r)|\>dr\leq c\>\Big\{\|\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}+\|U^{1-s}\|D\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}\|(X)\|_{L^{1}([a,b])}[[X]]_{\beta}^{s}\Big\}.

The remaining arguments are the same as in the proof of Theorem
2.6
.
∎

In the one-dimensional case
m=1
condition (
3.3
) follows from variability.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
0<s<1
,
\varepsilon>0
and
X\in C([a,b],\mathbb{R})
. If
\psi\in BV_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
and
X
is
(s,1)
-variable w.r.t.
\psi
, then
z\mapsto\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{1-\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|^{-s}\,dt\,d\theta\,dr
is in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R},\|D\psi\|)
and (
3.3
) holds with
\psi
in place of
\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}
.

Proof.

Note first that
\sup_{a\leq r\leq b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}d\theta\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}(b-a)^{\varepsilon}=:c_{a,b,\varepsilon}
. For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{1-\varepsilon}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{\theta,r}=0\}}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|^{-s}\,dt\,d\theta\,dr\leq c_{a,b,\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}|X(r)-z|^{-s}dr.

For
r,\theta\in[a,b]
such that
X_{\theta,r}\neq 0
let
I^{\theta,r}\subset\mathbb{R}
be the open interval with endpoints
X(\theta)
and
X(r)
. If
z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus I^{\theta,r}
, then
|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|\geq\min(|X(r)-z|,|X(\theta)-z|)
. For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
we therefore have

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{\theta,r}\neq 0,\>z\notin I^{\theta,r}\}}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|^{-s}\,dt\,d\theta\,dr\leq 2c_{a,b,\varepsilon}\int_{a}^{b}|X(r)-z|^{-s}dr.

For
z\in I^{\theta,r}
, let
t(z)=\frac{z-X(\theta)}{X_{\theta,r}}
. Then
t(z)\in(0,1)
,
z=t(z)X(r)+(1-t(z))X(\theta)
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|^{-s}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t(z)}|tX_{\theta,r}+X(\theta)-z|^{-s}\,dt+\int_{t(z)}^{1}|(1-t)X_{r,\theta}+X(r)-z|^{-s}\,dt

\displaystyle=t(z)\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{-s}\,d\tau\,|X(\theta)-z|^{-s}+(1-t(z))\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{-s}\,d\tau\,|X(r)-z|^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-s}(|X(\theta)-z|^{-s}+|X(r)-z|^{-s});

here we have used the change of variables
\tau=\frac{t}{t(z)}
for the first integral and
\tau=\frac{t-t(z)}{1-t(z)}
for the second. For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
we therefore have

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{\theta,r}\neq 0,\>z\in I^{\theta,r}\}}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|^{-s}t^{s}\,dt\,d\theta\,dr\leq\frac{2c_{a,b,\varepsilon}}{1-s}\int_{a}^{b}|X(r)-z|^{-s}dr.

∎

.

Let
0<s<1
,
\varepsilon>0
and
X\in C([a,b],\mathbb{R})

(3.7)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\theta,r}(t)-z|^{-s}\,dt\,d\theta\,dr<\infty,

X\in V(s,1,\psi)
for any
\psi\in BV(\mathbb{R})
and also (
3.3
) holds with any
\psi
in place of
\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}
.

Proof.

The claimed variability of
X
follow using
[
11
, Proposition 4.8]
. Lemma
3.5
gives (
3.7
), and (
3.7
) gives (
3.3
) with
\psi
in place of
\partial_{i}\varphi_{j}
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

(i)

(4.2)

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose that
R(r,\theta)\geq 0
for all

and

and that

By the stated assumptions we have

By (

) and since

\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}|r-\theta|^{\varepsilon-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbb{E}|tX(r)+(1-t)X(\theta)-z|^{-m+1-s}\mu(dz)\,dt\,d\theta\,dr\leq 2c_{a,b,\varepsilon}C_{\mu}.

Remark 4.3
.

A sufficient condition to ensure the existence of
\Omega_{s,\mu}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{s,\mu})=1
such that

for any

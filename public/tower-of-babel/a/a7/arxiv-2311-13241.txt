Duality Theory on Vector Spaces
Dang Van Cuong, Tuyen Tran

Abstract
In this paper, we study the Fenchel-Rockafellar duality and the Lagrange duality in the general frame work of vector spaces without topological structures. We utilize the geometric approach, inspired from its successful application by B. S. Mordukhovich and his coauthors in variational and convex analysis (see [ 13 , 14 , 16 , 15 , 31 , 30 ] ). After revisiting coderivative calculus rules and providing the subdifferential maximum rule in vector spaces, we establish conjugate calculus rules under qualifying conditions through the algebraic interior of the function’s domains. Then we develop sufficient conditions which guarantee the Fenchel-Rockafellar strong duality. Finally, after deriving some necessary and sufficient conditions for optimal solutions to convex minimization problems, under a Slater condition via the algebraic interior, we then obtain a sufficient condition for the Lagrange strong duality.



Dedicated to Prof. Boris Mordukhovich in honor of his 75th birthday

Keywords:
Algebraic interior, Algebraic core, Fenchel duality, Lagrangian duality, Calculus rules, Optimality conditions

Preliminaries

We begin by discussing the basic notions that will be used in the rest of the paper, see the books
[
25
,
26
,
30
,
37
]
and the papers
[
13
,
14
]
for relevant results. We use standard notation from convex and variational analysis; see, e.g.,
[
30
,
31
,
34
,
35
]
. Only real vector spaces are considered. Given such a vector space
X
, the algebraic dual space of
X
is

X^{\prime}:=\big\{f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}\;\big|\ f\;\text{ is a linear function}\big\}.

Next, we introduce a few basic notions for partially ordered vector spaces using definitions and notations from
[
28
]
, more material can be found in
[
15
,
27
]
. Given a vector space
Y
and a nonempty convex cone
C\subset Y
, we define a relation on
Y
by
z_{1}\leq_{C}z_{2}\;\text{if and only if}\;z_{2}-z_{1}\in C
. This defines a
partial ordering
on
Y
, and we say that
Y
is
partially ordered by
C
and refer to
Y
as an
ordered vector space
. Furthermore, an ordered vector space
Y
is considered
generating
if
Y=C-C
. In this paper, we only consider
Archimedean
ordered vector spaces, that is
n^{-1}y\downarrow_{C}0
whenever
y\in Y^{+}
.

Now consider a function between vector spaces,
\phi\colon X\to Y
. Here,
Y
is partially ordered by
C
. We then say
\phi
is
C
-convex
if for
a,b\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,_{C}(\phi)
and
\lambda\in[0,1]

\phi\big(\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b\big)\leq_{C}\lambda\phi(a)+(1-\lambda)\phi(b).

Given a set
\Lambda\subset X
, we define the
core
or
algebraic interior
of it by

\displaystyle\text{core}(\Lambda):=\big\{x\in\Lambda\;\big|\;\forall v\in X,\;\exists\delta>0,\;\forall t\mbox{ with }|t|<\delta:\;x+tv\in\Lambda\big\}.

In the case
X
is a topological vector space, we have
{\rm int}(\Lambda)\subset\text{core}(\Lambda)\subset\Lambda
, where
{\rm int}(\Lambda)
is the standard topological interior of
\Lambda
. A set
\Lambda\subset X
is
core-solid
if
\text{core}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
.

Two nonempty subsets
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset X
can be
separated
by a hyperplane if there exists a nonzero linear function
g\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup\big\{g(x)\;\big|\;x\in\Lambda_{1}\big\}\leq\inf\big\{g(x)\;\big|\;x\in\Lambda_{2}\big\}.

In addition, if
\inf\big\{g(x)\;\big|\;x\in\Lambda_{1}\big\}<\sup\big\{g(x)\;\big|\;x\in\Lambda_{2}\big\}
, that is there exists
x_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}
and
x_{2}\in\Lambda_{2}
with
g(x_{1})<g(x_{2})
, then
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are
properly separated
by a hyperplane.

In particular, the separability of a convex set
\Lambda
and singleton
\{x_{0}\}
can be characterized by whether
x_{0}
exists in the core of
\Lambda

[
14
, Theorems 3.3, 3.4]
. On the other hand,
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
14
, Lemma 5.2]
gave us versions of properly separated theorems to two convex sets in vector spaces.

Let
\Lambda\subset X
be nonempty and convex, then the
normal cone
to
\Lambda
at
\bar{z}\in\Lambda
is given by

N(\bar{z};\Lambda):=\big\{f\in X^{\prime}\;\big|\;f(z-\bar{z})\leq 0\;\text{ for all }\;z\in\Lambda\big\}

with
N(z;\Lambda):=\emptyset
for
z\notin\Lambda
. It is easy to see that if
z\in\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda)
, then
N(z;\Lambda)=\{0\}
. Note that
[
13
, Theorem 3.3]
gave us an important rule we will use a few times, a representation of the normal cone to the intersection of two convex sets in vector spaces.

3
Revisiting Generalized Differential Calculus in Vector Spaces

We first revisit some coderivative calculus rules with improvements utilizing qualifying conditions in
[
13
]
then provide the subdifferential maximum rule in vector spaces.

Consider a set-valued mapping
G\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
between vector spaces. The
domain
,
range
, and
graph
of it are defined by

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm dom}\,(G):=\big\{x\in X\;\big|\;G(x)\neq\emptyset\big\},\quad\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G):=\bigcup_{x\in X}G(x)

\displaystyle\text{gph}\,(G):=\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\;\big|\;y\in G(x)\big\}

Recall that
G
is convex if its graph is a convex subset of
X\times Y
, and
\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)
is also convex given the convexity of
G
.

Next, we revisit the
coderivative
of
G
at
(a,b)\in\text{gph}\,(G)
which is a set-valued mapping
D^{*}G(a,b)\colon Y^{\prime}\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;X^{\prime}
and is given by

D^{*}G(a,b)(h):=\big\{f\in X^{\prime}\;\big|\;(f,-h)\in N\big((a,b);\text{gph}\,(G)\big)\big\},\quad h\in Y^{\prime}.

(1)

The definition of the
composition
of two set-valued mappings
G\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
and
H\colon Y\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Z
is given by

(H\circ G)(x)=\bigcup_{y\in G(x)}H(y):=\{z\in H(y)\;|\;y\in G(x)\},\quad x\in X.

It is not difficult to see that
H\circ G
is convex given the convexity of both
G
and
H
. To derive the coderivative chain rule, let
\nu\in(H\circ G)(u)
and we consider the set
K(u,\nu):=G(u)\cap H^{-1}(\nu)
. In the following theorem, we utilize the core qualification condition on domains and ranges, instead of the graph qualifying conditions known from
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
, to derive the coderivative chain rule for set-valued mappings in vector spaces.

Theorem 3.1

Consider convex set-valued mappings
G\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
,
H\colon Y\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Z
and suppose that the following qualifying condition is satisfied

\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)\big)\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(H)\big)\neq\emptyset;

(2)

Then for
(a,c)\in\text{gph}\,(H\circ G)
and
s\in Z^{\prime}
we have the coderivative chain rule

D^{*}(H\circ G)(a,c)(s)=\bigcap_{b\in K(a,c)}D^{*}G(a,b)\circ D^{*}H(b,c)(s).

(3)

Proof.
The inclusion “
\supset
” is trivial. Let
f\in D^{*}(H\circ G)(a,c)(s)
and
b\in K(a,c)
. Then,
(f,-s)\in N((a,c);\text{gph}\,(H\circ G))
, which implies

\langle f,u-a\rangle-\langle s,\nu-c\rangle\leq 0\;\mbox{\rm for}\;(u,\nu)\in\text{gph}\,(H\circ G).

Let us define
\Lambda_{1}:=\text{gph}\,(G)\times Z
and
\Lambda_{2}:=X\times\text{gph}\,(H)
to be convex subsets of
X\times Y\times Z
. It can be easily seen that

\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}=X\times\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)-\mbox{\rm dom}\,(H)\big)\times Z.

(4)

Using (
4
), we arrive at the representation

\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2})=X\times\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)-\mbox{\rm dom}\,(H)\big)\times Z.

Applying
[
14
, Lemma 5.1]
yields

\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2})=X\times\big(\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)\big)-\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(H)\big)\big)\times Z.

From the definitions of
\Lambda_{1}
,
\Lambda_{2}
and (
2
) we have

0\in\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}),\;\mbox{ and so }\;\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{2})\neq\emptyset.

(5)

Observe that
(a,b,c)\in\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
. Hence, from the definitions

(f,0,-s)\in N((a,b,c);\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}).

Now, employing
[
14
, Theorem 5.4]
with the qualifying condition (
5
) we obtain

(f,0,-s)\in N((a,b,c);\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})=N((a,b,c);\Lambda_{1})+N((a,b,c);\Lambda_{2}),

and therefore we can find
k\in Y^{\prime}
such that

(f,0,-s)=(f,-k,0)+(0,k,-s)

with
(f,-k)\in N((a,b);\text{gph}\,(G)),\;(k,-s)\in N((b,c);\text{gph}\,(H)).
Using the coderivative definition (
1
) we arrive at
f\in D^{*}G(a,b)(k)\;\mbox{\rm and }\;k\in D^{*}H(b,c)(s)
, which means “
\subset
” in (
3
) is verified.
\hfill\square

Consider the given set
S\subset Y
and a set-valued mapping
G\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
. The
preimage
of
S
under
G
is denoted

G^{-1}(S):=\{x\in X\ |\ G(x)\cap S\neq\emptyset\}.

The next proposition provides a formula for the normal cone to
G^{-1}(S)
through the normal cone of
S
and the coderivative of
G
.

Proposition 3.2

Consider a convex set-valued mapping
G\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
, a convex set
S\subset Y
, and assume that

\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)\big)\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,(S)\neq\emptyset.

(6)

Then, for
u\in G^{-1}(S)
and
v\in G(u)\cap S
we have the formula

N(u;G^{-1}(S))=D^{*}G(u,v)\big(N(v;S)\big).

Proof.
Observe that the set
G^{-1}(S)
is clearly convex. Pick
f\in N(u;G^{-1}(S))
,

\langle f,x-u\rangle\leq 0\ \text{ for}\ x\in G^{-1}(S),\ \text{i.e.},\ G(x)\cap S\neq\emptyset.

Let us consider convex subsets of
X\times Y
,
\Lambda_{1}:=\text{gph}\,(G)\;\mbox{ and }\;\Lambda_{2}:=X\times S
. One can see that
\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}=X\times\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G)-S\big).

Using (
6
), we yield

0\in\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1})-\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{2})=\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2})=X\times\big(\mbox{\rm core}\,(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(G))-\mbox{\rm core}\,(S)\big),

and hence
\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{1})\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda_{2})\neq\emptyset
. Applying
[
14
, Theorem 5.4]
we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}(f,0)\in N((u,v);\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})&=N((u,v);\Lambda_{1})+N((u,v);\Lambda_{2})\\
&=N((u,v);\text{gph}\,(G))+[\{0\}\times N(v;S)]\end{array}

which gives us

(f,0)=(f,-k)+(0,k)\ \text{where}\ (f,-k)\in N((u,v);\text{gph}\,(G))\ \text{and}\ k\in N(v;S).

Hence,
f\in D^{*}G(u,v)(k)
and we get the inclusion

N(u;G^{-1}(S))\subset D^{*}G(u,v)\big(N(v;S)\big).

The opposite inclusion is trivial, and thus we are done.

\hfill\square

Next, we turn our attention into
parametric constraint systems
and present the precise formula for the coderivative of those. Given vector spaces
X,Y
and
Z
, we consider convex sets
\Lambda\subset X\times Y
and
S\subset Z
, and a mapping
\phi\colon X\times Y\to Z
such that
\phi^{-1}(S)
and
\mbox{\rm rge}\,(\phi)
are convex. Let the set-valued mapping
G\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
be defined by

G(x):=\{y\in Y\ |\ (x,y)\in\Lambda,\ \phi(x,y)\in S.\}

(7)

Note that the graph of
G
here is convex. We have the following result.

Theorem 3.3

Consider sets
S,\Lambda
and the function
\phi
as described above. Let
G
be the set-valued mapping in (
7
). Assume that
\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm rge}\,(\phi)\big)\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,(S)\neq\emptyset
. Furthermore, if either
\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\phi^{-1}(S)\big)\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\;\text{or}\;\phi^{-1}(S)\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
is satisfied.
Then for
(u,v)\in\text{gph}\,(G)
with
w=\phi(u,v)\in S
and any
h\in Y^{\prime}

D^{*}G(u,v)(h)=\{f\in X^{\prime}\ |\ (f,-h)\in D^{*}\phi(u,v,w)(N(w;S))+N((u,v);\Lambda)\}.

Proof.
We first notice that
\text{gph}\,(G)=\phi^{-1}(S)\cap\Lambda
. Given
(u,v)\in\text{gph}\,(G)
and
h\in Y^{\prime}
we obtain the coderivative of (
7
) as follows

D^{*}G(u,v)(h)=\{f\in X^{\prime}\ |\ (f,-h)\in N((u,v);\phi^{-1}(S)\cap\Lambda)\}.

Using the assumptions and applying
[
13
, Theorem 3.3]
give us

N((u,v);\text{gph}\,(G))=N((u,v);\phi^{-1}(S))+N((u,v);\Lambda).

Next, observe that
N\big((u,v);\phi^{-1}(S)\big)=D^{*}\phi(u,v,w)(N(w;S))
by using Proposition
3.2
,
and hence the proof is done.

\hfill\square

Given set-valued mappings
G_{1},G_{2}\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
, the intersection
G_{1}\cap G_{2}\colon X\;{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\;Y
is define by

(G_{1}\cap G_{2})(x):=G_{1}(x)\cap G_{2}(x),\;x\in X.

In the following theorem we present a representation of the coderivative for the intersection of two set-valued mapping.

Theorem 3.4

Consider convex set-valued mappings
G_{1},G_{2}\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
and assume

\text{core}(\text{gph}\,G_{1})\cap\text{gph}\,G_{2}\neq\emptyset.

Then, for
v\in(G_{1}\cap G_{2})(u)
and any
g\in Y^{\prime}
we have the representation

D^{*}(G_{1}\cap G_{2})(u,v)(g)=\bigcup_{g_{1}+g_{2}=g}\big(D^{*}G_{1}(u,v)(g_{1})+D^{*}G_{2}(u,v)(g_{2})\big).

(8)

Proof.
The inclusion “
\supset
” in (
8
) is straightforward. We now prove the inclusion “
\subset
” in (
8
). Let
v\in(G_{1}\cap G_{2})(u)
, and
f\in D^{*}(G_{1}\cap G_{2})(u,v)(g)
we have

(f,-g)\in N\big((u,v);{\text{gph}\,}(G_{1}\cap G_{2})\big)=N\big((u,v);(\text{gph}\,G_{1})\cap(\text{gph}\,G_{2})\big).

Then applying
[
13
, Theorem 3.3]
under the corresponding assumptions yields

\displaystyle(f,-g)\in N\big((u,v);{\rm gph}(G_{1}\cap G_{2})\big)=N\big((u,v);\text{gph}\,G_{1}\big)+N\big((u,v);\text{gph}\,G_{2}\big).

Thus,
(f,-g)=(f_{1},-g_{1})+(f_{2},-g_{2})
with
(f_{i},-g_{i})\in N((u,v);\text{gph}\,G_{i}))
for
i=1,2
. Therefore,
f\in D^{*}G_{1}(u,v)(g_{1})+D^{*}G_{2}(u,v)(g_{2})
and
g=g_{1}+g_{2}
, which justify the inclusion “
\subset
” in (
8
).
\hfill\square

By a simple proof, the following lemma gives a representation for
\mbox{\rm core}\,(\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi))
, where
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is an extended real value function, which will be used in proving the next theorem.

Lemma 3.5

Consider
\phi\colon X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
, if
\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)
is core-solid, then
\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi)
is also core-solid, and we have the representation

\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi)\big)=\{(x,\lambda)\in X\times\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;x\in\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\big),\phi(x)<\lambda\}.

We now derive a formula for the subdifferential of the maxima of finitely many convex functions, which will be used in proving the necessary optimality conditions for the constrained optimization problem on vector spaces, Theorem
5.3
. This will again use the normal cone intersection rule
[
14
, Theorem 5.4]
. Given functions
\phi_{i}\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, the maximum function
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is

\phi(x):=\max\{\phi_{i}(x)\;\big|\;i=1,\cdots,k\},\;x\in X.

(9)

Theorem 3.6

Consider the maximal function (
9
), where each
\phi_{i}\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is convex. Define

I(\bar{u}):=\{i=1,\cdots,k\;\big|\;\phi_{i}(\bar{u})=\phi(\bar{u})\}

(10)

as the active index set at
\bar{u}\in\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi_{i})
, it always holds

\partial\phi(\bar{u})\supset{\rm co}\big(\cup_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\partial\phi_{i}(\bar{u})\big).

(11)

If in addition, the core qualification

\bar{u}\in\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi_{i})\big),

(12)

is satisfied, the subdifferential maximum rule holds

\partial\phi(\bar{u})={\rm co}\big(\cup_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\partial\phi_{i}(\bar{u})\big).

(13)

Proof.
We first show that
\partial\phi_{i}(\bar{u})\subset\partial\phi(\bar{u})\;\text{holds whenever}\;i\in I(\bar{u}).
Let
i\in I(\bar{u})
and
f\in\partial\phi_{i}(\bar{u})
. Using (
10
) we obtain

\langle f,z-\bar{u}\rangle\leq\phi_{i}(z)-\phi_{i}(\bar{u})=\phi_{i}(z)-\phi(\bar{u})\leq\phi(z)-\phi(\bar{u})\;\forall z\in X.

Hence
f\in\partial\phi(\bar{u})
and (
11
) is deduced from the convexity of
\partial\phi(\bar{u})
.

Next, we justify the inclusion
``\subset"
in (
13
), notice that (
9
) implies

\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi)=\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i}).

Picking any
f\in\partial\phi(\bar{u})
, we get

(f,-1)\in N\big((\bar{u},\phi(\bar{u}));\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi)\big)=N\big((\bar{u},\phi(\bar{u}));\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big).

Under the qualification (
12
), Lemma
3.5
shows that
\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big)\neq\emptyset
. Applying
[
14
, Theorem 5.4]
we get

(f,-1)\in N\big((\bar{u},\phi(\bar{u}));\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}N\big((\bar{u},\bar{\gamma});\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big)\;\text{with}\;\bar{\gamma}:=\phi(\bar{u}).

Observe that
\bar{\gamma}>\phi_{i}(\bar{u})
when
i\notin I(\bar{u})
, and hence
(\bar{u},\bar{\gamma})\in\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big)
for such
i
, and therefore
N\big((\bar{u},\bar{\gamma});\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big)=\{0,0\}
for all
i\notin I(\bar{u})
.
Therefore,

(f,-1)\in\sum_{i\in I(\bar{u})}N\big((\bar{u},\phi_{i}(\bar{u});\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i}))\big),

and we yield the representation

(f,-1)=\sum_{i\in I(\bar{u})}(f_{i},-\gamma_{i}),\;\text{where}\;\ (f_{i},-\gamma_{i})\in N\big((\bar{u},\phi(\bar{u}));\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{i})\big).

We can also obtain
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
and
f_{i}\in\gamma_{i}\partial\phi_{i}(\bar{u})
when
i\in I(\bar{u})
. Note that since
\sum_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\gamma_{i}=1
, we can rewrite
f
as
f=\sum_{i\in I(\bar{u})}f_{i}\in{\rm co}\big(\cup_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\partial\phi_{i}(\bar{u})\big).

\hfill\square

4
Conjugate Calculus and Duality in Vector Spaces

Here, we further develop calculus rules for Fenchel conjugates in the vector space setting, using the relaxation of qualification conditions under the convex set extremality, see more in
[
13
]
. These results are then used to develop a new duality theorem in vector spaces. Similar work for polyhedral settings in LCTVS can be found in
[
16
]
.

Let us first recall the
support function
definition. Consider a nonempty subset
\Lambda\subset X
, the
support function
that is associated with
\Lambda
,
\sigma_{\Lambda}\colon X^{\prime}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

\sigma_{\Lambda}(f):=\sup\{\langle f,u\rangle\;\big|\;u\in\Lambda\},\;f\in X^{\prime}.

In the following theorem we establish a representation for the support function of the intersection of two convex sets in general vector spaces.

Theorem 4.1

Consider nonempty and convex subsets
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset X
and assume that
\text{core}(\Lambda_{1})\cap\Lambda_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Then, we have the support function of the intersection
\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
as follows

\big(\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}\big)(f)=\big(\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}\square\sigma_{\Lambda_{2}}\big)(f)\;\;\mbox{ for all }\;f\in X^{\prime}.

(14)

In addition, for any
f\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}})
, there exist
f_{1},f_{2}\in X^{\prime}
such that
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
and

(\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}})(f)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}(f_{1})+\sigma_{\Lambda_{2}}(f_{2}).

(15)

Proof.
We first prove the inequality “
\geq
” in (
14
). Let
f\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}})
, and denote
\omega:=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f)\in\mathbb{R}
. From the definition, we have
\langle f,x\rangle\leq\omega\;\mbox{\rm for}\;x\in\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
. We then define the nonempty convex subsets of

X\times\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\Pi_{1}:=\big\{(x,\gamma)\in X\times\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;x\in\Lambda_{1},\;\gamma\leq\langle f,x\rangle-\omega\big\},\quad\Pi_{2}:=\Lambda_{2}\times[0,\infty).\end{array}

From the construction of
\Pi_{1}
, we can easily see that

\text{core}(\Pi_{1})=\{x\in\text{core}(\Lambda_{1}),\;\gamma<\langle f,x\rangle-\omega\}

and hence
\text{core}(\Pi_{1})\cap\Pi_{2}=\emptyset
. Applying
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
we obtain the element
(0,0)\neq(g,\eta)\in X^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
for which

\langle g,x\rangle+\eta\gamma_{1}\leq\langle g,y\rangle+\eta\gamma_{2}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;(x,\gamma_{1})\in\Pi_{1},\;(y,\gamma_{2})\in\Pi_{2}.

(17)

Furthermore, there exist
(\tilde{x},\tilde{\gamma}_{1})\in\Pi_{1}
and
(\tilde{y},\tilde{\gamma}_{2})\in\Pi_{2}
satisfying

\langle g,\tilde{x}\rangle+\tilde{\gamma}_{1}\eta<\langle g,\tilde{y}\rangle+\tilde{\gamma}_{2}\eta.

(18)

If
\eta=0
we can reduce (
18
) to
\langle g,\tilde{x}\rangle<\langle g,\tilde{y}\rangle
. Using
(\bar{u},\langle f,\bar{u}\rangle-\omega)\in\Pi_{1}
and
(\bar{u},0)\in\Pi_{2}
in (
17
) gives us
\eta\geq 0
. Now we will show that
\eta>0
. By contradiction, suppose
\eta=0
. From (
17
) and (
18
),
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
can be properly separated which contradicts
\text{core}(\Lambda_{1})\cap\Lambda_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Thus,
\eta>0
.

Choosing the pairs
(x,\langle f,x\rangle-\omega)\in\Pi_{1}
in (
17
) and
(y,0)\in\Pi_{2}
yields

\langle g,x\rangle+\eta(\langle f,x\rangle-\omega)\leq\langle g,y\rangle\;\;\mbox{\rm with }\;\eta>0,

which leads us to
\big\langle g/\eta+f,x\big\rangle+\big\langle-g/\eta,y\big\rangle\leq\omega
for
x\in\Lambda_{1},\;y\in\Lambda_{2}
. Letting
f_{1}:=g/\eta+f
and
f_{2}:=-g/\eta
, we obtain the inequality “
\geq
” in (
14
).

The proof of the inequality “
\leq
” in (
14
) is trivial and hence (
15
) follows.
\hfill\square

Recall that the
Fenchel conjugate
of
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted
\phi^{*}\colon X^{\prime}\to[-\infty,\infty]
and is given by

\phi^{*}(f):=\sup\{\langle f,u\rangle-\phi(u)\ |\ u\in X\},\;f\in X^{\prime}.

Furthermore, if
\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\neq\emptyset
, then
\phi^{*}\colon X^{\prime}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is convex and

\phi^{*}(f):=\sup\{\langle f,u\rangle-\phi(u)\ |\ u\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\},\;f\in X^{\prime}.

Now there is a connection between the Fenchel conjugate of a function and the support function of its epigraph which our geometric approach to conjugate calculus relies upon. This crucial connection is shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2

Given a proper function
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, it holds that

\phi^{*}(f)=\sigma_{{\rm epi}(\phi)}(f,-1)\;\mbox{ whenever }\;f\in X^{\prime}.

Notice that the proof of this lemma is similar to
[
16
, Lemma 6.2]
and hence omitted here.

We are now ready to present the
conjugate sum rule
in vector spaces.

Theorem 4.3

Consider proper convex functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
on a vector space
X
. Suppose
\text{core}\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi_{1})\big)\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi_{2})\neq\emptyset
. We then have the conjugate sum rule

(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)=\big(\phi_{1}^{*}\square\phi_{2}^{*}\big)(f)\;\mbox{ for all }\;f\in X^{\prime}.

(19)

Furthermore, the infimum in
(\phi_{1}^{*}\square\phi_{2}^{*})(f)
is attained, i.e., for
f\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}
there exist
f_{1},f_{2}\in X^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)=\phi_{1}^{*}(f_{1})+\phi_{2}^{*}(f_{2}),\quad f=f_{1}+f_{2}.

Proof.
Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in X^{\prime}
with
f_{1}+f_{2}=f
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi_{1}^{*}(f_{1})+\phi_{2}^{*}(f_{2})&=\sup\big\{\langle f_{1},z\rangle-\phi_{1}(z)\;\big|\;z\in X\big\}+\sup\big\{\langle f_{2},z\rangle-\phi_{2}(z)\;\big|\;z\in X\big\}\\
&\geq\sup\big\{\langle f_{1},z\rangle-\phi_{1}(z)+\langle f_{2},z\rangle-\phi_{2}(z)\;\big|\;z\in X\big\}\\
&=\sup\big\{\langle f,z\rangle-(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})(z)\;\big|\;z\in X\big\}=(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f).\end{array}

Note that the given assumption is not used in the proof of this inequality.

Next, we prove that
(\phi_{1}^{*}\square\phi_{2}^{*})(f)\leq(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)
. It suffices to only consider the case when
(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)<\infty
. Define two convex sets by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\Lambda_{1}:=\big\{(z,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;\gamma_{1}\geq\phi_{1}(z)\big\}=\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi_{1})\times\mathbb{R},\\
&\Lambda_{2}:=\big\{(z,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in X\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\;\big|\;\gamma_{2}\geq\phi_{2}(z)\big\}.\end{array}

Similarly to Lemma
4.2
we obtain the representation

(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,-1,-1).

(24)

Since
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
satisfy the requirements in Theorem
4.1
, applying it to (
24
) produces triples
(f_{1},-u_{1},-u_{2})\in X^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
and
(f_{2},-\upsilon_{1},-\upsilon_{2})\in X^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
(f,-1,-1)=(f_{1},-u_{1},-u_{2})+(f_{2},-\upsilon_{1},-\upsilon_{2})
. Thus, (
24
) becomes

(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,-1,-1)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}(f_{1},-u_{1},-u_{2})+\sigma_{\Lambda_{2}}(f_{2},-\upsilon_{1},-\upsilon_{2}).

By the construction of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
, it must hold that
u_{2}=\upsilon_{1}=0
. Applying Lemma
4.2
and considering (
4
) gives us

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}(f)&=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,-1,-1)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}(f_{1},-1,0)+\sigma_{\Lambda_{2}}(f_{2},0,-1)\\
&=\sigma_{{\rm epi}(\phi_{1})}(f,-1)+\sigma_{{\rm epi}(\phi_{2})}(f_{2},-1)\\
&=\phi_{1}^{*}(f_{1})+\phi_{2}^{*}(f_{2})\geq\big(\phi_{1}^{*}\square\phi_{2}^{*}\big)(f).\end{array}

This validates the sum rule (
19
) and hence the infimum is attained.
\hfill\square

We next introduce the conjugate chain rule, which will be used in the proof of the composite convex Fenchel duality result.

Theorem 4.4

Consider a linear mapping between vector spaces
X,Y
,
A\colon X\to Y
and a convex function
\phi:Y\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. If

AX\cap\text{core}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\phi)\neq\emptyset,

(26)

then we have a representation of the conjugate chain rule

(\phi\circ A)^{*}(f)=\inf\{\phi^{*}(s)\;\big|\;s\in(A^{*})^{-1}(f)\},\;f\in X^{\prime}.

In addition, for
f\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi\circ A)^{*}
there exists
s\in(A^{*})^{-1}(f)
such that

(\phi\circ A)^{*}(f)=\phi^{*}(s).

Proof.
Let
s\in(A^{*})^{-1}(f)
. By definition,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi^{*}(s)&=\sup\big\{\langle s,v\rangle-\phi(v)\;\big|\;v\in Y\big\}\\
&\geq\sup\big\{\langle s,Au\rangle-\phi(Au)\;\big|\;u\in X\big\}\\
&=\sup\big\{\langle A^{*}s,u\rangle-(\phi\circ A)(u)\;\big|\;u\in X\big\}\\
&=\sup\big\{\langle f,u\rangle-(\phi\circ A)(u)\;\big|\;u\in X\big\}=(\phi\circ A)^{*}(f).\end{array}

Therefore,
\inf\big\{\phi^{*}(s)\;\big|\;s\in(A^{*})^{-1}(f)\big\}\geq(\phi\circ A)^{*}(f)
. This inequality is also clearly true if
(A^{*})^{-1}(f)=\emptyset
.

Next, we prove the opposite inequality. Let
f\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi\circ A)^{*}
and consider the following convex sets

\Lambda_{1}:=\text{gph}\,(A)\times\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{2}:=X\times\mbox{\rm epi}\,(\phi)\subset X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}.

(28)

From the structure of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
one can see that

(\phi\circ A)^{*}(f)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,0,-1)<\infty.

Applying Lemma
3.5
, qualification (
26
) gives

\text{core}(\mbox{\rm epi}\,\phi)=\{(y,\gamma)\;\big|\;y\in\text{core}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,\phi),\;\phi(y)<\gamma\},

and therefore,
\Lambda_{1}\cap\text{core}(\Lambda_{2})\neq\emptyset
. Using Theorem
4.1
, there exist triples
(f_{1},g_{1},u_{1})
and

(f_{2},g_{2},u_{2})
in
X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(f,0,-1)=(f_{1},g_{1},u_{1})+(f_{2},g_{2},u_{2}),

and
\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,0,-1)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}(f_{1},g_{1},u_{1})+\sigma_{\Lambda_{2}}(f_{2},g_{2},u_{2})
. From the structure of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
in (
28
), it follows that
u_{1}=0
and
f_{2}=0
. Hence, we obtain

\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,0,-1)=\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}}(f,g_{2},0)+\sigma_{\Lambda_{2}}(0,-g_{2},-1)

for some
g_{2}\in Y^{\prime}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,0,-1)&=\sup\big\{\langle f,u\rangle-\langle g_{2},Au\rangle\;\big|\;u\in X\big\}+\sigma_{{\rm epi}(\phi)}(g_{2},-1)\\
&=\sup\big\{\langle f-A^{*}g_{2},u\rangle\;\big|\;u\in X\big\}+\phi^{*}(g_{2}),\end{array}

so we can conclude
f=A^{*}g_{2}
. Therefore

\sigma_{\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}}(f,0,-1)=\phi^{*}(g_{2})\geq\inf\big\{\phi^{*}(s)\;\big|\;s\in(A^{*})^{-1}(f)\big\}.

Hence, our proof is done.
\hfill\square

The version of Fenchel strong duality in vector spaces under the core qualifications is then shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.5

Consider convex functions
\phi,\psi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
with further assumption
\text{core}\left(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\right)\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi)\neq\emptyset
. Then

\inf\{\phi(x)+\psi(x)\ |\ x\in X\}=\sup\{-\phi^{*}(-f)-\psi^{*}(f)\ |\ f\in X^{\prime}\}

Proof.
First we prove the inequality
``\leq
”. It is sufficient to only consider when
\omega:=\inf\{\phi(x)+\psi(x)\;\big|\;x\in X\}\in\mathbb{R}
. It’s easily seen that

\omega=\inf\{\phi(x)+\psi(x)\;\big|\;x\in X\}=-\sup\{\langle 0,x\rangle-(\phi+\psi)(x)\;\big|\;x\in X\}=-(\phi+\psi)^{*}(0).

Using the conjugate sum rule from Theorem
4.3
, we have a
f\in X^{\prime}
such that

\omega=-(\phi+\psi)^{*}(0)=-\phi^{*}(-f)-\psi^{*}(f).

So the inequality
``\leq
” is justified while
``\geq
” is straightforward.
\hfill\square

We next examine the
composite convex optimization
framework of Fenchel duality in general vector spaces that was discussed in another setting in
[
16
]
. Given vector spaces
X,Y
, proper convex functions
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
\psi\colon Y\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a linear operator
A\colon X\to Y
, we can consider the following primal minimization problem:

minimize

\displaystyle\phi(x)+\psi(Ax)\;\mbox{ subject to }\;x\in X.

(30)

The Fenchel dual problem of (
30
) is

maximize

\displaystyle-\phi^{*}(A^{*}g)-\psi^{*}(-g)\;\mbox{ subject to }\;g\in Y^{\prime}.

(31)

where
\phi^{*},\psi^{*}
are conjugate functions, and
A^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator.

We now establish a relationship between the solutions of the dual problem (
31
) and the primal problem (
30
), often termed
weak duality
. This requires no assumption of convexity on
\phi
or
\psi
, and the proof follows directly from definitions.

Proposition 4.6

Consider the primal problem (
30
) and its dual problem (
31
) with
\phi
and
\psi
not necessarily convex. Denote the optimal values for these problems by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\widehat{p}:=\inf_{x\in X}\big\{\phi(x)+\psi(Ax)\big\},\quad\displaystyle\widehat{d}:=\sup_{g\in Y^{*}}\big\{-\phi^{*}(A^{*}g)-\psi^{*}(-g)\big\}.\end{array}

Then
\widehat{p}\geq\widehat{d}
.

In the next theorem we present a version of the Fenchel-Rockafellar theorem on vector spaces under a core qualification.

Theorem 4.7

Consider the primal problem (
30
) and its dual problem (
31
). Suppose that the qualification (
26
) holds. In addition to the assumptions on
\phi,\psi
, and
A
in the problem formulation, assume either

\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi\circ A)\cap\text{core}\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\big)\neq\emptyset

or

\text{core}\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi\circ A)\big)\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\neq\emptyset.

Then
\widehat{p}=\widehat{d}
holds. Furthermore, if
\widehat{p}
is finite, the supremum in the definition of
\widehat{d}
is attained.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
4.6
, we just need to prove
\widehat{p}\leq\widehat{d}
. It is sufficient to only consider
\widehat{p}\in\mathbb{R}
. It is clear to see that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{p}:=\displaystyle\inf_{x\in X}\big\{\phi(x)+\psi(Ax)\big\}&=-\displaystyle\sup_{x\in X}\big\{\langle 0,x\rangle-[\phi+(\psi\circ A)](x)\big\}\\
&=-[\phi+(\psi\circ A)]^{*}(0).\end{array}

From the conjugate sum rule in Theorem
4.3
, there exists
f\in X^{\prime}
such that

\widehat{p}=-[\phi+(\psi\circ A)]^{*}(0)=-\phi^{*}(f)-(\psi\circ A)^{*}(-f).

Applying the conjugate chain rule in Theorem
4.4
yields
g\in Y^{\prime}
such that

A^{*}g=-f,\;\text{and}\;(\psi\circ A)^{*}(-f)=\psi^{*}(g).

Choosing
u=-g
, we obtain
\widehat{p}=-\phi^{*}(A^{*}u)-\psi^{*}(-u)\leq\widehat{d}
, which ensures the supremum in the definition of
\widehat{d}
is achieved.

\hfill\square

Corollary 4.8

The first qualification condition in Theorem
4.7
is satisfied if we have

\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi)\cap A(\text{core}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi))\neq\emptyset.

Proof.
Picking a
g_{0}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi)
with
g_{0}=A(u_{0})
for some value of
u_{0}\in\text{core}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi))
we have that
u_{0}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi\circ A)\cap\text{core}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi))
.
\hfill\square

5
Optimality Conditions in Convex Minimization and Lagrangian Duality in Vector Spaces

We first address the constrained optimization problem

\text{minimize}\;\phi(x)\;\text{subject to}\;x\in\Pi,

(34)

where
\Pi
is a nonempty and convex subset of a vector space
X
, and
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is convex. We call
\bar{u}\in\Pi
an
optimal solution
to (
34
) if

\phi(\bar{u})\leq\phi(x)\;\text{for all}\;x\in\Pi.

We also consider the unconstrained version of (
34
)

\text{minimize}\;\theta(x)\;\text{on}\;X,\;\text{where}\;\theta(x):=\phi(x)+\delta_{\Pi}(x).

(35)

Here,
\delta_{\Pi}(\cdot)
is the indicator function associated with
\Pi
. The following lemma presenting the relationship between (
34
) and (
35
) is easy to show.

Lemma 5.1

Given
\bar{u}\in\Pi
, in the convex setting of (
34
) the following are equivalent

(a)

\bar{u}
is an optimal solution to the constrained problem (
34
).

(b)

\bar{u}
is an optimal solution to the unconstrained problem (
35
).

Now we develop basic necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for optimal solutions to (
34
) in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2

Consider the setting of problem (
34
) with
\bar{u}\in\Pi\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)
. Suppose either

\Pi\cap\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\big)\neq\emptyset\quad\text{ or }\quad\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Pi)\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\neq\emptyset.

(36)

Then the following statements are equivalent

(a)

\bar{u}
is an optimal solution of (
34
).

(b)

0\in\partial\phi(\bar{u})+N(\bar{u};\Pi)
.

Proof.
Applying Lemma
5.1
, we only need to consider the unconstrained version (
35
) of problem (
34
). Using
[
30
, Proposition 3.29]
(the result also holds when
X
is a vector space without topological structure) provides us the equivalent characterization that
0\in\partial\theta(\bar{u})
. Applying the subdifferential sum rule from
[
13
, Corollary 4.3]
under the qualification conditions (
36
) yields

\partial\theta(\bar{u})=\partial\phi(\bar{u})+\partial\delta_{\Pi}(\bar{u})=\partial\phi(\bar{u})+N(\bar{u};\Pi).

Thus, the proof is done.
\hfill\square

Next, we add a functional constraint by a
C
-convex function to a partially ordered vector space by a convex cone. Let
\psi\colon X\to Y
, where
Y
is an Archimedean and generating partially ordered vector space by a cone
C\subset Y
. Lastly, let
\Lambda
be a convex subset of
X
and
\phi\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
a convex function. Then we will consider the problem

\displaystyle\text{minimize }\phi(x),

(P)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }x\in\Lambda,\;\psi(x)\in-C,

which can be rewritten in the form of (
34
) with

\Pi=\{x\in\Lambda\;|\;\psi(x)\in-C\}.

(37)

Each
x\in\Pi
is called a
feasible solution
of (
P
). The Lagrange function for (
P
) is then defined by

L(x,f):=\phi(x)+\langle f,\psi(x)\rangle,\;x\in X,\;f\in Y^{{}^{\prime}}.

(38)

For the next two theorems we will focus on the special case when
Y=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
C=\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}:=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\;\big|\;x_{i}\geq 0\}
. We are inspired by the work for
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
[
mn-easy
]
, and generalize the results to arbitrary vectors spaces. Then,
\psi(x)=\big(\psi_{1}(x),\dots,\psi_{m}(x)\big)
and for every feasible solution of (
P
) we can define the collection of active indices, given by

I(x):=\big\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\;\big|\;\psi_{i}(x)=0\big\}.

First, we present necessary optimality conditions for (
P
) in this special case.

Theorem 5.3

Suppose that
Y=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
C=\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
. Let
\bar{u}
be an optimal solution to (
P
) such that

\bar{u}\in\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\big)\cap\big(\cap_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{\rm core}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi_{i}))\big)\cap\Lambda.

(39)

Then there exist non negative multipliers
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}
, not all zero such that

0\in\gamma_{0}\partial\phi(\bar{u})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\partial\psi_{i}(\bar{u})+N(\bar{u};\Lambda),

(40)

and
\gamma_{i}\psi_{i}(\bar{u})=0
for
i=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.
Let us first define the convex maximum function

\eta(x):=\max\{\phi(x)-\phi(\bar{u}),\;\psi_{i}(x)\;\big|\;i=1,\dots,m\},\;x\in X.

Notice
\bar{u}
is a solution to

\text{minimize}\;\eta(x)\;\text{subject to}\;x\in\Lambda.

Under the qualification (
39
), Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
3.6
show that

0\in{\rm co}\left(\partial\phi(\bar{u})\cup\left(\cup_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\partial\psi_{i}(\bar{u})\right)\right)+N(\bar{u};\Lambda).

This yields
\gamma_{0}\geq 0
,
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
for
i\in I(\bar{u})
with
\gamma_{0}+\sum_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\gamma_{i}=1
and

0\in\gamma_{0}\partial\phi(\bar{u})+\sum_{i\in I(\bar{u})}\gamma_{i}\partial\psi_{i}(\bar{u})+N(\bar{u};\Lambda).

When
i\notin I(\bar{u})
, we can pick
\gamma_{i}=0
to get
\gamma_{i}\psi_{i}(\bar{u})=0
, while still maintaining
(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})\neq 0
. Then the theorem is proven.

\hfill\square

The Slater condition/constraint for (
P
) states that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists\widehat{x}\in\Lambda\;\mbox{\rm such that}\;\psi(\widehat{x})\in-\mbox{\rm core}\,(C).\end{array}

If
Y=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
C=\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
, then
\psi(\widehat{x})\in-\mbox{\rm core}\,(C)
reduces to
\psi_{i}(\widehat{x})<0,\;i=1,\dots,m
. With this we develop necessary and sufficient conditions for (
P
) in Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) form.

Theorem 5.4

Consider
Y=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
C=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
. Consider a feasible solution
\bar{u}
of (
P
) such that

\bar{u}\in\mbox{\rm core}\,\big(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\phi)\big)\cap\big(\cap_{i=1}^{m}\mbox{\rm core}\,(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(\psi_{i}))\big).

Suppose the Slater condition (
5
) is satisfied for (
P
). Then
\bar{u}
is an optimal solution to (
P
) if and only if there are nonnegative Lagrange multipliers
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}
such that

0\in\partial\phi(\bar{u})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\partial\psi_{i}(\bar{u})+N(\bar{u};\Lambda),

(43)

and
\gamma_{i}\psi_{i}(\bar{u})=0
for
i=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.
We first prove the necessity of (
43
), suppose by contradiction that
\gamma_{0}=0
in (
40
). Then we can find
0\neq(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
,
f_{i}\in\partial\psi_{i}(\bar{u})
, and
f\in N(\bar{u};\Lambda)
satisfying

0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}f_{i}+f.

Therefore,

0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\langle f_{i},x-\bar{u}\rangle+\langle f,x-\bar{u}\rangle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\big(\psi_{i}(x)-\psi_{i}(\bar{u})\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\psi_{i}(x)\;\text{for}\;x\in\Lambda,

which contradicts the Slater condition (
5
).

The sufficiency of (
43
) is shown by choosing a
f_{0}\in\partial\phi(\bar{u}),\;f_{i}\in\partial\psi_{i}(\bar{u})
, and
f\in N(\bar{u};\Lambda)
such that

0=f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}f_{i}+f

with
\gamma_{i}\psi_{i}(\bar{u})=0
and
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,m
. From the definition of subdifferential and normal cone we directly obtain that
\bar{u}
is an optimal solution of (
P
). Explicitly, for
x\in\Lambda
with
\psi_{i}(x)\leq 0,\;i=1,\dots,m

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle f_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}f_{i}+f,x-\bar{u}\rangle=\langle f_{0},x-\bar{u}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\langle f_{i},x-\bar{u}\rangle+\langle f,x-\bar{u}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\phi(x)-\phi(\bar{u})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\big(\psi_{i}(x)-\psi_{i}(\bar{u})\big)=\phi(x)-\phi(\bar{u})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\psi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(x)-\phi(\bar{u}),

which completes the proof.

\hfill\square

Consider the Lagrange function for (
P
) given by (
38
), the
Lagrange dual function

L^{\prime}\colon Y^{{}^{\prime}}\to[-\infty,\infty)
is defined by

L^{\prime}(f)=\inf_{x\in\Lambda}\{\phi(x)+\langle f,\psi(x)\rangle\},

and the Lagrange dual problem of (
P
) is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\mbox{\rm maximize }L^{\prime}(f),\;\mbox{\rm subject to }f\in C^{{}^{\prime}},\end{array}

where
C^{\prime}:=\{f\in Y^{\prime}\;|\;0\leq\langle f,c\rangle\;\text{for all}\;c\in C\}
is the dual cone of
C
. Then the optimal values in (
P
) and (
5
) are given by

p:=\inf\{\phi(x)\;|\;x\in\Pi\},\quad d:=\sup\{L^{\prime}(f)\;|\;f\in C^{\prime}\},

where
\Pi
is the set of feasible solutions of (
P
) defined in (
37
).

We now return to the case of a general
Y
as written in (
P
). At the end of this section we provide a sufficient condition which guarantees the
Lagrangian strong duality
(
p=d
) on vector spaces without topological structure. Results of this kind are one of the main goals of Lagrangian duality theory.

To proceed, we consider a system of inequalities with the unknown
x\in X
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi(x)<0,\\
&\psi(x)\in-C,\\
&x\in\Lambda.\end{aligned}\right.

(46)

We also consider a system of inequalities with unknowns
h\in Y^{\prime}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\phi(x)+\langle h,\psi(x)\rangle\geq 0\;\mbox{\rm for all }x\in\Lambda,\\
&h\in C^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

(47)

These two systems are deeply connected, as shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 5.5

Suppose the Slater condition (
5
) holds for (
P
). Then (
46
) has a solution if and only if (
47
) has no solution.

Proof.
Assume (
46
) has a solution
x_{0}
and suppose by contradiction (
47
) has a solution
\kappa_{0}\in C^{\prime}
. Then for the solution
x_{0}
of (
46
) one has

\phi(x_{0})+\langle\kappa_{0},\psi(x_{0})\rangle\leq\phi(x_{0})<0,

a clear contradiction. Note the Slater condition (
5
) is not used here.

For the opposite direction, suppose (
47
) is not solvable and by contradiction suppose (
46
) isn’t either. Consider the set

\Theta:=\{(\eta_{0},\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times Y\;|\;\phi(x)<\eta_{0},\;\psi(x)\leq_{C}\eta,\;\mbox{\rm for some }x\in\Lambda\}.

We first show that
\Theta
is a nonempty like-convex set and
(0,0)\notin\Theta
. Since (
47
) has no solution, we can find
x_{0}\in\Lambda
such that for
\kappa\in C^{\prime}
we get the strict inequality

\phi(x_{0})+\langle\kappa,\psi(x_{0})\rangle<0.

Fix
\overline{\kappa}\in C^{\prime}
, denote
\overline{\eta}=\psi(x_{0})
and
\overline{\eta}_{0}=-\langle\overline{\kappa},\psi(x_{0})\rangle
. Then
(\overline{\eta}_{0},\overline{\eta})\in\Theta
and hence
\Theta
is nonempty. Since (
46
) is not solvable,
(0,0)\notin\Theta
. In addition, the like-convexity of
\Theta
is implied by the
C
-convexity of
\psi
along with the convexity of
\phi
.

Next we show that
\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Theta)\neq\emptyset
. By the Slater condition (
5
) there exists
\hat{x}\in\Lambda
such that
\psi(\hat{x})\leq_{C}0

and observe that

\displaystyle\Theta\supset\{(\eta_{0},\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times Y\;|\;

\displaystyle\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0},\;\psi(\hat{x})\leq_{C}\eta\}

\displaystyle=\{\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0}\}\times\{\eta\in Y\;|\;\psi(\hat{x})\leq_{C}\eta\}.

Note that as
\psi(\hat{x})\leq_{C}0
, we have
C\subset\{\eta\in Y\;|\;\psi(\hat{x})\leq_{C}\eta\}
. Hence,

\Theta\supset\{\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0}\}\times C.

Now
\mbox{\rm core}\,(\{\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0}\})
is trivially non-empty due to being an open interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, using the Slater condition

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm core}\,(\{\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0}\}\times C)

\displaystyle=\mbox{\rm core}\,(\{\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0}\})\times\mbox{\rm core}\,(C)\neq\emptyset.

Hence
\emptyset\neq\mbox{\rm core}\,(\{\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;\phi(\hat{x})<\eta_{0}\}\times C)\subset\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Theta)
.

Since
(0,0)\notin\Theta
and
\mbox{\rm core}\,(\Theta)\neq\emptyset
,
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
shows that
\Theta
and
\{(0,0)\}
can be properly separated. This gives us multipliers
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\;\widetilde{\kappa}^{*}_{0}\in Y^{\prime}
, not all zero, such that

\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\eta_{0}+\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\eta\rangle\geq 0

(48)

whenever
(\eta_{0},\eta)\in\Theta
. In addition, there exists
(\widetilde{\eta}_{0},\widetilde{\eta})\in\Theta
satisfying the strict inequality

\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\widetilde{\eta}_{0}+\langle\widetilde{\kappa}^{*}_{0},\widetilde{\eta}\rangle>0.

(49)

By passing to the limit, observe that the inequality (
48
) also holds for any
(\eta_{0},\eta)\in\overline{\Theta}
. We will show that
\widetilde{\eta}_{0}>0
and
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*}\in C^{\prime}
.
By the Slater condition (
5
), there exists
\widehat{x}\in\Lambda
such that
\psi(\widehat{x})\leq_{C}0
. Fix any
\eta_{0}>0
such that
\eta_{0}>\phi(\widehat{x}),
one has
(\eta_{0},0)\in\Theta
and (
48
) shows that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\eta_{0}\geq 0.
If
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}<0,
then
\eta_{0}\leq 0,
and hence
\widehat{x}
is a solution of (
46
). This contradiction shows that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\geq 0.
To show
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*}\in C^{\prime}
, by a contradiction, suppose that
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*}\notin C^{\prime}
. Thus there exists
u_{0}\in C
, and hence
0\leq_{C}u_{0}
, such that
\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},u_{0}\rangle<0
. If
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}=0
, then we can see that
(\eta_{0},u_{0})\in\Theta
while (
48
) fails. If
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}>0,
set
u_{1}=(1-\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\eta_{0}}{\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},u_{0}\rangle})u_{0}
. Since
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\eta_{0}\geq 0
and
\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},u_{0}\rangle<0
, one has
u_{1}\in C
and hence
\psi(\widehat{x})\leq_{C}0\leq_{C}u_{1}
. We can see that
(\eta_{0},u_{1})\in\Theta
but (
48
) is not satisfied. This contradiction verifies that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\geq 0
and
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*}\in C^{\prime}
.

Since
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*}\in C^{\prime}
and
\psi(\widehat{x})\in-C
, one has

\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\psi(\widehat{x})\rangle\leq 0\leq\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},c\rangle\;\text{for all}\;c\in C.

(50)

Fix
x\in\Lambda
and see that

(\phi(x),\psi(x))\in\overline{\Theta}
. Thus,

\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}\phi(x)+\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\psi(x)\rangle\geq 0.

(51)

We are going to show that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}>0
. By a contradiction, suppose that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}=0
. Then, (
51
) shows
\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\psi(\widehat{x})\rangle\geq 0
and hence
\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\psi(\widehat{x})\rangle=\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},-\psi(\widehat{x})\rangle=0
follows from (
50
). Therefore, (
50
) can be written as

\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},-\psi(\widehat{x})\rangle=0\leq\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},c\rangle\;\text{for all}\;c\in C.

(52)

Using (
49
) gives us that
\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\widetilde{\eta}\rangle>0
.
Since
Y
is generating partially ordered by
C
and
\widetilde{\eta}\in Y
,
[
38
, Proposition 1.2]
shows that there exists
\widetilde{c}\in C
such that
\widetilde{\eta}\leq_{C}\widetilde{c}
. So,
\widetilde{c}-\widetilde{\eta}\in C
and hence

\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},-\psi(\widehat{x})\rangle=0<\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\widetilde{\eta}\rangle\leq\langle\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}^{*},\widetilde{c}\rangle.

(53)

The inequalities (
52
) and (
53
) show that the sets
C
and
\{-\psi(\widehat{x})\}
can be properly separated. Hence,
\psi(\widehat{x})\notin-\mbox{\rm core}\,(C)
, which contradicts with the Slater condition (
5
). Therefore,
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}>0
. Dividing (
51
) by
\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}
shows (
47
) has a solution. This contradicts our assumption, and so our proof is complete.
\hfill\square

Let

Proof.
The conclusion is implied directly from the problems’ definitions.

Applying Lemma
5.5
and Theorem
5.6
, we next state and prove the Lagrangian strong duality in vector spaces.

Theorem 5.7

Consider the primal problem (
P
) and its dual problem (
5
). Suppose the Slater condition (
5
) is satisfied. Then the Lagrangian duality holds, i.e.,
p=d
.

Proof.
If
p=-\infty
, then
d=-\infty
by Theorem
5.6
. Thus, we can assume that
p\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the system (
46
) can be extended to

\phi(x)-p<0,\psi(x)\in-C\;,x\in\Lambda

and it has no solution. Thus, applying Lemma
5.5
, the corresponding extension system of (
47
) has a solution, i.e., there exist
h\in C^{\prime}
such that

\phi(x)-p+\langle h,\psi(x)\rangle\geq 0\;\mbox{\rm for all }x\in\Lambda.

This gives us

d=\inf_{x\in\Lambda}\left\{\phi(x)+\langle h,\psi(x)\rangle\right\}\geq p.

Combining this inequality with Theorem
5.6
yields the result.
\hfill\square

Conclusion

This paper showed our efforts in revisiting and improving coderivative calculus rules in general vector spaces while comprehensively utilizing the qualifying conditions in
[
13
]
. We derived the subdifferential maximum rule by building on the normal cone intersection rule of
[
14

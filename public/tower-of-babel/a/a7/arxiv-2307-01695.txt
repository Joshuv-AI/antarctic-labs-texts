M.H.M. Rashid

Abstract
The Euclidean operator radius of two bounded linear operators in the Hilbert C ∗ \displaystyle C^{*} -module over 𝔄 \displaystyle\mathfrak{A} is given some precise bounds. Their relationship to recent findings in the literature that offer precise upper and lower bounds on the numerical radius of linear operators is also established.

Introduction

A common tool in operator and operator algebra theory is the Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-module. They provide a significant class of instances in the theory of operator
\displaystyle C^{*}
-modules. In addition, the theory of Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-modules is a fascinating subject in and of itself. interacting with operator algebra theory and incorporating concepts. It develops from non-commutative geometry and generates findings as well as new issues that draw attention.

An extension of the idea of a Hilbert space is the idea of a Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-module. Kaplansky
Kaplansky 1953
used such things for the first time. The induced representations of
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras by
Rieffel 1974
and Paschke’s PhD dissertation
Paschke 1973
marked the beginning of the study of Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-modules. The theory of operator algebras, operator
\displaystyle K
-theory, group representation theory, and theory of operator spaces all benefit from the employment of Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-modules. Additionally, it is utilized to research the Morita equivalence of
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras, the
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra quantum group, and the
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra
\displaystyle K
-theory (
Lance 1995
;
Wegge-Olsen 1993
).

A pre-Hilbert module over a
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
is a complex linear space
\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}
which is a right
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
-module (and
\displaystyle\lambda(ax)=(\lambda a)x=a(\lambda x)
, where
\displaystyle\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
\displaystyle a\in\mathfrak{A}
, and
\displaystyle x\in\mathfrak{E}
) equipped with an
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
-valued inner product

\displaystyle\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>:\mathfrak{E}\times\mathfrak{E}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}
satisfying:

(i)

\displaystyle\left<x,x\right>\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\left<x,x\right>=0
if and only if
\displaystyle x=0
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\left<x,\alpha y+\beta z\right>=\left<x,y\right>\alpha+\left<x,z\right>\beta
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\left<x,ya\right>=\left<x,y\right>a
;

(iv)

\displaystyle\left<x,y\right>=\left<y,x\right>^{*}
,

for all
\displaystyle x,y,z\in\mathfrak{E}
,
\displaystyle a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.

A pre-Hilbert
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
-module is called a Hilbert
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
-module or Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-module over
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
, if it is complete with respect to the norm
\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left<x,x\right>\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Suppose that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{F}
are Hilbert
\displaystyle C^{*}
-modules. We define
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{F})

to be the set of all maps
\displaystyle t:\mathfrak{E}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}
for which there is a map
\displaystyle t^{*}:\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{E}
which satisfies
\displaystyle\left<tx,y\right>=\left<x,t^{*}y\right>
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathfrak{E}
and
\displaystyle y\in\mathfrak{F}
.
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{E})
is simply denoted by
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{E})

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{E})
is a
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra. A state on a
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
is a positive linear
functional on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}

The major goal of this study is to examine other specific situations of interest and to extend several inequalities in the literature to the Euclidean radius of two operators in a Hilbert
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
-module. Also offered are conclusions that link the operator norm, the typical numerical radius of a composite operator, and the Euclidean operator radius.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{E})
. The Euclidean operator radius is
defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{e}(\mathbf{t}):

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{e}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup\left\{\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,\mathbf{x}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,\mathbf{y}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\xi\in\mathfrak{E},\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1\right\}.

And the Euclidean operator norm is defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}:

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sqrt{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}^{*}\mathbf{y}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup\left\{\left(\psi\left(|\mathbf{x}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|\mathbf{y}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\xi\in\mathfrak{E},\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1\right\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is a norm on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}
.

Proof.

If
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})=0
, then obviously
\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
. If
\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
, then for every
\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
and each
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
such that
\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)=1
, we have
\displaystyle\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|^{2}\right)=0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
. We want to show that
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}\xi=0
for each
\displaystyle x\in\mathfrak{E}
.
Fix
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
.

1.

If
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=0
, then by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we have

\psi\left(\left<t_{j}\xi,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)=\psi\left(\left<t_{j}^{*}t_{j}\xi,\xi\right>\right)\leq\psi\left(\left<t_{j}^{*}t_{j}\xi,t_{j}^{*}t_{j}\xi\right>\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=0,

and so
\displaystyle t_{j}=0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=0
.

2.

If
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\xi|\right)\neq 0
, then by taking
\displaystyle y=\frac{x}{\psi\left(|\xi|\right)}
, then
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\zeta|\right)=1
. By Definition
2
,

\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|t_{j}\zeta\right|\right)=0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
and so
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi\left(|\xi|\right)}\psi\left(\left|t_{j}\xi\right|\right)=0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
.
Thus
\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|t_{j}\xi\right|\right)=0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
. Since for every
\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
,
we have
\displaystyle\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)=0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
. We conclude that
\displaystyle|t_{j}\xi|=0
for each
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
and for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
. So
\displaystyle t_{j}=0

for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
and so
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=0
.

On the other hand
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
is an abelian
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra, then by
(
Jiang 2013
, Theorem 3.6)
,
\displaystyle|\xi+\zeta|\leq|\xi|+|\zeta|

for each
\displaystyle\xi,\zeta\in\mathfrak{E}
. Thus
\displaystyle\left|t\xi+s\xi\right|\leq|t\xi|+|s\xi|
for all
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
. Hence

\psi\left(\left|t\xi+s\xi\right|\right)\leq\psi\left(|t\xi|\right)+\psi\left(|s\xi|\right).

Now by Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left(\psi\left|(t_{1}+q_{1})\xi\right|\right)^{2}+\left(\psi\left|(t_{2}+q_{2})\xi\right|\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|\right)+\psi\left(|q_{1}\xi|\right)\right)^{2}+\left(\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|\right)+\psi\left(|q_{2}\xi|\right)\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\psi\left(|q_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|q_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Taking the supremum over all
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
with
\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)=1
and
\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
, we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{q}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{q}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Finally, if
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})
, then we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|c\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left(\psi\left(|ct_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|ct_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=|c|\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=|c|{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

for all
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\mathfrak{E}\right)
. If
\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}
is a Hilbert
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}
-Modules, then

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\sup\left\{\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\xi,\zeta\in\mathfrak{E},\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})\\
,\mbox{and}\,\,\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)=\psi\left(\left|\zeta\right|\right)=1\right\}.

Proof.

Let

\varrho=\sup\left\{\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\xi,\zeta\in\mathfrak{E},\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})\\
,\mbox{and}\,\,\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)=\psi\left(\left|\zeta\right|\right)=1\right\}.

It is sufficient to prove that
\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\varrho
. If
\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
and
\displaystyle\xi,\zeta\in\mathfrak{E}
, such that
\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)=\psi\left(\left|\zeta\right|\right)=1
,
then by using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(\left<t_{1}\xi,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\psi\left(\left<\zeta,\zeta\right>\right)+\psi\left(\left<t_{2}\xi,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\psi\left(\left<\zeta,\zeta\right>\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)\psi\left(|\zeta|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\psi\left(|\zeta|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

and so
\displaystyle\varrho\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
.

For
\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
and
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
with
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1
, we have

\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)^{4}=\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|^{2}\right)=\psi\left(\left<t_{j}\xi,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)=\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)^{2}\psi\left(\left<\frac{t_{j}\xi}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)}\right>,t_{j}\xi\right)^{2},

where assume that
\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|t_{j}\xi\right|\right)\neq 0
for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
. Thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left|\psi\left(t_{1}\xi\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(t_{2}\xi\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(\left<\frac{t_{1}\xi}{\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|\right)},t_{1}\xi\right>\right)^{2}+\psi\left(\left<\frac{t_{2}\xi}{\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|\right)},t_{2}\xi\right>\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left|\psi\left(\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varrho

and hence
\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\varrho
.
∎

Recall that an operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathcal{L}^{n}(\mathfrak{E})
is said to be self-adjoint if

\displaystyle\mathbf{t}^{*}=(t_{1}^{*},t_{2}^{*})=(t_{1},t_{2})=\mathbf{t}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}=\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{n}(\mathfrak{E})
is self-adjoint, then

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\sup\left\{\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\xi\in\mathfrak{E},\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle M=\sup\left\{\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}:\xi\in\mathfrak{E},\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1\right\}
. If

\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{t}
is a self-adjoint, then by using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\leq\psi\left(\left<t_{j}\xi,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)\psi\left(\left<\xi,\xi\right>\right)=\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|^{2}\right)\psi\left(|\xi|^{2}\right)

for all
\displaystyle j=1,2
. If
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

By taking the supremum over all
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{E}
with
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1
, we obtain

M\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

For the converse, let
\displaystyle\xi,\zeta\in\mathfrak{E}
and
\displaystyle\psi\in\varpi(\mathfrak{A})
. Then

\psi\left(\left<\xi+\zeta,t_{j}(\xi+\zeta)\right>\right)-\psi\left(\left<\xi-\zeta,t_{j}(\xi-\zeta)\right>\right)=4\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)\,\,\mbox{for $\displaystyle j=1,2$}.

Consequently, by Minkowski’s inequality we have

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left|\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
=\left(\frac{1}{16}\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi+\zeta,t_{1}(\xi+\zeta)\right>\right)-\psi\left(\left<\xi-\zeta,t_{1}(\xi-\zeta)\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi+\zeta,t_{2}(\xi+\zeta)\right>\right)-\psi\left(\left<\xi-\zeta,t_{2}(\xi-\zeta)\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(\left[\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi+\zeta,t_{1}(\xi+\zeta)\right>\right)\right|+\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi-\zeta,t_{1}(\xi-\zeta)\right>\right)\right|\right]^{2}+\left[\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi+\zeta,t_{2}(\xi+\zeta)\right>\right)\right|+\left|\psi\left(\left<\xi-\zeta,t_{2}(\xi-\zeta)\right>\right)\right|\right]^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
=\frac{1}{4}\left(\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|^{4}\right)\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{1}(\xi+\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|^{4}\right)\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{2}(\xi+\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
+\frac{1}{4}\left(\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|^{4}\right)\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi-\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{1}(\xi-\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|^{4}\right)\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi-\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{2}(\xi-\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
=\frac{1}{4}\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|^{2}\right)\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{1}(\xi+\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{2}(\xi+\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
+\frac{1}{4}\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|^{2}\right)\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi-\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{1}(\xi-\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi-\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{2}(\xi-\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}\right.

Since
\displaystyle\psi\left(\left|\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right|\right)=\frac{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}=1
, we obtain

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{1}(\xi+\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi+\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{2}(\xi+\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq M\,\,\mbox{and}\\
\left(\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi-\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{1}(\xi-\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(\left<\frac{\xi-\zeta}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)},\frac{t_{2}(\xi-\zeta)}{\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|\right)}\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq M.\end{array}\right.

Hence

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left|\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}M\left(\psi\left(|\xi+\zeta|^{2}\right)+\psi\left(|\xi-\zeta|^{2}\right)\right)\\
=\frac{1}{4}M\psi\left(2|\xi|^{2}+2|\zeta|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}M\psi\left(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}\right).\end{array}\right.

If
\displaystyle y=\frac{t_{j}\xi}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)}
and
\displaystyle\psi\left(|\xi|\right)=1
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left|\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi\left(Re\left<\zeta,t_{2}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}M\psi\left(|\xi|^{2}+\left|\frac{t_{j}\xi}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{M}{2}\psi\left(|\xi|^{2}+\frac{|t_{j}\xi|^{2}}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{M}{2}\left(\psi\left(|\xi|^{2}\right)+\frac{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|^{2}\right)}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|^{2}\right)}\right)=M.

Therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\left|\frac{1}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)}\psi\left(Re\left<t_{j}\xi,t_{1}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{1}{\psi\left(|t_{j}\xi|\right)}\psi\left(Re\left<t_{2}\xi,t_{j}\xi\right>\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left(\left|\frac{1}{\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|\right)}Re\left(\psi\left(|t_{1}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{1}{\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|\right)}Re\left(\psi\left(|t_{2}\xi|^{2}\right)\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle a\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{E})
, then we get

w^{2}(a)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left[{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|a^{*}a+a^{*}a\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|a^{*}a-aa^{*}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\right]+\frac{1}{2}w(a^{2}).

(44)

\displaystyle a^{*}a=aa^{*}
. In this case we get in both sides of (

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|a\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle w(a^{2})=w^{2}(a)={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|a\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

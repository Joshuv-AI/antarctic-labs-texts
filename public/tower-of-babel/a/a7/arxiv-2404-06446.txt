An effective version of Fekete’s theorem
Norm Levenberg and Mayuresh Londhe

Abstract
. A classical result of Fekete gives necessary conditions on a compact set in the complex plane so that it contains infinitely many sets of conjugate algebraic integers. We prove an effective version of Fekete’s theorem in terms of a height function. As an application, we give a lower bound on the growth of the leading coefficient of certain polynomial sequences, generalizing a result by Schur. Lastly, we give an upper bound on minimal asymptotics of height over sequences of algebraic numbers.

Primary: 11G50, 31A15; Secondary:11R06, 30C85

1.
Introduction

Let
\Sigma
be a compact set in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
Fekete studied which compact sets
\Sigma
contain
only a finite number of sets of conjugate algebraic integers.
This involves the notion of the
transfinite diameter of

\Sigma
;
it is given by

d(\Sigma)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sup_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in\Sigma}\prod_{i<j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|^{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}\Big).

The transfinite diameter of a circle of radius
r
is equal to
r
, and for
a line segment of length
l
it is equal to
l/4
. It turns out that the quantity

d(\Sigma)
coincides with the logarithmic capacity of
\Sigma
, denoted by
{c}(\Sigma)
; see
Subsection
2.1
for the definition. The product
\prod_{i<j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|

is related to the resultant of
P
and its derivative
P^{\prime}
, where
P(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\xi_{j})
; see (
2.4
).
This motivates the use of potential-theoretic tools in studying sets of conjugate algebraic numbers.
We now state a result by Fekete:

Result 1.1

(Fekete,
[
Fek23
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact and symmetric about the real axis.
If
{c}(\Sigma)<1
then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma

such that there are only a finite number of algebraic integers
\alpha
so that
\alpha
and all its conjugates lie in
U
.

The above result is not true if we replace algebraic integers
by algebraic numbers in the statement,
for example, if
\Sigma=\{|z|=1/2\}
then all roots of
2^{n}z^{n}-1
are in
\Sigma
.
In
[
FS55
]
, Fekete, together with Szegö showed that the converse
of Result
1.1
is also true.

Our main results involve a notion of height

h_{\Sigma}({\alpha})

of an algebraic number
\alpha
relative
to a compact set
\Sigma
as defined in
[
Rum99
]
.
Let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the set of algebraic numbers in
\mathbb{C}
.
Given a nonzero
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, there is a polynomial
P_{\alpha}

with integer coefficients and leading coefficient
b_{\alpha}
and degree
\deg\alpha

that is irreducible over the integers and satisfies
P_{\alpha}(\alpha)=0

(
P_{\alpha}
is unique up to the sign of
b_{\alpha}
). For an algebraic integer
\alpha
,
b_{\alpha}=1
or
-1
.
We denote by
{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha)
the set of all conjugates of
\alpha
which is same as the set
of all roots of
P_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{C}
.
Recall that the (absolute logarithmic) Weil height of
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is given by

h({\alpha})=\frac{1}{\deg\alpha}\Big(\log|b_{\alpha}|+\sum_{x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha)}\max\{0,\log|x|\}\Big).

We refer the reader to
[
BG06
]
for an introduction to the theory of heights.
Note that
\log^{+}|z|:=\max\{0,\log|z|\}
is the Green’s function of the unit circle centered at 0
with pole at
\infty
.
For an arbitary compact set
\Sigma
with positive capacity,
the
height of
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with respect to

\Sigma
is defined as

h_{\Sigma}({\alpha})=\frac{1}{\deg\alpha}\Big(\log|b_{\alpha}|+\sum_{x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha)}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(x,\infty)\Big),

(1.1)

where
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)
denotes the Green’s function of the set
\Sigma
with pole at
\infty
.
Loosely speaking,
h_{\Sigma}({\alpha})
quantifies the distances of conjugates of
\alpha
from
\Sigma
.
See Section
2
for more details about the Green’s function and height
h_{\Sigma}
.
Also, see
[
Rum99
]
and
[
Pri11
]
for an application of this height
to limit distributions of sets of conjugate algebraic numbers.

In the case
{c}(\Sigma)\geq 1
and
\Sigma
is symmetric about the real axis,
it follows from the Fekete-Szegö result in
[
FS55
]
that
there exists a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\to 0
. When
0<{c}(\Sigma)<1
, we show
a nonzero uniform lower bound on
\liminf_{n\to\infty}h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact and symmetric about the real axis.
Assume that
0<{c}(\Sigma)<1
. Then for every sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

such that
\deg\alpha_{n}\to\infty
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}.

For any
\Sigma
with positive capacity,
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)-\log^{+}|z|

is bounded, thus there exist a constant
C
such that

|h(\alpha)-h_{\Sigma}(\alpha)|<C
for every
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Therefore, the height
h_{\Sigma}
also has the
Northcott property
:
for every
M>0
and
D>0
, the set

\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}:h_{\Sigma}({\alpha})\leq M,\,\deg\alpha\leq D\}

is a finite set.
Theorem
1.2
and the Northcott property of
h_{\Sigma}
immediately give us
the following effective version of Result
1.1
:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact and symmetric about the real axis.
Assume that
0<{c}(\Sigma)<1
. Then for any
\epsilon>0

there are only a finite number of algebraic
numbers

\alpha
such that

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}-\epsilon.

It follows from the properties of
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(x,\infty)
that if
\alpha
is an algebraic integer
all of whose conjugates are in a small neighborhood
of
\Sigma
then
h_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
is also small.

We next give an application of Theorem
1.2
to the growth of the leading coefficient of certain
polynomial sequences.
Let
\mathbb{Z}[z]
denote the set of polynomials with integer coefficients.
Issai Schur showed that restricting the location of zeros of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
with
simple zeros to
[-1,1]
leads to geometric growth of the leading coefficients of these polynomials.

Result 1.4

(Schur
[
Sch18
]
, Satz VII)
.

Let
\{Q_{n}\}
be a sequence of
polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
with simple zeros such that
\deg Q_{n}\to\infty
and all roots of
Q_{n}
are in
[-1,1]
.
Then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|{\rm lead}(Q_{n})|^{1/\deg Q_{n}}\geq\sqrt{2},

where
{\rm lead}(Q_{n})
is the leading coefficient of
Q_{n}
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}^{k}[z]
the set of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[z]

whose zeroes are repeated at most
k
times.
A version of Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
3.5
, is valid in a
more general setting of a sequence
of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}[z]
, using the notion of Mahler measure of a polynomial
in place of the height of an algebraic number (see Subsection
2.2
).
As an application, we get a lower bound on the growth of the leading coefficients
of a sequence of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}[z]
whose roots are “near”
\Sigma
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be as in the statement of Theorem
1.2
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Assume that
\{Q_{n}\}
is a sequence of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}[z]
such that
\deg Q_{n}\to\infty

and

\frac{1}{\deg Q_{n}}\sum_{Q_{n}(x)=0}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(x,\infty)\to 0

(e.g., if all roots of
\{Q_{n}\}
eventually belong to any neighborhood of
\Sigma
). Then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}|{\rm lead}(Q_{n})|^{1/\deg Q_{n}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{{c}(\Sigma)}}.

For the interval
[-1,1]
we have
{c}([-1,1])=1/2
; compare with Result
1.4
.
Pritsker (Proposition 1.7 in
[
Pri05
]
) proved a similar bound with the assumption that the roots lie in the set and with
an additional hypothesis of irreducibility.

Having given a lower bound on heights in Theorem
1.2
, we now turn our attention
to an upper bound. By Result
1.1
, if

{c}(\Sigma)<1
, then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
such that there are only finitely many
algebraic integers
\alpha
with the property that
\alpha
and all its conjugates lie in
U
.
We then ask: can we find a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of algebraic integers such that
“most” conjugates of
\alpha_{n}
are eventually near
\Sigma
?
In general, this can be achieved if the heights
h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})
go to
\infty
;
we show that this can also be achieved while keeping the heights bounded.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact and symmetric about the real axis. If
0<{c}(\Sigma)<1
,
then for every neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
, there exists a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of
distinct algebraic integers such that

\frac{\sharp\{{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n})\cap U\}}{\deg\alpha_{n}}\longrightarrow 1\quad{\text{a}s}\quad n\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.2)

Moreover, there exists a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of distinct algebraic integers
such that
\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfy (
1.2
) for any neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
and

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\to-\log{{c}(\Sigma)}
as
n\to\infty
.

Owing to Result
1.1
,
\deg{\alpha_{n}}\to\infty
in the above theorem.
In fact, we can find a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
as in Theorem
1.6
such that
the limiting distribution of the probability measures uniformly distributed on all conjugates of
\alpha_{n}
of is given by the equilibrium measure of
\Sigma
(see Subsection
2.1
for
the definition; also see Remark
4.1
).
Theorem
1.6
together with Theorem
1.2
immediately give us
uniform bounds on minimal asymptotics of
\{h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\}
over sequences of algebraic numbers.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
\Sigma
be as in the statement of Theorem
1.2
.
Let
L
denote the infimum of
\liminf_{n\to\infty}h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})
over all sequences of
algebraic numbers. Then

\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}\leq L\leq\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}.

Outine of proofs:

We briefly mention the idea of the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}
be a sequence
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\{h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\}
is convergent.
Let
\mu_{n}
be the probability measure uniformly distributed on all conjugates of
\alpha_{n}
, i.e.,

\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{\deg\alpha_{n}}\sum_{x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n})}\delta_{x}.

It follows from Lemma
2.4
that the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
has a convergent subsequence. Although a limiting measure

\mu
need not be compactly supported, it turns out that such a

\mu
has finite logarithmic energy
I(\mu)
and satisfies

\int\log^{+}|z|\,d\mu<\infty.

This allows us
to use potential-theoretic methods. Refining the argument in Serre
[
Ser19
]
, we further show that

\mu
satisfies the following arithmetic property: for any
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
, we have

\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu(z)\geq-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|b_{n}|}{\deg\alpha_{n}}-{\mathcal{E}}(\Sigma;\{\alpha_{n}\}),

where
b_{n}:=b_{\alpha_{n}}

and
{\mathcal{E}}(\Sigma;\{\alpha_{n}\})
is the escape rate of the sequence
{\alpha_{n}}
as defined in (
3.1
).
We use this property
to get a bound on the logarithmic energy
I(\mu)
:

I(\mu)\leq 2\Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|b_{n}|}{\deg\alpha_{n}}+{\mathcal{E}}(\Sigma;\{\alpha_{n}\})\Big).

Finally, we use a convexity
property of the logarithmic energy to conclude our result.

To prove Theorem
1.6
, we construct a sequence of auxiliary sets
E_{n}
so that

{c}(\Sigma\cup E_{n})=1
. The set
E_{n}
is chosen to be a subset of the level set

{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)=n
and symmetric about the real axis. Let
\nu_{n}
be the equilibrium measure
of
\Sigma\cup E_{n}
.
We solve a Dirichlet problem to determine how much mass
\nu_{n}
puts on the set

E_{n}
. Using this information in the definition of height together with the Fekete-Szegö result
in
[
FS55
]
, we construct the appropriate sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}
.

Remarks on
{c}(\Sigma)\geq 1
case:

As noted earlier,
in
[
FS55
]
, Fekete–Szegö showed the converse
of Result
1.1
, i.e., if
{c}(\Sigma)\geq 1
, then every neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma

contains infinitely many
algebraic integers
\alpha
so that
\alpha
and all its conjugates lie in
U
.
By shrinking
U
, we can get a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of algebraic integers
such that the sets of conjugates of
\alpha_{n}
are eventually contained in any
neighborhood of
\Sigma
. Bilu
[
Bil97
]
studied limit distributions of the measures
\mu_{n}
associated to
such sequences of algebraic integers,
more generally, sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

h(\alpha_{n})\to 0
,
in the case of the unit circle centered at 0.
Using potential-theoretic methods, limit distributions of
\mu_{n}
associated to
sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\to 0

have been studied for general sets
\Sigma
of logarithmic capacity 1
by Rumely
[
Rum99
]
and Pritsker
[
Pri11
]
.
For such sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, they showed the
limiting distribution of
\mu_{n}
is given by the equilibrium measure of
\Sigma
.
Recently potential-theoretic methods
have been employed when
{c}(\Sigma)>1
in
[
Smi21
]
to study limit distributions in connection
with the Schur–Siegel–Smyth trace problem (see also
[
OS23
]
).

We include the necessary potential-theoretic background in the next section, including a non-standard
result on logarithmic potentials of certain measures with unbounded support. Sections 3 and 4 give the
proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
respectively.
We conclude in Section
5
with some further questions.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Potential theory

We work exclusively in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
The term measure will refer to a positive Borel measure in
\mathbb{C}
unless otherwise stated.
Given such a measure
\mu
, we can form its logarithmic potential function

U_{\mu}(z)=\int\log\frac{1}{\lvert z-w\rvert}\,d\mu(w)

provided this integral exists. In this case, if
\mu
has compact support, then

U_{\mu}(z)=\mu(\mathbb{C})(-\log|z|+O(1))\ \hbox{as}\ z\to\infty.

(2.1)

This is a lower semicontinuous, superharmonic function on
\mathbb{C}
which is harmonic
off of the support of
\mu
. The logarithmic energy of
\mu
is

I(\mu):=\int\int\log\frac{1}{\lvert z-w\rvert}\,d\mu(w)\,d\mu(z);

this may be
+\infty
. If
\mu
is a positive measure with unbounded support such that

\int\log^{+}|z|\,d\mu(z)<\infty,

then
U_{\mu}
is still locally integrable and superharmonic, and
I(\mu)>-\infty
(see
[
CKL98
]
).

Given a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathcal{M}(K)
denote the
cone of probability measures supported in
K
. The logarithmic capacity of
K
is

{c}(K):=\exp[-\inf\{I(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{M}(K)\}];

this coincides with the transfinite diameter
d(K)
in the introduction
(see, for instance,
[
Ahl10
, Ch 2]
or
[
Ran95
, Ch 5]
). If
{c}(K)>0
,
the Frostman conditions (cf.,
[
Ran95
]
) tell us that there is a unique measure

\mu_{K}\in\mathcal{M}(K)
with
{c}(K)=\exp[-I(\mu_{K})]
and
U_{\mu_{K}}=I(\mu_{K})

quasi-everywhere (abbreviated as q.e.) on
K
;
i.e., on all of
K
except for a polar set. Here,
E\subset\mathbb{C}
is polar if there exists a subharmonic
function
u\not\equiv-\infty
on a neighborhood of
E
with
E\subset\{u=-\infty\}
;
for
E
compact this is equivalent to
{c}(E)=0
.
If
E\subset\mathbb{C}

is compact and polar, for any
K\subset\mathbb{C}
compact we have
{c}(K\cup E)={c}(K)
.
The measure
\mu_{K}
is called the
equilibrium measure of

K
.

For non-polar compact
K
, we will have
use of the Green’s function

{\rm{g}}_{K}(z,\infty):=-U_{\mu_{K}}(z)+I(\mu_{K})

for (the unbounded component of the complement of)
K
with pole at
\infty
; note that

{\rm{g}}_{K}(z,\infty)=0
q.e. on
K
. This is an upper semicontinuous function which is harmonic
outside of
K
; from (
2.1
), it follows that
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)-\log^{+}|z|

is bounded as stated in the introduction. If
U_{\mu_{K}}
(equivalently,
{\rm{g}}_{K}
) is continuous,
we say that
K
is regular.
This is the same as
U_{\mu_{K}}=I(\mu_{K})
(
{\rm{g}}_{K}=0
) on
K
(cf.
[
Ran95
]
).
The Green’s function of the circle (or closed disk) of radius
r
centered at
a
is

\log^{+}(|z-a|/r)
. For the real interval
K=[-1,1]
, we have

{\rm{g}}_{K}(z,\infty)=\log|z+\sqrt{z^{2}-1}|.

More generally, the Green’s function of
K=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
is given by

{\rm{g}}_{K}(z,\infty)=\log|f(z)+\sqrt{f(z)^{2}-1}|,

where
f(z)=\frac{2}{b-a}z-\frac{a+b}{b-a}
, and
{c}({K})=\frac{b-a}{4}
.
If
K
is the Julia set of a polynomial
P
of degree
n\geq 2
then its Green’s function is

{\rm{g}}_{K}(z,\infty)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{j}}\log^{+}|P^{j}(z)|,

where
P^{j}
is the
j
-fold composition of
P
with itself and

{c}(K)=|{\rm lead}(P)|^{-1/(n-1)}
.

It is a standard fact from potential theory that the logarithmic energy of compactly
supported measures satisfies a convexity
property (see
[
Ahl10
, Lemma 2.1]
or
[
ST97
, Lemma I.1.8]
).
It turns out that this property also holds for certain measures that are not
necessarily compactly supported; later we will need to consider measures
\mu
with unbounded support.

Result 2.1

(Theorem 2.5 of
[
CKL98
]
)
.

Let
\tau
be a signed measure with
\tau(\mathbb{C})=0
that satisfies

\int\log^{+}|z|\,d|\tau|<\infty
and
I(|\tau|)<\infty
.
Then the logarithmic energy
I(\tau)
of
\tau
is well-defined and
I(\tau)\geq 0
.
In particular,
if
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are probability measures satisfying

\int\log^{+}|z|\,d\tau_{i}<\infty
and
I(\tau_{i})<\infty
for
i=1,2
, then, considering
\tau=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
,

2\int U_{\tau_{1}}(z)\,d{\tau_{2}}(z)\leq{I(\tau_{1})+I(\tau_{2})}.

In the case where we consider
\tau=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
, the inequality follows because

I(\tau)={I(\tau_{1})+I(\tau_{2})}-2\int U_{\tau_{1}}(z)\,d{\tau_{2}}(z).

2.2.
Heights and Mahler measure

We recall the notion of height of
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with respect to a non-polar compact
set
\Sigma
as defined in (
1.1
). Let
P_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be an irreducible
polynomial with leading coefficient
b_{\alpha}
and degree
\deg\alpha
.
Then

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha):=\frac{1}{\deg\alpha}\Big(\log|b_{\alpha}|+\sum_{x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha)}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(x,\infty)\Big).

The Mahler measure of a polynomial

P_{n}(z)=a_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_{j})
of degree
n
(thus
a_{n}\not=0
) is

M(P_{n}):=\exp\Big[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P_{n}(e^{it})|dt\Big]=|a_{n}|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\max\{1,|\alpha_{j}|\},

the latter equality following from Jensen’s formula. Since the Green’s function for the unit circle

\Sigma=\{|z|=1\}
is
g_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)=\log^{+}|z|:=\max\{0,\log|z|\}
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\log M(P_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n}|+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{j},\infty).

This coincides, in the case where
P_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
is an irreducible polynomial with
roots
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, to the Weil height
h(\alpha_{j})
.

More generally, following Pritsker
[
Pri11
]
, we define the Mahler measure of a polynomial

P_{n}(z)=a_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_{j})
of degree
n
with respect to any non-polar compact set
\Sigma
as

M_{\Sigma}(P_{n}):=|a_{n}|\exp\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{j},\infty)\Big]

so that, if
P_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
is an irreducible polynomial with
roots
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
we again have the relation

\frac{1}{n}\log M_{\Sigma}(P_{n})=h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{j}).

(2.2)

Observe that
if
P=P_{1}P_{2}\cdots P_{n}
with
\deg P_{i}>0
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, then we have

M_{\Sigma}(P)^{\frac{1}{\deg P}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(M_{\Sigma}(P_{i})^{\frac{1}{\deg P_{i}}}\Big)^{\frac{\deg P_{i}}{\deg P}}

(2.3)

where
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\deg P_{i}}{\deg P}=1
.

Using this generalized notion of Mahler measure, Pritsker proved the following:

Result 2.2

(Pritsker,
[
Pri11
]
)
.

Let
P_{n}(z)=a_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_{j,n})

be a sequence of polynomials with integer coefficients and simple zeros.
Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact with
{c}(\Sigma)=1
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}(M_{\Sigma}(P_{n}))^{1/n}=1

if and only if

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_{n}|^{1/n}=1
;

(2)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}(\prod_{|\alpha_{j,n}|\geq R}|\alpha_{j,n}|)^{1/n}=1
; and

(3)

\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{\alpha_{j,n}}\to\mu_{\Sigma}
in the weak* topology as
n\to\infty
.

As an immediate corollary, we have the following result, which will be used later on.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact and symmetric about the real axis. Suppose that

{c}(\Sigma)=1
. Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}
be a sequence of distinct algebraic integers such that
for every neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

{{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n})}\subset U
for all

\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{\deg\alpha_{n}}\sum_{x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n})}\delta_{x}\longrightarrow\mu_{\Sigma}\quad{\text{a}s}\quad n\longrightarrow\infty,

where
\mu_{\Sigma}
is the equilibrium measure of
\Sigma
.

Next, we recall that the
resultant
of two polynomials

\displaystyle P(z)

\displaystyle=a_{n}z^{n}+\cdots+a_{0}=a_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_{j})

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\quad Q(z)

\displaystyle=b_{m}z^{m}+\cdots+b_{0}=b_{m}\prod_{k=1}^{m}(z-\beta_{k})

is given by

\hbox{Res}(P,Q):=a_{n}b_{m}\prod_{j=1,...,n;\ k=1,...,m}(\alpha_{j}-\beta_{k}).

For
P,Q\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
it follows that
\hbox{Res}(P,Q)\in\mathbb{Z}
(cf.,
[
CLO92
]
, page 153).
Thus, if

do not have any factor in common then
|\hbox{Res}(P,Q)|\geq 1
.
The
discriminant
of
P
is related to the resultant of

and
P^{\prime}
:

\hbox{Disc}(P)=\frac{(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}}{a_{n}}\cdot\hbox{Res}(P,P^{\prime})=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}a_{n}^{2n-2}\prod_{j\not=k}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}).

(2.4)

The reader will notice the resemblance to the quantity

\prod_{i<j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|^{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}
in the definition of transfinite diameter from the introduction.

Finally, recall that a sequence of measures
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in B}
on
\mathbb{C}
is

if for

\epsilon>0
there exists a compact set

such that

for all
\beta\in B
.
If

is tight and uniformly bounded in the total variation
norm, Prokhorov’s theorem implies that it has a convergent subsequence.
We will need the following application.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}
be a sequence in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
such that corresponding
sequence of heights
\{h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\}
is bounded.
If

is the probability measure uniformly distributed on all conjugates of
\alpha_{n}

the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
has a convergent subsequence in the weak* topology.

Proof.

We shall show that the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
is tight. Then the result follows from Prokhorov’s theorem.
Assume that the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
is not tight. By definition, there exists
\rho>0
such that
for every compact set

, we have
\mu_{n}(\mathbb{C}\setminus K)>\rho
for some

Since

from the sequence

\{h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\}

Thus the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
is tight, and there exists a convergent subsequence of
\{\mu_{n}\}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

)

–

(

\{\alpha_{n}\}

Fix a polynomial

Let
\phi
be a non-negative compactly supported continuous function.
Since

is an upper semicontinuous function that is bounded from above, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu(z)\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int\phi(z)\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu_{n}(z)

\displaystyle-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int(1-\phi(z))\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu_{n}(z).

(3.2)

For the last inequality, we use the fact that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}(k_{n}+l_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}k_{n}+\limsup_{n\to\infty}l_{n}
.
Note that, owing to Result
3.1

makes sense.

Since

Thus if

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int

\displaystyle(1-\phi(z))\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\deg\alpha_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n})\\
|x|\geq r\end{subarray}}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(x,\infty)+\delta\cdot O(1).

(3.4)

Since the choice of

in (
3.2

3.3
) and (
3
) in (

\frac{1}{\deg Q}\int\log|Q(z)|\,d\mu(z)\geq-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|b_{n}|}{\deg\alpha_{n}}-{\mathcal{E}}(\Sigma;\{\alpha_{n}\}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let

–

(

Proof.

Let
\phi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int U_{\mu}(z)\,d\mu_{n}(z)

2.3
),

\frac{1}{\deg Q_{n}}\log M_{\Sigma}(Q_{n})=\frac{\deg Q^{1}_{n}}{\deg Q_{n}}\Big(\frac{\log M_{\Sigma}(Q^{1}_{n})}{\deg Q^{1}_{n}}\Big)+\frac{\deg Q^{2}_{n}}{\deg Q_{n}}\Big(\frac{\log M_{\Sigma}(Q^{2}_{n})}{\deg Q^{2}_{n}}\Big).

(3.12)

Similar to (
3.12
),

{\log M_{\Sigma}(Q^{1}_{n})}/{\deg Q^{1}_{n}}
can be further written as a convex combination
using its irreducible sub-factors. Since these sub-factors are from the set
\mathcal{P}
, we have

\frac{\log M_{\Sigma}(Q^{1}_{n})}{\deg Q^{1}_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}-\epsilon.

Also,
{\log M_{\Sigma}(Q^{2}_{n})}/{\deg Q^{2}_{n}}
can be further written as a convex combination
using its irreducible sub-factors. Therefore,

\frac{\log M_{\Sigma}(Q^{2}_{n})}{\deg Q^{2}_{n}}>\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}-\epsilon.

Since the zeroes of each
Q_{n}
are repeated at most
k
times,
\{\deg Q^{1}_{n}\}
is bounded. Therefore, by
(
3.12
), there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
n\geq N
, we have

\frac{1}{\deg Q_{n}}\log M_{\Sigma}(Q_{n})>\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{{c}(\Sigma)}-\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

Corollary
1.5
follows from Theorem
3.5
(if
\Sigma
is not regular, for the case when all roots of
\{Q_{n}\}
eventually belong to any neighborhood of
\Sigma
, we use an argument as in the last part of the proof
of Theorem
1.2
).

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
. As in the last section, we assume that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
is compact and symmetric about the real axis with
0<{c}(\Sigma)<1
. Since
\Sigma
is symmetric about the real axis,

{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)={\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(\bar{z},\infty)
; thus level sets of

{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)
are also symmetric about the real axis.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

L_{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)=n\}.

We choose a compact subset
E_{n}
of
L_{n}
that is symmetric about the real axis and satisfies
{c}(\Sigma\cup E_{n})=1
.
For sufficiently large
n
, such a set always exists. To see this, we start with a compact subset
E
of
L_{n}

that is invariant under complex conjugation and which satisfies
{c}(\Sigma\cup E)>1
. For example, if we choose
E
with
{c}(E)>1
then
{c}(\Sigma\cup E)>1
.
Since capacity is a monotone function, by considering a decreasing family of subsets of
L_{n}
, we eventually get
a compact subset
E_{n}
of
L_{n}
that satisfies
{c}(\Sigma\cup E_{n})=1
.
Since
{c}(\Sigma)<1
, the set
E_{n}
is non-polar.

We recall the Fekete-Szegö result in
[
FS55
]
discussed in the introduction, i.e., if
{c}(K)\geq 1
and
K
is symmetric about the real axis, then every neighborhood
U
of
K

contains infinitely many
algebraic integers
\alpha
so that
\alpha
and all its conjugates lie in
U
. Let
\nu_{n}
be the equilibrium measure of
\Sigma\cup E_{n}
. Since
{c}(\Sigma\cup E_{n})=1
,
we can get a sequence
\{\alpha_{n,j}\}
of algebraic integers such that

{{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n,j})}
eventually belongs to any neighborhood of
\Sigma\cup E_{n}
.
By Lemma
2.3
,

\mu_{n,j}:=\frac{1}{\deg\alpha_{n,j}}\sum_{x\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n,j})}\delta_{x}\longrightarrow\nu_{n}\quad{\text{a}s}\quad j\longrightarrow\infty.

(4.1)

Fix a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
. We want to determine what fraction of
{{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n,j})}
is in
U
for sufficiently large
j
; thus we want to determine the value of
\nu_{n}(\Sigma)
.
To do this, we solve a Dirichlet problem on the domain
D_{n}
in the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, where
D_{n}
is the
component of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\Sigma\cup E_{n})
containing
\infty
.

Let
\phi
be the function on the boundary
\partial D_{n}
of
D_{n}
such that

\phi\equiv 0
on the exterior boundary of
\Sigma
and
\phi\equiv 1
on the exterior boundary of
E_{n}
.
Since
\phi
is continous on
\partial D_{n}
, there exists a unique bounded harmonic function
f
on
D_{n}
such that

\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\phi(\xi)
for q.e.
\xi\in\partial D_{n}
. Then, by definition,
f(\infty)=\nu_{n}(E_{n})
.
We show

f(z)=\frac{{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)-{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma\cup E_{n}}(z,\infty)}{n}.

Observe that, since
g_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)\equiv n
on
E_{n}
,
\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\phi(\xi)
for q.e.
\xi\in\partial D_{n}
.
The function
f
is harmonic and bounded on
D_{n}
with

\displaystyle f(\infty)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)-{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma\cup E_{n}}(z,\infty)}{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n}\log{c}(\Sigma).

Thus the function
f
defined above solves the Dirichlet problem for
\phi
.
Therefore, we have
\nu_{n}(\Sigma)=1+\frac{1}{n}\log{c}(\Sigma)
.

Now, owing to the above observations, there exist
j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{n,j^{\prime}}

=:\alpha_{n}
satisfies

\frac{\sharp\{{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n})\cap U\}}{\deg\alpha_{n}}\geq 1+\frac{1}{n}\log{c}(\Sigma)-\frac{1}{n}.

(4.2)

Note that, since
{c}(\Sigma)>0
, the right hand side of (
4.2
) converges to 1 as
n\to\infty
.
Since
n
is arbitrary, we get a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of algebraic integers that satisfy (
1.2
).

We shall now construct a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
of distinct algebraic integers
that satisfy (
1.2
) for any neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
and

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\to-\log{{c}(\Sigma)}
as
n\to\infty
.
Let
\{\alpha_{n,j}\}
be a sequence of algebraic integers as above.
There exists
J\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq J
,
we get
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)\geq n-\frac{1}{n^{2}}
for at least
\deg\alpha_{n,j}\cdot\Big(\nu_{n}(E_{n})-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)

many
z\in{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n,j})
.
Therefore, for all
j\geq J
, we get

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n,j})\geq\Big(n-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\Big(-\frac{1}{n}\log{c}(\Sigma)-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big).

(4.3)

Let
r>0
be such that
{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n,j})\subset\mathbb{D}(0;r)
for all
j
. Since
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)
is an upper semicontinuous function, by (
4.1
), we get

\limsup_{j\to\infty}h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n,j})=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{\{|z|\leq r\}}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)\,d\mu_{n,j}(z)\leq\int_{\{|z|\leq r\}}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)\,d\nu_{n}(z).

(4.4)

Using the definition of
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)
and the fact that
U_{\nu_{n}}(z)=0
q.e. on
\Sigma\cup E_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\int{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(z,\infty)\,d\nu_{n}(z)=\int(U_{\mu_{\Sigma}}(z)-\log{c}(\Sigma))\,d\nu_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=-\log{c}(\Sigma)+\int U_{\nu_{n}}(z)\,d\mu_{\Sigma}(z)

\displaystyle=-\log{c}(\Sigma).

(4.5)

By (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), there exist
j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{n,j^{\prime}}
satisfies

\lvert h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n,j^{\prime}})+\log{c}(\Sigma)\rvert<\frac{1}{n}

and

\frac{\sharp\{{\rm{Conj}}(\alpha_{n,j^{\prime}})\cap U_{n}\}}{\deg\alpha_{n,j^{\prime}}}\geq 1+\frac{1}{n}\log{c}(\Sigma)-\frac{1}{n},

where
U_{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm dist}(x,z)<\frac{1}{n}{\textnormal{ for some }}x\in\Sigma\}
.
Since
n
is arbitrary, we get a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}:=\alpha_{n,j^{\prime}}\}
of algebraic integers
that satisfy the required properties. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Remark 4.1
.

We can show that the sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\}
constructed above converge
in the weak* topology to the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\Sigma}
(and hence so does the appropriate subsequence
of the measures
\{\mu_{n,j}\}
). First, by construction,
\{\nu_{n}\}
is tight and
uniformly bounded in the total variation norm, so by Lemma
2.4
there exists a weak* convergent
subsequence which we continue to denote by
\{\nu_{n}\}
. Let
\mu
be its weak* limit.
Writing
\nu_{n}:=\gamma_{n}+\zeta_{n}
where
\gamma_{n}:=\nu_{n}|_{\Sigma}
and

\zeta_{n}:=\nu_{n}|_{E_{n}}
, we have
\gamma_{n}(\Sigma)=1+\frac{1}{n}\log{c}(\Sigma)\to 1

so that
\mu
is the weak* limit of
\{\gamma_{n}\}
and
\mu
is a probability measure
supported in
\Sigma
. Since the logarithmic potential
U_{\mu}
is lower semicontinuous, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_{\gamma_{n}}(z_{n})\geq U_{\mu}(z)\quad\hbox{for $z\in\mathbb{C}$}

where
z_{n}\to z
(cf., Theorem I.6.8 of
[
ST97
]
).
We will show that
U_{\mu}(z)\leq-\log{c}(\Sigma)
for q.e.
z\in\Sigma
.
For then
I(\mu)\leq-\log{c}(\Sigma)=I(\mu_{\Sigma})
and the result follows.

To this end, since
\nu_{n}
is the equilibrium measure of a set of logarithmic capacity equal to 1, we have

U_{\nu_{n}}(z)=U_{\gamma_{n}}(z)+U_{\zeta_{n}}(z)=0\quad\hbox{for q.e. $z\in\Sigma\cup E_{n}$}.

In particular, the above holds for q.e.
z\in\Sigma
. Thus

-\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{\zeta_{n}}(z)\geq U_{\mu}(z)\quad\hbox{for q.e. $z\in\Sigma$}.

Now for
z\in\Sigma
, since
E_{n}\to\infty
and
\zeta_{n}(E_{n})\to 0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{\zeta_{n}}(z)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{E_{n}}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,d\zeta_{n}(w)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{E_{n}}\log\frac{1}{|w|}\,d\zeta_{n}(w)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Bigl(-\int_{E_{n}}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(w,\infty)\,d\zeta_{n}(w)\Bigr).

Since
{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(w,\infty)\equiv n
on
E_{n}
, we get

\int_{E_{n}}{\rm{g}}_{\Sigma}(w,\infty)\,d\zeta_{n}(w)=n\zeta_{n}(E_{n})=n\bigl(-\frac{1}{n}\log c(\Sigma)\bigr)=-\log{c}(\Sigma).

Therefore, we have

U_{\mu}(z)\leq-\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{\zeta_{n}}(z)=-\log{c}(\Sigma)\quad\hbox{for q.e. $z\in\Sigma$}.

5.
Further discussion

There remain several relevant questions related to Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
.

(1)

Can the lower bound
-(1/2)\log{c}(\Sigma)
in Theorem
1.2
be improved?

(2)

Does there exist a sequence of distinct algebraic integers
\{\alpha_{n}\}
such that
1.2
holds for every neighborhood

U
of
\Sigma
and
h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\to L<-\log{{c}(\Sigma)}
as
n\to\infty
?

(3)

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}
be a sequence of distinct algebraic integers
such that
\{\alpha_{n}\}
satisfy (
1.2
) for any neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
and

h_{\Sigma}(\alpha_{n})\to-\log{{c}(\Sigma)}
as
n\to\infty
. Does the corresponding sequence of
probability measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
converge to the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\Sigma}
? If not, what other measures
\mu
can be realized in this fashion?

Note that, in the case where
\Sigma
is a compact set symmetric about the real axis with

{c}(\Sigma)\geq 1
, Orloski and Sardari
[
OS23
]
have recently characterized the so-called

arithmetic probability measures
on
\Sigma
, i.e., probability measures
\mu
on
\Sigma
which
are weak* limits of measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
coming from
\{\alpha_{n}\}
where all all conjugates
of
\alpha_{n}
eventually lie inside any fixed neighborhood of
\Sigma
. In our setting when
c(\Sigma)<1
,
necessarily in the second question above we consider the less restrictive hypothesis (
1.2
).

On sections of arithmetic fundamental groups of open p p -adic annuli.
Mohamed Saïdi

Abstract
We show the non-existence of sections of arithmetic fundamental groups of open p p -adic annuli of small radii , this implies the non-existence of sections of arithmetic fundamental groups of formal boundaries of formal germs of p p -adic curves .



§0. Introduction/Statement of the Main Result

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime integer,
k
a
p
-adic local field
(i.e.,
k/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a finite extension), with ring of integers
O_{k}
, uniformiser
\pi
, and residue field
F
.
Thus,
F
is a finite field of characteristic
p
.

Let
X\to\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}
be a flat, proper, relative
O_{k}
-curve, with
X
normal,
and
X_{k}\overset\text{def}\to{=}X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}k
geometrically connected.
Assume
X(F)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
x\in X^{\operatorname{cl}}(F)
be a closed point,
O_{X,x}
the local ring at
x
,
\widehat{O}_{X,x}
its completion, and
E\overset\text{def}\to{=}\widehat{O}_{X,x}\otimes_{O_{k}}k=\widehat{O}_{X,x}[\frac{1}{\pi}]
.
Write
X\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Spec}E
, which we assume to be geometrically connected. We shall refer to
X
as the
formal germ of
X
at
x
.

Let
\eta
be a geometric point of
X
with values in its generic point. Thus,
\eta
determines an algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
, and a geometric point

\bar{\eta}
of
X_{\overline{k}}\overset\text{def}\to{=}X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}\overline{k}
. There exists a canonical exact sequence of profinite groups (cf. [Grothendieck], Exposé IX, Théorème 6.1)

1\to\pi_{1}(X_{\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})\to\pi_{1}(X,\eta)\to G_{k}\to 1.

[formula]

Here,
\pi_{1}(X,\eta)
denotes the arithmetic étale fundamental group of
X
with base
point
\eta
,
\pi_{1}(X_{\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})
the étale fundamental group of
X_{\overline{k}}
with base
point
\bar{\eta}
, and
G_{k}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
the absolute Galois group of
k
.

The sequence (1) splits if
X(k)\neq\emptyset
. This is for example the case if the morphism
X\to\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}
is smooth at
x
.
If
X(k)=\emptyset
; for instance if
X
is stable and regular, and
x
is an ordinary
F
-rational double point of
X_{F}\overset\text{def}\to{=}X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}}F
, the existence of sections
s:G_{k}\to\pi_{1}(X,\eta)
of the projection
\pi_{1}(X,\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
would provide examples of sections of the projection
\pi_{1}(X_{k},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

which are
non-geometric
(
\eta
induces a geometric point of
X_{k}
, denoted also
\eta
, via the morphism
X_{k}\to X_{k}
),
i.e., which do not arise from rational points. These in turn will provide
counter-examples to the
p
-adic version of the Grothendieck anabelian section conjecture
.
This prompt the following question.

Question A

With the above notations, assume
X(k)=\emptyset
. Does the exact sequence (1) split?

In this note we investigate the case where
X
is a
p
-adic open annulus
. Let

A\overset\text{def}\to{=}O_{k}[[S]],\ \ \ B\overset\text{def}\to{=}A\otimes_{O_{k}}k=A\left[\frac{1}{\pi}\right],

D\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Spf}A
is the formal standard open disc, and
D\overset\text{def}\to{=}D_{k}=\operatorname{Spec}B
its ”generic fibre” which is the standard open disc centred at the point
"S=0"
.
Let
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}=\operatorname{Spec}k[S]
,

Y_{k}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
its smooth compactification with function field
k(S)
, and
Y=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{O_{k}}
the smooth compactification of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{O_{k}}=\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}[S]
.
We shall identify
A
with the completion of the local ring of
Y
at the closed point
"S=0"
.
We have a natural morphism
D\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, which induces an identification between the set of closed points of
D
and the set

\{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}:|S(x)|<1\}.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, let

A_{n}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\frac{O_{k}[[S,T]]}{(S^{n}T-\pi)},\ \ \ B_{n}\overset\text{def}\to{=}A_{n}\otimes_{O_{k}}k,\ \ \text{and}\ \ C_{n}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Spec}B_{n}.

The natural embedding
C_{n}\hookrightarrow D
induces an identification between the set of closed points of
C_{n}
and the open annulus

\{x\in D:|\pi|^{\frac{1}{n}}<|S(x)|<1\}.

Further, let
P\overset\text{def}\to{=}A_{(\pi)}
be the localisation of
A
at the ideal
(\pi)
, and
\widehat{P}
the completion of
P
, which is a complete discrete valuation ring
isomorphic to

O_{k}[[S]]\{S^{-1}\}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\Big\{\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{i}S^{i}:a_{i}\in O_{k},\ \underset{i\to-\infty}\to{\lim}|a_{i}|=0\Big\},

where
|\ |
is a normalised absolute value of
O_{k}
(cf. [Bourbaki],
\lx@sectionsign 2
, 5).
Let
L\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Fr}(\widehat{P})
be the fraction field of
\widehat{P}
, and
C_{\infty}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Spec}L
. We shall refer to
C_{\infty}
as a
formal boundary
of the formal germs
D
, and

C_{i}
for
i\geq 1
.
We have natural scheme morphisms

C_{\infty}\to\cdots\to C_{n+1}\to C_{n}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to D\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}.

Let
\eta
be a geometric point of
C_{\infty}
, which induces a geometric point (denoted also
\eta
) of
C_{n}
for
n\geq 1
.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we have an exact sequence of arithmetic fundamental groups

1\to\pi_{1}(C_{i,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})\to\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)\to G_{k}\to 1,

[formula]

where
\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)
denotes the arithmetic étale fundamental group of
C_{i}
with base
point
\eta
,
\pi_{1}(C_{i,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})
the étale fundamental group of
C_{i,\overline{k}}\overset\text{def}\to{=}C_{i}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}{\overline{k}}
with base
point
\bar{\eta}
; which is induced by
\eta
, and
G_{k}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
the absolute Galois group of
k
. Here
\overline{k}
is the algebraic closure of
k
determined by
\eta
.

Our main result in this note is the following.

Theorem A

We use notations as above. There exists an integer
N\geq 1
, such that for every integer
n\geq N
,
the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

doesn’t split
.

The author ignores, for the time being, if the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{1},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
splits or not.

As a corollary of Theorem A, we obtain the following.

Theorem B

The projection
\pi_{1}(C_{\infty},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

doesn’t split
.

One of the consequences of Theorems A, and B, is that one can not produce examples of sections of hyperbolic curves over
p
-adic local fields, which arise from sections of arithmetic fundamental groups of boundaries of formal fibres, or open annuli with small radii. Those sections would be non-geometric, hence would provide counter-examples to the
p
-adic version of the Grothendieck anabelian section conjecture.

Finally we observe the following. For
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, let

\pi_{1}(C_{i,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})^{\operatorname{ab}}
be the maximal abelian quotient of
\pi_{1}(C_{i,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})
, and consider the push-out diagram

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>\pi_{1}(C_{i,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})@>{}>{}>\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)@>{}>{}>G_{k}@>{}>{}>1\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\Big\|\\
1@>{}>{}>\pi_{1}(C_{i,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})^{\operatorname{ab}}@>{}>{}>\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}@>{}>{}>G_{k}@>{}>{}>1\\
\end{CD}

[formula]

Thus,
\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}
is the geometrically abelian quotient of
\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)
.

Proposition C

The projection
\pi_{1}(C_{i},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

splits
,
\forall i\in\mathbb{N}
.

The author ignores, for the time being, if the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{\infty},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
splits or not.

§1. Proof of Theorem A

In this section we shall prove Theorem A. We use the notations in
\lx@sectionsign 0
.
We argue by contradiction, and
assume
that the projection

\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

splits
,
\forall n\geq 1
.

Proposition 1.1

There exists a relative curve
X\to\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}
with the following properties.

(i) The morphism
X\to\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}
is flat, proper,
stable
, and
X_{k}\overset\text{def}\to{=}X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}k
is geometrically connected.

(ii)
X
is
regular
.

(iii) The set of singular points
X_{F}^{\text{sing}}
of the special fibre
X_{F}\overset\text{def}\to{=}X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}F
of
X
consists
of
F
-
rational ordinary double points
,
U\overset\text{def}\to{=}X_{F}\setminus X_{F}^{\text{sing}}
is
F
-smooth, and

U(F)=\emptyset
holds.

(iv)
X(k)=\emptyset
holds.

Proof

First, assume
p\neq 2
. Let
\widetilde{C}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
with function field
k(\widetilde{C})
. Thus,
\operatorname{Card}(\widetilde{C}(F))=\operatorname{Card}F+1
is even.
Arrange the set
\widetilde{C}(F)
in pairs of
F
-rational points:
\widetilde{C}(F)=\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{1\leq i\leq\frac{\operatorname{Card}F+1}{2}}
. One can identify in
\widetilde{C}
the points
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
;

1\leq i\leq\frac{\operatorname{Card}F+1}{2}
,
to construct a stable proper
F
-curve
C
which is geometrically connected and geometrically reduced, with normalisation
\widetilde{C}\to C
. Moreover,
the set of singular points
C^{\operatorname{sing}}=\{c_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq\frac{\operatorname{Card}F+1}{2}}
consists of
F
-rational ordinary double points,
and the pre-image of
c_{i}
in
\widetilde{C}
consists of the two
F
-rational points
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
. In particular,
C(F)=C^{\operatorname{sing}}=\{c_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq\frac{\operatorname{Card}F+1}{2}}
.
More precisely, for
1\leq i\leq\frac{\operatorname{Card}F+1}{2}
, let

\widetilde{O}_{i}\overset\text{def}\to{=}O_{\widetilde{C},x_{i}}\cap O_{\widetilde{C},y_{i}}\subset k(\widetilde{C})
,
N_{x_{i}}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\mathfrak{m}_{x_{i}}\cap\widetilde{O}_{i}
, and

N_{y_{i}}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\mathfrak{m}_{y_{i}}\cap\widetilde{O}_{i}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{x_{i}}
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}_{y_{i}}
) is the maximal ideal of
O_{\widetilde{C},x_{i}}

(resp.
O_{\widetilde{C},y_{i}}
). Define
O_{c_{i}}\overset\text{def}\to{=}F+N_{x_{i}}N_{y_{i}}\subset\widetilde{O}_{i}
. Then
O_{c_{i}}
is a local ring (with maximal ideal

N_{x_{i}}N_{y_{i}}
, and residue field
F
) whose integral closure is
O_{c_{i}}
(cf. [Aubry-Lezzi], Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.4, and the references therein for the properties of
O_{c_{i}}
, as well as the existence of
C
with the required properties).

In case
p=2
. Consider the affine
F
-curve
\operatorname{Spec}(\frac{F[s,t]}{(st)})
, and
\tilde{C}
its smooth compactification. Thus,
\tilde{C}
consists of two
F
-smooth irreducible components

\tilde{C}_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
, and
\tilde{C}_{2}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{F}
, which intersect at the
F
-rational ordinary double point
c=(s,t)\in\operatorname{Spec}(\frac{F[s,t]}{(st)})
.
On each irreducible component
\tilde{C}_{i}
of
\tilde{C}
;
1\leq i\leq 2
, the set of
F
-rational points of
\tilde{C}_{i}\setminus\{c\}
is non-empty and comes into pairs of rational points

\{(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})\}_{1\leq j\leq\frac{\operatorname{Card}F}{2}}
.
As above we can identify each of those pairs of
F
-rational points
(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})
into an
F
-rational ordinary double point
c_{i,j}

to construct a reducible and geometrically connected stable curve
F
-curve
C
such that the set of singular points
C^{\operatorname{sing}}
consists of
F
-rational ordinary double points, a double point
c_{i,j}
lies on a unique irreducible component of
C
, and
C^{\operatorname{sing}}=C(F)
(the local ring at
c_{i}
is defined as above; the case
p\neq 2
. See. [Rosenlicht],
\lx@sectionsign 4
, for a discussion of this procedure and the existence of such a curve
C

in the case of reducible curves).

Now the stable
F
-curve
C
can be deformed to a semi-stable
O_{k}
-curve
X\to\operatorname{Spec}O_{k}
with special fibre
X_{F}=C
satisfying (i) and (ii)
(cf. [Talpo-Vistoli], Proposition 7.10, Corollary 7.11 and its proof). By our construction (iii) holds also. If
x\in X(k)
, then
X
specialises in a point
\bar{x}\in C(F)

which is a regular point of
C
and lies on a unique irreducible component of

(cf. [Liu], Corollary 9.1.32). Thus, (iv) follows from (iii).
∎

Let
X\to\operatorname{Spec}O_{K}
be a regular, proper, flat, and stable
O_{k}
-curve as in Proposition 1.1.
Let
y\in X_{F}(F)
be an
F
-rational point, which is an ordinary double point and a regular point of
X
(cf. Proposition 1.1 (ii) and (iii)).
We fix an isomorphism
\rho:\widehat{O}_{X,y}\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}R[[S,T]]/(ST-\pi)
, and identify
X\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Spec}(\widehat{O}_{X,y}\otimes_{O_{k}}k)
with
C_{1}
via the isomorphism
\rho_{k}:\widehat{O}_{X,y}\otimes_{O_{k}}k\overset\sim\to{\rightarrow}\frac{R[[S,T]]}{(ST-\pi)}\otimes_{O_{k}}k
induced by
\rho
.
Thus, we have scheme morphisms

C_{\infty}\to\cdots\to C_{n+1}\to C_{n}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to X_{k}.

For
n\geq 1
, write

\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)\overset\text{def}\to{=}\varprojlim_{S_{n}\subset X_{k}\setminus C_{n}}\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta),

where the projective limit is over all finite sets of closed points
S_{n}\subset X_{k}\setminus C_{n}
, and
\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)

is the arithmetic fundamental group of the affine curve
X_{k}\setminus S_{n}
with base point
\eta
.
(Here, we identify the set of closed points of
C_{n}
;
n\geq 1
, with a subset of the set of closed points of
X_{k}
which specialise in

.)
There is a natural projection

\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
, and we have a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)@>{}>{}>G_{k}\\
@V{}V{}V\Big\|\\
\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)@>{}>{}>G_{k}\\
\end{CD}

[formula]

where the left vertical map is induced by the morphism
C_{n}\to X_{k}
.

Further, we have a natural map

\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\to\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta),

and
\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)
is naturally identified with the absolute Galois group
G_{k(X)}\overset\text{def}\to{=}\operatorname{Gal}\left({k(X)}^{\operatorname{sep}}/k(X)\right)
, where

{k(X)}^{\operatorname{sep}}
is the separable closure of the function field

of

determined by the geometric point

.

Lemma 1.2

The projection
G_{k(X)}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

splits
.

Proof

First, our assumption that the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

splits
, implies that the projection

\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)\to G_{k}
splits,
\forall n\geq 1
(cf. diagram (4)).

Let
(H_{i})_{i\in I}
be a projective system of quotients
G_{k(X)}\twoheadrightarrow H_{i}
, where
H_{i}
sits in an exact sequence
1\to F_{i}\to H_{i}\to G_{k}\to 1
with
F_{i}
finite, and
G_{k(X)}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}H_{i}
.
[More precisely, write
G_{k(X)}
as a projective limit of finite groups
\{\tilde{H}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Then
\tilde{H}_{i}
fits in an exact sequence
1\to F_{i}\to\tilde{H}_{i}\to G_{i}\to 1
, where
G_{i}
is a quotient
of
G_{k}
, and
F_{i}
a quotient of
\operatorname{Gal}(k(X)^{\operatorname{sep}}/k(X)\overline{k})
. Let
1\to F_{i}\to H_{i}\to G_{k}\to 1
be the pull-back of the group extension
1\to F_{i}\to\tilde{H}_{i}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i}\to 1

by
G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i}
. Then
G_{k(X)}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}H_{i}
].
The set
\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},G_{k(X)})
of group-theoretic sections of the projection
G_{k(X)}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
is naturally identified with the projective limit
\varprojlim_{i\in I}\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},H_{i})

of the sets
\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},H_{i})
of group-theoretic sections of the projection
H_{i}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
. For each
i\in I
, the set
\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},H_{i})
is non-empty. Indeed,

H_{i}
(being a quotient of
G_{k(X)}
) is a quotient of
\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)
for some
n\geq 1
, this quotient
\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow H_{i}
commutes with the projections onto

, and we know the projection
\pi_{1}(X_{k}\setminus S_{n},\eta)\to G_{k}
splits.
Hence the projection
H_{i}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
splits.
Moreover, the set
\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},H_{i})
is, up to conjugation by the elements of

, a torsor under the group
H^{1}(G_{k},F_{i})
which is finite since

is a
p
-adic local field (cf. [Neukirch-Schmidt-Winberg], (7.1.8) Theorem (iii)). Thus,

\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},H_{i})
is a nonempty finite set. Hence the set
\operatorname{Sect}(G_{k},G_{k(X)})
is nonempty being the projective limit of nonempty finite sets. This finishes the proof of Lemma 1.1.
(See also the proof of Proposition 1.5 in [Saïdi] for similar arguments in a slightly different context.)
∎

Let
s:G_{k}\to G_{k(X)}
be a section of the projection
G_{k(X)}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
(cf. Lemma 1.2).

Lemma 1.3

The section
s
is
geometric
, i.e.,
s(G_{k})\subset D_{x}
, where
D_{x}\subset G_{k(X)}
is a decomposition group associated to a
(unique) rational point
x\in X(k)
. In particular,
X(k)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof

This follows from [Koenigsmann], Proposition 2.4 (2).
∎

Now the conclusion of Lemma 1.3 that
X(k)\neq\emptyset
contradicts the assertion (iv) in Proposition 1.1 that
X(k)=\emptyset
. This is a contradiction. Thus, our assumption that
the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}

splits
,
\forall n\geq 1
, can not hold. Let
N\geq 1
be such that the projection

\pi_{1}(C_{N},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
doesn’t splits. Then the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
doesn’t splits,
\forall n\geq N
as required.
Indeed, this follows from the fact that for
n\geq N
we have a natural homomorphism
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\to\pi_{1}(C_{N},\eta)
which commutes with the projections onto
G_{k}
.
Hence if the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
splits then the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{N},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
.

This finishes the proof of Theorem A.
∎

§2. Proof of Theorem B

Next, we explain how Theorem B can be derived from Theorem A.
We have,
\forall n\geq 1
, a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\pi_{1}(C_{\infty},\eta)@>{}>{}>G_{k}\\
@V{}V{}V\Big\|\\
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)@>{}>{}>G_{k}\\
\end{CD}

where the horizontal maps are the natural projections, and the left vertical map is induced by the morphism

C_{\infty}\to C_{n}
.

Now assume that the projection
\pi_{1}(C_{\infty},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
splits. Then the projection

\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
splits,
\forall n\geq 1
, by the above diagram. But this contradicts Theorem A.

This finishes the proof of Theorem B.
∎

§3. Proof of Proposition C

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, and
\ell_{1}
,
\ell_{2}
, distinct prime integers such that
\ell_{1}\geq 2n
, and
\ell_{2}\geq 2n
.
Let
O_{1}
, and
O_{2}
, be totally ramified extensions of
O_{k}
of degree
\ell_{1}
, and
\ell_{2}
, with fraction fields
L_{1}=\operatorname{Fr}(O_{1})
, and
L_{2}=\operatorname{Fr}(O_{2})
; respectively.
Thus, the extensions
L_{1}/k
and
L_{2}/k
, are disjoint and
C_{n}(L_{i})\neq\emptyset
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. A restriction-corestriction argument shows that the class

[\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}]
of the group extension
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}
in
H^{2}(G_{k},\pi_{1}(C_{n,\overline{k}},\bar{\eta})^{\operatorname{ab}})
is trivial. Thus
the group extension
\pi_{1}(C_{n},\eta)^{(\operatorname{ab})}
splits.

This finishes the proof of Proposition C.
∎

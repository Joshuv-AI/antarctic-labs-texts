Study of an entropy dissipating finite volume scheme for a nonlocal cross-diffusion system
Maxime Herda, Antoine Zurek

Abstract
. In this paper we analyse a finite volume scheme for a nonlocal version of the Shigesada-Kawazaki-Teramoto (SKT) cross-diffusion system. We prove the existence of solutions to the scheme, derive qualitative properties of the solutions and prove its convergence. The proofs rely on a discrete entropy-dissipation inequality, discrete compactness arguments, and on the novel adaptation of the so-called duality method at the discrete level. Finally, thanks to numerical experiments, we investigate the influence of the nonlocality in the system: on convergence properties of the scheme, as an approximation of the local system and on the development of diffusive instabilities. Keywords: Nonlocal cross-diffusion, finite volume schemes, entropy method, convergence. Mathematics Subject Classification: 65M08, 65M12, 35K51, 35Q92, 92D25.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in the numerical discretization of the following nonlocal cross-diffusion system

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{1}-\Delta((d_{1}+d_{11}\,\sigma_{1}\ast u_{1}+d_{12}\,\rho_{1}\ast u_{2})u_{1})

\displaystyle=R_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{2}-\Delta((d_{2}+d_{21}\,\rho_{2}\ast u_{1}+d_{22}\sigma_{2}\ast u_{2})u_{2})

\displaystyle=R_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}),

on a periodic domain
\Omega={\mathbb{T}}^{d}
(
d\leq 3
). For a given final time
T
we denote the space time domain by
Q_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
. The parameters
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
,
d_{11}
,
d_{12}
,
d_{21}
and
d_{22}
are some positive constants and
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are non-negative convolution kernels. System (
1
)–(
2
) is supplemented with initial conditions

(3)

\displaystyle u_{1}(\cdot,0)=u_{1}^{0}(\cdot),\quad u_{2}(\cdot,0)=u_{2}^{0}(\cdot).

In the case where the convolution kernels are given by the Dirac measure
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\delta_{0}
, the system coincides with the celebrated Shigesada, Kawasaki, and Teramoto (SKT) population model
[
42
]
which can describe segregation phenomena between competing species. It writes

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{1}-\Delta((d_{1}+d_{11}\,u_{1}+d_{12}\,u_{2})u_{1})

\displaystyle=R_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{2}-\Delta((d_{2}+d_{21}\,u_{1}+d_{22}\,u_{2})u_{2})

\displaystyle=R_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}).

Nonlocal cross-diffusion systems appear naturally as a mean field type of limit of interacting many-particle systems. For instance, the model (
1
)–(
2
) was introduced in
[
26
]
as the large population limit of a stochastic individual model. If these particle systems allow a precise description of the interactions between individuals, their numerical approximations are very time-consuming. Then, it is reasonable to investigate simpler macroscopic models. In this context we see nonlocal cross-diffusion models as intermediate models between individual based models and local cross-diffusion models. This interpretation has been mathematically justified in the literature, see
[
17
,
21
,
31
,
37
]
, where the derivation of some local cross-diffusion models from nonlocal models (some of them derived from microscopic models) are shown.

Besides, nonlocal cross-diffusion models can be more than a mathematical intermediate between two scales. Indeed, in population dynamics, they can model nonlocal sensing, as diffusion of a species is impacted by the population located (respectively to their position) on the support of the convolution kernels, see
[
28
,
40
]
. In the model (
1
)–(
2
) assume for instance that
\rho_{1}
is supported away from
0
. Then the resulting effect of the nonlocal cross-diffusion term is to enhance the diffusion of species
1
when species
2
is away, modeling for instance a hunting behavior in a predator-prey model. This could hardly be reproduced by local cross-diffusion terms.

The ability of the nonlocal cross-diffusion terms to model the dynamics of some natural phenomena explain the use of such models in other contexts. They are for instance applied to describe cell sorting
[
38
,
39
]
, tumour growth
[
22
]
, opinion formation
[
23
]
or interactions between spiking neurons
[
5
]
(just to name a few). In particular, the development of reliable numerical methods to approximate the solutions of nonlocal cross-diffusion systems can enhance our understanding of the “physical” mechanisms described by them. As a by-product this could also help the development of efficient models describing complex phenomena.

Motivated by these reasons, this manuscript deals with the design and analysis of a robust numerical scheme for (
1
)–(
3
). Our approach is inspired by the analysis performed at the continuous level in
[
21
,
37
]
. In particular, in
[
21
]
the authors show that there is a persisting entropy structure in the nonlocal case which yields a crucial
a priori
estimate for the analysis of the model. This extends for instance the approach developed in
[
29
,
30
]
in the local case. Indeed, it was shown that for the system (
1
)–(
3
) without reaction terms
R_{1}=R_{2}=0
and under the following symmetry hypotheses on the convolution kernels

(6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{1}(x)=\rho_{2}(-x)=\rho(x),\\
&\sigma_{1}(x)=\sigma_{1}(-x),\\
&\sigma_{2}(x)=\sigma_{2}(-x),\end{aligned}\right.

that the following entropy functional

\displaystyle H(u_{1},u_{2})=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{d_{12}}\left[u_{1}(\log(u_{1})-1)+1\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{d_{21}}\left[u_{2}(\log(u_{2})-1)+1\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,,

is dissipated along solutions of (
1
)–(
2
). More precisely one has

(7)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(u_{1},u_{2})+\frac{2d_{11}}{d_{12}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{1}(y)\left(\sqrt{u_{1}(x-y)}\,\nabla\sqrt{u_{1}(x)}+\sqrt{u_{1}(x)}\nabla\sqrt{u_{1}(x-y)}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\\
+\frac{2d_{22}}{d_{21}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{2}(y)\left(\sqrt{u_{2}(x-y)}\,\nabla\sqrt{u_{2}(x)}+\sqrt{u_{2}(x)}\nabla\sqrt{u_{2}(x-y)}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\\
+4\frac{d_{1}}{d_{12}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{u_{1}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+4\frac{d_{2}}{d_{21}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{u_{2}}|^{2}+4\int_{\Omega}\rho(y)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{u_{1}(x)u_{2}(x-y)}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=0\,.

Observe that if the convolution kernels are given by the Dirac measure
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\delta_{0}
, then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(u_{1},u_{2})+2\frac{d_{11}}{d_{12}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2\frac{d_{22}}{d_{21}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+4\frac{d_{1}}{d_{12}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{u_{1}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+4\frac{d_{2}}{d_{21}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{u_{2}}|^{2}+4\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{u_{1}\,u_{2}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\,,

which was already known for the local SKT system (see
[
18
,
19
,
27
]
).

The fact that (
1
)–(
2
) admits a Lyapunov functional is crucial for the study of the system. Indeed, in
[
21
]
, the authors used (
7
) together with the so-called duality method, see
[
20
,
34
,
37
]
, in order to prove (assuming (
6
) and without reaction terms) the existence of distributional solutions to (
1
)–(
3
).

Definition 1
.

Given
T>0
, let
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be some functions in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
u^{0}_{1}
and
u^{0}_{2}
be some initial functions in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, we say that the measurable functions
u_{1},u_{2}:Q_{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
are distributional solutions to (
1
)–(
3
) if for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega\times[0,T))
it holds

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\Big(u_{1}\partial_{t}\phi+\left(d_{1}u_{1}+d_{11}\sigma_{1}\ast u_{1}u_{1}+d_{12}\rho_{1}\ast u_{2}u_{1}\right)\Delta\phi\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}u^{0}_{1}(x)\phi(x,0)\mathrm{d}x,

and

(9)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\Big(u_{2}\partial_{t}\phi+\left(d_{2}u_{2}+d_{21}\rho_{2}\ast u_{1}u_{2}+d_{22}\sigma_{2}\ast u_{2}u_{2}\right)\Delta\phi\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}u^{0}_{2}(x)\phi(x,0)\mathrm{d}x.

In this paper we propose and analyze a finite volume scheme for (
1
)–(
3
). A particular focus is put on

(i)

the preservation of the entropy dissipation property at the discrete level;

(ii)

the non-negativity of the solution;

(iii)

the possibility to use the scheme in both the nonlocal and local regimes.

In order to achieve these goals we will design a fully implicit two point flux approximation (TPFA) finite volume scheme. As in the study of some numerical schemes for local cross-diffusion systems, see for instance the following (non-exhaustive) list of contributions
[
4
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
32
,
43
]
, the preservation of the entropy dissipation property at the discrete level is crucial. This ensures well-posedness and global stability in time
[
15
,
25
]
as well as with respect to the choice of convolution kernels (see Theorem
1
). Some of these methods are reminiscent of the second author’s work in
[
32
]
concerning the study of a finite volume scheme for the local SKT system. Besides, we are able to obtain additional estimates on the solution (see Theorem
2
) by adapting the duality method (see
[
20
,
34
,
37
]
) at the discrete level. This technique relies on the study of a discretized Kolmogorov equation, see Section
4
. The convergence of solutions of the numerical scheme towards distributional solutions in the sense of Definition
1
is shown in Theorem
3
. Let us mention that only
L^{\infty}
regularity of the convolution kernels is required to obtain convergence of the scheme. With smoother kernels one additionally obtains local in time
L^{\infty}
bounds on the discrete solution that are uniform in the mesh size (see Theorem
2
).

Let us notice that there already exists some works dealing with the design and the analysis of finite volume numerical schemes for nonlocal cross-diffusion systems. Indeed, in
[
2
,
3
]
the convergence of some semi-implicit TPFA finite volume schemes are proved. The convergence proofs are based (as in this work) on the adaptation at the discrete level of a Kruzhkov’s compactness result
[
33
]
(see also
[
41
]
) obtained in
[
4
]
. We mention
[
8
]
where numerical experiments are shown to illustrate the formation of gaps for a class of nonlocal cross-diffusion systems. In this paper the authors applied an explicit in time finite
volume scheme first introduced in
[
12
]
and then extended for the multi-species case in
[
13
]
. This scheme is a positivity and entropy preserving method as shown in
[
13
]
. Finally, we also refer to
[
14
]
. In this contribution the convergence of a semi-discrete finite volume scheme is proved. This scheme is also positivity preserving which allows the authors to establish a discrete energy estimate.

In order to illustrate and complement the theoretical results, we present several numerical experiments in the last section of this paper. We compute the experimental order of convergence of the numerical method when the mesh size goes to
0
for various initial data and convolution kernels. Then, for a fixed mesh, we investigate the rate of convergence for different metrics of the discrete solutions of the nonlocal system towards solution of the local system when convolution kernels tends to Dirac measures. Finally, we perform simulations of the model with nonzero reaction terms with parameters chosen to describe a prey-predator system with either linear diffusion or non-local cross diffusion modelling hunting behavior. For these models we illustrate the persistence and the modification of Turing patterns in the presence of cross-diffusion.

The paper is organized as follows, in Section
2
we introduce the scheme and state our main results. Section
3
is concerned with the proof of existence of positive solutions to the scheme. We introduce the discrete Kolmogorov equation in Section
4
. Then we deduce from the study of this problem some qualitative properties satisfied by the solutions to our scheme in Section
4.3
. Sections
5
deals with the convergence of the scheme. Finally, in Section
6
, we discuss the implementation and show some numerical experiments in one and two space dimensions.

2.
Numerical scheme and main results

The results of this paper apply to a periodic domain
\Omega=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{R}/L_{i}\mathbb{Z}
, where
L_{1},\dots,L_{d}>0
. However, for the sake of readability we will assume from now on that
d=1
and
L_{1}=1
, namely
\Omega={\mathbb{T}}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. The generalization in higher dimensions on Cartesian grid is immediate by defining the scheme as the tensorization of the one dimensional scheme.

2.1.
Notations and definitions

Let us define
N\geq 1
and
\Delta x=1/N
. A uniform mesh
\mathcal{T}
of
{\mathbb{T}}
consists in a finite sequence of cells denoted by

K_{i}=(x_{i-\frac{1}{2}},x_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\,,\quad i\in{\mathcal{I}}={\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}.

centered at
x_{i}=i\Delta x
and with extremities
x_{i\pm\frac{1}{2}}=(i\pm\frac{1}{2})\Delta x
. For
T>0
given, we define an integer
N_{T}
and a time step
\Delta t=T/N_{T}
and we introduce the sequence
(t_{k})_{0\leq k\leq N_{T}}
with
t_{k}=k\Delta t
. We denote by
{\mathcal{D}}
a space-time discretization of
Q_{T}={\mathbb{T}}\times(0,T)
composed of a space discretization
{\mathcal{T}}
of
{\mathbb{T}}
and the values
(\Delta t,N_{T})
.

Let us now introduce some discrete norms on the space of piecewise constant functions in space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{T}}}=\Bigg\{w:{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\,\,:\,\,w(x)=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}w_{i}\mathbf{1}_{K_{i}}(x)\Bigg\}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define the discrete
W^{1,p}
seminorm and discrete
W^{1,p}
norm on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}
by

\displaystyle|w|_{1,p,{\mathcal{T}}}=\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\left|\frac{w_{i+1}-w_{i}}{\Delta x}\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\|w\|_{1,p,{\mathcal{T}}}=|w|_{1,p,{\mathcal{T}}}+\|w\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})},

where, for
p\in[1,\infty)
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}
denotes the usual
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
norm. In the case
p=\infty
, we denote
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}
the
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
norm given by

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}=\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}|w_{i}|,\quad\forall w\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}.

Let us also recall the definition of the space
BV({\mathbb{T}})
, see
[
1
]
for more details. A function
w\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
belongs to the space
BV({\mathbb{T}})
if its total variation
TV(w)
given by

\displaystyle TV(w)=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{T}}w(x)\,\partial_{x}\phi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\phi\in C^{1}_{c}({\mathbb{T}}),\quad|\phi(x)|\leq 1\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{T}}\right\},

is finite. We endow the space
BV({\mathbb{T}})
with the norm

\|w\|_{BV({\mathbb{T}})}=\|w\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+TV(w),\quad\forall w\in BV({\mathbb{T}}).

In particular, we notice that for each function
w\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}\cap BV({\mathbb{T}})
we have
\|w\|_{BV({\mathbb{T}})}=\|w\|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}
.

Finally we introduce the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}}
of piecewise constant in time functions with values in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{D}}}=\Bigg\{w:{\mathbb{T}}\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}\,\,:\,\,w(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}w^{k}(x)\mathbf{1}_{(t_{k-1},t_{k}]}(t)\Bigg\}.

This space can be equipped, for
(p,q)\in[1,\infty)^{2}
, with the following discrete
L^{q}(0,T;W^{1,p}({\mathbb{T}}))
norm

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\,\|w\|^{q}_{1,p,{\mathcal{T}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\quad\forall w\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}},

or with the
L^{q}(0,T;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}))
norm

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\,\|w\|^{q}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\forall w\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}}.

In particular, in the case
p=q=2
, the
L^{2}(Q_{T})
norm can also be defined by duality as

(10)

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}=\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}wf\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\,\,:\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{D}}},\,\,\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|f^{k}_{i}|^{2}=1\right\},\quad\forall w\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}}.

This dual formulation of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}
will be needed later on.

2.2.
Numerical scheme

We discretize the initial conditions (
3
) as

(11)

\displaystyle u^{0}_{j,i}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{K_{i}}u_{j}^{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},\,j=1,2.

Now for given
(u^{k-1}_{1},u^{k-1}_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}
, the implicit in time numerical scheme writes as

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{u^{k}_{j,i}-u^{k-1}_{j,i}}{\Delta t}-\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{j}^{k}u_{j}^{k})\right)_{i}=R_{j}(u^{k}_{1,i},u^{k}_{2,j}),\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},\,j=1,2,

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}
denotes the discrete Laplacian, namely

(13)

\displaystyle\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{j}^{k}u_{j}^{k})\right)_{i}=\frac{\mu^{k}_{j,i+1}u^{k}_{j,i+1}-2\mu^{k}_{j,i}u^{k}_{j,i}+\mu^{k}_{j,i-1}u^{k}_{j,i-1}}{\Delta x^{2}},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},\,j=1,2,

and

(14)

\displaystyle\mu_{1,i}^{k}

\displaystyle=d_{1}+d_{11}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\sigma_{1,i-n}u^{k}_{1,n}+d_{12}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\rho_{1,i-n}\,u^{k}_{2,n},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},

(15)

\displaystyle\mu_{2,i}^{k}

\displaystyle=d_{2}+d_{21}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\rho_{2,i-n}\,u^{k}_{1,n}+d_{22}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\sigma_{2,i-n}u^{k}_{2,n},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},

with

(16)

\displaystyle\rho_{j,i-n}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{K_{i-n}}\rho_{j}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad\sigma_{j,i-n}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{K_{i-n}}\sigma_{j}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad\forall i,\,n\in{\mathcal{I}},\,j=1,2.

Let us notice, by construction, that if we consider
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\delta_{0}
then (
11
)–(
15
) yields a finite volume scheme for the local SKT model (
4
)–(
5
). We also remark that we could equivalently rewrite (
12
) as

(17)

\displaystyle\Delta x\frac{u^{k}_{j,i}-u^{k-1}_{j,i}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i-\frac{1}{2}}=R_{j}(u^{k}_{1,i},u^{k}_{2,i}),\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},\,j=1,2,

where for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
the numerical fluxes
\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}
are defined by

(18)

\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{u^{k}_{j,i}\mu^{k}_{j,i}-u^{k}_{j,i+1}\mu^{k}_{j,i+1}}{\Delta x}=\mu^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u^{k}_{j,i}-u^{k}_{j,i+1}}{\Delta x}+u^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mu^{k}_{j,i}-\mu^{k}_{j,i+1}}{\Delta x},\quad j=1,2,

with the centered approximation at interfaces

\displaystyle\mu^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\mu^{k}_{j,i}+\mu^{k}_{j,i+1}}{2},\quad u^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{u^{k}_{j,i}+u^{k}_{j,i+1}}{2},\quad j=1,2.

2.3.
Main results

Let us collect our assumptions.

(H1)

The domain is taken as
\Omega={\mathbb{T}}
.

(H2)

The diffusion coefficients
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
,
d_{11}
and
d_{22}
are non-negative constants and the cross-diffusion coefficients
d_{12}
and
d_{21}
are positive constants.

(H3)

The convolution kernels
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
functions that are non-negative and satisfy the symmetry hypotheses (
6
). In particular
\rho_{i}=\rho_{1,i}=\rho_{2,-i}
for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

(H4)

The initial data
u^{0}_{1}
and
u^{0}_{2}
are non-negative
L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
functions with finite entropy, namely
h_{1}(u_{1}^{0}),h_{2}(u_{2}^{0})\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
.

(H5)

The reaction terms satisfy
R_{1}=R_{2}=0
.

As already mentioned, hypothesis (H1) is only made for the convenience of the reader and one can adapt the design of the scheme and the results to a
d
-dimensional periodic domain
\Omega=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{R}/L_{i}\mathbb{Z}
. Observe that by assuming (H2) we require cross-diffusion on both species. While this is crucial in our proofs, the scheme performs well in practice even with
d_{12}=0
or
d_{21}=0
(see Section
6
). The assumption (H3) on the symmetry of the functions
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
,
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are needed, as at the continuous level, to show the discrete entropy inequality satisfied by the solutions of the scheme (
11
)–(
15
). However, in terms of practical use, the scheme performs well even when dropping this hypothesis (see Section
6.4
). Following for instance
[
32
]
, the assumption (H5) can be relaxed and one can extend the proofs of Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
in the case of the Lotka-Volterra source terms:

\displaystyle R_{j}(u_{1},u_{2})=u_{j}\,\left(a_{j0}-\sum_{k=1}^{2}a_{jk}\,u_{k}\right),\quad j=1,2,

with
a_{j0}
and
a_{jk}
some nonnegative constants for
j,k=1,2.

Our first main result deals with the existence of solutions to scheme (
11
)–(
15
) at each time step. But first let us recall the definition of the discrete entropy functional

\displaystyle H(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2})=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,h_{1}(u^{k}_{1,i})+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,h_{2}(u^{k}_{2,i}),

where the functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are defined by

(19)

\displaystyle h_{1}(x)=\dfrac{1}{d_{12}}\,\left(x\,(\log(x)-1)+1\right),\quad h_{2}(x)=\dfrac{1}{d_{21}}\,\left(x\,(\log(x)-1)+1\right),\quad\forall x\in(0,+\infty),

with the obvious continuous extension at
x=0
. The corresponding entropy dissipation functional is defined by

(20)

D(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2})=2\frac{d_{11}}{d_{12}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\sqrt{u^{k}_{1,i+1}u^{k}_{1,i+1-j}}-\sqrt{u^{k}_{1,i}u^{k}_{1,i-j}}\right)^{2}\\
+2\frac{d_{22}}{d_{21}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{2,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\sqrt{u^{k}_{2,i+1}u^{k}_{2,i+1-j}}-\sqrt{u^{k}_{2,i}u^{k}_{2,i-j}}\right)^{2}+4\frac{d_{1}}{d_{12}}\Big|\sqrt{u^{k}_{1}}\Big|^{2}_{1,2,{\mathcal{T}}}\\
+4\frac{d_{2}}{d_{21}}\Big|\sqrt{u^{k}_{2}}\Big|^{2}_{1,2,{\mathcal{T}}}+4\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\sqrt{u^{k}_{1,i+1}u^{k}_{2,i+1-j}}-\sqrt{u^{k}_{1,i}u^{k}_{2,i-j}}\right)^{2}.

Theorem 1

(Existence of solutions)
.

Let the assumptions (H1)–(H5) hold. Then, for every
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
there exists (at least) one nonnegative solution

Remark 4

Let us make few remarks concerning Theorem

•

If

If

Finally, with enough regularity on the data, one could derive quantitative error estimates between approximate and continuous solutions.

3.
Existence of solution and entropy dissipation estimate

The problem of existence of solution reduces to the resolution of a nonlinear system of equations. The natural unknowns for which a fixed point theorem will be easily applied are linked to the entropy. In our case, given
(u_{1},u_{2})\in((0,+\infty)^{N})^{2}
we define the new unknown
X=\Phi(u_{1},u_{2})
where
\Phi:((0,+\infty)^{N})^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}
is the smooth diffeomorphism defined by

\Phi(u_{1},u_{2})=(d_{12}^{-1}\log(u_{1,1}),\dots,d_{12}^{-1}\log(u_{1,N}),d_{21}^{-1}\log(u_{2,1}),\dots,d_{21}^{-1}\log(u_{2,N}))^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}.

From there finding a positive solution to the scheme (
11
)–(
15
) amounts to finding a zero
X^{k}=\Phi(u_{1}^{k},u_{2}^{k})
of the continuous map
P^{k}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}
defined for any
(u_{1},u_{2})\in((0,+\infty)^{N})^{2}
by its components

P_{i+N(j-1)}^{k}(\Phi(u_{1},u_{2}))=\Delta x(u_{j,i}-u^{k-1}_{j,i})-\Delta t\Delta x\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{j}u_{j})\right)_{i},\quad\forall i\in\{1,\dots,N\},j\in\{1,2\},

where
(u_{1}^{k-1},u_{2}^{k-1})
are given and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are related to
u_{1},u_{2}
through the relation (
14
) and (

) dropping the index

3.1.
Entropy dissipation and mass conservation

In the following
\left\langle,\right\rangle
denotes the Euclidean scalar product and
|\cdot|
the Euclidean norm.

Proposition 5
.

Let
(u_{1}^{k-1},u_{2}^{k-1})
be componentwise non-negative. Then for any
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}
,

(23)

\left\langle P^{k}(X),\mathds{1}_{j}\right\rangle=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}(u_{j,i}-u^{k-1}_{j,i})\Delta x\,,\quad\forall j\in\{1,2\},

and

(24)

\left\langle P^{k}(X),X\right\rangle\geq H(u_{1},u_{2})-H(u_{1}^{k-1},u_{2}^{k-1})+\Delta tD(u_{1},u_{2}),

where
\mathds{1}_{1}=(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)
,
\mathds{1}_{2}=(0,\dots,0,1,\dots,1)
,
(u_{1},u_{2})=\Phi^{-1}(X)
and
D(u_{1},u_{2})
denotes the entropy dissipation functional given by (
20
).

Proof.

In order to prove (
23
), it suffices to sum the components of
P^{k}(X)
and observe that

\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(\mu_{j}u_{j})\right)_{i}=0,

since it is a telescopic sum. Concerning the inequality, first observe that
H(u_{1}^{k-1},u_{2}^{k-1})
is well-defined since
(u_{1}^{k-1},u_{2}^{k-1})
is non-negative. Then, using the definition of
\Phi(u_{1},u_{2})
and
P^{k}
one obtains

\left\langle P^{k}(X),X\right\rangle=\Delta t(I_{1}+I_{2}+J_{1}+J_{2})

with

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{12}}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(u_{1,i}-u^{k-1}_{1,i})\,\log(u_{1,i}),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{21}}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(u_{2,i}-u^{k-1}_{2,i})\,\log(u_{2,i}),

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{12}\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}(-\mu_{1,i+1}u_{1,i+1}+2\mu_{1,i}u_{1,i}-\mu_{1,i-1}u_{1,i-1})\,\log(u_{1,i})

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{21}\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}(-\mu_{2,i+1}u_{2,i+1}+2\mu_{2,i}u_{2,i}-\mu_{2,i-1}u_{2,i-1})\,\log(u_{2,i})

Using the convexity of
x\mapsto(x\log(x)-x+1)
to bound both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
from below, one obtains

I_{1}+I_{2}\geq H(u_{1},u_{2})-H(u_{1}^{k-1},u_{2}^{k-1}).

Then for
J_{1}
, a discrete integration by parts (or summation by parts) yields

J_{1}=\frac{1}{d_{12}\,\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}\mu_{1,i+1}-u_{1,i}\mu_{1,i}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1})-\log(u_{1,i})\right),

and a similar formula holds for
J_{2}
. Using the definitions of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
(see (
14
) and (
15
) without the exponents), one has
J_{1}=J_{1}^{\text{diff}}+J_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}+J_{1}^{\rho_{1}}
and
J_{2}=J_{2}^{\text{diff}}+J_{2}^{\sigma_{2}}+J_{2}^{\rho_{2}}
with

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\text{diff}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{1}}{d_{12}\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}-u_{1,i}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1})-\log(u_{1,i})\right)\geq\frac{4d_{1}}{d_{12}\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\sqrt{u_{1,i+1}}-\sqrt{u_{1,i}}\right)^{2},

and a similar estimate for
J_{2}^{\text{diff}}
. For the second term one has

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{11}}{d_{12}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}u_{1,i+1-j}-u_{1,i}u_{1,i-j}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1})-\log(u_{1,i})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{d_{11}}{2d_{12}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}u_{1,i+1+j}-u_{1,i}u_{1,i+j}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1})-\log(u_{1,i})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{d_{11}}{2d_{12}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1-j}u_{1,i+1}-u_{1,i-j}u_{1,i}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1-j})-\log(u_{1,i-j})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{11}}{2d_{12}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}u_{1,i+1-j}-u_{1,i}u_{1,i-j}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1}u_{1,i+1-j})-\log(u_{1,i}u_{1,i-j})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2d_{11}}{d_{12}}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\sqrt{u_{1,i+1}u_{1,i+1-j}}-\sqrt{u_{1,i}u_{1,i-j}}\right)^{2}.

In the previous estimate, the second inequality is obtained by changing
j
into
-j
and using the symmetry of
\sigma_{1}
. For the third equality, one changes
i
into
i-j
. The fourth one is the combination of the first and third equalities. Once again a similar estimate holds for
J_{2}^{\sigma_{2}}
. Finally with the same changes of indices one can estimate the sum

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\rho_{1}}+J_{2}^{\rho_{2}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}u_{2,i+1-j}-u_{1,i}u_{2,i-j}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1})-\log(u_{1,i})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{-j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{2,i+1}u_{1,i+1-j}-u_{2,i}u_{1,i-j}\right)\left(\log(u_{2,i+1})-\log(u_{2,i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}u_{2,i+1-j}-u_{1,i}u_{2,i-j}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1})-\log(u_{1,i})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{2,i-j+1}u_{1,i+1}-u_{2,i-j}u_{1,i}\right)\left(\log(u_{2,i-j+1})-\log(u_{2,i-j})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(u_{1,i+1}u_{2,i+1-j}-u_{1,i}u_{2,i-j}\right)\left(\log(u_{1,i+1}u_{2,i-j+1})-\log(u_{1,i}u_{2,i-j})\right)

\displaystyle 4\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\sqrt{u_{1,i+1}u_{2,i+1-j}}-\sqrt{u_{1,i}u_{2,i-j}}\right)^{2}.

Remark 6

(Uniqueness under CFL)
.

Under a parabolic CFL condition, uniqueness of a solution to the scheme can also be proven. Indeed, if one denotes by
U^{k}
a vector of solution given by Theorem
1
, then the scheme may be rewritten as
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}^{k}(U^{k})U^{k}=U^{k-1}
. The matrix
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}^{k}
(which has a similar definition as (
26
) hereafter) depends on
U^{k}
through
\mu_{1}^{k}
and
\mu_{2}^{k}
and is a perturbation of the identity matrix of size
\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}(\|\mu_{1}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mu_{2}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}})
. Since
\|\mu_{i}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded uniformly in terms of the initial masses,
L^{\infty}
norms of the convolution kernels and diffusion coefficients,
\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}
can be made small enough with respect to these quantities only so that a contraction argument yields the uniqueness of solutions.

4.1.
Well-posedness of the scheme and

Let us first prove that the scheme (

Lemma 7

For any
(z^{k-1}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
there is a unique
(z^{k}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
satisfying (

z^{k-1}

Proof.

Let us write

for all

\mathbb{M}^{k}Z^{k}=Z^{k-1}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}}^{k}_{i,i-1}=-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}\mu^{k}_{i-1},\quad{\mathbb{M}}^{k}_{i,i}=1+2\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}\mu^{k}_{i},\quad{\mathbb{M}}^{k}_{i,i+1}=-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}\mu^{k}_{i+1},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}}.

We notice that

∎

We prove in the following result some

Lemma 8

Let us assume that there exists

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}\leq z^{0}_{i}\leq\tilde{\Gamma},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}}.

Then for every
k\geq 1
and every
\Delta t>0
such that

\displaystyle\Delta t<1/\max_{1\leq k\leq N_{T}}\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})},

the solution
Z^{k}
to (
25
) satisfies

(27)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}\,\Pi_{n=1}^{k}\left(1+\Delta t\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{n}]_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)^{-1}\leq z^{k}_{i}\leq\tilde{\Gamma}\,\Pi_{n=1}^{k}\left(1-\Delta t\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{n}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)^{-1},\,\,\,\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},

where
[x]_{+}=\max(x,0)
and
[x]_{-}=\min(x,0)
.

Proof.

We will only deal with the upper bound in (
27
) and the lower bound is obtained in the same way. Let
{\mathbb{M}}^{k}
denote the tridiagonal matrix defined by (
26
) and define

\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}=\tilde{\Gamma}\,\Pi_{n=1}^{k}\left(1-\Delta t\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{n}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)^{-1}.

We proceed by induction. Since
\tilde{\Gamma}^{0}=\tilde{\Gamma}
the bound holds by hypothesis at
k=0
. Then observe that for every
i\in{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle({\mathbb{M}}^{k}(Z^{k}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}))_{i}=Z^{k-1}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}+\Delta t\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}\,(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k})_{i}\leq\tilde{\Gamma}^{k-1}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}+\Delta t\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}\,(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k})_{i}.

Now we notice that by construction

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}^{k-1}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}=-\Delta t\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}\,\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Then we easily deduce that for every
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
it holds

\displaystyle({\mathbb{M}}^{k}(Z^{k}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}))_{i}=-\Delta t\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}\left(\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}-(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k})_{i}\right)\leq 0.

Therefore, since
{\mathbb{M}}^{k}
is a M-matrix we conclude that
z^{k}_{i}\leq\tilde{\Gamma}^{k}
for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
which concludes the proof of Lemma
8
.
∎

The bounds of Lemma
8
are exactly the discrete equivalent of the
L^{\infty}
estimates established at the continuous level in
[
21
, Corollary 18]
.

Proposition 9
.

Let us assume that it holds

\displaystyle\Delta t<1/\max_{1\leq k\leq N_{T}}\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Then the solution to (
25
) satisfies the following estimate

\|z^{k}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{k}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{T}}}(\mu^{n}_{i}+\mu^{n}_{i+1})\,\frac{(z^{n}_{i+1}-z^{n}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}\\
\leq\Pi_{n=1}^{k}\left(1-\Delta t\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{n}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)^{-1}\,\|z^{0}_{i}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}^{2},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq N_{T}.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 1
be fixed and let us first notice that we can rewrite for every
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
equation (
25
) as

\displaystyle\Delta x\frac{z^{k}_{i}-z^{k-1}_{i}}{\Delta t}+\mu^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(z^{k}_{i}-z^{k}_{i+1})}{\Delta x}-\mu^{k}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(z^{k}_{i-1}-z^{k}_{i})}{\Delta x}+z^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(\mu^{k}_{i}-\mu^{k}_{i+1})}{\Delta x}-z^{k}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(\mu^{k}_{i-1}-\mu^{k}_{i})}{\Delta x}=0,

where

\displaystyle\mu^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\mu^{k}_{i}+\mu^{k}_{i+1}}{2},\quad z^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{z^{k}_{i}+z^{k}_{i+1}}{2},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}}.

Now we multiply the above equation by
\Delta tz^{k}_{i}
and we sum over
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}=0,

where

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(z^{k}_{i}-z^{k-1}_{i})z^{k}_{i},

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\mu^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(z^{k}_{i}-z^{k}_{i+1})}{\Delta x}-\mu^{k}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(z^{k}_{i-1}-z^{k}_{i})}{\Delta x}\right)z^{k}_{i},

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(z^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(\mu^{k}_{i}-\mu^{k}_{i+1})}{\Delta x}-z^{k}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(\mu^{k}_{i-1}-\mu^{k}_{i})}{\Delta x}\right)z^{k}_{i}.

For
I_{3}
using the inequality
(a-b)a\geq(a^{2}-b^{2})/2
we obtain

(28)

\displaystyle I_{3}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\left(|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}-|z^{k-1}_{i}|^{2}\right).

For
I_{4}
applying a discrete integration by parts yields

(29)

\displaystyle I_{4}=\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\,\frac{(z^{k}_{i+1}-z^{k}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}.

Now we rewrite
I_{5}
as

\displaystyle I_{5}=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k})_{i}+\frac{\Delta t}{2\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(z^{k}_{i+1}z^{k}_{i}(\mu^{k}_{i}-\mu^{k}_{i+1})-z^{k}_{i-1}z^{k}_{i}(\mu^{k}_{i-1}-\mu^{k}_{i})\right),

and reordering the terms in the r.h.s. the second sum vanishes and we have

(30)

\displaystyle I_{5}=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k})_{i}.

Gathering (
28
)–(
30
) we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(z^{k}_{i+1}-z^{k}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{k-1}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}.

We deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{k}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(z^{n}_{i+1}-z^{n}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{0}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{k}\frac{\Delta t}{2}\|[\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{n}]_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|z^{n}_{i}|^{2}.

One ends the proof of Proposition
9
thanks to a discrete Grönwall inequality.
∎

4.2.
Study of the dual problem

The main objective of this section is to establish a discrete counterpart of the so-called duality inequality for the solution to (
25
), see for instance
[
37
, Theorem 3]
. In this aim, following
[
37
]
, we introduce a “dual” scheme associated to (
25
). Let
v^{N_{T}+1}_{i}
be given for every
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
, then for
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
we want to determine the solution to the following implicit backward in time scheme

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{v^{k}_{i}-v^{k+1}_{i}}{\Delta t}-\mu^{k}_{i}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v^{k}\right)_{i}=S^{k}_{i}\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},

where
\mu^{k}_{i}
is given and non-negative and
S^{k}=(S^{k}_{0},\ldots,S^{k}_{N-1})
is some given vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
for all
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
. Let us notice that (
31
) define a set of linear equation which can be rewritten as

(32)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{M}^{k})^{\top}V^{k}=V^{k+1}+\Delta tS^{k},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq N_{T},

where
\mathbb{M}^{k}
is the tridiagonal matrix given by (
26
). Therefore, it follows directly from the proof of Lemma
7
that the problem (
32
) admits a unique solution for every
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
.

Prior to the proof of the discrete duality estimate, see Theorem
11
below, we establish some uniform estimates satisfied by the solution of (
32
).

Proposition 10
.

Assume that
\min_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i}>0
for every
0\leq k\leq N_{T}
and that
v^{N_{T}+1}_{i}=0
for every
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
. Then the solution to (
32
) satisfies for every
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
the following estimate

(33)

\displaystyle|v^{k}|^{2}_{1,2,{\mathcal{T}}}+\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{n}_{i}(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v^{n})^{2}_{i}\Delta x\leq\|\mu^{-1/2}S\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})},

and there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\Delta x
such that

(34)

\displaystyle\|v^{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})})\,\|\mu^{-1/2}S\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{T})},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq N_{T},

where
\mu
and
S
denote the piecewise reconstruction functions in
\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
associated to the vectors
(\mu^{k})_{1\leq k\leq N_{T}}
and
(S^{k})_{1\leq k\leq N_{T}}
.

Proof.

Let us first establish estimate (
33
). In this purpose let
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
be fixed. We multiply equation (
31
) by
\Delta t(-v^{k}_{i+1}+2v^{k}_{i}-v^{k}_{i-1})/\Delta x
, we sum over
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
and we apply definition (
13
) of the operator
\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}
and we obtain

\displaystyle I_{6}+I_{7}=I_{8},

with

\displaystyle I_{6}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}(v^{k}_{i}-v^{k+1}_{i})\frac{(-v^{k}_{i+1}+2v^{k}_{i}-v^{k}_{i-1})}{\Delta x},

\displaystyle I_{7}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i}\frac{(v^{k}_{i+1}-2v^{k}_{i}+v^{k}_{i-1})^{2}}{\Delta x^{3}},

\displaystyle I_{8}

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}S^{k}_{i}\frac{(-v^{k}_{i+1}+2v^{k}_{i}-v^{k}_{i-1})}{\Delta x}.

For
I_{6}
reordering the terms leads to

\displaystyle I_{6}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}(v^{k}_{i+1}-v^{k}_{i})\left[(v^{k}_{i+1}-v^{k}_{i})-(v^{k+1}_{i+1}-v^{k+1}_{i})\right],

and using the inequality
a(a-b)\geq(a^{2}-b^{2})/2
we get

(35)

\displaystyle I_{6}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left[\frac{(v^{k}_{i+1}-v^{k}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}-\frac{(v^{k+1}_{i+1}-v^{k+1}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}\right].

For
I_{8}
applying the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequality yield

(36)

\displaystyle|I_{8}|\leq\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(\mu^{k}_{i})^{-1}|S^{k}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i}\frac{(-v^{k}_{i+1}+2v^{k}_{i}-v^{k}_{i-1})^{2}}{\Delta x^{3}}.

Collecting (
35
)–(
36
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\frac{(v^{k}_{i+1}-v^{k}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}+\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i}\frac{(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v^{k})^{2}_{i}}{\Delta x}\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\frac{(v^{k+1}_{i+1}-v^{k+1}_{i})^{2}}{\Delta x}+\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(\mu^{k}_{i})^{-1}|S^{k}_{i}|^{2}.

In order to prove (
33
) it remains to sum over
n\in\{k,\ldots,N_{T}\}
.

We now prove estimate (
34
). In this purpose we multiply (
31
) by
\Delta x\Delta t
, we sum over
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
n\in\{k,\ldots,N_{T}\}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xv^{k}_{i}=\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i}\frac{v^{n}_{i+1}-2v^{n}_{i}+v^{n}_{i-1}}{\Delta x}+\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xS^{n}_{i}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to

\left|\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xv^{k}_{i}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\mu^{n}_{i}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg[\left(\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{n}_{i}\frac{(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v^{n})^{2}_{i}}{\Delta x}\right)^{1/2}\\
+\left(\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(\mu^{n}_{i})^{-1}|S^{n}_{i}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg].

Using estimate (
33
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xv^{k}_{i}\right|\leq 2\left(\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\mu^{n}_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=k}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(\mu^{n}_{i})^{-1}|S^{n}_{i}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Now it remains to apply the discrete Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality on the torus obtained in
[
6
, Lemma 6]
in order to conclude the proof of Proposition
10
.
∎

We are now in position to establish the discrete dual estimate.

Theorem 11
.

Let us assume that
\min_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{i}>0
for every
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\Delta x
such that the solution
(Z^{k})_{1\leq k\leq N_{T}}
to (
25
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mu^{1/2}z\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\leq C\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}^{1/2}\right)\,\|z^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Proof.

Let
(v^{k}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
be given in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
for every
1\leq k\leq N_{T}+1
with
v^{N_{T}+1}_{i}=0
for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
. Now for
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
, we multiply (
25
) by
\Delta t\Delta xv^{k}_{i}
, we sum over
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,N_{T}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(z^{k}_{i}-z^{k-1}_{i})v^{k}_{i}-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(z^{k}\mu^{k})\right)_{i}v^{k}_{i}=0.

Reordering the terms we have

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xz^{k}_{i}\left(\frac{(v^{k}_{i}-v^{k+1}_{i})}{\Delta t}-\mu^{k}_{i}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v^{k}\right)_{i}\right)=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xz^{0}_{i}v^{1}_{i}.

We define
(S^{k}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
by

\displaystyle S^{k}_{i}=\frac{(v^{k}_{i}-v^{k+1}_{i})}{\Delta t}-\mu^{k}_{i}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v^{k}\right)_{i},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},\,1\leq k\leq N_{T}.

We first notice that
(S^{k}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
is well-defined since we know that equation (
31
) is well-posed. Besides applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in (
37
) we get

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xz^{k}_{i}S^{k}_{i}\right|\leq\|z^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}\|v^{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Now, thanks to (
34
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x(\mu^{k}_{i})^{1/2}z^{k}_{i}\,(\mu^{k}_{i})^{-1/2}S^{k}_{i}\right|\leq C\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}^{1/2}\right)\|\mu^{-1/2}S\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\|z^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

In the remaining of the proof we want to use the dual definition (
10
) of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}
. Observe that for any vector
F^{k}=(f^{k}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
, there exists a unique
V=(v_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
such that

\displaystyle(\mu^{k}_{i})^{-1/2}\frac{v_{i}-v^{k+1}_{i}}{\Delta t}-(\mu^{k}_{i})^{1/2}\,\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}v\right)_{i}=f^{k}_{i},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}},

where
(v^{k+1}_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
is a given vector. Indeed, this system rewrites
(\mathbb{M}^{k})^{\top}V=V^{k+1}+\Delta t\mathbb{D}^{k}F^{k}
, for all
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
where
\mathbb{M}^{k}
is the invertible tridiagonal matrix given by (
26
) and
\mathbb{D}^{k}=\mathrm{diag}((\mu^{k}_{i}))_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
. We deduce thanks to formula (
10
) that it holds

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\mu^{k}_{i}|z^{k}_{i}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(1+\|\mu\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}^{1/2}\right)\|z^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}})}.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
11
.
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
2

We are now able to prove Theorem
2
.

Step 1: Maximum principle

Let us first prove the maximum principle satisfies by the solutions to (
11
)–(
15
). Let us notice that for every
1\leq k\leq N_{T}
we have

\displaystyle\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left|\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}_{1}\right)_{i}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Bigg|d_{11}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xu^{k}_{1,j}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\sigma_{1}\right)_{i-j}+d_{12}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xu^{k}_{2,j}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\rho\right)_{i-j}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq d_{11}\|\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xu^{k}_{1,j}+d_{12}\|\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,u^{k}_{2,j}.

Now, let us recall that
m^{0}_{j}=\|u^{0}_{j}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}
for
j=1,2
, then thanks to the mass conservation property (
21
) we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left|\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}_{1}\right)_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq d_{11}m^{0}_{1}\,\|\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}+d_{12}m^{0}_{2}\,\|\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Similarly we establish the following bound

\displaystyle\max_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left|\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\mu^{k}_{2}\right)_{i}\right|\leq d_{22}m^{0}_{2}\,\|\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\sigma_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}+d_{21}m^{0}_{1}\,\|\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}.

As a direct consequence of the previous estimates and (
27
) (with
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
and
\tilde{\Gamma}=\Gamma
) one obtains point
(i)
of Theorem
2
.

Step 2: Duality estimate

Let us now show the discrete duality estimate satisfied by the solutions to (
11
)–(
15
). For every
0\leq k\leq N_{T}
we define the element
z^{k}_{i}=u^{k}_{1,i}+u^{k}_{2,i}
for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
. Observe that
z^{k}_{i}
is solution to

\displaystyle\frac{z^{k}_{i}-z^{k-1}_{i}}{\Delta t}+\Delta_{\mathcal{T}}(\mu^{k}z^{k})_{i}=0,\quad\text{where}\quad\mu^{k}_{i}=\frac{\mu_{1,i}^{k}u_{1,i}^{k}+\mu_{2,i}^{k}u_{2,i}^{k}}{u_{1,i}^{k}+u_{2,i}^{k}},\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}}.

Thanks to Theorem
1
, we have
u^{k}_{1,i}
,
u^{k}_{2,i}>0
for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
and the element
\mu^{k}_{i}
is well-defined. Besides, applying the discrete duality estimate established in Theorem
11
we deduce the existence of a constant
C>0
independent of
\Delta x
such that

(38)

\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\left(\mu^{k}_{1,i}u^{k}_{1,i}+\mu^{k}_{2,i}u^{k}_{2,i}\right)\left(u^{k}_{1,i}+u^{k}_{2,i}\right)\\
\leq C\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|\mu^{k}_{i}|\right)\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|u^{0}_{1,i}|^{2}+\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|u^{0}_{2,i}|^{2}\right).

Now we notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|\mu^{k}_{i}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|\mu^{k}_{1,i}|+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x|\mu^{k}_{2,i}|=I_{9}+I_{10}.

For
I_{9}
we have

\displaystyle I_{9}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\left(d_{1}+d_{11}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\sigma_{1,i-j}u^{k}_{1,j}+d_{12}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\rho_{i-j}u^{k}_{2,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\left(d_{1}+d_{11}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xu^{k}_{1,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\sigma_{1,i-j}+d_{12}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta xu^{k}_{2,j}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\rho_{i-j}\right).

Thus, bearing in mind the mass conservation property (
21
) we obtain

(39)

\displaystyle I_{9}\leq T(d_{1}+d_{11}m^{0}_{1}\,\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+d_{12}m^{0}_{2}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}),

and similarly

(40)

\displaystyle I_{10}\leq T(d_{2}+d_{22}m^{0}_{2}\,\|\sigma_{2}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+d_{21}m^{0}_{1}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}).

Collecting (
38
)–(
40
) we conclude that point
(ii)
of Theorem
2
holds.

5.
Convergence of the scheme

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
3
. In the following the subscript

\eta_{m}=\max\{\Delta x_{m},\Delta t_{m}\}

({\mathcal{D}}_{m})

. A keypoint is a discrete

[

. This result is the adaptation at the discrete level of a compactness lemma established by Kruzhkov in

]
(see also
[

]
). Finally in Section

, we prove Theorem

.

5.1.
Uniform estimates

In this section we establish some uniform estimates w.r.t.
\Delta x
and
\Delta t
fulfilled by the solutions to the scheme (
11
)–(
15
). They rely on the entropy dissipation inequality (
22
) and the conservation of mass (
21
) of Theorem
1
.

Proposition 12
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1
hold. Then there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
only depending on
d_{12}
,
d_{21}
,
m^{0}_{1}
,
m^{0}_{2}
and
H(u^{0}_{1},u^{0}_{2})
such that

(41)

\displaystyle\max_{k=1,\ldots,N_{T}}\|u^{k}_{j}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+\left(d_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\,\|u^{k}_{j}\|^{2}_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1},\quad\mbox{for }j=1,2.

Moreover, assuming that
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are positive constants, there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
only depending on
T
,
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
,
d_{11}
,
d_{12}
,
d_{21}
,
d_{22}
,
\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}
,
\|\sigma_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}
,
\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}
,
m^{0}_{1}
,
m^{0}_{2}
and
H(u^{0}_{1},u^{0}_{2})
such that

(42)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,\left|\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}\right|\leq C_{2},\quad\mbox{for }j=1,2,

where the numerical fluxes are defined by (
18
).

Proof.

The uniform
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))
estimate of the first term in the right hand side of (
41
) is a direct consequence of the conservation of mass (
21
). Then, for the uniform discrete
L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,1}({\mathbb{T}}))
estimate, we first notice, for
j=1
or
2
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,N_{T}\}
, that it holds

\displaystyle|u_{j}^{k}|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left|u^{k}_{j,i+1}-u^{k}_{j,i}\right|=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left|\left(\sqrt{u^{k}_{j,i+1}}-\sqrt{u^{k}_{j,i}}\right)\,\left(\sqrt{u^{k}_{j,i+1}}+\sqrt{u^{k}_{j,i}}\right)\right|.

Hence, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle|u^{k}_{j}|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}\leq\left|\sqrt{u^{k}_{j}}\right|_{1,2,{\mathcal{T}}}\,\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,\left(\sqrt{u^{k}_{j,i+1}}+\sqrt{u^{k}_{j,i}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Since
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
and
\|u^{k}_{j}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}=\|u^{0}_{j}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}=m^{0}_{j}
(conservation of mass), one has

\displaystyle|u^{k}_{j}|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}\leq 2\left(m^{0}_{j}\right)^{1/2}\,\left|\sqrt{u^{k}_{j}}\right|_{1,2,{\mathcal{T}}}.

Therefore, applying the entropy inequality (
22
), we get for the first species

\displaystyle d_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\,|u^{k}_{1}|^{2}_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}\leq 4d_{1}\,m^{0}_{1}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\,\left|\sqrt{u^{k}_{1}}\right|^{2}_{1,2,{\mathcal{T}}}\leq d_{12}m^{0}_{1}\,H(u^{0}_{1},u^{0}_{2}),

and the equivalent estimate holds for the second species. This yields the existence of
C_{1}
such that (
41
) holds.

It remains to establish (
42
). In this purpose we will consider the case
j=1
. Then, using the definition (
18
) of the numerical fluxes, we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,\left|\mathcal{F}^{k}_{1,i+\frac{1}{2}}\right|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\mu^{k}_{1,i+\frac{1}{2}}\,\left|u^{k}_{1,i}-u^{k}_{1,i+1}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}u^{k}_{1,i+\frac{1}{2}}\,\left|\mu^{k}_{1,i}-\mu^{k}_{1,i+1}\right|

\displaystyle=I_{11}+I_{12}.

For
I_{11}
, applying the regularity of the functions
\sigma_{1}
and
\rho
we have

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(d_{1}+d_{11}\,\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\|u^{k}_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+d_{12}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\|u^{k}_{2}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)\,\left|u^{k}_{1,i}-u^{k}_{1,i+1}\right|.

Hence, using the conservativity of the scheme and (
41
), we get

(43)

\displaystyle I_{11}\leq\left(d_{1}+d_{11}m^{0}_{1}\,\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}+d_{12}m^{0}_{2}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\right)\,T^{1/2}\,C_{1}.

For
I_{12}
, using the definition of
\mu^{k}_{1,i}
for
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
, we notice that it holds

I_{12}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x\,\left(u^{k}_{1,i}+u^{k}_{1,i+1}\right)\\
\times\left(d_{11}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sigma_{1,n}\,\left|u^{k}_{1,i-n}-u^{k}_{1,i+1-n}\right|+d_{12}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{n}\,\left|u^{k}_{2,i-n}-u^{k}_{2,i+1-n}\right|\right).

Then, thanks to the conservativity of the scheme, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle\leq 2m^{0}_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t\,\left(d_{11}\,\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\left|u^{k}_{1}\right|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}+d_{12}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\left|u^{k}_{2}\right|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}}\right).

Therefore, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
41
) we end up with

(44)

\displaystyle I_{12}\leq 2\,m^{0}_{1}\,T^{1/2}\,C_{1}\,\left(\frac{d_{11}}{d_{1}^{1/2}}\,\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}+\frac{d_{12}}{d_{2}^{1/2}}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\right).

Collecting (
43
) and (
44
) and the corresponding inequalities for the second species lead to the existence of
C_{2}
such that (
42
) holds. This concludes the proof of Proposition
12
.
∎

5.2.
Compactness properties

Let
(u_{1,m},u_{2,m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a family, constructed in Theorem
1
, of finite volume solutions to (
11
)–(
15
) associated to the sequence
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})
. In order to be able to apply
[
4
, Lemma 9.2]
, the first task is to rewrite the scheme (
11
)–(
15
) as the discretization of an evolution equation under divergence form. In this purpose we use the equivalent form of (
12
) given by (
17
). In particular, for
j=1,2
and
k=1,\ldots,N_{T}
, we associate to the family of fluxes
\left(\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+1/2}\right)_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}
the following piecewise reconstruction

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,m}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}_{m}}\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathbf{1}_{(x_{i},x_{i+1})}.

Then, for this discrete field
\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,m}
we define its
L^{1}
norm as

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,m}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}_{m}}\Delta x\,\left|\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}\right|,

and its discrete divergence by

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,m}\right)_{i}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\,\left(\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i+\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,i-\frac{1}{2}}\right),\quad i\in{\mathcal{I}}_{m}.

This definition allows us to rewrite (
18
) as

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{u^{k}_{j,i}-u^{k-1}_{j,i}}{\Delta t}+\mathrm{div}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,m}\right)_{i}=0,\quad\forall i\in{\mathcal{I}}_{m},\,j=1,2,

and we obtain the following result:

Proposition 13
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3
hold and let
(u_{1,m},u_{2,m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be
a sequence of discrete solutions to (
11
)–(
15
) constructed
in Theorem
1
. Then there exists a subsequence of
(u_{1,m},u_{2,m})
, which is not relabeled, and
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\left(L^{p}(Q_{T})\right)^{2}
, with
p\in[1,3)
, such that

\displaystyle u_{j,m}\rightarrow u_{j}\quad\mbox{strongly in }L^{p}(Q_{T})\mbox{ for }1\leq p<3,\quad\mbox{as }m\to\infty,

and almost everywhere.

Proof.

A direct consequence of Proposition
12
is that there is a constant
C
independent of
\Delta x_{m}
and
\Delta t_{m}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t_{m}\,\|u^{k}_{j,m}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t_{m}\,\|\mathcal{F}^{k}_{j,m}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t_{m}\,|u^{k}_{j,m}|_{1,1,{\mathcal{T}}_{m}}\leq C,\quad j=1,2.

By
[
4
, Lemma 9.2]
, which can be applied thanks to (
45
), there is a function
u_{j}\in L^{1}(Q_{T})
,
j=1,2
, such that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{j,m}\rightarrow u_{j}\quad\mbox{strongly in }L^{1}(Q_{T})\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

Moreover, Proposition
12
also implies that the sequence
(u_{i,m})
is uniformly bounded in the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))
and in
L^{2}(0,T;BV({\mathbb{T}}))
. The continuous embedding of
BV({\mathbb{T}})
in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
(see
[
1
]
) implies that the sequence
(u_{j,m})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))
. Hence, by interpolation, one has a uniform bound of
(u_{j,m})
in
L^{3}(Q_{T})
. Thus, Vitali’s theorem gives the strong convergence of
(u_{j,m})
towards
u_{j}
in
L^{p}(Q_{T})
for all
p\in[1,3)
. This concludes the proof of Proposition
13
.
∎

Remark 14
.

In dimension
d\geq 2
, we have the compact embedding of the space
BV({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
in
L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
. In particular in this case the sequence
(u_{j,m})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}(Q_{T})
. Therefore arguing as in the previous proof we deduce the existence for
j=1
and
2
of
u_{j}\in L^{p}(Q_{T})
for
p\in[1,{d}/{(d-1)})
, such that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{j,m}\rightarrow u_{j}\quad\mbox{strongly in }L^{p}(Q_{T})\mbox{ for }1\leq p<{\frac{d}{d-1}},\quad\mbox{as }m\to\infty.

Corollary 15
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition
13
hold. Then there exists a subsequence of
(u_{1,m},u_{2,m})
, such that for any
p\in[1,3)
one has

\displaystyle\mu_{1,m}\rightarrow\mu_{1}=d_{1}+d_{12}\rho_{1}\ast u_{2}+d_{11}\sigma_{1}\ast u_{1}\quad\mbox{strongly in }L^{p}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})),\quad\mbox{as }m\to\infty,

\displaystyle\mu_{2,m}\rightarrow\mu_{2}=d_{2}+d_{21}\rho_{2}\ast u_{1}+d_{22}\sigma_{2}\ast u_{2}\quad\mbox{strongly in }L^{p}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})),\quad\mbox{as }m\to\infty,

where we recall that
\rho_{1}(x)=\rho_{2}(-x)=\rho(x)
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{T}}
.

Proof.

Observe that by definition (
14
)-(
16
) one has for
x\in K_{i}
and
t\in(0,T)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,t)-\mu_{1,m}(x,t)

\displaystyle=d_{12}\,(\rho_{1}\ast(u_{2}-u_{2,m}))(x,t)+d_{11}\,(\sigma_{1}\ast(u_{1}-u_{1,m}))(x,t)

\displaystyle+d_{12}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}(\rho_{1}(x-y)-\rho_{1}(x_{i}-y))\,u_{2,m}(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+d_{11}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}(\sigma_{1}(x-y)-\sigma_{1}(x_{i}-y))\,u_{1,m}(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Therefore by dominated convergence one obtains the strong convergence of
\mu_{1,m}
towards
\mu_{1}
in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
and almost everywhere in
Q_{T}
. Besides, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
thanks to Young’s inequality and for
p\in(1,3)
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\rho_{1}\ast(u_{2}-u_{2,m})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\|(u_{2}-u_{2,m})(t)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Then, applying Proposition
13
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{1}\ast(u_{2}-u_{2,m})(t)\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\mathrm{d}t\leq\|\rho_{1}\|^{p}_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\int_{0}^{T}\|(u_{2}-u_{2,m})(t)\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\,\mathrm{d}t\to 0\quad\mbox{as }m\to\infty.

Let us now setting
\xi(x,y)=\rho_{1}(x-y)-\rho_{1}(x_{i}-y)
for a.e.
x\in K_{i}
and
y\in{\mathbb{T}}
. Hence, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, the Hölder inequality yields for
p\in(1,3)

\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\xi(\cdot,y)u_{2,m}(t,y)\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\sup_{|z|\leq\Delta x_{m}}\|\rho_{1}(z+\cdot)-\rho_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}({\mathbb{T}})}\|u_{2,m}(t)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

The first factor in the right hand side tends to
0
(by density of continuous functions in
L^{p/(p-1)}({\mathbb{T}})
) while, bearing in mind Proposition
13
, the second factor is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(Q_{T})
. Therefore one can conclude the strong convergence in
L^{p}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}))
by using Young’s inequality and the previous argument. This finishes the proof of Corollary
15
.
∎

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
3

It remains to prove that the functions
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
constructed in Section
5.2
are distributional solutions to (
1
)–(
3
) in the sense of (
8
). Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}\times[0,T))
, we multiply equation (
12
) by
\Delta t_{m}\Delta x_{m}\,\phi^{k-1}_{i}
, where
\phi^{k-1}_{i}=\phi(x_{i},t^{k-1})
, and we sum over
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,N_{T}\}
. We obtain
F^{m}_{1}+F^{m}_{2}=0
with

\displaystyle F^{m}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x_{m}\,(u^{k}_{1,i}-u^{k-1}_{1,i})\phi^{k-1}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x_{m}\,u^{k}_{1,i}(\phi^{k-1}_{i}-\phi^{k}_{i})-\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Delta x_{m}u^{0}_{1,i}\phi^{0}_{i},

and

\displaystyle F^{m}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t_{m}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\frac{-\mu^{k}_{1,i+1}u^{k}_{1,i+1}+2\mu^{k}_{1,i}u^{k}_{1,i}-\mu^{k}_{1,i-1}u^{k}_{1,i-1}}{\Delta x_{m}}\,\phi^{k-1}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{T}}\Delta t_{m}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\frac{-\phi^{k-1}_{i+1}+2\phi^{k-1}_{i}-\phi^{k-1}_{i-1}}{\Delta x_{m}}\,\mu^{k}_{1,i}u^{k}_{1,i}.

Let
\psi_{m}(x,t)=(\phi^{k}_{i}-\phi^{k-1}_{i})/\Delta t_{m}
for all
x\in K_{i}
and
t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
and
\phi_{m}^{0}(x)=\phi^{0}_{i}
for all
x\in K_{i}
. Then, since
\psi_{m}\to\partial_{t}\phi
in
L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
and
\phi^{0}_{m}\to\phi^{0}
in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
, so using the convergence results of Proposition
13
one obtains

\displaystyle F^{m}_{1}+\int_{Q_{T}}u_{1}\partial_{t}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}u^{0}_{1}(x)\phi(x,0)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}(u_{1}\partial_{t}\phi-u_{1,m}\psi_{m})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}(u^{0}_{1}\phi(\cdot,0)-u^{0}_{1}\phi_{m}^{0})\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0\quad\mbox{as }m\to 0.

Similarly, if one defines
\zeta_{m}(x,t)=(\phi^{k-1}_{i+1}-2\phi^{k-1}_{i}+\phi^{k-1}_{i-1})/(\Delta x_{m})^{2}
for all
x\in K_{i}
and
t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
, then
\zeta_{m}\to\Delta\phi
in
L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
and therefore using Proposition
13
and Corollary
15
one obtains

\displaystyle F^{m}_{2}+\int_{Q_{T}}u_{1}\mu_{1}\Delta\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{Q_{T}}(u_{1}\mu_{1}\Delta\phi-u_{1,m}\mu_{1,m}\zeta_{m})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\to 0\quad\mbox{as }m\to 0.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
3
.

6.
Numerical experiments

In this section, we perform several numerical experiments to illustrate the behavior of the scheme.

6.1.
Implementation

The scheme was implemented in dimension
d=1
and
d=2
using Matlab. The code is available at
https://gitlab.inria.fr/herda/nonlocal-skt
. In order to optimize the computational cost, a number of matrices can be pre-assembled and stored using a sparse matrix structure. This is the case for the matrix of the Laplacian and those related to the convolution kernels. Moreover, the assembling can be performed efficiently using the discrete Fourier transform. At each time step the nonlinear system is solved using a Newton method. Convergence of the Newton method is reached when the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of the residue divided by the norm of the first guess gets less than a given tolerance, which we took to be
10^{-10}
in our experiments. An adaptive time step procedure is implemented in case the Newton method fails to converge. After maximum number of steps (
50
in the experiments), if the target error is not attained,
\Delta t
is divided by
2
. If there was refinement on a given time step,
\Delta t
is multiplied by two for the next time step. In the experiments below the Newton method never failed to converge and the time step remained constant along all the simulations.

6.2.
Test case 1: Convergence for various convolution kernels and initial data

In this first test case, we investigate the convergence of the scheme in the case for the following nonlocal cross-diffusion system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{1}-\partial_{xx}^{2}((\rho\ast u_{2})u_{1})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{2}-2\partial_{xx}^{2}((\rho\ast u_{1})u_{2})

\displaystyle=0.

\rho_{0}

\chi_{A}
indicator function of the set

The final time of simulation is taken to be

. We run the scheme for a sequence of decreasing space and time steps. More precisely the number of points is

6.3.
Test case 2: From nonlocal to local cross-diffusion

As a second test case, we investigate numerically the rate of convergence for different metrics of the so-called localization limit. Namely we study the rate of convergence of solutions of the nonlocal cross-diffusion system (
1
)–(
2
) towards solutions of its corresponding local version (
4
)–(
5
) as the convolution kernel tends to a Dirac measure. Indeed, if theoretically this localization limit has been proved in
[
21
,
37
]
, the proofs rely on some compactness method and no explicit “error” bounds are available (see also for instance
[
31
]
). The establishment of such explicit estimates seems to be a complex task. In order to get a better understanding of this problem we aim to study this question thanks to our finite volume scheme.

More precisely, in this test case we consider the same system as in the first test case with
\rho=\rho_{\delta}
for various values of
\delta\in[0,L]
. The domain has length
L=25
, the final time is
T=1
and the mesh is such that
N=1024
and
\Delta t=10^{-2}
. We evaluate the error at time
T
between the solution
(u_{1}^{(\delta)},u_{2}^{(\delta)})
computed for the kernel
\rho_{\delta}
and
(u_{1}^{(0)},u_{2}^{(0)})
computed for the local cross-diffusion system in Wasserstein-1 norm,

W_{1}(u_{1}^{(\delta)},u_{1}^{(0)})+W_{1}(u_{2}^{(\delta)},u_{2}^{(0)}),

and in
L^{p}
norms

\|u_{1}^{(\delta)}-u_{1}^{(0)}\|_{L^{p}}+\|u_{2}^{(\delta)}-u_{2}^{(0)}\|_{L^{p}},

with
p=1
or
p=\infty
. For the computation of the Wasserstein-1 norm we recall that in dimension
1
, if
f
and
g
are non-negative integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}
with the same mass, one has
W_{1}(f,g)=\|F-G\|_{L^{1}}
, with
F^{\prime}=f
,
G^{\prime}=g
and
F(-\infty)=G(-\infty)
. In practice,
f

6.4.
Test case 3: Turing instabilities in prey-predator systems with nonlocal cross-diffusion

In this last test case, we consider the following system with nonlocal cross diffusion and reaction modelling a population of preys with density
u_{1}
and predators with density
u_{2}
. The system reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{1}-d_{1}\Delta u_{1}

\displaystyle=R_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{2}-\Delta((d_{2}+d_{21}\,\rho_{2}\ast u_{1})u_{2})

\displaystyle=R_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}).

The precise reaction terms will be specified below. On the one hand, preys are subject to linear diffusion with constant diffusivity coefficient
d_{1}
. However, the predators diffuse depending on the presence or the absence of preys. More precisely, the convolution kernel
\rho_{2}
is chosen such that it is close to
0
near the origin and large away form the origin (up to a given distance). This models the fact that predators need not seek for preys when they are available at their position, while they shall diffuse more rapidly if higher densities of preys are ahead. The reaction terms will be chosen following the phytoplankton-herbivore model of Segel and Levin
[
35
]
and a variation of Mimura-Nishiura-Yamaguti
[
36
]
. In both cases, the particularities are an autocatalytic effect on the phytoplankton’s (preys) growth rate and a density-dependent mortality of herbivore (predators). In the case of linear diffusion, this model is famous for exhibiting diffusive instabilities
[
35
]
around the homogenenous equilibrium. The corresponding Turing patterns have been invoked to justify the patchiness of phytoplankton’s distribution in the oceans
[
35
]
. In
[
35
]
Segel and Levin mention that in these models
the assumption of passive diffusion is made for simplicity only; more complicated movement patterns can also lead to diffusive instability.
Here we propose a more complex description model of the behavior of predators thanks to non-local cross-diffusion. In the following, we illustrate numerically the persistence and the modification of Turing patterns in the presence of nonlocal cross-diffusion.

6.4.1.
One dimensional case: Segel-Levin reaction term

We consider the one-dimensional case with the following reaction terms

\displaystyle R_{1}(u_{1},u_{2})=au_{1}+eu_{1}^{2}-bu_{1}u_{2},\quad R_{2}(u_{1},u_{2})=-du_{2}^{2}+cu_{1}u_{2}.

where the parameters are
a=b=c=d=1
and
e=\frac{1}{3}
. Concerning the diffusion we consider two cases. In the first case, both species are driven by linear diffusion with
d_{1}=0.05
for preys and
d_{2}=2
and without cross-diffusion
d_{21}=0
. In the second case the preys are driven by linear diffusion with
d_{1}=0.05
and the predators by nonlocal cross-diffusion with
d_{21}=1
and the kernel

\rho_{2}(x)=C_{r}[x^{2}\chi_{(-r,r)}(x)+(x-2r)^{2}\chi_{[r,2r)}(x)+(x+2r)^{2}\chi_{(-2r,-r]}(x)],

with
C_{r}
a normalizing constant such that
\int\rho_{2}=1
. This kernel vanishes at
0
, has support on
[-2r,2r]
and is maximal at
x=\pm r
. It is designed to model the hunting behavior of predators which will diffuse if most of the preys are away from their position, with a detection radius equal to
r
and a maximal distance of detection of
2r
. In both cases the simulation is performed on a domain of length
L=25
with
N=500
cells. The radius is taken to be
r=10L/49
. The final time of simulation
T=500
and the time step is
\Delta t=0.1

with
\varepsilon=10^{-2}
. With the chosen parameters, the homogeneous equilibrium is linearly unstable in both the linear diffusion and the nonlocal cross-diffusion cases. Numerically we observe the solution converges in time towards an heterogeneous equilibrium in both cases. On Figure

Monogamous subvarieties of the nilpotent cone
Simon M. Goodwin, Rachel Pengelly, David I. Stewart, Adam R. Thomas

Abstract
. Let G G be a reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field 𝕜 \Bbbk of prime characteristic not 2 2 , whose Lie algebra is denoted 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . We call a subvariety 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} of the nilpotent cone 𝒩 ⊂ 𝔤 \mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g} monogamous if for every e ∈ 𝔛 e\in\mathfrak{X} , the 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} -triples ( e , h , f ) (e,h,f) with f ∈ 𝔛 f\in\mathfrak{X} are conjugate under the centraliser C G ​ ( e ) C_{G}(e) . Building on work by the first two authors, we show there is a unique maximal closed G G -stable monogamous subvariety 𝒱 ⊂ 𝒩 \mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{N} and that it is an orbit closure, hence irreducible. We show that 𝒱 \mathcal{V} can also be characterised in terms of Serre’s G G -complete reducibility.

Preliminaries

Throughout,
\Bbbk

is classical, it is one of

,
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
or
\mathrm{SO}(V)
and write

for brevity; if
G
is exceptional, we take it to be simply connected.

Recall that a prime

is bad for
G
if

and
G
is of type
B
,
C
or
D
; if

and

is exceptional; or if

and

is of type

; otherwise it is good. In some examples we require a choice of base for the root system associated to

; we use Bourbaki notation
[
Bou05
]
. Finally, we fix a maximal torus

of

2.1.
Nilpotent orbits and Hasse diagrams

The orbits for the action of

on

are called nilpotent orbits. There are finitely many such and they are classified. In case

by a label indicating a Levi subalgebra in which

[

When

is well-known and can be found in

according to the Jordan decomposition of their elements’ actions on

where

is the partition of

and

with an even number of even parts and an even number of odd parts, respectively. In type

m
with an even number of even parts that is not very even; and two orbits for each very even partition of
m

to

. For exceptional types the picture is actually incomplete in general. But if

[

and are reproduced in
[

with labels closer to those in
[
LS12
]
. However, those in
[

]
are missing edges in the

and

3.
Monogamy of
\mathcal{V}

We start with an observation that
\mathcal{V}
can be characterised using the following partial order on

.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

. We say

(resp.

) if
\rk(\operatorname{ad}(x)^{p-1})\leq\rk(\operatorname{ad}(y)^{p-1})
(resp.
\rk(\operatorname{ad}(x)^{p-1})<\rk(\operatorname{ad}(y)^{p-1})

Note that

can be calculated from the adjoint Jordan blocks of

of size at least
p
, and if

is exceptional, this can be done by reference to
[
Ste16
, Section 3.1]
. The next lemma follows from a simple case-by-case check, using Tables
1
&
2
, the Hasse diagrams for nilpotent orbit closures and
[
Ste16
, Section 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{N}
such that
x\in\mathcal{V}
, and
y\notin\mathcal{V}
. Then
x\prec y
.

Remark 3.3
.

Comparing ranks of
(p-1)
-th powers is necessary for the partial order to differentiate nilpotent orbits contained in
\mathcal{V}
. For example, let be
G
of type

,

, and take

to be representatives of the
D_{4}(a_{1})
and
A_{4}
classes respectively. Then we have

and

. Using
[
Ste16
, Table 16]
we see that
\rk(\operatorname{ad}(x))=\rk(\operatorname{ad}(y))=78
, however
\rk(\operatorname{ad}(x)^{p-1})=11<15=\rk(\operatorname{ad}(y)^{p-1})
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
. We say that
\mathfrak{X}
is

if the following holds.

Whenever
(e,h,f)
and
(e,h^{\prime},f^{\prime})
are two
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples with

and

, then

and

are conjugate under the action of

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a subvariety of
\mathcal{N}_{p}
. Then
\mathfrak{X}
is monogamous if and only if it is partially monogamous.

Proof.

One direction is trivial. Suppose
\mathfrak{X}
is partially monogamous but not monogamous. Then there exist
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples
(e,h,f)
and
(e,h^{\prime},f^{\prime})
with

such that

. Since

and

are not conjugate.

Let
(f,\tilde{h},\tilde{e})
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple with

, which exists by Proposition
2.2
. Then the two

. But as

is conjugate to

, which is in turn conjugate to

Proposition 3.6

We now describe an ad-hoc method to prove that if

-triple with

Setup:

By Proposition
3.6
, there exists an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)
with
h\in\mathfrak{t}=\operatorname{Lie}(T)
and

in the same nilpotent class as
e
. Let
(e,h,f^{\prime})
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple with

and

. Since

[h,f^{\prime}]=-2f^{\prime}

we have

. We set up a generic element of the subspace

, namely

where the

are variables and

. One can view the set of all

of

. In Steps 1 to 3 below, we add in additional equations and

Step 1:
The equation

yields a set of linear equations among the

. We use these to constrain

and thus reduce the dimension of

. Now every element of

.

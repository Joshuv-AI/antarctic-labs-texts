κ \kappa -Deformed Fourier Transform Journal: Physica A
A.M. Scarfone

Abstract
We present a new formulation of Fourier transform in the picture of the κ \kappa -algebra derived in the framework of the κ \kappa -generalized statistical mechanics. The κ \kappa -Fourier transform is obtained from a κ \kappa -Fourier series recently introduced by us [2013 Entropy 15 624]. The kernel of this transform, that reduces to the usual exponential phase in the κ → 0 \kappa\to 0 limit, is composed by a κ \kappa -deformed phase and a damping factor that gives a wavelet-like behavior. We show that the κ \kappa -Fourier transform is isomorph to the standard Fourier transform through a changing of time and frequency variables. Nevertheless, the new formalism is useful to study, according to Fourier analysis, those functions defined in the realm of the κ \kappa -algebra. As a relevant application, we discuss the central limit theorem for the κ \kappa -sum of n n -iterate statistically independent random variables.

Introduction

As well known, Fourier transform is a very useful tool widely used in mathematical statistics and physics. It consists of a linear operator acting on a function
f(x)
, with a real
argument
x
, and transforming it into a function
\widehat{f}(\omega)\equiv{\cal F}[f(x)](\omega)
, with a complex argument
\omega
:

\displaystyle{\cal F}[f(x)](\omega)={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,e^{-i\,x\,\omega}\,dx\ .

(1.1)

More in general, it belongs to the family of integral transforms, defined in

\displaystyle{\cal H}[f(x)](\omega)=\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,h(x,\,\omega)\,dx\ ,

(1.2)

like Laplace, Mellin and Hankel transforms, with the kernel
h(x,\,\omega)
given by a complex exponential-like form.

Fourier transform finds applications in many fields of science, running from mathematics to physics and engineering. In statistical physics, and in general in probability theory, it is applied in the study of random walk, of infinite divisible distributions, in the analysis of distribution stability and in the study of their domains of attraction as well as in the large deviation theory and in the proof of several central limit theorems
[
1
]
.

In quantum mechanics, Fourier transform links the representation of a wave function in the configuration space with its dual representation in the momenta space. In addition, it is a powerful tool widely used in quantum field theory to evaluate the Green function of a quantum propagator thanks to its property of changing a derivative in a multiplicative power.

Its discrete version is largely applied in the theory of signals
[
2
]
, especially after the introduction of fast algorithms, the so called fast Fourier transform, that speeds up the computation process, allowing the elaboration of a large amount of information, which has permitted to develop relevant data compression algorithms and the realization of high-definition image devices.

In quantum computing theory, Fourier transform is powerfully used in the generation of quantum protocols for the factorization of large numbers and in solving the discrete logarithm problem
[
3
]
.

In the past, several versions of Fourier transform have been proposed in literature by using deformed algebraic structures like, for instance, in the framework of quantum groups where the Hopf algebra underlying the braiding groups has been used to define a Fourier transform in the noncommutative space
[
4
]
. In
[
5

, within the
basic
-calculus, it has been advanced a

-analogous of standard Fourier transform defined in

\displaystyle{\cal F}[f(x);\,q](\omega)=\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\exp(x|\omega;q)\,d_{q}x\ ,

(1.3)

that is realized by employing a complex version of the

[

On a different ground, in the framework of statistical mechanics based on the Tsallis entropy

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{q}[f(x)](\omega)=\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\otimes_{q}\exp_{q}(-i\,x\,\omega)\,dx\ ,

2
Mathematical formalism of the
\kappa
-algebra

In order to embody power-law distributions in statistical physics, in
[
14
]
it has been proposed the following generalized entropic form

\displaystyle S_{\kappa}[p]=-\int p(x)\,\ln_{\kappa}\big(p(x)\big)\,dx\ ,

(2.1)

which mimics the Boltzmann-Gibbs entropy by replacing the standard logarithm with its generalized version

\displaystyle\ln_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\kappa}-x^{-\kappa}}{2\,\kappa}\equiv{1\over\kappa}\,\sinh\big(\kappa\,\ln(x)\big)\ ,

(2.2)

In agreement with the maximal entropy principle, the corresponding equilibrium distribution reads

\displaystyle p(x)=\alpha\,\exp_{\kappa}\left(-{1\over\lambda}\,(\gamma+\beta\,x)\right)\ ,

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\kappa\,x+\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}\right)^{1/\kappa}\equiv\exp\left({1\over\kappa}\,{\rm arcsinh}\,(\kappa\,x)\right)\ ,

(2.4)

is the
\kappa
-exponential, with
\ln_{\kappa}(\exp_{\kappa}(x))=\exp_{\kappa}(\ln_{\kappa}(x))=x
.

Both these functions reduce to the standard exponential and logarithm in the
\kappa\to 0
limit:
\ln_{0}(x)\equiv\ln(x)
and
\exp_{0}(x)\equiv\exp(x)
and consequently, in the same limit, Eqs. (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) converge to the Boltzmann-Gibbs entropy and the Gibbs distribution, respectively.

We note that the shape of distribution (
2.3
), is characterized by an asymptotic power law tail, since
\exp_{\kappa}(x)\sim x^{\pm 1/\kappa}
, for
x\to\pm\infty
. Therefore, it differs from the exponential behavior of the Gibbs-distribution, a fact that justify the use of
\kappa
-statistical mechanics in the study of free-scale systems.

On a mathematical ground, the main features of
\kappa
-statistical physics follow from the analytical properties of the
\kappa
-exponential and the
\kappa
-logarithm
[
23
]
.

For any
|\kappa|<1
,
\log_{\kappa}(x)
and
\exp_{\kappa}(x)
are continuous, monotonic, increasing functions, normalized in
\ln_{\kappa}(1)=0
and
\exp_{\kappa}(0)=1
, with
\ln_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
\exp_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Another function useful in the formulation of the
\kappa
-deformed statistical mechanics is given by

\displaystyle u_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{x^{\kappa}+x^{-\kappa}}{2}\equiv{1\over\kappa}\,\cosh\big(\kappa\,\ln(x)\big)\ .

(2.5)

For any
|\kappa|<1
, the function
u_{\kappa}(x)
is continuous, with
u_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
u_{\kappa}(0)=u_{\kappa}(+\infty)=+\infty

and reaches its minimum at
x=1
, where
u_{\kappa}(1)=1
. Function
u_{\kappa}(x)
, that reduces to a constant in the
\kappa\to 0
limit (
u_{0}(x)=1
), is strictly related to
\ln_{\kappa}(x)
and
\exp_{\kappa}(x)
and appears recurrently in the study of their analytical properties.

These
\kappa
-functions, fulfil the following scaling-laws

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(\mu\,x)=\exp_{\kappa^{\prime}}(x)^{\mu}\ ,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\ln_{\kappa}(x^{\mu})=\mu\,\ln_{\kappa^{\prime}}(x)\ ,

(2.7)

\displaystyle u_{\kappa}(x^{\mu})=u_{\kappa^{\prime}}(x)\ ,

(2.8)

where
\kappa^{\prime}=\mu\,\kappa
. In particular, for
\mu=-1
and accounting for the symmetry relations
\exp_{\kappa}(x)\equiv\exp_{-\kappa}(x)
,
\ln_{\kappa}(x)\equiv\ln_{-\kappa}(x)
and
u_{\kappa}(x)\equiv u_{-\kappa}(x)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(x)\,\exp_{\kappa}(-x)=1\ ,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\ln_{\kappa}(x)+\ln_{\kappa}(1/x)=0\ ,

(2.10)

\displaystyle u_{\kappa}(x)-u_{\kappa}(1/x)=0\ .

(2.11)

Equations (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) reproduce the well-known relations of standard exponential and logarithm in the
\kappa
-formalism.

2.1
\kappa
-Algebra

In
[
24
]
, it has been shown that, starting from a pair of continuous, monotonic increasing functions, inverse each other, we can introduce two different algebraic structures endowed by a generalized sum and product, that form two distinct Abelian fields. By specializing this result to the present case, we can define two deformed algebras on the target space and on the probability space, respectively. Among them, the target space algebra is the relevant one in this work and is revisited in the following, remanding to
[
24
]
for a more detailed discussion.

To begin with, let us introduce the
\kappa
-numbers

\displaystyle x_{\{\kappa\}}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\,{\rm arcsinh}\,(\kappa\,x)\ ,

(2.1.1)

and their dual

\displaystyle x^{\{\kappa\}}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\,\sinh\,(\kappa\,x)\ ,

(2.1.2)

with

\displaystyle\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{\{\kappa\}}=\left(x^{\{\kappa\}}\right)_{\{\kappa\}}=x\ .

(2.1.3)

Mapping (
2.1.1
) [resp. (
2.1.2
)] defines a non uniform stretching of the real axis so that the space of the
\kappa
-numbers
\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
is isomorph to the space of the real numbers, with
(+\infty)_{\{\kappa\}}\equiv+\infty
,
(-\infty)_{\{\kappa\}}\equiv-\infty
and
0_{\{\kappa\}}\equiv 0
.

Generalized sum and product are defined on
\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
according to
[
22
]

\displaystyle x\oplus_{\kappa}y=\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}+y_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{\{\kappa\}}\ ,

(2.1.4)

\displaystyle x\otimes_{\kappa}y=\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}\cdot y_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{\{\kappa\}}\ ,

(2.1.5)

where, in the following, for the sake of notation, we simply mean
\oplus\equiv\oplus_{\kappa}
and
\otimes\equiv\otimes_{\kappa}
unless explicably stated.

These operations are associative, commutative and distributed, with the null element
\emptyset
and the identity
I
, and for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}
, there exist the opposite
(-x)
and the inverse
(1/x)
.
Therefore, the algebraic structure

(\mathbb{R}^{\kappa},\,\oplus,\,\otimes)
forms a commutative group.

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle x\oplus y={1\over\kappa}\,\sinh\Big(\,{\rm arcsinh}\,(\kappa\,x)+{\rm arcsinh}\,(\kappa\,y)\,\Big)\ ,

(2.1.6)

\displaystyle x\otimes y={1\over\kappa}\,\sinh\left({1\over\kappa}\,{\rm arcsinh}\,(\kappa\,x)\cdot{\rm arcsinh}\,(\kappa\,y)\right)\ ,

(2.1.7)

with
\emptyset\equiv 0
,
I\equiv\kappa^{-1}\,\,\sinh\kappa
,
(-x)\equiv-x
and
(1/x)\equiv\kappa^{-1}\,\sinh(\kappa^{2}/{\rm arcsinh}\,\kappa\,x)
.

In the
\kappa\to 0
limit, sum (
2.1.6
) and product (
2.1.7
) recover the standard operations and the
\kappa
-algebra reduces to the standard algebra of the real numbers.

Remark also that, for large
x
and
y
, the
\kappa
-sum approaches asymptotically to the standard product

\displaystyle x\oplus y\simeq x\cdot y\ ,\qquad\ x,\,y\gg 1\ ,

(2.1.8)

for
\kappa\not=0
, as well as

\displaystyle x\oplus y\simeq x+y\ ,\qquad\ x,\,y\to 0\ .

(2.1.9)

Starting from Eqs. (
2.1.6
) and (
2.1.7
), we can introduce other elementary operations like the difference
x\ominus y=x\oplus(-y)
and the quotient
x\oslash y=x\otimes(1/y)
.

However, the main property of
\kappa
-algebra follows from the definitions of
\kappa
-exponential and
\kappa
-logarithm

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(x)=e^{x_{\{\kappa\}}}\ ,\qquad\ln_{\kappa}(x)=\left(\ln x\right)_{\{\kappa\}}\ .

(2.1.10)

Among the many relations satisfied by
\exp_{\kappa}(x)
and
\ln_{\kappa}(x)
, let us recall that

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(x\oplus y)=\exp_{\kappa}(x)\cdot\exp_{\kappa}(y)\ ,

(2.1.11)

\displaystyle\ln_{\kappa}(x\cdot y)=\ln_{\kappa}(x)\oplus\ln_{\kappa}(y)\ ,

(2.1.12)

as well as the following, that will be used in the remainder

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(x\otimes y)=\exp_{\kappa}(x)^{y_{\{\kappa\}}}=\exp_{\kappa}(y)^{x_{\{\kappa\}}}\ ,

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(a\,x\otimes_{\kappa}a\,y)=\exp_{\kappa^{\prime}}(x\otimes_{\kappa^{\prime}}y)^{a^{2}}=\exp_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}}(a^{2}(x\otimes_{\kappa^{\prime}}y))\ ,

(2.1.13)

\displaystyle\exp_{\kappa}(a\,x\oplus_{\kappa}a\,y)=\exp_{\kappa^{\prime}}(x\oplus_{\kappa^{\prime}}y)^{a}=\exp_{\kappa}\left(a(x\oplus_{\kappa^{\prime}}y)\right)\ ,

with
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa\,a
and
\kappa^{\prime\prime}=\kappa/a
. Other relations can be obtained by inspection of

22

2.3
\kappa
-Calculus

Following
[
14
]
, we introduce the calculus in the
\kappa
-formalism by means of the
\kappa
-differential

, given by the differential of the
\kappa
-numbers

\displaystyle d_{\kappa}x={dx\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}}\ ,

(2.3.1)

that is
\kappa
-linear

\displaystyle d_{\kappa}(a\otimes x)=a_{\{\kappa\}}\cdot d_{\kappa}x\ ,

(2.3.2)

\displaystyle d_{\kappa}(x\oplus y)=d_{\kappa}x+d_{\kappa}y\ ,

(2.3.3)

where
a
is constant.

The
\kappa
-derivative is defined in

\displaystyle\left({d\over dx}\right)_{\kappa}\equiv{d\over d_{\kappa}x}\ ,

(2.3.4)

and is related to the Leibnitz derivative by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}=\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}\,{d\over dx}\equiv u_{\kappa}\left(\exp_{\kappa}(x)\right)\,{d\over dx}\ ,

(2.3.5)

where the last equality follows from the relation
u_{\kappa}(\exp_{\kappa}(x))=\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}
derived in
[
25
]
.

Within the standard calculus, the derivative of the
\kappa
-functions previously introduced read

\displaystyle{d\over dx}\exp_{\kappa}(x)={\exp_{\kappa}(x)\over u_{\kappa}\left(\exp_{\kappa}(x)\right)}\ ,

(2.3.6)

\displaystyle{d\over dx}\ln_{\kappa}(x)={u_{\kappa}(x)\over x}\ ,

(2.3.7)

\displaystyle{d\over dx}u_{\kappa}(x)=\kappa\,{\ln_{\kappa}(x)\over x}\ ,

(2.3.8)

\displaystyle{d\over dx}\sin_{\kappa}(x)={\cos_{\kappa}(x)\over u_{\kappa}\left(\exp_{\kappa}(x)\right)}\ ,

(2.3.9)

\displaystyle{d\over dx}\cos_{\kappa}(x)=-{\sin_{\kappa}(x)\over u_{\kappa}\left(\exp_{\kappa}(x)\right)}\ .

(2.3.10)

In this way, by using Eq. (
2.3.5
), we can rewrite these relations in

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}\,\exp_{\kappa}(x)=\exp_{\kappa}(x)\ ,

(2.3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}\,\sin_{\kappa}(x)=\cos_{\kappa}(x)\ ,

(2.3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}\,\cos_{\kappa}(x)=-\sin_{\kappa}(x)\ ,

(2.3.13)

that are consistent within the
\kappa
-formalism.

We observe that quantities like
\exp_{\kappa}(x)\,d_{\kappa}x
,
\sin_{\kappa}(x)\,d_{\kappa}x
and
\cos_{\kappa}(x)\,d_{\kappa}x
are all exact differentials in the standard calculus, since they correspond, respectively to the following differential forms:
d\exp_{\kappa}(x)
,
d\sin_{\kappa}(x)
and
d\cos_{\kappa}(x)
.

In addition, accounting for the
\kappa
-linearity, we can verify the relations

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}f(x\oplus y)=\frac{df(z)}{d_{\kappa}z}\Big|_{z=x\oplus y}\ ,

(2.3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}\exp_{\kappa}(x\otimes y)=y_{\{\kappa\}}\frac{df(z)}{d_{\kappa}z}\Big|_{z=x\otimes y}\ ,

(2.3.15)

and in particular

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}\exp_{\kappa}(x\oplus y)=\exp_{\kappa}(x\oplus y)\ ,

(2.3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d_{\kappa}x}\,\exp_{\kappa}(x\otimes y)=y_{\{\kappa\}}\,\exp_{\kappa}(x\otimes y)\ ,

(2.3.17)

that will be used in the remainder.

Finally, let us introduce the
\kappa
-integral
\int f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x
, as the inverse operator to the
\kappa
-derivative, according to

\displaystyle\left({d\over dx}\right)_{\kappa}\left(\int f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x+const.\right)=f(x)\ ,

(2.3.18)

extending, in this way, the fundamental integral theorem to the
\kappa
-formalism.

We observe that the
\kappa
-integral can be written like a weighted ordinary integral. In fact, recalling Eq. (
2.3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x=\int f(x)\,w(x)\,dx\equiv\int{f(x)\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}}\,dx\ ,

(2.3.19)

where the weight function

\displaystyle w(x)={1\over u_{\kappa}\left(\exp_{\kappa}(x)\right)}\equiv{1\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}}\ ,

(2.3.20)

coincides with that introduced in the Sturm-Liouville problem.

In addition, we can also use the following relation

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}\limits^{b}f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x=\int\limits_{a_{\{\kappa\}}}\limits^{b_{\{\kappa\}}}f^{\{\kappa\}}(x)\,dx\ ,

(2.3.21)

where

\displaystyle f^{\{\kappa\}}(x)\equiv f(x^{\{\kappa\}})=f\left({1\over\kappa}\sinh(\kappa\,x)\right)\ ,

(2.3.22)

that links the
\kappa
-integral on the
\kappa
-numbers to a standard integral on the real numbers.

3
\kappa
-Fourier transform

3.1
The transform

In
[
22
]
, it has been introduced a consistent formulation of Fourier series in the framework of the
\kappa
-algebra. There, it has been shown as any square-integrable function
f(x):\,(-h,\,h)\to\mathbb{R}
, may be expanded in the
\kappa
-Fourier series with respect to the orthogonal and complete system of functions
\sin_{\kappa}(a_{n}\otimes x)
and
\cos_{\kappa}(a_{n}\otimes x)
, that is

\displaystyle f(x)=c_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{n}\,\sin_{\kappa}(a_{n}\otimes x)+c_{n}\,\cos_{\kappa}(a_{n}\otimes x)\right)\ ,

(3.1.1)

where
a_{n}=(n\,\pi/h_{\{\kappa\}})^{\{\kappa\}}
are suitable constants.

This series expansion can be written in the complex form

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-a_{n}\otimes x)^{i}\ ,

(3.1.2)

where the complex Fourier coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}={\sqrt{2\over h}}\int\limits_{-h}\limits^{h}f(x)\,\exp_{\kappa}(a_{n}\otimes x)^{i}\,d_{\kappa}x\ ,

(3.1.3)

and are related to the real Fourier coefficients in
\gamma_{n}=(c_{n}-i\,s_{n})/2
and
\gamma_{-n}=\gamma_{n}^{\ast}
.

Starting from this result,
\kappa
-deformed Fourier transform can be formally obtained in the
h\to\infty
limit, as usually done in the standard theory.

Therefore, from Eq. (
3.1.3
) we derive the following integral transform for a real function
f(x)
given by

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,\exp_{\kappa}(-x\otimes\omega)^{i}\,d_{\kappa}x\ .

(3.1.4)

Accounting for Eqs. (
2.1.5
), (
2.1.10
) and (
2.3.1
), this formula can be written like a standard integral transform [cfr. Eq. (
1.2
)], with the kernel

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)={\exp(-i\,x_{\{\kappa\}}\,\omega_{\{\kappa\}})\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}x^{2}}}\ .

(3.1.5)

In the
\kappa\to 0
limit, kernel
h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)
reduces to the usual exponential phase
h(x,\,\omega)=\exp(i\,x\,\omega)
and the
\kappa
-Fourier transform collapses into the standard Fourier transform.

Kernel (
3.1.5
) is formed by a deformed phase factor, that arises from the generalization of the complex exponential in its
\kappa
-deformed version and by a damping factor, that arises from the measure of the
\kappa
-integral. As a consequence, the new transform acquires interesting analytical features. For instance, both the real part and the imaginary part of the phase factor have an asymptotically log-periodic behaviour, a fact that may be relevant in the study of log-oscillating phenomena
[
24
]
. Otherwise, the damping factor confers to the kernel a wavelet-like behaviour, as shown in Figure 3, where we have plotted both the real and the imaginary part of the kernel
h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)
.

However, many properties of the standard Fourier transform are preserved in the generalized version provided that they are reformulated in the
\kappa
-formalism.

Real (left panel) and imaginary (right panel) part of the kernel
h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)
, for
k=0.1
and
\omega=1
.

It is duty to note that, in addition to Eq. (
3.1.5
), there are other possible generalizations of the kernel. They should satisfy the following reasonable conditions: 1) the kernel must reduce to the standard complex exponential
\exp(i\,\omega\,x)
in a suitable limit; 2) the kernel should be an unimodular function on the whole real axis. These requests exclude the function

\displaystyle h^{(0)}_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)=\exp_{\kappa}(-i\,x\,\omega)\ ,

(3.1.6)

since condition 2) is violated in the far region of the real axis,
|x\,\omega|\geq 1/|\kappa|
. Otherwise, the following functions are suitable candidates

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}^{(1)}(x,\,\omega)=\exp_{\kappa}(-x)^{i\,\omega}\ ,

(3.1.7)

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}^{(2)}(x,\,\omega)=\exp_{\kappa}(-\omega)^{i\,x}\ ,

(3.1.8)

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}^{(3)}(x,\,\omega)=\exp_{\kappa}(-x\,\omega)^{i}\ ,

(3.1.9)

since they have all unitary modulus on
\mathbb{R}
and converge to
\exp(i\,\omega\,x)
in the
\kappa\to 0
limit. However, although some of these functions are equivalent each others, like, for instance

\displaystyle h_{\kappa}\left(x,\,\omega\right)=h_{\kappa}^{(1)}\left(x,\,\omega_{\{\kappa\}}\right)=h_{\kappa}^{(2)}\left(x_{\{\kappa\}},\,\omega\right)\ ,

(3.1.10)

they define different integral transforms with different analytical properties. By inspection, definition (
3.1.4
), with the kernel (
3.1.5
) is the most consistent choice. In fact, it turns out to be isomorph to the standard transform, so that all the analytical properties of the Fourier transform are preserved when rewritten in the
\kappa
-formalism.

Isomorphism follows from a changing of time and frequency variables according to
X=x_{\{\kappa\}}
and
\Omega=\omega_{\{\kappa\}}
, so that transform (
3.1.4
) can be related to a standard Fourier transform of a deformed function

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)\equiv{\cal F}[f^{\{\kappa\}}(X)]\left(\Omega\right)={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f^{\{\kappa\}}(X)\,e^{-i\,X\,\Omega}\,dX\ .

(3.1.11)

It is worthy to note that, if the Fourier transform of a function
f(x)
exists, then certainly exists its
\kappa
-Fourier transform since

\displaystyle\Big|{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)\Big|\leq\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}|f(x)|\,d_{\kappa}x=\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\left|{f(x)\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}x^{2}}}\right|\,dx\leq\|f(x)\|\ ,

(3.1.12)

where the norm of
f(x)
is defined in the Banach space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as usual

\displaystyle\|f(x)\|=\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}|f(x)|\,dx\ .

(3.1.13)

Therefore, the functions space of convergent
\kappa
-Fourier transform is wider than the functions space of convergent standard Fourier transform, thanks to the factor
\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}x^{2}}
that enforces the convergence of the integral.

Finally, when the function
f(x)
has a well defined parity, Eq. (
3.1.4
) can be rewritten in the form of a
\kappa
-cosine transform

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}}\int\limits_{0}\limits^{\infty}f(x)\,\cos_{\kappa}(\omega\otimes x)\,d_{\kappa}x\ ,

(3.1.14)

for even functions or a
\kappa
-sine transform

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)={i\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}}\int\limits_{0}\limits^{\infty}f(x)\,\sin_{\kappa}(\omega\otimes x)\,d_{\kappa}x\ ,

(3.1.15)

for odd functions.

3.2
The inverse transform

As expected, the original function
f(x)
can be reconstructed starting from the transformed function
\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)
by means of the inverse integral transform, given by

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left[\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\right](x)={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\,\exp_{\kappa}(\omega\otimes x)^{i}\,d_{\kappa}\omega\ .

(3.2.1)

This can be shown by using standard arguments, as follows

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa}\left[{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)]\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2\,\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(y)\,\exp_{\kappa}(\omega\otimes y)^{i}\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\omega\otimes x)^{i}\,d_{\kappa}\omega\,d_{\kappa}y

(3.2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2\,\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{\infty}f(y)\,e^{i\,\omega_{\{\kappa\}}\,\left(y_{\{\kappa\}}-x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)}\,d\omega_{\{\kappa\}}\,dy_{\{\kappa\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{\infty}f(y)\,\delta\left(y_{\{\kappa\}}-x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)\,dy_{\{\kappa\}}=f(x)\ ,

where we used the relations

\displaystyle\delta\left(y_{\{\kappa\}}-x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)=\delta(y-x)\,\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}\ ,

(3.2.3)

and

\displaystyle\delta\left(y_{\{\kappa\}}-x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)\,d_{\kappa}x=\delta(y-x)\,dx\ .

(3.2.4)

Otherwise, plugging Eq. (
3.2.1
) in Eq. (
3.1.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\kappa}\left[{\cal F}^{-1}_{\kappa}[\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)]\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2\,\pi}\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega^{\prime})\exp_{\kappa}(-\omega^{\prime}\otimes x)^{i}\exp_{\kappa}(\omega\otimes x)^{i}\,d_{\kappa}\omega^{\prime}\,d_{\kappa}x

(3.2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2\,\pi}\,\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\,e^{-i\,(\omega_{\{\kappa\}}^{\prime}-\omega_{\{\kappa\}})\,x_{\{\kappa\}}}\,d\omega_{\{\kappa\}}^{\prime}\,dx_{\{\kappa\}}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega^{\prime})\,\delta(\omega_{\{\kappa\}}^{\prime}-\omega_{\{\kappa\}})\,d\omega_{\{\kappa\}}^{\prime}=\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\ .

Many properties of standard Fourier transforms can be reformulated in the
\kappa
-formalism. For instance, let us consider the
\kappa

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,g^{\ast}(x)\,d_{\kappa}x=\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\,\widehat{g}_{\kappa}^{\,\ast}(\omega)\,d_{\kappa}\omega\ ,

(3.2.6)

that, rewritten in

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}{f(x)\,g^{\ast}(x)\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}}\,dx=\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}{\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\,\widehat{g}_{\kappa}^{\,\ast}(\omega)\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,\omega^{2}}}\,d\omega\ ,

(3.2.7)

states a relation between the product of the functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
with the product of their
\kappa

\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)
and
\widehat{g}_{\kappa}(\omega)
. Equation (

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\widehat{g}_{\kappa}^{\,\ast}(\omega)\,d_{\kappa}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\omega\otimes x)^{i}\,\widehat{g}^{\,\,\ast}(\omega)\,d_{\kappa}x\,d_{\kappa}\omega

(3.2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,\widehat{g}_{\kappa}^{\,\ast}(\omega)\,\exp_{\kappa}(\omega\otimes x)^{i}\,d_{\kappa}\omega\,d_{\kappa}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(x)\,g^{\ast}(x)\,d_{\kappa}x\ ,

and, in particular, for
f(x)=g(x)

\kappa

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}|f(x)|^{2}\,d_{\kappa}x=\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}|\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)|^{2}\,d_{\kappa}\omega\ .

(3.2.9)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}{|f(x)|^{2}\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,x^{2}}}\,dx=\int_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}{|\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)|^{2}\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,\omega^{2}}}\,d\omega\

(3.2.10)

that differs clearly from the well known Plancherel theorem formulation. Actually, Eq. (
3.2.9
) states a relationships between
\kappa

\kappa\to 0

4
Limiting distribution of
\kappa
-sum of
n
-iterate statistically independent random variables

As known, Fourier transform has many interesting applications both in mathematical statistics and in physics. Among them, the problem of searching for stable distributions of a large number of iterates of independent random variables can be easily handled within the canonical Fourier transform theory. Differently, the problem of searching for stable distributions of a large number of iterates of random variables with a specific statistics dependence or statistically independent random variables with a specific iteration, has been a topic of investigation in the recent years. It can be studied by introducing opportunely defined Fourier transforms
[
9
]
.

In this section we are looking for stable distributions of
\kappa
-sum of
n
-iterate statistically independent random variables.

In order to introduce this question in the framework of
\kappa
-statistics, let us consider a possible generalization of the characteristic function

\displaystyle\varphi\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)=\langle h(x,\,\omega)\rangle\equiv\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,{\cal F}[f(x)](\omega)\ .

(4.1)

For a given normalized probability distribution
f(x)
of a random variable
X
, is natural to define, in the picture of
\kappa
-algebra, the quantity

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)=\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)\ ,

(4.2)

that mimics Eq. (
4.1
) recovered in the
\kappa\to 0
limit.

Like in the standard case, Eq. (
4.2
) coincides with the linear average of the kernel (
3.1.5
) in the space of the real numbers

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)=\langle h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)\rangle\equiv\int f(x)\,h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)\,dx\ .

(4.3)

Equivalently,
\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)
can be written as the
\kappa
-average of the deformed phase factor

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)={\cal N}\,\langle\exp_{\kappa}(-x\otimes\omega)^{i}\rangle_{\kappa}\ ,

(4.4)

where the
\kappa
-average is defined in

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}(x)\rangle_{\kappa}={1\over\cal N}\int{\cal O}(x)\,f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x\ ,

(4.5)

with
{\cal N}=\int f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x
the normalization constant. Both Eqs. (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) reduce to the standard characteristic function
\varphi(f(x),\,\omega)=\langle\exp(-i\,x\,\omega)\rangle
in the
\kappa\to 0
limit.

As an example, let us consider the deformed Gibbs distribution

\displaystyle f_{\kappa}^{\rm G}(x)=\theta(x)\,\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta\,x)\ ,

(4.6)

where characteristic function
\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)
, with deformation parameter
\kappa^{\prime}=\beta\,\kappa
, can be easily calculated in

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(f^{\rm G}_{\kappa}(x),\,\omega\right)={\kappa^{\prime}\over\kappa^{\prime}\,\beta-i\,{\rm arcsinh}{(\kappa^{\prime}\,\omega)}}\ ,

(4.7)

and recovers, in the
\kappa\to 0
limit, the well-known result
\varphi\left(f^{\rm G}(x),\,\omega\right)=1/(\beta-i\,\omega)
.

Otherwise, the standard characteristic function of distribution (
4.6
),
\varphi\left(f^{\rm G}_{\kappa}(x),\,\omega\right)
, assumes a rather complicate expression by means of special functions which are somewhat cumbersome in the analytical manipulation. Remark that,
\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)
carries similar information of
\varphi\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)
since both these functions are related to the starting density
f(x)
by an analytic mapping. In fact, as like as
f(x)
and
\varphi\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)
are Fourier transform each other,
f(x)
and
\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)
are
\kappa
-Fourier transforms each other. This observation justify the use of the
\kappa
-Fourier transform instead of the classical Fourier transform in the analysis based on the
\kappa
-formalism.

Following standard arguments, the phase factor in Eq. (
4.4
) can be expanded in powers of
\omega_{\{\kappa\}}
, such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)={\cal N}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(-i\,\omega_{\{\kappa\}})^{n}\over n!}\,\left\langle\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{n}\right\rangle_{\kappa}\ .

(4.8)

Taking into account the inequality

\displaystyle{\cal N}\,\Big\langle\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{n}\Big\rangle_{\kappa}=\int\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{n}\,f(x)\,d_{\kappa}x<\int x^{n}\,f(x)\,dx=\langle x^{n}\rangle_{\kappa=0}\ ,

(4.9)

it follows that the
n
-order of the
\kappa
-linear momentum certainly exists if the standard momentum of the same order exists.

On the other hand, it is easy to find distributions with only the few first finite standard momenta in spite of the
\kappa
-linear momenta that exist for any order. Again, distribution (
4.6
) is a paradigm. In fact, in this case, it is easy to verify that only the few standard momenta with
n<1/\kappa-1
exist
[
26
]
, while the
\kappa
-momenta are finite for any order.

Finally, from Eq. (
4.8
) we can obtain the relation

\displaystyle\Big\langle\left(x_{\{\kappa\}}\right)^{n}\Big\rangle_{\kappa}={i^{n}\over\cal N}\,{d^{n}\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)\over d_{\kappa}\omega^{n}}\Bigg|_{\omega=0}\ .

(4.10)

provided that the left hand side exist, a relation that can be also derived straightforward by using the
\kappa
-derived of transform given in Table 2.

Definition (
4.2
) is useful to study the limit distribution of the
\kappa
-sum of
n
statistically independent random variables, that is, the limit distribution
f(S_{n})
, for large
n
, of
\kappa
-summed random variables

\displaystyle S_{n}=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus X_{n}\ ,

(4.11)

given by

\displaystyle f\left(S_{n}\right)=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})\ .

(4.12)

In this case, function
\varphi\left(f(S_{n}),\,\omega\right)
coincides with the product of the characteristic functions of
f(x_{i})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(S_{n}),\,\omega\right)=\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x_{1}),\,\omega\right)\cdot\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x_{2}),\,\omega\right)\cdot\ldots\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x_{n}),\,\omega\right)\ ,

(4.13)

as it follows from the distributive property of
\kappa
-sum and
\kappa
-product.
In particular, if the quantities
x_{i}
are also identically distributed, then
\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(S_{n}),\,\omega\right)=\varphi_{\kappa}\left(f(x),\,\omega\right)^{n}
.

To study the problem of searching families of stable distributions of
\kappa
-sum of
n
-iterate of statistically independent and identically distributed (iid) random variables, we consider a pair of iid random variables
X
, with density distribution
f_{1}(x)
. The density distribution
f_{2}(x)
of
S_{2}=X\oplus X
, can be obtain from the relation

\displaystyle f_{2}(y)=\int f_{1}(x)\,f_{1}(y\ominus x)\,dx\ .

(4.14)

Stable distributions fulfill the condition
f_{1}(x)=f_{2}(x)=\ldots=f_{n}(x)
, where
f_{i}(x)
, with
i=1,\ldots,\,n
, refers to the pdf of the
i
-iterate
S_{i}
given in Eq. (
4.11
).

They can be derived easily by using the property of
\kappa
-Fourier transform under
\kappa
-convolution (cfr. Table 2), since the characteristic function of stable distributions is invariant under
\kappa
-convolution.

Let us consider the following ansatz

\displaystyle f_{\kappa}(x;\,\sigma)=C_{\kappa}\,\exp_{\kappa}\left(-{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\otimes_{\kappa}{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)\ ,

(4.15)

where
C_{\kappa}=e^{-\kappa^{2}/4}/\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\sigma
is the normalization constant. Equation (
4.15
) represents a possible
\kappa
-generalization of Gaussian distribution. Standard Gaussian is recovered in the
\kappa\to 0
limit.

Note that, Eq. (
4.15
) differs from other versions of
\kappa
-Gaussian proposed in the literature
[
16
,
20
]
. For instance, the following function

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{\kappa}(x)=A_{\kappa}\,\exp_{\kappa}\left(-{x^{2}\over 2\,\sigma^{2}}\right)\ ,

(4.16)

that corresponds to the asymptotic solution of a diffusive process studied in
[
16
]
, has a power law tail different from that of distribution (
4.15
) that rather decays with a log-normal tail, being

\displaystyle f_{\kappa}(x;\,\sigma)\approx\exp\left(-{1\over\kappa^{2}}\,\ln^{2}\left({\sqrt{2}\,\kappa\over\sigma}\,x\right)\right)\ ,

(4.17)

for
x\gg 1
.

However, in spite of Eq. (
4.16
), Eq. (
4.15
) is invariant under
\kappa
-Fourier transform, since its
\kappa
-characteristic function
\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(f_{\kappa}(x;\,\sigma),\,\omega\right)
, with
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa/(\sqrt{2}\,\sigma)
, is yet a
\kappa
-Gaussian given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(f_{\kappa}(x;\,\sigma),\,\omega\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma\,C_{\kappa}\,\exp_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}}\left(-{\sigma\over\sqrt{2}}\,\omega\otimes_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}}{\sigma\over\sqrt{2}}\,\omega\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sigma\,f_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}}(\omega;\,1/\sigma)\ ,

where
\kappa^{\prime\prime}=\kappa/\sigma^{2}
.

More important, function (
4.15
) is invariant after
n
-iterates of
\kappa
-convolution, defined in

\displaystyle(f\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,g)(x)=\int\limits_{-\infty}\limits^{+\infty}f(y)\,g(x\ominus_{\kappa}y)\,d_{\kappa}y\ ,

(4.19)

that is commutative
(f\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,g)(x)=(g\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,f)(x)
, associative
((f\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,g)\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,h)(x)=(f\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,(g\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,h))(x)
and bilinear
((c_{1}\,f+c_{2}\,g)\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,h)(x)=c_{1}\,(f\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,h)+c_{2}\,(g\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa}$}\,h)
.

In fact, posing

\displaystyle F^{(n)}(x;\,\sigma)=(f_{\kappa}\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa^{\prime}}$}\,f_{\kappa}\,\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-11.38109pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa^{\prime}}$}\ldots\mbox{$\bigcirc\hskip-10.81204pt*\hskip 1.42262pt_{\kappa^{\prime}}$}\,f_{\kappa})(x;\,\sigma)\ ,

(4.20)

its
\kappa
-Fourier transform is related to Eq. (
4.18
) by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(F^{(n)}(x;\,\sigma),\,\omega\right)=\left(\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(f_{\kappa}(x;\,\sigma),\,\omega\right)\right)^{n}\ .

(4.21)

On the other hand, accounting for relations (
2.1.13
), the
n
-power of Eq. (
4.18
) can be written in

\displaystyle\left(\varphi_{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(f_{\kappa}(x;\,\sigma),\,\omega\right)\right)^{n}=\sigma_{n}\,C_{\kappa_{n}}\,\exp_{\kappa^{\prime\prime}_{n}}\left(-{\sigma_{n}\over\sqrt{2}}\,\omega\otimes_{\kappa_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{\sigma_{n}\over\sqrt{2}}\,\omega\right)\ ,

(4.22)

where
\sigma_{n}=\sigma\,\sqrt{n}
and
\kappa_{n}=k\,\sqrt{n}
,
that corresponds to the characteristic of the function

\displaystyle f^{(n)}_{\kappa_{n}}(x,\,\sigma_{n})=C_{\kappa_{n}}\,\exp_{\kappa_{n}}\left(-{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{n}}\otimes_{\kappa_{n}}{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{n}}\right)\ .

(4.23)

Remark that, the structure of Eq. (
4.19
) used in this proof differs from Eq. (
4.14
) since, the former, contains a
\kappa
-integral instead of a standard integral. Nevertheless, accounting for relation (
2.3.19
), it is easy to verify that the function

\displaystyle f_{\kappa}(x,\,\sigma)={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\sigma}{\exp_{\kappa}\left(-{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\otimes_{\kappa}{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)\over\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}\,\left(x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma\right)^{2}}}\ ,

(4.24)

properly normalized, is stable under composition (
4.14
). Therefore, we can confirm that

\displaystyle f^{(n)}_{\kappa_{n}}(x,\,\sigma_{n})={1\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\sigma_{n}}{\exp_{\kappa_{n}}\left(-{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{n}}\otimes_{\kappa_{n}}{x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{n}}\right)\over\sqrt{1+\kappa_{n}^{2}\,\left(x\over\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{n}\right)^{2}}}\ ,

(4.25)

is the density distribution of a random variable corresponding to the
\kappa
-sum of
n
-iterate random variables independent and identically distributed according to Eq. (
4.24
).

In analogy with the log-normal distribution and the sinh-normal distribution, Eq. (
4.24
) defines a family of arcsinh-normal distributions parameterized by the deformation parameter
\kappa
and belongs to the family of the Johnson
S_{U}
distributions introduced in
[
27
]
and given by

\displaystyle f(x)={\delta\over\sqrt{2\,\pi}\,\lambda}\,{e^{-{1\over 2}\,\left(\gamma+\delta\,{\rm arcsinh}\left({x-\xi\over\lambda}\right)\right)^{2}}\over\sqrt{1+\left({x-\xi\over\lambda}\right)^{2}}}\ .

(4.26)

Family (
4.26
) coincides with distribution (
4.24
) for
\gamma=\xi=0
,
\delta=\sqrt{2}/\kappa_{n}
and
\lambda=\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{n}/\kappa_{n}
.

In
[
14
]
it has been shown that the
\kappa
-sum is substantially equivalent to the relativistic addition of momenta and there it was conjectured a possible relation between the
\kappa
-statistics and the theory of the special relativity. In this sense, the
\kappa
parameter plays the role of a speed limit according to the relation
\kappa\propto 1/c
. Consequently, in the
\kappa\to 0
limit, corresponding to the Galilean relativistic limit (
c\to\infty
), the
\kappa
-sum reduces to the standard sum and consistently, the arcsinh-normal distribution (
4.25
) recovers the Gaussian distribution that, as stated by the standard central limit theorem, is the stable limiting distribution of the sum of iid random variables.

It is fair to note that a similar result has been derived recently in
[
28
]
by using a different approach.

Finally, like as like the standard Gaussian is the limiting distribution of iid summed random variables, we expect the same holds for distribution (
4.25
) when iid random variables are
\kappa
-summed.
We show the reliability of such statement by means of a numerical computation reported in Figure 6 (left panel) where we plot the distribution of the random variable
S_{n}
, with
\kappa_{1}=0.5
, after several
n

A further consistency of this result is supported by recalling that the
\kappa

corresponds to the product of

5
Conclusions

In this paper we have reformulated the standard Fourier transform in a formalism consistent with the
\kappa
-algebra and the
\kappa
-calculus. The new formulation has been derived starting from a
\kappa
-deformed Fourier series recently introduced by us in
[
22
]
.

The
\kappa
-Fourier transform
\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)\equiv{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)
, belongs to the integral transforms (
1.2
) and is characterized by a kernel
h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)
composed by a deformed phase and a damping factor that confer to
h_{\kappa}(x,\,\omega)
a wavelet-like shape. In addition, both the real part and the imaginary part of the phase factor have an asymptotical log-periodic behaviour.

We have shown that the
\kappa
-deformed transform of a function
f(x)
is isomorph to the canonical transform since
\widehat{f}_{\kappa}(\omega)
is equivalent to the standard Fourier transform of the function
f^{\{\kappa\}}(x)\equiv f(x^{\{\kappa\}})
, that is

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{\kappa}[f(x)](\omega)\equiv{\cal F}[f^{\{\kappa\}}(x)](\omega_{\{\kappa\}})\ .

(5.1)

However, in spite of this equivalence, the
\kappa
-Fourier transform turns out to be more appropriate to handle functions defined in the realm of the
\kappa
-algebra.

As a relevant example, we have applied our formalism to the study of the limit distribution of
\kappa
-summed statistically independent random variables, that is the distribution given in Eq. (
4.25
) of the random variables
S_{n}=X\oplus X\oplus\ldots\oplus X
by using similar arguments employed in the derivation of the central limit theorem.

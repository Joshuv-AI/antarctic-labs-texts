An ordinary differential equation for entropic optimal transport and its linearly constrained variants
Joshua Zoen-Git Hiew, Luca Nenna and Brendan Pass

Abstract
We characterize the solution to the entropically regularized optimal transport problem by a well-posed ordinary differential equation (ODE). Our approach works for discrete marginals and general cost functions, and in addition to two marginal problems, applies to multi-marginal problems and those with additional linear constraints. Solving the ODE gives a new numerical method to solve the optimal transport problem, which has the advantage of yielding the solution for all intermediate values of the ODE parameter (which is equivalent to the usual regularization parameter). We illustrate this method with several numerical simulations. The formulation of the ODE also allows one to compute derivatives of the optimal cost when the ODE parameter is 0 0 , corresponding to the fully regularized limit problem in which only the entropy is minimized.



Keywords.
optimal transport, multi-marginal optimal transport, entropic regularization, ODE, convex analysis.

Given probability measures
\mu^{1}

(1.1)

over the set

of joint measures on

having the

as marginals. This simply stated problem has grown exponentially in recent years. It has an extremely wide variety of applications and has spawned many extensions and variants; see the books of Villani
[

[

]
for detailed surveys.

A particularly popular modification of optimal transport is

, which arises when one penalizes the

H_{\mu^{1}\otimes\mu^{2}}(\gamma)=\int_{X^{1}\times X^{2}}\frac{d\gamma}{d(\mu^{1}\otimes\mu^{2})}\log\Big(\frac{d\gamma}{d(\mu^{1}\otimes\mu^{2})}\Big)d(\mu^{1}\otimes\mu^{2}),

The development of our ODE results in two distinct contributions. First, it yields a new numerical method to compute solutions to entropically regularized optimal transport and its linearly constrained variants, by solving the corresponding ODE. This is similar in spirit to the method proposed by two of the present authors in

We verify, through several numerical simulations, the feasability of the method presented here to compute solutions to a variety of problems. A key computational advantage of our approach is that it yields the

A second contribution arising from the formulation of the ODE is that it allows one to calculate derivatives of the optimal cost around

(the fully regularized limit, corresponding to

Our work here has another, modest and somewhat incidental, consequence. Aside from developing a general ODE method to solve linearly constrained optimal transport problems, we also provide a general framework for applying the Sinkhorn algorithm to this class of problems, unifying and extending previous work on particular cases
[

This paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we introduce optimal transport with extra linear constraints and the entropic regularization of the corresponding linear programming problem. In this setting, we derive the ODE and prove its well-posedness in Section 3. In Section 4, we derive formulas for higher-order derivatives of the optimal value of unconstrained optimal transport as a function of
\varepsilon

Optimal Transport with Extra Linear constraints

2.1
The basic problem

Let
\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n}
be probability measures
2

2

on respective bounded domains
X^{1},...,X^{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Set
X\coloneqq X^{1}\times...\times X^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{P}(X)
be the set of probability measures on

\Pi(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
be the set of couplings of
\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n}
; that is, measures

whose marginals are the
\mu^{i}
. Let

be a subspace of bounded continuous functions.

If

is the cost function, then the optimal transport problem with extra linear constraints is the following optimization problem:

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})}\int_{X}cd\gamma,

(2.1)

where
\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})\subset\Pi(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
is the set of couplings such that
\int qd\gamma=0
for all

Remark 2.1
.

The marginal constraints themselves can be represented within the linear constraint context; letting
Q^{i}=\{f^{i}-\int f^{i}d\mu^{i}|f^{i}\in C_{b}(X^{i})\}
, the subspace
Q\coloneqq\oplus Q^{i}
of functions enforces the marginal constraints. We choose here to distinguish these constraints, denoting them by
\Pi(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
, to emphasize our focus on optimal transportation. This is contrast to the notational convention in
[
4
]
, where they include the marginal constraints within the subspace
Q
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
Q=\{0\}
, then the problem reduces to unconstrained, multi-marginal optimal transport.

It is easy to see that the existence of an optimizer is equivalent to the non-emptiness of the feasible set, provided that the function
c
satisfies certain mild conditions
[
36
]
.

The corresponding dual problem is:

\sup_{(\psi,q)\in\Psi(Q,c)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{X_{i}}\psi^{i}d\mu^{i},

(2.2)

where
\Psi(Q,c)\coloneqq\{(\psi,q)|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{i}+q\leq c,\psi=(\psi^{1},...,\psi^{n}),\psi^{i}\in L^{1}(\mu^{i}),q\in Q\}

As proven in
[
36
]
, no duality gap exists between the primal (
2.1
) and dual (
2.2
) problems; that is, the optimal values in the two problems coincide.

For a given
\eta>0
, we are interested here in the following entropically regularized version of (
2.1
):

\boxed{\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})}\int_{X}cd\gamma+\eta H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma),}

(2.3)

where
H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma)
denotes the relative entropy of
\gamma
with respect to the product measure
\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}
of the corresponding marginals, defined as:

H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma)=\int_{X}\frac{d\gamma}{d(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i})}\log\left(\frac{d\gamma}{d(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i})}\right)d(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}),

when
\gamma
is absolutely continuous with respect to the product measure, and
+\infty
otherwise. This regularization has been studied extensively in the classical optimal transport problem and many variants. The dual of (
2.3
) is the unconstrained optimization problem:

\boxed{\sup_{(\psi,q)\in\Psi(Q,c)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{X^{i}}\psi^{i}d\mu^{i}-\eta\int_{X}\text{exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{i}+q-c}{\eta}\right)d(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}).}

(2.4)

As the objective function in (
2.3
) is strictly convex with respect to
\gamma
for
\eta>0
, the optimization problem has a unique optimal solution, denoted by
\gamma_{\eta}
. Similarly, as we show below (in the discrete case – see Lemmas
2.3
and
3.1
), if we restrict to an appropriate subspace of
\Psi(Q,c)
then the objective function in (
2.4
) is strictly concave with respect to
(\psi,q)
, also resulting in a unique solution
(\psi_{\eta},q_{\eta})
when
\eta>0
. It is well known in the unconstrained setting
Q=\{0\}
that cluster points of solutions of
\gamma_{\eta}
as
\eta\rightarrow 0
solve the unregularized problem (
2.1
); furthermore, under strong additional assumptions,
\gamma_{\eta}
converges to the unique measure
\gamma_{0}
having minimal entropy
among all minimizers of (
2.1
)
(see Theorem 18 in
[
22
]
for a fairly general result in this direction). These results carry over easily to the more general problem with nontrival
Q
; we provide a proof in Appendix
A
for the sake of completeness. We also show there that cluster points of
(\psi_{\eta},q_{\eta})
solve (
2.2
), at least in the discrete setting that we specialize to below (for convergence in the unconstrained case, see, for example,
[
28
]
).
We are interested here in the curve
\eta\mapsto\gamma_{\eta}
, which interpolates between the optimal transport
\gamma_{0}
at
\eta=0
and the entropy minimizing element of
\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
as
\eta\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, it will be convenient for us to view this interpolation in a slightly different (but equivalent) way: we fix
\eta
and introduce a second parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
to obtain the problem

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})}\int_{X}\varepsilon cd\gamma+\eta H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma).

(2.5)

For a fixed
\eta
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, this becomes equivalent to finding the couplings that satisfy the extra linear constraints while minimizing the relative entropy:

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma).

Note that in the unconstrained problem
Q=\{0\}
, a trivial solution arises—the product measure
\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
itself.

Conversely, if we let
\varepsilon\to\infty
, the problem reverts to one equivalent to the original unregularized optimal transport problem with extra linear constraints (
2.1
), while at
\varepsilon=1
we recover (
2.3
). We are interested here in the minimmizer
\gamma(\varepsilon)
in (
2.5
) as a function of
\varepsilon
, and will show in Section
3
below that for discrete marginals it can be characterized by an ODE in
\varepsilon
.

The dual of (
2.5
) is simply (
2.4
) with the cost function
\varepsilon c
replacing

\sup_{(\psi,q)\in\Psi(Q,c)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{X_{i}}\psi^{i}d\mu^{i}-\eta\int_{X}\text{exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{i}+q-\varepsilon c}{\eta}\right)d(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}).

(2.6)

2.2
The discrete regularized problem

We now specialize to the discrete marginal setting. Assume that each

\text{subject to}&\sum_{x\in X|_{x_{i}}}\gamma_{x}&=\mu_{x_{i}}^{i}&\forall x_{i}\in X_{i},\quad i=1,...,n\\
&\sum_{x\in X}q_{x}^{j}\gamma_{x}&=0&j=1,...,K\\
&\gamma_{x}&\geq 0&\forall x\in X,\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{lrll}\min&\lx@intercol\hfil\varepsilon c^{T}\gamma+\eta\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\log(\frac{\gamma_{\ell}}{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}})\gamma_{\ell}\hfil\lx@intercol&\\

ODE for entropic Optimal Transport

3.1
Formulation of the ODE

We now formulate a system of ODEs characterizing the optimal dual Kantorovich potential for problem (
2.11
). For a fixed
\eta>0
, we define the function:

\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)=-b^{T}\varphi+\eta\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\exp\left(\frac{A_{\ell}\varphi-\varepsilon c_{\ell}}{\eta}\right)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}.

(3.1)

By Lemma
2.3
, we can from now on always assume that
A
has full column rank. We then consider the optimization problem:

\min_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon).

(3.2)

Then, (
2.11
) is equivalent to (
3.2
) with the sign inverted. The first order optimality condition necessitates that at the minimizing
\varphi
:

\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)=0.

(3.3)

It is evident that
\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)
is smooth in
\varphi
and
\varepsilon
. The lemma below implies its strict convexity in
\varphi
, and therefore, for each
\varepsilon
, uniqueness of the solution
\varphi(\varepsilon)
to (
3.3
). Invertibility of the Hessian (also implied by the following lemma) then ensures
\varphi(\varepsilon)
is smooth, by the implicit function theorem.

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)=-b^{T}\varphi+\eta\sum_{\ell}^{m}\exp(\frac{1}{\eta}(A_{\ell}\varphi-\varepsilon c_{\ell}))\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
is uniformly convex in
\varphi
over any compact domain
V\times T\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{e}\times{\mathbb{R}}
where
e
is the dimension of
\varphi
.

Proof.

A straightforward calculation shows that the gradient and Hessian of
\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)
in
\varphi
are given by:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-b^{T}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}A_{\ell}^{T}\exp(\frac{1}{\eta}(A_{\ell}\varphi-\varepsilon c_{\ell}))\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}

\displaystyle D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}A_{\ell}^{T}\exp(\frac{1}{\eta}(A_{\ell}\varphi-\varepsilon c_{\ell}))A_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}.

The Hessian can be rewritten as:

D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\eta}A^{T}DA,

(3.4)

where
D
is a diagonal matrix with the
(\ell,\ell)
th element being
\exp(\frac{1}{\eta}(A_{\ell}\varphi-\varepsilon c_{\ell}))\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
. This implies that
D
and therefore
A^{T}DA
is positive definite, and as the entries are continuous, uniformly bounded below
A^{T}DA\geq d>0
on the compact domain
V\times T
.
∎

Differentiating (
3.3
) with respect to
\varepsilon
we obtain:

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)+D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\frac{d\varphi}{d\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)=0.

Using the lemma, we obtain the Cauchy problem that governs the behavior of the optimal potential under the change of
\varepsilon
:

\begin{cases}\frac{d\varphi}{d\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)&=-\left[D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]^{-1}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\\
\varphi(0)&=\phi_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.5)

Here,
\phi_{0}
represents the initial condition of the ODE corresponding to the optimal
\varphi
solving (
3.2
) when
\varepsilon=0
. This corresponds to finding the measure
\gamma
which minimizes the relative entropy among the admissable class
\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},\mu^{2},...,\mu^{n})
, and is often easier to find than the solution to (
3.2
) for
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, note that when
Q=\{0\}
, representing the unconstrained (multi-marginal) optimal transport problem, the minimizer
\gamma_{0}
is product measure, making it straightforward to verify that
\phi_{0}=0
.
It is straightforward to calculate:

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)=-\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}c_{\ell}A_{\ell}^{T}\exp(\frac{1}{\eta}(A_{\ell}\varphi-\varepsilon c_{\ell}))\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}.

Combined with equation (
3.4
)
for the Hessian, the ODE (
3.5
) can be written explicitly.

3.2
Well-posedness of the ODE

We now establish that the ODE (
3.5
) has a unique solution. This is a straightforward application of the Cauchy-Lipschitz Theorem, after the verification of a few preliminary properties.

As
\varphi(\varepsilon)
is clearly continuous, there exist an
M>0
such that
||\varphi(\varepsilon)||\leq M,\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Therefore, we aim to prove the well-posedness of (
3.2
) over
V\times[0,1]
where:

V\coloneqq\left\{\varphi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{e}|\,||\varphi||\leq M\right\}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\varphi(\varepsilon)
be the solution of (
3.2
) for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Then the trajectory
\varepsilon\mapsto\varphi(\varepsilon)
is smooth and is characterized as the unique solution to the initial value problem:

\begin{cases}\frac{d\varphi}{d\varepsilon}(\varepsilon)&=-\left[D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]^{-1}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\\
\varphi(0)&=\phi_{0}\end{cases}.

(3.6)

Here,
\phi_{0}
is the solution to the problem:

\min_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi,0),

(3.7)

corresponding to the dual solution to the entropic minimization with respect to the product measure satisfying the extra linear constraints.

Proof.

The smoothness of
\varphi(\varepsilon)
and the fact that it satisfies the ODE is demonstrated in section
3.1
.

For existence and uniqueness, to apply the Cauchy-Lipschitz Theorem, we only need that the function

(\varphi,\varepsilon)\mapsto-\left[D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]^{-1}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\nabla_{\varphi}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

is Lipschitz with respect to
\varphi
and continuous with respect to
\varepsilon
on the domain
V\times[0,1]
. This follows immediately from the smoothness and uniform convexity of
\Phi
(noting that the latter property ensures an upper bound on
[D_{\varphi\varphi}^{2}\Phi(\varphi(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)]^{-1}
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Due to the algebraic properties of the entropy, we can recover the curve of the solution to the primal
\gamma(\varepsilon)
by using the optimal dual variables, that is

\gamma(\varepsilon)=\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{i}^{n}\psi^{i}(\varepsilon)+\sum_{j=1}^{K}q^{j}p^{j}(\varepsilon)-\varepsilon c}{\eta}\right)d(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}).

The convergence of the Sinkhorn algorithm for unconstrained two or multi-marginal problems is well known
[
10
]
. On the other hand, while the Sinkhorn algorithm has been applied to various linearly constrained variants
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
, there does not seem to be a general convergence result in this context. The generalization of the Sinkhorn algorithm to this setting is a block coordinate descent method, and our Lemma’s
2.3
and
3.1
imply that the entropic dual problem (
2.11
) can always be formulated in a way that this scheme converges, as the following result confirms.

Proposition 3.4
.

For any initial
\phi_{0}
, the block coordinate descent iteration (Sinkhorn algorithm) will converge to the unique optimal solution of (
3.2
) with fixed
\varepsilon\geq 0
.

Proof.

Recall that we are assuming that
A
has full column rank (and that this assumption is justified by Lemma
2.3
). By Lemma
3.1
,
\Phi(\varphi,\varepsilon)
is strongly convex in
\varphi
for fixed
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Together with the differentiability of
\Phi
in
\varphi
, by Theorem 14.6.7 of
[
29
]
, the result follows.
∎

In particular, note that this result will be crucial in Section
5.4
below, as, to the best of our knowledge, a version of the Sinkhorn algorithm has not been implemented on multi-period martingale optimal transport problems before.

4
Derivatives of the optimal cost

In this section, we illustrate how the ODE (
3.5
) can be used to compute derivatives of the optimal cost at
\varepsilon=0
. For simplicity, we restrict our attention to the unconstrained two marginal optimal transport problem (that is,
n=2
,
Q=\{0\}
), although the techniques apply more generally. In this setting, adopting slightly different notation than before, the primal problem is

P(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times Y}\varepsilon c(x,y)d\gamma(x,y)+\eta H_{\mu\otimes\nu}(\gamma),

(4.1)

while the dual is:

D(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\sup_{u,v}\int_{X}ud\mu+\int_{Y}vd\nu-\eta\int_{X\otimes Y}e^{\frac{u+v-\varepsilon c}{\eta}}d\mu d\nu.

(4.2)

For the discrete marginals
\{\mu_{r}\},\{\nu_{s}\}
, if we denote
c_{rs}=c(x_{r},y_{s})
, then the dual can be written as an equivalent problem:

C(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\inf_{u,v}\Phi(u,v,\varepsilon)=-D(\varepsilon)=-P(\varepsilon),

(4.3)

where

\Phi(u,v,\varepsilon)=-\sum_{r}u_{r}\mu_{r}-\sum_{s}v_{s}\nu_{s}+\eta\sum_{r,s}\exp\left[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{r}+v_{s}-\varepsilon c_{rs})\right]\mu_{r}\nu_{s}.

(4.4)

We will see that one can use the ODE approach to evaluate all derivatives of
C(\varepsilon)
at
\varepsilon=0
in closed form (we provide the explicit calculations for derivatives up to order
2
, but it will be clear that higher order derivatives can be calculated similarly). A potential application is a Taylor expansion for
C(\varepsilon)
around
\varepsilon=0
to arbitrary order.

In this setting, it is actually simpler to eliminate
v
and express the problem in terms of
u
only. Letting
(u(\varepsilon),v(\varepsilon))
be the minimizer in (
4.4
), or equivalently, solutions to our ODE, we have, at
(u,v)=(u(\varepsilon),v(\varepsilon))
, by the first order optimality condition,
\forall j
,

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{j}}\Phi=-\nu_{j}+\sum_{r}\exp\left[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{r}+v_{j}-\varepsilon c_{rj})\right]\mu_{r}\nu_{j}=0.

Hence we have

v_{j}=-\eta\log\left[\sum_{r}\exp\left[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{r}-\varepsilon c_{rj})\right]\mu_{r}\right].

(4.5)

After we substitute (
4.5
) in (
4.4
), we get:

\Phi(u,\varepsilon)\coloneqq-\sum_{r}u_{r}\mu_{r}+\eta\sum_{s}\log\left[\sum_{r}\exp\left[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{r}-\varepsilon c_{rs})\right]\mu_{r}\right]\nu_{s}-\eta,

(4.6)

where we have abused the notation for
\Phi
; note that we can now write
C(\varepsilon)=\inf_{u}\Phi(u,\varepsilon)
.

For simplicity, we denote
e_{ij}=\exp[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{i}-\varepsilon c_{ij})]\mu_{i}
. Note that,

\displaystyle-P^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=-D^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=C^{\prime}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{u}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\frac{du}{d\varepsilon}+\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{s}\left[\frac{\sum_{r}e_{rs}c_{rs}}{\sum_{r}e_{rs}}\right]\nu_{s},

(4.7)

where we have used the first order optimality condition
\nabla_{u}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)=0,\forall\varepsilon\geq 0
. Since it is easy to verify that
\forall i,u_{i}(0)=0
, we have
e_{ij}=\mu_{i}
when
\varepsilon=0
, therefore:

C^{\prime}(0)=-\sum_{s}\left[\frac{\sum_{r}c_{rs}\mu_{r}}{\sum_{r}\mu_{r}}\right]\nu_{s}=-{\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)].

(4.8)

The expectation in (
4.8
) is taken with respect to the product measure
\mu\otimes\nu
.
4
4

4

Note that (
4.8
) could more easily be seen by applying the envelope theorem to the primal problem (
4.1
) and noting the unique optimality of product measure at
\varepsilon=0
in that equation. However, we will require (
4.7
) to compute higher order derivatives.

For the second order derivative,

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\nabla_{u}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]^{T}\frac{du}{d\varepsilon}+\frac{{\partial}^{2}}{{\partial}\varepsilon^{2}}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left[\nabla_{u}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]^{T}\left[-D^{2}_{uu}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]^{-1}\left[\nabla_{u}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\varepsilon}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon)\right]+\frac{{\partial}^{2}}{{\partial}\varepsilon^{2}}\Phi(u(\varepsilon),\varepsilon).

(4.9)

The last equality is due to the ODE (
3.5
). We note that evaluating this quantity at the point
\varepsilon=0
(where
u(0)=0
is known) involves only derivatives of
\Phi
. We relegate the detailed calculation to Appendix
B
and provide only the result here:

C^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\eta}\biggl(\left({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)]\right)^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}[c^{2}(X,Y)]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X])^{2}\right]-{\mathbb{E}}\left[({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|Y])^{2}\right]\biggr),

(4.10)

where the expectations are with respect to product measure
\mu\otimes\nu
, or, equivalently,

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}(0)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}\Biggl(\left(\int_{X\times Y}c(x,y)d\mu(x)d\nu(y)\right)^{2}+\int_{X\times Y}c^{2}(x,y)d\mu(x)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\int_{X}c(x,y)d\mu(x)\right)^{2}d\nu(y)-\int_{X}\left(\int_{Y}c(x,y)d\nu(y)\right)^{2}d\mu(x)\Biggr).

We note that higher order derivatives can be evaluated similarly; for instance, one can find
C^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)
by differentiating (
4.9
) using the chain rule, and then use the ODE (
3.5
) to eliminate the
\frac{du}{d\varepsilon}
terms and substitute
u(0)=0
to determine
C^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)
. The result is a closed form expression involving only derivatives of the function
\Phi
at
(u,\varepsilon)=(0,0)
, which can readily be computed.

Remark 4.1
.

P(\varepsilon)
as defined by (
4.1
) is clearly concave, as an infimum of affine functions. Therefore, we must have
C^{\prime\prime}(0)=-P^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
.

We do not see another way to prove that the expression in (
4.10
) is non-negative. However, the fact that expectations are taken with respect to product measure is crucial, as the expression can in fact be negative for more general couplings. To see this, note that

\displaystyle\text{Var}[{\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X]]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X])^{2}]-(E[c])^{2}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\text{Var}[c(X,Y)|X]]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[c^{2}]-{\mathbb{E}}[({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X])^{2}],

and consider the case
\mu=\nu
such that
\mu
is supported on more than one point,
c(x,y)=x+y
and the coupling is given by
X=Y
. In this case,
{\mathbb{E}}[({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|Y])^{2}]={\mathbb{E}}[({\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X])^{2}]
and the non-negativity of the quantity in (
4.10
) becomes

{\mathbb{E}}[\text{Var}[c(X,Y)|X]]\geq\text{Var}[{\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X]].

However, it is easy to calculate that
{\mathbb{E}}[\text{Var}[c(X,Y)|X]]=0
but
\text{Var}[{\mathbb{E}}[c(X,Y)|X]]=4\text{Var}[X]\neq 0
, which violates the above inequality.

To verify the accuracy of our new numerical method, we also compute solutions via the Sinkhorn algorithm in each example in the first four subsections below; in all cases, the values of the optimal costs obtained from the Sinkhorn are very close to the values obtained from the ODE method

We begin with the two marginal optimal transport problem. Let

c_{rs}=c(x_{r},y_{s})

\eta>0

\inf_{u,v}\Phi(u,v,\varepsilon),

(5.2)

where

\Phi(u,v,\varepsilon)=-\sum_{r}u_{r}\mu_{r}-\sum_{s}v_{s}\nu_{s}+\eta\sum_{r,s}\exp\left[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{r}+v_{s}-\varepsilon c_{rs})\right]\mu_{r}\nu_{s}.

(5.3)

It well known that if we fix

5.3

A
in (

As in the previous section, it will be convenient to further reduce the number of variables, using the first order optimality condition:

\displaystyle v_{j}

\displaystyle=-\eta\log\left[\sum_{r}\exp\left[\frac{1}{\eta}(u_{r}-\varepsilon c_{rj})\right]\mu_{r}\right]=:v_{j}(u).

(5.4)

\displaystyle=D_{uu}^{2}\Phi(u,v(u),\varepsilon)+D_{uv}^{2}\Phi(u,v(u),\varepsilon)Dv(u)+Dv(u)^{T}D_{vu}^{2}\Phi(u,v(u),\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\qquad+Dv(u)^{T}D_{vv}^{2}\Phi(u,v(u),\varepsilon)Dv(u)+\nabla_{v}\Phi(u,v(u),\varepsilon)D^{2}v(u).

The last term vanishes since

D^{2}\bar{\Phi}=[I,Dv^{T}]D^{2}\Phi\begin{bmatrix}I\\
Dv\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(5.6)

\Phi

Appendix A
Convergence of entropic optimal transport with extra linear constraints to the unregularized limit

We aim to show that the optimal measure for the entropic optimal transport problem with extra linear constraints will converge to an optimal solution of the unregularized problem that has the minimum entropy relative to the product measure.

Lemma A.1
.

\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
is compact in the weak topology.

Proof.

Since
\Pi(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})
is a weakly compact set, we only need to show that
\Pi^{Q}
is a closed subset of
\Pi
.
If
\gamma_{k}\to\gamma_{\infty}\in\Pi
weakly, then

\int_{X}qd\gamma_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}qd\gamma_{k}=0,\qquad\forall q\in Q

since
Q\subset C_{b}(X)
. Hence
\gamma_{\infty}\in\Pi^{Q}
.
∎

Given a lower semi-continuous cost
c
. Let
\eta_{k}\downarrow 0
and
\gamma_{\eta_{k}}
solve:

\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}(\mu^{1},...,\mu^{n})}\int_{X}cd\gamma+\eta_{k}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma).

(A.1)

Note that for general marginals, it is often the case that all minimizers of (
A.1
) have infinite entropy. To make sense of the statement that cluster points minimize the entropy among solutions to the unregularized problem, we therefore require an additional assumption (uniform boundedness of the entropy in the Lemma below). This assumption is certainly satisfied in the discrete case, which is the main focus of this paper.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that
\eta_{k}
is a sequence converging to
0
such that the minimizers
\gamma_{\eta_{k}}
in (
A.1
) converge weakly to some
\gamma_{0}=\lim\gamma_{\eta_{k}}
, and that the entropy of the
\gamma_{\eta_{k}}
is uniformly bounded, that is,
\exists M>0
such that
\forall k,H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma_{\eta_{k}})<M
.
Then
\gamma_{0}
solves the unregularized optimal transport problem with additional linear constraints (
2.1
). Moreover,
\gamma_{0}
has minimal entropy among all solutions of (
2.1
):

\gamma_{0}\in argmin_{\gamma\text{ solves }\eqref{eqn: unregularized primal}}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma).

Proof.

By the assumption of
\gamma_{\eta_{k}}
, for any measure
\gamma\in\Pi^{Q}
such that
H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma)<\infty
, we have

\int_{X}cd\gamma_{\eta_{k}}+\eta_{k}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma_{\eta_{k}})\leq\int_{X}cd\gamma+\eta_{k}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma).

By taking the limit of both side for

, with the lower semi-continuity of the cost and uniformly boundedness of the relative entropy of

, we thus have:

\int_{X}cd\gamma_{0}\leq\int_{X}cd\gamma.

Note that by Lemma

. Therefore
\gamma_{0}
solve the original optimal transport problem with extra linear constraints.

\gamma_{\eta_{k}}

\int_{X}cd\gamma_{\eta_{k}}+\eta_{k}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma_{\eta_{k}})\leq\int_{X}cd\bar{\gamma}+\eta_{k}H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\bar{\gamma}).

(A.2)

The optimality assumption for
\bar{\gamma}
to the unregularized problem implies

\int_{X}cd\gamma_{\eta_{k}}\geq\int_{X}cd\bar{\gamma}.

(A.3)

Combining (

H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma_{\eta_{k}})\leq H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\bar{\gamma}).

H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\gamma_{0})\leq H_{\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mu^{i}}(\bar{\gamma}).

This means
\gamma_{0}
has the minimum relative entropy among all optimal measures.

-b^{T}v_{k}+\eta_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\exp\left(\frac{A_{\ell}v_{k}-c_{\ell}}{\eta_{k}}\right)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}\leq-b^{T}\bar{v}+\eta_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\exp\left(\frac{A_{\ell}\bar{v}-c_{\ell}}{\eta_{k}}\right)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}\leq-b^{T}\bar{v}+\eta_{k}\leq\bar{M}

(A.6)

for some
\bar{M}>0
. The second inequality comes from the fact that
A_{\ell}\bar{v}\leq c_{\ell}
and hence the exponential term is less than 1. Since exponential function and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
are always positive, from (
A.6
) we obtain for all
\ell
:

A_{\ell}v_{k}\leq\eta_{k}\log\left[\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}(\bar{M}+b^{T}v_{k})\right]+c_{\ell}.

If
\{v_{k}\}_{k}
is unbounded and
||v_{k}||\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
and without loss of generality if
v_{k}/||v_{k}||\to\hat{v}\neq 0
, then

A_{\ell}\frac{v_{k}}{||v_{k}||}\leq\frac{\eta_{k}}{||v_{k}||}\log\left[\frac{1}{\eta_{k}}(\bar{M}+b^{T}v_{k})\right]+\frac{c_{\ell}}{||v_{k}||}\to 0.

We can conclude that
A\hat{v}\leq 0
. Moreover, the positive of exponential function and
\mu_{i}
also implies that
-b^{T}\hat{u}\leq 0
by the same argument. Therefore,
\bar{u}+\lambda\hat{u}
is also an optimal solution to the unregularized problem which violate the boundedness of
V_{0}
.

Assuming
v_{k}\to v_{0}
as
k\to\infty
. Since
\{v_{k}\}_{k}
is bounded, we can find another constant
\hat{M}>0
such that

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\sum_{\ell}\exp\left(\frac{A_{\ell}v_{k}-\varepsilon c_{\ell}}{\eta_{k}}\right)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{M}

\displaystyle A_{\ell}v_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{k}\log\left(\frac{\hat{M}}{\eta_{k}}\right)+c_{\ell}.

By taking
k\to\infty
for both sides, we deduce that
A_{i}u_{0}\leq c_{i}
. Again, by (
A.6
):

-b^{T}v_{0}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}-b^{T}v_{k}+\eta_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\exp\left(\frac{A_{\ell}v_{k}-c_{\ell}}{\eta_{k}}\right)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}\leq-b^{T}\bar{v}.

Therefore, we can conclude that
v_{0}
is also an optimal solution to the unregularized problem.
∎

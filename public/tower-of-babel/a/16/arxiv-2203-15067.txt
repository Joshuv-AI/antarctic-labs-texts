Triangular structures on flat Lie algebras
Amine Bahayou

Abstract
In this work we study a large class of exact Lie bialgebras arising from noncommutative deformations of Poisson-Lie groups endowed with a left invariant Riemannian metric. We call these structures exact metaflat Lie bialgebras . We give a complete classification of these structures. We show that given the metaflatness geometrical condition, these exact bialgebra structures arise necessarily from a nontrivial solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. Moreover, the dual Lie bialgebra is also flat and metaflat constituting an important kind of symmetry.

For this section, we address the reader to the book
[
9

\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on the space

Therefore, a bivector field

\{f,g\}:=\pi\left(df,dg\right)

\pi_{\sharp}:T^{*}M\to TM
:

\beta\left(\pi_{\sharp}(\alpha)\right)=\pi\left(\alpha,\beta\right).

A map
\phi:(M,\pi_{M})\rightarrow(N,\pi_{N})
between two Poisson manifolds is called a Poisson map if for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(N)
, one has

\{f\circ\phi,g\circ\phi\}_{M}=\{f,g\}_{N}\circ\phi

2.1
Lie bialgebras and Poisson Lie groups

A
Poisson-Lie group

(G,\pi)
is a Lie group
G

\pi

\pi(gh)=(L_{g})_{*}\pi(h)+(R_{h})_{*}\pi(g),

where
(L_{g})_{*}
(resp.
(R_{h})_{*}

G

g
(resp. the right translation of
G

[
7
]
, page 503.

The associated map
\pi_{g}:G\to\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, defined by
\pi_{r}(g)=(R_{g^{-1}})_{*}\pi(g)
satisfies

\pi_{r}(gh)=\pi_{r}(g)+\mathrm{Ad}_{g}\pi_{r}(h),

which means that
\pi_{r}
is a
1
-cocycle of
G
with values in
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
for the Adjoint representation

g\cdot\left(X\wedge Y\right):=\mathrm{Ad}_{g}X\wedge\mathrm{Ad}_{g}Y.

The linear map
\xi:\mathfrak{g}\to\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
defined by
\xi(X)=\mathscr{L}_{X}\pi_{r}(e)
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}

\operatorname{ad}_{x}(y\wedge z):=\operatorname{ad}_{x}y\wedge z+y\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{x}z.

which leads to the following definition:
A finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a
bialgebra structure
if it is endowed with a
1
-cocycle
\xi:\mathfrak{g}\to\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, i.e. for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}

\xi([x,y])=\operatorname{ad}_{x}\xi(y)-\operatorname{ad}_{y}\xi(x),

(1)

and the transpose map
\xi^{t}=[\cdot,\cdot]_{*}:\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\wedge\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
defined by

\langle\xi^{t}(\alpha\wedge\beta),x\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times\mathfrak{g}}:=\langle\alpha\wedge\beta,\xi(x)\rangle_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}

is a Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
.

It is important to note that a Lie bialgebra and its dual play symmetric roles: for any Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\xi)
there exists a dual Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},[\cdot,\cdot]_{*},\xi^{*})
where the Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is defined by
\xi^{t}=[\cdot,\cdot]_{*}

1

is given by dualizing the Lie bracket in

\xi^{*}(\alpha)\left(x\wedge y\right)=\alpha\left([x,y]\right)\ \text{for all}\ \alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\ \text{and all}\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

2.2
Contravariant connections and Metacurvature

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold. The associated Koszul Lie bracket on
1
-forms is defined by (
[
3
]
, page 28)

[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}=\mathscr{L}_{\pi_{\sharp}(\alpha)}\beta-\mathscr{L}_{\pi_{\sharp}(\beta)}\alpha-d\left(\pi(\alpha,\beta)\right),

where
\pi_{\sharp}
is the
ancor map

\pi_{\sharp}:T^{\ast}M\to TM
,
\beta\left(\pi_{\sharp}(\alpha)\right):=\pi(\alpha,\beta)
.

A contravariant connection on a
(M,\pi)
is an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map
D:\Omega^{1}(M)\times\Omega^{1}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M)
,
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto D_{\alpha}\beta
such that (
[
1
]
, page 444):

1.

D_{\alpha}\beta
is linear over
C^{\infty}(M)
with respect to
\alpha
, that is

D_{f\alpha}\beta=fD_{\alpha}\beta\ \text{for all}\ f\in C^{\infty}(M),

2.

D
satisfies the product rule

D_{\alpha}(f\beta)=fD_{\alpha}\beta+\pi_{\sharp}(\alpha)(f)\beta\ \text{for all}\ f\in C^{\infty}(M).

The torsion and the curvature of a contravariant connection
D
are formally identical to the usual definitions:

\displaystyle T(\alpha,\beta)=

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}\beta-D_{\beta}\alpha-[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}

\displaystyle R(\alpha,\beta)=

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}D_{\beta}-D_{\beta}D_{\alpha}-D_{[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi}}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M).

We will call
D
torsion-free (resp. flat) if
T
(resp.
R
) vanishes identically.

The Levi-Civita contravariant connection associated naturally to

(\pi,\langle\,,\rangle)
can defined by the Koszul formula

\displaystyle 2\langle D_{\alpha}\beta,\gamma\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \pi_{\#}(\alpha)\cdot\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle+\pi_{\#}(\beta)\cdot\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle-\pi_{\#}(\gamma)\cdot\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\langle[\gamma,\alpha]_{\pi},\beta\rangle+\langle[\gamma,\beta]_{\pi},\alpha\rangle+\langle[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi},\gamma\rangle.

2.3
Metacurvature

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold and
D
a torsion-free and flat contravariant connection with respect to
\pi
. Then, there exists a bracket
\{\;,\;\}
on the differential graded algebra of
differential forms
\Omega^{*}(M)
such that (
[
5
]
, page 390):

1.

\{\;,\;\}
is
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear antisymmetric of degree
0
, i.e.

\{\sigma,\rho\}=-(-1)^{\deg\sigma\deg\rho}\{\rho,\sigma\}.

2.

The differential
d
is a derivation with respect to

\{\;,\;\}
, i.e.

d\{\sigma,\rho\}=\{d\sigma,\rho\}+(-1)^{\deg\sigma}\{\sigma,d\rho\}.

3.

\{\;,\;\}
satisfies the product rule

\{\sigma,\rho\wedge\lambda\}=\{\sigma,\rho\}\wedge\lambda+(-1)^{\deg\sigma\deg\rho}\rho\wedge\{\sigma,\lambda\}.

4.

For any
f,h\in C^{\infty}(M)
and for any
\sigma\in\Omega^{*}(P)
the bracket
\{f,g\}

coincides with the initial Poisson bracket and

\{f,\sigma\}=D_{df}\sigma.

Hawkins called this bracket a generalized Poisson bracket and showed that there exists a
(2,3)
-tensor
M
such that the following assertions are equivalent:

1.

The generalized Poisson bracket satisfies the graded Jacobi identity

\{\{\sigma,\rho\},\lambda\}=\{\sigma,\{\rho,\lambda\}\}-(-1)^{\deg\sigma\deg\rho}\{\rho,\{\sigma,\lambda\}\}.

2.

The tensor
M
vanishes identically.

M
is called the metacurvature and is given by

M(df,\alpha,\beta)=\{f,\{\alpha,\beta\}\}-\{\{f,\alpha\},\beta\}-\{\{f,\beta\},\alpha\}.

Hawkins pointed out in
[
5
]
page 393, that for any parallel
1
-form
\alpha
and any
1
-form
\beta
, the generalized Poisson bracket of
\alpha
and
\beta
is given by

\{\alpha,\beta\}=-D_{\beta}d\alpha.

Then, one can deduce that for any parallel
1
-form
\alpha
and for any forms
\beta,\gamma
we have

M(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=-D_{\beta}D_{\gamma}d\alpha.

2.4
Flat Lie algebras

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(real of finite dimension) is said to be
flat
if it is endowed with a positive definite scalar product
\langle\,,\rangle
for which the associated infinitesimal Levi-Civita connection, defined for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
by

2\langle\nabla_{x}y,z\rangle=\langle[x,y],z\rangle+\langle[z,x],y\rangle+\langle[z,y],x\rangle

(2)

has zero curvature

R(x,y,z)=\nabla_{[x,y]}z-\left(\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}z-\nabla_{y}\nabla_{x}z\right)\equiv 0.

(3)

In other words, the associated Lie group
G
of
\mathfrak{g}
, endowed with the unique left invariant Riemannian metric extending
\langle\,,\rangle
has its Levi-Civita connection flat.

Milnor in
[
8
]
characterized these flat Lie algebras. Some refinements have been provided later in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\rangle)
be a flat Lie algebra. Then

\mathfrak{g}
decomposes orthogonally as

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{d},

where
\mathfrak{z}
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{s}
is an abelian Lie subalgebra,
\mathfrak{d}
is the commutator ideal satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\mathfrak{d}
is abelian and even dimensional,

2.

\operatorname{ad}_{x}=\nabla_{x}
, for any
x
in
\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{s}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It follows from the above proposition that
\mathfrak{g}
is a unimodular
2
-step solvable Lie algebra, whose nilradical is given by
\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{d}
.

Moreover, from
[
1
]

\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k_{0}},z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{2m}\}

where
\spann\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{2m}\}
is the commutator ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
which is abelian,
\spann\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}\}
its center (possibly trivial) and
\spann\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}\}
its abelian subalgebra such that

and

[s_{i},d_{2j-1}]=\lambda_{ij}\,d_{2j},\ [s_{i},d_{2j}]=-\lambda_{ij}\,d_{2j-1}\ \text{for all}\ i=1,\ldots,k_{0},\,\text{and}\ j=1,\ldots,m.

Indeed, the family
\{\operatorname{ad}_{s}:s\in\mathfrak{s}\}\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is an abelian subalgebra, then it is conjugate by an element in
SO(2m)
to a subalgebra of the maximal abelian subalgebra of

\mathfrak{t}^{m}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&-\lambda_{1}&&&\\
\lambda_{1}&0&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&0&-\lambda_{m}\\

\operatorname{ad}_{s_{k}}
can be written in the form
\operatorname{ad}_{s_{k}}=OJ(\lambda_{k_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{km})O^{t}

J(\lambda_{k1},\ldots,\lambda_{km})=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{matrix}0&\lambda_{k1}\\
-\lambda_{k1}&0\end{matrix}&0&\cdots&0\\

-\lambda_{k2}&0\end{matrix}&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\begin{matrix}0&\lambda_{km}\\
-\lambda_{km}&0\end{matrix}\end{pmatrix}.

Two flat Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are isomorphic if there exists a map
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
which is simultaneously an isomorphism of Lie algebras and of Euclidean spaces (i.e. an isometry).

Examples 2.3
.

1.

Any commutative Lie algebra is flat.

2.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},d_{2}\}

[s,d_{1}]=d_{2},\ [s,d_{2}]=-d_{1},\ [d_{1},d_{2}]=0,

Nondegenerate flat Lie algebras

A flat Lie algebra is

there exists a basis

\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}\}

\mathfrak{s}
such that

\text{for all}\ i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\,i\neq j,\ \text{there exists}\ k\in\{1,\ldots,k_{0}\},\ \text{such that}\ \lambda_{kj}^{2}\neq\lambda_{ki}^{2}.

(4)

Otherwise, the Lie algebra is
degenerate
.

.

1.

The flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\}

[s,d_{1}]=d_{2},\ [s,d_{2}]=-d_{1},\ [s,d_{3}]=\alpha d_{4},\ [s,d_{4}]=-\alpha d_{3},\ \alpha\neq 0,-1,1

2.

The flat Lie algebra

with the brackets

[s_{i},d_{2j-1}]=\delta_{ij}\,d_{2j},\,[s_{i},d_{2j}]=-\delta_{ij}\,d_{2j-1},\ i,j=1,2,

where
\delta
stands for the Kronecker symbol, is nondegenerate. Although for the basis
\{s_{1}+s_{2},s_{1}-s_{2}\}
of
\mathfrak{s}
the property (
4
) is not satisfied.

3.

The flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{4}\}
with the brackets

[s,d_{1}]=d_{2},\,[s,d_{2}]=-d_{1},\,[s,d_{3}]=d_{4},\,[s,d_{4}]=-d_{3},

is degenerate, since
\lambda_{12}=\lambda_{13}
and (
4
) is not satisfied for any other vector generating
\mathfrak{s}
.

In the following, we will deduce the necessary (and sufficient) condition for the degeneracy property to be satisfied regardless of the choice of a basis of
\mathfrak{s}
.

Let
\{s_{1},\dotsc,s_{k_{0}}\}
be a fixed basis of
\mathfrak{s}
and let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}
be linear forms on
\mathfrak{s}
such that
\lambda_{i}(s_{k})=\lambda_{ki}
for any
1\leq i\leq m
.
Denote by
C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{s})
the algebra of smooth functions, endowed with the pointwise product. We have for any
1\leq i,j\leq m
the quadratic form

q_{ij}=\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2}.

If
(s_{1}^{*},\dots,s_{k_{0}}^{*})
is the dual basis of
\mathfrak{s}^{*}
then
\lambda_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ki}\,s_{k}^{*}
and

\lambda_{i}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ki}^{2}\,s_{k}^{\ast 2}+2\sum_{1\leq p<q\leq k_{0}}\lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qi}\,s_{p}^{*}s_{q}^{*}.

The condition
\lambda_{ki}^{2}=\lambda_{kj}^{2}
for all
1\leq k\leq k_{0}
means

q_{ij}=2\sum_{1\leq p<q\leq k_{0}}\left(\lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qi}-\lambda_{pj}\lambda_{qj}\right)s_{p}^{\ast}s_{q}^{*}.

If
g\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{s})
then
gs_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}a_{ik}s_{i}
for some
a_{ik}\in\mathbb{R}
and
s_{i}^{*}(gs_{k})s_{j}^{*}(gs_{k})=a_{ik}a_{jk}
so

q_{ij}(gs_{k})=2\sum_{1\leq p<q\leq k_{0}}a_{pk}a_{qk}\left(\lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qi}-\lambda_{pj}\lambda_{qj}\right).

We will show that
q_{ij}=0
given that
q_{ij}(gs_{k})=0
for all
g\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{s})
and
1\leq k\leq k_{0}
. For any
1\leq p,q\leq k_{0}
the endomorphism
\tau_{pq}
of
V
given by
\tau_{pq}(s_{k})=s_{k}+\delta_{kp}s_{q}
is invertible (where
\delta
stands for the Kronecker symbol) and
\tau_{pq}(s_{p})=s_{p}+s_{q}
. Hence
q_{ij}(s_{p}+s_{q})=q_{ij}(\tau_{pq}s_{p})=0
for any
1\leq p,q\leq k_{0}
(if
p=q
then
q_{ij}(2s_{p})=4q_{ij}(s_{p})=0
).

Now let
\beta(u,v)=q_{ij}(u+v)-q_{ij}(u)-q_{ij}(v)
be the symmetric bilinear form determined by
q_{ij}
. Then for any
1\leq p,q\leq k_{0}
we have

\beta(s_{p},s_{q})=q_{ij}(s_{p}+s_{q})-q_{ij}(s_{p})-q_{ij}(s_{q})=0.

By bilinearity we deduce that
\beta\equiv 0
which shows that
q_{ij}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\beta(v,v)=0
for any
v\in V
. Thus
q_{ij}
is identically zero, i.e.

\lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qi}-\lambda_{pj}\lambda_{qj}=0\ \text{for any}\ 1\leq p<q\leq k_{0}.

Since
\lambda_{pi}=\varepsilon_{ij}^{p}\lambda_{pj}
and
\lambda_{qi}=\varepsilon_{ij}^{q}\lambda_{qj}
(
\varepsilon_{ij}^{q}=\pm 1
,
\varepsilon_{ij}^{q}=\pm 1
), then

\displaystyle\lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qi}-\lambda_{pj}\lambda_{qj}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\lambda_{pi}\lambda_{qi}\left(1-\varepsilon_{ij}^{p}\varepsilon_{ij}^{q}\right)=0.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\varepsilon_{ij}^{p}=\varepsilon_{ij}^{q}=\pm 1\ \text{or}\ \lambda_{pi}=\lambda_{pj}=0,\ \text{or}\ \lambda_{qi}=\lambda_{qj}=0.

We deduce the following characterization.

Proposition 2.5
.

A flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{d}
is degenerate if and only if for any basis of
\mathfrak{s}
there is
1\leq i<j\leq m
such that for all
k=1,\dotsc,k_{0}
,
\lambda_{ki}=\varepsilon_{ij}\lambda_{kj}
, with the property

\varepsilon_{ij}^{k}=\varepsilon_{ij}=\pm 1\ \text{independently of}\ k.

(5)

2.5
Metaflat Lie algebras

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\rangle,\xi)
be a flat Lie algebra endowed with a
1
-cocycle
\xi:\mathfrak{g}\to\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
.
The flat Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\rangle,\xi)
is
metaflat
if

\operatorname{ad}_{x}\operatorname{ad}_{y}\xi(z)=0\ \text{for all}\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{s}.

(6)

This is equivalent to the nullity of the metacurvature
6
. This has been proved in
[
1
]
.

A commutative Lie algebra is obviously flat and metaflat. In the sequel, we assume that the Lie algebra is not commutative.

3
Main theorem

In this section we prove the main theorem (i.e., Theorem
3.3
).

Let
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
be a bivector and let
\xi
be the associated
1

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}a_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge s_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}b_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge z_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\

1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}e_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge d_{2j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell_{0}}f_{ij}\,z_{i}\wedge z_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}g_{ij}\,z_{i}\wedge d_{2j-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}h_{ij}\,z_{i}\wedge d_{2j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}m_{ij}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j-1}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}n_{ij}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}p_{ij}\,d_{2i}\wedge d_{2j}.

For all

,
\xi(s_{k})=\operatorname{ad}_{s_{k}}r
, thus

\displaystyle\xi(s_{k})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}-\lambda_{kj}e_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge d_{2j-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}\lambda_{kj}c_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge d_{2j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}-\lambda_{kj}h_{ij}\,z_{i}\wedge d_{2j-1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}\lambda_{kj}g_{ij}\,z_{i}\wedge d_{2j}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left(-\lambda_{kj}n_{ij}+\lambda_{ki}n_{ji}\right)\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j-1}

For all

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{0}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ik}b_{ij}\right)z_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ik}c_{ij}\right)d_{2j-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ik}e_{ij}\right)d_{2j}.

.

Proof.

If

is non degenerate, i.e. for all

Suppose

is degenerate, i.e. for all

, (

. By (

), we get the lemma, since for a degenerate flat algebra, we have

and similarly for the other coefficients.

As a direct consequence, we get the following proposition:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a flat and metaflat Lie algebra and
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
.

1.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate, then

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}a_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge s_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\

2.

If

is degenerate then,

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}m_{ij}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j-1}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}n_{ij}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}p_{ij}\,d_{2i}\wedge d_{2j}

such that

and

(

).

We can now state and prove the main theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a flat Lie algebra and let
\xi
be a
1
-coboundary associated to some bivector
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
. If
(\mathfrak{g},\xi)
is metaflat, then
r
is a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation
[r,r]=0
. Moreover, the dual Lie bialgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is flat and metaflat for the dual positive-definite scalar product.

Proof.

In both the degenerate and nondegenerate cases, we can assume

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}a_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge s_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}b_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge z_{j}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell_{0}}f_{ij}\,z_{i}\wedge z_{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}m_{ij}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j-1}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq m}n_{ij}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}p_{ij}\,d_{2i}\wedge d_{2j}

Recall that the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket is
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear with respect to its two arguments and is defined decomposable mutlivectors as follows
[
3
]

[X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{m},Y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{n}]=\\
\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+j}[X_{i},Y_{j}]\wedge X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{X_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{m}\wedge Y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{Y}_{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{n}

where the hat sign indicates that the argument below has been omitted.

For all vector fields
X_{1},X_{2},Y_{1},Y_{2}
we have

[X_{1}\wedge X_{2},Y_{1}\wedge Y_{2}]=[X_{1},Y_{1}]\wedge X_{2}\wedge Y_{2}-[X_{1},Y_{2}]\wedge X_{2}\wedge Y_{1}-[X_{2},Y_{1}]\wedge X_{1}\wedge Y_{2}+[X_{2},Y_{2}]\wedge X_{1}\wedge Y_{1}.

Moreover, the bracket is symmetric when restricted to bivectors

[X_{1}\wedge X_{2},Y_{1}\wedge Y_{2}]=[Y_{1}\wedge Y_{2},X_{1}\wedge X_{2}].

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[r,r]=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}a_{ij}m_{kl}\,[s_{i}\wedge s_{j},d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell-1}]+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq m}a_{ij}n_{kl}\,[s_{i}\wedge s_{j},d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}a_{ij}p_{kl}\,[s_{i}\wedge s_{j},d_{2k}\wedge d_{2\ell}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}b_{ij}m_{kl}\,[s_{i}\wedge z_{j},d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell-1}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq m}b_{ij}n_{kl}\,[s_{i}\wedge z_{j},d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell}]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}b_{ij}m_{kl}\,[s_{i}\wedge z_{j},d_{2k}\wedge d_{2\ell}].

By using
\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}=\lambda_{j\ell}s_{i}-\lambda_{i\ell}s_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[r,r]=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}a_{ij}m_{kl}\left(\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}\wedge d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\wedge d_{2\ell-1}\wedge d_{2k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq m}a_{ij}n_{kl}\left(-\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}\wedge d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell-1}+\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\wedge d_{2k}\wedge d_{2\ell}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}a_{ij}p_{kl}\left(-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\wedge d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell}+\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}\wedge d_{2\ell-1}\wedge d_{2k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}b_{ij}m_{kl}\,z_{j}\wedge\left(-\lambda_{i\ell}\,d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell}+\lambda_{ik}\,d_{2\ell-1}\wedge d_{2k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq m}b_{ij}n_{kl}\,z_{j}\wedge\left(\lambda_{i\ell}\,d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell-1}-\lambda_{ik}\,d_{2k}\wedge d_{2\ell}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq m}b_{ij}p_{kl}\,z_{j}\wedge\left(\lambda_{ik}\,d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2\ell}-\lambda_{i\ell}\,d_{2\ell-1}\wedge d_{2k}\right).

Now, we show that the 2nd and the 5th terms are zero and by combining the terms 1st,4th with 3rd,6th respectively, we get
[r,r]=0
. We also show that above equality always holds, whether
\mathfrak{g}
is degenerate or not.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate, then

\displaystyle[r,r]=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}\sum_{1\leq k\leq m}a_{ij}n_{kk}\left(-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\wedge d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2k-1}+\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\wedge d_{2k}\wedge d_{2k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k_{0}\\
1\leq j\leq\ell_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq k\leq m}b_{ij}n_{kk}\,z_{j}\wedge\left(\lambda_{ik}\,d_{2k-1}\wedge d_{2k-1}-\lambda_{ik}\,d_{2k}\wedge d_{2k}\right)=0.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is degenerate, then for all
k<\ell
in
\{1,\ldots,m\}
and for all
i,j
in
\{1,\ldots,k_{0}\}
,

\lambda_{i\ell}=\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,\lambda_{ik},\ \lambda_{j\ell}=\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,\lambda_{jk},\ p_{k\ell}=\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,m_{k\ell},\ n_{\ell k}=\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,n_{k\ell}.

Therefore,
\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}=(\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,\lambda_{jk})\,s_{i}-(\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,\lambda_{ik})\,s_{j}=\varepsilon_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
, so

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\phantom{-}m_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}-p_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}&=\phantom{-}m_{k\ell}(\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,\Gamma_{ij}^{k})-(\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,m_{k\ell})\Gamma_{ij}^{k}&=&\ 0,\\
-m_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}+p_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}&=-m_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}+(\varepsilon_{k\ell}\,m_{k\ell})(\varepsilon_{k\ell}\Gamma_{ij}^{k})&=&\ 0.\end{array}

Thus the sum of the 1st term with the 3rd term is zero. For the same reasons, combining the other terms gives zero.

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the dual vector space with dual basis

\left\{s_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}^{\ast},z_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}},d_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,d_{2m}^{\ast}\right\}.

The subspace
\spann\{s_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}^{\ast},z_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}^{\ast}\}
is a commutative subalgebra,
\spann\{d_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,d_{2m}^{\ast}\}
is the commutator ideal; it is abelian and satisfies the following equations

\begin{array}[]{ll}[s_{i}^{\ast},d_{2j-1}^{\ast}]=-s_{i}^{\ast}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\,d_{2j}^{\ast},&[s_{i}^{\ast},d_{2j}^{\ast}]=s_{i}^{\ast}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\,d_{2j-1}^{\ast},\\
[z_{i}^{\ast},d_{2j-1}^{\ast}]=-z_{i}^{\ast}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\,d_{2j}^{\ast},&[z_{i}^{\ast},d_{2j}^{\ast}]=z_{i}^{\ast}\left(\Phi_{j}\right)\,d_{2j-1}^{\ast},\end{array}

where

\Phi_{k}=\left(\sum_{j=2}^{k_{0}}-\lambda_{jk}a_{1j}\right)s_{1}+\sum_{p=2}^{k_{0}-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\lambda_{ik}a_{ip}+\sum_{j=p+1}^{k_{0}}-\lambda_{jk}a_{pj}\right)s_{p}\\
+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}-1}\lambda_{ik}a_{ik_{0}}\right)s_{k_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{0}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ik}b_{ij}\right)z_{j}.

We denote by
\langle\,,\rangle^{\ast}
the positive-definite scalar product on
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
associated to
\langle\,,\rangle
, via the isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sharp:&\mathfrak{g}&\rightarrow&\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\\
&x&\mapsto&\sharp(x)(y):=\langle x,y\rangle,\end{array}

by setting,
\langle\sharp(x),\sharp(y)\rangle^{\ast}:=\langle x,y\rangle.

We conclude that
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
decomposes orthogonally

\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}=\spann\{s_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}^{\ast}\}\oplus\{z_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}^{\ast}\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,d_{2m}^{\ast}\},

i.e.
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is flat. Since
\xi^{\ast}(x)=0
for all
x
in
\spann\{s_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}^{\ast},z_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}^{\ast}\}
and for all
k=1,\ldots,m

\xi^{\ast}(d_{2k-1}^{\ast})=-\Psi_{k}\wedge d_{2k}^{\ast},\ \xi^{\ast}(d_{2k}^{\ast})=\Psi_{k}\wedge d_{2k-1}^{\ast}\ \text{where}\ \Psi_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\lambda_{ik}s_{i}^{\ast},

then the dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is metaflat.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The dual Lie bialgebra is not necessarily exact. For example, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a noncommutative flat Lie algebra with a trivial cocycle
\xi\equiv 0
then the dual Lie algebra is commutative and the dual cocyle
\xi^{\ast}
is not trivial (thus not exact).

4
Examples

1.

For the
3
-dimensional flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
, with the brackets

[s,d_{1}]=d_{2},\ [s,d_{2}]=-d_{1},\ [d_{1},d_{2}]=0,

any bivector
r=a\,s\wedge d_{1}+b\,s\wedge d_{2}+c\,d_{1}\wedge d_{2}
is a solution of the Yang-Baxter equation since
[r,r]=2(a^{2}+b^{2})\,s\wedge d_{1}\wedge d_{2}
is
\operatorname{ad}
-invariant.
The associated
1
-coboundary is given by

\xi(s)=-b\,s\wedge d_{1}+a\,s\wedge d_{2},\ \xi(d_{1})=a\,d_{1}\wedge d_{2},\ \xi(d_{2})=b\,d_{1}\wedge d_{2}.

We have

\xi\ \text{metaflat}\Longleftrightarrow a=b=0\Longleftrightarrow[r,r]=0.

2.

Triangular flat Lie bialgebra which is not metaflat.
The
4
-dimensional flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{z\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
, with brackets
[s,d_{1}]=d_{2}
,
[s,d_{2}]=-d_{1}
, the bivector
r=z\wedge d_{1}
is a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation but
\operatorname{ad}_{s}^{2}\xi(s)=-z\wedge d_{2}\neq 0
.

3.

For any flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s_{1},\dotsc,s_{k_{0}}\}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{d}
, the bivector

r=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k_{0}}a_{ij}\,s_{i}\wedge s_{j}

is a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (since
\mathfrak{s}
is a commutative subalgebra) and the associated
1
-cocycle
\xi(x)=\operatorname{ad}_{x}r
is metaflat. The dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is metaflat.

4.

For any flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},\dotsc,d_{2m}\}
, the bivector

r=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\,d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2i}

is a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (since
\mathfrak{d}
is an abelian ideal) and the associated
1
-cocycle
\xi(x)=\operatorname{ad}_{x}r
is metaflat and the dual Lie algebra

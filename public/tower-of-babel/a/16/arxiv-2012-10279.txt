Abstract
. Using an explicit construction, we show that the de Branges-Rovnyak spaces ℋ ⁡ ( b ) \mathscr{H}(b) , corresponding to rational b b , are contained in their associated Smirnov classes.

1.
Introduction

Let
H^{2}
denote the Hardy space of analytic functions
f
on the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
for which

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(r\xi)|^{2}dm(\xi)

is finite. In the above,
\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}
is the unit circle and
m
is normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
. One can see that
H^{2}
contains
H^{\infty}
, the bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, and that any
\varphi\in H^{\infty}
satisfies
\varphi H^{2}\subseteq H^{2}
. In fact, these are the only analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
which multiply
H^{2}
into itself. Furthermore,
\varphi H^{2}
is dense in
H^{2}
if and only if
\varphi
is a bounded outer function. A well-known theorem of Smirnov
[
5
, p. 16]
says that every function
f\in H^{2}
can be written as

f=\varphi/\psi,

where
\varphi,\psi\in H^{\infty}
and
\psi
is outer.

Taking inspiration from
H^{2}
, one can define, for a general reproducing Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, the
Smirnov class
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
, to be the set of quotients

\varphi/\psi
,
where
\varphi
and
\psi
are multipliers of
\mathcal{H}
(
\varphi\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and
\psi\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
) and the denominator
\psi
is a cyclic multiplier (
\psi\mathcal{H}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
). We say that
\mathcal{H}
satisfies
the
Smirnov property
if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
.
In general, multipliers of
\mathcal{H}
belong to
H^{\infty}
but not every
H^{\infty}
function is a multiplier of
\mathcal{H}
. Furthermore, the density of
\psi\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{H}
is usually more complicated than the simple criterion that
\psi
is a bounded outer function. Indeed, when the polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}
and the shift operator
S_{\mathcal{H}}
(the multiplication by
z
) acts as a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
, then
\psi\mathcal{H}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
if and only if

\psi
is a cyclic vector for
S_{\mathcal{H}}
. This problem of cyclicity is known to be notoriously difficult and is only completely understood for a handful number of cases.

A remarkable result in
[
1
]
says that reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the complete Nevanlinna–Pick property, along with the normalizing condition
k_{z_{0}}(z)=1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, have the property that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
. Two related papers
[
2
,
15
]
show that the harmonically weighted Dirichlet spaces
D(\mu)
and a class of de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
\mathscr{H}(b)
enjoy the complete Nevanlinna-Pick property and the above mentioned normalizing condition and thus belong to their associated Smirnov classes. The purpose of this paper is to give an explicit construction which proves the Smirnov property for a large class of
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces including the results mentioned above.

To state our main theorem (Theorem
1.1
), we make the following definitions. Let

H^{\infty}_{1}=\Big\{\varphi\in H^{\infty}:\|\varphi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|\leqslant 1\Big\}

denote the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}
and for
b\in H^{\infty}_{1}
the
de Branges–Rovnyak space

\mathscr{H}(b)
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space of analytic functions whose kernel is

k^{b}_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(\lambda)}}{1-\overline{\lambda}z},\quad\lambda,z\in\mathbb{D}.

See
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
for the details concerning
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces.
Recall that
f\in H^{\infty}
is an
inner function
if the radial boundary function
f(e^{i\theta})
satisfies
|f(e^{i\theta})|=1
almost everywhere with respect to normalized Lebesgue measure
m
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. An
f\in H^{\infty}
is
outer
if

f(z)=\exp\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}\log|f(\xi)|dm(\xi)\Big),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The main result of our paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
b\in H^{\infty}_{1}
is rational but not inner, then
\mathscr{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{H}(b))
.

We discuss an extension of this result when
b
is outer in Corollary
4.3
. Note that if
b
is rational and inner, it must be a finite Blaschke product. In this situation
\mathscr{H}(b)
becomes a model space
(bH^{2})^{\perp}
(in fact a finite dimensional one) and the multipliers of any model space are just the constant functions
[
7
]
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
is no longer true. The multipliers of
\mathscr{H}(b)
for general
b\in H_{1}^{\infty}
were studied in
[
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
but are far from being completely understood.

Isolated cases of Theorem
1.1
can be gleaned from results in
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
.
More precisely, when
b
is rational and not inner, there is a unique rational function
a
such that
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and
a(0)>0
. First, one can prove that if the zeros of
a
on
\mathbb{T}
are all simple, then
\mathscr{H}(b)
coincides with a
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
space (with equivalent norms)
[
3
]
and so, by
[
1
]
and
[
15
]
,
\mathscr{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{H}(b))
. Second, if
b(0)=0
and there exists an analytic function
h
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
h(b(z))=z
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and the function
(z-b(0))/h(z)
extends to be analytic on
\mathbb{D}
with

\left|\frac{z-b(0)}{h(z)}\right|\leqslant|1-\overline{b(0)}z|,\quad\mbox{for $z\in\mathbb{D}$},

then by
[
2
]

\mathscr{H}(b)
has the compete Nevanlinna Pick property; since
k^{b}_{0}\equiv 1
,
[
1
]
implies that
\mathscr{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{H}(b))
. In particular, the hypothesis of the above results exclude a large class of rational
b
.

One of the main reasons for this paper is to prove Theorem
1.1
directly for all rational
b
(not just the cases mentioned in the previous paragraph), and only using the tools of
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces. In addition, our construction is explicit.

The main idea that makes all of this work is that when
b\in H_{1}^{\infty}
is rational and not inner, then
\mathscr{H}(b)=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a})
(with equivalent norms), where
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a})=T_{\overline{a}}H^{2}
(equipped with the range norm),
a\in H^{\infty}_{1}
satisfies
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
, and
T_{\overline{a}}
is the co-analytic Toeplitz operator on
H^{2}
.
Furthermore, we know the exact structure of
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a})
(and hence
\mathscr{H}(b)
), as well as its multiplier algebra. Along the way to proving Theorem
1.1
, we prove the following curious representation theorem for
H^{2}
functions.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
p
is a polynomial whose roots all lie on
\mathbb{T}
, then any
h\in H^{2}
can be written as

(1.3)

h=\frac{u}{pv+1},

where
u,v\in H^{2}
,
pu,pv\in H^{\infty}
, and
pv+1
is outer.

Our methods also yield a characterization of the cyclic vectors for the shift operator
f\mapsto zf
on
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a})
, and hence
\mathscr{H}(b)
, for rational
b
(Proposition
3.8
).

2.
The Smirnov class for general spaces

Before getting to the Smirnov class for
\mathscr{H}(b)
, let us make a few remarks about the general case where
\mathcal{H}
is any reproducing kernel Hilbert space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Define the
multiplier algebra
of
\mathcal{H}
as

\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\varphi\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\}.

Standard facts say that
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq H^{\infty}
and if the constant function
1
belongs to
\mathcal{H}
, which will be the case of
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces we consider, then
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and hence
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathcal{H}\cap H^{\infty}
. Moreover, when
\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})
, an application of the closed graph theorem, says that the multiplication operator
M_{\varphi}f=\varphi f
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
. The quantity

\|M_{\varphi}\|=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{H},\|f\|=1}\|\varphi f\|,

the operator norm of
M_{\varphi}
, is called the
multiplier norm
of
\varphi
and satisfies
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|M_{\varphi}\|
. For the Hardy space
H^{2}
it is a standard fact that
\mathfrak{M}(H^{2})=H^{\infty}
and
\|M_{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
. For many other spaces,
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})
is a proper subset of
H^{\infty}
and it may happen that
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}<\|M_{\varphi}\|
.

The
Smirnov class
of
\mathcal{H}
is

\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H}):=\Big\{\frac{\varphi}{\psi}:\varphi,\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H}),\overline{\varphi\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{H}\Big\}.

In the above
\overline{\varphi\mathcal{H}}
denotes the closure of
\varphi\mathcal{H}
in the norm of
\mathcal{H}
.
The following fact is standard but we recall it here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
is an algebra.

Proof.

For a multiplier
\varphi
we have
\overline{\varphi\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{H}
if and only if
\operatorname{Ker}M_{\varphi}^{*}=\{0\}
.
For
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
, we need to prove that
f_{1}+f_{2}
and
f_{1}f_{2}
belong to
\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
. Write

f_{j}=\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\psi_{j}},\quad\varphi_{j},\psi_{j}\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H}),\quad\operatorname{Ker}M_{\psi_{j}}^{*}=\{0\}.

Since

f_{1}+f_{2}=\frac{\varphi_{1}\psi_{2}+\varphi_{2}\psi_{1}}{\psi_{1}\psi_{2}}

and
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})
is an algebra, it follows that
\varphi_{1}\psi_{2}+\varphi_{2}\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}\psi_{2}\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H})
. Furthermore, since
M_{\psi_{1}\psi_{2}}^{*}=M_{\psi_{2}}^{*}M_{\psi_{1}}^{*}
is injective, we see that
f_{1}+f_{2}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
. The proof that
f_{1}f_{2}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H})
is done in a similar way.
∎

While the following lemma might appear quite obvious, it is important to be stated here
since the complete Nevanlinna-Pick property, which plays quite some role in connection
with the Smirnov property, heavily depends on the norm of the space whereas the Smirnov
property does not.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
that are equal as sets. Then
\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

Proof.

The closed graph theorem shows that the norms on
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are equivalent in that there are positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leqslant c_{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}.

Thus
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
, and, for a multiplier
\varphi
, it follows that
\varphi\mathcal{H}_{1}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
if and only if
\varphi\mathcal{H}_{2}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Thus
\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{1})=\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.
∎

3.
Some reminders about
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces

Here are some of the basics of
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces needed for this paper. The details can be found in
[
10
,
11
,
14
]
. For
b\in H^{\infty}_{1}
, the de Branges–Rovnyak space
\mathscr{H}(b)
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space induced by the positive definite kernel

k^{b}_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(\lambda)}}{1-\overline{\lambda}z},\quad\lambda,z\in\mathbb{D}.

More properties of the specific class of
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces that we will consider are given in Proposition
3.3
.

When
b
is not an extreme point of
H^{\infty}_{1}
, which is equivalent to the condition
\log(1-|b|)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
(which will always be the case in this paper since
b
is rational but not inner), then there exists a unique (up to a unimodular scalar) outer
a\in H_{1}^{\infty}
such that
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. The pair
(a,b)
is call a
Pythagorean pair
and
a
is known as the
Pythagorean mate
to
b
. Below are some facts from
[
6
]
about Pythagorean pairs.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
b\in H^{\infty}_{1}
is rational but not inner. Then

(a)

a
is also rational;

(b)

(a,b)
form a corona pair in that

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(|a(z)|+|b(z)|)>0.

Two important classes of functions in
\mathscr{H}(b)
, where
b
is not an extreme point of
H^{\infty}_{1}
, are

\mathscr{M}(a):=aH^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathscr{M}(\overline{a}):=T_{\overline{a}}H^{2}.

Here
T_{\overline{a}}
is the co-analytic Toeplitz operator
T_{\overline{a}}f=P_{+}(\overline{a}f)
on
H^{2}
.
We mention that
a
is assumed outer (guaranteeing uniqueness up to a unimodular
scalar factor).
We will denote the zeros of
a
on
\mathbb{T}
by
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
and
the corresponding multiplicity by
m_{j}
, and define

(3.2)

a_{1}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\xi_{j})^{m_{j}},

Since
a
cannot vanish on
\mathbb{D}
and zeros outside the closure of
\mathbb{D}
define invertible factors, we have

\mathscr{M}(\overline{a})=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})

[
9
, Prop. 2.7]
.
The proposition below is from
[
3
,
6
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
b\in H^{\infty}_{1}
is rational but not inner. Then

(a)

\mathscr{M}(a_{1})\subseteq\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})=\mathscr{H}(b)
;

(b)

If
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
are the zeros of
a_{1}
on
\mathbb{T}
with corresponding multiplicities
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}
, then

(3.4)

\mathscr{H}(b)=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})=\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\xi_{j})^{m_{j}}\Big)H^{2}\dotplus\mathscr{P}_{N-1},

where
N=m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{n}
and
\mathscr{P}_{N-1}
are the polynomials whose degree is at most
N-1
, and
\dotplus
signifies the algebraic direct sum.

(c)

An equivalent norm on
\mathscr{H}(b)
is given by

(3.5)

\|a_{1}g+q\|^{2}=\|g\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|q\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

where
f=a_{1}g+q
is the decomposition given by (
3.4
).

We will prove that
\mathscr{H}(b)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{H}(b))
(Theorem
1.1
) by using the fact that
\mathscr{H}(b)=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
(as sets with equivalent norms) and then employing Lemma
2.2
. So we will now focus on proving the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
a_{1}
is a polynomial whose zeros are contained in
\mathbb{T}
, then
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
.

So far, we can readily identify the contents of
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
. We can also readily identify the multipliers. Recall that an analytic function
\varphi
is a
multiplier
of
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
if
\varphi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})\subseteq\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
. The multiplier algebra for
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
is denoted by
\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
. As mentioned previously,
\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))\subseteq H^{\infty}\cap\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
but equality does not always hold. The multiplier algebra for general
\mathscr{H}(b)
spaces has been studied in
[
4
,
12
,
13
]
and is often a complicated class of functions that is related to the invertibility of the product of certain Hankel operators and their adjoints
[
12
]
. Fortunately, in our case, they are particularly simple
[
9
]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

If
a_{1}
is a polynomial of degree
N
whose zeros lie in
\mathbb{T}
, then

\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})\cap H^{\infty}.

In particular,
\varphi\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
if and only if
\varphi=a_{1}\widetilde{\varphi}+r
, where
\widetilde{\varphi}\in H^{2}
,
r\in\mathscr{P}_{N-1}
, and
a_{1}\widetilde{\varphi}\in H^{\infty}
.

The Smirnov class of
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
is then

\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))=\Big\{\frac{\varphi}{\psi}:\varphi,\psi\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})),\overline{\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})}=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})\Big\}.

Proposition
3.7
describes
\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
. To describe
\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
, it remains to characterize the cyclic vectors for the shift operator on

\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
. This is done in the following. We remind the reader that
a_{1}
is a polynomial of the form in (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
a_{1}
be a polynomial of degree
N
whose zeros lie in
\mathbb{T}
and
let
\psi=a_{1}\widetilde{\psi}+r\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
, where
\widetilde{\psi}\in H^{2}
, with
a_{1}\widetilde{\psi}\in H^{\infty}
, and
r\in\mathscr{P}_{N-1}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
is dense in
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
;

(b)

\psi
is outer and
\operatorname{gcd}(a_{1},r)=1
.

Proof.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a): Assume
\psi
is outer and
\operatorname{gcd}(a_{1},r)=1
. In view of (
3.5
), we use the following inner product on
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
:

\langle a_{1}h_{1}+q_{1},a_{1}h_{2}+q_{2}\rangle=\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{H^{2}}+\langle q_{1},q_{2}\rangle_{H^{2}}.

Let
f\in\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
such that
f\perp\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
, that is,

(3.9)

\langle f,\psi g\rangle=0,\quad g\in\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}).

Writing
f=a_{1}\widetilde{f}+p
and applying (
3.9
) to
g=a_{1}h
for every
h\in H^{2}
we get

0=\langle a_{1}\widetilde{f}+p,a_{1}\psi h\rangle=\langle\widetilde{f},\psi h\rangle_{H^{2}}.

Since
\psi
is outer, we have
\overline{\psi H^{2}}=H^{2}
and hence
\widetilde{f}\equiv 0
. It remains to check that
p\equiv 0
. Since
\operatorname{gcd}(a_{1},r)=1
, apply the Euclidean algorithm to the ring of polynomials to obtain polynomials
q
and
s
such that

qr=a_{1}s+1.

Now apply (
3.9
) to
g=pq\in\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
(recall that
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
contains the polynomials). Then

0=\langle p,\psi pq\rangle=\langle p,a_{1}pq\widetilde{\psi}+pqr\rangle.

But note that

pqr=p(a_{1}s+1)=pa_{1}s+p

and
p\perp a_{1}pq\widetilde{\psi}+a_{1}ps
. Hence,

0=\langle p,p\rangle_{H^{2}}
which yields
p\equiv 0
.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Assume that
\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
is dense in
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
.
Since
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
embeds
boundedly into
H^{2}

and polynomials belong to

\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
we have

\mathbb{C}[z]\subseteq\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})=\overline{\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})}^{\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})}\subseteq\overline{\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})}^{H^{2}}\subseteq\overline{\psi H^{2}}^{H^{2}}

and so
\overline{\psi H^{2}}=H^{2}
.
By Beurling’s theorem
[
5
]
,
\psi
is outer. Now we need to check that
\operatorname{gcd}(a_{1},r)=1
. It follows from (
3.4
) (see
[
8
, Cor. 4.11]
for the details) that the radial limit of every function in
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
exists at each
\xi_{j}
(the zeros of the polynomial
a_{1}
) and

|f(\xi_{j})|\leqslant C_{j}\|f\|,\quad f\in\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}).

Using our assumption again that
\overline{\psi\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})}=\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
we produce a sequence
g_{n}\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
such that
\|1-\psi g_{n}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. If
\psi(\xi_{j})=0
then

1\leqslant C_{j}\|1-\psi p_{n}\|\to 0
which is an obvious contradiction. Thus
\psi(\xi_{j})\not=0
for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. From the representation
\psi=a_{1}\widetilde{\psi}+r
we see that
r(\xi_{j})\not=0
for all
j
. This means that the zeros of
r
do not meet the zeros of
a_{1}
and so
\operatorname{gcd}(a_{1},r)=1
.
∎

4.
\mathscr{H}(b)
is contained in its Smirnov class

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

For
h\in H^{2}
, set

(4.1)

F(z)=2\,p(z)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{it}}{e^{it}-z}\,\frac{|p(e^{it})|}{p(e^{it})}|h(e^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

A partial fraction decomposition with respect to the variable
e^{it}
yields

(4.2)

\frac{e^{it}p(z)}{(e^{it}-z)p(e^{it})}=\frac{z}{e^{it}-z}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{k}}\frac{p_{k,l}(z)}{(\xi_{k}-e^{it})^{l}},

where
p_{k,l}(z)
are polynomials.

Since
2z=e^{it}+z-(e^{it}-z)
, and with the above decomposition in mind, we get

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z}\,|p(e^{it})h(e^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|p(e^{it})h(e^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{k}}{p_{k,l}(z)}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|p(e^{it})|}{(\xi_{k}-e^{it})^{l}}|h(e^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Note that
|p(e^{it})|/|\xi_{k}-e^{it}|^{l}
is bounded, and so all the integrals appearing
in the above double sum are well defined coefficients. As a result we can write

F(z)=\underbrace{\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z}|p(e^{it})h(e^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}}_{F_{0}(z)}+q(z),

where
q
is a polynomial.
Hence

F=F_{0}+q.

Since

\Re\Big(\frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z}\Big)=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|e^{it}-z|^{2}}>0,

F_{0}
has positive real part.
We also let
Q=1-q
so that

F+Q=F_{0}+q+Q=F_{0}+1.

Setting

\alpha=\frac{Qh}{F_{0}+1},\quad\beta=\frac{F}{F_{0}+1},

we get

\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}=\frac{Qh/(F_{0}+1)}{1-F/(F_{0}+1)}=\frac{qh}{F_{0}+1-F}=\frac{Qh}{Q}=h.

It remains to show that

\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}

is of the form (
1.3
).

Since
\Re F_{0}>0
it follows that
|F_{0}+1|>1
. Since
Q
is a polynomial and
h\in H^{2}
,
we have
\alpha\in H^{2}
. For the boundedness of
p\alpha
it suffices to check that
ph/(F_{0}+1)
is bounded.
From the preceding discussions it is clear that
\Re F_{0}
is the Poisson extension
of
|ph|
, and so

\frac{|ph|}{|F_{0}+1|}\leqslant\frac{|ph|}{\Re F_{0}+1}=\frac{|ph|}{|ph|+1}\leqslant 1.

The function
\beta
is bounded by the following argument

|\beta|=\frac{|F|}{|F_{0}+1|}=\frac{|F_{0}+p|}{|F_{0}+1|}\leqslant\frac{|F_{0}|}{|F_{0}+1|}+\frac{|p|}{|F_{0}+1|},

and since
\Re F_{0}>0
both terms are bounded. From (
4.1
) the function
F
is
p
times the Cauchy transform of an
L^{2}
function and thus
F=pg
, where
g\in H^{2}
. Hence

\beta=\frac{pg}{F_{0}+1}\in pH^{2}.

Next we need to verify that

1-\beta=1-F/(F_{0}+1)=\frac{F_{0}+1-F}{F_{0}+1}=\frac{Q}{F_{0}+1}

is outer. This is equivalent to saying that
q
does not vanish on
\mathbb{D}
.
Since
Q=1-q
, and the coefficients in
p
are homogeneous in
h
, we can apply a
rescaling argument on
h
to make
p
small and hence
Q
is zero-free in
\mathbb{D}
.
Thus we have checked that
h=\alpha/(1-\beta)
satisfies the properties of (
1.3
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Observe that we have reduced this to proving Theorem
3.6
.

Since

\mathscr{P}_{N-1}\subseteq\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})\cap H^{\infty}=\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
and since
1\cdot\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
is trivially dense in
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
, then
p=p/1\in\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}}))
and the polynomial part of
\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})
can be written as required.. Thus by Lemma
2.1
we just need to show that

a_{1}H^{2}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{+}(\mathscr{M}(\overline{a_{1}})).

By Proposition
3.8
this is equivalent to showing that
a_{1}H^{2}
is a subset of the class of functions

\frac{a_{1}f_{1}+r_{1}}{a_{1}f_{2}+r_{2}},

where
f_{1},f_{2}\in H^{2}
with
a_{1}f_{1},a_{1}f_{1}\in H^{\infty}
,

,
a_{1}f_{2}+r_{2}
is outer, and

. But this follows from Theorem
1.2
. Note that we can choose

and

.

We end with the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

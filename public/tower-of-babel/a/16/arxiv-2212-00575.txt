Thermodynamics of parametric dark energy models
Samantha Rath

Abstract
A comparative study of a set of parametric dark energy models is performed by studying the evolution of dark energy both in the past and future epochs. In addition, the age of the universe and time till the distant future ( a = 1000 a=1000 ) are estimated. The validity of generalized second law of thermodynamic in different parametric models is also ascertained.

II
Thermodynamics of dark energy models

In a spatially flat (
k=0
) universe, the constituents of cosmic energy
density are non-relativistic matter (baryonic and dark matter), neutrinos,
CMB photons, and dark energy. In this work, contribution of neutrinos to the
cosmic energy density is neglected. With the equation of state
P=\omega\rho
, the Friedmann-Raychaudhury equations are written as

\displaystyle H^{2}(a)\;\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi G}{3}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(a)

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\overset{..}{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\;\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(a)\left[1+3w_{i}(a)\right]

(2)

The equation of state parameters
\omega_{M}=0
and
\omega_{R}=1/3
for
matter and radiation, respectively. In Section 3, the dark energy parameter
\omega_{X}
of different parametric models is discussed.

The energy densities
\rho_{i}(a)
and density parameters
\Omega_{i}(a)

are related by

\displaystyle\rho_{i}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{0i}f_{i}(a)

(3)

\displaystyle\Omega_{i}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{0i}f_{i}(a)}{E^{2}}

(4)

where the dark energy function
f_{i}(a)=

\rho_{i}(a)/\rho_{0i}
and
E(a)
are defined by

\displaystyle f_{i}(a)\;\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\;\exp\left[-3\int_{1}^{a}{\frac{1+w_{i}(a^{\prime})}{a^{\prime}}da^{\prime}}\right]

(5)

\displaystyle E^{2}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H^{2}(a)}{H_{0}^{2}}=\sum_{i}\Omega_{i0}f_{i}(a)

(6)

Using Eq. (
6
), the second Friedmann-Raychaudhury equation can be
written as

\frac{2q-1}{3}=\frac{\Omega_{R}}{3}+w_{X}\Omega_{x}

(7)

The age of the universe is calculated by using

\displaystyle t_{0}\;\;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\;\int_{0}^{1}\frac{da}{aH(a)}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{0}^{-1}\;\int_{0}^{1}\frac{da}{a\left[\Omega_{0M}a^{-3}+\Omega_{0R}a^{-4}+\Omega_{0X}f_{x}(a)\right]^{1/2}}

According to the GSL of thermodynamics, two requirements of thermodynamic
viability of dark energy models are given by
S^{\prime}(a)\geqslant 0
and
in the far future
S^{\prime\prime}(a)\leq 0
. The total entropy of the
universe

is due to the entropy of horizon

as well as

,
S_{M}(a)
,
S_{R}(a)
and

are written as follows

.

\displaystyle S_{H}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{4}\frac{A_{H}}{l_{p}^{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi kc^{2}}{4H^{2}}

\displaystyle S_{M}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle knV_{H}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi kc^{3}n_{0}}{3a^{3}H^{3}}

Entropy of radiation and dark energy are calculated using

TdS_{i}=d(\rho_{i}V_{H})+\rho_{i}\omega_{i}d(V_{H})

(11)

In dimensionless form

\widetilde{S}_{\alpha}(a)=\left(\frac{4G\hbar H_{0}^{2}}{3kc^{5}}\right)S_{\alpha}

where

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{H}^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{aE^{2}(a)}\left[1+\sum_{i}w_{i}(a)\Omega_{i}(a)\right]

(14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{M}^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{H_{0}\hbar}{kT_{0M}}\right)\frac{1}{a^{4}E^{3}(a)}\left[1+3\sum_{i}w_{i}(a)\Omega_{i}(a)\right]

(15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{i}^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{H_{0}\hbar}{kT_{i}}\right)\frac{\Omega_{i}(a)\left(1+w_{i}(a)\right)}{aE(a)}\left[1+3\sum_{j}w_{j}(a)\Omega_{j}(a)\right]

Here, “
i

defined as

\displaystyle T_{0M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3c^{2}H_{0}^{2}}{8\pi Gkn_{0}}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle T_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{0i}a^{3}f_{i}(z)

(18)

where

m
-3

T_{0R}=2.725
K and

total entropy

can be calculated from

V
Conclusions

A comparative study of a set of parametric dark energy models has been
performed by calculating the dynamical evolution of dark energy parameter
\Omega_{X}
in the past and future epochs. In comparison to
\Lambda
CDM
model, the variation in
\Omega_{X}
due to different parametrization is
about 5% in the expansion parameter range
a=10^{-14}-1000
. The maximum
variation in the age of the universe
t_{0}H
is about 7%. However,
t_{1000}H
(time till
a=1000
) varies by a factor of 30.

Thermodynamic viability of dark energy models has been investigated by
calculating the two requirements of GSL of thermodynamics i.e.
S^{\prime}(a)
and
S^{\prime\prime}(a)
. It has been observed that with reasonable
assumptions, GSL of thermodynamics is satisfied by
\Lambda
CDM,
quintessence, BA, PSY1 and PSY2 parametrizations. Parametrizations having
phantom behavior are not suitable from thermodynamic perspective.
Thermodynamic viability of parametric models cannot be conclusively
ascertained to the unknown value of
\tau_{X}
.

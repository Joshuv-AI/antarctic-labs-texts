On the Automorphism Group of a Binary Form Associated with Algebraic Trigonometric Quantities
Anton Mosunov

Abstract
Let F ⁡ ( x , y ) F(x,y) be a binary form of degree at least three and non-zero discriminant. In this article we compute the automorphism group Aut ⁡ F \Aut F for four families of binary forms. The first two families that we are interested in are homogenizations of minimal polynomials of 2 ​ cos ⁡ ( 2 ​ π n ) 2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right) and 2 ​ sin ⁡ ( 2 ​ π n ) 2\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right) , which we denote by Ψ n ​ ( x , y ) \Psi_{n}(x,y) and Π n ​ ( x , y ) \Pi_{n}(x,y) , respectively. The remaining two forms that we consider are homogenizations of Chebyshev polynomials of first and second kinds, denoted T n ​ ( x , y ) T_{n}(x,y) and U n ​ ( x , y ) U_{n}(x,y) , respectively.

Introduction

Let
F(x,y)
denote a binary form with complex coefficients of degree
d\geq 1
. Then for a matrix
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}s&u\\
t&v\end{smallmatrix}\right)

F_{M}(x,y)
as follows:

F_{M}(x,y)=F(sx+uy,tx+vy).

If
K

\mathbb{C}

M

K
-
automorphism
of
F

F_{M}=F
and
M
has entries in
K
. The set of all
K
-automorphisms of
F
forms a group and is denoted by
\Aut_{K}F
. We put
\Aut F=\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}}F
.

In what follows, we restrict our attention to binary forms
F
with integer coefficients, degree
d\geq 3
and non-zero discriminant
D_{F}
. In this case
\Aut F
is a finite subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})

[
18
]
. It is a fact that every finite subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
is
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
-conjugate to one of the groups listed in Table
1

[
12
]
.

The automorphism group
\Aut F
arises in the analysis of
Thue equations
.
A Thue
equation is a Diophantine equation of the form

F(x,y)=h,

(1)

where
h
is a fixed integer. In 1909, Thue
[
19
]
proved that this equation has only finitely many solutions in integers
x
and
y
. A solution
(x,y)
to such equation is called
primitive
if
x
and
y
are coprime. By fixing a finite subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, Stewart
[
17

constructed binary forms
F
such that
\Aut F=G
. If we now let
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}s&u\\
t&v\end{smallmatrix}\right)
to be an element of
\Aut F
and assume that
(x,y)
is a primitive solution to (
1
), then
(sx+uy,tx+vy)
is also a primitive solution. Therefore, for infinitely many integers
h

|\Aut F|
primitive solutions.

In this article we compute
\Aut F
and
W_{F}
, and estimate
C_{F}
for four families of binary forms. Let
\Psi_{n}(x)
and
\Pi_{n}(x)
denote the minimal polynomials of
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
and
2\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)

\Psi_{n}(x,y)
and
\Pi_{n}(x,y)

\Psi_{n}(x)
and
\Pi_{n}(x)

\Psi_{n}(x,y)=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}\\
\gcd(k,n)=1\end{subarray}}\left(x-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right)y\right).

(2)

Further, since
\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)=\cos\left(\frac{2\pi(n-4)}{4n}\right)
, we see that
\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
is an algebraic conjugate of
\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{c(n)}\right)

c(n)
is the denominator of
\frac{n-4}{4n}
(in lowest terms). Consequently,

\Pi_{n}(x,y)=\Psi_{c(n)}(x,y).

(3)

The formula for
c(n)
can be found in
[
11
, Corollary 1.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. The Galois group of the field
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\right)
is Abelian and it consists of field automorphisms
\sigma_{k}
defined by
\sigma_{k}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\right)=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right)
, where
k
is an integer coprime to
n
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{n}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}
. By
[
3
, Theorem 14.5.26]
, the Galois group of the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{n}\right)
is Abelian, because it is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, the multiplicative group of invertible elements in
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Since
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)=\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1}
, we see that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})
is a subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
, so the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})
is also Abelian. Furthermore, the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
consists of field automorphisms
\tau_{k}
defined by
\tau_{k}(\zeta_{n})=\zeta_{n}^{k}
, where
k
is an integer coprime to
n
. Restricting the field automorphism
\tau_{k}
to the field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})
gives us the field automorphism
\sigma_{k}
defined by
\sigma_{k}(\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1})=\zeta_{n}^{k}+\zeta_{n}^{-k}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and let
d=\varphi(n)/2
, so that
\deg\Psi_{n}=d
.

1.

If
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, then
\Psi_{n}(x)=g(x^{2})
, where
g(x)
is the minimal polynomial of
2+2\cos\left(\frac{4\pi}{n}\right)
.

2.

If
n
is odd, then
-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
is a conjugate of
2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)
. Consequently,

\Psi_{n}(x)=(-1)^{d}\Psi_{2n}(-x).

Proof.

1.

Suppose that
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
. Recall that
2\cos^{2}(x)=1+\cos(2x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,

4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)=2\left(1+\cos\left(\frac{4\pi}{n}\right)\right)=2+2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n/2}\right).

Let
g(x)
denote the minimal polynomial of
2+2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{(n/2)}\right)
. Note that
\deg g(x)=\varphi(n/2)/2
and

g\left(4\cos^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\right)=0.

Since for any positive integer
n
divisible by
4
it is the case that
\varphi(n)/2=\varphi(n/2)
, we have

\deg\Psi_{n}(x)=\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}=2\cdot\frac{\varphi(n/2)}{2}=2\deg g(x)=\deg g(x^{2}).

Since the polynomials
g(x^{2})
and
\Psi_{n}(x)
have equal degrees, both vanish at
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
, and the leading coefficient of
g(x^{2})
is positive, we conclude that
\Psi_{n}(x)=g(x^{2})
.

2.

Suppose that
n
is odd. Note that

-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)=2\cos\left(\pi+\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi(n+2)}{2n}\right).

Since
\gcd(2n,n+2)=1
, we see that
-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
is a conjugate of
2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)
. Thus
\Psi_{2n}\left(-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)\right)=0
.
But then
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
is a root of
(-1)^{d}\Psi_{2n}(-x)
, and since the leading coefficient of this polynomial is positive, it must be equal to the minimal polynomial of
2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be a binary form. Let
r
be a non-zero rational number and let
S\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. Then

\Aut rF_{S}=S^{-1}(\Aut F)S\quad\text{and}\quad\Aut|rF_{S}|=S^{-1}\left(\Aut|F|\right)S.

Proof.

We see that
F_{M}=F
if and only if

F_{S}=(F_{M})_{S}=F_{MS}=\left((F_{S})_{S^{-1}}\right)_{MS}=(F_{S})_{S^{-1}MS}.

We conclude that
M\in\Aut F
if and only if
S^{-1}MS\in\Aut F_{S}
. This means that
\Aut F_{S}=S^{-1}\left(\Aut F\right)S
. Since
\Aut rF_{S}=\Aut F_{S}
, the result follows. The equality
\Aut|rF_{S}|=S^{-1}\left(\Aut|F|\right)S
can be proved analogously.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
n
be an odd positive integer such that
\varphi(n)\geq 10
. Let
j
be an integer coprime to
n
. If

2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n}\right)=\frac{2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)v-u}{-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)t+s}

(8)

for some rationals
s
,
t
,
u
and
v
, then
s\neq 0
,
s=v
and
t=u=0
.

Proof.

For an integer
i
, let
\alpha_{i}=2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\right)
. Put
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
. Since
n
is odd, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that there exists a field automorphism
\sigma_{2}
in the Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
such that
\sigma_{2}(\alpha_{\ell})=\alpha_{2\ell}
for each
\ell
coprime to
n
. Therefore,

\alpha_{2j}=\sigma_{2}(\alpha_{j})=\sigma_{2}\left(\frac{v\alpha-u}{-t\alpha+s}\right)=\frac{v\sigma_{2}(\alpha)-u}{-t\sigma_{2}(\alpha)+s}=\frac{v\alpha_{2}-u}{-t\alpha_{2}+s}.

Since for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
it is the case that
2\cos(2x)=(2\cos(x))^{2}-2
, we conclude that
\alpha_{2i}=\alpha_{i}^{2}-2
for all
i
. Therefore,

\left(\frac{v\alpha-u}{-t\alpha+s}\right)^{2}-2=\alpha_{j}^{2}-2=\alpha_{2j}=\frac{v\alpha_{2}-u}{-t\alpha_{2}+s}=\frac{v(\alpha^{2}-2)-u}{-t(\alpha^{2}-2)+s}.

From the above equality we obtain

\left(-t(\alpha^{2}-2)+s\right)\left((v\alpha-u)^{2}-2(-t\alpha+s)^{2}\right)=(-t\alpha+s)^{2}\left(v(\alpha^{2}-2)-u\right).

We conclude that the polynomial

\displaystyle(2t^{3}-t^{2}v-tv^{2})x^{4}

\displaystyle+(-4st^{2}+2stv+2tuv)x^{3}

\displaystyle+(2s^{2}t-s^{2}v-2st^{2}+sv^{2}-4t^{3}+t^{2}u+2t^{2}v-tu^{2}+2tv^{2})x^{2}

\displaystyle+(4s^{2}t+8st^{2}-2stu-4stv-2suv-4tuv)x

\displaystyle+(-2s^{3}-4s^{2}t+s^{2}u+2s^{2}v+su^{2}+2tu^{2})

vanishes at
\alpha
. Since the degree of
\alpha
is
\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}\geq 5
and the above polynomial has degree at most

, it must be the case that this polynomial is identically equal to zero. That is,

\begin{array}[]{r l}t(t-v)(2t+v)&=0,\\

2s^{2}t+4st^{2}-stu-2stv-suv-2tuv&=0,\\
-2s^{3}-4s^{2}t+s^{2}u+2s^{2}v+su^{2}+2tu^{2}&=0.\end{array}

(9)

Depending on the value of
t
, we consider the following three cases.

1.

Suppose that
t=0
. Then the first two equations in (
9
) vanish, while the third and the fourth equations simplify to
sv(v-s)=0
and
suv=0
, respectively. Note that
s\neq 0
, for otherwise the denominator of (
8
) vanishes. Thus the last two equations further reduce to
v(v-s)=0
and

. If

, then the number
\alpha_{j}=-u/s
is rational, in contradiction to the fact that

. Thus it must be the case that

,

and

.

2.

Suppose that
t=v
and
t\neq 0
. Then the second equation in (
9
) simplifies to
v(u-s)=0
. But then

, and

\alpha_{j}=\frac{v\alpha-u}{-t\alpha+s}=\frac{v\alpha-u}{-v\alpha+u}=-1,

in contradiction to the fact that

.

3.

Suppose that

and

. Then the second equation simplifies to

. But then

,

\alpha_{j}=\frac{v\alpha-u}{-t\alpha+s}=\frac{-2t\alpha+2s}{-t\alpha+s}=2,

in contradiction to the fact that

.

Lemma 3.6

. For a positive integer

is equal to the smallest positive integer

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be positive integers such that
k<\ell
and
k,\ell\notin\{1,2,3,4,6\}
. Then
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)=\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)
if and only if
k
is odd and
\ell=2k
.

Proof.

Suppose that
k
is odd and
\ell=2k
. Then the degrees of
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)
and
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)
are both equal to
\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}
. Further,

2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)=\left(2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{k}\right)\right)^{2}-2=\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)^{2}-2,

which means that
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)
. Since
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)
is a subfield of
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)
of the same degree, it must be the case that the two fields are identical.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)=\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)
. Then degrees of these number fields are equal, i.e.,
\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}=\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{2}
. We claim that there exists a prime that divides
k
but not
\ell
, or vice versa. For suppose that this is not the case and

k=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}},\,\,\,\ell=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}}

for some positive integers
t,e_{1},\ldots,e_{t},f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}
and distinct primes
p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}
. Then

\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}-1}(p_{i}-1)=\varphi(k)=\varphi(\ell)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}-1}(p_{i}-1).

After dividing both sides by
\prod_{i=1}^{t}(p_{i}-1)
, we obtain

\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}-1},

which means that
e_{i}=f_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,t
. But then
k=\ell
, in contradiction to our assumption that
k
and
\ell
are distinct. This completes the proof of our claim.

Now, it follows from the result of Lehmer
[
9
, Theorem 3.8]
that the discriminant
D_{k}
of the field
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{k}\right)\right)
can be computed as follows:

D_{k}=\begin{cases}2^{(j-1)2^{j-2}-1}&\textrm{if $k=2^{j}$, $j>2$,}\\
p^{(jp^{j}-(j+1)p^{j-1}-1)/2}&\textrm{if $k=p^{j}$ or $2p^{j}$, $p>2$ prime,}\\
\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\omega(k)}p_{i}^{e_{i}-1/(p_{i}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}}&\textrm{if $\omega(k)>1,k\neq 2p^{j}$.}\end{cases}

(10)

An analogous formula applies to the discriminant
D_{\ell}
of
\mathbb{Q}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\right)\right)
, and of course we must have
D_{\ell}=D_{k}
. Suppose that
k=2^{j}
,
j>2
. Then
D_{k}
is a power of
2
. Since there is a prime that divides
k
but not
\ell
or vice versa, it must be the case that an odd prime
q
divides
\ell
. But then it follows from (
10
) that
q\mid D_{\ell}
, so
D_{k}\neq D_{\ell}
. Thus, this case is impossible, and so at least one odd prime divides
k
, i.e.,

k=2^{r}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}}

for some non-negative integer
r
, positive integers
t,e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
, and distinct odd primes
p_{1},\ldots,p_{t}
.

Notice how in (
10
), for every odd prime
p
,
p\mid k
if and only if
p\mid D_{k}
. Similarly, for every odd prime
q
,
q\mid\ell
if and only if
q\mid D_{\ell}
. Since
D_{k}=D_{\ell}
, we conclude that, for every odd prime
p
,
p\mid k
if and only if
p\mid\ell
. Thus,

\ell=2^{s}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}}

for some non-negative integer
s
and positive integers
f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}
. Further, since there exists a prime that divides
k
but not
\ell
or vice versa, it must be the case that either
k
or
\ell
is odd. At this point, we consider four cases.

1.

If
k=p^{j}
for some odd prime
p
, then it follows from (
10
) that
D_{k}
is odd. Further,
\ell=2^{s}p^{m}
for some positive integers
s
and
m
(recall that there must be a prime that divides
\ell
, but not
k
). Further, it must be the case that
s=1
, for otherwise it follows from (
10
) that
D_{\ell}
is even. Thus,
\ell=2p^{m}
, and so

p^{(jp^{j}-(j+1)p^{j-1}-1)/2}=D_{k}=D_{\ell}=p^{(mp^{m}-(m+1)p^{m-1}-1)/2}.

Since the function
f_{p}(x)=xp^{x}-(x+1)p^{x-1}-1
is monotonously increasing on the interval
[1,+\infty)
, we conclude that
m=j
, and so
\ell=2p^{j}=2k
.

2.

If
k=2p^{j}
for some odd prime
p
, then it follows from (
10
) that
D_{k}
is odd. Further,
\ell=2^{s}p^{m}
for some non-negative integer
s
and positive integer
m
. Since there must exist a prime that divides
k
but not
\ell
, we conclude that
\ell=2^{s}\geq 8
or
\ell=p^{m}
. The former is impossible, since
D_{\ell}
has to be odd. Thus,
\ell=p^{m}
, and so

p^{(jp^{j}-(j+1)p^{j-1}-1)/2}=D_{k}=D_{\ell}=p^{(mp^{m}-(m+1)p^{m-1}-1)/2}.

Since the function
f_{p}(x)=xp^{x}-(x+1)p^{x-1}-1
is monotonously increasing on the interval
[1,+\infty)
, we conclude that
m=j
. But then
\ell=p^{j}<2p^{j}=k
, which is impossible, since we assumed that
k<\ell
.

3.

If
k
is odd and it is not an odd prime power, then
t\geq 2
and

D_{k}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}-1/(p_{i}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}}.

Since
D_{k}
is odd,
D_{\ell}
is odd, which in turn implies that
\ell=2^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}}
for
s\in\{0,1\}
. Thus,

D_{\ell}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}-1/(p_{i}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{2}}.

Since
D_{k}=D_{\ell}
, the unique factorization tells us that
\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}\left(e_{i}-\frac{1}{p_{i}-1}\right)=\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{2}\left(f_{i}-\frac{1}{p_{i}-1}\right)
for every
i=1,\ldots,t
. Since
\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}=\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{2}
, we conclude that
e_{i}=f_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,t
. Since
k\neq\ell
, we conclude that
s=1
, and so
\ell=2k
.

4.

If
k=2^{r}\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
is even and it is not twice an odd prime power, then
\ell
must be odd. Consequently,
D_{\ell}
is odd. But then
D_{k}=D_{\ell}
is odd, which is only possible when
r=1
. Thus,

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}-1/(p_{i}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\varphi(k)}{2}}=D_{k}=D_{\ell}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{f_{i}-1/(p_{i}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{2}}.

Once again, we find that
e_{i}=f_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,t
, meaning that
k=2\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
and
\ell=\prod_{i=1}^{t}p_{i}^{e_{i}}=\frac{k}{2}<k
, which contradicts
k<\ell
.

∎

\tilde{U}_{15}(x,1)
are given by
\pm 1
, while the roots of
degree
2
are given by
\frac{\pm 1\pm\sqrt{5}}{2}
. Thus there are eight possible subcases to consider.

(i)

1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad-1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case, we obtain a homogeneous system of
3
linear equations in
4
unknowns:

\begin{array}[]{r l}s-t+u-v&=0\\
-s-t+u+v&=0\\
\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}s+\frac{-3-\sqrt{5}}{2}t+u+\frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}v&=0\end{array}

Since
s
,
t
,
u
and
v
are integers, this system is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{r l}s-t+u-v&=0\\
-s-t+u+v&=0\\
\frac{1}{2}s-\frac{3}{2}t+u-\frac{1}{2}v&=0\\
\frac{1}{2}s-\frac{1}{2}t-\frac{1}{2}v&=0\end{array}

Solving this system yields
s=v
and
u=v=0
. Since
\gcd(s,v)=1
, we conclude that
s=v=\pm 1
.

(ii)

-1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad 1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=t=u=v=0
, which is impossible.

(iii)

1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad-1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=t=u=v=0
, which is impossible.

(iv)

-1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad 1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=v=0
and
t=-u
. Since
\gcd(t,u)=1
, we conclude that
t=\pm 1
and
u=\mp 1
.

(v)

1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad-1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=v=0
and
t=u
. Since
\gcd(t,u)=1
, we conclude that
t=u=\pm 1
.

(vi)

-1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad 1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=t=u=v=0
, which is impossible.

(vii)

1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad-1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=t=u=v=0
, which is impossible.

(viii)

-1=\frac{v1-u}{-t1+s},\quad 1=\frac{v(-1)-u}{-t(-1)+s},\quad\frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=\frac{v\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)-u}{-t\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)+s}
.

In this case,
s=-v
and
t=u=0
. Since
\gcd(s,v)=1
, we conclude that
s=\pm 1
and
v=\mp 1
.

We conclude that
A\in\left\{\pm I,\pm M,\pm N,\pm MN\right\}
, where
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. By checking each possible value of
A
we find that

\Aut\tilde{U}_{15}=\Aut|\tilde{U}_{15}|=\{\pm I,\pm M\}.

∎

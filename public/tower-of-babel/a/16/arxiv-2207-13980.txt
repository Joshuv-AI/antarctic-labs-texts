L ∞ L_{\infty} -structures and cohomology theory of compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators and compatible dendriform algebras
Apurba Das, Shuangjian Guo, Yufei Qin

Abstract
. The notion of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator is a generalization of Rota-Baxter operator in the presence of a bimodule over an associative algebra. A compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator is a pair consisting of two 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators satisfying a compatibility relation. A compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator algebra is an algebra together with a bimodule and a compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator. In this paper, we construct a graded Lie algebra and an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra that respectively characterize compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators and compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator algebras as Maurer-Cartan elements. Using these characterizations, we define cohomology of these structures and as applications we study formal deformations of compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators and compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator algebras. Finally, we consider a brief cohomological study of compatible dendriform algebras and find their relation with the cohomology of compatible associative algebras and compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators.

1.
Introduction

Rota-Baxter operators are algebraic abstraction of the integral operator. They first appeared in a work of Baxter in the fluctuation theory of probability
[
6
]
. Subsequently, such operators were studied by Rota
[
27
]
, Cartier
[
8
]
, Atkinson
[
3
]
among others. Rota-Baxter operators appeared in many contexts of mathematics and mathematical physics. For instance, they appeared in the combinatorial study of shuffle algebras
[
18
]
, splitting of algebras and operads
[
4
]
, theory of bialgebras
[
2
]
and renormalizations in quantum field theory
[
10
]
. In
[
31
]
Uchino introduced a notion of generalized Rota-Baxter operator, also known as
\mathcal{O}
-operator or relative Rota-Baxter operator (mostly known as
\mathcal{O}
-operator in the literature
[
5
,
11
]
). Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. We write
~\cdot~
to denote the associative multiplication on
A
as well as both the left and right
A
-actions on
M
. A linear map
T:M\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
if the map
T
satisfies

\displaystyle T(u)\cdot T(v)=T(T(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T(v)),~\text{for }u,v\in M.

A Rota-Baxter operator on
A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A
(consider as the adjoint bimodule space) over the algebra
A
itself. Uchino observed that
\mathcal{O}
-operators can be seen as a noncommutative analogue of Poisson structures. More precisely, a skew-symmetric element
r\in A\otimes A
is an associative Yang-Baxter solution (noncommutative analogue of Poisson tensor) if and only if the map
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A^{*}
(consider as the coadjoint bimodule space) over the algebra
A
. He also observed that an
\mathcal{O}
-operator induces a dendriform algebra structure. Rota-Baxter operators and
\mathcal{O}
-operators are also well studied in the context of Lie algebras
[
28
,
21
]
. Recently, cohomology and deformation theory of Rota-Baxter operators and
\mathcal{O}
-operators are settled in
[
30
,
11
]
. See
[
19
]
for more results on
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

In the mathematical study of biHamiltonian mechanics, two Poisson structures on a manifold are said to be compatible if their sum also defines a Poisson structure
[
20
]
. Compatible Poisson structures are closely related to compatible Lie algebras via dualization
[
7
,
26
]
. Our aim in this paper is to study the noncommutative analogue of compatible Poisson structures. They are given by compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators introduced in
[
24
]
. A compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator is a pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
in which
T_{1},T_{2}
are both
\mathcal{O}
-operators satisfying an additional compatibility which is equivalent to the fact that
T_{1}+T_{2}
is also an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. We call the triple
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
consisting of an algebra
A
, an
A
-bimodule
M
and a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
as a ‘compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra’. We introduce (skew-symmetric) compatible associative Yang-Baxter solutions and show that they induce compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

Next, we focus on the cohomology of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators and compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras. Given an associative algebra
A
and an
A
-bimodule
M
, we first construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. Using this characterization, we define the cohomology of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
. On the other hand, we observed that a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator induces a compatible associative algebra and a compatible bimodule over it. The cohomology of the induced compatible associative algebra is isomorphic to the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
. We show that there is a morphism from the cohomology of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
to the cohomology of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T_{1}+T_{2}
. As an application of our cohomology, we study formal deformations of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
by keeping the underlying algebra
A
and the
A
-bimodule
M
intact.
We also consider finite order deformations of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Given a finite order deformation, we associate a
2
-cocycle, whose cohomology class is called the ‘obstruction class’. The given deformation is extensible to a deformation of next order if and only if the corresponding obstruction class vanishes. Next, following the approach of
[
13
]
in the context of compatible structures, we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras. Hence we may define the cohomology of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
. Using such cohomology, we study formal deformations of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
, where we allow to deform the underlying algebra
A
, the
A
-bimodule
M
and the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

In the last part, we consider compatible dendriform algebras introduced in
[
24
]
. Like
\mathcal{O}
-operators are related with dendriform algebras, compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators are related with compatible dendriform algebras.
By following the cohomology of dendriform algebras given in

23

, here we define cohomology of compatible dendriform algebras. Our cohomology of compatible dendriform algebras can be seen as a splitting of the cohomology of compatible associative algebras recently introduced in
[
9

2.
Compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators and compatible associative Yang-

Baxter solutions

In this section, we first consider compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators introduced in
[
24
]
and then define compatible associative Yang-Baxter solutions. We show that (skew-symmetric) compatible associative Yang-Baxter solutions give rise to compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

2.1 Definition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule.
A compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
consists of a pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of
\mathcal{O}
-operators satisfying the compatibility

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)\cdot T_{2}(v)+T_{2}(u)\cdot T_{1}(v)=T_{1}(T_{2}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2}(v))+T_{2}(T_{1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1}(v)),\text{ for }u,v\in M.

(1)

A triple
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
consisting of an algebra
A
, an
A
-bimodule
M
and a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
on
M
over the algebra
A
is called a ‘compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra’. As
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras are also called relative Rota-Baxter algebras, hence ‘compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras’ may be called ‘compatible relative Rota-Baxter algebras’.

It follows from the above compatibility that
\lambda T_{1}+\eta T_{2}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, for any
\lambda,\eta\in{\bf k}
. In particular, the sum
T_{1}+T_{2}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, called the ‘total
\mathcal{O}
-operator’.

2.2 Example
.

Let
T:M\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Then
\widetilde{T}:=-T
is also an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and the pair
(T,\widetilde{T})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

2.3 Example
.

Let
A=C^{1}([0,1])
and
M=C^{0}([0,1])
. Then
A
is an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Let
T_{1},T_{2}:M\rightarrow A
be defined by

\displaystyle T_{1}(f)(x)=\int^{x}_{0}k_{1}(t)f(t)dt~~\text{ and }~~T_{2}(f)(x)=\int^{x}_{0}k_{2}(t)f(t)dt,

for some functions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
in
C^{0}([0,1])
.
Then the pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

2.4 Example
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}:A\rightarrow M
be two derivations that are invertible and satisfying the compatibility

\displaystyle d_{1}^{-1}d_{2}(a\cdot b)+d_{2}^{-1}d_{1}(a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime})=a\cdot b^{\prime}+a^{\prime}\cdot b,\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Then it is easy to check that
(T_{1}=d_{1}^{-1},T_{2}=d_{2}^{-1})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

2.5 Definition
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
be two compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators on
M
over the algebra
A
. A morphism of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators from
(T_{1},T_{2})
to
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
is given by a pair
(\phi,\psi)
of an algebra morphism
\phi:A\rightarrow A
and a linear map
\psi:M\rightarrow M
satisfying

\displaystyle\phi\circ T_{1}=T^{\prime}_{1}\circ\psi,~~\phi\circ T_{2}=T^{\prime}_{2}\circ\psi,~~\psi(a\cdot u)=\phi(a)\cdot\psi(u)~~\text{ and }~~\psi(u\cdot a)=\psi(u)\cdot\phi(a),\text{ for }a\in A,u\in M.

It is well-known
[
31
]
that an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T:M\rightarrow A
induces an associative algebra structure on
M
given by

\displaystyle u\star v=T(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T(v),\text{ for }u,v\in M.

Moreover, there is an
M
-bimodule structure on
A
with left and right
M
-actions

\displaystyle l_{T}:M\otimes A\rightarrow A,~l_{T}(u,a)=T(u)\cdot a-T(u\cdot a)~~\text{ and }~~r_{T}:A\otimes M\rightarrow A,~r_{T}(a,u)=a\cdot T(u)-T(a\cdot u).

In the following, we will generalize these results in the context of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. First, recall that a compatible associative algebra is a triple
(A,\cdot_{1},\cdot_{2})
in which
(A,\cdot_{1})
and
(A,\cdot_{2})
are both associative algebras satisfying additionally

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{1}b)\cdot_{2}c+(a\cdot_{2}b)\cdot_{1}c=a\cdot_{2}(b\cdot_{1}c)+a\cdot_{1}(b\cdot_{2}c),\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

(2)

Like an associative algebra gives rise to a Lie algebra via skew-symmetrization, a compatible associative algebra gives rise to a compatible Lie algebra via skew-symmetrization of both associative products. See
[
9
]
for further relation between compatible associative and Lie algebras.

Let
(A,\cdot_{1},\cdot_{2})
be a compatible associative algebra. A compatible bimodule over it consists of a quintuple
(M,l_{1},r_{1},l_{2},r_{2})
of a vector space
M
with four bilinear operations

\displaystyle l_{1}:A\otimes M

\displaystyle\rightarrow M,\quad\quad r_{1}:M\otimes A\rightarrow A,\quad\quad l_{2}:A\otimes M\rightarrow M,\quad\quad r_{2}:M\otimes A\rightarrow A

\displaystyle(a,u)

\displaystyle\mapsto a\cdot_{1}u~~~~\quad\qquad\qquad~(u,a)\mapsto u\cdot_{1}a~~~\quad\qquad(a,u)\mapsto a\cdot_{2}u\qquad\qquad(u,a)\mapsto u\cdot_{2}a

so that
(M,l_{1},r_{1})
is a bimodule over the associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{1})
, and
(M,l_{2},r_{2})
is a bimodule over the associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{2})
satisfying additionally for any
a,b\in A
and
u\in M
,

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{1}b)\cdot_{2}u+(a\cdot_{2}b)\cdot_{1}u=~

\displaystyle a\cdot_{1}(b\cdot_{2}u)+a\cdot_{2}(b\cdot_{1}u),

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{1}u)\cdot_{2}b+(a\cdot_{2}u)\cdot_{1}b=~

\displaystyle a\cdot_{1}(u\cdot_{2}b)+a\cdot_{2}(u\cdot_{1}b),

\displaystyle(u\cdot_{1}a)\cdot_{2}b+(u\cdot_{2}a)\cdot_{1}b=~

\displaystyle u\cdot_{1}(a\cdot_{2}b)+u\cdot_{2}(a\cdot_{1}b).

These three compatibility conditions is equivalent to the fact that
(M,l_{1}+l_{2},r_{1}+r_{2})
is a bimodule over the associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{1}+\cdot_{2})
.
Note that any compatible associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{1},\cdot_{2})
is a bimodule over itself with
l_{1}=r_{1}=\cdot_{1}
and
l_{2}=r_{2}=\cdot_{2}
. This is called adjoint compatible bimodule.

2.6 Theorem
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then
(M,\star_{1},\star_{2})
is a compatible associative algebra, where

\displaystyle u\star_{1}v=T_{1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1}(v)~~~\text{ and }~~~u\star_{2}v=T_{2}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2}(v),\text{ for }u,v\in M.

Moreover,
(A,l_{T_{1}},r_{T_{1}},l_{T_{2}},r_{T_{2}})
is a compatible bimodule over the compatible associative algebra
(M,\star_{1},\star_{2})
.

Proof.

For any
u,v\in M
, we observe that

\displaystyle u\star_{1}v+u\star_{2}v=(T_{1}+T_{2})(u)\cdot v+u\cdot(T_{1}+T_{2})(v).

(3)

Since
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator, it follows that
T_{1}+T_{2}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Hence from (
3
), we conclude that
(M,\star_{1}+\star_{2})
is an associative algebra. In other words,
(M,\star_{1},\star_{2})
is a compatible associative algebra.

Similarly, for any
u\in M
and
a\in A
, we see that

\displaystyle(l_{T_{1}}+l_{T_{2}})(u,a)=l_{T_{1}+T_{2}}(u,a)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~(r_{T_{1}}+r_{T_{2}})(a,u)=r_{T_{1}+T_{2}}(a,u).

This shows that
(A,l_{T_{1}},r_{T_{1}},l_{T_{2}},r_{T_{2}})
is a bimodule over the compatible associative algebra
(M,\star_{1},\star_{2})
.
∎

Compatible associative Yang-Baxter solutions.
Let
A
be an associative algebra. For any element
r=\sum r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}\in A\otimes A
, Aguiar
[
1
]
considers the associative Yang-Baxter equation on
A
as

\displaystyle r^{13}r^{12}-r^{12}r^{23}+r^{23}r^{13}=0,

(4)

or, more explicitly,

\displaystyle\sum r^{[1]}\cdot\widetilde{r}^{[1]}\otimes\widetilde{r}^{[2]}\otimes r^{[2]}-\sum r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}\cdot\widetilde{r}^{[1]}\otimes\widetilde{r}^{[2]}+\sum\widetilde{r}^{[1]}\otimes r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}\cdot\widetilde{r}^{[2]}=0.

A solution of (
4
) is called an associative Yang-Baxter solution on
A
. If
r=\sum r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}\in A\otimes A
is an associative Yang-Baxter solution, then the map
T:A\rightarrow A
defined by
T(a)=\sum r^{[1]}\cdot a\cdot r^{[2]}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
A
(i.e. an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the adjoint bimodule space
A
over the algebra
A
)
[
1
,
2
]
. Later, Uchino
[
31
]
observed that skew-symmetric associative Yang-Baxter solutions give rise to
\mathcal{O}
-operators on the coadjoint bimodule space
A^{*}
over the algebra
A
. We will generalize these results in the context of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

2.7 Definition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. A compatible associative Yang-Baxter solution on
A
is a pair
(r_{1},r_{2})
, where
r_{1}=\sum r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r_{1}^{[2]}
and
r_{2}=\sum r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r_{2}^{[2]}
are both associative Yang-Baxter solutions on
A
satisfying

\displaystyle r_{1}^{13}r_{2}^{12}+r_{2}^{13}r_{1}^{12}-r_{1}^{12}r_{2}^{23}-r_{2}^{12}r_{1}^{23}+r_{1}^{23}r_{2}^{13}+r_{2}^{23}r_{1}^{13}=0,

(5)

equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum r_{1}^{[1]}\cdot r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r_{2}^{[2]}\otimes r_{1}^{[2]}+~

\displaystyle\sum r_{2}^{[1]}\cdot r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r_{1}^{[2]}\otimes r_{2}^{[2]}-\sum r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r_{1}^{[2]}\cdot r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}_{2}-\sum r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r_{2}^{[2]}\cdot r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}_{1}

\displaystyle+~

\displaystyle\sum r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r_{1}^{[2]}\cdot r_{2}^{[2]}+\sum r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r_{2}^{[2]}\cdot r_{1}^{[2]}=0.

(6)

2.8 Example
.

If
r=\sum r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}\in A\otimes A
is an associative Yang-Baxter solution, then the pair
(r,-r)
is a compatible associative Yang-Baxter solution on
A
.

2.9 Proposition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
(r_{1},r_{2})
be a compatible associative Yang-Baxter solution on
A
. Then the pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of linear maps
T_{1},T_{2}:A\rightarrow A
forms a compatible Rota-Baxter operator on
A
(i.e. compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the adjoint bimodule space
A
over the algebra
A
), where
T_{1}(a)=\sum r_{1}^{[1]}\cdot a\cdot r_{1}^{[2]}
and
T_{2}(a)=\sum r_{2}^{[1]}\cdot a\cdot r_{2}^{[2]}
, for
a\in A
.

Proof.

Note that, by replacing the first tensor product by
a
and the second tensor product by
b
in the identity (
2.7
), we get that

\displaystyle T_{1}(T_{2}(a)\cdot b)+T_{2}(T_{1}(a)\cdot b)-T_{1}(a)\cdot T_{2}(b)-T_{2}(a)\cdot T_{1}(b)+T_{2}(a\cdot T_{1}(b))+T_{1}(a\cdot T_{2}(b))=0.

This proves the compatibility condition among
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. Hence
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible Rota-Baxter operator on
A
.
∎

Recall that an element
r=\sum r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}\in A\otimes A
is said to be skew-symmetric if
r^{\tau}=-r
, where
r^{\tau}=\sum r^{[2]}\otimes r^{[1]}
. Then we have the following.

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
(r_{1},r_{2})
be a pair of skew-symmetric elements in
A\otimes A
. Let
r_{1}=\sum r_{1}^{[1]}\otimes r_{1}^{[2]}
and
r_{2}=\sum r_{2}^{[1]}\otimes r_{2}^{[2]}
. Then
(r_{1},r_{2})
is a compatible associative Yang-Baxter solution if and only if the pair
(r_{1}^{\sharp},r_{2}^{\sharp})
of maps
r_{1}^{\sharp},r_{2}^{\sharp}:A^{*}\rightarrow A
forms a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the coadjoint bimodule space
A^{*}
over the algebra

, where

\displaystyle r_{1}^{\sharp}(f)=\sum f(r_{1}^{[2]})r_{1}^{[1]}~~~\text{ and }~~~r_{2}^{\sharp}(f)=\sum f(r_{2}^{[2]})r_{2}^{[1]},\text{ for }f\in A^{*}.

Proof.

It has been shown in
[
31
, Example 2.6]
that an skew-symmetric element
r=\sum r^{[1]}\otimes r^{[2]}
is an associative Yang-Baxter solution if and only if the map
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\rightarrow A

r^{\sharp}(f)=\sum f(r^{[2]})r^{[1]}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A^{*}
over the algebra

. Hence the skew-symmetric elements
r_{1},r_{2},r_{1}+r_{2}
are associative Yang-Baxter solutions if and only if
r_{1}^{\sharp},r_{2}^{\sharp},r_{1}^{\sharp}+r_{2}^{\sharp}:A^{*}\rightarrow A
are all
\mathcal{O}
-operatots. In other words,
(r_{1},r_{2})
is a compatible associative Yang-Baxter solution if and only if
(r_{1}^{\sharp},r_{2}^{\sharp})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A^{*}
over the algebra
A
.
∎

3.
Cohomology Theory

Our aim in this section is to introduce cohomology for compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators and compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras. We first construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. This characterization of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
gives rise to a cohomology associated to
(T_{1},T_{2})
. In the second part of this section, we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras. Hence we may also define the cohomology of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra from this Maurer-Cartan characterization.

3.A. Cohomology of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.
The cohomology of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator (in the associative context) was defined in
[
11
]
. In this section, we introduce the cohomology of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator and finds a relation with the cohomology of the corresponding total
\mathcal{O}
-operator. An application of our cohomology will be given in the next section.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Using the derived bracket
[
33
]
, the author in
[
31
]
constructs a graded Lie bracket
[~,~]
on the graded space
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}C^{n}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),
where
C^{n}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A)=\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A)
. The explicit description of the bracket is given by (see
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle[P,Q](u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n})

(7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{(i-1)n}~P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},Q(u_{i},\ldots,u_{i+n-1})\cdot u_{i+n},\ldots,u_{m+n})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{in}~P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i}\cdot Q(u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{i+n}),u_{i+n+1},\ldots,u_{m+n})

\displaystyle-(-1)^{mn}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{(i-1)m}~Q(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},P(u_{i},\ldots,u_{i+m-1})\cdot u_{i+m},\ldots,u_{m+n})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{im}~Q(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i}\cdot P(u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{i+m}),u_{i+m+1},\ldots,u_{m+n})\bigg\}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{mn}\big\{P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\cdot Q(u_{m+1},\ldots,u_{m+n})-(-1)^{mn}~Q(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\cdot P(u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{m+n})\big\},

\displaystyle[P,a](u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}~P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},a\cdot u_{i}-u_{i}\cdot a,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{m})

(8)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\cdot a-a\cdot P(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}),

\displaystyle[a,b]=a\cdot b-b\cdot a,

(9)

for
P\in\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes m},A),Q\in\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A)
and
a,b\in A
.
The following result has been proved in
[
11
]
.

3.1 Proposition
.

A linear map
T:M\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
if and only if
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,A)
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
\big(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),[~,~]\big).

Let
T:M\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. It follows that
T
induces a differential

\displaystyle\delta_{T}:=[T,-]:C^{n}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),\text{ for }n\geq 0.

The cohomology groups of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),\delta_{T}\}
are called the cohomology of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
.

Next, we aim to construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. To construct such a graded Lie algebra, we recall a basic construction
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
.

3.2 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}^{n},[~,~])
be a graded Lie algebra. Consider the graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}_{c}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}(\mathfrak{g}_{c})^{n}
, where

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{g}_{c})^{0}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}~~~~\text{ and }~~~~(\mathfrak{g}_{c})^{n}=\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}^{n}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{n}}_{(n+1)\mathrm{~summand}}.

(i) Then
(\mathfrak{g}_{c},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie algebra, where

\displaystyle\llbracket(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1}),(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1})\rrbracket=\big([x_{1},y_{1}],\ldots,\underbrace{[x_{1},y_{i}]+[x_{2},y_{i-1}]+\cdots+[x_{i},y_{1}]}_{i\mathrm{-th~place}},[x_{m+1},y_{m+1}]\big).

(10)

(ii) The map
\Theta:\mathfrak{g}_{c}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
defined by
\Theta(x)=x
and
\Theta((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1}))=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m+1}
, for
x\in(\mathfrak{g}_{c})^{0}
and
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})\in(\mathfrak{g}_{c})^{m\geq 1}
, is a morphism of graded Lie algebras.

(iii) Two elements
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}
satisfies
[\theta_{1},\theta_{1}]=[\theta_{2},\theta_{2}]=[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]=0
if and only if
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in(\mathfrak{g}_{c})^{1}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}_{c},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
.

In the following, we apply this construction to the graded Lie algebra
\big(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),[~,~]\big).
Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Define a graded vector space
C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)
as

\displaystyle C^{0}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)=A~~~~\text{ and }~~~~C^{n\geq 1}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)=\underbrace{\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A)}_{(n+1)\text{ summand}}.

(11)

Then
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie algebra, where the bracket
\llbracket~,~\rrbracket
is defined by (
10
).

It follows that a pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of linear maps
T_{1},T_{2}:M\rightarrow A
forms a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator if and only if
(T_{1},T_{2})\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
. Therefore, a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
induces a differential

\displaystyle\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}:=\llbracket(T_{1},T_{2}),-\rrbracket:C^{n}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\text{ for }n\geq 0.

Explicitly, for
a\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)
and
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})\in C^{n\geq 1}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),
we have

\displaystyle\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(a)=~

\displaystyle([T_{1},a],[T_{2},a]),

\displaystyle\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1}))=~

\displaystyle\big([T_{1},f_{1}],\ldots,\underbrace{[T_{1},f_{i}]+[T_{2},f_{i-1}]}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,[T_{2},f_{n+1}]\big).

3.3 Definition
.

The cohomology groups of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}\}
are called the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
, and they are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(M,A)
.

Next, we show that the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
can be seen as the cohomology of the induced compatible associative algebra given in Theorem
2.6
. To show this, we first recall the general cohomology theory of compatible associative algebras. Let
(A,\cdot_{1},\cdot_{2})
be a compatible associative algebra and
(M,l_{1},r_{1},l_{2},r_{2})
be a compatible bimodule over it. For each
n\geq 0
, let
C^{n}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)
be the abelian group defined by

\displaystyle C^{0}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)=~

\displaystyle\{m\in M~|~a\cdot_{1}m-m\cdot_{1}a=a\cdot_{2}m-m\cdot_{2}a,\text{ for all }a\in A\},

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)=~

\displaystyle\underbrace{\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)}_{n\text{ times}},\text{ for }n\geq 1.

There is a map
\delta_{\mathrm{cAss}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)
, for
n\geq 0
, given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{cAss}}(m):=~

\displaystyle\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(m)=\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(m),

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{cAss}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{1}),\ldots,\underbrace{\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{i})+\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{i-1})}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{n})\big),

for
m\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)
and
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in C^{n\geq 1}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)
. Here
\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}
is the Hochschild coboundary operator of the associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{1})
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,l_{1},r_{1})
, and
\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}
is the Hochschild coboundary operator of the associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{2})
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,l_{2},r_{2})
. It has been observed in
[
9
]
that
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M),\delta_{\mathrm{cAss}}\}
is a cochain complex. The corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomology of the compatible associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{1},\cdot_{2})
with coefficients in the compatible bimodule
(M,l_{1},r_{1},l_{2},r_{2})
, and they are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(A,M)
.

Next, let
A
be an associative algebra,
M
be an
A
-bimodule and
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Consider the induced compatible associative algebra
(M.\star_{1},\star_{2})
and its compatible bimodule
(A,l_{T_{1}},r_{T_{1}},l_{T_{2}},r_{T_{2}})
. Then it can be easily checked that (similar to
[
11
, Proposition 3.3]
)

\displaystyle\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f)=(-1)^{n}~[T_{1},f]~~~\text{ and }~~~\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f)=(-1)^{n}~[T_{2},f],\text{ for }f\in\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A).

Here
\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}
(resp.
\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}
) is the Hochschild coboundary operator of the associative algebra
(M,\star_{1})
(resp.
(M,\star_{2})
) with coefficients in the bimodule
(A,l_{T_{1}},r_{T_{1}})
(resp.
(A,l_{T_{2}},r_{T_{2}})
). As a consequence, we get the following.

3.4 Theorem
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then the cohomology of
(T_{1},T_{2})
is isomorphic to the cohomology of the induced compatible associative algebra
(M,\star_{1},\star_{2})
with coefficients in the compatible bimodule
(A,l_{T_{1}},r_{T_{1}},l_{T_{2}},r_{T_{2}})
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Consider the total
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T_{1}+T_{2}
. We define a map

\displaystyle\Theta:C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)\rightarrow C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),~~~\Theta((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1}))=f_{1}+\cdots+f_{n+1},\text{ for }n\geq 0.

Then it follows from Proposition
3.2
that the map
\Theta
is a graded Lie algebra morphism from
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
to
(C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{O}}(M,A),[~,~])
. Moreover, we have
\Theta((T_{1},T_{2}))=T_{1}+T_{2}
. As a consequence, we get the following.

3.5 Theorem
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then
\Theta
induces a morphism
H^{\bullet}_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(M,A)\rightarrow H^{\bullet}_{T_{1}+T_{2}}(M,A)
from the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
to the cohomology of the total
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T_{1}+T_{2}
.

3.B. Cohomology of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras.
Here we construct an
L_{\infty}[1]
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras. Using this characterization, we define cohomology of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras. We start with some basics of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

3.6 Definition
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a pair
(L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
consisting of a graded vector space
L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
together with a collection
\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1
multilinear maps
l_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L
(for
k\geq 1
) that satisfy

- graded symmetry:
l_{k}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)})=\epsilon(\sigma)l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),
for any
\sigma\in S_{k}
and
k\geq 1
,

- higher Jacobi identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{(i,n-i)}}\epsilon(\sigma)l_{j}\left(l_{i}\left(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(i)}\right),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\right)=0,

for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in L
and
n\geq 1
. Here
S_{(i,n-i)}
is the set of all
(i,n-i)
-shuffles and
\epsilon(\sigma)=\epsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the standard Koszul sign in the graded context.

Throughout the paper, all
L_{\infty}
-algebras are assumed to be weakly filtered
[
13
]
. In other words, certain infinite summations are always convergent. See
[
17
]
for more details.

3.7 Definition
.

Let
(L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. An element
\alpha\in L_{0}
is said to be a Maurer-Cartan element if it satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}~\!l_{k}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)=0.

A Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha\in L_{0}
induces a degree
1
differential
l_{1}^{\alpha}:L\rightarrow L
given by

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i!}~\!l_{i+1}(\underbrace{\alpha,\ldots,\alpha}_{i\text{ times}},x).

In other words,
(L,l_{1}^{\alpha})
is a cochain complex. The corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomology induced by the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
.

An important class of
L_{\infty}
-algebras arise from
V
-datas
[
33
]
. Recall that a
V
-data is a quadrupe
(V,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
in which
V
is a graded Lie algebra (with the graded Lie bracket
[~,~]
),
\mathfrak{a}\subset V
is an abelian graded Lie subalgebra,
P:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}
is a projection map with the property that
\mathrm{ker}(P)\subset V
is a graded Lie subalgebra, and
\triangle\in\mathrm{ker}(P)_{1}
that satisfies
[\triangle,\triangle]=0
. Given a
V
-data, there are few constructions of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. In the following, we only mention the one which is relevant in our context
[
33
]
.

3.8 Theorem
.

Let
(V,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
be a
V
-data. Suppose
V^{\prime}\subset V
is a graded Lie subalgebra that satisfies
[\triangle,V^{\prime}]\subset V^{\prime}
. Then the graded vector space
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}
carries an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure with the multilinear operations

\displaystyle l_{1}(x[1],a)=~

\displaystyle\big(-[\triangle,x][1],~P(x+[\triangle,a])\big),

\displaystyle l_{2}(x[1],y[1])=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{|x|}~[x,y][1],

\displaystyle l_{k}(x[1],a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1})=~

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[x,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k-1}],~\text{ for }k\geq 2,

\displaystyle l_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=~

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[\triangle,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k}],~\text{ for }k\geq 2.

Here
x,y
are homogeneous elements in
V^{\prime}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
are homogeneous elements in
\mathfrak{a}
. Up to the permutations of the above entries, all other multilinear operations vanish.

Using the structure maps of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra constructed in Theorem
3.8
, we will now construct a new one which will be useful to study compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras.

3.9 Theorem
.

Let
(V,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
be a
V
-data. Suppose
V^{\prime}\subset V
is a graded Lie subalgebra that satisfies
[\triangle,V^{\prime}]\subset V^{\prime}
. Then the graded vector space
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c}
, where

\displaystyle(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{i}=V^{\prime}_{i+1}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{i}}_{i+2~\mathrm{times}},\text{ for }i\geq-1

carries an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure with multilinear operations

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{k}\big(\big(a_{1,0},(a_{1,1},\ldots,a_{1,i_{1}+2})\big),\ldots,\big(a_{k,0},(a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,i_{k}+2})\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(l_{k}(a_{1,0},\ldots,a_{k,0}),\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=2k-2}l_{k}(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{k,j_{k}}),\ldots,\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}+2k}l_{k}(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{k,j_{k}})\bigg).

Moreover, the collection of maps

\displaystyle\{\Theta_{i}:(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{i}\rightarrow(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{i}\}_{i=-1}^{\infty}\text{ defined by }\Theta_{i}(x,(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+2}))=(x,a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i+2})

is a morphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

This result generalizes
[
9
, Proposition 2.9]
from the context of graded Lie algebras to the context of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. The
L_{\infty}
identities of
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c}
follows from the
L_{\infty}
identities of
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}
. The verification is similar to the above mentioned reference. Hence we will repeat here. By restricting the above structure on the graded vector space
\mathfrak{a}_{c}
, we obtain the following.

3.10 Theorem
.

Let
(V,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
be a
V
-data. Suppose
V^{\prime}\subset V
is a graded Lie subalgebra that satisfies
[\triangle,V^{\prime}]\subset V^{\prime}
. Then the graded vector space
\mathfrak{a}_{c}=\oplus_{i\geq-1}(\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{i}
, where
(\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{i}=\underbrace{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{i}}_{i+2~\mathrm{times}}
, inherits an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure with multilinear operations

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{k}\big((a_{1,1},\ldots,a_{1,i_{1}+2}),(a_{2,1},\ldots,a_{2,i_{2}+2}),\ldots,(a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,i_{k}+2})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}\\
=2k-2\end{subarray}}l_{k}(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{k,j_{k}}),\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}\\
=2k-1\end{subarray}}l_{k}(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{k,j_{k}}),\ldots,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}\\
=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}+2k\end{subarray}}l_{k}(a_{1,j_{1}},\ldots,a_{k,j_{k}})\bigg).

We will use these results in appropriate setting to study compatible structures. Let
A
and
M
be two vectoe spaces. Let
\mathcal{A}^{k,l}
be the direct sum of all possible
(k+l)
tensor powers of
A
and
M
in which
A
appears
k
times (hence
M
appears
l
times). For example,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2,0}=A\otimes A,~~~\mathcal{A}^{1,1}=(A\otimes M)\oplus(M\otimes A)~\text{ and }~\mathcal{A}^{2,1}=(A\otimes A\otimes M)\oplus(A\otimes M\otimes A)\oplus(M\otimes A\otimes A).

Note that there is a vector space isomorphism
(A\oplus M)^{\otimes n+1}~\cong~\oplus_{k+l=n+1}\mathcal{A}^{k,l}
. Therefore, there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus M)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus M\big)~\cong~\sum_{k+l=n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,l},A)\oplus\sum_{k+l=n+1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,l},M).

A multilinear map
f\in\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus M)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus M\big)
is said to have bidegree
k|l
with
k+l=n
, if

\displaystyle f(\mathcal{A}^{k+1,l})\subset A,~~~f(\mathcal{A}^{k,l+1})\subset M~~\text{ and }~~f\text{ is zero otherwise.}

A multilinear map is called homogeneous if it has a bidegree. We denote the set of all homogeneous multilinear maps of bidegree
k|l
by
\mathrm{Hom}^{k|l}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}^{k|0}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)~\cong~

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes k+1},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,1},M),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}^{-1|l}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)~\cong~

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes l},A).

Next, we consider the graded vector space
V:=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus M)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus M\big)
with the Gerstenhaber graded Lie bracket

\displaystyle[f,g]_{\mathsf{G}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{(i-1)n}f\circ_{i}g~-~(-1)^{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{(i-1)m}g\circ_{i}f,~\text{ where }

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m+n+1})=f\big(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},g(v_{i},\ldots,v_{i+n}),\ldots,v_{m+n+1}\big),

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus M)^{\otimes m+1},A\oplus M\big)
,
g\in\mathrm{Hom}\big((A\oplus M)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus M\big)
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m+n+1}\in A\oplus M
. Then it has been observed by Uchino
[
32
]
that

\displaystyle V^{\prime}=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}^{k|0}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\big(\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes k+1},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,1},M)\big)\subset V

is a graded Lie subalgebra,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{l\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}^{-1|l+1}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)=\oplus_{l\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes l+1},A)\subset V\text{ is an abelian subalgebra.}

Let
P:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}
be the projection onto the subspace
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\mathrm{ker}(P)\subset V
is a graded Lie subalgebra. With all these notations, the quadruple
(V,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle=0)
is a
V
-data. Moreover,
V^{\prime}
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3.8
. Hence one obtains an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on the graded vector space
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{i\geq 0}(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{i}
with the structure maps

\displaystyle l_{1}(f[1],\theta)=~

\displaystyle P(f),

\displaystyle l_{2}(f[1],g[1])=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{|f|}~[f,g]_{\mathsf{G}}[1],

\displaystyle l_{k}(f[1],\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k-1})=~

\displaystyle P[\cdots[[f,\theta_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},\theta_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},\ldots,\theta_{k-1}]_{\mathsf{G}},~\text{ for }k\geq 2

and up to the permutations of the above entries, other multilinear operations vanish.

Next, suppose the vector spaces
A
and
M
are equipped with the following maps

\displaystyle\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A),~~~~l\in\mathrm{Hom}(A\otimes M,M),~~~~r\in\mathrm{Hom}(M\otimes A,M)~~\text{ and }~~T\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,A).

Consider the element
\pi=\mu+l+r\in\mathrm{Hom}^{1|0}(A\oplus M,A\oplus M)=V_{1}^{\prime}
. Note that
\pi
can be considered as an element
\pi[1]\in(V^{\prime}[1])_{0}.
Then the following result has been proved in
[
13
]
.

3.11 Theorem
.

With all the above notations, the triple
\big(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),T\big)
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra if and only if the element
(\pi[1],T)\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=1})
.

Let
\big(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),T\big)
be a given
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. Consider the Maurer-Cartan element
(\pi[1],T)\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{0}
in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}^{\infty}_{k=1})
. Thus, we may consider the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T),\delta_{\mathrm{OA}}\}
induced by the Maurer-Cartan element, where
C^{0}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T)=0
and

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T)=\begin{cases}(V^{\prime}[1])_{-1}=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(M,M)&\text{ if }n=1\\
\\
(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{n-2}&\text{ if }n\geq 2\\
=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{n-1,1},M)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n-1},A)\end{cases}

and the coboundary map
\delta_{\mathrm{OA}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T)
given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{OA}}(f,P)=(-1)^{n-2}~l_{1}^{(\pi[1],T)}(f[1],P),\text{ for }f\in V^{\prime}_{n-1},~P\in\mathfrak{a}_{n-2}.

The corresponding cohomology groups are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T)
and they are called the cohomology of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,T)
. See
[
13
]
for more details.

In the following, we use Theorem
3.9
to construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras.

3.12 Theorem
.

Let
A
and
M
be two vector spaces. Suppose there are maps
\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)
,
l\in\mathrm{Hom}(A\otimes M,M)
,
r\in\mathrm{Hom}(M\otimes A,M)
and
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,A)
. Then
(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),(T_{1},T_{2}))
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra if and only if
(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c},\{\widetilde{l}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
.

Proof.

For the element
(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{2}\big((\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\big)=-[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{G}}[1],

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{3}

\displaystyle\big((\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=\big(0,[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}+[[\pi,T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},[[\pi,T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}\big).

Note that, for the degree reason, the higher multilinear maps
\widetilde{l}_{k}
(for
k\geq 4
) are trivial when applying
k
times the element
(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{0}
into
\widetilde{l}_{k}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\widetilde{l}_{k}\big((\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),\ldots,(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}~\widetilde{l}_{2}\big((\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\big)+\frac{1}{6}~\widetilde{l}_{3}\big((\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2})),(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=\big(-\frac{1}{2}[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{G}}[1],~\frac{1}{6}[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},~\frac{1}{3}[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},~\frac{1}{6}[[\pi,T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}\big).

This is zero if and only if all the components are zero. Hence
(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))
is a Maurer-Cartan element if and only if
[\pi,\pi]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
,
[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
,
[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
and
[[\pi,T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
.

On the other hand, the triple
(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),(T_{1},T_{2}))
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra if and only if
A=(A,\mu)
is an associative algebra,
M=(M,l,r)
is an
A
-bimodule and
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Note that
(A,\mu)
is an associative algebra and
(M,l,r)
is an
A
-bimodule if and only if the element
\pi=\mu+l+r
satisfies
[\pi,\pi]_{G}=0
(see
[
11
]
). Finally, the pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
if and only if
\llbracket(T_{1},T_{2}),(T_{1},T_{2})\rrbracket=0
, or equivalently,
[T_{1},T_{1}]=0
,
[T_{1},T_{2}]=0
and
[T_{2},T_{2}]=0
. In view of the Gerstenhaber bracket (see
[
11
]
), these conditions are respectively equivalent to
[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
,
[[\pi,T_{1}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
and
[[\pi,T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}},T_{2}]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
. Hence the result follows.
∎

Let
(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),(T_{1},T_{2}))
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. It follows from the above theorem that the element
(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
. Consider the corresponding cochain complex induced by the Maurer-Cartan element
(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))
. More precisely, we consider the complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2})),\delta_{\mathrm{cOA}}\}
, where
C^{0}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))=0
and

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))=\begin{cases}(V^{\prime}[1])_{-1}=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(M,M)\qquad\qquad\text{ if }n=1\\
\\
(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{n-2}\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ if }n\geq 2\\
=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{n-1,1},M)\oplus\underbrace{\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n-1},A)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(M^{\otimes n-1},A)}_{n\text{ times}}\end{cases}

and the coboundary map
\delta_{\mathrm{cOA}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{cOA}}\big(f,(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\big):=(-1)^{n-2}~l_{1}^{(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))}\big(f[1],(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\big),\text{ for }f\in V^{\prime}_{n-1},~(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\in(\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{n-2}.

Let
Z^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
be the space of all
n
-cocycles and
B^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
be the space of all
n
-coboundaries. Then we have
B^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))\subset Z^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
, for all
n\geq 0
. The corresponding quotient groups

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2})):=\frac{Z^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))}{B^{n}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))},~\text{ for }n\geq 0

are called the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2})).

Let
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. Consider the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha=(\pi[1],(T_{1},T_{2}))\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{0}
in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c}
. On the other hand, the triple
(A,M,T_{1}+T_{2})
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. Hence there is a Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha^{\mathrm{Tot}}=(\pi[1],T_{1}+T_{2})\in(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{0}
in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}
. Therefore, if we consider the
L_{\infty}
-algebra morphism

\displaystyle\{\Theta_{i}:(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{c})_{i}\rightarrow(V^{\prime}[1]\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}

given in Theorem
3.9
, then we have
\Theta_{0}(\alpha)=\alpha^{\mathrm{Tot}}
. As a consequence, we obtain a morphism of cochain complexes from
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2})),\delta_{\mathrm{cOA}}\}
to
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T_{1}+T_{2}),\delta_{\mathrm{OA}}\}
. Hence we get the following result.

3.13 Theorem
.

Let
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
be a compatibel
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. Then there is a morphism of groups
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cOA}}(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))\rightarrow H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{OA}}(A,M,T_{1}+T_{2})
from the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
to the cohomology of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(A,M,T_{1}+T_{2})
.

3.C. Compatible homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operators.
The notion of homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operators was introduced in
[
13
]
as the homotopy analogue of
\mathcal{O}
-operators. In this section, we define compatible homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operators as the homotopy analogue of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. A compatible homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operator is a pair consisting of two homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operators satisfying suitable compatibilities.

We first recall some definitions and basic results about
A_{\infty}
-algebras and homotopy bimodule over them.

3.14 Definition
.

An
A_{\infty}
-algebra is a pair
(A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i},\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
consisting of a graded vector space
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
with a collection
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1
multilinear maps
\mu_{k}:A^{\otimes k}\rightarrow A
(for
k\geq 1
) satisfying the following set of identities

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{j}(-1)^{|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{\lambda-1}|}~\mu_{j}\big(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\lambda-1},\mu_{i}(a_{\lambda},\ldots,a_{\lambda+i-1}),a_{\lambda+i},\ldots,a_{n}\big)=0,

(12)

for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A
and
n\geq 1
.

Let
(A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i},\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra. A homotopy bimodule over this
A_{\infty}
-algebra consists of a pair
(M=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i},\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
of a graded vector space
M=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
and a collection
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1
multilinear maps
\eta_{k}:\mathcal{A}^{k-1,1}\rightarrow M
(for
k\geq 1
) satisfying the set of identities (
12
) with exactly one of the variables
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
comes from
M
and the corresponding multilinear map
\mu_{i}
or
\mu_{j}
replaced by
\eta_{i}
or
\eta_{j}
. Like ungraded case, here
\mathcal{A}^{k-1,1}
denotes the direct sum of all possible
k
tensor powers of
A
and
M
, in which
A
appears
k-1
times (hence
M
appears exactly once).

Given an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and a homotopy bimodule over it, we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra following Theorem
3.10
. This new
L_{\infty}
-algebra is suitable to study compatible homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operators. Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and
(M,\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be a homotopy bimodule over it. We consider the graded Lie algebra

\displaystyle V=\big(\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\overline{T}(A\oplus M),A\oplus M),[~,~]_{\mathsf{G}}\big),

where an element
f\in\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\overline{T}(A\oplus M),A\oplus M)
is given by a formal summation
f=\sum_{k\geq 1}f_{k}
with
f_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}^{n}((A\oplus M)^{\otimes k},A\oplus M)
is a
k
-ary multilinear map of degree
n
. The bracket
[~,~]_{\mathsf{G}}
is given by

\displaystyle[f_{k},g_{l}]_{\mathsf{G}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k+l-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{n(|v_{1}|+\cdots+|v_{i-1}|)}~f_{k}\big(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},g_{l}(v_{i},\ldots,v_{i+l-1}),v_{i+l},\ldots,v_{k+l-1}\big)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{m(|v_{1}|+\cdots+|v_{i-1}|)}~g_{l}\big(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},f_{k}(v_{i},\ldots,v_{i+k-1}),v_{i+k},\ldots,v_{k+l-1}\big),

for
f_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}^{m}((A\oplus M)^{\otimes k},A\oplus M)
,
g_{l}\in\mathrm{Hom}^{n}((A\oplus M)^{\otimes l},A\oplus M)
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k+l-1}\in A\oplus M
. Then we can easily observe that
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}^{n}(\overline{T}(M),A)
is an abelian subalgebra of
V
. Let
P:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}
be the projection onto the subspace
\mathfrak{a}
. We also consider the element
\triangle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(\mu_{k}+\eta_{k})\in\mathrm{ker}(P)_{1}
. Note that the
A_{\infty}
-algebra identities of
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
and the homotopy bimodule conditions of
(M,\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is simply equivalent to the condition
[\triangle,\triangle]_{\mathsf{G}}=0
. As a summary, we obtain a
V
-data
(V,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
. Hence by Theorem
3.10
, we get an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{a}_{c}
. In the ungraded case, this
L_{\infty}
-algebra turns out to be the graded Lie algebra given in (
11
). This generality allows one to define compatible homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operator as a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{a}_{c}
. We will study some properties of compatible homotopy
\mathcal{O}
-operators and their cohomological properties in a future project.

4.
Deformation Theory

Formal deformation theory began with the seminal work of Gerstenhaber for associative algebras
[
16
]
. In this section, we first consider formal deformations of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
. In such deformations, we only deform the
\mathcal{O}
-operators
T_{1},T_{2}
that are compatible and keep the underlying algebra and bimodule intact. Such deformations are deformations are governed by the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Next, we also consider formal deformations of a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2})).
Here we allow deformations of all the structures. Such deformations are governed by the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2})).

4.A. Deformations of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.
Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Consider the space
A[[t]]
of formal power series in
t
with coefficients from
A
. Then
A[[t]]
is an associative algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[[t]]
. Moreover, the
A
-bimodule structure on
M
can be extended to an
A[[t]]
-bimodule structure on
M[[t]]
.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. A formal one-parameter deformation of
(T_{1},T_{2})
consists of a pair
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
of formal sums

\displaystyle T_{1,t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}T_{1,i}~\text{ and }~T_{2,t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}T_{2,i},~\text{ where }~T_{1,i},T_{2,i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,A)\text{ with }T_{1,0}=T_{1},T_{2,0}=T_{2}

such that
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M[[t]]
over the algebra
A[[t]]
.

It follows that
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
is a formal one-parameter deformation if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T_{1,i}(u)\cdot T_{1,j}(v)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T_{1,i}\big(T_{1,j}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1,j}(v)\big),

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T_{2,i}(u)\cdot T_{2,j}(v)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T_{2,i}\big(T_{2,j}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2,j}(v)\big),

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\big(T_{1,i}(u)\cdot T_{2,j}(v)+T_{2,i}(u)\cdot T_{1,j}(v)\big)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T_{1,i}\big(T_{2,j}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2,j}(v)\big)+T_{2,i}\big(T_{1,j}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1,j}(v)\big)

(15)

holds, for
n=0,1,\ldots
. These system of equations are called deformation equations. Note that these equations are hold for
n=0
as the pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator. For
n=1
, it amounts that

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+T_{1,1}(u)\cdot T_{1}(v)=T_{1}\big(u\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+T_{1,1}(u)\cdot v\big)+T_{1,1}\big(u\cdot T_{1}(v)+T_{1}(u)\cdot v\big),

(16)

\displaystyle T_{2}(u)\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+T_{2,1}(u)\cdot T_{2}(v)=T_{2}\big(u\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+T_{2,1}(u)\cdot v\big)+T_{2,1}\big(u\cdot T_{2}(v)+T_{2}(u)\cdot v\big),

(17)

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+T_{2}(u)\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+T_{1,1}(u)\cdot T_{2}(v)+T_{2,1}(u)\cdot T_{1}(v)=T_{1}\big(u\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+T_{2,1}(u)\cdot v\big)

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+T_{2}\big(u\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+T_{1,1}(u)\cdot v\big)+T_{1,1}\big(u\cdot T_{2}(v)+T_{2}(u)\cdot v\big)+T_{2,1}\big(u\cdot T_{1}(v)+T_{1}(u)\cdot v\big),

for
u,v\in M
. The first identity is equivalent to
[T_{1},T_{1,1}]=0
, while the second identity is equivalent to
[T_{2},T_{2,1}]=0
. Finally, the third identity is equivalent to
[T_{1},T_{2,1}]+[T_{2},T_{1,1}]=0
. Therefore, from (
31
)-(
34
), we get that

\displaystyle\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}\big((T_{1,1},T_{2,1})\big)=\big([T_{1},T_{1,1}],[T_{1},T_{2,1}]+[T_{2},T_{1,1}],[T_{2},T_{2,1}]\big)=0.

In other words,
(T_{1,1},T_{2,1})
is a
1
-cocycle in the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}\}
. This is called the ‘infinitesimal’ of the deformation
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t}).

4.2 Definition
.

Let
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1,t},T^{\prime}_{2,t})
be two formal one-parameter deformations of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
. They are said to be equivalent if there exists an element
a_{0}\in A
, and maps
\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
,
\psi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,M)
for
i\geq 2
, such that the pair

\displaystyle\big(\phi_{t}:=\mathrm{id}_{A}+t(\mathrm{ad}^{l}_{a_{0}}-\mathrm{ad}^{r}_{a_{0}})+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}\phi_{i},~\psi_{t}:=\mathrm{id}_{M}+t(l_{a_{0}}-r_{a_{0}})+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}\psi_{i}\big)

is a morphism of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators from
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
to
(T^{\prime}_{1,t},T^{\prime}_{2,t})
.

Therefore, the following identities must hold for equivalence of deformations

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(a\cdot b)=\phi_{t}(a)\cdot\phi_{t}(b),

(19)

\displaystyle(\phi_{t}\circ T_{1,t})(u)=(T^{\prime}_{1,t}\circ\psi_{t})(u),~~~~(\phi_{t}\circ T_{2,t})(v)=(T^{\prime}_{2,t}\circ\psi_{t})(v),

(20)

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(a\cdot u)=\phi_{t}(a)\cdot\psi_{t}(u),~~~~\psi_{t}(u\cdot a)=\psi_{t}(u)\cdot\phi_{t}(a),~\text{ for }a,b\in A,u,v\in M.

(21)

By equating coefficients of
t
from both sides of the two identities in (
20
), we get

\displaystyle\big((T_{1,1},T_{2,1})-(T^{\prime}_{1,1},T^{\prime}_{2,1})\big)(u,v)

\displaystyle=\big(T_{1}(u)\cdot a_{0}-T_{1}(u\cdot a_{0})-a_{0}\cdot T_{1}(u)+T_{1}(a\cdot u),~T_{2}(v)\cdot a_{0}-T_{2}(v\cdot a_{0})-a_{0}\cdot T_{2}(v)+T_{2}(a\cdot v)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(a_{0})\big)(u,v).

As a summary of the above discussions, we get the following.

4.3 Theorem
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then the infinitesimal in any formal deformation of
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a
1
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. Moreover, the corresponding cohomology class depends only on the equivalence class of the deformation.

Rigidity of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.
In the next, we consider rigidity of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators following the classical concept of rigidity of associative structures
[
16
]
.

4.4 Definition
.

A compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
is said to be rigid if any formal one-parameter deformation
(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
is equivalent to the undeformed one.

Recall that Nijenhuis elements associated with an
\mathcal{O}
-operator was defined in
[
11
]
. The author also finds a sufficient condition for the rigidity of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator in terms of Nijenhuis elements. Our aim here is to generalize these in the compatible set-up.

4.5 Definition
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. An element
a_{0}\in A
is said to be a Nijenhuis element associated with
(T_{1},T_{2})
if the element
a_{0}
is a Nijenhuis element for both the
\mathcal{O}
-operators
T_{1},T_{2}
, i.e., the followings are hold

\displaystyle(a_{0}\cdot a-a\cdot a_{0})~\cdot~

\displaystyle(a_{0}\cdot b-b\cdot a_{0})=0,

\displaystyle a_{0}\cdot\big(l_{T_{1}}(u,a_{0})-r_{T_{1}}(a_{0},u)\big)~-~

\displaystyle\big(l_{T_{1}}(u,a_{0})-r_{T_{1}}(a_{0},u)\big)\cdot a_{0}=0,

\displaystyle a_{0}\cdot\big(l_{T_{2}}(u,a_{0})-r_{T_{2}}(a_{0},u)\big)~-~

\displaystyle\big(l_{T_{2}}(u,a_{0})-r_{T_{2}}(a_{0},u)\big)\cdot a_{0}=0,

\displaystyle(a_{0}\cdot a-a\cdot a_{0})~\cdot~

\displaystyle(a\cdot u-u\cdot a)=0,

\displaystyle(a\cdot u-u\cdot a)~\cdot~

\displaystyle(a_{0}\cdot a-a\cdot a_{0})=0,

for
a,b\in A
and
u\in M
. We denote the set of all Nijenhuis elements by
\mathrm{Nij}(T_{1},T_{2})
.

4.6 Remark
.

It is easy to observe that, if
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator and
a_{0}\in\mathrm{Nij}(T_{1},T_{2})
then
a_{0}\in\mathrm{Nij}(T_{1}+T_{2})
, where
T_{1}+T_{2}
is the total
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

The following result is a generalization of
[
11
, Proposition 4.16]
in the compatible set-up. Hence we omit the proof.

4.7 Proposition
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. If any
1
-cocycle in the cohomology complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}\}
is given by
\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(a_{0})
, for some
a_{0}\in\mathrm{Nij}(T_{1},T_{2})
, then the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
is rigid.

Finite order deformations.
Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
.

4.8 Definition
.

An order
N
deformation (
N\in\mathbb{N}
) of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
consists of a pair
(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})
of two degree
N
polynomials of the form

\displaystyle T_{1,t}^{N}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}t^{i}T_{1,i}~~~\text{ and }~~~T_{2,t}^{N}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}t^{i}T_{2,i},~\text{ where }~T_{1,i},T_{2,i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,A)\text{ with }T_{1,0}=T_{1},~T_{2,0}=T_{2}

that satisfies the identities (
24
)-(
27
), for
n=0,1,\ldots,N
.

The system of identities can be equivalently expressed as

\displaystyle[T_{1},T_{1,n}]=-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}[T_{1,i},T_{1,j}],\qquad[T_{2},T_{2,n}]=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}[T_{2,i},T_{2,j}],

\displaystyle[T_{1},T_{2,n}]

\displaystyle+[T_{2},T_{1,n}]=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\big([T_{1,i},T_{2,j}]+[T_{2,i},T_{1,j}]\big),

for
n=0,1,\ldots,n
. We may combine these identities and write as

\displaystyle\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(T_{1,n},T_{2,n})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\llbracket(T_{1,i},T_{2,i}),(T_{1,j},T_{2,j})\rrbracket,~\text{ for }n=0,1,\ldots,N.

(22)

Motivated by the right-hand side expression of (
22
), we define an element

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}:=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=N+1\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\llbracket(T_{1,i},T_{2,i}),(T_{1,j},T_{2,j})\rrbracket.

Note that this is a
2
-cochain in the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A),\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}\}
defining the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
. Using the graded Jacobi identity of the bracket
\llbracket~,~\rrbracket
and the identities (
22
), it can be shown that
\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}\big(\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}\big)=0.
See
[
11
]
for a similar calculation. In other words,
\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}
is a
2
-cocycle. The corresponding cohomology class
[\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}]\in{H^{2}_{(T_{1},T_{2})}(M,A)}
is called the ‘obstruction class’.

4.9 Definition
.

An order
N
deformation
(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})
of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
is said to be extensible if there are maps
T_{1,N+1},T_{2,N+1}:M\rightarrow A
which makes the pair

\displaystyle\big(T_{1,t}^{N+1}=T_{1,t}^{N}+t^{N+1}T_{1,N+1},~T_{2,t}^{N+1}=T_{2,t}^{N}+t^{N+1}T_{2,N+1}\big)

(23)

into an order
N+1
deformation.

4.10 Theorem
.

An order
N
deformation
(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})
of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
is extensible if and only if the corresponding obstruction class
[\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}]
vanishes.

Proof.

Suppose
(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})
is extensible. Then from the definition of
\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}
, we have
\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}=\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}((T_{1,N+1},T_{2,N+1}))
a coboundary. Hence the corresponding cohomology class vanishes.

Conversely, let
(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})
be a deformation of order
N
for which the obstruction class
[\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}]
vanishes. Then we have
\mathrm{Ob}_{(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})}=\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})}((T_{1,N+1},T_{2,N+1}))
, for some
(T_{1,N+1},T_{2,N+1})\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)
. This implies that the pair
(T^{N+1}_{1,t},T^{N+1}_{2,t})
given by (
23
) is a deformation of order
N+1
. In other words,
(T_{1,t}^{N},T_{2,t}^{N})
is extensible.
∎

4.B. Deformations of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras.

Here we will consider formal deformations of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras in which we allow to deform all the underlying structures.

4.11 Definition
.

Let
(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),(T_{1},T_{2}))
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. A
formal deformation of this compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra consist of formal sums

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\mu_{i},~~~~l_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}l_{i},~~~~r_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}r_{i},~~~~T_{1,t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}T_{1,i}~~\text{ and }~~T_{2,t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}T_{2,i},

where
\mu_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)
,
l_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A\otimes M,A)
,
r_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M\otimes A,M)
,
T_{1,i},T_{2,i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,A)
with
\mu_{0}=\mu,l_{0}=l,r_{0}=r,T_{1,0}=T_{1}
and
T_{2,0}=T_{2}
, such that the triple

\displaystyle\big(A[[t]]=(A[[t]],\mu_{t}),M[[t]]=(M[[t]],l_{t},r_{t}),(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})\big)

is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. We denote a formal deformation as above by the tuple
(\mu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},T_{1,t},T_{2,t}).

It follows that
(\mu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
is a formal deformation of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra if the following set of equations are hold:

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c)),

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}l_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),u)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}l_{i}(a,l_{j}(b,u)),

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}r_{i}(l_{j}(a,u),b)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}l_{i}(a,r_{j}(u,b)),

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}r_{i}(u,\mu_{j}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}r_{i}(r_{j}(u,a),b),

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}(T_{1,j}(u),T_{1,k}(v))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}T_{1,i}\big(l_{j}(T_{1,k}(u),v)+r_{j}(u,T_{1,k}(v))\big),

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}(T_{2,j}(u),T_{2,k}(v))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}T_{2,i}\big(l_{j}(T_{2,k}(u),v)+r_{j}(u,T_{2,k}(v))\big),

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}(T_{1,j}(u),T_{2,k}(v))+\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}(T_{2,j}(u),T_{1,k}(v))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}T_{1,i}\big(l_{j}(T_{2,k}(u),v)+r_{j}(u,T_{2,k}(v))\big)

(30)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i+j+k=n}T_{2,i}\big(l_{j}(T_{1,k}(u),v)+r_{j}(u,T_{1,k}(v))\big),

for
a,b,c\in A
,
u,v\in M
and
n=0,1,\ldots
. These equations are obviously hold for
n=0
as the triple
(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),(T_{1},T_{2}))
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. However, for
n=1
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b)\cdot c+\mu_{1}(a\cdot b,c)=~

\displaystyle a\cdot\mu_{1}(b,c)+\mu_{1}(a,b\cdot c),

(31)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b)\cdot u+l_{1}(a\cdot b,u)=~

\displaystyle a\cdot l_{1}(b,u)+l_{1}(a,b\cdot u),

(32)

\displaystyle r_{1}(a\cdot u,b)+l_{1}(a,u)\cdot b=~

\displaystyle a\cdot r_{1}(u,b)+l_{1}(a,u\cdot b),

(33)

\displaystyle u\cdot\mu_{1}(a,b)+r_{1}(u,a\cdot b)=~

\displaystyle r_{1}(u,a)\cdot b+r_{1}(u\cdot a,b),

(34)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(T_{1}(u),T_{1}(v))+T_{1}(u)\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+T_{1,1}(u)\cdot T_{1}(v)=~

\displaystyle T_{1}\big(T_{1,1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+l_{1}(T_{1}(u),v)+r_{1}(u,T_{1}(v))\big)

(35)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt+T_{1,1}\big(T_{1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1}(v)\big),

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(T_{2}(u),T_{2}(v))+T_{2}(u)\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+T_{2,1}(u)\cdot T_{2}(v)=~

\displaystyle T_{2}\big(T_{2,1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+l_{1}(T_{2}(u),v)+r_{1}(u,T_{2}(v))\big)

(36)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt+T_{2,1}\big(T_{2}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2}(v)\big),

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle(T_{1}(u),T_{2}(v))+T_{1}(u)\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+T_{1,1}(u)\cdot T_{2}(v)+\mu_{1}(T_{2}(u),T_{1}(v))+T_{2}(u)\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+T_{2,1}(u)\cdot T_{1}(v)

(37)

\displaystyle=T_{1}\big(T_{2,1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2,1}(v)+l_{1}(T_{2}(u),v)+r_{1}(u,T_{2}(v))\big)+T_{1,1}\big(T_{2}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{2}(v)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+T_{2}\big(T_{1,1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1,1}(v)+l_{1}(T_{1}(u),v)+r_{1}(u,T_{1}(v))\big)+T_{2,1}\big(T_{1}(u)\cdot v+u\cdot T_{1}(v)\big).

Combining all these relations, we get that

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{cOA}}(\mu_{1}+l_{1}+r_{1},(T_{1,1},T_{2,1}))=0.

In other words,
((\mu_{1}+l_{1}+r_{1}),(T_{1,1},T_{2,1}))
is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
. This is called the infinitesimal of the deformation
(\mu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
.

4.12 Definition
.

Let
(\mu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{t},l^{\prime}_{t},r^{\prime}_{t},T^{\prime}_{1,t},T^{\prime}_{2,t})
be two deformations of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra
(A=(A,\mu),M=(M,l,r),(T_{1},T_{2}))
. These two deformations are said to be equivalent if there exist formal maps
\phi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\phi_{i}
and
\psi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}\psi_{i}
, where
\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
,
\psi_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(M,M)
with
\phi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
and
\psi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
, such that the pair
(\phi_{t},\psi_{t})
is a morphism of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebras from
\big((A[[t]],\mu_{t}),(M[[t]],l_{t},r_{t}),(T_{1,t},T_{2,t})\big)
to
\big((A[[t]],\mu^{\prime}_{t}),(M[[t]],l^{\prime}_{t},r^{\prime}_{t}),(T^{\prime}_{1,t},T^{\prime}_{2,t})\big)
.

Thus, the equivalence of
(\mu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},T_{1,t},T_{2,t})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{t},l^{\prime}_{t},r^{\prime}_{t},T^{\prime}_{1,t},T^{\prime}_{2,t})
amounts the following system of equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\phi_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}^{\prime}\big(\phi_{j}(a),\phi_{k}(b)\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\psi_{i}(l_{j}(a,u))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}l_{i}^{\prime}\big(\phi_{j}(a),\psi_{k}(u)\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\psi_{i}(r_{j}(u,a))=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}r_{i}^{\prime}\big(\psi_{j}(u),\phi_{k}(a)\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\phi_{i}\circ T_{1,j}=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T^{\prime}_{1,i}\circ\psi_{j}

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\phi_{i}\circ T_{2,j}=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}T^{\prime}_{2,i}\circ\psi_{j},

for
a,b\in A
,
u\in M
and
n=0,1,\ldots
. These are obviously hold for
n=0
. However, for
n=1
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b)-\mu_{1}^{\prime}(a,b)=~

\displaystyle a\cdot\phi_{1}(b)-\phi_{1}(a\cdot b)+\phi_{1}(a)\cdot b,

\displaystyle l_{1}(a,u)-l_{1}^{\prime}(a,u)=~

\displaystyle a\cdot\psi_{1}(u)-\psi_{1}(a\cdot u)+\phi_{1}(a)\cdot u,

\displaystyle r_{1}(u,a)-r_{1}^{\prime}(u,a)=~

\displaystyle u\cdot\phi_{1}(a)-\psi_{1}(u\cdot a)+\psi_{1}(u)\cdot a,

\displaystyle T_{1,1}-T^{\prime}_{1,1}=~

\displaystyle T_{1}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{1}-\phi_{1}\circ T_{1},

\displaystyle T_{2,1}-T^{\prime}_{2,1}=~

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{1}-\phi_{1}\circ T_{2}.

Combining these relations, we get that

\displaystyle\big(\mu_{1}+l_{1}+r_{1},(T_{1,1},T_{2,1})\big)-\big(\mu^{\prime}_{1}+l^{\prime}_{1}+r^{\prime}_{1},(T^{\prime}_{1,1},T^{\prime}_{2,1})\big)=\delta_{\mathrm{cOA}}\big((\phi_{1},\psi_{1})\big).

As a consequence, we get the following.

4.13 Theorem
.

Let
(A,,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra. Then the infinitesimal in a formal deformation of this compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator algebra is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
(A,M,(T_{1},T_{2}))
. Moreover, the corresponding cohomology class depends only on the equivalence class of the formal deformation.

5.
Compatible dendriform algebras

In this section, we first consider compatible dendriform algebras introduced in
[
24
]
and find their relations with compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. Then we introduce cohomology of compatible dendriform algebras and relate with the cohomology of compatible associative algebras and compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

5.1 Definition
.

[
22
]
A dendriform algebra is a triple
(D,\prec,\succ)
consisting of a vector space
D
together with bilinear operations
\prec,\succ:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
satisfying for
x,y,z\in D
,

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec z=~

\displaystyle x\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z),

(38)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec z=~

\displaystyle x\succ(y\prec z),

(39)

\displaystyle(x\prec y+x\succ y)\succ z=~

\displaystyle x\succ(y\succ z).

(40)

5.2 Remark
.

(i) Let
(D,\prec,\succ)
be a dendriform algebra. It follows from the identities (
38
)-(
40
) that the bilinear operation
x\star y:=x\prec y+x\succ y
is associative. In other words,
(D,\star)
is an associaative algebra.

(ii) Let
T:M\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then
(M,\prec_{T},\succ_{T})
is a dendriform algebra, where
u\prec_{T}v:=u\cdot T(v)
and
u\succ_{T}v:=T(u)\cdot v
, for
u,v\in M
.

Dendriform algebras arise naturally in the arithmetic of planar binary trees and shuffle algebras
[
22
,
19
]
. See also
[
14
,
23
]
for some interesting results about dendriform algebras.

5.3 Definition
.

A compatible dendriform algebra is a quintuple
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
consisting of a vector space
D
together with four bilinear operations

\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2}:D\otimes D\rightarrow D

so that
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1})
and
(D,\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
are both dendriform algebras satisfying for
x,y,z\in D
,

\displaystyle(x\prec_{1}y)\prec_{2}z+(x\prec_{2}y)\prec_{1}z=~

\displaystyle x\prec_{2}(y\prec_{1}z+y\succ_{1}z)+x\prec_{1}(y\prec_{2}z+y\succ_{2}z),

(41)

\displaystyle(x\succ_{1}y)\prec_{2}z+(x\succ_{2}y)\prec_{1}z=~

\displaystyle x\succ_{2}(y\prec_{1}z)+x\succ_{1}(y\prec_{2}z),

(42)

\displaystyle(x\prec_{1}y+x\succ_{1}y)\succ_{2}z+(x\prec_{2}y+x\succ_{2}y)\succ_{1}z=~

\displaystyle x\succ_{2}(y\succ_{1}z)+x\succ_{1}(y\succ_{2}z).

(43)

5.4 Remark
.

The compatibility conditions (
41
)-(
43
) in a compatible dendriform algebra is equivalent to the fact that
(D,\lambda\prec_{1}+\eta\prec_{2},\lambda\succ_{1}+\eta\succ_{2})
is a dendriform algebra, for any
\lambda,\eta\in{\bf k}.

Let
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
and
(D^{\prime},\prec^{\prime}_{1},\succ^{\prime}_{1},\prec^{\prime}_{2},\succ^{\prime}_{2})
be two compatible dendriform algebras. A morphism between them is a linear map
\psi:D\rightarrow D^{\prime}
which preserve the respective structure maps.

5.5 Proposition
.

Let
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
be a compatible dendriform algebra. Then the triple
(D,\star_{1},\star_{2})
is a compatible associative algebra, where
x\star_{1}y:=x\prec_{1}y+x\succ_{1}y
and
x\star_{2}y:=x\prec_{2}y+x\succ_{2}y
. (This is called the total compatible associative algebra, denoted by
D^{\mathrm{Tot}}
).

Proof.

Since
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1})
and
(D,\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
are both dendriform algebras, it follows that
(D,\star_{1})
and
(D,\star_{2})
are both associative algebras. On the other hand, by adding the respective sides of the identities (
41
)-(
43
), we get that

\displaystyle(x\star_{1}y)\star_{2}z+(x\star_{2}y)\star_{1}z=x\star_{2}(y\star_{1}z)+x\star_{1}(y\star_{2}z),\text{ for }x,y,z\in D.

This shows that
(D,\star_{1},\star_{2})
is a compatible associative algebra.
∎

It is well-known that dendriform algebras are related to pre-Lie algebras
[
2
]
. In the same way, compatible dendriform algebras are related to compatible pre-Lie algebras. We first recall some definitions about pre-Lie algebras
[
2
,
24
]
.

5.6 Definition
.

(i) A (left) pre-Lie algebra is a pair
(P,\diamond)
consisting of a vector space
P
together with a bilinear operation
\diamond:P\otimes P\rightarrow P
satisfying

\displaystyle(x\diamond y)\diamond z-x\diamond(y\diamond z)=(y\diamond x)\diamond z-y\diamond(x\diamond z),~\text{ for }x,y,z\in P.

(ii) The ‘subadjacent Lie algebra’ of a pre-Lie algebra
(P,\diamond)
is the Lie algebra
(P,[~,~]_{\diamond})
, where

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\diamond}:=x\diamond y-y\diamond x,\text{ for }x,y\in P.

(iii) A compatible (left) pre-Lie algebra is a triple
(P,\diamond_{1},\diamond_{2})
in which
(P,\diamond_{1})
and
(P,\diamond_{2})
are both (left) pre-Lie algebras satisfying additionally

\displaystyle(x\diamond_{1}y)\diamond_{2}z

\displaystyle+(x\diamond_{2}y)\diamond_{1}z-x\diamond_{2}(y\diamond_{1}z)-x\diamond_{1}(y\diamond_{2}z)

\displaystyle=(y\diamond_{1}x)\diamond_{2}z+(y\diamond_{2}x)\diamond_{1}z-y\diamond_{2}(x\diamond_{1}z)-y\diamond_{1}(x\diamond_{2}z),~\text{ for }x,y,z\in P.

The following result is straightforward. Hence we omit the details.

5.7 Proposition
.

(i) Let

(ii) Let

(P,[~,~]_{\diamond_{1}},[~,~]_{\diamond_{1}})

.

5.8 Proposition
.

(i) Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then
(M,\prec_{T_{1}},\succ_{T_{1}},\prec_{T_{2}},\succ_{T_{2}})
is a compatible dendriform algebra, denoted by
M^{\mathrm{Ind}}
, where

\displaystyle u\prec_{T_{1}}v:=u\cdot T_{1}(v),~~u\succ_{T_{1}}v:=T_{1}(u)\cdot v,~~u\prec_{T_{2}}v:=u\cdot T_{2}(v)~\text{ and }~u\succ_{T_{2}}v:=T_{2}(u)\cdot v,~\text{for }u,v\in M.

(ii) Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
be two compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators on
M
over the algebra
A
. If
(\phi,\psi)
is a morphism of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators from
(T_{1},T_{2})
to
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
then
\psi:M\rightarrow M
is a morphism of compatible dendriform algebras from
(M,\prec_{T_{1}},\succ_{T_{1}},\prec_{T_{2}},\succ_{T_{2}})
to
(M,\prec_{T^{\prime}_{1}},\succ_{T^{\prime}_{1}},\prec_{T^{\prime}_{2}},\succ_{T^{\prime}_{2}})
.

Proof.

(i) Since
T_{1}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, it follows that
(M,\prec_{T_{1}},\succ_{T_{1}})
is a dendriform algebra. Similarly,
T_{2}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator implies that
(M,\prec_{T_{2}},\succ_{T_{2}})
is a dendriform algebra. Finally,
T_{1}+T_{2}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator implies that
(M,\prec_{T_{1}+T_{2}}=\prec_{T_{1}}+\prec_{T_{2}},\succ_{T_{1}+T_{2}}=\succ_{T_{1}}+\succ_{T_{2}})
is a dendriform algebra. This shows that
(M,\prec_{T_{1}},\succ_{T_{1}},\prec_{T_{2}},\succ_{T_{2}})
is a compatible dendriform algebra.

(ii) For any
u,v\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\psi(u\prec_{T_{1}}v)=\psi(u\cdot T_{1}(v))=\psi(u)\cdot T_{1}^{\prime}(\psi(v))=\psi(u)\prec_{T_{1}^{\prime}}\psi(v),

\displaystyle\psi(u\succ_{T_{1}}v)=\psi(T_{1}(u)\cdot v)=T_{1}^{\prime}(\psi(u))\cdot\psi(v)=\psi(u)\succ_{T_{1}^{\prime}}\psi(v).

Similarly, we have
\psi(u\prec_{T_{2}}v)=\psi(u)\prec_{T_{2}^{\prime}}\psi(v)
and
\psi(u\succ_{T_{2}}v)=\psi(u)\succ_{T_{2}^{\prime}}\psi(v)
. This proves the result.
∎

. Then
(M,\diamond_{1},\diamond_{2})
is a compatible pre-Lie algebra, where

\displaystyle u\diamond_{1}v:=u\succ_{1}v-v\prec_{1}u~~~\text{ and }~~~u\diamond_{2}v:=u\succ_{2}v-v\prec_{2}u,\text{ for }u,v\in M.

Cohomology of compatible dendriform algebras.

Here we define cohomology theory of compatible dendriform algebras. We first recall certain combinatorial maps defined in
[
12
]
. Let
C_{n}
be the set of first
n
natural numbers. We will treat elements of
C_{n}
as symbols. Hence we write
C_{n}=\{[1],[2],\ldots,[n]\}
. For each
m,n\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq m
, we put the elements of
C_{m+n-1}
into
m
many boxes as follows:

\displaystyle\framebox{$[1]$}\quad\framebox{$[2]$}\quad\cdots\quad\framebox{$[i-1]$}\quad\framebox{$[i]$ \quad$[i+1]$ $\cdots$ $[i+n-1]$}\quad\framebox{$[i+n]$}\quad\cdots\quad\framebox{$[m+n-1]$}~.

Observe that there is exactly one element in each box except the
i
-th box which contains
n
elements. We define maps
R_{m;i,n}:C_{m+n-1}\rightarrow C_{m}
and
S_{m;i,n}:C_{m+n-1}\rightarrow{\bf k}[C_{n}]
by

\displaystyle R_{m;i,n}([r])=~

\displaystyle k~~~\text{ if }[r]\text{ lies in the }k\text{-th box},

\displaystyle S_{m;i,n}([r])=~

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[1]+\cdots+[n]~&\text{ if }[r]\not\in\{[i],[i+1],\ldots,[i+n-1]\}\\
&~~~(\text{i.e. if }r\text{ doesn't lie in the }i\text{-th box})\\
[r-i+1]~&\text{ if }[r]\in\{[i],[i+1],\ldots,[i+n-1]\}.\end{cases}

For any vector space
D
(not necessarily a dendriform algebra), one may now define maps (called partial compositions)

\displaystyle\circ_{i}:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{m}]\otimes D^{\otimes m},D)~\otimes~\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n},D)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{m+n-1}]\otimes D^{\otimes m+n-1},D)~~\text{ by }

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)([r];x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n-1})=f\big(R_{m;i,n}[r];x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},g\big(S_{m;i,n}[r];x_{i},\ldots,x_{i+n-1}\big),x_{i+n},\ldots,x_{m+n-1}\big),

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{m}]\otimes D^{\otimes m},D)
,
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n},D)
and
[r]\in C_{m+n-1}
.
It has been observed in
[
12
]
that the collection
\{\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n},D)\}_{n\geq 1}
of spaces with the above partial compositions forms a nonsymmetric operad. Therefore, there is a degree
-1
graded Lie bracket on
\oplus_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n},D)
given by

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{f,g\}\!\!\}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{(i-1)(n-1)}f\circ_{i}g~-~(-1)^{(m-1)(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{(i-1)(m-1)}g\circ_{i}f.

Let
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
be a compatible dendriform algebra. Define
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{2}]\otimes D^{\otimes 2},D)
by

\displaystyle\pi_{1}([r];x,y)=\begin{cases}x\prec_{1}y&\text{ if }[r]=[1]\\
x\succ_{1}y&\text{ if }[r]=[2]\end{cases}~~~\text{ and }~~~\pi_{2}([r];x,y)=\begin{cases}x\prec_{2}y&\text{ if }[r]=[1]\\
x\succ_{2}y&\text{ if }[r]=[2].\end{cases}

Since
(\prec_{1},\succ_{1})
defines a dendriform structure on
D
, it follows that
\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\pi_{1}\}\!\!\}=0
. Similarly, we have
\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\pi_{2}\}\!\!\}=0
. Finally, the compatibility conditions (
41
)-(
43
) is equivalent to
\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\}\!\!\}=0
. We are now in a position to define the cohomology of the compatible dendriform algebra
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
.

For each
n\geq 1
, we define an abelian group
C^{n}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)
by

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)=\underbrace{\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n},D)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{k}[C_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n},D)}_{n\text{ summand}}

and define a map
\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)
by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}}((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}))=(-1)^{n-1}\big(\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{1}\}\!\!\},\ldots,\underbrace{\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i-1}\}\!\!\}}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n}\}\!\!\}\big).

5.10 Proposition
.

The map
\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}}
satisfies
(\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}})^{2}=0
.

Proof.

For any
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in C^{n}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)
, we have

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}})^{2}((f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}~\big(\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{1}\}\!\!\},\ldots,\underbrace{\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i-1}\}\!\!\}}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n}\}\!\!\}\big)

\displaystyle=-\big(\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{1}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{2}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{1}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{1}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\},\ldots,

\displaystyle~\qquad\underbrace{\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i-1}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i-1}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i-2}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}}_{3\leq i\leq n-1},\ldots,

\displaystyle~\qquad\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{n}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n-1}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n}\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\}\big)

\displaystyle=(0,0,\ldots,0)\qquad(\text{as }\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\pi_{1}\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},\pi_{2}\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\}\!\!\}=0).

This completes the proof.
∎

The cohomology groups of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D),\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}}\}
are called the cohomology of the compatible dendriform algebra
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
.

Relation with the cohomology of compatible associative algebras.
Here we find a morphism from the cohomology of a compatible dendriform algebra to the cohomology of the corresponding total compatible associative algebra.

Let
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
be a compatible dendriform algebra. Then we have seen in Proposition
5.5
that
D^{\mathrm{Tot}}=(D,\star_{1},\star_{2})
is a compatible associative algebra. Let
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(D^{\mathrm{Tot}},D^{\mathrm{Tot}}),\delta_{cAss}\}
be the cochain complex defining the cohomology of the compatible associative algebra
D^{\mathrm{Tot}}
with coefficients in the adjoint compatible bimodule. For each
n\geq 1
, we define a map

\Phi_{n}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)\rightarrow C^{n}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(D^{\mathrm{Tot}},D^{\mathrm{Tot}})
by

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}))=(f_{1}^{\mathrm{Tot}},\ldots,f_{n}^{\mathrm{Tot}}),\text{ where }f_{i}^{\mathrm{Tot}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{r=1}^{n}f_{i}([r];x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

It has been shown in
[
12
]
that
\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{i}^{\mathrm{Tot}})=(-1)^{n-1}\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{Tot}}
and
\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{i}^{\mathrm{Tot}})=(-1)^{n-1}\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{Tot}}
. Here
\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}
(resp.
\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}
) denotes the Hochschild coboundary operator of the associative algebra
(D,\star_{1})

(
resp.
(D,\star_{2})
)
with coefficients in itself. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{cAss}}\circ\Phi_{n})((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}))=~

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{cAss}}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{Tot}},\ldots,f_{n}^{\mathrm{Tot}})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{Tot}}),\ldots,\delta^{1}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{i}^{\mathrm{Tot}})+\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{i-1}^{\mathrm{Tot}}),\ldots,\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{Ass}}(f_{n}^{\mathrm{Tot}})\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\big(\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{1}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{Tot}},\ldots,\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{Tot}}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i-1}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{Tot}},\ldots,\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n}\}\!\!\}^{\mathrm{Tot}}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\Phi_{n+1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}})((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})).

As a consequence, we get the following.

5.11 Theorem
.

Let
(D,\prec_{1},\succ_{1},\prec_{2},\succ_{2})
be a compatible dendriform algebra and let
D^{\mathrm{Tot}}=(D,\star_{1},\star_{2})
be the corresponding total compatible associative algebra. Then the collection
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of maps induces a morphism
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(D,D)\rightarrow H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cAss}}(D^{\mathrm{Tot}},D^{\mathrm{Tot}})
between corresponding cohomologies.

Relation with the cohomology of compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators.
Let
A
be an associative algebra and
M
be an
A
-bimodule. Suppose
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
M
over the algebra
A
. Then we have seen in Proposition
5.8
that
M
carries a compatible dendriform algebra structure (denoted by
M^{\mathrm{Ind}}
). Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}({\bf k}[C_{2}]\otimes M^{\otimes 2},M)
be the elements given by

\displaystyle\pi_{1}([r];u,v)=\begin{cases}u\prec_{T_{1}}v&\text{ if }[r]=[1]\\
u\succ_{T_{1}}v&\text{ if }[r]=[2]\end{cases}~~~\text{ and }~~~\pi_{2}([r];u,v)=\begin{cases}u\prec_{T_{2}}v&\text{ if }[r]=[1]\\
u\succ_{T_{2}}v&\text{ if }[r]=[2].\end{cases}

In the following, we find a morphism from the cohomology of the compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
(T_{1},T_{2})
to the cohomology of the compatible dendriform algebra
M^{\mathrm{Ind}}
.

For each
n\geq 1
, we define a map

\Psi_{n}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{cO}}(M,A)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{cDend}}(M^{\mathrm{Ind}},M^{\mathrm{Ind}})
by

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})):=(f_{1}^{\mathrm{Ind}},\ldots,f_{n+1}^{\mathrm{Ind}}),~~\mathrm{where}

\displaystyle(f_{i}^{\mathrm{Ind}})([r];u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n+1}~u_{1}\cdot f_{i}(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n+1})&\text{ if }~[r]=[1]\\
f_{i}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\cdot u_{n+1}&\text{ if }~[r]=[n+1]\\
0&\text{ if }~[r]\neq[1],[n+1].\end{cases}

It has been shown in
[
11
, Subsec 3.1]
that
\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}^{\mathrm{Ind}}\}\!\!\}=(-1)^{n}~[T_{1},f_{i}]^{\mathrm{Ind}}
and
\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i}^{\mathrm{Ind}}\}\!\!\}=(-1)^{n}~[T_{2},f_{i}]^{\mathrm{Ind}}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n+1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}}\circ\Psi_{n})((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1}))=\delta_{\mathrm{cDend}}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{Ind}},\ldots,f_{n+1}^{\mathrm{Ind}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\big(\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{1}^{\mathrm{Ind}}\}\!\!\},\ldots,\{\!\!\{\pi_{1},f_{i}^{\mathrm{Ind}}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{i-1}^{\mathrm{Ind}}\}\!\!\},\ldots,\{\!\!\{\pi_{2},f_{n+1}^{\mathrm{Ind}}\}\!\!\}\big)

\displaystyle=\big([T_{1},f_{1}]^{\mathrm{Ind}},\ldots,[T_{1},f_{i}]^{\mathrm{Ind}}+[T_{2},f_{i-1}]^{\mathrm{Ind}},\ldots,[T_{2},f_{n+1}]^{\mathrm{Ind}}\big)

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{n+1}\circ\delta_{(T_{1},T_{2})})((f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})).

As a consequence, we get the following.

5.12 Theorem

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a compatible

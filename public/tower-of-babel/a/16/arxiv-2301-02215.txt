Ziming Shi

Abstract
. Given a formally integrable almost complex structure J J defined on the closure of a bounded domain D ⊂ ℂ n D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} , and provided that J J is sufficiently close to the standard complex structure, the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem asks whether there exists a global diffeomorphism defined on D ¯ \overline{D} that transforms J J into the standard complex structure, under certain geometric and regularity assumptions on D D . In this paper we prove a quantitative result of this problem. Assuming D D is a strictly pseudoconvex domain in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} with C 2 C^{2} boundary, and that the almost complex structure X X is of the Hölder-Zygmund class Λ r ​ ( D ¯ ) \Lambda^{r}(\overline{D}) for r > 3 2 r>\frac{3}{2} , we prove the existence of a global diffeomorphism (independent of r r ) in the class Λ r + 1 2 − ε ​ ( D ¯ ) \Lambda^{r+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\overline{D}) , for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 .

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a real differentiable manifold. An almost complex structure near a point
p
in
M
is defined as a tensor field
J
, which is, at every point in some neighborhood
U
of
p
, an endomorphism of the tangent space
T_{x}(M)
such that
J^{2}=-I
, where
I
denotes the identity transformation of
T_{x}(M)
. Consequently there exists a decomposition of the complexified tangent bundle into the
+i
and
-i
eigenspaces of
J
:
\mathbb{C}TM=\mathcal{S}^{+}_{J}\oplus\mathcal{S}^{-}_{J}
. If
M
is a complex manifold with local holomorphic coordinate chart
\{U^{k},z^{k}\}
, then the complex structure on
M
gives rise to the standard almost complex structure
J_{st}
, defined by
S^{+}_{J_{st}}=\operatorname{span}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{k}_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
on
U^{k}
.
Conversely, given an almost complex structure
J
on
M
, one wants to know whether
J
is induced by the complex structure on
M
, in other words, whether there exists a diffeomorphism
z_{\alpha}\to w_{\beta}
so that in the new coordinate
S^{+}_{J}=\operatorname{span}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{w}_{\alpha}}\right\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
. In this case we say that
J
is integrable, and
\{w_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
is a holomorphic coordinate chart compatible with
J
.

We call an almost complex structure
J
formally integrable if
S^{+}_{J}
is closed under the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
(An equivalent condition is the vanishing of the Nijenhuis tensor.) In particular, an integrable structure is formally integrable.
The classical Newlander-Nirenberg theorem asserts that the converse is also true locally, i.e. for a formally integrable almost complex structure
J
defined near an interior point of
M
, there exists a local holomorphic coordinate system in a neighborhood of
p
which is compatible with
J
.

For real analytic
J
, the Newlander-Nirenberg theorem follows easily from the analytic Frobenius theorem. However if
J
is only
C^{\infty}
or less regular, the proof becomes much more difficult. The reader may refer to the work of Newlander-Nirenberg
[
NN57
]
, Nijenhuis-Woolf
[
NW63
]
, Malgrange
[
Mal69
]
and Webster
[
Web89
]
. We point out that Webster’s proof yields the sharp regularity result in the Hölder space, namely, if
J
is
C^{k+\alpha}
for
k\geq 1
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
in a neighborhood of a point
p
, then there exists a local diffeomorphism near
p

of the class
C^{k+1+\alpha}
such that the new coordinate is compatible with
J
.

We now consider the analogous global problem. Suppose a formally integrable almost complex structure
J
is defined on the closure of a relatively compact subset
D
in a complex manifold
M
, such that
J
is a small perturbation of
J_{st}
(as measured by certain norms such as the Hölder or Hölder-Zygmund norm), one wants to know whether
J
is integrable on
\overline{D}
, or equivalently, if there exists a global diffeomorphism on
\overline{D}
inducing a holomorphic coordinate system compatible with
J
. Moreover, we are interested in the global regularity of such coordinate.
We shall henceforth refer to this problem as the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem.

Under the assumption that both the boundary
bD
and the almost complex structure
J
are
C^{\infty}
, Hamilton
[
Ham77
]
proved the existence of a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism on
\overline{D}
under which the new coordinate is compatible with
J
, if
D
satisfies
1)
H^{1}(D,\mathcal{T}D)=0
, where
\mathcal{T}
stands for the holomorphic tangent bundle of
D
; and 2) the Levi form on
bD
has either
n-1
positive eigenvalues or at least two negative eigenvalues. There is also a local version of Newlander-Nirenberg theorem with boundary for strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface, due to Catlin
[
Cat88
]
and Hanges-Jacobowitz
[
HJ89
]
, independently. We note that all these results are carried out in the
C^{\infty}
category (boundary, structure and the resulting diffeomoprhism are all
C^{\infty}
) using
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann-type methods.
More recently, Gan-Gong
[
GG
]
proved a global Newlander-Nirenberg theorem on a strictly pseudoconvex domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary. Assuming
J\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D})
,
r>5
, they proved the existence of a diffeomorphism in the class
\Lambda^{r-1}(\overline{D})
, assuming that
|J-J_{st}|_{\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D})}<\delta(r)
for some sufficiently small
\delta(r)
. Their method is based on the
\overline{\partial}
homotopy formula, an approach originally pioneered by Webster in his proof of the classical (local) Newlander-Nirenberg theorem
[
Web89
]
.

To formulate our results we first introduce some notations. Let
p\in M
, we identify an almost complex structure
J
near
p
by a set of vector fields
\{X_{\overline{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
such that

\text{ $X_{\overline{1}},\dots,X_{\overline{n}},\overline{X_{\overline{1}}},\dots,\overline{X_{\overline{n}}}$ are linearly independent at $p$},

where
\{X_{\overline{\alpha}}\}
spans the eigenspace
S_{J}^{+}
with eigenvalue
i
.
We can write

X_{\overline{\alpha}}=a_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\beta}}+b^{\beta}_{\overline{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{\beta}}.

Here we have used Einstein convention to sum over the repeated index
\beta
, and we shall adopt this convention throughout the paper.

\overline{X_{\overline{\alpha}}}
denotes the complex conjugate of
X_{\overline{\alpha}}
and
\{\overline{X_{\overline{\alpha}}}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
is a basis for the eigenspace
S_{J}^{-}
with eigenvalue
-i
.

For two vector fields
V,W
on
M
, we denote their Lie bracket as
[V,W]=VW-WV
. The formal integrability of
J
on
D
means there exist functions
C^{\overline{\gamma}}_{\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}
such that

[X_{\overline{\alpha}},X_{\overline{\beta}}]=C^{\overline{\gamma}}_{\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}X_{\overline{\gamma}},\quad\text{for $\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,n$}

everywhere on
D
.

By an
\mathbb{R}
linear change of coordinates, we can transform
X_{\overline{\alpha}}
to the form

X_{\overline{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{\alpha}}+A_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\beta}}.

Our main result is the following, where we use the notation
a^{-}
to mean that
a-\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
. We use
\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D})
or
|\cdot|_{D,r}
to denote the Hölder-Zygmund norm on
D
(See Definition
2.2
below).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary that is strictly pseudoconvex with respect to the standard complex structure in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, for
n\geq 2
. Let
3/2<m\leq\infty
and let
\{X_{\overline{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{\alpha}}+A_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\beta}}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
be
\Lambda^{m}(\overline{D})
vector fields defining
a formally integrable almost complex structure on
\overline{D}
. Let
\epsilon_{0},\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
be small positive constants such that
m>\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
and
0<\epsilon_{0}<\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
.
There exists
\hat{\varepsilon}_{0}=\hat{\varepsilon}_{0}(D,|A|_{\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}},\tilde{\epsilon}_{0})>0
such that if
|A|_{D,1+\epsilon_{0}}<\hat{\varepsilon}_{0}
, then there exists an embedding
F
of
\overline{D}
into
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
dF(X_{\overline{1}}),\dots,dF(X_{\overline{n}})
are in the span of
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{n}}\right\}
.
Furthermore,
F\in\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}^{-}}(\overline{D})
if
m<\infty
and
F\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
if
m=\infty
. The constant
\hat{\varepsilon}_{0}
needs to converge to
0
as
m\to\frac{3}{2}^{+}
(
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}\to 0
) and can be chosen to be independent of all
m
away from
\frac{3}{2}
.

Theorem
1.1
proves the almost
1/2
gain in regularity for the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem on strictly pseudoconvex domains. It is worthwhile to note that our result is achieved assuming only that the initial almost complex structure is a small perturbation in the
\Lambda^{1+\epsilon_{0}}(\overline{D})
norm from the standard structure
J_{st}
. This is a major improvement over the previous best known result in
[
GG
]
, where one needs to have control on the
\Lambda^{m}(\overline{D})
norm of the perturbation in order to obtain a diffeomorphism in
\Lambda^{m-1}(\overline{D})
, and they need
m>5
.

We now describe our method, which is based on a KAM type argument of
[
Web89
]
and
[
GG
]
. Given
D=D_{0}
with an initial almost complex structure
J_{0}
, we look for a succession of diffeomorphisms
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
that maps
D_{i}
with structure
J_{i}
to a new domain
D_{i+1}
with structure
J_{i+1}
. During the iteration, the perturbation
\|A_{i}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{D}_{i})}=\|J_{i}-J_{st}\|_{C^{0}(\overline{D}_{i})}
converges to
0
while each
D_{i}
remains a strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{2}
boundary.
The sequence of the domains
D_{i}
converges to a limiting domain
D_{\infty}
, while the sequence of diffeomorphisms
\widetilde{F}_{j}=F_{j}\circ F_{j-1}\cdots\circ F_{1}
converges to a diffeomorphism
F:D_{0}\to D_{\infty}
with
dF(J_{0})=J_{st}
.
For each
i
, the map
F_{i}
is constructed by applying a
\overline{\partial}
homotopy formula
A_{i}=\overline{\partial}P_{i}A_{i}+Q_{i}\overline{\partial}A_{i}
on
D_{i}
. In Webster’s proof for the classical Newlander-Nirenberg theorem, only a local homotopy formula is needed, namely the Bochner-Martinelli formula. The operator
P_{i}
gains one derivative, and as a result there is no loss of derivative for the almost complex structure at each iteration step.
Using the integrability condition, Webster is able to obtain the rapid convergence of the perturbation for all derivatives:
|A_{i+1}|_{C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D})}\leq|A_{i}|^{2}_{C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D})}
for any positive integer
k
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

For our problem, we need to apply a global homotopy formula on
\overline{D}
. The associated operators
P_{i},Q_{i}
gain only
1/2
derivative up to boundary, which amounts to a loss of
1/2
derivative for the new almost complex structure after each iterating diffeomorphism, and thus the iteration will break down after finite steps.
To avoid the loss of derivatives, Gan-Gong
[
GG
]
applied a Nash-Moser type smoothing operator
at each step. In this case they show that the lower-order norms
|A_{i}|_{\Lambda^{s}(\overline{D_{i}})}
converges to
0
for
s\in(2,3)
, while the higher-order norm
|A_{i}|_{\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D_{i}})}
blows up, for all sufficiently large
r
. By using the convexity of Hölder-Zygmund norms (interpolation), they can then show that the intermediate norms
|A_{i}|_{D_{i},m}
converges to
0
, for
s<m<r
. In the iteration scheme of
[
GG
]
, the higher-order norm blows up like
|A_{i+1}|_{r}\leq C_{r}t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|A_{i}|_{r}
,
r>5
, where
t_{i}
is the parameter in the smoothing operator that tends to
0
in the iteration. In our case, we modify their method by using a different diffeomorphism
F_{i}
which allows us to prove the estimate

(1.1)

|A_{i+1}|_{\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D}_{i+1})}\leq C_{r}|A_{i}|_{\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D}_{i})},\quad r>1.

To achieve the above goal, we construct a family of smoothing operators
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
acting on functions defined on a bounded Lipschitz domain and satisfying certain bounds in Hölder-Zygmund space, thus avoiding the use of the extension operator required for smoothing in
[
GG
]
. For our construction we use Littlewood-Paley theory, which is a convenient tool since the Hölder-Zygmund norm can be viewed as a Besov space.

The key feature in our estimate of
A_{i}
is the nice property enjoyed by the commutator
[\nabla,S_{t}]=\nabla S_{t}-S_{t}\nabla
, which replaces the role of the commutator
[\nabla,E]
in
[
GG
]
,
E
being some extension operator.
Our estimate roughly states that if
u\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
, then for
any
0<s<r
, the
\Lambda^{s}(\overline{\Omega})
norm of
[\nabla,S_{t}]
converges to
0
as
t\to 0
.

It is plausible to conjecture that one should be able to gain exactly
1/2
derivative in regularity for the global Newlander-Nirenberg problem, in view of the corresponding
1/2
gain for the regularity of the
\overline{\partial}
equation on strictly pseudoconvex domains. However, our method necessarily incurs a loss of arbitrarily small
\varepsilon
in smoothness, due to the use of the convex interpolation of norms. One could also ask whether the assumption that
J\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D})
, for
r>3/2
can be replaced by

J\in C^{1}(\overline{D})
or
J\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{D})
for
r>1
, which are the minimal smoothness required to define an almost complex structure. In our proof, the index
3/2
comes from the need to control the
C^{2}
norm (Levi form) of each iterating domain so that the domains remain strictly pseudoconvex.

The dynamical method of solving Newlander-Nirenberg problem using
\overline{\partial}
homotopy formula
was originated by Webster; it has found powerful applications in the CR vector bundle problem and the more difficult local CR embedding problem. See for example Gong-Webster
[
GW10
,
GW11
,
GW12
]
where they used such methods to obtain several sharp estimates on these problems. We also mention the paper by Polyakov
[
Pol04
]
which uses similar techniques for the CR Embedding problems for compact regular pseudoconcave CR submanifold.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic properties of the Hölder Zygmund space
\Lambda^{s}
. In particular we need a Littlewood-Paley characterization of
\Lambda^{s}
using the Besov norm
\mathscr{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
. In Section 2.2 we construct the important Moser-type smoothing operator on bounded Lipschitz domains. We also prove the commutator estimate for
[\overline{\partial},S_{t}]
in Proposition
2.22
, which plays a key role for bounding the norms of the perturbation in the iteration.
In Section 3 we derive for each iteration the estimates for the lower and higher order norms of the new perturbation
A_{i+1}
in terms of the norms of previous perturbation
A_{i}
. This constitutes the main estimates of the paper.
In Section 4 we set up the iteration scheme using estimates from Section 3 and induction arguments, and we establish the convergence of the lower order norms and the blow up of higher order norms. Finally, we use convexity of norms to show that the composition of the iterating maps converges to a limiting diffeomorphism in the appropriate norm.

We now fix some notations used in the paper. We will often write
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\alpha}}
as
\partial{}_{\alpha}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{\beta}}
as
\partial{}_{\overline{\beta}}
, and we use
A\lesssim B
to mean that there exists a constant
C
independent of
A,B
such that
A\leq CB
. For a map
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote its Jacobian matrix by
Df=\left[\frac{\partial f^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
. The set of integers is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}
, and the set of natural numbers
\{0,1,2,3,\dots\}
is denoted by
\mathbb{N}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Function spaces

In this section, we recall some basic results for the standard Hölder space
C^{r}(D)
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{D,r}
,
0\leq r<\infty
, and the Hölder-Zygmund space
\Lambda^{r}
with norm
|\cdot|_{D,r}
,
0<r<\infty
.

First, we define the notion of special and bounded Lipschitz domains.

Definition 2.1
.

A
special Lipschitz domain
is an open set
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the form
\omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\rho(x^{\prime})\}
with
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
. A
bounded Lipschitz
domain is a bounded open set
\Omega
whose boundary is locally the graph of some Lipschitz function. In other words,
b\Omega=\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{M}U_{\nu}
, where for each
1\leq\nu\leq M
, there exists an invertible linear transformation
\Phi_{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a special Lipschitz domain
\omega_{\nu}
such that

U_{\nu}\cap\Omega=U_{\nu}\cap\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu}).

Fix such covering
U_{\nu}
, we define the
Lipschitz norm of
\Omega
with respect to
U_{\nu}
, denoted as
\operatorname{Lip}_{U_{\nu}}(\Omega)
, to be
\sup_{\nu}\|\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{\nu}\|_{C^{0}}
.

For a bounded Lipschitz domain
D
, the following Hölder estimates for interpolation, product rule and chain rule are well-known. See for instance
[
H7̈6
]
.

\displaystyle\|u\|_{D,(1-\theta)a+\theta b}\leq C_{a,b}\|u\|_{D,a}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{D,b}^{\theta},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 1;

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{D,a}\leq C_{a}(\|u\|_{D,a}\|v\|_{D,0}+\|u\|_{D,0}\|v\|_{D,a})

\displaystyle\|u\circ\varphi\|_{D,a}\leq C_{a}\left(\|u\|_{D^{\prime},a}\|\varphi\|_{D,1}^{a}+\|u\|_{D^{\prime},1}\|\varphi\|_{D,a}+\|u\|_{D^{\prime},0}\right).

Here
a,b\geq 0
and
\varphi:D\to D^{\prime}
is a
C^{1}
map between two bounded Lipschitz domains
D,D^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.2

(Hölder-Zygmund space)
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open subset. We define the Hölder-Zygmund space
\Lambda^{s}(U)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
by the following:

•

For
0<s<1
,
\Lambda^{s}(U)
consists of all
f\in C^{0}(U)
such that
\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s}(U)}:=\sup\limits_{U}|f|+\sup\limits_{x,y\in U}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}<\infty
.

•

\Lambda^{1}(U)
consists of all
f\in C^{0}(U)
such that
\|f\|_{\Lambda^{1}(U)}:=\sup\limits_{U}|f|+\sup\limits_{x,y\in U;\frac{x+y}{2}\in U}\frac{|f(x)+f(y)-2f(\frac{x+y}{2})|}{|x-y|}<\infty
.

•

For
s>1
recursively,
\Lambda^{s}(U)
consists of all
f\in\Lambda^{s-1}(U)
such that
\nabla f\in\Lambda^{s-1}(U)
. We define
\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s}(U)}:=\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s-1}(U)}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|D_{j}f\|_{\Lambda^{s-1}(U)}
.

•

We define
C^{\infty}(U):=\bigcap_{s>0}\Lambda^{s}(U)
to be the space of bounded smooth functions.

In particular, for a non-integer positive
s
, the Hölder-Zgymund norm
\Lambda^{s}
is equivalent to the Hölder norm
C^{s}
.

Similar to that of the Hölder norm, we have the following estimates for the Hölder-Zygmund norm:

Lemma 2.3
.

[
GG
, Lemma 3.1]

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be connected bounded Lipschitz domains and let
\varphi
maps
D
into
D^{\prime}
. Suppose that
\|\varphi\|_{D,1}<C
. Then we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle|u|_{D,(1-\theta)a+\theta b}\leq C_{a,b,D}|u|_{D,a}^{1-\theta}|u|_{D,b}^{\theta},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 1,\quad a,b>0.

(2.2)

\displaystyle|uv|_{D,a}\leq C_{a}(|u|_{D,a}\|v\|_{D,\varepsilon}+\|u\|_{D,\varepsilon}|v|_{D,a}),\quad a>0;

\displaystyle|u\circ\varphi|_{D,1}\leq C_{D,D^{\prime}}|u|_{D^{\prime},1}(1+C_{1/\varepsilon}\|\varphi\|_{D,1+\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}});

(2.3)

\displaystyle|u\circ\varphi|_{D,a}\leq C_{a,D,D^{\prime}}[C_{1/\varepsilon}|u|_{D^{\prime},a}\|\varphi\|_{D,1+\varepsilon}^{\frac{1+2\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}+C_{1/\varepsilon}\|u\|_{D^{\prime},1+\varepsilon}|\varphi|_{D,a}+\|u\|_{D^{\prime},0}),\quad a>1.

\displaystyle|u\circ\varphi|_{D,a}\leq|u|_{D^{\prime},a}\|\varphi\|_{D,1}^{\alpha},\quad 0<a<1.

Here

is a positive constant depending on

.

We also need the following more general chain rule estimate. The proof for Hölder norms can be found in the appendix of
[

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
D_{i}
be a sequence of Lipschitz domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
\operatorname{Lip}(D_{i})
is uniformly bounded. Let
F_{i}=I+f_{i}
map

\displaystyle\|u\circ F_{m}\circ\cdots\circ F_{1}\|_{D_{0},r}\leq C_{r}^{m}\left(\|u\|_{r}+\sum_{i}\|u\|_{1}\|f_{i}\|_{r}+\|u\|_{r}\|f_{i}\|_{1}\right),\quad r\geq 0;

\displaystyle|u\circ F_{m}\circ\cdots\circ F_{1}|_{D_{0},r}\leq C_{r}^{m}\left(|u|_{r}+\sum_{i}\|u\|_{1+\varepsilon}|f_{i}|_{r}+C_{1/\varepsilon}|u|_{r}\|f_{i}\|_{1+\varepsilon}^{\frac{1+2\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}\right),\quad r>1.

We now recall the definition of Besov space, which includes the Hölder-Zygmund space as a special case.

In what follows we denote by
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of tempered distributions, and for an arbitrary open subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(U):=\{\widetilde{f}|_{U}:\widetilde{f}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
the space of distributions in
U
which can be extended to tempered distributions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition 2.5
.

A
classical Littlewood-Paley family

\lambda
is a a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
of Schwartz functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that the Fourier transform
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda_{j}(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}
satisfies

•

\widehat{\lambda}_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2}(\mathbf{0}))
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{0}\equiv 1
in
B_{1}(\mathbf{0})
;

•

\widehat{\lambda}_{j}(\xi)=\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)-\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(2^{-(j-1)}\xi)
for
j\geq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of all such families
\lambda
.

From the above definition, we see that if
\lambda=(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
, then
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\subset\{2^{j-1}<|\xi|<2^{j+1}\}
, for
j\geq 1
.
To define Besov space on bounded Lipschitz domain, we shall use a more general version of Littlewood-Paley family.

Definition 2.6
.

A
regular Littlewood-Paley family
is a sequence
\phi=(\phi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
of Schwartz functions, such that

•

\widehat{\phi}_{0}(0)=1
and
\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=1+O(|\xi|^{\infty})
as
\xi\to 0
.

•

\widehat{\phi}_{j}(\xi)=\widehat{\phi}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)-\widehat{\phi}_{0}(2^{-(j-1)}\xi)
, for
j\geq 1
.

We denote by

Definition 2.10
.

The

2.2.
Smoothing operator on bounded Lipschitz domains

In this subsection we construct a Moser-type smoothing operator on bounded Lipschitz domains.
In
[
Mos62
]
, Moser constructed a smoothing operator
L_{t}:C^{0}(U_{0})\to C^{\infty}(D_{0})
, where
D_{0},U_{0}
are open sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
D_{0}\subset\subset U_{0}
. Assume that
\operatorname{dist}(bD_{0},bU_{0})=t_{0}>0
. Then
L_{t}
is given by

L_{t}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(x-y)\chi_{t}(y)\,dy,\quad x\in D_{0},\;0<t<\frac{t_{0}}{C},

where
\chi_{t}(z):=t^{-d}\chi(z/t)
,
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<1\}
,
\int\chi(z)\,dz=1
, and

\int z^{I}\chi(z)\,dz=0,\quad 0<|I|\leq M,\quad M<\infty.

Moser showed that the following estimate hold for
0<t<t_{0}/C
:

\displaystyle\|L_{t}u\|_{D_{0},r}\leq C_{r,s}t^{s-r}\|u\|_{U_{0},s},\quad 0\leq s\leq r<\infty;

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|(I-L_{t})u\|_{D_{0},s}\leq C_{r,s}t^{r-s}\|u\|_{U_{0},r},\quad r\geq 0,\;s\leq r<s+M.

For our smoothing operator, we do not require
u
to be defined on a large domain
U_{0}
. Furthermore, we note that the smoothing operator
L_{t}
depends on a finite parameter
M
, whereas our smoothing operator has no such dependency and satisfies the corresponding
estimate (
2.11
) for

.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then there exist operators

such that for all

(1)

|S_{t}u|_{\Omega,r}\leq Ct^{s-r}|u|_{\Omega,s}
;

(2)

|(I-S_{t})u|_{\Omega,s}\lesssim C^{\prime}t^{r-s}|u|_{\Omega,r}

Here the constants

depend only on

and the Lipschitz norm of
\Omega

Proof.

First we prove the statements when the domain is a special Lipschitz domain of the form
\omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}>\rho(x^{\prime})\}
, where
|\nabla\rho|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}<1

\omega+\mathbb{K}=\omega
.
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
(\phi_{j},\psi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{K}
-dyadic pair. We define the following smoothing operator
S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\omega)
:

(2.12)

S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}u:=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}t^{-1}\rfloor}\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast u.

To make the proof simpler it suffices to take
t=2^{-N}
, so that the above sum becomes
\sum_{k=0}^{N}
.
Using the equivalence of the Hölder-Zygmund
\Lambda^{s}
norm and the Besov
B^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
-norm, it suffices to prove that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}|\lambda_{j}\ast(S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}u)|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\lesssim t^{s-r}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\phi_{j}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

We have

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}|\lambda_{j}\ast(S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}u)|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}\left|\lambda_{j}\ast\sum_{k=0}^{N}\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast u\right|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{j(r-s)}2^{js}\sum_{k=0}^{N}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}|_{L^{1}(-\mathbb{K})}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},

where in the last inequality we used Young’s inequality. By Proposition
2.9
, the last expression is bounded up to a constant multiple
C=C(M)
by

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{j(r-s)}2^{js}\sum_{k=0}^{N}2^{-M|j-k|}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}=2^{N(r-s)}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{(j-N)(r-s)}2^{js}\sum_{k=0}^{N}2^{-M|j-k|}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

If
j<N

2^{(j-N)(r-s)}2^{-\frac{M}{2}|j-k|}<1

|j-k|=j-k\geq j-N

if we choose

. In any case, the above estimate leads to

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}|\lambda_{j}\ast(S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}u)|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r,s}2^{N(r-s)}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}\sum_{k=0}^{N}2^{-\frac{M}{2}|j-k|}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N(r-s)}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{N}2^{(s-\frac{M}{2})|j-k|}2^{ks}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},

where we take
M>2s
. Let

u[a]:=2^{|a|(s-\frac{M}{2})},\quad a\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad v[b]:=2^{bs}|\phi_{b}\ast f|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},\quad b\in\mathbb{N}.

We denote
\left|u\right|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|u[a]\right|
and
\left|v\right|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}:=\sup_{b\in\mathbb{N}}\left|u[b]\right|
.
Then

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{N}2^{(s-\frac{M}{2})|j-k|}2^{ks}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq|u|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}|v|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}\lesssim_{r,s}|v|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\phi_{j}\ast f|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

Thus we get from (
2.13
)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}|\lambda_{j}\ast(S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}u)|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r,s}2^{N(r-s)}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\phi_{j}\ast f|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=t^{s-r}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\phi_{j}\ast f|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},\quad s\leq r,\quad t=2^{-N}.

In other words, we have shown that
|S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}u|_{\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\omega})}\lesssim_{r,s}t^{s-r}|u|_{\Lambda^{s}(\overline{\omega})}
,
s\leq r
.

(ii)
From (
2.12
) we have

(I-S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}})u=\sum_{k>N}\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast u.

It suffices to show that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\lambda_{j}\ast[(I-S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}})u]|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\lesssim t^{r-s}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\phi_{j}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

We have

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\lambda_{j}\ast[(I-S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}})u]|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}\left|\lambda_{j}\ast\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast u\right|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{js}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}\ast\psi_{k}|_{L^{1}(-\mathbb{K})}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

By Proposition
2.9
, the last expression is bounded up to a constant multiple
C=C(M)
by

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{j(s-r)}2^{jr}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}2^{-M|j-k|}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}=2^{N(s-r)}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{(j-N)(s-r)}2^{js}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}2^{-M|j-k|}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}.

If
j\geq N
, then
2^{(j-N)(s-r)}\leq 1
. If
j<N
, then
-(j-N)=|j-N|\leq|j-k|
for all
k\geq N+1
. Hence
2^{(j-N)(s-r)-\frac{M}{2}|j-k|}\leq 2^{-(j-N)(r-s)-\frac{M}{2}|j-k|}\leq 2^{|j-k|(r-s-\frac{M}{2})}<1
, where we choose
M>2(r-s)
. In all cases, we get from the above estimates that

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\lambda_{j}\ast[(I-S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}})u]|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r,s}2^{N(s-r)}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jr}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{M}{2}|j-k|}|\phi_{k}\ast u|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)},\quad t=2^{-N}.

The rest of the estimates follow identically as in (i), and consequently we prove that
|(I-S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}})u|_{\omega,s}\lesssim_{r,s}t^{r-s}|u|_{\omega,r}
for
0<s\leq r
.

Finally we prove both (i) and (ii) for general bounded Lipschitz domains. For this we use partition of unity. Take an open covering
\{U_{\nu}\}_{\nu=0}^{M}
of
\Omega
such that

U_{0}\subset\subset\Omega,\quad b\Omega\subseteq\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{M}U_{\nu},\quad U_{\nu}\cap\Omega=U_{\nu}\cap\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu}),\quad\nu=1,\dots,M.

Here each
\omega_{\nu}
is a special Lipschitz domain of the form
\omega_{\nu}=\{x_{d}>\rho_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\}
, with
|\rho_{\nu}|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}<1
, and
\Phi_{\nu}
,
1\leq\nu\leq M
are invertible affine linear transformations. Here we note that
|D\Phi_{\nu}|_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded (up to a constant) by the Lipschitz norm of
\Omega
.

If
f
has compact support in
\Omega
, we define the smoothing operator
S^{0}_{t}
by

S^{0}_{t}f=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\eta_{k}\ast\theta_{k}\ast f,\quad t=2^{-N}.

Here we can choose any Littlewood-Paley pair
(\theta_{j},\eta_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
with
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}
,
\eta\in\mathfrak{G}
and
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\eta_{j}\ast\theta_{j}=\delta_{0}
. Then the same proof as above shows that
|S_{t}^{0}f|_{\Omega,r}\lesssim t^{s-r}|f|_{\Omega,s}
and
|(I-S_{t}^{0})f|_{\Omega,s}\lesssim t^{r-s}|f|_{\Omega,r}
for
0<s\leq r
.

Fix a partition of unity
\{\chi_{\nu}\}_{\nu=0}^{M}
associated with
\{U_{\nu}\}_{\nu=0}^{M}
, such that
\chi_{\nu}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{\nu})
and
\chi_{0}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}^{2}=1
. For each
1\leq\nu\leq M
, we have the property
\omega_{\nu}+\mathbb{K}=\omega_{\nu}
, where
\mathbb{K}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}>|x^{\prime}|\}
. Let
S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}
be given as above, we define

(2.14)

S_{t}u:=S^{0}_{t}(\chi_{0}u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}S_{t}^{\nu}(\chi_{\nu}u),

where
S^{\nu}_{t}g:=[S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}(g\circ\Phi_{\nu})]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}
,
1\leq\nu\leq M
.

Applying the estimates for
S_{t}^{0}
and
S_{t}^{\mathbb{K}}
, we get

\displaystyle|S_{t}u|_{\Omega,r}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{s-r}\left(|\chi_{0}u|_{\Omega,s}+|(\chi_{\nu}u)\circ\Phi_{\nu}|_{\omega,s}\right)\lesssim t^{s-r}|u|_{\Omega,s},\quad 0<s\leq r,

where the constant depends only on
r,s
and
\operatorname{Lip}(\Omega)
.

On the other hand, since
u=\chi_{0}u+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}^{2}u
we have

\displaystyle|(I-S_{t})u|_{\Omega,s}

\displaystyle\leq|(I-S^{0}_{t})(\chi_{0}u)|_{\Omega,r}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}|\chi_{\nu}(I-S^{\nu}_{t})(\chi_{\nu}u)|_{\Omega,s}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{r-s}\left(|\chi_{0}u|_{\Omega,r}+|(\chi_{\nu}u)\circ\Phi_{\nu}|_{\omega,r}\right)\lesssim t^{r-s}|u|_{\Omega,r}.\quad 0<s\leq r,

where the constant depends only on
r,s
and
\operatorname{Lip}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\{S_{t}^{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{M}
be the smoothing operator constructed in the proof of Proposition
2.20
. Denote
\partial{}_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
i=1,2,\dots,d
. Then
[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}^{\nu}](\chi_{\nu}f)\equiv 0
for all
f\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
,
r>1
.

Proof.

Recall that
S_{t}^{\nu}g:=[S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}(g\circ\Phi_{\nu})]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}
, where
S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}
is the smoothing operator defined on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\omega_{\nu})
and given by (
2.12
). Hence we have

\displaystyle[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}^{\nu}](\chi_{\nu}f)

\displaystyle=\partial{}_{i}(S_{t}^{\nu}(\chi_{\nu}f))-S_{t}^{\nu}(\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{\nu}f))

\displaystyle=\partial{}_{i}(S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})-S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{v}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\nabla S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}])\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\partial{}_{i}\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}-S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[\nabla((\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu})]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\partial{}_{i}\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[\nabla((\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu})]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\partial{}_{i}\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}-S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}

\displaystyle=[\nabla,S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}]((\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu})\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\partial{}_{i}\Phi_{\nu}^{-1},

where in the last step we used the fact that
\Phi_{\nu}
is a linear transformation so that

\displaystyle S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}[\nabla((\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu})]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\partial{}_{i}\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\nu}^{j}}{\partial x_{1}}S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}\left(\frac{\partial(\chi_{\nu}f)}{\partial y_{j}}\circ\Phi_{\nu}\right)\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\Phi_{\nu}^{j}}{\partial x_{n}}S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}\left(\frac{\partial(\chi_{\nu}f)}{\partial y_{j}}\circ\Phi_{\nu}\right)\end{pmatrix}\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\partial{}_{i}(\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})^{1}\\
\vdots\\
\partial{}_{i}(\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})^{n}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}\left(\frac{\partial(\chi_{\nu}f)}{\partial y_{j}}\circ\Phi_{\nu}\right)\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\Phi^{j}_{\nu}}{\partial x_{\alpha}}\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}\cdot\frac{\partial(\Phi^{-1}_{\nu})^{\alpha}}{\partial y_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}\left(\frac{\partial(\chi_{\nu}f)}{\partial y_{i}}\circ\Phi_{\nu}\right)\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}.

Now since
S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}u=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}t^{-1}\rfloor}\psi_{k}\ast\phi_{k}\ast u
is a convolution operator, we have
[\nabla,S^{\mathbb{K}}_{t}]\equiv 0
on
\omega_{\nu}
. Thus
[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}^{\nu}](\chi_{\nu}f)\equiv 0
.
∎

Proposition 2.22
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
S_{t}
be the smoothing operator constructed in the proof of Proposition
2.20
. Denote
\partial{}_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
i=1,2,\dots,d
. Then for all
u\in\Lambda^{r}(\overline{\Omega})
with
r>1
, the following holds

(2.15)

\left|[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}]u\right|_{\Omega,s}\leq Ct^{r-s}|u|_{\Omega,r},\quad 0<s\leq r.

Here the constant
C
depends only
r,s
and the Lipschitz norm of the domain.

Proof.

By the formula for
S_{t}
(
2.14
), we can write

\displaystyle[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}]u

\displaystyle=\partial{}_{i}S_{t}u-S_{t}\partial{}_{i}u

\displaystyle=\partial{}_{i}S_{t}^{0}(\chi_{0}u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\partial{}_{i}[\chi_{\nu}S^{\nu}_{t}(\chi_{\nu}u)]-S_{t}^{0}(\chi_{0}(\partial{}_{i}u))-\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}S_{t}^{\nu}(\chi_{\nu}(\partial{}_{i}u))

\displaystyle=\{\partial{}_{i}S_{t}^{0}(\chi_{0}u)-S_{t}^{0}\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{0}u)\}+S_{t}^{0}((\partial{}_{i}\chi_{0})u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}(\partial{}_{i}\chi_{\nu})S_{t}^{\nu}(\chi_{\nu}u)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}\partial{}_{i}S^{\nu}_{t}(\chi_{\nu}u)-\chi_{\nu}S_{t}^{\nu}\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{\nu}u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}S_{t}^{\nu}((\partial{}_{i}\chi_{\nu})u)

\displaystyle=S_{t}^{0}((\partial{}_{i}\chi_{0})u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}(\partial{}_{i}\chi_{\nu})S_{t}^{\nu}(\chi_{\nu}u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}S_{t}^{\nu}((\partial{}_{i}\chi_{\nu})u).

Here to get the last line we use
[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}^{0}](\chi_{0}u)\equiv 0
and Lemma
2.21
.
Since
0=\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{0}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}^{2})=\partial{}_{i}\chi_{0}+2\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}\partial{}_{i}(\chi_{\nu})
, we can write

\displaystyle[\partial{}_{i},S_{t}]u

\displaystyle=(S_{t}^{0}-I)((\partial{}_{i}\chi_{0})u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}(\partial{}_{i}\chi_{\nu})(S_{t}^{\nu}-I)(\chi_{\nu}u)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}(S_{t}^{\nu}-I)((\partial{}_{i}\chi_{\nu})u).

Applying the proof of Proposition
2.20
to the right-hand side above we get (
2.15
).
∎

2.3.
Remarks on stability of constants

For our application, we need to construct a sequence of domains
\{D_{j}=F_{j-1}(D_{j-1})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
, where
F_{j-1}
is a sequence of diffeomorphisms constructed using the above defined smoothing and homotopy operators.
For the iteration to work, we need to make sure that each
D_{j}
is strictly pseudoconvex with
C^{2}
boundary, and also that the maps
F_{j}\circ F_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{1}
converge to a limiting map
F_{\infty}
in the desired norms. This requires the stability of constants in all the estimates under small
C^{2}
perturbation of domains. We now make precise this notion of stability, following
[
GG
]
.

Let
D_{0}=\{x\in\mathcal{U}:\rho_{0}<0\}\subset\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain with
C^{2}
boundary, where
\mathcal{U}
is a neighborhood of
\overline{D}_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
is a
C^{2}
defining function of
D_{0}
. Let

G_{\varepsilon_{0}}=\{\rho\in C^{2}(\mathcal{U}):\|\rho-\rho_{0}\|_{\mathcal{U},2}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\}.

Here
\varepsilon_{0}
is small positive number such that for all
\rho\in G_{\varepsilon_{0}}
, we have
d\rho(x)\neq 0
on
\{x\in\mathcal{U}:\rho(x)=0
.

We say that a function
C(\rho)\to(0,\infty)
is
upper stable
if there exists
\varepsilon(D_{0})>0
and a constant
C_{0}(D_{0})>1
, such that

C(\rho)\leq C_{0}(D_{0})C(\rho_{0})

for all
\rho
satisfying
\|\rho-\rho_{0}\|_{\mathcal{U},2}\leq\varepsilon(D_{0})
.

We make note of the following examples of upper stable mappings which are relevant to our proof.

(1)

The constants appearing in Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
2.4
are upper stable under small
C^{2}
perturbation of the domain.

(2)

The operator norms of Rychkov’s extension operator (Proposition
2.15
) depend only on the Lipschitz norm of the domain, which is upper stable under small
C^{1}
perturbation of the domain.

(3)

The operator norms for the smoothing operator are upper stable under small
C^{2}
perturbation of the domain
D_{0}
(see Proposition
2.20
).

(4)

The operator norms for the homotopy operator (
2.10
) are upper stable under small
C^{2}
perturbation of the domain.

3.
Transformation of perturbation under diffeomorphism

Let
D_{0}
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Given the initial integrable almost complex structure on
D_{0}
, which is given by the vector fields

\{X_{\overline{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\{\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}+A_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}\partial{}_{\beta}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}

F
defined on
\overline{D}_{0}
that transforms the structure to a new structure closer to the standard complex structure while
\overline{D}_{0}
is transformed to a new domain that is still strictly pseudoconvex. We shall assume the following initial condition for
A=[A_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}]_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
:

(3.1)

t^{-\frac{1}{2}}|A|_{D_{0},s}\leq\frac{1}{C^{\ast}},\quad s=1+\epsilon_{0},

where
C^{\ast}
is a sufficiently large absolute constant.
Here

is the parameter of the smoothing operator which will converge to
0
in the iteration, and
\epsilon_{0}
can be taken to be any sufficiently small positive number, which we will specify later.
We take the map in the form
F=I+f
. Applying the

\mathcal{E}_{D_{0}}
to

(Proposition
2.15
), we can assume that

is defined with compact support on some open set

containing
\overline{D}_{0}
. Let
B_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|<R\}

We will use
C_{m}
(resp.

D_{0}

As in the proof of Lemma
2.17

(0,1)

We then apply the homotopy formula component-wise to

|I_{1}|_{D_{0},m}\leq C_{m}|A|_{D_{0},m},\quad m>0

4.
Iteration scheme and convergence of maps

In this section we set up the iteration scheme. We apply an infinite sequence of coordinate transformation

as constructed in the previous section. The goal is to show that the composition of maps

converges to

For this scheme to work we need to ensure that for each

In what follows we assume that

is some large ball and

is a diffeomorphism from

onto itself, and the defining function
\rho_{j}
of the domain

\displaystyle a_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(t_{0}^{r-s-\gamma-\lambda}+t_{0}^{2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}-\lambda})\leq t_{0}^{d\alpha}=t_{1}^{\alpha};

\displaystyle L_{1}\leq t_{0}^{-\lambda}L_{0}\leq t_{0}^{-\beta d}L_{0}=t_{1}^{-\beta}L_{0},

where we have assumed the following constraints:

(4.14)

\begin{gathered}\alpha d<r-s-\gamma-\lambda\\
\alpha(2-d)>\frac{1}{2}+\lambda,\qquad\beta d>\lambda.\end{gathered}

Thus we have verified (iii) for
i=0
assuming the intersection of the above constraints is nonempty. We will see in the induction step that this is true provided
r>s+\frac{1}{2}
.

Now assume that (i) - (iii) hold for some
i-1\in\mathbb{N}
. We shall verify the induction step. Let

t_{i}=t_{i-1}^{d}
, where
d\in(1,2)
is to be specified. Suppose we have found
D_{i}=F_{i-1}(D_{i-1})
which is still strictly pseudoconvex with
C^{2}
boundary. Apply the homotopy formula to get
A_{i}=\overline{\partial}P_{i}A_{i}+Q_{i}\overline{\partial}A_{i}
on
D_{i}
.
Let
F_{i}=I+f_{i}
, where
f_{i}=-\mathcal{E}_{D_{i}}S_{t_{i}}P_{i}A_{i}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{D_{i}}
is the Rychkov extension operator on
D_{i}
.
Since
t_{i}<t_{0}
, by (
4.8
) we have

(4.15)

t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|A_{i}|_{D_{i},s}\leq t_{i}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{C^{\ast}},\quad t_{i}<t_{0}<\hat{t}_{0}.

Hence estimate (
4.10
) still holds for
f_{i}=-\mathcal{E}_{D_{i}}S_{t_{i}}P_{i}A_{i}
, and there exists
G_{i}=I+g_{i}
which is the inverse of
F_{i}
on
B_{0}
, where
|g_{i}|_{B_{0},m}\leq C_{m}|f_{i}|_{B_{0},m}
,
m>1
. This verifies the induction step for (i).

Define

D_{i+1}=\{z\in\mathcal{U}:\rho_{i+1}(z)<0\},

where
\rho_{i+1}(z)=\rho_{i}\circ G_{i}
. By (
3.2
) and the induction hypothesis for (iii), we have

(4.16)

\|f_{i}\|_{B_{0},2}\leq|f_{i}|_{B_{0},2+\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq C_{2}t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|A_{i}|_{D_{i},1+\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq C_{2}t_{i}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, we show that
C_{2}t_{i}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{\delta}{(i+1)^{2}}
, which will be true if we set

(4.17)

C_{2}\hat{t}_{0}^{(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})d^{i}}\leq\frac{\delta}{(i+1)^{2}}.

This has been achieved for
i=0
by (
4.12
). Suppose (
4.17
) holds for
i-1
. Then

C_{2}\hat{t}_{0}^{(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})d^{i}}=C_{2}\hat{t}_{0}^{(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})d^{i-1}}\hat{t}_{0}^{(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})d^{i-1}(d-1)}\leq\frac{\delta}{i^{2}}\hat{t}_{0}^{(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})d^{i-1}(d-1)}\leq\frac{\delta}{(i+1)^{2}},

where the last inequality holds for all
i\geq 1
.
Therefore by (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we have
\|f_{i}\|_{B_{0},2}\leq\frac{\delta}{(i+1)^{2}}
.
It then follows from Proposition
4.1
(i) that

\|\rho_{i+1}-\rho_{0}\|_{\mathcal{U},2}\leq\varepsilon(D_{0}),\qquad\operatorname{dist}(D_{i+1},\partial{}\mathcal{U})\geq\operatorname{dist}(D_{0},\partial{}\mathcal{U})-C\varepsilon.

This shows that
D_{i+1}
is still a strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{2}
boundary.

Next, we verify the induction step for (iii). On
D_{i+1}
, let
A_{i+1}
be the coefficient matrix such that

\{\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}+A_{i+1}\partial{}_{\alpha}\}=dF_{i}|_{\overline{D}_{i}}\left[\operatorname{span}\{\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}+A_{i}\partial{}_{\alpha}\}\right]
.
Since (
4.15
) holds, we can apply Proposition
3.1
to get:

\displaystyle a_{i+1}\leq C_{r,s}t_{i}^{r-s}L_{i}+C_{s}t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}a_{i}^{2}\leq C_{r,s}L_{0}t_{i}^{r-s-\beta}+C_{s}t_{i}^{2\alpha-\frac{1}{2}};

\displaystyle L_{i+1}\leq C_{r}L_{i}\leq C_{r}L_{0}t_{i}^{-\beta},

where we used the induction hypothesis
a_{i}\leq t_{i}^{\alpha}
and
L_{i}\leq L_{0}t_{i}^{-\beta}
.
Notice that by the condition (
4.13
), we still have
C_{r,s},C_{s},C_{r}\leq\frac{1}{2}t_{i}^{-\lambda}
since
t_{i}<t_{0}
. For
\gamma>0
, we further choose

(4.18)

\hat{t}_{0}\leq\left(\frac{1}{L_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}},\quad L_{0}:=|A_{0}|_{D_{0},r},

so that
L_{0}\leq t_{i}^{-\gamma}
. We have

\displaystyle a_{i+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(t_{i}^{r-s}t_{i}^{-\lambda-\gamma-\beta}+t_{i}^{-\lambda}t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}t_{i}^{2\alpha})\leq t_{i}^{d\alpha}=t_{i+1}^{\alpha},

\displaystyle L_{i+1}\leq t_{i}^{-\lambda}L_{i}\leq t_{i}^{-\lambda-\beta}L_{0}\leq t_{i}^{-d\beta}L_{0}=t_{i+1}^{-\beta}L_{0},

where we have assumed

(4.19)

\begin{gathered}\alpha d+\beta<r-s-\lambda-\gamma,\\
\alpha(2-d)>\frac{1}{2}+\lambda,\qquad\beta>\frac{\lambda}{d-1}.\end{gathered}

Notice that the above constraint is more strict than (
4.14
).
Let
\mathcal{D}(r,s,d,\lambda,\gamma)
be the set of
(\alpha,\beta)
such that (
4.19
) is satisfied. We now determine the values of
r,s,d,\lambda,\gamma
such that
\mathcal{D}(r,s,d,\lambda,\gamma)
is non-empty. Consider the limiting domain for fixed
r,s,d
and
\lambda,\gamma=0
:

\mathcal{D}(r,s,d,0)=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,\infty)^{2}:\alpha d+\beta<r-s,\quad\alpha(2-d)>\frac{1}{2},\quad\alpha>\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

By the defining inequalities of
\mathcal{D}(\xi,d,0)
, it is non-empty if and only if

r-s>p(d),\quad p(d):=\frac{d}{2(2-d)}.

On the interval
(1,2)
,
p
is a strictly increasing function with infimum value
p(1)=\frac{1}{2}
. This implies that

(4.20)

r-s>p(1)=\frac{1}{2},\quad r>s+\frac{1}{2}>\frac{3}{2}\quad(\text{since $s>1$}).

Notice that under the above condition for
r,s
,
\mathcal{D}(r,s,d,\lambda,\gamma)
is still non-empty for sufficiently small
\lambda,\gamma
. In summary, given
r=\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
, we first choose
s=1+\epsilon_{0}
for
0<\epsilon_{0}<\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
, such that (
4.20
) is satisfied. This is possible by choosing
d\in(1,2)
sufficiently close to
1
. We then choose
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{D}(r,s,d,\lambda,\gamma)
with
\lambda,\gamma
chosen sufficiently small, such that (
4.19
) holds. In view of (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and (
4.18
),
\hat{t}_{0}
needs to be chosen sufficiently small. In other words,
\hat{t}_{0}\to 0
as
r\to\frac{3}{2}^{+}
.
∎

The following consequence of the above result is what is actually used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
s=1+\epsilon_{0}
and
r_{0}=\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
, where
\epsilon_{0},\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
are small positive constants satisfying
0<\epsilon_{0}<\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
. Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain with a
C^{2}
defining function
\rho_{0}
on
\mathcal{U}
and
\{X_{\overline{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\{\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}+A_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}\partial{}_{\beta}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}\in\Lambda^{r_{0}}(\overline{D})
be a formally integrable almost complex structure. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F_{i}=I+f_{i},D_{i},A_{i}
be given as in the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
There exist
\hat{\varepsilon}=\hat{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\epsilon}_{0},|A|_{D,r_{0}},D)
,
1<d<2
,
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
,
\eta>0
, such that if
|A|_{D,s}<\hat{\varepsilon}
, then

|A_{i}|_{D_{i},s}\leq t_{i}^{\alpha},\quad|A_{i}|_{D_{i},m}\leq|A_{N}|_{D_{N},m}t_{i}^{-\eta},\quad\text{for all $i>N=N(m,d)$},

where
t_{i+1}=t_{i}^{d}
and
\eta
is independent of
m
. The constant
\hat{\varepsilon}
needs to converge to
0
as
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}\to 0
.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
4.3
with
r=r_{0}
and choose the parameters
\alpha,\beta,d,\lambda
satisfying the conditions in (
4.7
). Then as part of the conclusion of the proposition, we have
|A_{i}|_{D_{i},s}\leq t_{i}^{\alpha}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
and also
t_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|A_{i}|_{D_{i},s}\leq\frac{1}{C^{\ast}}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote
M_{i}:=|A_{i}|_{D_{i},m}
, for
m>1
. By (
3.32
) we have

M_{i+1}\leq C_{m}M_{i}.

Fix
\lambda>0
. One can find a large
N=N(m,d)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.21)

C_{m}\leq t_{i}^{-\lambda},\quad\text{for all $i\geq N$.}

We would like to show that there exists
\eta
, independent of
m
, such that
for all
i\geq N
, the following holds

M_{i}\leq M_{N}t_{i}^{-\eta},\quad i\geq N.

For
i=N
, the above inequality is obvious. Assume it holds for some
i\geq N
.
Then

M_{i+1}\leq C_{m}M_{i}\leq t_{i}^{-\lambda-\eta}M_{N}\leq t_{i}^{-d\eta}M_{N}=t_{i+1}^{-\eta}M_{N},\quad i\geq N,

where the third inequality holds if we choose

\eta(d-1)>\lambda.\qed

Finally, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{2}
boundary in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
\{X_{\overline{\alpha}}=\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}+A^{\beta}_{\overline{\alpha}}\partial{}_{\beta}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n}
be a formally integrable almost complex structure on
D
of the class
\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}}(\overline{D})
, for any small
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}>0
. Fix
\epsilon_{0}
with
0<\epsilon_{0}<\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
. There exists an

\hat{\varepsilon}=\hat{\varepsilon}\left(\tilde{\epsilon}_{0},|A|_{D,\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}},D\right)

such that if
|A|_{D,1+\epsilon_{0}}\leq\hat{\varepsilon}
,
then the following statements are true.

(1)

Let
A\in\Lambda^{m}(\overline{D})
, with
m>\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
. There exists a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
F:\overline{D}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that if
A\in\Lambda^{m}(\overline{D})
,
m>\frac{3}{2}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}
, then
F\in\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}^{-}}(\overline{D})
.

(2)

If
A\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
, then
F\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
.

(3)

dF(X_{\overline{\alpha}})
are in the span of
\{\partial{}_{\overline{1}},\dots,\partial{}_{\overline{n}}\}
and
F(\overline{D})
is strictly pseudoconvex.

The constant
\hat{\varepsilon}
needs to converge to
0
as
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}\to 0
.

Proof.

We will write
s=1+\epsilon_{0}
and denote
A_{0}=A
and
D_{0}=D
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F_{i}=I+f_{i},D_{i},A_{i}
be given as in the proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Write
l=(1-\theta)s+\theta m
, where
s<l<m
and
\theta\in(0,1)
is to be chosen. By Corollary
4.4
, there exist
d\in(1,2)
,
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
,
\eta>0
,
\{t_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle|A_{i}|_{D_{i},1+\epsilon_{0}}\leq t_{i}^{\alpha},\quad|A_{i}|_{D_{i},m}\leq t_{i}^{-\eta}M_{N},\quad t_{i+1}=t_{i}^{d},\quad i\geq N=N(m,d),

where we denote
M_{N}:=|A_{N}|_{D_{N},m}
.
Here
\eta
satisfies the condition

(4.22)

\eta>\frac{\lambda}{d-1},

where
\lambda>0
is some fixed constant for which

(4.23)

C_{m}\leq t_{i}^{-\lambda},\quad i\geq N=N(m,d).

Using convexity of Hölder-Zygmund norm (
2.1
), we have for all
i\geq N
,

(4.24)

|f_{i}|_{D_{i},\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{m}|f_{i}|^{1-\theta}_{D_{i},s+\frac{1}{2}}|f_{i}|^{\theta}_{D_{i},m+\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{m}|A_{i}|_{D_{i},s}^{1-\theta}|A_{i}|_{D_{i},m}^{\theta}\leq C_{m}M_{N}t_{i}^{(1-\theta)\alpha-\theta\eta}.

Consider the composition map
\widetilde{F}_{j}=F_{j}\circ F_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ F_{0}
, where
F_{j}=I+f_{j}
for
j\geq 0
. By using Lemma
2.4
and above estimate for
f_{j}
, we obtain for all
j\geq N
,

(4.25)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{F}_{j}-\widetilde{F}_{j-1}|_{D_{0},\ell+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=|f_{j}\circ F_{j-1}\circ\cdots F_{0}|_{D_{0},\ell+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ell}^{j}\left\{|f_{j}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq j}\left(|f_{j}|_{1+\epsilon_{0}}|f_{i}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}+|f_{j}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|f_{i}|_{1+\epsilon_{0}}\right)\right\}.

The second sum is bounded by
jC_{1}|f_{j}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\ell}^{j}|f_{j}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
. To bound the first sum, we use
|f_{i}|_{m}\leq C_{m}|f_{i-1}|_{m}
for any
m>1
, which implies that
\sum_{i\leq j}|f_{i}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\leq|f_{0}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j}C_{\ell}^{i}\right)\lesssim C_{\ell}^{j}|f_{0}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus

\displaystyle|f_{j}|_{1+\epsilon_{0}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq j}|f_{i}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|f_{j}|_{1+\epsilon_{0}}C_{\ell}^{j}|f_{0}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C_{\ell}^{j}|A_{0}|_{D_{0},\ell}|A_{j}|_{D_{0},1+\epsilon_{0}}\leq M_{0}C_{\ell}^{j}t_{j}^{\alpha},\quad M_{0}:=|A_{0}|_{D_{0},m}.

It follows from (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) that for all
j\geq N
,

(4.26)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{F}_{j}-\widetilde{F}_{j-1}|_{D_{0},\ell+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\ell}^{j}|f_{j}|_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}+|A_{0}|_{D_{0},m}C_{\ell}^{j}t_{j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ell}^{j}\left[C_{m}M_{N}t_{j}^{(1-\theta)\alpha-\theta\eta}+M_{0}t_{j}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}M_{0,N}C_{\ell}^{j}t_{j}^{\mu}=C_{m}M_{0,N}C_{\ell}^{j}t_{0}^{d^{j}\mu},

where we denote
M_{0,N}:=M_{0}+M_{N}

and
\mu:=(1-\theta)\alpha-\theta\eta
. By choosing
0<\theta<\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\eta}<1
, we have
\mu>0
. As a consequence,
\widetilde{F}_{j}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\Lambda^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D}_{0})
when
j
is sufficiently large. This can be seen by writing

\displaystyle C_{\ell}^{j}t_{0}^{d^{j}\mu}

\displaystyle=C_{\ell}^{j}(t_{0}^{\mu})^{d^{\frac{j}{2}}d^{\frac{j}{2}}}=C_{\ell}^{j}\left[(t_{0}^{\mu})^{d^{\frac{j}{2}}}\right]^{d^{\frac{j}{2}}}=C_{\ell}^{j}\left[(t_{0}^{\mu})^{d\frac{j}{2}}\right]^{d\frac{j}{2}}

\displaystyle=(C_{\ell}^{2})^{\frac{j}{2}}\left[\left(t_{0}^{\mu d}\right)^{\frac{j}{2}}\right]^{d\frac{j}{2}}\leq\left[C_{\ell}^{2}\left(t_{0}^{\frac{\mu d}{2}}\right)^{j}\right]^{d\frac{j}{2}}.

For fixed
t_{0},d,\mu
, by choosing
j>N(m,d)
, we can make the expression inside the bracket less than
1
, so

C_{m}M_{0,N}C_{\ell}^{j}t_{0}^{d^{j}\mu}
is a Cauchy sequence when
j
is sufficiently large.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, in view of (
4.22
), we can choose
\theta_{\ast}=\theta_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
close to
1
, such that
l=s+\theta_{\ast}(m-s)>m-\varepsilon
. We then choose
\eta_{\ast}=\eta_{\ast}(\varepsilon)
small such that
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\eta_{\ast}}>\theta_{\ast}
. By (
4.22
) this in turns requires
\lambda
to be chosen small while
d
is being fixed. Finally we choose
N=N(m,d,\varepsilon)
to be large such that (
4.23
) is satisfied. This shows that
\widetilde{F}_{j}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\overline{D_{0}})
if
j>N(m,d,\varepsilon)
.
In other words, we prove that if
A\in\Lambda^{m}(\overline{D})
, then
\widetilde{F}_{j}
converges to some limit
F\in\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\overline{D})
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

(ii) We have
A\in\Lambda^{m}(\overline{D}_{0})
for any
m>1
.
The same argument as in (i) shows that
\{\widetilde{F}_{j}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\overline{D_{0}})
for
j>N(m,d,\varepsilon)
.
Since this holds for all
m
, we have
F\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D_{0}})
.

Finally we show that
F
is a diffeomorphism. By the inverse function theorem, it suffices to check that the Jacobian of
F(x)
is invertible at every
x\in D
.
Write
DF=I-(I-DF)
, then
DF
is invertible with
inverse
(I-(I-DF))^{-1}
if
\|DF-I\|_{0}<1
. Write

\displaystyle DF-I

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}D\widetilde{F}_{j}-I

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}[D\widetilde{F}_{j}-D\widetilde{F}_{j-1}]+[D\widetilde{F}_{j-1}-D\widetilde{F}_{j-2}]+\cdots[D\widetilde{F}_{1}-D\widetilde{F}_{0}]+[D\widetilde{F}_{0}-D\widetilde{F}_{-1}],

where we set
\widetilde{F}_{-1}
to be the identity map and thus
D\widetilde{F}_{-1}=I
.
Then

\|DF-I\|_{D_{0},0}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|D\widetilde{F}_{j}-D\widetilde{F}_{j-1}\|_{D_{0},0}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|\widetilde{F}_{j}-\widetilde{F}_{j-1}\|_{D_{0},1}.

Using (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}_{j}-\widetilde{F}_{j-1}\|_{D_{0},1}

\displaystyle=\|f_{j}\circ F_{j-1}\circ\cdots F_{0}\|_{D_{0},1}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}^{j}\left(\|f_{j}\|_{1}+\|f_{j}\|_{1}\sum_{0\leq i\leq j-1}\|f_{i}\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{j}\|f_{j}\|_{1}\left(1+\sum_{0\leq i\leq j-1}t_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\lesssim C_{1}^{j}t_{j}^{\alpha},

where we use the estimate
\|f_{j}\|_{1}\leq|A_{j}|_{D_{j},1+\epsilon_{0}}\leq t_{j}^{\alpha}
.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\|DF-I\|_{D_{0},0}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|\widetilde{F}_{j+1}-\widetilde{F}_{j}\|_{D_{0},1}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}C_{1}^{j}t_{j}^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}C_{1}^{j}t_{0}^{d^{j}\alpha}

which is less than
1
if we choose
t_{0}
less than a fixed constant.

(iii)
By Lemma
2.18
,
d\widetilde{F}_{j}(X_{\overline{\alpha}})=dF_{j}\cdots dF_{0}(X_{\overline{\alpha}})\in\operatorname{span}\{\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}+(A_{j+1})_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\beta}\partial{}_{\beta}\}
. Since
dF(X_{\overline{\alpha}})=\lim_{j\to\infty}d\widetilde{F}_{j}(X_{\overline{\alpha}})
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}|A_{j}|_{D_{j},s}\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}t_{j}^{\alpha}=0
, we have
d\widetilde{F}(X_{\overline{\alpha}})=\operatorname{span}\{\partial{}_{\overline{\alpha}}\}
. Let
\rho_{0}
be the defining function for
D_{0}
, and for
j\geq 0
, let
\rho_{j+1}=\rho_{j}\circ F_{j}^{-1}
be the defining function for
D_{j+1}
. We have shown in the proof of
Proposition
4.3
that

\|f_{j}\|_{B_{0},2}\leq C_{2}\frac{\delta(\rho_{0})}{(j+1)^{2}},\quad j=0,1,2,\dots.

Therefore by Lemma
4.1
, we have
\|\rho_{j}-\rho_{0}\|_{\mathcal{U},2}\leq\varepsilon(D_{0})
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\rho_{j}
converges in the
C^{2}
norm on
B_{0}
to
\rho
, with
F(D_{0})=\{z\in\mathcal{U},\rho(z)<0\}
. By our choice of
\varepsilon(D_{0})
(see the remark after Lemma
4.2
),
F(D_{0})
is strictly pseudoconvex.
∎

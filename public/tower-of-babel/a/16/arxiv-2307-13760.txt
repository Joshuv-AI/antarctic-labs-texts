Carrollian limit of quadratic gravity
Poula Tadros, Ivan Kolář

Abstract
We study the Carrollian limit of the (general) quadratic gravity in four dimensions. We find that in order for the Carrollian theory to be a modification of the Carrollian limit of general relativity, the parameters in the action must depend on the speed of light in a specific way. By focusing on the leading and the next-to-leading orders in the Carrollian expansion, we show that there are four such nonequivalent Carrollian theories. Imposing conditions to remove tachyons (from the linearized theory), we end up with a classification of Carrollian theories according to the leading-order and next-to-leading-order actions. All modify the Carrollian limit of general relativity with quartic terms of the extrinsic curvature. To the leading order, we show that two theories are equivalent to general relativity, one to R + R 2 R+R^{2} theory, and one to the general quadratic gravity. To the next-to-leading order, two are equivalent to R + R 2 R+R^{2} while the other two to the general quadratic gravity. We study the two theories that are equivalent to R + R 2 R+R^{2} to the leading order and write their magnetic limit actions.

II
Pre-ultralocal parametrization

The
pre-ultralocal (PUL) parametrization
is a parametrization of the metric on a manifold using the decomposition of its tangent bundle into a vertical and horizontal subbundles (see below). It is the most convenient parametrization of the spacetime for the analysis of Carrollian gravity since it is well adapted to the ultralocal structure of the Carrollian limit and it displays the speed of light
c
explicitly, which makes the calculations more obvious. In what follows, we briefly explain the mathematical background of the PUL parametrization. By following the calculations and notations from
Hansen et al. 2022
, we present the PUL version of the Riemannian tensor which will be used to calculate terms in quadratic gravity action in later sections.

Let
(M,\boldsymbol{g})
be a
{(d+1)}
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold (with mostly positive signature). Let us denote the tangent bundle of
M
by
TM
and define two sub-bundles of
TM
according to the signature of the metric: The first is called the
vertical bundle

\mathrm{Ver}M
(or the timelike bundle) and it corresponds to the timelike direction, i.e., its fibers are endowed with a vector space isomorphic to the time coordinate. The second is referred to as the
horizontal bundle

\mathrm{Hor}M
(or the spatial bundle) and it represents the remaining
d
spacelike directions. It is easy to prove that
{TM=\mathrm{Ver}M\oplus\mathrm{Hor}M}
. Furthermore, it generates a foliation of the manifold whose slices are the sub-manifolds of a constant time coordinate
t
. This foliation allows us to define orthogonal spatial and timelike sections as follows: Consider a covector
T_{\mu}
and a vector
V^{\mu}
from
\mathrm{Ver}M
, where
{\mu,\nu,\ldots=1,2,\dots,d+1}
are tensor indices in
TM
. Next, we consider a symmetric tensor
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
from
\mathrm{Hor}M
, which is the induced metric (or the first fundamental form), and its inverse
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
.

By construction of the sub-bundles and the foliation we have

\displaystyle T_{\mu}V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-1,

(1a)

\displaystyle-V^{\mu}T_{\nu}+\Pi^{\rho\mu}\Pi_{\rho\nu}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu}^{\nu},

(1b)

\displaystyle T_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0,

(1c)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}V^{\nu}

\displaystyle=0.

(1d)

The PUL parametrization of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-c^{2}T_{\mu}T_{\nu}+\Pi_{\mu\nu},

(2a)

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}V^{\mu}V^{\nu}+\Pi^{\mu\nu}.

(2b)

The metric, its inverse, and the spatial tensors can be written in terms of vielbeins as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\eta_{AB}E^{A}_{\mu}E^{B}_{\nu},

(3a)

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\eta^{AB}\Theta_{A}^{\mu}\Theta_{B}^{\nu},

(3b)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\eta_{ab}E^{a}_{\mu}E^{b}_{\nu},

(3c)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\eta^{ab}\Theta_{a}^{\mu}\Theta_{b}^{\nu},

(3d)

where

and
\Theta_{A}^{\mu}
are the vielbeins. Indices

are vielbein labels running from 1 to

(the dimension of

) while

are vielbein labels running from 1 to
d
(the dimension of the

). Comparing the PUL parametrization with the vielbein definition we get
{E^{A}_{\mu}=(cT_{\mu},E^{a}_{\mu})}
and
{\Theta^{\mu}_{A}=(-c^{-1}V^{\mu},\Theta^{\mu}_{a})}
.

Following
Hansen et al. 2022
, we assume that all fields are analytic in

and expand them as follows:

\displaystyle V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=v^{\mu}+c^{2}M^{\mu}+O(c^{4}),

(4a)

\displaystyle T_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mu}+c^{2}N_{\mu}+O(c^{4}),

(4b)

\displaystyle\Theta_{a}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\theta_{a}^{\mu}+c^{2}\pi_{a}^{\mu}+O(c^{4}),

(4c)

\displaystyle E^{a}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=e^{a}_{\mu}+c^{2}F^{a}_{\mu}+O(c^{4}),

(4d)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h^{\mu\nu}+c^{2}\Phi^{\mu\nu}+O(c^{4}),

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h_{\mu\nu}+c^{2}\Phi_{\mu\nu}+O(c^{4}),

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu}v^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-1,

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}e^{a}_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{b},

III
Carrollian expansion of GR

Having derived the PUL parametrization of the Riemann tensor in (
18
), we can now review the Carrollian expansion of the GR following
Hansen et al. 2022
. Recall that the Einstein-Hilbert action in four dimensions (
d=3
) is,

S=c^{3}\int R\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\;.

(19)

Let us first calculate the PUL parametrization of the Ricci scalar
R
. By contracting
\rho
and
\mu
in (

), we can write the Ricci tensor in the form

\displaystyle R_{\lambda\nu}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\big[-\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\nu\lambda})-2V^{\mu}C^{\sigma}_{[\mu\lambda]}\mathcal{K}_{\nu\sigma}+\mathcal{K}_{\nu\lambda}\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{\mu\lambda}\Pi^{\mu\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{\nu\alpha}\big]+\big[\overset{c}{R}_{\lambda\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}(\Pi^{\mu\alpha}T_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\nu\alpha})-\nabla_{\nu}(T_{\lambda}\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+2C^{\mu}_{[\nu\beta]}\Pi^{\beta\alpha}T_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\alpha}+\mathcal{K}_{(\nu}^{\alpha}B_{\lambda)\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\nu\sigma}T_{\lambda}\Pi^{\sigma\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\nu}\Pi^{\mu\alpha}V^{\sigma}B_{\sigma\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\lambda}\big]

{\nabla_{\rho}\Pi^{\mu\nu}=-V^{(\mu}\Pi^{\nu)\sigma}B_{\sigma\lambda}[\delta_{\rho}^{\lambda}-V^{\lambda}T_{\rho}]}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\big[\mathcal{K}^{2}-\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}-2\nabla_{\nu}(V^{\nu}\mathcal{K})\big]+\big[-\overset{c}{R}-\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\nu}B_{\nu}^{\ \mu})]+c^{2}[\tfrac{1}{4}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\big],

\begin{split}R=\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\big[\mathcal{K}^{2}-\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}\big]+\big[-\overset{c}{R}\big]+c^{2}\big[\tfrac{1}{4}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\big]+\text{boundary terms},\end{split}

(24)

where we collected all the boundary terms from all orders. Note that the boundary terms will be used in the calculation of quadratic curvature terms. (They are not important in this section since we compute the LO of GR.)

Hence, the PUL parameterization of the Einstein-Hilbert action is then

S=c^{2}\int\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\big[\mathcal{K}^{2}-\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}\big]+\big[-\overset{c}{R}\big]+c^{2}\big[\tfrac{1}{4}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\big]Ed^{4}x,

(25)

where
E=\det(T_{\mu},E^{a}_{\mu})
, so after the Carrollian expansion to the LO, i.e. the electric limit, we arrive at

S=c^{2}\int\big[K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\big]ed^{4}x,

(26)

where
K_{\mu\nu}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}h_{\mu\nu}
,
K^{\mu\nu}=h^{\mu\alpha}h^{\nu\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}
and
e=\det(\tau_{\mu},e^{a}_{\mu})
. It is worth mentioning that although this integral is four-dimensional, one should be able to decompose it into a one-dimensional and a three-dimensional integrals. This was done explicitly in the ‘zero signature approach’ for gravity and other field theories
Henneaux 1979
;
Campoleoni et al. 2022
;
Henneaux and Salgado-Rebolledo 2021
. We will prove the equivalence between the two approaches (for any finite order gravity theory) in a future work.

In order to get the field equations, we first vary with respect to
v^{\mu}
and equate to zero,

\tfrac{1}{2}\tau_{\mu}\big[K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}]-h^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}[K_{\mu\nu}-Kh_{\mu\nu}\big]=0.

(27)

Since
\tau_{\mu}
and
h^{\mu\nu}
are independent, we can write two field equations as

\displaystyle K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0,

(28)

\displaystyle h^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\big[K_{\mu\nu}-Kh_{\mu\nu}\big]

\displaystyle=0.

Now varying (
26
) with respect to
h^{\mu\nu}
and equating to zero we get

\tfrac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}\big[K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\big]-KK_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}K^{2}-v^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}(h_{\mu\nu}K-K_{\mu\nu})=0.

(29)

From equation (
29
) the first two terms vanish and we end up with a third field equation,

-KK_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}K^{2}-v^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}(h_{\mu\nu}K-K_{\mu\nu})=0.

(30)

Following
Hansen et al. 2022
, we can rearrange the last equation into

\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\mu\nu}=-2K_{\mu}^{\alpha}K_{\nu\alpha}+KK_{\mu\nu}.

(31)

The left-hand side is the Lie derivative of the extrinsic curvature
K_{\mu\nu}
of the leaves of the foliation (equal time submanifolds) with respect to
\boldsymbol{v}
. Thus it tells us about the evolution of each point as we move along the integral curves of
v
, i.e., the time evolution. Other than that the equation only depends on the extrinsic curvature
K_{\mu\nu}
(Lie derivative of the induced metric
h_{\mu\nu}
), so the evolution of the spacetime in GR is ultralocal. However, unlike the nonrelativistic limit
Hansen et al. 2021
, the three field equations at the LO are nontrivial and define a distinct theory. In order to support motion we need to go to the NLO terms, which represent corrections to Carrollian theory towards the full Lorentzian theory of GR.

IV
Carrollian expansion of quadratic gravity

Quadratic gravity is a theory where quadratic curvature terms are added to the action, which makes it renormalizable
Stelle 1977
;
Stelle 1978
. It also emerges from string theory by imposing a cutoff for the maximum possible momenta
Nenmeli et al. 2021
. The action for the theory is given by

S=c^{3}\int\big[R-\alpha R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}+\beta R^{2}\big]\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x.

(32)

In Sec.
III
we computed the PUL parametrization of
R
. Now, we will do the same also for the two other terms in the action,
R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}
and
R^{2}
. Using (
20
), we can find the PUL parametrization of
R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}
,

\displaystyle R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big[\Pi^{\nu\alpha}\Pi^{\lambda\beta}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta})\nabla_{\rho}(V^{\rho}\mathcal{K}_{\nu\lambda})-2\mathcal{K}^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta})+\mathcal{K}^{\lambda\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda\nu}\mathcal{K}^{2}

(33)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-V^{\mu}V^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{K}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{K}+2\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha\beta}V^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{K}-(\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu})^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\big[-2\overset{c}{R}{}^{\lambda\nu}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda\nu})-\Pi^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta})\mathcal{K}^{\rho\beta}B_{\nu\rho}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Pi^{\lambda\beta}\mathcal{K}^{\rho\alpha}B_{\lambda\rho}V^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+2\overset{c}{R}_{\lambda\nu}\mathcal{K}^{\lambda\nu}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}^{\lambda\nu}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha}_{\lambda}B_{\nu\alpha}-\Pi^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\nabla_{\rho}\mathcal{K}^{\rho}_{\nu}+2\Pi^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+V^{\lambda}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\mathcal{K}^{\rho\alpha}B_{\lambda\rho}-\Pi^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{K}\nabla_{\alpha}\mathcal{K}-V^{\lambda}\nabla_{\alpha}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha\epsilon}B_{\lambda\epsilon}-2V^{\nu}V^{\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}(\Pi^{\mu\beta}B_{\alpha\beta})\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-V^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}^{\sigma\lambda}B_{\sigma\alpha}V^{\rho}\mathcal{K}^{\beta}_{\lambda}B_{\rho\beta}+2V^{\lambda}\nabla_{\mu}(\Pi^{\mu\rho}(dT_{\lambda\rho}))\mathcal{K}^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta}-B^{\lambda\nu}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda\nu})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\Pi^{\beta\nu}\Pi^{\alpha\lambda}B_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda\nu}\mathcal{K}\big]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\beta}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}-\Pi^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\mathcal{K}\nabla_{\rho}(\Pi^{\rho\beta}B_{\nu\beta})-\tfrac{1}{4}V^{\sigma}\mathcal{K}^{\nu\rho}B_{\sigma\rho}\nabla_{\mu}(\Pi^{\mu\alpha}B_{\nu\alpha})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\tfrac{1}{2}V^{\lambda}V^{\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}(\Pi^{\mu\beta}B_{\alpha\beta})\nabla_{\nu}(\Pi^{\nu\rho}B_{\lambda\rho})+\tfrac{3}{2}\overset{c}{R}{}^{\alpha\lambda}\mathcal{K}^{\beta}_{\lambda}B_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\overset{c}{R}{}^{\mu\nu}\overset{c}{R}_{\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{4}\mathcal{K}_{\beta\lambda}\mathcal{K}^{\rho\lambda}B^{\nu\beta}B_{\nu\rho}+\tfrac{1}{4}\mathcal{K}^{\beta\lambda}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha\rho}B_{\alpha\beta}B_{\lambda\rho}\big]

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+c^{4}\big[\tfrac{1}{16}(B^{\alpha\beta}B_{\alpha\beta})^{2}\big].

By expanding this expression to the LO, we arrive at

\displaystyle R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big[h^{\nu\alpha}h^{\lambda\beta}\nabla_{\mu}(v^{\mu}K_{\alpha\beta})\nabla_{\rho}(v^{\rho}K_{\nu\lambda})-2K^{\alpha\beta}K\nabla_{\mu}(v^{\mu}K_{\alpha\beta})+K^{\lambda\nu}K_{\lambda\nu}K^{2}

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-v^{\lambda}v^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}K\nabla_{\nu}K+2K_{\alpha\beta}K^{\alpha\beta}v^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}K-(K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu})^{2}\big].

The PUL parametrization of
R^{2}
can be computed from (
22
),

\displaystyle R^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big[\mathcal{K}^{4}-2\mathcal{K}^{2}\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}-4\mathcal{K}^{2}\nabla_{\nu}(V^{\nu}\mathcal{K})+(\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu})^{2}+4\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}(V^{\nu}\mathcal{K})+4\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K})\nabla_{\nu}(V^{\nu}\mathcal{K})\big]

(35)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\big[-\mathcal{K}^{2}\overset{c}{R}-\mathcal{K}^{2}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\lambda}B_{\lambda}^{\ \mu})+\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\overset{c}{R}+\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}(V^{\lambda}B_{\lambda}^{\ \rho})+2\overset{c}{R}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+2\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\mu}\mathcal{K})\nabla_{\nu}(V^{\lambda}B_{\lambda}^{\ \nu})\big]+\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\nu}B^{\sigma\rho}B_{\sigma\rho}-B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}(V^{\rho}\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\big(\overset{c}{R}\big)^{2}+\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\lambda}B_{\lambda}^{\ \mu})\nabla_{\rho}(V^{\sigma}B_{\sigma}^{\ \rho})+2\overset{c}{R}\nabla_{\mu}(V^{\lambda}B_{\lambda}^{\ \mu})\big]+c^{2}\big[-\tfrac{1}{4}\overset{c}{R}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}(V^{\sigma}B_{\sigma}^{\ \rho})\big]+c^{4}\big[\tfrac{1}{16}(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu})^{2}\big],

and its Carrollian expansion to the LO is

\begin{split}R^{2}&=\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big[K^{4}-2K^{2}K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}-4K^{2}\nabla_{\nu}(v^{\nu}K)+(K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu})^{2}+4K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}(v^{\nu}K)+4\nabla_{\mu}(v^{\mu}K)\nabla_{\nu}(v^{\nu}K)\big].\end{split}

(36)

Substituting (
24
), (
34
), (
36
) into the action (
32
) we get

\displaystyle S=\int

\displaystyle\big\{c^{2}\big[K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\big]-\alpha\big[h^{\nu\alpha}h^{\lambda\beta}\nabla_{\mu}(v^{\mu}K_{\alpha\beta})\nabla_{\rho}(v^{\rho}K_{\nu\lambda})-2K^{\alpha\beta}K\nabla_{\mu}(v^{\mu}K_{\alpha\beta})+K^{\lambda\nu}K_{\lambda\nu}K^{2}

(37)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-v^{\mu}v^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}K\nabla_{\nu}K+2K_{\alpha\beta}K^{\alpha\beta}v^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}K-(K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu})^{2}\big]+\beta\big[K^{4}-2K^{2}K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-4K^{2}\nabla_{\nu}(v^{\nu}K)+(K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu})^{2}+4K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}(v^{\rho}K)+4\nabla_{\mu}(v^{\mu}K)\nabla_{\nu}(v^{\nu}K)\big]\big\}ed^{4}x.

Interestingly, this formula can be rewritten purely the extrinsic curvature
K_{\mu\nu}
and its Lie derivatives along
\boldsymbol{v}
. In order to do that, we first write

\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\mu\nu}=v^{\sigma}\nabla_{\sigma}K_{\mu\nu}+K_{\sigma\nu}\nabla_{\mu}v^{\sigma}+K_{\sigma\mu}\nabla_{\nu}v^{\sigma}-K_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\sigma}v^{\sigma}+v^{\sigma}T^{\rho}_{\sigma\mu}K_{\rho\nu}+v^{\sigma}T^{\rho}_{\sigma\nu}K_{\rho\mu},

(38)

where
T^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}
is the torsion of the connection defined in Sec.
II
. Since the PUL-parameterization vector
v^{\mu}
is covariantly constant by definition and
T^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}
is given by (
14
), the relation reduces to

\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\mu\nu}=v^{\sigma}\nabla_{\sigma}K_{\mu\nu}-K^{\sigma}_{(\mu}K_{\nu)\sigma}.

(39)

Substituting in (
37
) and using the fact that
v^{\sigma}\nabla_{\sigma}
acts on scalars simply as
\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}
, we get

\displaystyle S=\int

\displaystyle\big\{c^{2}\big[K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\big]-\alpha\big[h^{\nu\alpha}h^{\lambda\beta}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\alpha\beta}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\nu\lambda}+2\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\nu\lambda}K^{\sigma(\nu}K^{\lambda)}_{\sigma}

(40)

\displaystyle+K^{\sigma}_{(\alpha}K_{\beta)\sigma}K^{\rho(\alpha}K^{\beta)}_{\rho}-2K^{\alpha\beta}K\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K_{\alpha\beta}-2K^{\alpha\beta}KK^{\sigma}_{(\alpha}K_{\beta)\sigma}+K^{2}K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle-(\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K)^{2}+2K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K-(K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu})^{2}\big]+\beta\big[K^{4}-2K^{2}K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle-4K^{2}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K+(K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu})^{2}+4K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K+(\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}K)^{2}\big]\big\}ed^{4}x.

Note that only the first two terms have the factor
c^{2}
. Thus, assuming
\alpha
and
\beta
being independent of
c
, the Carrollian limit of the theory would exclude the first two terms coming from the Carrollian limit of the Ricci scalar. This means that the resulting theory would not couple to
R
and it will be drastically different from the Carrollian limit of GR [cf. (
26
)]. Hence,
\alpha
and
\beta
should depend on
c
. In this case, we get an infinite number of nonequivalent Carrollian theories, but only four of them modify GR to LO or NLO. Notice that this limit is, as expected, ultralocal since there are no space derivatives in the Lagrangian, and therefore, there would not be space derivatives in the field equations. This means that the evolution of a point cannot be affected by neighboring points no matter how close they are.

Similar calculations were done in
Niedermaier 2023
by rescaling specific terms in the action. However, our approach gives more freedom to rescale terms differently and gives more nonequivalent theories. Other papers considered specific solutions for
f(R)
gravity
Abebe 2014
;
Abebe 2016
;
Abebe and Elmardi 2015
. A general classification of theories for the most general quadratic gravity theory will be provided in the next section.

VI
The magnetic limit

In this section we study the magnetic limit of the theories (2,4) and (4,2) because these two theories are free from tachyons and ghosts if
\beta^{\prime}\leq 0

VII
Conclusions

Appendix A
Mathematical Overview and relation to black hole physics

The Carroll algebra is given by Carrollian limit of the Poincaré group, also considered as the ultralocal Inönü-Wigner contraction for the Poincaré group
Inonu and Wigner 1953
. It was first constructed independently by Levy-Leblond
Levy-Leblond 1965
and Sen Gupta
Sen Gupta 1966
as the limit of the Poincaré group as the speed of light tends to zero or equivalently, when the time separation is much less than the space separation. In this limit the light cone converges into a line making any motion of particles with nonzero energy impossible. However, due to the lack of physical applications in this limit, the Carroll group and the geometry associated with it, i.e. Carrollian geometry, were studied solely by mathematicians and mathematical physicist for a long time. The study of Carrollian physics by physicists began when a connection between the Carrollian limit and physics near black-hole horizons was established
Donnay and Marteau 2019
. In their paper they showed that any null hypersurface is endowed with a Carrollian structure. Since then many papers came out studying Carrollian limit of GR, dynamics of particles near black-hole horizons, and the geometry on horizons of different gravity theories as well as mathematical aspects
Figueroa-O’Farrill et al. 2022a
;
Figueroa-O’Farrill et al. 2022b
;
Figueroa-O’Farrill 2023
;
Freidel and Jai-akson 2022
;
Herfray 2022
;
Morand 2020
;
Campoleoni et al. 2022
. For a review on Carrollian geometry and its relation with Galilean geometry i.e. Newton-Cartan geometry see
Bergshoeff et al. 2017
. In this appendix, we review the mathematical structures and properties of the Carroll group and the Carrollian geometric structures and their relation to black holes.

Beginning with the Poincaré group

Appendix B
Carrollian transformations and symmetries

In this appendix, we review the notions of Carrollian transformations, compare it to the Galilean transformations, then define Carrollian symmetries on a Carrollian manifold.

B.1
Galilean and Carrollian transformations

In this section we give a quick description of the Galilean and Carrollian transformations on a flat spacetime. We begin with the familiar Galilean boosts

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=x+vt,

(77a)

\displaystyle t^{\prime}=t,

(77b)

where
x
denotes the space coordinates,
t
is the time and
v
is the velocity. Along with space rotations, these transformations form the Galilean algebra which features an absolute time and no upper bound for the velocity. The Galilean algebra can be deduced from the Poincaré algebra by Inönü-Wigner contraction with
c\rightarrow\infty
. This aligns with the light cone opening up discussed in the previous appendix.

Carrollian boosts are defined by interchanging space and time in the Galilean boosts i.e.

\displaystyle t^{\prime}=t+vx,

(78a)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=x.

(78b)

These boosts feature an absolute space and a zero spatial velocity. Along with space rotation, it forms the Carrollian algebra (
73
). As discussed, it is the result of the opposite Inönü-Wigner contraction of the Galilean one i.e.
c\rightarrow 0
.

B.2
Carrollian symmetries on a general Carrollian manifold

Here, we take a look on how to generalise (
78
) to a General curved Carrollian manifold.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Carrollian manifold with
h_{\mu\nu}
being the components of the induced metric and
v^{\mu}
be the preferred ”time” vector field. For the boosts to preserve the Carrollian structure, they must leave
h_{\mu\nu}
and
v^{\mu}
invariant but not necessarily the inverse metric
h^{\mu\nu}
nor the one form associated to
v^{\mu}
which we call
\tau_{\mu}
.

Firstly, we define an analog of the velocity by the action of the boost on
\tau_{\mu}
i.e.

\delta\tau_{\mu}=\lambda_{\mu}(x),

(79)

where
\lambda_{\mu}
depends on the position on the spatial slice it is defined in as well as the slice itself. It is easy to see from (
6
) that
v^{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}=0
i.e. it is a spatial covector. Using this definition, by performing the Inönü-Wigner contraction, the Carrollian boosts on
h^{\mu\nu}
is given by

\delta h^{\mu\nu}=h^{\mu\rho}\lambda_{\rho}v^{\nu}+h^{\nu\rho}\lambda_{\rho}v^{\mu}.

(80)

These symmetries are called the Carrollian symmetries. Every Carrollian theory must have those symmetries, otherwise, the theories could not have been defined on a Carrollian manifold in the first place.

B.3

Here, we explain why the truncation of the NLO of gravity theories are crucial to get a Carrollian theory. In the LO, straightforward calculations using (

) to check that
K_{\mu\nu}
,

and

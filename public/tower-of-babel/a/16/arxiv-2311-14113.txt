𝑼 ⁡ ( 𝑵 ) U(N) Torus Link Invariants in the Large 𝑵 N limit from Matrix Model Approach
Archana Maji, Kushal Chakraborty, Suvankar Dutta, P. Ramadevi

Abstract
In this paper we study U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) colored HOMFLY-PT polynomials of torus links in the double scaling limit (polynomial variable q → 1 q\rightarrow 1 , N → ∞ N\rightarrow\infty keeping q N q^{N} fixed). We show that, in this limit, the colored HOMFLY-PT polynomial of any ( L ​ α , L ​ β ) (L\alpha,L\beta) torus link can be expressed in terms of the colored HOMFLY-PT polynomial of ( L , L ) (L,L) torus link. Using the connection between matrix models and the Chern-Simons field theoretic invariants, we show that the colored torus link invariants are uniquely expressed in terms of connected correlation functions of operators in U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) matrix model. We determine the leading and subleading contribution to some of the correlators at large N N from the matrix model approach and find that they match exactly with those obtained from the corresponding colored HOMFLY-PT polynomials.

Introduction

The famous discovery of Jones polynomial
jones1985polynomial
;
jones1987hecke
by Vaughan Jones in 1984 led to a rejuvenation of the mathematical theory of knots and links. A knot
\mathcal{K}
is an embedding of a circle
S^{1}
inside a three-manifold
M
whereas a link
\mathcal{L}
is a collection of two or more knots linked in a nontrivial way. Two knots
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
are considered to be equivalent if one can be deformed to another through any of the three Reidemeister moves. The central problem of knot theory is to classify different knots and links. To address this classification problem there comes various polynomial invariants viz Alexander polynomial
alexander1928topological
, Jones polynomial, HOMFLY-PT polynomial
freyd1985new
;
przytycki2016invariants

et cetra
in the order of increasing sophistication.

Knot theory has become of spectacular interest to physicists ever since Witten’s seminal work giving an intrinsically three dimensional definition of Jones polynomial from the perspective of quantum field theory
witten1989quantum
. The Chern-Simons theory, a three dimensional gauge theory with an underlying gauge group
G
provides a natural framework to compute the topological invariants of three-manifolds and of knots and links embedded inside those three-manifolds. In particular, the correlation function of the observables of Chern-Simons theory, viz. Wilson loop operators, is nothing but Jones polynomial when the gauge group at consideration is
SU(2)
and the representations put on the Wilson loops are that of the fundamental representation
\bigg(R\equiv\text{\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{&\opttoksa@YT={\font@YT}\getcolor@YT{\save@YT{\opttoksb@YT}}\nil@YT\getcolor@YT{\startbox@@YT\the\opttoksa@YT\the\opttoksb@YT}#\endbox@YT\cr\lower 0.39993pt\vbox{\kern 0.19997pt\hbox{\kern 0.39993pt\vbox to8.4pt{\vss\hbox to8.00006pt{\hss$\scriptstyle$\hss}\vss}\kern-8.4pt\vrule height=8.4pt,width=0.39993pt\kern 8.00006pt\vrule height=8.4pt,width=0.39993pt}\kern-0.19997pt\kern-8.4pt\hrule width=8.79993pt,height=0.39993pt\kern 8.00006pt\hrule width=8.79993pt,height=0.39993pt}\cr}}\kern 345.4pt}}$}}\bigg)
of
SU(2)
. Likewise, with the fundamental representations of
G=SU(N)
and
SO(N)
we obtain HOMFLY-PT polynomial and Kauffman polynomial
kauffman1990invariant
respectively. If other higher dimensional representations are placed on the Wilson loops then we obtain the corresponding colored polynomials.

In this work we confine ourselves to a class of links called torus links that can be embedded on the surface of a torus in such a way that they do not cross over themselves. Any
L
component torus link can be denoted as
(L\alpha,L\beta)
where
L\alpha
and
L\beta
denote the number of times the link wraps around the meridian and the longitude of the torus respectively. Here
\alpha
and
\beta
are coprime to each other. These torus links can be obtained as a closure of
L\alpha
strand braid with
(L\alpha-1)L\beta
crossings. We will study these torus link invariants in the double scaling limit of Chern-Simons theory associated with gauge group
U(N)
. This limit is defined as follows

\displaystyle N,k\rightarrow\infty~~~\text{such that}~~\lambda=\frac{N}{N+k}=\textrm{fixed},

(1)

where
k
is the coupling constant of the Chern-Simons theory. In terms of the variable
q=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{k+N}\right)
the above limit can be rephrased as

\displaystyle q\rightarrow 1,N\rightarrow\infty~~~\textrm{such that}~q^{N}=\textrm{fixed}.

(2)

By two of the authors, it has been shown that in this limit the invariants for a specific set of torus links viz.
(2,2m)
torus links can be expressed in terms of
(2,2)
torus link (also called Hopf link) invariant
chakraborty2022large
. Interestingly, this result can be generalised for any arbitrary
L
component torus link. In particular, we show that in the double scaling limit we can express the
(L\alpha,L\beta)
torus link invariant in terms of
(L,L)
torus link invariant.

Although the partition function of Chern-Simons theory can be obtained for some three-manifolds, the calculation of correlation functions is difficult in general. However, in the double scaling limit one can use the saddle point approximation to compute some of the correlation functions. In particular, the correlation function that corresponds to
(2,2)
torus link invariant was computed in Ref.
chakraborty2022large
by mapping the problem to that of an incompressible fluid with intial and final fluid densities being related to the representations placed on the two component knots. In this paper we further study the correlation functions corresponding to
(L,L)
torus link in the double scaling limit, for any arbitrary
L
. We obtain an exact expression for the leading contribution of such link invariants using the saddle point approximation
Chakraborty:2021oyq
;
chakraborty2022large
. We validate this result by explicitly computing the colored HOMFLY-PT polynomials for
(3,3)
torus link carrying same/different representations on its three components.

It is well known in knot theory, that all knot invariants reduce to unknot invariant in the double scaling limit. Similarly any
L
component link invariant reduces to the product of
L
unknot invariants. The intertwining between the components is not captured in this limit. These observations are also seen in the matrix model results using saddle point approximation. Specifically, the leading order contribution to any
n
-point correlator in the large
N
limit is the product of
n
one-point correlators. Hence, we need to investigate the subleading corrections to capture the intertwining between the component knots constituting the link.
The subleading corrections to the knot invariants are captured by the connected piece of correlation functions in the matrix model approach. We use the techniques developed in Ref.
eynard2015random
;
Ambjorn:1992gw
to compute the connected pieces. We then validate the results for the subleading contributions of Hopf link invariants for some specific representations placed on the components of the link. Further, we show that
any
knot or link invariant modulo the leading contribution (i.e. subtracting the leading contribution from the link invariant) can be uniquely written in terms of connected correlation function of operators in
U(N)
matrix model. These connected correlators are related to the
reformulated invariants
(which are written in terms of the colored HOMFLY-PT polynomials)
ramadevi2012reformulated
.

Link invariants in

Chern-Simons theory

The three dimensional Chern-Simons theory on a manifold

U(1)
Chern-Simons actions

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}\text{Tr}\bigg(A\wedge dA+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\bigg)+\frac{k_{1}}{4\pi}\int_{M}\text{Tr}\bigg(B\wedge dB\bigg)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}d^{3}x~\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}~\text{Tr}\left(A_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\rho}+\frac{2}{3}A_{\mu}A_{\nu}A_{\rho}\right)+\frac{k_{1}}{4\pi}\int_{M}d^{3}x~\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}~\text{Tr}\left(B_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}B_{\rho}\right)

(3)

where

are the gauge connections for the gauge groups

,

\displaystyle Z^{U(N)}[M]

\displaystyle=\int_{M}[\mathcal{D}A][\mathcal{D}B]e^{iS}\equiv Z^{SU(N)}[M]\times Z^{U(1)}[M].

(4)

As the action is explicitly metric independent, the corresponding partition function is a topological invariant of the three-manifold
M
. The other topological invariants associated with links can be described by expectation value of Wilson loop operators in Chern-Simons theory. For an oriented knot
\mathcal{K}
in an irreducible representation
\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle W_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\text{Tr}_{R}\mathbb{H}^{(A)}[\mathcal{K}]\bigg)\bigg(\text{Tr}_{n}\mathbb{H}^{(B)}[\mathcal{K}]\bigg)

(5)

where,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{(A)}[\mathcal{K}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}\bigg[\exp\oint_{\mathcal{K}}A\bigg]~~\text{and}~~\mathbb{H}^{(B)}[\mathcal{K}]=\mathcal{P}\bigg[\exp\oint_{\mathcal{K}}B\bigg]

(6)

are the holonomies of
SU(N)
and
U(1)
gauge connection respectively. The symbol
\mathcal{P}
denotes path ordering. Note that the representation
\mathcal{R}\in U(N)
involves representation
R\in SU(N)

U(N)
representation

\displaystyle n_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}l_{i}+s,~~~~i=1,...,N-1\\
s~~~~,~~~~~~i=N\end{cases}

(7)

where

(

row corresponding to the Young diagram of

(
U(N)
) representation
R
(
\mathcal{R}
) and

. The relation between

If instead we have a link

\displaystyle W_{\mathcal{R}_{1},\cdots\mathcal{R}_{L}}[\mathcal{L}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{L}W_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}[\mathcal{K}_{a}].

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{R}_{1},...,\mathcal{R}_{L}}({M},k,k_{1})

\displaystyle=\langle{W}_{\mathcal{R}_{1},...,\mathcal{R}_{L}}[\mathcal{L}]\rangle=\frac{1}{Z(M)}\int_{M}[\mathcal{D}A][\mathcal{D}B]\bigg(\prod_{a=1}^{L}{W}_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}[\mathcal{K}_{a}]\bigg)e^{iS}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{L}}_{n^{(1)},\cdots,n^{(L)}}(M,k_{1})\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{L}}_{R_{1},\cdots,R_{L}}(M,k).

These link invariants can be computed using one of the two framings:

\bullet

where the self-linking numbers of the component knots are zero,

\bullet

In fact, the link invariants in standard framing are unchanged under all the three Reidemeister moves and are called

In this work, we will confine ourselves to framed links [

\displaystyle q^{(f_{a}-\alpha\beta)\kappa_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}}\bigg)\sum_{\mathcal{R}_{t}\vdash\alpha(n^{(1)}+\cdots+n^{(L)})}\zeta^{\mathcal{R}_{t}}_{\mathcal{R}_{1},...,\mathcal{R}_{L}}q^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\kappa_{\mathcal{R}_{t}}}s^{\star}_{\mathcal{R}_{t}}(q,v)

(31)

where

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mathcal{R}_{t}}_{\mathcal{R}_{1},...,\mathcal{R}_{L}}=\prod_{a=1}^{L}\left(\sum_{\Lambda^{(a)}\vdash n^{(a)}}\frac{|c_{\Lambda^{(a)}}|}{(n^{(a)})!}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}(c_{\Lambda^{(a)}})\right)\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{t}}(c_{(\Lambda^{(1)}+\cdots+\Lambda^{(L)})_{(\alpha)}}).

4
Torus link invariants in the double scaling limit of

Chern-Simons theory

As discussed in the introduction, the double scaling limit (

(33)

For the representation

we define a set of
N

\{\theta_{1}^{(a)},...,\theta_{N}^{(a)}\}

\displaystyle\theta_{i}^{(a)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{N+k}\bigg(h_{i}^{(a)}-\frac{N-1}{2}\bigg),

(34)

\displaystyle\text{where,}\quad h_{i}^{(a)}

\displaystyle=n_{i}^{(a)}+N-i.

(35)

It is easy to check that in the double scaling limit the variables
\theta_{i}^{(a)}

-\pi
to
\pi

\mathcal{R}_{a}

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(N+k)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\sum_{i}{\theta_{i}^{(a)}}^{2}-\frac{N(N^{2}-1)}{24}.

In the large

\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\theta^{(a)}_{i}\rightarrow\theta^{(a)}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~\text{where}~~x=\frac{i}{N}\in[0,1].

(37)

All the discrete summations over

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}=\int_{0}^{1}dx.

(38)

Moreover the distribution of
\theta_{i}

such that

. Hence, for any arbitrary functions of
\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}f(\theta_{i})\rightarrow\int_{0}^{1}dxf(\theta(x))=\int d\theta\ \sigma(\theta)f(\theta).

(40)

Now we will incorporate the continuum limit discussed above for the

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(S^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},k)=\int\prod_{i}d\theta_{i}\prod_{i<j}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}}{2}\right)e^{-(N+k)^{2}S[\theta]},

(68)

where

S[\theta]=\frac{p\lambda}{N\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\theta_{i}^{2}}{4}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)+\frac{\pi p\lambda(1-\lambda)}{12}.

(69)

Note that, in order to arrive at the above result we have replaced
p
in equation (
56
) by
-ip
.
The normalised Hopf link invariant (
57
) is therefore given as the integral of two Schur polynomials
\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}(U)
and
\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(U)
over the unitary ensemble with the aforementioned weight factor

\displaystyle{{\mathscr{V}}}^{(2,2)}_{\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}}(S^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}(S^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},k)}\int[DU]e^{-(N+k)^{2}S[\theta]}\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}(U)\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(U)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}(U)\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(U)\right\rangle.

(70)

For large
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
(i.e. when the number of boxes
n_{i}^{(1)}
and
n_{i}^{(2)}
become of order
N
or
k
, in the double scaling limit) it is difficult to calculate the correlation function even using the saddle point approximation. However, if
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
are small, one can find the leading order contribution to
{\mathscr{V}}^{(2,2)}_{\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}}(S^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{p},k)
by computing
\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}(U)
and
\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(U)
on the classical solution that extremizes the partition function (
56
).

5.1
Leading contribution to

For a (2,2) torus link invariant when the “size” of

and

placed on the component knots are much smaller than

and
~\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(U)

Let us define a distribution function for the variables

in the large
N

\rho(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(\theta-\theta_{i}),\quad\text{such that}\quad\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta\rho(\theta)=1.

(71)

It was shown in Ref.
Chakraborty:2021oyq
that in the double scaling limit (
1

2
2

2

In this paper we have used the no-cap solution. In the double scaling limit CS theory admits a phase transition at some critical value of

\displaystyle\rho(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2\pi^{2}\lambda}\tanh^{-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{e^{-2\pi\lambda/p}}{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}},\quad\text{with}~-2\sec^{-1}e^{\pi\lambda/p}<\theta<2\sec^{-1}e^{\pi\lambda/p}

Therefore in the probe approximation the Hopf link invariant (

The Schur polynomial admits a character expansion

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}(U)=\sum_{\vec{k}^{(a)}}\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}(c(\vec{k}^{(a)}))}{z_{\vec{k}^{(a)}}}\Upsilon_{\vec{k}^{(a)}}(U),

\displaystyle=\left(k^{(a)}_{1},k^{(a)}_{2},k^{(a)}_{3},...\right)~\text{such that}~\sum_{r}rk^{(a)}_{r}=n^{(a)},

n^{(a)}
being the total number of boxes in representation
\mathcal{R}_{a}

In the large

Bayesian model averaging for nuclear symmetry energy from effective proton-neutron chemical potential difference of neutron-rich nuclei
Mengying Qiu, Bao-Jun Cai, Lie-Wen Chen, Cen-Xi Yuan, Zhen Zhang

Abstract
The data-driven Bayesian model averaging is a rigorous statistical approach to combining multiple models for a unified prediction. Compared with the individual model, it provides more reliable information, especially for problems involving apparent model dependence. In this work, within both the non-relativistic Skyrme energy density functional and the nonlinear relativistic mean field model, the effective proton-neutron chemical potential difference Δ ​ μ pn ∗ \Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}} of neutron-rich nuclei is found to be strongly sensitive to the symmetry energy E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\rm{sym}}(\rho) around 2 ​ ρ 0 / 3 2\rho_{0}/3 , with ρ 0 \rho_{0} being the nuclear saturation density. Given discrepancies on the Δ ​ μ pn ∗ \Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}} - E sym ​ ( 2 ​ ρ 0 / 3 ) E_{\rm{sym}}(2\rho_{0}/3) correlations between the two models, we carry out a Bayesian model averaging analysis based on Gaussian process emulators to extract the symmetry energy around 2 ​ ρ 0 / 3 2\rho_{0}/3 from the measured Δ ​ μ pn ∗ \Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}} of 5 doubly magic nuclei 48 Ca, 68 Ni, 88 Sr, 132 Sn and 208 Pb. Specifically, the E sym ​ ( 2 ​ ρ 0 / 3 ) E_{\mathrm{sym}}(2\rho_{0}/3) is inferred to be E sym ​ ( 2 ​ ρ 0 / 3 ) = 25.6 − 1.3 + 1.4 ​ MeV E_{\mathrm{sym}}(2\rho_{0}/3)=25.6_{-1.3}^{+1.4}\,\mathrm{MeV} at 1 ​ σ 1\sigma confidence level. The obtained constraints on the E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho) around 2 ​ ρ 0 / 3 2\rho_{0}/3 agree well with microscopic predictions and results from other isovector indicators.



As nuclear symmetry energy
E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho)
governs the isospin-dependence of nuclear matter equation of state (EOS), its density dependence has long been a subject of intense study in nuclear physics and astrophysics
Baran et al. 2005
;
Steiner et al. 2005
;
Lattimer and Prakash 2007
;
Li et al. 2008
. Thanks to the rapid development in terrestrial nuclear experiments and astrophysical observations, great progress has been achieved recently in exploring the density dependence of the symmetry energy
Baldo and Burgio 2016
;
Oertel et al. 2017
;
Roca-Maza and Paar 2018
;
Li et al. 2021
. However, various challenges emerge concurrently. For example, very recently, the PREX-2 and CREX collaborations have respectively measured the neutron skin thickness (one of the most important probes of the density dependence of symmetry energy) in
208
Pb and
48
Ca with minimum strong-interaction model dependence
Adhikari et al. 2021
;
Adhikari et al. 2022
. Unfortunately, the PREX-2 and CREX results exhibit a significant tension, which challenges current theoretical models and our understanding on the density dependence of the symmetry energy
Reed et al. 2021
;
Piekarewicz 2021
;
Reinhard et al. 2021
;
Reinhard et al. 2022
;
Yüksel and Paar 2023
;
Zhang and Chen 2023
.
The accurate determination of the density dependence of symmetry energy
remains an open challenge.

An important issue in the study of nuclear symmetry energy is the model dependence. Although there are some isovector indicators, like the neutron skin thickness
Brown 2000
;
Typel and Brown 2001
;
Furnstahl 2002
;
Chen et al. 2005
and the electric dipole polarizability
Roca-Maza et al. 2013
;
Zhang and Chen 2014
;
Zhang and Chen 2015
;
Roca-Maza et al. 2015
,
whose correlations to the symmetry energy have been proved to be model independent, it is not the case for most of other probes. Analyses with different models on the same observable may result in different and even contradictory conclusions, e.g., the case of charged pion ratio
Xiao et al. 2009
;
Feng and Jin 2010
;
Xie et al. 2013
;
Hong and Danielewicz 2014
;
Cozma 2017
;
Zhang and Ko 2017
;
Jhang et al. 2021
;
Yong 2021
. Even for the widely used nuclear energy density functional theory (EDF), such as the Skyrme EDF and the nonlinear relativistic mean field (RMF) model, there exist systematic discrepancies among their predictions for the saturation properties of nuclear matter
Chen et al. 2007
;
Chen et al. 2009
;
Dutra et al. 2012
;
Dutra et al. 2014
;
Sun et al. 2023
. The model dependence issue hinders the reliable uncertainty quantification of the density dependence of symmetry energy.

As a powerful statistical inference method, the Bayesian approach
has been extensively used to extract physics information from experiments and observations. Within the Bayesian framework, the model dependence issue can be addressed via the Bayesian model averaging(BMA), which mixes predictions of multiple models according to their capabilities of reproducing given data
von Toussaint 2011
;
Kejzlar et al. 2020
;
Jay and Neil 2021
. In nuclear physics studies, the BMA has been successfully used to
predict the neutron drip line using various nuclear EDFs
Neufcourt et al. 2019
and to extract the shear and bulk viscosities of quark-gluon plasma from measurements in heavy-ion collision
Everett et al. 2021
. It has also been proposed to quantify the inter-model uncertainties in some important nuclear physics problems, such as
the nuclear matrix elements governing neutrinoless double-beta decay
Cirigliano et al. 2022
.

In this work, within both the non-relativisitic Skyrme EDF and the nonlinear RMF model, we find the effective proton-neutron chemical potential difference
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
is strongly correlated with the symmetry energy around a subsaturation density of
2\rho_{0}/3
, with
\rho_{0}
being the saturation density. However, there exists systematic discrepancy between the correlations predicted by the two models. Based on Gaussian process (GP) emulators trained respectively with a number of Skyrme EDFs and nonlinear RMF parameter sets, the symmetry energy around
2\rho_{0}/3

are extracted from Bayesian analyses of the
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
in
{}^{48}\text{Ca}
,
{}^{68}\text{Ni}
,
{}^{88}\text{Sr}
,
{}^{132}\text{Sn}
and
{}^{208}\text{Pb}
. Further Bayesian model averaging over the results from the two models gives more reliable constraints on the symmetry energy around
2\rho_{0}/3
by taking into account the model dependence.

The neutron and proton chemical potential in a nucleus with
N
neutrons and
Z
protons can be approximated respectively to be

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial B(N,Z)}{\partial N}\approx\frac{B(N+2,Z)-B(N-2,Z)}{4},

(1)

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial B(N,Z)}{\partial Z}\approx\frac{B(N,Z+2)-B(N,Z-2)}{4},

(2)

with
B(N,Z)
being the binding energy of the nucleus. Although single nucleon separation energy can also be used to approximate the nucleon chemical potential, it suffers from pairing effects, and strong shell effects at magic numbers. Comparing with the single nucleon separation energy, the adopted approximation for
\mu_{\rm{n(p)}}
is relatively free of pairing and shell effects.
The effective proton-neutron chemical potential difference is then defined by

\displaystyle\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[B(N,Z+2)-B(N,Z-2)\right.

(3)

\displaystyle\left.-B(N+2,Z)+B(N-2,Z)\right].

The sensitivity of the
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
to the symmetry energy can be expected from the semi-empirical mass formula

B(N,Z)=a_{\rm{v}}A-a_{\rm{s}}A^{2/3}-a_{\rm{c}}\frac{Z^{2}}{A^{1/3}}-a_{\mathrm{sym}}I^{2}A+E_{\rm{mic}},

where
A=N+Z
is the mass number,
I=(N-Z)/A
is the isospin asymmetry,
a_{\rm{v}}
,
a_{\rm{s}}
,
a_{\rm{c}}
and
a_{\mathrm{sym}}
are the coefficients of volume, surface, Coulomb and asymmetry terms, respectively, and the microscopic correction
E_{\rm{mic}}
includes pairing and shell effects. Considering
2/A
as a small quantity, one can derive

\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}\simeq-2a_{\rm{c}}\frac{Z}{A^{1/3}}+4a_{\mathrm{sym}}I
,
which suggests that the
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
is essentially determined by the symmetry energy of finite nuclei, since the Coulomb coefficient
a_{\rm{c}}\simeq 0.71
MeV
Liu et al. 2011
is well determined. Given the well-confirmed empirical relationship
a_{\mathrm{sym}}\simeq E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho_{A})
at a subsaturation reference density
\rho_{A}
in numerous studies
Centelles et al. 2009
;
Chen 2011
;
Wang et al. 2013
, one can expect that there exist strong correlations between
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
of neutron-rich doubly magic nuclei and
E_{\mathrm{sym}}(\rho)
at subsaturation densities.
We note that the
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
is similar to the neutron-proton Fermi energy difference proposed in Ref.
Wang et al. 2013
. Nevertheless, the
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
can be directly calculated by nuclear energy density functionals and deduced from experimental measured nuclear masses.

To handle with the model dependence of the inferred
E_{\rm{sym}}(2\rho_{0}/3)
, we further carried out Bayesian model averaging
von Toussaint 2011
;
Kejzlar et al. 2020
;
Jay and Neil 2021
over the results from the two models. Within the BMA approach, the posterior distribution of any quantity

\mathcal{O}
is obtained by mixing the posterior predictive distributions
p(\mathcal{O}|\bm{y},\mathcal{M}_{i})
of each individual model
\mathcal{M}_{i}
with a probability weight
p(\mathcal{M}_{i}|\bm{y})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle p\left(\mathcal{O}|\bm{y}\right)=\sum_{i}p(\mathcal{O}|\bm{y},\mathcal{M}_{i})p(\mathcal{M}_{i}|\bm{y}).

(7)

The model weight, reflecting the validity of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
given experimental data
\bm{y}
, is given by

\displaystyle p\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\mid\bm{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p\left(\bm{y}\mid\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)\pi\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)}{\sum_{\ell}p\left(\bm{y}\mid\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\right)\pi\left(\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\right)},

(8)

where the prior model probability
\pi(\mathcal{M}_{i})
reflects our preference on
\mathcal{M}_{i}
before seeing the data, and the evidence
p\left(\bm{y}\mid\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
measures the probability that the model reproduces the experimental data
\bm{y}
with given prior distributions of model parameter and adopted errors
\pi(\theta,\sigma|\mathcal{M}_{i})
, i.e.,

p\left(\bm{y}\mid\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)=\int p(\bm{y}|\theta_{i},\sigma_{i},\mathcal{M}_{i})\pi(\theta,\sigma|\mathcal{M}_{i})d\theta_{i}d\sigma_{i}.

(9)

In our case, in prior, the Skryme and nonlinear RMF models are equally weighted with

\pi(\mathcal{M}_{i})=1/2
, and
the evidences
p\left(\bm{y}\mid\mathcal{M}_{i}\right)
of the two models are estimated with the sequential Monte Carlo method. The obtained ratio of the GP-Sky evidence to that of GP-RMF is estimated to be 3.3, which means the measured
\Delta\mu^{*}_{\rm{pn}}
for the chosen 5 doubly magic nuclei slightly prefers the Skyrme EDF rather than the nonlinear RMF model. Invoking Eqs.(
7
) and (
8
), we calculate the BMA posterior distribution of the
E_{\rm{sym}}(2\rho_{0}/3)
based on those from the Skyrme EDF and nonlinear RMF model, and show it as the purple solid line in Fig.
3
. Quantitatively, the
E_{\rm{sym}}(2\rho_{0}/3)
is inferred to be
25.6_{-1.3}^{+1.4}
MeV at 1
\sigma
confidence level from the BMA analysis.

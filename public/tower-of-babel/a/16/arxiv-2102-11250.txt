On Stability and Convergence of Distributed Filters
Sayed Pouria Talebi, Stefan Werner, Vijay Gupta, Yih-Fang Huang

Abstract
Recent years have bore witness to the proliferation of distributed filtering techniques, where a collection of agents communicating over an ad-hoc network aim to collaboratively estimate and track the state of a system. These techniques form the enabling technology of modern multi-agent systems and have gained great importance in the engineering community. Although most distributed filtering techniques come with a set of stability and convergence criteria, the conditions imposed are found to be unnecessarily restrictive. The paradigm of stability and convergence in distributed filtering is revised in this manuscript. Accordingly, a general distributed filter is constructed and its estimation error dynamics is formulated. The conducted analysis demonstrates that conditions for achieving stable filtering operations are the same as those required in the centralized filtering setting. Finally, the concepts are demonstrated in a Kalman filtering framework and validated using simulation examples.

II-A

Network Model

Following the conventional setting

,

]
, the networked agents are modelled as the connected graph
\mathcal{G}=\{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E}\}
with the node set
\mathcal{N}
representing the agents and their bidirectional communication links represented by the edge set

II-B

Distributed Filtering Paradigm

Consider the state vector sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{n},n=1,2,\ldots\}
described through the linear dynamics

\mathbf{x}_{n+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{n}+\mathbf{v}_{n}

(1)

with
\mathbf{A}
denoting the state evolution matrix and
\mathbf{v}_{n}
representing the state evolution noise at time
n
. The state vector is observed at node
l
, so that we have

\mathbf{y}_{l,n}=\mathbf{H}_{l,n}\mathbf{x}_{n}+\mathbf{w}_{l,n}

(2)

where at time instant
n

l

\mathbf{y}_{l,n}
is the observation and
\mathbf{H}_{l,n}
is the observation matrix, while
\mathbf{w}_{l,n}
denotes the observation noise.

Assumption 1
.

In keeping with the conventional distributed filtering framework
[
14
,
11
,
9
,
10
]
, all noise sequences are assumed to be stationary zero-mean Gaussian processes, with covariance matrix

\mathsf{E}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{n}\\
\mathbf{w}_{l,n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf{T}}_{m}&\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{T}}_{i,m}\end{bmatrix}\right\}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{v}}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{w}_{l}}\delta(l-i)\end{bmatrix}\delta(n-m).

A centralized filtering operation can be achieved through the following operations
[
10
,
11
]

\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n+1}=\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{col}}\right)\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{n}+\mathbf{G}\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{col},n}

(3)

where
\mathbf{G}
is an adaptation gain, whereas

\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{col}}=\mathsf{col}\left\{\mathbf{H}_{l}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\right\}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{col},n}=\mathsf{col}\left\{\mathbf{y}_{l,n}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\right\}.

Importantly, if
\{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{col}}\}
is detectable and
\{\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathbf{v}}\}
is stabilizable; then, the algebraic Riccati recursion

\mathbf{M}^{{}^{-1}}_{n+1}=\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{M}_{n}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}+\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{v}}\right)^{{}^{-1}}+\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{\mathrm{col}}\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{col}}

(4)

where
\mathbf{R}
is a block-diagonal weighting matrix given as

\mathbf{R}=\mathsf{diag}\left\{\mathbf{R}_{l}:l=1,\ldots,|\mathbf{N}|\right\}\hskip 3.41418pt\text{so~that}\hskip 3.41418pt\forall l:\mathbf{R}_{l}\geq 0

converges to a unique stabilizing solution
\mathbf{M}
, so that

\forall\mathbf{M}_{1}>0:\hskip 3.41418pt\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{M}_{n}=\mathbf{M}

for which the adaptation gain
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{M}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{\mathrm{col}}\mathbf{R}^{-1}
makes the estimation error of the filtering operations in (
3
) into a globally stable linear system
[
28
,
29
]
.

The aim in distributed filtering is to enable each node in the network to track the state vector sequence in (
1
) via observations available to the agent as described in (
2
) and cooperation achievable through communication only with its neighbouring nodes. In the sequel, a general framework for achieving this goal is introduced. Then, its behaviour is rigorously analysed, establishing its stable operating criteria. Finally, the results are shown to be applicable to a number of seminal distributed filtering approaches.

III
Distributed Filtering Framework

III-A

Distributed Filtering Operations

To achieve a distributed filtering operation, and without loss of generality, consider the following operations at each node

\forall l\in\mathcal{N}:\left\{\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\phi}_{l,n+1}=&\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right)\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{l,n}+\mathbf{G}_{l}\mathbf{y}_{l,n}\\
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{l,n+1}=&\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i,n+1}\end{aligned}\right.

(5)

where node
l
implements a local filtering operation using adaptation gain
\mathbf{G}_{l}
; then, combines its intermediate estimate,
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{l,n}
, with those of its neighbours using the combination weights
\{c_{l,i}:\forall l,i\in\mathcal{N}\}
to arrive at the final estimate
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{l,n}
.

Remark 1
.

The filtering operation in (
5
) is designed to encompass the seminal works in distributed Kalman filtering
[
14
,
18
,
10
]
. In addition, it is straightforward to simplify the operation in (
5
) to describe distributed filters based on gradient descent
[
21
,
13
,
27
]
.

Assumption 2
.

In keeping with the diffusion and consensus information fusion setting
[

,

,
32
,
21
]
, the combination weights in (
5
) are chosen so that

\forall l\in\mathcal{N}:\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}=1.

Thus, matrix
\mathbf{C}
with

row and

column element

\mathbf{C}^{\{l,i\}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}c_{l,i}&{}&\text{if}\hskip 3.41418pti\in\mathcal{N}_{l}\\
0&{}&\text{if}\hskip 3.41418pti\notin\mathcal{N}_{l}\end{matrix}\right.

would be right-stochastic and primitive
[
21
]
.

III-B

Stability and Convergence Analysis

The filtering operations in (
5
) are combined to give

\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{l,n+1}=\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\Big(\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{H}_{i}\right)\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i,n}+\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{y}_{i,n}\Big).

(6)

Now, subtracting
\mathbf{x}_{l,n+1}
from both sides of (
6
) yields

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{l,n+1}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{l,n+1}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{n+1}-\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\Big(\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{H}_{i}\right)\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i,n}+\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{y}_{i,n}\Big).

Substituting
\mathbf{x}_{n+1}
from (
1
) and
\mathbf{y}_{l,n}
from (
2
) into (
7
) gives the regressive error expression

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{l,n+1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{H}_{i}\right)\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i,n}-\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i,n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\forall i\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{i}\mathbf{H}_{i}\right)\mathbf{v}_{n}.

(8)

The expression in (
8
) shows how the performance of node
l
is linked to that of its neighbours, which are in turn, linked to their respective neighbours. Therefore, an over all analysis is only possible if the entire network is considered as unified filter. To this end, the network-wide error vector is defined as

\mathbfcal{E}_{n}=\mathsf{col}\left\{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{l,n}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\right\}.

(9)

After some mathematical manipulation, replacing the rows of
\mathbfcal{E}_{n}
in (
9
) with expressions in (
8
) gives

\mathbfcal{E}_{n+1}=\mathbfcal{C}\Big(\mathbfcal{F}\mathbfcal{E}_{n}+\mathbfcal{P}\left(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{v}_{n}\right)-\mathbfcal{G}\mathbfcal{W}_{n}\Big)

(10)

where
\mathbfcal{C}=\mathbf{C}\otimes\mathbf{I}
,
\mathbfcal{G}=\text{diag}\{\mathbf{G}_{l}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\}
, and

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{F}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{diag}\left\{\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right)\mathbf{A}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\right\}

(11)

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{P}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{diag}\left\{\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right):\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\right\}

(12)

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{W}_{n}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{col}\left\{\mathbf{w}_{l,n}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\right\}.

(13)

Theorem 1
.

If
\{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{col}}\}
is detectable and
\{\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathbf{v}}\}
is stabilizable; then, there exists a matrix set
\{\mathbf{M}_{l}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N}\}
so that

\forall l\in\mathcal{N}:\mathbf{G}_{l}=\mathbf{M}_{l}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}

(14)

makes the estimation error of the distributed filtering operations in (
5
) globally stable.

Proof:

Take the matrix recursions

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{l,n+1}=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{M}_{l,n}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}+\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{v}}\right)^{{}^{-1}}+\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}

(15a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}^{{}^{-1}}_{l,n+1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\forall l\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\mathbf{S}_{i,n+1}

(15b)

which can be carried out by the agents in a distributed fashion. Comparing (
15
) to (
4
), and assuming, without loss of generality, that
\forall l\in\mathcal{N}:0<\mathbf{M}_{1}\leq\mathbf{M}_{l,1}
, it can be concluded that
\forall l\in\mathcal{N}:0<\mathbf{M}_{n}\leq\mathbf{M}_{l,n}
. Proceeding on this basis, it follows algebraically that for
\forall l\in\mathcal{N}
:

\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{l,n}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right)\leq\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{n}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right).

(16)

Moreover, the assumption that
\{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{col}}\}
is detectable and
\{\mathbf{A},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathbf{v}}\}
is stabilizable, from the Riccati recursions in (
4
), we have

\mathsf{p}\left\{\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{n}\sum_{\forall l\in\mathcal{N}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right)\mathbf{A}\right\}<1.

(17)

Thus, from (
17
) and (
16
) it follows that,
(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{l,n}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l})\mathbf{A}
, would be stable on modes detectable at node

. Subsequently,
\sum_{\forall l\in\mathcal{N}_{l}}c_{l,i}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{i,n}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{i}\mathbf{H}_{i}\right)\mathbf{A}
, would be stable on modes detectable to nodes in
\mathcal{N}_{l}
.

From Assumption
2
, recall that
\mathbf{C}
is primitive, and hence
\mathbfcal{C}
is block primitive, that is, there exits
m
so that
\mathbfcal{C}^{m}=\mathbf{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbf{I}
consists of identity matrices scaled by positive non-zero real-valued numbers. Given the block diagonal structure of
\mathbfcal{F}
, extending the statements in the previous paragraph, there exists
k
for which
\left(\mathbfcal{C}\mathbfcal{F}\right)^{k}
is a block matrix, where each block consists of an appropriate combination of the matrix set

\left\{\left(\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{l,n}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}_{l}\mathbf{R}^{{}^{-1}}_{l}\mathbf{H}_{l}\right)\mathbf{A}\right)^{i}:\forall l\in\mathcal{N},i=1,\ldots,k\right\}

so that
\mathsf{p}\left\{(\mathbfcal{C}\mathbfcal{F})^{k}\right\}<1
. Therefore, the statistical expectation of any norm, including the second-order norm, of the estimation error sequence

\{\mathbfcal{E}_{n+k}:n=1,2,\dots\}
would become convergent, which concludes the proof.
∎

Note that Theorem

V
Conclusion

The performance of a general class of distributed filters has been considered. The analysis culminated in two theorems, which indicate that distributed filters and their centralized duals essentially require the same convergence criteria. The distributed filters considered were formulated to make the obtained results applicable in a wide range of distributed filtering frameworks. The concepts were verified using simulation examples on both modern and classical distributed Kalman filters, showing convergence is possible under much looser conditions than previously thought.

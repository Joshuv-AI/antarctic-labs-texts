Taylor Expansion of homogeneous functions

Abstract
We derive the Taylor polynomial of a function which is m m -times continuously differentiable and positive homogeneous of order m m . The Taylor polynomial in a a for f ⁡ ( b ) f(b) of order m m in general is a polynomial of order m m in b − a b-a . If the given function is positive homogeneous of order m m , the Taylor polynomial is a polynomial in b b rather than b − a b-a , and the order of all terms is m m . The result can be applied to powers of homogeneous functions of order 1 1 as well.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

To facilitate the handling of many dimensions we will use Einstein’s summation convention, i.e. we write

\sum_{k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{m}}D_{k}f(x)t_{k_{1}}\dots t_{k_{m}}=D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(x)t^{\mu_{1}}\dots t^{\mu_{m}}.

(6)

Note that Euler’s Theorem on homogeneous functions
[
3
, p. 6]
implies

D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}}f(a)a^{\mu_{k}}=\left(m-(k-1)\right)D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k-1}}f(a),

(7)

as
D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k-1}}f
is homogeneous of order
\left(m-(k-1)\right)
. Moreover, by continuously differentiability, we have the symmetry

D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}}f(a)=D_{\mu_{\sigma(1)}\dots\mu_{\sigma(k)}}f(a),

(8)

for all
1\leq k\leq m
and permutation
\sigma
. Taylor’s formula reads in Einstein notation

T^{(m)}f(a;b-a)=f(a)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}}f(a)(b-a)^{\mu_{1}}\cdots(b-a)^{\mu_{k}}.

(9)

We use (
7
) to write

D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}}f(a)=\frac{1}{(m-k)!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)a^{\mu_{k+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{m}},

(10)

that is (using the symmetry of the derivatives)

\begin{split}&T^{(m)}f(a;b-a)=\frac{1}{m!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}(b-a)^{\mu_{1}}\cdots(b-a)^{\mu_{k}}a^{\mu_{k+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{m}}\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{m!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}a^{\mu_{k+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{m}}\sum_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}{k\choose p}b^{\mu_{1}}\cdots b^{\mu_{k-p}}a^{\mu_{k-p+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{k}}\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{m!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}{m\choose k}{k\choose p}b^{\mu_{1}}\cdots b^{\mu_{k-p}}a^{\mu_{k-p+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{m}}.\end{split}

(11)

To collect powers of
b
, we write
q=k-p
and obtain

\begin{split}&T^{(m)}f(a;b-a)=\frac{1}{m!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)\sum_{q=0}^{m}\sum_{k=q}^{m}(-1)^{k-q}{m\choose k}{k\choose k-q}b^{\mu_{1}}\cdots b^{\mu_{q}}a^{\mu_{q+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{m}}\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{m!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)\sum_{q=0}^{m}b^{\mu_{1}}\cdots b^{\mu_{q}}a^{\mu_{q+1}}\cdots a^{\mu_{m}}\sum_{k=q}^{m}(-1)^{k-q}{m\choose k}{k\choose k-q}.\end{split}

(12)

For
q=m
, the inner sum over
k
equals 1. So let
0\leq q\leq m-1
. We write
p=k-q
,
r=m-q
and derive for
1\leq r\leq m

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=q}^{m}(-1)^{k-q}{m\choose k}{k\choose k-q}=\sum_{p=0}^{m-q}(-1)^{p}{m\choose p+q}{p+q\choose p}\\
&\quad=\sum_{p=0}^{r}(-1)^{p}{m\choose p+m-r}{p+m-r\choose p}=\sum_{p=0}^{r}(-1)^{p}\frac{m!}{p!\,(m-r)!\,(r-p)!}\\
&\quad={m\choose r}\sum_{p=0}^{r}(-1)^{p}{r\choose p}={m\choose r}(1-1)^{r}=0.\end{split}

(13)

Going back to equation (
12
), this implies

T^{(m)}f(a;b-a)=\frac{1}{m!}D_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{m}}f(a)b^{\mu_{1}}\cdots b^{\mu_{m}},

(14)

as was to be shown.

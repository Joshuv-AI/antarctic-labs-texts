It is therefore natural to expect that

Techniques used to prove our results:

The main idea in

Additionally, in
[
14

We now discuss the techniques used to prove our results: Let
A
be a Noetherian ring containing a field of characteristic zero. Let

We give standard grading on

Consider the Eulerian operator

The notion of Eulerian modules was introduced in the case when

(they also defined the notion of Eulerian

The next technique that we use is the technique of de Rham cohomology, Koszul homology of generalized Eulerian modules. We generalize several properties which were proved when

The final technique we use to prove vanishing and tameness property of local cohomology modules is that local cohomology modules are countably generated over

Practically, all the proofs in this paper, except the last section, use techniques that were first developed by several researchers (including the first author) for holonomic modules over some appropriate ring of differential operators. In the generality we consider, we do not have the notion of holonomic modules. However we have the notion of generalized Eulerian modules. After carefully rewriting several proofs we were able to deduce our results.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the definition of graded Lyubeznik functors and discuss its behavior under flat extension.

2.1
.

Lyubeznik functors

Let
B
be a commutative Noetherian ring.
If
M
is a
B
-module and
Y
is a locally closed subscheme of
\text{\rm Spec}(B)
, we denote by
H^{i}_{I}(M)
the
i^{th}
local cohomology module of
M
supported in
Y
. Suppose
Y=Y_{2}-Y_{1}
where
Y_{1}\subset Y_{2}
are two closed subsets of
\text{\rm Spec}(B)
, then we have an exact sequence of functors

(2.1.1)

\cdots\to H_{Y_{1}}^{i}(-)\to H_{Y_{2}}^{i}(-)\to H_{Y}^{i}(-)\to H_{Y_{1}}^{i+1}(-)\to\cdots.

A
Lyubeznik functor

\mathcal{T}
is any functor of the form
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{r}
where each
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is either
H_{Y}^{i}(-)
for some locally closed subset
Y
of
\text{\rm Spec}(B)
or the kernel, image, or cokernel of some arrow appearing in (
2.1.1
) for closed subsets
Y_{1},Y_{2}
of
\text{\rm Spec}(B)
such that
Y_{1}\subset Y_{2}
.

2.2
.

Graded Lyubeznik functors
.

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring, and let
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
be standard graded. We say
Y
is a homogeneous closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
if
Y=V(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
, where
f_{i}
’s are homogeneous polynomials in
R
. We say
Y
is a homogeneous locally closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
if
Y=Y^{\prime\prime}-Y^{\prime}
, where
Y^{\prime}\subset Y^{\prime\prime}
are homogeneous closed subsets of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
. Let
{}^{*}\text{\rm Mod}(R)
denote the category of graded
R
-modules. Then we have an exact sequence of functors on
{}^{*}\text{\rm Mod}(R)
,

(2.2.2)

\cdots\to H_{Y^{\prime}}^{i}(-)\to H_{Y^{\prime\prime}}^{i}(-)\to H_{Y}^{i}(-)\to H_{Y^{\prime}}^{i+1}(-)\to\cdots.

Definition 2.3
.

A
graded Lyubeznik functor

\mathcal{T}
is a composite functor of the form
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{r}
where each
\mathcal{T}_{j}
is either
H_{Y_{j}}^{i}(-)
for some homogeneous locally closed subset
Y_{j}
of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
or the kernel, image, or cokernel of any arrow appearing in (
2.2.2
) with
Y=Y_{j},~Y^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}_{j}
and
Y^{\prime\prime}=Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}
such that
Y_{j}=Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}-Y^{\prime}_{j}
and
Y^{\prime}_{j}\subset Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}
are homogeneous closed subsets of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
.

2.4
.

Graded Lyubeznik functor under flat maps
.

Let
\phi:A\to C
be a flat homomorphism of Noetherian rings, and let
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
be standard graded. We set
S=R\otimes_{A}C=C[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
. Clearly
\phi
induces a map
\phi^{\prime}:R\to S
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a graded Lyubeznik functor on
{}^{*}\text{\rm Mod}(R)
. Let
Y=V(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
be a homogeneous closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
.
Set
Y^{\prime}=V(f^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,f^{\prime}_{s})
, where
f^{\prime}_{i}=\phi^{\prime}(f_{i})
. Note that
Y^{\prime}
is a homogeneous closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(S)
.
Clearly we have a homogeneous isomorphism
H_{Y}^{i}(-)\otimes_{A}C\cong H_{Y^{\prime}}^{i}(-)
. If
Y
is a homogeneous locally closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
, say
Y=Y_{2}-Y_{1}
, then we put
Y^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}_{2}-Y^{\prime}_{1}
. Thus
Y^{\prime}
is a homogeneous locally closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(S)
.
Applying the functor
(-)\otimes_{A}C
to (
2.2.2
),
we get
a homogeneous isomorphism
H_{Y^{\prime}}^{i}(-)\cong H_{Y}^{i}(-)\otimes_{A}C
. More generally, if
\mathcal{T}
is a graded Lyubeznik functor on
{}^{*}\text{\rm Mod}(R)
, then
\mathcal{T}\otimes_{A}C
is a graded Lyubeznik functor on
{}^{*}\text{\rm Mod}(S)
.

Note
.

The flat maps, we are interested in are the following:

(i)
\phi:A\to A_{W}
, where

is a multiplicatively closed subset of
A
,

(ii)

\psi:A\to A[[Y]]_{Y}
, where

is a formal power series ring over

3.

-modules

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring containing a field

of characteristic zero. Let

be the

. Notice that

We can consider

graded, by giving

for all

,
\deg X_{i}=1,~\deg\partial_{i}=-1
. The Euler operator on

, denoted by

{\mathcal{E}}_{m}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}\partial_{i}
. Note that
\deg{\mathcal{E}}_{m}=0
. Let

be a graded
A_{m}(A)
-module. For any homogeneous element

of
M
, we set

.

Definition 3.1
.

A graded

-module

is said to be

if for each homogeneous element

of

there exists a positive integer

(depending on

({\mathcal{E}}_{m}-|y|)^{a}\cdot y=0.

We now recall the following well known result and outline a proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\Lambda
is left
(
resp. right
)
Noetherian
(
not necessarily commutative
)

is also left
(
resp. right
)
Noetherian for all

.

Proof.

As
\Lambda
is left
Noetherian so by
[

, a non-commutative version of Hilbert basis theorem, we get that
A_{1}(\Lambda)
is left Noetherian.
Since
A_{m}(\Lambda)=A_{1}(A_{m-1}(\Lambda))
, the result follows by induction.
∎

which says that the class of generalized Eulerian modules is closed under extension.

Lemma 3.3
.

[

Let

be a short exact sequence of

-modules. Then

is generalized Eulerian if and only if
M_{1}
and

are generalized Eulerian.

We first develop some basic properties that are known for

-modules.
All these features will be used extensively in Section 7 to obtain the rigidity property.
To prove
[

, the authors nowhere used the fact that

]
and
[

.
To provide the readers some idea, we repeat the

-module. Then for

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
M
be a generalized Eulerian
A_{1}(A)
-module. Then for
\nu=0,1
, the module
H_{\nu}(X_{1};M)
is concentrated in degree
0
, i.e.,
H_{\nu}(X_{1};M)_{n}=0

.

Proof.

As the map
M(-1)\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\longrightarrow}M
is
A
-linear so
H_{i}(X_{1};M)
are
A
-modules for

. Thus we have an exact sequence of

-modules

0\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{1};M)\rightarrow M(-1)\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\longrightarrow}M\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{1};M)\rightarrow 0.

Let
\eta\in H_{1}(X_{1};M)(1)\subseteq M
be non-zero and homogeneous of degree
u
. Since
M
is generalized Eulerian, we have

(X_{1}\partial_{1}-u)^{b}\eta=0\quad\text{for some }b\geq 1.

Using the relation
\partial_{1}X_{1}-X_{1}\partial_{1}=1
, we can write

0=(\partial_{1}X_{1}-(u+1))^{b}\eta=(*)X_{1}\eta+(-1)^{b}(u+1)^{b}\eta,

where by
(*)
we mean
\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{b}{i}(u+1)^{i}(\partial_{1}X_{1})^{b-i-1}\partial_{1}
. As
X_{1}\eta=0
and
\eta\neq 0
so we get

, that is,
\eta\in H_{1}(X_{1};M)(1)_{-1}=H_{1}(X_{1};M)_{0}
. Hence
H_{1}(X_{1};M)
is concentrated in degree
0
.

Let
\eta^{\prime}\in H_{0}(X_{1};M)
be non-zero and homogeneous of degree
v
. Therefore
\eta^{\prime}\in(M/X_{1}M)_{v}
and hence
\eta^{\prime}=\beta+X_{1}M
for some
\beta\in M_{v}
. Since

is generalized Eulerian, we have

(X_{1}\partial_{1}-v)^{a}\beta=0\quad\text{for some }a\geq 1.

Notice that
(X_{1}\partial_{1}-v)^{a}=X_{1}\cdot(\star)+(-1)^{a}v^{a}
. Thus

X_{1}\cdot(*)\beta+(-1)^{a}v^{a}\beta=0,

where
(\star)
stands for
\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}\binom{a}{i}(-1)^{i}v^{i}\partial_{1}(X_{1}\partial_{1})^{a-j-1}
. Going mod
X_{1}M
we get
(-1)^{a}v^{a}\eta^{\prime}=0
. As
\eta^{\prime}\neq 0
so

, i.e.,
\eta^{\prime}\in H_{0}(X_{1};M)_{0}
. Hence
H_{0}(X_{1};M)
is concentrated in degree
0
.
∎

4.
Some properties of
A_{m}(A)
-modules

Throughout this section, we write
X:=X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
and
\partial:=\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}
.

The following result is similar to
[
1
, Theorem 6.2]
. Note that in
[
1
, Theorem 6.2]
,
M
is assumed to be holonomic.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be an
A_{m}(A)
-module. Set
N_{1}=\{y\in M\mid\partial_{m}^{t}y=0
for some
t\geq 1\}
and
N_{2}=\{y\in M\mid X_{m}^{t}y=0
for some
t\geq 1\}
. Then

(1)

N_{1}
is an
A_{m}(A)
-submodule of
M
.

(2)

N_{1}=\partial_{m}N_{1}
.

(3)

N_{2}
is an
A_{m}(A)
-submodule of
M
.

(4)

N_{2}=X_{m}N_{2}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
y\in N_{1}
. Then
\partial_{m}^{s}y=0
for some
s\geq 1
. Since
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\partial_{i}
for all
i,j
and
X_{i}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}X_{i}
for all
i\neq j
, we get that
\partial_{m}^{s}\partial_{i}y=\partial_{i}\partial_{m}^{s}y=0
for all
i
and
\partial_{m}^{s}X_{i}y=X_{i}\partial_{m}^{s}y=0
for all
i\neq m
. Thus
\partial_{i}y\in N_{1}
for all
i
, and
X_{i}y\in N_{1}
for all
i\neq m
. Moreover, it is easy to check that
\partial_{m}^{\alpha}X_{m}=X_{m}\partial_{m}^{\alpha}+\alpha\partial_{m}^{\alpha-1}
for all
\alpha\geq 1
. Therefore,

\partial_{m}^{s+1}X_{m}y=X_{m}\partial_{m}^{s+1}y+(s+1)\partial_{m}^{s}y=0,

and hence
X_{m}y\in N_{1}
. It follows that
N_{1}
is an
A_{m}(A)
-submodule of
M
.

(2) Clearly
\partial_{m}N_{1}\subseteq N_{1}
. Let
y\in N_{1}
. Then
\partial_{m}^{s}y=0
for some
s\geq 1
. We use induction on
s
to show
N_{1}\subseteq\partial_{m}N_{1}
. If
s=1
, then
\partial_{m}(X_{m}y)=X_{m}(\partial_{m}y)+y=y
. As
X_{m}N_{1}\subseteq N_{1}
so we get
y\in\partial_{m}N_{1}
. We now assume that
s\geq 2
, and the result is true for
s-1
. If
\partial_{m}^{s}y=0
, then
\partial_{m}^{s}X_{m}y=X_{m}\partial_{m}^{s}y+s\partial_{m}^{s-1}y=s\partial_{m}^{s-1}y
. Thus

\partial_{m}^{s}X_{m}y-s\partial_{m}^{s-1}y=\partial_{m}^{s-1}(\partial_{m}X_{m}y-sy)=0.

So
\partial_{m}X_{m}y-sy\in N_{1}
, and hence by induction hypothesis
\partial_{m}X_{m}y-sy\in\partial_{m}N_{1}
. Since
\partial_{m}X_{m}y\in\partial_{m}N_{1}
, we get
sy\in\partial_{m}N_{1}
. Therefore,
y\in\partial_{m}N_{1}
and the result follows.

We skip the proofs of (3) and (4) since they can be obtained following
the same line of proofs given for (1) and (2), respectively.
∎

4.2
.

Fourier transform:
Let
\mathcal{F}:A_{m}(A)\to A_{m}(A)
be an automorphism
defined by
\mathcal{F}(\partial_{i})=-X_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}(X_{i})=\partial_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Clearly
\mathcal{F}(\underline{X}\underline{\partial})=\mathcal{F}(\underline{X})\mathcal{F}(\underline{\partial})
, where
\underline{X}:=X_{1}\cdots X_{m}
and
\underline{\partial}:=\partial_{1}\cdots\partial_{m}
. Notice that

\mathcal{F}(1)=\mathcal{F}(\partial_{i}X_{i}-X_{i}\partial_{i})=\mathcal{F}(\partial_{i})\mathcal{F}(X_{i})-\mathcal{F}(X_{i})\mathcal{F}(\partial_{i})=-X_{i}\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}X_{i}=1,

and the restriction map
\mathcal{F}|_{R}:R:=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]\to S:=A[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]

is an isomorphism. For any left
A_{m}(A)
-module
M
, let
M^{\mathcal{F}}
denote a new module such that (i)
M^{\mathcal{F}}=M
as abelian groups, and (ii) the action of
R
on
M^{\mathcal{F}}
is defined by

r\cdot y=\mathcal{F}(r)y\quad\mbox{ for all }r\in R\mbox{ and }y\in M^{\mathcal{F}}.

The new module
M^{\mathcal{F}}
is called the
Fourier transform
of
M
. Similarly we define

s*z=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(s)z\quad\mbox{ for all }s\in S\mbox{ and }z\in M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}}.

Notice that
r\cdot y=\mathcal{F}(r)*y=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{F}(r)\right)y=ry
for all
r\in R
, and all
y\in(M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}})^{\mathcal{F}}
. It follows that

(M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}})^{\mathcal{F}}=M
. Similarly, one can check that
(M^{\mathcal{F}})^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}}=M
.

Let
B
be a commutative Noetherian ring,
I
be an ideal in
B
, and let
L
be a
B
-module. Set
\Gamma_{I}(L)=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(0:_{L}I^{t})
, the submodule of
L
consisting of all elements of
L
which are annihilated by some power of
I
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring containing a field of characteristic zero. Let
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
and
S=A[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]
. Let
I
and
J
be homogeneous ideals in
R
and
S
respectively. If
M
is an
A_{m}(A)
-module, then
\Gamma_{I}(M)
and
\Gamma_{J}(M)
are graded
A_{m}(A)
-modules.

Proof.

Notice that
R
and
S
are commutative rings contained in the left Noetherian ring
A_{m}(A)
. Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal in
R
.

Claim
.

For any
y\in M
,

I^{t+1}\partial_{i}y\subseteq\partial_{i}I^{t+1}y+I^{t}y\quad\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,m\mbox{ and all }t\geq 0.

We know that
I^{t+1}=\langle g_{1}\cdots g_{t+1}\mid g_{j}\in I\rangle
. Fix
i
and set
\underline{g}:=g_{1}\cdots g_{t+1}
. Since
\partial_{i}\underline{g}=\underline{g}\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}(\underline{g})
,
we have
\underline{g}\partial_{i}y=\partial_{i}\underline{g}y-\partial_{i}(\underline{g})y
.
Using the chain rule, we get that

\partial_{i}(\underline{g})=\partial_{i}(g_{1}\cdots g_{t+1})=\sum_{j=1}^{t+1}g_{1}\cdots\partial_{i}(g_{j})\cdots g_{t+1}.

Thus

\underline{g}\partial_{i}y\in\partial_{i}I^{t+1}y+I^{t}y
. The claim follows.

Let
y\in\Gamma_{I}(M)
. Then
I^{s}y=0
for some
s\geq 0
. From the above claim we get
I^{s+1}\partial_{i}y\subseteq\partial_{i}I^{s+1}y+I^{s}y=0
. Therefore
\partial_{i}y\in\Gamma_{I}(M)
and hence
\partial_{i}\Gamma_{I}(M)\subseteq\Gamma_{I}(M)
for all
i
. Moreover, we have
A\Gamma_{I}(M)\subseteq\Gamma_{I}(M)
and
X_{i}\Gamma_{I}(M)\subseteq\Gamma_{I}(M)
for all
i
. It follows that
\Gamma_{I}(M)
is an
A_{m}(A)
-submodule of
M
.

Note that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{F}(I)}(M^{\mathcal{F}})=\{y\in M^{\mathcal{F}}\mid\mathcal{F}(I)^{s}y=0\text{ for some }s\geq 0\}
. As
\mathcal{F}
is a ring homomorphism so we get that
\mathcal{F}(I)^{t}y=\mathcal{F}(I^{t})y=I^{t}\cdot y
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore,
\Gamma_{I}(M)^{\mathcal{F}}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{F}(I)}(M^{\mathcal{F}})
. Let
J
be a homogeneous ideal in
S
. Set
I:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(J)
. It is easy to check that
I
is a homogeneous ideal in
R
, and
\mathcal{F}(I)=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(J))=J
. Since
M^{\mathcal{F}}
is an
A_{m}(A)
-module, by the above observation we have
\Gamma_{I}(M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}})\subseteq_{A_{m}(A)}M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}}
. Applying
\mathcal{F}
on both sides, we get that
\Gamma_{I}(M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}})^{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq_{A_{m}(A)}(M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}})^{\mathcal{F}}
,
i.e.,
\Gamma_{\mathcal{F}(I)}\left((M^{\mathcal{F}^{-1}})^{\mathcal{F}}\right)\subseteq_{A_{m}(A)}M
and hence
\Gamma_{J}(M)\subseteq_{A_{m}(A)}M
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Since
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
and
(\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m})
are homogeneous ideals in

and

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

be a generalized Eulerian

-module. Then

(1)

\Gamma_{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})}(M)_{n}=0

.

(1) We proceed by induction on

. Let

. From Lemma

(4.5.3)

Since

is a generalized Eulerian
A_{1}(A)
-module, by Lemma
3.3
and Remark

,

. From (

0\to\Gamma_{(X_{1})}(M)_{n-1}\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma_{(X_{1})}(M)_{n}
for all

\Gamma_{(X_{1})}(M)_{0}\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}\Gamma_{(X_{1})}(M)_{1}\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}\Gamma_{(X_{1})}(M)_{2}\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}\Gamma_{(X_{1})}(M)_{3}\overset{\cdot X_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}\cdots.

5.
Countable generation and local cohomology modules

Throughout this section,
B
is a commutative Noetherian ring.
We discuss some properties of a countably generated module, which we will use in the
following section. We further show that the local cohomology module
H^{i}_{I}(B)
is a countably generated
B
-module for any ideal
I\subseteq B
and each
i\geq 0
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say a
B
-module
L
is countably generated if there exists a countable set of elements which generates
L
as a
B
-module.

The following result is well-known, but we do not have a reference.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0\to L_{1}\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}L_{2}\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}L_{3}\to 0
be a short exact sequence of
B
-modules. Then
L_{2}
is countably generated if and only if
L_{1}
and
L_{3}
are countably generated.

Proof.

We may assume that
f
is inclusion and
g
is the quotient map. Let
L_{2}
be countably generated. So we have
L_{2}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}D_{i}
, where
D_{i}
’s are finitely generated
B
-modules and
D_{0}\subseteq D_{1}\subseteq D_{2}\cdots
. Since
f(L_{1})=L_{1}
is a submodule of
L_{2}
, we get
L_{1}=L_{2}\cap L_{1}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(D_{i}\cap L_{1})
. Moreover,
B
is a Noetherian ring and
D_{i}\cap L_{1}
is a submodule of
D_{i}
. Therefore
D_{i}\cap L_{1}
is finitely generated and hence
L_{1}
is countably generated. As
L_{3}\cong L_{2}/L_{1}
as
B
-modules so we get that
L_{3}
is countably generated with same generating set as of
L_{2}
.

Conversely, let
L_{1}
and
L_{3}
be generated by
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
and
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
respectively. Let
\beta_{i}\in L_{2}
be such that
g(\beta_{i})=\overline{\beta_{i}}=\gamma_{i}
.

Claim
.

L_{2}
is generated by
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\beta_{1},\ldots\}
.

Let
z\in L_{2}
. Then
g(z)=\sum b_{i}\gamma_{i}
where
b_{i}=0
for all but finitely many
i
. Set
z^{*}=\sum b_{i}\beta_{i}
. Then
g(z)=g(z^{*})
and hence
z-z^{*}\in\ker g=L_{1}
. So
z-z^{*}=\sum a_{i}\alpha_{i}
where
a_{i}=0
for all but finitely many
i
. Thus
z=\sum b_{i}\beta_{i}+\sum a_{i}\alpha_{i}
. The claim follows.
∎

5.3
.

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring containing a field
K
of characteristic zero. Let
R=A[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
and
S=A[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]
be polynomial rings. Let
A_{m}(A)
be the
m^{th}
Weyl algebra over
A
. We consider
R
,
S
, and
A_{m}(A)
as graded with
\deg z=0
for all
z\in A
,
\deg X_{i}=1
and
\deg\partial_{i}=-1
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
M
be an
A_{m}(A)
-module. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

M
is a countably generated
A_{m}(A)
-module.

(2)

M
is a countably generated
R
-module.

(3)

M
is a countably generated
S
-module.

Proof.

Notice that
R
and
S
are sub-rings of
A_{m}(A)
. So the implications
(2)\implies(1)
and
(3)\implies(1)
follow trivially. We now prove
(1)\implies(3)
.

Set
D:=A_{m}(A)
. Let
M
be a countably generated
D
-module. Therefore
M=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}M_{i}
where
M_{i}
’s are finitely generated
D
-modules, and
M_{0}\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq M_{2}\cdots
. Since
M_{i}
is a finitely generated
D
-module, there exists a surjctive map
D^{t_{i}}\to M_{i}\to 0
for some
t_{i}\geq 0
. Moreover,
D=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}D_{i}
as a
S
-module, where
D_{i}
is a left (resp. right)
S
-module generated by all monomials in
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
of degree less than or equal to
i
. Clearly
D_{i}
’s are finitely generated left (resp. right)
S
-modules, and
D_{0}\subseteq D_{1}\subseteq D_{2}\cdots
. Thus
D
is a countably generated left (resp. right)
S
-module and so is
D^{t_{i}}
. By Lemma
5.2
we get that
M_{i}
is a countably generated
S
-module for all
i
. Hence
M
is a countably generated
S
-module.

To prove the implication
(1)\implies(2)
, we only need to replace ‘
S
’ by ‘
R
’ and ‘
X_{i}
’ by ‘
\partial_{i}
’ in the above proof.
∎

The following result is
well-known.
Due to the lack of reference, we provide a proof here.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
{\bf Y}:0\to Y^{0}\overset{d^{0}}{\longrightarrow}Y^{1}\overset{d^{1}}{\longrightarrow}Y^{2}\overset{d^{2}}{\longrightarrow}\cdots
be a co-chain complex of countably generated
B
-modules. Then
H^{i}({\bf Y})
is countably generated for all
i
.

Proof.

Notice that
\ker d^{i}
is a submodule of
Y^{i}
. Since
Y^{i}
is countably generated, by Lemma
5.2
we get that
\ker d^{i}
is countably generated. From Lemma
5.2
, it follows that
H^{i}({\bf Y})=\ker d^{i}/\operatorname{image}d^{i-1}
is countably generated.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
B
. Let
L
be a countably generated
B
-module. Then
H_{I}^{i}(L)
is countably generated.

Proof.

Let
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
. Consider the Čech complex

{\bf C}:0\to L\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}L_{f_{i}}\to\cdots\to L_{f_{1}\cdots f_{s}}\to 0.

Claim
.

If
N
is a countably generated
B
-module, then so is
N_{f}

for any
f\in B
.

Clearly
N_{f}=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}N_{1/f^{i}}
. Choose a countable generating set
W
of
N
. Then
W_{1/f^{i}}:=\{z/f^{i}\mid z\in W\}
is a generating set of
N_{1/f^{i}}
as a
B
-module and hence
\bigcup_{i\geq 1}W_{1/f^{i}}
is a generating set of
N_{f}
. As
W
is a countable set, so is
W_{1/f^{i}}
. Thus
\bigcup_{i\geq 1}W_{1/f^{i}}
is a countable set. It follows that
N_{f}
is countably generated.

Since
L
is countably generated,
{\bf C}
is a complex of countably generated
C
-modules. By Lemma
5.5
, it follows that
H^{i}_{I}(L)=H^{i}({\bf C})
is countably generated.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a Lyubeznik functor on
\text{\rm Mod}(B)
. Then
\mathcal{T}(L)
is a countably generated
B
-module for any countably generated
B
-module
L
.

Proof.

Let
Y
be any locally closed subset of
\text{\rm Spec}(B)
with
Y=Y_{2}-Y_{1}
, where
Y_{1}\subset Y_{2}
are closed subsets of
\text{\rm Spec}(B)
. For any countably generated
B
-module
N
, we have an exact sequence

H_{Y_{2}}^{i}(N)\to H_{Y}^{i}(N)\to H_{Y_{1}}^{i+1}(N).

From Lemma
5.6
, we get that
H_{Y_{2}}^{i}(N)
and
H_{Y_{1}}^{i+1}(N)
are countably generated.
By Lemma
5.2
, it follows that
H_{Y}^{i}(N)
is countably generated.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{r}
.
By

2.1
,
\mathcal{T}_{j}(N)
is countably generated for each
j
, and any countably generated
B
-module
N
. Notice that
\mathcal{T}(L)=(\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{T}_{r})(L)=\mathcal{T}_{1}(\cdots(\mathcal{T}_{r}(L))\cdots)
. Hence
\mathcal{T}(L)
is a countably generated

-module.
∎

6.
Vanishing and tameness

In this section, we discuss the vanishing and tameness properties of generalized Eulerian modules.

The following result is well-known, see
[
7
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

A nonzero finite dimensional vector space over an uncountable field is not a union of countably many proper subspaces.

The following result from
[
13
]
is important for our study.

Lemma 6.2
.

[
13
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
L
be a countably generated
B
-module. Then
\operatorname{Ass}_{B}L
is a countable set.

We also need the following proposition from
[
14
]
to prove the forthcoming result.

Proposition 6.3
.

[
14
, Proposition 12.1]

Let
f:B\to C
be a homomorphism of commutative Noetherian rings. Let

be a
C
-module. Then

\operatorname{Ass}_{B}L=\{P\cap B~|~P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{C}L\}.

In particular, if
\operatorname{Ass}_{C}L
is a finite set, then so is
\operatorname{Ass}_{B}L
.

We are now in a position to establish the following result under the extra hypothesis that
K
is uncountable.

Proposition 6.4

(with hypothesis as in
5.3
)
.

Further, assume
K
is uncountable.
Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a countably generated,
generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module. Then there exists some homogeneous element
\eta\in K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
of degree
1
such that
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\eta}{\rightarrow}M_{n+1}
is an injective map for all
n\geq-m+1
, and some homogeneous element
\xi\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]
of degree
-1
such that
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\xi}{\rightarrow}M_{n-1}
is an injective map for all
n\leq-m
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
we have
\Gamma_{(X)}(M)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq-m+1
.
From the short exact sequence
0\to\Gamma_{(X)}(M)\to M\to M/\Gamma_{(X)}(M)\to 0
, we get that

M_{n}=\left(M/\Gamma_{(X)}(M)\right)_{n}
for all
n\geq-m+1
. As
R
is Noetherian and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
is an ideal in
R
so by
[
11
, Lemma 6.2]
we get

(6.4.5)

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\frac{M}{\Gamma_{(X)}(M)}=\{P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M\mid P\nsupseteq(X)\}.

Set
\overline{M}=M/\Gamma_{(X)}(M)
. Note that if
P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M
, then
P
is homogeneous. By Lemmas
5.4
and
5.2
,
\overline{M}
is a countably generated
R
-module. Therefore,
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\overline{M}
is a countable set by Lemma
6.2
. Since
C:=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]\subset R
is a sub-ring, by Proposition
6.3
we have
\operatorname{Ass}_{C}\overline{M}=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\overline{M}\cap C
. So
\operatorname{Ass}_{C}\overline{M}
is also a countable set. Additionally, for each
Q\in\operatorname{Ass}_{C}\overline{M}
, there exists some
P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\overline{M}
such that
Q=P\cap C
.
By (
6.4.5
),
P\nsupseteq(X)
for any
P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\overline{M}
. Hence
Q\cap C_{1}=P\cap C_{1}\subsetneq C_{1}
. As
\overline{M}
is a graded
C
-module so
Q
is homogeneous.
We denote
H:=\bigcup_{Q\in\operatorname{Ass}_{C}\overline{M}}Q\cap C_{1}
.
Since
K
is uncountable,
H\subsetneq C_{1}
by Lemma
6.1
. Thus there exists some
\eta\in C_{1}-H
. Note that
\eta:=b_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+b_{m}X_{m}\in K[X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}]
is a non-zero divisor on
\overline{M}
, where
b_{i}\in K
. Consequently,
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\eta}{\rightarrow}M_{n+1}
is an injective map for all
n\geq-m+1
.

By Proposition
4.5
we have
\Gamma_{(\partial)}(M)_{n}=0
for all
n\leq-m
. From the short exact sequence
0\to\Gamma_{(\partial)}(M)\to M\to M/\Gamma_{(\partial)}(M)\to 0
, we get that
M_{n}=\left(M/\Gamma_{(\partial)}(M)\right)_{n}
for all
n\leq-m
. As
S
is Noetherian and
(\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m})
is an ideal in
S
so by
[
11
, Lemma 6.2]
we get

(6.4.6)

\operatorname{Ass}_{S}\frac{M}{\Gamma_{(\partial)}(M)}=\{P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}M\mid P^{\prime}\nsupseteq(\partial)\}.

Set
\widetilde{M}=M/\Gamma_{(\partial)}(M)
. Note that if
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}M
, then
P^{\prime}
is homogeneous. Since
M
is a countably generated
A_{m}(A)
-module, by Lemma
5.4
, it is a countably generated
S
-module. From Lemma
5.2
it follows that
\widetilde{M}
is a countably generated
S
-module. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}\widetilde{M}
is a countable set by Lemma
6.2
. As
T=K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]\subset S
is a sub-ring so by Proposition
6.3
we have
\operatorname{Ass}_{T}\widetilde{M}=\operatorname{Ass}_{S}\widetilde{M}\cap T
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Ass}_{T}\widetilde{M}
is also a countable set. Moreover, for each
Q^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{T}\widetilde{M}
, there exists some
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}\widetilde{M}
such that
Q^{\prime}=P^{\prime}\cap T
.
By (
6.4.6
),
P^{\prime}\nsupseteq(\partial)
for any
P^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}\widetilde{M}
. Hence
Q^{\prime}\cap T_{-1}=P^{\prime}\cap T_{-1}\subsetneq T_{-1}
. As
\overline{M}
is a graded
T
-module so
Q^{\prime}
is homogeneous.
We denote
H^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{Q^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{T}\widetilde{M}}Q^{\prime}\cap T_{-1}
.
Since
K
is uncountable,
H^{\prime}\subsetneq T_{-1}
. Thus there exists some
\xi\in T_{-1}-H^{\prime}
. Note that
\xi:=a_{1}\partial_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}\partial_{m}\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]
is a non-zero divisor on
\widetilde{M}
, where
a_{i}\in K
. Consequently,
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\xi}{\rightarrow}M_{n-1}
is an injective map for all
n\leq-m
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
6.4
we get the following results.

Corollary 6.5

(Vanishing)
.

(
with hypothesis as in
5.3
)
.
Further, assume K is uncountable.
Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a countably generated,
generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module. If
M\neq 0
, then
M_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n\gg 0
OR
M_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n\ll 0
.

Proof.

Suppose if possible there exists
r\geq-m+1
such that
M_{r}\neq 0
and
M_{n}=0
for all
n>r
. By Proposition
6.4
, there exists some homogeneous element
\eta\in K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
of degree
1
such that the map
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\eta}{\rightarrow}M_{n+1}
is injective for all
n\geq-m+1
. In particular, we have an injective map
M_{r}\overset{\cdot\eta}{\rightarrow}M_{r+1}
. Thus
M_{r+1}\neq 0
, a contradiction.

Next, let if possible there exists
s\leq-m
such that
M_{s}\neq 0
and
M_{n}=0
for all
n<s
. By Proposition
6.4
, there exists some homogeneous element
\xi\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]
of degree
-1
such that
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\xi}{\rightarrow}M_{n-1}
is an injective map for all
n\leq-m
. In particular, we have an injective map
M_{s}\overset{\cdot\xi}{\rightarrow}M_{s-1}
. Thus
M_{s-1}\neq 0
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 6.6

(Tameness)
.

(
with hypothesis as in
5.3
)
.
Further, assume
K
is uncountable.
Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a countably generated,
generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module. Then

\displaystyle M_{n_{0}}\neq 0\text{ for some }n_{0}\geq-m+1\implies M_{n}\neq 0\text{ for all }n\geq{n_{0}},

\displaystyle M_{n_{0}}\neq 0\text{ for some }n_{0}\leq-m\implies M_{n}\neq 0\text{ for all }n\leq{n_{0}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.4
, there exists some homogeneous element
\eta\in K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
of degree one such that
M_{n}\overset{\eta}{\rightarrow}M_{n+1}
is an injective map for all
n\geq-m+1
. In particular, for given
n_{0}\geq-m+1
we have an injective map
M_{n_{0}}\overset{\cdot\eta}{\rightarrow}M_{n_{0}+1}
and hence
M_{n_{0}+1}\neq 0
. Repeating this process we get
M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq{n_{0}}
.

Again by Proposition
6.4
, there exists some homogeneous element
\xi\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{m}]
of degree
-1
such that
M_{n}\overset{\cdot\xi}{\rightarrow}M_{n-1}
is an injective map for all
n\leq-m
. In particular, for given
n_{0}\leq-m
we have an injective map
M_{n_{0}}\overset{\cdot\xi}{\rightarrow}M_{n_{0}-1}
and hence
M_{n_{0}-1}\neq 0
.
Repeating this process we get
M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\leq{n_{0}}
.
∎

7.
Rigidity

In this section, we
extend the rigidity results, proved in
[
14
]
, to the wider framework of generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-modules. To get
[
14
, Theorem 6.1, Theorem 6.2]
, the first author used the techniques of de Rham cohomology and Koszul homology of generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(K)
-modules. In Section 4, we have showed that
generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-modules also exhibit similar properties, see Propositions
3.4
,

3.5
,
3.6
and
3.7
.
Due to this, Theorem
7.1
and Theorem
7.2
can be proved following exactly the same method used in
[
14
]
.
We duplicate the proofs here for convenience of the reader. We begin with the following result.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module. Then

(I)

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

M_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n<0
.

(b)

There exists
r
such that
M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\leq r
.

(c)

M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\leq-m
.

(d)

M_{s}\neq 0
for some
s\leq-m
.

(II)

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

M_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n\geq 0
.

(b)

There exists
s
such that
M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq s
.

(c)

M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq 0
.

(d)

M_{t}\neq 0
for some
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

(I) The implications
(c)\implies(b)\implies(a)\implies(d)
follow trivially. We only have to prove
(d)\implies(c)
. We will do this by induction on
m
.

We first assume
m=1
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.1.7)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(\partial_{1};M)_{j}\rightarrow M_{j+1}\overset{\partial_{1}}{\longrightarrow}M_{j}\rightarrow H_{0}(\partial_{1};M)_{j}\rightarrow 0.

Since
M
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module so by Proposition
3.6
we get that
H_{\nu}(\partial_{1};M)
is concentrated in degree
-1
for
\nu=0,1
. Thus by exact sequence (
7.1.7
) it follows that

M_{j}\cong M_{-1}\quad\text{for all }j\leq-2.

Hence
M_{j}\cong M_{s}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-1
.

We now assume that
m\geq 2
and the result is proved for
m-1
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.1.8)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)_{j}\rightarrow M_{j+1}\overset{\partial_{m}}{\longrightarrow}M_{j}\rightarrow H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)_{j}\rightarrow 0.

By Proposition
3.4
we get
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module for
\nu=0,1
. We consider the following three cases:

Case 1:

H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\leq-m+1
.

By the induction hypothesis it follows that
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m+1
. Hence
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m
. So by exact sequence (
7.1.8
) we get that
M_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m
.

Case 2:

H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\leq-m+1
.

By the induction hypothesis it follows that
H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m+1
. Hence
H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m
. So by exact sequence (
7.1.8
) it follows that
M_{j+1}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m
which implies
M_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m+1
.

Case 3:

H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{j}=0
for
\nu=0,1
and for ALL
j\leq-m+1
.

Then we have
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)_{j}=0
for
\nu=0,1
and for all
j\leq-m
. By exact sequence (
7.1.8
) it follows that
M_{j}\cong M_{-m+1}
for all
j\leq-m+1
. Hence
M_{j}\cong M_{s}\neq 0
for all
j\leq-m
.

(II) Clearly
(c)\implies(b)\implies(a)\implies(d)
. We only have to prove
(d)\implies(c)
. To do this we use induction on
m
.

We first assume
m=1
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.1.9)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{1};M)_{j}\rightarrow M_{j-1}\overset{X_{1}}{\longrightarrow}M_{j}\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{1};M)_{j}\rightarrow 0.

Since
M
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module so by Proposition
3.7
we get that
H_{\nu}(X_{1};M)
is concentrated in degree
0
for
\nu=0,1
. Thus by the exact sequence (
7.1.9
) it follows that

M_{j}\cong M_{0}\quad\text{for all }j\geq 0.

Hence
M_{j}\cong M_{t}\neq 0
for all
j\geq 0
.

We now assume that
m\geq 2
and the result is proved for
m-1
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.1.10)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{j}\rightarrow M_{j-1}\overset{X_{m}}{\longrightarrow}M_{j}\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{j}\rightarrow 0.

By Proposition
3.5
we have
H_{\nu}(X_{m};M)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module. We consider the following three cases:

Case 1:

H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\geq 0
.

By the induction hypothesis we get
H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\geq 0
. So by exact sequence (
7.1.10
) it follows that
M_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\geq 0
.

Case 2:

H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\geq 0
.

By the induction hypothesis we get
H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\geq 0
. So by exact sequence (
7.1.10
) it follows that
M_{j-1}\neq 0
for all
j\geq 0
and hence
M_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\geq-1
.

Case 3:

H_{\nu}(X_{m};M)_{j}=0
for
\nu=0,1
and for ALL
j\geq 0
.

By exact sequence (
7.1.10
) we get
M_{j}\cong M_{-1}
for all
j\geq 0
. Hence
M_{j}\cong M_{t}\neq 0
for all
j\geq 0
.
∎

Next, we prove the following rigidity theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}
be a generalized Eulerian
A_{m}(A)
-module. If
m\geq 2
, then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

M_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

There exists
r
with
-m<r<0
such that
M_{r}\neq 0
.

Proof.

(i)\implies(ii)
is clear. We only have to prove
(ii)\implies(i)
. We use induction on
m
.

Let
m=2
. Then we have
M_{-1}\neq 0
.

Claim 1
.

M_{i}\neq 0
for infinitely many
i<0
.

Suppose if possible Claim 1 is false. Then by Theorem
7.1
we get
M_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-2
. Now we have exact sequence

(7.2.11)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(\partial_{2};M)_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+1}\overset{\partial_{2}}{\longrightarrow}M_{i}\rightarrow H_{0}(\partial_{2};M)_{i}\rightarrow 0.

Therefore
H_{\nu}(\partial_{2};M)_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-3
and
\nu=0,1
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.4
we have
H_{\nu}(\partial_{2};M)(-1)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{1}(A)
-module. Therefore by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
H_{\nu}(\partial_{2};M)(-1)_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-1
and hence
H_{\nu}(\partial_{2};M)_{i}=0
for
i\leq-2
. From the exact sequence (
7.2.11
) for
i=-2
, we get
M_{-1}\cong M_{-2}=0
which contradicts our hypothesis. So Claim 1 is correct. Thus by Theorem
7.1
we get
M_{i}\neq 0
for
i\leq-2
.

Claim 2
.

M_{i}\neq 0
for infinitely many
i\geq 0
.

Suppose if possible Claim 2 is false. Then by Theorem
7.1
we get
M_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.2.12)

0\to H_{1}(X_{2};M)_{i}\to M_{i-1}\overset{X_{2}}{\longrightarrow}M_{i}\to H_{0}(X_{2};M)_{i}\to 0.

So
H_{1}(X_{2};M)_{i}=0=H_{0}(X_{2};M)_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.5
we have
H_{\nu}(X_{2};M)
is generalized Eulerian for
\nu=0,1
. Thus by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
H_{\nu}(X_{2};M)_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 0
. From exact sequence (
7.2.12
) for
j=0,
we get

M_{-1}\cong M_{0}=0.

This contradicts our hypothesis. So Claim 2 is true.

Thus by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
M_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\geq 0
. Hence the result is true when
m=2
.

We now assume
m\geq 3
and the result is known for
m-1
. We have
M_{r}\neq 0
for some
r
with
-m<r<0
. We want to show
M_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Claim 3
.

M_{i}\neq 0
for infinitely many
i<0
.

Suppose if possible Claim 3 is false. Then by Theorem
7.1
we get
M_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-m
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.2.13)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+1}\overset{\partial_{m}}{\longrightarrow}M_{i}\rightarrow H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)_{i}\rightarrow 0.

Therefore
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-m-1
and for
\nu=0,1
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.4
we have
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module. Thus for
\nu=0,1;
by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{i}=0
for all
i\leq-m+1
and by induction hypothesis we get that
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{i}=0
for
-m+1<i\leq-1
. Hence
H_{\nu}(\partial_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
i\leq-2
. Now by exact sequence (
7.2.13
) we get

M_{-1}\cong M_{-2}\cong\cdots\cong M_{-m+1}\cong M_{-m}.

This implies
M_{-m}\cong M_{r}\neq 0
, a contradiction to our assumption. So Claim 3 is correct. Thus by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
M_{i}\neq 0
for
i\leq-m
.

Claim 4
.

M_{i}\neq 0
for infinitely many
i\geq 0
.

Suppose if possible Claim 4 is false. Then by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
M_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Consider the exact sequence

(7.2.14)

0\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{i}\rightarrow M_{i-1}\overset{X_{m}}{\longrightarrow}M_{i}\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{i}\rightarrow 0

So
H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0=H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
. Moreover, from Proposition
3.5
we have
H_{\nu}(X_{m};M)
is generalized Eulerian for
\nu=0,1
.
By Theorem
7.1
it follows that
H_{\nu}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 0
and by induction hypothesis we get
H_{\nu}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
-m+1<i<0
. Thus
H_{\nu}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
i\geq-m+2
. Now by exact sequence (
7.2.14
) we get

M_{-m+1}\cong M_{-m+2}\cong\cdots\cong M_{-1}\cong M_{0}.

This implies
M_{r}\cong M_{0}=0
, a contradiction to our hypothesis. So Claim 4 is correct. Thus by Theorem
7.1
it follows that
M_{i}\neq 0
for
i\geq 0
.

As
m\geq 3
we also have to prove that if
c\neq r
and
-m<c<0
,
then
M_{c}\neq 0
. Suppose if possible
M_{c}=0
. We will consider the following two cases:

Case
1
:

c<r
.

By Proposition
3.5
we have
H_{1}(X_{m};M)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module. Moreover, by exact sequence (
7.2.14
) we get
H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{c+1}=0
. Notice
-m+1<c+1\leq r<0
.
So by induction hypothesis
H_{1}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
-m+1<i<0
.

Again by exact sequence (
7.2.14
) we also get
H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{c}=0
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.5
we have
H_{0}(X_{m};M)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module. We will consider two sub-cases.

Sub-case
1.1
:

-m+1<c<r<0
.

By induction hypothesis we have
H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
-m+1<i<0
. Thus by exact sequence (
7.2.14
) we get,

M_{-1}\cong M_{-2}\cong\cdots\cong M_{-m+2}\cong M_{-m+1}.

Hence
M_{r}\cong M_{c}=0
, a contradiction to our hypothesis.

Sub-case
1.2
:

c=-m+1
.

We have
H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{-m+1}=0

H_{0}(X_{m};M)_{i}=0\quad\text{for}-m+1<i<0.

So by an argument similar to Sub-case
1.1
, we get

, a contradiction to our hypothesis.

Thus our assumption is false. Hence
M_{c}\neq 0
for

-m<c<r
.

Case
2
:

c>r
.

By Proposition
3.4
we have
H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module. Moreover, by exact sequence (
7.2.13
) we get that
H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{c}=H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)_{c-1}=0
. Notice
-m+1\leq r<c<0
.
So by induction hypothesis
H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{i}=0
for
-m+1<i<0
. Thus
H_{1}(\partial_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
-m<i<-1
.

Again by exact sequence (
7.2.13
) we also get
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)_{c}=0
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.4
we have
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)
is a generalized Eulerian
A_{m-1}(A)
-module.
We consider two sub-cases:

Sub-case
2.1
:

c\neq-1
.

We have
-m+2<c+1<0

and
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{c+1}=0
.
So by induction hypothesis we get
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)(-1)_{i}=0
for
-m+1<i<0
. Thus
H_{0}(\partial_{m};M)_{i}=0
for
-m<i<-1
. Therefore by exact sequence (

) we get

M_{-1}\cong M_{-2}\cong\cdots\cong M_{-m+2}\cong M_{-m+1}.

Hence

, a contradiction to our hypothesis.

Sub-case
2.2
:

Recall

I.

Reversibility of quantum resources through probabilistic protocols
Bartosz Regula, Ludovico Lami

Abstract
Among the most fundamental questions in the manipulation of quantum resources such as entanglement is the possibility of reversibly transforming all resource states. The key consequence of this would be the identification of a unique entropic resource measure that exactly quantifies the limits of achievable transformation rates. Remarkably, previous results claimed that such asymptotic reversibility holds true in very general settings; however, recently those findings have been found to be incomplete, casting doubt on the conjecture. Here we show that it is indeed possible to reversibly interconvert all states in general quantum resource theories, as long as one allows protocols that may only succeed probabilistically. Although such transformations have some chance of failure, we show that their success probability can be ensured to be bounded away from zero, even in the asymptotic limit of infinitely many manipulated copies. As in previously conjectured approaches, the achievability here is realised through operations that are asymptotically resource non-generating, and we show that this choice is optimal: smaller sets of transformations cannot lead to reversibility. Our methods are based on connecting the transformation rates under probabilistic protocols with strong converse rates for deterministic transformations, which we strengthen into an exact equivalence in the case of entanglement distillation.



How to measure and compare quantum resources? As evidenced by the plethora of commonly used quantifiers of resources such as entanglement
Horodecki 2001
;
Chitambar and Gour 2019
, this seemingly basic question has many possible answers, and it may appear as though there is no unambiguous way to resolve it. However, it is important to keep in mind that, besides simply assigning some numerical value to a given quantum state, one often wishes to compare quantum resources operationally. Is it then possible for a resource quantifier to indicate exactly how difficult it is to convert one resource state into another? Following this pathway is reminiscent of the operational approach used to study thermodynamics, where indeed a unique resource measure — the entropy — emerges naturally from basic axioms
Giles 1964
;
Lieb and Yngvason 1999
. A phenomenon that is intimately connected with the existence of such a measure is reversibility: two comparable states of equal entropy can always be connected by a reversible adiabatic transformation
Giles 1964
;
Lieb and Yngvason 1999
.

Reversibility was observed also in the asymptotic manipulation of quantum entanglement of pure states
Bennett et al. 1996a
, prompting several conjectures about the connections between entanglement and thermodynamics, and in particular about the existence of a unique operational measure of entanglement that would mirror the role of entropy
Popescu and Rohrlich 1997
;
Vedral and Kashefi 2002
;
Horodecki et al. 2002
;
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
.
In the asymptotic setting, reversibility is typically understood in terms of asymptotic transformation rates
r(\rho\to\omega)
: given many copies of a state
\rho
, how many copies of another state
\omega
can we obtain per each copy of
\rho
? The question of resource reversibility then asks whether
r(\rho\to\omega)=r(\omega\to\rho)^{-1}
, meaning that exactly as many copies of
\omega
can be obtained in the transformation as are needed to transform them back into
\rho
. Although entanglement of noisy states may exhibit irreversibility in many contexts
Horodecki et al. 1998
;
Vidal and Cirac 2001
;
Wang and Duan 2017
;
Lami and Regula 2023a
, hopes persisted that an operational approach allowing for universal reversibility could be constructed, leading to the identification of a unique asymptotic measure of entanglement
Plenio 2005
.

A remarkable axiomatic framework emerged, first for entanglement
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
and later for more general quantum resources
Brandão and Gour 2015
, which claimed that reversibility can indeed always be achieved under suitable assumptions. Such a striking property would not only establish a unique entropic measure of quantum resources, but also connect the broad variety of different resources in a common operational formalism. However, issues have transpired in parts of the proof of these results
Berta et al. 2023
;
Berta et al. 2024
, putting this general reversibility into question. As of now, there is no known framework that can establish the reversibility of general quantum resource theories — and in particular quantum entanglement — even under weaker assumptions. What is more, recent results demonstrated an exceptionally strong type of irreversibility of entanglement
Lami and Regula 2023a
, casting doubt on the very possibility of recovering reversible manipulation whatsoever.

In this work, we resolve the question by constructing the first complete reversible framework for general quantum resources, including entanglement.
Our setting closely resembles the original assumptions of the reversibility conjectures
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
, with only one change: we allow probabilistic conversion protocols. That is, we study transformations which allow for some probability of failure, and we demonstrate that in this setting the conversion rates are exactly given by the entropic resource measure known as the regularised relative entropy, identifying it as the unique operational resource quantifier.

The use of probabilistic protocols is a distinguishing feature of our approach, necessitating a careful consideration of how exactly to quantify the asymptotic rates of such transformations. We employ a definition which focuses on the number of copies of states that are undergoing the transformation, discounting the success probability of the protocols.
Although seemingly more permissive than some previously used definitions, we explicitly ensure that the protocols that we study are not unphysically difficult to realise: we only allow transformations whose probability of failure does not become prohibitively large, in the sense that there always remains a constant non-zero chance of successful conversion, even when manipulating an unbounded number of quantum states.
Such rates are well behaved and closely connected to conventional asymptotic transformation rates studied in quantum information.

We stress that, although conceptually similar, our approach follows an alternative pathway that does not exactly recover the reversibility conjectured in Refs.
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
, as the latter relied only on strictly deterministic transformation rates. However, in light of the similarities and relations that we establish between probabilistic and deterministic rates,
we consider our results to be strong supporting evidence in favour of reversibility being an achievable phenomenon in general quantum resource theories.

On the technical side, the way we avoid issues associated with the generalised quantum Stein’s lemma
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
that undermined the original reversibility claims, is to use only the
strong converse
part of the lemma, which is still valid
Berta et al. 2023
. Strong converse rates are typically understood as general no-go limitations on resource transformations, but here we turn them into achievable rates precisely by employing probabilistic protocols.
For the special case of entanglement distillation, we show that these two concepts — strong converse rates in deterministic transformations on one side, and probabilistic conversion rates on the other — are exactly equivalent, which holds true also
in the most practically relevant settings of entanglement manipulation such as under local operations and classical communication (LOCC).

Results

Resource transformation rates

Quantum resource theories represent various settings of restricted quantum information processing
Chitambar and Gour 2019
. Let us denote by
\mathbb{O}
the set of operations which are freely allowed within the physical setting of the given resource theory. Our discussion of reversibility will require a specific choice of
\mathbb{O}
, but for now it may be understood as a general set of permitted operations.

The
deterministic transformation rate

r_{p=1}(\rho\to\omega)
is defined as the supremum of real numbers
r
such that
n
copies of the state
\rho
can be converted into
\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor
copies of the target state
\omega
using the free operations
\mathbb{O}
. The conversion here is assumed to be deterministic, i.e. all transformations are realised by completely positive and trace-preserving maps (quantum channels). However, the process is only required to be approximate, in the sense that some error
\varepsilon_{n}
is allowed in the transformation, as long as it vanishes in the limit as
n\to\infty
.

In many practical contexts, one may be willing to relax the assumption that the error must tend to zero — it may, for instance, be appealing to tolerate some manageable error in the transformation if it could lead to increased transformation rates. The ultimate upper bound that constrains the improvements that can be gained through such trade-offs is represented by the
strong converse rate

r^{\dagger}_{p=1}(\rho\to\omega)
. It is defined as the least rate
r
such that, if we attempt the conversion
\rho^{\otimes n}\to\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor r^{\prime}n\right\rfloor}
at any larger rate
r^{\prime}>r
, then even approximate transformations with large error become impossible.

Another common way to increase the capabilities in resource manipulation is to allow for probabilistic transformations
Bennett et al. 1996b
;
Horodecki et al. 1999
;
Vidal 1999
;
Lo and Popescu 2001
;
Regula 2022
;
Kondra et al. 2022
;
Regula et al. 2023
. Probabilistic protocols in quantum information theory are represented by a collection of completely positive, but not necessarily trace-preserving maps
\{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\}_{i}
, such that the total transformation
\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}^{(i)}
preserves trace. We say that
\rho
can be converted to
\omega
if there exists a free probabilistic operation
\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{O}
such that
\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}^{(i)}(\rho)}=\omega
; the probability of this transformation is
p=\Tr\mathcal{E}^{(i)}(\rho)
.
The question then is how to exactly define the asymptotic rate of such protocols.

Consider a sequence of probabilistic operations
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
such that each
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
converts
\rho^{\otimes n}
to a state which is
\varepsilon_{n}
-close to the target state
\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}
with the error vanishing asymptotically.
We will write
p_{n}=\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})
for the probability of successful conversion.
One way to quantify the rate of such a protocol is to count the average number of copies of quantum states needed to realise the transformation, which means that our rate would be given by
p_{n}r
rather than just
r
, since the protocol needs to be repeated
1/p_{n}
times on average to ensure success.
But one may argue that there is an issue with such a definition: is it fair to say that manipulating
n
copies of a quantum state about
1/p_{n}
times is as difficult as manipulating the larger number of
n/p_{n}
copies all at once? This definition of a rate would make it seem so, since it counts the total number of copies needed in the protocol, and disregards the question of how many of those copies need to be coherently manipulated together. If the rate is supposed to quantify the difficulty in performing a state transformation, then this may not be an accurate assessment, considering that it is the manipulation of quantum states, rather than their generation, that is typically the bottleneck in practical quantum information processing.

An alternative way is then to simply say that, if
\rho^{\otimes n}
is approximately converted to
\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}
— even probabilistically — then the rate is
r
.
This definition focuses on the number of copies of states that are being transformed at once, and it does not count the probability
p_{n}=\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})
itself as part of the rate.
However, there is again a potential issue with this approach, as leaving the probability of the transformation unconstrained effectively allows for conditioning on exponentially unlikely events, which then makes possible transformations that are conventionally known to be unachievable
Regula et al. 2023
. Such a phenomenon happens when the overall probability of success
p_{n}
becomes vanishingly small.
In order to exclude such unphysical protocols which cannot be implemented in practice, it thus becomes necessary to carefully constrain the success probability.

Our approach will then aim to find a middle ground: we will quantify rates in a way that only counts the number of transformed states, but we will explicitly forbid the possibility of the success probability becoming unphysically small. Specifically, to ensure that any considered protocol remains practically realisable, we will assume that the conversion probability is bounded away from zero. We thus consider
probabilistic transformation rates with non-vanishing probability
,
r_{p>0}(\rho\to\omega)
, defined as

\displaystyle r_{p>0}(\rho\to\omega)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\sup_{(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}}\Bigg\{\,r\;\Bigg|\;\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O},

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}F\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})},\,\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)=1,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})>0\Bigg\},

where
F
denotes fidelity.
We stress that this imposes a strong restriction on the allowed protocols, as the overall probability of success must remain larger than a positive constant, even in the asymptotic limit where the number of transformed copies grows to infinity. In short, the number of copies of
\rho
that need to be
manipulated
at once to obtain
m
copies of
\omega
is asymptotically
n=m/r_{p>0}(\rho\to\omega)
, while the total number of copies of
\omega
that need to be
generated
is that number times a constant overhead.

To further motivate our choice of definition of a probabilistic rate, let us compare it with the two deterministic rates introduced in this section.
The fact that the deterministic transformation rate
r_{p=1}
is the smallest of the three types is clear from the definition. However, there is no obvious relation between the strong converse rate and the probabilistic one. We can nevertheless show that the rates actually form a hierarchy:

\displaystyle r_{p=1}(\rho\to\omega)\,\leq\,r_{p>0}(\rho\to\omega)\,\leq\,r^{\dagger}_{p=1}(\rho\to\omega).

(2)

This demonstrates in particular that the probabilistic rate
r_{p>0}
is well behaved, as it does not exceed conventional limitations imposed by strong converse rates. It also naturally fits into the information-theoretic framework for asymptotic transformations and may even provide a tighter restriction on deterministic transformation rates than those coming from strong converse bounds.

Free operations and reversibility

The asymptotic transformation rates depend heavily on the choice of the free operations
\mathbb{O}
. Typically, practically relevant choices of free operations are subsets of
resource–non-generating (RNG) operations
, defined as those maps
\mathcal{E}
(possibly probabilistic ones) that satisfy
\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}\in\mathbb{F}
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
.
Here,
\mathbb{F}
stands for the set of free (resourceless) states of the given theory. The definition of RNG operations then means that these maps are not allowed to generate any resources for free, which is a very basic and undemanding assumption to make.

The framework of
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
studied the manipulation of quantum resources under transformations which slightly relax the above constraint, imposing instead that small amounts of resources may be generated, as long as they vanish asymptotically. Specifically, let us consider the resource measure known as
generalised (global) robustness

R_{\mathbb{F}}^{g}
, defined as
Vidal and Tarrach 1999

\displaystyle R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\frac{\rho+\lambda\omega}{1+\lambda}\in\mathbb{F},\;\omega\in\mathbb{D}\right.\right\},

(3)

where
\mathbb{D}
denotes the set of all states. The
\delta
-approximately resource–non-generating operations
\mathbb{O}_{{{\rm RNG}_{\delta}}}
are then all maps
\mathcal{E}
such that

\displaystyle R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}\right)\leq\delta\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}.

(4)

Finally, the transformation rates under
asymptotically resource–non-generating maps

\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
, whether deterministic or probabilistic, are defined as those where each transformation
\rho^{\otimes n}\to\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}
is realised by a
\delta_{n}
-approximately RNG operation, with
\delta_{n}\to 0
in the limit as
n\to\infty
. We will denote deterministic rates under such operations as
r_{p=1}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.33728pt\hskip-17.33728pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.13638pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-17.33728pt\hskip-14.06819pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG}$}}}}$}}\hskip-14.06819pt\hskip 17.33728pt}}\omega)
, and analogously for the probabilistic rates
r_{p>0}
.

The main reason to study asymptotically RNG operations is their conjectured reversibility
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
.
Specifically, the claim is that the deterministic rates always equal

\displaystyle r_{p=1}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.33728pt\hskip-17.33728pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.13638pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-17.33728pt\hskip-14.06819pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG}$}}}}$}}\hskip-14.06819pt\hskip 17.33728pt}}\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\frac{D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)},

(5)

where
D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes the
regularised relative entropy of a resource
,

\displaystyle D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,\inf_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\,D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma_{n})

(6)

with
D(\rho\|\sigma)=\Tr\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)
being the quantum relative entropy. This would precisely identify
D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}
as the unique resource measure in the asymptotic setting. However, this conjecture relied crucially on the generalised quantum Stein’s lemma
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
, in whose proof a gap was recently discovered
Berta et al. 2023
. Hence, the statement in Eq. (
5
) is not known to be true
Berta et al. 2023
.

One may also wonder whether there are other possible candidates for operations that could lead to reversibility. This is especially relevant since the asymptotically resource non-generating maps
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
are defined in an axiomatic way, and it may be appealing to study smaller classes of transformations constructed through more practically-minded considerations.
However, such a possibility has been ruled out: in the context of deterministic transformations, essentially all sets of operations smaller than
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
have been shown to lead to an irreversible theory of entanglement
Lami and Regula 2023a
. Importantly, the choice of the measure
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
in the definition of
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
is crucial, and even a small change of the resource quantifier can preclude reversibility. What this means is that any reversible theory of entanglement must actually generate exponentially large amounts of entanglement according to certain measures
Lami and Regula 2023a
.
But even such entanglement generation is not enough on its own: if one requires the considered transformations to also be ‘dually’ resource non-generating (i.e., in the Heisenberg picture), then reversibility is impossible, even if one permits generating entanglement akin to
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}

Lami and Regula 2023b
.
On the other hand, choosing more permissive types of operations, such as ones where the generated resources are quantified with the relative entropy
D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}
, may be too lax of a constraint, as such a theory trivialises by allowing for the distillation of unbounded amounts of entanglement
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
.
Altogether, this provides a strong motivation to study reversibility precisely under the class
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
, as it constitutes a ‘Goldilocks’ set of operations that may allow for reversible manipulation while maintaining reasonable restrictions on the allowed transformations.

Probabilistic reversibility

The conjectured resource reversibility (Eq. (
5
)) was formulated in a remarkably general manner. The original claim was meant to apply not only to entanglement, but also to more general quantum resources, as long as the set
\mathbb{F}
satisfies a number of mild assumptions — notably, it must be convex, and it must be such that the tensor product of any two free states remains free, as does their partial trace
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
. These are weak assumptions obeyed by the vast majority of theories of practical interest.

Our main result is a general probabilistic reversibility of quantum resources under the exact same assumptions.

Theorem 1
.

For all quantum states
\rho
and
\omega
, the transformation rate with non-vanishing probability of success under asymptotically resource–non-generating operations satisfies

\displaystyle r_{p>0}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.33728pt\hskip-17.33728pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.13638pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-17.33728pt\hskip-14.06819pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG}$}}}}$}}\hskip-14.06819pt\hskip 17.33728pt}}\omega)=\frac{D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)}{D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)}.

(7)

This implies in particular a general reversibility of state transformations:
r_{p>0}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.33728pt\hskip-17.33728pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.13638pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-17.33728pt\hskip-14.06819pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG}$}}}}$}}\hskip-14.06819pt\hskip 17.33728pt}}\omega)=r_{p>0}(\omega\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 17.33728pt\hskip-17.33728pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.13638pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-17.33728pt\hskip-14.06819pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG}$}}}}$}}\hskip-14.06819pt\hskip 17.33728pt}}\rho)^{-1}
for all pairs of states.

Both the converse and the achievability parts of this result make use of the asymptotic equipartition property for the generalised robustness, which was shown by Brandão and Plenio
(
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
, Proposition II.1)
and independently by Datta
(
Datta 2009
, Theorem 1)
. This property says that, under a suitable ‘smoothing’, the generalised robustness
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
converges asymptotically to the regularised relative entropy of the resource:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\min_{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\omega_{n}-\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right\rVert_{\,1}\leq\varepsilon}\!\log\!\left(1+R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega_{n})\right)=r\,D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega).

(8)

Importantly, this finding directly leads to the strong converse of the generalised quantum Stein’s lemma
(
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
, Corollary III.3)
, but it does not appear to be enough to deduce the main achievability part of the lemma
Berta et al. 2023
, which underlies the previous reversibility conjectures. Our main contribution here is to show that the strong converse part is sufficient to show the reversibility of quantum resources, as long as probabilistic protocols are allowed.

Proof sketch of Theorem
1
.

The converse direction relies on the strong monotonicity properties of the generalised robustness
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
as well as the aforementioned asymptotic equipartition property (
8
). This follows a related approach that was recently used to study postselected probabilistic transformation rates
Regula et al. 2023
, and here we extend it to asymptotically resource–non-generating transformations ARNG. A point of note is that standard techniques for upper bounding transformations rates, based on the asymptotic continuity of the relative entropy
Horodecki 2001
;
Horodecki and Oppenheim 2013
;
Winter 2016
, do not seem to be sufficient to establish a converse bound on probabilistic rates
(
Regula et al. 2023
, Appendix H)
. Our approach requires the use of a different toolset that explicitly makes use of the features of
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

For the achievability part of the theorem,
we use the exact calculation of the strong converse exponent in the generalised quantum Stein’s lemma
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
. The lemma is concerned with the distinguishability of many copies of a quantum state
\rho^{\otimes n}
against all states in the set of the free states
\mathbb{F}
.
The result of
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
then says that, for every resource theory, there exists a sequence of measurement operators
(A_{n})_{n}
such that
\Tr\left(A_{n}\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\geq 1-\delta_{n}
and

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}}\Tr(A_{n}\sigma)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 13.38655pt\hskip-13.38655pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[20.23491pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-13.38655pt\hskip-10.11745pt\raisebox{-5.43109pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!n\rightarrow\infty\!$}}\hskip-10.11745pt\hskip 13.38655pt}}D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho).

(9)

Here,
\delta_{n}
denotes the probability of incorrectly guessing that
\rho^{\otimes n}
is a free state, while the quantity in Eq. (
9
) characterises the opposite error of incorrectly guessing that a free state is
\rho^{\otimes n}
.
The issue here is that the proof of Ref.
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
, and hence also Eq. (
9
), is only valid in the strong converse regime: this means that the error
\delta_{n}
is not guaranteed to vanish, but it may actually tend to a constant arbitrarily close to 1. This prevents a direct application of previous deterministic results
Brandão and Gour 2015
.

What we do instead is
define probabilistic operations of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(\tau)\coloneqq\Tr(A_{n}\tau)\,\omega_{n}+\mu_{n}\Tr[(\mathbbm{1}-A_{n})\tau]\,\pi_{n},

(10)

where:
\omega_{n}
are states appearing in (
8
) which are
{\varepsilon_{n}}
-close to the target states
\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor n\,D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)/D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega)\right\rfloor}
,
\pi_{n}
are some suitably chosen states, and
\mu_{n}\in[0,1]
are parameters to be fixed.

The basic idea is then that by decreasing
\mu_{n}
, we can make the output of this operation closer to
\omega_{n}
, even when
\Tr(A_{n}\rho^{\otimes n})\not\to 1
. However, one cannot just decrease
\mu_{n}
arbitrarily, as the maps
\mathcal{E}_{n}
must be ensured to be free operations. Our crucial finding is that
\mu_{n}
can always be chosen so that
\mu_{n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 13.38655pt\hskip-13.38655pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[20.23491pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-13.38655pt\hskip-10.11745pt\raisebox{-5.43109pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!n\rightarrow\infty\!$}}\hskip-10.11745pt\hskip 13.38655pt}}0
while the operations
\mathcal{E}_{n}
generate asymptotically vanishing amounts of resources and the overall probability of success does not vanish.
This means precisely that the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
is an ARNG protocol that realises the desired conversion.∎

The complete proof of Theorem
1
can be found in Section
II
of the
Supplementary Information
.

Optimality of ARNG transformations

To provide a stronger motivation for the choice of asymptotically resource–non-generating operations in the study of reversible transformations, we can show that this set of operations is essentially the smallest possible: more restrictive types of operations cannot lead to reversibility in general, even under probabilistic transformations.

One natural way to constrain the allowed resource transformations is to forbid resource generation — that is, instead of asymptotically resource–non-generating maps, consider strictly resource-non–generating ones.
An even more fine-grained restriction can be obtained by allowing for approximate resource generation, but choosing a more restrictive resource measure with which to quantify the generated resources.
To be precise, let us consider a modified notion of
\delta
-approximately RNG transformations that we will call
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG},\delta,s}
. They are defined in exactly the same manner as ARNG rates, but instead of constraining the generalised robustness
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
as in Eq. (
4
), we impose that

\displaystyle R^{s}_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}\right)\leq\delta\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F},

(11)

where
R^{s}_{\mathbb{F}}
denotes the
standard robustness

Vidal and Tarrach 1999

\displaystyle R^{s}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\frac{\rho+\lambda\sigma}{1+\lambda}\in\mathbb{F},\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right.\right\}.

(12)

This measure is very similar to the generalised robustness of Eq. (
3
), but the state
\sigma
is now required to be free; because of this, it holds that
R^{s}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\geq R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)
in general, making the constraint in (
11
) potentially more restrictive than before.
We stress that strictly resource–non-generating transformations
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG}}
are a subset of
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG},\delta,s}
, so all irreversibility results shown for the latter apply also to the former.

We can use this to define modified transformation rates
r\big(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 21.03926pt\hskip-21.03926pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[35.54034pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-21.03926pt\hskip-17.77017pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG},s$}}}}$}}\hskip-17.77017pt\hskip 21.03926pt}}\omega\big)
as those realised under
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG},\delta_{n},s}
transformations with
\delta_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

By extending the methods that we used previously in the study of deterministic irreversibility

, we can show the following.

Theorem 2
.

Even in the probabilistic setting, general reversibility is not possible under operations that do not generate any resources or ones that only generate asymptotically vanishing amounts of resources according to the standard robustness. Specifically, in the resource theory of entanglement there exist states
\rho,\omega
such that

\displaystyle r_{p>0}\big(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 21.03926pt\hskip-21.03926pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[35.54034pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-21.03926pt\hskip-17.77017pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG},s$}}}}$}}\hskip-17.77017pt\hskip 21.03926pt}}\omega\big)\,<\,r_{p>0}\big(\omega\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 21.03926pt\hskip-21.03926pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[35.54034pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-21.03926pt\hskip-17.77017pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{${\rm ARNG},s$}}}}$}}\hskip-17.77017pt\hskip 21.03926pt}}\rho\big)^{-1}.

(13)

Together with our achievability result in Theorem
1
, this provides a complete characterisation of the landscape of reversibility in the probabilistic setting: general reversibility is indeed achievable with the asymptotically resource–non-generating transformations
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
, and not only is it impossible under operations that are not allowed to create any resources, but even a slightly more restrictive choice of operations obtained by the change of the underlying resource measure from
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
to
R^{s}_{\mathbb{F}}
precludes reversibility in general quantum resource theories.

A detailed derivation of Theorem

, together with technical details and extensions, can be found in Section

of the

Entanglement distillation

Two of the most important problems in the understanding of asymptotic entanglement manipulation concern the tasks of extracting ‘entanglement bits’ (ebits), i.e. copies of the maximally entangled two-qubit singlet state
\Phi_{+}
, and the reverse task of converting ebits into general noisy states. The rates of these two tasks are known as, respectively, the
distillable entanglement

E_{d,\mathbb{O}}^{x}(\rho)\coloneqq r_{x}(\rho\to\Phi_{+})
and the
entanglement cost

E_{c,\mathbb{O}}^{x}(\rho)\coloneqq r_{x}(\Phi_{+}\to\rho)^{-1}
, where
x
stands for either
p\!\!=\!\!1
,
p\!>\!0
, or the strong converse rate
p\!\!=\!\!1,\!\dagger
.
Although exact expressions can be obtained for the entanglement cost in various settings
Hayden et al. 2001
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Audenaert et al. 2003
, the understanding of distillable entanglement appears to be an extremely difficult problem that has so far resisted most attempts at a conclusive solution, except in some special cases
Devetak and Winter 2005
;
Lami and Regula 2023b
. Of note is the conjectured result that
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Berta et al. 2023

\displaystyle E_{d,\rm NE}^{p=1}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}D^{\infty}_{\rm SEP}(\rho),

(14)

where NE stands for the class of non-entangling operations (equivalent to RNG maps in this theory) and
D^{\infty}_{\rm SEP}
is the regularised relative entropy of entanglement.
Establishing this result would recover the deterministic reversibility of entanglement theory (that is, Eq. (
5
))
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Berta et al. 2023
.
We note that distillation rates under NE operations are equal to rates under asymptotically non-entangling operations (ANE)
Lami and Regula 2023a
, which correspond to
\mathbb{O}_{\rm ARNG}
in the notation of this work.

We now introduce a close relation that connects entanglement distillation transformations in the probabilistic and strong converse regimes.
Namely, we show that one can always improve on the transformation error of a distillation protocol by sacrificing some success probability, and vice versa.
What this means in particular is that every rate that can be achieved in the deterministic strong converse regime (i.e. with a possibly large error
\varepsilon<1
) can also be achieved probabilistically with error going to zero.
Crucially, to construct the new, modified protocol from the original one we only need to employ local operations and classical communication (LOCC), which are the standard class of free operations in entanglement theory, meaning that the result applies to essentially all different types of operations that extend LOCC.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be any class of operations which is closed under composition with LOCC, i.e. such that
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O},\,\mathcal{F}\in\rm LOCC\,\Rightarrow\,\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
. This includes in particular the set
\rm LOCC
itself.
Then, for all states
\rho
,

\displaystyle E_{d,\mathbb{O}}^{p=1,\dagger}(\rho)=E_{d,\mathbb{O}}^{p>0}(\rho).

(15)

For the case of (asymptotically) non-entangling operations, we have that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle E_{d,{\rm(A)NE}}^{p=1,\dagger}(\rho)=E_{d,{\rm(A)NE}}^{p>0}(\rho)=D^{\infty}_{\rm SEP}(\rho).

(16)

Proof sketch.

Assume that two spatially separated parties, conventionally called Alice and Bob, share
n
copies of an entangled state
\rho_{AB}
.
Consider any sequence of protocols which allows them to distill entanglement from such states at a rate
r
with with error
\varepsilon_{n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 13.38655pt\hskip-13.38655pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[20.23491pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-13.38655pt\hskip-10.11745pt\raisebox{-5.43109pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!n\rightarrow\infty\!$}}\hskip-10.11745pt\hskip 13.38655pt}}\varepsilon
and probability
p_{n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 13.38655pt\hskip-13.38655pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[20.23491pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-13.38655pt\hskip-10.11745pt\raisebox{-5.43109pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!n\rightarrow\infty\!$}}\hskip-10.11745pt\hskip 13.38655pt}}p
. (This includes the deterministic strong converse case where
p_{n}=1
.)
After performing the considered distillation protocol, they share a many-copy state
\tau_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
which approximates
\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}
. What they can do now is to sacrifice a fixed number
k
of their qubit pairs in order to perform a state discrimination protocol: by testing whether the
k
-copy subsystem is in the state
\Phi_{+}^{\otimes k}
and discarding their whole state when it is not, they can probabilistically bring the state of the rest of their shared system closer to
\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor-k}
. We show that this can be done by a simple LOCC protocol wherein Alice and Bob perform measurements in the computational basis and compare their outcomes. Since
k
is arbitrary here, Alice and Bob can perform the modified protocol without reducing the asymptotic transformation rate.
Conversely, in a similar manner they can also increase their probability of success by sacrificing some transformation fidelity.
By deriving the exact conditions for when distillation protocols can be refashioned in such a away, we observe that another sequence of entanglement distillation protocols with error
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 13.38655pt\hskip-13.38655pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[20.23491pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-13.38655pt\hskip-10.11745pt\raisebox{-5.43109pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!n\rightarrow\infty\!$}}\hskip-10.11745pt\hskip 13.38655pt}}\varepsilon^{\prime}
and probability
p^{\prime}_{n}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 13.38655pt\hskip-13.38655pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[20.23491pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-13.38655pt\hskip-10.11745pt\raisebox{-5.43109pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!n\rightarrow\infty\!$}}\hskip-10.11745pt\hskip 13.38655pt}}p^{\prime}
can exist
if and only if

\displaystyle p\,(1-\varepsilon)=p^{\prime}\,(1-\varepsilon^{\prime}).

(17)

This directly implies Eq. (
15
).

To see Eq. (
16
), it suffices to combine Theorem
1
with the known result that

D^{\infty}_{\rm SEP}(\rho)
is a strong converse rate for distillation under ANE
Hayashi 2006
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
.
∎

We have already shown the probabilistic reversibility of entanglement theory in Theorem
1
, so let us now discuss the deterministic case. Here, reversibility is fully equivalent to the question of whether
E^{p=1}_{d,\mathbb{O}}(\rho)=E^{p=1}_{c,\mathbb{O}}(\rho)
holds for all quantum states, which has been conjectured to be true for the class of asymptotically non-entangling operations
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
. Combining our results with the known findings of Brandão and Plenio
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
,
we have that

\displaystyle E_{d,\rm ANE}^{p>0}(\rho)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Thm.~\ref{thm:entanglement}}}{=\joinrel=}E_{d,\rm ANE}^{p=1,\dagger}(\rho)\overset{\text{\scriptsize\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{brandao_2010,berta_2022}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}}{=\joinrel=}E_{c,\rm ANE}^{p=1}(\rho)

(18)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\scriptsize\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{brandao_2010}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}}{=\joinrel=}D^{\infty}_{\rm SEP}(\rho)\overset{\text{Thm.~\ref{thm:reversibility}}}{=\joinrel=}E_{c,\rm ANE}^{p>0}(\rho).

The missing link is thus the question if
E_{d,\rm ANE}^{p=1}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}E_{d,\rm ANE}^{p>0}(\rho)
, or the equivalent
Berta et al. 2023
question of whether
E_{c,\rm ANE}^{p=1,\dagger}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}E_{c,\rm ANE}^{p>0}(\rho)
. Showing either of these statements would complete the proof of the deterministic reversibility of quantum entanglement under asymptotically non-entangling operations. An interesting consequence of the above is that establishing the equivalent of Theorem
3
for entanglement
dilution
would be sufficient to recover a fully reversible entanglement theory.

We remark that other quantum resource theories may not be amenable to a characterisation in terms of distillation and dilution because they may not possess a suitably well-behaved unit of a resource resembling the maximally entangled state
Brandão and Gour 2015
;
Regula et al. 2020
;
Takagi et al. 2022
. Nonetheless, reversibility in all resource theories can be understood as in our Theorem
1
.

Discussion

We have shown that the conjectured reversibility of general quantum resources can be recovered, albeit in a
probabilistic
manner that employs probabilistic protocols with non-vanishing probability of success.
This allowed us to identify the setting of probabilistic resource transformations as one that is completely governed by a unique entropic quantity — the regularised relative entropy — thus solidifying the parallels between thermodynamics and diverse types of quantum resources. We further showed that the choice of asymptotically resource–non-generating operations is optimal in this setting, in the sense that
all smaller classes of probabilistic operations are necessarily irreversible, providing a strong no-go restriction on how reversibility could be achieved.

Although this precise characterisation of asymptotic rates is appealing, our setting departs from the original reversibility conjectures of
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
, since it considers transformations that are only required to be achieved with some probability. This may not be enough to ensure the existence of a repeatable, reversible transformation cycle in practice. Nevertheless, in view of the close relations between probabilistic and deterministic rates (Eq. (
2
)) we regard our results as evidence that reversibility could indeed be recovered also in the deterministic setting. This is further motivated by the fact that, in many quantum information processing tasks, strong converse rates actually coincide with the optimal achievable rates
Winter 1999
;
Ogawa and Nagaoka 2000
;
Ogawa and Nagaoka 1999
;
König and Wehner 2009
;
Bennett et al. 2014
;
Berta et al. 2011
;
Morgan and Winter 2014
;
Tomamichel et al. 2017
, meaning that
r_{p=1}=r^{\dagger}_{p=1}
and the hierarchy in Eq. (
2
) collapses. However, a complete proof of this fact in the setting of resource manipulation remains elusive, and it is still possible that one of the inequalities in Eq. (
2
) may be strict for some states, which would rule out deterministic reversibility.
We hope that our results stimulate further research in this direction, leading to an eventual resolution of the open questions that cloud the understanding of asymptotic resource manipulation.

Notation and basic properties

We begin by clarifying the assumptions and definitions used in this work.

I.1
Free operations and free states

Throughout this paper we assume that the underlying Hilbert space has finite dimension.
When discussing many-copy state transformations, we assume that each
n
-copy space of quantum states has its own associated set of
free states

\mathbb{F}_{n}
. We often use
\mathbb{F}
to refer to the whole family
(\mathbb{F}_{n})_{n}
for simplicity.

Following
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
, we will assume the following basic axioms about the resource theory in consideration:

Axiom I.

Each
\mathbb{F}_{n}
is convex and closed.

Axiom II.

There exists a full-rank state
\sigma
such that
\sigma^{\otimes n}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
for all
n
.

Axiom III.

The sets
\mathbb{F}_{n}
are closed under partial trace: if
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{n+1}
, then
\Tr_{k}\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
.

Axiom IV.

The sets
\mathbb{F}_{n}
are closed under tensor product: if
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
, then
\sigma\otimes\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{n+m}
.

The motivation for these assumptions is so that the theory is well behaved asymptotically
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
;
Datta 2009
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
.
1
1

1

In
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
;
Brandão and Gour 2015
, an additional axiom of permutation invariance was imposed. We do not need this here.

These undemanding conditions are obeyed by the vast majority of practically relevant resource theories. In addition, we will need one more assumption that was implicitly used in
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
and later appeared explicitly in
Brandão and Gour 2015
:

Axiom V.

The regularised relative entropy
D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{n}}D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma)
is non-zero for all states
\rho\notin\mathbb{F}
.

Once again, Axiom V holds true for most resources encountered in practice, in particular for quantum entanglement
Piani 2009
. However, there do exist theories in which
D^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}
vanishes for some (or even all) states
\rho\notin\mathbb{F}
, e.g. the theory of asymmetry
Gour et al. 2009
. The standard reversibility conjectures do not apply to such theories
Brandão and Gour 2015
.

We use CPTP to denote completely positive and trace-preserving maps (quantum channels), and CPTNI to denote completely positive and trace–non-increasing maps (probabilistic quantum operations). A
probabilistic protocol
is a collection of CPTNI maps
\{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\}_{i}
that forms a valid quantum instrument, i.e. the overall transformation
\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}^{(i)}
is trace preserving. The latter condition simply means that the outcome probabilities necessarily add up to one. Given some class of
free operations

\mathbb{O}
, we say that a protocol is free if
\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{O}\;\forall i
. When discussing transformations between two fixed states
\rho\to\omega
, we may coarse-grain the outcomes of the instrument and consider only two-outcome protocols
\{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\{0,1\}}
— either the protocol is successful in performing the given transformation, or it fails. We can then say that
\rho
can be converted to
\omega
probabilistically if there exists a free protocol
\{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\{0,1\}}
such that
\mathcal{E}^{(0)}(\rho)\propto\omega
.

Resource non-generating (RNG) operations
are the maps which can never transform a resourceless state into a resourceful one:

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}_{\rm RNG}\coloneqq\left\{\,\mathcal{E}\in{\mathrm{CPTNI}}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}\in\mathbb{F}\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right.\right\}.

(S1)

Their approximate variant is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG},\delta}\coloneqq\left\{\,\mathcal{E}\in{\mathrm{CPTNI}}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}\right)\leq\delta\;\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right.\right\}.

(S2)

We note here that the latter definition depends on the choice of the measure with which one quantifies the generated resources — in this case, this is the
generalised robustness

Vidal and Tarrach 1999

\displaystyle R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\frac{\rho+\lambda\omega}{1+\lambda}\in\mathbb{F},\;\omega\in\mathbb{D}\right.\right\}

(S3)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\left|\;\rho\leq(\lambda+1)\sigma,\;\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\right.\right\}.

This quantity can also be identified with the max-relative entropy of a resource
Datta 2009
,
D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\coloneqq\log\left(1+R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}(\rho)\right)
.
Other choices of measures may lead to completely different asymptotic behaviour
Lami and Regula 2023a
, and our choice here is motivated by the framework of
Brandão and Plenio 2008
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
that conjectured reversibility of quantum resources under operations defined in this way.

Under resource non-generating transformations
\mathbb{O}_{\rm RNG}
(or
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG},\delta}
), the existence of a probabilistic protocol that performs a transformation
\rho\to\omega
is fully equivalent to the existence of a map
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{\rm RNG}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\rho)/\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\omega
. This is because any such map can always be completed to a free instrument: we simply define
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(X)\coloneqq\left[\Tr X-\Tr\mathcal{E}(X)\right]\sigma
for some
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}
, yielding
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{O}
. For other sets of operations, it may be the case that a map
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}
cannot always be completed to a free instrument
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
— e.g. for separable operations in entanglement theory
Theurer et al. 2017
or stabiliser operations in the theory of magic states
Seddon 2022
— so more care may need to be taken when discussing probabilistic transformations.

Lemma S1
.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be any class of free operations such that, for an instrument
\{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\}_{i}

\mathbb{O}_{\rm RNG}
or

Then, for all quantum states
\rho

\displaystyle r_{p=1}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\omega)\,\leq\,r_{p>0}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\omega)

(S12)

Remark
.

Let us comment briefly on the assumption of this lemma. If there exists a classical system
C
with an orthonormal basis
\{\ket{i}_{C}\}_{i}
such that each element of the basis is a free state,
one can equivalently understand the realisation of any probabilistic protocol
\{\mathcal{E}^{(i)}\}_{i}
as a CPTP map

\displaystyle\rho\mapsto\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}^{(i)}(\rho)\otimes\ket{i}\!\bra{i}_{C}

(S13)

where the classical system is used to keep track of the probabilistic outcome of the protocol. If we are free to ‘forget’ this outcome — that is, if tracing out the classical system is an allowed operation — then the given set
\mathbb{O}
satisfies the conditions of the lemma. This is a very weak assumption that is satisfied by essentially all types of free operations in the majority of resource theories, but there are special cases in which it may not be obeyed, e.g. for thermal operations in the theory of thermodynamics
Alhambra et al. 2016
.

Proof.

As deterministic protocols are a special case of probabilistic ones, the first inequality is immediate. For the second inequality, assume that there exists a sequence of free probabilistic operations
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
which performs the transformation
\rho\to\omega
at a rate
r
with error
\varepsilon_{n}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=0
and probability
p_{n}
such that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_{n}=p>0
.
By definition of a probabilistic protocol, there exist operations
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
which complete each
\mathcal{E}_{n}
to a valid quantum instrument, that is, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{n}(X)\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{n}(X)+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{n}(X)

(S14)

is trace preserving. (When
\mathbb{O}
denotes resource non-generating operations, we can simply take
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{n}(X)=\left[\Tr X-\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(X)\right]\sigma_{n}
for some
\sigma_{n}\in\mathbb{F}
.)  By hypothesis, we have that
\mathcal{D}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}
.

Since
p_{n}=\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})
, the concavity of the fidelity then gives

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,F\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n}),\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,p_{n}\,F\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}{p_{n}},\,\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)

(S15)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,p_{n}(1-\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=p>0,

hence
r\leq r^{\dagger}_{p=1}(\rho\to\omega)
.

Let now
r
be any feasible rate for
r^{\dagger}_{p>0}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\omega)
, that is, such that there exists a sequence of free probabilistic operations
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
with non-vanishing error
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=\varepsilon<1
and probability of success
\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_{n}=p>0
. The same argument as above can be used to construct a deterministic protocol with

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,F\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n}),\omega^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,p_{n}(1-\varepsilon_{n})\geq p(1-\varepsilon)>0,

(S16)

yielding
r\leq r^{\dagger}_{p=1}(\rho\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 8.50737pt\hskip-8.50737pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\makebox[10.47656pt]{$\scriptstyle$}}$}\hskip-8.50737pt\hskip-5.23828pt\raisebox{-5.18333pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\vphantom{a}\smash{{\raisebox{0.0pt}{{$\mathbb{O}$}}}}$}}\hskip-5.23828pt\hskip 8.50737pt}}\omega)
and hence
r^{\dagger}_{p>0}\leq r^{\dagger}_{p=1}
. Since the opposite inequality between the strong converse rates follows straightforwardly from the fact that any deterministic protocol is a special case of a probabilistic one, the two rates must be equal.

The inequality

follows immediately by comparing (
S8
) and (
S9

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,p_{n}\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}(1-\varepsilon_{n})\geq p(1-\varepsilon)>0.

(S17)

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle=1-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{\mu_{n}\delta_{n}}{1-\delta_{n}}\right)^{-1}(1-\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle>0.

Lemma S3

(Probabilistic monotonicity of max-relative entropy)
.

For any probabilistic operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm RNG},\delta}

(S42)

Proof.

Consider any state
\rho^{\prime}
with
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho-\rho^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\,1}\leq\eta
.
The first step is to leverage the strong monotonicity of the generalised robustness

and let

be any feasible solution to this problem. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\,\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}

(S43)

\displaystyle=\lambda\,\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}\,\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma)}

where we used the fact that

for some

\displaystyle D_{\max}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}\right)\leq D_{\max,\mathbb{F}}(\rho^{\prime})-\log\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})+\log(1+\delta).

(S44)

Now, since

\lvert\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)-\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})\rvert\leq\eta
, so we can bound

. Furthermore, for any two states and any trace non-increasing positive map

an application of the data processing inequality for the generalised trace distance
(

shows that
(
Regula et al. 2023

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}-\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}\right\rVert_{\,1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}-\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right\rVert_{\,1}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}-\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}\right\rVert_{\,1}

(S45)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)-\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right\rVert_{\,1}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})-\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}\right\rVert_{\,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)-\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right\rVert_{\,1}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\prime})-\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right|

which altogether gives

(S46)

Recall that

(i)

Let

\displaystyle\varsigma(m,\varepsilon)\coloneqq\left(1-\varepsilon\right)\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}+\varepsilon\tau_{2^{m}},

\displaystyle\Phi_{d}\coloneqq\ket{\Phi_{d}}\!\bra{\Phi_{d}}\,,\qquad\ket{\Phi_{d}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{ii}\,,\qquad\tau_{d}\coloneqq\frac{\mathbbm{1}-\Phi_{d}}{d^{2}-1}\,.

Now, given a positive integer

representing the number of local qubits and two error probabilities

\displaystyle\left(1-\xi-\delta\right)\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}+\xi\,\tau_{2^{m}}+\delta\ket{ee}\!\bra{ee}

(S49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\xi}{1-4^{-m}}-\delta\right)\Phi_{2}^{\otimes m}+\frac{\xi}{1-4^{-m}}\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{4}\right)^{\otimes m}+\delta\ket{ee}\!\bra{ee},

(ii)

Instead of probabilistic transformations into isotropic states as in (

with some probability

with probability

, thus obtaining the state

with probability

.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a class of free operations in entanglement theory that includes LOCC. Given as above

(S51)

Lemma S4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer, and consider
\xi,\delta\in[0,1]
with
\xi+\delta\leq 1
. Then, for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
and all

\displaystyle\omega(m,\xi,\delta)\to\omega(m^{\prime}\!,\xi^{\prime}\!,\delta^{\prime})\,,\qquad

\displaystyle m^{\prime}=m\,,\quad

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}=\xi+\lambda\delta(1-2^{-m})\,,\quad

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}=\delta(1-\lambda)\,;

(S52)

\displaystyle\omega(m,\xi,\delta)\to\omega(m^{\prime\prime}\!\!,\xi^{\prime\prime}\!\!,\delta^{\prime\prime})\,,\qquad

\displaystyle m^{\prime\prime}\hskip-2.2pt=m-k\,,\quad

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime\prime}\hskip-2.2pt=\frac{1-4^{-m+k}}{1-4^{-m}}\,\frac{\xi}{2^{k}}\,,\quad

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime\prime}\hskip-2.2pt=\delta+\frac{1-2^{-k}}{1-4^{-m}}\,\xi\,.

(S53)

Proof.

The transformation of type (
S52
) is obtained by the following procedure:

(i)

Alice and Bob check if the system is in the error state
\ket{e}
; if it is not, they do nothing;

(ii)

if it is, with some probability
\lambda

The transformation of type (
S53

k
pairs of qubits to try and determine whether the system is in the maximally entangled state or nor. More precisely, we run the following protocol:

(i)

Alice and Bob check if the system is in the error state
\ket{e}
; if it is, they do nothing;

(ii)

if it is not, then they measure the first
r

(iii)

if upon communicating the outcomes they find that some of them differ, they declare an error, re-preparing the rest of the system in the state

(iv)

The elementary computations needed to verify that these protocols yield the claimed transformations are left to the reader.

S1
combined with a result of
Lami and Regula 2023a
: we have the chain of inequalities

\displaystyle E_{d,{\rm ANE},N}^{p>0}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(i)}}}}{{\leq}}E_{d,{\rm ANE},N}^{p=1,\dagger}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(ii)}}}}{{=}}E_{d,{\rm NE}}^{p=1,\dagger}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(iii)}}}}{{=}}D_{\text{\rm SEP}}^{\infty}(\rho)\,,

(S68)

where (i) follows from Lemma
S1
, (ii) from
(
Lami and Regula 2023a
, Lemma S17)
, and finally (iii) is known from the works of Brandão and Plenio
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
;
Brandão and Plenio 2010b
(see also Theorem
3
).

For completeness, we will give a more direct alternative argument. To this end, we would like to establish a converse result as in Theorem
1
; however, the probabilistic monotonicity of
R^{g}_{\text{\rm SEP}}
(Lemma
S3
) no longer holds when the operations
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,N}
are considered. Our idea will be to show that an approximate version of Lemma
S3
can still be established in the special case of distillation, that is, conversion into the maximally entangled state. This will in fact directly lead to a strong converse bound.

Consider then any sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
of operations
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta_{n},N}
such that

\displaystyle 1-\Tr\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}\,\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)=1-F\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})},\,\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\eqqcolon\varepsilon_{n},

(S69)

with
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})\coloneqq p>0
. It will be crucial to notice that, for any separable state
\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\Tr\left(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left[\left(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)^{\Gamma}\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)^{\Gamma}\right]

(S70)

\displaystyle\leq\Tr\left\lVert\left(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)^{\Gamma}\right\rVert_{\,\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)^{\Gamma}\right\rVert_{\,1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}}(1+2\delta_{n})\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}}(1+2\delta_{n}),

where in the third line we used the fact that the eigenvalues of
\left(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)^{\Gamma}
— which is proportional to the swap operator — are
\pm 2^{-\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}
. We further leveraged the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)^{\Gamma}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma)^{\Gamma}}\right\rVert_{\,1}-1\right)\leq\delta_{n}

(S71)

by the definition of
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta_{n},N}
, and the last line follows since
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is trace non-increasing.

Consider now any error sequence
(\zeta_{n})_{n}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0
, so that
\zeta_{n}<p
and hence also
\zeta_{n}<\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})
holds for all sufficiently large
n
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}_{n}
be any state such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho^{\otimes n}-\rho^{\prime}_{n}\right\rVert_{\,1}\leq\zeta_{n}
, and let
\sigma_{n}\in\text{\rm SEP}
be any state such that
\rho^{\prime}_{n}\leq\lambda_{n}\sigma_{n}
for some
\lambda_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor})}\,\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)

(S72)

\displaystyle=\Tr\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}\,\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)+\Tr\left(\left[\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor})}-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}\right]\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}\Tr\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\sigma_{n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}\,\Phi_{+}^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor})}-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}\right\rVert_{\,1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(iv)}}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{n}\frac{1}{2^{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}}(1+2\delta_{n})\frac{1}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}+\frac{\zeta_{n}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lambda_{n}\frac{1}{2^{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}}(1+2\delta_{n})+\zeta_{n}\right)\frac{1}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\zeta_{n}}.

Here, (iv) follows by Eq. (
S70
) together with the probabilistic data processing inequality for the trace distance that we previously showed in Eq. (
S45
).

So far, the above derivation made no assumption about the rate
r
. Let us now assume that

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle>D^{\infty}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)

(S73)

\displaystyle=\inf_{(\zeta_{n})_{n}}\left\{\left.\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\zeta_{n}}_{\max,\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho^{\otimes n})\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0\right\},

where the second line follows from the asymptotic equipartition property of Brandão–Plenio–Datta (see
(
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
, Proposition IV.2)

(
Datta 2009
, Theorem 1)
). This implies that there exists an asymptotically vanishing error sequence
(\zeta_{n})_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}D^{\zeta_{n}}_{\max,\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho^{\otimes n})<r,

(S74)

and hence that there exist states
\rho^{\prime}_{n}
with
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho^{\otimes n}-\rho^{\prime}_{n}\right\rVert_{\,1}\leq\zeta_{n}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{n}\leq\lambda_{n}\sigma_{n}
such that

\displaystyle c\coloneqq r-\frac{1}{n}\log\lambda_{n}>0

(S75)

for all sufficiently large
n
.
Plugging this into (
S72
), we have that

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{\log\lambda_{n}}2^{-rn+1}(1+2\delta_{n})+\zeta_{n}\right)\frac{1}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\zeta_{n}}

(S76)

\displaystyle=\left(2^{-cn+1}(1+2\delta_{n})+\zeta_{n}\right)\frac{1}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\zeta_{n}}.

Recalling that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0
while
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n})
is lower bounded by a constant, if we furthermore assume that
\delta_{n}
vanishes in the limit
n\to\infty
(or indeed even only that
\delta_{n}=2^{o(n)}
), the whole term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
S76
) goes to zero, and we thus obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=1.

(S77)

Since this holds for any
r>D^{\infty}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)
, it means that no rate achievable with error less than one can be larger than
D^{\infty}_{\text{\rm SEP}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle E_{d,{\rm ANE},N}^{p>0}(\rho)\leq E_{d,{\rm ANE},N}^{p>0,\ddagger}(\rho)\leq D^{\infty}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho),

(S78)

where
E_{d,{\rm ANE},N}^{p>0,\ddagger}
is constructed by using the definition of rate given in Eq. (
S9
).

∎

Our approach to the bounds on entanglement cost will be based on the ideas in
Lami and Regula 2023a
, where the monotone known as the (logarithmic)
tempered negativity

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(\rho)\coloneqq\log\max\left\{\left.\Tr X\rho\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\left\lVert X^{\Gamma}\right\rVert_{\,\infty}\leq 1,\;\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\,\infty}\leq\Tr X\rho\right\}

(S79)

was introduced.

Lemma S9

(Restrictions on probabilistic entanglement cost)
.

For any state
\rho
, the probabilistic entanglement cost under
{\rm ANE},s
and
{\rm ANE},N
operations is lower bounded by the tempered negativity:

\displaystyle E_{c,{\rm ANE},s}^{p>0}(\rho)\geq E_{c,{\rm ANE},N}^{p>0}(\rho)\geq E_{\tau}(\rho).

(S80)

For the operations
{\rm ANE},s
, we can make an even stronger statement: the probabilistic and deterministic entanglement cost are equal, and in fact they both equal the entanglement cost under strictly non-entangling operations:

\displaystyle E_{c,{\rm ANE},s}^{p>0}(\rho)=E_{c,{\rm ANE},s}^{p=1}(\rho)=E_{c,\rm NE}^{p=1}(\rho).

(S81)

Both parts of the lemma rely on a probabilistic monotonicity result for
R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}
, similar to the one that we previously showed for
R^{g}_{\mathbb{F}}
in Lemma
S3
. We state it as a separate lemma for clarity.

Lemma S10

(Probabilistic monotonicity of standard robustness and negativity)
.

For any probabilistic operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,N}
, it holds that

\displaystyle 1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)\geq\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)^{\Gamma}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right\rVert_{\,1}\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{2(1+2\delta)}.

(S82)

For any probabilistic operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,s}
, it holds that

\displaystyle 1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)\geq\left[1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right)\right]\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{1+2\delta}.

(S83)

Proof.

For the case of
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,N}
, consider any feasible solution for
R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)
, that is, a decomposition
\rho=(1+\lambda)\,\sigma_{+}-\lambda\sigma_{-}
with
\sigma_{\pm}\in\text{\rm SEP}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)^{\Gamma}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right\rVert_{\,1}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda)\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})^{\Gamma}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}\right\rVert_{\,1}+\lambda\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})^{\Gamma}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}\right\rVert_{\,1}

(S84)

\displaystyle\leq\left[(1+\lambda)\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}+\lambda\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right](1+2\delta)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+2\lambda}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}(1+2\delta)

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{1+\lambda}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}(1+2\delta),

where in the second line we used that
N\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{\pm})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{\pm})}\right)\leq\delta
by definition of
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,N}
, and in the third line we used that the operation
\mathcal{E}
may not increase trace. Optimising over all feasible
\lambda
we get the stated result.

For
\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,s}
, we will use three equivalent definitions of
R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}
, all of which follow straightforwardly from the original definition by using the fact that
\Tr\rho=1
:

\displaystyle R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho=(1+\lambda)\,\sigma_{+}-\lambda\sigma_{-},\;\sigma_{\pm}\in\text{\rm SEP}\right\}

(S85)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\leq_{\text{\rm SEP}}(1+\lambda)\sigma_{+},\;\sigma_{+}\in\text{\rm SEP}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left.\lambda\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\rho\geq_{\text{\rm SEP}}-\lambda\sigma_{-},\;\sigma_{-}\in\text{\rm SEP}\right\},

where
\leq_{\text{\rm SEP}}
denotes inequality with respect to the cone of separable operators.
Let
\rho=(1+\lambda)\,\sigma_{+}-\lambda\sigma_{-}
be any feasible decomposition for
\rho
.
Then, for any
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta,s}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda)\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}-\lambda\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\frac{\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}

(S86)

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{\rm SEP}}(1+\lambda)\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}(1+\delta)\,\sigma^{\prime}_{+}+\lambda\frac{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\delta\,\sigma^{\prime}_{-}

for some
\sigma^{\prime}_{\pm}\in\text{\rm SEP}
, where we used that
\mathcal{E}
can only generate at most
\delta
robustness from any separable state. Since the operator in the last line is a non-negative combination of separable operators it is separable itself, and therefore it constitutes a feasible solution for the robustness of
\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}
. We can find its normalisation by simply taking the trace. Thus

\displaystyle 1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(\rho)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\lambda)(1+\delta)\,\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{+})+\lambda\delta\,\Tr\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{-})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}

(S87)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\lambda)(1+\delta)+\lambda\delta}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\lambda)(1+\delta)+(1+\lambda)\delta}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\lambda)(1+2\delta)}{\Tr\mathcal{E}(\rho)}

using the trace–non-increasing property of
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
S9
.

Consider first
{\rm ANE},N
cost. Let
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
be any sequence of operations
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta_{n},N}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}-\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right\rVert_{\,1}\eqqcolon\varepsilon_{n}
.
Using that
R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})=2^{n}-1

Vidal and Tarrach 1999
, we have

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\log\left(1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})\right)

(S88)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(i)}}}}{{\geq}}\log\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})^{\Gamma}}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}\right\rVert_{\,1}+\log\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})-\log 2-\log(1+2\delta_{n})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(ii)}}}}{{\geq}}E_{\tau}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)+\log(1-2\varepsilon_{n})+\log\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})-\log 2-\log(1+2\delta_{n})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(iii)}}}}{{\geq}}\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor E_{\tau}(\rho)+\log(1-2\varepsilon_{n})+\log\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})-\log 2-\log(1+2\delta_{n}),

where: (i) follows from Lemma
S10
; (ii) follows from Proposition S5 and Lemma S6 of
Lami and Regula 2023a
(cf. proofs of Theorems 1 and S16 therein); (iii) follows from the super-additivity of
E_{\tau}
, shown in Proposition S5 of
Lami and Regula 2023a
.
Assuming that
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})=2^{-o(n)}
,
\delta_{n}=2^{o(n)}
and
\limsup_{n}\varepsilon_{n}<\frac{1}{2}
(which are all, in fact, weaker than our assumptions) we can divide by
n
and take the
\liminf
as
n\to\infty
to get

\displaystyle r^{-1}\geq E_{\tau}(\rho),

(S89)

which is what was to be shown.

For the case of
{\rm ANE},s
, it is immediate from the definitions that
E_{c,{\rm ANE},s}^{p>0}(\rho)\leq E_{c,{\rm NE}}^{p=1}(\rho)
, so we need to show the opposite inequality.
We know from the results of Brandão and Plenio
(
Brandão and Plenio 2010a
, Sec. V)
that

\displaystyle E_{c,{\rm NE}}^{p=1}(\rho)=\inf_{(\zeta_{n})_{n}}\left\{\left.\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[1+R^{s,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0\right\},

(S90)

where we denoted

\displaystyle R^{s,\zeta}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho)\coloneqq\min_{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho^{\prime}-\rho\right\rVert_{\,1}\leq\zeta}R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\rho^{\prime}).

(S91)

Although an exact expression for the quantity on the right-hand side of (
S90
) is not known (unlike in the asymptotic equipartition property for
R^{g}_{\text{\rm SEP}}
that we used earlier), we can still use it to establish a converse bound. Take any sequence of operations
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n}
with
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in\mathbb{O}_{{\rm NE},\delta_{n},s}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}-\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right\rVert_{\,1}\eqqcolon\varepsilon_{n}
with
\liminf_{n}\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})>0
and
\lim_{n}\varepsilon_{n}=0
. We then have that

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=\log\left(1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})\right)

(S92)

\displaystyle\geq\log\left(1+R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}{\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})}\right)\right)+\log\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})-\log(1+2\delta_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\log\left(1+R^{s,\,\varepsilon_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\right)+\log\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})-\log(1+2\delta_{n})

using Lemma
S10
. As before, the two rightmost terms will vanish asymptotically provided that
\Tr\mathcal{E}_{n}(\Phi_{+}^{\otimes n})=2^{-o(n)}
and
\delta_{n}=2^{o(n)}
. Dividing by
n
, taking the
\limsup
, and using that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=0
gives

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{(\zeta_{n})_{n}}\left\{\left.\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\right]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0\right\}.

(S93)

To argue that
r^{-1}\geq E_{c,{\rm NE}}^{p=1}(\rho)
, it thus remains to show that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\inf_{(\zeta_{n})_{n}}\left\{\left.\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\right]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0\right\}

(S94)

\displaystyle\geq r\,\inf_{(\zeta_{n})_{n}}\left\{\left.\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}=0\right\}

\displaystyle=r\,E_{c,{\rm NE}}^{p=1}(\rho).

To see this, let us begin by defining for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the corresponding quantity

\displaystyle n^{(r)}\coloneqq\min\left\{\left.\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;l\in\mathbb{N},\;\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor\geq n\right\}.

(S95)

For any fixed asymptotically vanishing error sequence
(\zeta_{n})_{n}
, we also define

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{(r)}\coloneqq\max\left\{\left.\zeta_{l}\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;l\in\mathbb{N},\;\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor\geq n\right\}.

(S96)

Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}{n}\frac{1}{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\right]

(S97)

\displaystyle=r\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(i)}}}}{{\geq}}r\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{(r)}}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}^{(r)}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes n^{(r)}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(ii)}}}}{{\geq}}r\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{(r)}}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}^{(r)}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(iii)}}}}{{\geq}}r\,\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n+\left\lceil r\right\rceil}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta_{n}^{(r)}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\scriptsize\mbox{(iv)}}}}{{\geq}}r\,\inf_{(\zeta^{\prime}_{n})_{n}}\left\{\left.\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[1+R^{s,\,\zeta^{\prime}_{n}}_{\text{\rm SEP}}\!\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right]\;\rule{0.0pt}{9.5pt}\right|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta^{\prime}_{n}=0\right\},

where: (i) follows since
(n^{(r)})_{n}
is simply a subsequence of
(\left\lfloor rn\right\rfloor)_{n}
, up to possible repetitions, and for any
l
such that
n^{(r)}=\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor
, it holds that
\zeta_{n}^{(r)}\geq\zeta_{l}
by definition; (ii)
n^{(r)}\geq n
and discarding copies can only decrease
R^{s}_{\text{\rm SEP}}
(as per Axiom III, clearly obeyed by the resource theory of entanglement); (iii) is a consequence of the fact that

\displaystyle n^{(r)}-\left\lceil r\right\rceil=\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor-\left\lceil r\right\rceil\leq\left\lfloor rl-r\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor r(l-1)\right\rfloor\leq n

(S98)

since each
n^{(r)}
is the
least
value of
\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor
such that
\left\lfloor rl\right\rfloor\geq n
; finally, (iv) follows since adding a constant in the denominator will not affect the asymptotic limit, and
(\zeta_{n}^{(r)})_{n}
is still a vanishing error sequence. This shows Eq. (
S94
) and thus concludes the proof of the Lemma.

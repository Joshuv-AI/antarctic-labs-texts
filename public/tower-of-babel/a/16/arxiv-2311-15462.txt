Abstract

Introduction

Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules as a generalization of Hilbert spaces were first introduced by
I. Kaplansky (
[
6
]
) in 1953 in special cases and later by W. Paschke (
[

]

-algebras in 1973.
Hilbert

-modules are crucial to Kasparov’s formulation of

-theory (
[

]
).
Early applications also include

-algebraic quantum group theory (see
[
16
]
). Later in the study of Cuntz -semigroups in connection with the classification of amenable

-algebras, Hilbert

-modules play an important role (see, for example,

[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
).

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Unlike Hilbert spaces,
bounded module maps on a Hilbert
A
-module

may not have adjoints and the dual module
H^{\sharp},
i.e., the Banach

-module of all bounded module
maps from
H
to
A
may not be identified as elements in

Moreover, the
C^{*}
-algebra
L(H)
of all bounded module maps
with adjoints may not be a
W^{*}
-algebras. If
H_{0}\subset H
is a Hilbert
A
-submodule,
a bounded module map
\varphi:H_{0}\to A
may not be extended to a bounded module map from

H
to
A.
In general, one should not expect that
H
to be decomposed to an orthogonal direct
sum of
H_{0}
and its orthogonal complement. In fact,

H_{0}
may not even have an orthogonal complement.
Study of these phenomena may be found, for eaxmples, in
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
and more recently
in
[
1
]
.

However, Paschke found (see
[
13
]
) out that, if
A
is a
W^{*}
-algebra, then
the dual module
H^{\sharp}
of a Hilbert
A
-module

can be made into
a Hilbert
A
-module in a natural way which extends

and

is a self-dual Hilbert

-module.
Even if

is not a
W^{*}
-algebra, one can extend

into an
A^{**}
-module

naturally. Then its dual

becomes a self-dual Hilbert

A^{**}
-module containing

In fact,

is the smallest

A^{**}
-module containing

as a Hilbert

-submodule (see Proposition

).
Paschke showed the Banach algebra of all bounded module maps on

becomes a

-algebra.

For a Hilbert

-module

the rank one module maps
are the module maps

of the form

Denote by

the linear span of rank one module maps and denote by

the norm closure of

is a

-algebra  and an important algebra related

It was proved by Kasparov (see Theorem 1 of
[
7
]
) that the
C^{*}
-algebra

may be identified with

the multiplier algebra of

and it was proved in

with the left multipliers of

(all Hilbert

Over the decades, we eventually realized
that it is rather convenient to work in

It is not difficult to establish a natural normal homomorphism

which extends beyond

and

It remained unknown for many years whether

\Psi
is an isomorphism.
The original motivation of this paper is to show that indeed

is
an isomorphism between
W^{*}
-algebras
K(H)^{**}
and
B(H^{\sim}).

As we study the relation among Hilbert modules
H,

H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptA^{**}
and

naturally, we ask how dense of

in
H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptA^{**}
and in

Since

We first note that it was shown (Theorem 6.1 of

there is a net

in

for all

such that
\lim_{\alpha}\|\xi(x)-\langle x_{\alpha},x\rangle\|=0
for all

However, we show here
that the unit ball of

is closed in

in the topology
where

if and only if

is extended to

On the other hand,

there is a net

in

such that

is the Hilbert space corresponding to the universal representation

of

To be a more useful approximation, one may ask

). We will present a

Since

is the dual of

it is relatively easy to show that, for any

is a net

in

It is more challenging to show that

there is a net
\{x_{\alpha}\}
in

with
\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq\|\xi\|
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\xi-x_{\alpha}\rangle)=0\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,f\in A^{*}.

\langle(F(a)-T)(x),y\rangle=0\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,x,y\in H.

Hence

In other words,

A Kaplansky density theorem in Hilbert modules

As mentioned in the introduction, in this section we study the density
of
H
in
H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptA^{**}.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
A\subset B(X)
be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra  of
B(H).

Let

the strong operator closure of

and

Recall, by Proposition

We consider the question how large

in

Let

and

be a finite subset of

For each

(e 4.146)

be the topology generated by

for all
\xi\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM,

\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\{0\},

any finite subset

if and only if

(e 4.147)

In the special case that

is the Hilbert space corresponding to the universal representation

of

and

we use
{\cal T}_{su}

for the topology
generated by

for all

It is easy to see that

is dense in

However, to be more useful, we will show in Theorem

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
x\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM
and
\{x_{\alpha}\}\subset H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM

Then

x_{\alpha}\to x
in
{\cal T}_{s}
if and only if, for any

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{\|\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle(v)\|:y\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM,\,\,\|y\|\leq 1\}=0.

(e 4.148)

Moreover, if
x_{\alpha}\to x
in
{\cal T}_{s},

then, for any
f\in M_{*},

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{|f(\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle)|:y\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM,\,\,\|y\|\leq 1\}=0.

(e 4.149)

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{\alpha}\to x
in
{\cal T}_{s}.

We have (see (ii) of Proposition 2.3 of
[
13
]
), for any

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}-x,y\rangle\cdot\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle\leq\|y\|^{2}\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle.

(e 4.150)

Then, for any
v\in X,
and any
y\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM
with
\|y\|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\|\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle(v)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\langle x_{\alpha}-x,y\rangle\cdot\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle v,v\rangle_{X}

(e 4.151)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y\|^{2}\langle\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle v,v\rangle_{X}

(e 4.152)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle v\|\|v\|\to 0

(e 4.153)

(where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
is the inner product in the Hilbert space
X
).
Conversely, let
K=\sup_{\alpha}\{\|x_{\alpha}\|+\|x\|\}+1.

Then

\displaystyle\|\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle(v)\|\leq K\sup\{\|\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle(v)\|:y\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM,\,\,\|y\|\leq 1\}\to 0

(e 4.154)

For the “Moreover” part of the lemma,
suppose that

in the strong operator topology.
Then it converges in the weak operator topology. However
\{\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle\}
is bounded,
this also implies that it converges to zero in
\sigma
-weak topology and in weak*-topology.
In other words,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}f(\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle)=0\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,f\in M_{*}.

(e 4.155)

Let
f\in M_{*}
be a positive normal functional.
Then,
f(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
defines a pseudo inner product on

Hence,
for any

we have, by Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|f(\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle)|^{2}\leq f(\langle y,y\rangle)f(\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle)\leq\|f\|^{2}\|y\|^{2}f(\langle x_{\alpha}-x,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle).

(e 4.156)

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{|f(\langle y,x_{\alpha}-x\rangle)|:y\in H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM,\,\,\|y\|\leq 1\}=0.

(e 4.157)

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be a Hilbert space,

be a

-subalgebra  and

Then the unit ball of

is dense in the unit ball of

in

Proof.

Let
\xi\in H_{A}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM
with
\|\xi\|\leq 1.

We will show that there is a net

such that

\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq\|\xi\|
and
\lim_{\alpha}\|\langle x_{\alpha}-\xi,x_{\alpha}-\xi\rangle(v)\|=0

for all

From the inequality

\|\langle x_{\alpha}-\xi,x_{\alpha}-\xi\rangle(v)\|\leq\|\langle x_{\alpha}-\xi,x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle^{1/2}\|\|\langle x_{\alpha}-\xi,x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle^{1/2}(v)\|\leq 2\|\|\langle x_{\alpha}-\xi,x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle^{1/2}(v)\|,

such that

\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq\|\xi\|
and
\lim_{\alpha}\|\langle x_{\alpha}-\xi,x_{\alpha}-\xi\rangle^{1/2}v\|=0

for all

Therefore
it suffices to show that, for any
\epsilon>0

and
any finite subset

there exists
z\in H
with
\|z\|\leq 1
such
that

\displaystyle\|(\langle\xi-z,\xi-z\rangle)^{1/2}(v)\|<\epsilon\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,v\in V.

(e 4.158)

To simplify notation, we may also assume
that
\|v\|\leq 1
for all
v\in V.

Denote by
R={\overline{AM}},
the closed right ideal of
M
generated by
A.

Note, by Proposition
3.6
,

\displaystyle H_{A}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM=\{\{b_{n}\}\in H_{B}:b_{n}\in R\}.

(e 4.159)

We write
\xi=\{b_{n}\}\in H_{A}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM.

There exists

such that, for all

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}b_{n}^{*}b_{n}\|<\epsilon/2.

(e 4.160)

Fix an integer

Let
P_{n}:H_{A}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM\to R^{(n)}=\{(c_{1},c_{2},...,c_{n}):c_{i}\in R\}
be the projection.
Put

\displaystyle S=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&0&0\cdots&0\\
b_{2}&0&0\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
b_{n}&0&0\dots&0\end{pmatrix}.

(e 4.161)

For any

put

\displaystyle u_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(e 4.162)

By the Kaplansky density theorem,

such that

\displaystyle\|L\|\leq\|S\|\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\|L(u_{v})-S(u_{v})\|<\epsilon/2

(e 4.163)

for all

Hence, denoting by

the inner product in

\displaystyle\langle(L-S)^{*}(L-S)u_{v},u_{v}\rangle_{X}<\epsilon/2\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,v\in V.

(e 4.164)

Define

Then

Replacing

we may write

\displaystyle L=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&0\cdots&0\\
a_{2}&0&0\cdots&0\\

(e 4.165)

where

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}\|=\|L^{*}L\|=\|L\|^{2}\leq\|S\|^{2}=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}\|\leq\|\xi\|^{2}.

(e 4.166)

It follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{*}(b_{i}-a_{i})(v),v\rangle_{X}<\epsilon/2.

Then, by (

\|x\|\leq\|\xi\|,
and

\displaystyle\hskip-28.90755pt\langle\langle\xi-x,\xi-x\rangle(v),v\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{*}(b_{i}-a_{i})(v),v\rangle_{X}+\langle\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}(v),v\rangle_{X}

(e 4.168)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\epsilon/2+\|\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}b_{i}^{*}b_{i}\|<\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon.

(e 4.169)

In other words, for any

\displaystyle\|(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\xi-x_{\alpha})^{1/2}(v)\|=\langle\langle\xi-x,\xi-x\rangle(v),v\rangle_{X}<\epsilon.

(e 4.170)

The lemma then follows.

5
Closeness of
H

Let
H
be a Hilbert
A
-module.,
Then, by Theorem 6.1 of
[
1
]

is
A
-weakly dense (see Definition 3.3 of
[

]
) in the unit ball of
H^{\sharp},

i.e., for any
f\in H^{\sharp},
there is a net
\{x_{\alpha}\}
in
H
with

such that

\lim_{\alpha}\|\langle f-x_{\alpha},y\rangle\|=0
for all
y\in H.
In the case that
A
is a
W^{*}
-algebra,

H^{\sharp}
is a Hilbert

one can find the net
\{x_{\alpha}\},
in addition,
\lim_{\alpha}\|\langle f-x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle\|=0
for all

We begin with the following example.

Example 5.1
.

Let
M
be a
W^{*}
-algebra which contains a self-adjoint element
a
with infinite spectrum.
Then, by the spectral theory, one obtains a sequence of mutually orthogonal non-zero projections

p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n},....

Let

and
let

Note that
\|\xi\|=\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}\|=1

(the convergence is in the strong operator topology and weak*-topology of

We claim that there is
no

\{x_{\alpha}\}
in

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\|\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle\|=0.

(e 5.180)

Otherwise,
there would be

such that

\displaystyle\|\langle\xi-x,\xi\rangle\|<1/4.

(e 5.181)

Since

there is

\displaystyle\|\sum_{N+1}a_{n}^{*}a_{n}\|<(1/16(1+\|x\|))^{2}.

(e 5.182)

Choose

Define

Then

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\langle\xi-x,\xi\rangle\|+\|\langle(1-P_{N})(x),\xi\rangle\|

(e 5.183)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1/4+\|(1-P_{N})(x)\|\|\xi\|<1/4+1/16=5/16.

(e 5.184)

On the other hand

\displaystyle 5/16

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\langle\xi-P(x),\xi\rangle\|\geq\|\langle\xi-P(x),\xi\rangle q\|

(e 5.186)

\displaystyle=\|(\sum_{N+1}^{\infty}p_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(p_{i}-a_{i})^{*}p_{i})q\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{N+1}^{\infty}p_{n}q\|=1.

(e 5.187)

A contradiction. In other words, the question at the beginning of this section is negative.
This also follows from Corollary
5.7
below. However, we think that the example above
might also be helpful.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra.
Suppose that
\xi\in H_{A}^{\sharp}
and
\{x_{\alpha}\}
is a bounded
net such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\|\xi(x)-x_{\alpha}(x)\|=0\,\,\,\text{for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,x\in H_{A}

(e 5.188)

and

Write
\xi=\{b_{n}\}
and
x_{\alpha}=\{a_{{\alpha},n}\},

where
\{b_{n}\}\in H_{A}^{\sharp},

a_{{\alpha},n}\in A

and, for each
{\alpha},

\{a_{{\alpha},n}\}\in H_{A}.

Put

\displaystyle M=1+\sup\{\|x_{\alpha}\|:{\alpha}\}+\|\xi\|<\infty\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,a=\lim_{\alpha}\langle x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle.

(e 5.189)

Note
\xi(x_{\alpha})=\langle\xi,x_{\alpha}\rangle\in A
for all
{\alpha}.
Hence
a\in A.

Put
P_{n}:H_{A}^{\sharp}\to H_{n}:=A^{(n)}
be the projection to the first

Then
P_{n}\xi\in H_{n}\subset H_{A}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\langle x_{\alpha},P_{n}(\xi)\rangle=\langle\xi,P_{n}(\xi)\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{*}b_{j}.

(e 5.190)

Fix
f\in A^{*}.
Let

By Lemma
3.8
, since
\{x_{\alpha}\}
is bounded, there is an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all

\displaystyle|f(\langle x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle)-f(x_{\alpha},P_{n}(\xi)\rangle)|<\epsilon/3\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,{\alpha}.

(e 5.191)

Fix any

Choose

such that, for all

\displaystyle\|\langle x_{\alpha},P_{n}(\xi)\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{*}b_{j}\|<\epsilon/3(1+\|f\|)\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,

(e 5.192)

\displaystyle\|\langle x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle-a\|<\epsilon/3(1+\|f\|).

(e 5.193)

It follows that, for all

\displaystyle|f(a)-f(\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{*}b_{j})|

\displaystyle|f(a-\langle x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle)|+|f(\langle x_{\alpha},\xi\rangle)-f(x_{\alpha},P_{n}(\xi)\rangle)|

\displaystyle+\|f\|\|\langle x_{\alpha},P_{n}(\xi)\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{*}b_{j}\|<\epsilon/3+\epsilon/3+\epsilon/3=\epsilon.

of

is a continuous

on

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}^{*}b_{j}\to a

(e 5.197)

in norm. This implies that

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra  and

a_{\alpha}\in H_{A}.
Then
\|a_{\alpha}\|\leq\|b\|.

Thus (
e 6.233
) holds.
On the other hand, by (
e 6.242
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\|({\bar{b}}-d_{\alpha}^{\prime})^{*}({\bar{b}}-d_{\alpha}^{\prime})P_{1}v\|=0.

(e 6.244)

Let
h\in X.
By (
e 6.244
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\,\,\,\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})^{*}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})h\|=0.

(e 6.245)

In other words,
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})^{*}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})=\langle b-a_{\alpha},b-a_{\alpha}\rangle\to 0
in the strong operator topology.
However,

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})^{*}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})\|=\|({\bar{b}}-d_{\alpha}^{\prime})\|^{2}\leq(\|\bar{b}\|+\|d_{\alpha}\|)^{2}\leq 4\|b\|^{2}.

(e 6.246)

Therefore
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})^{*}(b_{j}-a_{j,{\alpha}})\to 0
in the
\sigma
-weak operator topology
and hence in weak*-topology (see, for example, 4.6.13 of
[
15
]
). Therefore (
e 6.234
) holds.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A\subset B(X)
be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra
and let
M={\bar{A}}^{SOT}
with
1_{X}\in M.

Suppose that
H
is a countably generated Hilbert
A
-module.
Then
H
is dense in
(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
in the following sense:
For any
\xi\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},
there is a net
x_{\alpha}\in H
with

\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq\|\xi\|
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{|f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\zeta\rangle)|:\zeta\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}=0\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,f\in M_{*}.

(e 6.247)

Proof.

Let us first prove this for
H=H_{A},
even though when
A
is not
\sigma
-unital,

H_{A}
is not countably generated.
Lemma
6.2
provides a net
\{x_{\alpha}\}
in
H_{A}

with
\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq\|\xi\|
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\xi-x_{\alpha}\rangle)=0\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,f\in M_{*}.

(e 6.248)

Recall that, for any positive linear functional
f,

H_{M}^{\sharp}\times H_{M}^{\sharp}\to\mathbb{R}
defined
by
[x,y]_{f}=f(\langle x,y\rangle)
(for all
x,y\in H_{M}^{\sharp}
) is a pseudo inner product.
Therefore, by the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle f(\langle x,y\rangle)^{2}\leq f(\langle x,x\rangle)f(\langle y,y\rangle)\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,x,y\in H_{M}^{\sharp}.

(e 6.249)

It follows that, for any positive normal linear functional
f,

\displaystyle\sup\{|f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\zeta\rangle)|:\zeta\in H_{M}^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\{f(\langle\zeta,\zeta\rangle)f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\xi-x_{\alpha}\rangle):\zeta\in H_{M}^{\sharp},\,\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}

(e 6.250)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\xi-x_{\alpha}\rangle)\to 0.

Thus we proved (
e 6.247
) holds for
H=H_{A}.

Now let
H
be a countably generated Hilbert
A
-module.
Then, by Kasparov’s absorbing theorem, we may write
H_{A}=H\oplus H^{\perp}.

Hence
H_{A}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM=H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM\oplus(H^{\perp}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM).

It follows that
H_{M}^{\sharp}=(H_{A}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}=(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}\oplus(H^{\perp}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}.

Let
P:H_{M}^{\sharp}\to(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
be the projection such that
P|_{H}={\rm id}_{H}.

Then, by what has been proved for
H_{A},

there is a net
y_{\alpha}\in H_{A}
such
that
\|y_{\alpha}\|\leq\|\xi\|
and, for any
f\in M_{*},

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{f(\langle\xi-y_{\alpha},\zeta\rangle):\zeta\in H_{M}^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}=0.

(e 6.251)

Put
x_{\alpha}=P(y_{\alpha})\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}.

Note that
P(\xi)=\xi.
Then, for any
f\in M_{*},

\displaystyle\hskip-43.36243pt\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},\zeta\rangle):\zeta\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}

(e 6.252)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{f(\langle\xi-x_{\alpha},P(\zeta)\rangle):\zeta\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{f(\langle\xi-y_{\alpha},\zeta\rangle):\zeta\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\sup\{f(\langle\xi-y_{\alpha},\zeta\rangle):\zeta\in H_{M}^{\sharp},\|\zeta\|\leq 1\}=0.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,

A\subset B(X)
a
C^{*}
-subalgebra  and
M=\overline{A}^{SOT}

with
1_{M}={\rm id}_{X},

and let
H
be a Hilbert
A
-module.
Then the unit ball of
H
is dense in the unit ball of
(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
in
{\cal T}_{ws}
(of
(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
).

Proof.

Let
\xi\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
with
\|\xi\|\leq 1.
It suffices to show that,
for any
\epsilon>0,
any finite subset
Y\subset(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
and any finite subset
{\cal F}\subset M_{*},

there is
x\in H
such that

\displaystyle\|x\|\leq\|\xi\|\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,|f(\langle\xi-x,y\rangle)|<\epsilon\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\|y\|\leq 1,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,f\in{\cal F}.

(e 6.253)

Let us fix
\epsilon
and
{\cal F}.

Choose an approximate identity
\{E_{\lambda}\}
for
K(H).

It follows that
E_{\lambda}\nearrow{\rm id}_{H}.
Note that

{\rm id}_{H}\in M(K(H)).
By the last part of Proposition
2.13
,

\Psi_{0}({\rm id}_{H})={\rm id}_{H\hskip-0.72229pt\bullet\hskip-0.72229ptM}.
By 3.17 of
[
13
]
,

F\circ\Psi_{0}({\rm id}_{H})={\rm id}_{(H\hskip-0.72229pt\bullet\hskip-0.72229ptM)^{\sharp}},
where
F
is the map given by Proposition
2.16
.
Note also that, by Lemma
2.9
,
\{\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})\}
is an approximate identity for

K(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM).

In the universal representation of
K(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM),

1-\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})
converges to zero in the strong operator topology.
Note that
\|1-\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})\|\leq 1.

Therefore
(1-\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda}))^{*}(1-\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda}))
also converges to zero in the strong operator topology. Hence (since {
\|(1-\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda}))^{*}(1-\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda}))\|\}
is bounded,) it converges to zero in the weak*-topology.
By Proposition
2.16
,
we have, for all
f\in M_{*},

\displaystyle f(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi)\rangle)

(e 6.254)

\displaystyle=f(\langle(1-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda}))^{*}(1-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda}))(\xi),\xi\rangle)\to 0.

(e 6.255)

Next let
g
be a positive normal linear functional in
M_{*}.

Then, for any
y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
with
\|y\|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\hskip-36.135pt|g(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),y\rangle)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi)\rangle)g(\langle y,y\rangle)

(e 6.256)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|\|y\|^{2}g(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi)\rangle).

(e 6.257)

Hence, by (
e 6.255
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\big(\sup\{|g(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),y\rangle)|:y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\,\|y\|\leq 1\}\big)=0.

(e 6.258)

It follows that, for any
f\in M_{*},

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\big(\sup\{|f(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),y\rangle)|:y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\,\|y\|\leq 1\}\big)=0.

(e 6.259)

Thus we obtain

\lambda_{0}
such that,
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0},

\displaystyle|f(\langle\xi-F\circ\Psi_{0}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),y\rangle)|<\epsilon/2\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp},\,\|y\|\leq 1,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,f\in{\cal F}.

(e 6.260)

Let
H_{\lambda}=\overline{E_{\lambda}(H)}.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.12
,
H_{\lambda}
is countably
generated. Moreover, by Lemma
3.1
,

(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}=(H_{\lambda}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}\oplus((H_{\lambda}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp})^{\perp}.

Let
P_{\lambda}:(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}\to(H_{\lambda}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}

be the projection.
Note that

\Psi_{\lambda}(E_{\lambda})(\xi),\Psi_{\lambda}(E_{\lambda})(y)\in P_{\lambda}((H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp})=(H_{\lambda}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}

for all
y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}.

It follows from Lemma
6.3
that there is
x\in H_{\lambda}
with
\|x\|\leq\|\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi)\|\leq\|\xi\|

such that

\displaystyle|f(\langle\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi)-x,P_{\lambda}(y)\rangle)|<\epsilon/2\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,y\in(H_{\lambda}\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\|y\|\leq 1.

(e 6.261)

Since

and

we have, for all

\displaystyle|f(\langle\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi)-x,y\rangle)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(\langle P_{\lambda}\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi)-P_{\lambda}(x),y\rangle)|

(e 6.262)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(\langle\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi)-x,P_{\lambda}(y)\rangle)|<\epsilon/2.

(e 6.263)

Thus, for all
y\in(H\hskip-2.168pt\bullet\hskip-2.168ptM)^{\sharp}
with

and

\displaystyle|f(\langle\xi-x,y\rangle)|\leq|f(\langle\xi-\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi),y\rangle)|+|f(\langle\Psi(E_{\lambda})(\xi)-x,y\rangle)|<\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon.

(e 6.264)

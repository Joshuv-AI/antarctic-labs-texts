Semiclassical analysis and the Agmon-Finsler metric for discrete Schrödinger operators
Kentaro Kameoka

Abstract
The Agmon estimate for multi-dimensional discrete Schrödinger operators is studied with emphasis on the microlocal analysis on the torus. We first consider the semiclassical setting where semiclassical continuous Schrödinger operators are discretized with the mesh width proportional to the semiclassical parameter. Under this setting, the Agmon estimate for eigenfunctions is described by an Agmon metric, which is a Finsler metric rather than a Riemannian metric. Klein-Rosenberger (2008) proved this by a different argument in the case of a potential minimum. We also prove the Agmon estimate and the optimal anisotropic exponential decay of eigenfunctions for discrete Schrödinger operators in the non-semiclassical standard setting.

Introduction

We first discuss the semiclassical Agmon estimate for discrete Schrödinger operators.
We start with a continuous semiclassical Schrödinger operator

H^{\mathrm{cont}}=H^{\mathrm{cont}}(h)=-h^{2}\Delta+V(x)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{on}\hskip 8.5359ptL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

For simplicity, we put

In this paper, the semiclassical discrete Fourier transform

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic facts on microlocal analysis on the torus.
We identify functions on
T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}
or
\mathbb{T}^{d}

For

and for

we define

a(\xi,hD_{\xi})u(\xi)=(2\pi h)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\xi,x)e^{i\langle\xi-\eta,x\rangle/h}u(\eta)d\eta dx

The corresponding class of pseudodifferential operators is denoted by

Lemma 2.1
.

V(hD_{\xi})=\mathcal{F}_{h}V(x)\mathcal{F}_{h}^{-1}
for

.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle V(hD_{\xi})u(\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi h)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)e^{i\langle\xi-\eta,x\rangle/h}u(\eta)d\eta dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi h)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)\left(\sum_{x\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(2\pi)^{d/2}h^{d}(\mathcal{F}_{h}^{-1}u)(x)\delta_{x}\right)e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle/h}dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\sum_{x\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V(x)(\mathcal{F}_{h}^{-1}u)(x)e^{\langle\xi,x\rangle/h}

for

, which completes the proof.
∎

Although we use the spacial structure of the torus to define

,
we can employ the general theory of pseudodifferential operators on manifolds
including the functional calculus and the trace formula for pseudodifferential operators
(see
[

, Chapter 5, 14]
).
To illustrate these, we give the Weyl law.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
,
\underline{\lim}_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)\geq 0

and there exists
0<\theta\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}V(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}(1+|x|)^{-\theta|\alpha|}

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}
.
Then for any fixed
a<b<0
, the number
N_{[a,b]}(h)
of eigenvalues of
H(h)
in
[a,b]
satisfies

N_{[a,b]}(h)=(2\pi h)^{-d}\mathrm{Vol}(\{(\xi,x)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}|\,a\leq p(\xi,x)\leq b\})+o(h^{-d})

when
h\to 0
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Take small
\varepsilon>0
and
\chi_{1,\varepsilon},\chi_{2,\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])

such that
\chi_{1,\varepsilon}=1
on
[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]
,
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\chi_{1,\varepsilon}\subset[a-2\varepsilon,b+2\varepsilon]
,

\chi_{2,\varepsilon}=1
on
[a+2\varepsilon,b-2\varepsilon]
and
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\chi_{2,\varepsilon}\subset[a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon]
.
Then we have

\mathrm{tr}(\chi_{2,\varepsilon}(\widetilde{H}(h)))\leq N_{[a,b]}(\widetilde{H}(h))\leq\mathrm{tr}(\chi_{1,\varepsilon}(\widetilde{H}(h)))

since
N_{[a,b]}(\widetilde{H}(h))=\mathrm{tr}(\chi_{[a,b]}(\widetilde{H}(h)))

and
\chi_{2,\varepsilon}\leq\chi_{[a,b]}\leq\chi_{1,\varepsilon}
.

The functional calculus and the trace formula for pseudodifferential operators imply that

\mathrm{tr}(\chi_{j,\varepsilon}(\widetilde{H}(h)))=(2\pi h)^{-d}\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{j,\varepsilon}(p(\xi,x))d\xi dx+\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}(h^{-d+1})

for

.
We note that

, which follows from Fubini’s theorem
and the definition of

.
Then we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{j,\varepsilon}(p(\xi,x))d\xi dx=\mathrm{Vol}(\{(\xi,x)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}|\,a\leq p(\xi,x)\leq b\})

for

Take any

. Then for sufficiently small

, the above arguments imply that

-\delta-\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}(h)\leq(2\pi h)^{d}N_{[a,b]}(\widetilde{H}(h))-\mathrm{Vol}(\{(\xi,x)|\,a\leq p(\xi,x)\leq b\})\leq\delta+\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}(h).

Taking
h\to 0
and then taking

, the proof is finished.
∎

]

Semiclassical Agmon estimate

In this section, we prove Theorem

3.1
Calculation of exponentially conjugated operator

Take any
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
.
We compute
\widetilde{H}_{\rho}(h)=e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}\widetilde{H}(h)e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}
.
Since we have
e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}V(hD_{\xi})e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}=V(hD_{\xi})
,
we only have to consider
e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}p_{0}(\xi)e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}
,
where
p_{0}(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(2-2\cos\xi_{j})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
,

e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}p_{0}(\xi)e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}=a_{\rho}(\xi,hD_{\xi};h)\in\mathrm{Op}S,

where
a_{\rho}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h^{k}a_{\rho,k}(\xi,x)
with
a_{\rho,k}\in S
and

a_{\rho,0}(\xi,x)=p_{0}(\xi-i\partial\rho(x),x).

If moreover

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\rho(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}\langle x\rangle^{1-|\alpha|}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for any}\hskip 5.69046pt\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0},

(2)

then
a_{\rho}\in S^{0}
and
a_{\rho,k}\in S^{-k}

Proof.

We set
g(x)=e^{-|x|^{2}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle e^{-\rho(hD_{\hat{\eta}})/h}u(\hat{\eta})

\displaystyle e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}p_{0}(\xi)e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}u(\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi h)^{-2d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i\langle\xi-\hat{\eta},y\rangle/h}\hskip 2.84544pt{}^{t}L_{2}^{N}e^{\rho(y)/h}p_{0}(\hat{\eta})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i\langle\hat{\eta}-\eta,x\rangle/h}

\displaystyle\hskip 184.9429pt{}^{t}L_{1}^{2N}\bigl(e^{-\rho(x)/h}u(\eta)\bigr)dxd\eta dyd\hat{\eta}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(2\pi h)^{-2d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}e^{i\langle\xi-\hat{\eta},y\rangle/h}e^{\rho(y)/h}e^{i\langle\hat{\eta}-\eta,x\rangle/h}e^{-\rho(x)/h}u(\eta)

\displaystyle\hskip 176.407ptg(\varepsilon x)g(\varepsilon\eta)g(\varepsilon y)g(\varepsilon\hat{\eta})dxd\eta dyd\hat{\eta},

where

L_{2}=\frac{1-y\cdot hD_{\hat{\eta}}+(\xi-\hat{\eta})\cdot hD_{y}}{1+|y|^{2}+|\hat{\eta}-\xi|^{2}}.

We set
\rho(y)-\rho(x)=(y-x)\cdot\Phi(x,y)
with
\Phi(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial\rho(y+t(x-y))dt
.
We deform the integral and obtain

\displaystyle e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}p_{0}(\xi)e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}u(\xi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(2\pi h)^{-2d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}e^{i\langle\xi-\hat{\eta},y\rangle/h}e^{i\langle\hat{\eta}-\eta,x\rangle/h}p_{0}(\hat{\eta}-i\Phi(x,y))u(\eta)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378ptg(\varepsilon x)g(\varepsilon\eta)g(\varepsilon y)g(\varepsilon\hat{\eta}-\varepsilon i\Phi(x,y))d\eta dxd\hat{\eta}dy.

Using
{}^{t}L_{2}^{N}
and
{}^{t}L_{1}^{2N}
, we see that

\displaystyle e^{\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}p_{0}(\xi)e^{-\rho(hD_{\xi})/h}u(\xi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0}(2\pi h)^{-2d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}e^{i\langle\xi-\hat{\eta},y\rangle/h}e^{i\langle\hat{\eta}-\eta,x\rangle/h}p_{0}(\hat{\eta}-i\Phi(x,y))u(\eta)

has an analytic continuation to

\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}|\,-\mathrm{Im}\,\xi\in\Omega\}
.
Since
4\sin^{2}\xi_{j}/2=-4\sinh^{2}\mathrm{Im}\,\xi_{j}/2
for
\mathrm{Re}\,\xi_{j}=0
,
this implies
\Omega\subset K^{E}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For
d=1
, it is known that
u_{E}(x)=(|E|(4+|E|))^{-1/2}e^{-\rho_{E}(x)}
(

9

Ito-Jensen

proved that
u_{E}
is expressed

.
The precise asymptotics of

when

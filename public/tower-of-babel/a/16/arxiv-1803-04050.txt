On terminal Fano 3-folds with a 2-torus action, part II
Michele Nicolussi

Abstract
. We continue the classification of terminal Fano threefolds with an effective two-torus action. In earlier work we settled the ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -factorial case with Picard number one. Here we treat the larger class of varieties that do not admit any contraction of a prime divisor; these are called combinatorially minimal.



1.
Introduction

This article contributes to the classification of Fano varieties,
meaning normal projective varieties with an ample anticanonical divisor.
We work over an algebraically closed field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic zero.

From the point of view of the Minimal Model Program,
the most important subclass of Fano varieties are those having
terminal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial singularities.
In dimension two, these are just the smooth del Pezzo surfaces.
In dimension three, we know the list of smooth Fano threefolds
due to Iskovskikh
[
13
,
14
]
and Mori/Mukai
[
19
]
but
the singular case is still wide open.
Retricting to toric geometry, Kasprzyk
[
16
]
classified
all terminal toric Fano threefolds by means of lattice polytopes,
following an idea of Borisov and Borisov
[
6
]
.
Their combinatorial approach can be outlined in geometrical terms as follows:

(i)

find all terminal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric Fano threefolds with Picard number one;

(ii)

determine the maximal possible rank for the divisor class group of terminal toric Fano threefolds
that do not admit any contraction of a prime divisor;

(iii)

find all such varieties;

(iv)

“grow” the varieties found in (i) and (iii) to obtain all terminal toric Fano threefolds.

This paper contributes to the generalization of this approach
to varieties
X
of complexity one, i.e.
coming with an effective action of a torus
T
such that
\dim(T)=\dim(X)-1
holds.
The foundation was laid in a joint work by the author
together with Bechtold, Hausen and Huggenberger: in
[
5
]
,
a polyhedral complex called the
anticanonical complex

is associated to any rational Fano variety
X
of complexity one.
Properties of the singularities (such as terminality) are characterized by
the lattice points inside of the anticanonical complex.
In the same article, item (i) of the approach was generalized:
the anticanonical complex was used to obtain
the full list of terminal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial Fano threefolds
X

of complexity one with Picard number
\rho(X)=1
.

The first main result of this paper is a generalization of
item (ii) for varieties of complexity one.
We denote with
\delta(X)
the rank of the divisor class group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
of a variety
X
.
One always has
\rho(X)\leq\delta(X)
,
since the Picard group is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
.
We call a variety
combinatorially minimal

if it does not admit any contraction of a prime divisor.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a non-toric rational combinatorially minimal terminal Fano
threefold with an effective two-torus action.
Then
\rho(X)\leq\delta(X)\leq 3
holds.

This Theorem allows a methodical approach to the classification of
combinatorially minimal terminal Fano threefolds of complexity one,
i.e. the analog of item (iii).
Our second result is the classification of these varieties in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial case
by means of their Cox rings;
see
[
2
, Sec. 1.4]
for a precise formulation of Cox rings.

2.
T
-varieties with a torus action of complexity one

For convenience, we gather here notation and basic facts
on rational projective varieties
X

that come with an effective torus action of complexity one;
the original references are
[
10
,
8
,
5
]
.
The Cox rings
\mathcal{R}(X)
of these varieties are
obtained in the following way.

Construction 2.1
.

Fix
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and a sequence

\overline{n}=(n_{0},\ldots,n_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}^{r+1}
, set

n:=n_{0}+\ldots+n_{r}
, and fix
integers
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
0<s<n+m-r
.
A pair
(A,P)
of
defining matrices

consists of

•

a matrix
A:=[a_{0},\ldots,a_{r}]
of size
2\times(r+1)

with pairwise linearly independent column vectors from
{\mathbb{K}}^{2}
,

•

an integral block matrix
P
of size
(r+s)\times(n+m)
,
whose columns are pairwise different primitive
vectors generating
{\mathbb{Q}}^{r+s}
as a cone.

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}L&0\\
d&d^{\prime}\end{array}\right],

where
d
is an
(s\times n)
-matrix,
d^{\prime}
an
(s\times m)
-matrix
and
L
is an
(r\times n)
-matrix built from tuples

l_{i}:=(l_{i1},\ldots,l_{in_{i}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}^{n_{i}}

as follows

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}-l_{0}&l_{1}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-l_{0}&0&\ldots&l_{r}\end{array}\right].

We denote with
v_{ij}
and
v_{k}
the columns of the matrix
P
,
where
0\leq i\leq r
,
1\leq j\leq n_{i}
and
1\leq k\leq m
.
Over the same indices define the variables
T_{ij}
and
S_{k}
and
consider the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{ij},S_{k}]
.
For every
0\leq i\leq r
, define a monomial

\displaystyle T_{i}^{l_{i}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle T_{i1}^{l_{i1}}\cdots T_{in_{i}}^{l_{in_{i}}}.

Denote by
\mathfrak{I}
the set of
all triples
I=(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})
with

0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}\leq r

and define for any
I\in\mathfrak{I}

a trinomial

g_{I}\ :=\ g_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\ :=\ \det\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}T_{i_{1}}^{l_{i_{1}}}&T_{i_{2}}^{l_{i_{2}}}&T_{i_{3}}^{l_{i_{3}}}\\
a_{i_{1}}&a_{i_{2}}&a_{i_{3}}\end{array}\right].

Let
P^{*}
denote the transpose of
P
,
consider the factor group

K:={\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}/\rm{im}(P^{*})

and the projection
Q\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+m}\to K
.
We define a
K
-grading on
{\mathbb{K}}[T_{ij},S_{k}]
via

\deg(T_{ij})\ :=\ Q(e_{ij}),\qquad\deg(S_{k})\ :=\ Q(e_{k}).

Then the trinomials
g_{I}
are
K
-homogeneous,
all of the same degree
\mu\in K
.
In particular, we obtain a
K
-graded factor ring

\displaystyle R(A,P)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{K}}[T_{ij},S_{k};\;0\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq j\leq n_{i},1\leq k\leq m]/\ \langle g_{I};\;I\in\mathfrak{I}\rangle.

Remark 2.2
.

The ring
R(A,P)
of Construction
2.1

is a complete intersection: with
g_{i}:=g_{i,i+1,i+2}

we have

\langle g_{I};\;I\in\mathfrak{I}\rangle\ =\ \langle g_{0},\ldots,g_{r-2}\rangle,\qquad\quad\dim(R(A,P))\ =\ n+m-(r-1).

We can always assume that
P
is
irredundant

in the sense that
l_{i1}+\ldots+l_{in_{i}}\geq 2

holds for all
i=0,\ldots,r
.
A redundant matrix
P
allows the elimination of variables in
R(A,P)
.

Remark 2.3
.

The following elementary column and row operations
on the defining matrix
P
are called
admissible operations
.
They do not change the isomorphy type
of the
K
-graded ring
R(A,P)
;

(i)

swap two columns inside a block

v_{ij_{1}},\ldots,v_{ij_{n_{i}}}
,

(ii)

swap two whole column blocks

v_{ij_{1}},\ldots,v_{ij_{n_{i}}}

and
v_{i^{\prime}j_{1}},\ldots,v_{i^{\prime}j_{n_{i^{\prime}}}}
,

(iii)

add multiples of the upper
r
rows
to one of the last
s
rows,

(iv)

any elementary row operation among the last
s

rows,

(v)

swap two columns inside the
d^{\prime}
block.

Remark 2.4
.

The
anticanonical class
of the
K
-graded
ring
R(A,P)
is

\kappa(A,P)\ :=\ \sum_{i,j}Q(e_{ij})\ +\ \sum_{k}Q(e_{k})\ -\ (r-1)\mu\ \in\ K

and the
moving cone
of
R(A,P)
is

{\rm Mov}(A,P)\ :=\ \bigcap_{i,j}{\rm cone}(Q(e_{uv},e_{t};\;(u,v)\neq(i,j))\ \cap\ \bigcap_{k}{\rm cone}(Q(e_{uv},e_{t};\;t\neq k)\ \subseteq\ K_{\mathbb{Q}}.

The
K
-graded ring
R(A,P)
is the Cox ring of
a Fano variety if and only if
\kappa(A,P)
lies in
the relative interior of
{\rm Mov}(A,P)
.
This Fano variety can be constructed from
R(A,P)
as follows.

.

Consider the

Proposition 2.15

Let

3.
Combinatorial minimality

In this Section, we derive bounds on the Picard
number of combinatorially minimal,
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial,
log terminal Fano varieties with torus action
of complexity one.
We begin by recalling the necessary notions
and facts from
[
11
,
7
]
.

A
small quasimodification

is a birational map
X\dasharrow X^{\prime}

of complete varieties that restricts to
a regular isomorphism
U\to U^{\prime}
between
open subsets
U\subseteq X
and
U^{\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}

having complements of codimension at least two
in
X
and
X^{\prime}
respectively.
We say that
X
is
combinatorially minimal
if it
has no contractible prime divisors in the sense that
any birational map
X\dasharrow X^{\prime}
which is defined in
codimension two is a small quasimodification.

Remark 3.1
.

A projective variety

with finitely generated Cox ring
is combinatorially minimal if and only if
its cone of movable divisor classes
coincides with its cone of effective divisor classes.
In particular for a variety
X=X(A,P)
of complexity one,
this precisely means that every extremal ray of the effective cone

{\rm Eff}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Cl}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)
hosts the degrees of
at least two of the generators
T_{ij},S_{k}
of the
Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(X)=R(A,P)
, see
[
7
]
.

First we give a characterization for the defining matrix
P

of a combinatorially minimal variety
X=X(A,P)
.
Consider two mutually dual exact sequences of
finite dimensional rational vector spaces

0
0
L_{\mathbb{Q}}
K_{\mathbb{Q}}
F_{\mathbb{Q}}
E_{\mathbb{Q}}
N_{\mathbb{Q}}
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
0
0
Q

Denote by
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
a basis for

and by
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
its dual basis for
E_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Moreover define
\delta:={\rm cone}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})

and
\gamma:={\rm cone}(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
.
We call an element

an

-invariant separating linear form

for

if

e|_{L_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0,\quad e|_{\delta_{1}}=0,\quad e|_{\delta_{2}}=0,\quad\delta_{1}\cap e^{\perp}=\delta_{2}\cap e^{\perp}=\delta_{1}\cap\delta_{2}.

Lemma 3.2

(Invariant Separation Lemma
[
2
, 2.2.3.2]
)
.

Consider
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\preceq\delta
and
their corresponding faces
\gamma_{i}:=\delta_{i}^{\perp}\cap\gamma\preceq\gamma
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

•

there exists an

-invariant separating linear form for
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}

•

Q(\gamma_{1})^{\circ}\cap Q(\gamma_{2})^{\circ}\neq\emptyset

Define the weights
w_{ij}:=\deg(T_{ij})
and
w_{k}:=\deg(S_{k})
.
We call a variable

extremal

if its weight
w_{ij},w_{k}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)

sits on an extremal ray of the effective cone

{\rm Eff}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Cl}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)
.
Moreover, we call a weight
w\in\{w_{ij},w_{k}\}

exceptional
,
if
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}w
is an extremal ray of
{\rm Eff}(X)
and
no other weight lies on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}w

All variables with exceptional weights
are extremal, but the converse does not hold.
As a matter of fact, by Remark
3.1
,

is combinatorially minimal if and only if no weight is exceptional.

Only in this Section, we rename the weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{n+m}

and let

be the corresponding columns of

.

Lemma 3.3
.

The weight
w\in\{w_{ij},w_{k}\}
is exceptional if and only if

{\mathbb{Q}}^{r+s}
is already generated as a cone by all columns of P
except the one that corresponds to
w
.

Proof.

An exceptional weight
w_{i}
is characterized by the fact that

{\rm cone}(w_{j};j\neq i)
and
{\rm cone}(w_{i})
allow a separating linear form.
By the Invariant Separation Lemma
3.2
, this is equivalent to the fact
that the corresponding Gale dual cones intersect in their relative interiors, i.e.

{\rm cone}(v_{j};j\neq i)^{\circ}\cap{\rm cone}(v_{i})^{\circ}\neq\emptyset.

This is the case if and only if
v_{i}\in{\rm cone}(v_{j};j\neq i)^{\circ}
holds.
Since the cone over all columns is
{\mathbb{Q}}^{r+s}
,
the last condition is equivalent to
{\rm cone}(v_{j};j\neq i)={\mathbb{Q}}^{r+s}
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

The variety
X=X(A,P)
is combinatorially minimal if and only if
for every column

of

the following holds:

{\rm cone}(v_{\varrho^{\prime}};\ \varrho^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{(1)}\setminus\{\varrho\})\ \neq\ {\mathbb{Q}}^{r+s}.

The assertion follows directly from Lemma

.
∎

Given a matrix

and an index-set

,

.

Corollary 3.5
.

Consider the variety

{\rm cone}(w_{i})={\rm cone}(w_{j})

Proof.

By construction we have

\displaystyle\tau_{i}:=

\displaystyle{\rm cone}(v_{1},\ldots,\widehat{v_{i}},\ldots,v_{n+m}),

\displaystyle{\rm cone}(v_{1},\ldots,\widehat{v_{j}},\ldots,v_{n+m}).

Lemma 3.6

Let

Then one of the following holds:

and

•

Proof.

The effective cone

is

Since

is combinatorially minimal,
the number

is

If the number

\dim(X)\ =\ n+m+2-(r+1)-\delta(X)\ \geq\ m+2-\delta(X)\ \geq\ \delta(X).

So, consider the case

There are at least two extremal variables of type

having their weights on different rays

.
For each of these

The assertion then follows from

\displaystyle=\ n+m+2-(r+1)-\delta(X)

\displaystyle\geq\ \frac{2\delta(X)+2\alpha-m}{2}+m+2-\delta(X)

For

Lemma 3.7

Let

Just observe that

Lemma 3.8

Let

The lineality part

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
X=X(A,P)
be a log terminal
Fano variety, such that
\mu\in{\rm Eff}(X)^{\circ}
holds.
Assume
\alpha=1
and
\zeta\leq r-2
.
Then we have

\delta(X)\ \leq\ \dim(X)+3+\zeta-r-m\ \leq\ \dim(X)+1-m.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{-},\omega_{+}\subseteq{\rm Eff}(X)
be the
minimal pair of subcones as in Lemma
3.12

and denote by
a_{-},a_{+}
their respective numbers
of extremal rays. Then we have

a_{-}\ \geq\ 2,\qquad a_{+}\ \geq\ 2,\qquad a_{-}+a_{+}\ \geq\ \delta(X)+1.

Consider a monomial
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
with only extremal
variables
T_{ij}
and let
\omega_{i}
be the cone
generated by the
\deg(T_{ij})
.
We say that
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
is of type
(-)
if

\omega_{-}\subseteq\omega_{i}
holds and
of type
(+)
otherwise.
Lemma
3.12
, with
\mu\in{\rm Eff}(X)^{\circ}
,
shows that any
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
of type
(+)

satisfies
\omega_{+}\subseteq\omega_{i}
.
Let
b_{-}
and
b_{+}
denote the respective numbers of
monomials of these types that occur in the
(r-1)
relations.
Then we have

b_{-}+b_{+}\ \geq\ r+1-\zeta,\qquad\eta\ \geq\ b_{-}a_{-}+b_{+}a_{+}.

For the first estimate, we use that there are at
least
r+1-\zeta
monomials
involving only extremal variables.
For the second one, note that every monomial of type

(\pm)
has at least
a_{\pm}
distinct variables.
We conclude

\displaystyle\dim(X)+\delta(X)+r-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta+\zeta+m

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle b_{-}a_{-}+b_{+}a_{+}+\zeta+m

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(\delta(X)+1)+(b_{-}-2)a_{-}+(b_{+}-2)a_{+}+\zeta+m

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(\delta(X)+1)+2(r+1-\zeta-4)+\zeta+m.

∎

Proposition 4.1

Let

Proof.

Firstly assume that
\mu\in{\rm Eff}(X)^{\circ}
holds.
If
\alpha=0
holds, then Lemma
3.7

with
\zeta\geq r-2

\delta(X)\leq 4

\delta(X)\leq 3

If
\alpha=1
holds, Lemma
3.7

with
\zeta\geq r-2
gives
\delta(X)\leq 2

\zeta\leq r-3

\delta(X)\leq 3

So we have to exclude

which only appears in the case
\alpha=0

\zeta\geq r-2

Lemma
3.7
yields in this case

\zeta\leq r-2
, thus we have
\zeta=r-2
.
In particular we have a relation involving
only extremal variables, say the one
with the monomials
T_{0}^{l_{0}},T_{1}^{l_{1}},T_{2}^{l_{2}}
.
Together with
\mu\in{\rm Eff}(X)^{\circ}
and
\alpha=0

On the other hand, Lemma
3.11

Now assume that the degree

According to Lemma

In particular the weights

6.
Growing the anticanonical complex

This last Section outlines the future road towards a complete classification
of terminal Fano threefolds of complexity one.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety of complexity one.
The Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(X)
is

R\ :=\ \mathcal{R}(X)\ =\ {\mathbb{K}}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{r},T_{r+1}]/\!\braket{g_{1},\ldots,g_{s}}

with grading given by
K:=\operatorname{Cl}(X)
and degrees
w_{i}:=\deg(T_{i})\in K
.
Recall that the weight
w_{i}
is
exceptional
,
if
w_{i}\notin{\rm cone}(w_{j};j\neq i)
holds.

Remark 6.1
.

Paraphrasing Remarks
2.4
and
2.10
we say that
the
moving cone
and the
anticanonical class
of
R
are respectively

\displaystyle{\rm Mov}(R)\

\displaystyle:=\ \bigcap_{i=0}^{r+1}{\rm cone}(w_{j};j\neq i)\ \subset\ K_{\mathbb{Q}},

\displaystyle\kappa(R)\

\displaystyle:=\ \sum_{i=1}^{r+1}w_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\deg(g_{j})\ \in\ K.

We say that
R
is
Fano
if
\kappa(R)\in{\rm Mov}(R)^{\circ}
holds.
The last definition is justified by the fact that
\kappa(R)\in{\rm Mov}(R)^{\circ}
characterizes a ring
R

that can be realized as Cox ring of a normal Fano variety,
which we then call
X_{F}(R)
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
R
be Fano as in Remark
6.1
.
We say that
R
is
terminal

(resp. canonical, log-terminal,
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, etc…)

if the Fano variety
X_{F}(R)
has that property.

Construction 6.3
.

Let
R
and
K
be as above. Assume that
w_{r+1}
is exceptional.
Define polynomials
g_{j}^{\prime}:=g_{j}|_{T_{r+1}=1}

and the ring
R^{\prime}:={\mathbb{K}}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}]/\!\braket{g_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g_{s}^{\prime}}
.
Its grading is given by
K^{\prime}:=K/\!\braket{w_{r+1}}

via the canonical projection map
\pi:=K\to K^{\prime}

and the degrees
\deg(T_{i}):=w_{i}^{\prime}:=\pi(w_{i})
.
Then
R^{\prime}
is again the Cox ring of a normal projective variety of complexity one.

Remark 6.4
.

We have the commutative diagram with exact rows

0
K_{\mathbb{Q}}
K^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}}
{\mathbb{Q}}^{r+1}
{\mathbb{Q}}^{r}
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
0
\pi

\pi_{r}

hence in particular
\pi\circ Q=Q^{\prime}\circ\pi_{r}
and
(P^{\prime})^{*}=\pi_{r}\circ P^{*}
.
Therefore considering the presentation
R=R(A,P)
,
the ring
R^{\prime}
is given as
R^{\prime}=R(A,P^{\prime})
, where
P^{\prime}
is obtained from

by deleting the column corresponding to the exceptional weight.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
R
and
R^{\prime}
be as in Construction
6.3
.
If
R
is (terminal) Fano, then so is
R^{\prime}
.

Proof.

To prove that the Fano property is preserved by Construction
6.3
,
one only need to see that

\kappa(R^{\prime})\ =\ \pi(\kappa(R))\ \in\ \pi({\rm Mov}(R))^{\circ}\ \subseteq\ {\rm Mov}(R^{\prime})^{\circ}.

In order to study terminality, define
X
and
X^{\prime}
as
the Fano varieties corresponding to
R
and
R^{\prime}
respectively.
The goal is to show that
A_{X^{\prime}}^{c}\subseteq A_{X}^{c}
holds,
because then the assertion holds by Theorem
2.7
.
This can be done by considering the commutative diagram of Remark
6.4

and proving that
B_{X^{\prime}}\supseteq B_{X}
holds.
For this one uses
\kappa(R^{\prime})=\pi(\kappa(R))
and
\pi_{r}(B(g_{j}))=B(g_{j}^{\prime})
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

Note that Proposition
6.5

does not hold for
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factoriality.
There are examples where
X(A,P)
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial
but
X(A,P^{\prime})
is not, and vice versa.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
X=X(A,P)
be a terminal Fano threefold
of complexity one.
If
X
is not combinatorially minimal,
then there is an equivariant small quasimodification
X\dasharrow X^{\prime}

onto a combinatorially terminal Fano threefold
X^{\prime}
of complexity one (which could be toric).

Remark 6.8
.

Much like in the case of toric Fano varieties with the growing Fano polytopes (see
[
16
]
),
Proposition
6.5
ensures that
we can approach the classification of terminal Fano varieties of complexity one
with purely combinatorial methods,
by taking the anticanonical complex of a combinatorially minimal variety and
successively adding new integral vertices, each time checking if all required properties still hold.

Introduction

Alan Turing (1912 –1954) was a British scientist credited for developing the first modern computer, artificial intelligence and for breaking the Enigma code during World War II. One of his scientific legacies pertains to the chemical basis of morphogenesis, that is, a biological process that causes a tissue or organ to develop its shape through the spatial distribution of its cells.
Turing’s research
[
29
]
was related to pattern formation due to the breakdown of symmetry and homogeneity in
initially homogeneous continuous media. He concluded that small variances in chemical concentrations
can amplify and act as symmetry-breaking in the biological development process and thus, they may give rise to patterns. Mathematically, this process is described by two reaction-diffusion equations where the unknowns quantify the concentration of two chemicals. Let us point out that patterns may result from different processes such as cell polarity (Lawrence
[
26
]
) or the presence of two periodic
events featuring different wavelengths (Goodwin and Cohen
[
16
]
).

Based on Turing’s idea, Gierer and Meinhardt
[
14
]
proposed in 1972 a mathematical model for pattern formation that emphasizes the existence of two antagonist chemicals: there is a short-range autocatalytic activator on one hand, and a long-range, cross-catalytic inhibitor on the other hand, see Figure 1.

Figure 1:
The activator-inhibitor interdependence in the Gierer-Meinhardt model.

Their mathematical model, also expressed as a reaction-diffusion system, reads as follows:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle a_{t}-d_{1}\Delta a=-\mu_{a}a+\rho\frac{a^{p}}{h^{q}}+\rho_{a},\\[11.38092pt]
\displaystyle h_{t}-d_{2}\Delta h=-\mu_{h}h+\rho\frac{a^{m}}{h^{s}},\\[5.69046pt]
\end{cases}

where:

•

a
and
h
represent the concentration of the activator and inhibitor respectively.

•

a_{t}
and
b_{t}
account for the change of activator and inhibitor in time unit.

•

\rho
is the self-production rate of the activator.

•

the nonlinear terms
\displaystyle\frac{a^{p}}{h^{q}}
and
\displaystyle\frac{a^{m}}{h^{s}}
describe the production rates of the activator and inhibitor in the process of tissue formation. They depend on the nonlinear terms
a^{p}
and
a^{m}
, being slowed down by the inhibitor input
\displaystyle 1/h^{q}
and
\displaystyle 1/h^{s}
respectively. It is assumed that

p>1
and
q,m,s>0
.

The above system is said to have a common source for activator and inhibitor if
(p,q)=(m,s)
.

•

\mu_{a}
,
\mu_{h}
represent the decay rates of the two chemicals.

•

\rho_{a}
is a small activator-independent production rate of the activator and is required to initiate the activator autocatalysis at very low activator concentration.

•

d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are the diffusion rates of the activator and inhibitor respectively. The activator diffuses slowly while the inhibitor has a fast diffusion, so
d_{1}<<d_{2}
.

. For instance,

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta u+u=\frac{u^{2}}{v}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v+v=u^{2}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[7.22743pt]

One of the main results of

The following system related to (

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=\frac{u^{p}}{v^{q}}+\rho(x)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},N\geq 3,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v+\mu v=\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[7.22743pt]

where

,

In the present work we use a different approach to investigate qualitative properties of solutions to the steady-state Gierer-Meinhardt system in exterior domains. Precisely, we are concerned with the existence and nonexistence of positive solutions to the system

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{u^{p}}{v^{q}}+\lambda\rho(x)\,,u>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\,(N\geq 2),\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v=\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}\,,v>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;u(x),v(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

where
\rho\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
is a nonnegative function,
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
p,q,m,s,\lambda>0
. We are looking for positive classical solutions of (
1.2
), that is, functions
u,v\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
such that
u,v>0
and satisfy (
1.2
) at every point of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K}
.

In the above problem,
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 2)
is a compact set that satisfies the following two conditions:

(K1)

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
is connected and there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
B_{r_{0}}\subset K
.

(K2)

We have

(1.3)

\nu(x)\cdot x\leq 0\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\partial K,

where
\nu(x)
denotes the inner normal vector to
\partial K
at the point
x\in\partial K
.

As it can be easily seen, closed balls or ellipsoids whose interior contains the origin satisfy the above condition (
1.3
). More generally, if
K
is described geometrically by
K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x)\leq 0\}
with a continuously differentiable function

, then (
1.3
) holds provided that
\nabla f(x)\cdot x\leq 0
for all
x\in\partial K
.

From the biological point of view, the homogeneous Neumann conditions on
\partial K
in (
1.2
) indicate that there is no exchange with the exterior of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. This means that the concentrations
u
and
v
have no effect on
K
which is a region with a different structure, such as a hard tissue, e.g., a bone.

Main results

Our first main result provides sufficient conditions for which (
1.2

Theorem 2.1
.

(Nonexistence)

The system (
1.2
) has no positive solutions in any of the following situations:

(i)

N=2
;

(ii)

N\geq 3
and
\displaystyle m\leq\frac{2}{N-2}

(iii)

N\geq 3
and
\displaystyle 0<p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
.

We next discuss the existence of a positive solution
(u,v)
to (
1.2
) if
N\geq 3
. We have seen in Theorem
2.1
(iii) that (
1.2
) has no solutions if
N\geq 3
and
\displaystyle 0<p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
. Thus, we shall assume
p>\frac{N}{N-2}

and let us introduce the quantity

\sigma:=\frac{mq}{(p-1)(1+s)}>0

To be more precise in our approach, we shall assume that

has the following behaviour at infinity:

(2.1)

C_{1}|x|^{-k}\leq\rho(x)\leq C_{2}|x|^{-k}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\quad\mbox{ for some }k>0,

where

Given two positive functions

If

and

and

are positive and superharmonic in

(see Lemma

to (

has a minimal growth at infinity, that is,

Our result reads as follows:

Theorem 2.2
.

(Solutions with minimal growth in the activator concentration)

Assume

,
\sigma<1
and
\rho(x)
satisfies (

). If the system (

) we have

(3.5)

-\Delta w=\Delta w_{1}-\Delta w_{2}\leq\Psi(x)\Big(g(w_{2})-g(w_{1})\Big)<0\quad\mbox{ in }\Sigma.

Since
\Omega
(and thus
\Sigma
) is bounded, there exists
x_{0}\in\overline{\Sigma}
such that
w(x_{0})=\max_{\overline{\Omega}}w>0
.

Case 1.

x_{0}\in\Omega
. Then,
x_{0}
is an interior point of
\Omega
and thus
-\Delta w(x_{0})\geq 0
. This contradicts (
3.5
).

Case 2.

x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Let us note that
w=w_{2}-w_{1}
is subharmonic in
\Sigma
which achieves its maximum at the boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Sigma\cap\partial\Omega
. Thus, by the Hopf boundary point lemma (see
[
9
, Section 6.4.2]
) we have

\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}(x_{0})>0\Longrightarrow\frac{\partial w_{2}}{\partial\nu}(x_{0})>\frac{\partial w_{1}}{\partial\nu}(x_{0}).

Now, the above inequality together with
w_{2}(x_{0})>w_{1}(x_{0})
contradict (
3.4
) at
x=x_{0}
.
Hence, in both the above cases we raised a contradiction, which proves that
\Sigma
is empty and thus
w_{1}\geq w_{2}
in
\Omega
.
∎

The following result is a counterpart of Lemma
3.3
for the case of exterior domains.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a compact set and let
\Psi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K)
,
g\in C(0,\infty)
be positive functions.
Let
a,b\in C(\partial K)
be such that

a,b\geq 0,\quad(a(x),b(x))\neq(0,0)\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\partial K.

Assume
w_{1},w_{2}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
satisfy:

(3.6)

\Delta w_{1}+\Psi(x)g(w_{1})\leq\Delta w_{2}+\Psi(x)g(w_{2})\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,

(3.7)

a(x)w_{1}+b(x)\frac{\partial w_{1}}{\partial\nu}\geq a(x)w_{2}+b(x)\frac{\partial w_{2}}{\partial\nu}\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,

where
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal vector at
\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K)
and

(3.8)

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}w_{1}(x)\geq\lim_{|x|\to\infty}w_{2}(x).

Then,
w_{1}\geq w_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. By (
3.8
) we can find
R>0
large enough such that
K\subset B_{R}
and

(3.9)

w_{1}+\varepsilon\geq w_{2}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}.

Let
\Omega=B_{R}\setminus K
. From (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) we deduce that
\widetilde{w}_{1}=w_{1}+\varepsilon
and
\widetilde{w}_{2}=w_{2}
satisfy

\Delta\widetilde{w}_{1}+\Psi(x)g(\widetilde{w}_{1})\leq\Delta\widetilde{w}_{2}+\Psi(x)g(\widetilde{w}_{2})\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

and

\widetilde{a}(x)\widetilde{w}_{1}(x)+\widetilde{b}(x)\frac{\partial\widetilde{w}_{1}}{\partial\nu}(x)\geq\widetilde{a}(x)\widetilde{w}_{2}(x)+\widetilde{b}(x)\frac{\partial\widetilde{w}_{2}}{\partial\nu}(x)\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b}\in C(\partial\Omega)
are given by

\widetilde{a}(x)=\begin{cases}a(x)&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial K\\[2.84544pt]
1&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial B_{R}\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widetilde{b}(x)=\begin{cases}b(x)&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial K\\[2.84544pt]
0&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial B_{R}\end{cases}.

By Lemma
3.3
we deduce
\widetilde{w}_{1}\geq\widetilde{w}_{2}
in
\Omega
and thus, by the choice of
R>0
in (
3.9
), we have
w_{1}+\varepsilon\geq w_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. Passing to the limit with
\varepsilon\to 0
, we derive
w_{1}\geq w_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 3)
be a compact set which satisfies conditions
(K1)
and
(K2)
. Let also
\Psi\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and
g\in C(0,\infty)
be positive functions such that:

(i)

g\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
is nonincreasing;

(ii)

A(t):=\displaystyle\sup_{|x|=t}\Psi(x)
satisfies
\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}tA(t)dt<\infty
.

Then, there exists a unique
w\in C^{2,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
such that

(3.10)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\Psi(x)g(w)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;w>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K},\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;w(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

Proof.

Our approach relies on a version of the sub- and supersolution method as described in Theorem 2.1 in
[
27
, Section 3.2]
. We divide the proof into four steps as follows:

•

Step 1 discusses the existence of a supersolution.

•

Step 2 and Step 3 detail the construction of a subsolution to (
3.10
). This is a more delicate issue due to the fact that
g
is nonincreasing, and thus may be singular around the origin. Precisely, in Step 2 we construct a subsolution which satisfies a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial K
. Then, in Step 3 below we construct a proper subsolution to (
3.10
) which is a
C^{2}
function up to (and including) the boundary of
K
.

•

In Step 4 we deduce the existence of a
C^{2,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
solution to (
3.10
).

Step 1:
There exists
W\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
a positive supersolution of (
3.10
), that is,
W
satisfies

(3.11)

\begin{cases}-\Delta W\geq\Psi(x)g(W)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;W>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K},\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial W}{\partial\nu}\geq 0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;W(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

By
(K1)
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
B_{r_{0}}\subset K
. Using the integral condition on
A
and a direct integration by parts, for
R>r_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{R}t^{1-N}\int_{r_{0}}^{t}\tau^{N-1}A(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{t^{2-N}}{N-2}\int_{r_{0}}^{t}\tau^{N-1}A(\tau)d\tau\Bigg|_{t=r_{0}}^{t=R}+\frac{1}{N-2}\int_{r_{0}}^{R}tA(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N-2}\int_{r_{0}}^{R}tA(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N-2}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}tA(t)dt<\infty.

Thus, we may define

(3.12)

Z(x)=\int_{|x|}^{\infty}t^{1-N}\int_{r_{0}}^{t}\tau^{N-1}A(\tau)d\tau\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{0}}.

Also,
Z
satisfies

(3.13)

\begin{cases}-\Delta Z(x)=A(|x|)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r_{0}},\\[2.84544pt]
\;\;\;\;\;Z(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

We further define

W:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\to\mathbb{R}
by

(3.14)

\int_{0}^{W(x)}\frac{1}{g(t)}dt=Z(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

Then
W\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
,
W(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
, and

(3.15)

\nabla W(x)=g(W(x))\nabla Z(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K}.

Using (
3.13
), (
3.15
) and
g^{\prime}\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle-\Delta W(x)

\displaystyle=-{\rm div}(\nabla W(x))

\displaystyle=-g(W(x))\Delta Z(x)-g^{\prime}(W(x))\nabla W(x)\cdot\nabla Z(x)

\displaystyle=A(|x|)g(W(x))-g^{\prime}(W(x))g(W(x))|\nabla Z(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq A(|x|)g(W(x))\quad\mbox{(since $g^{\prime}\leq 0$)}

\displaystyle\geq\Psi(x)g(W(x))\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

Finally, using (
1.3
), (
3.12
) and (
3.15
), for all
x\in\partial K
we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial W}{\partial\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla W(x)\cdot\nu(x)=g(W(x))\nabla Z(x)\cdot\nu(x)

\displaystyle=-|x|^{-N}\Big(\int^{|x|}_{r_{0}}t^{N-1}A(t)dt\Big)\nu(x)\cdot x\geq 0.

Thus,
W
defined by (
3.14
) is a supersolution of (
3.10
).

Step 2:
Fix
\delta>0
. There exists
U\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
such that

(3.16)

\begin{cases}-\Delta U=\Psi(x)g(U+\delta)\;,\;U>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;U=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial U}{\partial\nu}<0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;U(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

Let
n_{0}\geq 1
be a positive integer such that
K\subset B_{n_{0}}
. For all
n\geq n_{0}
consider the problem

(3.17)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n}=\Psi(x)g(u_{n}+\delta)\;,\;u_{n}>0&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;u_{n}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;u_{n}=W&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial B_{n}.\end{cases}

The existence of
u_{n}\in C^{2}(\overline{B_{n}\setminus K})
follows from the sub and supersolution method once we observe that
\underline{u}_{n}\equiv 0
and
\overline{u}_{n}=W
are respectively sub and supersolutions to (
3.17
). We should point out that
g
may be singular around zero but the function
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto g(t+\delta)
is of class
C^{1}
and this yields the existence of a
C^{2}
-solution in the sub and supersolution process. Next, we apply Lemma
3.3
for

\Omega=B_{n}\setminus K,\quad a\equiv 1,\quad b\equiv 0,\quad w_{1}=u_{n+1},\quad w_{2}=u_{n},

to deduce
u_{n+1}\leq u_{n}
in
B_{n}\setminus K
.
We extend
u_{n}=W
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{n}
and thus we have

(3.18)

0\leq u_{n+1}\leq u_{n}\leq W\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\,,\mbox{ for all }n\geq n_{0}.

By the standard elliptic arguments (see
[
1
,
2
]
) we deduce that
U(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(x)
satisfies
U\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
and
-\Delta U=\Psi(x)g(U+\delta)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. Clearly
U
is not constant and thus the maximum principle yields
U>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. Also, for all
x\in\partial K
one has
U(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(x)=0
. Thus, any
x\in\partial K
is a minimum point of
U
and thus, by the Hopf boundary point lemma (see
[
9
, Section 6.4.2]
) one has
\frac{\partial U}{\partial\nu}<0
on
K
. Finally, from (
3.18
) we deduce
U\leq W
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
, so
U(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Hence,
U
satisfies (
3.16
).

Step 3:
There exists
V\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
such that

(3.19)

\begin{cases}-\Delta V=\Psi(x)g(V+\delta)&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;V>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K},\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;V(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

As before, let
n_{0}\geq 1
be a positive integer such that
K\subset B_{n_{0}}
. For all
n\geq n_{0}
consider the problem

(3.20)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{n}=\Psi(x)g(v_{n}+\delta)\;,\;v_{n}>0&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;v_{n}=W&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial B_{n}.\end{cases}

Notice that (
3.20
) is the counterpart of (
3.17
) in which the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial K
is replaced by a Neumann one.
To check the existence of a solution to (
3.20
) we first observe that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
\underline{v}_{n}=U
(where
U
satisfies (
3.16
)) is a subsolution to (
3.20
) while
\overline{v}_{n}=W
is a supersolution, where
W
satisfies (
3.11
). As in Step 2 above, we now derive the existence of a solution
v_{n}\in C^{2}(\overline{B_{n}\setminus K})
to (
3.20
) such that
U\leq v_{n}\leq W
in
B_{n}\setminus K
. By the maximum principle we have
v_{n}>0
in
B_{n}\setminus K
. Apply now Lemma
3.3
with
\Omega=B_{n}\setminus K
and
a(x)
,
b(x)
given by

(3.21)

a(x)=\begin{cases}0&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial K\\[2.84544pt]
1&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial B_{n}\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad b(x)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial K\\[2.84544pt]
0&\quad\mbox{ if }x\in\partial B_{n}\end{cases},

we find
v_{n+1}\leq v_{n}
in
B_{n}\setminus K
. Extending
v_{n}=W
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{n}
, we have that

(3.22)

U\leq v_{n+1}\leq u_{n}\leq W\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\,,\mbox{ for all }n\geq n_{0}.

Set
V(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}(x)
.
By standard elliptic arguments (see
[
1
,
2
]
) we deduce that
\{v_{n}\}_{n\geq n_{0}}
is bounded in
C^{2,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
and thus
V
satisfies

(3.23)

-\Delta V=\Psi(x)g(V+\delta)\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}=0\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K.

Passing to the limit in (
3.22
) we find
U\leq V\leq W
, so
V>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
and
V(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. It remains to check that
V>0
on
\partial K
which can be done as in Step 2 above: if
x_{0}\in\partial K
satisfies
V(x_{0})=0
, then
x_{0}
is a minimum point of
V
at which, by Hopf boundary point lemma (see
[
9
, Section 6.4.2]
), one has
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}(x_{0})<0
. This clearly contradicts the boundary condition in (
3.23
) and proves
V>0
on
\partial K
. Thus,
V
satisfies (
3.19
).

Step 4:
Construction of a solution to (
3.10
).

For any
n\geq n_{0}
consider the problem

(3.24)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{n}=\Psi(x)g(w_{n})\;,\;w_{n}>0&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[2.84544pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;w_{n}=W&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial B_{n}.\end{cases}

Then,
\underline{w}_{n}=V
(where
V
satisfies (
3.19
)) is a subsolution to (
3.24
) while
\overline{w}_{n}=W
is a supersolution, where
W
satisfies (
3.11
). Thus, the sub and supersolution method yields the existence of a solution
w_{n}\in C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{B_{n}\setminus K})
to (
3.24
) such that
V\leq w_{n}\leq W
in
B_{n}\setminus K
. We should note at this stage that
V>0
in
\overline{B_{n}\setminus K}
yields
w_{n}\in C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{B_{n}\setminus K})
. By Lemma
3.3
for
\Omega=B_{n}\setminus K
and
a(x)
,
b(x)
given by (
3.21
), we deduce
w_{n}\leq w_{n+1}
in
B_{n}\setminus K
.
Hence, extending
w_{n}=W
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{n}
we have

(3.25)

V\leq w_{n+1}\leq w_{n}\leq W\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\,,\mbox{ for all }n\geq n_{0}.

Set
w(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}(x)
. From (
3.25
) one has
V\leq w\leq W
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
, so
w>0
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K}
and
w(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Now, standard elliptic arguments allow us to conclude that
w\in C^{2,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
is a solution to (
3.10
). The uniqueness of the solution to (
3.10
) follows from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
s>0
and
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 3)
be a compact set that satisfies
(K1)
and
(K2)
. Assume
\phi\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, is positive and
\phi(x)\simeq|x|^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.
Then, the problem

(3.26)

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\phi(x)w^{-s}&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;w>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K},\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;w(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

has a solution
w\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
if and only if
\alpha>2
. Moreover, we have:

(i)

If
\alpha>2
then (
3.26
) has a unique solution
w\in C^{2,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
.

(ii)

w
satisfies

(3.27)

w(x)\simeq\begin{cases}|x|^{-\frac{\alpha-2}{1+s}}&\quad\mbox{ if }2<\alpha<N+s(N-2),\\[7.22743pt]
|x|^{2-N}\log^{\frac{1}{1+s}}&\quad\mbox{ if }\alpha=N+s(N-2),\\[7.22743pt]
|x|^{2-N}&\quad\mbox{ if }\alpha>N+s(N-2).\end{cases}

If
2<\alpha<N+s(N-2)
, Lemma
3.6
shows that the asymptotic behavior of the unique solution
w
to (
3.26
) depends on
\alpha
. Further, if
\alpha\geq N+s(N-2)
, then, the asymptotic behavior of the unique solution
w
is independent on
\alpha
.

Proof.

(i) Let
A(t)=t^{-\alpha}
and
g(t)=t^{-s}
. By Lemma
3.2
we deduce that (
3.26
) has no positive solutions if
\alpha\leq 2
. On the other hand, if
\alpha>2
, Proposition
3.5
yields the existence of a solution
w\in C^{2,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
to (
3.26
). This completes the proof of part (i).

(ii1) Assume
2<\alpha<N+s(N-2)
. The function
z(x)=|x|^{-\frac{\alpha-2}{1+s}}
satisfies

-\Delta z(x)=m|x|^{-\alpha}z^{-s}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,

where

m=\frac{\alpha-2}{1+s}\Big(N-2-\frac{\alpha-2}{1+s}\Big)>0.

We now choose a small constant
0<c<m^{-\frac{1}{1+s}}
so that
cz\leq w
on
\partial K
. Thus,
w
and
cz
satisfy:

(3.28)

\displaystyle\Delta w+|x|^{-\alpha}w^{-s}

\displaystyle=0\leq\Delta(cz)+|x|^{-\alpha}(cz)^{-s}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\geq cz\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,

\displaystyle w(x),\,cz(x)

\displaystyle\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.

We now apply Lemma
3.4
with
a\equiv 1
,
b\equiv 0
on
\partial K
to deduce
w\geq cz
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. This yields the lower bound in (
3.27
). For the upper bound in (
3.27
), let
C>m^{-\frac{1}{1+s}}
be large enough such that
Cz\geq w
on
\partial K
. Then:

(3.29)

\displaystyle\Delta w+|x|^{-\alpha}w^{-s}

\displaystyle=0\geq\Delta(Cz)+|x|^{-\alpha}(Cz)^{-s}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\leq Cz\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,

\displaystyle w(x),\,Cz(x)

\displaystyle\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.

As above and with the help of Lemma
3.4
we find
Cz\geq w
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
and this concludes the proof of (
3.27
) in the case
2<\alpha<N+s(N-2)
.

(ii2) Assume
\alpha=N+s(N-2)
. The function
z(x)=|x|^{2-N}\log^{\frac{1}{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r_{0}}\Big)
satisfies

-\Delta z(x)=\frac{N-2}{1+s}|x|^{-N}\log^{-\frac{s}{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r_{0}}\Big)+\frac{s}{(1+s)^{2}}|x|^{-N}\log^{-\frac{1+2s}{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r_{0}}\Big)\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

Hence,

-\Delta z(x)\simeq|x|^{-N}\log^{-\frac{s}{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r_{0}}\Big)\simeq|x|^{-\alpha}z^{-s}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

The above estimates show that
z
satisfies (
3.28
) for some constant
c>0
and then, by Lemma
3.4
with
a\equiv 1
,
b\equiv 0
on
\partial K
, we find
w\geq cz
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. In a similar way, for some large constant

For any

where

and

are the constants that satisfy (

Let
(u,v)\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
. We fix
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\in\mathcal{A}
, a

-extension of

to the whole
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K}
. Let

be the solution of (

. We define

is continuous in the sense that if

in

as

and

is continuous.

Since

we have
\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{A}_{n})\subset\mathcal{A}_{n}
for all

. Furthermore, by standard elliptic regularity,

maps
\mathcal{A}_{n}
into

. This shows that

\mathcal{H}_{n}:\mathcal{A}_{n}\to\mathcal{A}_{n}\quad\mbox{ is compact.}

Hence,
\mathcal{H}_{n}
fulfils all conditions in the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem and thus, there exists

a fixed point of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. This yields

(5.23)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u_{n}=\frac{u_{n}^{p}}{v_{n}^{q}}+\lambda\rho(x)\,,u_{n}>0&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus K,\\[10.84006pt]

-\Delta u_{n}=f_{n}(x)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad-\Delta v_{n}=h_{n}(x)\quad\mbox{ in }B_{n}\setminus K,

where, by the definition of

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\;f_{n}(x)=\frac{u_{n}^{p}}{v_{n}^{q}}+\lambda\rho(x)\leq(E^{p}F^{-q}+C_{2}\lambda)(|x|^{-p(N-2)}\psi(x)^{-q}+|x|^{-k})\\[7.22743pt]

7
Further extensions

The approach we developed in this work can be extended to more other systems with various sign of exponents.
Consider for instance the systems

(7.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{1}{u^{p}v^{q}}+\lambda\rho(x)\,,u>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v=\frac{u^{m}}{v^{s}}\,,v>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;u(x),v(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

(7.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{1}{u^{p}v^{q}}+\lambda\rho(x)\,,u>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v=\frac{1}{u^{m}v^{s}}\,,v>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;u(x),v(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

and

(7.3)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{v^{q}}{u^{p}}+\lambda\rho(x)\,,u>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;u(x),v(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

where
\rho\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, is a nonnegative function and
p,q,m,s,\lambda>0
.

Using an approach from dynamical systems, positive radial solutions of the following system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=|x|^{\alpha}u^{a}v^{b}\\[2.84544pt]
-\Delta v=|x|^{\beta}u^{c}v^{d}\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

were studied in
[

]
for

and in
[

where

(see also
[

]
for further extensions to quasilinear elliptic operators). Our systems (

and complemented by Neumann boundary condition on

. Thus, (

.

(ii2)

(u,v)

(i)-(ii) with

(ii) Suppose

are superharmonic, by Lemma

it follows that

to (
7.3
) with minimal decay at infinity, that is,

(7.4)

u(x)\simeq v(x)\simeq|x|^{2-N}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

The interested reader may explore other features of (
7.3
) such as existence of solutions with different asymptotic behaviour at infinity, as we did in Theorem
2.3
. Our last result in this section is stated and proved below.

Theorem 7.2
.

(Existence of solutions to (
7.3
) with minimal decay)

Assume
N\geq 3
,
p,q,m,s>0
and
\rho(x)
satisfies (
2.1
). Then, for some
\lambda>0
the system (
7.3
) has a positive solution
(u,v)
which satisfies (
7.4
) if and only if

(7.5)

k>N,\quad\;q>p+\frac{N}{N-2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad m>s+\frac{N}{N-2}.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
(u,v)
is a positive solution of (
7.3
) which satisfies (
7.4
). Then, by Theorem
7.1
we have
q(N-2)>2
and
m(N-2)>2
. Also,

-\Delta u\geq\lambda\rho(x)\geq\lambda C_{2}|x|^{-k}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

By (
7.4
) and the asymptotic (
3.31
) in Lemma
3.7
it follows that
k>N
.
Now, the first equation of (
7.3
) together with (
7.4
) yield

-\Delta u\geq\phi(x)u^{-p}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,

where
\phi(x)=v(x)^{q}\simeq|x|^{-q(N-2)}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. If
q(N-2)\leq N+p(N-2)
, then by the asymptotic behaviour (
3.27
) in Lemma
3.6
together with the comparison principle in Lemma
3.4
we find

v(x)\geq c\begin{cases}|x|^{-\frac{q(N-2)-2}{1+p}}&\quad\mbox{ if }2<q(N-2)<N+p(N-2)\\[7.22743pt]
|x|^{2-N}\log^{\frac{1}{1+p}}&\quad\mbox{ if }q(N-2)=N+p(N-2)\\[7.22743pt]
\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K.

This contradicts the asymptotic (
7.4
). Hence,
q(N-2)>N+p(N-2)
which yields
q>p+\frac{N}{N-2}
. Similarly,
v
satisfies

-\Delta v=\psi(x)v^{-s}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,

where
\psi(x)=u(x)^{m}\simeq|x|^{-m(N-2)}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
. With a similar argument we deduce
m>s+\frac{N}{N-2}
.

Conversely, assume now that
p,q,m,s,k
satisfy (
7.5
). The following result is the counterpart of Lemma
5.1
which suits our system (
7.3
).

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume (
7.5
) holds. Then, there exist two positive constants
C_{6}>C_{5}>0
depending on
N,p,q,m,s
(but not on
\lambda
) such that:

(i)

Any function
w\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
which is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta w\geq|x|^{-m(N-2)}w^{-s}\,,w>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;w(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

satisfies
w(x)\geq C_{5}|x|^{2-N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
.

(ii)

Any function
w\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
which is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta w\leq|x|^{-m(N-2)}w^{-s}\,,w>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;w(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

satisfies
w(x)\leq C_{6}|x|^{2-N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
.

(iii)

Any function
w\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
which is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta w\geq|x|^{-k}\,,w>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;w(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

satisfies
w(x)\geq C_{3}|x|^{2-N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
.

(iv)

Any function
w\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})
which is a solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta w\leq|x|^{-(q-p)(N-2)}+|x|^{-k}\,,w>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[5.69046pt]
\;\;\;w(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

satisfies
w(x)\leq C_{6}|x|^{2-N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K
.

The proof of Lemma
7.3
is similar to that of Lemma
5.1
and will be omitted here.

Let
C_{2}>C_{1}>0
be the constants from (
2.1
) and
C_{6}>C_{5}>0
be the constants introduced in Lemma
7.3
. Define

(7.6)

D=C_{1}C_{5}\lambda\,,\quad E=2C_{2}C_{6}\lambda\,,\quad F=D^{\frac{m}{1+s}}C_{5}\,,\quad G=E^{\frac{m}{1+s}}C_{6}.

Then
D<E
and
F<G
. Define next

\mathcal{A}=\Bigg\{(u,v)\in C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})\times C^{0,\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K}):\;\begin{aligned} D|x|^{2-N}&\leq u(x)\leq E|x|^{2-N}\\
F|x|^{2-N}&\leq v(x)\leq G|x|^{2-N}\end{aligned}\;\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K\Bigg\}.

For any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{A}
, let
(Tu,Tv)
be the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\Delta(Tu)=\frac{v^{q}}{u^{p}}+\lambda\rho(x)\,,Tu>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta(Tv)=u^{m}(Tv)^{-s}\,,Tv>0&\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;\frac{\partial(Tu)}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial(Tv)}{\partial\nu}=0&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial K,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\;\;\;Tu(x),Tv(x)\to 0&\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{cases}

We further define

\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{A}\to C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})\times C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus K})\,,\quad\mathcal{H}[u,v]=(Tu,Tv).

Clearly, any fixed point of
\mathcal{H}
is a solution to (
7.3
). Let

\lambda^{**}:=\left[\frac{C_{2}(C_{1}C_{5})^{p}}{(2C_{2}C_{6})^{\frac{mq}{1+s}}C_{6}^{q}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\frac{mq}{1+s}-(p+1)}}.

From the definition of
D,E,F,G
in (
7.6
) we can check that for all
0<\lambda\leq\lambda^{**}
we have

C_{6}(G^{q}D^{-p}+C_{2}\lambda)\leq E,

which is the counterpart of (
5.17
). From now on, the approach follows the same steps as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
to deduce
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{A}
and to obtain a solution to (
7.3
).

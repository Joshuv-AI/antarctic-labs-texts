A class of weighted isoperimetric inequalities in hyperbolic space
Haizhong Li, Botong Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a class of weighted isoperimetric inequalities for bounded domains in hyperbolic space by using the isoperimetric inequality with log-convex density in Euclidean space. As a consequence, we remove the horo-convex assumption of domains in a weighted isoperimetric inequality proved by Scheuer-Xia. Furthermore, we prove weighted isoperimetric inequalities for star-shaped domains in warped product manifolds. Particularly, we obtain a weighted isoperimetric inequality for star-shaped hypersurfaces lying outside a certain radial coordinate slice in the anti-de Sitter-Schwarzschild manifold.

Research on weighted isoperimetric inequalities for bounded domains in manifolds is popular in recent years.
In Euclidean space

The main result of this paper is a class of weighted isoperimetric inequalities for bounded domains in

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with
C^{1}
boundary. Let
\phi(t)
be a smooth, positive, even function on
\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. Then

(1.1)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi(\sinh r)\cosh rd\mu\geq\psi\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\sinh r)\cosh rdv\right),

where
\psi
satisfies

(1.2)

\psi\left(\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)=\omega_{n}\phi(t)t^{n}\sqrt{t^{2}+1},\quad\forall\ t\geq 0.

Equality holds in (
1.1

\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.

Theorem
1.1

[
5

in hyperbolic space. Furthermore, by letting

Corollary 1.1

Let
\Omega

\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}\cosh rd\mu\right)^{2}\geq\left((n+1)\int_{\Omega}\cosh rdv\right)^{2}+\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\left((n+1)\int_{\Omega}\cosh rdv\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}.

Equality holds if and only if
\Omega

Denote by

Assume that

Let
M^{n+1}=[a,b]\times\mathbb{S}^{n}

\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},

Define

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a star-shaped,
C^{1}
hypersurface in
M^{n+1}
. Let
\Omega
be the domain bounded by
\Sigma
and
\{a\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Assume that
\Lambda(t)
is smooth, non-decreasing and satisfies
\left(\Lambda^{\prime}(t)t\right)^{\prime}\geq 0
for all
t\in[\lambda(a),\lambda(b)]
.
Let
\phi(t)
be a smooth, positive, even function on
[-\lambda(b),\lambda(b)]

\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. Then

(1.3)

\int_{\Sigma}\phi(\lambda(r))\lambda^{\prime}(r)d\mu\geq\tilde{\psi}\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda(r))\lambda^{\prime}(r)dv\right),

where
\tilde{\psi}
satisfies

\tilde{\psi}\left(\omega_{n}\int_{\lambda(a)}^{t}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)=\omega_{n}\phi(t)t^{n}\Psi(t),\quad\forall\ t\in[\lambda(a),\lambda(b)].

Equality holds in (
1.3
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a radial coordinate slice
\{r_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
r_{0}\in[a,b]
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the case
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we have
\lambda(r)=\sinh r
,
\Psi(t)=\cosh\left(\sinh^{-1}(t)\right)=\sqrt{t^{2}+1}
and
\Lambda(t)=1

1.2

1.1

The anti-de Sitter-Schwarzschild manifold can be viewed as a special type of warped product manifolds, where
\lambda(r)
satisfies

(1.4)

\lambda^{\prime}(r)=\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}-m\lambda^{1-n}}

As a consequence,

[

If we remove the restrictions of

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M^{n+1}=[a,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
be equipped with the anti-de Sitter-Schwarzschild metric, where

\lambda(a)=m^{\frac{1}{n+1}}

Let
\Sigma
be a star-shaped,

hypersurface in

. Let
\Omega
be the domain bounded by
\Sigma

\{a\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}(r)d\mu\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(n+1\right)\widehat{\eta}\left(\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}(r)dv\right)+\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(r)}{\lambda^{n}(r)\sqrt{\left(\lambda^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}-1}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left((n+1)\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}(r)dv+\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}m\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}},

Equality holds in (
1.5
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a radial coordinate slice
\{r_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
r_{0}\in[a,+\infty)
.

2.
Weighted isoperimetric inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

In Section
2
, we give the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollaries
1.2
–
1.3
.

The Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is an
(n+2)
-dimensional vector space with metric

\langle X,Y\rangle=\langle(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n+1}),(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n+1})\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}-x_{n+1}y_{n+1}.

We write
X=(x,x_{n+1})
for
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The hyperboloid model of the hyperbolic space is
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}\left|\langle X,X\rangle=-1,x_{n+1}>0\right.\}
. That implies that
X
is the normal vector of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
. For any
X\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, the polar coordinates of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
give that
X=(x,x_{n+1})=\left(\sinh r\theta,\cosh r\right)
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
r\geq 0
. Here
r
represents the geodesic distance from
X
to
(0,1)
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.
In this section, we let
\lambda=\lvert x\rvert=\sinh r
and
\lambda^{\prime}=x_{n+1}=\cosh r
.
For the above facts, we refer readers to Petersen’s book
[
14
]
.

Now we define the projection map
\pi:\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by
\pi\left(X\right)=x
.
Let
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
be a closed,
C^{1}
hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We define
\widehat{\Omega}=\pi(\Omega)
and
\widehat{\Sigma}=\pi(\Sigma)
. We use
d\widehat{v}
to denote the volume element of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Obviously,
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a closed,
C^{1}
hypersurface as
\pi
is a diffeomorphism from
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let
\widehat{\nu}
denote the unit outer normal of
\widehat{\Sigma}
and
\widehat{u}:=\langle x,\widehat{\nu}\rangle
denote the Euclidean support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
. For any point
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we let
\rho=\lvert x\rvert
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be any continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
.
The weighted volume of a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
equals to the weighted volume of
\widehat{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, i.e.

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv=\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
dv=\lambda^{n}drd\sigma
and
d\widehat{v}=\rho^{n}d\rho d\sigma
, where
d\sigma
is the area element of unite sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Since
\rho=\lvert\pi(X)\rvert=\lambda(r)
, we have

(2.2)

\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv=\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{n}drd\sigma=\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{n}d\lambda d\sigma=\phi(\rho)\rho^{n}d\rho d\sigma=\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}.

Then we get Lemma
2.1
by integrating both sides of (
2.2
) on
\Omega
and
\widehat{\Omega}
respectively.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed,
C^{1}
hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.
A unit normal vector of
\Sigma
at
X\in\Sigma
can be given as

(2.3)

\nu=\frac{(\widehat{\nu}+\widehat{u}x,\lambda^{\prime}\widehat{u})}{\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}},

where
\widehat{u}=\langle x,\widehat{\nu}\rangle
is the Euclidean support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}=\pi(\Sigma)
at
x=\pi(X)
.

Proof.

We only need to check the following facts:
\langle X,\nu\rangle=0
,
\langle\nu,\nu\rangle=1
and
\langle\pi^{-1}_{*}e,\nu\rangle=0
for any vector
e\in T_{x}\widehat{\Sigma}
. Here
\pi^{-1}_{*}e
denotes the pushforward vector of
e
via map
\pi^{-1}
. The first two facts imply that
\nu
is a unit vector in
T_{X}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then, the third fact confirms that
\nu
is a normal vector at
X\in\Sigma
.

By a direct calculation, we have

(2.4)

\langle X,\nu\rangle=\frac{\langle(x,\lambda^{\prime}),(\widehat{\nu}+\widehat{u}x,\lambda^{\prime}\widehat{u})\rangle}{\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}}=\frac{\langle x,\widehat{\nu}\rangle+\widehat{u}\lvert x\rvert^{2}-\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}\widehat{u}}{\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}}=0,

where we used
\lvert x\rvert^{2}=\lambda^{2}
and
\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}-\lambda^{2}=1
. Similarly, we find that

(2.5)

\langle\nu,\nu\rangle=\frac{\lvert\widehat{\nu}+\widehat{u}x\rvert^{2}-\left(\lambda^{\prime}\widehat{u}\right)^{2}}{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}=\frac{1+2\widehat{u}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\widehat{u}^{2}-\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}\widehat{u}^{2}}{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}=1.

Since
\pi\left((x,x_{n+1})\right)=x
,
for any
e\in T_{x}\widehat{\Sigma}
, we can let
\pi^{-1}_{*}e=\left(e,\alpha\right)
for some real number
\alpha
. As
\pi^{-1}_{*}e\in T_{X}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we know
\langle X,\left(e,\alpha\right)\rangle=0
. Hence
\alpha\lambda^{\prime}=\langle x,e\rangle
. Therefore, we get

\pi^{-1}_{*}e=\left(e,\frac{\langle x,e\rangle}{\lambda^{\prime}}\right).

This together with the assumption
\langle e,\widehat{\nu}\rangle=0
gives

(2.6)

\langle\pi^{-1}_{*}e,\nu\rangle=\frac{\langle e,\widehat{\nu}+\widehat{u}x\rangle-\widehat{u}\langle x,e\rangle}{\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}}=\frac{\langle e,\widehat{\nu}\rangle+\widehat{u}\langle x,e\rangle-\widehat{u}\langle x,e\rangle}{\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}}=0.

Then Lemma
2.2
follows from (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).
∎

In the next lemma, we transform the weighted area of
\Sigma
to an integral on
\widehat{\Sigma}
. We use
\lrcorner
to denote the interior multiplication. We refer to Lee’s book
[
11
]
for common properties of the interior multiplication.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed,
C^{1}
hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.
Let
\phi
be any continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
.
It holds that

(2.7)

\int_{\Sigma}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}d\mu=\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}.

Proof.

Using (
2.3
), we have

(2.8)

\langle\pi_{*}\nu,\widehat{\nu}\rangle=\frac{\langle\widehat{\nu}+\widehat{u}x,\widehat{\nu}\rangle}{\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}}=\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}.

Using (
2.2
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

(2.9)

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}d\mu=

\displaystyle\nu\lrcorner\left(\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\pi_{*}\nu\right)\lrcorner\left(\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\langle\pi_{*}\nu,\widehat{\nu}\rangle d\widehat{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}.

Then Lemma
2.3
follows by integrating both sides of (
2.9
) on
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\Sigma}
respectively.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\phi(t)
be a function that satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
. Then

(2.10)

\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}\lambda dv\geq\eta\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right),

where

(2.11)

\eta\left(\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)=\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(s)s^{n+1}ds,\quad\forall\ t\geq 0.

If
\phi(\lambda(r))
is not constant on
\Omega
, then equality holds in (
2.10
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.

Proof.

We set
dm=\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv
. Recall that
B(r)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at the origin in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.
Let
r_{0}
satisfy
\int_{B(r_{0})}dm=\int_{\Omega}dm
.
Hence,

(2.12)

\int_{\Omega/B(r_{0})}dm=\int_{B(r_{0})/\Omega}dm.

Using (
2.11
), we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\eta\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)=

\displaystyle\eta\left(\int_{B(r_{0})}dm\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\left(\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\lambda(r_{0})}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{n}d\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\lambda{(r_{0})}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda^{n+1}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(r_{0})}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm.

Since
\phi
is even on
\mathbb{R}
, we have
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
. Then the assumption
\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
directly implies that
\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime}\geq 0
on
[0,+\infty)
.
Then,

(2.14)

\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(t)t}{\phi(t)}\right)^{\prime}=\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime\prime}(t)t+(\log\phi)^{\prime}(t)\geq 0.

Now we can apply the symmetrization method to get

(2.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}\lambda dv=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B(r_{0})}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm+\int_{\Omega/B(r_{0})}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B(r_{0})}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm+\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda(r_{0}))\lambda(r_{0})}{\phi(\lambda(r_{0}))}\int_{\Omega/B(r_{0})}dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(r_{0})\cap\Omega}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm+\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda(r_{0}))\lambda(r_{0})}{\phi(r_{0})}\int_{B(r_{0})/\Omega}dm

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B(r_{0})}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda}{\phi(\lambda)}dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right),

where we used (
2.14
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
). Thus we proved (
2.10
).
If
\phi(\lambda)
is not constant in
\Omega
, then we know that (
2.14
) is strict on some interval
[r_{1},r_{2}]
such that
0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
and
\int_{\Omega/B(r_{2})}dm>0
. In the case that equality holds in (

is not
B(r_{0})
. However, if equality holds in (

. That is a contradiction. Hence, in the case that

is not constant in

, equality holds in (

) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.
We complete the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The key step in the proof of Lemma

). Similar arguments can be applied to many other comparisons of volumes with different weights.

Chambers

proved the following isoperimetric inequality with log-convex density. For convenience, we will use the settings and notations in this paper.

Theorem C

(
[

)
.

Let
\widehat{\Omega}
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with

\widehat{\Sigma}
.
Let

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}\geq\xi\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right),

where

\widehat{\Sigma}

(2.16)

\xi\left(\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)=\omega_{n}\phi(t)t^{n},\quad\forall\ t\geq 0.

Now we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It is simple to see that
\chi(t)=\sqrt{t^{2}+1}

(2.17)

\frac{\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}}{\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}}\geq\sqrt{\left(\frac{\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\widehat{u}d\widehat{\mu}}{\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}}\right)^{2}+1}.

By the divergence theorem, we know that

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\widehat{u}d\widehat{\mu}=\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\mathrm{div}\left(\phi(\rho)x\right)d\widehat{v}=(n+1)\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}+\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho d\widehat{v}.

Then we can simplify (
2.17
) to get

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\widehat{u}^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left((n+1)\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}+\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho d\widehat{v}\right)^{2}+\left(\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left((n+1)\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}+\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho d\widehat{v}\right)^{2}+\xi\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right)^{2},

where we used Theorem C in the second inequality.
By using (

(2.19)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left((n+1)\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv+\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}\lambda dv\right)^{2}+\xi\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\left((n+1)\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv+\eta\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)\right)^{2}+\xi\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)^{2},

where we used (

2.11

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{0}}(n+1)\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)+\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\phi^{\prime}(s)s^{n+1}ds\right)^{2}+\left(\omega_{n}\phi(t_{0})t_{0}^{n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{n}\phi(t_{0})t_{0}^{n}\sqrt{t_{0}^{2}+1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\left(\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\psi\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)^{2},

We complete the proof of Theorem

.
∎

In the following Lemma
2.5
, we study the comparison between the weighted volume and the canonical volume of domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Although the proof is similar to that of Lemma
2.4
, it is interesting in its own right. For convenience, we set
\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv
and
r_{\Omega}:=\tilde{f}_{0}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)\right)
for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Recall that

was defined in Section

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, then

\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv\geq\frac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}\lambda^{n+1}(r_{\Omega}).

Equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{Vol}\left(\Omega/B(r_{\Omega})\right)=\mathrm{Vol}\left(B(r_{\Omega})/\Omega\right)
by the definition of
r_{\Omega}
. Since
\lambda^{\prime}(r)=\cosh r
is strictly increasing in
r
for
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{w}\left(\Omega\cap B(r_{\Omega})\right)+\mathrm{Vol}_{w}\left(\Omega/B(r_{\Omega})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{w}\left(\Omega\cap B(r_{\Omega})\right)+\lambda^{\prime}(r_{\Omega})\mathrm{Vol}\left(\Omega/B(r_{\Omega})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{w}\left(B(r_{\Omega})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}\lambda^{n+1}(r_{\Omega}).

Equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
. Recall that Corollary
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.1
directly.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Using Corollary
1.1
and the divergence theorem, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(\cosh r-u)d\mu=\int_{\partial\Omega}\cosh rd\mu-(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left((n+1)\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)\right)^{2}+\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\left((n+1)\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega).

It is easy to see that the right hand side of (
2.20
) is monotone increasing as a single-variable function of
\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)
. Therefore, by using Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(\cosh r-u)d\mu\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(\omega_{n}\sinh^{n+1}(r_{\Omega})\right)^{2}+\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\left(\omega_{n}\sinh^{n+1}(r_{\Omega})\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\omega_{n}\sinh^{n+1}(r_{\Omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}e^{-r_{\Omega}}\sinh^{n}(r_{\Omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{0}\circ\tilde{f}_{0}^{-1}(\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)).

Equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin. We complete the proof of Corollary
1.2
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Since
f_{0}=\tilde{f}_{0}
, we have
r_{\Omega}=\tilde{f}_{0}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)\right)=f_{0}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)\right)
.
Using Corollary
1.1
and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\cosh rd\mu\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left((n+1)\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)\right)^{2}+\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\left((n+1)\mathrm{Vol}_{w}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(\omega_{n}\sinh^{n+1}(r_{\Omega})\right)^{2}+\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\left(\omega_{n}\sinh^{n+1}(r_{\Omega})\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\cosh\left(r_{\Omega}\right)\sinh^{n}\left(r_{\Omega}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0}\circ f_{0}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)\right).

Equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin. We complete the proof of Corollary
1.3
.
∎

3.
Weighted isoperimetric inequalities in warped product manifolds

In Section
3
, we give the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

Let
M^{n+1}=[a,b]\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a warped product manifold with metric

\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},

where
\lambda(a)\geq 0
,
\lambda^{\prime}\geq 1
on
[a,b]
, and
b
could be infinity.
Then the volume element of
M^{n+1}
is
dv:=\lambda^{n}drd\sigma
, where
d\sigma
denotes the area element of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

A star-shaped,
C^{1}
hypersurface
(\Sigma,g)\subset(M^{n+1},\bar{g})
can be written as

\Sigma=\{(r(\theta),\theta)\left|\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\right.\},

where
r(\theta)
is a
C^{1}
function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
a\leq r(\theta)\leq b
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
We define
\Omega
as the domain bounded by
\Sigma
and the slice
\{a\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
in
M^{n+1}
.
For a function
f(\theta)
defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we use
Df
to denote the gradient of
f
.
Let
V=\lambda(r)\partial_{r}
be a conformal Killing vector field in
M^{n+1}
. The support function of
\Sigma
is given by
u:=\langle V,\nu\rangle
, where
\nu
is the unit outer normal of
\Sigma
. Let
\sigma_{ij}
denote the standard metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Then we know that (see e.g.
[
9
]
)

\displaystyle g_{ij}=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\sigma_{ij}+D_{i}rD_{j}r,

(3.1)

\displaystyle u=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\lvert Dr\rvert^{2}}}.

Hence we get

(3.2)

d\mu=\sqrt{\det\left(g_{ij}\right)}d\sigma=\lambda^{n-1}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\lvert Dr\rvert^{2}}d\sigma=\frac{\lambda^{n+1}}{u}d\sigma.

We remark that the above formulas are valid for star-shaped hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by viewing
\lambda(r)=r
.

In Section
3
, we define
\pi:M^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by
\pi\left((r,\theta)\right)=\rho\theta
, where
\rho=\lambda(r)
. We denote
\widehat{\Sigma}=\pi(\Sigma)
and
\widehat{\Omega}=\pi(\Omega)
as in Section
2
. It is easy to see that (
2.1
) is valid in this situation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a star-shaped,
C^{1}
hypersurface in
M^{n+1}
. Let
\phi
be any continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
.
Then

(3.3)

\int_{\Sigma}\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}d\mu=\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\frac{\Psi^{2}(\rho)-1}{\rho^{2}}\widehat{u}^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu},

where
\widehat{u}
denotes the support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\Psi(t):=\lambda^{\prime}\circ\lambda^{-1}(t)
.

Proof.

Since
\rho=\lambda(r)
, formula (
3.1
) yields that

(3.4)

\widehat{u}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\lvert D\rho\rvert^{2}}}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}\lvert Dr\rvert^{2}}},

By a direct calculation, (
3.4
) deduces that

\lvert Dr\rvert^{2}=\frac{\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}-\widehat{u}^{2}\right)}{\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}\widehat{u}^{2}}.

Inserting this formula into (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.5)

u=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}(\lambda^{2}-\widehat{u}^{2})}{\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}\widehat{u}^{2}}}}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda\widehat{u}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+\left(\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}-1\right)\widehat{u}^{2}}}.

Now we focus on the relationship between area elements of
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\Sigma}
. Formula (
3.2
) implies that

(3.6)

d\mu=\frac{\lambda^{n+1}}{u}d\sigma,\quad d\widehat{\mu}=\frac{\rho^{n+1}}{\widehat{u}}d\sigma.

Combining (
3.6
) with (
3.5
), we have

\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}d\mu=\frac{\phi(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{n+1}}{u}d\sigma=\frac{\phi(\rho)\lambda^{\prime}\widehat{u}}{u}d\widehat{\mu}=\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\frac{\Psi^{2}(\rho)-1}{\rho^{2}}\widehat{u}^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}.

Then Lemma
3.1
follows by integrating both sides of the above formula on
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\Sigma}
respectively.
∎

Now we use Theorem C to derive the following weighted isoperimetric inequalities for domains in an annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a star-shaped hypersurface in the domain bounded by the geodesic spheres
\{\lambda(a)\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\{\lambda(b)\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
\widehat{\Omega}
be the domain bounded by
\widehat{\Sigma}
and
\{\lambda(a)\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
\phi(t)
be a smooth, positive, even function on
[-\lambda(b),\lambda(b)]
that satisfies
\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. Then

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}\geq\tilde{\xi}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right),

where
\rho
denotes the radial function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
, and
\tilde{\xi}
satisfies

\tilde{\xi}\left(\omega_{n}\int_{\lambda(a)}^{t}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)=\omega_{n}\phi(t)t^{n},\quad\forall\ t\in[\lambda(a),\lambda(b)].

Proof.

Theorem C implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}\geq

\displaystyle\xi\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}\cup B(\lambda(a))}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}+\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\lambda(a)}\phi(s)s^{n}ds\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right),

where we used (
2.16
).
We complete the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Next, we prove the weighted isoperimetric inequalities in warped product manifolds.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Jensen’s inequality yields that

(3.7)

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\left(\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}\right)^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}\geq\sqrt{\left(\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}d\widehat{\mu}\right)^{2}+\left(\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}\right)^{2}},

with equality if and only if
\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}
is constant on
\widehat{\Sigma}
.
By the divergence theorem, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}d\widehat{\mu}-\omega_{n}\phi(\lambda(a))\Lambda(\lambda(a))\lambda^{n+1}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)d\widehat{v}+\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\left(\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)\right)^{\prime}\rho d\widehat{v}.

The assumptions of
\Lambda(t)
and
\phi(t)
imply that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)}{\phi(\rho)}\right)^{\prime}\geq

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left(\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)\right)^{\prime}\rho}{\phi(\rho)}\right)^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime\prime}\lambda\rho+\left(\log\phi\right)^{\prime}\left(\Lambda\rho\right)^{\prime}+\left(\Lambda^{\prime}\rho\right)^{\prime}\geq 0.

Then we can apply the symmetrization method as in Lemma
2.4
to get

(3.9)

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}d\widehat{\mu}\geq\tilde{\eta}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right),

where
\tilde{\eta}
is a single-variable, increasing function such that equality holds in (
3.9
) when
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a geodesic sphere centered at the origin. Meanwhile, Lemma
3.2
asserts that

(3.10)

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{\mu}\geq\tilde{\xi}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right).

Using (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) in (
3.7
), we have

(3.11)

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\phi(\rho)\sqrt{\left(\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}\right)^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}\geq\tilde{\psi}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\phi(\rho)d\widehat{v}\right).

If equality holds in (
3.11
), then
\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}
is constant. Let
\rho_{\max}=\max_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho(\theta)
and
\rho_{\min}=\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho(\theta)
. Then (
3.4
) implies that
\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}=\Lambda(\rho_{\max})\rho_{\max}=\Lambda(\rho_{\min})\rho_{\min}
. Since
\Lambda^{\prime}\geq 0
, we know that
\rho
is constant on
\widehat{\Sigma}
in this case, which implies that
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a geodesic sphere centered at the origin. Finally, using (
2.1
) and (
3.3
) in (
3.11
), we obtain (
1.3
). Besides, equality holds in (
1.3
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a radial coordinate slice
\{r_{0}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Finally, we prove a weighted isoperimetric inequality in the anti-de Sitter-Schwarzschild manifold.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Here we adapt the notations in the proof of Theorem
1.2
by viewing
\phi(\rho)=1
.
By the property of
\lambda(r)
in (
1.4
), we have for all

,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}-1=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(r)\left(1-m\lambda^{-n-1}(r)\right)\geq 0,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime}(r)\lambda(r)-\left(\lambda^{\prime}(r)\right)^{2}+1=

\displaystyle\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\lambda^{1-n}(r)>0.

Hence
\Lambda(t)
is well defined and
\Lambda(\lambda(a))=0
.
Then we use
\rho=\lambda(r)
and (
3.12
) to get

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\frac{d\Lambda(\lambda(r))}{dr}\frac{dr}{d\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}-1}}{\lambda}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}\lambda-\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}+1}{\lambda^{2}\sqrt{\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\frac{1}{\lambda^{n+1}\sqrt{\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\frac{1}{\rho^{n+2}\Lambda(\rho)}\geq 0.

Therefore, by a similar symmetrization argument as in Lemma
2.4
, we have

(3.14)

\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\Lambda(\rho)d\widehat{v}\geq\widehat{\eta}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}d\widehat{v}\right),

where
\widehat{\eta}
was defined in (
1.6
).
Equality holds in (

\widehat{\Sigma}
is a geodesic sphere of radius greater than

centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
From (
3.13
), we have

(3.15)

\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\Lambda^{\prime}(\rho)\rho d\widehat{v}=\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}\Lambda(\rho)}d\widehat{v}.

Inserting (
3.14
) and (

), we obtain

(3.16)

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}d\widehat{\mu}\geq\left(n+1\right)\widehat{\eta}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}d\widehat{v}\right)+\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}\Lambda(\rho)}d\widehat{v}.

The classical isoperimetric inequality asserts

(3.17)

\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}d\widehat{\mu}\geq\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\left((n+1)\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}d\widehat{v}+\omega_{n}m\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}.

Here we remark that
\frac{\omega_{n}m}{n+1}
is the volume of

. Using (

) and (

) in (

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\sqrt{\left(\Lambda(\rho)\widehat{u}\right)^{2}+1}d\widehat{\mu}\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(n+1\right)\widehat{\eta}\left(\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}d\widehat{v}\right)+\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}\frac{1}{\rho^{n+1}\Lambda(\rho)}d\widehat{v}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left((n+1)\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\int_{\widehat{\Omega}}d\widehat{v}+\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}m\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}.

Then we can apply (

) and (

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\left(n+1\right)\widehat{\eta}\left(\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv\right)+\frac{m(n+1)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda^{n}\sqrt{\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)^{2}-1}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left((n+1)\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv+\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}m\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}.

Thus we proved (

).
By (

) and the same argument as in the proof of Theorem

, we know that equality holds in (

) if and only if

is a radial coordinate slice

, where

. We complete the proof of Theorem

.
∎

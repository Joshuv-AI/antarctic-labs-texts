K-Primitivity : A Literature Survey
Monimala Nej

Abstract
A nonnegative matrix A is said to be primitive if there exists a positive integer m m such that entries in A m A^{m} are positive and smallest such m m is called the exponent of A . A. Primitive matrices are useful in the study of finite Markov chains theory. In 1998, in the context of finite Markov chains, Ettore Fornasini and Maria Elena Valcher [ 6 ] extended the notion of primitivity for a nonnegative matrix pair ( A , B ) (A,B) by considering a positive discrete homogeneous two-dimensional ( 2 ​ D ) (2D) state model. Further generalization to this notion of primitivity for k k -tuple ( A 1 , A 2 , … , A k ) (A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}) of nonnegative matrices A 1 , A 2 , … , A k A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k} is quite natural and known as k k -primitivity . In this paper we present various results on k k -primitivity given by different researchers from time to time.

3
Concept of
k
-primitivity and relevant results

Primitive matrices plays an important role in the study of finite Markov chains theory. In finite Markov chains, different kinds of discrete models are studied. One such simple homogeneous discrete model is

x(t+1)=Ax(t),\hskip 14.22636ptt=0,1,\ldots\hskip 142.26378pt(1)

where
x(k)
represent the
k
-th state of the model and
x(0)
is the given initial condition,
A
is a nonnegative matrix associated with the model. It is well-known that
A
is said to be primitive if and only if for every nonzero, nonnegative initial condition
x(0),
the state of the said model becomes positive in a finite number of steps. Later, this notion of primitivity has been extended to a nonnegative matrix pair
(A,B)
by considering a discrete homogeneous
2D
-system, namely,

x(h+1,k+1)=Ax(h,k+1)+Bx(h+1,k),\hskip 8.53581pth,k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\;h+k\geq 0,\hskip 22.76219pt(2)

where

denotes the local states of the system and
x(h,-h)\;(h\in{\mathbb{Z}})
are given initial conditions which are nonnegative
n\times 1
matrices and
A
,
B
are
n\times n
nonnegative matrices. Now the initial values

are said to be admissible provided there exists an integer

such that
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}x(h+i,-h-i)
is nonzero and nonnegative for all
h.

Various properties of pairs of

nonnegative matrices are useful in the context of certain dynamical systems that generalize Markov chains. In 1998 E. Fornasini and M.E. Valcher
[
6
]
investigated a dynamical

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
6
]

A pair of nonnegative matrices

Here we would like to mention that primitivity of circulant matrices plays an important role to the primitivity of the pair
(A,B)
whenever the initial conditions of the associated

Definition 3.2
.

.

A pair of nonnegative matrices

implies
k
-primitivity, but not vice versa. That is, primitivity is more generalized concept than
k
-primitivity for a
k
-tuple
\mathscr{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k})
of nonnegative matrices.

3.1
K
-Primitivity of a digraph
D

In continuation of research in this topic, people obtained a uncolored digraph
D
from a
k
-colored digraph
D^{k}.
If
D^{k}
is primitive, then
D
is also primitive, and
exp(D)\leq exp(D^{k}).
Suppose that
D
is a simple directed graph on at least two vertices, possibly with loops. A
k
-coloring of
D
is a
k
-tuple of spanning subgraphs
(D_{1},\ldots,D_{k})
such that the subgraphs
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
partition the arcs of
D
into
k
(nonempty) subsets. Each
D_{i}
containing the arcs given color
i
in the directed graph
D,
see, for instance,
[
1
]
. Now a digraph
D
is said to be
k
-primitive if there exists a
k
-coloring
(D_{1},\ldots,D_{k})
of
D
such that there is a
k
-tuple of positive integers
(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{k})
and between any pair of vertices
u,v
of
D,
there is a walk from
u
to
v
in
D
having exactly
r_{i}
arcs in
D_{i}
for each
i,

i=1,\ldots,k.
Equivalently, that walk has exactly
r_{i}
arcs of color
i
for each
i=1,\ldots,k.
It is clear that if there is a
k
-coloring of
D
that is
k
-primitive, then
D
itself must be a primitive directed graph. On other hand, if
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
be
(0,1)
-matrices such that their sum
A
is also a
(0,1)
-matrix, that is, each nonzero entry of
A
corresponds to exactly one nonzero entry in some
A_{i},
then it is known that
A
is the disjoint sum of
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}.
This decomposition of
A
corresponds in a natural way to a
k
-coloring of the digraph
D.
Now
A
is said to be
k
-primitive if
k
-tuple
(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k})
is
k
-primitive. Thus
A
is
k
-primitive if and only if
D
is so.

It is known from
[
20
]
that if digraph
D
is
k
-primitive, then necessarily

D
must have at least
k
cycles. In 2003, LeRoy B. Beasley a , Steve Kirkland
[
1
]
showed that any primitive directed graph is a
2
-primitive and for each
k\geq 4,
they provided an example of a primitive directed graph having exactly
k
cycles, but not
k
-primitive. For
k=3,
they provided one conjecture whose confirmation would be a key step in proving that every primitive directed graph with at least three cycles admits a
3
-coloring, that is
3
-primitive. This conjecture is not yet proved. In 2014, LeRoy B. Beasley and S. Mousley
[
2
]
established conditions for primitive digraphs to be
k
-primitive which is basically an improvement of result that any primitive directed graph is a
2
-primitive. In the same paper they proved that
\binom{n-1}{2}\leq k_{max}(n)<\binom{n}{2}-\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil
, where
k_{max}(n)
be the maximum
k
for which there exists a
k
-coloring of some strongly connected
n
-tournament that is
k
-primitive. This result is an extention of results in
[
3
]
, where properties of 2-primitive tournament digraphs have been discussed. In 2009, Y. Gao, Y. Shao
[
10
]
showed that there are
k
-colorings that are
k
-primitive for two different class of digraphs, namely, the class of primitive symmetric digraph of order
n
and for the class of primitive digraphs such that for each primitive digraph
D
if there exist cycles
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{k},
then
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
have no common arc for each pair
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq k.

3.2
Results on

-primitivity and

-exponent of

As of now, in the context of

Let

For different values of

3
the bound on the

]

[

]

[

]

-cycle (

[
13
]

2
-exponent and the corresponding extremal 2-colored digraphs have been characterized. Further, a much simpler bound on the

[
30

[
30

obtained an exact formula for

-primitive digraphs consisting of two arbitrary cycles. Using that formula they showed that the

This is clearly an improvement of previously proposed bound of

in
[
14
]
where

-colored digraphs of the primitive ministrong digraphs with a given exponent are considered. In 2008 Y. Shao and Y. Gao
[

considered

-colored digraphs of strongly connected symmetric digraphs with loops. In this paper they characterized

-primitive digraphs and found the sharp upper bound

-colored digraphs that attain the upper bound. Moreover, they established the exponent set

[

]

-colored digraphs of strongly connected symmetric digraphs without loops are considered. Furthermore, S. Suwilo

The
(n,s)
-lollipops whose

-lollipop, a simple algorithm to find its 2-exponent can be found in this work.

In 1990 R.A. Brualdi and B. Liu.

4

introduce the concept of three different kind of

extend this concept to a primitive

The scrambling index for the class of 2-colored Hamiltonian digraphs on

. The

Similar work on digraphs with two cycles under various conditions, vertex exponent, generalized exponent, local exponent, (see

-colored digraphs can be found in literature which are written in some other language than English. We expect that from the above discussion, reader will get a clear idea about almost all the works have been done in the context of

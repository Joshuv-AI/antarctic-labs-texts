THE DAMPED FOCUSING CUBIC WAVE EQUATION ON A BOUNDED DOMAIN
Thomas Perrin

Abstract




Abstract.
For the focusing cubic wave (or Klein-Gordon) equation on a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
3
, the dichotomy between global existence and blow-up for solutions starting below the energy of the ground state is known since the work of Payne and Sattinger. In the case of a damped equation, we prove that the dichotomy between global existence and blow-up still holds. In particular, the damping does not prevent blow-up. Assuming that the damping satisfies the geometric control condition, we then prove that any global solution converges to a stationary solution along a time sequence, and that global solutions below the energy of the ground state can be stabilised, adapting the proof of a similar result in the defocusing case.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
be the interior and the boundary of a smooth compact connected Riemannian manifold of dimension
3
. Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that the Poincaré inequality

\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+\beta|u|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\gtrsim\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

is satisfied, for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. This specifically requires
\beta>0
if
\partial\Omega
is empty. For real-valued initial data
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, consider the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\square u+\beta u&=&u^{3}&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,\\
(u(0),\partial_{t}u(0))&=&\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&&\text{on }\mathbb{R}\times\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

If
\beta=0
, then (
Introduction
) is the focusing cubic wave equation, and if
\beta>0
, it is the focusing cubic Klein-Gordon equation. For
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, write
\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla u^{0}\right|^{2}+\beta|u^{0}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x
and set

J(u^{0})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\quad\text{ and }\quad E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)=J(u^{0})+\frac{1}{2}\left\|u^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The functionals
J
and
E
are respectively called
static energy
and
energy
. The energy of a solution of (
Introduction
) at time
t\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by
E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)
, and is conserved. Let
Q
be a ground state of (
Introduction
), that is, a positive stationary solution of (
Introduction
) of minimal energy

E(Q,0)=J(Q)=d>0.

See Theorem
13
for a precise definition. For
u^{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, write

K(u^{0})=\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}-\left\|u^{0}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4},

(1)

and set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathscr{K}^{+}=\left\{\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega),E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)<d,K(u^{0})\geq 0\right\},\\
\mathscr{K}^{-}=\left\{\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega),E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)<d,K(u^{0})<0\right\}.\end{array}\right.

The following result is due to Payne and Sattinger
[
15
]
.

Theorem 1
.

The spaces
\mathscr{K}^{+}
and
\mathscr{K}^{-}
are stable under the flow of
(
Introduction
)
. A solution starting from
\mathscr{K}^{+}
is defined on
\mathbb{R}
, and a solution starting from
\mathscr{K}^{-}
blows up in finite positive and negative times.

For
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{+})
, our main focus is the damped equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\square u+\gamma\partial_{t}u+\beta u&=&u^{3}&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
(u(0),\partial_{t}u(0))&=&\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&&\text{on }\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

with
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. For
t\geq 0
, the energy equality is

E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)=E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)\left|\partial_{t}u(s,x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s.

In particular, the energy of a solution is nonincreasing. Our first result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Fix
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{+})
. The spaces
\mathscr{K}^{+}
and
\mathscr{K}^{-}
are stable under the forward flow of
(
Introduction
)
. A solution of
(
Introduction
)
initiated in
\mathscr{K}^{+}
is defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and a solution of
(
Introduction
)
initiated in
\mathscr{K}^{-}
blows up in finite time
t>0
.

In particular, below the energy of the ground state, a blow-up solution of (
Introduction
) cannot be stabilised by the addition of a damping term of the form
\gamma\partial_{t}u
.

We will use the notion of generalized geodesic, for which we refer to
[
14
]
. In this article, we always assume that no generalized geodesic has a contact of infinite order with
\partial\Omega
(see
[
3
]
for some details about this assumption).

Definition 3
.

For
\omega\subset\Omega
, we say that
\omega
satisfies the
Geometric Control Condition
(in short, GCC) if there exists
L>0
such that any generalized geodesic of
\Omega
of length
L
meets the set
\omega
.

In the defocusing case, that is, with the non-linearity
u^{3}
changed into
-u^{3}
, the energy of a solution is positive and governs its size. All corresponding solutions are defined on
\mathbb{R}
, and the only stationary solution is zero. In this case, Joly and Laurent
[
12
]
proved a stabilization result under the GCC, on bounded or unbounded domains. In
[
11
]
, the same authors consider the case of a non-linearity that is asymptotically defocusing and establish a semi-global controllability result. For this type of non-linearity, non-zero stationary solutions exist, but no blow-up phenomenon occurs.

In the focusing case we consider here, few stabilization or controllability results exist in the literature. For a focusing equation on the entire space, or outside a star-shaped obstacle, Alaoui, Ibrahim, and Nakanishi
[
2
]
established stabilization for solutions starting from
\mathcal{K}^{+}
when the damping is greater than a positive constant outside a ball. Note that assuming that the obstacle is star-shaped is stronger than the GCC. Burq, Raugel, and Schlag
[
5
]
proved a dichotomy property for radial solutions of the focusing damped Klein-Gordon equation on the entire space with a constant positive damping: such a solution either blows up in a finite positive time or exists globally in positive time and converges to a stationary solution. In
[
4
]
, the same authors established a similar dichotomy property, again in the radial case, with a damping independent of
x
and converging to zero as
t
tends to infinity. In the case of the cubic focusing radial Klein-Gordon equation on a closed ball of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, Krieger and Xiang
[
13
]
studied the stability of the system near the static solution
u=1
, under different dissipative boundary conditions.

Our second result is the following theorem, which can be seen as an extension of the stabilisation property proved in
[
12
]
to the focusing case.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
\gamma(x)\geq\alpha
for almost all
x\in\omega
, with
\alpha>0
a constant and
\omega\subset\Omega
an open set fulfilling the GCC. Then for any

, there exist

and

such that for all

, the solution

of
(
Introduction
)
satisfies

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\lambda t},\quad t\geq 0,

if
E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\leq E_{0}
.

For a solution starting from
\mathscr{K}^{+}
, we will see that the energy can be used almost as in the defocusing case, allowing us to adapt most of the arguments of
[
12
]
. Note that

and
\lambda
depend on

(see Remark
29
).

The proof of Theorem
4
will rely on an asymptotic compactness result, which roughly states that for all uniformly bounded sequence of global solutions of (
Introduction
), one can find a subsequence which converges to a stationary solution of (
Introduction
) along a time-sequence; see Proposition
22
for a precise statement. It implies the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Fix
\gamma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfying the assumption of Theorem
4
. Let
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that the associated solution

of
(
Introduction
)
exists on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

1.

For all sequence
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a stationary solution

of
(
Introduction
)
such that

\left(u(T_{\phi(n)}+\cdot),\partial_{t}u(T_{\phi(n)}+\cdot)\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}(w,0)

in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega))
.

2.

Assume that there exists an at most countable number of stationary solution

of
(
Introduction
)
such that

J(w)=\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right).

(2)

Then there exists a stationary solution

of
(
Introduction
)
such that

\left(u(t+\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t+\cdot)\right)\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow+\infty}(w,0)

in

.

Remark 6
.

For example, if
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\liminf E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\leq J(Q)
, then there are at most 3 stationary solutions satisfying (
2
), which are
0
,
Q
and
-Q
(see Theorem
13
).

Remark 7
.

In a companion paper
[
16
]
, we prove that all solutions of (
Introduction
) defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
are bounded in the energy space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, generalizing a result of Cazenave
[
6
]
. Our proof of Theorem
5
relies on this fact.

On the one hand, observe that Theorem
5
applies to any solution defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, while Theorem
4
only applies to solutions with energy below the energy of the ground state. On the other hand, Theorem
4
provides an exponential convergence rate, whereas Theorem
5
provides no convergence rate.

In
[
5
]
, the authors proved that any radial solution defined on

converges to an asymptotic solution, when considering
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. They make no assumption regarding the number of stationary solutions at a fixed energy level. Their damping is a positive constant, whereas Theorem

holds true for a damping satisfying the GCC. The fact that a solution defined on

is bounded in the energy space arises as a consequence of their main result, using dynamical systems arguments.

(3)

1.2
Ground state and some related properties

Consider
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
u\neq 0
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, set
j(\lambda)=J(\lambda u)
, that is

j(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{H_{0}^{1}}-\frac{\lambda^{4}}{4}\left\|u\right\|^{4}_{L^{4}}.

Write
\lambda^{\ast}=\lambda^{\ast}(u)>0
for the positive argument of the maximum of

. Using
j^{\prime}(\lambda^{\ast})=0
, one finds
K\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)=0
, where

is defined by (

). Set

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{J\left(\lambda^{\ast}(u)u\right),u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),u\neq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{J(w),w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),w\neq 0,K(w)=0\right\}.

(7)

The fact that equality (

) holds is clear, because for
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
w\neq 0
, there exists a unique

such that

\lambda\left\|w\right\|^{2}_{H_{0}^{1}}-\lambda^{3}\left\|w\right\|^{4}_{L^{4}}=0

so that if

then

. Note that if

satisfies

, then

. Note also that one has

\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(w^{4}-v^{4}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right|\lesssim\left\|w-v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|w\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{3}+\left\|v\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{3}\right)

so that

and

are continuous by the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
. The following result is due to Payne and Sattinger
[

]
, in the case of a bounded subset of

. The extension to the case of a compact manifold is straightforward, except for the uniqueness part.

Theorem 13
.

One has
d>0
, and there exists
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

,

, and

. Such a function

We prove some technical results for later use. The first one states that a function
u
such that
K(u)<0
is far from the zero function.

Lemma 14
.

Let
(u_{n})
be a sequence of elements of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
K(u_{n})<0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
K(u_{n})\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0
. Then,
\liminf J(u_{n})\geq d
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one has
J(u_{n})=\frac{1}{4}K(u_{n})+\frac{1}{4}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}
, implying

\liminf J(u_{n})=\frac{1}{4}\liminf\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}.

If
\liminf J(u_{n})=+\infty
, the result holds. Otherwise, up to a subsequence, one can assume that
\left(J(u_{n})\right)_{n}
and
\left(\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}\right)_{n}
converge. In particular,
(u_{n})
is bounded and we can assume that (up to a subsequence) it converges weakly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and strongly in
L^{4}(\Omega)
. Denote by
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{4}(\Omega)
the limit. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, using the Sobolev embedding and the fact that
K(u_{n})\leq 0
, one obtains

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\lesssim\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{4}\leq\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{8}.

As
u_{n}\neq 0
, this gives
\|u\|_{L^{4}}\gtrsim 1
, and
u\neq 0
as a result. One also has

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}\leq\lim\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{L^{4}}^{4},

yielding
K(u)\leq 0
. We split the end of the proof into two cases.

First, assume that
K(u)=0
. Then
\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}}=\lim\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}
, and hence,
(u_{n})_{n}
converges to
u
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. In particular, by definition of
d
, one has

\lim J(u_{n})=J(u)\geq d

so the proof is complete in that case.

Second, assume that
K(u)<0
, that is,

\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}}<\lim\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}.

As above, there exists
\lambda^{\ast}\in(0,1)
such that
K\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)=0
, and
d\leq J\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)
. The fact that
K\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)=0
gives

J\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left\|\lambda^{\ast}u\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}.

Writing
J(u_{n})=\frac{1}{2}K(u_{n})+\frac{1}{4}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
, one finds
\lim J\left(u_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
. This gives

d\leq J\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)=\left(\lambda^{\ast}\right)^{4}\lim J\left(u_{n}\right)<\lim J\left(u_{n}\right),

implying the result in the second case.
∎

Now, let

,

\left(\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta\right)\left(\phi\partial_{t}^{k}u\right)\in L^{2}(I\times\Omega),

\phi\partial_{t}^{k}u\in H^{2}(I\times\Omega)
. By Theorem
26
, applied in a smooth open subset of
I\times\Omega
containing
\supp\phi\times\Omega
, one obtains (
25
) for
\ell+1
. This proves (

).

Step 3: identification of the limit.

Here, we complete the proof of Proposition

by showing that

is a stationary solution of (

Theorem 27

(Corollary 3.2 of
[
12
]
)
.

Let

and let

,
(c_{i})_{i=1,2,3}

. Hence,

3.2
Convergence of a global solution

Here, we prove Theorem
5
. Let
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
be such that the solution
u
of (
Introduction
) with initial data
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)
exists on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In a companion paper
[
16
]
, we prove that there exists
C>0
such that

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,\quad t\geq 0.

(26)

By (
26
), the energy of
u
is bounded. Hence, as the energy is nonincreasing, there exists
E_{\infty}\leq E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)
such that

E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow+\infty}E_{\infty}.

The energy equality gives

E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)-E_{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t<\infty

and this allows us to use Proposition
22
. More precisely, let
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a time-sequence satisfying
T_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
. For all
T>0
, one has

\int_{T_{n}-T}^{T_{n}+T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

Using also (
26
), we see that all the assumptions of Proposition
22
are satisfied by the constant sequence
\left(u^{0}_{n},u^{1}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, there exist an increasing function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a stationary solution
w
of (
Introduction
), both depending on the sequence
(T_{n})
, such that for all
T>0
,

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left(\left\|u(T_{\phi(n)}+t)-w\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(T_{\phi(n)}+t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

In particular, this implies

J(w)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\left(u(T_{\phi(n)}),\partial_{t}u(T_{\phi(n)})\right)=E_{\infty}.

Now, we assume that

\text{there is an at most countable number of stationary solution }w\text{ such that }J(w)=E_{\infty}.

(27)

We show that this implies that
w
is, in fact, independent of
(T_{n})
. Assume by contradiction that there exist two sequences
(T_{n})
and
(T_{n}^{\prime})
such that for all
T>0
, one has

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left(\left\|u(T_{n}+t)-w\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(T_{n}+t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0

and

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left(\left\|u(T_{n}^{\prime}+t)-w^{\prime}\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(T_{n}^{\prime}+t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0

where
w
and
w^{\prime}
are two distinct stationary solutions of (
Introduction
). Fix
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}w\varphi\mathrm{d}x<\int_{\Omega}w^{\prime}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

For
t\geq 0
, set
\alpha(t)=\int_{\Omega}u(t,x)\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x
. The function
\alpha
is real and continuous. One has

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(T_{n})=\int_{\Omega}w\varphi\mathrm{d}x<\int_{\Omega}w^{\prime}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(T_{n}^{\prime})

so by the intermediate value theorem, for all
\ell\in\left[\int_{\Omega}w\varphi\mathrm{d}x,\int_{\Omega}w^{\prime}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\right]
, there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n}
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
and

\alpha(t_{n})\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\ell.

Hence, for all such
\ell
, there exists a stationary solution
w^{\prime\prime}
such that
J(w^{\prime\prime})=E_{\infty}
and

\ell=\int_{\Omega}w^{\prime\prime}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

This is a contradiction with (
27
).

Summarizing, we have proved that there exists a stationary solution
w
such that for all sequence
(T_{n})_{n}
such that
T_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, there exists a subsequence
(T_{\phi(n)})_{n}
such that for all
T>0
, one has

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left(\left\|u(T_{\phi(n)}+t)-w\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(T_{\phi(n)}+t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

A basic contradiction argument ends the proof of Theorem
5
.

4
Stabilisation of global solutions below the ground state energy

4.1
Case of a positive damping

In the case of a positive damping, it is possible to make a short proof of the stabilization of solutions of (
Introduction
) initiated in
\mathscr{K}^{+}
. That proof is based on the arguments of the proof of Proposition 2.5 of
[
12
]
and on Lemma
15
.

Theorem 28
.

Assume that
\gamma(x)\geq\alpha>0
on
\Omega
. Then for any
E_{0}\in[0,d)
, there exists
C>0
and
\lambda>0
such that for all
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
such that
E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in[0,E_{0}]
, if
u
is the solution of
(
Introduction
)
with initial data
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)
, then

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\lambda t},\quad t\geq 0.

Remark 29
.

Note that
C
and
\lambda
depend on
E_{0}
. The following result is false: there exist
C>0
and
\lambda>0
such that for
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
and
t\geq 0
, one has

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}.

(28)

Indeed, for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, consider
\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)=\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)Q,0\right)
and write
u_{n}
for the solution with initial data
\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)
. Recall that if
j(\lambda)=J(\lambda Q)
, then one has
j^{\prime}(\lambda)>0
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
j^{\prime}(\lambda)<0
for
\lambda>1
. In particular, this implies
K(u_{n}^{0})>0
and
\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
. Hence, if (
28
) is true, then there exists
T>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, one has

\left\|u_{n}(T)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|Q\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}.

By continuity of the source-to-solution operator (see Lemma
20
), one has

\left\|u_{n}(T)-Q\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0

and this is a contradiction.

Proof.

Fix
E_{0}\in[0,d)
, and
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
such that
E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\leq E_{0}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define

E_{\varepsilon}(t)=E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u(t,x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

Recall that as
K(u(t))\geq 0
, one has

E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad t\geq 0.

For
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, one finds

\varepsilon\left|\int_{\Omega}u(t,x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)

implying

\frac{1}{2}E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq\frac{3}{2}E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right),\quad t\geq 0.

(29)

Using the energy equality, for
\varepsilon>0
and
t\geq 0
, one obtains

E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega}(\varepsilon-\gamma)\left|\partial_{t}u(t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\gamma u(t)\partial_{t}u(t)\mathrm{d}x.

As
\gamma\geq\alpha
, this gives

E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\leq(\varepsilon-\alpha)\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\gamma u(t)\partial_{t}u(t)\mathrm{d}x.

Note that for
t\geq 0
, one has

J(u(t))\leq E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\leq E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\leq E_{0}<d

so by Lemma
15

(i)
, there exists
C>0
such that
K(u(t))\geq C\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}
for all
t\geq 0
. This gives

E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\leq(\varepsilon-\alpha)\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon C\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\gamma u(t)\partial_{t}u(t)\mathrm{d}x.

Writing

\varepsilon\left|\int_{\Omega}u(t,x)\partial_{t}u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

one finds

E_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)\leq(\varepsilon-\alpha+C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon})\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left(C^{\prime}\sqrt{\varepsilon}-C\right)\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}

for some
C^{\prime}>0
. Fix
T>0
. For
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, one has

E_{\varepsilon}(T)-E_{\varepsilon}(0)\lesssim-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t,

and using (
29
), one finds

E\left(u(T),\partial_{t}u(T)\right)-3E\left(u(0),\partial_{t}u(0)\right)\lesssim-\int_{0}^{T}E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

As the energy is decreasing, this gives

E\left(u(T),\partial_{t}u(T)\right)-3E\left(u(0),\partial_{t}u(0)\right)\lesssim-TE\left(u(T),\partial_{t}u(T)\right).

Choosing
T
sufficiently large, one obtains

E\left(u(T),\partial_{t}u(T)\right)\leq\mu E\left(u(0),\partial_{t}u(0)\right)

for some
\mu\in(0,1)
. As
\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
for all
t\geq 0
, we can iterate this process to get

E\left(u(nT),\partial_{t}u(nT)\right)\leq\mu^{n}E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Case of a damping satisfying the GCC

Here, we prove Theorem
4
, using Proposition
22
. We spit the proof into two steps.

Step 1: observability estimate and application of Proposition
22
.

To prove Theorem
4
, it suffices to show the following observability inequality: there exist
C>0
and
T>0
such that for all
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
with
E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\leq E_{0}
, the solution
u
of (
Introduction
) with initial data
\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)
satisfies

E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right)\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)\left|\partial_{t}u(t,x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Indeed, if that observability inequality holds, then using the energy equality, one obtains

E\left(u(T),\partial_{t}u(T)\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\right)E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right).

As
\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
for all
t\geq 0
, we can iterate this process to get

E\left(u(nT),\partial_{t}u(nT)\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\right)^{n}E\left(u^{0},u^{1}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

This proves that
t\mapsto E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)
decays exponentially. As
K(u(t))\geq 0
, one has

E\left(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and this gives the conclusion.

We prove the observability inequality by contradiction. We assume that there exists a sequence
(T_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, satisfying
T_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 1
, there exists
\left(u^{0}_{n},u^{1}_{n}\right)\in\mathscr{K}^{+}
such that
E\left(u^{0}_{n},u^{1}_{n}\right)\leq E_{0}
and

\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)\left|\partial_{t}u_{n}(t,x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t<\frac{1}{n}E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right).

(30)

For
n\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
, as
K(u_{n}(t))\geq 0
, one has

\left\|u_{n}(t)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim E\left(u_{n}(t),\partial_{t}u_{n}(t)\right)\leq E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)\leq E_{0}.

Write
T_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{T_{n}}{2}
. For all
T>0
, if
n\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large, then

\int_{T_{n}^{\prime}-T}^{T_{n}^{\prime}+T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\left|\partial_{t}u_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\left|\partial_{t}u_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

This gives

\int_{T_{n}^{\prime}-T}^{T_{n}^{\prime}+T}\int_{\Omega}\gamma\left|\partial_{t}u_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0,\quad T>0.

Hence, we can apply Proposition
22
: there exist an increasing function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a stationary solution
w
of (
Introduction
) such that

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left(\left\|u_{\phi(n)}(T_{\phi(n)}^{\prime}+t)-w\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}+\left\|\partial_{t}u_{\phi(n)}(T_{\phi(n)}^{\prime}+t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0,\quad T>0.

As the energy is nondecreasing, one has

J(w)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\left(u_{\phi(n)}(T_{\phi(n)}^{\prime}),\partial_{t}u_{\phi(n)}(T_{\phi(n)}^{\prime})\right)\leq E_{0}<d.

Hence,
w
is a stationary solution satisfying
J(w)\in[0,d)
: this gives
w=0
. Using the energy equality, one has

E\left(u_{n}(T_{n}^{\prime}),\partial_{t}u_{n}(T_{n}^{\prime})\right)\geq E\left(u_{n}(T_{n}),\partial_{t}u_{n}(T_{n})\right)=E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)-\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\gamma(x)\left|\partial_{t}u_{n}(t,x)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

so that (
30
) gives

E\left(u_{n}(T_{n}^{\prime}),\partial_{t}u_{n}(T_{n}^{\prime})\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right).

In particular, one obtains

E\left(u_{\phi(n)}^{0},u_{\phi(n)}^{1}\right)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, set
\alpha_{n}=\sqrt{E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)}
. In the next step, we will consider the equation scaled by
\alpha_{n}
to get a contradiction.

Step 2: the scaled equation.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
w_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
,
w_{n}
is the solution of

\square w_{n}+\beta w_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{3}

and one has

\left\|w_{n}(T_{n})\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{n}(T_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\left(\left\|u_{n}(T_{n})\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(T_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\gtrsim\frac{E\left(u_{n}(T_{n}),\partial_{t}u_{n}(T_{n})\right)}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}.

Using the energy equality and (
30
), one finds

\left\|w_{n}(T_{n})\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{n}(T_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\left(E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)-\frac{1}{n}E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)\right)=1-\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{1}{2},\quad n\geq 2.

(31)

Recall that
A
denotes the infinitesimal generator of the linear part of (
Introduction
), that
e^{tA}
is the associated semi-group, and set

W_{n}=\left(w_{n},\partial_{t}w_{n}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{n}=\left(0,\alpha_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{3}\right).

Using the Duhamel formula, we can write

\displaystyle W_{n}(T_{n})

\displaystyle=e^{T_{n}A}W_{n}(0)+\int_{0}^{T_{n}}e^{(T_{n}-s)A}F_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=e^{T_{n}A}W_{n}(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor T_{n}\rfloor-1}e^{(T_{n}-k)A}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}F_{n}(k+s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\lfloor T_{n}\rfloor}^{T_{n}}e^{(T_{n}-s)A}F_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s.

(32)

As
K(u_{n}(0))\geq 0
, one has

\left\|w_{n}(0)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{n}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}\left(\left\|u_{n}(0)\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(0)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\lesssim\frac{E\left(u_{n}(0),\partial_{t}u_{n}(0)\right)}{\alpha_{n}^{2}}=1.

Hence, the sequence
\left(W_{n}(0)\right)
is bounded, and linear stabilisation (Theorem
12
) gives

e^{T_{n}A}W_{n}(0)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0

in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. Next, write

\left\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}F_{n}(k+s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|F_{n}(k+\cdot)\right\|_{L^{1}((0,1),H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2})}=\left\|\alpha_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{3}\right\|_{L^{1}((k,k+1),L^{2})}.

A Sobolev embedding gives

\left\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}F_{n}(k+s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{3}((k,k+1),L^{6})}^{3}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\left\|\left(u_{n},\partial_{t}u_{n}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}((k,k+1),H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2})}^{3}.

Using
K(u_{n})\geq 0
and the fact that the energy is nonincreasing, one obtains

\left\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}F_{n}(k+s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{E\left(u_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{1}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\alpha_{n}}=\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}.

Hence linear stabilisation gives

\left\|e^{(T_{n}-k)A}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}F_{n}(k+s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim e^{\lambda(k-T_{n})}\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}

where
\lambda>0
is a constant, implying

\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor T_{n}\rfloor-1}e^{(T_{n}-k)A}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}F_{n}(k+s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor T_{n}\rfloor-1}e^{\lambda(k-T_{n})}\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}\lesssim\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}.

Similarly, one has

\left\|\int_{\lfloor T_{n}\rfloor}^{T_{n}}e^{(T_{n}-s)A}F_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{H_{0}^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}.

As
\alpha_{\phi(n)}\rightarrow 0
, coming back to (
32
), one finds

W_{\phi(n)}(T_{\phi(n)})\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0

in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, a contradiction with (
31
). This completes the proof of Theorem

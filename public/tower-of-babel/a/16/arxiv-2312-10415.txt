Remarks on a groupoid approach to the Wodzicki residue and the Kontsevich-Vishik trace
Omar Mohsen

Abstract
Building on the work of Debord and Skandalis, van Erp and Yuncken introduced a groupoid approach to pseudo-differential operators which has various advantages over the classical approach using Hörmander’s symbolic calculus. In a recent work by Couchet and Yuncken, they showed that for pseudo-differential operators of order − dim ( M ) -\dim(M) acting on a smooth manifold M M the Wodzicki residue can be naturally obtained from van Erp and Yuncken’s approach. In this short note, we extend their work to pseudo-differential operators of any order. We also give a description of the Kontsevich-Vishik trace.



Introduction

In the 80s, Connes
[
Con94a
]
constructed the tangent groupoid and gave a short proof of Atiyah-Singer index theorem. In
[
DS14a
]
,
Debord and Skandalis noticed that the tangent groupoid admits a natural
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
action which has the crucial property that the crossed product of a natural ideal of the
C^{*}
-algebra of the
tangent groupoid is Morita equivalent to the
C^{*}
-algebra of classical pseudo-differential operators of order
0
. This essentially says that up to Morita equivalent classical pseudo-differential operators are distributions on the tangent groupoid.
This idea was taken further by van Erp and Yuncken who gave an elegant and simple criterion for distributions on the tangent groupoid to come from pseudo-differential operators.
Furthermore, they showed that one can relatively easily prove the standard properties of a classical pseudo-differential calculus immediately from this criterion.
This approach also has the advantage of being quite easy to generalise to contexts where using local coordinates and symbolic calculus becomes quite complicated, for example for filtered manifolds (also called Carnot manifolds)
[
DH20a
,
AMY22a
]
.

Some notions like the principal symbol are quite direct to obtain from van Erp and Yuncken’s approach. On the other hand, this is less clear with the Wodzicki residue
[
Wod87a
,
Gui85a
]
and the Kontsevich-Vishik trace
[
KV95a
]
.
Couchet and Yuncken
[
CY23a
]
recently gave a simple description of the Wodzicki residue for operators of order
-\dim(M)
. In this article, we extend their formula so that it holds for pseudo-differential operators of any order.
We also derive a closely related formula which we show agrees with the Kontsevich-Vishik trace.

This approach to the Wodzicki residue and the Kontsevich-Vishik trace generalises directly to the case of filtered manifolds, which has been considered by Dave and Haller
[
DH20a
]
and Ponge
[
Pon08a
,
Pon07a
]
.
We finally remark that the approach given here together with the groupoid constructed by the author
[
Moh22a
]
,
might shed some light on how to construct a Wodzicki residue and a Kontsevich-Vishik trace for the more general case of filtered manifolds, where one is given vector fields
satisfying Hörmander’s condition but no longer supposes that their rank is locally constant.

1
The groupoid approach to classical pseudo-differential calculus

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. We denote by

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}M=M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\cup TM\times\{0\}

the tangent groupoid defined by Connes
[
Con94a
]
. The space
\mathbb{T}M
is naturally a Lie groupoid. In fact, there is a unique smooth structure on
\mathbb{T}M
such that the following maps are smooth:

•

The map

\displaystyle\pi:\mathbb{T}M\to M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times},\quad\pi(y,x,t)=(y,x,t),\quad\pi(x,X,0)=(x,x,0),

where
x\in M
,
X\in T_{x}M
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
.

•

The map

\displaystyle\dnc(f):\mathbb{T}M\to\mathbb{R},\quad\dnc(f)(y,x,t)=\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{t},\quad\dnc(f)(x,X,0)=df_{x}(X),

where
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is any smooth function.

The space
\mathbb{T}M
is a groupoid whose space of objects is
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Its source and range map are given by

\displaystyle s(y,x,t)=(x,t),\quad s(x,X,0)=(x,0),

\displaystyle r(y,x,t)=(y,t),\quad r(x,X,0)=(x,0),

The inverse and the product are defined by

\displaystyle(y,x,t)^{-1}=(x,y,t),\quad(x,X,t)^{-1}=(x,-X,0)

\displaystyle(z,y,t)\cdot(y,x,t)=(z,x,t),\quad(x,X,0)\cdot(x,Y,0)=(x,X+Y,0).

One can quite easily see that the two vector bundles

|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\ker(ds)\otimes|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\ker(dr)\simeq|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}T(\mathbb{T}M)

are canonically isomorphic. We denote this vector bundle by
\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
The space
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
of compactly supported smooth sections of
\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}}
is naturally a
*
-algebra.
We refer the reader to the article of Debord and Lescure
[
DL10a
]
for an introduction to Lie groupoids and their convolution algebras, as well as the tangent groupoid.

The manifold
\mathbb{T}M
is naturally equipped with a smooth
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
-action called the Debord-Skandalis action
[
DS14a
]
given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}:\mathbb{T}M\to\mathbb{T}M,\quad\alpha_{\lambda}(y,x,t)=(y,x,\lambda^{-1}t),\quad\alpha_{\lambda}(x,X,0)=(x,\lambda X,0),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}.

One easily sees that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
,
\alpha_{\lambda}
is a groupoid automorphism. Therefore,
\alpha_{\lambda}
acts by algebra automorphism on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
It also acts on
C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
, which denotes the topological dual of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

Definition 1.1

(van Erp and Yuncken
[
EY19a
]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{C}
. The space
\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
denotes the subspace of
u\in C^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
which satisfy the following:

1.

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{WF}(u)\cap\ker(ds)^{\perp}=\mathrm{WF}(u)\cap\ker(dr)^{\perp}=\emptyset.

(1)

2.

The maps
r:\supp(u)\to M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
s:\supp(u)\to M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
are proper.

3.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}(\lambda):=\lambda^{-k}\alpha_{\lambda}(u)-u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})

(2)

Distributions satisfying (
1
) are called admissible in
[
LMV17a
]
. They are precisely the distributions for which convolution law coming from the Lie groupoid structure is well-defined, i.e.,
if
u
and
v
are admissible, then one can define
u\ast v
which is again admissible.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{T}M\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the natural projection. The distribution
u
disintegrates along the fibers of
\pi
, i.e., we can define the pullback of
u

on each fiber of
\pi
, see
[
Hör90a
, Theorem 8.2.4]
. We denote by
u_{t}
the restriction of
u
to the fiber of
\pi
at
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Therefore,
u_{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{-\infty}(M\times M,|\Lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}T(M\times M))
for
t>0
and

u_{0}\in C^{-\infty}(TM,|\Lambda|^{1}TM)
.

Theorem 1.2

(van Erp and Yuncken
[
EY19a
]
)
.

If
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
u\in\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
, then
u_{1}
is the kernel of a properly supported classical pseudo-differential operator of order
k
. Conversely, the kernel of any
properly supported classical pseudo-differential operator of order
k
can be written as
u_{1}
for some
u\in\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

The elegance of
Theorem

1.2
is that the principal cosymbol of
u_{1}
is
u_{0}
, thus
u
gives a deformation between the pseudo-differential operator
u_{1}
and its cosymbol
u_{0}
, see
[
EY19a
, Section 6]
.

Remark 1.3
.

The existence
u\in\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
starting from a properly supported classical pseudo-differential operator is constructive, see
[
EY19a
, Section 11]
.
In particular if one has a family of properly supported classical pseudo-differential operators which depends holomorphically on some parameter,
then one can construct a corresponding family of elements in
\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
which also depends holomorphically on the same parameter.

The following proposition shows that
\Phi_{u}
is a jointly smooth function in both
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
and
\mathbb{T}M
.

Proposition 1.4

(
[
EY19a
, Lemma 21]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
u\in\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
. The following map is smooth

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\to C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}}),\quad\lambda\to\Phi_{u}(\lambda).

2
The Wodzicki residue and the Kontsevich-Vishik trace

Let
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
u\in\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
, since
\Phi_{u}(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
, we can take its restriction
on the space of objects,
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, to obtain a smooth section of
|\Lambda|^{1}TM
over
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We denote this section by
\Phi_{u}(\lambda,x,t)
. It satisfies the cocycle relation

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},x,t)=\lambda_{2}^{-k-\dim(M)}\Phi_{u}(\lambda_{1},x,t\lambda_{2})+\Phi_{u}(\lambda_{2},x,t).

This cocycle relation follows immediately from the relation

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})=\lambda_{2}^{-k}\alpha_{\lambda_{2}}(\Phi_{u}(\lambda_{1}))+\Phi_{u}(\lambda).

The factor
\lambda_{2}^{-\dim(M)}
comes the action of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
on densities, see for example
[
Yi21a
, Proposition 3.2]
for more details.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space,
k\in\mathbb{C}
,
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to V
a smooth function which satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},t)=\lambda_{2}^{-k}\phi(\lambda_{1},t\lambda_{2})+\phi(\lambda_{2},t),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}.

(
C_{k}
)

Then, there exists a smooth map
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to V
and
c\in V
such that

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda,t)=\lambda^{-k}\psi(\lambda t)-\psi(t)+t^{k}\log(\lambda)c,\quad\forall(\lambda,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

(3)

Furthermore, if

•

k\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then
c=0
and
\psi
is unique.

•

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then
c
is unique and is given by

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{k!\log(\lambda)}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\Bigr|_{t=0}\phi(\lambda,t),

(4)

which we show is independent of
\lambda
.

Remark 2.2
.

Before we give the proof let us explain the motivation behind
2.1
.
If we apply
2.1
to
\Phi_{u}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
with
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
, then there is an obvious choice for
\psi
which is simply the restriction of
u
itself on the space of objects, i.e., the diagonal.
Therefore,
2.1
gives a way to extend the definition of the restriction to the diagonal beyond the case
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
. This is precisely the Kontsevich-Vishik trace.

Proof.

The proof is divided into a series of straightforward to check steps.

1.

If
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to V
is any smooth function, then
(\lambda,t)\mapsto\lambda^{-k}\psi(\lambda t)-\psi(t)
satisfies
(C_{k})
.

2.

Suppose
\Re(k)<0
. If
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to V
is any smooth function that satisfies
(C_{k})
, and
\phi(\frac{1}{2},t)=0
for all
t\geq 0
, then
\phi=0
. To see this, we apply
(C_{k})
twice to obtain

\displaystyle\phi\left(\frac{\lambda}{2},t\right)=\lambda^{-k}\phi\left(\frac{1}{2},t\lambda\right)+\phi(\lambda,t)=\phi(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle\phi\left(\frac{\lambda}{2},t\right)=2^{k}\phi\left(\lambda,\frac{t}{2}\right)+\phi\left(\frac{1}{2},t\right)=2^{k}\phi\left(\lambda,\frac{t}{2}\right).

Therefore,

\phi(\lambda,t)=2^{k}\phi\left(\lambda,\frac{t}{2}\right)

which by recurrence implies that

\phi(\lambda,t)=2^{kn}\phi\left(\lambda,\frac{t}{2^{n}}\right).

Taking the limit as
n\to+\infty
, we conclude.

3.

Suppose
\Re(k)<0
. We define

\displaystyle\psi(t):=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{t}{2^{j}}\right)2^{jk}

(5)

It is straightforward to see that
\psi
is smooth and that

\phi\left(\frac{1}{2},t\right)=2^{k}\psi\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)-\psi\left(t\right).

Therefore, the function

(\lambda,t)\mapsto\phi(\lambda,t)-\lambda^{-k}\psi(\lambda t)-\psi(t)

satisfies
(C_{k})
and vanishes when
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda,t)=\lambda^{-k}\psi(\lambda t)-\psi(t).

This finishes the proof of the existence of
\psi
and
c
in the case
\Re(k)<0
.

4.

If
\phi
satisfies
(C_{k})
and
\phi(\lambda,0)=0
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
, then
t^{-1}\phi
defines a smooth function which satisfies
(C_{k-1})
. Furthermore, if
t^{-1}\phi
has the form (
3
),
then
\phi
also has the same form.

5.

If
\phi
satisfies
(C_{0})
, then it is clear that
\phi(\lambda,0)=c\log(\lambda)
for some
c\in V
.

6.

If
\phi
satisfies
(C_{k})
and
k\neq 0
, then there exists a unique
v\in V
such that
\phi(\lambda,0)=(\lambda^{-k}-1)v
. To see this, first suppose
\Re(k)\neq 0
, then by applying
C_{k}
twice, we have

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},0)=\lambda_{2}^{-k}\phi(\lambda_{1},0)+\phi(\lambda_{2},0)

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},0)=\lambda_{1}^{-k}\phi(\lambda_{2},0)+\phi(\lambda_{1},0)

By taking the difference, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(\lambda_{1},0)}{\lambda_{1}^{-k}-1}=\frac{\phi(\lambda_{2},0)}{\lambda_{2}^{-k}-1}.

The result follows. If
\Re(k)=0
, then there are only discrete values of
\lambda
for which
\lambda^{-k}=1
. We apply the above argument for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
avoiding such values together with continuity of
\phi
to conclude.

7.

The proof of the existence of
\psi
and
c
is now a straightforward recursive argument combining Steps
4,5,6
together with the case
\Re(k)<0
which we already proved.

8.

Suppose
k\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We will show that
c=0
and
\psi
is unique. We have
c=0
because (
3
) implies that
t^{k}\log(\lambda)c
is smooth at
t=0
.
By taking the difference of two
\psi
’s which satisfy (
3
), without loss of generality, we can suppose that

\lambda^{-k}\psi(\lambda t)-\psi(t)=0.

This implies that

\psi(t)=t^{k}\psi(1)

The left-hand side is smooth at
t=0
while the right-hand side is smooth only if
\psi(1)=0
. This shows that
\psi=0
.

9.

If
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we will show that
c
is unique. By taking the difference of two
\psi
and
c
which satisfy (
3
), without loss of generality, we can suppose that

\lambda^{-k}\psi(\lambda t)-\psi(t)=-t^{k}\log(\lambda)c.

This implies that

\psi(t)=t^{k}\psi(1)-t^{k}\log(t)c.

This is smooth at
t=0
only if
c=0
.

10.

It remains to show that if
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then
c
is given by (
4
). This follows by noticing that if
\phi
satisfies
(C_{k})
, then
\frac{d}{dt}\phi
satisfies
(C_{k-1})
. We then apply Step 5.∎

We now apply
2.1
to
\Phi_{u}(\lambda,x,t)
.

1.

If
\Re(k)\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-\dim(M)}
, we obtain a smooth section
\Psi_{u}(x,t)
of
|\Lambda|^{1}TM
over
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{u}(\lambda,x,t)=\lambda^{-k-\dim(M)}\Psi_{u}(x,\lambda t)-\Psi_{u}(x,t).

2.

If
\Re(k)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-\dim(M)}
, we obtain a smooth section of
|\Lambda|^{1}TM
over
M
given by

\displaystyle C_{u}(x):=\frac{1}{l!\log(\lambda)}\frac{d^{l}}{dt^{l}}\Bigr|_{t=0}\Phi_{u}(\lambda,x,t),

where
l=k+\dim(M)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{\Psi}^{k}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

1.

If
\Re(k)\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-\dim(M)}
, then the Kontsevich-Vishik density of
u_{1}
at
x\in M
is equal to
\Psi_{u}(x,1)
. In particular, if
M
is compact, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{TR}(u_{1})=\int_{M}\Psi_{u}(x,1)

2.

If
\Re(k)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-\dim(M)}
, then the Wodzicki density of
u_{1}
at
x\in M
is equal to
C_{u}(x)
. In particular, if
M
is compact, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}(u_{1})=\int_{M}C_{u}(x)

Proof.

If
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M,\Omega^{\frac{1}{2}})
, then by
2.2
, we know that
\Psi_{u}(x,1)
agrees with the restriction to the diagonal of
u_{1}
, which is by definition the Kontsevich-Vishik density of
u_{1}
at
x\in M
.
Since the Kontsevich-Vishik trace is a holomorphic extension of the trace for trace class operators whose residue is the Wodzicki density, see for example
[
Vas01a
, Théorème 2.3]
.
Theorem

2.3
follows from
1.3
and the following Proposition.∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space,
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
an open subset,
\phi:U\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to V
a smooth function which is holomorphic in
z\in U
such that
\phi(z,\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies
(C_{z})
for every
z\in U
.

•

For every
z\in U\backslash\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
\psi(z,t)
be the unique map obtained from
2.1
applied to
\phi(z,\cdot,\cdot)
.

•

For every
z\in U\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
c(z)
be the element obtained from (
4
) applied to
\phi(z,\cdot,\cdot)
.

Then, the map
\psi(z,t)
is smooth in
t
and meromorphic in
z
. It has simple poles at
z\in U\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
whose residue is equal to
-c(z)
.

Proof.

By (
5
),
\psi
is clearly holomorphic on
\{z\in U:\Re(z)<0\}
. We will show that it extends to
\{z\in U:\Re(z)<1\}
with a simple pole at
z=0
.
For
z\neq 0
, let
v(z)
be the unique element of
V
such that
\phi(z,\lambda,0)=(\lambda^{-z}-1)v(z)
. It is clear that
v(z)
is holomorphic at
z\neq 0
. It has a simple pole at
z=0
which is equal to

\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z\phi(z,\lambda,0)}{\lambda^{-z}-1}=-\frac{\phi(0,\lambda,0)}{\log(\lambda)}=-c(0).

We write

\phi(z,\lambda,t)=t\frac{\phi(z,\lambda,t)-\phi(z,\lambda,0)}{t}+\phi(z,\lambda,0).

The function
t^{-1}(\phi(z,\lambda,t)-\phi(z,\lambda,0))
is holomorphic in
z
and satisfies
(C_{z-1})
.
Let
\tilde{\psi}(z,t)
be the map obtained from (
5
) applied to
t^{-1}(\phi(z,\lambda,t)-\phi(z,\lambda,0))
. The function
\tilde{\psi}(z,t)
is clearly holomorphic in
\{z\in U:\Re(z)<1\}
.
By uniqueness of
\psi
in
2.1
for
k\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we deduce that

\psi(z,t)=t\tilde{\psi}(z,t)+v(z).

Hence,
\psi(z,t)
is meromorphic on
\{z\in U:\Re(z)<1\}
with a simple pole at
z=1
whose residue is
-c(0)
.
The case of
\{z\in U:\Re(z)<n\}
is done by induction and is left to the reader.

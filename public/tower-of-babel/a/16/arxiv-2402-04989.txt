Abstract
. We introduce two families of inequalities. Large ensemble decoupling is connected to the continuous restriction phenomenon. Tight decoupling is connected to the discrete Restriction conjecture for the sphere. Our investigation opens new grounds and answers some questions.



1.
The large ensemble problem

Given
S\subset[0,1]^{2}
,
F:S\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, the restriction to the two dimensional paraboloid
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is

E_{S}F(x)=\int_{S}F(\xi)e((\xi,|\xi|^{2})\cdot x)d\xi.

We write
EF
for
E_{[0,1]^{2}}F
, and we denote by
B_{R}
an arbitrary ball/cube of radius/side length
R
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. For context, we recall the main result in
[
4
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}
denote the
\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}
-squares
q
in
[0,1]^{2}
of the form
[\frac{j}{\sqrt{R}},\frac{j+1}{\sqrt{R}}]\times[\frac{i}{\sqrt{R}},\frac{i+1}{\sqrt{R}}]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
2\leq p\leq 4
we have

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(\sum_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}}\|E_{q}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{2})^{1/2}.

To be fully precise,
L^{p}(B_{R})
on the right hand side should be
L^{p}(w_{B_{R}})
, where
w_{B_{R}}
is a smooth approximation of
1_{B_{R}}
. To keep notation simple, this will be ignored, as it has no impact on our arguments.
This inequality is known as (canonical)
l^{2}
decoupling. It immediately implies the superficially weaker inequality called (canonical)
l^{p}
decoupling

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}(\sum_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}}\|E_{q}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p})^{1/p}.

There are similar decouplings for other manifolds, but we only focus on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
here. The phenomenon we are about to describe is already complex in this case.

The scale
1/\sqrt{R}
of the squares
q
has a special relation to the scale
R
of the balls
B_{R}
. It allows the crucial use of parabolic rescaling in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For this reason, the squares
q
(or their lifts to
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
) are sometimes called
canonical
caps. One notable variant that has been considered is called small cap decoupling. This time
[0,1]^{2}
is partitioned into smaller
\frac{1}{R^{\alpha}}
-squares,
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1
, called
small caps
. See
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
.

Here, we go in the opposite direction and replace the squares
q
with larger sets. Considering just larger squares does not lead to anything new or interesting. Instead, we will allow arbitrary unions of such squares. Examples include thin rectangles and disconnected sets.

Definition 1.2
.

A disjoint union of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}
-squares
q\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}
will be called a large ensemble.

We kick-start our investigation with the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3

(Large ensemble decoupling for the paraboloid)
.

Let
N=O(R)
. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{N}
be a partition of
[0,1]^{2}
into large ensembles. Then for
2\leq p\leq 3
we have

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{2})^{1/2}.

(1)

The case
p=2
follows from
L^{2}
orthogonality. As we will see in the next section, the case
p=3
implies the full Restriction conjecture for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. The same computations will show why the restriction
p\leq 3
is needed. No interpolation is available in this general setting.

A well known consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the fact that for each
2\leq p\leq 4
and each exponential sum
{\mathcal{E}}(x)=\sum_{q}a_{q}e(\xi_{q}\cdot x)
associated with
1/\sqrt{R}
-separated points
\xi_{q}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
we have

\|{\mathcal{E}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\approx\|a_{q}\|_{l^{2}}|B_{R}|^{1/p}.

Here and in the following we use the notation
\approx
to denote an equality modulo
R^{\epsilon}
multiplicative losses, for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
. This immediately shows that when
EF
is such an exponential sum, (
1
) holds with
\lesssim
replaced with
\approx
, in the larger range
2\leq p\leq 4
, and for each
S_{i}
.

The reason we anticipate the validity of the conjecture for arbitrary
F
is because it is implied by the following reverse square function estimate, widely believed to be true for
2\leq p\leq 3

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|(\sum_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}}|E_{q}F|^{2})^{1/2})\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}.

(2)

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume (
2
) is true. Then Conjecture
1.3
holds.

Proof.

We first combine (
2
) with Minkowski’s inequality to get

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|(\sum_{q\in S_{i}}|E_{q}F|^{2})^{1/2}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{R})})^{1/2}.

Note that

\|(\sum_{q\in S_{i}}|E_{q}F|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\approx\|(\sum_{q\in S_{i}}|\eta_{R}E_{q}F|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

where
\eta_{R}
is a smooth approximation of
1_{B_{R}}
, with compactly supported Fourier transform. The Fourier transforms of the functions
\eta_{R}E_{q}F
are supported on a finitely overlapping family of
1/\sqrt{R}
-cubes near
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

To end, we use Rubio de Francia’s square function estimate for this collection of cubes

\|(\sum_{q\in S_{i}}|\eta_{R}E_{q}F|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\lesssim\|\sum_{q\in S_{i}}\eta_{R}E_{q}F\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\approx\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}.

∎

In Section
2
we prove that Conjecture
1.3
implies the Restriction conjecture for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Since proving it for arbitrary partitions is out of reach, in Section
3
we introduce methods aimed to solve a particular case of interest.

2.
Conjecture
1.3
implies the Restriction Conjecture

Here is the main result in this section. A union of
M
canonical squares
q
is called an
M
-set.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
0<\gamma\leq 1
. Assume that the following large ensemble
l^{p}
decoupling holds for some
p\in[2,4]
,
M\sim R^{1-\gamma}
(and all
\epsilon>0
, all
R\gtrsim 1
)

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{R/M}\|E_{P_{j}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p},

(3)

for all
F
and all partitions
{\mathcal{P}}
of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}
into
M
-sets
P_{j}
.
Then for each
\|F\|_{\infty}\lesssim R
we have

\int_{B_{\sqrt{R}}}|EF|^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(R^{\frac{3+p}{2}}+R^{3}(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}).

Before we prove the theorem, we reveal its significance with two immediate consequences.

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume (
3
) holds true for
p=3
and all
\gamma
arbitrarily close to
0
. Then the Restriction conjecture for the paraboloid holds: for each
F
we have

\int_{B_{\sqrt{R}}}|EF|^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\|F\|_{\infty}^{3}.

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume (
3
) holds true for some
p\in(3,4]
and
M\sim R^{\frac{4-p}{p-2}}
. Then the Restriction conjecture for the paraboloid holds in
L^{p}
: for each
F
we have

\int_{B_{\sqrt{R}}}|EF|^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\|F\|_{\infty}^{p}.

Moreover, if
\Delta>0
then (
3
) is false for
p\in(3,4]
,
M\sim R^{\frac{4-p}{p-2}+\Delta}
and some partition into
M
-sets.

To see the last part of Corollary
2.3
, note that if (
3
) held for some
M\sim R^{\frac{4-p}{p-2}+\Delta}
, then Theorem
2.1
would imply the false inequality (use
F=1_{[0,1]^{2}}
)

\int_{B_{\sqrt{R}}}|EF|^{p}\lesssim R^{-\beta}\|F\|_{\infty}^{p},

for some
\beta>0
.

The next two lemmas establish average
l^{2}
and
l^{4}
estimates, via elementary expansion and counting arguments. These will later be interpolated to get
l^{3}
estimates. Such pointwise estimates are then integrated on
B_{R}
to get
L^{3}
estimates. The idea is that averaging over partitions produces square root cancellation. When combined with the conjectured decoupling into large ensembles, this leads to restriction estimates as described above.

In the following, sums are taken over all partitions
{\mathcal{P}}
of
\{1,\ldots,R\}
into sets
P_{j}
with uniform size
M\leq R
. Partitions are always considered unordered.

Lemma 2.4
.

For each
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
1\leq i\leq R

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{M}{R}|\sum_{i}a_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}.

Proof.

We have

\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{2}=\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i_{1}\not=i_{2}}\#{\mathcal{P}}(i_{1},i_{2})\overline{a_{i_{1}}}a_{i_{2}}

where
{\mathcal{P}}(i_{1},i_{2})
are the special partitions that put
{i_{1}},{i_{2}}
into the same cell. Note that we have

\frac{1}{(R/M)!}{R\choose M}{R-M\choose M}\ldots{2M\choose M}

partitions
{\mathcal{P}}
. Indeed, we first select the first cell, which can be done in
R\choose M
ways. The second to last cell can be selected in
2M\choose M
ways. We need to divide with
(R/M)!
, as the partition is not ordered.

The number of partitions
{\mathcal{P}}(i_{1},i_{2})
is

\frac{1}{(\frac{R}{M}-1)!}{R-2\choose M}{R-M-2\choose M}\ldots{2M-2\choose M}.

We select the first
\frac{R}{M}-1
cells from
[1,R]\setminus\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
, and put
i_{1},i_{2}
in the small cell left in the end. The number of selected cells needs to be divided by the number of permutations. Note that

\#{\mathcal{P}}(i_{1},i_{2})=\#{\mathcal{P}}\times\frac{R}{M}\frac{M(M-1)}{R(R-1)}\sim\#{\mathcal{P}}M/R.

We conclude that

\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{2}=

\#{\mathcal{P}}(\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}+\frac{M-1}{R-1}\sum_{i_{1}\not=i_{2}}\overline{a_{i_{1}}}a_{i_{2}})=\#{\mathcal{P}}(\frac{R-M}{R-1}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}+\frac{M-1}{R-1}|\sum_{i}a_{i}|^{2}).

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For each
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\;P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{4}\lesssim

\frac{M^{3}}{R^{3}}|\sum_{i}a_{i}|^{4}+\frac{M^{2}}{R^{2}}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}|\sum_{i}a_{i}|^{2}+\frac{M}{R}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{3}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|+\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{4}.

Proof.

The expansion of the term on the left will contain four types of quadruple products.

First, there are the terms
a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}
with
i_{1}\not=i_{2}\not=i_{3}\not=i_{4}
. Reasoning as in the previous lemma, each such term appears roughly
M^{3}\#{\mathcal{P}}/R^{3}
many times in the summation, as this is the number of partitions that place the four indices in the same cell.

Second, there are the terms of the form
a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{1}}}a_{i_{2}}\overline{a_{i_{3}}}
(and their permutations) with
i_{1}\not=i_{2}\not=i_{3}
. These collect all products where exactly three of the four indices are distinct. Each such term appears roughly
M^{2}\#{\mathcal{P}}/R^{2}
many times.

Third, we will have products containing two distinct indices, of the form
a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{1}}}a_{i_{2}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}
,
a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{1}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}
and their permutations. Each of those appear roughly
M\#{\mathcal{P}}/R
many times.

Finally, each term
|a_{i}|^{4}
appears exactly
\#{\mathcal{P}}
many times.

The contributions from the first category is dominated by (we add the last term to “complete the square”)

\lesssim\frac{M^{3}\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R^{3}}(|\sum_{i}a_{i}|^{4}+|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{\text{not all four distinct}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|).

We also have the following estimate for the term we added

\frac{M^{3}\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R^{3}}|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{\text{not all four distinct}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|\leq

\frac{M^{3}\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R^{3}}(|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{\text{three distinct indices}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|+|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{\text{two distinct indices}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|+\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{4}).

These contributions are absorbed by the remaining three terms, as the exponent three in the coefficient
(M/R)^{3}
here is larger than those in the remaining cases.

Indeed, the contribution from the second case is

\lesssim\frac{M^{2}\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R^{2}}|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{\text{three distinct indices}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|.

Let us analyze the case
i_{1}=i_{2}
, the other ones are similar. Then, completing the square again

\frac{M^{2}\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R^{2}}|\sum_{i_{1},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{{i_{1}\not=i_{3}\not=i_{4}}}}|a_{i_{1}}|^{2}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|\leq\frac{M^{2}\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R^{2}}(\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}|\sum_{i}a_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\atop{\text{not all three distinct}}}|a_{i_{1}}|^{2}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}).

As before, the term we added is absorbed by the contributions of the remaining two cases.

Indeed, the contribution from the third case is

\frac{M\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R}|\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\atop{\text{only two distinct indices}}}a_{i_{1}}\overline{a_{i_{2}}}a_{i_{3}}\overline{a_{i_{4}}}|.

This is dominated by

\frac{M\#{\mathcal{P}}}{R}((\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2})^{2}+\sum_{i_{1}}|a_{i_{1}}|^{3}\sum_{i_{2}}|a_{i_{2}}|).

The second term dominates because of Cauchy–Schwarz.
The contribution from the fourth case is

\#{\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{4}.

∎

Next, we interpolate.

Corollary 2.6
.

Assume
|a_{i}|\lesssim 1
and let
L=\sum_{i=1}^{R}|a_{i}|
,
S=|\sum_{i=1}^{R}a_{i}|
.
Then for
2\leq p\leq 4

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\;P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{p}\lesssim(\frac{M}{R})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}L^{p/2}+L+(\frac{M}{R})^{p/2}S^{p}.

Proof.

Recall that

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{M}{R}S^{2}+L

and

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\;P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{4}\lesssim\frac{M^{3}}{R^{3}}S^{4}+\frac{M^{2}}{R^{2}}LS^{2}+\frac{M}{R}L^{2}+L.

We combine these with the inequality
\|\cdot\|_{l^{p}}^{p}\leq\|\cdot\|_{l^{2}}^{4-p}\|\cdot\|_{l^{4}}^{2p-4}
.

Case 1. If
L\geq\frac{M}{R}S^{2}
then we get

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\;P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{p}\lesssim L^{2-\frac{p}{2}}(\frac{M}{R}L^{2}+L)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\lesssim(\frac{M}{R})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}L^{p/2}+L.

Case 2. If
L\leq\frac{M}{R}S^{2}
then the term
\|\cdot\|_{l^{4}}^{4}
is dominated by
\frac{M^{3}}{R^{3}}S^{4}+L
. Since
S\leq L
and
M\leq R
, we must have that
L\geq 1
. Thus
L\leq L^{2}\leq\frac{M^{2}}{R^{2}}S^{4}
, and we may further dominate
\|\cdot\|_{l^{4}}^{4}
by
\frac{M^{2}}{R^{2}}S^{4}
.
We get

\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{\;P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}|\sum_{i\in P_{j}}a_{i}|^{p}\lesssim(\frac{M}{R}S^{2})^{2-\frac{p}{2}}(\frac{M^{2}}{R^{2}}S^{4})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\lesssim(\frac{M}{R})^{p/2}S^{p}.

∎

We now verify Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

The first part of the argument follows the lines of
[
9
]
.
We may assume
B_{\sqrt{R}}
is centered at the origin. Due to the smoothing effect of the convolution with the Fourier transform of the characteristic function of this ball, we may assume
F
is constant on each
q
. For such
F
, we will in fact prove the superficially stronger inequality on the larger ball
B_{R}
centered at the origin

\int_{B_{R}}|EF|^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(R^{\frac{3+p}{2}}+R^{3}(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}).

On
B_{R}
, each
E_{q_{i}}F
is a single wave packet
\Phi_{i}
that is spatially localized near a
(R,R^{1/2},R^{1/2})
-tube
T_{i}
through the origin, and has
L^{\infty}
norm
\lesssim 1
. We may write this as

E_{q_{i}}F(x)=\Phi_{i}(x)1_{R^{\delta}T_{i}}(x)+O_{\delta}(R^{-1000}),\;\;x\in B_{R},

for
\delta
as small as we wish. The first key inequality we use is the well-known bush estimate

\|\sum_{i=1}^{R}1_{R^{\delta}T_{i}}\|_{r}\lesssim R^{1+\frac{3}{2r}+O(\delta)}

valid for
r\geq 3/2
. This follows from an easy computation. It shows that the main contribution to the integral comes from the core
B_{\sqrt{R}}
where all tubes intersect. This justifies the fact that moving integration from this smaller ball to
B_{R}
is not lossy.

We average (
3
) over all partitions
{\mathcal{P}}
into
M
-sets to get

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\leq C_{R}(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}^{R/M}\|\sum_{q_{i}\in P_{j}}E_{q_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p},

(4)

with
C_{R}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}
. Writing

a_{i}(x)=\Phi_{i}(x)1_{R^{\delta}T_{i}}(x)
, we have

(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}^{R/M}|\sum_{q_{i}\in P_{j}}E_{q_{i}}F(x)|^{p}=(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}^{R/M}|\sum_{q_{i}\in P_{j}}a_{i}(x)|^{p}+O_{\delta}(R^{-100}).

Write

S(x)=|\sum_{i}a_{i}(x)|=|EF(x)|+O_{\delta}(R^{-100})

(5)

and

L(x)=\#\{i:\;x\in 1_{R^{\delta}T_{i}}\}.

Corollary
2.6
implies that

(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{P_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}}^{R/M}|\sum_{q_{i}\in P_{j}}a_{i}(x)|^{p}\lesssim L(x)^{p/2}+(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}L(x)+\frac{M}{R}S(x)^{3}.

We integrate this over
B_{R}
and combine with (
4
) and (
5
) to conclude that

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\lesssim C_{R}(\int_{B_{R}}L^{p/2}+(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int L+\frac{M}{R}\|EF\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{3})+O_{\delta}(R^{-10}).

If
M\ll R/C_{R}
this leads to

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\lesssim C_{R}(\int_{B_{R}}L^{p/2}+(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{B_{R}}L)+O_{\delta}(R^{-10}).

Using the bush estimate and the trivial

\int_{B_{R}}L\lesssim R^{3+O(\delta)}
, we get

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(R^{\frac{3+p}{2}}+R^{3}(\frac{R}{M})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}),

by choosing
\delta
small.
∎

The conclusion of Corollary
2.2
remains true if we average over a more restricted collection of partitions of the following form. We fix
M
collections
S_{k}=S_{k}(M)
each consisting of
R/M
squares
q
, in such a way that the
S_{k}
form a partition of all squares. The partitions
{\mathcal{P}}
we will use in the next theorem consist of
M
-sets
P_{j}
, each having exactly one element from each
S_{k}
. There are
(\frac{R}{M}!)^{M-1}
such partitions. The proof is left as an exercise to the reader.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that for all
M
sufficiently close to
R
we have

\|EF\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{j}\}}(\frac{R}{M})^{1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{R/M}\|E_{P_{j}}F\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{3}.

Then the Restriction conjecture for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
holds.

The situation is even more dramatic for
l^{2}
decoupling.

Theorem 2.8
.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1)
. Assume (
1
) holds for all partitions into
R^{\beta}
-sets
S_{i}
when
p=3
. Then the Restriction conjecture for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
holds.

Proof.

Fix
\delta
such that
\frac{\beta-1}{2}+\delta\leq 0
. Write
R^{\prime}=R^{\beta+2\delta}
and
M=R^{\beta}=(R^{\prime})^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+2\delta}}
. We consider
F
supported on
[0,R^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}+\delta}]^{2}
. Inequality (
1
) and Hölder’s inequality implies that for each such
F
and each partition
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{j}\}
of the collection of
1/\sqrt{R}
-squares
q
in
[0,R^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}+\delta}]^{2}
into
M
-sets
P_{j}
we have

\|EF\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(\frac{R^{\prime}}{M})^{1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{R^{\prime}/M}\|E_{P_{j}}F\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{3}.

Using parabolic rescaling
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\to\xi^{\prime}=R^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}-\delta}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
on the ball
B_{R}=[-R,R]^{3}
, this can be written as

\|E\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}([-R^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}+\delta},R^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}+\delta}]^{2}\times[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}])}^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime})^{\epsilon}(\frac{R^{\prime}}{M})^{1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{R^{\prime}/M}\|E_{P_{j}}\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}([-R^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}+\delta},R^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}+\delta}]^{2}\times[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}])}^{3}.

This holds true for all
\tilde{F}:[0,1]^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and all partitions of
[0,1]^{2}
into
M
-sets
P_{j}
consisting of
1/\sqrt{R^{\prime}}
-squares
q^{\prime}
.
We will use the superficially weaker consequence

\|E\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}([-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}]^{3})}^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime})^{\epsilon}(\frac{R^{\prime}}{M})^{1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{R^{\prime}/M}\|E_{P_{j}}\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}])}^{3}.

(6)

The rest of the argument is identical to the one for Theorem
2.1
. We consider an arbitrary
\tilde{F}
with
\|\tilde{F}\|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{\prime}
and prove that

\int_{[0,\sqrt{R^{\prime}}]^{3}}|\tilde{F}|^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime})^{3+\epsilon}.

We may assume
\tilde{F}
is constant on each
q^{\prime}
. For such
\tilde{F}
we prove the superficially stronger inequality

\|E\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}([-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}]^{3})}^{3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime})^{3+\epsilon}.

(7)

We then average (
6
) over all partitions, and proceed as before to find

\int_{[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}]^{3}}|E\tilde{F}|^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime})^{\epsilon}((R^{\prime})^{3}+(R^{\prime})^{3}(\frac{R^{\prime}}{M})^{3/2}).

We have used that

\|E_{P_{j}}\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}])}^{3}\approx\|E_{P_{j}}\tilde{F}\|_{L^{3}([-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}]^{3})}^{3}.

This is because the mass of
E_{P_{j}}\tilde{F}
is concentrated in a tube that lives in
[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}]^{3}
.

Recall that
R^{\prime}/M=R^{2\delta}
. The estimate above holds uniformly for each
R^{\prime}\gg 1
and
\delta
. Inequality (
7
) is seen to hold by letting
\delta,\epsilon\to 0
.

∎

When

Conjecture 2.9
.

Assume each
S_{i}
is a
\sqrt{R}
-set.
The inequality

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+\epsilon}(\sum_{i=1}^{R^{1/2}}\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p})^{1/p}

(8)

holds for

.

We offer evidence for the conjecture by verifying the case

a single wave packet corresponding to each

),
|EF_{T}|\approx 1_{T}
on

First, an easy consequence of Tao’s bilinear theorem
[

This inequality holds true for arbitrary

Next, note that

\|(\sum_{T}|EF_{T}|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{10/3}(B_{R})}\approx\|\sum_{T}1_{T}\|_{L^{5/3}}^{1/2}.

Then, using Wolff’s Kakeya estimate
[

(there is one tube per direction) we have

\|\sum_{T}1_{T}\|_{5/3}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|\sum_{T}1_{T}\|_{L^{1}}^{3/5}R^{1/10+\epsilon}.

Putting these together we get

\|EF\|_{L^{10/3}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|\sum_{T}1_{T}\|_{L^{1}}^{3/10}R^{1/10+\epsilon}.

Finally, note that by using Rubio de Francia’s square function estimate as before we find

\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{10/3}(B_{R})}\gtrsim\|(\sum_{T=T_{q}\atop{q\subset S_{i}}}|EF_{T}|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{L^{10/3}(B_{R})}\approx\|\sum_{T=T_{q}\atop{q\subset S_{i}}}1_{T}\|_{L^{5/3}}^{1/2}\gtrsim\|\sum_{T=T_{q}\atop{q\subset S_{i}}}1_{T}\|_{L^{1}}^{3/10}.

The last inequality is due to the fact that the sum is either zero or greater than 1.

When

Lower bounds for

for the right hand side of (

3.
An unconditional large ensemble decoupling

Here is the main result in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\sqrt{R}\lesssim N\leq R
. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{N}
be a partition of
[0,1]^{2}
into large ensembles, such that each square
Q\subset[0,1]^{2}
with side length
L\geq R^{-1/2}

intersects at most
O(LN)
of these sets. Then for each
F:[0,1]^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
2\leq p\leq 3
we have

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p})^{1/p}.

The proof relies on recoupling. We formulate it in a way that fits our needs here.

Theorem 3.2

(Recoupling)
.

Assume
F
is supported on pairwise disjoint squares
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{M}
with side length
1/K
. Then for each ball
B
with radius at least
K
and for each
p\geq 2
we have

(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|E_{\alpha_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B)}^{p})^{1/p}\lesssim\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B)}.

Proof.

The result is immediate when
p\in\{2,\infty\}
.
Use
l^{2}(L^{2})-l^{\infty}(L^{\infty})
interpolation. Alternatively, use Rubio de Francia’s square function estimate, as in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

∎

We also use the following unconditional version of Theorem
1.4
. Its proof mirrors the one of Theorem
1.4
, using the trilinear reverse square function estimate in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The trilinear version of Conjecture
1.3
holds. More precisely, if
F=F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}
and
F_{i}
are supported transversely, then for
2\leq p\leq 3

\|(\prod_{i}EF_{i})^{1/3}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{2})^{1/2}.

Proof.

(of Theorem
3.1
)

Let
C_{R}
be the best constant such that

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C_{R}N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p})^{1/p}

holds for all
F
,
B_{R}
,
N
and
S_{i}
as in the statement of Theorem
3.1
. Fix
K=O(1)
, large enough.

We follow a standard Bourgain–Guth argument, see
[
6
]
. Split
[0,1]^{2}
into
1/K
-squares
\alpha
. Consider a finitely overlapping cover of
B_{R}
with balls
B_{K}
. For each
B_{K}\subset B_{R}
, there is a flat
1\times 1/K
-strip
S
(that is, the collection of all
1/K
-squares
\alpha
whose slight enlargements intersect a given line) inside
[0,1]^{2}
, and there are transverse (that is, any three points from the three squares determine an area
\gtrsim_{K}1
)
1/K
-squares
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
such that

|EF(x)|\lesssim K^{O(1)}|\prod_{i=1}^{3}E_{\alpha_{i}}F(x)|^{1/3}+|\sum_{\alpha\subset S}E_{\alpha}F(x)|,\;x\in B_{K}.

Small cap
l^{p}(L^{p})
decoupling for the parabola (
[
11
]
) gives

\int_{B_{K}}|\sum_{\alpha\subset S}E_{\alpha}F|^{p}\lesssim K^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sum_{\alpha\subset S}\int_{B_{K}}|E_{\alpha}F|^{p}.

Summing up over
B_{K}
we get

\|EF\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon}K^{O(1)}R^{\epsilon}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{S_{i}}F\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}^{2})^{p/2}+K^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sum_{\alpha\subset[0,1]^{2}}\int_{B_{R}}|E_{\alpha}F|^{p}.

(9)

The first term is the transverse contribution, estimated using Theorem
3.3
and the triangle inequality to rebuild the fractured pieces
S_{i}
(no need for recoupling, as further losses in
K
are harmless for this term).

For the second term, parabolic rescaling gives

\int_{B_{R}}|E_{\alpha}F|^{p}\lesssim C_{R/K^{2}}^{p}(\frac{N}{K})^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sum_{S_{i}\cap\alpha\not=\emptyset}\int_{B_{R}}|E_{S_{i}\cap\alpha}F|^{p}.

Here
S_{i}\cap\alpha
is the union of the
1/\sqrt{R}
-squares lying inside
\alpha
.
Under rescaling
\alpha
becomes
[0,1]^{2}
, and the sets
S_{i}
that intersect
\alpha
become unions of
K/\sqrt{R}
-squares. There are
O(N/K)
such sets, and they satisfy the induction hypothesis relative to squares
Q
. The ball
B_{R}
becomes a tubular region, covered by a finitely overlapping union of balls of radius
R/K^{2}
.

Thus, the contribution from the second term in (
9
) is

\lesssim C_{R/K^{2}}^{p}N^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sum_{S_{i}}\int_{B_{R}}\sum_{\alpha}|E_{\alpha\cap S_{i}}F|^{p},

which is dominated via recoupling by

C_{R/K^{2}}^{p}N^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sum_{S_{i}}\int_{B_{R}}|E_{S_{i}}F|^{p}.

We conclude that

C_{R}\leq C_{\epsilon}K^{O(1)}R^{\epsilon}+C_{1}C_{R/K^{2}},

with
C_{1}
independent of
K,R
.
Iterating this
R\to R/K^{2}\to R/K^{4}\ldots
leads to
C_{R}\lesssim_{\epsilon}1
.

∎

Recoupling is typically lossy, more so than decoupling. The reason it is harmless in this argument is because its loss is compensated by the gain in the Bourgain–Guth argument, that comes from the one dimensional reduction to strips.

One example where Theorem
3.1
is applicable with
N\sim\sqrt{R}
is when each
S_{i}
consists of all the squares
q
intersecting some curve
\gamma_{i}
. We assume each
Q
with side length
L
intersects at most
L\sqrt{R}
such curves. If these curves are consecutive parallel lines at distance
\sim 1/\sqrt{R}
from each other, the large ensembles

are flat strips. But the curves can also be e.g. concentric circles.

Question 3.4

. Note that this does not follow from Corollary

or

4.
Tight decoupling

Let
p\geq 2
and let
R
be a large number. To motivate the forthcoming discussion, we ask how many points
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|\xi_{n}|\lesssim 1
can we find, so that we have
L^{p}
square root cancellation on the ball
B(0,R)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\;|x|\leq R\}
(or equivalently, on the square
[0,R]^{d}
), namely

\frac{1}{|B(0,R)|}\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{n}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}

(10)

for each
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We ask that the implicit constant in (
10
) is independent of
R
. If (
10
) holds for some
p
, it will also hold for smaller indices, due to Hölder’s inequality.

It is easy to see that the upper bound
N\lesssim R^{\frac{2d}{p}}
is necessary, by considering constant weights
a_{n}=1
. This is due to constructive interference near the origin

|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|\sim N,\;\;\text{for }|x|<1/100,

leading to the inequality

\frac{N^{p}}{R^{d}}\lesssim N^{p/2}.

Interestingly, there is a choice (in fact many) of
N\sim R^{2d/p}
points
\xi_{n}
such that (
10
) holds. This is a consequence of the
\Lambda(p)
phenomenon, settled by Bourgain in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For each orthonormal system
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}
of functions with
\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and each
p>2
, there is a set
S\subset\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with size
\sim n^{2/p}
such that

\|\sum_{i\in S}a_{i}\varphi_{i}\|_{p}\lesssim_{p}(\sum_{i\in S}|a_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}

for each
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. The implicit constant is independent of
n
and
a_{i}
. A random subset
S
with this size will work, with high probability.

Consider the functions
\varphi_{{\bf n}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=e({n_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}x_{d}})
with
n_{i}
positive integers smaller than
R
. They are
L^{2}
orthogonal on
[0,1]^{d}
, and there are
\sim R^{d}
of them. Theorem
4.1
shows the existence of
\sim(R^{d})^{2/p}
such functions satisfying

\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\sum_{{\bf n}\in S}a_{\bf n}e({\bf n}\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{\bf n}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}.

Rescaling, it follows that taking
\xi_{n}
to be the points in
\{\frac{{\bf n}}{R}:\;{\bf n}\in S\}
satisfies (
10
).

For generic
S
, the points
\frac{{\bf n}}{R}
are spread out somewhat uniformly in the cube
[0,1]^{d}
. We will be interested in the case when the points lie on a prescribed manifold. Some degree of curvature will be needed, as the points cannot lie inside any given hyperplane
{\mathbb{H}}
. Indeed, if
\xi_{n}\in{\mathbb{H}}
and
a_{n}=1
, we have constructive interference on the bigger set
E=\{x\in B(0,R):\;{\operatorname{dist}\,}(x,{\mathbb{H}}^{\perp})\leq 1/100\}
of volume
\sim R
. This forces the more severe restriction
N\lesssim R^{\frac{2(d-1)}{p}}
.

If (
10
) is satisfied for a collection of
\xi_{n}
with maximal size
N\sim R^{2d/p}
, we will refer to (
10
) as
tight decoupling.
Slightly weaker versions may also be considered, where
R^{\epsilon}
losses are tolerated.

Here is our main theorem in this section. To keep things simpler, we will restrict attention to the constant coefficient case, which is by far the most relevant for applications. But see also Question
4.5
.

Theorem 4.2

(Tight decoupling for frequencies in thin sets)
.

Let
p\geq 2
and let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a compact set.
Assume that there is a Borel probability measure
d\sigma
with
{\operatorname{supp}\,}(d\sigma)\subset E
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\widehat{d\sigma}(x)|^{p}dx<\infty.

(11)

We also assume that for each
\epsilon
there is a partition of
{\operatorname{supp}\,}(d\sigma)
into Borel sets with equal
d\sigma
measure and diameter less than
\epsilon
.

Then for each
R\gg 1
there is a collection of points
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset E
with cardinality
\sim R^{\frac{2d}{p}}
such that we have square root cancellation on
B(0,R)
in
L^{p}

for
a_{\xi}\equiv 1

\frac{1}{|B(0,R)|}\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}.

(12)

Conversely, if such
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset E
exists for a sequence of
R=R_{k}\to\infty
, then
E
must support a probability measure
d\sigma
satisfying
(
11
)
.

Inequality (
11
) forces the support of
d\sigma
to have Hausdorff dimension at least
2d/p
.

Example 4.3

(
Hypersurfaces
)
.

Perhaps the most interesting class of examples comes from the case when
d\sigma
is supported on a smooth hypersurface
\Sigma
. If we assume
\Sigma
has nowhere zero Gaussian curvature, then it is known that the surface measure
d\sigma
satisfies (
11
) for each
p>\frac{2d}{d-1}
, since in fact

|\widehat{d\sigma}(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}
. Theorem
4.2
shows that tight decoupling is possible in this range.

On the other hand, the Hausdorff dimension of
\Sigma
is
d-1
, making it a Salem set. No measure supported on
\Sigma
can satisfy (
11
) with
p<\frac{2d}{d-1}
, so tight decoupling is impossible in this range.

The case
p=\frac{4d}{d-1}
is particularly revealing.
When the points
\xi_{n}\in\Sigma
are
1/\sqrt{R}
-separated, it was proved in
[
4
]
, as a consequence of decoupling, that (
10
) holds for
p\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
for all
a_{n}
, albeit with mild
R^{\epsilon}
losses. At this level of generality, this exponent is best possible. To see this, assume
\Sigma
is parametrized by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1},\Phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1}))
. Let
\xi_{\bf n}=(\frac{{\bf n}}{\sqrt{R}},\Phi(\frac{{\bf n}}{\sqrt{R}}))\in\Sigma
, with
{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}\cap B_{d-1}(0,\sqrt{R})
. These points are
1/\sqrt{R}
-separated, and there are
\sim R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
many of them. The new phenomenon is that the associated exponential sums have constructive interference in many regions of
B(0,R)
, not just near the origin. More precisely,

|\sum_{{\bf n}}e(\xi_{\bf n}\cdot x)|\sim R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}

whenever
|x_{i}-l_{i}\sqrt{R}|\leq\frac{1}{100d}
for each
1\leq i\leq d-1
and some integer
|l_{i}|\lesssim\sqrt{R}
, and
|x_{d}|\leq 1/100
. This set has measure
\sim R^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}
, which can be seen to force the restriction
p\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
in (
10
).

As a consequence of Theorem
4.2
with
p=\frac{4d}{d-1}
, we find that each
\Sigma
as above supports a collection
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
of roughly
R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
points
\xi
, such that we have square root cancellation in
L^{\frac{4d}{d-1}}

\frac{1}{|B(0,R)|}\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{\frac{4d}{d-1}}dx\lesssim R^{d}.

There is no guarantee that these points are
1/\sqrt{R}
-separated, but our proof of Theorem
4.2
will show that this can be arranged to be true for a significant fraction of all pairs of points.

The examples provided by our proof of Theorem
4.2
are non-deterministic, they arise via random selections. We are not aware of any deterministic example that is known to satisfy the case
p=\frac{4d}{d-1}
of the theorem. However, we recall two conjectures that fit into this category. This time, we allow
R^{\epsilon}
losses.

The first one is about lattice points on the sphere. Consider the typical eigenfunction of the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, given by

S_{N,d}({\textbf{x}})=\sum_{{\bf n}\in\sqrt{N}{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{{\bf n}}e({\bf n}\cdot{\textbf{x}}).

It has been conjectured, see e.g.
[
7
]
, that if
d\geq 3
and
p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2}

\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|S_{N,d}(x)|^{p}dx\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\epsilon}\|a_{\bf n}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}.

(13)

This inequality, sometimes referred to as the discrete Restriction conjecture for the sphere, has been verified in
[
4
]
in the smaller range
p\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
, that is accessible via decoupling.

Let
d=3
, so
\frac{2d}{d-2}=6
coincides with
\frac{4d}{d-1}
. Consider an integer
N
for which
\sqrt{N}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
contains
\approx\sqrt{N}
points (this is the case for infinitely many
N
). The rescaled collection
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(\sqrt{N}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{3})
is conjectured to satisfy the tight decoupling (
12
) with
R=\sqrt{N}
,
p=6
and
R^{\epsilon}
losses.

As a second example, we recall a conjecture from
[
12
]
. Consider the set of lattice points in the thin annulus

{\mathcal{A}}_{R,R^{-1/2}}=\{{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:|{\bf n}-R|\leq R^{-1/2}\}.

Moreover, we may assume that there are
\approx R^{1/2}
such points. The rescaled points
\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}{\mathcal{A}}_{R,R^{-1/2}}
do not belong to the circle
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, but rather to a thin neighborhood of it. It is conjectured in
[
12
]
(Conjecture B) that

\int_{[0,1]^{2}}|\sum_{{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:|{\bf n}-R|\leq R^{-1/2}}e({\bf n}\cdot x)|^{8}dx\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{2+\epsilon}.

Thus, the rescaled points are expected to satisfy the tight decoupling (
12
)
with
p=8
(and
R^{\epsilon}
losses). The arbitrary coefficient version of this inequality is false, as
{\mathcal{A}}_{R,R^{-1/2}}
may contain many points in arithmetic progression.

When
d\geq 4
, (
13
) provides further candidates for tight decoupling, but the value of
\frac{2d}{d-2}
is now smaller than
\frac{4d}{d-1}
. For infinitely many
N
, the set
\sqrt{N}{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
contains
\approx N^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
points. This matches
R^{2d/p}
with
R=\sqrt{N}
and
p=\frac{2d}{d-2}
. Interestingly, (
13
) was verified for
d=4
and
a_{\bf n}=1
in
[
3
]
, using the Siegel mass formula. We are not aware of any other deterministic example for which tight decoupling is known to hold for frequencies localized on a hypersurface. This example gives the heuristics for why (
13
) is extremely difficult. Tight decoupling is the highest degree on square root cancellation, and verifying it is expected to involve high-level number theory.

Remark 4.4
.

In
[
18
]
(see also Lemma 6 in
[
16
]
and the main result in
[
15
]
), a probability measure
\nu
supported on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is shown to exist for each
0<\alpha<1
such that for each
\xi\in{\operatorname{supp}\,}(\nu)
and
0<r\lesssim 1

r^{\alpha+\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\nu(B(\xi,r))\lesssim_{\epsilon}r^{\alpha-\epsilon}

and

|\widehat{\nu}(x)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}(1+|x|)^{-\alpha/2+\epsilon}.

(14)

We use this with
\alpha=1/2
. Consider
R^{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N_{R}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
pairwise disjoint arcs
J
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, with length
1/R
and such that

R^{-\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\nu(J)\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

Let
\psi_{R}(x)=e^{-(\frac{|x|}{R})^{2}}
, so
\widehat{\psi_{R}}(\xi)=R^{2}e^{-(R{|\xi|})^{2}}
. Let
c_{J}
be the center of
J
. Let
\eta
be smooth, compactly supported, positive, with
|\widehat{\eta}(x)|\geq 1
for
|x|\leq 1
. Note that for each
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

0\leq\sum_{J}\eta(R(\xi-c_{J}))\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{-3/2+\epsilon}(d\nu)*\widehat{\psi_{R}}(\xi).

Since
8
is an even integer, this implies that, taking Fourier transforms on both sides

\|{\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{J}\widehat{\eta}(\frac{x}{R})e(c_{J}\cdot x)}\|_{L^{8}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{-3/2+\epsilon}\|\widehat{\nu}\psi_{R}\|_{L^{8}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}.

The last inequality follows from (
14
). We find that

\|\sum_{J}e(c_{J}\cdot x)\|_{L^{8}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}.

This is tight decoupling with
R^{\epsilon}
loss. The fact that
8
is even integer is crucial to this argument.

This construction is also non deterministic, as
\nu
is a random measure. The frequency points
c_{J}
belong to a fractal. This is very different from our main construction, where
\xi_{n}
are well spaced, with high probability.

We close the discussion about hypersurfaces with the following question, open even for
{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
.

Question 4.5
.

Let
\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}\leq p\leq\frac{4d}{d-1}
. Given a hypersurface
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with positive curvatures (e.g. the sphere), and
R\gg 1
, is there a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}\subset\Sigma
with
\sim R^{2p/d}
points such that

\frac{1}{|B(0,R)|}\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R}}a_{\xi}e(\xi\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim\|a_{\xi}\|_{l^{2}}^{p}

holds for arbitrary
a_{\xi}
? How about with
R^{\epsilon}
losses, when
p>\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
?

The additional restriction
p\geq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
is forced
by lower dimensional concentration. There is a cap with diameter
\sim R^{-1/2}
on
\Sigma
containing
N\sim R^{\frac{2d}{p}-\frac{d-1}{2}}
of the points in
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
. Test the inequality with
a_{\xi}=1
on this set, and zero otherwise. The points lie inside a box with dimensions
R^{-1/2}
(
d-1
times) and
1/R
(once). There is constructive interference on the box centered at the origin with dimensions
R^{1/2}
(
d-1
times) and
R
(once). Thus

R^{-d}R^{\frac{d-1}{2}+1}N^{p}\lesssim N^{p/2},

which means
p\geq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
.

Example 4.6

(
Curves
)
.

Consider a curve with nonzero torsion in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, such as the moment curve

\Gamma^{d}=\{(t,\ldots,t^{d}):\;0\leq t\leq 1\}.

The main result in
[
13
]
proves that
\Gamma^{d}
does not support any measure with Fourier transform in
L^{p}
for
p<\frac{d^{2}+d+2}{2}
. The second part of Theorem
4.2
concludes that it is impossible to achieve tight decoupling in the range
p<\frac{d^{2}+d+2}{2}
, with frequencies in
\Gamma^{d}
.

On the other hand, it is known (
[
1
]
) that the uniform measure on
\Gamma^{d}
satisfies
(
11
) for
p>\frac{d^{2}+d+2}{2}
. When combined with the first part of Theorem
4.2
, we see that tight decoupling is possible in this range.

Let us specialize to
p=2d^{2}
. Theorem (
4.2
) shows the existence of roughly
R^{1/d}
points on
\Gamma^{d}
such that (
12
) holds for this
p
. On the other hand it was proved in
[
5
]
that (
12
) holds for
p\leq d(d+1)
, arbitrary coefficients and arbitrary collections
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
of points on
\Gamma^{d}
that are
1/R^{1/d}
-separated. This range follows from decoupling and is sharp under just the separation condition, as it can be seen by considering the rescaled lattice points on
\Gamma^{d}
corresponding to
t=n/R^{1/d}
,
1\leq n\leq R^{1/d}
.

We use two classical results. The first one is due to Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be independent, mean zero random variables belonging to
L^{p}
for some
p\geq 1
. Then

\int|\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}|^{p}\sim_{p}\int(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|X_{n}|^{2})^{p/2}.

The second one is usually attributed to Rosenthal, see Lemma 1 in
[
17
]
. It does not require mean zero.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N}
be independent, random variables belonging to
L^{q}
for some
q\geq 1
. Then

\int|\sum_{n=1}^{N}Y_{n}|^{q}\lesssim_{q}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int|Y_{n}|^{q}+(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int|Y_{n}|)^{q}.

Let us now prove the first part in Theorem
4.2
.

Consider a partition of
{\operatorname{supp}\,}(d\sigma)
into sets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{N}
with
d\sigma
measure equal to
1/N
and diameter
\lesssim R^{-1-\frac{d}{p}}
. Pick any point
\xi_{n}\in S_{n}
. Then, if
|x|<R

\displaystyle|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)-\widehat{d\sigma}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\int_{S_{n}}(e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)-e(\xi\cdot x))d\sigma(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\max_{\xi\in S_{n}}|e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)-e(\xi\cdot x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\max_{n}\max_{\xi\in S_{n}}R|\xi_{n}-\xi|\lesssim R^{-d/p}.

Combining this with (
11
) it follows that

\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim N^{p}(1+\int_{B(0,R)}|\widehat{d\sigma}(x)|^{p}dx)\lesssim N^{p}.

Assume
\frac{1}{N}\leq\delta\leq 1
.
Consider the
\{0,1\}
-valued independent random variables
f_{n}(\omega)
with mean
\delta
, defined on the probability space
\Omega
. We first write for each
x\in B(0,R)

\int_{\Omega}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}(\omega)e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}d\omega\lesssim\delta^{p}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}+\int_{\Omega}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}(f_{n}(\omega)-\delta)e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}d\omega.

For the second term, we use Theorem
4.7
with
X_{n}(\omega)=(f_{n}(\omega)-\delta)e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)
and then Theorem
4.8
with
q=p/2
and
Y_{n}(\omega)=|f_{n}(\omega)-\delta|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}(f_{n}(\omega)-\delta)e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}d\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|f_{n}(\omega)-\delta|^{2})^{p/2}d\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}(\omega)-\delta|^{p}d\omega+(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}(\omega)-\delta|^{2}d\omega)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(\delta+\delta^{p})+(N(\delta+\delta^{2}))^{p/2}\sim(N\delta)^{p/2}.

Integrating on
B(0,R)
we get

\frac{1}{|B(0,R)|}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}(\omega)e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}dxd\omega\lesssim(N\delta)^{p/2}+\frac{(N\delta)^{p}}{R^{d}}.

We select
\delta
such that
N\delta\sim R^{2d/p}
. Let

\Omega^{\prime}=\{\omega\in\Omega:\;|\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}(\omega)-N\delta|\geq\frac{N\delta}{2}\}.

Then

|\Omega^{\prime}|\leq\frac{4\int_{\Omega}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}(f_{n}(\omega)-\delta)|^{2}d\omega}{N^{2}\delta^{2}}=\frac{4N(\delta+\delta^{2})}{N^{2}\delta^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N\delta}.

Thus
|\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}|\geq 1/2
if
R
is large enough. We may thus pick
\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
such that

\frac{1}{|B(0,R)|}\int_{B(0,R)}|\sum_{n=1}^{N}f_{n}(\omega)e(\xi_{n}\cdot x)|^{p}dx\lesssim(N\delta)^{p/2}.

It suffices to note that
f_{n}(\omega)=1
for
\sim N\delta
values of
n
. The set
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}
will consist precisely of those
\xi_{n}
corresponding to these
n
.

We close with the proof of the second part in Theorem
4.2
. For each
R=R_{k}
, consider the probability measure on
E

\mu_{k}=\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{R_{k}}|}\sum_{\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}_{R_{k}}}\delta_{\xi}.

Note that

\widehat{\mu_{k}}(0)=1

and

\int_{B(0,R_{k})}|\widehat{\mu_{k}}(x)|^{p}dx\lesssim 1.

Consider a weak limit point
d\sigma
of the sequence. Since
\widehat{\mu_{k}}(x)\to\widehat{d\sigma}(x)
for each
x
, we find that
\sigma
is a probability measure. Also, the dominated convergence theorem shows that for each ball
B(0,r)

\int_{B(0,r)}|\widehat{d\sigma}(x)|^{p}dx=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B(0,r)}|\widehat{\mu_{k}}(x)|^{p}dx\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B(0,R_{k})}|\widehat{\mu_{k}}(x)|^{p}dx\lesssim 1.

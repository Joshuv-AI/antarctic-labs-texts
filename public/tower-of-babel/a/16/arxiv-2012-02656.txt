1
Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the analyticity of the solution of the following Monge-Ampère equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\det D^{2}u=\Lambda_{q}(-u)^{q}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
q>0
and
\Omega
is a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This problem was first studied by Lions
[
12
]
. In
[
12
]
, Lions proved that for
q=n
, (
1.1
) admits a unique eigenvalue
\Lambda_{n}
and eigenfunction
u\in C^{1,1}(\bar{\Omega})\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
(up to multiplications of positive constants) provided
\Omega
is smooth and uniformly convex.

Later, Chou
[
18
]
studied the problem (
1.1
) for
q>0
. His approach is based on the Monge-Ampère functional

\displaystyle J(u)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Omega}(-u)\det D^{2}udx-\frac{1}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q+1}dx

(1.2)

and the following logarithmic gradient flow

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\ln\det(D_{x}^{2}u)-q\ln(-u),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

Then Chou obtained the existence of non-trivial solutions
u\in C^{0,1}(\bar{\Omega})\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
provided
\Omega
is smooth and uniformly convex. Also the uniqueness of non-trivial solution was established in
[
18
]
for
0<q<n
and
q=n
(up to multiplications of positive constants). Later, Hartenstine
[
2
]
extended it to bounded and strictly convex domain
\Omega
for
0<q<n
. Recently, Le
[
10
]
proved the existence(
q>0
) of solutions of (
1.1
) and the uniqueness(
0<q\leq n
) of solutions of (
1.1
) for general bounded convex domain
\Omega
. For
q>n
, the uniqueness of non-trivial solutions of (
1.1
) is less known. Recently, the first named author
[
7
]
proved that there exists a constant
\varepsilon(n)>0
such that (
1.1
) admits a unique non-trivial solution for
q\in(n,n+\varepsilon(n))
.

The higher order global regularity of the solutions of (
1.1
) remains unknown until rencent 10 years. The main difficulty arises from the degeneracy of the equation (
1.1
) on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. In a survey paper, Trudinger and Wang [
[
17
]
,P21] proposed the problem that whether
u
is smooth up to the boundary for
q=n
when
\Omega
is smooth and uniformly convex. Later, Hong, Huang and Wang
[
4
]
gave an affirmative answer to this problem in dimension 2. Their approach relies on an auxiliary function

H=u_{22}u_{1}^{2}-2u_{12}u_{1}u_{2}+u_{11}u_{2}^{2}

(1.4)

which is related to the Gauss curvature of the level set of
u
. For arbitrary dimensions, Savin
[
16
]

made a first contribution on the global
C^{2}
regularity of solutions of (
1.1
). Later, Le and Savin
[
11
]
completely solved the above problem in arbitrary dimensions. The key observation of the work
[
16
]
and
[
11
]
is that near the boundary,

u(x)\sim\frac{1}{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{1}{(q+1)(q+2)}x_{n}^{q+2}

(1.5)

which allows them to use blow-up and perturbation arguments to show
u\in C^{2,\alpha}
.
Then they can raise the regularity up to
C^{\infty}
by investigating a linear degenerate elliptic equation.

It is natural to ask that whether the solution of (
1.1
) is analytic up to the boundary provided the domain
\Omega
is analytic and uniformly convex. The analyticity of the solutions of uniformly elliptic equations(systems) are well studied. We refer readers to
[
15
]
for linear equations(systems),
[
14
,
13
]
for non-linear equations(systems) and
[
1
]
for more general regularity results. There seems no unified results for degenerate elliptic cases. In the present case, the model degenerate equation is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}(u)=u_{nn}+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u=f\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
u(x^{\prime},0)=g(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\end{cases}

(1.6)

where
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
. Usually, people call (
1.6
) a Grushin type degenerate elliptic equation. To the authors’s best knowledge, there seems no results considering the analyticity of solutions of fully non-linear elliptic equations with degeneracy as in (
1.6
).

Now we state our main results in the present paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
u
is a non-trivial solution of (
1.1
) and
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is analytic and uniformly convex. Then
u
is analytic in
\bar{\Omega}
provided
q\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, i.e.
u\in C^{\omega}(\bar{\Omega})
.

Remark 1.1
.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
originated from
[
8
]
for semi-linear elliptic equation. For the fully non-linear uniform elliptic case, we refer the readers to
[
3
]
.

The present paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will collect some basic estimates for the linear degenerate elliptic equation (
1.6
). Then we apply the ideas of
[
8
]
to show that
u
is analytic up to the boundary in Section 3.

2
Estimates for linear model equation

In the present section, we consider the following linear degenerate elliptic equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{nn}+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u=f\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
u(x^{\prime},0)=g(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
.

Firstly, we introduce some notations. Let
m
be a positive integer. For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
, we denote
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
with
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
and
u_{i}=\partial_{i}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial{x_{i}}}
(
i=1,2,\cdots,n
).

For
k\geq 0
, we define the following weighted Sobolev space
\widetilde{W}^{k,2}(G_{1})
as

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}(G_{1})}=&\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial_{n}^{2}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\sum_{|\alpha|=k+2,\alpha_{n}\leq 1}\|x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial_{n}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\\
+&\sum_{|\alpha|=k+1,\alpha_{n}=0}\|x_{n}^{m-1}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{n}u(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\|u\|_{H^{k}(G_{1})},\end{split}

where
G_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,1)
.

In the following, we introduce two lemmas which are the key ingredients to prove the analyticity of the solutions to Monge-Ampère equations.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
solves (
2.1
) with supp
u\subset G_{1}
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{0,2}(G_{1})}\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})})

(2.2)

for some constant
C
depending only on
n
.

Proof.

We divide the proof of the present lemma into two cases.

Case 1.

For
m=1
, we follow the steps of standard
W^{2,2}
-estimates as Laplacian equation. By (
2.1
), one gets

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(u_{nn}+x_{n}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f^{2}dx.

(2.3)

Integrating by parts the crossproduct term yields

\begin{split}&2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}u_{nn}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}udx=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{nn}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}udx\\
=&2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}|^{2}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}udx\\
=&2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}g|^{2}dx^{\prime}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Integrating by parts the other terms of (
2.3
), one gets

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{2}_{nn}+x_{n}^{2}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}^{2}u|^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}g|^{2}dx^{\prime}.\end{split}

(2.5)

Multiplying
-u
on both sides of (
2.1
) and integrating by parts, one obtains

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u_{n}^{2}+x_{n}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u|^{2}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}u(x^{\prime},0)u_{n}(x^{\prime},0)dx^{\prime}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}fudx.\end{split}

(2.6)

Suppose

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}u^{2}_{n}(x^{\prime},t)dx^{\prime}=\inf_{x_{n}\in[0,1]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}u^{2}_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{n})dx^{\prime}.

(2.7)

Then we know

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}u(x^{\prime},0)u_{n}(x^{\prime},0)dx^{\prime}\right|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}g^{2}dx^{\prime}+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}u_{n}^{2}(x^{\prime},0)dx^{\prime}\\
\leq&C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}g^{2}dx^{\prime}+C\varepsilon(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}u_{n}^{2}(x^{\prime},t)dx^{\prime}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u_{nn}^{2}dx)\\
\leq&C(\|g\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{2})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u_{n}^{2}dx.\end{split}

(2.8)

In getting the last inequality, we use (
2.5
), (
2.7
) and take suitable
\varepsilon>0
small.
Also, one has

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}g^{2}dx^{\prime}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u_{n}^{2}dx\end{split}

(2.9)

since
u
has compact support.
Noticing that
\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u=\partial_{n}(x_{n}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u)-x_{n}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}
, one knows

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u|^{2}dx\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\partial_{n}(x_{n}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u)|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}|^{2}dx\\
=&2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}^{2}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}|^{2}dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\cdot(2\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}+x_{n}\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{nn})dx\\
=&4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}^{2}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

(2.10)

Combining estimates (
2.5
)-(
2.10
) and the assumption supp
u\subset G_{1}
yield the present lemma for
m=1
.

Case 2.

For
m\geq 2
, the above method fails. We need employ some previous estimates for (
2.1
).
One may view the solutions of (
2.1
) as
u=v+w
where
v
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{nn}v+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}v=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
v(x^{\prime},0)=g(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\end{cases}

(2.11)

and
w
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{nn}w+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}w=f\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
w(x^{\prime},0)=0,\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.\end{cases}

(2.12)

Since
g(x^{\prime})
is compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, Hong-Wang [Lemma 3.1,
[
6
]
] proved that there exists an operator
\mathcal{B}
such that
v(x)=\mathcal{B}(g)(x)
solves (
2.11
) and satisfies the following estimates

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial_{nn}(\mathcal{B}(g))(x_{n})\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Lambda_{1}\partial_{n}(\mathcal{B}(g))(x_{n})\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\\
&+\|x_{n}^{m}\Lambda_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{B}(g))(x_{n})\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\|\partial_{n}(\mathcal{B}(g))(x^{\prime},0)\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\\
&+\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Lambda_{1}^{\frac{m+4}{m+2}}(\mathcal{B}(g))(x_{n})\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq C\|\Lambda^{\frac{4}{m+2}}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})},\end{split}

(2.13)

where
C
is a universal constant depending only on
n
,
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda
represent the singular integral operators with symbols
|\xi|
and
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1})
. Also the norm
L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
represents the
L^{2}-
norm over
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
in
x^{\prime}
variable.
Then by a simple integration over
x_{n}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial^{2}_{n}(\mathcal{B}(g))\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Lambda_{1}\partial_{n}(\mathcal{B}(g))\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|x_{n}^{m}\Lambda_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{B}(g))\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\\
+&\|\partial_{n}(\mathcal{B}(g))\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Lambda_{1}^{\frac{m+4}{m+2}}(\mathcal{B}(g))\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{n}(\mathcal{B}(g))(x^{\prime},0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\\
+&\|\mathcal{B}(g)\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq C\|\Lambda^{\frac{4}{m+2}}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}.\end{split}

(2.14)

Since
f
is compactly supported in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
. Hong-Li [Theorem 3.2,
[
5
]
](also see Lemma 3.4 in
[
6
]
) proved that there exists an operator
\mathcal{T}
such that
w=\mathcal{T}(f)
solves (
2.12
) and satisfies the following estimates

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial_{n}^{2}(\mathcal{T}f)\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|x_{n}^{m}\Lambda\partial_{n}(\mathcal{T}f)\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|x_{n}^{m}\Lambda^{2}(\mathcal{T}f)\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{n}\mathcal{T}f\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\\
+&\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Lambda(\mathcal{T}f)\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{n}\mathcal{T}f(x^{\prime},0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\|\mathcal{T}f\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}.\end{split}

(2.15)

Then combining (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), one gets

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial_{n}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\|x_{n}^{m}\partial_{in}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\|x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{n}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\\
+&\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\partial_{i}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{n}u(x^{\prime},0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\\
\leq&C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\right).\end{split}

(2.16)

The last inequality of (
2.16
) comes from

\|\Lambda^{\frac{4}{m+2}}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}=\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{2}{m+2}}\hat{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq\|g\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

because of
m\geq 2
.
Noticing that
\frac{m}{2}\leq m-1
for
m\geq 2
, one knows

\|x_{n}^{\frac{m}{2}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\geq\|x_{n}^{m-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}

which implies the present lemma for
m\geq 2
.

∎

In fact, differentiating the linear equation (
2.1
), one can get the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
solves (
2.1
) with supp
u\subset G_{1}
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}(G_{1})}\leq C_{k}\left(\|f\|_{H^{k}(G_{1})}+\|g\|_{H^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\right),\quad k\geq 0

(2.17)

for some positive constant
C_{k}
depending only on
k,m,n
.

Proof.

For
k=0
, this is just (
2.2
). We now prove the present lemma by induction on
k
. Suppose the estimate (
2.17
) is true for
k
, we need to prove (
2.17
) holds for
k+1
.

First, we consider
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u
,
|\alpha|=k+1
,
\alpha_{n}=0
. Differentiating (
2.1
) with respect to
x^{\prime}
for
k+1
times, one gets

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}f\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u(x^{\prime},0)=\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}g(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.\end{cases}

(2.18)

Applying (
2.2
) to (
2.18
), one gets

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{0,2}(G_{1})}\leq C(\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha}g\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}).

(2.19)

Especially, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{|\beta|=k+1,\beta_{n}=0}\|\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}u(x^{\prime},0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}+\sum_{|\beta|=k+2,\beta_{n}=0}\|x_{n}^{m-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\\
&\leq C(\|f\|_{H^{k+1}(G_{1})}+\|g\|_{H^{k+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}).\end{split}

(2.20)

Then we consider the case
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u=\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}u
with
|\alpha^{\prime}|=k
. Differentiating (
2.1
) with respect to
x^{\prime}
for
k
times and then with respect to
x_{n}
, one can derive

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{2}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}u+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}u=\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}f-mx_{n}^{m-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

(2.21)

For the boundary term, by (
2.20
), we know

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n-1}})}\leq C(\|f\|_{H^{k+1}(G_{1})}+\|g\|_{H^{k+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}).

(2.22)

Applying (
2.2
) to (
2.21
) and using (
2.20
) and (
2.22
), one gets

\begin{split}\|\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{0,2}(G_{1})}\leq&C(\|f\|_{H^{k+1}(G_{1})}+\|x_{n}^{m-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}+\|\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{x^{\prime}}u_{n}(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})})\\
\leq&C(\|f\|_{H^{k+1}(G_{1})}+\|g\|_{H^{k+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}).\end{split}

The last case is
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u=\partial_{n}^{l}\partial^{\beta}_{x^{\prime}}u
,
l\geq 2
,
|\beta|=k+1-l
. Differentiating (
2.1
) with respect to
x^{\prime}
for
k+1-l
times and then with respect to
x_{n}
for

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{n}^{2}(\partial_{n}^{l}\partial^{\beta}_{x^{\prime}}u)+x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{n}^{l}\partial^{\beta}_{x^{\prime}}u)\\
=&\partial_{n}^{l}\partial^{\beta}_{x^{\prime}}f-\sum_{r=1}^{\min(m,l)}\frac{l!}{r!(l-r)!}\partial_{n}^{r}(x_{n}^{m})\partial_{n}^{l-r}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{split}

(2.23)

We first consider the boundary term. Applying
\partial_{n}^{l-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}
to (
2.1
), one gets

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{l}\partial^{\beta}_{x^{\prime}}u=-\partial_{n}^{l-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}(x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)+\partial_{n}^{l-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}f.

(2.24)

Restricting (
2.24
) on

, then we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{n}^{l-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}f(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{k+1}(G_{1})}

(2.25)

by the trace theorem of Sobolev space. For the term
\partial_{n}^{l-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}(x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)
,
it matters only if

and equals to
m!C_{l-2}^{m}\partial_{n}^{l-2-m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u(x^{\prime},0)
.

If

\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq\|g\|_{H^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}.

If

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled. Suppose (
3.12
) holds for sufficiently large
A_{0},A_{1}
. Then there holds

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-i,2}}\leq C_{1}\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3-i)!,\quad i=0,1,2

for some constant
C_{1}>0
and
\tilde{A}_{0}
is the constant in Lemma
2.4
.

Proof.

For
i=1
, we know

\begin{split}\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-1,2}}=&\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u)-(N-1)\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-1,2}}\\
\leq&C_{1}\|\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}+C_{1}(N-1)\|\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-1,2}}\\
\leq&CA_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-4)!.\end{split}

In getting the last inequality, we used (
3.12
).
By a similar argument, we can prove the case
i=2
.

It remains to prove the case
i=0
. Recall that
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u)+\partial^{2}_{n}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u)\\
=&\quad\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}G+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)+[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\\
=&\quad\mathcal{L}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u(x^{\prime},0)=\hat{\eta}^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}\varphi(x^{\prime}),\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(3.19)

Here
\hat{\eta}(x^{\prime})=\eta(x^{\prime},0)
.
By Lemma
2.2
, we know

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\leq C(\|\mathcal{L}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u)\|_{H^{k}}+\|\hat{\eta}^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}\varphi\|_{H^{k+1}}).

(3.20)

By the analyticity of
\varphi
, we may assume

\|\hat{\eta}^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}\varphi\|_{H^{k+1}}\leq A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.

In the following, we only need to estimate the terms in
\mathcal{L}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u)
. By the definition of
G
, we may denote
G
as
G=x_{n}^{m}\tilde{G}
where
\tilde{G}
is a polynomial of
\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u
.

Firstly, we rewrite
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}G
as

\begin{split}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}G=&\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{2}(x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})-2(N-1)\partial_{x^{\prime}}((\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})\\
&+(N-1)(N-2)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-3}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}\\
&+(N-1)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}.\end{split}

Then we estimate the terms in
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}G
one by one.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{2}(x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})\|_{H^{k}}\leq\|x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}\|_{H^{k+2}}\\
\leq&C\|\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-4)!.\end{split}

(3.21)

In getting the above inequality, we used Lemma
2.4
and induction assumption (
3.12
). And also

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}((\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})\|_{H^{k}}\leq\|(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}\|_{H^{k+1}}\\
\leq&C\|\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-1,2}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-5)!.\end{split}

(3.22)

Similar arguments yield that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-3}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}\|_{H^{k}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-6)!

(3.23)

if we notice that
\frac{\eta_{j}\eta_{l}}{\eta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
,
j,l=1,2,\cdots,n-1
. Combining the estimates (
3.21
)-(
3.23
), one gets

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}G\|_{H^{k}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-4)!.

(3.24)

In the following, we estimate
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)
. As previous, we rewrite this term as

\begin{split}&\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)\\
=&\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{2}(x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u))-2(N-1)\partial_{x^{\prime}}((\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u))\\
&+(N-1)(N-2)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-3}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)\\
&+(N-1)(\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\eta)x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u).\end{split}

(3.25)

Then one knows

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{2}(x_{n}^{m}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u))\|_{H^{k}}\leq\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}(a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\\
\leq&cr\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}}+\sum_{l=1}^{N-2}\frac{(N-2)!}{l!(N-2-l)!}\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}a^{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2-l}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\\
\leq&cr\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}}+C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.\end{split}

(3.26)

The constant
c
in the above inequality is independent of
r
.
In getting the last inequality of (
3.26
), we need to use the induction assumption (
3.12
) and do calculations as in Lemma
2.4
.
Since only the order of derivative in
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}a^{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2-l}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u
matters to the estimate, we make a convention that all the terms
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}a^{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2-l}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u
are the same for a fixed
l
.

Similarly, we estimate the other terms in
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)
and then get

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)\|_{H^{k}}\leq cr\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}}+C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.

(3.27)

For the last term
[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u
, the part
[x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u
always contains the factor
x_{n}^{m}
, we can deduce the
H^{k}
-norm for
[x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u
exactly the same as previous two terms in
\mathcal{L}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u)
.

In the following, we estimate the remaining term
[\partial_{n}^{2},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[\partial_{n}^{2},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u=2(N-1)\eta_{n}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\\
&\quad\quad+(N-1)(N-2)\eta_{n}^{2}\eta^{N-3}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u+(N-1)\eta_{nn}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u.\end{split}

(3.28)

By the definition of
\eta
, we know

\text{supp }\eta_{n}\subset[-2r,2r]^{n-1}\times[r,2r](:=Q_{r}).

From the discussion at the beginning of this section, we know
u
is analytic in
\{x_{n}>0\}
. Hence, in the following, we can always assume

\displaystyle\|\eta^{\tilde{N}-2}\partial_{x}^{\tilde{N}+2}u\|_{H^{k}(Q_{r})}\leq A_{0}A_{1}^{(\tilde{N}-4)^{+}}(\tilde{N}-4)^{+}!,\quad\forall\tilde{N}=0,1,2,\cdots.

(3.29)

Then from (
3.28
) and (
3.29
), one obtains

\|[\partial_{n}^{2},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{H^{k}}\leq CA_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.

(3.30)

Combining all the above estimates, one gets

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\leq cr\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}+C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.

By taking
r
small enough, one proves the present lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled. Suppose (
3.12
) holds for sufficiently large
A_{0},A_{1}
. Then there holds

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-i,2}}\leq C_{1}\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3-i)!,\quad i=0,1,2.

Proof.

As the proof in Lemma
3.3
, we only need to show it for
i=0
.
Recall that
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u)+\partial^{2}_{n}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u)\\
=&\quad\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}G+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)\\
&\quad\quad+[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u-mx_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{split}

(3.31)

We first consider the boundary term
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u(x^{\prime},0)
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{k+1}}\\
=&\|\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u(x^{\prime},0))\|_{H^{k}}\\
\leq&\|\partial_{n}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u(x^{\prime},0))\|_{H^{k}}+(N-1)\|\eta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}(\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u(x^{\prime},0))\|_{H^{k}}\\
\leq&C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.\end{split}

(3.32)

In the calculation of (
3.32
), the property of
\eta(x)=\chi(x_{1})\cdots\chi(x_{n})
is used in the commutation of
\partial_{n}
and
\eta^{N-1}
on
x_{n}=0
.
In getting the last inequality of (
3.32
), we used Lemma
3.3
.

In the following, we estimate the
H^{k}
-norm for
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}G,\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{x^{\prime}}u)
,
[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u,mx_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u
due to Lemma
2.2
and (
3.31
).

As in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we rewrite
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}G
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}G=&\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}(x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\tilde{G})\\
=&\partial_{nx^{\prime}}(\eta^{N-1}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})-(N-1)\partial_{n}(\eta_{x^{\prime}}\eta^{N-2}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})\\
&-(N-1)\partial_{x^{\prime}}(\eta_{n}\eta^{N-2}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G})\\
&+(N-1)(N-2)\eta_{n}\eta_{x^{\prime}}\eta^{N-3}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}+(N-1)\eta_{nx^{\prime}}\eta^{N-2}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\tilde{G}.\end{split}

(3.33)

Then all the terms on the right hand-side of (
3.33
) can be estimated as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
due to the presence of
x_{n}^{m}
. Thus we have

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}(x_{n}^{m}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\tilde{G})\|_{H^{k}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-4)!.

Similarly,
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}u_{x^{\prime}})
,
[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u
can be estimated exactly as in Lemma
3.3
. We only need to take care of
x_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N}u\\
=&\partial_{x^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)-(N-1)x_{n}^{m-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}(\eta_{x^{\prime}}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)\\
+&(N-1)(N-2)x_{n}^{m-1}\eta_{x^{\prime}}\eta_{x^{\prime}}\eta^{N-3}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u+(N-1)\eta_{x^{\prime}x^{\prime}}\eta^{N-2}x_{n}^{m-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-2}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u.\end{split}

(3.34)

For the first term
\partial_{x^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)
, we have

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{x^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)\|_{H^{k}}\leq\|x_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{H^{k+1}}\\
\leq&C_{1}\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\leq C_{2}\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.\end{split}

(3.35)

In getting the above inequality, we used Lemma
3.3
and the definition of
\widetilde{W}^{k,2}
. All the remaining terms in (
3.34
) can be estimated in a similar way.

Combining all the above estimates together yield the present lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
are fulfilled. Suppose (
3.12
) holds for sufficiently large
A_{0},A_{1}
.
For
2\leq l\leq N+1
, there holds

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k-i,2}}\leq C_{1}\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3-i)!,\quad i=0,1,2.

Proof.

As previous, we only need to show the case for
i=0
.
Recall that
\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}(\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u)+\partial^{2}_{n}(\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u)\\
=&\quad\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l-1}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}G+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l-1}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1+l}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{n}u)+[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u\\
&\quad-\eta^{N-1}\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{\min(l-1,m)}\frac{(l-1)!}{l^{\prime}!(l-1-l^{\prime})!}\partial_{n}^{l^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m})\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\end{split}

(3.36)

where

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u=[x_{n}^{m}\Delta_{x^{\prime}},\eta^{N-1}]\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u+[\partial_{n}^{2},\eta^{N-1}]\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u.

For the boundary term
\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u(x^{\prime},0)
, we distinguish with two cases:

(1)

2\leq l\leq m+1
. Then by the equation (
3.11
), we know
\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u(x^{\prime},0)\equiv 0
.

(2)

m+2\leq l\leq N+1
. Differentiating (
3.11
) with respect to
x_{n}
for
l-2
times and then with respect to
x^{\prime}
for
N+1-l
times, one gets

\begin{split}&\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u(x^{\prime},0)\\
=&-\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(x_{n}^{m}(-u_{n})^{n+2}\det D^{2}_{x^{\prime}}u)\bigg|_{x_{n}=0}\\
=&-\eta^{N-1}\frac{(l-2)!}{(l-2-m)!}\partial^{l-2-m}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(x_{n}^{m}(-u_{n})^{n+2}\det D^{2}_{x^{\prime}}u)(x^{\prime},0).\end{split}

(3.37)

Then following the same calculations as in Lemma
2.4
and using the induction assumptions, one obtains

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u(x^{\prime},0)\|_{H^{k+1}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-4)!.

(3.38)

For (
3.36
), we can deduce the
H^{k}
-norm for
\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l-1}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}G
,
[\mathcal{L},\eta^{N-1}]\partial^{l}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}u
and
\eta^{N-1}\partial^{l-1}_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1+l}(a^{ij}x_{n}^{m}\partial_{ij}\partial_{n}u)
exactly the same as previous lemmas.

Afterwards, we only need to take care of the term
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m})\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)
. This term can be discussed in the following different cases:

(1)

l=2,l^{\prime}=1
. We have

\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m})\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)=mx_{n}^{m-1}\eta^{N-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u.

Then similar arguments as (
3.34
) and by the results of Lemma
3.4
, one knows

\|\eta^{N-1}x_{n}^{m-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N-1}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u\|_{H^{k}}\leq C\tilde{A}_{0}A_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.

(2)

l\geq 3
,
1\leq l^{\prime}\leq\min(l-1,m)
. We can rewrite
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)
as

\begin{split}&\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)=\partial_{n}^{2}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)\\
-&2(N-1)\partial_{n}(\eta_{n}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)+(N-1)\eta_{nn}\eta^{N-2}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u\\
+&(N-1)(N-2)\eta_{n}^{2}\eta^{N-3}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u.\end{split}

By the induction assumption (
3.12
), we know

\begin{split}&\|\partial_{n}^{2}(\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)\|_{H^{k}}\\
\leq&\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}\Delta_{x^{\prime}}u)\|_{\widetilde{W}^{k,2}}\\
\leq&A_{0}A_{1}^{(N-3-l^{\prime})^{+}}(N-3-l^{\prime})^{+}!.\end{split}

Similar estimates also hold for the remaining terms of
\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)
by noticing that these terms contain
\eta_{n}
.

Then

\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n}u)\|_{H^{k}}\leq CA_{0}A_{1}^{(N-3-l^{\prime})^{+}}(N-3-l^{\prime})^{+}!.

This implies

\begin{split}&\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{\min(l-1,m)}\frac{(l-1)!}{l^{\prime}!(l-1-l^{\prime})!}\|\eta^{N-1}\partial_{n}^{l^{\prime}}(x_{n}^{m})\partial_{n}^{l-l^{\prime}-1}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{N+1-l}(\Delta_{x^{\prime}}\partial_{n})\|_{H^{k}}\\
\leq&CA_{0}A_{1}^{N-4}(N-3)!.\end{split}

(3.39)

Thus, applying Lemma
2.2
, one proves the present lemma.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Combining the estimates of Lemma
3.3
-
3.5

Quasilinear elliptic problems with singular nonlinearities in half-spaces
Phuong Le

Abstract
. We study the monotonicity and one-dimensional symmetry of positive solutions to the problem − Δ p ​ u = f ⁡ ( u ) -\Delta_{p}u=f(u) in ℝ + N \mathbb{R}^{N}_{+} under zero Dirichlet boundary condition, where p > 1 p>1 and f : ( 0 , + ∞ ) → ℝ f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R} is a locally Lipschitz continuous function with a possible singularity at zero. Classification results for the case f ⁡ ( u ) = 1 u γ f(u)=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}} with γ > 0 \gamma>0 are also provided.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the qualitative properties of solutions to the
p
-Laplacian problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

(1)

where
p>1
and
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function with a possible singularity at zero. As usual,
\Delta_{p}
denotes the
p
-Laplacian and the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
is defined as

\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{x:=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x_{N}>0\}.

When
f(t)
blows up as
t\to 0^{+}
, this problem is usually called a singular quasilinear elliptic problem. One may think of a prototype for (
1
) as

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\dfrac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+g(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

(2)

where
\gamma>1
and
g:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function.
The problems in half-spaces like this one are important because the half-space represents the simplest unbounded domain with an unbounded boundary. When performing a blow-up near the boundary in smooth domains, the problems often reduce to those in a half-space. This reduction is essential for understanding the behavior of solutions near boundaries in more complex domains, see
[
5
,
19
]
.

The monotonicity of solutions in the
x_{N}
-direction to problem (
1
) when
f
is not singular was studied by several authors in the literature via the moving plane method. Berestycki, Caffarelli, and Nirenberg demonstrated in
[
3
,
2
]
that if
f:[0,+\infty)
is a Lipschitz continuous function with
f(0)\geq 0
, then any positive classical solution of (
1
) with
p=2
is increasing in the
x_{N}
-direction, and additionally,
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Earlier monotonicity results for the problem (
1
) with
p=2
can also be found in the works of Dancer
[
11
,
12
]
. When
f
is only locally Lipschitz continuous on
[0,+\infty)
, similar monotonicity can be established for positive solutions that are bounded on strips, as shown in
[
20
,
38
]
. The case where
f(0)<0
is more complex, with a complete proof of monotonicity for solutions in this case being available only in dimension
N=2
in the works of Farina and Sciunzi
[
25
,
26
]
.

Studying problem (
1
) when
p\neq 2
presents several challenges, primarily due to the nonlinearity of the
p
-Laplacian for
p\neq 2
. This nonlinearity indicates that comparison principles are not equivalent to maximum principles for the
p
-Laplacian. Additionally, the operator’s singular or degenerate nature (corresponding to
1<p<2
and
p>2
, respectively) leads to a lack of
C^{2}
regularity in the solutions at their critical points. Overcoming these difficulties, the moving plane method for problem (
1
) has been extensively developed in a series of papers by Farina, Montoro, Sciunzi, and their collaborators
[
21
,
22
,
17
,
24
,
23
]
. Assuming that
f
is positive on
(0,+\infty)
, they proved the monotonicity of solutions in the
x_{N}
-direction for the case
1<p<2
in
[
21
,
22
]
. For
p>2
, the monotonicity result was established in
[
24
,
23
]
under the conditions that
f
is either sublinear or superlinear. Recently, it was shown in
[
17
]
that when
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
, the requirement for
f
to be positive can be relaxed.
In all of these works, it is assumed that
f:[0,+\infty)
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function and solutions
u
to (
1
) satisfy
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
and

|\nabla u|\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\quad\text{ for all }\lambda>0,

(3)

where the sets
\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid 0<x_{N}<\lambda\}
are called strips. By the mean value theorem and the Dirichlet boundary condition, one can check that assumption (
3
) implies

u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\text{ for all }\lambda>0\text{ and }\lim_{x_{N}\to 0^{+}}u(x^{\prime},x_{N})=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{+}.

(4)

Conversely, if
u
satisfies (
4
), then
u
and
f(u)
are bounded in each strip
\Sigma_{\lambda}
and the gradient bound (
3
) can be obtained via standard elliptic estimates (see
[
15
,
40
]
).

To the best of our knowledge, problem (
1
) with a singular nonlinearity such as (
2
) has not been well studied in the literature, except for the case
p=2
(see
[
36
,
37
,
31
]
). Compared to the regular nonlinearity, the difficulty in studying this problem is magnified by the fact that the gradient of solutions to singular problems usually exhibits singularity on the boundary of the domain (see
[
28
]
). Hence it is not appropriate to enforce assumptions
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
as well as (
3
) when studying (
1
) or (
2
). Instead, we are interested in qualitative properties of weak solutions
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
which satisfy assumption (
4
). By weak solutions, we mean

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}f(u)\varphi\quad\text{ for all }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}).

On the other hand, the
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
regularity is a natural one taking into account the standard regularity results in
[
15
,
40
,
35
]
. Moreover, we have the following criterion for the uniform convergence of
u
near the boundary mentioned in (
4
).

Proposition 1
.

Assume that
p>1
and
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\rho)
for some
\rho>0
.
Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to (
1
). If
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})}<\rho
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
, then

\lim_{x_{N}\to 0^{+}}u(x^{\prime},x_{N})=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{+}.

As we can see in problem (
2
), the assumption that the nonlinearity is strictly decreasing near zero is reasonable for singular problems. To state our main results, we denote by
Z_{f}
the set of zeros of
f
in
(0,+\infty)
, that is,

Z_{f}:=\{t\in(0,+\infty)\mid f(t)=0\}.

Our main idea in tackling (
1
) or (
2
) is to isolate a small strip
\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
where the singular phenomenon may appear. We show that
u
is monotone increasing in this strip via a weak comparison principle. Then in the remaining area
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
, we basically combine the techniques in
[
21
,
22
,
17
,
24
,
23
]
to address the monotonicity of solutions to (
1
) in the full domain. Our first result is the following:.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that

(i)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}f(t)>0
,

(ii)

f
is strictly decreasing on
(0,t_{0})
for some
t_{0}>0
,

(iii)

either
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
and
Z_{f}
is a discrete set, or
p>1
and
f(t)>0
for
t>0
.

Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to problem (
1
) satisfying (
4
).
Then
u
is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
.

Remark 1
.

Once the monotonicity of
u
is obtained, we may argue as in
[
30
, Lemma 14]
to further derive

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus(Z_{f(u)}\cap Z_{u})

provided that
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
, where
Z_{f(u)}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid f(u(x))=0\}
and
Z_{u}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid|\nabla u(x)|=0\}
(see also
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
).

If
g:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz continuous, then
t\mapsto\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)
is strictly decreasing on
(0,t_{0})
for some
t_{0}>0
.
If we further assume that either
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
and
\{t\in(0,+\infty)\mid\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)=0\}
is a discrete set, or
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)>0
for
t>0
. Then Theorem
2
and Proposition
1
indicate that every solution
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
to problem (
2
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})
for all
\lambda>0
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})}<t_{0}
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
. By Remark
1
, we further derive
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0
provided that
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
and
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)>0
for
t>0
.

In fact, due to the appearance of the explicit singular term
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}
, we would expect stronger results on the monotonicity of solutions to problem (
2
), in particular, on their behavior near the boundary. In
[
19
]
Esposito and Sciunzi showed that inward directional derivatives near the boundary of solutions to problem (
2
) posed in a bounded domain is necessarily positive for all
p>1
regardless of the sign of
t\mapsto\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)
. In the next theorem, we not only prove a similar claim and a monotonicity result for (
2
) but also provide a sharp estimate on derivatives, which indicates that they must blow up at a proper rate near the boundary.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
p>1
,
\gamma>1
and
g:[0,+\infty)
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function.
Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to problem (
2
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})
for all
\lambda>0
. Then for every
\beta\in(0,1)
, there exist
c_{1},c_{2},\lambda_{0}>0
such that

c_{1}x_{N}^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}<\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\eta}<c_{2}x_{N}^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}

(5)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
with
(\eta,e_{N})\geq\beta
, where
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap\partial B_{1}(0)
and
e_{N}:=(0,\dots,0,1)
. If we further assume that either
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
and
\{t\in(0,+\infty)\mid\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)=0\}
is a discrete set, or
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)>0
for
t>0
, then
u
is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
. Moreover, we have
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
provided that
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
and
\frac{1}{t^{\gamma}}+g(t)>0
for
t>0
.

The one-dimensional (1D) symmetry of solutions to problem (
1
), commonly referred to as a rigidity result in the literature, has been explored in the semilinear case where
p=2
by Berestycki, Caffarelli, and Nirenberg
[
4
,
2
]
, Angenent
[
1
]
, and Clément and Sweers
[
6
]
. However, this topic is not well understood in the case
p\neq 2
. For
p\neq 2
, there are some results in lower dimensions under the condition that the solutions and their gradients are bounded, as seen in
[
10
]
for the case
N=2
,
p>\frac{3}{2}
, and
[
23
,
22
]
for
N=3
,
p>\frac{8}{5}
. In higher dimensions, Du and Guo
[
16
]
have addressed the 1D symmetry of bounded positive solutions to (
1
), assuming the condition that
f(t)>0
for all
0<t<1
,
f(t)<0
for all
t>1
and
f(t)\geq c_{0}t^{p-1}
in
(0,\sigma)
for some
c_{0},\sigma>0
. Under this assumption, the uniqueness of bounded solutions to (
1
) is ensured by the method of sub-super solutions, leading to the symmetry of solutions due to the symmetry of the domain. A more general rigidity result for bounded solutions was obtained recently in
[
33
]
using a similar method. We also mention the excellent work
[
18
]
, where various maximum and comparison principles were exploited with the moving plane method to show the 1D symmetry of bounded solutions to a
p
-Laplace equation in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with uniform limits. Later, these analytic tools were resorted to a more convenient sliding method to study similar problems in the whole and a half-space
[
32
,
29
]
. In this paper, we exploit such a sliding method to prove the following rigidity result.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
and
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that

(i)

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{t^{p-1}}>0
,

(ii)

f(t)>0
for
t>0
,

(iii)

f
is strictly decreasing on
(t_{0},+\infty)
for some
t_{0}>0
.

Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to problem (
1
) satisfying (
4
) and

\lim_{x_{N}\to+\infty}|\nabla u(x^{\prime},x_{N})|=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{+}.

(6)

Then
u
depends only on
x_{N}
and is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
.

4
) is useful for problems with positive nonlinearities. For sign-changing nonlinearities, we have the following partial result.

is a nonempty discrete set,

be a solution to problem (

) satisfying (

), (

) and

\liminf_{x_{N}\to+\infty}u(x^{\prime},x_{N})>t_{0}\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

(7)

Then

.

As a consequence of Theorem

, we have the following result for nonlinearities that change sign once.

.

Assume that
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
and
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that

(i)

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(t)}{t^{p-1}}>0
,

(ii)

f(t)>0
in

,

,

be a bounded solution to problem (

) satisfying (

). Then

depends only on

and is monotone increasing in

. Moreover,
0<u<t_{0}

If we further assume

\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{s}|h(t)|dt\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}ds<\infty

(8)

for some

and some continuous function

for all

Now we consider a special case where
f
is strictly decreasing in the whole domain. In such a case, we can remove the restriction on

.

.

Assume that
p>1
and

,

) with

.

.

Moreover, if either (

Lastly, we focus our attention on problem (
2
) with
g\equiv 0
and
\gamma>0
. This problem is of particular interest due to its application in blow-up analysis (see
[
19
]
). For this problem, we have the following classification result for
1<p<N
:

Theorem 8
.

Assume
1<p<N
. Let
\gamma>1
and let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\dfrac{1}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\end{cases}

(9)

with

u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})\text{ for some }\overline{\lambda}>0.

(10)

Then
u
is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
and

\limsup_{x_{N}\to+\infty}\frac{u(x^{\prime},x_{N})}{x_{N}}<+\infty\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{+}.

(11)

If we further assume that

is sublinear in the sense that

\lim_{x_{N}\to+\infty}\frac{u(x^{\prime},x_{N})}{x_{N}}=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{+},

then

u(x)\equiv\left[\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}x_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

If else

, then (
9

u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
satisfying (

).

To prove Theorem
8
, we extend some estimates in
[
36
]
from
p=2
to
p\neq 2
, then we apply Theorem
7
.
Theorem
8
improves a classification result in
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
, where the exact asymptotic behavior

cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq u(x)\leq Cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

is assumed instead of (
12
). We stress that assumption (
12
) is sharp in the sense that solutions which do not satisfy (

) do exist (see Theorem

). We recall that all solutions to problem (
9
) when
p=2
were classified in
[
36
]
without assumption (

) and in
[
37
]
without also (

). The key tools available in the case
p=2
to study such a problem are a maximum principle for bounded solutions in unbounded domains (see
[

) and the Kelvin transform. It seems not to be easy to extend such a result to the case

due to the lack of the Kelvin transform for the
p
-Laplacian and the nonlinear nature of this operator. Nevertheless, such a transform is available for the
N
-Laplacian besides the Laplacian one. For this reason, we can utilize the Kelvin transform to classify all solutions to (
9
) with

without assumptions (

) and (

).

Theorem 9
.

Let
\gamma>1
and let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{N}u=\dfrac{1}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\

(13)

Then either

If else

, then (

) has no solution in

.

Moreover, we can classify all solutions to (

The proof of Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

We always assume that
\Omega
is a connected domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
f
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function.
In the quasilinear case, the maximum principle is not equivalent to the comparison one. Therefore, we also need to recall the classical version of the strong comparison principle for
p
-Laplace equations.

Theorem 11

(Strong comparison principle 1
[
7
]
)
.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}(\Omega)
be two solutions to

-\Delta_{p}w=f(w)\quad\text{ in }\Omega

such that
u\leq v
in
\Omega
, with
p>1
and let

Z=\{x\in\Omega\mid|\nabla u(x)|+|\nabla v(x)|=0\}.

If
x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus Z
and
u(x_{0})=v(x_{0})
, then
u=v
in the connected component of
\Omega\setminus Z
containing
x_{0}
.

Theorem
11
only holds far from the degenerate set. Now we present a result that holds, under stronger assumptions, on the entire domain
\Omega
.

Theorem 12

(Strong comparison principle 2
[
8
]
)
.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be two solutions to

-\Delta_{p}w=f(w)\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

where
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
. Assume
u\leq v
in
\Omega
and at least one of the following two conditions holds:

(i)

either

f(u(x))>0\quad\text{ in }\overline{\Omega}

or

f(u(x))<0\quad\text{ in }\overline{\Omega},

(ii)

either

f(v(x))>0\quad\text{ in }\overline{\Omega}

or

f(v(x))<0\quad\text{ in }\overline{\Omega}.

Then either
u=v
in
\Omega
or
u<v
in
\Omega
.

In the situations where the above strong comparison principles do not apply, we will make use of the following weak sweeping principle by Dancer and Du.

Theorem 13

(Weak sweeping principle
[
14
]
)
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
(x,s)\mapsto h(x,s)
is measurable in
x\in\Omega
,
continuous in
s
, and for each finite interval
J
, there exists a continuous increasing function
L(s)
such
that
s\mapsto h(x,s)+L(s)
is nondecreasing in
s
for
s\in J
and
x\in\Omega
. Let
u_{t}
and
v_{t}
,
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, be functions in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfy in the weak sense,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u_{t}\geq h(x,u_{t})+\varepsilon_{1}(t)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p}v_{t}\leq h(x,v_{t})-\varepsilon_{2}(t)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{t}\geq v_{t}+\varepsilon&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
\end{cases}

for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, where

\varepsilon_{1}(t)+\varepsilon_{2}(t)\geq\varepsilon>0.

Moreover, suppose that
u_{t_{0}}\geq v_{t_{0}}
in
\Omega
for some
t_{0}\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
t\mapsto u_{t}
,
t\mapsto v_{t}
are continuous from the finite closed interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
to
C(\overline{\Omega})
. Then

u_{t}\geq v_{t}\text{ in }\Omega\text{ for all }t\in[t_{1},t_{2}].

The statement of Theorem
13
is taken from
[
14
]
. The proof of this theorem is almost identical to that of
[
13
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Throughout the paper, we denote generic positive constants by
C
(with dependent subscripts in some cases) and they will be allowed to vary within a single line or formula. We also denote by
f^{+}
the positive part of a function
f
, that is,
f^{+}=\max\{f,0\}
and by
B_{R}
,
B_{R}^{\prime}
the open balls of radius
R>0
centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, respectively. For brevity, we drop

in the integral notations when it is clear from the context.

2.1.
Weak comparison principle for strips

The aim of this section is the following weak comparison principle, which can be applied to problems with singular nonlinearities.

Proposition 14
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f
is strictly decreasing on
(0,t_{0})
for some
t_{0}>0
and let
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{\lambda}
with
\lambda>0
. Assume that
u,v\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Sigma)\cap C(\overline{\Sigma})
satisfy

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
-\Delta_{p}v=f(v)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
0<u<t_{0}&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
v>\delta&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
u\leq v&\text{ on }\partial\Sigma\end{cases}

(15)

for some
\delta>0
. Then
u\leq v
in
\Sigma
.

To prove Proposition
14
, we need the following elementary lemma, which would appear somewhere in the literature. However, we cannot find a suitable reference. Therefore, we provide a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 15
.

Let
-\infty\leq m<M\leq+\infty
.
If
g:(m,M)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and strictly decreasing, then

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\in[l_{1},l_{2}]\\
t_{2}-t_{1}\geq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{g(t_{2})-g(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}<0

(16)

for every
\varepsilon>0
and every interval
[l_{1},l_{2}]\subset(m,M)
with
l_{2}-l_{1}\geq\varepsilon
.

If we further assume that
-\infty<m<M=+\infty
and
g
is differentiable with

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}g^{\prime}(t)<0,

(17)

then

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\in(m,+\infty)\\
t_{2}-t_{1}\geq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{g(t_{2})-g(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}<0

(18)

for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that (
16
) does not hold, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
, an interval
[l_{1},l_{2}]\subset(m,M)
with
l_{2}-l_{1}\geq\varepsilon
and two sequences
(b_{n}),(c_{n})
such that
l_{1}\leq b_{n}\leq c_{n}\leq l_{2}
,
c_{n}-b_{n}\geq\varepsilon
and

\frac{g(c_{n})-g(b_{n})}{c_{n}-b_{n}}\to 0.

(19)

Up to a subsequence,
b_{n}\to b
and
c_{n}\to c
with
b,c\in[l_{1},l_{2}]
such that
c-b\geq\varepsilon
. Consequently, (
19
) implies

\frac{g(c)-g(b)}{c-b}=0.

This is a contradiction with the assumption that
g
is strictly decreasing. Hence (
16
) is proved.

Now we assume that (
18
) does not hold for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then we can find three sequences
(a_{n}),(b_{n}),(c_{n})
such that
m\leq b_{n}\leq a_{n}\leq c_{n}
,
c_{n}-b_{n}\geq\varepsilon
and

g^{\prime}(a_{n})=\frac{g(c_{n})-g(b_{n})}{c_{n}-b_{n}}\to 0.

(20)

This implies that
(a_{n})
is bounded. Therefore,
(b_{n})
is also bounded. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume
b_{n}\to b\in[m,+\infty)
and
c_{n}\to c\in[m,+\infty]
with
c-b\geq\varepsilon
if
c<+\infty
. Consequently, (
20
) implies

\frac{g(c)-g(b)}{c-b}=0\quad\text{ if }c<+\infty,

\frac{g(c_{n})-g(b)}{c_{n}-b}\to 0\quad\text{ if }c_{n}\to+\infty.

However, the former contradicts the fact that
g
is strictly decreasing, while the latter contradicts (
17
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

Due to Lemma
15
, the assumptions (1.5) in
[
29
]
and (F2) in
[
32
]
can be reduced to the requirement that
f
is strictly decreasing in the corresponding intervals.

For later use, we recall the following elementary inequalities

\displaystyle(|\xi|^{p-2}\xi-|\xi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\xi^{\prime},\xi-\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}(|\xi|+|\xi^{\prime}|)^{p-2}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{2},

(21)

\displaystyle\left||\xi|^{p-2}\xi-|\xi^{\prime}|^{p-2}\xi^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(|\xi|+|\xi^{\prime}|)^{p-2}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|,

(22)

which hold for all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\xi|+|\xi^{\prime}|>0
, where
p>1
and
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depend only on
N
and
p
.

Now we provide a proof of Proposition
14
.

Proof of Proposition
14
.

For each
R>0
, let
\varphi_{R}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be a standard cutoff function, which satisfies

\begin{cases}0\leq\varphi_{R}\leq 1&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\\
\varphi_{R}=1&\text{ in }B_{R}^{\prime},\\
\varphi_{R}=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{2R}^{\prime},\\
|\nabla\varphi_{R}|\leq\frac{2}{R}&\text{ in }B_{2R}^{\prime}\setminus B_{R}^{\prime},\end{cases}

(23)

where we recall that
B_{r}^{\prime}
is the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
of radius
r
and center at the origin.

Fix some
\alpha>N-2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then we set
w=(u-v-\varepsilon)^{+}
and

\psi(x):=w^{\alpha}(x)\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}(x^{\prime})\chi_{\Sigma}(x).

Since the support of
\psi
is compactly contained in
\Sigma
, we can use
\psi
as a test function in the equations
-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)
and
-\Delta_{p}v=f(v)
. Then subtracting, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla w)w^{\alpha-1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}

(24)

\displaystyle+(\alpha+1)\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi_{R})w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(f(u)-f(v)\right)w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}.

Using (
21
) and (
22
), we deduce from (
24
) that

\displaystyle\alpha C_{1}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}w^{\alpha-1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}

(25)

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+1)C_{2}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{p-2}|\nabla w||\nabla\varphi_{R}|w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha}+\int_{\Sigma}\left(f(u)-f(v)\right)w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+1)C_{2}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi_{R}|w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha}+\int_{\Sigma}\left(f(u)-f(v)\right)w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}.

In the set
{\Sigma}\cap\{w>0\}
, we have

\delta<v<v+\varepsilon<u<t_{0}.

(26)

Since
f
is strictly decreasing on
(0,t_{0})
, Lemma
15
gives

f(u)-f(v)\leq-C_{\varepsilon}(u-v)\leq-C_{\varepsilon}w\quad\text{ in }{\Sigma}\cap\{w>0\}

(27)

for some
C_{\varepsilon}>0
.
On the other hand, from (
26
) we have that
f(u)
and
f(v)
are bounded. Hence the standard gradient estimate yields

|\nabla u|<C_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla v|<C_{0}\quad\text{ in }{\Sigma}\cap\{w>0\}.

(28)

Substituting (
27
), (
28
) into (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha C_{1}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}w^{\alpha-1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+1)C_{2}(2C_{0})^{p-1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi_{R}|w^{\alpha}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha}-C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma}w^{\alpha+1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}.

Applying the weighted Young inequality with exponents
\alpha+1
and
\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha C_{1}\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}w^{\alpha-1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{[(\alpha+1)C_{2}(2C_{0})^{p-1}]^{\alpha+1}}{(\alpha+1)(\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}C_{\varepsilon})^{\alpha}}|\nabla\varphi_{R}|^{\alpha+1}+C_{\varepsilon}w^{\alpha+1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}\right)-C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma}w^{\alpha+1}\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{N-\alpha-2}.

Since
\alpha>N-2
, by letting
R\to+\infty
, we derive

\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{p-2}|\nabla w|^{2}w^{\alpha-1}=0.

This yields
u\leq v+\varepsilon
in
{\Sigma}
.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that
u\leq v
in
\Sigma
.
∎

Remark 3
.

It is clear from the proof that Proposition
14
still holds true if we replace the first equation of (
15
) by

-\Delta_{p}u\leq f(u)\text{ in }\Sigma,\quad|\nabla u|\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma\cap\{u>\varepsilon\})\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0,

or replace the second equation of (
15
) by

-\Delta_{p}v\geq f(v)\text{ in }\Sigma,\quad|\nabla v|\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma).

As an application of Proposition
14
, we prove Proposition
1
, which provides a criterion for the uniform convergence of solutions to zero as
x_{N}\to 0^{+}
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Let
h:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
function such that

\displaystyle\max\{f(t),0\}<h(t)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }(0,\rho),

\displaystyle h(t)=\frac{c}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }[\rho,+\infty)

for some
c>0
.
We set
H(t)=\int_{\rho}^{t}h(s)ds
for
t\geq 0
, then
H
is strictly increasing in
(0,+\infty)
and
H(t)<\int_{\rho}^{+\infty}h(s)ds=\frac{c}{\rho}
. For each
\mu\geq\frac{c}{\rho}
, we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{ds}{\left[\mu-H(s)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}=+\infty\text{ and }\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\left[\mu-H(s)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}<+\infty\text{ for }0<t<+\infty,

which is due to

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{ds}{\left[\mu-H(s)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}>\int_{\rho}^{+\infty}\frac{ds}{\mu^{\frac{1}{p}}}\text{ and }\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\left[\mu-H(s)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}<\frac{t}{\left[\mu-H(t)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Hence the formula

\int_{0}^{w_{\mu}(t)}\frac{ds}{\left[\mu-H(s)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}}=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}t\quad\text{ for all }t\geq 0

uniquely define a function
w_{\mu}:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, which is a
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}})
solution to the ODE problem

\begin{cases}-(|w^{\prime}|^{p-2}w^{\prime})^{\prime}=h(w)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
w(t)>0,~w^{\prime}(t)>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
w(0)=0.\end{cases}

Moreover,
\lim_{\mu\to+\infty}w_{\mu}(t)=+\infty
for all
t>0
.

We fix some
\mu>0
such that
w_{\mu}(\overline{\lambda})>\rho
. Then we choose
\lambda_{0}<\overline{\lambda}
satisfying
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})}<w_{\mu}(\lambda_{0})<\rho
. By abuse of notation, we will write
w_{\mu}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=w_{\mu}(x_{N})
. Then
0<w_{\mu}<\rho
in
\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}
and
u<w_{\mu}
on
\{x_{N}=\lambda_{0}\}
.

For small
\varepsilon>0
such that
w_{\mu}(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)<\rho
, we define

w_{\mu,\varepsilon}(x):=w_{\mu}(x+\varepsilon e_{N}).

Then

\begin{cases}w_{\mu}(\varepsilon)<w_{\mu,\varepsilon}<w_{\mu}(\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon)<\rho&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}},\\
-\Delta_{p}w_{\mu,\varepsilon}=h(w_{\mu,\varepsilon})>f(w_{\mu,\varepsilon})&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}},\\
u\leq w_{\mu,\varepsilon}&\text{ on }\partial\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}.\end{cases}

Now Proposition
14
implies
u\leq w_{\mu,\varepsilon}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have
u\leq w_{\mu}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}
and the conclusion follows from that fact that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}w_{\mu}(t)=0
.
∎

2.2.
A priori bounds for solutions

Motivated by
[
36
]
, we prove some a priori bounds for solutions to (
1
). The following lemma provides an upper bound for solutions near the boundary.

Lemma 16
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f(t)<\frac{c_{0}}{t^{\gamma}}
for all
0<t<t_{0}
, where
c_{0},t_{0}>0
,
\gamma>1
.
Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to (
1
) with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})}<+\infty
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
. Then

u(x)\leq Cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}

for some constants
C>0
. In particular, such a solution satisfies (
4
).

Proof.

Setting

M=\max\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})},t_{0}\},

then, by continuity, there exists
c_{1}\geq c_{0}
such that
f(t)<\frac{c_{1}}{t^{\gamma}}
for all
0<t<M
.
Let

w(t):=\left[\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}t^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}},

then
v_{s}(x):=sw(x_{N})
solves
-\Delta_{p}v_{s}=\frac{s^{\gamma+p-1}}{v_{s}^{\gamma}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. We choose sufficiently large
s
such that
s^{\gamma+p-1}\geq c_{1}
and
sw(\overline{\lambda})\geq M
. For small
\varepsilon>0
, we define

v_{s,\varepsilon}(x):=v_{s}(x+\varepsilon e_{N})=sw(x_{N}+\varepsilon).

We have

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)<\frac{c_{1}}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}},\\
-\Delta_{p}v_{s,\varepsilon}=\frac{s^{\gamma+p-1}}{v_{s,\varepsilon}^{\gamma}}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{v_{s,\varepsilon}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}},\\
0<u<M+1&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}},\\
sw(\varepsilon)<v_{s,\varepsilon}<sw(\overline{\lambda}+\varepsilon)&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}},\\
u\leq v_{s}&\text{ on }\partial\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}.\end{cases}

We know that
|\nabla u|\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}\cap\{u>\varepsilon\})\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0
by the standard regularity estimate.
Therefore, Proposition
14
and Remark
3
imply
u\leq v_{s,\varepsilon}
in
\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude the proof.
∎

The following lemma provides a lower bound for solutions.

Lemma 17
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f(t)>\frac{c_{0}}{t^{\gamma}}
for all
0<t<t_{0}
, where
c_{0},t_{0}>0
,
\gamma\geq 0
. Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to (
1
). Then

u(x)\geq\min\{Cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}},t_{0}\}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

for some constant
C>0
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\phi_{1}\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{1}})
be the first eigenvalue and a corresponding positive eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
B_{1}
, namely,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\phi_{1}=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}^{p-1}&\text{ in }B_{1},\\
\phi_{1}>0&\text{ in }B_{1},\\
\phi_{1}=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

Setting

w=s\phi_{1}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}},

where
s>0
will be chosen later. Direct calculation yields that in the weak sense

-\Delta_{p}w=\frac{\alpha(x)}{w^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }B_{1},

where

\alpha(x):=s^{\gamma+p-1}\left(\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left[\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}|\nabla\phi_{1}(x)|^{p}+\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}(x)^{p}\right].

Now we fix
s>0
such that
\sup_{x\in B_{1}}\alpha(x)\leq c_{0}
and hence

-\Delta_{p}w\leq\frac{c_{0}}{w^{\gamma}}\text{ in }B_{1}.

Let
R_{0}>0
be such that
R_{0}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}w(0)=t_{0}
.

For any
0<R\leq R_{0}
and
x_{0}=(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
with
x_{0,N}\geq R+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, we set

w_{x_{0},R}(x):=R^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}w\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{R}\right)\quad\text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0}).

Then

w_{x_{0},R}\leq t_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\Delta_{p}w_{x_{0},R}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{w_{x_{0},R}^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0}).

On the other hand, since
w_{x_{0},R}=0<u
on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, we can use
(w_{x_{0},R}-u)^{+}\chi_{B_{R}(x_{0})}
as a test function in

-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)\quad\text{ and }\quad-\Delta_{p}w_{x_{0},R}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{w_{x_{0},R}^{\gamma}}

to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(|\nabla w_{x_{0},R}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{x_{0},R}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(w_{x_{0},R}-u)^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\frac{c_{0}}{w_{x_{0},R}^{\gamma}}-f(u)\right)(w_{x_{0},R}-u)^{+}.

In
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\{w_{x_{0},R}>u\}
we have
f(u)\geq\frac{c_{0}}{u^{\gamma}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(|\nabla w_{x_{0},R}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{x_{0},R}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(w_{x_{0},R}-u)^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\frac{c_{0}}{w_{x_{0},R}^{\gamma}}-\frac{c_{0}}{u^{\gamma}}\right)(w_{x_{0},R}-u)^{+}\leq 0.

By (
21
), this implies

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(|\nabla w_{x_{0},R}|+|\nabla u|)^{p-2}|\nabla(w_{x_{0},R}-u)^{+}|^{2}\leq 0.

Hence
u\geq w_{x_{0},R}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
with
x_{0,N}\geq R+\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we deduce

u\geq w_{x_{0},R}\text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0})\text{ for all }0<R\leq R_{0}\text{ and }x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\text{ with }x_{0,N}\geq R.

In particular, if
x_{0,N}=R<R_{0}
, then

u(x_{0})\geq w_{x_{0},R}(x_{0})=w(0)R^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}=w(0)x_{0,N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

If
x_{0,N}\geq R=R_{0}
, then

u(x_{0})\geq w_{x_{0},R}(x_{0})=w(0)R_{0}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}=t_{0}.

The conclusion follows from the fact that
x_{0}
is chosen arbitrarily in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.
∎

We still have a lower bound under weaker assumptions on
f
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f(t)>c_{0}t^{p-1}
for all
0<t<t_{0}
, where
c_{0},t_{0}>0
. Let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to (
1
).
Then

u(x)\geq\min\{Cx_{N},t_{0}\}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

for some constant
C>0
.

A weaker result was proved in
[
30
]
exploiting the weak sweeping principle (see also
[
23
, Lemma 3]
for the case that
p>2
and
f
is positive).
More precisely, Lemma 10 in
[
30
]
is stated for nonlinearity
f
that is continuous at zero and the conclusion there does not provide an explicit lower bound for
u
. To get a stronger result, we still use the weak sweeping principle, but in a different way.

Proof of Lemma
18
.

Let
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\phi_{1}\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{1}})
be the first eigenvalue and the corresponding positive eigenfunction of the
p
-Laplacian in
B_{1}
such that
\phi_{1}(0)=t_{0}
. We take
R=\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{1}}{c_{0}}}
and set
\phi_{R}(x)=\phi_{1}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)
, then

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}\phi_{R}=\frac{c_{0}}{2}\phi_{R}^{p-1}&\text{ in }B_{R},\\
\phi_{R}>0&\text{ in }B_{R},\\
\phi_{R}=0&\text{ on }\partial B_{R}.\end{cases}

Since
\phi_{R}
is radially symmetric and by abuse of notation, we may write
\phi_{R}(x)=\phi_{R}(|x|)
.
For each
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{R}
we set

\phi_{R}^{x_{0}}(x)=\phi_{R}(x-x_{0})\quad\text{ for }x\in B_{R}(x_{0}).

We will show that

u\geq\phi_{R}^{x_{0}}\text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0})\quad\text{ for every }x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{R}.

(29)

To this end, we let any
x_{0}:=(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{R}
.

We only consider the case
x_{0,N}>R
since the case
x_{0,N}=R
can be obtained by continuity. Let
s_{0}\in(0,1)
be such that
\delta:=\min_{\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}}u>s_{0}\phi_{R}^{x_{0}}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
and let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
\phi_{R}(R-\varepsilon)<\frac{\delta}{2}
. We denote
\tilde{\phi}_{s}=s\phi_{R}^{x_{0}}
. Then for all
s\in[s_{0},1]
, we have

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }B_{R-\varepsilon},\\
-\Delta_{p}\tilde{\phi}_{s}=\frac{c_{0}}{2}\tilde{\phi}_{s}^{p-1}\leq f(s\phi_{R}^{x_{0}})-\gamma&\text{ in }B_{R-\varepsilon},\\
u\geq\tilde{\phi}_{s}+\frac{\delta}{2}&\text{ on }\partial B_{R-\varepsilon},\end{cases}

where

\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\min_{[s_{0}\phi_{R}(R-\varepsilon),t_{0}]}f>0.

Moreover,
u>\tilde{\phi}_{s_{0}}
in
B_{R-\varepsilon}
. Thus we can apply the weak sweeping principle (Theorem
13
) to deduce that
u\geq\tilde{\phi}_{s}
in
B_{R-\varepsilon}
for all
s\in[s_{0},1]
. In particular,
u\geq\tilde{\phi}_{1}=\phi_{R}^{x_{0}}
in
B_{R-\varepsilon}
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, (
29
) must hold. This implies

u(x)\geq\begin{cases}\phi_{R}(R-x_{N})&\text{ if }x_{N}<R,\\
\phi_{R}(0)&\text{ if }x_{N}\geq R.\end{cases}

The conclusion follows immediately from the fact that
\phi_{R}^{\prime}(R)<0
and
\phi_{R}(0)=t_{0}
.
∎

3.
Monotonicity of solutions

For
\lambda>0
, we define

u_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{N}):=u(x^{\prime},2\lambda-x_{N}),

which is obtained by reflecting
u
with respect to the hyperplane
T_{\lambda}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x_{N}=\lambda\}
. The following proposition allows us to initiate the moving plane procedure.

Proposition 19
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2
, we have

u\leq u_{\lambda}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda}\quad\text{ for all }0<\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda},

where
\overline{\lambda}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\lambda}>0
be such that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})}<t_{0}
.
Using Lemma
17
with
\gamma=0
, one may check that for
0<\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda}
,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda},\\
-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda}=f(u_{\lambda})&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda},\\
0<u<t_{0}&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda},\\
\min\{C\lambda^{\frac{p}{p-1}},t_{1}\}\leq u_{\lambda}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2\lambda})}&\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda},\\
u\leq u_{\lambda}&\text{ on }\partial\Sigma_{\lambda},\end{cases}

where
t_{1}>0
.
Hence, Proposition
14
yields
u\leq u_{\lambda}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}
for all
0<\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Due to Proposition
19
, the set

\Lambda:=\left\{\lambda>0\mid u\leq u_{\mu}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\mu}\text{ for all }0<\mu\leq\lambda\right\}

is nonempty. Thus, we can define

\lambda_{0}=\sup\Lambda.

(30)

To obtain the monotonicity of
u
, it suffices to show that
\lambda_{0}=+\infty
. By contradiction arguments, we assume
\lambda_{0}<+\infty
. Then
u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}
. We can reach a contradiction by showing that for some small
\varepsilon>0
we have

u\leq u_{\lambda}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda}\quad\text{ for all }\lambda_{0}<\lambda<\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon.

Due to Lemma
17
(with
\gamma=0
), there exist
\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\delta}>0
small such that

u+\tilde{\delta}\leq u_{\lambda}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}\quad\text{ for all }\lambda>\lambda_{0}.

Therefore, we only need to show that

u\leq u_{\lambda}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}\quad\text{ for all }\lambda_{0}<\lambda<\lambda_{0}+\varepsilon

(31)

for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
By Lemma
17
again, we know that

\min\{C\tilde{\lambda}^{\frac{p}{p-1}},t_{1}\}\leq u,u_{\lambda}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2\lambda})}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}.

Hence
f(u)
and
f(u_{\lambda})
are bounded in
\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
. Therefore, by standard gradient elliptic estimates, we have
|\nabla u|,|\nabla u_{\lambda}|\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}})
for every
\lambda>0
. Hence, we can repeat the techniques in
[
21
,
22
,
24
,
23
,
17
]
, which are based on various comparison principles and compactness arguments for problems with a regular nonlinearity, to prove (
31
). More precisely, if
f
is positive and
1<p<2
, we use the arguments in
[
21
]
. If
f
is positive and
p>2
, we follow the ones in
[
23
]
. When
f
is sign-changing and
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
, we argue as in
[
17
]
(see also
[
34
,
30
]
for simplified arguments).

The details, therefore, will be omitted.
∎

Next, we prove Theorem
3
. Our proof is motivated by the scaling technique in
[
39
]
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Since
g:[0,+\infty)
is a locally Lipschitz continuous, there exist
t_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\frac{c_{1}}{t^{\gamma}}<f(t)<\frac{c_{2}}{t^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }(0,t_{0}).

Hence Lemmas
16
and
17
imply the existence of
\lambda_{0},c,C>0
such that

cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq u(x)\leq Cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}.

(32)

Let any
A>a>0
and any positive sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We define

w_{n}(x):=\varepsilon_{n}^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u(\varepsilon_{n}x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

For
n
sufficiently large, we deduce from (
32
)

ca^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq w_{n}(x)\leq Cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq CA^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a}.

(33)

In particular,
(w_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a})
and it solves

-\Delta_{p}w_{n}=\frac{1}{w_{n}^{\gamma}}+\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{\gamma p}{\gamma+p-1}}g(\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}w_{n})\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

(34)

By the standard regularity
[
27
]
,
(w_{n})
is also uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a})
and in
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a}})
, for
0<\alpha<1
. Since

|\nabla w_{n}(x)|=\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}|\nabla u(\varepsilon_{n}x)|\geq\varepsilon_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(\varepsilon_{n}x)}{\partial\eta},

for
\varepsilon_{n}
sufficiently small, we get the
estimate from above in (
5
).

Now we prove the estimate from below. Suppose by contradiction that there exist
\beta>0
, a sequence of normal vectors
\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
with
(\eta_{n},e_{N})\geq\beta
and a sequence of points
x_{n}=(x_{n}^{\prime},x_{n,N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
such that

x_{n,N}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p-1}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\eta_{n}}\to 0\text{ and }x_{n,N}\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(35)

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume
\eta_{n}\to\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
with
(\eta,e_{N})\geq\beta
as
n\to\infty
.
We define
w_{n}
as above with
\varepsilon_{n}=x_{n,N}
and
\tilde{w}_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N})=w_{n}(x^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}x^{\prime}_{n},x_{N})
, namely,

\tilde{w}_{n}(x):=x_{n,N}^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u(x_{n,N}x^{\prime}+x_{n}^{\prime},x_{n,N}x_{N})\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Then (
33
) and (
34
) still hold for
\tilde{w}_{n}
.
Moreover,
(\tilde{w}_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a}})
. Hence, up to a subsequence, we have

\tilde{w}_{n}\to w_{a,A}\quad\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a}}),

where
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
.
Moreover, passing (
34
) to the limit, we get

-\Delta w_{a,A}=\frac{1}{w_{a,A}^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{A}\setminus\Sigma_{a}.

Now we take
a=\frac{1}{j}
and
A=j
, for large
j\in\mathbb{N}
and we construct
w_{\frac{1}{j},j}
as above. For
j\to\infty
, using a standard diagonal process, we can construct a limiting profile
w_{\infty}\in C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})
so that

-\Delta w_{\infty}=\frac{1}{w_{\infty}^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

and
w_{\frac{1}{j},j}=w_{\infty}
in
\Sigma_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{\frac{1}{j}}
. Moreover, from (
33
) we know that

cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\leq w_{\infty}(x)\leq Cx_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Hence by defining
w_{\infty}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, we have
w_{\infty}\in C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
and
w_{\infty}
is a solution to (
9
).
By
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
,

w_{\infty}(x)\equiv\left[\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}x_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

On the other hand, (
35
) gives
\frac{\partial\tilde{w}_{n}(e_{N})}{\partial\eta_{n}}=x_{n,N}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+p+1}}\frac{\partial u(x_{n})}{\partial\eta_{n}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This is a contradiction since
\frac{\partial\tilde{w}_{n}(e_{N})}{\partial\eta_{n}}\to\frac{\partial w_{\infty}(e_{N})}{\partial\eta}=w_{\infty}^{\prime}(1)\eta_{N}>0
.

Hence (
5
) is proved. In particular,

u\leq u_{\lambda}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda}\quad\text{ for all }0<\lambda\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}.

From this, we can proceed as in the proof of Theorem
2
to deduce the monotonicity of
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.
∎

4.
1D symmetry of solutions

4.1.
Weak comparison principles for half-spaces

We start this section with the following comparison principles for half-spaces.

Proposition 20
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f
is strictly decreasing on
(t_{0},+\infty)
for some
t_{0}>0
and let
\Sigma:=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\overline{\Sigma}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda>0
. Assume that
u,v\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Sigma)\cap C(\overline{\Sigma})
satisfy

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
-\Delta_{p}v=f(v)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
v>t_{0}&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
u\leq v&\text{ on }\partial\Sigma,\end{cases}

and

u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{\mu})\text{ for all }\mu>\lambda,

\lim_{x_{N}\to+\infty}(u-v)=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

(36)

Then
u\leq v
in
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Fix some
\alpha>N-2
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then we set
w=(u-v-\varepsilon)^{+}
and

\psi(x):=w^{\alpha}(x)\varphi_{R}^{\alpha+1}(x^{\prime})\chi_{\Sigma}(x),

(37)

where
\varphi_{R}
is defined as in (
23
).
Using (
36
), we find that
w=0
if
x_{N}>M
for some
M>\lambda
independent of
R
. Hence, the support of
\psi
is compactly contained in
\Sigma
, and we can use
\psi
as a test function in the equations
-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)
and
-\Delta_{p}v=f(v)
. We can proceed as the proof of Proposition
14
until we reach (
25
).

In the set
{\Sigma}\cap\{w>0\}
, we have

t_{0}<v<v+\varepsilon<u\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{M})}.

(38)

Since
f
is strictly decreasing on
(t_{0},+\infty)
, Lemma
15
gives

f(u)-f(v)\leq-C_{\varepsilon}(u-v)\leq-C_{\varepsilon}w\quad\text{ in }{\Sigma}\cap\{w>0\}

(39)

for some
C_{\varepsilon}>0
.
On the other hand, from (
38
) and the fact that
f
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
(0,+\infty)
, the standard gradient estimate yields

|\nabla u|<C_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla v|<C_{0}\quad\text{ in }{\Sigma}\cap\{w>0\}.

(40)

Now we can plug (
39
) and (
40
) into (
25
) and proceed as in the proof of Proposition
14
until we finish the proof.
∎

Proposition 21
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f
is differentiable and strictly decreasing on
(t_{0},+\infty)
for some
t_{0}>0
and

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}f^{\prime}(t)<0.

Let
\Sigma:=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\overline{\Sigma}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda>0
. Assume that
u,v\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\Sigma)\cap C(\overline{\Sigma})
satisfy

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u\leq f(u)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
-\Delta_{p}v\geq f(v)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
v>t_{0}&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
u\leq v&\text{ on }\partial\Sigma\end{cases}

and

|\nabla u|,|\nabla v|\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma).

Then
u\leq v
in
\Sigma
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
20
. However, the support of function
\psi
defined as in (
37
) may be unbounded. Instead, we will define
\psi
as

\psi(x):=w^{\alpha}(x)\phi_{R}^{\alpha+1}(x^{\prime})\chi_{\Sigma}(x),

where
\alpha>N-1
and
\phi_{R}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a standard cutoff function such that

\begin{cases}0\leq\phi_{R}\leq 1&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\phi_{R}=1&\text{ in }B_{R},\\
\phi_{R}=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R},\\
|\nabla\phi_{R}|\leq\frac{2}{R}&\text{ in }B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}.\end{cases}

(41)

With this new choice of test function, we can proceed as in the proofs of Propositions
20
and
21
. Notice that in our situation, (
38
) is replaced with

t_{0}<v<v+\varepsilon<u

and (
39
) still holds thanks to Lemma
15
.
∎

4.2.
Positive nonlinearity

In this subsection, we consider the case that
f
is positive and
p>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
.
For each
\lambda>0
, we define

u_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x):=u(x+\lambda\nu).

We aim to show that

u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\quad\text{ for all }\lambda>0.

(42)

From (i) and (ii), there exists
c_{0}>0
such that
f(t)>c_{0}
for
t>t_{0}+1
. By Lemma
18
, there exists
\lambda^{*}>0
such that

u(x)\geq t_{0}+1\text{ for }x_{N}>\lambda^{*}.

(43)

Hence
u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\geq t_{0}+1
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
for all
\lambda>\lambda_{\nu}^{*}
, where
\lambda_{\nu}^{*}:=\frac{\lambda^{*}}{(\nu,e_{N})}
. Moreover, from (
6
) and the mean value theorem, we deduce

\lim_{x_{N}\to+\infty}(u-u_{\lambda}^{\nu})=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

Let any
\lambda>\lambda_{\nu}^{*}
. Since

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda}^{\nu}=f(u_{\lambda}^{\nu})&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u_{\lambda}^{\nu}>t_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

we can apply Proposition
20
to derive

u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\quad\text{ for all }\lambda>\lambda_{\nu}^{*}.

(44)

Now that the set

\Lambda=\{\lambda>0\mid u\leq u_{\mu}^{\nu}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\text{ for all }\mu>\lambda\}

is nonempty, we can define

\lambda_{0}=\inf\Lambda.

We will show that

\lambda_{0}=0.

Assume, on the contrary, that
\lambda_{0}>0
. By continuity of
u
, we have
u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. To reach a contradiction, we will search for some
\varepsilon_{0}
small such that

u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

(45)

for all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0}-\varepsilon_{0},\lambda_{0})
.

\circ
Due to Lemma
18
, there exist
\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\delta}>0
sufficiently small such that

u+\tilde{\delta}\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}

(46)

for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}/2
.

\circ
We claim that

u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma

(47)

for all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0}-\varepsilon_{0},\lambda_{0})
, where
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is sufficiently small and

\Sigma=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid\tilde{\lambda}\ \leq x_{N}\leq\lambda^{*}\}.

Assume that (
47
) does not hold. Then there exist two sequences
\lambda_{n}\nearrow\lambda_{0}
and
x_{n}:=(x^{\prime}_{n},(x_{n})_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[\tilde{\lambda},\lambda^{*}]
such that

u(x_{n})>u_{\lambda_{n}}^{\nu}(x_{n}).

(48)

Moreover, we may assume
(x_{n})_{N}\to y_{0}\in[\tilde{\lambda},\lambda^{*}]
. Now we set

v_{n}(x^{\prime},x_{N})=u(x^{\prime}+x^{\prime}_{n},x_{N}).

Since
\min\{C\tilde{\lambda}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}},t_{0}+1\}\leq v_{n}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
, we have that
f(v_{n})
is bounded in
\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
for each
\lambda>\tilde{\lambda}
. The standard regularity gives
\|v_{n}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}})}<C_{\lambda}
. By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, via a standard diagonal process, we have

v_{n}\to v\quad\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}})

up to a subsequence, for
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. Moreover,
v
weakly solves
-\Delta_{p}v=f(v)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
.
Using the definition of
\lambda_{0}
and passing (
48
) to the limit, we have

\displaystyle v\leq v_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}},

\displaystyle v(x_{0})=v_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{0}),

where
x_{0}=(0^{\prime},y_{0})
. On the other hand, by (
46
) we have
v+\tilde{\delta}\leq v_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}
on
\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}})
. Hence the strong comparison principle (Theorem
12
) implies
v<v_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
. This contradicts the fact that
v(x_{0})=v_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{0})
. Therefore, (
47
) must hold.

\circ
Next, we show that

u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda^{*}}

(49)

for all
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0}-\varepsilon_{0},\lambda_{0})
.

From (
47
) and the continuity, we already have
u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}
on
\partial(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda^{*}})
. Moreover,
u_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x)\geq t_{0}+1
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda^{*}}
. Hence (
49
) follows by applying Proposition
20
with
u
and
v:=u_{\lambda}^{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda^{*}}
.

Combining (
46
), (
47
) and (
49
), we obtain (
45
). This contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{0}
and hence (
42
) is proved.

Therefore,
u
is monotone increasing in direction
\nu
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
. That is,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}:=(\nabla u,\nu)\geq 0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

To deduce the 1D symmetry of
u
, we take
\zeta
be any direction in
\{x\in\partial B_{1}\mid x_{N}=0\}
. Let
\nu_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
be a sequence converging to
\zeta
, we have
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{n}}\geq 0
. By sending
n\to\infty
, we deduce

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\zeta}\geq 0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Similarly, let another sequence
\tau_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}_{+}
converging to
-\zeta
, we obtain

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\zeta}\leq 0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Therefore,
u
is constant in direction
\zeta
. Since
\zeta
is arbitrary, we deduce that
u
does not depend on
x^{\prime}
. Hence
u
depends only on
x_{N}
and is monotone increasing in
x_{N}
.
∎

4.3.
Sign-changing nonlinearity

In this subsection, we consider the case that
f
is sign-changing and
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
.

In this case, the strong comparison principle does not hold in all of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Hence, a delicate analysis of the critical set of solutions plays a vital role.
To this end, we denote

\displaystyle Z_{u}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid\nabla u(x)=0\},

\displaystyle Z_{u_{\lambda}^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid\nabla u_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x)=0\},

\displaystyle Z_{f(u)}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid u(x)\in Z_{f}\},

\displaystyle Z_{f(u_{\lambda}^{\nu})}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid u_{\lambda}^{\nu}(x)\in Z_{f}\}.

Motivated by
[
18
, Proposition 4.3]
and
[
32
, Lemma 13]
, we prove the following strong comparison type principle.

Lemma 22
.

Assume
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<2
and
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function with
Z_{f}
being a discrete set. Let
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\tilde{\lambda}}
for some
\tilde{\lambda}>0
and
u\in C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Sigma)\cap C(\overline{\Sigma})
be a solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\text{ in }\Sigma,\\
u>0&\text{ in }\Sigma.\end{cases}

Furthermore, assume that

u\leq u_{\lambda}^{\nu}\text{ in }\Sigma\quad\text{ for all }\lambda\geq\lambda_{0},

(50)

u<u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}\text{ on }\partial\Sigma,

(51)

where
\nu\in S^{N-1}_{+}
and
\lambda_{0}>0
.
Then

u<u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}\text{ in }\Sigma\setminus\left(Z_{f(u)}\cap Z_{f(u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu})}\cap Z_{u}\cap Z_{u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows the technique in
[
32
, Lemma 13]
(see also
[
29
,
18
]
).
We denote all zeroes of
f
by

0<z_{1}<z_{2}<\cdots

We also denote
z_{0}=0
. By contradiction, assume that there exists

x_{0}\in\Sigma\setminus\left(Z_{f(u)}\cap Z_{f(u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu})}\cap Z_{u}\cap Z_{u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}}\right)

such that
u(x_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{0})
. There are four cases to be considered:

\circ

Case 1:

x_{0}\in\Sigma\setminus Z_{f(u)}
. That is,

z_{k}<u(x_{0})<z_{k+1}\text{ for some }k\geq 0.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be the connected component of
\Sigma\setminus Z_{f(u)}
containing
x_{0}
. Then for all
x\in\partial\Omega_{0}
, we have either
u(x)=z_{k}
or
u(x)=z_{k+1}
. By Theorem
12
, since
u(x_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{0})
, we have

u=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{0}.

(52)

Because
\Omega_{0}
is open, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that

B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\Omega_{0}.

We slide the ball
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
in
\Omega_{0}
, towards to
\partial\Sigma
in direction
-\nu
and keep its center on the ray
\Gamma_{x_{0}}:=\{x_{0}-t\nu\mid t\geq 0\}
. The ball will touch
\partial\Omega_{0}
for the first time at some point
\hat{x}_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{0}
. We denote by
\tilde{x}_{0}=x_{0}-t_{0}\nu
the new center of the slid ball.

Using (
50
) and (
52
), for every
x\in B_{r_{0}}(\tilde{x}_{0})
, which is the slid point of
x+t_{0}\nu\in B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, we have

z_{k}<u(x)\leq u_{\lambda_{0}+t_{0}}^{\nu}(x)= u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x+t_{0}\nu)=u(x+t_{0}\nu)\leq\max_{\overline{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}}u<z_{k+1}.

Therefore, the touching point
\hat{x}_{0}
must satisfy
u(\hat{x}_{0})=z_{k}
. Moreover, by continuity, we have
u(\hat{x}_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(\hat{x}_{0})
. We consider two possibilities.

- Possibility (i):
\hat{x}_{0}\in\partial\Sigma
. Then
u(\hat{x}_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(\hat{x}_{0})=u(\hat{x}_{0}+\lambda_{0}\nu)
contradicts assumption (
51
).

- Possibility (ii):
\hat{x}_{0}\notin\partial\Sigma
. Let us define the function

w(x):=u(x)-z_{k}\quad\text{ for }x\in B_{r_{0}}(\tilde{x}_{0}).

Since
p<2
and
f
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
(0,+\infty)
, we have

Cw^{p-1}+f(u)=Cw^{p-1}+f(u)-f(z_{k})\geq Cw^{p-1}-K(u-z_{k})\geq 0

for sufficiently large
C
.
Hence
w
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}w+Cw^{p-1}\geq 0&\text{ in }B_{r_{0}}(\tilde{x}_{0}),\\
w>0&\text{ in }B_{r_{0}}(\tilde{x}_{0}),\\
w(\hat{x}_{0})=0.\end{cases}

By Hopf’s lemma
[
41
]
, it follows that

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\eta}(\hat{x}_{0})<0,

(53)

where
\eta=\frac{\hat{x}_{0}-\tilde{x}_{0}}{|\hat{x}_{0}-\tilde{x}_{0}|}
is the outward normal at
\hat{x}_{0}
. In particular,
|\nabla u(\hat{x}_{0})|\neq 0
. Since
u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists a ball
B_{\rho_{0}}(\hat{x}_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
|\nabla u|\neq 0
in
B_{\rho_{0}}(\hat{x}_{0})
. By Theorem
11
, since
u(\hat{x}_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(\hat{x}_{0})
, we have

u=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }B_{\rho_{0}}(\hat{x}_{0}).

From (
53
), we can find a point
x_{1}\in\{\hat{x}_{0}+t\eta\mid t>0\}\cap B_{\rho_{0}}(\hat{x}_{0})
which is close to
\hat{x}_{0}
such that

z_{k-1}<u(x_{1})<u(\hat{x}_{0})=z_{k}.

Therefore, from a point
x_{0}
with
u(x_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{0})
and
z_{k}<u(x_{0})<z_{k+1}
, we have found a new point
x_{1}
satisfying
u(x_{1})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{1})
and
z_{k-1}<u(x_{1})<z_{k}
. Repeating this argument a finite number of times, we finally find a ball that touches
\partial\Sigma
. Then we have a contradiction as in Possibility (i).

\circ

Case 2:

x_{0}\in\Sigma\setminus Z_{f(u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu})}
. Since
u(x_{0})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{0})
, this case is actually Case 1.

\circ

Case 3:

x_{0}\in\Sigma\setminus Z_{u}
.

Since
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Sigma})
, we deduce
|\nabla u|\neq 0
in
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Theorem
11
now comes into play to yield

u=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}\quad\text{ in }B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}).

Moreover, since
u
is not constant in
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
, we can find
x_{1}\in B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
such that
x_{1}\notin Z_{f(u)}
. Using
u(x_{1})=u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}(x_{1})
, we will reach a contradiction as in Case 1.

\circ

Case 4:

x_{0}\in\Sigma\setminus Z_{u_{\lambda_{0}}^{\nu}}
. This case is similar to Case 3.
∎

5.2.
Dimension one

In this subsection, we classify all solutions to the ODE problem

\begin{cases}-(|v^{\prime}|^{p-2}v^{\prime})^{\prime}=\dfrac{1}{v^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
v(t)>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
v(0)=0,\end{cases}

(61)

where
p>1
and
\gamma>0
.

Proof of Theorem
10
.

By Theorem
7
, we know that
v^{\prime}\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We show that actually
v^{\prime}>0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Clearly, there exists at least one
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
v^{\prime}(t_{0})>0
. Let
(a,b)
be the maximal interval containing
t_{0}
such that
v^{\prime}>0
in
(a,b)
. We need to show that
a=0
and
b=+\infty
. We only prove the latter since the former can be done similarly.

Assume now that
b<+\infty
and
v^{\prime}(b)=0
. The case
v^{\prime}=0
in
(b,+\infty)
cannot happen since it contradicts the first equation of (
61
). Hence, there exists some
t_{1}>b
with
v^{\prime}(t_{1})>0

(t_{2},t_{1}]\subset(b,t_{1}]
such that

v^{\prime}>0\text{ in }(t_{2},t_{1}],\quad v^{\prime}(t_{2})=0.

From the standard elliptic regularity, we know that

is

in
(t_{2},t_{1}]

(p-1)(v^{\prime})^{p-2}v^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{1}{v^{\gamma}}.

(62)

Hence

in
(t_{2},t_{1}]

v^{\prime}(t_{2})>v^{\prime}(t_{1})
, which is a contradiction.

We have showed that
v^{\prime}>0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence
v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
by the standard elliptic regularity and
v
verifies (
62
) in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. This implies

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{p}(v^{\prime})^{p}-\frac{v^{1-\gamma}}{\gamma-1}=M\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ if }0<\gamma\neq 1,

(63)

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{p}(v^{\prime})^{p}+\ln v=M\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ if }\gamma=1,

(64)

for some constant
M
. On the other hand, by Lemma

, we have
\lim_{t\to+\infty}v(t)=+\infty
. Therefore, both (

) with

64
) yield a contradiction by letting

) has no solution for

In what follows, we assume

\left(M+\frac{v^{1-\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}v^{\prime}=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By integrating and using

(65)

When
M=0
, a direct calculation yields

v_{0}(t)=\left[\frac{(\gamma+p-1)^{p}}{p^{p-1}(p-1)(\gamma-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma+p-1}}t^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}.

Moreover, by change of variables in (
65
), we can show that all other solutions are related to each other via the formula
v_{M}(t)\equiv\lambda^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}v_{1}(\lambda t)
, where
M=\lambda^{\frac{(\gamma-1)p}{\gamma+p-1}}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.3.
Higher dimensions

We employ the technique from
[
36
, Proposition 5]
to show that solution
u
of problem (
9
) grows at most at a linear rate as
x_{N}\to+\infty
.

Lemma 24
.

Let
1<p\leq N
,
\gamma>0
and let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to problem (
9
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(p,\gamma,\theta,N)
such that

u(x)\leq Cx_{N}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}}.

Proof.

If
u
is a solution to (
9
), then

v(x):=\left(\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{2}\right)^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u\left(\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{2}x\right)

(66)

is also a solution. Therefore, we may assume that our solution
u
is bounded in the strip
\Sigma_{2}
.

Let any
x_{0}=(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
with
x_{0,N}:=4R>2
. We set

u_{R}(x):=R^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u(x_{0}+R(x-x_{0})),

then
u_{R}>0
in
B_{4}(x_{0})
and

-\Delta_{p}u_{R}=\frac{1}{u_{R}^{\gamma+1}}u_{R}\quad\text{ in }B_{4}(x_{0}).

By Lemma
17
, we have

u_{R}^{\gamma+1}(x)=\left(R^{-\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}u(x_{0}+R(x-x_{0}))\right)^{\gamma+1}\geq C^{\gamma+1}\quad\text{ in }B_{2}(x_{0}).

Hence

c(x):=\frac{1}{u_{R}^{\gamma+1}}\leq\frac{1}{C^{\gamma+1}},

where
C
is independent of
x_{0}
. By Harnack’s inequality, we have

\sup_{B_{1}(x_{0})}u_{R}\leq C_{H}\inf_{B_{1}(x_{0})}u_{R},

where
C_{H}=C_{H}(N,p,\gamma)
. In particular, by setting
u_{0}=u(x_{0})
, we have

u_{0}\leq\sup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u=R^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\sup_{B_{1}(x_{0})}u_{R}\leq C_{H}R^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}\inf_{B_{1}(x_{0})}u_{R}=C_{H}\inf_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u\leq C_{H}u(x)

for all
x\in B_{R}(x_{0})
. Hence

u(x)\geq C_{H}^{-1}u_{0}\quad\text{ on }\partial B_{R}(x_{0}).

Now we consider, for
p<N
, the fundamental solution of the
p
-Laplace operator

v_{c,k}=c\left(\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}+k\right),

which satisfies

\Delta_{p}v_{c,k}=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\{x_{0}\}

for all
c,k\in\mathbb{R}
. We can choose
c,k
such that

\begin{cases}v_{c,k}=C_{H}^{-1}u_{0}&\text{ on } \partial B_{R}(x_{0}),\\
v_{c,k}=0&\text{ on } \partial B_{4R}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

More precisely, the above condition is fulfilled with

c=\frac{C_{H}^{-1}u_{0}(4R)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}{4^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}-1}:=\tilde{c}u_{0}R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad k=-\frac{1}{(4R)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}.

Using
(v_{c,k}-u-\varepsilon)^{+}
, where
\varepsilon>0
, as a test function in
\Delta_{p}v_{c,k}=0
and
-\Delta_{p}u=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{4R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}(|\nabla v_{c,k}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{c,k}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(v_{c,k}-u-\varepsilon)^{+})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left(0-\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}\right)(v_{c,k}-u)^{+}\leq 0.

Hence
v_{c,k}\leq u+\varepsilon
in
B_{4R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore,

u\geq v_{c,k}\quad\text{ in }B_{4R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}(x_{0}).

In particular,

\displaystyle u(x_{0}^{\prime},1)

\displaystyle\geq v_{c,k}(x_{0}^{\prime},1)

\displaystyle=c\left(\frac{1}{|(x_{0}^{\prime},1)-(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,N})|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}+k\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{c}u_{0}R^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\left(\frac{1}{|4R-1|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}-\frac{1}{(4R)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\right).

By the mean value theorem for the function
h(t)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}
, we have

\frac{1}{|4R-1|^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}-\frac{1}{(4R)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}}\geq\frac{N-p}{p-1}\frac{1}{(4R)^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}}.

Therefore,

u(x_{0}^{\prime},1)\geq\frac{N-p}{p-1}\frac{\tilde{c}u_{0}}{4^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}R}.

Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{2})
, we deduce

u(x_{0})=u_{0}\leq CR,

where
C
does not depend on
R
. Since
x_{0}
is arbitrary and
x_{0,N}=4R
, we obtain that

u(x)\leq\frac{C}{4}x_{N}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{2}.

Scaling back, using (
66
), we obtain the thesis for
p<N
.

The case
p=N
follows by repeating the same argument but replacing the
fundamental solutions with the logarithmic one

w_{c,k}=c\left(k-\ln|x-x_{0}|\right).

More precisely, by choosing

c=\frac{C_{H}^{-1}u_{0}}{\ln 4}:=\hat{c}u_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad k=\ln(4R),

we have

\begin{cases}w_{c,k}=C_{H}^{-1}u_{0}&\text{ on } \partial B_{R}(x_{0}),\\
w_{c,k}=0&\text{ on } \partial B_{4R}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

Hence, as before

u\geq w_{c,k}\quad\text{ in }B_{4R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{R}(x_{0}).

In particular,

\displaystyle u(x_{0}^{\prime},1)

\displaystyle\geq w_{c,k}(x_{0}^{\prime},1)

\displaystyle=c\left(k-\ln|(x_{0}^{\prime},1)-(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,N})|\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{c}u_{0}\left(\ln(4R)-\ln(4R-1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\hat{c}u_{0}}{4R}.

Then we get the thesis as in the previous case.
This completes the proof.
∎

Given the previous asymptotic bounds on
u
, we can apply the scaling technique as in
[
36
, Proposition 7]
to establish a bound on the gradient.

Lemma 25
.

Let
1<p\leq N
,
\gamma\geq 1
and let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to problem (
9
) with
u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\overline{\lambda}})
for some
\overline{\lambda}>0
. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{\lambda}>0
such that

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C_{\lambda}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}

for all
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}
and set
R=x_{0,N}>\lambda
. We define

u_{R}(x):=\frac{u(Rx)}{R}\quad\text{ in }B_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right).

By Lemma
24
we have
u_{R}\leq C_{\lambda}
. Moreover, from Lemma
17
, we deduce

-\Delta_{p}u_{R}=\frac{R}{u^{\gamma}(Rx)}\leq CR^{-\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}}<C_{\lambda}^{\prime}\quad\text{ in }B_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right),

where
C_{\lambda}^{\prime}
is independent of
x_{0}
. By the standard gradient estimate, we have
|\nabla u_{R}|\leq C_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}
in
B_{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right)
. This indicates
|\nabla u|\leq C_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}
in
B_{\frac{R}{4}}(x_{0})
. The thesis follows from the arbitrariness of
x_{0}
.
∎

Similarly, we have the following estimate on the gradient of solutions if (
12
) holds.

Lemma 26
.

Let
p>1
,
\gamma\geq 1
and let
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a solution to problem (
9
) satisfying (
12
). Then

\lim_{x_{N}\to+\infty}|\nabla u(x^{\prime},x_{N})|=0\text{ uniformly in }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{+}.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}
and set
R=x_{0,N}>\lambda
. Then let
\varepsilon>0
and define

u_{R}(x):=\frac{u(Rx)}{\varepsilon R}\quad\text{ in }B_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right).

By assumption (
12
), there exists
R_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
u_{R}\leq 1
in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right)
whenever
R>R_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, from Lemma
17
, we deduce

-\Delta_{p}u_{R}=\frac{R}{\varepsilon^{p-1}u^{\gamma}(Rx)}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{p-1}}R^{-\frac{(\gamma-1)(p-1)}{\gamma+p-1}}<C^{\prime}\quad\text{ in }B_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right),

where
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\varepsilon,R_{\varepsilon})
is independent of
R>R_{\varepsilon}
. By the standard gradient estimate, we have
|\nabla u_{R}|\leq C
in
B_{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{x_{0}}{R}\right)
. This indicates
|\nabla u|\leq C\varepsilon
in
B_{\frac{R}{4}}(x_{0})
for
R>R_{\varepsilon}
and the thesis follows.
∎

Now we can conclude the last main result of this paper, namely, Theorem
8
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

For all
\gamma>0
, Proposition
1
and Lemma
24
imply that (
4
) holds. Estimate (
11
) is provided by Lemma
24
. Now we differentiate between two cases:

Case 1:

\gamma>1
. By Lemma
26
, condition (
6
) is fulfilled if (
12
) holds. The conclusion now follows from Theorem
7
with the aid of Theorem
10
.

Case 2:

0<\gamma<1
. In this case, Lemma
17
yields
u(x)\geq x_{N}^{\frac{p}{\gamma+p-1}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, which is contradict to (
11
). Hence, such solutions do not exist.
∎

5.4.
The conformal case

In this subsection, we study problem (
9
) with
p=N
, namely,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{N}u=\dfrac{1}{u^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\end{cases}

(67)

This case is usually referred to as the conformal case, since
\Delta_{N}
is invariant under the Kelvin transform

\hat{u}(x):=u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right).

Formally, we have
\Delta_{N}\hat{u}=\frac{1}{|x|^{2N}}(\Delta_{N}u)\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
9
.

Since
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
is a solution to (
67
), then one can verifies that
\hat{u}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\setminus\{0\})
and
\hat{u}
solves

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{N}\hat{u}=\dfrac{1}{|x|^{2N}\hat{u}^{\gamma}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
\hat{u}>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
\hat{u}=0&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\{0\}.\end{cases}

(68)

Moreover,

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\hat{u}(x)=\lim_{|x|\to 0}u(x)=0.

Solutions to (
68
) are still understood in the weak sense

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}(|\nabla\hat{u}|^{N-2}\nabla\hat{u},\nabla\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}\frac{\varphi}{|x|^{2N}\hat{u}^{\gamma}}\quad\text{ for all }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}).

For any
\lambda<0
, we denote
\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}=\{x:=(x_{1},\tilde{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\mid x_{1}<\lambda\}
,
x_{\lambda}=(2\lambda-x_{1},\tilde{x})
and
\hat{u}_{\lambda}(x)=\hat{u}(x_{\lambda})
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. By the reflection invariance of the
N
-Laplacian, we deduce

-\Delta_{N}\hat{u}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{|x_{\lambda}|^{2N}\hat{u}_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}

(69)

in the weak sense. Let any
\varepsilon>0
. We can find a small
\delta>0
such that
\hat{u}<\varepsilon
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap B_{\delta}(0_{\lambda})
. Now we set
w=(\hat{u}-\hat{u}_{\lambda}-\varepsilon)^{+}
, then
w=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap B_{\delta}(0_{\lambda})
. Moreover, since

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}(\hat{u}(x)-\hat{u}_{\lambda}(x))=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{u}(x)-\hat{u}_{\lambda}(x)=0\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\{0,0_{\lambda}\},

we deduce that the support of
w
is compactly contained in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\cap B_{R}
for some
R>0
. Hence, we can use
w\chi_{\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}
as a test function in the weak formulation of (
68
) and (
69
). By subtracting, we deduce

\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}(|\nabla\hat{u}|^{N-2}\nabla\hat{u}-|\nabla\hat{u}_{\lambda}|^{N-2}\nabla\hat{u}_{\lambda},\nabla w)=\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}\left(\dfrac{1}{|x|^{2N}\hat{u}^{\gamma}(x)}-\frac{1}{|x_{\lambda}|^{2N}\hat{u}_{\lambda}^{\gamma}(x)}\right)w\leq 0

since
\hat{u}\geq\hat{u}_{\lambda}
on the support of
w
and
|x|\geq|x_{\lambda}|
in
\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}
. Using (
21
), we get

\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}}(|\nabla\hat{u}|+|\nabla\hat{u}_{\lambda}|)^{N-2}|\nabla w|=0.

Hence
w=0
in
\hat{\Sigma}_{\lambda}
, which means
\hat{u}\leq\hat{u}_{\lambda}+\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we deduce
\hat{u}\leq\hat{u}_{\lambda}
in
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda<0
.

Repeating the argument in the opposite direction we conclude that
\hat{u}(x_{1},\tilde{x})=\hat{u}(-x_{1},\tilde{x})
. This indicates
u(x_{1},\tilde{x})=u(-x_{1},\tilde{x})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Since problem (
67
) is invariant with respect to translation and rotation, we deduce that
u
is symmetric with respect to any hyperplane perpendicular to
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. In other words,
u
depends only on
x_{N}
. The explicit formula for
u
in the case
\gamma>1
and a nonexistence result in the case
0<\gamma\leq 1
then follows from Theorem
10
.
∎

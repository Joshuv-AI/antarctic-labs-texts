A Functorial Approach to Categorical Resolutions
R. Hafezi and M. H. Keshavarz

Abstract
. Using a relative version of Auslander’s formula, we give a functorial approach to show that the bounded derived category of every Artin algebra admits a categorical resolution. This, in particular, implies that the bounded derived categories of Artin algebras of finite global dimension determine bounded derived categories of all Artin algebras. Hence, this paper can be considered as a typical application of functor categories, introduced in representation theory by Auslander, to categorical resolutions.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a singular algebraic variety of finite type over an algebraically closed field. A resolution of singularities of
X
is a certain (proper and birational) morphism
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
, where
\widetilde{X}
is a non-singular algebraic variety. As remarked in
[
Ku
, Page 1, Line -4]
, the functor
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}\widetilde{X})\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}X)
induced by
\pi
enjoys some remarkable properties.
The bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
\widetilde{X}
and
X
are related by two natural functors, known as the derived pushforward

\pi_{\ast}:\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}\widetilde{X})\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}X)
and the derived pullback functor
\pi^{\ast}:\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{perf}}}({\rm{coh}}X)\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{b}({\rm{coh}}\widetilde{X})
, such that
\pi^{\ast}
is left adjoint to
\pi_{\ast}
. Here
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}X)
stands for the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
X
and
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{perf}}}({\rm{coh}}X)
stands for the full subcategory of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}X)
consisting of perfect complexes. If furthermore,
X
have rational singularities, then the composition
\pi_{\ast}\circ\pi^{\ast}
is isomorphic to the identity functor
[
Ku
, Page 2]
.

Based on this observation, as he mentioned, Kuznetsov
[
Ku
, Page 2]
introduced the notion of a categorical resolution of singularities. By definition a categorical resolution of a triangulated category
\mathbb{D}
is a regular triangulated category
\widetilde{\mathbb{D}}
and a pair of functors
\pi_{\ast}
and
\pi^{\ast}
satisfying almost similar conditions as above, see
[
Ku
, Definition 3.2]
.
Recall that a triangulated category
{\mathfrak{T}}
is called regular if it is equivalent to an admissible subcategory of the bounded derived category of a smooth algebraic variety
[
Ku
, Definition 3.1]
.

Note that, as remarked in
[
BO
, Page 7, Line -1]
, the pushforward functor
\pi_{\ast}
identifies
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}X)
with the quotient of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{coh}}\widetilde{X})
by the kernel of
\pi_{\ast}
. Based on this observation, as they mentioned, Bondal and Orlov
[
BO
]
defined a categorical desingularization of a triangulated category
{\mathfrak{T}}
as a pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category of finite homological dimension and
\mathcal{K}
is a thick triangulated subcategory of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}(\mathcal{A})
such that
{\mathfrak{T}}\simeq\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}(\mathcal{A})/\mathcal{K}
, see
[
BO
, §5]
.
Recall that a full triangulated subcategory of a triangulated category
{\mathfrak{T}}
is called thick if it is closed under taking direct summands.

Recently, Zhang
[
Z
]
combined these two categorical levels of the notion of a resolution of singularities and suggested a new definition for a categorical resolution of a non-smooth triangulated category
[
Z
, Definition 2.2]
.

He then proved that if
\Lambda
is an Artin algebra of infinite global dimension and has a module
T
with
{\rm{id}}_{\Lambda}T<1
such that
{}^{\perp}T
is of finite type, then the bounded derived category
\mathbb{D}^{b}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
admits a categorical resolution
[
Z
, Theorem 6.1]
. The main technique for proving this result is the notion of relative derived categories studied by several authors in different settings, see e.g
[
N
]
,
[
B
]
and
[
GZ
]
. Recall that a triangulated category
{\mathfrak{T}}
is called smooth if it is triangle equivalent to the bounded derived category of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathbb{D}^{b}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{D}^{b}_{hf}(\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathbb{D}^{b}_{hf}(\mathcal{A})
is the subcategory of
\mathbb{D}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of homological finite objects
[
Z
, Definition 2.1]
.

In this paper, we generalize Zhang’s result to arbitrary Artin algebras and show that bounded derived category of every Artin algebra admits a categorical resolution. The technique for the proof is based on a relative version of the so-called Auslander’s Formula
[
Au66
]
and
[
L
]
. Besides Auslander’s formula we use the following known result of Auslander. In his Queen Mary College lectures
[
Au71
, §III.3]
he proved the following important result:

Theorem 1

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra with radical
J
and
n
be the nilpotency index of

. Then the Artin algebra
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}={\rm{End}}_{\Lambda}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda/J^{i})^{{\rm{op}}}
has the following properties:

(i)

The global dimension of
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}
is finite;

(ii)

There is a finitely generated projective
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}
-module

such that

and
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}
are isomorphic Artin algebras.

Hence, as he mentioned, Artin algebras of finite global dimension determine all Artin algebras
[
Au71
, Page 47, Line 34]
. Our volunteer for the proof of the main theorem via a functorial approach is
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}
, that throughout for ease of reference we call it A-algebra of
\Lambda
. ‘A’ stands both for ‘Auslander’ and also ‘Associated’ algebra. Of course, instead of Auslander’s result, one can also use of an important result due to O. Iyama
[

Note that, as remarked in
[
DR
, Page 1]
, there exists an idempotent

.
For the proof see
[

, Page 243, Line -2]

2.1.
Functor Category

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive skeletally small category. The Hom sets will be denoted either by

An

-module

\mathcal{X}(-,X)\rightarrow\mathcal{X}(-,X^{\prime})\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0,

with

and

in
\mathcal{X}
. All finitely presented
\mathcal{X}
-modules form a full subcategory of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}\mathcal{X}
, denoted by
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}
or sometimes
\rm{f.p.}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{A}b)

Covariant additive functors and its full subcategory consisting of finitely presented (left)
\mathcal{X}
-modules will be denoted by

-Mod and
\mathcal{X}
-
{\rm mod}
, respectively. Since every finitely generated subobject of a finitely presented object is finitely presented
[
Au66
, Page 200, Line 7]
, Auslander called them coherent functors. Recall that an object

in

is finitely generated if given any family
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and an epimorphism

(with

of

such that

(with

) is an epimorphism
[
Au66
, Page 192, Line 6]

The Yoneda embedding
\mathcal{X}\hookrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}
, sending each
X\in\mathcal{X}
to

, is a fully faithful functor. Note that for each
X\in\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{X}(-,X)
is a projective object of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}
.
Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
. A morphism
\varphi:X\rightarrow A
with
X\in\mathcal{X}
is called a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation of

if
\mathcal{A}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}(-,A)|_{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow 0
is exact, where

\mathcal{A}(-,A)|_{\mathcal{X}}
is the functor
\mathcal{A}(-,A)
restricted to
\mathcal{X}
. Hence

has a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation if and only if
(-,A)|_{\mathcal{X}}
is a finitely generated object of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}\mathcal{X}
.
\mathcal{X}
is called contravariantly finite if every object of
\mathcal{A}
admits a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation. Dually, one can define the notion of left
\mathcal{X}
-approximations and covariantly finite subcategories.
\mathcal{X}
is called functorially finite, if it is both covariantly and contravariantly finite.

3.
A Derived Version of Auslander’s Formula

In this section, we give a derived version of the Auslander’s formula. To this end, we first prove the following proposition.
This proposition has been proved in

Let
\mathcal{X}

{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda

{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda

The exact functor
\vartheta:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda

It acts on objects, as well as roofs, terms by terms (i.e. for every complex

\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}):=(\vartheta(F^{i}),\vartheta(\partial^{i}))

\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})

4.
Categorical resolutions of bounded derived categories

In this section, we give a functorial approach to show that
\mathbb{D}^{b}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
, the bounded derived category of
\Lambda
, admits a categorical resolution, where
\Lambda
is an arbitrary Artin algebra.

We begin by the definition of a categorical resolution of the bounded derived category of an Artin algebra. Although, the definition in literature is for arbitrary triangulated categories, in this paper we only concentrate on the bounded derived categories of Artin algebras.

We follow the definition presented by
[
Z
, Definition 2.2]
, which is a combination of a definition due to Bondal and Orlov
[
BO
]
and also another one due to Kuznetsov
[
Ku
, Definition 3.2]
, both as different attempts for providing a categorical translation of the notion of the resolutions of singularities.

Definition 4.1
.

(
[
Z
, Definition 2.2]
)
Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra of infinite global dimension. A categorical resolution of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
, is a triple
(\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime}),\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
, where
\Lambda^{\prime}
is an Artin algebra of finite global dimension and
\pi_{*}:\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
and
\pi^{*}:\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime})
are triangle functors satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\pi_{*}
induces a triangle-equivalence
\frac{\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime})}{{\rm{Ker}}(\pi_{*})}\simeq\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
;

(ii)

\pi^{*}
is left adjoint to
\pi_{*}
on
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
. That is, for every
{\bf P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
and every
{\bf X^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime})
, there exists a functorial isomorphism

\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime})(\pi^{*}({\bf P}),{\bf X^{\prime}})\cong\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)({\bf P},\pi_{*}({\bf X^{\prime}}));

(iii)

The unit
\eta:1_{\mathbb{K}^{b}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}\pi^{*}
is a natural isomorphism.

Furthermore, a categorical resolution
(\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\prime}),\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
is called
weakly crepant
if
\pi^{*}
is also a right adjoint to
\pi_{*}
on
\mathbb{K}^{b}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
.

Now we are in a position to give a functorial approach to show that the bounded derived category of every Artin algebra admits a categorical resolution.

Theorem 4.2
.

\Lambda
be an Artin algebra of infinite global dimension and

denote its A-algebra. Then

.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a contravariantly finite subcategory of

containing

. We prove the theorem in 6 steps.

Step I.
As it is mentioned in Section
3
, the exact functor

, defined in Remark
2.1

(i)
, can be extended naturally to
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
to induce a triangle functor

\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}:\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda).

It acts on objects, as well as roofs, terms by terms.

Step II.
We define the functor
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}:\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
as follows: For every complex
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
,
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}):=(-,\mathbf{P})
. So in fact, it is a functor from
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
to
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}))
. That is, for every complex
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
,
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})=(-,\mathbf{P})
is a bounded complex of projective
\mathcal{X}
-modules.

Step III.
As it is mentioned in Section
3
,
denote the kernel of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}
by
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
.
By Proposition
3.3
, the induced functor

\frac{\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})}{\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})}\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}(\Lambda),

denoted by
\widetilde{\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}},
is an equivalence of triangulated categories.

Step IV.

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}
is left adjoint to
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}
on
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
. To prove this we show that for every complexes
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
and
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
, there exists an isomorphism

\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F})\cong\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F})),

of abelian groups.
Let
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
.
By Proposition
3.1
, there exists an exact sequence of complexes

0\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}\longrightarrow(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))|_{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{1}\longrightarrow 0,

such that
\mathbf{F}_{0}
and
\mathbf{F}_{1}
are complexes over
{\rm{mod}}_{0}\mbox{-}\mathcal{X}
. This sequence can be divided into the following two short exact sequences of complexes

0\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}\longrightarrow\mathbf{K}\longrightarrow 0\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ 0\rightarrow\mathbf{K}\longrightarrow(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{1}\longrightarrow 0.

These two sequences, in turn, induce the following two triangles

\mathbf{F}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}\longrightarrow\mathbf{K}\rightsquigarrow
and
\mathbf{K}\longrightarrow(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{1}\rightsquigarrow,

in
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
, where
\mathbf{F}_{0}
and
\mathbf{F}_{1}
are considered as objects of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{mod}}_{0}\mbox{-}\mathcal{X})
.
Applying the functor
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),-)
on these triangles, there are the induced two long exact sequences of abelian groups

(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F_{0}})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{K})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F_{0}}[1])

and

(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F_{1}}[-1])\longrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{K})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F})))\longrightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F_{1}}),

respectively, where all Hom groups are taken in
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
.

But since
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
,
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})=(-,\mathbf{P})\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}))
. Hence, by applying some known abstract facts in triangulated categories, e.g.
[
We
, Corollary 10.4.7]
, all these Hom sets can be also considered in
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
.

On the other hand by applying Yoneda lemma terms by terms, one can easily show that for every
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
and
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{mod}}_{0}\mbox{-}\mathcal{X})
,

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})((-,\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{G})=0
. Thus, there exists the following isomorphism of abelian groups.

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),\mathbf{F})\cong\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))).

Therefore, to complete the proof, it is enough to show that

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}),(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F})))\cong\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F})).

This is a consequence of Yoneda lemma applying terms by terms in view of the fact that
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})=(-,\mathbf{P})
.
Note that since
\mathbf{P}
is a bounded complex of projectives, by
[
We
, Corollary 10.4.7]
,
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))\cong\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)(\mathbf{P},\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))
. The proof of this Step is now complete.

Step V.
For every bounded complex of projectives
\mathbf{P}
,
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})=\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}((-,\mathbf{P}))=\mathbf{P}.

Step VI.
Since
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}
is A-algebra of
\Lambda
, there is a
\Lambda
-module
M
such that
\widetilde{{\Lambda}}={\rm{End}}_{\Lambda}(M)

[
Au71
, Page 47, Line 26]
. Set
\mathcal{X}:={\rm{add}\mbox{-}}M
. Then
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}\simeq{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\widetilde{\Lambda}.
Therefore, the triple

(\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}(\widetilde{\Lambda}),\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta},\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}})
is a categorical resolution of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}(\Lambda)
.
∎

Towards the end of the paper, we show that if
\Lambda
is a self-injective Artin algebra of infinite global dimension, then the triple
(\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\widetilde{\Lambda}),\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta},\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}})
introduced in the above theorem, provides a
weakly crepant
categorical resolution of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
. To do this, we need some lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra,
\mathcal{X}
be a contravariantly finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
containing
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
, and
I\in{\rm{inj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
. Then the functor
(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}}
is an injective object of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, there exists an exact sequence
X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}I\longrightarrow 0
of
\Lambda
-modules such that
d_{0}
and
d_{1}
are right
\mathcal{X}
-approximations of
I
and
{\rm{Ker}}(d_{0})
, respectively. This guarantees the existence of the exact sequence

(-,X_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-,d_{1})}}{{\longrightarrow}}(-,X_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-,d_{0})}}{{\longrightarrow}}(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow 0

in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}.
Hence
(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}}
is a finitely presented functor. To show that it is injective, pick a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow F^{\prime}\rightarrow F\rightarrow F^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
of
\mathcal{X}
-modules and apply the functor
(-,(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})
on it to get the sequence

0\longrightarrow(F^{\prime\prime},(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})\longrightarrow(F,(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})\longrightarrow(F^{\prime},(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})\longrightarrow 0.

As an immediate consequence of
[
AHK
, Theorem 3.7]
,
(\vartheta,\vartheta_{\rho})
is an adjoint pair and so we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(F^{\prime\prime},(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(F,(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(F^{\prime},(-,I)|_{\mathcal{X}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\vartheta(F^{\prime\prime}),I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\vartheta(F),I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\vartheta(F^{\prime}),I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where the vertical arrows are isomorphisms. But, the lower row is exact, because
I
is an injective module and
\vartheta
is an exact functor by Remark
2.1
. Hence the upper row should be exact, that implies the result.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a self-injective Artin algebra. So
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda={\rm{inj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
. Hence a complex
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
is also a bounded complex of injectives. So by the above lemma,
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})=(-,\mathbf{P})
is a complex of injective
\mathcal{X}
-modules. Therefore, by
[
We
, Corollary 10.4.7]
, all Hom sets with either
\mathbf{P}
or
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})
in the second variants, can be calculated either in
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
or in
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
, i.e. for every complex
\mathbf{F}
in
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
,

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbf{F},\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}))\cong\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbf{F},\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\mathcal{X}
be as in our convention. Then for every complexes
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{mod}}_{0}\mbox{-}\mathcal{X})
and
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
,

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbf{G},(-,\mathbf{M})|_{\mathcal{X}})=0.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{G}=(G^{i},\partial^{i}_{\mathbf{F}})
and
\mathbf{M}=(M^{i},\partial^{i}_{\mathbf{M}})
. As an immediate consequence of
[
AHK
, Theorem 3.7]
,
(\vartheta,\vartheta_{\rho})
is an adjoint pair and so for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the isomorphism

{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}({G^{i}},(-,{M^{i}})|_{\mathcal{X}})\cong{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda(\vartheta({G^{i}}),{M^{i}}).

Hence
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}({G^{i}},(-,{M^{i}})|_{\mathcal{X}})=0
, because
{G^{i}}\in{\rm{mod}}_{0}\mbox{-}\mathcal{X}={\rm{Ker}}(\vartheta)
. This fact can be extended naturally, terms by terms, to bounded complexes to prove the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a self-injective Artin algebra of infinite global dimension. Then the triple
(\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\widetilde{\Lambda}),\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta},\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}})
introduced in Theorem
4.2
, is a weakly crepant categorical resolution of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
.

Proof.

We just should show that
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}
is a right adjoint of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}
on
\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
. Pick
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})
and
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda)
. As it is mentioned in
Step IV
, there exist the two triangles

\mathbf{F}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}\longrightarrow\mathbf{K}\rightsquigarrow
and
\mathbf{K}\longrightarrow(-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}))\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}_{1}\rightsquigarrow,

where
\mathbf{F}_{0}
and
\mathbf{F}_{1}
are objects of
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{mod}}_{0}\mbox{-}\mathcal{X})
. By applying the functor
\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(-,\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}))
on these triangles, we get two exact sequences of abelian groups. Now we use Remark
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
, to conclude the isomorphism

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})(\mathbf{F},\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}))\cong\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})((-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F})),\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P})).

The extended version of Yoneda lemma finally helps us to establish the isomorphism

\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X})((-,\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F})),\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta_{\lambda}}(\mathbf{P}))\cong\mathbb{K}^{{\rm{b}}}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)(\mathbb{D}^{{\rm{b}}}_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{F}),\mathbf{P})

of abelian groups. The proof is hence complete.
∎

Abstract




Inequalities for the
k
-Regular Overpartitions

Abstract.

Bessenrodt and Ono, Chen, Wang and Jia, DeSalvo and Pak were the first to discover the log-subadditivity, log-concavity, and the third-order Turán inequality of partition function, respectively.
Many other important partition statistics are proved to enjoy
similar properties.
This paper focuses on the partition function
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
, which counts the number of overpartitions of
n
with no parts divisible by
k
.
We provide a combinatorial proof to establish that for any
k\geq 2
, the partition function
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
exhibits strict log-subadditivity. Specifically, we show that
{\overline{p}}_{k}(a){\overline{p}}_{k}(b)>{\overline{p}}_{k}(a+b)
for integers
a\geq b\geq 1
and
a+b\geq k
.
Furthermore, we investigate the log-concavity and the satisfaction of the third-order Turán inequality for
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
, where
2\leq k\leq 9
.

Keywords
:
k
-regular overpartition, strict log-subadditivity, log-concavity, third order Turán inequalities

AMS Classifications
: 11P82, 05A19, 30A10

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to present certain inequality relations satisfied by
k
-regular overpartition function, specifically focusing on strict log-subadditivity, log-concavity, and the third-order Turán inequality.
Recall that a partition of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
.
Let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
.
Bessenrodt and Ono
[
3
]
showed that
p(n)
satisfies the strict log-subadditivity result, that is, for
a,b>1
and
a+b>9

\displaystyle p(a)p(b)>p(a+b).

(1.1)

Alanazi, Gagola and Munagi
[
1
]
resolved the conjecture proposed by Bessenrodt and Ono
[
3
]
, providing a combinatorial proof for equation (
1.1
).
Since then, various partition functions have been proven to enjoy the same strict log-subadditivity as
p(n)

for instance, the overpartition function
[

[

and the spt-function
[

Recall that an overpartition of a nonnegative integer
n
is a
partition of
n
where the first occurrence of each distinct part may be overlined.
In
[
16
]
, Lovejoy initiated the exploration of an overpartition function, characterized by its count of overpartitions of
n
with parts that are indivisible by
k

Subsequently, Shen
[
18

reinterpreted this function, giving it the appellation of
k
-regular overpartition
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
, and elucidated its generating function as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)q^{n}=\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}(-q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}},

(1.2)

where
(a;q)_{\infty}
stands for the
q

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n-1}),\quad|q|<1.

The first result of this paper is the demonstration, using combinatorial methods, that
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
satisfies strict log-subadditivity.

Theorem 1.1
.

For integers
k
,
a
and
b

k\geq 2
,
a\geq b\geq 1
,
a+b\geq k
, then

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}_{k}(a){\overline{p}}_{k}(b)>{\overline{p}}_{k}(a+b).

Having set the stage with our first result, demonstrating the strict log-subadditivity of
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
through combinatorial methods, we now transition towards a broader context. We delve into the realm of log-concavity, also known as Turán inequalities, along with their higher-order counterparts, known as cubic inequalities, originate from the examination of the Maclaurin coefficients of real entire functions within the Laguerre-Pólya class.
A sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is considered to be log-concave if it fulfills the Turán inequalities, which are given by

\alpha_{n}^{2}\geq\alpha_{n-1}\alpha_{n+1},~~\text{for}~n\geq 1.

Moreover, a sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is defined as satisfying the third order Turán inequalities if for
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle 4(\alpha_{n}^{2}-\alpha_{n-1}\alpha_{n+1})(\alpha_{n+1}^{2}-\alpha_{n}\alpha_{n+2})\geq(\alpha_{n}\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n-1}\alpha_{n+2})^{2}.

The initial exploration of the Turán inequalities, along with their higher-order variants for the partition function, can be credited to the research undertaken by Chen, Jia and Wang
[
6

, DeSalvo and Pak
[

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
To establish log-concavity, we need to show that
{Q_{k}(n)}\leq 1
for
2\leq k\leq 9
.
From Theorem
4.1
, we see that for
2\leq k\leq 9
and
{n}\geq\tilde{n}_{k}
,

{Q_{k}(n)}<\tilde{R}_{k}<1,

where
\tilde{n}_{k}
and
\tilde{R}_{k}
are defined in Table
4.4
.
Besides,
{Q_{k}(n)}<1
holds as long as one can verify it holds for
\bar{n}_{k}\leq n\leq\tilde{n}_{k}
,
where
\bar{n}_{k}
is defined in Theorem
1.3
.
So
{Q_{k}(n)}<1
for
n\geq\bar{n}_{k}
and
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
is log-concave for
n\geq\bar{n}_{k}
when
2\leq k\leq 9
.

To demonstrate that
{\overline{p}}_{k}(n)
for
2\leq k\leq 9
satisfies the third order Turán inequalities for
n\geq\hat{n}_{k}
, we rely on Chen, Jia, and Wang’s work
[
6
]
. It is sufficient to prove that when
n\geq\hat{n}_{k}
,

\displaystyle 4(1-{Q_{k}(n)})(1-Q_{k}(n+1))-(1-{Q_{k}(n)}Q_{k}(n+1))^{2}>0.

(4.16)

Recall that Jia
[
13
, Lemma 5.1]
obtained the following result.
If
u
and
v
are positive real numbers satisfying
\frac{15}{16}\leq u<v<1
and
u+\sqrt{(1-u)^{3}}>v
holds, then

\displaystyle 4(1-u)(1-v)-(1-uv)^{2}>0.

(4.17)

Using (
4.17
), we come to prove that for
\mu_{k}\geq\tilde{n}_{k}
and
2\leq k\leq 9
,

\frac{15}{16}\leq{Q_{k}(n)}<Q_{k}(n+1),

(4.18)

and

Q_{k}(n+1)<{Q_{k}(n)}+\sqrt{(1-{Q_{k}(n)})^{3}}.

(4.19)

Next, we will prove the case when
k=2
.
By Theorem
4.1
, we have for
{\mu_{2}}\geq 167
,

{Q_{2}(n)}>1-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{3}}+\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{4}}-\frac{7}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{5}}-\frac{130}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{6}}.

It can be checked that when
{{\mu_{2}}}\geq 4

1-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{3}}+\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{4}}-\frac{7}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{5}}-\frac{130}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{6}}\geq\frac{15}{16}

Hence, for
{\mu_{2}}\geq 167
,

{Q_{2}(n)}>\frac{15}{16}.

Moreover, by Theorem
4.1
, it can be computed directly that for
{{\mu_{2}}}\geq 11
,

\displaystyle Q_{2}(n+1)-{Q_{2}(n)}>

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{3}}+\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{4}}-\frac{7}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{5}}-\frac{130}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{3}}+\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{4}}-\frac{6}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{5}}+\frac{120+\frac{\pi^{8}}{256}}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{4}}{16}\left(\frac{1}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{3}}-\frac{1}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{3}}+\frac{3}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{4}}-\frac{3}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{6}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{5}}-\frac{7}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{5}}-\frac{250+\frac{\pi^{8}}{256}}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{6}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

Here we realize (
4.18
) is valid.
And we find that for
{\mu_{2}}\geq 167
,

\displaystyle Q_{2}(n+1)-{Q_{2}(n)}

\displaystyle<\left(1-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{3}}+\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{4}}-\frac{6}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{5}}+\frac{120+\frac{\pi^{8}}{256}}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(1-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{3}}+\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16{{\mu_{2}}}^{4}}-\frac{7}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{5}}-\frac{130}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{4}}{16}\left(\frac{1}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{3}}-\frac{1}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{3}}\right)-\frac{3\pi^{4}}{16}\left(\frac{1}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{4}}-\frac{1}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{6}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{5}}+\frac{7}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{5}}+\frac{120+\frac{\pi^{8}}{256}}{{{\mu_{2}^{+}}}^{6}}+\frac{130}{{{\mu_{2}}}^{6}}.

(4.20)

Notice that when

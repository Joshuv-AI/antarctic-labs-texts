Edge coloring of products of signed graphs
Robert Janczewski, Krzysztof Turowski, Bartłomiej Wróblewski

Abstract
In 2020 2020 , Behr 2020 defined the problem of edge coloring of signed graphs and showed that every signed graph ( G CLOSE (G , OPEN σ ) \sigma) can be colored using exactly Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) or Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 \Delta(G)+1 colors, where Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) is the maximum degree in graph G G . In this paper, we focus on products of signed graphs. We recall the definitions of the Cartesian, tensor, strong, and corona products of signed graphs and prove results for them. In particular, we show that ( 1 ) (1) the Cartesian product of Δ \Delta -edge-colorable signed graphs is Δ \Delta -edge-colorable, ( 2 ) (2) the tensor product of a Δ \Delta -edge-colorable signed graph and a signed tree requires only Δ \Delta colors and ( 3 ) (3) the corona product of almost any two signed graphs is Δ \Delta -edge-colorable. We also prove some results related to the coloring of products of signed paths and cycles.

This article focuses on simple, finite, and undirected graphs. A graph

A concept of signed graphs emerged in the

In the context of a signed graph

\sigma^{\prime}(uv)=\begin{cases}-\sigma(uv)\text{,}&\text{if }V^{\prime}\text{ includes exactly one of }u\text{, }v\text{,}\\
\sigma(uv)\text{,}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If a graph

Additionally, by

also have odd numbers of negative edges. Thus it follows from
Lemma

11
that
\chi^{\prime}(H_{1})=\Delta(H_{1})+1
and
\chi^{\prime}(H_{2})=\Delta(H_{2})+1
, so none of
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
are balanced, a contradiction. So
S^{\prime}
does not have such a decomposition into
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and
\chi^{\prime}(S^{\prime})=\Delta(S^{\prime})+1
. Since
S
and
S^{\prime}
are switching equivalent,
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)+1
.

The first case corresponds to exactly
1/4
of possible signatures of
S
and it is the only case with
\chi^{\prime}(S)
being equal to
\Delta(S)
, so it follows that
\mathcal{C}(S)=1/4
.
∎

3
Tensor products

Let
S_{1}=(G_{1}
,
\sigma_{1})
,
S_{2}=(G_{2}
,
\sigma_{2})
be signed graphs. The tensor product of graphs
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
is a signed graph
S=(G
,
\sigma)
, such that
V(S)=V(S_{1})\times V(S_{2})
and there is an edge
e=(u
,
u^{\prime})(v
,
v^{\prime})
in
S
, where
u
,
v\in V(S_{1})
,
u^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}\in V(S_{2})
, if and only if
uv\in E(S_{1})
and
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(S_{2})
. Then
\sigma(e)=\sigma_{1}(uv)\sigma_{2}(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})
.

We denote
S
by
S_{1}\times S_{2}
. Clearly,
|S|=G_{1}\times G_{2}
.

First, let us recall a simple relation between the maximum degree of graphs and their tensor product:

Lemma 15
.

For arbitrary graphs
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
it holds that
\Delta(G_{1}\times G_{2})=\Delta(G_{1})\Delta(G_{2})
.

Proof.

From the definition of a tensor product it follows that
\deg_{G_{1}\times G_{2}}((u
,
u^{\prime}))=\deg_{G_{1}}(u)\deg_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})
for any two vertices
u\in V(G_{1})
,
u^{\prime}\in V(G_{2})
. Let
u
,
u^{\prime}
be arbitrary vertices of maximum degree in
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, respectively. It is clear that
(u
,
u^{\prime})
is a vertex of maximum degree in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
and its degree is equal to
\deg_{G_{1}}(u)\deg_{G_{2}}(u^{\prime})=\Delta(G_{1})\Delta(G_{2})
.
∎

Now we proceed with main results of this section, starting with tensor products of arbitrary graphs and paths on
2
vertices:

Theorem 16
.

Let
S_{1}=(G_{1}
,
\sigma_{1})
,
S_{2}=(P_{2}
,
\sigma_{2})
be signed graphs and
S=S_{1}\times S_{2}
. If
\chi^{\prime}(S_{1})=\Delta(S_{1})
, then
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

Proof.

Since
\Delta(S_{2})=1
, it follows from
Lemma

15
that
\Delta(S)=\Delta(S_{1})
. We also observe that
m(S)=2m(S_{1})
. Let
c_{1}
be an arbitrary optimal coloring of
S_{1}
, using exactly
\Delta(S_{1})
colors (denoted by
R_{1}
). For every pair of colors
\pm\alpha
with
\alpha\geq 0
(note that
\alpha
might be possibly equal to
0
) we consider graph
S_{1}^{\alpha}
such that
S_{1}^{\alpha}\subseteq S_{1}
and it contains only the edges colored with
\pm\alpha
in
c_{1}
. By
S^{\alpha}
we denote a subgraph of
S
such that
S^{\alpha}=S_{1}^{\alpha}\times S_{2}
. It is easy to observe that
\bigcup_{\pm\alpha\in R_{1}}S^{\alpha}=S
and graphs
S^{|\alpha|}
,
S^{|\beta|}
are edge-disjoint for any
\alpha
,
\beta\in R_{1}
such that
|\alpha|\neq|\beta|
.

We observe that
S_{1}^{\alpha}
is a graph of a maximum degree
2
with all the connected components balanced. We show the coloring
c
of
S
by showing how to color incidences of
S^{\alpha}
using only colors used by incidences of
S_{1}^{\alpha}
. Let
H
be any connected component of graph
S_{1}^{\alpha}
. We consider 3 cases:

1.

H
is a path. Let
P=H\times S_{2}
,
P\subseteq S^{\alpha}
. It is easy to observe that
P
consists of two vertex-disjoint paths isomorphic to
H
, so they can be colored using colors
\pm\alpha
in the coloring
c
.

2.

H
is a cycle
C_{r}
. It follows that
H
is balanced, so has an even number of negative edges. We consider two cases separately:

(a)

r=2k
. We observe that since
H
has an even number of edges and an even number of negative edges, it also has an even number of positive edges. Let
C=H\times S_{2}
.
C
consists of two vertex-disjoint cycles
(C^{\prime}
,
\sigma^{\prime})
,
(C^{\prime\prime}
,
\sigma^{\prime\prime})
such that
n(C^{\prime})=n(C^{\prime\prime})=r
.

We consider two cases:

i.

The only edge of
S_{2}
is positive. Every edge in
C^{\prime}
has the same sign as the corresponding edge in
H
, so there is the same number of negative edges in
C^{\prime}
as in
H
, thus
C^{\prime}
is balanced.

ii.

The only edge of
S_{2}
is negative. Every edge in
C^{\prime}
has the opposite sign as the corresponding edge in
H
, so there is the same number of negative edges in
C^{\prime}
as the number of positive edges in
H
.
H
has an even number of positive edges, so
C^{\prime}
is balanced.

The considerations above are exactly the same for
C^{\prime\prime}
, so we infer that both cycles are balanced and can be colored in
c
using colors
\pm\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
.

(b)

r=2k+1
. Let
C=H\times S_{2}
. It is easy to observe that
C
is a cycle with
2r
nodes and there are
r
pairs of edges with the same signs in
C
. It follows that
C
is a balanced cycle, so can be colored in
c
using colors
\pm\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
.

We note that if
0\in R_{1}
, then
S^{0}
is a matching, that is, a collection of paths
P_{2}
. In this case all
H\times S_{2}
consist of two vertex-disjoint paths
P_{2}
, which of course can be colored with color
0
.

It follows that graph
S^{\alpha}
can be colored using colors
\pm\alpha
and since
\bigcup\limits_{\pm\alpha\in R_{1}}S^{\alpha}=S
it follows that
S
can be colored using colors
R_{1}
, so
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
and
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.
∎

Now let us proceed to the tensor products of arbitrary graphs with trees:

Lemma 17
.

For an arbitrary graph
G
and a tree
T
it holds that
\Delta(G\times T)=\Delta(T)\Delta(G\times P_{2})
.

Proof.

It follows from
Lemma

15
that
\Delta(G\times P_{2})=\Delta(G)
and
\Delta(G\times T)=\Delta(T)\Delta(G)
, so
\Delta(G\times T)=\Delta(T)\Delta(G\times P_{2})
.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be arbitrary graphs,
P_{2}
be a path and
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
,
G_{P}=G_{1}\times P_{2}
. Then,
G
has a decomposition into exactly
m(G_{2})
copies of
G_{P}
. Moreover, every copy may be assigned a corresponding edge in
G_{2}
in such a way that two copies have any vertices in common if and only if their respective edges are adjacent in
G_{2}
.

Proof.

The lemma follows directly from the definition of a tensor product. We first consider the case
G_{2}=P_{2}
. In this case is clear that
G
has a decomposition into
m(G_{2})=1
copies of
G_{P}
, since
G=G_{P}
. That single copy has a corresponding edge in
G_{2}
—the only edge of
G_{2}
.

We observe that when a new edge is added to
G_{2}
, there appears a new copy of
G_{P}
in
G
, denoted by
H^{\prime}
, so intuitively we assign the new edge in
G_{2}
as a corresponding edge for
H^{\prime}
. It is easy to observe that
H^{\prime}
has some vertices in common with other copy
H^{\prime\prime}
if and only if the corresponding edges of
H^{\prime}
,
H^{\prime\prime}
are adjacent in
G_{2}
. It follows from the definition of a tensor product that if copies have vertices in common, there are exactly
\frac{n(G_{P})}{2}
of them.
∎

Theorem 19
.

Let
T
be a tree,
S_{1}=(G_{1}
,
\sigma_{1})
,
S_{2}=(T
,
\sigma_{2})
and
S=(G\times T,\sigma)=S_{1}\times S_{2}
. If
\chi^{\prime}(S_{1})=\Delta(S_{1})
, then
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

Proof.

Let
H=|S_{1}|\times P_{2}
. It follows from
Lemma

18
that
|S|
has a decomposition into exactly
m(T)
copies of graph
H
. Moreover, copies of
H
are connected in the same way as edges of
T
. It means that each edge of
T
has a respective copy of
H
in
S
and two copies of
H
share a set of vertices if and only if their respective edges are adjacent in
T
.

We observe that graph
S
can be switched to a switching equivalent in such a way that all the copies of
H
in the latter signed graph have exactly the same signs. That way we can consider them as copies of some
S^{\prime}=(H,\sigma)
– and without loss of generality we treat these copies of
S^{\prime}
as a decomposition of
S
.

Let
c^{\prime}
be an arbitrary optimal edge-coloring of
S^{\prime}
. Since
S^{\prime}=(S_{1}\times P_{2},\sigma)
, from
Theorem

16
we know that
c^{\prime}
uses exactly
\Delta(S^{\prime})
colors. By
k
let us denote
\lfloor\frac{\Delta(S^{\prime})}{2}\rfloor
. Note that
c^{\prime}
uses colors
\{\pm 1
,
\ldots
,
\pm k\}
and additionally color
0
in case when
\Delta(S^{\prime})
is odd.

We define sets
R_{1}
,
\ldots
,
R_{\Delta(T)}
such that
R_{i}=\{\pm((i-1)k+1)
,
\ldots
,
\pm ik\}
if
c^{\prime}
does not use color
0
(i.e. when
\Delta(S^{\prime})
is even), otherwise
R_{i}=\{0\}\cup\{\pm((i-1)k+1)
,
\ldots
,
\pm ik\}
. We observe that these sets contain colors that are shifted in such a way that
R_{1}\cup\ldots\cup R_{\Delta}(T)
contains all the colors
\pm 1
,
\ldots
,
\pm k\Delta(T)
and moreover
R_{i}\cap R_{j}\subseteq\{0\}
for any two distinct
i
,
j
.

We observe that any of the copies of graph
S^{\prime}
in
S
could be colored using any of the
R_{1}
,
\ldots
,
R_{\Delta(T)}
sets of colors and the coloring of that single copy would be correct.

Additionally, let
c^{\prime\prime}
be an arbitrary optimal edge-coloring of a tree
T
(without considering its signs). It is a well-known fact that such a coloring uses
\Delta(T)
colors, namely
\{1
,
\ldots
,
\Delta(T)\}
.

Now, we will construct a coloring
c
of
S
. We first color separately every copy
S^{\prime}_{i}
of
S^{\prime}
that we obtained from decomposition of
S
. We remind that by
Lemma

18
each such copy has an edge of
T
assigned to it, which we will denote by
e_{i}
. This way, we color
S^{\prime}_{i}
using only colors from the
R_{c^{\prime\prime}(e_{i})}
set and respecting the only requirement that we color all the copies such that corresponding edges of different copies get either the color
0
or the colors with a difference equal to some multiple of
k
—which by construction of the colors sets is always possible.

We observe that since
c^{\prime\prime}
is a correct coloring of
T
,
c
is
almost
a correct coloring of
S
—because there may arise conflicts in coloring
c
, but occurring only on edges colored with
0
, since it is the only color that might be shared between different sets
R_{i}
,
R_{j}
. It follows that in the case when
\Delta(S^{\prime})
is even, color
0
is not used in
c
at all and
c
is a proper coloring. Then
c
uses exactly
\Delta(T)\Delta(S^{\prime})=\Delta(S)
colors.

Consider now the case when
\Delta(S^{\prime})
is odd. Clearly, color
0
is used in the colorings of all the copies of
S^{\prime}
in
S
. By
X\subseteq S
we denote a subgraph containing all the edges colored by
c
with color
0
. We will show the procedure of recolloring incidences of
X
to get a correct coloring of
S
. It is easy to observe that
X
is an acyclic graph. We note that each copy of
S^{\prime}
has a corresponding edge in
T
, each copy has exactly the same edges colored using color
0
and the edges colored with
0
in a given copy create a matching. It is also easy to observe that
\Delta(X)=\Delta(T)
.

We observe that
c
uses exactly
\Delta(T)(\Delta(S^{\prime})-1)
colors other than
0
. We note that
\Delta(T)(\Delta(S^{\prime})-1)
is even since
\Delta(S^{\prime})
is odd. Since signed forests are always
\Delta
-edge-colorable
[
16
]
, we can easily color
X
using a symmetric set of
\Delta(T)
colors that are different than all the colors used by
c
on the edges of
S
that do not belong to
X
. That way the new coloring uses exactly
\Delta(T)(\Delta(S^{\prime})-1)+\Delta(T)=\Delta(T)\Delta(S^{\prime})=\Delta(S)
colors.
∎

Since every path is a tree and every signed path can be edge-colored using
\Delta
colors, it follows that:

Corollary 20
.

Let
S_{1}=(P_{r}
,
\sigma_{1})
,
S_{2}=(P_{s}
,
\sigma_{2})
and
S=S_{1}\times S_{2}
. If
r
,
s>1
, then
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
. ∎

This, together with
Theorem

2
and the fact that

are exactly graphs with no edges proves that the tensor products of paths always belong to class
1^{\pm}
.

4
Strong products

Let
S_{1}=(G_{1}
,
\sigma_{1})
,
S_{2}=(G_{2}
,
\sigma_{2})
be signed graphs. The strong product of graphs
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
is a signed graph
S=(G
,
\sigma)
, such that
V(S)=V(S_{1})\times V(S_{2})
and there is an edge
e=(u
,
u^{\prime})(v
,
v^{\prime})
in
S
, where
u
,
v\in V(S_{1})
,
u^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}\in V(S_{2})
, if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

1.

u=v
,
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(S_{2})
. In such case
\sigma(e)=\sigma_{2}(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})
;

2.

uv\in E(S_{1})
,
u^{\prime}=v^{\prime}
. In such case
\sigma(e)=\sigma_{1}(uv)
;

3.

uv\in E(S_{1})
and
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(S_{2})
. In such case
\sigma(e)=\sigma_{1}(uv)\sigma_{2}(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})
.

We denote
S
by
S_{1}\boxtimes S_{2}
. Clearly,
|S|=G_{1}\boxtimes G_{2}
. It is clear that
S_{1}\boxtimes S_{2}
has a decomposition into
S_{1}\square S_{2}
and
S_{1}\times S_{2}
. We observe that
\deg_{S}(u
,
u^{\prime})=\deg_{S_{1}\square S_{2}}(u
,
u^{\prime})+\deg_{S_{1}\times S_{2}}(u
,
u^{\prime})=\deg_{S_{1}}(u)+\deg_{S_{2}}(u^{\prime})+\deg_{S_{1}}(u)\deg_{S_{2}}(u^{\prime})
, thus it follows that
\Delta(S)=\Delta(S_{1}\square S_{2})+\Delta(S_{1}\times S_{2})=\Delta(S_{1})+\Delta(S_{2})+\Delta(S_{1})\Delta(S_{2})
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
be signed graphs and
H_{1}=S_{1}\square S_{2}
,
H_{2}=S_{1}\times S_{2}
,
S=S_{1}\boxtimes S_{2}
. If either of
\Delta(H_{1})
,
\Delta(H_{2})
is even,
\chi^{\prime}(H_{1})=\Delta(H_{1})
and
\chi^{\prime}(H_{2})=\Delta(H_{2})
, then
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\Delta(H_{2})
is even.

Let
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
be arbitrary optimal colorings of graphs
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
, respectively.
c_{1}
uses colors
\pm 1
, …,
\pm\lfloor\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}\rfloor
and possibly color
0
if
\Delta(H_{1})
is odd, and
c_{2}
uses colors
\pm 1
, …,
\pm\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2}
. It is clear that
H_{1}
,
H_{2}\subseteq S
and
H_{1}\cup H_{2}=S
. Let
c
be an edge coloring of
S
such that:

c(u\mbox{:}uv)=\begin{cases}c_{1}(u\mbox{:}uv),&\mbox{if }uv\in E(H_{1})\mbox{;}\\
c_{2}(u\mbox{:}uv)+\lfloor\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}\rfloor,&\mbox{if }uv\in E(H_{2})\mbox{ and }c_{2}(u\mbox{:}uv)>0\mbox{;}\\
c_{2}(u\mbox{:}uv)-\lfloor\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}\rfloor,&\mbox{if }uv\in E(H_{2})\mbox{ and }c_{2}(u\mbox{:}uv)<0\mbox{.}\end{cases}

We observe that incidences of
H_{1}
are colored by
c
using the same colors as by
c_{1}
, so their coloring is proper. The set of colors used to color incidences of graph
H_{2}
has been shifted from
\pm 1
, …,
\pm\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2}
to
\pm(\lfloor\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}\rfloor+1)
, …,
\pm(\lfloor\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}\rfloor+\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2})
. Clearly, incidences of
H_{2}
are properly colored in
c
. It is easy to observe that the sets of colors used to color incidences of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in
c
are disjoint. We consider two cases:

1.

\Delta(H_{1})
is even. Graph
S
is colored using a set of colors
R=\{\pm 1
, …,
\pm(\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}+\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2})\}
. Therefore
|R|=2(\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}+\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2})=\Delta(H_{1})+\Delta(H_{2})=\Delta(S)
, so
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

2.

\Delta(H_{1})
is odd. Graph
S
is colored using a set of colors
R=\{0
,
\pm 1
, …,
\pm(\lfloor\frac{\Delta(H_{1})}{2}\rfloor+\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2})\}
. Therefore
|R|=1+2(\frac{\Delta(H_{1})-1}{2}+\frac{\Delta(H_{2})}{2})=\Delta(H_{1})+\Delta(H_{2})=\Delta(S)
, so
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

∎

Theorem 22
.

Let
S_{1}=(P_{r}
,
\sigma_{1})
,
S_{2}=(P_{s}
,
\sigma_{2})
and
S=(P_{r}\boxtimes P_{s},\sigma)=S_{1}\boxtimes S_{2}
. If

or

, then
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

. Let
H_{1}=S_{1}\square S_{2}
,
H_{2}=S_{1}\times S_{2}
. We observe that
H_{1},H_{2}
is a decomposition of

and consider 3 cases:

1.

r=1
and
s\geq 2
. Graph
S
is a path, so can be colored using
\Delta(S)
colors (see
Theorem

2
).

2.

r=2
and
s=2
. Graph

is a complete graph

. It follows from
Corollary

7
that incidences of graph

can be colored using colors
\pm 1
. Graph

is a matching so its incidences can be colored using color

. It follows that graph

can be colored using colors

,
\pm 1
, so

.

3.

r=2
and

. It follows from

that incidences of graph

can be colored using colors

,
\pm 1
. It follows from

that incidences of

can be colored using colors

, so they can also be colored using

. It follows that graph

can be colored using colors

,
\pm 1
,

, so

.

4.

and

. We observe that

that

and from

∎

5
Corona products

Intuitively, a corona product of graphs
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
consists of graph
G_{1}
,
n(G_{1})
copies of graph
G_{2}
and edges connecting the
i
-th vertex of
G_{1}
with all the vertices of the
i
-th copy of graph
G_{2}
(see example in
Figure

4
).
More formally, the corona product of graphs
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
is a graph
G=G_{1}\odot G_{2}
, such that
V(G)=V(G_{1})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n(G_{1})}V(G_{2}^{i})
, where
G_{2}^{i}
denotes an
i
th copy of graph
G_{2}
, and
E(G)=E(G_{1})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n(G_{1})}E(G_{2}^{i})\cup\{(u,v)\colon u\in V(G_{1}),v\in V(G_{2}^{i}),\text{and $u$ is the $i$-th vertex of $G_{1}$}\}
.

In this section, we consider the edge coloring problem of signed corona products of graphs.
It turns out that the signature of
\sigma
of a signed corona product
S=(G_{1}\odot G_{2}
,
\sigma)
does not matter for the chromatic index of
S
as far as
\Delta(G_{1})\geq 2
.

Figure 4
:
An example corona product
P_{4}\odot K_{3}
. Edges of
P_{4}
and copies of
K_{3}
are marked with solid lines. Edges connecting them are marked with dashed lines.

Theorem 23
.

Let
S=(G_{1}\odot G_{2}
,
\sigma)
be a signed corona product of graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. If
\Delta(G_{1})\geq 2
, then
\chi^{\prime}(S)=\Delta(S)
.

Proof.

First, we note that
\Delta(S)=\Delta(G_{1})+n(G_{2})
.

Let
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n(G_{1})}
be any sequence of vertices in
G_{1}
and
G_{2,1}
,
G_{2,2}
, …,
G_{2,n(G_{1})}
be a sequence of copies of graph
G_{2}
such that
v_{i}
is the only vertex connecting graph
G_{1}
with graph
G_{2,i}
.

By
S_{1}
we denote a subgraph of
S
that has all the vertices and edges of

Analogously,

Our main idea is to start from some edge coloring

of

Clearly, for

Now, we have two possible cases:

, so we set

as

2.

M_{\Delta(S_{1})+1}\nsubseteq M_{\Delta(S)}

0\notin M_{\Delta(S)}
– and then we define

\displaystyle c_{0}(v\text{:}e)=\begin{cases}\pm\frac{\Delta(S)}{2}&\text{if $c(v$:$e)=0$,}\\
c(v\text{:}e)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that since
0\notin M_{\Delta(S)}
,
\Delta(S)
is even and the resulting coloring is correct, since
\pm\frac{\Delta(S)}{2}\notin M_{\Delta(S_{1})+1}

0

Now, let us also fix
c^{\prime}_{i}
as an edge coloring of
S_{2,i}+v_{i}
with
n(G_{2})=\Delta(G_{2}+v)+1
colors.
Let
R^{\prime}_{i}=\{c^{\prime}_{i}(v_{i},u)\colon u\in V(G_{2,i})\}
be the set of colors on the incidents of

in

Clearly

|R^{\prime}_{i}|=\deg_{S_{2,i}+v_{i}}(v_{i})=n(G_{2}+v)-1=|M_{n(G_{2})}|-1
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that

, since if the missing color is non-zero, then we can always swap pairs of colors
\pm\alpha
with
\pm\beta
(or possibly swap
\alpha
with

) in

in such a way that the missing color in

is

.

Let us proceed with a proof of the inductive step for some fixed

This proof will consist of two steps:
(a)

and mapping some pairs of colors from

to them, and
(b)
coloring the remaining edges (i.e. the ones with colors from

which were not mapped in the previous step). It turns out that this procedure always finishes successfully.

So we first initialize

to be tentatively equal to

Next, for
j=l^{\prime}+1,\ldots,n(G_{2})

(a1)

if there exists a color

such that
k,-k\in M_{\Delta(S)}
are both absent at

in

, then in

we assign
c_{i}(v
:

for all
c^{\prime}_{i}(v
:

(a2)

in
c_{i}
(in particular, the ones with the smallest absolute values, so that
|k_{1}|>|k_{2}|\geq 0
), then in
S_{2,i}+v_{i}
we assign
c_{i}(v
:
e)=\pm k_{1}
for all
c^{\prime}_{i}(v
:

(preserving the signs, but note that if

is negative, then

-k_{1}
,
-j

:

, for which we assign

:

(and the respective color

and it is used only for a single edge

Moreover, it is easy to check that after the above loop there hold the following invariant (X): if no incident of
v_{i}
is colored with

in

at this point, it means that we always fell in the case
(a1)
and never in

– as otherwise

There is also a second invariant. Let us note that if no incident of

is colored with

at this point, then either no incident of
v_{i}
is also colored with

in

It turns that neither

can, since

1.

if

was not present at all at

, then

cannot kick in, since there is
\{k,-k\}

will be invoked instead,

2.

-k
was used on some incident of
v_{i}
in
c_{i-1}
, then application of
(a2)
cannot violate the invariant.

Either way, we have an invariant (Y): if no incident of

is colored with

in

, then no incident from

is colored with

in

.

Now we have to split the remainder of the proof into several cases:

(b1)

if

, then it is sufficient to set

:

:

.

(b2)

, then we also have to recolor only incidents with

which still not used by any incident of

in

(b3)

,

, and

(c)

(b4)

, and

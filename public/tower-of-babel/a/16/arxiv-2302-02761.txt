Achiral words
Shrinit Singh, A. Satyanarayana Reddy

Abstract
A word w w in a free group is achiral if for every group G , G, G w = G w − 1 , G_{w}=G_{w^{-1}}, where G w G_{w} is the image of the word map w w on G . G. We will give few classes of examples of achiral words. Cocke and Ho asked whether Engel words are achiral or not. We will prove that it is enough to apply Whitehead’s algorithm to check the same.

Introduction

Let
F_{n}
be a free group on
n
generators
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}.
A non-empty reduced word
w\in F_{n}
is an expression
\prod_{j=1}^{s}x_{i_{j}}^{a_{j}}
, where
i_{j}\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\},a_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
and for every
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,s\},

x_{i_{j}}\neq(x_{i_{j+1}})^{-1}.
In this paper, word, we mean a reduced word. The length of the reduced word
w
is defined by
\Sigma_{j=1}^{s}|a_{j}|.
For any group
G
, let
G^{(n)}
denote the direct product of
n
copies of
G
. Corresponding to the word
w
, the word map
w
on
G
is an evaluation map from
G^{(n)}
to
G
, defined as

(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})\mapsto w(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}g_{i_{j}}^{a_{j}}.

The image of the word map
w
on
G
is denoted by
G_{w}.
It is clear that
G_{w}
is nonempty as the identity element
e\in G_{w}.

For example, if
w=x_{1}x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{-1}\in F_{2}
is a word of length
4
and
G
is an abelian group, then
G_{w}=\{e\}=G_{w^{-1}}.

,

It is easy to see that the image of a word map on a group
G
corresponding to any given word is invariant under endomorphism of
G
. A group
G
is said to satisfy the
property
\mathcal{P}
if for every subset
S
of
G
with
e\in S
and
S
is invariant under every automorphism of
G
can be seen as an image of a word map. In 2012, Alexander Lubotzky
[
10
]
proved that all finite simple groups satisfy the property
\mathcal{P}.
Carolyn Ashurst
[
3
]
, in her thesis, asked that for any finite group
G
, whether under every word map, the cardinality of preimage of an element
g
is same as the cardinality of preimage of
g^{-1}.
Cocke and Ho
[
5
]
by using Lubotzky’s result showed that this is not true and defined
chiral word
. A word
w\in F_{n}
is said to be
chiral
if there exists a group
G
such that
G_{w}\neq G_{w^{-1}}
. We define a word to be
achiral
if it is not
chiral
. A group
G
is said to be
chiral
if there exists a word
w\in F_{n}
for some
n
, such that
G_{w}\neq G_{w^{-1}}
. If
G
is not chiral then we call
G
as
achiral
. Let
X
be a subset of a group
G
. Then
X
is inverse closed if
X=X^{-1},
where
X^{-1}=\{g^{-1}|g\in X\}.
It is clear that
{G_{w}}^{-1}=G_{w^{-1}}
. Hence a word
w
is achiral if for every group
G,

G_{w}=(G_{w})^{-1}.
That is a word
w
is achiral if and only if
G_{w}
is inverse closed for every group
G.
For example, the sets
G^{k}=\{g^{k}|g\in G\},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are inversely closed for any group
G,
hence the words
w=x_{1}^{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are achiral. Consequently, every word from
F_{1}
is achiral. Let
G
be a group then the subset
S=\{ghg^{-1}h^{-1}|g,h\in G\}
of
G
is inverse closed. Hence the word
w=x_{1}x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{-1}
is achiral. If the word map
w
is onto for every group
G

i.e.,

G_{w}=G,
then
w
is achiral. For example,
w=x_{3}x_{1}x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{-1}\in F_{3}
and
w=x_{1}x_{2}x_{1}x_{2}^{2}\in F_{2}
are achiral. Let

A_{n}=\{w\in F_{n}|\;\mbox{$w$ is achiral}\}

B_{n}=\{w\in F_{n}|\;\mbox{for every group}\;G,\;G_{w}=G\}.

Then it is clear that
B_{n}\subseteq A_{n}.

It is known that the Nielsen transformations do not change the image of a word map in a group. Let
Aut(F_{n})
denote the set of all automorphisms of
F_{n}.
Thus if
w\in F_{n}
and
\sigma\in Aut(F_{n}),
then
G_{w}=G_{\sigma(w)}.
Hence the automorphic image of an achiral word is achiral. We will prove this result as a consequence of Proposition
2
in section
2
. This observation will help us to provide a few elements in
B_{n}.
A word
w\in F_{n}
is said to be
primitive
if
w
can be a part of a basis of
F_{n}
. A primitive word will always be achiral as it is an automorphic image of achiral word
x_{1}
. Since
x_{1}\in B_{n},
the set of all primitive words is a subset of
B_{n}.
But there are words in
B_{n}
which are not primitive , for example
x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{3}
. Akbar Rhemtulla
[
16
]
gave a complete description of

which is as follows.

(Akbar Rhemtulla

Let

Then
w\in B_{n}
if and only if there exists integers

gcd(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n})=1
such that
w\in{x_{1}}^{e_{1}}\ldots{x_{n}}^{e_{n}}{F_{n}}^{\prime},
where
{F_{n}}^{\prime}

It is known
[
1

that the probability of an element
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that

gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})=1
is
\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}.
Hence the probability for a word
w\in F_{n}
belongs to
A_{n}
is positive. In section

w\in F_{n}
is achiral. By using this characterization, one can get more elements in

In the last subsection we will prove that it is enough to apply Whitehead’s algorithm for Engel word’s achirality.

Main Results

The following result was stated in the thesis
of William Cocke
[
4
]
. We are giving the proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2
.

Following two statements are equivalent for a word
w\in F_{n}
:

1.

w\in A_{n}.

2.

There exists an endomorphism
\psi
of
F_{n}
such that
\psi(w)=w^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
w\in F_{n}.
We can view
w
as a word map on
F_{n}
as
w:{F_{n}}^{n}\longrightarrow F_{n}
and
w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=w

i.e.,

w\in(F_{n})_{w}.
Suppose
w
is achiral. If we take
G=F_{n}
, then
w^{-1}\in{(F_{n})}_{w^{-1}}={(F_{n})}_{w}.
It implies that there exists
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in F_{n}
such that
w(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})=w^{-1}
. This suggests that the endomorphism
\psi
of
F_{n}
defined by the map
x_{i}\mapsto w_{i}

\forall i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
will give
\psi(w)=w^{-1}.

Let
\psi
be an endomorphism of
F_{n}
such that
\psi(w)=w^{-1}.
Let
G
be a group and
g\in G_{w}.

Then there exists
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
such that
w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=g.
We have

\psi(w(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}))=\psi(w)=w^{-1}.

Thus
g^{-1}=w^{-1}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=w((\psi(x_{1}),\ldots,\psi(x_{n}))(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}))\in G_{w}.

∎

Let
End(F_{n})
denote the set of all endomorphisms of
F_{n}.
Then
w\in F_{n}
is achiral if and only if there exist
\sigma\in End(F_{n})
such that
\sigma(w)=w^{-1}.

The following observations are immediate from the Proposition
2
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
w\in F_{n}.

1.

If
w
is achiral, then
\sigma(w)
will also be achiral for all
\sigma\in Aut(F_{n}).

2.

w^{n}
is achiral for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

3.

If
w^{n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
is achiral then so is
w
.

Proof of Part
1
.

Since
w
is achiral, there exists an endomorphism
\phi
taking
w
to
w^{-1}
. Then the endomorphism defined by
\sigma\circ\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}
takes
\sigma({w})
to
\sigma(w)^{-1}.

Proof of Part
2
.

Let
f:F_{n}\to F_{n}
be an endomorphism such that
f(w)=w^{-1}
. Then
f(w^{k})={f(w)}^{k}=w^{-k}\;\;\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof of Part
3
.

Let
\phi
be endomorphism inverting the word
w^{n}
. Suppose
\phi(w)=u
, then we have
\phi(w^{n})=u^{n}=w^{-n}
. Hence
u=w^{-1}
.

We have seen that the automorphic image of an achiral word will always be achiral. Cocke and Ho
[
5
]
proved that a homomorphic image of achiral group will always be achiral group. But a homomorphic image of achiral word need not be achiral. In particular, let
w
be a chiral word, then we can construct a required homomorphism
\phi
such that
\phi(x_{1})=w.
The next result states that a word being achiral is independent of free groups.

Proposition 4
.

Let
w\in F_{m}\subseteq F_{n}
for
m\leq n.
If
w
is achiral (chiral) in
F_{m}
if and only if
w
is achiral (chiral) in
F_{n}
.

Proof.

We will prove for achiral, chiral part will automatically follow. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
be a basis of
F_{m}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, extending the basis of
F_{m}
, be the chosen basis of
F_{n}
. Let
i
be the inclusion map
F_{m}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}F_{n}
and
\alpha
be the surjection map
F_{n}\overset{\alpha}{\twoheadrightarrow}F_{m}
defined by the map
\alpha(x_{i})
goes to
x_{i}
if
1\leq i\leq m
,
1
otherwise.

Suppose
w
is achiral in
F_{m}.
Then there exists an endomorphism
\phi
of
F_{m}
such that
\phi(w)=w^{-1}.
We can extend this endomorphism to an endomorphism
\bar{\phi}
of
F_{n}
sending each basis element which is not in
F_{m}
to identity. Thus we have
\bar{\phi}(w)=w^{-1}.

Let
w
be achiral in
F_{n}.
Then there exists an endomorphism
\psi
of
F_{n}
such that
\psi(w)=w^{-1}.

F_{m}{\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}}F_{n}{\overset{\psi}{\rightarrow}F_{n}\overset{\alpha}{\twoheadrightarrow}}F_{m}

The composition of these maps
\alpha\circ\psi\circ i
is an endomorphism of
F_{m}
such that
\alpha\circ\psi\circ i(w)=w^{-1}.

∎

For a shorter proof one can use chiral words.

Remark 5
.

In fact the proof of above proposition also suggests that if there exists an automorphism of
F_{m}
inverting the word
w
if and only if there exists an automorphism of
F_{n}
inverting the word

Corollary 6
.

There exists an automorphism inverting the following words.

1.

Every palindromic word is achiral.

2.

Every word of type
x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}
, where
m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is achiral.

3.

Every word of type
x_{1}^{m}x_{2}^{\epsilon_{1}}x_{1}^{n}x_{2}^{\epsilon_{2}}
where
m,n,\epsilon_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\epsilon_{1}=\pm\epsilon_{2}
is achiral.

Proof of Part
1
.

Let
w\in F_{n}
be a palindromic word. Take the automorphism generated by
f(x_{i})={x_{i}}^{-1}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.
Then
f(w)=w^{-1}.

Proof of Part
2
.

Here we prove the existence of automorphism inverting the word for
F_{2}
. Hence by remark of previous proposition the result holds. It is enough to prove for
x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}
. Take automorphism
f
generated by sending
x_{1}
to
x_{2}^{-m_{2}}x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{m_{2}}
and
x_{2}
to
x_{2}^{-1}
. Then
f(x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}})=x_{2}^{-m_{2}}x_{1}^{-m_{1}}.

Proof of Part
3
.

Here we prove the existence of automorphism inverting the word for
F_{2}
. Hence by remark of previous proposition the result holds. When
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}
, take automorphism generated by sending
x_{1}
to
x_{1}^{-1}
and
x_{2}
to
x_{1}^{m}x_{2}^{-1}x_{1}^{-m}
. If
\epsilon_{1}=-\epsilon_{2}
, take automorphism generated by sending
x_{1}
to
x_{1}^{-1}
and
x_{2}
to
x_{1}^{m}x_{2}x_{1}^{-m}.

Makanin
[
11
]
has proved that whether a system of equations over a free group has a solution or not is algorithmically decidable. The following result shows that the achirality of a word is a decidable problem.

Lemma 7
.

Showing achirality of a word
w
in a free group
F_{n}
is equivalent to solving an equation over
F_{n}.

Proof.

Let
w=\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{i_{j}}^{t_{j}}\in F_{n}
be achiral. From the Proposition
2
there exist an endomorphism
\phi
of
F_{n}
such that
\phi(w)=w^{-1}.
Since endomorphism of a free group is determined by the images of its generators, let
\phi(x_{i})=w_{i}
So
\phi(w)=\prod_{j=1}^{l}w_{i_{j}}^{t_{j}}
. Thus
w
is achiral if and only if

\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{i_{j}}^{t_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}w_{i_{j}}^{t_{j}}=1.

Hence checking a given word
w\in F_{n}
is achiral is equivalent to solving an equation in
F_{n}.

∎

2.1
Achiral words in
F_{2}

Let
F_{2}
be a free group of rank
2
with alphabet
X=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}.
If
w\in F_{2},
then
w
is reduced word with letters
x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1}.
We denote the length of word
w
by

\ell(w).
Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
denote the set of all words of length
n

i.e.,

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}=\{w\in F_{2}|\ell(w)=n\}.

It is easy to see that

|{\mathcal{W}}_{n}|=4\cdot 3^{n-1}.
For example,

{\mathcal{W}}_{1}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1}\}.

{\mathcal{W}}_{2}=\{x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{1}^{-2},x_{2}^{-2},x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{-1},x_{2}x_{1},x_{2}^{-1}x_{1}^{-1},x_{1}^{-1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{2}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1}x_{1},x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}\}.

Let

{\mathcal{W}}^{n}=\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{n}|\exists\sigma\in\;Aut(F_{2}),\sigma(w)=w^{-1}\}.

Hence
{\mathcal{W}}^{n}\subseteq A_{2}.

Before we proceed, given an automorphism
f\in\;Aut(F_{2})
, we define

{\mathcal{W}}_{f}^{n}=\{w\in{\mathcal{W}}^{n}|f(w)=w^{-1}\}.

If
f(x_{1})=x_{1}^{-1},f(x_{2})=x_{2}^{-1};g(x_{1})=x_{2}^{-1},g(x_{2})=x_{1}^{-1}
and
h(x_{1})=x_{2},h(x_{2})=x_{1},
then it is easy that

{\mathcal{W}}_{f}^{2}=\{x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{1}^{-2},x_{2}^{-2}\},{\mathcal{W}}_{g}^{2}=\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{-1},x_{2}x_{1},x_{2}^{-1}x_{1}^{-1}\},{\mathcal{W}}_{h}^{2}=\{x_{1}^{-1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{2}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1}x_{1},x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}\}.

Thus
{\mathcal{W}}_{2}={\mathcal{W}}^{2}.
That is every word of length
2
over
F_{2}
is achiral. From the observations listed in Corollary
3
and
6
, it is easy to see that

{\mathcal{W}}_{i}={\mathcal{W}}^{i}
for
i\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
and
W_{6}=A_{6}
but the word

and it is an achiral word of shortest length such that it is not inverted by any automorphism (see

]

2.2
Test word and achiral word

Let
w\in F_{n}.
We define

End_{w}=\{f\in End(F_{n})|f(w)=w^{-1}\}\;\;\;Aut_{w}=\{f\in Aut(F_{n})|f(w)=w^{-1}\}.

It is clear that
Aut_{w}\subseteq End_{w}.
From Proposition
2
we have
w\in A_{n}
if and only if
End_{w}\neq\emptyset.
Let
w\in F_{n}
be a primitive word. Then we can construct an endomorphism
\sigma
satisfying
\sigma\in End_{w}\setminus Aut_{w}.

As discussed earlier if
w=x^{2}y^{2}xy^{-1},
then
w\in A_{2}
that is
End_{w}\neq\emptyset
but
Aut_{w}=\emptyset.
Let
D_{n}=\{w\in A_{n}|End_{w}=Aut_{w}\}.

Definition 8
.

A word
w\in F_{n}
is a test word if every endomorphism which fixes
w
is an automorphism.

We denote set of all test words of
F_{n}
as
T_{n}
that is

T_{n}=\{w\in F_{n}|\;\mbox{if $\sigma\in End(F_{n})$ with $\sigma(w)=w,$ then $\sigma\in Aut(F_{n})$}\}.

Turner
[
17
]
gave a criterion to check a given word is a test word or not.

Definition 9
.

A subgroup
H
of
G
is said to be retract if there exists an endomorphism
\phi
such that
\phi:G\longrightarrow H
with the property
\phi(h)=h
for all
h\in H.

Lemma 10

(Turner
[
17
]
)
.

A word
w\in F_{n}
is a test word if and only if it is not in any proper retract of
F_{n}
.

Turner also showed that
T_{2}={F_{2}}^{\prime}\setminus\{e\}.

Theorem 11
.

If
n\in{\mathbb{N}},
then
T_{n}\cap A_{n}=D_{n}.

Proof.

Let
w\in(T_{n}\cap A_{n})\setminus D_{n}.
Then there exists
\phi\in End_{w}\setminus Aut_{w}.
Which leads to a contradiction that
\phi^{2}
fixes
w
hence
\phi\in Aut_{w}.
Hence
(T_{n}\cap A_{n})\setminus D_{n}=\emptyset
or equivalently
D_{n}\subseteq T_{n}\cap A_{n}.
Let
w\in T_{n}\cap A_{n}
and
\phi\in End_{w}.
Then
\phi^{2}
fixes
w.
Hence
\phi^{2}\in Aut(F_{n})
so is
\phi.
Thus
\phi\in Aut_{w}.

∎

The above theorem suggests that all words in
T_{n}\setminus D_{n}
are chiral. Cocke and Ho has given an explicit example of chiral word
[x^{440}(x^{440})^{(y^{440})}x^{440},(y^{440})^{(x^{440}y^{440})}y^{440}]
which lies inside
T_{2}\setminus D_{2}
in
F_{2}
. They asked whether engel words
e_{n}=[x,_{n}y]
are achiral or not. From Theorem
11
it is enough to check for an automorphism inverting
e_{n}
. For this case, we have Whitehead’s algorithm
[
18
]
.

It is easy to see that
B_{1}=\{x,x^{-1}\}
and
D_{1}=T_{1}=F_{1}\setminus\{e\}.
But if
n\geq 2,
then we have
B_{n}\cap T_{n}=\emptyset.
As let
w\in B_{n}.
Take
H=\langle w\rangle.
We will show that
H
is a proper retract of
F_{n}
. Hence
w
can’t be a test word. Write
w=x_{1}^{r_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{r_{n}}c
where
c\in{F_{n}}^{\prime}
. Since
w\in B_{n},
we have
gcd(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})=1.
Hence there exists
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}
such that
r_{1}m_{1}+\ldots+r_{n}m_{n}=1.
Define a homomorphism
\phi:F_{n}\longrightarrow H
generated by
\phi(x_{i})=w^{m_{i}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.
So we get
\phi(w)=w.
Hence
H
is a proper retract of
F_{n}.
So by Turner’s result,
w
can not be test word.

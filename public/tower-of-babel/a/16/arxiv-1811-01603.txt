Compact connected components in relative character varieties of punctured spheres
Nicolas Tholozan, Jérémy Toulisse

Abstract




Abstract.

We prove that some relative character varieties of the fundamental group of a punctured sphere into the Hermitian Lie groups
\textrm{SU}(p,q)
admit compact connected components. The representations in these components have several counter-intuitive properties. For instance, the image of any simple closed curve is an elliptic element. These results extend a recent work of Deroin and the first author, which treated the case of
\textrm{PU}(1,1)=\textrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Our proof relies on the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence between relative character varieties and parabolic Higgs bundles. The examples we construct admit a rather explicit description as projective varieties obtained via Geometric Invariant Theory.

Keywords.
Character varieties, parabolic Higgs bundles, Geometric Invariant Theory, quiver representations.

1. Introduction

Let
\Sigma_{g,s}
be an oriented surface of genus
g
with
s
punctures, let
\Gamma_{g,s}
denote its fundamental group and let
G
be a non compact semi-simple Lie group. The character variety
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{g,s},G)
is roughly the space of morphisms from
\Gamma_{g,s}
to
G
up to conjugation by
G
. Character varieties of closed surfaces (
i.e.
without puncture) carry a natural symplectic structure (constructed by Atiyah-Bott
[
AB83
]
and Goldman
[
Gol84
]
), which is preserved by the action of the mapping class group of the surface. The study of these character varieties, from a topological, algebraic, geometric or dynamical point of view, has attracted a lot of interest in the last decades.

The fundamental group of a surface with punctures is a free group, so its character varieties carry an action of the outer automorphism group of this free group. These character varieties, however, do not reflect all the geometric properties of the surface. Indeed, two punctured surfaces with the same Euler characteristic (such as
\Sigma_{0,3}
and
\Sigma_{1,1}
) have the same fundamental group, hence the same character varieties.

One can nevertheless endow these character varieties with some extra structure that truly depends on the topology of
\Sigma_{g,s}
. Indeed, Goldman’s construction has been extended to define a Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{g,s},G)
(
[
GHJW97
,
Law09
]
), which is preserved by the action of the mapping class group of
\Sigma_{g,s}
. On the smooth locus, this Poisson structure defines a foliation by symplectic leaves. These leaves are the so called
relative character varieties
, and consist of representations of
\Gamma_{g,s}
into
G
for which the conjugacy classes of the images of the loops going around the punctures are fixed. Given an element
h\in G^{s}
, we will denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{g,s},G)
the relative character variety whose representations map the loop around the
i
th
puncture to the conjugacy class of
h_{i}
(here
i\in\{1,...,s\}
). In many aspects, these relative character varieties appear as the analogs for punctured surfaces of character varieties.

Deroin and the first author studied in
[
DT19
]
the relative character varieties of the punctured sphere
\Sigma_{0,s}
into
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\cong\textrm{PSU}(1,1)
. For all
s\geq 3
, they exhibit representations with surprising properties that they call
supra-maximal representations
, and prove that these representations form compact connected components of certain relative character varieties. Prior to their work, such compact components were only known to exist for
s=4

[
BG99
]
.

The existence of such compact components may come out as a surprise, given the great flexibility of surface group representations. Indeed, Deroin–Tholozan’s supra-maximal representations have several counter-intuitive properties:

•

Though these representations are typically Zariski dense, the image of any
simple
closed curve has a fixed point in the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

•

More generally, the eigenvalues of the image of a closed curve
c
are bounded by a constant that depends only on the number of self-intersections of
c
.

•

For any such representation
\rho
and any identification of
\Sigma_{0,s}
with a punctured Riemann sphere, there exists a
\rho
-equivariant holomorphic map from
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0,s}
to
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

The goal of the present paper is to construct such components for a larger class of Lie groups.

To this end, we will exploit a very powerful tool for understanding the topology of relative character varieties: the
non-Abelian Hodge correspondence
. When we fix a Riemann surface structure on the closed surface
\Sigma_{g,0}
, this correspondence associates to every irreducible linear representation of
\Gamma_{g,0}
a
stable Higgs bundle
over the Riemann surface. One can use tools from complex geometry to understand topological properties of the moduli space of Higgs bundles, and infer results on character varieties (see
[
Hit92
]
). The non-Abelian Hodge correspondence has been extended by Simpson and others
[
Sim90
,
BGPMiR20
]
to a correspondence between representations of punctured surfaces and
parabolic Higgs bundles
.

Most of the theorems (if not all) about the topology of (relative) character varieties of surfaces can be recovered and improved using non-Abelian Hodge theory. As a matter of fact, shortly after Deroin–Tholozan’s results, Mondello used non-Abelian Hodge theory to give a complete description of relative character varieties of punctured surfaces into
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})

[
Mon16
]
, showing in particular that Deroin–Tholozan’s compact components could be recovered via Higgs bundle techniques.

Here, we use non-Abelian Hodge theory to construct compact components in relative character varieties of punctured spheres into Lie groups of Hermitian type. We will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be one of the classical Hermitian Lie groups
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2n)
. For any
s\geq 3
, there exists a tuple
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})\in G^{s}
such that the relative character variety

\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{0,s},G)

has a compact connected component which contains a Zariski dense representation.

Remark 1.1
.

Most of the paper focuses on representations into
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
. We discuss
\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2n)
in Section
6.2
. Note that the only family of classical Hermitian Lie groups which our theorem does not cover is
\mathrm{SO}(2,n)
. See Section
6.2.3
.

The starting point of the proof is to convert this theorem into a result about moduli spaces of parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles. The general theory of Higgs bundles corresponding to representations into real Lie groups started with Hitchin’s paper
[
Hit87a
]
, and was fully sorted out by Garcia-Prada and Mundet i Riera
[
GPMiR04
]
, while the parabolic version of the theory was developped by Biquard, Garcia-Prada and Mundet i Riera
[
BGPMiR20
]
. Parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles have been specifically studied in
[
GPLM09
]
, where the authors chose to put aside the punctured sphere case.

Let us identify
\Sigma_{0,s}
with the punctured Riemann sphere
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\backslash\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. Very briefly, parabolic Higgs bundles on the punctured Riemann sphere corresponding to representations into
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
are the data of
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma,\delta)
where
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
are holomorphic vector bundles over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
of respective rank
p
and
q
with a
parabolic structure
at each puncture (which involves a choice of
weights
between
0
and
1
) and
\gamma
and
\delta
are meromorphic sections of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathcal{K}
and
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U})\otimes\mathcal{K}
respectively, with at most simple poles at the punctures and satisfying certain constraints depending on the weights (here
\mathcal{K}
is the canonical bundle on
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
).

One associates to representations into Hermitian Lie groups a topological invariant called the
Toledo invariant
(see Paragraph
2.1.1
), which is of main importance in the topology of character varieties. Under some open condition on the weights, we show that, if the Toledo invariant of the corresponding representation is small, then
\delta
must vanish for stable Higgs bundles. Though this condition is very restrictive, we prove that it can be satisfied for
s>2+\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}
, giving rise to compact components in relative character varieties. The underlying holomorphic bundles
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
in these components are both direct sums of a fixed line bundle. This fact permits a parametrization of these components by certain quiver varieties called
Kronecker varieties
. For more generic weights, the parabolic structure of the Higgs bundles give rise to a decoration of those quivers that we call
feathered Kronecker varieties
and construct using Geometric Invariant Theory.

This construction only works for
s
large enough. However, restricting these representations to subsurfaces, one can prove the existence of compact components for all
s
(see Section
6.3
).

Finally, let us point out that the representations we obtain have the same kind of properties as supra-maximal representations into
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be one of the classical Hermitian Lie groups
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2n)
. There exists an open set in
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_{0,s},G)
consisting of representations
\rho
satisfying the following properties:

•

For every homotopy class of simple closed curve
c
in
\Sigma_{0,s}
,
the complex eigenvalues of
\rho(c)
have modulus
1
.

•

More generally, for every
k\geq 0
, there is a constant
C(k)
such that, if
c
is the homotopy class of a closed curve with at most
k
self interserctions, then the eigenvalues of
\rho(c)
have modulus less than
C(k)
.

•

For every identification of
\Sigma_{0,s}
with a punctured Riemann sphere, there is a
\rho
-equivariant holomorphic map from
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{0,s}
to the symmetric space of
G
.

Structure of the article

Section
2
introduces some background notations and recalls briefly the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence between relative character varieties and parabolic Higgs bundles. In Section
3
, we recall some classical facts about Geometric Invariant Theory and its application to the construction of certain quiver varieties. Section
4
contains the core of our work. We give a condition for a component of the moduli space of parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles to be compact and prove that, for some specific choices of weights, this component is indeed non empty and isomorphic to a Kronecker variety. In Section
5
, we describe some properties of the representations in these components, proving Theorems
1
and
2
for
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
, under the condition
s>\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}
. Finally, in Section
6
, we discuss some extensions of our construction (to other Hermitian Lie groups, and to spheres with fewer punctures), concluding the proof of Theorems
1
and
2
.

2. Character varieties and Higgs bundles

2.1. Relative character varietes

Throughout the paper,
p
and
q
denote two positive integers. Let
\mathbb{C}^{p,q}
be the complex vector space
\mathbb{C}^{p+q}
equipped with the following Hermitian form of signature
(p,q)
:

\textbf{h}(z,w)=z_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+\cdots+z_{p}\overline{w}_{p}-\cdots-z_{p+q}\overline{w}_{p+q}.

We define
\mathrm{U}(p,q)
as the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(p+q,\mathbb{C})
preserving
h
and by
G=\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
the subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(p,q)
consisting of matrices of (complex) determinant
1
.

Given an element
g
in a semi-simple Lie group
G
, we define
C(g)
as the set of elements
h\in G
such that the closures of the conjugacy orbits of
g
and
h
intersect. For instance, if
g
is diagonalizable with distinct eigenvalues, then
C(g)
is exactly the conjugacy orbit of
g
. In general,
C(g)
consists in a finite union of conjugacy orbits, a single one of which is closed.

Let
\Sigma_{g,s}
denote the oriented surface of genus
g
with
s
punctures. We denote by
\Gamma_{g,s}
its fundamental group. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}
denote homotopy classes of loops going counter-clockwise around the punctures of
\Sigma_{g,s}
. Finally, fix
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})\in G^{s}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A morphism
\rho:\Gamma_{g,s}\to G
is
of type
h
if
\rho(c_{k})
belongs to
C(h_{k})
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. The
relative character variety

\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{g,s},G)
is defined as

\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{g,s},G)=\{\rho:\Gamma_{g,s}\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)\mid\rho(c_{k})\in C(h_{k})\}/\penalty\!/\penalty G\penalty\ ,

where
/\penalty\!/\penalty G
denotes the equivalence relation identifying two representations
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
when the closures of their conjugation orbits under
G
intersect.

Remark 2.2
.

If
G
is a complex algebraic group, then the quotient
/\penalty\!/\penalty G
is an algebraic quotient in the sense of Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT). For real Lie groups, however, some subtleties make it harder to define a nice algebraic quotient.

Remark 2.3
.

Most morphisms from
\Gamma
to
G
are irreducible and thus have a closed orbit under conjugation. Actually, if
s>0
, then all morphisms of type
h
are irreducible for a generic choice of
h
, so that
\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{g,s},G)
is simply the space of conjugacy classes of morphisms from
\Gamma_{g,s}
to
G
of type
h
.

2.1.1. Toledo invariant

The Lie group
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
is Hermitian, meaning that its symmetric space carries a
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-invariant Kähler structure. The Kähler form induces a continuous cohomology class
\omega
of degree
2
on
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
(seen as a topological group). Given a morphism
\rho:\Gamma_{g,0}\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
, one can pull back
\omega
by
\rho
to obtain an element of
H^{2}(\Gamma_{g,0},\mathbb{R})\cong H^{2}(\Sigma_{g,0},\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}
. For a surface with punctures, this construction does not work directly since
H^{2}(\Sigma_{g,s},\mathbb{R})=\{0\}
, but one can fix this issue using bounded cohomology
[
BIW10
]
. This eventually defines a function

\mathrm{Tol}:\mathfrak{X}(\Gamma_{g,s},G)\to\mathbb{R}

called the
Toledo invariant
. The Toledo invariant is continuous and additive in the following sense:

Proposition 2.4

(see
[
BIW10
]
)
.

If
b
is a simple closed curve on
\Sigma_{g,s}
separating
\Sigma_{g,s}
into two surfaces
\Sigma^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
, then for every representation
\rho:\Gamma_{g,s}\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
,

\mathrm{Tol}(\rho)=\mathrm{Tol}(\rho_{|\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})})+\mathrm{Tol}(\rho_{|\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime})|})\penalty\ .

For closed surfaces, the Toledo invariant takes values into
r\mathbb{Z}
for some rational number
r
(depending on the normalization of the cohomology class
\omega
). For punctured surfaces, the image of
\mathrm{Tol}
is not discrete, but the congruence of
\mathrm{Tol}(\rho)
modulo
r\mathbb{Z}
only depends on
C(h_{1}),\ldots,C(h_{s})

[
BIW10
]
. In particular, the Toledo invariant is constant on connected components of relative character varieties. We will denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{h}^{\tau}(\Sigma_{g,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q))
the set of representations
\rho\in\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{g,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q))
such that
\mathrm{Tol}(\rho)=\tau
. We will sometimes call this space a
relative component
, though it may not be connected in general.

2.2. Parabolic Higgs bundles

The non-Abelian Hodge correspondence, developed by Hitchin, Simpson and many others
[
Hit87a
,
Sim88
]
, gives a homeomorphism between the character variety of a closed Riemann surface and the moduli space of Higgs bundles over this surface. This correspondence was refined by Simpson
[
Sim90
]
and Biquard–García-Prada–Mundet i Riera
[
BGPMiR20
]
to a correspondence between relative character varieties of punctured Riemann surfaces and moduli spaces of
parabolic Higgs bundles
. In this subsection, we introduce parabolic Higgs bundles and state the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence in the case that interests us.

2.2.1. Parabolic vector bundles

Let
X
be a closed Riemann surface and
x
a point on
X
. By a vector bundle over
X
, we always mean a holomorphic vector bundle. We denote vector bundles with curly letters and use the same capital letter to denote the corresponding sheaf of holomorphic sections.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
X
, a
parabolic structure at
x\in X
is a filtration
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
of the germs of meromorphic sections of
\mathcal{E}
at
x
, such that

•

\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
is decreasing, that is
E_{\alpha^{\prime}}\supset E_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
.

•

\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
is left continuous,
i.e.
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
with
E_{\alpha-\varepsilon}=E_{\alpha}
.

•

E_{0}
are the germs of holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{E}
at
x
and
E_{\alpha+1}=E_{\alpha}(-x)
(that is,
s\in E_{\alpha}
if and only if
zs\in E_{\alpha+1}
, where
z
is a local coordinate centered at
x
).

Remark 2.6
.

Given a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
X
, one can always equip
\mathcal{E}
with the
trivial parabolic structure

\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
at
x\in X
by setting
E_{\alpha}=E_{0}(-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor x)
, where
E_{0}
is the space of germs of holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{E}
at
x
and
\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
is the integer part of
\alpha
.

Given two vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
with a parabolic structure at
x\in X
, their direct sum and tensor product are respectively defined to be the bundles
\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}
with the parabolic structure
\left\{(E\oplus F)_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
and
\{(E\otimes F)_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
at
x
where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\big(E\oplus F\big)_{\alpha}&=&E_{\alpha}\oplus F_{\alpha}\\
\big(E\otimes F\big)_{\alpha}&=&\mathrm{Span}\,\displaystyle{\left(\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha}E_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes F_{\alpha_{2}}\right)\penalty\ .}\end{array}\right.

If
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
is a subbundle and
\mathcal{E}
has a parabolic structure at
x
, then
\mathcal{F}
inherits a parabolic structure
\{F_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
where
F_{\alpha}
are the germs of meromorphic section of
\mathcal{F}
at
x
contained in
E_{\alpha}
.

Given a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
with a parabolic structure at
x\in X
, we call a
jump
of the filtration
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
a number
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
E_{\alpha}\neq E_{\alpha+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. The normalization condition
E_{\alpha+1}=E_{\alpha}(-x)
implies that the parabolic structure is fully determined by the set
\{(E_{\alpha_{1}},\alpha_{1}),\ldots,(E_{\alpha_{r}},\alpha_{r})\}
where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in[0,1)
are the jumps of the filtration in
[0,1)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{x}
the fiber of
\mathcal{E}
over
x
. The evaluation map

\textbf{ev}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}E_{0}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{E}_{x}\\
s&\longmapsto&s(x)\end{array}\right.,

has kernel
E_{1}=E(-x)
. In particular,
ev
gives an isomorphism
E_{0}/E_{1}\cong\mathcal{E}_{x}
and identifies
E_{\alpha_{i}}\subset E_{0}
with a linear subspace
\mathcal{E}_{x,i}\subset\mathcal{E}_{x}
. In other words, a parabolic structure at
x
is equivalent to a weighted flag

\mathcal{E}_{x}=\mathcal{E}_{x,1}\supset\mathcal{E}_{x,2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{E}_{x,r},\penalty\ 0\leq\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{r}<1.

Setting
k_{i}=\dim\left(\mathcal{E}_{x,i+1}/\mathcal{E}_{x,i}\right)
, the
parabolic type
of
\mathcal{E}
at
x
is the
n
-tuple
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\alpha}_{n})\in[0,1)^{n}
(where
n
is the rank of
\mathcal{E}
) such that
\alpha_{1}=\tilde{\alpha}_{1}=\cdots=\tilde{\alpha}_{k_{1}},\penalty\ \alpha_{2}=\tilde{\alpha}_{k_{1}+1}=\cdots=\tilde{\alpha}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}
and so on.

Definition 2.7
.

A morphism
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
of vector bundles with parabolic structure of type
\alpha
and
\beta
at
x\in X
is
parabolic
if
\alpha_{i}\geq\beta_{j}
implies
f(\mathcal{E}_{x,i})\subset\mathcal{F}_{x,j}
. It is
strongly parabolic
if
\alpha_{i}\geq\beta_{j}
implies

f(\mathcal{E}_{x,i})\subset\mathcal{F}_{x,j+1}
.

Given a punctured Riemann surface
X
(
i.e.
a Riemann surface with cusps) with closure
\overline{X}=X\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
, a
parabolic bundle of rank
n
over
X
will be a rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\overline{X}
together with a parabolic structure at each
x_{i}\in\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
such a parabolic bundle. The
parabolic type
of
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
is
\alpha=(\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{s})
where
\alpha^{i}=(\alpha^{i}_{1},\ldots,\alpha^{i}_{n})\in[0,1)^{n}
is the parabolic type of
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
at
x_{i}
. Finally, we denote by
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\mathcal{F}_{\bullet})
the bundle of strongly parabolic morphisms from
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
to
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
, and write
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})
.

The
dual
of a parabolic bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
is the parabolic bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\mathcal{O})
where
\mathcal{O}
is the trivial bundle equipped with the trivial parabolic structure. Note that the underlying bundle of
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}^{\vee}
is
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}(-D)
where

is the effective divisor

.

Definition 2.8
.

The
parabolic degree
of a parabolic bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
of type
\alpha
over
X
is the number

\deg(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\deg(\mathcal{E})+\|\alpha\|,

where
\|\alpha\|=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha^{i}_{j}
.

Example 2.9

(Parabolic line bundles over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
)
.

Recall that
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
carries a unique line bundle of degree
l
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, denoted
\mathcal{O}(l)
. Let
X=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
be a punctured Riemann sphere. A parabolic line bundle over
X
is thus given by a parabolic structure over some
\mathcal{O}(l)
at each
x_{i}
. Since a flag in
\mathcal{O}(l)_{x_{i}}
is necessarily trivial, such a parabolic structure is simply given by its type
\alpha^{i}\in[0,1)
. We will denote this parabolic line bundle by
\mathcal{O}\big(l+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\alpha^{i}x_{i}\big)
.

Consider now two parabolic line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{O}\big(l+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\alpha^{i}x_{i}\big)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{O}\big(m+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\beta^{i}x_{i}\big)
. Let
\varepsilon^{i}=\lfloor\alpha^{i}+\beta^{i}\rfloor\in\{0,1\}
be the integer part of
\alpha^{i}+\beta^{i}
and set
|\varepsilon|=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\varepsilon^{i}
. The tensor product
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}
is thus the line bundle of degree
l+m+|\varepsilon|
whose parabolic weight at
x_{i}
is
\alpha^{i}+\beta^{i}-\varepsilon^{i}\in[0,1)
.

If
\alpha^{i}<\beta^{i}
, then any morphism
f:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{M}
of line bundles is strongly parabolic at
x_{i}
. On the other hand, if
\alpha^{i}\geq\beta^{i}
, a morphism
f:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{M}
is strongly parabolic at
x_{i}
only if it comes from a morphism
f:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{M}(-x_{i})
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{L}_{\bullet},\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{O}(m-l-k)
, where
k
is the number of punctures
x_{i}
with
\alpha^{i}\geq\beta^{i}
.

2.2.2. Parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles

Recall that
X=\overline{X}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. We denote by
D
the effective divisor
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the canonical bundle of
X
.

Definition 2.10
.

A
parabolic Higgs bundle
over
X
is a pair
(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\Phi)
where
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
is a parabolic bundle and
\Phi
is a holomorphic section of
\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})
.

Remark 2.11
.

In this definition, the Higgs field
\Phi
is a strongly parabolic morphism from
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}
to
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{K}(D)
. Stricto sensu, this is the definition of a
strongly parabolic Higgs bundle
. In this paper, however, we limit ourselves to strongly parabolic Higgs bundles, so we omit this precision.

Definition 2.12
.

A parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundle is a parabolic Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\Phi)
such that

–

\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
where
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
are parabolic bundles of respective rank
p
and
q
.

–

With respect to the splitting
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
, the Higgs field
\Phi
takes the form
\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\delta\\
\gamma&0\end{array}\right)
where
\gamma\in H^{0}\big(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})\big)
and
\delta\in H^{0}\big(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\big)
.

–

\det(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})=\det(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})
has trivial parabolic structure and is isomorphic to the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}
.

We will denote parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles by
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
.

Definition 2.13
.

A parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
is
stable
(respectively
semistable
) if any
\gamma\oplus\delta
-invariant subbundle
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}^{\prime}
where
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
satisfies
\deg(\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet})<0
(respectively
\deg(\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet})\leq 0
). It is
polystable
if it is the direct sum of stable parabolic Higgs bundles of degree
0
.

The
parabolic type
of a parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
is the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
where
\alpha
and
\beta
are respectively the parabolic types of
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
.

Note that the parabolic type of the parabolic line bundle
\det(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})
at
x_{i}
is given by the quantity
\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{p}\alpha_{j}^{i}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{q}\beta_{j}^{i}
. The condition
\det(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{O}
implies that
\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{p}\alpha_{j}^{i}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{q}\beta_{j}^{i}
is integral. We denote by
\mathcal{W}(s,p,q)
the subset of
\left(\mathbb{R}^{p}\right)^{s}\times\left(\mathbb{R}^{q}\right)^{s}
consisting of tuples
(\alpha,\beta)
such that

0\leq\alpha_{1}^{i}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{p}^{i}<1\penalty\ ,

0\leq\beta_{1}^{i}\leq\cdots\leq\beta_{q}^{i}<1

and

\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{p}\alpha_{j}^{i}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{q}\beta_{j}^{i}\in\mathbb{N}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Elements of
\mathcal{W}(s,p,q)
will be called
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweights
.

2.2.3. Moduli spaces

Moduli spaces of semistable Higgs bundles can be constructed using Geometric Invariant Theory. This was done by Yokogawa in a fairly general setting which includes parabolic Higgs bundles
[
Yok95
,
Yok93
]
. In our setting, we have the following

Theorem 2.14
.

For every
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweight
(\alpha,\beta)
, there is a normal quasi-projective variety
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)
and, for every algebraic family of semistable parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles of type
(\alpha,\beta)
over a base
B
, an algebraic map
f_{B}:B\to\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)
such that:

•

Every point of
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)
is in the image of
f_{B}
for some family
B
,

•

given two such families over bases
B
and
B^{\prime}
and two points
x\in B
and
y\in B^{\prime}
, if the Higgs bundles attatched to these two points are polystable, then they are isomorphic if and only if
f_{B}(x)=f_{B^{\prime}}(y)
.

There is a continuous map

\varphi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}\\
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)&\longmapsto&\deg(\mathcal{U})-\deg(\mathcal{V})\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
, where
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)=\varphi^{-1}(d)
.

Remark 2.15
.

Using the equality
\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})+\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=\deg(\mathcal{U})+\deg(\mathcal{V})+\|\alpha\|+\|\beta\|=0
, one sees that if
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
, then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\deg(\mathcal{U})&=&-\frac{1}{2}(\|\alpha\|+\|\beta\|-d)\\
\deg(\mathcal{V})&=&-\frac{1}{2}(\|\alpha\|+\|\beta\|+d)\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

Example 2.16
.

In this example, we describe the case
p=q=1
and
X=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. Recall that
\mathcal{O}(d)
denotes the only line bundle of degree
d
over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

In a parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
,
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
are parabolic line bundles
\mathcal{O}(l+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\alpha^{i}x_{i})
and
\mathcal{O}(m+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\beta^{i}x_{i})
respectively. The condition
\det(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{O}
implies that
\alpha^{i}+\beta^{i}\in\{0,1\}
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
.

We now consider the case where
\alpha^{i}<\beta^{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. This implies that
\alpha^{i}=1-\beta^{i}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and
\deg(\mathcal{U})+\deg(\mathcal{V})=-s
so
d=\deg(\mathcal{U})-\deg(\mathcal{V})=2l+s
. One sees that if the parity of
d
is different from the one of
s
, then
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)=\emptyset
. Consider now the Higgs field. Since
\alpha^{i}<\beta^{i}
, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\gamma\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}))&=&H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))&=&H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(-2l-2))\\
\delta\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}))&=&H^{0}(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U}))&=&H^{0}\big(\mathcal{O}(2l+s-2))\end{array}\right..

Finally, let us describe a particular component. Suppose
s
is odd and look at the case
d=1
. In particular,
l=\deg(\mathcal{U})=-\frac{s-1}{2}
and
\deg(\mathcal{V})=-\frac{s+1}{2}
. It follows that

\deg(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U}))=2l+s-2=-1<0

and so
\delta=0
. One also sees that
\gamma\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(s-3))
. Such a parabolic Higgs bundle is stable if and only if
\|\beta\|=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\beta^{i}<\frac{s+1}{2}
and
\gamma\neq 0
.

Two such stable Higgs bundles given by
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(s-3))
are isomorphic if and only if
[\gamma]=[\gamma^{\prime}]
in
\mathbb{P}\left(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(s-3))\right)
. We thus have
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,1)\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{s-3}
.

This component corresponds to a lift to
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
of a supra-maximal component in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{PU}(1,1)
studied by Deroin and the first author in
[
DT19
]
. The goal of the present paper is to extend this phenomenon to
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
.

Definition 2.17
.

Given a rank
n
parabolic Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\Phi)
over
X
, one can associate a vector
\Pi_{\textrm{Hitchin}}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet},\Phi)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}(\mathrm{tr}(\Phi^{i}))_{1\leq i\leq n}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)^{i})
. This defines the
Hitchin fibration

\Pi_{\textrm{Hitchin}}:\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)^{i})\penalty\ .

The following was proven by Hitchin
[
Hit87b
]
for closed Riemann surfaces and extended by Yokogawa
[
Yok93
]
to the parabolic case:

Theorem 2.18
.

The Hitchin fibration is proper.

2.2.4. Non-abelian Hodge correspondence

Our main interest in the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)
of parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles of type
(\alpha,\beta)
is the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence
[
Sim90
,
BGPMiR20
]
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweight. Let
h=h(\alpha,\beta)
be the tuple of matrices
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
where
h_{k}
is the diagonal matrix of size
p+q
with diagonal coefficients

e^{2\pi i\alpha_{1}^{k}},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\alpha_{p}^{k}},e^{2\pi i\beta_{1}^{k}},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\beta_{q}^{k}}\penalty\ .

The definition of a
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweight implies that each
h_{k}
belongs to
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
.

Theorem 2.19

(Non-Abelian Hodge correspondence)
.

There exist a homeomorphism
\mathsf{NAH}
from the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)
to the relative character variety
\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q))
. Through this correspondence, stable Higgs bundles correspond to irreducible representations.

This correspondence is highly transcendental in the sense that it relies on the resolution of a geometric PDE on Hermitian metrics on the bundle. It is thus quite difficult to understand geometric properties of a representation associated to a given Higgs bundle. There are nevertheless a few attributes that transit well through the correspondence, among which is the Toledo invariant.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
be a polystable
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundle and let
\rho
be its image by
\mathsf{NAH}
. Then (with the proper normalization of the Toledo invariant) one has

\mathrm{Tol}(\rho)=\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\penalty\ .

Remark 2.21
.

This Proposition is proved in
[
GPLM09
]
, where a different normalization of the Toledo invariant is used. The one we use here is not standard, but it will simplify notations.

Recall that
\|\alpha\|
(resp.
\|\beta\|
) denotes the sum of the weights
\alpha_{j}^{i}
(resp.
\beta_{j}^{i}
) for all
j
and
i
. By definition, we have

\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})=\deg(\mathcal{V})-\deg(\mathcal{U})+\|\beta\|-\|\alpha\|\penalty\ .

It follows that the non Abelian Hodge correspondence restricts to a homeomorphism

\mathsf{NAH}:\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{X}_{h}^{\|\beta\|-\|\alpha\|-d}\left(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q)\right)\penalty\ .

2.2.5. Parabolic Higgs bundles over the Riemann sphere

In this paper, we consider the special case of
X=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
. Let us briefly recall the classification of holomorphic vector bundles over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Recall that we denote by
\mathcal{O}(d)
the unique line bundle of degree
d
over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Theorem 2.22

(Birkhoff–Grothendieck)
.

Any rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(d_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(d_{n})
for some
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Moreover, the tuple
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is unique.

We call
\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}(\mathcal{E})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
the
degree vector
of
\mathcal{E}
. Note that the degree of
\mathcal{E}
is

\deg\mathcal{E}=\deg\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{E}=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}\penalty\ .

The degree vector defines a map

(\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}_{1},\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}_{2}):\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}^{p}\times\mathbb{Z}^{q}\\
\left(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta\right)&\longmapsto&(\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}\penalty\ \mathcal{U},\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}\penalty\ \mathcal{V})\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

The components
\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}_{1}
and
\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}_{2}
are only upper semi-continuous for the lexicographic order on
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{q}
. Their level sets define a stratification of
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)
.

3. Geometric Invariant Theory and quiver varieties

In this section, we recall a few facts about Mumford’s Geometric Invariant Theory
[
Mum65
]
and apply it to the construction of certain quiver varieties called
Kronecker varieties
, as well as a mix between these and spaces of flag configurations, that we call
feathered Kronecker varieties
. These varieties will arise in our construction of compact components in the next sections.

3.1. GIT quotients of complex varieties

Let
Y
be a smooth quasi-projective variety with an algebraic action of a complex reductive algebraic group
G
. The goal of Geometric Invariant Theory (often abreviated in GIT) is to define a good notion of quotient of
Y
under the action of
G
in the category of complex algebraic varieties. We briefly recall here Mumford’s construction, which relies on the choice of an ample
G
-linearized line bundle. We restrict for simplicity to the case where
Y
is projective over an affine base. (This will be sufficient for our applications.)

We do not want to assume the action to be faithful. Let us denote by
Z
the kernel of the action, that is, the subgroup of
G
that acts trivially on
Y
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
G
-linearized line bundle

L
is a line bundle over
Y
together with an algebraic
G
-action on the total space of
L
that lifts the action on
Y
, and such that
Z
acts trivially on
L
.

Let
L
be a
G
-linearized ample line bundle and
k
an integer. The group
G
acts on the space of sections of
L^{k}
. Denote by
H^{0}(L^{k})^{G}
the subspace of
G
-invariant sections of
L^{k}
. The tensor product of sections defines a product
H^{0}(L^{k})^{G}\times H^{0}(L^{l})^{G}\to H^{0}(L^{k+l})^{G}
, endowing the space

R(L)^{G}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{+\infty}H^{0}(L^{k})^{G}

with the structure of a graded algebra.

Definition 3.2
.

The GIT quotient of the polarized variety
(Y,L)
by
G
is the projective scheme

Y^{L}/\penalty\!/\penalty G:=\mathrm{Proj}\left(R(L)^{G}\right)\penalty\ .

Note that
Y^{L}/\penalty\!/\penalty G
is a scheme over
\mathrm{Spec}\left(H^{0}(\mathcal{O})^{G}\right)
, where
H^{0}(\mathcal{O})^{G}
is the algebra of
G
-invariant functions on
Y
.

The main discovery of Mumford is that this algebraic construction has a geometric interpretation: the (closed) points of
Y^{L}/\penalty\!/\penalty G
“almost” parametrize the
G
-orbits in
Y
.

Definition 3.3
.

A point
x
in
(Y,L)
is called

•

semistable
if there exists
m>0
and a
G
-invariant section
s
of
L^{m}
such that
s(x)\neq 0
,

•

polystable
if it is semistable and its
G
-orbit is closed in the subset of semistable points,

•

stable
if it is polystable and its stabilizer is finite modulo
Z
,

•

unstable
if it is not semistable.

Let
Y^{ss}(L)
denote the space of semistable points in
(Y,L)
.
Y^{ss}(L)
is clearly open in
Y
for the Zariski topology (in particular, it is dense when non-empty). Let
Y^{ss}(L)/\penalty\!/\penalty G
denote the quotient of
Y^{ss}(L)
by the equivalence relation where
x\sim y
if the closures of the orbits of
x
and
y
in
Y^{ss}(L)
intersect.

Theorem 3.4

(Mumford)
.

The topological space
Y^{ss}(L)/\penalty\!/\penalty G
is homeomorphic to the variety
Y^{L}/\penalty\!/\penalty G
.

The semistability of a point
x
can be checked by looking at the action of one parameter subgroups of
G
, thanks to the
Hilbert–Mumford criterion
. This criterion is often stated for projective varieties, but it has been extended to other settings. We present here an
ad hoc
version adapted from
[
GHH15
, Corollary 1.1]
.

Let
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to G
be a
1
-parameter subgroup and
x
a point in
Y
. Let
x_{0}
denote the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot x
, when it exists. Then
x_{0}
is fixed by
\lambda(\mathbb{C}^{*})
and
\lambda(\mathbb{C}^{*})
thus acts linearly on the fiber
L_{x_{0}}
of
L
at
x_{0}
.

Definition 3.5
.

The
Hilbert–Mumford weight

\mu_{L}(\lambda,x)
is the integer
m
such that

\lambda(t)\cdot v=t^{-m}v

for all
v\in L_{x_{0}}
and all
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. If the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot x
does not exist, we set
\mu_{L}(\lambda,x)=+\infty
.

Theorem 3.6

(Hilbert–Mumford criterion)
.

Assume that
Y=Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
where
Y_{1}
is affine and
Y_{2}
is projective, and that the
G
-action on
Y
is induced by algebraic
G
-actions on
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
. Let
L
be a
G
-linearized ample line bundle on
Y
. Then a point
x
in
(Y,L)
is semistable if and only if

\mu_{L}(\lambda,x)\geq 0

for every
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to G
. It is stable if the inequality is strict unless
\lambda
is trivial.

This stability criterion will prove particularly handy when we look at GIT quotients of products. Indeed, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(Y_{i},L_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
be a family of quasi-projective varieties with an algebraic
G
-action and a
G
-linearized ample line bundle. Let
Y
denote the product
\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
with the diagonal action of
G
. Let
p_{i}:Y\to Y_{i}
denote the projection to the
i
-th factor and let
L
be the
G
-linearized line bundle on
Y
defined as

L=\bigotimesop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{*}L_{i}\penalty\ .

Then for every
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in
Y
and every
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to G
, we have

\mu_{L}(\lambda,x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{L_{i}}(\lambda,x_{i})\penalty\ .

3.2. Kronecker varieties

Let
p,q,r
be positive integers. The actions of
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{GL}(q,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}
induce a linear action of the group

G_{p,q}\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}(q,\mathbb{C})

on the space

E(p,q,r)=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{p},\mathbb{C}^{q})^{r}

given by

(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot(A_{j})_{1\leq j\leq r}=(g_{2}\circ A_{j}\circ g_{1}^{-1})_{1\leq j\leq r}\penalty\ .

The kernel of this action is the central subgroup
Z_{p,q}=\mathbb{C}^{*}(\mathrm{I}_{p},\mathrm{I}_{q})
.

Let
\chi:G_{p,q}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
denote the character defined by

\chi:(g_{1},g_{2})\mapsto\det(g_{2})^{p^{\prime}}\det(g_{1})^{-q^{\prime}}\penalty\ ,

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{\gcd(p,q)}
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{\gcd(p,q)}
. This character induces an action of
G_{p,q}
on the trivial bundle
E(p,q,r)\times\mathbb{C}
over
E(p,q,r)
, with kernel
Z_{p,q}
, defined by

g\cdot(x,z)=(g\cdot x,\chi^{-1}(g)z)\penalty\ .

This provides
E(p,q,r)\times\mathbb{C}
with the structure of a
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundle, that we denote by
L_{\chi}
.

Remark 3.8
.

Since every line bundle over
E(p,q,r)
is trivial and since every character on
G_{p,q}
with kernel containing
Z_{p,q}
is a power of
\chi
, every
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundle over
E(p,q,r)
is a power of
L_{\chi}
.

We can now define a GIT quotient of the semistable locus of
E(p,q,r)
under the action of

. This quotient is a particular case of quiver variety sometimes refered to as a Kronecker moduli space (see for instance
[

]
). Here we call it a

Definition 3.9
.

The
Kronecker variety

\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
is the GIT quotient

E(p,q,r)^{L_{\chi}}/\penalty\!/\penalty G_{p,q}\penalty\ .

Note that the only

-invariant regular functions on

are constant (see
[
Kin94
]
). Thus
\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
is a (reduced) projective scheme over
\mathbb{C}
, that is, a projective variety.

Remark 3.10
.

The Kronecker varieties
\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
are a particular case of
quiver varieties
. More precisely,
\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
is the representation variety of the Kronecker quiver :

Figure 1.
The Kronecker quiver:
(r)
indicates
r
copies of the same oriented arrow while
p
and
q
indicate the dimensions of the vector spaces attached to the dots.

We will now apply the Hilbert–Mumford criterion (Theorem
3.6
) to characterize (semi)stable points of
E(p,q,r)
. Let
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to G_{p,q}
be a
1
-parameter subgroup. Then we have

\lambda(e^{t})=\left(\exp(tu),\exp(tv)\right)

where
u
and

are diagonalizable endomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}
respectively, with integral eigenvalues. Let
m_{1}>\cdots>m_{k}
and

denote the eigenvalues of
u
and

respectively, and let
F_{i}
and
H_{j}
denote the eigenspaces of
u
and

associated respectively to the eigenvalues
m_{i}
and

, so that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u=m_{1}\mathrm{Id}_{F_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus m_{k}\mathrm{Id}_{F_{k}}\\
v=n_{1}\mathrm{Id}_{H_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus n_{l}\mathrm{Id}_{H_{l}}\end{array}\right.

with respect to the decompositions
\mathbb{C}^{p}=F_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus F_{k}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}=H_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus H_{l}
. We then define decreasing filtrations of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}
associated to

Definition 3.11
.

The filtrations
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and

associated to

are defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}U_{n}(\lambda)&=&\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m_{i}\geq n}F_{i}\penalty\ ,\\
V_{n}(\lambda)&=&\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{n_{j}\geq n}H_{j}\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{r})
be a point in
E(p,q,r)
,
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to G_{p,q}
be a one parameter subgroup and
(U_{n}(\lambda))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(V_{n}(\lambda))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
the associated filtrations. Then
\mu_{L_{\chi}}(\lambda,\mathbf{A})
is finite if and only if
A_{j}(U_{n}(\lambda))\subset V_{n}(\lambda)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. In that case, we have

\mu_{L_{\chi}}(\lambda,\mathbf{A})=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{\prime}\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))-q^{\prime}\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))\penalty\ ,

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{\gcd(p,q)}
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{\gcd(p,q)}
.

Remark 3.13
.

The sum is well-defined because for
n>\max(m_{1},n_{1})
we have
\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))=\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))=0
and for
n\leq\min(m_{k},n_{l})
, we have
\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))=p
and
\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))=q
, so
p^{\prime}\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))=q^{\prime}\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))
.

Proof of Proposition
3.12
.

The following computation is a particular case of a more general result of King for quiver varieties
[
Kin94
]
. We give it here for completeness.

Writing the
A_{j}
with respect to diagonalization bases for
u
and
v
, one easily verifies that
\lambda(t)\cdot\mathbf{A}
converges when

goes to
0
if and only if
A_{j}(U_{n}(\lambda))\subset V_{n}(\lambda)
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Otherwise, there is a vector
w
in some

and some

such that
A_{j}(w)
has a non-zero component in

for some

. Then
\lambda(t)\cdot A_{j}(w)
grows at least like
t^{(n_{b}-m_{a})}
when

goes to
0
.

If
\lambda(t)\cdot\mathbf{A}
converges as

, then the action of
\lambda(t)
on the fiber of
L_{\chi}
at the limit is simply given by multiplication by

and we thus obtain

(1)

\mu_{L_{\chi}}(\lambda,\mathbf{A})=p^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{l}n_{i}\dim(H_{i})-q^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}\dim(F_{i})\penalty\ .

Let us take as a convention that
m_{0}=n_{0}=b>\max(m_{1},n_{1})
and
m_{k+1}=n_{l+1}=a<\min(m_{k},n_{l})
. Note that we have
\dim(F_{i})=\dim U_{m_{i}}(\lambda)-\dim U_{m_{i-1}}(\lambda)
,
\dim(H_{j})=\dim V_{n_{j}}(\lambda)-\dim V_{n_{j-1}}(\lambda)
for

and
1\leq j\leq l
. Applying an Abel transform to (

), we get

\displaystyle\mu_{L_{\chi}}(\lambda,\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{l}(n_{j}-n_{j+1})\dim(V_{n_{j}}(\lambda))-q^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{k}(m_{i}-m_{i+1})\dim(U_{m_{i}}(\lambda))

\displaystyle+p^{\prime}n_{l+1}\dim(V_{n_{l}}(\lambda))-q^{\prime}m_{k+1}\dim(U_{m_{k}}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{l}\sumop\displaylimits_{n=n_{j+1}+1}^{n_{j}}\dim(V_{n_{j}}(\lambda))-q^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{n=m_{i+1}+1}^{m_{i}}\dim(U_{m_{i}}(\lambda))+a(p^{\prime}q-q^{\prime}p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{l}\sumop\displaylimits_{n=n_{j+1}+1}^{n_{j}}\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))-q^{\prime}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{n=m_{i+1}+1}^{m_{i}}\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n=a}^{b}p^{\prime}\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))-q^{\prime}\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))\penalty\ .

∎

Proposition
3.12
easily implies King’s characterization of semi-stable points in

:

Theorem 3.14

(King,
[
Kin94
]
)
.

A point
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{r})\in E(p,q,r)
is semistable for the action of
G_{p,q}
if and only if all subspaces
U
and
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}
such that
A_{j}(U)\subset V
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
satisfy

\dim V\geq\frac{q}{p}\dim U\penalty\ .

It is stable if, moreover, the equality only holds when
U
and
V
are reduced to
\{0\}
or when
U=\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
V=\mathbb{C}^{q}
.

Corollary 3.15

(see
[
Wei13
]
)
.

•

If
\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}\geq r
, then
\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
is empty except when
r=1
or
2
and
p=q
.

•

If
\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}<r
, then
E(p,q,r)
contains stable points and
\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
has positive dimension.

•

If
p
and
q
are coprime, then every semistable point in

is stable.

The cases
r\leq 2
and
p=q
are special and are often excluded of the study of Kronecker varieties. However, they are relevant here. The following proposition deals with them.

Proposition 3.16
.

•

The Kronecker variety
\mathcal{R}(p,p,1)
is reduced to a single point. There is no stable point. A point of
E(p,p,1)=\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
is semistable if and only if it is invertible.

•

The Kronecker moduli space
\mathcal{R}(p,p,2)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{p}
. There is no stable point.

The proof will rely on the following linear algebra lemma:

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
A
and
B
be two endomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Then
(A,B)
is semistable in
E(p,p,2)
if and only if there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
A+tB
is invertible.

Proof.

Assume first that
A+tB
is invertible for some
t
. If
V
and
W
are subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
such that
A(V)\subset W
and
B(V)\subset W
, then
A+tB(V)\subset W
. Hence
\dim W\geq\dim V
, proving semistability.

Conversely, assume that
A+tB
has a kernel for all
t
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, let
u_{n}
be a non-zero vector in the kernel of
A+\frac{1}{n}B
. Up to scaling
u_{n}
and extracting a subsequence, we can assume that
u_{n}
converges to a non-zero vector
u
in the kernel of
A
. Set
V=\mathrm{Span}(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
W=A(V)
. Since
u_{n}
is in the kernel of
A+\frac{1}{n}B
,
Bu_{n}
is colinear to
Au_{n}
for all
n
, hence
B(V)\subset W
. Finally, since
V
is closed (as a linear subspace in finite dimension), it contains the vector
u
. Thus
A:V\to W
is not injective and
\dim W<\dim V
, contradicting semistability.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.16
.

•

Case
r=1,p=q
.

If
A\in\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
is not invertible, then the dimension of the image of

is strictly less than

, which contradicts stability. If
A
is invertible, then
\dim A(U)=\dim U
for every subspace

of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
, hence

is semistable and not stable. Since any invertible matrice is similar to the identity, there is a unique semistable orbit. Hence
\mathcal{R}(1,p,p)
is reduced to a point.

•

Case

.

By Lemma
3.17
, there is a well-defined map

\Phi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}E(p,p,2)^{ss}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{P}(\mathbb{C}_{p}[X,Y])\\
(A_{1},A_{2})&\longmapsto&[\det(XA_{1}+YA_{2})]\end{array}\right.\penalty\ ,

where
\mathbb{C}_{p}[X,Y]
is the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
p
in two variables. The map
\Phi
is invariant under the action of

. It is a linear algebra exercise to verify that
\Phi
is surjective and
\Phi(\mathbf{A})=\Phi(\mathbf{B})
if and only if the closures of the
G_{p,q}
-orbits of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
intersect. The map

Finally, if
(A_{1},A_{2})
is a semistable point, let

be such that

is invertible and let

be an invertible matrix commuting with

3.3. Flag configurations

Another classical application of GIT is the construction of configuration spaces of flags (see for instance
[
FMS20
]
).

Recall that a
complete flag

F
in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
is a nested sequence of linear subspaces

\mathbb{C}^{p}=F_{0}\supset F_{1}\supset\cdots\supset F_{p}=\{0\}\penalty\ ,

where
\dim F_{i}=p-i
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
the set of complete flags in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
.

Remark 3.18
.

All the content of this subsection and the next one would easily extend to
partial flags
(
i.e.
decreasing sequences of subspaces). We chose to restrict to complete flags to avoid proliferation of indices. For the rest of this section, “flag” will mean “complete flag”.

Let
\text{Gr}_{i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
denote the Grassmanian of
i
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
, and define

\iota_{i}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{p})&\longrightarrow&\text{Gr}_{p-i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})\\
(F_{j})_{0\leq j\leq p}&\longmapsto&F_{i}\end{array}\right.\penalty\ .

Then the map
\iota=(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{p-1})
embeds
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
into
\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p-1}\text{Gr}_{p-i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
.

Denote by
\mathcal{O}_{i}(a)
the pullback to
\text{Gr}_{i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
of the unique line bundle of degree
a
on
\mathbf{P}\left(\bigwedgeop\displaylimits^{i}\mathbb{C}^{p}\right)
by the Plücker embedding. The following is classical.

Proposition 3.19
.

Every line bundle over
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p-1})\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\bigotimesop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p-1}\iota_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{p-i}(a_{i})

for some
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p-1}
. This bundle is ample if and only if all the
a_{i}
are positive.

The group
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
acts on each
\text{Gr}_{i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
with kernel
\mathbb{C}^{*}\mathrm{Id}_{p}
. There is a canonical lift of this action to the total space of
\mathcal{O}_{i}(1)
, such that
\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{p}
acts by multiplication by
\lambda^{-i}
in each fiber. This action, however, is not a
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
linearization according to Definition
3.1
since it does not have
\mathbb{C}^{*}\mathrm{Id}_{p}
in its kernel. One can nonetheless linearize the action on
\mathcal{O}_{i}(p)
. More precisely, if we simply denote by
g(v)
the action of
g
on a point
v\in\mathcal{O}_{i}(p)
induced by the canonical action of
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{O}_{i}(1)
, then we get a new action by setting

g\cdot v=\det(g)^{i}\penalty\ g(v)

with kernel
\mathbb{C}^{*}\mathrm{Id}_{p}
, thus providing
\mathcal{O}_{i}(p)
with a
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
-linearization. The computation of the Hilbert–Mumford weights in this setting is classical.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
\lambda
be a
1
-parameter subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
and let
U_{n}(\lambda)
be the filtration defined in Definition
3.11
.
For
F
in
\text{Gr}_{i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
, we have

\mu_{\mathcal{O}_{i}(p)}(\lambda,F)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}i\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))-p\dim(U_{n}(\lambda)\cap F)\penalty\ .

Now, let
s
be a positive integer. We denote respectively by
\mathcal{F}^{s}
and
\mathcal{H}^{s}
the spaces
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{p})^{s}
and
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{s}
.
The group
G_{p,q}=\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}(q,\mathbb{C})
acts on
\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
with kernel
\mathbb{C}^{*}\mathrm{Id}_{p}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\mathrm{Id}_{q}
. One can endow
\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
with many
G_{p,q}
-linearized ample line bundles via its embedding into

\Pi\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p-1}\text{Gr}_{p-i}(\mathbb{C}^{p})\times\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q-1}\text{Gr}_{q-i}(\mathbb{C}^{q})\right)\penalty\ .

More precisely, for any family of positive integers
\mathbf{a}=(a_{i}^{j})_{1\leq j\leq s,1\leq i\leq p-1}
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{i}^{j})_{1\leq k\leq s,1\leq i\leq q-1}
,
define the
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{O}(p\mathbf{a},q\mathbf{b})
as the restriction to
\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
of the line bundle

\bigotimesop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\left(\pi_{j}^{*}\mathcal{O}(pa_{1}^{j},\ldots,pa_{p-1}^{j})\otimes{\pi^{\prime}_{j}}^{*}\mathcal{O}(qb_{1}^{j},\ldots,qb_{q-1}^{j})\right)\penalty\ ,

where
\pi_{j}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{j}
denote respectively the projections of
\Pi
to the

-th factors
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^{q})
. Then the actions of
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{GL}(q,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{O}_{p-i}(p)
and
\mathcal{O}_{q-i}(q)

-linearization of
\mathcal{O}(p\mathbf{a},q\mathbf{b})

For
(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})=((F_{i}^{j}),(H_{i}^{j}))\in\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}

1
-parameter subgroup, write

(2)

\mu_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\lambda,(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H}))=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}^{j}\mu_{\mathcal{O}_{p-i}(p)}(\lambda_{1},F_{i}^{j})+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q-1}\mu_{\mathcal{O}_{q-i}(q)}(\lambda_{2},H_{i}^{j})\right)\penalty\ .

We want to express this formula in terms of “induced weights” on the filtration associated to
\lambda
. In order to do so, let us choose

and

\eta_{i+1}^{j}-\eta_{i}^{j}=a_{i}^{j}

and

\zeta_{i+1}^{j}-\zeta_{i}^{j}=b_{i}^{j}

for all

. We see
\eta^{j}=(\eta_{i}^{j})_{1\leq i\leq p}
(resp.

(resp.

U
(resp.
V
) is a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
(resp.
\mathbb{C}^{q}

(resp.
|\zeta^{j}(V\cap H^{j})|

(resp.

) by
(F^{j},\eta^{j})
(resp.
(H^{j},\zeta^{j})
),
i.e.

|\eta^{j}(U\cap F^{j})|=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}^{j}\left(\dim U\cap F_{i-1}^{j}-\dim U\cap F_{i}^{j}\right)

and

|\zeta^{j}(V\cap H^{j})|=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q}\zeta_{i}^{j}\left(\dim V\cap H_{i-1}^{j}-\dim V\cap H_{i}^{j}\right)\penalty\ .

Finally, we set

|\eta(U\cap\mathbf{F})|=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}|\eta^{j}(U\cap F^{j})|\penalty\ ,

|\zeta(V\cap\mathbf{H})|=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}|\zeta^{j}(V\cap H^{j})|\penalty\ ,

\|\eta\|=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p}\eta_{i}^{j}=|\eta(\mathbb{C}^{p}\cap\mathbf{F})|

and

\|\zeta\|=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q}\zeta_{i}^{j}=|\zeta(\mathbb{C}^{q}\cap\mathbf{H})|\penalty\ .

Proposition 3.21
.

For
(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})\in\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
and
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to G_{p,q}
a
1
-parameter subgroup, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\lambda,(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(\|\eta\|\dim(U_{n}(\lambda))-p|\eta(U_{n}(\lambda)\cap\mathbf{F})|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}}+\|\zeta\|\dim(V_{n}(\lambda))-q|\zeta(V_{n}(\lambda)\cap\mathbf{H})|\big)\penalty\ .

Proof.

Replacing
\mu(\lambda,F_{i}^{j})
and
\mu(\lambda,H_{i}^{j})
in (

, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\lambda,(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}^{j}(p-i)\dim U_{n}(\lambda)-pa_{i}^{j}\dim U_{n}(\lambda)\cap F_{i}^{j}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q-1}b_{i}^{j}(q-i)\dim V_{n}(\lambda)-qb_{i}^{j}\dim V_{n}(\lambda)\cap H_{i}^{j}\right)\penalty\ .

Writing

3.4. Feathered Kronecker varieties

Let us define

E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)=E(p,q,r)\times\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}\penalty\ .

In this section, we consider GIT quotients of
E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
with different choices of
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundles.

Let
\eta=(\eta_{i}^{j})_{1\leq j\leq s,1\leq i\leq p}
and
\zeta=(\zeta_{i}^{j})_{1\leq j\leq s,1\leq i\leq q}
be tuples of
real
numbers such that
\eta_{1}^{j}<\cdots<\eta_{p}^{j}
and
\zeta_{1}^{j}<\cdots<\zeta_{q}^{j}
for all

E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
depending on
\eta
and
\zeta
. We will then prove that this stability condition is equivalent to Mumford’s stability for a suitable choice of ample
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundle.

Definition 3.22
.

A point
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})\in E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
is
(\eta,\zeta)
-semistable
if, for every subspaces
U
and
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}
such that
A_{i}(U)\subset V
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we have

\mu_{\eta,\zeta}(U,V)\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\left(\frac{\|\eta\|}{p}-q\right)\dim U-|\eta(U\cap\mathbf{F})|+\left(\frac{\|\zeta\|}{q}+p\right)\dim V-|\zeta(V\cap\mathbf{H})|\geq 0\penalty\ .

It is
(\eta,\zeta)
-stable
if, moreover, equality only holds for
U=V=\{0\}
or
U=\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
V=\mathbb{C}^{q}
. The
feathered Kronecker variety

\mathcal{R}_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s,\eta,\zeta)
is the largest Hausdorff quotient of the set of
(\eta,\zeta)
-semistable points of
E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
under the action of

.

The following proposition asserts that these spaces are indeed GIT quotients of
E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
. In particular,
\mathcal{R}_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s,\eta,\zeta)
is homeomorphic to a projective variety.

Proposition 3.23
.

There exists an ample
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundle
L
on
E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
(depending on
\eta
and
\zeta
) such that
L
-semistable (resp. stable) points are exactly
(\eta,\zeta)
-semistable (resp. stable) points.

Proof.

Let us first remark the following: one can find
rational
weights
(\eta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
close to
(\eta,\zeta)
such that the
(\eta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
-(semi)stable points are exactly the
(\eta,\zeta)
-(semi)stable points. Indeed, all the (semi)stability conditions in Definition
3.22
for all points in
E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
are linear equations and inequalities in
(\eta,\zeta)
with integral coefficients bounded by some constant. There are thus only finitely many such conditions, and one can find a rational point
(\eta^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
close to
(\eta,\zeta)
which satisfies the same exact conditions.

We can therefore assume without loss of generality that the weights
(\eta,\zeta)
are rational. Let
k
be a positive integer such that
(\frac{k}{p}\eta,\frac{k}{q}\zeta)
is integral. Set

a_{i}^{j}=\frac{k}{p}\eta_{i+1}^{j}-\frac{k}{q}\eta_{i}^{j}\penalty\ ,\quad b_{i}^{j}=\frac{k}{q}\zeta_{i+1}^{j}-\frac{k}{q}\zeta_{i}^{j}\penalty\ .

Let
L_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}
denote the
G_{p,q}
-linearized line bundle over
\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
defined in Section
3.3
. Let also

be the

-linearized line bundle over

defined in Section
3.2
. Finally let

defined by

where

and

respectively denote the projections to the factors

and
\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
. We now write

for
\mu_{L_{k,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}}
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})
be a point in

. Given a one parameter subgroup

, putting together Propositions

3.20

\mu_{k,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})=+\infty

for all

\displaystyle\mu_{k,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}(\lambda,(\mathbf{A},F,H))=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{k}{p}\|\eta\|\dim U_{n}(\lambda)-k|\eta(U_{n}(\lambda)\cap\mathbf{F})|+\frac{k}{q}\|\zeta\|\dim V_{n}(\lambda)-k|\zeta(V_{n}(\lambda)\cap\mathbf{H})|\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\right.}\left.\phantom{\frac{k}{p}}+kp\dim V_{n}(\lambda)-kq\dim U_{n}(\lambda)\right)\penalty\ .

If

-stability.

Conversely, if
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})
is not
(\eta,\zeta)
-semistable, one can find

and

(*)=p\dim(V)-q\dim(U)+\frac{\|\eta\|}{p}\dim U-|\eta(V\cap\mathbf{F})|+\frac{\|\zeta\|}{q}\dim V-|\zeta(W\cap\mathbf{H})|<0\penalty\ .

Define

and

Despite being given by an explicit formula, the
(\eta,\zeta)
-stability criterion is not very enlightening in general. For future use, let us specialize to the case where

and

Corollary 3.24
.

For
(\eta,\zeta)
sufficiently close to
(0,0)
, a point
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{H})\in E_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s)
is
(\eta,\zeta)
-semistable if and only if, for every proper subspaces

and
V
of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{q}
such that

for all

,

•

either
\dim V>\frac{q}{p}\dim U

•

or
\dim V=\frac{q}{p}\dim U
and

(3)

|\eta(U\cap\mathbf{F})|+|\zeta(V\cap\mathbf{H})|\leq\left(\|\eta\|+\|\zeta\|\right)\frac{\dim V}{q}\penalty\ .

It is stable if it is semistable and equality in (

and

,

.

In particular, if

and

\mathcal{R}_{\spadesuit}(p,q,r,s,\eta,\zeta)
fibers over
\mathcal{R}(p,q,r)
with fibers isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}^{s}\times\mathcal{H}^{s}
.

Proof.

For
(\eta,\zeta)
small enough,
\mu_{\eta,\zeta}(U,V)
has the same sign as
q\dim V-p\dim U
when the latter is non-zero. If

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta,\zeta}(U,V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\dim U}{q}\|\eta\|+\frac{\dim V}{p}\|\zeta\|-|\eta(U\cap\mathbf{F})|-|\zeta(V\cap\mathbf{H})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\dim V}{q}\left(\|\eta\|+\|\zeta\|\right)-|\eta(U\cap\mathbf{F})|-|\zeta(V\cap\mathbf{H})|\penalty\ .

The first part of the proposition follows.

Assume now that
p
and
q
are coprime. Then we cannot have

for proper subspaces

and

. Hence, for

is

is stable. We thus have

and the projection on the first factor induces a morphism from

. The fact that this map is a fibration with fibers isomorphic to

∎

4. Compact components

4.1. A compactness criterion

In this subsection, we find a sufficient criterion for a relative component
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
of parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles over
X=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
to be compact.

Recall from Subsection
2.2.2
that a parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundle of type
(\alpha,\beta)=\big\{(\alpha^{1},\beta^{1}),\ldots,(\alpha^{s},\beta^{s})\big\}
is a pair
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
where:

•

\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
are parabolic bundles of respective rank
p
and
q
, with respective parabolic structures of type
\alpha^{k}
and
\beta^{k}
at
x_{k}\in\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\}
and such that
\det(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{O}
.

•

\gamma\in H^{0}\big(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})\big)
and
\delta\in\mathrm{Hom}\big(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\big)
, where
D
is the effective divisor
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{s}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweight satisfying

(1)

\quad\alpha_{p}^{j}<\beta_{1}^{j}\quad\textrm{for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,

(2)

\quad\varepsilon\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\beta_{q}^{j}-\alpha_{1}^{j}<2
.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
be a semistable parabolic Higgs bundle of type
(\alpha,\beta)
. If
\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})<2-\varepsilon
, then
\delta
vanishes identically.

Remark 4.2
.

Since we only consider strongly parabolic Higgs bundles, the assumption that
\alpha^{j}_{p}\leq\beta^{j}_{1}
would be sufficient to deduce the vanishing of
\delta
. We wrote a strict inequality because we will use later that condition (1) is open.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the Higgs bundle proof of the Milnor–Wood inequality (see
[
BGPG+03
]
), where a violation of the Milnor–Wood inequality for
\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})
would imply the vanishing of one of the terms of the Higgs field (contradicting stability).

The condition
\alpha_{p}^{j}<\beta_{1}^{j}
implies that
\delta
does not have poles at the puncture
x_{j}
,
i.e.

\delta
is a holomorphic section of
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathcal{K})
. Let
N
and
I\otimes\mathcal{K}
be the subsheaves of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathcal{K}
respectively given by the kernel and the image of
\delta
, so that
\delta
induces an exact sequence of sheaves

0\to N\to V\to I\otimes\mathcal{K}\to 0.

In particular, we have

(4)

\deg(V)=\deg(N)+\deg(I\otimes\mathcal{K}).

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{U}
be respectively the saturation of the sheaves
N
and
I
. Denote by
n
the rank of
\mathcal{N}
. We have
\deg(N)\leq\deg(\mathcal{N})
and
\deg(I)\leq\deg(\mathcal{I})
. Equation (
4
) implies

(5)

\deg(\mathcal{V})\leq\deg(\mathcal{N})+\deg(\mathcal{I})-2(q-n).

The bundles
\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
are
\gamma\oplus\delta
-invariant. By semistability, they must have non-positive parabolic degree. To write this more explicitly, let us define

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}A(m)&=&\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}^{j}\\
B(m)&=&\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{q+1-i}^{j}\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

In particular
A(p)=\|\alpha\|
and
B(q)=\|\beta\|
.

The parabolic weights on
\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}
(resp.
\mathcal{I}_{\bullet}
) at the point
x_{j}
are larger or equal to
\beta^{j}_{1},\ldots,\beta^{j}_{n}
(resp.
\alpha^{j}_{1},\ldots,\alpha^{j}_{q-n}
). The stability condition thus implies

(6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\deg(\mathcal{N})+B(q)-B(q-n)\leq 0\\
\deg(\mathcal{V})+\deg(\mathcal{I})+B(q)+A(q-n)\leq 0\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

Adding the two lines in (
6
) and using Equation (
5
), we get

(7)

2\deg(\mathcal{V})+2B(q)+2(q-n)+A(q-n)-B(q-n)\leq 0.

Since
\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=-\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})=\deg(\mathcal{V})+B(q)
, we can rewrite this as

\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})\geq 2(q-n)-(B(q-n)-A(q-n))\penalty\ .

Finally, by definition of
A(q-n)
and
B(q-n)
, we have

\displaystyle B(q-n)-A(q-n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q-n}\beta_{q+1-i}^{j}-\alpha_{i}^{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}(q-n)(\beta_{q}^{j}-\alpha_{1}^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-n)\varepsilon\penalty\ .

We thus obtain

\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})\geq(q-n)(2-\varepsilon)\penalty\ .

If
\varepsilon<2
and
\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})<2-\varepsilon
, then clearly this inequality only holds for
n=q
, which means that
\mathcal{N}=\ker\delta=\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Consider
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
4.1
and, as before, denote by
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
the moduli space of polystable parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles of type
(\alpha,\beta)
such that
\deg(\mathcal{U})-\deg(\mathcal{V})=d
.

If
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
is a stable Higgs bundle in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
, then
\delta=0
by the previous proposition. In particular,
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
is
\gamma\oplus\delta
-invariant and
\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})<0
by stability. Using
\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})+\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=0
, stability gives
d>\|\beta\|-\|\alpha\|
. This provides a necessary condition to have stable point in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
.

Considering the interval

J_{\alpha,\beta}:=\big(\|\beta\|-\|\alpha\|,\|\beta\|-\|\alpha\|+2-\varepsilon\big)\penalty\ ,

then Proposition
4.1
gives the following compactness criterion:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweight satisfying the conditions of Proposition
4.1
. If
d
is an integer in
J_{\alpha,\beta}
, then
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
is compact.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
be a point in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
. By definition of
J_{\alpha,\beta}
, one has

\deg(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})-\deg(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=\|\alpha\|-\|\beta\|+d<2-\varepsilon\penalty\ .

Therefore,
\delta
vanishes identically by Proposition
4.1
. In particular, the Higgs field
\gamma\oplus\delta
is nilpotent and the Hitchin map sends
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
to
0
. The properness of the Hitchin map (see Theorem
2.18
) thus implies that
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
is compact.
∎

4.2. A non trivial example

When
\varepsilon<1
, the interval
J_{\alpha,\beta}
has length at least
1
and thus contains an integer. It is not easy, however, to construct a stable Higgs bundle with
\deg(\mathcal{U})-\deg(\mathcal{V})=d
for generic
(\alpha,\beta)
. Here, we restrict to more specific choices of weights that are “constant” at each puncture, for which computations are easier. This will allow us to construct examples of moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
that are compact and isomorphic to the Kronecker moduli space
\mathcal{R}(p,q,s-2)
. In the next subsection, we will look at generic choices of weights close to these constant weights.

We will say that a multiweight
(\alpha,\beta)
is
constant
if for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{1}^{j}=\cdots=\alpha_{p}^{j}=\alpha^{j}\\
\beta_{1}^{j}=\cdots=\beta_{q}^{j}=\beta^{j}\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

In this section, all multiweights
(\alpha,\beta)
are assumed to be constant. We write

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}|\alpha|=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\alpha^{j}\\
|\beta|=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\beta^{j}\penalty\ ,\end{array}\right.

so that
\|\alpha\|=p|\alpha|,\penalty\ \|\beta\|=q|\beta|
and
\varepsilon=|\beta|-|\alpha|
.

Recall also that, by Birkhoff-Grothendieck’s Theorem (see Theorem
2.22
), any rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
decomposes holomorphically as
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}(d_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(d_{n})
where
d_{1}\geq\cdots\geq d_{n}
, and
\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}(\mathcal{E})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is the degree vector of
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a constant multiweight satisfying the following conditions:
\alpha^{j}<\beta^{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and
\varepsilon\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}|\beta|-|\alpha|<2
. If
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
for
d\in J_{\alpha,\beta}
, then
\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}(\mathcal{V})=(-a,\ldots,-a)
for some
a>0
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
be such a parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundle.

By Remark
2.15
,
\deg(\mathcal{V})=-\frac{1}{2}(p|\alpha|+q|\beta|+d)
. Using
d<q|\beta|-p|\alpha|+2-\varepsilon
, we get

\deg\mathcal{V}>-\left(q|\beta|+1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\penalty\ .

By semistability,
\deg(\mathcal{V})<0
, so we can write
\deg(\mathcal{V})=-aq+r
with
a\in\mathbb{N},\penalty\ a>0
and
r\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}
. The previous equation implies

(8)

-|\beta|<\frac{\deg(\mathcal{V})}{q}+\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}{q}=-a+\frac{r+1-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}{q}.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{V}
be a line bundle of maximal degree
m
. By semistability,
m+|\beta|\leq 0
, so Equation (
8
) gives

m\leq-|\beta|<-a+\frac{r+1-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}{q}.

Using
\frac{r+1-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}{q}<1
, we get
m\leq-a
. By maximality of
m
we get
r=0
,
m=-a
and
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{O}(-a)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)
.
∎

The previous lemma shows that the assumptions on the triple
(\alpha,\beta,d)
in Corollary
4.3
are rather restrictive. Nonetheless, we will be able to find
(\alpha,\beta,d)
satisfying these conditions and for which
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Given an integer
a\in\left[\frac{s+p}{p+q},\frac{(p+q-1)s+p}{p+q}\right]
, there exists a constant multiweight
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfying

(a)

\alpha^{j}<\beta^{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,

(b)

\varepsilon\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}|\beta|-|\alpha|\in\left(\frac{2pq-2p-2q}{2pq-p-q},1\right)
,

(c)

d\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}(q-p)a+p\in J_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Moreover, if
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
, then
\,\overrightarrow{\deg\>}(\mathcal{U})=(-a+1,\ldots,-a+1)
.

Remark 4.6
.

The interval
\left[\frac{s+p}{p+q},\frac{(p+q-1)s+p}{p+q}\right]
has length
\left(1-\frac{2}{p+q}\right)s
which is at least
1
as soon as
s\geq 3
and
p
or
q
is greater than
1
. In that case this inteval always contains an integer. On the other hand, when
p=q=1
, it is reduced to
\{\frac{s+1}{2}\}
, which is an integer if and only if
s
is odd (this corresponds to Example
2.16
).

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

The condition
a\in\left[\frac{s+p}{p+q},\frac{(p+q-1)s+p}{p+q}\right]
is equivalent to

(9)

s\leq(p+q)a-p\leq(p+q-1)s\penalty\ .

Since
a
is an integer, it implies
a\leq s
.

Write
(p+q)a-p=ks+r
with
0\leq r<s
and define
k^{j}=k+1
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and
k^{j}=k
for
j\in\{r+1,\ldots,s\}
, so that
\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}k^{j}=(p+q)a-p
. The inequality (
9
) implies that
1\leq k^{j}\leq p+q-1
for all
j
.

We now choose
(\varepsilon^{j})_{1\leq j\leq s}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfying the following conditions:

(10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\varepsilon^{j}<\min\left(\frac{k^{j}}{q},\frac{p+q-k^{j}}{p}\right)\quad\textrm{for all $j$}\\
\frac{2pq-2p-2q}{2pq-p-q}<\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\varepsilon^{j}<1\penalty\ .\end{array}\right.

Let us first show that these conditions can be simultaneously satisfied. If
k\leq q-1
, then
\min\left(\frac{k^{j}}{q},\frac{p+q-k^{j}}{p}\right)=\frac{k^{j}}{q}
, and the conditions (
10

\displaystyle|\eta(U^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{F})|+|\zeta(V^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{H})|\penalty\ .

Comparing to Corollary

small enough, the Higgs bundle

is semistable if and only if

is
(\eta,\zeta)
-semistable. Since

is a coarse moduli space, we thus have a morphism
\varphi:E^{ss}_{\spadesuit}(p,q,s-2,s,\eta,\zeta)\to\mathcal{M}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},d)

.

Again, one easily verifies that two points in

-Higgs bundles if and only if they are in the same

Finally, repeating the proofs of Lemmas

and

(see Remark

∎

5. Properties of our compact relative components

In this section we return to the “representation variety” side of the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence where we summarize the results obtained so far and prove Theorems

5.1. An open set of compact components

Recall that, given a

Recall also from Subsection

\mathsf{NAH}:\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)\overset{\cong}{\to}\mathfrak{X}_{h(\alpha,\beta)}^{\|\beta\|-\|\alpha\|-d}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q))\penalty\ .\\

Theorem 5.1

It would be interesting to replace “irreducible” by “Zariski dense” in the above theorem. Though we strongly suspect that each of our compact components contains a Zariski dense representation, we do not know of a quick argument to rule out all other possible Zariski closures in general. We can nevertheless make the improvement for “almost all” weights

Lemma 5.2

The union

\Omega=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in W(\alpha,\beta)}\mathfrak{X}_{h(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})}^{\|\beta^{\prime}\|-\|\alpha^{\prime}\|-d}\big(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q)\big)

Now, the set of Zariski dense representations has full measure in

5.2. Proof of Theorem

Let us now describe some properties of the representations contained in these compact components and prove Theorem

. Assume

Theorem 5.4
.

Every representation

(i)

For every identification of

with a punctured Riemann sphere, there is a
\rho

\mathrm{SU}(p,q)

(ii)

For every homotopy class of simple closed curve

.

(iii)

More generally, for every

We first describe the geometry of the symmetric space associated to
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
. Recall that
\mathbb{C}^{p,q}
is the usual
(p+q)
-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a signature
(p,q)

. Let
\mathcal{Y}
be the Grassmanian of

-dimensional (complex) linear subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{p,q}
on which the restriction of

is positive definite. Given

in
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
is a maximal compact subgroup conjugated to

\mathcal{Y}

The Grassmanian

carries two natural tautological bundles. Consider the trivial bundle

and define the rank

by

The tangent space

is identified with
\mathrm{Hom}{C}(P,P^{\bot})

is isomorphic to the bundle
\mathrm{Hom}{C}(U,V)

T{C}\mathcal{Y}=T^{1,0}\mathcal{Y}\oplus T^{0,1}\mathcal{Y}=\mathrm{Hom}{C}(U,V)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}{C}(V,U),

where
T^{1,0}\mathcal{Y}
and

are respectively the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic tangent bundle of

.

The non-Abelian Hodge correspondence relies on the existence, for any irreducible representation
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
and Riemann surface structure

\Sigma

\rho
-equivariant harmonic map

\mathrm{SU}(p,q)

A map

takes value in

. Since this is the case for all parabolic Higgs bundles satisfying our compactness criterion (Corollary

), Property
(i)

Properties
(ii)
and
(iii)

(i)
using the contraction property of holomorphic maps. We first introduce the Kobayashi metric on

d_{K}(x,y)=\inf\big\{d_{P}(a,b),\penalty\ a,b\in\mathclose{\,\mathit{d}},\penalty\ g(a)=x,\penalty\ g(b)=y\text{ for some }g:\mathclose{\,\mathit{d}}\to\mathcal{Y}\text{ holomorphic}\big\}.

The Kobayashi metric is a

Given an element

where the

\displaystyle t(\rho(\gamma))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{Y}}\big(f(x),f(\gamma x)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{Y}}\big(f(x),\rho(\gamma)f(x)\big)

\displaystyle Cd_{K}\big(f(x),\rho(\gamma)f(x)\big)

6. More compact components

In this section, we give ways of constructing more compact components : by tensorizing with a line bundle, by restricting to representations into other Hermitian Lie groups, and by restricting to subsurfaces. These examples show how far we are from a classification of compact connected components.

6.1. Tensor product with a line bundle

There is a notion of projective equivalence for parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles.

Under the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence, projectively equivalent polystable
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles correspond to representations
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
with the same image in
\mathrm{PU}(p,q)=\mathrm{SU}(p,q)/Z
, where

Z=\left\{e^{\frac{2ik\pi}{p+q}}\mathrm{Id},\penalty\ k=0,\ldots,p+q-1\right\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p+q}

is the center of
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
. In particular, given a representation
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
, the set of representations
\rho^{\prime}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
with the same image in
\mathrm{PU}(p,q)
is parametrized by
H^{1}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathbb{Z}_{p+q})
.

Definition 6.1
.

Two parabolic Higgs bundles
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
and
(\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet},\gamma^{\prime}\oplus\delta^{\prime})
are
projectively equivalent
if there is a parabolic line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}
such that
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\penalty\ \mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
and
(\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})=(\gamma,\delta)
.

The condition
\det(\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet})=\det(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{O}
implies that
(\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})^{p+q}=\mathcal{O}
. That is, two projectively equivalent parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-Higgs bundles differ by a (parabolic)
(p+q)
th
root of the trivial bundle. If we denote by
\mathcal{J}_{X}
the group of degree
0
parabolic line bundles over
X
, the set of
(p+q)
th
roots of the trivial bundle is the subgroup of
(p+q)
torsion points and is denoted
\mathcal{J}_{X}[p+q]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The group
\mathcal{J}_{X}[p+q]
of parabolic line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}
on
X
such that
(\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})^{p+q}=\mathcal{O}
is isomorphic to
H^{1}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathbb{Z}_{p+q})
.

Proof.

Consider the presentation
\Gamma_{0,s}=\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}|c_{1}\cdots c_{s}=1\rangle
of the fundamental group
\Gamma_{0,s}
of
\Sigma_{0,s}
. An element
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathbb{Z}_{p+q})
is thus a map
\varphi:\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p+q}
such that
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\varphi(c_{i})=0\in\mathbb{Z}_{p+q}
.

The map
\varphi
has a unique representative
\widehat{\varphi}:\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\}\to\{0,\ldots,p+q-1\}
such that
|\widehat{\varphi}|=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\widehat{\varphi}(c_{i})\equiv 0\mod[p+q]
. Define the corresponding parabolic line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\varphi}
by

\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\varphi}=\mathcal{O}\left(-\frac{|\widehat{\varphi}|}{p+q}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(c_{i})}{p+q}x_{i}\right).

One easily checks that
(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi}_{\bullet})^{p+q}=\mathcal{O}
and that the map
\varphi\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\varphi}
is a group morphism.

Let us show that the map is surjective. Given
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{O}\big(l+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{s}\alpha^{i}x_{i}\big)
a
(p+q)^{th}
root of
\mathcal{O}
, the condition
(\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})^{p+q}=\mathcal{O}
implies that
\alpha^{i}=\frac{k_{i}}{p+q}
for some
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
l=-\frac{|k|}{p+q}\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, there is a unique
\varphi\in H^{1}\big(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathbb{Z}_{p+q}\big)
with
\widehat{\varphi}(c_{i})=k_{i}
.
∎

We get the following corollary:

Corollary 6.3
.

If
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
are two
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
-multiweights such that, at any point
x_{i}
, we have

{(\alpha^{\prime}}^{i}_{1}-\alpha^{i}_{1}),\penalty\ \ldots\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ({\alpha^{\prime}}^{i}_{p}-\alpha^{i}_{p}),\penalty\ ({\beta^{\prime}}^{i}_{1}-\beta^{i}_{1}),\penalty\ \ldots\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ({\beta^{\prime}}^{i}_{q}-\beta^{i}_{q})\in\frac{1}{p+q}\mathbb{Z}\penalty\ ,

then there exists
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{J}_{X}[p+q]
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\otimes-:\penalty\ \mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
is an isomorphism.

As a result, given a compact conponent
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
, one gets a family of compact components

\big\{\mathcal{M}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},d^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d),\penalty\ \mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{J}_{X}[p+q]\big\}.

These components correspond to different lifts of the same representations in
\mathrm{PU}(p,q)
.

Note that the condition
\alpha_{i}^{1}<\beta_{i}^{q}
we imposed when looking for compact components is generally not preserved by taking the tensor product with
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{J}_{X}[p+q]
. This shows that the conditions of our compactness criterion (Corollary
4.3
) are too restrictive to account for all compact components.

6.2. Other Hermitian Lie groups

We extend here our construction to other classical Hermitian Lie groups, namely

6.2.1. Compact components in

Let

The group
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
acts on the complexification

\textbf{h}(u,v)=i\omega{C}(u,\overline{v})

-linear extension of

. In particular,

\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
.

Via this embedding, the relative
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})

\mathrm{SU}(p,p)

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume

is odd. Then we can find a subset
\Omega\subset\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,p))

\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})

Let us explain first why the lemma implies Theorems
1
and
2
for
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R}))
with

odd. Indeed, the intersection of
\Omega\cap\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R}))
is an open subset of the
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
character variety which thus contains a Zariski dense representation. Moreover, the intersection of
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R}))
with a compact component of a relative
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)

\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
character variety. Hence
\Omega\cap\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R}))
is a union of compact relative components, proving Theorem

. Finally, properties of Theorem

are satisfied by all the representations in
\Omega
, including those in
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R}))
(for the third property, it is important that the embedding

induces a holomorphic embedding of the symmetric spaces).

Proof of Lemma
6.4
.

We transit once again through the non-Abelian Hodge correspondence. One can indeed detect which parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
-Higgs bundles give rise to a representation in
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})

A parabolic
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle is a
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
such that

•

\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathbb{C})
,

•

\gamma\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee}))
and
\delta\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}))
are self-adjoint.

In particular, if
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}
has parabolic type
\alpha_{k}=(\alpha^{k}_{1},\ldots,\alpha^{k}_{p})
at

, then
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee}
has type
\beta^{k}=(\beta^{k}_{1},\ldots,\beta^{k}_{p})
with
\beta^{k}_{i}=1-\alpha^{k}_{p+1-i}
. It follows that
\|\alpha\|+\|\beta\|=ps
.

Let us give explicitly an integer

and a constant
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
-multiweight
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma

such that
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,d)
contains a
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle.

Take

, and set
\alpha^{j}=\frac{1-\varepsilon^{j}}{2}
and
\beta^{j}=\frac{1+\varepsilon^{j}}{2}

\varepsilon^{j}\in(0,1)
satisfy

\varepsilon\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\varepsilon^{j}\in\left(1-\frac{1}{p-1},1\right)\penalty\ .

One easily verifies that these multiweights satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
4.5
(with
p=q
).

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}
be the parabolic line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-\frac{s-1}{2}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\alpha^{j}x_{j})
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\vee}=\mathcal{O}(-\frac{s+1}{2}+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{s}\beta^{j}x_{j})
. By Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
, every parabolic
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
-Higgs bundle in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,p)
has the form
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus 0)
, where
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{p}
.

The standard bilinear pairing of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
given by

(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w})\mapsto z_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+z_{p}w_{p}

induces an isomorphism between
\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee}
. Finally, if
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{s-2})
is a tuple of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
which are symmetric (with respect to the standard bilinear pairing), then the associated section

\gamma_{\mathbf{A}}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{U}^{\vee}_{\bullet}))
is self-adjoint. If, moreover, one of the
A_{i}
is invertible, the parabolic bundle
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma_{\mathbf{A}}\oplus 0)
is a semistable
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle, giving a
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
-point in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,p)
.

Let
\Omega
be the subset of the
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
character variety given by

\Omega=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in W(\alpha,\beta)}\mathfrak{X}_{h(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})}^{p}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,p))

where
W(\alpha,\beta)
is an open neighborhood of
(\alpha,\beta)
in
\mathcal{W}(p,p,s)

contains a representation in
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Note that, for the above choice of
(\alpha,\beta)
, the relative component
\mathfrak{X}_{h(\alpha,\beta)}^{p}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R}))
could be described as a GIT quotient of the space
\mathcal{S}(p,s-2)
of
s-2
-tuples of symmetric matrices of size
p
by the action of
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
given by

g\cdot\mathbf{S}=(gS_{1}g^{T},\ldots,gS_{s-2}g^{T})\penalty\ .

One could also define a “feathered version” of this space to describe the nearby relative components. We don’t know if these spaces have been studied in the litterature.

6.2.2. Compact components in
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)

Recall that
\mathbb{C}^{p,p}
is the complex vector space
\mathbb{C}^{2p}
equipped with the signature
(p,p)
Hermitian form
h
defined by

\textbf{h}(z,w)=z_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+\cdots+z_{p}\overline{w}_{p}-z_{p+1}\overline{w}_{p+1}-\cdots-z_{2p}\overline{w}_{2p}.

Consider the
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear quadratic form
q
on
\mathbb{C}^{2q}
defined by
\textbf{q}(z)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{p}z_{k}z_{k+p}
. The group
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
is the group of linear transofrmations of
\mathbb{C}^{2p}
preserving both
h
and
q
. In particular,
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)\subset\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
.

Similarly to the
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
case, one can see the
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
character variety as a closed subset of the
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
character variety and Theorems
1
and
2
for
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
and
s
odd will follow from the following lemma:

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume
s\geq 5
is odd. Then we can find a subset
\Omega\subset\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,p))
as in Section
5
which contains a
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
representation.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
case and we only highlight the main differences between the two cases.

A parabolic
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
-Higgs bundle is a
\mathrm{SU}(p,p)
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{\bullet},\gamma\oplus\delta)
such that

•

\mathcal{V}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathbb{C})
,

•

\gamma\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee}))
and
\delta\in H^{0}(\mathcal{K}(D)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee},\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}))
are
antiself-adjoint
.

If we make the same choices of
(\alpha,\beta)
as in the proof of Lemma
6.4
,
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
-Higgs bundles in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,p)
will be of the form
(\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{U}_{\bullet}^{\vee},\gamma_{\mathbf{A}}\oplus 0)
, where
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{s-2})
is a tuple of
antisymmetric
matrices of size
p
. We are thus left with the problem of finding a semistable point in
E(p,p,s-2)
consisting of antisymmetric matrices.

This is slightly more elaborate than the case of symmetric matrices. If
p
is even, any choice of antisymmetric matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}
with
A_{1}
invertible will give a semistable point. If
p
is odd, on the other side, every antisymmetric matrix has a non-trivial kernel. In particular, there is no stable point in
E(p,p,2)
consisting of antisymmetric matrices by Lemma
3.17
, so one needs at least
3
matrices.

Let us assume
p
is odd and write
p=2p^{\prime}+1
. Let
(e_{0},\ldots e_{2p^{\prime}})
denote the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Define
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
by

•

A_{1}(e_{0})=0
and for
1\leq k\leq p^{\prime}
,
A_{1}(e_{2k-1})=e_{2k}
and
A_{1}(e_{2k})=-e_{2k-1}
,

•

A_{2}(e_{0})=0
and for
1\leq k\leq p^{\prime}
,
A_{1}(e_{2k-1})=ke_{2k}
and
A_{1}(e_{2k})=-ke_{2k-1}
,

•

A_{3}(e_{0})=-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{p^{\prime}}e_{2k}
and for
1\leq k\leq p^{\prime}
,
A_{1}(e_{2k-1})=A^{\prime}_{3}(e_{2k-1})
and
A_{1}(e_{2k})=A^{\prime}_{3}(e_{2k})+e_{0}
,
where
A^{\prime}_{3}
is an invertible antisymmetric endomorphism of
\mathrm{Span}(e_{1},\ldots,e_{2p^{\prime}})
such that, for every subset
I
of
\{1,\ldots,p^{\prime}\}
, the image of
\mathrm{Span}(e_{2i-1})_{i\in I}
is not contained in
\mathrm{Span}(e_{2i})_{i\in I}
. Note that the set of all such
A^{\prime}_{3}
is the complement of a finite union of proper algebraic subsets of the space of antisymmetric matrices of size
2p^{\prime}
. In particular, it is non-empty.

We leave to the reader the verification that
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
are antisymmetric.

We claim that
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
is a semistable point of
E(p,p,3)
. By contradiction, assume that there exists
V
and
W\subset\mathbb{C}^{p}
such that
A_{i}(V)\subset W
for
i=1,2,3
and
\dim W<\dim V
. Since the kernel of
A_{1}
is
\mathbb{C}e_{0}
we have that
V
contains
e_{0}
and
\dim W=\dim V-1
. We can thus write
V=\mathbb{C}e_{0}\oplus V^{\prime}
where
V^{\prime}\subset e_{0}^{\perp}
. Since
e_{0}
is also the kernel of
A_{2}
, we have
W=A_{1}(V^{\prime})=A_{2}(V^{\prime})
.

Remark that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
preserve
e_{0}^{\perp}
. Let
A^{\prime}_{1}
and
A^{\prime}_{2}
be the induced endomorphisms of
e_{0}^{\perp}
. Then
V^{\prime}
is invariant by
{A^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}A_{2}
and it is thus spanned by eigenvectors of
{A^{\prime}_{1}}^{-1}A_{2}
. Moreover, we also have
A_{3}(V^{\prime})\subset W\subset e_{0}^{\perp}
. Since
\langle A_{3}(e_{2k}),e_{0}\rangle=1
, we must have
V^{\prime}=\mathrm{Span}(e_{2i-1})_{i\in I}
for some subset
I
of
\{1,\ldots,p^{\prime}\}
. But we then have
A_{3}(V^{\prime})\subset A_{1}(V^{\prime})=\mathrm{Span}(e_{2i})_{i\in I}
, which contradicts the condition on
A_{3}
.

Therefore
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
is a semistable point of
E(p,p,3)
and every choice of antisymmetric matrices
(A_{i})_{4\leq i\leq s-2}
will give a semistable point in
E(p,p,s-2)
corresponding to a semistable
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
point in
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\beta,p)
.
∎

Similarly to the
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
case, one could parametrize the coresponding compact
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
relative components by a GIT quotient of the space of tuples of antisymmetric matrices of size
p
by the action of
\mathrm{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
.

We expect compact components in relative character varieties of punctured spheres to exist for all Hermitian Lie groups. The simple Hermitian Lie groups that are not covered by the present paper are the
\mathrm{SO}(2,p),p\geq 5
and some real forms of
\mathrm{E}_{6}

\mathrm{E}_{7}

4

The case

,

\mathrm{SO}(2,3)\simeq\mathrm{Sp}(4,\mathbb{R})

\mathrm{SO}(2,4)\simeq\mathrm{SU}(2,2)

Note that the standard embedding

In order to embed holomorphically the symmetric space of

A careful analysis of the spin representation might show that the domain

6.3. Restriction to a subsurface

So far, we only proved Theorems
1
and
2
in the following cases:

•

G=\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
and
s>\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}+2
,

•

G=\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
and
s\geq 5
odd,

•

G=\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
and
s\geq 5
odd.

Here we deduce Theorems
1
and
2
for any value of
s\geq 3
, by arguing that one can always cut a sphere with a lot of punctures into spheres with fewer punctures and restrict representations in a compact component to these subsurfaces.

To be more precise, let
G
be one of the Lie groups
\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
or
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
. Let
s_{0}
be greater than
\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}+2
if
G=\mathrm{SU}(p,q)
and
s_{0}
be odd and greater than
4
if
G=\mathrm{Sp}(2p,\mathbb{R})
or
\mathrm{SO}^{*}(2p)
.

We will say that an open domain
\Omega
of the character variety
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s_{0}},G)
is
foliated by compact relative components
if for every representation
\rho
in
\Omega
, the connected component of
\rho
in its relative character variety is compact and contained in
\Omega
. We proved in Sections
5.1
and
6.2
that such a domain exists.

Now, let
b
be an oriented simple closed curve that separates
\Sigma_{0,s_{0}}
into a sphere with
s
holes
\Sigma^{\prime}
and a sphere with
s_{0}-s+2
holes
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
. We choose a point on
b
as a basepoint for our fundamental groups, so as to identify
\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})
and
\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime})
with subgroups of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
.

We have the following presentations:

\Gamma_{0,s_{0}}=\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{s_{0}}\mid c_{1}\ldots c_{s_{0}}=1\rangle\penalty\ ,

\Gamma_{0,s}\cong\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})=\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{s-1},b\mid c_{1}\ldots c_{s-1}b=1\rangle\penalty\ ,

\Gamma_{0,s_{0}-s+2}\cong\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime})=\langle c_{s},\ldots,c_{s_{0}},b\mid b^{-1}c_{s}\ldots c_{s_{0}}=1\rangle\penalty\ .\\

There is a well-defined restriction map

\mathrm{Res}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s_{0}},G)&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},G)\\
\left[\rho\right]&\mapsto&\left[\rho_{|\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})}\right]\end{array}\right.,

which is clearly open.

Let
\Omega
be an open domain in
\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s_{0}},G)
foliated by compact relative components. Let
′
be the subset of
\Omega
consisting of representations
\rho
such that
\rho(b)
is diagonalizable with distinct eigenvalues. Note that
′
is open and non-empty since the map
\rho\mapsto\rho(b)
is open. Theorem
1
in full generality now follows from the following proposition:

Proposition 6.6
.

The domain
\mathrm{Res}({}^{\prime})\subset\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{0,s},G)
is foliated by compact connected components.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{0}
be a representation in
′
. Denote respectively by
\rho_{0}^{\prime}
and
\rho_{0}^{\prime\prime}
the restrictions of
\rho_{0}
to
\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})
and
\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime\prime})
. Define

h=(\rho_{0}(c_{1}),\ldots,\rho_{0}(c_{s_{0}}))\penalty\ ,

h^{\prime}=(\rho_{0}(c_{1}),\ldots,\rho_{0}(c_{s-1}),\rho(b))

and

h^{\prime\prime}=(\rho(b)^{-1},\rho_{0}(c_{s}),\ldots,\rho_{0}(c_{s_{0}}))\penalty\ .

We want to prove that the connected component of
\rho_{0}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{X}_{h^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{0,s},G)
is compact and contained in
\mathrm{Res}({}^{\prime})
.

Let
K
denote the subset of
\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Gamma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q))\cap\Omega
consisting of representations
\rho
such that
\rho(b)
is conjugate to
\rho_{0}(b)
.

Since
\rho_{0}(b)
is diagonalizable, its conjugation orbit is closed. The set
K
is therefore closed in
\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Gamma_{0,s},\mathrm{SU}(p,q))\cap\Omega
, hence compact. It is moreover contained in
′
.

By definition of
K
, the restriction map
\mathrm{Res}
sends
K
to
\to\mathfrak{X}_{h^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{0,s},G)
. Let us prove that its image contains the connected component of
[\rho_{0}^{\prime}]
. Let
[\rho_{1}^{\prime}]
be a point in this connected component and let
[\rho_{t}^{\prime}]_{0\leq t\leq 1}
be a continuous path from
[\rho_{0}^{\prime}]
to
[\rho_{1}^{\prime}]
. For each
t
, we know that
\rho_{t}^{\prime}(b)
belongs to
C(\rho_{0}(b))
. Since
\rho_{0}(b)
has distinct eigenvalues, this implies that
\rho_{t}^{\prime}(b)
and
\rho_{0}(b)
are conjugate. Let
g_{t}\in G
be such that

g_{t}\rho^{\prime}(b)g_{t}^{-1}=\rho_{0}(b)\penalty\ .

We can assume that
g_{t}
varies continuously with
t
.
Define

\rho_{t}:\Gamma_{0,s_{0}}\to\mathrm{SU}(p,q)

such that

{\rho_{t}}_{\mid\Gamma^{\prime}}=g_{t}\rho^{\prime}g_{t}^{-1}

and

{\rho_{t}}_{\mid\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}=\rho_{0}^{\prime\prime}\penalty\ .

By construction, we have
[\rho_{t}]\in\mathfrak{X}_{h}(\Sigma_{0,s_{0}},G)
for all
t
. Since
\Omega
contains the connected component of
\rho_{0}
in its relative character variety, we deduce that
\rho_{t}\in\Omega
for all
t
. Moreover,
\rho_{t}(b)
is conjugate to
\rho_{0}(b)
. Hence
[\rho_{t}]
belongs to
K
. Finally,

{\rho_{t}}_{|\Sigma^{\prime}}
is conjugate to
\rho_{t}^{\prime}
. Thus
[\rho_{t}^{\prime}]
belongs to
\mathrm{Res}(K)\subset\mathrm{Res}({}^{\prime})
.
∎

Finally, to prove Theorem
2
in full generality, we prove the following:

Proposition 6.7
.

Every representation
\rho\in\mathrm{Res}(\Omega)

.

Proof.

Properties
(ii)
and
(iii)
immediately pass to the restriction of a representation to a subsurface. Property

A representation

For any Fuchsian representation

of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma^{\prime})\cong\Gamma_{0,s}

of
\Gamma_{0,s_{0}}

.

Now let
\rho
be a representation in
\Omega
. By Theorem

, for every

(j_{n},\rho)
-equivariant holomorphic map

from

to the symmetric space

of

-equivariant holomorphic map from

to

. Since

satisfies Property
(i)
.

∎

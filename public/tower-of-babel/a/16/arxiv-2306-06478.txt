Relative De Rham cohomology in diffeological spaces
Enrique Macías-Virgós, Reihaneh Mehrabi

Abstract
. We define two different versions of the relative De Rham cohomology groups of a diffeological space. Additionally, we study a variant of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence and discuss the existence of a relative cup product. Our approach is categorical, replacing open coverings with generating families and unions and intersections with pull-back and join constructions. MSC 2020: 14F40, 58A40



Key words:
Diffeological space, De Rham cohomology, Mayer-Vietoris sequence, Cup product

1.
Introduction

Diffeological spaces are a generalization of differentiable manifolds, which provides a framework for non-classical objects like non-Hausdorff quotients of manifolds and spaces of smooth functions. Also, diffeology theory seems to be a convenient setting in various areas of physics.

A diffeology in the set
X
is a family
\mathcal{D}
of so-called
plots
—that is, set maps
\alpha\colon U\to X
whose domains are open subsets
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, — satisfying three axioms: covering, locality, and compatibility (see Definition
2.1
).

Many classical constructions can be generalized to this context, among them, the graded algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
of differential forms, the exterior derivative
\mathrm{d}
and the De Rham
cohomology algebra
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(X)=\mathrm{H}(\Omega^{\bullet}(X),\mathrm{d})
, see Section
3
. In this paper we study two different versions of
relative
De Rham cohomology.

In the setting of topological spaces, the relative cohomology groups are defined (see
[
5
, Section 3.1]
or
[
14
, Example 5.4.5]
) for any pair
(X,A)
with
A\subset X
a subspace.
In the diffeological setting, a relative De Rham cohomology group
\mathrm{H}(X,\alpha)
was defined, in our previous paper
[
11
]
, for any smooth map
\alpha\colon U\to X
(see Definition
3.6
), as the cohomology of the complex
\Omega(X,\alpha)=\ker\alpha^{*}
, the kernel of the induced morphism
\alpha^{*}\colon\Omega(X)\to\Omega(U)
.

However, the relative complex
\Omega(X,\alpha)
does not have an associated long exact sequence in cohomology.
The reason is that, in general, the morphism
\alpha^{*}
is not surjective, as an argument based on partitions of unity is missing in the diffeological setting.

In order to address this issue, we introduce
a second relative De Rham complex, denoted by
\varOmega(X,\alpha)
. This new complex mirrors the analogous one given by Bott-Tu in
[
2
, Prop. 6.49]
.
It is defined as

\varOmega^{r}(X,\alpha)=\Omega^{r}(X)\oplus\Omega^{r-1}(\alpha),

where
\Omega(\alpha)\subset\Omega(U)
is the subcomplex of the so-called
horizontal forms

as considered by us in
[
11
]
. The differential

\mathit{d}\colon\varOmega^{r}(X,\alpha)\to\varOmega^{r+1}(X,\alpha)

is defined as

\mathit{d}(\omega,\theta)=(\mathrm{d}\omega,\alpha^{*}\omega-\mathrm{d}\theta)
.

The cohomology of
(\varOmega(X,\alpha),\mathit{d})
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}(X,\alpha)
.
We then obtain a short exact sequence of complexes
and the associated long exact sequence in cohomology (see Equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)).

As an auxiliary tool we will introduce in Section
4
the
join
of two arbitrary mooth maps
\alpha\colon U\to X
and
\beta\colon V\to X
. This
join
is a
smooth map
\alpha\!\ast\!\beta\colon U\!\ast\!V\to X
which plays the role of the union of two open sets in the topological setting; actually, it is the categorical push-out of the pull-back of
\alpha
and
\beta
.

In Section
5
we study how the De Rham complex
of
X
is determined by giving a
generating family

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
of the diffeology (
[
8
, Art. 1.66]
), see Definition
5.1
).
Actually,
X
is diffeomorphic to the join
\alpha_{1}\!\ast\!\cdots\!\ast\!\alpha_{n}
, as we proved in
[
12
]
.
This is again an indication that generating families play the role of open coverings in the diffeological setting

Section
6
is dedicated to constructing a Mayer-Vietoris sequence

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(\alpha)\oplus\Omega(\beta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(p_{X})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varPi}

where
\Omega(p_{X})
is the complex of pull-back horizontal forms.
It improves upon the one we defined in
[
11
]
.

When the morphism
\varPi
in
(

) is surjective, we will say that the Mayer-Vietoris sequence is
split
. In this case we have a long exact sequence in cohomology, see Proposition
6.5
. For example, the sequence associated with a nice cover considered by Iwase and Izumida in
[

is split.

In Section
7
we introduce a relative cup product for the first type cohomology,

\mathrm{H}^{r}(X,\alpha)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{s}(X,\beta)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{r+s}(X,\alpha*\beta),

where
(\alpha,U)
,
(\beta,V)
are arbitrary plots of the diffeology. This product is achieved
by considering the exterior product of differential forms.

\mathcal{H}^{r}(X,\alpha)\otimes\mathcal{H}^{s}(X,\beta)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{r+s}(X,\alpha*\beta)

for the second type cohomology, but only under the condition that the Mayer-Vietoris sequence is split. The formulae required are intricate and far from obvious, inspired by those in
[

]
.

2.
Diffeological spaces

The main reference for Diffeology Theory is Iglesias’s book
[
8
]
.

A
parametrization
on the set
X
is any set map
\alpha\colon U\to X
, where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for some
n\geq 0
depending on
U
. Often, we shall denote it by
(\alpha,U)
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
8
, Art. 1.5]

Let
X
be a non-empty set. A
diffeology
on
X
is any set
\mathcal{D}
of parametrizations such that the three following
axioms are satisfied:

(1)

Covering
. Any constant parametrization belongs to
\mathcal{D}
.

(2)

Locality
. If the parametrization
(\alpha,U)
satisfies that for any
p\in U
there exists an open neighborhood
V\subset U
of
p
such that the restriction
\alpha_{|V}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
, then
\alpha
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
.

(3)

Compatibility
. If
(\alpha,U)
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
, and
h\colon V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\to U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-differentiable map, then
(\alpha\circ h,V)
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
.

The pair
(X,\mathcal{D})
will be called a
diffeological space
. Axiom 1 ensures that

\mathcal{D}\neq\emptyset
; from Axiom 2,
parametrizations can be glued together; Axiom 3 means that we can change coordinates inside
\mathcal{D}
.

A set map
f\colon(X,\mathcal{D}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{D}_{Y})
between diffeological spaces is a
smooth map
if
f\circ\alpha\in\mathcal{D}_{Y}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
.

3.
De Rham cohomology

3.1.
Differential forms

We begin by recalling the basic definitions of Cartan calculus and De Rham theory in diffeological spaces, as presented in

[
8
, Art. 6.28]
. Later we provide a definition of the relative cohomology groups of a smooth map (Definition
3.6
).

Definition 3.1
.

A differential form of degree
r\geq 0
in the diffeological space
(X,\mathcal{D})

is a map
\omega
which associates to each plot
(\alpha,U)\in\mathcal{D}
a differential
r
-form
\omega_{\alpha}\in\Omega^{r}(U)
on the domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
of the plot, in such a way that, for any
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
change of coordinates
h\colon V\to U
, it happens that

\omega_{\alpha\circ h}=h^{*}\omega_{\alpha}
.

We denote by
\Omega^{r}(X,\mathcal{D})
or simply by
\Omega^{r}(X)
the vector space of
r
-forms in
X
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
exterior produc
t of the forms
\omega\in\Omega^{r}(X)
and
\mu\in\Omega^{s}(X)
is the form
\omega\wedge\mu\in\Omega^{r+s}(X)
defined as

(\omega\wedge\mu)_{\alpha}=\omega_{\alpha}\wedge_{U}\mu_{\alpha},\quad(\alpha,U)\in\mathcal{D},

where
\wedge_{U}
is the usual exterior product in
\Omega(U)
.

Analogously,

Definition 3.3
.

The
exterior derivative

\mathrm{d}\colon\Omega^{r}(X,\mathcal{D})\to\Omega^{r+1}(X,\mathcal{D})
is defined as

(\mathrm{d}_{X}\omega)_{\alpha}=\mathrm{d}_{U}\omega_{\alpha},\quad(\alpha,U)\in\mathcal{D}.

The
De Rham cohomology groups

\mathrm{H}(X,\mathcal{D})
of the diffeological space
(X,\mathcal{D})
are those of the complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(X,\mathcal{D}),\mathrm{d})
.

Any smooth map
f\colon X\to Y
between diffeological spaces induces a morphism
f^{*}\colon\Omega(Y)\to\Omega(X)
given by
the pull-back

(f^{*}\omega)_{\alpha}=\omega_{f\circ\alpha}
. In particular, if
\alpha\colon U\to X
is a plot and
\omega
is a form on
X
, then
\omega_{\alpha}=\alpha^{*}\omega
.

Since

\mathrm{d}_{X}\circ f^{*}=f^{*}\circ\mathrm{d}_{Y}
,
there is an induced morphism

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}(Y)\to\mathrm{H}(X)
in cohomology given as

f^{*}([\omega])=[f^{*}\omega]
.

3.2.
Horizontal forms

For later purposes, we need to recall the notion of
horizontal form
that we considered in
[
11
, Section 2.3]
. The
\alpha
-horizontal forms are called
basic forms
in Iglesias’ book
[
8
, Art. 6.38]
.

If
\alpha\colon U\to X
is a smooth map between diffeological spaces (in particular, if
\alpha
is a plot on
X
), the image of the induced map
\alpha^{*}\colon\Omega(X)\to\Omega(U)
is contained in the subcomplex of
\alpha
-horizontal forms
.

Definition 3.4
.

The differential form
\theta\in\Omega(U)
is
\alpha
-horizontal
when for any changes of coordinates
h,h^{\prime}\colon V\to U
such that
\alpha\circ h=\alpha\circ h^{\prime}
, the following condition holds:

h^{*}\theta=(h^{\prime})^{*}\theta
.

The horizontal forms form a subcomplex
\Omega(\alpha)
of
\Omega(U)
because if
\theta\in\Omega^{k}(\alpha)
and
\alpha\circ h=\alpha\circ h^{\prime}
then

h^{*}(\mathrm{d}_{U}\theta)=\mathrm{d}_{V}h^{*}\theta=\mathrm{d}_{V}(h^{\prime})^{*}\theta=(h^{\prime})^{*}(\mathrm{d}_{U}\theta),

hence
\mathrm{d}_{U}\theta\in\Omega^{k+1}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}Z
are smooth maps between diffeological spaces and
\omega\in\Omega(g)\subset\Omega(Y)
is a
g
-horizontal form, then
f^{*}\omega\in\Omega(X)
is a
(g\circ f)
-horizontal form.

3.3.
Relative De Rham cohomology

The following is the analogous version of a usual notion of relative cohomology for manifolds, as in Godbillon’s book
[
4
]
.

Let
\alpha\colon U\to X
be a smooth map between diffeological spaces.
Let
\alpha^{*}\colon\Omega(X)\to\Omega(U)
be the induced morphism of De Rham complexes.

Definition 3.6
.

The
relative cohomology groups
(first version)
\mathrm{H}(X,\alpha)
of the smooth map
\alpha\colon U\to X
are those of the complex
\Omega(X,\alpha)=\ker\alpha^{*}
, the kernel of

\alpha^{\ast}
.

Note that if
\alpha^{*}\omega=0
, then
\alpha^{*}d\omega=d\alpha^{*}\omega=0
, hence
\ker\alpha^{*}
is a subcomplex of
\Omega(X)
and we have an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(X,\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(U).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{*}}

However, this definition has a limitation: the morphism
\alpha^{*}
is not surjective due to the lack of partitions of unity in diffeological spaces. As a consequence, it is not possible to construct a long exact sequence in cohomology.

A different relative De Rham theory for manifolds was introduced by Bott-Tu in
[
2
, Prop. 6.49]
. We will introduce a similar theory for diffeological spaces as our second version of the relative cohomology groups.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\alpha\colon U\to X
be a smooth map between diffeological spaces. The
relative De Rham complex
(second version)
\varOmega(X,\alpha)
is defined as

\varOmega^{r}(X,\alpha)=\Omega^{r}(X)\oplus\Omega^{r-1}(\alpha),\quad r\geq 0,

with differential

\mathit{d}\colon\varOmega^{r}(X,\alpha)\to\varOmega^{r+1}(X,\alpha)

given by

\mathit{d}(\omega,\theta)=(\mathrm{d}\omega,\alpha^{*}\omega-\mathrm{d}\theta)
.

The differential is well defined because
\omega\in\Omega(X)
implies
\alpha^{*}\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
. It is easy to check that
\mathit{d}\circ\mathit{d}=0
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{r}(X,\alpha)
the cohomology groups of
(\varOmega^{\bullet}(X,\alpha),\mathit{d})
. A cohomology class is represented by a closed form
\omega
in
X
which becomes exact when pulled back to
U
.

Proposition 3.8
.

There is
the short exact sequence of complexes

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{r-1}(\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varOmega^{r}(X,\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{r}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

where
i(\theta)=(0,\theta)
and
\pi(\omega,\theta)=\omega
. This induces a long exact sequence in cohomology:

(3.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{r-1}(\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}^{r}(X,\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{r}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{r}(\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

Notice that
i

) is just
\alpha^{*}
.

Remark 3.9
.

For a differentiable manifold
X
and an open subet
i_{U}\colon U\hookrightarrow X
, the complexes
\Omega(X,U)
and
\varOmega(X,i_{U})
are not isomorphic but have isomorphic cohomology groups, by a partition of unity argument.

The diffeological De Rham complexes
\Omega(X,\alpha)
and
\varOmega(X,\alpha)
are related in the following way.

Proposition 3.10
.

We define a morphism of complexes
F\colon\Omega(X,\alpha)\to\varOmega(X,\alpha)
given by
F(\omega)=(\omega,0).

Let
F^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}(X,\alpha)\to\mathcal{H}(X,\alpha)
be the morphism induced in cohomology.
If
\alpha^{*}
is surjective then
F^{*}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We have
dF(\omega)=d(\omega,0)=(d\omega,\alpha^{*}\omega)=(d\omega,0)
because
\omega\in\ker\alpha^{*}
hence
dF=Fd
.
Then
F^{*}([\omega])=[(\omega,0)]
,
d\omega=0
and
\alpha^{*}\omega=0
.

Firstly we show that
F^{*}
is injective.
Let
F^{*}([\omega])=0
, that is,
[(\omega,0)]=0
, hence if

(\omega,0)=d(\omega_{1},\theta_{1})=(d\omega_{1},\alpha^{*}\omega_{1}-d\theta_{1}),

then
\omega=d\omega_{1}
.
The problem is that
\omega_{1}
is not
\alpha
-horizontal. Let
\tilde{\theta_{1}}\in\ \Omega(X)
such that
\alpha^{*}\tilde{\theta_{1}}=\theta_{1}
.
Then
\omega=d(\omega_{1}-d\tilde{\theta_{1}})
; but

\alpha^{*}(\omega_{1}-d\tilde{\theta_{1}})=\alpha^{*}\omega_{1}-\alpha^{*}d\tilde{\theta_{1}}=\alpha^{*}\omega_{1}-d\alpha^{*}\tilde{\theta_{1}}=\alpha^{*}\omega_{1}-d\theta_{1}=0

because
\alpha^{*}\omega_{1}=d\theta_{1}
. Hence
\omega=d(\alpha-\text{horizontal form})
, so
[\omega]=0
.

Now, we show that
F^{*}
is surjective.
Let
[(\omega,\theta)]\in\ \mathcal{H}(X,\alpha)
, that is,
d\omega=0
,
\alpha^{*}\omega=d\theta
. Let
\tilde{\theta}\in\ \Omega(X)
such that
\alpha^{*}\tilde{\theta}=\theta
. Then

(\omega-d\tilde{\theta},0)+d(\tilde{\theta},0)=(\omega-d\tilde{\theta},0)+(\mathrm{d}\tilde{\theta},\alpha^{*}\tilde{\theta})=(\omega,\theta),

hence

[(\omega,\theta)]=[(\omega-d\tilde{\theta},0)]=F^{*}([\omega-d\tilde{\theta}]).

Notice that

\alpha^{*}(\omega-d\tilde{\theta})=\alpha^{*}\omega-\alpha^{*}d\tilde{\theta}=\alpha^{*}\omega-d\theta=0.\qed

Example 3.11

(The circle)
.

The cohomology of the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, with its diffeology as a manifold, is the usual De Rham cohomology (see
[
7
]
), that is

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X)=\langle 1\rangle=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X)=\langle\mathrm{vol}\rangle=\mathbb{R}
.

Example 3.12

(The irrational torus)
.

Let
a
be an irrational number. The irrational torus
X=\mathbb{T}_{a}
(
[
3
]
, cf.
[
8
, Exercise 1.4]
) is the quotient of
U=\mathbb{R}
by the equivalence relation
s\sim t
iff
s-t=m+na
for some integers
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

This diffeological space
X
is the space of leaves of the flow
\mathcal{F}
on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
by lines of constant slope
a
. This is an example of a foliation with dense leaves, which is defined by the closed form
\mathrm{d}y-a\mathrm{d}x
.

It has been proved that the
basic cohomology

\mathrm{H}(M/\mathcal{F})
of any foliation equals the De Rham cohomology of its space of leaves

as a diffeological space (
[
7
]
), hence
\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{T}_{a})\cong\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{T}^{2}/\mathcal{F})

On the other hand, it is well known that the basic cohomology

) of the flow

is the cohomology of the abelian Lie algebra

, that is,
\mathrm{H}^{0}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}=\mathbb{R}
.

3.4.
Subductions

Subductions (in the terminology of
[
8
, Art. 1.47]
) are an important particular example of smooth maps in diffeology. They are surjective maps characterized by the local factorization of the plots on the codomain through the diffeology of the domain (
[
8
, Art. 1.43]
).

Theorem 3.13

(
[
8
, Art. 6.39]
)
.

If
\pi\colon U\to X
is a subduction then the induced morphism

\pi^{*}\colon\Omega(X)\to\Omega(U)
is injective.

Corollary 3.14
.

For a subduction
\pi\colon U\to X
, the complex
\Omega(X)
is isomorphic to the complex
\Omega(\pi)\subset\Omega(U)
of
\pi
-horizontal forms.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in\Omega^{k}(\pi)\subset\Omega^{k}(U)
be a
\pi
-horizontal form. We define a form
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(X)
as follows: if
\gamma\colon W\to X
is a plot, and
p\in W
, then
\gamma
locally factors through
\pi
, say

\gamma=\pi\circ h
for some
h\colon W_{p}\to U
. We define
\omega_{\gamma}=h^{*}\theta
in the neighbourhood
W_{p}
.

It is easy to check that this definition does not depend on
h
(because
\theta
is
\pi
-horizontal), that the form
\omega
is well defined (that is, it verifies the compatibility with the changes of coordinates), and that
\pi^{*}\omega=\theta
.

This proves that the image of
\pi^{*}
is
\Omega(\pi)
. Hence
\Omega(X)\cong\Omega(\pi)
by Theorem
3.13
.
∎

Then, for any subduction
\pi\colon U\to X
, the first type relative cohomology group is
\mathrm{H}(X,\pi)=0
because
\ker\pi^{*}=0
.

On the other hand, the second type De Rham complex
is isomorphic by
\pi^{*}
with the complex

(3.3)

\cdots\to\Omega^{r}(\pi)\oplus\Omega^{r-1}(\pi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{r+1}(\pi)\oplus\Omega^{r}(\pi)\to\cdots

with differential

\mathrm{d}(\omega,\mu)=(\mathrm{d}\omega,\omega-\mathrm{d}\mu)
.

Example 3.15
.

Let
X=\mathbb{T}_{a}
be the irrational torus of Example
3.12
and let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be the subduction map for the equivalence relation defining
X
.
Since
\pi
is a subduction, the forms in
\Omega(X)
are the
\pi
-horizontal forms in
M=\mathbb{R}
.

First, the horizontal
0
-forms are the constant functions. Indeed, let

be a
0
-form, that is, a real function. We consider the maps

given by

h(t)=t,\quad h^{\prime}(t)=t+m+n\alpha.

Clearly
\pi\circ h=\pi\circ h^{\prime}
. The condition
h^{*}f=(h^{\prime})^{*}f
means

f\circ h=f\circ h^{\prime}
,
that is,

f(t)=f(t+m+n\alpha)
, for all
t
.
Since the integers
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
are arbitrary and the set

\Delta(\alpha)=\{m+n\alpha\colon m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is dense in
\mathbb{R}
, we obtain that
f
is a constant function.

Analogously, the
\pi
-horizontal
1
-forms are
\omega=c\,dt
where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant.
Then we can write
\Omega^{0}(\pi)=\langle 1\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}
and
\Omega^{1}(\pi)=\langle dt\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}
,
and the differential
d\colon\Omega^{0}\to\Omega^{1}
is
d=0
.

We can now compute the two types of relative cohomology groups for
\pi
.
The first type relative groups are
\mathrm{H}(X,\pi)=0
because they are the cohomology of
\ker\pi^{*}=0
.
For the second type groups
\mathcal{H}(\pi)
we consider the complex (

):

\cdots\to\Omega^{0}(\pi)\oplus 0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}(\pi)\oplus\Omega^{0}(\pi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\oplus\Omega^{1}(\pi)\to\cdots

with differential

\mathrm{d}_{0}(c,0)=(0,c),\quad\mathrm{d}_{1}(a\,dt,b)=(0,a\,dt).

Then

\mathcal{H}^{0}(X,\pi)=\ker\mathrm{d}_{0}=0

and

\mathcal{H}^{1}(X,\pi)=\ker\mathrm{d}_{1}/\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\mathrm{d}_{0}=0
.

4.
Limits and colimits

In our previous paper
[
12
]
we outlined the importance of several categorical constructions in the context of diffeological spaces.

4.1.
Join

The category of diffeological spaces and smooth maps
is stable under limits and colimits (see
[
1
]
). This allows us to introduce the
join
of two maps.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\colon U\to X
and

5.
Generating families

Definition 5.1
.

[
8
, Art. 1.66]
]
A collection

\mathcal{F}=\{(\alpha_{\lambda},U_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of plots is a
generating family
of the diffeology
\mathcal{D}
if
\mathcal{D}
is the least diffeology on
X
containing the family
\mathcal{F}
. Equivalently, any plot of
\mathcal{D}
locally factors through some element of the family
\mathcal{F}
.

Without loss of generality, we will assume that the family
\mathcal{F}
contains all constant plots.

The following result was proved by us in
[
12
]
. In short, a diffeological space is the join of any generating family.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{D})
be a diffeological space. A family
\mathcal{F}=\{\alpha_{i}\colon U_{i}\to X\}
of plots is a generating family for
\mathcal{D}
if and only if there is a diffeomorphism
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\!\ast\!\cdots\!\ast\!\alpha_{n}\colon U=U_{1}\!\ast\!\cdots\ast U_{n}\to X

commuting with the maps
\alpha_{i}
and the natural maps
j_{i}\colon U_{i}\to U
, that is,
\alpha\circ j_{i}=\alpha_{i}
for all
i
.

Corollary 5.3
.

If
\{(\alpha_{1},U_{1}),\dots,(\alpha_{n},U_{n})\}
is a generating family of the diffeology
\mathcal{D}
on
X
, then

(1)

the universal map

\alpha=\alpha_{1}\ast\cdots\ast\alpha_{n}\colon U=U_{1}\ast\cdots\ast U_{n}\to X
is a diffeomorphism (hence a subduction), so

\mathrm{H}(X,\mathcal{D})\cong\mathrm{H}(U_{1}\ast\cdots\ast U_{n})=\mathrm{H}(\alpha_{1}\ast\cdots\ast\alpha_{n}).

(2)

\mathrm{H}(X,\alpha_{1}\!\ast\!\cdots\!\ast\!\alpha_{n})=0
for the first type relative cohomoloy.

(3)

\mathcal{H}(X,\alpha_{1}\!\ast\!\cdots\!\ast\!\alpha_{n})=0
for the second type relative cohomology.

Remark 5.4
.

The two latter results easily extend to any infinite generating family.

In the next Lemmas we further explore how the De Rham complex of
X
is determined by a generating family.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\{(\alpha_{\lambda},U_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a generating family of plots for the diffeological space
X
. If
\omega\in\Omega(X)
is a form such that
\alpha_{\lambda}^{*}\omega=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then
\omega=0
.

Proof.

Any plot
\gamma\colon W\to X
locally factors through some
\alpha_{\lambda}
, say
\gamma=\alpha_{\lambda}\circ h
for some change of variables
h\colon W\to U_{\lambda}
. Then, locally,

\omega_{\gamma}=\gamma^{*}\omega=h^{*}\alpha_{\lambda}^{*}\omega=h^{*}0=0.\qed

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\{(\alpha_{\lambda},U_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a generating familiy of the diffeological space
X
. Let
P
be the pullback of those plots, with projections
p_{\lambda}\colon P\to U_{\lambda}
as in Diagram (
4.2
). If
\theta_{\lambda}\in\Omega(U_{\lambda})
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, are differential forms of the same degree
r
, such that
p_{\lambda}^{*}\theta_{\lambda}=p_{\mu}^{*}\theta_{\mu}
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
, then there is a unique form
\theta\in\Omega(X)
such that
\alpha_{\lambda}^{*}\theta=\theta_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

In other words, any form
\omega
in
X
is completely determined by the pull-backs
\alpha_{\lambda}^{*}\omega
.

Proof.

The form
\theta
will be defined by giving its value
\theta_{\gamma}
in any plot
\gamma\colon W\to X
. Let
p\in W
, then
\gamma
locally factors through some
\alpha_{\lambda}\colon U_{\lambda}\to X
, say

\gamma=\alpha_{\lambda}\circ h
for some
h\colon W_{p}\to U_{\lambda}
. Then we define

\theta_{\gamma}=h^{*}\theta_{\lambda}
on
W_{p}
.

To prove that it is well defined, assume that there are
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda

such that

\alpha_{\lambda}\circ h_{\lambda}=\gamma_{|W_{p}}=\alpha_{\mu}\circ h_{\mu}
.
Then there exists
F\colon W_{p}\to P_{\lambda\mu}
such that

p^{\prime}_{\lambda}\circ F=h_{\lambda}
and
p^{\prime}_{\mu}\circ F=h_{\mu}
, where
P_{\lambda\mu}
is the pull-back of
\alpha_{\lambda}
and
\alpha_{\mu}
. By Lemma
4.3
there exists
F_{\lambda\mu}\colon P\to P_{\lambda\mu}
such that
p_{\lambda}=p^{\prime}_{\lambda}\circ F_{\lambda\mu}
and
p_{\mu}=p^{\prime}_{\mu}\circ F_{\lambda\mu}
. By hypothesis, we have

p_{\lambda}^{*}\theta_{\lambda}=p_{\mu}^{*}\theta_{\mu}
, hence

F_{\lambda\mu}^{*}(p^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{*}\theta_{\lambda}=F_{\lambda\mu}^{*}(p^{\prime}_{\mu})^{*}\theta_{\mu}.

But
F_{\lambda\mu}
is a subduction map, so the induced morphism
F_{\lambda\mu}^{*}
is injective, by Theorem
3.13
. That means that
(p^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{*}\theta_{\lambda}=(p^{\prime}_{\mu})^{*}\theta_{\mu}
, so

\displaystyle h^{*}_{\lambda}\theta_{\lambda}=

\displaystyle(p^{\prime}_{\lambda}\circ F)^{*}\theta_{\lambda}=F^{*}(p^{\prime}_{\lambda})^{*}\theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{*}(p^{\prime}_{\mu})^{*}\theta_{\mu}=(p^{\prime}_{\mu}\circ F)^{*}\theta_{\mu}=h^{*}_{\mu}\theta_{\mu}.\qed

Corollary 5.7
.

For the disjoint union
U\sqcup V
, a form
\theta\in\Omega(U\sqcup V)
is completely determined by giving the forms
i_{U}^{*}\theta\in\Omega(U)
and
i_{V}^{*}\theta\in\Omega(V)
.

Proof.

The pullback of
i_{U}
and
i_{V}
is
P=\emptyset
.
∎

7.
Relative cup product

7.1.
Absolute cup product

We can define a structure of algebra in the De Rham complex
\Omega^{*}(X)=\oplus_{r\geq 0}\Omega^{r}(X)
by means of the exterior product of forms. Since the exterior derivative
d
verifies

d(\omega\wedge\mu)=(d\omega)\wedge\mu+(-1)^{r}\omega\wedge(d\mu),\qquad\omega\in\Omega^{r}(X),\,\mu\in\Omega^{s}(X),

the exterior product induces a structure of algebra in the De Rham cohomology
\mathrm{H}(X,\mathcal{D})
.

Definition 7.1
.

The morphism

\smallsmile\colon{\rm H^{r}}(X)\otimes{\rm{\rm H^{s}}}(X)\to H^{r+s}(X),\quad[\omega]\smallsmile[\mu]=[\omega\wedge\mu],

is called the
absolute cup product.

7.2.
Relative cup product, first type

Definition 7.2
.

Let
(\alpha,U)
,
(\beta,V)
be two plots and let
(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta,U\!\ast\!V)
be their join.
The
relative cup product

\smallsmile\colon\mathrm{H}^{r}(X,\alpha)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{s}(X,\beta)\to\mathrm{H}^{r+s}(X,\alpha\!\ast\!\beta),

for the first type relative cohomology,
is defined as

[\omega]\smallsmile[\mu]=[\omega\wedge\mu]
.

Proposition 7.3
.

The relative cup product in Definition
7.2
is well defined.

Proof.

We have differential forms

\omega\in\Omega^{r}(X)
with
\mathrm{d}_{X}\omega=0
and
\alpha^{*}\omega=0
, analogously
\mu\in\Omega^{s}(X)
, with
\mathrm{d}_{X}\mu=0
and
\beta^{*}\mu=0
.

Clearly
\omega\wedge\mu\in\Omega^{r+s}(X)
and

\mathrm{d}_{X}(\omega\wedge\mu)=0

Denote

\rho\colon\!\!\!=(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)^{*}(\omega\wedge\mu)\in\Omega(U\!\ast\!V).

It only remains to show that
\rho=0
.

Let
\gamma\colon W\to U\!\ast\!V
be a plot in
U\!\ast\!V
. We have to show that
(\rho_{\gamma})_{p}=0
, for any
p\in W
. By definition of subduction diffeology, there is some neighbourhood
W_{p}\subset W
such that
\gamma_{|W_{p}}
factors through
U\sqcup V
, say

\gamma_{|W_{p}}=\mathcal{C}\circ\tilde{\gamma}

where
\mathcal{C}\colon U\sqcup V\to U\!\ast\!V
and
\tilde{\gamma}\colon W_{p}\to U\sqcup V
.

Assume for instance that
\tilde{\gamma}(p)\in U
. Then, by definition of coproduct diffeology, there is some neigbourhood
W^{\prime}_{p}\subset W_{p}
such that
\gamma
factors through
U
, that is,

\gamma_{|W^{\prime}_{p}}=\mathcal{C}\circ i_{U}\circ\bar{\gamma}
,
for some map
\bar{\gamma}\colon W^{\prime}_{p}\to U
.

Then in a neighbourhood
W^{\prime}_{p}
of
p\in W
we have

(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)\circ\gamma=(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)\circ\mathcal{C}\circ i_{u}\circ\bar{\gamma}=\alpha\circ\bar{\gamma},

hence

\displaystyle\rho_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{*}\rho=\gamma^{*}(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)^{*}(\omega\wedge\mu)=((\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)\circ\gamma)^{*}(\omega\wedge\mu)

\displaystyle=(\alpha\circ\bar{\gamma})^{*}(\omega\wedge\mu)=(\bar{\gamma})^{*}\alpha^{*}(\omega\wedge\mu)=0,

because
\alpha^{*}(\omega\wedge\mu)=\alpha^{*}\omega\wedge\alpha^{*}\mu

and
\alpha^{*}\omega=0
.

The proof is analogous when
\tilde{\gamma}(p)\in V
.
∎

If the Mayer-Vietoris sequence is split, then we have the map

where

We have

\varPi K=\varPi-\varPi S\varPi=\varPi-\varPi=0

because
\varPi S=\mathrm{id}
. Hence

K\colon\ \Omega^{r}(\alpha)\oplus\Omega^{r}(\beta)\to\Omega^{r}(\alpha\ast\beta)

and it is more appropriate to write

(7.1)

JK=\mathrm{id}-S\Pi.

Moreover

KJ=(\mathrm{id}-S\varPi)J=J-S\varPi J=J.

Notice that
K
is not a morphism of complexes, in fact

\mathrm{d}K-K\mathrm{d}=-\Delta\varPi

because

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}K-K\mathrm{d}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{id}-S\varPi)-(\mathrm{id}-S\varPi)\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{d}S\varPi-\mathrm{d}+S\varPi\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\mathrm{d}S+S\mathrm{d})\varPi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta\varPi.

Definition 7.4
.

We define a relative cup product,

\displaystyle\smallsmile\colon\mathcal{H}^{r}(X,\alpha)\otimes\mathcal{H}^{s}(X,\beta)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{r+s}(X,\alpha*\beta),

for differentiable maps
\alpha\colon U\to X
and
\beta\colon V\to X
, as

(7.2)

\displaystyle[(\omega,\theta)]\smallsmile[\omega^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})]=[(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime},\xi)],

where

\xi=K(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})+(-1)^{\omega}\Delta(p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}).

In what follows we will verify that this product is well defined
and skew-symmetric.

Proposition 7.5
.

The product in (
7.2
) is well defined.

Proof.

First,
\mathrm{d}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=0

because

\mathrm{d}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\mathrm{d}\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime}+(-1)^{r}\omega\wedge\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}
, with

\mathrm{d}\omega=0

and
\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}=0
.

Now we will prove that
(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)^{*}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\mathrm{d}\xi
.

We have

\alpha_{U}^{*}(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)^{*}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\alpha^{*}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\alpha^{*}\omega\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\mathrm{d}\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime})

and

\beta_{V}^{*}(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)^{*}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\beta^{*}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\beta^{*}{\omega^{\prime}}=\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}=(-1)^{\omega}\mathrm{d}(\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime}),

because

\mathrm{d}\beta^{*}\omega=0
and

\mathrm{d}(\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})=(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}.

Hence

(7.3)

J(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)^{*}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime}).

Now we compute
J\mathrm{d}\xi
.
We have

\displaystyle J\mathrm{d}\xi=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}J\xi=\mathrm{d}(JK(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\omega}\mathrm{d}J\Delta(p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})-\mathrm{d}S\varPi(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\omega}\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}S-S\mathrm{d})(p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})-\mathrm{d}S(p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{U}^{*}\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime}-(-1)^{\omega}p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\omega}\mathrm{d}S(\mathrm{d}p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}+(-1)^{\theta}p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge\mathrm{d}p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})-\mathrm{d}S(p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{U}^{*}\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime}-(-1)^{\omega}p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{\omega}\mathrm{d}S(p_{U}^{*}\mathrm{d}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}+(-1)^{\theta}p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime})-\mathrm{d}S(p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{U}^{*}\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime}-(-1)^{\omega}p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle-(-1)^{\omega}\mathrm{d}S(p_{U}^{*}\alpha^{*}\omega\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}+(-1)^{\theta}p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}\omega^{\prime}).

Now, since
\alpha\circ p_{U}=p_{X}=\beta\circ p_{V}
, it follows that

p_{U}^{*}\alpha^{*}=p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}
. Thus, we have

J\mathrm{d}\xi=\mathrm{d}(\theta\wedge\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime},(-1)^{\omega}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge\theta^{\prime}),

like in Equation (
7.3
),
because the term in

p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{U}^{*}\alpha^{*}\omega^{\prime}=p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}\omega^{\prime}=p_{U}^{*}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}

has coefficient

(-1)-(-1)^{\omega}(-1)^{\theta}=(-1)-(-1)^{\omega+\omega-1}=0,

while the term

p_{V}^{*}\beta^{*}\omega\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}=p^{*}_{U}\alpha^{*}\omega\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}=p_{U}^{*}\mathrm{d}\theta\wedge p_{V}^{*}\theta^{\prime}

has coefficient

(-1)^{\omega}-(-1)^{\omega}=0
.

Theorem 7.6
.

The cup product is skew-symmetric, that is

[(\omega^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})]\smallsmile[(\omega,\theta)]=(-1)^{\omega\omega^{\prime}}[(\omega,\theta)]\smallsmile[(\omega^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})].

Proof.

First we will carefully review the notation we are using. Given two smooth maps
\alpha\colon U\to X
and
\beta\colon V\to X
we consider the pullback
P
of
\alpha
and
\beta
and the map
p_{X}=\alpha p_{U}=\beta p_{V}
, see Diagram (
4.2
).

If we consider the pullback
P^{\prime}
of the maps
\beta
and
\alpha
in reverse order, there is an obvious isomorphism

induced by the

\phi\colon U\times V\to V\times U,\quad\phi(u,v)=(v,u).

Notice that
p_{U}=p_{U}^{\prime}\circ\phi,p_{V}=p_{V}^{\prime}\circ\phi

p_{X}=p^{\prime}_{X}\circ\phi

The induced isomorphism in the horizontal De Rham complexes

\phi^{*}\colon\Omega(p^{\prime}_{X})\cong\Omega(p_{X})

\Sigma\xi_{i}^{U}\otimes\xi_{i}^{V}

\Sigma\xi_{i}^{V}\otimes\xi_{i}^{U}
.
Then, in order to simplify notation, we will consider that
\phi^{*}=\mathrm{id}
.

Analogously, there is an obvious isomorphism between the joins
\alpha\!\ast\!\beta\cong\beta\!\ast\!\alpha
induced by
U\sqcup V\cong V\sqcup U
. Again we shall consider that

\Omega(\alpha\!\ast\!\beta)\cong\Omega(\beta\!\ast\!\alpha)

Now remember that the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is
split
if the map
\varPi

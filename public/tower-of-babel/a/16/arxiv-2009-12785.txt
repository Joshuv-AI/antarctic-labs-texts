Abstract
We show that equivariant tilting modules over equivariant algebras induce equivalences of derived factorization categories. As an application, we show that the derived category of a noncommutative resolution of a linear section of a Pfaffian variety is equivalent to the derived factorization category of a noncommutative gauged Landau–Ginzburg model ( Λ , χ , w ) 𝔾 m (\Lambda,\chi,w)^{\mathbb{G}_{m}} , where Λ \Lambda is a noncommutative resolution of the quotient singularity W / GSp ⁡ ( Q ) W/\operatorname{GSp}(Q) arising from a certain representation W W of the symplectic similitude group GSp ⁡ ( Q ) \operatorname{GSp}(Q) of a symplectic vector space Q Q .

Introduction

1.1
Backgrounds

Derived categories of coherent sheaves on varieties are one of the important invariants in algebraic geometry, and they have interesting links to other fields of mathematics. For example, if a smooth variety
X
admits a tilting bundle
\mathcal{T}
, the derived category
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
of coherent sheaves on
X
is equivalent to the derived category
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)
of finitely generated right modules over the endomorphism ring
\Lambda\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{End}_{X}(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
.
Once we have such an equivalence, we can study the derived category of coherent sheaves by the representation theory of noncommutative algebras. However, if
X
is a smooth projective Calabi–Yau variety,
X
can never admit a tilting bundle.

Recently, Okonek–Teleman proved that the derived category of a regular zero section in a certain smooth variety is equivalent to the derived factorization category of a noncommutative gauged Landau–Ginzbubrg (LG) model
[
13
]
. In particular, they proved that the derived category
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z)
of a Calabi–Yau complete intersection
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is equivalent to the derived factorization category
\operatorname{Dmod}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\Lambda,\chi,w)
of a noncommutative gauged LG model
(\Lambda,\chi,w)^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
, where
\Lambda
is a noncommutative crepant resolution of some quotient singularity. This result gives a new approach to the study of the derived categories of Calabi–Yau complete intersections. For example, it is interesting to interpret autoequivalences of
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z)
with autoequivalences of
\operatorname{Dmod}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\Lambda,\chi,w)
induced by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant tilting modules/complexes over
\Lambda
, and it is expected that this interpretation enables us to study the fundamental group action on
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z)
, constructed in
[
5
]
using variations of GIT quotients, by equivariant tilting theory over
\Lambda
.

In this paper, for any reductive affine algebraic group
G
,
we generalize Okonek–Teleman’s result to a
G
-equivariant setting. More precisely, we prove that
G
-equivariant tilting modules over
G
-equivariant algebras induce equivalences of derived factorization categories of noncommutative gauged LG models. Moreover, combining Rennemo–Segal’s results in
[
16
]
with our result, we prove that the derived category of a noncommutative resolution of a generic linear section of a Pfaffian variety is equivalent to the derived factorization category of a noncommutative gauged LG model
(\Lambda,\chi,w)^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
, where
\Lambda
is a non-commutative resolution of the quotient singularity
W/\operatorname{GSp}(Q)
arising from a certain representation
W
of the symplectic similitude group
\operatorname{GSp}(Q)
of a symplectic vector space
Q
.

1.2
Equivariant algebras and noncommutative LG models

Let
X
be a scheme, and
G
an algebraic group acting on
X
. Denote by
\sigma\colon G\times X\to X
the morphism defining the
G
-action on
X
, and write
\pi\colon G\times X\to X
for the natural projection. Let
\uprho\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}
be a (not necessarily commutative)
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra that is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. A
G
-equivariant structure
on
\mathcal{A}
is an isomorphism
\uptheta^{\mathcal{A}}\colon\pi^{*}\mathcal{A}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\sigma^{*}\mathcal{A}
of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{G\times X}
-algebras such that the pair
\big(\mathcal{A},\uptheta^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
defines the
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, and we call the pair
\big(\mathcal{A},\uptheta^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
a
G
-equivariant coherent
X
-algebra
. Similarly, we define a
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module
to be the pair
\big(\mathcal{M},\uptheta^{\mathcal{M}}\big)
of a right
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{M}
and an isomorphism
\uptheta^{\mathcal{M}}\colon\pi^{*}\mathcal{M}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\sigma^{*}\mathcal{M}
of
\pi^{*}\mathcal{A}
-modules such that the pair
\big(\mathcal{M},\uptheta^{\mathcal{M}}\big)
defines a
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. We write
\operatorname{coh}_{G}\mathcal{A}
for the category of
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{A}
-modules. Then we have an equivalence

\displaystyle\operatorname{coh}_{G}\mathcal{A}\cong\operatorname{coh}[\mathcal{A}/G]

of categories, where
[\mathcal{A}/G]
is the associated coherent
[X/G]
-algebra (see Appendix
A
).

Let
\chi\colon G\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
be a character of
G
, and
w\colon X\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
a
\chi
-semi-invariant regular function on
X
, i.e.,
W(g\cdot x)=\chi(g)W(x)
for any
(g,x)\in G\times X
. We call the data
(\mathcal{A},\chi,w)^{G}
a
gauged Landau–Ginzburg
(LG)
model
, and it is said to be
noncommutative
if the algebra
\mathcal{A}
is not commutative. We define a
factorization
of
(\mathcal{A},\chi,w)^{G}
to be a sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}\mathcal{M}_{0}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\chi)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}_{1}

consisting of
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{A}
-modules
\mathcal{M}_{i}
and
G
-equivariant
\mathcal{A}
-linear maps
\varphi_{i}
such that the compositions
\varphi_{0}\circ\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{1}(\chi)\circ\varphi_{0}
are the multiplications by
w
.
To a gauged LG model
(\mathcal{A},\chi,w)^{G}
we associate the
derived factorization category

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dcoh}_{G}(\mathcal{A},\chi,w)

whose objects are factorizations of
(\mathcal{A},\chi,w)^{G}
. If
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
, the
G
-equivariant
X
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
corresponds to the
G
-equivariant
R
-algebra
A\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{A})
, and we write
\operatorname{Dmod}_{G}(A,\chi,w)
for the corresponding derived factorization category.

1.3
Results

Let
X
be a variety,
G
an affine algebraic group acting on
X
, and
H
a closed normal subgroup of
G
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{T}\in\operatorname{coh}_{G}\mathcal{A}
is called a
(G,H)
-tilting
(resp.
partial
(G,H)
-tilting
) if the restriction
\mathcal{T}_{H}\in\operatorname{coh}_{H}\mathcal{A}
satisfies the following conditions (1), (2) and (3) (resp. (1) and (2)):

1.

We have
\operatorname{Ext}_{\operatorname{coh}_{H}\mathcal{A}}^{i}(\mathcal{T}_{H},\mathcal{T}_{H})=0
for all
i>0
.

2.

The object
\mathcal{T}_{H}
is
compact
in
\operatorname{D}\big(\operatorname{Qcoh}_{H}\mathcal{A}\big)
.

3.

The object
\mathcal{T}_{H}
is a
generator
of
\operatorname{D}\big(\operatorname{Qcoh}_{H}\mathcal{A}\big)
, i.e., for any complex
A^{\bullet}
in
\operatorname{Qcoh}_{H}\mathcal{A}
,
\operatorname{{\mathbb{R}}Hom}(T,A^{\bullet})\cong 0
in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{T}_{H}))
implies
A^{\bullet}\cong 0
in
\operatorname{D}\big(\operatorname{Qcoh}_{H}\mathcal{A}\big)
.

Let
\operatorname{Spec}R
be an affine variety with a
G
-action such that the induced
H
-action is trivial so that
\operatorname{Spec}R
has the induced
G/H
-action.
Let
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be a
G
-equivariant morphism such that the associated morphism
\overline{f}\colon[X/H]\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is proper. Let
\mathcal{T}\in\operatorname{coh}_{G}\mathcal{A}
be a
G
-equivariant coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module, and write
\Lambda\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{End}_{\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{T})
for the endomorphism ring of the underlying
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{T}
. Then
\Lambda
has a natural
H
-action, and the
H
-invariant ring
\Lambda^{H}
is a
G/H
-equivariant coherent
R
-algebra.
Let
\chi\colon G/H\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
be a character of
G/H
, and define the character
\widehat{\chi}\colon G\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
to be the composition of
\chi
and the natural projection
G\to G/H
. Let
w_{R}\colon\operatorname{Spec}R\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
be a
\chi
-semi-invariant regular function on
\operatorname{Spec}R
, and write
w\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=f^{*}w_{R}
. Then we have two gauged LG models

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{A},\widehat{\chi},w\big)^{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\big(\Lambda^{H},\chi,w_{R}\big)^{G/H}.

The following is a generalization of Okonek–Teleman’s result
[
13
, Theorem 1.11]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.7
)
.

Assume that
G/H
is reductive and
\Lambda^{H}
is of finite global dimension.

1.

If
\mathcal{T}
is partial
(G,H)
-tilting, then we have a fully faithful functor

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dmod}_{G/H}\big(\Lambda^{H},\chi,w_{R}\big)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Dcoh}_{G}\big(\mathcal{A},\widehat{\chi},w\big).

2.

If
\mathcal{T}
is
(G,H)
-tilting and every quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module has a finite injective resolution, we have an equivalecne

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dcoh}_{G}\big(\mathcal{A},\widehat{\chi},w\big)\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{Dmod}_{G/H}\big(\Lambda^{H},\chi,w_{R}\big).

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
v
. For an integer
q
with
0\leq 2q\leq v
, we have a Pfaffian variety

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big\{x\in\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}\mid\operatorname{\sf rk}(x)\leq 2q\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}\big(\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}\big),

and we consider its linear section

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\cap\,\,\mathbb{P}(L),

where
L\subset\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}
is a subspace of
V
such that
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}\neq\varnothing
. If we choose a generic
L
, there is a noncommutative resolution
\mathcal{B}_{L}
of
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}

[
17
,
18
]
, and if
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}
is smooth
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{B}_{L})
is equivalent to
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L})
. Combining Theorem
1.2
and the results in
[
16
]
, we have the following.

Corollary 1.3

(Corollary
5.1
)
.

There is a noncommutative gauged LG model
(\Lambda,\chi,w)^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
such that we have an equivalence

\displaystyle\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{B}_{L})\cong\operatorname{Dmod}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\Lambda,\chi,w)

and that
\Lambda
is a noncommutative resolution of the affine quotient
W/\operatorname{GSp}(Q)
of a certain representation
W
of the symplectic similitude group
\operatorname{GSp}(Q)
of a symplectic vector space
Q
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions and basic properties about derived factorization categories, equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves and tilting objects.

2.1
Derived factorization categories

We recall the basics of derived factorization categories mainly to fix notation. See, for example,
[
2
,
8
,
15
]
for more details.
Throughout this section,
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category with small coproducts such that the small coproducts of families of short exact sequences are exact. In what follows, we fix a triple

(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)

(2.1)

consisting of an exact subcategory
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{A}
, an exact autoequivalence
\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
preserving
\mathcal{E}
and a functor morphism
w\colon\operatorname{id}\to\Phi
that is compatible with
\Phi
, i.e., the equality
w({\Phi(E)})=\Phi(w(E))
holds for every object
E\in\mathcal{E}
. The functor morphism
w\colon\operatorname{id}\to\Phi
is called a
potential
of
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
factorization
of
(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
is a sequence

\displaystyle E=\Bigl(E_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{E}_{1}}E_{0}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{E}_{0}}\Phi(E_{1})\Bigr)

in
\mathcal{E}
such that
\varphi^{E}_{0}\circ\varphi^{E}_{1}=w(E_{1})
and
\Phi\big(\varphi^{E}_{1}\big)\circ\varphi^{E}_{0}=w(E_{0})
. Objects
E_{1}
and
E_{0}
in the above sequence are called the
components
of
E
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
be the above triple.

1.

For two factorizations
E,F
of
(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
, we define the complex
\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)^{\bullet}
with differential
d^{\bullet}_{(E,F)}\colon\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)^{\bullet}\to\operatorname{Hom}(E,F)^{\bullet+1}
by

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}(E,F)^{2n}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(E_{1},\Phi^{n}(F_{1}))\oplus{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(E_{0},\Phi^{n}(F_{0})),

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}(E,F)^{2n+1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(E_{1},\Phi^{n}(F_{0}))\oplus{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{E}}(E_{0},\Phi^{n+1}(F_{1})),

where
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
are the components of
E
and
F
respectively, and

\displaystyle d^{\bullet}_{(E,F)}(f):=\varphi^{F}\circ f-(-1)^{{\rm deg}(f)}f\circ\varphi^{E}\qquad\text{if}\quad f\in{\rm Hom}(E,F)^{{\rm deg}(f)}.

This defines the dg category

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)

of factorizations of
(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
.

2.

The dg category
\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
defines the additive categories

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=Z^{0}(\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)),

\displaystyle\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=H^{0}(\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)).

We call
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
the
homotopy category
of
(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
.

The category
\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
is an exact category, and the homotopy category
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
is a triangulated category (see
[
8
, Propositions 3.5 and 3.9]
). To define the derived factorization categories, we define the totalizations of complexes of factorizations: For an object
E\in\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
, let us set

\displaystyle{\rm Com}(E)^{2i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Phi^{i}(E_{0}),\qquad{\rm Com}(E)^{2i-1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Phi^{i}(E_{1}),

\displaystyle d_{E}^{2i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Phi^{i}\big(\varphi_{0}^{E}\big),\qquad d_{E}^{2i-1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Phi^{i}\big(\varphi_{1}^{E}\big).

Then a periodic (up to twists by
\Phi
) infinite sequence

\displaystyle{\rm Com}(E)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big({\rm Com}(E)^{\bullet},d_{E}^{\bullet}\big)

in
\mathcal{E}
satisfies
d_{E}^{i+1}\circ d_{E}^{i}=w({\rm Com}(E)^{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E^{\bullet}=\big(\cdots\rightarrow E^{i}\xrightarrow{\delta^{i}}E^{i+1}\rightarrow\cdots\big)
be a bounded complex of
\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
. We define the
totalization

\operatorname{Tot}(E^{\bullet})\in\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
of
E^{\bullet}
by

\displaystyle{\rm Tot}(E^{\bullet})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big(T_{1}\xrightarrow{t_{1}}T_{0}\xrightarrow{t_{0}}\Phi(T_{1})\big),

where

\displaystyle T_{l}:=\bigoplus_{i+j=-l}{\rm Com}(E^{i})^{j},

\displaystyle t_{l}|_{{\rm Com}(E^{i})^{j}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\rm Com}(\delta^{i})^{j}+(-1)^{i}d_{E^{i}}^{j}.

Taking totalizations defines an exact functor

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tot}\colon\ \operatorname{Ch^{b}}(\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w))\rightarrow\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\operatorname{A}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
be the smallest thick subcategory of
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
that contains totalizations of all short exact sequences in
\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
. Then we define the
derived factorization category

\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
of the triple
(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
by the Verdier quotient

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)/\operatorname{A}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w).

If
\mathcal{E}
is closed under coproducts in
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by

\operatorname{A^{co}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
the smallest thick subcategory of
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
that contains totalizations of all short exact sequences in
\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
and closed under coproducts. Then we define the
coderived factorization category

\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)/\operatorname{A^{co}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w).

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an exact subcategory of another abelian category
\mathcal{B}
satisfying the same properties of
\mathcal{A}
, and let

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F},\Psi,v)

be a triple as in (
2.1
).

Definition 2.5
.

An additive functor

\displaystyle F\colon\ \mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}

is
factored
with respect to the potentials
(\Phi,w)
and
(\Psi,v)
if there is a functor isomorphism

\displaystyle\alpha\colon\ F\circ\Phi\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\Psi\circ F

such that for every object
E\in\mathcal{E}
, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\Phi(E))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi(F(E)).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(w(E))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v(F(E))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha(E)}

If
F\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
is a factored functor with respect to
(\Phi,w)
and
(\Psi,v)
, then it induces a dg functor

\displaystyle F\colon\ \operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{F},\Psi,v).

This dg functor defines the additive functors

\displaystyle F\colon\ \operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{F},\Psi,v),

\displaystyle F\colon\ \operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{F},\Psi,v),

where the latter functor is an exact functor. To define the derived functors of exact functors between homotopy categories, we need the following results due to
[
2
]
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
2
, Corollary 2.25]
)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
has enough injectives and that the coproducts of injectives are injective. Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the subcategory of injective objects. Then the natural functor

\displaystyle\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{I},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)

is an equivalence.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the subcategory of projective objects in
\mathcal{A}
, and assume that
\mathcal{A}
has enough projectives. Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an abelian subcategory that is preserved by
\Phi

2.2
Quasi-coherent modules over sheaves of algebras

In this subsection, we recall fundamental properties of quasi-coherent modules over sheaves of algebras over schemes.

Let
X
be a scheme. An
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
quasi-coherent
if for any point
x\in X
, there are an open neighborhood
U
of
x

and an exact sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus I}\to\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus J}\to\mathcal{F}|_{U}\to 0,

where
I
and
J
are sets. A quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
is called a
coherent

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, if for every affine open subset
U=\operatorname{Spec}R
of
X
,
\Gamma(U,\mathcal{F})
is a finitely generated
R
-module. We denote by
\operatorname{Qcoh}X
(resp.
\operatorname{coh}X
) the category of quasi-coherent (resp. coherent)
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.

Definition 2.9
.

An
X
-algebra
is a sheaf
\mathcal{A}
of not necessarily commutative algebras together with a morphism
\uprho\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}
of sheaves of algebras such that the image of
\uprho
lies in the center of
\mathcal{A}
and that
\mathcal{A}
is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. An
X
-algebra
\uprho\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}
is said to be
coherent
if
\mathcal{A}
is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. A
morphism
of
X
-algebras is a morphism
\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of sheaves of algebras that is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear.

For an
X
-algebra
\uprho\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}
, a
quasi-coherent
(
resp. coherent
)

\mathcal{A}
-module
is a right
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{M}
that is a quasi-coherent (resp. coherent)
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. For quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-modules
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
, a
morphism
from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{N}
is a morphism of sheaves of right
\mathcal{A}
-modules. We denote by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}

the category of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-modules, and we write
\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{A}
for the full subcategory of coherent
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

Remark 2.10
.

Since the category of right
\mathcal{A}
-modules and the category
\operatorname{Qcoh}X
are both abelian,
\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}
is also an abelian category. Furthermore, in Proposition
3.19
we will see that
\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}
is a Grothendieck category.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a morphism of
X
-algebras. For an object
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{B}
, we define a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{N}_{\varphi}
by the composition

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\times\varphi}\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{N},

where the second morphism is the right
\mathcal{B}
-action of
\mathcal{N}
. This defines the functor

(-)_{\varphi}\colon\ \operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{B}\to\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A},

(2.2)

and we call the functor
(-)_{\varphi}
the
restriction by
\varphi
.

Conversely, for an object
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}
, the tensor product

\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}
has a natural right
\mathcal{B}
-action, and this defines the functor

(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}\colon\ \operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{B},

(2.3)

and we call this functor the
extension by
\varphi
.
It is standard that we have an adjunction

\displaystyle(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}\dashv(-)_{\varphi}.

Although the following propositions might be well known, we give the proofs for reader’s convenience.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
X
be a scheme, and
\uprho\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}
an
X
-algebra.

1.

For every
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}
and every point
x\in X
, there are an open neighborhood
U
of
x
and a surjective
\mathcal{A}|_{U}
-linear map

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}|_{U},

where
J
is a set.

2.

A right
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is quasi-coherent if and only if for every point
x\in X
there are an open neighborhood
U
and an exact sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus I}\to\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}\to\mathcal{M}|_{U}\to 0.

in the category of right
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

Proof.

(1) Let
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}
and
x\in X
. Then there are an open neighborhood
U
of
x
, a set
J
and an surjective morphism

\pi\colon\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus J}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\uprho}|_{U}

in
\operatorname{Qcoh}X
. Since the functor
(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\mathcal{A}|_{U}\colon\operatorname{Qcoh}U\to\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathcal{A}|_{U}
has a right adjoint functor, it is right exact and commutes with small direct sums. Hence we have a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus J}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\mathcal{A}_{U}\cong\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}
and the morphism

\displaystyle\pi\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\mathcal{A}|_{U}\colon\ \mathcal{A}_{U}^{\oplus J}\to\mathcal{M}_{\uprho}|_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\mathcal{A}|_{U}

is surjective. Composing this surjection with a natural surjective morphism

\mathcal{M}_{\uprho}|_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}\mathcal{A}|_{U}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}|_{U}
;
m\otimes a\mapsto ma
, we have a surjective morphism
\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}|_{U}
.

(2) (
\Rightarrow
) Assume that a right
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is quasi-coherent and let
x\in X
be a point. Then by (1) there are an open neighbborhood
U
of
x
and a surjective
\mathcal{A}|_{U}
-linear map
p\colon\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}|_{U}
. Denote by
\mathcal{K}
the Kernel of
p
and by
i\colon\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}
the natural inclusion. Then
\mathcal{K}
is also a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module, and thus, by shrinking
U
if necessary, we have a surjective
\mathcal{A}|_{U}
-linear map
q\colon\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus I}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}|_{U}
. Then the sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus I}\xrightarrow{i\circ q}\mathcal{A}|_{U}^{\oplus J}\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal{M}|_{U}\to 0

is an exact.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume that a right
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is locally isomorphic to the cokernel of a morphism between free modules. Then, since
\mathcal{A}
is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module and
\mathcal{M}
is locally isomorphic to the cokernel of a morphism of quasi-coherent modules,
\mathcal{M}
is a locally quasi-coherent module. Thus
\mathcal{M}
is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
∎

2.3
Equivariant sheaves

We briefly recall the basics of equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves. For more details, see, for example,
[
3
, Section 2]
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group, and denote by
\varepsilon\colon\operatorname{Spec}k\to G
,
\mu\colon G\times G\to G
and
\iota\colon G\to G
the morphisms defining the identity, the multiplication and the inversion of
G
.
Let
X
be a scheme with an algebraic left
G
-action
\sigma\colon G\times X\to X
. We denote by
\pi\colon G\times X\to X
the natural projection, and we write

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\colon\ X\to G\times X

for the composition
X\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{Spec}k\times X\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\times\operatorname{id}_{X}}G\times X
.

Definition 2.12
.

A
G
-equivariant structure
on a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}X
is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\uptheta\colon\ \pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}

of
\mathcal{O}_{G\times X}
-modules such that the equations

\varphi^{*}\uptheta\circ(\operatorname{id}_{G}\times\pi)^{*}\uptheta=(\mu\times\operatorname{id}_{X})^{*}\uptheta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varepsilon_{X}^{*}\uptheta=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{F}}

(2.4)

hold, where
\varphi\colon G\times G\times X\to G\times X
is the morphism defined by
\varphi(g,h,x)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(h,gx)
.

Remark 2.13
.

For any closed point
g\in G
, there is the corresponding morphism
g\colon\operatorname{Spec}k\to G
, and this induces the morphism
g\times\operatorname{id}_{X}\colon X\to G\times X
. The pull-back of a
G
-equivariant structure
\uptheta\colon\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}
by
g\times\operatorname{id}_{X}
defines the isomorphism

\displaystyle\uptheta_{g}\colon\ \mathcal{F}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\sigma_{g}^{*}\mathcal{F},

where
\sigma_{g}\colon X\to X
is the isomorphism defined by
\sigma_{g}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sigma\circ(g\times\operatorname{id}_{X})
. The pull-back of the first equation in (
2.4
) by the morphism
g\times h\times\operatorname{id}_{X}\colon X\to G\times G\times X
implies the equation

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}^{*}\uptheta_{h}\circ\uptheta_{g}=\uptheta_{gh},

and the pull-back of the second equation is nothing but the equation
\uptheta_{1_{G}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

A
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaf
is a pair
(\mathcal{F},\uptheta)
of a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}X
and a
G
-equivariant structure
\uptheta
on
\mathcal{F}
. We say that
(\mathcal{F},\uptheta)
is
coherent
(resp.
locally free
) if
\mathcal{F}
is coherent (resp. locally free). If there is no risk of confusion, a
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaf
(\mathcal{F},\uptheta)
is denoted simply by
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
\big(\mathcal{F},\uptheta^{\mathcal{F}}\big)
and
\big(\mathcal{G},\uptheta^{\mathcal{G}}\big)
be
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
. A morphism
\varphi
:
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is said to be
G
-equivariant
if the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}\mathcal{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\uptheta^{\mathcal{F}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\uptheta^{\mathcal{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}\varphi}

(2.5)

is commutative.

Remark 2.14
.

Let
\big(\mathcal{F},\uptheta^{\mathcal{F}}\big)
and
\big(\mathcal{G},\uptheta^{\mathcal{G}}\big)
be
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
. Assume that
X
is of finite type over
k
. Then the set of closed points of
G\times X
is equal to the set of pairs
(x,g)
of closed points
x\in X
and
g\in G
. This implies that a morphism
\varphi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
of the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is
G
-equivariant if and only if for any closed point
g\in G
the pull-back

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{g}^{*}\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{g}^{*}\mathcal{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\uptheta^{\mathcal{F}}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\uptheta^{\mathcal{G}}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}_{g}\varphi}

of the diagram (
2.5
) by
g\times\operatorname{id}_{X}
is commutative.

G
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
and
G
-equivariant morphisms define the abelian category

\displaystyle\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X.

It is standard that the category
\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X
is equivalent to the category
\operatorname{Qcoh}[X/G]
of quasi-coherent sheaves on the quotient stack
[X/G]
;

\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\cong\operatorname{Qcoh}[X/G],

(2.6)

and for
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X
we denote by
[\mathcal{F}/G]\in\operatorname{Qcoh}[X/G]
the image of
\mathcal{F}
by the equivalence (
2.6
). We denote by
\operatorname{coh}_{G}X
the subcategory of
G
-equivariant coherent sheaves on
X
. This category is an exact subcategory, and if
X
is noetherian, it is an abelian subcategory. Furthermore, if
X
is of finite type over
k
, by Remark
2.14
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X}\big(\big(\mathcal{F},\uptheta^{\mathcal{F}}\big),\big(\mathcal{G},\uptheta^{\mathcal{G}}\big)\big)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})^{G},

(2.7)

where the right hand side
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})^{G}
is the
G
-invariant subset with respect to the
G
-action on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
defined by

g\cdot\varphi\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(\uptheta^{\mathcal{G}}_{g})^{-1}\circ\sigma_{g}^{*}\varphi\circ\uptheta^{\mathcal{F}}_{g}
.

Let
Y
be another
G
-scheme, and
f\colon X\to Y
a
G
-equivariant morphism that is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Then

induces the direct image

\displaystyle f_{*}\colon\ \operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}Y

and the inverse image

\displaystyle f^{*}\colon\ \operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}Y\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X.

It is standard that
f^{*}
is a left adjoint functor of
f_{*}
.

Tensor products and sheaf Hom define bi-functors

\displaystyle(-)\otimes_{X}(-)\colon\ \operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\times\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X,

\displaystyle\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}om}(-,-)\colon\ (\operatorname{coh}_{G}X)^{\rm op}\times\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X,

and it is also standard that for any
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{coh}_{G}X
, the functor
\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}om}(\mathcal{F},-)\colon\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X
is right adjoint to the functor
(-)\otimes_{X}\mathcal{F}\colon\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}X
.

2.4
Tilting objects and derived equivalences

We recall that tilting objects induce derived equivalences.
Throughout this subsection,
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category with small coproducts and enough injectives, and we assume that small coproducts of families of short exact sequences in
\mathcal{A}
are exact. These properties of
\mathcal{A}
are satisfied if
\mathcal{A}
is a Grothendieck category.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{A}
be an object. The object
T
is called a
tilting object
if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{i}(T,T)=0
for all
i>0
.

2.

T
is
compact
in
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
, i.e., the natural map

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})}(T,A_{i}^{\bullet})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})}\Big(T,\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}A_{i}^{\bullet}\Big)

is an isomorphism of abelian groups for any set
\mathcal{I}
and any family
\{A_{i}^{\bullet}\}_{i\in I}
.

3.

T
is a
generator
of
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
, i.e., for an object
A^{\bullet}\in\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
,

\operatorname{{\mathbb{R}}Hom}(T,A^{\bullet})\cong 0in\operatorname{D}\big(\operatorname{Mod}\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(T)\big)

implies
A^{\bullet}\cong 0
in
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
.

We call
T
a
partial tilting object
if the conditions (1) and (2) are satisfied.

For an object
T\in\mathcal{A}
, we denote by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Add}T

the smallest additive subcategory containing all direct summands of small coproducts of
T
. We write
\operatorname{add}T\subset\operatorname{Add}T
for the subcategory consisting of direct summands of finite coproducts of
T
. If
T
is a partial tilting object, then for arbitrary sets
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
, we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathcal{A}}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}T,\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}T\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}}T,\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(T[n])\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})}\Big(T,\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(T[n])\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Big(\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})}(T,T[n])\Big).

This implies that for any
X,Y\in\operatorname{Add}T
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(X,Y)=0
for any
n>0
. Thus any short exact sequence
0\to X\to Z\to Y\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
with
X,Y\in\operatorname{Add}T
splits, and in particular
\operatorname{Add}T
is an exact subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
.

In the remaining of this subsection,
T\in\mathcal{A}
is a partial tilting object, and we assume that every right module over the endomorphism ring
\Lambda\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(T)
is of finite projective dimension. Consider the functor

\displaystyle{\operatorname{\sf{F}}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(T,-)\colon\ \mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda

and assume that

(i)

\operatorname{\sf{F}}
has a left adjoint functor
\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\to\mathcal{A}
such that the adjunction morphisms
\sigma({\Lambda})\colon\Lambda\to\operatorname{\sf{F}}\operatorname{\sf{G}}(\Lambda)
and
\tau(T)\colon\operatorname{\sf{G}}\operatorname{\sf{F}}(T)\to T
are isomorphisms.

(ii)

The right derived functor
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
restricts to the functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\ \operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)

between bounded derived categories.

Note that both of the functors
\operatorname{\sf{F}}
and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
commute with small coproducts since
T
is compact and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
admits a right adjoint functor. In particular,
\operatorname{\sf{F}}
and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
restrict to the following functors

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\ \operatorname{Add}T\to\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\ \operatorname{Proj}\Lambda\to\operatorname{Add}T,

where
\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda
is the category of projective right
\Lambda
-modules.

Lemma 2.16
.

The functor
\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{Add}T\to\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda
is an equivalence, and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}\cong\operatorname{\sf{F}}^{-1}
.

Proof.

The adjunction morphisms
\sigma({P})\colon P\to\operatorname{\sf{F}}\operatorname{\sf{G}}(P)
and
\tau(S)\colon\operatorname{\sf{G}}\operatorname{\sf{F}}(S)\to S
are bijective for any
P\in\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda
and
S\in\operatorname{Add}T
, since
\sigma({\Lambda})
and
\tau({T})
are bijective and
\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda=\operatorname{Add}\Lambda
. Hence, the functors
\operatorname{\sf{F}}
and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
are equivalences, and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}\cong\operatorname{\sf{F}}^{-1}
.
∎

By our assumptions, the functor
\operatorname{\sf{F}}
defines the right derived functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\ \operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)

between bounded derived categories, and the functor
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
also defines the left derived functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\ \operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})

between bounded derived categories.
The following is well known to experts.

Theorem 2.17
.

The functor
\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})

is fully faithful. Furthermore, if
T
is a tilting object, then
\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}
is an equivalence and
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\cong(\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}})^{-1}
.

Proof.

We have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{K^{b}}(\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{K^{b}}(\operatorname{Add}T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\sf{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\,\,\,\,\,\,}

where the vertical arrows are the natural quotient functors. The left vertical functor is an equivalence, since every right
\Lambda
-module has a finite projective resolution. Moreover, by Lemma
2.16
, the top horizontal functor is also an equivalence. Hence, for the former assertion, it is enough to show that the natural functor

\displaystyle\operatorname{K^{b}}(\operatorname{Add}T)\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})

is fully faithful, and this follows from an identical argument as in
[
6
, Chapter III, Lemma 2.1]
.

For the latter assertion, assume that
T
is a tilting object. It is enough to prove that
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}
is also fully faithful. For any
A^{\bullet}\in\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
, consider the following triangle

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}(A^{\bullet})\xrightarrow{\tau}A^{\bullet}\to C(\tau)\to\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}(A^{\bullet})[1],

where
\tau
is the adjunction morphism. Applying the functor
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}
to the above triangle and using the natural isomorphism
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\circ\,\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\cong\operatorname{id}
, we see that
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}(C(\tau))\cong 0
in
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
. Since
T
is a generator of
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
, this implies that
C(\tau)\cong 0
in
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})
. Hence the adjunction morphism
\tau
is an isomorphism. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

Our assumption that every
\Lambda
-module has a finite projective resolution might be weakened. However, the result under our assumption is enough for our purpose.

3
Equivariant algebras and equivariant tilting modules

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)^{p}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E[p],F)^{0}
, we may assume that
p=0
. If
\varepsilon\colon F\to J^{\bullet}
is an injective resolution, then we have the induced injective resolutions
\varepsilon_{i}\colon F_{i}\to J^{\bullet}_{i}
for
i=0,1
.
Then the map
\varepsilon_{*}\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)^{0}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,J^{\bullet})^{0}
of cochain complexes is the direct sum of two maps

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{*})_{i}\colon\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(E_{i},F_{i})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(E_{i},J^{\bullet}_{i})\qquad(i=0,1)

of cochain complexes. The
i
-th cohomology of the cochain complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(E_{i},J^{\bullet}_{i})
is nothing but
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{A}}(E_{i},F_{i})
. Hence, by the assumption, the map
(\varepsilon_{*})_{i}
is a quasi-isomorphism, and so is
\varepsilon_{*}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{A}}(A,B)=0
for arbitrary objects

and all
i>0
. Then the natural functor
\operatorname{K}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
is fully faithful.

Proof.

Let
E,F\in\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
be objects. We need to show that the map

\displaystyle Q\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{{\rm K}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)

defined by the Verdier quotient
Q\colon{\rm K}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
is an isomorphism. By
[
2
, Proposition 2.19]
we can take an injective resolution
\iota\colon F\to(I^{\bullet},d_{I}^{\bullet})=\big(\cdots\to 0\to I^{0}\xrightarrow{d_{I}^{0}}I^{1}\xrightarrow{d_{I}^{1}}\cdots\big)
of
F
in
\operatorname{Ch}(\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w))
in the sense that all
I^{i}
has injective components. Then, we have the induced map

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tot}(\iota)\colon\ F\to\operatorname{Tot}(I^{\bullet})

in
\operatorname{Fact}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
which becomes an isomorphism in
\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
. Consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{{\rm K}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{{\rm K}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,\operatorname{Tot}(I^{\bullet}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,\operatorname{Tot}(I^{\bullet})).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tot}(\iota)_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Tot}(\iota)_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}

In the above diagram, the vertical arrow on the right hand side is an isomorphism, and the horizontal arrow on the bottom is also an isomorphism by
[
2
, Lemma 2.24]
and
[
12
, Proposition B.2(I)]
(see also
[
12
, Remark 2.14]
). Hence it suffices to show that the vertical arrow on the left hand side is an isomorphism, and for this we prove that the map

(4.1)

of cochain complexes is a quasi-isomorphism. The complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,F)
can be seen as the double complex
X^{\bullet,\bullet}
defined by

0,&q\neq 0.\end{cases}

On the other hand, we can consider the double complex

defined by

where the differentials

and

is the differential of the complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\sf{Fact}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}(E,I^{q})^{\bullet}
. Then the injective map

defining the resolution

gives the morphism

of double complexes. By definition, we have isomorphisms

and

, and the map

By Lemma

the map

, and therefore the map in (

) is also a quasi-isomorphism by

, Theorem 1.9.3]
(see also

). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be an object, and let

be an integer such that
F^{k}=0
for any

. Then there are a family
\{F^{\bullet}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of objects in
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})
with
F^{k}_{i}=0
for any
k<m
and
i
and an exact triangle

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}\to F^{\bullet}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}[1].

Proof.

Denote by
\tau_{\leq i}(F^{\bullet})
the canonical truncation of
F^{\bullet}
defined by
\tau_{\leq i}(F^{\bullet})^{j}=F^{j}
if
j<i
,
\tau_{\leq i}(F^{\bullet})^{i}=\operatorname{Ker}(d^{i}\colon F^{i}\to F^{i+1})
and
\tau_{\leq i}(F^{\bullet})^{j}=0
if

. If we set
F_{i}^{\bullet}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\tau_{\leq i}(F^{\bullet})
,
F_{i}^{\bullet}
lies in
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})
and
F^{k}_{i}=0
for any
k<m
and
i
. The object

is the cokernel of an injective morphism

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}

in the abelian category
\operatorname{Ch}(\mathcal{A})
given by

\operatorname{id}\oplus(-\iota_{i})\colon F^{\bullet}_{i}\to F^{\bullet}_{i}\oplus F^{\bullet}_{i+1}
, where
\iota_{i}\colon F^{\bullet}_{i}\to F^{\bullet}_{i+1}
is a natural injection. Hence we have a triangle

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}\to F^{\bullet}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{\bullet}_{i}[1]

in
\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Grothendieck category with enough projectives, and let
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be an additive functor such that it commutes with small direct sums and has a left adjoint functor
G\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
. The adjunction
G\dashv F
implies that
F
is left exact and
G
is right exact. By
[
10
, Proposition 14.3.4]
,
F
admits a right derived functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}F\colon\ \operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{B}),

which commutes with small direct sums, and
\mathbb{R}F
restricts to a functor
\mathbb{R}^{+}\!F\colon\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{B})
. On the other hand, by
[
10
, Theorem 14.4.3]
,
G
admits a left derived functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}G\colon\ \operatorname{D}(\mathcal{B})\to\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{A}),

and by
[
10
, Theorem 14.4.5]
the adjunction
G\dashv F
induces an adjunction

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}G\dashv\mathbb{R}F.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
\mathbb{R}^{+}\!F\colon\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{B})
restricts to the functor
\mathbb{R}F\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{B})
and that there exists a positive integer
d>0
such that for every object
B\in\mathcal{B}
the projective dimension of
B
is less than
d
.
Then, we have the following:

1.

The functor
\mathbb{L}G
restricts to a functor
\mathbb{L}^{+}G\colon\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{B})\to\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
.

2.

If
\mathbb{R}F\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{B})
is fully faithful, so is
\mathbb{R}^{+}\!F\colon\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

(1)
Let
B^{\bullet}\in\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{B})
be an object, and let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer such that
B^{k}=0
for any
k<m
. Then by Lemma
4.3
there are a family
\{B^{\bullet}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of objects in
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{B})
with
B_{i}^{k}=0
for any
k<m
and
i
and an exact sequence

\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{\bullet}_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{\bullet}_{i}\to B^{\bullet}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{\bullet}_{i}[1].

(4.3)

We set
C_{i}^{\bullet}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathbb{L}G(B^{\bullet}_{i})
. Then, by the assumption,
C_{i}^{k}=0
for any
k<m-d
and
i
, and so
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C_{i}^{\bullet}
lies in
\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
.
Since
\mathbb{L}G
admits a right adjoint functor, it commutes with small direct sums.
Applying the functor
\mathbb{L}G
to the triangle (
4.3
), we obtain a triangle

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{\bullet}_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{\bullet}_{i}\to\mathbb{L}G(B^{\bullet})\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{\bullet}_{i}[1],

which implies that
\mathbb{L}G(B^{\bullet})\in\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
.

(2) Since
\mathbb{R}^{+}\!F
admits a left adjoint functor
\mathbb{L}^{+}G
by (1), it is enough to show that the adjunction morphism

\displaystyle\varepsilon\colon\ \Upphi\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathbb{L}^{+}G\circ\mathbb{R}^{+}\!F\to\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})}

is an isomorphism of functors. Let
A^{\bullet}\in\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
be an object. Then by Lemma
4.3
there are a family

of objects in
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})
and an exact triangle

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}\xrightarrow{f}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}\xrightarrow{g}A^{\bullet}\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}[1].

Consider the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upphi\big(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upphi\big(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upphi(A^{\bullet})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A^{\bullet}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\bullet},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upphi(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Upphi(g)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where the horizontal sequences are triangles and the vertical arrows are induced by

. Since

commutes with direct sums, the vertical arrows on the left and middle are the direct sums of morphisms

is fully faithful and

. Hence the vertical arrow on the right hand side is also an isomorphism.
∎

Now we are ready to prove that a tilting object induces an equivalence of derived factorization categories. Let

be a partial tilting object such that the exact subcategory

is preserved by
\Phi
. Set

, and

be a potential on

. We define

\displaystyle\operatorname{D^{co}\hskip-0.28453ptMod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{D^{co}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda,\Psi,v),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda,\Psi,v).

Consider the functor

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sf{F}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(T,-)\colon\ \mathcal{A}\to\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda,

and assume that there is a left adjoint functor
\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda\to\mathcal{A}
, and that
\operatorname{\sf{F}}
and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
are factored with respect to

and

. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}

be an abelian subcategory preserved by
\Phi
such that
\operatorname{add}T\!\subset\mathcal{C}
,
\operatorname{\sf{F}}(\mathcal{C})\!\subset\operatorname{mod}\Lambda
and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)\subset\mathcal{C}
. Assume that the natural functors
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})
and
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
are fully faithful and that
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
restricts to
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{C})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)
. Then
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}\hskip-0.28453ptMod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)
restricts to the functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\ \operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v).

Moreover we assume that

is of finite global dimension. Then we have the left derived functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\ \operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\to\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)

that is left adjoint to
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that the adjunction morphisms
\Lambda\to\operatorname{\sf{F}}\operatorname{\sf{G}}(\Lambda)
and
\operatorname{\sf{G}}\operatorname{\sf{F}}(T)\to T
are isomorphisms.

1.

The functor
\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\to\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)
is fully faithful, and it factors through an equivalence

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{Kadd}(T,\Phi,w).

2.

Assume that every object in
\mathcal{A}
has finite injective resolution and that the right derived functor
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
is an equivalence. Then the functor

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\ \operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}\hskip-0.28453ptMod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)

is an equivalence, and it restricts to the equivalence

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\ \operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v).

Proof.

(1) Recall that the functor
\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\to\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)
is the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{K}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda,\Psi,v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Kadd}(T,\Phi,w)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\sf{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}

where the first equivalence follows from Proposition
2.7
and

is the natural quotient functor. Since we have the equivalence
\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\operatorname{proj}\Lambda\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{add}T
, the middle functor
\operatorname{\sf{G}}
is also an equivalence. Thus the statement follows from Lemma
4.2
and the assumption that the natural functor
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
is fully faithful.

(2) Since
(\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}})^{+}\colon\operatorname{D}^{+}(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
is fully faithful by Lemma
4.4
, the functor
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}
:
\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}\hskip-0.28453ptMod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)
is also fully faithful by
[
2
, Lemma 4.11]
. Consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{K}\operatorname{Add}(T,\Phi,w)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{K}\operatorname{Proj}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}}

is an equivalence, the top horizontal arrow is an equivalence. Moreover, the vertical arrow on the right hand side is essentially surjective by Proposition

. Hence

is essentially surjective, and so it is an equivalence.

Since the natural embedding functors
\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\to\operatorname{D^{co}}(\mathcal{A},\Phi,w)
and

are fully faithful, the functor
\mathbb{R}\operatorname{\sf{F}}\colon\!\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{C},\Phi,w)\!\to\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)
and its left adjoint
\mathbb{L}\operatorname{\sf{G}}\colon\!\operatorname{Dmod}(\Lambda,\Psi,v)\to\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{E},\Phi,w)
are fully faithful. Hence

∎

4.2

In this short subsection, we define (noncommutative) gauged Landau–Ginzburg models, and its derived factorization categories.

Definition 4.6
.

We call the data
(\mathcal{A},L,w)^{G}
a
gauged Landau–Ginzburg model
when
G
is an algebraic group acting on a scheme
X
,
\mathcal{A}
is a
G
-equivariant coherent
X
-algebra,
L
is a
G
-equivariant line bundle on
X
, and

is a
G
-invariant global section of
L
. A gauged Landau–Ginzburg model
(\mathcal{A},L,w)^{G}
is said to be
noncommutative
, if the algebra
\mathcal{A}
is not commutative. We write
(X,L,w)^{G}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(\mathcal{O}_{X},L,w)^{G}
, and for a character
\chi\colon G\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
of
G
we write
(\mathcal{A},\chi,w)^{G}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O}(\chi),w)^{G}
.

If
(\mathcal{A},L,w)^{G}
is a gauged LG model, we have the triple

\displaystyle\big(\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}\mathcal{A},L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-),w\big)

as in (
2.1
), where

is the tensor product with
L
, and
w\colon\operatorname{id}\to L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-)
is the functor morphism defined by the multiplication by
w
. Then we define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}(\mathcal{A},L,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Fact}\big(\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}\mathcal{A},L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-),w\big),

\displaystyle\operatorname{D^{co}\hskip-0.28453ptQcoh}_{G}(\mathcal{A},L,w)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{D^{co}}\big(\operatorname{Qcoh}_{G}\mathcal{A},L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-),w\big),

5
Linear sections of Pfaffian varieties

and noncommutative resolutions

In this section, we prove that derived categories of noncommutative resolutions of linear sections of Pfaffian varieties are equivalent to the derived factorization categories of noncommutative gauged LG models.

5.1
Noncommutative resolutions of Pfaffian varieties and its linear sections

Following
[
16
]
, we recall noncommutative resolutions of Pfaffian varieties and its linear sections.
Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
v
. For an integer
q
with
0\leq 2q\leq v
, we have a Pfaffian variety

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big\{x\in\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}\mid\operatorname{\sf rk}(x)\leq 2q\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}\big(\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}\big),

and we denote its affine cone by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}_{q}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}\subseteq\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}.

Then we have
\dim\operatorname{Pf}_{q}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}=q(2v-2q-1)
.
The Pfaffian variety
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}
is smooth if and only if
q=1
, where it defines a Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}(2,V)
, or
q=\lfloor v/2\rfloor
, where it defines the whole space
\mathbb{P}\big(\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}\big)
. In other cases, the singular locus is the subvairiety
\operatorname{Pf}_{(q-1)}\subset\operatorname{Pf}_{q}
. Let
L\subset\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}V^{*}
be a subspace of codimension
c
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\cap\,\,\mathbb{P}(L)\neq\varnothing

and
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}
has the expected dimension
\dim\operatorname{Pf}_{q}-c
. If
c>\dim\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{Pf}_{q})=(q-1)(2v-2q+1)-1
, we can take a generic
L
so that
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}
is smooth. If
c
is smaller than the bounds, then
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}
is never smooth, and in this case the usual bounded derived category
\operatorname{D^{b}}\big(\operatorname{coh}\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}\big)
does not behave well (for example, in the context of homological projective duality
[
16
]
).

Let
(Q,\omega)
be a symplectic vector space of dimension
2q
with a symplectic form
\omega\in\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\tiny\!$2$\,}}$}Q^{\ast}
. Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sp}(Q)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{f\in\operatorname{GL}(Q)\mid f^{*}\omega=\omega\}

be the symplectic group of
Q
, and

\displaystyle\operatorname{GSp}(Q)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{f\in\operatorname{GL}(Q)\mid\exists\,t\in k^{*}\mbox{ such that }f^{*}\omega=t\,\omega\}

the symplectic similitude group of
Q
.
Then we have a short exact sequence

1\to\operatorname{Sp}(Q)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(Q)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 1

(5.1)

that induces a semi-direct product
\operatorname{GSp}(Q)=\operatorname{Sp}(Q)\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\operatorname{GSp}(Q)
is the diagonal subgroup. Let us set

\displaystyle Y\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(V,Q)

and consider the following quotient stacks

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=[Y/\operatorname{Sp}(Q)]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{Y}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=[Y/\operatorname{GSp}(Q)].

Then the surjective morphism

\displaystyle p\colon\quad Y\to\operatorname{Pf}_{q}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}},\qquad\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{*}\omega

induces the morphisms

\displaystyle\pi^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}\colon\ \mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}\to\operatorname{Pf}_{q}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi\colon\ \mathcal{Y}\to\big[\operatorname{Pf}_{q}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\big],

and if we set
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{ss}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left[\left(Y\backslash\,p^{-1}(0)\right)/\operatorname{GSp}(Q)\right]
, the morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\big[\operatorname{Pf}_{q}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\big]
restricts to a (stacky) resolution

\displaystyle\pi\colon\ \mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{ss}}\to\operatorname{Pf}_{q}

of the Pfaffian variety
\operatorname{Pf}_{q}
.

Recall that irreducible representations of
\operatorname{Sp}(Q)
are indexed by Young diagrams of height at most
q
. We denote by
Y_{q,s}
the set of Young diagrams of height at most
q
and width at most
s\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\lfloor v/2\rfloor-q

are nothing but

\operatorname{Sp}(Q)
defines a vector bundle
on
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{\sf aff}}

whose pull-back to

by the natural projection

is the vector bundle associated to

(see
[
16
, Section 2.4]
). Then the vector bundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}:=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in Y_{q,s}}\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\in\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{Y}

is a partial tilting bundle on
\mathcal{Y}
. Restricting
\mathcal{V}
to the open substack
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{ss}}
we obtain a partial tilting bundle

\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{ss}}
on
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{ss}}
, and the
{\operatorname{Pf}_{q}}
-algebra

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\pi_{*}\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}}nd}_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{ss}}}(\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{ss}})

by
[
18

.
By the noncommutative Bertini theorem

17

, for a generic
L

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{L}:=\mathcal{B}|_{\operatorname{Pf}_{q}\!|_{L}}

is a noncommutative resolution of the linear section

Proposition A.10
.

The functor
[(-)/_{p}R]\colon\operatorname{Qcoh}\big(\mathcal{A},\uptheta^{\mathcal{A}}\big)\to\operatorname{Qcoh}[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]
is an equivalence.

Proof.

This follows from a similar argument as in the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 13.1; 06WT]
. We construct a quasi-inverse
of the functor
[(-)/_{p}R]
as follows.

First, for a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
[U/_{p}R]_{\operatorname{fppf}}
, we define a sheaf
\mathcal{F}^{R}
on the small étale site
U_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{R}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big(\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{F}\big)|_{U_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}},

where
\alpha\colon(\operatorname{Sch\!}/U)\to[U/_{p}R]
is the morphism in (
A.3
). By construction we have
\mathcal{O}_{[U/_{p}R]}^{R}=\mathcal{O}_{U_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}}
. For each étale morphism
(u\colon T\to U)\in U_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
, we have

\displaystyle[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]^{R}(u\colon T\to U)=\Gamma(T,u^{*}\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}(u\colon T\to U).

Thus we have a natural identification
[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]^{R}=\mathcal{A}
, and so if
\mathcal{M}
is a quasi-coherent right
[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]
-module, the small étale sheaf
\mathcal{M}^{R}
is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module.

Next, we define a category
\{U/_{p}R\}
containing the category
[U/_{p}R]
by replacing
S
-schemes
T
in the definition of
[U/_{p}R]
with algebraic spaces over
S
. If
(f,\varphi)\colon(W,u)\to(W^{\prime},u^{\prime})
is a morphism in
\{U/_{p}R\}
, this morphism induces a natural (functorial) commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[U/_{p}R\right].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\circ u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\circ u^{\prime}}

Then, for any quasi-coherent right
[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]
-module
\mathcal{M}
, we have the composition

\displaystyle(\alpha\circ u^{\prime})^{-1}\mathcal{M}\to f_{*}f^{-1}\big((\alpha\circ u^{\prime})^{-1}\mathcal{M}\big)\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,f_{*}(\alpha\circ u)^{-1}\mathcal{M},

where the first morphism is the adjunction morphism and the second isomorphism follows from the above commutative diagram. By the adjunction
f^{-1}\dashv f_{*}
, this composition map induces the morphism

f^{-1}(\alpha\circ u^{\prime})^{-1}\mathcal{M}\to(\alpha\circ u)^{-1}\mathcal{M}

and restricting this morphism to
W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
, we have the comparison map

\displaystyle c_{(f,\varphi)}\colon\ f^{*}u^{\prime*}\mathcal{M}^{R}\to u^{*}\mathcal{M}^{R}

and by
[
19
, Lemma 11.6; 06WK]
this is an isomorphism.

Finally, we define a functor
F\colon\operatorname{Qcoh}[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]\to\operatorname{Qcoh}\big(\mathcal{A},\uptheta^{\mathcal{A}}\big)
by

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{M})\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\big(\mathcal{M}^{R},\uptheta^{\mathcal{M}^{R}}\big),

where
\uptheta^{\mathcal{M}^{R}}\colon s^{*}\mathcal{M}^{R}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,t^{*}\mathcal{M}
is defined to be the inverse of the comparison map

\displaystyle c_{(\operatorname{id}_{R},\operatorname{id}_{R})}\colon\ t^{*}\mathcal{M}^{R}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,s^{*}\mathcal{M}^{R}

induced by the morphism
(\operatorname{id}_{R},\operatorname{id}_{R})\colon(R,s)\to(R,t)
in
\{U/_{p}R\}
. By construction,
F
is a quasi-inverse of the functor
[(-)/_{p}R]
.
∎

\displaystyle[\mathcal{A}/R]\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\widetilde{[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]}

for the sheaf of rings on the quotient stack
[U/R]
corresponding to
[\mathcal{A}/_{p}R]

A.1
). By Lemma
A.4
and Proposition
A.10

19
, Proposition 13.1; 06WT]
.

Proposition A.11

We have an equivalence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Qcoh}\big(\mathcal{A},\uptheta^{\mathcal{A}}\big)\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{Qcoh}[\mathcal{A}/R].

Abstract
. We consider time-dependent space isotropic and time stationary spherical Gaussian random fields. We establish Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm and solve the small probabilities problem. Our results depend on the high-frequency behaviour of the angular power spectrum: the speed of decay of the small ball probability is faster as either the memory parameter or the space-parameter decreases.

We say that a real-valued random field
\{X_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
satisfies Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm (LIL) at
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with rate function
\phi
if there exists a constant
C
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)\max_{t\in B_{r}(t_{0})}|X_{t}-X_{t_{0}}|=C\;\;\;a.s.

where
B_{r}(t_{0})
is an appropriate ball of radius
r>0
centered at
t_{0}
. When
X_{t}
is a Brownian motion and
t_{0}=0
, it was proven in a famous paper by K.-L. Chung in 1948
[
11
]
that (
1.1
) holds with
\phi(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\log|\log r|}{r}}
and
C=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{8}}
.

Chung’s LIL has many applications and we refer to the survey
[
30
]
for more details. In this paper we are mostly interested in connections to small probabilities. The small ball problem (SBP) consists in finding a function
\theta
and a constant
c
such that

(1.2)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\theta(\varepsilon)\log\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{t\in B_{r}(t_{0})}|X_{t}-X_{t_{0}}|<\varepsilon\right)=c.

The small ball problem and Chung’s LIL for stochastic processes have been extensively studied, and the literature on the subject is vast. In
[
3
]
the authors considered the case of a one-dimensional Brownian motion and Hölder norms, in
[
20
]
a Brownian sheet in Hölder norms has been considered,
[
19
]
studied the integrated Brownian motion in the uniform norm, and
[
10
]
the
m

-norm, and in
[
7
]
the
n
-step Kolmogorov diffusion. In
[
41
]
and
[
8
]
a small deviation problem and Chung’s law of iterated logarithm is proven for a class of stochastic integrals and for the hypoelliptic Brownian motion on the Heisenberg group respectively.

As far as random fields
\{X_{t},t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

In the current paper we consider a time-dependent spherical Gaussian random field
\{T(x,t),\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
which is stationary in time and isotropic in space. Our main results are collected in Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
2.8
. The former provides the rate function for Chung’s LIL, and the latter describes the small ball probability asymptotics. Both the rate function in (
1.1
) and the asymptotics in (
1.2
) depend on the high frequency behaviour of the angular power spectrum of the field
\{T(x,t),\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
. The asymptotic behaviour of the spectral density depends on a space-parameter
\alpha
and a time-parameter
\beta
. The former describes the high-energy behaviour of the field and the latter can be thought as a ”memory” parameter. The small ball probability dacays to zero like
\exp\left(\frac{-c}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{4}{\alpha-2}}}\right)
, for some positive constant
c
. In particular, for a fixed
\alpha
, the smaller the time-memory parameter
\beta
the faster the speed of the decay. We refer to (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) for a more explicit rate and asymptotics function.

Let us now explain the strategy of the proof. Generally, the first step is to show that the LHS of (
1.1
) is measurable with respect to a tail
\sigma

2.8
to prove that the LHS of (
1.1
) is constant. More precisely, we first show that is almost surely positive and finite. Then by means of stationarity and isotropy of the field we are able to connect (
1.1
) to (
1.2

C
in (
1.1
) is constant by giving an explicit expression in terms of

in (

2.1.
Upper and lower bounds for Gaussian random fields

Small ball probabilities of random fields have been extensively studied, see
[
40
,
28
,
49
,
47
]
and references therein. In this sections we recall results from
[
43
]
that we will use throughout the paper. Let
\{X_{t},\,t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
be a real-valued random field, and
d_{X}
be its canonical metric, that is,

(2.1)

d_{X}(t,s):=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{t}-X_{s})^{2}\right]}.

One of the main ingredients in our proofs is Talagrand’s bound on small ball probabilities for Gaussian processes.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
43
, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2]

Let
\{X_{t},\,t\in K\}
be a separable, real-valued, mean zero Gaussian process indexed by a compact set
K
with canonical metric
d_{X}
. Let
N(K,d_{X},\varepsilon)
be the smallest number of
d_{X}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover the set
K
. Suppose that there is a function
\Psi:(0,\delta)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that
N(K,d_{X},\varepsilon)\leqslant\Psi(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
and there are constants
1<a_{1}\leqslant a_{2}
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{1}\Psi(\varepsilon)\leqslant\Psi\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\leqslant a_{2}\Psi(\varepsilon).

Then, there is a finite constant
k
depending only on
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
such that for all
u\in(0,\delta)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s,t\in K}|X_{t}-X_{s}|\leqslant u\right)\geqslant\exp\left(-k\Psi(u)\right).

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
c
such that for any

(2.4)

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{s,t\in K}|X_{s}-X_{t}|\geqslant c\left(u+\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N(K,d_{X},\varepsilon)}d\varepsilon\right)\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{D^{2}}\right),

where

.

2.2.
Time-dependent spherical random fields

Let
\mathbb{S}^{2}
be the unit 2-dimensional sphere and
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
defined as

\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\bigg\{\sin\theta\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\bigg\}+\dfrac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}},\mbox{ }0\leq\theta\leq\pi,\mbox{ }0\leq\varphi<2\pi.

It is well-known that the spectrum of
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
is purely discrete, its eigenvalues are of the form
-\lambda_{\ell}=-\ell(\ell+1)
where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and the eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{\ell}
is the
(2\ell+1)
-dimensional space
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}:=\text{span}\{Y_{\ell m}(\cdot):m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
of degree-
\ell
spherical harmonics. Note that
\{\mathcal{S}_{\ell},\,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},dx)-
orthonormal basis, where
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In particular, the functions
Y_{\ell m}
satisfy

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{\ell m}+\lambda_{\ell}Y_{\ell m}=0,

and the addition and duplication formulae

(2.5)

\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{\ell m}(x){Y}_{\ell m}(y)=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle)\text{ ,}

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle x,z\right\rangle)\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle z,y\right\rangle)dz=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle)\text{ ,}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2},
and
P_{\ell}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, denotes the Legendre polynomial of degree
\ell
:

P_{\ell}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}\ell!}\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}(t^{2}-1)^{\ell}\quad\ell=1,2,\dots

and
P_{\ell}(1)=1.
We refer to
[
36
, Eq. (3.42) and Sec. 13.1.2]
for more details.

A space-time real-valued spherical random field
T
is a collection of random variables
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\}
defined on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
T:\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
-measurable map, where
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
denotes the Borel sigma-algebra of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
. We are interested in a space-time real-valued spherical random field
T
that is

(1) Gaussian, i.e. its finite-dimensional distributions are Gaussian;

(2) centered, i.e.
\mathbb{E}\left[T(x,t)\right]=0
for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
;

(3) isotropic in space and stationary in time, that is,
T(x,t)
and
T(g.x,\tau+t)
have the same distribution for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,
g\in SO(3)
, and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
;

(4) mean-square continuous.

Condition (3) implies that, for every
(x,t),(y,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{E}\left[T(x,t)T(y,s)\right]=\Gamma(\langle x,y\rangle,t-s),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
\Gamma:[-1,1]\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a positive-semidefinite function. Under the above conditions, it is well known that
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]

[
35
, Theorem 3]

\Gamma
can be written as a uniformly convergent series

(2.7)

\Gamma(\eta,\tau)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(\tau)P_{\ell}(\eta),\quad(\eta,\tau)\in[-1,1]\times\mathbb{R},

where
\{C_{\ell}(\cdot)\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of continuous positive semi-definite functions on
\mathbb{R}
, and
\{P_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
denotes the Legendre polynomial. Let

\displaystyle a_{\ell m}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}T(x,t)Y_{\ell m}(x)dx,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
Y_{\ell m}
denotes the
(\ell,m)-
th spherical harmonic. By our assumptions it follows that
\{a_{\ell m},\,m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
is a family of independent, stationary, centered Gaussian processes on
\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.8)

E[a_{\ell m}(t)a_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(s)]=C_{\ell}(t-s)\delta_{\ell}^{\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{m}^{m^{\prime}}.

From the spectral representation for
\Gamma
one can deduce the following Karhunen–Loève expansion

(2.9)

T(x,t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}(t)Y_{\ell m}(x),

where the series converges in
L^{2}\left(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\right)
for any
T>0
.

Throughout the paper we will assume the following.

Assumption I
There exists
0<\beta<2
such that the family of functions
\{C_{\ell}(\cdot)\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
satisfies

(2.10)

C_{\ell}(\tau)=C_{\ell}(0)(1-|\tau|^{\beta}),

for all
|\tau|<1
. Moreover, there exists an
\alpha
with
2+\beta<\alpha<4
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle C_{\ell}(0)=G(\ell)\ell^{-\alpha}>0,\;\;\ell=1,2,\ldots,,

(2.12)

\displaystyle C_{0}(0)=c_{1},

and we assume that

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-1}\leqslant G(\ell)\leqslant c_{0},

for some finite constants
c_{0}\geqslant 1
,
c_{1}>0
.

Remark 2.2
.

The isotropic Gaussian spherical random field (not time-dependent)
\{Z(x),\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\}
with zero mean, can be represented by

(2.13)

\displaystyle Z(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{C_{\ell}}T_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{C_{\ell}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(2.14)

\displaystyle Z_{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

where the random variables
\{a_{\ell m},m=-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
are standard Gaussian and independent, the sequence
\{C_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0,1,\dots}
is called angular power spectrum of
Z(x)
and it is non-negative and such that
\sum_{\ell}C_{\ell}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}=\mathbb{E}Z^{2}<\infty
(see
[
36
,
37
]
). The series (
2.13
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
, and in
L^{2}(\Omega)
at every fixed
x
.

The Assumption I on the power spectrum translates here simply as the following.

Condition (A):

The angular power spectrum
C_{\ell}
is such that

(2.15)

\displaystyle C_{\ell}=G(\ell)\ell^{-\alpha}>0,\;\;\ell=1,2,\ldots,

\displaystyle C_{0}(0)=c_{1},

where
\alpha>2
is a constant, and we assume that

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-1}\leqslant G(\ell)\leqslant c_{0},

for some finite constant
c_{0}\geqslant 1
,
c_{1}>0
.

We recall that (
2.15
) has been used in
[
23
]
and it does not require stronger regularity assumptions such as the ones used in
[

,
22
,
16
,
42

among others. Moreover, Condition (A) also cover the Matérn class of covariance functions
[

, which is the prototype of covariance models in the applications. The Condition (

) with

is suggested by many models for data analysis of the Cosmic Microwave Background radiation
[

,

. For the time-dependent case, the restriction

will be needed in Proposition

Remark 2.3
.

We remark that there is a fractal behaviour for
2<\alpha<4
. For these values of
\alpha
the modulus of continuity decays slower than linearly with respect to the angular distance, and hence making the sample function of
Z(x)
non-differentiable, see
[
23
, Theorem 2]
. Many applied fields do satisfy
2<\alpha<4
. For example, the value of
\alpha
for Cosmic Microwave Background data is known to be very close to
2
, see
[
13
]
. For this reason, the estimate (
2.21
) is not believed to hold true for
\alpha>4
, but it is conjectured for the critical case
\alpha=4
, and we refer to
[
23
]
for more details.

Let us consider the function
\mu_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
given by

(2.16)

\mu^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}((x,t),(y,s)):=|t-s|^{\beta}+(1-|t-s|^{\beta})\rho_{\alpha}^{2}(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)),

where

(2.17)

\rho_{\alpha}(t):=t^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}.

In
[
23
]
it was shown that the function
\rho_{\alpha}
is equivalent to the canonical metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
induced by a spherical random field satisfying (
2.15
) with
2<\alpha<4
. This function was introduced in
[
23
]
to prove the strong non-local determinism for a spherical Gaussian random field
Z
on the sphere. More precisely, if (
2.15
) holds for some
2<\alpha<4
, then there exist two positive and finite constants
C
and
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for all integers
n\geqslant 1
, and all
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
we have that

(2.18)

\text{Var}\left(Z(x_{0})\,|\,Z(x_{1}),\ldots,Z(x_{n})\right)\geqslant C\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\rho_{\alpha}\left(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{0},x_{k})\right)^{2},

where
\rho_{\alpha}
is given by (
2.17
).

One of our main ingredients is Proposition

, where we prove a strong non-local determinism similar to (
2.21
) for the time-space random field

. We end this section by showing that the function

is equivalent to the canonical distance on

induced by

.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\}
be a Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then there exist constants
1\leqslant c_{\alpha}<\infty
and
0<\varepsilon<1
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)<\varepsilon
and all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
|t-s|<1
one has that

(2.19)

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}^{-1}\mu^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}((x,t),(y,s))\leqslant d_{T}^{2}((x,t),(y,s))\leqslant c_{\alpha}\mu^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}((x,t),(y,s)).

Proof.

By (
2.10
) we have that

\displaystyle d^{2}_{T}((x,t),(y,s))=\mathbb{E}\left[T(x,t)^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[T(y,s)^{2}\right]-2\mathbb{E}\left[T(x,t)T(y,s)\right]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(0)-2\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(t-s)P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}(C_{\ell}(0)-C_{\ell}(t-s)P_{\ell}(\cos\theta))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}\left(C_{\ell}(0)-C_{\ell}(t-s)\right)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}C_{\ell}(t-s)\left(1-P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=|t-s|^{\beta}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}C_{\ell}(0)+(1-|t-s|^{\beta})\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{2\pi}C_{\ell}(0)\left(1-P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\right),

and the result then follows by
[
23
, Lemma 4]
.
∎

2.3.
Main Results

We want to study the asymptotic behaviour of the maximum of a Gaussian random field
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\}
in a small time-space regime. Thus, we assume
(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]
, for some fixed
T>0
. On the product metric space
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]
one has the natural
q
-product metrics given by

(2.20)

\displaystyle d_{q}((x,t),(y,s)):=\left(|t-s|^{q}+d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for any
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
. These metrics are all equivalent and induce the same topology. It is easy to see that our results are independent of the choice of
q
, and for this reason we fix
q=2
. Let

B_{r}(x,t):=\left\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\,:\,d_{2}((x,t),(y,s))<r\right\}.

First, let us state an auxiliary result: the strong non-local determinism for space-time real-valued spherical random fields, whose proof can be found in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then there exist two positive and finite constants
C
and
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for all integers
n\geqslant 1
, and all
(x_{0},t_{0}),\ldots,(x_{n},t_{n})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]
with
\min_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}d_{2}\left((x_{0},t_{0}),(x_{k},t_{k})\right)\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, we have that

(2.21)

\text{Var}\left(T(x_{0},t_{0})\,|\,T(x_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,T(x_{n},t_{n})\right)\geqslant C\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\rho_{\alpha}^{2}(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,x_{k}))).

Let us introduce the following notation, and state our main results.

Notation 2.6
.

For any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]
, and
r>0
let us set

\displaystyle M_{r}(x,t):=\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|,

where
B_{r}(x,t)
is defined above. Moreover, for any
r>0
and
\varepsilon>0
we define the following functions

(2.22)

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(r):=\left(\frac{\log|\log r|}{r^{3}}\right)^{p},

(2.23)

\displaystyle\psi(r,\varepsilon)=\psi_{\alpha,\beta}(r,\varepsilon):=r^{3}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{p}},

where

(2.24)

p=p(\alpha,\beta):=\left(\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{4}{\alpha-2}\right)^{-1}.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a time-spherical Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then there exists a finite positive constant
\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}
such that, for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]

(2.25)

\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)M_{r}(x,t)=\kappa_{\alpha,\beta},\;\;\text{a.s.}

where
\phi=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}
is given by (
2.22
).

As an application of Theorem
2.7
we solve the small ball problem for the random field
T(x,t)
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I, and
\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}
be given by (
2.25
). Then, for every
r>0

(2.26)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)=r^{3}\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}^{\frac{1}{p}},

We remark that the constant

As we also mentioned for the time-dependent spherical Gaussian field, a key tool to prove Theorem

3.
Preliminary results

We first prove a weaker version of Theorem

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)>0,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)<\infty.

This is the content of Proposition

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a time-spherical Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then there exists a positive finite constant

only depending on
\alpha
and
\beta

and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-A_{1}\psi(r,\varepsilon)\right),

where
\psi=\psi_{\alpha,\beta}
is given by (
2.23
).

Proof.

The proof is based on the strong non-local determinism for the field
T(x,t)
Propositon
2.5
, and on a conditioning argument similar to
[
28
, Proposition 4.2]
. Let us recall that

B_{r}(x,t):=\left\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\,:\,d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)^{2}+|t-s|^{2}<r^{2}\right\}.

Let us consider the sets

\displaystyle B_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(x):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\,:\,d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y)<\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}\right\},

\displaystyle I_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(t):=\left[0,t+\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

and note that
B_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(x)\times I_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(t)\subset B_{r}(x,t)
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{y\in B_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(x)}\max_{s\in I_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(t)}\left|T(y,s)-T(x,t)\right|<\varepsilon\right).

Let
F:A_{r}\rightarrow B_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(x)
be a local diffeomorphism, where
A_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a rectangle and
F(0,0)=x
, and for
r
small enough such that
[0,r]\times[0,r]\subset A_{r}
. We can choose
F
in such a way that
d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{i},x_{j})\geqslant d_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\theta_{i},\varphi_{i}),(\theta_{j},\varphi_{j})\right)
.

Let
G:[0,r]\rightarrow[0,t+r/\penalty\sqrt{2}]
be the bijection given by
G(\eta)=(t/\penalty r+1/\penalty\sqrt{2})\eta
so that
G(0)=0
and
G(r)=t+r/\penalty\sqrt{2}
. We then have a local diffeomorphism

H:[0,r]\times[0,r]\times[0,r]\longrightarrow B_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(x)\times I_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(t).

Let us divide
[0,r]\times[0,r]
into sub-rectangles of side-length

\frac{r}{\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2p}}}\bigg\rfloor+1},

and
[0,r]
into sub-rectangles of side-length
r/\penalty(\lfloor r\rfloor+1)
. We then get a division of
[0,r]\times[0,r]\times[0,r]
into
N
sub-rectangles, where

N=\left(\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2p}}}\bigg\rfloor+1\right)^{2}(\lfloor r\rfloor+1)\geqslant\frac{r^{3}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Note that
N>1
since
r>\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3p}}
. Now let
(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,N
denote the upper right-most vertex in the subdivision of
[0,r]\times[0,r]
, and
\xi_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
be the right-most endpoint in the subdivision of
[0,r]
, and set
p_{i}:=(x_{i},t_{i})
, where
x_{i}:=F(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})
,
t_{i}:=G(\xi_{i})
. Note that
p_{i}\in B_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(x)\times I_{\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}}(t)\subset B_{r}(x,t)
, and by construction we have that

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\theta_{i},\varphi_{i}),(\theta_{j},\varphi_{j})\right)\geqslant c\,\frac{r}{\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2p}}}\bigg\rfloor+1}\quad\text{ for all }i,j=1,\ldots,N

for some constant
c>0
. Set

\displaystyle A_{j}:=\left\{\max_{i=1,\ldots,j}|T(p_{i})-T(x,t)|<\varepsilon\right\},

and write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{j})=\mathbb{E}\left[1_{A_{j-1}}\,\mathbb{P}\left(|T(p_{i})-T(x,t)|<\varepsilon\,\left.\right|T(p_{i})\,:\,i=0,\ldots,j-1\right)\right].

By Proposition
2.5
we have that

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left(T(p_{j})\,|\,T(p_{i})\,:\,i=0,\ldots,j-1\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{2}\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j-1}\rho^{2}_{\alpha}(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{j},x_{i}))=c_{2}\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j-1}d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x_{j},x_{i})^{\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{2}\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j-1}d_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\theta_{j},\varphi_{j}),(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})\right)^{\alpha-2}

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{2}c^{\alpha-2}\left(\frac{r}{\bigg\lfloor\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2p}}}\bigg\rfloor+1}\right)^{\alpha-2}=:\gamma.

By the estimate
x\leqslant\lfloor x\rfloor+1\leqslant 2x
, for
x\geqslant 1
, it follows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle c_{2}\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^{\alpha-2}\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2p}}}\leqslant c_{2}c^{\alpha-2}.

The conditional distribution of
T(p_{j})
given all the
T(p_{i})
is Gaussian with conditional variance
\text{Var}\left(T(p_{j})\,|\,T(p_{i})\,:\,i=0,\ldots,j-1\right)
since the field
T(p)
is Gaussian. Then by the above lower bound on the conditional variance and Anderson’s inequality
[
1
]
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|T(p_{j})-T(x,t)|<\varepsilon\,\left.\right|T(p_{i})\,:\,i=0,\ldots,j-1\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(|Z|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2p}}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)\leqslant\exp(-C),

where

is a standard Gaussian random variable and the last inequality holds for some constant

thanks to (

). Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{N})\leqslant\exp(-C)\mathbb{P}(A_{N-1})\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\exp(-NC),

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}|T(p_{i})-T(x,t))|<\varepsilon\right)=\mathbb{P}(A_{N})

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp(-NC)\leqslant\exp\left(-C\,\frac{r^{3}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}}\right).

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a time-spherical Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then there exist a positive finite constant
A_{2}=A_{2}(\alpha,\beta)
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
we have that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(M_{r}(x,t)<\varepsilon\right)\geqslant\exp\left(-A_{2}\psi(r,\varepsilon)\right),

where
\psi=\psi_{\alpha,\beta}
is given by (
2.23
).

Proof.

Let
N(B_{r}(x,t),d_{T},\varepsilon)
be the smallest number of
d_{T}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover
B_{r}(x,t)
. By Proposition
2.4
we know that the canonical metric
d_{T}
is equivalent to
\mu_{\alpha,\beta}
, and hence it is enough to consider
N(B_{r}(x,t),\mu_{\alpha,\beta},\varepsilon)
. The
\mu_{\alpha,\beta}
-ball of radius
\varepsilon
is given by

\displaystyle B^{\mu_{\alpha,\beta}}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\left\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\,:\,\mu_{\alpha,\beta}((x,t),(y,s))<\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\,:\,|t-s|^{\beta}+(1-|t-s|^{\beta})\rho_{\alpha}^{2}(d_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,y))<\varepsilon^{2}\right\}.

Due to isotropy and stationary of the field, we can assume
t=0
and
x=N
, the north pole. Then
d(N,y)=\theta
and in spherical coordinates we get

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(B^{\mu_{\alpha,\beta}}_{\varepsilon}(N,0))=\int_{B^{\mu_{\alpha,\beta}}_{\varepsilon}(N,0)}dyds=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\int_{\left\{|s|^{\beta}\left(1-\theta^{\alpha-2}\right)+\theta^{\alpha-2}<\varepsilon^{2}\right\}}\sin\theta d\theta ds.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\{|s|^{\beta}\left(1-\theta^{\alpha-2}\right)+\theta^{\alpha-2}<\varepsilon^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(s,\theta)\,:\,0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}\,,\,|s|<\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-\theta^{\alpha-2}}{1-\theta^{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\right\}\cup\left\{(s,\theta)\,:\,1<\theta<\pi\,,\,\left(\frac{\theta^{\alpha-2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{\theta^{\alpha-2}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}<s<T\right\},

and since we are interested in the asymptotics near
(x,t)=(N,0)
, we can rule oiut the second set since
d(N,y)=\theta>1
. We then have that

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(B^{\mu_{\alpha,\beta}}_{\varepsilon}(N,0))=2\pi\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}}\int_{-\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-\theta^{\alpha-2}}{1-\theta^{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}^{\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-\theta^{\alpha-2}}{1-\theta^{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}\sin\theta dsd\theta

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}}2\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-\theta^{\alpha-2}}{1-\theta^{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\sin\theta d\theta=4\pi\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1-u^{\alpha-2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}u^{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\sin\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}u\right)du

\displaystyle=4\pi\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{4}{\alpha-2}}\int_{0}^{1}u\left(\frac{1-u^{\alpha-2}}{1-\varepsilon^{2}u^{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\frac{\sin\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}u\right)}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}u}du=:\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}F_{\alpha,\beta}(\varepsilon),

where the function
F_{\alpha,\beta}(\varepsilon)
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
above and below by a positive constant. Similarly, one can show that

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(B_{r}(N,0))=2\pi r^{3}\int_{0}^{1}u(1-ru)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\sin(ru)}{ru}du=:r^{3}H(r),

where the function
H(r)
is bounded uniformly in
r
above and below by a positive constant. Thus,

(3.4)

\displaystyle N(B_{r}(N,0),\mu_{\alpha,\beta},\varepsilon)\leqslant\frac{\text{Vol}\left(B_{r}(N,0)\right)}{\text{Vol}(B^{\mu_{\alpha,\beta}}_{\varepsilon}(N,0))}=\frac{r^{3}H(r)}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}F_{\alpha,\beta}(\varepsilon)},

and hence we can take

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha,\beta}(r,\varepsilon):=\frac{r^{3}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}},

which satisfied the assumption of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The proof of Proposition

does not rely on the strong non-local determinism property from Proposition

as well.

4.
Chung’s law of the iterated logarithm

The goal of this section is to show that the random variable

\displaystyle\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}:=\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)M_{r}(x,t),

where

\displaystyle M_{r}(x,t):=\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|,

satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(0<\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty\right)=1.

In the next section we will show that
\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}
is constant a.s. as a byproduct of Theorem
2.8
. Let us recall that

\displaystyle p=p(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{4}{\alpha-2}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(r):=\left(\frac{\log|\log r|}{r^{3}}\right)^{p}.

Firs, let us prove that
\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}>0
a.s.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a time-spherical Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then

(4.1)

\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}\geqslant A_{1}^{p}\;\;\;a.s.

where
\phi(r)
,
p
, and
A_{1}
are given by (
2.22
), (
2.24
), and (
3.1
) respectively.

Proof.

For
n\geqslant 1
and
R>1
let us set

\displaystyle r_{n}:=R^{-n},\quad 0<\gamma<\left(\frac{A_{1}}{R^{3}}\right)^{p},

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\left\{\phi(r_{n-1})M_{r_{n}}(x,t)\leqslant\gamma\right\}.

Then by (
3.1
) we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n})\leqslant\exp\left(-A_{1}\psi\left(r_{n},\frac{\gamma}{\phi(r_{n-1})}\right)\right)=\exp\left(-A_{1}\frac{r_{n}^{3}}{\gamma^{\frac{1}{p}}}\phi(r_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}\right),

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-A_{1}\frac{r_{n}^{3}}{r_{n-1}^{3}}\log|\log r_{n-1}|\gamma^{-\frac{1}{p}}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{A_{1}}{R^{3}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{p}}\log((n-1)\log R)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(n-1)\log R}\right)^{\frac{A_{1}}{R^{3}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{p}}}

and hence
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{n})<\infty
since
A_{1}>\gamma^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{3}
. Note that
\phi(s)>\phi(t)
for
0<s<t\ll 1
and then

\displaystyle\inf_{r_{n}\leqslant r\leqslant r_{n-1}}\phi(r)\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|\geqslant\phi(r_{n-1})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|.

Note that, for any
0<\gamma<\frac{A_{1}^{p}}{R^{3}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|<\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{k\geqslant 1}\bigcap_{n\geqslant k}\left\{\inf_{r_{n}\leqslant r\leqslant r_{n-1}}\phi(r)\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|<\gamma\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{k\geqslant 1}\bigcap_{n\geqslant k}\left\{\phi(r_{n-1})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|<\gamma\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}\right)=0,

where the latter is zero by the first Borel-Cantelli Lemma. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r}(x,t)}|T(y,s)-T(x,t)|\geqslant\gamma\;\;a.s.\quad\text{ for every }0<\gamma<\left(\frac{A_{1}}{R^{3}}\right)^{p},

and the result follows by letting
R
go to 1 and taking the supremum over
\gamma
on both sides.
∎

We now prove that
\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty
a.s.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\{T(x,t),\,(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}
be a time-spherical Gaussian random field satisfying Assumption I. Then

(4.2)

\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}\leqslant A_{2,}^{p}\;\;\;a.s.

where
\phi(r)
,
p
, and
A_{2}
are given by (
2.22
), (
2.24
), and (
3.3
) respectively.

Proof.

Let us set

\displaystyle r_{n}:=e^{-n}\lfloor e^{n^{2}}\rfloor^{-1},

\displaystyle d_{n}:=\lfloor e^{n^{2}}\rfloor,

and note that

\displaystyle r_{n}d_{n}=e^{-n}

\displaystyle r_{n}d_{n+1}>e^{n}.

Let us consider the random fields

\displaystyle\Xi_{n}(x,t):=\sum_{\ell=d_{n}+1}^{d_{n+1}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}(t)Y_{\ell m}(x),

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(x):=T(x,t)-\Xi_{n}(x,t)

that is,

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(x,t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_{n}}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}(t)Y_{\ell m}(x)+\sum_{\ell=d_{n+1}+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}(t)Y_{\ell m}(x).

For a fixed
(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]
, the sequence of random fields
\{\Xi_{n}(x,t),(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is independent. Indeed, if

then
d_{n}+1<d_{n+1}<d_{m}
and the sets

\displaystyle\{a_{\ell m}(t),\,m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell,\;\;\ell=d_{n}+1,\ldots,d_{n+1}\},

\displaystyle\{a_{\ell m}(t),\,m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell,\;\;\ell=d_{m}+1,\ldots,d_{m+1}\}

are uncorrelated by (
2.8
) and hence independent because they are Gaussian. Moreover, for each
n
and
(x,t),\,(y,s)\in S^{2}\times[0,T]
,
\Xi_{n}(x,t)
is independent of
\Theta_{n}(y,s)
since they are Gaussian and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Xi_{n}(x,t)\Theta_{n}(y,s)\right]=0,

by (

).
The proof is completed once we show that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\phi(r_{n})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|\Xi_{n}(y,s)-\Xi_{n}(x,t)|\leqslant\gamma\right)=\infty,\quad\text{ for any }\gamma>A_{2}^{p},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\phi(r_{n})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|\Theta_{n}(y,s)-\Theta_{n}(x,t)|>\varepsilon\right)<\infty,\quad\text{ for any }\varepsilon>0.

Indeed, by Borel-Cantelli Lemmas and (
4.3
) (
4.4
) it follows that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\phi(r)M_{r}(x,t)\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi(r_{n})M_{r_{n}}(x,t)\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi(r_{n})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|\Xi_{n}(y,s)-\Xi_{n}(x,t)|

\displaystyle+\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi(r_{n})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|\Theta_{n}(y,s)-\Theta_{n}(x,t)|\leqslant\gamma+\varepsilon,

for any

and

. The proof is then completed by letting

go to zero and
\gamma
to
A_{2}^{p}
.

Let us first prove (

). By Anderson inequality
[
1
]
and Proposition
3.2
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\phi(r_{n})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|\Xi_{n}(y,s)-\Xi_{n}(x,t)|\leqslant\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\phi(r_{n})\max_{(y,s)\in B_{r_{n}}(x,t)}|\Xi_{n}(y,s)-\Xi_{n}(x,t)+\Theta_{n}(y,s)-\Theta_{n}(x,t)|\leqslant\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\phi(r_{n})M_{r_{n}}(x)\leqslant\gamma\right)\geqslant\exp\left(-A_{2}\psi\left(r_{n},\frac{\gamma}{\phi(r_{n})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-A_{2}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{p}}\log|\log r_{n}|\right)=\left(\frac{1}{|\log r_{n}|}\right)^{A_{2}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{p}}},

and (
4.3
) follows by Borel-Cantelli Lemma for independent events since
\gamma>A_{2}^{p}
.
We now prove (
4.4
) by means of (
2.4
) with
K=B_{r}(x,t)
and
X=\Theta_{n}
.
First, let us bound the diameter

Lemma B.1
.

Assume condition I. For any
(x_{j},t_{j})
and
(x_{j^{\prime}},t_{j^{\prime}})
points on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,T]
and for any
\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,\dots,n
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(0)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{j^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t_{j^{\prime}})}{C_{\ell}(0)}P_{\ell}(d(x_{j^{\prime}},x_{j}))-\frac{C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t)C_{\ell}(t-t_{j^{\prime}})}{C_{\ell}(0)C_{\ell}(0)}P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j}))P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j^{\prime}})\bigg)\geq 0

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(0)\bigg(\frac{C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t_{j^{\prime}})}{C_{\ell}(0)}P_{\ell}(d(x_{j^{\prime}},x_{j}))-\frac{C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t)C_{\ell}(t-t_{j^{\prime}})}{C_{\ell}(0)C_{\ell}(0)}P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j}))P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j^{\prime}})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{(2\ell+1)C_{\ell}(0)}\bigg(C_{\ell}(0)\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(0)C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t_{j^{\prime}})\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(d(x_{j^{\prime}},x_{j}))

\displaystyle-C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t)P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j}))\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(t-t_{j^{\prime}})\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j^{\prime}}))\bigg)

Let us set
C_{\ell}(x,y,t,s):=C_{\ell}(t-s)\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell}(d(x,y))
. Then
C_{\ell}(x,y,t,s)
is the covariance function of the random field
T_{\ell;t}(x):=\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}(t)Y_{\ell m}(x)
. By Proposition 1
[
32
]
the function
C_{\ell}(x,x,t,t)C_{\ell}(x_{1},x_{2},t_{1},t_{2})-C_{\ell}(x,x_{1},t,t_{1})C_{\ell}(x,x_{2},t,t_{2})
is still a covariance function, and as such it is positive definite. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}C_{\ell}(0)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{j^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t_{j^{\prime}})}{C_{\ell}(0)}P_{\ell}(d(x_{j^{\prime}},x_{j}))-\frac{C_{\ell}(t_{j}-t)C_{\ell}(t-t_{j^{\prime}})}{C_{\ell}(0)C_{\ell}(0)}P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j}))P_{\ell}(d(x,x_{j^{\prime}})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{4\pi}{(2\ell+1)C_{\ell}(0)}\left(C_{\ell}(x,x,t,t,)C_{\ell}(x_{j},x_{j^{\prime}},t_{j},t_{j^{\prime}})-C_{\ell}(x,x_{j},t,t_{j})C_{\ell}(x,x_{j^{\prime}},t,t_{j^{\prime}})\right)\geq 0

since
C_{\ell}(0)
is also positive.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
P_{\ell}
be the
\ell
-th Legendre polynomial. Then there exists a constant
c
such that for any
\theta
small enough

(B.1)

0<1-P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\leqslant c(\ell^{2}\theta^{2}+\ell^{4}\theta^{4}).

Proof.

The Taylor expansion of
P_{\ell}(t)
at
t=1
reads

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(t)=1+P^{\prime}_{\ell}(t)(t-1)+R_{\ell}(t)=1+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}(t)(t-1)+R_{\ell}(t).

Note that

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(t)\leqslant P^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(1)=\frac{(\ell+2)!}{8(\ell-2)!}\leqslant c\ell^{4},

and

\displaystyle|R_{\ell}(t)|=\frac{1}{2}|P^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}(\xi)|(t-1)^{2}\leqslant c\ell^{4}(t-1)^{2},

for some
\xi\in[t,1]
. Thus,

\displaystyle 1-P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\leqslant\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}(1-\cos\theta)+c\ell^{4}(1-\cos\theta)^{2}

\displaystyle=\ell(\ell+1)\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}+c\ell^{4}\sin^{4}\frac{\theta}{2}\leqslant c\left(\ell^{2}\theta^{2}+\ell^{4}\theta^{4}\right).

∎

Lemma B.3
.

For any
q<1
and
0<\delta<1
there exists an explicit constant
c_{q,\delta}
such that for any
0<a<\delta<1

(B.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}u^{-q}\sqrt{-\log u}du\leqslant c_{q,\delta}a^{1-q}\sqrt{-\log a}.

Proof.

A simple integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}u^{-q}\sqrt{-\log u}du=\frac{1}{1-q}a^{1-q}\sqrt{-\log a}+\frac{1}{2(1-q)}\int_{0}^{a}u^{-q}(-\log u)^{-\frac{1}{2}}du

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-q}a^{1-q}\sqrt{-\log a}+\frac{1}{2(1-q)}(-\log a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{a}u^{-q}du

\displaystyle=\sqrt{|\log a|}a^{1-q}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}+\frac{1}{2(1-q)^{2}}\frac{1}{|\log a|}\right)\leqslant c_{q,\delta}\sqrt{|\log a|}a^{1-q},

where

\displaystyle c_{q,\delta}:=\frac{1}{1-q}+\frac{1}{2(1-q)^{2}}\frac{1}{|\log\delta|}.

∎

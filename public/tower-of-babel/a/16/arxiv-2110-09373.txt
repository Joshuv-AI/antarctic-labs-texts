Towards a Hypergraph version of the Pósa–Seymour Conjecture
Matías Pavez-Signé, Nicolás Sanhueza-Matamala, Maya Stein

Abstract
We prove that for fixed r ≥ k ≥ 2 r\geq k\geq 2 , every k k -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices having minimum codegree at least ( 1 − ( ( r − 1 k − 1 ) + ( r − 2 k − 2 ) ) − 1 ) ​ n + o ⁡ ( n ) (1-(\binom{r-1}{k-1}+\binom{r-2}{k-2})^{-1})n+o(n) contains the ( r − k + 1 ) (r-k+1) th power of a tight Hamilton cycle. This result may be seen as a step towards a hypergraph version of the Pósa–Seymour conjecture. Moreover, we prove that the same bound on the codegree suffices for finding a copy of every spanning hypergraph of tree-width less than r r which admits a tree decomposition where every vertex is in a bounded number of bags.

1
Introduction

1.1
Powers of tight Hamilton cycles

A central problem in extremal graph theory is the study of degree conditions that force a graph
G
to contain a copy of some large or even spanning subgraph. A classical result in this area is Dirac’s theorem
[
8
]
from the 1950’s which states that every graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices with minimum degree at least
n/2
contains a Hamilton cycle, i.e. a cycle that passes through all vertices of
G
.

Over the decades to follow, there have been numerous efforts to extend Dirac’s theorem to other spanning subgraphs. One of the most important extensions concerns
r
th powers of Hamilton cycles
(i.e. Hamilton cycles with an additional chord for each pair of vertices at distance at most
r
).
Pósa (for
r=3
, see
[
10
]
) and Seymour (for
r>3

[
30
]
) conjectured that for all
r\geq 3
and all
n\geq r
,
every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(r-1)n/r
must contain the
(r-1)
th power of a Hamilton cycle. This conjecture has been confirmed
for every
r\geq 3
and for all large
n\geq n_{0}(r)
by Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi
[
19
]
.

Many extremal results for graphs have found extensions to hypergraphs, in particular to
k
-uniform hypergraphs, which we will call
k
-graphs
for short. In a
k
-graph
H
, the minimum degree condition is often translated to a condition on the
minimum codegree

\delta(H)
, which is defined as the maximum number
m
such that each set of
k-1
vertices lies in at least
m
edges of
H
.
For
j\geq k\geq 1
, the
j
th power of a
k
-cycle
on
n
vertices, denoted by
C^{j}_{k,n}
, is the
k
-graph on
n
cyclically ordered vertices that contains every
k
-subset of every set of
k+j-1
consecutive vertices. The
k
-graph
C^{1}_{k,n}
is also called a
tight Hamilton cycle
.

Extending Dirac’s theorem to this setting,
Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
28
]
showed that any
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq(1/2+o(1))n
contains a tight Hamilton cycle. For powers of tight hypergraph cycles, not much is known in general, but
Bedenknecht and Reiher
[
2
]
showed recently that any
3
-graph on
n
vertices with codegree at least
(4/5+o(1))n
contains a copy of
C^{2}_{3,n}
.

Our first main result establishes a generalisation of these results to arbitrary powers of
k
-cycles and arbitrary
k\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
r\geq k\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that every
k
-graph
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with

\delta(H)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\binom{r-1}{k-1}+\binom{r-2}{k-2}}+\alpha\right)n.

contains a copy of
C^{r-k+1}_{k,n}
.

Let us compare this result with what one can get from previously established results.
Given
d<k
and a
k
-graph
H
, the
minimum
d
-degree

\delta_{d}(H)
is the maximum number
m
such that each set of
d
vertices lies in at least
m
edges of
H
.
Lang and the second author
[
21
]
showed that
n
-vertex
k
-graphs
H
with
\delta_{d}(H)\geq(1-1/(2(k-d))+o(1))\binom{n}{k-d}
contain tight Hamilton cycles.
By applying this result to the
r
-uniform clique graph (it will be defined in
Section

3.5
) and
d=k-1
,
a short computation yields that
n
-vertex
k
-graphs with
\delta(H)\geq(1-1/(2(r-k+1)\binom{r}{k})+o(1))n
contain a copy of
C^{r-k+1}_{k,n}
.

theorem

1.1
yields the same outcome with a substantially lower minimum codegree condition.

For
k=2
, and any
r\geq 2
,
theorem

1.1

gives an asymptotic version of the Pósa–Seymour conjecture, as shown by Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi
[
20
]
before their proof of the exact version in
[
19
]
. For
r=k\geq 2
, the degree condition of
theorem

1.1
becomes
\delta(H)\geq(1/2+o(1))n
, and we recover the Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi’s Dirac-type threshold for tight Hamilton cycles
[
28
]
. For
r=k+1\geq 3
,
theorem

1.1
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

For all
k\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that every
k
-graph
H
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(H)\geq(1-1/({2k-1})+\alpha)n
contains the square (i.e. second power) of a tight Hamilton cycle.

For
k=3
, this equals the above-cited result by Bedenknecht and Reiher
[
2
]
.
As in their proof, we will also rely on ‘absorption’ techniques combined with ‘connection’ steps. For an outline of our proof see
Section

2
.

1.2
Hypergraphs of bounded tree-width

With the same conditions as in
theorem

1.1
, we can also embed a larger family of
k
-graphs, namely those of sufficiently bounded
tree-width
, which we define now. For a hypergraph
H
and
j\geq 1
, the
j
th shadow of
H
is the
j
-graph
\partial_{j}(H)
on
V(H)
where a
j
-set
X
is an edge if and only if it is contained in some edge of
H
.
An
r
-uniform graph
T
is an
r
-tree
if

(i)

T
consists of a single edge, or

(ii)

there exists an
r
-tree
T^{\prime}
,
a set
X\in\partial_{r-1}(T^{\prime})
,
and a vertex
v\notin V(T^{\prime})
such that

T
is obtained from
T^{\prime}
by adding
v
and the edge
X\cup\{v\}
.

The tree-width of a graph is a parameter measuring how closely the graph resembles a tree.
One of the usual definitions of the tree-width
uses the notion of ‘partial
k
-trees’, which is equivalent to defining the
tree-width

\treewidth(G)
of a graph
G

as the minimum
r
such that there is an
(r+1)
-tree
T
with
G\subseteq\partial_{2}(T)

(see, e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 35]
, for this and equivalent definitions of tree-width).
For
k\geq 3
, define the
tree-width
\treewidth(G)
of a
k
-graph
G
as the minimum
r
such that there is an
(r+1)
-tree
T
with
G\subseteq\partial_{k}(T)
, in which case we say that
G

admits

T
.

With these definitions at hand, we can state our second main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
r\geq k\geq 2
,
\Delta>0
and
\alpha>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that the following holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
k
-graph
with
\treewidth(G)<r
,
which admits an
r
-tree
T
with
\Delta_{1}(T)\leq\Delta
, and let
H
be an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with

\delta(H)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\binom{r-1}{k-1}+\binom{r-2}{k-2}}+\alpha\right)n.

Then
G\subseteq H
.

For
r\geq k=2
,
the codegree condition of

theorem

1.3
is
\delta(H)\geq(1-1/r+o(1))n
, and the graph
G
needs to admit an
r
-tree
T
of bounded degree.
It is not difficult to see that any such
G
has bounded-degree and is
(r-1)
-degenerate, and
thus has chromatic number at most
r
.
Also, graphs of bounded degree and tree-width have sublinear bandwidth
[
4
]
,
so our result recovers a particular instance of the Bandwidth Theorem by Böttcher, Schacht and Taraz
[
5
]
.

For
r=k\geq 2
, the codegree condition of

theorem

1.3
becomes
\delta(H)\geq(1/2+o(1))n
, and the
k
-graph
G
needs to admit a
k
-tree
T
of bounded degree, which is equivalent to
G
itself being a bounded-degree
k
-tree.
For
r=k=2
this is a result by Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi
[
18
]
on embedding spanning bounded-degree trees, and for
r=k>2
we recover a previous result by the authors
[
26
]
on the embedding of spanning bounded-degree
k
-trees.

1.3
Lower bounds and clique tilings

Most of the results implied by our two theorems are asymptotically tight, that is, the codegree conditions in
[
5
,
18
,
19
,
26
,
28
]
are best possible apart from the
o(n)
term.
For this reason, also

theorem

1.1
and
theorem

1.3
are asymptotically tight for some choices of
k
and
r
, e.g. when
r=k
or
k=2
.
We do not know if our
results are best possible in general.

We remark that lower bounds for the codegree in our results can be inferred from bounds on
clique tiling thresholds
.
A
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-clique tiling
is a collection of vertex-disjoint cliques of order
r
in a
k
-graph.
Note that
C^{r-k+1}_{k,n}
contains a
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-clique tiling if
r
divides
n
,
and the tree-width of a
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-clique is less than
r
.
This shows that, apart from the
o(n)
term, the codegree condition in
theorems

1.1
and
1.3
could not be lower than the
codegree threshold
t_{k-1}(\smash{K_{r}^{k}},n)
for finding
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-clique tilings
in
n
-vertex
k
-graphs (precise definitions are in Section
5.2
).

From this connection, our results give upper bounds on
t(\smash{K_{r}^{k}},n)
,
in particular showing that
t_{k-1}(\smash{K_{r}^{k}})\leq 1-\left(\binom{r-1}{k-1}+\binom{r-2}{k-2}\right)^{-1}
holds for all
r\geq k\geq 3
.
For some values of
k,r
this bound seems to be new, but we emphasize that the better bound
t_{k-1}(\smash{K_{r}^{k}},n)\leq 1-\binom{r-1}{k-1}^{-1}
was shown by Lo and Markström
[
23
]
to hold for some values of
r,k
, in particular for all
r
sufficiently large in terms of
k
.
We discuss tiling thresholds with detail in Section
5.2
.

Lower bounds on
t(\smash{K_{r}^{k}},n)
can be used as a benchmark for our results, but little is known about these thresholds in general.
Asymptotics
[
27
,
23
]
for
t(\smash{K_{4}^{3}},n)
(and exact values for large
n

[
17
]
) imply that the term
4/5
in the result from
[
2
]
, and in our
theorem

1.1
for
(r,k)=(4,3)
could at best be improved to
3/4
.
For
k
fixed and
r
large, the main constant in our bounds in
theorems

1.1
and
1.3
is of the form
1-cr^{1-k}
, while the clique tiling threshold can be bounded from below by the

codegree Turán threshold
guaranteeing a single copy of
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
which in turn has been bounded from below by
1-c^{\prime}r^{1-k}\log r

[
24
]
, where
c
and
c^{\prime}
are constants (depending on
k
only).

1.4
Bounding only the treewidth

Our result in
theorem

1.3
applies to
k
-graphs
G
of bounded tree-width with a ‘hypertree decomposition’ of bounded-degree, or equivalently, with the property that every vertex is in a bounded number of bags.
This latter condition is necessary for our approach and it does not follow automatically for any
G
of bounded maximum degree: there are bounded-degree graphs which do not admit any tree decomposition with bags of optimal size where every vertex is in a bounded number of bags (see, e.g.
[
9
, Figure 1]
).
However, if we increase the bag sizes we can obtain a tree decomposition where every vertex is in a bounded number of bags.
We use this fact to prove a variant of
theorem

1.3
which applies to arbitrary
k
-graphs of bounded degree and bounded tree-width (see Section
8
).

2
Proof overview

Our first main result,
theorem

1.1
, will be deduced as a relatively quick consequence of
theorem

1.3
, so we focus on the proof of the latter result.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\treewidth(G)<r
that admits an
r
-tree
T
of bounded vertex-degree, and let
H
be a
k
-graph satisfying the conditions of
theorem

1.3
.
Instead of working directly with
H
, we will work in the auxiliary
r
-clique
r
-graph

K_{r}(H)
which captures the
r
-cliques in
H
(see Section
3.5
for a definition).
This is because in order to embed
G
into
H
, it will be enough to embed
T
into
K_{r}(H)
.
We will use a similar strategy as the one used in
[
26
]
. The difference is that in
[
26
]
, a codegree condition on a
k
-graph was used to embed
k
-trees, while now, wishing to embed an
r
-tree, we do not necessarily have such a condition in the
r
-graph
K_{r}(H)
.

However, using the minimum codegree condition on the
k
-graph
H
,
we will ensure that
K_{r}(H)
satisfies the following three properties, which will be crucial to the proof.

(i)

Connections.

Every pair of distinct
(r-1)
-tuples
f,f^{\prime}\in\partial_{r-1}(K_{r}(H))
is connected by many tight walks of bounded length.

(ii)

Uniformly dense clique matching.

There are a small set
V_{0}\subseteq V(H)
and a partition
\{V_{j}^{i}\}_{(i,j)\in[t]\times[r]}
of
V(H)\setminus V_{0}
such for every
i\in[t]
, the
r
-partite graph
K_{r}(H)[V_{1}^{i},\dotsc,V_{r}^{i}]
is uniformly dense.

(iii)

Absorbers.

There is a set
\mathcal{A}
of tuples of vertices of
H
such that for each
v\in V(H)
, many tuples
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{A}
can ‘absorb’
v
.

We embed the absorbers from
(iii)
, and then
partition
T
into a bounded number of small subtrees
T_{0},T_{1},\dotsc,T_{\ell}
such that
T_{0}\cup T_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup T_{i}
is (tightly) connected for each
i\in[t]
. We iteratively embed the trees
T_{i}
, first finding an element of the uniformly dense clique matching from
(ii)
having enough unused vertices, and then
using Property
(i)
to connect the embedding of
T_{0}\cup T_{1}\cup\dotsb\cup T_{i-1}
with this free space (by embedding the first levels of
T_{i}
). We then finish by using the absorber, which allow us to increase the embedding of the tree vertex by vertex.

To carry out the proof as described, we invoke lemmas already present in
[
26
]
, which allows us to shorten the proof.
The new ingredients here are the connections and the clique matching, whose proofs we describe in more detail now.

We show in Section
4
that
K_{r}(H)
has Property
(i)
via induction on
r
starting from
k
.
The base case
r=k
is equivalent to finding bounded-length tight walks between pairs of
(k-1)
-tuples in
k
-graphs with large codegree, and this was already proven in
[
26
]
(a similar lemma, although with different quantification, was also shown already by Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
28
, Lemma 2.4]
).
For the induction step, we show here that for
k\leq j\leq r-1
and a walk
P
in
K_{j}(H)
of bounded length, the codegree in
H
allow us to find a bounded number of vertices which together with
P
define a tight walk in
K_{j+1}(H)
.

To get Property
(ii)
, in Section
5
, we prove that
K_{r}(H)
contains a uniformly dense clique matching using the hypergraph regularity method.
After applying the hypergraph regularity lemma to
H
, it suffices to find an almost spanning
r
-clique matching in the corresponding reduced
k
-graph
R
.
Unfortunately, the reduced
k
-graph usually does not inherit the codegree conditions of
H
due to the presence of ‘irregular’ tuples.
We overcome this issue by working in a supergraph
R^{\prime}\supseteq R
with the ‘correct’ codegrees, then edge-colouring
R^{\prime}
suitably and finding an
r
-clique matching under some colour constraints.
By choosing the edge-colouring appropriately we can make sure that most of the cliques of the found matching are actually contained in
R
.
We combine this with powerful tools to find matchings in hypergraphs by Keevash and Mycroft
[
17
]
.

3
Tools

We introduce some notation and results that will be used throughout the paper.
During the paper we will use the common hierarchy notation for quantifiers: the phrase “

b>0
, there exists
a_{0}>0

Sequences of longer chained hierarchies are defined similarly.
If

3.1
Ordered shadow

Given a
k
-graph
H
, its
ordered shadow

\orderedshadow(H)\subseteq V(H)^{k-1}
is the set of all tuples of
k-1
distinct vertices whose (unordered) underlying set belongs to
\partial_{k-1}(H)
. Throughout the paper, we will use bold letters to denote elements from
\partial^{\circ}(H)
and for ordered tuples in general. Elements from
\partial^{\circ}(H)
will be used as their underlying set for set-theoretical operations. For example, if
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}),\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{k})\in\partial^{\circ}(H)
, then
\mathbf{x}\cup\mathbf{y}=\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}\}\cup\{y_{1},\dotsc,y_{k}\}
.

A function

To simplify notation throughout the paper, we define

f_{k}(j):=\frac{1}{\binom{j-1}{k-1}+\binom{j-2}{k-2}}

for all integers
j\geq k
. Let us observe that
f_{k}
is monotone decreasing and
f_{k}(k)=1/2

3.4
Hypertrees

By definition, an

(i)

(ii)

there exists

such that

It is clear that an ordering of the edges implies an ordering of the vertices and vice versa. Any such ordering will be called a

An

Lemma 3.6

Let

,

5
Uniformly dense clique matching

In this section we show a matching lemma which is required in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

5.1
Matching lemma and outline of its proof

Given a
k
-graph
G
and disjoint subsets
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{k}\subseteq V(G)
,
let
G[V_{1},\dotsc,V_{k}]
be the
k
-partite graph with vertex classes
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{k}
, having all edges of
G
that intersect each of the sets
V_{i}
exactly once.
Let
e_{G}(V_{1},\dotsc,V_{k})
denote the number of edges of
G[V_{1},\dotsc,V_{k}]
.
A
k
-partite
k
-graph
G
on sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
of the same size
m
is called
(\varepsilon,d)
-uniformly dense
if for all choices of
W_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
with
|W_{i}|=h\geq\varepsilon m
for all
1\leq i\leq k
,

e_{G}(W_{1},\dotsc,W_{k})\geq dh^{k}.

(5.1)

Let
G
be a
k
-graph, let
\varepsilon,d>0
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that
G
has an
(\varepsilon,d)
-uniformly dense perfect matching

\mathcal{M}=\{(V_{1}^{i},\dotsc,V_{k}^{i})\}_{i\in[t]}
and we call sets
\{V_{j}^{i}\}_{j\in[k]}
its
edges
if

(i)

\{V_{a}^{i}\}_{a\in[k],i\in[t]}
partitions
V(G)
,

(ii)

|V_{a}^{i}|=|V_{b}^{j}|
for all
a,b\in[k]
,
i,j\in[t]
, and

(iii)

G[V_{1}^{i},\dotsc,V_{k}^{i}]
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-uniformly dense for each
i\in[t]
.

The aim of this section is to show the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1

(Matching lemma)
.

Let
r\geq k\geq 2
, let
1/n\ll 1/M\ll d\ll\varepsilon,\delta,\alpha,1/r
, and let
H
be an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with

\delta(H)\geq\left(1-f_{k}(r)+\alpha\right)n
. Then
H
has a subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
with
|V(H^{\prime})|\geq(1-\delta)n
such that
K_{r}(H^{\prime})
admits an
(\varepsilon,d)
-uniformly dense perfect matching with at most
M
edges.

The proof can be summarised in the following three steps.

(i)

Obtaining a regular partition.

An application of hypergraph regularity to
H
will yield a partition of
H
into few parts
\mathcal{P}
and a reduced
k
-graph
R
on vertex set
\mathcal{P}
,
consisting on
k
-tuples of clusters which are both ‘dense’ and ‘regular’.
Each clique on
r
vertices in
R
will correspond to a collection of
r
clusters which could form an edge in a uniformly dense cluster matching of
H
.
Given this, the goal is to find a
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-matching (for definitions see below)
in
R
which is as large as possible.

(ii)

Finding a large clique factor.

Our approach to find such a matching is to use the codegree tiling threshold for clique factors.
More precisely, in this step we will verify that the codegree of
H
suffices to find a
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-factor in
H
, that is, a
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-matching which covers every vertex.
Hence, if we could show that
R
inherits the relative codegree conditions of
H
(up to scaling and a small error),
we would find the desired large
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
-matching in
R
and we would be done.

(iii)

Avoiding the irregular edges.

However, an obstacle is that
R
usually will
not
inherit the codegree conditions of
H
due to the presence of ‘irregular’ edges, which constitute a
k
-graph
I
on
V(R)
. Since the union graph
R\cup I

will
inherit the codegree conditions of
H
, we can use tools from the previous step to find a clique factor in
R\cup I
.
We would like to have the factor avoiding as much as possible of
I
, which can be done by defining an edge-colouring and introducing some restraints with respect to this edge-colouring.

Although the structure of the proof is simple, the use of hypergraph regularity requires long and technical definitions, whose use has however become quite standard.
To avoid clutter here, we defer the technical details of step
(i)

to Appendix
A
,
where the proof of
lemma

5.1
is given in its entirety.
In what remains of this section we will prove step
(ii)
(in Lemma
5.2
) and step
(iii)
(in
lemma

5.4
), which constitute the novel and non-standard ingredients of the proof.

We also remark that (as pointed to us by a referee) the outcome of steps
(ii)
–
(iii)
can be achieved in an alternative way by considering the codegree threshold of
almost
perfect clique tilings.
We decided to keep our original proof because we believe it to be novel and independently interesting, but we will sketch this alternative briefly in
Remark

5.6
.

5.2
Clique factors in hypergraphs

For
k
-graphs
G
and

,
an
H
-matching
in
G
is a set of vertex-disjoint copies of
H
in
G
, and an
H
-factor
is a spanning
H
-matching.
Clearly, if
G
has an
H
-factor, then
|V(H)|
divides
|V(G)|
.
Given
1\leq d<k
and
an
r
-vertex

-graph

the

-degree

-tiling threshold

is the minimum

such that every

-vertex

-graph

with
\delta_{d}(G)\geq h
and

has an

-factor.
The
asymptotic

t_{d}(H)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}t_{d}(H,nr)/\binom{nr-d}{k-d}
.

For

,
\smash{K_{r}^{k}}
is the
k
-uniform clique on

The Hajnal-Szemerédi theorem
[

states that

.
Estimates on

for

are given in the following lemma, which is the core ingredient to find the clique factor needed in step

of our proof of

.

Lemma 5.2
.

For all

, we have
t_{k-1}(\smash{K_{r}^{k}})\leq 1-f_{k}(r).

We point out that better upper bounds on

are known by the work of Lo and Markström
[
23
, Corollary 1.5]
, but unfortunately their results are stated only for specific combinations of

which do not cover all the cases

which we need.

For the proof of Lemma

, we use the powerful framework for hypergraph matchings of Keevash and Mycroft
[
17
]
(see also Han
[
14
]
for a simplified proof).

We need the following standard concepts.
The

\mathcal{H}
of a

-graph

is the usual downward closure of the edge set of

.
More generally, for

-complex
is a hypergraph

whose edges have size at most

, and whose edge set is closed under inclusion. For

, let

The

of

is the minimum of

taken over all
i
-edges

.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{V_{1},\dotsc,V_{t}\}
be a partition of the vertex set of a
k
-graph

.
The

\mathbf{i}_{\mathcal{P}}(S)\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}

has coordinates
\mathbf{i}_{\mathcal{P}}(S)_{i}=|S\cap V_{i}|
for

A vector

is a

if its coordinates are nonnegative and their sum equals

. Moreover, if

a
\mu
-robust edge vector
.

Let

be the lattice generated by the set of all

-robust edge-vectors. This lattice is

if it does not contain all
k
-vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{t}
.
For
1\leq j\leq t
, let

be the

th unit vector, i.e. the vector which has a

in the

th coordinate and

otherwise.
A

is the vector

for some

Theorem 5.3

(
[

)
.

Let
1/n\ll\mu\ll\alpha,1/r
with

be an

contains a perfect matching, or

(ii)

has a

, i.e. there is a partition

of

into at most
r

, such that

is incomplete and transferral-free.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

It suffices to prove that, given any
\alpha>0
, for

sufficiently large and divisible by

, any

-graph

We select

according to the relations in Theorem
5.3

Given a

-graph

satisfying the degree conditions, set

and let

be the down-closure of

where the last inequality follows from the fact that

which in turn is easy to check for

Now, let
\mathcal{P}
be any partition of

into

is incomplete (so in particular,

is not transferral-free. Then, by Theorem

has a perfect matching, and we are done.

corollary

.
At most

-vectors, that is, vectors corresponding to the index vector of an

So by the pigeonhole principle, there is an

5.3
Almost spanning factors which avoid bad edges

Now we carry out step

This is given by the following lemma, which states that any

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

and

To prove

.
Given an edge-colouring of a

\mu n

(
[

, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
1/n\ll\mu\ll\gamma,1/r
and

with

, let

be a

-graph on

be a

-graph on

Proof of

.

Let

.
Set

,
and let

be sufficiently large. Let

and

be given, as well as

, as in the lemma.
We need to show that

has an

First, remove at most

.
Set

.

We define an edge-colouring

.

We claim that, with non-zero probability,

Since each colour

-bounded. Apply

to see that

-factor

.
Obtain

from

Remark 5.6
.

We finish by remarking that we can replace the use of Lemmas
5.2
and
5.4
with a different approach.
Lo and Markström
[
22
, Corollary 1.7]
showed that for every
\varepsilon>0,\alpha>0
, and sufficiently large
n
, every
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq(1-\binom{r-1}{k-1}^{-1}+\alpha)n
admits a collection of vertex-disjoint copies of

which leave at most

vertices uncovered.
Then, to prove

5.4
, one can follow the approach of Ferber and Kwan
[

.
Partition the vertex set of

It is possible to show (cf.
[

, Lemma 3.4]
) that for almost every set

in such a partition, the (relative) minimum codegree of

is close to

.
So the result of Lo and Markström gives an almost-perfect matching in each
(H\setminus I)[S]
. The union of these matchings is an almost-perfect matching in
H\setminus I
.

Hypergraphs with bounded tree-width

In this section we give the proof of Theorem

First, we will need to recall or adapt some facts about absorbers originally used in

6.1
Absorbing structures

We now introduce an absorption technique for hypergraphs which will allow us to complete an embedding of almost all of a spanning tree to a full embedding.
We start by introducing our absorbing structure, after a quick definition.
If

is an

for the

-hypergraph consisting of all

with

Definition 6.1

(Absorbing

.

Let

(r-1)
-tree on

(

The next lemma and its proof resembles that of Proposition 9.4 in

Lemma 6.4
.

Let

Let

We construct an absorbing

.

and

and

.

.

.

Now, suppose

-uniformly dense matching.

,

, and for each set

Let

and

be the

-reduced
k

, respectively.

an arbitrary

\displaystyle\overline{\deg}(Y;R\cup I)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equation:regularslice-dreduced+irregular}}{\geq}\overline{\deg}(Y;R(H^{\prime\prime}))-d_{k}\overset{\eqref{equation:uniformlydensematching-slice}}{\geq}\overline{\deg}(\mathcal{J}_{k-1}[V_{Y}];H^{\prime\prime})-\varepsilon_{k}-d_{k}

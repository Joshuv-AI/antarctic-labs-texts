1.
Introduction

Moduli spaces of vector bundles on a complex projective curve have a long history. Apart from
algebraic geometry, the context in which these moduli spaces were introduced, they also arise
in symplectic geometry, geometric representation theory, differential geometry and mathematical
physics. Line bundles and higher rank vector bundles on these moduli spaces play central
role in their study. On the other hand, these moduli spaces contain a distinguished class
of rational curves known as Hecke curves. They can be characterized as minimal degree rational
curves passing through a general point on the moduli spaces
[
Ty
]
(also proved in

[
Su
]
). These Hecke curves play important role in the geometric
representation theoretic aspect of the moduli spaces and also in the computation of cohomology
of coherent sheaves on the moduli spaces.

Here we study restriction of vector bundles on moduli spaces to the Hecke curves. To describe
the result proved here, fix a smooth complex projective curve
X
of genus at least three.
Let
\xi
be a line bundle on
X
with genus
g\,\geq\,3
and
r\,\geq\,2
an integer coprime to
\deg(\xi)
.
Let
M_{X}(r,\xi)
be the moduli space of stable vector bundles on
X
of rank
r
and determinant

\xi
. We prove the following (see Theorem
6.4
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on
M_{X}(r,\xi)
such that its restriction to every Hecke curve on

M_{X}(r,\xi)
is trivial. Then
E
is trivial.

Our motivation to study this problem comes from the result that says that a
vector bundle on a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is trivial when the
restriction of it to every line is trivial (in fact, it is enough to check
it for lines through a fixed point, cf.
[
OSS
, p. 51, Theorem
3.2.1]
). In this article we are replacing
\mathbb{P}^{N}
by

M_{X}(r,\xi)
, and lines in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
by Hecke curves in
M_{X}(r,\xi)

(which are also rational curves of minimal degree).

To prove Theorem
1.1
we crucially use a theorem of Simpson which says that a semistable vector
bundle
W
on a smooth complex projective variety admits a flat holomorphic connection if

c_{1}(W)\,=\,0\,=\,c_{2}(W)
. It is relatively straightforward to deduce that the vector bundle
E
in
Theorem
1.1
is semistable and
c_{1}(E)\,=\,0
. Almost all of our work is devoted in proving
that
c_{2}(E)\,=\,0
.

Cohomology of moduli space

Let

Applying this to the Poincaré bundle

The cohomology classes

3.
Hecke curves

In this section we recall the definition and basic properties of Hecke
curves.
Let
x\in X
be a point. Let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
with
determinant
\xi(x)=\xi\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(x)
. Let
q:F\longrightarrow k_{x}
be a
surjection to the sky-scraper sheaf
k_{x}
. We obtain a short exact
sequence

0\,\longrightarrow\,E\,\longrightarrow\,F\,\longrightarrow\,k_{x}\,\longrightarrow\,0

(3.1)

and we say that
E
is the Hecke transform of
E
, at the point
x
,
with respect to the quotient
q
. If we let
q
vary among all the
quotients of
F
with image
k_{x}
,
we obtain a family of vector bundles of determinant
\xi

parametrized by the projective
space
\mathbb{P}(F^{\vee}_{x})
(we take the convention that
\mathbb{P}
is the
projective space of lines)

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{E}}\,\longrightarrow\,p^{*}_{X}F\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathcal{O}}_{\{x\}\times\mathbb{P}(F^{*}_{x})}(1)\,\longrightarrow\,0

(3.2)

Definition 3.1

(
[
NR
, p. 306, Definition 5.1 and Remark 5.2]
)
.

Let
l
,
m
be integers.
A vector bundle

F
over
X
is
(l,\,m)
–
stable
if, for every
proper subbundle
G
of
F
of positive rank,

\frac{\deg(G)+l}{\operatorname{rk}G}\,<\,\frac{\deg(F)+l-m}{\operatorname{rk}F}\,.

We remark that
(l,\,m)
–stability is a Zariski open condition
[
NR
, Proposition 5.3]
.
Note that
(0,\,0)
–stability is the same thing as usual stability.
If
F
is
(l,\,m)
–stable in (
3.1
), then
E
is

(l,\,m-1)
–stable
[
NR
, Lemma 5.5]
.
Indeed, if a subbundle
G
of
E
contradicts

(l,\,m-1)
–stability, then the subbundle of
F
generated by
G
will
contradict the
(l,\,m)
–stability of
F
.

Let
F
be a
(0,\,1)
–stable bundle of determinant
\xi(x)
. The family of Hecke
transforms (
3.2
) gives a morphism, which we call Hecke space:

\psi\,:\,\mathbb{P}(F_{x}^{\vee})\,\longrightarrow\,M_{X}(r,\xi)

This morphism is an embedding
[
NR
, Lemma 5.9]
.
A Hecke curve is the restriction of this morphism to a line

\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset\mathbb{P}(F_{x}^{\vee})

\psi|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\,:\,\mathbb{P}^{1}\,\longrightarrow\,M_{X}(r,\xi)

We use the term Hecke “curve” instead of “line”,
because the degree of this morphism is not one. In fact, using formula
(
2.2
) we can calculate
\deg\psi|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\,:=\,\deg\psi|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{M}(1)\,=\,r
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
r
and
d\,=\,\deg\xi
are coprime, and
g\,\geq\,3
,
then the
(0,\,2)
–stable vector bundles with determinant

\xi(2x)
give a non-empty Zariski open subset of
M_{X}(r,\xi(2x))
.

Proof.

The case of
(0,\,1)
–stability and
(1,\,0)
–stability is considered in

[
NR
, Proposition 5.4]
. We
sketch here the analogous argument for
(0,\,2)
–stability.

We shall estimate the dimension of the complement of the subset of

(0,\,2)
–stable bundles.
A vector bundle
F
of degree
d+2
and rank
n
is not

(0,\,2)
–stable if it has a subbundle
F^{\prime}

of rank
n^{\prime}\,<\,n
and degree
d^{\prime}
with
0\,\geq\,((d+2)-2)r^{\prime}-d^{\prime}r\,=\,dr^{\prime}-d^{\prime}r
.
Since
r
and
d
are coprime, equality cannot hold, so we have

dr^{\prime}-d^{\prime}r\,<\,0\,.

(3.3)

We have

h\,:=\,\dim H^{1}(Hom(F/G,\,G))\,=\,(d+2)r^{\prime}-d^{\prime}r+r^{\prime}(r-r^{\prime})(g-1)

so the subset of
M_{X}(r,\xi(2x))
corresponding to bundles which are
not
(0,\,2)
–stable has dimension at most

\big(r^{2}(g-1)+1\big)+\big((r-r^{\prime})^{2}(g-1)+1\big)-g+h-1\,=\,

(r^{2}-rr^{\prime}+r^{\prime}{}^{2}-1))(g-1)+r^{\prime}(d+2)-d^{\prime}r

and this is smaller than
\dim M_{X}(r,\xi(2x))\,=\,(r^{2}-1)(g-1)
if and only if

r^{\prime}(r-r^{\prime})(g-1)\,>\,r^{\prime}(d+2)-d^{\prime}r\,.

(3.4)

Now, using (
3.3
), it is easy to see that this holds if
g\geq 3
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x\in X
be a point and let

be a closed subset of codimension

. Then a general Hecke curve (with respect to

Proof.

This is proved, for instance, in

Let

be the universal bundle on

, and let

A point of

over

corresponds to a point in the projective space

\mathbb{P}(E_{x})\,=\,\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(k_{x},E))
, so it corresponds to a short exact
sequence as (
3.1
). Let
H_{x}\,\subset\,P_{x}
be the open subset
such that
F
is
(0,1)
–stable. Then there is a morphism
q\,:\,H_{x}\,\longrightarrow\,M_{X}(r,\xi(x))
, whose image we call
V
.

\textstyle{{H_{x}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{V\subset M_{X}(r,\xi(x))}
\textstyle{{M_{X}(r,\xi)}}

The fiber of

is an open subset of
\mathbb{P}(E_{x})
, and the fiber of

is
\mathbb{P}(F_{x}^{\vee})
. Let
S^{\prime}\,=\,p^{-1}(S)
. It is either empty or
\dim S^{\prime}\,=\,\dim S+r-1
. Consider now the morphism

The fiber of

is

the intersection of

If

Theorem 6.4
.

Let

be a vector bundle on

satisfying the condition that

to every Hecke curve is trivial. Then

is trivial.

Proof.

The restriction of

to a Hecke curve on

is trivial. From this

is semistable. Indeed, if

is not semistable, there is a
coherent subsheaf

such that

is torsionfree, and

\frac{\deg(V)}{{\rm rank}(V)}\,>\,\frac{\deg(E)}{{\rm rank}(E)}\,=\,0

(see (

)). Now, for a general Hecke curve

, the

is torsionfree (

locally free in codimension at least 2, so we can apply Lemma
3.3
). Moreover, for any smooth closed curve
C\,\subset\,M
, and any torsionfree coherent sheaf

on
M
which is locally free on

,
we have

\deg(W|_{C})\,=\,\deg(C)\cdot\deg(W)\,.

(6.6)

Indeed, this follows from the facts that

•

\text{Pic}(M)\,=\,\mathbb{Z}
(see (

)), and

•

\deg(C)
and
\deg(W)
are computed using the same ample line bundle bundle, namely
the ample generator of
\text{Pic}(M)
.

Also, we have
\deg(C)\,>\,0
because the restriction, to

, of the ample generator of
\text{Pic}(M)

is ample. Now, from (

) and (

) it is deduced that

\deg(V|_{\mathbb{P}})\,>\,0

for any Hecke curve
{\mathbb{P}}\,\subset\,M
that is contained in the open subset where

is locally free. But the trivial bundle
E|_{\mathbb{P}}
on
\mathbb{P}
does not
contain any subsheaf of
positive degree. From this contradiction we conclude that

is semistable.

Since

•

is semistable,

•

c_{1}(E)\,=\,0
(

) and

•

(Proposition

),

the vector bundle

admits a filtration of subbundles

such that for every

, the quotient

is a stable vector

(note that

[

(set the Higgs field to be zero in
[

);

[

Since

∎

As we explained in the introduction, this result is analogous to the
result that says that a vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is trivial when it is
trivial when restricted to every line passing through some fixed point

p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}

[
OSS
, p. 51, Theorem
3.2.1]
. We will finish with some remarks, comparing our result
to the classical one.

The idea of the proof of in
[
OSS
, p. 51, Theorem
3.2.1]
is the
following. Let
\sigma\,:\,\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{N}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{N}

at
p
. Note that
E\,=\,E\otimes\sigma_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}\,=\,\sigma_{*}\sigma^{*}E
,
so it is enough to show that
the pullback
\sigma^{*}E
is trivial.
By hypothesis, the pullback
\sigma^{*}E
is trivial on the
fibers of the projection of
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
to the exceptional divisor

G
, so it descends to a vector bundle
F
on
G
. But the restriction
of the blowup
\sigma
to the exceptional divisor
G
is constant, so
it follows that
F
is trivial (with fiber canonically isomorphic to
the fiber of
E
on
p
). Therefore, the pullback
\sigma^{*}E

of
E
to the blowup
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
is trivial, and the result is proved.

In this argument, we use the fact that the lines passing through a
point

sweep the whole projective space (in order to prove

\sigma_{*}\sigma^{*}E\,=\,E
).
But the Hecke curves through a fixed point in the moduli space do not
sweep the whole moduli space. This is why we have to use a different
argument to show that
E
is trivial.

If we apply our method to the classical setting, we get a slightly
stronger result.

.
If

is the subset where

is not locally free, then there is an
automorphism
\varphi
of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is disjoint to

Z(\varphi^{*}(E))\,=\,f^{-1}(E)
. Since
E
is unstable if and only if

\varphi^{*}(E)
is unstable, we may assume that
Z(E)
is disjoint with

\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and then

\deg(S)\,=\,\deg(S|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})\,\leq\,0

where the last equality follows from the fact that
E|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is
trivial, and hence semistable. This proves that
E
is semistable.

We will now show that
c_{2}(E)\,=\,0
. It is enough to show that
the restriction
E^{\prime}\,=\,E|_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
has
c_{2}(E^{\prime})\,=\,0
.
Now let
\sigma\,:\,\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at a
point. To show that
c_{2}(E^{\prime})\,=\,0
, it is enough to show
c_{2}(\sigma^{*}E^{\prime})\,=\,0
.
By hypothesis, the restriction of
\sigma^{*}E^{\prime}
to the fiber of
the projection of
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
to the exceptional divisor
G
are
trivial, so
E^{\prime}
is the pullback of a vector bundle
F
on
G
. But
the dimension of
G
is one, so
c_{2}(F)\,=\,0
, and also
c_{2}(E^{\prime})\,=\,0
.

Summing up,
E
is semistable,
c_{1}(E)\,=\,0
and
c_{2}(E)\,=\,0
, so apply
Simpson’s results as in the proof of Theorem
6.4
.
∎

In the case of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
it is enough to check with the lines passing

\mathbb{P}^{N}
, so it is natural to ask if something
similar holds for the moduli space. Our proof would not work if we

E
, we consider a potentially
destabilizing subsheaf
V
, and we restrict to a Hecke curve where
V

might not be locally free on the chosen point

. In the proof of Lemma
6.5
this is solved by
using the large automorphism group of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, but this trick does not
seem possible in the case of the moduli space, whose automorphism is
very small. Another point where our proof would fail is Lemma

6.1
we use Hecke curves which do not intersect.

Abstract
. Let π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} be cuspidal automorphic representations of GL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{GL}(n) and GL ⁡ ( n ′ ) \mathrm{GL}(n^{\prime}) with unitary central characters. We establish a new zero-free region for all GL ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathrm{GL}(1) -twists of the Rankin–Selberg L L -function L ⁡ ( s , π × π ′ ) L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}) , generalizing Siegel’s celebrated work on Dirichlet L L -functions. As an application, we prove the first unconditional Siegel–Walfisz theorem for the Dirichlet coefficients of − L ′ ( s , π × π ′ ) / L ( s , π × π ′ ) -L^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})/L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}) . Also, for n ≤ 8 n\leq 8 , we extend the region of holomorphy and nonvanishing for the twisted symmetric power L L -functions L ⁡ ( s , π , Sym n ⊗ χ ) L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\otimes\chi) of any cuspidal automorphic representation of GL ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathrm{GL}(2) .

1.
Introduction and the main result

In 1896, Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin independently proved that the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
does not vanish in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. This statement is equivalent to the asymptotic form of the prime number theorem. In 1899, de la Vallée Poussin established the classical zero-free region for
\zeta(s)
, which was quickly extended to Dirichlet
L
-functions. In particular, let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)
be a Dirichlet character. There exists an absolute and effectively computable constant
>0
such that
L(s,\chi)
has at most one zero
\beta
(necessarily real and simple) in the region

(1.1)

\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1-\Cr{ZetaZFR}/\log(q(|\mathrm{Im}(s)|+3)).

If
\beta
exists, then
\chi
is quadratic. In this case, Siegel’s lower bound on
L(1,\chi)
implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
=\Cr{SiegelDirichlet}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(1.2)

L(\sigma,\chi)\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{SiegelDirichlet}q^{-\varepsilon}.

See
[
36
,
39
]
for the original references.

The method of de la Vallée Poussin can be modified to establish a zero-free region for many
L
-functions. Specifically, let
F
be a number field, let
\mathbb{A}_{F}
be the ring of adeles over
F
, and let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
whose central character
\omega_{\pi}
is unitary. Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient and
L(s,\pi)
be the
L
-function of
\pi
. Let
C(\pi)\geq 3
denote the analytic conductor, which measures the arithmetic and spectral complexity of
\pi
. Given
(\pi,\chi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, let
\pi\otimes\chi
be the cuspidal automorphic representation
g\mapsto\pi(g)\chi(\det g)
. For convenience, we introduce the subset
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
consisting of
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
for which
\omega_{\pi}
is trivial on the diagonally embedded positive reals. For each
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, there exist unique
\pi^{*}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
and
t_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\pi=\pi^{*}\otimes|\cdot|^{it_{\pi}},\qquad L(s,\pi)=L(s+it_{\pi},\pi^{*}).

Given
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, let
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
be the associated Rankin–Selberg
L
-function, whose basic properties were established by Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
14
,
16
,
17
]
. If
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}^{*}
, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is holomorphic away from a possible pole at
s=1
, which occurs if and only if
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}
. If
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{*}
is trivial, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=L(s,\pi)
.

In 1976, Jacquet and Shalika
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
proved that
L(s,\pi)
does not vanish in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. In 1981, Shahidi
[
35
, Theorem 5.2]
established the same zero-free region for
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
. Replacing
\pi
with
\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{it}
and varying
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we find that these results are equivalent to proving that for all
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, we have that
L(\sigma,\pi)\neq 0
and
L(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0
when
\sigma\geq 1
.

In some cases, the method of de la Vallée Poussin can extend this zero-free region.

In this paper, we develop a new method for establishing zero-free regions for
L
-functions. We apply our method to extend Siegel’s celebrated result (
1.2
) to every
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twist of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
, where
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. Our proof relies crucially on the group structure of
\mathfrak{F}_{1}
. We substantially improve (
1.6
) in the
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twist aspect, but the dependence on
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
is no longer effective.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
=\Cr{ZFR2}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0
such that if
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then

(1.7)

|L(\sigma,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|\geq\Cr{ZFR2}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon},\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR2}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon}.

Remark
.

By replacing
\chi
with
\chi|\cdot|^{it}
, the bound (
1.7
) becomes

(1.8)

|L(\sigma+it,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|\geq\Cr{ZFR2}C(it,\chi)^{-\varepsilon},\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR2}C(it,\chi)^{-\varepsilon},

where
C(it,\chi)\ll C(\chi)(|t|+1)^{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
. In particular, there exists an ineffective constant
=\Cr{C777}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0
such that

|L(\sigma+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|\geq\Cr{C777}(|t|+1)^{-\varepsilon},\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{C777}(|t|+1)^{-\varepsilon}.

This
t

on

\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n^{\prime}}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n+n^{\prime}}
. See

and its proof for details.

2.
Two applications

2.1.
Generalizing the Siegel–Walfisz theorem

Let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
F
, and define the norm
\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
on the nonzero ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
by
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}=|\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a}|
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a nonzero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, and let
I(\mathfrak{q})
be the group of fractional ideals that are coprime to
\mathfrak{q}
. Let
P(\mathfrak{q})
be the subgroup of
I(\mathfrak{q})
consisting of principal fractional ideals
(\alpha)
such that
\alpha
is totally positive and
\alpha\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,\mathfrak{q})
. The narrow ray class group
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})
is the finite abelian quotient
I(\mathfrak{q})/P(\mathfrak{q})
. Let
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})
be the set of primitive ray class characters that induce the characters of
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})
.

If there exists
u\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
satisfies
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}\otimes|\cdot|^{iu}
, then we define
\mathcal{M}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x)=x^{1-iu}/(1-iu)
. For all other pairs
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, we define
\mathcal{M}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x)=0
. We also define
\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})
by the Dirichlet series identity

(2.1)

\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{s}}=-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}),\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

For a class
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})
and
x\geq 2
, we define

(2.2)

\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})-\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})|}\sum_{\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})}\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{C})\mathcal{M}_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(x).

The bound
\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})=o(x)
is equivalent to the fact that if
\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
, then
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\neq 0
.

Unconditionally, Brumley’s narrow zero-free region (
1.6
) suffices to prove that there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{Brumley_{P}NTAP1}(n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}])>0
such that if
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}\leq(\log x)^{\Cr{Brumley_{P}NTAP1}}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}x(\log x)^{-\Cr{Brumley_{P}NTAP1}}
. Before the present work, the bound
\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},A}x(\log x)^{-A}
for all
A>0
was only known in limited cases, as a consequence of the zero-free regions proved in
[
9
,
11
,
10
,
18
]
. We prove the following result using
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
,
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
,
x\geq 2
, and
A>0
. For a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, let
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})
be the associated ray class group, and let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})
. If
\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})
is given by (
2.2
) and
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}\leq(\log x)^{A}
, then

\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},A}x(\log x)^{-A}.

The implied constant is ineffective.

Remark
.

Let
a,q\geq 1
be integers such that
\gcd(a,q)=1
, and let
\varphi(q)
be Euler’s totient function. If
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
q
is coprime to the conductors of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq x\\
m\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(m)=\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x)}{\varphi(q)}+O_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},A}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{A}}\right),\qquad q\leq(\log x)^{A}

by
Theorem

2.1
. This generalizes the Siegel–Walfisz theorem
[
36
,
39
]
.

2.2.
Holomorphy and nonvanishing of symmetric power
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
. Let
v
be a place of
F
, and let
F_{v}
be the completion of
F
relative to
v
. We express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth, admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})
. If
n\geq 2
, then
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
is the
(n+1)
-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on symmetric tensors of rank
n
. Let
\varphi_{v}
be the two-dimensional representation of the Deligne–Weil group attached to
\pi_{v}
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi_{v})
be the smooth admissible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(F_{v})
attached to the representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\circ\varphi_{v}
. By the local Langlands correspondence,
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi_{v})
is well-defined for every place
v
of
F
. Writing

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{v}L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{2}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}}

for the
L
-function of
\pi
, we define

L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n})=\prod_{v}L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi_{v}))\doteq\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}}\prod_{m=0}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{m}\alpha_{2,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{n-m}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}}.

Here,
\doteq
means that we have suppressed the more complicated Euler factors at the prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
dividing the conductor
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
of
\pi
.

Langlands conjectured that
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi)=\bigotimes_{v}\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi_{v})
is an automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. In settings where the conjecture is proved, we write
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi))
instead of
L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n})
. As of now, the conjecture is proved for
n\leq 4

[
3
,
20
,
22
]
, in which case there exists a least integer
r_{n,\pi}\geq 0
such that
(s+int_{\pi}-1)^{r_{n,\pi}}L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi))
is entire. Moreover, there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{symm_{s}td}(n,[F:\mathbb{Q}])>0
such that
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi))
has at most one zero
\rho=\beta-int_{\pi}
, necessarily simple with real part
\beta<1
, in the region

(2.3)

\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1-\Cr{symm_{s}td}/\log(C(\pi^{*})(|\mathrm{Im}(s)+nt_{\pi}|+3)).

Kim and Shahidi
[
21
]
used the results of
[
3
,
20
,
22
]
, Rankin–Selberg theory, and the Clebsch–Gordan identities to prove that if
5\leq n\leq 8
, then there exists a least integer
r_{n,\pi}\geq 0
such that
(s+int_{\pi}-1)^{r_{n,\pi}}L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n})
is holomorphic and nonvanishing in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. The region of holomorphy and nonvanishing can be widened inside the critical strip using Brumley’s narrow zero-free region (
1.6
), and the region will depend effectively on
\pi
.

Using
Theorem

1.1
, we establish an even larger region of holomorphy and nonvanishing, albeit with ineffective dependence on
\pi
. We also accommodate
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twists. In keeping with the notation in
[
21
]
, if
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then we write

\displaystyle L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\otimes\chi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{v}L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi_{v})\otimes\chi_{v})

\displaystyle\doteq\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}}\prod_{m=0}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{m}\alpha_{2,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})^{n-m}\chi(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}}

for the twisted
n
-th symmetric power
L
-function of
\pi
. If
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi)
is an automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, then we write
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi)\otimes\chi)
instead.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(\pi,\chi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
. Let
n\leq 8
, and let
r_{n,\pi,\chi}\geq 0
be the least integer such that the function

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\otimes\chi)=\left(\frac{s+int_{\pi}+it_{\chi}-1}{s+int_{\pi}+it_{\chi}+1}\right)^{r_{n,\pi,\chi}}L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\otimes\chi)

holomorphically continues to
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
=\Cr{Sym}(\pi,\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that if
\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{Sym}^{-1}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon}
, then
\mathcal{L}(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\otimes\chi)
is holomorphic at
s=\sigma
and satisfies

\Cr{Sym}^{-1}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon}\leq|\mathcal{L}(\sigma,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\otimes\chi)|\leq\Cr{Sym}C(\chi)^{\varepsilon}.

This is the strongest known region of holomorphy and nonvanishing for general fixed
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
. Several remarks are in order. First, in
Theorem

2.2
, both the lower bound and the upper bound rely on
Theorem

1.1
. In particular, one cannot deduce the upper bound solely from convexity (see
Lemma

3.2
). Second, for certain
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
, much more is known. For example, if
F
is totally real and
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{2}
corresponds with a primitive holomorphic non-CM Hilbert cusp form over
F
of weights
k_{v}\geq 2
for

, then
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{n+1}
holds for all
n\geq 1
by the recent work of Newton and Thorne
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
. Hence, if
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then
L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi)\otimes\chi)
is entire and has the zero-free region (
2.3
). Third, as a corollary of the aforementioned work of Gelbart and Lapid
[
4
, Corollary 2]
, there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{Sym9}(\pi)>0
such that if
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

|L(1+it,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{9})|\geq(|t|+3)^{-\Cr{Sym9}}.

Our methods cannot say anything about the holomorphy or nonvanishing of
L(s,\pi,\mathrm{Sym}^{9})
without first knowing that
\mathrm{Sym}^{5}(\pi)
is automorphic.

Let
F

\mathbb{A}_{F}
. Let
D_{F}
be the absolute discriminant of
F
,
\mathcal{O}_{F}
the ring of integers of
F
, and
\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
the norm defined on nonzero ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
by
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}=|\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a}|
. For a place
v
of
F
, let
v\mid\infty
(resp.
v\nmid\infty
) denote that
v
is archimedean (resp. non-archimedean), and let
F_{v}
be the corresponding completion of
F
. Each

corresponds with a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}

3.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
whose central character
\omega_{\pi}
is unitary, and let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
be the subset of those
\pi
’s for which
\omega_{\pi}
is trivial on the diagonally embedded positive reals. Every
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
can be written uniquely as

(3.1)

\pi=\pi^{*}\otimes|\cdot|^{it_{\pi}},\qquad\pi^{*}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*},\quad t_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}.

If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
, then for each place
v
, there exists an irreducible admissible representation
\pi_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
, with
\pi_{v}
ramified for at most finitely many
v
, such that
\pi
is a restricted tensor product
\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}
. When
v\nmid\infty
and
\mathfrak{p}
corresponds with
v
, then we write
\pi_{v}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
interchangeably. For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, there exist
n
Satake parameters
\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p}),\dotsc,\alpha_{n,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathbb{C}
such that the standard
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
of
\pi
is the absolutely convergent Euler product

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

For
v\mid\infty
, define

\Gamma_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$.}\end{cases}

There exist
n
Langlands parameters
\mu_{1,\pi}(v),\dotsc,\mu_{n,\pi}(v)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)).

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
denote the conductor of
\pi
, and let
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{*}
denote the trivial representation (whose
L
-function is the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{F}(s)
). Let
\delta_{\pi}=1
if
\pi=\mathbbm{1}
, and
\delta_{\pi}=0
otherwise. If

L_{\infty}(s,\pi)=\prod_{v\mid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}),

then the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(1-s))^{\delta_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})^{s/2}L_{\infty}(s,\pi)L(s,\pi)

is entire of order
1
. There exists
W(\pi)\in\mathbb{C}
of modulus
1
such that
\Lambda(s,\pi)
satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi})=W(\pi)\overline{\Lambda(1-\overline{s},\pi)}.

The nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi)
are the zeros of
\Lambda(s,\pi)
, and they lie in the critical strip
0<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1
. It is conjectured (GRH) that they actually lie on the critical line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=1/2
. Finally, the analytic conductor is
C(\pi)=C(0,\pi)
, where

C(it,\pi)=D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(|\mu_{j,\pi}(v)+it|+3)^{[F_{v}:\mathbb{R}]}.

More generally, these quantities are defined for all
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, and we have in particular

L(s,\pi)=L(s+it_{\pi},\pi^{*}),\qquad C(it,\pi)=C(it+it_{\pi},\pi^{*}).

3.2.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}^{*}
. For each
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}
, there exist complex numbers
\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})
with
1\leq j\leq n
and
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq n
such that if

L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}$,}\\
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}(1-\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s})^{-1}&\mbox{if $\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}$,}\end{cases}

then the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
equals the absolutely convergent product

(3.2)

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

By
[
19
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have the bound

(3.3)

|\lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})|\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{a})\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})}.

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}
be the conductor of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
. For each
v\mid\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
, and
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq n^{\prime}
, there exists a Langlands parameter
\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)
such that if

(3.4)

L_{\infty}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)),\quad\delta_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

then the completed
L
-function

(3.5)

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=(s(1-s))^{\delta_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}}(D_{F}^{nn^{\prime}}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}})^{s/2}L_{\infty}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})

is entire of order 1. There exists
W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}
of modulus
1
such that
\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
satisfies the functional equation

(3.6)

\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})=W(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\overline{\Lambda(1-\overline{s},\pi\times\pi^{\prime})}.

The absolute convergence of (
3.2
) ensures that
\mathrm{Re}(\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v))\geq-1
. The nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
are the zeros of
\Lambda(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
, and they lie in the critical strip
0<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1
. It is conjectured (GRH) that they actually lie on the critical line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=1/2
. Finally, the analytic conductor is
C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=C(0,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
, where

(3.7)

C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=D_{F}^{nn^{\prime}}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}(|\mu_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(v)+it|+3)^{[F_{v}:\mathbb{R}]}.

We have the bounds
[
9
, Lemma A.2]

(3.8)

C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{n,n^{\prime}}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})(|t|+1)^{nn^{\prime}[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\ll_{n,n^{\prime}}C(\pi)^{n^{\prime}}C(\pi^{\prime})^{n}(|t|+1)^{nn^{\prime}[F:\mathbb{Q}]}.

More generally, for
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, we have

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=L(s+it_{\pi}+it_{\pi^{\prime}},\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*}),\quad C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=C(it+it_{\pi}+it_{\pi^{\prime}},\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*}).

3.3.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}

3.4.
Isobaric sums

The Langlands theory of Eisenstein series associates to any
\ell
-tuple

(\pi_{1},\dotsc,\pi_{\ell})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n_{1}}\times\dotsb\times\mathfrak{F}_{n_{\ell}}
an automorphic representation of

\Pi=\pi_{1}\boxplus\dotsb\boxplus\pi_{\ell}
. The contragredient is
\widetilde{\Pi}=\widetilde{\pi}_{1}\boxplus\dotsb\boxplus\widetilde{\pi}_{\ell}

Given two isobaric sums
\Pi=\pi_{1}\boxplus\dotsb\boxplus\pi_{\ell}
and
\Pi^{\prime}=\pi_{1}^{\prime}\boxplus\dotsb\boxplus\pi_{m}^{\prime}
, we define the Rankin–Selberg
L

L(s,\Pi\times\Pi^{\prime})=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{k=1}^{m}L(s,\pi_{j}\times\pi_{k}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi\times\Pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})}{\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

We let
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
denote the set of isobaric automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
8
, Lemma a]
)
.

If
\Pi\in\mathfrak{A}_{n}
, then
L(s,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})
has nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients, as do
\log L(s,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})
and
-L^{\prime}(s,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})/L(s,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})
.

3.5.
Convexity

Our proofs require strong bounds for Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions and their derivatives.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, consider the holomorphic function

(3.11)

\mathcal{L}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\left(\frac{s+it_{\pi}+it_{\pi^{\prime}}-1}{s+it_{\pi}+it_{\pi^{\prime}}+1}\right)^{\delta_{\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*}}}L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}),\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>-1.

If
j\geq 0
,
\sigma\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, then

(3.12)

\mathcal{L}^{(j)}(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],j,\varepsilon}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\max(1-\sigma,0)/2+\varepsilon}.

In particular, if
\delta_{\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*}}=0
or
|t_{\pi}+t_{\pi^{\prime}}|>1
, then

L^{(j)}(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],j,\varepsilon}C(\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\max(1-\sigma,0)/2+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Using the notation
t=t_{\pi}+t_{\pi^{\prime}}
, we can rewrite the bound (
3.12
) as

(3.13)

\mathcal{L}^{(j)}(\sigma+it,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*})\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],j,\varepsilon}C(it,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*})^{\max(1-\sigma,0)/2+\varepsilon},

where

\mathcal{L}(s,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*})=\left(\frac{s-1}{s+1}\right)^{\delta_{\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*}}}L(s,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*}),\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>-1.

We shall prove (
3.13
) for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and at this point we relabel
(\pi^{*},\pi^{\prime*})
as
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
for notational simplicity. That is, we shall assume
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}^{*}
for the rest of this proof.

Without loss of generality,
0<\varepsilon<1
. It suffices to show for all
\sigma\geq-\varepsilon/2
that

(3.14)

\mathcal{L}(\sigma+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon}C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\max(1-\sigma,0)/2+3\varepsilon/4}.

Indeed, assuming this statement and using the bound

|\mathcal{L}^{(j)}(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|\leq j!(2/\varepsilon)^{j}\sup_{|z-s|=\varepsilon/2}|\mathcal{L}(z,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|

that follows from Cauchy’s formula and the triangle inequality for complex line integrals, we readily obtain the required conclusion (for all
j\geq 0
,
\sigma\geq 0
, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
).

It remains to prove (
3.14
) for all
\sigma\geq-\varepsilon/2
. Assume first that
\sigma\geq 1+\varepsilon/2
. It follows from
(
3.3
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

|L(\sigma+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|\leq(L(\sigma,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})L(\sigma,\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}))^{1/2}.

The right-hand side is decreasing in
\sigma
; hence, by
[
24
, Theorem 2]
we infer that

\log|L(\sigma+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})|\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon}\frac{\log C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})}{\log\log C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})}+\frac{\log C(\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})}{\log\log C(\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime})}.

Then, using
[
37
, Lemma 2.1]
, we conclude that

(3.15)

L(\sigma+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon}C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{\varepsilon/4}.

Concerning the application of
[
37
, Lemma 2.1]
, three remarks are in order. First, this result is meant for

F=\mathbb{Q}
, but a generalization to an arbitrary number field
F
is straightforward (with extra constants depending on
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
in the exponents). Second,
[
37
]
tacitly assumes that each central character is trivial on the diagonally embedded positive reals, but this assumption is not used in the proof of
[
37
, Lemma 2.1]
. Third, we rely on the fact that
C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
in (
3.15
) equals
C(\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes|\cdot|^{it}))
.

Finally, we verify (
3.14
) in the strip
-\varepsilon/2\leq\sigma<1+\varepsilon/2
. We express the boundary value
L(-\varepsilon/2+it,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
from
L(1+\varepsilon/2-it,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
using the functional equation (
3.6
) and the definitions (
3.4
)–(
3.5
). Then, applying
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
and recalling (
3.7
), we obtain from (
3.15
) the bound

(3.16)

L(-\varepsilon/2+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon}C(it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})^{1/2+3\varepsilon/4}.

We interpolate between (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) using the Phragmén–Lindelöf principle, and the lemma follows.
∎

3.6.
A weak Brun–Titchmarsh bound

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. Recall the Dirichlet series definition of
\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})
in (
2.1
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. There exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{BTrange}(n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}])>0
such that if

X\geq\max(C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}),C(\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}))^{\Cr{BTrange}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1\leq T\leq X^{1/(16\max(n,n^{\prime})^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}])},

then

\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq Xe^{1/T}}|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})|\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\frac{X}{T}.

Proof.

By
[
37
, Proposition A.1]
, we have the bound

(3.17)

|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})|\leq\sqrt{\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{a})\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})}.

Combined with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
3.17
) yields

(3.18)

\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq Xe^{1/T}}|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})|\leq\left(\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq Xe^{1/T}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{a})\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq Xe^{1/T}}\Lambda_{\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})\right)^{1/2}.

By
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
, there exist absolute and effectively computable constants
>0
and
>0
such that if
\log\log C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})\geq\Cr{BT1}n^{4}[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}
,
X\geq e^{\Cr{BT2}n^{4}[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{2}}C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})^{32n^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
, and
1\leq T\leq X^{1/(16n^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}])}
, then

\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq Xe^{1/T}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{a})\ll n^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}]\frac{X}{T}.

A careful inspection of the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
shows if the lower bound on
C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
is removed, then the dependency on
n
and
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
in the implied constant and the range of
X
become worse, which is not a concern here. We conclude that there exists an effectively computable constant
\Cr{BTrange}=\Cr{BTrange}(n,[F:\mathbb{Q}])>0
such that if
X\geq C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})^{\Cr{BTrange}}
and
1\leq T\leq X^{1/(16n^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}])}
, then

\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq Xe^{1/T}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}}(\mathfrak{a})\ll_{n,[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\frac{X}{T}.

To finish, we similarly bound the second factor on the right-hand side of (
3.18
).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\chi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
. If

X\geq 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad X\geq Y\geq X^{1-1/(16\max(n,n^{\prime})^{2}[F:\mathbb{Q}])},

then

(3.19)

\sum_{X<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq X+Y}|\Lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(\mathfrak{a})|\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}Y.

Proof.

We define
X_{0}=\max(C(\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}),C(\pi^{\prime}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime}))^{\Cr{BTrange}}
. If
X\geq X_{0}
, then (
3.19
) follows from
Lemma

3.3
upon setting

. If
X<X_{0}
, then (
3.19
) follows from (
3.17
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
∎

4.
Overall strategy

Our proof of
Theorem

1.1
rests on the following key proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\chi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, and
\beta\in(1-\varepsilon/8,1)
.
Assume that the following
L
-functions are entire:

(4.1)

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}),\qquad L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)),\qquad L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi^{2})).

If
L(\beta,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=0
, then

(4.2)

|L(1,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|\gg_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},\beta,\varepsilon}C(\chi)^{-(n+n^{\prime})^{2}\varepsilon}.

Assuming
Proposition

4.1
, we will prove that, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, there exists an ineffective constant
=\Cr{ZFR3}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0
such that for any
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
we have

(4.3)

|L(1,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|\geq\Cr{ZFR3}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon}.

After this,
Theorem

1.1
follows in a routine way (see
Section

7
). Let us define

(4.4)

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\varepsilon}{8(n+n^{\prime})^{2}}.

By a standard argument (see
Section

6.1
), we only need to prove (
4.3
)
in the case when
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
has a zero in the half-plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1-\varepsilon^{\prime}
.

It is instructive to first consider the case
n\neq n^{\prime}
of (
4.3
). Indeed, in this case, the
L
-functions in (
4.1
) are automatically entire. Moreover, by the “existence of bad zero” hypothesis, there exists
(\beta,\psi)\in(1-\varepsilon^{\prime},1)\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
depending at most on
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)
such that

(4.5)

L(\beta,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\psi))=0.

Writing
\pi^{\prime\prime}=\pi^{\prime}\otimes\psi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
and
\chi^{\prime}=\overline{\psi}\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, we apply
Proposition

4.1
with

(\pi,\pi^{\prime\prime},\chi^{\prime},8\varepsilon^{\prime})
in the role of
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\chi,\varepsilon)
. By
n\neq n^{\prime}
and (
4.5
), all the assumptions of
Proposition

4.1
are satisfied, hence we conclude that

|L(1,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|=|L(1,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime\prime}\otimes\chi^{\prime}))|\gg_{\pi,\pi^{\prime\prime},\beta,\varepsilon}C(\chi^{\prime})^{-\varepsilon}.

This implies (
4.3
) readily, since
\pi^{\prime\prime}
(resp.
\chi^{\prime}
) differs from
\pi^{\prime}
(resp.
\chi
) by a twist of
\psi
, and both
\psi
and
\beta
depend only on
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)
.

If
n=n^{\prime}
, then we must account for the possible poles of the
L
-functions in (
4.1
) when applying
Proposition

4.1
. Keeping this in mind, we shall prove (
4.3
) in three steps. Each step relies on an application of

Proposition

4.1
and allows us to verify (
4.3
) for a larger
subgroup
of characters
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}

is entire. At each application of

, we

5.
Establishing the key proposition

The goal of this section is to prove

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
and
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. There are finitely many
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{*}
such that
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
has a pole.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}^{*}
. Choose
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{*}
such that
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
has a pole. Then
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi=\widetilde{\pi}
, and
n=n^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\chi
can only ramify where
\pi
or
\pi^{\prime}
ramifies. Moreover,
\chi^{n}
is uniquely determined by the equation

\omega_{\pi^{\prime}}\chi^{n}=\overline{\omega_{\pi}}
. Therefore, it suffices to show that if
S
is a finite set of places containing all archimedean places and

G=\prod_{v\in S}{F_{v}^{\times}}^{n}\prod_{v\not\in S}\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}}^{\times},

then
F^{\times}G
has finite index in
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
. Let
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
be the group of ideles of norm
1
, and let
G^{1}=\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}\cap G
. We have that

\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}/(F^{\times}G)\cong\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}/(F^{\times}G^{1})\cong(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}/F^{\times})/(F^{\times}G^{1}/F^{\times}),

where
G
is open in
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
,
G^{1}
is open in
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
, and
F^{\times}G^{1}/F^{\times}
is open in
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}/F^{\times}
.
As
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}/F^{\times}
is compact, the right-hand side is finite, and so is the left-hand side.
∎

The next lemma prepares the scene for our main residue calculation.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f_{0}(s),\dotsc,f_{m}(s)
be
m+1
complex functions that are holomorphic in an open neighborhood of
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
. If there exists
c\geq 0
such that
|f_{j}(s_{0})|=c
for all
j\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
, then

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=s_{0}}\frac{f_{0}(s)\dotsb f_{m}(s)}{(s-s_{0})^{m}}

equals
c
times a
\mathbb{C}
-linear combination of monomials of the derivative values
f_{j}^{(k)}(s_{0})
for
(j,k)\in\{0,\dotsc,m\}\times\{0,\dotsc,m-1\}
. The monomials in the linear combination, and the modulus of each coefficient in the linear combination depend at most on
m
.

Proof.

Expanding
f_{0}(s),\dotsc,f_{m}(s)
into Taylor series around
s_{0}
, we obtain

\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=s_{0}}\frac{f_{0}(s)\dotsb f_{m}(s)}{(s-s_{0})^{m}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{0},\dotsc,k_{m}\geq 0\\
k_{0}+\dotsb+k_{m}=m-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=0}^{m}\frac{f_{j}^{(k_{j})}(s_{0})}{k_{j}!}.

Since
k_{0}+\dotsb+k_{m}=m-1
, at least one of
k_{1},\dotsc,k_{m}
equals zero. Consequently, the
j
-product above has a factor of
f_{j}(s_{0})
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, and the result follows.
∎

Remark
.

The proof of

relies crucially on the nonnegativity of the Dirichlet coefficients of

. It is instructive to see that in the special case when

and

, the auxiliary

) for Dirichlet

-functions. Moreover, if

and

are two quadratic characters, then for

and

the auxiliary

becomes

The nonnegativity of the Dirichlet coefficients of this

We begin with the straightforward bound

For

). On the other hand, by

), there exists a constant
=\Cr{ZFRN2}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0
such that

Define

=\Cr{ZFRN3}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)=\min\left(\frac{\Cr{ZFR3}}{1+\Cr{ZFRN2}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2nn^{\prime}}\right).

If

\displaystyle|L(\sigma,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|

\displaystyle\geq|L(1,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))|-\Cr{ZFRN3}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon}\cdot\Cr{ZFRN2}C(\chi)^{\varepsilon/2}

\displaystyle\geq(\Cr{ZFR3}-\Cr{ZFRN2}\Cr{ZFRN3})C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon/2}

\displaystyle\geq\Cr{ZFRN3}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon/2}.

Let

,

, and
y=x(\log x)^{-A}

(8.1)

\displaystyle\phi(r)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{(0,x]}(r)+\mathbf{1}_{(x,x+y]}(r)\frac{x+y-r}{y},\qquad r>0;

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(r)r^{s-1}\,dr=\frac{(x+y)^{s+1}-x^{s+1}}{y(s^{2}+s)},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>0.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a})+O\left(\sum_{x<\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq x+y}|\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})|\right).

, the error term above is

Let

be the group of characters of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a})=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})|}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})}}\overline{\psi}(\mathcal{C})\mathcal{\sum}_{\mathfrak{a}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})\psi(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}).

Using (
3.9
) and (

\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})
is the primitive ray class character that induces
\psi
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})\psi(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a})-\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\Lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a})

\displaystyle\ll nn^{\prime}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi^{\prime}}}\sum_{\ell\leq\frac{\log(2x)}{\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}}}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{\ell(\theta_{n}+\theta_{n^{\prime}})}\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}

\displaystyle\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}x^{1-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}-\frac{1}{(n^{\prime})^{2}+1}}(\log x)(\log\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}).

In light of the hypothesis
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}\leq(\log x)^{A}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a})=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})|}\sum_{\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})}\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{C})\sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\Lambda_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a})+O_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},A}(y).

Recall the definition of

\mathcal{E}_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(x;\mathfrak{q},\mathcal{C})=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})|}\sum_{\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{C})}{2\pi i}\int_{3-i\infty}^{3+i\infty}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\,\widehat{\phi}(s)\,ds\\
-\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})|}\sum_{\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})}\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{C})\mathcal{M}_{\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)}(x)+O_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},A}(y).

Define
\varepsilon=1/(3A+2)
. By (
1.8
),
Lemma

3.2
, (
3.8
), and the bound
C(\chi)\ll_{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}

=\Cr{C7777}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0
such that on the piecewise smooth parametric curve

\mathscr{C}(t)=1-\Cr{C7777}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}(|t|+1))^{-\varepsilon}+it,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

there holds

(8.2)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\mathscr{C}(t),\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon}(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}(|t|+1))^{2\varepsilon}.

Note that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}(t)\ll_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon}1

If there exists

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{q})|}\sum_{\chi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{q})}\frac{\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{C})}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathscr{C}}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\,\widehat{\phi}(s)\,ds+O_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},A}(y)

SCOTT MCCULLOUGH, GEORGIOS TSIKALAS

Abstract
. Let ( ℋ k , ℋ ℓ ) (\mathcal{H}_{k},\mathcal{H}_{\ell}) be a pair of Hilbert function spaces with kernels k , ℓ k,\ell . In a 2005 paper, Shimorin showed that a certain factorization condition on ( k , ℓ ) (k,\ell) yields a commutant lifting theorem for multipliers ℋ k → ℋ ℓ \mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell} , thus unifying and extending previous results due to Ball-Trent-Vinnikov and Volberg-Treil. Our main result is a strong converse to Shimorin’s theorem for a large class of holomorphic pairs ( k , ℓ ) , (k,\ell), which leads to a full characterization of the complete Pick property for such pairs. We also present a short alternative proof of sufficiency for Shimorin’s condition. Finally, we establish necessary conditions for abstract pairs ( k , ℓ ) (k,\ell) to satisfy the complete Pick property, further generalizing Shimorin’s work with proofs that are new even in the single-kernel case k = ℓ . k=\ell. Our approach differs from Shimorin’s in that we do not work with the Nevanlinna-Pick problem directly; instead, we are able to extract vital information for ( k , ℓ ) (k,\ell) through Carathéodory-Fejér interpolation.

(ii)

Theorem

Necessary conditions

Despite the negative results of subsection

Theorem 1.9

(

]

If

Given a kernel

Theorem 1.10
.

Let

be a CP pair of kernels on a set

In the special case of a complete Pick kernel, where

2.1.
Vector-valued kernels

Let
X
be a non-empty set. A function

k:X\times X\to\mathbb{C}

is a
positive kernel
or just a
kernel
, denoted
k\succeq 0,
if it is positive semi-definite in the sense that the
n\times n
complex matrix
[k(z_{i},z_{j})]
is positive semi-definite for every choice of points
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in X.
Here we make the standing assumption that our kernels never vanish along the diagonal. The associated reproducing kernel Hilbert space will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{k}
or
\mathcal{H}_{k}(X)
to emphasize the domain, while the algebra of all functions
\phi:X\to\mathbb{C}
that multiply
\mathcal{H}_{k}
into itself will be denoted by
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k})
.

We will often have to work with vector-valued versions of these objects. Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, we regard elements of

\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
as
\mathcal{E}
-valued functions on
X.
It is not hard to see that the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
is equal to
k\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}}
, where
I_{\mathcal{E}}
is the identity operator on
\mathcal{E}
. If
\mathcal{F}
is another Hilbert space and
\ell
is another kernel on
X,
we write
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{F})
to denote the space of all
B(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
-valued functions on
X
that multiply
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
into
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{F}
. A multiplier
\Phi\in\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{F})

is contractive if the corresponding multiplication operator
M_{\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{F}
is contractive. The following characterization of contractive multipliers is standard (see e.g.
[
36
, Theorem 6.28]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k},\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
be reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces on
X.
Fix Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}.
A function
\Phi:X\to B(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is a contractive multiplier in
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{F})
if and only if

\ell(z,w)I_{\mathcal{F}}-k(z,w)\Phi(z)\Phi(w)^{*}\succeq 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ on }X\times X

if and only if the operator
T^{*}
densely defined by

T^{*}(\ell_{w}\otimes v)=k_{w}\otimes\Phi(w)^{*}v

extends to be a contractive operator from
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E}
, in which case
T^{*}=M^{*}_{\Phi.}

The following result will be used repeatedly in the sequel, usually without special mention. Its proof follows from the definition of positivity.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
X
. If
\delta:X\to\mathbb{C}
does not vanish, then the function

\widetilde{k}(z,w):=\delta(z)\overline{\delta(w)}k(z,w)

is a positive kernel on
X.
Moreover, given any Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F},
we have

\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{k}}\otimes\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{k}}\otimes\mathcal{F})

isometrically.

We also record the following well-known maximum modulus principle for multipliers. For the readers convenience we provide a proof.

Lemma 2.3

(Maximum Modulus for multipliers)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
X
such that
k
is non-vanishing. Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, assume the row-valued multiplier
\Phi\in\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H}_{k})
is contractive. If there exists
z\in X
such that
\|\Phi(z)\|=1,
then
\Phi
is constant.
In particular, if
B
is a positive kernel on
X
and
(I-B(z,w))k(z,w)
is a (positive) kernel, then either
|B(z,w)|<1
for all
z,w\in X

of
B(z,w)=1
for all
z,w\in X.

Proof.

Observe, for
u\in X,
that the operator
\Phi(u)^{*}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{E}
is naturally identified with an element of
\mathcal{E}

and if
v
is also in
X,
then

\langle k_{u}\otimes\Phi(u)^{*},k_{v}\otimes\Phi(v)^{*}\rangle=\langle\Phi(u)^{*},\Phi(v)^{*}\rangle\,k(u,v).

Since
M_{\Phi}^{*}
is a contraction, the function
F:X\times X\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

F(u,v)=(1-\Phi(v)\Phi(u)^{*})k(u,v)=(1-\langle\Phi(u)^{*},\Phi(v)^{*}\rangle)k(u,v)

is a kernel by Lemma
2.1
. By assumption
F(z,z)=0.
By positivity,
F(z,w)=0
for all
w\in X.
Thus
\langle\Phi(z)^{*},\Phi(w)^{*}\rangle=1.

Since also
\|\Phi(z)^{*}\|=1\geq\|\Phi(w)^{*}\|,
we conclude
\Phi(w)=\Phi(z).

To prove the last statement, by standard reproducing kernel Hilbert space considerations, there exists an auxiliarly Hilbert space

\mathscr{E}
and a function
\Phi:X\to\mathscr{E}
such that
B(z,w)=\Phi(z)\Phi(w)^{*}.
By assumption
(I-\Phi(z)\Phi(w)^{*})k(z,w)\succeq 0

and thus
\Phi
is a contractive multiplier by Lemma
2.1
. Hence the last part of the lemma follows from the first part.
∎

Finally, we will also make repeated use of a simple formula for the reproducing kernels of zero-based invariant subspaces.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on

and fix a point

The reproducing kernel

of the subspace

2.2.
The complete Pick property

A reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{k}
on a set
X
is said to be
irreducible
if
X
cannot be partitioned into two non-empty disjoint sets
X_{1},X_{2}
so that
k(z_{1},z_{2})=0
for all
z_{1}\in X_{1}
and
z_{2}\in X_{2}.
The kernel of an irreducible complete Pick space satisfies
k(z,w)\neq 0
for all
z,w\in X,
see
[
2
, Lemma 1.1]
. By the Agler-McCarthy universality theorem
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
, the space
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is an irreducible complete Pick space if and only if there exist a function
\delta:X\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, an integer
1\leq d\leq\infty
and a function
b:X\to\mathbb{B}_{d}
, where
\mathbb{B}_{d}

denotes the open unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, so that

k(z,w)=\frac{\delta(z)\overline{\delta(w)}}{1-\langle b(z),b(w)\rangle},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }z,w\in X.

(2.1)

Equivalently (see
[
2
]
),
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is an irreducible complete Pick space if and only if
k
is non-vanishing and there exists
z\in X
such that

\frac{k^{z}}{k}\succeq 0.

(2.2)

We note that, in view of Lemma
2.2
, one may rescale any irreducible complete Pick kernel
k
so that
\delta\equiv 1
in (
2.1
). Also, given any
z_{0}\in X
, one may choose

\delta(z)=\frac{\sqrt{k(z_{0},z_{0})}}{k(z,z_{0})},

so that
k(z,z_{0})=1
for all
z\in X.

The study of general complete Pick kernels can be reduced to the study of irreducible ones.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Every complete Pick space can be decomposed as an orthogonal
direct sum of irreducible complete Pick spaces. Explicitly, if
s
is a complete
Pick kernel on a nonempty set

then there is a unique partition

such that for each
i
the function
s|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
is non-vanishing and
for each
i\neq j
the function
s|_{X_{i}\times X_{j}}
is identically
0.

Complete Pick kernels actually satisfy a stronger positivity condition than (
2.2
). Given two kernels
\ell,k
on a set

we will say that

is a factor of

if there exists a kernel
g
on
X
such that

.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
19
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
\ell
be a kernel with an irreducible complete Pick factor

If

is a
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell})
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell},
and

is the reproducing kernel for

then

\frac{\ell_{M}}{s}\succeq 0.

2.3.
Diagonal holomorphic kernels

Given
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{g}},
let

z\overline{w}=(z_{1}\overline{w_{1}},z_{2}\overline{w_{2}},\cdots,z_{\mathsf{g}}\overline{w_{\mathsf{g}}}).

Recall, from the introduction, that a kernel

in its interior is

The diagonal holomorphic kernel

\Omega_{f}
as described in equation (

) and thus it gives rise to the reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces

and

Among other things, Lemma

below says these two spaces, as well as their spaces of multipliers, are canonically isometrically isomorphic. Indeed, throughout this paper, we will often have to work with restrictions of holomorphic kernels to open subsets of their domain of convergence. Fortunately, no important information is lost by doing so.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
k
be a holomorphic kernel on a domain
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{g}}.
If
\Omega\subseteq\Delta
is a non-trivial open subset, then the restriction mapping

\iota:f\mapsto f|_{\Omega}

unitarily onto
\mathcal{H}_{k}(\Omega).
Moreover,
\iota
is an isometric isomorphism from
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}(\Delta))
onto
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}(\Omega)).

Proof.

This result follows from combining the Identity Principle with basic facts regarding restrictions of reproducing kernels; see
[
36
, Section 5.4]
.
∎

The following proposition says more is true for a diagonal holomorphic kernel

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose

Lemma 2.9

y_{j}=tq_{j}+(1-t)p_{j}
for

y_{j}=tq_{j}\neq 0
for

Suppose

Thus,

\frac{\eta}{2}\geq\epsilon>|p_{j}-q_{j}|=\frac{|p_{j}|}{t}\geq\eta,

a contradiction. Hence all the coordinates of

Another application of Lemma

to

says that the polydisc

Let

denote the power series, for

In particular, as power series,

It follows that

is contained in the domain of convergence of

Since

the function

contains the polydisc

∎

The following lemma is used in the proof of Proposition

.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let

If

for all

then

Proof.

Let

The domain of convergence of the power series

is,
by definition,
\Omega_{f}.
By assumption,
\Omega_{f}
contains a neighborhood of the

is open and star-like with respect to the origin. Since

defines a holomorphic function on

the set
\Omega_{f}^{1}

is open and contains
\mathbf{0}.
Moreover,
\Omega_{f}^{1}
is the intersection
of two sets that are star-like with respect to
\mathbf{0},
namely
\Omega_{f}
(
[
30
, Proposition 2.3.15]
)
and
\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{g}}:\sum f_{a}|x|^{2a}<1\}.
Hence,

is

∎

Informed by Proposition

, we conclude this subsection with the following observation.
A normalized holomorphic diagonal kernel

can also
be understood as follows. A point

in the Hilbert space

Thus we obtain a mapping

and, if

then

\langle E_{w},E_{z}\rangle=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}}f_{a}z^{a}\overline{w^{a}}=f(z,w).

(2.3)

From standard facts about reproducing kernels, the monomials

form

2.4.
The complete Carathéodory property

As we saw in Section
1
, the classical Carathéodory problem asks, given
c_{0},\dots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{C},
does there exist
an analytic function
g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that the function

f=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\overline{c}_{j}z^{j}+z^{n+1}g

satisfies
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}?
From an operator-theoretic viewpoint,
there is a natural necessary condition that, when combined with Parrott’s Lemma,
can be seen to be sufficient. A function
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
determines an operator
M_{f}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

defined by
M_{f}h=fh
for
h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and

\|M_{f}\|\leq 1.
The subspace
M_{n}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
spanned by the orthonormal
set
\{1,z,\dots,z^{n}\}
is invariant
for
M_{f}^{*}
and moreover, the matrix representation of
M_{f}^{*}
with respect to this basis is

C_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\dots&c_{n-1}&c_{n}\\
0&c_{0}&c_{1}&\dots&c_{n-2}&c_{n-1}\\
0&0&c_{1}&\dots&c_{n-3}&c_{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&c_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus,
\|C_{n}\|\leq 1
is a necessary condition for a solution to the Carathéodory problem.
While not the original proof,
sufficiency can be proved using the following version of the Parrott Lemma (see, for instance,
[
2
, Lemma B1]
).

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
p,q
and
m,n
are positive integers such that
p+q=m+n,

(a)

A\in M_{p,m};

(b)

C\in M_{q,m};

(c)

D\in M_{q,n};

such that

\big\|\begin{pmatrix}A\\
C\end{pmatrix}\big\|,\ \ \big\|\begin{pmatrix}C&D\end{pmatrix}\big\|\leq 1,

then there exists a matrix
B\in M_{p,n}
such that

\big\|\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\big\|\leq 1.

With
c_{n+1}
to be determined, consider the following partition of the matrix
C_{n+1},

C_{n+1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc|c}c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\dots&c_{n-1}&c_{n}&c_{n+1}\\
\hline\cr 0&c_{0}&c_{1}&\dots&c_{n-2}&c_{n-1}&c_{n}\\
0&0&c_{1}&\dots&c_{n-3}&c_{n-2}&c_{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&c_{0}&c_{1}\\
0&0&0&\dots 0&0&0&c_{0}\end{array}\right).

Since
\|C_{n}\|\leq 1,

\big\|\begin{pmatrix}C_{n}\\
0\end{pmatrix}\big\|,\ \ \big\|\begin{pmatrix}0&C_{n}\end{pmatrix}\big\|\,\leq 1.

Thus, by Parrott’s Lemma there is a choice of
c_{n+1}
such that

\|C_{n+1}\|\leq 1.
An induction argument now produces
c_{m}
such
that
\|C_{m}\|\leq 1,
for all
m
, which in turn implies, with
f=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\overline{c}_{j}z^{j},

that the restriction of
M_{f}^{*}
to the subspaces
M_{m}
is a contraction. Thus, we obtain an
f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\overline{c_{j}}z^{j}
such that
M_{f}^{*}
defines an
operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
of norm at most one. It is also not hard to see that
M_{f}^{*}\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}=\overline{f(\lambda)}\,\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda},
where
\mathfrak{s}
is Szego’s kernel
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

Hence
\|f(\lambda)\|\leq 1
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, as desired.
Note that the argument goes through unchanged if the
c_{j}
’s are
replaced by
J\times J
matrices for any positive integer
J.

We highlight the fact that this argument proceeds in two parts.
There is the
matrix completion problem
: Starting with the contraction
C_{n},

find
c_{m}
for
m>n
such that
C_{m}
is a contraction for each
m.
And there
is the
function-theoretic operator theory interpretation
:
the assumption that each
C_{m}
is a contraction
implies that the power series
\sum\overline{c}_{j}z^{j}
defines a function

f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}.
Equivalently,
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
and
M_{f}^{*}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

is a contraction.

In the remainder of this section, we consider the complete Carathéodory property for a pair of kernels
(k,\ell),

where
k
and
\ell
are (normalized) diagonal holomorphic. First, we will restate the definition given in the introduction. Given
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}},
we will write
a\leq b
to mean
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,{\mathsf{g}}\}.

Definition 2.13
.

A non-empty set
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}
is
co-invariant

if
\{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}:a\leq b\}\subseteq F
for each
b\in F.
∎

Thus, if
F
is co-invariant, then
F
contains
(0,\dots,0)=\mathbf{0}.

Definition 2.14
.

A pair
(k,\ell)
of (normalized) diagonal holomorphic kernels
is
a
complete Carathéodory pair
if, for any positive integer
J
, any finite co-invariant set of indices
\varnothing\neq F\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}
and any collection of matrix coefficients
\{c_{a}:a\in F\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{J}
, the positivity of
the block upper-triangular matrix
C
indexed by
F\times F
with block
J\times J
entries

C_{a,b}=\begin{cases}c_{b-a}\sqrt{\cfrac{k_{a}}{\ell_{b}}},\hskip 14.22636ptb\geq a,\\
\mathbf{0},\hskip 29.87547pt\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2.4)

is equivalent to the existence of a collection

\{c_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}\setminus F\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{J}

such that the function

\Phi(z)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}}z^{a}\otimes c_{a}^{*}

(2.5)

is a contractive multiplier from
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}.
In the special case
k=\ell,
the kernel

is known as a
complete Carathéodory kernel
.
∎

Just as in the classical Carathéodory problem above, it is possible to reformulate Definition
2.14
so that it does not involve function theory.

Proposition 2.15
.

A pair of diagonal holomorphic kernels
(k,\ell)
is a complete Carathéodory pair if and only if,
if for each
J\in\mathbb{N},
each finite co-invariant
\varnothing\neq F\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
and each collection
\{c_{a}:a\in F\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{J}
such that the block upper-triangular matrix

indexed by
F\times F
with block

)
is a contraction, there exists a collection
\{c_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}\setminus F\}
such that the (infinite) block matrix
\mathscr{C}
indexed by
\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}\times\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
with
\mathscr{C}_{a,b}
entries

\mathscr{C}_{a,b}=\begin{cases}c_{b-a}\sqrt{\cfrac{k_{a}}{\ell_{b}}},\hskip 14.22636ptb\geq a,\\
\mathbf{0},\hskip 29.87547pt\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2.6)

is a contraction.

Remark 2.16
.

Assume
k,\ell
are normalized. By considering
F=\{\mathbf{0}\}
and choosing
\{c_{\mathbf{0}}=1\}
we obtain the
1\times 1

matrix
\Big(c_{\mathbf{0}}\sqrt{\frac{k_{\mathbf{0}}}{\ell_{\mathbf{0}}}}\Big)=1,
which is a contraction. Hence,
assuming that
(k,\ell)
is a CC pair, there exists

for

such that the matrix

in (

)

.
∎

The remainder of this subsection will be devoted to a proof sketch of Proposition
2.15
. We begin with a function-theoretic interpretation of the contractivity of (

). Let

e_{a}(z)=\sqrt{k_{a}}z^{a},\hskip 5.69046ptf_{a}=\sqrt{\ell_{a}}z^{a},\hskip 5.69046pta\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}},

so that
\{e_{a}\}
and
\{f_{a}\}
form orthonormal bases for
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, respectively. Fix
J\in\mathbb{N}
and a co-invariant
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
and let
\mathcal{H}_{k,F}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,F}
denote the subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
spanned by
\{e_{a}:a\in F\}
and
\{f_{a}:a\in F\}
, respectively. Further, assume the collection
\{c_{a}:a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{J}
is such that the function

\Phi(z)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}}z^{a}\otimes c_{a}^{*}

is a multiplier
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}
. A short computation reveals that

M^{*}_{\Phi}(f_{a}\otimes h)=\sum_{u\leq a}\sqrt{\frac{k_{u}}{\ell_{a}}}e_{u}\otimes c_{a-u}h,\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}\text{ and }h\in\mathbb{C}^{J}.

(2.7)

Thus,
M_{\Phi}^{*}
maps
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,F}
into
\mathcal{H}_{k,F}.
Moreover, the matrix representation of the restriction of
M_{\Phi}^{*}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,F}
is given by the matrix of equation (

). Since
M_{\Phi}^{*}
is, by assumption,
a contraction, so is

Next, we will show that if the
\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}\times\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
matrix
\mathscr{C}
given by (
2.6
)
is a contraction, then the function

\Phi(z)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}}z^{a}\otimes c_{a}^{*}

(2.8)

is a contractive multiplier
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}
(the converse follows immediately from our previous argument). First, observe that, since
\mathscr{C}
is a contraction, the
\ell^{2}
-norm of the first row (

) is bounded. Thus,

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}}\frac{\|c_{b}\|^{2}}{\ell_{b}}<\infty.

(2.9)

If

are defined on

then, by Proposition

So,

does actually define a function on

. Now, arguing as earlier in this proof, we see that

defining a contractive operator from

to

. Denote that operator by

. It is then not hard to verify that

for all

3.
Shimorin and Strong Shimorin Certificates

In this section, we will introduce the notion of a Shimorin certificate for a pair of abstract (not necessarily holomorphic) kernels. Our motivation behind Definition
3.1
was to come up with the weakest set of conditions that guarantee the CP property for a general pair
(k,\ell)
. Further, even though having a Shimorin certificate will turn out to be weaker than having a strong Shimorin certificate, we will see that the two properties coincide in many interesting cases. For convenience, we will make the assumption that all pairs
(k,\ell)
consist of kernels that are non-vanishing along the diagonal.

3.1.
Shimorin certificates

In this subsection we define a Shimorin certificate and collect some consequences. Recall that

k^{z}(w,v)=k(w,v)-\cfrac{k(w,z)k(z,v)}{k(z,z)}.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume
k,\ell
are kernels on the non-empty set
X.

A family of kernels
\{p[z]\}_{z\in X}
on
X
is a
Shimorin certificate
for the pair
(k,\ell)
if,
for each
z\in X,

\ell^{z}\succeq p[z]\ell\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ and }\hskip 8.5359ptk^{z}\preceq p[z]k.

(3.1)

Remark 3.2
.

A strong Shimorin certificate is a CP kernel
s
on
X
, while a Shimorin certificate is a family of kernels
\{p[z]\}
all defined on
X
and indexed by
X
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
k,\ell
be kernels on
X.
If
(k,\ell)
has a strong Shimorin certificate, then it also has a Shimorin certificate.

Proof.

Suppose
(k,\ell)
has a strong Shimorin certificate
s.
Thus, there exist kernels
h
and
g
on
X
such that

k=(1-h)s,\hskip 19.91684pt\ell=gs.

By Lemma
2.5
, there exists a decomposition
X=\cup_{i\in I}X_{i}
such that
s_{i}:=s|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
never vanishes,

k(z,w)=\ell(z,w)=s(z,w)=0
for
i\neq j
and
z\in X_{i}
and
w\in X_{j},
and, for each
i,
either
k|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
is non-vanishing or identically
0.

Now, set
\ell_{i}=\ell|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
and
g_{i}=g|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
and observe that, for any
i\in I
and
z\in X_{i}
, we have

\ell^{z}_{i}\succeq\frac{s_{i}^{z}}{s_{i}}\ell_{i}\hskip 11.38092pt\text{ and }\hskip 11.38092ptk^{z}_{i}\preceq\frac{s_{i}^{z}}{s_{i}}k_{i}.

(3.2)

Indeed, after a little computation the first inequality is seen to be equivalent to

\frac{g_{i}(w,z){g_{i}(z,v)}}{g_{i}(z,z)}\preceq g_{i}(w,v),\hskip 8.5359ptw,v\in X_{i},

which holds because
g
is positive. The second inequality in (
3.2
) is trivial if
1-h\equiv 0.
If
1-h
is non-vanishing on
X_{i}\times X_{i},
then, setting
t_{i}=(1-h|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}})^{-1}=s_{i}/k_{i},
the second inequality becomes

\frac{t_{i}^{z}}{t_{i}}\succeq 0,

which holds for all
z\in X_{i}
because
t
is a CP kernel. We now define a Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
. Given
z\in X_{i}
, we set

p[z](w,v)=\begin{cases}\cfrac{s_{i}^{z}(w,v)}{s_{i}(w,v)},\hskip 10.81218pt\text{ if }w,v\in X_{i},\\
1,\hskip 41.25641pt\text{ if }w,v\in X_{j}\text{ with }j\neq i,\\
0,\hskip 42.10995pt\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Combining (
3.2
) with the observation that
k^{z}|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}=k|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
and
\ell^{z}|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}=\ell|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
whenever
z\in X_{j}
with
i\neq j
, we conclude that
\{p[z]\}_{z\in X}
is a Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
and the proof is complete.
∎

The converse of Proposition
3.3
does not hold, as the following example shows.

Example 3.4
.

Given an arbitrary kernel
k
, we can always find a second kernel
\ell
with the property that
(k,\ell)
has a Shimorin certificate. Indeed, choose
\ell
to be a kernel on
X
with
\ell(z,w)=0
whenever
z\neq w.
Now, for any
t\in X,
set

p[t](z,w)=\begin{cases}0\hskip 17.07182pt\text{ if }z=t\text{ or }w=t,\\
1,\hskip 18.49411pt\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
\ell^{t}=p[t]\ell
for all
t
and

p[t](z,w)k(z,w)-k^{t}(z,w)=p[t](z,w)\frac{k(z,t)\,k(t,w)}{k(t,t)}\succeq 0,

\{p[t]\}_{t\in X}
is a Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
.

It is now easy to construct an example of a pair
(k,\ell)
with a Shimorin certificate, but without a strong Shimorin certificate. Let
X=\{1,2,3\}
and let

k=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\
1&2&1\\
0&1&2\end{pmatrix},

so that
k(a,b)
is the
(a,b)
entry of this matrix. Note
k
is positive definite. If
B
and
s
are positive kernels and
k=(1-B)s,
then either
B(1,3)=1
or
s(1,3)=0.
If
s
is a complete Pick kernel and
s(1,3)=0
, then, by Lemma
2.5
, either
s(2,3)=0
or
s(1,2)=0.
Either way one obtains a contradiction, since
k(1,2)\neq 0\neq k(2,3).
If
B(1,3)=1,
then as
B
is positive and
B(1,1),B(3,3)\leq 1
(since
k(1,1),k(3,3),s(1,1),s(3,3)\geq 0
), we obtain
B(1,1)=1
and so
k(1,1)=0,
a contradiction. Thus, choosing an
\ell
such that
(k,\ell)
has a Shimorin certificate, which is possible from the discussion above, gives the desired example.

We close this subsection by recording some basic restrictions that are imposed on a pair of a kernels by the existence of a Shimorin certificate.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose

then

then

(v)

if

\ell(z,w)=0.

(a)

\ell(z,v)=\ell(w,v)=0
;

(b)

either
k(z,v)=0
or
k(w,v)=0
.

Proof.

By definition,
\ell^{z}\succeq p\ell
means

X\times X\ni(x,y)\mapsto\ell(x,y)-\frac{\ell(x,z)\ell(z,y)}{\ell(z,z)}-p(x,y)\ell(x,y)

is a kernel (PsD). In particular,
\ell(w,w)(1-p(w,w))\geq\frac{|\ell(z,w)|^{2}}{\ell(z,z)}\geq 0.

Thus,
p(w,w)\leq 1.
Moreover, if
p(w,w)=1,
then necessarily,
\ell(z,w)=0.

Assuming
p(w,w)=1,
from what has already been proved
\ell(z,w)=0
and thus
the positivity condition
\ell^{z}\succeq p\ell
gives

\begin{bmatrix}\ell^{z}(w,w)&\ell^{z}(w,v)\\
\ell^{z}(v,w)&\ell^{z}(v,v)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\ell(w,w)&\ell(w,v)\\
\ell(v,w)&\ell^{z}(v,v)\end{bmatrix}\succeq\begin{bmatrix}\ell(w,w)&p(w,v)\ell(w,v)\\
p(v,w)\ell(v,w)&p(v,v)\ell(v,v)\end{bmatrix}.

Hence

\begin{bmatrix}0&\big(-p(w,v)+1\big)\ell(w,v)\\
\big(-p(v,w)+1\big)\ell(v,w)&-p(v,v)\ell(v,v)+\ell^{z}(v,v)\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

Thus, either
\ell(w,v)=0
or
p(w,v)=1.
In the later case, by positivity,
p(v,v)=1,

concluding the proof of the first three items of the lemma. Items (
iv
) and (
v
) are proved in an entirely analogous manner.

Assume
\ell^{z}\succeq p\ell
and
k^{z}\preceq pk.
If
k(z,w)=0,
then, by item (
iv
),
p(w,w)=1
and thus, by item (
ii
),
\ell(z,w)=0,
proving item (
vi
).

Now suppose
(k,\ell)
has a Shimorin certificate. In particular, there exist kernels

p[z]
and
p[w]
such that
\ell^{z}\succeq p[z]\,\ell
and
k^{z}\preceq p[z]\,k

as well as
\ell^{w}\succeq p[w]\,\ell
and
k^{w}\preceq p[w]\,k.

If
k(z,v)\neq 0\neq k(w,v),
then, by item (
v
),
p[z](v,v)=p[w](v,v)=1.

Thus, by item (
ii
) (twice),
\ell(z,v)=0=\ell(w,v)
and the proof of item (
vii
), and thus the lemma, is complete.
∎

We point out that the conclusions of items (vi)-(vii) of Lemma
3.5
continue to hold under the weaker (in view of Theorem
4.5
) assumptions that
(k,\ell)
have the CP property and
k(z,w)=0
; see Proposition
7.1
.

3.2.
Shimorin versus strong Shimorin certificates

In this section we consider two sufficient conditions under which the existence of a Shimorin certificate implies the existence of a strong Shimorin certificate. The first is a general condition that applies in a number of cases of interest. The second includes the assumption that
k
and
\ell
are holomorphic kernels
on a domain

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
k,\ell
be kernels on

If

is non-vanishing, then
(k,\ell)
has a Shimorin certificate if and only if
(k,\ell)
has a strong Shimorin certificate if and only if there exists a kernel

on

and a point

(3.3)

The proof of Proposition
3.6
will be based on the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
k,\ell
are kernels over the set

and

then,

for

Proof.

Since

and

Lemma 3.8

Suppose

Proof.

By positivity,

that

is a complete Pick kernel. From

k(w,v)=\frac{k(w,z_{0})k(z_{0},v)}{k(z_{0},z_{0})}\frac{\Big(1-\frac{k(z_{0},z_{0})}{k(w,z_{0})k(z_{0},v)}C(w,v)\Big)}{1-p(w,v)},

\begin{split}h(w,v)&=\frac{k(z_{0},z_{0})}{k(w,z_{0})k(z_{0},v)}C(w,v)\\

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Since we have already shown that having a strong Shimorin certificate implies the existence of a Shimorin certificate (which, in turn, clearly implies (

For every

Proof.

As Remark

Given

The following lemma is well-known. We include a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\Omega
denote a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{g}}.
If

The following is our main result in this subsection.

Proposition 3.13
.

Assuming
(k,\ell)
are non-zero holomorphic kernels on the (connected) domain

the pair
(k,\ell)
has a Shimorin certificate if and only if it has a strong Shimorin certificate, in which case

is non-vanishing.

Proof.

We first show the existence of a Shimorin certificate implies
k
is non-vanishing. Assuming
k
has a Shimorin certificate and arguing by contradiction, suppose

and
k(z,w)=0
and consider the sets

\begin{split}\Omega_{1}&=\{v\in\Omega:k_{z}(v)=k(v,z)=0\},\\
\Omega_{2}&=\{v\in\Omega:k_{w}(v)=k(v,w)=0\},\\
\Omega_{3}&=\{v\in\Omega:\ell_{z}(v)=\ell(v,z)=0\}.\end{split}

Since the kernels
k,\ell
are holomorphic in their first argument, the sets
\Omega_{i}
are the zero sets for (non-zero) holomorphic functions on
\Omega
and, by Lemma
3.5
(

contradicting the conclusion of Lemma

.

Since the existence of a strong Shimorin certificate implies the existence of a Shimorin certificate (Proposition
3.3
), what remains to be proved is that the existence of a Shimorin certificate implies that of a strong Shimorin certificate. In view of Lemma

, it suffices to show there is a point

and a (PsD) kernel

on

and

for all

then tells us that

is contained in the zero set of

while

Since the union of these two sets is equal to all of

gives a contradiction. Thus,

and consequently,

Hence, by Lemma

has a strong Shimorin certificate.
∎

4.
Sufficient Conditions for the CP property

This section contains a new proof of Theorem
1.3
, one based upon a general version of the Leech factorization Theorem. It also contains a generalization of Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
4.5
, proved using a one-step extension argument that replaces the strong Shimorin certificate assumption of Theorem
1.3
with the weaker assumption of the existence of a Shimorin certificate.

4.1.
Theorem
1.3

This subsection begins with our Leech-based proof of Theorem
1.3
and concludes with a corollary that generalizes
a result of
[
5
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
s
denote a strong certificate for
(k,\ell).
Thus,
s
is a CP kernel and there exist kernels
h
and
g
on
X
such that
k=(1-h)s
and
\ell=sg
.
From Lemma
3.7
, there exists a decomposition
X=\cup_{i\in I}X_{i}
such that
k(z,w)=s(z,w)=\ell(z,w)=0
whenever
z\in X_{i}
and
w\in X_{j}
with
i\neq j.
Further,
s|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
is non-vanishing and
k|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}}
is non-vanishing. Since
(k,\ell)
has the CP property if and only if every restriction
(k|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}},\ell|_{X_{i}\times X_{i}})
does,
we assume, without loss of generality, that both
k
and
s
are non-vanishing on
X.
In particular,
|h(z,z)|<1

for
z\in X.

There exist Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
and functions
\Psi:X\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathbb{C}),
and
G:X\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathbb{C})
such that
h(z,w)=\Psi(z)\Psi(w)^{*}
and
g(z,w)=G(z)G(w)^{*}
. Since
(1-\Psi\Psi^{*})s=k\succeq 0
, it follows that
\Psi\in\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{s})
is contractive. Further,
\|\Psi(z)^{*}\|^{2}=|h(z,z)|<1.

Now, assume
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in X
and
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
are
N\times N
matrices that satisfy

\big[\ell(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}-k(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n}\succeq 0.

(4.1)

Rewriting (
4.1
) as

g(z_{i},z_{j})s(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}+h(z_{i},z_{j})s(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}-s(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\succeq 0,

gives

\big(U_{i}U_{j}^{*}-W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big)s(z_{i},z_{j})\succeq 0,

where

U_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{N\times N}\otimes G(z_{i})&W_{i}\otimes\Psi(z_{i})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{B}\big((\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2})\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}),\mathbb{C}^{N}\big),

for all
i.
Applying the generalized version of Leech’s Theorem (
[
2
, Theorem 8.57]
) produces a contractive multiplier

\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{Mult}\big(\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N},\mathcal{H}_{s}\otimes\big((\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2})\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1})\big)\big)

such that
W_{i}=U_{i}\Phi(z_{i}),
for all
i.
This last equality can be rewritten as

\begin{split}W_{i}&=\big(I_{N\times N}\otimes G(z_{i})\big)\Phi_{1}(z_{i})+\big(W_{i}\otimes\Psi(z_{i})\big)\Phi_{2}(z_{i})\\
&=\big(I_{N\times N}\otimes G(z_{i})\big)\Phi_{1}(z_{i})+W_{i}\big(I_{N\times N}\otimes\Psi(z_{i})\big)\Phi_{2}(z_{i}),\end{split}

(4.2)

for all
i.

Since
\Phi
is a contractive multiplier,
\|\Phi_{2}(z)\|\leq 1
for all
z.
Since also
\|\Psi(z)^{*}\|<1,
it follows that, for
each

the
N\times N
matrix
(I_{N\times N}\otimes\Psi(z))\Phi_{2}(z)
is a strict contraction and therefore

I_{N\times N}-(I_{N\times N}\otimes\Psi(z))\Phi_{2}(z)
is pointwise invertible. Define
R:X\to\mathbb{M}_{N}
by

R(z)=\big(I_{N\times N}\otimes G(z)\big)\Phi_{1}(z)\big(I_{N\times N}-\big(I_{N\times N}\otimes\Psi(z)\big)\Phi_{2}(z)\big)^{-1}.

Solving equation
4.2
for
W_{i}
gives

W_{i}=R(z_{i})

for all
i.
Defining

H:X\to\mathcal{B}\big((\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2})\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}),\mathbb{C}^{N}\big)

by

H(z)=\begin{bmatrix}I_{N\times N}\otimes G(z)&R(z)\otimes\Psi(z)\end{bmatrix},

it follows from the definition of

that
R(z)=H(z)\Phi(z),
for all
z\in X
,
and, from the definition of

that
H(z_{i})=U_{i}.

Hence

\begin{split}\ell(z,w)I_{N\times N}-k(z,w)R(z)R(w)^{*}&=\big(H(z)H(w)^{*}-R(z)R(w)^{*}\big)s(z,w)\\
&=H(z)\big((I-\Phi(z)\Phi(w)^{*})s(z,w)\big)H(w)^{*}\succeq 0,\end{split}

since
(I-\Phi(z)\Phi(w)^{*})s(z,w)\succeq 0
. Thus,

is an interpolating contractive multiplier and our proof is complete.
∎

4.2.
Shimorin Certificates and the CP property for pairs

Next, we will briefly investigate how the CP property for
(k,\ell)
places restrictions on the linear independence of kernel functions before turning to generalizing Theorem
1.3
to pairs
(k,\ell)
that have a Shimorin certificate. We again assume that all pairs
(k,\ell)
consist of kernels that are non-vanishing along the diagonal.

In the case of a single CP kernel
k
on a set
X,
it is known
(see
[
2
, Lemma 7.5]
) that if
k_{z},k_{w}
are linearly independent for every choice of
distinct points
z,w\in X
then the same must hold for any finite collection
\{k_{z_{1}},\dots,k_{z_{n}}\},
where
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in X
are
n
distinct points. Moving to the two-kernel setting, Remark
3.4
tells us that, even if, for each
n
and distinct point
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in X
the collection
\{\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n}}\}
is linearly independent, we cannot draw any conclusions concerning
k_{z_{1}},\dots,k_{z_{n}}
without further knowledge of
(k,\ell)
. But what if
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in X
are distinct, but
\{\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n}}\}
is linearly dependent?

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
(k,\ell)
is a CP pair on
X
and
n\geq 2.
Further, assume
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
are distinct points in
X,
the vectors
\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n-1}}
are linearly independent and there exist scalars
c_{1},\dots c_{n-1}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\ell_{z_{n}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{i}\ell_{z_{i}}.

(4.3)

If
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
c_{j}\neq 0
, then
k_{z_{n}},k_{z_{j}}
are linearly dependent.

Proof.

Assume that there exist scalars
c_{1},\dots c_{n-1}
, not all of which can be zero, such that (
4.3
) holds. Given

e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1}\in\mathbb{C},
define

R:\underset{1\leq i\leq n-1}{\text{span}}\{\ell_{z_{i}}\}\to\underset{1\leq i\leq n-1}{\text{span}}\{k_{z_{i}}\}

by
R\ell_{z_{i}}=e_{i}k_{z_{i}}
(where we suppress the dependence of
R
on
e
). By the independence of
\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n-1}},
there exists
\delta>0
such that
R
is a contraction whenever
|e_{i}|<\delta
for all
i.
Thus, for any such choice of
e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1},
the CP property of
(k,\ell)
implies the existence of
a\in\mathbb{C}
such that the extension

R_{a}:\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\text{span}}\{\ell_{z_{i}}\}\to\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\text{span}}\{k_{z_{i}}\}

of
R
determined by
R_{a}\ell_{z_{n}}=ak_{z_{n}}
is a contraction. Thus, (
4.3
) gives us

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}e_{i}c_{i}k_{z_{i}}=R\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{i}\ell_{z_{i}}\bigg)=R\big(\ell_{z_{n}}\big)\in\text{span}\{k_{z_{n}}\},

(4.4)

for any
e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1}
with modulus less than
\delta.
Now, assume
c_{j}\neq 0
and choose
e_{j}\neq 0
sufficiently small with
e_{i}=0
whenever
i\neq j.
The equality (
4.4
) implies
k_{z_{j}}\in\text{span}\{k_{z_{n}}\},
as desired.
∎

We will conclude this section by showing that the existence of a Shimorin certificate is sufficient to guarantee the CP property. The proof employs the standard one-point-extension argument in combination with Parrott’s Lemma, which were also the main ingredients in Shimorin’s proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
(k,\ell)
is a pair of kernels that possesses a Shimorin certificate, then
(k,\ell)
has the CP property.

Proof.

We will first prove the theorem under the assumption that for each
n
and distinct points
z_{1},\dots,z_{n},
the kernel functions
\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n}},
are linearly independent.

Assume
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
are given distinct points in
X
and
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
are
N\times N
matrices that satisfy

\big[\ell(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}-k(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n}\succeq 0.

(4.5)

We will first show that, for any
z_{n+1}\in X\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\},
there exists a matrix
W_{n+1}
so that

\big[\ell(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}-k(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n+1}\succeq 0.

(4.6)

Let
\{u^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{N}
be a basis for
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and set

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{n}=\text{span}\{k_{z_{i}}\otimes u^{\alpha}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq\alpha\leq N\},

\mathcal{M}^{\ell}_{n}=\text{span}\{\ell_{z_{i}}\otimes u^{\alpha}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq\alpha\leq N\}.

Define
R:\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\ell}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}^{k}
by

R:\ell_{z_{i}}\otimes u^{\alpha}\mapsto k_{z_{i}}\otimes W^{*}_{i}u^{\alpha}

(4.7)

and extend linearly. The operator
R
is a contraction if and only if for every choice of scalars
\{a^{\alpha}_{i}\}
we have

\big\langle\big(I-R^{*}R\big)\sum_{j,\beta}a^{\beta}_{j}\ell_{z_{j}}\otimes u^{\beta},\sum_{i,\alpha}a^{\alpha}_{i}\ell_{z_{i}}\otimes u^{\alpha}\big\rangle

=\sum_{i,j,\alpha,\beta}a^{\beta}_{j}\overline{a}^{\alpha}_{i}\Big(\ell(z_{i},z_{j})\langle u^{\beta},u^{\alpha}\rangle-k(z_{i},z_{j})\big\langle W_{i}W^{*}_{j}u^{\beta},u^{\alpha}\big\rangle\Big)\geq 0,

which is equivalent to (
4.5
). Thus,
\|R\|\leq 1
.

Now, by the independence assumption on
\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n+1}},
for each choice of
W=W_{n+1}
there is an extension
R_{W}
of
R
on
\mathcal{M}_{n+1}^{\ell}
uniquely determined by

R_{W}:\ell_{z_{n+1}}\otimes u^{\alpha}\mapsto k_{z_{n+1}}\otimes W^{*}u^{\alpha}.

(4.8)

When does there exist
W
so that
\|R_{W}\|\leq 1
(which is equivalent to (
4.6
))? The answer is given by the following lemma, the proof of which rests on an application of Parrott’s Lemma to an appropriate matrix decomposition of
R_{W}.
The single-kernel version of this argument is well-known (see e.g.
[
38
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and the unpublished work of Agler), and there are essentially no new wrinkles in this generalized two-kernel version. Thus, we omit the details.

Lemma 4.6
.

In the above setting, there exists
W
so that
\|R_{W}\|\leq 1
if and only if

\big[\ell^{z_{n+1}}(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}-k^{z_{n+1}}(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n}\succeq 0.

(4.9)

Now, let
J
denote the matrix all of whose entries are
1
with respect to
\{u^{\alpha}\}
. By assumption, there exists a Shimorin certificate
\{p[x]\}_{x\in X}
for
(k,\ell).
Thus,

\begin{split}&\ell^{z_{n+1}}(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}-k^{z_{n+1}}(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\\
=&I_{N\times N}\cdot\big[\ell^{z_{n+1}}(z_{i},z_{j})\otimes J\big]-W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\cdot\big[k^{z_{n+1}}(z_{i},z_{j})\otimes J\big]\\
\succeq&I_{N\times N}\cdot\big[p[z_{n+1}](z_{i},z_{j})\ell(z_{i},z_{j})\otimes J\big]-W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\cdot\big[p[z_{n+1}](z_{i},z_{j})k(z_{i},z_{j})\otimes J\big]\\
=&\big[\ell(z_{i},z_{j})I_{N\times N}-k(z_{i},z_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]\cdot\big[p[z_{n+1}](z_{i},z_{j})\otimes J\big],\end{split}

which is positive, being the Schur product of (
4.5
) with a positive matrix. Thus, Lemma
4.6
tells us that, whenever
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in X
and
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
are
N\times N
matrices that satisfy (
4.5
) and
z_{n+1}
is any distinct point in
X,

there exists a matrix
W_{n+1}
so that (
4.6
) holds. In other words, we have shown that one can always extend a multiplier defined on a finite subset of
X
to any other point without increasing the norm. We can now employ either transfinite induction (as in
[
38
, Section 3]
) or a compactness argument (as in
[
40
, Proposition 2.9]
)
to obtain a contractive multiplier satisfying the initial conditions. We omit the details.

Finally, we prove the general case, where it is possible to have linearly dependent kernel functions. As before, assume
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
are given distinct points in
X
and
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
are
N\times N
matrices that satisfy (
4.5
). Without loss of generality we assume that
\{\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{m}}\}
is a maximal linearly independent subset of
\{\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n}}\}
.
By Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal subset
Y\subseteq X
with the properties that no finite subcollection of
\{\ell_{v}:v\in Y\}
is linearly dependent and
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}\subseteq Y.
Let
\widetilde{k}=k|_{Y\times Y}
and
\widetilde{\ell}=\ell|_{Y\times Y}
. Since
(k,\ell)
has the CP property, the same must be true for
(\widetilde{k},\widetilde{\ell})
. Note that (
4.5
) continues to hold if we restrict to the points
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
. Thus, in view of what has already been proved, there exists
\widetilde{\Phi}:Y\to\mathbb{M}_{N}
such that
\widetilde{\Phi}
is a contractive multiplier
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{k}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{\ell}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
that satisfies
\widetilde{\Phi}(z_{i})=W_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m.

Since the span of
\{\ell_{x}:x\in X\}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, by construction, the span of
\{\ell_{y}:y\in Y\}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}

and, in fact, the restriction map
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\to\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{\ell}}
is unitary.
Thus
\widetilde{\Phi}
induces a contractive
map
M^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
determined by
M^{*}\ell_{y}\otimes u=k_{y}\otimes\widetilde{\Phi}(y)^{*}u
for
y\in Y
and
u\in\mathbb{C}^{N}.
Now, we argue that the function
\widetilde{\Phi}:Y\to\mathbb{M}_{N}
has a unique extension to a function
\Phi:X\to\mathbb{M}_{N}
such that
M^{*}=M_{\Phi}^{*}.
The function
\Phi
so extended satisfies
\Phi(z_{j})=W_{j}
for all
j
and thus solves the original interpolation problem. Indeed, given
x\in X\setminus Y,
we know (by definition of
Y
) that there exist unique points
v_{1},\dots,v_{p}\in Y
and unique non-zero scalars
c_{1},\dots,c_{p}
such that

\ell_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}c_{i}\ell_{v_{i}}.

In view of Lemma
4.4
, there exist, for each
1\leq i\leq p,
(non-zero) scalars
d_{1},\dots,d_{p}
such that
k_{v_{i}}=d_{i}k_{x}.
Thus,

M^{*}\ell_{x}\otimes u=M^{*}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{p}c_{i}\ell_{v_{i}}\otimes u\bigg)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}c_{i}k_{v_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{\Phi}(v_{i})^{*}u=k_{x}\otimes\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{p}c_{i}d_{i}\widetilde{\Phi}(v_{i})^{*}u\bigg].

Hence, setting
\Phi^{*}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}c_{i}d_{i}\widetilde{\Phi}(v_{i})^{*},
we have
M^{*}=M_{\Phi}^{*}
and this condition uniquely determines

\Phi.
By construction,
\Phi(z_{j})=W_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m.
While not as transparent, it is also true that
\Phi(z_{j})=W_{j}

for
j>m.
Indeed, applying the argument above to
j>m=p
and
\ell_{z_{j}}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\ell_{z_{i}}
yields

k_{z_{j}}\otimes\Phi^{*}(z_{j})u=M_{\Phi}^{*}\big(\ell_{z_{j}}\otimes u\big)=k_{z_{j}}\otimes\sum c_{i}d_{i}W_{i}^{*}u.

On the other hand, the matrices
W_{j}
for
j>m
are determined by the
W_{j}
for
j\leq m.
To verify this claim,
let
\mathcal{E}
denote the span of
\{\ell_{j}:1\leq j\leq n\}
and
\mathcal{F}
the span of
\{k_{j}:1\leq j\leq n\}.

The inequality of equation
4.5
is equivalent to the statement that the mapping
T^{*}:\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
determined by
T^{*}\big(\ell_{z_{j}}\otimes u\big)=k_{z_{j}}\otimes W_{j}^{*}u
is well-defined and a contraction. In particular,
arguing as above with the same notations,

k_{z_{j}}\otimes W_{j}^{*}u=T^{*}\big(\ell_{z_{j}}\otimes u\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}k_{z_{i}}\otimes W_{i}^{*}u=k_{z_{j}}\otimes\sum c_{i}d_{i}W_{i}^{*}u.

Thus
W_{j}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}d_{i}W_{i}^{*}=\Phi(z_{j})^{*}.

∎

5.
The CC Property for Diagonal Holomorphic Pairs

Throughout this section, we work exclusively with diagonal holomorphic kernels
k,\ell.
Unless otherwise noted, they are assumed normalized.

5.1.
One-step extensions

Proposition
5.2
below, whose proof depends upon the following lemma,
interprets Proposition

Recall, for

the

of

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
\varnothing\neq F\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
is finite and co-invariant and
d\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
satisfies

(i)

d\notin F;

(ii)

if
a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
and
|a|<|d|,
then

Setting

the set
F^{+}\cup\{\mathbf{0}\}
is co-invariant and if

and

then

Proof.

If
a,b\in F
, we get the desired conclusion because
F
is co-invariant. If
b=d,
then, since
|a|\geq 1,
we must have
|b-a|<|b|=|d|,
hence

in view of our hypothesis for
d.
Finally, if

then we cannot have

(since

would imply that

, by co-invariance), and so we must have

Now, to check co-invariance, assume

and let
a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
satisfy
a\leq b
. If
b\in F,
then we obtain

since
F
is co-invariant. Thus, either

or
a=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Further, if
b=d,
then either
a=d
(hence
a\in F^{+}
) or

which implies

in view of our hypothesis for
d.

∎

5.2.
Sufficient conditions for the CC property

For the purposes of this section, we will temporarily recast Definition
1.2
in terms of formal power series. Domain consideration issues will not trouble us until Section
6
.

Given diagonal holomorphic kernels
(k,\ell)
,
a
formal Shimorin certificate
for
(k,\ell)
is a formal power series

t(x)=\sum_{|a|>0}t_{a}x^{a},

where
t_{a}\geq 0
for all
a
, for which there exist

g(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}}g_{a}x^{a}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ and }\hskip 5.69046pth(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}}h_{a}x^{a}

such that
g_{a},h_{a}\ \geq 0
for all
a
and

\ell(1-t)=g,\ \ \ 1-k(1-t)=h,

in the sense of formal power series. These
conditions are equivalent to
g_{\mathbf{0}}=\ell_{\mathbf{0}}
and
h_{\mathbf{0}}=1-k_{\mathbf{0}}
and, for
|a|>0,

\ell_{a}=g_{a}+\sum_{0<u\leq a}t_{u}\ell_{a-u}

(5.4)

and

k_{a}+h_{a}=\sum_{0<u\leq a}t_{u}k_{a-u}.

(5.5)

We will now show that existence of a formal Shimorin certificate guarantees the CC property. Even though this result is subsumed by Theorem
6.4
(to be proved in the next section), we have chosen to include the proof as it requires only a short detour and might be of independent interest.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose the kernels
k(z,w)=1+\sum_{|a|\geq 1}k_{a}z^{a}\overline{w}^{a}
and
\ell(z,w)=1+\sum_{|a|\geq 1}\ell_{a}z^{a}\overline{w}^{a}

(k,\ell),
then

has the CC property.

Before turning to the proof of Theorem

, we record
the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume

is co-invariant. If the block matrix

with entries from

is positive, then, for each

.

The statement of Lemma

has used the assumption

Since

is positive, there exists

is positive.
∎

=g_{a}\delta_{a,b}I_{J}+\sum_{0<u\leq a,b}h_{u}c^{*}_{a-u}c_{b-u}+\sum_{0<v\leq a}t_{v}\Big(\ell_{a-v}\delta_{a,b}I_{J}-\sum_{v\leq u\leq a,b}k_{u-v}c^{*}_{a-u}c_{b-u}\Big)

=g_{a}\delta_{a,b}I_{J}+\sum_{0<u\leq a,b}h_{u}c^{*}_{a-u}c_{b-u}+\sum_{0<v\leq a}t_{v}\sqrt{\ell_{a-v}\ell_{b-v}}X_{a-v,b-v},

for all

where for the last equality we have used

) (note that, since

both

assuming they are defined). Thus, we obtain

where the

and defined as follows:

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

where

if either

or

for all
a,b\in F^{+}.
Clearly,

is positive, while a short computation reveals that

where

for all

Thus,

must be positive as well. Finally, every

is positive because of Lemma

and the fact that

was positive to begin with. Hence, (

is positive, as desired.

5.3.
Existence of a master certificate and a necessary condition for the CP property

Let
k
be a normalized diagonal holomorphic kernel with coefficients
\{k_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}}.
We recall Definition
1.5
from the introduction: the
master certificate associated with
k
is the formal power series in
{\mathsf{g}}
complex variables defined as

\vartheta(x)=\sum_{b\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}}\vartheta_{b}x^{b},

where
\vartheta_{\mathbf{0}}=0
,
\vartheta_{e_{j}}=k_{e_{j}}
for all
j
(recall that
e_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}
denotes the element with
1
in the
j
-th entry and
0

elsewhere) and

\vartheta_{b}=\max\Bigg\{0,k_{b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w+u=b,\\
w,u\neq\mathbf{0}\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{w}k_{u}\Bigg\},

(5.9)

for all
b\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
with
|b|>1.

In this subsection, we show that, for any diagonal holomorphic
\ell,
if
(k,\ell)
has the CC property, then
\vartheta
is a formal Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell).
Thus, in combination with our results from the previous subsection, we obtain a complete characterization of those pairs of diagonal holomorphic kernels possessing the CC property. Recall the standing assumption that
k
and
\ell
are normalized.

Two things are immediate from the construction of
\vartheta
. First,
k_{b}\geq\vartheta_{b}\geq 0
for all
b
and second

k_{b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w+u=b,\\
w\neq\mathbf{0}\end{subarray}}k_{u}\vartheta_{w}\leq 0

(5.10)

for all
\mathbf{0}\neq b\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}},
so that, as a formal power series, the coefficients of
h=1-k(1-\vartheta)
are all non-negative. These observations are
summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.9
.

The master certificate
\theta
associated to a normalized diagonal holomorphic kernel
k

satisfies
k_{b}\geq\vartheta_{b}
for all
b
and the coefficients of the formal power series

h=1-k(1-\vartheta)
are all non-negative.

Theorem 5.10
.

Suppose
\ell
is a normalized diagonal holomorphic kernel. If
(k,\ell)

has the CC property, then the coefficients of the formal power series
g=\ell(1-\vartheta)
are all non-negative;
that is,
h_{0}=0
and

g_{a}=\ell_{a}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq a,\\
u\neq\mathbf{0}\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{a-u}\geq 0

for
\mathbf{0}\neq a\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{g}}.

The proof of Theorem
5.10
is postponed in favor of
the following key result.

Theorem 5.11
.

A pair of diagonal holomorphic kernels
(k,\ell)
has the CC property if and only if
the master certificate
\vartheta
for
k
is a formal Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell).

Proof.

Suppose
(k,\ell)
has the CP property. By Theorems
5.9
and
5.10
,
the formal power series
h=1-k(1-\vartheta)
and
g=\ell(1-\vartheta)
have non-negative coefficients.
Hence,
\vartheta
is a formal certificate for
(k,\ell).

The converse is Theorem
5.6
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
5.10
consumes the remainder
of this subsection. It
uses the Lemmas

and
5.13

immediately below.

|u|=m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}.

(5.22)

But since
|b|=|d|-1-m
and
u<d-b
imply
|u|<m+1,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\bigg(k_{d-b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
0<|u|<m\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\bigg(k_{d-b}-\sum_{\mathbf{0}<u<d-b}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}\bigg)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
|u|=m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{d-b}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
|u|=m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}.

Thus, (
5.22
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{a<d}v_{a}k_{d-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d,\\
0<|u|\leq m\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{d-b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d,\\
|u|\leq|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w<d-u,\\
|w|=m\end{subarray}}v_{u}\vartheta_{w}k_{d-w-u}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|\leq|d|-2-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\bigg(k_{d-b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
0<|u|<m+1\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}\bigg)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
|u|=m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|\leq|d|-2-m\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
|u|=m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d,\\
0<|u|\leq m\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|=|d|-1-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\vartheta_{d-b}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<d,\\
|b|\leq|d|-2-m\end{subarray}}v_{b}\bigg(k_{d-b}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<d-b,\\
0<|u|<m+1\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}k_{d-b-u}\bigg),

concluding the proof of the inductive step. Thus, (
5.20
) holds for every
2\leq m\leq|d|-1.
Choosing
m=|d|-1
then yields (as the 3rd term disappears)

\displaystyle\sum_{a<d}v_{a}k_{d-a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{0}<u<d}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u}+\sum_{|b|=0}v_{b}\vartheta_{d-b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{0}<u<d}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u}+v_{\mathbf{0}}\vartheta_{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{0}<u<d}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u}+\vartheta_{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{0}<u\leq d}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u},

and
(
5.14
)
is proved. We may now conclude

\ell_{d}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq d,\\
u\neq\mathbf{0}\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{u}\ell_{d-u}=\ell_{d}-\sum_{a<d}v_{a}k_{d-a}=v_{d}\geq 0,

so (
5.13
) holds.
∎

5.4.
CP implies CC

The last ingredient that will be needed for our proof of Theorem
1.6
(contained in Section
6
) is a direct passage from the CP to the CC property. Establishing such a passage will be our main objective for this subsection. The idea is to apply the complete Pick pair assumption to
tuples of points near
\mathbf{0}
and then take a limit - letting these points tend to
\mathbf{0}
. Before proceeding we highlight an ingredient
in the proof, Proposition
5.17
. Namely,
for a
(k,\ell)
pair with
k
normalized, multiplication by a monomial
z^{b}
defines a bounded multiplier from
\mathcal{H}_{k}
to
\mathcal{H_{\ell}}

and moreover, as pointed out by an anonymous referee, in fact the mutiplier norm of
z^{b}
coincides with its Hilbert space norm in
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}.

We will require the following elementary Hilbert space lemma.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
H
denote a Hilbert space and assume
M\subset H
is a non-trivial finite-dimensional subspace.
Further, suppose
\gamma:[0,\delta]\to H
is a continuous function such that
\gamma(t)
does not lie in
M
, for any
t.
Then, letting

P_{t}
denote the projection onto
\textup{span}\big(M\cup\{\gamma(t)\}\big)
, we have
P_{t}\to P_{0}
in operator norm as
t\to 0.

Proof sketch.

Fix an orthonormal basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
for
M
and apply Gram-Schmidt to the basis

\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m},\gamma(t)\}
to obtain the orthonormal basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m},\rho(t)\}

for
\textup{span}\big(M\cup\{\gamma(t)\}\big)
, noting that
\rho(t)
depends continuously on
t.

Since, for
f\in H,

(P_{t}-P_{0})f=\langle f,\rho(t)\rangle(\rho(t)-\rho(0))+\langle f,\rho(t)-\rho(0)\rangle\rho(0)

it follows that
\|P_{t}-P_{0}\|\leq 2\|\rho(t)-\rho(0)\|.

∎

We will work with a lexicographic order on
\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
. Given
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}},
the expression
a\prec b
means that either
|a|<|b|
or
|a|=|b|
and there exists
0\leq r\leq{\mathsf{g}}-1
such that

a_{1}=b_{1}

\vdots

a_{r}r=b_{r}

a_{r+1}<b_{r+1},

where
r=0
simply means
a_{1}<b_{1}.
Clearly,
(\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}},\prec)
is totally ordered. Set
\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}=\{a^{0},a^{1},\dots\}
accordingly.

Now, let
\ell=1+\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}},\\
|a|>0}\ell_{a}(z\overline{w})^{a}
be a normalized diagonal holomorphic kernel on some domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
containing
\mathbf{0}
. Set
p_{a^{j}}(z)=z^{a^{j}},
for all
j
. For
m\geq 0
and
\Lambda\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, let
M_{m,\Lambda}
and
M_{\Lambda}

denote the spans of
\{p_{a^{j}}:0\leq j\leq m\}\cup\{\ell_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
and
\{\ell_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
, respectively, with

P_{m,\Lambda}
and
P_{\Lambda}
the associated projections. We will also write
M^{0}_{m,\Lambda}
and
M^{0}_{\Lambda}
for the spans of
\{p_{a^{j}}:1\leq j\leq m+1\}\cup\{\ell_{\lambda}-1:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
and
\{\ell_{\lambda}-1:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
, respectively, with
P^{0}_{m,\Lambda}
and
P^{0}_{\Lambda}
denoting the associated projections.
For what is to follow, it should be kept in mind that, since all coefficients
\ell_{a}
are non-zero, the polynomials are contained in
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
. Hence, there exist no (finite) collections of linearly dependent kernel functions, and this continues to be the case even if we have vanishing derivatives up to a certain order.

Lemma 5.15
.

Fix
m\geq-1
. With notation as above, for any normalized diagonal holomorphic kernel
\ell
and any finite
\Lambda\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},
there exists a continuous map
v:(0,\delta]\to\Omega
such that, with
\Lambda_{t}=\Lambda\cup\{v(t)\}
,
we have

P_{m,\Lambda_{t}}\to P_{m+1,\Lambda}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ and }\hskip 8.5359ptP^{0}_{m,\Lambda_{t}}\to P^{0}_{m+1,\Lambda}

in operator norm as
t\to 0
, where
P_{-1,\Lambda_{t}}\equiv P_{\Lambda_{t}}
and
P^{0}_{-1,\Lambda_{t}}\equiv P^{0}_{\Lambda_{t}}
.

Proof.

We assume
m\geq 0
(the case
m=-1
can be treated analogously). Set
b=a^{m+1},c=a^{m+2}
and choose
r_{j}=|c|^{{\mathsf{g}}-j}+N
, where
N>\sum(b_{j}+c_{j})|c|^{{\mathsf{g}}-j}
. Put
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{{\mathsf{g}}}).
Observe that, for each
\rho>m+2
and
|a^{\rho}|=|c|,
the inequality
c\prec a^{\rho}
yields

\sum c_{j}r_{j}=\sum c_{j}|c|^{{\mathsf{g}}-j}\,+\,N\,|c|<\sum a^{\rho}_{j}|c|^{{\mathsf{g}}-j}\,+\,N\,|c|=\sum a^{\rho}_{j}r_{j}.

Our choice of
N
guarantees that the same inequality continues to hold when
|c|<|a^{\rho}|.
One can similarly show
\langle r,b\rangle<\langle r,c\rangle
, and thus we obtain

\langle r,b\rangle<\langle r,c\rangle<\langle r,a^{\rho}\rangle,

(5.23)

for all
\rho>m+2.

Now, fix
\delta>0
sufficiently small and, for
0<t\leq\delta,
set
v=v(t)=(t^{r_{1}},\dots,t^{r_{{\mathsf{g}}}})\in\Omega
. Define
\gamma:[0,\delta]\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
and
\gamma^{0}:[0,\delta]\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
as

\gamma(t)=\begin{cases}\cfrac{\ell_{v}-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a^{j}}}{\sqrt{\ell(v,v)-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\ell_{a^{j}}v^{2a^{j}}}},\hskip 17.07182pt0<t\leq\delta,\\
\sqrt{\ell_{a^{m+1}}}p_{a^{m+1}},\hskip 65.44142ptt=0,\end{cases}

and

\gamma^{0}(t)=\begin{cases}\cfrac{\ell_{v}-\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a^{j}}}{\sqrt{\ell(v,v)-\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}\ell_{a^{j}}v^{2a^{j}}}},\hskip 17.07182pt0<t\leq\delta,\\
\sqrt{\ell_{a^{m+2}}}p_{a^{m+2}},\hskip 65.44142ptt=0.\end{cases}

Clearly, both
\gamma
and
\gamma^{0}
are continuous in
(0,\delta]
. We will show that they are continuous at
0
as well. Since the proofs are essentially identical, we will only examine
\gamma
. For
0<t\leq\delta,
we have

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=\cfrac{\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a^{j}}}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\ell_{a^{j}}v^{2a^{j}}}}=\cfrac{\cfrac{\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a^{j}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}}{\cfrac{\sqrt{\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\ell_{a^{j}}v^{2a^{j}}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}}.

(5.24)

Now, (
5.23
) tells us that

\frac{v^{a^{j}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}=\frac{t^{\langle r,a^{j}\rangle}}{t^{\langle r,a^{m+1}\rangle}}=t^{\langle r,a^{j}\rangle-\langle r,a^{m+1}\rangle}

has a positive exponent, for every
j>m+1.
Thus, by elementary power series arguments, we have

\cfrac{\sqrt{\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\ell_{a^{j}}v^{2a^{j}}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}\longrightarrow\sqrt{\ell_{a^{m+1}}}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ as }t\to 0.

(5.25)

Further, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg|\Bigg|\frac{\sum\limits_{j\geq m+1}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a^{j}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}-\ell_{a^{m+1}}p_{a^{m+1}}\Bigg|\Bigg|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}=\Bigg|\Bigg|\frac{\sum\limits_{j\geq m+2}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a_{j}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}\Bigg|\Bigg|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}=\frac{\sum\limits_{j\geq m+2}\ell_{a^{j}}v^{2a^{j}}}{v^{2a^{m+1}}},

which converges to
0
as
t\to 0
, as we already saw. Thus,

\cfrac{\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\ell_{a^{j}}p_{a^{j}}v^{a^{j}}}{v^{a^{m+1}}}\longrightarrow\ell_{a^{m+1}}p_{a^{m+1}}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ as }t\to 0,

(5.26)

where convergence is taken in the norm of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
. Combining (
5.24
) with (
5.25
)-(
5.26
), we obtain that
\gamma
is continuous at
0
.

Now, let
\Lambda\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
be finite and set
\Lambda_{t}=\Lambda\cup\{v(t)\}.
It is easily verified that, for all
t\in(0,\delta],

M_{m,\Lambda_{t}}=\textup{span}\big(M_{m,\Lambda}\cup\{\gamma(t)\}\big),

while
\textup{span}\big(M_{m,\Lambda}\cup\{\gamma(0)\}\big)=M_{m+1,\Lambda}
. Since we also have
\gamma(t)\notin M_{m,\Lambda}
(for
\delta
sufficiently small), Lemma
5.14
implies
P_{m,\Lambda_{t}}\to P_{m+1,\Lambda}.
Similarly, since

M^{0}_{m,\Lambda_{t}}=\textup{span}\big(M^{0}_{m,\Lambda}\cup\{\gamma^{0}(t)\}\big)
and
\textup{span}\big(M^{0}_{m,\Lambda}\cup\{\gamma^{0}(0)\}\big)=M^{0}_{m+1,\Lambda}
, we obtain
P^{0}_{m,\Lambda_{t}}\to P^{0}_{m+1,\Lambda},
as desired.
∎

Let
F_{n}=\{a^{0},\dots,a^{n}\}.
In particular,
F_{n}
is co-invariant in view of the lexicographic ordering. Let

L^{n}(z,w)=\sum_{a\notin F_{n}}\ell_{a}z^{a}\overline{w}^{a}.

Lemma 5.16
.

If
(k,\ell)
is a CP pair of diagonal holomorphic kernels, then

\frac{L^{n}}{k}\succeq 0.

Proof.

Fix a set
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{j}:0\leq j\leq n\}
of distinct points near
\mathbf{0}
with
\lambda_{0}=\mathbf{0}.
Abusing notation slightly, we will write
P_{m,\Lambda}\ell
to denote the reproducing kernel associated with the subspace
M_{m,\Lambda},
which is obtained by applying the projection
P_{m,\Lambda}
to the kernel functions
\ell_{w}.
By Theorem
1.10
,

\frac{(I-P_{0,\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}})\ell}{k}=\frac{\ell^{\Lambda}}{k}\succeq 0.

We will now show, by induction on
m
, that

\frac{(I-P_{m,\{\lambda_{m+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}})\ell}{k}\succeq 0,

(5.27)

for all
0\leq m\leq n
and any choice of distinct nonzero points
\lambda_{m+1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
near
\mathbf{0}.
Assume that it holds for some fixed
0\leq m\leq n-1
and let
\lambda_{m+2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\Omega
be distinct, non-zero. By Lemma
5.15
, there exists
v:(0,\delta]\to\Omega
continuous such that

P_{m,\{v(t),\lambda_{m+2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}}\to P_{m+1,\{\lambda_{m+2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}}

in operator norm as
t\to 0.
By our inductive hypothesis,

\frac{(I-P_{m,\{v(t),\lambda_{m+2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}})\ell}{k}\succeq 0,

for all
t\in(0,\delta].

Since pointwise limits of PsD kernels are PSD kernels, we may let
t
tend to
0
to obtain

\frac{(I-P_{m+1,\{\lambda_{m+2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}})\ell}{k}\succeq 0.

Thus, (
5.27
) holds for every
m\leq n.
Setting
m=n
then yields

\frac{L^{n}}{k}=\frac{(I-P_{n,\emptyset})\ell}{k}\succeq 0,

as desired, where
P_{n,\emptyset}
denotes the projection onto the span of
\{z^{a}:a\in F_{n}\}.

∎

Next, we show that polynomials automatically yield bounded multipliers
\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
whenever
(k,\ell)
is a CP pair of diagonal holomorphic kernels.

Proposition 5.17
.

If
(k,\ell)
is a CP pair of diagonal holomorphic kernels, then all
\mathbb{M}_{N}
-valued polynomials belong to
\text{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N},\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N})
, for all
N\geq 1.
In particular, if
k
is normalized, we have

||z^{b}||_{\text{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k},\mathcal{H}_{\ell})}=||z^{b}||_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}},

for all
b\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}.

Proof.

Fix
n
and set
b=a^{n}.

From Lemma
5.16
,
\frac{L^{n}}{k}=g
for some positive
kernel
g.
Since
L^{n}
and
k
are holomorphic diagonal kernels, so is
g.
Writing

L^{n}=g\,k,
we find
g_{a^{j}}=0
for
j<n
(that is
g_{a}=0
whenever
a\prec b
with respect to the lexicographic ordering) and

\ell_{b}=\sum_{0\leq u\leq b}g_{u}k_{b-u}.

Now, if
u\leq b
then
|u|\leq|b|.
If the inequality is strict, then
g_{u}=0.
If
|u|=|b|
, then
we must have
u=b.
Hence,
\ell_{b}=g_{b}k_{0}
. In particular,
g_{b}>0.
Now, for
a\succeq b,

\ell_{a}=\sum_{u\leq a}g_{u}k_{a-u}=g_{b}\,k_{a-b}+\sum_{b\neq u\leq a}g_{u}k_{a-u}\geq g_{b}\,k_{a-b}.

We conclude

\frac{1}{g_{b}}\ell(z,w)-z^{b}\overline{w}^{b}k(z,w)\succeq 0.

Hence,
||z^{b}||_{\text{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k},\mathcal{H}_{\ell})}\leq 1/\sqrt{g_{b}}
, and, consequently, multiplication by all matrix-valued polynomials is bounded. Finally, if
k
is normalized, then
g_{b}=\ell_{b}
and so we may write

||z^{b}||_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}=||z^{b}\cdot 1||_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}\leq||z^{b}||_{\text{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k},\mathcal{H}_{\ell})}\leq 1/\sqrt{\ell_{b}}=||z^{b}||_{\mathcal{H}_{\ell}},

which proves the second assertion.
∎

The following lemma tells us that
(k,\ell)
is CC if and only if it has the one-point extension property with respect to the lexicographic order.

Lemma 5.18
.

A pair
(k,\ell)
of diagonal holomorphic kernels
has the CC property if and only if for all positive integers
J
and
n
and collections
\{c_{a}:a\in\{a^{0},a^{1},\dots,a^{n}\}\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{J}
such that the block (upper-triangular) matrix
C
indexed by
\{a^{0},a^{1},\dots,a^{n}\}\times\{a^{0},a^{1},\dots,a^{n}\}
with block
J\times J
entries,

C_{a,b}=\begin{cases}c_{b-a}\sqrt{\cfrac{k_{a}}{\ell_{b}}},\hskip 14.22636ptb\geq a,\\
\mathbf{0},\hskip 29.87547pt\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(5.28)

is a contraction,
the matrix
C^{+}
indexed by
\{a^{1},\dots,a^{n+1}\}\times\{a^{1},\dots,a^{n+1}\}
, and given by

C^{+}_{a,b}=\begin{cases}c_{b-a}\sqrt{\cfrac{k_{a}}{\ell_{b}}},\hskip 14.22636ptb\geq a,\\
\mathbf{0},\hskip 29.87547pt\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(5.29)

is also a contraction.

Proof.

We need two observations:
\{a^{0},a^{1},\dots,a^{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{{\mathsf{g}}}
is co-invariant for every
n
, and also
b-a\in\{a^{0},\dots,a^{n}\}
whenever
a\leq b
with
a,b\in\{a^{1},\dots,a^{n+1}\}
. We omit the rest of the proof, since it is essentially identical to that of Proposition
5.2
.
∎

We are now ready to give a direct proof that the CP property implies the CC property.

Theorem 5.19
.

If the normalized diagonal holomorphic pair
(k,\ell)
is CP, then it is also CC.

Proof.

First, we establish some notation. Given
m\geq 0
and
\Lambda\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, as before let
P_{\Lambda}
and
P_{m,\Lambda}
denote the projections onto the spans of
\{\ell_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
and
\{z^{a^{j}}:0\leq j\leq m\}\cup\{\ell_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
, respectively. Define
P^{0}_{\Lambda}
and
P^{0}_{m,\Lambda}
likewise, with
\ell
replaced by
\ell-1.
Finally, define
Q_{\Lambda},Q_{m,\Lambda},Q^{0}_{\Lambda}
and
Q^{0}_{m,\Lambda}
in an analogous manner, with
\ell
replaced by
k
throughout.

Now, fix
n\geq 1
, choose
J\geq 1
and
\{c_{a}:a\in\{a^{0},\dots,a^{n}\}\}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{J}
. Set
F=\{a^{0},\dots,a^{n}\}
and
p(z)=\sum_{a\in F}c^{*}_{a}z^{a}
and assume that the block matrix
C=\big(C_{a,b}\big)_{a,b\in F}
as in (
5.28
) is a contraction. Further, set
F^{+}=\{a^{1},\dots,a^{n+1}\}
and define the
F^{+}\times F^{+}
block matrix
C^{+}
as in (
5.29
). Our goal is to show that
C^{+}
is also a contraction. By Lemma
5.17
,
p
yields a bounded operator
M_{p}:\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}
. Setting
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{k}=\mathcal{H}_{k-1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\ell}=\mathcal{H}_{\ell-1}
, let
T_{p}:\mathcal{H}^{0}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}
denote the restricted operator
M_{p}|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}}
. Since
(k,\ell)
has the CP property, Lemma
4.6
implies that
the inequality of equation (
4.5
) implies the inequality of equation (
4.9
).
Here, we replace
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
by
\Lambda
and
z_{n+1}
by
0,
so that
\ell^{z_{n+1}}
in equation (
4.9
) becomes
\ell-1.
Hence

||(Q_{\Lambda}\otimes I_{J\times J})M^{*}_{p}(P_{\Lambda}\otimes I_{J\times J})||\geq||(Q^{0}_{\Lambda}\otimes I_{J\times J})T^{*}_{p}(P^{0}_{\Lambda}\otimes I_{J\times J})||,

(5.30)

for any
(n+1)
-point subset
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.
Set
\Lambda^{m}=\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{m}\},
for any
0\leq m\leq n.
By Lemma
5.15
, there exists a continuous map
v:(0,\delta]\to\Omega
such that, with
\Lambda^{0}_{t}=\Lambda^{0}\cup\{v(t)\}
, we have
P_{\Lambda^{0}_{t}}\to P_{0,\Lambda^{0}},\,P^{0}_{\Lambda^{0}_{t}}\to P^{0}_{0,\Lambda^{0}},\,Q_{\Lambda^{0}_{t}}\to Q_{0,\Lambda^{0}}
and
Q^{0}_{\Lambda^{0}_{t}}\to Q^{0}_{0,\Lambda^{0}}
in operator norm as
t\to 0
. Thus, we may replace
\Lambda
by
\Lambda^{0}_{t}
in (
5.30
) and let
t\to 0
to obtain

||(Q_{0,\Lambda^{0}}\otimes I_{J\times J})M^{*}_{p}(P_{0,\Lambda^{0}}\otimes I_{J\times J})||\geq||(Q^{0}_{0,\Lambda^{0}}\otimes I_{J\times J})T^{*}_{p}(P^{0}_{0,\Lambda^{0}}\otimes I_{J\times J})||,

for any
\Lambda^{0}=\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.
One may now proceed by induction; successive uses of Lemma
5.15
yield

||(Q_{m,\Lambda^{m}}\otimes I_{J\times J})M^{*}_{p}(P_{m,\Lambda^{m}}\otimes I_{J\times J})||\geq||(Q^{0}_{m,\Lambda^{m}}\otimes I_{J\times J})T^{*}_{p}(P^{0}_{m,\Lambda^{m}}\otimes I_{J\times J})||,

for every
0\leq m\leq n
and any
\Lambda^{m}=\{\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}\subseteq\Omega\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.
Setting
m=n
then yields

||(Q_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})M^{*}_{p}(P_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})||\geq||(Q^{0}_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})T^{*}_{p}(P^{0}_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})||,

(5.31)

where
P_{n}
and
P^{0}_{n}
are the projections onto
\textup{span}\{z^{a^{j}}:0\leq j\leq n\}
and
\textup{span}\{z^{a^{j}}:1\leq j\leq n+1\},
respectively, with
Q_{n}
and
Q^{0}_{n}
defined analogously. But
C
being a contraction is equivalent to
||(Q_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})M^{*}_{p}(P_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})||\leq 1
, which, in view of (
5.31
), yields
||(Q^{0}_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})T^{*}_{p}(P^{0}_{n}\otimes I_{J\times J})||\leq 1
. Thus,

is a contraction and our proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 6.2

Example 6.6

Fix

and
z,w,v\in X
are distinct. If
k(z,w)=0,
then,
\ell(z,w)=0
and at least one of the following assertions holds:

(i)

\ell(z,v)=\ell(w,v)=0
;

(ii)

either
k(z,v)=0
or
k(w,v)=0
.

Proof.

Fix
z,w\in X
and assume
k(z,w)=0
. Define
\psi:\{z\}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\psi(z)=\sqrt{\frac{\ell(z,z)}{k(z,z)}}.

Clearly,
[\ell(z,z)-|\psi(z)|^{2}k(z,z)]=[0]\succeq 0,
thus, by the CP property,
\psi
extends to a multiplier on
\{z,w\}
satisfying

\begin{split}0&\preceq\begin{bmatrix}\ell(z,z)-|\psi(z)|^{2}k(z,z)&\ell(z,w)-\psi(z)\overline{\psi(w)}k(z,w)\\
\ell(w,z)-\psi(w)\overline{\psi(z)}k(w,z)&\ell(w,w)-|\psi(w)|^{2}k(w,w)\end{bmatrix}\\
&=\begin{bmatrix}0&\ell(z,w)\\
\ell(w,z)&\ell(w,w)-|\psi(w)|^{2}k(w,w)\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

This gives us
\ell(z,w)=0
, as desired.

Now, let
v\in X\setminus\{z,w\}
and assume
k(z,v)\neq 0\neq k(w,v).
We will show that
\ell(z,v)=\ell(w,v)=0.
Define
\phi:\{z,w\}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\phi(x)=e^{i\vartheta_{x}}\sqrt{\frac{\ell(x,x)}{k(x,x)}},
where each
\vartheta_{x}\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen arbitrarily with the dependency of
\phi
on
\vartheta_{z},\vartheta_{w}
suppressed.
Clearly,
\ell(x,x)-|\phi(x)|^{2}k(x,x)=0
for
x\in\{z,w\}.
Since we also have
\ell(z,w)=k(z,w)=0,
the Pick matrix for
\phi
over
\{z,w\}
is identically zero. By the CP property,
\phi
can then be extended to a multiplier, still denoted
\phi
on
\{z,w,v\}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

0\preceq\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\ell(z,v)-\phi(z)\overline{\phi(v)}k(z,v)\\
0&0&\ell(w,v)-\phi(w)\overline{\phi(v)}k(w,v)\\
*&*&\ell(v,v)-\psi(v)\overline{\psi(v)}k(v,v)\end{bmatrix}.

Thus, we obtain
\ell(z,v)-\psi(z)\overline{\psi(v)}k(z,v)=\ell(w,v)-\psi(w)\overline{\psi(v)}k(w,v)=0.
Since
\ell(z,v)\neq 0,
we also have
k(z,v)\neq 0
. Solving for
\overline{\psi(v)}
twice,

\begin{split}e^{-i\vartheta_{z}}\frac{\ell(z,v)}{k(z,v)}\frac{\sqrt{k(z,z)}}{\sqrt{\ell(z,z)}}=\overline{\psi(v)}=e^{-i\vartheta_{w}}\frac{\ell(w,v)}{k(w,v)}\frac{\sqrt{k(w,w)}}{\sqrt{\ell(w,w)}}.\end{split}

Thus,

\ell(w,v)e^{-i(\vartheta_{w}-\vartheta_{z})}=k(w,v)\frac{\ell(z,v)}{k(z,v)}\frac{\sqrt{k(z,z)}}{\sqrt{\ell(z,z)}}\frac{\sqrt{\ell(w,w)}}{\sqrt{k(w,w)}}.

Since
\vartheta_{z},\vartheta_{w}\in\mathbb{R}
are arbitrarily,
\ell(z,v)=\ell(w,v)=0,
as desired.
∎

3.13

7.2.
Positivity conditions

We will now show that taking Schur complements with respect to
\ell
(i.e. replacing
\ell
by
\ell^{w}
) preserves the CP property for
(k,\ell).

Recall, for
Y\subseteq X
is a finite set
\ell^{Y}
denotes the kernel for the subpace of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
consisting of those functions that
vanish on
Y.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
(k,\ell)
be a CP pair of kernels on a set
X.
Given any finite
Y\subseteq X
, the pair
(k,\ell^{Y})
is a CP pair.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement with
Y=\{w\}
a singleton and we will show that
(k,\ell^{w})
is a CP pair by using the definition. Accordingly, suppose
Z=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset X
is a finite set of points,
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}\in\mathbb{M}_{J}
and

\big[\ell^{w}(x_{i},x_{j})-k(x_{i},x_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\succeq 0.

(7.1)

We are required to establish the existence of a contractive multiplier
\Phi\in\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J},\mathcal{H}_{\ell^{w}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J})
such that
\Phi(x_{i})=W_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n.

Assume first that
w\notin Z.
Set
w=x_{0}
and
consider the matrix

\big[\ell(x_{i},x_{j})-k(x_{i},x_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{0\leq i,j\leq n},

(7.2)

where we have set
W_{0}=\mathbf{0}.
Taking the Schur complement with respect to the
(0,0)
entry, we obtain that (
7.2
) is positive if and only if

\big[\ell^{x_{0}}(x_{i},x_{j})-k(x_{i},x_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\succeq 0,

which is simply (
7.1
). Since
(k,\ell)
is a CP pair, we obtain the existence of a contractive multiplier
\Phi:\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}\to\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}
such that
\Phi(x_{i})=W_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
\Phi(x_{0})=\mathbf{0}.
Thus, the range of
\Phi
is actually contained in

\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J}:f(x_{0})=\mathbf{0}\}=\mathcal{H}_{\ell^{w}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{J},

as desired.

Assume now that
w\in Z.
Without loss of generality,
w=x_{1}.
In this case, the
(1,1)
entry of (
7.1
) is
-W_{1}W_{1}^{*}k(x_{1},x_{1}),
which has to be non-negative. Since
k(x_{1},x_{1})>0,
we obtain
W_{1}=W_{1}W_{1}^{*}=\mathbf{0}
. Thus, the first row and column of (
7.1
) are identically zero and so that positivity condition is equivalent to

\big[\ell^{x_{1}}(x_{i},x_{j})-k(x_{i},x_{j})W_{i}W_{j}^{*}\big]_{2\leq i,j\leq n}\succeq 0

The existence of an interpolating multiplier
\Phi
can now be obtained via the CP property for
(k,\ell)
as in the previous case.
∎

Let
k,\ell,\tilde{s}
be kernels on
X
such that
k
is non-vanishing,
\tilde{s}
is a non-vanishing CP kernel and
\frac{\tilde{s}}{k},\frac{\ell}{\tilde{s}}
are both positive kernels. Further, let
M
be any closed
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell})
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
and denote its kernel by
\ell_{M}.
By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\frac{\ell_{M}}{\tilde{s}}\succeq 0.

Taking the Schur product with
\frac{\tilde{s}}{k}
gives

\frac{\ell_{M}}{k}\succeq 0.

(7.3)

Thus, the existence of a CP kernel
\tilde{s}
such that both
\frac{\tilde{s}}{k},\frac{\ell}{\tilde{s}}
are positive kernels forces
(k,\ell)
to satisfy a much stronger positivity condition than the one obtained in the conclusion of equation (
1.11
) in Theorem
1.10
. This stronger condition does not, however, imply
\tilde{s}
is a strong Shimorin certificate as Proposition
7.4
below shows. The obstruction is that, to be a strong Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
, the equality
k=(1-h)\tilde{s}
requires (for
k,\tilde{s}
non-vanishing)

\frac{\tilde{s}}{k}=\frac{1}{1-h},

and so
\frac{\tilde{s}}{k}
needs to be not just a kernel, but a CP kernel as well.

Proposition 7.4
.

There exist diagonal holomorphic kernels
k,\ell,\tilde{s}
such that
\tilde{s}
is a CP kernel and
\frac{\tilde{s}}{k},\frac{\ell}{\tilde{s}}
are positive kernels, but
(k,\ell)
is not a CP pair.

Proof.

For convenience, let
x=z\overline{w}.

Set

k(z,w)=\frac{1}{(1+x+4x^{2})(1-3x)}=\frac{1}{1-2x+x^{2}-12x^{3}}=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{2}[1-\frac{12x^{3}}{(1-x)^{2}}]}\succeq 0.

The first four coefficients of the power series expansion for
k
are
1,2,3,16,
starting with
k_{0}.
Thus,
computing the master certificate,
\vartheta_{1}=k_{1}=2;
since
k_{2}-k_{1}\vartheta_{1}=-1,
we have
\vartheta_{2}=0;
and next, since

k_{3}-k_{2}\vartheta_{1}=16-6=10>0,
we have
\vartheta_{3}=10.
Thus
\vartheta=2x+10x^{3}+\dots\,.

Set
s=\frac{1}{1-\vartheta}
and

\tilde{s}(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-3x}.

Thus,

\frac{\tilde{s}}{k}=1+x+4x^{2}\succeq 0.

On the other hand, the coefficient of the cubic term in the power series expansion of

f(z,w)=\frac{\tilde{s}(z,w)}{s(z,w)}=\frac{1-2x-10x^{3}-\dots}{1-3x}

is
-1
and thus
f
is not positive. Hence, by Theorem
6.4
,
(k,\tilde{s})
is not a CP pair, even though the CP kernel
\tilde{s}
has
k
as a factor.
∎

8.
A Closer Look at the Bergman Kernel

This section investigates pairs of kernel
(\mathfrak{b},\ell),
where
\mathfrak{b}
is the Bergman kernel and
\ell
is not necessarily diagonal.
It also contains a proof of Theorem
1.8
.

8.1.
Non-diagonal certificates

Let
\mathfrak{b}(z,w)=(1-z\overline{w})^{-2}
denote the Bergman kernel on
\mathbb{D}.
As seen in Example
6.6
, the master certificate
\vartheta
associated with
\mathfrak{b}
is

s(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-\vartheta(z\overline{w})}=\frac{1}{1-2z\overline{w}},\hskip 5.69046ptz,w\in\Omega^{1}_{\vartheta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}.

This kernel
s
satisfies
\mathfrak{b}=(1-h)s,
where
h(z,w)=(z\overline{w})^{2}\mathfrak{b}(z,w).
By Theorem
6.4
, given any diagonal holomorphic kernel
\ell
on a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C},
the pair
(\mathfrak{b},\ell,\Omega)
is CP if and only if
\Omega\subseteq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
and
\frac{\ell}{s}\succeq 0
. In this subsection, we will compute further examples of CP kernels
\tilde{s}
such that
\mathfrak{b}=(1-\tilde{h})\tilde{s}
for some
\tilde{h}.
In particular, we will utilize these examples to show that Theorem
6.4
fails if we do not assume
\ell
is diagonal.

Example 8.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
0<|\lambda|<2.
Define
g:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\begin{split}g_{\lambda}(z)=g(z)=&\frac{1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}(z,\lambda)}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{b}(\lambda,\lambda)}}}\\
=&\frac{2z\overline{\lambda}-(z\overline{\lambda})^{2}}{\sqrt{2\lambda\overline{\lambda}-(\lambda\overline{\lambda})^{2}}}\\
=&\frac{\overline{\lambda}z}{|\lambda|}\frac{(2-z\overline{\lambda})}{\sqrt{2-\lambda\overline{\lambda}}}.\end{split}

We have
|g|\leq\frac{2+|\lambda|}{\sqrt{2-|\lambda|^{2}.}}
on
\mathbb{D}.
We want
g
to be a contractive multiplier, so restrict
\lambda
to
0<|\lambda|<1
and set

D_{\lambda}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|g_{\lambda}(z)|<1\}.

Notice that
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}\subseteq D_{\lambda}
for every
0<|\lambda|<1
. Now, for
z,w\in D_{\lambda,}
we have

\begin{split}(g(z)\overline{g(w)}-1)\mathfrak{b}(z,w)+1=&\bigg[\frac{z\overline{w}(2-z\overline{\lambda})\,(2-\lambda\overline{w})}{2-\lambda\overline{\lambda}}-1\bigg]\mathfrak{b}(z,w)+1.\end{split}

The common denominator above is

(1-z\overline{w})^{2}\,(2-\lambda\overline{\lambda}).

The numerator is,

z\overline{w}(2-z\overline{\lambda})(2-\lambda\overline{w})-(2-\lambda\overline{\lambda})+(2-\lambda\overline{\lambda})(1-z\overline{w})^{2}=2z\overline{w}(z-\lambda)\overline{(w-\lambda)}.

Thus,

(g(z)\overline{g(w)}-1)\mathfrak{b}(z,w)+1=2z\overline{w}\frac{(z-\lambda)\overline{(w-\lambda)}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2}\,(2-\lambda\overline{\lambda})}=h_{\lambda}(z,w),

(8.1)

which is positive. In other words, for every
0<|\lambda|<1,
there exists a positive kernel
h_{\lambda}
on
D_{\lambda}
such that

\mathfrak{b}(z,w)=\frac{1-h_{\lambda}(z,w)}{1-g_{\lambda}(z)\overline{g_{\lambda}(w)}}=(1-h_{\lambda}(z,w))s_{\lambda}(z,w),

(8.2)

where
s_{\lambda}(z,w)=(1-g_{\lambda}(z)\overline{g_{\lambda}(w)})^{-1}
defines a CP kernel on
D_{\lambda}
.

Remark 8.2
.

Choosing
0<\lambda<1
in Example
8.1
, it can be verified that
|g_{\lambda}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})|<1
. Thus,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
does not contain

D_{\lambda}
, even though
(\mathfrak{b},s_{\lambda},D_{\lambda})
is a CP pair. Comparing with
the inclusions (
6.2
), we see that the conclusion of Theorem
6.4
does not apply to
(\mathfrak{b},s_{\lambda},D_{\lambda})
. At the same time, observe that given
0\neq\lambda=e^{i\theta}|\lambda|,
we have
|g_{\lambda}(-e^{i\theta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})|>1,
hence
D_{\lambda}
can never contain
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.8
, which we restate here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
denote the Bergman kernel on
\mathbb{D}
. There exists an
0<r<1
, and a one-parameter family of CP kernels
\{s_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

on
r\mathbb{D}
with the following properties:

(i)

For every
\lambda\in\Lambda,
there exists a kernel
h_{\lambda}
on
r\mathbb{D}
such that

\mathfrak{b}=(1-h_{\lambda})s_{\lambda};

(ii)

There do not exist kernels
s,h,f_{\lambda}
on
r\mathbb{D}
such that
s
is a CP kernel,
\mathfrak{b}=(1-h)s
and,
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda,

s_{\lambda}=sf_{\lambda}.

(8.3)

Proof.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
0<|\lambda|<\frac{1}{3},
define
g_{\lambda},s_{\lambda},h_{\lambda}
as in Example
8.1
. Further, set
\Lambda=\{0<|\lambda|<\frac{1}{3}\}
and
r=\frac{1}{3}.
It is straightforward to verify that
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}\subseteq D_{\lambda}.
Thus
\{s_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a family of CP kernels defined on
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}
and such that (
8.2
) holds, giving item (
i
).

Now suppose there is a kernel
s
defined on
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}
such that

\mathfrak{b}=(1-h)s

(8.4)

and, for each
\lambda,
there exists a (PsD) kernel
f_{\lambda}
on
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}
such that
s_{\lambda}=sf_{\lambda}.

Fix
\lambda\in\Lambda.

Since
\mathfrak{b}
is non-vanishing, so is
s.
Since
b=(1-h_{\lambda})s_{\lambda},
it follows that

0\preceq f_{\lambda}=\frac{s_{\lambda}}{s}=\frac{1-h}{1-h_{\lambda}}.

From
h_{\lambda}(0,0)=h_{\lambda}(0,\lambda)=h_{\lambda}(\lambda,\lambda)=0,
it follows that

0\preceq\begin{pmatrix}f_{\lambda}(0,0)&f_{\lambda}(\lambda,0)\\
f_{\lambda}(0,\lambda)&f_{\lambda}(\lambda,\lambda)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1-h(0,0)&1-h(\lambda,0)\\
1-h(0,\lambda)&1-h(\lambda,\lambda)\end{pmatrix}.

(8.5)

But
h
is a kernel (PsD), so the resulting inequality

\begin{pmatrix}h(0,0)&h(\lambda,0)\\
h(0,\lambda)&h(\lambda,\lambda)\end{pmatrix}\preceq\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}

implies
h(0,0)=h(\lambda,0)=h(0,\lambda)=h(\lambda,\lambda).

Since
\lambda
is arbitrary in
\Lambda
, it follows that
h(\lambda,\mu)=c
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D},
where
0\leq c=B(0,0)<1.
Thus

\mathfrak{b}=(1-c)s
is a complete Pick kernel over
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}
, which is easily seen to be false. Thus, item (
ii
) holds and the proof is complete.
∎

Thus, (
6.2
) and the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iv) in Theorem
6.4
might both fail if
\ell
is non-diagonal.

Letting

We conclude this subsection with another example of a (necessarily non-diagonal) certificate for

Example 8.5
.

Let

\begin{split}P(z,w)&=z\overline{w}\left[3-2z-2\overline{w}+2z\overline{w}\right]\,+\,8\frac{(z\overline{w})^{3}}{1-z\overline{w}}\\

We claim

1-\frac{k}{s}\succeq 0,

(8.6)

\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{s}\not\succeq 0,

in contrast to the kernels
G_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda}\overline{g_{\lambda}}

\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{s_{\lambda}}\succeq 0,

\begin{split}1-\frac{k}{s}&=\frac{(1-x)^{2}-(1-P)}{(1-x)^{2}}\\

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&1&z&z^{2}&z^{3}\\

8.2.
The largest domain disc for the Bergman kernel

If
\ell
is a diagonal holomorphic kernel and
(\mathfrak{b},\ell,\Omega)
is a CP pair, then
\Omega\subseteq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
by Theorem
6.4
. In this subsection, we prove that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
is actually the maximal disc that can serve as the domain of
any
holomorphic (but not necessarily diagonal)
\ell
such that
(\mathfrak{b},\ell)
is a CP pair. Compare with Remark
8.2
. For convenience, we will impose a few mild regularity assumptions on
\ell.

Definition 8.6
.

Let
\ell(z,w)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\ell_{ij}z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}
be a holomorphic kernel defined on an open neighborhood of the origin. We will say
\ell
is
regular
if

(i)

every collection of kernel functions
\{\ell_{z_{1}},\dots,\ell_{z_{n}}\}
with
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
distinct and sufficiently close to
0
forms a linearly independent set and

(ii)

all principal determinants of the coefficient matrix
\big(\ell_{ij}\big)^{\infty}_{i,j\geq 0}
are non-zero.

We point out that all diagonal holomorphic kernels with non-zero coefficients are regular.

Theorem 8.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
denote the Bergman kernel on
\mathbb{D}
and fix
0<r\leq 1
. If
\ell
is a holomorphic kernel on
r\mathbb{D}
such that
(\mathfrak{b},\ell)
is CP, then

r\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

We need a few preliminary lemmas. Let
\ell(z,w)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\ell_{ij}z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}
be any regular holomorphic kernel on
r\mathbb{D}
. Given
n\geq 1
and
0<u_{1}<u_{2}<\dots<u_{n}
sufficiently close to
0
, define the kernels

\ell_{[1]}(z,w):=\ell(z,w)-\frac{\ell(z,u_{1})\ell(u_{1},w)}{\ell(u_{1},u_{1})}

and

\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}(z,w):=\ell_{[1,\dots,n-1]}(z,w)-\frac{\ell_{[1,\dots,n-1]}(z,u_{n})\ell_{[1,\dots,n-1]}(u_{n},w)}{\ell_{[1,\dots,n-1]}(u_{n},u_{n})},

for every
n\geq 2.
Item
i
of Definition
8.6
ensures that all of the above denominators are non-vanishing. We also define, for every
n\geq 0,
the functions

\ell^{(n)}(z,w)=\sum_{i,j\geq n}\ell^{(n)}_{ij}z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}

recursively by setting
\ell^{(0)}\equiv\ell
and, for
n\geq 1
,

\ell^{(n)}_{ij}=\ell^{(n-1)}_{ij}-\frac{\ell^{(n-1)}_{i(n-1)}\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)j}}{\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)(n-1)}}.

Note that the denominators are non-vanishing because of item (
ii
) from Definition
8.6
.

Observe that the kernels
\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}
are generated by considering Schur complements of matrices of the form
\big[\ell(z_{i},z_{j})\big]
, while each
\ell^{(n)}
is generated by taking Schur complements of the coefficient matrix
\big(\ell_{ij}\big)
. The connection between these two types of Schur complements is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 8.8
.

\lim_{u_{n}\to 0}\cdots\lim_{u_{1}\to 0}\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}(z,w)=\ell^{(n)}(z,w)
pointwise on
r\mathbb{D}\times r\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. The base case
n=1
follows from observing that

\lim_{u_{1}\to 0}\frac{\ell(z,u_{1})\ell(u_{1},w)}{\ell(u_{1},u_{1})}=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{\ell_{i0}\ell_{0j}}{\ell_{00}}z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}.

Now, assume that the statement of the lemma holds for
n\geq 1.
We then have

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{n+1}\to 0}\lim_{u_{n}\to 0}\cdots\lim_{u_{1}\to 0}\ell_{[1,\dots,n+1]}(z,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{n+1}\to 0}\lim_{u_{n}\to 0}\cdots\lim_{u_{1}\to 0}\bigg(\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}(z,w)-\frac{\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}(z,u_{n+1})\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}(u_{n+1},w)}{\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{n+1}\to 0}\bigg(\ell^{(n)}(z,w)-\frac{\ell^{(n)}(z,u_{n+1})\ell^{(n)}(u_{n+1},w)}{\ell^{(n)}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq n}\bigg(\ell^{(n)}_{ij}-\frac{\ell^{(n)}_{in}\ell^{(n)}_{nj}}{\ell^{(n)}_{nn}}\bigg)z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq n+1}\bigg(\ell^{(n)}_{ij}-\frac{\ell^{(n)}_{in}\ell^{(n)}_{nj}}{\ell^{(n)}_{nn}}\bigg)z^{i}\overline{w}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\ell^{(n+1)}(z,w).\qed

We now combine Lemma
8.8
with Theorem
1.10
to obtain new necessary conditions for a pair of kernels
(k,\ell),
with
\ell
holomorphic, to be CP.

Lemma 8.9
.

Assume
k,\ell
are two kernels such that
\ell
is regular holomorphic on
r\mathbb{D}.
If
(k,\ell,r\mathbb{D})
is CP, then, with notation as above and
n
a non-negative integer,

\frac{\ell^{(n)}}{k}\succeq 0.

Proof.

The case
n=0
follows immediately from Theorem
1.9
. Now, assume
n\geq 1
and let
0<u_{1}<\dots<u_{n}.
By Theorem
1.10
, we know that
(k,\ell)
being CP implies that
(k,\ell^{z})
is also CP, for every
z\in r\mathbb{D}
. Thus,
(k,\ell^{u_{1}})=(k,\ell_{[1]})
must be CP as well. Continuing inductively, we obtain that
(k,\ell_{[1,\dots,n]})
is CP, hence, by Theorem
1.9
,

\frac{\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}}{k}\succeq 0.

Since pointwise limits preserve positive kernels, this last condition yields (in combination with Lemma
8.8
)

\frac{\ell^{(n)}}{k}=\lim_{u_{n}\to 0}\cdots\lim_{u_{1}\to 0}\frac{\ell_{[1,\dots,n]}}{k}\succeq 0.

∎

We will now apply Lemma
8.9
to pairs of the form
(\mathfrak{b},\ell).

Lemma 8.10
.

If
(\mathfrak{b},\ell,r\mathbb{D})
is a CP pair and
\ell
regular holomorphic, then, with notation as above and
n
a positive integer,

\ell^{(n)}_{nn}\geq 2\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)(n-1)}.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
be given. The coefficients of
z^{n}\overline{w}^{n},z^{n}\overline{w}^{n+1},z^{n+1}\overline{w}^{n},z^{n+1}\overline{w}^{n+1}
in the expansion of
\frac{\ell^{(n-1)}}{\mathfrak{b}}
form the
2\times 2
matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)(n-1)}&\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)n}\\
\ell^{(n-1)}_{n(n-1)}&\ell^{(n-1)}_{nn}-2\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)(n-1)}\end{bmatrix}

Since, by Lemma
8.9
,
\frac{\ell^{(n-1)}}{\mathfrak{b}}
is positive, the same must be true for the above
2\times 2
minor. Thus,

\big(\ell^{(n-1)}_{nn}-2\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)(n-1)}\big)\ell^{(n-1)}_{(n-1)(n-1)}\geq\ell^{(n-1)}_{n(n-1)}\ell^{(n)}_{(n-1)n},

which is precisely what we want.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this subsection.

Proof of Theorem
8.7
.

Assume
\ell
is regular holomorphic on
r\mathbb{D}
with
r>\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and
(k,\ell)
is a CP pair. By Lemma
8.10
, we know that

\ell^{(n+1)}_{(n+1)(n+1)}\geq 2\ell^{(n)}_{nn}\geq\dots\geq 2^{n+1}\ell_{00},

for all
n\geq 1,
where
\ell_{00}>0.
It is evident that, for all
n
,

\ell^{(n+1)}_{(n+1)(n+1)}\leq\ell^{(n)}_{(n+1)(n+1)}\leq\dots\leq\ell_{(n+1)(n+1)}.

Combining this with the previous inequalities, we obtain

\ell_{nn}\geq 2^{n}\ell_{00},

(8.7)

for all
n\geq 1.
Now, since
r>\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, we obtain the continuous function
F:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{C}
given by

F(\theta)=\ell\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\theta},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\theta}\big)=\sum_{r,p\geq 0}\ell_{rp}\frac{e^{i(r-p)\theta}}{(\sqrt{2})^{r+p}}.

Integrating term-wise with respect to
\theta
annihilates the off-diagonal terms and thus

\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(\theta)\ d\theta=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\ell_{ii}}{2^{i}}

is finite, contradicting (
8.7
).
∎

Remark 8.11
.

There are two key points in the proof of Theorem
8.7
.
First, given a CP pair
(\mathfrak{b},\ell)
with
\ell
diagonal holomorphic, it is possible to arrive at the conclusion that
s=(1-\vartheta)^{-1}
is a factor of
\ell
by only considering conditions of the form

\frac{\ell_{M}}{\mathfrak{b}}\succeq 0,

where
M
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
determined by the vanishing of all derivatives up to some order. In particular, the necessary conditions of Lemma
8.9
coincide with (
9.8
) if we assume that
\ell
is diagonal holomorphic.
Second, the disc is circularly symmetric, so the off diagonal terms of
\ell

can be annihilated by averaging.

Remark 8.12
.

Theorem
8.7
extends to more general Bergman-like kernels. In particular, given a holomorphic CP pair
(b,\ell,\Omega),
where
b
is as in Examples
6.6
,
6.8
, one obtains that

\Omega
does not properly contain the Reinhardt domain
\Omega_{\vartheta},

where
\vartheta
is, as usual, the master certificate associated with
b
. To get there, we observe again that the conclusion “
s=(1-\vartheta)^{-1}
is a factor of
\ell
” can be reached by only considering conditions of the form
\ell_{M}/b\succeq 0,
where
M
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
determined by the vanishing of all Taylor coefficients up to some order. Thus, the two observations given in Remark
8.11
carry over to the
(b,\ell,\Omega)
setting and the proof method of Theorem
8.7
generalizes accordingly. We omit the details.

9.
A Reformulation of Theorem
1.6

The algorithm from the proof of Theorem
5.10
gives us the following set of necessary and sufficient conditions for

(k,\ell)
to be a CP pair that make no explicit reference to the master certificate associated with
k
. It is our hope that this version of the theorem will lend itself more naturally to generalization.
For simplicity, we treat the single variable case; that is
{\mathsf{g}}=1.

Theorem 9.1
.

A pair
(k,\ell)
of normalized diagonal holomorphic kernels on an open disc centered at
0
, so that

k(z,w)=1+\sum_{j\geq 1}k_{j}(z\overline{w})^{j},\hskip 8.5359pt\ell(z,w)=1+\sum_{j\geq 1}\ell_{j}(z\overline{w})^{j},

is a CP pair if and only if

\frac{\ell}{k}\succeq 0

and also the following conditions are satisfied:
For every strictly increasing (infinite) sequence
0\leq m_{0}<m_{1}<m_{2}<\dots
and for all
j\geq 0,

\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{j})}}{k}\succeq 0,

where the kernels
\ell_{(m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{j})}
are defined inductively as follows:

(i)

First, set

\ell_{(m_{0})}(z,w):=\ell(z,w)-\bigg(\frac{\ell}{k}\bigg)_{m_{0}}(z\overline{w})^{m_{0}};

(ii)

Assuming
\ell_{(m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{j})}
has been defined, put

\ell_{(m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{j+1})}(z,w):=\ell_{(m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{j})}(z,w)-\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{j})}}{k}\bigg)_{m_{j+1}}(z\overline{w})^{m_{j+1}}.

Proof.

Define the sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
as follows: choose either
t_{1}=0
or
t_{1}=k_{1}
and then define, inductively,

\text{ either}\hskip 5.69046ptt_{n}=k_{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}t_{j}k_{n-j}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ or}\hskip 5.69046ptt_{n}=0.

(9.1)

Thus, we have a family of possible sequences here, one of which is the “distinguished” one originating from the master certificate
\vartheta
, as in Definition
1.5
. In the general case, we might have negative
t_{i}
’s too.

Now, fix such a sequence
\{t_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}^{n+1}
, where
n\geq 1
. Set
t_{0}=1
and define
v_{m}
for
0\leq m\leq n+1
inductively as follows. First, set

v_{0}=\begin{cases}0,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }t_{n+1}=0\\
1,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }t_{n+1}\neq 0\end{cases}

and then, for
1\leq m\leq n+1,
put

v_{m}=\begin{cases}0,\hskip 113.81102pt\text{ if }t_{n+1-m}=0\\
\ell_{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}v_{j}k_{m-j},\hskip 34.14322pt\text{ if }t_{n+1-m}\neq 0.\end{cases}

Finally, we also set

a_{m}=\begin{cases}0,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }t_{n+1-m}=0\\
1,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ if }t_{n+1-m}\neq 0\end{cases}

and
\tilde{a}_{m}=1-a_{m}
.
Thus,

v_{m}=a_{m}\bigg(\ell_{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}v_{j}k_{m-j}\bigg).

An application of Lemma
5.13
(with
d=n+1
and
S=\{0\leq k\leq n+1:t_{n+1-k}=0\}
) then yields
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n+1}\geq 0
and, in particular,

v_{n+1}=\ell_{n+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{n}v_{m}k_{n+1-m}\geq 0.

(9.2)

We now establish new notation. Set

\ell_{[0]}=\ell-\tilde{a}_{0}\bigg(\frac{\ell}{k}\bigg)_{0}

and then, inductively,

\ell_{[0,1,\dots,m]}(z,w)=\ell_{[0,1,\dots,m-1]}(z,w)-\tilde{a}_{m}\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,m-1]}}{k}\bigg)_{m}(z\overline{w})^{m},

(9.3)

for all
0\leq m\leq n
(remember that
n
is fixed). Our goal will be to show that

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,n]}}{k}\bigg)_{n+1}=\ell_{n+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{n}v_{m}k_{n+1-m}.

(9.4)

We proceed with complete induction. First, take
n=0
. If
t_{1}=0,
we have

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0]}}{k}\bigg)_{1}=\bigg(\frac{\ell-\big(\frac{\ell}{k}\big)_{0}}{k}\bigg)_{1}=\bigg(\frac{\ell-1}{k}\bigg)_{1}=\ell_{1}=\ell_{1}-v_{0}k_{1}.

Similarly, if
t_{1}\neq 0
, we have

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0]}}{k}\bigg)_{1}=\bigg(\frac{\ell}{k}\bigg)_{1}=\ell_{1}-k_{1}=\ell_{1}-v_{0}k_{1},

as desired. Now, for the inductive step, assume we have showed

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,q]}}{k}\bigg)_{q+1}=\ell_{q+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{q}v_{m}k_{q+1-m},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall\text{ }0\leq q\leq\rho,

(9.5)

Setting

\frac{1}{k(z,w)}=1+\sum_{j\geq 1}b_{j}(z\overline{w})^{j},

we compute

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho+1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+2}=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}-\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\Big(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}}{k}\Big)_{\rho+1}(z\overline{w})^{\rho+1}}{k}\Bigg)_{\rho+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho+2}+b_{1}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho+1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho}+b_{3}\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho-1}+\dots+b_{\rho+2}\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho+2}+b_{1}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho+1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-1]}\big)_{\rho}-\tilde{a}_{\rho}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{3}\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-1]}\big)_{\rho-1}+\dots+b_{\rho+1}\big(\ell_{[0,1]}\big)_{1}+b_{\rho+2}\big(\ell_{[0]}\big)_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho+2}+b_{1}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}\big)_{\rho+1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-1]}\big)_{\rho}-\tilde{a}_{\rho}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{3}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-2]}\big)_{\rho-1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho-1}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho-2]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\dots+b_{\rho+1}\bigg(\big(\ell_{[0]}\big)_{1}-\tilde{a}_{1}\bigg(\cfrac{\ell_{[0]}}{k}\bigg)_{1}\bigg)+b_{\rho+2}(1-\tilde{a}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{\rho+2}+b_{1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-\tilde{a}_{\rho}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho-1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{3}\bigg(\ell_{\rho-1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho-1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho-1}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho-2}v_{m}k_{\rho-1-m}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\dots

\displaystyle+b_{\rho+1}\big(\ell_{1}-\tilde{a}_{1}\big(\ell_{1}-v_{0}k_{1}\big)\big)+b_{\rho+2}v_{0}.

Since
b_{\rho+2}+k_{1}b_{\rho+1}+\dots+b_{1}k_{\rho+1}+k_{\rho+2}=0,
this last equality yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho+1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+2}=

\displaystyle\ell_{\rho+2}-v_{0}k_{\rho+2}+b_{1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-v_{0}k_{\rho+1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-v_{0}k_{\rho}-\tilde{a}_{\rho}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho-1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{3}\bigg(\ell_{\rho-1}-v_{0}k_{\rho-1}-\tilde{a}_{\rho-1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho-1}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho-2}v_{m}k_{\rho-1-m}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\dots

\displaystyle+b_{\rho+1}\big(\ell_{1}-v_{0}k_{1}-\tilde{a}_{1}\big(\ell_{1}-v_{0}k_{1}\big)\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho+1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+2}=

\displaystyle\ell_{\rho+2}-v_{0}k_{\rho+2}+b_{1}\bigg(a_{\rho+1}\big(\ell_{\rho+1}-v_{0}k_{\rho+1}\big)+\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\sum_{m=1}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(a_{\rho}\big(\ell_{\rho}-v_{0}k_{\rho}\big)+\tilde{a}_{\rho}\sum_{m=1}^{\rho-1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\dots+b_{\rho+1}v_{1}.

Proceeding by induction, assume we have showed for
0\leq t\leq\rho-1,

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho+1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+2}=

\displaystyle\ell_{\rho+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{\rho+2-m}

\displaystyle+b_{1}\bigg(a_{\rho+1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)+\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\sum_{m=t+1}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(a_{\rho}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)+\tilde{a}_{\rho}\sum_{m=t+1}^{\rho-1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{\rho-t}\bigg(a_{t+2}\bigg(\ell_{t+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{t+2-m}\bigg)+\tilde{a}_{t+2}v_{t+1}k_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{\rho+1-t}v_{t+1}.

Since
b_{\rho+1-t}+k_{1}b_{\rho-t}+\dots+k_{\rho+1-t}=0,
this last equality allows us to write

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho+1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{\rho+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{t+1}v_{m}k_{\rho+2-m}

\displaystyle+b_{1}\bigg(a_{\rho+1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)-v_{t+1}k_{\rho-t}+\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\sum_{m=t+1}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(a_{\rho}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)-v_{t+1}k_{\rho-1-t}+\tilde{a}_{\rho}\sum_{m=t+1}^{\rho-1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\dots

\displaystyle+b_{\rho-t}\bigg(a_{t+2}\bigg(\ell_{t+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{t}v_{m}k_{t+2-m}\bigg)-v_{t+1}k_{1}+\tilde{a}_{t+2}v_{t+1}k_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{\rho+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{t+1}v_{m}k_{\rho+2-m}

\displaystyle+b_{1}\bigg(a_{\rho+1}\bigg(\ell_{\rho+1}-\sum_{m=0}^{t+1}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)+\tilde{a}_{\rho+1}\sum_{m=t+2}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+1-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+b_{2}\bigg(a_{\rho}\bigg(\ell_{\rho}-\sum_{m=0}^{t+1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)+\tilde{a}_{\rho}\sum_{m=t+2}^{\rho-1}v_{m}k_{\rho-m}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\dots

\displaystyle+b_{\rho-t}v_{t+2}.

Setting
t=\rho-1
yields

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,\rho+1]}}{k}\bigg)_{\rho+2}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\rho+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+2-m}+b_{1}v_{\rho+1}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\rho+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho}v_{m}k_{\rho+2-m}-k_{1}v_{\rho+1}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\rho+2}-\sum_{m=0}^{\rho+1}v_{m}k_{\rho+2-m},

which is (
9.5
) for
q=\rho+1.
Thus, (
9.5
) holds for all
0\leq q\leq n.
Setting
q=n
then yields (
9.4
), which, in view of (
9.2
), implies

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,n]}}{k}\bigg)_{n+1}\geq 0,

(9.6)

for all
n\geq 0.

We are now ready to conclude our proof. Let
0\leq m_{0}<m_{1}<\dots<m_{j}
and fix
n\geq 0.
We want to show

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{j})}}{k}\bigg)_{n}\geq 0.

If
n<m_{0},
then

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{j})}}{k}\bigg)_{n}=\bigg(\frac{\ell}{k}\bigg)_{n}\geq 0.

Otherwise, let
m_{i}
denote the largest integer in
\{m_{0},\dots,m_{j}\}
such that
m_{i}\leq n.
Since

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{j})}}{k}\bigg)_{n}=\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{i})}}{k}\bigg)_{n},

it suffices to show

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{i})}}{k}\bigg)_{n}\geq 0.

(9.7)

If
m_{i}=n,
one obtains

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,n)}}{k}\bigg)_{n}=\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{i-1})}}{k}-\frac{1}{k}\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{i-1})}}{k}\bigg)_{n}(z\overline{w})^{n}\bigg)_{n}=0,

so (
9.7
) is satisfied. Next, assume
m_{i}\leq n-1.
Define the sequence
\{t_{\rho}\}^{n}_{\rho=1}
as in (
9.1
) and in such a way that
\tilde{a}_{\rho}=1
if
\rho\in\{m_{0},\dots,m_{i}\}
and
0

t_{n-\rho}=k_{n-\rho}-\sum_{r=1}^{n-\rho-1}t_{r}k_{n-\rho-r}

if
\rho\notin\{m_{0},\dots,m_{j}\}
and
t_{n-\rho}=0
otherwise. This way, we obtain (using notation as in (
9.3
))

\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{i})}}{k}=\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,n-1]}}{k}.

Equation (
9.6
) now yields

\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{i})}}{k}\bigg)_{n}=\bigg(\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,n-1]}}{k}\bigg)_{n}\geq 0,

as desired.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

Assume
(k,\ell)
is a normalized diagonal holomorphic CP pair. Choosing
m_{k}=k
, for all
k
, in Theorem
9.1
, one obtains

\frac{\ell_{[0,1,\dots,j]}}{k}=\frac{\ell-\sum_{k=0}^{j}\ell_{k}(z\overline{w})^{k}}{k}\succeq 0,

(9.8)

for all
j\geq 0.
Since (
9.8
) is of the form
\ell_{M}/k\succeq 0,
where
M
is the
\operatorname{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell})
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
that is determined by the vanishing of the first
j
derivatives, we actually have the stronger conclusion that

\big(k,\ell-\sum_{k=0}^{j}\ell_{k}(z\overline{w})^{k}\big)

(9.9)

is a CP pair for every
j\geq 0.
Indeed, letting
s
denote any strong Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
, Lemma
2.6
tells us that
s
is a strong Shimorin certificate for (
9.9
) as well. Note that (
9.9
) being CP implies (
9.8
) because of Theorem
1.9
.

Remark 9.3
.

Unfortunately, the conclusion of Remark
9.2
no longer holds for more general conditions of the form

\frac{\ell_{(m_{0},\dots,m_{j})}}{k}\succeq 0.

Indeed, consider the Bergman kernel
\mathfrak{b}
and set
\ell(z,w)=(1-\vartheta(z\overline{w}))^{-1}=(1-2z\overline{w})^{-1}
. Since
(\mathfrak{b},\ell)
is a CP pair, Theorem
9.1
tells us that

\frac{\ell_{(2)}}{\mathfrak{b}}\succeq 0,

where

\displaystyle\ell_{(2)}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\ell(z,w)-\bigg(\frac{\ell}{\mathfrak{b}}\bigg)_{2}(z\overline{w})^{2}

\displaystyle=\ell(z,w)-(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}+(\mathfrak{b}^{2}_{1}-\mathfrak{b}_{2}))(z\overline{w})^{2}

\displaystyle=1+2(z\overline{w})+3(z\overline{w})^{2}+\dots.

However,
(\mathfrak{b},\ell_{(2)})
is not a CP pair; if it were, then we would have

\frac{\ell_{(2)}-1}{\mathfrak{b}}\succeq 0,

which is easily seen to be false.

Remark 9.4
.

It is important that the sequence
m_{0},m_{1},m_{2},\dots
be strictly increasing. Indeed, choosing
k=\mathfrak{b}
and
\ell
as in Remark
9.3
, we have:

\ell_{(2,0)}=\ell_{(2)}-\bigg(\frac{\ell_{(2)}}{\mathfrak{b}}\bigg)_{0}=2(z\overline{w})+3(z\overline{w})^{2}+\dots,

but
\frac{\ell_{(2,0)}}{\mathfrak{b}}
is not positive.

10.
Open Questions

We have seen that if a pair
(k,\ell)
possesses a Shimorin certificate, then it is a CP pair (Theorem
4.5
). Does the converse hold?

Question 10.1
.

Let
(k,\ell)
be a CP pair of kernels on a set
X.
Does there exist a Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
?
∎

Specializing to holomorphic pairs over connected domains, Question
10.1
becomes (in view of Proposition
3.13
) the following.

Question 10.2
.

Let
(k,\ell)
be a CP pair of holomorphic kernels on a connected domain
\Omega.
Does there exist a strong Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
?
∎

Instead of tackling Questions
10.1
-
10.2
directly, it might be easier to first attempt to address certain consequences of the existence of Shimorin certificates. For instance, if the pair
(k,\ell)
has a strong Shimorin certificate, the same holds for
(k,\ell_{M}),
for any multiplier-invariant subspace
M
of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
(recall that
\ell_{M}
denotes the reproducing kernel of
M
). As such, we may ask:

Question 10.3
.

Let
(k,\ell)
be a CP pair of kernels on
X
. Given a
\text{Mult}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell})
-invariant subspace
M
of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, is it necessary that
(k,\ell_{M})
is also CP?
∎

One could also observe that, if the pair
(k,\ell)
has a (strong) Shimorin certificate, the same must be true for
(k,\ell h)
, for any kernel
h
, which leads to:

Question 10.4
.

Let
(k,\ell)
be a CP pair of kernels on
X
. Given an arbitrary kernel
h
on
X
, is it necessary that
(k,\ell h)
is also CP?
∎

Question 10.5
.

Let
(k,\ell,\Omega)
be a CP pair of holomorphic kernels with
\mathbf{0}\in\Omega
and such that
k
is also (normalized) diagonal.
Let
\vartheta
denote the master certificate associated with
k.
Can
\Omega
contain the closure of
\Omega_{\vartheta}^{1}?

∎

If no restriction is placed on
\ell
(e.g. analyticity), then
\Omega
can be anything; see Example
3.4
. We also point out that it is possible to have
\Omega\not\subset\Omega_{\vartheta}^{1}
in the above setting, see Remark
8.2
. However, we believe that the answer to the first (and thus also to the second) part of Question
10.5
is no. This is indeed the case when
k
is Bergman-like (as in Examples
6.6
,
6.8
); see Remark
8.12
. The main obstacle in generalizing the proof of Theorem
8.7
to arbitrary (holomorphic) pairs
(k,\ell)
lies in the fact that the necessary conditions of Lemma
8.9
will not, in general, be sufficient for the CP property, even if both kernels are diagonal (Proposition
7.4
).

We can also formulate a version of Question
10.5
where the assumption of the CP property is replaced by the (a priori stronger) assumption of the existence of a Shimorin certificate. Our motivation stems from the observation that all non-diagonal certificates associated with the Bergman kernel in Section
8
have domains that do not contain
\Omega^{1}_{\vartheta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
; see Remark
8.2
and Example
8.5
.

Question 10.6
.

Suppose
k
is a normalized diagonal holomorphic kernel
with master certificate
\vartheta.
If
k=(1-h)s
for a kernel
h
and a holomorphic CP kernel
s
on
\mathbf{0}\in\Omega
with
\Omega^{1}_{\vartheta}\subseteq\Omega
that is also normalized at
\mathbf{0}
, must
s
be diagonal (in which case
s=(1-\vartheta)^{-1}
, by definition of
\vartheta
, and
\Omega\equiv\Omega^{1}_{\vartheta}
)? What if
s
is not holomorphic?
∎

A pair
(k,\ell)
of non-vanishing holomorphic kernels on a domain
\mathbf{0}\in\Omega

has a Shimorin certificate if and only if there is a kernel
p
such that

\ell^{\mathbf{0}}\succeq p\,\ell
and
k^{\mathbf{0}}\preceq p\,k
, by Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
3.13
. Motivated by this, we offer the following definition: given a (non-vanishing) holomorphic kernel
k
and another kernel
p
on
\Omega,
we will say that
p
is a
pre-certificate for
k
if
k^{\mathbf{0}}\preceq p\,k
. Observe that if
p,q
are both pre-certificates for
k
with
q\preceq p
and
p
is also a Shimorin certificate for
(k,\ell)
, then
q
has to be a Shimorin certificate as well. We will say that the pre-certificate
p
for
k
is
minimal
if, whenever
q
is a pre-certificate with
q\preceq p,
then
q=p
.
In the case
k=\mathfrak{b}
is the Bergman kernel, the kernels
p_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda}(z)\overline{g_{\lambda}(w)}
are minimal pre-certificates, where we set
p_{0}(z,w)=2z\overline{w}
. Indeed, if
q
is any pre-certificate for
\mathfrak{b}
satisfying
q\preceq p_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda
, then
p_{\lambda}
being rank
1
implies the existence of
0\leq c\leq 1
such that
q=cp_{\lambda}
. It is easy to see that
2c^{2}z\overline{w}=c^{2}p_{0}(z,w)
cannot satisfy
\mathfrak{b}=(1-h)/(1-c^{2}p_{0})
if
c<1
. Also, for
\lambda\neq 0
and
c<1
, we have

(c^{2}p_{\lambda}(\lambda,\lambda)-1)\mathfrak{b}(\lambda,\lambda)+1<0,

and minimality has been established.

Question 10.7
.

Do there exist minimal pre-certificates for the Bergman kernel
\mathfrak{b}
that are not of the form
p_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda}(z)\overline{g_{\lambda}(w)}
?

Question 10.8
.

As a variation on Question
10.7
and still with
\mathfrak{b}
the Bergman kernel, if
\ell
is holomorphic and
(\mathfrak{b},\ell)
has a strong Shimorin certificate, then does it have a rank one certificate; that is, a certificate of the form
\frac{1}{1-g(z)\overline{g(w)}}
for a function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
?
∎

There is a competing notion of minimal certificate. Given holomorphic kernels
k
and
s
on
\mathbf{0}\in\Omega
with
s
CP, we could define
s
to be a minimal certificate for
k
over
\Omega
if
k=(1-h)s
for some kernel
h
and also the following is satisfied: whenever
\tilde{s}
is a holomorphic CP kernel on
\Omega
satisfying
k=(1-\tilde{h})\tilde{s}
for some kernel
\tilde{h}
and also
s/\tilde{s}\succeq 0
, we have that
s
and
\tilde{s}
are equal up to a dyad. We point out that, given any sub-domain
\mathbf{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
,
s
is a minimal certificate for
k
over
\Omega^{\prime}
if and only if it is one over
\Omega.
This can be proved using standard reproducing kernel arguments; see e.g. the proof of the inclusion
\Omega^{1}_{\vartheta}\subset\Omega_{k}
in Proposition
6.2
.

Question 10.9
.

Are the certificates
(1-g_{\lambda}(z)\overline{g_{\lambda}(w)})^{-1}
(where
|\lambda|<1
) minimal for
\mathfrak{b}
over
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}
with respect to this competing definition of minimality?

So, we can find minimal certificates, but what about minimum ones? Given a kernel
k
and a CP kernel
s
on a set
X
with
k=(1-h)s
over
X,
we will say that
s
is a
minimum
certificate for
k
over
X
if, whenever
\tilde{s}
is another CP kernel on
X
with
k=(1-\tilde{h})\tilde{s}
, then
\tilde{s}/s\succeq 0.
It is not hard to see that such a certificate will be unique up to a dyad. Now, if
k
is CP, then, for any kernel
\ell,

(k,\ell)
is CP if and only if
\ell/k\succeq 0
(this follows from Theorems
1.3
and
1.9
). Thus,
k
is a minimum certificate for itself, and this continues to hold over any sub-domain
X^{\prime}\subset X.
This motivates the following question.

Question 10.10
.

Let
k
be a kernel on a non-empty set
X
. Suppose there exists a CP kernel
s
on
X
with
k=(1-h)s
and such that, for any non-empty
X^{\prime}\subset X,
the kernel
s|_{X^{\prime}\times X^{\prime}}
is a minimum certificate for
k|_{X^{\prime}\times X^{\prime}}
. Does that imply that
k
is CP (in which case
k=s
up to a dyad)?
∎

Now, Corollary
6.5
tells us that no minimum certificate exists for
k=\mathfrak{b}
over
\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}.
What if we insist on a larger domain?

Question 10.11
.

Does a minimum certificate exist for
\mathfrak{b}
over
\Omega^{1}_{\vartheta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{D}
?
∎

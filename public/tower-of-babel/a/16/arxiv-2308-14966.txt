Asymptotics of a Modified Holomorphic Analytic Torsion
Andrés Larraín-Hubach

Abstract
We prove a formula for the first few terms of the asymptotic expansion of the holomorphic analytic torsion of the Dirac operator modified by the Clifford action of a real three-form.



Keywords:
Getzler Grading, Dirac Operator, Heat Kernel, Holomorphic Analytic Torsion.

MSC 2020:
58J20, 58J99.

1
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to analyze the asymptotic behavior of a modification of the holomorphic analytic torsion of Ray-Singer and obtain results similar to those of
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
.

Let
(M,J,g^{TM})
be a Kähler manifold with complex structure
J
,
L
a
positive
holomorphic line bundle over
M
, and
E
a holomorphic bundle over
M
. Both
L
and
E
are equipped with their Chern connections denoted by
\nabla^{L}
and
\nabla^{E}
respectively. Using the canonical Spin
c
structure induced by the complex structure, one defines the Dirac operator
D_{p}
acting on
\Gamma(\Lambda^{0,*}T^{*}M\otimes L^{p}\otimes E)
, where
L^{p}
denotes the
p
-tensor power of
L.
In this setting, Bismut and Vasserot
[
2
]
proved an explicit formula for the leading term of the holomorphic analytic torsion of
D_{p}
as
p\to\infty
. The subleading terms of this expansion were computed, under additional hypotheses on the line bundle
L
, by Finski in
[
4
]
.

Here, we consider a similar situation but change
D_{p}
to
\mathfrak{D}_{p}=D_{p}+c(B)
, where
c(B)
denotes Clifford multiplication by a real closed
3
-form
B
. The new operator is still odd with respect to the canonical
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading induced by the spin
c
structure. This type of modification of
D_{p}
has already been used in the context of non-commutative cocycles in
[
10
]
and index theory in
[
1
]
.

Mathai and Wu defined a similar twisting for the analytic torsion
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
. They used an operator
D^{E}=\nabla^{E}+H\wedge\cdot
, where
\nabla^{E}
is a flat connection on a bundle
E
and
H
is an odd-degree closed differential form. The operator
D^{E}
acts on
E
-valued differential forms. Our main motivation was to obtain analogous results for a special twisting in the spin
c
case, but using the holomorphic analytic torsion instead of the analytic one. For twistings of the Capell-Miller holomorphic torsion see
[
5
]
.

Denoting by
\theta_{p}(z)
the
\theta
-function associated to
\mathfrak{D}_{p}
(see definition 2 below), our main result is the following

Theorem 1
.

As
p\to\infty
, there is an asymptotic expansion

\theta_{p}^{\prime}(0)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(\alpha_{i}\ln p+\beta_{i})p^{n-i}

(1)

such that

\begin{split}\alpha_{0}&=\frac{n\,\mathrm{rk}(E)}{2}\int_{M}\mathrm{det}(\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi)\,dv_{y}\\
\beta_{0}&=\frac{\mathrm{rk}(E)}{2}\int_{M}\mathrm{det}(\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi)\ln(\mathrm{det}(\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi))\,dv_{y}\\
\end{split}

(2)

Under the additional hypothesis for the curvature of
L
that

\omega=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{L}(\cdot,\cdot)=g^{TM}(J\cdot,\cdot)

(3)

we also compute

\begin{split}\alpha_{1}&=\frac{n\mathrm{rk}(E)}{2\pi}\int_{M}|\mathfrak{B}|^{2}_{y}dv+\frac{n}{2}\int_{M}c_{1}(E)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\\
&\,\,\,+\frac{(3n+1)\mathrm{rk}(E)}{12}\int_{M}c_{1}(TM)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\\
&\,\,\,-\int_{M}\big[\dfrac{\mathrm{str}(N\mathfrak{H}_{j\overline{j}}e^{-2\pi uN})}{(1-e^{-2\pi u})^{n+1}}({u}+{u}e^{-2\pi u}-\frac{1}{\pi}(1-e^{-2\pi u}))\big)\big]_{u=0}\,dv,\end{split}

(4)

where
\mathfrak{H}
is a certain two-form defined in (
87
) that depends on
\mathfrak{B}
, and
\int_{M}[\cdot]_{u=0}\,dv
means taking the
u=0
coefficient.

Notice that the leading terms of the asymptotic expansion are the same as in the untwisted case
[
2
]
. The proof that we present of (
2
) in the upcoming sections is based on the modified Getzler’s grading technique explained in
[
6
, sect 3.]
. The proof of (
4
) is adapted from the argument in
[
4
, Sec. 4]
and it is based on Getzler’s rescaling technique. It is interesting to see the grading and rescaling techniques, both ubiquitous in index theory, used in the same calculation.

For future research, we would like to understand what an anomaly formula would look like in the case of the torsion of
\mathfrak{D}_{p}
. This seems to be a difficult question because the familiar rescaling techniques used for example in
[
7
]
do not work in this situation.

2
Holomorphic Torsion modified by a Three-Form

Let
M
be a closed and complex manifold of complex dimension
n
with complex structure
J
and Kähler metric
g^{TM}
. The corresponding Kähler form is denoted by

\tilde{\Theta}=g^{TM}(J\cdot,\cdot)=\langle J\cdot,\cdot\rangle

(5)

It’s important to mention that we use both notations
g^{TM}
and
\langle\rangle
for the metric and its
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear extension throughout the paper. The volume form is given by
dv=\frac{\tilde{\Theta}^{n}}{n!}.

The metric induces a canonical spin
c
-structure on
M
. The corresponding spin bundles over
M
can be identified as
S^{+}=\Lambda^{0,\mathrm{Even}}T^{*}M
and
S^{-}=\Lambda^{0,\mathrm{Odd}}T^{*}M
. The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{TM}
induces a connection on the spin bundle that coincides with the connection
\nabla^{\Lambda^{0,*}}
on the bundle
\Lambda^{0,*}T^{*}M
.

The underlining real manifold has real dimension
2n.
We denote by
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
an orthonormal frame of
T^{1,0}M
, while
\{\bar{w}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
denotes the corresponding orthonormal frame of
T^{0,1}M
. Using these frames, we define a real orthonormal frame
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{2n}
for
TM
by

\begin{split}e_{2j-1}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(w_{j}+\bar{w}_{j})\\
e_{2j}&=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\sqrt{2}}(w_{j}-\bar{w}_{j})\\
\end{split}

(6)

We use Einstein’s summation convention. That is, repeated indices are summed over.

2.1
The Modified Dirac Operator

Given a real vector
v\in TM
, its Clifford action on
S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
is defined by

c(v)=\sqrt{2}\big(\bar{v}^{(1,0),\star}\wedge-\iota_{v^{(0,1)}}\big),

where
v=v^{(1,0)}+v^{(0,1)}\in T^{(1,0)}M\oplus T^{(0,1)}M
and
\bar{v}^{(1,0),\star}
is the metric dual of
v^{(1,0)}.

We use two holomorphic bundles over
M
. The first one is denoted by
E
with hermitian metric
h^{E}
and Chern connection
\nabla^{E}.
The second one is a line bundle
L
with metric
h^{L}
and Chern connection
\nabla^{L}
. The curvature
R^{L}
induces an endomorphism
\dot{R}^{L}
of
T^{(1,0)}M
defined by
R^{L}(X,\bar{Y})=g(\dot{R}^{L}X,\bar{Y})
for
X,Y\in T^{(0,1)}M.
We denote

\omega_{d}=-\sum_{l,m}R^{L}(w_{l},\bar{w}_{m})\bar{w}^{m}\wedge\iota_{\bar{w}_{l}}.

(7)

Given a natural number
p\geq 1
, the
p
-tensor power of
L
is denoted by
L^{p}.

For the arguments in this paper, we also assume that
L
is a positive line bundle. In other words, the curvature
R^{L}
associated to
h^{L}
is such that
\sqrt{-1}R^{L}
is a positive
(1,1)
-form. We use the notation
\omega=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{L}.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{p}=\Lambda^{0,*}\otimes E\otimes L^{p}=(S^{+}\oplus S^{-})\otimes E\otimes L^{p}
, the connections on each bundle induce a connection on
\mathcal{E}
which we denote by
\hat{\nabla}^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}.
It follows that
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded with
\mathcal{E}_{p}^{\pm}=S^{\pm}\otimes E\otimes L^{p}.

The corresponding Dirac operator that acts on smooth sections of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is defined by

D^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}c(e_{j})\hat{\nabla}^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}_{e_{j}}.

(8)

This is an odd operator with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading defined above.

Let
B\in\Omega^{3}(M)
be a
closed
real three-form such that
c(B)^{\star}=c(B)
. The twisted Dirac operator is defined by

\mathfrak{D}_{p}=D^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}+c(B)

(9)

This is still a self-adjoint and odd operator.

Definition 2
.

We use
B
and
\nabla^{\Lambda^{0,*}}
to define a new connection on the spin bundle given by

\nabla^{S}_{X}=\nabla^{\Lambda^{0,*}}+c(\iota_{X}B),

(10)

for any vector
X\in TM.

The new connection
\nabla^{S}
induces a new connection on
\mathcal{E}_{p}
denoted by
\nabla^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}
. Using this new connection, we get a familiar formula for the square of
\mathfrak{D}_{p}
given by

\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}=(\nabla^{\mathcal{E}_{p}})^{\star}\nabla^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}+\frac{r^{M}}{4}+c(R^{E}+\frac{1}{2}R^{\det}+pR^{L})-2|B|^{2},

(11)

where
r^{M}
is the scalar curvature of
M
,
R^{\det}
is the curvature of the induced connection on the determinant bundle of
S
by the Levi-Civita connection, and
|B|
is the pointwise norm of the closed three-form
B
.

The following spectral gap is crucial to this paper.

Lemma 3
.

Given
p
large enough, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2})\subset\{0\}\cup(Cp,\infty).

(12)

Also,
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathfrak{D}^{-}_{p})=\{0\}
and
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{+})\subset\Gamma(\Lambda^{(0,0)}\otimes E\otimes L^{p}).

Proof.

This is a consequence of (
11
), the positivity of
L
, and the compactness of
M
. See
[
3
, thm. 1.5.8.]
.
∎

2.2
The Modified Torsion

Given a vector bundle endomorphism
A:\mathcal{E}_{p}\to\mathcal{E}_{p}
,
\mathrm{str}(A_{y})
denotes its supertrace over the fiber
(\mathcal{E}_{p})_{y}
for
y\in M
, and
\mathrm{Str}(A)=\int_{M}\mathrm{str}(A_{y})\,dv_{y}
. Here, we integrate against the volume form
dv_{y}
.

Let
\Pi_{p}
denote the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathrm{Ker}\,\mathfrak{D}_{p}
and
\Pi_{p}^{\perp}=1-\Pi_{p}.
The
\theta
-function of
\mathfrak{D}_{p}
is defined by

\theta_{p}(z)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}\Pi_{p}^{\perp})\,du,

(13)

where
N
is the number operator which acts on
\Lambda^{0,k}T^{*}M
by multiplication by
k
. It is well known that
\theta_{p}(z)
is a meromorphic function which is holomorphic at
z=0
.

Definition 4
.

The twisted analytic torsion is defined by
e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{p}^{\prime}(0)}
. See
[
3
, def. 5.5.4]

For the proof of (
2
), two useful observations are needed: first, the spectral gap (
12
) implies that
\mathrm{Str}(N\Pi_{p})=0
for
p
large enough and, second, a simple substitution modifies the definition of
\theta_{p}(z)
to

p^{z}\theta_{p}(z)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}\Pi_{p}^{\perp})\,du,

(14)

3
Expansions

In this section we obtain two asymptotic expansions of the heat kernel
e^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}}
. One in powers of
p
, valid for
p
large, and the other one in terms of powers of
u
which is valid as
u\to 0^{+}.

3.1
Heat Kernel

Let
y\in M
be the point that we use as the center of our fixed normal coordinate chart. We use parallel transport, with respect to the connection
\nabla^{S}
, along geodesics emanating from
y
to extend a frame of
S_{y}
to the whole coordinate chart. Notice that we do not use the Levi-Civita connection to build this local frame. Similarly, using parallel transport, we extend orthonormal frames of
E_{y}
and
L_{y}
to synchronous frames over the whole coordinate chart. From this point on,
\{\mathrm{x}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2n}
denote the normal local coordinates where
y
corresponds to
\mathrm{x}=0.

In these local coordinates (see (
98
)) we have that

\nabla^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}_{\partial_{\mathrm{x}_{i}}}=\partial_{\mathrm{x}_{i}}+\beta_{ij}\mathrm{x}_{j}+p\Gamma_{i}^{L}+\Gamma_{i}^{E},

(15)

where
\beta_{ij}\in\mathrm{End}(S_{y}).
The connection symbols
\Gamma^{L}
and
\Gamma^{E}
vanish at
\mathrm{x}=0.

Define

q_{t}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{n}}e^{\frac{-d^{2}(x,y)}{4t}},

(16)

where
d(x,y)
is the geodesic distance between
x
and
y
in
M.
We denote the integral kernel of
e^{-t\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}
by
e^{-t\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}(x,y).

The classical theory developed in
[
11
, ch. 7]
or
[
12
, ch. 3]
implies that there is an asymptotic expansion

e^{-t\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}(x,y)\sim_{t\to 0^{+}}\chi(d(x,y))q_{t}(x,y)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{j}\Theta_{j,p}(x,y).

(17)

Here
\chi
is a smooth cut-off equal to
1
for
d(x,y)\leq\frac{\mathrm{inj^{2}}}{4}
, where
\mathrm{inj}
denotes the injectivity radius of
M
. This expansion is valid in the
C^{l}
-norm of
M\times M
for any
l>0.
It is also uniform in
t
if
t
takes values inside a compact subset of
(0,\infty).
See
[
6
, sec. 2]
.

Using the coordinates
\{\mathrm{x}_{j}\}
, we can write
\Theta_{j,p}(\mathrm{x})
instead of
\Theta_{j,p}(x,y)
. The
\Theta_{j,p}(\mathrm{x})
are smooth functions supported near
\mathrm{x}=0
and defined by the following recurrence relation

\begin{split}\Theta_{0,p}(\mathrm{x})&=C|g|^{-1/4}(\mathrm{x})\\
\Theta_{j,p}(\mathrm{x})&=\frac{-1}{|g|^{1/4}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{j-1}|g|^{1/4}(s\mathrm{x})(\mathfrak{D}^{2}_{p}\Theta_{j-1,p})(s\mathrm{x})\,ds,\end{split}

(18)

where
|g|
denotes the determinant of the metric
g
and we integrate along geodesics emanating from
y
, which corresponds to
\mathrm{x}=0
.

From (
17
) we get the following expansion on the diagonal

p^{-n}e^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}(y,y)\sim\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{j-n}}{2^{j}p^{j}}\Theta_{j,p}(y,y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi p)^{n}}\sum_{j=-n}^{\infty}\Theta_{j+n,p}(y)\frac{u^{j}}{2^{j}p^{j}}

(19)

Since convergence in (
19
) is uniform in
y\in M
, we also get

p^{-n}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\sim\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{j-n}}{2^{j}p^{j}}\int_{M}\mathrm{str}(N\Theta_{j,p}(y,y))\,dv_{y}

(20)

Using the local coordinates defined above, each
\Theta_{j,p}(\mathrm{x})
can be expanded as a formal sum of the form
\sum_{I,l}a_{I,l}\mathrm{x}_{I}p^{l}
with the
a_{I,l}\in\mathrm{End}(S_{y}\otimes E_{y}\otimes L^{p}_{y})
independent of
p
and
\mathrm{x}
. In these expansions, for a multi-index
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
, we define
\mathrm{x}_{I}=\mathrm{x}_{i_{1}}\ldots\mathrm{x}_{i_{k}}.
We need to know the largest possible power of
p
in each
\Theta_{j,p}(y,y)=\Theta_{j,p}(0).

Definition 5
.

Consider the vector space of differential operators written formally as sums

T=\sum_{l,I,J}a_{l,I,J}p^{l}\mathrm{x}_{J}\partial_{\mathrm{x}}^{J},

(21)

where the
a_{l,I,J}\in\mathrm{End}(S_{y}\otimes E_{y}\otimes L^{p}_{y})
are independent of
p
. The
p
-grading assigns the following values:
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{x}_{j})=-1
,
\mathrm{deg}(p)=2
and
\mathrm{deg}(\partial_{\mathrm{x}_{j}})=1.
The coefficients
a_{l,I,J}
have degree zero. The
p
-grading of a sum is the maximum of the
p
-gradings of its summands.

The most important property of the
p
-grading is the following

Lemma 6
.

Given two differential operators
T
and
S
as in (
21
), one has
\mathrm{deg}(T\circ S)\leq\mathrm{deg}(T)+\mathrm{deg}(S)
.

Proof.

See
[
6
, thm. 3]
.
∎

It follows from (
15
) and definition
5
above that
\mathrm{deg}(\nabla^{\mathcal{E}_{p}}_{\partial_{\mathrm{x}_{i}}})\leq 1
,
\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{D}_{p})\leq 1,
and
\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2})\leq 2
. The recurrence (
18
) implies that
\mathrm{deg}(\Theta_{0,p})\leq 0
and
\mathrm{deg}(\Theta_{j,p})\leq 2j.
Notice that the highest possible power of
p
that appears in
\Theta_{j,p}(0)
is
p^{j}
. This follows from (
18
) and the observation that a summand in
\Theta_{j,p}
with a power of
p
higher than
j
, that does not vanish at
\mathrm{x}=0
, would have
\mathrm{deg}>2j.

This implies that we have an expansion

\Theta_{l,p}(y)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta_{l,p,i}(y)p^{l-i},

(22)

where the
\theta_{l,p,i}(y)
are independent of
p.

Replacing in (
19
) we get

e^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}(y,y)\sim\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}}\sum_{j\geq-n}\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta_{j+n,p,i}(y)\frac{p^{n-i}u^{j}}{2^{j}}

(23)

This gives, comparing with
[

, Thm. 2.5]
, that

e^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}(y,y)\sim\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i,u}(y)p^{n-i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\big(\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}}\sum_{j\geq-n}\theta_{j+n,p,i}(y)\frac{u^{j}}{2^{j}}\big)p^{n-i}

(24)

Similarly,
[
4
]
uses an expansion, valid as
u\to 0^{+}
, of each
a_{i,u}(y)
given by

\begin{split}a_{i,u}(y)&=\sum_{j\geq-n}a_{i}^{[j]}(y)u^{j}\\
&=\sum_{j\geq-n}\big(\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}}2^{-j}\theta_{j+n,p,i}(y)\big)u^{j}\end{split}

(25)

3.2
Expansion of Analytic Torsion

We use (
24
) and (
25
) to expand
\theta_{p}^{\prime}(0)
in powers of
p>>0
. We need the following well-known fact

\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}=z-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)z^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3}),\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{as}\,\,z\to 0.

Start differentiating both sides of (
14
)

\begin{split}\theta_{p}(0)\ln p+\theta_{p}^{\prime}(0)&=\frac{d}{dz}|_{z=0}\left(-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du\right)\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{d}{dz}|_{z=0}\left(-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Na_{i,u})\,du\right)p^{n-i}\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}p^{n-i}\end{split}

(26)

Now,

\begin{split}\theta_{p}(z)&=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du\\
&=-\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{p^{n-i}}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Na_{i,u})\,du-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du\\
&=-\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{p^{n-i}}{\Gamma(z)}\sum_{j\geq-n}\int_{0}^{1}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Na_{i}^{[j]})u^{j}\,du-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du\\
&=-\sum_{i\geq 0}\left(\frac{p^{n-i}}{\Gamma(z)}\sum_{j\geq-n}\frac{\mathrm{Str}(Na_{i}^{[j]})}{z+j}\right)-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du\\
\end{split}

(27)

Notice that
\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Ne^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}})\,du
is a holomorphic function that vanishes at
z=0.
This implies that

\begin{split}\theta_{p}(0)=-\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{Str}(Na_{i}^{[0]})p^{n-i}\end{split}

(28)

Now (
26
), (
27
) and (
28
) imply that

\begin{split}\theta_{p}^{\prime}(0)&=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{Str}(Na_{i}^{[0]})p^{n-i}\ln p+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}p^{n-i}\\
&=\sum_{i\geq 0}(\alpha_{i}\ln p+\beta_{i})p^{n-i},\end{split}

(29)

which proves (
1
). More explicitly, we have the following identities

\begin{split}\alpha_{0}&=\mathrm{Str}(Na_{0}^{[0]})\\
\beta_{0}&=\frac{d}{dz}|_{z=0}\big(-\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{z-1}\mathrm{Str}(Na_{0,u})\,du\big)\\
\alpha_{1}&=\mathrm{Str}(Na_{1}^{[0]})\end{split}

(30)

Our main result is a calculation of explicit expressions, in terms of integrals of characteristic classes, for
\alpha_{0}
,
\beta_{0}
and, under additional hypotheses, also
\alpha_{1}
.

3.2.1
Computation of
\alpha_{0}

In order to compute
\alpha_{0}
, we need to isolate the terms in (

) with the highest
p-
power and use them in (

). This is done with the aid of the modified Getzler rescaling of definition

Lemma 7
.

As
p\to\infty
and
u
near zero,

p^{-n}e^{-\frac{u}{2p}\mathfrak{D}_{p}^{2}}(y,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\dfrac{e^{uw_{d}}\mathrm{det}(\dot{R}^{L})}{\mathrm{det}(1-e^{-u\dot{R}^{L}})}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E}(y)+o(p^{-1/2}),

(31)

in the
C^{l}

l>0
.

Proof.

The contributing terms with the highest possible power of
p
in
\theta_{j,p}(0)
can be found among the terms in the asymptotic expansion of the heat kernel of
-(\partial_{\mathrm{x}_{i}}+p\Gamma^{L})^{2}+pc(R^{L})
which is the result of removing all terms with non-maximal
p
-grading from (
11
). The rest of the proof follows as in
[
6
, thm. 4]
. For a different proof of (
31
) in the case
B=0
see
[
3
, thm 5.5.8]
.
∎

Giant component of the soft random geometric graph
Mathew D. PenroseUniversity of Bath

Abstract
Consider a 2-dimensional soft random geometric graph G ⁡ ( λ , s , ϕ ) G(\lambda,s,\phi) , obtained by placing a Poisson( λ ​ s 2 \lambda s^{2} ) number of vertices uniformly at random in a square of side s s , with edges placed between each pair x , y x,y of vertices with probability ϕ ⁡ ( ‖ x − y ‖ ) \phi(\|x-y\|) , where ϕ : ℝ + → [ 0 , 1 ] \phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,1] is a finite-range connection function. This paper is concerned with the asymptotic behaviour of the graph G ⁡ ( λ , s , ϕ ) G(\lambda,s,\phi) in the large- s s limit with ( λ , ϕ ) (\lambda,\phi) fixed. We prove that the proportion of vertices in the largest component converges in probability to the percolation probability for the corresponding random connection model, which is a random graph defined similarly for a Poisson process on the whole plane. We do not cover the case where λ \lambda equals the critical value λ c ​ ( ϕ ) \lambda_{c}(\phi) . AMS classifications: 60C05, 60D05, 60K35.

Let

nonincreasing function.
Given a locally finite point set

,
let

, where
for each
\{x,y\}\subset{\cal X}
, the
edge
xy

\phi(\|y-x\|)
,
independently of
the other pairs. Here
\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm.

Given
\lambda>0
,
let
{\cal H}_{\lambda}
denote a homogeneous Poisson process
of intensity
\lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Given also
s>0
, set
B(s):=[-s/2,s/2]^{2}
and let
{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}
denote the restriction
of
{\cal H}_{\lambda}
to
B(s)
, which is
a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity
\lambda
in
B(s)
.
We are interested in the graphs
G({\cal H}_{\lambda},\phi)
and

G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi)
, which are
known as the
random connection model

[
6
]

and
soft random geometric graph

[
9
]

respectively, with connection function
\phi
.

Let
{\cal H}^{{\bf o}}_{\lambda}
denote the point process
{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cup\{{\bf o}\}
,
where
{\bf o}
is the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\pi_{k}(\phi,\lambda)
denote the probability that
the component of
G({\cal H}^{{\bf o}}_{\lambda},\phi)
containing the origin is of
order
k
.
The

is

{\bf o}
lies in an
infinite component of the graph

, that
is,

A standard coupling argument shows that
\theta(\phi,\lambda)
is nondecreasing in
\lambda
.
The
critical value

(continuum percolation threshold)

\lambda_{\rm c}(\phi)
is defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm c}(\phi):=\inf\{\lambda>0:\theta(\phi,\lambda)>0\}.

(1.1)

It is known (see
[
6
]
) that

0<\lambda_{\rm c}<\infty
, provided
0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(\|x\|)dx<\infty
.

For any finite graph
G
, let

denote the order of
its
j
th-largest component, that is, the
j
th-largest of the
orders of its components, or zero if it has fewer than
j

components. In this paper we prove the following results about
convergence in probability of
s^{-2}L_{j}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))
for

j=1,2
, as
s\to\infty
with
\phi,\lambda
fixed.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\lambda>0
with
\theta(\phi,\lambda)=0
.
Then

\displaystyle s^{-2}L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0{\rm~~~~~~as~~}s\to\infty.

(1.2)

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\sup\{r>0:\phi(r)>0\}\in(0,\infty)

and
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi)
. Then
as
s\to\infty
we have that

s^{-2}L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda\theta(\phi,\lambda)
and

s^{-2}L_{2}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

These theorems do not address the case with
\lambda=\lambda_{c}(\phi)
,
unless we know
\theta(\phi,\lambda_{c}(\phi))=0
(if we had this, we could
apply Theorem
1.1
when

).
Theorem

also does not address the case of

having unbounded

In the case with
\phi={\bf 1}_{[0,1]}
, these results

[
7
]
, but the method here provides

[
7
]

relies on a lengthy RSW argument from
[
6
]
, as well as not

When

The same inequality holds if

and

are both decreasing.

Proof.

See
[
2
]
, where measurability issues are also dealt with.
∎

Corollary 2.2

(Square Root trick)
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
.
Suppose for
i=1,\ldots,k
we
have increasing events
A_{i}

defined on
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})
,
such that
{\mathbb{P}}[\cup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}]>1-{\varepsilon}
.

Then
\max_{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathbb{P}}[A_{i}]>1-{\varepsilon}^{1/k}
.

Proof.

Set
M=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathbb{P}}[A_{i}]
. The
events

are all decreasing, so by Lemma

,

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}>{\mathbb{P}}[\cap_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}^{c}]\geq\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{P}}[A_{i}^{c}]\geq(1-M)^{k},

1-M<{\varepsilon}^{1/k}
and
M>1-{\varepsilon}^{1/k}
.
∎

\lambda>0
,

. It is not hard to show that

.

Another useful fact is the
Mecke formula
for the random
connection model. Let
s,\lambda>0
and suppose

is defined
for
all pairs
(x,G)

is a finite graph with vertex
set

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{x\in{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}}f(x,G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}))=\lambda\int_{B(s)}{\mathbb{E}}[f(x,G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{x}))]dx

and moreover if

is defined whenever additionally

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sum\sum_{x,y\in{\cal H}_{\lambda,s},x\neq y}g(x,y,G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}))=\lambda^{2}\int_{B(s)}\int_{B(s)}{\mathbb{E}}[g(x,y,G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{x,y}))]dydx.

(2.2)

The Mecke formulae (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)
can be derived by conditioning on
{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}
and
using the usual Mecke formulae from e.g.
[
3
]
.

Also of use to us is the following
sequential construction

of clusters in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})
. Let
\lambda,s>0
and
A\subset B(s)

(typically a disk).
The set
\cup_{x\in{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap A}{\cal C}_{x}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})

can be created as follows:

First generate
{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap A
. Denote the points so created
as

and let the initial intensity function
of
unexplored points
, i.e. Poisson points that
are not yet generated, is
g=\lambda{\bf 1}_{B(s)\setminus A}(\cdot)
.

Next, choose an active point
x
and generate a Poisson process
of intensity

g(\cdot)\phi(\cdot-x)
,
representing the
previously unexplored points
of
{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}

that are connected directly to

points as ‘active’, and change the status
of

.

Then pick a new active point and repeat the above, using the new intensity of

then stop.

We shall refer to the above procedure as

growing the cluster sequentially
. This method is described in detail
for the case
\phi={\bf 1}_{[0,1]}
in
[
8
]
, and for the general random connection
model in
[
5
]
.

2.2
The subcritical case

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose
\lambda>0
with
\theta(\phi,\lambda)=0
.

Let
{\varepsilon}>0
. Let
N_{s}:=\sum_{x\in{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}}{\bf 1}\{|{\cal C}_{x}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})|\geq{\varepsilon}s^{2}\}
.
If

,
then

. Hence by Markov’s inequality and the Mecke formula,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}))\geq{\varepsilon}s^{2}]\leq({\varepsilon}s^{2})^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}[N_{s}]=({\varepsilon}s^{2})^{-1}\int_{B(s)}{\mathbb{P}}[|{\cal C}_{x}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{x})|\geq{\varepsilon}s^{2}]\lambda dx

\displaystyle\leq({\varepsilon}s^{2})^{-1}\int_{B(s)}{\mathbb{P}}[|{\cal C}_{x}({\cal H}_{\lambda}^{x})|\geq{\varepsilon}s^{2}]\lambda dx

\displaystyle=\lambda{\varepsilon}^{-1}\sum_{k\geq{\varepsilon}s^{2}}\pi_{k}(\phi,\lambda)

which tends to zero as
s\to\infty
. Therefore

s^{-2}L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.
∎

E:=\{D_{K}\leftrightarrow I{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\mu}\cap S_{3N+1})\}=\{{\cal C}\cap I\neq\varnothing\}
.

Recall that
\nu
was defined just after (
2.3
).
Suppose
\nu<Z<\infty
.
Then we can find
i_{1},\ldots i_{\lceil\nu/9\rceil}\in{\cal I}
such that the squares

Q_{i_{1}}^{+},\ldots,Q^{+}_{i_{\lceil\nu/9\rceil}}

are disjoint.
Then sprinkling an independent Poisson process
{\cal H}_{\lambda-\mu}

on top of
{\cal H}_{\mu}
in these squares, for each square we have a chance
at least
\eta
to join
{\cal C}
to
{\cal C}^{\prime}
via the sprinkled points in
that square. Since we may assume
{\cal H}_{\lambda}={\cal H}_{\mu}\cup{\cal H}_{\lambda-\mu}
,
setting
F^{\prime}=\{D_{K}\leftrightarrow D_{K}({\bf x})~{\rm in}~G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap(S_{3N+1}\cup S_{N+1}({\bf x})))\}

we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[(F^{\prime})^{c}|E\cap\{Z>\nu\}]\leq(1-\eta)^{\nu/9}<{\varepsilon}/3.

(2.10)

Note that if
Z=+\infty
then
F^{\prime}
must occur so this case
is included in (
2.10
).

Now suppose
Z\leq\nu
. If also event
E
occurs
then it is not possible to find paths in
S_{N+1}({\bf x})
both
from
D_{K}({\bf x})
to
J^{+}
, and from
D_{K}({\bf x})
to
J^{-}
,
with neither path passing through
\cup_{\{i:{\cal C}\cap Q_{i}\neq\varnothing\}}Q_{i}^{+}

(see Figure 1, and also
[
1
, Figure 2.2]
; note

I\cap J^{+}=I\cap J^{-}=\varnothing
since

\alpha_{3N}/4<\alpha_{N}
).
Therefore we have not yet achieved event
A^{+}\cap A^{-}

at this stage.

The top cluster is the part of
{\cal C}
within

S_{N+1}({\bf x})
. The other clusters are
{\cal C}^{\prime}
,
which is grown within the square
S_{N+1}({\bf x})
but without
further exploration from the points created in the shaded region,
which is
\cup_{i:{\cal C}\cap Q_{i}\neq\varnothing}Q_{i}^{+}
.
In this case
Z=5
; the left-most branch of
{\cal C}^{\prime}
reaching
the shaded region contributes 2 to
Z
.
It is not possible for
{\cal C}^{\prime}
to reach both

J^{+}
and
J^{-}
by this stage.

At the next stage
sample all of the new Poisson points (not part of
{\cal C}
or
{\cal C}^{\prime}
) in
the union of squares
Q_{i}^{++}
,

i\in{\cal I}
. With probability at least
e^{-25\mu\nu}

no new Poisson points are generated at this stage, and if this is the
case then
A^{+}\cap A^{-}
does not occur because the cluster
{\cal C}^{\prime}

dies out without having reached
J^{+}
and
J^{-}
.
Thus we have

{\mathbb{P}}[(A^{+}\cap A^{-})^{c}|E\cap\{Z\leq\nu\}]\geq\exp(-25\mu\nu)\geq\exp(-25\lambda\nu).

Therefore if
{\mathbb{P}}[A^{+}\cap A^{-}\cap E]>1-\delta
we have

\delta>{\mathbb{P}}[(A^{+}\cap A^{-})^{c}\cap E\cap\{Z\leq\nu\}]>\exp(-25\lambda\nu){\mathbb{P}}[E\cap\{Z\leq t\}],

so that

{\mathbb{P}}[E\cap\{Z\leq\nu\}]<{\varepsilon}/3
by (
2.4
).
Combined with (

),
this shows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[(F^{\prime})^{c}]\leq{\mathbb{P}}[E^{c}]+{\mathbb{P}}[(F^{\prime})^{c}|E\cap\{Z>\nu\}]+{\mathbb{P}}[E\cap\{Z\leq\nu\}]<{\varepsilon}.

(2.11)

Since

(S_{3N+1}\cup S_{N+1}({\bf x}))\subset D_{6N}\subset D_{3\|{\bf x}\|}
,
we have
F^{\prime}\subset\{D_{K}\leftrightarrow D_{K}({\bf x})

in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap D_{3\|{\bf x}\|})\}
.
By rotation invariance,
with
M:=\|{\bf x}\|
,
we thus have

{\mathbb{P}}[F_{K,M,\lambda}]>1-{\varepsilon},

as required.
∎

2.4
Connection probability

Given
s>0
, let
V_{s},W_{s}
be independent uniformly
distributed points in

B(s)
, independent of
{\cal H}_{\lambda}
.
We shall characteraize the giant component of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})

in terms of those vertices
which are path-connected to a fixed disk centred at
{\bf o}
. For this, the
following is useful.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose

\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi)
. Let
{\varepsilon}>0
.
Then there exists
K>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}[D_{K}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]>1-{\varepsilon}
, and

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\{V_{s}\}\leftrightarrow D_{K}~{\rm in}~G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s}})]\geq\theta(\phi,\lambda)-{\varepsilon}.

(2.12)

Proof.

Assume
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi)
.
Let
{\varepsilon}_{1}\in(0,{\varepsilon}/3)
be chosen such that if
(X_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is
a
7
-dependent Bernoulli random
field on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
{\mathbb{P}}[X_{x}=1]>1-5{\varepsilon}_{1}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
than for all
s>0
and all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap B(s)
, there
is a lattice path of 1’s from
x
to
{\bf o}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap B(s)
with
probability greater than
1-{\varepsilon}/3
.
The proof that such an
{\varepsilon}_{1}
exists is standard, using
e.g.
[
4
]
and a Peierls argument (e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 9.8]
).

Using Proposition
2.4
, choose

K,M
such that
0<K<M/3
, and

\min({\mathbb{P}}[D_{K}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})],{\mathbb{P}}[U_{K,M/3,\lambda}],{\mathbb{P}}[F_{K,M,\lambda}])>1-{\varepsilon}_{1}.

For each
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\|x-y\|=1
,
let

U_{x}
denote the event that there is a unique component
of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap D_{(M/3)+1}(Mx))
that meets both
D_{K}(Mx)

and
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{M/3}(Mx)
.
Let
F_{xy}:=\{D_{K}(Mx)\leftrightarrow D_{K}(My)
in

G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap D_{3M}(Mx))\}
.

By translation and rotation invariance of
{\cal H}_{\lambda}
,

{\mathbb{P}}[U_{x}]>1-{\varepsilon}_{1}
for
each
x
, and
{\mathbb{P}}[F_{xy}]>1-{\varepsilon}_{1}
for each
(x,y)
.

For each
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
let us set
X_{x}=1
if event
U_{x}
occurs, and
also
F_{xy}
occurs for each of the four
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

with
\|y-x\|=1
;
otherwise set
X_{x}=0
.
Then
by the union bound
{\mathbb{P}}[X_{x}=1]\geq 1-5{\varepsilon}_{1}
.
Also
X_{x}
is determined by
{\cal H}_{\lambda}|_{D_{3M}(Mx)}
, so

(X_{x},x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is a
7
-dependent Bernoulli random field.
By the choice of
{\varepsilon}_{1}
,
for any
x\in B(s/M)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}

there is a lattice path
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap B(s/M)
from
x
to
{\bf o}

of sites
z
with
X_{z}=1
,
with probability at least
1-{\varepsilon}/3
.

Since
{\mathbb{P}}[D_{M+K}\leftrightarrow\infty
in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]\geq{\mathbb{P}}[D_{K}\leftrightarrow\infty
in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]>1-{\varepsilon}_{1}
,
by a similar argument to (
2.5
) we can
(and do) take
M_{1}>M+K
such that

{\mathbb{P}}[U_{M+K,M_{1},\lambda}]>1-{\varepsilon}_{1}
.

Let
x_{V}
be the closest point in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap B(s/M)

to
M^{-1}V_{s}
.
Consider the following events:

•

A_{1}:=\{V_{s}\in B(s-4M_{1})\setminus D_{3M_{1}}\}
.
Provided
s
is large enough

{\mathbb{P}}[A_{1}]>1-{\varepsilon}/3
.

•

A_{2}
is the event that there is a lattice path from
x_{V}

to
{\bf o}
within
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap B(s/M)

with
X_{x}=1
for all sites
x
in the path.
By the previous discussion,
{\mathbb{P}}[A_{2}]>1-{\varepsilon}/3
.

•

A_{3}
is the event that there is a unique
component
in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda}^{V_{s}}\cap D_{M_{1}+1}(V_{s}))

that meets both
D_{M+K}(V_{s})

and
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{M_{1}}(V_{s})
.
Then
{\mathbb{P}}[A_{3}]>1-{\varepsilon}/3
.

•

A_{4}
is the event that
{\cal C}_{V_{s}}({\cal H}_{\lambda}^{V_{s}})\cap D_{M_{1}}(V_{s})^{c}\neq\varnothing
. Then
{\mathbb{P}}[A_{4}]\geq\theta(\phi,\lambda)
.

By the union bound

{\mathbb{P}}[\cap_{i=1}^{4}A_{i}]\geq\theta(\phi,\lambda)-{\varepsilon},

for all large enough
s
. Therefore it suffices to prove
that if
\cap_{i=1}^{4}A_{i}

occurs, then
\{V_{s}\}\leftrightarrow D_{K}
in

G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s}})
.

To see this, suppose
\cap_{i=1}^{4}A_{i}
occurs.
Then we have
\|Mx_{V}-V_{s}\|\leq M
so using
A_{1}
we have

{\rm dist}(Mx_{V},B(s)^{c})\geq 2M_{1}-M>(M/3)+1
.
Since
X_{x_{V}}=1
by
A_{2}
,
there is a unique component of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap D_{(M/3)+1}(Mx_{V}))

that meets both
D_{K}(Mx_{V})
and
D_{(M/3)}(Mx_{V})^{c}
, and
since
D_{(M/3)+1}(Mx_{V})\subset B(s)
, this
extends to a unique component of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})
that meets
both
D_{K}(Mx_{V})
and
D_{M/3}(Mx_{V})^{c}
. We denote this component by

{\cal C}
.

By
A_{2}
, the component
{\cal C}
includes a vertex in
D_{K}
.
Choose such a vertex and denote it by
z
.

Next, using
A_{1}
observe that

\|V_{s}-z\|\geq\|V_{s}\|-\|z\|\geq 3M_{1}-K\geq 2M_{1}
.
Also
D_{K}(Mx_{V})\subset D_{M+K}(V_{s})
, so
{\cal C}
meets
both
D_{M+K}(V_{s})
and
D_{M_{1}}(V_{s})^{c}
. Therefore
using
A_{3}
and
A_{4}
we have that
V_{s}
is connected to
{\cal C}
,
and thus
\{V_{s}\}\leftrightarrow D_{K}
in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s}})

as required.
∎

2.5
Proof of the giant component phenomenon

We now write
L_{i,s}
for
L_{i}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}))
(we are thinking of

\lambda
and
\phi
as fixed with
\sup\{r:\phi(r)>0\}=1
).
For convenience,
we re-state Theorem
1.2
, which we are now ready to prove.

Theorem 2.7
.

If

\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi)
, then

s^{-2}L_{1,s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda\theta(\phi,\lambda)
and

s^{-2}L_{2,s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

as
s\to\infty
.

Proof.

Assume
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi)
.
Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and using Proposition
2.6
, choose
K>0
such
that

{\mathbb{P}}[D_{K}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]>1-{\varepsilon}
, and
(
2.12
) holds.
Consider the sum

N_{s}:=\sum_{x\in{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}}{\bf 1}\{\{x\}\leftrightarrow D_{K}{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})\}.

Let
V_{s},W_{s}
be as in Section
2.4
.
By the Mecke formula,

{\mathbb{E}}N_{s}=\lambda s^{2}{\mathbb{P}}[\{V_{s}\}\leftrightarrow D_{K}{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s}})].

Then using (
2.12
),
and writing just

\theta
for
\theta(\phi,\lambda)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to\infty}s^{-2}{\mathbb{E}}N_{s}\geq\lambda(\theta-{\varepsilon}).

(2.13)

Next, let
N^{\prime}_{s}=\sum_{x\in{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}}{\bf 1}\{|{\cal C}_{x}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})|\geq s^{1/2}\}
(here
|\cdot|
represents number of elements).
Using the Mecke formula (
2.1
) we have that

{\mathbb{E}}[N^{\prime}_{s}]=\lambda s^{2}{\mathbb{P}}[|{\cal C}_{V_{s}}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s}})|\geq s^{1/2}]
,
and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{-2}{\mathbb{E}}N^{\prime}_{s}=\lambda\theta.

(2.14)

Also

{\mathbb{E}}[N^{\prime}_{s}(N^{\prime}_{s}-1)]=\lambda^{2}s^{4}{\mathbb{P}}[|{\cal C}_{V_{s}}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s},W_{s}})|\geq s^{1/2},|{\cal C}_{W_{s}}({\cal H}_{\lambda,s}^{V_{s},W_{s}})|\geq s^{1/2}|]

by (
2.2
),
so that

\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{-4}{\mathbb{E}}[N^{\prime}_{s}(N^{\prime}_{s}-1)]=\lambda^{2}\theta^{2}.

Thus

s^{-2}N^{\prime}_{s}\to\lambda\theta
in
L^{2}
and
hence in probability, as
s\to\infty
.

Since
(N_{s}-N^{\prime}_{s})^{+}\leq{\cal H}_{\lambda}(D_{K+s^{1/2}})

we have that
s^{-2}{\mathbb{E}}[(N_{s}-N^{\prime}_{s})^{+}]\to 0
as
s\to\infty
.
Hence by (
2.13
) and (
2.14
),

\limsup{\mathbb{E}}[s^{-2}(N^{\prime}_{s}-N_{s})^{+}]=\limsup{\mathbb{E}}[s^{-2}(N^{\prime}_{s}-N_{s})]\leq\lambda{\varepsilon}.

Hence by Markov’s inequality

\limsup_{s\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}(N_{s}^{\prime}-N_{s})\geq{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]\leq\lambda{\varepsilon}^{1/2},

and hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}N_{s}\leq\lambda\theta-2{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]\leq\limsup_{s\to\infty}

\displaystyle\left({\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}N^{\prime}_{s}\leq\lambda\theta-{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}(N_{s}-N^{\prime}_{s})\leq-{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]\right)\leq\lambda{\varepsilon}^{1/2}.

As at (
2.5
),
we can and do choose
M_{2}>K
so
{\mathbb{P}}[U_{K,M_{2}}]>1-{\varepsilon}
.
If
(s/2)>M_{2}+1
and
U_{K,M_{2}}
occurs
then
L_{1,s}\geq N_{s}-{\cal H}_{\lambda}(D_{M_{2}})
,
since all
x\in{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}\setminus D_{M_{2}}
that are path-connected
to
D_{K}
lie in the same component of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s})
.
Therefore

{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}L_{1,s}\leq\lambda\theta-3{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]\leq{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}N_{s}\leq\lambda\theta-2{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]+{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}{\cal H}_{\lambda}(D_{M_{2}})\geq{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]+{\mathbb{P}}[U_{K,M_{2}}^{c}]

so that

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}L_{1,s}\leq\lambda\theta-3{\varepsilon}^{1/2}]\leq\lambda{\varepsilon}^{1/2}+{\varepsilon}.

(2.15)

Conversely, note that if

s^{2}\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})>s^{1/2}

then

{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}L_{1,s}\geq\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})]\leq{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}N^{\prime}_{s}\geq\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})]

which tends to zero. Combined with (
2.15
)
this shows that

s^{-2}L_{1,s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda\theta
.

If

s^{2}\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})>s^{1/2}
and

L_{1,s}+L_{2,s}\geq s^{2}\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})

then either
N^{\prime}_{s}\geq s^{2}\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})
or

L_{1,s}+s^{1/2}\geq s^{2}\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})
.
Hence

{\mathbb{P}}[s^{-2}(L_{1,s}+L_{2,s})>\lambda(\theta+{\varepsilon})]\to 0.

Combined with (
2.15
) this shows that

s^{-2}(L_{1,s}+L_{2,s})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda\theta

and hence by Slutsky’s theorem,

s^{-2}L_{2,s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.
∎

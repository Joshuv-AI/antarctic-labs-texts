Abstract
. Piecewise contractions (PCs) are piecewise smooth maps that decrease distance between pairs of points in the same domain of continuity. The dynamics of a variety of systems is described by PCs. During the last decade, a lot of effort has been devoted to proving that in parametrized families of one-dimensional PCs, the ω \omega -limit set of a typical PC consists of finitely many periodic orbits while there exist atypical PCs with Cantor ω \omega -limit sets. In this article, we extend these results to the multi-dimensional case. More precisely, we provide criteria to show that an arbitrary family { f μ } μ ∈ U \{f_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in U} of locally bi-Lipschitz piecewise contractions f μ : X → X f_{\mu}:X\to X defined on a compact metric space X X is asymptotically periodic for Lebesgue almost every parameter μ \mu running over an open subset U U of the M M -dimensional Euclidean space ℝ M \mathbb{R}^{M} . As a corollary of our results, we prove that piecewise affine contractions of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} defined in generic polyhedral partitions are asymptotically periodic.

Introduction

Piecewise contractions (PCs) are piecewise smooth self-maps of a metric space that act on each of its continuity domains by decreasing the distance between pairs of points. Their importance stems from countless dynamical systems whose dynamics is governed by them: pseudo-billiards and switched server systems

[

, just to name a few. PCs also arise in subjects of pure mathematics such as non-trivial recurrence on surfaces
[

[

While a contraction acting on a compact or complete metric space always has a unique globally attractive fixed-point, a piecewise contraction with discontinuities may have lots of locally attractive limit cycles and Cantor attractors coexisting side by side.

Thanks to a series of recent works, the dynamics of one-dimensional PCs (i.e., PCs of the interval with finitely many discontinuities) is by now well-understood. For instance, it is known that the attractor of locally injective PCs of the interval is the union of a finite number of periodic orbits with a finite number of Cantor sets
[
10
]
. However, the existence of Cantor attractors is rather exceptional. Indeed, in the measure-theoretical sense, a generic PC of the interval is asymptotically periodic, that is, the global attractor is formed by a finite number of periodic orbits
[
32
,
33
,
18
]
. Moreover, in some cases, the set of exceptional parameters where Cantor attractors exist has zero Hausdorff dimension
[
20
]
. The following facts are also known: the subword complexity of symbolic orbits of PCs of the interval is either uniformly bounded or a linear function
[
11
,
34
]
; PCs of the interval with no periodic orbits and a Cantor attractor can be constructed as suspension of any minimal interval exchange transformation
[
34
]
.

A paradigmatic example of one-dimensional PC is the contracted rotation on
[0,1)
:
f_{\lambda,b}(x)=\lambda x+b\pmod{1}
with
0<\lambda<1
and
1-\lambda<b<1
. Lifting

to
\mathbb{R}

\rho(f_{\lambda,b})\in[0,1)
that describes the asymptotic average rotation of any orbit of
f_{\lambda,b}
. It is known that
f_{\lambda,b}
has a Cantor attractor if and only if
\rho(f_{\lambda,b})
is an irrational number. When
\rho(f_{\lambda,b})
takes a rational value
\frac{p}{q}
with co-prime positive integers

has a single global attracting periodic orbit with period

. Since the rotation number varies continuously and non-decreasingly with the parameter

, every rotation number in the interval
(0,1)
is realizable by some
b\in(1-\lambda,1)
. Thus, the set of parameters
(\lambda,b)
, where

has Cantor attractors is uncountable, has Hausdorff dimension
\geq 1
, but has zero Lebesgue measure
[
6
,
29
]
.

In contrast to one-dimensional PCs, less is known about the dynamics of multi-dimensional PCs. In
[
12
]
, it is proved that multi-dimensional PCs are asymptotically periodic whenever the attractor and the set of discontinuities are disjoint. However, such sufficient condition turns out to be rather difficult to be verified in parametrized families of PCs, which are relevant for many applications. In
[
5
]
, it is proved that asymptotic periodicity is a generic property (in the measure-theoretical sense) of a family of piecewise-affine conformal contractions defined in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and sharing the same domains of continuity. The family is obtained by vertical translation of the graph of any PC in the family.
Very recently, it was proved in
[
24
]
that for a generic partition, in a Baire residual sense, the attractor of locally injective PCs on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is disjoint from the set of discontinuities, implying that the attractor consists of a finite number of periodic orbits. The authors also prove that PCs are generically topologically stable. However, the results of
[
24
]
cannot be applied to parametrized families of multi-dimensional PCs.

In this article, we study genericity, from a measure-theoretical point of view, of asymptotic periodicity in parametrized families of multi-dimensional PCs. More precisely, we provide a criteria (Theorem
2.1
) to show that an arbitrary family
\{f_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in U}
of locally bi-Lipschitz piecewise contractions
f_{\mu}:X\to X
defined on a compact metric space

is asymptotically periodic for Lebesgue almost every parameter
\mu

U
of the
M

2.6
and Hypothesis (T) in Definition
2.7

To show the strength and powerfulness of Theorem
2.1

with varying domains of continuity. More precisely, Theorem

shows that asymptotic periodicity is a generic property in families of one-dimensional piecewise contractions while Theorem

and its corollary are multi-dimensional versions of the same result. Theorem

appeared previously in
[

but the proof presented therein does not generalize to higher dimensions. Such a generalization is one of the main contributions of this article.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In Section

Main results

To give the statement of our main results, we first introduce two hypotheses: Hypothesis (E) and Hypothesis (T). In order to do so, we need the formal definitions of piecewise contraction, regular point, asymptotic periodic self-map, itineraries of a piecewise contraction and family of piecewise contractions.

Throughout this section, unless otherwise specified,

(X,d)

Definition 2.1

(Piecewise contraction)
.

A map

(ii)

(iii)

\text{Lip}\,(\varphi_{i})\leq\lambda

The set

Definition 2.2

(Regular point)

Given a piecewise

Given a self-map

Definition 2.3

(Asymptotically periodic)

Given a self-map

Remark 2.1
.

If

is finite and
\omega(f,Y)\cap S(f)=\emptyset

Let

In concrete families, it is much more difficult to verify Hypothesis (E) than Hypothesis (T). For this reason, in Proposition

As in

, we introduce the multiplicity entropy.

The

Informally, the multiplicity entropy measures the exponential growth rate of the number of itineraries sharing a common prefix.

The following result establishes a sufficient condition for Hypothesis (E) that is easier to check.

Proposition 3.8

If

big enough. Therefore, by the compactness of

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m+1}\in\mathcal{I}_{p}(f_{\mu^{*}})

as

and
x_{k}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m+1}\overline{A_{\mu_{k}}^{\alpha_{i}}}

d(x,A_{\mu^{*}}^{\alpha_{i}})\leq d(x,x_{k})+d\big(x_{k},{A_{\mu_{k}}^{\alpha_{i}}}\big)+d_{H}\big({A_{\mu_{k}}^{\alpha_{i}}},A_{\mu^{*}}^{\alpha_{i}}\big),\,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,m+1\}

be as in Claim A, then

is an open convex polytope since it is the intersection of the relative interior of the polytope

with finitely many open half-spaces with bounding hyperplanes
H_{j}(\mu)
,

. Indeed, we can write

A_{i,\mu}=\text{relint}(X)\cap\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{\ell}H^{s_{j}}_{j}(\mu)\right),

where
H^{s_{j}}_{j}(\mu):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon s_{j}\langle v^{(j)},x\rangle>s_{j}\mu_{j}\right\}
for each

. Using the matrices introduced just before the statement of the lemma, we can write

as follows

A_{i,\mu}=\text{relint}(X)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon C_{i}Vx>C_{i}\mu\}.

Now, given

and

, we get

(\varphi^{\alpha_{k}})^{-1}(A_{i_{k},\mu})=(\varphi^{\alpha_{k}})^{-1}(\text{relint}(X))\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon C_{i_{k}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{k}}(x)>C_{i_{k}}\mu\}.

Putting all together (see (

A_{\mu}^{\alpha}=\bigcap_{k=0}^{n-1}(\varphi^{\alpha_{k}})^{-1}(\mathrm{relint}(X))\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon C_{i_{k}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{k}}(x)>C_{i_{k}}\mu\}.

In order to finish the proof, just notice that

\mathrm{relint}(X)\cap(\varphi^{\alpha_{k}})^{-1}(\mathrm{relint}(X))=\mathrm{relint}(X),\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

Indeed, by assumption, for all

,
\varphi_{i_{j}}(X)\subset\mathrm{relint}(X)
, which implies that
\varphi^{\alpha_{k}}(\mathrm{relint}(X))=\varphi_{i_{k-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{0}}(\mathrm{relint}(X))\subset\mathrm{relint}(X)
. Hence,
\mathrm{relint}(X)\subset(\varphi^{\alpha_{k}})^{-1}(\mathrm{relint}(X))
.

Let

Given

with

being the length of the tuple

(Definition

Lemma 5.2
.

Z_{\alpha}
is convex for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Given any
\alpha=(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-1})\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
with
n\geq 1
, define

\Pi_{\alpha}:=\{(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\colon x\in A_{\mu}^{\alpha}\}.

It follows from Lemma
5.1
that
(x,\mu)\in\Pi_{\alpha}
iff
x\in\mathrm{relint}(X)
and

C_{i_{j}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{j}}(x)>C_{i_{j}}\mu,\quad\forall\,j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}.

Notice that
\mathrm{relint}(X)
is a convex set since the relative interior of a convex set is also convex. Now, we claim that
\Pi_{\alpha}
is convex, i.e., the convex combination of any two points
(x_{0},\mu_{0})
and
(x_{1},\mu_{1})
of
\Pi_{\alpha}
also belongs to
\Pi_{\alpha}
. To show that, let
t\in[0,1]
and

(x_{t},\mu_{t}):=\big((1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1},(1-t)\mu_{0}+t\mu_{1}\big).

By the hypotheses on
(x_{0},\mu_{0})
and
(x_{1},\mu_{1})
, we have that for all
0\leq j\leq n-1
,

x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathrm{relint}(X),\quad C_{i_{j}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0})>C_{i_{j}}\mu_{0}\quad\textrm{and}\quad C_{i_{j}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{j}}(x_{1})>C_{i_{j}}\mu_{1}.

Because
\mathrm{relint}(X)
is convex,
x_{t}\in\mathrm{relint}(X)
for every
t\in[0,1]
. On the other hand, for all
0\leq j\leq n-1
and
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle C_{i_{j}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{j}}(x_{t})

\displaystyle=(1-t)C_{i_{j}}V\varphi^{\alpha_{j}}(x_{0})+tC_{i_{j}}\varphi^{\alpha_{j}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle>(1-t)C_{i_{j}}\mu_{0}+tC_{i_{j}}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{i_{j}}\mu_{t}.

This proves the claim. Finally, since
Z_{\alpha}
is the projection of
\Pi_{\alpha}
onto the second variable, we conclude that
Z_{\alpha}
is also convex.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Since

In what follows, keep

and

Given

and
\beta\in\mathcal{Z}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})

Given

Theorem A.1

(Theorem 0.1 in

]

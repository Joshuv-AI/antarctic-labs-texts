Chamber decomposition for K-semistable domains and VGIT Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 14J45. Keywords : Log Fano pair, K-stability, K-semistable domain, K-moduli, VGIT. Competing interests : The author declares none.
Chuyu Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore the relationship between the chamber decomposition for K-semistable domains and VGIT. We also study the relationship between the K-moduli generically parametrizing K-semistable Fano complete intersections of the form S d 1 ∩ … ∩ S d k S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}} and the K-moduli generically parametrizing K-semistable log Fano manifolds of the form ( ℙ n , ∑ j = 1 k x j ​ S d j ) (\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}S_{d_{j}}) , where x j ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) ∩ ℚ x_{j}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q} and S d j ⊂ ℙ n S_{d_{j}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n} is a hypersurface of degree d j d_{j} for each 1 ≤ j ≤ k 1\leq j\leq k .



1.
Introduction

Based on a rather complete algebraic K-stability theory developed in the past few years (e.g.
[
11
,
5
,
26
,
1
,
7
,
25
,
18
]
), people are now able to construct a projective separated scheme as a good moduli space to parametrize Fano varieties with K-stability. In particular, a wall crossing theory for K-moduli spaces is also established in
[
2
]
(see also
[
30
]
), which turns out to be useful in the study of birational geometry of different moduli spaces of varieties (e.g.
[
3
,
4
]
).
In the wall crossing picture, it is widely recognized that the first K-moduli space is related to the GIT moduli space (e.g.
[
2
,
10
,
27
]
). For example, fixing a K-polystable Fano variety
X
and a sufficiently divisible positive integer
l
, then there is a natural
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action on the linear system
\frac{1}{l}|-lK_{X}|
(note that
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
is reductive due to
[
1
]
). We conclude that
D\in\frac{1}{l}|-lK_{X}|
is GIT-(semi/poly)stable if and only if
(X,\epsilon D)
is K-(semi/poly)stable for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
(e.g.
[
27
]
). Recently, a wall crossing theory for K-moduli with multiple boundaries is established in
[
29
]
, thus it is also natural to figure out the similar relationship (as in the above example) in the VGIT picture.

We fix a K-polystable Fano variety
X
and
k
sufficiently divisible positive integers
l_{1},...,l_{k}
. Denote
\mathbb{P}_{j}:=\frac{1}{l_{j}}|-l_{j}K_{X}|,\ j=1,...,k
. Then there is a natural
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action on
\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}
and one could consider the VGIT under the linearization
\mathcal{O}(c_{1},...,c_{k})
as
c_{j}
varies in
(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
(e.g.
[
24
]
). On the other hand, by
[
29
]
, there is a finite chamber decomposition of

\Delta^{k}:=\{(x_{1},...,x_{k})\ |\ \textit{$x_{j}\in[0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}$ {\rm{and}} $\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}<1$}\}

such that for any
(D_{1},...,D_{k})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}
, the K-semistability of
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}D_{j})
does not change as
(c_{1},...,c_{k})
varies in the interior domain of each chamber. We have the following result which reveals the relationship between the VGIT and the chamber decomposition of
\Delta^{k}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in(\Delta^{k})^{\circ}
,
(D_{1},...,D_{k})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}

is GIT-(semi/poly)stable under
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action with respect to the linearization
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
if and only if
(X,\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}D_{k})
is K-(semi/poly)stable for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. Moreover, the chamber decomposition of
\Delta^{k}
at the original point exactly gives the chamber decomposition for VGIT.

We next turn to a concrete setting. Fix a positive integer
n
and
k
positive integers
\vec{d}:=(d_{1},...,d_{k})
. For general smooth hypersurfaces
S_{d_{j}}
of degrees
d_{j}
, if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}<n+1
, then the complete intersection
\cap_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
gives a Fano manifold of dimension
n-k
. We denote
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}
(resp.
M^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}
) to be the K-moduli stack (resp. K-moduli space) generically parametrizing such Fano manifolds which are K-semistable. Let
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{x}}
(resp.
M^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{x}}
) be the K-moduli stack (resp. K-moduli space) generically parametrizing K-semistable log Fano manifolds of the form
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}S_{d_{j}})
, where
\vec{x}:=(x_{1},...,x_{k})
. Then it is natural to ask what is the relationship
between
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{x}}
. First recall the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
28
, Theorem 8.1]
)

Consider the log pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},S_{d_{1}}+S_{d_{2}}+...+S_{d_{k}})
, where
S_{d_{j}},j=1,...,k,
are general smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degrees
d_{j}
with
n\geq 2
and
d_{j}\leq n+1
. Suppose all the Fano complete intersections are K-semistable. Then
{\rm{Kss}}(\mathbb{P}^{n},S_{1}+...+S_{k})
is a polytope generated by the following equations

\begin{cases}0\leq x_{i}\leq 1,\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq k\\
\beta_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{i}})\geq 0,\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq k\\
\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}d_{j}\leq n+1.\end{cases}

By the proof of
[
28
, Theorem 8.1]
, if
\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}<n+1
, the equations

\beta_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{i}})=0,\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq k

admit a unique solution, denoted by
\vec{a}:=(a_{1},...,a_{k})
, where

a_{j}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}+(n-k+1)d_{j}-(n+1)}{(n-k+1)d_{j}},

and
\vec{a}
is automatically an extremal point of
{\rm{Kss}}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
. This point is definitely different. We have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a log smooth pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
, where
S_{d_{j}}
are hypersurfaces of degrees
d_{j}

with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}<n+1
, the log Fano pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})
is K-semistable if and only if the complete intersection
\cap_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
is K-semistable of dimension
n-k
. Moreover, there exists a morphism

\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{a}}\to\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}

which descends to a surjective morphism
M^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{a}}\to M^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}.

2.
Preliminaries

We say that
(X,\Delta)
is a
log pair
if
X
is a normal projective variety and
\Delta
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. The log pair
(X,\Delta)
is called
log Fano
if it admits klt singularities and
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is ample; if
\Delta=0
, we just say
X
is a
Fano variety
.
For various types of singularities in birational geometry, e.g. klt, lc, and plt singularities, we refer to
[
13
,
14
]
.

2.1.
K-stability

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log pair. Suppose
f\colon Y\to X
is a proper birational morphism between normal varieties and
E
is a prime divisor on
Y
, we say that
E
is a prime divisor over
X
and define the following invariant

A_{X,\Delta}(E):=1+{\rm{ord}}_{E}(K_{Y}-f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)).

It is called the
log discrepancy
of
E
associated to the log pair
(X,\Delta)
.
If
(X,\Delta)
is a log Fano pair, we define the following invariant

S_{X,\Delta}(E):=\frac{1}{{\rm{vol}}(-K_{X}-\Delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm{vol}}(-f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)-tE){\rm{d}}t.

Put
\beta_{X,\Delta}(E):=A_{X,\Delta}(E)-S_{X,\Delta}(E)
. By the works
[
9
,
17
]
, one can define K-stability of a log Fano pair by beta criterion as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair.
We say that
(X,\Delta)
is
K-semistable
if
\beta_{X,\Delta}(E)\geq 0
for any prime divisor
E
over
X
.

The following lemma on K-stability of projective cones is well known, see e.g.
[
19
, Prop 2.11]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(V,\Delta)
be an n-dimensional log Fano pair, and L an ample line bundle on V such that
L\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-\frac{1}{r}(K_{V}+\Delta)
for some
0<r\leq n+1
. Suppose Y is the projective cone over V associated to L with infinite divisor
V_{\infty}
, then
(V,\Delta)
is K-semistable if and only if
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+(1-\frac{r}{n+1})V_{\infty})
is K-semistable, where
\Delta_{Y}
is the divisor on Y naturally extended by
\Delta
.

2.2.
Test configuration

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log pair and
L
an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
X
. A test configuration
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\rm{tc}};\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
is a degenerating family over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
consisting of the following data:

(1)

\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
is a projective flat morphism from a normal variety
\mathcal{X}
,
\Delta_{\rm{tc}}
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{L}
is a relatively ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
,

(2)

the family
\pi
admits a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action which lifts the natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
such that
(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\rm{tc}};\mathcal{L})\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{C}^{*}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariantly isomorphic to
(X,\Delta;L)\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Suppose
(X,\Delta)
is a log Fano pair and
L=-K_{X}-\Delta
. Let
(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\rm{tc}};\mathcal{L})
be a test configuration such that
\mathcal{L}=-K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{A}^{1}}-\Delta_{\rm{tc}}
. We call it a special test configuration if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0}+\Delta_{{\rm{tc}}})
admits plt singularities (or equivalently, the central fiber
(\mathcal{X}_{0},\Delta_{{\rm{tc}},0})
is a log Fano pair).

Remark 2.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a K-semistable log Fano pair. Suppose
S
is a prime divisor on
X
such that
\beta_{X,\Delta}(S)=0
, then
S
induces a special test configuration with vanishing generalized Futaki invariant (e.g.
[
16
,
9
]
). By
[
15
]
or
[
6
]
, we know that the central fiber of the test configuration is also K-semistable. Assume further that
S
is Cartier and
S\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-\lambda(K_{X}+\Delta)
for some rational
0<\lambda<1
, then it is not hard to see that
the central fiber of the test configuration is isomorphic to the projective cone over
S
with respect to the polarization
S|_{S}
. Actually, first note that

X={\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(X,mS).

By
[
7
, Section 2.3.1]
, the special test configuration induced by
S
could be formulated as

\mathcal{X}:={\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,mS-iS)t^{-i},

and the central fiber is

\mathcal{X}_{0}={\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(H^{0}(X,mS-iS)/H^{0}(X,mS-(i+1)S)\right).

Note that for
i\leq m
we have

H^{0}(X,mS-iS)/H^{0}(X,mS-(i+1)S)\cong H^{0}(S,(m-i)S|_{S}).

Thus
\mathcal{X}_{0}\cong{\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(S,mS|_{S})s^{i}
, which is the projective cone over
S
with respect to
S|_{S}
.

3.
Chow-Mumford line bundle

Let
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\to T
be a flat family of projective normal varieties of dimension
n
over a normal base
T
, where
\mathcal{D}
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
\mathcal{X}
whose components are all flat over
T
, and
\mathcal{L}
is a relative ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
. By the work of Mumford-Knudsen(
[
12
]
), there exist
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles
\lambda_{i},i=0,1,...,n+1
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{i},i=0,1,...n,
on
T
such that we have the following expansions for all sufficiently large
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\det\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{k})=\lambda_{n+1}^{\binom{k}{n+1}}\otimes\lambda_{n}^{\binom{k}{n}}\otimes...\otimes\lambda_{1}^{\binom{k}{1}}\otimes\lambda_{0},

\det\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{D}}^{k})=\tilde{\lambda}_{n}^{\binom{k}{n}}\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_{n-1}^{\binom{k}{n-1}}\otimes...\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_{0}.

By Riemann-Roch formula, cf
[
8
, Appendix]
, we have

c_{1}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}^{k})=\frac{\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{n+1})}{(n+1)!}k^{n+1}+\frac{\pi_{*}(-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}\mathcal{L}^{n})}{2n!}k^{n}+...,

c_{1}({\pi}_{*}{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathcal{D}}^{k})=\frac{{\pi}_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{n}\mathcal{D})}{n!}k^{n}+....

Then it’s not hard to see

\lambda_{n+1}=\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{n+1}),\ \lambda_{n}=\frac{n}{2}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{*}(-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}\mathcal{L}^{n}),\ \tilde{\lambda}_{n}=\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{n}\mathcal{D}).

By the flatness of
\pi
and
\pi_{\mathcal{D}}
, we write

h^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{t},k\mathcal{L}_{t})=a_{0}k^{n}+a_{1}k^{n-1}+o(k^{n-1})\quad\text{and}\quad h^{0}(\mathcal{D}_{t},k{\mathcal{L}_{t}}|_{\mathcal{D}_{t}})=\tilde{a}_{0}k^{n-1}+o(k^{n-1}),

which do not depend on the choice of
t\in T
. Then we have

a_{0}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}}{n!},\ a_{1}=\frac{-K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}{\mathcal{L}_{t}}^{n-1}}{2(n-1)!},\ \tilde{a}_{0}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{t}}{(n-1)!}.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the CM-line bundles for the family
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\to T
as follows:

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L};\pi):=\lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_{1}}{a_{0}}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_{n}^{-2(n+1)},

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi):=\lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_{1}-\tilde{a}_{0}}{a_{0}}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_{n}^{-2(n+1)}\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_{n}^{n+1}.

For a rational number
0\leq\beta\leq 1
, we define

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi):=\lambda_{n+1}^{\frac{2a_{1}-\beta\tilde{a}_{0}}{a_{0}}+n(n+1)}\otimes\lambda_{n}^{-2(n+1)}\otimes\tilde{\lambda}_{n}^{\beta(n+1)}.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\to T
is a test configuration of the general fiber
(\mathcal{X}_{t},\mathcal{D}_{t};\mathcal{L}_{t})
, then it is well known that the generalized Futaki invariant
{\rm{Fut}}(\mathcal{X},\beta\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})
is equal to
w(\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi))
up to a positive factor, where
w(\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi))
is the total weight of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi)
(e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L})\to T
be a family satisfying:

(1)

\mathcal{X}\to T
is a flat family of projective normal varieties of dimension
n
over a normal base
T
such that
-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and relative ample over
T
;

(2)

\mathcal{D}
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
\mathcal{X}
such that every component is flat over
T
and
\mathcal{D}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},T}-\mu K_{\mathcal{X}/T}
for some rational
\mu>0
;

(3)

\mathcal{L}=-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}
.

Then we have the following formula

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{L};\pi)=-(1+\beta\mu n)\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}+\beta(n+1)\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{n}\mathcal{D}).

Proof.

Apply Definition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Chamber decomposition for VGIT

In this section, we fix a K-polystable Fano variety
X
of dimension
n
with
(-K_{X})^{n}=v
and
k
sufficiently divisible positive integers
l_{1},...,l_{k}
. Put

\mathbb{P}_{j}:=\frac{1}{l_{j}}|-l_{j}K_{X}|,\ j=1,...,k.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{j}\subset X\times\mathbb{P}_{j}
be the universal divisor with respect to the linear system
\frac{1}{l_{j}}|-l_{j}K_{X}|
. Denote

T:=\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}_{j}:=\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{j-1}\times\mathcal{D}_{j}\times\mathbb{P}_{j+1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}.

For a given rational point
\vec{c}:=(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in(\Delta^{k})^{\circ}
, we aim to compute the Chow-Mumford line bundle

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\vec{c}):=\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(X\times T,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j},-K_{X\times T/T};\pi_{\vec{c}})

for the family

\pi_{\vec{c}}:(X\times T,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j})\to T

for rational
0\leq\beta\leq 1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Notation as above, for a rational point
\vec{c}:=(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in(\Delta^{k})^{\circ}
, we have the following formula:

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\vec{c})=\mathcal{O}(\frac{\beta(n+1)vc_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{\beta(n+1)vc_{k}}{l_{k}}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\vec{c})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(n+1)\beta c_{j}{\pi_{\vec{c}}}_{*}((-K_{X\times T/T})^{n}\mathcal{B}_{j}).

It suffices to compute
{\pi_{\vec{c}}}_{*}((-K_{X\times T/T})^{n}\mathcal{B}_{j})
. Recall that

\mathcal{B}_{j}:=\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{j-1}\times\mathcal{D}_{j}\times\mathbb{P}_{j+1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k},

and

{\pi_{j}}_{*}((-K_{X\times\mathbb{P}_{j}/\mathbb{P}_{j}})^{n}\mathcal{D}_{j})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{j}}(\frac{v}{l_{j}})

for
\pi_{j}:X\times\mathbb{P}_{j}\to\mathbb{P}_{j}
. Thus we have

{\pi_{\vec{c}}}_{*}((-K_{X\times T/T})^{n}\mathcal{B}_{j})=\mathcal{O}(0_{1},0_{2}...0_{j-1},\frac{v}{l_{j}},0_{j+1},...,0_{k}).

The proof is finished.
∎

It is not hard to see that
\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\beta}(\vec{c})
is proportional to
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in(\Delta^{k})^{\circ}
,
(D_{1},...,D_{k})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}

is GIT-(semi/poly)stable under
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action with respect to the linearization
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
if and only if
(X,\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}D_{k})
is K-(semi/poly)stable for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is essentially the same as that of
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Suppose
(X,\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}D_{k})
is K-(semi/poly)stable, to show that
(D_{1},...,D_{k})
is GIT-(semi/poly)stable under
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action with respect to the linearization
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
, by Remark
3.2
, it suffices to show that
\lambda_{{\rm{CM}},\epsilon}(\vec{c})
is proportional to
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
. This is exactly Lemma
4.1
.

Conversely, suppose
(D_{1},...,D_{k})
is GIT-(semi/poly)stable under
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action with respect to the linearization
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
, then there is a family
(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\epsilon c_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j})\to C
over a smooth pointed curve
0\in C
such that

(1)

there is a morphism
C\setminus{0}\to\mathbb{P}_{j}
for each
j
;

(2)

(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{j}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{j,0})
is obtained via pulling back
(X\times\mathbb{P}_{j},\mathcal{D}_{j})
under the morphism
C\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}_{j}
;

(3)

(\mathcal{X}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}_{j,0})\cong(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})
;

(4)

(\mathcal{X}_{t},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\epsilon c_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j,t})

is K-semistable for any
t\in C\setminus\{0\}
.

By the properness of K-moduli, up to a finite base change, one could replace the central fiber
(\mathcal{X}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}_{j,0})\cong(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})
with a K-semistable log Fano pair
(X^{\prime},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\epsilon c_{j}D_{j}^{\prime})
and we denote the new family by
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\epsilon c_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\prime})\to C
for convenience. We claim that it suffices to show that
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}\cong X
. Suppose
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}\cong X
, then
(D_{1}^{\prime},...,D_{k}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{k}
and it is GIT-semistable under
{\rm{Aut}}(X)
-action with respect to the linearization
\mathcal{O}(\frac{c_{1}}{l_{1}},...,\frac{c_{k}}{l_{k}})
by what we have proved. By the separatedness of GIT moduli space, we know that

-action. Thus

The rest of the proof is devoted to show that

. Since

\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}
is K-semistable. Note that
X
is K-polystable, thus
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}\cong X
by
[
27
, Theorem 3.5]
. The proof is finished.
∎

5.
K-moduli of Fano complete intersections

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
. Throughout the section, we fix a positive integer
n
and
k
positive integers
\vec{d}:=(d_{1},...,d_{k})
with
\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}<n+1
. We exclude the case
d_{1}=d_{2}=...=d_{k}=1
since it is not interesting.
Denote
\vec{a}:=(a_{1},...,a_{k})
for

a_{j}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}+(n-k+1)d_{j}-(n+1)}{(n-k+1)d_{j}}.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}
(resp.
M^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}
) be the K-moduli stack (resp. K-moduli space) generically parametrizing
n-k
dimensional Fano manifolds of the form
\cap_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
which are K-semistable, and
\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{x}}
(resp.
M^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{x}}
) the K-moduli stack (resp. K-moduli space) generically parametrizing K-semistable log Fano manifolds of the form
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}S_{d_{j}})
, where
\vec{x}:=(x_{1},...,x_{k})
and
S_{d_{j}}
is a hypersurface of degree
d_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq k
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})
is a K-semistable log Fano pair (here
S_{d_{j}}
are not necessarily smooth), then
S_{d_{j}},\ j=1,...,k,
are different from each other and every
S_{d_{j}}
is irreducible.

Proof.

By the choice of
a_{j},j=1,...,k
, we know

1-a_{i}-S_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{i}})=0

for any
1\leq i\leq k
. We first show that
S_{d_{1}}
is irreducible. Suppose not, there is an irreducible component of
S_{d_{1}}
, denoted by
D
, satisfying

\beta_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(D)=1-a_{1}-S_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(D)<0.

The inequality holds since

S_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(D)>S_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{i}}).

Thus we get a contradiction to the K-semistability. Similarly, all
S_{d_{j}}
are irreducible. Next we show that
S_{d_{j}}
are mutually different. Suppose
S_{d_{1}}=S_{d_{2}}
, then

\beta_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{1}})=1-(a_{1}+a_{2})-S_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{1}})<0,

which is again a contradiction to the K-semistability. The proof is finished.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})
is a K-semistable log Fano pair (here
S_{d_{j}}
are not necessarily smooth), then
\cap_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
is a K-semistable Fano variety of dimension
n-k
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Since
\beta_{\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}}(S_{d_{1}})=0
, by Remark
2.4
, one could produce a special test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j})\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
of
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})
such that the central fiber is the projective cone over
(S_{d_{1}},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})
with the polarization
\mathcal{O}_{S_{d_{1}}}(d_{1})
, and

{\rm{Fut}}(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j};-K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{A}^{1}})=0.

Thus the central fiber
(\mathcal{X}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j,0})
is also K-semistable. Note that we have

\mathcal{O}_{S_{d_{1}}}(d_{1})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-\frac{1}{r}(K_{S_{d_{1}}}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})\quad\text{and}\quad a_{1}=1-\frac{r}{n}

for

r=\frac{n+1-d_{1}-\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}d_{j}}{d_{1}}.

By Lemma
2.2
, we see that
(S_{d_{1}},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})
is K-semistable.

Step 2.
By the similar proof of Proposition
5.1
, one concludes that
{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}},j=2,...,k,
are different from each other and every
{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}}
is irreducible. Note that

\beta_{S_{d_{1}},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}}}({S_{d_{2}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})=0,

thus similar as Step 1, there exists a special test configuration
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j})\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
of
(S_{d_{1}},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}{S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})
such that the central fiber is the projective cone over

\left({S_{d_{2}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}},\sum_{j=3}^{k}a_{j}{({S_{d_{j}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})}|_{({S_{d_{2}}}|_{S_{d_{1}}})}\right)=(S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}},\sum_{j=3}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}\cap S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}})

with the polarization
\mathcal{O}_{S_{d_{1}}\cap S_{d_{2}}}(d_{2})
, and

{\rm{Fut}}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\prime};-K_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}/\mathbb{A}^{1}})=0.

Thus the central fiber
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j,0})
is also K-semistable. Note that we have

\mathcal{O}_{S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}}}(d_{2})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-\frac{1}{r}(K_{S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}}}+\sum_{j=3}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}\cap S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}})\quad\text{and}\quad a_{2}=1-\frac{r}{n-1}

for

r=\frac{n+1-d_{1}-d_{2}-\sum_{j=3}^{k}a_{j}d_{j}}{d_{2}}.

By Lemma
2.2
, we see that
(S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}},\sum_{j=3}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}}\cap S_{d_{2}}\cap S_{d_{1}})
is K-semistable.

Step 3.
Continuing the same process, we get that the log pair

(S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}},a_{k}S_{d_{k}}\cap S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}})

is K-semistable and
S_{d_{k}}\cap S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}}
is irreducible. Since

\beta_{S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}},a_{k}S_{d_{k}}\cap S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}}}(S_{d_{k}}\cap S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}})=0,

there exists a special test configuration
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime},a_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime\prime})\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
of

(S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}},a_{k}S_{d_{k}}\cap S_{d_{k-1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}})

such that the central fiber is K-semistable and it is the projective cone over
S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}
with the polarization
\mathcal{O}_{S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}}(d_{k})
. Moreover, we have

\mathcal{O}_{S_{d_{k}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}}}(d_{k})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-\frac{1}{r}K_{S_{d_{k}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{1}}}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{k}=1-\frac{r}{n-k+1}

for

r=\frac{n+1-\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{j}}{d_{k}}.

Thus
S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}
is K-semistable by Lemma
2.2
. The proof is finished.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
is log smooth. If
\cap_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}}
is K-semistable of dimension
n-k
, then
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})
is a K-semistable log Fano pair.

Proof.

In the log smooth setting, the proof of Proposition
5.2
can be reversed by Lemma
2.2
. Concluded.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Suppose the log pair
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
is a degeneration of log smooth pairs of the form
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
via a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein flat family, i.e. there exists a family over a smooth pointed curve
0\in C
, denoted by
(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}_{j})\to C
, such that

(1)

\mathcal{X}\to C
is a flat family such that
-K_{\mathcal{X}/C}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and relative ample over
C
,

(2)

each
\mathcal{G}_{j}
is a Weil divisor on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{G}_{j}
is proportional to
-K_{\mathcal{X}/C}
over
C
,

(3)

(\mathcal{X}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}_{j,0})\cong(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
,

(4)

(\mathcal{X}_{t},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}_{j,t})
is of the form
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
for
t\in C\setminus\{0\}
, where
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
is log smooth,

then Propositions 5.1 and 5.2 stated for
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
also apply for
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
. Indeed, for Proposition
5.1
, the proof is similar for
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
to derive that
B_{j}
are irreducible and mutually different. For Proposition
5.2
, it is a little different since
B_{j}
may not be Cartier and Remark
2.4
cannot be directly applied. However, the idea is the same and we only perform the first step since the rest could be done similarly. Suppose
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
is a degeneration of log smooth pairs of the form
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
via a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein flat family such that
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j})
is a K-semistable log Fano pair. Then
B_{j}
are prime divisors and we have
\beta_{X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j}}(B_{i})=0
for every
1\leq i\leq k
. Thus
B_{1}
induces a special test configuration of
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j})
. Note that

X={\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mB_{1})).

By
[
7
, Section 2.3.1]
, the special test configuration can be reformulated as

\mathcal{X}={\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,mB_{1}-iB_{1})t^{-i},

and the central fiber can be formulated as

\mathcal{X}_{0}={\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(H^{0}(X,mB_{1}-iB_{1})/H^{0}(X,mB_{1}-(i+1)B_{1})\right).

Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-B_{1})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{B_{1}}\to 0.

Tensoring with the divisorial sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}((m-i)B_{1})
, we show that we still have the exactness

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-B_{1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((m-i)B_{1})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}((m-i)B_{1})

for
i\leq m
. If
i=m
, it is clear that the exactness holds true. If
i<m
, we have the exactness since
X
is
S_{2}
(note that
X
has klt singularities).
By Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing,
H^{1}(X,(m-i-1)B_{1})=0
for
i\leq m
, hence

H^{0}(X,mB_{1}-iB_{1})/H^{0}(X,mB_{1}-(i+1)B_{1})\cong H^{0}(B_{1},\mathcal{O}_{B_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((m-i)B_{1})),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{0}

\displaystyle\ \cong{\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(B_{1},\mathcal{O}_{B_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((m-i)B_{1}))

\displaystyle\ \cong{\rm{Proj}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(B_{1},\mathcal{O}_{B_{1}}(mB_{1}))s^{i}.

Thus we see that
(\mathcal{X}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j,0})
is the projective cone over
(B_{1},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}B_{j}\cap B_{1})
with respect to
{B_{1}}|_{B_{1}}
,
and the infinite divisor is
\mathcal{B}_{1,0}\cong B_{1}
. By the same computation as in the step 1 of the proof of Proposition
5.2
, we see that
(B_{1},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}B_{j}\cap B_{1})
is K-semistable (e.g.
[
19
, Proposition 2.11]
). Replacing
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j})
with
(B_{1},\sum_{j=2}^{k}a_{j}B_{j}\cap B_{1})
and applying the similar proof of Proposition
5.1
, we see that
B_{j}\cap B_{1}
are irreducible and mutually different for
2\leq j\leq k
, and

\beta_{B_{1},\Delta_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j}\cap B_{1}}(B_{2}\cap B_{1})=0.

Continuing the same process as above we obtain a K-semistable log Fano pair

(B_{2}\cap B_{1},\sum_{j=3}^{k}a_{j}B_{j}\cap B_{2}\cap B_{1})

and finally we see that
B_{1}\cap...\cap B_{k}
is K-semistable.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Propositions
5.2
and
5.3
, the log smooth pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})
is K-semistable if and only if
S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}
is K-semistable of dimension
n-k
. By Remark
5.4
, given
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j})\in\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{a}}
, then
\cap_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}
is also K-semistable of dimension
n-k
. Thus we could define the morphism

\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{a}}\to\mathcal{M}^{K}_{n,\vec{d}},\ \ (X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}B_{j})\mapsto\cap_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}.

It descends to a morphism between K-moduli spaces:

\phi:M^{K}_{n,\vec{d},\vec{a}}\to M^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}.

We aim to show that
\phi
is surjective. Suppose
[Y]\in M^{K}_{n,\vec{d}}
is of the form
S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}
deduced by a log smooth pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
, by
Proposition
5.3
,
[(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}S_{d_{j}})]
is a preimage. Suppose
[Y]
is not of the form
S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}
deduced by a log smooth pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
. Then there is a flat family
\mathcal{Y}\to C
over a smooth pointed curve
0\in C
such that
-K_{\mathcal{Y}/C}
is ample over
C
,
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cong Y
, and
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
is of the form
S_{d_{1}}\cap...\cap S_{d_{k}}
deduced by a log smooth pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
. Let
\mathcal{Y}^{*}\to C^{*}:=C\setminus\{0\}
be the family obtained via base change under the inclusion
C^{*}\to C
. Then there exists a family

(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{d_{j}})\to C^{*}

such that for any
t\in C^{*}
, the fiber
(\mathcal{X}_{t},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{d_{j},t})
is isomorphic to some log smooth pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{d_{j}})
and
\cap_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{d_{j},t}\cong\mathcal{Y}_{t}
. Note that
(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\mathcal{S}_{d_{j}})\to C^{*}
is a family of K-semistable log Fano pairs, by the properness of K-moduli space, we could find a complete family

(\tilde{\mathcal{X}},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{d_{j}})\to C

up to a finite base change and the central fiber
(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{d_{j},0})
is also K-semistable. By Remark
5.4
,
\cap_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{d_{j},0}
is K-semistable and
[Y]=[\cap_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{d_{j},0}]
by the separatedness of K-moduli space (e.g.
[
7
]
). Thus
[(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{d_{j},0})]
is a preimage of
[Y]
and
\phi
is surjective.
∎

Remark 5.7

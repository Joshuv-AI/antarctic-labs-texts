Schubert cells of mixed type in complex Lagrangian Grassmannians
Hyunmoon Kim

Abstract
We describe CW decompositions of complex Lagrangian Grassmannians, that contain as subcomplexes, CW decompositions of real Lagrangian Grassmannians by Schubert-Arnol’d cells. The degrees of attaching maps are explicitly computed in terms of quantities that can be read off from the corresponding shifted Young diagrams of mixed type. The signs are determined by a choice of lexicographical ordering on coordinates. As an immediate consequence, we obtain the homotopy extension property for real and complex Lagrangian Grassmannians. We also show some torsion classes in the integral homology of the real Lagrangian Grassmannian are contractible inside the complex Lagrangian Grassmannian.



1
Introduction

This paper solves a problem in algebraic topology motivated by geometric quantization. A key ingredient in geometric quantization is given by polarizations, which can loosely be described as some information to keep track of choices of observables that (Poisson) commute. At a linear level, these choices can be viewed as points on complex (or real) Lagrangian Grassmannians. As these Grassmannians have nontrivial topology, topologically nontrivial families of such choices exist. A famous example is the Maslov cycle. Its relevance in quantization was first observed by Maslov (cf Section II.2.2
[
Mas72
]
) and understood by Arnol’d in
[
Arn67
]
in terms of the geometry of the real Lagrangian Grassmannian.

For real Lagrangian Grassmannians, higher dimensional homology classes were described by Arnol’d in his seminar (see footnote in Fuks
[
Fuk68
]
) and the cohomology ring structures were studied by Borel
[
Bor51
]
(Proposition 31.4, for the oriented Lagrangian Grassmannian),
[
Bor53
]
(Theorem 12.1), and Fuks
[
Fuk68
]
. The torsion classes are all
2
-torsion, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
cohomology ring is an exterior algebra, and the integral cohomology ring modulo torsion is another exterior algebra (see Section 22 of Vassilyev
[
Vas88
]
for an exposition). The cellular decompositions into Schubert-Arnol’d cells were described Fuchs
[
Fuc04
]
, with the incidence coefficients specified up to sign. The signs were recently computed explicitly in Rabelo
[
Rab16
]
from Lie theoretic techniques.

The cohomology ring of complex Lagrangian Grassmannians were also studied by Borel
[
Bor51
]
(Theorem 26.1). Complex Lagrangian Grassmannians also have cellular decompositions into complex Schubert cells. These cells appear in only even degrees, so all attaching maps have degree zero, and there are no torsion classes in integral homology. These structures were described by Borel in
[
Bor54
]
(Theorem 3), Bernstein-Gel’fand-Gel’fand in
[
BGG73
]
(Theorem 2) and revisited by Pragacz in
[
Pra91
]
in the language of shifted Young diagrams, together with a purely combinatorial description of the integral cohomology ring.

While these CW complexes are concrete combinatorial models for real and complex Lagrangian Grassmannians, they are insufficient to describe the induced map of chain complexes for the natural embedding of real Lagrangian Grassmannians inside complex Lagrangian Grassmannians. We construct CW decompositions of complex Lagrangian Grassmannians, that contain the CW complexes of real Lagrangian Grassmannians by real Schubert cells (Schubert-Arnol’d cells) as subcomplexes. They are subdivisions of the CW structure given by complex Schubert cells. Here are precise statements.

Theorem
.

There is a partition of the complex Lagrangian Grassmannian of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})=\bigsqcup_{\lambda,\mu:\mu\leq\lambda}C_{\lambda+i\mu}

where
\lambda
,
\mu
are shifted Young diagrams associated to subsets of
\{1,2,...,n\}
such that the following holds:

1.

Each
C_{\lambda+i\mu}
is diffeomorphic to a product of finitely many copies of
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, and
\mathbb{C}
, of real dimension
|\lambda|+|\mu|
.

2.

The partition is a stratification with the following frontier condition:

C_{\lambda+i\mu}\subseteq\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}}\iff C_{\lambda+i\mu}\cap\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}}\neq\phi\iff\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}\text{ and }\mu\leq\mu^{\prime}.

3.

When
\mu
is the empty diagram,
C_{\lambda+i\mu}
is a real Schubert cell
C_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{R}}
of the embedded image of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
in
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
by
\cdot\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
.

4.

Each complex Schubert cell
C_{\lambda}
of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})

\bigsqcup_{\mu:\mu\leq\lambda}C_{\lambda+i\mu}
.

Theorem

Let
\{C_{\lambda+i\mu,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}(\mu)}
be the connected components of
C_{\lambda+i\mu}
. There is a CW decomposition of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
having the
C_{\lambda+i\mu,\varepsilon}
’s as cells and a closed formula for the degrees for the attaching maps
\partial C_{\lambda+i\mu,\varepsilon}\to C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime}}
(equations (
14
), (
15
), (
16
)).

By a CW complex, we refer to the definitions of Sections 4, 5 of Whitehead
[
Whi49
]
. In particular, these definitions require attaching maps (or characteristic maps) to be realized by actual maps on the boundaries of balls. We note that in the literature the terms cell, cellular, CW complexes are often used in a weaker sense (eg Borel
[
Bor54
]
), or only the incidence coefficients are provided (eg Fuchs
[
Fuc04
]
, Rabelo
[
Rab16
]
). We provide an explicit construction of the attaching maps in Proposition
8
.

We call the cells of this complex
Schubert cells of mixed type.
They are indexed by shifted Young diagrams of Pragacz
[
Pra91
]
with additional labels on the boxes. The degrees of the attaching maps are obtained using a straightforward, but involved computation in row reduction. It agrees up to sign with Fuchs
[
Fuc04
]
and Rabelo
[
Rab16
]
(and has a different sign convention from
[
Rab16
]
). Some low dimensional examples are given in Figures
1
and
2
.

An immediate consequence of this realization is that we have the homotopy extension property for the pair
(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}),(\cdot\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C})(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})))
. This construction is compatible with adding more variables (Corollary
20
). In fact, we can realize
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
as a subcomplex of any
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2(n+m)})
, and with all inclusion maps being compatible.

This construction is relevant in geometric quantization for the following reason. Geometric quantization requires a passage to the complex numbers, as observables are quantized from real valued smooth functions on symplectic manifolds to complex linear Hermitian operators on a Hilbert space. Locally, this task is performed by tensoring with
\mathbb{C}
. Our construction enables us to understand how some topologically nontrivial families of real Lagrangian subspaces behave after complexification. We refer to Corollary
21
for some examples.

In Section
2
we revisit the classical Schubert (Schubert-Arnol’d) calculus for real and complex Lagrangian Grassmannians in the language of shifted Young diagrams. In Section
3
we describe the Schubert calculus for Schubert cells of mixed type.

Review of Schubert cells

2.1
Shifted Young diagrams of Schubert cells

For the real Lagrangian Grassmannian, their Schubert stratification has been developed independently by Arnol’d (cf
[
Fuc04
]
). We will review the Schubert cells of the complex Lagrangian Grassmannian following Pragacz
[
Pra91
]
, with their indexing by shifted Young diagrams.

There are
2^{n}
Schubert cells of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
, corresponding to the
2^{n}
subsets
J
of
[n]:=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.

The reduced row echelon forms of these Schubert cells are described as follows. We will fix a Darboux basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{e}_{n},\mathbf{f}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{f}_{n}\}
, so that

\omega(\mathbf{e}_{j},\mathbf{e}_{k})=\omega(\mathbf{f}_{j},\mathbf{f}_{k})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(\mathbf{e}_{j},\mathbf{f}_{k})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }j=k\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will denote

\mathbf{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\mathbf{f}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{2n}

as a row vector, with the
p_{k}
’s in reverse order:

[\mathbf{v}]_{\{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{f}\}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc|ccc}q_{1}&\cdots&q_{n}&p_{n}&\cdots&p_{1}\end{array}\right].

If
\Gamma
is a complex Lagrangian subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
, there is a unique

complex matrix in reduced row echelon form corresponding to
\Gamma
in the fixed Darboux basis. Since
\Gamma
is Lagrangian,
\mathbf{e}_{k}
and
\mathbf{f}_{k}
cannot both be pivot locations for two different row vectors of the reduced row echelon form. Let
I:=[n]\backslash J
. Then denote by

the
k
th smallest element of
J
and by
i_{\ell}
the
\ell
th smallest element of
I
so that
J=\{j_{k}\}_{k=1}^{|J|}
and

. Then the reduced row echelon form corresponding to
J
is such that the
k
th row has pivots at the
k
th element of

\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}},\cdots,\mathbf{e}_{i_{|I|}},\mathbf{f}_{j_{|J|}},\cdots\mathbf{f}_{j_{1}},

with additional relations (equations (
1

More precisely, an

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<i_{a}\\

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{if }i_{a}>j_{b}

(1)

\displaystyle\omega^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbf{w}_{a},\mathbf{w}_{b})=

\displaystyle q_{aj_{b}}-q_{bj_{a}}

\displaystyle=0.

(2)

We can assign and count the number of independent parameters is as follows. All the coefficients of
\mathbf{v}_{a}
’s are determined by the coefficients of
\mathbf{w}_{b}
’s, and among the

\mathbf{w}_{b^{\prime}}

. So each
\mathbf{w}_{b}

The sequence

(or equivalently,

) determines a shifted Young diagram

\lambda_{J^{\vee}}:=\{(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}:1\leq k\leq|J|,\quad k\leq\ell\leq j_{k}^{\vee}+k-1\}

and we will identify
\lambda_{J^{\vee}}
with the box diagram corresponding to it. Following the indexing conventions of entries of matrices, we will take the first component of

to denote the vertical position of the boxes, and the second component of

to denote the horizontal position of the boxes. The cardinality of

. We will denote an element of

2.2
Attaching maps of Schubert cells

We will denote by
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
if
\lambda
is contained in
\lambda^{\prime}
, or equivalently, if there exist
J,J^{\prime}\subseteq[n]
such that
\lambda=\lambda_{J^{\vee}}
,
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda_{(J^{\prime})^{\vee}}
,
|J|\leq|J^{\prime}|
and
j_{k}^{\vee}\leq(j_{k}^{\prime})^{\vee}
for all
1\leq k\leq|J|
. Since every nonempty
\lambda
contains
(1,1)
, the containment of shifted Young diagrams assumes they are aligned on the bottom left corner. For a comparison with the Bruhat ordering, we refer the reader to Proposition 6.1 of Ikeda and Naruse
[
IN09

Every
C_{\lambda}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{|\lambda|}

C_{\lambda}
inside some
\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}}}

C_{\lambda}

\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}
are such that
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}

There are two cases to consider, i) when
\lambda^{\prime}
has more rows than
\lambda

\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}

\lambda=\lambda_{J^{\vee}}

Lemma 12
.

If
\mu\nleq\mu^{\prime}
, then there exists an element in
\mu\setminus\mathring{\mu}
not in
\mu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

\mu^{\prime}\cap\mu<\mu
, so there exists a
\mu^{\prime\prime}
with
|\mu^{\prime\prime}|=|\mu|-1
such that
\mu^{\prime}\cap\mu\leq\mu^{\prime\prime}<\mu
. Then the unique square in
\mu\setminus\mu^{\prime\prime}
is contained in
\mu\setminus\mathring{\mu}
but is not contained in
\mu^{\prime}
.
∎

Suppose
\mu\neq\mu^{\prime}
, and without loss of generality
\mu\nleq\mu^{\prime}
. Then the square given by Lemma
12
has the coordinate
(k,\ell)
. Then
y_{k\ell}^{\lambda}(\Gamma)
is nonzero for
\Gamma\in C_{\lambda+i\mu}
and zero for
\Gamma\in C_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}
. So the
C_{\lambda+i\mu}
’s are disjoint. Moreover,
\mathbb{C}^{|\lambda|}
is partitioned by how many of the last
k
coordinates are real. So we have the partitions

C_{\lambda}=\bigsqcup_{\mu:\mu\leq\lambda}C_{\lambda+i\mu},\quad\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\mu\\
\mu\leq\lambda\end{subarray}}C_{\lambda+i\mu}.

Lemma 13

(Frontier condition of
C_{\lambda}
)
.

The following are equivalent

1.

C_{\lambda+i\mu}\cap\overline{C_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}}\cap C_{\lambda}\neq\phi
.

2.

C_{\lambda+i\mu}\subseteq\overline{C_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}}\cap C_{\lambda}
.

3.

\mu\leq\mu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(2)\implies(1)
is immediate, as
C_{\lambda+i\mu}
is nonempty.
(3)\implies(2)
:
U_{\lambda+i\mu}\subseteq\overline{U_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}}
, so
(z^{\lambda})^{-1}(U_{\lambda+i\mu})\subseteq(z^{\lambda})^{-1}(\overline{U_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}})
.
(1)\implies(3)
: If
\mu\nleq\mu^{\prime}
, by Lemma
12
, there exists a
(k,\ell)\in\mu\setminus\mathring{\mu}
not in
\mu^{\prime}
. Then
y_{k\ell}^{\lambda}(\Gamma_{k})=0
for any sequence
\Gamma_{k}\in C_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}
, but
y_{k\ell}^{\lambda}(\Gamma)\neq 0
for all
\Gamma\in C_{\lambda+i\mu}
.
∎

Proposition 16

(Frontier condition)
.

The following are equivalent

1.

C_{\lambda+i\mu}\cap\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}}\neq\phi
.

2.

C_{\lambda+i\mu}\subseteq\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}}
.

3.

\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
and
\mu\leq\mu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(2)\implies(1)
is immediate as
C_{\lambda+i\mu}
is nonempty.
(3)\implies(2)
: There exists an increasing sequence
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{\ell}
such that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
,
\lambda_{\ell}=\lambda^{\prime}
, and
|\lambda_{k+1}|=|\lambda_{k}|+1
for
1\leq k<\ell=|\lambda^{\prime}|-|\lambda|
, and a sequence of embeddings
\{\varphi_{\lambda_{k}}^{\lambda_{k+1}}\}_{k=1}^{\ell}
. For each
\Gamma\in C_{\lambda+i\mu}
, let

\Gamma_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}}:=\varphi_{\lambda_{\ell-1}}^{\lambda_{\ell}}(\cdots(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{3}}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}(\Gamma,t_{1}),t_{2}),\cdots),t_{\ell})\quad t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}

where if
t_{j}=0
for some
j\in\{1,\cdots,\ell\}
, then
t_{k}=0
for all
k\geq j
. Then by equations
3
,
5
and Proposition
15
,
\Gamma_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}}\in C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu}\subseteq\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}}
, and

\lim_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}\to 0}\Gamma_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{\ell}}=\Gamma.

(1)\implies(3)
: If
\lambda\nleq\lambda^{\prime}
, then
C_{\lambda}\cap\overline{C_{\lambda^{\prime}}}=\phi
, which contradicts the assumption. So
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
. By assumption there exists a sequence
\Gamma^{\prime}_{m}\to\Gamma
with
\Gamma^{\prime}_{m}\in C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}
. By the computation of Jacobian determinants of equation (
10
),
\varphi_{\lambda_{k}}^{\lambda_{k+1}}
are local homeomorphisms from
C_{\lambda_{k}+i\mu^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}
to
C_{\lambda_{k+1}+i\mu^{\prime}}
. By induction, we get a local homeomorphism
C_{\lambda+i\mu^{\prime}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{|\lambda^{\prime}|-|\lambda|}
to
C_{\lambda^{\prime}+i\mu^{\prime}}
. Let
(\Gamma_{m},(t^{m}_{1},\cdots,t^{m}_{\ell}))
be the image
\Gamma_{m}^{\prime}
under these local homeomorphisms, which exist for
m
sufficiently large. Then if
\mu\nleq\mu^{\prime}
, there exists
(a,b)\in\mu\setminus\mathring{\mu}
not in
\mu^{\prime}
. Then
y_{ab}^{\lambda}(\Gamma_{m})=0
but
y_{ab}^{\lambda}(\Gamma)\neq 0
, which is a contradiction. So
\mu\leq\mu^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 17

(Attaching maps of Schubert cells of mixed type)
.

(14)

4
Applications

If
n,m
are positive integers, let
\iota_{n,m}:\mathbb{C}^{2n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2(n+m)}
be an embedding given by the linear extension of
\mathbf{e}_{i}\mapsto\mathbf{e}_{i+m}
,
\mathbf{f}_{i}\mapsto\mathbf{f}_{i+m}
.
\iota_{n,m}
induces an embedding of complex Lagrangian Grassmannians
I_{n,m}:\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2(n+m)})
as

I_{n,m}(\Gamma):=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{e}_{m}\}\oplus\iota_{n,m}(\Gamma).

If
M_{\Gamma}
is the
n\times 2n
matrix representing the reduced row echelon form of
\Gamma\in\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
, then the
(n+m)\times 2(n+m)
matrix representing the reduced row echelon form of
I_{n,m}(\Gamma)
is

\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Id}_{m,m}&0_{m,2n}&0_{m,m}\\
0_{n,m}&M_{\Gamma}&0_{m,m}\end{bmatrix}.

I_{n,m}
maps each Schubert cell of mixed type
C_{\lambda+i\mu,\varepsilon}
of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
to the Schubert cell of mixed type
C_{\lambda+i\mu,\varepsilon}
of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2(n+m)})
.
I_{n,m}
is continuous, so it preserves all incidence relations. Moreover,
\varphi_{\lambda}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\circ I_{n,m}=I_{n,m}\circ\varphi_{\lambda}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
, so it preserves all degrees of attaching maps as well. So the
I_{m,n}(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
can be realized as a subcomplex of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2(n+m)})
.

Similarly, let
\iota_{m,n}^{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{R}^{2m}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and
I_{n,m}^{\mathbb{R}}:\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2m})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
be the corresponding maps for real coefficients. Suppressing these identifications, we will regard
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
as subcomplexes of
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2(n+m)})
.

Corollary 20

(Homotopy extension property)
.

(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2(n+m)}),\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}))
satisfies the homotopy extension property.

Corollary 21
.

If
0<k\leq n
and
k
is even then
\overline{C}_{\lambda_{\{k\}}}^{\mathbb{R}}
defines a nontrivial torsion class in
H_{k}(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n});\mathbb{Z})
, and is homotopic to a
2k
-sphere inside
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

Proof.

By equation (
12b
),
\overline{C}_{\lambda_{\{k\}}+i\phi}
defines a homology class in
H_{k}(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n});\mathbb{Z})
.
By induction on
k
,
\overline{C}_{\lambda_{\{k-1\}}+i\lambda_{\{1\}},+}
is contractible. Taking the quotient of this subcomplex inside
\overline{C}_{\lambda_{\{k\}}+i\lambda_{\{1\}},+}
we get a homotopy equivalence between
\overline{C}_{\lambda_{\{k\}}+i\lambda_{\{1\}},+}
and the closed
2k+1
-ball. This equivalence identifies the subcomplex
\overline{C}_{\lambda_{\{k\}}+i\phi}
with its boundary.
∎

Remark 22

(

(cf Liu
[
Liu18
]
). So the generator of
H_{2}(\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n});\mathbb{Z})
is spherical, which is something we cannot conclude from the Hurewicz theorem. By Corollary
21
this pinched torus is homotopic to a
2
-sphere inside
\operatorname{Lag}^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
.

A study of the cohomological rigidity property
Mohsen Asgharzadeh, Olgur Celikbas, Arash Sadeghi

Abstract
. In this paper, motivated by a work of Luk and Yau, and Huneke and Wiegand, we study various aspects of the cohomological rigidity property of tensor product of modules over commutative Noetherian rings. We determine conditions under which the vanishing of a single local cohomology module of a tensor product implies the vanishing of all the lower ones, and obtain new connections between the local cohomology modules of tensor products and the Tate homology. Our argument yields bounds for the depth of tensor products of modules, as well as criteria for freeness of modules over complete intersection rings. Along the way, we also give a splitting criteria for vector bundles on smooth complete intersections.

1.
Introduction

A vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on a projective scheme
X
equipped with a very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
is said to have the
cohomological rigidity property
if there is a positive integer
i
such that the vanishing of

{}\hh^{i}_{\ast}(X,\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}}^{\ast}):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hh^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\ast}(n))

implies that
\mathcal{E}
is trivial, i.e., isomorphic to a direct sum of line bundles. A paradigm for this rigidity property is a result of Luk and Yau
[
43
]
, which is concerned with
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathcal{O}(1))
.

Our motivation in this paper comes from a beautiful work of Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
]
, which reproves and extends the aforementioned rigidity result of Luk and Yau via the machinery of commutative algebra. Huneke and Wiegand investigates suitable conditions under which if one local cohomology module of a tensor product of finitely generated modules vanishes, then all lower ones vanish. As a consequence, Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
]
obtained remarkable results that relate Serre’s conditions to the vanishing of a single local cohomology module of a tensor product.

In this paper, following the work of Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
]
, we study the (non) vanishing of local cohomology modules, and investigate depth and torsion properties of tensor products of modules. The new advantage we have is that we work with local cohomology functors with respect to specialization-closed subsets of
\Spec R
; this allows us to generalize the results of Huneke and Wiegand in this direction, as well as various results from the literature, especially those stated in terms of Serre’s conditions.

One of our main results is Theorem
3.8
, which can be considered as a generalization of rigidity theorem of Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
, 2.4]
. A special case of Theorem
3.8
can be stated as follows; see Proposition
3.11
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be a regular local ring and let
M
and
N
be non-zero finitely generated
R
-modules. Let
\mz\subset\Spec R
be a specialization-closed subset and let
n\geq 0
be an integer. Assume the following hold:

(i)

\NF(M)\cap\NF(N)\subseteq\mz
.

(ii)

\hh_{\mz}^{n}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
.

(iii)

\gr_{R}(\mz,M)\geq n
and
\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\geq n
.

Then it follows that
\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
, and
\Tor_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
j\geq 1
.

In Theorem
1.1
,
\NF(M)
denotes the set
\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R):M_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is not free over }R_{\mathfrak{p}}\}
, i.e., the
non-free locus
of
M
. Recall that a subset
\mz
of
\Spec R
is called
specialization-closed
if every prime ideal of
R
containing some prime ideal in
\mz
belongs to
\mz
. Clearly, every closed subset of
\Spec R
in the Zariski topology is specialization-closed. For an integer
n
, we denote by
\hh^{n}_{\mz}(-)
the
n
-th local cohomology functor with respect to
\mz
. Moreover, the
grade of
M
with respect to
\mz
is denoted by
\gr_{R}(\mz,M)
, and is defined as the infimum of the set of integers
n
such that
\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M)
is non-zero; see
2.8
and
2.15
for further details.

The torsion in tensor product of modules was initially studied by Auslander in his seminal paper “Modules over unramified regular local rings”
[
4
]
. For nonzero finitely generated modules
M
and
N
over an unramified (or equi-characteristic) regular local ring, Auslander proved that
M
and
N
are torsion-free and
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, provided that
M\otimes_{R}N
is torsion-free; subsequently, the ramified case of Auslander’s result was established by Lichtenbaum
[
41
]
. Theorem
1.1
provides a generalization of the aforementioned results of Auslander and Lichtenbaum; this is because the torsion submodule of a module
M
can be characterized by the
\mz
-torsion submodule
\Gamma_{\mz}(M)
of
M
for a suitable choice of
\mz
; see Proposition
2.19
and Corollary
3.12
. To the best of our knowledge, the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
is new even for the closed subsets of
\Spec(R)
.

In Section
3
we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
. Sections 4 and 5 are devoted to several applications; see, for example, Corollaries
4.6
,
4.1
and
5.2
.
In Section 6 we apply our results and study the vanishing of local cohomology modules of the Frobenius powers over local rings of prime characteristic; see Theorem
6.6
. We make use of the fact that the Frobenius endomorphism
{{}^{\varphi^{r}}\!}R
is Tor-rigid over such complete intersection rings, and obtain the following as an application of Theorem
6.6
; see Corollary
6.10
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional local complete intersection ring of prime characteristic. Then
R
is regular provided that at least one of the following conditions hold:

(i)

R
is reduced and
\hh^{d-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}({{}^{\varphi^{r}}\!}R\otimes_{R}{{}^{\varphi^{s}}\!}R)=0
for some integers
r,s\geq 1
.

(ii)

R
is normal and
\hh^{d-2}_{\mathfrak{m}}({{}^{\varphi^{r}}\!}R\otimes_{R}{{}^{\varphi^{s}}\!}R)=0
for some integers
r,s\geq 1
.

In Section 7 we determine a new connection between local cohomology of tensor products of modules and the Tate homology
\widehat{\Tor}
; see
2.20
for the definition. The next result follows from Theorem
7.1
; it is the second main theorem of this paper besides Theorem
3.8
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring, and let
M
and
N
be finitely generated
R
-modules. Assume
\mz\subset\Spec R
is a specialization-closed subset, and that the following conditions hold:

(i)

\NF(M)\cap\NF(N)\subseteq\mz
.

(ii)

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\depth(R_{\mathfrak{p}})+n
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mz
and for some integer
n\geq 0
.

Then it follows that:

(1)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{-i}^{R}(M,N)
for all
i=0,\ldots,n-1
.

(2)

There is an injection
\widehat{\Tor}_{-n}^{R}(M,N)\hookrightarrow\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)
.

As far as we know, Theorem
1.3
is new, even for closed subsets of
\Spec(R)
. Various applications of Theorem
1.3
corroborating the literature include Corollaries
7.3
,
7.4
and
7.15
. Furthermore, Theorem
1.3
determines a useful bound on depth of tensor products
M\otimes_{R}N
of certain modules
M
and
N
over complete intersection rings:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection ring of codimension
c
, and let
M
and
N
be nonzero finitely generated
R
-modules, each of which is locally free on the punctured spectrum of
R
. If
\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)-\depth(R)\geq c
, then
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\leq\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)-\depth(R)
.

The conclusion of Corollary
1.4
seems interesting to us since it does not assume the vanishing of Tor modules; see Corollary
7.5
and cf.
[
22
, 3.1]
.

Our aim in Section 8 is, motivated by the work of Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
]
, to determine some new criteria for freeness of modules in terms of the vanishing of local cohomology. More precisely, we are concerned with the cohomological rigidity property of tensor products in the sense that the freeness of a module
M
follows from the vanishing of
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M^{\ast})
for some integer
i
. Our work extends several results from the literature. For example, as a consequence of Theorem
8.1
, we proved the following result in Corollary
8.14
; it extends
[
18
, 3.9]
which establishes the case where
n=0
:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection ring of even dimension
d
, and let
M
be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module which is locally free on the punctured spectrum of
R
. If
\hh^{n}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})=0
for some integer
n
where
0\leq n<d
, then
M
is free.

The cohomological rigidity property on hypersurfaces of odd dimension was first studied by Dao
[
28
]
: a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on an odd dimensional hypersurface of dimension at least
3
splits if and only if
\hh^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\ast}(i))=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}

[
28
, 1.5]
. This result has been recently studied by Česnavičius, who proved that a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on a smooth complete intersection of dimension at least
3
splits into a sum of line bundles if and only if
\hh^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\ast}(i))=0=\hh^{2}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\ast}(i))
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}

[
24
, 1.2]
. As an application of our study, for an arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay vector bundle on a smooth complete intersection of odd dimension we obtain a stronger result; see Corollary
8.15
:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
k
be a field and let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a globally complete intersection of odd dimension
d
. Assume
\mathcal{E}
is an arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay vector bundle and that
\hh^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{*}(j))=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and for some
i
where
0<i<d
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is a direct sum of powers of
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Furthermore, for an arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay vector bundle on a hypersurface we have:

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
k
be a field and let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a hypersurface. Assume
\mathcal{E}
is an arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay vector bundle such that

\hh^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{*}(j))=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and for some even integer
i
where
0<i<\dim X
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is direct sum of powers of
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

2.
Notations and preliminary results

Throughout,
R
,
\Md(R)
,
\md(R)
and
\mathcal{P}(R)
denote a commutative Noetherian ring, the category of
R
-modules, the category of finitely generated
R
-modules, and the subcategory of
\md(R)
of finitely generated projective
R
-modules, respectively.

If
R
is a local ring, i.e., a commutative Noetherian local ring, then
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the unique maximal ideal of
R
, and
k
denotes the residue field of
R
.

(-)^{*}
stands for the algebraic dual
\Hom_{R}(-,R)
, and
e_{M}
is the natural map
M\to M^{**}
.

M,N\in\Md(R)
are said to be
stably isomorphic
, denoted by
M\approx N
, provided that
M\oplus P\cong N\oplus Q
for some
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}(R)
.

2.1
.

Right and left projective approximations.
An
R
-module homomorphism
f:X\to M
(respectively,
f:M\to X
) with
X\in\mathcal{P}(R)
is called a
right
(respectively,
left
)
projective approximation
of
M
provided that every
R
-module homomorphism
g:Y\to M
(respectively,
g:M\to Y
) with
Y\in\mathcal{P}(R)
factors through
f
, that is,
g=f\circ h
(respectively,
g=h\circ f
) for some
R
-module homomorphism
h:Y\to X
(respectively,
h:X\to Y
).

A right (respectively, left) projective approximation
f:X\to M
(resp.
f:M\to X
) is called
minimal
if every endomorphism
g:X\to X
satisfying
f=f\circ g
(resp.
f=g\circ f
) is an automorphism.

Note that a right projective approximation (respectively, a minimal right projective approximation) is nothing but a surjective homomorphism from a projective
R
-module (respectively, a projective cover). Note also that, if
M\in\md(R)
and
\phi:P\twoheadrightarrow M^{*}
is an epimorphism with
P\in\mathcal{P}(R)
, then
e_{M}\circ\phi^{*}
is a left projective approximation of
M
.

2.2
.

Auslander transpose and (co)syzygy.

Let
M\in\md(R)
that has a right projective approximation
P_{0}\overset{\partial_{0}}{\to}M
. Then the kernel of
\partial_{0}
is called the first

syzygy
of
M
; it is denoted by
\Omega^{1}M
and unique up to projective equivalence. Inductively,
we define the
n
-th syzygy module of
M
as
\Omega^{n}M:=\Omega^{1}(\Omega^{n-1}M)
for all
n\geq 1
. We set, by convention,
\Omega^{0}M=M
.

Let
P_{1}\overset{\partial_{1}}{\rightarrow}P_{0}\overset{\partial_{0}}{\to}M\to 0
be a finite projective presentation of
M
. Then the
transpose
of
M
, denoted by
\Tr M
, is
\coker{\partial_{1}}^{*}
given in the following exact sequence

(
2.2
.1)

0\rightarrow M^{*}\rightarrow P_{0}^{*}\overset{\partial_{1}^{\ast}}{\rightarrow}P_{1}^{*}\rightarrow\Tr M\rightarrow 0.

Note that
M^{*}\approx\Omega^{2}\Tr M
. Note also that
\Tr M
is unique, up to projective equivalence, and the minimal projective
presentations of
M
represent isomorphic transposes of
M
.

For every
M\in\md(R)
and
N\in\Md(R)
, there exists the following exact sequence:

(
2.2
.2)

0\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)\to\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\to 0,

where the middle map is the evaluation map
[
5
, 2.6]
. In particular, setting
N=R
, we see that the canonical
map
M\to M^{**}
is part of the exact sequence

(
2.2
.3)

0\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{**}\to\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\to 0.

Also, there is a
4
-term exact sequence
[
5
, 2.8]
:

(
2.2
.4)

\Tor_{2}^{R}(\Tr\Omega^{n}M,N)\rightarrow\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,R)\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(\Tr\Omega^{n}M,N)\rightarrow 0.

Suppose
M\in\md(R)
equipped with a left projective approximation
M\overset{\partial_{-1}}{\to}P_{-1}
. Then we call the cokernel of
\partial_{-1}
the first

cosyzygy
of
M
and denote it by
\Omega^{-1}M
. Inductively, we define the
n
-th cosyzygy module
of
M
as
\Omega^{-n}M:=\Omega^{-1}(\Omega^{-(n-1)}M)
for all
n\geq 1
. It follows that
\Omega^{-i}M\approx\Tr\Omega^{i}\Tr M
for all
i\geq 1
; see
[
50
, 2.4]
. Hence, by replacing
M
with
\Tr M
in (
2.2
.4), we obtain the following exact sequence:

(
2.2
.5)

\Tor_{2}^{R}(\Omega^{-n}M,N)\rightarrow\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(\Omega^{-n}M,N)\rightarrow 0.

For each
M\in\md(R)
and integer
i\geq 1
, it follows by the definition that there exists an exact sequence:

(
2.2
.6)

0\to\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,R)\to\Tr\Omega^{i-1}M\to X\to 0,

where
X\approx\Omega\Tr\Omega^{i}M
. By replacing
M
with
\Tr M
in (
2.2
.6) and using the fact that
\Omega^{-i}M\approx\Tr\Omega^{i}\Tr M
for all
i\geq 1
, we obtain the
following fact that will be used throughout the paper:

(
2.2
.7)

\text{If }\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0\text{ for some }i\geq 1,\text{ then }\Omega^{-(i-1)}M\approx\Omega\Omega^{-i}M.

2.3
.

Complete intersection and Gorenstein dimensions.
The notion of Gorenstein dimension was initially introduced by Auslander
[
3
]
and subsequently developed by Auslander and Bridger in
[
5
]
.

An
R
-module
M
is called
totally reflexive
provided that the natural map
M\rightarrow M^{**}
is an isomorphism and
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0=\Ext^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)
for all
i\geq 1
. The
Gorenstein dimension
of
M
, denoted
\G-dim_{R}(M)
, is defined to be the infimum of all nonnegative integers
n
, such that there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow G_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

in which each
G_{i}
is a totally reflexive
R
-module.

Every finitely generated module over a Gorenstein ring has finite Gorenstein dimension. Moreover, if
R
is local and
\G-dim_{R}(M)<\infty
, then it follows that
\G-dim_{R}(M)=\depth R-\depth_{R}(M)
; see
[
5
, 4.13]
.

A
quasi-deformation
of
R
is a diagram
R\rightarrow A\twoheadleftarrow Q
of local homomorphisms, in which
R\rightarrow A
is faithfully flat, and
A\twoheadleftarrow Q
is surjective with kernel generated by a regular sequence. The
complete intersection dimension
of
M
, introduced by Avramov, Gasharov and Peeva
[
9
]
, is:

\CI_{R}(M)=\inf\{\pd_{Q}(M\otimes_{R}A)-\pd_{Q}(A)\mid R\rightarrow A\twoheadleftarrow Q\text{ is a quasi-deformation}\}.

Therefore, an
R
-module
M
has finite complete intersection dimension if there exists a quasi-deformation
R\rightarrow A\twoheadleftarrow Q
for which
\pd_{Q}(M\otimes_{R}A)
is finite.

Note that, if
M
is finitely generated
R
-module, then it follows
\G-dim_{R}(M)\leq\CI_{R}(M)\leq\pd_{R}(M)
, and
\CI_{R}(M)<\infty
if
R
is a complete intersection ring. Moreover, if
R
is local and
\CI_{R}(M)<\infty
, then one has by
[
9
, 1.4]
that

(
2.3
.1)

\G-dim_{R}(M)=\CI_{R}(M)=\depth R-\depth_{R}(M).

2.4
.

Complexity.
Assume
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is local and
M,N\in\md(R)
. Then the
complexity
of the pair
(M,N)
, defined by Avramov and Buchweitz
[
8
]
, is:

\cx_{R}(M,N)=\inf\{b\in\mathbb{N}\mid\exists a\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }\nu_{R}(\Ext^{n}_{R}(M,N))\leq an^{b-1}\text{ for all }n\gg 0\},

where
\nu_{R}(-)
denotes the minimal number of generators. Accordingly, the complexity
\cx_{R}(M)
of
M
, initially introduced by Avramov
[
7
]
in local algebra, can be given as
\cx_{R}(M,k)
.

Avramov, Gasharov and Peeva
[
9
, 5.3]
proved that every module of finite complete intersection dimension also has finite complexity.

Note,
\cx_{R}(M,N)=0
if and only if
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\gg 0
. Also, it follows by the definition that
\pd_{R}(M)<\infty
if and only if
\cx_{R}(M)=0
, and
M
has bounded Betti numbers if and only if
\cx_{R}(M)\leq 1
.

If
R
is a complete intersection, then one has
[
8
, 5.7]

(
2.4
.1)

\cx_{R}(M,N)=\cx_{R}(N,M)\leq\min\{\cx_{R}(M),\cx_{R}(N)\}\leq\cod R.

2.5

Depth formula.
If
R
is local, then a pair
(M,N)
in

\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)=\depth R+\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N).

Auslander

.
Auslander’s result has been extended by Huneke and Wiegand for complete intersection rings: Tor-independent modules over complete intersection rings satisfy the depth formula; see
[

More generally, one has:

Theorem 2.6

(Araya and Yoshino

\CI_{R}(M)<\infty

\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0

[

, 2.5]
.

2.8

Specialization-closed subsets of the spectrum
[
31
]
.
A subset
\mz\subset\Spec R
is called

provided that the following condition holds:

If
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)
, where
\mathfrak{p}\in\mz

\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, then it follows that

We collect some examples of specialization-closed subsets of
\Spec R
:

Example 2.9
.

(i)

Every closed subset of
\Spec(R)
with respect to Zariski toplogy is specialization-closed.

(ii)

If

is a domain, then
\Spec(R)\setminus\{0\}
is specialization-closed subset of
\Spec(R)
.

(iii)

Let
0\leq n\leq\dim R
. Then

is a specialization-closed subset of
\Spec(R)
.

One of the aims of this paper is to extend the following result of Auslander and Lichtenbaum
[
4
,
41
]
: if
R
is a regular local ring,
M,N\in\md(R)
and

is torsion-free, then

and
N
are Tor-independent torsion-free modules. Our technique relies upon using local cohomology theory with respect to specialization-closed subsets
[

]
.

2.10

Local cohomology with respect to specialization-closed subsets.
Let
\mz
be a specialization-closed subset of
\Spec R
. For each

, we define the following submodule of

:

\Gamma_{\mz}(M)=\{m\in M\mid\Supp_{R}(Rm)\subseteq\mz\}.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
i
-th right derived functor of
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
, denoted

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(-)
, is referred to as the
i
-th
local cohomology functor with respect to
\mz
.

If
\mz
is a closed subset of
\Spec R
, i.e.,
\mz=\V(\mathfrak{a})
for some ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, then we denote
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
(respectively,

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(-)
(respectively,

M\in\Md(R)

We set, for a specialization-closed subset
\mz
of

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mz
and
\mathcal{W}
be specialization-closed subsets of
\Spec(R)
. Then,

(i)

\Gamma_{\mz}(M)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{a}\in\Sigma}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
.

(ii)

Each short exact sequence

in

2.15
.

Grade of a module with respect to a specialization-closed subset.

Let
\mz\subset\Spec R
be a specialization-closed subset and let

. Then the

with respect to
\mz
is:

\gr_{R}(\mz,M)=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M)\neq 0\}.

If
\mz=\V(\mathfrak{a})
for some ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, then we denote
\gr_{R}(\mz,M)
by
\gr_{R}(\mathfrak{a},M)
. Note that, if
M\in\md(R)
, then

\gr(\mathfrak{a},M)
is equal to the maximal length of an
M
-regular sequence contained in
\mathfrak{a}
.

The next result, for the case where
\mz
is a closed subset of
\Spec R
, was initially proved by Grothendieck; see
[
31
, III.2.9]
. This fact can be viewed as a generalization of
[
51
, 4.1]
.
As the result plays an important role for our arguments in this paper, we provide the details.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mz\subset\Spec R
be specialization-closed and
M\in\md(R)
. Then it follows that

\gr_{R}(\mz,M)=\inf\{\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})|\mathfrak{p}\in\mz\}.

Proof.

Let
0\to M\to E^{0}(M)\overset{\partial_{0}}{\to}E^{1}(M)\overset{\partial_{1}}{\to}\cdots
be a minimal injective resolution of
M
, where we have that
E^{i}(M)=\underset{\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec R}{\bigoplus}E(R/\mathfrak{q})^{\mu_{i}(\mathfrak{q},M)}
. Apply the functor
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
to the above resolution, we get the complex

(
2.16
.1)

0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mz}(M)\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{0}(M))\overset{\Gamma_{\mz}(\partial_{0})}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{1}(M))\overset{\Gamma_{\mz}(\partial_{1})}{\longrightarrow}\cdots.

Set
n:=\inf\{\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})|\mathfrak{p}\in\mz\}
. Hence, there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\mz
such that
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}})=n
. It follows from
[
48
, Theorem 2]
that

(
2.16
.2)

\mu_{n}(\mathfrak{p}_{0},M)\neq 0=\mu_{i}(\mathfrak{p},M)\text{ for all }\mathfrak{p}\in\mz\text{ and }i<n.

Note that
\Supp_{R}(E(R/\mathfrak{p}))\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R
. As
\mz
is specialization-closed, we see that

(
2.16
.3)

\Gamma_{\mz}(E(R/\mathfrak{q}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}E(R/\mathfrak{q})&\text{ if}\ \ \mathfrak{q}\in\mz\\
0&\text{ if }\ \ \mathfrak{q}\notin\mz.\\
\end{array}\right.

It follows from (
2.16
.3) that

(
2.16
.4)

\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{i}(M))\cong\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec R}\Gamma_{\mz}(E(R/\mathfrak{q}))^{\mu_{i}(\mathfrak{q},M)}\cong\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}\in\mz}\Gamma_{\mz}(E(R/\mathfrak{q}))^{\mu_{i}(\mathfrak{q},M)}.

Hence, by (
2.16
.2) and (
2.16
.4) we have

(
2.16
.5)

\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{n}(M))\neq 0=\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{i}(M))\ \text{ for all }i<n.

Therefore, by (
2.16
.1) and (
2.16
.5) we see that
\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M)\cong\ker(\Gamma_{\mz}(\partial_{i}))/\im(\Gamma_{\mz}(\partial_{i-1}))=0
for all
i<n
. In other words,

\gr_{R}(\mz,M)\geq n=\inf\{\depth(M_{\mathfrak{p}})|\mathfrak{p}\in\mz\}
. Thus, it is enough to show that
\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M)\neq 0
. By using (
2.16
.1) and (
2.16
.5) we obtain the following exact sequence

(
2.16
.6)

0\longrightarrow\hh_{\mz}^{n}(M)\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{n}(M))\overset{\Gamma_{\mz}(\partial_{n})}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{n+1}(M)).

Note that
E^{n}(M)
is an essential extension of
\im\partial_{n-1}
. In other words,
\im\partial_{n-1}\cap L\neq 0
for any non-zero submodule
L
of

E^{n}(M)
. It follows from (
2.16
.5) that
\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{n}(M))
is a non-zero submodule of
E^{n}(M)
. Therefore, by the exact sequence (
2.16
.6) we obtain the following:

\hh_{\mz}^{n}(M)=\ker(\Gamma_{\mz}(\partial_{n}))=\ker\partial_{n}\cap(\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{n}(M)))=\im\partial_{n-1}\cap(\Gamma_{\mz}(E^{n}(M)))\neq 0,

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

Let

Applying the functor

to the above exact sequence, we obtain the following exact sequence

2.18
.

Serre’s condition and torsion submodule.
If
M\in\md(R)
and
n\geq 0
is an integer, then
M
is said to satisfy
Serre’s condition

(S_{n})
provided that
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\min\{n,\Ht\mathfrak{p}\}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R
(recall that, by convention,
\depth_{R}(0)=\infty
.)

If
N\in\Md(R)
, then the
torsion submodule
of
N
, denoted by
\T(N)
, is the kernel of the natural homomorphism
N\to Q(R)\otimes_{R}N
where
Q(R)
is the total quotient ring of
R
. The module
N
is said to be
torsion-free
if
\T(N)=0
, and
torsion
if
\T(N)=N
. Note that, if
R
is unmixed, i.e., all associated prime ideals of
R
are minimal, e.g.,
R
is reduced, and
N\in\md R
, then
\T(N)=0
if and only if
N
satisfies
(S_{1})
.

If
M\in\Md(R)
and
n\geq 1
, then
M
is called
n
-
torsion-free
if
\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

In the following we investigate the relation between Serre’s condition and the vanishing of local cohomology with respect to specialization-closed subsets of
\Spec R
.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
M\in\Md(R)
and let
N\in\md(R)
.

(i)

If
R
is unmixed and
\mz=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\mid\Ht\mathfrak{p}\geq 1\}
, then it follows that
\Gamma_{\mz}(M)=\T(M)
.

(ii)

Let
\mz=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\mid\Ht\mathfrak{p}\geq n\}
for some integer
n\geq 1
. Then
N
satisfies
(S_{n})
if and only if
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(N)=0
for all
i<n
and
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\setminus\mz
.

(iii)

Assume
R
is local and Cohen-Macaulay,
\mz
is a specialization-closed subset of
\Spec R
, and
n\geq 1
is an integer. Assume further that
\Supp_{R}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr N,R))\subseteq\mz
for
1\leq i\leq n
(e.g.,
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(N)\setminus\mz
.) If
\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\geq n
, then
N
is
n
-torsion-free.

Proof.

Note that part (i) follows easily form the definition, and part (ii) follows from Proposition
2.16
. Hence we proceed to prove part (iii).

Set
t=\inf\{i\geq 1\mid\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr N,R)\neq 0\}
and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}(\Ext^{t}_{R}(\Tr N,R))
. Assume contrarily that
t\leq n
. Hence by our assumption
\mathfrak{p}\in\mz
. As
\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr N,R)_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for all
0<i<t
, we have by (
2.2
.7) that

\Omega^{-(i-1)}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}N_{\mathfrak{p}}\approx\Omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\Omega^{-i}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}N_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
0<i<t
. Hence, we obtain the following

(
2.19
.1)

N_{\mathfrak{p}}\approx\Omega^{t-1}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\Omega^{-(t-1)}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}N_{\mathfrak{p}}.

It follows from the inclusion
\Ext^{t}_{R}(\Tr N,R)\hookrightarrow\Omega^{-(t-1)}N
(see (
2.2
.6)) that
\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}(\Omega^{-(t-1)}N)
. Now it is easy to see that
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\Omega^{t-1}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\Omega^{-(t-1)}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}N_{\mathfrak{p}})=t-1
and so by (
2.19
.1)
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})=t-1
. This is a contradiction because Proposition
2.16
implies that
t\leq n\leq\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\leq\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

2.20
.

Tate (co)homology.
We call a (homologically indexed) complex
acyclic
if it has zero homology. An acyclic complex
T
of free
R
-modules is called
totally
acyclic
if the dual complex
\Hom_{R}(T,R)
is also acyclic. For
M\in\md(R)
, it follows that
M
is totally reflexive if and only if there is a totally acyclic complex
T
with
M\cong\coker(T_{1}\to T_{0})
.

A
complete resolution
of
M\in\md(R)
is a diagram
T\overset{\tau}{\to}P\overset{\simeq}{\to}M
, where
P\overset{\simeq}{\to}M
is a projective resolution,
T
is a totally acyclic complex of free
R
-modules, and
\tau_{i}
is an isomorphism for all
i\gg 0
. It is known that
M
has finite Gorenstein dimension if and only if it has a complete resolution
[
11
]
.

Suppose
M\in\md(R)
is equipped with a complete resolution
T\to P\to M
. For each
N\in\Md(R)
and for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
Tate (co)homology
of
M
and
N
is defined as:

\widehat{\Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N)=\hh_{i}(T\otimes_{R}N)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \widehat{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)=\hh^{i}(\Hom_{R}(T,N)).

In the following we catalog some basic properties of Tate (co)homology; for details, please see
[
8
,
11
,
26
]
.

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
M\in\md(R)
,
N\in\Md(R)
, and assume
\G-dim_{R}(M)<\infty
.

(i)

If
\pd_{R}(M)<\infty
, then
\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0=\widehat{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

If
\G-dim_{R}(M)=0
, i.e., if
M
is totally reflexive, then
\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{-i-1}_{R}(M^{*},N)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

\widehat{\Tor}^{R}_{i+n}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}^{R}_{i}(\Omega^{n}M,N)
and
\widehat{\Ext}^{i+n}_{R}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(\Omega^{n}M,N)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and for all
n\geq 0
.

(iv)

\widehat{\Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,N)\cong\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)
and
\widehat{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,N)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N)
for all
i>\G-dim_{R}(M).

Next is a property for the vanishing of Tate homology for modules of finite complete intersection dimension:

Theorem 2.22
.

(
[
8
, 4.9]
) Let
M\in\md(R)
and let
N\in\Md(R)
. Assume
\CI_{R}(M)<\infty
. Then,

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0\ \text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\Longleftrightarrow\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0\ \text{ for all }i\ll 0\Longleftrightarrow\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0\ \text{ for all }i\gg 0.

for all

. Then the following hold:

(i)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\Ext^{i+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)
for all
i=0,\ldots,n-1
.

(ii)

There is an injection

\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\hookrightarrow\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence (
2.2
.2):

(
3.5
.1)

0\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)\rightarrow\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow 0.

By applying the functor
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
to the 4-term exact sequence (
3.5
.1)
and noting that
\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)
is torsion with respect to
\mz
we get the injection

\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)
. Hence, from now on we may assume that
n>0
. The exact sequence (
3.5
.1) induces the following short exact sequences:

(
3.5
.2)

0\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0,

(
3.5
.3)

0\rightarrow X\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)\rightarrow\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\rightarrow 0.

As
\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N)
is torsion with respect to
\mz
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, by Theorem
2.11
(ii) we have

(
3.5
.4)

\Gamma_{\mz}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N))=\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\text{ and }\hh^{j}_{\mz}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N))=0\text{ for all }j>0\text{ and }1\leq i\leq n.

By Lemma
2.17
,
\gr_{R}(\mz,\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\geq\min\{2,\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\}>0
.
It follows from the exact sequence (
3.5
.3) that
\gr_{R}(\mz,X)>0
. In other words,
\Gamma_{\mz}(X)=0
. Applying the functor
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
to the exact sequences (
3.5
.2), (
3.5
.3) and using (
3.5
.4) we get the following isomorphisms and exact sequence:

(
3.5
.5)

\Gamma_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\Gamma_{\mz}(\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N))=\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\ \text{ and }\ \hh^{1}_{\mz}(X)\cong\hh^{1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N),

(
3.5
.6)

\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N)=\Gamma_{\mz}(\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N))\hookrightarrow\hh^{1}_{\mz}(X)\rightarrow\hh^{1}_{\mz}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N)),

(
3.5
.7)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\hh^{i}_{\mz}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\text{ for all }i>1.

The case
n=1
is clear by (
3.5
.5) and (
3.5
.6).
Let
n>1
. Therefore,
\gr_{R}(\mz,\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\geq 2
and so
\hh^{1}_{\mz}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))=0
. It follows from (
3.5
.5) and (
3.5
.6) that

(
3.5
.8)

\hh^{1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,N).

Note that
M^{*}\approx\Omega^{2}\Tr M
. Hence, we have the following exact sequence

(
3.5
.9)

\Ext^{3}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\cong\Ext^{1}_{R}(M^{*},N)\hookrightarrow\hh^{2}_{\mz}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\cong\hh^{2}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N),

where the injection follows from Lemma
3.2
and the last isomorphism follows from (
3.5
.7). Now the assertion is clear by (
3.5
.5), (
3.5
.8) and (
3.5
.9) for the case
n=2
. Finally, let
n>2
. By (
3.5
.7) and Lemma
3.2
, we get the following isomorphisms:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)&\cong\hh_{\mz}^{i}(\Hom_{R}(M^{*},N))\\
&\cong\Ext^{i-1}_{R}(M^{*},N)\\
&\cong\Ext^{i+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\end{array}

for all
1<i<n
and also we obtain an injection
\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\hookrightarrow\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)
as desired.
∎

The next result is used throughout the paper.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
M,N\in\md(R)
, and let
\X\subseteq\Spec R
. Assume
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\X
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\X
.

(ii)

\Supp_{R}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N))\bigcup\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))\subseteq\Spec R\setminus\X
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\X
.
Note that
M^{*}\approx\Omega^{2}\Tr M
. We apply this along with parts (ii),(iii) and (iv) of Theorem
2.21
to deduce, for all
i\leq 0
, that

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{-i-1}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M^{*}_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{-i+1}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\Tr_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\Ext^{-i+1}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\Tr_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Also, since
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
we know
\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\Tor_{j}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})

for all
j\geq 1
. Hence the assertion follows.
∎

Recall that
M\in\Md(R)
is called
Tor-rigid
provided that the vanishing of a single
\Tor^{R}_{j}(M,N)
for some
N\in\Md(R)
and for some
j\geq 1
forces the vanishing of
\Tor^{R}_{i}(M,N)
for all
i\geq j
. Clearly, over a local ring, every syzygy module of the residue field is Tor-rigid. Note that, it follows from Theorem
3.1
that each finitely generated module over a regular local ring is Tor-rigid. For more examples of Tor-rigid modules, see, for example,
[
30
]
.

The following is the key for our proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume
R
is local,
M,N\in\md(R)
, and
N
is Tor-rigid. Assume further, for a specialization-closed subset
\mz\subseteq\Spec R
, that the following conditions hold:

(i)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a totally reflexive
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
(e.g.,
\NF(M)\subseteq\mz
).

(ii)

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\setminus\mz
and for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
(e.g.,
\NF(M)\subseteq\mz
).

(iii)

\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for some
n
, where
0\leq n\leq\gr_{R}(\mz,N)
.

Then the following hold:

(a)

\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0
. Moreover, if
\gr_{R}(\mz,R)\geq n+1
, then
\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M)=0
.

(b)

\Tor_{j}^{R}(\Omega^{-n}M,N)=0
for all
j\geq 1
.

(c)

If
n\geq 1
, then
\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\otimes_{R}N
. Therefore, if
n\leq\gr_{R}(\mz,R)
, then
\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M)\otimes_{R}N
.

Proof.

We prove the statements simultaneously.

Note that by Lemma
3.6
,
\Supp_{R}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N))\subseteq\mz
for all
i>0
. It follows from Lemma
3.5
and assumption (iii) that
\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)=0
.
By (
2.2
.5), there exists the following exact sequence

(
3.7
.1)

\Tor_{2}^{R}(\Omega^{-(n+1)}M,N)\rightarrow\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,N)\twoheadrightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(\Omega^{-(n+1)}M,N).

It follows from (
3.7
.1) that
\Tor_{1}^{R}(\Omega^{-(n+1)}M,N)=0
. As
N
is Tor-rigid, we have

(
3.7
.2)

\Tor_{j}^{R}(\Omega^{-(n+1)}M,N)=0\text{ for all }j\geq 1.

Again, by the exact sequence (
3.7
.1), we get that
\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\otimes_{R}N=0
which implies that
\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0
. Note that by assumption (i) we have
\Supp_{R}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,R))\subseteq\mz
for all
i\geq 1
; see
[
5
, 4.9]
. If
\gr_{R}(\mz,R)\geq n+1
, then
\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M)\cong\Ext^{n+1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0
by Lemma
3.5
and so the assertion (a) follows.

It follows from part (a) and (
2.2
.7) that
\Omega^{-n}M\approx\Omega\Omega^{-(n+1)}M
. Therefore, by (
3.7
.2), we get
\Tor_{j}^{R}(\Omega^{-n}M,N)=0
for all
j>0
which proves part (b).

Now assume that
n>0
. By part (b) and (
3.7
.1), we get the following isomorphism:

(
3.7
.3)

\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\otimes_{R}N\cong\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,N).

Now the assertion (c) follows from (
3.7
.3) and Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Now we are ready to state and prove the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
R
is local,
M,N\in\md(R)
, and
N
is Tor-rigid. Assume further, for an integer
n\geq 0
and a specialization-closed subset
\mz\subseteq\Spec R
, that the following conditions hold:

(i)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
.

(ii)

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec R\setminus\mz
.

(iii)

\gr_{R}(\mz,M)\geq n
and
\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\geq n
.

(iv)

\hh_{\mz}^{n}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
.

If, either
n\leq\gr_{R}(\mz,R)
, or
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for all
i\leq n
, and
M
and
N
are Tor-independent, i.e.,
\Tor_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the integer
n
.

If
n=0
, then the required result follows from the assumption
\hh_{\mz}^{n}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
and Lemma
3.7
(b). Hence we assume
n\geq 1
. Note that it suffices to show
\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
; as then the induction hypothesis implies
\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for all
i\leq n-1
and
\Tor_{j}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
j\geq 1
, as required.

First suppose
n\leq\gr_{R}(\mz,R)
. Then, by Lemma
3.7
(c), we have the following isomorphism:

(
3.8
.1)

\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M)\otimes_{R}N.

As
\gr_{R}(\mz,M)\geq n
, we conclude from (
3.8
.1) that
\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
; see
2.15
.

Next suppose
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. Then it follows from Lemma
3.7
(c) that the following holds:

(
3.8
.2)

\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\otimes_{R}N.

As
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
and
\gr_{R}(\mz,M)\geq n
, Proposition
2.19
(iii) shows that
M
is
n
-torsion-free. Thus
\Ext^{n}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0
and hence (
3.8
.2) implies
\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
; this completes the proof.
∎

The following example shows that the Tor-rigidity assumption in Theorem
3.8
is necessary:

Example 3.9
.

Let
R=k[\![x,y,u,v]\!]/(xy-uv)
,
M=(x,u)
,
N=M^{\ast}
, and
\mz=\V(\mathfrak{m})
. It has been established in
[
36
, 1.8]
that

M
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module which is locally free on the punctured spectrum of
R
. Also, one can check that there is an isomorphism
M\otimes_{R}N\simeq\mathfrak{m}
.

We set
n=2
. Then all of the hypotheses of Theorem
3.8
, except Tor-rigidity, hold:
\Tor^{R}_{2}(M,N)=0
and
\Tor^{R}_{3}(M,N)\simeq\Tor^{R}_{1}(M,N)\simeq k\neq 0
.
The desired claim is not true because
\hh_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(M\otimes_{R}N)\simeq\hh_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\neq 0
.

Our next aim is to show that Theorem
3.8
yields a new proof for Theorem
3.1
; first we note:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M,N\in\md(R)
and let
\X\subseteq\Spec R
be a subset. Assume
\CI_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\X.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\X
.

(ii)

\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))\subseteq\Spec R\setminus\X
for all
i\gg 0
.

(iii)

\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))\subseteq\Spec R\setminus\X
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

The equivalence of parts (i) and (ii) is due to Theorem
2.22
. Moreover, the equivalence of parts (ii) and (iii) follows from (
2.3
.1) and the dependency formula
2.7
.
∎

Theorem
1.1
, advertised in the introduction, is a special case of the following proposition: thanks to Theorem
3.8
, it can now be easily established.

Proposition 3.11
.

Assume
R
is local and a complete intersection,
M,N\in\md(R)
are nonzero, and
\mz\subset\Spec R
is a specialization-closed subset. Assume further
N
is Tor-rigid and, for an integer
n\geq 0
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
.

(ii)

\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))\subseteq\mz
for all
i\gg 0
.

(iii)

\hh_{\mz}^{n}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
.

(iv)

\gr_{R}(\mz,M)\geq n
and
\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\geq n
.

Then
\hh_{\mz}^{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for all
i\leq n
, and
M
and
N
are Tor-independent.

Proof.

Note, by (
2.3
.1) and assumption (i), we have that
\CI_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
.
Thus, letting
\X=\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
, we see that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive over
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M)\setminus\mz
. Moreover, Lemma
3.10
yields the vanishing of
\Tor_{i}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\X
. So the assertion is clear by Theorem
3.8
.
∎

We can now make use of Proposition
3.11
, which is a vast generalization of Theorem

, and prove:

Theorem 3.12
.

(Auslander and Lichtenbaum) Assume

is local and regular, and let
M,N\in\md(R)
. If

is nonzero and torsion-free, then
M
and

are torsion-free and Tor-independent.

Recall that finitely generated modules are Tor-rigid over regular local rings
[

,

]
.
Set
\mz:=\Spec(R)\setminus\{0\}
. By Proposition

,

for every

. Now the assertion follows from Lemma

(a), (b) and Proposition

.
∎

4.
Applications of the main theorem

In this section we give several examples, and applications of the results we obtain from Section 3. The first result, Corollary
4.4
, we aim to prove is an application of Theorem
3.8
.

When
R
is local, we denote by
\md_{0}(R)
, the subcategory of finitely generated
R
-modules which are locally free on the punctured spectrum of
R
, namely

\md_{0}(R)=\{M\in\md(R)\mid M_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is free over }R_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ for each }\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume
R
is local such that
\depth(R)\geq 1
, and assume
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
satisfying the following condition:

\text{ If }X\in\md(R)\text{ and }\Tor^{R}_{t}(X,R/I)=0\text{ for some integer }t\geq 0,\text{ then it follows that }\pd_{R}(X)<t.

Then
\hh^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}I)\neq 0
for each
M\in\md_{0}(R)
such that
0\neq M
and
\depth_{R}(M)\geq 1
.

Proof.

We assume contrary that
\hh^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}I)=0
, and seek a contradiction.

Letting
N=I
, we obtain from Theorem
3.8
that
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,I)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, and
\hh^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}I)=0
, i.e.,
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}I)\geq 2
.
Now it follows by our assumption on the ideal
I
that
\pd_{R}(M)<\infty
. Therefore Theorem
2.6
yields:

(
4.1
.1)

\pd_{R}(M)=\depth R-\depth_{R}(M)=\depth_{R}(I)-\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}I).

As
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary, we have that
\depth_{R}(I)=1
. Thus (
4.1
) shows
\pd_{R}(M)<0
, or equivalently,
M=0
. This contradiction shows that
\hh^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}I)\neq 0
.
∎

Next we recall a class of ideals that enjoys the rigidity property stated in Proposition
4.1
:

4.2
.

(
[
19
, 2.10]
) Assume
R
is local, and let
I
and
J
be ideals of
R
. Assume
\depth(R)\geq 1
, and the following conditions hold:

(i)

(I:_{R}J)=(\mathfrak{m}I:_{R}\mathfrak{m}J)
.

(ii)

I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary and
0\neq I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}J
.

If
\Tor^{R}_{t}(M,R/I)=0
for some
M\in\md(R)
and some integer
t\geq 0
, then it follows that
\pd_{R}(M)<t
.

When
R
is local ring and
\depth(R)\geq 1
, an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) of
4.2
for the case where
J=R
; see
[
19
, the paragraph following 2.1 and 2.4]
,
[
17
, 2.1 and 2.10]
and also
[
27
, 3.3]
. On the other hand, if

is as in

, then it does not need to be integrally closed, in general; see
[

, 2.7 and 2.8]
and
[
19
, 4.3]
. Although it is difficult to check whether or not a given ideal is integrally closed, in view of

, one can easily construct examples of ideals that have the rigidity property stated in Proposition

:

.

Assume
R
is local and

, and let

be an

such that

and

.

As a consequence of Proposition
4.1
and Remark

.

Assume

is local and
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of

. Set

. If
\depth(R)\geq 1
and

, then
\hh^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(J\otimes_{R}J)\neq 0
.

When the module considered in Proposition
4.1
has depth at least two, we can say more about
\hh^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}I)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume
R
is local,
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
, and
M\in\md(R)
is such that
\depth_{R}(M)\geq 2
. Then it follows

\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}I)\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I)&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if }\ \ i=0\\
M/IM&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if }\ \ i=1\\
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if }\ \ i>1\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Applying
-\otimes M
to the exact sequence
0\to I\to R\to R/I\to 0
, we get the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I)\longrightarrow I\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow R/I\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow 0.

We break it down into the following exact sequences:

(a):\ 0\to\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I)\to I\otimes_{R}M\to X\to 0,\ \ (b):\ 0\to X\to M\to R/I\otimes_{R}M\to 0.

As
\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I)
has finite length,
\hh^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I))=\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I)
and
\hh^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,R/I))=0
for all
j>0
. It follows from the exact sequence
(b)
that
\depth(X)>0
. The exact sequence
(a)
induces the isomorphisms

Proposition 4.7

Assume

Theorem 4.9

Assume

Proof.

(i). As

(ii). Set

(iii). This is an immediate consequence of parts (i) and (ii).

5.
On the depth of tensor powers of modules

Auslander studied the torsion-freeness of tensor powers of modules and obtained a criteria for freeness. More precisely, over an unramified regular local ring
R
of dimension
d
, Auslander
[
4
]
proved that an
R
-module
M
is free provided that the
d
-fold tensor product
M^{\otimes d}
of
M
is torsion-free. The aim of this section is to generalize Auslander’s result over more general rings, and find some new criteria for freeness of modules in terms of the local cohomology; see Corollary
5.4
.

We call
M\in\Md(R)
a
self-test
module provided that the following condition holds:

\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,M)=0\text{ for all }i\gg 0\Longleftrightarrow\pd_{R}(M)<\infty.

It is an open question whether or not each module is self-test over local rings. However, it is known that, if
R
is a complete intersection or Golod, then each module in
\md(R)
is self-test; see
[
8
, Theorem IV]
and
[
38
, 3.6]
. We refer the reader to
[
23
]
for further details about the self-test modules.

The next theorem is the main result of this section; to the best of our knowledge, it is new, even if the ring in question is regular.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
R
is local,
\mz\subset\Spec R
is a specialization-closed subset, and
M\in\md(R)
is nonzero and Tor-rigid such that
\NF(M)\subseteq\mz
.

(i)

If
\gr_{R}(\mz,M)<\gr_{R}(\mz,R)
, then
\hh^{\gr_{R}(\mz,M)}_{\mz}(\otimes_{R}^{n}M)\neq 0
for all
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

Assume, for some
n\geq 2
, and for some
i
, where
i<\min\{\gr_{R}(\mz,M),\gr_{R}(\mz,R)\}
, that we have
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(\otimes_{R}^{n}M)=0
. Then:

(a)

\Tor_{k}^{R}(M,M)=0
for all
k\geq 1
, and
\hh^{j}_{\mz}(\otimes_{R}^{m}M)=0
for all
m=1,\ldots,n
and for all
j=0,\ldots,i
.

(b)

If
M
is self-test, then
\pd_{R}(M)<(\depth R-i)/n
and also
\dim_{R}(\NF(M))<\dim R-n-i
.

Proof.

We prove the statements simultaneously. For each
j\geq 1
, set
M_{j}:=\overset{j}{\otimes}M
. Assume
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(\overset{n}{\otimes}M)=0
for some
i\leq\gr_{R}(\mz,M)
and
n\geq 2
. If

, then by Lemma
3.7
(a), we get
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M_{n-1})=0
. By repeating this argument, inductively we get the following:

(
5.1
.1)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M_{k})=0\text{ for all }1\leq k\leq n.

In particular,
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M)=0
. Hence,
i<\gr_{R}(\mz,M)
. This prove part (i).
Next we claim the following:

Claim:

\gr_{R}(\mz,M_{j+1})>i
and
\Tor_{k}^{R}(M_{j},M)=0
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
k>0
.

Proof of Claim. We argue by induction on
j
. By (
5.1
.1),
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}M)=0
. It follows from Theorem
3.8
that
\Tor_{k}^{R}(M,M)=0
for

and
\gr_{R}(\mz,M_{2})>i
. Hence the case
j=1
follows. Now assume
j>1
. By induction hypothesis,
\gr_{R}(\mz,M_{j})>i
and
\Tor_{k}^{R}(M_{j-1},M)=0
for all
k>0
. By (
5.1
.1),
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M_{j}\otimes_{R}M)=0
. It follows from Theorem
3.8
that
\Tor_{k}^{R}(M_{j},M)=0
for all
k>0
and
\gr_{R}(\mz,M_{j+1})>i
. Thus, the proof of part (a), as well as that of the claim is completed.

Now assume
M
is self-test. By the Claim
\Tor_{k}^{R}(M_{j},M)=0
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
k>0
. Hence,
\pd_{R}(M_{j})<\infty
for all

and so by
[
3
, 1.3]
we get the following equality:

(

\pd_{R}(M_{j+1})=\pd_{R}(M)+\pd_{R}(M_{j})\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq n-1.

It follows from Auslander–Buchsbaum formula and (

.2) that

\depth R-\depth_{R}(M_{n})=\pd_{R}(M_{n})=n.\pd_{R}(M).

By the claim,
\gr_{R}(\mz,M_{n})>i
. In particular, by Proposition
2.16
,
\depth_{R}(M_{n})>i
. Therefore,
\pd_{R}(M)<(\depth R-i)/n
.

Let
\dim_{R}(\NF(M))=t=\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\NF(M)
. By the Claim
\Tor_{k}^{R}(M_{j},M)_{\mathfrak{p}}=0

Therefore, we obtain the following equality:

(
5.1
.3)

n.\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\pd_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((M_{n})_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Assume contrary that

(

By the Claim and Proposition

.4) we have

(

It follows from (

Theorem 6.1

If

is a

-dimensional complete intersection such that

(R_{n-1})

It suffices to prove that we may assume

We consider the local ring extension

Let

There are two spectral sequences converging to the same point:

7.
A relation between the local cohomology and the Tate homology

In this section, we determine a new relation between the local cohomology of tensor products of modules and the Tate homology over Gorenstein rings. Our main result is Theorem
7.1
, which will be a new tool in the study of the depth of tensor products of modules. To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
7.1
is new, even if the specialization-closed subset
\mz
considered is a closed subset of
\Spec(R)
.

Theorem
7.1
has various applications that contribute to the literature; we state and prove these applications following the proof of the theorem in this section. Several of the applications we give should be of independent interest. For example, Theorem
7.1
improves a result that has been initially proved by Dao
[
29
, 7.7]
, and subsequently studied by Celikbas
[
16
, 3.4]
and Celikbas, Iyengar, Piepmeyer and Wiegand
[
20
, 3.14]
; see Corollary
7.4
.

Recall that
R
denotes a commutative Noetherian ring throughout.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mz\subset\Spec R
be a specialization-closed subset and let
M,N\in\md(R)
. Assume
\G-dim_{R}(M)<\infty
. Assume further, for an integer
n\geq 0
, the following hold:

(i)

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}+n
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mz
.

(ii)

\Supp_{R}\big(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\big)\bigcap\Supp_{R}\big(\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R}(M,N)\big)\subseteq\mz
for all
i\geq 1
and for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
(e.g.,
\NF(M)\subseteq\mz
).

Then it follows:

(a)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{-i}^{R}(M,N)
for each
i=0,\ldots,n-1
.

(b)

There is an injection
\widehat{\Tor}_{-n}^{R}(M,N)\hookrightarrow\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{W}:=\Supp_{R}(M)
and
\mz^{\prime}:=\mz\cap\mathcal{W}
. Clearly,
\mz^{\prime}
is specialization-closed. First we prove:

Claim I.

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\hh^{i}_{\mz^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{R}N)
for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof of Claim. Set
L=M\otimes_{R}N
and let
0\to L\to\E^{0}(L)\to\E^{1}(L)\to\cdots
be the minimal injective resolution of
X
. Note that
\Supp_{R}(\E^{i}(L))\subseteq\Supp_{R}(L)\subseteq\mathcal{W}
for all
i\geq 0
. Therefore,
\Gamma_{\mathcal{W}}(L)=L
and also
\Gamma_{\mathcal{W}}(\E^{i}(L))=\E^{i}(L)
for all
i\geq 0
.
It follows from Theorem
2.11
(iv) that
\Gamma_{\mz^{\prime}}(L)=\Gamma_{\mz}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{W}}(L))=\Gamma_{\mz}(L)
. Similarly,

\Gamma_{\mz^{\prime}}(\E^{i}(L))=\Gamma_{\mz}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{W}}(\E^{i}(L)))=\Gamma_{\mz}(\E^{i}(L))
for all
i\geq 0
which implies that
\hh^{i}_{\mz}(L)\cong\hh^{i}_{\mz^{\prime}}(L)
for all
i\geq 0
. Thus, the proof of the claim is completed.

By Claim I, without loss of generality, by replacing
\mz
with
\mz^{\prime}
, we may assume that
\mz\subseteq\Supp_{R}(M)
. Consider the following exact sequence

(
7.1
.1)

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow X\rightarrow G\rightarrow 0

where
\pd_{R}(X)<\infty
and
\G-dim_{R}(G)=0
(see
[
25
, 2.17]
).
By
[
26
, 2.9]
, the exact sequence (
7.1
.1) induces a
doubly infinite long exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow\widehat{\Tor}_{j+1}^{R}(G,N)\rightarrow\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R}(M,N)\rightarrow\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R}(X,N)\rightarrow\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R}(G,N)\rightarrow\cdots,

of stable homology modules. Also by Theorem
2.21
(i),
\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R}(X,N)=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence we get the following isomorphism

(
7.1
.2)

\widehat{\Tor}_{j+1}^{R}(G,N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{j}^{R}(M,N)\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{Z}.

As
G
is totally reflexive, by Theorem
2.21
(iv), (
7.1
.2) and assumption (ii), we have

(
7.1
.3)

\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(G,N))=\Supp_{R}(\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(G,N))=\Supp_{R}(\widehat{\Tor}_{i-1}^{R}(M,N))\subseteq\mz\text{ for all }i>0.

Applying the functor
-\otimes_{R}N
to the exact sequence (
7.1
.1), we get the following exact sequence

(
7.1
.4)

\cdots\rightarrow\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\to\Tor_{i}^{R}(X,N)\to\Tor_{i}^{R}(G,N)\rightarrow\cdots.

In view of assumption (ii), (
7.1
.3) and (
7.1
.4) we have

(
7.1
.5)

\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(X,N))\subseteq\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))\cup\Supp_{R}(\Tor_{i}^{R}(G,N))\subseteq\mz\text{ for all }i>0.

On the other hand, by the exact sequence (
7.1
.1) and Auslander–Bridger formula, we see that
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(X_{\mathfrak{p}})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mz\subseteq\Supp_{R}(M)
. Therefore, by assumption (i)

(
7.1
.6)

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(X_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}+n\quad\forall\mathfrak{p}\in\mz.

Next we claim the following.

Claim II.

\Tor_{i}^{R}(X,N)=0
for all
i>0
.

Proof of claim. Set
t=\sup\{j\mid\Tor_{j}^{R}(X,N))\neq 0\}
. Assume contrarily that
t>0
and that
\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}(\Tor_{t}^{R}(X,N))
. By (
7.1
.5),
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mz
. As
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\Tor_{t}^{R}(X,N)_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
, by
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
we have the equality

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(X_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})=\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}-t,

which is a contradiction by (
7.1
.6). Therefore
t=0
and the proof of the claim is completed.

By Claim II, the long exact sequence (
7.1
.4) induces the following exact sequence

(
7.1
.7)

0\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow X\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow G\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow 0.

Applying the functor
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
to the exact sequence (
7.1
.7), we obtain the following injection:

(
7.1
.8)

\hh^{0}_{\mz}(\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N))\hookrightarrow\hh^{0}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N).

Note by (
7.1
.3) that
\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N)
is torsion with respect to
\mz
. Hence, by (
7.1
.2), Theorem
2.21
(iv) and (

.8) we get the following injection:

\widehat{\Tor}_{0}^{R}(M,N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{1}^{R}(G,N)\cong\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N)\cong\hh^{0}_{\mz}(\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N))\hookrightarrow\hh^{0}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N).

Therefore, from now on we may assume that
n>0
. By Theorem
2.6
and Claim II,

(
7.1
.9)

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(X_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})=\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((X\otimes_{R}N)_{\mathfrak{p}})\quad\forall\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(X\otimes_{R}N).

In view of (
7.1
.6) and (
7.1
.9), we have

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((X\otimes_{R}N)_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq n
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mz
. By Proposition
2.16

(
7.1
.10)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(X\otimes_{R}N)=0\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq n-1.

Consider the following two exact sequences, induce from (
7.1
.7):

(
7.1
.11)

0\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}N\to Y\to 0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ 0\to Y\rightarrow X\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow G\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow 0.

Applying the functor
\Gamma_{\mz}(-)
to the above exact sequences and using Theorem

(ii), (iii), (

.10) and (

.2), one can easily obtain the following isomorphisms:

(

.12)

\hh^{0}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\hh^{0}_{\mz}(\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N))\cong\Tor_{1}^{R}(G,N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{1}^{R}(G,N)\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{0}^{R}(M,N),

(
7.1
.13)

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\hh^{i}_{\mz}(Y)\cong\hh^{i-1}_{\mz}(G\otimes_{R}N)\text{ for all }0<i<n.

Also, we have the following injection

(
7.1
.14)

\hh^{n-1}_{\mz}(G\otimes_{R}N)\hookrightarrow\hh^{n}_{\mz}(Y)\cong\hh^{n}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N).

Note by (
7.1
.2) and assumption (ii) that
\Supp_{R}(\widehat{\Tor}^{R}_{i}(G,N))\subseteq\mz
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. As
G
is totally reflexive, by Lemma

we have
\Supp_{R}(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr G,N))\subseteq\mz
for all

. As

has finite Gorenstein dimension, by assumption (i) and Auslander–Bridger formula, we have
\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\G-dim_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+n\geq n
for all

. Therefore, by Proposition

we conclude that
\gr_{R}(\mz,N)\geq n
. Since
G
is totally reflexive, so is

by
[

.
Hence, by using Theorem
2.21
and Lemma

and noting that

\begin{array}[]{rl}\hh^{j-1}_{\mz}(G\otimes_{R}N)&\cong\Ext^{j}_{R}(\Tr G,N)\\
&\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{j}_{R}(\Tr G,N)\\
&\cong\widehat{\Ext}^{j-2}_{R}(G^{*},N)\\
&\cong\widehat{\Tor}_{-j+1}^{R}(G,N),\end{array}

for all

.12), (

.13), (

.15) and (

.2).
The second assertion follows from (

.2).

Corollary 7.2
.

Assume

is a local complete intersection and let

be a specialization-closed. Let

, and let

and

be integers such that

and

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}+n

\hh^{i}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for all

for all

Then it follows
\gr_{R}(\mz,M\otimes_{R}N)\geq n+1
, and
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all

Proof.

We first note, by Theorem

and assumption (iii), that we have
\Supp_{R}(\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,N))\subseteq\mz
for all

for all

for all

(see for example
[

\widehat{\Tor}_{i}^{R}(Y,N)=0
for all

. Equivalently, it follows that

.1).

Corollary 7.5
.

Assume
R
is a local complete intersection. Assume further
\NF(M)\cap\NF(N)\subseteq\{\mathfrak{m}\}
for some
M,N\in\md(R)
. If
\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)-\depth R\geq\cx_{R}(M,N)
, then it follows that
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R\leq\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

Set
c=\cx_{R}(M,N)+1
and
n=\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)-\depth R
. Then we have
n\geq c-1
. Assume contrarily that
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)>n
. Therefore,
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for
n-c+1\leq i\leq n
. By Corollary
7.2
, it follows
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
. In view of Theorem
2.6
, we have
\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)=\depth R+\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N).
Thus,
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=n
which is a contradiction.
∎

Example 7.6
.

The first item says that the bound
\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)+\depth R\leq\depth_{R}(M)+\depth_{R}(N)
presented in Corollary

can be achieved.
The second item shows that the locally free assumption is necessary in Corollary

(i)

Let

and let

. Then it follows

(ii)

Let

Recall that

Corollary 7.7
.

Assume

Corollary 7.8
.

Assume

is a Gorenstein local ring, and let

Assume that

\widehat{\Tor}_{-(n-1)}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0

∎

Corollary 7.9
.

Assume

is a

-dimensional hypersurface that has an isolated singularity, where

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0

It follows that

; see

, 4.7]
. Therefore, since

8.
Splitting criteria for vector bundles

In this section, we are concerned with some splitting criteria for vector bundles on schemes. Recall that a vector bundle is called
trivial
if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of line bundles. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on the projective scheme
X
equipped with a very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
. The dual of
\mathcal{E}
is denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{*}:=\mhom(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{X})
. We say that
\mathcal{E}
has the
cohomological rigidity property
provided that the the following condition holds:

{}\text{If }\hh^{i}_{\ast}(X,\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}}^{\ast}):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hh^{i}(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\ast}(n))=0\text{ for some }i\geq 1\Longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\text{ is trivial. }

The cohomological rigidity property, considered as above, was initially studied by Kempf
[
45
]
and Luk and Yau
[
43
]
by using different approaches. A commutative algebra interpretation of the cohomological rigidity property has been examined by Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
]
. More precisely, given a reflexive module
M
over a hypersurface ring
R
, Huneke and Wiegand determined new criteria for the freeness of the module
M
in terms of the vanishing of
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})
for some
i
. Furthermore, Huneke and Wiegand generalized the aforementioned result of Luk and Yau via the machinery of commutative algebra.

Our work in this section is motivated by the work of Huneke and Wiegand
[
36
]
. The main purpose of this section is to obtain some new criteria for the freeness of modules in terms of the vanishing of local cohomology. Along the way, as an application, we obtain a new splitting criteria - for an arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay vector bundle on a smooth complete intersection of an odd dimension – in terms of the cohomological rigidity property; see Corollary
8.15
.

We start by the following result which is an extension of a result of Huneke and Wiegand; see
[
36
, 4.1(1)]
, and also
2.11
for the terminology.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
\mz\subset\Spec R
be a specialization-closed subset where
\gr_{R}(\mz,R)\geq 1
. Assume
M\in\md(R)
is such that
\NF(M)\subseteq\mz
. If
\hh^{1}_{\mz}(M\otimes_{R}M^{*})=0
, then
M=F\oplus T
, where
F
is free and
T
is
\mz
-torsion. In particular, if
M
is torsion-free with respect to
\mz
, then
M
is free.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.17
and our assumption,
\gr_{R}(\mz,M^{*})\geq\min\{2,\gr_{R}(\mz,R)\}\geq 1
. Hence, by using Lemma
3.5
(ii) we conclude that

(
8.1
.1)

\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,M^{*})=0.

It follows from (
8.1
.1) and (
2.2
.5) that
\Tor_{1}^{R}(\Omega^{-2}M,M^{*})=0
. Note that
\Omega^{-2}M\approx\Tr\Omega^{2}\Tr M\approx\Tr M^{*}
(see
2.2
). Therefore,
\Tor_{1}^{R}(\Tr M^{*},M^{*})=0
. In other words, by (
2.2
.4), the natural map
M^{\ast}\otimes M^{\ast\ast}\to\Hom(M^{\ast},M^{\ast})
is surjective. In view of
[
6
, A.1]
, we conclude that
M^{*}
is free. It follows from (
8.1
.1) that
\Ext^{2}_{R}(\Tr M,R)=0
.
Therefore, by (
2.2
.3), the natural map
e_{M}:M\to M^{**}
is surjective and we get the following exact sequence
0\rightarrow\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{**}\rightarrow 0
. As
M^{**}
is free, the above exact sequence splits. Hence,
M\cong F\oplus T
, where
T=\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr M,R)
which is
\mz
-torsion and
F:=M^{**}
.
∎

Theorem 8.25

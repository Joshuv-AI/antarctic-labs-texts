Foliated and compactly supported isotopies of regular neighborhoods
Oleksandra Khokhliuk, Sergiy Maksymenko

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a foliation with a ‘‘singular’’ submanifold B B on a smooth manifold M M and p : E → B p:E\to B be a regular neighborhood of B B in M M . Under certain ‘‘homogeneity’’ assumptions on ℱ \mathcal{F} near B B we prove that every leaf preserving diffeomorphism h h of M M is isotopic via a leaf preserving isotopy to a diffeomorphism which coincides with some vector bundle morphism of E E near B B . This result is mutually a foliated and compactly supported variant of a well known statement that every diffeomorphism h h of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} fixing the origin is isotopic to the linear isomorphism induced by its Jacobi matrix of h h at 0 0 . We also present applications to the computations of the homotopy type of the group of leaf preserving diffeomorphisms of ℱ \mathcal{F} .

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a smooth
n
-manifold and
{B}
be a submanifold whose connected components may have distinct dimensions.
Then a
singular foliation on
M
of dimension
k
and a singular set
{B}
is a partition
\mathcal{F}
of
M
such that every connected component of
{B}
is an element of
\mathcal{F}
, and the induced partition of
M\setminus{B}
is a
k
-dimension foliation in a usual sense, e.g.
[
7
]
.

Denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
the group of diffeomorphisms of
M
leaving invariant each leaf of
\mathcal{F}
, and by
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})
, resp.
\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})
, its subgroup consisting of diffeomorphisms fixed on
{B}
, resp. on some neighborhood of
{B}
.
The paper is motivated by study of the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
.

Notice that we have a natural group homomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{D}({B})
associating to each
h\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
its restriction to
{B}
, that is
\rho(h)=h|_{{B}}
.
Evidently,
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})
is the kernel of
\rho
.

The present authors shown in
[
18
]
that if
\mathcal{F}
is of codimension
1
and its singularities are of Morse-Bott type, then
\rho
is a locally trivial fibration over its image.
For instance, this holds when there exists a Morse-Bott function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
on some neighborhood

of
{B}
such that
\mathcal{F}
in

consists of connected components of level sets of
f
and
{B}
is a union of all critical submanifolds of
f
.

That statement can be regarded as a foliated analogue of well known results by J. Cerf
[
8
]
, R. Palais
[
30
]
and E. Lima
[
22
]
on local triviality of the restriction maps for embeddings.

In particular, one gets a long exact sequence of homotopy groups of the fibration
\rho
:

(1.1)

\cdots\ \to\ \pi_{i+1}\mathcal{D}({B})\ \to\ \pi_{i}\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})\ \to\ \pi_{i}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\ \to\ \pi_{i}\mathcal{D}({B})\ \to\ \cdots,

and thus can reduce the computation of the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
to two more simple problems of computing the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}({B})
and
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})
.
This is especially useful if the dimension of
{B}
is
1
or
2
since in those cases the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}({B})
is explicitly computed,
[
33
,
9
,
11
,
10
,
15
]
.

In the present paper we make further step in studying the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
and obtain certain information about
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})
.
Let
{p}:{E}\to{B}
be a structure of a vector bundle on some regular neighborhood
{E}
of
{B}
in
M
, and
\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
the group of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
vector bundle self-isomorphisms

over
\mathrm{id}_{{B}}
, i.e. satisfying
{p}=h\circ{p}
.
We will prove that, under certain

assumptions on
\mathcal{F}
near
{B}
, the group
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})
can be deformed into a subgroup
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})
consisting of diffeomorphisms which coincide near

with vector bundle isomorphisms of
{E}
, see Theorem
4.1.1
.
This is mutually a
parametrized
,
foliated
, and

variant of the well known result that each self diffeomorphism

with

is isotopic to the linear isomorphism given by the Jacobi matrix of

at
0
.
Moreover, the result also include not only foliations but even ‘‘continuous’’ and possibly ‘‘fractal’’ partitions having certain ‘‘homogeneity’’ properties, see Example

.

Evidently, we have another restriction homomorphism
{\rho}:\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\to\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
associating to each

a unique vector bundle isomorphism

such that
h=\hat{h}
near
{B}
, and the kernel of
{\rho}
is
\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})
.
This allows to reduce the study of the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})
to two simpler groups: the image of
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})
in
\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
and the group

Further notice that for connected

-bundle, and thus can be studied by purely homotopic methods, see Lemma

On the other hand, the group

consists of diffeomorphisms supported out of the set

of singular leaves, so in this group we ‘‘get rid of the singularity

In particular, if

is compact, then

is the group of compactly supported diffeomorphism of the non-singular (usual) foliation on

, which is widely studied,
[

,
21
,

,

,

,

,

]
.
The applications of obtained results and concrete computations will be presented elsewhere.

1.1.
Main technical result

Recall that every diffeomorphism
h:(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}},0)
is isotopic to the linear map
J_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined by the Jacobi matrix
J_{h}
of
h
at
0
via the following isotopy
H:[0;1]\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
,
H_{\tau}(v)=\frac{h(\tau v)}{\tau}
for
\tau>0
, and
H_{0}(v)=J_{h}v
.
The smoothness of
H
is guaranteed by the Hadamard lemma, see Lemma
6.1.1
.
This kind of isotopies is used for the proof of existence of isotopies between (open) regular neighborhoods of a submanifold in an ambient manifold, e.g.
[
16
, Chapter 4, Theorem 5.3]
.
On the other hand, such isotopies are not compactly supported and therefore it is not always possible to extend them globally.
The following Theorem
1.1.1
is a compactly supported variant of the above observation, where we replace
\tau
with some function
\phi
.

Let us fix once and for all a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\mu:\mathbb{R}\to[0;1]
such that
\mu=0
on
[0;0.2]
and
\mu=1
on
[0.8;+\infty)
.
Let also
{p}:{E}\to{B}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
vector bundle over a smooth manifold
{B}
equipped with some orthogonal structure (see §
3.1
),
\|\cdot\|:{E}\to[0;+\infty)
be the corresponding norm,

R_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{E}\mid\|x\|\leq\varepsilon\}

for
\varepsilon>0
be the closed tubular neighborhood of
{B}
in
{E}
,
{N}\subset{E}
be a smooth submanifold being also a neighborhood of
{B}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}({N},{E})

\displaystyle=\{h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({N},{E})\mid h({B})\subset{B}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})

\displaystyle=\{h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}({N},{E})\mid h\ \text{is an embedding}\}.

Notice that each
h\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}({N},{E})
induces a certain vector bundle morphism
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h:{E}\to{E}
which can be regarded as a ‘‘partial derivative of
h
along
{B}
in the direction of fibers’’, see below §
3.3
and (
3.5
).

Now, given a function
{\delta}:\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})\to(0;+\infty)
continuous with respect to the weak Whitney topology
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
, see §
2.1
, define the following maps

(1.2)

\displaystyle\phi:\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})\times[0;1]\times{E}\to[0;1],

\displaystyle\phi(h,t,x)=t+(1-t)\mu\bigl(\tfrac{\|x\|}{{\delta}(h)}\bigr),

(1.3)

\displaystyle{H}:\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})\times(0;1]\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}({N},{E}),

\displaystyle{H}(h,t)(x)=\frac{h(\phi(h,t,x)\,x)}{\phi(h,t,x)}.

Evidently, for all
h\in\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})
we have that

(A)

{H}
is
\mathsf{W}^{\infty,\infty}
-continuous, i.e. continuous with respect to the corresponding topologies
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
;

(B)

\phi(h,t,x)>0
for
t>0
, so
{H}
is well-defined;

(C)

\phi(h,1,x)\equiv 1
, whence
{H}(h,1)=h
, i.e.
{H}_{1}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})}
;

(D)

\phi(h,t,x)\equiv 1
for
\|x\|\geq 0.8{\delta}(h)
, whence
{H}(h,1)=h
on
{E}\setminus R_{0.8{\delta}(h)}
.

Notice that, depending on
{\delta}
, the map
{H}(h,t)
is not necessarily an embedding, even if
h
is so.
One of the difficulties which is not presented in the case when
{B}
is a point, is that an embedding
h:{N}\to{E}

does not necessarily preserves the transversal directions of fibers of
{E}
at
{B}
.
Moreover, the deformation (
1.3
) always preserves projection of the image of the tangent spaces of fibers under the tangent map
\mathsf{T}h
onto
\mathsf{T}{B}
, and therefore
{H}(h,t)
can not be made arbitrary close to

in Whitney topology
\mathsf{W}^{r}
for

, see Lemma
6.3.1
(b)
.

Nevertheless, the following theorem claims that if
{\delta}
is sufficiently small, one can guarantee that
{H}(h,t)
for all
t\in(0;1]
is an embedding, and has a limit at
t\to 0
which coincides with a vector bundle morphism.

Theorem 1.1.1

(Linearization theorem)
.

Let
{B}
be a compact manifold, and
{p}:{E}\to{B}
a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
vector bundle equipped with some orthogonal structure.
Then there exists a strictly positive
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
-continuous function
{\delta}:\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})\to(0;+\infty)
such that the image of the map (
1.3
) is contained in
\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})
and
{H}
extends to a
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
-continuous map
{H}:\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})\times[0;1]\to\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})
such that for all
h\in\mathcal{E}_{0}({N},{E})
,

(1)

{H}(h,t)(R_{\delta(h)})\subset{N}
for
t\in[0;1]
;

(2)

{H}(h,0)
coincide with the vector bundle morphism
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h:{E}\to{E}
on
R_{0.2\delta(h)}
.

In fact we will prove a more general statement about ‘‘linearization’’ of smooth maps between maps of pairs of manifolds
(M,{B})\to(N,{C})
, Theorem
6.4.1
.

2.2.
Homomorphisms as principal fibrations

We recall here a simple lemma which will be used several times.
For a topological group
G
denote by
e_{G}
its unit element, by
G_{0}
the path component of
e_{G}
in
G
, and by
\pi_{0}G
the set of path components of
G
.
Then
G_{0}
is a normal subgroup of
G
and we have a natural bijection
\pi_{0}G=G/G_{0}
which allows to endow
\pi_{0}G

Let
\phi:G\to H
be a continuous homomorphism of topological groups.
Say that
\phi

admits a local section
if there exists an open neighborhood

of

and a continuous map

\phi

\phi\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{U}
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
\phi:G\to H

Denote

If
\phi

(1)

\phi:G\to q(G)

;

(2)

is a union of path components of
H
;

(3)

the restriction
\phi:G_{0}\to H_{0}
is surjective and is a principal

-fibration;

(4)

we have a long exact sequence of homotopy groups

\displaystyle\cdots\to\pi_{k}(K_{0},e_{G})\to\pi_{k}(G_{0},e_{G})

\displaystyle\to\pi_{k}(H_{0},e_{H})\to\pi_{k-1}(K_{0},e_{G})\to\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\to\pi_{1}(H_{0},e_{H})\to K/K_{0}\to G/G_{0}\to G/K;

(5)

\phi

(6)

Proof.

(1)

Note that the natural action of

-fibration.
We also have a continuous bijection
\hat{\phi}:G/K\to q(G)

.
Then the existence of a section

implies that

•

is a homeomorphism, so the map
\phi:G\to q(G)

•

that fibration
\phi:G\to q(G)

(2)
&
(3)

Recall that every locally trivial fibration over a paracompact Hausdorff space satisfies path lifting property, e.g.

.
This implies that

.

There is a canonical vector bundle isomorphism

Q
, so that the restriction
{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}|_{Q}:Q\to{B}
is a principal
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
-bundle over
{B}
, and
{p}:{E}\to{B}
is the associated
\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
-bundle corresponding to the natural action of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
on
\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
.

Let
X=\{(a,b)\in Q\times Q\mid{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}(a)={p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}(b)\}
be the fiberwise product of the total space
Q
, so it consists of pairs of frames over the same point, and the correspondence
X\to{B}
,
(a,b)\mapsto{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}(a)
, is the pull back of the fibration
{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}:Q\to{B}
corresponding to the same map
{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}:Q\to{B}
.

Notice that for every such pair
(a,b)\in X
there exists a unique matrix
A\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
such that
b=aA
.
Thus
(a,b)
can be regarded as an automorphism of the fiber
{E}_{{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}(a)}
written in the basis
a
.

Let us get rid of dependence on the basis.
To do that notice that the group
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
naturally acts from the right on
X
by the rule, if
A\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
, and
(a,b)\in X
, then
(a,b)A=(aA,bA)
.
Let
E_{aut}=X/\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
be the quotient space.
Then we have a natural projection
{q}:E_{aut}\to{B}
, and the fibers
{q}^{-1}(x)
can be regarded as automorphisms of
{E}_{x}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(E_{aut})
be the space of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-sections of
{q}
.
Then
\mathcal{S}(E_{aut})
is a group with respect to the pointwise composition of automorphisms
.
Indeed, the multiplication in the fibers of
\mathcal{S}(E_{aut})
is defined as follows.
For
(a,b)\in X
denote by
[a,b]
its equivalence class in
E_{aut}
.
Let
(a,b),(c,d)\in X
be two pairs of frames in the same fiber of
\mathcal{S}(E_{aut})
, i.e.
{p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}(a)={p}_{{\mathsf{m}}}(c)
.
Then there is a unique matrix
U\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
such that
bU=c
, and we put
[a,b]\cdot[c,d]:=[aU,d]
.
Notice that this definition does not depend on a choice of representatives: if
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
are any matrices, so
(aA,bA),(cB,dB)
are some other representatives of
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
, then
bA(A^{-1}UB)=cB
, whence

[aA,bA]\cdot[cB,dB]:=[aA(A^{-1}UB),dB]=[aUB,dB]=[aU,d].

One easily checks that this operation is associative, the unit is the class
[a,a]
for any frame
a
, while
[a,b]^{-1}=[b,a]
.

We also have a free action of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
on
E_{aut}
by
[a,b]A=[aA,b]
, which is transitive on each fiber of
{q}
, so
{q}:E_{aut}\to{B}
is a principal
\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
-fibration.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

There is a bijection
\theta:\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})\to\mathcal{S}(E_{aut})
being a
\mathsf{W}^{r,r}
- as well as
\mathsf{S}^{r,r}
-homeomorphism for all
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,\infty\}
.

Proof.

1) First suppose that
{p}
is a trivial vector bundle, so
{E}={B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
.
Then
{q}
is also a trivial fibration, whence
E_{aut}={B}\times\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
and
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
sections are just
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
maps
A:{B}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
.
Furthermore, each
h\in\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map given by
h:{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
,
h(x,v)=(x,A_{h}(x)v)
, for some map
A_{h}:{B}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}})
.
It follows from the formula for
h
that
A_{h}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
iff
h
is so.
Then
\theta
can be defined by
\theta(h)=A_{h}
.

2) Consider the general case.
Let
h\in\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
.
Then
h({E}_{x})={E}_{x}
, and the restriction
h|_{{E}_{x}}:{E}_{x}\to{E}_{x}
is a linear isomorphism for every
x\in{B}
.
Hence we can define the following section
\theta(h):{B}\to E_{aut}
of
{q}
by
\theta(h)(x)=h|_{{E}_{x}}
,
x\in{B}
.
Passing to local coordinates, see 1), we obtain that
\theta(h)
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
, and thus we get a well-defined map
\theta:\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})\to\mathcal{S}(E_{aut})
.
Moreover, again it follows from 1) that
\theta
is a continuous bijection and its inverse is also continuous with respect to all
\mathsf{W}^{r}
and
\mathsf{S}^{r}
topologies.
∎

4.4.
Conclusion

Using short exact sequences of the corresponding fibrations, the above observations allow to reduce (at least partially) the computation of the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B})
to study

•

the identity path components of simpler groups
\mathcal{D}_{nb}(M,{B})
,
\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
, and
\mathcal{D}({B})
,

•

and the images of all arrows
{\kappa}
and
{\rho}
in the corresponding groups
\pi_{0}\mathcal{D}({B})
,
\pi_{0}\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
, and
\pi_{0}\mathrm{GL}({E})
.

The group
\mathcal{D}({B})
might be simpler, since
\dim({B})<\dim(M)
, and the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}({B})
for manifolds of dimensions

are completely known, and they also mostly computed for
\dim({B})=3
.

Also the group
\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
reduces to the space of sections of certain
\mathrm{GL}
-fibration over
{B}
, see Lemma
4.3.1
, and can be studied by purely homomopical methods of obstruction theory.

Finally, the group
\mathcal{D}_{nb}(M,{B})
, on the one hand, might be regarded simpler in a ‘‘conceptual’’ sense: if we regard
{B}
as a ‘‘singularity’’, then
\mathcal{D}_{nb}(M,{B})
consists of diffeomorphisms supported out of that singularity.
On the other hand, if
M
is compact, then
\mathcal{D}_{nb}(M,{B})
can be identified with the group of compactly supported diffeomorphism of
M\setminus{B}
.
Such groups are widely studied, e.g.
[
35
,
21
,
36
,
31
,
12
,
13
,
32
]
.

5.
Linearization theorem for leaf preserving diffeomorphisms

In this section we will apply linearization theorem to leaf preserving diffeomorphisms for singular foliations, see Theorems
5.1.5
,
5.1.8
below.

5.1.
Homogeneous partitions

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a partition of a manifold
M
.
The elements of
\mathcal{F}
will also be called
leaves
.
A subset
{B}\subset M
is
\mathcal{F}
-saturated
, if
{B}
is a union of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.
For an open subset
U\subset M
denote by
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
the partition of
U
into path components of the non-empty intersections
U\cap\omega
for all
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.
We will call
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
the
restriction of
\mathcal{F}
onto
U
.

Let
U\subset M
be a subset.
Then a map
h:U\to M
will be called

•

\mathcal{F}
-foliated
if for each
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
the image
h(\omega\cap U)
is contained in some (possibly distinct from
\omega
) leaf of
\mathcal{F}
;

•

\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving
if
h(\omega\cap U)\subset\omega
for all
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
, the group of
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphisms of
M
, and for each subset
{B}\subset M
put:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B}):=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B}),\qquad\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B}):=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{fix}(M,{B}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B}):=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{nb}(M,{B}).

If
{B}
is a submanifold with a regular neighborhood
{p}:{E}\to{B}
, then we also denote:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B},{p}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{fix}(M,{B},{p}).

Definition 5.1.1
.

Let
{B}
be an
\mathcal{F}
-saturated submanifold of
M
with a regular neighborhood
{p}:{E}\to{B}
.
Say that a neighborhood
U
of
{B}
in
{E}
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous (with respect to
p
)
, whenever it has the following property:

•

if
x,y\in U
and
\tau>0
are such that
\tau x,\tau y\in U
and
x,y
belong to the same element of
\mathcal{F}
, then
\tau x,\tau y
also belong to the same element
\mathcal{F}
.

Evidently, if
U
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous, then so is any other neighborhood
V\subset U
of
{B}
in
{E}
.

Before stating our main result Theorem
5.1.5
we will present a class of partitions admitting
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous neighborhoods of saturated submanifolds which will be useful to keep in mind.

Definition 5.1.2
.

Let
f:M\to R
be a map into some set
R
, and
{B}\subset M
a subset.
Then by an
(f,{B})
-partition
we will call a partition
\mathcal{F}_{f,{B}}
of
M
into path components of
{B}
and path components of the sets
f^{-1}(c)\setminus{B}
for all
c\in R
.

Lemma 5.1.3
.

Let
{p}:{E}\to{B}
be a vector bundle and
f:{E}\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous function such that

(1)

f
is homogeneous of some degree
k>0
(possibly fractional) on fibers, i.e.
f(\tau v)=\tau^{k}f(v)
for all
\tau>0
and
v\in{E}
;

(2)

for some
a<0
and
b>0
the path components of
f^{-1}(a)
and
f^{-1}(b)
are closed in
{E}
.

Such a function will be called
admissible homogeneous
.
Let also
\mathcal{F}
be the
(f,{B})
-partition of
{E}
.
Then

(a)

each neighborhood
U
of
{B}
in
{E}
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous;

(b)

\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega\subset{B}
for all
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

First we show that for any
c\not=0
the path components of the set
f^{-1}(c)
are closed in
{E}
.
For definiteness assume that
c>0
.
Put
\tau=(c/b)^{1/k}
.
Then we have a well-defined homeomorphism
h:{E}\to{E}
,
h(v)=\tau v
.

Notice that
h(f^{-1}(b))=f^{-1}(c)
.
Indeed, if
f(v)=b
, then

f(h(v))=f(\tau v)=\tau^{k}f(v)=(c/b)\cdot b=c.

Hence
h
maps the path components of
f^{-1}(b)
onto path components of
f^{-1}(c)
.
Since path components of
f^{-1}(b)
are closed in
{E}
, so must be path components of
f^{-1}(c)
.
The proof for
c<0
is similar and uses
a
instead of
b
.

Further let us mention that
f({B})=0
.
Indeed, let
v\in{B}
, and
\tau>1
.
Then
v=\tau v
and
f(v)=f(\tau v)=\tau^{k}f(v)
, which is possible only when
f(v)=0
.

We will show that
{E}
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous.
Then, as mentioned above, any other neighborhood of
{B}
in
{E}
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous as well.
Let
\omega
be a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
,
x,y\in\omega
any two points, and
\tau>0
.
It is necessary to show that
\tau x,\tau y
also belong to the same leaf and
\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega\subset{B}
.
Consider the following cases.

(i) If
\omega
is a path component of
{B}
, then
\tau x=x,\tau y=y
, so they belong to the same leaf
\omega
of
\mathcal{F}
.
Moreover, since
{B}
is closed in
{E}
and contains
\omega
, we have that
\overline{\omega}\subset{B}
, whence
\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega\subset{B}
as well.

(ii) Suppose
\omega
is a path component of the set
f^{-1}(c)\setminus{B}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
, so there exists a path
\gamma:[0;1]\to\omega
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.
In particular,
f\circ\gamma:[0;1]\to\mathbb{R}
is a constant map into
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\tau\gamma:[0;1]\to{E}
is a path connecting
\tau x
and
\tau y
.
Since
\gamma([0;1])\subset{E}\setminus{B}
, we also have that
\tau\gamma([0;1])\subset{E}\setminus{B}
.
Moreover,
f(\tau\gamma(t))=\tau^{k}f(\gamma(t))=\tau^{k}c
,
t\in[0;1]
.
Thus
\tau\gamma
is a path between
\tau x
and
\tau y
in some path component of
f^{-1}(\tau^{k}c)\setminus{B}
, being by definition a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.
This proves
(a)
.

If
c\not=0
, then
\omega
is a path component of
f^{-1}(c)
and is closed as shown above.
Hence
\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega=\varnothing\subset{B}
.

On the other hand, suppose
c=0
.
Since
\omega\subset f^{-1}(0)\setminus{B}
, and
f^{-1}(0)
is closed, we have that
\overline{\omega}\subset f^{-1}(0)
, whence
\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega\subset f^{-1}(0)\setminus\bigl(f^{-1}(0)\setminus{B}\bigr)={B}
.
∎

Fiberwise homogeneous functions on tangent and cotangent bundles are widely studies, see e.g.
[
4
,
6
]
and references therein.

Let us also mention that the function
f
in Lemma
5.1.3
is assumed to be only continuous, whence the corresponding homogeneous partition
\mathcal{F}
is not necessarily smooth, and might have even a <<fractal>> structure.

Example 5.1.4
.

Let
{p}:{E}={B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to{B}
be a trivial vector bundle over connected manifold
{B}
,
k>0
a positive real number,
g:S^{{\mathsf{m}}-1}\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous (possibly even nowhere differentiable) function, and
f:{E}\to\mathbb{R}
a function given by

f(x,v)=\begin{cases}0,&v=0,\\
\|v\|^{k}\cdot g(v/\|v\|)\,,&v\not=0,\end{cases}

where
\|v\|=\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+\cdots+v_{{\mathsf{m}}}^{2}}
is the usual Euclidean norm of a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{{\mathsf{m}}})
.
Notice that if
g(v)=0
for some
v\in S^{{\mathsf{m}}-1}
, then
f
is zero on the line
\mathbb{R}v
passing through the origin and
v
.

Evidently, if
k=1
and
g\equiv 1
is constant, then
f(x,v)=\|v\|
.
Also if
k=2
,
{\mathsf{m}}=2
, and
g:S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
g(\phi)=\cos(\phi)\sin(\phi)=\tfrac{\sin(2\phi)}{2}
, then
f(x,u,v)=uv
for
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We claim that
f
satisfies assumptions of Lemma
5.1.3
.

(1)

Indeed, let
\tau>0
and
(x,v)\in{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
.
Then
\tau(x,v)=(x,\tau v)
.
Hence if
v=0
, then
f\bigl(\tau(x,0)\bigr)=f\bigl(x,0)\bigr)=0=\tau^{k}f(x,v)
.
On the other hand, if
v\not=0
, then

f(x,\tau v)=\|\tau v\|^{k}\cdot g(\tau v/\|\tau v\|)=\tau^{k}\|v\|^{k}\cdot g(v/\|v\|)=\tau^{k}f(x,v),

so
f
is homogeneous of degree
k
.

(2)

Let
G_{-}={B}\times g^{-1}\bigl((-\infty,0)\bigr)
.
Then for each
a<0
we have a homeomorphism
\alpha:f^{-1}(a)\to G_{-}
,
\alpha(x,v)=(x,v/\|v\|)
, whose inverse is given by
\alpha^{-1}(x,v)=\bigl(x,-\bigl|a/g(v)\bigr|^{1/k}v\bigr)
.
Indeed, if
(x,v)\in G_{-}
, so
\|v\|=1
, then
f\circ\alpha^{-1}(x,v)=-\bigl|a/g(v)\bigr|g(v)=-|a|=a
.

Since
G_{-}
is an open subset of the manifold
{B}\times S^{{\mathsf{m}}-1}
, its path components are open closed in
G_{-}
.
Hence each path component
\omega
of
f^{-1}(a)
is open closed in
f^{-1}(a)
.
But
f^{-1}(a)
is closed in
{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
, whence
\omega
is closed in
{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
as well.

Theorem 5.1.5

(Foliated linearization theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a partition of
M
,
{B}
an
\mathcal{F}
-saturated submanifold with a regular neighborhood
{p}:{E}\to{B}
.
Suppose that there exists a compact neighborhood
U\subset{E}
of
{B}
such that

(a)

U
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous;

(b)

U\cap(\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega)\subset{B}
for each
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.

Then the inclusion of pairs of groups of
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphisms:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p}),\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\bigr)\subset\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B}),\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\bigr),

is mutually a
\mathsf{W}^{\infty,\infty}
- and
\mathsf{S}^{\infty,\infty}
-homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Let
{G}:\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B})\times[0;1]\to\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B})
be the deformation of the group
\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B})
into
\mathcal{D}_{inv}(M,{B},{p})
constructed in Theorem
4.1.1
for
{N}=U
.
It suffices to check that
{G}(\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\times[0;1])\subset\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})
.
Then the restriction of
{G}
to
\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\times[0;1]
will be a deformation of the right pair in (
5.1
) into the corresponding left pair.

Let
h\in\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})
and
V=R_{0.8{\delta}(h)}
.
Let also
x\in M
, and
\omega
be the leaf of
\mathcal{F}
containing
x
.
We need to show that
{G}(x,t)\in\omega
for all
t\in[0;1]
.
This will imply that
{G}_{t}(h)
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving.

1)

If
(x,t)\in\bigl({B}\cup\overline{M\setminus V}\bigr)\times[0;1]
, then by
(D)
, formula (
4.2
) for
{G}
, and
(iv)
from the proof of Theorem
4.1.1
,
{G}(h,t)(x)=h(x)\in\omega
, since
h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving.

2)

Suppose
(x,t)\in V\times(0;1]
and let
\tau=\phi(x,t)
.
Then by (
1.2
),
\tau>0
.
We claim that the four points
x,\tau x,h(\tau x),{G}(h,t)(x):=\tfrac{1}{\tau}(h(\tau x))
belong to
U
.

Indeed,
x\in V\subset U
by definition.
Since
V
is star-like and
\tau=\phi(x,t)\in[0;1]
, we have that
\tau x\in V\subset U
.
Moreover, by Theorem
1.1.1
,
{H}(h,t)(V)\subset{N}=U
for all
t\in[0;1]
, and thus
{H}(h,t)(\tau x)\in U
as well.
For
t=1
, this implies that
h(\tau x)={H}(h,1)(\tau x)={G}(h,1)(\tau x)\in U
.
Also for other
t
, we get that
\tfrac{1}{\tau}(h(\tau x))={H}(h,t)(\tau x)={G}(h,t)(x)\in U
as well.

Since
h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving, the points
\tau x
and
h(\tau x)
belong to the same leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.
Moreover, as
U
is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous, their respective images under multiplication by
\tfrac{1}{\tau}
, that is
x:=\tfrac{1}{\tau}\tau x
and
{G}(h,t)(x)=\tfrac{1}{\tau}h(\tau x)
, must also belong to the same leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.
In other words,
{G}(h,t)(x)\in\omega
.

3)

It remains to show that
{G}(x,0)\in\omega
if
x\in V
.
Due to
1)
and
2)
,
{G}(h,t)(x)\in U\cap\omega
for
t\in(0;1]
.
Suppose
{G}(x,0)\not\in\omega
.
Then by continuity of
{G}
,
{G}(x,0)\in U\cap(\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega)\subset{B}
.
But
{G}_{0}
is a diffeomorphism of
M
which coincide with
h
on
{B}
, and therefore it yields a self-bijection of
{B}
.
Hence
x\in{B}
, and thus by
1)
,
{G}(x,0)=h(x)\in\omega
.
∎

Remark 5.1.7
.

As in the non-foliated case we have a homomorphism
{\rho}:\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\to\mathrm{GL}({E})
associating to each

the unique vector bundle morphism

such that

near

In particular,

preserves leaves of

‘‘near

However, the description of the image of that map and the question whether

is a locally trivial fibration, are essentially harder than in the non-foliated case and essentially depend on the structure of

We will now consider the case when the map

is indeed a locally trivial fibration over its image.

Let

be a vector bundle over a compact manifold

, so

can be regarded as a subgroup of

, and

Theorem 5.1.8
.

Let

be an admissible homogeneous function, and
\mathcal{F}
be the
(f,{B})
-partition of

.
Then the homomorphism
{\rho}:\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\to\mathrm{GL}({E})
is a retraction onto its image,
\ker({\rho})=\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\rho}(\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})=\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E},{B}),

(5.3)

\displaystyle{\rho}(\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E})=\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E}).

Whence we have the following homotopy equivalences (
\simeq
) and homeomorphisms (
\cong
):

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})\simeq\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})\times{\rho}(\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})),

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\simeq\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})\times{\rho}(\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p}))

with respect to
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
and
\mathsf{S}^{\infty}
topologies.

Proof.

(1)

The following statements are evident:

\displaystyle\ker({\rho})=\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E})=\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})=\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E},{B}).

Moreover, (
5.2
) follows from (

) and the observation that
h={\rho}(h)
on
{B}
, so
h
is fixed on

iff
{\rho}(h)
is fixed on
{B}
.

Also, by Lemma

,

satisfies assumptions of Theorem

, whence we get the homotopy equivalences in (

) and (

).

(2)

Let us show that
{\rho}(\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p}))\subset\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\cap\mathrm{GL}({E})
.
Let

and

.
We should show the vector bundle morphism

is also

-leaf preserving, i.e. for each

, its image

belong to the same leaf as

.

Indeed, since

near

, there exists small

, where the latter equality holds since

is a vector bundle morphism (linear of fibres).
As

-leaf preserving,

.
Hence by

-homogeneity of

,

and

also belong to the same leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.

(3)

Now let

.
Then
{\rho}(h)
and

are two vector bundle isomorphisms which coincide near

.
Then they should coincide on all of

.
In other words,
{\rho}(h)=h
, and thus
q
is a retraction of
\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})
onto its subgroup

Together with Lemma

∎

Then

Corollary 5.1.9
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to\mathbb{R}
be an admissible homogeneous function with respect to the vector bundle

over a point.
Let also

be a trivial vector bundle over a compact manifold, and

the function given by

Then

is admissible homogeneous, so Theorem

holds for the
(f,{B})
-partition
\mathcal{F}
.
Moreover, in this case there is a

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sigma:{\rho}(\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p}))\cong\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({B},\mathcal{L}^{*}(g))\times\mathcal{D}({B}),

such that

Hence we get the following homotopy equivalences (
\simeq
) and homeomorphisms (
\cong
):

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B})\simeq\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({B},\mathcal{L}^{*}(g)),

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B})\simeq\mathcal{D}_{inv}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}_{nb}(\mathcal{F},{B})\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({B},\mathcal{L}^{*}(g))\times\mathcal{D}({B})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\times\mathcal{D}({B})

with respect to
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
and
\mathsf{S}^{\infty}

Proof.

1) Let us show that
f
is admissible homogeneous.
Notice that for each
\tau>0
we have that

f\bigl(\tau(x,v)\bigr)=f(x,\tau v)=g(\tau v)=\tau^{k}g(v)=\tau^{k}f(x,v),

so
f
is homogeneous of the same degree as
g
.
Moreover, for each

each path component
\beta
of

is a product of some path component

of

with some path component
\alpha
of

But

is closed since

is admissible homogeneous, while

is closed since

is locally path connected.
Hence

is closed in

.
Therefore

is admissible homogeneous.

2) Let us construct a homeomorphism
\sigma
.
Recall that we have a natural homomorphism
\rho:\mathrm{GL}({E})\to\mathcal{D}({B})
,
\rho(h)=h|_{{B}}
, whose kernel is
\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})
.
Since

is trivial,

admits a global section

,

, being

- and
\mathsf{S}^{r,r}
-continuous for all

.
Hence by Lemma

we have a homeomorphism
\mathrm{GL}({E})\cong\mathrm{GL}({E},{B})\times\mathcal{D}({B})
.

On the other hand, since

is a trivial vector bundle, there is another homeomorphism

given in the part 1) of the proof of Lemma

.
This finally gives a homeomorphism

\sigma:\mathrm{GL}({E})\cong\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({B},\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}))\times\mathcal{D}({B}).

Now let

and

.
Then

iff the image of

is contained in

Moreover, in this case

-homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Notice that, by definition, for each
x\in{B}
the set
{E}_{x}:=\phi_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is an open neighborhood of
x
, while the constant map
{p}_{x}:{E}_{x}\to x
is regular neighborhood of
x
.
One can decrease

and assume that
\phi_{x}(U_{x})\cap\phi_{x^{\prime}}(U_{x^{\prime}})=\varnothing
for

.
Hence, changing
\phi_{x}
out of

we can also assume that
{E}_{x}\cap{E}_{x^{\prime}}=\varnothing
.

Denote
{E}=\cup_{x\in{B}}{E}_{x}
.
Then the map
{p}:{E}\to{B}
,
{p}({E}_{x})=x
is a trivial vector bundle over
{B}
.
It also follows from definition, that
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{lin}(\mathcal{F})
coincides with the group
\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})
.

Let
U\subset{E}
be any compact neighborhood of
{B}
.
We claim that

is
\mathcal{F}
-homogeneous and
U\cap(\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega)\subset{B}
for each
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.
Then it will follow from Theorem
5.1.5
, that the inclusion
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{lin}(\mathcal{F})\equiv\mathcal{D}_{fix}(\mathcal{F},{B},{p})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
is a homotopy equivalence.

Consider the following function

given by

Then

satisfies assumptions

and

of Lemma

.
Indeed, by definition

holds.
Moreover, each level set

.
Hence

Therefore, the path components of level sets of

Now by Lemma

of

is

∎

6.
Linearization of smooth maps between vector bundles

In Theorem
1.1.1
we considered smooth self maps of a total space of some vector bundle
{p}:{E}\to{B}
which leaves invariant the zero section
{B}
.
In this section we will consider smooth maps between total spaces of vector bundles which send zero section to zero section, and formulate a more general statement (Theorems
6.4.1
) about deformations of such maps to their ‘‘derivative along fibers’’.

6.1.
Maps into Euclidean spaces

Let
{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{b}}}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
submanifold (possibly with corners) and
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{{\mathsf{c}}}):{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{c}}}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
map.
For each
{j}=1,\ldots,{\mathsf{b}}
define the map

\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial v_{{j}}}:{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{c}}},\qquad\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial v_{{j}}}=\bigl(\tfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial v_{{j}}},\ldots,\tfrac{\partial f_{{\mathsf{c}}}}{\partial v_{{j}}}\bigr),

whose coordinate functions are partial derivatives of coordinate functions of
f
in
v_{{j}}
.
Such a map will often be denoted by
f^{\prime}_{v_{{j}}}
.

For a continuous path
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{{\mathsf{c}}}):[a;b]\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{c}}}
we put

\smallint\limits_{a}^{b}\gamma(s)ds:=\bigl(\smallint\limits_{a}^{b}\gamma_{1}(s)ds,\ldots,\smallint\limits_{a}^{b}\gamma_{{\mathsf{c}}}(s)ds\bigr).

The following variant of Hadamard lemma is easy and we leave it for the reader.

Lemma 6.1.1

(e.g.
[
14
, Lemma II.6.10]
)
.

Let
f:{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{n}}}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
map,
1\leq r\leq\infty
.
Then for all
(x,v)\in{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
we have the following identity:

(6.1)

f(x,bv)-f(x,av)=\smallint\limits_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\bigl(f(x,sv)\bigr)ds=\sum_{{j}=1}^{{\mathsf{m}}}v_{{j}}\smallint\limits_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}_{v_{{j}}}(x,sv)ds,

where we assume that the middle and right parts of this identity are zero whenever
v=0
.

In particular, if
f(x,0)\equiv 0
for all
x\in{B}
, then
f(x,v)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{{\mathsf{m}}}v_{i}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(x,sv)ds
.
Moreover, define the following
\mathcal{C}^{r-1}
map
{\alpha}:[0;1]\times{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{n}}}
by

(6.2)

{\alpha}(\tau,x,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{{\mathsf{m}}}v_{i}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(x,s\tau v)ds.

Then

(a)

f(x,\tau v)=\tau{\alpha}(\tau,x,v)
, so
f(x,v)={\alpha}(1,x,v)
and
{\alpha}(\tau,x,v)=\frac{f(x,\tau v)}{\tau}
for
\tau>0
;

(b)

{\alpha}(0,x,v)=\sum\limits_{{j}=1}^{{\mathsf{m}}}v_{{j}}f^{\prime}_{v_{{j}}}(x,0)
, so for each
x\in{B}
we have a well-defined
linear
map
\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{m}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{\mathsf{n}}}
,
v\mapsto{\alpha}(0,x,v)
;

(c)

for all
{i}=1,\ldots,{\mathsf{b}}
,
{j}=1,\ldots,{\mathsf{m}}
,
x\in{B}
, and
\tau\in[0;1]

\displaystyle{\alpha}^{\prime}_{x_{{i}}}(\tau,x,0)=0,

\displaystyle{\alpha}^{\prime}_{v_{{j}}}(\tau,x,0)=f^{\prime}_{v_{i}}(x,0).

6.2.
Maps between total spaces of vector bundles

Let

be vector bundles of ranks
{\mathsf{m}}
and

As above, we identify

(resp.

(resp.

Given a star-like neighborhood

Our aim is to prove that if

and

Let

Proof.

(b)

Lemma 6.2.2

(Hadamard lemma for vector bundles)
.

For each

there exists a unique
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

(i)

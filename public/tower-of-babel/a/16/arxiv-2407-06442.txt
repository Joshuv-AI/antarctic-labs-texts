2.
Preliminaries

Fractional Laplacian and Sobolev spaces

Let
d\geq 1
and
s>0
. The fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of a function in

is defined by means of the Fourier transform

(2.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}u:=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left({|\cdot|}^{2s}\mathscr{F}(u)\right).

Thus, in view of (
1.6
) and (
2.1
), the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be defined as the completion of

with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

see e.g.,
[
22
]
*Chapter 3, page 70. If
1<p<\frac{d}{s}
, then
\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
can be identified with a space of functions having
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as a dense subspace, i.e., it is isomorphic to the
Riesz potential space
often denoted by
I_{s}(L^{p})
, see
[
26
]
*Chapter 5, Corollary 1 and
[
20
]
*Lemma 1.8. In particular,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad p^{*}:=\frac{dp}{d-sp}.

On the other hand, it is well known that for
p\geq\frac{d}{s}
the homogeneous fractional Sobolev spaces can not be identified as spaces of tempered distributions; cf.
[
23
]
*Theorem 4.
Similarly, if
1<p<\infty
and
s>0
we can define the inhomogeneous fractional Sobolev space as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}:=\|{(1-\Delta)}^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\quad{(1-\Delta)}^{\frac{s}{2}}u:=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left({(1+|\cdot|}^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathscr{F}(u)\right).

Such a space is equivalent to the
Bessel potential space
often denoted by

; see e.g.,
[
26
]
*eq. (27.27). In particular, if

then

coincides with

, see
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.2.3. Moreover, the following embeddings hold (
[

]
*Theorem 3.1.3):

(2.3)

\displaystyle H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow\begin{cases}L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),&\mbox{if }1<p<\frac{d}{s}\ \text{and}\ p\leq q\leq p^{*},\ \mbox{or }p=\frac{d}{s}\ \text{and}\ p\leq q<\infty,\\
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),&\mbox{if }p>\frac{d}{s}.\end{cases}

In what follows, we denote by
\dot{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(respectively

) the dual space of
\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(respectively

) the corresponding duality pairing. In the Hilbert setting (

), we for the sake of the reader, we drop the exponent

(respectively

) in the definition of

Note that, if

, Riesz representation theorem guarantees that for every

there exists a unique element

Moreover,

\left\|{U}_{T}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\|T\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

3.1
) against
(1-\eta_{R})\psi
obtaining

(3.3)

\begin{split}0\!=\!&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)L_{m,s}((1-\eta_{R})\psi)(x)dx\!=\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)L_{m,s}\psi(x)dx\!-\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)L_{m,s}(\eta_{R}\psi)(x)dx\\
&{=}\int_{B_{R}}u(x)\varphi(x)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)L_{m,s}(\eta_{R}\psi)(x)dx.\end{split}

Then, by (
2.7
), (
2.8
), (
3.3
) and Fubini’s theorem we obtain that there exists a positive constant
K_{d,m,s}
such that

(3.4)

\begin{split}&K_{d,m,s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)L_{m,s}(\eta_{R}\psi)(x)dx\\
&\!=\!\int_{B_{R}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\!\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left(\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}\frac{\eta_{R}(x+ky)}{|x+ky-z|^{d-2s}}\!\!\right)dy\,u(x)dx\!\!\right)\!\varphi(z)dz\\
&\!=K_{d,m,s}\int_{B_{R}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}J_{R}(x,z)u(x)dx\right)\varphi(z)dz=K_{d,m,s}\int_{B_{R}}u(z)\varphi(z)dz,\end{split}

where
K_{d,m,s}=(2/(A_{2s}C_{d,m,s}))
and

(3.5)

J_{R}(x,z):=\frac{A_{2s}C_{d,m,s}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left(\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}\frac{\eta_{R}(x+ky)}{|x+ky-z|^{d-2s}}\right)dy.

In particular, from (
3.4
) we deduce that for almost every
z\in B_{R}

(3.6)

u(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}J_{R}(x,z)u(x)dx.

From now on, we focus on proving estimates for (
3.6
).
First of all we notice that the function
i_{R}(x,z)
defined by

i_{R}(x,z):=\frac{\eta_{R}(x)}{|x-z|^{d-2s}}

satisfies the following the properties:

•

if
|z|<R
and
|x|<\frac{3R}{2}
then
i_{R}(x,z)=0
;

•

if
|z|<R
and
|x|\geq\frac{3R}{2}
then
|x-z|\geq\frac{R}{2}.

In particular,
i_{R}(x,z)\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{B_{R}})
.
Moreover, in view of the above analysis, the function
i_{R}(\cdot,z)\in C^{\infty}({B_{R}})\cap\mathscr{L}^{1}_{2s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
J_{R}(\cdot,z)=A_{2s}L_{m,s}(i_{R}(\cdot,z))
from which (
3.5
) is well defined.
Furthermore, by differentiating, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
multi-index the following inequalities hold

(3.7)

\displaystyle|D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(x,z)|\leq C(R+|x-z|)^{-(|n|+d-2s)},

(3.8)

\displaystyle|D^{2m}_{x}D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(x,z)|\leq CR^{-2m}(R+|x-z|)^{-(|n|+d-2s)},

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
R
. For the convenience of reader we point out that, unless stated, all the constants from now on depend only on
d,m,s
and
n
.
By combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
) with
[
3
]
*Lemma 2.4 we obtain that

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left|\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(x+ky,z)\right|dy\\
&=\int_{B_{R}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left|\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(x+ky,z)\right|dy\\
&\hphantom{XXX}+\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left|\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(x+ky,z)\right|dy\\
&\!\!\leq\!\left\|D^{2m}_{x}\!D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(\cdot,z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\!\!\int_{B_{R}}\!\!|y|^{2m-(d+2s)}dy\!\\
&\hphantom{XXX}+\!C_{m}\!\left\|D^{n}_{z}i_{R}(\cdot,z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\!\!\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\!\!|y|^{-(d+2s)}dy\\
&\leq CR^{-(|n|+d)},\end{split}

for some positive constant
C
.
Thus, (
3.9
) implies that
J_{R}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}({B_{R}})
and

|D^{n}_{z}J_{R}(x,z)|\leq CR^{-(|n|+d)}\quad\forall(x,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times B_{R}.

Next, we further claim that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
multi-index there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(3.10)

|D^{n}_{z}J_{R}(x,z)|\leq\frac{C}{R^{|n|-2s}(R^{d+2s}+|x|^{d+2s})}\quad\forall(x,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times B_{R}.

To this aim, we first notice that if
|x|\leq 4mR
, the inequality (
3.10
) is obtained from (
3.9
). Then we only need to consider the case
|x|>4mR
.

Assume that
|x|>4mR
and
|z|<R
. Since
I_{2s}
is the fundamental solution for
(-\Delta)^{s}
and the function
x\mapsto\frac{A_{2s}}{|x-z|^{d-2s}}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap\mathscr{L}^{1}_{2s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
provided
|x|>4mR
and
|z|<R
, by applying again
[
3
]
*Lemma 1.5 we infer that for every
(x,z)\in B^{c}_{4mR}\times B_{R}

(3.11)

\frac{A_{2s}C_{d,m,s}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left(\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}\frac{1}{|x+ky-z|^{d-2s}}\right)dy=0.

Hence, by combining (
3.5
), (
3.11
) with the fact that
\eta_{R}(x)=1
if
|x|>4mR
, we deduce the equality

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\!\frac{A_{2s}C_{d,m,s}}{2}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\!|\bar{y}-z|^{-(d-2s)}\!\underbrace{\left(\sum_{k=-m,k\neq 0}^{m}\!\!(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}\frac{(\eta_{R}(\bar{y})-1)}{|x+k\bar{y}|^{(d+2s)}}\right)}_{:=h_{R}(x,\bar{y})}d\bar{y}\\
&=\frac{A_{2s}C_{d,m,s}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+2s}}\left(\,\sum_{k=-m}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{2m}{m-k}\frac{(\eta_{R}(x+ky)-1)}{|x+ky-z|^{d-2s}}\right)dy\\
&=J_{R}(x,z).\end{split}

Furthermore, for fixed
|x|>4mR
, we see that if
|\bar{y}|\geq 2R
then
h_{R}(x,\bar{y})=0
while if
|\bar{y}|<2R
we still have
|x+k\bar{y}|>2mR
. We have therefore proved that,

h_{R}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\quad\forall|x|>4mR.

Furthermore, by differentiating, we have the following estimate

(3.13)

|D^{n}_{\bar{y}}h_{R}(x,\bar{y})|\leq CR^{-|n|}|x|^{-(d+2s)}\quad\forall|x|>4mR,\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},

for some positive constant
C
. Hence, putting together (
3.12
) with (
3.13
) yields

(3.14)

|D^{n}_{z}J_{R}(x,z)|\leq\frac{C}{R^{|n|}|x|^{d+2s}}\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{d\bar{y}}{|\bar{y}-z|^{d-2s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{C}}{R^{|n|-2s}|x|^{d+2s}}.

In particular, from (
3.14
), if
|x|>4mR
we deduce (
3.10
). Finally, since
u\in\mathscr{L}^{1}_{2s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, from (
3.6
) and (
3.10
) we can differentiate under the sign of the integral obtaining that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
there exists
C>0
such that

|D^{n}u(z)|\leq\frac{C}{R^{|n|-2s}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)|}{R^{d+2s}+|x|^{d+2s}}dx\quad\forall|z|<R,

concluding the proof.
∎

The following result refines Lemma
3.1
by further assuming some Lebesgue integrability.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
d\geq 1
,
0<s<\frac{d}{2}
and
R>0
. Let
u=\sum^{k}_{i=1}u_{i}
, where
u_{i}\in L^{p_{i}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
p_{i}\in[1,\infty)
. If
u
solves

(-\Delta)^{s}u=0\quad\text{in}\ \mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B_{2R}),

then
u\in C^{\infty}({B_{R}})
and moreover, for every multi-index
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
we have

(3.15)

\|D^{n}u\|_{L^{\infty}({B_{R}})}\leq C\sum^{k}_{i=1}R^{-|n|-\frac{d}{p_{i}}}\|u\|_{L^{p_{i}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

for some positive constant
C
indenpendent of
R
.

Proof.

Clearly
u\in\mathscr{L}^{1}_{2s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Hence, Lemma
3.1
yields

(3.16)

\|D^{n}u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq CR^{2s-|n|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)|}{(R^{d+2s}+|x|^{d+2s})}dx.

Then, if we denote by
\omega_{d-1}
the surface area of the unitary ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
p^{\prime}_{i}
the Hölder conjugate of
p_{i}
, we obtain

(3.17)

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{dx}{(R^{d+2s}+|x|^{d+2s})^{p^{\prime}_{i}}}=\omega_{d-1}R^{d-(d+2s)p^{\prime}_{i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{d-1}}{{(1+s^{d+2s})}^{p^{\prime}_{i}}}ds.\end{split}

Next, by combining (
3.16
), (
3.17
) and Hölder inequality we derive (
3.15
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From Lemma
3.1
we obtain that
u
is smooth and

\|D^{n}u\|_{L^{\infty}({B_{R}})}\leq CR^{2s-|n|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)|}{(R^{d+2s}+|x|^{d+2s})}dx\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},

where
C
is a constant not depending on
R
. By sending
R
to infinity we obtain that
D^{n}u\equiv 0
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
such that
|n|\geq 2s
.
∎

Next, we present a result in the spirit of
[
13
]
*Theorem 2.4 where the authors find representation formulae in the case of the polyharmonic operator
(-\Delta)^{k},\,k~{\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, as well as related Liouville theorems.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We only focus on
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
, the other implication being trivial. By
[
20
]
*eq. (2.3) we have that
U_{T}=I_{2s}*T
. Moreover, by (
2.11
),

(3.18)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}(I_{2s}*T)=T\quad\text{in}\ \mathscr{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Hence, by Theorem
1.1
we infer that
u
can be written as

u(x)=(I_{2s}*T)(x)+P(x),\quad P(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\text{deg(P)}}P_{i}(x),

where
P_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
and by
deg(P)
we denote the degree of
P
.
In particular, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(3.19)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{R<|x-y|<2R}|P(y)|dy\geq CR^{\text{deg(P)}}+o(R^{\text{deg(P)}})\quad\text{as}\ R\to+\infty.\end{split}

Assume now by contradiction that
u
satisfies (
1.4
) and
P\not\equiv l
. Then, by (
3.19
) we conclude that
\text{deg(P)}\geq 1
and there exists a positive constant

such that

(3.20)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{R<|y-x|<2R}|(I_{2s}*T)(y)+P(y)-l|dy\geq CR^{\text{deg(P)}}+o(R^{\text{deg(P)}}),\end{split}

where we have used that

and the Sobolev embedding to conclude that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{R^{d}}\int_{R<|y-x|<2R}(I_{2s}*T)(y)dy=0.

The inequality (

) contradicts (

) completing the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We recall that Theorem

admits several variations in the spirit of

]
*Corollary 4.9. As a matter of fact, by arguing as in the first part of the proof of Theorem

, for any

. In particular, by (

Moreover, in Theorem

concerning the regularity of Riesz potential operator.

, can be applied for instance to the study of Thomas–Fermi type integral equations, see e.g.,

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
0<s<\tfrac{d}{2}
and

. Assume that

. Then

, where

is the potential defined by (

). In particular,
{I_{2s}*T}~\in\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap\dot{H}^{2s,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

Since
T\in\dot{H}^{-s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, by (

) and (

) there exists an element

such that

On the other hand, since

, the Riesz potential

is well defined as a Lebesgue integral and solves (

) as well. Note that, by the Sobolev embedding (

) and

]
*Lemma 1.8,

and

. Hence,

–harmonic.
Then, again in view of (

), we can apply Corollary

to

and, by sending

to infinity, we derive the equality

.

4.
Brezis–Browder type results

In this section we first focus on proving validity of Brezis–Browder type results for fractional Sobolev spaces defined as in (
2.12
), under extra (local)integrabilty conditions. Next, as a matter of completeness, in the low order regime
0<s<1
, we show that the same holds true without further integrability in the framework of the fractional Sobolev spaces defined via the Gagliardo–Slobodeckiĭ seminorm. This provides an improvement to Theorem
4.1
for
0<s<1
and
p=2
; see Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.3
.

To begin with, we recall the following technical result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
s>0
,
1<p<\infty
and
X
be as in (
2.12
). Assume that
u\in X
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If we define
\varphi_{\lambda}(x):=\varphi(\lambda^{-1}x)
with
\lambda\geq 1
, then
u\varphi_{\lambda}\in{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, if
\varphi=1
in a neighborhood of the origin then

\displaystyle\|u\varphi_{\lambda}-u\|_{X}\to 0\quad\text{as}\ \lambda\to\infty.

Proof.

Assume first that
X=\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then, from
[
22
]
*Chapter 3, Section 3.7 we conclude that
u\varphi_{\lambda}\in\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\displaystyle\|u\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C_{d,s,\varphi}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

for some positive constant
C_{d,s,\varphi}
(independent of
\lambda
).
Moreover, by the Sobolev embedding (
2.2
) we further have that
u\varphi_{\lambda}\in H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Next, by arguing as in
[
25
]
*Lemma 5 we infer that
u\varphi_{\lambda}
converges to
u
in
\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Assume now that
X=H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. If
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by performing an explicit computation it is easy to verify that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|u\varphi_{\lambda}\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C_{d,s,\varphi}\|u\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

If
s\notin{\mathbb{N}}
, then (
4.1
) follows by interpolation with the integer cases. As a result, by arguing again as in
[
25
]
*Lemma 5 we conclude that
u\varphi_{\lambda}
converges to
u
in
H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
∎

An intermediate step toward Theorem
4.1
follows at once. Indeed, let us focus on
s\notin{\mathbb{N}}
. If
X
and
\bar{q}
are as in (
2.12
) and (
1.7
), and
T\in X{{}^{\prime}}\cap L^{\bar{q}}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, by a density argument and the Sobolev embeddings (
2.2
)-(
2.3
), it is easy to verify that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\langle T,u\rangle_{X{{}^{\prime}},\,X}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}T(x)u(x)dx\quad\forall u\in X_{c},

where
X_{c}
denotes the set of
X
-functions with compact support. Hence, in view of Proposition
4.1
and (
4.2
), we are now ready to state our main result of this section:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
s>0
,
1<p<\infty
,
X
be as in (
2.12
) and
\bar{q}
as in (
1.7
). Assume that
T\in X{{}^{\prime}}\cap L_{loc}^{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u\in X
is such that
Tu\geq-|f|
for some
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then,
Tu\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\langle T,u\rangle_{X^{{}^{\prime}}\!,\,X}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x)u(x)dx.

Proof.

Assume first that
s\notin{\mathbb{N}}
. Let us consider
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
, and
\varphi=1
in
B_{1}
. Let us also set
u_{\lambda}(x):=u(x)\varphi(\lambda^{-1}x)
with
\lambda\geq 1
.
In particular,
u_{\lambda}\in X_{c}
. Thus, Proposition
4.1
and (
4.2
) yield

(4.3)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\langle T,u_{\lambda}\rangle_{X^{{}^{\prime}},\,X}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x)u_{\lambda}(x)dx=\langle T,u\rangle_{X^{{}^{\prime}},\,X}.

Therefore, by the inequality
Tu\geq-|f|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, Fatou’s Lemma and (
4.3
), we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x)u(x)dx\leq\liminf_{\lambda\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x)u_{\lambda}(x)dx=\langle T,u\rangle_{X^{{}^{\prime}},\,X}.\end{split}

By (
4.4
) and using again the inequality
Tu\geq-|f|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we also get
Tu\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Finally, since
|Tu_{\lambda}|\leq|Tu|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by dominated convergence we deduce that

Tu_{\lambda}
converges to
Tu
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, concluding the proof in the non-integer setting.

Assume that
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
X=H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
we directly refer to
[
4
]
*Theorem 3.4.2. If
X=\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
1<p<\frac{d}{s}
we simply argue as follows. By the trivial embedding
H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
Tu_{\lambda}\geq-|f|
, the fact that
u_{\lambda}\in H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
[
4
]
*Theorem 3.4.2, we infer

(4.5)

\displaystyle\langle T,u_{\lambda}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}},\,\dot{H}^{s,p}}=\langle T,u_{\lambda}\rangle_{{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}},\,{H}^{s,p}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}T(x)u_{\lambda}(x)dx.

Then, by (
4.5
) and arguing as above we conclude the proof.
∎

Low order regime:
0<s<1
.

Up to this point, our analysis has focused on fractional Sobolev spaces characterized via the Riesz and Bessel potentials. By replacing the former spaces with those defined through the Gagliardo–Slobodeckiĭ seminorm, in Theorem
4.2
we show that, for
0<s<1
, an analogue of Theorem
4.1
holds in such a different framework, with no further local integrability assumptions required. Namely,
for
d\geq 1
,
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, we can define
\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with respect to

\displaystyle[u]_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}:=\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}dx\,dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

respectively to

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+[u]_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

In particular,
W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(respectively
\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by further assuming
1<p<\frac{d}{s}
) can be identified as the space of functions
u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(respectively
L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
) such that
[u]_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
is finite.
Moreover, similarly to (
2.12
), we then define
Y
to be

(4.7)

\displaystyle{Y}:=\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\ \big(\text{by further assuming}\ 1<p<\tfrac{d}{s}\big)\ \text{or}\ W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and we understand
Y^{\prime}\cap L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in analogy with (
2.13
)-(
2.14
).
It is well known that this construction gives rise, in general, to a space distinct from
X
in (
2.12
); see, for instance,
[
6
]
*Theorem 3.26 for a comprehensive treatment of the inhomogeneous case, and
[
10
]
together with the references therein for the homogeneous counterpart.
The proof of Theorem
4.2
is now based on Proposition
4.2
where, unlike Proposition
4.1
, we provide a bounded approximating sequence for elements in
Y
.

In what follows, we define
[u]_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
[u]_{-}:=|u|-[u]_{+}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
Y
be as in (
4.7
). Let
u\in Y
be a non-negative function. Then, there exists a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset Y\cap L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of non-negative functions such that

(i)

0\leq u_{n}(x)\leq u(x)
;

(ii)

u_{n}\to u
in
Y
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

If
Y=\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
1<p<\frac{d}{s}
, by
[
10
]
*Lemmas A.1, B.1 there exists a non-negative sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging to
u
in
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Similarly, if
Y=W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, thanks to the
L^{p}
-integrability,
[
10
]
*Lemma B.1 easily adapts to the cases
p\geq\frac{d}{s}
as well. As a result, for any
0\leq u\in Y
we can find a non-negative sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging to
u
in
Y
. Next, we define

u_{n}:=\min\left\{\varphi_{n},u\right\}=u-{[\varphi_{n}-u]}_{-}.

By construction,
0\leq u_{n}\leq u
.
Moreover, from
\big[[\,\cdot\,]_{\pm}\big]_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq[\,\cdot\,]_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
(cf.
[
24
]
*page
3
for the case
p=2
, the case
p\neq 2
following by the same argument) and (
4.6
), we conclude that
u_{n}\in Y\cap L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and converges to
u
in
Y
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
Y
be as in (
4.7
). Let
T\in{Y}^{\prime}\cap L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u\in Y
. If
Tu\geq-|f|
for some
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
then
Tu\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\langle T,u\rangle_{Y^{\prime},\,Y}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T(x)u(x)dx.

Proof.

Let
u\in Y
. Then,
[u]_{\pm}\in Y
and, by Proposition
4.2
there exist two sequences
{(u^{\pm}_{n})}_{n}\subset Y\cap L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
converging to
[u]_{\pm}
in
Y
such that
0\leq u^{\pm}_{n}(x)\leq[u(x)]_{\pm}
a.e. Then, the function
\bar{u}_{n}:=u^{+}_{n}-u^{-}_{n}
converges to
u
in
Y
. Furthermore, by a direct computation we have that

|\bar{u}_{n}(x)|\leq|u(x)|
and
\bar{u}_{n}(x)u(x)\geq 0
. Next, by approximating
\bar{u}_{n}\in Y\cap L^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
via convolution with mollifiers
(\rho_{k})_{k}
(see again
[
10
]
*Lemma A.1), we notice that for every fixed
n

\begin{split}\langle T,\bar{u}_{n}\rangle_{Y^{\prime},\,Y}&=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}T(x)(\bar{u}_{n}*\rho_{k})(x)dx\\
&=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\text{supp}(\bar{u}_{n})}(T*\rho_{k})(x)\bar{u}_{n}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}T(x)\bar{u}_{n}(x)dx.\end{split}

Then, by arguing as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
we infer that
Tu\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\begin{split}\langle T,u\rangle_{Y^{\prime},\,Y}=\!\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle T,\bar{u}_{n}\rangle_{Y^{\prime},\,Y}\!=\!\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}T(x)\bar{u}_{n}(x)dx\!=\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}T(x)u(x)dx,\end{split}

concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Because of the equivalence between
Y
and
X
if
p=2
and
0<s<1
(see e.g.,
[
5
]
*Propositions 1.37, 1.59), Theorem
4.2
provides a sharper result than Theorem
4.1
in this range of the parameters.

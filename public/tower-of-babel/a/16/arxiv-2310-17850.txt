Lemma 4

Let

Proof.

Let

\chi_{1}^{m}=1
if and only if

\displaystyle m\langle\mu,\gamma_{1*}^{-1}\rangle=(\det\gamma)^{-1}(\mu_{1}md-\mu_{2}mb)\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}\qquad\text{for all $\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$.}

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{m}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\in\begin{cases}\mathrm{lcm}\Big(\frac{\det\gamma}{\gcd(b,\det\gamma)},\frac{\det\gamma}{\gcd(d,\det\gamma)}\Big)\,\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $j=1$,}\\
\mathrm{lcm}\Big(\frac{\det\gamma}{\gcd(a,\det\gamma)},\frac{\det\gamma}{\gcd(c,\det\gamma)}\Big)\,\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $j=2$.}\end{cases}

On the other hand, using elementary properties of the greatest common divisor one shows that

\displaystyle\frac{|x|\gcd(x,y,z)}{\gcd(x,y)\gcd(x,z)}=\gcd\!\Big(\frac{x}{\gcd(x,z)},\frac{x}{\gcd(x,y)}\Big)\qquad\text{for all $x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}$ with $x\not=0$.}

Finally the lemma follows from the remark that

4.1.
Some elements of the Ehrhart theory of polytopes

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a rational convex polytope in the

\mathcal{P}
is convex and it has vertices in

for all

is an integral polytope, so

has vertices in

Theorem

(Ehrhart, 1962)

If

where each

\mathrm{G}_{i}\big(\mathcal{P},\,m+\mathrm{den}(\mathcal{P})\big)=\mathrm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{P},m)\qquad\qquad\text{for all $m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.}

Thanks to the theorem above we say that
\mathrm{G}(\mathcal{P},m)
is a quasi-polynomial in

is the
i
th coefficient of

for any

. In the case when
\mathcal{P}
has vertices in

is actually a polynomial in

are constants and they have furthermore a geometric meaning. For instance, if

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}(\mathcal{P},m)=\mathrm{area}(\mathcal{P})m^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\#(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\partial\mathcal{P})m+1\qquad\qquad\text{for all $m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.}

In particular, Equation (
24

For any

, denote by

the smallest positive integer

Then we obviously have

for all

. However, we will need more refined information for which an additional concept of geometric nature is required.

For any

, each face of
\mathcal{P}
of dimension

i

\mathcal{P}
. For any

of

\displaystyle\mathrm{aff}(M):=\Bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}x_{j}\ \Big|\ m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\ \alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R},\ \sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}=1,\ x_{j}\in M\Bigg\}.

Then we define the

of

as the smallest positive integer

Theorem

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}_{i}(m\mathcal{P},t)=m^{i}\mathrm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{P},mt)\qquad\qquad\text{for all $i\in\{0,\dots,\dim(\mathcal{P})\}$.}

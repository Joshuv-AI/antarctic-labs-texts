Number of integers represented by families of binary forms II: binomial forms
Étienne Fouvry, Michel Waldschmidt

Abstract
We consider some families of binary binomial forms a ​ X d + b ​ Y d aX^{d}+bY^{d} , with a a and b b integers. Under suitable assumptions, we prove that every rational integer m m with | m | ≥ 2 |m|\geq 2 is only represented by a finite number of the forms of this family (with varying d , a , b d,a,b ). Furthermore the number of such forms of degree ≥ d 0 \geq d_{0} representing m m is bounded by O ⁡ ( | m | ( 1 / d 0 ) + ϵ ) O(|m|^{(1/d_{0})+\epsilon}) uniformly for | m | ≥ 2 |m|\geq 2 . We also prove that the integers in the interval [ − N , N ] [-N,N] represented by one of the form of the family with degree d ≥ d 0 d\geq d_{0} are almost all represented by some form of the family with degree d = d 0 d=d_{0} . In a previous paper we investigated the particular case where the binary binomial forms are positive definite. We now treat the general case by using a lower bound for linear forms of logarithms.

Definition 2.1

We will say that an infinite set

3
Isomorphisms between binomial binary forms and their automorphisms

We recall the action of the group of matrices
{\rm GL}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
on the set
{\rm Bin}(d,{\mathbb{Q}})
of binary forms with degree
d
, with rational coefficients and with non zero discriminant. If
F=F(X,Y)
and
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}\end{pmatrix}
respectively belong to
{\rm Bin}(d,{\mathbb{Q}})
and
{\rm GL}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
, we define

(F\circ\gamma)(X,Y)=F(a_{1}X+a_{2}Y,a_{3}X+a_{4}Y).

By definition, we say that the two forms
F
and
G
are isomorphic if and only if there exists
\gamma\in{\rm GL}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
F\circ\gamma=G.

The group of automorphisms of a form
F
is

{\rm Aut}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\gamma\in{\rm GL}(2,{\mathbb{Q}}):F\circ\gamma=F\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
a
,
b
,
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
be integers different from zero. Then the two binary forms
aX^{d}+bY^{d}
and
a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}
are isomorphic if and only if at least one of the following two conditions hold

1.

the ratios
a/a^{\prime}
and
b/b^{\prime}
are both
d
–th powers of a rational number,

2.

the ratios
a/b^{\prime}
and
b/a^{\prime}
are both
d
–th powers of a rational number.

Proof.

The proof is an extension of the proof of
[
FW2
, Lemma 1.14]

which worked under the restrictions that
d
is an even integer and
a
,
b
,
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
are all positive. We quickly give the necessary modifications to obtain
Proposition
3.1
. Indeed the beginning of the proof of
[
FW2
, Lemma 1.14]
does not require these restrictions. They are only used at the very last item of the proof where we appeal to them to prove that if
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{2}&a_{4}\end{pmatrix}
with
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}\neq 0
, the equality

F_{a,b,d}\circ\gamma=F_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime},d}

cannot hold. Indeed, if it holds, a computation leads to the equalities

\frac{a_{1}}{a_{3}}=-\frac{b}{a}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}\right)^{d-1},\ \left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{3}}\right)^{2}=-\frac{b}{a}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}\right)^{d-2}.

Dividing the second equality by the first one we obtain the equality
a_{1}/a_{3}=a_{2}/a_{4}
. This is impossible since
\det\gamma\neq 0
.
∎

Let

5
A diophantine result

The central tool in the proof of Theorem
4.1
is a lower bound coming from the theory of linear forms in logarithms, more precisely
[
W
, Corollary 9.22]
. The usual height of the rational number
p/q
, written under its irreducible form, is defined by

\mathrm{H}(p/q)=\max\{|p|,q\}
and its logarithmic height is

\mathrm{h}(p/q)=\log\mathrm{H}(p/q)=\log\max\{|p|,q\}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2}
be rational numbers,
b_{1},b_{2}
be positive integers,
A_{1},A_{2},B
be real positive numbers. We suppose for
j=1,2
that

B\geq\max\{\mathrm{e},\;b_{1},\;b_{2}\},\hskip 10.00002pt\log A_{j}\geq\max\{\mathrm{h}(a_{j}),\;1\}.

Then, if
a_{1}^{b_{1}}a_{2}^{b_{2}}\not=1
, we have the inequality

|a_{1}^{b_{1}}a_{2}^{b_{2}}-1|\geq\exp\left\{-C(\log B)(\log A_{1})(\log A_{2})\right\}

with
C=2^{79}3^{15}
.

This lower bound follows from Corollary 9.22 in
[
W
, p. 308]
by taking

D=1,\hskip 10.00002ptm=2,\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{1}=a_{1},\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{2}=a_{2}

and the constant
C(m)
defined in
[
W
, p. 252]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
d
,
a
,
b
,
x
and
y
be rational integers. Let

\mathcal{A}:=\max\{|a|,|b|\},\hskip 10.00002ptX:=\max\{|x|,|y|\}.

,

Lemma 7.3

Let

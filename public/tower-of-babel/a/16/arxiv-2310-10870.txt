Maximum Principles and Consequences for γ \gamma -translators in ℝ n + 1 {\mathbb{R}}^{n+1} II
José Torres Santaella

Abstract
. This paper focuses on the translating solitons of fully nonlinear extrinsic curvature geometric flows in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} . We present a generalization of the Spruck-Xiao’s and Spruck-Sun’s convexity results for 1 1 -homogeneous convex/concave curvature functions, and further provide several characterizations of the family of grim reaper cylinders under curvature constraints.

Introduction

Extrinsic geometric flows (EGFs) belong to the theory of geometric flows, which are powerful tools for investigating the deformation of geometric objects such as manifolds, maps, tensors, or geometric structures over time. The goal is to simplify a geometric quantity (usually by making it constant) and characterize the resulting deformation in terms of the shape, curvature and topology of the geometric object.

In the theory of EGFs, we study the evolution/deformation of an initial immersed hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}=F_{0}(\Sigma)
in a Riemannian manifold (in this paper,
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with its Euclidean metric structure). The speed and direction of this deformation are determined by extrinsic geometric objects of the evolution
\Sigma_{t}=F(\Sigma,t)
, where
F:\Sigma\times(0,T)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a
1
-parameter family of immersion.

More precisely, we say that
\Sigma_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
evolves under an EGF given by the curvature function
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
, or
\gamma
-flow for short, if there exists a
1
-parameter family of immersions
F:\Sigma\times[0,T)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left<{\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial t}(x,t),\nu(x,t)}\right>=\gamma(\lambda(x,t)),\mbox{ in }\Sigma\times(0,T),\\
F(x,0)=F_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
\lambda(x,t)=(\lambda_{1}(x,t),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(x,t))\in\Gamma
, is the principal curvature vector at
F(x,t)\in\Sigma_{t}
,
\left<{\cdot,\cdot}\right>
and
\nu(x,t)
, respectively, are the euclidean inner product and the positively oriented (see Sec.
2
) unit normal vector of
\Sigma_{t}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, respectively.

Furthermore, we consider the class of
1
-homogeneous curvature function

\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty),

where
\Gamma_{+}=\left\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0\right\}\subset\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a symmetric open cone and the following properties hold:

(1)

\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
is a smooth symmetric, i.e.,
\gamma((\lambda_{\sigma(i)}))=\gamma((\lambda_{i}))
for every permutation
\sigma

(2)

\gamma
is positively

\gamma(c\lambda)=c\gamma(\lambda)

(3)

\gamma

\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0

(4)

\gamma

\Gamma

To illustrate, in one dimension we only have the curve shortening flow (CSF) in this class of EGFs, where
\gamma(\lambda)=\lambda
. A relevant result of CSF is that simple closed initial curves collapse into a round point in finite time, i.e., once the curve becomes convex, it deforms into a shrinking circle before collapsing. This phenomenon is known as the development of a singularity under the flow, which is commonly studied with the blow-up rate of a certain extrinsic curvature term of
\Sigma_{t}
.

In higher dimensions, there is no unique analog to CSF due to the presence of multiple principal direction which a hypersurface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is curved. For this reason, we mention some examples of EGFs that have been of interest due to their diverse applications in geometry and topology: The mean curvature flow (MCF), where the

The class of

More specifically, a flow is called contractive when strictly convex (or convex, in the case of the MCF) closed (compact without boundary) initial data collapse to a round point in finite time. Unlike the CSF, in higher dimensions, a normalization of the
\gamma

Preliminaries

In this section, we focus on the equations related to the extrinsic geometry of
\gamma
-translators in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
is a fixed curvature function.

Firstly, we note that the principal curvatures of a hypersurface
\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
are the eigenvalues of the shape operator
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}:T_{p}\Sigma\to T_{p}\Sigma
defined by
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}(X)=-\nabla_{X}\nu
, where
T_{p}\Sigma
is the tangent space at
p\in\Sigma
,
\nu
is the positively oriented unitary normal vector field of
\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. With this orientation, the principal curvatures of a convex graph are non-negative.

Furthermore, in local coordinates, the shape operator is represented by the matrix
A^{i}_{j}=g^{ik}A_{kj}
, where

g^{ij}
denotes the coefficients of the inverse of the metric tensor
g=(g_{ij})
of
\Sigma
, and
A=(A_{ij})
denotes the coefficients of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
. We note that Einstein’s notation of repeated indexes is used, and will be used throughout this paper as well. To elaborate, the second fundamental form corresponds to the bilinear symmetric form related to the Shape operator, i.e:
A(X,Y)=\left<{-\nabla_{X}\nu,Y}\right>
. In local coordinate the entries are given by
A_{ij}=\left<{-\nabla_{i}\nu,\tau_{j}}\right>
, where
\left\{\tau_{i}\right\}
denotes a local frame of
T_{p}\Sigma
and
\nabla_{i}\nu=\nabla_{\tau_{i}}\nu
.

Next, we describe how the properties defining a curvature function influence the differential operators in the main equations related to
\gamma
-translators in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

(1)

Property
1
implies the existence of a smooth function

\boldsymbol{\gamma}:\left\{A\in\mbox{Sym}(n):\lambda(A)\in\Gamma\right\}\to{\mathbb{R}}

such that
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(A)=\gamma(\lambda)
when
A=\mbox{diag}(\left\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right\})
. Here
\mbox{Sym}(n)
denotes the vector space of real symmetric matrices.

(2)

Property
2
allows us to write for any diagonal matrix
A=\mbox{diag}(\left\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right\})
that
\dfrac{\partial\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{\partial A_{ab}}(A)=\delta_{ab}\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{a}}(\lambda)
, where
\delta_{ab}=1
, if
a=b
, and
\delta_{ab}=0
otherwise,

Furthermore, if the principal curvature are simple, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}<\ldots<\lambda_{n}
, then

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{\partial A_{ab}\partial A_{cd}}(A)T_{ab}T_{cd}=\dfrac{\partial^{2}\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{a}\partial\lambda_{b}}(\lambda)T_{aa}T_{bb}+2\sum_{a<b}\dfrac{\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{b}}(\lambda)-\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{a}}(\lambda)}{\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a}}|T_{ab}|^{2},

for every symmetric matrix

. In particular, if
\gamma
is convex (resp. concave), the above term has a non-negative (resp. non-positive) sign.

Throughout this paper, we will abuse notation by using the same symbol for smooth symmetric functions evaluated at both
\lambda\in\Gamma
and

\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a complete
\gamma
-translator with
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\alpha}=\left\{\lambda\in\Gamma:\alpha|A|\leq H\right\}

\alpha\in(0,1)
. In addition, through this section we label the principal curvatures as
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
.

Then, by Lemma
2.5
,
\gamma
is an uniformly elliptic curvature function, i.e., there exists a positive constant
C=C(\alpha,\gamma,\Gamma)
such that
C^{-1}H\leq\gamma\leq CH
. This implies that
\Sigma
has uniformy bounded second fundamental form, since
\gamma=\left<{\nu,e_{n+1}}\right>\leq 1
and
|A|\leq C\alpha^{-1}\gamma
in
\Sigma
.

Moreover, since
\alpha<1
, we have

(9)

\displaystyle-1<c(\alpha)=\alpha^{2}-1<\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}<0,

for every
p\in\Sigma^{-}=\left\{p\in\Sigma:\lambda_{1}(p)<0\right\}
.

We begin by proving Thm.
1.1
for the case where
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
is a concave curvature function with
(0,1)\in\Gamma
. For this purpose, we consider the function
h:\Sigma\to(0,\infty)
given by

\displaystyle h(p)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda_{2}}=\gamma\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}},1\right).

We note that
h(p)
is smooth in the open set
\Sigma^{-}
, as it does not contain any umbilical points of
\Sigma
. Moreover, since
\gamma
is strictly increasing in each argument,
h(p)\in(\gamma(c(\alpha),1),\gamma(1,1)]
.

Subsequently, we may write

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-}=\left\{p\in\Sigma:h(p)\in\left(\gamma(c(\alpha),1),\gamma(0,1)\right)\right\}.

Therefore, the convexity of
\Sigma
will follow by establishing that

\displaystyle I=\inf\limits_{p\in\Sigma}h(p)\geq\gamma(0,1).

In the following lemmas, we will argue by contradiction that
I\in(\gamma(c(\alpha),1),\gamma(0,1))
cannot occur.

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
h(p)
cannot attain an interior minimum in
\Sigma^{-}
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction, assuming that there exists a point
p_{0}\in\Sigma^{-}
such that
h(p_{0})=I
. Then, by choosing an adapted frame of principal directions
\tau_{i}
around a fixed point
p\in\Sigma^{-}
, the equations in Lemma
2.1
hold. Therefore, we have the following equations related to
h
:

(10)

\displaystyle\nabla h=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\lambda_{2}}-h\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}h=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Delta_{\gamma}\gamma}{\lambda_{2}}-\left<{\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\lambda_{2}},\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}}\right>_{\gamma}-\left<{\nabla h,\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}}\right>_{\gamma}+h\left|\left|\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}-\dfrac{h}{\lambda_{2}}\Delta_{\gamma}\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\left<{\nabla h,\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}}\right>_{\gamma}-\nabla_{3}h+\dfrac{h}{\lambda_{2}}\left(\partial^{2}\gamma^{ab;cd}\nabla_{2}A_{ab}\nabla_{2}A_{cd}-2\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}\right).

Then, since at
p\in\Sigma^{-}
the principal curvatures are simple, Property
3
implies

\displaystyle\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|^{2}_{\gamma}=(\nabla_{1}A_{12},\nabla_{2}A_{12})\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial A_{11}}&\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial A_{12}}\\
\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial A_{21}}&\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial A_{22}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{1}A_{12}\\
\nabla_{2}A_{12}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

Moreover, since
\gamma
is a concave curvature function, we observe that

\displaystyle\partial^{2}\gamma^{ab;cd}\nabla_{2}A_{ab}\nabla_{2}A_{cd}=\dfrac{\partial^{2}\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{a}\partial\lambda_{b}}\nabla_{2}A_{aa}\nabla_{2}A_{bb}+2\sum_{a<b}\dfrac{\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{b}}-\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{a}}}{\lambda_{b}-\lambda_{a}}|\nabla_{2}A_{ab}|^{2}\leq 0.

Therefore, we obtain the following differential inequality

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}h+2\left<{\nabla h,\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}}\right>_{\gamma}+\nabla_{3}h=\dfrac{h}{\lambda_{2}}\left(\partial^{2}\gamma^{ab;cd}\nabla_{2}A_{ab}\nabla_{2}A_{cd}-2\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}\right)\leq 0.

Consequently, since
h(p)
reaches its minimum at
p_{0}\in\Sigma^{-}
, by the strong maximum principle, we conclude that
h
is constant on
\Sigma^{-}
.

Now, since
h
is constant on
\Sigma^{-}
, from Eq. (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}\mbox{ in }\Sigma^{-}.

This implies that
0=\nabla_{1}A_{12}=\nabla_{2}A_{12}
, and by the Gauss-Codazzi equations, we observe that
\nabla_{2}A_{11}=\nabla_{1}A_{22}=0
in
\Sigma^{-}
. In other words,
\left\{\tau_{i}\right\}
is a parallel orthonormal frame of
\Sigma^{-}
, and therefore,
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=0
in
\Sigma^{-}
, gives a contradiction since
\lambda_{1}(p_{0})<0<\lambda_{2}(p_{0})
.
∎

Next, we consider the case where the infimum is attained at infinity in
\Sigma^{-}
, i.e., there is an unbounded sequence
p_{k}\in\Sigma^{-}
such that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}h(p_{k})=I
. To address this, we apply Lemma
2.4
on
\Sigma^{-}
(possibly after considering a subsequence) to obtain a sequence
p_{k}\in\Sigma^{-}
such that

(12)

\displaystyle h(p_{k})\to I,\>|\nabla h(p_{k})|\to 0,\text{ and }\Delta_{\gamma}h(p_{k})\to\delta\geq 0\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Then, the sequence of surfaces

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma-p_{k}=\left\{p-p_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:p\in\Sigma\right\}

satisfies the following:
\Sigma_{k}
is a
\gamma
-translator,
0\in\Sigma_{k}
, and possess uniformly bounded second fundamental form for every

. Therefore, by compactness, the sequence
\Sigma_{k}
possesses a subsequence that converge smoothly on compact subset to a surface
\Sigma_{\infty}
, which is not necessarily connected. However, we note that smooth convergence on compact sets implies that
\Sigma_{\infty}
is a complete
\gamma
-translator.

We claim that the connected component
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
of
\Sigma_{\infty}
that contains the origin is a vertical plane. Indeed, if this were not the case, then
\gamma>0
on
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
, and consequently, the function
h(p)
attains a positive infimum at the origin of
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
contradicting Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The infimum
I
cannot be attained at infinity on
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Firstly, we note that the quadratic form given by

(13)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{2}(p_{k})}A(\cdot,\cdot)(p_{k})

will converge to a quadratic form in
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
with eigenvalues
1
and
a<0
. To see this, we first note that the
1
-homogeneity of
\gamma
implies that

\displaystyle\gamma(\lambda)=\partial_{1}\gamma(\lambda)\lambda_{1}+\partial_{2}\gamma(\lambda)\lambda_{2}.

Then, for every
p_{k}\in\Sigma^{-}
, we observe that

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\partial_{2}\gamma}{\partial_{1}\gamma}-\dfrac{\partial_{2}\gamma\lambda_{2}+\partial_{1}\gamma\lambda_{1}}{\partial_{1}\gamma\lambda_{2}}=\dfrac{\partial_{2}\gamma-h}{\partial_{1}\gamma}\geq C\left(\partial_{2}\gamma(\lambda_{1},1)-h\right),

in the last inequality we used that
\gamma
is uniformly elliptic and
\partial_{2}\gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
is decreasing in the second argument do to the concavity of
\gamma(\lambda)
. Consequently, the claim follows since

\displaystyle a=\lim_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\leq C(I-\partial_{2}\gamma(0,1))=C(I-\gamma(0,1))<0,

where
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}h(p_{k})=I<\gamma(0,1)
,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{i}(p_{k})=0
and
\gamma(0,1)=\partial_{2}\gamma(0,1)
.

On the other hand, by taking derivatives of
\gamma
at
p_{k}
, we observe that

(14)

\displaystyle\nabla\gamma=\nabla\left<{\nu,e_{3}}\right>=A(e_{3}^{\top},\cdot),

where
(\cdot)^{\top}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
T\Sigma
. At
p_{k}
, we have that
e_{3}^{\top}\to e_{3}
as
k\to\infty
since
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
is a vertical plane passing through the origin. This gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\lambda_{2}}(p_{k})\to\vec{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\mbox{ as }k\to\infty,

because the eigenvalues of the quadratic form (
13
) are non-zero. Consequently, from equations (
10
) and (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle h(p_{k})\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}(p_{k})}{\lambda_{2}(p_{k})}\to\vec{v},\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Furthermore, by writing
\nabla h
differently, we see that

\displaystyle\nabla h=\dfrac{\partial_{1}\gamma\nabla\lambda_{1}+\partial_{2}\gamma\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}-h\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}.

Then, by multiplying the above equation by
h
, we have

\displaystyle h\nabla h

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{2}\gamma-h\right)h\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}+h\partial_{1}\gamma\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}.

Consequently, by evaluating at
p_{k}
and taking limits, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\gamma h\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\to-\left(\partial_{2}\gamma(a,1)-I\right)\vec{v},\>\partial_{2}\gamma h\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\to\partial_{2}\gamma(a,1)\vec{v}.

On the other hand, by multiplying Eq. (
11
) by
h
, we see that

(15)

\displaystyle h\Delta_{\gamma}h+2h\left<{\nabla h,\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}}\right>_{\gamma}+h\nabla_{3}h\leq-2h^{2}\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})}.

Moreover, we can write the right-hand side of the above equation as

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{1-\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}}\left(\partial_{1}\gamma(\lambda)\left(\dfrac{h|\nabla_{2}\lambda_{1}|}{\lambda_{2}}\right)^{2}+\partial_{2}\gamma(\lambda)\left(\dfrac{h|\nabla_{1}\lambda_{2}|}{\lambda_{2}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\to\dfrac{1}{1-a}\left(\dfrac{(\partial_{2}\gamma(a,1)-I)^{2}|v_{2}|^{2}}{\partial_{2}\gamma(a,1)}+\dfrac{|v_{1}|^{2}}{\partial_{2}\gamma(a,1)}\right)>0,

as

, where

and
\tau_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\tau_{i}(p_{k})
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{0}\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
.

Consequently, by taking limits in Eq.
15
at

, we obtain
0\leq\delta I<0
, and therefore, that infimum of

cannot be attained at infinity, finalizing the proof.
∎

Now, we proceed with the case where
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
is a convex curvature function. The proof will follow the same steps as in the concave case but will use a different function. Therefore, we will only highlight the main differences from the previous case.

We consider the function
j:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

. We note that
j(p)
is continuous and bounded, since

\displaystyle\dfrac{c(\alpha)}{\gamma(1,1)}\leq\dfrac{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}}{\gamma\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}},1\right)}=\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma(\lambda)}\leq\dfrac{C\lambda_{1}}{H}\leq C.

Moreover, as in the previous case,

is smooth in
\Sigma^{-}
.

Consequently,
\Sigma
will be convex if we show that
J=\inf\limits_{\Sigma}j(p)\geq 0
. We will achieve this by assuming the contrary, i.e.,
J\in\left(\dfrac{c(\alpha)}{\gamma(1,1)},0\right)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The function
j(p)
cannot attain a local negative minimum in
\Sigma^{-}
.

Proof.

Let us assume that there exists
p_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
j(p_{0})=J
. Then, in an adapted frame of principal directions at
p\in\Sigma
, the following equations hold:

(16)

\displaystyle\nabla j=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}-j\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}j=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Delta_{\gamma}\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}-\left<{\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\gamma^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}},\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}-\left<{\nabla j,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}+j\left|\left|\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}-j\dfrac{\Delta_{\gamma}\gamma}{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\left<{\nabla j,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}-\nabla_{3}j-\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\left(\partial^{2}\gamma^{ab;cd}\nabla_{1}A_{ab}\nabla_{1}A_{cd}+2\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}\right).

Moreover, we observe that

(17)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}j+2\left<{\nabla j,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}+\nabla_{3}j=-\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\gamma}{\partial A_{ab}\partial A_{cd}}\nabla_{1}A_{ab}\nabla_{1}A_{cd}+2\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}\right).

Therefore, by the convexity of
\gamma
, we have that the right-hand side of Eq. (
17
) is nonpositive. Finally, we note that from this point onward, Lemma
3.3
is satisfied by arguments analogous to those used at the end of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

As in the concave case, by the Omori-Yau maximum principle, we may choose a sequence
p_{k}\in\Sigma^{-}
such that

(18)

\displaystyle j(p_{k})\to J<0,\>|\nabla j(p_{k})|\to 0\mbox{ and }\Delta_{\gamma}j(p_{k})\to\epsilon\in[0,\infty),\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Then, the sequence of
\gamma
-translators

possesses a subsequence that converges smoothly on compact sets to a surface
\Sigma_{\infty}
. In addition, as in the concave case, we have that the connected component
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
of
\Sigma_{\infty}
that contains the origin is a vertical plane.

Lemma 3.4
.

The function
j(p)
cannot attain a negative infimum at infinity in
\Sigma^{-}
.

Proof.

Firstly, we observe that the quadratic form
\gamma^{-1}(p_{k})A(\cdot,\cdot)

will converge to a quadratic form in
\Sigma_{\infty}^{\prime}
with eigenvalues

and

, since

\displaystyle b=\lim_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{\lambda_{2}(p_{k})}{\gamma(p_{k})}\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}-\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(p_{k})}{\gamma(p_{k})}\geq-J>0.

In addition, by Eq. (

is a vertical hyperplane passing through the origin, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}(p_{k})\to\vec{w}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Then, by combining equations (
16
) with (

)
we obtain
\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}(p_{k})\to J\vec{w}
as

. Furthermore, by writing

differently, we get

\displaystyle\nabla j

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}-\dfrac{j}{\gamma}(\partial_{1}\gamma\nabla\lambda_{1}+\partial_{2}\gamma\nabla\lambda_{2})=\left(1-j\partial_{1}\gamma\right)\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}-j\partial_{2}\gamma\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\gamma}.

In particular, after taking limit at

as

, we obtain

\displaystyle j\partial_{2}\gamma\dfrac{\nabla\lambda_{2}}{\gamma}\to\left(1-J\partial_{1}\gamma\left(\dfrac{J}{b},1\right)\right)J\vec{w}.

Here, we used that
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}(p_{k})}{\lambda_{2}(p_{k})}\to\dfrac{J}{b}<0
and

0
-homogeneous function.

Then, after multiply Eq. (

, we have

(19)

\displaystyle j\Delta_{\gamma}j+2j\left<{\nabla j,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}+j\nabla_{3}j\geq-\dfrac{2j}{\gamma}\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}.

Additionally, we note that at

\displaystyle\dfrac{2j}{\gamma}\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{12}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle\to\dfrac{-2J^{2}}{1-\frac{b}{J}}\left(\partial_{1}\gamma(b^{-1}J,1)|w_{2}|^{2}+\dfrac{(1-J\partial_{1}\gamma(b^{-1}J,1))}{\partial_{2}\gamma(b^{-1}J,1)}|w_{1}|^{2}\right)<0,

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
n\geq 3

In this section we will prove Thm.
1.1
for dimension

. In this case, we assume that
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is a complete
\gamma
-translator such that the principal curvatures are labeled by

and belong to

\displaystyle\tilde{\Gamma}=\left\{\lambda\in\Gamma:H>0,\mbox{ and }\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0\right\}.

Firstly, since

, we have that

, and therefore

\displaystyle|A|^{2}=H^{2}-2S_{2}(\lambda)\leq H^{2}.

Furthermore, we note that
\tilde{\Gamma}
is compactly supported in
\Gamma

\overline{\tilde{\Gamma}}\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

, for all

. Consequently, since
\gamma
is
1
-homogeneous, we have
\gamma H^{-1}\in[C^{-1},C]
in
\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}\cap\left\{\lambda_{1}<0\right\}
.

Next, throughout the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will adopt the notation established in
[
21
]
. This will allow us to extend the techniques from the previous section to higher dimensions effectively.

Let
\left\{E_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{m}
be the eigendistributions associated with the second fundamental form

,where

is the positive unit normal vector field of

. Then, the set

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}^{-}=\left\{p\in\Sigma^{-}:E_{i}(p)\mbox{ is constant on a neighborhood of }p\right\}

Furthermore, for every

, we choose the associated orthonormal basis of principal directions

\displaystyle\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d_{1}}\in E_{1},\>\tau_{d_{1}+1},\ldots,\tau_{d_{2}}\in E_{2},\ldots,\tau_{d_{1}+d_{2}+\ldots d_{m-1}},\ldots,\tau_{n}\in E_{m},

associated with

, counted without multiplicity.

Now, we begin with the case where
\gamma
is a strictly convex curvature function in off-radial directions, such that

. For this purpose, we consider the function

(20)

\displaystyle g=f\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma(\lambda)}\right),\mbox{ where }f(r)=\begin{cases}-r^{4}e^{\frac{-1}{r^{2}}}&,\mbox{if }r<0,\\
0&,\mbox{if }r\geq 0.\end{cases}

This function satisfies

and
f^{\prime}>0
where

, for any

Moreover, we note that
g(p)
is bounded in
\Sigma
, since

\displaystyle\dfrac{-1}{C(n-2)}\leq\dfrac{-\lambda_{1}}{CH}\leq\dfrac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\gamma}<\dfrac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma}\leq\ldots\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{n}}{\gamma}\leq\dfrac{H}{\gamma}\leq 1,

and it is smooth on
\Sigma^{-}
. Then, as in the previous section, we have the following equations for

in
\Sigma_{A}^{-}
:

(21)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}g

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}\left(\dfrac{\nabla_{i}\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}-\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma^{2}}\nabla_{i}\gamma\right).

\displaystyle L_{\gamma}g

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\gamma}g+2\left<{\nabla g,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}+\nabla_{n+1}g

\displaystyle=f^{\prime\prime}\left|\left|\nabla\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\right)\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}-\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{\gamma}\left(\partial^{2}\gamma^{\>ab;cd}\nabla_{1}A_{ab}\nabla_{1}A_{cd}+2\dfrac{\left|\left|\nabla A_{1i}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Next, we assume by contradiction that
\Sigma
is not convex, i.e.,

\displaystyle I=\inf_{\Sigma}\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\in\left[\dfrac{-1}{C(n-2)},0\right)\Leftrightarrow\inf_{\Sigma}g(p)\in[\beta,0),\mbox{ where }\beta=f\left(I\right).

Lemma 4.1
.

The function
g
cannot attain a negative minimum on
\Sigma^{-}
.

Proof.

Let assume by contradiction that
g
attains its minimum at
p_{0}\in\Sigma^{-}
. Then, since
\Sigma_{A}^{-}
is dense in
\Sigma^{-}
, we have
L_{\gamma}(g)\leq 0

\Sigma^{-}
. Therefore, the strong maximum principle implies that
g
is constant on
\Sigma^{-}
.

Moreover, we note that on any connected component of
\Sigma_{A}^{-}
, the following equation holds:

\displaystyle\left|\left|\nabla\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\right)\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}=\partial^{2}\gamma^{\>ab;cd}\nabla_{1}A_{ab}\nabla_{1}A_{cd}=(1-\delta_{1i})\left|\left|\nabla A_{1i}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}=0.

Then, since
\gamma(\lambda)
is strictly increasing, we have at any fixed point

that the equations

\displaystyle 0=\left|\left|\nabla A_{1i}\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}=\partial\gamma^{ab}\nabla_{a}A_{1i}\nabla_{b}A_{1i}=\dfrac{\partial\gamma}{\partial\lambda_{j}}|\nabla_{j}A_{1i}|^{2}

give that

for every

. Consequently, by the Codazzi equations, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{i}\lambda_{1}=\nabla_{1}\lambda_{i},\mbox{ for }i\neq 1.

Moreover, since
\gamma
is strictly convex in off-radial directions, Lemma
2.7
implies

\displaystyle|\nabla_{1}A_{ab}|=0,\quad\text{for all }a,b.

Consequently, we have
|\nabla\lambda_{1}|=0
on every connected component of
\Sigma_{A}^{-}
. This implies that
\lambda_{1}
is constant on the connected components of
\Sigma_{A}^{-}
, as is
\gamma
, since
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}
is a negative constant because

. However, Eq. (
3
), implies that

, which is not possible since
\lambda_{1}<0<\lambda_{2}
.
∎

Next, we will use the Omori-Yau maximum principle on the sequence of translators given by
\Sigma_{k}=\left\{p+p_{k}:p\in\Sigma\right\}
, where

-homogeneity allow us to write

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}\gamma(1,0,\ldots,0,)=\gamma(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)=\partial_{1}\gamma(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)\lambda_{1},

which gives
1=\partial_{1}\gamma(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)=\partial_{1}\gamma(\lambda)
on
\Sigma
. Then, we note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\partial_{2}\gamma(\lambda)\lambda_{2}\ldots+\partial_{n}\gamma(\lambda)\lambda_{n}=\gamma(\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)=\lambda_{1}.

This implies that
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i=2,\ldots,n
since
\gamma
is increasing in each variable and
\Sigma
is convex. Therefore, the results follows by Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Now, we give the proof of Thm.
1.5
, restated here.

Theorem 5.6

(Thm.
1.5
)
.

Let
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
be a convex non-degenerate normalized curvature function.
Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a complete convex non-totally geodesic
\gamma
-translator such that there exists a point
p_{0}\in\Sigma
that satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(p_{0})=\ldots=\lambda_{n}(p_{0})=0,

where the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
are ordered by
\lambda_{n}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{1}
. Then,
\Sigma
is a Grim Reaper cylinder.
In particular, if
\Sigma
contains a straight line, then
\Sigma
is a Grim Reaper cylinder.

Proof.

Let
\left\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}\right\}
be an orthonormal frame of principal directions at
p_{0}\in\Sigma
. Then, by ordering the principal curvatures by
\lambda_{n}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{1}
, it follows that
\lambda_{i}(p_{0})=A_{ii}(p_{0})=0
for
i=2,\ldots,n
, and the entries
A_{ii}
reach a local minimum at
p_{0}
for
i=2,\ldots,n-1
.

On the other hand, we have Eq. (
4
)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}A_{ii}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}\gamma}{\partial A_{ab}\partial A_{cd}}\nabla_{i}A_{ab}\nabla_{i}A_{cd}+|A|_{\gamma}^{2}A_{ii}+\nabla_{n+1}A_{ii}=0.

Then, the strong maximum principle applied to
A_{ii}
implies that
\lambda_{i}
vanish identically on
\Sigma
for
i=2,\ldots,n-1
. Moreover, since
\sup\limits_{p\in\Sigma}\lambda_{n}(p)=\lambda_{n}(p_{0})=0
, the principal curvatures of in
\Sigma
are
(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)
and the result follows by proposition
5.4
.
∎

Next, we give the proof of Thm.
1.6
, restated here.

Theorem 5.7

(Thm.
1.6
)
.

Let
\gamma:\Gamma\to(0,\infty)
be a non-degenerate normalized concave curvature function. Let
\Sigma
be a complete convex not totally geodesic
\gamma
-translator. Then,
\Sigma
is a grim reaper cylinder if, and only if, the function
|A|^{2}\gamma^{-2}
attains a local maximum on
\Sigma
.

Proof.

We consider
f(p)=|A|^{2}\gamma^{-2}
. Then, by using an orthonormal frame
\left\{\tau_{i}\right\}\subset T_{p}\Sigma
of principal directions, we have the following equations at
p\in\Sigma
:

\displaystyle\nabla f=\dfrac{\nabla|A|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}-2f\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}f=\dfrac{\Delta_{\gamma}|A|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}-2\dfrac{f}{\gamma}\Delta_{\gamma}\gamma-4\left<{\nabla f,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}-2\dfrac{f}{\gamma^{2}}\left|\left|\nabla\gamma\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}.

Furthermore, by Proposition
2.1
, we observe that

(25)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}f+2\dfrac{\lambda_{i}}{\gamma^{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\gamma}{\partial A_{ab}A_{cd}}\nabla_{i}A_{ab}\nabla_{i}A_{cd}+\nabla_{n+1}f+2\left<{\nabla f,\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{\gamma}}\right>_{\gamma}=2\gamma^{-4}Q^{2},

where

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\left|\left|\nabla A\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}+|A|^{2}\left|\left|\nabla\gamma\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}-\gamma\left<{\nabla|A|^{2},\nabla\gamma}\right>_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\gamma\nabla A-\nabla\gamma\otimes A\right|\right|_{\gamma}^{2}\geq 0.

Then, the maximum principle applied to the Equation (
25
) implies that
f(p)
is constant on
\Sigma
, since the term

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda_{i}}{\gamma^{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\gamma}{\partial A_{ab}A_{cd}}\nabla_{i}A_{ab}\nabla_{i}A_{cd}\leq 0,

by the concavity of
\gamma
and the convexity of
\Sigma
.

In particular, the term
\gamma^{-4}Q^{2}
in Equation (
25
) must vanishes identically on
\Sigma
. This means that

(26)

\displaystyle\gamma\left(\nabla_{i}A(\tau_{k},\tau_{j})\right)-\nabla_{i}\gamma A(\tau_{k},\tau_{j})=0,\mbox{ for all }i,,j,k.

Furthermore, by the Codazzi equations we have

(27)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}\gamma)A(\tau_{j},\tau_{k})=(\nabla_{j}\gamma)A(\tau_{i},\tau_{k}),\mbox{ for all }i,j,k.

Now, we analyze two cases from Equation (
27
).

Case 1:
Let us assume that
\gamma
is a positive constant. Then, Eq. (
3
) implies that
|A|_{\gamma}^{2}\gamma=0
. But since condition
1
and
3
holds, it follows that
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i
, but this contradicts that
\Sigma
is not totally geodesic.

Case 2:
Now we choose a simply connected neighborhood
U
such that
|\nabla\gamma|\neq 0
. Then, for each point
p\in U
we choose an orthonormal frame on
T_{p}U
given by

\displaystyle\left\{\tau_{1}=\dfrac{\nabla\gamma}{|\nabla\gamma|},\tau_{2},\ldots,\tau_{n}\right\}.

We note that in this frame
\nabla_{j}\gamma=0
and
\nabla_{1}\gamma=|\nabla\gamma|
. Moreover, Equation (
27
) implies that
A(\tau_{j},\tau_{k})=0
for
k\geq 1
and
j\geq 2
.
Consequently, we have
\Sigma
posses only one nonzero principal curvature on
U
.

Unfortunately, we cannot apply Proposition
5.4
because
U
is not necessarily complete. However, we consider the nullity distribution
\mathcal{D}:\Sigma\to T\Sigma
associated to the second fundamental form
A
, i.e:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(p)=\left\{v\in T_{p}\Sigma:A(v,\cdot)=0\right\}.

It is a well know fact that
\mathcal{D}
is smooth, integrable and autoparallel, i.e: for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
we have
\nabla_{X}Y\in\mathcal{D}
. The integral submanifolds of
\mathcal{D}
are totally geodesic in
\Sigma
and their images via the immersion
F:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
are totally geodesic submanifolds of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. Moreover, the gauss map
\nu:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
of the immersion
F
is constant along the leaves of
\mathcal{D}
.

Now, let
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}=\mbox{span}(\tau_{i})
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{D}
and we fix
p_{0}\in\Sigma
and set
\tau=\tau_{1}(p_{0})
. Then, we extend
\tau
by parallel transport to a vector field along the integral curve
\alpha:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\Sigma

with direction of
\tau_{1}
passing through
p_{0}
.

Then, due to Eq. (
26
), the shape operator
{\mathcal{W}}:T\Sigma\to T\Sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\tau_{1}}{\mathcal{W}})v=\tau_{1}(\gamma)v,

for any
v\in T\Sigma
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}|\left({\mathcal{W}}-\gamma\mbox{Id}\right)\tau|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left<{\left(\nabla_{\tau_{1}}{\mathcal{W}}\right)\tau-\tau_{1}(\gamma)\tau,{\mathcal{W}}\tau-\gamma\tau}\right>=0.

Therefore
\tau=\tau_{1}
is a parallel vector field along the integral curves of

\tau_{1}
. Then, since
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
is spanned by
\tau_{1}
, we obtain that
the orthogonal complement
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
of
\mathcal{D}
is autoparallel in
T\Sigma
.

Subsequently, since
T\Sigma=\mathcal{D}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
and trivially
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
are parallel to each other, the de Rham decomposition Theorem give us that
F(U)
splits as the Cartesian product of a plane curve
G
with a euclidean
factor
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then, since
\Sigma
is
\gamma
-translator this curve must be a Grim Reaper curve.

Consequently,
F(\Sigma)
contains an open neighborhood that is part of a Grim Reaper cylinder, and by Theorem
5.1
, we finally obtain that
\Sigma
is a Grim Reaper cylinder.
∎

6.
Conclusions

In summary, in this paper, we used maximum principle-type arguments to generalize well-known results on the characterization scheme used for
H
-translators in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for fully nonlinear curvature functions

\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
with a sign on
\text{Hess}(\gamma)
.

On one hand, as a corollary of the main result and the characterization of Grim Reaper cylinders obtained in this paper we can give the proof of Corollary
1.7
:

Proof.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a
\gamma
-translator with
\gamma:\overline{\Gamma}\to[0,\infty)
being a normalized non-degenerate curvature function as in Thm.
1.1
. Then, if
\Sigma
is not strictly convex, it holds
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}
where
\Sigma^{\prime}
is strictly convex graph in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k+1}
: in the case of
\gamma
is convex, there exists a point
p_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
\lambda_{1}(p_{0})=0<\lambda_{2}(p_{0})
. Then, by Theorem
1.5
,
\Sigma
is a Grim Reaper cylinder. On the other hand, if
\gamma
concave, then
f(p)=\dfrac{|A|^{2}}{\gamma}
is a
0
-homogeneous function such that
\partial_{i}f(1,0,\ldots,0)=-2\partial_{i}\gamma(1,0,\ldots,0)
, if
i\neq 1
and
\partial_{i}f(1,0,\ldots,0)=0
, otherwise. Therefore,
f(p)
attains a local maximum at
p_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
\lambda(p_{0})=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\Gamma
. Then, by Thm.
1.6
,
\Sigma
is a Grim Reaper cylinder.
∎

Consequently, the possible graphical solutions to Eq. (
2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
under the above assumptions are the Grim Reaper cylinders and the strictly convex solutions, including among them the “bowl”-type solutions constructed in
[
17
]
.

Finally, we would like to propose the following open questions related to the characterization of convex
\gamma
-translators in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

Q1:
Let
\Sigma=\left\{(x,u(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\right\}
be a strictly convex, complete
\gamma
-translator. Under which geometric constraints does the following growth estimate

\displaystyle u(x)\leq C|x|^{\alpha+1},\quad\text{for }|x|\geq R\gg 1,\mbox{ hold ?}

This result is well-known for
\gamma=H^{\alpha}
, with the case
\alpha=1
first proven in
[
20
]
, and the case
\alpha\neq 1
in
[
4
]
.

Q2:
What class of curvature functions allows the existence of the family of
\Delta
-wing solutions? These solutions are well-known for
\gamma=H
, see
[
9
]
.

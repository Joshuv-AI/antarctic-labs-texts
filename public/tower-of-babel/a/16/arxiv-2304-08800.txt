On the Existence of H 1 H^{1} solutions for Stationary Linearized Boltzmann Equations in a Small Convex Domain
I-KUN CHEN, PING-HAN CHUANG, CHUN-HSIUNG HSIA, DAISUKE KAWAGOE AND JHE-KUAN SU

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the incoming boundary value problem for the stationary linearized Boltzmann equations in Ω ⊆ 𝐑 3 \Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{3} . For a C 2 C^{2} bounded domain with boundary of positive Gaussian curvature, the existence theory is established in H 1 ​ ( Ω × 𝐑 3 ) H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3}) provided that the diameter of the domain Ω \Omega is small enough.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we are interested in the existence and regularity theory of the following stationary linearized Boltzmann equation:

(1.1)

v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(x,v)=L(f)(x,v)\mbox{ for }(x,v)\in\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3}.

Here,
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
with
C^{2}
boundary
\partial\Omega
and
L
is the linearized collision operator.
The existence of solutions of lower regularity has been studied for linearized and full equations with various boundary conditions, see
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
14
]
.
As for regularity issue, based on existence results above, the pointwise regularity is studied in
[
5
,
10
,
11
,
6
]
.
A recent result
[
7
]
studies the regularity in fractional Sobolev spaces. It proves that solutions are in
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3},H^{s}(\Omega))
for
0\leq s<1
. Furthermore, the
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{3},H^{1}(\Omega))
regularity cannot be achieved by the estimates provided in
[
7
]
. It is natural to make a comparison with a famous regularity result
[
22
]
, in which the
W^{1,p}
regularity is proved for the time evolutional full Boltzmann equation with the diffuse boundary condition for
1\leq p<2
and a counterexample for
H^{1}
solution is provided for the free transport equation. However, one should be aware that the time evolutional problem does not provide much information for the stationary problem in regularity issue. For example, the Laplace equation can be regarded as the stationary equation of the wave equation while they behave very differently in regard of regulaity. This motivates us to study whether
H^{1}
is the critical function space for regularity of the stationary Boltzmann equation. With the help of the smallness of the domain, we prove existence of the
H^{1}
solution for the stationary linearized Boltzmann equation with the incoming boundary condition, which gives a partial answer.

We introduce the setup of problem as follows. We assume that
L(f)
satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption A
.

The operator
L(f)
can be decomposed into the multiplicative term
-\nu(v)f(x,v)
and the integral operator term

(1.2)

K(f)(x,v):=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}k(v,v^{*})f(x,v^{*})dv^{*}

such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle k(v,v^{*})=k(v^{*},v),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\nu_{0}(1+|v|)^{\gamma}\leq\nu(v)\leq\nu_{1}(1+|v|)^{\gamma},

(1.5)

\displaystyle|k(v,v^{*})|\lesssim\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|(1+|v|+|v^{*}|)^{1-\gamma}}e^{-\frac{1-\rho}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2}+(\frac{|v|^{2}-|v^{*}|^{2}}{|v-v^{*}|})^{2})},

(1.6)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{v}k(v,v^{*})|\lesssim\frac{1+|v|}{|v-v^{*}|^{2}(1+|v|+|v^{*}|)^{1-\gamma}}e^{-\frac{1-\rho}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2}+(\frac{|v|^{2}-|v^{*}|^{2}}{|v-v^{*}|})^{2})},

(1.7)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{v}\nu(v)|\lesssim(1+|v|)^{\gamma-1},

where
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
.

Here we adopt the convention
f\lesssim g
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
f\leq Cg
.

Meanwhile, the problem (
1.1
) is supplemented with the incoming boundary condition:

(1.8)

f(x,v)=g(x,v)\mbox{ for }(x,v)\in\Gamma^{-},

where

(1.9)

\Gamma^{-}:=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3}\mid n(x)\cdot v<0\}

and
n(x)
is the outward unit normal of
\partial\Omega
at
x
.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

If we adopt the idea of Grad
[
15
]
and consider the Grad angular cut-off potentials which include the hard sphere, hard potential, and Maxwellian molecular condition, then the condition of (
1.5
) and the upper bound of (
1.4
) hold. See Caflisch
[
1
]
.

(2)

It is worth mentioning that the commonly used cross section
B(|v-v^{*}|,\theta)=b|v-v^{*}|^{\gamma}\cos\theta
, where
b
is a positive constant, leads to all the estimates in Assumption A.

For the convenience of further discussion, we define

\displaystyle\tau_{x,v}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{s>0\mid x-sv\in\Omega^{c}\},

\displaystyle q(x,v)

\displaystyle:=x-\tau_{x,v}v.

With this notation, the equation (
1.1
) can be expressed by the following integral form:

(1.10)

\begin{split}f=&e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}g(q(x,v),v)+\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}e^{-\nu(v)s}Kf(x-sv,v)ds\\
=&Jg+S_{\Omega}Kf,\end{split}

where

(1.11)

\displaystyle Jg(x,v):=

\displaystyle e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}g(q(x,v),v),

(1.12)

\displaystyle S_{\Omega}h(x,v):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}e^{-\nu(v)s}h(x-sv,v)ds.

Notice that we say
f
is a solution to (
1.1
) with boundary condition (
1.8
) if
f
satisfies (
1.10
).

We assume that the domain
\Omega
possesses the following property.

Assumption B
.

\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{3}
is a
C^{2}
bounded domain such that
\partial\Omega
is of positive Gaussian curvature.

Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
L
satisfies
Assumption A
, then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that: for any domain
\Omega
satisfying
Assumption B
with
\diam(\Omega)<\epsilon
, the boundary value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.8
) has a unique solution
f\in H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
if and only if
Jg\in H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
.

We remark that the condition
Jg\in H^{1}
in the statement of Theorem
1.2
is implicit. To demonstrate that there is a wide class of functions satisfying this condition, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
g:\Gamma^{-}\to\mathbf{R}.
Suppose
g
satisfies the following conditions.

(1.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(z)dv<

\displaystyle\infty,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{\nu(v)^{2}}{|v|^{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(z)dv<

\displaystyle\infty,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{x}g(z,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(z)dv<

\displaystyle\infty,

(1.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{v}g(z,v)|^{2}N^{2}(z,v)d\Sigma(z)dv<

\displaystyle\infty,

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{v}^{-}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in\partial\Omega\mid n(x)\cdot v<0\},

\displaystyle N(x,v)

\displaystyle:=-n(q(x,v))\cdot\frac{v}{|v|},

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}h(z,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle:=g^{ij}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}h\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}h,

d\Sigma(z)
is the surface measure of
\partial\Omega
and
g^{ij}
is the
(i,j)
element of the inverse matrix of the metric tensor on
\partial\Omega
. Then we have
Jg\in H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
.

The detailed definition of
|\nabla_{x}h(z,v)|^{2}
and the proof is presented in the subsection.
It can be verified by the following standard scaling analysis:

(1.17)

v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\frac{1}{\kappa}(-\nu(v)f+K(f)),\quad(x,v)\in\Omega^{\prime}\times\mathbf{R}^{3}.

Notice that by defining
f_{\kappa}(x,v):=f(\kappa x,v)
, we have

(1.18)

v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\kappa}=-\nu(v)f_{\kappa}+K(f_{\kappa}),(x,v)\in\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3},

where
\Omega:=\frac{1}{\kappa}\Omega^{\prime}.

1.1.
Sketch of Proof

Here, we briefly sketch the idea of the proof of Theorem 1.1. A formal Picard iteration gives the following solution formula for (
1.10
)

(1.19)

f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(S_{\Omega}K)^{i}Jg.

This is a valid solution of (
1.10
) if the right hand side of (
1.19
) converges in
H^{1}
.
We first consider the
L^{2}
convergence. We have

Lemma 1.4
.

For any
h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
, we have

(1.20)

\|S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}\lesssim\diam(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}.

By Lemma
1.4
above, we can see that
S_{\Omega}K
is a contraction mapping in
L^{2}
provided the diameter of
\Omega
is small. This provides the
L^{2}
convergence of (
1.19
) in case
\diam(\Omega)
is small. However, concerning the
H^{1}
convergence of (
1.19
), we do not have a direct analogy of Lemma
1.4
. Instead, we establish the following key lemmas:

Lemma 1.5
.

Given
h\in H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
, we have

(1.21)

\|S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}\lesssim\diam(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}+C(\Omega)\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})},

where
\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}
is defined in trace sense.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
C(\Omega)
be as defined in Lemma
2.1
, then for any
h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
we have

(1.22)

\|S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}\lesssim C(\Omega)\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}.

Hence, we have

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
C(\Omega)
be as defined in Lemma
2.1
, then for any
h\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})
we have

(1.23)

\|S_{\Omega}KS_{\Omega}Kh\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}\lesssim\diam(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}+C(\Omega)^{2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}.

By Lemma
1.5
we have

\displaystyle\|(S_{\Omega}K)^{i}Jg\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\diam(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(S_{\Omega}K)^{i-1}Jg\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}+C(\Omega)^{2}\|(S_{\Omega}K)^{i-2}Jg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}.

For small
\diam(\Omega)
, we have

(1.24)

\begin{split}&\|(S_{\Omega}K)^{i}Jg\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2}\|(S_{\Omega}K)^{i-1}Jg\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}+CC(\Omega)^{2}\|(S_{\Omega}K)^{i-2}Jg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}.\end{split}

Combining (
1.24
) with Lemma
1.4
, we conclude the
H^{1}
convergence of (
1.19
) for the case where
\diam(\Omega)
is small. Notice that the constant
C(\Omega)
depends on
\Omega
. More precisely, it depends on the maximum radius of curvature. Nevertheless, it is the diameter of
\Omega
that affects the convergence, no mater what value
C(\Omega)
is. Uniqueness of the
H^{1}
solution follows from the contraction mapping argument on the
L^{2}
space.

We review an important geometric property from

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary with positive Gaussian curvature. Then, there exists a positive constant
C(\Omega)
depending on
\Omega
such that for any
z\in\partial\Omega
we have

(2.1)

|z-q(z,v)|\lesssim C(\Omega)N(z,v).

Here, we investigate some properties on integrability of
k
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\mu_{1}\geq 0
and
\mu_{2}>0
with
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}<3
, there exists a positive constant
C(\rho,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
such that

(2.2)

\sup_{v^{*}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}}}|k(v,v^{*})|^{\mu_{2}}dv<C(\rho,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Proof.

Using (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}}}|k(v,v^{*})|^{\mu_{2}}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|(1+|v|+|v^{*}|)^{1-\gamma}}e^{-\frac{1-\rho}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2}+(\frac{|v|^{2}-|v^{*}|^{2}}{|v-v^{*}|})^{2})}\right)^{\mu_{2}}dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}}}\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|^{\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}|v-v^{*}|^{2}}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|v|\leq|v-v^{*}|}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}}}\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|^{\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}|v-v^{*}|^{2}}dv

\displaystyle+\int_{|v|\geq|v-v^{*}|}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}}}\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|^{\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}|v-v^{*}|^{2}}dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|v|\leq|v-v^{*}|}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}(|v|^{2})}dv+\int_{|v|\geq|v-v^{*}|}\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2})}dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|v|\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v|^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}(|v|^{2})}dv+\int_{|v|\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}}e^{-\frac{(1-\rho)\mu_{2}}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2})}dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C.

∎

We note that, due to the symmetry of the function

, we have

(2.3)

\sup_{v\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{v^{*}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v^{*}|^{\mu_{1}}}|k(v,v^{*})|^{\mu_{2}}dv^{*}<C(\rho,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

To investigate the integrability of derivative of

,

For any

(2.4)

\int_{v^{*}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v-v^{*}|^{\mu}}e^{-\frac{1-\rho}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2}+(\frac{|v|^{2}-|v^{*}|^{2}}{|v-v^{*}|})^{2})}dv^{*}<C(\rho,\mu)(1+|v|)^{-1}.

Applying (

), we have the following estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
0<\mu<\frac{3}{2}
, there exists a positive constant
C(\mu)
such that

(2.5)

\int_{v^{*}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{v}k(v,v^{*})|^{\mu}dv^{*}<C(\mu)(1+|v|)^{\mu-1}

Proof.

Using the assumption

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{v^{*}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{v}k(v,v^{*})|^{\mu}dv^{*}\\
&\lesssim\int_{v^{*}\in\mathbf{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{1+|v|}{(1+|v|+|v^{*}|)^{\gamma}|v-v^{*}|^{2}}e^{-\frac{1-\rho}{4}(|v-v^{*}|^{2}+(\frac{|v|^{2}-|v^{*}|^{2}}{|v-v^{*}|})^{2})}\right)^{\mu}dv^{*}\\

(2.7)

\begin{split}&\lesssim(1+|v|)^{(1-\gamma)\mu}(1+|v|)^{-1}\\

Here, we use (
2.4
) in the third inequality above.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For nonnegative measurable function

, we have

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}f(x,v,s)\,dsdxdv=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{y,-u}}f(y+tu,u,t)\,dtdydu

Remark 2.6
.

For the sake of convenience, we put a proof of Lemma
2.5
in the appendix.

We define

\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\partial\Omega}h^{2}(z,v)d\Sigma(z)dv.

We decompose the proof of Lemma
1.5
into three parts. Recall that

\|S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{H^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}=\|S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|\nabla_{v}S_{\Omega}Kh\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}.

We will give estimates for each term in the right hand side of the above identity.

Notice that the first part is exactly Lemma
1.4
. The proof is as follows.

Proof of Lemma
1.4
.

First, by the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|S_{\Omega}Kh(x,v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}e^{-\nu(v)s}Kh(x-sv,v)ds\right|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}e^{-\nu(v)s}ds\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}e^{-\nu(v)s}\left|Kh(x-sv,v)\right|^{2}ds\right)dxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\tau_{x,v}\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}e^{-\nu(v)s}\left|Kh(x-sv,v)\right|^{2}dsdxdv.

We observe
\tau_{x,v}\leq\diam(\Omega)/|v|
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|S_{\Omega}Kh(x,v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\diam(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}\frac{1}{|v|}e^{-\nu(v)s}|Kh(x-sv,v)|^{2}dsdxdv.

Applying the change of variables in Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}\frac{1}{|v|}e^{-\nu(v)s}\left|Kh(x-sv,v)\right|^{2}\,dsdxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{y,-u}}\frac{1}{|u|}e^{-\nu(u)t}\left|Kh(y,u)\right|^{2}\,dtdydu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|u|}\left|Kh(y,u)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{y,-u}}e^{-\nu(u)t}\,dtdydu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|u|}\left|Kh(y,u)\right|^{2}\frac{1}{\nu(u)}\left(1-e^{\frac{-\nu(u)|y-q(x,-u)|}{|u|}}\right)\,dydu

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|u|}\left|Kh(y,u)\right|^{2}\,dydu.

Here, we observe

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|u|}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}k(u,v^{*})h(y,v^{*})\,dv^{*}\right|^{2}\,dydu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|u|}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|k(u,v^{*})|\,dv^{*}\right)\left(\int_{R^{3}}|k(u,v^{*})h^{2}(y,v^{*})|\,dv^{*}\right)\,dydu

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|u|}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}|k(u,v^{*})|h^{2}(y,v^{*})\,dv^{*}dydu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|u|}|k(u,v^{*})|h^{2}(y,v^{*})\,dudydv^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}h^{2}(y,v^{*})\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|u|}|k(u,v^{*})|\,dudydv^{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}h^{2}(y,v^{*})\,dydv^{*}.

Notice that, in the above inequalities, we use

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\left|k(v,v^{*})\right|dv\lesssim 1,\quad\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\left|v\right|}|k(v,v^{*})|\,dv\lesssim 1,

which are conclusions of Lemma
2.2
.

Summarizing the above estimates, we obtain

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|S_{\Omega}Kh(x,v)|^{2}dxdv\lesssim\diam(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}h^{2}(x,v)dxdv.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1.5
.

Observe that

\nabla_{x}S_{\Omega}Kh=S_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}Kh(x,v)+\nabla_{x}\tau_{x,v}e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}Kh(q(x,v),v).

From Lemma
1.4
, we have

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|S_{\Omega}K\nabla_{x}h(x,v)|^{2}dxdv\lesssim\diam(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}h(x,v)|^{2}dxdv.

Thus, we focus on the estimate for the second term.
It is known in
[
14
]
that,

(2.9)

\nabla_{x}\tau_{x,v}=\frac{-n(x,v)}{N(x,v)|v|}.

Notice that for fix
v
, we can parametrize
\Omega
by the part of the boundary
\Gamma^{-}_{v}
and the travel distance from it in
v
direction, i.e.,
performing the change of variables
x=z+s\frac{v}{|v|}
with
z\in\Gamma^{-}_{v}
and
0<s<|z-q(z,-v)|
. Notice that the Jacobian of the change of variables is
N(z,v)
, which works in favor with our goal. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}\tau_{x,v}e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}Kh(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{N^{2}(x,v)|v|^{2}}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}|Kh(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}\int_{0}^{|z-q(x,-v)|}e^{-2\nu(v)\frac{s}{|v|}}|g(z,v)|^{2}N(z,v)dsd\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}\int_{0}^{|z-q(z,-v)|}e^{-2\nu(v)\frac{s}{|v|}}dsd\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\diam(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(z)dv.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
g
satisfying the assumptions in Lemma
1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}Jg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{\nu(v)^{2}}{|v|^{2}}\int_{\Gamma^{-}_{v}}|g(z,v)|^{2}\,d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma^{-}_{v}}|\nabla_{x}g(z,v)|^{2}\,d\Sigma(z)dv.

Proof.

Recalling (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}Jg(x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{x}e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}})g(q(x,v),v)+e^{-\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}\nabla_{x}(g(q(x,v),v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(v)e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}\frac{n(x,v)}{N(x,v)|v|}g(q(x,v),v)+e^{-\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}\nabla_{x}(g(q(x,v),v)).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}Jg(x,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\nu(v)^{2}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}\frac{1}{N^{2}(x,v)|v|^{2}}|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}+e^{-2\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}|\nabla_{x}(g(q(x,v),v))|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\nu(v)^{2}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}\frac{1}{N^{2}(x,v)|v|^{2}}|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}+e^{-2\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}|\nabla_{x}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}|\nabla_{x}q(x,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(v)^{2}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}\frac{1}{N^{2}(x,v)|v|^{2}}|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}+e^{-2\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}|\nabla_{x}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}(x,v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{5}+I_{6}.

Here, we used the fact from
[
14
]
that

(3.3)

|\nabla_{x}q(x,v)|=\frac{1}{N(x,v)}.

By changing the coordinates again as we do in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we see that the integral of
I_{5}
is bounded.
For
I_{6}
, we also use change of variable by letting
y=q^{\prime}(x,v)
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}|\nabla_{x}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\frac{1}{N^{2}(x,v)}dxdv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{x}g(y,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(y)dv,

which is finite.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
g
satisfying the assumptions in Lemma
1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{v}Jg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbf{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma^{-}_{v}}|g(z,v)|^{2}\,d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{\nu(v)^{2}}{|v|^{2}}\int_{\Gamma^{-}_{v}}|g(z,v)|^{2}\,d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma^{-}_{v}}|\nabla_{x}g(z,v)|^{2}\,d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma^{-}_{v}}|\nabla_{v}g(z,v)|^{2}N^{2}(z,v)\,d\Sigma(z)dv.

Proof.

From (
2.11
), we have

(3.4)

\begin{split}&|\nabla_{v}e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\\
\lesssim&|(1+|v|)^{\gamma-1}\tau_{x,v}e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\\
&+\nu^{2}(v)\frac{|x-q(x,v)|^{2}}{|v|^{4}N^{2}(x,v)}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\\
&+e^{-2\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}|\nabla_{x}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\frac{|x-q(x,v)|^{2}}{|v|^{2}N(x,v)^{2}}+|e^{-\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}(\nabla_{v}g)(q(x,v),v)|^{2}.\end{split}

In what follows, we estimate the integral of each term in the right hand side of (
3.4
).

For the first term, we use change of variable. Let
x=z+sv/|v|
, where
z\in\Gamma^{-}_{v}
, so we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|(1+|v|)^{\gamma-1}\tau_{x,v}e^{-\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}(1+|v|)^{2\gamma-2}\tau_{x,v}^{2}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}}|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}\int_{0}^{|z-q(z,-v)|}(1+|v|)^{2\gamma-2}\frac{s^{2}}{|v|^{2}}e^{-2\nu(v)\frac{s}{|v|}}|g(z,v)|^{2}N(z,v)dsd\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|v|^{2}}(1+|v|)^{2\gamma-2}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}N(z,v)|g(z,v)|^{2}\int_{0}^{|z-q(z,-v)|}s^{2}e^{-2\nu(v)\frac{s}{|v|}}dsd\Sigma(z)dv.

From (
2.12
), it suffices to show that the integral

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}(1+|v|)^{2\gamma-2}N(z,v)|z-q(z,-v)|d\Sigma(z)dv

converges.

By Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}(1+|v|)^{2\gamma-2}N(z,v)|z-q(z,-v)|d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}N^{2}(z,v)d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(z)dv.

For the second term, once we use the exact same change of variable as we did before and calculate the exactly same integral we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}(\nu^{2}(v)\frac{|x-q(x,v)|^{2}}{|v|^{4}N^{2}(x,v)}e^{-2\nu(v)\tau_{x,v}})|g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}\frac{\nu^{2}(v)}{N(x,v)|v|^{4}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{\nu(v)^{2}}|z-q(z,-v)|d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|g(z,v)|^{2}\frac{1}{|v|^{2}}d\Sigma(z)dv.

For the third term, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}e^{-2\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}|\nabla_{x}g(q(x,v),v)|^{2}\frac{|x-q(x,v)|^{2}}{|v|^{2}N(x,v)^{2}}dxdv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{x}g(z,v)|^{2}\frac{1}{N(x,v)|v|^{2}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{\nu(v)^{2}}|z-q(z,-v)|d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{x}g(z,v)|^{2}d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

For the last term, notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|e^{-\nu(s)\tau_{x,v}}(\nabla_{v}g)(q(x,v),v)|^{2}dxdv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{v}g(z,v)|^{2}N(x,v)|z-q(z,-v)|d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}^{-}}|\nabla_{v}g(z,v)|^{2}N^{2}(x,v)d\Sigma(z)dv

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

This completes the proof.

∎

so that the regularity of

Corollary 3.4

Suppose for given

\displaystyle g(x,v)\lesssim

Lemma 4.1
.

For non-negative measurable function
f
, we have

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{x,v}}f(x,v,s)dsdxdv=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau_{y,-u}}f(y+tu,u,t)dtdydu.

Proof.

Consider the following sets:

\displaystyle A:=\{(v,x,s)\mid v\in\mathbf{R}^{3},x\in\Omega,0\leq s\leq\tau_{x,v}\},

\displaystyle B:=\{(u,y,t)\mid u\in\mathbf{R}^{3},y\in\Omega,0\leq t\leq\tau_{y,-u}\}.

Also, Consider the change of variable:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}:A\longrightarrow B,

\displaystyle\quad(\sigma_{1}(v),\sigma_{1}(x),\sigma_{1}(s)):=(v,x-vs,s),

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}:B\longrightarrow A,

\displaystyle\quad(\sigma_{2}(u),\sigma_{2}(y),\sigma_{2}(t)):=(u,y+ut,t).

Notice that the for any
(v,x,s)\in A
we have
x-vs\in\Omega
,
\tau_{\sigma_{1}(x),-\sigma_{1}(v)}=\tau_{x-sv,-v}=\tau_{x,-v}+s\geq s=\sigma_{1}(s)\geq 0
. Hence the map
\sigma_{1}
is well defined. Similarly, the map
\sigma_{2}
is also well defined, and the fact
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}=I_{B},\sigma_{2}\circ\sigma_{1}=I_{A}
, show that the map is a bijection. This with the identities of Jacobean implies (
2.8
).

∎

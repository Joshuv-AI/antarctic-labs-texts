Global existence and asymptotic behavior for a reaction-diffusion system with unbounded coefficients
Mohamed Majdoub and Nasser-eddine Tatar

Abstract
. We consider a reaction-diffusion system which may serve as a model for a ferment catalytic reaction in chemistry. The model consists of a system of reaction diffusion equations with unbounded time dependent coefficients and different polynomial reaction terms. An exponential decay of the globally bounded solutions is proved. The key tool of the proofs are properties of analytic semigroups and some inequalities.

1.
Introduction

In the past three decades a strong interest in the questions of global existence and asymptotic behavior of solutions to various classes of reaction-diffusion systems has been witnessed. A particular attention was paid to autonomous systems and different sufficient conditions were assumed to ensure global existence of classical solutions. A main difficulty in proving global existence is the lack of maximum principle estimates or invariant regions. See, among many,
[
1
,
4
,
3
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
17
]
. Recently, the global existence of classical solutions was shown in
[
2
]
for quasi-positive nonlinearities having a (slightly super-) quadratic growth and obeying a mass control assumption. This, in particular, includes the cases of mass conservation and mass dissipation. See also
[
11
]
for initial data of low regularity.

Our goal in this paper is to study the following non-autonomous system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}u_{t}-(d_{1}\Delta-b_{1})u&=&a_{1}(t)w^{m}-a_{2}(t)u^{n}v^{k},\\
v_{t}-d_{2}\Delta v&=&\left(a_{1}(t)+a_{3}(t)\right)w^{m}-a_{2}(t)u^{n}v^{k},\\
w_{t}-(d_{3}\Delta-b_{2})w&=&-\left(a_{1}(t)+a_{3}(t)\right)w^{m}-a_{4}(t)w+a_{2}(t)u^{n}v^{k},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}&=&0,\\
(u,v,w)(x,0)&=&(u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x),w_{0}(x)),\end{array}\right.

where
t>0
,
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{N},

N\geq 1
,
\Omega
is a bounded domain with
smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\partial/\partial\nu
denotes the
outward normal derivative on
\partial\Omega.
The diffusion coefficients
d_{i},

i=1,2,3
are assumed positive constants and
u_{0},

v_{0}
and
w_{0}
are given nonnegative bounded initial functions. The coefficients
a_{i}(t)
are of the form
t^{\sigma_{i}}h_{i}(t),
where
\sigma_{i}\geq 0,

i=1,2,3
and
\sigma_{4}=0.
The functions

are nonnegative
continuous functions. The powers
m,

n
and

are assumed greater than

Systems with time dependent nonlinearities were considered in
[
7
]
by Kahane.
Specifically, a system of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-u_{t}+Lu=f(x,t,u,v)\;in\;\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
-v_{t}+Mv=g(x,t,u,v)\;in\;\Omega\times(0,\infty)\end{array}\right.

where
L
and
M
are uniformly elliptic operators, with boundary conditions
of Robin type is studied. He proved that the solution converges to the
stationary state, that is the solution of the limiting elliptic problem. To
this end he assumed that

f(x,t,u,v)\rightarrow\bar{f}(x,u,v)

and

g(x,t,u,v)\rightarrow\bar{g}(x,u,v)

uniformly in
\Omega
and
(u,v)
in any bounded subset of the first
quadrant in
\mathbf{R}^{2}.
The matrix formed by the partial derivatives
\bar{f}_{u},\bar{f}_{v},\bar{g}_{u}
and
\bar{g}_{v}
satisfies a column
diagonal dominance type condition. In the present work we do not make such
restrictions.

1
). In the case
m=n=k=1,

b_{1}=b_{2}=0
and

are constants, this problem has been studied by Wang
[
19

[

Unfortunately, his method seems to be not valid for

To our knowledge, a system with time-dependent coefficients like (

(1.6)

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present the notation that will be used in this paper and
prepare some material which will be useful in our proofs.

By
W^{l,p}(\Omega)
we denote the usual Sobolev space of order
l\geq 0

for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The space
C^{\mu}(\bar{\Omega}),\mu\geq 0
is
the Banach space of
[\mu]-
times continuously differentiable functions in
\bar{\Omega}
whose
[\mu]-
th order derivatives are Hölder continuous
with exponent
\mu-[\mu].

Definition 1
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define

D(A_{p})=D(B_{p})=D(G_{p})=\left\{y\in W^{2,p}(\Omega):\partial y/\partial\nu\left|{}_{\partial\Omega}\right.=0\right\},

A_{p}y=-(d_{1}\Delta-b_{1})y,

B_{p}y=-d_{2}\Delta y,

G_{p}y=-(d_{3}\Delta-b_{2})y.

The operators
-A_{p},

-B_{p}
and
-G_{p}
defined in this way are
sectorial operators (see Henry
[
4
]
) and generate analytic semigroups

\left\{e^{-tA_{p}}\right\}_{t\geq 0},

\left\{e^{-tB_{p}}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\left\{e^{-tG_{p}}\right\}_{t\geq 0},
respectively.

Definition 2
.

By
Q_{0}
and
Q_{+}
we designate the following projection operators, for
y\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\in(1,\infty)

Q_{0}y=\left|\Omega\right|^{-1}\int\limits_{\Omega}y(x)dx\;and\;Q_{+}y=y-Q_{0}y

where
\left|\Omega\right|
is the volume of
\Omega.

Definition 3
.

The restriction of
B_{p}
onto
Q_{+}L^{p}(\Omega)
will be denoted by
B_{p+}
i.e.
B_{p+}=B_{p}\left|{}_{Q_{+}L^{p}(\Omega)}\right..

For
p\in(1,\infty),
the operator
B_{p+}
generates an analytic
semigroup denoted by
\left\{e^{-tB_{p+}}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
in
Q_{+}L^{p}(\Omega).

We define the fractional powers of the above operators in the usual way (see
Henry
[
4
]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
A
be a sectorial operator in
X=L^{p}(\Omega),

1\leq p<\infty

with
D(A)=X^{1}\subset W^{m,p}(\Omega)
for some
m\geq 1.
Then, for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, we have
X^{\alpha}\subset C^{\mu}
when
0\leq\mu<m\alpha-\frac{N}{p}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\lambda
denote the least positive eigenvalue of the Laplacian with
homogeneous Neumann boundary condition. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. For every
\alpha\in[0,1)
there exist positive constants
C_{i},

i=1,2,3
such
that for
t>0
and
y\in L^{p}(\Omega)

\left\|A_{p}^{\alpha}e^{-tA_{p}}y\right\|_{p}\leq C_{1}t^{-\alpha}e^{-b_{1}t}\left\|y\right\|_{p},

\left\|B_{p+}^{\alpha}e^{-tB_{p+}}Q_{+}y\right\|_{p}\leq C_{2}t^{-\alpha}e^{-d_{2}\lambda t}\left\|Q_{+}y\right\|_{p},

\left\|G_{p}^{\alpha}e^{-tG_{p}}y\right\|_{p}\leq C_{3}t^{-\alpha}e^{-b_{2}t}\left\|y\right\|_{p}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty),

l\geq 1
and
\alpha\in[0,1).
Then, there exists
a positive constant
C
such that

\left\|y\right\|_{pl}\leq C\left\|A_{p}^{\alpha}y\right\|_{p}^{\theta}.\left\|y\right\|_{p}^{1-\theta}

where
\theta
satisfies

\frac{N(l-1)}{2pl\alpha}<\theta<1.

See Henry
[
4
]
for the proofs of Lemmas
1
-
2
-
3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\beta\in\mathbf{R}.
There exists a positive
constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta)
such that

\int\limits_{0}^{t}s^{-\alpha}e^{\beta s}ds\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Ce^{\beta t}\;if\;\beta>0\\
C(t+1)\;if\;\beta=0\\
C\;if\;\beta<0.\end{array}\right.

See Hoshino and Yamada
[
6
]
, for instance, for the proof of Lemma
4
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mu,

\nu,

\tau
and

, then

z^{1-\nu}\int\limits_{0}^{z}(z-\xi)^{\nu-1}\xi^{\mu-1}e^{-\tau\xi}d\xi\leq C\tau^{-\mu}

See Michalski
[

Let

We define

(2.2)

The following Bihari-type inequality is classical and can be found in many references. See for instance

]

Lemma 6

Under the above assumptions on

, we have

(2.3)

(2.4)

Remark 2

The classical Gronwall’s inequality corresponds to

Proof of Theorem
1

Define
\mathbf{z}=(u,v,w)
, we rewrite (
1
) as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}u_{t}-d_{1}\Delta\,u&=&f_{1}(t,\mathbf{z}),\\

where

\left(u+v+w+y\right)_{t}-\Delta\left(d_{1}u+d_{2}v+d_{3}w+y\right)=0.

(3.5)

It follows that

(3.6)

where

(3.7)

and

(3.8)

Therefore

\|\mathbf{z}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}}\leq\|\Delta\mathbf{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathbf{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{T},

(3.9)

where

depends continuously in
T>0
.
Using (
3.6

(3.10)

where

0<\underline{B}\leq B(x,t)=\frac{u(x,t)+v(x,t)+w(x,t)+y(x,t)}{d_{1}u(x,t)+d_{2}v(x,t)+d_{3}w(x,t)+y(x,t)}\leq\overline{B}<\infty.

Lemma 3.5 in
[
2
]
implies

|\mathbf{m}(x,t)-\mathbf{m}(x^{\prime},t)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{T}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\delta},\;\;\;\forall\;0<t<T,

(3.11)

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
. Equation (
3.6
) also implies

\partial_{t}\mathbf{m}-\Delta\mathbf{m}=(d_{1}-1)u+(d_{2}-1)v+(d_{3}-1)w+g(x).

(3.12)

By using the Hölder continuity of
\mathbf{m}
and
[
2
, Lemma 1.1, p. 283]
, we infer

|\nabla\mathbf{m}|\leq\mathcal{C}_{T}\,|\mathbf{z}|^{\frac{1-\delta}{2-\delta}}.

(3.13)

Applying
[
2
, Lemma 1.1, p. 283]
to each equation of

leads to

|\nabla\,q|\leq\mathcal{C}_{T}\,\left(1+|\mathbf{z}|\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(3.14)

Using

2
, Lemma 3.9, p. 295]
together with (
3.13
) and (

) gives

\displaystyle|\Delta\mathbf{m}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}\,|\nabla\mathbf{m}|^{1/2}\,\left(|\nabla\,u|+|\nabla\,v|+|\nabla\,w|+\,|\nabla\,g|\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}\,|\mathbf{z}|^{\frac{1-\delta}{2(2-\delta)}}\,\left(1+|\mathbf{z}|^{3/2}\right)^{1/2}

\begin{array}[]{c}e^{bt}\left\|G_{p}^{\alpha}w\right\|_{p}\leq C_{3}\delta^{-\alpha}\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{p}+C_{3}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}h_{4}(s)e^{bs}\left\|w\right\|_{p}ds\\

+C_{8}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}e^{-(b-\varepsilon)(l-1)s}(s^{\sigma_{1}}h_{1}+s^{\sigma_{3}}h_{3})(s)W(s)^{l}ds\\

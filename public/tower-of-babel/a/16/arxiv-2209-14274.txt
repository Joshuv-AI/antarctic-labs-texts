On the Classical Integrability of Root- 𝑻 ​ 𝑻 ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu Flows
Riccardo Borsato, Christian Ferko, Alessandro Sfondrini

Abstract
The Root- T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu flow was recently introduced as a universal and classically marginal deformation of any two-dimensional translation-invariant field theory. The flow commutes with the (irrelevant) T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu flow and it can be integrated explicitly for a large class of actions, leading to non-analytic Lagrangians reminiscent of the four-dimensional Modified-Maxwell theory (ModMax). It is not a priori obvious whether the Root- T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu flow preserves integrability, like it is the case for the T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu flow. In this paper we demonstrate that this is the case for a large class of classical models by explicitly constructing a deformed Lax connection. We discuss the principal chiral model and the non-linear sigma models on symmetric and semi-symmetric spaces, without or with Wess-Zumino term. We also construct Lax connections for the two-parameter families of theories deformed by both Root- T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu and T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu for all of these models.

Introduction

Given the action
\mathcal{S}_{0}
of a quantum field theory (QFT) and an integrated local operator
\mathcal{O}
, it is possible to formally deform the action infinitesimally by setting

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{S}_{0}+\lambda\,\mathcal{O}+O(\lambda^{2})\,.

(1)

For instance, in the case where
\mathcal{S}_{0}
describes a conformal field theory (CFT) and
\mathcal{O}
is a
relevant
operator in the spectrum of the theory, it is natural to interpret
\mathcal{S}_{0}
as an ultraviolet (UV) theory, and
\mathcal{O}
as the source of a renormalisation group (RG) flow. Geometrically,
\mathcal{O}
is a tangent vector to the RG flow at a fixed point, and the RG flow equations determine the remainder of the
\lambda
-expansion in (
1
). Determining the flow completely is a very difficult problem in general. Remarkably, in two-dimensions it is sometimes possible to investigate such flows quite explicitly in the special setup where the perturbing operator preserves “sufficiently many symmetries”. In this case, the flow is integrable and can be studied
e.g.
by thermodynamic Bethe ansatz techniques, see
e.g.

Zamolodchikov:1989cf
;
Zamolodchikov:1991vx
;
Mussardo:1992uc
;
Bazhanov:1994ft
.

For two-dimensional QFTs it is possible to construct other deformations quite explicitly. If
\mathcal{S}_{0}
enjoys at least two conservation laws with currents
j^{\mu}_{0}
and
k^{\mu}_{0}
,

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{0}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}_{0}=0\,,

(2)

then we may define the combination

\mathcal{O}_{0}^{j\wedge k}=\int\text{d}\tau\int\text{d}\sigma\;j^{\mu}_{0}(\tau,\sigma)\,k^{\nu}_{0}(\tau,\sigma)\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\,.

(3)

It turns out that this composite operator can be defined in the quantum theory without incurring any short-distance singularities
Smirnov:2016lqw
. Then, it is possible to define a deformed action by the equation

\partial_{\lambda}\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{j\wedge k}\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{j\wedge k}
is defined in the deformed theory, out of the deformed currents
j^{\mu}_{\lambda}
and
k^{\mu}_{\lambda}
(which are conserved with respect to
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
). This is a rather general definition that encompasses many well-studied examples, including the
marginal

J\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muJ\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation (when
J^{\mu}=j^{\mu}
and
\epsilon^{\mu\nu}J_{\nu}=k^{\mu}
are both conserved), and the
irrelevant

T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation
Zamolodchikov:2004ce
;
Smirnov:2016lqw
;
Cavaglia:2016oda
, which arises when
T^{0\mu}=j^{\mu}
and
T^{1\mu}=k^{\mu}
.
In that case

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{(2)}=-\int\text{d}\tau\int\text{d}\sigma\;\det\left[T^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda}(\tau,\sigma)\right]\,.

(5)

The
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation is universal: it can be constructed for any translation invariant QFT. Remarkably, it can be studied very explicitly both at the classical and at the quantum level.
Moreover, if the seed theory
\mathcal{S}_{0}
happens to be a CFT, or an integrable QFT (IQFT), infinitely-many analogues of
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
may be constructed

4

\pm s

Very remarkably, each of these operators

theories (and perhaps ModMax theories too), it is natural to ask if this deformation has any further property, at least at the classical level. In particular, does it preserve integrability, like it is the case for
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and its higher-spin analogues generated by
\mathcal{O}^{(s)}

In this work we will not answer this question in full generality, but instead give evidence for a (rather large) class of theories. These are the principal chiral model (PCM) on a Lie group
G
, and two classes of non-linear sigma models (NLSMs) on coset spaces
G/H
, where
G
is a Lie (super)group and
H
a bosonic subgroup thereof, as well as the extensions of these models by a Wess-Zumino (WZ) term. These NLSMS are the (bosonic) symmetric-space sigma model (SSSM) and its supersymmetric extension, the “semi-symmetric space” sigma model (sSSSM). For all these models we construct the deformed Lax connection
\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}
, and check that imposing its flatness

\partial_{+}\mathfrak{L}_{-}-\partial_{-}\mathfrak{L}_{+}+\left[\mathfrak{L}_{+},\,\mathfrak{L}_{-}\right]=0\,,

(12)

is equivalent to the equations of motion of the deformed model. We also construct the Lax connection for the
(\gamma,\lambda)
doubly-deformed models, where we combine
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations. Our construction follows a rather explicit case-by-case analysis, similarly to the construction of
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deformed Lax connections of
Chen:2021aid
.

It should be noted that the semi-symmetric space sigma model reduces to the symmetric space sigma model by setting its odd (fermionic) part to zero. In turn, the PCM can also be thought of as a symmetric space coset
(G\times G)/G
. In a sense, we might as well only describe the most general sSSSM case (with the addition of a WZ term), and all the others would follow from it. However, especially given that the construction of the sSSSM is somewhat technical, we find it clearer to first work out in detail the PCM, which is substantially simpler, and then “work our way up” to the SSSM and sSSSM.

Let us stress that the identification of a Lax connection is only the first step in the proof of classical integrability of the deformed models. One should further check that the conserved charges constructed from the monodromy matrix have mutually vanishing Poisson brackets, i.e. they are in involution. In this regard, the models that we are deforming are known to possess some technical complications due to a non-ultralocal Poisson structure. That means that when taking the Poisson brackets not only the Dirac delta function appears but also its derivative. Nevertheless, it is still possible to recast the Poisson brackets of the Lax connection in a useful form known as Maillet brackets
Maillet:1985ec
;
Maillet:1985ek
that allows one to construct the mutually conserved charges and argue for the classical integrability of the models. We expect the deformed models analysed here to display a modification of the original structure of Maillet brackets of the undeformed models. It would be very important, and at the same time very interesting, to construct the Maillet brackets explicitly.

3.1
The Principal Chiral Model

Consider a Lie group
G
and its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
The left- and right-invariant Maurer-Cartan forms are

j=g^{-1}\text{d}g\,,\qquad\tilde{\jmath}=-(\text{d}g)g^{-1}\,,

(34)

with
j\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\tilde{\jmath}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
These are related by the adjoint action
\tilde{\jmath}=-\Ad_{g}{j}=-g^{-1}jg
.
Let
g=g(\tau,\sigma)\in G
be a
G
-valued field.
We will drop the
(\tau,\sigma)
dependence to keep our notation lighter. Define
j_{\mu}
and
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
to be the pull-back of the Maurer-Cartan forms

j_{\mu}=g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g\,,\qquad\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}=-\left(\partial_{\mu}g\right)g^{-1}\,,

(35)

which both take values in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Here
j_{\mu}
is invariant under left-multiplication by a
constant
group element
g_{0}\in G
,
g^{\prime}=g_{0}\,g
, and
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
is invariant under right-multiplication by a constant group element,
g^{\prime}=g\,g_{0}
.
Both
j_{\mu}
and
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
satisfy the flatness condition, which we express in light-cone coordinates,

\partial_{+}j_{-}-\partial_{-}j_{+}+\left[j_{+},\,j_{-}\right]=0=\partial_{+}\tilde{\jmath}_{-}-\partial_{-}\tilde{\jmath}_{+}+\left[\tilde{\jmath}_{+},\,\tilde{\jmath}_{-}\right]\,.

(36)

The Lagrangian of the principal chiral model takes a simple form in terms of
j_{\mu}
, namely

L_{\text{PCM}}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\tr\left[j_{\mu}\,j_{\nu}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\tr\left[j_{+}\,j_{-}\right]\,,

(37)

which can be equivalently written as

L_{\text{PCM}}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\tr\left[\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}\,\tilde{\jmath}_{\nu}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\tr\left[\tilde{\jmath}_{+}\,\tilde{\jmath}_{-}\right]\,.

(38)

Both expressions are manifestly invariant under left-multiplication and right-multiplication by
g_{0}\in G
, hence the action is invariant under
G\times G
.

As it is easy to verify explicitly, the Noether current associated to the invariance under right-multiplication is
j_{\mu}
, and its conservation equation

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0\,,

(39)

is equivalent to the equations of motion of the model, see also Appendix
A.1
for the explicit computation. Therefore, we may define the Lax connection

\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}=\frac{j_{\pm}}{1\mp z}\,,

(40)

which depends on the spectral parameter
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Requiring
\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}
to be flat for any
z
amounts to imposing the equations of motions of the model (
39
)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}\mathfrak{L}_{-}-\partial_{-}\mathfrak{L}_{+}+\left[\mathfrak{L}_{+},\mathfrak{L}_{-}\right]

(41)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\Big(\partial_{+}j_{-}-\partial_{-}j_{+}+\left[j_{+},j_{-}\right]-z\left(\partial_{+}j_{-}+\partial_{-}j_{+}\right)\Big),

as it may be verified explicitly by using the Maurer-Cartan equation (
36
).
Similarly, the Noether current related to left-multiplication is
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
, whose conservation equation

\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\jmath}{}^{\mu}=0\,,

(42)

is also equivalent to the equations of motion, which yields an equivalent construction of the Lax connection.

3.2
Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
Deformation

It is immediate to write down a deformed Lagrangian, by plugging into eq. (
25
) the appropriate expressions for the PCM, which we may read off (
37
). We have

x_{0}=0,\qquad x_{1}=-\tr[j_{+}j_{-}]\,,\qquad x_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tr[j_{+}j_{+}]\tr[j_{-}j_{-}]+\left(\tr[j_{+}j_{-}]\right)^{2}\right)\,,

(43)

so that

L_{\text{PCM}}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\ch(\gamma)\tr[j_{+}j_{-}]+\sh(\gamma)\sqrt{\tr[j_{+}j_{+}]\tr[j_{-}j_{-}]}\right).

(44)

We can equivalently write

L_{\text{PCM}}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\ch(\gamma)\tr[\tilde{\jmath}_{+}\tilde{\jmath}_{-}]+\sh(\gamma)\sqrt{\tr[\tilde{\jmath}_{+}\tilde{\jmath}_{+}]\tr[\tilde{\jmath}_{-}\tilde{\jmath}_{-}]}\right),

(45)

either by expressing the seed Lagrangian in terms of
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
in the first place, or by using the explicit form of
j_{\mu}
and
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
.

The above expressions make it manifest that the deformed model still has
G\times G
symmetry.
It is possible to derive the resulting conserved current by Noether’s theorem. In Appendix
A.1
we do so for any Lagrangian which depends on
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
as in eq. (
43
),
L(x_{1},x_{2})
, obtaining for the current related to the right-multiplication symmetry

\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}=2\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}j_{\mu}+4\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}g^{\nu\rho}\tr[j_{\mu}j_{\nu}]\,j_{\rho}\,,\qquad\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{J}^{\mu}=0\,.

(46)

We immediately see that for the seed Lagrangian
L_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}x_{1}
this reduces to the current
j^{\mu}
.
For the action (
44
) this gives

\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}=\ch(\gamma)\,j_{\pm}-\sh(\gamma)\sqrt{\frac{\tr[j_{\pm}j_{\pm}]}{\tr[j_{\mp}j_{\mp}]}}\,j_{\mp}\,,\qquad\partial_{+}\mathfrak{J}_{-}+\partial_{-}\mathfrak{J}_{+}=0\,.

(47)

Note that we may rewrite the Lagrangian (
44
) as

L_{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{2}\tr\left[j_{+}\mathfrak{J}_{-}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\tr\left[\mathfrak{J}_{+}j_{-}\right]\,.

(48)

The current
\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}
is conserved; it is however not flat. To see this, let us consider the commutator of the
\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}
, which actually obeys a nice relation:

[\mathfrak{J}_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}]=\ch(\gamma)^{2}[j_{+},j_{-}]+\sh(\gamma)^{2}[j_{-},j_{+}]=[j_{+},j_{-}]\,.

(49)

This relation will be crucial for our construction below. To begin with, it means that

\partial_{+}\mathfrak{J}_{-}-\partial_{-}\mathfrak{J}_{+}+[\mathfrak{J}_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}]\neq 0\,,

(50)

unless
\gamma=0
, because the first two terms are
\gamma
-dependent while the commutator is not.
However, this structure suggests a modification of the Lax connection which is reminiscent of that discussed in
Chen:2021aid
,

\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{j_{\pm}\pm z\,\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}}{1-z^{2}}\,.

(51)

By construction, this expression reduces to (
40
) when
\gamma=0
and
\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}=j_{\mu}
.
Noting the commutators

[\mathfrak{J}_{\pm},j_{\mp}]=\ch(\gamma)[j_{\pm},j_{\mp}]\,,

(52)

which in particular imply that

[\mathfrak{J}_{+},j_{-}]-[j_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}]=0\,,

(53)

and exploiting the important relation (
49
),
we can work out the condition for
\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}^{(\gamma)}
to be flat,
4
4

4

We use the shorthands

A_{[\mu}B_{\nu]}=A_{\mu}B_{\nu}-A_{\nu}B_{\mu}
and
A_{(\mu}B_{\nu)}=A_{\mu}B_{\nu}+A_{\nu}B_{\mu}
.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}\mathfrak{L}^{(\gamma)}_{-}-\partial_{-}\mathfrak{L}^{(\gamma)}_{+}+[\mathfrak{L}^{(\gamma)}_{+},\mathfrak{L}^{(\gamma)}_{-}]

(54)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left(\partial_{[+}j_{-]}-z\,\partial_{(+}\mathcal{J}_{-)}+\frac{[j_{+},j_{-}]-z([j_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}]-[\mathfrak{J}_{+},j_{-}])-z^{2}[\mathfrak{J}_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}]}{1-z^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left(\partial_{[+}j_{-]}+[j_{+},j_{-}]-z\,\partial_{(+}\mathcal{J}_{-)}\right)\,.

Using the Maurer-Cartan equation (
36
) we see that
\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}^{(\gamma)}
is flat for any
z
if and only if the equations of motion (
47
) are satisfied.

It is quite clear that we could have performed an analogous construction for the current related to left-multiplication by
g_{0}
, which would have given

\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}_{\pm}=\ch(\gamma)\,\tilde{\jmath}_{\pm}-\sh(\gamma)\sqrt{\frac{\tr[\tilde{\jmath}_{\pm}\tilde{\jmath}_{\pm}]}{\tr[\tilde{\jmath}_{\mp}\tilde{\jmath}_{\mp}]}}\,\tilde{\jmath}_{\mp}\,,\qquad\partial_{+}\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}_{-}+\partial_{-}\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}_{+}=0\,.

(55)

This current is related to
\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}
much in the same way as
\tilde{\jmath}_{\pm}
is related to
j_{\pm}
, namely

\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}_{\mu}=-\text{Ad}_{g}\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}\,.

(56)

In a similar way, we can define the Lax connection in terms of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}_{\pm}
, namely

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{L}}_{\pm}(z)=\frac{\tilde{\jmath}_{\pm}\pm z\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}_{\pm}}{1-z^{2}}\,.

(57)

As in the undeformed case, the two choices are related by a gauge transformation of the Lax connection

\displaystyle g(\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}(z)+\partial_{\pm})g^{-1}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{L}}(z^{-1})\,.

(58)

3.3
Two-Parameter Deformation

An important property of the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow is that it commutes with the usual (irrelevant)
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow
Ferko:2022lol
. Following the discussion of Section
2.1
, for a theory whose “seed” Lagrangian is

L_{0}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{2}x_{1}\,,

(59)

we have from eq. (
33
)

L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(\sqrt{1+2\lambda x_{1}^{(\gamma)}+2\lambda^{2}\left((x_{1}^{(\gamma)})^{2}-x_{2}^{(\gamma)}\right)}-1\right),

(60)

with
x_{1}^{(\gamma)}
and
x_{2}^{(\gamma)}
as in (
24
).
This can be immediately adapted to the case of the PCM by substituting the values (
43
) for
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
Then, we get a deformed action of the type discussed in Section
3.1
. In particular, the equations of motion are still given by the conservation of
\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}
as in eq. (
46
). We can write
\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}
a little more explicitly in light-cone coordinates,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{J}_{+}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)j_{+}-2\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\tr[j_{+}j_{+}]j_{-}\,,

(61)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{J}_{-}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)j_{-}-2\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\tr[j_{-}j_{-}]j_{+}\,,

where
j_{\mu}
is again the pull-back of the left-invariant Maurer-Cartan one form.
5
5

5

As before, the whole discussion could be repeated in terms of the right-invariant current
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
.

We will now see that the Lax connection takes once again the form

\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}^{(\lambda,\gamma)}=\frac{j_{\pm}\pm z\,\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}}{1-z^{2}}\,,

(62)

just like in eq. (
51
), where now
\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}
is given by (
61
).
To see why this is the case, we first observe that

\displaystyle\big[\mathfrak{J}_{+},j_{-}\big]=2\left(\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)\big[j_{+},j_{-}\big],

(63)

\displaystyle\big[j_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}\big]=2\left(\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)\big[j_{+},j_{-}\big],

so that in particular

\big[\mathfrak{J}_{+},j_{-}\big]-\big[j_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}\big]=0\,,

(64)

like we had before (
53
).
Next, we compute again

\big[\mathfrak{J}_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}\big]=4\left[\left(\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{\partial L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}\big(2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}\big)\right]\,\big[j_{+},j_{-}\big].

(65)

where we used that
\tr[j_{+}j_{+}]\tr[j_{-}j_{-}]=2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}
to rewrite the whole coefficient on the right-hand side in terms of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
An explicit computation using the form of
L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}
in (
33
) then shows that,
for any
\gamma
and
\lambda
, the whole coeffiecient on the right-hand side simplifies to one. In other words,

\big[\mathfrak{J}_{+},\mathfrak{J}_{-}\big]=\big[j_{+},j_{-}\big],

(66)

just like in (
49
). Using these two observations, the derivation of eq. (
54
) goes through immediately, showing that for this model too the flatness of
\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}^{(\lambda,\gamma)}
is equivalent to the equations of motion.
6
6

6

Conversely, we could obtain a whole class of integrability-preserving Lagrangians
L(x_{1},x_{2})
by demanding that the coefficient on the right-hand side of (
65
) equals one. Unfortunately, it is not immediate to construct the most general solution of this nonlinear partial differential equation.

We note that, when
\gamma=0
, we recover the Lax connection for the
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deformed PCM which was obtained in
Chen:2021aid
.

3.4
Adding a Wess-Zumino Term

The PCM on the Lie group
G
can be supplemented by a Wess-Zumino (WZ) term. The resulting action, prior to any (Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
) deformation, reads

\mathcal{S}_{\text{PCMWZ}}=\hay\int\limits_{\partial B}\text{d}^{2}\sigma L_{\text{PCM}}+\kay\int\limits_{B}\text{d}^{3}\sigma\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon^{ijk}\tr\big[j_{i}\,[j_{j},j_{k}]\big]\,.

(67)

A few remarks are in order. First of all, we have expressed the WZ term as a three-dimensional integral on a space
B
whose boundary
\partial B
is the usual
(1+1)
-dimensional Minkowski space.
7
7

7

Here
\varepsilon^{ijk}
is the three-dimensional Levi-Civita tensor.

For this to lead to a well-defined (quantum) theory it may be necessary to impose a constraint for the coefficient
\kay
. In particular, if
G
is a compact simple Lie group, we should take

4\pi\kay\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(68)

The coefficient
\hay
, instead, is unconstrained. We could have of course introduced
\hay
in the discussion of the PCM above, but it would only have amounted to a rescaling of the action.

Let us briefly review the construction of the Lax connection in the undeformed model, see also
e.g.
the review
Hoare:2021dix
. First of all
j_{\mu}
(and
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
) still satisfy the flatness condition (
36
). However, the equations of motion are now modified and read

(\hay+\kay)\partial_{+}j_{-}+(\hay-\kay)\partial_{-}j_{+}=0\,,

(69)

or equivalently

(\hay-\kay)\partial_{+}\tilde{\jmath}_{-}+(\hay+\kay)\partial_{-}\tilde{\jmath}_{+}=0\,.

(70)

It is easy to see that the Lax connection

\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}=\left(1\mp\frac{\kay}{\hay}\right)\,\frac{j_{\pm}}{1\mp z}

(71)

reduces to (
40
) for
\kay=0
and that its flatness is equivalent to the equations of motion. We review the computation as it will be useful in a moment. We have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{[+}\mathfrak{L}_{-]}+[\mathfrak{L}_{+},\mathfrak{L}_{-}]

(72)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left((1-z)(1+\tfrac{\kay}{\hay})\partial_{+}j_{-}-(1+z)(1-\tfrac{\kay}{\hay})\partial_{-}j_{+}+(1-\tfrac{\kay^{2}}{\hay^{2}})[j_{+},j_{-}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left(\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}-z\right)\left[(1+\tfrac{\kay}{\hay})\partial_{+}j_{-}+(1-\tfrac{\kay}{\hay})\partial_{-}j_{+}\right],

where in the last step we used the Maurer-Cartan equation to eliminate the commutator. As a result, eq. (
72
) is equivalent to the equations of motion (
69
).

Let us now consider the case of the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation (or the combined Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation) of the PCM with WZ term. The action of the deformed model is simply

\mathcal{S}_{\text{PCMWZ}}^{(\gamma)}=\hay\int\limits_{\partial B}\text{d}^{2}\sigma L_{\text{PCM}}^{(\gamma)}+\kay\int\limits_{B}\text{d}^{3}\sigma\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon^{ijk}\tr\big[j_{i}\,[j_{j},j_{k}]\big]\,,

(73)

where
L_{\text{PCM}}^{(\gamma)}
is the very same Lagrangian as in (
44
). This construction also applies verbatim if
L_{\text{PCM}}^{(\gamma)}
is replaced by the doubly-deformed Lagrangian
L_{\text{PCM}}^{(\lambda,\gamma)}
of (
33
). In fact, as we discussed in Section
2
, the terms of the action which do not couple to the two-dimensional metric do not affect the flow. This is the case for the WZ term, which is topological.
The derivation of the equations of motion splits now in two parts. The part scaling with
\hay
is exactly as in Section
3.2
(see also Appendix
A.1
for the detailed derivation). The part scaling with
\kay
goes like in the standard WZ term construction. All in all, the equations take the form

\partial_{+}\left(\hay\mathfrak{J}_{-}+\kay j_{-}\right)+\partial_{-}\left(\hay\mathfrak{J}_{+}-\kay j_{+}\right)=0\,,

(74)

which should be compared to eq. (
69
) above; a similar equation holds for the right-invariant currents.
These equations of motion suggest a straightforward guess for the Lax connection of the deformed model, namely

\mathfrak{L}_{\pm}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{\left(j_{\pm}\mp\frac{\kay}{\hay}\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}\right)\pm z\,\left(\mathfrak{J}_{\pm}\mp\frac{\kay}{\hay}j_{\pm}\right)}{1-z^{2}}\,.

(75)

Let us now prepare to study the flatness condition for (
75
). By making use of the identities of the previous section, in particular eqs. (
49
) and (
53
), we can compute

\big[\mathfrak{L}^{(\gamma)}_{+},\mathfrak{L}^{(\gamma)}_{-}\big]=\frac{1-\frac{\hay^{2}}{\kay^{2}}}{1-z^{2}}\,[j_{+},j_{-}]\,.

(76)

Then the computation follows closely that of eq. (
72
), and reads

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{[+}\mathfrak{L}_{-]}+[\mathfrak{L}_{+},\mathfrak{L}_{-}]

(77)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\Big((1-\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}z)\partial_{+}j_{-}+(\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}-z)\partial_{+}\mathfrak{J}_{-}

\displaystyle-(1-\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}z)\partial_{-}j_{+}+(\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}-z)\partial_{-}\mathfrak{J}_{+}+(1-\tfrac{\kay^{2}}{\hay^{2}})[j_{+},j_{-}]\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z^{2}}\left(\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}-z\right)\left[\partial_{+}\left(\mathfrak{J}_{-}+\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}j_{-}\right)+\partial_{-}\left(\mathfrak{J}_{+}-\tfrac{\kay}{\hay}j_{+}\right)\right],

where again in the last step we used the Maurer-Cartan equation to eliminate the term involving
[j_{+},j_{-}]
. The above equation is therefore equivalent to the equations of motion (
74
). This construction can be repeated in terms of the right-invariant current too.

3.5
About the Wess-Zumino-Witten Point

The physics of the PCM with a WZ term depends on the ratio of
\kay/\hay
. In the undeformed model, it is interesting to consider the equations of motion in the limit when
\hay\to\kay
. One finds that the equations of motion imply that the left- and right-invariant currents become chiral,

\partial_{+}j_{-}=0\,,\qquad\partial_{-}\tilde{\jmath}_{+}=0\,.

(78)

In the opposite limit, when
\hay\to-\kay
, the currents
j_{\mu}
and
\tilde{\jmath}_{\mu}
swap roles.
In the quantum theory, these points correspond to the WZW model. At these points, the chiral conservation laws (
78
) lead to the existence of two (chiral and anti-chiral) Kač-Moody algebras built out of
G
.
It is natural to wonder whether this is the case in the deformed model. In the limit
\hay\to\kay
, the equations of motion (
74
) become

\partial_{+}\left(\mathfrak{J}_{-}+j_{-}\right)+\partial_{-}\left(\mathfrak{J}_{+}-j_{+}\right)=0\,,

(79)

which is not chiral. This is still a classical statement, but it has intriguing implications when we consider a Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation of the WZW model. It points to a rather radical deformation of the current algebra underlying the WZW model, which would be very interesting to study at the quantum level.

4
Non-Linear Sigma Models

Let us consider now a sigma model having as target space the quotient
G/H
, where
G
is a Lie group and
H
is a subgroup, see also
Arutyunov:2009ga
;
Zarembo:2017muf
;
Seibold:2020ouf
for reviews. Indicating the Lie algebra as
\mathfrak{g}
and its subalgebra as
\mathfrak{h}
, we have the vector-space decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{f}\,.

(80)

Symmetric space sigma models
correspond to the special case where the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
automorphism such that
8
8

8

The reason for choosing subscript indices
0
and
2
rather than
0
and
1
will become clear in a few lines, when we will introduce the
semi-symmetric space
sigma model.

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\mathfrak{f}\,,\qquad[\mathfrak{g}_{n},\mathfrak{g}_{m}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{(n+m)\text{mod}2}\,.

(81)

As we will briefly review below, this fact is sufficient to guarantee the existence of a Lax connection for the model, and we will show that the same is true even if we perform a Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation of the model.

A further generalisation of such cosets is given by
semi-symmetric space sigma models
. These are constructed from Lie supergroups, whose tangent spaces are Lie superalgebras. Let
G
be a Lie supergroup, and
\mathfrak{g}
its superalgebra. By definition, the superalgebra has a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading, given by the “Fermion sign”
(-1)^{F}
. This fact by itself is not sufficient to guarantee the existence of a Lax connection on a suitably-defined supercoset. If, however, the algebra can be endowed with a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
automorphism
\Omega
, such that
\Omega^{2}=(-1)^{F}
, then it is possible to construct such an integrable supercoset. We decompose the Lie superalgebra in its eigenspaces under
\Omega
,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{3},\qquad\Omega\,\mathfrak{g}_{n}=i^{n}\,\mathfrak{g}_{n}\,,\qquad[\mathfrak{g}_{n},\mathfrak{g}_{m}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{(n+m)\text{mod}4},

(82)

where the odd eigenspaces corresponds to fermionic generators and the even ones to bosonic generators. It follows in particular that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is a (bosonic) subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Calling
H
the corresponding subgroup, the coset space
G/H
is the target manifold of the semi-symmetric space sigma model. This structure is sufficient to construct a Lax connection for this model, as well as for its Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation.

4.1
Symmetric-Space Sigma Model (SSSM)

Here we consider the coset
G/H
, with
H
a subgroup of
G
such that the corresponding Lie algebras enjoy the decomposition (
81
).
Similarly to what we did for the PCM in Section
3.1
, let us consider an element
g\in G
and denote the pull-back of the (left-invariant) Maurer-Cartan form as
j_{\mu}=g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g
. Since
j_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, it can be decomposed as

j_{\mu}=j^{(0)}_{\mu}+j^{(2)}_{\mu}\,,\qquad j^{(n)}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{g}_{n}\,.

(83)

Hence
j^{(0)}_{\mu}
takes value in the subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}
, while
j^{(2)}_{\mu}
takes value in the orthogonal complement. It is worth noting how these two components transform under the subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Letting
h\in\mathfrak{h}
, and acting on
g
by right multiplication,
g^{\prime}=g\,h

5
Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper we have shown that a large class of classical integrable models still admit a Lax connection after an arbitrary combination of Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and (irrelevant)
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations.
While this is not an indication of integrability for the deformation of
all
classically integrable models, it is still a striking and non-trivial statement about the interplay of Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations with symmetries. Moreover, having explicit Lax connections at our disposal for a large class of models provides us with a powerful tool to study properties of classical solutions in the deformed theory, such as the value of the conserved charges under deformations.

There are many natural and pressing questions.
Firstly, properly establishing integrability at the classical level requires not only the existence of a Lax connection, but also its compatibility with the Poisson structure of the model. This is what allows us to construct charges that are not just conserved under time-evolution, but also mutually Poisson-commuting
Sklyanin:1980ij
;
Maillet:1985ec
;
Maillet:1985ek
. This is a natural step to truly establish classical integrability of these models (this is sometimes called “strong integrability”).
Naturally, it would also be interesting to study the classical integrability of even more general classes of theories such as Toda field theories, see
e.g.

Corrigan:1994nd
, or more general sigma models such as the ones that arise as Yang-Baxter deformations of (semi-)symmetric sigma models
Klimcik:2008eq
;
Delduc:2013qra
;
Kawaguchi:2014qwa
;
vanTongeren:2015soa
(see also
Seibold:2020ouf
). This would be a strong hint of integrability in general, and provide an even larger playground for the study of these deformations.

For the irrelevant
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation, it is possible to generate solutions by recasting the flow as a dynamical change of coordinates
Conti:2018tca
. It would be interesting to see if a similar construction is possible here, as this might provide a way to prove that Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
preserves integrability in general.
It would also be interesting to extend this construction to non-relativistic models and one-dimensional (quantum-mechanical) systems, like it was done for
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, see
Cardy:2018jho
;
Ceschin:2020jto
;
Esper:2021hfq
and
Marchetto:2019yyt
;
Pozsgay:2019ekd
;
Gross:2019ach
;
Gross:2019uxi
;
Ebert:2022ehb
, respectively.
In fact, it was recently shown that a Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation of a simple quantum-mechanical system preserves integrability too
Garcia:2022wad
. Again, it would be important to understand whether this is a general feature of such deformations.

An important outstanding question is how to quantise Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
theories (as well as ModMax theories
Bandos:2020jsw
;
Bandos:2020hgy
;
Bandos:2021rqy
;
Lechner:2022qhb
;
Sorokin:2021tge
). It might be worth undertaking this study on some particular examples which are integrable. For instance, we have seen in Section
3.5
that the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation of a
G
-valued Wess-Zumino-Witten model is still classically integrable and conformal, but it does not enjoy the conservation of two (anti-)chiral
\mathfrak{g}
-currents. This suggests that, at the very least, the Kač-Moody symmetry of the quantum model is modified — if the model remains conformal at the quantum level, which is not obvious. It would be interesting to perform an analysis similar to what was done for current-current deformations
Chaudhuri:1988qb
;
Forste:2003km
;
Borsato:2018spz
to determine the effect of the deformation.
14
14

14

Another direction worth exploring with the aim of quantisation, following recent work on ModMax
Lechner:2022qhb
, would be to understand the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
model by adding auxiliary fields, which should make the deformed action analytic.

An immediate by-product of our construction is the observation that Root-

and deformations thereof.
Even if the deformed actions are not of the sigma-model type, it would be very interesting to consider them as potential deformations of a string action. In particular, they may still enjoy a local fermionic symmetry inherited from the

For Root-

Appendix A
Derivation of the Equations of Motion

We collect here the derivation of the equations for the (deformed) models of our interest.

A.1
Deformed Principal Chiral Model

If we consider an infinitesimal variation of the group element
g\in G
as
\delta g=g\epsilon
with
\epsilon\in\mathfrak{g}
, then the variation of the Maurer-Cartan current reads
\delta j_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon+[j_{\mu},\epsilon]
. Taking
x_{1}=\tr(j_{\mu}j^{\mu})
and
x_{2}=\tr(j_{\mu}j_{\nu})\tr(j^{\mu}j^{\nu})
(as done in (
43
)) one finds

\displaystyle\delta x_{1}

\displaystyle=2\tr(j^{\mu}\delta j_{\mu})=2\tr(j^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}\epsilon+[j_{\mu},\epsilon]))=2\tr(j^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\epsilon),

(133)

where we used that the term with
[j_{\mu},\epsilon]
vanishes thanks to the cyclicity of the trace. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\delta x_{2}

\displaystyle=4\tr(j^{\mu}j^{\nu})\tr(j_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\epsilon).

(134)

It follows that the variation of the generic Lagrangian
L(x_{1},x_{2})
is

\displaystyle\delta L

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}\tr\left(\partial_{\mu}\epsilon\,j^{\mu}\right)+4\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}\tr\left(j_{\mu}j_{\nu}\right)\tr\left(\partial^{\mu}\epsilon\,j^{\nu}\right)\,.

(135)

When considering the variation of the action
\mathcal{S}=\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,L
, we can integrate by parts and conclude that

\delta\mathcal{S}=-\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,\tr\left[\epsilon\,\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{J}^{\mu}\right]\,,

(136)

where
\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}
is

\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}=2\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}j_{\mu}+4\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}\tr[j_{\mu}j_{\nu}]\,j^{\nu}\,,

(137)

as we presented in eq. (
46
) of the main text.

Adding a Wess-Zumino Term.

When adding a WZ term to the action, of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}=\frac{\kay}{6}\int\text{d}^{3}\sigma\,\epsilon^{ijk}\tr\left(j_{i}[j_{j},j_{k}]\right),

(138)

the equations of motion are modified by the additional contribution

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}=\frac{\kay}{2}\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,\tr\left(\epsilon(\partial_{+}j_{-}-\partial_{-}j_{+})\right).

(139)

Therefore we have that they take the form of the conservation of

\partial_{+}\left(\hay\mathfrak{J}_{-}+\kay j_{-}\right)+\partial_{-}\left(\hay\mathfrak{J}_{+}-\kay j_{+}\right)=0\,,

(140)

with
\mathfrak{J}_{\mu}
as in (
137
), that is eq. (
74
) of the main text.

A.2
Deformed Symmetric Space Sigma Model

In the case of the SSSM it is conventient to rewrite

\delta j_{\mu}=D_{\mu}\epsilon+\left[j^{(2)}_{\mu},\,\epsilon\right]\,,

(141)

where
D_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative defined in (
85
). Then, writing (
93
) as

x_{1}=g^{\mu\nu}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu}j^{(2)}_{\nu}]\,,\qquad x_{2}=g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu}j^{(2)}_{\nu}]\tr[j^{(2)}_{\rho}j^{(2)}_{\sigma}]\,,

(142)

one obtains

\displaystyle\delta x_{1}

\displaystyle=2g^{\mu\nu}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu}\delta j^{(2)}_{\nu}]=2g^{\mu\nu}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu}\delta j_{\nu}]=2g^{\mu\nu}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu}D_{\nu}\epsilon],

(143)

where we used that
\tr(x^{(0)}y^{(2)})=0
for any
x^{(0)}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
y^{(2)}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, and the cyclicity of the trace, and all indices are contracted with the metric. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\delta x_{2}

\displaystyle=4g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu}j^{(2)}_{\nu}]\tr[j^{(2)}_{\rho}D_{\sigma}\epsilon].

(144)

This is enough to conclude that the variation of a Lagrangian
L(x_{1},x_{2})
is

\displaystyle\delta L

\displaystyle=2g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}\tr\left[D_{\mu}\epsilon\,j^{(2)}_{\nu}\right]+4g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}\tr\left[j^{(2)}_{\mu}j^{(2)}_{\nu}\right]\tr\left[D_{\rho}\epsilon\,j^{(2)}_{\sigma}\right]\,,

(145)

and that the variation of the action
\mathcal{S}=\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,L
is

\delta\mathcal{S}=-\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,g^{\mu\nu}\tr\left[\epsilon\,\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\nu}\right],

(146)

where

\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\mu}=2\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}j^{(2)}_{\mu}+4g^{\nu\rho}\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}\tr[j^{(2)}_{\mu},j^{(2)}_{\nu}]\,j^{(2)}_{\rho}\,,

(147)

as in eq. (
95
) of the main text.

A.3
Deformed Semi-Symmetric Space Sigma Model

The calculations in the case of the sSSSM follow closely what was done above. In this case we want to rewrite

\delta j_{\mu}=D_{\mu}\epsilon+\left[j^{(1)}_{\mu}+j^{(2)}_{\mu}+j^{(3)}_{\mu},\,\epsilon\right]\,.

(148)

Now we take
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
as in (
142
) above, and compute the variations

\displaystyle\delta x_{1}

\displaystyle=2g^{\mu\nu}\str\left[j^{(2)}_{\mu}D_{\nu}\epsilon+\epsilon[j^{(2)}_{\mu},j^{(1)}_{\nu}+j^{(3)}_{\nu}]\right],

(149)

\displaystyle\delta x_{2}

\displaystyle=4g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\str\left[j^{(2)}_{\mu}j^{(2)}_{\nu}\right]\str\left[j^{(2)}_{\rho}D_{\sigma}\epsilon+\epsilon[j^{(2)}_{\rho},j^{(1)}_{\sigma}+j^{(3)}_{\sigma}]\right].

The Lagrangian of the sSSSM can be written as the sum
L_{\text{sSSSM}}=L_{\text{sSSSM}}^{g}+L_{\text{sSSSM}}^{\varepsilon}
where

\displaystyle L_{\text{sSSSM}}^{g}=\frac{1}{2}\,\str\left[g^{\mu\nu}j^{(2)}_{\mu}j^{(2)}_{\nu}\right],\qquad\qquad L_{\text{sSSSM}}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\,\str\left[\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}j^{(1)}_{\mu}j^{(3)}_{\nu}\right],

(150)

and after promoting
L_{\text{sSSSM}}^{g}
to
L^{g,\gamma}_{\text{sSSSM}}(x_{1},x_{2})
one finds that

\displaystyle\delta S^{g,\gamma}_{\text{sSSSM}}=-\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,g^{\mu\nu}\str\left[\epsilon\,(\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\nu}+[j^{(1)}_{\mu}+j^{(3)}_{\mu},\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\nu}])\right],

(151)

where
\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\mu}
is still given by the same expression.
Finally, one also has

\delta S^{\varepsilon}_{\text{sSSSM}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\str\left[\epsilon\left(D_{\mu}(j^{(1)}_{\nu}-j^{(3)}_{\nu})+\left[j^{(1)}_{\mu}+j^{(2)}_{\mu}+j^{(3)}_{\mu},j^{(1)}_{\nu}-j^{(3)}_{\nu}\right]\right)\right].

(152)

We conclude by noting that the equations of motion for the deformed sSSSM can be written in the form

\displaystyle 0=D_{\mu}\Lambda^{\mu}+\left[j_{\mu},\Lambda^{\mu}\right],\qquad\text{where}\quad\Lambda^{\mu}=g^{\mu\nu}\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\left(j^{(1)}_{\nu}-j^{(3)}_{\nu}\right).

(153)

Projecting this expression over the four subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
gives

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{1}:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}j^{(1)}_{\nu}=\left[\left(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}j^{(2)}_{\mu}-2g^{\mu\nu}\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\mu}\right),j^{(3)}_{\nu}\right],

(154)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{2}:

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\left(\left[j^{(1)}_{\mu},j^{(1)}_{\nu}\right]-\left[j^{(3)}_{\mu},j^{(3)}_{\nu}\right]\right),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{3}:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}j^{(3)}_{\nu}=\left[\left(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}j^{(2)}_{\mu}+2g^{\mu\nu}\mathfrak{J}^{(2)}_{\mu}\right),j^{(1)}_{\nu}\right],

while the equation is automatically satisfied along
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.
Using the Maurer-Cartan equation (
105
), this reduces to (
114
) and, in the undeformed case, to (
108
).

Adding a Wess-Zumino term.

If we consider the action (
125
), we have an additonal contribution to the equations of motion, which is proportional to
\kay
. In particular, let us define

\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}=\kay\int\limits_{B}\text{d}^{3}\sigma\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon^{ijk}\str\left(j^{(2)}_{i}[j^{(2)}_{j},j^{(2)}_{k}]+3j^{(1)}_{i}[j^{(3)}_{j},j^{(2)}_{k}]\right).

(155)

Now, to compute the variation of
\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}
, we cannot use the compatibility of the supertrace with the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
grading. One finds that the variation of the integrand can be separated into two contributions, namely a total derivative and an expression to be integrated in 3 dimensions

\delta\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}=\kay\int\limits_{B}\text{d}^{3}\sigma\ \varepsilon^{ijk}\str\left(\partial_{i}B_{jk}+Z_{ijk}\right),

(156)

where

B_{ij}=\epsilon^{(2)}([j^{(2)}_{i},j^{(2)}_{j}]+[j^{(1)}_{i},j^{(3)}_{j}])+\epsilon^{(1)}[j^{(3)}_{i},j^{(2)}_{j}]+\epsilon^{(3)}[j^{(1)}_{i},j^{(2)}_{j}].

(157)

Importantly,
Z_{ijk}
identically vanishes thanks to the Maurer-Cartan identity and the Jacobi identity on the superalgebra. This is crucial in order to have a well-defined WZ term, yielding equations of motion that only depend on the boundary (i.e. 2-dimensional) degrees of freedom. To conclude, the equations of motion receive the additional contribution of
\kay\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}
, and they read as in (
129
).

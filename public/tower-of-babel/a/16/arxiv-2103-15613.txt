On gyration radius distributions of star-like macromolecules
Yury A. Budkov, Andrei L. Kolesnikov

Abstract
Using the path integral approach, we obtain the characteristic functions of the gyration radius distributions for Gaussian star and Gaussian rosette macromolecules. We derive the analytical expressions for cumulants of both distributions. Applying the steepest descent method, we estimate the probability distribution functions of the gyration radius in the limit of a large number of star and rosette arms in two limiting regimes: for strongly expanded and strongly collapsed macromolecules. We show that in both cases, in the regime of a large gyration radius relative to its mean-square value, the probability distribution functions can be described by the Gaussian functions. In the shrunk macromolecule regime, both distribution functions tend to zero faster than any power of the gyration radius. Based on the asymptotic behavior of the distribution functions and the behavior of statistical dispersions, we demonstrate that the probability distribution function for the rosette is more densely localized near its maximum than that for the star polymer. We construct the interpolation formula for the gyration radius distribution of the Gaussian star macromolecule which can help to take into account the conformational entropy of the flexible star macromolecules within the Flory-type mean-field theories.



I
Introduction

Polymers with a branched structure are very promising compounds for modern materials science and engineering. Branched macromolecules are usually used as elementary structural units of a vast range of functional polymer materials, such as liquid gels and aerogels, elastomers, melts, thermoplastics,
etc.
High functionality of branched polymers opens new opportunities for their use in drug delivery, robotics, antibacterial and antifouling surfaces, food industry and others applications. In biological processes, such as stabilization of globular proteins or transcriptional regulation of genes, an important role is played by multiple loop formation in branched macromolecules. That is why it is of fundamental interest to study the conformational properties of complex polymer architectures
Haydukivska, Blavatska, and Paturej 2020
;
Douglas, Roovers, and Freed 1990
.

In order to understand the role of ring constituents in compactification of branched polymers, the authors of paper
Blavatska and Metzler 2015
considered a model of a star-like polymer, where one part of the arms were linear Gaussian polymers, while the rest were ring ones. The authors studied the conformational behavior of star-like Gaussian macromolecules containing a total of
f_{1}
ring arms (petals) and
f_{2}
linear chains (branches). Within the framework of the continuous model of a Gaussian macromolecule, they applied the path integral approach to estimate the gyration radius and asphericity of the typical conformation as functions of the
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
parameters. In particular, their results reveal the extent of anisotropy of star-like topologies relative to the rosette structures of the same total molecular weight. Moreover, their analytical results quantitatively confirm the compactification (decrease in the effective size) of multiple loop polymer structures as compared with structures containing only linear segments. The authors of paper
Haydukivska, Blavatska, and Paturej 2020
determined the mean-square gyration radius and the average hydrodynamic radius of the star-like Gaussian polymer, and estimated their ratio with its dependence on the numbers of petals and branches. Moreover, they obtained quantitative estimate of the compactification degree of these polymers with an increasing number of petals as compared to linear or star-shaped molecules of the same total molecular weight
Haydukivska, Blavatska, and Paturej 2020
.

Despite the fact that the behavior of the mean-square gyration radius of branched macromolecules has been extensively studied
Blavatska and Metzler 2015
;
Haydukivska, Blavatska, and Paturej 2020
, the behavior of the probability distribution function (PDF) of the gyration radius of star-like macromolecules remains unclear. In particular, it is important to understand how the replacement of the linear branches with ring ones can qualitatively change the PDF behavior. In this paper, we attempt to answer this question, considering two limiting examples of the branched polymer: a Gaussian star polymer with
f
identical branches (see Fig. 1a) and a Gaussian rosette (Fig. 1b) with
f
petals. Each arm is a Gaussian linear polymer for the star macromolecule and a Gaussian ring polymer – for the rosette. In the framework of the path integral approach proposed in our previous paper
Budkov and Kolesnikov 2016
, we for the first time obtain analytical expressions for characteristic functions of the gyration radius distributions and then estimate the asymptotics of the corresponding PDF for star and rosette macromolecules in the limit of a large number of arms.

II
Statement of the problem

The probability distribution function (PDF) of the gyration radius of star-like Gaussian macromolecules with
f
linear or ring arms (see Fig. 1(a,b)) can be written as the following path integral

P(R_{g}^{2})=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}_{1}}{Z_{0}^{(1)}}..\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}_{f}}{Z_{0}^{(f)}}e^{-\frac{3}{2b^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{f}\int\limits_{0}^{N}d\tau\dot{\mathbb{r}}_{i}^{2}(\tau)}\delta\left(R_{g}^{2}-\frac{1}{2N^{2}f^{2}}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{f}\int\limits_{0}^{N}\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{N}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\left(\mathbb{r}_{i}(\tau_{1})-\mathbb{r}_{j}(\tau_{2})\right)^{2}\right),

(1)

where

Z_{0}^{(i)}=\int{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}_{i}}\exp\left[-\frac{3}{2b^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{N}d\tau\dot{\mathbb{r}}_{i}^{2}(\tau)\right]

(2)

are the normalization constants of the Gaussian measures;
N
is the polymerization degree and
b
is the Kuhn length. Using the Fourier-representation of the delta-function

\delta(x)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}e^{-i\xi x},

(3)

we obtain

P(R_{g}^{2})=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}e^{-i\xi R_{g}^{2}}K(\xi),

(4)

where

K(\xi)=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}_{1}}{Z_{0}^{(1)}}..\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}_{f}}{Z_{0}^{(f)}}e^{-\frac{3}{2Nb^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{f}\int\limits_{0}^{1}ds\dot{\mathbb{r}}_{i}^{2}(s)+\frac{i\xi}{2f^{2}}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{f}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{1}ds_{1}ds_{2}\left(\mathbb{r}_{i}(s_{1})-\mathbb{r}_{j}(s_{2})\right)^{2}}

(5)

is the characteristic function
Gnedenko 2018
, where we turned to the variables
s=\tau/N\in[0,1]
. Our aim is to calculate the characteristic function
K(\xi)
as the Gaussian path integral and then to evaluate the PDF by the steepest descent method
Lavrentiev and Shabat 1987
. As is well known, the characteristic function can be related to the moments
\mu_{k}
and cumulants
\chi_{k}
by the expansions

K(\xi)=1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}}{k!}(i\xi)^{k},

(6)

\ln K(\xi)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{k}}{k!}(i\xi)^{k},

(7)

so that the moments
\mu_{k}
and cumulants
\chi_{k}
are related to each other by the expressions

\mu_{1}=\chi_{1},\penalty\ \mu_{2}=\chi_{2}+\mu_{1}^{2},..

(8)

III
Characteristic functions

In this section, we demonstrate a derivation of the analytical expressions for the characteristic functions of star and rosette Gaussian polymers within the path integral approach.

III.1
Star polymer

Here, we consider the case of a star-like Gaussian polymer – a set of
f
identical Gaussian chains grafted to one point (see, Fig. 1a). Using the equality

e^{-\frac{\mathbb{b}^{2}}{4a}}=\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)^{3/2}\int d\mathbb{x}e^{-a\mathbb{x}^{2}+i\mathbb{b}\mathbb{x}},

(9)

the characteristic function can be rewritten as follows

K(\xi)=\int..\int\prod\limits_{i=1}^{f}\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}_{i}}{Z_{0}^{(i)}}e^{-\frac{3}{2Nb^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}ds\left(\dot{\mathbb{r}}_{i}^{2}(s)-\omega^{2}\mathbb{r}_{i}^{2}(s)\right)}\exp\left[-\frac{i\xi}{f^{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{f}\int\limits_{0}^{1}ds\mathbb{r}_{i}(s)\right)^{2}\right]=

\left(\frac{f^{2}}{4\pi i\xi}\right)^{3/2}\int d\mathbb{x}e^{\frac{if^{2}\mathbb{x}^{2}}{4\xi}}\left[\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}}{Z_{0}}e^{-S[\mathbb{r}]}\right]^{f},

(10)

where the auxiliary functional

S[\mathbb{r}]=\frac{3}{2Nb^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}ds\left(\dot{\mathbb{r}}^{2}(s)-\omega^{2}\mathbb{r}^{2}(s)\right)-i\mathbb{x}\int_{0}^{1}ds\mathbb{r}(s),

(11)

and notation
\omega^{2}=2Nb^{2}i\xi/3f
are introduced. Then it is necessary to calculate the path integral in eq. (
10
). Using the saddle-point method
Budkov and Kolesnikov 2016
;
Paradezhenko, Gascoigne, and Brilliantov 2020
;
Zinn-Justin 2010
, we obtain

\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}}{Z_{0}}e^{-S[\mathbb{r}]}=\left(\frac{3}{2\pi Nb^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{\omega}{\sin{\omega}}\right)^{3/2}\int d\mathbb{R}e^{-S[\mathbb{v}_{SP}]},

(12)

where

\mathbb{v}_{SP}(s)=-\frac{i\mathbb{x}Nb^{2}}{3\omega^{2}}\left(1-\cos{\omega s}-\frac{\sin{\omega s}(1-\cos{\omega})}{\sin{\omega}}\right)+\frac{\mathbb{R}\sin{\omega s}}{\sin{\omega}}

(13)

is the solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation
\delta S[\mathbb{r}]/\delta\mathbb{r}(s)=0
with the boundary conditions
\mathbb{r}(0)=0
and
\mathbb{r}(1)=\mathbb{R}
. Taking into account that

S[\mathbb{v}_{SP}]=\frac{3R^{2}\omega}{2Nb^{2}\tan{\omega}}-\frac{i\mathbb{R}\mathbb{x}\tan{\frac{\omega}{2}}}{\omega}+\frac{i\mathbb{x}^{2}f}{4\xi}-\frac{i\mathbb{x}^{2}f}{2\xi}\frac{\tan{\frac{\omega}{2}}}{\omega},

(14)

after integration over the end point of the chain,
\mathbb{R}
, in eq. (
12
), and subsequent integration over the auxiliary variable,
\mathbb{x}
, we arrive at the new analytical expression for the characteristic function

K(\xi)=\left(\frac{\omega}{\sin{\omega}}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{1}{\cos{\omega}}\right)^{3(f-1)/2}.

(15)

Note that for a linear Gaussian chain (
f=1
), eq. (
15
) transforms into the well-known characteristic function
K(\xi)=(\omega/\sin{\omega})^{3/2}
that was first obtained by Fixman
Fixman 1962
within the Wang-Uhlenbeck method
Yamakawa 1971
. The logarithm of the characteristic function can be expanded into the following power series

\ln K(\xi)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{k-1}\frac{2^{2k-1}}{k(2k)!}(1+(f-1)(2^{2k}-1))B_{2k-1}\left(\frac{Nb^{2}}{f}\right)^{k}(i\xi)^{k},

(16)

so that the cumulants (see, eq. (
7
)) are determined by

\chi_{k}=\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{k-1}\frac{2^{2k-1}(k-1)!}{(2k)!}(1+(f-1)(2^{2k}-1))B_{2k-1}\left(\frac{Nb^{2}}{f}\right)^{k},

(17)

where we use the following power series

\ln\frac{\omega}{\sin{\omega}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2k-1}B_{2k-1}}{k(2k)!}\omega^{2k},

(18)

\ln\cos{\omega}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2k-1}B_{2k-1}(1-2^{2k})}{k(2k)!}\omega^{2k},

(19)

with the Bernoulli numbers
B_{1}=1/6
,
B_{3}=1/30
,
B_{5}=1/42
,…
In particular, we obtain the well-known expression
Zimm and Stockmayer 1949
;
Blavatska and Metzler 2015

\left<R_{g}^{2}\right>=\mu_{1}=\chi_{1}=\frac{3f-2}{f}\frac{Nb^{2}}{6},

(20)

and the second moment

\left<R_{g}^{4}\right>=\mu_{2}=\chi_{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}=\frac{(Nb^{2})^{2}}{540}\frac{135f^{2}-120f+4}{f^{2}}.

(21)

In a similar way, using eq. (
17
), we can calculate the higher moments of the distribution.

III.2
Rosette

Now let us calculate the characteristic function for a Gaussian rosette with
f
petals (Fig. 1b). Performing the same calculations as in the previous subsection, we obtain

K(\xi)=\left(\frac{f^{2}}{4\pi i\xi}\right)^{3/2}\int d\mathbb{x}e^{\frac{if^{2}\mathbb{x}^{2}}{4\xi}}\left[\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}}{Z_{0}}e^{-S[\mathbb{r}]}\right]^{f},

(22)

where the random vector-function
\mathbb{r}(s)
satisfies the following boundary conditions
\mathbb{r}(0)=\mathbb{r}(1)=0
(the rosette center is placed at the origin) and the
S[\mathbb{r}]
functional is determined by eq.(
11
). Expanding the random vector-function,
\mathbb{r}(s)
, into a Fourier series

\mathbb{r}(s)=\sqrt{2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\bf\rho}_{n}\sin{\pi ns},

(23)

we turn to the integration over the Fourier components
Budkov and Kolesnikov 2016
,
\mathbb{\rho}_{n}
, i.e.

\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\mathbb{r}}{Z_{0}}e^{-S[\mathbb{r}]}=\frac{\int..\int\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}d\mathbb{\rho}_{n}\exp\left[-\frac{3}{2Nb^{2}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(\pi^{2}n^{2}-\omega^{2})\mathbb{\rho}_{n}^{2}+\frac{2i\sqrt{2}\mathbb{x}}{\pi}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{\rho}_{2k-1}}{2k-1}\right]}{\int..\int\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}d\mathbb{\rho}_{n}\exp\left[-\frac{3}{2Nb^{2}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi^{2}n^{2}\mathbb{\rho}_{n}^{2}\right]}=

\left(\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\pi^{2}n^{2}}{\pi^{2}n^{2}-\omega^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\exp\left[-\frac{4Nb^{2}\mathbb{x}^{2}}{3\pi^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)^{2}(\pi^{2}(2k-1)^{2}-\omega^{2})}\right]=

\left(\frac{\omega}{\sin\omega}\right)^{3/2}\exp\left[\frac{i\mathbb{x}^{2}f}{4\xi}\left(\frac{2}{\omega}\tan{\frac{\omega}{2}-1}\right)\right].

(24)

Therefore, substituting eq. (
24
) for eq. (
22
), after the integration over variable
\mathbb{x}
, we arrive at the new analytical expressions for the characteristic function for the Gaussian rosette

K(\xi)=\left(\frac{\omega}{\sin{\omega}}\right)^{3(f-1)/2}\left(\frac{\omega}{2\sin{\frac{\omega}{2}}}\right)^{3}.

(25)

Eq. (
25
) allows us to obtain analytical expressions for cumulants. Indeed, the logarithm of the characteristic function takes the form

\ln{K(\xi)}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2k-1}}{k(2k)!}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{k-1}\left(1+2^{2k-1}(f-1)\right)\left(\frac{Nb^{2}}{f}\right)^{k}(i\xi)^{k},

(26)

so that the cumulants can be calculated by the following analytical expression:

\chi_{k}=\frac{(k-1)!}{(2k)!}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{k-1}\left(1+2^{2k-1}(f-1)\right)B_{2k-1}\left(\frac{Nb^{2}}{f}\right)^{k}.

(27)

Using eqs. (
28
), we obtain the moments:

\mu_{1}=\left<R_{g}^{2}\right>=\frac{Nb^{2}}{12}\frac{2f-1}{f},\penalty\ \mu_{2}=\left<R_{g}^{4}\right>=\frac{60f^{2}-44f+1}{f^{2}}\frac{N^{2}b^{4}}{2160},...

(28)

Note that the above expression for the first moment,
\mu_{1}
, was obtained recently in paper
Blavatska and Metzler 2015
.

IV
Probability distribution functions

In this section, we apply the steepest descent method to study the asymptotic behavior of the PDF of the Gaussian star-like macromolecules discussed above
Lavrentiev and Shabat 1987
.

IV.1
Star polymer

For the star polymer, the PDF can be rewritten in the form

P(R_{g}^{2})=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}\exp\left[-W(\xi)\right],

(29)

where we introduce the following auxiliary function

W(\xi)=\frac{(3f-2)\alpha^{2}\omega^{2}(\xi)}{4}-\frac{3}{2}\ln{\frac{\omega(\xi)}{\sin{\omega(\xi)}}}+\frac{3(f-1)}{2}\ln{\cos{\omega(\xi)}}

(30)

and expansion factor
\alpha=R_{g}/\left<R_{g}^{2}\right>^{1/2}
. In order to estimate the PDF, we use the steepest descent method
Lavrentiev and Shabat 1987
. At first, we have to find the saddle-point,
\xi_{0}
, from the solution of the saddle-point equation

W^{\prime}(\xi_{0})=\frac{3}{2\xi_{0}}\left(\frac{(3f-2)\alpha^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{6}-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\tan{\omega_{0}}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}(f-1)\omega_{0}\tan{\omega_{0}}\right)=0,

(31)

where
\omega_{0}=\omega(\xi_{0})
. Note that the contour of integration must be deformed along the steepest descent line. In the vicinity of
\xi_{0}
we have

W(\xi)=W(\xi_{0})+\frac{1}{2}W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})(\xi-\xi_{0})^{2}+..,

(32)

where the second derivative takes the form

W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})=\frac{3}{8\xi_{0}^{2}}\left(2-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\tan{\omega_{0}}}-\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{\sin^{2}{\omega_{0}}}+(f-1)\left(\omega_{0}\tan{\omega_{0}}-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\cos^{2}{\omega_{0}}}\right)\right).

(33)

In what follows, we will consider only the case of a large number of arms (
f\gg 1
) for two limiting regimes –
\alpha\gg 1
and
\alpha\ll 1
. In this
"
thermodynamic
"
limit, the steepest descent method must yield the exact asymptotic behavior of the PDF. The saddle-point equation at
f\gg 1
takes the following simplified form

\frac{\tan{\omega_{0}}}{\omega_{0}}=\alpha^{2},

(34)

whereas the second derivative is

W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{3f}{8\xi_{0}^{2}}\left(\omega_{0}\tan{\omega_{0}}-\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{\cos^{2}{\omega_{0}}}\right).

(35)

For the highly expanded polymer star (
\alpha\gg 1
), the saddle-point is
\omega_{0}\simeq\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{2}{\pi\alpha^{2}}
, which yields

W(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{3f\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{16}-\frac{3f}{2}\ln{\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{2}}-\frac{3f}{2},\penalty\ W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{\alpha^{4}N^{2}b^{4}}{6f}.

(36)

Thus, calculating the integral, we obtain

P(R_{g}^{2})\simeq\left(\frac{3f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{e\pi}{2}\right)^{3f/2}\frac{\alpha^{3f}}{Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{3\pi^{2}f\alpha^{2}}{16}\right].

(37)

In the opposite case,
\alpha\ll 1
, the saddle-point is
\omega_{0}\simeq{i}/{\alpha^{2}}
. In this case, we have

W(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{3f}{4\alpha^{2}}-\frac{3f}{2}\ln{2},\penalty\ W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{\alpha^{6}N^{2}b^{4}}{6f},

(38)

and the PDF acquires the following form

P(R_{g}^{2})=\left(\frac{3f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\frac{2^{3f/2}}{\alpha^{3}Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{3f}{4\alpha^{2}}\right].

(39)

Collecting together the above results, we eventually obtain the following limiting laws for the PDF

P(R_{g}^{2})\simeq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{3f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{e\pi}{2}\right)^{3f/2}\frac{\alpha^{3f}}{Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{3\pi^{2}f\alpha^{2}}{16}\right],&\alpha\gg 1\\
\left(\frac{3f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\frac{2^{3f/2}}{\alpha^{3}Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{3f}{4\alpha^{2}}\right],&\alpha\ll 1.\end{cases}

(40)

It is worth noting that the PDF can be described by the Gaussian function only at
\alpha\gg 1
(PDF has a Gaussian tail). At small
\alpha
, the PDF goes to zero faster than any positive power of
\alpha
. The obtained asymptotic relations (
40
) allow us to estimate the limiting behavior of the conformational entropy,
S(R_{g}^{2})=\ln{P(R_{g}^{2})}
, of the Gaussian star polymer in the limit
f\gg 1
. Thus, we have

{S(R_{g}^{2})}/{f}\simeq\begin{cases}-\frac{3\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{16}+3\ln{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}\ln{\frac{\pi e}{2}},&\alpha\gg 1\\
-\frac{3}{4\alpha^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}\ln{2},&\alpha\ll 1,\end{cases}

(41)

where we kept only the terms that are proportional to
f
. We would like to note that the limiting relations (
57
) can be used for a theoretical description of the conformational behavior of star macromolecules in solvent media in the same manner as the conformational entropy of the linear Gaussian chain
Budkov and Kolesnikov 2016
;
Fixman 1962
can be used within the Flory-type mean-field theories
Budkov and Kolesnikov 2016
;
Budkov and Kolesnikov 2018
.

IV.2
Rosette

In a similar way, for the Gaussian rosette, the PDF can be written in the form

P(R_{g}^{2})=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}\exp\left[-W(\xi)\right],

(42)

where we introduce the following auxiliary function

W(\xi)=\frac{(2f-1)\alpha^{2}\omega^{2}(\xi)}{8}-3\ln{\frac{\omega(\xi)}{2\sin{\frac{\omega(\xi)}{2}}}}-\frac{3(f-1)}{2}\ln{\frac{\omega(\xi)}{\sin{\omega(\xi)}}},

(43)

with the expansion factor,
\alpha^{2}=R_{g}^{2}/\left<R_{g}^{2}\right>
. The saddle-point equation is

W^{\prime}(\xi_{0})=\frac{3}{2\xi_{0}}\left(\frac{(2f-1)\alpha^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{12}+\frac{\omega_{0}}{2\tan{\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}}}-1-\frac{f-1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\tan{\omega_{0}}}\right)\right),

(44)

where
\omega_{0}=\omega(\xi_{0})
. The second derivative is

W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})=\frac{3}{4\xi_{0}^{2}}\left(f+1-\frac{\omega_{0}}{2\tan{\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}}}-\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{4\sin^{2}{\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}}}-\frac{1}{2}(f-1)\frac{\omega_{0}}{\sin{\omega_{0}}}\left(\cos{\omega_{0}}+\frac{\omega_{0}}{\sin{\omega_{0}}}\right)\right).

(45)

As for the star polymer, we consider only the case of a large number of arms (
f\gg 1
). The saddle-point equation in this case is simplified to

\frac{3}{\omega_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\tan{\omega_{0}}}\right)={\alpha^{2}}.

(46)

In the regime of a highly expanded macromolecule (
\alpha\gg 1
), the solution of the saddle-point equation is
\omega_{0}\simeq\pi-3/\pi\alpha^{2}
, which yields

W(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}f}{4}-\frac{3f}{2}\ln{\frac{\pi^{2}e\alpha^{2}}{3}},\penalty\ W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{\alpha^{4}N^{2}b^{4}}{144f}.`

(47)

The PDF in this regime has the following asymptotic behavior

P(R_{g}^{2})=\left(\frac{72f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{e\pi^{2}}{3}\right)^{3f/2}\frac{\alpha^{3f}}{Nb^{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi^{2}f\alpha^{2}}{4}}.

(48)

In the opposite regime of a collapsed macromolecule,
\alpha\ll 1
, the saddle-point is
\omega_{0}\simeq 3i/{\alpha^{2}}
. Thus, one can get

W(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{9f}{4\alpha^{2}}+\frac{3f}{2}\ln{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}},\penalty\ W^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{0})\simeq\frac{N^{2}b^{4}\alpha^{6}}{162f}.

(49)

After the integration, we arrive at

P(R_{g}^{2})\simeq 6^{3f/2}\left(\frac{243f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\alpha^{-3f}}{Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{9f}{4\alpha^{2}}\right].

(50)

Collecting together the obtained results, we get

P(R_{g}^{2})\simeq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{72f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}e}{3}\right)^{3f/2}\frac{\alpha^{3f}}{Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{\pi^{2}f\alpha^{2}}{4}\right],&\alpha\gg 1\\
\left(\frac{243f}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}6^{3f/2}\frac{\alpha^{-3f}}{Nb^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{9f}{4\alpha^{2}}\right],&\alpha\ll 1.\end{cases}

(51)

The conformational entropy in the thermodynamic limit
f\gg 1
has the following limiting behavior

S(R_{g}^{2})/f\simeq\begin{cases}-\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{4}+3\ln{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}\ln{\frac{\pi^{2}e}{3}},&\alpha\gg 1\\
-\frac{9}{4\alpha^{2}}-3\ln{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}\ln{6},&\alpha\ll 1.\end{cases}

(52)

\frac{6^{5}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}Nb^{2}}\alpha^{-9}\exp\left[-\frac{9}{2\alpha^{2}}\right],&\alpha\ll 1.\end{cases}

\sim\exp\left[-\pi^{2}f\alpha^{2}/4\right]
, whereas for a star polymer - as
\sim\exp\left[-3\pi^{2}f\alpha^{2}/16\right]
. Thus, the first distribution function goes to zero at
\alpha\to\infty
much faster than the second one. At
\alpha\ll 1
, the distribution function, described by the function
\sim\exp\left[-9f/(4\alpha^{2})\right]
, also tends to zero much faster than the second distribution function, which behaves as
\sim\exp\left[-3f/(4\alpha^{2})\right]
. Therefore, the probability distribution function of the Gaussian rosette gyration radius is localized near its maximal value more densely than the corresponding distribution function for the Gaussian star. Thus, we can say that the Gaussian star with a large number of arms is not only larger
Blavatska and Metzler 2015
, but also much softer than the Gaussian rosette. This conclusion can be made using other arguments. As is well known
Gnedenko 2018
, the localization of the distribution can be quantitatively characterized by the dispersion
\chi_{2}=\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}^{2}
. In order to compare the localization of the gyration radius distributions in the limit
f\to\infty
, it is necessary to calculate
\lim_{f\to\infty}\chi_{2}^{(r)}(f)/\chi_{2}^{(s)}(f)
, where
\chi_{2}^{(r)}(f)
and
\chi_{2}^{(s)}(f)
are the dispersions of the gyration radius distributions for the rosette and the star, respectively. Using eqs. (
17

27

k=2

\lim_{f\to\infty}\frac{\chi_{2}^{(r)}(f)}{\chi_{2}^{(s)}(f)}=\frac{1}{15}.

(55)

It should be mentioned that the interpolation formula of the probability distribution function allows us to describe the thermodynamic behavior of the star polymer. Starting from the entropy of the ideal polymeric star:

S(R_{g}^{2})/k_{B}f=-\frac{3}{4\alpha^{2}}-\frac{3\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}}{16}+f^{-1}\ln\biggl(\alpha^{2nf}+\alpha^{2mf}\biggl)+\textit{const},

VI
Conclusions

In conclusion, using the path integral approach, we have for the first time calculated the characteristic functions of the gyration radius distributions for Gaussian star and Gaussian rosette polymers. We have also been the first to obtain analytical expressions for the cumulants of both distributions. Using the steepest descent method, we have estimated the probability distribution functions of the gyration radius in the limit of a large number of arms of the star and rosette in two limiting regimes: for strongly expanded and strongly collapsed polymers. We have found that in the regime of a large gyration radius relative to its mean-square value the probability distribution functions in both cases can be described by the Gaussian functions. In the opposite regime of a strongly collapsed macromolecule both distribution functions tend to zero faster than any power of the gyration radius. Based on the asymptotic analysis, we have found that the probability distribution function for the rosette is more densely localized near its maximum than that is for the star polymer. Using the nonlinear least-square approach, we have proposed the interpolation formula for the probability distribution function of the gyration radius for the Gaussian star macromolecule.

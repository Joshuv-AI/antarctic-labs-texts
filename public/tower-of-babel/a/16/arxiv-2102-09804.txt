Local Convergence of Adaptive Gradient Descent Optimizers Thanks: This paper expands the results and presents proofs that are referenced in [ 7 ] .
Sebastian Bock and Martin Georg Weiß

Abstract
Adaptive Moment Estimation (ADAM) is a very popular training algorithm for deep neural networks and belongs to the family of adaptive gradient descent optimizers. However to the best of the authors knowledge no complete convergence analysis exists for ADAM. The contribution of this paper is a method for the local convergence analysis in batch mode for a deterministic fixed training set, which gives necessary conditions for the hyperparameters of the ADAM algorithm. Due to the local nature of the arguments the objective function can be non-convex but must be at least twice continuously differentiable. Then we apply this procedure to other adaptive gradient descent algorithms and show for most of them local convergence with hyperparameter bounds.



I
Introduction

Many problems in machine learning lead to a minimization problem in the weights
of a neural network:
Consider e.g. training data
(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{N},y_{N})
consisting of inputs
x_{i}
and
outputs
y_{i}
, and the task to determine a neural network
that has learned the relationship between inputs and outputs. This
corresponds to a function
y=F(w,x)
, parametrized by the weights
w
,
which minimizes the average loss function

f(w)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}l(x_{i},y_{i},w)=:\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(w)

over the training data. Typically the loss is built using
some norm for regression problems, e.g.

l(x,y,w)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert y-F(w,x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
, or using cross entropy for classification. Optimization algorithms construct a sequence

\{w_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of weights starting from an initial value
w_{0}
, which under
appropriate assumptions converges to some local minimum
w_{\star}
for general
non-convex
f
.
The most simple optimization algorithm for differentiable
f

is gradient descent with the update

w_{t+1}=w_{t}-\alpha\nabla f(w_{t})
and a learning
rate
\alpha>0
. For convex
f
conditions on the Lipschitz constant
L
of
\nabla f

guarantee convergence and give estimates for the rate of convergence, see
[
21
]
.
However
L
is hard to get in practice, and choosing
\alpha
too big leads to
oscillatory behaviour. Besides it is well known from optimization that the gradient
is not the only descent direction for
f
, neither is it optimal for finite step lengths,
see
[
22
]
,
but computation of the Hessian is usually prohibitive. This has led to the development of a family of algorithms which compute moments of first order,
that is approximate descent directions based on previous iterates of the gradient
like the initial momentum method
[
21
]
,
as well as second order moments to control the componentwise scaling and / or to adapt the
learning rate in AdaGrad
[
12
]
and ADAM
[
18
]
. For the ADAM
variant studied in this paper see Algorithm 1.
More algorithms exist with variants like
batch mode vs. online or incremental mode
– using
\nabla f(w_{t})
in iteration
t
vs.

\nabla f_{k}(w_{t})
where
k
iterates in a cyclic fashion over
1,\ldots,N
,
or deterministic vs. stochastic choice of the index
k
for
\nabla f_{k}(w_{t})
,
stochastic assumptions for the observation of
\nabla f(w_{t})
or
\nabla f_{k}(w_{t})
,
and so on.

However for most of these algorithms only partial convergence results are known.
The original proof of
[
18
]
is wrong as has been noted by several authors,
see
[
5
,
26
]
.
Modifying the algorithm to AMSGrad,

[
24
]
establishes bounds on
\left\lVert\nabla f(w_{t})\right\rVert
,
similar to the results in
[
10
]
for a class of algorithms
called Incremental Generalized ADAM.
Though none of the results shows convergence of the sequence
\{w_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
Also the proofs are lengthy and hardly reuse results from each other,
giving not much insight. General results from optimization cannot be used for several
reasons: First, the moments usually cannot be proven to be a descent direction. Second, the
learning rate cannot be shown to be a step size valid for the Wolfe conditions
for a line search, see
[
22
]
. The algorithm for the step taken in iteration

t
may explicitly contain the variable
t
in much more complicated ways than

\frac{1}{t}
in the Robbins-Monro approach
[
25
]
.

The contribution of this paper is a generally applicable method, based on the
theory of discrete time dynamical systems, which proves local convergence of ADAM. The results are purely qualitative because they hold
for learning rates sufficiently small, where ”sufficiently small” is defined
in terms of the eigenvalues of the Hessian in the unknown minimum
w_{\star}
.

The outline of this paper is as follows: In Section
II
we will discuss the preliminaries and the idea of the convergence prove, which will be discussed in Section
III
. The generality of this method is shown in Section
IV
, where we apply it to other optimizers like AdaDelta or AdaGrad. In numerical experiments in Section
V
we show the heuristic evidence of our theoretical proof. Finally, in Section
VI
we summarize the paper and give an outlook on ways to expand the convergence proof.

II-C

Idea

We consider the learning algorithm from
the standpoint of dynamical systems and define a common state vector
x
consisting
of the moments – like
m
and
v
for ADAM – and the weights, so we have

x=(m,v,w)
. Then the optimization can be written as an iteration

x_{t+1}=T(t,x_{t})
for some function
T:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times X\to X
with
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
which defines a non-autonomous dynamical system. The function
f
to be minimized
in the learning process, or rather its gradient, becomes a part of
T
.
If
f
is at least continuously differentiable a local minimum gives the necessary
condition
\nabla f(w_{\star})=0
. We show that this condition leads to a fixed point
x_{\star}
of
T
,
where the moments are all zero. We analyse the stability properties of this fixed
point and prove local asymptotic stability. This is done by considering a
time-variant iteration
T
as the perturbation of a time-invariant iteration

\bar{T}
where Banach-like fixed point arguments can be applied. We use the second method of Lyapunov
for stability analysis where the vanishing moments simplify the computation and
estimates for the eigenvalues.
Asymptotic stability however is equivalent to convergence of the iteration defined

T
to
x_{\star}
for all
x_{0}
sufficiently close to
x_{\star}
. The conditions needed
for the fixed point analysis and stability results require the learning rate to be
sufficiently small.
Note that these results cannot be obtained directly from standard fixed point
theorems for autonomous systems, because the iteration index
t
enters the dynamics.
Therefore also estimates of the eigenvalues depend on the iteration
t
, and even
a bound on the spectral radius uniform in
t
does not give the convergence results
presented here: It is well known that
\rho(A)<1
implies the existence of
a vector norm with induced matrix norm such that
\left\lVert A\right\rVert<1
, but this norm depends on

A
. So
\rho(A_{t})\leq c<1
for some
c
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
does not imply
the existence of a
single
norm such that
\left\lVert A_{t}\right\rVert<1
for all
t
.
We emphasize that the result is purely qualitative, giving no explicit guidance to the choice of the learning rates. The main advantage of our approach is the clearness of the proof, only computation of eigenvalues is needed once the iteration has been written in terms of
T
and
\bar{T}
. These calculations are much more simple than the lengthy estimates in
[
18
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
10
]
.

We stress that local convergence result is all one can hope for:
A global convergence proof cannot be obtained for most algorithms
including ADAM because a 2-cycle of the iteration exists for all values of
hyperparameters for simple quadratic objective functions, see
[
8
]
.

II-D

Preliminaries

We recall some standard definitions and facts from the theory of difference equations
and discrete time dynamical systems, see e.g.
[
2
, Definition 5.4.1]

or
[
16
]
.
Consider
T:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times M\to M
with
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
which defines a
non-autonomous dynamical system by the iteration

x_{t+1}=T(t,x_{t}),\quad t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},x_{0}\in M

(2)

with solutions

x:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to M
,
t\mapsto x_{t}
depending on the initial value
x_{0}
.
We use the notations
x_{t}=x(t;x_{0})
and
x=x(\cdot;x_{0})

to emphasize the dependence of solutions on the initial value if necessary.
We always use the initial time
t_{0}=0
.

Autonomous systems constitute the special case where
T
does not depend on
t
,
so we can abbreviate to
\bar{T}:M\to M
and write

x_{t+1}=\bar{T}(x_{t}),\quad t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},x_{0}\in M

(3)

A point
x_{\star}\in M
is called
equilibrium
or
fixed point

if
T(t,x_{\star})=x_{\star}
for all

t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, so the constant function
x_{t}=x_{\star}
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is a solution
of (
2
). In the following the asterisk will always denote
equilibria or their components.
Consider a solution
x=x(\cdot;x_{0})
of (
2
).

x
is called
stable
,
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
such that any
solution
\tilde{x}=\tilde{x}(\cdot;\tilde{x}_{0})
of (
2
) with

\left\lVert\tilde{x}_{0}-x_{0}\right\rVert<\delta
fulfills
\left\lVert\tilde{x}_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert<\varepsilon

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

x
is called
attractive
if there exists
\delta>0
such that any solution

\tilde{x}
with
\left\lVert\tilde{x}_{0}-x_{0}\right\rVert<\delta
fulfills

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left\lVert\tilde{x}_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert=0
.
x
is called

asymptotically stable
if it is stable and attractive.

Recall that a
contraction
is a self-mapping on some set with Lipschitz constant

L<1
, i.e. a mapping
\bar{T}:M\to M
,
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
\left\lVert\bar{T}(x)-\bar{T}(y)\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert
for all

x,y\in M
. If
M
is complete, i.e. all Cauchy sequences converge, then
a unique fixed point
x_{\star}\in M
of
\bar{T}
exists by the Banach fixed point theorem.

Theorem II.1
.

Linearized asymptotic stability implies local nonlinear stability

Consider
\bar{T}:M\to M
with a fixed point
x_{\star}
and
\bar{T}

continuously differentiable
in an open neighbourhood
B_{r}(x_{\star})\subset M
of
x_{\star}
.
Denote the Jacobian by
D\bar{T}_{x_{\star}}
, and assume

\left\lVert D\bar{T}_{x_{\star}}\right\rVert<1
for some norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
Then there exists
0<\varepsilon\leq r

and
0\leq c<1
such that for all
x_{0}
with
\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert<\varepsilon

\left\lVert x(t;x_{0})-x_{\star}\right\rVert\leq c^{t}\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

i.e.
x_{\star}
is locally exponentially and asymptotically stable.

The theorem is the core of the first method of Lyapunov for discrete time systems.
For a proof see
[
13
, Corollary 4.35.]
.

III
Convergence Proof

Let
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the weights of the function
f\left(w\right)\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}\right)
,
which has to be minimized. We also define
g\left(w\right):=\nabla f(w)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the gradient of
f

and the state variable of our dynamical system

x=\left(m,v,w\right)
. With these definitions we can rewrite the ADAM-Optimizer as a system of the form
(
2
).

\displaystyle m_{t+1}

\displaystyle:=\beta_{1}m_{t}+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)

\displaystyle v_{t+1}

\displaystyle:=\beta_{2}v_{t}+\left(1-\beta_{2}\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle w_{t+1}

\displaystyle:=w_{t}-\alpha\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}}}{\left(1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}\right)}\frac{m_{t+1}}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}

So the ADAM optimizer can be written as the iteration of a time-variant dynamical system

x_{t+1}=[m_{t+1},v_{t+1},w_{t+1}]^{\perp}=T\left(t,x\right)=T\left(t,[m_{t},v_{t},w_{t}]^{\perp}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3n}
.
We split the system into an autonomous and a non-autonomous part

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=T\left(t,x_{t}\right)=\bar{T}\left(x_{t}\right)+\Theta\left(t,x_{t}\right)

(5)

with

\displaystyle\bar{T}\left(x_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{1}m_{t}+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)\\
\beta_{2}v_{t}+\left(1-\beta_{2}\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}\\
w_{t}-\alpha\frac{m_{t+1}}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}\end{bmatrix}

(6)

and

\displaystyle\Theta\left(t,x_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=[0,0,\alpha\Theta_{3}\left(t,x_{t}\right)]^{\perp}

(7)

\displaystyle\Theta_{3}\left(t,x_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}}}{1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}}\right)\frac{m_{t+1}}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}

\displaystyle J_{\bar{T}}\left(m_{t},v_{t},w_{t}\right)

\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\max_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mu_{i}\right)\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)<2\beta_{1}+2
holds
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then Algorithm
1
converges locally with exponential
rate of convergence.

Proof.

Consider the non-autonomous system (
5
) with
\bar{T}(x_{t})
and
\Theta(t,x_{t})
as defined in equations (
6
) and (
7
). The Hessian of
f
is continuous, so the gradient
of
f
is locally Lipschitz with some constant
L>0
,

\left\lVert g(w_{1})-g(w_{2})\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert w_{1}-w_{2}\right\rVert
for all

w_{1},w_{2}
in some neighbourhood of
w_{\star}
. Let all other parameters be defined as in Theorem
III.2
, especially
\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\max_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mu_{i}\right)\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)<2\beta_{1}+2
. Using
m_{\star}=0
and
g(w_{\star})=0
we estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Theta\left(t,x\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\alpha\left|\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}}}{1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}}-1\right|\cdot\frac{\left\lVert\beta_{1}m+(1-\beta_{1})g(w)\right\rVert}{\sqrt{\beta_{2}v+(1-\beta_{2})g(w)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\left|\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}}-\left(1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}\right)}{\left(1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}\right)}\right|\cdot\left\lVert\beta_{1}m+(1-\beta_{1})g(w)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)}\left|\frac{\left(1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}\right)-\left(1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}}+\left(1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\beta_{1}\left\lVert m\right\rVert+(1-\beta_{1})\left\lVert g(w)\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{4}\left|\left(1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}\right)-\left(1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\beta_{1}\left\lVert m-m_{\star}\right\rVert+(1-\beta_{1})\left\lVert g(w)-g\left(w_{\star}\right)\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{4}\left|-\beta_{2}^{t+1}-2\beta_{1}^{t+1}+\beta_{1}^{2(t+1)}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\cdot(\beta_{1}\left\lVert m-m_{\star}\right\rVert+(1-\beta_{1})L\left\lVert w-w_{\star}\right\rVert)

\displaystyle\leq C\beta^{t+1}\big(\beta_{1}\left\lVert m-m_{\star}\right\rVert+(1-\beta_{1})L\left\lVert w-w_{\star}\right\rVert\big)

where we have used the Lipschitz continuity of
g
, and set
\beta=\max\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{1}^{2}\}
,
C:=\frac{4\alpha}{\epsilon\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)\left(\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}}+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)\right)}
.
The term
\beta_{1}\left\lVert m-m_{\star}\right\rVert+(1-\beta_{1})L\left\lVert w-w_{\star}\right\rVert
corresponds to a norm

\left\lVert(\tilde{m},\tilde{w})\right\rVert_{*}:=\beta_{1}\left\lVert\tilde{m}\right\rVert+(1-\beta_{1})L\left\lVert\tilde{w}\right\rVert,\quad\tilde{m},\tilde{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
(which does not depend on
w_{\star}
).
By the equivalence of norms in finite dimensional spaces we can
estimate
\left\lVert(\tilde{m},\tilde{w})\right\rVert_{*}\leq\tilde{C}\left\lVert(\tilde{m},\tilde{w})\right\rVert
for some
\tilde{C}>0
. We continue the
estimate:

\displaystyle\phantom{\left\lVert\Theta\left(t,x\right)\right\rVert}\leq

\displaystyle C\beta^{t+1}\tilde{C}\left\lVert(m-m_{\star},w-w_{\star})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C\beta\tilde{C})\beta^{t}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert=:\bar{C}\beta^{t}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert

for some
\bar{C}>0
.
With this estimate and Theorem
B.1
, it is sufficient to prove exponential stability
of a fixed point of
\bar{T}
.
By Theorem
III.3
we get

\rho\left(J_{\bar{T}}\left(0,0,w_{\star}\right)\right)<1
. Thus with Theorem
II.1
the fixed point

(0,0,w_{\star})
corresponding to the minimum
w_{\star}
is
locally exponentially stable,
and Theorem
B.1
gives local exponential convergence of
the non-autonomous system
T\left(t,x\right)
, i.e. the ADAM algorithm.
∎

Corollary III.5
.

Assume the original ADAM without bias correction
\tilde{x}_{t+1}=\bar{T}\left(x_{t}\right)+h\left(x_{t}\right)
as defined in equation (
8
) and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
strictly convex with minimum
w_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume
f\in C^{2}
and
\nabla^{2}f\left(w_{\star}\right)
positive definite. Assume inequality (
1
) holds for the hyperparameters. Then the original ADAM without bias correction converges locally with exponential rate of convergence.

Proof.

We start the proof by considering the disturbance
h(x_{t})
from equation (
9
). We can estimate the difference in
h(x_{t})
as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}+\epsilon}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}-\left(\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)}{\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}\left(\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left(\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}-\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)\left(\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}+\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)}{\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}\left(\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)\left(\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}+\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{v}\epsilon}{\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}\left(\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)\left(\sqrt{v+\epsilon^{2}}+\sqrt{v}+\epsilon\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{v}\epsilon}{\sqrt{v}\epsilon\left(2\epsilon\right)}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}

The estimate holds
for any
v
, also for
v=0
, and especially for
v_{t+1}
which appears in
h_{3}
.
Therefore we can estimate the whole disturbance
\left\lVert h\left(x_{t}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\left\lVert h\left(x_{t}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\left\lVert m_{t+1}-m_{\star}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\left\lVert\beta_{1}m_{t}+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)-m_{\star}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\left(\left\lVert\beta_{1}m_{t}-m_{\star}\right\rVert+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)\left\lVert g\left(w_{t}\right)\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\left(\beta_{1}\left\lVert m_{t}-m_{\star}\right\rVert+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)\left\lVert g\left(w_{t}\right)-g\left(w_{\star}\right)\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\left(\beta_{1}\left\lVert m_{t}-m_{\star}\right\rVert+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)L\left\lVert w_{t}-w_{\star}\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\beta_{1}\left\lVert m_{t}-m_{\star}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\epsilon\right)\left\lVert x_{t}-x_{\star}\right\rVert

with
C\left(\epsilon\right)=\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}
.
Thus, the first assumption of Theorem
B.5
is fulfilled. We know with the Theorems
II.1
and
III.3
, that the undisturbed system (
6
) converges locally and exponentially to

. With this knowledge and the estimation above, we can apply Theorem
B.5
. It follows that
\tilde{x}_{\star}=x_{\star}
is a global exponentially stable fixed point for the system (
8
). ∎

Involving the bias correction, we have to adjust system (

) to

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{t+1}=T\left(x_{t}\right)+h\left(x_{t}\right)+\tilde{\Theta}\left(t,x\right)

(10)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}\left(t,x\right)

\displaystyle:=\left[0,0,\alpha\tilde{\Theta}_{3}\left(t,x\right)\right]^{\perp}

(11)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}_{3}\left(t,x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{1-\beta_{2}^{t+1}}}{1-\beta_{1}^{t+1}}\right)\frac{m_{t+1}}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}}+\epsilon}

Corollary III.6
.

Assume the original ADAM with bias correction as the non-autonomous system
\tilde{x}_{t+1}=\bar{T}\left(x_{t}\right)+h\left(x_{t}\right)+\tilde{\Theta}\left(t,x\right)
like defined in equation (
10
). All other assumptions are adopted by Corollary
III.5
. Then system (
10
) converges locally with exponential rate of convergence.

Proof.

Assume the non-autonomous system (
7
) with
\bar{T}\left(x_{t}\right)
,

and
\tilde{\Theta}\left(t,x\right)

) and (

is analogous to the estimation in the proof of Corollary

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{\Theta}\left(t,x\right)\right\rVert\leq

\displaystyle+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)L\left\lVert w-w_{\star}\right\rVert)

Here

RMSProp

First we observe the RMSProp algorithm by Hinton et. al.
[
15
]
. We can define it with the following system

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=T\left(x_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\beta v_{t}+\left(1-\beta\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}\\
w_{t}-\alpha\frac{g\left(w_{t}\right)}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}\end{bmatrix}

Herewith we can calculate the Jacobian analogous to the convergence proof of the ADAM Optimizer.

\displaystyle J_{\bar{T}}\left(v_{t},w_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{2}I&\frac{\partial v_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}\\
\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial v_{t}}&\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}\end{bmatrix}

with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}=

\displaystyle 2\left(1-\beta\right)\diag(g\left(w_{t}\right))\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial v_{t}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}\diag\left(\frac{g\left(w_{t}\right)}{\left(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}=

\displaystyle I-\alpha\left(\diag(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\diag\left(g\left(w_{t}\right)(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}g\left(w_{t}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{t}\right)

This is simplified in the minimum
w_{\star}
to

\displaystyle J_{T}\left(0,w_{\star}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\beta I&0\\
0&I-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{\star}\right)\end{bmatrix}

As you can see we reach
n
times the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=\beta
, which is per definition smaller than
1
. The submatrix
I-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{\star}\right)
is again symmetric since
\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{\star}\right)
is the Hessian of
f
. Therefore we can diagonalize the matrix and get
n
times the eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}=1-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\mu_{i}
. Next step is to analyze the absolute value of the eigenvalues.

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}|

\displaystyle=|1-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\mu_{i}|<1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle-2

\displaystyle<-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\mu_{i}<0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\mu_{i}<\frac{2\epsilon}{\alpha}

Due to the fact that we look at eigenvalues of a positive definite matrix,
0<\mu_{i}
is clear. So for local convergence with exponential rate in the RMSProp algorithm we only have to fulfill:

\displaystyle\max\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\mu_{i})<\frac{2\epsilon}{\alpha}

AdaGrad

\displaystyle J_{T}\left(v_{t},w_{t}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}I&2g(w_{t})\nabla_{w}g(w_{t})\\

In the minimum
(0,w_{\star})

\displaystyle J_{T}\left(0,w_{\star}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&I-\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon}\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{\star}\right)\end{bmatrix}

we see that
\lambda_{1}=1
no matter which
\max\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mu_{i}\right)
we calculate. Thus, no convergence statement is possible using our approach.

AdaDelta

Finally, we look at the AdaDelta algorithm from
[
30

. This algorithm is somewhat more difficult to bring into the system structure. But with different time steps
t
and
t+1
for
v
and

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=T\left(x_{t}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\beta v_{t}+\left(1-\beta\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}\\
\beta m_{t}+\left(1-\beta\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}\frac{m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}}{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}\\
w_{t}-\alpha\frac{\sqrt{m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}}}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}g\left(w_{t}\right)\end{bmatrix}

Note that we add the learning rate
\alpha
to the optimizer different to the original paper. If we set
\alpha=1
we reach the original formulation from Zeiler
[
30
]
. We will discuss some different learning rates in Section
V
. For
T(x_{t})
we get the Jacobian

\displaystyle J_{T}\left(v_{t},m_{t},w_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\beta I&0&\frac{\partial v_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}\\
\frac{\partial m_{t+1}}{\partial v_{t}}&\frac{\partial m_{t+1}}{\partial m_{t}}&\frac{\partial m_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}\\
\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial v_{t}}&\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial m_{t}}&\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}\end{bmatrix}

with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial m_{t+1}}{\partial v_{t}}=

\displaystyle-\beta\left(1-\beta\right)\diag\left(\frac{g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}\left(m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}\right)}{\left(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial m_{t+1}}{\partial m_{t}}=

\displaystyle\beta I+\left(1-\beta\right)\diag\left(\frac{g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}}{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial m_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}=

\displaystyle 2\left(1-\beta\right)\diag\left(\frac{\left(m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}\right)\left(\beta v_{t}+\epsilon^{2}\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)}{\left(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla g\left(w_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}=

\displaystyle 2\left(1-\beta\right)\diag(g\left(w_{t}\right))\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial v_{t}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta\alpha}{2}\diag\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}}g\left(w_{t}\right)}{\left(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial m_{t}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\diag\left(\frac{g\left(w_{t}\right)}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{t+1}}{\partial w_{t}}=

\displaystyle I-\alpha\diag\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}}}{\sqrt{v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\sqrt{m_{t}+\epsilon^{2}}\left(1-\beta\right)g\left(w_{t}\right)^{2}}{\left(v_{t+1}+\epsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{t}\right)

Fortunately, by inserting
x_{\star}
, this Jacobian is greatly simplified to

\displaystyle J_{T}\left(0,0,w_{\star}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\beta I&0&0\\
0&\beta I&0\\
0&0&I-\alpha\nabla_{w}g\left(w_{\star}\right)\end{bmatrix}

We can easily identify
2n
times the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=\beta
of the Jacobian. We diagonalize the Hessian of
f
and identify
\lambda_{2,i}=1-\alpha\mu_{i}
. By observing the spectral radius of the Jacobian we see, that
\lambda_{1}=\beta
is by definition between

and
1
. For
\lambda_{2}
we generate the following general inequality.

\displaystyle 0<\max_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mu_{i}\right)<\frac{2}{\alpha}

Thus, when the inequality above is satisfied, AdaDelta is locally convergent with exponential rate of convergence.

Conclusion

These proofs of convergence, show the generality of this method. Only for AdaGrad we are not able to proof the convergence due to the missing decay rate
\beta

\epsilon

\alpha

\epsilon
. At this point it should be noted that due to the addition with

VI
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper we have presented a local convergence proof of Algorithm
1
, the original ADAM
[
18
]
, SGD
[
25
]
, RMSProp
[
15
]
and AdaDelta
[
30
]
. To the best of our knowledge it is the first at all for the ADAM optimizer. We also give an a posteriori boundary for the hyperparameters and show, that the choice of
\beta_{2}
does not matter for the convergence near a minimum.

However the proof is based on the vanishing gradient condition
\nabla f(w_{\star})=0

and cannot be used for an incremental algorithm for

f(w)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(w)
where different component gradients

g\left(w_{t}\right)=\nabla f_{i_{t}}(w_{t})
are used in the iterations for the moments. Clearly

\nabla f(w_{\star})=0
does not imply
\nabla f_{i}(w_{\star})=0
for all components.
We are investigating how the incremental dynamical system can be related to the batch
system.

The analysis applies to any local minimum with positive definite Hessian
and therefore does not require overall convexity.
In order to show global convergence of ADAM-like
algorithms other methods have to be applied.

Theorem B.4
.

Exponential stability implies existence of a Lyapunov function, arbitrary fixed point

Let
x_{\star}
be an fixed point for the nonlinear system the autonomous system

x(t+1)=f\left(x\left(t\right)\right)

where
f:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuously differentiable and
D=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert<r\}
. Let
k,\lambda
and
r_{0}
be positive constants with
r_{0}<r/k
. Let
D_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert<r_{0}\}
. Assume that the solution of the system satisfy

\displaystyle\left\lVert x(t,x_{0})\right\rVert\leq k\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert e^{-\lambda t},\forall x_{0}\in D_{0},\forall t\geq 0

(14)

Then there there is a function
V:D_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle c_{1}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq V\left(x\right)\leq c_{2}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle V\left(f\left(x\right)\right)-V\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\leq-c_{3}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle|V\left(x\right)-V\left(y\right)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\left(\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert+\left\lVert y-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)

Note: In the proof of this theorem the norm denotes the 2-norm. The equivalence of norms
then easily transfers the result to other norms.

Proof.

Let
\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)
be the solution of
x_{t+1}=f\left(x_{k}\right)
at time
t
starting from
x_{0}
at time
k=0
and
x_{\star}
be an equilibrium point of the system. Let

\displaystyle V\left(x_{0}\right)=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star})^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

for some integer variable
N
to be set. Then

\displaystyle V\left(x_{0}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(x_{0}-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(x_{0}-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N-1}(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star})^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(x_{0}-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(x_{0}-x_{\star}\right)=\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert

and on the other hand, using (
14
) we have

\displaystyle V\left(x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\left(x_{t}-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(x_{t}-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}k^{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}e^{-2\lambda t}

\displaystyle\leq k^{2}\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda N}}{1-e^{-2\lambda}}\right)\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

We have shown that there exists
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\displaystyle c_{1}\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq V\left(x_{0}\right)\leq c_{2}\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

is satisfied. Now, since
\phi\left(t,f\left(x_{0}\right)\right)=\phi\left(t,\phi\left(1,x_{0}\right)\right)=\phi\left(t+1,x_{0}\right)
,

\displaystyle V\left(f\left(x\right)\right)-V\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\left(\phi\left(t+1,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t+1,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left(\phi\left(j,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(j,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\phi\left(N,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(N,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(x_{0}-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(x_{0}-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k^{2}e^{-2\lambda n}\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-k^{2}e^{-2\lambda N}\right)\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

Now we choose
N
big enough so that
1-k^{2}e^{-2\lambda N}
is greater than
0
and also the second property has been proven. For the third property, since
f
is continuously differentiable it is also Lipschitz over the bounded domain
D
, with a Lipschitz constant
L
, for which it holds
\left\lVert f\left(x\right)-f\left(y\right)\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert
. Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi\left(t+1,x_{0}\right)-\phi\left(t+1,y_{0}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)\right)-f\left(\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\left\lVert\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)\right\rVert

and by induction

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)\right\rVert\leq L^{t}\left\lVert x_{0}-y_{0}\right\rVert

Consider now the difference
|V\left(x_{0}\right)-V\left(y_{0}\right)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N-1}\Big(\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}\left(\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\big(\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)-\left(\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\left(\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)-\left(\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right)\right)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\big(\left\lVert\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)\right\rVert\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}\left(\left\lVert\phi\left(t,x_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\phi\left(t,y_{0}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)L^{t}\left\lVert x_{0}-y_{0}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\sum\limits_{t=0}^{N-1}ke^{-\lambda t}L^{t}\right]\left(\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert+\left\lVert y_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert x_{0}-y_{0}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}\left(\left\lVert x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert+\left\lVert y_{0}-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert x_{0}-y_{0}\right\rVert

and so we have proven the last inequality.
∎

Theorem B.5
.

Exponential convergence under disturbances

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
Lipschitz continuous with
L_{f}
and the discrete time system

x_{t+1}=f(x_{t}),\quad x(0)=x_{0}

Assume
f(x_{\star})=x_{\star}
and that

x_{\star}
is a global exponentially stable fixed point.
Assume
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
Lipschitz continuous with
L_{h}
, with
h(x_{\star})=0
and

\left\lVert h(x)\right\rVert\leq L_{h}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and the discrete time system

\tilde{x}_{t+1}=f(\tilde{x}_{t})+h(\tilde{x}_{t}),\quad\tilde{x}(0)=\tilde{x}_{0}

(15)

Then, if
L_{h}
is small enough,
\tilde{x}_{\star}=x_{\star}

is a global exponentially stable fixed point for (
15
) as well.

Proof.

The converse Lyapunov theorem ensures the existence of a function
V
with properties

\displaystyle c_{1}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq V\left(x\right)\leq c_{2}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta V(x)

\displaystyle=V\left(x_{t+1}\right)-V\left(t\right)\leq-c_{3}\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle|V\left(x\right)-V\left(y\right)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\left(\left\lVert x-x_{\star}\right\rVert+\left\lVert y-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)

We show that, for
L_{h}
small enough,
\tilde{V}(\tilde{x}):=V(\tilde{x})

is also a Lyapunov function for
(
15
). We use the tilde symbol to denote time derivatives
along (
15
):

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}\tilde{V}\left(\tilde{x}\right)=

\displaystyle\tilde{V}\left(f\left(\tilde{x}\right)+h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right)-\tilde{V}\left(\tilde{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\left(f\left(\tilde{x}\right)+h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right)-V\left(\tilde{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\left(f\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right)-V\left(\tilde{x}\right)+V\left(f\left(\tilde{x}\right)+h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-V\left(f\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right)

The properties of
V
and
h
give

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}\tilde{V}\left(\tilde{x}\right)\leq

\displaystyle-c_{3}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+c_{4}\left\lVert h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\left\lVert f\left(\tilde{x}\right)+h\left(\tilde{x}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f\left(\tilde{x}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c_{3}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+c_{4}\left\lVert h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\left\lVert f\left(\tilde{x}\right)-x_{\star}+h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right\rVert+\left\lVert f\left(\tilde{x}\right)-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-c_{3}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+c_{4}L_{h}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\cdot(\left\lVert f\left(\tilde{x}\right)-f\left(x_{\star}\right)\right\rVert+\left\lVert h\left(\tilde{x}\right)\right\rVert

\displaystyle+\left\lVert f\left(\tilde{x}\right)-f\left(x_{\star}\right)\right\rVert)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-c_{3}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+c_{4}L_{h}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\cdot\left(L_{f}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert+L_{h}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert+L_{f}\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(c_{3}-c_{4}L_{h}\left(L_{f}+L_{h}+L_{f}\right)\right)\left\lVert\tilde{x}-x_{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

So for
L_{h}
small enough we get exponential convergence.
∎

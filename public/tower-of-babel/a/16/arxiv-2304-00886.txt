Gromov’s tori are optimal
Anton Petrunin

Abstract
We give an optimal bound on normal curvatures of immersed n n -torus in a Euclidean ball of large dimension.



1
Introduction

Let us denote by
\mathbb{B}^{q}
the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
centered at the origin.
Further,
\mathbb{T}^{n}
will denote the
n
-dimensional torus — the smooth manifold diffeomorphic to the product of
n
circles.

This note is inspired by examples of embeddings
\mathbb{T}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}^{q}
for large
q
with constant normal curvatures
K_{n}=\sqrt{3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n/(n+2)}
.
In other words, any geodesic in the torus has constant curvature
K_{n}
as a curve in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
.
These examples were found by Michael Gromov among geodesic subtori in Clifford’s tori

[
5
, 2.A]
,
[
4
, 1.1.A.]
.
In particular, Gromov’s tori have flat induced metrics.
(Recall that Clifford’s torus is a product of
m
circles of radius
{1}/{\sqrt{m}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}m}
;
its normal curvatures lie in the range
[\,1,\sqrt{m}\,]
.)

Gromov’s examples lead to the following surprising facts:

any closed smooth manifold
L
admits a smooth embedding into
\mathbb{B}^{q}
for large
q
with normal curvatures less than
\sqrt{3}
;

moreover, the induced Riemannian metric on
L
can be chosen to be proportional to any given metric
g
;

see
[
5
, 1.D]
and
[
4
, 1.1.C]
.

The next theorem implies that Gromov’s tori have the best upper bound on normal curvatures;
in particular, the
\sqrt{3}
-bound is optimal.

1.1. Theorem.

Suppose
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is smoothly immersed in
\mathbb{B}^{q}
.
Then its maximal normal curvature is at least

\sqrt{3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n}{n+2}}.

To make the statement more exact, we need one more notation.
Assume that
L
is
a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
;
we will always assume that
L
is equipped with the induced Riemannian metric.
Let us denote by
\mathrm{T}_{x}
and
\mathrm{N}_{x}
the tangent and normal spaces of
L
at
x
.

Recall that the
second fundamental form

\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}
at
x
is a symmetric quadratic form on
\mathrm{T}_{x}
with values in
\mathrm{N}_{x}
.
It is uniquely defined by the identity
\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}(\text{\sc{v}},\text{\sc{v}})\equiv\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{\text{\sc{v}}}(0)
,
where
\text{\sc{v}}\in\mathrm{T}_{x}
and
\gamma_{\text{\sc{v}}}
is an
L
-geodesic that starts at
x
with initial velocity vector
v
.

Given
x\in L
,
denote by
\textsl{Ж}(x)
the average value of
|\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}(\text{\sc{u}},\text{\sc{u}})|^{2}
for
\text{\sc{u}}\in\mathrm{T}_{x}
such that
|\text{\sc{u}}|=1
.
Since
K(\text{\sc{u}})=|\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}(\text{\sc{u}},\text{\sc{u}})|
is the normal curvature in the direction
u
,
we have that

\textsl{Ж}(x)
is the average value
K^{2}(\text{\sc{u}})
.
(The Cyrillic zhe
Ж
is used since it resembles
K^{2}
.)

1.2. Theorem.

Suppose
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is smoothly immersed in
\mathbb{B}^{q}
.
Let us equip
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with the induced Riemannian metric;
so we can take average values with respect to the induced volume.

(a)

If
n=2
, then the average value of
Ж
is at least
\tfrac{3}{2}
.

(b)

If the metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is flat, then the average value of
Ж
is at least
3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n}{n+2}
.

(c)

If the image of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
lies in
\partial\mathbb{B}^{q}
, then
\textsl{Ж}\geqslant 3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n}{n+2}
at some point of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

(d)

If
n\leqslant 4
, then
\textsl{Ж}\geqslant 3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n}{n+2}
at some point of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

(e)

If the normal curvatures of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
do not exceed
2
, then
\textsl{Ж}\geqslant 3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n}{n+2}
at some point of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Note that part
1
is a stronger version of
1
.
The remaining statements
1
–
1
are stronger versions of
1
in some partial cases.
All this follows since the normal curvature in some direction at
x
is at least
\sqrt{\textsl{Ж}(x)}
.

All proofs use our version of the Gauss formula; see below.
The proofs of
1
–
1
use in addition that

the torus does not admit a metric with positive scalar curvature

[
7
, Corollary A]
.

1.3. Open question.

Is it true that for any smooth immersion
\mathbb{T}^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{B}^{q}
the inequality
\textsl{Ж}\geqslant 3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n}{n+2}
holds at some point?

1.4. Open question.

Suppose
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\looparrowright\mathbb{B}^{q}
is a smooth immersion.
Is it true that its normal curvature is at least
\sqrt{2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n/(n+1)}
in some direction?

The last question asks if
the Veronese embedding is optimal
.
Recall that the Veronese embedding
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}^{q}
has all normal curvatures
\sqrt{2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n/(n+1)}
; here
q=\tfrac{1}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+1)(n+2)
.
An analogous question for immersions into unit spheres is open as well
[
9
]
.
You may also ask for the optimal constant for your favorite closed manifold.

2
Gauss formula

Recall that
L
is a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
.
Given
p\in L
,
denote by
\mathrm{Sc}(p)
and
H(p)

the scalar curvature and the mean curvature vector at
p
.

The following version of the Gauss formula plays a central role in all proofs;
it is used instead of the formula in
[
6
, 5.B]
.

2.1. Gauss formula.

The following identity

\mathrm{Sc}=\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|H|^{2}-\tfrac{n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2)}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\textsl{Ж}

holds for any smooth
n
-dimensional immersed manifold in a Euclidean space.

Proof.

Choose a point
p\in L
.

Assume
\operatorname{\rm codim}L=1
.
Denote by
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}
the principal curvatures of
L
at
p
.
Note that

|H|^{2}=\sum_{i}k_{i}^{2}+2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sum_{i<j}k_{i}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}k_{j}.

Further,

n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\textsl{Ж}=3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{2}+2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sum_{i<j}k_{i}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}k_{j}.

The last identity follows since
Ж
is the average value of
\left(\sum_{i}k_{i}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathrm{T}_{p}
;
here
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
are the standard coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
One has to take into account that the following functions have unit average values:

\tfrac{1}{3}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}x_{i}^{4}
and
n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}x_{i}^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}x_{j}^{2}
for
i\neq j
.

By the standard Gauss formula,

\mathrm{Sc}=2{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sum_{i<j}k_{i}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}k_{j}.

It remains to rewrite the right-hand side using the expressions for
|H|^{2}
and
Ж
.

If
\operatorname{\rm codim}L=k>1
, then the second fundamental form at
p
can be presented as a direct sum of
k
real-valued quadratic forms
\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}_{k}
;
that is,

\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}=e_{1}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}_{1}+\dots+e_{k}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}_{k},

where
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{N}_{p}
.
Denote by
\mathrm{Sc}_{i}
,
H_{i}
, and
\textsl{Ж}_{i}
the values associated with
\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}_{i}
.
From above, we get

\mathrm{Sc}_{i}=\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|H_{i}|^{2}-\tfrac{n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2)}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\textsl{Ж}_{i}

for each
i
.

Note that

\mathrm{Sc}=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Sc}_{i},\qquad|H^{2}|=\sum_{i}|H_{i}|^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\textsl{Ж}=\sum_{i}\textsl{Ж}_{i}.

Hence the general case follows.
∎

Remark.

A more direct proof of this formula can be obtained using the so-called
extrinsic curvature tensor
which is defined by

\Phi(\text{\sc{x}},\text{\sc{y}},\text{\sc{v}},\text{\sc{w}})\mathrel{{:}{=}}\langle\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}(\text{\sc{x}},\text{\sc{y}}),\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}(\text{\sc{v}},\text{\sc{w}})\rangle
;
the necessary properties of this tensor are discussed in
[
8
]
.
As a bonus, one gets an explicit expression for the second fundamental forms of all Gromov’s tori.

3
Special cases

The following statement appears in the book of Yuri Burago and Viktor Zalgaller
[
2
, Theorem 28.2.5]
;
it generalizes the result of István Fáry about average curvature of a curve in the unit ball
[
3
,
12
]
, but the proof is essentially the same.

3.1. Lemma.

Let
L
be a closed
n
-dimensional manifold that is smoothly immersed in
\mathbb{B}^{q}
.
Then the average value of
|H|
on
L
is at least
n
.

Proof.

Consider the function
u\colon x\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|x|^{2}
on
L
.
Note that

(\Delta u)(x)=n+\langle H(x),x\rangle.

It follows that the average value of
\langle H(x),x\rangle
is
-n
.
Since
|x|\leqslant 1
, we get the result.
∎

Proof of
1
.

By
3
, the average value of
|H|^{2}
is at least 4.
Further, by the Gauss–Bonnet formula, the scalar curvature (which is twice the Gauss curvature in this case) has zero average.
Therefore
2
implies the statement.
∎

Proof of
1
.

By
3
,
the average value of
|H|^{2}
is at least
n^{2}
.
Since
\mathrm{Sc}\equiv 0
, it remains to apply
2
.
∎

Proof of
1
.

Since the image lies in the unit sphere, we have that
|H|^{2}
is at least
n^{2}
at each point.
Since
\mathbb{T}^{n}
does not admit a metric with positive scalar curvature
[
7
, Corollary A]
, we have
\mathrm{Sc}(x)\leqslant 0
at some point
x
.
It remains to apply
2
at
x
.
∎

4
Main case

The following lemma is an easy corollary of the bow lemma of Axel Schur
[
11
,
10
]
.
It explains how we use the assumption on normal curvatures in
1
.
If
n\leqslant 4
, then the proof works without this assumption.

4.1. Lemma.

Let
L
be a manifold smoothly immersed in
\mathbb{B}^{q}
.
Suppose its normal curvatures are at most
2
.
Given
x\in L
, denote by
\beta=\beta(x)
the angle between vector
x
and the normal space
\mathrm{N}_{x}
.
Then
|x|\leqslant\cos\beta
.

Proof.

Let
\xi
be a tangent direction at
x
such that
\measuredangle(x,\xi)=\penalty\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\beta
.
In the plane spanned by
x
and
\xi
, choose a unit-speed circle arc
\sigma
from
0
to
x
that comes to
x
in the direction opposite to
\xi
;
extend
\sigma
after
x
by a unit-speed semicircle
\tilde{\gamma}
with curvature
2
in such a way that the concatenation
\sigma*\tilde{\gamma}
is an arc of a
C^{1}
-smooth convex plane curve; see the figure.

Observe that if
|x|>\cos\beta
, then
\tilde{\gamma}
leaves
\mathbb{B}^{q}
; that is,
|\tilde{\gamma}(t_{0})|>1
for some
t_{0}
.

Let
\gamma
be the unit-speed geodesic in
L
that runs from
x
in the direction
\xi
.
Note that curvatures of
\sigma*\gamma
do not exceed the curvatures of
\sigma*\tilde{\gamma}
at the corresponding points.
Applying the bow lemma for
\sigma*\gamma
and
\sigma*\tilde{\gamma}
, we get
|\gamma(t_{0})|\geqslant|\tilde{\gamma}(t_{0})|
.
It follows that
L
does not lie in
\mathbb{B}^{q}
— a contradiction.
∎

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Suppose
n\geqslant 3
, and
u\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth positive function.
Recall that

\left(\mathrm{Sc}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}u-4{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\tfrac{n-1}{n-2}{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\Delta u\right){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}u^{\frac{n-2}{n+2}}

is the scalar curvature of the metric
u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}g
;
see for example
[
1
, 6.3]
.
Since
any Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
has nonpositive scalar curvature at some point

[
7
, Corollary A]
, we get the following.

4.2. Claim.

For any Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}

and any positive smooth function
u\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the function

\mathrm{Sc}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}u-4{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\tfrac{n-1}{n-2}{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\Delta u

returns a nonpositive value at some point.

Proof of
1
and
1
.

The case
n=2
follows from
1
;
so we can assume that
n\geqslant 3
.
Consider the function
u\colon x\mapsto\exp(-\tfrac{k}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|x|^{2})
on the torus.

We will apply the following formula

\Delta(\varphi\circ f)=(\varphi^{\prime}\circ f){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\Delta f+(\varphi^{\prime\prime}\circ f){\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|\nabla f|^{2}

to
f\colon x\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|x|^{2}
and
\varphi\colon y\mapsto\exp(-k{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}y)
; so
u=\varphi\circ f
.

Set
r(x)=|x|
,
\alpha(x)=\measuredangle(H(x),x)
, and
\beta(x)
as in
4
.
Note that

\beta\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\pi-\beta.

➊

Observe that

\Delta f=|H|{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\cos\alpha+n,\quad|\nabla f|=r{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sin\beta,\quad\varphi^{\prime}=-k{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\varphi,\quad\varphi^{\prime\prime}=k^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\varphi.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Delta u

\displaystyle=u{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}[-k{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|H|{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\cos\alpha-k{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n+k^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sin^{2}\beta].

Recall that

\mathrm{Sc}=-\tfrac{n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2)}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\textsl{Ж}+\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|H|^{2}
; see
2
.
By
4
, the function

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sc}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}u-4{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\tfrac{n-1}{n-2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\Delta u

\displaystyle=u{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\biggl[-\tfrac{n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2)}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\textsl{Ж}+\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|H|^{2}+4{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\tfrac{n-1}{n-2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}k{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}|H|{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\cos\alpha+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+4{{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}}\tfrac{n-1}{n-2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(k{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n-k^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sin^{2}\beta)\biggr]

x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

At the point

\displaystyle\tfrac{n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(n+2)}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\textsl{Ж}

\displaystyle\geqslant\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}(|H|+n{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\cos\alpha)^{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\cos^{2}\alpha+

➋

\displaystyle+3{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n^{2}-\tfrac{9}{4}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\tfrac{n-2}{n-1}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}r^{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}\sin^{2}\beta\geqslant\tfrac{3}{2}{\hskip 0.5pt\cdot\hskip 0.5pt}n^{2}.

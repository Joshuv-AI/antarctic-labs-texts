On the genealogical structure of critical branching processes in a varying environment
Götz Kersting

Abstract
Critical branching processes in a varying environment behave much the same as critical Galton-Watson processes. In this note we like to confirm this finding with regard to the underlying genealogical structures. In particular, we consider the most recent common ancestor given survival and the corresponding reduced branching processes, in the spirit of Zubkov (1975) and Fleischmann and Siegmund-Schultze (1977). Keywords and phrases. branching process, varying environment, Galton-Watson process, critical process, reduced process, most recent common ancestor, exponential distribution, Yule process MSC 2010 subject classification. Primary 60J80.

Introduction

Critical Galton-Watson processes (GW processes) constitute a prominent, but small portion of the whole family of GW processes, and could be considered just as the separating borderline between the larger classes of supercritical and subcritical GW processes. From this point of view it might come as a surprise that within the vaster family of branching processes in a varying environment the subclass of critical processes is much broader and pushes apart the super- and subcritical processes. Here we consider a branching process in a varying environment to be critical if it shares properties characteristic of critical GW processes. As in
[
8
]
, we think of Kolmogorov’s asymptotics on the survival probabilities and Yaglom’s asymptotic exponential law given survival. In this note we like to back this approach by widening it to the genealogical structure of the underlying branching trees, in the spirit of Zubkov’s result
[
14
]
on the most recent common ancestor within a critical GW tree and the treatment of its reduced process by Fleischmann and Siegmund-Schultze
[
5
]
. Here again critical branching processes in a varying environment resemble critical GW processes.

In order to define a branching process in a varying environment (BPVE), let
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
denote a sequence of random variables with values in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots
their distributions. Let
Y_{in}
,
i,n\in\mathbb{N}
, be independent random variables such that
Y_{in}
and
Y_{n}
coincide in distribution for all
i,n\geq 1
. Define the random variables
Z_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, with values in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
recursively as

Z_{0}:=1\ ,\quad Z_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{Z_{n-1}}Y_{in}\ ,\ n\geq 1\ .

Then the process
(Z_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is called a
branching process in the varying environment
v=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)
with initial value
Z_{0}=1
.
These processes may be considered as a model for the development of the size of a population where individuals reproduce independently with offspring distributions
f_{n}
potentially changing among generations. Without further mention we always require that
0<\mathbf{E}[Y_{n}]<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
.

In this introduction we recall the notion of a critical BPVE, as it was initiated by Jagers in
[
7
]
and developed only recently both by Bhattacharya and Perlman
[
1
]
and by Kersting
[
8
]
. First, we separate certain exceptional BPVEs out, behaving in an extraordinary manner unknown for ordinary GW processes (like possessing multiple rates of growth, see
[
11
]
). As explained in
[
8
]
, these exotic possibilities can be precluded by certain uniform
L^{2}
-integrability conditions. Here we impose the requirement that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a constant
c_{\varepsilon}<\infty
such that for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[Y_{n}^{2};Y_{n}>c_{\varepsilon}(1+\mathbf{E}[Y_{n}])\big]\leq\varepsilon\mathbf{E}\big[Y_{n}^{2};Y_{n}\geq 2\big]<\infty\ ,

(
\ast
)

which is introduced in
[
8
]
as condition (B). There it is demonstrated in detail that this assumption is widely satisfied. Let us mention just two examples: if either the
Y_{n}
are uniformly bounded by some constant
c<\infty
, or if else all
Y_{n}
are Poisson distributed (without any constrain on the parameters), then (
\ast
) holds true. In particular, the latter example shows that condition (
\ast
) does not entail any restriction on the sequence of expectations of
Y_{n}
respectively
Z_{n}
.

It turns out that under assumption (
\ast
) the behaviour of a BPVE
(Z_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is essentially dictated by two sequences of numbers, namely

\mu_{n}:=\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}]\ \text{ and }\ \rho_{n}:=\frac{\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}(Z_{n}-1)]}{\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}]^{2}}\ ,\ n\geq 0\ .

With these quantities the family of BPVEs can be classified into subcritical, critical, supercritical and asymptotically degenerate processes (where the process may freeze in a positive state). For complete details we refer to
[
8
]
, let us recall just that statement which is relevant in our context. It is a version of
[
8
, Theorem 4]
(also taking
[
8
, Lemma 4]
into account).

Theorem 1.

Assume
(
\ast
).
If the probability of extinction is 1, that is if
\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There is a sequence
b_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, of positive numbers such that
Z_{n}/b_{n}
conditioned on the event
\{Z_{n}>0\}
converges in distribution to a standard exponential distribution as
n\to\infty
,

(ii)

\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0]\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,

(iii)

\mu_{n}\rho_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Under these conditions we may set
b_{n}:=\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0]
, and we have

\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0]\sim\frac{1}{2}\mu_{n}\rho_{n}\ ,

or equivalently

\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0)\sim\frac{2}{\rho_{n}}

as
n\to\infty
.

This theorem characterizes in the context of BPVEs the range, where Yaglom’s
[
13
]
asymptotics for critical GW processes on the conditional distribution of
Z_{n}
holds. For a probabilistic approach via a 2-spine decomposition see
[
3
]
. In addition, the final statement of the theorem provides an extension to BPVEs of Kolmogorov’s
[
10
]
asymptotics on the survival probability of critical GW processes (in its general version, as given in
[
9
]
). Therefore, assuming (
\ast
) it is natural to transfere by means of Theorem 1 the notion of criticality from GW processes to BPVEs, that is, to name a BPVE
(Z_{n})_{n\geq 0}

critical
if both

\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0)\to 0\ \text{ and }\ \mathbf{E}[Z_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0]\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
are satisfied.
[
8
, Proposition 1]
states that this pair of requirements can equivalently be expressed by the conditions

\displaystyle\rho_{n}\to\infty\ \text{ and }\ \mu_{n}\rho_{n}\to\infty

(1)

as
n\to\infty
. If the first one fails then we enter the region of supercritical or asymptotic degenerate processes, and if the second one goes wrong then we access the subcritical domain.

To conclude this introduction we recall how
\mu_{n}
and
\rho_{n}
may be derived from the varying environment
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)
.
For this purpose let us agree on the notational facilitation used in
[
8
]
: for a probability measure
f
on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
with weights
f[k]
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we set

f(s):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s^{k}f[k]\ ,\ 0\leq s\leq 1\ .

Thus, we denote the probability measure
f
and its generating function by one and the same symbol. Then, each operation applied to these measures has to be understood as an operation applied to their generating functions. For example,
f_{1}\circ f_{2}
expresses the composition of generating functions as well as the resulting probability measure. We shall consider the mean and the normalized second factorial moment of a random variable
Y
with distribution
f
,

\mathbf{E}[Y]=f^{\prime}(1)\ \text{ and }\ \nu:=\frac{\mathbf{E}[Y(Y-1)]}{\mathbf{E}[Y]^{2}}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(1)}{f^{\prime}(1)^{2}}\ .

With these convention in mind and setting

\nu_{n}:=\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{f_{n}^{\prime}(1)^{2}}\ ,

we obtain
\mu_{n}
and
\rho_{n}
by means of the formulas

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=f_{1}^{\prime}(1)\cdots f_{n}^{\prime}(1)\ ,\quad\rho_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\nu_{k}}{\mu_{k-1}}\ .

(2)

Thus
(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is an increasing sequence, and also
\mu_{0}=1
and
\rho_{0}=0
. For the proof we refer to
[
8
, Lemma 4]
.

Examples.

1. Let
f_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, be a sequence of Poisson distributions with parameters
\lambda_{n}
. Then
f_{n}^{\prime}(1)=\lambda_{n}
and
\nu_{n}=1/\lambda_{n}
. Therefore, because of (
1
) the BPVE is critical, if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}=\infty\ \text{ and }\ \frac{1}{\mu_{n}}=o\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\Big)\ .

(3)

The first condition is violated, if
\mu_{n}
increases slightly faster than linearly, then we observe supercritical behaviour. The second conditions fails, if
\mu_{n}
decreases at an exponential rate, then we encounter subcritical processes. In between, a variety of behaviour for
\mu_{n}=\mathbf{E}[Z_{n}]
can be set up. This demonstrates the broad range of critical BPVEs.

2. In the case of binary offspring (meaning that
f_{n}[0]+f_{n}[2]=1
for all
n\geq 1
) we have once again
\nu_{n}=1/f_{n}^{\prime}(1)
. Therefore, the criterion (
3
) for criticality applies equally.

3. These examples are in a way typical and generalize as follows: Assume that for some
a>0
we have for all
n\geq 1

f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(1)\geq af_{n}^{\prime}(1)\ .

Additionally, from formula (
5
) below we see that (
\ast
) implies
f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(1)\leq bf_{n}^{\prime}(1)(1+f_{n}^{\prime}(1))
for some
b>0
. These estimates imply

a\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\nu_{k}}{\mu_{k-1}}\leq b\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{k-1}}\Big)\leq 2b\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\ .

Then (
1
) and (
3
) are equivalent conditions.

2
The genealogical structure of a critical BPVE

Conditional on the event that
Z_{n}>0
we may define the generation
G_{n}
of the most recent common ancestor of the
Z_{n}
particles in generation
n
. Zubkov
[
14
]
proved that for critical GW processes
G_{n}/n
has asymptotically a uniform distribution on the interval
[0,1]
(for another proof see
[
6
]
). The following theorem generalizes this remarkable result to BPVEs.

Theorem 2.

Assuming
(
\ast
)
we have for a critical BPVE

\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\Big|\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\leq k\mid Z_{n}>0)-\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho_{n}}\Big|\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.

Remarkably, the approximating cumulative distribution function
\rho_{k}/\rho_{n}
,
0\leq k\leq n
, also appears in
the recent paper
[
3
]
by Natalia Cardona-Tobón and Sandra Palau. There it determines the moment of bifurcation of spines in a 2-spine construction of a (twofold) size-biased critical BPVE. One may wonder, whether Theorem 2 can be derived as well within this framework.

For a corollary we note that in (
2
) the summands of
\rho_{n}
are uniformly of smaller order than
\rho_{n}
itself. This is the content of Lemma 1 below. Therefore
\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\leq k\mid Z_{n}>0)\to 0
as well as
\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq n-k\mid Z_{n}>0)\to 0
for any
k\geq 1
as
n\to\infty
. In other terms the following holds.

Corollary.

Under the assumptions of Theorem 2, given the events
\{Z_{n}>0\}
we have

G_{n}\to\infty\ \text{ and }\ n-G_{n}\to\infty

in probability as
n\to\infty
.

These properties are characteristic: For supercritical (and asymptotically degenerate) BPVRs one expects that the random variables
G_{n}
, given that
Z_{n}>0
, remain bounded in probability uniformly in
n
, and for subcritical the same will hold true for the quantities
n-G_{n}
.

The next examples discuss cases where, after a suitable normalization,
G_{n}
given
\{Z_{n}>0\}
has a limiting distribution.

Examples.

1.
\rho_{[tn]}/\rho_{n}
converges for all
0<t<1
to a limiting value, if and only if the sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is regularly varying with an exponent
\lambda\geq 0
. This means that for any
0<t<1

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\leq nt\mid Z_{n}>0)\to t^{\lambda}

as
n\to\infty
. Thus
G_{n}/n
has a non-degenerate conditional limiting distribution, if and only if
(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is regularly varying with an exponent
\lambda>0
.

2. In the Poisson case, suppose that
\mu_{n}\sim n
, and so
\rho_{n}\sim\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-1}\sim\log n
. Thus (
1
) is satisfied, and we are in a critical region close to supercritical behaviour. Then for
0<t<1

\mathbf{P}(\log G_{n}\leq t\log n\mid Z_{n}>0)=\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\leq n^{t}\mid Z_{n}>0)\sim\frac{\log n^{t}}{\log n}=t\ .

Here, the conditional distribution of
(\log G_{n})/\log n
is asymptotically uniform.

3. In the Poisson case, suppose that
\mu_{n}\sim\exp(-\sqrt{n})
. Since by l’Hospital’s rule

\int_{0}^{n}\exp(\sqrt{y})\,dy\sim 2\sqrt{n}\exp(\sqrt{n})

as
n\to\infty
, we have
\rho_{n}\sim 2\sqrt{n}\exp(\sqrt{n})
. Again, we are in the critical domain, now close to subcritical processes. In this case for
t>0

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\leq n-2t\sqrt{n}\mid Z_{n}>0)\sim\frac{\rho_{n-[2t\sqrt{n}]}}{\rho_{n}}\sim\exp\Big(\sqrt{n-2t\sqrt{n}}-\sqrt{n}\Big)\sim\exp(-t)\ .

In other words: the conditional distribution of
(n-G_{n})/(2\sqrt{n})
is asymptotically standard exponential.

From the generation of the most recent common ancestor, we step forward to the more elaborate
reduced branching trees
given the events
\{Z_{n}>0\}
. As introduced by Fleischmann and Siegmund-Schultze
[
5
]
in the case of GW trees, these are the subtrees containing all individuals which possess descendants in generation
n
. Accordingly, the
reduced branching process

(Z_{k,n})_{0\leq k\leq n}
is made up of the numbers
Z_{k,n}
of individuals in generation
k\leq n
having descendants in generation
n
. Just as in the GW case, this process is a non-homogeneous Markov chain, actually a BPVE. For critical GW processes the structure of the reduced process has been investigated in
[
5
]
. It turns out that asymptotically the process
(Z_{[nt],n})_{0\leq t<1}
coincides with a properly time-changed Yule process. It is noteworthy that in the case of a critical branching processes in a
random
environment the situation is markedly different, as clarified by Borovkov,
Vatutin
[
2
]
and Vatutin
[
12
]
.

By contrast, for critical BPVEs we encounter much the same behavior as for GW processes. Again, we regard the increasing sequence
\rho_{k}/\rho_{n}
,
0\leq k\leq n
, as a cumulative probability distribution function. We use its (right-continuous) generalized inverse
k_{n}:[0,1)\to\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
given by

k_{n}(t):=\max\{k\geq 0:\rho_{k}\leq t\rho_{n}\}\ ,\quad 0\leq t<1\ .

By means of this function we rescale distances between generations in the reduced process. The following theorem contains the result of Fleischmann and Siegmund-Schultze as a special case.

Theorem 3.

Assume
(
\ast
).
Then we have for the reduced process of a critical BPVE in the limit
n\to\infty

\Big((Z_{k_{n}(t),n})_{0\leq t<1}\,\big|\,Z_{n}>0\Big)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\Rightarrow}}\ \big(Y(\log\tfrac{1}{1-t})\big)_{0\leq t<1}\ ,

where
(Y(u))_{u\geq 0}
denotes a standard Yule process.

Here the notation
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\Rightarrow}}
indicates that for any
0<\eta<1
we have the convergence in the Skorohod sense of the sequence of processes restricted to times
t\in[0,\eta]
.

Proofs

We denote by
f_{k,n}
,
0\leq k\leq n
,
the distribution (respectively the generating function) of
Z_{n}
given that
Z_{k}=1
. Therefore, for
k<n

f_{k,n}:=f_{k+1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{n}\ .

Our proofs rest on techniques from the paper
[
8
]
. In particular we use its formula (16), reading

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0)=1-f_{0,n}(0)=\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}\Big)^{-1}\ ,

(4)

where
\varphi_{k}(s)
,
0\leq s<1
, is given by the equation
(1-f_{k}(s))^{-1}=(f_{k}^{\prime}(1)(1-s))^{-1}+\varphi_{k}(s)
. For a detailed discussion of this
shape function
of
f_{k}
we refer to
[
8
]
. We preface the proofs of the theorems with several lemmas.

Lemma 1.

Assume
(
\ast
).
Then condition (
1
) implies

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{\nu_{i}}{\mu_{i-1}}=o(\rho_{n})

as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

From (
\ast
) we have
\sum_{k>c_{1/2}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))}k^{2}f_{i}[k]\leq\sum_{2\leq k\leq c_{1/2}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))}k^{2}f_{i}[k]
, thus

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(1)\leq\sum_{k\geq 2}k^{2}f_{i}[k]\leq 2\sum_{k\leq c_{1/2}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))}k^{2}f_{i}[k]\leq 2c_{1/2}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))f_{i}^{\prime}(1),

(5)

and therefore for all
i\leq n
and any natural number
k_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{i}}{\mu_{i-1}\rho_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{1/2}\frac{1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1)}{f_{i}^{\prime}(1)\mu_{i-1}\rho_{n}}=2c_{1/2}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{i}\rho_{n}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{i-1}\rho_{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{1/2}\sup_{k>k_{0}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}\rho_{k}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{k-1}\rho_{k-1}}\Big)+2c_{1/2}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\max_{k\leq k_{0}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{k-1}}\Big)\ .

In view of (
1
), by adapting
k_{0}
the first term on the right-hand side can be made arbitrarily small, and similarly the second term by afterwards increasing
n
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.

Assume
(
\ast
)
and let
0<\eta<1
. Then, in case of a critical BPVE we have for all
0\leq i\leq n
with
\rho_{i}\leq\eta\rho_{n}

\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{i}=1)=\frac{2+o(1)}{\mu_{i}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}

as
n\to\infty
, where the
o(1)
-term goes to zero uniformly in all
i
under consideration.

Proof.

Conditioning on the event
\{Z_{i}=1\}
means that we have to replace the terms
\mu_{j}
for
j\geq i
in (
4
) by the product
f_{i+1}^{\prime}(1)\cdots f_{j}^{\prime}(1)=\mu_{j}/\mu_{i}
. Thus, for any
0\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{i}=1)=\Big(\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{n}}+\mu_{i}\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}\Big)^{-1}\ .

(6)

Next we recall from Lemma 8 and formula (7) of
[
8
]
the approximation

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}=\frac{\rho_{n}}{2}(1+o(1))

as
n\to\infty
, valid under the assumptions (
\ast
) and (
1
). Therefore, because of (
1
)

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}-\frac{\rho_{n}-\rho_{i}}{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}-\frac{\rho_{n}}{2}\Big|+\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{i}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}-\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}\Big|

\displaystyle=o(\rho_{n})+o(\rho_{n})=o(\rho_{n})

uniformly for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Also
\rho_{n}=O(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})
uniformly for all
i
satisfying
\rho_{i}\leq\eta\rho_{n}
, hence we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\frac{\varphi_{k}(f_{k,n}(0))}{\mu_{k-1}}=\frac{\rho_{n}-\rho_{i}}{2}(1+o(1))

(7)

uniformly for all
i
satisfying
\rho_{i}\leq\eta\rho_{n}
.

Also, because of (
1
) we have

\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}=o(\rho_{n})=o(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})

uniformly for all
i
under consideration. Using this formula together with (
7
) in equation (
6
) we obtain our claim.
∎

Lemma 3.

Under the assumptions of Lemma 2 we have

\frac{f_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i,n}(0))}{f_{i}^{\prime}(1)}=\exp\Big(-\frac{\nu_{i}(2+o(1))}{\mu_{i-1}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}\Big)\ ,

with the
o(1)
-term applying uniformly to all
0\leq i\leq n
fulfilling
\rho_{i}\leq\eta\rho_{n}
.

Proof.

First let us show that for any numbers
s_{i,n}\in[f_{i,n}(0),1]
we have

\displaystyle f_{i,n}^{\prime\prime}(s_{i,n})=f_{i,n}^{\prime\prime}(1)(1+o(1))

(8)

as
n\to\infty
, uniformly for all
i
under consideration. Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
By means of assumption (
\ast
) we obtain with
s:=s_{i,n}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(1)-f_{i,n}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}k(k-1)f_{i}[k](1-s^{k-2})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k>c_{\varepsilon}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))}k^{2}f_{i}[k]+\sum_{k\leq c_{\varepsilon}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))}k(k-1)f_{i}[k](k-2)(1-s)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\sum_{k\geq 2}k^{2}f_{i}[k]+c_{\varepsilon}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))(1-s)\sum_{k\geq 2}k(k-1)f_{i}[k]

\displaystyle\leq\big(2\varepsilon+c_{\varepsilon}(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))(1-f_{i,n}(0))\big)f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(1)\ .

(9)

Note that
1-f_{i,n}(0)=\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{i}=1)
. Therefore, from Lemma 2, uniformly for all
i
under consideration,

\displaystyle(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))(1-f_{i,n}(0))

\displaystyle=(1+f_{i}^{\prime}(1))\frac{2+o(1)}{\mu_{i}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2+o(1)}{1-\eta}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{i}\rho_{n}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{i-1}\rho_{n}}\Big)\ .

As in the proof of Lemma 1, the right-hand side can be made arbitrarily small, uniformly in
i
. This estimate together with (
9
) implies (
8
).

Now, from (
8
) we get for suitable
s_{i,n}\in[f_{i,n}(0),1]

f_{i}^{\prime}(1)-f_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i,n}(0))=f^{\prime\prime}(s_{i,n})(1-f_{i,n}(0))=f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(1)(1-f_{i,n}(0))(1+o(1))

or

\frac{f_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i,n}(0))}{f_{i}^{\prime}(1)}=1-\frac{f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{f_{i}^{\prime}(1)}(1-f_{i,n}(0))(1+o(1))\ .

Noting
f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(1)/f_{i}^{\prime}(1)=\nu_{i}\mu_{i}/\mu_{i-1}
and applying Lemma 2 to
1-f_{i,n}(0)=\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{i}=1)
we rewrite this formula as

\frac{f_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i,n}(0))}{f_{i}^{\prime}(1)}=1-\frac{\nu_{i}(2+o(1))}{\mu_{i-1}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}\ .

Since
\rho_{n}-\rho_{i}\geq(1-\eta)\rho_{n}
, Lemma 1 implies that the right-hand fraction is uniformly of order
o(1)
. Therefore, we may transform the formula into

\frac{f_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i,n}(0))}{f_{i}^{\prime}(1)}=\exp\Big(-\frac{\nu_{i}(2+o(1))}{\mu_{i-1}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}\Big)\ ,

which is our claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem 2.

For the proof of the theorem it is sufficient to show that for any
0<t<1
and for any sequence of natural numbers
g_{n}\leq n
,
n\geq 1
, fulfilling

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{g_{n}}}{\rho_{n}}\to t

(10)

we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\leq g_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0)\to t

(11)

as
n\to\infty
.

Let us recall from
[
14
]
the formula for the probability of the event
\{G_{n}\geq k\}
with
k<n
, which readily generalizes from GW processes to BPVEs. (We leave
G_{n}
undefined on the event
Z_{n}=0
, hence
\{G_{n}\geq k\}\subset\{Z_{n}>0\}
.)
Note that
Z_{k}
takes the value
z>0
with probability
f_{0,k}[z]
. Then, in order to realize the event
\{G_{n}\geq k\}
, precisely one of the
z
individuals in generation
k
will have offspring in generation
n
.
Therefore

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k\mid Z_{k}=z)=z\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{k}=1)\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}=0\mid Z_{k}=1)^{z-1}

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z=0}^{\infty}z\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{k}=1)\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}=0\mid Z_{k}=1)^{z-1}f_{0,k}[z]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{k}=1)f_{0,k}^{\prime}(\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}=0\mid Z_{k}=1))

(12)

Moreover,
f_{0,k}^{\prime}(s)=f_{1}^{\prime}(f_{1,k}(s))f_{2}^{\prime}(f_{2,k}(s))\cdots f_{k}^{\prime}(s)
and
\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}=0\mid Z_{k}=1)=f_{k,n}(0)
for
k\leq n
. Therefore, we end up with

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k)=\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{k}=1)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i,n}(0))\ .

We use this formula with
k
replaced by
g_{n}
. Since these numbers satisfy (
10
), we have
\rho_{g_{n}}\leq\eta\rho_{g}
with some
\eta\in(t,1)
and
n
sufficiently large. Thus we may use the approximations given by Lemmas 2 and 3 uniformly for all
i\leq g_{n}
yielding

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq g_{n})=\frac{2+o(1)}{\mu_{g_{n}}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{g_{n}})}\exp\Big(-\sum_{i=1}^{g_{n}}\frac{\nu_{i}(2+o(1))}{\mu_{i-1}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{g_{n}}f_{i}^{\prime}(1)\ .

Note that the term
\mu_{g_{n}}
and the product cancel out. Due to the uniform convergence of the
o(1)
-terms inside of the sum, we may replace the factors
2+o(1)
(which depend on
i
) by one factor
2+o(1)
outside of the sum. Note also from (
2
) that
\nu_{i}/\mu_{i-1}=\rho_{i}-\rho_{i-1}
implying

\sum_{i=1}^{g_{n}}\frac{\nu_{i}(2+o(1))}{\mu_{i-1}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}=(2+o(1))\sum_{i=1}^{g_{n}}\frac{1}{1-\rho_{i}/\rho_{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\rho_{n}}-\frac{\rho_{i-1}}{\rho_{n}}\Big)\ .

The right-hand sum may be viewed as a Riemann approximation to
\int_{0}^{\rho_{g_{n}}/\rho_{n}}(1-x)^{-1}dx
, using the partition
\rho_{i}/\rho_{n}
,
i=0,\ldots,g_{n}
. Lemma 1 shows, that its mesh converges to zero. Also taking (
10
) into account we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{g_{n}}\frac{\nu_{i}(2+o(1))}{\mu_{i-1}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{i})}=(2+o(1))\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dx}{1-x}=-(2+o(1))\log(1-t)\ ,

consequently

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq g_{n})=\frac{2+o(1)}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{g_{n}}}\exp\big((2+o(1))\log(1-t)\big)=\frac{2+o(1)}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{g_{n}}}(1-t)^{2}\ .

Again using Lemma 2 with
i=0
and (
10
) we arrive at

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq g_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0)=\frac{\rho_{n}(1+o(1))}{\rho_{n}-\rho_{g_{n}}}(1-t)^{2}=1-t+o(1)\ .

Finally, because of Lemma 1 and (
10
) we have
\rho_{g_{n}+1}/\rho_{n}=t+o(1)
, too, therefore we may replace
g_{n}
by
g_{n}+1
in our considerations. Hence we obtain as well

\mathbf{P}(G_{n}>g_{n}\mid Z_{n}>0)=1-t+o(1),

which implies the desired assertion (
11
).
∎

Proof of Theorem 3.

Fix some
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Let
0=T_{0,n}<T_{1,n}<\cdots
denote the jump times of the process
X_{n}(t):=Z_{k_{n}(t),n}
, and
0=T_{0}<T_{1}<\cdots
the jump times of the limiting process
X(t):=Y(\log\tfrac{1}{1-t})
,
0\leq t<1
. Since otherwise the processes have constant paths, and since
X(t)
possesses only finitely many jumps during the restricted time interval
[0,\eta]
, it is sufficient for the proof to show that for all
j\geq 1
the random variables
X_{n}(T_{j,n})\cdot 1_{T_{j,n}\leq\eta}
converge to
X(T_{j})=j+1
in probability and that the random vectors
(T_{1,n}\cdot 1_{T_{0,n}\leq\eta},\ldots,T_{j,n}\cdot 1_{T_{j-1,n}\leq\eta})
converge in distribution to the corresponding limiting vector as
n\to\infty
. To see this, one has to show convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions and relative compactness of the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. The former requirement follows from

\{T_{j,n}>t,X_{n}(T_{j,n})=j+1\}\subset\{X_{n}(t)\leq j+1\}\subset\{T_{j,n}>t\}\ ,

and the latter one e.g. by means of
[
4
, Theorem 7.2]
. Namely, the compact containment condition arises from the monotonicity of the sample paths of
X_{n}
, and for the modulus of continuity
w^{\prime}
we have
w^{\prime}(X_{n},\delta,\eta)=0
for
0<\delta<\min\{T_{j+1,n}\wedge\eta-T_{j,n}:j\geq 0,T_{j,n}<\eta\}
.

The standard Yule process is a pure birth process characterized by the property that the waiting time at any state
j\in\mathbb{N}
, given the previous waiting times, is equal in distribution to the minimum of
j
independent, standard exponential random variables. Hence, this property holds for the terms

\log(1-T_{j})^{-1}-\log(1-T_{j-1})^{-1}=\log\Big(1-\frac{T_{j}-T_{j-1}}{1-T_{j-1}}\Big)^{-1}.

This readily translates to the characterization of the process
X(t)
,
0\leq t<1
, as a (non-homogeneous) pure birth process with the property that for any
j\geq 1
, given the values of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{j-1}
, the random variable
(T_{j}-T_{j-1})/(1-T_{j-1})
has the distribution of the minimum of
j
independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on the interval
(0,1)
. We are going to check this property for the limiting process.

To ease notation we set
\mathbb{P}_{n}(\cdot)=\mathbf{P}(\cdot\mid Z_{n}>0)
. We prepare the proof by two approximations. Let
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq\eta
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n}(T_{1,n}>t^{\prime}\mid X_{n}(t)=1)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{n}(X_{n}(t^{\prime})=1)}{\mathbb{P}_{n}(X_{n}(t)=1)}=\frac{\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k_{n}(t^{\prime}))}{\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k_{n}(t))}\ .

Using Theorem 2 it follows that in the limit
n\to\infty

\mathbb{P}_{n}(T_{1,n}>t^{\prime}\mid X_{n}(t)=1)\sim\frac{1-\rho_{k_{n}(t^{\prime})}/\rho_{n}}{1-\rho_{k_{n}(t)}/\rho_{n}}\ ,

and because of
\rho_{k_{n}(u)}\leq u\rho_{n}\leq\rho_{k_{n}(u)+1}
with
u=t,t^{\prime}
we obtain by means of Lemma 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n}(T_{1,n}>t^{\prime}\mid X_{n}(t)=1)\to\frac{1-t^{\prime}}{1-t}

(13)

uniformly in all
t\leq\eta
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n}(X_{n}(T_{1,n})=2\mid X_{n}(t)=1,T_{1,n}=t^{\prime})=\frac{\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k_{n}(t^{\prime})-1,Z_{k_{n}(t^{\prime}),n}=2)}{\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k_{n}(t^{\prime})-1)}\ .

Let us write
k^{\prime}=k_{n}(t^{\prime})
for short. In order to realize the event
\{G_{n}=k^{\prime}-1,Z_{k^{\prime},n}=2\}
it is required that from
z
individuals in generation
k^{\prime}-1
just one has descendants in generation
n
, and from its
z^{\prime}
-many offspring precisely two succeed in that respect. Given
z
and
z^{\prime}
this event has probability

\displaystyle zf_{0,k^{\prime}-1}[z]\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}=0

\displaystyle\mid Z_{k^{\prime}-1}=1)^{z-1}

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{z^{\prime}(z^{\prime}-1)}{2}f_{k^{\prime}}[z^{\prime}]\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{k^{\prime}}=1)^{2}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}=0\mid Z_{k^{\prime}}=1)^{z^{\prime}-2}\ .

Summing over
z
and
z^{\prime}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k^{\prime}-1,Z_{k^{\prime}}=2)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{P}(Z_{n}>0\mid Z_{k^{\prime}}=1)^{2}f_{0,k^{\prime}-1}^{\prime}\big(f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0)\big)f_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime\prime}\big(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0)\big)\ .

Moreover, from (
12
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k^{\prime}-1)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k^{\prime}-1)-\mathbf{P}(G_{n}\geq k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(1-f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0))f_{0,k^{\prime}-1}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0))-(1-f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))f_{0,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))\ ,

and because of
f_{0,k^{\prime}}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))=f_{0,k^{\prime}-1}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0))f_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))
and
f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0)=f_{k^{\prime}}(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k^{\prime}-1)

\displaystyle=f_{0,k^{\prime}-1}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0))\cdot\big(1-f_{k^{\prime}}(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))-(1-f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))f_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))\big)

\displaystyle=f_{0,k^{\prime}-1}^{\prime}(f_{k^{\prime}-1,n}(0))\cdot\frac{1}{2}f_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime\prime}(s_{t^{\prime},n})(1-f_{k^{\prime},n}(0))^{2}\ ,

with suitable
s_{t^{\prime},n}\in[f_{k^{\prime},n}(0),1]
. Consequently,

\frac{\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k_{n}(t^{\prime})-1,Z_{k_{n}(t^{\prime}),n}=2)}{\mathbf{P}(G_{n}=k_{n}(t^{\prime})-1)}=\frac{f_{k_{n}(t^{\prime})}^{\prime\prime}\big(f_{k_{n}(t^{\prime}),n}(0))}{f_{k_{n}(t^{\prime})}^{\prime\prime}(s_{t^{\prime},n})}\ .

Since
\rho_{k_{n}(t^{\prime})}\leq t^{\prime}\rho_{n}\leq\eta\rho_{n}
, we may use (
8
) implying that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{n}(X_{n}(T_{1,n})=2\mid X_{n}(t)=1,T_{1,n}=t^{\prime})=1+o(1)

(14)

uniformly in all
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq\eta
.

We are now ready to prove that for any
j\geq 1
the random vector

\displaystyle(T_{1,n}\cdot 1_{T_{0,n}\leq\eta},X_{n}(T_{1,n})\cdot 1_{T_{1,n}\leq\eta},\ldots,T_{j,n}\cdot 1_{T_{j-1,n}\leq\eta},X_{n}(T_{j,n})\cdot 1_{T_{j,n}\leq\eta})

(15)

has the announced limiting behaviour. We proceed by induction on
j
. For
j=1
the claim follows immediately from (
13
) and (
14
) by setting
t=0
(taking into account the uniform convergence in (
14
) concerning
t^{\prime}
). For the induction step suppose that our claim is valid for
j-1
. Then the induction hypothesis can be applied to the left part in (
15
). In particular, with increasing
n
the probability approaches 1 that the term
X_{n}(T_{j-1,n})\cdot 1_{T_{j-1,n}\leq\eta}
takes the value
j
. Then precisely
j
individuals in generation
k_{n}(T_{j-1,n})
will have descendants until generation
n
. Due to the properties of the underlying branching process the induced
j
family trees are independent and identically distributed. Therefore, the waiting times
W_{1,j,n}
, …,
W_{j,j,n}
within these subtrees up to their first branching events are i.i.d. random variables. Due to the Markov property of the reduced process
their distributions, given
T_{j-1,n}=t
, depend only on the distributions
f_{k}
with
k_{n}(t)\leq k\leq n
, and not on
j
. In particular, for
1\leq i\leq j
and
0\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq 1

\mathbb{P}_{n}(W_{i,j,n}>t^{\prime}-t\mid T_{j-1,n}=t)=\mathbb{P}_{n}(T_{1,n}>t^{\prime}\mid X_{n}(t)=1)\ ,

so that because of (
13
) the conditional distribution of
W_{i,j,n}
is asymptotically uniform on
[0,1-t]
, and the asymptotic distribution of
T_{j,n}-T_{j-1,n}=\min\{W_{1,j,n}
, …,
W_{j,j,n}\}
given
T_{j-1,n}
coincides in distribution with the minimum of
j
uniform random variables on
[0,1-T_{j-1,n}]
.
Also, with probability going to 1 all
W_{1,j,n}
, …,
W_{j,j,n}
differ from each other. Then, in view of (
14
) and in case of
T_{j,n}\leq\eta
, the process
X_{n}
will increase at time
T_{j,n}
just by 1 and
X_{n}(T_{j,n})
will take the value
j+1
in the limit
n\to\infty
. This finishes the induction and concludes the theorem’s proof.
∎

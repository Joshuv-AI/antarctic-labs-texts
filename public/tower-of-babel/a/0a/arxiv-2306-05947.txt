Abstract
Central limit theorems (CLTs) have a long history in probability and statistics. They play a fundamental role in constructing valid statistical inference procedures. Over the last century, various techniques have been developed in probability and statistics to prove CLTs under a variety of assumptions on random variables. Quantitative versions of CLTs (e.g., Berry–Esseen bounds) have also been parallelly developed. In this article, we propose to use approximation theory from functional analysis to derive explicit bounds on the difference between expectations of functions.



1
Description of the problem

Suppose we have a sequence of
d
-dimensional random vectors
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and a
d
-dimensional random vector
{Z}
such that
{Z}
is a gaussian random variable distributed with mean equal to
\mathbb{E}\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{X}_{i}\right)
and variance equal to
\mathrm{Var}\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{X}_{i}\right)
. For notational simplicity, set
S_{n,{X}}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{X}_{i}
.

Note that, if
{X_{1}},\cdots,{X_{n}}
are i.i.d distributed with mean
{0}
and variance
{\Sigma}
, then
{Z}\sim\mathcal{N}({0},{\Sigma})
. If
{X_{1}},\cdots,{X_{n}}
are independent with mean
{0}
, then
{Z}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({0},n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}({X_{i}})\right)
.

Suppose
f
is a Borel measurable function. We wish to bound

\Delta_{f}=\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert,

(1)

with a constant which depends on
n
,
d
and the fucntion
f
. Classical Berry–Esseen bounds
(
Bentkus, 2004
;
Raič, 2019
)
provide inequalities for
\Delta_{f}
when
f(x)=\mathbf{1}{x\in A},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some sets
A
(e.g., convex sets, Euclidean balls). Results that bound
\Delta_{f}
for Borel measurable functions with specific polynomial growth are also well-known; see
Sazonov, 1981
,
Bhattacharya and Rao, 2010
, and
Angst and Poly, 2017
. These bounds are derived using smoothing inequalities and are valid for all Borel measurable functions. Unfortunately, these bounds are not sharp enough to imply correct dimension on dimension or smoothness for high-dimensional functions that are “highly” smooth (e.g., high-dimensional functions that depend only on a subset of coordinates); see, for example,
Bentkus, 2003a
for sharp bounds on
\Delta_{f}
for functions
f
in Hölder classes. Further, the dependence on dimension is much better than that implied by the general result in
Sazonov, 1981
and
Bhattacharya and Rao, 2010
.

In this paper, we propose an approximation theory and level sets based approach to obtain bounds for
\Delta_{f}
. The bounds we obtain have sharp dependence on the dimension as well as the sample size even when the dimension grows faster than the sample size. On the flip side, our bounds do not apply to all Borel measurable functions but only to a special class of functions.

The remaining article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide a discussion of existing Berry–Esseen bounds for independent random vectors from
Bentkus, 2004
and
Raič, 2019
. These results will be the backbone of our approach. In Section
3
, we provide our first result that bounds
\Delta_{f}
, for a bounded
f
, in terms of the upper/lower level sets of
f
. In Section
4
, we provide an application of this result to bound
\Delta_{f}
for bounded quasi-concave functions. In Section
5
, we provide a discussion of non-uniform Berry–Esseen bound and apply our main result to get bounds for functions in Barron space. We conclude the article with a brief discussion in Section
6
.

3
Level sets and bounds on
\Delta_{f}

If the conditions of
Theorem

2.1
are met, then we have

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left({Z}\in A\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{b_{d}(\mathcal{A})}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|\Sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}X_{i}|^{3}

or in other words, we have,

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{b_{d}(\mathcal{A})}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|\Sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}X_{i}|^{3}

where,
f
is defined as
f(x)=\mathbb{I}(x\in A)
,
\Sigma=\mathrm{Var}\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)
and
b_{d}(\mathcal{A})
is as defined in
theorem

2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
be a bounded Borel measurable function. For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define the upper level sets of
f(\cdot)
at level
t
as
A_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,f(x)\geq t\}
. Then, we have,

\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}(Z\in A_{t})\right|.

Theorem

3.1
implies that for bounded functions
f
,
\Delta_{f}
can be controlled using classical Berry–Esseen bounds if the level sets belong to a “favorable” class.
The scope of
Theorem

3.1
can be expanded significantly using the following two combinations.

3.1
Combination 1

Suppose
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}
are bounded functions (satisfying the conditions of
theorem

2.1
) for which the upper level sets all belong to
\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is any one of the favorable classes of convex sets or half-spaces or Euclidean balls. Then, for a function
f
such that
f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}f_{j}(x)
, where
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert\leq c
for some constant
c
, by
Theorem

3.1
, we have,

\begin{split}\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert&=\left\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left({Z}\right)\bigg\}\right\rvert\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left({Z}\right)\bigg\}\right\rvert\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Bigg[\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert\left\lvert\bigg\{\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left({Z}\right)\Bigg\}\right\rvert\bigg]\\
&\leq\sup_{j}\left\lvert\bigg\{\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left({Z}\right)\bigg\}\right\rvert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert\\
&\leq c\sup_{j}\left\lvert\bigg\{\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f_{j}\left({Z}\right)\bigg\}\right\rvert\\
&\leq c.\sup_{j}\Bigg[2\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\sup_{t}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{j,t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{j,t}\right)\right\rvert\Bigg]\\
&\qquad[\text{we got the above step using~\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lemma 4.1}}]\\
&=2c.\sup_{j}\Bigg[\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\sup_{t}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(\in A_{j,t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{j,t}\right)\right\rvert\Bigg]\\
&\leq 2c.\sup_{j}\Bigg[\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\frac{b_{d}(\mathcal{A})}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|\Sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}X_{i}|^{3}\right)\Bigg]\\
&\qquad[\because\text{ each }f_{j}\text{ has its upper level sets in the same favorable class }\mathcal{A}]\\
&=\frac{2b_{d}(\mathcal{A})c}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|\Sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}X_{i}|^{3}\right)\sup_{j}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

A direct application of this result can yield bounds for functions that are uniformly approximable by a class of functions whose level sets belong in the favorable class. This will be further explored in Part II.
The following result shows that one can construct an infinite class of functions with the same upper-level sets from a given function.

Theorem 3.2
.

If the upper-level sets of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
belong in
\mathcal{A}
, then the upper-level sets of
g\circ f
also belong in
\mathcal{A}
for any non-decreasing function
g:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
A^{\prime}_{t}
be the upper level sets of
g
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, Then, we have,
A^{\prime}_{t}=\{x:g(f(x))\geq t\}
.
Now, since
g
is a non-decreasing function, we have,
g(f(x))\geq t\iff f(x)\geq g^{-1}(t)
.
Thus, we have
A^{\prime}_{t}=\{x:g(f(x))\geq t\}=\{x:f(x)\geq g^{-1}(t)\}=A_{g^{-1}(t)}
, where,
A_{t}=\{x:f(x)\geq t\}
denotes the upper level set of
f
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We know that
A_{g}^{-1}(t)
belongs in
\mathcal{A}
and hence
A^{\prime}_{t}
also belongs in
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

3.3
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Proof.

To begin with, we consider a non-negative function
f
. Then, we can write,

\begin{split}f(x)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{I}(f(x)\geq t)dt=\int_{0}^{f(x)}dt+\int_{f(x)}^{\infty}0dt=f(x)\end{split}

Now,
\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
. Thus, we can now write,

\begin{split}&f(x)=\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{I}(f(x)\geq t)dt\\
\implies&\mathbb{E}f(W)=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{I}(f(W)\geq t)dt\end{split}

By Tonelli’s theorem, we know that the swapping of the expectation operator and integral is valid for non-negative summands. For the convenience of the reader, we recall Tonelli’s theorem here: Suppose that
(T,\mathcal{T},\mu)
is a
\sigma
-finite measure space, and that
X_{t}
is a real-valued random variable for each
t\in T
. We assume that
(\omega,t)\mapsto X_{t}(\omega)
is measurable, as a function from the product space
(\Omega\times T,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{T})
into
\mathbb{R}
. If
X_{t}
is non-negative for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then,

\mathbb{E}\int_{T}X_{t}d\mu(t)=\int_{T}\mathbb{E}(X_{t})d\mu(t)

Thus, we have,

\mathbb{E}f(W)=\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{I}(W\geq t)dt=\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbb{P}(f(W)\geq t)dt

Now, we consider the expression
\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert
.

\begin{split}\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\geq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq t\right)\right]dt\right\rvert\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\geq t\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq t\right)\right\rvert dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right\rvert dt\\
&\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right\rvert\end{split}

Now, suppose that
f
is not non-negative, but bounded. Then, we have,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)&=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{I}(f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)\geq t)dt-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\mathbf{I}(f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)\leq t)dt\Bigg]\\
&\quad-\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbf{I}(f(Z)\geq t)dt-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\mathbf{I}(f(Z)\leq t)dt\Bigg]\end{split}

By Tonelli’s theorem, we know that the swapping of the expectation operator and integral is valid for non-negative summands. Thus, we have,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)&=\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{I}(f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)\geq t)dt-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{I}(f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)\leq t)dt\Bigg]\\
&\quad-\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{I}(f(Z)\geq t)dt-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{I}(f(Z)\leq t)dt\Bigg]\\
&=\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt\\
&\quad-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in B_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in B_{t}\right)\right]dt\\
&\quad\text{where, }B_{t}\text{is the lower level sets of }f\text{ defined as }B_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:f(x)\leq t\}\end{split}

In the case of general functions
f
, which may not be non-negative, since the integral doesn’t run only from
0
to
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
, there is an occurrence of the lower level sets
B_{t}
’s. But since the probability of lying in
B_{t}
’s can be expressed in terms of lying in
A_{t}
’s, we can easily write the whole bound in terms of
A_{t}
’s.

\begin{split}\therefore\left\lvert\mathbb{E}f\left(S_{n,{X}}\right)-\mathbb{E}f\left({Z}\right)\right\rvert&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in B_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in B_{t}\right)\right]dt\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt-\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt\right\rvert\\
&[\because\mathbb{P}(W\in B_{t})=1-\mathbb{P}(W\in A_{t})]\\
&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt+\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{0}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right]dt\right\rvert\\
&\leq\int_{-\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}^{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Z\in A_{t}\right)\right\rvert dt\\
&\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n,{X}}\in A_{t}\right)-\mathbb{P}(Z\in A_{t})\right\rvert\end{split}

∎

4.1
Classes of functions whose upper-level sets are convex sets

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
X
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A function
f:X\longrightarrow R
is quasiconcave if

f(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\geq\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\quad\forall\alpha\in[0,1],x,y\in X.

(2)

It is well-known that quasiconcave functions are precisely those functions whose upper-level sets are convex. It is reproduced below for reader’s convenience.

Theorem 4.2
.

A function
f:X\longrightarrow R
is quasiconcave if and only if the upper level sets of
f
, i.e., the sets
A_{t}=\{x:f(x)\geq t\}
are convex for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f:X\longrightarrow R
is quasiconcave.

Fix a
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider the points
x,y\in A_{t}=\{z:f(z)\geq t\}
. Then,
f(x)\geq t
and
f(y)\geq t
. Then by (
2
),
\forall\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have,

f(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\geq\min\{f(x),f(y)\}\geq t.

This means that
\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y\in A_{t}
, thus proving that
A_{t}
is convex.
Now, suppose that the upper level sets of
f
,
A_{t}=\{z:f(z)\geq t\}
is convex
\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider two points
x,y
. Let
t^{\prime}=\min\{f(x),f(y)\}
. Then,

x\in A_{t^{\prime}}\text{ and }y\in A_{t^{\prime}}.

Now,
A_{t^{\prime}}
is convex
\implies\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y\in A_{t^{\prime}}\forall\alpha\in[0,1].

Then, from the definition of
A_{t^{\prime}}
we have,

f(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\geq t^{\prime}=\min\{f(x),f(y)\}

Since,
x,y
are arbitrary, we have proved that
f
is quasiconcave.
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

be independent random variables with distribution functions
F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{n}
and
\mathbb{E}X_{k}=0
,
\sigma^{2}_{k}:=\mathbb{E}X_{k}<\infty
,

S_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k},B_{n}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{2}>0.

Let us denote

\bar{F}_{n}(x):=P(S_{n}<xB_{n}),\quad\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}dt,\quad\Delta_{n}(x)=\left\lvert\bar{F}_{n}(x)-\Phi(x)\right\rvert,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

L_{n}(\varepsilon):=\frac{1}{B_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert X_{k}\right\rvert>\varepsilon B_{n}),\quad\varepsilon>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then,

\begin{split}\Delta_{n}(x)&\leq\frac{A}{(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{3})B_{n}}\int_{0}^{(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert)B_{n}}L_{n}(z)dz\\
&=A\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigg[\frac{EX_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert X_{k}\right\rvert>(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert)B_{n})}{(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert)^{2}B_{n}^{2}}+\frac{EX_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert X_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert)B_{n})}{(1+\left\lvert x\right\rvert)^{3}B_{n}^{3}}\Bigg]\end{split}

(3)

where
A
is an absolute constant.

5.1
Bounds for the ReLU and squared ReLU functions

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\sigma
denote the ReLU function, i.e.,
\sigma(x)=\max\{0,x\}
. Let
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{n}
be independent univariate random variables with
\mathbb{E}W_{k}=0
and
\sigma_{k}^{2}=\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}<\infty
,
\forall k=1,2,\dots,n
. Let us denote

S_{n,W}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}W_{k},\quad B_{n,W}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}^{2}.

Let
Z
be a standard normal random variable. Then, for
t\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma(S_{n,W}-t)-\mathbb{E}\sigma(B_{n,W}Z-t)\right\rvert&\leq\frac{A}{B_{n,W}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})+\frac{A}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert<t+B_{n,W})}{(t+B_{n,W})^{2}}.\end{split}

for an absolute constant
A
.

Proof.

For
t\geq 0
,

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\geq w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\geq w\right)\right]dw\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)<w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)<w\right)\right]dw\right\rvert\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)<w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)<w\right)\right\rvert dw\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg|[\mathbb{P}\left(\max\Bigg\{0,Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\Bigg\}<w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\max\Bigg\{0,\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\Bigg\}<w\right)\Bigg|dw.\end{split}

(Since we are considering the integral from
0
to
\infty
, so we have
w>0
.) So, for a random variable
X,w>\max\{0,X\}
means that
w>0
and
w>X
. But because of the lower limit of the integral, we already have
w>0
. So, the event
w>0
and
w>X
boils down to the event
w>X
.

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\left[\mathbb{P}\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}<w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{n}}{B_{n}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}<w\right)\right]dw\right\rvert\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\left[\mathbb{P}\left(Z<\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}+w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{n}}{B_{n}}<\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}+w\right)\right]dw\right\rvert\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{n}\left(w+\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)dw\\
&=\int_{{t}/{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\Delta_{n}(s)ds\quad\Bigg[\text{by the change of variable }s=w+\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\Bigg]\end{split}

(4)

By
Theorem

5.1
, we know that,

\Delta_{n}(s)\leq A\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\frac{\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert>(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)B_{n,W})}{(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)^{2}B_{n,W}^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)B_{n,W})}{(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)^{3}B_{n,W}^{3}}\right]

(5)

for an absolute constant
A
.
Then, we have

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{n}(s)ds\\
&=A\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert>(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)B_{n,W}}{(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)^{2}B_{n,W}^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)B_{n,W})}{(1+\left\lvert s\right\rvert)^{3}B_{n,W}^{3}}\Bigg]ds\\
&=A\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert>(1+s)B_{n,W}}{(1+s)^{2}B_{n,W}^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+s)B_{n,W})}{(1+s)^{3}B_{n,W}^{3}}\Bigg]ds.\end{split}

(6)

Now, the first term on the right hand side can be simplified as follows:

\begin{split}&\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert>(1+s)B_{n,W})}{(1+s)^{2}B_{n,W}^{2}}ds\\
&=\mathbb{E}\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\frac{W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert>(1+s)B_{n,W})}{(1+s)^{2}B_{n,W}^{2}}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}\left(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1\geq\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1}\frac{1}{(1+s)^{2}}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1}\frac{1}{(1+s)^{2}}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})\Bigg[-\frac{1}{1+s}\Bigg]_{0}^{\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1}\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})\Bigg[1-\frac{1}{1+(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1)}\Bigg]\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})\Bigg[1-\frac{B_{n,W}}{\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert}\Bigg]\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})-\frac{1}{B_{n,W}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W}).\end{split}

(7)

The second term on the right-hand side of the bound on
|\mathbb{E}\sigma(S_{n,W}/B_{n,W}-t/B_{n,W})-\mathbb{E}\sigma(Z-t/B_{n,W})|
can be simplified as follows:

\begin{split}&\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+s)B_{n,W})}{(1+s)^{3}B_{n,W}^{3}}ds\\
&=\mathbb{E}\int_{\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}}^{\infty}\frac{\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+s)B_{n,W})}{(1+s)^{3}B_{n,W}^{3}}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\int_{\max\{tB_{n,W}^{-1},\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1\}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+s)^{3}}ds\\
&=\frac{1}{B_{n,W}^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\Bigg[-\frac{1}{2(1+s)^{2}}\Bigg]_{\max\{tB_{n,W}^{-1},\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1\}}^{\infty}\\
&=\frac{1}{2B_{n,W}^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\frac{1}{(1+\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1)^{2}}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1\geq tB_{n,W}^{-1})\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2B_{n,W}^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\frac{1}{(1+tB_{n,W}^{-1})^{2}}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert B_{n,W}^{-1}-1<tB_{n,W}^{-1})\\
&=\frac{1}{2B_{n,W}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})+\frac{1}{2B_{n,W}}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\frac{1}{(t+B_{n,W})^{2}}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert<t+B_{n,W})\end{split}

(8)

Combining these inequalities, we have

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\
&\leq A\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{tB_{n,W}^{-1}}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert>(1+s)B_{n,W}}{(1+s)^{2}B_{n,W}^{2}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\leq(1+s)B_{n,W})}{(1+s)^{3}B_{n,W}^{3}}\Bigg]ds\\
&\leq\frac{A}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})-\frac{A}{2B_{n,W}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})+\\
&\quad\qquad\frac{A}{2B_{n,W}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert<t+B_{n,W})}{(t+B_{n,W})^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{A}{B_{n,W}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})+\frac{A}{2B_{n,W}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert<t+B_{n,W})}{(t+B_{n,W})^{2}}\end{split}

(9)

Finally, using the fact that
\sigma(\cdot)
is a positively homogeneous function (i.e.,
\sigma(cx)=c\sigma(x)
for all
c>0
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma(S_{n,W}-t)-\mathbb{E}\sigma(B_{n,W}Z-t)\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\max\{0,S_{n,W}-t\}-\mathbb{E}\max\{0,B_{n,W}Z-t\}\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\mathbb{E}B_{n,W}\max\Bigg\{0,\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\Bigg\}-\mathbb{E}B_{n,W}\max\Bigg\{0,\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\Bigg\}\right\rvert\\
&=B_{n,W}\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{A}{B_{n,W}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}W_{k}^{2}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert\geq t+B_{n,W})+\frac{A}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert^{3}\mathbf{I}(\left\lvert W_{k}\right\rvert<t+B_{n,W})}{(t+B_{n,W})^{2}}\quad[\text{using }\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq: eq 6}]\end{split}

(10)

This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\sigma(\cdot)

\sigma(x)=\max\{0,x\}
. Let
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{n}

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma^{2}\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma^{2}\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\
&=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma^{2}\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\geq w\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma^{2}\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\geq w\right)\right]dw\right\rvert\\

Since we are considering the integral from

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\mathbb{E}\sigma^{2}\left(\frac{S_{n,W}}{B_{n,W}}-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\sigma^{2}\left(Z-\frac{t}{B_{n,W}}\right)\right\rvert\\

(14)

This concludes the proof.

Discussion

In this paper, we have provided bounds on the difference between functions of random variables using level sets of functions. Using classical uniform and non-uniform Berry–Esseen bounds for univariate random variables. The resulting bounds can be applied to single-layer neural networks and functions on
[-1,1]^{d}
with finite weighted norm integrable Fourier transform. These functions belong to the functions in Barron space. Unlike the classical bounds that depend on the oscillation function of
f
, our bounds do not have an explicit dimension dependence.
In part II, we will explore extensions of these results to functions with integrable Fourier transforms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and moreover, explore bounds obtained using function approximation theory using radial basis or neural networks.

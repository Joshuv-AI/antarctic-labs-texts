Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth projective variety. We show that the map that sends a codimension one distribution on X X to its singular scheme is a morphism from the moduli space of distributions into a Hilbert scheme. We describe its fibers and, when X = ℙ n X={\mathbb{P}^{n}} , compute them via syzygies. As an application, we describe the moduli spaces of degree 1 distributions on ℙ 3 {\mathbb{P}^{3}} . We also give the minimal graded free resolution for the ideal of the singular scheme of a generic distribution on ℙ 3 {\mathbb{P}^{3}} .

1.
Introduction

The theory of distributions has its origins in the studies of nineteenth-century mathematicians such as Clebsch, Darboux, Frobenius, Grassmann and Jacobi. They were motivated by the fundamental work of Pfaff, who proposed a geometric approach to the study of differential equations, see
[
16
,
21
]
. When a distribution is integrable, in Frobenius’s sense, it defines a foliation, whose study also goes back to the qualitative theory of differential equations initiated by Poincaré, Darboux and Painlevé. Since then, these theories have developed into many distinct interesting active areas of research.

In this paper we are interested in the study of families of distributions on algebraic varieties. A distribution
{\mathscr{F}}
on a scheme
X
, over a field
\kappa
, may be defined by a subsheaf
T_{\mathscr{F}}\hookrightarrow TX
, called the tangent sheaf of
{\mathscr{F}}
. In the integrable case, this has been initiated by Jouanolou
[
23
]
with the description of the spaces of codimension one foliations on complex projective spaces, of degrees
0
and
1
, see Section
2
for the definition of degree. Later Cerveau and Lins Neto
[
9
]
described the case of degree
2
. Recently, a partial classification in degree
3
was obtained in
[
13
]
. These works rely on a naive definition of moduli.

A systematic study of flat families of (not necessarily integrable) distributions was recently initiated in
[
4
]
, see also
[
29
]
. The authors of
[
4
]
showed that there exists a quasi-projective variety
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
that parametrizes isomorphism classes of distributions, on a complex projective manifold
X
, whose tangent sheaves have a fixed Hilbert polynomial
P
; a more detailed description of
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
is given in Section
2
below, where we also address some technical issues. In addition, they successfully described certain moduli spaces of codimension one distributions, see
[
4
, Section 11]
, using a forgetful map to the moduli space of sheaves.

Each distribution
{\mathscr{F}}
has a (possibly empty) singular scheme
\sing({\mathscr{F}})
. If
{\mathscr{F}}
has codimension one, the Hilbert polynomial of
\sing({\mathscr{F}})
is determined by that of
T_{\mathscr{F}}
. This defines a set-theoretic map from
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
, for
P
of degree
\dim X-1
, to a Hilbert scheme. One goal of this work is to establish that this map is a morphism of schemes. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective scheme over an algebraically closed ground field
\kappa
and fix
H\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
ample. Let
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
be the scheme parameterizing codimension one distributions (up to isomorphism) on
X
with tangent sheaves having Hilbert polynomial equal to
P
. Then there exists a polynomial
Q
depending on
P
and a morphism

\Sigma\colon\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)\longrightarrow{\rm Hilb}^{Q}(X),

which assigns to each distribution its singular scheme. Moreover, if
{\rm Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
then
Q(t)=P_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(t)-P_{TX}(t-c)+P(t-c)
, with
c\in\mathbb{Z}
depending on
P
, and:

•

For any
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
, the fiber of the morphism
\Sigma
, parameterizing distributions with singular scheme equal to
Z\in\operatorname{Hilb}^{Q}(X)
is a Zariski open subset of
\mathbb{P}\left(H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(c)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})\right)
;

•

When
X={\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and
Z
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree less than or equal to
c-2
, then the elements of
H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}(c)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})
can be obtained from the linear first syzygies of the homogeneous ideal associated with
Z
.

This result will be proved in Theorem
3.2
, Proposition
3.3
, Proposition
4.5
and Remark
4.6
below. The properties of the morphism
\Sigma
are related to two important problems in the theory of holomorphic distributions,
\kappa=\mathbb{C}
:

(i)

Determination of the distributions by their singular schemes;

(ii)

Construction of distributions with prescribed singular schemes.

The first is equivalent to the injectivity of
\Sigma
, the second to its surjectivity.

Problem (i) was considered by Gomez-Mont and Kempf
[
17
]
for foliations by curves in projective spaces, with reduced, zero dimensional singular schemes. Campillo and Olivares showed in
[
7
]
that the hypothesis that the singular scheme is reduced may be removed. Araujo and Corrêa
[
1
]
provided sufficient conditions for distributions of arbitrary dimension to be uniquely determined by their singular schemes.

On the other hand, Problem (ii) was first considered by Campillo and Olivares in
[
6
]
in order to understand under which conditions a subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
can be the singular subscheme of a foliation; they provided a Cayley–Bacharach type theorem for codimension one foliations on
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
. The same problem for the case of codimension one foliations in
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
also has been proposed by Cerveau in
[
8
]
.

As a first application, we consider generic codimension one distributions on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, that is, those distributions with 0-dimensional singular schemes. It was proved in
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
that if
d\geq n
, then a generic distribution of degree
d
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
with zero dimensional singularities is determined by its singular scheme; however, a careful reader will notice that their argument works in greater generality; in fact, it holds for every
d
and
n
, except for
d=1
and
n=2,3
. Degree 1 distributions (foliations) on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
are not determined by their singular schemes, as one can easily define non isomorphic foliations with three nondegenerate singularities in general position, see
[
5
, p. 882]
.
For
n=3
we will use our syzygetic approach to describe distributions with a given 0-dimensional singular scheme.

Theorem B
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a degree
d\geq 1
distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. If
Z
, the singular scheme of
{\mathscr{F}}
, is zero-dimensional then the homogeneous ideal
I(Z)
has minimal graded free resolution given by

0\longrightarrow S(-3d-2)\longrightarrow S(-2d-1)^{4}\oplus S(-d-2)\longrightarrow S(-d-1)^{4}\oplus S(-2d)\longrightarrow I(Z)\longrightarrow 0

where
S=\kappa[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}]
.
Moreover, the space of distributions singular at
Z
has dimension
4
if
d=1
and, if
d\geq 2
then
{\mathscr{F}}
is the unique degree
d
distribution singular at
Z
.

In particular, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a degree
d
distribution
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with isolated singularities. Then
{\mathscr{F}}
is determined by its singular scheme if
(d,n)\notin\{(1,2),(1,3)\}
.

2.
Families of Distributions

Let
\kappa
be an algebraically closed field and let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\kappa
. A
codimension
r
distribution

{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
is given by an exact sequence

(1)

\mathscr{F}:\ 0\longrightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}TX\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}N_{{\mathscr{F}}}\longrightarrow 0,

where
T_{\mathscr{F}}
is a coherent sheaf of rank
m:=\dim(X)-r
, and
N_{{\mathscr{F}}}
is a torsion free sheaf. The sheaves
T_{\mathscr{F}}
and
N_{{\mathscr{F}}}
are called the
tangent
and the
normal
sheaves of
\mathscr{F}
, respectively. Note that
T_{\mathscr{F}}
must be reflexive
[
22
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Two distributions
\mathscr{F}
and
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism
\beta\colon T_{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}^{\prime}}
such that
\phi^{\prime}\circ\beta=\phi
. By taking exterior power
\bigwedge^{m}\phi\colon\det(T_{\mathscr{F}})\rightarrow\bigwedge^{m}TX
, every codimension
r
distribution induces a section of
H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{m}TX\otimes\det(T_{\mathscr{F}})^{\vee})
. If
\mathscr{F}
and
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
are isomorphic distributions, then
\bigwedge^{m}\phi^{\prime}=\lambda\cdot\bigwedge^{m}\phi
, for some constant
\lambda\in\kappa^{*}
. Therefore, every isomorphism class of codimension
r
distribution on
X
induces an element in the projective space

\mathbb{P}\!\left(H^{0}\!\left(\bigwedge^{m}TX\otimes\det(T_{\mathscr{F}})^{\vee}\right)\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(H^{0}\!\left(\Omega^{r}_{X}\otimes\det(TX)\otimes\det(T_{\mathscr{F}})^{\vee}\right)\right).

The
singular scheme
of
\mathscr{F}
is defined as follows. Consider the the dual morphism
\phi^{\vee}\colon\Omega^{1}_{X}\rightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
. Taking the maximal exterior power and a twist we obtain

\Omega^{m}_{X}\otimes\det(T_{\mathscr{F}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X};

the image of such a morphism is the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
of a subscheme
\sing({\mathscr{F}}):=Z\subset X
, called the singular scheme of
\mathscr{F}
.

Next, we examine the notion of a family of distributions parameterized by a scheme
S
of finite type over
\kappa
. In fact, the authors of
[
29
]
and
[
4
]
have proposed two slightly different notions for a
family of distributions parameterized by
S
, namely:

Definition 2.1
.

According to
[
29
]
, a family of distributions parameterized by
S
is given by an
S
-scheme
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow S
and a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbf{F}\longrightarrow T\mathcal{X}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathbf{N}\longrightarrow 0,

where
T\mathcal{X}_{S}
stands for the relative tangent sheaf. Moreover, the family is called flat over
S
if so is
\mathbf{N}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
TX_{S}
denote the relative tangent sheaf of
X\times_{\kappa}S\rightarrow S
. According to
[
4
]
, a family of distributions on
X
parameterized by
S
is given by a subsheaf
\boldsymbol{\phi}\colon\mathbf{F}\hookrightarrow TX_{S}
such that for each
s\in S
the restriction
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{s}\colon\mathbf{F}|_{s}\rightarrow TX_{s}
is injective and
\operatorname{coker}(\phi_{s})
is torsion free.

Throughout this work we will stick with the latter definition but, in fact, we can reconcile both proposals. In order to achieve that we must make some technical considerations. Recall the following consequence
[
19
, Exposé IV, p.81]
of the Local Flatness Criterion, see also
[
22
, p.35]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow(B,\mathfrak{n})
be a morphism of Noetherian local rings
(\mathfrak{m}B\subset\mathfrak{n})
and let
u\colon M^{\prime}\rightarrow M
be a morphism of finitely generated
B
-modules. Suppose that
M
is flat over
A
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

u
is injective and
\operatorname{coker}(u)
is flat over
A
;

(ii)

u\otimes 1\colon M^{\prime}\otimes_{A}A/\mathfrak{m}\rightarrow M\otimes_{A}A/\mathfrak{m}
is injective.

Suppose that
S
and
X
are noetherian and
X
is flat over
S
. As any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{N}
on
X
is the quotient of a locally free sheaf,

0\longrightarrow\ker(\phi)\longrightarrow E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow 0.

Then Lemma
2.3
tells us that
\mathcal{N}
is flat over
S
if and only if the sequence above base changes correctly, i.e., for every
s\in S
we have

\ker(\phi)|_{s}\simeq\ker(\phi_{s}).

Note that we have only used that
X
is flat over
S
to imply that
E
being locally free (hence flat over
X
) is also flat over
S
.

Proposition 2.4
.

A family of distributions according to Definition
2.2
is equivalent to a flat family in Definition
2.1
such that
\mathcal{X}=X\times_{\kappa}S
and
\mathbf{N}_{s}
is torsion free for every
s\in S
.

Proof.

Since
X
is trivially flat over
\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa)
we know that
X\times S
is flat over
S
since flatness has base change property. Also note that both are noetherian since they are of finite type over
\kappa
. Now
TX_{S}
is a locally free sheaf on
X\times S
hence flat over
X\times S
and, by transitivity of flatness
[
20
, Proposition 9.1A]
,
TX_{S}
is flat over
S
. Then we apply Lemma
2.3
to the morphism
\boldsymbol{\phi}\colon\mathbf{F}\rightarrow TX_{S}
to show that
\coker(\boldsymbol{\phi})
is flat over
S
if and only if
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{s}\colon\mathbf{F}|_{s}\rightarrow TX_{s}
is injective for every
s\in S
.
∎

Note that since
TX_{S}
is flat over
S
,
\coker(\boldsymbol{\phi})
being flat implies that
\mathbf{F}
is flat over
S
as well.

3.
Moduli of Codimension One Distributions and Hilbert Schemes

We now recall the definition of the moduli space of distributions, which is based on the notion of family of distributions explained in the previous section.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and fix
H=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
an ample line bundle (a polarization). We will write
\mathcal{O}_{X}(k)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, for its tensor powers.

Let
\mathfrak{Sch}_{/\kappa}
denote the category of schemes of finite type over
\kappa
, and
\mathfrak{Sets}
be the category of sets. Fix a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
, and consider the functor

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}{\it ist}^{P}_{X}\colon\mathfrak{Sch}_{/\kappa}^{\rm op}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{Sets},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}{\it ist}^{P}_{X}(S):=\left\{(\mathbf{F},\boldsymbol{\phi})\mid\text{\parbox[c]{170.71393pt}{\raggedright\ $\mathbf{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\phi}}}{{\hookrightarrow}}T\mathcal{X}_{S}$ is a family of distributions with $\chi(F|_{s}(t))=P(t),\,\forall s\in S$ \@add@raggedright}}\ \right\}/\sim,

where a family is considered as in Definition
2.2
, and we say that
(\mathbf{F},\boldsymbol{\phi})\sim(\mathbf{F}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\prime})
if there exists and isomorphism
\boldsymbol{\beta}\colon\mathbf{F}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}^{\prime}
such that
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\prime}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}
.

One can show that the functor
{\mathcal{D}}{\it ist}^{P}_{X}
is represented by a quasi-projective scheme
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
, that is, there exists an isomorphism of functors
{\mathcal{D}}{\it ist}^{P}_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\Hom(\makebox[6.45831pt]{{$\cdot$}},\mathcal{D}^{P}(X))

[
4
, Proposition 2.4]
. Furthermore, the scheme
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
is an open subset of the quot scheme
{\rm Quot}^{P_{TX}-P}(TX)
. This last observation implies that the Zariski tangent space of
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
at the point
[F,\phi]
is
\Hom(F,\coker\phi)
and leads to the following smoothness criterion as an immediate consequence of
[
22
, Proposition 2.2.8]

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
[\mathscr{F}]\in\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
be the isomorphism class of a distribution on
X
. If
\Ext^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}},N_{\mathscr{F}})=0
, then
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
is smooth at
[\mathscr{F}]
and
\dim T_{[\mathscr{F}]}\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)=\dim\Hom(T_{\mathscr{F}},N_{\mathscr{F}})
.

Unfortunately, the vanishing condition
\Ext^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}},N_{\mathscr{F}})=0
is not satisfied by most of the codimension one distributions of degree 1, treated in Section
5
below. For these examples we compute
\dim\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
and
\dim\Hom(T_{\mathscr{F}},N_{\mathscr{F}})
separately and show that they match.

We are finally in position to prove our first main result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective scheme over an algebraically closed ground field
\kappa
and fix
H\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
ample. Let
\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
be the scheme parameterizing codimension one distributions (up to isomorphism) on
X
with tangent sheaves having Hilbert polynomial equal to
P
. Then there exists a polynomial
Q
depending on
P
and a morphism

\Sigma\colon\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)\longrightarrow{\rm Hilb}^{Q}(X),

which assigns to each distribution its singular scheme.

Proof.

We claim that there exists a natural transformation between the functors
\mathcal{D}ist^{P}_{X}
and
\mathcal{H}ilb^{Q}_{X}
. In fact, for each
S\in\mathfrak{Sch}_{\kappa}
, we have that an element of
\mathcal{D}ist^{P}_{X}(S)
defines an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}TX_{S}\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0

of coherent sheaves on
X\times S
, such that for each
s\in S
, the sequence

(2)

0\longrightarrow F_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}TX\longrightarrow N_{s}\longrightarrow 0

is a distribution on
X
and
F_{s}
has Hilbert polynomial (with respect to
H
) equal to
P
. As
F_{s}
is of codimension
1
we have that

N_{s}
is a rank one torsion free sheaf. As
X
is smooth,
N^{\vee\vee}
is locally free owing to
[
24
, Lemma 6.13]
. Moreover,
N^{\vee\vee}
must coincide with
\det(N)
whose formation commutes with base change
[
22
, p.39]
.

Since
N_{s}
is torsion free for every
s\in S
, due to Lemma
2.3
, we have an injection
N\hookrightarrow N^{\vee\vee}
hence we have

0\longrightarrow N\otimes N^{\vee}\longrightarrow N^{\vee\vee}\otimes N^{\vee}=\mathcal{O}_{X\times S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\longrightarrow 0

where
Z\subset X\times S
is flat over
S
, and
Z_{s}
is the singular scheme of the distribution defined by (
2
). This construction commutes with base change and thus defines a morphism
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)\longrightarrow{\rm Hilb}^{Q}(X)
, where
Q
is a polynomial depending on
P
.

∎

In the study of the morphism
\Sigma
it is useful to understand its fibers. Let
X
and
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{D}^{P}(X)
be as in Theorem
3.2
, and set
Z=\sing({\mathscr{F}})
. In addition, assume that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}\cdot H
where
H
is the ample generator; we then have that
Q(t)=P_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(t)-P_{TX}(t+c)+P(t+c)
, where
cH=c_{1}(TX)-c_{1}(T_{{\mathscr{F}}})
.

It then follows that any distribution on
X
singular at
Z
is defined by a twisted
1
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(c)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})
. In particular, the fiber of
\Sigma
over
Z
lies inside

\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(c)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})).

A priori, the singular scheme of
\omega
could be larger, even of codimension one. However, the locus of
1
-forms vanishing only on
Z
is a (nonempty) Zariski open set, thanks to the flattening stratification
[
26
, Chapter 8]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective scheme over an algebraically closed field
\kappa
. Assume further that
{\rm Pic}(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a codimension one distribution on
X
. Then the fiber

\Sigma^{-1}\left(\Sigma({\mathscr{F}})\right)\subset\mathbb{P}\!\left(H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(c)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})\right)

parameterizing distributions with singular scheme equal to
Z
is Zariski open.

Proof.

Let
S=\mathbb{P}\left(H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(c)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})\right)
and consider
\omega_{S}\in H^{0}\!\left(X_{S},\Omega^{1}_{X}(c)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)\right)
the “tautological”
1
-form - here
\boxtimes
means the external tensor product. Then we have the family
\mathcal{Z}
of subschemes defined by

TX(-c)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{S}(-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega_{S}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}}\longrightarrow 0

Note that for each point
s\in S
the fiber
\mathcal{Z}_{s}
is the singular scheme of
\omega_{s}=\omega_{S}|_{X_{s}}
, the restriction of
\omega_{S}
to the fiber over
s
. We may apply the flattening stratification to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}}
,
[
26
, Corollary, p.60]
.

It follows that the map
s\mapsto\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{s}}(t))
is locally constant. We denote its finite set of values, by
N
. Then each
f\in N
corresponds to a stratum
S_{f}
whose underlying set
|S_{f}|
is composed by the closed points
s\in S
such that
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}_{s}}(t))=f(t)
. Moreover the Zariski closure of
|S_{f}|
is contained in
\cup_{g\geq f}|S_{g}|
, where
f>g
if and only if

4.
Syzygies and Codimension one Distributions on
\mathbb{P}^{n}

The degree of a codimension one distribution
{\mathscr{F}}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is geometrically defined by the tangency between
{\mathscr{F}}
and a general line
L
. If a global section of
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(k)
defines
{\mathscr{F}}
then its restriction to
L
defines a degree
k-2
divisor (on
L
), called the tangency divisor. Thus the degree is defined by
d=k-2
, see
[
12
, section 2.5]
. In this particular case, the sequence (
1
) becomes

(3)

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+2)\longrightarrow 0.

Given the singular scheme
Z
of a degree
d
distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the distributions singular at
Z
are defined by twisted
1
-forms vanishing on
Z
. We will now show a correspondence between these forms and linear first syzygies of the homogeneous ideal
I(Z)
, provided that
Z
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree
\leq d
.

As a consequence of the classification obtained in
[
4
, Section 8]
, every codimension one distribution of degree 1 on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
satisfies
h^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}}(-2))=0
. Thus (
3
) implies that
h^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))=h^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}}(-2))=0
, i.e.,
Z=\sing({\mathscr{F}})
cannot be contained in a hypersurface of degree
\leq d
. We prove below that this condition is also verified for codimension one distributions on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
with zero-dimensional singular scheme, which we call generic.

Definition 4.1
.

A codimension one distribution on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is said to be
generic
if its singular scheme is either empty or a zero-dimensional subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
.

The previous definition is justified by the fact that codimension one distributions of degree
d
with zero-dimensional singular scheme form an open subset of
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)))
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Z
be a zero-dimensional subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. If
Z
is the singular scheme of a degree
d
codimension one distribution on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
then
Z
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree
\leq d
.

Proof.

Since there are no hypersurfaces of degree zero, the conclusion holds vacuously for
d\leq 0
; we may assume
d\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
be the ideal sheaf of
Z
. To prove that
Z
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree at most
d
we will show that
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))=\{0\}
.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
is the image of a morphism
\omega\colon{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-d-2)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, the hypothesis on the dimension implies that
Z
is locally a complete intersection, thus the associated Koszul complex is exact. Then we may use it to compute
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))
. Twisting the Koszul resolution by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
and splitting it into short exact sequences yields the following.

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}((d+1)(1-n))\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\bigwedge\limits^{n-1}{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-(n-1)(d+2)+d)\longrightarrow F_{n-2}\longrightarrow 0

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow F_{j}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\bigwedge\limits^{j}{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-j(d+2)+d)\longrightarrow F_{j-1}\longrightarrow 0

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-2)\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d)\longrightarrow 0

Due to Bott’s formulas
[
28
, p.4]
and Serre duality, we have that for
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
d\geq 1
,

H^{j-1}\!\left(\bigwedge^{j}{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-j(d+2)+d)\right)=\{0\}.

Then the long exact sequences of cohomology yields the following injections

H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))\hookrightarrow H^{1}(F_{1})\hookrightarrow\dots\hookrightarrow H^{n-2}(F_{n-2})\hookrightarrow H^{n-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}((d+1)(1-n)))=\{0\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The previous lemma has an alternative proof in the case
n=3
. Let

{\mathscr{F}}~:~0\longrightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+2)\longrightarrow 0

be a generic codimension one distribution of degree
d
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
; set
Z:=\sing({\mathscr{F}})
. Since
\dim Z=0
, we have that
{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}})=0
. Thus dualizing the exact sequence above, we obtain the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-d-2)\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}(d-2)\longrightarrow 0

where we used the identity
T_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}\simeq T_{\mathscr{F}}(d-2)
valid for rank 2 reflexive sheaves. Using these two sequences we have that
h^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))=h^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}}(-2))=0
, as desired.

Furthermore, we can also check that if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a codimension one distribution on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
whose tangent sheaf splits as a sum of line bundles, then
\sing({\mathscr{F}})
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree
d
. Indeed, if
T_{\mathscr{F}}
splits then
h^{1}(T_{\mathscr{F}}(-2))=0
.

Remark 4.4
.

This discussion leads to the following natural question: does there exist a degree
d
codimension one distribution
{\mathscr{F}}
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
such that
\sing({\mathscr{F}})
is contained in a hypersurface of degree
d
?

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a closed subscheme and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. Suppose that
Z
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree less than or equal to
d
. Then there exists a linear isomorphism between the spaces of degree
d+2
twisted
1
-forms singular at
Z
and linear first syzygies of the homogeneous ideal
I(Z)
.

Proof.

The space of degree
d+2
twisted
1
-forms singular at
Z
is
H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})
, where
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
is the ideal sheaf of
Z
. Consider the saturated homogeneous ideal is
I(Z)=\bigoplus_{j}H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(j))
. By hypothesis,
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(j))=\{0\}
for
j\leq d
hence the space of linear first syzygies of
I(Z)
is
{\rm Tor}^{S}_{1}(I(Z),\kappa)_{d+2}
, where
S=\kappa[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Consider the second exterior power of the Euler sequence twisted by
\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+2)
:

0\longrightarrow\Omega^{2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d)^{\oplus\binom{n+1}{2}}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z}\longrightarrow 0

which is exact since
{\rm Tor}_{1}^{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2),\mathcal{I}_{Z})=\{0\}
. From the long sequence of cohomology we have that

H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))^{\oplus\binom{n+1}{2}}\rightarrow H^{0}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(\Omega^{2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))^{\oplus\binom{n+1}{2}}

is exact. We have that
\phi
is injective, by hypothesis, and from
[
14
, Theorem 5.8]
the image of
\phi
is precisely
{\rm Tor}^{S}_{1}(I(Z),\kappa)_{d+2}
. Thus we have the desired isomorphism.
∎

Now we present this correspondence in an effective way. Fix
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Z}
. Suppose, as above, that
h^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))=0
. Also fix
\{G_{1},\dots,G_{r}\}
a (linear) basis of
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+1))
.

First let

\omega=F_{0}dx_{0}+\dots+F_{n}dx_{n}

be a degree
d+2
twisted
1
-form vanishing on
Z
, i.e.,
F_{0},\dots,F_{n}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+1))
. Then
F_{j}=\sum f_{ij}G_{j}
for
f_{ij}\in\kappa
. It follows that

0=\iota_{R}\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}F_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}f_{ij}\right)G_{j}

hence
\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}f_{i1},\dots,\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}f_{ir}\right)
provides a first syzygy for any set of generators of
I(Z)
containing
\{G_{1},\dots,G_{r}\}
.

Conversely, fix a minimal generating set
\{G_{1},\dots,G_{r},H_{1}\dots,H_{k}\}
for
I(Z)
such that
\deg H_{j}\geq d+2
for every
j
. Then a linear first syzygy for this set of generators can be written as
(L_{1},\dots L_{r},0,\dots,0)
for
L_{j}
linear homogeneous polynomials. Define

\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{r}G_{j}dL_{j}.

Note that
\iota_{R}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{r}G_{j}L_{j}=0
hence
\omega
defines a twisted
1
-form on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
that is clearly singular at
Z
.

Remark 4.6
.

The discussion above leads to an algorithm to compute the generic
1
-form
\omega
vanishing on a subscheme
Z
. It goes as follows: The input is the ideal
I(Z)
and the output is the generic
1
-form
\omega
singular at

.

•

Take

an ordered minimal generating set for

;

•

Compute

•

Define additional variables

;

•

Return

where

stands for the entrywise exterior derivative and the dots are matrix products.

To conclude this section we take a closer look at generic codimension one distributions. For

, such distributions are determined by their singular schemes, see

On the other hand, we argue that generic codimension one distributions of degree 1 on

Proof.

Let
\omega=\sum_{j=0}^{3}A_{j}dx_{j}
be a
1

. We claim that

is the Pfaffian of the matrix defined by

From

is arithmetically Gorenstein. Then it follows from

that

has a minimal resolution given by

where

is a free

and the resolution becomes

From the degrees in the resolution we can compute the degrees of the entries of the map

Now we prove the claim. Let

is the saturation of

. Since

We break it into short exact sequences that, with the appropriate identifications, are:

(4)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-3d-2)\longrightarrow\,

\displaystyle\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-2d)\longrightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}(-d-2)\longrightarrow 0,

(5)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow T_{\mathscr{F}}(-d-2)\longrightarrow\,

\displaystyle{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-d-2)\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}\longrightarrow 0.

From (

is precisely

. On the other hand (

Therefore

except for

where the latter has one more generator. We only need to produce this missing generator. Since

is the saturation of the ideal generated by

so that the Euler relation

To show that

be a generic codimension one distribution of degree

is determined by its singular scheme if

In order to proceed with our intentions, let us recall the classification of codimension one distributions
\mathscr{F}
of degree 1 on

obtained in

. Such distributions are given by exact sequences of the following form

and the classification was given in terms of the Chern classes of the tangent sheaf

5.1.
Twisted Cubic

Let
\mathcal{D}(0,0)
be the scheme parameterizing isomorphism classes of codimension one distributions
\mathscr{F}
of degree 1 on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that
(c_{2}(T_{\mathscr{F}}),c_{3}(T_{\mathscr{F}}))=(0,0)
; according to the classification outlined in Table
1
, we have that
T_{\mathscr{F}}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1)
. It follows from
[
4
, Corollary 8.9]
that
C:={\rm Sing}(\mathscr{F})
is an arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay curve (aCM, for short) of degree 3 and genus 0, that is,
C
is a, possibly degenerate, twisted cubic.

Let
H
be the irreducible component of
{\rm Hilb}^{3t+1}(\mathbb{P}^{3})
that contains twisted cubics. There exists a map
f\colon H\rightarrow\mathbb{G}(3,H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2)))
sending a curve to the net of quadrics that defines it. The image is the space of determinantal nets and
f
is an isomorphism on the aCM open subset, see
[
15
,
31
]
. On the other hand, one can easily list the possible irreducible components of an aCM degree three curve. The nonreduced ones have support consisting on one line or the union of two lines; such multiple structures are described in
[
27
]
. It follows that aCM degree three curves defined by a determinantal net of quadrics are projectively equivalent to one of the following:

(i)

I=(xz-y^{2},xw-yz,yw-z^{2})
a twisted cubic;

(ii)

I=(xz-y^{2},xw,yw)
a conic meeting a noncoplanar line;

(iii)

I=(xw,xy,yz)
three noncoplanar lines meeting twice;

(iv)

I=(xy,xz,yz)
three noncoplanar concurrent lines;

(v)

I=(x^{2},xz,yz)
a planar double line meeting a line not in its plane;

(vi)

I=(x^{2},xw,xz-yw)
a double line of genus -1 meeting another line;

(vii)

I=(x^{2}-yz,xz,z^{2})
a triple line in a quadratic cone;

(viii)

I=(x^{2},xy,y^{2})
a infinitesimal neighborhood of a line.

Next, we use the results in the previous section to describe
\mathcal{D}(0,0)
in terms of the morphism
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{D}(0,0)\rightarrow H
and compute its dimension. For each ideal

listed above, we compute a generic

-form vanishing at

, showing that the image of the morphism
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{D}(0,0)\rightarrow H
is precisely the open subset

consisting of non planar aCM curves apart from triple lines in case (viii). We then find that the space of linear first syzygies of the ideals in the list above have, in all cases, dimension equal to 2, thus, according to Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
4.5
, the fibers of
\Sigma
are open subsets of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We infer that they are of the form
D_{+}(h)
for some homogeneous polynomial

. As a consequence of the theorem on the dimension of the fibers, we conclude that
\mathcal{D}(0,0)
is irreducible and

\dim\mathcal{D}(0,0)=\dim H+1=13.

Moreover, one can apply Proposition

\displaystyle u\otimes y

\displaystyle\longmapsto(x\mapsto u(x)\otimes y)

see
[
2
, Chapter II §4.2]
. Now, given sheaves
F
and
G
on a variety
X
, the previous observation leads to the following morphism of sheaves

\eta\colon F^{\vee}\otimes G\longrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G)

which we call
Tensor-Hom relation
. The following technical lemma is useful in our proof of smoothness for
\mathcal{D}(2,2)
and
\mathcal{D}(1,1)
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold and let
F
and
G
be sheaves on
X
. If
F
is reflexive and
G
is torsion free then:

(i)

{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,G)\simeq{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes G
;

(ii)

\Tor_{1}(F,G)=0
;

(iii)

The morphism
\eta
is surjective and the induced cohomology map
H^{i}(F^{\vee}\otimes G)\rightarrow H^{i}({\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G))
is surjective for
i=0
and an isomorphism for
i\geq 1
;

(iv)

\eta
is an isomorphism if either
\sing(F)\cap\sing(G)=\emptyset
or
G
is reflexive;

(v)

if
H^{i}(F^{\vee}\otimes G)=0
for
i>0
, then
\Ext^{1}(F,G)=H^{0}({\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes G)
and
\Ext^{i}(F,G)=0
for
i>1
.

Proof.

Since
F
is reflexive, it admits a resolution of the form

(12)

0\longrightarrow L_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\longrightarrow}}L_{0}\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow 0,

where
L_{1}
and
L_{0}
are locally free sheaves. Applying the functor
\makebox[6.45831pt]{{$\cdot$}}\otimes G
to this sequence we get

0\longrightarrow\Tor_{1}(F,G)\longrightarrow L_{1}\otimes G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu\otimes\mathbf{1}_{G}}}{{\longrightarrow}}L_{0}\otimes G\longrightarrow F\otimes G\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
L_{0}\otimes G
is torsion free, while the support of
\Tor_{1}(F,G)
is contained in
\sing(F)\cap\sing(G)
. Then
\Tor_{1}(F,G)\subset L_{1}\otimes G
is only possible if
\Tor_{1}(F,G)=0
. This proves (ii).

Dualizing the sequence in display (
12
), we obtain

(13)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow F^{\vee}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle L_{0}^{\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu^{\vee}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K\longrightarrow 0,

(14)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow

\displaystyle L_{1}^{\vee}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X})\longrightarrow 0,

where
K:=\img\mu^{\vee}
. On the one hand, we apply the functor
\makebox[6.45831pt]{{$\cdot$}}\otimes G
to the sequence in display (
13
) and obtain

(15)

0\longrightarrow\Tor_{1}(K,G)\longrightarrow F^{\vee}\otimes G\longrightarrow L_{0}^{\vee}\otimes G\xrightarrow{\mu^{\vee}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{G}}K\otimes G\longrightarrow 0

On the other hand, the apply the functor
{\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(\makebox[6.45831pt]{{$\cdot$}},G)
to the sequence in display (
12
) to obtain

(16)

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G)\longrightarrow L_{0}^{\vee}\otimes G\xrightarrow{\mu^{\vee}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{G}}{L_{1}^{\vee}\otimes G}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,G)\longrightarrow 0.

As tensor product preserves cokernels, we get that
{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,G)\simeq{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes G
. This proves (i).

The sequence (
16
) also shows that
\ker\mu^{\vee}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{G}={\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G)
and the sequence in display (
15
) induces

0\longrightarrow\Tor_{1}(K,G)\longrightarrow F^{\vee}\otimes G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G)\longrightarrow 0.

In the same fashion, we apply the functor
\makebox[6.45831pt]{{$\cdot$}}\otimes G
to the sequence in display (
14
) to obtain

\Tor_{2}({\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X}),G)\simeq\Tor_{1}(K,G);

if
\sing(F)\cap\sing(G)=\emptyset
, then
\Tor_{2}({\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,\mathcal{O}_{X}),G)=0
hence
\eta
is an isomorphism. Alternatively, item (2) also implies that
\Tor_{1}(K,G)=0
when
G
is reflexive, since the sequence (
13
) implies that
K
is torsion free. This completes the proof of item (iv).

Moreover,
\Tor_{1}(K,G)
is a zero-dimensional sheaf whenever non trivial, so the induced cohomology map

H^{i}(F^{\vee}\otimes G)\longrightarrow H^{i}({\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G))

is surjective for
i=0
and an isomorphism for
i\geq 1
. This completes the proof of (iii).

Finally, the item (v) is an immediate consequence of the spectral sequence for local to global Ext’s. Indeed, if
h^{i}(F^{\vee}\otimes G)=0
for
i>0
, then
h^{i}({\mathcal{H}}{\it om}(F,G))=0
in the same range by item (iii). Since
\dim{\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{j}(F,G)=0
due to the fact that
F
is reflexive, the only nontrivial entries in the second page of the spectral sequence
E_{2}^{ij}=H^{i}({\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{j}(F,G))
occur for
(i,j)=(0,0),(0,1)
, thus, in addition, all differentials
d_{2}^{ij}
must vanish. It follows that
E_{2}^{ij}=H^{i}({\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{j}(F,G))
already converges in the second page, therefore
\Ext^{1}(F,G)=H^{0}({\mathcal{E}}{\it xt}^{1}(F,G))
and
\Ext^{k}(F,G)=0
for
k=2,3
.
∎

Next, we recall the following version of the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem, compare with
[
22
, Lemma 6.1.3]
:

(17)

\chi(E,F):=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim\Ext^{k}(E,F)=\int_{X}{\rm ch}(E)^{\vee}\cdot{\rm ch}(F)\cdot{\rm td}(X)

If we know that
\Ext^{k}(E,F)=0
for
k>1
(situation described in the Lemma
A.1
), then

(18)

\dim\Hom(E,F)=\chi(E,F)+\dim\Ext^{1}(E,F).

The calculation of
\dim\Ext^{1}(E,F)
in our examples will rely on the following technical fact from commutative algebra.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\kappa
be a field and let
R=\kappa[x,y,z]_{(x,y,z)}
be the local ring at the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{\kappa}
. Let
I\subset R
be an ideal, then

\dim_{\kappa}\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes_{R}I=\begin{cases}3,&\text{if $V(I)$ is the closed point;}\\
3,&\text{if $V(I)$ is a smooth rational curve with a simple embedded point;}\\
6,&\text{if $V(I)$ is a double point.}\end{cases}

Proof.

First assume that
I=(x,y,z)
the maximal ideal. Therefore
\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)=R/I
and

\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes_{R}I=\frac{(x,y,z)}{(x,y,z)^{2}}\simeq\kappa^{3}.

Next assume that
V(I)
is a smooth rational curve with a simple embedded point. Up to automorphism of
R
we can suppose that
I=(x,y)\cap(x^{2},y,z)=(x^{2},xz,y)
which gives the following resolution

0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}-y\\
-x\\
z\end{pmatrix}}R^{3}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}z&0&y\\
-x&y&0\\
0&-xz&-x^{2}\end{pmatrix}}R^{3}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}x^{2}&xz&y\end{pmatrix}}R\longrightarrow R/I\longrightarrow 0

Therefore
\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)
is determined by the leftmost map in this sequence, indeed it is the cokernel of its dual, i.e.,
\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)=R/(x,y,z)
. It follows that

\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes_{R}I=\frac{(x^{2},xz,y)}{(x^{2},xz,y)\cdot(x,y,z)}=\frac{(x^{2},xz,y)}{(x^{3},x^{2}z,x^{2}z,xy,y^{2},yz)}\simeq\kappa^{3}.

Now consider that
V(I)
is a double point. We will follow the same path. Up to automorphism,
I=(x,y,z)^{2}+(x,y)=(x,y,z^{2})
. Then we have the resolution

0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z^{2}\end{pmatrix}}R^{3}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}0&z^{2}&-y\\
-z^{2}&0&x\\
y&-x&0\end{pmatrix}}R^{3}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}x&y&z^{2}\end{pmatrix}}R\longrightarrow R/I\longrightarrow 0

and it follows that
\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)=R/I
. Therefore

\Ext^{3}_{R}(R/I,R)\otimes_{R}I=\frac{I}{I^{2}}=\frac{(x,y,z^{2})}{(x^{2},xy,xz^{2},y^{2},yz^{2},z^{4})}\simeq\kappa^{6}.

We can give a basis taking the classes of
\{x,y,z^{2},xz,yz,z^{3}\}
.
∎

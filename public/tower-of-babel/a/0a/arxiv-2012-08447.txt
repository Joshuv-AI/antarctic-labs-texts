Connections on Lie groupoids and Chern-Weil theory
Indranil Biswas, Saikat Chatterjee, Praphulla Koushik, Frank Neumann

Abstract
. Let 𝕏 = [ X 1 ⇉ X 0 ] \mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}] be a Lie groupoid equipped with a connection, given by a smooth distribution ℋ ⊂ T ​ X 1 \mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1} transversal to the fibers of the source map. Under the assumption that the distribution ℋ \mathcal{H} is integrable, we define a version of de Rham cohomology for the pair ( 𝕏 , ℋ ) (\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}) , and we study connections on principal G G -bundles over ( 𝕏 , ℋ ) (\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}) in terms of the associated Atiyah sequence of vector bundles. Introduction

The geometry of principal bundles over Lie groupoids and its applications in geometry and physics have been a
very active areas of research in recent years. In particular, the concepts of a connection on Lie groupoids and
connections on principal bundles over Lie groupoids as well as its geometric, analytic, and algebraic properties,
have been discussed by several authors in the
process of extending concepts of differential geometry from smooth manifolds to Lie
groupoids
[
LGTX2
,
BX
,
Be2
,
CM
,
CLW
,
Ta
,
Sa
,
DE
]
. An important
ingredient of any such framework is the construction of the associated Chern-Weil theory and a theory of
characteristic classes via de Rham cohomology. For example, Laurent-Gengoux, Tu and Xu
[
LGTX2
,
CM
]

study Chern-Weil theory for principal bundles on Lie groupoids via de Rham cohomology defined by using simplicial
manifolds associated to the Lie groupoid nerves (compare also
[
Du
,
FN
]
). Behrend in
[
Be2
]

investigates flat connections on Lie groupoids via the theory of integrable distributions and cofoliations and
studies the associated de Rham cohomology. Tang in
[
Ta
]
defines in a similar fashion flat connections
for Lie groupoids, which he called étalifications and investigates its applications in symplectic and Poisson
geometry and for deformation quantization (see also
[
Ha
]
). In
[
BX
]
, Behrend and Xu define
connections and curvature and the associated Chern-Weil theory and theory of characteristic classes in the general
setting of groupoid
S^{1}
-central extensions. More recently, Arias Abad and Crainic
[
AC
]
introduced more general
and flexible Ehresmann connections for any Lie groupoids. Trentinaglia
[
Tr
]
investigates and describes the
space of Cartan or multiplicative connections on proper Lie groupoids. Furthermore, Herrera and Ortiz
[
HO
]
are
currently developing aspects of the geometry of principal 2-bundles over Lie groupoids involving Atiyah
LA-groupoids. Furthermore, connections and “higher gauge transformations” (see
[
MP1
,
MP2
,
MP3
,
Ma-Mi
,
BCKMM
]
and the references
therein for recent developments in higher gauge theory) have been studied in terms of Atiyah sequences of
principal 2-bundles over Lie groupoids in
[
CCK
]
.
For a general discussion and recent results on the geometry of Lie groupoids, we refer to

[
dH
,
MM
,
Ma1
,
Ma2
]
.

Another important reason for the interest in the geometry of Lie groupoids is its association with the geometry
of differentiable stacks. Differentiable stacks have presentations by Lie groupoids, in fact, they are basically
Morita equivalence classes of Lie groupoids (see for example
[
He
,
LGSX
,
Me
,
CK
,
Ca
,
Gi
]
). Many concepts from the geometry of Lie groupoids can be extended to differentiable stacks via Morita
invariance as long as they respect the multiplicative groupoid structure.

The objective of this article is to provide a new and more flexible approach for connections on principal bundles
over Lie groupoids (with integrable connections) in terms of Atiyah sequences of vector bundles associated to
transversal tangential distribution. A main application is the development of the associated Chern-Weil theory
and a theory of characteristic classes for such principal Lie groupoid bundles. Our approach is directly inspired
by the classical work of Atiyah
[
At
]
about connections on fiber bundles in complex geometry. In the course
of this article, the necessary framework for de Rham cohomology on a Lie groupoid with integrable connection is
developed and studied. More precisely, given a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
equipped with an
integrable connection, defined by a distribution
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
which is transversal to the fibers of
the source map, we define a new version of de Rham cohomology for such a pair
(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
and study
general connections on principal
G
-bundles over
(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
in terms of the associated Atiyah sequence
of vector bundles. This approach allows then for a natural way to develop the corresponding Chern-Weil theory and
construction of characteristic classes for principal
G
-bundles over a given pair
(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
. We will
also discuss extensions of our constructions respecting the multiplicative structures to differentiable stacks,
namely to the special class of Deligne-Mumford stacks, which are presented by étale Lie groupoids. Étale Lie
groupoids always admit a connection. Special cases of Deligne-Mumford stacks also include orbifolds and foliated
spaces and have many applications in geometry and physics. We are currently exploring extensions of our constructions to more general differentiable stacks in related
work
[
BCKN1
]
(compare also with
[
BN
]
). Our approach presented here is also closely related to that of
Behrend on cofoliations for differentiable stacks
[
Be2
]
.

The main objective of this article is to study connections in terms of splittings of the Atiyah sequence and cohomologically via the corresponding Chern-Weil theory. In order to do so, we introduce connections on Lie groupoids and develop the corresponding de Rham cohomology. While
[
LGTX2
]
discusses the Chern-Weil theory and de Rham cohomology for principal bundles over Lie groupoids, their approach uses simplicial nerves of Lie groupoid, without involvement of any given Lie groupoid connections. In comparison, in our article a given Lie groupoid connection is a necessity to study the Atiyah sequence for a principal bundle over a Lie groupoid. For this, see in particular the discussion around Equation
3.15
, where we distinguish the two approaches with an illustrating example.
Nonetheless, a simplicial and stacky approach can be adopted as well in our framework, as explained in Subsection
3.3
. However, the observation is cursory and we have not pursued this approach here in more detail. On the other hand, Behrend
[
Be2
]
works with a given connection on a Lie groupoid, but their definition is slightly more strict than ours, and uses a particular de Rham cohomology theory associated to a flat Lie groupoid connection. The main purpose of their paper is to study the cofoliations of stacks with respect to flat Lie groupoid connections.

for some of the material

2.1.
Smooth spaces, Lie groupoids, and principal bundles

We will work over the category of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-manifolds and refer to it also as the
category
\mathfrak{S}
of smooth spaces and smooth maps
. All manifolds considered here will be second countable, but they are not necessarily Hausdorff spaces.
We do not impose the Hausdorff condition to ensure that certain interesting examples of non-Hausdorff groupoids, such as foliation groupoids, are not left out.

A
smooth space
or
smooth manifold
will mean an object in the category
\mathfrak{S}
. The tangent bundle of any
smooth space
X
will be denoted by
TX
. A
smooth map
will refer to a morphism in
\mathfrak{S}
, and by a
submersion we mean a smooth map whose differential restricted to
T_{x}X
is surjective for every point
x
of the
domain
X
. An
étale map
is a smooth immersion in
\mathfrak{S}
which is
also a submersion. An étale map corresponds, therefore,
to the notion of a local diffeomorphism.

For any smooth space
X
, its structure sheaf will be denoted by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. A
vector bundle on
X
will be identified with its sheaf of sections, which is a locally free
sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules. The cotangent bundle of
X
will be denoted by

\Omega^{1}(X)\,:=\,T^{*}X
, and the
p
-th exterior power of
T^{*}X
is

\Omega^{p}(X)\,:=\,\bigwedge^{p}T^{*}X
.

The
big étale site

{\mathfrak{S}}_{et}
on the category

\mathfrak{S}
is given by the following Grothendieck
topology on
\mathfrak{S}
: We call a family
\{U_{i}\longrightarrow X\}

of morphisms in
\mathfrak{S}
with target
X
a
covering
family
of
X
, if all smooth maps
U_{i}\longrightarrow X
are étale
and the total map from the disjoint union

\coprod_{i}U_{i}\,\longrightarrow\,X

is surjective. This
defines a pretopology on
\mathfrak{S}
generating a Grothendieck
topology, which is known as the
étale topology
on
\mathfrak{S}

(compare also
[
Vi
]
). If either of the two morphisms
U\rightarrow X
and
V\rightarrow X
in
\mathfrak{S}
is a submersion, then the fiber product exists.

Remark 2.1
.

As in
[
BCKN1
]
, we could as well here instead of working over the category of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
-manifolds consider
the category of complex analytic manifolds or the category of smooth schemes of finite type over the field of complex numbers.
We leave it to the reader to make the necessary amendments in the definitions and constructions presented below.

Definition 2.2

(Lie groupoid)
.

A
Lie groupoid

\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
is a groupoid
internal to the category
\mathfrak{S}
of smooth spaces, meaning the set
X_{1}
of
arrows and the set
X_{0}
of objects are objects of
\mathfrak{S}
(so they are smooth manifolds)
and all structure morphisms

s,\,t\,:\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}\,,\ \ m\,:\,X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},t}X_{1}\longrightarrow X_{1}\,,

i\,:\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{1}\,,\ \ e\,:\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{1}

are morphisms in
\mathfrak{S}
(so they are smooth maps). Here
s
is the source map,
t
is the target map,
m
is the multiplication map,
e
is the identity section, and
i
is the inversion map of the groupoid. The source map
s
is a submersion. Using
i
, this implies that the target map
t
is also a submersion. If
s
and
t
are étale, the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
is
called
étale
. If the
anchor map

(s,\,t)\,:\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}\times X_{0}

is
proper
, the groupoid
is called a
proper
Lie groupoid.

Definition 2.3

(Morphisms of Lie groupoids)
.

A
morphism
between Lie groupoids
{\mathbb{X}}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
and
{\mathbb{Y}}\,=\,[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}]
is a functor
\phi\,=\,(\phi_{1},\,\phi_{0})\,:\,{\mathbb{X}}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{Y}}
such that

\phi_{0}\,:\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,Y_{0}\,,\ \ \phi_{1}\,:\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,Y_{1}

are smooth maps
which commute with all structure morphisms of the groupoids. A
morphism
\phi\,:\,{\mathbb{X}}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{Y}}
of
Lie groupoids is a
Morita morphism
if

(i)

\phi_{0}\,:\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,Y_{0}
is a surjective submersion, and

(ii)

the diagram

\textstyle{X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(s,t)}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{1}}
\textstyle{X_{0}\times X_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{0}\times\phi_{0}}
\textstyle{Y_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(s,t)}
\textstyle{Y_{0}\times Y_{0}}

is a pullback diagram, that is,
X_{1}\,\cong\,Y_{1}\times_{Y_{0}\times Y_{0}}(X_{0}\times X_{0})
.

Two Lie groupoids
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
are
Morita
equivalent
, if there exists a third Lie groupoid
\mathbb{Z}
and Morita morphisms

{\mathbb{X}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longleftarrow}}\,{\mathbb{Z}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mathbb{Y}}\,.

Now we will recall the general notions of principal Lie groupoid bundles over smooth manifolds. First, we need to
define Lie groupoid actions.

Definition 2.4

(Action of a Lie groupoid on a manifold)
.

Let

\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid.
Let
P
be a smooth manifold.
A
left action of the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
on the manifold
P
is given by a
pair of smooth maps

a\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \mu\,\colon\,X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},a}P\,\longrightarrow\,P

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\mu(1_{a(p)},\,p)\,=\,p
for all
p\,\in\,P
,

(2)

a(\mu(\gamma,\,p))\,=\,t(\gamma)
for all
(\gamma,\,p)\,\in\,X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},a}P
, and

(3)

\mu(\gamma^{\prime},\,\mu(\gamma,\,p))\,=\,\mu((\gamma^{\prime}\circ\gamma),\,p)
for all

(\gamma^{\prime},\,\gamma,\,p)\,\in\,X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},t}X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},a}P
.

For convenience, the map
\mu
in Definition
2.4
will usually be denoted
simply by a dot “
\cdot
”. A
right action of the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
on the manifold
P
is defined likewise, with the action map
instead given by

\mu\,\colon\,P\times_{a,X_{0},t}X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,P\,.

Example 2.5
.

Consider a Lie group
G
as the Lie groupoid
[G\,\rightrightarrows\,*]
with singleton object set
*
and morphism set
G
. The actions of

[G\rightrightarrows*]
on a smooth manifold
P
are then precisely the smooth actions of the Lie group
G
on
P
.

Example 2.6
.

Given a smooth manifold
M
, we get a Lie groupoid
[M\,\rightrightarrows\,M]
, where both maps
s
and
t
are given by
the identity map of
M
. An action of
[M\rightrightarrows M]
on a
smooth manifold
P
is the same as a smooth map
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
.

Example 2.7
.

Suppose a Lie group
G
is acting on a smooth manifold
M
. Then we have a Lie groupoid
[M\times G\,\rightrightarrows\,M]
, the
action groupoid
, with
source and target maps
given by
(m,\,g)\,\longmapsto\,m
and
(m,\,g)\,\longmapsto\,m\cdot g

respectively. An action of
[M\times G\rightrightarrows M]
on a manifold
P
is then an action of the Lie group
G
on the manifold
P

together with a
G
-equivariant smooth map
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
.

Definition 2.8

(Principal
G
-bundle over a Lie groupoid)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid. A
right principal
G
-bundle
over
\mathbb{X}
is a (right) principal
G
-bundle
\pi\,\colon\,E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}

over the smooth manifold
X_{0}
together with a left action

(\pi:E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\ \mu\colon X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},\pi}E_{G}\rightarrow E_{G}),

of the Lie groupoid

\mathbb{X}
on the manifold
E_{G}
, such that
(\gamma\cdot p)\cdot g\,=\,\gamma\cdot(p\cdot g)
for all
(\gamma,\,p,\,g)\,\in\,X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}E_{G}\times G
.
A principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
will be denoted as
\bigl(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X}\bigr)
. A
morphism of right principal
G
-bundles
from
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
to
(E_{G}^{\prime}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
is
a morphism

\psi\,\colon\,(E_{G},\,\pi,\,X_{0})\,\longrightarrow\,(E_{G}^{\prime},\,\pi^{\prime},\,X_{0})

of the underlying
principal bundles over the
manifold
X_{0}
, such that

\psi(\mu(\gamma,\,e))\,=\,\mu^{\prime}(\gamma,\,\psi(e))

for all
(\gamma,\,e)\,\in\,X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},a}E_{G}
. Similarly, we also have the notion of a
left principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
and

morphisms of left principal
G
-bundles
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
G,\,H
be a pair of Lie groups. A principal
G
-bundle over the Lie groupoid
[H\rightrightarrows*]
is the same as a
left-action of
H
on
G
that commutes with the right-translation action of
G
on itself, meaning that

h\cdot(gg^{\prime})\,=\,(h\cdot g)g^{\prime}
for all
h\,\in\,H
and
g,\,g^{\prime}\,\in\,G
.

Example 2.10
.

Let
G
be a Lie group. Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[M\rightrightarrows M]
be the Lie groupoid associated to a smooth manifold
M
. Then, a principal

G
-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
is the same as a principal
G
-bundle
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
over the manifold
M
.

Example 2.11
.

Let
G,\,H
be a pair of Lie groups. Then an
H
-equivariant smooth principal
G
-bundle
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
over a smooth manifold
M
defines a principal
G
-bundle over the Lie groupoid
[M\times H\rightrightarrows M]
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
\pi\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
be a principal
G
-bundle over a manifold
M.
Consider the groupoid

[P\times P\rightrightarrows P]
with source and target
maps as first and second projections respectively and with the obvious multiplication. The Lie group

G
acts on
P\times P
by
(p_{1},\,p_{2})\cdot g\,=\,(p_{1}\cdot g,\,p_{2}\cdot g).
Under the quotient by the action of
G
, the
groupoid
[P\times P\rightrightarrows P]
descends to a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}:=\,[\frac{P\times P}{G}\rightrightarrows M\,].
This groupoid is often called the
gauge groupoid
or
Atiyah groupoid
in the literature. It plays an
important role in gauge theory and the theory of Lie algebroids (see
[
Lo
,
KoSt
]
).
We will show that
\pi\colon P\rightarrow M
is a
G
-bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm Gauge}
. To see this we first note that for any
\bigl([(p_{1},\,p_{2})],\,q\bigr)\,\in\,s^{*}{P}
we have a
g\in G
such that
q\cdot g=p_{1}.
Of course
g
depends on the choice of the representative element
(p_{1},\,p_{2})\,\in\,[(p_{1},\,p_{2})].

Now define a smooth map
\mu\colon s^{*}{P}\rightarrow P
by
\mu\bigl([(p_{1},p_{2})],q\bigr)=p_{2}\cdot g^{-1}
. Observe that the map is well defined for the following reason. Take any other representative element
(p_{1}\cdot g^{\prime},p_{2}\cdot g^{\prime})=(p^{\prime}_{1},p^{\prime}_{2}).
Then
q\cdot(gg^{\prime})=p^{\prime}_{1}
and thus
p^{\prime}_{2}\cdot(gg^{\prime})^{-1}\,=\,p_{2}\cdot(g^{\prime}g^{\prime-1}g^{-1})\,=\,p_{2}\cdot g^{-1}.
Moreover it is easily verified that

\mu([p_{1},\,p_{2}],\,q)g\,=\,\mu([p_{1},\,p_{2}],\,qg)
. Thus
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
is a
principal
G
-bundle over the Lie groupoid
{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm Gauge}.

Definition
2.8
also gives the definition of real and complex vector
bundles (of rank
k
) over a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
, which are basically principal
\mathrm{GL}(k,\mathbb{F})
-bundles over
\mathbb{X}
, where
\mathbb{F}
is the field of real or complex numbers.

Definition 2.13

(Vector bundle over a Lie groupoid)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid. A (rank
k
)
vector bundle over the Lie groupoid

\mathbb{X}
is a (rank
k
) vector bundle
\pi\,\colon\,E\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
over the manifold
X_{0}
together with a left action

\bigl(\pi\,:\,E\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0},\ \mu\,\colon\,X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},\pi}E\,\longrightarrow\,E\bigr)

of
\mathbb{X}
on
E
such that the map
\mu_{\gamma}\,\colon\,E_{s(\gamma)}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{t(\gamma)}
,
a\,\longmapsto\,\gamma\cdot a
, is linear for every
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
.

Morphisms of vector bundles over Lie groupoids are defined by imitating the definition of morphisms of principal bundles.

We also will use the following general notion of a principal Lie groupoid bundle (compare
with

BX
]
and
[
LGSX
]
).

Definition 2.14

(Principal
\mathbb{X}
-bundle over a manifold)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid. Let
M
be a smooth manifold. A

principal right
\mathbb{X}
-bundle over
M
is a surjective submersion
\pi\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
together with a right action

\bigl(a\colon P\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0},\ \mu\colon P\times_{a,X_{0},t}X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,P\bigr)

of the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
on the manifold
P
, such that following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\pi(p\cdot h)\,=\,\pi(p)
for all
(p,\,h)\,\in\,P\times_{X_{0}}X_{1}
, and

(2)

the map
P\times_{a,X_{0},t}X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,P\times_{\pi,M,\pi}P
given by

(p,\,h)\,\longmapsto\,(p,\,p\cdot h)
is a diffeomorphism.

Similarly, we also have the notion of a
principal left
\mathbb{X}
-bundle over
M
.

Let us present two important examples with more details.

Example 2.15
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a given Lie groupoid. The source map
s\,\colon\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}

defines a principal

\mathbb{X}
-bundle over
X_{0}.
Similarly, the target map
t\,\colon\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
along with the composition

X_{1}\times_{s,X_{0},t}X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{1}
defines an action of
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
on the manifold

X_{1}.

Example 2.16
.

Given a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
, a principal
\mathbb{X}
-bundle
\pi\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
and a map of smooth manifolds
f\,\colon\,N\,\longrightarrow\,M,
we have the obvious
notion of a pullback
\mathbb{X}
-bundle
f^{*}P\,\longrightarrow\,N
. The action of the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
on
f^{*}(P)
is given by
{\widetilde{a}}\,=\,a\circ{\rm pr}_{1}
and
((p,\,n),\,\gamma)\,\longmapsto\,(\mu(p,\,\gamma),\,n),
where
(\mu,\,a)

is the original action of
\mathbb{X}
on
P
.

Definition 2.18

(Fibered category)
.

A functor
\pi_{\mathscr{X}}\,:\,\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{C}
is a

fibered category
over
\mathcal{C}
if for every

(\eta,f)\,\in\,\mathscr{X}_{0}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{0},t}\mathcal{C}_{1}
there exists a Cartesian arrow
\theta\,\colon\,\xi\,\longrightarrow\,\eta
with
\pi_{\mathscr{X}}(\theta)\,=\,f
. We call such
\xi
to be a
pullback of
\eta
along
f
.

A
morphism of fibered categories over
\mathcal{C}
from
(\mathscr{X},\,\pi_{\mathscr{X}},\,\mathcal{C})

to
(\mathscr{Y},\,\pi_{\mathscr{Y}},\,\mathcal{C})
is a functor
F\,\colon\,\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathscr{Y}
that maps a
Cartesian arrow in
\mathscr{X}
to a Cartesian arrow in
\mathscr{Y}
satisfying
\pi_{\mathscr{Y}}\circ F\,=\,\pi_{\mathscr{X}}
.

Example 2.19
.

Given a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
, consider the category
{\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{X}
of principal
\mathbb{X}
-bundles.
The functor
\pi_{\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}}\,:\,\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
that sends a
principal
\mathbb{X}
-bundle
P\,\longrightarrow\,M

to the smooth manifold
M
is a fibered category over
\mathfrak{S}
.

Example 2.20
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
{\underline{M}}
the comma category. The functor

{\underline{M}}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
which sends an object
N\,\longrightarrow\,M
in
{\underline{M}}

to the object
N
in
\mathfrak{S}
is a fibered category over
\mathfrak{S}
.

Let
\pi_{\mathscr{X}}\,:\,\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{C}
be a fibered category. To an object
U
of
\mathcal{C}
we
associate a category
\mathscr{X}(U)
with

\displaystyle\text{Obj}(\mathscr{X}(U))\,=\,\{\eta\in\text{Obj}(\mathscr{X})\,\mid\,\pi_{\mathscr{X}}(\eta)=U\},

\displaystyle\text{Mor}_{\mathscr{X}(U)}(\eta,\,\eta^{\prime})\,=\,\{f\in\text{Mor}(\mathscr{X})\,\mid\,\pi_{\mathscr{X}}(f)\,=\,1_{U}\}\,.

The category
\mathscr{X}(U)
is called the
fiber of
U
. In this fashion we obtain a pseudo-functor
associated to
\pi_{\mathscr{X}}\,:\,\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{C}
,

\mathscr{X}\,:\,\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\,\longrightarrow\,\rm{Cat}

that sends
U
to the category
\mathscr{X}(U)
. It should be clarified that by abuse of notation this pseudo-functor
will be also denoted by
\mathscr{X}.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category with a specified Grothendieck topology. We refer to such a category as a
site

(compare
[
Vi
]
). In particular, by the site
{\mathfrak{S}}
we mean the big étale site
\mathfrak{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
as
defined before.

Let
\pi_{\mathscr{X}}\,:\,\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{C}
be a fibered category over a site
\mathcal{C}
. For the ease of
exposition we will also simplify our notation here:
U_{ij}
will denote the fiber product
U_{i}\times_{U}U_{j}
in

\mathcal{C}
and so on, while
{\rm pr}_{i},\,{\rm pr}_{ij}
etc. will denote various projection maps obtained from the
pullback diagrams; for an arrow
f\,:\,U\,\longrightarrow\,V
in
\mathcal{C}
the functor

{\mathscr{X}}(f)\,:\,\mathscr{X}(V)\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{X}}(U)
will be denoted by
f^{*}
.

Let
(\mathscr{X},\,\pi_{\mathscr{X}},\,\mathcal{C})
be a fibered category over a site
{\mathcal{C}}
, and let

\{\sigma_{i}\,\colon\,U_{i}\,\longrightarrow\,U\}
be a covering of an object
U
in
\mathcal{C}
.
To the collection
\{U_{i}\,\longrightarrow\,U\}
we associate the following
descent category

\mathscr{X}(\{U_{i}\rightarrow U\})
. An object in
\mathscr{X}(\{U_{i}\rightarrow U\})
is a family of
pairs
(\{\xi_{i}\},\,\{\phi_{ij}\})
, where each
\xi_{i}
is an object of
\mathscr{X}(U_{i})
and each

\phi_{ij}\,\colon\,\rm{pr}_{2}^{*}(\xi_{j})\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}(\xi_{i})
is an isomorphism in

\mathscr{X}(U_{ij})
satisfying the cocycle relation
{\rm pr}_{13}^{*}(\phi_{ik})\,=\,{\rm pr}_{12}^{*}(\phi_{ij})\circ{\rm pr}_{23}^{*}(\phi_{jk})
in
\mathscr{X}(U_{ijk})
. An arrow

\big(\{\xi_{i}\},\,\{\phi_{ij}\})\,\longrightarrow\,(\{\eta_{i}\},\,\{\psi_{ij}\}\big)

is a collection
\{\alpha_{i}\,\colon\,\xi_{i}\,\longrightarrow\,\eta_{i}\}
satisfying

\psi_{ij}\circ{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\alpha_{j}\,=\,{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\alpha_{i}\circ\phi_{ij}
for every pair of indices
i,\,j
.
Here, for any morphism
\sigma_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow U
in
\mathcal{C}
, we have the associated functor
\sigma_{i}^{*}:\mathscr{X}(U)\rightarrow\mathscr{X}(U_{i})
. So, for the object
\xi
in
\mathscr{X}(U)
, we have the object
\sigma_{i}^{*}(\xi)
in
\mathscr{X}(U_{i})
. Moreover we get a functor

\mathscr{X}(U)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathscr{X}(\{U_{i}\rightarrow U\})

(2.2)

that sends an object
\xi
in
\mathscr{X}(U)
to the object
\{\sigma_{i}^{*}(\xi),\,\phi_{ij}\}
, where

\phi_{ij}\,\colon\,{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}(\sigma_{j}^{*}(\xi))\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}(\sigma_{i}^{*}(\xi))

are the isomorphisms
given by the universal property of a pullback.

Definition 2.21

(Stack)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a site. A fibered category
(\mathscr{X},\,\pi_{\mathscr{X}},\,\mathcal{C})
is said to be
a
stack (over the site

\mathcal{C})
if for each object
U
of
\mathcal{C}
, and a covering

\{U_{\alpha}\,\longrightarrow\,U\}
of
U
, the functor defined in (
2.2
) is an
equivalence of categories. A
morphism
of stacks is a morphism of the underlying fibered categories.

We are mainly interested here in
categories fibered in groupoids
; that is, a fibered category
\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{C}
such that each fiber
\mathscr{X}(U)
is a groupoid. Similarly,
stack
will mean here that the underlying fibered category is a category fibered in groupoids.

The following standard and useful examples will be referred to time and again.

Example 2.22
.

The fibered category
{\underline{M}}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
associated to the manifold
M
in
Example
2.20
is a stack over the
site
\mathfrak{S}
with respect to the étale topology. Here and onwards, we denote this stack simply by
M
.

Example 2.23
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid. Then the
fibered category
\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
in Example
2.19
is a stack over the
site
\mathfrak{S}
with respect to the étale topology. This is also known as the

classifying stack
associated to the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
.

Now we can introduce the main class of stacks, which are important for geometry and analysis, generalizing smooth manifolds.

Definition 2.24

(Differentiable stack)
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
be the big étale site.
A stack
\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
is called a
differentiable stack
if there exists
a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
such that there is an isomorphism of stacks
\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}\,\cong\,\mathscr{X}
.

The classifying stack
\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
of a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
and the stack
{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}

associated to a smooth manifold
M
are both examples of differentiable stacks over the site
\mathfrak{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
.
It is a known fact that a pair of Morita equivalent Lie groupoids yields a pair of isomorphic stacks (see

[
BX
]
,
[
BCKN1
]
).

A useful equivalent definition of a differentiable stack is given by using the notion of a ‘presentation’ or ‘atlas’
(see
[
BX
]
,
[
He
]
and
[
BCKN1
]
).

Definition 2.25

(Representable surjective submersion)
.

A morphism of
stacks
F\,:\,\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{Y}}
over
\mathfrak{S}
is called a
representable surjective submersion

if for every smooth manifold
U
and every morphism of stacks
U\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{Y}}
, the following two conditions hold:

(1)

the
2
-fiber product of stacks
\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{Y}}U
is representable, i.e., isomorphic to a smooth manifold, and

(2)

the induced morphism of smooth manifolds
\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{Y}}U\,\longrightarrow\,U
is a surjective submersion.

A stack
\mathscr{X}
over
\mathfrak{S}
is
differentiable

if there exists an object
X
of
\mathfrak{S}
together with a
representable surjective submersion
x\,:\,X\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{X}}
(see
[
BX
]
,
[
He
]
).
The map of stacks
x\,:\,X\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{X}}
is called an
atlas
or

presentation
for
\mathscr{X}
.

Example 2.26
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid. Let
t\,\colon\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
be the associated principal

\mathbb{X}
-bundle, as in Example
2.15
. Then we have a functor
x_{0}\colon X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{X}
which sends an object
f\colon N\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
to the pullback principal
\mathbb{X}
-bundle

f^{*}X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,N
. This functor in fact defines an atlas.

Conversely given an atlas
x\,:\,X\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{X}}
for the differentiable stack
\mathscr{X},
we can
produce a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X\times_{\mathscr{X}}X\rightrightarrows X].
Then we have an isomorphism
\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}\,\cong\,\mathscr{X}.
If
y\,:\,Y\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{X}}
is any other atlas for
\mathscr{X}
, then
\mathbb{X}
and

\mathbb{Y}\,=\,[Y\times_{\mathscr{X}}Y\rightrightarrows Y]
are Morita equivalent Lie groupoids.

Definition 2.27

(Deligne-Mumford stack)
.

A differentiable stack
\mathscr{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{S}
will be called a
(proper) Deligne-Mumford stack
if there exists a (proper) étale presentation
x\,:\,X\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathscr{X}}.

Example 2.28
.

Proper Deligne-Mumford stacks correspond to smooth orbifolds and are presented by proper
étale Lie groupoids (see
[
Le
]
).

Note that by the second part of Example
2.26
for any Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathscr{X}
there exists
an étale Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X\times_{\mathcal{D}}X\rightrightarrows X]
such that
\mathscr{B}\mathbb{X}\,\cong\,\mathscr{X}.
However the property of being étale is not preserved under Morita equivalence. Thus there may exist a non-étale Lie groupoid presenting the same Deligne-Mumford stack.

3.
de Rham cohomology for Lie groupoids and differentiable stacks

In this section, we study connections and de Rham cohomology for Lie groupoids and Deligne-Mumford stacks. The existence of an
integrable connection will hereby play an important role.

3.1.
Connections and integrable connections on Lie groupoids

In this section, we will define the notion of an (integrable) connection
\mathcal{H}
on a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
and introduce the de Rham
cohomology ring of the pair
(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
. We begin by recalling the Atiyah sequence in the classical set-up for smooth manifolds.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
G
a Lie group and
\pi\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
a principal
G
-bundle. The Lie algebra of
G

will be denoted by
\mathfrak{g}
.
Consider the diagonal action of
G
on
P\times{\mathfrak{g}}
induced by the adjoint action of
G
on

\mathfrak{g}
. Then the quotient

\text{ad}(P)\,:=\,(P\times\mathfrak{g})/G\,\longrightarrow\,P/G\,=\,M

becomes a vector bundle over
M
; it is known as the
adjoint bundle
of
P
.
On the other hand, the action of
G
on
P
induces an action of
G
on the tangent bundle
TP
of
P
. The quotient

\text{At}(P)\,:=\,(TP)/G\,\longrightarrow\,P/G\,=\,M

is a vector bundle, which is known as the
Atiyah bundle
(see
[
At
]
). The action of
G
on
P
identifies
the trivial bundle
P\times\mathfrak{g}\,\longrightarrow\,P
with the relative tangent bundle for the projection
\pi
,
and hence
\text{ad}(P)
is a subbundle of
\text{At}(P)
. Consequently, we
obtain a short exact sequence of vector bundles on
M

0\,\longrightarrow\,\text{ad}(P)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j^{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\text{At}(P)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,TM\,\longrightarrow\,0

(3.1)

which is called the
Atiyah sequence
associated to the principal
G
-bundle
P
(see
[
At
]
). Note that the
projection
d\pi
in (
3.1
) gives the quotient by
G
of the
differential
d\pi\,:\,TP\,\longrightarrow\,\pi^{*}TM
of the map
\pi
.

A
connection
on a principal
G
-bundle
P
is then defined to be a splitting of the Atiyah sequence. In other
words, a connection on
P
is a homomorphism

D\,:\,TM\,\longrightarrow\,\text{At}(P)

such that
(d\pi)\circ D\,=\,\text{Id}_{TM}
, where
d\pi
is the homomorphism in
(
3.1
).

Now consider a principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\,\rightarrow\,X_{0},\,\,[X_{1}\,\rightrightarrows\,X_{0}])
over a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\,\rightrightarrows\,X_{0}]
. The
underlying (classical) principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
yields the Atiyah sequence of vector bundles over
X_{0}
,

0\,\longrightarrow\,\text{ad}(E_{G})\,\xrightarrow{j^{P}}\,\text{At}(E_{G})\,\xrightarrow{d\pi}\,TX_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,0

(3.2)

(see (
3.1
)).
Our aim is to interpret (
3.2
) as an exact sequence of vector bundles over the Lie
groupoid
\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
. For that we need an action of the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
on the tangent space manifold
TX_{0}
. We already
have the tangent bundle projection map
TX_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
. It remains to provide the smooth map

\mu\,\colon\,X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}TX_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,TX_{0}

(3.3)

satisfying the conditions in Definition
2.4
. As
TX_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
is surjective, (by condition (2) in Definition
2.4
) the map
\mu

in (
3.3
) should produce a linear
map
T_{s(\gamma)}X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,T_{t(\gamma)}X_{0}
for every
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
. Precisely for this
purpose we introduce below the notion of a connection on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid. Let
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
be a distribution on the
manifold
X_{1}
which is a complement of the kernel of the differential

ds\,\colon\,TX_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,TX_{0}

of the source map
s
; so

\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\oplus\ker(ds)_{\gamma}\,=\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}

(3.4)

for every
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
. Let

P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}\,:\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\,\subset\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}

(3.5)

be the projection constructed using the decomposition in (
3.4
). For any
v\,\in\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}
,

P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v)\,\in\,\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}

is called the
horizontal component
of
v
.

Since
s
is a submersion, the decomposition in (
3.4
)
yields an isomorphism
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,T_{s(\gamma)}X_{0}
. In fact,
we have an isomorphism of vector bundles

\mathcal{H}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s^{*}TX_{0}

over
X_{1}
. The following compositions of homomorphisms

s^{*}TX_{0}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\mathcal{H}\,\hookrightarrow\,TX_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle dt}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,t^{*}TX_{0}

(3.6)

over
X_{1}
will be denoted by
\theta
, where
dt\,:\,TX_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,t^{*}TX_{0}
is the differential of the map
t
.

We now recall the definition of a connection on Lie groupoids from
[
BN
, Def. 3.1]
(see also

[
BCKN1
, 4.1.]
)

Definition 3.1

(Connection on a Lie groupoid)
.

A
connection

\mathcal{H}
on a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
is a distribution
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}

satisfying the following three conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{H}
is a complement of kernel of
ds
,

(2)

(de)_{x}(T_{x}X_{0})\,=\,\mathcal{H}_{e(x)}X_{1}
for all
x\,\in\,X_{0}
, and

(3)

\theta_{\gamma}\circ\theta_{\gamma^{\prime}}\,=\theta_{\gamma\circ\gamma^{\prime}}
for every composable
pair
\gamma,\,\gamma^{\prime}\,\in\,X_{1}
, where
\theta
is the homomorphism in (
3.6
).

A connection
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
is said to be
integrable

(or
flat
) if the distribution
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
is integrable.

Remark 3.2
.

Behrend
[
Be2
]
defines a connection on a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]

as a subbundle
E\,\subset\,TX_{1}
such that
[E\rightrightarrows TX_{0}]
is a subgroupoid of the tangent groupoid
[TX_{1}\rightrightarrows TX_{0}]
,
which is integrable (or flat) if
E\subset TX_{1}
is an integrable distribution. Tang
[
Ta
]
called these integrable
connections also étalifications and studies them in the context of deformation quantization. These distributions
E\,\ \subset TX_{1}
are also referred to in the literature as multiplicative distributions
[
DE
]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
be a connection on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. Consider a
principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\ \mathbb{X})
over
\mathbb{X}
. Then

{{\rm pr}_{1}}\,:\,s^{*}E_{G}\,=\,X_{1}\times_{s}E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{1}

is a principal
G
-bundle over

X_{1}
. We get an associated Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
whose source map

s^{*}E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{G}
is the second projection
(a,\,\gamma)\,\longmapsto\,a
and
the target map
s^{*}E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{G}
is the action
(a,\,\gamma)\,\longmapsto\,a\cdot\gamma

(the map
\mu
in Definition
2.8
).
Let

d{\rm pr}_{1}\,\colon\,Ts^{*}E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,TX_{1}

(3.7)

be the differential of the projection
{\rm pr}_{1}\,:\,s^{*}E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{1}
,

(a,\,\gamma)\,\longmapsto\,\gamma
.

The following lemma is then evident.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
be a connection on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
.
Then

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,:=\,({d\rm pr}_{1})^{-1}(\mathcal{H})\,\subset\,Ts^{*}E_{G}

is a connection on the Lie
groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
, where
{d\rm pr}_{1}
is the map in (
3.7
).
If the connection
\mathcal{H}
is integrable, then so is
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
.

The connection
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
in Lemma
3.3
will be called
the
pullback
of
\mathcal{H}
. For the purpose of future reference we define here the
map
\widetilde{\theta}
(see (
3.6
)) to be given by the following compositions of homomorphisms

\begin{split}\widetilde{\theta}:\,\,&{{\rm pr}_{2}}^{*}TE_{G}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,\hookrightarrow\,Ts^{*}E_{G}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d\,\mu}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{\mu}^{*}TE_{G},\\
&\qquad w\,\longmapsto\,{\widetilde{\theta}}_{\gamma,a}(w)\,=\,{d\mu}_{(\gamma,a)}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v),\,w)\end{split}

(3.8)

for any
v\in T_{\gamma}X_{1},\,w\,\in\,T_{a}E_{G}
satisfying
ds(\gamma,\,v)\,=\,d\pi(a,\,w)
(in other words, for
any
(v,\,w)\,\in\,T_{(\gamma,a)}s^{*}E_{G}
).
It is immediate that the map
\widetilde{\theta}
is well defined since
P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}
is actually trivial on
{\ker}(ds).

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=[M\rightrightarrows M]
be the Lie groupoid associated to a smooth manifold
M
. Then
\ker(ds)\,=\,\{0\}
and
the map
m\,\longmapsto\,\mathcal{H}_{m}M\,:=\,T_{m}M
,
m\,\in\,M
, defines an integrable connection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\subset TM
on
\mathbb{X}
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold equipped with an action of a Lie group
G
.
Let
\mathbb{X}=[M\times G\rightrightarrows M]
be the associated action Lie groupoid. As the source map

s\,\colon\,M\times G\,\longrightarrow\,M
is the projection map,
the differential
(ds)_{(m,g)}\,\colon\,T_{(m,g)}(M\times G)\,\longrightarrow\,T_{m}M
is given by

(v,\,A)\,\longmapsto\,v
for all
v\,\in\,T_{m}M,\ A\,\in\,T_{g}G
. Thus,

\ker((ds)_{(m,g)})\,=\,\{(v,\,A)\,\in\,T_{m}M\times T_{g}G\,\mid\,v\,=\,0\}\,=\,T_{g}G

for each
(m,\,g)\,\in\,M\times G
. Now defining
\mathcal{H}_{(m,g)}(M\times G)\,=\,T_{m}M\,\subset\,T_{(m,g)}(M\times G)
, we obtain a connection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\,\subset\,T(M\times G)
on the action groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. This
connection is clearly integrable.

Example 3.6
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be an étale Lie groupoid, meaning the map
s\,\colon\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
is a local
diffeomorphism. Then the differential
ds\,\colon\,TX_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,s^{*}TX_{0}
is an
isomorphism and the distribution
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}=\,TX_{1}
is a connection on the Lie
groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. This connection is again integrable.

Example 3.7
.

Let
\pi\,\colon\,E\,\longrightarrow\,M
be a finite rank vector bundle over the manifold
M.
We can view

\mathbb{X}=[E\rightrightarrows M]
as a groupoid with both the source and target maps both being
\pi
(thus, a pair of composable morphisms will belong to the same fiber), and composition is simply the addition of vectors on the corresponding fiber. Then a connection
on the vector bundle
\pi\,\colon\,E\,\longrightarrow\,M
defines a connection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}
on
\mathbb{X}
by smoothly splitting

E
into the horizontal component complementing
\ker{d\pi}.
Obviously, if one connection is integrable, then so is the other.

Example 3.8
.

Let
\pi\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
be a principal
G
-bundle over a manifold
M
and
\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}:=\,[\frac{P\times P}{G}\rightrightarrows M\,]
the gauge groupoid of Example
2.12
.
Let
A
be a connection on the
principal bundle
\pi\,\colon\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
. The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}\subset TP
defined by
the connection
A

induces a connection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}}
on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}
as follows. Define

{\mathcal{H}}_{[p,q]}\,:=\,\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\oplus T_{q}P}{T_{p,q}\,{(p,q)\cdot G}}\,\subset\,T_{[p,q]}\frac{P\times P}{G}\,,

where

(p,\,q)\cdot G\,\subset\,P\times P
is the orbit of the element
(p,\,q)\,\in\,P\times P.
Now since
\mathcal{H}
complements

\ker{d\pi}
and it is
G
-invariant,
{\mathcal{H}}_{[p,q]}
defines a connection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}}
on
\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}.

If the connection
A
is integrable, then so is the induced connection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}}
on the gauge groupoid
\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}.

We refer to
[
Be2
, Section 2.3]
and
[
Ta
, Section 2.1.1]
for other interesting examples of connections on Lie groupoids.

3.2.
Differential forms and de Rham cohomology of a pair

(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{H})

Let us fix a pair
(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{H})
consisting of a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
together with an existing connection

\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
on
\mathbb{X}
. We recall from
[
BN
]
the notion of a
differential
k
-form on
\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 3.9

(Horizontal component of a differential form)
.

Let
\varphi\,:\,X_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,\bigwedge^{k}T^{*}X_{1}
be a differential
k
-form on the manifold
X_{1}
. The

horizontal component
of
\varphi
(with respect to the connection
\mathcal{H}
) is the differential

k
-form

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(\varphi)(\gamma)(v_{1},\,\cdots,\,v_{k})\,=\,\varphi(\gamma)(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1}),\,\cdots,\,P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{k})),

for all
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
and
v_{i}\,\in\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}
,
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,k
, where

P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}
is the projection in (
3.5
).

Definition 3.10

(Differential forms on a Lie groupoid)
.

A differential
k
-form
\varphi\,\colon\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,\bigwedge^{k}T^{*}X_{0}
on the manifold
X_{0}
is said
to be a
differential
k
-form
on
\mathbb{X}
(with respect to the connection
\mathcal{H}
) if

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(s^{*}\varphi)\,=\,\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(t^{*}\varphi)\,,

where
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
is constructed as in Definition
3.9
.
We will denote the vector space of differential
k
-forms of the pair
(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
by

\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
.

The following lemma, which was stated in
[
BN
]
without proof, shows that differential forms on a Lie
groupoid with connection are closed with respect to exterior derivation under the assumption that the connection on
the Lie groupoid is integrable.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
be an integrable connection on a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
.
Let
\varphi\,:\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,\bigwedge^{k}T^{*}X_{0}
be a differential
k
-form on
\mathbb{X}
.
Then
d\varphi\,:\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,\bigwedge^{k+1}T^{*}X_{0}
is a differential
(k+1)
-form on
\mathbb{X}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the case
k\,=\,1
. Indeed, for an arbitrary
k
, the proof runs almost verbatim.

Let
\varphi
be a differential
1
-form on
\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
. This means that we have

(s^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v))\,=\,(t^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v))

for all
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
and
v\,\in\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}
.
Hence we get

(s^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(v)\,=\,(t^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(v)

(3.9)

for all
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
and
v\,\in\,\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}
.

To prove the lemma we need to show that

d(s^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1}),\,P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2}))\,=\,d(t^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1}),\,P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2}))

(3.10)

for all
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
and
v_{1},\,v_{2}\,\in\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}
.

Let
v_{1},\,v_{2}\,\in\,\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}
. Let
Z_{1}
(respectively,
Z_{2}
) be a section of
\mathcal{H}
defined
around
\gamma
such that
Z_{1}(\gamma)\,=\,v_{1}
(respectively,
Z_{2}(\gamma)\,=\,v_{2}
). We have

\displaystyle d(s^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(Z_{1},\,Z_{2})\,=\,Z_{1}((s^{*}\varphi)(Z_{2}))-Z_{2}((s^{*}\varphi)(Z_{1}))-(s^{*}\varphi)([Z_{1},\,Z_{2}])

\displaystyle d(t^{*}\varphi)(\gamma)(Z_{1},\,Z_{2})\,=\,Z_{1}((t^{*}\varphi)(Z_{2}))-Z_{2}((t^{*}\varphi)(Z_{1}))-(t^{*}\varphi)([Z_{1},\,Z_{2}])

Now, since
[Z_{1},\,Z_{2}]
is a section of
\mathcal{H}
, using this and (
3.9
) we conclude that
(
3.10
) holds.
∎

Now let us introduce morphisms of Lie groupoids with connections.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\bigl(\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)
and
\bigl(\mathbb{Y}\,=\,[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)

be a pair of Lie groupoids equipped with connections. A morphism of Lie groupoids

(F,\,f)\,\colon\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]\,\longrightarrow\,[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}]
will be called
a
morphism of Lie groupoids with connections
if

dF(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}})\,=\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\,,

where
dF
is the differential of the map
F
. We shall employ the notation

(F,\,f)\,\colon\,\bigl([X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)\,\longrightarrow\,\bigl([Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)

for such a morphism.

The following lemma is a straight-forward consequence of the definition.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(F,\,f)\,\colon\,\bigl(\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)\,\longrightarrow\,\bigl(\mathbb{Y}=[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)
be a morphism of Lie groupoids with connections.
Let
\varphi\,:\,Y_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,\bigwedge^{k}T^{*}Y_{0}
be a differential form on the Lie groupoid

\mathbb{Y}
. Then
f^{*}\varphi\,:\,X_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,\bigwedge^{k}T^{*}X_{0}

is a differential form on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
.

When the morphism
F
in Lemma
3.13
is a surjective submersion,
the following converse also holds.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
(F,\,f)\,\colon\,\bigl(\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)\,\longrightarrow\,\bigl(\mathbb{Y}=[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)

be a morphism of Lie groupoids with connection such that
F
is a surjective submersion. Let
\varphi

be a differential
k
-form on
Y_{0}
such that
f^{*}\varphi
is a differential
k
-form on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
.
Then
\varphi
is a differential form on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{Y}
.

Proof.

Since
F
is a surjective submersion, for any two differential
k
-forms
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
on
Y_{1}
,
if
F^{*}\omega_{1}\,=\,F^{*}\omega_{2}
, then
\omega_{1}\,=\,\omega_{2}
. The lemma now follows from this.
∎

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
now be a Lie groupoid equipped with an integrable connection
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
. Let
{\Omega}^{k}(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{H})
denote the vector space of differential
k
-forms on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
,
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
. In view of Lemma
3.11

we have a cochain complex
\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
given by

\cdots\,\longrightarrow\,{\Omega}^{k-1}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,\longrightarrow\,{\Omega}^{k}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,\longrightarrow\,{\Omega}^{k+1}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,\longrightarrow\,\cdots.

The
k^{th}
-cohomology group of this cochain complex, denoted by
H^{k}_{dR}(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{H})
, is called the

k^{th}
-de Rham cohomology of the pair

(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
and we have the

de Rham cohomology ring of the pair

(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
by setting

H_{\rm dR}^{*}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,:=\,\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\dim X_{0}}H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,.

(3.11)

Also, there is a natural homomorphism
H_{\rm dR}^{*}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,\longrightarrow\,H_{\rm dR}^{*}(X_{0},\,{\mathbb{R}})
which is, in general, neither injective nor surjective.

Remark 3.15
.

It should be noted that differential forms are defined on any Lie groupoid equipped with a connection.
However, the connection needs to be integrable in order to be able to define the exterior derivative.
A different definition of the de Rham cohomology of a Lie groupoid was introduced by
Laurent-Gengoux, Tu and Xu,
[
LGTX2
]
,
using the simplicial manifold given by the nerve associated to the Lie groupoid
(compare also with
[
Du
,
Be2
,
BX
,
FN
]
).

Example 3.16
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[M\rightrightarrows M]
be the associated Lie groupoid. Fix the connection

\mathcal{H}_{m}M\,=\,T_{m}M
as in Example
3.4
. Then the
k
-th de Rham cohomology
group of the pair
(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
is isomorphic to the
k
-th de Rham cohomology group
H^{k}_{\rm dR}(M)
of

M
and so we have
H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,\cong\,H^{k}_{\rm dR}(M)
.

Example 3.17
.

Let
G
be a Lie group acting on a smooth manifold
M
. Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[M\times G\rightrightarrows M]
be the associated action
Lie groupoid. In particular, the source and target maps respectively are given by
{\rm pr}_{1}\,\colon\,(m,\,g)\,\longmapsto\,m
and
\mu\,\colon\,(m,\,g)\,\longmapsto\,m\cdot g.
Define a connection
\mathcal{H}_{(m,g)}(M\times G)\,=\,T_{m}M
as in Example
3.5
. Then, a differential
k
-form
\omega
on

\mathbb{X}
is a differential form on
M
satisfying the condition
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\omega)(m,\,g)=\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(\mu^{*}\omega)(m,\,g)
for all
(m,\,g)\,\in\,M\times G
. That means,
\omega(m)\,=\,({R_{g}}^{*}\omega)(m),
where
{R_{g}}^{*}
is the induced action of
G
on the
space of differential forms. Thus the space of differential forms of the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[M\times G\rightrightarrows M]
with connection
\mathcal{H}_{(m,g)}\,=\,T_{m}M
is the space of
G
-invariant differential forms on
M
. In turn,
we have
H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,=\,H^{k}(\Omega^{\bullet}(M)^{G})
, where
H^{k}(\Omega^{\bullet}(M)^{G})
denotes the
cohomology corresponding to the
G
-invariant differential forms. In particular if
G
is compact and connected
then, by a classical theorem of E. Cartan, the inclusion map
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)^{G}\,\longrightarrow\,\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
induces an isomorphism
H^{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}(M)^{G})\,\longrightarrow\,H^{*}(M)
of cohomology rings
and thus we have
H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\,\cong\,H^{k}(M)
. Note that the simplicial de Rham cohomology
associated to the simplicial nerve of the action Lie groupoid has been computed in
[
Be1
,
BX
]
. In
contrast, this simplicial de Rham cohomology of
\mathbb{X}
is isomorphic to the whole
G
-equivariant cohomology

H^{*}_{G}(M)
of
M
. See Subsection
3.3
for a brief comparison between our approach to de
Rham cohomology of Lie groupoids with connections and that of Behrend
[
Be1
]
and Behrend-Xu

[
BX
]
.

Example 3.18
.

Let
\pi\,\colon\,\,P\,\longrightarrow\,M
be a principal bundle and
A
a connection on it. Consider the
Lie groupoid
{\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}}\,=\,[\frac{P\times P}{G}\rightrightarrows M]
and the connection
\mathcal{H}
on it, as
described in Example
3.8
. It is not difficult to verify that an

\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{k}(M),\ 0\,\leq\,k
, is a differential form on
{\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}}
if it satisfies
the condition
\pi^{*}\alpha(p)\,=\,\pi^{*}\alpha(q)
for all
p,\,q\,\in\,P.
In particular if
\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{0}(M),
the condition says that
\pi^{*}\alpha\,=\,\alpha\circ\pi
is a constant
function (and hence by surjectivity of
\pi,

\alpha
as well). For

k\,>\,0
we have
\Omega^{k}({\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge}},\,\mathcal{H})\,=\,\{\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{k}(M)\,\mid\,\pi^{*}\alpha\,=\,0\}.
In turn when the connection is integrable, we get

\begin{split}&H^{0}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge},\,\mathcal{H})\,=\mathbb{R},\\
&H^{1}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{P}_{\rm Gauge},\,\mathcal{H})\,=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)\,\mid\,\pi^{*}\alpha\,=\,0,\ d\alpha\,=\,0\},\end{split}

and so on for the higher cohomology groups.

We end this section with a result regarding the cohomology ring homomorphism associated with a morphism of Lie groupoids with connection.
The following proposition is deduced using Lemma
3.11
.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}
be integrable connections on the Lie groupoids
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
and
\mathbb{Y}\,=\,[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}]
respectively. Then a morphism of Lie groupoids with connections

(F,\,f)\,\colon\,\bigl(\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)\,\longrightarrow\,\bigl(\mathbb{Y}=[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)

induces a homomorphism of de Rham cohomology
groups

H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{Y},\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}})\,\longrightarrow\,H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}})

that sends any
[\alpha]
to
[f^{*}\alpha]
. These
maps produce a morphism of graded
\mathbb{R}
-algebras
H^{*}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{Y},\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}})\,\longrightarrow\,H^{*}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}})
.

Proof.

Let
[\alpha]\,\in\,H^{k}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{Y},\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}).
In particular, that means that
\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{Y})
and
0\,=\,d\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{k+1}(\mathbb{Y}).
Then, using
Lemma
3.13
we have that
f^{*}\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{X})
and
0\,=\,df^{*}\alpha\,\in\,\Omega^{k+1}(\mathbb{X}).
Also Lemma
3.11
implies that

df^{*}\theta\,\in\,\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{Y})
for any
\theta\,\in\,\Omega^{k-1}(\mathbb{X})
. Thus we have
[f^{*}\alpha]\,=\,[f^{*}(\alpha+d\theta)].

∎

3.3.
Simplicial de Rham cohomology of a Lie groupoid

In
[
BX
]
Behrend and Xu introduced the notion of de Rham cohomology for Lie groupoids, combining the construction of Čech cohomology and the de Rham cohomology of differential forms, relying on the simplicial manifold associated to the groupoid nerve. Our definition (and the motivation behind it) of the de Rham cohomology for Lie groupoids with connections is very much different. First of all, we introduce the notion of a connection on
a Lie groupoid to study Atiyah sequences over a Lie groupoid (see the discussion around (
3.2
)). A
natural consequence for the existence of a connection on a Lie groupoid is the definition of differential forms as introduced in Definition
3.10
, and the associated de Rham cohomology. In
Section
5
this definition will allow us to describe connections
on principal bundles over Lie groupoids as differential forms and subsequently to develop the associated Chern-Weil
theory and theory of characteristic classes (see Section
6
).
More succinctly, one may say the groupoid structure is exploited in our model by taking into account the connection on the Lie groupoid (see condition 3 in Definition
3.1
), not through the groupoid nerves. However, our framework can be adopted for the set-up of simplicial smooth manifolds, which will be pursued in detail in follow-up work
[
BCKN2
]
. Here we will give a brief outline of the constructions. For this,
let us recall the constructions in
[
BX
]
and compare them with ours.

5.
Forms for connections on principal Lie groupoid bundles

Let
G
be a Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra. The vector space of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued differential
k
-forms on
a manifold
Y
will be denoted by
\Omega^{k}(Y,\,\mathfrak{g}).

Let
P
be a principal
G
-bundle over a manifold
M
.
Then, there is a bijective correspondence between the following two sets:

(1)

The set of all splittings of the Atiyah sequence for
P

(see (
3.1
)).

(2)

The set of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-forms
A
on
P
satisfying the following conditions:

•

the map
A\,:\,TP\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is
G
-equivariant for the adjoint action
of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
, and

•

the restriction of
A
to every fiber of the projection
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
coincides
with the Maurer-Cartan form for the action of
G
on the fiber.

Given a splitting homomorphism
\rho\,:\,TM\,\longrightarrow\,\text{At}(P)
for the
Atiyah sequence for
P
, the corresponding
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form
A
on
P
is uniquely determined by
the condition that the quotient by
G
of the kernel of
A
coincides with the image of
\rho
.
Conversely, given a form
TP\,\longrightarrow\,P\times\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the above two
conditions, after taking the quotient
by the action of
G
, we get a homomorphism
\text{At}(P)\,\longrightarrow\,\text{ad}(P)
. This homomorphism
gives a splitting of the Atiyah sequence for
P
.

The above bijective correspondence provides an alternative definition of a connection on
P
.
For more details on the above correspondence and for the Atiyah sequence approach to connections on principal bundles, we refer also to
[
Ma2
, Appendix A]
.

It was observed in Proposition

that given a Lie groupoid

equipped with a

on

Assume

Lemma 5.1

Let
(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0},\ \mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}])
be a principal

\mathbb{X}
. Let
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
be a connection on

, and let

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,\subset\,T(s^{*}E_{G})
be the pullback connection on the Lie groupoid

(see Lemma

, and

be the pullback form on the manifold

Then,

if and only if

is a differential

Proof.

Consider the morphism of Lie groupoids

Now the lemma follows immediately

The following lemma classifies connections on the pair
(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
in terms of

\mathfrak{g}
-valued differential
1
-forms on the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\,\rightrightarrows\,E_{G}]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0},\,\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}])
be a principal
G
-bundle over a Lie groupoid with
integrable connection
\bigl(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}\bigr)
. Let
\omega
be a connection
1
-form
on the principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
. Let

\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,{{\rm At}(E_{G})}\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm ad}(E_{G})
be the corresponding homomorphism
of vector bundles over
X_{0}
. Then
\mathcal{D}
defines a connection on

(E_{G}\,\xrightarrow{\pi}\,X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
if and only if
\omega
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form on the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\,\rightrightarrows\,E_{G}]
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{D}}\,\colon\,TE_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{G}\times\mathfrak{g}
be the homomorphism
of vector bundles given by the
1
-form
\omega\,\in\,\Omega^{1}(E_{G},\,\mathfrak{g})
.
Then,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
descends to a homomorphism

\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,\text{At}(E_{G})\,\longrightarrow\,\text{ad}(E_{G})
of vector bundles over
X_{0}
.

Suppose that the above homomorphism
\mathcal{D}
is a connection on the principal

G
-bundle
(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
.
We have to show that
\omega
is a differential form on
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is a connection on
(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
, we know that

\mathcal{D}
is a morphism of vector bundles over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
, and hence

\mathcal{D}(\gamma\cdot[r])\,=\,\gamma\cdot\mathcal{D}([r])
for all
(\gamma,\,[r])\,\in\,X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}(TE_{G})/G
; equivalently, the homomorphism
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies the
condition that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}\bigl(\gamma\cdot(a,\,v)\bigr)\,=\,\gamma\cdot\overline{\mathcal{D}}(a,\,v)

for all
\bigl(\gamma,\,(a,\,v)\bigr)\,\in\,X_{1}\times_{X_{0}}TE_{G}\,=\,s^{*}E_{G}
.

Let
\widetilde{\theta}
be the
map in (
3.6
) corresponding to the pullback connection
{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}
on
s^{*}E_{G}\,\rightrightarrows\,E_{G}.

Recall that
\gamma\cdot(a,\,v)\,=\,\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v)
and
\overline{\mathcal{D}}\bigl((a,\,v)\bigr)\,=\,\bigl(a,\omega(a)(v)\bigr)
(see (
3.8
)). Thus we conclude that

\begin{split}\bigl(\gamma\cdot a,\,\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v)\bigr)&\,=\,\bigl(\gamma\cdot a,\,\omega(a)(v)\bigr)\\
\Rightarrow\,\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v))&\,=\,\omega(a)(v).\end{split}

Consequently, for any
(v,\,r)\,\in\,T_{(\gamma,\,a)}s^{*}E_{G}
,

\displaystyle\bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}\omega)(\gamma,\,a)\bigr)(v,\,r)

\displaystyle\,=\,\omega(\gamma.a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v))

\displaystyle\,=\,\omega(a)(v)

\displaystyle\,=\,\bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega)(\gamma,a)\bigr)(v,\,r).

The last equality establishes that

\omega
is a differential
1
-form on the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
.

To prove the converse, suppose that
\omega\,\in\,\Omega^{1}(E_{G},\,\mathfrak{g})
is a connection
1
-form on
the underlying principal

G
-bundle
E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
. Assume
\omega
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form on the Lie groupoid

[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,\text{At}(E_{G})\,\longrightarrow\,\text{ad}(E_{G})
be the homomorphism of vector bundles
given by
\omega
. To prove the converse it
suffices to show that the morphism
\mathcal{D}
is a morphism of vector
bundles over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
.

Since
\omega
is a differential
1
-form on
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
, we have,

\bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}\omega)(\gamma,\,a)\bigr)(v,\,r)\,=\,\bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega)(\gamma,\,a)\bigr)(v,\,r)

for
(\gamma,\,a)\,\in\,s^{*}E_{G}

and
(v,\,r)\,\in\,T_{(\gamma,a)}(s^{*}E_{G})
. Observe that the
two equations

\bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}\omega)(\gamma,a)\bigr)(v,r)\,=\,\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,\,a)}(v)),\ \bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\omega)(\gamma,\,a)\bigr)(v,\,r)\,=\,\bigl(\omega(a)\bigr)(v)

together imply that

\bigl(\omega(\gamma\cdot a)\bigr)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,\,a)}(v))\,=\,\bigl(\omega(a)\bigr)(v).

After plugging the last equality into the equation

\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\gamma\cdot(a,\,v))\,=\,(\gamma\cdot a,\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,\,a)}(v)))\,,

and comparing with
\gamma\cdot\overline{\mathcal{D}}((a,\,v))\,=\,(\gamma\cdot a,\omega(a)(v))
, we
obtain that

\displaystyle\{\overline{\mathcal{D}}(\gamma\cdot r)\,=\,\gamma\cdot\overline{\mathcal{D}}(r)\}\,\Longrightarrow\,\{{\mathcal{D}}(\gamma\cdot[r])\,=\,\gamma\cdot{\mathcal{D}}([r])\}.

Thus,
\mathcal{D}
gives a connection on the principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\ \mathbb{X})
. This
completes the proof.
∎

We now recall the Lie bracket operation on the Lie algebra valued differential forms on a
smooth manifold
Y
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. Let
\omega
and
\eta
be
\mathfrak{g}
-valued differential

k
-form and
l
-form respectively on
Y
. Then, the Lie bracket
[\omega,\,\eta]

is a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued differential
(k+l)
-form on
Y
which is constructed as follows:

[\omega,\,\eta](a)(v_{1},\,\cdots,\,v_{k+l})

(5.1)

=\,\frac{1}{(k+l)!}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{k+l}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)[\omega(a)(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k)}),\omega(a)(v_{\sigma(k+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k+l)})]

for all
a\,\in\,Y
and
v_{i}\,\in\,T_{a}Y
,
1\,\leq\,i\,\leq\,k+l
and where
\Sigma_{n}
denotes the symmetric group of order
n!
.

We then have the following basic property of connection forms on Lie groupoids.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\bigl(\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}\bigr)
be a Lie
groupoid with an integrable connection, and let

(E_{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
be
a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,\subset\,T(s^{*}E_{G})
be the pullback
of the connection
\mathcal{H}
to the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
. Let
\mathcal{D}

be a connection on the principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}
on
\mathbb{X}
, and let
\omega\,\colon\,E_{G}\,\,\longrightarrow\,T^{*}E_{G}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
be the connection
1
-form corresponding to
\mathcal{D}

(see Lemma
5.2
). Then the Lie bracket
[\omega,\,\omega]\,\in\,\Omega^{2}(E_{G},\,{\mathfrak{g}})
is a differential
2
-form on the Lie groupoid with connection

\bigl([s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}],\,{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\bigr)
.

Proof.

By definition,
[\omega,\omega](a)(v_{1},\,v_{2})\,=\,[\omega(a)(v_{1}),\,\omega(a)(v_{2})]

for all
a\,\in\,E_{G}
and
v_{1},\,v_{2}\,\in\,T_{a}E_{G}
.
For
(\gamma,a)\,\in\,s^{*}E_{G}
and
(v_{1},\,r_{1}),\,(v_{2},\,r_{2})\,\in\,T_{(\gamma,a)}(s^{*}E_{G})
, we observe that

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}([\omega,\omega]))(\gamma,a)

\displaystyle((v_{1},\,r_{1}),\,(v_{2},\,r_{2}))\,=\,[\omega(a)(v_{1}),\,\omega(a)(v_{2})],

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}([\omega,\,\omega]))(\gamma,\,a)

\displaystyle((v_{1},\,r_{1}),(v_{2},\,r_{2}))\,=\,[\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v_{1})),\,\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,\,a)}(v_{2}))].

As it was seen in the proof of Lemma
5.2
,
\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,\,a)}(v,\,r))\,=\,\omega(a)(v)
for all
(\gamma,\,a)\,\in\,s^{*}E_{G}
and for all
(v,\,r)\,\in\,T_{(\gamma,a)}(s^{*}E_{G})
. In particular,

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}([\omega,\,\omega]))(\gamma,a)((v_{1},\,r_{1}),(v_{2},\,r_{2}))

\displaystyle=\,[\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v_{1})),\,\omega(\gamma\cdot a)(\widetilde{\theta}_{(\gamma,a)}(v_{2}))]

\displaystyle=\,[\omega(a)(r_{1}),\,\omega(a)(r_{2})]

\displaystyle=\,\bigl(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}([\omega,\,\omega]))(\gamma,\,a)\bigr)\bigl((v_{1},\,r_{1}),(v_{2},\,r_{2})\bigr).

Thus, we have established that

\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}([\omega,\omega]))\,=\,\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}([\omega,\,\omega]))\,.

So
[\omega,\,\omega]\,\in\,\Omega^{2}(E_{G},\,{\mathfrak{g}})
is a differential
2
-form on the Lie
groupoid
\bigl([s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}],\,{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\bigr)
.

6.
Chern-Weil map and characteristic classes

We will now describe the Chern-Weil theory and associated characteristic classes for principal bundles over Lie groupoids
with integrable connections.

6.1.
Chern-Weil theory for principal bundles

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid equipped with an integrable connection

\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
. Let
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
be a principal
G
-bundle. Let

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\,\subset\,T(s^{*}E_{G})
be the pullback connection on the Lie groupoid

[s^{*}E_{G}\,\rightrightarrows\,E_{G}]
. Furthermore, let

0\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm ad}(E_{G})\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm At}(E_{G})\,\longrightarrow\,TX_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,0

be the Atiyah
sequence for the principal
G
-bundle
\bigl(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X}\bigr)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a connection on the principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
, given by
a homomorphism
\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,{\rm At}(E_{G})\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm ad}(E_{G})
, or, equivalently, given by a homomorphism

\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,TX_{0}\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm At}(E_{G})
. Let
\omega\,\in\,\Omega^{1}(E_{G},\,{\mathfrak{g}})
be the corresponding connection
1
-form on the Lie
groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\,\rightrightarrows\,E_{G}]
. Consider the curvature
2
-form

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}\,\in\,\Omega^{2}(X_{0},\,\mathfrak{g})
for the connection
\mathcal{D}
;
so
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}
is a
2
-form on the Lie groupoid
[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
.
We have the pullback form

\Omega\,=\,\pi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}\,\in\,\Omega^{2}(E_{G},\,\mathfrak{g})

on the groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\,\rightrightarrows\,E_{G}]
.

We briefly recall the construction of the Chern-Weil morphism for the above pair
(\omega,\,\Omega)

(see for example
[
KN
]
). Let

\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
be the set of all symmetric
k
-linear mappings
\mathfrak{g}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{g}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{R}}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Define the adjoint action of the Lie group

G
on
\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
by

({\rm Ad}_{g}f)(x_{1},\,\cdots,\,x_{k})\,:=\,f({\rm Ad}_{g}x_{1},\,\cdots,\,{\rm Ad}_{g}x_{k})

for all
f\,\in\,\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

and
g\,\in\,G
. Let us define the
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant
forms as

\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,:=\,\big\{f\,\in\,\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\,\,\mid\,\,{\rm Ad}_{g}f\,=\,f\,\ \forall\,\,g\,\in\,G\big\}.

To any given
f\,\in\,\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}
we assign a closed
2k
-form

f(\Omega)
on
E_{G}
defined by

f(\Omega)(X_{1},\,\cdots,\,X_{2k}):=\,\frac{1}{(2k)!}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{2k}}\epsilon{{}_{\sigma}}f\bigl(\Omega(X_{\sigma(1)},\,X_{{\sigma(2)}}),\,\cdots,\,\Omega(X_{\sigma(2k-1)},\,X_{{\sigma(2k)}})\bigr)\,,

(6.1)

for vector fields
X_{i}
on
E_{G}
for
1\leq i\leq 2k

where
\Sigma_{2k}
is the symmetric group of order
(2k)!
, and

\epsilon_{\sigma}\,\in\,\{\pm 1\}
is the parity of the permutation
\sigma\,\in\,\Sigma_{2k}
.
Consider the map
\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,\longrightarrow\,\Omega^{2k}(E_{G})
given by
f\,\longmapsto\,f(\Omega)
. It has the following properties:

(1)

There exists a unique closed
2k
-form
{\widetilde{f(\Omega)}}_{\omega}\,\in\,\Omega^{2k}(X_{0})
such that
\pi^{*}{\widetilde{f(\Omega)}}_{\omega}\,=\,f(\Omega)
.

(2)

The map
\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,\longrightarrow\,H_{\rm dR}^{2k}(X_{0},\,{\mathbb{R}})
given by
f\,\longmapsto\,[{\widetilde{f(\Omega)}}_{\omega}]

is independent of the choice of the connection
\omega
on the principal
G
-bundle

E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0}
.

By linear extension the map in (2) naturally defines a homomorphism of algebras, namely

\begin{split}\text{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,:=\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}&\,\longrightarrow\,H_{\rm dR}^{*}(X_{0},\,{\mathbb{R}})\\
f&\,\longmapsto\,[{\widetilde{f(\Omega)}}_{\omega}]\,.\end{split}

(6.2)

The map in (
6.2
) is called the
Chern-Weil homomorphism
.

Since
\pi\,:\,E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}

is a surjective submersion, it follows that

\widetilde{f(\Omega)}_{\omega}\,=\,f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})\,.

(6.3)

We have so far treated the pair
(\omega,\,\Omega)
only as connection and
curvature of the underlying principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}\,\longrightarrow X_{0}
. In particular,
if
\omega^{\prime}
is any other connection
1
-form on the principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
, then

{\widetilde{f(\Omega)}}_{\omega}-{\widetilde{f(\Omega^{\prime})}}_{\omega^{\prime}}\,=\,d\widetilde{\Phi}

for some
(2k-1)
-form
\widetilde{\Phi}
on
X_{0}
. However in order to build
Chern-Weil theory for a
principal bundle over a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
we need to show the following two properties:

(1)

\pi^{*}{\widetilde{f(\Omega)}}_{\omega}\,=\,f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})
is a closed
2k
-form
on
\mathbb{X}
, and

(2)

the
(2k-1)
-form
\widetilde{\Phi}
on
X_{0}
is actually a
(2k-1)
-form on
\mathbb{X}
,
when both
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are connection forms on
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
.

In turn we will get a homomorphism

\begin{split}\text{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}&\,\longrightarrow\,H_{\rm dR}^{*}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})\\
f&\,\longmapsto\,[f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})].\end{split}

(6.4)

The part (1) of the above two statements was stated in the note
[
BN
]
.
Both of the statements will be proved here.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid equipped with an integrable connection

\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
. Let
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
be a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
. Let

\mathcal{D}
be a connection on the principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
, and let
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}
be the associated curvature
2
-form on
\mathbb{X}
. Then
the following two hold:

(1)

f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})
is a differential
2k
-form on
\mathbb{X}
.

(2)

The map
{\rm{Sym}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,\longrightarrow\,H_{\rm dR}^{*}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
defined by

does not depend on the connection
\mathcal{D}

on the principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
.

Proof.

Part (1):
We will prove it for the case
k=1
. The general case is
deduced following the same line of arguments.

We will prove the following: if
f\,:\,\mathfrak{g}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{R}
is a linear map satisfying
the condition
f(x)\,=\,f({\rm Ad}_{g}x)
for all
g\,\in\,G
and all
x\,\in\,\mathfrak{g}
,
then

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(s^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})))\,=\,\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(t^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})))\,.

Take
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}
and
v_{1},\,v_{2}\,\in\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}
. We have the following:

\displaystyle\biggl(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(s^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})))(\gamma)\biggr)(v_{1},\,v_{2})

\displaystyle\,=\,\biggl(s^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}))(\gamma)\biggr)(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1}),\,P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle=\,\biggl(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})(s(\gamma)\biggr)(s_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1})),\,s_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2})).

Recall that by Lemma
5.4
,
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}
is a differential
2
-form on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. Then for each
\gamma\,\in\,X_{1}

and
v_{1},\,v_{2}\,\in\,T_{\gamma}X_{1}
, we have

\biggl(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}(s(\gamma))\biggr)(s_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1})),s_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2}))\,=\,\biggl(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}(t(\gamma))\biggr)(t_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1})),t_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2})).

Thus, for
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(s^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})))\,\in\,\Omega^{2}(X_{1},\,\mathfrak{g})
,

\displaystyle\biggl(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(s^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})))(\gamma)\biggr)(v_{1},\,v_{2})

\displaystyle\,=\,f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}(s(\gamma))(s_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1})),\,s_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2})))

\displaystyle=\,f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}(t(\gamma))(t_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{1})),t_{*,\gamma}(P_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}(v_{2})))

\displaystyle=\,\widehat{\mathcal{H}}(t^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})))(\gamma)(v_{1},v_{2}).

Hence
f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})
is a differential
2
-form on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
.

Part (2):
Let
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be a pair of connections on the principal
G
-bundle

(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\ \mathbb{X})
and
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}},

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
the respective curvature
2
-forms on
the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. Given an element
f\,\in\,\text{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}
, we prove that

[f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})]\,=\,[f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}})]\,\in\,{H}^{2k}(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{H})
, i.e., there
is a
(2k-1)
-form
\widetilde{\Phi}
on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
such that

f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})-f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}})\,=\,d\widetilde{\Phi}
.

Let
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
be the associated connection
1
-forms for connections
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
respectively.
Let
\alpha\,=\,\omega-\omega^{\prime}
. We define a family of connection
1
-forms
\alpha_{t}\,=\,\omega^{\prime}+t\alpha
for

0\,\leq\,t\,\leq\,1
on the principal
G
-bundle
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
.
Let
\Omega_{t}
denote the associated curvature
2
-forms. Consider the differential
(2k-1)
-form

\Phi\,=\,k\int_{0}^{1}f(\alpha_{t},\Omega_{t},\Omega_{t},\cdots,\Omega_{t})dt

on
E_{G}
. According to the classical Chern-Weil
theory (see
[
KN
]
), this
(2k-1)
-form
\Phi
on
E_{G}
uniquely projects
to a
(2k-1)
-form
\widetilde{\Phi}
on
X_{0}
as
\Phi\,=\,\pi^{*}(\widetilde{\Phi})
,
and then
f(\Omega)-f(\Omega^{\prime})\,=\,d\Phi
. So, we have

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})-f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}(d(\widetilde{\Phi})).

Since
\pi\,\colon\,E_{G}\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0}
is a surjective submersion, this means
that
f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})-f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}})\,=\,d\widetilde{\Phi}
.

In order to prove that
\widetilde{\Phi}
is a differential form on the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
, it suffices
to show that
\Phi
is a differential form on the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
(see
Lemma
5.1
). Observe that

\displaystyle{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\Phi

\displaystyle={\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\left(k\int_{0}^{1}f(\alpha_{t},\Omega_{t},\Omega_{t},\cdots,\Omega_{t})dt\right)

\displaystyle=\,\left(k\int_{0}^{1}f({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}(\alpha_{t}),{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}(\Omega_{t}),\cdots,{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}(\Omega_{t}))dt\right)

\displaystyle\rm{and}

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\Phi)

\displaystyle=k\int_{0}^{1}f(\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\alpha_{t}),\cdots,\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}(\Omega_{t})))dt.

On the other hand, the differential forms
\alpha_{t},\,\Omega_{t}
on the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]

satisfy
\widehat{{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\alpha_{t})\,=\,\widehat{{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}}(\mu^{*}\alpha_{t})

and
\widehat{{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}(\Omega_{t}))\,=\,\widehat{{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}}(\mu^{*}(\Omega_{t}))
. Plugging these relations into the last equation, we conclude that

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}({\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\Phi)\,=\,\widehat{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\mu^{*}\Phi).

Thus,
\Phi
is a differential
(2k-1)
-form on the Lie groupoid
[s^{*}E_{G}\rightrightarrows E_{G}]
and so is
\widetilde{\Phi}
.

. Assume
that
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
admits a connection. Then we have a well-defined Chern-Weil map

\begin{split}\text{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,\longrightarrow\,H_{\rm dR}^{*}(\mathbb{X},\mathcal{H})\\
\end{split}

given by (
6.4
)
and this map does not depend on the choice of the connection on the principal bundle

(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
. Along with Proposition
3.19
, this allows us to construct
characteristic classes for principal
G
-bundles over Lie groupoids with connections.

Remark 6.2
.

In light of the discussions in Subsection
3.4
and prior to
Subsection
5.2
, it is reasonable to expect that the constructions and results described
in this section extend naturally to the Deligne-Mumford stacks. A detailed study of it
has been carried out in
[
BCKN1
]
.

6.2.
Characteristic classes

Let
\mathbb{X}\,=\,[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}]
be a Lie groupoid equipped with an integrable connection
\mathcal{H}\,\subset\,TX_{1}
, and
let
(E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{X}
. Let

\begin{split}{\rm Ch}_{E_{G}}\,\colon\,\rm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,&\longrightarrow\,H^{*}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}),\\
f&\longmapsto\,[f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})]\end{split}

(6.5)

be the map in Theorem
6.1
. We call
{\rm Ch}_{E_{G}}(f)
the

characteristic class
of
f.

Let

(\Phi,\,\phi)\,\colon\,\bigl(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)\,\longrightarrow\,\bigl(\mathbb{Y}\,=\,[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)

be a morphism of Lie groupoids with connections, and let

(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}Y_{0},\,\mathbb{Y})
be a principal
G
-bundle over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{Y}
.
We have seen in Proposition
5.8
that the pullback of the principal
G
-bundle

E_{G}\,\xrightarrow{\pi}Y_{0}
along
\phi
is a principal
G
-bundle

(\phi^{*}E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}.
Moreover, any connection
\mathcal{D}
on

(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}Y_{0},\,\mathbb{Y})
pulls back to a connection
\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}
on
(\phi^{*}E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0},\,\mathbb{X}).
The associated curvature
2
-form
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}\,\in\,\Omega^{2}(Y_{0},\mathfrak{g})
on the
Lie groupoid
\mathbb{Y}
evidently pulls back to the curvature
\phi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}
of
\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}
on the
Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
, namely we have

\phi^{*}\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}\,=\,\mathcal{K}_{\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}}.

Now for any
f\,\in\,\rm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}
it is immediate from (
6.1
) that

f(\mathcal{K}_{\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}})\,=\,\phi^{*}f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}).

Then using Proposition
3.19
it follows that
[f(\mathcal{K}_{\phi^{*}{\mathcal{D}}})]\,=\,[\phi^{*}f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})]\,=\,\phi^{*}[f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})].

We arrive at the following ‘naturality’ condition of the Chern-Weil map for principal
G
-bundles over Lie groupoids with integrable connections.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(\Phi,\,\phi)\,\colon\,\bigl(\mathbb{X}=[X_{1}\rightrightarrows X_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigr)\,\longrightarrow\,\bigl(\mathbb{Y}\,=\,[Y_{1}\rightrightarrows Y_{0}],\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigr)
be a morphism of Lie groupoids with integrable connections, and
let
(E_{G}\xrightarrow{\pi}Y_{0},\,\mathbb{Y})
be a principal
G
-bundle over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{Y}
. Let

(\phi^{*}E_{G}\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
be the pullback principal
G
-bundle over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}.
Then,

\begin{split}{\rm Ch}_{\phi^{*}E_{G}}\,=\,\phi^{*}\circ{\rm Ch}_{E_{G}}\,,\end{split}

(6.6)

where
\phi^{*}\colon H^{*}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{Y},\,\mathcal{H})\,\longrightarrow\,H^{*}_{\rm dR}(\mathbb{X},\,\mathcal{H})
is the
algebra homomorphism in Proposition
3.19
.

Let
(E\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
be a rank
r
vector bundle over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
(see
Definition
2.13
). Consider the underlying vector bundle
E\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{0}

on
X_{0}.
Let

{\rm Fr}(E)=\bigsqcup_{x\in X_{0}}{\rm Iso}({\mathbb{F}}^{k}\rightarrow E_{x}),

where
E_{x}\,:=\,\pi^{-1}(x)
is
the fiber over
x\,\in\,X_{0},

\mathbb{F}
is the field of complex or real numbers and
{\rm Iso}({\mathbb{F}}^{r}\rightarrow E_{x})
is the set of linear isomorphisms. The right action of
{\rm GL}(r,{\mathbb{F}})
on
{\rm Fr}(E)

given by
(x,\,\sigma)\cdot g\,=\,(x,\,\sigma\circ g)
defines a (right) principal
{\rm GL}(r,\mathbb{F})
-bundle

{\rm Fr}(E)\,\longrightarrow\,X_{0},\ (x,\,\sigma)\,\longmapsto\,x
, called the
frame bundle
. Now since

(E\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
is a vector bundle over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X},
we get a left action

\mu\,\colon\,X_{1}\times_{\pi}E\,\longrightarrow\,E

of
\mathbb{X}
on
E
such that the restriction for each
\gamma\in X_{1}
defines a linear map
\mu_{\gamma}\,\colon\,E_{s(\gamma)}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{t(\gamma)}.
This induces a left action
X_{1}\times_{{\pi}_{\rm Fr}}{\rm Fr}(E)\,\longrightarrow\,{\rm Fr}(E)

by
\gamma\cdot(x,\,\sigma)\,=\,(y,\,\mu_{\gamma}\circ\sigma),
for
x\xrightarrow{\gamma}y.
The compatibility condition

\gamma\cdot((x,\,\sigma)\cdot g)\,=\,(\gamma\cdot(x,\,\sigma))\cdot g
is immediate. That is to say, the
frame bundle
{\rm Fr}(E)\,\longmapsto\,X_{0}
is in fact a principal
{\rm GL}(r,{\mathbb{F}})
-bundle over the
Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
. Following our notation, we write

({\rm Fr}(E)\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X}).

For a given rank
r
-vector bundle
(E\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
over the Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
, we define
the
Chern-Weil homomorphism
as

\begin{split}{\rm Ch}_{E}\,:=\,{\rm Ch}_{{\rm Fr}(E)}.\end{split}

(6.7)

Now given an element
\mathfrak{A}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})
of
{\rm GL}(r,\mathbb{F})
, we
find the coefficients
c_{i}(\mathfrak{A})
of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathfrak{A}
from the following expansion

{\rm Det}(\mathfrak{A}+tI)\,=\,\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}(\mathfrak{A})t^{n-k}\,.

Each
c_{i}\,\colon\,\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})^{*}\,=\,\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{R}}
is in fact a degree
i
homogeneous polynomial invariant under the adjoint action
of
{\rm GL}(r,\mathbb{F}).
Thus
c_{i}
can be identified with an
{\rm Ad}({\rm GL}(r,\mathbb{F}))
-invariant, multilinear,
symmetric map (see
[
KN
]
)

{\overline{c}_{i}}\,:\,\text{Sym}^{i}(\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})^{*})^{G}\,=\,\text{Sym}^{i}(\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F}))^{G}\,\longrightarrow\,{\mathbb{R}}\,.

Note that the bilinear form
(A,\,B)\,\longrightarrow\,\text{trace}(AB)
on

\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})
identifies the dual
\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})^{*}
with

\mathfrak{gl}(r,\mathbb{F})
.
Then the various characteristic classes of a vector bundle
(E\rightarrow X_{0},\,\mathbb{X})
are given as the images of
the classes
{\overline{c}_{i}}
under the homomorphism
{\rm Ch}_{{\rm Fr}(E)}
.

As an application of the construction in this section we consider the following example.

Example 6.4
.

Let
G,\,H
be a pair of Lie groups. Recall that in Example
5.7
, we have seen
an
H
-equivariant smooth principal
G
-bundle
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
defines a principal
G
-bundle over the action Lie groupoid
[M\times H\rightrightarrows M],
and an
H
-invariant connection
1
-form
\omega
on the principal
G
-bundle
P\,\longrightarrow\,M
defines a connection on the
G
-bundle
P\rightarrow M
over the Lie groupoid
[M\times H\rightrightarrows M].
The associated curvature form
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}}
is
H
-invariant, and thus for any
f\in\rm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G},

f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})
is an
H
-invariant closed form in
\Omega^{*}([M\times H\rightrightarrows M],\,\mathcal{H}).
Now applying Theorem
6.1
we see that the Chern-Weil map

\begin{split}{\rm Ch}_{P}\,\colon\,\rm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}\,&\longrightarrow\,H^{*}_{\rm dR}([M\times H\rightrightarrows M],\,\mathcal{H}),\\
f&\longmapsto\,[f(\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{D}})]\end{split}

(6.8)

takes values in the subring of
H^{*}_{\rm dR}([M\times H\rightrightarrows M],\,\mathcal{H})
defined by the equivalence classes of
H
-invariant closed forms. Note this subring is not same as the
H
-equivariant de Rham cohomology.

In this article we have studied the Chern-Weil theory of principal
G
-bundles over a Lie groupoid
\mathbb{X}
(with integrable connection
\mathcal{H}
). A natural direction of enquiry would be to study the behaviour of the constructions described here under Morita equivalences and, therefore, whether these structures can be further extended to more general differentiable stacks, which are not Deligne-Mumford stacks or orbifolds. While a conclusive answer to this question for the most general case still seems to be elusive, in
[
BCKN1
]
we have discussed the problem in certain important cases of interest.

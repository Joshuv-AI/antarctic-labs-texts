Ramified and Unramified Motivic Multiple t t -, T T - and S S -Values
Ce Xu and Jianqiang Zhao[1mm] a. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241002, P.R. Chinab. Department of Mathematics, The Bishop’s School, La Jolla, CA 92037, USA

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper we shall consider a few variants of the motivic multiple zeta values of level two by restricting the summation indices in the definition of multiple zeta values to some fixed parity patterns. These include Hoffman’s multiple
t
-values, Kaneko and Tsumura’s multiple
T
-values, and the multiple
S
-values studied previously by the authors. By applying Brown and Glanois’s descent theory on the motivic versions of these values we shall derive some criterion for when these values are ramified and unramified. Assuming Grothendieck’s period conjecture, our results partially confirms a conjecture of Kaneko and Tsumura about when multiple
T
-values can be expressed as a rational linear combination of multiple zeta values (i.e., unramified) if their depth is less than four. Similar results are obtained for motivic multiple
S
-values. Further, we are able to generalize a result of Charlton to more families of unramified multiple
t
-values with unit components (i.e. components equal to 1). We propose some more unsolved problems at the end of the paper.

Keywords
: motivic iterated integrals, (motivic) multiple zeta values, (motivic) Euler sums, (motivic) multiple
T
-values, (motivic) multiple
t
-values, (motivic) multiple
S
-values.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we
define the
multiple zeta values
(MZVs) by

\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{d}):=\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{d}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}.

We need to impose the condition
k_{d}\geq 2
to guarantee convergence and we call the composition

{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})

admissible
in this case. Conventionally,
d
is called the
depth
and

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{d}
is called the
weight
.

MZVs and their higher level generalizations
have played significant roles in both mathematics and physics in recent years due to their
far-reaching connections with many areas such as algebra geometry, number theory, knot theory and theoretical physics (see e.g.
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
13
,
17
]
). At level two, we obtain the so-called
Euler sums

(also called alternating MZVs) by introducing alternating signs on the numerators: for any
{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}=\pm 1
,

\zeta(k_{1},\dots,k_{d};{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{d}):=\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{d}}\displaystyle\frac{{\varepsilon}_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots{\varepsilon}_{d}^{n_{d}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}.

As a convention we put a bar on top of
k_{j}
if the corresponding
{\varepsilon}_{j}=-1
. For example,
\zeta(\bar{1})=\zeta(1;-1)=-\log 2
.

In the mid 1990s, Broadhurst
[
4
]
already noticed that some of the Euler sums actually lie in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span
\mathsf{MZV}
of MZVs, and he called these values honorary MZVs. Such a phenomenon is very interesting because it reflects the important Galois descent structure studied recently by Glanois in her thesis
[
8
,
9
]
. Following her terminology, we call such values unramified.

The
multiple
T
-values
(MTVs) are a class of infinite sums which can be regarded as a level two variation of
the multiple zeta values, first defined by Kaneko and Tsumura
[
11
]
:

T(k_{1},\dots,k_{d}):=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{d}\\
n_{j}\equiv j\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\displaystyle\frac{2^{d}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}},

for all admissible compositions
(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
. We again call
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|
its
weight
and
d
its
depth
.
From numerical evidence, they propose the following conjecture
(cf.
[
11
, Conjecture 5.2]
).

Conjecture 1.1
.

1) For even weights,
T(k,l,m)
with
p,r
: odd
\geq 3
and

q
: even (and their duals) are in
\mathsf{MZV}
. Together with the single
T
-values
these (and their duals) are the only unramified MTVs (i.e., contained in
\mathsf{MZV}
).

2) For odd weights,
T(p,1,r)
with
p,r
: even (and their duals) are in
\mathsf{MZV}
.
Together with the single and double
T
-values these (and their duals) are the
only unramified MTVs.

In
[
12
]
, Murakami proved that the conditions in the above conjecture are sufficient by using the motivic theory of Euler sums developed in
[
5
,
9
]
. In this paper, we will use this machinery to further show that,
on the motivic level, these conditions are also necessary, at least when the depth is bounded by three.
This would completely confirm the above conjecture for depth
\leq 3
if we assume Grothendieck’s period conjecture.

.

Suppose
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
has depth at most three. We have the following necessary and sufficient conditions for

to be unramified.

(1)

If

{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=k\geq 2
.

(2)

If

then

with

odd.

(3)

If

then either

(i)

{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(1,1,2),(1,1,3),(1,2,2)
or

(ii)

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|
is even,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(O_{>1},E,O_{>1})
, or

(iii)

|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|
is odd,
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}|=(E,1,E)

Consequently,
S^{\mathfrak{m}}(1,{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}})
are ramified for all nontrivial
{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}
.

Proof.

Part (1) of the lemma is
[
12
, Lemma 43]
by Murakami.
By the same argument, we see that in general

D_{1}S^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=\delta_{k_{1}=1}\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{p}=\pm 1}(\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})\eta_{3}\dots\eta_{p}I^{\mathfrak{l}}(0;\eta_{1},\eta_{2}){\otimes}I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;\eta_{2},0_{k_{2}-1},\dots,\eta_{p},0_{k_{p}-1};1)

for
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{p})
. Thus
D_{1}S^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=0
if
k_{1}\geq 2
. On the other hand,
if
k_{1}=1
then we see that

D_{1}S^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=-2\log^{\mathfrak{l}}2{\otimes}T^{\mathfrak{m}}(k_{2},\dots,k_{p})\neq 0

which implies that
S^{\mathfrak{m}}(1,k_{2},\dots,k_{p})
is ramified by Thm.
2.1
. This proves (2).
∎

4
Reduction formula for double Euler sums of odd weight

In this section, we will express every motivic double Euler sum of odd weight as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination
of products of Riemann zeta values (after setting
\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(0)=-1/2
). As corollaries, we can
write all the elements
t^{\mathfrak{l}}(k,l)
,
T^{\mathfrak{l}}(k,l)
, and
S^{\mathfrak{l}}(k,l)
in
{\mathcal{L}}

as rational multiples of
\zeta^{\mathfrak{l}}(k+l)
when the weight
k+l
is odd.

First, we generalize a result of Zagier
[
16
, Prop. 7]
concerning double zetas to double Euler sums.
The non-motivic version can be found in
[
2
, (74)]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}=\pm 1
. Put
\zeta(0;\pm 1)=-1/2
.
Then the double zeta value
\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(m,n;\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
of weight

m+n=W=2K+1
is given by

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(m,n;\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=

\displaystyle\,-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w;\eta_{1}\eta_{2})+(-1)^{m}\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{K-1}\bigg[\binom{w-2s-1}{m-1}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w-2s;\eta_{1})\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(2s;\eta_{1}\eta_{2})

\displaystyle\,+\binom{w-2s-1}{n-1}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w-2s;\eta_{2})\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(2s;\eta_{1}\eta_{2})\bigg]+{\delta}_{2|n}\,\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(m;\eta_{1})\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(n;\eta_{2}).

Proof.

We will follow Zagier idea in the proof of
[
16
, Prop. 7]
. For
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}=\pm 1
and
w\geq 3
we define

\displaystyle P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{r,s\in\mathbb{N},r+s=w}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}\binom{r}{\eta_{1}}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}\binom{s}{\eta_{2}}x^{r-1}y^{s-1},

\displaystyle D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{r,s\in\mathbb{N},r+s=w}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}\binom{r\,,\,s}{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}x^{r-1}y^{s-1}.

Then we can derive

\displaystyle P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{2},\eta_{1}}(y,x)+\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w;\eta_{1}\eta_{2})F_{w}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2},\eta_{2}}(x,x+y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2},\eta_{1}}(y,x+y),

where
F_{w}(x,y)=\displaystyle\frac{x^{w-1}-y^{w-1}}{x-y}
.
Setting
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=(-1,-1)
and
(-1,1)

\displaystyle P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(x,y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(y,x)+\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)F_{w}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(x,x+y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(y,x+y),

\displaystyle P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(y,x)+\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(x,x+y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(y,x+y)

and repeatedly applying the above two equations, we get (setting
z=x-y
)

\displaystyle\,D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)-D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(y,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(y,z)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(z,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(y,z)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(z,x)-D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(y,z)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(z,x)

\displaystyle\,-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,-y)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(-y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(y,z)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(z,x)

\displaystyle\,-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,-y)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(-y,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)F_{w}(-y,z)-D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(z,-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(y,z)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(z,x)

\displaystyle\,-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,-y)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(-y,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)F_{w}(-y,z)-P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(-x,z)+D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(-x,-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)-\zeta(\overline{w})F_{w}(x,y)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(-y,y-x)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{w})F_{w}(z,x)

\displaystyle\,+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(-x,y)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(-y,z)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w)F_{w}(-y,z)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y-x)-D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y)+Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(z,x)+Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(z,-y)-D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,+}(x,y),

where

Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)=P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)+P^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(-x,y)-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w,\eta_{1}\eta_{2})F_{w}(x,y).

Similarly,

\displaystyle 2D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(x,y)+Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{+,-}(z,-y)+Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{-,-}(z,x).

In general

\displaystyle 2D^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)+Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(z,-y)+Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{2},\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(z,x).

Hence, putting
c_{m}=(1-(-1)^{m})
for all
m
we get

\displaystyle[x^{m-1}y^{n-1}]Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\,c_{m}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(m,\eta_{1})\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(n,\eta_{2})-\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w,\eta_{1}\eta_{2}),

\displaystyle[x^{m-1}y^{n-1}]Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(z,-y)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{r=m}^{w-1}\binom{r-1}{m-1}(-1)^{n}\Big[\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w,\eta_{2})-c_{r}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(r,\eta_{1})\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w-r,\eta_{1}\eta_{2})\Big],

\displaystyle[x^{m-1}y^{n-1}]Q^{\mathfrak{m}}_{\eta_{2},\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(z,x)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{r=n}^{w-1}\binom{r-1}{n-1}(-1)^{n}\Big[\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w,\eta_{1})-c_{r}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(r,\eta_{2})\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}(w-r,\eta_{1}\eta_{2})\Big].

Therefore, the proposition follows by the substitution
r=w-2s
and the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{r=m}^{w-1}\binom{r-1}{m-1}=\binom{w-1}{m}=\binom{w-1}{n-1},\qquad\displaystyle\sum_{r=n}^{w-1}\binom{r-1}{n-1}=\binom{w-1}{n}=\binom{w-1}{m-1}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m+n=W=2K+1
is given by

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mathfrak{l}}(m,n;\eta_{1},\eta_{2})

\displaystyle\,=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{\mathfrak{l}}(w;\eta_{1}\eta_{2})-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{m}\bigg[\binom{w-1}{m-1}\zeta^{\mathfrak{l}}(w;\eta_{1})+\binom{w-1}{n-1}\zeta^{\mathfrak{l}}(w;\eta_{2})\bigg],

\displaystyle\tilde{\zeta}^{\mathfrak{l}}(m,n)

\displaystyle\,=-\bigg(1+(-1)^{m}\binom{w}{m}\bigg)\tilde{\zeta}^{\mathfrak{l}}(w),\quad t^{\mathfrak{l}}(m,n)=-t^{\mathfrak{l}}(w),

\displaystyle T^{\mathfrak{l}}(m,n)

\displaystyle\,=(-1)^{n}\binom{w-1}{m-1}T^{\mathfrak{l}}(w),\quad S^{\mathfrak{l}}(m,n)=(-1)^{n}\binom{w-1}{n-1}S^{\mathfrak{l}}(w).

Remark 4.3
.

The formula for
\zeta^{\mathfrak{l}}(m,n;\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
is depth two case of a more general reduction
formula of Glanois’s in
[
8
, Cor. 4.2.4]
.

\displaystyle\,-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{b}(-1)^{i}\displaystyle\frac{(a+b+1)!}{a!i!(b+1-i)!}T^{\mathfrak{l}}(a+b+2)

\displaystyle\,\binom{a+b+1}{a}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{b}(-1)^{i}\binom{b+1}{i}T^{\mathfrak{l}}(a+b+2)=0.

Thus

Similar computation shows that

(1)

Lemma 9.7
.

For any nontrivial composition of positive integers
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
we have

D_{1}\tilde{\zeta}^{\mathfrak{m}}_{a}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}},1)=-\log^{\mathfrak{l}}2{\otimes}\tilde{\zeta}^{\mathfrak{m}}_{a}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}).

Proof.

Observe that nontrivial cuts of
D_{1}\tilde{\zeta}^{\mathfrak{m}}_{a}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
can appear when
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
has unit components. First, assume
such a component appears in the following block:

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}=\pm 1}D_{1}I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},0,\eta_{i},\eta_{i+1},\dots,\eta_{j-1},\eta_{j},0,{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}};1)

for some strings
{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}
and
{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}}
. We observe that nontrivial cuts produced from the above block
0,\eta_{i},\dots,\eta_{j},0

lead to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}=\pm 1}\Big(I^{\mathfrak{l}}(0;\eta_{i};\eta_{i+1}){\otimes}I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},0,\eta_{i+1},\eta_{i+2},\dots,\eta_{j-1},\eta_{j},0,{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}};1)

\displaystyle+I^{\mathfrak{l}}(\eta_{j-1};\eta_{j};0){\otimes}I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},0,\eta_{i},\eta_{i+1},\dots,\eta_{j-2},\eta_{j-1},0,{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}};1)\Big)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}=\pm 1}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{j-1}I^{\mathfrak{l}}(\eta_{\ell-1};\eta_{\ell};\eta_{\ell+1}){\otimes}I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},0,\eta_{i},\dots,\widehat{\eta_{\ell}},\dots,\eta_{j},0,{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}};1).

The first two terms cancel if we apply to the second term the substitution

\eta_{i}\to\eta_{i+1}\to\cdots\to\eta_{j-1}\to\eta_{j}\to\eta_{j}\eta_{i}/\eta_{i+1}
.
Further, for each fixed
\ell
, by Lemma
2.4
the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta_{\ell}=\pm 1}I^{\mathfrak{l}}(\eta_{\ell-1};\eta_{\ell};\eta_{\ell+1})=0.

Therefore we see that
D_{1}\tilde{\zeta}^{\mathfrak{m}}_{a}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=D_{1}\zeta^{\mathfrak{m}}_{a}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=0
.

Now if a unit components at the end then the above argument shows that all but one term is left after cancelation
and the lemma follows easily.
∎

\tilde{\zeta}_{c}^{\mathfrak{l}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}){\otimes}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
which fall into three categories: either
all components of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}
are at least two, or
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(2,1,{\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}}^{\prime})
with all components of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}}^{\prime}
are at least two,
or
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(2,1,3,2,1,{\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}}^{\prime})
with
\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}}^{\prime})<\dep({\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}})
.
Thus, all these
t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
’s are unramified by Thm.
9.8
, Lemma
9.1
and induction, respectively.
The first factor
\tilde{\zeta}_{c}({\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}})
in the above is always unramified by
Thm.
2.3
or Prop.
9.6
. Note that at most two unit components can appear in
{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}
and
they must be at the one or both ends if they do appear.

To summarize, we have shown that
D_{1}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=0
and
D_{r}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
are unramified for all odd
r<\ell
.
This finishes the proof of the theorem by Thm.
2.1
.
∎

By Lemma
9.2
,
t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
is ramified if
t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
has a unit components at either or both ends.
We now provide a family of ramified motivic MtV
t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
with components at the both ends greater than 1.

Theorem 9.13
.

Let
1\leq j<n<\ell
. Suppose
k_{j}\geq 3
is odd and
k_{i}
is even for all
1\leq i<n
and
i\neq j
.
Suppose further that
k_{n}=1
and
k_{i}\geq 2
for all
n<i\leq\ell
.
Then the motivic MtV
t^{\mathfrak{m}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell})
is ramified.

Proof.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell})
and
r=|{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}_{1,n-1}|
. Then
r
is odd by the given conditions. We show that
D_{r}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
is ramified.
Indeed, the cut starting at the first 0 and ends at
\eta_{n}
yields

t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}_{1,n-1}){\otimes}t^{\mathfrak{m}}(1,{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}_{n+1,\ell})

(9.21)

which is ramified since
D_{1}t^{\mathfrak{m}}(1,{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}_{n+1,\ell})\neq 0
by Lemma
9.2
. All other cuts of
D_{r}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})

have the form
\pm\tilde{\zeta}_{c}^{\mathfrak{l}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}){\otimes}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})
with
\tilde{\zeta}_{c}^{\mathfrak{l}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}})
being unramified
and the components at the two ends of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}
both
\geq 2
. Thus
D_{1}t^{\mathfrak{m}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}})=0
by Lemma
9.2
.
Consequently, the unramified term (
9.21
) cannot be canceled. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

10
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have applied Brown’s motivic MZV theory and Glanois’s descent theory of Euler sums, further developed by Murakami and Charlton, to study the ramified and unramified motivic mixed values which are variants of the multiple zeta values of level two. We are able to extend a result of Murakami on a conjecture of Kaneko and Tsumura concerning MTVs. Murakami showed that certain conditions discovered by Kaneko and Tsumura are sufficient for MTVs to be unramified. On the motivic level, we showed in Thm.
1.3
that these conditions, at least for depth less than four, are also necessary.

We are also able to generalize a result of Charlton (see Thm.
1.4
(2))
concerning a family of unramified MtVs with unit components
and provide two more such families in Thm.
1.5
.
We remark that Thm.
1.5
(2) is a special case of the following more general
conjecture for which we have some quite strong numerical evidence.

Conjecture 10.1
.

Let
m,n,e\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that all components of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}}
are at least two.
Then the MtV
t(\{2n\}_{e},2m,\{2n\}_{e},1,{\boldsymbol{\sl{q}}})
is unramified.

We further provide a family of ramified motivic MtVs in Thm.
1.6
.

Furthermore, we are able to describe completely when a motivic MSV is ramified if its depth is bounded by three in Thm.
1.3
.
We also numerically searched for possible unramified MSVs of depth greater than three and found

S(2,1,1,1,4)=\tfrac{3577}{768}\zeta(9)+\tfrac{343}{384}\zeta(3)^{3}-\tfrac{63}{64}\zeta(2)^{3}\zeta(3)-\tfrac{217}{640}\zeta(2)^{2}\zeta(5)

seems to be the only one when the weight is at most 15.
S. Charlton kindly informed us that using the MZV data mine
[
1
]
he checked that
S(2,1,1,1,4)
is indeed the the only unramified MSVs of depth greater than three if the weight is less than 12. We now end our paper with the following questions.

Problem 10.2
.

Suppose the depth of
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
is at least four.

(1)

Is
S({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
unramified only when

?

Does Thm.

(1) and Conjecture

(3)

(3) exhaust all unramified MtV
t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
where
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}
has exactly two unit components?

(4)

Is there any unramified MtV
t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
where

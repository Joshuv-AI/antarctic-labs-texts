Differential Operator Representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , R ) \mathfrak{sl\left(\mathrm{2,R}\right)} as Modules over Univariate and Bivariate Hermite Polynomials
Manouchehr Amiri

Abstract
This paper presents the connections between univariate and bivariate Hermite polynomials and associated differential equations with specific representations of Lie algebra sl(2,R) whose Cartan sub-algebras coincide the associated differential operators of these differential equations . Applying the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula to generators of these algebras, results in new relation for one-variable and Bivariate Hermite polynomials. A general form of sl(2,R) representation by differential operators and arbitrary polynomial basis such as Laguerre and Legendre polynomials is introduced.

e^{\mathfrak{D}}\sum_{k=0}^{min(m,n)}(-1)^{n}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\left[\frac{2b(1-e)}{\sqrt{ac}}\right]^{k}H_{n-k}^{e}(x)H_{m-k}^{e}(y)=e^{m+n}H_{n}^{e}(x)H_{m}^{e}(y)

Comparing this with equation (35) shows the replacement

\hat{H}_{n,m}(x,y,\Lambda^{\prime})=a^{n/2}c^{m/2}\sum_{k=0}^{min(n,m)}(-1)^{k}k!\binom{m}{k}\binom{n}{k}(\frac{b^{\prime}}{\sqrt{ac}})^{k}H^{e}_{n-k}(x)H^{e}_{m-k}(y)

A General form of sl(2,R) Representation By Differential Operators and Polynomial Basis

Rodrigues’ formula for some orthogonal polybnomials such as Hermite, Laguerre and Legendre polynomilas are defined by by the action of specific differential operators on the n-th integer power of some function
B(x)
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
:

P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(x^{2}-1)^{n}

(73)

L_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{d}{dx}-1\right)^{n}x^{n}

(74)

The
B(x)
in these equations are
B(x)=(x^{2}-1)
and
B(x)=x
for Legendre and Laguerre polynomials respectively. Let
B(x)
and its integer powers form a set
\left\{1,B(x),B^{2}(x),...\right\}=\left\{B^{n}(x)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
of independent basis in the infinite dimensional polynomial space. One can interpret these formulas as transformations from polynomial space basis
\left\{1,B(x),B^{2}(x),...\right\}
to new basis i.e Legendre, and Laguerre polynomials. The definition of
B(x)
in Rodrigues’ formula limited to a polynomial with degree at most 2. However in this section we use
B(x)
with no limitation and as any kind of smooth functions.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\left\{1,B(x),B^{2}(x),...\right\}=\left\{B^{n}(x)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

modules on these polynomials is represented as

\textbf{h}=\frac{B}{B^{\prime}}D-\frac{n}{2}\quad\textbf{e}=\frac{D}{B^{\prime}}\quad\textbf{f}=\frac{B^{2}}{B^{\prime}}D-nB

(75)

Where
B^{\prime}(x)
is the derivative of
B(x)
and
D
is derivative operator respect to

.

Proof.

It is straightforward to prove that these bases satisfy the commutation relations of
\mathfrak{sl\left(\mathrm{2,R}\right)}
in equation (8).

7
Conclusion

By introducing isomorphic Lie algebras to
\mathfrak{sl\left(\mathrm{2,R}\right)}
whose generators are defined by differential operators involved in univariate and bivariate Hermite differential equations, we showed that these Hermite polynomials, are eigefunctions of Cartan suba-lgebras of
\mathfrak{sl\left(\mathrm{2,R}\right)}
. By using the commutation relations of these algebras and the BCH formula, new relations for Hermite polynomials are obtained. A general form of sl(2,R) representation by differential operators and polynomial basis such as Laguerre, and Legendre differential polynomials, is introduced.

Reccurence Sets for Partial Inverse Semigroup Actions and Related Structures
M. Măntoiu

Abstract
Two types of recurrence sets are introduced for inverse semigroup partial actions in topological spaces. We explore their connections with similar notions for related types of imperfect symmetries (prefix inverse semigroup expansions, partial group and groupoid actions).



1
Introduction

In the theory of classical dynamical systems, a special role is played by
recurrence sets
(also called
dwelling sets
); this can be seen in any of the standard texts, as
[
1
,
4
]
for example. Suppose that the group
{\sf G}
acts continuously on the topological space
\Sigma
, the action being denoted by
\alpha
. For
M,N\subset\Sigma
one defines

{\sf G}(\alpha)_{M}^{N}:=\big\{g\in{\sf G}\,\big|\,\alpha_{g}(M)\cap N\neq\emptyset\big\}\,.

(1.1)

Such subsets of
{\sf G}
are relevant for the behavior of the dynamical systems in many ways. As a basic example, for singletons
M=\{\sigma\}
and
N=\{\tau\}
, the set
{\sf G}(\alpha)_{\sigma}^{\tau}\equiv{\sf G}(\alpha)_{\{\sigma\}}^{\{\tau\}}
is non-void if and only if
\sigma
and
\tau
belong to the same orbit. This serves to characterize invariant subsets. Many other important properties may be defined or described in terms of recurrence sets; we mention only (topological) transitivity, limit sets, (non-)wandering points, periodic and almost periodic points, minimality, mixing, but there are others. The versatility of the notion comes both from the nature and the relative position of the sets
M,N
and from the requirements on the size of the correspondent recurrent set.

To study more general and subtle notions of (local or partial) symmetry, in the last decades groups have been replaced by more general mathematical objects as groupoids or inverse semigroups. Even in the setting of groups, partial actions are an important way to encode imperfect symmetries. One important application (which has been our initial motivation) is towards the theory of
C^{*}
-algebras, but we will not refer to this in the present paper.

Dynamical notions of such general type of actions have mostly been introduced in a non-systematic ad hoc manner. In
[
8
]
the topological dynamics of groupoid actions has been treated in a more consistent way, but only the basic theory has been developed. We are also interested in a similar project for inverse semigroup actions. In starting this project, we become aware of a certain intricacy of the topic and especially of the fact that well-known connections of the inverse semigroup actions with other mathematical notions may be a fruitful technical tool. Actually the present short article is devoted merely to explore these connections in the setting of recurrence, and the development of the theory itself is postponed to a further publication.

We are interested in triples
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a (discrete) inverse semigroup,
\Sigma
is a topological space and the action
\theta
is composed of a family of homeomorphisms
\{\theta_{s}\!\mid\!s\in\mathcal{S}\}
between open subsets of
\Sigma
, having suitable properties. The precise definition and the basic theory is presented in Section
2
, but let us just note three peculiar features that are not present for groups: (a) The transformations
\theta_{s}
are not defined everywhere. (b) Two transformations
\theta_{s}
and
\theta_{t}
may act in the same way on an open subset of the intersection of their domains. (c) The composition
\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t}
has to be defined in a precise (maximal) way. We speak of a (genuine) action of
\mathcal{S}
if this composition equals
\theta_{st}
. Actually, to include the concept of partial group action, guided by
[
2
]
, we allow partial inverse semigroup actions, in which
\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t}
is only required to be a restriction of
\theta_{st}
.

By analogy to (
1.1
), one can define recurrence sets of the form

\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{M}^{N}:=\big\{s\in\mathcal{S}\,\big|\,\theta_{s}(M)\cap N\neq\emptyset\big\}\,.

(1.2)

Since it is easier, and since sharp results will be obtained for this situation, let us restrict to the case when
M=\{\sigma\}
is a one-point set. As a consequence of (a) , it is understood that only elements
s
for which
\theta_{s}
is defined in
\sigma
are candidates in (
1.2
) . On the other hand, because of (b), the set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
seems redundant. This could be important, since for some dynamical properties its size (infinite, complement of a finite set, syndetic) should play an important role. This is why, besides the ”naive” set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
we also define in (
2.7
) a quotient notion
\mathscr{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
, that is more suited to the developments we have in view in the future. Comparing these two types of recurrence sets with others, attached to related mathematical structures, is the main goal of the paper.

In
[
5
]
, to each group
{\sf G}
one attaches an inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{{\sf G}}
, suitably generated by
{\sf G}
, such that the partial actions of
{\sf G}
transform into actions of
\mathcal{S}_{\sf G}
, the procedure satisfying a certain universal property. In
[
11
]
and
[
3
]
this is extended to partial inverse semigroup actions: One starts with an inverse semigroup
\mathcal{A}
, generates another inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
satisfying certain relations, and then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the partial actions of
\mathcal{A}
and the genuine actions of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
. In Section
3
we study the fate of orbits and recurrence sets under this correspondence of actions.

In Section
4
we work with quotients
\mathcal{S}/_{\!\approx}
of
\mathcal{S}
through
idempotent pure congruences
. When
\mathcal{S}
is
E
-unitary, and
\approx
is the minimum group congruence, then
\mathcal{S}/_{\!\approx}
is actually
the maximal homomorphic group image of

\mathcal{S}
. By
[
9
]
, there is a canonical way to transform a partial action of
\mathcal{S}
into a partial action of
\mathcal{S}/_{\!\approx}
in the same topological space (the case of genuine actions is simpler). We show that the two connected actions have the same invariant sets, and we find relations between the respective recurrence sets.

In Sections
5
and
6
we use well-known connections between inverse semigroups and étale groupoids, looking for their effect on recurrence sets. In
[
8
]
a rather detailed study of recurrence and related dynamical properties for continuous groupoid actions can be found, so the results of these two sections will be combined in a future work to convert available groupoid information in terms of inverse semigroup action. But more work will be needed to have a comprehensive theory.

To an inverse semigroup partial action one associates its
groupoid of germs
; there is a perfect correspondence of invariant sets. Natural recurrence sets (see Definition
5.4
) may be expressed in pure groupoid lenguaje, as (maybe non-invariant) reductions, as shown in Proposition
5.5
. Their connection with the two types of recurrence sets of the initial partial action are then subject of Propositions
5.7
and
5.9
.

Section
6
. For a groupoid action on a topological space, Definition
6.4
indicates the natural recurrence sets. If the groupoid is étale, there is a canonical inverse semigroup formed of bisections. The actions of the groupoid generate actions of this inverse semigroup. Theorem
6.8
and Proposition
6.9
indicate the way the relevant recurrece sets are linked.

The results on recurrent sets, in all the situations we treat, show a clear preference for
\mathscr{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
upon
\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
. The first set is each time in a one-to-one correspondence with the analog set of the related structure. The second one is only mapped surjectively. All the maps defining the connections seem interesting.

2
Inverse semigroup partial actions and their recurrence sets

For general facts on inverse semigroups and their partial actions we refer mainly to
[
10
,
12
,
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An inverse semigroup
is a semigroup
\mathcal{S}
such that for every
s\in\mathcal{S}
there is a
unique
element
s^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{S}
satisfying

s^{\sharp}ss^{\sharp}=s^{\sharp},\quad ss^{\sharp}s=s\,.

We are always going to assume that
\mathcal{S}
is discrete and infinite. In an inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}
a partial order relation is defined by

s\leq t\quad{\rm if\ and\ only\ if}\quad s=ts^{\sharp}s\,.

On the commutative subsemigroup of idempotents
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
this reduces to
e=fe
.
The order may be reformulated:

s\leq t\,\Leftrightarrow\,\exists\,e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})\,,\,s=et\,\Leftrightarrow\,\exists\,f\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})\,,\,s=tf\,.

(2.1)

{\rm PHomeo}(\Sigma)
, the family of all the homeomorphisms between open subsets of
\Sigma
, is an inverse semigroup with obvious composition and inversion, the order relation being now just the restriction of functions.

Definition 2.2
.

A partial action
of the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}
in the topological space
\Sigma
is a family

\theta:=\Big\{\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\!\overset{\theta_{s}}{\longrightarrow}\Sigma_{s}\,\Big|\,s\in\mathcal{S}\Big\}\,,

where
\theta_{s}:\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\!\equiv{\rm dom}(\theta_{s})\!\to\Sigma_{s}\equiv{\rm im}(\theta_{s})
is a homeomorphism between open subsets of
\Sigma
, satisfying for every
s,t\in\mathcal{S}
:

(i)

\theta_{s^{\sharp}}=\theta_{s}^{-1}
,

(ii)

\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t}
is a restriction of
\theta_{st}
,

(iii)

if
s\leq t
then
\theta_{s}
is a restriction of
\theta_{t}
,

(iv)

\Sigma=\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}\!\Sigma_{e}
(non-degeneracy).

We speak of a
(genuine) action
whenever

\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t}=\theta_{st}\,,\,\forall\,s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

Note that (ii) implies that

\Sigma_{t}\cap\theta_{t}\big(\Sigma_{s}\big)\subset\Sigma_{ts}\,,\quad\forall\,s,t\in\mathcal{S},

(2.2)

from which one deduces immediately

\Sigma_{t^{\sharp}}\subset\Sigma_{t^{\sharp}t}\,,\quad\Sigma_{t}\subset\Sigma_{tt^{\sharp}}\,,\quad\forall\,t\in\mathcal{S}.

In addition,
\theta_{e}={\rm id}_{\Sigma_{e}}
for any
e\in{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{S})
. We will often use the notation

\theta_{s}(\sigma)\equiv s\diamond\sigma\equiv s\diamond_{\theta}\!\sigma.

We define now several dynamical notions. It is useful to set

\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}:=\big\{s\in\mathcal{S}\,\big|\,\sigma\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\!={\rm dom}(\theta_{s})\big\}

for the family of points of
\mathcal{S}
which can be applied to
\sigma
. In virtue of (
2.2
)

t\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,,\,s\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,t\diamond\sigma\}\,\Rightarrow\,st\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,.

An equivalence relation is defined in
\Sigma
by

\sigma\overset{\theta}{\sim}\tau\quad{\rm if\ and\ only\ if}\quad\exists\,s\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\ \,{\rm with}\ \,\theta_{s}(\sigma)=\tau.

(2.3)

Consequently, one has usual notions as
orbit, orbit closure, invariant subset
, etc.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
be an inverse semigroup action.
The naïve recurrence set assigned to
M,N\subset\Sigma
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\!:

\displaystyle=\big\{s\in\mathcal{S}\,\big|\,\theta_{s}(M)\cap N\neq\emptyset\big\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\big\{s\in\mathcal{S}\,\big|\,\exists\,\sigma\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\cap M,\ \theta_{s}(\sigma)\in N\big\}\,.

Clearly, if
s\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}
and
s\leq t
then
t\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}
. In the case
M=\{\sigma\}
and
N=\{\tau\}
(with simplified notations)

r,s,t\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{\tau}_{\sigma}\,\Rightarrow s^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\tau}^{\sigma}\ \,{\rm and}\ \,rs^{\sharp}t\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{\tau}_{\sigma}\,.

In particular
\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{\sigma}_{\sigma}
is an inverse subsemigroup of
\mathcal{S}
, and
\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}
is contained in
\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}=\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{\Sigma}
for every
N\subset\Sigma
.

Definition 2.4
.

We introduce an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
by

s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t\quad{\rm if\ and\ only\ if}\quad\exists\,r\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,,\ r\leq s,t\,.

(2.5)

The quotient space is denoted by
\mathscr{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}:=\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}/_{\!\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}}
. The equivalence class of
s
will be denoted by
\langle s\rangle_{\sigma}^{\theta}
or by
\pi_{\sigma}^{\theta}(s)
, giving rise to the quotient map

\pi_{\sigma}^{\theta}:\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\mathscr{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,.

(2.6)

Remark 2.5
.

It follows from the definition that if
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
then
\theta_{s}(\sigma)=\theta_{t}(\sigma)
(the converse fails in general). Concerning the connection between
\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}
and the minimum group congruence
\leftrightarrow
, see below.

The next definition is meant to eliminate redundant contributions to recurrence.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
be an inverse semigroup action.
The recurrence set assigned to
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
N\subset\Sigma
is

\mathscr{S}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\!:=\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\!/_{\!\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}}=\big\{\langle s\rangle^{\theta}_{\sigma}\,\big|\,s\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\big\}\,.

(2.7)

A standard notion
[
10
,
12
]
is the following:

Definition 2.7
.

The maximum group homomorphic image
of the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}
is the quotient
\mathcal{S}/_{\!\leftrightarrow}
of
\mathcal{S}
through
the minimum group congruence

s\leftrightarrow t\,\Leftrightarrow\,\exists\,r\in\mathcal{S}\,,\,r\leq s,t\,.

(2.8)

The class of
s\in\mathcal{S}
is denoted by
\langle s\rangle
or by
\pi(s)
.

Remark 2.8
.

The congruence
\leftrightarrow
is intrinsic to
\mathcal{S}
. If
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
is a partial action, on every set
\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
we have defined the equivalence relation
\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}
in (
2.5
) , which does depend on the given action. If
s,t\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
for some
\sigma
, then
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
implies
s\leftrightarrow t
, but the opposite implication generally fails, since in (
2.8
)
r
is not bound to belong to
\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
. In general the restriction of the equivalence relation
\leftrightarrow
to
\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
is weaker than
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
. However, see Lemma
2.9
.

Recall that the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}
is called
E
-unitary
if every element larger than an idempotent must be an idempotent. This can be restated in several ways, for instance: if
e\leftrightarrow s
and
e
is idempotent, then
s
must also be idempotent. For us, an important fact is the following result, that we did not find in the literature:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
be the partial action of an
E
-unitary inverse semigroup. If
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
s,t\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
, then

s\leftrightarrow t\,\Leftrightarrow\,s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t\,.

Proof.

As mentioned in Remark
2.8
, we only need to check
\Rightarrow
. So let
s,t\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
such that
s\leftrightarrow t
. From
[
10
, Pag. 25, 66]
it follows that they are
compatible
, i.e.
s^{\sharp}t
and
st^{\sharp}
are idempotents, and
s\wedge t:=st^{\sharp}t=ts^{\sharp}s
is the infimum of
s
and
t
with respect to
\leq
. The domain of
\theta_{s\wedge t}
contains

{\rm dom}\big(\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t^{\sharp}}\circ\theta_{t}\big)={\rm dom}\big(\theta_{s}\big)\cap{\rm dom}\big(\theta_{t}\big)=\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\cap\Sigma_{t^{\sharp}}\ni\sigma\,,

so
\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\ni s\wedge t\leq s,t
, thus
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
be the partial action of an inverse semigroup and let
M,N\subset\Sigma
. Then the recurrence set
\mathscr{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}
may be introduced directly as the quotient of the naïve recurrence set
\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}
through the minimum group congruence
\leftrightarrow
:

\mathscr{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\!:=\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}/_{\!\leftrightarrow}\,.

If
\mathcal{S}
is
E
-unitary, this may be written in terms of (
2.7
) as

\mathscr{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}=\!\bigcup_{\sigma\in M}\!\mathscr{S}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}.

3
Inverse semigroup expansions

Following
[
3
]
, we describe briefly
the prefix expansion inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
associated to an inverse semigroup

\mathcal{A}
. A different point of view can be found in
[
11
]
; the group case appeared in
[
5
]
(see also
[
7
]
).
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
is generated by elements
[a]
, where
a\in\mathcal{A}
, subject to the relations

(i)\ \,[a^{\sharp}][a][b]=[a^{\sharp}][ab]\,,\quad(ii)\ \,[a][b][b^{\sharp}]=[ab][b^{\sharp}]\,,\quad(iii)\ \,[a][a^{\sharp}][a]=[a]\,.

Buss and Exel show that there is an involutive anti-automorphism
\,{}^{\#}\!:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
such that
[a]^{\#}\!=[a^{\sharp}]
for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
. In fact
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
is an inverse semigroup; it is
E
-unitary if and only if
\mathcal{A}
is
E
-unitary (in this case Lemma
2.9
applies). The elements
\epsilon_{a}:=[a][a^{\sharp}]=[a][a]^{\#}
are commuting idempotents. It is also shown that every element of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
has a unique
normal form

\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]\,,\quad c_{1},\dots c_{n},a\in\mathcal{A},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\,,

(3.1)

under the conditions
c_{1}c_{1}^{\sharp}=\dots=c_{n}c_{n}^{\sharp}=aa^{\sharp}
and
a,aa^{\sharp}\in\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}
. It is useful to use the notations

\iota:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\,,\quad\iota(a):=[a]\,,

q:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{A}\,,\quad q\big(\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]\big)\!:=a\,.

Clearly
\iota
is a section of
q
.

The main property of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
is its
universal property
: If
\mathcal{T}
is an inverse semigroup and
\psi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{T}
is a map satisfying for all elements
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
the conditions

\psi(a^{\sharp})\psi(a)\psi(b)=\psi(a^{\sharp})\psi(ab)\,,\quad\psi(a)\psi(b)\psi(b^{\sharp})=\psi(ab)\psi(b^{\sharp})\,,\quad\psi(a)\psi(a^{\sharp})\psi(a)=\psi(a)\,,

there is a unique inverse semigroup morphism
\Psi:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{T}
such that
\Psi([a])=\psi(a)
for each
a\in\mathcal{A}
.

It is then easy to see that there is a one-to-one correspondence between partial actions of the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{A}
and (genuine) actions of its prefix expansion inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
(in the same topological space
\Sigma
).

Proposition 3.1
.

The two actions have the same invariant sets.

Proof.

Start with the partial action
(\mathcal{A},\vartheta,\Sigma)
and denote by
\theta:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm PHomeo}(\Sigma)
the associated inverse semigroup action. For an element in normal form (
3.1
) one has

\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]}=\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}}\!\circ\dots\circ\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{n}}}\!\circ\vartheta_{a}\,.

(3.2)

Since
\epsilon_{c_{k}}
is an idempotent,
\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{k}}}
is the identity map on its domain. Consequently, for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
belonging to the (common) domain

{\rm dom}\big(\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]}\big)=\Sigma_{a^{\sharp}}\cap\vartheta_{a}^{-1}\big(\Sigma_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}}\cap\dots\cap\Sigma_{\epsilon_{c_{n}}}\big)

(3.3)

of the two transformations in (
3.2
), one has

\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}\!\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]}(\sigma)=\vartheta_{a}(\sigma)\,.

From this the conclusion can be deduced easily.
∎

Let us now relate the recurrence sets of the partial inverse semigroup action
\vartheta
to the two types of recurrence sets of the associated prefix expansion inverse semigroup action
\theta
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\theta:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm PHomeo}(\Sigma)
be the inverse semigroup action attached to the partial action
(\mathcal{A},\vartheta,\Sigma)
and let
M,N\subset\Sigma
. Then

\iota\big[\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{M}^{N}\big]=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\cap\iota(\mathcal{A})\,,

(3.4)

q\Big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\Big)=\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{M}^{N}\,.

(3.5)

Proof.

One has
s=[a]\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\cap\,\iota(\mathcal{A})
if and only if there is some
\sigma\in M\cap\Sigma_{[a]^{\#}}\!=M\cap\Sigma_{a^{\sharp}}
with
\theta_{[a]}(\sigma)=\vartheta_{a}(\sigma)\in N
, and this means exactly that
[a]=\iota(a)\in\iota\big[\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{M}^{N}\big]
. Thus (
3.4
) is proven.

The relation (
3.5
) follows by analyzing the definitions, or we may write

\displaystyle q\Big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\Big)

\displaystyle=q\Big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\Big)\cap q\big(\iota(\mathcal{A})\big)

\displaystyle=q\Big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\cap\iota(\mathcal{A})\Big)

\displaystyle=q\Big(\iota\big[\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{M}^{N}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{M}^{N}\,.

We used the fact that
\iota
is a section of
q
, equation (
3.4
) and the fact that
\iota(\mathcal{A})
is saturated (to distribute
q
to the intersection).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\theta:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm PHomeo}(\Sigma)
be the inverse semigroup action attached to the partial action
(\mathcal{A},\vartheta,\Sigma)
and let
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

(i)

The map
q:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{A}
restricts to a surjection
q_{\sigma}\!:\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\mathcal{A}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
.

(ii)

The map

Q_{\sigma}:\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\{\theta,\sigma\}\!:=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\{\theta,\sigma\}/_{\!\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}}\to\mathcal{A}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}\,,\quad Q_{\sigma}\big[\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}(s)\big]:=q_{\sigma}(s)

is a well-defined bijection. One has bijectively

Q_{\sigma}\big[\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big]=\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{\sigma}^{N},\quad\forall\,N\subset\Sigma\,.

(3.6)

Proof.

(i)
As mentioned above, the domain of
\theta_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]}
is
\Sigma_{a^{\sharp}}\cap\,\vartheta_{a}^{-1}\big(\Sigma_{\epsilon_{c_{1}}}\cap\dots\cap\Sigma_{\epsilon_{c_{n}}}\big)
, contained in
\Sigma_{a^{\sharp}}
, the domain of
\vartheta_{a}
, where
a=q\big(\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]\big)
. This shows that
q\big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\{\theta,\sigma\}\big)\subset\mathcal{A}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
. Surjectivity is obvious: if
a\in\mathcal{A}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
then
[a]\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\{\theta,\sigma\}
and
q([a])=a
.

(ii)
The statement about
Q_{\sigma}
relies on showing that
q_{\sigma}(s)=q_{\sigma}(t)
if and only if
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
; this proves both the correctness of the definition and the injectvity. (Surjectivity follows from the fact that
q_{\sigma}
is onto.) Using normal forms

s:=\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}[a]\,,\quad t:=\epsilon_{d_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{d_{m}}[b]\,,

the assumption
q_{\sigma}(s)=q_{\sigma}(t)
reads
a=b
. Then

r\!:=\epsilon_{c_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{c_{n}}\epsilon_{d_{1}}\dots\epsilon_{d_{m}}[a]\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}

satisfies
r\leq s,t
. From (
3.3
) one gets easily

\sigma\in{\rm dom}\big(\theta_{r}\big)={\rm dom}\big(\theta_{s}\big)\cap{\rm dom}\big(\theta_{t}\big)\,,

so
r\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\{\theta,\sigma\}
and we get
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
. For the inverse implication, using (
2.1
) and normal forms, one sees that
r\leq s
implies
q(r)=q(s)
. Then
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
implies
r\leq s,t
for some
r
, therefore
q(t)=q(r)=q(s)
. The relation (
3.6
) follows from previous results and definitions:

\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)_{\sigma}^{N}\overset{\eqref{babulic}}{=}q\Big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\Big)=Q_{\sigma}\Big[\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}\Big(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\Big)\Big]\overset{\eqref{reciurset}}{=}Q_{\sigma}\Big[\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{A}}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\Big]\,.

∎

4
Quotients through idempotent pure congruences

Let us fix an
idempotent pure congruence

\approx
in
\mathcal{S}
. By definition
[
10
]
, this means that
\approx
is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{S}
such that

s\approx t\,,\ u\approx v\ \Rightarrow\ su\approx tv,

s\approx e\,,\ e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})\ \Rightarrow\ s\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})\,.

We set
p(s)
for the
\approx
-equivalence class of
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Note that
p:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}/_{\!\approx}
is an inverse semigroup epimorphism, where the inversion in the quotient is
p(s)^{\dagger}\!:=p\big(s^{\sharp}\big)
. It is convenient to set
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{S}/_{\!\approx}
. A crucial fact (
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
) is that
if
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
is a partial action of the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}
, there exists a unique partial action
\big(\mathcal{R},\vartheta,\Sigma\big)
such that

•

\Sigma^{\vartheta}_{a}=\bigcup_{p(s)=a}\Sigma^{\theta}_{s}\,,\ \forall\,a\in\mathcal{R}
,

•

\vartheta_{p(s)}(\sigma)=\theta_{s}(\sigma)\,,\ \forall\,\sigma\in\Sigma^{\theta}_{s^{\sharp}}\big(\!\subset\Sigma^{\vartheta}_{p(s)^{\dagger}}\big)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{S},\theta,\Sigma)
and
\big(\mathcal{R},\vartheta,\Sigma\big)
as above.

(i)

The two partial actions have the same orbits and the same invariant sets.

(ii)

For every
\sigma\in\Sigma
the map
p:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{R}
restricts to a surjection
p_{\sigma}\!:\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\mathcal{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
. For
N\subset\Sigma
one has
p_{\sigma}\big[\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big]=\mathcal{R}(\vartheta)_{\sigma}^{N}
.

(iv)

The map

P_{\sigma}:\mathscr{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\!:=\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}/_{\!\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}}\to\mathscr{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}:=\mathcal{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}/_{\!\overset{\vartheta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}}\,,\quad P_{\sigma}\big[\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}(s)\big]:=\pi^{\vartheta}_{\sigma}\big[p_{\sigma}(s)\big]

is a well-defined surjection. For
N\subset\Sigma
one has

P_{\sigma}\big[\mathscr{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big]=\mathscr{R}(\vartheta)_{\sigma}^{N}.

(4.1)

Proof.

(i)
follow easily from the definitions and from the way
\vartheta
has been constructed out of
\theta
.

For
(ii)
, we have

a\in\mathcal{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\sigma\in\Sigma^{\vartheta}_{a^{\dagger}}\!=\bigcup_{s\in a}\Sigma^{\theta}_{s^{\sharp}}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\exists\,s\in a\,,\sigma\in\,\Sigma^{\theta}_{s^{\sharp}}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\exists\,s\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,,\,p(s)=a\,.

Including the set
N
in the arguments is trivial.

(iii)
It is clear that
P_{\sigma}
is onto. One still has to check that if
s,t\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
and
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
then
p_{\sigma}(s)\overset{\vartheta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}p_{\sigma}(t)
, ensuring that
P_{\sigma}
is well-defined. This follows easily from the definition of the equivalences, since a homomorphism of inverse semigroups preserves the orderings. Then (
4.1
) is a consequence of the previous definitions and results:

\mathscr{R}(\vartheta)_{\sigma}^{N}=\pi^{\vartheta}_{\sigma}\big[\mathcal{R}(\vartheta)^{N}_{\sigma}\big]=\pi^{\vartheta}_{\sigma}\big[p_{\sigma}\big(\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big)\big]=P_{\sigma}\big[\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}\big(\mathcal{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big)\big]=P_{\sigma}\big[\mathscr{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big]\,.

∎

Example 4.2
.

Let us suppose now that
\mathcal{S}
is a
E
-unitary inverse semigroup. It is known then
[
10
, Sect. 2.4]
that
\leftrightarrow
is an idempotent pure congruence, that we use instead of
\approx
in the above arguments. In addition
\mathcal{R}:=\mathcal{S}/_{\!\leftrightarrow}
is a group (the maximum group homomorphic image of
\mathcal{S}
). To the partial action
\theta
of
\mathcal{S}
on
\Sigma
we associate as above the partial action
\vartheta
of
\mathcal{R}
in
\Sigma
. Since now
\mathcal{R}
is a group, the equivalence relation
\overset{\vartheta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}
on
\mathcal{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
is just the equality, so
\mathscr{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}=\mathcal{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
. By Theorem
4.1
one has the well-defined surjection
P_{\sigma}:\mathscr{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\mathscr{R}\{\vartheta,\sigma\}
. In fact,
it is even a bijection
. To show this, one has to check for
s,t\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}
that
p_{\sigma}(s)=p_{\sigma}(t)
implies that
\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}(s)=\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}(s)
. Taking the definitions into consideration, this amounts to
s\leftrightarrow t\,\Rightarrow\,s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
. This is solved by Lemma
2.9
. Consequently, in this case,
the recurrence sets
\mathscr{S}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
and
\mathscr{R}(\vartheta)_{\sigma}^{N}
are in a one-to-one correspondence for every
N\subset\Sigma
.

5
From partial actions of inverse semigroups to groupoids

Groupoids
are small categories in which all the arrows are inverible. The object part of such a category
\Xi
is also called
the unit space
and denoted by
\Xi^{(0)}\!\equiv X
.

The source and range maps
, denoted by
{\rm d,r}:\Xi\to\Xi^{(0)}
, define the family of composable pairs

\Xi^{(2)}\!=\{(\xi,\eta)\!\mid\!{\rm d}(\xi)={\rm r}(\eta)\}\!\subset\Xi\times\Xi\,.

For
M,N\subset X
one sets

\Xi_{M}\!:={\rm d}^{-1}(M)\,,\quad\Xi^{N}\!:={\rm r}^{-1}(M)\,,\quad\Xi_{M}^{N}:=\Xi_{M}\cap\Xi^{N},

(5.1)

with the particular notations
\Xi_{x}\equiv\Xi_{\{x\}}
and
\Xi^{x}\equiv\Xi^{\{x\}}
.

A topological groupoid
is a groupoid
\Xi
with a topology such that the inversion
\xi\mapsto\xi^{-1}
and multiplication
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\xi\eta
are continuous.

Following
[
2
]
, we now define and use
the groupoid of germs
associated to the partial action
\theta
of the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}
on the topological space
\Sigma
. (For the case of genuine actions, see
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
and references therein.) In this generality, this also covers the transformation groupoids of partial group actions from
[
5
]
. First define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\,\square\,\Sigma\!:

\displaystyle=\big\{(s,\sigma)\in\mathcal{S}\!\times\!\Sigma\,\big|\,\sigma\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\big\}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\big\{(s,\sigma)\in\mathcal{S}\!\times\!\Sigma\,\big|\,s\in\mathcal{S}\{\theta,\sigma\}\big\}\,.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
(s,\sigma),(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{S}\,\square\,\Sigma
are
germ equivalent
, and write
(s,\sigma)\overset{\theta}{\rightleftharpoons}(t,\tau)
, if
\sigma=\tau
and
s\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}t
.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that
\overset{\theta}{\leftrightharpoons}
is an equivalence relation, that depends on the partial action
\theta
. However, when
\mathcal{S}
is
E
-unitary, by Lemma
2.9
, it becomes independent of
\theta
.
In the case of partial group actions,
\leftrightharpoons
is just the equality.

The quotient
\mathcal{S}\,\square\,\Sigma/_{\!\overset{\theta}{\leftrightharpoons}}
is also denoted as
\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma
.
Its generic elements are denoted by
\langle s,\sigma\rangle\!:=[(s,\sigma)]_{\overset{\theta}{\leftrightharpoons}}
. Then
\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma
is a groupoid with unit space
\Sigma
, with the well-defined algebraic structure

\mathfrak{d}\big(\langle s,\sigma\rangle\big):=\sigma\,,\quad\mathfrak{r}\big(\langle s,\sigma\rangle\big):=\theta_{s}(\sigma)\,,

(5.3)

\big\langle t,\theta_{s}(\sigma)\big\rangle\langle s,\sigma\rangle:=\langle ts,\sigma\rangle\,,\quad\langle s,\sigma\rangle^{\star}\!:=\big\langle s^{\sharp},\theta_{s}(\sigma)\big\rangle\,.

Note that
\langle e,\sigma\rangle
is independent of
e\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
if
\sigma\in\Sigma_{e}
, hence it will be identified with
\sigma
.

One defines a topology on
\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma
as follows: Given
s\in\mathcal{S}
and an open set
U\subset\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}
, one sets

\langle s,U\rangle:=\big\{\langle s,\sigma\rangle\in\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma\,\big|\,\sigma\in U\big\}\,.

The family of all these sets forms a basis for a topology that makes
\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma
an étale topological groupoid.

We are going to use systematically groupoid actions in the next section. One only needs here a particular case (the canonical action, cf. Example
6.2
). For a topological groupoid
\Xi
with unit space
\Sigma
, we write
\xi\ast{\rm d}(\xi):={\rm r}(\xi)
for every
\xi\in\Xi
. This may be reformulated as
\xi\ast\sigma:=\xi\sigma\xi^{-1}
if
\sigma={\rm d}(\xi)
. Orbit equivalence is denoted by
\sigma\overset{\Xi}{\sim}\tau
, and it means that
\sigma={\rm d}(\xi)
and
\tau={\rm r}(\xi)
for some
\xi\in\Xi
, i.e.
\Xi_{\sigma}^{\tau}\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Use the shorthand
\,\Xi\equiv\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma
and consider its canonical action on its unit space. One has
\sigma\overset{\Xi}{\sim}\tau
if and only if
\sigma\overset{\theta}{\sim}\tau
. Hence the orbits in the unit space of the germ groupoid coincide with the orbits of the initial inverse semigroup partial action. Invariant subsets are the same.

Proof.

By (
2.3
),
\sigma\overset{\theta}{\sim}\tau
if and only if
\theta_{s}(\sigma)=\tau
for some
s\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\sigma\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}
. Equivalently,
\langle s,\sigma\rangle\ast\sigma=\tau
. Indeed, the only not-straightforward piece is the computation

\langle s,\sigma\rangle\ast\sigma=\langle s,\sigma\rangle\langle s^{\sharp}s,\sigma\rangle\langle s,\sigma\rangle^{-1}=\langle ss^{\sharp}s,\sigma\rangle\langle s^{\sharp},\theta_{s}(\sigma)\rangle=\langle ss^{\sharp},\theta_{s}(\sigma)\rangle=\theta_{s}(\sigma)=\tau

and the proof is finished. The remaining assertions are direct consequences.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Let us set
\,\Gamma:2^{\mathcal{S}}\to 2^{\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\Sigma}

one has

\big(\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma\big)^{N}_{M}\subset\Gamma\big[\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\big]\,.

The inclusion may be strict. For a one-point set

, it is an equality:

\big(\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma\big)^{N}_{\sigma}\!=\Gamma\big[\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{\sigma}\big]\,.

Proof.

For
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma
we have

\displaystyle\langle s,\tau\rangle\in\big(\mathcal{S}\triangleright_{\theta}\!\Sigma\big)^{N}_{M}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \tau\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\,,\ \mathfrak{d}\langle s,\tau\rangle\in M\,,\ \mathfrak{r}\langle s,\tau\rangle\in N

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\ \tau\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\,,\ \theta_{s}(M)\cap N\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \tau\in\Sigma_{s^{\sharp}}\,,\ s\in\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \langle s,\tau\rangle\in\Gamma\big[\mathcal{S}(\theta)^{N}_{M}\big]\,.

We indicate briefly the connection between Propositions

6
From groupoid actions to inverse semigroup actions

Definition 6.1
.

A continuous groupoid action

(\Xi,\rho,\kappa,\Sigma)
consists of a topological groupoid
\Xi
, a topological space
\Sigma
, a continuous surjective map
\rho:\Sigma\rightarrow X
(
the anchor
) and the continuous action map

\kappa:\Xi\!\Join\!\Sigma:=\{(\xi,\sigma)\!\mid\!{\rm d}(\xi)=\rho(\sigma)\}\ni(\xi,\sigma)\mapsto{\kappa_{\xi}(\sigma)\equiv\xi\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma}\in\Sigma

satisfying the axioms:

1.

\rho(\sigma)\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma=\sigma,\,\forall\,\sigma\in\Sigma
,

2.

if
(\xi,\eta)\in\Xi^{(2)}
and
(\eta,\sigma)\in\Xi\!\Join\!\Sigma
, then
(\xi,\eta\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma)\in\Xi\!\Join\!\Sigma
and
(\xi\eta)\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma=\xi\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!(\eta\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma)
.

If the action
\kappa
is understood, we will write
\xi\bullet\sigma
instead of
\xi\bullet_{\kappa}\sigma
. The theory is developed in
[
13
]
.

Example 6.2
.

Each topological groupoid acts continuously in a canonical way on its unit space:
\xi
sends
{\rm d}(\xi)
into
{\rm r}(\xi)
. Here
\Sigma=X
and
\rho={\rm id}_{X}
, and then (special notation)
\xi\!\ast\!x:=\xi x\xi^{-1}
if
{\rm d}(\xi)=x
.

Example 6.3
.

The topological groupoid
\Xi
also acts on itself, with
\Sigma:=\Xi
,
\rho:={\rm r}
and
\xi\bullet\eta:=\xi\eta
.

For
\xi\in\Xi\,,\,{\sf A},{\sf B}\subset\Xi\,,\,M\subset\Sigma
we use the notations

{\sf A}{\sf B}:=\big\{\xi\eta\,\big|\,\xi\in{\sf A}\,,\,\eta\in{\sf B}\,,\,{\rm d}(\xi)={\rm r}(\eta)\big\}\,,

(6.1)

{\sf A}\bullet M:=\big\{\xi\bullet\sigma\,\big|\,\xi\in{\sf A}\,,\sigma\in M\,,{\rm d}(\xi)=\rho(\sigma)\big\}=\bigcup_{\xi\in{\sf A}}\xi\bullet M\,.

A subset
M\subset\Sigma
is called

if
\xi\bullet M\subset M
, for every
\xi\in\Xi
. Particular cases are
the orbits

\mathfrak{O}_{\sigma}\!:=\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}\!\bullet\sigma
and
the orbit closures

\overline{\mathfrak{O}}_{\sigma}
.
The orbit equivalence relation
will be denoted by
\overset{\kappa}{\sim}
or by
\overset{\bullet}{\sim}
.

Definition 6.4
.

For every
M,N\subset\Sigma
one defines
the recurrence set

\widetilde{\Xi}_{M}^{N}=\{\xi\in\Xi\!\mid\!(\xi\bullet M)\cap N\neq\emptyset\}\,.

Remark 6.5
.

Note that

\widetilde{\Xi}_{M}^{N}\subset\Xi_{\rho(M)}^{\rho(N)}
. When
\rho
is also injective, one has
\,\widetilde{\Xi}_{M}^{N}=\Xi_{\rho(M)}^{\rho(N)}
. In Exemple
6.2
one has
\widetilde{\Xi}_{M}^{N}=\Xi_{M}^{N}=\{\xi\in\Xi\!\mid\!{\rm d}(\xi)\in M,\,{\rm r}(\xi)\in N\}
, making the connection with the previous section.

A bisection
of the topological groupoid
\Xi
is an open subset of the groupoid on which the restrictions of both
{\rm d}
and
{\rm r}
are injective. We recall that the groupoid
\Xi
is called
étale
if
{\rm d}:\Xi\to\Xi
is a local homeomorphism. If
\Xi
is étale,
X\equiv\Xi^{(0)}
is a clopen (closed and open) subset of
\Xi
, all the fibres
\Xi^{x}
and
\Xi_{x}
are discrete, and
{\rm d},{\rm r}
and the multiplication are open maps.

Let
\Xi
be an étale groupoid over the unit space

and let

be
the inverse semigroup associated to

, formed of bisections (they form a basis of clopen sets for the topology of

) . The multiplication is given by (

, under which

\mathcal{E}[{\rm Bis}(\Xi)]={\rm Top}(X)
is formed exactly of the open subsets of the unit space,

being the unit and

the zero element.

.

Let

\rho(\sigma)\in{\rm d}({\sf A})
and
\xi_{\rho(\sigma)}^{\sf A}\bullet\sigma=\tau
. This is equivalent with
\sigma\overset{\kappa}{\sim}\tau
, taking into account the defining property of a bisection. The remaining assertion of the proposition follows from this.
∎

To simplify, we are going to use the notation
\mathcal{B}:={\rm Bis}(\Xi)
. Thus there are also notations as
\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}
,
\mathscr{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}
, for instance. We recall the quotient map (
2.6
), that in our concrete situation reads

\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}:\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\mathscr{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}:=\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}/_{\!\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}}\,.

We are looking for connections between different types of recurrence sets, attached to the actions, cf. Definitions
2.3
,
2.6
and
6.4
.

Theorem 6.8
.

The map

\dot{\delta}_{\sigma}:\mathscr{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\widetilde{\Xi}_{\sigma}^{\Sigma}=\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}\,,\quad\dot{\delta}_{\sigma}\big[\pi^{\theta}_{\sigma}({\sf A})\big]:=\xi^{\sf A}_{\rho(\sigma)}

is a well-defined bijection. For every
N\subset\Sigma
one has

\dot{\delta}_{\sigma}\big[\mathscr{B}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}\big]=\widetilde{\Xi}_{\sigma}^{N}.

(6.3)

Proof.

First set

\delta_{\sigma}:\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}\to\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}\,,\quad\delta_{\sigma}({\sf A}):=\xi^{\sf A}_{\rho(\sigma)}\,,

which is onto, since the bisections cover the groupoid. Let
{\sf A},{\sf B}\in\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}
, which means that
{\sf A},{\sf B}
are bisections and
\rho(\sigma)\in{\rm d}({\sf A})\cap{\rm d}({\sf B})
. The first statement of the Proposition follows if we check that

\delta_{\sigma}({\sf A})=\delta_{\sigma}({\sf B})\,\Leftrightarrow\ {\sf A}\overset{\theta}{\underset{\sigma}{\leftrightarrow}}{\sf B}\,.

(6.4)

It is easy to see for two bisections that
{\sf C}\leq{\sf D}
if and only if
{\sf C}\subset{\sf D}
. Using the definitions, we see that (
6.4
) reads in detail

\xi^{\sf A}_{\rho(\sigma)}\!=\xi^{\sf B}_{\rho(\sigma)}\,\Leftrightarrow\ \exists\,{\sf C}\subset{\sf A}\cap{\sf B}\ {\rm with}\ \rho(\sigma)\in{\rm d}({\sf C})\,.

(6.5)

The implication
\Rightarrow
in (
6.5
) is solved by taking
{\sf C}:={\sf A}\cap{\sf B}\ni\xi^{\sf A}_{\rho(\sigma)}\!=\xi^{\sf B}_{\rho(\sigma)}
. The other implication is trivial, by the unicity involved in the definition of the bisection
{\sf C}
.
Then (
6.3
) is a consequence of (
6.2
) and of the definitions of the recurrence sets
\mathscr{B}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{N}
and
\widetilde{\Xi}_{\sigma}^{N}
.
∎

The next result yields a second point of view upon the connection between recurrence sets. We set

\Delta:2^{\Xi}\to 2^{\mathcal{B}},\quad\Delta(E):=\{{\sf A}\in\mathcal{B}\!\mid\!{\sf A}\cap E\neq\emptyset\}\,.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
(\Xi,\rho,\kappa,\Sigma)
be an étale groupoid action and denote by
\big(\mathcal{B},\theta,\Sigma\big)
the associated inverse semigroup partial action, where
\mathcal{B}={\rm Bis}(\Xi)
.
Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
M,N\subset\Sigma
.

(i)

One has

\mathcal{B}(\theta)^{N}_{M}=\Delta\big(\widetilde{\Xi}^{N}_{M}\big)\,.

(6.6)

(ii)

Let us denote by
\Delta_{\sigma}:2^{\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}}\to 2^{\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}}
the restriction of
\Delta
to
2^{\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}}
. Then
\Delta_{\sigma}=\delta_{\sigma}^{-1}
(the last one regarded as a set function).

Proof.

(i)
For
{\sf A}\in\mathcal{B}
one may write

\displaystyle{\sf A}\in\mathcal{B}(\theta)^{N}_{M}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \theta_{A}(M)\cap N\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \exists\ \sigma\in M\cap\rho^{-1}[{\rm d}({\sf A})]\,,\ \theta_{\sf A}(\sigma)\in N

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \exists\ \sigma\in M\cap\rho^{-1}[{\rm d}({\sf A})]\,,\ \xi^{\sf A}_{\rho(\sigma)}\!\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma\in N

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \exists\ \sigma\in M\cap\rho^{-1}[{\rm d}({\sf A})]\,,\ \exists\,\xi\in{\sf A}\cap\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}\,,\ \xi\bullet_{\kappa}\!\sigma\in N

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \exists\,\xi\in{\sf A}\,,\ \big(\xi\bullet_{\kappa}\!M\big)\cap N\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ {\sf A}\cap\widetilde{\Xi}^{N}_{M}\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ {\sf A}\in\Delta\big(\widetilde{\Xi}^{N}_{M}\big)\,.

The forth equivalence is due to the uniqueness of an element
\xi
of the bisection
{\sf A}
such that
{\rm d}(\xi)=\rho(\sigma)
.

(ii)
One gets as a particular case of (
6.6
) the relation

\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}=\mathcal{B}(\theta)_{\sigma}^{\Sigma}=\Delta\big(\,\widetilde{\Xi}_{\sigma}\big)=\Delta\big(\,\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}\big)\,.

This and the fact that
\Delta
is increasing justifies the restriction
\Delta_{\sigma}
in the diagram. If
E\in 2^{\Xi_{\rho(\sigma)}}
then for every
\eta\in E
one has
{\rm d}(\eta)=\rho(\sigma)
, which justifies the non-trivial part of the third step below:

\displaystyle\delta_{\sigma}^{-1}(E)

\displaystyle=\big\{{\sf A}\in\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,\big|\,\delta_{\sigma}({\sf A})\in E\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{{\sf A}\in\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,\big|\,\xi^{\sf A}_{\rho(\sigma)}\!\in E\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{{\sf A}\subset\mathcal{B}\{\theta,\sigma\}\,\big|\,{\sf A}\cap E\neq\emptyset\big\}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\sigma}(E)\,.

∎

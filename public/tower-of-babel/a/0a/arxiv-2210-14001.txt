Abstract
. We study the Hodge standard conjecture for varieties over finite fields admitting a CM lifting, such as abelian varieties or products of K3 surfaces. For those varieties we show that the signature predicted by the conjecture holds true modulo 4 4 . This amounts to determining the discriminant and the Hilbert symbol of the intersection product. The first is obtained by ℓ \ell -adic arguments whereas the second needs a careful computation in p p -adic Hodge theory.



1.
Introduction

The standard conjecture of Hodge type predicts the signature of the intersection product of algebraic classes on a smooth projective variety. In this paper we study the discriminant and the Hilbert symbol of the intersection product and we show that
they coincide with those predicted by the conjecture for varieties over finite fields which admit a CM lifting, in particular for abelian varieties and for products of K3 surfaces.
This can be reformulated by saying that the expected signature holds true modulo
4
.

This conjecture was formulated by Grothendieck in the sixties
[
24
]
. It is modelled on positivity results such as the Hodge index theorem, the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations and the positivity of the Rosati involution. For a panorama on the history, the original motivation and potential applications of this conjecture see
[
4
,
3
,
27
]
.
This conjecture did not progress until the 21st century. Then, Milne showed that the classical Hodge conjecture for complex abelian varieties would imply the Hodge standard conjecture for abelian varieties in positive characteristic
[
36
]
and Ito studied the behaviour of this conjecture under blow-ups
[
28
]
.

Very recently, some progress has been made. The first author proved the conjecture for motives of rank two using
p
-adic Hodge theory
[
3
, Theorem 8.1]
. This result implies the conjecture for abelian fourfolds
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
and for some other abelian varieties
[
1
,
2
]
. On the other hand, Ito–Ito–Koshikawa proved the conjecture for the square of a K3 surface, using the Kuga–Satake construction and ultimately relying on the positivity of the Rosati involution
[
27
]
.

The purpose of this article is to push the
p
-adic methods initiated in
[
3
]
to CM motives of higher rank
and to describe completely the Hilbert symbol of the intersection product.
To be more precise, let us recall the formulation of the standard conjecture of Hodge type.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth, projective and geometrically connected variety of dimension
d
and let
L
be a hyperplane section of
X
. We denote by
\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
the finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic cycles on
X
of codimension
n
modulo numerical equivalence.
For
n\leq d/2
we define the space of primitive cycles as

\mathcal{Z}^{n,\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{num}}(X,L)_{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}},\hskip 5.69046pt\alpha\cdot L^{{d-2n+1}}=0\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{in}\hskip 5.69046pt\mathcal{Z}^{d-n+1}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\}

and we define the pairing

q_{X,L,n}:\mathcal{Z}^{n,\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{num}}(X,L)_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathcal{Z}^{n,\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{num}}(X,L)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}

via the intersection product

\alpha,\beta\mapsto(-1)^{n}\alpha\cdot\beta\cdot L^{d-2n}.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(Hodge standard conjecture,
[
24
, Conjecture 2]
.) Let
X
be a smooth, projective and geometrically connected variety of dimension
d
and let
L
be a hyperplane section of
X
. Then for all
n\leq d/2
, the quadratic form
q_{X,L,n}

is positive definite.

Remark 1.3
.

The original formulation of
[
24
]
is with cycles modulo homological equivalence. As another standard conjecture predicts that homological and numerical equivalence should coincide, the two formulations should be equivalent. See also
[
3
, 3.11-3.12]
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

(1)

Let
(X,L)
be an abelian variety over a finite field together with a hyperplane section induced by a CM lifting. Let
n\leq\dim(X)/2
be any integer and
(s_{+},s_{-})
be the signature of
q_{X,L,n}
. Then
s_{-}
is divisible by
4
.

(2)

Let
X=S_{1}\times\ldots\times S_{m}
be the product of K3 surfaces over a finite field,
L_{i}
be a hyperplane section induced by a CM lifting of
S_{i}
, cf.

[
26
]
, and let
L=\oplus_{i}p^{*}_{i}L_{i}
be the induced hyperplane section on
X
. Let
n\leq\dim(X)/2=m
be any integer and
(s_{+},s_{-})
be the signature of
q_{X,L,n}
. Then
s_{-}
is divisible by
4
.

The above theorem follows from a general result on motives endowed with quadratic forms (see Theorem
3.2
for the precise definitions).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be a CM motive in mixed characteristic with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that its special fiber
M_{p}
is supersingular and that
M
is endowed with a CM quadratic form
q
whose Betti realization is a polarisation of the underlying Hodge structure. Let
q_{Z}
be the restriction of
q
to all algebraic classes of
M_{p}
. Write
(s_{+},s_{-})
for the signature of
q_{Z}
, then
s_{-}
is divisible by
4
.

Let us give a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
For a quadratic form over
\mathbb{Q}
, the condition
4|s_{-}
is equivalent to the fact that the discriminant is positive and that the Hilbert symbol at infinity is
+1
. Both conditions can be checked by studying the quadratic form in the non-archimedean places, thanks to the classical product formula on Hilbert symbols. In particular, Theorem
1.5
boils down to the study of the quadratic form
q_{Z}
after its
\ell
-adic and crystalline realization. The
\ell
-adic computation is obtained from general results on
\ell
-adic cohomology, and it is sufficient to prove that the discriminant is positive, as in
[
3
]
.
Instead, to compute the Hilbert symbol, we need to control the crystalline contribution. Our arguments rely heavily on
p
-adic Hodge theory.
Let us give the mainlines below.

We begin by extending the coefficients of the motive
M
from
\mathbb{Q}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Precisely, let
F
be the CM number field acting on
M
, the motive
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is endowed with an action of
F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Such an action decomposes the motive into a sum of direct factors and we study them separately. Thus, we reduce to the situation where the motive
M
has an action of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, which we will denote again by
F
, endowed with a non trivial involution
*\colon F\rightarrow F
. The crystalline and
p
-adic étale realisations of
M
are endowed by hypothesis with a non-degenerate quadratic form. Faltings’s crystalline comparison theorem
[
17
]
allows to compare these realisations via a matrix of periods laying in Fontaine’s ring
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}
of
p
-adic periods. By using the CM action we characterise this matrix by a single invertible period
\lambda\in\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}^{\times}
, where
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}
is the smallest
subring of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}
containing
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}
and
F
. This period is unique up to a scalar in
F^{\times}
and it controls completely the arithmetic of our problem. Namely it produces a “re-normalisation factor”
\lambda\lambda^{*}
belonging to
F_{0}^{\times}
, where
F_{0}
denotes the subfield of
F
of elements fixed by
*
. Thanks to a theorem of Milnor on CM quadratic forms on
p
-adic fields
[
37
]
, we show that our problem is equivalent to control precisely whether the re-normalisation factor is a norm of an element of
F^{\times}
. We verify that this problem is multiplicative with respect to the tensor product of motives: hence, we are reduced to treat the case of small Hodge weights. For those motives we need to prove that the re-normalisation factor
\lambda\lambda^{*}
is not a norm.

This is the crucial point of the article.
Even if the definition of the period
\lambda
, based on the crystalline comparison theorem, is not explicit,
this period satisfies a strong uniqueness property with respect to the behaviour under the Frobenius and the de Rham filtration.
Such a uniqueness follows from the fundamental exact sequences in
p
-adic Hodge theory.
Therefore, we can describe
\lambda
through any other periods with similar behaviour. For this purpose, we use
Lubin–Tate periods of Colmez
[
14
]
to get some control of
\lambda
. It turns out that such a control is precisely what we need to apply
a theorem of Dwork

[
16
]
in order to compute the image of
the re-normalisation factor
\lambda\lambda^{*}
through the local reciprocity map
F^{\times}_{0}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gal}({F}/{F_{0}})
.
As the kernel of the reciprocity map is the group of norms
N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
, we obtain that
\lambda\lambda^{*}
is not a norm.

Remark 1.6
.

The main result of
[
3
]
is an instance of Theorem
1.5
, namely the case where the motive
M
has rank two. In that particular case the field
F
is a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
As there are finitely many such extensions, it was possible in
[
3
]
*Section 12 to compute the period
\lambda
case by case.
Moreover, those periods could be described in terms of Lubin–Tate periods and elements algebraic over
F
.

In higher rank,
F
is a quadratic extension of
F_{0}
,
whose degree over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is equal to the half of the rank of the motive, with

a priori
no restriction on the ramification of
F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
nor of
F/F_{0}
.
Hence an analysis case by case is impossible.
Moreover, the period
\lambda
is computed in terms of Lubin–Tate periods and a non-explicit element in
\widehat{F}^{\mathrm{nr}}
, the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
. In general such an element is
transcendental over
F
, see Remark
9.6
.

Remark 3.3
.

Hypothesis (4) is crucial and absolutely necessary. Indeed, in order to conclude a positivity statement one has to assume some positivity property.
The action of
F
makes the hypothesis (1) essentially automatic. Indeed if
\dim V_{Z}\neq\dim M
then

and the statement is trivial. See
[
3

for details.

The statement should be true without the hypothesis (2) and (3), see Conjecture
3.1
.
On the other hand, the standard conjecture of Hodge type can be reduced to the case of varieties over finite fields
[
3
, Proposition 3.16]
and for such a variety the Tate conjecture predicts that its motive should be a motive of abelian type
[
35
]
. In particular the hypothesis of Theorem
3.2
should not be restrictive in the study of the standard conjecture of Hodge type.

Finally, homological and numerical equivalence should always coincide hence one would like to replace

with the space of cycles modulo homological equivalence. The following proposition is the crucial reason for which one needs to work with numerical equivalence.

Keep notation from Theorem

R(M_{/_{k}})=V_{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}E,\hskip 14.22636pt\textrm{and}\hskip 14.22636ptR(q_{/_{k}})=q_{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}E.

As numerical motives form a semisimple category (

[

M_{/_{k}}\cong{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}^{\oplus\dim M}
of numerical motives. Let us fix such an isomorphism

and let

be its inverse. Consider a map

at the level of homological motives whose reduction modulo numerical equivalence is

a lifting of

The ring of endomorphisms of the unit object

Hence, the composition

is the identity, as so is

.
This implies that the map
\tilde{g}\circ\tilde{f}
is a projector inducing a decomposition of the form

for some homological motive

For dimensional reasons, the realization of

is zero, and so is

by the very definition of homological motives.

Conversely, the existence of an homological isomorphism

clearly implies hypothesis
(1)
.
∎

In what follows

Let

-quadratic form and let

[

[

Let

-quadratic form. Then for all but a finite number of places

Corollary 4.4
.

Let

\mathbb{Q}
-quadratic forms and let

The

Proposition 4.5
.

5.
Reduction to CM-simple objects

In this section we study quadratic forms in the context of

-adic Hodge theory.
The setting we choose is inspired by the previous section and in particular by Propositions
4.5
and

.

Definition 5.1
.

A supersingular pair
(V_{B,p},V_{Z,p})
is the collection of the following objects.

(1)

A
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
V_{B,p}
endowed with the action of the absolute Galois group of a
p
-adic field
K
which makes the representation crystalline.

(2)

A
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
V_{Z,p}
which generates the (admissible) filtered
\varphi
-module corresponding to
V_{B,p}
and on which the Frobenius acts trivially.

An orthogonal supersingular datum
(V_{B,p},V_{Z,p},q_{B,p},q_{Z,p})
, or simply an orthogonal supersingular pair
(q_{B,p},q_{Z,p})
is the collection of the following objects.

(1)

A supersingular pair
(V_{B,p},V_{Z,p})
.

(2)

A non-degenerate
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-quadratic form

q_{B,p}
on
V_{B,p}
for which the Galois action is isometric.

(3)

The non-degenerate
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-quadratic form

q_{Z,p}
on
V_{Z,p}
corresponding to
q_{B,p}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(q_{B,p},q_{Z,p})
be an orthogonal supersingular pair. Then the discriminants of
q_{B,p}
and
q_{Z,p}
coincide.

Proof.

The quadratic form on
V_{Z,p}
induces a quadratic form on the one dimensional vector space
\operatorname{det}V_{Z,p}
. This form is characterized by an element of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
, which is the discriminant of
q_{Z}
. (The same holds for
V_{B,p}
.) As the Frobenius acts trivially on
\operatorname{det}V_{Z,p}
, admissibility implies that the filtration is trivial as well. This means that the comparison isomorphism sends the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-structure
\operatorname{det}V_{Z,p}
on the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-structure
\operatorname{det}V_{B,p}
. As the comparison isomorphism respects the underlined quadratic forms the discriminants coincide. ∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(V_{B,p},V_{Z,p},q_{B,p},q_{Z,p})
be an orthogonal supersingular datum, let
F
be a number field and let
*
be a non trivial field involution on

. Then the following holds true.

(1)

The ring

is a product of
p
-adic fields

F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}

(2)

The involution
*
acts on this product as a composition of disjoint transpositions, i.e. there is an integer

*(F_{i})=F_{i+s}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for}\hskip 5.69046pti\leq s,\hskip 5.69046pt*(F_{i})=F_{i-s}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for}\hskip 5.69046pts<i\leq 2s\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{and}\hskip 5.69046pt*\!(F_{i})=F_{i}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for}\hskip 5.69046pti>2s.

is non trivial.

(3)

Suppose that

acts on

and that this action commutes
with the Galois action. Then

as well and the two actions are compatible with respect to the comparison theorem. In particular the decomposition of point (1) induces two decompositions

(4)

Suppose moreover that for all

is

. Then the same holds true for

. Moreover the decompositions in

is a separable extension.

Lemma 5.5
.

A supersingular pair

is good if and only if one of the following situation happens:

(1)

The quadratic forms

and

are isomorphic and

is even.

(2)

The quadratic forms
q_{B,p}
and
q_{Z,p}
are not isomorphic and

is odd.

Proof.

The two quadratic spaces have the same rank by definition of supersingular pair and the same discriminant by Lemma

Proposition 5.6

Keep notation as in Lemma

If for each

First of all, let us show that the pair

We need to show that the height of the corresponding Hodge polygon is even.

Now, as the right hand side of the equation (

) is additive on direct sums, let us show that the left hand side is additive as well, this will imply the statement. Consider two orthogonal supersingular pairs

By the definition of the Hilbert symbol of a quadratic form

denotes the Hilbert symbol of a couple of elements

,

If we write the quotient of the two equalities above the contribution of the discriminants simplifies as

6.
CM-quadratic forms over
p
-adic fields

From now on (except for Section
10
) let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
We assume that
F
is endowed with a non trivial involution
*\colon F\rightarrow F
,
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{*}
. We collect here some basic results on some quadratic forms endowed with an action of
F
which we call CM
(Definition
6.1
).

Throughout the section we fix a Galois closure
\tilde{F}\supset F
of
F
, and denote by
\Gamma
the set of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-embeddings of
F
in
\tilde{F}
.
We recall that
F_{0}
denote the subfield of
F
where
*
acts as the identity,
cf.
Convention
2.1
.
We denote still by
*
the action of
*
on
\Gamma
by pre-composition.

Denote respectively by
\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\colon F_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
N_{F/F_{0}}\colon F\rightarrow F_{0}
the trace of the extension
F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and the norm of
F/F_{0}
; the extension
F/F_{0}
has degree two and we have
N_{F/F_{0}}(x)=x^{*}x
for every
x\in F
.

Definition 6.1
.

(1)

A CM-space (with respect to
F
) is the data of a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
V
and an action
of
F
on
V
such that
V
as an
F
-vector space has dimension one.

(2)

A CM-quadratic space (with respect to
F
) is the data of a CM-space
V
and a non-degenerate
quadratic form
q\colon V\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}
on the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
V
, such that
for all
\alpha
in
F
, the adjoint with respect to
q
of the multiplication by
\alpha
is the multiplication by
\alpha^{*}
.

For simplicity we may say that
q
(or
V
) is a
CM
-quadratic form without mentioning explicitly the other structures.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
V
be a CM-quadratic space.
Fix a nonzero vector
v
in
V
and consider the induced identification
V\cong F
as
F
-vector spaces. Under this identification,
there exists a unique
a\in F_{0}^{\times}
such that for every
x\in F
, we have

q(x)=\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(aN_{F/F_{0}}(x)).

If one changes the choice of the vector
v
, the element
a\in F_{0}^{\times}
is multiplied by some norm in
N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
. In particular the class of
a
in
F^{\times}_{0}/N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
is well-defined and depends only on
V
.

Proof.

Let
b\colon F\times F\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the symmetric form associated with
q
.
Since
q
is a CM-quadratic form, we have, for every
x\in F
,
q(x)=b(x,x)=b(1,x^{*}x)=b(1,N_{F/F_{0}}(x))
.
The restriction of
b(1,-)
to
F_{0}
gives a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-linear form on
F_{0}
. Since
(u,v)\mapsto\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(uv)
is a non-degenerate bilinear form on
F_{0}
we can conclude that there exists such an element
a\in F_{0}
. It must be nonzero otherwise
q
would be zero.

Let
w
be a new nonzero vector in
V
and
q^{\prime}(x)
be the induced quadratic form on
F
under this new identification. There is a unique nonzero element
z\in F
such that
zv=w
. This gives the relation
q^{\prime}(x)=q(zx)
. Hence one deduces

q^{\prime}(x)=\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(aN_{F/F_{0}}(zx))=\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}((az^{*}z)\cdot N_{F/F_{0}}(x)).\qed

Definition 6.3
.

Let
q
be a CM-quadratic form. We define the gauge of
q
as the element of
F^{\times}/N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
associated with
q
through the above proposition.

Theorem 6.4

(Milnor)
.

(1)

The group
F^{\times}/N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
has cardinality two.

(2)

All CM-quadratic forms (with respect to the same
F
) have the same discriminant.

(3)

Two CM-quadratic forms are isomorphic if and only if they have the same gauge in
F^{\times}/N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
.

(4)

Up to isomorphism there are exactly two CM-quadratic spaces.

Proof.

The extension
F/F_{0}
has degree
2
. The reciprocity isomorphism

F^{\times}/N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})\cong\mathrm{Gal}({F}/{F_{0}})

of local class field theory proves (
1
).

Consider
F
as an
F_{0}
-vector space of dimension 2 and consider on it the non-degenerate
F_{0}
-quadratic forms
\tilde{q}_{a}(x)=aN_{F/F_{0}}(x)
, for
a\in F_{0}^{\times}
.
By definition a CM-quadratic form
q
of gauge
a
is isometric to
\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\circ\,\tilde{q}_{a}
.
Now, notice that all quadratic forms
\tilde{q}_{a}
have the same discriminant in
F_{0}^{\times}/(F_{0}^{\times})^{2}
,
independent of
a
, as
\tilde{q}_{a}(x)=a\tilde{q}_{1}(x)
and the quadratic space has dimension
2
.
(Actually this discriminant is the class of the opposite of the discriminant of the extention
F/F_{0}
.) By
[
37
, Lemma 2.2]
this implies (
2
).

For (
3
) we argue as above and consider the quadratic forms
\tilde{q}_{a}
. We claim that
\tilde{q}_{a}\cong\tilde{q}_{b}
if and only if
a
and
b
are in the same class of
F^{\times}/N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
. This will give (
3
) thank to
[
37
, Theorem 2.3]
. The claim is elementary: if
a
and
b
are in the same class, we can write
a/b=N_{F/F_{0}}(z)
and the change of variable
x\mapsto z\cdot x
gives the isometry. Conversely, if the quadratic forms are isomorphic then the images of
F^{\times}
by them must be the same, but those images are precisely
a\cdot N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
and
b\cdot N_{F/F_{0}}(F^{\times})
.
Point (
4
) is the combination of (
1
) and (
3
).
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
V
be a CM-space and consider

V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}F
. It is endowed with two different actions of
F
, one on the left, induced by the action of

on
V
, and one on the right. To distinguish them we will write, for all

and

for the left multiplication and

for the right multiplication.
The inclusion
F\subset\tilde{F}
gives a fixed inclusion
V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}F\subset V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\tilde{F}
and, for all
\sigma\in\Gamma
, let us denote again by
\sigma
the induced embedding
V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}F\subset V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\tilde{F}.
Then the following hold.

(1)

There exists a nonzero (eigen)vector

such that
z(v)=v\cdot z
. Such a

is unique up to (left or right) multiplication by an element of
F^{\times}
.

(2)

The (eigen)vectors
\{\sigma(v)\}_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
form a basis of

over
\tilde{F}
.
We set

, it is an eigenline with respect to the embedding
\sigma
, i.e.
z(\sigma(v))=\sigma(v)\cdot\sigma(z)

Proof.

Follows immediately from the classical description

and the fact that the dimension of

over

is one. ∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
(V,q)
be a CM-quadratic space and let

v\in V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}F
be the eigenvector constructed in Proposition
6.5
above. Let
b
be the bilinear pairing induced by
q
. Then the following holds.

(1)

One has
b(\sigma(v),\tau(v))=0
except if
\sigma=\tau^{*}
.

(2)

The number
2b(v,v^{*})\in F_{0}^{\times}
is the gauge of
q
.

Proof.

For all
z\in F
we have
b(\sigma(z(v)),\tau(v))=b(\sigma(v),\tau(z^{*}(v)))
by definition of CM-quadratic forms. On the other hand we have the relations

b(\sigma(z(v)),\tau(v))=b(\sigma(v),\tau(v))\cdot\sigma(z)
and

b(\sigma(v),\tau(z^{*}(v)))=b(\sigma(v),\tau(v))\cdot\tau^{*}(z)
, by construction of
v
. These relations together give (1).

For the second point, choose
\{\sigma(v)\}_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
as basis. A vector
w\in V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\tilde{F}
is actually in
V
if and only if there exists a scalar
z\in F
such that the coordinates of
w
with respect to this basis are
\{\sigma(z)\}_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
.
Let us now compute the quadratic form on such a vector
w\in V
:

q(w)=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in\Gamma}b(\sigma(zv),\tau(zv))=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}b(\sigma(zv),\sigma^{*}(zv))=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_{0}}2\sigma(zz^{*})\sigma(b(v,v^{*})),

where the second equality comes from part (
1
) and
\Gamma_{0}
denotes the set of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-embeddings of
F_{0}
in
\tilde{F}
.
In conclusion we have

q(w)=\tr_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(2b(v,v^{*})\cdot N_{F/F_{0}}(z))

which computes the gauge.
∎

and

We define the tensor product of two

A.10.

Recall from

For any

\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma\colon D\otimes_{F_{a}}F\rightarrow D\otimes_{F_{a}}\tilde{F},

Lemma A.11
.

For any
z\in F
and
d\in D_{\tilde{F}}=D\otimes_{F_{a}}\tilde{F}
denote by
z(d)
the action induced by the action of
F
on
D
and by
d\cdot z
the action by multiplication by the scalar
z\in\tilde{F}
on the right.
Let
w_{\sigma}\in D_{\tilde{F}}
be the element defined
A.10
. The following are true.

(1)

For every
\sigma\in\Gamma
, we have
\varphi_{\pi}^{f}(w_{\sigma})=\sigma(\pi)w_{\sigma}
.

(2)

For every
\sigma\in\Gamma
and for every
z\in F
, we have

z(w_{\sigma})=w_{\sigma}\cdot\sigma(z)
.

(3)

The family
(w_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
forms a basis of
D_{\tilde{F}}
as vector space over
\tilde{F}
.

Proof.

The proof of
(
1
)
is a formal argument:

\varphi_{\pi}^{f}(w_{\sigma})=(\varphi_{\pi}^{f+\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(w)=(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(\varphi_{\pi}^{f}(w))=(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(\pi w)=\sigma(\pi)w_{\sigma}.

Let us prove (
2
):

z(w_{\sigma})=z((\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(w))=(z\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(w)=(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}z\otimes\sigma)(w)=(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(z(w)),

since the
F
-action on
D
commutes with
\varphi_{\pi}
by construction. Now by definition of
w
, we have
z(w)=w\cdot z
, hence
we finish by

(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(z(w))=(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)(w\cdot z)=w_{\sigma}\cdot\sigma(z).

Point (
3
) follows from (
2
) : the
w_{\sigma}
are eigenvectors of multiplicity one diagonalizing the left action of
F
on
D_{\tilde{F}}
.
∎

Proposition A.12
.

Let
\pi\in F
be a uniformizer and
D
the associated Lubin–Tate filtered
\varphi
-module from Definition

A.5
. Then the element
\alpha\coloneqq\alpha_{\pi}\in\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}\cap\mathrm{Fil}^{1}\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}
associated with it in (
A.9.1
) is a Lubin–Tate period relative to
\pi\in F
in the sense of Definition
9.1
.

Proof.

We compute

\varphi_{\mathrm{cris}}^{f}(\alpha)=((\varphi_{\mathrm{cris}}^{f}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{F})\circ(x\otimes\iota))(w)=((x\otimes\iota)\circ(\varphi_{\pi}^{f}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{F}))(w)=(x\otimes\iota)(w\cdot\pi)=\pi\alpha,

so the condition (
3
) of Definition
9.1
holds.

Let us check the other properties of Definition
9.1
.
By construction
v_{\mathrm{dR}}(\alpha)\geq 1
and,
for all
\sigma\in\Gamma\backslash\{\iota\}
, we compute

(A.12.1)

\sigma_{\mathrm{cris}}(\alpha)=((\varphi_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma)\circ(x\otimes\iota))(w)=((x\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}})\circ(\varphi_{\pi}^{\nu(\sigma)}\otimes\sigma))(w)=(x\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}})(w_{\sigma}),

and in particular
v_{\mathrm{dR}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{cris}}(\alpha))\geq 0
, since

(x\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}})(D_{\tilde{F}})\subset\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{F}}\cap\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}
.

Let us define

(A.12.2)

P\coloneqq\prod_{\sigma\in\Gamma}\sigma_{\mathrm{cris}}(\alpha)\in\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{F}}.

We claim the following:

(1)

P
belongs to
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}
.

(2)

P
is invertible in
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}
.

(3)

v_{\mathrm{dR}}(P)=1
.

These claims conclude the proof.
Indeed the equality
\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}v_{\mathrm{dR}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{cris}}(\alpha))=v_{\mathrm{dR}}(P)=1
implies
that the inequalities we have shown above on the
v_{\mathrm{dR}}(\sigma_{\mathrm{cris}}(\alpha))
must be equalities.
Moreover,
\alpha
is invertible in
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}
, since it divides
P
which is invertible.

Let us first prove claim
(1)
.
Consider the Galois group
G\coloneqq\mathrm{Gal}({\tilde{F}}/{F^{\prime}})
, where
F^{\prime}\coloneqq\tilde{F}_{a}F
.
Let
G
act on
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{F}}=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{\tilde{F}_{a}}\tilde{F}
as the identity on
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}
and via its natural action on the right.
By its very construction
P
is stable under
G
hence it belongs to

(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{F}})^{G}=B_{\mathrm{cris},F^{\prime}}=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{\tilde{F}_{a}}\tilde{F}_{a}F=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{F_{a}}F=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}.

To show claims
(2)
and
(3)
consider the
determinant
\Delta
of the filtered
\varphi
-module
D

\Delta\coloneqq\operatorname{det}_{F_{a}}(D)=\bigwedge^{ef}D.

It is admissible of slope one (both Newton and Hodge), hence

V_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\vee}(\Delta)
is a rank one crystalline representation of Hodge-Tate weight one.
To conclude, it is enough to show that, up to a constant in
F^{\times}
,
P
is the period
\delta\in\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\times}
of
\Delta
.

The left
F
-action on
V_{\tilde{F}}\coloneqq V_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\vee}(D)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\tilde{F}
decomposes the element
x\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}}
as sum of eigenvectors
x\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}x_{\sigma}
.
By Lemma
A.11
(
3
),
(w_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
is a basis of
D_{\tilde{F}}
, and similarly

(x_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
is a basis of
V_{\tilde{F}}
.
We have
x_{\tau}(w_{\sigma})=0
whenever
\tau\not=\sigma
, because
x_{\tau}
and
w_{\sigma}
live in eigenlines of different eigenvalues.
Hence, up to a constant
u
in
\tilde{F}^{\times}
, we can compute the period
\delta
by using these bases, as

u\delta=\prod_{\sigma\in\Gamma}(x_{\sigma}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}})(w_{\sigma})=\prod_{\sigma\in\Gamma}(x\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\tilde{F}})(w_{\sigma}),

which is equal to
P
by (
A.12.1
).
Finally, by claim
(1)
the constant
u
must belong to
F^{\times}
.
∎

Proposition A.14
.

Let
\pi\in F
be a uniformizer and
D
the associated Lubin–Tate filtered
\varphi
-module, from Definition

A.5
. Then the crystalline representation
V_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\vee}(D)
is given by the Lubin–Tate character associated with
\pi\in F
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a Lubin–Tate group over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
associated with
\pi
. Let
T
be its Tate module,
given by the Lubin–Tate character
\psi\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F}
; set
V\coloneqq T[1/p]
. We want to show that

V_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\vee}(D)=V
. Colmez constructs an element

, satisfying Definition
9.1
and
such that for every

,
g(t_{\mathfrak{F}})=\psi(g)t_{\mathfrak{F}}
, see for example
[
22
, §3.6]
.
Let
\alpha=\alpha_{\pi}\in\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris},F}^{\times}
the Lubin–Tate period in Proposition
A.12
.
By Lemma
8.5
the period
t_{\mathfrak{F}}\alpha^{-1}
belongs to
F^{\times}
, whence we have also, for every

.
The map

∎

Convergence analysis of a fully discrete energy-stable numerical scheme for the Q-tensor flow of liquid crystals
Varun M. Gudibanda, Franziska Weber, Yukun Yue

Abstract
. We present a fully discrete convergent finite difference scheme for the Q-tensor flow of liquid crystals based on the energy-stable semi-discrete scheme by Zhao, Yang, Gong, and Wang (Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg. 2017). We prove stability properties of the scheme and show convergence to weak solutions of the Q-tensor flow equations. We demonstrate the performance of the scheme in numerical simulations.



1.
Introduction

Liquid crystals constitute a state of matter that is intermediate between solids and liquids. On one hand, they have properties that are typical for fluids, in particular they have the ability to flow, on the other hand, they exhibit properties of solids, as an example, their molecules are oriented in a crystal-like manner. A common characteristic of materials exhibiting a liquid crystal phase is that they consist of elongated molecules of identical size. They may be pictured as ‘rods’ or ‘ribbons’ and are subject to molecular interactions that make them align alike
[
11
]
.

Liquid crystals play an important role in nature: As an example, phospholipids which constitute the main component of cell membranes, are a form of liquid crystal. They also appear in many daily applications, such as soaps, shampoos and detergents. Further applications include displays of electronic devices (LCD), where one makes use of the optical properties of liquid crystals in the presence or absence of an electric field; thermometers, optical switches
[
6
,
16
]
, and biotechnological applications.
One generally distinguishes three types of liquid crystals, nematics, cholesterics and smectics. We focus here on the numerical discretization of a liquid crystal model for nematic liquid crystals, the so-called Q-tensor model.

1.1.
Q-tensor model

In the Q-tensor model by Landau and de Gennes
[
5
]
, the main orientation of the liquid crystal molecules is represented by the Q-tensor, a symmetric, trace-free matrix that is assumed to minimize the Landau-de Gennes free energy

E_{LG}(Q)=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}_{B}(Q)+\mathcal{F}_{E}(Q),

in equilibrium situations. Here
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, is the spatial domain occupied by the liquid crystal molecules,
\mathcal{F}_{B}
is a bulk potential and
\mathcal{F}_{E}
is the elastic energy given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{B}(Q)=\frac{a}{2}\tr(Q^{2})-\frac{b}{3}\tr(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{4}(\tr(Q^{2}))^{2},\quad\mathcal{F}_{E}(Q)=\frac{L_{1}}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|\operatorname{div}Q|^{2}+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{d}\partial_{i}Q_{jk}\partial_{k}Q_{ji},\end{split}

where
a,b,c,L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
are constants with
c,L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}>0
.

Non-equilibrium situations can be described by the gradient flow
[
1
,
9
]
,

(1.1)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial t}=M&\Bigg(L_{1}\Delta Q_{ij}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\partial_{ik}Q_{jk}+\partial_{jk}Q_{ik})-\frac{2}{d}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{d}\partial_{k\ell}Q_{k\ell}\delta_{ij}\right)\\
&-\left(aQ_{ij}-b\left((Q^{2})_{ij}-\frac{1}{d}\tr(Q^{2})\delta_{ij}\right)+c\tr(Q^{2})Q_{ij}\right)\Bigg),\end{split}

where
M>0
is a constant, and one approach to obtaining equilibrium states is to follow this gradient flow.
Adding the dynamics of the mean flow of the liquid crystal fluid to this, one obtains the Beris-Edwards system
[
2
]
.

Analysis of the Q-tensor flow has been done, e.g.,
[
7
,
4
,
15
]
, and numerical methods for the Q-tensor flow have been constructed in
[
17
,
10
,
8
,
13
]
. To the best of our knowledge, none of these methods has been shown to be convergent to a weak solution of (
1.1
). An exception is the work by Cai, Shen and Xu
[
3
]
, where under the assumption of smallness of the initial data, convergence of a time discretization in 2D is proved. Our goal is to show convergence to weak solutions of a fully discrete method for (
1.1
) in 2 and 3D under only the natural assumption that the initial energy is bounded. Our numerical method is based on the invariant energy quadratization idea (IEQ) by Zhao et al.
[
17
]
which we combine with a finite difference discretization in space.

This method takes as a basis the reformulation of the Q-tensor flow using the auxiliary variable
r
:

(1.2)

r(Q)=\sqrt{2\left(\frac{a}{2}\tr(Q^{2})-\frac{b}{3}\tr(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{4}\tr^{2}(Q^{2})+A_{0}\right)},

where
A_{0}>0
is a constant ensuring that
r
is positive. Defining

(1.3)

S(Q)=aQ-b\left[Q^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\text{tr}(Q^{2})I\right]+c\text{tr}(Q^{2})Q,

it follows that

(1.4)

\frac{\delta r(Q)}{\delta Q}=\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)}:=P(Q),

for symmetric, trace free tensors
Q
.
Then one can formally write the gradient flow (
1.1
) as a system for
(Q,r)
:

(1.5a)

\displaystyle Q_{t}

\displaystyle=M\left(L_{1}\Delta Q+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\alpha(Q)-rP(Q)\right):=MH,

(1.5b)

\displaystyle r_{t}

\displaystyle=P(Q):Q_{t},

where

\alpha(Q)_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\partial_{ik}Q_{jk}+\partial_{jk}Q_{ik})-\frac{2}{d}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{d}\partial_{k\ell}Q_{k\ell}\delta_{ij}.

It is easy to see that this reformulation comes with a formal energy law: Multiplying the first equation (
1.5a
) with
-H
and (
1.5b
) with
r
, adding and integrating, and integrating by parts, we obtain

(1.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(L_{1}|\nabla Q|^{2}+(L_{2}+L_{3})|\operatorname{div}Q|^{2}+r^{2}\right)dx=-M\int_{\Omega}|H|^{2}dx.

In
[
17
]
, a time discretization of the system (
1.5
) is proposed that retains a discrete version of the energy law (
1.6
). Based on this prior work, we propose a fully discrete finite difference method for (
1.5
) and prove its convergence to weak solutions of (
1.5
) as defined in the following Definition
2.3
.

We then proceed to showing that weak solutions of (
1.5
) are in fact weak solutions of (
1.1
) and so achieve convergence to the original system (
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first convergence proof for a fully discrete numerical scheme discretizing (
1.1
).
The proof is based on the derivation of discrete energy stability of the fully discrete scheme, then using this to derive the existence of a precompact sequence that allows us to pass to the limit in the approximations. We proceed to showing Lipschitz continuity of the function
P
and use a Lax-Wendroff type argument to show that the limit of the approximating sequence is a weak solution of (
1.5
). The last step is to show that weak solutions of (
1.5
) are in fact weak solutions of (
1.1
). We achieve this through showing that a weak form of the chain rule holds in this case. We conclude with numerical experiments in 2D. Our scheme and analysis is for the 3D case but adaptions to 2D can be made easily.

2.
Preliminaries

Notation 2.1
.

We introduce the following general notation for matrix-valued functions
A,B:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
:

•

A:B=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}A_{ij}B_{ij}
,

•

\langle A,B\rangle=\int_{\Omega}A:B\,dx
,

•

|A|:=|A|_{F}=\sqrt{A:A}
,

•

\left\lVert A\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{\Omega}|A|_{F}^{2}\,dx
,

•

\partial_{i}A=(\partial_{i}A_{jk})_{jk}
,
\partial_{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}
,

•

\nabla A=(\partial_{1}A,\dots,\partial_{d}A)
,

•

|\nabla A|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}A|_{F}^{2}
,

•

\left\lVert\nabla A\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla A|^{2}\,dx
.

We assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded, connected domain with Lipschitz boundary and
Q_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{d\times d}
takes values in the symmetric trace-free
d\times d
matrices and satisfies

\left.Q_{0}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.
Fix
T>0
an arbitrary time horizon. We then define weak solutions of (
1.1
) as follows:

Definition 2.2
.

By a weak solution of (
1.1
), we mean a function
Q:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
that is trace-free and symmetric for every
(t,x)
and satisfies

Q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\quad Q_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega),

and

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Q:\partial_{t}\varphi dxdt-\int_{\Omega}Q(T,x):\varphi(T,x)dx+\int_{\Omega}Q_{0}(x):\varphi(0,x)dx\\
&=M\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(L_{1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\nabla Q_{ij}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{ij}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{k}Q_{jk}\partial_{i}\varphi_{ij}+\partial_{k}Q_{ik}\partial_{j}\varphi_{ij}-\frac{2}{d}\,\partial_{i}Q_{ki}\partial_{k}\varphi_{jj}\right)\right)dxdt\\
&\quad+M\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(aQ-b\left((Q^{2})-\frac{1}{d}\tr(Q^{2})I\right)+c\tr(Q^{2})Q\right):\varphi\,dxdt,\end{split}

for all smooth
\varphi=(\varphi_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
that are compactly supported within
\Omega
for almost every
t\in[0,T]
. Furthermore,
Q
satisfies the energy inequality

(2.2)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}L_{1}|\nabla Q(t,x)|^{2}+(L_{2}+L_{3})|\operatorname{div}Q(t,x)|^{2}+2\mathcal{F}_{B}\left(Q(t,x)\right)\,dx\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}L_{1}|\nabla Q_{0}|^{2}+(L_{2}+L_{3})|\operatorname{div}Q_{0}|^{2}+2\mathcal{F}_{B}\left(Q_{0}\right)\,dx-M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|H(s,x)|^{2}\,dx\,ds,

for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Similarly, we define weak solutions of the reformulation (
1.5
):

Definition 2.3
.

By a weak solution of (
1.5
), we mean a pair of functions
Q:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
r:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, with
Q(t,x)
trace-free and symmetric for every
(t,x)
, and satisfying

Q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\quad Q_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega),\quad r\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

and

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Q:\partial_{t}\varphi dxdt-\int_{\Omega}Q(T,x):\varphi(T,x)dx+\int_{\Omega}Q_{0}(x):\varphi(0,x)dx\\
&=M\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(L_{1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\nabla Q_{ij}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{ij}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{k}Q_{jk}\partial_{i}\varphi_{ij}+\partial_{k}Q_{ik}\partial_{j}\varphi_{ij}-\frac{2}{d}\,\partial_{i}Q_{ki}\partial_{k}\varphi_{jj}\right)\right)dxdt\\
&\quad+M\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}rP(Q):\varphi\,dxdt,\end{split}

and

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r\,\phi_{t}dxdt-\int_{\Omega}r(T,x)\phi(T,x)dx+\int_{\Omega}r_{0}(x)\phi(0,x)dx=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\,\phi\,dxdt,

where
r_{0}=r(Q_{0})
and
P(Q)
is defined in (
1.4
), for all smooth
\varphi=(\varphi_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
\phi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
that are compactly supported within
\Omega
for every
t\in[0,T]
. Furthermore,
(Q,r)
satisfies for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
the energy inequality

(2.5)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}L_{1}|\nabla Q(t,x)|^{2}+(L_{2}+L_{3})|\operatorname{div}Q(t,x)|^{2}+|r(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}L_{1}|\nabla Q_{0}|^{2}+(L_{2}+L_{3})|\operatorname{div}Q_{0}|^{2}+|r_{0}|^{2}\,dx-M\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|H(s,x)|^{2}\,dx\,ds

3.
The numerical scheme

We start by introducing notation to define our numerical scheme.
We let
\Delta t>0
be a time step size and
t^{n}:=n\Delta t
time levels at which we intend to compute approximations. For the ease of notation, we present the scheme for the case
\Omega=[0,1]^{3}
and
h>0
is a uniform grid size in each spatial dimension. Extensions to square prisms of different side lengths and nonuniform grid sizes are not hard but notationally cumbersome, therefore we restrict our analysis to the cube in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and uniform mesh sizes. The 2D case can easily be derived from the 3D scheme presented here.
We let
x_{ijk}=(x_{i},y_{j},z_{k})=(ih,jh,kh)
be grid points,
i,j,k=0,\dots,N+1
, with
N+1=1/h\in\mathbb{N}
.
For approximations
(f_{ijk})_{ijk=0}^{N+1}
on this grid, we define the averages

f_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{f_{ijk}^{n+1}+f_{ijk}^{n}}{2},\quad\overline{f_{ijk}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{3}{2}f_{ijk}^{n}-\frac{1}{2}f_{ijk}^{n-1}

and difference operators:

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\quad D_{t}^{\pm}f^{n}_{ijk}=\pm\frac{f^{n\pm 1}_{ijk}-f^{n}_{ijk}}{\Delta t},\\
D^{\pm}_{1}f^{n}_{ijk}=\pm\frac{f^{n}_{i\pm 1,j,k}-f^{n}_{ijk}}{h},&\quad D^{\pm}_{2}f^{n}_{ijk}=\pm\frac{f^{n}_{i,j\pm 1,k}-f^{n}_{ijk}}{h},\quad D^{\pm}_{3}f^{n}_{ijk}=\pm\frac{f^{n}_{i,j,k\pm 1}-f^{n}_{ijk}}{h},\\
D^{c}_{1}f^{n}_{ijk}=\frac{f^{n}_{i+1,j,k}-f^{n}_{i-1,j,k}}{2h},&\quad D^{c}_{2}f^{n}_{ijk}=\frac{f^{n}_{i,j+1,k}-f^{n}_{i,j-1,k}}{2h},\quad D^{c}_{3}f^{n}_{ijk}=\frac{f^{n}_{i,j,k+1}-f^{n}_{i,j,k-1}}{2h},\end{split}

for
i,j,k=1,\cdots,N
.
We will also need the discrete gradient, Laplacian and divergence operators:

\displaystyle\nabla_{h}^{\pm}f_{ijk}

\displaystyle=(D^{\pm}_{1}f_{ijk},D^{\pm}_{2}f_{ijk},D^{\pm}_{3}f_{ijk})^{\top},

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}f_{ijk}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}D_{\alpha}^{-}D_{\alpha}^{+}f_{ijk},

\displaystyle\quad(\operatorname{div}_{h}f_{ijk})_{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}D^{c}_{\alpha}(f_{ijk})_{\alpha\beta},

where
(f_{ijk})_{\alpha\beta}
is the
(\alpha,\beta)
-entry of the
3\times 3
-matrix
f_{ijk}
.
We approximate the initial data using cell averages:

Q_{ijk}^{0}=\frac{1}{h^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{ijk}}Q_{0}(x)dx,\quad r_{ijk}^{0}=\frac{1}{h^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{ijk}}r(Q_{0}(x))dx,\quad i,j,k=1,\dots,N,

where
\mathcal{C}_{ijk}=[x_{i}-0.5h,x_{i}+0.5h)\times[y_{j}-0.5h,y_{j}+0.5h)\times[z_{k}-0.5h,z_{k}+0.5h)
, for
x_{ijk}=(x_{i},y_{j},z_{k})
,
and use Dirichlet boundary conditions

(3.2)

Q_{0,j,k}=Q_{N+1,j,k}=Q_{i,0,k}=Q_{i,N+1,k}=Q_{i,j,0}=Q_{i,j,N+1}=0,\quad i,j,k=0,1,\dots,N,N+1.

For ease of notation throughout, we will also impose boundary conditions on ghost nodes

(3.3)

Q_{-1,j,k}=Q_{N+2,j,k}=Q_{i,-1,k}=Q_{i,N+2,k}=Q_{i,j,-1}=Q_{i,j,N+2}=0,\quad i,j,k=0,1,\dots,N,N+1.

We then propose the following method

(3.4)

\begin{cases}D_{t}^{+}Q_{ijk}^{n}=M\left(L_{1}\Delta_{h}Q_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-r_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{P}_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\alpha_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right):=MH_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\\
r_{ijk}^{n+1}-r_{ijk}^{n}=\overline{P}_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:(Q_{ijk}^{n+1}-Q_{ijk}^{n})\end{cases}

Here,
Q_{ijk}^{n}
is an approximation for
Q
and
r^{n}_{ijk}
is an approximation of
r
at spatial point
(x_{i},y_{j},z_{k})
and time step
n
.
We defined
\alpha_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^{n}}{2}
where
\alpha^{n}=\alpha_{h}(Q_{ijk}^{n})
and
\alpha^{n+1}=\alpha_{h}(Q_{ijk}^{n+1})
and
\alpha_{h}
is a discretization of
\alpha(Q)
:

(3.5)

\left(\alpha_{h}(Q_{ijk}^{n})\right)_{ws}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\left[D_{w}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(Q_{ijk}^{n}\right)_{s\beta}+D_{s}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(Q_{ijk}^{n}\right)_{w\beta}\right]-\frac{2}{3}\sum_{\beta,\gamma=1}^{3}D_{\beta}^{c}D_{\gamma}^{c}\left(Q_{ijk}^{n}\right)_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{ws}

where the notation
(Q_{ijk}^{n})_{ws}
indicates the element in row
w
and column
s
of the matrix
Q_{ijk}^{n}
.

4.
Analysis of the numerical scheme

For the proof of energy stability of this scheme, we will need the following useful lemma which is proved in the appendix (as Lemma
A.1
):

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A_{ijk}
and
B_{ijk}
be scalar quantities at grid point
(x_{i},y_{j},z_{k})
such that
A_{ijk}=0
at boundary values, i.e. boundary conditions (
3.2
), (
3.3
).
Then

\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}A_{ijk}D_{\beta}^{+}B_{ijk}=-\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}B_{ijk}D_{\beta}^{-}A_{ijk},\quad\quad\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}A_{ijk}D_{\beta}^{-}B_{ijk}=-\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}B_{ijk}D_{\beta}^{+}A_{ijk},

and

\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}A_{ijk}D_{\beta}^{c}B_{ijk}=-\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}B_{ijk}D_{\beta}^{c}A_{ijk}.

for
\beta=1,2\text{ or }3
.

4.1.
Energy stability

We start by defining the following norms and semi-norms for difference approximations. For sequences of approximations
\{f_{ijk}\}
,
\{g_{ijk}\}
,
\{A_{ijk}\}
, and
\{B_{ijk}\}
of scalar-or vector-valued functions
f,g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and matrix-valued functions
A,B:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, defined on our grid, we let

\displaystyle\langle A,B\rangle_{h}=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}A_{ijk}:B_{ijk},

\displaystyle\qquad\langle f,g\rangle_{h}=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}f_{ijk}\cdot g_{ijk},

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{h}^{2}=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|A_{ijk}|_{F}^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{h}^{2}=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|f_{ijk}|^{2},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla_{h}A\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\left\lVert D^{-}_{m}A\right\rVert_{h}^{2}.

We start by using Lemma
4.1
to show some simple summation by parts identities that will be useful later in the proofs of energy stability of the scheme.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{A_{ijk}\}
and
\{B_{ijk}\}
be grid functions satisfying homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (
3.2
). Then

\langle A,\Delta_{h}B\rangle_{h}=-\langle\nabla_{h}A,\nabla_{h}B\rangle_{h}

Proof.

We write

\langle A,\Delta_{h}B\rangle_{h}=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}A_{ijk}:(D_{\alpha}^{+}D_{\alpha}^{-}B_{ijk})=-h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}(D_{\alpha}^{-}A_{ijk}):(D_{\alpha}^{-}B_{ijk})=-\langle\nabla_{h}A,\nabla_{h}B\rangle_{h},

where we used Lemma
4.1
and the boundary conditions for the second equality.
∎

For the
\alpha
-term, we have

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\{A_{ijk}\}
and
\{B_{ijk}\}
be symmetric and trace-free grid functions satisfying homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, (
3.2
). Then

\langle A,\alpha_{h}(B)\rangle_{h}=-2\langle\operatorname{div}_{h}A,\operatorname{div}_{h}B\rangle_{h}.

Proof.

We compute (denoting
\alpha_{ijk}:=\alpha_{h}(B_{ijk})
)

\displaystyle\langle A,\alpha_{h}(B)\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}\left(\alpha_{ijk}\right)_{ws}

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\Big(\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\left[\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{w}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}+\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{s}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{= h^d\sum_{i,j,k=1}^N \Big(}-\frac{2}{3}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta,\gamma=1}^{3}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}D_{\gamma}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{ws}\Big)

Focusing on the last term of the inner sum and using that
\delta_{ws}=1\Leftrightarrow w=s

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta,\gamma=1}^{3}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}D_{\gamma}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{ws}&=\sum_{\beta,\gamma=1}^{3}\sum_{w=1}^{3}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ww}D_{\beta}^{c}D_{\gamma}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{\beta\gamma}\\
&=\sum_{\beta,\gamma=1}^{3}D_{\beta}^{c}D_{\gamma}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{\beta\gamma}\sum_{w=1}^{3}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ww}\\
&=0,\end{split}

where in the last equality we used that
A_{ijk}
is trace-free.
For the first two terms, we use Lemma
4.1
and the symmetry assumption to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle A,\alpha_{h}(B)\rangle_{h}&=-h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{w,s,\beta=1}^{3}\left[D_{w}^{c}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}+D_{s}^{c}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right]\\
&=-h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{w,s,\beta=1}^{3}\left[D_{w}^{c}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{sw}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}+D_{s}^{c}\left(A_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(B_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right]\\
&=-2\,\langle\operatorname{div}_{h}A,\operatorname{div}_{h}B\rangle_{h},\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we show that the scheme preserves the trace-free and symmetry property of
Q
. To this end, we rewrite the scheme (
3.4
) as

\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1})=\mathbb{F}(Q^{n})
where

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})&=\frac{Q^{n+1}_{ijk}}{\Delta t}-\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}_{ijk}+\frac{M}{2}\left(\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}:Q^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}-M\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{4}\alpha_{h}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})\\
\mathbb{F}(Q^{n}_{ijk})&=\frac{Q^{n}_{ijk}}{\Delta t}+\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\Delta_{h}Q^{n}_{ijk}+\frac{M}{2}\left(\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}:Q^{n}_{ijk}\right)\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}-Mr^{n}_{ijk}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}+M\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{4}\alpha_{h}(Q^{n}_{ijk})\end{cases}

where we have used that

r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}:Q^{n+1}_{ijk}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}:Q^{n}_{ijk}+r^{n}_{ijk}.

Proposition 4.4
.

If
Q^{n}
and
Q^{n-1}
are trace-free and symmetric, then
Q^{n+1}
computed by the scheme (
3.4
) is also trace-free and symmetric.

Proof.

Since we assume that
Q^{n}_{ijk},Q^{n-1}_{ijk}
are trace-free, it follows that also
\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}
is trace-free.
Moreover,
\alpha_{h}(Q)
is trace-free without any assumptions on
Q
.
Hence, we find that
\tr(\mathbb{F}(Q^{n}_{ijk}))=0
.
But since
\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})=\mathbb{F}(Q^{n}_{ijk})
then we must have
\tr(\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk}))=0
.
Hence,

\tr(\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk}))=\frac{\tr(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})}{\Delta t}-\frac{ML_{1}}{4}\Delta_{h}\tr(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})=0

Taking the inner product of this with
\tr(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})
we then find

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\tr(Q^{n+1})\right\rVert^{2}_{h}}{\Delta t}-\frac{ML_{1}}{4}\langle\Delta_{h}\tr(Q^{n+1}),\tr(Q^{n+1})\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=0.

We use Lemma
4.1
for the second term:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\Delta_{h}\tr(Q^{n+1}),\tr(Q^{n+1})\rangle_{h}&=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\left(D^{+}_{\ell}D^{-}_{\ell}\sum_{w=1}^{3}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})_{ww}\right)\sum_{s=1}^{3}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})_{ss}\\
&=-h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\left(D^{-}_{\ell}\sum_{w=1}^{3}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})_{ww}\right)\left(D^{-}_{\ell}\sum_{s=1}^{3}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})_{ss}\right)\\
&=-\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\tr(Q^{n+1})\right\rVert_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

Thus we must have that
\tr(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})=0
for all
i,j,k=1,\dots,N
and we see that the trace-free condition is preserved.

For the symmetry, we notice that if
Q^{n}_{ijk}
and
Q^{n-1}_{ijk}
are symmetric, then also
\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}
and
\alpha_{h}(Q^{n}_{ijk})
are symmetric. Hence
\mathbb{F}(Q^{n}_{ijk})=(\mathbb{F}(Q^{n}_{ijk}))^{\top}
and therefore
\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})=(\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1}_{ijk}))^{\top}
. Denoting
V^{n+1}_{ijk}:=Q^{n+1}_{ijk}-(Q^{n+1}_{ijk})^{\top}
, this implies

\frac{V^{n+1}_{ijk}}{\Delta t}-\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\Delta_{h}V^{n+1}_{ijk}-M\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{4}\alpha_{h}(V_{ijk}^{n+1})=0.

Note that
V^{n+1}_{ijk}
is skew-symmetric and trace-free. We take the inner product with
V^{n+1}_{ijk}
and obtain

0=\frac{\left\lVert V^{n+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{h}}{\Delta t}-\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\langle\Delta_{h}V^{n+1},V^{n+1}\rangle_{h}-M\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{4}\langle\alpha_{h}(V^{n+1}),V^{n+1}\rangle_{h}.

Using Lemma
4.2
, this can be rewritten as

(4.3)

0=\frac{\left\lVert V^{n+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{h}}{\Delta t}+\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-M\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\langle\alpha_{h}(V^{n+1}),V^{n+1}\rangle_{h}.

The term involving
\alpha
on the right hand side is

\displaystyle\langle V^{n+1},\alpha_{h}(V^{n+1})\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\Big(\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\left[\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{w}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}+\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{s}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{= h^d\sum_{i,j,k=1}^N \Big(}-\frac{2}{3}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta,\gamma=1}^{3}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}D_{\gamma}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{\beta\gamma}\delta_{ws}\Big)

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\left[\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{w}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}+\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{s}^{c}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right],

using that
V^{n+1}
is trace-free, as in (
4.1
) (replacing
A
and
B
by
V^{n+1}
). Using Lemma
4.1
and the skew-symmetry of
V^{n+1}
, the remaining terms are:

\displaystyle\langle V^{n+1},\alpha_{h}(V^{n+1})\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=-h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\left[D_{w}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}+D_{s}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\sum_{w,s=1}^{3}\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\left[D_{w}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{sw}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{s\beta}-D_{s}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{ws}D_{\beta}^{c}\left(V^{n+1}_{ijk}\right)_{w\beta}\right]=0

Plugging this into (
4.3
), we see that
V^{n+1}_{ijk}=0
for all
i,j,k
. ∎

The next theorem guarantees the existence of a unique solution of the system of equations (
3.4
) (or (
4.2
)).

Theorem 4.5
.

The operator
\mathbb{A}
is symmetric and positive definite for grid functions that are symmetric and trace-free.

Proof.

Let
Q^{1}=(Q_{ijk}^{1})_{ijk}
and
Q^{2}=(Q_{ijk}^{2})_{ijk}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mathbb{A}(Q^{1}),Q^{2}\rangle_{h}=&\frac{1}{\Delta t}\langle Q^{1},Q^{2}\rangle_{h}-\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\langle\Delta_{h}Q^{1},Q^{2}\rangle_{h}+\frac{M}{2}\langle\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:Q^{1},\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:Q^{2}\rangle_{h}\\
&-M\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{4}\langle\alpha_{h}(Q^{1}),Q^{2}\rangle_{h}\end{split}

By Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, we have

\langle\Delta_{h}Q^{1},Q^{2}\rangle_{h}=-\langle\nabla_{h}Q^{1},\nabla_{h}Q^{2}\rangle_{h},\quad\langle\alpha_{h}(Q^{1}),Q^{2}\rangle_{h}=-2\langle\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{1},\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{2}\rangle_{h}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mathbb{A}(Q^{1}),Q^{2}\rangle_{h}=&\frac{1}{\Delta t}\langle Q^{1},Q^{2}\rangle_{h}+\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\langle\nabla_{h}Q^{1},\nabla_{h}Q^{2}\rangle_{h}+\frac{M}{2}\langle\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:Q^{1},\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:Q^{2}\rangle_{h}\\
&+\frac{M\,(L_{2}+L_{3})}{2}\,\langle\operatorname{div}^{-}_{h}Q^{1},\operatorname{div}^{-}_{h}Q^{2}\rangle_{h},\end{split}

which we see is symmetric in
Q^{1}
and
Q^{2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mathbb{A}(Q),Q\rangle_{h}=&\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert^{2}_{h}+\frac{ML_{1}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{M}{2}\left\lVert\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:Q\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{M\,(L_{2}+L_{3})}{2}\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q\right\rVert^{2}_{h}\geq 0,\end{split}

with equality if and only if
Q\equiv 0
.
∎

By the previous two results, we conclude there is a unique solution
Q^{n+1}
to
\mathbb{A}(Q^{n+1})=\mathbb{F}(Q^{n})
that is trace-free and symmetric.

Next, we will prove an energy estimate for the scheme:

Theorem 4.6
.

Define the energy

(4.4)

E^{n}=\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert r^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

where
Q^{n},r^{n}
solve (
3.4
).
Then

E^{n+1}-E^{n}=-\Delta tM\left\lVert H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}.

Proof.

We take the inner product of the first equation in (
3.4
) with
-\Delta tH^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}
, multiply by
h^{d}
and sum over all grid points,

(4.5)

-\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}=-\Delta t\,M\left\lVert H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

Taking the inner product of the second equation with
r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}
, multiplying by
h^{d}
, and summing over all grid points, gives

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle r^{n+1}-r^{n},r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}&=\langle r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},Q^{n+1}-Q^{n}\rangle_{h}\\
\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert r^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert r^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}&=\langle r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},Q^{n+1}-Q^{n}\rangle_{h}\end{split}

Next we shall work with the term
\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}
.

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}=&\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},L_{1}\Delta_{h}Q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\alpha^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}\\
=&\frac{L_{1}}{2}\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}+\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}-\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},q^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}\\
&+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{4}\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^{n}\rangle_{h}\end{split}

We shall deal with these terms individually. Using bilinearity, we have

(4.8)

\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}+\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}=\langle Q^{n+1},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}\rangle_{h}+\langle Q^{n+1},\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}-\langle Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}\rangle_{h}-\langle Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}.

We shall focus on the first term of the right hand side. Using Lemma
4.2
and the boundary conditions, we have

\langle Q^{n+1},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}\rangle_{h}=-\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\rVert_{h}^{2}

Similarly, we see that
\langle Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}=-\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}
,
\langle Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}\rangle_{h}=-\langle\nabla_{h}Q^{n},\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\rangle_{h}
and
\langle Q^{n+1},\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}=-\langle\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1},\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}
.
Hence putting all these results into (
4.8
) we find

\displaystyle\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\Delta_{h}Q^{n+1}+\Delta_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=-\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-\langle\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1},\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\rangle_{h}+\langle\nabla_{h}Q^{n},\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\rangle_{h}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

Next we consider the term
\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^{n}\rangle_{h}
in (
4.7
) :

\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^{n}\rangle_{h}=\langle Q^{n+1},\alpha^{n+1}\rangle_{h}+\langle Q^{n+1},\alpha^{n}\rangle_{h}-\langle Q^{n},\alpha^{n+1}\rangle_{h}-\langle Q^{n},\alpha^{n}\rangle_{h}.

Using Lemma
4.3
for each of these terms, we obtain

\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^{n}\rangle_{h}=-2\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+2\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}.

Overall we have shown that

\displaystyle\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left(-\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\right)+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\left(-\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}+\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\langle Q^{n+1}-Q^{n},r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle_{h}.

Combining this with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we obtain

\frac{L_{1}}{2}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-\left\lVert\nabla_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\right)+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\left(\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-\left\lVert\operatorname{div}_{h}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\right)\\
\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert r^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-\left\lVert r^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\right)=-\Delta t\,M\left\lVert H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert^{2}_{h}.

∎

Based on the energy estimate, Theorem
4.6
, we can derive further stability bounds on the approximations
\{Q_{ijk}^{n}\}
and
\{r_{ijk}^{n}\}
.
Specifically, it follows from the bound on
\{H^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\}
that
D_{t}^{+}Q_{ijk}^{n}=MH^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}
is bounded:

Corollary 4.7
.

We have

\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\left\lVert D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\leq E^{0},

where
N_{T}
is such that
T=N_{T}\Delta t
.

Using this corollary and the energy estimate, we can also derive a uniform (in
\Delta t
and
h
) bound on
\left\lVert Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

The following estimate holds for any
\Delta t,h>0
:

\left\lVert Q^{m}\right\rVert_{h}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\left\lVert D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|Q^{0}\|_{h}<\infty,

for any
0\leq m\leq N_{T}
where
N_{T}
is such that
N_{T}\Delta t=T
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}-\left\lVert Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|Q_{ijk}^{n+1}|_{F}^{2}-h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|Q_{ijk}^{n}|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}(Q_{ijk}^{n+1}+Q_{ijk}^{n}):(Q_{ijk}^{n+1}-Q_{ijk}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\left[h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|Q_{ijk}^{n+1}+Q_{ijk}^{n}|_{F}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\left[h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|Q_{ijk}^{n+1}-Q_{ijk}^{n}|_{F}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|Q_{ijk}^{n+1}|_{F}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}|Q_{ijk}^{n}|_{F}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\,\left[h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\left|\frac{Q_{ijk}^{n+1}-Q_{ijk}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right|_{F}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\Delta t

\displaystyle=\left(\left\lVert Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}+\left\lVert Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}\right)\,\|D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\|_{h}\,\Delta t,

and so

\left\lVert Q^{n+1}\right\rVert_{h}-\left\lVert Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}\leq\|D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\|_{h}\,\Delta t,

for any
0\leq n\leq N_{T}
.
Summing over
n
on both sides, we obtain that for any
0\leq m\leq N_{T}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q^{m}\right\rVert_{h}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\|D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\|_{h}\,\Delta t+\|Q^{0}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\|D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\|_{h}^{2}\,\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|Q^{0}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\|D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\|_{h}^{2}\,\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|Q^{0}\|_{h}.

∎

4.2.
Lipschitz continuity of
P(Q)

In order to derive a stability bound on
\{D_{t}^{+}r_{ijk}^{n}\}
, we need an auxiliary result, which is the Lipschitz continuity of
P(Q)
.
Recall that we can write
P(Q)
as

P(Q)=\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)}
where
S
and
r
have been defined in (
1.3
) and (
1.2
).
Note that we can express the Frobenius norm as

|Q|_{F}=\sqrt{\tr(Q^{2})}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}},

where
\lambda_{i}
is the
i
th eigenvalue of matrix
Q
.

We start with a few preliminary lemmas. First, note that
r(Q)
is bounded from below by some constant
A>0
(see also
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
). We will also need an upper bound. Since
c>0
, there exists constant
K_{1}>0
such that for any
Q
for which
|Q|_{F}\geq K_{1}
,

\left|{a}\tr(Q^{2})-\frac{2b}{3}\tr(Q^{3})+2A_{0}\right|\leq\frac{c}{4}\tr^{2}(Q^{2}).

Then

r(Q)\geq\sqrt{\frac{{c}}{2}\text{tr}^{2}(Q^{2})-\left|{a}\text{tr}(Q^{2})-\frac{2b}{3}\text{tr}(Q^{3})+2A_{0}\right|}\geq\sqrt{\frac{{c}}{2}\tr^{2}(Q^{2})-\frac{{c}}{4}\tr^{2}(Q^{2})}=\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}\,\tr(Q^{2}),

and

r(Q)\leq\sqrt{\frac{{c}}{2}\text{tr}^{2}(Q^{2})+\left|{a}\tr(Q^{2})-\frac{2b}{3}\text{tr}(Q^{3})+2A_{0}\right|}\leq\sqrt{\frac{{c}}{2}\text{tr}^{2}(Q^{2})+\frac{{c}}{4}\tr^{2}(Q^{2})}\leq{\sqrt{c}}\tr(Q^{2}).

So whenever
|Q|_{F}\geq K_{1}
, we can bound
r(Q)
by

(4.9)

\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}\,|Q|^{2}_{F}=\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}\,\text{tr}(Q^{2})\leq r(Q)\leq\sqrt{c}\tr(Q^{2})=\sqrt{c}\,|Q|_{F}^{2}.

On the other hand, when
Q
is bounded by constant
K_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle r(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\,\left[\frac{|a|}{2}\tr(Q^{2})+\frac{|b|}{3}\left|\tr(Q^{3})\right|+\frac{c}{4}\tr^{2}(Q^{2})+A_{0}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\,\left(\frac{|a|}{2}K^{2}_{1}+\frac{|b|}{3}K_{1}^{3}+\frac{c}{4}K_{1}^{4}+A_{0}\right)}\triangleq K_{2},

where we have used the fact that

\tr(Q^{4})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{4}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}\leq\tr^{2}(Q^{2}),

and then

|\tr(Q^{3})|\leq\tr^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q^{4})\tr^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q^{2})\leq\tr^{\frac{3}{2}}(Q^{2})=K_{1}^{3}.

Combining the two results, we obtain that

(4.10)

r(Q)\leq K_{2}+\sqrt{c}|Q|^{2}_{F},

for some constant
K_{2}>0
. This bound will be used subsequently. The following lemmas are important steps towards our Lipschitz estimate for
P(Q)
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
Q
, there exist constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
such that

\frac{1}{r(Q)}\leq C_{1},\quad\frac{|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq C_{2},\quad\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq C_{3}.

Proof.

The first estimate follows from the fact that
r(Q)
is bounded from below.
For the third estimate, we split it into two cases. When
|Q|_{F}\leq K_{1}
, we have

\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq\frac{K^{2}_{1}}{A}.

When
|Q|_{F}\geq K_{1}
, by (
4.9
), we know that

\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{\frac{\sqrt{c}}{2}|Q|^{2}_{F}}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{c}}.

Define
C_{3}\triangleq\max\left\{\frac{K^{2}_{1}}{A},\frac{2}{\sqrt{c}}\right\}
, then
\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq C_{3}
.
To prove the second estimate, we note that if
|Q|_{F}\leq 1
, then

\frac{|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq\frac{1}{r(Q)}\leq C_{1}.

Else, we have that

\frac{|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq\frac{|Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}\leq C_{3}.

Defining
C_{2}\triangleq\max\{C_{1},C_{3}\}
, we obtain
\frac{|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq C_{2}
which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For any matrix
Q
,
\left|\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|_{F}
is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

Note that

\left|S(Q)\right|_{F}=\left|aQ-b\left[Q^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\text{tr}(Q^{2})I\right]+c\text{tr}(Q^{2})Q\right|_{F}\leq|a||Q|_{F}+|b||Q^{2}|_{F}+\frac{|b|}{d}\,|Q|^{2}_{F}+c|Q|_{F}^{2}|Q|_{F}.

Since

|Q^{2}|_{F}=\sqrt{\tr(Q^{4})}\leq\sqrt{\tr^{2}(Q^{2})}=\tr(Q^{2})=|Q|_{F}^{2},

we obtain

|S(Q)|_{F}\leq|a||Q|_{F}+\frac{(d+1)|b|}{d}|Q|^{2}_{F}+c\,|Q|_{F}^{3}.

Then from Lemma
4.9
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|a|\,|Q|_{F}+\frac{(d+1)|b|}{d}\,|Q|^{2}_{F}+c\,|Q|_{F}^{3}}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|a|}{r(Q)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}+\frac{\frac{(d+1)|b|}{d}}{r(Q)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}+c\left(\frac{|Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|a|}{A^{\frac{1}{2}}}C_{2}+\frac{(d+1)|b|}{dA^{\frac{1}{2}}}C_{3}+c\,C_{3}^{\frac{3}{2}}\triangleq K_{3},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Now we are in a position to prove that
P(Q)
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the Frobenius norm.

Theorem 4.11
.

There exists a constant
L>0
such that for any matrices
Q,\delta Q\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
,

|P(Q+\delta Q)-P(Q)|_{F}\leq L|\delta Q|_{F}.

Proof.

We will split the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

\delta Q
is so large such that
|\delta Q|_{F}\geq 2|Q|_{F}
and
|\delta Q|_{F}\geq\max\{2K_{1},\,K_{3}\sqrt{K_{2}}\}\triangleq G.
In this case, we can see that

|\delta Q+Q|_{F}\geq|\delta Q|_{F}-|Q|_{F}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,|\delta Q|_{F}\geq K_{1},

therefore, by (
4.9
), we have

(4.11)

r(Q+\delta Q)\leq\sqrt{c}|Q+\delta Q|_{F}\leq\sqrt{c}(|Q|_{F}+|\delta Q|_{F})\leq\frac{3\sqrt{c}}{2}|\delta Q|_{F}.

We use this to compute the difference between
P(Q+\delta Q)
and
P(Q)
,

\displaystyle|P(Q+\delta Q)-P(Q)|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq|P(Q+\delta Q)|_{F}+|P(Q)|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)}{r(Q+\delta Q)}\right|_{F}+\left|\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)}\right|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)}{r(Q+\delta Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|_{F}\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)}+\left|\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|_{F}\sqrt{r(Q)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem }\ref{QoverrQ}}}{{\leq}}K_{3}\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)}+K_{3}\sqrt{r(Q)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{rqestimate2},\eqref{eq:rdeltaestimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{3K_{3}\sqrt{c}}{2}|\delta Q|_{F}+K_{3}(\sqrt{K_{2}}+c^{\frac{1}{4}}|Q|_{F})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3K_{3}\sqrt{c}}{2}|\delta Q|_{F}+|\delta Q|_{F}+\frac{K_{3}c^{\frac{1}{4}}}{2}|\delta Q|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3K_{3}\sqrt{c}}{2}+1+\frac{K_{3}c^{\frac{1}{4}}}{2}\right)|\delta Q|_{F},

which proves the result in this case.

Case 2:

|\delta Q|_{F}\leq 2|Q|_{F}
or
|\delta Q|_{F}\leq G
.

In this case, we write the difference of
P(Q+\delta Q)
and
P(Q)
as

\displaystyle|P(Q+\delta Q)-P(Q)|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)}{r(Q+\delta Q)}-\frac{S(Q)}{r(Q)}\right|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)-S(Q)}{r(Q)}\,+\,S(Q+\delta Q)\left(\frac{1}{r(Q+\delta Q)}-\frac{1}{r(Q)}\right)\right|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)-S(Q)}{r(Q)}\right|_{F}}_{I}+\underbrace{\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)}{r(Q+\delta Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,\,\frac{\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)}\left[r(Q+\delta Q)-r(Q)\right]}{r(Q)}\right|_{F}}_{{II}}.

To compute
{I}
, we expand
S(Q+\delta Q)
by plugging in
(Q+\delta Q)
into (
1.3
):

\displaystyle S(Q+\delta Q)=

\displaystyle S(Q)+a\,\delta Q-b\,(Q\,\delta Q+\delta Q\,Q)-b\,(\delta Q)^{2}+\frac{2b}{d}\tr\left(Q\delta Q\right)I

\displaystyle+\frac{b}{d}\tr\left((\delta Q)^{2}\right)I+c\tr((\delta Q)^{2})\delta Q+2c\tr(Q\,\delta Q)Q

\displaystyle+2c\tr(Q\,\delta Q)\delta Q+c\tr(Q^{2})\delta Q+c\tr\left((\delta Q)^{2}\right)Q.

Then by Lemma
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle{I}\leq

\displaystyle|a|\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}+\frac{2(d+1)|b|}{d}\frac{|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}+\frac{(d+1)|b|}{d}\frac{|\delta Q|^{2}_{F}}{r(Q)}+c\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}+3|Q|_{F}^{2}|\delta Q|_{F}+3|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a|\,C_{1}\,|\delta Q|_{F}+\frac{2(d+1)C_{2}|b|}{d}|\delta Q|_{F}+\frac{(d+1)|b|}{d}\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}+3c\,C_{3}|\delta Q|_{F}+c\,\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}}{r(Q)}+3c\frac{|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}.

We still need to bound
\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}
,
\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}}{r(Q)}
and
\frac{|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}
in terms of
\delta Q
. Based on our assumption in this case, if
|\delta Q|_{F}\leq 2|Q|_{F}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2|Q|_{F}\,|\delta Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq 2C_{2}\,|\delta Q|_{F},

\displaystyle\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}}{r(Q)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4|Q|_{F}^{2}|\delta Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq 4C_{3}\,|\delta Q|_{F}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\frac{|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|Q|^{2}_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}}{r(Q)}\leq 2C_{3}|\delta Q|_{F}.

Hence, plugging this into (
4.12
), we obtain

{I}\leq\left(|a|\,C_{1}+\frac{4(d+1)}{d}|b|\,C_{2}+13c\,C_{3}\right)|\delta Q|_{F}

On the other hand, if
|\delta Q|_{F}\leq G,
then we can bound
\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}
,
\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}}{r(Q)}
and
\frac{|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}
by

\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}\leq GC_{1}|\delta Q|_{F},\quad\frac{|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}}{r(Q)}\leq G^{2}C_{1}|\delta Q|_{F},\quad\frac{|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)}\leq G\,C_{2}|\delta Q|_{F}.

Plugging these into (
4.12
), we arrive at

\displaystyle{I}\leq\left(|a|\,C_{1}+\frac{2(d+1)}{d}|b|\,C_{2}+\frac{d+1}{d}|b|\,G\,C_{1}+3c\,C_{3}+c\,G^{2}\,C_{1}+3c\,G\,C_{2}\right)|\delta Q|_{F}.

Therefore,
{I}\leq Z_{1}|\delta Q|_{F}
for some constant
Z_{1}
depending on
C_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
,
G,a,b
and
c
.
To bound term
{II}
, note that

\displaystyle{II}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{S(Q+\delta Q)}{r(Q+\delta Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|_{F}\,\frac{\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)}|r(Q+\delta Q)-r(Q)|}{r(Q)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem }\ref{lem:SQbound}}}{{\leq}}K_{3}\,\frac{\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)}\,\,|r(Q+\delta Q)-r(Q)|}{r(Q)}

\displaystyle=K_{3}\,\frac{\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)}\,\,|r(Q+\delta Q)^{2}-r(Q)^{2}|}{r(Q)\,\,[r(Q+\delta Q)+r(Q)]}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}\,\frac{\,\,|r(Q+\delta Q)^{2}-r(Q)^{2}|}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

where we have used the fact

r(Q+\delta Q)+r(Q)\geq\,2\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)\,r(Q)}\geq\,\sqrt{r(Q+\delta Q)\,r(Q)}.

Expanding
r(Q+\delta Q)
by plugging in
(Q+\delta Q)
into (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle r(Q+\delta Q)^{2}=

\displaystyle r(Q)^{2}+2a\tr(Q\,\delta Q)+a\tr((\delta Q)^{2})-\frac{2b}{3}\tr((\delta Q)^{3})-2b\tr(Q^{2}\,\delta Q)

\displaystyle-2b\tr(Q\,(\delta Q)^{2})+\frac{c}{2}\text{tr}^{2}((\delta Q)^{2})+2c\tr^{2}(Q\,\delta Q)

\displaystyle+2c\tr(Q^{2})\tr(Q\,\delta Q)+c\tr(Q^{2})\tr((\delta Q)^{2})+2c\tr((\delta Q)^{2})\tr(Q\,\delta Q).

We plug this into (
4.13
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{3}}\,{II}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2|a|\,|Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}+|a|\,|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2|b|}{3}|\delta Q|_{F}^{3}+2|b||Q|_{F}^{2}|\delta Q|_{F}+2|b||Q|_{F}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{c}{2}|\delta Q|^{4}_{F}+3c|Q|_{F}^{2}|\delta Q|_{F}^{2}+2c\,|Q|_{F}^{3}|\delta Q|_{F}+2c|\delta Q|^{3}_{F}|Q|_{F}}{r(Q)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

In a similar way as for term
{I}
, we can find constant
Z_{2}
such that

{II}\leq Z_{2}|\delta Q|_{F}
.
To sum up, if we choose
L=\max\{\frac{3K_{3}\sqrt{c}}{2}+1+\frac{K_{3}c^{\frac{1}{4}}}{2},Z_{1},Z_{2}\}
, then

|P(Q+\delta Q)-P(Q)|_{F}\leq L|\delta Q|_{F},

for any
Q
and
\delta Q
.
∎

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
P(Q)
, it is now easy to prove the following bound on
\{D_{t}^{+}r_{ijk}^{n}\}
:

Lemma 4.12
.

We have

\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}|D_{t}^{+}r_{ijk}^{n}|\right)^{2}\leq C<\infty,

for
0\leq m\leq N_{T}
where
N_{T}
is such that
N_{T}\Delta t=T
and
C>0
is a constant independent of
h
and
\Delta t
.

Proof.

We take absolute values of the scheme for
r_{ijk}^{n}
, the second equation in (
3.4
) divided by
\Delta t
:

\left|D_{t}^{+}r_{ijk}^{n}\right|=\left|\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}:D_{t}^{+}Q_{ijk}^{n}\right|,

and sum over
i,j,k=1,\dots N
, then multiply by
h^{3}
, square and sum over
n=0,\dots,m
and use Hölder’s inequality:

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left|D_{t}^{+}r_{ijk}^{n}\right|\right)^{2}&=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left|\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}:D_{t}^{+}Q_{ijk}^{n}\right|\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left|\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}\right|^{2}\right)\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left|D_{t}^{+}Q_{ijk}^{n}\right|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Next, we use the Lipschitz continuity of
P(Q)
, and then Lemma
4.8
,

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left(h^{3}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left|D_{t}^{+}r_{ijk}^{n}\right|\right)^{2}&\leq Ch^{6}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left(\left|Q_{ijk}^{n}\right|^{2}+\left|Q_{ijk}^{n-1}\right|^{2}\right)\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\left|D_{t}^{+}Q_{ijk}^{n}\right|^{2}\\
&\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left\lVert D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\,\max_{0\leq\ell\leq m}\left\lVert Q^{\ell}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem. }\ref{lem:l2bound}}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left\lVert D_{t}^{+}Q^{n}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}\end{split}

Multiplying by
\Delta t
and using Corollary
4.7
, we obtain the result.

∎

5.
Convergence of the scheme

Using the estimates established in the previous section, we proceed to proving convergence of the scheme (
3.4
) to a weak solution of (
1.5
).
To do so, we define piecewise constant interpolations of the grid functions
\{Q^{n}_{ijk}\}
,
\{r^{n}_{ijk}\}
and
\{\overline{P}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}\}
,

(5.1)

Q^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)=\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}Q_{ijk}^{n}\,\chi_{C_{ijk}},\quad r^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)=\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}r_{ijk}^{n}\,\chi_{C_{ijk}},\quad P^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)=\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{N+1}\overline{P}_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\,\chi_{C_{ijk}}.

where

C_{ijk}=[(i-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})h,(i+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})h]\times[(j-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})h,(j+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})h]\times[(k-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})h,(k+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})h]
and
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
. Then, we define
piecewise constant interpolations in time,

(5.2)

\displaystyle Q_{h,\Delta t}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\,Q^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)\chi_{S_{n}}(t),

(5.3)

\displaystyle r_{h,\Delta t}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\,r^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)\chi_{S_{n}}(t),

(5.4)

\displaystyle P_{h,\Delta t}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\,P^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)\chi_{S_{n}}(t),

where
T=N_{T}\Delta t
and
S_{n}=[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t)
.
We will show that a subsequence of these converges to a weak solution of (
1.5
):

Theorem 5.1
.

The piecewise constant interpolations (
5.2
)–(
5.4
) computed using scheme (
3.4
) converge up to a subsequence to a weak solution of (
1.5
) (as in Definition
2.3
) as
h,\Delta t\to 0
.

Proof.

Step 1: Compactness.

We apply the first order finite difference operator
D_{t}^{+}
on
Q_{h,\Delta t}
and
r_{h,\Delta t}
,

(5.5)

D^{+}_{t}Q_{h,\Delta t}(t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\frac{Q^{n+1}_{h,\Delta t}(x)-Q^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)}{\Delta t}\chi_{S_{n}},\quad\text{and }\quad D^{+}_{t}r_{h,\Delta t}(t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\frac{r^{n+1}_{h,\Delta t}(x)-r^{n}_{h,\Delta t}(x)}{\Delta t}\chi_{S_{n}}.

From the energy stability of the scheme, Theorem
4.6
, it follows that
\{\nabla_{h}Q_{h,\Delta t}\}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

and
\{r_{h,\Delta t}\}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

uniformly in
\Delta t,h>0
. Corollary
4.7
yields
\{D_{t}^{+}Q_{h,\Delta t}\}\subset L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
uniformly in
h,\Delta t>0
.
Moreover, from Lemma
4.8
, we get

\left\lVert Q_{h,\Delta t}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D_{t}^{+}Q_{h,\Delta t}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|Q_{h,\Delta t}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty,

and hence
\{Q_{h,\Delta t}\}\subset L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
.
Therefore, we can apply a discretized version of the Aubin-Lions lemma
[
14
, Lemma A.1]
, to conclude that
there exists
Q\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))
and a subsequence
\{Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\}_{m}
such that
Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\to Q
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
as
m\to\infty
. Due to the uniform bounds, we also obtain
\nabla_{h_{m}}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\rightharpoonup\nabla Q
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)

and we can extract a weakly convergent subsequence of
\{D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\}_{m}
and
\{r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\}_{m}
, for simplicity still indexed by
m
. In summary, we have the following:

(5.6)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\to Q,\qquad\text{in }L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega),\quad

\displaystyle D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\rightharpoonup Q_{t}\qquad\text{in }L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega),

\displaystyle\nabla_{h}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\rightharpoonup\nabla Q,\qquad\text{in }L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega),

and

(5.7)

r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}g,\quad\text{ in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)).

Since we have shown that
P(Q)
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
Q
in Theorem
4.11
, we obtain from the strong convergence of
\{Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\}_{m}
that

(5.8)

P(\,Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,)\to P(Q),\qquad\text{in }L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega).

Step 2: Passing to the limit
m\to\infty
.

Next, we show that the sequences
\{Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\}_{m}
,
\{r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\}_{m}
converge to a weak solution of (
1.5
), that is, that the limit
(Q,r)
is a weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.3
.
We start with the equation for the variable
r
.
From the numerical scheme (
3.4
), it follows that

(5.9)

D^{+}_{t}r_{h,\Delta t}=P_{h,\Delta t}(t,x):D^{+}_{t}Q_{h,\Delta t}.

For any smooth test function
\phi
with compact support in
[0,T]\times\Omega
, we have
P(Q)\,\phi\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
. Therefore, using the weak convergence
D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\rightharpoonup Q_{t}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
we find that,

(5.10)

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}P(Q):D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\phi\,dxdt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\phi\,dxdt.

Moreover, by the strong convergence of
P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\,P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}-P(Q)\,):D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\phi\,dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T])}\,\|P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}-P(Q)\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\,\|D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Therefore, we can multiply
\phi
on both sides of (
5.9
), integrate over both space
\Omega
and time interval
[0,T]
and apply (
5.10
) to obtain,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D^{+}_{t}r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\phi\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}:D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\phi\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\,P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}-P(Q)\,):D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\phi\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P(Q):D^{+}_{t}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\phi\,dxdt

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\,\phi\,dxdt.

For the left hand side of (
5.9
), we combine the definition of the piecewise constant functions, (
5.1
) and (
5.5
), rename the integration variables so that the difference operator acts on the smooth test function:

LHS

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\,\frac{r^{n+1}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}(x)-r^{n}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}(x)}{\Delta t_{m}}\,\phi(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\,r^{n}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,\frac{\phi(x,t-\Delta t_{m})-\phi(t,x)}{\Delta t_{m}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\Delta t_{m}}\,\int_{S_{N_{T}-1}}\int_{\Omega}r^{N_{T}}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,\phi(t,x)\,dxdt-\frac{1}{\Delta t_{m}}\,\int_{S_{0}}\int_{\Omega}r^{0}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,\phi(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,\frac{\phi(x,t-\Delta t_{m})-\phi(t,x)}{\Delta t_{m}}\,dxdt

(5.11)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\Delta t_{m}}\,\int_{S_{N_{T}-1}}\int_{\Omega}r^{N_{T}}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,\phi(t,x)\,dxdt-\frac{1}{\Delta t_{m}}\,\int_{S_{0}}\int_{\Omega}r^{0}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,\phi(t,x)\,dxdt.

When
\phi
has compact support in
[0,T)\times\Omega
, the second term on the right hand side vanishes, and we can use the weak* convergence of
\{r_{h,\Delta t}\}
, (
5.7
), to pass the limit
h,\Delta t\to 0
,

\displaystyle\text{LHS}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,g\phi_{t}dxdt-\int_{\Omega}r^{0}(x)\phi(0,x)dx.

Using
[
12
, Lemma 1.1, p. 250]
, this implies that
r
is weakly continuous in time on
L^{1}(\Omega)
, since
P(Q):Q_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
by the Lipschitz continuity of
P
. Lemma
A.2
then implies that also
\int r_{h,\Delta t}(t,x)\phi(t,x)dx\to\int g(t,x)\phi(t,x)dx
for every
t\in[0,T]
up to a subsequence as
h,\Delta t\to 0
, and hence we can pass to the limit in the left hand side (
5
) when
\phi
is compactly supported in
[0,T]\times\Omega
.
Thus the limit
g
satisfies (
2.4
).

Next we show that the limit
Q
satisfies (
2.3
). We take the inner product of the first equation in (
3.4
) with a smooth matrix-valued function
\varphi=(\varphi_{\alpha\beta})_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
integrated over
S_{n}\times C_{ijk}
, i.e.,
\iint_{S_{n}\times C_{ijk}}\varphi\,dxdt
and then sum over
n
and
i,j,k
. We obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\int_{S_{n}}\,\int_{C_{ijk}}\frac{Q_{ijk}^{n+1}-Q_{ijk}^{n}}{\Delta t}\,:\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\int_{S_{n}}\,\int_{C_{ijk}}M\,(L_{1}\Delta_{h}Q_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{ijk}\overline{P}_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\alpha_{ijk}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\,:\varphi\,dxdt.

We rewrite this in terms of the piecewise constant functions (
5.1
):

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\,\int_{\Omega}D_{t}^{+}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n}\,:\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\,\int_{\Omega}M\,(L_{1}\Delta_{h}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\overline{P}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\alpha_{h}(Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}})^{n+\frac{1}{2}})\,:\varphi\,dxdt.

(Here
\alpha_{h}(Q_{h,\Delta t})^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\alpha_{h}(Q_{h,\Delta t}^{n})+\alpha_{h}(Q_{h,\Delta t}^{n+1}))
.)
Since
\{D_{t}^{+}Q_{h,\Delta t}\}
is weakly convergent in
L^{2}
, c.f. (
5.6
), we can pass to the limit
m\to\infty
in the left hand side and obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\,\int_{\Omega}D_{t}^{+}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n}\,:\varphi\,dxdt\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}Q_{t}:\varphi\,dxdt.

Integrating by parts, we obtain the left hand side of (
2.3
).
To deal with the right hand side of (
5.12
), we introduce the discrete forward and difference operators
D_{k}^{+}
and
D^{-}_{k}
for matrix functions
\varphi=(\varphi_{\alpha\beta})_{\alpha\beta}
,
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq d
. Similar to (
3.1
),
D_{k}^{+}
denotes the forward difference in the coordinate direction
k
. For example, for
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and
k=1
, we define

\left(D_{1}^{\pm}\varphi(x)\right)_{\alpha\beta}=\pm\,\frac{\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(t,x_{1}\pm h,x_{2},x_{3})-\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(t,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}{h}.

In addition, we introduce the discrete gradient and divergence operators for smooth
\varphi
:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{h}^{\pm}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta}=\left((D^{\pm}_{1}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta},\,(D^{\pm}_{2}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta},\,(D^{\pm}_{3}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta}\right)^{\top},

\displaystyle(\operatorname{div}_{h}\varphi)_{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{d}(D^{c}_{i}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta}

where
\varphi_{\alpha\beta}
is the
(\alpha,\beta)
-entry of the matrix
\varphi
.
Renaming the integration variables such that the difference operators act on the test functions in the right hand side of (
5.12
) and then using (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), the right hand side of (
5.12
) satisfies

RHS

\displaystyle=-ML_{1}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla^{-}_{h_{m}}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\nabla_{h_{m}}^{-}\varphi-M\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}r^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\overline{P}_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}:\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-M\frac{(L_{2}+L_{3})}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1}^{d}\left(D_{\gamma}^{c}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n}\right)_{\beta\gamma}(D_{\alpha}^{c}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\quad-M\frac{(L_{2}+L_{3})}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1}^{d}\left(D_{\gamma}^{c}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n}\right)_{\alpha\gamma}(D_{\beta}^{c}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{M(L_{2}+L_{3})}{d}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-1}\int_{S_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha\beta,\gamma=1}^{d}\left(D_{\alpha}^{c}Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}^{n}\right)_{\gamma\alpha}(D_{\gamma}^{c}\varphi)_{\beta\beta}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}-ML_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\nabla Q_{\alpha\beta}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{\alpha\beta}\,dx\,dt-M\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}:\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-M\,\frac{L_{2}+L_{3}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{\gamma}Q_{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}+\partial_{\gamma}Q_{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{2}{d}\partial_{\alpha}Q_{\gamma\alpha}\partial_{\gamma}\varphi_{\beta\beta}\right)\,dxdt,

where
\nabla_{h}^{-}Q_{h,\Delta t}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\nabla^{-}_{h}\varphi=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{d}\,\nabla_{h}^{-}(Q_{h,\Delta t}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}\cdot(\nabla_{h}^{-}\varphi)_{\alpha\beta}.

It remains to show

(5.13)

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r_{h_{m},\Delta,t_{m}}\,P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}:\varphi\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}g\,P(Q):\varphi\,dx\,dt.

To prove (
5.13
), we take the difference of the two terms, that is,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}:\varphi\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}g\,P(Q):\varphi\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,(P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}-P(Q)):\varphi\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(g-r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}})\,P(Q):\varphi\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\,(P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}-P(Q)):\varphi\,dx\,dt\right|}_{{I}}+\underbrace{\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(g-r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}})\,P(Q):\varphi\,dx\,dt\right|}_{{II}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
5.8
), and the energy estimate, Theorem
4.6
,

{I}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T])}\,\|P_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}-P(Q)\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\,\|r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\to 0.

Note that
P(Q)\,\varphi\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and
r_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}\rightharpoonup g
in
L^{2}
,
therefore
{II}\to 0
. This proves (
5.13
).
Combining the estimates for the left and the right hand side, we see that
Q
satisfies (
2.3
).
The trace-free condition and the symmetry are linear constraints and therefore conserved under the
L^{2}
-convergence of
Q_{h,\Delta t}
. The energy inequality is a direct result by passing the limits in Theorem
4.6
and using Fatou’s lemma. Hence the limit
(Q,r)
is a weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.3
.
∎

5.1.
Equivalence of weak formulations (
r=r(Q)
)

Now that we have established that the scheme converges to a weak solution of (
1.5
), it remains to show that such a weak solution is in fact a weak solution of (
1.1
). To do so, we show that the limit
g
established above in (
5.7
) satisfies
g=r(Q)
weakly, where
Q
is the limit of
Q_{h_{m},\Delta t_{m}}
and
r(Q)
is defined in (
1.2
). Plugging this into the weak formulation (
2.1
), we see that
Q
is in fact a weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.2
. We thus need to prove the following lemma:

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
(Q,g)
is a weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.3
. Then for any smooth
\psi
with compact support in
(0,T)\times\Omega
(compactly supported in both time and space), we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,g\,\psi dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,\,r(Q)\,\psi\,dxdt,

where
r(Q)
is defined in (
1.2
).

Proof.

Since
(Q,g)
is a weak solution of (
1.5
), we have that

(5.14)

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}g\psi_{t}\,dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\,\psi\,dxdt

for
\psi
smooth and compactly supported in
(0,T)\times\Omega
.
For the right hand side, if
Q
is a smooth function, we can use chain rule and integration by parts to get

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\,\psi=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,r(Q)_{t}\,\psi=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r(Q)\psi_{t}.

Since
Q\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))
and
Q_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
, we can find a sequence of smooth function
\left\{Q_{n}\right\}_{n}
with
Q_{n}\to Q
in
L^{2}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))
and
(Q_{n})_{t}\to Q_{t}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.
We note that by mean value theorem,

r(Q)-r(Q_{n})=P(\tilde{Q}):(Q-Q_{n}),

for some
\tilde{Q}=\lambda_{1}Q+\lambda_{2}Q_{n}
where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1]
and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
. Noting that
P(Q)
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
Q
, so
|P(\tilde{Q})|_{F}\leq\tilde{L}|\tilde{Q}|_{F}
for some constant
\tilde{L}>0
. Therefore,

\left|r(Q)-r(Q_{n})\right|_{F}=\left|P(\tilde{Q}):(Q-Q_{n})\right|_{F}\leq\tilde{L}\,(\left|Q\right|_{F}+\left|Q_{n}\right|_{F})\,\left|Q-Q_{n}\right|_{F}.

Integrating it over time and space and we obtain

\displaystyle\|r(Q)-r(Q_{n})\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{L}\,\left(\|Q\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|Q_{n}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\right)\,||Q-Q_{n}||_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\to 0,

since
Q
and
Q_{n}
are both bounded in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
.
So if we use smooth functions to approximate
Q
, we obtain,

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,(\,r(Q)-r(Q_{n})\,)\,\psi_{t}\right|\leq\|\psi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\,\left\|r(Q)-r(Q_{n})\right\|_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\to 0.

On the other hand, using the Lipschitz continuity of
P(Q)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,(\,P(Q):Q_{t}\,\psi-P(Q_{n}):(Q_{n})_{t}\,\psi\,)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,(\,P(Q):Q_{t}-P(Q):(Q_{n})_{t}\,)\,\psi\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,(\,P(Q):(Q_{n})_{t}-P(Q_{n}):(Q_{n})_{t}\,)\,\psi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|P(Q)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\|Q_{t}-(Q_{n})_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|P(Q)-P(Q_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\|(Q_{n})_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Therefore, we have for any
Q\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{1}(\Omega))
with
Q_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\,\psi=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}P(Q_{n}):(Q_{n})_{t}\,\psi=-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}r(Q_{n})\psi_{t}=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\Omega}r(Q)\psi_{t}.

We use this in (
5.14
) to obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,g\psi_{t}=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}P(Q):Q_{t}\,\psi=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r(Q)\psi_{t}.

From
[
12
, Lemma 1.1, p. 250]
, we obtain that
g
as well as
r(Q)
are absolutely continuous and satisfy for every test function
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and almost every
t\in[0,T]

\int_{\Omega}g(t,x)\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}r(Q(t,x))\psi(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}f(x)\psi(x)\,dx

for some
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
However, since
g
satisfies (
2.4
), by letting
T
be
0
in (
2.4
), we find

\int_{\Omega}g(0,x)\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}r^{0}(x)\psi(x)\,dx.

and so
f=0
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. This proves the lemma.

∎

This lemma shows that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}g\,P(Q):\varphi\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}r(Q)\,P(Q):\varphi\,dx\,dt,

for any smooth and compactly supported
\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Plugging this into (
2.3
), we see that the identity becomes (
2.1
) and hence any weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.3
is in fact a weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Hence we have shown:

Theorem 5.3
.

Approximations computed by the numerical scheme (
3.4
) converge as
\Delta t,h\to 0
, up to a subsequence, to weak solutions of (
1.1
) as in Definition
2.2
.

A new result for the local well-posedness of the Camassa-Holm type equations in critial Besov spaces B p , 1 1 + 1 p , 1 ≤ p < + ∞ B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1},1\leq p<+\infty
Weikui Ye and, Zhaoyang Yin and, Yingying Guo

Abstract
For the famous Camassa-Holm equation, the well-posedness in B p , 1 1 + 1 p ​ ( ℝ ) B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}) with 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 1\leq p\leq 2 and the ill-posedness in B p , r 1 + 1 p ​ ( ℝ ) B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}) with 1 ≤ p ≤ + ∞ , 1 < r ≤ + ∞ 1\leq p\leq+\infty,\ 1<r\leq+\infty had been studied in [ 23 , 24 , 31 ] . That is to say, it left an open problem in the critical case B p , 1 1 + 1 p ​ ( ℝ ) B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}) with 2 < p ≤ + ∞ 2<p\leq+\infty proposed by Danchin in [ 23 , 24 ] . In this paper, we solve this problem. The main difficulty is to prove the uniqueness, which usually needs to use the Moser-type inequality, resulting in the index p p belongs to [ 1 , 2 ] [1,2] . To overcome the difficulty, inspired by Linares, Ponce and Thomas [ 37 ] , we combine the Lagrange coordinate transformation and small time conditions to avoid using the Moser-type inequality. As a result, we obtain the local well-posedness for the Camassa-Holm equation in critical Besov spaces B p , 1 1 + 1 p ​ ( ℝ ) B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}) with 1 ≤ p < + ∞ 1\leq p<+\infty . It is worth mentioning that our method is suitable for many Camassa-Holm type equations such as the Novikov equation and the two-component Camassa-Holm system, which can also improve their index on the local well-posedness.

The main difficulty is to prove the uniqueness. For instance, one should use the following Moser-type inequality

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{{B}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\leq C\|f\|_{{B}^{s_{1}}_{p,1}}\|g\|_{{B}^{s_{2}}_{p,1}},\quad s_{1},s_{2}\leq\frac{d}{p},\ s_{1}+s_{2}>d\max\{0,\frac{2}{p}-1\}

That is why one needs the condition

T>0

1.1

1.1

To establish the well-posedness for the Camassa-Holm type equations, we first consider the following Cauchy problem for a general abstract equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+A(u)\partial_{x}u=F(u),&\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(t,x)|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
A(u)
is a polynomial of
u
and
F
is called a ‘good operator’ such that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and any
\epsilon>0
small enough, the following fact holds

\text{if}\quad u_{n}\varphi\rightarrow u\varphi\quad\text{in}\quad B^{1+\frac{1}{p}-\epsilon}_{p,1},\quad\text{then}\quad\langle F(u_{n}),\varphi\rangle\longrightarrow\langle F(u),\varphi\rangle.

This definition is reasonable for the Camassa-Holm type equations. For example, it’s easy to prove that
-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\Big(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}\Big)
is a ‘good operator’ by an approximation argument, owing to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

The associated Lagrangian scale of (
1.2
) is the following initial valve problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{{{\rm d}}y}{{{\rm d}}t}=A(u)\big(t,y(t,\xi)\big),&\quad t>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
y(0,\xi)=\xi,&\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

Introduce the new variable
U(t,\xi)=u\big(t,y(t,\xi)\big)
. Then, (
1.2
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U_{t}=\Big(F(u)\Big)(t,y(t,\xi)):=\widetilde{F}(U,y),&\quad t>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
U(t,\xi)|_{t=0}=U_{0}(\xi)=u_{0}(\xi),&\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

Before giving our main results, let’s introduce two key abstract theorems:

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}\leq C\Big(\|u\|^{k+1}_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}+1\Big);

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}(U,y)-\widetilde{F}(\bar{U},\bar{y})\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}\leq C_{u_{0}}\Big(\|U-\bar{U}\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}+\|y-\bar{y}\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}\Big);

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|F(u)-F(\bar{u})\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}\leq C_{u_{0}}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}.

(1.7)

Then, there exists a time

such that

(1)

Existence: If (

Similarly, for the two-component Camassa-Holm type equations, we can investigate the following abstract equations :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+A(u)\partial_{x}u=F\big(u,v\big),&\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\

In this section, we first recall some basic properties on the Littlewood-Paley theory, which can be found in
[

Let
\chi
and
\varphi

[0,1]

For any

Let

The nonhomogeneous Besov space

B^{s}_{p,r}=B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Big\{u\in S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\big\|(2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}})_{j}\big\|_{l^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\Big\}.

The nonhomogeneous Bony’s decomposition is defined by

uv=T_{u}v+T_{v}u+R(u,v)

T_{u}v=\sum_{j}S_{j-1}u\Delta_{j}v,\ \ R(u,v)=\sum_{j}\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{j}u\Delta_{j^{\prime}}v.

Step 1. Existence.

F
is ‘good’ operator by an approximation argument and

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}+\|u_{x}\|_{B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}\Big)\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}+1\Big);

\displaystyle\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|_{B^{\frac{1}{p}+1}_{p,1}}\leq C\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}.

So (
1.5
) and (
1.7
) hold, which implies (
4.1
) has a solution
u\in E^{p}_{T}
. To prove the local well-posedness, one also need to verify (
1.6
).

To prove (
1.6
), we establish the Lagrangian coordinates:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{{{\rm d}}}{{{\rm d}}t}y=u\big(t,y(t,\xi)\big),\\
y(0,\xi)=\xi.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Setting
U_{1}:=u_{1}(t,y_{1}(t,\xi))
and
U_{2}:=u_{2}(t,y_{2}(t,\xi))
, we see for
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\frac{{{\rm d}}}{{{\rm d}}t}U_{i}=\widetilde{F}(U_{i},y_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{i}(t,\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{i}(t,\xi)-x|}(u_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{ix}^{2}){{\rm d}}x,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{{{\rm d}}}{{{\rm d}}t}U_{i\xi}=\left(\widetilde{F}(U_{i},y_{i})\right)_{\xi}=U_{i}^{2}y_{i\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{U^{2}_{i\xi}}{y_{i\xi}}-\frac{y_{i\xi}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-|y_{i}(t,\xi)-x|}(u_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{ix}^{2}){{\rm d}}x.

(4.4)

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
in
Step 2
, for sufficiently small
T>0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle U_{i}(t,\xi)\in L^{\infty}_{T}(W^{1,p}\cap W^{1,\infty}),~y_{i}(t,\xi)-\xi\in L^{\infty}_{T}(W^{1,p}\cap W^{1,\infty})~\text{and}~\frac{1}{2}\leq y_{i\xi}\leq C_{u_{0}}.

(4.5)

First, we estimate
\|\widetilde{F}(U_{1},y_{1})-\widetilde{F}(U_{2},y_{2})\|_{L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}}
. The main difficulty in it is to estimate

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{1}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-x|}u_{1x}^{2}{{\rm d}}x-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{2}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-x|}u_{2x}^{2}{{\rm d}}x.

Since
y_{i}(i=1,2)
is monotonically increasing, then
{\rm sign}\big(y_{i}(\xi)-y_{i}(\eta)\big)={\rm sign}\big(\xi-\eta\big)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{1}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-x|}u_{1x}^{2}{{\rm d}}x-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{2}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-x|}u_{2x}^{2}{{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{1}(\xi)-y_{1}(\eta)\big)e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-y_{1}(\eta)|}\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta)\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta)|}\frac{U_{2\eta}^{2}}{y_{2\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(\xi-\eta\big)e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-y_{1}(\eta)|}\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(\xi-\eta\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta)|}\frac{U_{2\eta}^{2}}{y_{2\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(\xi-\eta\big)\left(e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-y_{1}(\eta)|}-e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta)|}\right)\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(\xi-\eta\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta)|}\left(\frac{U_{2\eta}^{2}}{y_{2\eta}}-\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}\right){{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

(4.6)

If
\xi>\eta

(\text{or}\ \xi<\eta)
, then
y_{i}(\xi)>y_{i}(\eta)

\big(\text{or}\ y_{i}(\xi)<y_{i}(\eta)\big)
. Hence, we gain

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\left(e^{-(y_{1}(\xi)-y_{1}(\eta))}-e^{-(y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta))}\right)\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta-\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}\left(e^{y_{1}(\xi)-y_{1}(\eta)}-e^{y_{2}(\xi)-y_{2}(\eta)}\right)\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}e^{-(\xi-\eta)}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{t}(U_{1}(\xi)-U_{1}(\eta)){{\rm d}}\tau}-e^{-\int_{0}^{t}(U_{2}(\xi)-U_{2}(\eta)){{\rm d}}\tau}\right)\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle-\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}e^{\xi-\eta}\left(e^{\int_{0}^{t}U_{1}(\xi)-U_{1}(\eta){{\rm d}}\tau}-e^{\int_{0}^{t}U_{2}(\xi)-U_{2}(\eta){{\rm d}}\tau}\right)\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}e^{-(\xi-\eta)}\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta+\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}e^{\xi-\eta}\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}{{\rm d}}\eta\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}\Big].

(4.7)

In the same way, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1\eta}-U_{2\eta}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1\eta}-U_{2\eta}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)\Big].

(4.8)

Combining with the identities (
4.6
)–(
4.8
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{1}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-x|}u_{1x}^{2}{{\rm d}}x-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{2}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-x|}u_{2x}^{2}{{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}\Big]

\displaystyle+C\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1\eta}-U_{2\eta}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1\eta}-U_{2\eta}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)\Big].

(4.9)

Similar to (
4.9
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{1}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{1}(\xi)-x|}u_{1}^{2}{{\rm d}}x-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm sign}\big(y_{2}(\xi)-x\big)e^{-|y_{2}(\xi)-x|}u_{2}^{2}{{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(U_{1\eta}^{2}y_{1\eta}\Big)+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(U_{1\eta}^{2}y_{1\eta}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+C\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1}-U_{2}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1}-U_{2}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)\Big].

(4.10)

It follows from (
4.3
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{F}(U_{1},y_{1})-\widetilde{F}(U_{2},y_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1}-U_{2}|+|U_{1\eta}-U_{2\eta}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+C\Big[1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(|U_{1}-U_{2}|+|U_{1\eta}-U_{2\eta}|+|y_{1\eta}-y_{2\eta}|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+C\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[1_{\geq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}+U_{1\eta}^{2}y_{1\eta}\Big)+1_{\leq 0}(x)e^{-|x|}\ast\Big(\frac{U_{1\eta}^{2}}{y_{1\eta}}+U_{1\eta}^{2}y_{1\eta}\Big)\Big].

Combining the Young’s inequality and (
4.5
), we infer that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}(U_{1},y_{1})-\widetilde{F}(U_{2},y_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}+\|U_{1\xi}-U_{2\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}+\|y_{1\xi}-y_{2\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}\Big).

(4.11)

Then, we estimate
\|(\widetilde{F}(U_{1},y_{1})-\widetilde{F}(U_{2},y_{2}))_{\xi}\|_{L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}}
. Since
-(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\partial_{xx}={\rm{Id}}-(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}
, taking some similar but more simple operations, one can obtain

\displaystyle\|\big(\widetilde{F}(U_{1},y_{1})\big)_{\xi}-\big(\widetilde{F}(U_{2},y_{2})\big)_{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}+\|U_{1\xi}-U_{2\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}+\|y_{1\xi}-y_{2\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{p}}\Big).

(4.12)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}(U_{1},y_{1})-\widetilde{F}(U_{2},y_{2})\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}\leq C\Big(\|U_{1}-U_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}+\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}\Big),

i.e. (
1.6
) holds. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Next, we give another well-posedness result for the CH equation in a more general Banach space which also doesn’t contain the peakons (
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\cap W^{1,\infty},~p,r\in[1,\infty)
):

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\cap W^{1,\infty}
with
1\leq p,\ r<\infty
. Then there exists a time
T>0
such that the CH equation is locally well-posed in
u(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\big)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}\big([0,T];B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\big)
in the sense of Hadamard.

Proof.

Let
A(u)=u,\ F(u)=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\Big(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}\Big)
.
Then, we can easily deduce that

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\cap W^{1,\infty}}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\cap W^{1,\infty}};

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{F}(U,y)-\widetilde{F}(\bar{U},\bar{y})\|_{W^{1,p}\cap W^{1,\infty}}\leq C_{u_{0}}\Big(\|U-\bar{U}\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}+\|y-\bar{y}\|_{W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{1,p}}\Big);

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|F(u)-F(\bar{u})\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\cap W^{1,\infty}}\leq C_{u_{0}}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\cap W^{1,\infty}}.

(4.15)

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can obtain a unique solution
u(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}\big([0,T];B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\big)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}\big([0,T];B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}\big)
. But only for
W^{1,\infty}
, one can not obtain the continuous dependence since
W^{1,\infty}
is indivisible, see
[
37
]
for more details.
∎

Likewise, for the Novikov equation and two-component Camassa-Holm system, one can also obtain the local well-posedness by using Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. It also makes sense for the general Bansch spaces in Theorem
4.1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
:
.

Let
A(u)=u^{2},\ F(u,u_{x})=-(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}\Big(\partial_{x}\big(\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}^{2}+u^{3}\big)+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{3}\Big)
. Then (
1.2
) becomes the Novikov equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-u_{xxt}=3uu_{x}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{xxx}-4u^{2}u_{x},&\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(t,x)|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

Applying Theorem
1.1
, we can also gain the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for Novikov equation in critial Besov space
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.5
:
.

Let
A(u)=u,\ F(u,\eta)=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}+\eta)
and
h(\eta)=1+\eta
. Then we can get the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for two-component Camassa-Holm system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{xx})^{-1}(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}+\eta),&\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\partial_{t}\eta+u\partial_{x}\eta=-\partial_{x}u(1+\eta),&\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),\ \eta|_{t=0}=\eta_{0},&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\end{array}\right.

in critial Besov space
B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}\times B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}
by Theorem
1.2
.
∎

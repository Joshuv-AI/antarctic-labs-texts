Rigorous derivation of the full primitive equations by the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation
Xueke Pu, Wenli Zhou

Abstract
The primitive equations of large-scale oceanic dynamics form a fundamental model in geophysical flows. It is well-known that the primitive equations can be formally derived by the hydrostatic approximation. On the other hand, the mathematically rigorous derivation of the primitive equations without coupling with the temperature is also known. In this paper, we generalize the above result from the mathematical point of view. More precisely, we prove that the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation converge to the full primitive equations in a strong sense, globally in time, with the convergence rate O ⁡ ( ε ) O(\varepsilon) , as the aspect ratio ε \varepsilon goes to zero.



AMS Subject Classification: 35Q35, 35Q86, 86A05, 86A10

Key Words: Boussinesq equations; Primitive equations; Hydrostatic approximation; Strong convergence

Introduction

For large-scale oceanic dynamics, an important feature is that the vertical scale of ocean is much smaller than the horizontal scale. From a physical point of view, this scale analysis means that we can use the hydrostatic approximation to simulate the motion of ocean in the vertical direction. Due to this fact, the primitive equations of oceanic dynamics can be formally derived from the Boussinesq equations with rotation (see
[
27
,
9
]
).

Mathematically, there are already several results on the rigorous derivation of the primitive equations or the rigorous justification of the hydrostatic approximation. The weak convergence from the Navier-Stokes equations to the primitive equations was firstly proved by Azérad-Guillén
[
1
]
, while the strong convergence from the Navier-Stokes equations to the primitive equations is due to the work of Li-Titi
[
29
]
, see also Furukawa
et al.
[
13
]
in the
L^{p}
settings. For the case where the limiting system is the primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity, we refer to the work of Li-Titi-Yuan
[
31
]
. Note that the limiting system they obtained was the primitive equations without temperature. Therefore, the aim of this paper is mathematically to derive the full primitive equations.

Let
\varOmega_{\varepsilon}=G\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)

\varepsilon

\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\Delta_{h}\theta+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Rt_{2}}\partial_{zz}\theta,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

in a thin domain

, pressure

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=(v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon}),~v_{\varepsilon}(x,y,z,t)=v(x,y,\varepsilon z,t),

\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}w(x,y,\varepsilon z,t),~p_{\varepsilon}(x,y,z,t)=p(x,y,\varepsilon z,t),

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon}~\textnormal{and}~\theta_{\varepsilon}~\textnormal{are periodic in}~x,y,z,

(1.2)

\displaystyle(v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})|_{t=0}=(v_{0},w_{0},\theta_{0}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon}~\textnormal{and}~\theta_{\varepsilon}~\textnormal{are even,~odd,~even and odd in}~z,~\textnormal{respectively}.

(1.4)

\displaystyle v_{0},w_{0}~\textnormal{and}~\theta_{0}~\textnormal{are periodic in}~x,y,z,

\displaystyle v_{0},w_{0}~\textnormal{and}~\theta_{0}~\textnormal{are even,~odd~and odd in}~z,~\textnormal{respectively}.

\displaystyle(v,\theta)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},\theta_{0}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\left(\partial_{t}W_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\Delta_{h}W_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\partial_{zz}W_{\varepsilon}+U_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla W_{\varepsilon}+U_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla w+u\cdot\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\right)

.

Given a periodic function pair
(v_{0},\theta_{0})\in H^{2}(\varOmega)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t)+\int^{T}_{0}{\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt

and the convergence rate is of the order

(i) The convergence results in Theorem

(iii) Roughly speaking, Proposition 4.2 in

By virtue of the Gronwall inequality, from Proposition
2.1
,
2.2
and
3.1
it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left\lVert\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C_{\sigma}\exp\bigg\{C_{\sigma}\int^{t}_{0}\left(1+\left\lVert\nabla\theta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\theta\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C_{\sigma}\int^{t}_{0}\left[(1+\varepsilon^{2})\left\lVert V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C_{\sigma}\int^{t}_{0}\left[\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{4})\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right]ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\times\bigg\{\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\Delta v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{4}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\varepsilon^{2}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{t}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\Delta v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\theta\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)\left\lVert V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C_{\sigma}\varepsilon^{2}\exp\left\{C_{\sigma}(t+1)\left[1+\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{3}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{2}(t)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\times\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{3}(t)+(t+1)\left[\mathcal{M}^{2}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{3}(t)\right]\right\}.

note that we have used the fact
(V_{\varepsilon},W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})|_{t=0}=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Proof of Theorem
1.1

With the help of Proposition
2.2
and
3.2
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Note that we also need to eliminate the effect of the smallness condition in Proposition
3.2
. This can be achieved by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Denote by
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}
the maximal existence time of the strong solution
(v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})
to (SBE) with (
1.2
)-(
1.4
). Then, for any
T>0
, there is a small positive constant
\varepsilon(T)=\frac{2\lambda_{0}}{3\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{4}(T)}}
such that
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}>T
as long as
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon(T))
. Furthermore, the following energy estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)ds\leq\varepsilon^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{4}(t)\right),

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where both
\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)
and
\mathcal{M}_{4}(t)
are nonnegative continuously increasing functions that do not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For any
T>0
, we set
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\min\{T^{*}_{\varepsilon},T\}
. Then it follows from Proposition
2.2
that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}{\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\leq\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{M}_{2}(t),

(4.1)

for every
t\in[0,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
, where

\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)=Ce^{\left\{Ct+C(t+1)\left[\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{1}(t)\right]\right\}}\left[\left(1+t\right)^{2}+\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{1}(t)\right].

Define

t^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=\sup\left\{t\in(0,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\bigg|\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)\leq\lambda^{2}_{0}\right\},

note that
\lambda_{0}
is the small positive constant in Proposition
3.2
. Thanks to Proposition
3.2
, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\leq\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{M}_{4}(t),

(4.2)

for every
t\in[0,t^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=C\exp\left\{C(t+1)\left[1+\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{3}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{2}(t)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\left\{\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{3}(t)+(t+1)\left[\mathcal{M}^{2}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{3}(t)\right]\right\}.

Let
\varepsilon(T)=\frac{2\lambda_{0}}{3\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{4}(T)}}
. Owing to (
4.2
), we reach

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{0}\mathcal{M}_{4}(t)}{9\mathcal{M}_{4}(T)}\leq\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{0}}{9}<\lambda^{2}_{0},

for every
t\in[0,t^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
, and for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon(T))
, which leads to

\sup_{0\leq s<t^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)<\lambda^{2}_{0}.

(4.3)

The definition of
t^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
and (
4.3
) imply that
t^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
. On account of this, combining (
4.1
) with (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)ds\leq\varepsilon^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{4}(t)\right),

(4.4)

for every
t\in[0,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
, and for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon(T))
.

Next, it suffices to prove that
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}>T
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon(T))
. Assume that
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}\leq T
, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\min\{T^{*}_{\varepsilon},T\}=T^{*}_{\varepsilon}
. Then it is clear that

\limsup_{t^{\prime}\rightarrow(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})^{-}}\left(\left\lVert(v_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t^{\prime})=\infty,

(4.5)

since
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}
is the maximal existence time of the strong solution
(v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})
to (SBE). By virtue of Proposition
2.1
and
3.1
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(v_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(v,\varepsilon w,\theta)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t^{\prime})+\sup_{0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\varepsilon^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{3}(t))+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{M}_{4}(t)\right),

for every
t\in[0,\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})
, where the incompressible condition and estimate (
4.4
) are used. It is obvious that (
4.5
) contradicts to the above estimate. This contradiction deduces that
T^{*}_{\varepsilon}>T
and hence
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=T
, completing the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
is shown as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For any
T>0
, from Proposition
4.1
it follows that (SBE) corresponding to (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) exists a unique strong solution
(v_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})
on the time interval
[0,T]
for every
\varepsilon

\in

(0,\varepsilon(T))
. Moreover, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon W_{\varepsilon},\Pi_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t)+\int^{T}_{0}{\left(\frac{1}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{T}_{0}{\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{Re_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Rt_{1}}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{Rt_{2}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\varepsilon^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}(T)+\mathcal{M}_{4}(T)\right)=:\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{M}_{5}(T),

where
\mathcal{M}_{5}(t)
is a nonnegative continuously increasing function that does not depend on
\varepsilon
. By virtue of the above estimate, we obtain the following strong convergences

\displaystyle(v_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon w_{\varepsilon},\theta_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(v,0,\theta)~in~L^{\infty}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\varOmega)\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\nabla_{h}v_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Re_{1}}},\frac{\partial_{z}v_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Re_{2}}},\frac{\varepsilon\nabla_{h}w_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Re_{1}}}\right)\rightarrow\left(\frac{\nabla_{h}v}{\sqrt{Re_{1}}},\frac{\partial_{z}v}{\sqrt{Re_{2}}},0\right)~in~L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\varOmega)\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varepsilon\partial_{z}w_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Re_{2}}},\frac{\nabla_{h}\theta_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Rt_{1}}},\frac{\partial_{z}\theta_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Rt_{2}}}\right)\rightarrow\left(0,\frac{\nabla_{h}\theta}{\sqrt{Rt_{1}}},\frac{\partial_{z}\theta}{\sqrt{Rt_{2}}}\right)~in~L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\varOmega)\right).

Due to the incompressible condition, it deduces from
\frac{\nabla_{h}v_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{Re_{1}}}\rightarrow\frac{\nabla_{h}v}{\sqrt{Re_{1}}}~in~L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\varOmega)\right)
and
v_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow v~in~L^{\infty}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\varOmega)\right)
that

\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow w~in~L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{1}(\varOmega)\right),

\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow w~in~L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

respectively. In addition, it is obvious that the convergence rate is of the order
O(\varepsilon)
. Theorem
1.1
is thus proved.
∎

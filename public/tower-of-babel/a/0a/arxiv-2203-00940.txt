Fast Decoding of AG Codes
Peter Beelen, Johan Rosenkilde, and Grigory Solomatov

Abstract
We present an efficient list decoding algorithm in the style of Guruswami-Sudan for algebraic geometry codes. Our decoder can decode any such code using 𝒪 ~ ​ ( s ​ ℓ ω ​ μ ω − 1 ​ ( n + g ) ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g)) operations in the underlying finite field, where n n is the code length, g g is the genus of the function field used to construct the code, ℓ \ell is the designed list size and μ \mu is the smallest positive element in the Weierstrass semigroup at some chosen place; the “soft-O” notation 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ⋅ ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot) is similar to the “big-O” notation 𝒪 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \mathcal{O}(\cdot) , but ignores logarithmic factors. For the interpolation step, which constitutes the computational bottleneck of our approach, we use known algorithms for univariate polynomial matrices, while the root-finding step is solved using existing algorithms for root-finding over univariate power series.

Introduction

Containing some of the best error-correcting codes currently known, algebraic geometry (AG) codes have received a lot of attention since their introduction by Goppa in
[
15
]
. The celebrated Guruswami-Sudan decoder
[
16
]
for these codes relies on an interpolation step as well as a root-finding step and is capable of decoding beyond half the designed minimum distance by returning a list of all codewords within a certain Hamming distance
\tau
from the received word. In this article, we present an efficient realization of this decoder, achieving the best known complexity in the fully general setting of arbitrary AG codes. Moreover, except for the particularly simple case of Reed-Solomon codes, our decoder is at least as fast as all existing decoders which are tailored for specific families of codes. This article is based on a chapter of the PhD thesis of the third author
[
42
]
.

Following the common practice, we will measure algorithmic complexity by asymptotically upper-bounding the number of arithmetic operations in the underlying finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, relying on the
big-O
notation
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
as well as the
soft-O
notation
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
, which ignores logarithmic factors. Formally,
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(h)=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}(h\log(h)^{j})
for any function
h:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
denotes the set of non-negative real numbers. Analogously to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we will also write
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for the non-negative and the positive integers respectively. Our complexity estimates will also involve
\omega
, which denotes some real number such that the product of any two matrices in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times m}
can be computed using
\mathcal{O}(m^{\omega})
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The naive algorithm for matrix multiplication yields
\omega=3
, and it is clear that
\omega\geq 2
in general; the current record with
\omega<2.37286
is due to
[
2
]
.

Our decoder has the complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{\omega+1}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
, however, in a series of remarks throughout the article we explain how it can be slightly improved to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
; here
n
is the code length,
g
is the genus of the function field used to construct the code,
\ell
is the designed list size,
s\leq\ell
is the multiplicity, and
\mu
is the smallest element in the Weierstrass semigroup of some rational place
P_{\infty}
which is not one of the evaluation places. As we will see in
Section

2.2
, the existence of such
P_{\infty}
can be assumed without any loss of generality.

1.1
Related work

As mentioned earlier, the paradigm of Guruswami-Sudan list decoding revolves around two main steps: interpolation and root-finding. As former is generally more computationally demanding, it has historically received the most attention.
Several authors, including
[
33
,
34
,
27
,
1
,
10
]
, formulated the interpolation step as a problem of finding a polynomial, minimal with respect to a weighted monomial order, in a certain vanishing ideal. Prompted by this, Lee and O’Sullivan developed a technique for obtaining such a polynomial from a Gröbner basis (of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-modules), that was itself computed starting from a particular generating set – first for RS codes
[
25
]
, and then for one-point Hermitian codes
[
26
]
. The complexity of this strategy was further improved by Beelen and Brander in
[
4
]
by utilizing Alekhnovich’s algorithm for row reduction of polynomial matrices
[
1
]
. Furthermore, their decoder was applicable to the wider family of one-point codes over
C_{ab}
curves, making it more general. Specializing back to one-point Hermitian codes, Rosenkilde and Beelen
[
32
]
sped up this approach even more by delegating the row-reduction phase to the algorithm by Giorgi, Jeannerod and Villard
[
13
]
, which is more efficient than the one by Alekhnovich. Doing this required additional improvements to keep up with the new target complexity, including efficient computation of the initial
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-basis, as well as a way of handling fractional weights. The result was the first list-decoder of one-point Hermitian codes having sub-quadratic complexity in the code length.
In the current article, we generalize the tools from
[
32
]
to be applicable to all AG codes, relying on the conceptual framework from
[
24
]
to represent function field elements using
Apéry systems
.

Before shifting our attention to the root-finding step, we ought to mention the multivariate interpolation algorithm by Chowdhury, Jeannerod, Neiger, Schost and Villard
[
9
]
– it was the first to enable the currently best complexity in the special case of RS codes, albeit in a probabilistic manner. A deterministic algorithm with the same complexity was later given in
[
21
]
.

Some of the earliest root-finding algorithms for Guruswami-Sudan list-decoding include Roth and Ruckenstein’s
[
36
]
as well as Gao and Shokrollahi’s
[
12
]
. Alekhnovich described in
[
1
]
an efficient approach for computing the
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
-roots modulo
x^{\beta}
of a polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!][z]
; its complexity was shown in
[
32
]
to be
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\beta^{2}\ell)
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\ell
is the
z
-degree of
Q
. Another technique by Berthomieu, Lecerf and Quintin
[
7
]
achieved the cost
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\beta\ell^{2})
. In this article, we rely on the algorithm by Neiger, Rosenkilde and Schost
[
29
]
, whose complexity of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\beta\ell)
operations is provably quasi-optimal.

The complexity of our decoder is at least as good as, and often faster than, the complexity of previous decoders based on the Guruswami-Sudan paradigm. As far as we know, there is only one exception: in the case of RS codes, the complexity of the algorithms from
[
9
]
or
[
21
]
is a factor of
\ell/s
better. To illustrate the strength and versatility of our results, in
Section

6
, examples are given of the list decoding of various families of AG codes. One further remark should be made, namely the case of bounded distance decoding. Setting
s=\ell=1
and assuming that
g\in\mathcal{O}(n)
, the complexity our decoder simplifies to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\mu^{\omega-1}n)
. In this case, the decoder can always correct up to
(d^{*}-1-g)/2
errors, where
d^{*}
denotes the designed minimum distance of an AG code, also known as the Goppa bound. The same decoding radius is achieved in
[
38
]
with complexity
\mathcal{O}(\mu n^{2})
. Since
\mu\leq g
and we assumed
g\in\mathcal{O}(n)
, our complexity is better. However, Sakata’s extension of the Berlekamp-Massey decoder
[
37
,
39
]
yields a decoding algorithm able to correct up to at least
(d^{*}-1)/2
errors. In
[
18
]
, the complexity in the case of certain so-called one-point AG codes is
\mathcal{O}(\mu n^{2}+q^{t+1}(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{t})+tnq^{t})
, where
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}
form a minimal set of generators of the Weierstrass semigroup at
P_{\infty}
. To achieve the same decoding radius with our decoder, we could choose
s
and
\ell
in
\mathcal{O}(g)
, but doing so might not be as efficient, since our complexity would then increase by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(g^{\omega+1})\subseteq\mathcal{O}(n^{\omega+1})
.

1.2
Strategy outline and contributions

With the aim of making the exposition easier in the subsequent sections of the article, we now present an overview of the main steps in the proposed decoder. This consists of a way of simplifying the general setting as well as a way of efficiently carrying out the classical steps of interpolation and root-finding. The complete decoder is presented in
Section

6
, where it is also exemplified for special cases of AG codes.

•

Simplified setting:
In
Section

2.2
, we show how extending the constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
allows us to make certain simplifying assumptions. The important takeaway here is that no generality is sacrificed in the process, while only a minor penalty is introduced into the computational complexity. In return, we may assume existence of certain rational places, as well as existence of a special function field element
x
with controlled zeroes and poles. Having access to additional rational places is useful for a variety of reasons, among which is efficient multiplication of function field elements in a pointwise manner; the carefully chosen function
x
acts as a fundamental building block in the way we represent function field elements.

•

Interpolation step:
This is the most involved part of the article and requires all of the computational tools from
Section

5
– except for
Section

5.6
, which deals with root-finding. In
Section

4.1
, it is explained how the interpolation step can be viewed as a problem of finding a “small” element
Q
in a certain interpolation module whose underlying ring consists of all functions that have no poles except for possibly at a fixed rational place
P_{\infty}
. This ring, denoted by
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
, is itself a free module over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, which essentially means that we can represent everything as tuples of univariate polynomials. The computational path for obtaining
Q
boils down to first constructing a generating set of the interpolation module over
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
, then expanding this to a generating set over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and finally, using efficient algorithms for matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
to reduce this generating set to a “small” basis that contains a satisfactory
Q
.

•

Root-finding step:

Structurally, the obtained
Q
is a univariate polynomial whose coefficients are function field elements; and according to the Guruswami-Sudan paradigm, list decoding reduces to finding the roots of this polynomial. We accomplish this by expressing the coefficients of
Q
as power series in
x
, which is always possible in our simplified setting in which
x
is a local parameter of some appropriate rational place. An existing algorithm for root-finding over the ring of power-series is then used to obtain the sought roots, albeit represented as power series; the final step of our decoder therefore consists of converting these roots back to the original representation as well as discarding any potential “spurious” solutions. All of this is detailed in
Section

5.6
.

Our decoder relies on a mixture of new and existing results; the novel contributions include:

•

reduction of the fully general setting to a simpler one (
Section

2.2
),

•

an algorithm for encoding general AG codes with complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\mu n)
(
Section

5.1
),

•

an interpolation algorithm with complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
(
Section

5.2
),

•

a root-finding algorithm with complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{2}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
(
Section

5.6
),

•

an algorithm for computing an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-basis of
\langle h\rangle_{\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}}
for any function
h
(
Section

5.4
).

Not counting precomputation, all of the algorithms above are sufficiently efficient to reach our target cost. Although the cost of precomputation has not been investigated in detail, it is not expected to be much more expensive than that of Gaussian elimination. A list of all precomputed objects can be found in
Section

6
.

2.2
Reduction to a simpler setting

In this subsection, we will show that without significant increase of decoding complexity, we can assume several things about the function field
F
and the AG code
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
that will make the exposition of our decoding algorithm simpler later on. For example, it will be convenient to have an additional rational place

of

that is not used in the evaluation map
\ev_{D}
. In fact, for some of our later algorithms, it will be convenient to have additional rational places as well. An easy way out is to increase the constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
for some small value of

F\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}

\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}

As far as decoding is concerned, the AG code
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
is in a trivial way a subcode (not
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
-linear, but
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear) of the AG code obtained from the function field
F\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
using the divisors
\Con(D)
and
\Con(G)
, where
\Con
denotes the conorm with respect to
F\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}/F
,
[
43
, Definition 3.1.8]
. Since all places in
\supp(D)
are rational, we may with slight abuse of notation write
\Con(D)=D
. Hence if for a given
\tau
, one has a list decoding algorithm for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,\Con(G))
that produces all codewords at distance at most
\tau
from a received word, one immediately obtains a list decoding algorithm for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
. However, since multiplication of two elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
can be done in
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(e)
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

[
8
]
, the value of
e
should be small for complexity reasons. Therefore we now give a series of lemmas, each aiming to show that for small

F
and the code
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F
be a function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus

the number of rational places of the function field
F\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}

.

Proof.

The Hasse-Weil bound

implies that

\log_{q}N_{e}>\log_{q}(q^{e}-2q^{e/2}g)=e/2+\log_{q}(q^{e/2}-2g)\geq e/2\geq\log_{q}N\ .

∎

Now we show that if the function field
F
has sufficiently many rational places, then one of them has particularly simple local parameter. Recall that a function

is called a local parameter for a place

, where

denotes the valuation at

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F
be a function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus

having a rational place
P_{\infty}
. Let
\mu
be the smallest positive element from the Weierstrass semigroup of

. Any set containing at least

, contains a place
P_{0}
with local parameter from
\mathcal{L}(\mu P_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mu P_{\infty})
be a function satisfying
v_{P_{\infty}}(x)=-\mu
.
First of all, note that the extension
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
is separable. Indeed, assume that
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
is inseparable. Then by the general theory of inseparable extensions, we can find an intermediate field
E
such that
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
is separable and

is purely inseparable. Then by
[
43
, Proposition 3.10.2]

\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)\subseteq F^{p}=\{f^{p}\mid f\in F\}

Since

only of order

P_{\infty}
only of order
\mu/p
. This gives a contradiction with the minimality of
\mu
.

The above implies that the Hurwitz genus formula (see for example
[
43
, Corollary 3.4.14]
) applies to the extension

. To prove the lemma, we estimate the genus of
F
using this formula. We write
Q_{\infty}=P_{\infty}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, which is the place at infinity of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. Since
v_{P_{\infty}}(x)=-\mu
and
v_{Q_{\infty}}(x)=-1
, we see that
e(P_{\infty}|Q_{\infty})=\mu
. Now suppose we have
N>3g
rational places distinct from
P_{\infty}
, say
P_{1},\dots,P_{N}
. We write
Q_{i}=P_{i}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
for their restrictions to
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. For these rational places, we have

v_{P_{i}}(x-x(P_{i}))=e(P_{i}|Q_{i})v_{Q_{i}}(x-x(P_{i}))=e(P_{i}|Q_{i}).

Suppose that for every rational place

it holds that
e(P_{i}|Q_{i})\geq 2
.

Since
\mu=[F:\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)]
by
[

, the Hurwitz genus formula combined with the estimate

implies that

\displaystyle 2g-2

\displaystyle\geq-2[F:\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)]+d(P_{\infty}|Q_{\infty})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}d(P_{i}|Q_{i})

\displaystyle\geq-2\mu+(\mu-1)+N(2-1)\ .

Since

, we conclude that

, a contradiction.
Hence for one of the places

we have
v_{P_{i}}(x-x(P_{i}))=1.

∎

Next we consider a lemma showing that we can assume that the divisor

Lemma 2.5
.

Let the function field
F
and divisors

be as before. Then either, the AG code

is degenerate or
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,\Con(G))

Proof.

Consider the finite field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
and for convenience, let us write
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,\Con(G))
as well as
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\{c\in\mathcal{C}\mid c_{i}=0\}.
If
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
is nondegenerate, then so is
\mathcal{C}
. In this case
\mathcal{C}\neq\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all

.
If every codeword in

has at least one zero coordinate, then
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}
, which implies that
(q^{e})^{k}\leq n(q^{e})^{k-1}

We see that in this case

, implying

This contradiction shows that
\mathcal{C}
contains a codeword of full Hamming weight

for some
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\Con(G))

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,\Con(G)+(\tilde{f}))

. Note that the divisor

is effective and has support disjoint from

.
∎

Degenerate codes are not very interesting from the error-correcting point of view. Indeed, if the
i
-th coordinate of all codewords is zero, it is trivial to correct errors in that position. On the other hand that position does not carry any information, so one might as well consider the punctured code where such a position has been removed, which will have the same dimension and minimum distance. Decoding a degenerate code can therefore be reduced using puncturing to decoding a nondegenerate code. Note that since the codes
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,\Con(G))
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G^{\prime})
are monomially equivalent, any (list) decoding algorithm for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G^{\prime})
immediately gives a (list) decoding algorithm for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,\Con(G))
. The added complexity is that of dividing and multiplying with the column multipliers
\tilde{f}(P_{i})
, which only costs
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
and hence
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(ne)
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, as we will see, we will be able to choose
e
so small that it will not affect the decoding complexity at all in the
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
notation.

Now we state the simplifying assumptions and notation that will be used in the remainder of this article.

1.

We assume that
G
is an effective divisor, whose degree satisfies equation (
2.1
).

2.

We assume that apart from the rational places in
D=P_{1}+\cdots+P_{n}
, the function field
F
has at least one more rational place
P_{\infty}
. The place
P_{\infty}
may or may not be in
\supp(G)
.

3.

There exists a rational place
P_{0}
of
F
which has
x
as a local parameter, where
x\in F
is a function with pole at
P_{\infty}
only of minimal pole order
\mu
. The place
P_{0}
may be in
\supp(D)
, but is not in
\supp(G)
.

Let us quickly assess the size of the needed extension degree
e
in order to satisfy all three item simultaneously. Although one likely can do better, for our purposes it is sufficient to pick
e=e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}
, where
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
are given below: to satisfy the first item, we extend
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e_{1}}}
, where
e_{1}=1+\lceil\log_{q}(n)\rceil
, using Lemma
2.5
. To satisfy the second item, we apply Lemma
2.3
with
N=n+1
. Hence we can choose
e_{2}=\lceil 2\log_{q^{e_{1}}}\max\{n+1,2g+1\}\rceil
and extend
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e_{1}}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e_{1}e_{2}}}
. For the third item, we need apart from
P_{\infty}
and possible rational places in
\supp(G)
, an additional
3g+1
rational places. Since
G
is effective, we can apply Lemma
2.3
with
N=1+\deg(G)+3g+1
, so using equation (
2.1
), we can choose
e_{3}=\lceil 2\log_{q^{e_{1}e_{2}}}(5g+1+n)\rceil
extending
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e_{1}e_{2}}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
.
Using that
\log_{q^{f}}(A)=\log_{q}(A)/f
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle e=e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log_{q}(5g+1+n)+e_{1}e_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log_{q}(5g+1+n)+2\log_{q}\max\{n+1,2g+1\}+e_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log_{q}(5g+1+n)+2\log_{q}\max\{n+1,2g+1\}+\log_{q}(n)+2.

Hence the overall conclusion is that in terms of complexity only a logarithmic factor in
n+g
is introduced when reducing from the general case to the simpler setting. In the remainder of this article, instead of writing
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
, we will simply write
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and assume
q
is large enough so that all three simplifying assumptions stated above are satisfied.

2.3
Shifted Popov forms of polynomial matrices

Our decoder relies on efficient algorithms for (free)
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-submodules of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m}
; in the current subsection, we present well known results and definitions that we need needed for our use cases. For a comprehensive introduction, the reader is referred to
[
44
]
and the references within.

We begin with a definition which, among other things, allows us to measure “size” of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m}
.

Definition 2.6
.

For any polynomial vector
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m}
and any
\bm{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
(which we refer to as a
shift
), we define the
\bm{s}
-degree
of
\bm{v}
as

\deg_{\bm{s}}\bm{v}=\max_{k}\{\deg v_{k}+s_{k}\}\ .

Furthermore, if
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
is maximal such that
\deg v_{k}+s_{k}=\deg_{\bm{s}}\bm{v}
, then we say that
v_{k}
is the
\bm{s}
-pivot
of
\bm{v}
, and
k
is its
\bm{s}
-pivot index
. If
\bm{s}=0
, then we might omit writing
\bm{s}
in the above notation, i.e. we might simply write:
pivot
,
pivot index
and
degree
, denoting the latter by
\deg\bm{v}:=\deg_{\bm{0}}\bm{v}
.pp

Any
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-basis of a submodule
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m}
of rank
m
can be described using a nonsingular polynomial matrix
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m\times m}
by identifying the basis elements with the rows of
\bm{V}
. This way,
\mathcal{V}
is viewed as the
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-row space of
\bm{V}
. We will be interested in obtaining the basis whose elements are “smallest possible”; the following definition makes this notion precise in the context of polynomial matrices.

Definition 2.7
.

Given a shift
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, a nonsingular matrix
\bm{P}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m\times m}
is said to be in
\bm{s}
-Popov form
if all of the
\bm{s}
-pivots of its rows lie on the diagonal, are monic and have degrees strictly greater than all other entries in their respective columns. Furthermore, if
\bm{P}
shares its
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-row space with some matrix
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{r\times m}
, where
m\leq r
, then
\bm{P}
is said to be
the
\bm{s}
-Popov form
of
\bm{V}
.

Below, we summarize a few important structural properties of shifted Popov forms.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
44
, Section 1.1]
)
.

For any nonsingular matrix
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m\times m}
and any shift
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, there exists a unique matrix
\bm{P}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m\times m}
in
\bm{s}
-Popov form having the same
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-row space as
\bm{V}
. Furthermore,
\bm{P}
has minimal shifted row degrees in the following sense: for any
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m\times m}
with the same row space as
\bm{P}
, there exists a bijection between the rows of the two matrices such that the
\bm{s}
-degree of any row of
\bm{V}
is no smaller than that of the corresponding row of
\bm{P}
. Finally, for any nonzero vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{1\times m}
in the row space of
\bm{P}
with
\bm{s}
-pivot index
k
it holds that
\deg_{\bm{s}}\bm{v}\geq\deg_{\bm{s}}\bm{p}^{(k)}
, where
\bm{p}^{(k)}
denotes the
k
-th row of
\bm{P}
.

We conclude this subsection with a few complexity bounds.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
30
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

There is a deterministic algorithm which for any shift
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
computes the
\bm{s}
-Popov form of any nonsingular matrix
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{m\times m}
using
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(m^{\omega}\deg\bm{M})
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\deg\bm{M}
denotes the maximal degree among all entries in
\bm{M}
.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
46
]
)
.

There is a deterministic algorithm which for any matrix
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{r\times m}
with
m\leq r
computes an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-basis of the row space of
\bm{V}
using
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(rm^{\omega-1}\deg\bm{V})
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Combining
Proposition

2.10
with
Proposition

2.9
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 2.11
.

For any shift
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and any matrix
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{r\times m}
with rank
m\leq r
, we can compute the
\bm{s}
-Popov form of
\bm{V}
using
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(rm^{\omega-1}\deg\bm{V})
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

3
Representation of function field elements

For any divisor
A
of
F
, let
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)=\bigcup_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty}+A)
and let
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}=\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(0)
. Note that
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
is a ring and
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
a
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
-module. In fact more can be said:
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
is a Dedekind domain and
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
is a fractional ideal of
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
,
[
31
, Section 1.2]
.

Modules of the form
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
are essentially already considered for decoding in
[
23
]
, also see
[
5
,
24
]
.
As in
[
24
]
, for any nonzero
a\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
we denote by
\delta_{A}(a)
the smallest integer
m
such that
a\in\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty}+A)
, i.e.
\delta_{A}(a)=-v_{P_{\infty}}(a)-v_{P_{\infty}}(A)
and let
\delta(a)=\delta_{0}(a)=-v_{P_{\infty}}(a)
. We will take as convention that
\delta_{A}(0)=-\infty
. Note that for any
a\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
and
b\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(B)
, one has
\delta_{A+B}(ab)=\delta_{A}(a)+\delta_{B}(b).

It is well known that any fractional ideal of a Dedekind domain can be generated by at most two elements
[
11
, Corollary 2 to Theorem 4]
, but for our purposes we need to know some properties of these generators.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A=\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}Q_{i}
be a divisor of
F
and write
\mathfrak{a}=\sum_{i}\deg Q_{i}
.
Then
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
can be generated as a
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
-module by two elements
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
satisfying
\delta_{A}(a_{1})\leq 2g-1-\deg(A)+\mathfrak{a}
and
\delta_{A}(a_{2})\leq 4g-2-\deg(A)+\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Prime ideals of
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
correspond exactly to places of
F
distinct from
P_{\infty}
. Therefore, from the proof of Corollary 2 to Theorem 4 in
[
11
]
, we see that two elements
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
generate
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
as
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
-module if and only if for all places
Q_{i}\in\supp(A)
distinct from
P_{\infty}
, we have
\min\{v_{Q_{i}}(a_{1}),v_{Q_{i}}(a_{2})\}=-n_{i}
and for any other place
Q\neq P_{\infty}
of
F
we have
\min\{v_{Q}(a_{1}),v_{Q}(a_{2})\}=0.
We will construct two such elements.

Write
m_{1}=2g-1-\deg(A)+\mathfrak{a}
. For
j=1,\dots,t
, choose
a_{1}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}(A-\sum_{i\neq j}Q_{i}+m_{1}P_{\infty})\setminus\mathcal{L}(A-\sum_{i}Q_{i}+m_{1}P_{\infty})
. Note that such
a_{1}^{(j)}
exist, since by the Riemann-Roch theorem,
l(A-\sum_{i\neq j}Q_{i}+m_{1}P_{\infty})>l(A-\sum_{i}Q_{i}+m_{1}P_{\infty})
. Defining
a_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}a_{1}^{(j)}
, we see that
v_{Q_{i}}(a_{1})=-n_{i}
for
j=1,\dots,t
, while
v_{Q}(a_{1})\geq 0
for any other place
Q
distinct from
P_{\infty}
. In particular
a_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(A+m_{1}P_{\infty})
, whence
\delta_{A}(a_{1})\leq m_{1}.

Now suppose that
Q_{t+1},\dots,Q_{t+s}
are the zeroes of
a_{1}
not in
\supp(A)\cup\{P_{\infty}\}
. Since
a_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(A+m_{1}P_{\infty})
, we see that
\sum_{i=t+1}^{t+s}\deg(Q_{i})\leq\deg(A)+m_{1}
. Now define
m_{2}=2g-1+m_{1}=4g-2-\deg(A)+\mathfrak{a}
. Similarly as above, we can construct
a_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(A+m_{2}P_{\infty})
, such that
v_{Q_{i}}(a_{2})=0
for
i=t+1,\dots,t+s
. By construction
\delta_{A}(a_{2})\leq m_{2}
. For
i=1,\dots,t
, we have
v_{Q_{i}}(a_{2})\geq-n_{i}
and
v_{Q_{i}}(a_{1})=-n_{i}
, whence
\min\{v_{Q_{i}}(a_{1}),v_{Q_{i}}(a_{2})\}=-n_{i}
. If
Q\not\in\supp(A)\cup\{P_{\infty}\}
is not a zero of
a_{1}
, then
\min\{v_{Q}(a_{1}),v_{Q}(a_{2})\}=0
, since
v_{Q}(a_{2})\geq 0
. If
Q\not\in\supp(A)\cup\{P_{\infty}\}
is a zero of
a_{1}
, then
v_{Q}(a_{2})=0
, so that also in this case
\min\{v_{Q}(a_{1}),v_{Q}(a_{2})\}=0
. Hence
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
as constructed above, generated
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
as a
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
-module.
∎

As
x\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we can also view
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
as a free
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-module. Following
[
24
]
, we consider a special set of generators of
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
as
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-module, which they called the Apéry system of
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
.

Definition 3.2
.

For any divisor
A
let
y^{(A)}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
be such that
\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{i})\leq\delta_{A}(a)
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, where
i=0,\dots,\mu-1
and

\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{a\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)\mid\delta_{A}(a)\equiv i\mod\mu\}\ .

We also define
y_{i}=y^{(0)}_{i}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any divisor
A
it holds that

1.

y^{(A)}_{0},\dots,y^{(A)}_{\mu-1}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-basis of
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
and

2.

-\deg A\leq\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{i})\leq 2g-1-\deg(A)+\mu
for
i=0,\dots,\mu-1
.

Proof.

The first statement is from
[
24
]
. For the convenience of the reader we give a proof. From the strict triangle inequality for
v_{P_{\infty}}
, it is clear that the elements
y^{(A)}_{0},\dots,y^{(A)}_{\mu-1}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Also, it is clear that
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
, where
\mathcal{Y}=\langle y^{(A)}_{0},\dots,y^{(A)}_{\mu-1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}
. If
\mathcal{Y}\neq\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
, then there would exist
a\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)\setminus\mathcal{Y}
, such that
\delta_{A}(a)>-\infty
is minimal. Write
\delta_{A}(a)=m\mu+r
and
\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{r})=m^{\prime}\mu+r
, where
m,m^{\prime},r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq r<\mu
. Note that
m^{\prime}\leq m
by definition of
y^{(A)}_{r}
.
Since

\delta_{A}(x^{m-m^{\prime}}y^{(A)}_{r})=\delta(x^{m-m^{\prime}})+\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{r})=(m-m^{\prime})\mu+(m^{\prime}\mu+r)=\delta_{A}(a)\ ,

there exists a constant
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\delta_{A}(c)<\delta_{A}(a)
, where
c=a-\beta x^{m-m^{\prime}}y^{(A)}_{r}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
.
The minimality of

guarantees that

, however, this would imply that

. Hence
\mathcal{Y}=\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
, which is a contradiction.

In the second statement, the lower bound simply follows from the fact that
y^{(A)}_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{i})P_{\infty}+A)\neq\{0\}
. For the upper bound it is sufficient to show that for every integer
m>2g-1-\deg(A)
there exists an
a\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
with
\delta_{A}(a)=m
.
But indeed, if
m>2g-1-\deg(A)
, then
\deg(mP_{\infty}+A)>2g-1
, and so
[
43
, Theorem 1.5.17]
implies that

\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty}+A)\neq\mathcal{L}\big((m-1)P_{\infty}+A\big)\ ,

which concludes the proof.
∎

For later use, we also state the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
a=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}a_{i}y^{(A)}_{i}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
, where

and

Simply observe that for

it holds that

\delta_{A}(a)=\max_{j}\delta_{A}(a_{j}y^{(A)}_{j})\geq\delta(a_{i})+\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{i})\geq\delta(a_{i})-\deg A\ ,

where the equality follows from the strict triangle inequality for

and second inequality is given by

But then

\deg a_{i}=\delta(a_{i})/\mu\leq\frac{1}{\mu}(\delta_{A}(a)-\delta_{A}(y^{(A)}_{i}))\leq\frac{1}{\mu}(\delta_{A}(a)+\deg A)\ .

∎

4
Guruswami-Sudan Decoding

In this section, we paraphrase the Guruswami-Sudan list decoding algorithm
[
17
]
for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
and formulate it in terms of
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
modules. For the remainder of this paper fix
s,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
s\leq\ell
, where
s
is the multiplicity parameter and
\ell
the designed list size of the Guruswami-Sudan list decoder. The corresponding list decoding radius will be denoted by
\tau
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P
be a rational place of
F
,
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
Q\in F[z]
. We will say that “
Q
has a root of multiplicity
s
at
(P,r)
” if for any local parameter
\phi
of
P
, there exist
c_{a,b}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

Q=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\geq 0\\
a+b\geq s\end{subarray}}c_{a,b}\phi^{a}(z-r)^{b}

with
c_{a,s-a}\neq 0
for at least one
0\leq a\leq s
.

A consequence of this definition is the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

If
Q\in F[z]
has a root of multiplicity
s
at
(P,r)
and
f\in F
is such that
f(P)=r
, then

v_{P}(Q(f))\geq s
.

Proof.

Writing

Q(f)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\geq 0\\
a+b\geq s\end{subarray}}c_{a,b}\phi^{a}(f-r)^{b}\ ,

where
\phi
is any local parameter of
P
and
c_{a,b}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the triangle inequality directly implies that

v_{P}(Q(f))\geq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\geq 0\\
a+b\geq s\end{subarray}}\big(v_{P}(\phi^{a})+v_{P}((f-r)^{b})\big)\geq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\geq 0\\
a+b\geq s\end{subarray}}(a+b)\geq s\ .

∎

For any
Q=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}Q^{(t)}
with
Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
we define
\delta_{G}(Q)=\max_{t}\delta_{-tG}(Q^{(t)})
. Moreover, for a given received word
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we write

\begin{multlined}\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)=\{Q=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}Q^{(t)}\in F[z]\mid Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG),\\
\text{$Q$ has a root of multiplicity at least $s$ at $(P_{j},r_{j})$ for $j=1,\dots,n$}\}.\end{multlined}

(4.1)

Theorem 4.3

(Special case of Guruswami–Sudan
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\bm{r}
be a received word and
Q\in\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
with
\delta_{G}(Q)<s(n-\tau)
. If
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
such that the Hamming weight of
\bm{r}-\ev_{D}(f)
is at most
\tau
, then
Q(f)=0
.

Proof.

Since
f^{t}\in\mathcal{L}(tG)
and
Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
, then
f^{t}Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
, and consequently

.
Furthermore, since

We write

. Note that the cardinality of

is at most

. Since

4.1
Structure of
\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
as a
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
-module

The set

introduced in equation (

\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
be a received word and
R\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G)
be such that

for

. Then it holds that

\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)=\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell}(z-R)^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{t})\ .

Proof.

Note that for all
j
and all

,
v_{P_{j}}(h)\geq\max\{0,s-t\}
. Further
(z-R)^{t}
has a root of multiplicity

at
(P_{j},r_{j})
, since

. Hence any element in
(z-R)^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{t})

s

(P_{j},r_{j})

Moreover, since

(z-R)^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{t})=\left(\sum_{u=0}^{t}z^{u}\binom{t}{u}(-R)^{t-u}\right)\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{t})\subseteq\bigoplus_{u=0}^{t}z^{u}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{u})
. Hence
(z-R)^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{t})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
is a
\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
-module, this implies that

We will prove the reverse inclusion

by induction on

.
Let

and using Newton’s binomium, we obtain

since

Now observe that

implies that

Now if we assume

for

If

Since

∎

Remark 4.8
.

The

. Therefore the element of the ideal class group of

. See

for more details.

and in particular the Steinitz invariant of

is the element in the ideal class group of

corresponding to

\prod_{u=0}^{\ell}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{u})=\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(\sum_{u=0}^{\ell}G_{u})=\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-\binom{\ell+1}{2}G-\binom{s+1}{2}D).

5
Algorithms

In this section, we present the algorithms that we will use to execute the Guruswami-Sudan list decoder. We start with discussing multi-point evaluation and interpolation algorithms that will form the backbone of the algorithms discussed later in the section.

5.1
Multi-Point Evaluation

When defining
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
, we used the evaluation map
\ev_{D}
. We will later need to be able to compute
\ev_{D}(f)
fast, meaning we want to be able to evaluate the function
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
in the multiple points
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
fast. As a matter of fact, since we will need a slightly more general setting later on, we phrase the results in this and the next subsection in terms of a very similar evaluation map, but avoid to use the divisors
D
and
G
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A
be a divisor and
E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{N}
for distinct rational places
E_{1},\dots,E_{N}
of
F
such that
\supp(A)\cap\supp(E)=\emptyset
. Further denote by
\ev_{E}:\mathcal{L}(A)\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
the evaluation map defined by
\ev_{E}(a)=(a(E_{1}),\dots,a(E_{N})).
Then

1.

\ev_{E}
is injective when
\deg A<\deg E
,

2.

\ev_{E}
is surjective when
\deg A\geq\deg E+2g-1
.

Proof.

For the first item, simply observe that the dimension of the kernel of
\ev_{E}
is
l(A-E)=0
, since
\deg(A-E)<0
.

For the second item, observe that the dimension of the image of
\ev_{E}
is

l(A)-l(A-E)=\deg A-g+1-(\deg A-\deg E-g+1)=\deg E\ ,

since
\deg(A)\geq 2g-1
and
\deg(A-E)\geq 2g-1
, see
[
43
, Theorem 1.5.17]
.
∎

Now we state Algorithm
1
, which computes
\ev_{E}(a)
using the representation of function field elements as introduced in Section
3
.

Algorithm 1

\mathsf{Evaluate}(a,E,A,\bm{x},\bm{y})

1:

Input:

•

Divisors
A
and
E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{N}
, where
E_{1},\dots,E_{N}\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\setminus\{P_{\infty}\}
are distinct rational places and
\supp(A)\cap\supp(E)=\emptyset
,

•

a function
a=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}a_{i}y^{(A)}_{i}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(A)
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
,

•

evaluations
\bm{x}=(x_{j})_{j=1,\dots,N}
, where
x_{j}=x(E_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

•

evaluations
\bm{y}=(y_{i,j})^{i=0,\dots,\mu-1}_{j=1,\dots,N}
, where
y_{i,j}=y^{(A)}_{i}(E_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

2:

Output:

•

Evaluations
\ev_{E}(a)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
.

3:

for

i=0,\dots,\mu-1

do

4:

(a_{i,1},\dots,a_{i,N})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}\leftarrow(a_{i}(x_{1}),\dots,a_{i}(x_{N}))

\triangleright
Univariate MPE

5:

return

\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}(a_{i,1}y_{i,1},\dots,a_{i,N}y_{i,N})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}

Lemma 5.2
.

Algorithm

1
is correct and costs
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\mu N+\delta_{A}(a)+\deg A)
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Correctness simply follows from the fact that for

\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}a_{i,j}y_{i,j}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}a_{i}(x(E_{j}))y^{(A)}_{i}(E_{j})=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}(a_{i}y^{(A)}_{i})(E_{j})=a(E_{j})\ .

For complexity, notice that the total cost of the for-loop on Line 1 amounts to that of evaluating each of the univariate polynomials

on

,

\deg a_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\mu}(\delta_{A}(a)+\deg A)\quad\text{for }i=0,\dots,\mu-1\ ,

hence the total cost of the for-loop is bounded by

Line

∎

5.2

In this subsection, we address the interpolation problem. We start with an existence result.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A

Lemma 5.11
.

Let

for distinct rational places

Suppose that

and

S_{i,k}(x(E_{j}))=y^{(A)}_{i}(E_{j})\text{ for all }E_{j}\in\supp(U_{k}).

(-\bm{d})
-Popov basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\bm{u}}(\bm{A})

\bm{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{S}\\
\hline\cr\bm{T}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(\mu+1)\times\mu}\ ,

for

.

for

Proof.

Observe that according to

5.3
there exists a

such that

for

. If we write

.
To see this let

for

and observe that for any

it holds that

which implies that

\bm{b}\bm{A}_{k}=c_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{u_{k}}\ ,

where
\bm{A}_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{(\mu+1)\times 1}
denotes the
k
-th column of
\bm{A}
. But then indeed

Note that in the
(-\bm{d})
-degree, the leading position of
\bm{b}
is the last position. The
(-\bm{d})
-Popov basis of

will contain a vector

B_{v}^{(u)}\in\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
for
u=0,\dots,\ell
and

are as in

and

\bm{M}_{s,\ell}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{M}_{s,\ell}^{(1)}\\
\hline\cr\bm{M}_{s,\ell}^{(2)}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2\mu(\ell+1)\times\mu(\ell+1)}\ ,

where for
v=1,2

\bm{M}_{s,\ell}^{(v)}=\begin{pmatrix}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\curlyvee_{z}(y_{0}B_{v}^{(0)})\\
\hline\cr\vdots\\
\hline\cr\curlyvee_{z}(y_{\mu-1}B_{v}^{(0)})\end{array}\right]^{\top}\ \vline\ &\cdots&\vline\ \;\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\curlyvee_{z}(y_{0}B_{v}^{(\ell)})\\
\hline\cr\vdots\\
\hline\cr\curlyvee_{z}(y_{\mu-1}B_{v}^{(\ell)})\end{array}\right]^{\top}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\ ,

then
\curlyvee_{z}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
and the row space of
{\bm{M}}_{s,\ell}
. Moreover, for any
Q
as before, it holds that
\delta_{G}(Q)=\mu\deg_{\bm{d}}\curlyvee_{z}(Q)
, where

\bm{d}=(\bm{d}^{(0)}|\cdots|\bm{d}^{(\ell)})\in(\frac{1}{\mu}\mathbb{Z})^{\mu(\ell+1)}
with

\bm{d}^{(t)}=\frac{1}{\mu}\big(\delta_{-tG}(y^{(-tG)}_{0}),\dots,\delta_{-tG}(y^{(-tG)}_{\mu-1})\big)\in(\frac{1}{\mu}\mathbb{Z})^{\mu}

for
t=0,\dots,\ell
.

Proof.

Corollary

4.6
immediately implies that
\curlyvee_{z}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
and the row space of
{\bm{M}}_{s,\ell}
. Further, writing
Q^{(t)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}Q_{i}^{(t)}y^{(-tG)}_{i}
for
t=0,\dots,\ell
, where
Q_{i}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, gives that

\displaystyle\delta_{G}(Q)

\displaystyle=\max_{t}\delta_{-tG}(Q^{(t)})

\displaystyle=\max_{t,i}\{\delta_{-tG}(Q_{i}^{(t)}y^{(-tG)}_{i})\}

\displaystyle=\max_{t,i}\{\delta(Q_{i}^{(t)})+\delta_{-tG}(y^{(-tG)}_{i})\}

\displaystyle=\max_{t,i}\{\mu\deg Q_{i}^{(t)}+\delta_{-tG}(y^{(-tG)}_{i})\}

\displaystyle=\mu\deg_{\bm{d}}\curlyvee_{z}(Q)\ .

∎

Lemma

5.25
implies that we can find a nonzero
Q\in\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
satisfying
\delta_{G}(Q)<s(n-\tau)
, if it exists, by computing the
\bm{d}
-Popov form of the matrix
\bm{M}_{s,\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2\mu(\ell+1)\times\mu(\ell+1)}
. According to

2.11

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{\omega}\mu^{\omega}\deg\bm{M}_{s,\ell})
. To estimate

\deg{\bm{M}}_{s,\ell}\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\max_{i,r,v,u}\{-r\deg G+\delta(y_{i})+\delta_{-rG}(b_{r,v}^{(u)})\}\ .

Then
Lemma

3.3
and inequality (
5.3
) imply that

\deg{\bm{M}}_{s,\ell}\leq\max_{r,u}\frac{6g-2+\mu+(u-r)(n+2g-1)+(s+1)n+\deg G}{\mu}\in\mathcal{O}(\mu^{-1}\ell(n+g))\ ,

(5.4)

which means that we can compute the
\bm{d}
-Popov form of
\bm{M}_{s,\ell}
within our target complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(l^{\omega+1}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
.

Remark 5.26
.

Using the alternative generating set from

, we again get an improvement on the running time. In equation (

), the expression

corresponded to the exponent of

in the expression

5.6
Root-finding

In this subsection, we consider the final computational ingredient that we will need for Guruswami-Sudan list-decoding: given a polynomial
Q(z)\in\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
, compute the set
L=\{f\in\mathcal{L}(G)\mid Q(f)=0\}
of all roots of
Q
. We accomplish this by changing the representation of
Q
from
\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
to
\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
, which will allow us to use the root-finding algorithm from
[
29
]
.

Let
P_{0}\not\in\supp G\cup\{P_{\infty}\}
be the fixed rational place of
F
for which
x
is a local parameter. For any nonzero
h\in F
let
\widehat{h}\in x^{v_{P_{0}}(h)}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
denote the
P_{0}
-adic power series expansion of
h
in
x
and define
\widehat{0}=0
. Furthermore, for any
Q=\sum_{t}z^{t}Q^{(t)}\in F[z]
let
\widehat{Q}=\sum_{t}z^{t}\widehat{Q}^{(t)}
. Recall that if
Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
for all
t
, then
\delta_{G}(Q)=\max_{t}\delta_{-tG}Q^{(t)}
. The following definition is from
[
29
]
, and it describes the output of their root-finding algorithm:

Definition 5.27
.

If
\widehat{Q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!][z]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then a
basic root set
of
\widehat{Q}
to precision
\beta
is a set
\{(\widehat{f}_{r},\alpha_{r})\}_{r=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
m\leq\deg\widehat{Q}
such that

1.

\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f}_{r}+x^{\alpha_{r}}z)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}
for
r=1,\dots,m
, and

2.

\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}\iff\widehat{f}\in\bigcup_{r=1}^{m}(\widehat{f}_{r}+x^{\alpha_{r}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!])
for every
\widehat{f}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
.

Our algorithm for computing the sought roots of
Q\in\mathcal{M}_{s,\ell}(D,G)
will fundamentally rely on the following result:

Theorem 5.28

(
[
29
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

There is an algorithm which for any
\widehat{Q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!][z]
and any precision
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
computes a basic root set of
\widehat{Q}
to precision
\beta
using
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell\beta)
deterministic operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, together with an extra
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\mathsf{R}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\ell)\beta)
operations, where
\mathsf{R}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\ell)
is the cost of finding all
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-roots of a degree
\ell
polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]
. Here, we can choose to use a Las Vegas algorithm with
\mathsf{R}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\ell)\in\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell)
, e.g.
[
45
, Corollary 14.16]
, or a deterministic one from
[
41
]
with
\mathsf{R}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\ell)\in\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell\kappa^{2}\sqrt{p})
, where
|\mathbb{F}_{q}|=p^{\kappa}
for some prime
p
.

In order to use
Theorem

5.28
in our setting, we will need to address the following:

1.

how to choose the precision
\beta
,

2.

how to convert
Q\in\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
to
\widehat{Q}\in\bigoplus_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
and

3.

how to obtain the roots
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
of
Q
from a basic root set of
\widehat{Q}
.

The second item in the above list is the simplest – writing
Q=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}Q^{(t)}
with
Q^{(t)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}Q^{(t)}_{i}y^{(-tG)}_{i}
, where
Q^{(t)}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, we can compute
\widehat{Q}=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}\widehat{Q}^{(t)}
by simply relying on the identity
\widehat{Q}^{(t)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}Q^{(t)}_{i}\widehat{y}^{(-tG)}_{i}
. Assuming that we have precomputed the
\widehat{y}^{(-tG)}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!]
to sufficiently high precision, this is just basic arithmetic in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.

When it comes to the choice of the precision
\beta
, then there are two restrictions that ought to be considered. The first one comes from making sure that we don’t return “spurious” roots, i.e. those
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
such that
\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}
while
Q(f)\neq 0
. As we are about to see in the following lemma, this issue is easily avoided by choosing
\beta>\delta_{G}(Q)
.

Lemma 5.29
.

Let
Q(z)=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}Q^{(t)}
with
Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
, and let
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
. If
\beta>\delta_{G}(Q)
and
\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f})\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}
, then
Q(f)=0
.

Proof.

Notice that since
f^{t}Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
for all
t
, then
Q(f)\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
. Furthermore, since

\delta(f^{t}Q^{(t)})=\delta_{tG}(f^{t})+\delta_{-tG}(Q^{(t)})\leq\delta_{-tG}(Q^{(t)})\leq\delta_{G}(Q)\ ,

where the first inequality is due to
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
, then
\delta(Q(f))\leq\delta_{G}(Q)
. Combining this with the assumption that
\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f})=\widehat{Q(f)}\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}
, we may conclude that
Q(f)\in\mathcal{L}(\delta_{G}(Q)P_{\infty}-\beta P_{0})
, and if
\beta>\delta_{G}(Q)
, then this Riemann-Roch space is trivial.
∎

The second restriction on the precision
\beta
is posed by the task of converting the truncated power series roots of
\widehat{Q}
back to
\mathcal{L}(G)
. Indeed, a basic root set
\{(\widehat{f}_{r},\alpha_{r})\}_{r=1}^{m}
describes each root
\widehat{f}_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of
\widehat{Q}
only to precision
\alpha_{r}
, and if this
\alpha_{r}
is too small, then there could exist two distinct functions
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(G)
satisfying
\widehat{h}_{1}\equiv\widehat{h}_{2}\equiv\widehat{f_{r}}\pmod{x^{\alpha_{r}}}
. In
Lemma

5.31
, we will see how we can indirectly control
\alpha_{r}
by increasing
\beta
; but first, let us show that conversion from truncated power series to
\mathcal{L}(G)
is guaranteed to be unambiguous as long as
\alpha_{r}>\deg G
.

Lemma 5.30
.

If
\alpha>\deg G
, then for any
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
it holds that
|\mathcal{L}(G)\cap(h+x^{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!])|\leq 1
.

Proof.

If
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\cap(h+x^{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\![x]\!])
, then
\widehat{h_{1}}\equiv\widehat{h_{2}}\equiv h\pmod{x^{\alpha}}
, which means that
h_{1}-h_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(G-\alpha P_{0})=\{0\}
.
∎

Now we proceed by showing that the
\alpha_{r}
from
Definition

5.27
can be made arbitrarily large by choosing the precision
\beta
appropriately.

Lemma 5.31
.

If
Q(z)=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}z^{t}Q^{(t)}\neq 0
with
Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
, and if
f\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G)
satisfies
\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f}+x^{\alpha}z)\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{\ell}(\beta-\delta_{G}(Q))-\delta_{G}(f)
.

Proof.

We begin by defining

T=Q(z+f)=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}(z+f)^{t}Q^{(t)}=\sum_{t=0}^{\ell}\sum_{u=0}^{t}\binom{t}{u}z^{u}f^{t-u}Q^{(t)}=\sum_{u=0}^{\ell}z^{u}T_{u}\ ,

where
T_{u}=\sum_{t=u}^{\ell}\binom{t}{u}f^{t-u}Q^{(t)}
. Since
f^{t-u}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}((t-u)G)
and
Q^{(t)}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-tG)
, then
T_{u}\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(-uG)
. Furthermore,
x^{\alpha u}\widehat{T}_{u}\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}
for all
u
because

\widehat{Q}(\widehat{f}+x^{\alpha}z)=\widehat{T}(x^{\alpha}z)=\sum_{u=0}^{\ell}z^{u}x^{\alpha u}\widehat{T}_{u}\equiv 0\pmod{x^{\beta}}\ .

Letting
r\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
be such that
v_{P_{0}}(T_{r})<\infty
is maximal, observe that

\displaystyle\alpha\ell+v_{P_{0}}(T_{r})\geq\alpha r+v_{P_{0}}(T_{r})=v_{P_{0}}(x^{\alpha r}T_{r})\geq\beta\ ,

which implies that
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{\ell}(\beta-v_{P_{0}}(T_{r}))
.
Finally, noting that

0\neq T_{r}\in\mathcal{L}(\delta_{-rG}(T_{r})P_{\infty}-rG-v_{P_{0}}(T_{r})P_{0})\ ,

then the sought conclusion follows from

\displaystyle v_{P_{0}}(T_{r})

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{-rG}(T_{r})-r\deg G\leq\delta_{-rG}(T_{r})

\displaystyle=\delta_{-rG}\big(\sum_{t=r}^{\ell}\binom{t}{r}f^{t-r}Q^{(t)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t}\{\delta_{-rG}(f^{t-r}Q^{(t)})\}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t}\{(t-r)\delta_{G}(f)+\delta_{-tG}(Q^{(t)})\}

\displaystyle=\ell\delta_{G}(f)+\delta_{G}(Q)\ .

∎

Combining
Lemma

5.31
and
Lemma

5.30
, we obtain the final restriction

\beta\geq 2\ell\deg G+s(n-\tau)\ ,

which ensures that unambiguous conversion from the truncated power series roots of
\widehat{Q}
to
\mathcal{L}(G)
is always possible. Indeed, this bound follows immediately from the fact that
\delta_{G}(f)\leq\deg G
for all
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
and the assumption that
\delta_{G}(Q)<s(n-\tau)
. Knowing that such conversion is possible, however, is not enough – we also need to know how to actually carry it out. In the following simple lemma, we show how to do this.

Lemma 5.32
.

If
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}f_{i}\widehat{y}_{i}^{(G)}\equiv\hat{f}\pmod{x^{\alpha}}
for some
f_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
\deg f_{i}\leq-\frac{1}{\mu}\delta_{G}(y_{i}^{(G)})
and
\alpha>\deg G
, then
\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}f_{i}y_{i}^{(G)}=f
.

Proof.

Since
\delta(f_{i})=\mu\deg f_{i}
, then
\delta_{G}(f_{i}y_{i}^{(G)})\leq\mu\deg f_{i}+\delta_{G}(y_{i}^{(G)})\leq 0
. But then
\sum_{i=0}^{\mu-1}f_{i}y_{i}^{(G)}\in\mathcal{L}(G)
, and the conclusion follows from
Lemma

5.30
.
∎

Using the notation from
Definition

5.7
in the context of
Lemma

5.32
, we see that
(f_{0},\dots,f_{\mu-1},1)\in\mathcal{H}_{x^{\alpha}}(\bm{A})
, where
\bm{A}=[\widehat{y}_{0}^{(G)},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{\mu-1}^{(G)},-\widehat{f}]\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{1\times(\mu+1)}
.
Recovering
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
from
\widehat{f}\rem x^{\alpha}
thus translates to finding a polynomial vector
\bm{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{x^{\alpha}}(\bm{A})
whose rightmost entry is
1
and
\deg_{\bm{d}}\bm{f}=0
, where

\bm{d}=\frac{1}{\mu}(\delta_{G}(y_{0}^{(G)}),\dots,\delta_{G}(y_{\mu-1}^{(G)}),0)\in(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\mathbb{Z})^{\mu+1}\ .

But this is easily accomplished by relying on
Theorem

5.8
and
Corollary

5.10
. We conclude this subsection by presenting our root-finding approach in its entirety in
Algorithm

.

Proof.

For correctness, our goal is to prove that

6

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)

We are now ready to state our Guruswami-Sudan list decoding algorithm for the code
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)

1.

divisor
E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{N}
, where
E_{1},\dots,E_{N}
are distinct rational places different from

not occurring in
\supp G
and

2.

evaluations
\bm{g}=(g_{v,j}^{(u)})

u=0,\dots,\ell
,

and

g_{v,j}^{(u)}=g_{v}^{(u)}(E_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
where
\langle g_{1}^{(u)},g_{2}^{(u)}\rangle_{\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}}=\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}(G_{u})

4.5

3.

evaluations
\bm{x}=(x_{j})_{j=1,\dots,N}
, where
x_{j}=x(E_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

Note that the decoding algorithm returns the functions from

giving rise to all codewords within radius

Theorem 6.1
.

The Guruswami-Sudan algorithm for the AG code
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
can be carried out in complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{\omega+1}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
. Using the alternative generating set from
Remark

4.7
, we obtain the complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}\mu^{\omega-1}(n+g))
.

We now give several examples comparing this result with previously known results.

6.1
Examples

Example 6.2
.

AG codes obtained from the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
are known as
generalized Reed-Solomon (GRS) codes
. In this case
g=0
and
\mu=1
, which specializes the complexity of
Algorithm

7
to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}n)
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The same complexity is achieved for families of function fields having fixed small genus, e.g. those arising from elliptic curves. The best known complexity for Guruswami-Sudan list-decoding of GRS codes is
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s^{2}\ell^{\omega-1}n)

[
9
]
.

Example 6.3
.

By definition, any maximal function field
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
attains the Hasse-Weil bound – it has exactly
N_{1}=q+1+2g\sqrt{q}
rational places, where
q
is necessarily a square. If
F
is such a function field, then any place
P
of
F\widebar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
\widebar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denotes the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, necessarily contains a positive element no larger than
\sqrt{q}
in its Weierstrass semigroup
[
19
, Theorem 10.6]
, i.e. we are guaranteed that
\mu\leq\sqrt{q}
in the complexity of
Algorithm

7
. Furthermore, it is well known that all maximal function fields satisfy
g\leq\sqrt{q}(\sqrt{q}-1)/2\in\mathcal{O}(q)
. This implies that any code of length
n\in\Omega(q)
over such a function field can be decoded using no more that
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}q^{(\omega-1)/2}n)\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}n^{(\omega+1)/2})
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is sub-quadratic in the code length. Here, and in the rest of the examples,
u\in\Omega(v)
if and only if
v\in\mathcal{O}(u)
for any functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

We obtain even better results for long codes over specific maximal function fields:

Example 6.4
.

In the case of Hermitian function field
F=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
, where
x_{2}^{q}+x_{2}=x_{1}^{q+1}
, we have
N_{1}=q^{3}+1
rational places and genus
g=q(q-1)/2
. The usual choice of
P_{\infty}
in one-point codes of
F
gives
\mu=q
. Consequently, we can decode any such code of length
n\in\Omega(q^{3})
using

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}q^{\omega-1}q^{3})=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}q^{\omega+2})=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}n^{(\omega+2)/3})

operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
n=q^{3}
, our approach specializes to the one from
[
32
]
.

Example 6.5
.

The Giulietti-Korchmaros function field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
from
[
14
]
, where
x_{2}^{q}+x_{2}=x_{1}^{q+1}
and
x_{3}^{q^{2}-q+1}=x_{1}^{q^{2}}-x_{1}
, is also maximal – it has
\mu\leq q^{3}
,
g=(q^{5}-2q^{3}+q^{2})/2
and
N_{1}=q^{8}-q^{6}+q^{5}+1
. In this case, we can decode any code of length
n\in\Omega(q^{8})
with cost
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}n^{(3\omega+5)/8})
.

Example 6.6
.

The Suzuki function field
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{1},x_{2})
, where
q=2^{2e+1}
is an odd power of two and
x_{2}^{q}+x_{2}=x_{1}^{2^{e}}(x_{1}^{q}+x_{1})
, has genus
g=2^{e}(q-1)
and
N_{1}=q^{2}+1
rational places. Although it is not maximal in the sense of the Hasse-Weil bound, no other function field with the same genus and constant field can surpass its number of rational places
[
40
, Section 5.4]
. From
[
3
]
, it immediately follows that the Weierstrass semigroup of any place
P
contains a positive element no greater than
q
, i.e.
\mu\leq q
. This means that for any code over
F
of length
n\in\Omega(q^{2})
, the complexity of
Algorithm

7
specializes to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}n^{(\omega+1)/2})
.

Example 6.7
.

Let
F
be a function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
having a rational place
P_{\infty}
whose Weierstrass semigroup can be generated by two positive integers, say
a
and
b
, where
a<b
. Note that necessarily
\gcd(a,b)=1
, since otherwise the semigroup generated by
a
and
b
has infinitely many gaps. The genus of such a function field is
(a-1)(b-1)/2
, since this is the number of gaps of the semigroup generated by
a
and
b
. Now let
x,y\in\mathord{\mathchoice{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}{\mbox{Я}}}
be such that
\delta(x)=a
and
\delta(y)=b
. Then
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)
and
x^{b}+\alpha y^{a}+g(x,y)=0
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
and
g(X,Y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,Y]
has
(a,b)
-weighted degree strictly less then
ab
. The curve defined by the equation
X^{b}+\alpha Y^{a}+g(X,Y)=0
is sometimes called a
C_{ab}
-curve or a Miura-Kayima curve
[
28
]
; codes defined over such curves are of particular interest for practical applications, as they can be encoded efficiently
[
6
]
. When it comes to decoding, the additional assumptions that
G=mP_{\infty}
and that
D-nP_{\infty}
is a principal divisor were used in
[
4
]
to decode the code
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
in complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{5}a^{3}(n+g))
.

Let us compare this to our results. Knowing that
F
has a rational point
P_{\infty}
whose Weierstrass semigroup contains two positive, relatively prime integers
a
and
b
, implies that
g\leq(a-1)(b-1)/2
and
\mu\leq a
. Using this weaker assumption and not needing the additional requirement that
G=mP_{\infty}
and that
D-nP_{\infty}
is a principal divisor, we can decode
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{L}}(D,G)
in complexity
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(s\ell^{\omega}a^{\omega-1}(n+g))
. Hence, our results can both handle more general settings and decode faster.

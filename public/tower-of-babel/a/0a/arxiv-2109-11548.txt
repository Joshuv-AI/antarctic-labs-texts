Multipartite Mixed Maximally Entangled States: Mixed States with Entanglement 1
Samuel R. Hedemann

Abstract
We present a full definition of mixed maximally entangled (MME) states for multipartite systems, generalizing their existing definition for bipartite systems by using multipartite Schmidt decomposition. MME states are a special kind of maximally entangled mixed state (MEMS) for which every pure decomposition state in all decompositions is maximally entangled. Thus, MME states have entanglement 1 by all valid unit-normalized entanglement measures, whereas general MEMS can have entanglement less than 1. Multipartite MME states likely have important applications such as remote state preparation, and also set critical performance goals for entanglement measures.

{\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}(|2\rangle+|3\rangle)}&\!\!{=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}(|1,2\rangle+|2,1\rangle).}\\
\end{array}

(14)

Thus, in terms of the
scalar
index
argument
to
\mathbf{v}_{j,h}\equiv\mathbf{v}_{\textnormal{\raisebox{0.5pt}[0.5pt][0.5pt]{$(L_{{\text{ME}}})_{j,h}$}}}^{\textnormal{\raisebox{1.3pt}[1.3pt][1.3pt]{$\{N,\mathbf{n}\}$}}}
[which is
(L_{{\text{ME}}})_{j,h}
], the
j
references the
j
th ME TGX tuple [such as
\{2,3\}
for
j=2
in
(
13
)
], while
h
references the
h
th element of that ME TGX tuple [such as
3
for
h=2
in ME TGX tuple
\{2,3\}
]. Then,
\mathbf{v}_{j,h}
is the inverse indical register function applied to
(L_{{\text{ME}}})_{j,h}
, for instance converting
3
to
\{2,1\}
, as seen in
(
13
)
.

From
HedE
, generally
non
TGX states of the same entanglement as
|\Phi_{j}(\bm{\theta})\rangle
can be formed from
|\Phi_{j}(\bm{\theta})\rangle
by local unitary (LU) equivalence as

\begin{array}[]{*{20}l}{|\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta})\rangle}&\!\!{\equiv(U^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes U^{(N)})|\Phi_{j}(\bm{\theta})\rangle}\\
&\!\!{=\sum\limits_{h=1}^{L_{*}}{}x_{h}^{[L_{*}]}(\bm{\theta})|U_{:,v_{j,h}^{(1)}}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|U_{:,v_{j,h}^{(N)}}^{(N)}\rangle,}\\
\end{array}

(15)

where
U^{(m)}
is an
n_{m}
-level unitary operator in mode
m
, and
A_{:,c}
is the
c
th column vector of matrix
A
so that
|A_{:,c}\rangle
is the ket formed from column
c
of
A
; thus
|U_{\textnormal{\raisebox{1.9pt}[1.9pt][1.9pt]{${\kern 4.0pt}:,{\kern 2.5pt}v_{j,h}^{(m)}$}}}^{\textnormal{\raisebox{0.8pt}[0.8pt][0.8pt]{$(m)$}}}\rangle\equiv U^{(m)}|v_{j,h}^{(m)}\rangle
. (Note: as explained in
HedE
, we could define an equivalent set of states with a generally
nonlocal
diagonal unitary
D
inserted in
(
15
)
as
(U^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes U^{(N)})D|\Phi_{j}(\bm{\theta})\rangle
, but the zeros of the TGX core
|\Phi_{j}(\bm{\theta})\rangle
cause
D
to always have a form that allows it to be absorbed into the LU so that
(
15
)
is sufficient.)

The form in
(
15
)
is the
multipartite Schmidt decomposition
(MSD) of a general pure state
|\psi\rangle\equiv|\Phi_{j}^{\prime}(\bm{\theta})\rangle
in the context of full
N
-partite entanglement. In general, systems posses multiple different
L_{*}
values, so any given
|\psi\rangle
may have multiple different MSDs, and since many different TGX cores are possible, multiple MSDs even exist when there is only one
L_{*}
value.

The
x_{h}^{[L_{*}]}(\bm{\theta})
are
multipartite Schmidt coefficients
, and instead of requiring coincidence-index matching among the modes (such as in bipartite systems where the common Schmidt decomposition
Schm
is
|\psi\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{q=1}^{n_{S}}{x_{q}^{[n_{S}]}|U_{:,q}^{(1)}\rangle|U_{:,q}^{(2)}\rangle}
where
n_{S}\equiv\min\{\mathbf{n}\}
, since
L_{*}=n_{S}
for
N=2
), here we use the more general
TGX-indexing
which means that the indices must simply be the vector-indices of each level in any ME TGX tuple of the system. (We give a more general example in
Sec.
, but in
2\times 2
this shows that the Schmidt decomposition could also be written as
|\psi\rangle=

x_{1}^{[2]}|U_{:,1}^{(1)}\rangle|U_{:,2}^{(2)}\rangle+x_{2}^{[2]}|U_{:,2}^{(1)}\rangle|U_{:,1}^{(2)}\rangle
which is based on the ME TGX tuple
\{2,3\}=\{\{1,2\},\{2,1\}\}
, rather than the more popular
|\psi\rangle=x_{1}^{[2]}|U_{:,1}^{(1)}\rangle|U_{:,1}^{(2)}\rangle+x_{2}^{[2]}|U_{:,2}^{(1)}\rangle|U_{:,2}^{(2)}\rangle
, based on ME TGX tuple
\{1,4\}=\{\{1,1\},\{2,2\}\}
.)

From
HedE
, any pure state is maximally full-
N
-partite entangled iff
x_{{\kern 1.5pt}h}^{\textnormal{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$[L_{*}]$}}}=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{L_{*}}}}\;\forall h\in 1\ldots,L_{*}
, so that generally nonTGX ME pure states have the form

\begin{array}[]{*{20}l}{|\Phi_{j}^{\prime}\rangle}&\!\!{\equiv(U^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes U^{(N)})|\Phi_{j}\rangle,}\\
&\!\!{={\frac{1}{{\sqrt{L_{*}}}}}\sum\limits_{h=1}^{L_{*}}{|U_{:,v_{j,h}^{(1)}}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|U_{:,v_{j,h}^{(N)}}^{(N)}\rangle},}\\
\end{array}

(16)

so
all ME states are LU equivalent to ME TGX states
,

\begin{array}[]{*{20}l}{|\Phi_{{\text{ME}}_{{\text{TGX}}}}\rangle\equiv|\Phi_{j}\rangle}&\!\!{\equiv\frac{1}{{\sqrt{L_{*}}}}\sum\limits_{h=1}^{L_{*}}{|(L_{{\text{ME}}})_{j,h}\rangle}}\\
&\!\!{=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{L_{*}}}}\sum\limits_{h=1}^{L_{*}}{|v_{j,h}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|v_{j,h}^{(N)}\rangle,}}\\
\end{array}

(17)

which reflects the fact proven in
HedE
that all ME TGX states have
L_{*}
nonzero probability amplitudes of equal magnitude. Since those probability amplitude magnitudes are exactly the multipartite Schmidt coefficients
x_{h}^{[L_{*}]}(\bm{\theta})
, then the LU equivalence of all pure states to phaseless TGX states is why having
L_{*}
multipartite Schmidt coefficients of equal values implies ME for
non
TGX states as well, as stated before
(
16
)
.

In the context of full
N
-partite entanglement, ME pure states are those for which all
N
single-mode reductions have the lowest possible purity combination that they can simultaneously have given a pure parent state (minimum physical simultaneous reduction purities). (Note: as proven in
HedE
, this does
not
always mean that all reductions are maximally mixed states,
nor
does it mean that their reductions are necessarily diagonal.)

Now we have the concepts we need to define multipartite MME, which we do next.

IV
Multipartite MME Conditions

Here we derive some necessary conditions for multipartite MME, and then identify some sufficient conditions and show that they are also necessary, yielding
(
5
)
.

By definition,
all
decompositions of an MME state
\rho
must contain
only
ME pure states, which therefore
includes
the
spectral decomposition
. Therefore, a necessary condition for a rank-
r
multipartite state
\rho
to be MME is that it has spectral decomposition

\rho=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}{\lambda_{k}|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\langle\Phi_{k}^{\prime}|},

(18)

where
\lambda_{k}\in(0,1)
are eigenvalues such that
\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{r}{\lambda_{k}=1}
, and its eigenstates
|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle
are the generally
non
TGX ME pure states of
(
16
)
. Again, by the definition of MME, as in
LZFF
, any
other
decomposition of
\rho
must have the form

\rho=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{D\geqslant r}{p_{j}|w_{j}\rangle\langle w_{j}|},

(19)

where each
|w_{j}\rangle
is
also
ME, and given by

|w_{j}\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{{\sqrt{p_{j}}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}{U_{j,k}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle},

(20)

where
U\equiv U^{[D\geqslant r]}
is any
D
-level unitary, and

p_{j}\equiv\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}{|U_{j,k}|^{2}\lambda_{k}},\;\;\text{so also}\;\;\frac{1}{{p_{j}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}{|U_{j,k}|^{2}\lambda_{k}}=1.

(21)

Then, the single-mode reductions of each
|w_{j}\rangle
are

{\rho}{\kern-5.3pt}{{\textnormal{\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{\textasciicaron}}}}}{\kern-4.2pt}{~}^{(m)}_{|w_{j}\rangle}=\frac{1}{{p_{j}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}{}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}{}U_{j,k}U_{j,l}^{*}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}}\text{tr}_{{\kern 0.0pt}\mathop{m}\limits^{{\kern-0.3pt}{\overline{{\kern 5.5pt}}}}{\kern 0.0pt}}(|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\langle\Phi_{l}^{\prime}|),

(22)

for
m\in 1,\ldots,N
and
j\in 1,\ldots,D
where
D\geqslant r
, and
\rho_{|\psi\rangle}\equiv|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
, so
{\rho}{\kern-5.3pt}{{\textnormal{\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{\textasciicaron}}}}}{\kern-4.2pt}{~}^{(m)}_{|\psi\rangle}\equiv\text{tr}_{{\kern 0.0pt}\mathop{m}\limits^{{\kern-0.3pt}{\overline{{\kern 5.5pt}}}}{\kern 0.0pt}}(\rho_{|\psi\rangle})
.

Since
MME requires
all

|w_{j}\rangle
to be ME for any decomposition (a given
U
), an equivalent MME condition is

{\rho}{\kern-5.3pt}{{\textnormal{\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{\textasciicaron}}}}}{\kern-4.2pt}{~}^{(m)}_{|w_{j}\rangle}={\rho}{\kern-5.3pt}{{\textnormal{\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{\textasciicaron}}}}}{\kern-4.2pt}{~}^{(m)\prime}_{\text{MP}_{j}}\;\;\;\forall m,\;\forall j,

(23)

where
{\rho}{\kern-5.3pt}{{\textnormal{\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{\textasciicaron}}}}}{\kern-4.2pt}{~}^{(m)\prime}_{\text{MP}_{j}}
is a mode-
m
state of minimum physical simultaneous reduction purity (MPSRP) given a pure ME parent state [the prime is to match those in
(
22
)
].

Since MME requires
(
23
)
for
all
decompositions (and hence all
U
), and since MPSRP states have specific reduction purities, another MME condition is that

P({\rho}{\kern-5.3pt}{{\textnormal{\raisebox{-1.2pt}{\scalebox{1.2}{\textasciicaron}}}}}{\kern-4.2pt}{~}^{(m)}_{|w_{j}\rangle})\;\;\text{is independent of }U\;\;\;\forall m,\;\forall j,

(24)

where again
P(\sigma)\equiv\text{tr}(\sigma^{2})
is
purity
.

Furthermore, to find the full set of MME states, we want to maximize the rank
r
of
\rho
, so a side condition is

k\in 1,\ldots,\max\{r\}\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\;\rho\;\;\text{is MME}.

(25)

Further necessary conditions are implied by
(
18
)
as follows. First, note that since the generally nonTGX ME states
\{|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\}
are orthonormal [since they are eigenstates of
\rho
in
(
18
)
], they can be expressed as the first
r
columns in a single unitary operator
W
(where the remaining
n-r
columns could be filled in by the maximally entangled basis (MEB) theorem from
HedE
), so

|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\equiv|W_{:,k}\rangle=W|k\rangle,

(26)

where
|k\rangle
is the
k
th computational basis state. Similarly, by the MEB, there always exists an orthonormal set of ME TGX states
\{|\Phi_{k}\rangle\}
and some unitary
V
such that

|\Phi_{k}\rangle\equiv|V_{:,k}\rangle=V|k\rangle,

(27)

so, inverting
(
27
)
as
|k\rangle=V^{{\dagger}}|\Phi_{k}\rangle
, then
(
26
)
becomes

|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle=WV^{{\dagger}}|\Phi_{k}\rangle.

(28)

Thus, the MEBs
\{|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\}
and
\{|\Phi_{k}\rangle\}
are unitarily related. Furthermore, if
\{|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\}
all have
core ME TGX states

\{|\Phi_{k}\rangle\}
of the same
L_{*}
in their MSD of
(
16
)
, then the unitary
WV^{{\dagger}}
in
(
28
)
is LU as

|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle=U_{{\text{LU}}}|\Phi_{k}\rangle=(U^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes U^{(N)})|\Phi_{k}\rangle.

(29)

Thus, each
|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle
in
(
18
)
has a core ME TGX state
|\Phi_{k}\rangle
preserving the orthonormality of
\{|\Phi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\}
, and if all
|\Phi_{k}\rangle
have the same
L_{*}
, these sets are related by a
single
LU, so there is no need to give index
k
to
U_{{\text{LU}}}
. (MME states with
\{|\Phi_{k}\rangle\}
of
multiple different

L_{*}
values may exist, but would be related to single-
L_{*}
MME states by a nonlocal EPU, so we lose no generality by focusing on single-
L_{*}
MME states in our quest to find all MME states.)

Now that we have some necessary conditions, we propose the following two mechanisms for achieving them as candidates for sufficient conditions for MME (they will require refining further below to achieve this sufficiency).

1.

One way to achieve

24
)

and

(

)

2.

However
,

In fact, for every mode

For each mode label

-reduction

). Note that

To see if the candidate conditions of

\forall m\in 1,\ldots,N

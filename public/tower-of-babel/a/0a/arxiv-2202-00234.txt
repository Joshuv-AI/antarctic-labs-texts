Periodic Traveling Waves in an Integro-Difference Equation With Non-Monotonic Growth and Strong Allee Effect
Michael Nestor and Bingtuan Li

Abstract
We derive sufficient conditions for the existence of a periodic traveling wave solution to an integro-difference equation with a piecewise constant growth function exhibiting a stable period-2 cycle and strong Allee effect. The mean traveling wave speed is shown to be the asymptotic spreading speed of solutions with compactly supported initial data under appropriate conditions. We then conduct case studies for the Laplace kernel and uniform kernel.



Key words:
Integro-difference equation, period two cycle, Allee effect, periodic traveling wave.

AMS Subject Classification:
92D40, 92D25.

Introduction

Integro-difference equations in the form

\displaystyle u_{n+1}(x)\;=\;Q[u_{n}](x)\;:=\,\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}k(x-y)\,g\left(u_{n}(y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

(1.1)

are of great interest in the studies of invasions of populations with discrete generations and separate growth and dispersal stages.
They have been used to predict changes in gene frequency
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
17
]
, and applied to ecological problems
[

,
3
,

,

,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
Previous rigorous studies on integro-difference equations have assumed that the growth function is nondecreasing
[
17
,
18
]
, or is non-monotone without Allee effect
[
10
,
16
]
.
The results show existence of constant spreading speeds and travelng waves with fixed shapes and speeds.
Sullivan et al.
[
15
]
demonstrated numerically that an integro-difference equation with a non-monotone growth function exhibiting a strong Allee effect can generate traveling waves with fluctuating speeds, and Otto
[
13
]
showed that such an equation can have robust non-spreading solutions.
In this paper we give conditions for the existence of a periodic traveling wave with two intermediate speeds for an integro-difference equation with a piecewise constant growth function that exhibits a strong Allee effect and a period-2 cycle.

Piecewise constant growth functions have been used in the studies of integro-difference equations; see for example
[
6
,
11
,
13
,
15
]
.
Such an equation is analytically tractable and it can provide specific insights into the dynamics of solutions.
For the piecewise constant growth function

g(u)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }u<a,\\
1,&\text{if }u\geq a,\end{cases}

(1.2)

with
0<a<1
, which exhibits a strong Allee effect and monotonicity, Kot et al.
[
6
]
and Lutscher
[
11
]
(Section 6.1) investigated the range expansion of the population and the spreading speed, and Sullivan et al.

[
15
]
studied oscillations in spreading speeds when the dispersal kernel is density dependent.
More discussions about integro-difference equations with piecewise growth functions can be found in Lutscher
[
11
]
(Chapter 15).

In this paper, we consider the integro-difference equation (
1.1
) with

g(u)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }u<a,\\
1,&\text{if }a\leq u\leq b,\\
m,&\text{if }u>b,\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
0<a<m<b<1
.

g(u)
is a piecewise constant non-monotone growth function exhibiting a strong Allee effect
[
1
]
.
Specifically, it has a stable fixed point at zero and a stable period two cycle
(1,m)
with
a

This is the function considered in Otto
[
13

where non-spreading solitions are studied.
It may be viewed as an extension of (

A graphical demonstration of

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that traveling waves with oscillating speeds have been analytically established for scalar spatiotemporal equations with constant parameters.
We also show that the mean traveling wave speed is the spreading speed of solutions with compactly supported initial data under appropriate conditions.
We finally conduct case studies for the Laplace kernel and uniform kernel.

In this section, we present our main results about our model.
Throughout, we assume all population density functions are bounded elements of
C(\mathbb{R})
, the space of real-valued continuous functions.
It is straightforward to verify this space is closed under iteration of
Q

We denote the norm of a function

We make the following assumptions about the dispersal kernel.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

k(x)

(H1)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1

(H2)

k(x)=k(-x)

(H5)

for all

2.1
Periodic traveling waves

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in C(\mathbb{R})
be bounded, continuous functions defined by

w_{1}(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}k(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.1)

and

w_{2}(x):=Q[w_{1}](x+c^{*})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k(x+c^{*}-y)g(w_{1}(y))\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.2)

where
c^{*}
is a constant defined by

c^{*}:=\frac{1}{2}\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid Q[w_{1}](x)=a\}.

(2.3)

The following theorem shows that
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
generate a traveling wave-like solution to (
1.1
). We term this solution a
periodic traveling wave
since it alternates between two distinct wave profiles (with different limits at
-\infty
) while traveling with mean speed
c^{*}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(W_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bounded, continuous functions defined by

W_{n}(x):=\begin{cases}w_{1}(x-nc^{*}),&n\text{ even},\\
w_{2}(x-nc^{*}),&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

(2.4)

If
||w_{2}||_{\infty}<b
, then
W_{n+1}=Q[W_{n}]
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, define the right inverse of
w_{i}
by

\Phi_{i}(\ell):=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid w_{i}(x)=\ell\},\quad i=1,2.

(2.5)

Note that
\Phi_{i}(\ell)
is finite for
0<\ell<||w_{i}||_{\infty}
, where
||w_{1}||_{\infty}=1
, and
a<||w_{2}||_{\infty}<b
by the assumption of this theorem.

By the translation invariance property of
Q
, it suffices to show

Q[w_{1}](x)=w_{2}(x-c^{*})

(2.6)

and

Q[w_{2}](x)=w_{1}(x-c^{*})

(2.7)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
The first equation follows immediately from definition (
2.2
).
To prove the second, observe that
w_{1}
is monotonically decreasing and satisfies
w_{1}(\infty)=0
and
w_{1}(-\infty)=1
(see for example Figure
2
).
Thus,

g(w_{1}(x))=\begin{cases}m,&x<\Phi_{1}(b),\\
1,&\Phi_{1}(b)\leq x\leq\Phi_{1}(a),\\
0,&x>\Phi_{1}(a).\end{cases}

Applying the integro-difference operator yields

\displaystyle w_{2}(x-c^{*})

\displaystyle=Q[w_{1}](x)

\displaystyle=m\int_{-\infty}^{\Phi_{1}(b)}k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Phi_{1}(b)}^{\Phi_{1}(a)}k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x-\Phi_{1}(a))-(1-m)\,w_{1}(x-\Phi_{1}(b)).

We can then differentiate with respect to
x
:

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w_{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\Big|_{x-c^{*}}=-k(x-\Phi_{1}(a))+(1-m)\,k(x-\Phi_{1}(b)).

From (H
4
),
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w_{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}
has at most 1 sign change; thus,
w_{2}
has at most 1 turning point.
We also have
w_{2}(x)<b
for all
x
, hence
g(w_{2}(x))\in\{0,1\}
.

We claim

g(w_{2}(x))=\begin{cases}1,&x\leq c^{*},\\
0,&x>c^{*},\end{cases}

(2.8)

where
c^{*}
is defined by (
2.3
).
This can be shown by arguing in cases depending on the number of turning points of
w_{2}
.

Case 1.

If
w_{2}
has no turning points, then
w_{2}
is monotone decreasing with
0\leq w_{2}(x)\leq m
for all
x
.
It follows by the intermediate value theorem (IVT) that
c^{*}=\Phi_{2}(a)
satisfies (
2.8
).

Case 2.

If
w_{2}
has one turning point, say
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then
w_{2}
must be increasing for
x<x_{0}
and decreasing for
x>x_{0}
.
It follows that
x_{0}
is a global maximum, and
w_{2}
is increasing on
(-\infty,x_{0})
and decreasing on
(x_{0},\infty)
.
We have
g(w_{2}(x))=1
for
x<x_{0}
.
By the IVT,
c^{*}=\Phi_{2}(a)
is the unique solution to (
2.8
).

Taking the convolution of equation (
2.8
) with
k(x)
yields

Q[w_{2}](x)\,=\,\int_{-\infty}^{c^{*}}k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,=\,w_{1}(x-c^{*}).

This completes the proof.
∎

We investigated the intermediate wave speed generated by the periodic traveling wave solution (
2.4
).
To do this, we define a sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
of continuous functions
(0,m)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\xi_{n}(\ell):=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}:W_{n}(x)=\ell\},\quad\ell\in(0,m).

(2.9)

This expression yields the rightmost intersection of the curve
u=W_{n}(x)
with the horizontal line
u=\ell
.
By computing the first difference, we obtain a new function sequence
(c_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
(with the same domain and range) defined by

c_{n}(\ell):=\xi_{n+1}(\ell)-\xi_{n}(\ell),\quad\ell\in(0,m).

(2.10)

From Theorem

, we have that
c_{n}(\ell)
converges to a period-

\ell\in(0,m)

Our results about the intermediate wave speed sequence are summarized in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume the hypothesis of Theorem
2.2
holds. Then for all
\ell\in(0,m)
, the sequence
c_{n}(\ell)
satisfies

c_{n}(\ell)=c^{*}+\begin{cases}\Phi_{1}(\ell)-\Phi_{2}(\ell),&n\text{ even},\\
\Phi_{2}(\ell)-\Phi_{1}(\ell),&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

(2.11)

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\xi_{n}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid w_{1}(x-nc^{*})=\ell\},&n\text{ even},\\
\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid w_{2}(x-nc^{*})=\ell\},&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=nc^{*}+\begin{cases}\Phi_{1}(\ell),&n\text{ even},\\
\Phi_{2}(\ell),&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

for every
\ell\in(0,m)
. Taking the first difference yields

\displaystyle c_{n}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\xi_{n}(\ell)-\xi_{n-1}(\ell)

\displaystyle=nc^{*}+\begin{cases}\Phi_{1}(\ell),&n\text{ even},\\
\Phi_{2}(\ell),&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}-(n-1)\,c^{*}+\begin{cases}\Phi_{2}(\ell),&n\text{ even},\\
\Phi_{1}(\ell),&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=c^{*}+\begin{cases}\Phi_{1}(\ell)-\Phi_{2}(\ell),&n\text{ even},\\
\Phi_{2}(\ell)-\Phi_{1}(\ell),&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

∎

Thus, for each fixed
\ell\in(0,m)
, we can define intermediate traveling wave speeds
c_{1}^{*}
and
c_{2}^{*}
by

c_{1}^{*}(\ell):=c^{*}+\Phi_{1}(\ell)-\Phi_{2}(\ell)

(2.12)

and

c_{2}^{*}(\ell):=c^{*}+\Phi_{2}(\ell)-\Phi_{1}(\ell).

(2.13)

It follows that
(
1.1
)
has a periodic traveling wave with wave profiles
w_{1}(x)
and
w_{2}(x)
, intermediate wave speeds
c_{1}^{*}(\ell)
and
c_{2}^{*}(\ell)
, and mean wave speed
\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{1}^{*}(\ell)+c_{2}^{*}(\ell)\right)=c^{*}
.
Note that the intermediate wave speeds depend on
\ell
, but their sum is identically equal to
2c^{*}
.

2.2
Periodic spreading solutions

By applying the results of Theorem
2.2
, we were able to prove the asymptotic spreading speed of solutions with compact initial data.
In particular, we showed that
c^{*}
is the asymptotic spreading speed of compactly supported initial data with sufficient weight above the Allee threshold but below the overcompensation threshold.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume the hypothesis of Theorem
2.2
holds. Suppose
u\in C(\mathbb{R})
is bounded, non-negative, and has compact support. If

i.

||u||_{\infty}<b
,

ii.

the set
A:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:u(x)\geq a\}
is connected and has sufficiently large length, and

iii.

c^{*}>0
,

then the sequence
(u_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined by
u_{0}=u
and
u_{n+1}=Q[u_{n}]
for
n\geq 0
spreads asymptotically with mean speed
c^{*}
.

Proof.

We define two continuous functions
\tilde{w_{1}},\tilde{w_{2}}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{w_{1}}(x,r):=w_{1}(x)-w_{1}(x+2r)=\int_{-2r}^{0}k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.14)

and

\tilde{w_{2}}(x,r):=Q[\tilde{w_{1}}(\,\cdot\,,r)](x+c^{*}).

(2.15)

Observe that
\tilde{w_{1}}
is symmetric with respect to
x=-r
, and
\tilde{w_{2}}
is symmetric with respect to
x=-r-c^{*}
.
This notation can be justified by observing that
\tilde{w_{1}}(x,r)\to w_{1}(x)
and
\tilde{w_{2}}(x,r)\to w_{2}(x)
as
r\to\infty
for all
x
.
We also set

\phi_{i}(r,\ell):=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid\tilde{w}_{i}(x,r)=\ell\},\quad i=1,2.

(2.16)

\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are the right-inverse of
\tilde{w_{1}}
and
\tilde{w_{2}}
, respectively.
For a fixed
r>0
, the value of
\phi_{i}(r,\ell)
is finite if
0<\ell<||\tilde{w}_{i}(\,\cdot\,,r)||_{\infty}
, with the latter bound converging to
||w_{i}||_{\infty}
as
r\to\infty
.
They satisfy

\lim_{r\to\infty}\phi_{i}(r,\ell)=\Phi_{i}(\ell),\quad 0<\ell<||w_{i}||_{\infty},\quad i=1,2.

We now apply the growth function to
\tilde{w_{1}}
, assuming
r
is sufficiently large enough so that
a<||\tilde{w_{1}}(\,\cdot\,,r)||_{\infty}<b
; this is guaranteed by taking
r>\Phi_{1}\left(\frac{1-a}{2}\right)
.
We have

g\left(\tilde{w_{1}}(x,r)\right)=\begin{cases}0,&x<-2r-\phi_{1}(r,a),\\
1,&-2r-\phi_{1}(r,a)<x<-2r-\phi_{1}(r,b),\\
m,&-2r-\phi_{1}(r,b)<x<\phi_{1}(r,b),\\
1,&\phi_{1}(r,b)<x<\phi_{1}(r,a),\\
0,&\phi_{1}(r,a)<x.\end{cases}

Note that the above expression converges to
g(w_{1}(x))
as
r\to\infty
for all
x
.
We then apply the convolution operator:

\displaystyle\tilde{w_{2}}(x-c^{*},r)=Q[\tilde{w_{1}}(\,\cdot\,,r)](x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-2r-\phi_{1}(r,a)}^{\phi_{1}(r,a)}k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y-(1-m)\,\int_{-2r-\phi_{1}(r,b)}^{\phi_{1}(r,b)}k(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x-\phi_{1}(r,a))-(1-m)\,w_{1}(x-\phi_{1}(r,b))

\displaystyle+(1-m)\,w_{1}(x+2r+\phi_{1}(r,b))-w_{1}(x+2r+\phi_{1}(r,a)).

Computing the derivative of this expression yields

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{w_{2}}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\Big|_{x-c^{*}}

\displaystyle=-k(x-\phi_{1}(r,a))+(1-m)\,k(x-\phi_{1}(r,b))

\displaystyle-(1-m)\,k(x+2r+\phi_{1}(r,b))+k(x+2r+\phi_{1}(r,a)).

By (H
5
), the number of turning points of
\tilde{w_{2}}
is at most 3 (assuming
r
is sufficiently large).
Furthermore,
\tilde{w_{2}}
is symmetric around
x=-r-c^{*}
; thus the sign changes must come in pairs or occur exactly at
x=-r-c^{*}
.
We can immediately exclude the cases of 0 and 2 turning points because
\tilde{w_{2}}
is non-negative, vanishes at
\pm\infty
, and is not identically zero.
As in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, this leaves two cases:

Case 1.

If
\tilde{w_{2}}
has 1 turning point, then it must occur at
x=-r-c^{*}
.
We have
\tilde{w_{2}}(-r-c^{*},r)=w_{2}(-r-c^{*})-w_{2}(r+c^{*})\to m
as
r\to\infty
; hence, it is sufficient to take
r
large enough so that
|\tilde{w_{2}}(-r-c^{*},r)-m|<\min\{|a-m|,|b-m|\}
.
It follows by the triangle inequality that
\tilde{w_{2}}(x,r)\leq\tilde{w_{2}}(-r-c^{*},r)<b
for all
x
.

Case 2.

If
\tilde{w_{2}}
has 3 turning points, then again by symmetry, they must be given by
\{-r-c^{*}-t,-r-c^{*},-r-c^{*}+t\}
, for some
t>0
.
We can label them in increasing order by
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
.
It follows that
t_{1}
and
t_{3}
are maxima with
\tilde{w_{2}}(t_{1},r)=\tilde{w_{2}}(t_{3},r)\to||w_{2}||_{\infty}
as
r\to\infty
, and
t_{2}
is a local minima with
\tilde{w_{2}}(t_{2},r)\to m
as
r\to\infty
.
Thus
\tilde{w_{2}}
is monotone increasing on
(-\infty,t_{1})\cup(t_{2},t_{3})
and monotone decreasing on
(t_{1},t_{2})\cup(t_{3},\infty)
.
By taking
r
sufficiently large, we can make
\left|\tilde{w_{2}}(t_{1},r)-||w_{2}||_{\infty}\right|=\left|\tilde{w_{2}}(t_{3},r)-||w_{2}||_{\infty}\right|<\left|b-||w_{2}||_{\infty}\right|
and
\left|\tilde{w_{2}}(t_{2},r)-m\right|<\left|a-m\right|
.

In both cases, we have
a\leq\tilde{w_{2}}(x,r)\leq b
on a closed interval of radius
r+c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a)
and
\tilde{w_{2}}(x,r)<a
elsewhere.
Taking composition with
g
yields

g(\tilde{w_{2}}(x-c^{*},r))=\begin{cases}0,&x<-2r-c^{*}-\phi_{2}(r,a),\\
1,&-2r-c^{*}-\phi_{2}(r,a)\leq x\leq c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a),\\
0,&x>c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a).\end{cases}

Hence,

\displaystyle Q^{2}[\tilde{w_{1}}(\,\cdot\,,r)](x)

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x-c^{*}-\phi_{2}(r,a))-w_{1}(x+2r+c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a)).

Shifting the expression left by
c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a)
units yields

\displaystyle Q^{2}[\tilde{w_{1}}(\,\cdot\,,r)](x+c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a))

\displaystyle=\tilde{w_{1}}(x,r+c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a)).

(2.17)

We are now prepared to prove the theorem using an inductive argument.
Let
A
be the level set defined in the statement of the theorem.
Without loss of generality, we write
A=[-r_{0},r_{0}]
, for some
r_{0}>0
.
We have

g(u_{0}(x))=\begin{cases}1,&-r_{0}\leq x\leq r_{0},\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

for all
x
. Applying the convolution operator yields

\displaystyle u_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x-r_{0})-w_{1}(x+r_{0})

\displaystyle=\tilde{w_{1}}(x-r_{0},r_{0}).

Let
(r_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real numbers satisfying

r_{n+1}=r_{n}+c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r_{n},a).

(2.18)

If
r_{0}
is sufficiently large, we can show that
r_{n}
is strictly increasing and unbounded.
This is due to our assumption that
c^{*}>0
.
Since
\phi_{2}(r,a)\to c^{*}
, it suffices to assume
|\phi_{2}(r,a)-c^{*}|<c^{*}
, i.e.
r_{0}
is sufficiently large so that
\phi_{2}(r,a)>0
for all
r>r_{0}
.
Plugging this into the recurrence (
2.18
) yields
r_{n+1}-r_{n}=c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r,a)>c^{*}>0
.

This argument shows that, assuming
r_{0}
is sufficiently large, equation (
2.17
) may be applied recursively.
From the principle of induction, it follows that
u_{2n+1}(x)=\tilde{w_{1}}(x-r_{n},r_{n})
for all
n\geq 0
.
Knowing that
(r_{n})
is unbounded allows us to use equation (
2.18
) to compute the asymptotic spreading speed as the following limit.

\lim_{n\to\infty}(r_{n+1}-r_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(c^{*}+\phi_{2}(r_{n},a))=c^{*}+\lim_{r\to\infty}\phi_{2}(r,a)=2c^{*}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Examples

In this section, we construct the periodic traveling wave solution for uniform and Laplace kernels and derive formulas for the mean spreading speed
c^{*}
in terms of the model parameters.

3.1
Laplace kernel

We applied our main results to the Laplace dispersal kernel, defined

k(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}.

(3.1)

k(x)
is symmetric and has connected support, satisfying (H
1
), (H
2
), and (H
3
).
We will now prove that
k(x)
satisfies (H
5
).
Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
with
A>B
, let
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and define
f(x)=k(x-A)-\lambda k(x-B)
.
For
r>0
sufficiently large, we have

f(x-r)-f(-x-r)=k(x-r-A)-\lambda k(x-r-B)-k(x+r+A)+\lambda k(x+r+B)

=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}e^{x}\left(-e^{r+A}+me^{r+B}-me^{-r-B}+e^{-r-A}\right),&x<-r-A,\\
\frac{1}{2}e^{-x-r-A}+\frac{1}{2}e^{x}\left(e^{-r-A}-me^{-r-B}+me^{r+B}\right),&-r-A\leq x\leq-r-B,\\
\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}\left(-e^{-r-A}+me^{-r-B}\right)+\frac{1}{2}e^{x}\left(-me^{-r-B}+e^{-r-A}\right),&-r-B<x<r+B,\\
\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}\left(-e^{-r-A}+me^{-r-B}-me^{r+B}\right)+\frac{1}{2}e^{x-r-A},&r+B\leq x\leq r+A\\
e^{-x}\left(-e^{-r-A}+me^{-r-B}-me^{r+B}+e^{r+A}\right),&x>r+A.\end{cases}

f
is monotone on
(-\infty,-r-A)\cup(r+A,\infty)
with
f(\pm\infty)=0
; thus it cannot have any sign changes there.
On each of the intervals
[-r-A,-r-B]
,
(-r-B,r+B)
, and
[r+B,r+A]
,
f
has the form
Ce^{x}+De^{-x}
.
Depending on the sign of
C
and
D
,
f
can have either one or zero sign changes on each interval.
Summing over each interval, it follows that
f
has at most
3
sign changes.
This proves (H
5
); the proof of (H
4
) follows a similar argument.

Assuming
||w_{2}||_{\infty}<b
, the periodic traveling wave profiles are given by

w_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{1}{2}e^{x},&\text{if }x\leq 0,\\
\frac{1}{2}e^{-x},&\text{if }x>0,\end{cases}

(3.2)

and

w_{2}(x-c^{*})=\begin{cases}m+\left(\frac{1}{2}(1-m)e^{-\Phi_{1}(b)}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\Phi_{1}(a)}\right)e^{x},&x<\Phi_{1}(b),\\
1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\Phi_{1}(a)}e^{x}-\frac{1}{2}(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)}e^{-x},&\Phi_{1}(b)\leq x\leq\Phi_{1}(a),\\
\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{\Phi_{1}(a)}-\frac{1}{2}(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)}\right)e^{-x},&\Phi_{1}(a)<x,\end{cases}

(3.3)

with

c^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\log\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2a}\left(e^{\Phi_{1}(a)}-(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)}\right),&a\leq\ell_{a},\\
e^{\Phi_{1}(a)}\left(1-a+\sqrt{(1-a)^{2}-(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)-\Phi_{1}(a)}}\right),&\ell_{a}<a<\ell_{b},\\
2\,(m-a)\left[e^{-\Phi_{1}(a)}-(1-m)e^{-\Phi_{1}(b)}\right]^{-1},&a\geq\ell_{b}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

\ell_{a}
and
\ell_{b}
are constants given by

\ell_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)-\Phi_{1}(a)}\right),\quad\ell_{b}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+m-e^{\Phi_{1}(b)-\Phi_{1}(a)}\right).

(3.5)

The formulas for
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are given by

\Phi_{1}(\ell)=\begin{cases}-\log(2\ell),&\ell\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\log(2-2\ell),&\ell>\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

(3.6)

and

\Phi_{2}(\ell)=-c^{*}+\log\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\ell}\left(e^{\Phi_{1}(a)}-(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)}\right),&\ell\leq w_{2}(\Phi_{1}(a)),\\
e^{\Phi_{1}(a)}\left(1-\ell+\sqrt{(1-\ell)^{2}-(1-m)e^{\Phi_{1}(b)-\Phi_{1}(a)}}\right),&w_{2}(\Phi_{1}(a))<\ell<w_{2}(\Phi_{1}(b)),\\
2\,(m-\ell)\left[e^{-\Phi_{1}(a)}-(1-m)e^{-\Phi_{1}(b)}\right]^{-1},&\ell\geq w_{2}(\Phi_{1}(b)).\end{cases}

(3.7)

The critical spreading speed
c^{*}
for the Laplace kernel thus falls into one of three cases:

Case 1.

a\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
b\leq\frac{1}{2}
:

c^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\log\begin{cases}\frac{b-a(1-m)}{4a^{2}b},&a<\frac{b-a(1-m)}{2b},\\
\frac{1-a+\sqrt{b(1-a)^{2}-a(1-m)}}{2a\sqrt{b}},&\frac{b-a(1-m)}{2b}\leq a\leq\frac{b(1+m)-a}{2b},\\
\frac{a-m}{b(1-m)-a},&a>\frac{b(1+m)-a}{2b}.\end{cases}

Case 2.

a\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
:

c^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\log\begin{cases}\frac{1-4a(1-m)(1-b)}{4a^{2}},&a<\frac{1-4a(1-m)(1-b)}{2},\\
\frac{1-a+\sqrt{(1-a)^{2}-4a(1-m)(1-b)}}{2a},&\frac{1-4a(1-m)(1-b)}{2}\leq a\leq\frac{1+m-4a(1-b)}{2},\\
\frac{4(a-m)(1-b)}{1-m-4a(1-b)},&a>\frac{1+m-4a(1-b)}{2}.\end{cases}

Case 3.

a>\frac{1}{2}
,
b>\frac{1}{2}
:

c^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\log\begin{cases}\frac{1-a-(1-m)(1-b)}{a},&a<\frac{1-a-(1-m)(1-b)}{2(1-a)},\\
2\,(1-a)\left(1-a+\sqrt{\frac{(1-a)^{3}-(1-m)(1-b)}{1-a}}\right),&\frac{1-a-(1-m)(1-b)}{2(1-a)}\leq a\leq\frac{b-1+m(1-a)}{2(1-a)},\\
\frac{4(m-a)(1-a)(1-b)}{1-b+m(1-a)},&a>\frac{b-1+m(1-a)}{2(1-a)}.\end{cases}

3.2
Uniform kernel

The uniform dispersal kernel is given by

k(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\text{if }|x|\leq 1,\\
0,&\text{if }|x|>1.\end{cases}

(3.8)

Like the previous example,
k(x)
is symmetric around zero.
We can verify (H
4
) by letting
A>B
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
and defining
f(x)=k(x-A)-\lambda k(x-B)
.
There are two cases:

Case 1.

If
|A-B|<2
, then

f(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\leq B-1,\\
-\frac{\lambda}{2},&B-1<x\leq A-1,\\
\frac{1-\lambda}{2},&A-1<x<B+1,\\
\frac{1}{2},&B+1\leq x<A+1\\
0,&x\geq A+1.\end{cases}

Case 2.

If
|A-B|\geq 2
, then

f(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\leq B-1,\\
-\frac{\lambda}{2},&B-1<x<B+1,\\
0,&B+1\leq x\leq A-1,\\
\frac{1}{2},&A-1<x<A+1\\
0,&x\geq A+1.\end{cases}

In both cases, the number of sign changes is exactly 1.
To extend this argument to (H
5
), observe that since
k(x)
has compact support, we have

\displaystyle f(x-r)-f(r-x)

\displaystyle=k(x-r-A)-\lambda k(x-r-B)-k(x+r+A)+\lambda k(x+r+B)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-f(r-x),&x<-r+1,\\
0,&-r+1\leq x\leq r-1,\\
f(x-r),&x>r-1,\end{cases}

for sufficiently large
r
.

The alternating wave profiles are given by

\displaystyle w_{1}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x\in(-\infty,-1),\\
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}x,&x\in[-1,1],\\
0,&x\in(1,\infty),\end{cases}

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle w_{2}(x+c^{*})=\begin{cases}m,&x\in(-\infty,-2b),\\
\frac{1-m}{2}x+m+b-mb,&x\in[-2b,-2a),\\
-\frac{m}{2}x+m+b-mb-a,&x\in[-2a,2-2b),\\
-\frac{1}{2}x-a+1,&x\in[2-2b,2-2a],\\
0,&x\in(2+2a,\infty),\end{cases}

(3.10)

with

c^{*}=\begin{cases}1-2a,&\text{if }a\leq b/2,\\
1-b+\frac{b-2a}{m},&\text{if }a>b/2.\end{cases}

(3.11)

w_{2}
has a global maximum at
x=-2a
so that
||w_{2}||_{\infty}=m+(b-a)(1-m)
.
Thus, a sufficient condition for the existence of the periodic traveling wave is
w_{2}(-2a)=m+(b-a)(1-m)<b
.

The intermediate wave speeds are given by

\displaystyle c_{1}^{*}(\ell)

\displaystyle=1-2\ell

(3.12)

and

\displaystyle c_{2}^{*}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-4a+2\ell,&a\leq b/2,\\
1-2b+\frac{2b-4a}{m}+2\ell,&a>b/2,\end{cases}

(3.13)

for
0<\ell<m+(b-a)(1-m)
.
By evaluating the intermediate wave speeds at
\ell=a
, we obtain
c_{1}^{*}(a)=c_{2}^{*}(a)
if
a\leq b/2
, and
|c_{1}^{*}(a)-c_{2}^{*}(a)|=2(2a-b)(\frac{1-m}{m})>0
if
a>b/2
.
So for
a>b/2
, the traveling wave is periodic with two different intermediate wave speeds.
Furthermore, the difference between these two intermediate speeds is increasing with respect to
a
, the Allee threshold, and decreasing with respect to
b
, the overcompensation threshold.

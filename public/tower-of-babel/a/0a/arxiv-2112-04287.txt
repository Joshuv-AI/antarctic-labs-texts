Abstract.

In this article, we obtain higher Hölder regularity results for weak solutions to nonlocal problems driven by the fractional double phase operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x):=

\displaystyle 2\;{\rm P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps_{1}}}dy

\displaystyle+2\;{\rm P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dy,

where
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
0<s_{2},s_{1}<1
and the modulating coefficient
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is a non-negative bounded function. Specifically, we prove higher space-time Hölder continuity result for weak solutions of time depending nonlocal double phase problems for a particular subclass of the modulating coefficients. Using suitable approximation arguments, we further establish higher (global) Hölder continuity results for weak solutions to the stationary problems involving the operator
\mathcal{L}
with modulating coefficients that are locally continuous.

1.
Introduction

In this article, our aim is to obtain space-time Hölder continuity results for the local weak solutions to the following nonlocal parabolic problem:

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u=f\;\;\text{ in }\Omega\times I,

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) is a bounded domain,
I:=(t_{0},t_{1}]\subset\mathbb{R}
,
f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\times I)
and the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x):=

\displaystyle 2\;{\rm P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps_{1}}}dy

\displaystyle+2\;{\rm P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dy,

with
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
0<s_{2},s_{1}<1
and
a(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a non-negative symmetric function.

The operator
\mathcal{L}
is known as the fractional double phase operator (as introduced in
[
17
]
), which is a nonlocal analogue of the double phase operator
\mathcal{D}u:={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)
with
1<p<q
and
0\leq a(\cdot)
bounded. The zero set of the modulating coefficient
a(\cdot)
dictates the ellipticity of operator
\mathcal{D}
, that is, when
a(x)>0
, the problem has the
q
-Laplacian behaviour while on the zero set it has the
p
-Laplacian behaviour.
The corresponding energy functional falls into the category of the so-called functionals with nonstandard growth conditions of
(p,q)
-type, according to Marcellini’s terminology
[
32
]
. These kinds of functionals involve integrals of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}F(x,\nabla u(x))~dx,

where the energy density
F
, satisfies the unbalanced growth conditions

c_{1}|\xi|^{p}\leq|F(x,\xi)|\leq c_{2}(|\xi|^{q}+1),\quad 1\leq p\leq q.

The physical significance of these models lies in the field of nonlinear elasticity, specifically in homogenization theory. A particular form of the functional
\mathcal{E}
is the double phase energy functional given by

u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q})dx,\quad 0\leq a(x)\leq M,\ 1<p<q.

This functional was first introduced by Zhikov in
[
41
]
, to model the Lavrentiev phenomenon on strongly anisotropic materials.

The regularity for weak solutions to problems involving the operator
\mathcal{D}
or the minimizers of corresponding functionals has been extensively studied in recent years, see
[
3
,
7
,
13
,
16
,
25
,
32
]
and the survey article
[
34
]
. Particularly, it is known that for
q\leq p+\beta
(where
a(\cdot)\in C^{0,\beta}
), the gradient of the bounded weak solutions (or the minimizers) are locally Hölder continuous.
Concerning the regularity results for time dependent problems involving nonstandard growth operators, we refer to
[
15
,
26
]
. For the existence results of problems involving these kinds of operators, we mention
[
2
,
4
,
22
,
33
,
39
]
.

In the stationary case and when
a\equiv 0
, the problem (

P
t

) is driven by the fractional
p
-Laplacian (
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}
), which is a nonlinear analogue of the well known fractional Laplacian. The study of problems involving fractional Sobolev spaces and corresponding nonlocal operators has gained ample attention due to their wide applications in the real world problems, such as game theory, image processing, anomalous diffusion, finance, conservation laws, phase transition, obstacle problems and material science, see
[
19
]
and references therein. The regularity results for weak solutions to problems involving these kinds of nonlocal operators (or minimizers of corresponding energy functional) are well known, see for instance
[
5
,
10
,
14
,
18
,
28
,
35
]
and their references. Specifically, the weak solution is known to be
C^{0,s}
-regular up to the boundary, which is optimal.

As far as nonlocal problems involving the fractional double phase operator are concerned, the corresponding regularity theory is a relatively new topic and has not been explored much barring a few works in the stationary case. In this direction, we mention
[
17
]
, where De Filippis and Palatucci obtained an interior Hölder continuity result for viscosity solutions to the problem
\mathcal{L}u=f\in L^{\infty}
. For the case of weak solutions, in
[
21
]
, Fang and Zhang proved a local Hölder continuity result (with unspecified Hölder exponent) of bounded weak solutions by using the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser type method, for
qs_{2}\leq ps_{1}
and a non-negative bounded modulating coefficient. Here, additionally authors established that bounded weak solutions are viscosity solutions. Subsequently, by obtaining a suitable Logarithmic lemma and applying the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration technique, Byun et al. in
[
8
]
obtained a similar local Hölder regularity result for the case
ps_{1}<qs_{2}
when the modulating coefficient is assumed to be in the space
C^{0,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
qs_{2}-ps_{1}<\beta<1
. We also quote
[
37
]
for self improving properties of the bounded weak solutions to problems involving the operator
\mathcal{L}
, for the case
qs_{2}\leq ps_{1}
. For the constant modulating coefficient case, we mention the regularity results for weak solutions obtained in
[
23
,
24
,
27
]
. Particularly, in
[
23
]
, the almost optimal Hölder continuity result, up to the boundary, is proved in the super-quadratic case, while in
[
24
]
, the global Hölder continuity result is established for all
p,q>1
. See also the recent work of Chaker et al.
[
11
]
for the regularity results of nonlocal problems with nonstandard growth operators. However, our problem does not fall into the category of
[
11
]
due to assumptions on the nonlinear growth and fractional differentiability orders.

The regularity theory for time dependent nonlocal problems has been developing significantly since the last decade. For instance,
[
9
,
29
,
12
]
deals with the Hölder regularity of weak solutions by following different methods (weak Harnack inequality or De Giorgi’s type approach) for the linear or non-degenerate nonlocal operator case. On the other hand, the development concerning the nonlinear nonlocal operators case is recent. In
[
40
]
, Stömqvist obtained a local boundedness result for parabolic problems for a large class of nonlocal problems whose prototype includes the fractional
p
-Laplacian for
p>2
. For similar problems and involving a non-zero reaction term, as a parabolic counterpart for
[
18
]
, Ding et al. in
[
20
]
proved the local boundedness property for all
p\geq 2N/(N+2s)
. In continuation to
[
5
]
, Brasco et al.
[
6
]
obtained a higher interior space-time Hölder continuity result with precise exponents for the local weak solutions to fractional
p
-Laplacian parabolic problem in the super-quadratic case. Recently, in
[
1
,
31
]
, authors have obtained the local Hölder regularity results for weak solution to fractional
p
-Laplacian type problems, for all
p>1
, by means of suitable De Giorgi type iteration method and intrinsic scaling arguments. Concerning the nonstandard growth parabolic problems, in
[
36
]
, authors have obtained a local boundedness result for variational solutions to a class of nonlocal parabolic double phase equations.

After these works the regularity results for parabolic problems driven by the fractional double phase operators were completely open. Addressing this issue, we establish space-time Hölder continuity result with specific exponents for the local weak solutions to problem (

P
t

) when the modulating coefficient is locally translation invariant in
\Omega\times\Omega
.
To achieve this aim, we first obtain an iterative scheme involving the discrete differential of the solution with respect to the space variable using Moser’s iteration technique much in the spirit of
[
6
]
. Here, we highlight that handling the terms involving the exponent
q
is more tactical and tricky as compared to the constant modulating coefficient case (see
[
23
]
).
Subsequently, we obtain higher time regularity for weak solutions by using the higher space regularity estimates. In order to motivate our main regularity results, we sketch the proof of the existence of the weak solution to an initial value parabolic problem (valid for all
0<s_{1},s_{2}<1
) in the Appendix. The novelty in this regard lies in introducing a new reflexive Banach space and proving fundamental properties such coercivity and continuity for the operator involved. Indeed, because of the varying ellipticity of the operator
\mathcal{L}
, it is not expected that the solution should lie on the fractional space
W^{s_{1},p}\cap W^{s_{2},q}
. Additionally, due to the absence of a suitable weighted fractional Poincaré type inequality with respect to the exponent
q
, we take into account appropriate fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces and corresponding time dependent Banach space to define the notion of (local) weak solutions.

Regarding the stationary case, the higher Hölder regularity for solutions to fractional double phase problems was not explored before. To this aim, we establish Hölder continuity results with explicit Hölder exponent for weak solutions to problems involving a more general source term and a modulating coefficient that is continuous along the diagonal in
\Omega\times\Omega
. For this, we perform approximation arguments based on the methods presented in
[
5
,
10
]
. However, the lack of scaling property of the leading operators makes it difficult to pass the higher Hölder continuity results of problems with locally translation invariant modulating coefficients and homogeneous right hand side to our case. To overcome this, we choose the radius of the ball, where Hölder continuity is obtained, appropriately depending a priori on the data of the problem.
We emphasize that such results to the best of our knowledge were not known even for non-homogeneous operators where the modulating coefficients are bounded away from zero.
Finally, we obtain a higher global Hölder continuity result for the weak solutions to the stationary problem when the right hand side is bounded, which is new to the current literature on the subject.

Turning to the layout of the rest of the paper, in Section 2, we mention the functional framework and our main results. Section 3 contains some technical results which will be needed in the subsequent sections. In Section 4, we establish higher space-time Hölder continuity result for locally translation invariant modulating coefficients. In Section 5, we obtain higher Hölder continuity results in the stationary case for more general modulating coefficients and source terms. Finally, in the appendix, we sketch the proof of the existence of weak solutions to the parabolic problem.

\displaystyle\ +C\int_{-R^{s_{1}p}}^{0}\int_{B_{R}}|f(x,t)|\tilde{u}(x,t)\psi^{q}(x)dxdt

\displaystyle\ +C\int_{-R^{s_{1}p}}^{0}\int_{B_{R}}\tilde{u}^{2}(x,t)\psi^{q}(x)|\partial_{t}\eta(t)|dxdt.

\displaystyle\|a-\tilde{a}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\delta_{1}.

(5.21)

We set

\displaystyle u_{z}(x):=\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}}u(r_{z}x+z)\quad\mbox{for }x\in B_{2}.

By the scaling property of the fractional operators, it is easy to observe that
u_{z}
solves

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}u_{z}+(-\Delta)_{q,a_{1}}^{s_{2}}u_{z}=f_{z}\quad\mbox{in }B_{2},

where
a_{1}(x,y)=\mathcal{M}^{q-p}r_{z}^{s_{1}p-s_{2}q}a(r_{z}x+z,r_{z}y+z)
and
f_{z}(x):=\frac{r_{z}^{ps_{1}}}{\mathcal{M}^{p-1}}f(r_{z}x+z)
.
Moreover, since
z\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})
, we see that

\displaystyle|y-z|\geq|y-x_{0}|\Big(1-\frac{|z-x_{0}|}{|y-x_{0}|}\Big)\geq\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2}\quad\mbox{for all }y\in B_{R_{0}/2}(x_{0})^{c}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle T_{s\ell}^{\ell}(u;z,r_{z})

\displaystyle\leq r_{z}^{\ell s}\left[2^{N+\ell s}\int_{B_{\frac{R_{0}}{2}}(x_{0})^{c}}\frac{|u(y)|^{\ell-1}\,dy}{|y-x_{0}|^{N+\ell s}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{R_{0}}{2}}(x_{0}))}^{\ell-1}\int_{B_{r_{z}}(z)^{c}}\frac{dy}{|y-z|^{N+\ell s}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N+\ell s}T_{s\ell}^{\ell}(u;x_{0},R_{0}/2)+\Big(\frac{N|B_{1}|}{\ell s}\Big)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}/2}(x_{0}))}^{\ell-1}.

(5.22)

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{M}
and using (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{(\ell,s)}T^{\ell}_{\ell s}(u_{z};0,1)^{1/(\ell-1)}\leq 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f_{z}\|_{L^{\Bbbk}(B_{1})}\leq\delta_{1}.

Furthermore, for
\tilde{a}_{1}(x,y):=\mathcal{M}^{q-p}r_{z}^{s_{1}p-s_{2}q}\tilde{a}(r_{z}x+z,r_{z}y+z)
, there holds

\displaystyle\|a_{1}-\tilde{a}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}=r_{z}^{s_{1}p-s_{2}q}\mathcal{M}^{q-p}\|a-\tilde{a}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\delta_{1},

where we have used (
5.21
) and (
5.19
). Therefore, from Proposition
5.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle[u_{z}]_{C^{0,{\Theta_{1}}-\epsilon}({B_{1/8}})}\leq C_{1}(N,p,q,s_{1},s_{2},\Bbbk,\|f\|_{L^{\Bbbk}},\|a\|_{\infty},\epsilon).

By scaling back to
u
and since
z\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})
was arbitrary, by a standard covering argument, we can complete the proof of the theorem.

Remark 5
.

It is clear from the above proof that for
\Bbbk=\infty
, Theorem

holds for the case
ps_{1}=qs_{2}
, too. Indeed, in this case, we approximate only the modulating coefficient

in Lemma

by keeping

fixed. In the proof of Theorem

, we proceed with

instead of
\mathcal{M}(R_{0})
and take
{\delta_{1}\mathcal{M}(R)^{p-q}}
in place of
\delta_{1}
in (
5.20
).

5.2.
Boundary regularity

We define the distance function as
d(x):={\rm dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
. Then, we have the following estimate

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
and for
s_{1}<q^{\prime}s_{2}
, we assume that
a(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{0,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\beta>s_{2}
.
Let
u\in\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
be such that
|\mathcal{L}u|\leq K
, weakly in
\Omega
, for some
K>0
. Then, for all
0<\sigma<s_{1}
, there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}_{u}>0
(depending only on data of the problem and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)}
) such that

|u|\leq\mathcal{C}_{u}\;d^{\sigma}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega.

Proof.

We first obtain a lower bound on the quantity
(-\Delta)_{q,a}^{s_{2}}d^{\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in[s_{2},1)
. Indeed, proceeding as in
[
23
, Lemma 3.12]
, the case
\alpha>q^{\prime}s_{2}
is exactly the same as Case (i) of the proof presented there, that is,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{q,a}^{s_{2}}\ d^{\alpha}=g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho})\quad\mbox{weakly in }\Omega_{\rho},

(5.23)

where
\Omega_{\rho}:=\{x\in\Omega\ :\ d(x)<\rho\}
.
For the case
\alpha<q^{\prime}s_{2}
, a similar procedure to that of
[
23
, Lemma 3.12]
yields that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{q,\tilde{a}(\cdot)}^{s_{2}}\ d^{\alpha}\geq C_{1}d^{\alpha(q-1)-qs_{2}}\quad\mbox{weakly in }\Omega_{\rho},

(5.24)

where
\tilde{a}(x)=a(x,x)
and
C_{1}
is a positive constant. Furthermore, proceeding with the same notation as in
[
23
, Lemma 3.12]
,
the integrand
\tilde{I}_{2,\epsilon}(X)
is estimated as below:

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{2,\epsilon}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\epsilon}(X)^{c}\cap B_{2\rho}}a(\Phi(X),\Phi(X))\frac{[(X_{N})_{+}^{\alpha}-(Y_{N})_{+}^{\alpha}]^{q-1}}{|\nabla\Phi(X)(X-Y)|^{N+qs_{2}}}J_{\Phi}(X)dY

\displaystyle\,\,+\int_{B_{\epsilon}(X)^{c}\cap B_{2\rho}}\big[a(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))-a(\Phi(X),\Phi(X))\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\frac{[(X_{N})_{+}^{\alpha}-(Y_{N})_{+}^{\alpha}]^{q-1}}{|\nabla\Phi(X)(X-Y)|^{N+qs_{2}}}J_{\Phi}(X)dY

\displaystyle=:I_{5,\epsilon}(X)+I_{6,\epsilon}(X).

Using the
\beta
-Hölder continuity of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and the Lipschitz nature of
\Phi
and
\nabla\Phi
, we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{6,\epsilon}(X)|\leq C\int_{B_{\epsilon}(X)^{c}\cap B_{2\rho}}\frac{1}{|X-Y|^{N+qs_{2}-\alpha(q-1)-\beta}}dY=C_{\rho}<\infty,

where we have used the fact that
\beta>qs_{2}-\alpha(q-1)
. The term
I_{5,\epsilon}(X)
is handled as in the estimate to prove (
5.24
). This concludes that, for
\alpha<q^{\prime}s_{2}
,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{q,a}^{s_{2}}\ d^{\alpha}\geq C_{2}d^{\alpha(q-1)-qs_{2}}\quad\mbox{weakly in }\Omega_{\rho}.

(5.25)

Also, note that
d^{\alpha}\in W^{s_{1},p}(\Omega^{\prime})\cap W^{s_{2},q}(\Omega^{\prime})
, for some
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
and for all
s_{2}-1/q<\alpha<1
. Consequently, proceeding similar to
[
23
, Proposition 3.14]
and taking into account the local boundedness of
u
together with (
5.23
) and (
5.25
), we can complete the proof of the proposition.
∎

Finally, we have our global Hölder continuity result.

On the existence result

Here, we give the functional setup and a basic framework for the existence result for the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)_{p}^{s_{1}}u+(-\Delta)_{q,a}^{s_{2}}u&=f\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times I,\\

(
GNP
)

where

Definition A.1
.

We say that a function

⁢
G
N
P

) if the following hold:

for a.e.

\displaystyle-\int_{J}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{J}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{[u(x,t)-u(y,t)]^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}p}}(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t))dxdydt

Theorem A.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and suppose that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\|g(\cdot,t)-g_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0\quad\mbox{for some }g_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Then, there exists a unique solution to problem (

⁢
G
N
P

).

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
6
, Theorem A.3]
. Here, we highlight only the key steps, for instance, boundedness and coercivity of the operators involved in the proof. For a.e.
t\in I
, we define
\mathcal{A}_{t}:X_{{\rm g}(t)}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\to(\mathcal{W}(\Omega^{\prime}))^{*}
by

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}_{t}(v),\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,s)}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}a_{\ell}(x,y)\frac{[v(x)-v(y)]^{\ell-1}}{|x-y|^{N+s\ell}}(\phi(x)-\phi(y))dxdy

\displaystyle+2\sum_{(\ell,s)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}a_{\ell}(x,y)\frac{[v(x)-g(y,t)]^{\ell-1}}{|x-y|^{N+s\ell}}\phi(x)dxdy

\displaystyle=:\langle\mathcal{A}^{p}_{t}(v),\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{A}^{q}_{t}(v),\phi\rangle.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and recalling the definition of

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{A}^{q}_{t}(v),\phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}a(x,y)\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{q-1}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}q}}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+c(q)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}a(x,y)\frac{|v(x)|^{q-1}+|g(y,t)|^{q-1}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}q}}|\phi(x)|dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+c\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}T^{q}_{qs_{2},a}(g;z,r)^{p/(p-1)}

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\|v\|^{q-1}_{\mathcal{W}(\Omega^{\prime})}+T^{q}_{qs_{2},a}(g;z,r)^{p/(p-1)}\big)\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}(\Omega^{\prime})},

where

Abstract
. We define the Bernstein-Sato ideal associated to a tuple of ideals and we relate it to the jumping points of the corresponding mixed multiplier ideals.



1.
Introduction

Let
R
be either the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
over the complex numbers or the ring
\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
of convergent power series in the neighbourhood of the origin, or any other point.
The
multiplier ideals
of a element
f
or an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
R
are a family of nested ideals that play a prominent role in birational geometry (see Lazarsfeld’s book
[
Laz04
]
). Associated to these ideals we have a set of invariants, the
jumping numbers
, that are intimately related to other invariants of singularities. For instance, Ein, Lazarsfeld, Smith and Varolin
[
ELSV04
]
and independently Budur and Saito
[
BS05
]
, proved that the negatives of the jumping numbers of
f
in the interval
(0,1)
are roots of the
Bernstein-Sato polynomial
of
f
.
Budur, Mustaţă and Saito
[
BMS06
]
extended the classical theory of Bernstein-Sato polynomials to the case of ideals and also proved that the jumping numbers of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in the interval
(0,1)
are roots of the
Bernstein-Sato polynomial
of
\mathfrak{a}
.

There is a natural extension of the theory of multiplier ideals to the context of tuples of germs
F:=f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}
or tuples of ideals
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
in
R
. The main differences that we encounter in this setting is that, whereas the multiplier ideals come with the set of associated jumping numbers, the mixed multiplier ideals come with a set of
jumping walls
. On the other side of the story we have the notion of
Bernstein-Sato ideal
associated to a tuple of germs
F
given by Sabbah
[
Sab87
]
. In the case of a tuple of plane curves, Cassou-Noguès and Libgober
[
CNL11
]
related the Bernstein-Sato ideal with the so-called
faces of quasi-adjunction
which is a set of invariants equivalent to the jumping walls.

The aim of this short note is to fill out the theory introducing the notion of Bernstein-Sato ideal associated to a tuple ideals
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
. To such purpose we are going to follow the approach given by Mustaţă
[
Mus19
]
where he relates the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of a single ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\big)
to the reduced Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
g=f_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+f_{r}y_{r}
, where the
y_{j}
’s are new variables.
Finally, we show in Theorem
3.11
that the negative of the jumping points of the mixed multiplier ideals of the tuple
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
that are in the open ball of radius one centered at the origin belong to the zero locus of the Bernstein-Sato ideal of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
.

The theory of Bernstein-Sato polynomials and its relations with other invariants such as the multiplier ideals is vast and rich. In this note we tried to introduce only the essential concepts that we needed so we recommend those who are not that familiar with these topics to take a look at the
surveys of Budur
[
Bud15
]
, Granger
[
Gra10
]
or Jeffries, Núñez-Betancourt and the author
[
ÀJNB21
]
for further insight.

We would like to thank Guillem Blanco, Jack Jeffries and Luis Núñez-Betancourt for many helpful conversations regarding this work.

2.
Bernstein-Sato ideal of a tuple of ideals

Let
R
be either
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
or
\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and denote
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
the (homogeneous) maximal ideal.
Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
R
. For each ideal described by a set of generators
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\big(f_{i,1},\dots,f_{i,r_{i}}\big)

we consider
g_{i}=f_{i,1}y_{i,1}+\cdots+f_{i,r_{i}}y_{i,r_{i}}
where the
y_{i,j}
’s are new variables. In particular we get a tuple
G:=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
in the ring
A
that will be either
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1,1},\dots,y_{\ell,r_{\ell}}]
or
\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1,1},\dots,y_{\ell,r_{\ell}}\}
. In the sequel,
d:=n+r_{1}+\cdots+r_{\ell}
will denote the number of variables in
A
.

Associated to
R
or
A
we have the corresponding ring of differential operators

D_{R}=R\langle\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}\rangle\hskip 5.69054pt,\hskip 8.53581ptD_{A}=A\langle\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n},\partial_{1,1},\dots,\partial_{\ell,r_{\ell}}\rangle

where
\partial_{i}
(resp.
\partial_{i,j}
) is the partial derivative with respect to
x_{i}
(resp.
y_{i,j}
). That is,
D_{R}
(resp.
D_{A}
) is the

\mathbb{C}
-subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(R)
(resp.
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(A)
) generated by the ring and the partial derivatives.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Bernstein-Sato ideal
of the tuple
G
is the ideal
B_{G}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}]
generated by all the polynomials
b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})
satisfying the
Bernstein-Sato functional equation

\delta(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})g_{1}^{s_{1}+1}\cdots g_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}+1}=b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})g_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots g_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}}

where
\delta(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\in D_{A}[s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}]
and
b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\in\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}]
.

Sabbah
[
Sab87
]
proved that
B_{G}\neq 0
in the convergent power series case. The proof of
B_{G}\neq 0
in the polynomial ring case is completely analogous to the classical case of a single element. Indeed, it is enough to consider the local case.

Remark 2.2
.

Briançon and Maisonobe showed in
[
BM02
]
that

B_{G}^{\mathbb{C}[x]}=\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{C}^{d}}B_{G}^{\mathbb{C}\{x-p\}}

where
B_{G}^{\mathbb{C}[x]}
denotes the Bernstein-Sato ideal of a tuple
G
over the polynomial ring and
B_{G}^{\mathbb{C}\{x-p\}}
is the Bernstein-Sato ideal of
G
in the convergent power series around a point
p\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Now, since the
g_{i}
are pairwise without common factors, we have

B_{G}\subseteq\Big((s_{1}+1)\cdots(s_{\ell}+1)\Big).

(see
[
May97
,
BM99
]
for details).

Definition 2.3
.

The
reduced Bernstein-Sato ideal
of the tuple
G
is the ideal
\widetilde{B}_{G}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}]
generated by the polynomials

\frac{b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})}{(s_{1}+1)\cdots(s_{\ell}+1)},

with
b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\in B_{G}
.

Following the approach given by Mustaţă
[
Mus19
]
for the case of a single ideal, we consider the following:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X,O}
and let
G:=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
be its associated tuple of hypersurfaces.
We define the
Bernstein-Sato ideal
of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
as

B_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}}:=\widetilde{B}_{G}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}]

Our next result shows that
B_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}}
does not depend on the generators of the ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
and thus it is an invariant of the tuple
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals and, for each ideal, consider two different sets of generators

\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\big(f_{i,1},\dots,f_{i,r_{i}}\big)
and
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\big(f^{\prime}_{i,1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{i,s_{i}}\big)
. Consider the tuple
G=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
with

g_{i}=f_{i,1}y_{i,1}+\cdots+f_{i,r_{i}}y_{i,r_{i}}
and the tuple
G^{\prime}=g^{\prime}_{1},\dots,g^{\prime}_{\ell}

with
g^{\prime}_{i}=f^{\prime}_{i,1}y^{\prime}_{i,1}+\cdots+f^{\prime}_{i,s_{i}}y^{\prime}_{i,s_{i}}
. Then
\widetilde{B}_{G}=\widetilde{B}_{G^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that, for each ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
, the set of generators
f^{\prime}_{i,1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{i,s_{i}}
is just
f_{i,1},\dots,f_{i,r_{i}},h_{i}
for a given
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}
.
Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r_{i}}
such that
h_{i}=z_{1}f_{i,1}+\cdots+z_{r_{i}}f_{i,r_{i}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle g_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=f_{i,1}y^{\prime}_{i,1}+\cdots+f_{i,r_{i}}y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}}+h_{i}y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}+1}

\displaystyle=f_{i,1}y^{\prime}_{i,1}+\cdots+f_{i,r_{i}}y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}}+(z_{1}f_{i,1}+\cdots+z_{r_{i}}f_{i,r_{i}})y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}+1}

\displaystyle=f_{1}(y^{\prime}_{i,1}+z_{1}y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}+1})+\cdots+f_{\ell}(y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}}+z_{r_{i}}y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}+1}).

After a change of variables
y_{i,j}\mapsto y^{\prime}_{i,j}+z_{j}y^{\prime}_{i,r_{i}+1}
, this polynomial becomes
g_{i}
.
Since Bernstein-Sato ideals do not change by change of variables, we conclude that
B_{G}=B_{G^{\prime}}
and the result follows.
∎

3.
Mixed multiplier ideals

Let
\pi:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow X
be a common log-resolution of a tuple of ideals
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
in
R
.
Namely,
\pi
is a birational morphism such that

\cdot

X^{\prime}
is smooth,

\cdot

\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\left(-F_{i}\right)
for some effective Cartier divisor
F_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,\ell
,

\cdot

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}F_{i}+E
is a divisor with simple normal crossings where
E=Exc\left(\pi\right)
is the exceptional locus.

The divisors
F_{i}=\sum_{j}e_{i,j}E_{j}
are integral
divisors in
X^{\prime}
which can be decomposed into their
exceptional
and
affine
part according to the support, i.e.

F_{i}=F_{i}^{\rm exc}+F_{i}^{\rm aff}
where

F_{i}^{\rm exc}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}e_{i,j}E_{j}\hskip 14.22636pt{\rm and}\hskip 14.22636ptF_{i}^{\rm aff}=\sum_{j=s+1}^{t}e_{i,j}E_{j}.

Whenever
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, the divisor
F_{i}
is just supported on the exceptional
locus. i.e.
F_{i}=F_{i}^{\rm exc}
. We will also consider the
relative canonical divisor

K_{\pi}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{j}E_{j}

which is a divisor in
X^{\prime}
supported on the
exceptional locus
E
defined by the Jacobian determinant of the morphism
\pi
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
mixed multiplier ideal
associated to a tuple
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
of ideals in
R
and a point

\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\ell}
is defined
as

\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\mathbf{\lambda}}):=\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}^{\lambda_{\ell}})=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\left(\left\lceil K_{\pi}-\lambda_{1}F_{1}-\cdots-\lambda_{\ell}F_{\ell}\right\rceil\right)

In the classical case of a single ideal we have the notion of
jumping numbers
associated to the sequence of multiplier ideals.
The corresponding notion in the context of mixed multiplier ideals is more involved.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
R
. Then, for each
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\ell}
, we
define:

\cdot

The
region
of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
:
\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right)=\left\{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\ell}\hskip 5.69054pt\left|\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}}})\supseteq\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\right\}\,\right.
.

\cdot

The
constancy region
of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
:
\mathcal{C}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right)=\left\{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\ell}\hskip 5.69054pt\left|\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}}})=\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\right\}\,\right.
.

The boundaries of these regions is where we have a strict inclusion of ideals. Therefore we may define:

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
R
. The
jumping wall
associated to
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{\geqslant 0}
is the boundary of the region

\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
.

In particular, we will be interested in the points of these jumping walls. In the sequel,
B_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
stands for the open ball of radius
\varepsilon
centered at
a point
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
R
. We say that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{\geqslant 0}
is a

jumping point
of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
if

\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}}})\varsupsetneq\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})

for all
\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}}\in\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\ell}\}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

From the definition of mixed multiplier ideals we have that the
jumping points
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{\geqslant 0}
must lie on
hyperplanes of the form
H_{j}:\hskip 2.84526pte_{1,j}z_{1}+\cdots+e_{\ell,j}z_{\ell}=k_{j}+\nu_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,s
and
\nu_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

For
\lambda\in(0,1)
we have
\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda})=\mathcal{J}(g_{\alpha}^{\lambda})
where
\mathfrak{a}=\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\big)
is a single ideal in
R
and
g_{\alpha}=\alpha_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}f_{r}\in R
with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
is a general element (see
[
Laz04
, Prop. 9.2.28]
). As a consequence of a more general result of Mustaţă and Popa given in
[
MP20
, Theorem 2.5]
we also have a relation between
\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda})
and the multiplier ideal of the associated hypersurface
g=f_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+f_{r}y_{r}
in
A
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{J}=\big(Q_{1}(y),\dots,Q_{s}(y)\big)
be an ideal in
A
. Then,
{\rm Coeff}(\mathcal{J})\subseteq R
is the ideal
generated by

\{Q_{1}(\alpha),\dots,Q_{s}(\alpha)\hskip 5.69054pt|\hskip 5.69054pt\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\}

The result of Mustaţă and Popa in the form that we need is the following

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\big)
be an ideal in
R
and let
g=f_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+f_{r}y_{r}
be the associated hypersurface in
A
. Then, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)
we have

\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda})={\rm Coeff}\big(\mathcal{J}(g^{\lambda})\big)

In particular, the set of jumping numbers in the interval
(0,1)
of
\mathfrak{a}
and
g
coincide.

The mixed multiplier ideals version of this result follows immediately from the following observation.

Remark 3.7
.

Consider a ray through the origin
L:(0,\dots,0)+\mu(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
where the
\alpha_{i}
’s are positive integers.

Then, the jumping points of a tuple
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
lying on
L
are the jumping numbers of the ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}^{\alpha_{\ell}}

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
R
,
G:=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
its associated tuple of hypersurfaces and
consider
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}_{\geqslant 0}
with Euclidean norm
\parallel\boldsymbol{\lambda}\parallel<1
. Then,
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a jumping point of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
if and only if it is a jumping point of
G
.

Proof.

After Remark
3.7
we may assume that we have a single ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\big)
so its associated hypersurface is
g=f_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+f_{r}y_{r}
. The result then follows from
3.6
.
∎

In order to prove the main result of this section we will need the analytic definition of mixed multiplier ideal associated to a tuple
G=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
G=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
be a tuple in
A
. Let
{\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(O)}
be a closed ball of radius
\varepsilon
and center the origin
O\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
. The mixed multiplier ideal (at the origin
O
) of
G
associated with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}_{>0}
is

\mathcal{J}(g_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots g_{\ell}^{\lambda_{\ell}})_{O}=\big\{h\in A\ \big|\ \exists\,\epsilon\ll 1\ \textnormal{such that}\ \int_{\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(O)}\frac{|h|^{2}}{|g_{1}|^{2\lambda_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2\lambda_{\ell}}}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}<\infty\big\}.

Remark 3.10
.

As in the case of Bernstein-Sato ideals it is enough to consider this local case since we
have

\mathcal{J}(g_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots g_{\ell}^{\lambda_{\ell}})=\cap_{p\in\mathbb{C}^{d}}\mathcal{J}(g_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots g_{\ell}^{\lambda_{\ell}})_{p}.

If it is clear from the context we will omit the subscript referring to the point.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}
be a tuple of ideals in
R
. Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}_{\geqslant 0}
be a
jumping point of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
with Euclidean norm
\parallel\boldsymbol{\lambda}\parallel<1
. Then
-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in Z(B_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}})
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ell}_{\geqslant 0}
be a
jumping point of the tuple
G=g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}
associated to
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{a}}
with
\parallel\boldsymbol{\lambda}\parallel<1
and take
h\in\mathcal{J}(f^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}})\smallsetminus\mathcal{J}(f^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}\in\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{\ell}\}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Therefore

\frac{|h|^{2}}{|g_{1}|^{2\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell}}}

is integrable but when we take the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we end up with

\frac{|h|^{2}}{|g_{1}|^{2\lambda_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2\lambda_{\ell}}}

that is not integrable. Set
d=n+r_{1}+\cdots+r_{\ell}
and consider the complex zeta function

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}|g_{1}|^{2s_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2s_{\ell}}\varphi(x,y,\bar{x},\bar{y})dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y},

where
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}
are indeterminate variables and
\varphi(x,\bar{x})\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is a
test function
, i.e. an infinitely many times differentiable function with compact support. Moreover
\varphi
has holomorphic and antiholomorphic part. For any
b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\in B_{G}
we have a Bernstein-Sato functional equation

\delta(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})g_{1}^{s_{1}+1}\cdots g_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}+1}=b(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})g_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots g_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}}

Therefore

\displaystyle b^{2}(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\varphi(x,y,\bar{x},\bar{y})|g_{1}|^{2s_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2s_{\ell}}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\bar{\delta}^{*}\delta^{*}(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\big(\varphi(x,y,\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)|g_{1}|^{2(s_{1}+1)}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2(s_{\ell}+1)}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}.

where
\bar{\delta}^{*}
and
\delta^{*}
denote the
conjugate
and the
adjoint
differential operators associated to
\delta
.
Notice that
|h|^{2}\varphi(x,\bar{x})
is still a test function so

\displaystyle b^{2}(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}|h|^{2}\varphi(x,y,\bar{x},\bar{y})|g_{1}|^{2s_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2s_{\ell}}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\bar{\delta}^{*}\delta^{*}(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\big(|h|^{2}\varphi(x,y,\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)|g_{1}|^{2(s_{1}+1)}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2(s_{\ell}+1)}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}.

Now we take a test function
\varphi
which is zero outside the ball
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(O)
and identically one on a smaller ball
\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(O)\subseteq\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(O)
and thus we get

\displaystyle b^{2}(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(O)}|h|^{2}|g_{1}|^{2s_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2s_{\ell}}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(p)}\bar{\delta}^{*}\delta^{*}(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}))\big(|h|^{2}\big)|g_{1}|^{2(s_{1}+1)}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2(s_{\ell}+1)}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}.

Taking
s=-(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell})
we get

\displaystyle b^{2}(-\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,-\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(O)}\frac{|h|^{2}}{|g_{1}|^{2\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell}}}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(O)}\bar{\delta}^{*}\delta^{*}(-\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,-\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell})\big(|h|^{2}\big)|g_{1}|^{2(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{1})}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell})}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}

but the right-hand side is uniformly bounded for all
\varepsilon>0
. Thus we have

b^{2}(-\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,-\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell})\int_{\overline{B}_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(O)}\frac{|h|^{2}}{|g_{1}|^{2\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots|g_{\ell}|^{2\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell}}}dxdyd\bar{x}d\bar{y}\leq M<\infty

for some positive number
M
that depends on
h
. Then, by the monotone convergence theorem we have to have
b^{2}(-\lambda_{1},\dots,-\lambda_{\ell})=0
and thus
-\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in Z(\tilde{B}_{G})=Z(B_{\mathfrak{a}})
.
∎

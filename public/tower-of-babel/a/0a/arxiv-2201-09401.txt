Hochschild cohomology, monoid objects and monoidal categories
Magnus Hellstrøm-Finnsen

Abstract
. This paper expands further on a category theoretical formulation of Hochschild cohomology for monoid objects in monoidal categories enriched over abelian groups, which has been studied in [ 1 ] . This topic was also presented at ISCRA, Isfahan, Iran, April 2019. The present paper aims to provide a more intuitive formulation of the Hochschild cochain complex and extend the definition to Hochschild cohomology with values in a bimodule object. In addition, an equivalent formulation of the Hochschild cochain complex in terms of a cosimplicial object in the category of abelian groups is provided.

Primary 18D10; Secondary 18D20, 18G60, 16E40.

Introduction

Hochschild cohomology was initially studied by G. Hochschild in
[

]
and
[
3
]
, and provides a cohomology theory for associative algebras. In
[

, M. Gerstenhaber discovered that the cohomology ring has a rich structure, which later has been called a Gerstenhaber algebra. The rich structure provides interesting implications, not only restricted to mathematics, but also to physics and related fields.

At Isfahan School & Conference on Representations of Algebras (ISCRA), April 2019, I reported from
[
1
]
. This article gives a description of Hochschild cohomology in terms of monoid objects (“ring like objects”) in
\Ab

[
5
]
,
[

]
and
[
7
]
), and they provided an axiomatic system to describe the categories with tensor product, like modules over a ring
R
with tensor product over
R

\ot_{R}
, or abelian groups with the tensor product over the integers, i.e.
\ot_{\mathbb{Z}}

In this paper, we will first improve the construction of the Hochschild cochain complex given in
[
1
]
, by taking a more intuitive (and perhaps less combinatorial) approach to this complex. Thereafter, we approach Hochschild cohomology by a cosimplicial object in the category of abelian groups. We will discover that these formulations are equivalent.

Convention 2.4

\displaystyle M^{\ot k}=(\cdots((M\ot M)\ot M)\cdots)\ot M,

\displaystyle C^{k}=\begin{cases}0&\text{for }k<0\\

For the inner summands, we will apply the multiplication rule

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\alpha_{M,M^{\ot(k-2)},M}}M\ot M^{\ot(k-1)}.

We denote this composition by

\displaystyle\alpha^{1}_{M^{\ot k}}:=\alpha_{M,M^{\ot(k-2)},M}\circ\cdots\circ(\cdots(\alpha_{M,M^{\ot 2},M})\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}\circ(\cdots(\alpha_{M,M,M}\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}:

\displaystyle M^{\ot k}\to M\ot M^{\ot(k-1)}.

It should be noted that any procedure to get a specific reconfiguration of a tuple is equivalent, since natural coherence in monoidal categories (Remark
1.2
) provides that any diagram constructed to determine the relationship between such procedures commutes.

Now, we can define the differential in the Hochschild cochain complex.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathscr{K},\ot,\one,\alpha,\lambda,\rho)
be an
\Ab
-enriched monoidal category, let
(M,\mu,\eta)
be a monoid object in
\mathscr{K}
and let
(X,\nu,\omega)
be a bimodule object over
M
. We define the
Hochschild cochain complex

(C^{\bullet},d^{\bullet})
to have objects
C^{k}(M;X)
as given above, i.e.

\displaystyle C^{k}(M;X)=\begin{cases}0&\text{for }k<0\\
\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\one,X)&\text{for }k=0\\
\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot k},X)&\text{for }k>0,\end{cases}

and the differentials,

\displaystyle d^{k}:C^{k}(M;X)\to C^{k+1}(M;X),

are defined as:

•

For
k<0
:
d^{k}=0
.

•

For
k=0
: Let
f\in C^{0}(M;X)=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\one,X)
. The differential,
d^{0}:\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\one,X)\to\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M,X)
, evaluated on
f
is defined to be the sum of the compositions of:

\displaystyle M\xrightarrow{\lambda^{-1}_{M}}\one\ot M\xrightarrow{1_{M}\ot f}X\ot M\xrightarrow{\omega}X

\displaystyle-M\xrightarrow{\rho^{-1}_{M}}M\ot\one\xrightarrow{f\ot 1_{M}}M\ot X\xrightarrow{\nu}X.

•

For
k>0
: We distinguish between the inner and outer summands, as discussed above. Denote the summands by
\chi_{i}
. Let
f\in C^{k}(M;X)=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot k},X)
. The differential,
d^{k}:\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot k},X)\to\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot(k+1)},X)
, evaluated on
f
is defined to be the alternating sum,
\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\chi_{i}
, where the
\chi_{i}
’s are defined as follows:

For
i=0,
we get the first outer summand
\chi_{0}
, which is defined to be the composition of

\displaystyle M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{1}_{M^{\ot(k+1)}}}M\ot M^{\ot k}\xrightarrow{1_{M}\ot f}M\ot X\xrightarrow{\nu}X.

For
i=k
, we get the other outer summand
\chi_{k}
, which is defined to be the composition of

\displaystyle M^{\ot(k+1)}=M^{\ot k}\ot M\xrightarrow{f\ot 1_{M}}X\ot M\xrightarrow{\omega}X.

For
0<i<k
, we get the inner summands
\chi_{i}
, which are defined to be the compositions of the form

\displaystyle M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{(\cdots(\alpha_{M^{\ot i},M,M}\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}(\cdots(M^{\ot i}\ot M^{\ot 2})\ot M\cdots)\ot M

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\cdots(1_{M^{\ot i}}\ot\mu)\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}

\displaystyle M^{\ot k}\xrightarrow{f}X.

The formulation of the Hochschild cochain complex is a coherently equivalent description to that in
[
1
, Definition 3.1]
. Hence,
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
confirms that
(C^{\bullet}(M;X),d^{\bullet})
is, in fact, a cochain complex, and its cohomology groups are well-defined.

Definition 2.6
.

The
Hochschild cohomology groups
are defined to be the cohomology of the cochain complex
(C^{\bullet}(M;X),d^{\bullet})
given in Definition
2.5
, i.e.

\displaystyle\HH^{i}(M;X)=\ker(d^{i})/\im(d^{i-1}).

Some of the classical results for the lower dimensional Hochschild cohomology groups are also proved in
[
1
]
. Here,
\HH^{0}(M;M)=\HH^{0}(M)
is used to define some notion of “quasi centre” of
M
. Similarly, we can define the
quasi centre
,
Z(X)
, for a bimodule object
X
to be:

\displaystyle Z(X):=\HH^{0}(M;X)\cong\ker d^{0}.

With this formulation of Hochschild cohomology, it is proved that the Hochschild cohomology ring

\displaystyle\HH^{*}(M)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\HH^{i}(M)

(where
\HH^{i}(M):=\HH^{i}(M;M)
) is graded commutative with the cup product (see
[
1
, Theorem 5.5]
).

3.
Cosimplicial description of the Hochschild cochain complex

The objective for this section is to formulate the Hochschild cochain complex of a monoid object
M
in a monoidal category
\mathscr{K}
with values in a bimodule
X
in terms of a cosimplicial object in
\Ab
. We argue that this formulation is equivalent to that which was given in the previous section (Section
2
). In
[
11
, Section 9.1]
, Hochschild cohomology for a
k
-algebra is introduced as a cosimplicial object in the vector space over
k
(
k
is a field).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{\Delta}
denote category of non-empty finite ordinals and order preserving maps. The non-empty finite ordinals are denoted by

\displaystyle[k]=\{0,1,\dots,k\}

with the ordering
0\leq 1\leq\cdots\leq k
. This category is called the
simplex category
or the
\mathbf{\Delta}
-category
. Let
\mathscr{K}
be an arbitrary category. A
cosimplicial object

A
in
\mathscr{K}
is a functor
A:\mathbf{\Delta}\to\mathscr{K}
. We write
A([n])=A^{n}
.

We should first note that
A
, in the definition above, is often referred to as a
\mathscr{K}
-valued cosimplicial object
. It should also be noted that a
\mathscr{K}
-valued simplicial object

B
is a functor
B:\mathbf{\Delta}^{\op}\to\mathscr{K}
.

The category
\mathbf{\Delta}
can be described several ways. Since we try to explore category theoretical properties of the Hochschild cochain complex, we should perhaps describe the
\mathbf{\Delta}
-category categorically.

Remark 3.2
.

The
\mathbf{\Delta}
-category can equivalently be defined as the category, where the objects are free categories on linear single arrowed and directed graphs/quivers and the morphisms are functors.

We recall the face and degeneracy maps in
\mathbf{\Delta}
(see
[
11
, Section 8.1]
).

Definition 3.3
.

For each
[k]\in\mathbf{\Delta}
, let
\epsilon_{i}:[k-1]\to[k]
denote the (
i
-th)
face map
, that is the unique injective map that misses
i
:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}j&\qquad\text{if $j<i$}\\
j+1&\qquad\text{if $j\geq i$}.\end{cases}

Dually, for each
[k]\in\mathbf{\Delta}
, let
\zeta_{i}:[k+1]\to[k]
denote the (
i
-th)
degeneracy map
, that is the unique surjective map that sends two elements to
i
:

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}j&\qquad\text{if $j\leq i$}\\
j-1&\qquad\text{if $j>i$}.\end{cases}

Face and degeneracy maps satisfies certain identities (see
[
11
, Exercise 8.1.1]
). Let
A
be a cosimplicial object in an arbitrary category
\mathscr{K}
, then the face maps and degeneracy maps generalise to coface operations and codegeneracy operations on
A
, respectively. We recall
[
11
, Corollary 8.1.4]
, which states how we can describe cosimplicial objects.

Proposition 3.4
.

To describe a cosimplicial object
A
in an arbitrary category
\mathscr{K}
, it is sufficient and necessary to give a sequence of objects
A^{0},A^{1},\dots
together with coface operations

\displaystyle\delta^{i}:A^{k-1}\to A^{k}

and codegeneracy operations

\displaystyle\sigma^{i}:A^{k+1}\to A^{k},

such that the following identities are satisfied:

\displaystyle\delta^{j}\delta^{i}

\displaystyle=\delta^{i}\delta^{j-1}\qquad\text{if $i<j$},

\displaystyle\sigma^{j}\sigma^{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{i}\sigma^{j+1}\qquad\text{if $i\leq j$},

\displaystyle\sigma^{j}\delta^{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\delta^{i}\sigma^{j-1}&\text{if $i<j$}\\
1&\text{if $i=j$ or $i=j+1$}\\
\sigma^{i-1}\delta^{j}&\text{if $i>j+1$}.\end{cases}

Definition 3.5
.

We will refer to the identities given in Proposition
3.4
as the
cosimplicial identities
.

The objective now is to construct a cosimplicial object in
\Ab
, which we later will reformulate to the Hochschild cochain complex (in Theorem
3.8
).

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(\mathscr{K},\ot,\one,\alpha,\lambda,\rho)
be an
\Ab
-enriched monoidal category,
(M,\mu,\eta)
a monoid object in
\mathscr{K}
and
(X,\nu,\omega)
a bimodule object over
M
. The object
A
in
\Ab
is constructed in the following way. To each
[k]\in\mathbf{\Delta}
we assign:

\displaystyle[k]\mapsto\begin{cases}A^{0}=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\one,X)&\text{for $k=0$}\\
A^{k}=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot k},X)&\text{for $k>0$}.\end{cases}

The coface operations are given by: For
k=0
, let
f\in A^{0}=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(\one,X)
. Then
\delta^{i}:A^{0}\to A^{1}
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
, evaluated on
f
, is defined as the composition of:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta^{0}(f)=M\xrightarrow{\lambda^{-1}_{M}}\one\ot M\xrightarrow{f\ot 1_{M}}X\ot M\xrightarrow{\omega}X\\
\delta^{1}(f)=M\xrightarrow{\rho^{-1}_{M}}M\ot\one\xrightarrow{1_{M}\ot f}M\ot X\xrightarrow{\nu}X.\end{cases}

For
k>0
, let
f\in A^{k}=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot k},X)
. Then
\delta^{i}:A^{k}\to A^{k+1}
, for
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
, evaluated on
f
, is defined as the composition of:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta^{0}(f)=M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{1}_{M^{\ot(k+1)}}}M\ot M^{\ot k}\xrightarrow{1_{M}\ot f}M\ot X\xrightarrow{\nu}X\\
\\
\delta^{i}(f)=\begin{cases}&M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{(\cdots(\alpha_{M^{\ot i},M,M}\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}(\cdots(M^{\ot i}\ot M^{\ot 2})\ot M\cdots)\ot M\\
&\xrightarrow{(\cdots(1_{M^{\ot i}}\ot\mu)\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}M^{\ot k}\xrightarrow{f}X\end{cases}\qquad\text{for $0<i<k$}\\
\\
\delta^{k}(f)=M^{\ot(k+1)}=M^{\ot k}\ot M\xrightarrow{f\ot 1_{M}}X\ot M\xrightarrow{\omega}X.\end{cases}

The codegeneracy operations are given by: For
k=0
, let
f\in A^{1}=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M,X)
. Then
\sigma^{0}:A^{1}\to A^{0}
, evaluated on
f
, is defined as the composition of:

\displaystyle\sigma^{0}(f)=\one\xrightarrow{\eta}M\xrightarrow{f}X.

For
k>0
, let
f\in A^{k+1}=\Hom_{\mathscr{K}}(M^{\ot(k+1)},X)
. Then
\sigma^{i}:A^{k+1}\to A^{k}
, for
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
, evaluated on
f
, is defined as the composition of:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma^{0}(f)=\begin{cases}M^{\ot k}\xrightarrow{(\cdots((\lambda^{-1}_{M}\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M})\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}(\cdots((\one\ot M)\ot M)\ot M\cdots)\ot M\\
\xrightarrow{(\cdots(((\eta\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M})\ot 1_{M})\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{f}X\end{cases}\\
\\
\sigma^{i}(f)=\begin{cases}M^{\ot k}\xrightarrow{(\cdots(1_{M^{\ot i}}\ot\lambda^{-1}_{M})\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}(\cdots(M^{\ot i}\ot(\one\ot M))\ot M\cdots)\ot M\\
\xrightarrow{(\cdots(1_{M^{\ot i}}\ot(\eta\ot 1_{M}))\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}(\cdots(M^{\ot i}\ot(M\ot M))\ot M\cdots)\ot M\\
\xrightarrow{(\cdots(\alpha_{M^{\ot i},M,M}^{-1}\ot 1_{M}\cdots)\ot 1_{M}}M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{f}X\end{cases}\qquad\text{for $0<i<k$}\\
\\
\sigma^{k}(f)=\begin{cases}M^{\ot k}=M^{\ot(k-1)}\ot M\xrightarrow{1_{M^{\ot(k-1)}}\ot\rho^{-1}_{M}}M^{\ot(k-1)}\ot(M\ot\one)\\
\xrightarrow{1_{M^{\ot(k-1)}}\ot(1_{M}\ot\eta)}M^{\ot(k-1)}\ot(M\ot M)\xrightarrow{\alpha^{-1}_{M^{\ot(k-1)}\ot(M\ot M)}}M^{\ot(k+1)}\xrightarrow{f}X.\end{cases}\end{cases}

We refer to this construction,
(A,\delta,\sigma)
, as the
Hochschild cosimplicial object
.

The Hochschild cosimplicial object is, in fact, a cosimplicial object in

We observe that the coface operations,
\delta^{i}
, in the Hochschild cosimplicial object
A
in Definition
3.6
, equal to the summands,
\chi_{i}
, we used to define the differentials,
d^{i}
, in the Hochschild cochain complex in Definition
2.5
. The cosimplicial object can then be turned into a cochain complex by adding
0
’s on the “left side” of the object, such that it is defined for negative
k
. This gives an equivalent formulation of Hochschild cohomology.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(\mathscr{K},\ot,\one,\alpha,\lambda,\rho)
be an
\Ab
-enriched monoidal category,
(M,\mu,\eta)
a monoid object in
\mathscr{K}
and
(X,\nu,\omega)
a bimodule object over
M
. Let
A
be the Hochschild cosimplicial object defined in Definition
3.6
. The following procedure turns the comsimplicial object
A
into a cochain complex. For
k<0
define
A^{k}=0
and add these to the complex. The differential is defined to be the alternating sum of the coface operations for non-negative
k
and
0
otherwise, i.e.

\displaystyle d^{k}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $k<0$}\\
\delta^{0}-\delta^{1}&\text{if $k=0$}\\
\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}(-1)^{i}\delta^{i}&\text{if $k>0$}.\end{cases}

Moreover, this cochain complex equals the Hochschild cochain complex defined in Definition
2.5
.

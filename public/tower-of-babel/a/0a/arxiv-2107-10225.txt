Pricing Exchange Option Based on Copulas by MCMC Algorithm
Wen Su

Abstract
This paper focus on pricing exchange option based on copulas by MCMC algorithm. Initially, we introduce the methodologies concerned about risk-netural pricing, copulas and MCMC algorithm. After the basic knowledge, we compare the option prices given by different models, the results show except Gumbel copula, the other model provide similar estimation.



Keywords
: Exchange Option; Copulas; MCMC

1
Risk-Netural Pricing with C.D.F.

A call option price can be expressed as an expectation (conditional expectation) under risk-netural measure
Q
:

c\left(t,S,T,K\right)=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.\left(S_{T}-K\right)^{+}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

(1)

this expectation can be simplified by using the conditional terminal c.d.f. of the underlying asset
S_{T}
denoted by
F_{t}(\cdot)
, since a non-negative random variable
X
enjoys a property

E\left(X\right)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{P\left(X>x\right)dx}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\left[1-F_{X}\left(x\right)\right]dx}.

(2)

So the call option price can be expressed as

\displaystyle c\left(t,S,T,K\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{Q_{t}\left\{\left(S_{T}-K\right)^{+}>x\right\}dx}

(3)

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}\int_{K}^{+\infty}{Q_{t}\left(S_{T}>x\right)dx}

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}\int_{K}^{+\infty}{\left[1-F_{t}\left(x\right)\right]dx}.

Thus in other words, pricing option is equal to finding
F_{t}(x)
. We differentiate (3) w.r.t.
K
then we have

\frac{\partial c\left(t,S,T,K\right)}{\partial K}=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}\left[1-F_{t}\left(K\right)\right],

(4)

which may be helpful for finding
F_{t}(x)
.

2
Copulas

The copula developed by Sklar (1959) is a useful tool for handling multivariate distributions with given univariate marginals. Formally, a copula
C
is a distribution function, defined on the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
, with uniform one-dimensional marginals. For example, a random vector
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{d})^{T}
has joint c.d.f.
F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})
and marginals
F_{1},\cdots,F_{d}
, then copula is defined as

\displaystyle C\left(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(F_{1}\left(X_{1}\right)\leq u_{1},\cdots,F_{d}\left(X_{d}\right)\leq u_{d}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle=F\left(F_{1}^{-}\left(u_{1}\right),\cdots,F_{d}^{-}\left(u_{d}\right)\right),

where
u_{i}\in[0,1]
,
i=1,\cdots,d
. Generally, copula is considered to be the joint c.d.f. of
(F_{1}(X_{1}),\cdots,F_{d}(X_{d}))
. Thus, for multivariate distributions with continuous marginals, the univariate marginals and multivariate dependence structure can be separated, and the dependence structure can be represented by a copula. In some conditions, the survival copula is more effective. The survival copula is defined as

\bar{C}\left(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d}\right)=P\left(F_{1}\left(X_{1}\right)>1-u_{1},\cdots,F_{d}\left(X_{d}\right)>1-u_{d}\right),

(6)

where
u_{i}\in[0,1]
,
i=1,\cdots,d
. In real problems,
2
-d copulas are applied most often, so the following part will introduce some common
2
-d copulas.

1.

Independent Copula:

\Pi\left(u,v\right)=uv.

2.

Comonotonicity Copula:

M\left(u,v\right)=\min\{u,v\}.

3.

Countermonotonicity Copula:

m\left(u,v\right)=\max\{u+v-1,0\}.

4.

Frechet Copula:

C^{Fre}\left(u,v\right)=\alpha M\left(u,v\right)+\beta\Pi\left(u,v\right)+\gamma m\left(u,v\right).

5.

Gumbel Copula:

C^{Gum}_{\theta}\left(u,v\right)=\exp\left\{-\left[\left(-\ln u\right)^{\theta}+\left(-\ln v\right)^{\theta}\right]^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right\}.

6.

Clayton Copula:

C^{Cla}_{\theta}\left(u,v\right)=\left(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}.

7.

Frank Copula:

C^{Fra}\left(u,v\right)=-\frac{1}{\theta}\ln\left(1+\frac{\left(e^{-\theta u}-1\right)\left(e^{-\theta v}-1\right)}{e^{-\theta}-1}\right).

8.

Gaussian Copula:
(X,Y)\sim N(\mu_{1},\mu_{2};\sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2};\rho)
, copula for it is

C^{Gau}_{\rho}\left(u,v\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{N^{-1}\left(u\right)}{\int_{-\infty}^{N^{-1}\left(v\right)}{e^{-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}}dxdy}}.

3
MCMC Algorithm

Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is a simulation method which applies Markov Chain into Monte Carlo procedure. In Monte Carlo simulation, if we want to calculate expectation
E[f(X)]
, we will try to produce random sample
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}
from population
X
, usually
n
is large, and then the law of large number ensures

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{f\left(x_{i}\right)}\xrightarrow{P}E\left[f\left(X\right)\right].

(7)

However, random sample from
X
sometimes may be not easy to produced. But if a homogeneous Markov Chain sequence
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\cdots
(with some technical conditions) which converges to
X
can be produced, then we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{f\left(x_{i}\right)}\xrightarrow{P}E\left[f\left(X\right)\right].

(8)

However, since currently
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
are not i.i.d., so it depends on
x_{t-1}
to produce
x_{t}
, so the LHS of (8) may depend on the initial point
x_{1}
(or
x_{0}
). So usually the estimate is given by

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+n}{f\left(x_{i}\right)}.

Gibbs Sampler is one of the best-known MCMC methods to produce a Markov Chain sequence, it takes two steps to get
(x_{t+1},y_{t+1})
from
(x_{t},y_{t})
:

1.

produce
x_{t+1}
from
X\mid Y=y_{t}
;

2.

produce
y_{t+1}
from
Y\mid X=x_{t+1}
.

Continuing in this fashion, the Gibbs Sampler generates a sequence of random variables
\{(x_{k},y_{k}),k=1,2,\cdots,n\}
which converges to
(X,Y)
.

4
Pricing Based on GBM

An exchange option
V(t,S_{1},S_{2},T)
is an European derivative contract with the terminal payoff

V\left(T,S_{1},S_{2},T\right)=\max\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right)-S_{2}\left(T\right),0\right\}.

(9)

In so-called Black-Scholes economics, the underlying assets dynamics are given by GBM, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{dS_{1}\left(t\right)}{S_{1}\left(t\right)}=\mu_{1}dt+\sigma_{1}dB_{1}\left(t\right),

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{dS_{2}\left(t\right)}{S_{2}\left(t\right)}=\mu_{2}dt+\sigma_{2}dB_{2}\left(t\right),

besides,
B_{1}(t)
and
B_{2}(t)
are often not independent,
dB_{1}\left(t\right)dB_{2}\left(t\right)=\rho dt.

There is an unique risk-netural measure
Q
which ensures

\displaystyle\frac{dS_{1}\left(t\right)}{S_{1}\left(t\right)}=rdt+\sigma_{1}dB^{Q}_{1}\left(t\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{dS_{2}\left(t\right)}{S_{2}\left(t\right)}=rdt+\sigma_{2}dB^{Q}_{2}\left(t\right),

and of course,
dB^{Q}_{1}\left(t\right)dB^{Q}_{2}\left(t\right)=\rho dt.
Actually, we can decompose
dB_{2}(t)
as
dB_{2}(t)=\rho dB_{1}(t)+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dW(t)
where
W(t)
is a BM uncorrelated with
B_{1}(t)
and then we can easily construct the Radon-Nikodym derivative.

Under risk-netural measure, the option price can be expressed as a conditional expectation

V\left(t,S_{1},S_{2},T\right)=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.\max\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right)-S_{2}\left(T\right),0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

(12)

Note that
\frac{e^{-rt}S_{2}\left(t\right)}{S_{2}(0)}=e^{\sigma_{2}B_{2}^{Q}\left(t\right)-\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}t}{2}}
can be used as a Radon-Nikodym derivative
Z_{t}=\left.\frac{d\nu}{dQ}\right|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}
, which makes
B_{2}^{\nu}(t)=-\sigma_{2}t+B_{2}^{Q}(t)
a BM in
\nu
. So we have

\displaystyle V\left(t,S_{1},S_{2},T\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.\max\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right)-S_{2}\left(T\right),0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

(13)

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.S_{2}\left(T\right)\max\left\{\frac{S_{1}\left(T\right)}{S_{2}\left(T\right)}-1,0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{rt}S_{2}\left(0\right)E^{Q}\left[\left.Z_{T}\max\left\{\frac{S_{1}\left(T\right)}{S_{2}\left(T\right)}-1,0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{rt}S_{2}\left(0\right)Z_{t}E^{\nu}\left[\left.\max\left\{\frac{S_{1}\left(T\right)}{S_{2}\left(T\right)}-1,0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=S_{2}\left(t\right)E^{\nu}\left[\left.\max\left\{\frac{S_{1}\left(T\right)}{S_{2}\left(T\right)}-1,0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

By Itô’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\left(\frac{S_{1}}{S_{2}}\right)}{\frac{S_{1}}{S_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left(-\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}dt+\sigma_{1}dB_{1}^{Q}\right)+\left(\sigma_{2}dt-\sigma_{2}dB_{2}^{Q}\right)

(14)

\displaystyle=\left(\rho\sigma_{1}dB_{2}^{\nu}+\sigma_{1}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{3}^{\nu}\right)-\sigma_{2}dB_{2}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{3}^{\nu}+\left(\rho\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\right)dB_{2}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}-2\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}dW^{\nu}.

So the expectation can be calculated by Black-Schoels formula,

E^{\nu}\left[\left.\max\left\{\frac{S_{1}\left(T\right)}{S_{2}\left(T\right)}-1,0\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\frac{S_{1}\left(t\right)}{S_{2}\left(t\right)}N\left(d_{1}\right)-N\left(d_{2}\right),

(15)

where
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are given by

d_{1}=\frac{\ln\frac{S_{1}\left(t\right)}{S_{2}\left(t\right)}+\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}-2\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}{2}\left(T-t\right)}{\sqrt{\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}-2\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\right)\left(T-t\right)}},\quad d_{2}=d_{1}-\sqrt{\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}-2\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\right)\left(T-t\right)}.

Finally, we have the exchange option price

V\left(t,S_{1},S_{2},T\right)=S_{1}\left(t\right)N\left(d_{1}\right)-S_{2}\left(t\right)N\left(d_{2}\right).

(16)

5
Pricing Based on Copulas

Actually, the underlying assets dynamics may be not given by GBM. Learnt from section 1, we have

\displaystyle V\left(t,S_{1},S_{2},T\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.\left(S_{1}\left(T\right)-S_{2}\left(T\right)\right)^{+}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

(17)

\displaystyle=e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.S_{1}\left(T\right)-\min\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right),S_{2}\left(T\right)\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=S_{1}\left(t\right)-e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}E^{Q}\left[\left.\min\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right),S_{2}\left(T\right)\right\}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=S_{1}\left(t\right)-e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{Q_{t}\left(\min\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right),S_{2}\left(T\right)\right\}>x\right)dx}

\displaystyle=S_{1}\left(t\right)-e^{-r\left(T-t\right)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\bar{C}_{t}\left(Q_{t}\left(S_{1}\left(T\right)>x\right),Q_{t}\left(S_{2}\left(T\right)>x\right)\right)dx}.

However, survival copula for the asset prices may be not easy to find, let

S_{1}(T)=S_{1}(t)e^{X_{1}}\quad,S_{2}(T)=S_{2}(t)e^{X_{2}},

(18)

then we can focus only on the copula of rate of return
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}{Q_{t}\left(\min\left\{S_{1}\left(T\right),S_{2}\left(T\right)\right\}>z\right)dz}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{Q_{t}\left(X>\ln\frac{z}{S_{1}\left(t\right)},Y>\ln\frac{z}{S_{2}\left(t\right)}\right)dz}

(19)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\bar{C}_{t}\left(1-F_{1}\left(\ln\frac{z}{S_{1}\left(t\right)}\right),1-F_{2}\left(\ln\frac{z}{S_{2}\left(t\right)}\right)\right)dz}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\left[1-u-v+C_{t}\left(u,v\right)\right]dz},

where
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
are the conditional c.d.f. for
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, respectively,
u_{1}=u_{1}(z)=F_{1}(\ln\frac{z}{S_{1}(t)})
,
v=v(z)=F_{2}(\ln\frac{z}{S_{2}(t)})
, and note that in
2
-d situation, survival copula can be expressed as
\bar{C}(1-u,1-v)=1-u-v+C(u,v)
.

From (19) we clearly see why pricing exchange option is linked to copula. However, in order to price exchange option, besides type of copula, we should also know the of marginal c.d.f. of
X
and
Y
. Generally, marginal normal may be a common choice but remember the joint distribution may be not normal.

In order to calculate
E^{Q}\left[\left.\left(S_{1}\left(T\right)-S_{2}\left(T\right)\right)^{+}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]
, consider
X_{1}\sim N(r(T-t),\sigma_{1}^{2}(T-t))
,
X_{2}\sim N(r(T-t),\sigma_{2}^{2}(T-t))
,
U_{1}\sim U(0,1)
,
U_{2}\sim U(0,1)
and
(U_{1},U_{2})\sim C(u_{1},u_{2})
, we divide the procedure into following steps:

1.

Draw
u_{1}^{(1)}\sim U(0,1)
;

2.

Draw
u_{2}^{(1)}\sim U_{2}\mid U_{1}=u_{1}^{(1)}
;

3.

Draw
u_{1}^{(2)}\sim U_{1}\mid U_{2}=u_{2}^{(1)}
;

4.

Draw
u_{2}^{(2)}\sim U_{2}\mid U_{1}=u_{1}^{(2)}
;

5.

Continuing in this fashion, the Gibbs Sampler generates a sequence of random variables

\{(u_{1}^{(k)},u_{2}^{(k)}),k=1,2,\cdots,n+m\}

which converges to
(U_{1},U_{2})
;

6.

Let
x_{1}^{(k)}=r(T-t)+\sigma_{1}\sqrt{T-t}N^{-1}(u_{1}^{k)})
,
x_{2}^{(k)}=r(T-t)+\sigma_{2}\sqrt{T-t}N^{-1}(u_{2}^{(k)})
,
k=1,2,\cdots,n+m
.

Then the MCMC estimate of
E^{Q}\left[\left.\left(S_{1}\left(T\right)-S_{2}\left(T\right)\right)^{+}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]
is given by

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m}{\left(S_{1}\left(t\right)e^{X_{1}}-S_{2}\left(t\right)e^{X_{2}}\right)^{+}}.

In the above procedure,
r
is the risk-free rate,
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
are the volatilities for two assets. Besides, the parameters in the copula should be estimated, too. Actually, if we want to obtain the MLE of all parameters from data
(x_{11},x{21})
,
(x_{12},x_{22})
,
\cdots
,
(x_{1n},x_{2n})
, we should maximize the log likelihood function

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\ln c\left(F_{1}\left(x_{1k}\right),F_{2}\left(x_{2k}\right)\right)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(\ln f_{1}\left(x_{1k}\right)+\ln f_{2}\left(x_{2k}\right)\right)},

where
c(u_{1},u_{2})
is the p.d.f. of
C(u_{1},u_{2})
. It may be too tedious, we take it into two parts: we firstly maximize

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(\ln f_{1}\left(x_{1k}\right)+\ln f_{2}\left(x_{2k}\right)\right)},

then apply the estimated parameters and maximize

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\ln c\left(F_{1}\left(x_{1k}\right),F_{2}\left(x_{2k}\right)\right)}.

This two-part method is called inference for the margins (IFM), which reduced the complexity in computing a lot.

7
Conclusion

Despite it seems copula model selection will exert a tremendous influence on pricing exchange option, the results show except Gumbel copula, the other models provide similar option prices. The facts we get in this research offer an insight that if we want to apply copula into pricing option (or c.d.f. method in section 1), any common type of copula may give similar results, so the estimation procedure (e.g: volatility, parameters in the copula) is still the most important thing.

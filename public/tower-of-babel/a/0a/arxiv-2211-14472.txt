A graph discretized approximation of semigroups for diffusion with drift and killing on a complete Riemannian manifold
Satoshi Ishiwata and Hiroshi Kawabi

Abstract
In the present paper, we prove that the C 0 C_{0} -semigroup generated by a Schrödinger operator with drift on a complete Riemannian manifold is approximated by the discrete semigroups associated with a family of discrete time random walks with killing in a flow on a sequence of proximity graphs, which are constructed by partitions of the manifold. Furthermore, when the manifold is compact, we also obtain a quantitative error estimate of the convergence. Finally, we give examples of the partition of the manifold and the drift term on two typical manifolds: Euclidean spaces and model manifolds. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 47D08, 58J65, 05C81. Keywords: Riemannian manifold, graph discretization, proximity graph, drifted Schrödinger semigroup, random walk in a flow with killing.



1
Introduction

Let
{M}=({M},g)
be a smooth
n
-dimensional
Riemannian manifold and
\mathfrak{m}
be the Riemannian volume measure on
M
.
We assume that
M
is geodesically complete and connected,
but not necessarily compact.
We denote by
C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
and
C_{0}(M)
the spaces of smooth functions on
M

with compact support
and continuous functions on
M
vanishing at infinity, respectively.
Let
b
be a smooth vector field on
M
, and

V
be a non-negative smooth function defined on
M
.
We consider a

drifted Schrödinger operator

{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}_{V}

having the form

{\mathcal{A}}f(x)=-\Delta f(x)-(bf)(x)+V(x)f(x),\quad x\in M,~f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M),

where
\Delta
is the negative
Laplacian on
M
.
Since
V
is non-negative,
the maximal principle of
the Laplacian
\Delta
implies that

(-{\mathcal{A}},C^{\infty}_{c}(M))
is dissipative, thus is closable in
C_{0}(M)

(see
[
See84
, Lemma 2.1]
for details).
Moreover
by the Lumer-Phillips theorem,
the closure of
(-{\mathcal{A}},C^{\infty}_{c}(M))
generates a contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup

\{{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}\}_{t\geq 0}

in
C_{0}(M)
under the
the condition

(A):

(\lambda+{\mathcal{A}})(C^{\infty}_{c}(M))
is dense in
C_{0}(M)

for some
\lambda>0
.

See e.g.,
[
Paz85
,
Ebe99
]

for the definition of dissipativity and the Lumer-Philips theorem.

Many problems in analysis on Riemannian manifolds naturally lead to the study of the

drifted Schrödinger semigroup

\{{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}\}_{t\geq 0}
and hence
it is an important problem to find an efficient approximation scheme of
{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}f
for a given
f\in C_{0}(M)
.
When
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{A}=-\Delta
,
we have the explicit formula for the heat kernel of the Laplacian
\Delta
. Thus
the integral representation formula

{\rm{e}}^{t\Delta}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{n/2}}\exp\big(-\frac{|x-y|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}}{4t}\big)f(y){\rm{d}}y,\quad f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

enables us to study discrete approximations for
{\rm{e}}^{t\Delta}f
by
applying
numerical integration
methods.
However, such an exact formula of the kernel function of
{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}

for general
M
and
\mathcal{A}

has been proved only in limited situations.
When
M
is compact, it is known that the spectrum of the positive Laplacian
-\Delta

consists of eigenvalues

0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots

and the corresponding (orthonormal) eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
are smooth on
M
.
Then the heat semigroup
\{{\rm{e}}^{t\Delta}\}_{t\geq 0}
can be written by

{\rm{e}}^{t\Delta}f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda_{i}}\phi_{i}(x)\int_{M}f(y)\phi_{i}(y){\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(y).

By this spectral expansion formula, we see that the
data
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
have an important role
to approximate
{\rm{e}}^{t\Delta}f
.
Needless to say, on compact manifolds, eigenvalues and eigenfunctions themselves
are interesting objects.
See, for instance,
[
Cha84
,
Ros97
,
Fuj95
,
Ots03
]
.
It is worth mentioning that this kind of argument
has been received a lot of attention
in the study of

manifold learning
.
See
[
BN03
,
BN08
,
BIK14
,
Tew17
,
SW17
,
Ain21
]
and references therein
for recent results.
As a matter of fact, these papers motivated us to write the present paper.
In contrast to the above situation, such an expansion for the drifted Schrödinger
semigroup
\{{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}\}_{t\geq 0}
on a non-compact manifold
M

has been less-developed in general.

On the other hand, probability theory
provides us a functional integral
point of view to the studies of Schrödinger operators on Riemannian manifolds.
We return to the case when the manifold
M
is
not necessarily compact and
consider a diffusion process
{\bf X}=(x_{t},{\mathbb{P}}_{x})
starting from
x\in M
, which is
generated by
\Delta+b
.
Thanks to
smoothness of
M
and
b
, we can construct this diffusion
process up to the explosion time
\zeta(x):=\inf\{t>0;x_{t}\notin M\}

by solving a stochastic differential equation on
M
. Obviously,

\zeta(x)=\infty
for all
x\in M
when
M
is compact.
The diffusion process
\bf X

gives a probabilistic representation for
the semigroup
\{{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}\}_{t\geq 0}
. More precisely, by
the Feynman-Kac formula, we have

{\rm{e}}^{-t{\cal A}}f(x)={\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}_{x}}\Big[\exp\Big(\hskip-1.42262pt-\hskip-1.42262pt\int_{0}^{t}V(x_{s})\,{\rm{d}}s\Big)f(x_{t});t<\zeta(x)\Big],~~t\geq 0,~x\in M

(1.1)

for all
f\in C_{0}(M)
.
See e.g.,
[
IW89
,
Gün17
]
for details.
In view of Feynman’s original path integral approach to quantum physics,
there are many interests of
finite dimensional integral approximations of the
Feynman-Kac type functional integral (
1.1
) over Riemannian manifolds
in many branches of mathematics such as functional analysis, geometric analysis and probability theory.
Actually, this topic has been studied intensively and extensively by many authors.
See e.g.,
[
ET81
,
IM85
,
Ino86
,
AD99
,
BP08
,
MMRS23
]
and references therein for further related results.
We should mention that, in the case
V=0
, the central limit theorem (CLT, in short) for
geodesic random walks on
Riemannian manifolds also gives a finite dimensional integral approximation of
(
1.1
).
For early work in this direction, see e.g.,
[
Jør75
,
Pin76
,
Sun81
,
Blu84
]
.

Under these circumstances of finite dimensional integral approximations of the
functional integral (
1.1
) together with developments
of numerical analysis and manifold learning theory,
it is fundamental and important to
study a discrete approximation scheme
for the drifted Schrödinger semigroup
\{{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
To tackle this problem,
in the present paper, we introduce a family of
discrete time

random walks in the flow
generated by the drift
b
with
killing

on a sequence of proximity graphs, which are
constructed by partitions cutting
the Riemannian manifold
M
into small pieces.
Due to the effect of the drift
b
, these random walks are not necessarily
symmetric in general.
This makes our problem difficult and at the same time interesting.
As a main result, under condition
(A)
,
we prove that
\{{\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}\}_{t\geq 0}

in
C_{0}(M)

is approximated by the discrete semigroups
generated by
the family of random walks
with a suitable scale change (see Theorem
2.1
for the precise statement).
Furthermore, when
M
is compact, we also obtain
a quantitative error estimate of the convergence (see Theorem
2.4
).
As we shall state in Corollary
2.2
, these results give us a finite dimensional

summation
approximation of the Feynman-Kac type functional integral (
1.1
)
which would be a theoretical basis of a new numerical method.

We note that it is possible to study our problem in
L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-setting
in parallel to
C_{0}(M)
-setting,
where
L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})
,

1<p<\infty
, denotes
the usual real
L^{p}
-space on
M
with respect to the volume measure
{\mathfrak{m}}

equipped with the
L^{p}
-norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})}=\big(\int_{M}|f(x)|^{p}{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)\big)^{1/p}
.
In fact,
under
two conditions

(A1)
p
:

(-\lambda-{\mathcal{A}},C^{\infty}_{c}(M))
is
dissipative
in
L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})
for some
\lambda\geq 0
;

(A2)
p
:

(\lambda^{\prime}+{\mathcal{A}})(C^{\infty}_{c}(M))
is dense in
L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})

for some
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
,

the closure of
(-{\mathcal{A}},C^{\infty}_{c}(M))
generates a

C_{0}
-semigroup
\{{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})
,
and we see that most of the arguments in the proof of the above results apply

L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
-setting (see also Theorem
2.1
and
Corollary
2.2
for details).
Note that the semigroup
\{{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is not necessarily contractive in

L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})
.

We now mention
related works.
Burago-Ivanov-Kurylev
[
BIK14
]
studied a discrete approximation of the Laplacian
on a compact Riemannian manifold
based on partitions of the manifold.
Although our framework is influenced by this paper,
we need to extend their argument slightly to the case where
the underlying manifold is non-compact.
Chen-Kim-Kumagai
[
CKK13
]
also studied a similar discrete approximation for a large
class of symmetric jump processes
on metric measure spaces satisfying
the volume doubling condition.
(Note that the volume doubling condition does not necessarily
hold for general non-compact complete Riemannian manifolds.)
In a series of papers
[
IKK17
,
IKN20
,
IKN21
]
, we studied CLTs for
non-symmetric random walks on
infinite graphs having a periodic structure such as
crystal lattices and nilpotent covering graphs.
Introducing a family
of random walks which interpolates between the original non-symmetric
random walk with the symmetrized one, as the limit, we captured the Brownian motion with
a constant drift on a suitable space in which the graph is realized.
However, this kind of interpolation is different from the family
of random walks in a flow introduced in the present paper.
It is also worth mentioning
that Kotani
[
Kot02
]

studied a semigroup CLT for
a generalized Harper operator on a crystal lattice
in
L^{2}
-setting,
and obtained a (uniform) magnetic
Schrödinger semigroup on an Euclidean space as the limit.

The rest of
the present paper is organized as follows: In Sections 2.2 and 2.3, we introduce our framework of
the graph discretization of the manifold
M
and
a random walk in a flow with killing on the graph. In Section 2.4,
we state main results (Theorems
2.1
,
2.4
and
Corollaries
2.2
,
2.3
).
In Section 3, we devote ourselves to prove main results. In particular, in
Theorem
3.2
, we obtain
a convergence rate of the generators of the
family of random walks under the suitable scale change
mentioned above.
Combining this theorem
with Trotter’s approximation theorem (cf.
[
Tro58
,
Kur69
]
)
and a recent result on its convergence rate (cf.
[
Nam23
]
), we obtain Theorems

2.1
and
2.4
, respectively.
In Section 4, we discuss sufficient conditions for conditions
(A)
,
(A1)
p

and
(A2)
p
.
Finally in Section 5, we give examples of the partition of the manifold and the drift on
two typical manifolds: Euclidean spaces and model manifolds.

2
Framework and main results

2.1
Notations

We introduce some notations to describe our results in detail.
Let
C(M)
be the space of all real-valued continuous function on
the Riemannian manifold
M
.
Let

C_{0}(M)
be the subspace of

C(M)
vanishing at infinity, i.e.,
\lim_{d(x,o)\to\infty}f(x)=0
, where

d(x,o)
denotes
the geodesic distance between
x\in M
and a base point
o\in M
.
This is a Banach space endowed with the uniform convergence topology

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in M}|f(x)|
.
Let
C^{\infty}(M)
be the space of all real-valued smooth functions on
M
.
Obviously, if
M
is compact,
C_{0}(M)
and

C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
coincide with
C(M)
and
C^{\infty}(M)
, respectively.

Let
\partial
be a
cemetery
point
added to

M
so that

M_{\partial}:=M\cup\{\partial\}
is the one-point compactification of
M
.
As usual, we may regard
\partial
as the point at infinity and

{\bf{X}}=(x_{t},{\mathbb{P}}_{x})
as a continuous Markov process
on
M_{\partial}
.
Note that we may rewrite the explosion time as
\zeta(x)=\inf\{t>0;x_{t}=\partial\}
.
Since
M
is complete,
x\to\partial
is equivalent to

d(x,o)\to\infty
,
and thus

C_{0}(M)
may be identified with the space of all continuous functions

f:M_{\partial}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying
f(\partial)=0
.
For simplicity of notation, we write
\bm{\partial}=\{\partial\}
.

Throughout the present paper, we use
c
,

C

to denote positive constants which may change from line to line. We also use the Landau symbols

O(\cdot)
and
o(\cdot)
. If the dependence of
C
,
O(\cdot)
and

o(\cdot)
are significant, we denote them like
C(N)
,
O_{N}(\cdot)
and
o_{N}(\cdot)
,
respectively. For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\lfloor a\rfloor
(resp.
\lceil a\rceil
)
the greatest integer less than or equal to
a
(resp. the least integer greater than or equal to
a
).
Unless otherwise specified, we use the Einstein summation convention,
which means that an index variable that appears twice in an expression is
implicitly summed over all its possible values.

2.2
Graph discretization

In what follows, we present a framework of a graph discretization of the Riemannian manifold
M
.
For
x\in M
and
r>0
, let
B_{r}(x)
be the open geodesic ball of radius

r>0
centered at
x\in M
.
For any subset
A
of
M
, we denote by

U_{r}(A)

the
r
-neighborhood of
A
, i.e.,

U_{r}(A)=\cup_{x\in A}B_{r}(x).

For two subsets
A
and
B
of

{M}
, we define the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}(A,B)

between
A
and
B
by

d_{H}(A,B):=\inf\{r>0;~A\subset U_{r}(B),~B\subset U_{r}(A)\}.

See
[
BBI01
, Section 7.3]
for details of the Hausdorff distance.
Moreover, we set

d_{H}(\{\partial\},A):=\infty,\quad A\subset M.

A countable collection
\mathbb{X}

of connected measurable subsets of
M
with finite measures
is called
a
partition
of
{M}

if

M=\bigcup_{X\in{\mathbb{X}}}X,\quad X\cap Y=\emptyset~~(X,Y\in\mathbb{X},X\neq Y).

(2.1)

Throughout the present paper, we assume

(B):

\sharp\hskip 1.42262pt\big\{X\in\mathbb{X};X\subset B_{r}(x)\big\}<\infty

for all
x\in M
and
r>0
;

(C):

|{\mathbb{X}}|:=\sup_{X\in\mathbb{X}}{\rm diam}(X)<\infty.

For given
x\in M
,
we denote by
X(x)
the unique element
X\in{\mathbb{X}}
containing
x
.
We also define
X(\partial)={\bm{\partial}}
.
For each
X\in\mathbb{X}
, we take a reference point
x\in X
and write
x(X)
.
Set
\mathscr{X}=\{x(X)\}_{X\in\mathbb{X}}
.
It follows from (
2.1
) that
x(X)\neq x(Y)
for
X\neq Y
.

If the manifold
M
is compact, condition
(B)
implies that such a partition
{\mathbb{X}}
is a finite set.
It is always possible to construct a partition

\mathbb{X}
with
(B)
and
(C)
in the following manner:
For fixed a constant
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
maximal

\varepsilon
-separated
subset
{\mathscr{X}}=\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

of
M
.
Note

B_{\varepsilon/2}(x_{i})\cap B_{\varepsilon/2}(x_{j})=\emptyset
,
i\neq j
, and

\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})=M

(see e.g.,
[
Kan85
,
Kan86
]
for details).
We set

X_{i}:=B_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})\setminus\cup_{j<i}B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}),\quad i\in{\mathbb{N}},

and divide each
X_{i}
into a finite number of connected components
X_{i}^{1},\ldots,X_{i}^{k(i)}
.
Obviously,
\mathbb{X}_{{\mathscr{X}}}=\{X_{i}^{k};k=1,\ldots,k(i),i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a partition of
M

satisfying
(C)
because
|{\mathbb{X}}_{\mathscr{X}}|\leq 2\varepsilon
. Besides,
since
{\mathscr{X}}
is
\varepsilon
-separated and
M
is complete and smooth,
we easily see that
B_{r}(x)\cap{\mathscr{X}}
is a finite set
for all
x\in M
and
r>0
. This means that
{\mathbb{X}}_{\mathscr{X}}
satisfies
(B)
.
As another example of the partition of
M
, we may
consider the
Voronoi decomposition
of
M
with respect to
{\mathscr{X}}
, which is
defined by

X_{1}:={\widetilde{X_{1}}},\quad X_{i}:={\widetilde{X_{i}}}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{i-1}X_{j},~~i=2,3,\ldots,

where

{\widetilde{X_{i}}}:=\big\{y\in M;~d(x_{i},y)\leq d(x_{j},y),~j\in{\mathbb{N}},i\neq j\big\},\quad i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

For given
\rho>0
, let
\mathbb{X}
be a partition of
M
with
{\bf{(B)}}
and
{\bf{(C)}}

satisfying
|\mathbb{X}|<\rho
.
We say that
X
and
Y
in
\mathbb{X}
are
adjacent
if
d_{H}(X,Y)<\rho
and write
X\sim_{\rho}Y
.
Then we define an oriented graph
\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{X},\rho)=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
called
a (
\rho
-)
proximity graph
of the manifold
M
by
\mathbb{V}:={\mathbb{X}}
and

\mathbb{E}:=\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X};X\sim_{\rho}Y\}.

Since
M
is connected,
{\mathbb{G}}({\mathbb{X}},\rho)
is also connected, and

(B)
implies that
{\mathbb{G}}({\mathbb{X}},\rho)
is
locally finite, that is, for any
X\in\mathbb{X}
,
the (
\rho
-)neighborhood of
X
defined by

N_{\rho}(X):=\{Z\in\mathbb{X};Z\sim_{\rho}X\}

is a finite set. We should mention that

{\mathbb{G}}({\mathbb{X}},\rho)
is uniform, i.e.,

\sup_{X\in{\mathbb{X}}}\sharp N_{\rho}(X)<\infty
,
provided that the Ricci curvature of
M
is bounded from below.
See
[
Kan85
, Lemma 2.3]
for details.

For later purpose, we introduce a
graph
{\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho)=({\mathbb{V}}_{\partial},{\mathbb{E}}_{\partial})

constructed by the sum of
the proximity graph
\mathbb{G}({\mathbb{X}},\rho)
and the point
\partial
mentioned above.
To be precise,

{\mathbb{V}}_{\partial}:={\mathbb{V}}\cup{\bm{\partial}}

and

{\mathbb{E}}_{\partial}:={\mathbb{E}}\cup\{(X,{\bm{\partial}}),({\bm{\partial}},X);X\in\mathbb{X}\}\cup\{({\bm{\partial}},{\bm{\partial}})\}.

We denote by
C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho))
the space of all
bounded functions
F:{\mathbb{X}}\cup\{{\bm{\partial}}\}\to\mathbb{R}
with
F({\bm{\partial}})=0
endowed with

\|F\|_{C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho))}:=\sup_{X\in{\mathbb{X}}}|F(X)|,

and by
L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho))
,
1<p<\infty
, the set of all functions

F:{\mathbb{X}}\cup\{{\bm{\partial}}\}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
F({\bm{\partial}})=0
and

\|F\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho))}:=\Big(\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{X}}}|F(X)|^{p}{\mathfrak{m}}(X)\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

For a given partition
\mathbb{X}

and a set of reference points
{\mathscr{X}}=\{x(X)\}_{X\in\mathbb{X}}
associated with

\mathbb{X}
,
we define two kinds of
discretization maps

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{X}}:L^{p}({\mathfrak{m}})\rightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X},\rho))

and

[\cdot]_{\mathscr{X}}:C_{0}(M)\rightarrow C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X},\rho))

by

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{X}}f(X)=\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(X)}\int_{X}f\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)=f(x(X)),\quad

respectively.

2.3
Random walk in a flow with killing

Let us recall that
b
is a smooth vector field on the
complete Riemannian manifold
M
and
V
is a non-negative smooth
potential function on
M
.

We first introduce the notion of the flow generated by
b
.
For each
x\in M
, there exist
an open interval
I=I_{x}=(-t(x),t(x))

around
0\in\mathbb{R}
and a
smooth curve
(\varphi_{t}(x))_{t\in I}
on
M
satisfying

\varphi_{0}(x)=x,\quad\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{t}(x)=b(\varphi_{t}(x)),\quad t\in I.

Since the solution to the above differential equation depends smoothly on the initial point
x\in M
,
it induces a local flow
\varphi=(\varphi_{t}(x)):\{(t,x);~t\in I_{x},x\in M\}\to M
generated by the vector
field
b
. Note that
t(x)

is continuous with respect to
x
.
We set

s(x):=\inf\{t>0;\varphi_{t}(x)\notin M\},\quad x\in M.

Obviously,

s(x)\geq t(x)

holds for all
x\in M
.
Moreover
\varphi_{s}(x)=\partial
provided

s\geq s(x)
.

For
|{\mathbb{X}}|<\rho
,
X\in\mathbb{X}
and
s\geq 0
,
we set

N_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X):=\{Y\in\mathbb{X};Y\sim_{\rho}X(\varphi_{s}(x(X)))\}

and

\mathcal{N}_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X):=\bigcup_{Y\in N_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X)}Y.

In the case
b=0
,
N_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X)=N_{\rho}(X)
. If
s\geq s(x(X))
,

N_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X)={\bm{\partial}}
.

Inspired by an idea of
[
Mad89
]
,
we introduce a random walk in the flow
\varphi
with killing on
the proximity graph

({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho),{\mathscr{X}})
.
Let
\alpha,s\geq 0
. We
define the transition probability
p_{\alpha,s}=p_{\alpha,s,\mathscr{X}}

on
({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho),{\mathscr{X}})
as follows:
For each
X\in\mathbb{V}
,
Y\in\mathbb{V}_{\partial}
and

0\leq s<s(x(X))
,

p_{\alpha,s}(X,Y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle{\min\{\alpha V(x(X)),1\}}}&\mbox{if }Y={\bm{\partial}},\\
{\displaystyle{\max\big\{1-\alpha V(x(X)),0\big\}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(Y)}{\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X))}}}&\mbox{if }Y\in N_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X),\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and for
s\geq s(x(X))

p_{\alpha,s}(X,Y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }Y={\bm{\partial}},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Besides, we set

p_{\alpha,s}({\bm{\partial}},{\bm{\partial}}):=1,\quad p_{\alpha,s}({\bm{\partial}},X):=0,\quad X\in{\mathbb{X}}~~~\mbox{for all }s\geq 0.

In the usual manner, the transition probability
p_{\alpha,s}
induces a
time homogeneous Markov chain on
{\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho)
. We call it the
random walk in the flow

\varphi_{s}
with a
killing rate

\alpha V
.

The corresponding transition operator
L_{\alpha,s}=L_{\alpha,s,\mathscr{X}}:C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho))\to C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}({\mathbb{X}},\rho))

is given by

L_{\alpha,s}F({\bm{\partial}}):=F({\bm{\partial}})=0
and

\displaystyle L_{\alpha,s}F(X)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{V}}_{\partial}}p_{\alpha,s}(X,Y)F(Y),\quad X\in{\mathbb{X}}.

(2.2)

This means

L_{\alpha,s}F(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle{\max\big\{1-\alpha V(x(X)),0\big\}\sum_{Y\in N_{\rho,{\mathscr{X}}}(s;X)}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(Y)}{{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(s;X))}F(Y)}}&\mbox{if }s<s(x(X)),\\
0&\mbox{if }s\geq s(x(X)).\end{array}\right.

We abbreviate
L_{\alpha,\alpha}
to
L_{\alpha}
for brevity.

Here we give a remark. Let
b=0
and
V=0
, and
put

m(X):={\mathfrak{m}}(X){\mathfrak{m}}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(s;X))
,
X\in\mathbb{X}
,
and
{m}({\bm{\partial}})={\mathfrak{m}}({\bm{\partial}}):=0
.
Then we easily see

p_{\alpha,s}(X,Y)m(X)={\mathfrak{m}}(X){\mathfrak{m}}(Y)=p_{\alpha,s}(Y,X)m(Y),\quad X,Y\in{\mathbb{V}}_{\partial},

which means that the corresponding random walk is
m
-symmetric
.
On the other hand,
the random walk in the flow
\varphi
is not
necessarily symmetric in general.
Indeed, by taking
s>0
large enough and
\rho>0
small enough, the distances
among
x\in X
,
\varphi_{s}(x)
and
\varphi_{2s}(x)

can be very large. In this situation, if
Y
is close to
\varphi_{s}(x)
, we observe

p_{\alpha,s}(X,Y)>0
and
p_{\alpha,s}(Y,X)=0
. This
means that the random walk cannot be symmetric.

Proof of main results

3.1
Convergence of generators

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{\mathbb{X}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of partitions of
M
satisfying

|\mathbb{X}_{k}|\searrow 0

as
k\to\infty
, and
{\mathscr{X}}_{k}
be a set of reference points associated with
\mathbb{X}_{k}
.
Then the sequence of spaces
L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))
and

C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))

together with the maps
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}:L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))
and

[\cdot]_{{\mathscr{X}}_{k}}:C_{0}({M})\rightarrow C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))

approximate
L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
and
C_{0}({M})
in the sense of
Trotter
[
Tro58
]
, respectively.
Namely, for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})},

(3.2)

and for any
f\in C_{0}(M)

\displaystyle\|[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}_{k}}\|_{C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C_{0}(M)},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}_{k}}\|_{C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C_{0}(M)}.

(3.4)

Proof.

The estimate (
3.1
) follows directly from Hölder’s inequality. Noting that

\{{\mathscr{X}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

satisfies

d_{H}({\mathscr{X}}_{k},M)\rightarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
, we also obtain
the estimate (
3.3
) and the convergence (
3.4
) immediately.

To prove (
3.2
), it suffices to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})

there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for any
k\geq k_{0}

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}<\varepsilon.

(3.5)

Moreover, since
C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
,
it suffices to show the estimate in (
3.5
) for any
f_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
.
Indeed, for any
f\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})

take
f_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
such that
\|f-f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}<\varepsilon
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f-f_{0}+f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}(f-f_{0}+f_{0})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f-f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}+\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}

\displaystyle~-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f_{0}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}+\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}(f-f_{0})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f-f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}+\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f_{0}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon+\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f_{0}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}.

To prove

\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f_{0}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}\searrow 0
,
it suffices to prove that

\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}^{p}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f_{0}\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}\searrow 0

by the continuity of function
x^{1/p}
defined on
[0,\infty)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})}^{p}-\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k}}f_{0}\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k},\rho))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\mathbb{X}_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}|f_{0}|^{p}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}-\left|\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}f_{0}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\right|^{p}\right)\mathfrak{m}(X)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathfrak{m}}\big(B_{2\rho}(\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits f_{0})\big)\max_{X\subset B_{2\rho}(\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits f_{0})}\left(\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(X)}\int_{X}|f_{0}|^{p}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}-\left|\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}f_{0}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\right|^{p}\right).

Since
X\in\mathbb{X}_{k}
is connected, the integral-type mean value theorem implies that
there exists
z\in X
such that

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}f_{0}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}=f_{0}(z).

Then we obtain

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}|f_{0}|^{p}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}-\left|\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}f_{0}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\right|^{p}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}\big(|f_{0}(y)|^{p}-|f_{0}(z)|^{p}\big)\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(y).

Since
f_{0}
is uniformly continuous, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

\delta>0
such that if
\mathrm{diam}X<\delta

then

|f_{0}(y)|^{p}\leq(|f_{0}(z)|+\varepsilon)^{p}.

Then we obtain for
y\in X

|f_{0}(y)|^{p}-|f_{0}(z)|^{p}\leq p(|f_{0}(z)|+\varepsilon)^{p-1}\varepsilon,

which concludes that

\max_{X\subset B_{2\rho}(\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits f_{0})}\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}|f_{0}|^{p}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}-\left|\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}f_{0}\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\right|^{p}\right)\leq\varepsilon p\big(\sup_{x\in M}|f_{0}(x)|+\varepsilon\big)^{p-1}.

Hence, the proof of the convergence in (
3.2
) is completed.

and

. We denote by

the flow

.
Since

We emphasize that

For

\|f\|_{C^{k}}:=\max_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sup_{x\in M}\left|\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}f}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}u^{(1)}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}u^{(n)}}(x)\right|,\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

.

The following theorem plays a crucial role in the proof of our main results.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, and
take
\rho>0
small enough such that

\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}\leq\min\left\{s(f),\|V\|_{\infty,S(f)}^{-1},1\right\}

(3.6)

and

\rho<\mathrm{inj}_{M}(S(f)),

where
\mathrm{inj}_{M}(S(f))
is the infimum of the injectivity radius in
S(f)
.
Then for any partition
{\mathbb{X}}
satisfying

|\mathbb{X}|<\rho/3
, there exist
positive constants

{K}_{1}
and
{K}_{2}

depending
on
\|f\|_{C^{3}}

\|b\|_{\infty,S(f)}

\|\nabla_{b}b\|_{\infty,S(f)}

\displaystyle\left|\frac{2(n+2)}{\rho^{2}}(I-L_{\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}})[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)-[{\mathcal{A}}f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)\right|\leq{K}_{1}\frac{|\mathbb{X}|}{\rho^{2}}+{K}_{2}\rho,\quad X\in{\mathbb{X}}.

Proof.

We set
\delta=\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}
and
fix
X\in{\mathbb{X}}
.
We denote the reference point
x(X)\in{\mathscr{X}}
by

for the simplicity of notation.
Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle(I-L_{\delta})[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)-[{\mathcal{A}}f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\big(f(x)-f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta bf(x)\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~-V(x)\big(f(x)-f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta bf(x)\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~+\big(\Delta f(x)-\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta b\Delta f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~+\delta\big(V(x)bf(x)-b\Delta f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~-\frac{1}{\delta}L_{\delta}[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)-V(x)f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\frac{1}{\delta}f(\varphi_{\delta}(x)).

(3.8)

Applying the Taylor expansion formula, we have

f(x)-f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))=-\delta\int_{0}^{1}bf(\varphi_{\delta\theta}(x)){\rm{d}}\theta

(3.9)

and

f(x)-f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta bf(x)=-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big\{{\rm Hess}f(b,b)(\varphi_{\delta\theta}(x))+\left(\nabla_{b}b\right)f(\varphi_{\delta\theta}(x))\big\}{\rm{d}}\theta,

(3.10)

where
\nabla
and
{\rm{Hess}}f
stand for
the Levi-Civita connection of the Riemannian metric
g
and
the Hessian of
f
, respectively.
In a local coordinate
(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(n)})
of
M
with the natural basis

\{{\partial}_{i}={\partial}/{\partial}x^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, by using the expansion

b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b^{i}{\partial}_{i}
,
we may write

{\rm Hess}f(b,b)=b^{i}b^{j}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{(i)}\partial x^{(j)}}-b^{k}b^{i}\Gamma_{ki}^{j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{(j)}},\quad\left(\nabla_{b}b\right)f=b^{i}\Big(\frac{\partial b^{j}}{\partial x^{(i)}}\partial_{j}+b^{j}\Gamma_{ji}^{k}\partial_{k}\Big)f,

where
\{\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\}_{i,j,k=1}^{n}
are the Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection.

It follows from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{\delta}\big(f(x)-f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta bf(x)\big)\Big|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\big(\|{\rm{Hess}}f\|_{\infty}+\|(\nabla_{b}b)f\|_{\infty}\big)

and

\displaystyle\Big|

\displaystyle-V(x)\big(f(x)-f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta bf(x)\big)+\big(\Delta f(x)-\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\delta b\Delta f(x)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\big(\|V\|_{\infty,S(f)}\|bf\|_{\infty}+\|b\Delta f\|_{\infty}\big).

Besides, we also have

\big|\delta\big(V(x)bf(x)-b\Delta f(x)\big)\big|\leq\delta\big(\|V\|_{\infty,S(f)}\|bf\|_{\infty}+\|b(\Delta f)\|_{\infty}\big).

Noting
\max\{(1-\delta V)_{,}0\}=(1-\delta V)
in
S(f)
,
we expand the final line of
the right-hand side of (
3.8
) as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle L_{\delta}[f]_{{\mathscr{X}}}(X)-V(x)f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\frac{1}{\delta}f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta V(x)-1}{\delta}\sum_{Y\in{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(Y)}{\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X))}f(x(Y))

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~~~+\frac{1}{\delta}f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-V(x)f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\delta V(x)}{\delta\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X))}\Big\{\sum_{Y\in{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\mathfrak{m}(Y)\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(x(Y))\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{B(\varphi_{\delta}(x),\rho)}\left(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\right){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)\Big\}

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~+\big(1-\delta V(x)\big)\Big\{\frac{1}{\delta\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho}(\delta;X))}\int_{B(\varphi_{\delta}(x),\rho)}\left(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\right){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)+\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\Big\}

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~+\delta V(x)\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x)).

(3.11)

Here we easily have

\displaystyle|\delta V(x)\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))|\leq\delta\|V\|_{\infty,S(f)}\|\Delta f\|_{\infty}\leq\|\Delta f\|_{\infty}.

To estimate the first term
on the right-hand side of (

),
we recall the definition of the Riemannian integral. Indeed,
taking into account that

B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\subset\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)\subset B_{\rho+|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x)),

(3.12)

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{Y\in N_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\mathfrak{m}(Y)\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(x(Y))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\big){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)+\sum_{Y\in N_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\int_{Y}\left(f(z)-f(x(Y))\right){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))}\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\big){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ }~~~+\int_{B_{\rho+|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\backslash B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))}\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\big){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ }~~~-\int_{B_{\rho+|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\backslash\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\big){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ }~~~+\sum_{Y\in N_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\int_{Y}\left(f(z)-f(x(Y))\right){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z).

Here we note that there exist positive constants

and

depending only on geometry on

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r+h}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)-\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r-h}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)

\displaystyle c_{3}r^{n}\leq\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{r}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}r^{n}.

Combining these estimates with the assumption of

, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1-\delta V(x)}{\delta\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X))}\Big\{\sum_{Y\in N_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\mathfrak{m}(Y)\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(x(Y))\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }\hskip 142.26378pt-\int_{B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))}\big(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\big){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)\Big\}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta c_{3}(\rho-|\mathbb{X}|)^{n}}\Big\{\Big|\sum_{Y\in N_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)}\int_{Y}\big(f(z)-f(x(Y))\big){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)\Big|

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+2\ \int_{B_{\rho+|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\backslash B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))}\big|f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\big|{\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{n}}{\delta c_{3}\rho^{n}}\Big\{\|f\|_{C^{1}}|\mathbb{X}|\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\rho+|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }\hskip 42.67912pt+2\|f\|_{C^{1}}(\rho+|\mathbb{X}|)\Big(\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\rho+|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)-\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{n}(4c_{1}+2^{n}c_{2})(n+2)}{c_{3}}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\frac{|\mathbb{X}|}{\rho^{2}}.

To estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.11
),
we now apply the spherical mean approximation
of the Laplacian.
Let us take a Riemannian normal coordinate system

(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(n)})
at
\varphi_{\delta}(x)\in M
via exponential map

\exp_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}:T_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}M\rightarrow M
.
More precisely, for an orthonormal basis
\{{\bf{e}}_{1},\ldots,{\bf{e}}_{n}\}
of
T_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}M
,
the map

(u^{(1)},\cdots,u^{(n)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E}}{{\longmapsto}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}u^{(j)}{\bf{e}}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\exp_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}}}{{\longmapsto}}\exp_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}u^{(j)}{\bf{e}}_{j}\right)

gives a diffeomorphism between

B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(r)
, the open ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

centered at origin and
B_{r}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))

for small
r>0
and then the pair
(B_{r}(\varphi_{\delta}(x)),E^{-1}\circ\exp_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}^{-1})
can be regarded
as a chart containing
\varphi_{\delta}(x)
.

Now take
z\in S(f)
and
r=\rho<\mathrm{inj}_{M}(S(f))
.
Applying the Taylor expansion formula
to the function
\tilde{f}=f\circ\exp_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}\circ E
on

B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
at the origin,
we have
for
u=(u^{(1)},\cdots,u^{(n)})=E^{-1}\circ\exp_{\varphi_{\delta}(x)}^{-1}(z)

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}(u^{(1)},\cdots,u^{(n)})

\displaystyle={\tilde{f}}(0)+\frac{\partial{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}}(0)u^{(j)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}\partial u^{(k)}}(0)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}+{\mathcal{T}}_{jkl}({\tilde{f}})(u)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(l)},

where

{\mathcal{T}}_{jkl}({\tilde{f}})(u)=\int_{0}^{1}{\rm{d}}\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}{\rm{d}}\theta_{2}\int_{0}^{\theta_{2}}{\rm{d}}\theta_{3}\frac{\partial^{3}{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}\partial u^{(k)}\partial u^{(l)}}(\theta_{3}u).

Now we make use of the fact that the Riemannian volume element

{\sqrt{{\rm det}({\tilde{g}}(u))}}
in the normal coordinates has the expansion

\displaystyle{\sqrt{{\rm det}({\tilde{g}}(u))}}=1-\frac{1}{6}{\rm Ric}_{jk}(0)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}+{\mathcal{G}}_{3}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u\big),

where
\mathrm{Ric}_{jk}(0):=\mathrm{Ric}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))(e_{j},e_{k})
, the
(j,k)
-component
of the Ricci curvature tensor
at
\varphi_{\delta}(x)
, and

{\mathcal{G}}_{3}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u\big)

is the reminder term. Here we should remark that there exists a
continuous non-negative function

on
M
such that

{\mathcal{G}}_{3}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u\big)

satisfies for all
|u|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}<\rho

\sup_{0<\rho^{\prime}<\rho}\big|{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho^{\prime}}(\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}(x));u)\big|\leq G(\varphi_{\delta}(x))|u|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{3},

(3.13)

where
\delta^{\prime}=\frac{(\rho^{\prime})^{2}}{2(n+2)}
.
See e.g.,
[
Sak96
, Lemma 3.5 in Chapter II]
and
[
Sak71
, Lemma 3.4]
for details.
We also mention here that

\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}{\rm{d}}u=0,\quad\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}u^{(k)}{\rm{d}}u=\delta_{jk}\frac{\omega_{n}\rho^{n+2}}{n+2},

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball
B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1)
.

We then obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(\varphi_{\delta}(x),\rho)}\left(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\right){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}\big({\tilde{f}}(0)-{\tilde{f}}(u)\big)\sqrt{\det({\tilde{g}}(u))}\hskip 1.42262pt{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}\Big(-\frac{\partial{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}}(0)u^{(j)}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}\partial u^{(k)}}(0)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}+{\mathcal{T}}_{jkl}({\tilde{f}})(u)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(l)}\Big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }\hskip 51.21495pt\times\Big(1-\frac{1}{6}{\rm Ric}_{jk}(0)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}+{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u)\Big){\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}{\rm{d}}u-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}u^{(k)}}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}u^{(k)}{\rm{d}}u+{\mathcal{R}}_{f}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega_{n}\rho^{n+2}}{2(n+2)}\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))+{\mathcal{R}}_{f}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big),

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{f}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x)))

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{6}\frac{\partial{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(l)}}(0)\mathrm{Ric}_{jk}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(l)}{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle~~~~~+\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{T}}_{jkl}({\tilde{f}})(u)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(l)}{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle~~~~~-\frac{\partial{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u){\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle~~~~~+\frac{1}{12}\frac{\partial^{2}{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}u^{(k)}}(0)\mathrm{Ric}_{rs}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(r)}u^{(s)}{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle~~~~~-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}{\tilde{f}}}{\partial u^{(j)}u^{(k)}}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}u^{(j)}u^{(k)}{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u){\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle~~~~~-\frac{1}{6}\mathrm{Ric}_{rs}(0)\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{T}}_{jkl}({\tilde{f}})(u)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(l)}u^{(r)}u^{(s)}{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle~~~~~+\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{T}}_{jkl}({\tilde{f}})(u)u^{(j)}u^{(k)}u^{(l)}{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u){\rm{d}}u.

Repeating the same calculation as above, we also have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)=\omega_{n}\rho^{n}-{\rm{Scal}}(0)\frac{\omega_{n}\rho^{n+2}}{n+2}+\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u){\rm{d}}u,

stands for the scalar curvature at

Recalling (
3.13

|{\mathcal{R}}_{f}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)|+\Big|\int_{B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{G}}_{3}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x));u){\rm{d}}u\Big|\leq c_{4}\omega_{n}\rho^{n+3},

and

Using (

\displaystyle\left|(1-\delta V(x))\Big\{\frac{1}{\delta\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X))}\int_{B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))}\left(f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))-f(z)\right){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(z)+\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\Big\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\omega_{n}\rho^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X))}-1\right|\cdot|\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))|+\frac{c_{4}\omega_{n}\rho^{n+3}}{\delta^{2}\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))|}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x)))}\Big\{\big|\omega_{n}\rho^{n}-\mathfrak{m}(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x)))\big|

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~~~~~~~~+\mathfrak{m}\big(B_{\rho}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\backslash B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)+\mathfrak{m}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\rho,\mathscr{X}}(\delta;X)\backslash B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big)\Big\}

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~+\frac{2c_{4}\omega_{n}(n+2)\rho^{n+1}}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\rho-|\mathbb{X}|}(\varphi_{\delta}(x)))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big|\Delta f(\varphi_{\delta}(x))\big|}{c_{3}(\rho-|\mathbb{X}|)^{n}}\Big(|{\rm{Scal}}(0)|\frac{\omega_{n}\rho^{n+2}}{n+2}+c_{4}\omega_{n}\rho^{n+3}+2c_{1}|\mathbb{X}|\rho^{n-1}\Big)+\frac{2c_{4}\omega_{n}(n+2)\rho^{n+1}}{c_{3}(\rho-|\mathbb{X}|)^{n}}

Putting it all together, we finally obtain our desired estimate
(
3.7
).
∎

\left|\int_{U}{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)-\sum_{X\in\mathbb{X}_{k(\rho)},X\subset U}\int_{X}{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)\right|\rightarrow 0\quad(\rho\rightarrow 0).

Then the first term of the right-hand side in (
3.18
) converges to
0
.

By using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{X\in\mathbb{X}_{k(\rho)},X\subset U}\int_{X}{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)-\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{X}}_{k(\rho)},X\subset U}L_{\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}}^{\lfloor\frac{2(n+2)}{\rho^{2}}t\rfloor}{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k(\rho)}}f(X){\mathfrak{m}}(X)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{X\in\mathbb{X}_{k(\rho)},X\subset U}\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)-L_{\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}}^{\lfloor\frac{2(n+2)}{\rho^{2}}t\rfloor}{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k(\rho)}}f(X)\right){\mathfrak{m}}(X)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{X\in\mathbb{X}_{k(\rho)},X\subset U}\left|\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(X)}\int_{X}{\rm{e}}^{-t{\mathcal{A}}}f(x){\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}(x)-L_{\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}}^{\lfloor\frac{2(n+2)}{\rho^{2}}t\rfloor}{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k(\rho)}}f(X)\right|^{p}{\mathfrak{m}}(X)\right)^{1/p}\mathfrak{m}(U)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k(\rho)}}({\rm{e}}^{-t\mathcal{A}}f)-L_{\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(n+2)}}^{\lfloor\frac{2(n+2)}{\rho^{2}}t\rfloor}{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathbb{X}}_{k(\rho)}}f\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}}_{\partial}(\mathbb{X}_{k(\rho)},\rho))}\mathfrak{m}(U)^{1/q}.

Hence (
2.4
) implies that the second term of the right-hand side
in (
3.18
) converges to
0
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
.
Then the proof of (
2.6
) is completed.
∎

4
Sufficient conditions for

(A)
,
(A1)
p
and
(A2)
p

In this section, we
discuss sufficient conditions for conditions

(A)
,
(A1)
p
and
(A2)
p
on
the drifted Schrödinger operator

{\cal A}_{V}=-\Delta-b+V
given in the Introduction.
As mentioned in the proof of Theorem 1.3,
these conditions hold in the case where
M
is compact.
Hence, in this section, we focus on the case where

M
is non-compact.
Although the non-negativity of the
the potential function
V
was always assumed in previous sections,
we discuss our problems without assuming it in advance in this section.

First of all, we give a basic criterion for condition
(A)

essentially due to Seeley
[
See84
, Theorem 2]
.
We give an outline of the proof for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a non-compact complete smooth Riemannian manifold,
and
V
be a non-negative smooth function on
M
.
Suppose that there exists a sequence of
smooth cut-off functions
\{\chi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(M)

satisfying
the following three conditions:

(a1):

0\leq\chi_{m}(x)\leq 1
for all
x\in M
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(a2):

For every compact set
K\subset M
, there exists an integer
m_{0}(K)
such that

\chi_{m}(x)=1
for all
x\in K
and
m\geq m_{0}(K)
.

(a3):

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(\Delta+b)\chi_{m}(x)\leq C

(4.1)

for all
x\in M

and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then condition
(A)
holds.

Proof.

To prove that
(1+\mathcal{A}_{V})(C_{c}^{\infty}(M))
is dense in
C_{0}(M)
,
it
suffices
to show that
\nu\in C_{0}(M)^{*}
equals to
0
provided

\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\big\langle\nu,(1+{\mathcal{A}}_{V})\varphi\big\rangle}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{C_{0}(M)^{*}}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\big\langle\nu,(1+{\mathcal{A}}_{V})\varphi\big\rangle}_{C_{0}(M)}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}=0\quad\mbox{for all }\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).

(4.2)

By the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem,
\nu\in C_{0}(M)^{*}
is identified with
a finite signed Borel measure
{\rm{d}}\nu
on
M
. Moreover combining (
4.2
) with
the hypoellipticity of elliptic operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
, we have

{\rm{d}}\nu(x)=u(x){\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)
,
where
u\in C^{\infty}(M)\cap L^{1}({\mathfrak{m}})
(see e.g.,
[
IW89
, Proposition 4.5]
).
Hence we may rewrite
(
4.2
) as

\int_{M}u(x)\,(1+\mathcal{A}_{V})\varphi(x)\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)=0\quad\mbox{for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M).

(4.3)

Since
u\in L^{1}({\mathfrak{m}})
, for any
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists a compact set
K=K_{\varepsilon}\subset M

such that

\int_{K^{c}}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\leq\varepsilon\int_{M}|u|\,{\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m},

(4.4)

where
K^{c}
is the complement of
K
. Namely,

\int_{K}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\int_{M}|u|\,{\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}.

(4.5)

By
(a1)
,
(a2)
and (
4.3
), we obtain
for all
m\geq m_{0}(K)

\displaystyle\int_{K}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\chi_{m}(x)|u(x)|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{-}}\int_{M}\chi_{m}(x)\mathcal{A}^{*}_{V}u(x)\mathrm{sgn}(u(x))\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{M}\chi_{m}(x)\mathcal{A}^{*}_{0}u(x)\mathrm{sgn}(u(x))\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{M}\chi_{m}(x)\mathcal{A}^{*}_{0}|u(x)|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\mathcal{A}_{0}\chi_{m}(x)|u(x)|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)=\int_{M}|u(x)|(\Delta+b)\chi_{m}(x)\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x),

(4.6)

where

{\rm{sgn}}(a):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}a/|a|&\mbox{if }a\neq 0\\
0&\mbox{if }a=0\end{array}\right.,\quad\mathcal{A}^{*}_{V}u=-\Delta u+{\rm{div}}(ub)+Vu

and
we used the non-negativity of
V
for the third line.
We also used
Kato’s inequality
(cf.
[
See84
, Theorem 2]
)

-\mathcal{A}^{*}_{0}|u|\geq{\rm{sgn}}(u)(-{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{0}u)

for the fourth line.

We note here that
(a2)
implies

(\Delta+b)\chi_{m}(x)=0,\quad x\in K

for all
m\geq m_{0}(K)
. Then by
(a3)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u(x)|(\Delta+b)\chi_{m}(x)\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)\leq C\int_{K^{c}}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}.

(4.7)

Combining (
4.4
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
) with (
4.7
),
we obtain

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\int_{M}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{K^{c}}|u|{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}\leq C\varepsilon\int_{M}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}},

that is,

0\leq\left\{C\varepsilon-(1-\varepsilon)\right\}\int_{M}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}.

We now take
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
Then
u
must satisfy

\int_{M}|u|\,{\rm{d}}{\mathfrak{m}}=0,

which implies
u=0
. Hence
we complete the proof.
∎

Example 4.4

4.1
Manifolds with an empty
cut-locus

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
M
be a non-compact manifold with an empty cut-locus.
Suppose that there exist some base point
o\in M

and a positive non-increasing smooth function

\kappa=\kappa(r):(0,\infty)\to(0,1]

such that

\sup_{r>0}|\kappa^{\prime}(r)|<\infty,\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa(r)\,{\rm{d}}r=\infty

(4.11)

and
the radial function
r(x)=d(o,x)
satisfies

\kappa(r(x))\big(\Delta r(x)+br(x)\big)\geq-C\int_{0}^{r(x)}\kappa(r)\,{\rm{d}}r,\quad x\in M\setminus\{o\}

(4.12)

for some positive constant
C
.
Then condition
(A)
holds.

Proof.

We first put
\gamma=4
, for instance, such that the function
\phi
satisfies

-1\leq\phi^{\prime}(u)\leq 0,\quad|\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)|\leq 1,\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.13)

We define a sequence of smooth cut-off functions

\{\chi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
by

\chi_{m}(x):=\phi\Big(\frac{h(r(x))}{m}\Big),\quad x\in M,~m\in\mathbb{N},

where

h(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\kappa(s){\rm{d}}s,\quad r\geq 0.

We should remark here that (
4.11
) implies
h(r)\nearrow\infty
as
r\to\infty
.
Since we easily see

\chi_{m}(x)=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle{1}}&\text{~if $\displaystyle{r(x)\leq h^{-1}(m)}$},\\
{\displaystyle{0}}&\text{~if $\displaystyle{r(x)\geq h^{-1}(4m)}$},\end{cases}

it is sufficient to check (
4.1
)
for all
h^{-1}(m)\leq r(x)\leq h^{-1}(4m)
.
Combining a direct calculation with
(
4.13
),
0<\kappa\leq 1
, the basic Lipschitz estimate
|\nabla r(x)|_{T_{x}M}\leq 1

and condition (
4.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\chi_{m}(x)+b\chi_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa(r(x))^{2}}{m^{2}}\phi^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{h(r(x))}{m}\Big)|\nabla{r}(x)|^{2}_{T_{x}M}+\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(r(x))}{m}\phi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{h(r(x))}{m}\Big)|\nabla{r}(x)|^{2}_{T_{x}M}

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~+\frac{\kappa(r(x))}{m}\phi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{h(r(x))}{m}\Big)\Delta r(x)+\frac{\kappa(r(x))}{m}\phi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{h(r(x))}{m}\Big)br(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}+\Big(\sup_{r\geq R}|\kappa^{\prime}(r)|\Big)-\frac{1}{m}\big\{\kappa(r(x))\cdot\big(\Delta r(x)+br(x)\big)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\frac{h(r(x))}{m}\Big)\leq 5C,

where we used
h(r(x))\leq 4m
for the final inequality.
Thus we have shown
(a3)
, and Proposition
4.1
leads us
to the desired condition
(A)
. This completes the proof.
∎

the case where

, that is,
the exponential map
\exp:T_{o}M\rightarrow M
is a diffeomorphism.
In this case, we have the following criterion for condition

.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume that
M
has a pole
o\in M
and there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\mathrm{Ric}(x)\geq-C(1+r(x)^{2}),\quad x\in M,

(4.14)

where
r(x):=d(o,x)
is the radial function from the pole

.
Furthermore assume that there exists a positive constant
C

such that

\kappa(r(x))br(x)\geq-C\int_{0}^{r(x)}\kappa(r){\rm{d}}r,\quad x\in M\setminus\{o\},

(4.15)

where the function
\kappa
is introduced in Proposition

4.5
.
Then condition
(A)
holds.

Proof.

By
[
See84
, Appendix]
,
the
reverse
Laplacian comparison theorem

\Delta r(x)\geq-Cr(x),\quad x\in M\setminus\{o\}

(4.16)

holds for some positive constant

. Since the function
\kappa:(0,\infty)\to(0,1]

is non-decreasing and smooth, it follows from (

) that

\kappa(r(x))\Delta r(x)\geq-C\kappa(r(x))r(x)\geq-C\int_{0}^{r(x)}\kappa(r){\rm{d}}r.

Combining this estimate with (
4.15
), we have
(
4.12
). Thus we may apply

4.5
to complete the proof.
∎

Example 4.7
.

If

)

4.2
Manifolds with variable lower Ricci curvature bounds

We consider the case where the Ricci curvature is bounded from below
by a (possibly unbounded) nonpositive function of the radial function

r(x):=d(o,x)
from some base point
o\in M
.
Throughout this subsection, we assume (
4.14
).
Thanks to
[
BS18
, Theorem 2.1]
, there exist a smooth exhaustion
function
{\mathfrak{r}}:M\to[0,\infty)
and constants
0<D_{1}<D_{2}

and
D_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle D_{1}r(x)^{2}\leq{\mathfrak{r}}(x)\leq D_{2}\max\{1,r(x)^{2}\},\quad x\in M,

(4.17)

\displaystyle|\nabla{\mathfrak{r}}(x)|\leq D_{3}r(x),\quad x\in M\setminus{\overline{B_{1}(o)}},

(4.18)

\displaystyle|\Delta{\mathfrak{r}}(x)|\leq D_{3}r(x)^{2},\quad x\in M\setminus{\overline{B_{1}(o)}}.

(4.19)

Corollary 4.8
.

Assume
(
4.14
)
for some base point
o\in M
.
Furthermore assume that there exist
two constants
C>0

and
R\geq 1

such that

b{\mathfrak{r}}(x)\geq-Cr(x)^{2},\quad x\in M\setminus{\overline{B_{R}(o)}}.

(4.20)

Then condition
(A)
holds. In particular, if
the vector field
b
satisfies

|b(x)|_{T_{x}M}\leq C\big(1+r(x)\big),\quad x\in M

(4.21)

for some
C>0
,

(
4.20
)
and hence
condition
(A)
hold.

Proof.

We put
\gamma=D_{2}/D_{1}
and define a sequence of smooth cut-off functions

\{\chi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
by

\chi_{m}(x):=\phi\Big(\frac{{\mathfrak{r}}(x)}{D_{1}m^{2}}\Big),\quad x\in M,~m\in\mathbb{N}.

Recalling (
4.17
) and noting
\gamma>1
, we easily see

\chi_{m}(x)=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle{1}}&\text{~if $\displaystyle{r(x)\leq\gamma^{-1/2}m}$},\\
{\displaystyle{0}}&\text{~if $\displaystyle{r(x)\geq\gamma^{1/2}m}$},\end{cases}

and thus
\Delta\chi_{m}(x)+b\chi_{m}(x)=0
holds for
r(x)<\gamma^{-1/2}m
and

r(x)>\gamma^{1/2}m
.

Hence it is sufficient to check condition
(a3)
for

\gamma^{-1/2}m\leq r(x)\leq\gamma^{1/2}m
.
Combining a direct calculation with (
4.10
), (
4.18
), (
4.19
)
and (
4.20
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta\chi_{m}(x)+b\chi_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{1}^{2}m^{4}}\phi^{\prime\prime}\Big(\frac{{\mathfrak{r}}(x)}{D_{1}m^{2}}\Big)|\nabla{\mathfrak{r}}(x)|^{2}_{T_{x}M}+\frac{1}{D_{1}m^{2}}\phi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{{\mathfrak{r}}(x)}{D_{1}m^{2}}\Big)\Delta{\mathfrak{r}}(x)

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~+\frac{1}{D_{1}m^{2}}\phi^{\prime}\Big(\frac{{\mathfrak{r}}(x)}{D_{1}m^{2}}\Big)b{\mathfrak{r}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8D_{3}^{2}}{(D_{2}-D_{1})^{2}m^{4}}r(x)^{2}+\frac{2D_{3}}{(D_{2}-D_{1})m^{2}}r(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{ }~~+\frac{2C}{(D_{2}-D_{1})m^{2}}r(x)^{2}.

(4.22)

Since

, the right-hand side (

)
is bounded from above by a positive constant
C
independent of
m
.
It means that we have shown
(a3)
, and thus Proposition
4.1
leads us
to the desired condition
(A)
.

Besides, it follows from
(
4.18
) and (
4.21
) that

\displaystyle b{\mathfrak{r}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq-|b(x)|_{T_{x}M}|\nabla{\mathfrak{r}}(x)|_{T_{x}M}

\displaystyle\geq-C(1+r(x))D_{3}r(x)

\displaystyle\geq-CD_{3}(r(x)+r(x))r(x)=-Cr(x)^{2},\quad x\in M\setminus{\overline{B_{1}(o)}}.

Thus we have shown (
4.20
) with
R=1
. This completes the proof.
∎

,

(B)
and

.

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial b^{i}}{\partial x^{(i)}}(x)\geq-\gamma,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

c^{i}_{1}(x),\ldots,c^{i}_{k_{i}}(x)\geq 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and

\big|(c^{1}_{0}(x),\ldots,c^{n}_{0}(x))\big|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq C^{\prime},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with some positive constant
C^{\prime}
, respectively.

Next, we find condition
(A)
by applying Proposition
4.5
.
In this case, the condition (
4.12
) with
\kappa\equiv 1
is rewritten by

\sum_{i=1}^{n}b^{i}(x)x^{(i)}\geq-Cr(x)^{2}-(n-1),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(5.4)

For example, we also consider the vector field
b
given by (
5.3
).
By a direct calculation, we easily see that (
5.3
) satisfies
(
5.4
) provided that

\big|(c_{0}^{1}(x),\ldots,c_{0}^{n}(x))\big|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leq C^{\prime}r(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and

c^{i}_{1}(x)\geq-C^{\prime},\quad c^{i}_{2}(x),\ldots c^{i}_{k_{i}}(x)\geq 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,~~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with some
positive constant
C^{\prime}
.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is regarded as a model manifold, other examples of the vector field
b
satisfying

(A)
,

(A1)
p

and

(A2)
p

can be found in Section
5.2.4
below.

5.2
Model manifolds

5.2.1
Basic facts for model manifolds

For
r_{0}\in(0,+\infty]
and a smooth positive function
\psi=\psi(r)
on
(0,r_{0})
,
let
(M,g_{\psi})
be an
n
-dimensional
model manifold
with weight
\psi
, that is,
there exists one chart on
M
that covers all of
M
and the image of this chart in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

B^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(r_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~;~|x|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}<r_{0}\}
.
The metric
g_{\psi}
in the polar coordinates
(r,\theta)=(r,\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{n-1})
in the above chart
has the form

g_{\psi}(r,\theta)={\rm{d}}r^{2}+\psi(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\theta),

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
is the standard Riemannian metric
on the
(n-1)
-dimensional unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which has the form

g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\theta)=\gamma_{ij}(\theta){\rm{d}}\theta^{i}{\rm{d}}\theta^{j}.

To avoid the singularity at the origin
o
, we always assume

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\psi(r)=0,\quad\lim_{r\searrow 0}\psi^{\prime}(r)=1,\quad\lim_{r\searrow 0}\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)=0.

Moreover, when
r_{0}<\infty
, we assume

\lim_{r\nearrow r_{0}}\psi(r)=0,\quad\lim_{r\nearrow r_{0}}\psi^{\prime}(r)=-1,\quad\lim_{r\nearrow r_{0}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)=0

to ensure the completeness and non-singularity
of the manifold.
Some of typical manifolds can be regarded as a model manifold. Indeed,

•

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
r_{0}=\infty
and
\psi(r)=r
;

•

{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
(without a pole) with
r_{0}=\pi

and
\psi(r)=\sin r
,
0<r<\pi
;

•

\mathbb{H}^{n}
with
r_{0}=\infty
and
\psi(r)=\sinh r

are model manifolds.
We refer to
[
Gri09
, Section 3.10]
for the precise definition and basic results of model manifolds.

On a model manifold
M
, the Riemannian volume measure
\mathfrak{m}
is
given in the polar coordinates by

{\rm{d}}\mathfrak{m}=\psi(r)^{n-1}{\rm{d}}r{\rm{d}}\theta,

where
{\rm{d}}\theta
stands for the Riemannian volume measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
The Laplacian has the form

\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{\psi(r)^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

In particular,

\Delta r=(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}.

5.2.2
Ricci curvature on model manifolds

When
n
=2, we can compute the Ricci curvature on a model manifold by using the weight function
\psi
explicitly.
Indeed, the Christoffel symbol
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
can be given by

\Gamma_{ij}^{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-\psi(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)&\mbox{ if }(i,j)=(\theta,\theta)\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.,~\Gamma_{ij}^{\theta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}&\mbox{ if }(i,j)=(r,\theta)\mbox{ or }(\theta,r)\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Since the component of Ricci curvature tensor
R_{ij}
is given by

R_{ij}=\partial_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}-\partial_{j}\Gamma_{ik}^{k}+\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\Gamma_{km}^{m}-\Gamma_{im}^{k}\Gamma_{jk}^{m},

we obtain

(R_{ij})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}&0\\
0&-\psi(r)\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)\end{array}\right).

Hence, we obtain

\mathrm{Ric}\geq-\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}.

(5.5)

When
\psi
is polynomial, that is, for some positive constant
C_{1}
and for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

\psi(r)=C_{1}r^{\alpha}

for all large
r>0
, (
5.5
) implies that

\mathrm{Ric}\geq-\alpha(\alpha-1)r^{-2}

for all large
r>0
.

When
\psi
is exponential, that is, for some positive constant
C_{1}
and some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\psi(r)=C_{1}\exp(\alpha r^{\beta})

for all large
r>0
, (
5.5
) implies that

\mathrm{Ric}\geq-\alpha\beta r^{\beta-2}\big((\beta-1)+\alpha\beta r^{\beta}\big).

In particular, if
\alpha>0
and
\beta>2
, then

\mathrm{Ric}\geq-C^{\prime}r^{2\beta-2}

for some
C^{\prime}>0
and all large
r>0
, which fails the variable lower Ricci bound (
4.14
).

5.2.3
Partition of model manifolds

In this section, we give an example of the partition of the model manifold
(M,g_{\psi})
satisfying
conditions
(B)
and

(C)
.
First of all, let us construct a partition of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
by induction in dimension.
We first consider the case
n=2
, that is,
\mathbb{S}^{1}\simeq[0,2\pi)
. For any
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we take
a partition consisting of
K
-pieces given by

\left[0,\frac{2\pi}{K}\right),~\left[\frac{2\pi}{K},2\frac{2\pi}{K}\right),\cdots,\left[(K-1)\frac{2\pi}{K},2\pi\right).

(5.6)

Next, as a partition of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, take a
spherical suspension
(cf.
[
BBI01
, Section 3.6.3]
)
of each piece in (
5.6
)
as a subset of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and decompose it with same interval
\frac{\pi}{K}

in the extended angle. This partition is nothing but a partition of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
by
K

longitude lines
and
K
latitude lines.
Noting that for any piece of this partition
X\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

\mathrm{diam}(X)\leq\frac{3\pi}{K},\quad\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(X)\leq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{K^{2}}.

we repeat this procedure to construct a partition of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Then the given partition
\mathbb{Y}_{K}

consisting of
K^{n-1}
elements satisfies for any
Y\in\mathbb{Y}_{K}

\mathrm{diam}(Y)\leq\frac{n\pi}{K},\quad\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(Y)\leq 2\left(\frac{\pi}{K}\right)^{n-1}.

Now we construct a partition of a model manifold
M=(M,g_{\psi})
as follows:
Let
\Pi:M\backslash\{o\}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be the canonical projection, that is,

\Pi(r,\theta)=\theta,\quad(r,\theta)\in M\backslash\{o\}.

For
l,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

A_{l}(m)=B_{\frac{m}{l}}(o)\backslash B_{\frac{m-1}{l}}(o),\quad\psi_{l}(m)=\max_{\frac{m-1}{l}\leq r\leq\frac{m}{l}}\psi(r).

(Note that
A_{l}(m)=\emptyset
if
r_{0}<\infty
and
\frac{m-1}{l}\geq r_{0}
.)

Next we define a partition
\{X_{k}^{l,m}\}_{k=1,\ldots,K^{n-1}}
of
A_{l}(m)
by

X_{k}^{l,m}=\Pi^{-1}(Y_{k})\cap A_{l}(m),\quad k=1,\ldots,K^{n-1}.

We note that

\mathrm{diam}(X^{l,m}_{k})\leq\frac{1}{l}+\mathrm{diam}(Y_{k})\psi_{l}(m)\leq\frac{1}{l}+\frac{n\pi}{K}\psi_{l}(m)

and

\mathfrak{m}(X_{k,l,m})\leq\frac{1}{l}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(Y_{k})\psi_{l}(m)^{n-1}\leq\frac{2}{l}\left(\frac{\pi}{K}\right)^{n-1}\psi_{l}(m)^{n-1}.

Now choose
K=K(l,m):=\lceil l\psi_{l}(m)n\pi\rceil
. Then for all
k=1,\ldots,K(l,m)^{n-1}
and

l,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\mathrm{diam}(X^{l,m}_{k})\leq\frac{2}{l},\quad\mathfrak{m}(X^{l,m}_{k})\leq\frac{2}{n^{n-1}l^{n}}.

Consequently, for each
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{X}_{l}:=\{X_{k}^{l,m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N},k=1,\ldots,K(l,m)}
is the partition of
M
.
It is easy to check conditions
(B)
and
(C)
.

5.2.4
A sufficient condition for
(A)
,

(A1)
p
and

(A2)
p

In this section, we study a sufficient condition on a vector field
b
on the non-compact model manifold

(M,g_{\psi})
for conditions
(
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.12
) with
\kappa\equiv 1
.
For a smooth vector field
b=b(r,\theta)
on
M
, let
b^{r},b^{\theta}_{1},\ldots,b^{\theta}_{n-1}

be the coefficients of
b
in the polar coordinates
(r,\theta^{(1)},\ldots,\theta^{(n-1)})
. Namely, they satisfy

b(r,\theta)=b^{r}(r,\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+b^{\theta}_{1}(r,\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{(1)}}+\cdots+b^{\theta}_{n-1}(r,\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{(n-1)}}.

Then conditions
(
4.8
) and
(
4.9
)
are rewritten by

\displaystyle(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}b^{r}(r,\theta)+\frac{\partial b^{r}}{\partial r}(r,\theta)+\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(b^{\theta}(r,\theta))\geq-\gamma

and
b^{r}(r,\theta)\geq-C
, respectively, where

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(b^{\theta}(r,\theta))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(\gamma)}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{(i)}}\left(\sqrt{\det(\gamma)}b^{\theta}_{i}(r,\theta)\right).

Also, condition (
4.12
) with
\kappa\equiv 1
is rewritten by

b^{r}(r,\theta)+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\geq-Cr.

We now study a sufficient condition of
b
in the cases where

\psi
are polynomial and exponential separately.

(I) Polynomial case:
For a positive constant
C_{1}
and some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, suppose

\psi(r)=C_{1}r^{\alpha}

for all large
r
.
In this case,
for the vector filed
b
satisfying (
4.8
), assume that

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(b^{\theta}(r,\theta))\geq-C^{\prime}

(5.7)

for some constant
C^{\prime}
. For example, this condition is true if
b^{\theta}_{1},\ldots,b^{\theta}_{n-1}

are constants for all large
r>0
. Then the condition (
4.8
) is rewritten by

(n-1)\frac{\alpha}{r}b^{r}+\frac{\partial b^{r}}{\partial r}\geq C^{\prime}-\gamma=:-\gamma^{\prime}.

(5.8)

For example, we consider the case

b^{r}(r,\theta)=c_{0}(\theta)+c_{1}(\theta)r+\cdots+c_{k}(\theta)r^{k},

(5.9)

where

c_{0},\ldots,c_{k}
are smooth functions on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Then conditions (
5.7
) and

\left((n-1)\alpha+l\right)c_{l}(\theta)\geq 0,\quad\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\quad l=2,\ldots,k

imply (
5.8
) with some
\gamma^{\prime}
. Hence condition

(A1)
p

holds.
Moreover, if
c_{k}
in (
5.9
) is positive,
then
b
satisfies also (
4.9
) with some constant
C
, which implies condition

(A2)
p
.

On the other hand, condition (
4.12
) is rewritten by

b^{r}(r,\theta)\geq-Cr-(n-1)\frac{\alpha}{r}.

For example,
if

b^{r}(r,\theta)\geq-C^{\prime}r

(5.10)

for some
C^{\prime}>0
,
then
b
satisfies condition (
4.12
), whence the condition
(A)
holds.

(II) Exponential case:

For a positive constant
C_{1}
and some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, suppose

\psi(r)=C_{1}\exp(\alpha r^{\beta})

for all large
r
.
To find a vector field
b
satisfying

(A1)
p

and

(A2)
p
, we assume (
5.7
).
Then condition (
4.8
) is rewritten by

(n-1)\alpha\beta r^{\beta-1}b^{r}+\frac{\partial b^{r}}{\partial r}\geq-\gamma^{\prime}.

(5.11)

For example, a vector filed
b
with (
5.7
) and
b^{r}(r,\theta)=c(\theta)r
with a smooth function
c(\theta)
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

satisfying

c(\theta)\alpha\beta\geq 0,\quad\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

satisfies (
5.11
), and thus condition

(A1)
p

holds.
Moreover, if
c(\theta)
is positive, then
b
satisfies also (
4.9
), which implies condition

(A2)
p
.

The condition (
4.12
) can be rewritten by

b^{r}(r,\theta)\geq-Cr-(n-1)\alpha\beta r^{\beta-1}.

(5.12)

If
\beta\leq 2
, the leading term in the right-hand side of (
5.12
) is
-Cr
. Hence, for example,
if

b^{r}(r,\theta)\geq-C^{\prime}r,

(5.13)

then
b
satisfies the condition (
4.12
)
for some positive constant
C^{\prime}
and then the condition
(A)
holds.

If
\beta>2
, then the leading term in the right-hand side of (
5.12
) is

-(n-1)\alpha\beta r^{\beta-1}
. Hence, for example,
b
satisfies the condition (
4.12
)
if

b^{r}(r,\theta)\geq-(n-1)\alpha\beta r^{\beta-1}.

(5.14)

In particular, the case where
n=2
,
\alpha>0
and
\beta>2
, the vector filed
b

with (
5.7
) and (
5.14
) gives a new example of

\mathcal{A}
with
(A)
without the variable lower Ricci bound (

).

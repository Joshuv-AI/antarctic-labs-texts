Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras: Basics, Constructions and related structures
Taoufik Chtioui , Sami Mabrouk , Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. University of Gabes, Faculty of Sciences Gabes, City Riadh 6072 Zrig, Gabes, Tunisia.2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. Université de Haute Alsace, IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques,18, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
The main purpose of this paper is to introduce and investigate the notion of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. They are a generalization of Jacobi-Jordan algebras which correspond to the case in which the formal parameter λ \lambda equals 0 0 . We consider some related structures such as conformal modules, corresponding representations and 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators. Therefore, conformal derivations from Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras to their conformal modules are used to describe conformal derivations of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras of semidirect product type. Moreover, we study a class of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras called quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras, which are characterized by mock-Gel’fand Dorfman bialgebras. Finally, the ℂ [ ∂ ] \mathbb{C}[\partial] -split extending structures problem for Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras is studied. Furthermore, we introduce an unified product of a given Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra J J and a given ℂ [ ∂ ] \mathbb{C}[\partial] -module K K . This product includes some other interesting products of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras such as twisted product or crossed product. Using this product, a cohomological type object is constructed to provide a theoretical answer to the ℂ [ ∂ ] \mathbb{C}[\partial] -split extending structures problem.



Keywords
: Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra, conformal module, mock-Gel’fand-Dorfman bialgebra, unified product, extending structure.

Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras and their modules

In this section, we provide some basic notions of (left) anti-associative and Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras along with their conformal modules. We introduce the notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operator on Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras with respect a given module as a generalization of Rota-Baxter operators.

2.1
Definitions and Basic results

A conformal algebra is a vector space
A
equipped with a linear map
\partial:A\to A
and a countable family of bilinear operations
-_{(n)}-:A\times A\to A,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

For every
a,b\in A
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
a_{(n)}b=0
for all
n\geq N
,

(ii)

\partial a_{(n)}b=-na_{(n-1)}b
,

(iii)

a_{(n)}\partial b=\partial(a_{(n)}b)+na_{(n-1)}b
.

Every conformal algebra
A
is a left module over the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
.
The structure of a conformal algebra on an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module
A
may be expressed by means of a single polynomial-valued map called
\lambda
-product

-_{\lambda}-:A\times A\to A[\lambda],\quad a_{\lambda}b=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\lambda^{(n)}a_{(n)}b,

where
\lambda
is a formal variable and
\lambda^{(n)}=\lambda^{n}/n!
. In terms of the
\lambda
-product, the definition of a conformal algebra is as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A conformal algebra is a module
A
over the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{F}[\partial]

equipped with a polynomial-valued
\mathbb{F}
-linear operation

A\times A\to A[\lambda]
denoted by
a\times b\to a_{\lambda}b
,
where
\lambda
is a formal variable (this operation is called
\lambda
-product), satisfying the
following axioms

\displaystyle\partial a_{\lambda}b=-\lambda a_{\lambda}b,\ \ a_{\lambda}\partial b=(\lambda+\partial)a_{\lambda}b,\hskip 56.9055pt(\textrm{Conformal sesquilinearity}).

(2.1)

A conformal algebra is said to be finite if it is finitely generated as a module
over
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
.
The rank of a finite conformal algebra
A
is its rank as a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module.

Let
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a conformal algebra. Then by conformal sesquilinearity, the following equalities hold true

\displaystyle(a_{-\lambda-\partial}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c=

\displaystyle(a_{\mu}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c,

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(b_{-\lambda-\partial}c)=

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(b_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}c),

(2.3)

\displaystyle(a_{-\mu-\partial}b)_{-\lambda-\partial}c=

\displaystyle(a_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}b)_{-\lambda-\partial}c

(2.4)

for all
a,b,c\in A
.

We prove the first identity by the following computation.

\displaystyle(a_{-\lambda-\partial}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-\lambda-\partial)^{k}}{k!}a_{(k)}b\bigg)_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-\lambda+\lambda+\mu)^{k}}{k!}((a_{(k)}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c)\quad\textrm{(by conformal sesquilinearity) }

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\mu^{k}}{k!}a_{(k)}b\bigg)_{\lambda+\mu}c=(a_{\mu}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c.

Let
M,N
be
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-modules. A conformal linear map from
M
to
N
is a sequence
f=\{f_{(n)}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
of
f_{(n)}\in Hom_{\mathbb{F}}(M,N)
satisfying

\displaystyle\partial f_{(n)}-f_{(n)}\partial=-nf_{(n-1)},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,
f_{(0)}
is an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphism. Set
f_{\lambda}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}f_{(n)}
. Then
f=\{f_{(n)}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
is a conformal linear map if and only if
f_{\lambda}:M\to N[\lambda]
is
\mathbb{F}
-linear and
f_{\lambda}\partial=(\lambda+\partial)f_{\lambda}
.

We denote by
Chom(M,N)
the set of conformal linear maps from
M
to
N
. It turns out that

Chom(M,N)
is a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module via:

\partial f_{(n)}=-nf_{(n-1)},\quad\text{equivalently},\quad\partial f_{\lambda}=-\lambda f_{\lambda}.

The composition
fg:L\to N
of conformal linear maps
f:M\to N
and
g:L\to M

is given by

\displaystyle(fg)_{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}n\\
k\\
\end{pmatrix}f_{(k)}g_{(n-k)},\quad\text{equivalently},\quad(f_{\lambda}g)_{\lambda+\mu}=f_{\lambda}g_{\mu}.

If
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module, then
Cend(M):=Chom(M,M)
is an
associative conformal algebra with respect to the above composition. Thus,
Cend(M)
becomes a Lie conformal algebra, denoted as
gc(M)
,
with respect to the following
\lambda
-bracket:

\displaystyle[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}=f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda},\quad\text{equivalently},\quad[f_{\lambda}g]=f_{\lambda}g-g_{-\lambda-\partial}f.

Define the conformal dual of an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module
M
as
M^{*c}=Chom(M,\mathbb{F})
, that is

M^{*c}=\{f:M\to\mathbb{F}[\lambda]\;|\;f\ \textrm{is }\ \mathbb{F}\textrm{-linear and}\ f_{\lambda}(\partial m)=\lambda f_{\lambda}(m)\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f,g\in Cend(M)
, then for any
m\in M
we have

(1)

f_{\lambda}(g_{-\mu-\partial}m)=(f_{\lambda}g)_{-\mu-\partial}m
,

(2)

f_{-\lambda-\partial}(g_{\mu}m)=(f_{-\mu-\partial}g)_{-\lambda-\partial+\mu}m
,

(3)

f_{-\lambda-\partial}(g_{\mu-\partial}m)=(f_{-\lambda-\partial+\mu}g)_{-\mu-\partial}m
.

The proof follows from a straightforward computation.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a conformal algebra. A conformal linear map
d\in Cend(A)
is a conformal anti-derivation on
A
if for all
a,b\in A
,

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}(a_{\mu}b)=-(d_{\lambda}a)_{\lambda+\mu}b-a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}b).

(2.5)

Now, we introduce the notion of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. It generalizes Jacobi-Jordan algebras, which correspond to
\lambda=0
. On the other hand, this variety can be viewed as as a variety of commutative Leibniz conformal algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

A Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra is a conformal algebra
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
such that
the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}b=b_{-\lambda-\partial}a,

(2.6)

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)+(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c+b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)=0,

(2.7)

for any
a,b,c\in A
.

Remark 2.1
.

If we take in the above definition,
\lambda=\mu=0
and
\partial=0
, then we recover the definition of a Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

We consider a conformal linear map
d_{\lambda}:A\to Cend(A)
defined by
d_{\lambda}(a)(b)=a_{\lambda}b
. Then, Eq. (
2.7
) is equivalent to say that
d_{\lambda}(a)
is a conformal anti-derivation, for any
a\in A
.

Example 2.2
.

Consider one of the simplest (through important) examples of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras.
Let
(A,\cdot)
be a Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Then we naturally define a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra
\textrm{Cur}A=\mathbb{F}[\partial]\otimes A
with the
\lambda
-product

a_{\lambda}b=a\cdot b,~~~\forall a,b\in A.

\textrm{Cur}A
is called the current Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra associated with
A
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
A
be an associative commutative algebra and
(B,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. Then the tensor product
A\otimes B
becomes a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra via

\displaystyle\partial(a\otimes x)=a\otimes\partial x,~~~(a\otimes x)_{\lambda}(b\otimes y)=(ab)\otimes(x_{\lambda}y).

Definition 2.4
.

An anti-associative conformal algebra is a conformal algebra
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
satisfying

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c+a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)=0,

(2.8)

for all
a,b,c\in A
.

Similarly as left symmetric conformal algebras are closely related to Lie conformal algebras, we introduce the notion of left anti-symmetric conformal algebras whose commutator gives rise to a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

A conformal algebra
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
is called left anti-symmetric conformal algebra if for all
a,b,c\in A
, one has

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c+a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)+(b_{\mu}a)_{\lambda+\mu}c+b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)=0.

(2.9)

It is obvious that every anti-associative conformal algebra is left anti-symmetric.
A conformal algebra
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
is said to be Jacobi-Jordan admissible if
(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra, where
a\circ_{\lambda}b=a_{\lambda}b+b_{-\lambda-\partial}a
, for all
a,b\in A
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a left anti-symmetric conformal algebra.
Then
A
is a Jacobi-Jordan admissible conformal algebra.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in A
. Then
b\circ_{-\lambda-\partial}a=b_{-\lambda-\partial}a+a_{\lambda}b=a\circ_{\lambda}b
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\mu}c)+(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c+b\circ_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c+c_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(a_{\lambda}b+b_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}c+b\circ_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c+c_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c+c_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(b_{\mu}c+c_{-\mu-\partial}b)_{-\lambda-\partial}a+(a_{\lambda}b+b_{-\lambda-\partial}a)_{\lambda+\mu}c

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b+b_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c+c_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(a_{\lambda}c+c_{-\lambda-\partial}a)_{-\mu-\partial}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c+a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)+(b_{-\lambda-\partial}a)_{\lambda+\mu}c+b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}c)_{-\mu-\partial}b+a_{\lambda}(c_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(c_{-\lambda-\partial}a)_{-\mu-\partial}b+c_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Then
(A,\circ_{\lambda})
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Any anti-associative conformal algebra is a Jacobi-Jordan admissible conformal algebra.

Similarly, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Any Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra is Jacobi-Jordan admissible.

Definition 2.6

An
\mathbb{F}[\partial]

M

a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra

\pi:A\to Cend(M)

\displaystyle\pi(\partial a)_{\lambda}=-\lambda\pi(a)_{\lambda},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\pi(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}=-\pi(a)_{\lambda}\pi(b)_{\mu}-\pi(b)_{\mu}\pi(a)_{\lambda}.

(2.11)

Proposition 2.7
.

Given a finite dimensional complex Jacobi-Jordan algebra
A
. Let
\rho:A\to End(M)
be a finite dimensional representation of
A
. Then the free
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes M
is a conformal module of
Cur\ A
, where the module structure
\pi:Cur\ A\to Cend(\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes M)
is given by

\displaystyle\pi(f(\partial)\otimes a)_{\lambda}(g(\partial)\otimes m)=f(-\lambda)g(\lambda+\partial)\otimes(\rho(a)m).

Proof.

For any
a,b\in A,m\in M
and
f(\partial),g(\partial),h(\partial)\in\mathbb{F}[\partial]
, we have

\displaystyle\pi(f(\partial)a)_{\lambda}\pi(g(\partial)b)_{\mu}(h(\partial)m)=

\displaystyle\pi(f(\partial)a)_{\lambda}\Big(g(-\mu)h(\partial+\mu)\rho(b)m\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(-\lambda)g(-\mu)h(\partial+\lambda+\mu)\rho(a)\rho(b)m,

and

\displaystyle\pi(g(\partial)b)_{\mu}\pi(f(\partial)a)_{\lambda}(h(\partial)m)=

\displaystyle\pi(g(\partial)b)_{\mu}\Big(f(-\lambda)h(\partial+\lambda)\rho(a)m\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(-\mu)f(-\lambda)h(\partial+\lambda+\mu)\rho(b)\rho(a)m.

Then

\displaystyle\pi(f(\partial)a_{\lambda}g(\partial)b)_{\lambda+\mu}(h(\partial)m)=

\displaystyle\pi(f(-\lambda)g(\partial+\lambda)ab)_{\lambda+\mu}(h(\partial)m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(-\lambda)g(-\mu)h(\lambda+\mu+\partial)\rho(ab)m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(-\lambda)g(-\mu)h(\lambda+\mu+\partial)\big(\rho(a)\rho(b)m+\rho(b)\rho(a)m\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\pi(f(\partial)a)_{\lambda}\pi(g(\partial)b)_{\mu}(h(\partial)m)-\pi(g(\partial)b)_{\mu}\pi(f(\partial)a)_{\lambda}(h(\partial)m).

Hence the proof.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra,
M
an
A
-conformal module and
\pi:A\to\mathbb{F}[\partial]\otimes Cend(M):\ r\mapsto\pi(r)_{\lambda}
the corresponding representation. Define the following
\lambda
-product on
A\oplus M
:

\displaystyle(a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n)=a_{\lambda}b+\pi(a)_{\lambda}n+\pi(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}m.

(2.12)

Then
A\oplus M
becomes a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra with respect to the above
\lambda
-product,
called the semidirect product of
A
and
M
, and denoted as
A\ltimes M

M
is an abelian ideal of

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle\partial(a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n)=

\displaystyle\partial a_{\lambda}b+\pi(\partial a)_{\lambda}n+\pi(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}\partial m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda(a_{\lambda}b+\pi(a)_{\lambda}n+\pi(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}m)=-\lambda(a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n).

Using a similar computation, one can easily check that

\displaystyle(a+m)_{\lambda}\partial(b+n)=(\lambda+\partial)(a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n).

Moreover,

\displaystyle(b+n)_{-\lambda-\partial}(a+m)=(b_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\pi(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}m+\pi(a)_{\lambda}m)=(a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n).

In addition,

\displaystyle(a+m)_{\lambda}\Big((b+n)_{\mu}(c+p)\Big)=

\displaystyle(a+m)_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)+\pi(b)_{\mu}p+\pi(c)_{-\mu-\partial}n

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)+\pi(a)_{\lambda}(\pi(b)_{\mu}p)+\pi(a)_{\lambda}(\pi(c)_{-\mu-\partial}n)+\pi(b_{\mu}c)_{-\lambda-\partial}m),

\displaystyle\Big((a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n)\Big)_{\lambda+\mu}(c+p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b+\pi(a)_{\lambda}n+\pi(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}m)_{\lambda+\mu}(c+p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big((a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c+\pi(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}p+\pi(c)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(\pi(a)_{\lambda}n)+\pi(c)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(\pi(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}m)\big)

Then

\displaystyle(a+m)_{\lambda}\Big((b+n)_{\mu}(c+p)\Big)+\Big((a+m)_{\lambda}(b+n)\Big)_{\lambda+\mu}(c+p)+(b+n)_{\mu}\Big((a+m)_{\lambda}(c+p)\Big)

\displaystyle Y=\pi(a)_{\lambda}(\pi(c)_{-\mu-\partial}n)+\pi(c)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(\pi(a)_{\lambda}n)+\pi(a_{\lambda}c)_{-\mu-\partial}n,

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a finite dimensional representation of a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})

\pi^{*}
be an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphism from

\displaystyle(\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f)_{\mu}m=f_{\mu-\lambda}(\pi(a)_{\lambda}m),\ \forall a\in A,f\in M^{*c},m\in M.

(2.13)

Then
(M^{*c},\pi^{*})
is a representation of

.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in A
,
m\in M
and
f\in M^{*c}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\big(\pi^{*}(\partial a)_{\lambda}f\big)_{\mu}m=f_{\mu-\lambda}\big(\pi(\partial a)_{\lambda}m\big)=-\lambda f_{\mu-\lambda}\big(\pi(a)_{\lambda}m\big)=-\lambda\big(\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f\big)_{\mu}m.

Then (
2.10
) holds. To prove (
2.11

\displaystyle\big(\pi^{*}(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}f\big)_{\nu}m=

\displaystyle f_{\nu-\lambda-\mu}\big(\pi(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}m\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{\nu-\lambda-\mu}\big(\pi(a)_{\lambda}\pi(b)_{\mu}m\big)-f_{\nu-\lambda-\mu}\big(\pi(b)_{\mu}\pi(a)_{\lambda}m\big).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}\big(\pi^{*}(b)_{\mu}f\big)_{\nu}m=\big(\pi^{*}(b)_{\mu}f\big)_{\nu-\lambda}(\pi(a)_{\lambda}m)=f_{\nu-\lambda-\mu}\big(\pi(b)_{\mu}\pi(a)_{\lambda}m\big),

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(b)_{\mu}\big(\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f\big)_{\nu}m=\big(\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f\big)_{\nu-\mu}(\pi(b)_{\mu}m)=f_{\nu-\lambda-\mu}\big(\pi(a)_{\lambda}\pi(b)_{\mu}m\big).

Therefore

\displaystyle\big(\pi^{*}(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}f\big)_{\nu}m=-\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}\big(\pi^{*}(b)_{\mu}f\big)_{\nu}m-\pi^{*}(b)_{\mu}\big(\pi^{*}(a)_{\lambda}f\big)_{\nu}m,

which implies that
(M^{*c},\pi^{*})
is a representation of
A
.
∎

2.2
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra and
\pi:A\to Cend(M)
be a representation. An
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
T:M\to A

satisfying

\displaystyle Tu_{\lambda}Tv=T\big(\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v+\pi(Tv)_{-\lambda-\partial}u\big),\ \ \forall u,v\in M,

(2.14)

is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

If
M=A
and
\pi
is the adjoint representation, then
T
is called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on
A
denoted by
R
and the condition (
2.14
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle R(a)_{\lambda}R(b)=R\big(R(a)_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}R(b)\big),\ \ \forall a,b\in A.

(2.15)

More generally, we can extend the notion of weighted Rota-Baxter operators of Jacobi-Jordan algebras to conformal case by considering the following weighted Rota-Baxter identity:

\displaystyle R(a)_{\lambda}R(b)=R\big(R(a)_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}R(b)\big)+\alpha R(a_{\lambda}b),\ \ \forall a,b\in A.

(2.16)

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
A
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra and
\pi:A\to Cend(M)
be a representation of
A
.
Suppose
T:M\to A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated with
\pi
. Then, the
following
\lambda
-product:

\displaystyle u\ast_{\lambda}v=\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v,\ \ \forall u,v\in M,

(2.17)

endows
M
with a left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure.
Therefore,
M
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra which is the sub-adjacent Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra of this left anti-symmetric
conformal algebra, and
T:M\to A
is a homomorphism of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras.
Moreover,
\{T(M)\subset A\}
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal subalgebra of
A
and there is also a natural
left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure on
T(M)
defined as follows:

T(u)_{\lambda}T(v)=T(u\ast_{\lambda}v)=T(\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v),\ \ \forall u,v\in M.

In addition, the sub-adjacent Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra of this left anti-symmetric conformal
algebra is a subalgebra of
A
and
T:M\to A
is a homomorphism of left anti-symmetric
conformal algebras.

Proof.

For any
u,v,w\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\partial u\ast_{\lambda}v=\pi(T(\partial u))_{\lambda}v=\pi(\partial Tu)_{\lambda}v=-\lambda\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v=-\lambda u\ast_{\lambda}v,

\displaystyle u\ast_{\lambda}\partial v=\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}(\partial v)=(\partial+\lambda)\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v=(\partial+\lambda)u\ast_{\lambda}v.

Furthermore we have

\displaystyle(u\ast_{\lambda}v)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}w+u\ast_{\lambda}(v\ast_{\mu}w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}w+u\ast_{\lambda}(\pi(Tv)_{\mu}w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(T(\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}v))_{\lambda+\mu}w+\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}\pi(Tv)_{\mu}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(T(u)_{\lambda}T(v))_{\lambda+\mu}w-\pi(T(\pi(Tv)_{-\lambda-\partial}u)_{\lambda+\mu}w-\pi(T(u)_{\lambda}bT(v))_{\lambda+\mu}w-\pi(Tv)_{\mu}\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\pi(T(\pi(Tv)_{-\lambda-\partial}u)_{\lambda+\mu}w-\pi(Tv)_{\mu}\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\pi(T(\pi(Tv)_{\mu}u)_{\lambda+\mu}w-\pi(Tv)_{\mu}\pi(Tu)_{\lambda}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(v\ast_{\mu}u)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}-v\ast_{\mu}(u\ast_{\lambda}w).

Hence
(M,\ast_{\lambda})
is a left ant-symmetric conformal algebra.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
A
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra and
T:A\to A
be a Rota–Baxter
operator of weight zero. Then, there is a left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure
on
A
with the following
\lambda
-product:

a\circ_{\lambda}b=T(a)_{\lambda}b,\quad a,b\in A.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. There is a compatible left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure on
A
if and only if there exists a bijective
\mathcal{O}
-operator

T:M\to A
associated with some representation
(M,\pi)
of
A
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a bijective
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T:M\to A
of
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
associated
with a representation
(M,\pi)
.
Then by Theorem
2.10
, with a straightforward checking, we have that

a\ast_{\lambda}b=T(\pi(a)_{\lambda}T^{-1}(b)),\ \ \forall a,b\in A,

defines a compatible left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure on
A
.

Conversely, suppose that there is a compatible left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure
on

. Then the identity map

is a bijective
\mathcal{O}
-operator of

∎

2.3
Symplectic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras

Next, we introduce a definition of symplectic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras and show a relationship with left anti-symmetric conformal algebras.
First, let us recall some notations about conformal bilinear forms.

Let
V
be an
\mathbb{F}[\lambda]
-module. A conformal bilinear form on
V
is an

\mathbb{F}
-linear map
\phi_{\lambda}:V\otimes V\to\mathbb{F}[\lambda]
satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda}(\partial u,v)=-\lambda\phi_{\lambda}(u,v),\quad\phi_{\lambda}(u,\partial v)=\lambda\phi_{\lambda}(u,v).

(2.18)

A conformal bilinear form is called skew-symmetric if

\phi_{\lambda}(u,v)=-\phi_{-\lambda}(v,u)
.
Suppose that there is a conformal bilinear form on a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module
V
. Then, we
have an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
T:V\to V^{*c},\ u\mapsto T_{u}
given by

(T_{u})_{\lambda}v=\phi_{\lambda}(u,v),\ u,v\in V.

A conformal bilinear form is called non-degenerate if
T
is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-modules between
V
and
V^{*c}
.

Definition 2.8
.

A Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra
A
is called a symplectic Jacobi-Jordan conformal
algebra if there is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric conformal bilinear form
\phi_{\lambda}
on
A

satisfying

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda}(a_{,}b_{\mu}c)+\phi_{\mu}(b,a_{\lambda}c)+\phi_{\lambda+\mu}(a_{\lambda}b,c)=0.

(2.19)

Example 2.13
.

Let
(A,\phi)
be a symplectic Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Then,
(CurA,\phi_{\lambda})
is a
symplectic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra where
\phi_{\lambda}

is defined by

\phi_{\lambda}(f(\partial)a,g(\partial)b)=f(-\lambda)g(\lambda)\phi(a,b),\ \forall f(\partial),g(\partial)\in\mathbb{F}[\partial],a,b\in A.

The following result relates left anti-symmetric conformal algebras with symplectic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{\lambda},\phi_{\lambda})
be a symplectic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. Then there exists a compatible left anti-symmetric conformal algebra structure
\circ_{\lambda}
on
A
given by

\displaystyle\phi_{\mu}(a\circ_{\lambda}b,c)=\phi_{\mu-\lambda}(b,a_{\lambda}c),\ \forall a,b,c\in A.

(2.20)

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

3
Quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras and mock-Gel’fand Dorfman bialgebras

3.1
Mock-Gel’fand Dorfman bialgebras

In this section, we introduce a new class of algebras which will be useful to characterize quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras. This class will be called mock-Gel’fand Dorfman bialgebras. We first provide some relevant definitions and constructions.

Definition 3.1
.

An anti-Novikov algebra is a pair
(A,\circ)
, where
A
is a vector space and
\circ
is an operation on
A
satisfying the following axioms:

\displaystyle a\circ(b\circ c)=-b\circ(a\circ c),

(3.1)

\displaystyle(a,b,c)^{-}_{\circ}+(a,c,b)^{-}_{\circ}=0,

(3.2)

for any
a,b,c\in A
and where
(a,b,c)^{-}_{\circ}=(a\circ b)\circ c+a\circ(b\circ c)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be an anti-Novikov algebra. Then
(A,\ast)
is a Jacobi-Jordan algebra, where, for any
a,b\in A
,
a\ast b=a\circ b+b\circ a
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-commutative anti-associative algebra and
d:A\to A
be a derivation on
A
. Then
(A,\circ)
is an anti-Novikov algebra, where

a\circ b=d(a)\cdot b,\ \forall a,b\in A.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in A
. Then we have

\displaystyle a\circ(b\circ c)=

\displaystyle d(a)\cdot(d(b)\cdot c)=-d(a)\cdot(c\cdot d(b))=(d(a)\cdot c)\cdot d(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d(b)\cdot(d(a)\cdot c)=-b\circ(a\circ c).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c+a\circ(b\circ c)=d(d(a)\cdot b)\cdot c+d(a)\cdot(d(b)\cdot c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d^{2}(a)\cdot b)\cdot c+(d(a)\cdot d(b))\cdot c+d(a)\cdot(d(b)\cdot c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d^{2}(a)\cdot(b\cdot c)=d^{2}(a)\cdot(c\cdot b).

Since
b
and
c
have symmetric role, then we have

\displaystyle(a\circ c)\circ b+a\circ(c\circ b)==-d^{2}(a)\cdot(c\cdot b).

Therefore,
(A,\circ)
is an anti-Novikov algebra.
∎

Now, we introduce the notion of mock-Gel’fand Dorfman bialgebra.

Definition 3.2
.

A mock-Gel’fand Dorfman bialgebra is a vector space
A
with two operations
\circ
and
\ast
, such that
(A,\circ)
is an anti-Novikov algebra,
(A,\ast)
is a Jacobi-Jordan algebra and the following compatibility condition holds (for any
a,b,c\in A
):

\displaystyle a\circ(b\ast c)+a\ast(b\circ c)+(a\ast b)\circ c+b\circ(a\ast c)+b\ast(a\circ c)=0.

(3.3)

Such an algebra will be denoted by
(A,\ast,\circ)
.

In the sequel, we shall
simply call a mock-Gel’fand-Dorfman bialgebra a mock-GD bialgebra.

The following construction of mock-GD bialgebras is from anti-Novikov algebras, analogous to the
fundamental construction of Gel’fand-Dorfman bialgebras from Novikov algebras via the commutator bracket.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be an anti-Novikov algebra. Define a new product
\ast
on
A
by

a\ast b=a\circ b+b\circ a,\ \text{for}\ a,b,\in A.

(3.4)

Then
(A,\ast,\circ)
is a mock-GD bialgebra.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
(A,\ast)
is a Jacobi-Jordan algebra. So we only, prove the compatibility condition (
3.3
). For any
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle a\circ(b\ast c)+a\ast(b\circ c)+(a\ast b)\circ c+b\circ(a\ast c)+b\ast(a\circ c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ(b\circ c)+a\circ(c\circ b)+a\circ(b\circ c)+(b\circ c)\circ a+(a\circ b)\circ c

\displaystyle+(b\circ a)\circ c+b\circ(a\circ c)+b\circ(c\circ a)+b\circ(a\circ c)+(a\circ c)\circ b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c+a\circ(b\circ c)+(a\circ c)\circ b+a\circ(c\circ b)

\displaystyle+(b\circ a)\circ c+b\circ(a\circ c)+(b\circ c)\circ a+b\circ(c\circ a)

\displaystyle+a\circ(b\circ c)+b\circ(a\circ c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-commutative anti-associative algebra and
d:A\to A
be a derivation. Then
(A,\ast,\circ)
is a mock-GD algebra, where
\ast
and
\circ
are defined by

\displaystyle a\circ b=d(a)\cdot b,\quad a\ast b=d(a)\cdot b+d(b)\cdot a,\ \forall a,b\in A.

(3.5)

Example 3.5
.

Let
A
be a
3
-dimensional vector space with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. Define the following product on
A
:

e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=-e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{3}.
Then it is easy to check that
(A,\cdot)
is an anti-commutative anti-associative algebra. Consider the linear map
d:A\to A
defined by:

d(e_{1})=e_{1},\quad d(e_{2})=2e_{2},\quad d(e_{3})=3e_{3}.

Then
d
is a derivation on
A
. Therefore, according to the above proposition,
(A,\ast,\circ)
is a mock-GD bialgebra, where

e_{i}\circ e_{i}=e_{i}\circ e_{i}=0,\quad e_{1}\circ e_{2}=e_{3},\quad e_{2}\circ e_{1}=-2e_{3},\quad e_{1}\ast e_{2}=e_{2}\ast e_{1}=e_{3}.

3.2
Quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras and their characterization

Definition 3.3
.

Let
A
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. If there exists a vector space
V
such that
A=\mathbb{F}[\partial]V
is an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module over
V
and for all
a,b\in V
the
\lambda
-product is of the following form:

a_{\lambda}b=\partial u+\lambda v+w,

where
u,v,w\in V
, then
A
is called a quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra.

We have the following characterization result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
V
be a vector space equipped with two operations
\ast
and
\circ
. Let
A=\mathbb{F}[\partial]V
be the free
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module over
V
. Define the
\lambda
-product
\cdot_{\lambda}:A\otimes A\to A[\lambda]
on
A
by

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}v=u\ast v+\partial(u\circ v)+\lambda(u\circ v-v\circ u),\ \ u,v\in A.

(3.6)

Then
(A,\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot)
is a quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra if and only if
(V,\ast,\circ)
is a mock-GD algebra.

Proof.

Suppose that
A
is a quadratic Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra. By its definition, we set

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}v=u\ast v+\partial(u\circ v)+\lambda(u\circ v-v\circ u),\ \forall u,v\in V,

(3.7)

where
\ast
and
\circ
are two
\mathbb{F}
-bilinear maps from
V\times V\to V
.
Next, we consider axioms (
2.6
) and (
2.7
). For any
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle b_{-\lambda-\partial}a=

\displaystyle b\ast a+\partial(b\circ a)-(\lambda+\partial)(b\circ a-a\circ b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\ast a+\partial(a\circ b)+\lambda(a\circ b-b\circ a).

In addition, we have

a_{\lambda}b=a\ast b+\partial(a\circ b)+\lambda(a\circ b-b\circ a)
.
Since (
2.6
) holds, then

\displaystyle a\ast b=b\ast a.

(3.8)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)=a_{\lambda}(b\ast c+\partial(b\circ c)+\mu(b\circ c-c\circ b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(b\ast c)+(\lambda+\partial)a_{\lambda}(b\circ c)+\mu a_{\lambda}(b\circ c-c\circ b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\ast(b\ast c)+\partial(a\circ(b\ast c))+\lambda(a\circ(b\ast c)-(b\ast c)\circ a)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\lambda+\partial)a\ast(b\circ c)+(\lambda+\partial)\partial(a\circ(b\circ c))+\lambda(\lambda+\partial)(a\circ(b\circ c)-(b\circ c)\circ a)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu a\ast(b\circ c-c\circ b)+\mu\partial a\circ(b\circ c-c\circ b)+\mu\lambda(a\circ(b\circ c-c\circ b)-(b\circ c-c\circ b)\circ a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\ast(b\ast c)+\partial\big(a\circ(b\ast c)+a\ast(b\circ c)\big)+\lambda\big(a\circ(b\ast c)-(b\ast c)\circ a+a\ast(b\circ c)\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu a\ast(b\circ c-c\circ b)+\lambda\partial\big(2a\circ(b\circ c)-(b\circ c)\circ a\big)+\lambda\mu\big(a\circ(b\circ c-c\circ b)-(b\circ c-c\circ b)\circ a\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\partial a\circ(b\circ c-c\circ b)+\partial^{2}a\circ(b\circ c)+\lambda^{2}\big(a\circ(b\circ c)-(b\circ c)\circ a\big).

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c)=b\ast(a\ast c)+\partial\big(b\circ(a\ast c)+b\ast(a\circ c)\big)+\mu\big(b\circ(a\ast c)-(a\ast c)\circ b+b\ast(a\circ c)\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda b\ast(a\circ c-c\circ a)+\mu\partial\big(2b\circ(a\circ c)-(a\circ c)\circ b\big)+\lambda\partial b\circ(a\circ c-c\circ a)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda\mu\big(b\circ(a\circ c-c\circ a)-(a\circ c-c\circ a)\circ b\big)+\partial^{2}b\circ(a\circ c)+\mu^{2}\big(b\circ(a\circ c)-(a\circ c)\circ b\big).

Furthermore, we can obtain

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c=(a\ast b)\ast c+\partial(a\ast b)\circ c+\lambda\big((a\ast b)\circ c-c\circ(a\ast b)-(b\circ a)\ast c\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\big((a\ast b)\circ c-c\circ(a\ast b)-(a\circ b)\ast c\big)-\lambda\partial(b\circ a)\circ c-\mu\partial(a\circ b)\circ c

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda\mu\big((b\circ a-a\circ b)\circ c+c\circ(a\circ b+b\circ a)\big)-\mu^{2}\big((a\circ b)\circ c-c\circ(a\circ b)\big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\big(c\circ(b\circ a)-(b\circ a)\circ c\big).

According to the identity (
2.7
) and comparing the coefficients of
\lambda^{2},\mu^{2},\partial^{2},\lambda\mu,\lambda\partial,\mu\partial,\lambda,\mu,\partial
and
\lambda^{0}\mu^{0}\partial^{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=a\ast(b\ast c)+(a\ast b)\ast c+b\ast(a\ast c),

(3.9)

\displaystyle 0=a\circ(b\ast c)+a\ast(b\circ c)+b\circ(a\ast c)+b\ast(a\circ c)+(a\ast b)\circ c,

(3.10)

\displaystyle 0=a\circ(b\ast c)-(b\ast c)\circ a+a\ast(b\circ a)+b\ast(a\circ c-c\circ a)

\displaystyle\qquad+(a\ast b)\circ c-c\circ(a\ast b)-(b\circ a)\ast c,

(3.11)

\displaystyle 0=a\ast(b\circ c-c\circ b)+b\circ(a\ast c)-(a\ast c)\circ b+b\ast(a\circ c)

\displaystyle\qquad+(a\ast b)\circ c-c\circ(a\ast b)-(a\circ b)\ast c,

(3.12)

\displaystyle 0=2a\circ(b\circ c)-(b\circ c)\circ a+b\circ(a\circ c-c\circ a)-(b\circ a)\circ c,

(3.13)

\displaystyle 0=a\circ(b\circ c-c\circ b)+2b\circ(a\circ c)-(a\circ c)\circ b-(a\circ b)\circ c,

(3.14)

\displaystyle 0=a\circ(b\circ c-c\circ b)-(b\circ c-c\circ b)\circ a+b\circ(a\circ c-c\circ a)

\displaystyle\quad-(a\circ c-c\circ a)\circ b+(b\circ a-a\circ b)\circ c+c\circ(a\circ b+b\circ a),

(3.15)

\displaystyle 0=a\circ(b\circ c)-(b\circ c)\circ a+c\circ(b\circ a)-(b\circ a)\circ c,

(3.16)

\displaystyle 0=b\circ(a\circ c)-(a\circ c)\circ b+c\circ(a\circ b)-(a\circ b)\circ c,

(3.17)

\displaystyle 0=a\circ(b\circ c)+b\circ(a\circ c).

(3.18)

By (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we deduce that
(V,\ast)
is a Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Note that (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) are the same. So if we take, for example (
3.17
) with (
3.18
), we get that
(A,\circ)
is an anti-Novikov algebra. On the other hand, identities (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) are equivalent to (
3.17
).
Furthermore, equalities (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) are equivalent to (
3.10
) which is just the compatibility condition (
3.3
). Hence,
(A,\ast,\circ)
is a mock-GD bialgebra.

Conversely, if
(A,\ast,\circ)
is a mock-GD bialgebra, then by a similar computation, we can prove according to the above discussions that
(A,\cdot_{\lambda})
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra.
The proof is finished.

∎

.

. Then there is an associated Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra whose

4
The
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem

In this section, the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem for Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras is studied. We introduce a definition of unified product of a given Jacobi-Jordan conformal
algebra
J
and a given
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
K
. This product includes some other interesting products of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras such as twisted product.
Using this product, a cohomological type object is constructed to provide a theoretical answer to the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem.

We introduce the following definition which is relevant for studying
the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(J,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra,
K
a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module and
E=J\oplus K
, where the direct sum is the sum of
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules. Let
\varphi:E\rightarrow E
be a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism. We consider the following
diagram:

\textstyle{{J}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Id}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Id}
\textstyle{{J}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{{K}}

where
\pi:E\rightarrow K
is the natural projection of
E=J\oplus K
onto
K
and

is the inclusion map. If the left square
(resp. the right square) of the above diagram are commutative,
we say that

\varphi:E\rightarrow E

J
(resp.
co-stabilizes

).

Let
\ast_{\lambda}
and
\square_{\lambda}
be two Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra structures on

If there exists a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra isomorphism
\varphi:(E,\ast_{\lambda})\rightarrow(E,\square_{\lambda})

\ast_{\lambda}

\square_{\lambda}

. In this case, we denote it by

(E,\ast_{\lambda})\equiv(E,\square_{\lambda})
.
If there exists a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra isomorphism
\varphi:(E,\ast_{\lambda})\rightarrow(E,\square_{\lambda})
which stabilizes

J
and co-stabilizes
K
, then
\ast_{\lambda}
and
\square_{\lambda}
are called
cohomologous
. In this case, we denote it by

(E,\ast_{\lambda})\approx(E,\square_{\lambda})
.

Obviously,
\equiv
and
\approx
are equivalence relations on the set of all Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra structures on

E
containing
J
as a Jacobi-Jordan conformal subalgebra. We denote the set
of all equivalence classes via
\equiv
(resp.
\approx
) by
\text{CExtd}(E,J)
(resp.
\text{CExtd}^{{}^{\prime}}(E,J)
). It is easy to see that
\text{CExtd}(E,J)
is the classifying object of the

\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem, and there exists a canonical projection
\text{CExtd}^{{}^{\prime}}(E,J)\twoheadrightarrow\text{CExtd}(E,J)
.

4.1
Unified products of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras

In this section, a unified product of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras is defined and used to provide a theoretical answer to the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem for Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(J,\cdot_{\lambda})
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra and
K
a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. An
extending datum
of
J
by
K
is
a system
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
consisting of four conformal bilinear maps

\displaystyle\lhd_{\lambda}:K\times J\rightarrow K[\lambda],

\displaystyle~~~\rhd_{\lambda}:K\times J\rightarrow J[\lambda],

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}:K\times K\rightarrow J[\lambda],

\displaystyle~~~\circ_{\lambda}:K\times K\rightarrow K[\lambda].

Let
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
be an extending datum of
J
by
K
. Denote by
J\natural K
the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module

J\oplus K
with the natural
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module action:
\partial(a,x)=(\partial a,\partial x)
and the bilinear map
\ast_{\lambda}:(J\oplus K)\times(J\oplus K)\rightarrow(J\oplus K)[\lambda]
defined by

\displaystyle(a,x)\ast_{\lambda}(b,y)=(a_{\lambda}b+x\rhd_{\lambda}b+y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+\omega_{\lambda}(x,y),x\lhd_{\lambda}b+y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\circ_{\lambda}y),

(4.1)

for all
a
,
b\in J
,
x
,
y\in K
. Since
\lhd_{\lambda}
,
\rhd_{\lambda}
,
\omega_{\lambda}
and
\circ_{\lambda}
are conformal bilinear maps, the
\ast_{\lambda}
-product defined by (
4.1
) satisfies conformal sesquilinearity. Then

J\natural K
is called the
unified product
of
J
and
K
associated with
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
if it is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra with the
\ast_{\lambda}
-product given by (
4.1
). In this case, the extending datum
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
is called a
Jacobi-Jordan conformal extending structure
of
J
through
K
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}(J,K)
the set of all Jacobi-Jordan conformal extending structures of
J
through
K
.

The maps
\lhd_{\lambda}
and
\rhd_{\lambda}
, are called the actions of
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
and
\omega_{\lambda}
is called the cocycle of
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that by Eq. (
4.1
), the following identities hold in
J\natural J
, for any
a,b\in J
and
x,y\in K

\displaystyle(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(b,0)=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b,0),\qquad\qquad\quad(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(0,y)=(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a),

(4.2)

\displaystyle(0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(b,0)=

\displaystyle(x\rhd_{\lambda}b,x\lhd_{\lambda}b),\qquad(0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(0,y)=(\omega_{\lambda}(x,y),x\circ_{\lambda}y).

(4.3)

The next theorem provides the set of axioms that need to be fulfilled by an extending datum
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
such that
J\natural K
is a unified product.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
J
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra,
K
be a
\mathbb{F}|\partial]
-module and
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
be an extending datum of
J
by
K
. Then
J\natural K
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra if and only if the following conditions are satisfied for any
a,b\in J
and
x,y\in K

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{1})}\qquad\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)=\omega_{-\lambda-\partial}(y,x),\quad x\circ_{\lambda}y=y\circ_{-\lambda-\partial}x,

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{2})}\qquad a_{\lambda}(x\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+b_{\mu}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{3})}\qquad(x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b=0.,

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{4})}\qquad(a_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(x,y)+(x\circ_{\mu}y)\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,y)+(x\rhd_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+\omega_{\mu}(x,y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{5})}\qquad(x\circ_{\mu}y)\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+y\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+x\lhd_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+x\circ_{\mu}(y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a))=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{6})}\qquad x\rhd_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(y,z)+\omega_{\lambda}(x,y\circ_{\mu}z)+y\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y,z)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+y\rhd_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(x,z)+\omega_{\mu}(y,x\circ_{\lambda}z)=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(U_{7})}\qquad x\lhd_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(y,z)+x\circ_{\lambda}(y\circ_{\mu}z)+y\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)+(x\circ_{\lambda}y)_{\lambda+\mu}z

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+y\lhd_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(x,z)+y\circ_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)=0.

Before going into the proof of the theorem, we make a few remarks on the compatibility in Theorem
4.1
. Aside from the fact that
K
is not a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra, the identities (
U_{3}
) and (
U_{4}
)
are exactly the compatibility defining a matched pair of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras.
The compatibility condition (
U_{5}
) is called the twisted module condition for the
action
\lhd_{\lambda}
; in the case when
K
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra it measures how far
(J,\lhd_{\lambda})
is from being a left
K
-module.
The condition (
U_{6}
) is called the twisted cocycle condition: if
\lhd_{\lambda}
is the trivial action
and
(K,\circ_{\lambda})
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra then the compatibility condition (
U_{6}
) is exactly the
2
-cocycle condition for Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras. Finally, the identity (
U_{7}
) is called the twisted conformal Jacobi condition:
it measures how far
\circ_{\lambda}
is from being a Jacobi-Jordan conformal structure on
K
. If either
\rhd_{\lambda}
or
\omega_{\lambda}
is the
trivial map, then (
U_{7}
) is equivalent to
\circ_{\lambda}
being a Jacobi-Jordan conformal product on
K
.

Proof.

Since
\rhd_{\lambda},\ \lhd_{\lambda},\ \omega_{\lambda}
and
\circ_{\lambda}
are conformal linear maps, then the conformal sesquilinearity of
\ast_{\lambda}
is naturally checked. Therefore, we need only to show that the commutativity condition Eq. (
2.6
)
and the Jacobi identity Eq. (
2.7
) hold for
\ast_{\lambda}
if and only if the identities
{\rm(U_{1})}
-
{\rm(U_{7})}
are satisfied.

First, we can easily prove that
\ast_{\lambda}
is commutative, i.e.
(a,x)\ast_{\lambda}(b,y)=(b,y)\ast_{-\lambda-\partial}(a,x)
if and only if

\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)=\omega_{-\lambda-\partial}(y,x)
and
x\circ_{\lambda}y=y\circ_{-\lambda-\partial}x
, that is
{\rm(U_{1})}
holds.

Then
J\natural K
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra if and only if
the conformal Jacobi-identity Eq. (
2.7
) holds for the
\ast_{\lambda}
-product, i.e.

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle(a,x)\ast_{\lambda}((b,y)\ast_{\mu}(c,z))+((a,x)\ast_{\lambda}(b,y))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(c,z)+(b,y)\ast_{\mu}((a,x)\ast_{\lambda}(c,z)),

(4.4)

for all
a
,
b
,
c\in J
and
x
,
y
,
z\in K
.
Since in
J\natural K
, we have
(a,x)=(a,0)+(0,x)
; it follows that (
4.4
) holds if and only if it holds for all generators of
J\natural K
, i.e. the set

\{(a,0)|a\in J\}\cup\{(0,x)|x\in K\}
.
First, we should notice that (
4.4
) holds for the
triple
(a,0),(b,0),(c,0)
as we have

\displaystyle(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}((b,0)\ast_{\mu}(c,0))+((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(b,0))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(c,0)+(b,0)\ast_{\mu}((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(c,0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)+(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c+b_{\mu}(a_{\lambda}c),0)=(0,0).

Next, we prove that (
4.4
) holds for
(a,0),(b,0),(0,x)
if and only if
{\rm(U_{2})}
and
{\rm(U_{3})}
hold. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}((b,0)\ast_{\mu}(0,x))+((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(b,0))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,x)+(b,0)\ast_{\mu}((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(0,x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(x\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b,x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(a_{\lambda}b,0)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,x)+(b,0)\ast_{\mu}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}(x\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,(x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b),x\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(b_{\mu}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b,(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b).

The right hand side varnishes if and only if

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}(x\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)+(x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+b_{\mu}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\rhd_{-\mu-\partial}b=0

and

\displaystyle(x\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b)\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(a_{\lambda}b)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\lhd_{-\mu-\partial}b=0.

Thus
{\rm(U_{2})}
and
{\rm(U_{3})}
hold.
Now, we show that (
4.4
) holds for
(a,0),(0,x),(0,y)
if and only if
{\rm(U_{4})}
and
{\rm(U_{5})}
hold. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}((0,x)\ast_{\mu}(0,y))+((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(0,x))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,y)+(0,x)\ast_{\mu}((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(0,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(\omega_{\mu}(x,y),x\circ_{\mu}y)+(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,y)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+(0,x)\ast_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(x,y)+(x\circ_{\mu}y)\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,(x\circ_{\mu}y)\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,y),y\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}y)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(x\rhd_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+\omega_{\mu}(x,y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a),x\lhd_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+x\circ_{\mu}(y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)).

Then
(a,0)\ast_{\lambda}((0,x)\ast_{\mu}(0,y))+((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(0,x))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,y)+(0,x)\ast_{\mu}((a,0)\ast_{\lambda}(0,y))=0
if and only if

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(x,y)+(x\circ_{\mu}y)\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a,y)+(x\rhd_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+\omega_{\mu}(x,y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)=0

and

\displaystyle(x\circ_{\mu}y)\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+y\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)\circ_{\lambda+\mu}y)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle x\lhd_{\mu}(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+x\circ_{\mu}(y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a))=0.

Thus
{\rm(U_{4})}
and
{\rm(U_{5})}
hold.
Finally, we prove that (
4.4
) holds for
(0,x),(0,y),(0,z)
if and only if
{\rm(U_{6})}
and
{\rm(U_{7})}
hold. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle(0,x)\ast_{\lambda}((0,y)\ast_{\mu}(0,z))+((0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(0,y))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,z)+(0,y)\ast_{\mu}((0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(0,z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(\omega_{\mu}(y,z),y\circ_{\mu}z)+(\omega_{\lambda}(x,y),x\circ_{\lambda}y)\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,z)+(0,y)\ast_{\mu}(\omega_{\lambda}(x,z),x\circ_{\lambda}z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\rhd_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(y,z)+\omega_{\lambda}(x,y\circ_{\mu}z),x\lhd_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(y,z)+x\circ_{\lambda}(y\circ_{\mu}z))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(y\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y,z),y\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)+(x\circ_{\lambda}y)_{\lambda+\mu}z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(y\rhd_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(x,z)+\omega_{\mu}(y,x\circ_{\lambda}z),y\lhd_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(x,z)+y\circ_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)).

Therefore

(0,x)\ast_{\lambda}((0,y)\ast_{\mu}(0,z))+((0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(0,y))\ast_{\lambda+\mu}(0,z)+(0,y)\ast_{\mu}((0,x)\ast_{\lambda}(0,z))=0
if and only if

\displaystyle x\rhd_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(y,z)+\omega_{\lambda}(x,y\circ_{\mu}z)+y\rhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)+\omega_{\lambda+\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}y,z)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+y\rhd_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(x,z)+\omega_{\mu}(y,x\circ_{\lambda}z)=0

\displaystyle x\lhd_{\lambda}\omega_{\mu}(y,z)+x\circ_{\lambda}(y\circ_{\mu}z)+y\lhd_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)+(x\circ_{\lambda}y)_{\lambda+\mu}z

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+y\lhd_{\mu}\omega_{\lambda}(x,z)+y\circ_{\mu}(x\circ_{\lambda}z)=0.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
be an extending datum of a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra
J
through a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module
K
, where
\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda}
and
\circ_{\lambda}
are trivial conformal bilinear maps.
Then the corresponding extending datum denoted by
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda}\}
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal extending structure of
J
by
K
if and only if
K
is a right
K
-module under
\rhd_{\lambda}
. Therefore, the associated unified product is just the semi-direct product of
J
and
K
.

Given an extending structure
\mathcal{U}(J,K)
. It is obvious that
J
can be seen as a Jacobi-Jordan conformal
subalgebra of
J\natural K
. Conversely, we will prove that any Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra structure on a
vector space E containing
J
as a subalgebra is isomorphic to a unified product.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
J
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra and
K
an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module. Set
E=J\oplus K
, where the direct sum is the sum of
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-modules. Suppose that
E
has a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra structure
\ast_{\lambda}
such that
J
is a subalgebra. Then
there exists a Jacobi-Jordan conformal extending structure
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
of
J
by
K
and an isomorphism of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra
E\cong J\natural K
which stabilizes
J
and co-stabilizes
K
.

Proof.

Since
E=J\oplus K
, there exists a natural
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-homomorphism
p:E\to J
such that
p(a)=a
, for any
a\in J
. Then, we can define an extending datum
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
of
J
by
K
as follows (
a\in J
and
x,y\in K
)

\displaystyle\rhd_{\lambda}:K\times J\to J[\lambda],\qquad x\rhd_{\lambda}a=

\displaystyle p(x\ast_{\lambda}a)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lhd_{\lambda}:K\times J\to K[\lambda],\qquad x\lhd_{\lambda}a=

\displaystyle x\ast_{\lambda}a-p(x\ast_{\lambda}a)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}:K\times K\to J[\lambda],\qquad\omega_{\lambda}(x,y)=

\displaystyle p(x\ast_{\lambda}y)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\circ_{\lambda}:K\times K\to K[\lambda],\qquad x\circ_{\lambda}\,y=

\displaystyle x\ast_{\lambda}y-p(x\ast_{\lambda}y).

(4.8)

Obviously,
\lhd_{\lambda}
,
\rhd_{\lambda}
,
\omega_{\lambda}
and
\circ_{\lambda}
are four conformal bilinear maps. Next, we shall prove that
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
of
J
is a Jacobi-Jordan conformal extending structure and
E\cong J\natural K
as Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebras.
Set
\psi:J\times K\to E
by
\psi(a,x)=a+x
. It is easy to see that
\psi
is an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module isomorphism. Its inverse is
\psi^{-1}:E\to J\times K
defined by
\psi^{-1}(e)=(p(e),e-p(e))
. Therefore, if
\psi
is also an isomorphism of Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra, there exists a unique
\lambda
-product
\square_{\lambda}
on
J\times K
given by

\displaystyle(a,x)\square_{\lambda}(b,y)=\psi^{-1}(\psi(a,x)\ast_{\lambda}\psi(b,y)),

(4.9)

for any
a,b\in J
and
x,y\in K
. Hence, for finishing the proof, we only need to prove
that the
\lambda
-product defined by (
4.9
) is just the one given by
(
4.1
) associated with
the above extending system
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
. We check it as follows

\displaystyle(a,x)\square_{\lambda}(b,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}(\psi(a,x)\ast_{\lambda}\psi(b,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}((a+x)\ast_{\lambda}(b+y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}(a\ast_{\lambda}b+a\ast_{\lambda}y+x\ast_{\lambda}b+x\ast_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p(a\ast_{\lambda}b)+p(a\ast_{\lambda}y)+p(x\ast_{\lambda}b)+p(x\ast_{\lambda}y),

\displaystyle a\ast_{\lambda}b+a\ast_{\lambda}y+x\ast_{\lambda}b+x\ast_{\lambda}y-p(a\ast_{\lambda}b)-p(a\ast_{\lambda}y)-p(x\ast_{\lambda}b)-p(x\ast_{\lambda}y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b+p(y\ast_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+p(x\ast_{\lambda}b)+p(x\ast_{\lambda}y),

\displaystyle.y\ast_{-\lambda-\partial}a-p(y\ast_{-\lambda-\partial}a)+x\ast_{\lambda}b-p(x\ast_{\lambda}b)+x\ast_{\lambda}y-p(x\ast_{\lambda}y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b+y\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\rhd_{\lambda}b+\omega_{\lambda}(x,y),y\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+x\lhd_{\lambda}b+x\circ_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a,x)\ast_{\lambda}(b,y).

Moreover, it is easy to see that
\psi
stabilizes
J
and co-stabilizes
K
.
Then, the proof is finished.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
J
be a Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra and
K
an
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module. Two Jacobi-Jordan conformal algebra extending structures of
J
by
K
,
\mathcal{U}(J,K)=\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}

\mathcal{U}(J,K)\equiv\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(J,K)
, if there exists a pair of
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphisms
(r,s)
, where
r:K\to J
and

such that
\{\lhd^{\prime}_{\lambda},\rhd^{\prime}_{\lambda},\omega^{\prime}_{\lambda},\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}
is implemented from
\{\lhd_{\lambda},\rhd_{\lambda},\omega_{\lambda},\circ_{\lambda}\}
using
(r,s)
via:

\displaystyle x\rhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a+r(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)=a_{\lambda}r(x)+s(x)\rhd^{\prime}_{-\lambda-\partial}a,

(4.10)

\displaystyle s(x\lhd_{-\lambda-\partial}a)=s(x)\lhd^{\prime}_{-\lambda-\partial}a,

(4.11)

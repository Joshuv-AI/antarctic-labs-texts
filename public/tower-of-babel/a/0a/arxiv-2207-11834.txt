On Rota-Baxter and Nijenhuis anti-flexible algebras
Mafoya Landry Dassoundo

Abstract
. We define and derive basic properties of the notion of Rota-Baxter operator on anti-flexible algebra. Pre-anti-flexible algebra structure and associated left(right)-symmetric algebra are constructed from a given Rota-Baxter operator on an anti-flexible algebra. The notion of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator on anti-flexible algebra are recalled and use to build left(right)-symmetric algebra as well as related properties. Furthermore, we introduce Nijenhuis anti-flexible algebra and derive associated properties. Nijenhuis operator on anti-flexible algebra is used to build pre-anti-flexible algebra structure and related left(right)-symmetric algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-flexible algebra. A vector space
M

is said to be an
A
-bimodule if
A
induces two actions “left”
am
and “right”
ma
in
M

for any
a\in A,

m\in M
and satisfying,

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)m-a(bm)-(mb)a+m(b\cdot a)=0,\;\;\forall a,b\in A,m\in M,

(3.1a)

\displaystyle a(mb)-(am)b-b(ma)-(bm)a=0,\;\;\forall a,b\in A,m\in M.

(3.1b)

Remark 3.2
.

For a given bimodule
M
over an anti-flexible algebra
(A,\cdot)
,
there are two linear maps
l:A\otimes M\rightarrow M,(a,m)\mapsto l(a,m)
and

r:M\otimes A\rightarrow M,(m,a)\mapsto r(m,a)

satisfying for any
a,b\in A
and for any
m\in M,

\displaystyle l(a\cdot b,m)-l(a,l(b,m))=r(r(m,b),a)-r(m,b\cdot a),

(3.2a)

\displaystyle l(a,r(m,b))-r(l(a,m),b)=l(b,r(m,a))-r(l(b,m),a).

(3.2b)

Clearly, Eq. (
3.1a
) corresponds to Eq. (
3.2a
) while
Eq. (
3.1b
) corresponds to Eq. (
3.2b
).

Example 3.3
.

The dual space
A^{*}
of
A
equipped with

l^{*}:A^{*}\otimes A\rightarrow A^{*}
and

r^{*}:A\otimes A^{*}\rightarrow A^{*}
given by:

\displaystyle l^{*}(a,f)(b):=f(b\cdot a)\mbox{ and }r^{*}(f,a)(b):=f(a\cdot b),\forall a,b\in A,f\in A^{*},

carries an
A
-bimodule
structure, i.e., for any
a,b,c\in A
and for any
f\in A^{*},

\displaystyle l^{*}(a\cdot b,f)(c)-l^{*}(a,l^{*}(b,f))(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(c\cdot(a\cdot b))-l^{*}(b,f)(c\cdot a)=f(c\cdot(a\cdot b)-(c\cdot a)\cdot b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(b\cdot(a\cdot c)-(b\cdot a)\cdot c)=r^{*}(r^{*}(f,b),a)(c)-r^{*}(f,b\cdot a)(c)

Hence,

l^{*}(a\cdot b,f)-l^{*}(a,l^{*}(b,f))=r^{*}(r^{*}(f,a),b)-r^{*}(f,b\cdot a)
.
Thus Eq. (
3.2a
) is satisfied.

\displaystyle l^{*}(a,r^{*}(f,b))(c)-r^{*}(l^{*}(a,f),b)(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{*}(f,b)(c\cdot a)-l^{*}(a,f)(b\cdot c)=f(a\cdot(c\cdot b)-(a\cdot c)\cdot b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l^{*}(b,r^{*}(f,a))(c)-r^{*}(l^{*}(b,f),a)(c)

Hence,

l^{*}(a,r^{*}(f,b))-r^{*}(l^{*}(a,f),b)=l^{*}(b,r^{*}(f,a))-r^{*}(l(b,f),a)
.
Thus Eq. (
3.2b
) is satisfied.

Proposition 3.4
.

For a given anti-flexible algebra
(A,\cdot)

and an
A
-bimodule

there is an anti-flexible algebra
structure “
\ast
” on the vector space

given by

\displaystyle(a,m)\ast(b,n)=(a\cdot b,l(a,n)+r(m,b)),\forall a,b\in A,\forall m,n\in M.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in A
and
m,n,s\in M
. The associator of
the bilinear product given by Eq. (

)
is derived by:

\displaystyle[(a,m),(b,n),(c,s)]_{{}_{\ast}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([a,b,c],l(a\cdot b,s)-l(a,l(b,s))+r(l(a,n),c)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle l(a,r(n,c))+r(r(m,b),c)-r(m,b\cdot c))

and similarly

\displaystyle[(c,s),(b,n),(a,m)]_{{}_{\ast}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([c,b,a],l(c\cdot b,m)-l(c,l(b,m))+r(l(c,n),a)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle l(c,r(n,a))+r(r(s,b),a)-r(s,b\cdot a))

According to Eq. (
3.2a
)
and Eq. (
3.2b
), we deduce that
for any

and

[(a,m),(b,n),(c,s)]_{{}_{\ast}}=[(c,s),(b,n),(a,m)]_{{}_{\ast}}

hence

is an anti-flexible algebra.

Proposition 3.8

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an anti-flexible algebra. A linear map

\displaystyle T_{N_{J}}(x,y)=N_{J}(x)\cdot N_{J}(y)-N_{J}(N_{J}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N_{J}(y)-N_{J}(x\cdot y))=0,\forall x,y\in A.

(4.1)

Proposition 4.2

\displaystyle[N_{J}(x),N_{J}(y),z]+[N_{J}(x),y,N_{J}(z)]+[x,N_{J}(y),N_{J}(z)]+N_{J}^{2}\left([x,y,z]\right)

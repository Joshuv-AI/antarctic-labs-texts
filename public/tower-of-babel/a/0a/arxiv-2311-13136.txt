Equivariant Global Hopf Bifurcation in Abstract Nonlinear Parabolic Equations Journal: J. Differential Equations
Zalman Balanov, Wiesław Krawcewicz, Arnaja Mitra, Dmitrii Rachinskii

Abstract
In this paper we study local and global symmetric Hopf bifurcation in abstract parabolic systems by means of the twisted equivariant degree.

The presence of symmetries (the so-called equivariance) results in the following paradigmatic question:
what is the impact of symmetries of a dynamical system on its actual dynamics, for example, on the existence, multiplicity (the number of bifurcating branches), the global behavior of the branches, and symmetric properties of periodic solutions?

In this paper we present a new self-contained treatment of the global symmetric Hopf bifurcation problem of the type

\dot{u}(t)+Au(t)=\mathcal{F}(\alpha,u(t)),\quad{\text{a}.e.}\;\;t\in\mathbb{R},

(1)

where
A:{\mathfrak{D}}(A)\subset H\to H
is an accretive self-adjoint equivariant operator on a Hilbert representation of a compact Lie group
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}\times{\mathfrak{D}}(A)\to H
is a family of completely continuous equivariant maps (with respect to
{\mathfrak{D}}(A)
equipped with the graph topology).

Our main goal is to detect unbounded branches of non-constant periodic solutions bifurcating from an equilibrium and provide a comprehensive description of their symmetric properties. Under additional assumption that
\mathcal{F}
is odd with respect to second variable, it is possible to reduce the Fuller space for (
1
) to a subspace where the only equilibria to (
1
) are
(\alpha,\beta,0)
and in this context, one can provide sufficient conditions for the existence of unbounded branches.

We use the method based on the twisted equivariant degree theory
[
1
,
42
]
. In a nutshell, the twisted equivariant degree is a topological tool, which allows “counting" orbits of solutions to symmetric equations
according to symmetric properties of the solutions. As such, it is a counterpart of the usual Brouwer degree, which “counts" solutions to non-symmetric equations.
For a given dynamical system respecting some spatial symmetries
\Gamma
, spatio-temporal symmetries of its non-constant periodic solutions are subgroups of the group
G:=\Gamma\times S^{1}
of the type

K^{\theta,l}:=\{(\gamma,z)\in K\times S^{1}:\theta(\gamma)=z^{l}\},

where
K
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
and
\theta:K\to S^{1}
is a group homomorphism (with
l\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}
), which are called
twisted subgroups
of
G
. As a matter of fact, the twisted degree can detect non-constant periodic solutions with twisted spatio-temporal symmetries
K^{\theta,l}\leq G
(when
N_{\Gamma}(K)/K
is a finite group). To be more precise, problem (
1
) can be reformulated in appropriate Banach
G
-representation
\mathscr{E}
as a two parameter equation

\mathscr{F}(\alpha,\beta,u)=0,\quad u\in\mathscr{E},

(2)

where
\beta>0
represents an unknown frequency of a periodic solution, and
\mathscr{F}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
is a
G
-equivariant completely continuous field.
Then, with a bifurcation point
(\alpha,\beta,u_{o})
, one can associate a local
G
-equivariant bifurcation invariant

\omega_{G}(\alpha,\beta,u_{o})

provided by the twisted equivariant degree of the map
\mathscr{F}
(complemented by an auxiliary function
\varphi
) in a neighborhood of
(\alpha,\beta,u_{o})
, containing equivariant topological information on the symmetries of bifurcating from
(\alpha,\beta,u_{o})
branches of non-constant periodic solutions. The invariant
\omega_{G}(\alpha,\beta,u_{o})
is represented as a formal sum (indexed by the conjugacy classes
(H_{j})
of twisted subgroups in
G
) of the form

\omega_{G}(\alpha,\beta,0)=n_{1}(H_{1})+n_{2}(H_{2})+\dots+n_{m}(H_{m}),\quad n_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then, knowing that
n_{j}\not=0
allows us to predict the existence of a branch of non-constant periodic solutions, bifurcating from
(\alpha,\beta,u_{o})
with the symmetries at least
H_{j}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

A.1
Accretive operators

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space and let
|\cdot|
stand for the norm in
H
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

for the inner product in
H
. The complexification of
H

(denoted

) is equipped with the complex inner product
[\cdot,\cdot]
given by

[u+iv,x+iy]:=\langle u,x\rangle+\langle v,y\rangle+i(\langle v,x\rangle-\langle u,y\rangle),\quad u,\,v,\,x,\,y\in H.

(132)

Clearly, the formula

\langle z,w\rangle:=\text{\rm Re\,}[z,w],\qquad(z,w\in H^{c})

(133)

defines a
real
inner product on
H\oplus H
.

Assume that
A:{\mathfrak{D}}(A)\subset H\to H
is a real unbounded linear operator. The operator
A
is said to be
accretive
5
5

5

A
is also called a
monotone
operator according to the terminology used by Brezis
[
2
]
if

\forall_{v\in{\mathfrak{D}}(A)}\;\;\;\langle Av,v\rangle\geq 0.

(134)

An
accretive
operator
A:{\mathfrak{D}}(A)\subset H\to H
is called
m
-accretive
if for every
\lambda>0
one has
\text{\rm Im}(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})=H
. Actually, for an accretive operator, it is sufficient to assume that
\text{\rm Im}(A+\text{\rm Id\,})=H
to deduce that
{\mathfrak{D}}(A)
is dense,
A
is closed and
\text{\rm Im}(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})=H
for all
\lambda>0
(see Brezis
[
2
]
, Proposition 7.1). In such a case, one can easily deduce that
(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})^{-1}:H\to H
exists and

\|(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Also, if
A:{\mathfrak{D}}(A)\subset H\to H
is
m
-accretive and

is
m
-accretive and bounded, then
A+B
defined on
{\mathfrak{D}}(A)
is
m
-accretive as well. Finally,
if an accretive operator

is self-adjoint, then it is automatically
m
-accretive. Moreover, the following statement is true.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
A:{\mathfrak{D}}(A)\subset H\to H
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator satisfying (
134

\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}

(a)

[(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})z,z]\geq\text{\rm Re\,}\lambda|z|^{2}
(in particular,

(b)

\text{\rm Im}(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})=H^{c}
;

(c)

\left\|(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\text{\rm Re\,}\lambda}

Proof.

For the unbounded operator

(a) From (

[(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})z,z]=\langle Az+\text{\rm Re\,}\lambda z,z\rangle+i\text{Im}\lambda|z|^{2}.

(135)

Hence,

[(A+\lambda\text{\rm Id\,})z,z]=\langle Az,z\rangle+(\text{\rm Re\,}\lambda)|z|^{2}\geq\text{\rm Re\,}\lambda|z|^{2},

(b) First, let us show that

B.1

Let us recall the (non-equivariant) background from the monograph

Definition B.1

One can easily notice that any nontrivial continuum is uncountable. Also, the following concept generalizes the property of connectedness.

A compact space

The

Proposition B.3

Assume that a compact space

and

. Then, there exists a continuous function
\varphi:K\to\{0,1\}
such that
\varphi^{-1}(0)\supset A_{0}
,
\varphi^{-1}(1)\supset A_{1}
.

The following well-known result (cf.
[
44
]
, Theorem 3, p. 170) is a starting point for our discussion.

Theorem B.4

Assume that a compact space
K
is connected between its two closed non-empty subsets

B.2

The following Proposition provides technical means for the equivariant finite-dimensional reductions which are needed for effective computation of the related

Proof.

Since this result in the non-equivariant case was proved in

(see Theorem 4.4.5), we only present the main steps of the construction of the homotopy

Put

where
\varphi:H\to L
and

\varphi

of
\mathscr{H}

\mathscr{H}
is the amalgamated notation can be written as

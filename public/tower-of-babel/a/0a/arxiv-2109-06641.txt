Generalized integral transform method for solving multilayer diffusion problems Journal: arXiv Mathematics
Mohamed Akel a,b,∗, Hillal M. Elshehabey a,b, Ragaa Ahmed a,b

Abstract
Multilayer diffusion problems have found significant important that they arise in many medical, environmental and industrial applications of heat and mass transfer. In this article, we study the solvability of one-dimensional nonhomogeneous multilayer diffusion problem. We use a new generalized integral transform, namely, 𝕄 ρ , m − {\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}- transform [Srivastava et al., https://doi.org/10.1016/S0252-9602(15)30061-8]. First, we reduce the nonhomogeneous multilayer diffusion problem into a sequence of one-layer diffusion problems including time-varying given functions, followed by solving a general nonhomogeneous one-layer diffusion problem via the 𝕄 ρ , m − {\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}- transform. Hence, by means of general interface conditions, a renewal equations’ system is determined. Finally, the 𝕄 ρ , m − {\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}- transform and its analytic inverse are used to obtain an explicit solution to the renewal equations’ system. Our results generalize those ones in [Rodrigo and Worthy, http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/ j.jmaa.2016.06.042].

1.1
Mathematical modeling for nonhomogeneous n-layer diffusion systems

A one-dimensional diffusion problem in an n-layered permeable medium is set out as follows. Let

\alpha=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n-1}<x_{n}=\beta

be a finite partition of the interval
[\alpha,\beta]

[x_{j-1},x_{j}]

\varphi_{j}(x,t)

\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial t}=d_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{j}}{\partial x^{2}}+\lambda(t,\uptau)r_{j}(x,t),\ x\in(x_{j-1},x_{j}),\ \ \ t,\uptau>0,

(1.1)

where
d_{j}\geqslant 0

1\leqslant j\leqslant n
, are the diffusion coefficients and

\lambda(t,\uptau)=\bigg(\frac{t^{m}}{\uptau^{m}}+\uptau^{m}\bigg)^{-\rho},\quad t\geqslant 0,\;\uptau>0,

(1.2)

with
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{1,2,3,\cdots\},\ \rho\in{\mathbb{C}},\mbox{Re}(\rho)>0
. Here, the function-term
\lambda(t,\uptau)r_{j}(x,t)
physically means the external source term that could be applied to the diffusion equation with
r_{j}(x,t)
depends on time and space while the other factor of the source term i.e.,
p
depends only on time. This last term could be for instance, a periodic-time magnetic source.

The initial conditions (ICs) are assumed as

\varphi_{j}(x,0)=\eta_{j}(x),\quad x\in[x_{j-1},x_{j}],\,1\leqslant j\leqslant n.

(1.3)

The boundary conditions (BCs) are posited as

1.

The outer BCs (at the inlet
x=\alpha
and the outlet
x=\beta
) are general Robin boundary conditions as

\displaystyle\imath\varphi_{1}(\alpha,t)+\iota\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial x}(\alpha,t)=\lambda(t,\uptau)\zeta(t),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\ell\varphi_{n}(\beta,t)+\l\frac{\partial\varphi_{n}}{\partial x}(\beta,t)=\lambda(t,\uptau)\xi(t),

(1.5)

for all

, with
\imath,\iota,\ell
and
\l
are constants satisfying,
|\imath|+|\iota|>0,\ |\ell|+|\l|>0
.

2.

The inner BCs (the interface conditions) are

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}(x_{j},t)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{j}\varphi_{j+1}(x_{j},t),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\nu_{j}\varphi_{j}(x_{j},t)+\mu_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x}(x_{j},t)

1.2
Srivastava-Luo-Raina generalized integral transform

In
[
33
]
, Srivastava et al. introduced the following generalized integral transform

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-st}\varphi(\uptau t)}{(t^{m}+\uptau^{m})^{\rho}}dt,

(1.14)

for a continuous (or piecewise continuous) function
\varphi
on
[0,\infty)
, where
\rho\in{\mathbb{C}};\operatorname{Re}(\rho)\geqslant 0;m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
s>0
is the transform variable and
\uptau>0
is a parameter. The basic properties of the
{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}-
transform are given in
[
33
]
. Next we recall some of these properties, which are needed in the present work. Indeed, as introduced in
[
33
]
the
{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}-
transform is closely related with the well-known integral transforms, the Laplace, natural and Sumudu transforms. The Laplace transform is defined by

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{L}}}[\varphi(t)](s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\varphi(t)dt,\;\operatorname{Re}(s)>0.

(1.15)

So, from (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) we have the following duality relations

{\mathbb{L}}[\varphi(t)](s)={\mathbb{M}}_{0,m}[\varphi(t)](s,1),\;\operatorname{Re}(s)>0,

{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)={\mathbb{L}}\left[\frac{\varphi(\uptau t)}{(t^{m}+\uptau^{m})^{\rho}}\right](s),\;s,\uptau>0,

{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)=\frac{1}{\uptau}{\mathbb{L}}\left[\frac{\varphi(t)}{\left(\frac{t^{m}}{\uptau^{m}}+\uptau^{m}\right)^{\rho}}\right](\frac{s}{\uptau}),\;s,\uptau>0,

and,

{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}\left[\left(\frac{t^{m}}{\uptau^{m}}+\uptau^{m}\right)^{\rho}\varphi(t)\right](s,\uptau)={\mathbb{L}}\left[\varphi(\uptau t)\right](s),\;s,\uptau>0.

Setting
\rho=0
in (
1.14
), we recover the natural transform defined as (see
[
4
]
,
[
31
]
)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{N}}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\varphi(\uptau t)dt,\;s>0,\uptau>0.

(1.16)

Thus, we have the following
{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}-{\mathbb{N}}-
transforms duality

{\mathbb{N}}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)={\mathbb{M}}_{0,m}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)={\mathbb{N}}\left[\frac{\varphi(t)}{\left(\frac{t^{m}}{\uptau^{m}}+\uptau^{m}\right)^{\rho}}\right](s,\uptau),\;s>0,\uptau>0,

(1.17)

and,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}\left[\left(\frac{t^{m}}{\uptau^{m}}+\uptau^{m}\right)^{\rho}\varphi(t)\right](s,\uptau)={\mathbb{N}}\left[\varphi(t)\right](s,\uptau),\;s>0,\uptau>0.

(1.18)

The Sumudu transform is defined by (
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}[\varphi(t)](\uptau)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\varphi(\uptau t)dt,\;\uptau>0

Thus,

{\mathbb{S}}[\varphi(t)](\uptau)={\mathbb{M}}_{0,m}[\varphi(t)](0,\uptau),\;\uptau>0,

and,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}}_{\rho,m}[\varphi(t)](s,\uptau)=\frac{1}{s}{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\varphi(t)}{\left(\frac{t^{m}}{\uptau^{m}}+\uptau^{m}\right)^{\rho}}\right]\left(\frac{\uptau}{s}\right),\;s,\uptau>0.

4
Conclusion

Throughout the current contribution, a one-dimensional n-layer nonhomogeneous diffusion problem with time-varying data and general interface conditions have been concluded by means of a generalized integral transform. Although, most of the previous works have been focused on solving the problems of the homogeneous diffusion equation, the nonhomogeneous diffusion equation problem arises in many physical application. We have obtained the exact solutions for one- and multi-layer nonhomogeneous diffusion problems. The former case has been solved by a new generalized integral transform, the later one (n-layer problem) has been recast in a sequence of one layer problems. The obtained results generalize and extend those in

,

and
[

Our results motivate to deal with other types of diffusion problems. For example, reaction diffusion problems, Advection-reaction diffusion problems and non-autonomous reaction diffusion problems, etc.

On the other hand, more general partial differential equations (PDEs) and systems can be considered. for example, system of coupled PDEs, nonlinear diffusion PDEs and non-autonomous reaction diffusion PDEs. Those kinds of PDEs appear widely as epidemiological models to study and analyze the spread of diseases and pandemics

On upper bounds of frieze patterns
Jon Cheah, Antoine de Saint Germain

Abstract
. In this note, we show that the sequence of maximum values in frieze patterns of type A n A_{n} is the sequence of Fibonacci numbers, and that of frieze patterns of type C n C_{n} is the sequence of odd Fibonacci numbers.



1.
Introduction

For a positive integer
n
, a (Coxeter)
frieze pattern
of width
n
is an infinite array of
(n+2)
staggered rows

(1)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccccc}\cdots&&1&&1&&1&&\cdots&&&&&&\\
&\cdots&&a_{1}&&b_{1}&&c_{1}&&\cdots&&&&&\\
&&\cdots&&a_{2}&&b_{2}&&c_{2}&&\cdots&&&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&&\ddots&&\ddots&&&&&\\
&&&&\cdots&&a_{n}&&b_{n}&&c_{n}&&\cdots&&\\
&&&&&\cdots&&1&&1&&1&&\cdots&\\
\end{array}

consisting entirely of positive integers satisfying the so-called
diamond rule
:
ad=1+bc
whenever the positive integers
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
form a “diamond”

\begin{array}[]{ccc}&b&\\
a&&d\\
&c&\end{array}

in the array. Frieze patterns were introduced by H. S. M. Coxeter
[
Cox71
]
, and further studied together with J.H. Conway
[
CC73
]
. In the years following their inception, many modifications of the diamond rule were considered (see
[
MG15
, §5]
for a large list). One important generalisation was given in the mid-2000s, when P. Caldero and F. Chapoton
[
CC06
]
realised frieze patterns of a given width n
as evaluations of distinguished ring homomorphisms
on all cluster variables in the cluster algebra (with trivial coefficients) of type
A_{n}
, thus unveiling the cluster algebra nature of frieze patterns. Motivated by this, I. Assem, C. Reutenauer and D. Smith
[
ARS10
]

introduced the following generalisation of Coxeter’s frieze patterns.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})
be an
n\times n
symmetrisable generalised Cartan matrix (see Definition
2.1
). A
frieze pattern of type A
is a map

f:\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that

f(i,m)f(i,m+1)=1+\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}f(j,m)^{-a_{j,i}}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}f(j,m+1)^{-a_{j,i}},\quad\forall i,\forall m.

The set of frieze patterns of type A is denoted
{\rm Frieze}(A)
.

When
A=(a_{i,j})
is the so-called standard Cartan matrix of type
A_{n}
(see §
2.1
), one recovers (Coxeter) frieze patterns of width
n
by placing
each tuple
(f(1,m),\ldots,f(n,m)),m\in\mathbb{Z}
in the
m^{\rm th}
North-West to South-East diagonal in (
1
).

Remarkably, the set
{\rm Frieze}(A)
is finite if and only if
A
is of “finite type”, i.e.
A
is positive definite (c.f.
[
FZ03
,
GM22
]
). In other words, frieze patterns in the sense of Definition
1.1
exhibit a classification similar to the Cartan-Killing classification of semisimple Lie algebras over
\mathbb{C}
, or to the Fomin-Zelevinsky classification
[
FZ03
]
of cluster algebras! It is well known that such a classification can be broken down into 4 infinite families (denoted
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
and
D_{n}
) and five exceptional types
G_{2},F_{4},E_{6},E_{7}
and
E_{8}
.

When
A
is of finite type, it is natural to ask for the cardinality
c(A)
of the set
{\rm Frieze}(A)
. A first step in this direction was achieved by Coxeter and Conway
[
CC73
]
, who constructed a beautiful bijection from
{\rm Frieze}(A_{n})
to the set of triangulations of the regular
(n+3)
-gon, whereby
c(A_{n})=C_{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+2}\binom{2n+2}{n+1}
,
the
(n+1)^{\rm st}
Catalan number
. Subsequently, formulas
have been obtained for
c(A)
when
A
is of type
B_{n},C_{n},D_{n},G_{2},F_{4}
and
E_{6}
. At the time of writing,

c(E_{7})
and
c(E_{8})
are not known (but see
[
FP16
,
GM22
]
for conjectural values).

Keeping with the assumption that
A
is of finite type, Coxeter
[
Cox71
]
proved in type
A_{n}
and
[
Kel13
]
proved in general that every
f\in{\rm Frieze}(A)
is periodic in the second component, i.e. there exists
N_{A}
such that

f(i,m)=f(i,m+N_{A}),\quad\forall i,\forall m.

In light of the finiteness of

{\rm Frieze}(A)
and the periodicity of each
f\in{\rm Frieze}(A)
, one has the well-defined
number

(2)

u_{A}:=\max(f(i,m):f\in{\rm Frieze}(A),i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},m\in\mathbb{Z}).

In this article, we determine
u_{A}
when
A
is of type
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
. Our results are summarised as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be a Cartan matrix of finite type. Then,

(1)

if
A
is of type
A_{n}
, then
u_{A}=F_{n+2}
; and

(2)

if
A
is of type
C_{n}
then
u_{A}=F_{2n+1}
,

where
F_{n}
is the
n^{\rm th}
Fibonacci number, defined by
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
and
F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_{k}
.

We point out that G. Muller
[
Mul23
]
has recently obtained a (very large but explicit) upper bound for
u_{A}
, using only elementary properties of Cartan matrices.

Statement (1) of Theorem 1 and its proof have been formally verified by the Lean4 proof assistant. The formalisation project is available at
https://antoine-dsg.github.io/frieze_patterns
.

The article is structured as follows. In §
2.1
, we recall the definitions of Cartan matrices and folding. We prove statement (1) of Theorem
1
in §
2.2
and statement (2) of Theorem
1
in §
2.3
.

2.1.
Cartan matrices and folding

Definition 2.1
.

A
symmetrisable generalised Cartan matrix
is an
n\times n
integral matrix

A=(a_{i,j})_{i,j=1,\ldots,n}
such that

(1)

a_{i,i}=2
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

(2)

a_{i,j}\leq 0
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
i\neq j
, with
a_{i,j}=0
if and only if
a_{j,i}=0
, and

(3)

there exists a diagonal matrix
D
with positive integers on the diagonal such that
DA
is symmetric.

A symmetrisable generalised Cartan matrix is said to be of
finite type
if it is positive definite. If there exists a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns of
A
such that
A
is block diagonal, we say that
A
is
decomposable
. If not, we say that
A
is
indecomposable
.

It is well-known that indecomposable Cartan matrices of finite type are classified into four classical series
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
and five exceptional types labeled as
G_{2},F_{4},E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
. For example,
G_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&-3\\
-1&2\end{array}\right)
. In this article, the
standard Cartan matrix of type
A_{n}
is the
n\times n
integer matrix

(3)

A_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}2&-1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-1&2&-1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-1&2&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&2&-1\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-1&2\end{array}\right).

Similarly, the
standard Cartan matrix of type
C_{n}
is the
n\times n
integer matrix

(4)

C_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}2&-1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-1&2&-1&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-1&2&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&2&-2\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-1&2\end{array}\right).

It is well-known (see
[
FP16
]
) that

C_{n}
is a “folding” of type
A_{2n-1}
via a certain automorphism
\sigma
. This allows us to reduce questions about
C_{n}
to
“
\sigma
-invariant” questions about
A_{2n-1}
. The precise meaning of “
\sigma
-invariant” depends on the question; a precise formulation of “
\sigma
-invariant” frieze patterns will be given in §
2.3
.

2.3.

We rewrite Definition

Definition 2.9
.

A
frieze pattern of type
C_{n}
is a map
f:\{1,\dots,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}

(7)

f(i,m)f(i,m+1)=\begin{cases}1+f(i+1,m)f(i-1,m+1)&\quad\text{ if }i=1,\ldots,n-1,\\

where by convention
f(0,m):=1

The following lemma is a phenomenon of “folding” from friezes of type
A_{2n-1}
to friezes of type
C_{n}
; a geometric version can be found in
[
FP16
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
a\in{\rm Frieze}(A_{2n-1})
, and suppose that for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(8)

a(n+j,m)=a(n-j,m+j),\quad j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}.

Then the function
c:\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
given by

(9)

c(n-i,m)=a(n-i,-m+i),\quad i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},m\in\mathbb{Z},

is a frieze pattern of type
C_{n}
. Moreover, every frieze pattern of type
C_{n}
arises in this way.

Proof.

To prove the first claim, it is sufficient to show that the function
c
thus defined satisfies (
7
) for all indices. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be arbitrary. We proceed by a case-by-case check on the index
i
.

Case 1:
Suppose that
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Set
k=n-i
, so that
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.

\displaystyle c(k,m)\;c(k,m+1)

\displaystyle=a(k,-m+i)\;a(k,-(m+1)+i)

\displaystyle=1+a(k+1,-(m+1)+i)\;a(k-1,-m+i)

\displaystyle=1+a(n-(i-1),-m+(i-1))\,a(n-(i+1),-(m+1)+(i+1))

\displaystyle=1+c(k+1,m)\;c(k-1,m+1).

Case 2:
Suppose that
i=0
. Then

\displaystyle c(n,m)\,c(n,m+1)

\displaystyle=a(n,-m)\,a(n,-m-1)

\displaystyle=1+a(n+1,-m-1)\,a(n-1,-m)

\displaystyle=1+a(n+1,-m-1)\,a(n-1,-m-1+1)

\displaystyle=1+a(n-1,-(m+1)+1)^{2},

\displaystyle=1+c(n-1,m+1)^{2},

where in the penultimate line we have used the assumption in the statement of the Lemma. This concludes the proof that every
A_{2n-1}
frieze pattern gives rise to a
C_{n}
frieze. To see that the reverse hold, let
c\in{\rm Frieze}(C_{n})
, and define

a:\{1,\ldots,2n-1\}\times\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0},

via the conditions (
8
) and (
9
). To show that the resulting function satisfies (
5
), we again proceed by cases on
i\in\{1,\ldots,2n-1\}
.

Case 1:

i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Set
k=n-i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Then,

\displaystyle a(i,m)\,a(i,m+1)

\displaystyle=a(n-k,m)\,a(n-k,m+1)

\displaystyle=a(n-k,-(-m+k)+k)\,a(n-k,-(-m-1+k)+k)

\displaystyle=c(n-k,-m+k)\,c(n-k,-m+k-1)

\displaystyle=1+c(n-k+1,-m+k-1)\,c(n-k-1,-m+k)

\displaystyle=1+c(n-(k-1),-m+k-1)\,c(n-(k+1),-m+k)

\displaystyle=1+a(i+1,m)\,a(i-1,m+1).

Case 2:

i=n

\displaystyle a(n,m)\,a(n,m+1)

\displaystyle=c(n,-m)\,c(n,-m-1)

\displaystyle=1+c(n-1,-m)^{2}

\displaystyle=1+a(n-1,m+1)^{2}

\displaystyle=1+a(n+1,m)\,a(n-1,m+1).

The remaining case
i\in\{n+1,\ldots,2n-1\}
reduces to the first case via (
8

\{i,j\}
of non-consecutive vertices. Each diagonal can be uniquely written as an ordered pair
(i,j)
with
i<j
. We say that two diagonals
(i_{1},i_{2})
and
(j_{1},j_{2})

intersect
if
i_{1}=j_{1}
and
i_{2}=j_{2}
, or if

i_{1}<j_{1}<i_{2}<j_{2},\text{ or }j_{1}<i_{1}<j_{2}<i_{2}.

Otherwise, the two diagonals are said to be
non-intersecting
. A
triangulation
of
P_{n+3}
is any maximal (w.r.t. inclusion) collection of (pairwise) non-intersecting diagonals. Each triangulation contains exactly
n
diagonals. By
[
CC73
]
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between frieze patterns of type
A_{n}
and triangulations of
P_{n+3}
(see Remark
2.8
for an example of a triangulation).

According to the proof of
[
FP16
, Theorem 4.2]
, frieze patterns of type
C_{n}
are in one-to-one correspondence (using the algebraic procedure from Lemma
2.10
) with frieze patterns of type
A_{2n-1}
coming from triangulations of
P_{2n+2}
that are symmetric under central symmetry, i.e. the symmetry which sends the vertex
i
to the vertex
i+n+1
.

Proof of (2) of Theorem
1
.

By Lemma
2.10
and statement (1) of Theorem
1
, we have
u_{C_{n}}\leq u_{A_{2n-1}}=F_{2n+1}
. It remains to show that there exists a frieze pattern of type
C_{n}
that realises this upper bound. By Remark
2.8
and the discussion following Lemma
2.10
, it is sufficient to show that the zig-zag triangulation of
P_{2n+2}
is symmetric under the symmetry sending the vertex
i
to the vertex
i+n+1
. One checks directly that diagonal of the zig-zag triangulation is of the form
(2+i,2n+2-i)
for
i=0,\ldots,n-2
or
(2+i+1,2n+2-i)
for
i=0,\ldots,n-3
, and that the set of diagonals in this triangulation is indeed preserved.
The resulting frieze pattern of type
C_{n}
, denoted
c
, satisfies
c(i,0)=F_{2i+1}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
(see Figure
2.11
for the case
n=3
). ∎

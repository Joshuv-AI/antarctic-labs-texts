Junfeng Li, Changxing Miao, Ankang Yu

Abstract
. In this paper, we studied the space-time estimates for the solution to the Schrödinger equation. By polynomial partitioning, induction arguments, bilinear to linear arguments and broad norm estimates, we set up several maximal estimates for the Schrödinger equation with high frequency input data. By these maximal estimates we obtain the sharp global space-time estimate when n = 2 n=2 and improve the known results in the critical cases when n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . The maximal estimate for n = 2 n=2 is also used to extend the results of the local space-time estimates for the solution to the Schrödinger equation.

1.
Introduction

Given the free Schr
\ddot{\text{o}}
dinger equation:

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=0,\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{n+1}

with an initial data
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, its solution can be written as

u(t,x):=e^{it\Delta}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i(x\cdot\xi-2\pi t|\xi|^{2})}d\xi,

where
\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx
. A nature problem is to understand the solution
u(t,x)
with an initial data
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the Sobolev space. The well-known Srichartz estimate is a typical estimate. It is a time-space type estimate which weight the solution in
L^{r}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Such a estimate is crucial in the study of the linear and nonlinear Schrödinger equations. By the end of the last century, it was fully established.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
21
,
29
]
.
Let
r\geq 2
,
2\leq p<\infty
and
\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2}{r}\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Then

(1.1)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{t}^{r}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},\ \ \ \ s=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p}-\frac{2}{r}.

In this paper, we first consider the space-time counter part of the Strichartz estimate. That is weight the function
u(t,x)
in the space
L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.
In
[
27
]
Planchon formulated the following space-time conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
2\leq r,p<\infty
and
\frac{n+1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Then

(1.2)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{p}L^{r}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},\ \ \ \ s=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p}-\frac{2}{r}.

Both inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are the anisotropic generalization of the adjoint estimate of the Fourier restriction estimate. They satisfy some common symmetries such as translation invariance and scaling invariance. The former allows us to do translations over
x
and
t
, and the latter predicts the relation
s=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p}-\frac{2}{r}
. The necessary conditions for the index pairs
(p,r)
can be verified by Knapp’s example.

The estimates (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) behave differently under Galilean transforms. The Strichartz estimate (
1.1
) is Galilean invariant. By which we can make translation in the frequency space. While the space-time estimate (
1.2
) is not Galilean invariant which makes space-time estimate more difficult. And the estimate would depend on both the size and the position of the support of
\widehat{f}
.

1.1.
Maximal estimates

The space-time estimate and its variant has close relation with several important problems in harmonic analysis. One of the endpoint index pair
(\frac{2(n+1)}{n},\infty)
of its critical case appears naturally in the study of the well-known Carleson’s problem. Here and after, the index pair
(p,r)
is critical if
\frac{n+1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{n}{2}
. And the pair in the set {
(p,r):\frac{n+1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}<\frac{n}{2}
} will be called subcritical. In
[
4
]
Carleson formulated the problem to find the the infimum regularity
s_{c}
such that

\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}f(x)=f(x)\quad a.e.\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\forall f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In the last few decades, there were abundant literature such as
[
22
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
26
]
on this problem. So far, Carleson’s problem is well understood, and the critical index is
s_{c}=\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
for
n\geq 1
. The one dimensional case follows from the results of Carleson
[
4
]
and Dahlberg and Kenig
[
11
]
. For the high dimensional case, the necessary part follows from the counterexample formulated by Bourgain in
[
2
]
. To achieve the positive result, Du, Guth, and Li
[
9
]
set up the following
\epsilon
lost estimate

(1.3)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{3}L_{t}^{\infty}(B^{3}(0,R))}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

for
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{B}^{2}(0,1)
when
n=2
.
Motivated by the two dimensional case result, one may expect to set up the
\epsilon
lost estimate

(1.4)

\|e^{it\Delta}f(x)\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+1)}{n}}L^{\infty}_{t}(\mathbb{B}^{n+1}(0,R))}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,1)
. Which corresponding to endpoint index of Conjecture
1.2
. With (
1.4
), combining the
\epsilon
removal argument in
[
6
]
, one can obtain (
1.2
) for the whole subcritical case.

While Du, Kim, Wang, and Zhang
[
12
]
showed that the estimate (
1.4
) fails for
n\geq 3
. As a replacement, Du and Zhang
[
13
]
proved

(1.5)

\|\sup\limits_{0<t\leq R}|e^{it\Delta}f|\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,R))}\leq R^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}+\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\ \ \ \ \forall\epsilon>0,

for
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,1)
. It is a meaningful problem to find the sharp range of
p
such that

(1.6)

\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}|e^{it\Delta}f(x)|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,R))}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,1)
. Recently, Wu
[
34
]
, Cao, Miao, and Wang
[
7
]
have maken great improvement on this problem.

Since the local estimate (
1.4
) does not hold therefore the corresponding global estimate cannot hold either. Thus, Conjecture
1.2
becomes a meaningful replacement of the maximal estimate. To achieve this aim, we set up several maximal estimates with high frequency input functions. Which also reflect the fact that the frequency of the input function affects the space-time estimates.
Given
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
,
N\geq 1
and a usually very large number
R\geq 1
,
Q_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes a cube centered at
x_{0}
with side length
R
. A parallelepiped with direction
(4\pi Ne_{1},1)
and center
z_{0}
is

P_{R}(z_{0}):=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[-R+t_{0},R+t_{0}]:x-4\pi tNe_{1}\in Q_{R}(x_{0})\},

here
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
. The parameter
R
will be called as its size of
P_{R}(z_{0})
. Let
P_{R}=P_{R}(z_{0})
with
z_{0}=(0,0)
. We now state our main results in this part.

Theorem 1.3

(High frequency input endpoint estimate for
n=2
)
.

Let
\widehat{f}
be supported in
\mathbb{B}^{2}(Ne_{1},1)
with
N\geq 1
. For any
\epsilon>0
, we have

(1.7)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{3}L_{t}^{\infty}(P_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{3}}R^{\epsilon}||f||_{L^{2}}.

Theorem 1.4

(High frequency input estimate for
n\geq 3
)
.

Let
\widehat{f}
be supported in
\mathbb{B}^{n}(Ne_{1},1)
with
N\geq 1
. For any
\epsilon>0
, we have

(1.8)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\infty}(P_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\epsilon}||f||_{L^{2}},

whenever

p\geq\tilde{p}_{n}=2+\frac{4}{n+1+s_{2}^{n}}\ \text{with}\ \ s_{2}^{n}=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{i}.

Remark 1.5
.

The propagation speed of the Schrödinger wave
e^{it\Delta}f
depends on the location of
\widehat{f}
. In this case, the Schrödinger wave
e^{it\Delta}f
roughly propagates at speed
N
in the direction
(4\pi Ne_{1},1)
. Then for
|t|\leq R
, the propagation region of the wave is essentially concentrated in
P_{R}
. Since there is no Galilean invariant, the estimate with different frequency input is inevitable.

2.
High frequency input endpoint estimate for
n=2

We devote this section to prove Theorem
1.3
by an induction argument. Moreover, we set up the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p>3
and
\ M\geq 1
,
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{B}^{2}(\xi_{0},\frac{1}{M})\subset\mathbb{B}^{2}(Ne_{1},1)
for some
N\geq 1
.
For any
\epsilon>0
,
R\geq 1
, and
r>1/\epsilon^{4}
, there exists a constant
C_{p,\epsilon}
such
that

(2.1)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}(P_{R})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}M^{-\epsilon^{2}}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Since the constant
C_{p,\epsilon}
does not depend on
r
, by taking
r\to\infty
and
M=1
, we have

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\infty}(P_{R})}\leq C_{p,\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

By interpolating with the trivial estimate

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}(P_{R})}\lesssim(NR^{2})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

we arrive that

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{3}L_{t}^{\infty}(P_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{3}}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

2.1.
Wave packet decomposition

Wave packet decomposition is a fundamental tool in the proofs. In this subsection, we collect several basic concepts and properties of the wave packets without proof. We refer the readers to
[
32
]
for the details.

Let
\phi
be a Schwartz function satisfying
\text{supp}\,\widehat{\phi}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,3/2)
and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k)=1,\ \ \ \ \text{for all}\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Given
\lambda>0
, let

(2.2)

\widehat{\phi_{\theta}}(\xi):={\lambda}^{\frac{n}{2}}\widehat{\phi}({\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi-c(\theta))),\ \widehat{\phi_{\theta,\nu}}(\xi):=e^{-2\pi ic(\nu)\cdot\xi}\widehat{\phi_{\theta}}(\xi),

where
c(\theta)\in{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
c(\nu)\in{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
\theta
denotes a
{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
-ball in the frequency space and
\nu
denotes a
{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}
-ball in the physical space. Usually
\phi_{\theta,\nu}
is called as a wave packet with scale
\lambda
.
Let
\Omega:=\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A wave packet decomposition of
f
at scale
\lambda
is

(2.3)

f=\sum_{\theta,\nu}c_{\theta,\nu}\phi_{\theta,\nu}:=\sum_{\theta,\nu}f_{\theta,\nu},\ \ \theta\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing.

Then

(2.4)

e^{it\Delta}f=\sum_{\theta,\nu}c_{\theta,\nu}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{\theta,\nu}:=\sum_{\theta,\nu}e^{it\Delta}f_{\theta,\nu}.

These decompositions satisfy the following properties.

•

The Fourier transform of
f_{\theta,\nu}
is supported on
\theta
, and the Fourier transform of
e^{it\Delta}f_{\theta,\nu}
is supported on the paraboloid

\big\{(\xi,\tau);\tau=2\pi|\xi|^{2},\xi\in\theta\big\}.

•

The functions

\sum_{\theta,\nu}|c_{\theta,\nu}|^{2}\approx\sum_{\theta,\nu}\|f_{\theta,\nu}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\approx\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

•

By the stationary phase method,

|e^{it\Delta}\phi_{\theta,\nu}|\leq{\lambda}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\chi_{T_{\theta,\nu}}(x,t)+\text{RapDec}(\lambda),\ 0\leq t\leq\lambda.

.

with the direction

Since the Fourier transform of

The induction argument and the polynomial partitioning

Theorem

When

When

If

Then, for

and

\displaystyle||e^{it\Delta}f||_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}(P_{R})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big|\sum_{\theta,\nu}c_{\theta,\nu}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{\theta,\nu}\Big|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{p}{2}}L_{t}^{\frac{r}{2}}(P_{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{O(\delta)}\sup_{\theta_{0}}\Big\|\sum_{\nu}\Big|c_{\theta_{0},\nu}e^{it\Delta}\phi_{\theta_{0},\nu}\Big|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{p}{2}}L_{t}^{\frac{r}{2}}(P_{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{O(\delta)}N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}R^{\frac{3}{2p}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}}||f||_{L^{2}}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}M^{-\epsilon^{2}}R^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore it is sufficient to consider the case

and

.

Induction Assumption:

For simplicity, assume

[

•

covers

.

By the construction of

|X|\sim\frac{M}{N}|A_{H}|.

Decompose
P_{R}
into parallelepipeds

with size

3.1.
2
-broad norm

BL_{A}^{p,\infty}

Let parameter

\mathbb{B}^{n}(\xi_{0},\frac{1}{M})

\tau

Let

Given a linear subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we denote
\tau\in V
if and only if

\angle(G(\tau),V)<(KM)^{-1},

where

\angle(G(\tau),V)=\min\{\angle(v,v^{\prime}):\ \text{non-zero vectors}\ v,v^{\prime}\ \text{satisfy}\ v\in V\ \text{and}\ v^{\prime}\in G(\tau)\}.

Otherwise, we write
\tau\notin V
. The cube
Q_{R}
is decomposed into sub-cubes
Q_{K}
of side length
K
. And the time interval
[0,R]
is also decomposed into intervals
\ I_{K/N}
of length
K/N
. Denote

D_{K}:=\{x:(x,t)\in\mathcal{G}^{-1}(B_{K}\times I_{K/N})\},

then we obtain a finite overlap decomposition

Given a positive integer
A
, we define

\mu_{e^{it\Delta}f}(D_{K}\times I_{K/N}):=\min_{V_{1},...V_{A}}\Big(\max_{\tau\notin V_{a}\ \text{for all}\ a}\int_{D_{K}\times I_{K/N}}|e^{it\Delta}f_{\tau}|^{p}dxdt\Big),

where
V_{1},...,V_{A}
are
lines
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. For any subset

, the
2
-broad norm
BL_{A}^{p,\infty}(U)
is defined by

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{BL_{A}^{p,\infty}(U)}^{p}:=\sum_{D_{K}}\max_{I_{K/N}}\frac{|U\cap(D_{K}\times I_{K/N})|}{|D_{K}\times I_{K/N}|}\mu_{e^{it\Delta}f}(D_{K}\times I_{K/N}).

The

-norm is not continuous, thus it can not be used to run the polynomial partitioning argument. For this purpose, we recall the

-norm defined as

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{BL_{A}^{p,q}(U)}^{p}:=\sum_{D_{K}}\Big[\sum_{I_{K/N}}\Big(\frac{|U\bigcap(D_{K}\times I_{K/N})|}{|D_{K}\times I_{K/N}|}\mu_{e^{it\Delta}f}(D_{K}\times I_{K/N})\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big]^{\frac{p}{q}}.

The

Proposition 3.2
.

[
8
,
16
]

Let

. The following properties hold:

(i)

Let

and

. Then

(ii)

Let

and

. Then

(iii)

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
1\leq p,p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
and
A\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that

satisfies

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\alpha}{p_{0}}+\frac{\alpha}{p_{1}}.

Moreover,

estimate can be reduced to

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
\epsilon>0
, and

with
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(\xi_{0},\frac{1}{M})\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}(Ne_{1},1)
. If

||e^{it\Delta}f||_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\infty}(P_{R})}\lesssim_{p,\epsilon}N^{\frac{1}{p}}W||f||_{L^{2}}

holds for some

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{BL_{A}^{p,\infty}(P_{R})}\lesssim_{p,\epsilon}K^{O(1)}W\|f\|_{L^{2}}

Proof.

By the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{BL_{A}^{p,\infty}(P_{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{D_{K}}\max_{I_{K/N}}\mu_{e^{it\Delta}f}(D_{K}\times I_{K/N})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{D_{K}}\max_{I_{K/N}}\sum_{\tau}\|e^{it\Delta}f_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(D_{K}\times I_{K/N})}^{p}

Since

and the number of

is at most

∎

Appendix B
A bilinear space-time estimate conjecture

We now formulate a bilinear space-time conjecture which implies Conjecture
1.2
.

Conjecture B.1

(Bilinear space-time estimate)
.

Suppose that
\widehat{f_{1}},\ \widehat{f_{2}}
are supported on subsets of
\mathbb{B}^{n}(Ne_{1},1)
with
\text{dist}(\text{supp}\widehat{f_{1}},\text{supp}\widehat{f_{2}})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

(B.1)

\||e^{it\Delta}f_{1}e^{it\Delta}f_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq C_{p,r}N^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\|f_{1}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}},

holds if and only if
\frac{n+2}{p}+\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{n+1}{2},2\leq p,r<\infty
.

Remark B.2
.

(I)

By the same argument of Subsection
5
of this paper, we have Conjecture
B.1

\implies
Conjecture
1.2
.

(II)

Conjecture
B.1
is also closely related to Conjecture
1.9
. By Proposition
6.4
, we have

\text{Conjecture \ref{c1.2}}\ \implies\ \text{Conjecture \ref{c1.4} holds for}\ p>\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\ \text{and}\ r\geq 2(n+1).

The above conjecture is also deeply related to the Fourier restrictive conjecture of paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(see
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
). However, it is very difficult to prove the restrictive conjecture with the endpoint
\frac{2(n+1)}{n}
. Conjecture

provides a new viewpoint to improve Conjecture

.

We now give a necessary condition for (
B.1
) by modifying an example from Foschi-Klainerman
[

]
.

Proposition B.3
.

Let
p,r\in[2,\infty)
. If (

) is true, then

\frac{n+2}{p}+\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{n+1}{2}.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
,

\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)=\chi_{\{|\xi_{1}-(N+1/2)|<\epsilon\}}(\xi_{1})\chi_{\{|\xi_{2}-1/2|<\epsilon^{2}\}}(\xi_{2})\chi_{\{|\xi_{3}|<\epsilon\}}(\xi_{3})...\chi_{\{|\xi_{n}|<\epsilon\}}(\xi_{n})

An easy calculus shows that

\displaystyle|\xi|^{2}

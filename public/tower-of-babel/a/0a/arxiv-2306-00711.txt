Long time well-posedness and full justification of a Whitham-Green-Naghdi system
Louis Emerald, Martin Oen Paulsen

Abstract
. We establish the full justification of a “Whitham-Green-Naghdi” system modeling the propagation of surface gravity waves with bathymetry in the shallow water regime. It is an asymptotic model of the water waves equations with the same dispersion relation. The model under study is a nonlocal quasilinear symmetrizable hyperbolic system without surface tension. We prove the consistency of the general water waves equations with our system at the order of precision O ​ ( μ 2 ​ ( ε + β ) ) O(\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)) , where μ \mu is the shallow water parameter, ε {\varepsilon} the nonlinearity parameter, and β \beta the topography parameter. Then we prove the long time well-posedness on a time scale O ⁡ ( 1 max ⁡ { ε , β } ) O(\frac{1}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}) . Lastly, we show the convergence of the solutions of the Whitham-Green-Naghdi system to the ones of the water waves equations on the later time scale.

1.
Introduction

In this article, we study a full dispersion Green-Naghdi system that describes strongly dispersive surface waves over a variable bottom. The system under consideration is described in terms of the unknowns
\zeta
,
v
, and
b
. Here
\zeta(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the surface elevation,
v(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
is related to the velocity field described by the full Euler equations, and
b
is the elevation of the bathymetry. The system reads,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\zeta+\partial_{x}(hv)=0\\
(h+\mu h\mathcal{T}[h,\beta b])\big(\partial_{t}v+{\varepsilon}v\partial_{x}v\big)+h\partial_{x}\zeta+\mu\varepsilon h(\mathcal{Q}[h,v]+\mathcal{Q}_{b}[h,b,v])=0,\end{cases}

where
h=1+{\varepsilon}\zeta-\beta b
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[h,\beta b]v

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3h}\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(h^{3}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v\big)+\frac{1}{2h}\big(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)v)-h^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+(\beta\partial_{x}b)^{2}v,

and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}[h,v]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3h}\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(h^{3}(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v)^{2}\big)

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{b}[h,\beta b,v]

\displaystyle=h(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v)^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)+\frac{1}{2h}\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{2}v^{2}\beta\partial_{x}^{2}b)+v^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}^{2}b)(\beta\partial_{x}b),

with
\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}
being a Fourier multiplier associated with the dispersion relation of the water waves system. Specifically, if we let
\hat{f}(\xi)
be the Fourier transform of
f
, then the symbol is defined in frequency by

(1.5)

\widehat{\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}f}(\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\mu\xi^{2}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{\mu}\xi}{\tanh(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)}-1\Big)}\hat{f}(\xi).

The parameters
\mu,{\varepsilon}
, and
\beta
are defined by the comparison between characteristic quantities of the system under study. Among those are the characteristic water depth
H_{0}
, the characteristic wave amplitude
a_{s}
, the characteristic bathymetry amplitude
a_{b}
, and the characteristic wavelength
L
. From these comparisons appear three adimensional parameters of main importance:

•

\mu:=\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{L^{2}}
is the shallow water parameter,

•

\varepsilon:=\frac{a_{\mathrm{s}}}{H_{0}}
is the nonlinearity parameter,

•

\beta:=\frac{a_{b}}{H_{0}}
is the bathymetry parameter.

Replacing the Fourier multiplier
\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}
by identity in system (
1.1
) we retrieve the classical Green-Naghdi system. The later system is proved to be consistent with the water waves equations, in the sense of Definition 5.1 in
[
31
]
, at the order of precision
O(\mu^{2})
for parameters
(\mu,\varepsilon,\beta)
in the shallow water regime:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mu_{\max}>0
, then we define the shallow water regime to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SW}}:=\{(\mu,\varepsilon,\beta):\mu\in(0,\mu_{\max}],\varepsilon\in[0,1],\beta\in[0,1]\}.

Taking
{\varepsilon}
to be zero in (
1.1
), we get the linearized water waves equations around the rest state with the following dispersion relation

(1.6)

\omega_{WW}(\xi)^{2}=\xi^{2}\frac{\tanh(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)}{\sqrt{\mu}\xi}.

This is why we say that system (
1.1
) is a full dispersion Green-Naghdi model. Moreover, it is proved in the present paper that the water waves equations are consistent, in the sense of Proposition
3.2
, with system (
1.1
) at the order of precision
O(\mu^{2}(\varepsilon+\beta))
. The improved precision compared to the classical Green-Naghdi system allows for a change in the propagation of the waves. Such occurrences have been studied in the Dingemans experiments
[
7
]
. In these experiments, they investigated a long wave passing over a submerged obstacle. They observed that waves tend to steepen due to a compression effect from the bottom, where high harmonics generated by topography-induced nonlinear interactions are freely released behind the obstacle. This last phenomenon makes it natural that one wants to improve the frequency dispersion of the classical shallow water models. Deriving such models has been the subject of active research. Here are some references in the case of the Boussinesq model
[
24
,
33
,
5
]
. In the case of the Green-Naghdi model, one can consult
[
42
]
and
[
6
]
, where the authors compared the classical Green-Naghdi model with one-parameter and three-parameters Green-Naghdi models in one case of the Dingemans experiments for which the propagation and interaction of highly dispersive waves are under study. By tuning the parameters, they are able to describe the dispersion relation of the water waves equations for a larger set of frequencies. As an example, the dispersion relation of the three-parameter model is

(1.7)

\displaystyle\omega_{GN}(\xi)^{2}=\xi^{2}\frac{(1+\mu\frac{\theta+\gamma}{3}\xi^{2})(1+\mu\frac{\alpha-1}{3}\xi^{2})}{(1+\mu\frac{\gamma}{3}\xi^{2})(1+\mu\frac{\alpha+\theta}{3}\xi^{2})},

where the parameters
\alpha,\gamma
and
\theta
are chosen such that (
1.7
) approximates well the dispersion relation of the water waves equations, (
1.6
), for higher frequencies. In particular, for
(\theta,\alpha,\gamma)=(-1,1,1)
we obtain the original Green-Naghdi system. Moreover, in the case
(\theta,\alpha,\gamma)=(0.207,1,0.071)
it was demonstrated in
[
6
]
, that (
1.7
) is a better approximation of (
1.6
) (see Figure
1
). This improvement allowed the authors to describe strongly dispersive waves with uneven bathymetry accurately.

Figure 1.

\omega_{WW}(\xi)/\xi^{2}

In fact, in the case where high frequencies are dominant, the improved Green-Naghdi models tend to describe the propagation of the waves more correctly. However, in general, one can expect to have even higher frequency interactions for which one needs to keep the full dispersion relation of the water waves equations.

The first full dispersion model, called the Whitham equations, was introduced by Whitham in
[
43
]
to study breaking waves and Stokes waves of maximal amplitude. The existence of these phenomena for this model has been proved in the recent papers
[
20
,
25
,
38
,
40
]
. The Whitham is a classical model in oceanography and can be seen as a modified version of the Kordeweg-de Vries equations with lower frequency dispersion. In addition, the existence of periodic waves was proved in
[
19
]
, and the existence of Benjamin-Feir instabilities was demonstrated in
[
26
,
37
]
. See also the series of papers on the stability of traveling waves
[

,

,

,

]
,

The study of bidirectional full dispersion models for a flat bottom has also been the subject of active research. One class of such systems is the Whitham-Boussinesq ones. They are the full dispersion versions of the Boussinesq system, meaning they have the same dispersion relation as the water waves equations (
1.6
). Like the Whitham equation, these type of systems features solitary waves
[
8
,
34
]
, Benjamin-Feir instabilities
[
27
,
35
,
41
]
, high-frequency instabilities of small-amplitude periodic traveling waves
[
18
]
. See also some comparative studies between the Boussinesq and the Whitham-Boussinesq models
[
4
,
11
]
.

The full dispersion Whitham-Green-Naghdi models are next order approximations of the water waves equations when compared to the Whitham-Boussinesq systems. These systems were recently derived in
[
21
]
for a flat bottom and extended to include bathymetry in
[
14
]
. See also
[
13
]
where the authors derived a two-layers Whitham-Green-Naghdi system. There is still a lot of research left to be done on the study of qualitative properties of these systems, but we mention the work of Duchene et. al
[
16
]
, which proved the existence of solitary waves where they consider both surface gravity waves and internal waves.

An important part of the study of the full dispersion systems is the full justification as an asymptotic model of the water waves equations in the shallow water regime. To be more precise, we say a model is fully justified if the following points are proven:

•

The solutions of the water waves equations exist on the scale
O(\frac{1}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}})
.

•

The solutions of the asymptotic model exist on the scale
O(\frac{1}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}})
.

•

Solutions of the water waves equations solve the asymptotic model up to remainder terms of a specified order of precision in terms of the adimensional parameters
\mu,\varepsilon
, and
\beta
. This last point is called the consistency of the water waves equations with respect to the asymptotic model.

•

By virtue of the previous points, one has to show that the difference between the solutions of the water waves equations and the asymptotic model satisfies an error estimate depending polynomially on
\mu,{\varepsilon}
and
\beta
.

If we can verify these four points, then we can compare solutions of the water waves equations with solutions of the asymptotic models up to times of order
O(\frac{1}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}})
. The first point is proved by Alvarez-Samaniego and Lannes in
[
1
]
.

The three remaining points are specific to the asymptotic model. For instance, in the case of the Whitham equation, the local well-posedness in the relevant time scale follow by classical arguments on hyperbolic systems. The consistency of the water waves equations with this model has been recently proved in
[
22
]
at the order of precision
O(\mu\varepsilon)
in the unidirectional case, but the method supposes well-prepared initial conditions. In the bidirectional case, the author proved an order of precision
O(\mu\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})
and doesn’t suppose well-prepared initial conditions. In conclusion, we have the full justification of the Whitham equation at the order of precision
O(\mu\varepsilon)
in the unidirectional case under the restriction of well-prepared initial conditions. In the bidirectional case, the order of precision is
O(\mu\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})
.

Regarding the Whitham-Boussinesq systems for flat bottoms, the consistency of the water waves equations with the later models has been proved in
[
21
]
with an order of precision
O(\mu\varepsilon)
in the shallow water regime. When nonflat bottoms are considered, it has been proved in
[
14
]
to be consistent with the water waves with a precision
O(\mu({\varepsilon}+\beta))
. With respect to the second point of the justification, it has been proved for a large class of Whitham-Boussinesq systems with flat bottoms
[
36
,
23
]
, to be well-posed on the time scale
O(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}})
. Lastly, we also mention earlier results on the local-well posedness on a fixed time scale given in
[
9
,
10
,
12
]
.

For the Whitham-Green-Naghdi systems, it is proved in
[
21
]
that for a flat bottom, the water waves equations are consistent with the later systems at the order of precision
O(\mu^{2}\varepsilon)
in the shallow water regime. Moreover, in the case of uneven bathymetry, it has been proved in
[
14
]
that the precision order is
O(\mu^{2}(\varepsilon+\beta))
. In
[
13
]
, the authors proved the local well-posedness with a relevant time scale for a two-layer full dispersion Green-Naghdi model with surface tension. This system can be seen as a generalization of (
1.1
). However, their method relies on adding surface tension, where the time of existence tends to zero as the surface tension parameter goes to zero. Moreover, this system has only been proved to be consistent with the water waves equations at the order of precision
O(\mu^{2})
even if, based on numerical experiments, the expected seems to be
O(\mu^{2}\varepsilon)
.

In the present paper, we prove the full justification of the Whitham-Green-Naghdi system without surface tension (
1.1
) as an asymptotic model of the water waves equations at the order of precision
O(\mu^{2}(\varepsilon+\beta))
.

Definition and notations

•

a\leq c\>b
.

•

Let

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{G}f}(\xi)=G(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi).

For any

For any

.

•

The Sobolev space

is equivalent to the weighted

.

•

For any

.

•

Let

and

. A function

We say

If

Main results

Throughout this paper, we will always make the following fundamental assumption.

Definition 1.2

(Non-cavitation assumption)
.

Let
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
. We say the initial surface elevation
\zeta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and the bottom profile
b\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies the “non-cavitation assumption”  if there exist
h_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(1.8)

1+{\varepsilon}\zeta_{0}(x)-\beta b(x)\geq h_{0},\quad\text{for all}\>\>\>x\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, before we state the main results, we define the energy space associated to (
1.1
).

Definition 1.3
.

We define the complete function space
Y^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})\times X^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
X^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})
is a subspace of
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}})
equipped with the norm

|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}:=|v|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\sqrt{\mu}|\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}v|_{H^{s}}^{2},

and we make the definition

|(\zeta,v)|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}:=|\zeta|_{H^{s}}^{2}+|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

The following Theorem is one of the main results of the paper and concerns the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) on the relevant time scale
O(\frac{1}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}})
in the energy space.

Theorem 1.4

(Well-posedness)
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
(\mu,{\varepsilon},\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SW}}
. Assume that
(\zeta_{0},v_{0})\in Y^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the non-cavitation condition (
1.8
) and
b\in H^{s+2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then there exists
T=c\big(|(\zeta_{0},v_{0})|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}\big)^{-1}
such that (
1.1
) admits a unique solution

(\zeta,v)\in C([0,\frac{T}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]:Y^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}))\cap C^{1}([0,\frac{T}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]:Y^{s-1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})),

that satisfies

(1.9)

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\frac{T}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]}|(\zeta,v)|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}\lesssim|(\zeta_{0},v_{0})|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}.

Furthermore, there exists a neighborhood of
(\zeta_{0},v_{0})
such that the flow map

:Y_{\mu}^{s}({\mathbb{R}})\>\rightarrow\>C([0,\tfrac{T}{2\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}];Y^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})),\quad(\zeta_{0},v_{0})\>\>\mapsto\>\>(\zeta,v),

is continuous.

Remark 1.5
.

For the sake of simplicity, we restrict our study to the one-dimensional setting. We comment on the possible extension to two dimensions at the end of Section 3.

For the next Theorem, we will state the full justification of (
1.1
) as a water waves model. To give the result, we first state the water waves equations:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\zeta-\frac{1}{\mu}\mathcal{G}^{\mu}[\varepsilon\zeta]\psi=0\\
\partial_{t}\psi+\zeta+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(\partial_{x}\psi)^{2}-\frac{\mu\varepsilon}{2}\frac{(\frac{1}{\mu}\mathcal{G}^{\mu}[\varepsilon\zeta]\psi+\varepsilon\partial_{x}\zeta\cdot\partial_{x}\psi)^{2}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\partial_{x}\zeta)^{2}}=0,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{G}^{\mu}[\varepsilon\zeta]
stands for the Dirichlet-Neumann operator and
\psi
is the trace at the surface of the velocity potential
\Phi
, see
[
31
]
for more information.
To compare solutions between the water waves equations and system (
1.1
), we define the vertical average of the horizontal component of the velocity field through the formula

(1.11)

\displaystyle\overline{V}=\frac{1}{h}\int_{-1+\beta b}^{\varepsilon\zeta}\partial_{x}\Phi\>\mathrm{d}z,

where
\Phi
stands for the velocity potential in the water domain
\Omega_{t}:=\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},-1+\beta b\leq z\leq\varepsilon\zeta\}
. It is the solution of the following elliptic problem

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{z}^{2}\Phi+\mu\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi=0,\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega_{t}\\
\Phi|_{z=\varepsilon\zeta}=\psi,\ \ \partial_{n}\Phi|_{z=-1+\beta b}=0,\end{cases}

where
\partial_{n}\Phi|_{z=-1+\beta b}=\partial_{z}\Phi-\mu\beta\partial_{x}b\partial_{x}\Phi
. We may now state the final result of this paper.

Theorem 1.6

(Full justification)
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
s\geq 4
and
(\mu,{\varepsilon},\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SW}}
. Then for
b\in H^{s+2}({\mathbb{R}})
and any initial data
(\zeta_{0},\psi_{0})\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})\times\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying the non-cavitation assumption (
1.8
), there exist a unique classical solution of the water waves equations (
1.10
) given by

\mathbf{U}=(\zeta,\psi)\in C([0,\frac{\tilde{T}}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]:H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})\times\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}})),

from which we define
\overline{V}\in C([0,\frac{\tilde{T}}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]:H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}))
through (
1.12
) and (
1.11
).

Moreover, if we let
v_{0}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}=\overline{V}|_{t=0}
. Then
v_{0}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}\in X^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})
and there exist a unique classical solution, denoted by

\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}=(\zeta^{\text{\tiny WGN}},v^{\text{\tiny WGN}})\in C([0,\frac{T}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]:Y^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})),

of the Whitham-Green-Naghdi system (
1.1
) sharing the same initial data

\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}|_{t=0}=(\zeta,\overline{V})|_{t=0}.

Comparing the two solutions, we have that for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough such that for all
0\leq\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}t\leq\min\{\tilde{T},T\}
there holds

|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}|_{L^{\infty}([0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}})}\leq C(|\zeta_{0}|_{H^{s}},|\overline{V}|_{t=0}|_{H^{s}},|b|_{H^{{s+2}}})\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)t,

with
\tilde{T},T,C
positive constants uniform with respect to
(\mu,{\varepsilon},\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{SW}
.

2.1.
Classical estimates

In this section, we state some classical results that will be used throughout the paper. First, recall the embedding results (see, for example,
[
32
]
).

Proposition 2.1

(Sobolev embedding)
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}})
and
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Then
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
with
p=\frac{2}{1-2s}
, and there holds

(2.1)

|f|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|\mathrm{D}^{s}f|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, In the case
s>\frac{1}{2}
, then
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
is continuously embedded in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Next, we state the Leibniz rule for the Riesz potential.

Proposition 2.2

(Fractional Leibniz rule
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\sigma=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\in(0,1)
with
\sigma_{i}\in[0,\sigma]
and
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. Then, for
f,g\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}})

(2.2)

|\mathrm{D}^{\sigma}(fg)-f\mathrm{D}^{\sigma}g-g\mathrm{D}^{\sigma}f|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|\mathrm{D}^{\sigma_{1}}f|_{L^{p_{1}}}|\mathrm{D}^{\sigma_{2}}g|_{L^{p_{2}}}.

Moreover, the case
\sigma_{2}=0
,
p_{2}=\infty
is also allowed.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
r\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
f\in H^{r}({\mathbb{R}})
and
g\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then

(2.3)

|fg|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|\mathrm{D}^{r-\frac{1}{2}}f|_{L^{2}}|g|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}

(2.4)

Proof.

To prove (

to be fixed later. Then combine Hölder’s inequality with the conjugate pair

and (

\displaystyle|fg|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|f|_{L^{\frac{1}{\nu}}}|g|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-2\nu}}}\lesssim|\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1-2\nu}{2}}f|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{D}^{\nu}g|_{L^{2}}.

However, for any

Next, we prove (

as above to deduce

\displaystyle|\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}(fg)|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}(fg)-f\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}g-g\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}f|_{L^{2}}+|f\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}g|_{L^{2}}+|g\mathrm{D}^{\frac{1}{2}}f|_{L^{2}}

where we used (

\mathcal{F}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sqrt{\mu}|\mathrm{D}|\leq 1\}}f)(\xi)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|\leq 1\}}\hat{f}(\xi)

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
f,g\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}})
and
t_{0}>\frac{1}{2}
. Then for
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
there holds,

(2.14)

|[\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}},f]g|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|f|_{H^{\max\{t_{0}+1,s-\frac{1}{2}\}}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}g|_{H^{s-1}}.

In the case

(2.15)

|[\mathrm{J}^{s},\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(f\cdot)]\partial_{x}g|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f|_{H^{s}}|\partial_{x}g|_{H^{t_{0}}}+|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}g|_{H^{s}}|\partial_{x}f|_{H^{t_{0}}}.

Moreover, in the case
s=0
we have that

(2.16)

|[\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}},f]g|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|f|_{X^{t_{0}+1}_{\mu}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}g|_{H^{-1}}.

Proof.

To prove (
2.14
) we note that the Fourier multiplier
\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is of order
s-\frac{1}{2}
in the sense of Definition
2.4
. Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}})\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Thanks to the commutator estimates of Proposition
2.5
and estimate (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle|[\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}},f]g|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}|f|_{H^{\max\{t_{0}+1,s-\frac{1}{2}\}}}|g|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim|f|_{H^{\max\{t_{0}+1,s-\frac{1}{2}\}}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}g|_{H^{s-1}},

and proves estimate (
2.14
).

For the proof of (
2.15
), we start by estimating the bilinear form:

\mathrm{a}(\mathrm{D})(f,g)=[\mathrm{J}^{s},\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(f\cdot)]\partial_{x}g,

given by

\displaystyle|\hat{a}(\xi)(f,g)|\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)|\xi|\big|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}-\langle\rho\rangle^{s}\big||\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho.

First, if
|\xi|\leq|\rho|
we can use the mean value theorem to deduce that

\displaystyle|\hat{a}(\xi)(f,g)|\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)|\rho|\langle\rho\rangle^{s-1}|\xi-\rho||\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho,

since both
\omega\mapsto\langle\omega\rangle^{s-1}
and
\omega\mapsto F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\omega)|\omega|
are increasing functions. By extension, we make a change of variable
\gamma=\xi-\rho
, apply Minkowski integral inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz to find the estimate

\displaystyle|[\mathrm{J}^{s},\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(f\cdot)]\partial_{x}g|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot-\gamma)|\cdot-\gamma|\langle\cdot-\gamma\rangle^{s-1}|\gamma||\hat{f}(\gamma)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\cdot-\gamma)|\>d\gamma\Big|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}g|_{H^{s}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\gamma||\hat{f}(\gamma)|\>d\gamma

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}g|_{H^{s}}|\partial_{x}f|_{H^{t_{0}}}.

On the other hand, when
|\rho|\leq|\xi|
then we can argue similarly to find that

\displaystyle|\hat{a}(\xi)(f,g)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi-\rho)|\xi-\rho|\langle\xi-\rho\rangle^{s-1}|\xi-\rho||\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)|\rho|\langle\rho\rangle^{s-1}|\xi-\rho||\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho,

and as using the estimate above we conclude in this case that

\displaystyle|[\mathrm{J}^{s},\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(f\cdot)]\partial_{x}g|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot-\rho)|\cdot-\rho|\langle\cdot-\rho\rangle^{s-1}|\cdot-\rho||\hat{f}(\cdot-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho\Big|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}g|_{H^{s}}|\partial_{x}f|_{H^{t_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}f|_{H^{s}}|\partial_{x}g|_{H^{t_{0}}}.+|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}g|_{H^{s}}|\partial_{x}f|_{H^{t_{0}}}.

Adding the two cases completes the proof.

Next, we prove (
2.16
) by estimating the bilinear form:

\displaystyle|\hat{a}(\xi)(f,g)|\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big|F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)-F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\big||\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)||\hat{g}(\rho)|\>d\rho.

Clearly, it is enough to prove that

(2.17)

\displaystyle k(\xi,\rho):=\big|F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)-F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\big|F^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\langle\rho\rangle\lesssim 1+|\xi-\rho|F^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\xi-\rho).

Indeed, assuming the claim (
2.17
) and using Plancherel, Minkowski integral inequality, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
2.11
) we obtain the desired estimate

\displaystyle|[\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}},f]g|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}k(\xi,\rho)\>|\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\langle\rho\rangle^{-1}|\hat{g}(\rho)|\>d\rho\Big|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(1+|\gamma|F^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\gamma)\big)\>|\hat{f}(\gamma)|\>F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi-\gamma)\langle\xi-\gamma\rangle^{-1}|\hat{g}(\xi-\gamma)|\>d\gamma\Big|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{X^{t_{0}+1}_{\mu}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}g|_{H^{-1}}.

Now, to prove the claim (
2.17
), we consider three cases. First, in the case
|\rho|\leq 1
it follows directly that

\displaystyle k(\xi,\rho)\lesssim 1,

since
\xi\mapsto F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)
and
\rho\mapsto F^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho)\langle\rho\rangle
is bounded. Next, consider the case
|\rho|>1
and
|\xi|\leq|\rho|
. Then we note that since
\xi\mapsto F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)
is decreasing for
\xi>0
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{F(\rho)}{F(\xi)}\leq 1,

and moreover since
\xi\mapsto\big(\frac{\sqrt{\mu}\xi}{\tanh(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)}-1\big)
is increasing for
\xi>0
, we get that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi|}{|\rho|}\leq\bigg(\frac{|\xi|}{|\rho|}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\bigg(\frac{F(\rho)}{F(\xi)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1.

Thus, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle k(\xi,\rho)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{F(\rho)}{F(\xi)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{F(\xi)}{F(\rho)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\rho\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\rho|-|\xi|)\bigg(\frac{F(\xi)}{F(\rho)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, to conclude this case, we make the observation that if
|\xi|\sim|\rho|
, then

\bigg(\frac{F(\xi)}{F(\rho)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1.

Otherwise, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{F(\xi)}{F(\rho)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1+\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|\rho|^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1+\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|\rho-\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Gathering these estimates allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle k(\xi,\rho)\lesssim(|\rho|-|\xi|)\bigg(\frac{F(\xi)}{F(\rho)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1+|\xi-\rho|F^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\xi-\rho).

On the other hand, the case
|\xi|>|\rho|>1
follows directly by changing the role of
\xi
and
\rho
in (
2.18
). Indeed, we obtain that

\displaystyle k(\xi,\rho)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{F(\xi)}{F(\rho)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)\langle\rho\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\xi|-|\rho|),

and the proof of (
2.16
) is complete.

∎

\displaystyle|V_{1}^{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|v|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Next, we consider
V_{1}^{2}
and observe that we can have a similar bound. Indeed, using (
4.5
), (
2.5
), and (
2.14
) we observe that

\displaystyle|V_{1}^{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}|[\mathrm{J}^{s},h^{3}]\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}^{-1}(h\mathcal{Q})|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}{\varepsilon}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}^{-1}(h\mathcal{Q})|_{H^{s}}|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu{\varepsilon}|h\mathcal{Q}|_{H^{s-1}}|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}},

and we use the previous estimates to obtain that

\displaystyle|V_{1}^{2}|\lesssim N(s)|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Moreover, we note that it is straightforward to estimate
|V_{1}^{3}|+...+|V_{1}^{5}|
arguing as we did for
V_{1}^{1}
and
V_{1}^{2}
. Thus, gathering all these estimates and using (
4.7
) yields,

\displaystyle|V_{1}|\lesssim N(s)E_{s}(\mathbf{U}).

Next, we estimate
V_{2}
using Hölder’s inequality, (
4.5
) and (
2.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle|V_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu{\varepsilon}(1+|h^{3}-1|_{H^{s}})|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v|_{H^{s}}^{2}|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|v|^{2}_{X^{s}_{\mu}}.

Lastly, for
V_{3}
, we inject a commutator and use Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, (
4.5
) and (
2.5
) to get

\displaystyle|V_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu{\varepsilon}|\big(h^{3}[\mathrm{J}^{s},(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v)](\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v),\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}|+\mu{\varepsilon}|\big(h^{3}(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v)\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v,\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu{\varepsilon}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v|_{H^{s}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v|_{H^{s-1}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\mathrm{J}^{s}v|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
VI
. To complete the proof we need to estimate the remaining part:

\displaystyle VI

\displaystyle=-{\varepsilon}\mu\big([\mathrm{J}^{s},\mathscr{T}]\mathscr{T}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{b},\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}-{\varepsilon}\mu\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathcal{Q}_{b},\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}((\beta\partial_{x}b)v),\mathrm{J}^{s}\zeta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:VI_{1}+VI_{2}+VI_{3}.

The estimate in
VI
is similar to the one of
V
, where we now have to deal with the following terms

\displaystyle VI_{1}

\displaystyle=-\mu{\varepsilon}\big([\mathrm{J}^{s},h]\mathscr{T}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{b},\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\big([\mathrm{J}^{s},h^{3}]\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(\mathscr{T}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{b}\big),\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\big([\mathrm{J}^{s},h^{2}\beta(\partial_{x}b)]\mathscr{T}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{b}\big),\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}}{2}\big([\mathrm{J}^{s},h^{2}\beta(\partial_{x}b)]\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(\mathscr{T}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{b}),\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}\big([\mathrm{J}^{s},h(\beta\partial_{x}b)^{2}]\mathscr{T}^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{b},\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:VI_{1}^{1}+VI_{1}^{2}+VI_{1}^{3}+VI_{1}^{4}+VI_{1}^{5}.

Each term is treated similarly. For instance, take
VI_{1}^{2}
, which is the term with the least margin. Arguing as above, we use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
2.5
), (
2.22
) and (
4.5
) to deduce that

\displaystyle|VI_{1}^{2}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}\mu|\mathcal{Q}_{b}|_{H^{s-1}}|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}},

where use the algebra property of
H^{s-1}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
to get:

\displaystyle\mu|\mathcal{Q}_{b}|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu|h^{2}(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v)^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)|_{H^{s-1}}+\mu|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{2}v^{2}\beta\partial_{x}^{2}b)|_{H^{s-1}}+\mu|hv^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}^{2}b)(\beta\partial_{x}b)|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Using similar estimates for the remaining terms, it is easy to deduce that

|VI_{1}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}|v|_{X^{s}_{\mu}}^{3}.

For
IV_{2}
, we use integration by parts to make the decomposition:

\displaystyle VI_{2}

\displaystyle=-{\varepsilon}\mu\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}(h^{2}(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v)^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)),\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}\mu}{2}\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}(h^{2}v^{2}\beta\partial_{x}^{2}b),\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-{\varepsilon}\mu\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}(hv^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}^{2}b)(\beta\partial_{x}b)),\mathrm{J}^{s}v\big)_{L^{2}}.

Each term is estimated by Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, the algebra property of
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
, and (
4.7
), leaving us with the estimate

\displaystyle|VI_{2}|\lesssim N(s)E_{s}(\mathbf{U}).

Lastly,
VI_{3}
is estimated using the same estimates and gives

|VI_{3}|\lesssim\beta|\partial_{x}b|_{H^{s}}|v|_{H^{s}}|\zeta|_{H^{s}}.

Consequently, we have the estimate

|VI|\lesssim N(s)E_{s}(\mathbf{U}),

and thus completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

∎

5.
Estimates on the difference of two solutions

We will now estimate the difference between two solutions of (
1.1
) given by
\mathbb{U}_{1}=(\zeta_{1},v_{1})^{T}
and
\mathbb{U}_{2}={\varepsilon}(\zeta_{2},v_{2})^{T}
. For convenience, we define
(\eta,w)=(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})
. Then
\mathbb{W}=(\eta,w)^{T}
solves

(5.1)

\partial_{t}\mathbb{W}+(M_{1}+S^{-1}M_{2})(\mathbb{U}_{1})\partial_{x}\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{F},

with
S,M_{1},M_{2},Q,Q_{b}
defined as in (
4.1
) and

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle=-\Big[(M_{1}+(S^{-1}M_{2}))(\mathbb{U}_{1})-(M_{1}+S^{-1}M_{2})(\mathbb{U}_{2})\Big]\partial_{x}\mathbb{U}_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\Big[(S^{-1}(Q+Q_{b}))(\mathbb{U}_{1})-(S^{-1}(Q+Q_{b}))(\mathbb{U}_{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=:\mathbb{F}_{1}+\mathbb{F}_{2}.

The energy associated to (
7.1
) is given in terms of the symmetrizer
S(\mathbb{U}_{1})
and reads

(5.2)

\tilde{E}_{s}(\mathbb{W}):=\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathbb{W},S(\mathbb{U}_{1})\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}.

The main result of this section reads:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
(\mu,{\varepsilon},\beta)\in\mathcal{A}_{SW}
, and
(\zeta_{1},v_{1}),(\zeta_{2},v_{2})\in C([0,T];Y^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution to (
4.1
) on a time interval
[0,T]
for some
T>0
. Moreover, assume
b\in H^{s+2}({\mathbb{R}})
and there exist
h_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

h_{0}-1+\beta b\leq{\varepsilon}\zeta_{i}(x,t),\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T],

for
i=1,2
, and suppose also that

(5.3)

N(s):={\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|(\zeta_{i}(t,\cdot),v_{i}(t,\cdot))|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}+\beta|b|_{H^{s+2}}\leq N^{\star},

for some
N^{\star}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Define the difference to be
\mathbb{W}=(\eta,w)=(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})
. Then, for the energy defined by (
5.2
), there holds

(5.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{0}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}^{2},

and

(5.5)

|(\eta,w)|^{2}_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}\lesssim\tilde{E}_{0}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim|(\eta,w)|^{2}_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}.

Furthermore, we have the following estimate at the
Y^{s}_{\mu}-
level:

(5.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{s}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim|\big(\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathbb{F},S(\mathbb{U}_{1})\mathrm{J}^{s}\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}|+N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}^{2},

and

(5.7)

|(\eta,w)|^{2}_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}\lesssim\tilde{E}_{s}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim|(\eta,w)|^{2}_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}.

Proof.

We note that (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) follow by the same arguments as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
and is therefore omitted.

To prove (
5.4
), we use (
7.1
), the self-adjointness
S(\mathbf{U})
, and Proposition
2.11
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{0}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathbf{W},(\partial_{t}S(\mathbf{U}_{1}))\mathbf{W}\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(M_{1}(\mathbf{U}_{1})\partial_{x}\mathbf{W},S(\mathbf{U}_{1})\mathbf{W}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\big(M_{2}(\mathbf{U}_{1})\partial_{x}\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W}\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\mathbf{F}_{1},S(\mathbf{U}_{1})\mathbf{W}\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\mathbf{F}_{2},S(\mathbf{U}_{1})\mathbf{W}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{II}+\mathcal{III}+\mathcal{IV}+\mathcal{V}.

Control of
\mathcal{I}
.
The estimate of
\mathcal{I}
is a direct consequence of Hölder’s inequality, (
5.3
), (
4.8
), and (
5.3
):

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}|\lesssim N(s)|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}^{2},

for
s>\frac{3}{2}
.

Control of
\mathcal{II}
.
By definition of
\mathcal{II}
, after performing an integration by parts, yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{II}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\varepsilon}}{2}\big((\partial_{x}(v_{1}h_{1}))w,w\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{1}\partial_{x}w),h_{1}^{3}\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(v_{1}\partial_{x}w,\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b)w)\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(v_{1}\partial_{x}w,h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b)\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(v_{1}\partial_{x}w,h_{1}(\beta\partial_{x}b)^{2}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{II}_{1}+\mathcal{II}_{2}+\mathcal{II}_{3}+\mathcal{II}_{4}+\mathcal{II}_{5}.

For
\mathcal{II}_{1}
and
\mathcal{II}_{5}
, we simply use Hölders inequality and Sobolev embedding to obtain

|\mathcal{II}_{1}|+|\mathcal{II}_{5}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}(1+|h_{1}-1|_{H^{s}}+(1+|h_{1}-1|_{H^{s}})|b|^{2}_{H^{s+1}})|v_{1}|_{H^{s}}|w|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For
\mathcal{II}_{2}
, we observe that is similar to
II_{2}^{2}
in the proof of Proposition
4.1
where
w
plays the role of
\mathrm{J}^{s}v
. Then reapplying the same estimates yields:

|\mathcal{II}_{2}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}(1+|h_{1}^{3}-1|_{H^{s}})|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}^{3}.

For
\mathcal{II}_{3}
, we integrate by parts to make the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{II}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}((\partial_{x}v_{1})\partial_{x}w),h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b)w\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{1}\partial_{x}^{2}w),h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b)w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{II}_{3}^{1}+\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2}.

Here
\mathcal{II}_{3}^{1}
is similar to
II_{3}^{2}
in the proof of Proposition
4.1
and applying the estimates yields,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{II}_{3}^{1}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}(1+|h_{1}^{2}-1|_{H^{s}})|b|_{H^{s+1}}|v_{1}|_{H^{s}}|w|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}.

On the other hand,
\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2}
is similar to
II_{3}^{3}
and we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big([\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}},v_{1}]\partial_{x}^{2}w,h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b)w\big)_{L^{2}}-\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(v_{1}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}w,h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b)\partial_{x}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}\beta}{2}\big(v_{1}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}w,\big(\partial_{x}(h^{2}_{1}(\partial_{x}b))\big)w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,1}+\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,2}+\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,3}.

Then we observe that
\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,2}=-\mathcal{II}_{4}
, while for
\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,1}
and
\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,3}
we apply Hölder’s inequality, (
2.16
), and Sobolev embedding to obtain the bound

\displaystyle|\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,1}|+|\mathcal{II}_{3}^{2,3}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}|v_{1}|_{H^{s}}(1+|h_{1}^{2}-1|_{H^{s}})|b|_{H^{s+1}}|w|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}.

Gathering these estimates and using (
5.3
) yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{II}|\lesssim N(s)|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathcal{III}
.
By definition of
\mathcal{III}
we must estimate the terms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{III}

\displaystyle=-{\varepsilon}\big(v_{1}\partial_{x}\eta,\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(h_{1}\partial_{x}w,\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(h_{1}\partial_{x}\eta,w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{III}_{1}+\mathcal{III}_{2}+\mathcal{III}_{3}.

Starting with
\mathcal{III}_{1}
, we simply integrate by parts and use Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding to deduce

\displaystyle|\mathcal{III}_{1}|\lesssim{\varepsilon}|v_{1}|_{H^{s}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, for
\mathcal{III}_{2}+\mathcal{III}_{3}
we use integration by parts, the Sobolev embedding, and (
5.3
) to get that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{III}_{2}+\mathcal{III}_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{x}h_{1}|_{L^{\infty}}|w|_{L^{2}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|w|_{L^{2}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}.

In conclusion, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle|\mathcal{III}|\lesssim N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathcal{IV}
.
First define the notation

\mathscr{T}_{i}=\mathscr{T}[h_{i},\beta b],

for
i=1,2
and consider the terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{IV}

\displaystyle=-{\varepsilon}\big(w\partial_{x}\zeta_{2},\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-{\varepsilon}\big(\eta\partial_{x}v_{2},\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big((\mathscr{T}_{1}^{-1}(h_{1}\cdot)-\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\cdot))\partial_{x}\zeta_{2},\mathscr{T}_{1}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-{\varepsilon}\big(w\partial_{x}v_{2},\mathscr{T}_{1}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{IV}_{1}+\mathcal{IV}_{2}+\mathcal{IV}_{3}+\mathcal{IV}_{4}.

For the first two terms, we use Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding to deduce the bound:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{1}|+|\mathcal{IV}_{2}|\leq{\varepsilon}|\zeta_{2}|_{H^{s}}|w|_{L^{2}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}|v_{2}|_{H^{s}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}^{2},

for
s>\frac{3}{2}
. Next, we make the observation

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{1}(\mathscr{T}_{1}^{-1}f_{1}-\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}f_{2})=(f_{1}-f_{2})-(\mathscr{T}_{1}-\mathscr{T}_{2})\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}f_{2}.

Using (
5.8
) and invertability of
\mathscr{T}_{i}
we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{IV}_{3}

\displaystyle=-{\varepsilon}\big(\eta\partial_{x}\zeta_{2},w\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(\eta(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}h_{2}+h_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\partial_{x}\zeta_{2})),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\eta(h_{1}+h_{2})(\beta\partial_{x}b)\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\partial_{x}\zeta_{2})),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(\eta(h_{1}+h_{2})(\beta\partial_{x}b)\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\partial_{x}\zeta_{2}),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\mu{\varepsilon}\big(\eta(\beta\partial_{x}b)^{2}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\partial_{x}\zeta_{2}),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{1}+\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{2}+\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{3}+\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{4}+\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{5},

where
\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{1}=\mathcal{IV}_{1}
which is already treated. While for the second term, we use integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, (
5.3
), and (
2.22
) to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon}|\eta|_{L^{2}}|(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}h_{2}+h_{2}^{2})|_{L^{\infty}}|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\partial_{x}\zeta_{2}))|_{L^{\infty}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|\eta|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}

for
s>\frac{3}{2}
. For
II_{3}^{3}
we apply the same estimates together with (
2.21
) to deduce

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}|(h_{1}+h_{2})|_{L^{\infty}}|\beta\partial_{x}b|_{L^{\infty}}|h_{2}|_{L^{\infty}}|\partial_{x}\zeta_{2}|_{L^{\infty}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|\eta|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}.

Next, we see that
\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{4}
is estimated similarly to
\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{2}
and we get that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{4}|

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)|\eta|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}.

The part
\mathcal{IV}_{3}^{5}
is easily treated with Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding. Thus, gathering these estimates and applying (
5.5
) yields,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{3}|\lesssim N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Lastly, we deal with
\mathcal{IV}_{4}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{IV}_{4}

\displaystyle=-{\varepsilon}\big(w\partial_{x}v_{2},h_{1}w\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(w\partial_{x}v_{2},\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h_{1}^{3}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}w)\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(w\partial_{x}v_{2},\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(h_{1}^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)w)\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{\mu{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(w\partial_{x}v_{2},h_{1}^{2}(\beta\partial_{x}b)\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\mu{\varepsilon}\big(w\partial_{x}v_{2},h_{1}(\beta\partial_{x}b)^{2}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{1}+\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{2}+\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{3}+\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{4}.

Each term is treated similarly, and we only give the details for
\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{2}
since it is the term with the least margin. In particular, using integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, (
5.3
),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mu}(1+|h_{1}^{3}-1|_{H^{s}})|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(w\partial_{x}v_{2})|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(s)\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|w\partial_{x}v_{2}|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}.

Then we use Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, and (
2.4
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|w\partial_{x}v_{2}|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}(|\partial_{x}v_{2}|_{L^{\infty}}|w|_{L^{2}}+|D^{\frac{1}{2}}(w\partial_{x}v_{2})|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim|v_{2}|_{H^{s}}|w|_{L^{2}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|v_{2}|_{H^{r+1}}|w|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}},

for any
r>\frac{1}{2}
. Now choose

such that

allowing us to conclude that

\displaystyle\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|w\partial_{x}v_{2}|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim|v_{2}|_{H^{s}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}},

and from which we obtain:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}_{4}^{2}|\lesssim N(s)|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}^{2}.

To summarize this part, we can use (
5.3
) to obtain the estimate

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IV}|\lesssim N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathcal{V}
.
Define the notation

\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\mathcal{Q}[h_{i},v_{i}],\quad\mathcal{Q}_{b,i}=\mathcal{Q}_{b}[h_{i},\beta b,v_{i}],

with
i=1,2
, and using the identity (
5.8
), then we obtain the following terms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=\beta\big(\partial_{x}bw,\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\mu{\varepsilon}\big(h_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{1}-h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2},w\big)_{L^{2}}+\mu{\varepsilon}\big((\mathscr{T}_{1}-\mathscr{T}_{2})\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2}),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\mu{\varepsilon}\big(h_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{b,1}-h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{b,2},w\big)_{L^{2}}+\mu{\varepsilon}\big((\mathscr{T}_{1}-\mathscr{T}_{2})\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{b,2}),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{V}_{1}+\mathcal{V}_{2}+\mathcal{V}_{3}+\mathcal{V}_{4}+\mathcal{V}_{5}.

The estimate of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
follows directly by Hölder’s inequality:

|\mathcal{V}_{1}|\lesssim\beta|\partial_{x}b|_{L^{\infty}}|w|_{L^{2}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}.

For
\mathcal{V}_{2}
, we use the definition of
Q_{i}
and then integration by parts to make the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mu{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(\big(h_{1}^{3}(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{1})^{2}-h_{2}^{3}(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{2})^{2}\big),\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mu{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(\big(\eta(h_{1}+h_{2})\big)(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{1})^{2},\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{2\mu{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(h_{2}^{3}\big(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{1}+v_{2})\big)(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w,),\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w\big)_{L^{2}}.

Now, estimate each term by Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding to obtain that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu{\varepsilon}\big(|h_{1}+h_{2}|_{L^{\infty}}|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{1}|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|\eta|_{L^{2}}|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w|_{L^{2}}+|h_{2}^{3}|_{L^{\infty}}|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{1}+v_{2})|_{L^{\infty}}|\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}w|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}\max_{i=1,2}\big((1+|h_{i}-1|_{{H^{s}}})|v_{1}|_{H^{s}}^{2}|\eta|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}+(1+|h_{2}^{3}-1|_{{H^{s}}})|v_{i}|_{H^{s}}|w|^{2}_{X^{0}_{\mu}}\big).

Then conclude this estimate by applying (
5.3
):

|\mathcal{V}_{2}|\lesssim N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{0}_{\mu}}^{2}.

For
\mathcal{V}_{3}
, we use the same decomposition as for
\mathcal{IV}_{3}
and find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}}{3}\big(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}\big(\eta(h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}h_{2}+h_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2})\big),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\eta(h_{1}+h_{2})(\beta\partial_{x}b)\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2})\big),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}}{2}\big(\eta(h_{1}+h_{2})(\beta\partial_{x}b)\partial_{x}\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2}),w\big)_{L^{2}}-\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}\big(\eta(\beta\partial_{x}b)^{2}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2}),w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{V}_{3}^{1}+\mathcal{V}_{3}^{2}+\mathcal{V}_{3}^{3}+\mathcal{V}_{3}^{4}.

Each term is treated similarly, but the term with the least margin is
\mathcal{V}_{3}^{1}
. In fact, we use integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding
H^{s-1}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, (
5.3
), and (
2.22

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}_{3}^{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{2}{\varepsilon}|\eta|_{L^{2}}|h_{1}^{2}+h_{1}h_{2}+h_{2}^{2}|_{L^{\infty}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{T}_{2}^{-1}(h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2})|_{H^{s}}|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}w|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu{\varepsilon}|h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2}|_{H^{s-1}}|\eta|_{L^{2}}|w|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}.

Then using (
2.13

and the boundedness of
\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle|h_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{2}|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}(h_{2}^{3}(\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v_{2})^{2})|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim(1+|h_{2}^{3}-1|_{H^{s}})|\mathrm{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}v_{2}|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Consequently, we may gather these estimates to deduce the bound

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}_{3}^{1}|+...+|\mathcal{V}_{3}^{4}|\lesssim N(s)|(\eta,w)|_{X^{0}_{\mu}}^{2},

where
|\mathcal{V}_{3}^{2}|+...+|\mathcal{V}_{3}^{4}|
are easier versions of

To conclude we must estimate
\mathcal{V}_{4}
and
\mathcal{V}_{5}
. However, since

contains fewer derivatives than
\mathcal{Q}
, these terms could be considered to be of lower order. In fact,

is estimated by a similar decomposition to the one of

Gathering all these estimates, we obtain (

6.
Long time Well-posedness of (

)

For the proof of Theorem

we will use the parabolic regularisation method for the existence of solutions and a Bona–Smith regularisation
argument
[
3
]
to prove the continuous dependence of the solutions with respect to the initial data. This method is classical in the case of quasilinear equations and we will only outline the steps that are unique to system (
1.1
) and needed to run the argument. In particular, one can read
[

]
for a similar argument in the case of the classical Green-Naghdi system. Lastly, the reader might also find it useful to read the detailed proof, using these methods, in the case of the Benjamin-Ono equation in
[

Proof.

Step 1:

Existence of solutions for a regularised system.
Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
\alpha\in(1,\frac{3}{2}]
and take
\nu>0
small. Moreover let
\mathbf{U}_{0}=(\zeta_{0},v_{0})^{T}\in Y^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})
,
b\in H^{s+2}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying (
1.2
) and define
T_{\nu}>0
such that

(6.1)

\displaystyle T_{\nu}\searrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad\nu\searrow 0,\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\nu}=T_{\nu}(|(\zeta_{0},v_{0})|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}),

with the property that

(6.2)

\text{if}\quad a<b\quad\text{then}\quad T_{\nu}(a)>T_{\nu}(b).

Then we claim there is a unique solution
\mathbf{U}^{\nu}=(\zeta^{\nu},v^{\nu})^{T}\in C([0,T_{\nu}];Y^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}}))
associated to
\mathbf{U}_{0}
that satisfy the regularised version of (
4.1
) given by,

(6.3)

\displaystyle S(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})(\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}^{\nu}+M_{1}(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})\partial_{x}\mathbf{U}^{\nu})+M_{2}(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})\partial_{x}\mathbf{U}^{\nu}+Q(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})+Q_{b}(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})=-\nu S(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})\mathrm{J}^{\alpha}\mathbf{U}^{\nu}.

To prove the claim, we first suppose the non-cavitation condition for
\mathbf{U}^{\nu}
and use Proposition
2.11
to apply the inverse of
\mathscr{T}[h,\beta b]
on the second equation in (
6.3
). Then we study the Duhamel formulation:

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{\nu}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}t}\mathbf{U}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}(t-s)}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})(s)\>\mathrm{d}s,

where
\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}t}
is the Fourier multiplier defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}t}f)(\xi)=e^{-\nu\langle\xi\rangle^{\alpha}t}\hat{f}(\xi),

and with

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})=(M_{1}+S^{-1}M_{2})(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})\partial_{x}\mathbf{U}^{\nu}+(S^{-1}(Q+Q_{b}))(\mathbf{U}^{\nu}).

In particular, we prove that the application

(6.4)

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathbf{U}^{\nu}\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}t}\mathbf{U}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}(t-s)}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U}^{\nu})(s)\>\mathrm{d}s,

is a contraction map on the subspace

\displaystyle B(R,h_{0})=\Big\{\mathbf{U}=(\zeta,v)\in C([0,T];Y^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}}))\>:\>|(\zeta,v)|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}<R,\>\>\inf\limits_{t\in(0,T)}(1+{\varepsilon}\zeta^{\nu}-\beta b)\geq h_{0}\Big\},

with
R>0
to be determined. First, observe by Plancherel’s identity and then splitting in high and low frequencies that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}t}\mathbf{U}|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mathbf{U}|_{L^{2}}+(\nu t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}|(\nu t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\xi|e^{-((\nu t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\xi|)^{\alpha}}\hat{\mathbf{U}}|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+(\nu t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}})|\mathbf{U}|_{H^{s-1}},

and trivially that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{e}^{-\nu\langle\mathrm{D}\rangle^{\alpha}t}\mathbf{U}|_{H^{s}}\leq|\mathbf{U}|_{H^{s}}.

Thus, as a consequence of these estimates and Remark
4.2
we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\Phi(\mathbf{U^{\nu}})(t)|_{H^{s}}\leq c|\mathbf{U}_{0}|_{H^{s}}+cT^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}|\mathbf{U}|_{H^{s}}.

Now, choose
R
to be

\displaystyle R=2c|(\zeta_{0},v_{0})|_{Y^{s}_{\mu}}.

Additionally, since
1-\frac{1}{\alpha}>0
we may take
T
positive depending on
\nu
and
R
on the form

\displaystyle T^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sim\frac{\nu^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{R},

small enough, and such that

\displaystyle 1+{\varepsilon}\zeta^{\nu}(x,t)-\beta b(x)

\displaystyle=h_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\zeta^{\nu}(x,s)\>\mathrm{d}s\geq h_{0}-cT(R+R^{2})\geq\frac{h_{0}}{2},

using the Fundamental theorem of calculus and (

). Then the map (
6.4
) is well-defined on
B(R,\frac{h_{0}}{2})
, and the contraction estimate is obtained similarly after some straightforward algebraic manipulations. We may therefore conclude this step by the Banach fixed point Theorem.

Remark 6.1

(The blow-up alternative)

This is due to the fact that if (

) does not hold, one can use Step
1

given by (

) and (

Now, use Proposition
3.2
and formulation (
4.1
) to say that for some
\tilde{T}>0
the functions
\mathbf{U}=(\zeta,\overline{V})^{T}
solves

\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}+(M_{1}+(S^{-1}M_{2}))(\mathbf{U})\partial_{x}\mathbf{U}+(S^{-1}Q)(\mathbf{U})+(S^{-1}Q_{b})(\mathbf{U})=\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)\mathbf{R},

for any
t\in[0,\frac{\tilde{T}}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]
and with
S,M_{1},M_{2},Q,Q_{b}
defined as in (
4.1
) and
\mathbf{R}=(0,R)\in L^{\infty}([0,\frac{\tilde{T}}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}];X_{\mu}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}))
for some
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The next step is to let
v^{\text{\tiny WGN}}_{0}=\overline{V}|_{t=0}\in X^{s}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})
and then use Theorem
1.4
deduce the existence of
T>0
such that

\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}=(\zeta^{\text{\tiny WGN}},v^{\text{\tiny WGN}})\in C([0,\frac{T}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}];Y_{\mu}^{s}({\mathbb{R}})),

solves system (
4.1
):

\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}+(M_{1}+(S^{-1}M_{2}))(\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}})\partial_{x}\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}+(S^{-1}Q)(\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}})+(S^{-1}Q_{b})(\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}})=\mathbf{0},

for any
t\in[0,\frac{T}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]
. Consequently, taking the difference between the two solutions

\mathbf{W}=(\eta,w)^{T}=\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}},

we obtain the following system

(7.1)

\partial_{t}\mathbb{W}+(M_{1}+S^{-1}M_{2})(\mathbb{U})\partial_{x}\mathbb{W}=\tilde{\mathbb{F}},

similar to (
7.1
) and with

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{F}}

\displaystyle=-\Big[(M_{1}+(S^{-1}M_{2}))(\mathbb{U})-(M_{1}+S^{-1}M_{2})(\mathbb{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}})\Big]\partial_{x}\mathbb{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\Big[(S^{-1}(Q+Q_{b}))(\mathbb{U})-(S^{-1}(Q+Q_{b}))(\mathbb{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}})\Big]+\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}+\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)\mathbf{R},

for any
t\in[0,\frac{\min\{\tilde{T},T\}}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]
. Then using the estimates (
5.6
),(
5.7
), and Remark
5.2
we deduce for
r>\frac{3}{2}
that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{r}(\mathbb{W})

\displaystyle\lesssim|\big(\mathrm{J}^{r}\tilde{\mathbb{F}},S(\mathbf{U})\mathrm{J}^{r}\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}|+N(r)\tilde{E}_{r}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)|\big(\mathrm{J}^{r}R,\mathscr{T}[h,\beta b]\mathrm{J}^{r}w\big)_{L^{2}}|+N(r+1)\tilde{E}_{r}(\mathbf{W}).

However, by definition of
\mathscr{T}[h,\beta b]
and using integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding we easily obtain the estimate

\displaystyle|\big(\mathrm{J}^{r}R,\mathscr{T}[h,\beta b]\mathrm{J}^{r}w\big)_{L^{2}}|\lesssim N(r)|R|_{X^{r}_{\mu}}|w|_{X^{r}_{\mu}}.

Gathering these estimates, together with (
5.7
), we observe

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{r}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)|R|_{X^{r}_{\mu}}(\tilde{E}_{r}(\mathbf{W}))^{\frac{1}{2}}+N(r+1)\tilde{E}_{r}(\mathbf{W}).

Now, a simple application of Grönwall’s inequality and (
5.7
) yields

(7.2)

|(\eta,w)|_{Y^{r}_{\mu}}\lesssim\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)t\>|R|_{X^{r}_{\mu}}e^{N(r+1)t}.

Finally, to conclude we use that
Y^{r}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}})\subset H^{r}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
for
r>\frac{3}{2}
, and (
7.2
) to get

\displaystyle|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];{\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|(\eta,w)|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];Y^{r}_{\mu}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)t\>|R|_{X_{\mu}^{r}}e^{N(r+1)t}.

To conclude, we let
s
be large enough such that
r+1<s
to get that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}^{\text{\tiny WGN}}|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];{\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\mu^{2}({\varepsilon}+\beta)t,

for all
t\in[0,\frac{\min\{\tilde{T},T\}}{\max\{{\varepsilon},\beta\}}]
.

∎

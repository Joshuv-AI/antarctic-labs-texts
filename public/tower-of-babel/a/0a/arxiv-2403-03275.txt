A two-line representation of stationary measure for open TASEP
Włodek Bryc, Pavel Zatitskii

Abstract
. We show that the stationary measure for the totally asymmetric simple exclusion process on a segment with open boundaries is given by a marginal of a two-line measure with a simple and explicit description. We use this representation to analyze asymptotic fluctuations of the height function near the triple point for a larger set of parameters than was previously studied. As a second application, we determine a single expression for the rate function in the large deviation principle for the height function in the fan and in the shock region. We then discuss how this expression relates to the expressions for the rate function available in the literature.

1.
Introduction

A totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP) with open boundaries is a continuous-time finite-state Markov process that models the movement of particles along the
N
sites
\{1,\dots,N\}
from the left reservoir to the right reservoir. The particles cannot occupy the same site, and can move only to the nearest site on the right at rate
1
. The particles arrive at the first location, if empty, at rate
\alpha>0
and leave the system from the
N
-th site, if occupied, at rate
\beta>0
.
For a description of the infinitesimal generator of this Markov process, we refer to e.g.
[
23
, Section 3]
. We will be interested solely in the stationary measure of this process.

The stationary measure for open TASEP has been studied for a long time, with explicit expressions available in
[
25
]

and
[
20
]
.
In this paper we establish a two-line representation for this stationary measure in terms of a pair of weighted random walks.
We remark that there are numerous other representations for the stationary measure of TASEP; a representation in
[
24
, Section 5.2]
does not separate the ”two lines”, but it covers a more general ASEP. Integral representation of the probability generating function
[
8
,
26
]
is useful in studying Laplace transformations of limiting fluctuations.
Ref.
[
21
, Section 3.2]
represents the stationary measure of a sequential TASEP as a marginal of a
”two-layer” configuration that has a different form than ours.

The Gibbs measure (or line ensemble) representations have been valuable in studying integrable
probabilistic models on full or half-space and have been extended to time-homogeneous models on an interval with two-sided boundary conditions in
[
1
]
.
Barraquand, Corwin, and Yang
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
establish that stationary measures for the free-energy increment process of geometric last passage percolation on a diagonal strip are described as marginals of explicitly defined two-layer Gibbs measures. They further pose the question of obtaining an explicit description of the open TASEP stationary measure from its implicit connection to the stationary solution of the exponential large passage percolation recurrence. This paper proposes an alternative approach implicitly based on the matrix method
[
14
]
. We demonstrate that a representation akin to their two-layer Gibbs measure holds for the stationary measure of the TASEP.
We then use this representation to analyze asymptotic fluctuations of particle density for a larger set of parameters than was previously studied, and to prove the large deviations principle with a single expression for the rate function valid for all
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
.

We now introduce configuration spaces and probability measures that will facilitate canonical representations of the random variables that we need. We begin with the stationary measure of TASEP which defines a (discrete) probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TASEP}}
on the configuration space
\Omega=\Omega^{(N)}=\{0,1\}^{N}
. (We will omit the superscript
N
when it is fixed in an argument and clearly recognizable from the context.) We assign probability

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TASEP}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})
to a sequence
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})\in\Omega
that encodes the occupied and empty sites,
where
\tau_{j}\in\{0,1\}
is the occupation indicator of the
j
-th site.
It will be convenient to parameterize
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TASEP}}
using

(1.1)

\mathsf{a}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha},\quad\mathsf{b}=\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}.

Formula (
1.1
) makes sense for all
\alpha,\beta>0
,
and then
{\mathbb{P}}_{TASEP}
is determined by
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}>-1
in formula (
2.1
), but in this paper we will only consider
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
, so that
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}>0
.

The steady state
height function

{\boldsymbol{H}}_{N}
is defined by

(1.2)

H_{N}(k)=\tau_{1}+\dots+\tau_{k},\quad k=0,1,\dots,N.

The invariant law

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TASEP}}
is uniquely determined by the law
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{(N)}
induced by
{\boldsymbol{H}}_{N}
on the configuration space

{\mathcal{S}}:=\left\{{\boldsymbol{s}}=(s_{j})_{0\leq j\leq N}:\;s_{0}=0,\;s_{j}-s_{j-1}\in\{0,1\},\;1\leq j\leq N\right\}.

Indeed,

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{s}})={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TASEP}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})

with unique
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
such that
{\boldsymbol{s}}={\boldsymbol{H}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})
, as
{\boldsymbol{H}}_{N}:\Omega\to{\mathcal{S}}
is a bijection.
Instead of determining
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TASEP}}
, we will therefore determine
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{(N)}
as a marginal law of the top line of the two-line ensemble on the configuration space
{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
.

Denote by
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}
,
the uniform law on
{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
defined by two independent random walks with i. i. d.
\mathrm{Bernoulli}
increments
\Pr(\xi=0)=\Pr(\xi=1)=1/2
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}({\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2})=\Pr({\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}={\boldsymbol{s}}_{1})\Pr({\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}={\boldsymbol{s}}_{2})=1/4^{N},\quad{\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}.

The
two-line ensemble
(TLE) is the probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}
on
{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
, defined
as follows:

(1.3)

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}\left({\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}\right)=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}}}\;\frac{\mathsf{b}^{s_{1}(N)-s_{2}(N)}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{\min_{0\leq j\leq N}\left\{s_{1}(j)-s_{2}(j)\right\}}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}({\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}),

where
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}}
is the normalization constant and
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N)}
.
We will write
{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}
for the expected value with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}
.
Of course,
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}^{(N)}
depends on
N
.

The two canonical coordinate mappings
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
given by

(1.4)

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},\quad{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{s}}_{2},

give a canonical realization on
({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}})
of the pair of independent Bernoulli random walks
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}
and at the same time they give
a realization of the two-line ensemble on
({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}})
.

Our main result is the following TASEP analog of
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}>0
, the marginal law of random sequence
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}
given by (
1.4
)
under measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}
is the (unique) law of the steady state height function

{\boldsymbol{H}}_{N}
of the TASEP with parameters
\alpha,\beta
given by (
1.1
).
That is, for
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

(1.5)

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{H}}({\boldsymbol{s}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}({\boldsymbol{s}},{\boldsymbol{s}}^{\prime}).

Remark 1.2
.

As pointed out to us by an anonymous reviewer, Theorem
1.1
in fact holds for
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}\geq 0
, i.e., for
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
. The only change that is required is to rewrite (

) as an expression in
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}
with non-negative exponents:

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{C}_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}}}\;\mathsf{b}^{s_{1}(N)-s_{2}(N)-\min_{0\leq j\leq N}\left\{s_{1}(j)-s_{2}(j)\right\}}\mathsf{a}^{\max_{0\leq j\leq N}\left\{s_{2}(j)-s_{1}(j)\right\}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}({\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}).

Then the identities that we establish in the proof for
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}>0
extend to
\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}\geq 0
by continuity.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
appears in Section
2

In Theorem

we use Theorem

and Donsker’s theorem to obtain convergence of the fluctuations of TASEP to the process conjectured to be a stationary measure of a KPZ fixed point on an interval in the full range of parameters. To our knowledge, previously available results of this form, see

The large deviation principle for the height function of a more general ASEP has been

Specified to TASEP, the basic weight equations say that the unique stationary measure of TASEP under reparameterization (

2.1.
The key identity and the proof of Theorem
1.1

We introduce a family of functions
f_{N}:\Omega^{(N)}\to(0,\infty)
that we will use to prove (

For
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})\in\Omega^{(N)}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})\in\Omega^{(N)}
, let

(2.6)

s_{1}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau_{j},\quad s_{2}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\xi_{j}

with
s_{1}(0):=0
and
s_{2}(0):=0
. Formula (
2.6
) defines a pair of bijections
\Omega^{(N)}\to{\mathcal{S}}
and throughout this proof we will treat

{\boldsymbol{s}}_{1}={\boldsymbol{s}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})
as a function of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}={\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})
as a function of
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
.

With the above convention, we introduce

(2.7)

f_{N}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})=f_{N}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})=\sum_{(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})\in\Omega^{(N)}}\frac{\mathsf{b}^{s_{1}(N)-s_{2}(N)}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{\min_{0\leq j\leq N}\{s_{1}(j)-s_{2}(j)\}}}.

Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of the following identity:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})\in\Omega^{(N)}
and
N\geq 1
, we have

(2.8)

f_{N}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})=p_{N}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N}),

with
p_{N}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})
from representation (
2.1
).

Proof.

It is clear that (
2.8
) holds for
N=1
. Indeed, in this case (
2.7
) is the sum of two terms corresponding to
\xi_{1}=0
and
\xi_{1}=1
:

f_{1}(0)=\frac{\mathsf{b}^{0}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{0}}+\frac{\mathsf{b}^{-1}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{-1}}=1+\mathsf{a},

f_{1}(1)=\frac{\mathsf{b}^{1}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{0}}+\frac{\mathsf{b}^{0}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{0}}=\mathsf{b}+1,

matching the initial conditions (
2.2
).

Next, we show that
f_{N}
satisfies the same three recursions as
p_{N}
for
N\geq 2
.
Throughout this proof, for
k=0,\dots,N
we consider partial sums
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N)}

and
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}}_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N-1)}
that depend on
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}
and partial sums
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N)}
and
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N-1)}
that depend on the auxiliary
\{0,1\}
-valued variables
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
that appear under the sum in (
2.7
). In one place in the last part of the proof,
the sequence
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}}_{1}
will be an explicit function of both sequences
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
.
We express
f_{N}
in terms of
{\boldsymbol{s}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{s}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N)}
and relate it to
f_{N-1}
written in terms of
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}}_{1},\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{(N-1)}
.

First, we verify that

f_{N}(0,\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{N})=(1+\mathsf{a})f_{N-1}(\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{N}).

To see this, we define
\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau_{j+1}
and
\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\xi_{j+1}
so that
s_{1}(N)=\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)
,
s_{2}(N)=\xi_{1}+\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1)
and

\min_{0\leq j\leq N}\left\{s_{1}(j)-s_{2}(j)\right\}=-\xi_{1}+\min_{0\leq j\leq N-1}\left\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(j)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(j)\right\}
. Then (
2.7
) gives

f_{N}(0,\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{N})=\sum_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{N})\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\frac{\mathsf{b}^{-\xi_{1}}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{-\xi_{1}}}\cdot\frac{\mathsf{b}^{\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1)}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{\min_{0\leq j\leq N-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(j)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(j)\}}}\\
=\sum_{\xi_{1}\in\{0,1\}}\mathsf{a}^{\xi_{1}}\sum_{(\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{N})\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}}\frac{\mathsf{b}^{\widetilde{s}_{1}(N)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N)}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{\min_{0\leq j\leq N-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(j)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(j)\}}}\\
=(1+\mathsf{a})f_{N-1}(\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{N}).

Next, by a similar argument we verify that

f_{N}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N-1},1)=(1+\mathsf{b})f_{N-1}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N-1}).

In this case, we introduce
\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau_{j}
and
\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\xi_{j}
so that

s_{1}(N)=\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)+1
,
s_{2}(N)=\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1)+\xi_{N}
. We note that

s_{1}(N)-s_{2}(N)=\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1)+1-\xi_{N}\geq\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1),

so in this case

\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\{s_{1}(j)-s_{2}(j)\}=\min_{1\leq j\leq N-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(j)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(j)\}
. Thus
(
2.7
) gives

f_{N}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N-1},1)=\sum_{\xi_{N}\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N-1}\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}}\mathsf{b}^{1-\xi_{N}}\frac{\mathsf{b}^{\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1)}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{\min_{1\leq j\leq N-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(j)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(j)\}}}\\
=\sum_{\xi_{N}\in\{0,1\}}\mathsf{b}^{1-\xi_{N}}\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N-1}\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}}\frac{\mathsf{b}^{\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1)}}{(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{b})^{\min_{1\leq j\leq N-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(j)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(j)\}}}\\
=(1+\mathsf{b})f_{N-1}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N-1}).

Finally, we verify that for a fixed
1\leq n\leq N-1
we have

(2.9)

f_{N}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1},1,0,\tau_{n+2},\dots,\tau_{N})=f_{N-1}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1},1,\tau_{n+2},\dots,\tau_{N})\\
+f_{N-1}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1},0,\tau_{n+2},\dots,\tau_{N}).

Here for
k\leq n-1
we let
\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)=s_{1}(k)
and
\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)=s_{2}(k)
. For
n\leq k\leq N-1
we set

\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)=s_{2}(n-1)+\sum_{j=n}^{k}\xi_{j+1}
, skipping over
\xi_{n}
. On the other hand, for
n\leq k\leq N-1

we let
\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)=s_{1}(n-1)+(1-\xi_{n})+\sum_{j=n+1}^{k}\tau_{j+1}
.
(This is one place in the proof where
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}}_{1}
depends on both
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
.)
Note that putting this choice of
\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{s}}}_{1}
into expression (
2.7
)
leads to the formula for
f_{N-1}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1},1-\xi_{n},\tau_{n+2},\dots,\tau_{N})
.

It is clear that

(2.10)

s_{1}(N)-s_{2}(N)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tau_{j}+1+0+\sum_{j=n+2}^{N}\tau_{j}\right)-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j}+\xi_{n}+\sum_{j=n+1}^{N}\xi_{j}\right)\\
=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tau_{j}+(1-\xi_{n})+\sum_{j=n+1}^{N-1}\tau_{j+1}\right)-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_{j}+\sum_{j=n}^{N-1}\xi_{j+1}\right)=\widetilde{s}_{1}(N-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(N-1).

The same calculation shows that

s_{1}(k)-s_{2}(k)=\widetilde{s}_{1}(k-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(k-1)
for
k=n+1,\dots,N
.
Since
s_{1}(k)-s_{2}(k)=\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
,
and by the same rewrite as in (
2.10
) we get

s_{1}(n)-s_{2}(n)=1-\xi_{n}+s_{1}(n-1)-s_{2}(n-1)\geq s_{1}(n-1)-s_{2}(n-1),

we see that
s_{1}(n)-s_{2}(n)
does not contribute to the minimum. This shows that the two minima are the same,

\min_{0\leq k\leq N}\{s_{1}(k)-s_{2}(k)\}=\min_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\{s_{1}(k)-s_{2}(k)\}\wedge\min_{n+1\leq k\leq N}\{s_{1}(k)-s_{2}(k)\}\\
=\min_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)\}\wedge\min_{n+1\leq k\leq N}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(k-1)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(k-1)\}=\min_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\{\widetilde{s}_{1}(k)-\widetilde{s}_{2}(k)\}.

Therefore summing in (
2.7
) over
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n-1},\xi_{n+1},\dots,\xi_{N}\in\{0,1\}
and isolating the sum over
\xi_{n}\in\{0,1\}

Then

\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(2H_{N}(\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor)-\left\lfloor Nx\right\rfloor\right)\right\}_{x\in[0,1]}\Rightarrow\{B_{x}+X_{x}\}_{x\in[0,1]}\mbox{ as $N\to\infty$},

We remark that a linear interpolation of the height function similar to the one that appears in Theorem

leads to the same conclusion under weak convergence in the space

Proof.

Consider the coordinate processes

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}
defined on the probability space
({\mathcal{S}}^{(N)}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{(N)},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}})
by (
1.4
).
We shall determine the limit of the process

(3.3)

{\boldsymbol{W}}_{1}^{(N)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left\{2S_{1}(\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor)-\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor\right\}_{x\in[0,1]}.

We will establish a more general claim that under measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}
, we have joint convergence of the pair of processes:

(3.4)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\{S_{1}(\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor)+S_{2}(\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor)-\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor\}_{x\in[0,1]},\{S_{1}(\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor)-S_{2}(\left\lfloor xN\right\rfloor)\}_{x\in[0,1]}\right)\\
\Rightarrow\left(\{B_{x}\}_{x\in[0,1]},\{X_{x}\}_{x\in[0,1]}\right),

where
B,X
are independent processes from the conclusion of the theorem. Noting that the process

), this will end the proof.

Fix a bounded continuous function
\Phi:D([0,1];{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to{\mathbb{R}}

and write

{\boldsymbol{W}}_{\pm}^{(N)}:=\tfrac{1}{2}({\boldsymbol{W}}_{1}^{(N)}\pm{\boldsymbol{W}}_{2}^{(N)})

for the two processes on the left hand side of

3.4

{\boldsymbol{W}}_{2}^{(N)}
is defined as in (
3.3

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}

Our goal is to prove that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}\left[\Phi({\boldsymbol{W}}_{+}^{(N)},{\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\right]={\mathbb{E}}[\Phi(B,X)].

Following the approach in
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
, we use Donsker’s theorem.
Since

, by Donsker’s theorem, under probability measure

where

are independent Wiener processes and convergence is in

where

Using the Radon-Nikodym density (

\Phi

A.1
) and Remark

we see that the sequence of real valued random variables

is uniformly integrable with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}
.
Uniform integrability and weak convergence imply convergence of expectations (
[
5

),
so Donsker’s theorem implies that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left[\Phi({\boldsymbol{W}}_{+}^{(N)},{\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{B,B^{\prime}}\left[\Phi(B,B^{\prime})\mathcal{E}(B^{\prime})\right],

denotes integration with respect to the law of

Using this with

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{Z}_{\mathsf{u},\mathsf{v}}(N)=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left[\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{B,B^{\prime}}\left[\mathcal{E}(B^{\prime})\right]=\mathfrak{K}(\mathsf{u},\mathsf{v}).

Thus

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}\left[\Phi({\boldsymbol{W}}_{+}^{(N)},{\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\right]=\frac{\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left[\Phi({\boldsymbol{W}}_{+}^{(N)},{\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{W}}_{-}^{(N)})\right]}{\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathsf{Z}_{\mathsf{u},\mathsf{v}}(N)}\\

where we used independence of the Brownian motions

and (
3.1

(Here,
{\mathbb{E}}_{B,X}
denotes integration with respect to the (product) law of
(B,X)
on

3.5
) and ends the proof.

Large deviations for the height function process
\{H_{N}(\left\lfloor Nx\right\rfloor)\}_{x\in[0,1]}

have been discussed in Refs.
[
17
]
and
[

with the height function interpreted as particle density profile. (There are also nice expositions in

[
18

[

In this section, we use Theorem
1.1
to deduce large deviations for the height function of the TASEP directly from Mogulskii’s theorem
[
10
, Theorem 5.1.2]
. In Section
4

we show how to recover formulas discovered in
[

]
, and we determine the additive normalization.

Let
(\mathbb{X},d)
be a complete separable metric space. Consider a sequence of probability spaces

(\Omega^{(N)},{\mathbb{P}}^{(N)})
and a family of random variables
X_{N}:\Omega^{(N)}\to\mathbb{X}
,
N=1,2,\dots
. A standard statement of the large deviation principle in Varadhan’s sense involves a family of Borel
subsets
A
of
\mathbb{X}
, their interiors
\rm{int}(A)
and closures
\rm{cl}(A)
and specifies
asymptotics of probabilities in terms of a rate function
I
by the following upper/lower bounds:

-\inf_{x\in\rm{int}(A)}I(x)\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{P}}^{(N)}(X_{N}\in A)\\
\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{P}}^{(N)}(X_{N}\in A)\leq-\inf_{x\in\rm{cl}(A)}I(x).

It will be more convenient to use an equivalent definition which we now recall; see
[
10
, Theorem 4.4.13]
. (Compare also
[
22
, Definitions 1.1.1 and 1.2.2]
.)

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{X},d)
be a complete separable metric space. The sequence
\{X_{N}\}
satisfies the
large deviation principle
(LDP), if
there exists a lower semicontinuous function
I:\mathbb{X}\to[0,\infty]
, called the
rate function
, such that

(i)

for every bounded continuous function
\Phi:\mathbb{X}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

(3.6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\exp(N\Phi(X_{N}))\right]=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\{\Phi(x)-I(x)\},

where
{\mathbb{E}}_{N}[\cdot]=\int_{\Omega^{(N)}}(\cdot)d{\mathbb{P}}_{N}
denotes the expected value.

(ii)

I
has compact level sets,
I^{-1}[0,w]
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{X}
for every
w\geq 0
.

To prove the LDP for the height function, we consider the sequence
\{X_{N}\}
of
\mathbb{X}=C([0,1],{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
-valued random variables defined on
probability spaces
\Omega^{(N)}=({\mathcal{S}}^{(N)}\times{\mathcal{S}}^{(N)},{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{TLE}}^{(N)})
obtained by
linear interpolation between the points

\left(\frac{k}{N},\frac{S_{1}(k)}{N}\right)
,
k=0,\dots,N
, in the first component and the points
\left(\frac{k}{N},\frac{S_{2}(k)}{N}\right)
,
k=0,\dots,N
, in the second component.
By Theorem
1.1
, the first component of
X_{N}
then has the same law as the continuous interpolation of
the height function (
1.2
) based on the points
\left(\frac{k}{N},\frac{H_{N}(k)}{N}\right)
,
k=0,\dots,N
.

To introduce the rate function, we need additional notation. Let

h(x)=\begin{cases}x\log x+(1-x)\log(1-x)&0\leq x\leq 1,\\
\infty&x<0\mbox{ or }x>1.\end{cases}

Denote by
\mathcal{AC}_{0}
the set of absolutely continuous functions
f\in C[0,1]
such that
f(0)=0
. Let

(3.7)

K(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b})=\log(\bar{\rho}(1-\bar{\rho})),

where

(3.8)

\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\mathsf{a}}&\mathsf{a}>1,\mathsf{a}<\mathsf{b},\\
\frac{1}{2}&\mathsf{a}\leq 1,\mathsf{b}\leq 1,\\
\frac{\mathsf{b}}{1+\mathsf{b}}&\mathsf{b}>1,\mathsf{b}>\mathsf{a},\end{cases}

denotes the limiting particle density, as indicated on the phase diagram in Fig.
2
.
(Notation
\log(\bar{\rho}(1-\bar{\rho}))
was introduced

16
]

Theorem 3.2

If

Proof.

By a theorem of Mogulskii, see

and

In order to use (
3.6

It is clear that

According to Varadhan’s lemma

A.1

3.12

From Varadhan’s lemma
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.1]
we get

The Lévy-Ottaviani maximal inequality and tail integration give the following bound:

Lemma A.1
.

For

Proof.

Recall that for sums of independent symmetric random variables

, the Lévy-Ottaviani maximal inequality says that

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\left|S_{1}(j)-S_{2}(j)\right|>t\right)\leq 2{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left(\left|S_{1}(N)-S_{2}(N)\right|>t\right),\quad t\geq 0.

0\leq-\min_{0\leq j\leq N}(S_{1}(j)-S_{2}(j))\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|S_{1}(j)-S_{2}(j)|,

we see that if

) is bounded by
1

\mathsf{c}>1
,

{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left[\mathsf{c}^{-\min_{0\leq j\leq N}(S_{1}(j)-S_{2}(j))}\right]\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathrm{rw}}\left[\mathsf{c}^{\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\left|S_{1}(j)-S_{2}(j)\right|}\right]\\

Litmus tests of the flat Λ ​ CDM {\Lambda\text{CDM}} model and model-independent measurement of H 0 ​ r d H_{0}r_{\text{d}} with LSST and DESI
Benjamin L’Huillier, Ayan Mitra, Arman Shafieloo, Ryan E Keeley, and Hanwool Koo

Abstract
In this analysis we apply a model-independent framework to test the flat Λ \Lambda CDM cosmology using simulated SNIa data from the upcoming Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) and combined with simulated Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) five-years Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) data. We adopt an iterative smoothing technique to reconstruct the expansion history from SNIa data, which, when combined with BAO measurements, facilitates a comprehensive test of the Universe’s curvature and the nature of dark energy. The analysis is conducted under four different mock true cosmologies: Two curvatures ( Ω k , 0 = 0 \Omega_{k,0}=0 and 0.1) and two models of dark energy: a cosmological constant Λ \Lambda and the phenomenologically emergent dark energy. We forecast that our reconstruction technique can constrain cosmological parameters, such as the curvature ( Ω k , 0 \Omega_{k,0} ) and c / H 0 ​ r d c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}} , with spread due to the SNIa uncertainties up to ± 4 % \pm 4\% and ± 0.1 \pm 0.1 respectively, without assuming any form of dark energy.

Theory

In an FLRW universe, when radiation is negligible, the expansion history reads

\displaystyle h^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}(z)}{H_{0}^{2}}=\Omega_{\text{m},0}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{k,0}(1+z)^{2}+(1-\Omega_{\text{m},0}-\Omega_{k,0})f_{\text{DE}}(z),

(1a)

where

\displaystyle f_{\text{DE}}(z)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(3\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1+w(x)}{1+x}\mathrm{d}x\right)

(1b)

describes the time evolution of the dark energy density,

\Omega_{\text{m},0}
and
\Omega_{k,0}
are the matter and curvature density parameters today respectively, and
w=p/\rho
is the equation of state of dark energy.
For the cosmological constant
\Lambda
,
w=-1
and
f_{\text{DE}}(z)\equiv 1
.

The dimensionless comoving distance is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\Omega_{k,0}}}\sin\left[\sqrt{-\Omega_{k,0}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{h(x)}\right]

(2)

for all signs of
\Omega_{k,0}
. In particular, for
\Omega_{k,0}=0
, eq. (
2
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{h(x)}.

(3)

The angular diameter and luminosity distances are related to the comoving distance by

\displaystyle(1+z)d_{\text{A}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{\text{L}}(z)}{1+z}=d_{\text{M}}(z)=\frac{c}{H_{0}}\mathcal{D}(z).

(4)

The distance modulus from supernovae is

\displaystyle\mu(z)

\displaystyle=5\log_{10}\frac{d_{\text{L}}}{$1\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{c}$}+25.

(5)

BAO, on the other hand, measure both the distances and expansion.
Strictly speaking, the transverse and radial modes of the BAO give

\displaystyle\frac{d_{\text{A}}(z)}{r_{\text{d}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}\frac{\mathcal{D}(z)}{1+z}\mbox{, and}

(6a)

\displaystyle\frac{c}{H(z)r_{\text{d}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}\frac{1}{h(z)}\mbox{, where}

(6b)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\int_{0}^{\tfrac{1}{1+z_{\mathrm{d}}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}a}{a^{2}h(a)\sqrt{1+\frac{3\omega_{b}}{4\omega_{\gamma}}a}}

(6c)

and
r_{\text{d}}
is the sound horizon at the drag epoch
z_{\mathrm{d}}

Several litmus tests have been put forward to test various aspects of the

\displaystyle=\frac{\Theta^{2}(z)-1}{\mathcal{D}^{2}(z)},

(9a)

\displaystyle\Theta(z)

\displaystyle=h(z)\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(z),

(9b)

where
′

In an FLRW universe,

\Theta(z)=\sqrt{1+\Omega_{k,0}\mathcal{D}^{2}}

in a flat-FLRW universe,

The
\mathcal{O}m
diagnostic, introduced by

]

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}m(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}(z)-1}{(1+z)^{3}-1}=\Omega_{\text{m},0}

(10)

in a flat-
{\Lambda\text{CDM}}
universe.
These two diagnostics are litmus tests respectively of the FLRW metric (and its curvature) and of the flat-
{\Lambda\text{CDM}}
model.
Departure from

\mathcal{O}_{k}=

Another widely used quantity is the deceleration parameter

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{H}\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-(1+z)\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}-1,

(11)

where

4.1
Curvature test

The transverse and radial BAO modes respectively give us
{d_{\text{A}}}/{r_{\text{d}}}=d_{\text{M}}/((1+z)r_{\text{d}})

and
d_{\text{H}}/r_{\text{d}}=c/{(Hr_{\text{d}})}
,
while the supernovae provide
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, which are independent of both
H_{0}
and
r_{\text{d}}
.
Intuitively, the SNIa constrain (the log of)
\mathcal{D}
, and we can obtain
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
by calculating the smooth derivative of
\mathcal{D}
(see Section
4.2
).
We can therefore combine these two measurements to estimate
H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
in two
independent ways:

\displaystyle\frac{c}{H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}(z)}\frac{d_{\text{M}}(z)}{r_{\text{d}}}\mbox{, and}

(12a)

\displaystyle\frac{c}{H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}

\displaystyle=h(z)\frac{c}{Hr_{\text{d}}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(z)}\frac{d_{\text{H}}(z)}{r_{\text{d}}},

(12b)

where the last equality in eq. (
12b
) holds only for a flat-FLRW universe.
Therefore, we can combine the BAO observables with
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
measured by SNIa to constrain
H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
.
One way to do this is to reconstruct a smooth comoving distance
\mathcal{D}(z)
, its derivative
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(z)
, and expansion history

from SNIa at any arbitrary redshift

.
To do the reconstructions, we apply the iterative smoothing method to simulated LSST SNIa as described in Section

.
We then simply divide the central value of the BAO measurements

and
d_{\text{H}}/r_{\rm d}
by the value of the reconstructed
{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
evaluated at the BAO redshifts
and then propagate the BAO error.

We can then calculate

\displaystyle\Theta(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{H(z)r_{\text{d}}}{H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(z)=\frac{d_{\text{M}}(z)/r_{\text{d}}}{d_{\text{H}}(z)/r_{\text{d}}}\frac{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(z)}{\mathcal{D}(z)}

(13)

and
\mathcal{O}_{k}
only with the BAO and the smooth reconstructions from the SNIa data.
Similarly to
H_{0}r_{\rm d}
, we evaluate
\Theta(z)
by multiplying the central values of the BAO data (

and
{d_{\text{M}}}/{r_{\text{d}}}
) with the smooth functions reconstructed from the SNIa
(
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}/\mathcal{D}
), evaluated at the BAO redshifts

, and propagating the error by taking the covariance between

and

as detailed in Appendix

4.2
Iterative Smoothing

We used the iterative smoothing method to reconstruct the distance-redshift relation from SNIa
[
48
,
49
,
32
]
.
The strength of this method is that we make no assumption regarding the evolution of dark energy.

Starting with some initial guess
\hat{\mu}_{0}
, we iteratively calculate the reconstructed
\hat{\mu}_{n+1}
at iteration
n+1
with the inverse covariance matrix of the SNIa data
\bm{C}_{\text{SN}}^{-1}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{n+1}(z)

\displaystyle=\hat{\mu}_{n}(z)+\frac{\bm{\delta\mu}_{n}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}_{\text{SN}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W}(z)}{\mathbbm{1}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}_{\text{SN}}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W}(z)},

(14a)

where

\displaystyle\bm{W}_{i}(z)

\displaystyle={\exp{\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}\left(\frac{1+z}{1+z_{i}}\right)}{2\Delta^{2}}\right)}}

(14b)

is the smoothing kernel,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}

\displaystyle=(1,\dots,1)^{\intercal}

(14c)

is a column vector,

In order to assess the power and significance of the test, we perform a hypothesis test.
One must bear in mind that the problem is not symmetric here: the null hypothesis
H_{0}
to test is “
\Omega_{k0}=0
”.
For the two flat models, we are interested in type I errors: what is

To assess these, we resimulate 1000 realizations of the data.
Since different iterations of the smoothing are correlated, one cannot simply combine them.

6
Summary and Conclusion

We combined forecast BAO data for a DESI 5-year-like survey and a realistic forecast for LSST SNIa to assess the ability of the joint surveys to provide model-independent litmus tests of the flat
{\Lambda\text{CDM}}
model.
For this purpose, we generated mock data for four cosmologies: flat
{\Lambda\text{CDM}}
a non-flat
{\Lambda\text{CDM}}
universe with
\Omega_{k,0}=0.1
, and two examples of dynamical dark energy, the PEDE model, with
\Omega_{k,0}=0
and
\Omega_{k,0}=0.1
.

We applied the iterative smoothing method to reconstruct the expansion history in a model-independent manner, only assuming flatness.

The expected quality of LSST SNIa will allow to reconstruct very accurately certain diagnostics such as
\mathcal{O}m
and
q
.
In addition, we quantify the ability of the joint surveys to constrain
c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
.
We then proceed to testing the FLRW metric, measuring
\Theta
and
\mathcal{O}_{k}
.
The combination of LSST 3-year and DESI 5-years will allow to constrain
O_{k}
to within
\sim 0.035
, and
\Theta
to within 0.0075, with a spread due to the SNIa uncertainties of up to
\pm 0.1
and 0.03 respectively.
It is worth noting that the credible interval on
\Omega_{k,0}
reported by DESI DR1 is
(-0.078,0.068)
for the flat
{\Lambda\text{CDM}}
model without any external data.
We show that with the expected five-year data accuracy, a model-independent precision on the curvature parameter will be competitive.

In the case of the
\Omega_{k,0}=0.1
universes, the flatness assumption of eq. (
3
) is now incorrect, which leads to a biased expansion history and inconsistent estimations of
c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
from our two methods.
Therefore,
H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
itself can be seen as a curvature test.
An inconsistency between the two estimates of
H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
, or with
\mathcal{O}_{k}(z)=
constant or
\Theta(z)=1
can just be interpreted either as evidence of departure from flatness, or inconsistency between the two data sets.

Appendix A
Smooth Derivatives

An interesting property of smoothing is that the derivative of the smooth function can be readily obtained from the derivative of the smoothing kernel.

Suppose we observe a function
y_{i}=f(t)+\bm{\epsilon_{i}}
, where
\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{C})
.
We want to reconstruct the smooth function
\hat{f}(t)
and its successive derivative
\hat{f}^{(i)}(t)
.

Since smoothing is essentially a convolution by a smoothing kernel, one can straightforwardly define the smooth reconstruction of the successive derivatives as

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{y}_{n}(t)+\frac{B(t)}{A(t)},

(25)

where

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\bm{\delta y}_{n}\cdot\bm{C}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W(t)},\text{and}

(26)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W(t)}.

(27)

The first and second smooth derivatives is obtained by deriving the previous equation with respect to
t
:

\displaystyle\widehat{y}^{\prime}_{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\widehat{y^{\prime}}_{n}(t)+\frac{B^{\prime}}{A}-\frac{BA^{\prime}}{A^{2}},

(28)

\displaystyle\widehat{y}^{\prime\prime}_{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\widehat{y^{\prime\prime}_{n}}(t)+\frac{B^{\prime\prime}}{A}-\frac{2A^{\prime}B^{\prime}+A^{\prime\prime}B}{A^{2}}+\frac{2A^{\prime 2}B}{A^{3}},

(29)

where

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{\delta y}_{n}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W}^{\prime}(t),

(30)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W}^{\prime}(t),

(31)

\displaystyle B^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{\delta y}_{n}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W}^{\prime\prime}(t),\text{and}

(32)

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}^{-1}\cdot\bm{W}^{\prime\prime}(t).

(33)

In this work, we apply the algorithm to the LSST simulated SNIa with
t=\ln(1+z)
.

Appendix B
Error propagation

The errors are propagated using the usual formula: for
\bm{Y}=\bm{f}(\bm{X})
,
the covariance
\bm{C}_{Y}
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\bm{J}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}_{X}\cdot\bm{J},

(34)

where
\bm{J}
is the Jacobian of
\bm{f}
, and
\bm{Y}
stands for c/
H_{0}r_{\text{d}}
,
\Theta
, or
\mathcal{O}_{k}
.

Since the correlation between two redshift bins in the BAO data is negligible, we can propagate the errors independently at fixed redshift
z
. We then have

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{J}_{c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}_{\mathrm{BAO}}\cdot\bm{J}_{c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}

(35)

where

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{c/H_{0}r_{\text{d}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}(z)}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(z)}\end{pmatrix}.

(36)

For
\Theta
and
\mathcal{O}_{k}
, the covariance matrix is simply the variance. The variance of
\Theta
is obtained by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Theta}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bm{J}_{\Theta}^{\intercal}\cdot\bm{C}_{\text{BAO}}\cdot\bm{J}_{\Theta},

(37)

where

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\Theta\begin{pmatrix}\frac{r_{\text{d}}}{d_{\text{M}}}\\
\frac{r_{\text{d}}}{d_{\text{H}}}\end{pmatrix},

(38)

and that of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}=2\frac{\Theta}{\mathcal{D}^{2}}\sigma_{\Theta}.

(39)

In all cases, the total
\chi^{2}
for reconstruction
n
is obtained by

\displaystyle\chi^{2}_{n}=\chi^{2}_{\text{SN},n}+\chi^{2}_{\text{BAO},n}.

(40)

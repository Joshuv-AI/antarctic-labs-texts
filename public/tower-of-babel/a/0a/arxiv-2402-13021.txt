Dirichlet Problems in Perforated Domains
Robert Righi, Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
In this paper we establish W 1 , p W^{1,p} estimates for solutions u ε u_{\varepsilon} to Laplace’s equation with the Dirichlet condition in a bounded and perforated, not necessarily periodically, C 1 C^{1} domain Ω ε , η \Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta} in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . The bounding constants depend explicitly on two small parameters ε \varepsilon and η \eta , where ε \varepsilon represents the scale of the minimal distance between holes, and η \eta denotes the ratio between the size of the holes and ε \varepsilon . The proof relies on a large-scale L p L^{p} estimate for ∇ u ε \nabla u_{\varepsilon} , whose proof is divided into two parts. In the first part, we show that as ε , η \varepsilon,\eta approach zero, harmonic functions in Ω ε , η \Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta} may be approximated by solutions of an intermediate problem for a Schrödinger operator in Ω \Omega . In the second part, a real-variable method is employed to establish the large-scale L p L^{p} estimate for ∇ u ε \nabla u_{\varepsilon} by using the approximation at scales above ε \varepsilon . The results are sharp except in the case d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and p = d p=d or d ′ d^{\prime} . Keywords : Uniform Estimates; Dirichlet Problem; Perforated Domain; Homogenization. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35Q35; 35B27; 76D07.

Introduction

In this paper we continue the study of the Dirichlet problem for Laplace’s equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{\varepsilon}&=F+\text{\rm div}(f)&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is a domain perforated, not necessarily periodically, with a large number of tiny holes.
Given
F\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it is well known that the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) possesses a unique solution

u_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, if
1<p<\infty
and
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

d\geq 2
.
Let
A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
denote the smallest constants for which the
W^{1,p}
estimate,

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

(1.2)

holds. We are interested in bounds of
A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
that
exhibit explicit and sharp dependence on the sizes of the holes as well as on the distances between them.

Let
Q(x,r)
denote the open cube centered at
x
with side length
r
.
To describe the perforated domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
,
let
\{Y_{z}^{s}:z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
be a family of bounded domains with connected and uniform
C^{1}
boundaries such that

B(0,c_{0})\subset Y_{z}^{s}\subset B(0,1/8)

(1.3)

for some
c_{0}>0
.
Let
\{x_{z}:z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
be a sequence of points in
Q(0,1/2)
and

T_{z,\eta}=z+x_{z}+\eta\overline{Y_{z}^{s}},

(1.4)

where
\eta\in(0,1/2)
.
For a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, define

\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{z}\varepsilon{T_{z,\eta}},

(1.5)

where the union is taken over those

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for which
\varepsilon(z+Y)\subset\Omega
, where

Thus, the perforated domain

\varepsilon(z+x_{z})

\varepsilon\eta

We point out that the holes need not to be identical, nor they are placed periodically,
unless the sequences
\{x_{z}\}
, and

To state the main results for
A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
,
we note that
A_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=1
, and that
A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=A_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})

, by duality.
As a result, it suffices to consider the case
2<p<\infty
.
Let

\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\sigma_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
&\varepsilon|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\quad&\text{ if }d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

The asymptotic behaviors of
A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, as
\varepsilon,\eta\to 0
,
depend on
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
Our first theorem treats the case of relatively large holes, where
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
, while our second theorem handles the case of relatively small holes,
where
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that

.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded

Then

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\eta^{-d|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
&C\eta^{-2|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}|\ln\eta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\quad&\text{ if }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

where

depends only on
d
,
p
,
\Omega
, and
\{Y_{z}^{s}\}
.

(1.9)

in the case

in the case
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1

\varepsilon
and
\eta
.
See Theorem

Note that in the case

The main results for

are given in Theorems

The general case

Both the bounded domain

The upper bounds obtained in

In
[
14

Under the additional assumption that the holes

The argument in
[
14

uses a large-scale Lipschitz estimate for harmonic functions

in perforated domains,
whose proof relies on the observation that the difference

It is not clear how to extend this proof to the non-periodic case and to the case of bounded domains.

In this paper we introduce a new approach to the study of

estimates in perforated domains, which allows us to handle the case of non-periodic holes as well as
boundary estimates for bounded perforated domains.
To show Theorems

we start with a localization argument as in

and reduce the

S_{\varepsilon}(F,f)=\left(\fint_{Q(x,2\varepsilon)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

by zero. It is not hard to see that

\|S_{\varepsilon}(F,f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}.

Thus, by energy estimates,

\|S_{\varepsilon}(F,f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}.

(1.13)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.5
).
Then

\|S_{\varepsilon}(F,f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\}

depends only on

,

\{Y_{z}^{s}\}
and

.

By a localization argument as well as

estimates for

in
[

]
,

1.1
and
1.2

1.3
.
Since
S_{\varepsilon}
is defined by averaging
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
over a cell of size

estimate for

.

behaves much better than

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we use a real-variable method from
[
10
]
, which reduces the problem to a reverse
Hölder inequality for harmonic functions
u_{\varepsilon}
in perforated domains.
The proof of the reverse Hölder inequality again relies on the same real-variable argument.
As such, for each subdomain

of size greater than
\varepsilon
, one needs to approximate
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
by a
function that behaves well in the
L^{p}
norm.
To this end, for each hole in the domain, we introduce an associated nonnegative potential,
supported in a neighborhood of the hole.
Let
V_{\varepsilon}
denote the sum of these potentials.
A key observation of this paper is that a harmonic function

in a perforated domain

is well approximated by

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}v_{\varepsilon}
, where
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is a corrector for

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=1

\partial D
,
and

is the solution of an intermediate equation,

(-\Delta+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon})v_{\varepsilon}=0

on
\partial D

We note that even though the holes may not be periodically placed, the potential

behaves like a constant.
As a result,

satisfies the reverse Hölder inequality for any
p>2
.

4
Correctors: the case

This section is dedicated to constructing and establishing estimates for the corrector
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}

in the case
d\geq 3
.
The two-dimensional case will be handled separately in the next section.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.5
), where
T_{z,\eta}
be given by (
1.4
).
The corrector
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
for
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is defined in a piecewise manner, following an idea in
[
1
]
.
For
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
y_{z}=z+x_{z}
and
Q_{z}=z+Q(0,1)
.
If
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega
,
we define

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon Q_{z}\setminus\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5),\\
\phi^{z}_{*}(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\varepsilon\eta})&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon B(y_{z},1/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta},\\
0&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon T_{z,\eta},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
\phi^{z}_{*}
is a solution to the exterior problem,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\phi^{z}_{*}&=0\qquad&&\text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{Y^{s}_{z}},\\
\phi^{z}_{*}&=0\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial Y^{s}_{z},\\
\phi^{z}_{*}&\rightarrow 1\qquad&&\text{as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(4.2)

In the region
\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5)\setminus\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/6)
we let
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
solve the following Dirichlet problem,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=0\qquad&&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5)\setminus\varepsilon\overline{B(y_{z},1/6)},\\
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=\phi_{*}^{z}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\varepsilon\eta}\Big)\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6),\\
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=1\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/5).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(4.3)

Finally, if
\varepsilon Q_{z}
does not lie entirely in
\Omega
, we define
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=1
in
\varepsilon Q_{z}
.
As a result, the corrector
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, taking value
1
in
\Omega^{c}
and
0
in the holes
\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}

inside
\Omega
.
The definition in (
4.3
) bridges the gap between the boundary data of the exterior problem and 1. Thus, we see that

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=1
on
\partial\Omega
and

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\setminus\partial\Omega
.

It is known that for each
\phi_{*}^{z}
that satisfies (

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \phi^{z}_{*}({x})&=1-c^{z}_{*}|x|^{2-d}+O(|x|^{1-d}),\\
\nabla\phi^{z}_{*}({x})&=-c^{z}_{*}\nabla(|x|^{2-d})+O(|x|^{-d}),\\
\nabla^{2}\phi^{z}_{*}({x})&=-c^{z}_{*}\nabla^{2}(|x|^{2-d})+O(|x|^{-d-1}),\end{aligned}\end{cases}

as

. See
[
3

15
]
.
The condition (

ensures that there exist

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be defined as above. Then

\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\begin{cases}C\eta^{d-2}&\text{for }\ \ 1\leq p<\frac{d}{d-2},\\
C\eta^{d-2}|\ln\eta|^{d-2}&\text{for}\ \ p=\frac{d}{d-2},\\
C\eta^{\frac{d}{p}}&\text{for}\ \ p>\frac{d}{d-2},\end{cases}

where
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
or
\eta
.

Proof.

Suppose
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega
.
Note that
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1=0

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/{5})\backslash B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/{6}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/{6}))}.

Since
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x)=\phi_{*}^{z}((x-\varepsilon y_{z})/(\varepsilon\eta))

\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/{5})\backslash B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/{6}))}\leq C\eta^{d-2}.

Next, we note that

\displaystyle\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\backslash\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\backslash\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\phi^{z}_{*}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\varepsilon\eta}\Big)-1|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\backslash\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{d}\eta^{d}\int_{B(0,\frac{1}{6\eta})\backslash Y^{s}_{z}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{(d-2)p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{d}\eta^{d}\int_{c}^{\frac{1}{6\eta}}r^{d-1-dp+2p}\,dr.

\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\varepsilon Q_{z}\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta})}\leq\begin{cases}C\varepsilon^{d}\eta^{p(d-2)}&\text{for }\ \ 1\leq p<\frac{d}{d-2},\\
C\varepsilon^{d}\eta^{d}|\ln\eta|&\text{for}\ \ p=\frac{d}{d-2},\\
C\varepsilon^{d}\eta^{d}&\text{for}\ \ p>\frac{d}{d-2}.\end{cases}

(4.7)

\varepsilon Q_{z}

Suppose

Proof.

We start by decomposing the integral along the sub-regions within the cube to obtain

\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}=\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{5})\setminus B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}+\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}.

(4.9)

By Remark
4.2
,
the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.9
) is bounded by
C\varepsilon^{d}(\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2})^{p}
.
For the second term, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\backslash\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\backslash\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\nabla(\phi_{*}^{z}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\varepsilon\eta}\Big))|^{p}\,dx

(4.10)

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon\eta)^{-p}\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\frac{\varepsilon}{6})\backslash\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\nabla\phi_{*}^{z}(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\epsilon\eta})|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon\eta)^{d-p}\int_{B(0,\frac{1}{6\eta})\backslash Y_{z}^{s}}|\nabla\phi_{*}^{z}(y)|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon\eta)^{d-p}\int_{c}^{\frac{1}{6\eta}}r^{(1-d)p+d-1}dr,

where we have used (
4.4
) as well as the fact that
\nabla\phi_{*}^{z}
is

integrable near
\partial Y_{z}^{s}
under the
condition that
Y_{z}^{s}
is uniformly

It follows that

\left(\fint_{\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/6)}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\begin{cases}C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-p}{p}}&\text{if }p>d^{\prime},\\
C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-p}{p}}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{if }p=d^{\prime},\\
C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}&\text{if }p<d^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(4.11)

Combining (
4.11
) with the estimate for
B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/5)\setminus B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)

gives (
4.8
).
∎

Recall that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\eta^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}
for
d\geq 3

\mu_{*}^{z}

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega

\left|\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\mu_{*}^{z}\phi\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\phi|,

(4.12)

where
\phi\in H^{1}(\varepsilon Q_{z})
and
\phi=0
in
\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\mu_{*}^{z}\phi

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}\setminus B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi+\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\mu_{*}^{z}\phi.

By Remark
4.2
,

\left|\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}\setminus B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\phi|.

(4.14)

For the remaining two terms in the right-hand side of (

we use integration by parts to get

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\left(\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}-\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\right)\cdot\left(\phi-\alpha\right)

\displaystyle+\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\phi

(4.15)

\displaystyle-\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\mu_{*}^{z}\phi,

where

denotes the outward unit normal and

\alpha

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is harmonic in

Using (

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\left(\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}-\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\right)\cdot\left(\phi-\alpha\right)\right|

where we have used a trace inequality for the last step (see Remark

gives the desired bound.

To bound

\left|\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\phi-\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\mu_{*}^{z}\phi\right|,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}

As a result, (

\mu_{*}^{z}(\varepsilon\eta)^{d-2}\left|\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/4)}\phi-\fint_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\phi\right|,

C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\phi|.

∎

(4.20)

Correctors: the two-dimensional case

In this section we introduce a corrector
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
for the case

4.1
,
4.3
and
4.4

For

, let
\phi^{z}_{*}
be a solution to the exterior problem,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\phi^{z}_{*}&=0\qquad&&\text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{Y^{s}_{z}},\\
\phi^{z}_{*}&=0\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial Y^{s}_{z},\\
\phi^{z}_{*}(x)-\ln|x|&=O(1)\qquad&&\text{as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(5.1)

It is known that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla\phi_{*}^{z}(x)&=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-2}),\\
\nabla^{2}\phi_{*}^{z}(x)&=O(|x|^{-2}),\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

as
|x|\to\infty
.
Recall that
y_{z}=z+x_{z}
and
Q_{z}=z+Q(0,1)

If
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega
, we let

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon Q_{z}\setminus\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5),\\
\phi^{z}_{*}(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\varepsilon\eta})/|\ln\eta|&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon B(y_{z},1/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta},\\
0&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon T_{z,\eta}.\end{cases}

(5.3)

In the region
\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5)\setminus\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/6)

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
solve the following Dirichlet problem,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=0\qquad&&\text{in}\ \ \varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5)\setminus\varepsilon\overline{B(y_{z},1/6)},\\
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=\phi_{*}^{z}\Big(\frac{x-\varepsilon y_{z}}{\varepsilon\eta}\Big)/|\ln\eta|\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6),\\
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}&=1\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/5).\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(5.4)

Finally, if
\varepsilon Q_{z}
is not contained in
\Omega

let
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=1
in
\varepsilon Q_{z}
, as in the case of
d\geq 3
.
By construction, we have
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=1
in
\Omega^{c}
and
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=0
in

\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
Furthermore, in the region
\varepsilon B(y_{z},1/5)\setminus B(y_{z},1/6)

|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1|\leq C|\ln\eta|^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}.

(5.5)

As in the case

from the maximum principle and the Lipschitz estimates for harmonic functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be defined as above. Then for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C|\ln\eta|^{-1},

(5.6)

where
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
or
\eta
.

Proof.

Note that if
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\ln\eta|^{-p}+\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}\left|\frac{\ln|(x-\varepsilon y_{z})/\varepsilon|}{\ln\eta}\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{2}}{|\ln\eta|^{p}}\left\{\int_{B(0,1)}|\ln|x||^{p}\,dx+1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon^{2}}{|\ln\eta|^{p}},

where we have used (
5.5
) as well as the fact that
\phi_{*}^{z}(x)=\ln|x|+O(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Summing over all such cubes yields

\|\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C|\ln\eta|^{-p},

which yields (
5.6
).
∎

Recall that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon|\ln\eta|^{1/2}
for
d=2
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega
. Then

\left(\fint_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\begin{cases}C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1/2}&\text{if }1<p<2,\\
C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}&\text{if }p=2,\\
C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\eta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}|\ln\eta|^{-1/2}&\text{if }2<p<\infty,\end{cases}

(5.7)

where the constant
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
or
\eta
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2-p}|\ln\eta|^{-p}+\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=C\varepsilon^{2-p}|\ln\eta|^{-p}+\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}\left|\left(\frac{C}{|\ln\eta||x-\varepsilon y_{z}|}\right)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=C\varepsilon^{2-p}|\ln\eta|^{-p}\left\{1+\int_{c\eta}^{1}r^{1-p}dr\right\},

where we have used (
5.5
) and (
5.2
).
It follows that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\begin{cases}C\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}&\text{if }1<p<2,\\
C\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }p=2,\\
C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{2-p}{p}}|\ln\eta|^{-1}&\text{if }2<p<\infty,\end{cases}

which gives (
5.7
)
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
\varepsilon Q_{z}\subset\Omega
. Then

\left|\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-2\pi\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\phi\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}|\nabla\phi|,

where
\phi\in H^{1}(\varepsilon Q_{z})
and

in
\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
4.4
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-2\pi\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/5)\setminus B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi+\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\frac{2\pi}{|\ln\eta|}\fint_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/5)\setminus B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi+\left\{\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\phi-\frac{2\pi}{|\ln\eta|}\fint_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\phi\right\}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2},

where we have used integration by parts and the fact that
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is harmonic in
B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)\setminus\varepsilon T_{z,\eta}
.
By (
5.5
), it is easy to see that
|I_{1}|
is bounded by the right-hand side of (
5.8
).

To bound
I_{2}
, we write it as

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\left(\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}-\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\right)\cdot\left(\phi-\alpha\right)

(5.10)

\displaystyle+\left\{\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\frac{\partial\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\partial n}\fint_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}\phi-\frac{2\pi}{|\ln\eta|}\fint_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\phi\right\}

\displaystyle=I_{21}+I_{22},

where

\displaystyle|I_{21}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon|\ln\eta|}\int_{\partial B(\varepsilon y_{z},\varepsilon/6)}|\phi-\alpha|

6
Convergence Rates

In this section we study the Dirichlet problem with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{\varepsilon}&=0\qquad&&\text{in}\ \ \Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=h\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=0\qquad&&\text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\setminus\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(6.1)

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is given in (
1.5
) and
h\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
.
Let
V_{\varepsilon}(x)=V(x/\varepsilon)
, where

V(y)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mu_{*}^{z}\chi_{Q(z,1)}(y),

(6.2)

and
\mu_{*}^{z}
is given by (
4.5
) for
d\geq 3
and
\mu_{*}^{z}=2\pi
for
d=2
.
In (
6.2
) we have used
\chi_{Q(z,1)}
to denote the characteristic function of the cell
Q(z,1)

Note that
c_{0}\leq V(y)\leq c_{1}
for some
c_{0},c_{1}>0

Let
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be the corrector constructed in the last two sections for the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}u_{0,\varepsilon}

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{0,\varepsilon}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon}(x)u_{0,\varepsilon}&=0&\quad&\text{in}\ \ \Omega,\\

\sigma_{\varepsilon}

The rest of this section will be divided into two parts.

6.1

Theorem 6.1

u_{\varepsilon}

C

\varepsilon
or
\eta

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for any
p>2

\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=1
on
\partial\Omega
and
\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\setminus\partial\Omega

r_{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
. Then

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon}\phi\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla\phi|,

(6.6)

where
V_{\varepsilon}=V(x/\varepsilon)
and
V(y)
is defined in (
6.2
).

Proof.

We extend
\phi
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by zero.
Note that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon}\phi\right|

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\int_{\varepsilon Q_{z}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon}\phi\right|+\left|\int_{\mathcal{F}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon}\phi\right|,

\Omega^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
is the union of cubes
\varepsilon Q_{z}
lying entirely inside of
\Omega

\mathcal{F}
is the union
of cubes with non-empty intersection with the boundary of
\Omega
.
By Lemma
4.4
, the first term in the right-hand side of (
6.7

the right-hand side of (
6.6
).
For the second term we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathcal{F}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}V_{\varepsilon}\phi\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\int_{\text{\rm dist(}x,\partial\Omega)\leq C\varepsilon}|\phi|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{-2}\int_{\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq C\varepsilon}|\nabla\phi|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}|\nabla\phi|,

where we have used a Poincaré-Sobolev inequality for the second inequality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of (
6.1
) and
u_{0,\varepsilon}
a solution of (
6.3
).
Assume further that
u_{0,\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for some

.
Let
r_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}u_{0,\varepsilon}
.
Note that for any
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla r_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}(\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi)u_{0,\varepsilon}-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\left(\nabla u_{0,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\right)

(6.8)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla(u_{0,\varepsilon}\phi)+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}[\nabla(\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1)\cdot\nabla u_{0,\varepsilon}]\phi

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}(\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1)\left(\nabla u_{0,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\right)-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\left(\nabla u_{0,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\right).

By using integration by parts, the right-hand side of (
6.8
) becomes

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla(u_{0,\varepsilon}\phi)

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}(\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1)\left(\nabla u_{0,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}(\chi_{\varepsilon,\eta}-1)\Delta u_{0,\varepsilon}\cdot\phi+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\left(\Delta u_{0,\varepsilon}\cdot\phi\right),

which, in view of (
6.3

Proof.

Let

Theorem 7.5

\Omega

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla G\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C\lambda\|G\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

(9.8)

holds. By a duality argument we have

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=A_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}),\quad D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=D_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=C_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}),

(9.9)

where
1<p<\infty
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
.

We begin with the sharpness of
D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and

\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
9.7
).
Then, for
d\geq 2
and
1<p<\infty
,

D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\approx\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1).

(9.10)

Proof.

The upper bound
D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1)
is contained in Theorem
2.3
.
To show the lower bound
D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1)
, by (
9.9
), we may assume
1<p\leq 2
.
Without loss of generality we may also assume
Q(0,4r_{0})\subset\Omega
.

Suppose
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small and
R\varepsilon\leq r_{0}
for some
R\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q(0,2R\varepsilon))
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
\phi=1
in
Q(0,R\varepsilon)
,

|\nabla\phi|\leq C/(R\varepsilon)
and
|\nabla^{2}\phi|\leq C/(R\varepsilon)^{2}
.
Note that

-\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi)=\varepsilon^{-2}\eta^{d-2}\phi-2\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\cdot\nabla\phi-\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\Delta\phi

(9.11)

in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle cR^{d/p}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\leq\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq CD_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}\eta^{d-2}R^{\frac{d}{p}}+\varepsilon^{-1}R^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}+\varepsilon^{-2}R^{\frac{d}{p}-2}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\right\},

where we have used the periodicity of
\psi_{\epsilon,\eta}
.
This gives

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{p}}\fint_{Q(0,\varepsilon)}|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|

(9.12)

\displaystyle\leq CD_{p}{(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\left\{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{p}-2}\eta^{d-2}+R^{-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\left(\fint_{Q(0,\varepsilon)}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+(\varepsilon R)^{-2}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\right\},

where we have used the fact
1<p\leq 2
.

Case 1:

d\geq 3
and
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1.

By applying Lemmas
9.1
and
9.2
to (
9.12
), we obtain

c\leq D_{p}{(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\left\{\varepsilon^{-2}\eta^{d-2}+R^{-1}\varepsilon^{-2}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+(\varepsilon R)^{-2}\right\}.

(9.13)

Since
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\leq 1
,
we may choose
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that
R\approx\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}
in (
9.13
) to get

c\leq D_{p}{(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\cdot\varepsilon^{-2}\eta^{d-2}.

Hence,

D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1).

Case 2:

d\geq 3
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
.

In this case we choose
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that
R\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
9.13
). It follows that

c\leq D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-2}\eta^{d-2}+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+1\right\}.

Since
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
, we obtain

D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1).

Case 3:

d=2
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
.

Applying Lemmas
9.1
and
9.2
to (
9.12
) now yields

c|\ln\eta|\leq D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-2}+R^{-1}\varepsilon^{-2}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\varepsilon R)^{-2}|\ln\eta|\right\}.

(9.14)

Picking
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that
R\approx|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}
in (
9.14
) gives

D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\,\varepsilon^{2}|\ln\eta|=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1).

Case 4:

d=2
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
.

Choosing
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that
R\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
9.14
) yields

\displaystyle c|\ln\eta|

\displaystyle\leq D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-2}+\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\ln\eta|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq CD_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})|\ln\eta|.

It follows that
D_{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon})\geq c=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2},1)
.
∎

Next, we consider the case
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
for
1<p\leq 2
.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
9.7
).
Then

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\approx\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)

(9.15)

for
d\geq 2
and
1<p\leq 2
,

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
2.3
that

C_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)

for
2\leq p<\infty
.
Since
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=C_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, we obtain the upper bound

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)

for
1<p\leq 2
.
To establish the lower bounds, we first note that

D_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)B_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, which follows (
2.2
).
As a result, by Theorem
9.3
,

B_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)
.

For the case
1<p<2
,
we proceed as in the proof of Theorem
9.3
.
It follows from (
9.11
) that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\|\nabla(\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq CB_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{p}-2}\eta^{d-2}R^{\frac{d}{p}}+\varepsilon^{-1}R^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}+\varepsilon^{-2}R^{\frac{d}{p}-2}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\right\},

where
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{d}
.
This implies that

\fint_{Q(0,\varepsilon)}|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|\leq CB_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}R+\left(\fint_{Q(0,\varepsilon)}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{2}\right)^{{1}/{2}}+(\varepsilon R)^{-1}\left(\fint_{Q(0,\varepsilon)}|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right\}.

(9.16)

Case 1:

d\geq 3
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1.

We apply Lemmas
9.1
and
9.2
to (
9.16
) to obtain

c\leq B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{d-2}R+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\right\}.

(9.17)

Letting
R\approx\eta^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}
in (
9.17
) yields

c\leq B_{p}(\Omega_{\epsilon})\cdot\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Thus,

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\sigma_{\varepsilon}=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)
.

Case 2:

d\geq 3
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1.

Let
R\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
9.17
). Then

c\leq B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-2}+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}+C\right\}.

Thus,
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)
.

Case 3:

d=2
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
.

Applying Lemmas
9.1
and
9.2
to (
9.16
) now yields

c|\ln\eta|\leq B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-1}R+\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\varepsilon R)^{-1}|\ln\eta|\right\}.

(9.18)

Picking
R\approx|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}
in (
9.18
) gives

c|\ln\eta|\leq B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\cdot\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus,

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\varepsilon|\ln\eta|^{1/2}=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)
.

Case 4:

d=2
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
.

Taking
R\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
9.18
) leads to

\displaystyle c|\ln\eta|

\displaystyle\leq B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-2}+\varepsilon^{-1}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\ln\eta|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq CB_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})|\ln\eta|.

This implies that
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c=c\min(\sigma_{\varepsilon},1)
.
∎

We now consider
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
for
2<p<\infty
.

Theorem 9.5
.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
9.7
).
Assume
d\geq 3
. Then if
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
and
2<p<\infty
,

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\approx\varepsilon\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}}.

(9.19)

If
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
and
2<p<d
, we have

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\approx 1+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}.

(9.20)

Proof.

Suppose
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
. The upper bound

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C\varepsilon\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}}
is contained in Theorem
3.2
.
To prove the lower bound, we use the Poincaré inequality,

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\min(\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{p}},1)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

(9.21)

for
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, where
2<p<d
.
The inequality (
9.21
) follows readily from Lemma
2.1
.
As a result, we see that

D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq CB_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\min(\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{p}},1).

(9.22)

By Theorem
9.3
, we have
D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2}=c\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}
.
This, together with (
9.22
), gives (
9.19
) for
the case
2<p<d
.

Next, we use a convexity argument to handle the case
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
and
d\leq p<\infty
.
Choose
2<q<d
and
t\in(0,1)
so that

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-t}{2}+\frac{t}{p}.

By Riesz-Thorin Theorem we have

B_{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq\left[B_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\right]^{1-t}\left[B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\right]^{t}.

It follows that

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq\left[B_{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\right]^{\frac{1}{t}}\left[B_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\right]^{1-\frac{1}{t}}.

The desired estimate follows by using
B_{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}
and
B_{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\varepsilon\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{q}}
.

Finally, we consider the case
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
and
2<p<d
.
The upper bound is contained in Theorem
3.3
.
Since
D_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c
,
by (
9.22
), we obtain

B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}

However, (
9.22
) also yields
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq cD_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c
.
As a result, we obtain
B_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c(1+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1})
.
∎

Finally, we establish lower bounds for
A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.

Theorem 9.6
.

Let
2<p<\infty
.
Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
9.7
).
Then, if
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
,

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &c\eta^{-d|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}\qquad&&\text{if}\ d\geq 3,\\
&c\eta^{-2|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}|\ln\eta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\qquad&&\text{if}\ d=2.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(9.23)

If
\sigma_{\epsilon}\geq 1
we have

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &c(1+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1})\qquad&&\text{if}\ d\geq 3\text{ and }2<p<d,\\
&c\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\qquad&&\text{if}\ d\geq 3\text{ and }d\leq p<\infty,\\
&c\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}\qquad&&\text{if}\ d=2\text{ and }2<p<\infty.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(9.24)

The constants
c>0
only depend on
d
,
p
,
Y^{s}
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution to
-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}

with
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
By a Sobolev imbedding, we have

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

where
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{d}
and
1<p^{\prime}<d.

By duality this implies that if
-\Delta v_{\varepsilon}=G
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
v_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},
then

\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\|G\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}.

Thus, if
-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
with
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, then

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\right\},

(9.25)

where
d^{\prime}<p<\infty
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{d}
.

Let
\phi
and
\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be the same as in the proof of Theorem
9.3
. Note that

-\Delta(\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi)=\varepsilon^{-2}\eta^{d-2}\phi-2\text{\rm div}(\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\nabla\phi)+\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\Delta\phi

in
\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.
It follows from (
9.25
) that

\displaystyle R^{\frac{d}{p}}\|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\leq C\|\nabla(\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\phi)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}

\displaystyle\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{q}-2}\eta^{d-2}R^{\frac{d}{q}}+\varepsilon^{-1}R^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}+\varepsilon^{-2}R^{\frac{d}{q}-2}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{q}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\right\},

where we have used the periodicity of
\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}

(9.26)

\displaystyle\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\eta^{d-2}R+\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}+\varepsilon^{-2}R^{-1}\|\psi_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{q}(Q(0,\varepsilon))}\right\}.

Case 1:

d\geq 3
and
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1.

Applying Lemma
9.2
to (
9.26
) yields

\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\eta^{d-2}R+R^{-1}\right\}.

(9.27)

Since
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
, we may pick

R\approx\eta^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}
in (
9.27
) and obtain

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq C\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}}=C\eta^{-d|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|}.

Case 2:

d=2
and
0<\sigma_{\varepsilon}\leq 1.

Applying Lemma
9.2
to (
9.26
) now yields

\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{R+|\ln\eta|R^{-1}\right\}.

(9.28)

Picking
R\approx|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}
in (
9.28
) gives

\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}},

which leads to

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\eta^{-2|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}|\ln\eta|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Case 3:

d\geq 3
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1.

Choosing
R\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
9.27
) yields

\displaystyle\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}

\displaystyle\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\eta^{d-2}\varepsilon^{-1}+\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}).

Thus,

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\,\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}.

This gives the lower bound in (
9.24
) for the case
d\leq p<\infty
.
Note that if
2<p<d
, by the Poincaré inequality
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}
, we have

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c\,C_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})=c\,B_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c.

Hence,

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq c(1+\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}).

Case 4:

d=2
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq 1.

Now we take
R\approx\varepsilon^{-1}
in (
9.28
). This gives

\displaystyle\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}

\displaystyle\leq CA_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\left\{\varepsilon^{-1}+\varepsilon|\ln\eta|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})|\ln\eta|,

which leads to

A_{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq C\varepsilon^{-1}\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

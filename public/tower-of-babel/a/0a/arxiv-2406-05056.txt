l 2 {l}^{2} Decoupling for certain degenerate surfaces in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}
Kalachand Shuin

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
42B37, 42B10

Abstract:
In this article, we aim to study decoupling inequality for a specific degenerate hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Inspired by the work of Bourgain–Demeter and Li–Zheng, we consider the hypersurface
\mathcal{S}^{3}_{4}:=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi^{4}_{1}+\xi^{4}_{2}+\xi^{4}_{3}):0\leq\xi_{j}\leq 1,\text{for}~j=1,2,3\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and study decoupling estimates.

1.
Introduction

Decoupling inequality, a novel concept in Euclidean harmonic analysis, manifests as a variant of almost orthogonality in the non-Hilbert spaces. It’s inception traces back to Wolff’s exploration in his local smoothing work, as documented in
[
15
]
. Subsequently, in 2015, Bourgain and Demeter
[
3
]
proved the sharp decoupling conjecture for any compact
C^{2}
hypersurface with positive definite secound fundamental form. Since then, numerous mathematicians have delved into the decoupling problem.
Decoupling inequality has numerous applications in the field of number theory, distance problems and PDEs
[
5
,
4
,
10
]
. Very recently, Gan and Wu
[
6
]
have established weighted decoupling inequality and using that idea they have improved the maximal Bochner–Riesz conjecture in every dimensions. In
[
2
]
Bourgain and Demeter proved decoupling theory for general nondegenerate surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Also see
[
14
,
8
]
for more about decoupling inequalities associated with quadratic forms and
3
-dimensional nondegenerate surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
. On the other hand, Biswas et. al.
[
1
]
have investigated decoupling inequality for finite-type curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Recently, Li and Zheng
[
12
]
have studied decoupling inequality of degenerate hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The result of
[
12
]
can be deduced from the work of Li and Yang
[
16
]
. In
[
16
]
, the authors have investigated decoupling for mixed-homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Therefore, it is natural to investigate decoupling inequality for degenerate hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
Given a function
f\in L^{1}([0,1]^{3})
and each subset
Q
of
[0,1]^{3}
, we consider the following Fourier extension operator

\mathcal{E}_{Q}f(x):=\int_{Q}f(\vec{\xi})e(x_{1}\xi_{1}+x_{2}\xi_{2}+x_{3}\xi_{3}+x_{4}(\xi^{4}_{1}+\xi^{4}_{2}+\xi^{4}_{3}))d\vec{\xi},

where
e(t)=e^{2\pi\iota t}
,
\vec{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
and
d\vec{\xi}=d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}d\xi_{3}
.
The hypersurface
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{2}:=\{(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\xi^{2}_{i})\}
denotes the paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Fourier extension operator associated with the paraboloid
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{2}
and defined on the unit cube
[0,1]^{n-1}
is denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{Par}_{[0,1]^{n-1}}
. Bourgain and Demeter proved the following result

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

For
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
and any
\epsilon>0
the following estimate holds

\|\mathcal{E}^{Par}_{[0,1]^{n-1}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C(\epsilon,p)R^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta:R^{-1/2}-\text{cubes in }~[0,1]^{n-1}}\|\mathcal{E}^{Par}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the weight function
\omega_{B_{R}}
associated with a ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
of radius
R
and centered at
x_{0}
is given by
\omega_{B_{R}}(x)=(1+\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{R})^{-100n}
.
In
[
12
]
, Li and Zheng have studied decoupling inequality for finite type surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In fact, they have proved the following result

Theorem 1.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

For
2\leq p\leq 4
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C(\epsilon,p)
s.t.

\|\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C(\epsilon,p)R^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{F}_{3}(R,4)}\|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the Fourier extension operator
\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^{2}}
is associated with the phase function
\xi^{4}_{1}+\xi^{4}_{2}
on the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{3}(R,4)
is defined in
[
12
]
. Also see
[
13
]
, where the authors have studied restriction estimates for the hypersurface
\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi^{4}_{1}+\xi^{4}_{2}):0\leq\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\leq 1\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Motivated by the work of Li and Zheng
[
12
]
, we consider the following degenerate hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}

\mathcal{S}^{3}_{4}:=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi^{4}_{1}+\xi^{4}_{2}+\xi^{4}_{3}):0\leq\xi_{j}\leq 1,\text{for}~j=1,2,3\}.

Our goal is to prove decoupling inequality for the extension operator
\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^{3}}
associated with the phase function
\xi^{4}_{1}+\xi^{4}_{2}+\xi^{4}_{3}
.

1.1.
Notations and main results

Given
1\ll R
, we partition the unit interval
[0,1]
as

[0,1]=\cup_{k}\theta_{k},

where
\theta_{0}=[0,R^{-1/4}]
, and
\theta_{k}=[2^{k-1}R^{-1/4},2^{k}R^{-1/4}]
for
1\leq k\leq\frac{1}{4}\log_{2}R
. Further, we decompose each
\theta_{k}
as follows

(1)

\displaystyle\theta_{k}=\cup^{2^{2(k-1)}}_{\mu=1}\theta_{k,\mu},

where
\theta_{k,\mu}=[2^{k-1}R^{-1/4}+(\mu-1)2^{-(k-1)}R^{-1/4},2^{k-1}R^{-1/4}+\mu 2^{-(k-1)}R^{-1/4}]
. Then we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{4}(R,4)=\Big\{\theta_{k_{1},\mu_{1}}\times\theta_{k_{2},\mu_{2}}\times\theta_{k_{3},\mu_{3}},\theta_{0}\times\theta_{k_{2},\mu_{2}}\times\theta_{k_{3},\mu_{3}},\theta_{k_{1},\mu_{1}}\times\theta_{0}\times\theta_{k_{3},\mu_{3}},

\displaystyle\theta_{k_{1},\mu_{1}}\times\theta_{k_{2},\mu_{2}}\times\theta_{0},\theta_{k_{1},\mu_{1}}\times\theta_{0}\times\theta_{0},\theta_{0}\times\theta_{k_{2},\mu_{2}}\times\theta_{0},\theta_{0}\times\theta_{0}\times\theta_{k_{3},\mu_{3}},\theta_{0}\times\theta_{0}\times\theta_{0}\Big\},

where
1\leq k_{i}\leq\frac{1}{4}\log_{2}R
and
1\leq\mu_{i}\leq 2^{2(k_{i}-1)}
for
i=1,2,3
.
The similar types of partition can be found in
[
13
]
.
Now we state our main result

Theorem 1.3
.

For
2\leq p\leq\frac{10}{3}
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C(\epsilon,p)
such that

(2)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^{3}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C(\epsilon,p)R^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{F}_{4}(R,4)}\|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 1.4
.

It is important to note that the range of
p
in Theorem
1.3
is same as the range of
p
in Theorem
1.1
for dimension
n=4
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer. Then we consider the operator

\mathcal{E}^{m}_{[0,1]^{3}}f(x):=\int_{[0,1]^{3}}f(\vec{\xi})e(x_{1}\xi_{1}+x_{2}\xi_{2}+x_{3}\xi_{3}+x_{4}(\xi^{2m}_{1}+\xi^{2m}_{2}+\xi^{2m}_{3}))d\vec{\xi}.

Remark 1.5
.

For
2\leq p\leq\frac{10}{3}
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C(\epsilon,p)
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}^{m}_{[0,1]^{3}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C(\epsilon,p)R^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{F}_{4}(R,2m)}\|\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The proof of the remark
1.5
can be completed using the similar proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Observe that the Gaussian curvature of the hypersurface is

\kappa=12^{3}(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3})^{2}.

Therefore, the curvature vanishes on the planes
\xi_{1}=0,\xi_{2}=0
or
\xi_{3}=0
. The main concepts we aim to use to prove the theorem are the decoupling inequality of perturbed paraboloid and induction on scales. Therefore,
we decompose the unit cube
[0,1]^{3}
into following eight pieces. Let
1\ll K\ll R^{\epsilon}
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
, such that
K^{-1/4},R^{-1/4}
and
(\frac{R}{K})^{-1/4}
are dyadic numbers. Then

[0,1]^{3}=\cup^{7}_{i=0}\Omega_{i},

where

•

\Omega_{0}:=[0,K^{-1/4}]^{3}
,

•

\Omega_{1}=[K^{-1/4},1]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]^{2}
,
\Omega_{2}=[0,K^{-1/4}]\times[K^{-1/4},1]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]
,
\Omega_{3}=[0,K^{-1/4}]^{2}\times[K^{-1/4},1]
,

•

\Omega_{4}=[0,K^{-1/4}]\times[K^{-1/4},1]^{2}
,
\Omega_{5}=[K^{-1/4},1]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]\times[K^{-1/4},1]
,
\Omega_{6}=[K^{-1/4},1]^{2}\times[0,K^{-1/4}]
,

•

\Omega_{7}=[K^{-1/4},1]^{3}
.

Using Minkowski’s inequality and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

\|\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^{3}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq 2^{3/2}\Big(\sum^{7}_{j=0}\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{j}}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Note that unlike
[
12
]
, we have more Fourier extension operators with degenerate hypersurfaces.
Therefore, we need to deal with each operator separately.
We shall prove Theorem
1.3
in the following three sections.

2.
Estimate of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{7}}f

We exploit the ideas of
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
. We employ Bourgain and Demeter’s decoupling for perturbed paraboloid. In order to do that we divide the region
\Omega_{7}
into small pieces
\Omega_{7}=\cup\Omega_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}
, where
\Omega_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}):\lambda_{j}\leq\xi_{j}\leq 2\lambda_{j}\}
for
\lambda_{j}\in[K^{-1/4},1/2]
and
j=1,2,3
. Note that
\Omega_{7}:=[K^{-1/4},1]\times[K^{-1/4},1]\times[K^{-1/4},1]
. Therefore, the Gaussian curvature of
\mathcal{S}^{3}_{4}
is almost a constant and non-zero in the region
\Omega_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}
. Further, we divide each
\Omega_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}
into small pieces

\displaystyle\Omega_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}=\bigcup_{j,l,m}I^{j}_{\lambda_{1}}\times I^{l}_{\lambda_{2}}\times I^{m}_{\lambda_{3}}:=\bigcup\tau^{j,l,m}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}},

where

\displaystyle I^{j}_{\lambda_{1}}=[\lambda_{1}+\frac{j-1}{\lambda_{1}K^{1/2}},\lambda_{1}+\frac{j}{\lambda_{1}K^{1/2}}],~1\leq j\leq\lambda_{1}K^{1/2},

\displaystyle I^{l}_{\lambda_{2}}=[\lambda_{2}+\frac{l-1}{\lambda_{2}K^{1/2}},\lambda_{2}+\frac{l}{\lambda_{2}K^{1/2}}],~1\leq l\leq\lambda_{2}K^{1/2},

\displaystyle I^{m}_{\lambda_{3}}=[\lambda_{3}+\frac{m-1}{\lambda_{3}K^{1/2}},\lambda_{3}+\frac{m}{\lambda_{3}K^{1/2}}],~1\leq m\leq\lambda_{3}K^{1/2}.

We abbreviate each
\tau^{j,l,m}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}
by
\tau
and w.l.o.g. we consider

(3)

\displaystyle\tau=[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}+\lambda^{-1}_{1}K^{-1/2}]\times[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}+\lambda^{-1}_{2}K^{-1/2}]\times[\lambda_{3},\lambda_{3}+\lambda^{-1}_{3}K^{-1/2}].

The proof for other
\tau
can be followed by similar methods.
Now applying the following change of variables

(4)

\displaystyle\xi_{j}=\lambda_{j}+\lambda^{-1}_{j}K^{-1/2}\eta_{j},~~~\text{for}~~j=1,2,3

we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\tau}f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}(\vec{\eta})e(\tilde{x_{1}}\eta_{1}+\tilde{x_{2}}\eta_{2}+\tilde{x_{3}}\eta_{3}+\tilde{x_{4}}\psi(\vec{\eta}))d\vec{\eta}\Big|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}^{pertp}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})|,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{7}(x),~~\tilde{x}_{j}=\lambda_{j}K^{-1/2}x_{j}+4\lambda^{2}_{j}K^{-1/2}x_{4},~j=1,2,3,~\text{and}~\tilde{x}_{4}=K^{-1}x_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\vec{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3})^{-1}K^{-3/2}f(\lambda_{1}+\lambda^{-1}_{1}K^{-1/2}\eta_{1},\lambda_{2}+\lambda^{-1}_{2}K^{-1/2}\eta_{2},\lambda_{3}+\lambda^{-1}_{3}K^{-1/2}\eta_{3}),

\displaystyle\psi(\vec{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{3}_{j=1}(6\eta^{2}_{j}+4\lambda^{-2}_{j}K^{-1/2}\eta^{3}_{j}+\lambda^{-4}_{j}K^{-1}\eta^{4}_{j})

and
\mathcal{E}^{pertp}_{[0,1]^{3}}
denotes the Fourier extension operator associated with the phase function
\psi
.
We claim that for
2\leq p\leq\frac{10}{3}
and any
\epsilon>0
,

(5)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{7}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C(\epsilon,p)R^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\in\mathcal{F}_{4}(R,4)}\|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

\Omega_{7}=\bigcup_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}\bigcup_{j,l,m}\tau^{j,l,m}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}},

therefore using Minkowski’s integral inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{7}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{K})}\lesssim K^{3/4}\Big(\sum_{\tau\subset\Omega_{7}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{K})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Summing over all balls
B_{K}\subset B_{R}
we get,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{7}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim K^{3/4}\Big(\sum_{\tau\subset\Omega_{7}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, applying the above change of variables (
4
) we get

\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}K^{5/2}\|\mathcal{E}^{pertp}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{T}_{7}(B_{R}))},

where
\tilde{f}
is defined above.
Observe that the phase function
\psi
corresponding to the operator
\mathcal{E}^{pertp}_{[0,1]^{3}}
can be written as
\psi(\vec{\eta})=\sum^{3}_{j=1}\phi_{j}(\eta_{j})
. Since
\lambda_{j}\geq K^{-1/4}
and
0\leq\xi_{j}\leq 1
, therefore each
\phi_{j}
satisfies the following non-degenerate conditions

(6)

\displaystyle\phi_{j}^{\prime\prime}\sim 1,~|\phi_{j}^{(3)}|\lesssim 1,~|\phi_{j}^{(4)}|\lesssim 1~\text{and}~|\phi_{j}^{(l)}|=0,~~~\text{for}~~l\geq 5.

Hence, applying Bourgain–Demeter’s decoupling from Section
7
of
[
3
]
we get for
2\leq p\leq\frac{10}{3}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}^{pertp}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{R}{K}})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)(\frac{R}{K})^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tilde{\theta}:K^{1/2}R^{-1/2}~\text{cubes}}\|\mathcal{E}^{pertp}_{\tilde{\theta}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{\frac{R}{K}}})}\Big)

Note that
\mathcal{T}_{7}(B_{R})
can be covered by
B_{\frac{R}{K}}
balls.
Now summing over all
B_{\frac{R}{K}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{7}(B_{R})
and applying the inverse change of variables we deduce the claim (
5
). More details can be found in Lemma
3.1
of
[
12
]
.

3.
Estimate of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{0}}f

This can be proved by similar method as
[
12
]
.
For the sake of self containment we give a sketch of the proof. We employ re-scaling in order to prove the theorem. Using the following change of variables

\xi_{j}=K^{-1/4}\eta_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
, we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{0}}f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}(\vec{\eta})e(\tilde{x}_{1}\eta_{1}+\tilde{x}_{2}\eta_{2}+\tilde{x}_{3}\eta_{3}+\tilde{x}_{4}(\eta^{4}_{1}+\eta^{4}_{2}+\eta^{4}_{3}))d\vec{\eta}\Big|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})|,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{x}=(K^{-1/4}x_{1},K^{-1/4}x_{2},K^{-1/4}x_{3},K^{-1}x_{4}),

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\vec{\eta})=K^{-3/4}f(K^{-1/4}\eta_{1},K^{-1/4}\eta_{2},K^{-1/4}\eta_{3}).

Using re-scaling and the idea of Lemma
3.2
of
[
12
]
we get

\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq D_{p}(\frac{R}{K})\Big(\sum_{\theta\subset\Omega_{0}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
{D}_{p}(R)
denote the smallest constant s.t. inequality (
2
) holds and
\theta\in\mathcal{F}_{4}(R,4)
.

4.
Estimates of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1}}f
,
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{2}}f
and
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{3}}f

We need to deal with
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1}}f
only. The other two operators
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{2}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{3}}
can be handled with similar arguments due to symmetry of the regions
\Omega_{j}
, for
j=1,2,3
.
We decompose the region
\Omega_{1}
into small dyadic pieces

\Omega_{1}:=\bigcup_{\lambda}\Omega_{1,\lambda},~~\Omega_{1,\lambda}=[\lambda,2\lambda]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]\times[0,K^{-1/4}].

Further, we decompose
\Omega_{1,\lambda}
as

\Omega_{1,\lambda}=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq\lambda^{2}K^{1/2}}[\lambda+\frac{j-1}{\lambda K^{1/2}},\lambda+\frac{j}{\lambda K^{1/2}}]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]:=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq\lambda^{2}K^{1/2}}\tau^{j}_{\lambda}.

Now we claim the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
2\leq p\leq 6
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C({\epsilon},p)>0
such that

(7)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\tau_{\lambda}\subset\Omega_{1,\lambda}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{\lambda}}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

In order to prove the above lemma, we freeze two variables
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
. Indeed, we consider a bump function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
with
supp(\varphi)\subset B(0,1)
and
|\check{\varphi}(x)|\geq 1
for all
x\in B(0,1)
. Define
F:=\check{\varphi}_{K^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1}}f
, where
\varphi_{K^{-1}}(y)=K^{4}\varphi(Ky)
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We define
G:=F(\cdot,x_{2},x_{3},\cdot)
. From Lemma
4.1
of
[
9
]
we get that
supp(\hat{G})
is contained in the projection of
supp(\hat{F})
on the plane
\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}=0
, i.e. in the
K^{-1}
neighborhood of the curve
\Gamma_{\lambda}=\{(t,t^{4}):t\in[\lambda,2\lambda]\}
. Therefore, invoking Lemma
3.4
of
[
12
]
(see
[
16
]
for the same lemma) we get,

\|F(\cdot,x_{2},x_{3},\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\|G_{\tau}(\cdot,x_{2},x_{3},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\Big)^{1/2},

for
2\leq p\leq 6
.
Therefore, integrating both sides w.r.t
x_{2},x_{3}
variables we get

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\|F_{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1,\lambda}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{K})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\|F_{\tau}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{\lambda}\subset\Omega_{1,\lambda}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{\lambda}}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{K}})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Further, taking sum over
\lambda
we get

\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{K})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\tau_{\lambda}\subset\Omega_{1,\lambda}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{\lambda}}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{K}})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Now, considering the sum over all balls
B_{K}\subset B_{R}
we get Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Next, we need to estimate
\|\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{\lambda}}f\|_{L^{p}({B_{R}})}
for the typical case of
\tau=[\lambda,\lambda+\lambda^{-1}K^{-1/2}]\times[0,K^{-1/4}]^{2}
.
The estimate for other
\tau
can be deduced by the same arguments. We apply the following change of variables

(8)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}=\lambda+\frac{\eta_{1}}{\lambda K^{1/2}},~\xi_{2}=\frac{\eta_{2}}{K^{1/4}},~\xi_{3}=\frac{\eta_{3}}{K^{1/4}}.

Therefore, we get

|\mathcal{E}_{\tau}f(x)|=\Big|\int_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}(\vec{\eta})e(\tilde{x}_{1}\eta_{1}+\tilde{x}_{2}\eta_{2}+\tilde{x}_{3}\eta_{3}+\tilde{x}_{4}\psi_{1}(\vec{\eta}))d\vec{\eta}\Big|:=\Big|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})\Big|,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{x}=(\lambda^{-1}K^{-1/2}x_{1}+4\lambda^{2}K^{-1/2}x_{4},K^{-1/4}x_{2},K^{-1/4}x_{3},K^{-1}x_{4}),

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\vec{\eta})=\lambda^{-1}K^{-1}f(\lambda+\frac{\eta_{1}}{\lambda K^{1/2}},\frac{\eta_{2}}{K^{1/4}},\frac{\eta_{3}}{K^{1/4}}),

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(\vec{\eta})=(6\eta^{2}_{1}+4\lambda^{-2}K^{-1/2}\eta^{3}_{1}+\lambda^{-4}K^{-1}\eta^{4}_{1})+\eta^{4}_{2}+\eta^{4}_{3}.

Therefore, the extension operator
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{[0,1]^{3}}
is associated with the

hypersurface
\mathcal{S}_{1,4}:=\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\phi_{1}(\xi)+\xi^{4}_{2}+\xi^{4}_{3}):(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\in[0,1]^{3}\}
, with

Using the decomposition (

We claim the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2

[

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{R}{K}})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)(\frac{R}{K})^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tilde{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}_{4}(R/K,1,4)}\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{\theta}}\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{\frac{R}{K}}})}\Big)^{1/2}.

Summing over all balls

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{[0,1]^{3}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{T}(B_{R}))}\leq C({\epsilon},p)(\frac{R}{K})^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tilde{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}_{4}(R/K,1,4)}\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{\theta}}\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{T}(B_{R}))}\Big)^{1/2}.

Taking the inverse change of variable we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau_{\lambda}}{f}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)(\frac{R}{K})^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\subset\tau_{\lambda}}\|{\mathcal{E}}_{{\theta}}{f}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{1/2}.

This proves (
10
). Now plugging (
10

7

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega_{1}}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C({\epsilon},p)K^{\epsilon}(\frac{R}{K})^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\tau_{\lambda}}\sum_{\theta\subset\tau_{\lambda}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle C({\epsilon},p)R^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\subset\Omega_{1}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{1/2},

for

Now it remains to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma

Therefore,

4.1.
Estimation of

Trivially for any

of size

(12)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\leq C({\epsilon},p)(\frac{R}{K})^{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\theta\subset\nu}\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta}f\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}\Big)^{1/2},

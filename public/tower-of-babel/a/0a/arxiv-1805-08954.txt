Abstract
We show how to obtain linear combinations of polynomials in an orthogonal sequence { P n } n ≥ 0 \{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0} , such as Q n , k ​ ( x ) = ∑ i = 0 k a n , i ​ P n − i ​ ( x ) Q_{n,k}(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}a_{n,i}P_{n-i}(x) , a n , 0 ​ a n , k ≠ 0 a_{n,0}a_{n,k}\neq 0 , that characterize quasi-orthogonal polynomials of order k ≤ n − 1 k\leq n-1 . The polynomials in the sequence { Q n , k } n ≥ 0 \{Q_{n,k}\}_{n\geq 0} are obtained from P n P_{n} , by making use of parameter shifts. We use an algorithmic approach to find these linear combinations for each family applicable and these equations are used to prove quasi-orthogonality of order k k . We also determine the location of the extreme zeros of the quasi-orthogonal polynomials with respect to the end points of the interval of orthogonality of the sequence { P n } n ≥ 0 \{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0} , where possible.

Introduction

A sequence of polynomials
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, where each polynomial
P_{n}
has degree
n
, is orthogonal with respect to the weight function
w(x)>0
on the finite (or infinite) interval
[a,b]
if

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}x^{m}P_{n}(x)w(x){\rm d}x=0,\qquad m\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}.

A consequence of orthogonality is that each polynomial
P_{n}(x)
has
n
real, simple zeros in
(a,b)
. In order for orthogonality conditions to hold, we often need restrictions on the parameters of the classical orthogonal polynomials and when the parameters deviate from these restricted values in an orderly way, the zeros may depart from the interval of orthogonality in a predictable way. This phenomenon can be explained in terms of the concept of quasi-orthogonality. The sequence of polynomials
\{Q_{n,k}\}_{n\geq 0}
, where each polynomial
Q_{n,k}
has degree
n
, is quasi-orthogonal of order
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
with respect to the weight function
w(x)>0
on
[a,b]
if

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}x^{m}Q_{n,k}(x)w(x){\rm d}x=0,\qquad m\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-k-1\}.

(1.1)

It is clear that when
k=0
in (
1.1
), the sequence
\{Q_{n,k}\}_{n\geq 0}
is orthogonal with respect to
w(x)
on
[a,b]
.

Quasi-orthogonality was first studied by Riesz
[
25
]
, followed by Fejér
[
12
]
, Shohat
[
26
]
, Chihara
[
4
]
, Dickinson
[
6
]
, Draux
[
7
]
, Maroni
[
22
]
and Joulak
[
17
]
. The quasi-orthogonality of Jacobi, Gegenbauer and Laguerre sequences is discussed in
[
1
]
, the quasi-orthogonality of Meixner sequences in
[
16
]
and of Meixner–Pollaczek, Hahn, dual Hahn and continuous dual Hahn sequences in
[
15
]
. More recently, interlacing of zeros of quasi-orthogonal Meixner, Jacobi, Laguerre and Gegenbauer polynomials were studied in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
and in
[
2
]
interlacing properties of zeros of quasi-orthogonal polynomials were used to prove results on Gaussian-type quadrature.

Quasi-orthogonal polynomials are characterized by the following property:

Lemma 1.1

(
[
1
,
4
]
)
.

Let
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a family of orthogonal polynomials on
[a,b]
with respect to the weight function
w(x)>0
. A necessary and sufficient condition for a polynomial
Q_{n,k}
of degree
n
to be quasi-orthogonal of order
k\leq n-1
with respect to
w
on
[a,b]
, is that

\displaystyle Q_{n,k}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{n,i}P_{n-i}(x),\qquad a_{n,0}a_{n,k}\neq 0,\qquad n>k.

(1.2)

We show at the end of this section how to derive equations of type (
1.2
), where
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a sequence of orthogonal polynomials in the Askey or
q
-Askey scheme
[
18
]
. The polynomials in the Askey scheme are defined in terms of the generalized hypergeometric series

\displaystyle\mbox{${}_{p}F_{q}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[2.84526pt]
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}b_{1},\ldots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\right|\displaystyle{x}\right)$}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{m}\cdots(a_{p})_{m}}{(b_{1})_{m}\cdots(b_{q})_{m}}\frac{x^{m}}{m!},

and
(a)_{m}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol (or shifted
factorial) defined by

\displaystyle(a)_{m}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }m=0,\\
a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+m-1)&\text{if }m\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

The polynomials in the
q
-Askey scheme are defined in terms of the basic hypergeometric series
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
, given by

\displaystyle\mbox{${}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[2.84526pt]
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\right|\displaystyle{q;z}\right)$}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{m}}{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{m}}\big((-1)^{m}q^{\binom{m}{2}}\big)^{1+s-r}\frac{z^{m}}{(q;q)_{m}},

where the
q
-Pochhammer symbol
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{m}:=(a_{1};q)_{m}\cdots(a_{r};q)_{m},

with

\displaystyle(a_{i};q)_{m}=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{m-1}\big(1-a_{i}q^{j}\big)&\text{if}\ m\in\{1,2,3,\ldots\},\\
1&\text{if}\ m=0.\end{cases}

We will discuss the quasi-orthogonality of monic polynomial systems on a
q
-linear lattice in Section

, the quasi-orthogonality of the monic Askey–Wilson and

-Racah polynomials, that lie on a

-quadratic lattice, in Section

4

we will make some concluding remarks. Throughout this paper,

,

, denote the zeros of a polynomial of degree

in the following order:

. The following results will be referred to in this paper and we state them here for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
1
,
26
]
)
.

If
Q_{n,k}
is quasi-orthogonal of order
k\geq 1
on
[a,b]
with respect to

, then at least
(n-k)
real, distinct zeros of

lie in the interval
(a,b)
.

Lemma 1.3

(
[
1
,
17
]
)
.

Suppose
Q_{n,1}(x)=P_{n}(x)+a_{n}P_{n-1}(x)
,
a_{n}\neq 0
. Let
y_{n,j}
,
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, be the zeros of
Q_{n,1}(x)
and let
f_{n}(x)=\frac{P_{n}(x)}{P_{n-1}(x)}
. We have

(i)

y_{n,1}<a
if and only if
-a_{n}<f_{n}(a)<0
;

(ii)

b<y_{n,n}
if and only if
-a_{n}>f_{n}(b)>0
;

(iii)

Q_{n,1}
has all its zeros in
(a,b)
if and only if
f_{n}(a)<-a_{n}<f_{n}(b)
.

Lemma 1.4

(
[
1
,
17
]
)
.

Suppose
Q_{n,1}(x)=P_{n}(x)+a_{n}P_{n-1}(x)
,
a_{n}\neq 0
. Let
x_{n,j}
,
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, denote the zeros of
P_{n}(x)
and
y_{n,j}
,
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, the zeros of
Q_{n,1}(x)
. Then

(i)

a_{n}<0
if and only if
x_{n,1}<y_{n,1}<x_{n-1,1}<x_{n,2}<y_{n,2}<\dots<x_{n-1,n-1}<x_{n,n}<y_{n,n}
;

(ii)

a_{n}>0
if and only if
y_{n,1}<x_{n,1}<x_{n-1,1}<y_{n,2}<x_{n,2}<\dots<x_{n-1,n-1}<y_{n,n}<x_{n,n}
.

In order to find the equations of type (
1.2
), that are needed to prove quasi-orthogonality, we consider
the structure relation (cf.
[
13
,
20
,
23
]
)

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=a_{n}DP_{n+1}(x)+b_{n}DP_{n}(x)+c_{n}DP_{n-1}(x),

(1.3)

where the constants
a_{n}
,
b_{n}
and

are explicitly given and

is a derivative or difference operator. Most of the classical orthogonal polynomials we consider in this paper (see
[
18

) satisfy

\displaystyle DP_{n}(x)=S(n)P_{n-1,k}(x),\qquad k\in\{-1,0,1,2\},

(1.4)

where

does not depend on
x
and

denotes the polynomial obtained when each of the parameters on which the polynomial

depends, can be shifted by
k

q
-classical systems, when the parameters can each be multiplied by

. If we substitute (
1.4
) in (
1.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=a_{n}S(n+1)P_{n,k}(x)+b_{n}S(n)P_{n-1,k}(x)+c_{n}S(n-1)P_{n-2,k}(x)

or, by making a parameter shift,

\displaystyle P_{n,-k}(x)=a_{n}^{\prime}S^{\prime}(n+1)P_{n}(x)+b_{n}^{\prime}S^{\prime}(n)P_{n-1}(x)+c_{n}^{\prime}S^{\prime}(n-1)P_{n-2}(x),

where
a_{n}^{\prime}
,
b_{n}^{\prime}
,

and

are the values of the coefficients taking into consideration the parameter shift, and we have a linear combination of polynomials in an orthogonal sequence as in (
1.2
). For the so-called very-classical orthogonal polynomials, the general expression for the parameters
a_{n,i}
,

, in

\displaystyle Q_{n,2}(x)=P_{n}(x)+a_{n,1}P_{n-1}(x)+a_{n,2}P_{n-2}(x)

were given in
[
21
, equation (76)]
in terms of the coefficients of the differential equations they satisfy.

We can also apply the operator
D
to (
1.3
) to obtain

\displaystyle DP_{n}(x)=a_{n}D^{2}P_{n+1}(x)+b_{n}D^{2}P_{n}(x)+c_{n}D^{2}P_{n-1}(x).

(1.5)

Replacing (
1.5
) in (
1.3
) and using (
1.4
) twice, yields

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=a_{n}a_{n+1}S(n+2)S(n+1)P_{n,2k}(x)+a_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n+1})S(n+1)S(n)P_{n-1,2k}(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{P_n(x)=}{}+\big(a_{n}c_{n+1}+a_{n-1}c_{n}+b_{n}^{2}\big)S(n)S(n-1)P_{n-2,2k}(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{P_n(x)=}{}+c_{n}(b_{n}+b_{n-1})S(n-1)S(n-2)P_{n-3,2k}+c_{n}c_{n-1}S(n-2)S(n-3)P_{n-4,2k}(x).

By making a parameter shift again, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{n,-2k}(x)=a_{n}^{\prime}a_{n+1}^{\prime}S^{\prime}(n+2)S^{\prime}(n+1)P_{n}(x)+a_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}^{\prime}+b_{n+1}^{\prime})S^{\prime}(n+1)S^{\prime}(n)P_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{P_{n,-2k}(x)=}{}+\big(a_{n}^{\prime}c_{n+1}^{\prime}+a_{n-1}^{\prime}c_{n}^{\prime}+(b_{n}^{\prime})^{2}\big)S^{\prime}(n)S^{\prime}(n-1)P_{n-2}(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{P_{n,-2k}(x)=}{}+c_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}^{\prime}+b_{n-1}^{\prime})S^{\prime}(n-1)S^{\prime}(n-2)P_{n-3}+c_{n}^{\prime}c_{n-1}^{\prime}S^{\prime}(n-2)S^{\prime}(n-3)P_{n-4}(x).

These induction arguments show that equations of type (
1.2
) are structurally valid. We also refer the reader to
[
13
]
, where we have so-called connection formulae for the classical orthogonal polynomial of the

4
Quasi-orthogonality of polynomials

on a linear and quadratic lattice

In this section we consider the quasi-orthogonality of the monic Wilson and Racah polynomials, that are defined on a quadratic lattice. The quasi-orthogonality of the dual Hahn and continuous dual Hahn polynomials, that also fall in this category, was discussed in
[
15
]
. We also prove the quasi-orthogonality of the monic continuous Hahn polynomials that are defined on a linear lattice.

4.1
The Wilson polynomials

The Wilson polynomials

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{n}\big(x^{2};a,b,c,d\big)=\frac{(-1)^{n}(a+b,a+c,a+d)_{n}}{(n+a+b+c+d-1)_{n}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{W}_n\big(x^2;a,b,c,d\big)=}{}\times\mbox{${}_{4}F_{3}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}-n,n+a+b+c+d-1,a+ix,a-ix\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[2.84526pt]
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}a+b,a+c,a+d\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\right|\displaystyle{1}\right)$},

are orthogonal on
(0,\infty)
with respect to

\displaystyle w(x;a,b,c,d)=\left|\frac{\Gamma(a+ix)\Gamma(b+ix)\Gamma(c+ix)\Gamma(d+ix)}{\Gamma(2ix)}\right|^{2},

(4.1)

for
\operatorname{Re}(a,b,c,d)>0

a
,

,

and

4.2
The Racah polynomials

The Racah polynomials

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{n}(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=\frac{(\alpha+1,\beta+\delta+1,\gamma+1)_{n}}{(n+\alpha+\beta+1)_{n}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{R}_n(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=}{}\times\mbox{${}_{4}F_{3}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}-n,n+\alpha+\beta+1,-x,x+\gamma+\delta+1\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\\[2.84526pt]
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}\alpha+1,\beta+\delta+1,\gamma+1\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\right|\displaystyle{1}\right)$},

n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,N\}
, with
\lambda(x)=x(x+\gamma+\delta+1)
, are orthogonal on
(0,N)
with respect to the weight function

\displaystyle w(x)=\frac{(\alpha+1)_{x}(\beta+\delta+1)_{x}(\gamma+1)_{x}(\gamma+\delta+1)_{x}((\gamma+\delta+3)/2)_{x}}{(-\alpha+\gamma+\delta+1)_{x}(-b+\gamma+1)_{x}(\delta+1)_{x}((\gamma+\delta+1)/2)_{x}}

if
\alpha+1=-N
or
\beta+\delta+1=-N
or
\gamma+1=-N

N
a nonnegative integer. Since shifting

\delta

\lambda(x)

\alpha

Proposition 4.4
.

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{n}(\lambda(x);\alpha-1,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=\tilde{R}_{n}(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{R}_n(\lambda(x);\alpha-1,\beta,\gamma,\delta) =}{}-{\frac{\left(\beta+n\right)\left(\beta+\delta+n\right)\left(\gamma+n\right)n}{\left(2n+\alpha+\beta\right)\left(2n+\alpha+\beta-1\right)}}\tilde{R}_{n-1}(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta);

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{n}(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta-1,\gamma,\delta)=\tilde{R}_{n}(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{R}_n(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta-1,\gamma,\delta) =}{}-{\frac{\left(\alpha+n\right)\left(\alpha-\delta+n\right)\left(\gamma+n\right)n}{\left(2n+\alpha+\beta\right)\left(2n+\alpha+\beta-1\right)}}\tilde{R}_{n-1}(\lambda(x);\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta).

(4.3b)

4.3

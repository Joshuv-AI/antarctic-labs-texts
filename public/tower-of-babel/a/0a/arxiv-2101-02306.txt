Introduction

In this paper we present an algorithm for the construction of a general rational inner function from
\mathbb{D}
to the tetrablock. The algorithm is based on a known solution of the Nevanlinna-Pick interpolation problem on
\mathbb{D}
. Different versions of the Nevanlinna-Pick interpolation problem have been studied by many authors, beginning with G. Pick in 1916
[
29
]
and continuing
with R. Nevanlinna in 1922
[
28
]

[
7
]
for some references.
We should mention particularly papers of J. A. Ball and J. W. Helton
[
8
]
,
D. Sarason
[

, D. R. Georgijević
[
20
]
, G.-N. Chen and Y.-J. Hu
[

and V. Bolotnikov and A. Kheifets
[

, and the books of J. A. Ball, I. C. Gohberg and L. Rodman
[

]
, and of V. Bolotnikov and H. Dym
[

]
. There are many further papers on this interesting topic and applications (see, for example,
[

,

]
).

The closed tetrablock
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is the set in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
defined by

\overline{\mathcal{E}}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:\ 1-x_{1}z-x_{2}w+x_{3}zw\neq 0\ \text{whenever}\ |z|<1,|w|<1\}.

The original motivation for the study of
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
was an attempt to solve a
\mu
-synthesis problem
[
1
]
, which is itself motivated by basic unsolved problems in
H^{\infty}
control theory
[
21
,
22
]
. The tetrablock has attracted considerable interest in recent years. It has interesting complex geometry
[
1
,
23
,
34
,
25
,
26
]
, rich function theory
[
16
,
5
,
6
]
and associated operator theory
[
11
]
.
The solvability of the
\mu
-synthesis problem connected to
\mathcal{E}
can be expressed in terms of the existence of rational inner functions from the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
to the closure of
\mathcal{E}

[
16
]
.

Observe that if
x:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is analytic then, by Fatou’s Theorem, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
with respect to Lebesgue measure, the radial limit

\lim_{r\uparrow 1}x(r\lambda)

exists. We say that an analytic map
x:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is a
tetra-inner function
, or alternatively, an
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner function
if, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
with respect to Lebesgue measure,

\lim_{r\uparrow 1}x(r\lambda)

lies in the distinguished boundary
b\overline{\mathcal{E}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
. The distinguished boundary
b\overline{\mathcal{E}}
of

\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is homeomorphic to the solid torus
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\times\mathbb{T}
, which has a boundary
[
1
]
. The

\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner functions constitute a natural analogue (in the context of the tetrablock) of the
inner functions
introduced by A. Beurling
[
10
]
, which play an important role in the function theory of the unit disc and in operator theory
[
17
]
.

A basic question about rational inner functions
\varphi
from
\mathbb{D}
to
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
was studied by W. Blaschke
[
12
]
. Specifically, he obtained (inter alia) a formula for the general rational inner function
\varphi
of degree
n
in terms of its zeros. Indeed, by the Argument Principle, any rational inner function
\varphi
of degree
n
has exactly
n
zeros in
\mathbb{D}
, counted with multiplicity. From this fact one can see that
\varphi
is a “finite Blaschke product”, having the form

\varphi(\lambda)=c{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}\frac{\lambda-\alpha_{j}}{1-\overline{\alpha_{j}}\lambda}\

(1.1)

for some unimodular constant
c
and some
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. The
\alpha_{j}
are the zeros of
\varphi
. It is evident from equation (
1.1
) that
\varphi
extends to a continuously differentiable function on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, given by the same formula.
In this paper our aim is to write down a formula analogous to equation (
1.1
) for the general rational
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner function of degree
n
. The first question that arises is: what data should replace the
\alpha_{j}
, the zeros of
\varphi
? We have found that an effective choice is the set of
royal nodes
of the tetra-inner function, which we shall now define.
It was shown in
[
5
]
that if
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is a rational
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner function of degree
n
then
x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2}

either is identically
0
or has exactly
n
zeros in the closed unit disc
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, counted with an appropriate notion of multiplicity.
Here, the
degree
of a rational
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner function
x
is defined to be the topological degree of the restriction of
x
that maps
\mathbb{T}
continuously to
b\overline{\mathcal{E}}
. Since
b\overline{\mathcal{E}}
is homeomorphic to the solid torus
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\times\mathbb{T}
, which is homotopic to
\mathbb{T}
, the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(b\overline{\mathcal{E}})
is
\mathbb{Z}
, and so the degree of
x
is an integer; it will be denoted by
\deg(x)
.

The variety

\mathcal{R}_{\overline{\mathcal{E}}}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}:x_{3}=x_{1}x_{2}\}

has an important role in the function theory of
{\mathcal{{E}}}
; it is called the
royal variety of
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
. For any rational tetra-inner function
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, the zeros of
x_{1}x_{2}-x_{3}
in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are the points
\lambda\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}
such that
x(\lambda)\in\mathcal{R}_{\bar{\mathcal{E}}}
. We call these points the
royal nodes of
x
. If
\sigma\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is a royal node of
x
, so that
x(\sigma)=(\eta,\tilde{\eta},\eta\tilde{\eta})
for some
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then we call
\eta,\tilde{\eta}
the
royal values of
x
corresponding to the royal node
\sigma
of
x
. In this paper, in Theorem
1.7
, we give a prescription for the construction of a general
rational tetra-inner function of degree
n
in terms of its royal nodes and royal values. We shall make use of a known solution of an interpolation problem for finite Blaschke products.

The
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner functions
x
such that
x(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\overline{\mathcal{E}}}
are simply the functions of the form
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})
where
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are inner functions. These
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
-inner functions behave differently from the others, and we shall often specifically exclude them from consideration.

To describe our main results on the construction of a general rational tetra-inner function we need to recall some definitions and results on the Blaschke interpolation problem.

Definition 1.2
.

[
2

For any differentiable function

the phasar derivative of

Thus, if

(1.2)

We shall find it useful in the sequel.

Problem 1.3
.

(The Blaschke interpolation problem)

For given Blaschke interpolation data
(\sigma,\eta,\rho)
of type
(n,k)
, find all rational inner functions
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}
of degree
n
with the properties

\varphi(\sigma_{j})=\eta_{j}\quad\text{ for}\ j=1,...,n

(1.3)

and

A\varphi(\sigma_{j})=\rho_{j}\quad\text{for}\ j=1,...,k.

(1.4)

Problem
1.3
has been analysed by several authors
[
31
,
32
,
8
]
. In the absence of the tangential conditions
(
1.4
) the problem would be ill-posed, in that the solvability of the problem would depend only on the interpolation conditions at nodes in
\mathbb{D}
, and the conditions at
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}
would be irrelevant. With the conditions (
1.4
), however, the problem has a pleasing solution.
The existence of a solution of the Blaschke interpolation problem can be characterized in terms of an associated “Pick matrix”, and all solutions
\varphi
are parametrized by a linear fractional expression in terms of a parameter
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. There are polynomials
a,b,c
and
d
of degree at most
n
such that the general solution of Problem
1.3
is

\varphi=\frac{a\zeta+b}{c\zeta+d}

where the parameter
\zeta
ranges over a cofinite subset of
\mathbb{T}
.
The polynomials
a,b,c
and
d
are unique subject to a certain normalization.

Definition 1.4
.

[
3
, Definition 3.10]

Let
(\sigma,\eta,\rho)
be Blaschke interpolation data of type
(n,k)
. Suppose that Problem

is solvable. We say that

is a normalized linear fractional parametrization of the solutions of Problem

if

(i)

a,b,c,d
are polynomials of degree at most
n
;

(ii)

for all but at most
k
values of
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
, the function

\varphi(\lambda)=\frac{a(\lambda)\zeta+b(\lambda)}{c(\lambda)\zeta+d(\lambda)}

(1.5)

is a solution of Problem
1.3
;

(iii)

for some point
\tau\in\mathbb{T}\ \backslash\{\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{k}\}
,

\begin{bmatrix}a(\tau)&b(\tau)\\
c(\tau)&d(\tau)\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix};

(iv)

every solution
\varphi
of Problem
1.3
has the form (
1.5
) for some
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
.

The data for the construction of a rational tetra-inner function

Lemma 2.4

Let

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\omega}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|=1

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}(1-|x_{2}(e^{i\theta})|^{2})_{|\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}(\overline{x_{3}(e^{i\theta})}(x_{3}(e^{i\theta})-x_{1}(e^{i\theta})x_{2}(e^{i\theta}))_{|\xi}

\displaystyle\overline{x_{3}(e^{i\xi})}\bigg[ie^{i\xi}x_{3}^{\prime}(e^{i\xi})-(x_{1}(e^{i\xi})ie^{i\xi}x_{2}^{\prime}(e^{i\xi})+ie^{i\xi}x_{1}^{\prime}(e^{i\xi})x_{2}(e^{i\xi}))\bigg].

4.
Criteria for the solvability of the Blaschke interpolation problem

In this section, for the convenience of the reader, we collect some known facts about finite Blaschke products that we need. They may be found in several places, but the most economical source for our purposes is
[
3
]
, which assembles precisely the results which we require.

As mentioned in the Introduction, there is an extensive literature on boundary interpolation problems. A very valuable source of information about all manner of complex
interpolation problems is the book of Ball, Gohberg and Rodman
[
9
]
.
The authors of
[
8
,
32
,
15
,
9
,
14
]
make use of Krein spaces, moment theory, measure theory, reproducing kernel theory, realization theory and de Branges space theory. They obtain far-reaching results, including generalizations to matrix-valued functions and to functions allowed to have a limited number of poles in a disc or half-plane. See also papers of
[
13
,
27
,
30
]
for elementary treatments of interpolation problems.
The monograph
[
14
]
by Bolotnikov and Dym is entirely devoted to
boundary interpolation problems for the Schur class. They reformulate the problem within the framework of the Ukrainian school’s Abstract Interpolation Problem
and solve it by means of operator theory in de Branges-Rovnyak spaces.

The Blaschke interpolation Problem
1.3
as described in
[
3
]
is an algebraic variant of the classical Pick interpolation problem.
One looks for a Blaschke product of degree
n
satisfying
n
interpolation conditions, rather than a Schur-class function as in the original Pick interpolation problem. We admit interpolation nodes both in the open unit disc and on the unit circle. There is a criterion for the solvability of the Blaschke interpolation problem in terms of the positivity of a “Pick matrix” formed from the interpolation data. To obtain a well-posed problem one imposes additional interpolation conditions, on phasar derivatives at the interpolation nodes on the unit circle. These bounds on the phasar derivatives appear as the diagonal entries of the Pick matrix.

Definition 4.1
.

The Schur class is the set of analytic functions
S
from
\mathbb{D}\ to\ \bar{\mathbb{D}}
,
S:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{D}}
.

Definition 4.2
.

A function
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is inner if it is an analytic map such that the radial limit

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}f(r\lambda)\ \mbox{exists and belongs to }\mathbb{T}

for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Definition 4.3
.

The Pick matrix associated with Blaschke interpolation data
(\sigma,\eta,\rho)
of type
(n,k)
is the
n\times n
matrix
M=[m_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
where

m_{ij}=\begin{cases}\rho_{i}&\text{if $i=j\leq k$}.\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1-\overline{\eta_{i}}\eta_{j}}{1-\overline{\sigma_{i}}\sigma_{j}}}&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 4.4
.

The Pick matrix
M=[m_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
is minimally positive if
M\geq 0
and there is no strictly positive diagonal
n\times n
matrix
D
such that
M\geq D
.

The following is a refinement of the Sarason Interpolation Theorem
[
32
]
.

.

[

, Theorem 3.3]

(i)

6.

In this section we will prove Theorem

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
a,b,c,d,x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ}\in\mathbb{C}
, and suppose that
|x_{3}^{\circ}|=1,x_{1}^{\circ}=\overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}x_{3}^{\circ},x_{1}^{\circ}c\neq-d
and
|x_{1}^{\circ}|<1,|x_{2}^{\circ}|<1
. Let

x_{2}=\frac{x_{3}^{\circ}c+x_{2}^{\circ}d}{x_{1}^{\circ}c+d}.

Then

(1)\ \ |x_{2}|\leq 1\ \text{ if and only if}\ |c|\leq|d|,

and

(2)\ \ |x_{2}|<1\ \text{ if and only if}\ |c|<|d|.

Proof.

(1)

\displaystyle|x_{2}|\leq 1\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\Bigg|\frac{x_{3}^{\circ}c+x_{2}^{\circ}d}{x_{1}^{\circ}c+d}\Bigg|}\leq 1

(6.1)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle|x_{3}^{\circ}c+x_{2}^{\circ}d|^{2}\leq|x_{1}^{\circ}c+d|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle|c|^{2}+2\text{Re}(x_{3}^{\circ}\ c\ \overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}\ \overline{d})+|x_{2}^{\circ}|^{2}|d|^{2}\leq|x_{1}^{\circ}|^{2}|c|^{2}+2\text{Re}(x_{1}^{\circ}\ c\ \overline{d})+|d|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle|c|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}|d|^{2}-|x_{1}^{\circ}|^{2}|c|^{2}-|d|^{2}\leq 0\ \ (\text{since}\ x_{1}^{\circ}=\overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}x_{3}^{\circ})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(1-|x_{1}^{\circ}|^{2})(|c|^{2}-|d|^{2})\leq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle|c|\leq|d|\quad(\text{since}\ (1-|x_{1}^{\circ}|^{2})>0).

(2) The same calculation leads to
|x_{2}|<1\ \iff\ |c|<|d|
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let a, b, c, d be polynomials in the variable

Let rational functions

(6.2)

and define a rational function

in the indeterminate

Then, as rational functions in

Let

be defined by equations (

Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{\omega x_{3}(\lambda)-x_{1}(\lambda)}{x_{2}(\lambda)\omega-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle{\frac{\omega x_{2}^{\circ}b(\lambda)+\omega x_{3}^{\circ}a(\lambda)}{x_{1}^{\circ}c(\lambda)+d(\lambda)}-\displaystyle{\frac{x_{1}^{\circ}a(\lambda)+b(\lambda)}{x_{1}^{\circ}c(\lambda)+d(\lambda)}}}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\omega x_{3}^{\circ}c(\lambda)+\omega x_{2}^{\circ}d(\lambda)}{x_{1}^{\circ}c(\lambda)+d(\lambda)}}-1}

\displaystyle\frac{\omega x_{2}^{\circ}b(\lambda)+\omega x_{3}^{\circ}a(\lambda)-x_{1}^{\circ}a(\lambda)-b(\lambda)}{\omega x_{3}^{\circ}c(\lambda)+\omega x_{2}^{\circ}d(\lambda)-x_{1}^{\circ}c(\lambda)-d(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\frac{a(\lambda)\zeta(\omega)+b(\lambda)}{c(\lambda)\zeta(\omega)+d(\lambda)},

7.
The algorithm

In this section we present a concrete algorithm for the solution of the royal
\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}
-interpolation problem.

Let
(\sigma,\eta,\tilde{\eta},\rho)
be royal interpolation data of type
(n,k)
for the tetrablock, as in Definition
1.5
. One can consider the associated Blaschke interpolation data
(\sigma,\eta,\rho)
of type
(n,k)
. To construct a rational
\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}
-inner function
x:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}
of degree
n
having royal nodes
\sigma_{j}
for
j=1,...,n
, royal values
\eta_{j},\tilde{\eta_{j}}
, and phasar derivatives
\rho_{j}
at
\sigma_{j}
for
j=1,...,k
, we proceed as follows.

(1) Consider the Pick matrix
M=[m_{i,j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
for the data
(\sigma,\eta,\rho)
. It has entries

m_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\rho_{i}&\quad\quad\text{if }i=j\leq k\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1-\overline{\eta_{i}}\eta_{j}}{1-\overline{\sigma_{i}}\sigma_{j}}}&\quad\quad\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

(7.1)

Assume that
M
is positive definite; otherwise the interpolation problem
1.3
is not solvable. Introduce the notation

x_{\lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{1-\overline{\sigma}_{1}\lambda}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{1}{1-\overline{\sigma}_{n}\lambda}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad\ \ \ y_{\lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\overline{\eta}_{1}}{1-\overline{\sigma}_{1}\lambda}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\overline{\eta}_{n}}{1-\overline{\sigma}_{n}\lambda}\end{bmatrix},

(7.2)

as in equations (
4.8
).

(2) Choose a point
\tau\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},...,\sigma_{k}\}
such that the set of
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
for which

\langle M^{-1}x_{\tau},e_{j}\rangle=\zeta\langle M^{-1}y_{\tau},e_{j}\rangle\quad\text{for some}\ j\in\{1,...,n\}

is finite. Here
e_{j}
is the
j
th standard basis vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(3) Let

g(\lambda)={\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}\frac{1-\overline{\sigma_{j}}\lambda}{1-\overline{\sigma_{j}}\tau}\ ,

(7.3)

and let polynomials
a,b,c,d
be defined by

\displaystyle a(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\lambda)(1-(1-\overline{\tau}\lambda)\langle x_{\lambda},M^{-1}x_{\tau}\rangle),

(7.4)

\displaystyle b(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\lambda)(1-\overline{\tau}\lambda)\langle x_{\lambda},M^{-1}y_{\tau}\rangle,

(7.5)

\displaystyle c(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\lambda)(1-\overline{\tau}\lambda)\langle y_{\lambda},M^{-1}x_{\tau}\rangle

(7.6)

\displaystyle d(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\lambda)(1+(1-\overline{\tau}\lambda)\langle y_{\lambda},M^{-1}y_{\tau}\rangle).

(7.7)

Observe that

\begin{bmatrix}a(\tau)&b(\tau)\\
c(\tau)&d(\tau)\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(7.8)

(See Theorem 3.9 in
[
3
]
).

(4) Find
x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

|x_{3}^{\circ}|=1,\ \ \ |x_{1}^{\circ}|<1,\ \ \ |x_{2}^{\circ}|<1,\ \ \ x_{1}^{\circ}=\overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}x_{3}^{\circ},

and

\frac{x_{3}^{\circ}c(\sigma_{j})+x_{2}^{\circ}d(\sigma_{j})}{x_{1}^{\circ}c(\sigma_{j})+d(\sigma_{j})}=\tilde{\eta_{j}},\ j=1,...,n.

If there is no
(x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ})
satisfying these conditions, then by Theorem
6.4
, the royal
\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}
-interpolation problem is not solvable.

(5) If there are such
(x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ})\in\mathbb{C}
, we define

\displaystyle x_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{\circ}a+b}{x_{1}^{\circ}c+d}(\lambda),

\displaystyle x_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{3}^{\circ}c+x_{2}^{\circ}d}{x_{1}^{\circ}c+d}(\lambda),

\displaystyle x_{3}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}^{\circ}b+x_{3}^{\circ}a}{x_{1}^{\circ}c+d}(\lambda),\ \text{for}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

It is easy to see that, since the equation (
7.8
) is satisfied,

x_{1}(\tau)=x_{1}^{\circ},\ x_{2}(\tau)=x_{2}^{\circ}\ \text{and}\ x_{3}(\tau)=x_{3}^{\circ}.

Then, by Theorem
6.4
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is a rational
\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}
-inner function of degree at most
n
such that
x(\sigma_{j})=(\eta_{j},\tilde{\eta}_{j},\eta_{j}\tilde{\eta}_{j})
for
j=1,...,n
, and
Ax_{1}(\sigma_{j})=\rho_{j}
for
j=1,...,k
. By assumption,
|x_{3}^{\circ}|=1,|x_{1}^{\circ}|<1
and
|x_{2}^{\circ}|<1
, and hence
x_{3}(\tau)\neq x_{1}(\tau)x_{2}(\tau)
. Hence,
x(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
is not in the royal variety of the tetrablock, and the degree of

is exactly
n
.

Now let us relate the steps of algorithm to results earlier in the paper.

(1)

If the royal
\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}
-interpolation problem with data
(\sigma,\eta,\tilde{\eta},\rho)
for some
\tilde{\eta_{j}}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is solvable, then by Theorem
5.1
, the Blaschke interpolation problem with data
(\sigma,\eta,\rho)
is solvable. By
[
3

,
M>0
.

(2)

The conditions that

are equivalent to
(x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ})\in b\overline{\mathcal{{E}}}

(3)

The equations for
x_{1},x_{2}
and

are equations (

), (
6.16
) and (
6.17
) respectively.

(8.4)

\displaystyle 1(1+(1-\overline{\tau}\lambda)\frac{\eta\overline{\eta}}{1-|\eta|^{2}})=\frac{1-|\eta|^{2}\overline{\tau}\lambda}{1-|\eta|^{2}}.

Step 2
. The next step is to determine whether there exist
x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

|x_{3}^{\circ}|=1,\ \ \ |x_{1}^{\circ}|<1,\ \ \ |x_{2}^{\circ}|<1,\ \ x_{1}^{\circ}=\overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}x_{3}^{\circ},

(8.5)

and

\frac{x_{3}^{\circ}c(0)+x_{2}^{\circ}d(0)}{x_{1}^{\circ}c(0)+d(0)}=\tilde{\eta}.

(8.6)

Here,

a(0)=\displaystyle{\frac{-|\eta|^{2}}{1-|\eta|^{2}}},\quad b(0)=\displaystyle{\frac{\eta}{1-|\eta|^{2}}},\quad c(0)=\displaystyle{\frac{-\overline{\eta}}{1-|\eta|^{2}}},\quad d(0)=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{1-|\eta|^{2}}}.

Let
x_{3}^{\circ}=\omega
for
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{x_{3}^{\circ}c(0)+x_{2}^{\circ}d(0)}{x_{1}^{\circ}c(0)+d(0)}=\tilde{\eta}\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle x_{2}^{\circ}=-x_{1}^{\circ}\overline{\eta}\tilde{\eta}+\tilde{\eta}+\omega\overline{\eta}.

Since
x_{1}^{\circ}=\overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}x_{3}^{\circ}
and
x_{3}^{\circ}=\omega
, we have the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{3}^{\circ}&=&\omega\\
x_{1}^{\circ}&=&\overline{x_{2}^{\circ}}\omega\\
x_{2}^{\circ}&=&-x_{1}^{\circ}\overline{\eta}\tilde{\eta}+\tilde{\eta}+\omega\overline{\eta}.\end{array}\right.

(8.7)

For given
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in\mathbb{D}
, we want to find a solution
x_{1}^{\circ},x_{2}^{\circ},x_{3}^{\circ}
of the above system such that
x_{3}^{\circ}\in\mathbb{T}
,
|x_{1}^{\circ}|<1
, and
|x_{2}^{\circ}|<1
.
This is equivalent to finding
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
and
|x_{1}^{\circ}|<1
, such that the equation

\omega\overline{\tilde{\eta}}+\eta=x_{1}^{\circ}+\overline{x_{1}^{\circ}}\omega\overline{\tilde{\eta}}\eta

(8.8)

holds.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in\mathbb{D}
. There are
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
and
|x_{1}^{\circ}|<1
such that equation (
8.8
) holds.

Proof.

Choose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
.
Let
\xi=\omega\overline{\tilde{\eta}}
, and so
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
.
Let
s=\xi+\eta
and
p=\xi\eta
; then
(s,p)
is a point of the symmetrized bidisc
G=\{(z_{1}+z_{2},z_{1}z_{2}):|z_{1}|<1,|z_{2}|<1\}
. One can see that

\beta=\frac{s-\bar{s}p}{1-|p|^{2}}

satisfies the equation
s=\beta+\bar{\beta}p
and the inequality
|\beta|<1
. Hence,
for
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
and for
x_{1}^{\circ}=\beta
,
equation (
8.8
) holds.
∎

(8.9)

Step 1.

,

\displaystyle=

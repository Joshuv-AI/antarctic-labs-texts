Abstract
. Let K K be an algebraically closed field and M ⁡ ( 2 , K ) \mathrm{M}(2,K) be the 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrix algebra over K K and GL ⁡ ( 2 , K ) \mathrm{GL}(2,K) be the invertible elements in M ⁡ ( 2 , K ) \mathrm{M}(2,K) . We explore the image of polynomials with constants, namely from the free algebra M ⁡ ( 2 , K ) ​ ⟨ x , y ⟩ \mathrm{M}(2,K)\langle x,y\rangle . In this article, we compute the images of the polynomial maps given by (a) generalized sum of powers A ​ x k 1 + B ​ y k 2 Ax^{k_{1}}+By^{k_{2}} and (b) generalized commutator map A ​ x ​ y − B ​ y ​ x Axy-Byx , where A A , B B are non-zero elements of M ⁡ ( 2 , K ) \mathrm{M}(2,K) . We compute this in the first case by fixing a simultaneous conjugate pair for A , B A,B and it turns out that it is surjective in most of the cases. In the second case, we show that the image of the map is always a vector space.

1.
Introduction

Polynomial maps on algebras

For a given polynomial, finding a solution in an algebra has been a driving force behind much research since antiquity.
Be it algebraic geometry, the Diophantine equations or the Waring-type
problem, the common link among them is finding a solution to a polynomial
equation, equivalently
finding the image of the given polynomial. For a
K
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and an element
w\in\mathscr{F}_{n}=K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
, the
free algebra of rank
n
, we get a natural map

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}\colon\mathscr{A}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{A},

by substitution. These are known as
polynomial maps
.
The motivation behind studying the polynomial maps in algebra comes from the famous Kaplansky-Lv́ov conjecture (see
[
9
]
) (which asserts that the image of a multilinear map on the full matrix algebra
over a field is a vector space) and the expectation of
results in simple algebra that are parallel to the results in simple groups. The celebrated result of
Larsen, Shalev and Tiep
(see
[
14
]
)
states that for a non-trivial
w\in\mathscr{F}_{d}
, the free group on

d
generators, there exists a constant
N=N_{w}
such that
for all finite non-abelian simple groups
G
of order greater than
N
, we have

w(G)^{2}=G
. Larsen suggested an analogous result for
\mathrm{M}(n,q)
. It was proved that for sufficiently
large
q
, every matrix in
\mathrm{M}(n,q)
can be written as a sum of two
K
-th powers, in

[
12
]
and
[
13
]
. This is equivalent to stating that
the map
x^{k}+y^{k}
is surjective on
\mathrm{M}(n,q)
for sufficiently large
q
.
This was further generalized in
[
18
]
, for the map
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{k_{i}}
, for
m\geq 2
and the rings
\mathrm{M}(n,D)
with
D=\mathbb{C},\mathbb{F}_{q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
. This is commonly known as
Waring-type problem
which asks if
the elements of the subalgebra generated by the image
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathscr{A}^{m})
can be written as a sum of
\ell
many elements of

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathscr{A}^{m})
, where
\ell
is a fixed positive integer. Let us list out some references where other algebras have been considered, the case
central simple algebras in
[
19
]
, the case of
\mathrm{M}(n,R)
with
R
being a commutative ring in
[
10
]
, the case of
\mathrm{M}(n,\mathbb{Z})
in
[
15
]
, the upper triangular matrix algebra in

[
11
]
.
Other instances of Waring-type problems occur in the works
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
.
The Kaplansky-Lv́ov conjecture has been quite a driving force to a tremendous amount of research in recent times. While it
remains open for most of the cases, some progress has been made for the case of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
in

[
8
]
, the case of
\mathrm{M}(3,K)
in
[
7
]
. We remark that the last two articles do not
solve the problem for a general field, but do it with mild restrictions on the field. A parallel
result holds in the case of upper triangular matrix algebras, which is known as Fagundes-Mello
conjecture, and was solved in
[
4
]
an
[
16
]
independently. A more generalized
result has been proved in
[
17
]
. For a survey, we suggest the reader look into
[
6
]
and the references therein.

Polynomial maps with constants on algebras

Let
K
be a field,
\mathscr{F}_{n}
denote the free algebra of rank
n
over
K
and
\mathscr{A}
be an associative algebra over
K
. An element
\omega
of the free associative algebra
\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{F}_{n}=\mathscr{A}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
induces a map on
\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}\colon\mathscr{A}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{A},

through evaluation. These maps are called
polynomial maps with constants
. These are a natural generalization of polynomial maps.
Before moving further we present a few examples.

Example 1.1
.

Let
K
be a field and
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{M}(n,k)
be the matrix algebra. For positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}
we consider
\omega=A_{1}x_{1}^{k_{1}}+\cdots+A_{m}x_{m}^{k_{m}}\in\mathrm{M}(n,k)\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
for
A_{1},\ldots A_{m}\in\mathrm{M}(n,k)
. This could be called a generalised diagonal map.

Example 1.2
.

Let
K
be a field and
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{M}(n,k)
be the matrix algebra. Consider
\omega=Axy-Byx\in\mathrm{M}(n,k)\langle x,y\rangle
for
A,B\in\mathrm{M}(n,k)
.

There is a parallel study of these maps, in the case of groups, which are known as
word maps with constants
, see
[
5
]
. The goal of this paper is to broaden the viewpoint on the subjectivity problem from polynomial maps on algebras to polynomial maps with constants on algebras. In this paper, we study the maps in the above examples over
\mathrm{M}(2,k)
with two variables and determine the images. We prove the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field. Consider the polynomial map

\widetilde{w}
given by
Ax^{k_{1}}+By^{k_{2}}
on
M_{2}(k)
where
A,B\in\mathrm{M}(2,K)
both non-zero. Then,

\widetilde{w}
is surjective if and only if
A
and
B
can be simultaneously
conjugated to a pair of matrices such that both the matrices do not have the
same zero rows. Further, for a choice of
A,B
up to simultaneous conjugation
the images are described in
Table

1.1
,
which are vector spaces of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
.

The above theorem can be seen as an analogue of the Matrix-Waring problem.

Theorem B
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field. Consider the polynomial map
\widetilde{w}
given by
Axy-Byx
on
M_{2}(k)
where
A,B\in\mathrm{M}(2,K)
both non-zero. Then, the image is a vector space.

This theorem is parallel to that of the Lv́ov-Kaplansky
conjecture.

2.
Representatives for the simultaneous conjugacy classes

In a previous work
[
18
]
, the authors have proved that
the map induced by
\alpha x^{k_{1}}+\beta y^{k_{2}}
for
\alpha\beta\neq 0
,
is surjective on

\mathrm{M}(n,\mathbb{C})
, and it also follows from their proof that
the result holds for any algebraically closed field. This motivates us to look
into the case of the polynomial
Ax^{k_{1}}+By^{k_{2}}
. Now we explain our
strategy to tackle the mentioned problem in
Section

3
. A
somewhat similar method will be further used in
Section

4
and we will keep referring to this section, whenever needed.
For each
A\in\mathrm{M}(2,K)
, there exists
P_{A}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,K)
such that
P_{A}AP_{A}^{-1}=J_{A}
where
J_{A}
denote the Jordan canonical form of
A
. We want to comprehend for which matrices in
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
the solution of equation
Ax^{k_{1}}+By^{k_{2}}
exists i.e. for which
C
there exists matrices
X
and
Y
in
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
such that
C=AX^{k_{1}}+BY^{k_{2}}
.
On conjugating by
P_{A}
, we get
C_{A}=J_{A}x^{k_{1}}+B_{A}y^{k_{2}}
where
B_{A}
and
C_{A}
represent matrices corresponding to
B
and
C
obtained under conjugation by
P_{A}
respectively.

Let
C_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\left(J_{A}\right)
denote the centralizer of
J_{A}
in
\mathrm{GL}(2,K)
. Consider the group action of
C_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\left(J_{A}\right)
on
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
given as

\displaystyle C_{GL_{2}}\left(J_{A}\right)\times\mathrm{M}(2,K)\longrightarrow\mathrm{M}(2,K)

\displaystyle(T,B_{A})\longmapsto TB_{A}T^{-1}

We consider the orbit space under the group action and determine the image i.e. for each
T\in C_{GL_{2}}\left(J_{A}\right)
, it is sufficient to determine the solution of
J_{A}x^{k_{1}}+TB_{A}T^{-1}y^{k_{2}}
and instead of taking

independently we consider the representative of each type. Thus we determine the orbit space under the action of each centralizer of each type of Jordan form existing over
K

0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}_{\lambda\in k^{\times}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\

3.
Proof of theorem A

Let
\widetilde{B}
denote the representative of an orbit space. By the action of
C_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}

J_{A}x^{k_{1}}+B_{A}y^{k_{2}}
to
J_{A}x^{k_{1}}+\widetilde{B}y^{k_{2}}
. We lay
emphasis on

which can be written as
J_{A}X^{k_{1}}+\widetilde{B}Y^{k_{2}}
for some matrices
X

and
Y
in
\mathrm{M}(2,K)

C^{\prime}

and
Y
in
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
then the original map is not

where

is the

such that

and

.
If
C
is non-singular and

or

is non-singular then taking

or

-th

\mathrm{M}(2,K)

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1\\
&\lambda\end{pmatrix}^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{n}&n\lambda^{n-1}\\

and

are

.

Let

3.1

&\mu_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\
1&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
&a_{4}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}&\mu_{1}a_{1}b_{2}-\mu_{1}a_{2}b_{1}-a_{2}-\mu_{1}a_{4}b_{2}\\
\mu_{2}a_{4}-\mu_{1}a_{1}&\mu_{1}a_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

If
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\neq 0
, the above expression gives all the matrices of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
but the matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\\
&0\end{pmatrix}
. But this matrix can be obtained by plugging
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
, and
y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\alpha/\mu_{1}\end{pmatrix}
. Hence assume
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}=0
hereafter, but not both being zero. In case
\mu_{1}=0
, the equation

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
1&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
1&\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
1&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
1&\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-a&-b\\
\mu_{2}e&\mu_{2}f\end{pmatrix},

proves that the map is surjective and hence the image is a vector space. Moving to the case

\mu_{2}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&1\\
&\mu_{1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}g-\mu_{1}f-h&\mu_{1}h-\mu_{1}e-g\\
-\mu_{1}h&-\mu_{1}g\end{pmatrix},

which shows that if we choose
g
,
h
,
f
and
e
one after another, we get all of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
. This
proves that the map is surjective. A little bit of modification of the equation also gives
the surjectivity of the map when
{B}
is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
1&\mu_{1}\end{pmatrix}
,
\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{2}&1\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}
, and
\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{2}&\\
1&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix},
. We move to the next case.

4.2.2.
Take
{B}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&1\\
0&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}

First, let us assume that
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\neq 0
. Observe that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
1&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&1\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
1&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\mu_{1}f-h&\mu_{1}af+\mu_{1}bh-\mu_{1}be-\mu_{1}fd-dh\\
\mu_{2}e-\mu_{2}h&\mu_{2}f\end{pmatrix}.

Hence choosing
f
,
h
,
e
in order, and other variables appropriately, attains all the matrices of

\mathrm{M}(2,K)\setminus\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha\neq 0\}
, where
U_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\\
&0\end{pmatrix}
. Indeed if
e=f=h=0
then the resultant matrix is zero. But, we can get the
matrices
U_{\alpha}
by plugging
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&\\
&\alpha\end{pmatrix}
, and
Y
to be the
2\times 2
identity matrix.

Now, suppose
\mu_{2}=0
. Then plugging
X=\begin{pmatrix}&\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
and
Y
to be the identity matrix, we get that the image consists of all matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
, which is a vector space. The case
\mu_{1}=0
and
\mu_{2}\neq 0
is similar. Hence, in this
subcase, the image is a vector space. A similar argument proves the case
B=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
1&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}
.

4.2.3.
Consider
B=\begin{pmatrix}&z_{2}\\
1&\end{pmatrix}

Since
\det(A-B)=0
, we conclude that
z_{2}=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}
. Since

z_{2}\neq 0
, we get that
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\neq 0
, forcing each of
\mu_{i}
’s to be nonzero. For
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2
, we divide this into two subcases, depending on the
value of
\mu_{1}
. For
\mu_{1}\neq 1/2
, choosing

\displaystyle x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }y=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\\
&1\end{pmatrix},

we get the image to be
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
which is a vector space. For the
other case we get the full of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
by setting

\displaystyle x=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\mu_{2}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }y=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix}.

The
\mathrm{char}(k)=2
can be proved by a choice of same kind of matrices.

4.2.4.
Consider
B=\begin{pmatrix}&z_{2}\\
1&z_{3}\end{pmatrix}
, with
z_{3}\neq 0

First we deal with the case
z_{2}=0
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
1&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}&0\\
1&z_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
1&\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}g&\mu_{1}h\\
\mu_{2}e-z_{2}h&\mu_{2}f-z_{2}g\end{pmatrix}.

Since we have four linear equation in four variables and the coefficient matrix of
the equations have non-zero determinant, we get that the image is set of all matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}&\\
x&y\end{pmatrix}
, if
\mu_{1}=0
, or the whole of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
otherwise. This proves that
the image is a vector space. For the other case choose
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\neq 0
such that
\mu_{1}^{2}\neq\lambda
and
\mu_{1}^{2}\lambda\neq z_{2}^{2}\lambda^{\prime}
and

\mu_{2}^{2}\lambda^{\prime}\neq\lambda
. Then choosing

\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}&\lambda\\
\lambda^{\prime}&\end{pmatrix},\text{ and }Y=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix},

we get the image of the polynomial to be vector space as it is
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
. A similar argument works for the case
B=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&z_{2}\\
1&\end{pmatrix}
.

4.2.5.
Lastly, assume
B=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&z_{2}\\
1&z_{3}\end{pmatrix}
, with
z_{i}\neq 0

Since
\det(A-B)=0
, we get that
z_{2}=(\mu_{1}-z_{1})(\mu_{2}-z_{3})
. Furthermore,
z_{2}\neq 0
implies that
\mu_{1}\neq z_{1}

and
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\neq z_{2}
. First we assume that
\mu_{1}\neq 0
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&f\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&z_{2}\\
1&z_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&f\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(\mu_{1}-z_{1})a-z_{2}fc&b(\mu_{1}f-z_{1})-z_{2}fd\\
(\mu_{2}-z_{3}f)c-a&d(\mu_{2}-z_{3})f-b\end{pmatrix}.

Note that the coefficient matrix for
a,b,c,d
is

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}-z_{1}&&-z_{2}f&\\
&\mu_{1}f-z_{1}&&-z_{2}f\\
-1&&\mu_{2}-z_{3}f&\\
&-1&&(\mu_{2}-z_{3})f\end{pmatrix}.

Then the determinant of the coefficient matrix is
-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}z_{2}f(f-1)^{2}
.
Then choosing
f=2
, we get the image of the map to be whole of
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
and
hence it is
a vector space. We do the case
\mu_{1}=0
and leave the other case for the reader as the trick is similar.
We have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&\\
&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
\lambda&\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&z_{2}\\
1&z_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
\lambda&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-z_{2}\lambda-z_{1}c&-z_{2}\lambda b-z_{1}d\\
\mu_{2}d\lambda-\lambda z_{3}a-c&\mu_{2}c-\lambda z_{3}b-d\end{pmatrix}.

Choose
\lambda
such that
\lambda z_{2}^{2}(1-\lambda\mu_{2}^{2})+\lambda^{2}z_{1}^{2}z_{3}^{2}\neq 0
, which
is the coefficient matrix of the equations involving
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
.
This proves that in this case the image is surjective on
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
. The case when

\mu_{2}=0
and
\mu_{1}\neq 0
, is very similar to this case. This finishes all the subcases, proving it to be a vector space.

4.3.
Now we conclude the final case when
A
is a unipotent matrix

We fix
A
to be the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\mu&1\\
&\mu\end{pmatrix}
. As in all other cases, this will be divided into a few subcases. Also, we should remember our standing assumption that
\det(A-B)=0
. The previously covered cases will not be repeated.

4.3.1.
Take
B=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
z&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}
, with
z\neq 0

Since
\det(A-B)=0
, we get that

\lambda\neq\mu_{2}
.
In this case

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1\\
&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
1&h\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\\
z&\mu_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
1&h\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda-\mu_{1})a+\lambda b+c+d&(\lambda h-\mu_{1})b+d\\
-(z+\mu_{2})a+(\lambda-\mu_{2}h)c+\lambda d&-(z+\mu_{2})b+h(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{2})d\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\lambda
and
\mu_{2}
can not be simultaneously zero and the determinant of the coefficient matrix of
a,b,c,d
is

h[(\lambda-\mu_{2})\lambda(h-1)+\mu_{2}][(\lambda-\mu_{1})\mu_{2}(1-h)+\mu_{2}g]
, we can choose
h
such a way that
the determinant is nonzero. Hence we get the image is
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
which is a vector space.

4.3.2.
Consider
B=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&z\\
&\lambda\end{pmatrix}

Firstly assume that
z\neq 0
. In this case

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1\\
&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}&f\\
1&\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&z\\
&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}&f\\
1&\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\lambda b+d-\lambda fc-za&\lambda af+cf-\lambda fd-zb\\
\lambda d-\lambda a&\lambda cf-\lambda b\end{pmatrix}.

Then
f
can be chosen such a way that the coefficinet matrix of the system of equations in
q,b,c,d
has non-zero determinant. This shows
that the image in this case if
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
.

The case when
z=0
is easier, and it can be shown that the image of this polynomial is given by the set of matrices of the form
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
&\end{pmatrix}
, which is again a vector space. This finishes the proof of
B
.

5.
Concluding remarks

In summary, we have proved that the generalized Waring-type problem has a positive solution
for
\mathrm{M}(2,K)
under mild conditions on the matrices. Furthermore, the
image of
Axy-Byx
for nonzero
A,B\in\mathrm{M}(2,K)
is a vector space for all choices of

A
,
B
. It will be interesting to see images of other polynomials in general

\mathrm{M}(n,K)
. Generalizing
A
we propose the following problem.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field. Let
A
and
B
be two nonzero
matrices in
\mathrm{M}(n,K)
. Then the map induced by
Ax^{k_{1}}+By^{k_{2}}
with

k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2
, on
\mathrm{M}(n,K)
is surjective if and only if
under simultaneous conjugation, there exists representative of the orbit space say
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
such that
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
have disjoint places
of zero rows.

A polynomial
F\in\mathscr{A}\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
will be called a
multilinear polynomial if it is linear in each variable. For example

\displaystyle x_{1}A_{1}x_{2}A_{2}x_{3}+x_{2}A_{4}x_{1}x_{3}A_{5}-x_{3}x_{1}A_{6}x_{2},

where
A_{i}\in\mathrm{M}(n,K)
, is a multilinear polynomial in
3
variables in algebra
\mathrm{M}(n,K)
. Generalizing
B
, we present a generalized version of the
Lv́ov-Kaplansky conjecture.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
K
be a field. For any multilinear polynomial in
\omega\in\mathrm{M}(n,K)\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
, the image
\widetilde{\omega}\left(\mathrm{M}(n,K)\right)
is a vector space.

Abstract
. We construct moduli stacks of two-dimensional mod p p representations of the absolute Galois group of a p p -adic local field, as well as their resolutions by moduli stacks of two-dimensional Breuil–Kisin modules with tame descent data. We study the local geometry of these moduli stacks by comparing them with local models of Shimura varieties at hyperspecial and Iwahori level.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Moduli of Galois
representations

Let
K/{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
be a finite extension,
let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
, and let

\overline{r}:\Gal(\overline{K}/K)\to\GL_{d}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})
be a continuous
representation.
The
theory of deformations of
\overline{r}
— that is, liftings of
\overline{r}
to
continuous representations
r:\Gal(\overline{K}/K)\to\GL_{d}(A)
, where
A
is
a complete local ring with residue field
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p}
— is extremely
important in the Langlands program, and in particular is crucial for
proving automorphy lifting theorems via the Taylor–Wiles method.
Proving such theorems often comes down to studying the moduli spaces
of those deformations which satisfy various
p
-adic Hodge-theoretic
conditions.

From the point of view of algebraic geometry, it seems unnatural to
study only formal deformations of this kind, and Kisin observed about
fifteen years ago that results on the reduction modulo
p
of
two-dimensional crystalline representations suggested that there
should be moduli spaces of
p
-adic representations in which the
residual representations
\overline{r}
should be allowed to vary. In
particular, the special fibres of these moduli spaces
would be moduli
spaces of mod
p
representations of

\Gal(\bar{K}/K)
.

In this paper and its sequels
[
CEGS20c
,
CEGS20a
]
we construct such a
space (or rather stack)
{\mathcal{Z}}
of mod
p
representations in the
case
d=2
, and describe its geometry. In particular, over the course
of these three papers we show that their irreducible components are
naturally labelled by Serre weights, and that our spaces give a
geometrisation of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture.

In the present paper we prove in particular the following
theorem (see Theorem
5.1.2
and Proposition
5.2.20
,
as well as Definition
A.3
for the notion of a très
ramifiée representation).

Theorem 1.1.1
.

There is an algebraic stack
{\mathcal{Z}}
of
finite type over
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p}
, which
is equidimensional of
dimension
[K:{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}]
, and whose
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p}
-points are in natural
bijection with the continuous representations

\overline{\rho}:G_{K}\to\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})
which are not très ramifiée.

1.2.
The construction

The reason that we restrict to the case
of two-dimensional representations is that in this case one knows that
most mod
p
representations are “tamely potentially finite flat”;
that is, after restriction to a finite tamely ramified extension, they
come from the generic fibres of finite flat group schemes. Indeed,
the only representations not of this form are the so-called très
ramifiée representations, which are twists of certain extensions of the
trivial character by the mod
p
cyclotomic character, and can be
described explicitly in terms of Kummer theory. (This is a local
Galois-theoretic analogue of the well-known fact that, up to twist,
modular forms of any weight and level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, with
N
prime to

p
, are congruent modulo
p
to modular forms of weight two and level

\Gamma_{1}(Np)
; the corresponding modular curves acquire semistable
reduction over a tamely ramified extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
.)

These Galois representations, and the corresponding finite flat group
schemes, can be described in terms of semilinear algebra data. Such
descriptions also exist for more general
p
-adic Hodge theoretic
conditions (such as being crystalline of prescribed Hodge–Tate
weights), although they are more complicated,
and can be used to construct
analogues
for higher dimensional representations
of the moduli stacks we construct here;
this construction is the subject of

[
EG22
]
.

The semilinear algebra data that we use in the present paper are Breuil–Kisin modules
and étale
\varphi
-modules. A Breuil–Kisin module is a module with
Frobenius over a power series ring, satisfying a condition on the
cokernel of the Frobenius which depends on a fixed integer, called the

height
of the Breuil–Kisin module. Inverting the formal variable in the power
series ring gives a functor from the category of Breuil–Kisin modules to the
category of
étale
\varphi
-modules. By Fontaine’s
theory
[
Fon90
]
, these étale
\varphi
-modules correspond to
representations of
\Gal(\overline{K}/K_{\infty})
, where
K_{\infty}
is
an infinite non-Galois extension of
K
obtained by
extracting
p
-power roots of a uniformiser. By work of Breuil and
Kisin (in particular
[
Kis09
]
), for étale

\varphi
-modules that arise from a Breuil–Kisin module of height at most
1

the corresponding
representations admit a natural extension to
\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
,
and in this way one obtains precisely the finite flat
representations.
This is the case that we will consider throughout this paper, extended
slightly to
incorporate descent data from a finite tamely ramified
extension
K^{\prime}/K
and thereby allowing us to study tamely potentially finite
flat representations.

Following Pappas and Rapoport
[
PR09
]
, we then consider the moduli stack
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
of rank two
projective Breuil–Kisin modules with descent data, and the moduli stack
{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
of étale

\varphi
-modules with descent data, together with the natural
map
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}\to{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
. We
deduce from the results of
[
PR09
]
that the stack
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
is algebraic (that is, it
is an Artin stack); however
{\mathcal{R}}
is not algebraic, and indeed is infinite-dimensional. (In fact, we consider
versions of these stacks with
p
-adic coefficients, in which
case
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
is a
p
-adic formal algebraic stack, but we suppress
this for the purpose of this introduction.) The analogous construction
without tame descent data was considered
in
[
EG21
]
, where it was shown that one can
define a notion of the “scheme-theoretic image” of the morphism
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}\to{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
,
and that the scheme-theoretic image is algebraic. Using similar
arguments, we define our moduli stack
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
of two-dimensional Galois
representations to be the scheme-theoretic image, under the
morphism
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}\to{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
, of a substack of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}

cut out by a condition modeling that of being tamely potentially Barsotti–Tate.

By construction, we know that the finite type points of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
are in
bijection with the (non-très ramifiée) representations

\Gal(\overline{K}/K)\to\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})
, and by using standard results on
the corresponding formal deformation problems, we deduce that
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
is
equidimensional of dimension
[K:{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}]
. The finite type points of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}

correspond to potentially finite flat models of these Galois
representations, and we are able to deduce that
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
is also
equidimensional of dimension
[K:{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}]
(at least morally, this is by
Tate’s theorem on the uniqueness of prolongations of
p
-divisible
groups).

An important tool in our proofs is that
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}
has rather mild
singularities, and in particular is Cohen–Macaulay and
reduced.
We show this by relating the singularities of (certain substacks
of)
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}

to the local
models at Iwahori level of Shimura varieties of
\GL_{2}
-type; such a
relationship was first found in
[
Kis09
]
(in the context of formal
deformation spaces, with no descent data) and
[
PR09
]
(in the
context of stacks of Breuil–Kisin modules, although again
without descent data) and developed further
by AC and Levin in
[
CL18
]
.

-adic Hodge theory

We normalise Hodge–Tate weights so that all Hodge–Tate weights of

2.
Integral
p
-adic Hodge theory with tame descent data

In this section we introduce various objects in semilinear algebra which
arise in the study of potentially Barsotti–Tate Galois representations with tame
descent data. Much of this material is standard, and none of it will surprise an
expert, but we do not know of a treatment in the literature in the level of
generality that we require; in particular, we are not aware of a
treatment of the theory of tame descent data for Breuil–Kisin modules
with coefficients. However, the arguments are almost identical
to those for strongly divisible
modules and Breuil modules, so we will be brief.

The various equivalences of categories between the objects we consider and
finite flat group schemes or
p
-divisible groups will not be
relevant to our main arguments, except at a motivational level, so we
largely ignore them.

2.1.
Breuil–Kisin modules and
\varphi
-modules
with descent data

Recall that we have a finite
tamely ramified Galois extension
K^{\prime}/K
. Suppose further that there exists a uniformiser
\pi^{\prime}
of

{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}

\pi:=(\pi^{\prime})^{e(K^{\prime}/K)}

K
,
where

is the ramification index of

K^{\prime}/K
.
Recall that
k^{\prime}
is the residue field of
K^{\prime}
, while

are the

K^{\prime}
respectively.
Let

be the minimal polynomial of

over

Let
\varphi
denote the arithmetic Frobenius automorphism of

, which lifts uniquely

. Define

\mathfrak{S}:=W(k^{\prime})[[u]]
, and extend

By our assumptions that

Let

-algebra, set
\mathfrak{S}_{A}:=(W(k^{\prime})\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A)[[u]]

The actions of

Definition 2.1.1
.

Fix a

-adically complete
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
-algebra

consisting of

and a

such that:

—

-module

is finitely generated and

—

is an isomorphism after

(here as usual we write
\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}:=\mathfrak{S}_{A}\otimes_{\varphi,\mathfrak{S}_{A}}\mathfrak{M}

together with

.

If

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Suppose either that

2.3.
Galois representations

The theory of fields of norms
[
FW79
]

was used in
[
Fon90
]
to relate étale
\varphi
-modules with descent data to representations
of a certain absolute Galois group; not the group
G_{K}
,
but rather the group
G_{K_{\infty}}
,
where
K_{\infty}
is a certain infinite extension of
K
(whose
definition is recalled below). Breuil–Kisin modules of height
h\leq 1
are closely related to finite
flat group schemes (defined over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}
, but with descent data to
K

on their generic fibre). Passage from a Breuil–Kisin module to its associated
étale
\varphi
-module can morally be interpreted as the
passage from a finite flat group scheme (with descent data) to
its corresponding Galois representation (restricted to
G_{K_{\infty}}
).
Since the generic fibre of a finite flat group scheme over
{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}
,
when equipped with descent data to
K
,
in fact gives rise to a representation of
G_{K}
,
in the case
h=1
we may relate Breuil–Kisin modules with descent data
(or, more precisely, their associated étale
\varphi
-modules),
not only to representations of
G_{K_{\infty}}
, but to representations
of
G_{K}
.

In
this subsection, we recall
some results coming from this connection,
and draw some conclusions for Galois deformation rings.

2.3.1.
From étale
\varphi
-modules to

We begin by recalling from

]

]
.

Our assumption that each
\sigma
is absolutely irreducible furthermore
shows that
\End_{G}(\sigma^{\circ})={\mathcal{O}}
for each
\sigma
,
so that
\End_{G}(P)=\prod_{\sigma}{\mathcal{O}}
.

Standard Morita theory then shows that the functor

M\mapsto\Hom_{G}(P,M)
induces an equivalence between the category
of
{\mathcal{O}}[G]
-modules and the category of
\prod_{\sigma}{\mathcal{O}}
-modules.
Of course, a
\prod_{\sigma}{\mathcal{O}}
-module is just given by a tuple

(N_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules,
and in this optic, the functor
\Hom_{G}(P,\text{--})

can be written as
M\mapsto\bigl(\Hom_{G}(\sigma^{\circ},M)\bigr)_{\sigma}
,
with a quasi-inverse functor being given by
(N_{\sigma})\mapsto\bigoplus_{\sigma}\sigma^{\circ}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}}N_{\sigma}.

It is easily seen (just using the fact that
\Hom_{G}(P,\text{--})

induces an equivalence of categories)
that
M
is a finitely generated projective
A
-module,
for some
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra
A
,
if and only if each
\Hom_{G}(\sigma^{\circ},M)

is a finitely generated projective
A
-module.

The preceding discussion shows that
giving a rank
d
representation of
G
over an
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra
A

amounts to giving a tuple
(N_{\sigma})_{\sigma}
of projective

A
-modules, of ranks
n_{\sigma}
, such that
\sum_{\sigma}n_{\sigma}\dim\sigma=d.

For each such tuple of ranks
(n_{\sigma})
, we obtain a corresponding
moduli stack

\operatorname{Rep}_{(n_{\sigma})}(G)
classifying rank
d
representations of
G

which decompose in this manner,
and
\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G)
is isomorphic to the disjoint union
of the various stacks
\operatorname{Rep}_{(n_{\sigma})}(G).

If we write
\tau=\oplus_{\sigma}\sigma^{n_{\sigma}}
,
then we may relabel
\operatorname{Rep}_{(n_{\sigma})}(G)
as
\operatorname{Rep}_{\tau}(G)
; statements (1) and (2) are then proved.
By construction, there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Rep}_{\tau}(G)=\operatorname{Rep}_{(n_{\sigma})}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}\prod_{\sigma}[\Spec{\mathcal{O}}/\GL_{n_{\sigma}}].

Noting that
G_{\tau}:=\Aut(\tau)=\prod_{\sigma}\GL_{n_{\sigma}/{\mathcal{O}}}
,
we find that statement (3) follows as well.
∎

For each
\tau
, it follows from the identification of
\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G)_{\tau}
with

[\Spec{\mathcal{O}}/G_{\tau}]
that there is a natural map

\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G)_{\tau}\to\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
. We let
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G))
denote the
disjoint union of copies of
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
, one for each isomorphism
class
\tau
; then there is a natural map

\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G)\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G))
. While we do not want to develop a
general theory of the étale
\pi_{0}
groups of algebraic stacks, we
note that it is natural to regard
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G))
as the
étale
\pi_{0}
of
\operatorname{Rep}_{d}(G)
.

Write

Since we are assuming that

defined via

.

Let

Remark 3.3.3
.

If

then

gives an

However if

[

, Rem. 3.7]
), which necessarily cannot

is not flat

. In the following sections, when

, and can be thought of as taking the Zariski closure of

and such that all pairs of labeled Hodge–Tate

(see Remark

below).

Definition 3.3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}^{(\tau_{i})}
be the

which associates to

-algebra

.

.

Each

By Lemma

,

∎

Our aim in this section is to use

described in Section
3

4.1.
Local models: generalities

Throughout this section we allow
d
to be arbitrary; in Section
4.2
we specialise to the case
d=2
, where we relate
the local models considered in this section to the local models
considered in the theory of Shimura varieties. We will usually
omit
d
from our notation, writing for example
{\mathcal{C}}
for
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
,
without any further comment. We begin with the
following lemma, for which we allow
h
to be arbitrary.

Lemma 4.1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a rank
d
Breuil–Kisin
module of height
h
with
descent data over an
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra
A
. Assume further
either that
A
is
p^{n}
-torsion for some
n
, or that
A
is Noetherian
and
p
-adically complete.
Then

\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)^{h}\mathfrak{M}
is a finite projective

A
-module, and is a direct summand of
\mathfrak{M}/E(u)^{h}\mathfrak{M}
as an

A
-module.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
Kis09
, Lem. 1.2.2]
. We have a short
exact sequence

0\to\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)^{h}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}/E(u)^{h}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}/\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}\to 0

in which the
second term is a finite projective
A
-module (since it is a finite
projective
{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A
-module), so it is enough to show
that the third term is a projective
A
-module. It is therefore enough to show
that the finitely generated
A
-module
\mathfrak{M}/\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}

is finitely presented and
flat.

To see that it is finitely presented, note that we have the
equality

\mathfrak{M}/\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}=(\mathfrak{M}/E(u)^{h})/(\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)^{h}),

and the right hand side admits a presentation
by finitely generated projective

A
-modules

\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{M}/E(u)^{h})\to\mathfrak{M}/E(u)^{h}\to(\mathfrak{M}/E(u)^{h})/(\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)^{h})\to 0.

To see that it is flat, it is enough to show that for every finitely generated ideal
I

of
A
, the map

I\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{M}/\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}\to\mathfrak{M}/\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}

is injective. It follows easily (for
example, from the snake lemma)
that it is enough to check that the complex

0\to\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{M}/\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}\to 0,

which is exact by Lemma
2.1.2
,
remains
exact after tensoring with
A/I
. Since
I
is finitely generated we
have
\mathfrak{M}\otimes_{A}A/I\cong\mathfrak{M}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A/I
by
Remark
2.1.5
, and the desired exactness amounts
to the injectivity of

\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A/I}
for the Breuil–Kisin module
\mathfrak{M}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A/I
.
This follows immediately from
Lemma
2.1.2
in the case that
A
is killed by

p^{n}
, and otherwise follows from the same lemma once we check that
A/I
is

p
-adically complete, which
follows from the
Artin–Rees lemma (as
A
is assumed Noetherian and
p
-adically
complete).
∎

We assume from now on that
h=1
, but
we continue to allow arbitrary
d
. We allow
K^{\prime}/K
to be any
Galois extension such that
[K^{\prime}:K]
is prime to
p
(so in particular
K^{\prime}/K

is tame).

Definition 4.1.2
.

We let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{K^{\prime}/K,a}
be the algebraic stack of finite presentation

(or equivalently, for which

we will explain how to relate these local model stacks (or more precisely, their refinements satisfying a certain determinant condition that will be developed in Section

shows that they are also connected to our stacks of Breuil–Kisin modules, as follows.

Definition 4.1.3
.

By Lemma
4.1.1
, we have a natural morphism

\Psi:{\mathcal{C}}_{1,K^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{dd}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{K^{\prime}/K}
, which takes a Breuil–Kisin module
with descent data

of height

to the pair

(\mathfrak{M}/E(u)\mathfrak{M},\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}).

Remark 4.1.4
.

The definition of the stack
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{K^{\prime}/K,a}
does not include
any condition that mirrors the commutativity between the Frobenius
and the descent data on a Breuil–Kisin module, and so in general the
morphism
\Psi_{A}:{\mathcal{C}}_{1,K^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{dd}}(A)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{K^{\prime}/K}(A)
cannot be essentially surjective.

It will be convenient to consider
the twisted group rings
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]

and
({\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A)[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
,
in which the elements
g\in\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)
obey the following
commutation relation with elements
s\in\mathfrak{S}_{A}
(resp.
s\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}
):

g\cdot s=g(s)\cdot g.

(In the literature these twisted group rings are more often written as

\mathfrak{S}_{A}*\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)
,
({\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A)*\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)
, in order
to distinguish them from the usual (untwisted) group rings, but as we
will only use the twisted versions in this paper, we prefer to use
this notation for them.)

By definition, endowing a finitely generated
\mathfrak{S}_{A}
-module
P
with a semilinear

\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)
-action is equivalent to giving it the structure of a
left
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module. If
P
is projective as an

\mathfrak{S}_{A}
-module, then it is also projective as
an
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module. Indeed,
\mathfrak{S}_{A}
is a direct summand
of
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
as a
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module, given by the
central idempotent
\frac{1}{|\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)|}\sum_{g\in\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)}g
,
so
P
is a direct summand of the projective module

\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{A}}P
. Similar remarks apply to the case
of
({\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A)[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-modules.

Theorem 4.1.5
.

The
morphism
\Psi:{\mathcal{C}}_{1,K^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{dd}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{K^{\prime}/K}
is representable by
algebraic spaces and smooth.

Proof.

We first show that the morphism
\Psi
is formally smooth,
in the sense that it satisfies the infinitesimal lifting criterion for nilpotent
thickenings of affine test objects
[
EG21
, Defn. 2.4.2]
.
For this,
we follow the proof of
[
Kis09
, Prop. 2.2.11]
(see also the proof
of
[
CL18
, Thm. 4.9]
). Let
A
be an
{\mathcal{O}}/\varpi^{a}
-algebra and
I\subset A
be a nilpotent ideal.
Suppose that we are given
\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1,K^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{dd}}(A/I)

and
(\mathfrak{L}_{A},\mathfrak{L}^{+}_{A})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{K^{\prime}/K}(A)

together with an isomorphism

\Psi(\mathfrak{M}_{A/I})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathfrak{L}_{A},\mathfrak{L}^{+}_{A})\otimes_{A}A/I=:(\mathfrak{L}_{A/I},\mathfrak{L}^{+}_{A/I}).

We must show that there exists

\mathfrak{M}_{A}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{1,K^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{dd}}(A)
together with an isomorphism

\Psi(\mathfrak{M}_{A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathfrak{L}_{A},\mathfrak{L}^{+}_{A})
lifting the
given isomorphism.

As explained above, we can and do think of
\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}
as a finite
projective
\mathfrak{S}_{A/I}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module, and
\mathfrak{L}_{A}
as a finite
projective
({\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A)[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module. Since the
closed 2-sided ideal
of
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
generated by
I
consists of
nilpotent elements, we may lift

\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}
to a finite
projective
\mathfrak{S}_{A}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module
\mathfrak{M}_{A}
. This is presumably
standard, but for lack of a reference we indicate a proof. In fact the
proof of
[
Sta13
,
Tag
0D47
]
goes over unchanged to our setting. Writing

\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}
as a direct summand of a finite free

\mathfrak{S}_{A/I}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module
F
, it is enough to lift the
corresponding idempotent in
\End_{\mathfrak{S}_{A/I}[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]}(F)
, which
is possible
by
[
Sta13
,
Tag
05BU
]
. (See also
[
Lam91
, Thm. 21.28]
for another proof of the existence
of lifts of idempotents in this generality.) Note that since
\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}
is of
rank
d
as a projective
\mathfrak{S}_{A/I}
-module,
\mathfrak{M}_{A}
is of rank
d
as
a projective
\mathfrak{S}_{A}
-module.

Since
\mathfrak{M}_{A}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}_{A}
is a projective

({\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A)[\Gal(K^{\prime}/K)]
-module, we may lift the
composite

\mathfrak{M}_{A}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}_{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{L}_{A/I}

to a morphism
\theta:\mathfrak{M}_{A}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}_{A}\to\mathfrak{L}_{A}
.
Since the composite of

\theta
with
\mathfrak{L}_{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{L}_{A/I}
is
surjective, it follows by Nakayama’s lemma that
\theta
is
surjective. But a surjective map of projective modules of the same
rank is an isomorphism, and so
\theta
is
an isomorphism lifting the given isomorphism
\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{L}_{A/I}
.

We let
\mathfrak{M}_{A}^{+}
denote the
preimage in
\mathfrak{M}_{A}
of
\theta^{-1}(\mathfrak{L}^{+}_{A})
. The image of the induced
map
f:\mathfrak{M}_{A}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{M}_{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{A}\,\widehat{\otimes}_{A}A/I\cong\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}
is precisely
\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}}
, since the same is true modulo

and because

\mathfrak{M}^{+}_{A}
,
\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}_{A/I}}

contain

for (3), note that morphisms that are representable by algebraic spaces and smooth
are also flat, and take into account the final statement
of
[
Eme

),
and so
by Propositions
4.4.7
and

we reduce to checking the assertions of the theorem
for
{\mathcal{M}}^{\operatorname{BT}}_{\mathrm{loc},\operatorname{Iw}}.

Proposition
4.5.1

then reduces us to checking these assertions for the
\varpi
-adic completion
of the naive local model at Iwahori level for

.

Since this naive local model is a scheme of finite presentation over

,
its special fibre (i.e. its base-change to

and it is excellent; thus its

The special fibre of its

to its own special fibre, and so verifying (2) for the first of these special fibres
is equivalent to verifying it for the second. Finally, the
\varpi
-adic completion

-flat, and so the
\varpi
-adic

-flat if the naive local

All the properties of the naive local model that are described in the preceding

other than the Cohen–Macaulay property,
are contained
in
[

, Thm. 2.4.3]
,
if we can identify the naive local models at Iwahori level with the vertical local

[
PR03
, §8]

(see also the discussion above
loc. cit.

[

The vertical local models are obtained
by intersecting the preimages at Iwahori level of the flat local models at hyperspecial level. Although naive local models
are not flat in general, for the Weil restriction of
\GL_{2}

at hyperspecial level they are, by a special case

[

[

]
apply to restrictions of scalars

totally ramified. However, thanks

the local

Finally, Cohen–Macaulayness can be proved as in
[
Gör01

and the discussion immediately following. We thank

Remark 4.5.4
.

There is another structural result about vertical local models
that is proved in
[
PR03
, §8]
but which we haven’t incorporated
into our preceding results, namely, that the irreducible components of
the special fibre are normal. Since the notion of irreducible component
is not étale local (and so in particular not smooth local), this statement
does not imply the corresponding statement for the special fibres of

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}
or
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}

and somewhat
more technical, statement that each of the analytic branches passing through each
closed point of the special fibre of these

-adic formal algebraic
stacks is normal. We won’t discuss this further here, since we don’t
need this result.

4.6.
The Dieudonné stack

In this subsection we assume that

\eta\neq\eta^{\prime}
. As previously mentioned, the construction described in this
section will not be needed elsewhere in the present paper; however, it is
convenient to include it here because of its reliance on the discussion of
determinant conditions in Section
4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a Breuil–Kisin module with
A
-coefficients and descent data of type

\tau
and height at most
1
, and let
D:=\mathfrak{M}/u\mathfrak{M}
be
its corresponding Dieudonné module as in Definition
2.2.1
.
If we write

D_{i}:=e_{i}D
, then this Dieudonné module is given by rank two
projective modules

D_{j}
over
A
(
j=0,\ldots,f^{\prime}-1
) with linear maps
F:D_{j}\to D_{j+1}

and
V:D_{j}\to D_{j-1}
(subscripts understood modulo
f^{\prime}
)
such that
FV=VF=p
.

Now,

I(K^{\prime}/K)
is abelian of order prime to
p
, so we can write

D=D_{\eta}\oplus D_{\eta^{\prime}}
, where
D_{\eta}
is the submodule on which

I(K^{\prime}/K)
acts via
\eta
. Since
\mathfrak{M}_{\eta}
is obtained from the projective
\mathfrak{S}_{A}
-module
\mathfrak{M}

by applying a projector, each
D_{\eta,j}
is an invertible

A
-module, and
F,V
induce linear
maps
F:D_{\eta,j}\to D_{\eta,j+1}
and
V:D_{\eta,j+1}\to D_{\eta,j}

such that
FV=VF=p
.

We can of course apply the same construction with
\eta^{\prime}
in the place
of
\eta
, obtaining a Dieudonné module
D_{\eta^{\prime}}
.
We now prove some lemmas relating these various Dieudonné
modules. We will need to make use of a variant of the strong
determinant condition, so we begin by discussing this and its relationship to
the strong determinant condition of Subsection
4.2
.

Definition 4.6.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}^{+})
be a
pair consisting of a rank two projective

{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A
-module
\mathfrak{L}
, and an

{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A
-submodule
\mathfrak{L}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{L}
, such that
Zariski locally on
\Spec A
,
\mathfrak{L}^{+}
is a direct summand of
\mathfrak{L}

as an
A
-module.

Then we say that the pair
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}^{+})

satisfies the Kottwitz
determinant condition over
K^{\prime}
if for all
a\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}
, we
have

\det{\!}_{A}(a|\mathfrak{L}^{+})=\prod_{\psi:K^{\prime}\hookrightarrow E}\psi(a)

as polynomial functions on
{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}
in the sense of
[
Kot92
, §5]
.

There is a finite type stack
{\mathcal{M}}_{K^{\prime},\det}
over
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
, with

{\mathcal{M}}_{K^{\prime},\det}(\Spec A)
being the groupoid of pairs
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}^{+})
as
above which satisfy the Kottwitz determinant condition over
K^{\prime}
. As we
have seen above, by a
result of Pappas–Rapoport, this
stack is flat over
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}
(see
[
Kis09
, Prop. 2.2.2]
).

Lemma 4.6.2
.

If
A
is an

E
-algebra, then a pair
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}^{+})
as in Definition
4.6.1
satisfies the Kottwitz
determinant condition over
K^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathfrak{L}^{+}
is a rank one
projective
{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A
-module.

Proof.

We may write

{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A=K^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}A\cong\prod_{\psi:K^{\prime}\hookrightarrow E}A
, where
the embedding
\psi:K^{\prime}\hookrightarrow E
corresponds to an idempotent

e_{\psi}\in K^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}A
. Decomposing
\mathfrak{L}^{+}
as
\oplus_{\psi}e_{\psi}\mathfrak{L}^{+}
, the left-hand side of the Kottwitz determinant condition
becomes
\prod_{\psi}\det{\!}_{A}(a|e_{\psi}\mathfrak{L}^{+})=\prod_{\psi}\psi(a)^{\mathrm{rk}_{A}e_{\psi}\mathfrak{L}^{+}}
. It follows that the Kottwitz
determinant condition is satisfied if and only if the projective

A
-module
e_{\psi}\mathfrak{L}^{+}
has rank one for all
\psi
, which is equivalent to
\mathfrak{L}^{+}
being a
rank one projective
K^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}A
-module, as required.
∎

Proposition 4.6.3
.

If
\mathfrak{M}
is an object
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}(A)
,
then
the pair

(\mathfrak{M}/E(u)\mathfrak{M},\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)\mathfrak{M})

satisfies the

K^{\prime}

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}^{\prime}}
be the closed substack
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau}
consisting of those

for which the

(\mathfrak{M}/E(u)\mathfrak{M},\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)\mathfrak{M})

determinant condition for

. We need to show
that
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}
is a closed substack
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}^{\prime}}
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}
is flat over
\Spf{\mathcal{O}}

by Corollary
4.5.3
,
it is enough to show that if
A^{\circ}
is a flat
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra, then

{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}(A^{\circ})={\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}^{\prime}}(A^{\circ})
.

To see this, let
\mathfrak{M}^{\circ}
be an object
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau}(A^{\circ})
. Since
A^{\circ}
is flat over
{\mathcal{O}}
, the Kottwitz determinant conditions
for

to be an object of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}^{\prime}}(A^{\circ})
or

. Accordingly, we write

, and write
\mathfrak{M}
for

is an object of

is a rank one

\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}=\oplus_{\xi}(\im\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}})_{\xi}/E(u)\mathfrak{M}_{\xi},

∎

Lemma 4.6.4
.

If
(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}^{+})
is an object of
{\mathcal{M}}_{K^{\prime},\det}(A)
(i.e. satisfies the Kottwitz determinant condition over
K^{\prime}
),
then the morphism
\bigwedge^{2}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A}\mathfrak{L}^{+}\to\bigwedge^{2}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A}\mathfrak{L}
induced
by the inclusion
\mathfrak{L}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{L}
is identically zero.

Remark 4.6.5
.

Note that, although
\mathfrak{L}^{+}
need not be locally free over

{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A
, its exterior square is nevertheless defined,
so that the statement of the lemma makes sense.

Proof of Lemma
4.6.4
.

Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{K^{\prime},\det}
is
{\mathcal{O}}
-flat,
it is enough to treat the case that
A
is
{\mathcal{O}}
-flat. In this case
\mathfrak{L}
, and thus
also
\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{L}
, are
{\mathcal{O}}
-flat. Given this additional assumption,
it suffices to prove that the morphism of the lemma becomes zero after tensoring
with
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
over
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
. This morphism may naturally be identified with
the morphism

\bigwedge^{2}_{K^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A}\mathfrak{L}^{+}\to\bigwedge^{2}_{K^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}A}\mathfrak{L}

Locally on

, this is the embedding of a free

of the source in fact vanishes, and hence so does

∎

Lemma 4.6.6
.

If
\mathfrak{M}
is an object of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}(A)
, then

\bigwedge^{2}\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}:\bigwedge^{2}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\hookrightarrow\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{M}

It follows from Proposition

that the reduction of

vanishes, so we can think

.
We need to show that the cokernel

,

is a finitely generated

Since the formation of cokernels is compatible with

A
is an

{\mathbf{F}}
-algebra. Since the special fibre

is of finite

{\mathbf{F}}

{\mathbf{F}}

The special fibre of

∎

Lemma 4.6.7
.

There is a canonical isomorphism

\text{``}(F\otimes F)/p\text{''}:D_{\eta,j}\otimes_{A}D_{\eta^{\prime},j}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}D_{\eta,j+1}\otimes_{A}D_{\eta^{\prime},j+1},

Since
{\mathcal{C}}^{\operatorname{BT}}
is flat over

, we see that in the universal case,
the formula

\text{``}(F\otimes F)/p\text{''}
(if it exists). Since any

change of scalars, we see that the isomorphism

\text{``}(F\otimes F)/p\text{''}
is indeed characterised by the properties
stated in the lemma, provided that it exists.

To check that the isomorphism exists, we can again consider the universal case,
and hence assume that
A
is a flat
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra.
In this case, it suffices to check that the morphism

F\otimes F:D_{\eta,j}\otimes_{A}D_{\eta^{\prime},j}\to D_{\eta,j+1}\otimes_{A}D_{\eta,j+1}
is divisible by
p
, and that the formula

is indeed an isomorphism.
Noting that the direct sum over

of these morphisms may
be identified with the reduction modulo
u
of the morphism

\bigwedge^{2}\Phi_{\mathfrak{M}}:\bigwedge^{2}\varphi^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{M}

.
∎

\text{``}(F\otimes F)/p\text{''}
of the preceding lemma
may be rewritten as an isomorphism of invertible

\Hom_{A}(D_{\eta,j},D_{\eta,j+1})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}\Hom_{A}(D_{\eta^{\prime},j+1},D_{\eta^{\prime},j}).

to

Proof.

The claim of the lemma is equivalent to showing that

We now consider the moduli stacks classifying the Dieudonné modules
with the properties we have just established, and the maps from the

in the following way. Choose a

\tau=\eta\oplus\eta^{\prime}
with
\eta\neq\eta^{\prime}
.
Fix a uniformiser
\pi
of
K

\tau
is a tame

, while
if
\tau
is a tame cuspidal type, we let

be an unramified

, and set

be the maximal unramified extension of

in

. In

is a Galois extension; in the principal series

,

Suppose first that we are in the principal series case. Then there is a moduli stack classifying the data of the

together with the

F

{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}:=\Big[\bigl(\Spec W(k)[X_{0},Y_{0},\ldots,X_{f-1},Y_{f-1}]/(X_{j}Y_{j}-p)_{j=0,\ldots,f-1})\bigr)/\mathbf{G}_{m}^{f}\big],

where the

act as follows:

To see this, recall that the stack

[\text{point}/{\mathbf{G}}_{m}]

classifies line bundles, so the

copies of
\mathbf{G}_{m}

in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\eta}
correspond to
f

(

and

and

are then encoding possible changes of trivialisation, by units

There is then a natural map

classifying the Dieudonné modules

There is a more geometric way to think about what

classifies.
To begin with, we just rephrase what we’ve already indicated:

-scheme the

and

.

Equivalently, we can think of this as giving the line bundle

, and then the

and

.
Note that it superficially looks like we are remembering

rather than

, but this is illusory, since in fact

is trivial; indeed,

We now turn to the case that

and which identify

.

If we choose local trivialisations of the line bundles

and

and

respectively. The identification of

and

identifies

and

\textstyle{{\mathcal{X}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Lemma 5.2.15
.

If
S
is a complete Noetherian local
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra and

is a proper
morphism of schemes, then

is flat over
\Spec{\mathcal{O}}

(resp. normal) if and only

\widehat{Y}
(the
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-adic completion of

)
is flat over
\Spf{\mathcal{O}}
(resp. is analytically normal).

Proof.

The properties of
Y
that are in question can be tested by considering the
various local rings
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y}
, as
y
runs over the points of
Y
; namely,
we have to consider whether or not these rings are flat over
{\mathcal{O}}
,
or normal. Since
any point
y
specializes to a closed point
y_{0}
of
Y
, so that

{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y}
is a localization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y_{0}}
, and thus
{\mathcal{O}}
-flat (resp. normal) if
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y_{0}}
is, it suffices to consider the rings
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y_{0}}

for closed points
y_{0}
of
Y
.
Note also that since
Y
is proper over
\Spec S
, any closed point of
Y
lies over
the closed point of
\Spec S
.

Now let
\Spec A
be an affine neighbourhood of a closed point
y_{0}
of
Y
;
let
\mathfrak{m}
be the corresponding maximal ideal of
A
. As we noted,

\mathfrak{m}
lies over
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
, and so gives rise to a maximal
ideal
\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}:=\mathfrak{m}\widehat{A}
of
\widehat{A}
,
the
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-adic completion of
A
;
and any
maximal ideal of
\widehat{A}
contains
\mathfrak{m}_{S}\widehat{A}
,
and so arises from a maximal ideal of
A
in this manner (since
A/\mathfrak{m}_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\sim}}\widehat{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{S}
). Write
\widehat{A}_{\mathfrak{m}}
to denote
the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
A
(which maps isomorphically to
the
\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion of
\widehat{A}
). Then
\widehat{A}

is faithfully flat over the localization
A_{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y_{0}}
,
and hence
A_{\mathfrak{m}}
is flat over
{\mathcal{O}}
if and only if

\widehat{A}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is. Consequently we see that
Y
is flat over

{\mathcal{O}}
if and only if, for each affine open subset
\Spec A
of
Y
,
the corresponding
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
-adic completion
\widehat{A}
becomes flat over

{\mathcal{O}}
after completing at each of its maximal ideals. Another application
of faithful flatness of completions of Noetherian local rings shows
that this holds if and only if each such
\widehat{A}
is flat over
{\mathcal{O}}
after localizing
at each of its maximal ideals, which holds if and only each
such
\widehat{A}
is flat over
{\mathcal{O}}
. This is precisely what it means for

\widehat{Y}
to be flat over
{\mathcal{O}}
.

The proof that analytic normality of
\widehat{Y}
implies that

is normal
is similar. Indeed, analytic normality by definition means that the
completion of
\widehat{A}
at each of its maximal ideals is normal. This completion
is faithfully flat over the localization of
\Spec A
at its corresponding
maximal ideal,
and so
[
Sta13
,
Tag 033G
]

implies that this localization is also normal. The discussion of the first paragraph
then implies that

is normal. For the converse direction, we have to deduce
normality of the completions
\widehat{A}_{\mathfrak{m}}
from the normality of
the corresponding localizations
A_{\mathfrak{m}}
. This follows from that fact
that
Y

is an excellent scheme (being of finite type over the complete local ring

),
so that each
A
is an excellent
ring
[
Sta13
,
Tag
0C23
]
.
∎

Appendix A
Très ramifiée representations and Serre weights

To complete our characterization in Theorem
5.1.2
(3) of the
{\mathbf{F}}
-points of the
stack
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{dd},1}

By definition, a
Serre weight
is an irreducible

{\mathbf{F}}
-representation of
\GL_{2}(k)
, where we recall that
k
denotes the residue field of
K

representation is of the form

\overline{\sigma}_{\vec{t},\vec{s}}:=\otimes^{f-1}_{j=0}(\det{\!}^{t_{j}}\Sym^{s_{j}}k^{2})\otimes_{k,\sigma_{j}}{\mathbf{F}},

where

t_{j}
are equal to

p-1
. We say that a Serre weight is
Steinberg
if
s_{j}=p-1
for all
j
,
and
non-Steinberg
otherwise.

We now recall the definition of the set of Serre weights
W(\overline{r})

associated to a representation
\overline{r}:G_{K}\to\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})
.

Definition A.1
.

We say that a crystalline representation
r:G_{K}\to\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p})
has
type
\overline{\sigma}_{\vec{t},\vec{s}}

provided that for each embedding
\sigma_{j}:k\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}

\widetilde{\sigma}_{j}:K\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}

\sigma_{j}

such that the
\widetilde{\sigma}_{j}
-labeled Hodge–Tate weights
of

\{-s_{j}-t_{j},1-t_{j}\}

(e-1)f
pairs of Hodge–Tate weights
of

\{0,1\}
.
(
In particular the representations of

\overline{\sigma}_{\vec{0},\vec{0}}

(

)

.
)

Definition A.2

Given a representation

\overline{\sigma}

It follows easily from the formula

(1) By the main result of

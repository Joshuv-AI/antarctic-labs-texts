Generalized hydrodynamics of the repulsive spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} Fermi gas
Stefano Scopa 1, Pasquale Calabrese 1,2, Lorenzo Piroli 3

Abstract
We study non homogeneous quantum quenches in a one-dimensional gas of repulsive spin- 1 / 2 1/2 fermions, as described by the integrable Yang-Gaudin model. By means of generalized hydrodynamics (GHD), we analyze in detail the real-time evolution following a sudden change of the confining potential. We consider in particular release protocols and trap quenches, including a version of the quantum Newton’s cradle. At zero temperature, we employ a simplified phase-space hydrodynamic picture to characterize the dynamics of the particle- and spin-density profiles. Away from zero temperatures, we perform a thorough numerical study of the GHD equations, and provide quantitative predictions for different values of the temperature, external magnetic field, and chemical potential. We highlight the qualitative features arising due to the multi-component nature of the elementary excitations, discussing in particular effects of spin-charge separation and dynamical polarization.

II
The Yang-Gaudin model

We consider a
1
D system of size
\ell
containing a gas of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particles with repulsive contact interactions, described by the Yang-Gaudin Hamiltonian
Yang1967
;
Gaudin1967

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\ell}\mathrm{d}x\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(x)\left(\partial^{2}_{x}+\mu+\sigma h\right)\hat{\Psi}_{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{\ell}\mathrm{d}x\ \hat{\Psi}_{+}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{-}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{+}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{-}(x),

(1)

where
\sigma=\pm
denote the two spin components,
c>0
is the interaction coupling,
\mu
is the chemical potential and
h
is the external magnetic field . The fields
\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}
,
\hat{\Psi}_{\sigma}
are creation and annihilation fermionic operators satisfying canonical anticommutation relations

\{\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(x),\hat{\Psi}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\}=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\delta(x-x^{\prime}).

(2)

It is well-known that the Yang-Gaudin model can be solved by the Bethe ansatz
Yang1967
;
Gaudin1967
;
Takahashi1999
;
Batchelor2010
;
Guan2013
. Focusing on the sector with
N
fermions,
M
of which have spin down, the Hamiltonian (
1
) can be written in the first-quantized form

\hat{\cal H}_{N,M}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{j}}+\mu\right)-h(N-2M)+2c\sum_{i<j}\delta(x_{i}-x_{j})

(3)

with associated many-body eigenstates

\hat{\cal H}_{N,M}\ \psi_{\bm{k},\bm{\lambda}}(\vec{x})=E({\bm{k},\bm{\lambda}})\ \psi_{\bm{k},\bm{\lambda}}(\vec{x}),

(4)

\vec{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
, whose explicit form can be found e.g. in Ref.
Yang1967
. Importantly, these eigenstates are labeled by two sets of spectral parameters (or rapidities), namely
\bm{k}
and
\bm{\lambda}
, which identify two different quasiparticles species. The first set
\bm{k}=\{k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\}
is composed by the quasimomenta of the physical particles in the system while the second set
\bm{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M}\}
is related to the spin degrees of freedom. Imposing periodic boundary conditions on the system, from Eq. (
3
) one can derive the following algebraic Bethe equations

e^{\textbf{i}k_{j}\ell}=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\frac{k_{j}-\lambda_{\alpha}+\textbf{i}c/2}{k_{j}-\lambda_{\alpha}-\textbf{i}c/2}\,,

(5a)

\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}-k_{j}+\textbf{i}c/2}{\lambda_{\alpha}-k_{j}-\textbf{i}c/2}=\prod_{\beta\neq\alpha,\beta=1}^{M}\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}-\lambda_{\beta}+\textbf{i}c}{\lambda_{\alpha}-\lambda_{\beta}-\textbf{i}c}\,,

(5b)

which constrain the quantum numbers
\bm{k},\bm{\lambda}
to take only some specific values. For the repulsive gas (
c>0
),
k_{j}
are real numbers while, for
\ell\gg 1
, the rapidities
\lambda_{\alpha}
are grouped together in symmetric patterns around the real axis called strings
Takahashi1999
, which correspond to bound states of spin quasiparticles. For instance, a string of size
n
is composed by the rapidities

\lambda_{\alpha,j}=\lambda_{\alpha}^{n}+\textbf{i}(n+1-2j)c/2,\quad j=1,\dots,n\,,

(6)

with
\lambda_{\alpha}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}
known as string center.

In the thermodynamic limit where
\ell,N,M\to\infty
at fixed density
N/\ell
and
M/\ell
, the spectrum of the model becomes densely populated and the rapidities
\bm{k}
,
\bm{\lambda}
can be replaced by the density distributions (or root densities)

\rho_{1}(k_{j})\propto\frac{1}{\ell(k_{j+1}-k_{j})},\ \rho_{2,n}(\lambda_{\alpha}^{n})\propto\frac{1}{\ell(\lambda_{\alpha+1}^{n}-\lambda_{\alpha}^{n})}.

(7)

The root density
\rho_{2,n}
for any
n\geq 1
depends only on the value of the string center
\lambda\equiv\lambda^{n}_{\alpha}
.
Taking the logarithm of the Bethe equations (
5
), and the thermodynamic limit, we arrive at the Bethe-Gaudin-Takahashi (BGT) equations

\rho_{1}^{\text{tot}}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\phi_{n}\ast\rho_{2,n}](k)\,,

(8a)

\rho_{2,n}^{\text{tot}}(k)=[\phi_{n}\ast\rho_{1}](k)-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[\Phi_{n,m}\ast\rho_{2,m}](k).

(8b)

Here, we defined

\phi_{n}(k)=\frac{1}{\pi}\ \frac{2nc}{(nc)^{2}+4k^{2}},

(9)

\begin{split}\Phi_{n,m}(k)&=(1-\delta_{n,m})\phi_{|n-m|}(k)+2\phi_{|n-m|+2}(k)+\dots\\
&+2\phi_{n+m-2}(k)+\phi_{n+m}(k)\,,\end{split}

(10)

and used the notation

[g_{1}\ast g_{2}](k)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}\ g_{1}(k-k^{\prime})\ g_{2}(k^{\prime}).

(11)

Furthermore, we have introduced the total densities
\rho_{1}^{\text{tot}}\equiv\rho_{1}+\rho^{h}_{1}
,
\rho_{2,n}^{\text{tot}}\equiv\rho_{2,n}+\rho^{h}_{2,n}
, where
\rho_{1}^{h}
and
\rho_{2,n}^{h}
are the densities of unoccupied rapidities, or
holes
.

Equations (
8a
) and (
8b
) do not uniquely determine the distributions
\rho_{1},\rho_{2,n}
and an additional set of equations must be derived, depending on the macroscopic state of the model. Let us consider, for instance, the case where the system is at thermal equilibrium and at temperature
T
. Introducing the free-energy functional
Takahashi1999

\begin{split}&{\cal G}(T,\mu,h)/\ell\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\big\{(k^{2}-\mu-h)\rho(k)-Ts_{1}(k)\big\}\\
&+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\lambda\big\{2nh\ \rho_{2,n}(\lambda)-Ts_{2,n}(\lambda)\big\}\end{split}

(12)

with entropy densities

\begin{split}&s_{1}=\rho_{1}\log(1+e^{\epsilon_{1}/T})+\rho^{h}_{1}\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_{1}/T});\\[3.0pt]
&s_{2,n}=\rho_{2,n}\log(1+e^{\epsilon_{2,n}/T})+\rho^{h}_{2,n}\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_{2,n}/T}),\end{split}

(13)

and dressed energies

\frac{\rho_{1}^{h}}{\rho_{1}}=\exp(\epsilon_{1}(k)/T),\quad\frac{\rho^{h}_{2,n}}{\rho_{2,n}}=\exp(\epsilon_{2,n}(\lambda)/T)\,,

(14)

one can derive the following thermodynamic Bethe ansatz (TBA) equations by minimizing
{\cal G}
according to Eqs. (
8
)
Takahashi1999
;
Takahashi1971
;
Takahashi1973

\frac{\epsilon_{1}(k)}{T}=\frac{k^{2}-\mu-h}{T}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\phi_{n}\ast\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_{2,n}/T})](k),

(15a)

\begin{split}\frac{\epsilon_{2,n}(k)}{T}=&\frac{2nh}{T}-[\phi_{n}\ast\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_{1}/T})](k)\\
&+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}[\Phi_{n,m}\ast\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_{2,m}/T})](k).\end{split}

(15b)

Finally, let us consider an arbitrary function of the rapidities
\vec{\zeta}=\{\zeta_{1}(k),\zeta_{2,n}(\lambda)\}
. Introducing the

\theta_{1}\equiv\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}^{\text{tot}}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\epsilon_{1}/T}};\ \theta_{2,n}\equiv\frac{\rho_{2,n}}{\rho_{2,n}^{\text{tot}}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\epsilon_{2,n}/T}}\,,

(19a)

\displaystyle q=\frac{\braket{\hat{\cal Q}}}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\ {\tt q}_{1}(k)\rho_{1}(k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\ {\tt q}_{2,n}(k)\rho_{2,n}(k)\,,

(22)

i.e., they correspond to

III
The Quench setup and the GHD equations

III.1
The quench protocols

Let us discuss the class of quench protocols of interest in this paper. First, we prepare the system in an equilibrium state of the Yang-Gaudin Hamiltonian in the presence of a nonhomogeneous potential
U_{\sigma}(x)
confining the gas in the spatial region
-\frac{L}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{L}{2}
, namely

\begin{split}\hat{H}=&\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(x)\left[-\partial^{2}_{x}+U_{\sigma}(x)\right]\hat{\Psi}_{\sigma}(x)\\
&+c\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\ \hat{\Psi}_{+}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{-}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{+}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{-}(x),\end{split}

(26)

where the external potential reads

U_{\sigma}(x)=V(x)-\mu-\sigma(h+g(x)).

(27)

The presence of a trap potential
V(x)
and of an inhomogeneous magnetic field
g(x)
spoils the exact solvability of the model discussed in Sec.
II
. However, by assuming that the potential
U_{\sigma}(x)
is a slowly-varying function on macroscopically large scales, it is possible to focus on a mesoscopic description of the model over fluid cells of size
\ell
such that

a\ll\ell\ll L\,,

(28)

where
a
is the microscopic scale of the problem, typically of order of the inverse local density of particles
a\sim{\cal O}(\varrho(x)^{-1})
in the fluid cell labeled by
x
. Under such a scale separation hypothesis, at each fluid cell
x
the system appears locally homogeneous,
i.e.
,
U_{\sigma}(x)\simeq\text{cst}
, but it still contains a sufficiently large number of constituents. As a result, one obtains a thermodynamic description of the intial non-homogeneous state in terms of some local root densities
\vec{\rho}(x,k)
.

Next, we consider the quench protocol where the shape of the initial potential is suddenly changed at time
t=0

U_{\sigma}(x)\;\overset{t=0}{\mapsto}\;U^{\prime}_{\sigma}(x)\equiv V^{\prime}(x)-\mu^{\prime}+\sigma(h^{\prime}+g^{\prime}(x))\,,

(29)

and we investigate the non-equilibrium dynamics generated by the Hamiltonian (
26
) with potential
U^{\prime}_{\sigma}(x)
. Note that prime denotes the post-quench fields, not derivatives. Importantly, we require the same smoothness assumptions (
28
) for the post-quench potential
U^{\prime}_{\sigma}(x)
so that our quench problem is suitably described by the Euler hydrodynamic equations detailed below. An illustration of our working assumptions and of the quench protocols is shown in Fig.
2
.

III.2
The GHD equations

In this section, we derive the set of GHD equations for the evolution of the root densities
\vec{\rho}=\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2,n}\}
in the presence of a non-homogeneous potential
U^{\prime}_{\sigma}(x)
. They take the form

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho_{1}+\partial_{x}(\nu_{1}\rho_{1})+\partial_{k}(a_{1}\rho_{1})=0\ ,\\[4.0pt]
&\partial_{t}\rho_{2,n}+\partial_{x}(\nu_{2,n}\rho_{2,n})+\partial_{k}(a_{2,n}\rho_{2,n})=0,\end{split}

(30)

or equivalently in terms of the filling functions
\vec{\theta}=\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2,n}\}

\begin{split}&\left(\partial_{t}+\nu_{1}\partial_{x}+a_{1}\partial_{k}\right)\theta_{1}=0\ ,\\[4.0pt]
&\left(\partial_{t}+\nu_{2,n}\partial_{x}+a_{2,n}\partial_{k}\right)\theta_{2,n}=0,\end{split}

(31)

with effective velocities
\vec{\nu}=\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2,n}\}
and effective accelerations
\vec{a}=\{a_{1},a_{2,n}\}
to be determined. To this end, we rewrite the post-quench inhomogeneous Hamiltonian (
26
) as

\begin{split}\hat{H}=&\hat{H}_{0}+\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\left(V^{\prime}(x)\hat{N}(x)-g^{\prime}(x)\hat{M}(x)\right)\,,\end{split}

(32)

where
\hat{H}_{0}
is the homogeneous part of
\hat{H}
(obtained setting
V^{\prime}=g^{\prime}=0
in Eq. (
26
)) and

\begin{split}&\hat{N}(x)\equiv\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\hat{\Psi}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{\sigma}(x)\,,\\[4.0pt]
&\hat{M}(x)\equiv\hat{\Psi}_{-}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}_{-}(x)\,,\end{split}

(33)

are the total particle-number and the spin-down particle-number operators, respectively.

Following Ref.
Doyon-IGHD
, we introduce the pseudopotential

\vec{w}(x,k)\!\equiv\!\begin{pmatrix}\left[V^{\prime}(x)-g^{\prime}(x)\right]{\tt q}^{(N)}_{1}(k)\\[4.0pt]
2g^{\prime}(x){\tt q}^{(M)}_{2,n}(k)\end{pmatrix}\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}V^{\prime}(x)-g^{\prime}(x)\\[4.0pt]
2ng^{\prime}(x)\end{pmatrix}\,.

(34)

Equation (
34
) follows from the fact that the single-particle eigenvalues associated to the pertubations induced by
\hat{N}
and
\hat{M}
are
\vec{\tt q}^{(N)}=\{1,0\}
and
\vec{\tt q}^{(M)}=\{0,n\}
, respectively
Doyon-IGHD
. The effective velocities for the model are then obtained from

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}(x,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\partial_{k}[\vec{e}(x,k)+\vec{w}(x)]\right)^{\text{dr}}}{\left(\partial_{k}\vec{p}(x,k)\right)^{\text{dr}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{k}\vec{\varepsilon}(x,k)}{2\pi\vec{\rho}^{\text{\ tot}}(x,k)}\equiv\vec{v}(x,k)\,,

(35)

i.e., the effective velocities in Eq. (
16
) are not modified by the presence of
U^{\prime}_{\sigma}(x)
since the resulting pseudopotential (
34
) does not depend on the rapidity
Doyon-IGHD
. For each fluid cell
x
, the effective velocities
\vec{v}(x,k)
can be determined by solving Eqs. (
17
) with effective chemical potential
\tilde{\mu}=\mu^{\prime}-V^{\prime}(x)
and magnetic field
\tilde{h}=h^{\prime}+g^{\prime}(x)
.

The effective accelerations are instead nontrivial and read
Doyon-IGHD

\vec{a}(x,k)=\frac{\left(-\partial_{x}\vec{w}(x)\right)^{\text{dr}}}{\left(\partial_{k}\vec{p}\right)^{\text{dr}}}=\frac{\left(\{-\partial_{x}V^{\prime}+\partial_{x}g^{\prime},2n\partial_{x}g^{\prime}\}\right)^{\text{dr}}}{2\pi\vec{\rho}^{\text{\ tot}}(x,k)}.

(36)

This expression greatly simplifies in the presence of a constant magnetic field
g^{\prime}=0

\vec{a}(x,k)\equiv\vec{a}(x)=-\partial_{x}V^{\prime}\ \{1,1\}\,,

(37)

where we used Eq. (
20
). Note that the trap potential
V^{\prime}(x)
induces a forcing term
a_{2,n}=-\partial_{x}V^{\prime}
appearing in the spin species even in the absence of inhomogeneous magnetic fields (
g^{\prime}=0
), due to backreaction effects in the nested dressing operation (
19
).

The GHD equations (
31
) are formally solved with the method of characteristics

\vec{\theta}(t,x,k)=\vec{\theta}(0,\tilde{x}(t),\tilde{k}(t))\,,

(38)

with trajectories given by

\begin{split}&\tilde{x}(t)=x-\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\ \vec{v}(s,\tilde{x}(s),\tilde{k}(s)),\\

V
Zero-Temperature limit

The GHD equations become particularly simple in the limit
T\to 0
, where they allow for further analytic insight, as we discuss in this section. As a preliminary step, we first discuss the
T\to 0
limit of the model at equilibrium, and review its ground-state phase diagram.

V.1
The phase diagram

We begin by recalling that the TBA equations (
15
) admit an alternative, partially decoupled form
Takahashi1999
;
Mestyan2019

\begin{split}\frac{\epsilon_{1}(k)}{T}=&\frac{k^{2}-\mu-h}{T}-[r\ast\log(1+e^{-\epsilon_{1}/T})](k)\\
&-[s\ast\log(1+e^{\epsilon_{2,1}/T})](k),\end{split}

(40a)

\frac{\epsilon_{2,1}(k)}{T}=\left[s\ast\log\left(\frac{1+e^{\epsilon_{2,2}/T}}{1+e^{-\epsilon_{1}/T}}\right)\right](k),

(40b)

\frac{\epsilon_{2,n\geq 2}(k)}{T}=\left[s\ast\log\left((1+e^{\epsilon_{2,n-1}/T})(1+e^{\epsilon_{2,n+1}/T})\right)\right](k),

(40c)

where

s(k)=\frac{{\rm sech}(\pi k/c)}{2c},\quad r(k)=[\phi_{1}\ast s](k).

(41)

These equations must be supplemented by the asymptotic condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\epsilon_{2,n}}{T}=\frac{2nh}{T}\,.

(42)

These equations simplify considerably in the limit
T\to 0
. Using that the quasienergies
\epsilon_{2,n}(k)
are always non-negative, we see that the bound states of spin quasiparticles, corresponding to
n\geq 2
in the above equations, are exponentially suppressed at low temperatures. Therefore, at
T=0
, we have
Takahashi1999
;
Batchelor2010
;
Mestyan2019

\epsilon_{1}(k)=k^{2}-\mu-h+[\phi_{1},\epsilon_{2}]_{\bm{2}}(k),

(43a)

\epsilon_{2}(k)=2h+[\phi_{1},\epsilon_{1}]_{\bm{1}}(k)-[\phi_{2},\epsilon_{2}]_{\bm{2}}(k),

(43b)

where the
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bm{a}}
operation is defined as

[g_{1},g_{2}]_{\bm{a}}(k)=\int_{-Q_{a}}^{Q_{a}}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}\ g_{1}(k-k^{\prime})g_{2}(k^{\prime}),\quad a=1,2.

(44)

Here,
\vec{Q}=\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
are cutoffs in the rapidity space for the two quasiparticles species, typically referred to as
Fermi points
. They are defined from the condition

\epsilon_{a}(\pm Q_{a})=0,\quad a=1,2.

(45)

Accordingly, the BGT equations (
8
) also simplify to

\rho_{1}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+[\phi_{1},\rho_{2}]_{\bm{2}}(k),

(46a)

\rho_{2}(k)=[\phi_{1},\rho_{1}]_{\bm{1}}(k)-[\phi_{2},\rho_{2}]_{\bm{2}}(k),

(46b)

while Eqs. (
17
) become

v_{1}(k)\rho_{1}(k)=\frac{k}{\pi}+[\phi_{1},\rho_{2}v_{2}]_{\bm{2}}(k),

(47a)

v_{2}(k)\rho_{2}(k)=[\phi_{1},\rho_{1}v_{1}]_{\bm{1}}(k)-[\phi_{2},\rho_{2}v_{2}]_{\bm{2}}(k).

(47b)

Once again, these equations can be solved by elementary methods. An example of our numerical solution is reported in Fig.

V.2
Zero-entropy GHD

We finally return to the inhomogeneous Yang-Gaudin model (
26
) to discuss its generalized hydrodynamic description at zero temperature. As previously argued, under some smoothness assumptions on the potential
U_{\sigma}(x)
, a local density approximation at mesoscopic scales provides a reliable initial condition for the state at
T=0
, which is the ground state of the model at each spatial point
x
. The latter is suitably described in terms of the local filling functions

\vec{\theta}(x,k)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{if $k\in[-\vec{Q}(x),\vec{Q}(x)]$};\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(49)

Filling functions of this form correspond to a vanishing Yang-Yang entropy
Takahashi1999
. Such zero-entropy condition is then preserved at later times by the structure of the GHD equations (
31
), as pointed out in Ref.
Doyon2017
. It follows that the information about the dynamics of
\vec{\theta}
can be entirely encoded in a pair of Fermi contours
\vec{\Gamma}(t)
, keeping track of the evolution of the local Fermi rapidities
\vec{Q}_{\alpha}(t,x)
in the rapidity-position plane. More precisely, we can define a pair of split Fermi seas at position
x
and time
t
as

\Gamma_{a}(t,x)\equiv\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^{l_{a}}\left[{Q}_{a;2\alpha-1}(t,x),{Q}_{a;2\alpha}(t,x)\right]\,,

(50)

and the Fermi contours

\Gamma_{a}(t)=\bigcup_{x}\Gamma_{a}(t,x),

(51)

where
{Q}_{a;1}(t,x)<{Q}_{a;2}(t,x)<\dots<{Q}_{a;2l_{a}}(t,x)
are solutions of the zero-entropy GHD equations
Doyon2017
;
Ruggiero2020
;
Scopa2021
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{pmatrix}x\\[4.0pt]
Q_{a;\alpha}(t,x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{a;\alpha}^{F}(t,x)\\[4.0pt]
a_{a}(x,Q_{a;\alpha}(t,x))\end{pmatrix}

(52)

with species index
a=1,2
and split Fermi sea index
\alpha=1,\dots,2l_{a}
. We recall that the effective accelerations
a_{a}(x,k)
are those appearing in Eq. (
36
) while the Fermi velocities
\vec{v}^{F}_{\alpha}(t,x)\equiv\vec{v}(t,x,\vec{Q}_{\alpha}(t,x))
with
\vec{v}
given in Eq. (
47
), provided the definition of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bm{a}}
for the case with split Fermi seas

[g_{1},g_{2}]_{\bm{a}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{l_{a}}\int_{Q_{a;2\alpha-1}}^{Q_{a;2\alpha}}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}\ g_{1}(k-k^{\prime})g_{2}(k^{\prime}),\quad a=1,2.

(53)

In the case of a spatially homogeneous post-quench magnetic field (
g^{\prime}=0
), the expression for the effective acceleration simplifies and the zero-entropy GHD equations (
52
) become

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{pmatrix}x\\[4.0pt]
Q_{a;\alpha}(t,x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{a;\alpha}^{F}(t,x)\\[4.0pt]
-\partial_{x}V^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

(54)

where
V^{\prime}
is the post-quench confining potential coupled to the system at
t>0
.

VI
Dynamical polarization and spin-charge separation effects

In this section we focus on two features of the quench dynamics which arise due to the multi-component nature of the elementary excitations, and which are particularly clear to study at zero temperature. They include a dynamical polarization and the spin-charge separation effects. In the latter case, we will also explore its robustness against nonzero temperatures, expanding the results presented in Ref.
Scopa2021
.

VI.1
Dynamical polarization

We begin by describing a dynamical polarization process occurring in the gas during the quench dynamics. For simplicity, we consider the case of a harmonic trap
V(x)=\omega^{2}x^{2}
but the following discussion straightforwardly extends to other confining potentials.

In this case, the inhomogeneous ground state displays different local phases, determined by the mutual value of the effective chemical potential
\mu-V(x)
and of the external magnetic field
h
. For sufficiently weak
h\lesssim h_{c}(\mu,c)
, by moving from the center to the edges of the trap, we can detect a phase transition occurring at positions
\pm x^{\ast}
such that
h=h_{c}(\mu-V(\pm x^{\ast}),c)
and separating a partially polarized phase (
|x|\leq x^{\ast}
) from the fully polarized phases (
|x|>x^{\ast}
). In general, a similar scenario is found for any trap
V(x)
that confines the particles in a finite spatial region.

By releasing the trap
\omega\overset{t=0}{\to}0
at fixed value of
\mu
and
h
, the regions where the system is polarized change dynamically. In particular, the gas remains depolarized in the region
-x^{\ast}(t)\leq x\leq x^{\ast}(t)
, and polarized outside of it. The locations of the phase boundary
\pm x^{\ast}(t)
during the nonequilibrium dynamics are obtained from the zero-entropy GHD solution as
x^{\ast}(t)=\max_{x}[x\in Q_{2}(x,t)]
.
A similar definition of the phase boundary
x^{\ast}(t)
can be derived for more generic quench protocols. In Fig.
9
, we show the dynamics of the Fermi points
\vec{Q}_{\alpha}
in the rapidity-position plane and the corresponding nonequilibrium evolution of some conserved charges and currents. From the figures one clearly sees a propagating front at position
x^{\ast}(t)
that separates the partially polarized regions of the expanding gas (colored areas) from the fully polarized tails. We note that, since the velocity of the first quasiparticle species is larger, the size of the region where the particle density is non-zero but the gas is polarized is effectively growing in time, despite
|x^{\ast}(t)|
is increasing.

VI.2
Spin-charge separation effects

Finally, we turn to the analysis of spin-charge separation effects following a trap quench. In our previous paper, Ref.
Scopa2021
, we analyzed SCS taking place at
T=0
and
h=0
. In trap release protocols, we showed that SCS was visible in the dynamics of the profiles of particle and magnetization densities, which completely decouple up to perturbatively small corrections. These features were established at
T=0

T
. Here, we expand our previous results and study the effects of SCS at finite

Giamarchi-book

Mestyan2019

VII
Summary and conclusions

We investigated the nonequilibrium dynamics generated by the sudden variation of an external inhomogeneous potential in a 1D gas of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fermions with repulsive contact interactions, as described by the inhomogeneous Yang-Gaudin Hamiltonian (
26
). We have performed a thorough numerical study of the GHD equations, and provided quantitative predictions for different values of the temperature, external magnetic field and chemical potential, highlighting the qualitative features arising due to the multicomponent nature of the elementary excitations.

It would be interesting to extend our analysis to more general integrable systems solvable by the nested Bethe ansatz, including multicomponent Bose gases
Fuchs2005
;
Kollath2008
;
Kleine2008
;
Patu2015
;
Patu2015b
;
Robinson
;
Patu2018
, Bose-Fermi mixtures
Patu2019
;
Wang2020
;
Patu2022
, or the anyonic interpolation of the bosonic and fermionic Yang-Gaudin models
Patu2019b
. It would also be interesting to better understand how the number of components affects some of the features studied here, for instance in the fermionic Yang-Gaudin model with
K>2
components. Finally, while we focused on repulsive interactions, several interesting questions pertains to the attractive case. In this context, a natural setting is the one recently considered in Ref.
Koch2021
;
Koch2022
for 1D Bose gases, where GHD was exploited to predict the formation of bound states following an adiabatic change of the interactions. We expect that similar protocols in the Yang-Gaudin model could provide valuable insight into its out-of-equilibrium dynamics close to the BEC-BCS transition
BEC-BCS-1
;
BEC-BCS-2
.

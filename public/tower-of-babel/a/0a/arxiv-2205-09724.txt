), the terms corresponding to temporal variation are solved using a semi-implicit Runge-Kutta method of second order. The two steps of this computational process are depicted in the following. First, the right side of equations (
1
) are rewritten as

\displaystyle F(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{0}\Delta u+\alpha u(1-\frac{u}{K(x,y)})-\frac{buv}{u+a},

\displaystyle G(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}\Delta v+\gamma\frac{buv}{u+a}-\frac{cvw}{v+d}-\mu v,

(19)

\displaystyle H(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}\Delta w+\beta\frac{cvw}{v+d}-\nu w-\nabla\cdot(\chi_{2}(v,w)\nabla v).

The first step of the RK–method of second order consists in an one Euler step computed at central point of each time interval.

\displaystyle u^{m+1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{m}+\frac{dt}{2}\cdot F(u^{m},v^{m},w^{m})

(20)

\displaystyle v^{m+1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{m}+\frac{dt}{2}\cdot G(u^{m},v^{m},w^{m})

(21)

\displaystyle w^{m+1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{m}+\frac{dt}{2}\cdot H(u^{m},v^{m},w^{m})

(22)

In the second step, computations are made at time
m+1
like

\displaystyle u^{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{m}+dt\cdot F(u^{m+1/2},v^{m+1/2},w^{m+1/2})

(23)

\displaystyle v^{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{m}+dt\cdot G(u^{m+1/2},v^{m+1/2},w^{m+1/2})

(24)

\displaystyle w^{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{m}+dt\cdot H(u^{m+1/2},v^{m+1/2},w^{m+1/2})

(25)

Now we considered the diffusion in an implicit form, then the schema becomes a semi-implicit one. For each step, the equations are solved by applying the FEM Galerkin-Ritz method described above.

Weighted cone-volume measures of pseudo-cones
Rolf Schneider

Abstract
A pseudo-cone in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} is a nonempty closed convex set K K not containing the origin and such that λ ​ K ⊆ K \lambda K\subseteq K for all λ ≥ 1 \lambda\geq 1 . It is called a C C -pseudo-cone if C C is its recession cone, where C C is a pointed closed convex cone with interior points. The cone-volume measure of a pseudo-cone can be defined similarly as for convex bodies, but it may be infinite. After proving a necessary condition for cone-volume measures of C C -pseudo-cones, we introduce suitable weights for cone-volume measures, yielding finite measures. Then we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a Borel measure on the unit sphere to be the weighted cone-volume measure of some C C -pseudo-cone. Keywords: pseudo-cone, surface area measure, cone-volume measure, weighting, Minkowski type problem 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 52A20, Secondary 52A38

Introduction

The classical theory of convex bodies deals mainly with properties that are invariant under translations of the bodies. This changed with the seminal papers
[
17
,
18
,
12
]
on
L_{p}
and dual Brunn–Minkowski theories, where the family
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
of convex bodies in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
containing the origin
o
in the interior is in the foreground. These theories increased considerably the challenge of various Minkowski type problems. Many questions on the existence of convex bodies with given measures of various types have been solved, and several others are open. Let us briefly recall the classical issue.

For a convex body
K
(a compact, convex set with interior points) in Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the surface area measure
S_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
is defined as the image measure of
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
(where
\mathcal{H}^{k}
denotes the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure) under the Gauss map of
K
. Thus, it is a finite Borel measure on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Minkowski’s existence theorem provides necessary and sufficient conditions for a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
to be the surface area measure of a convex body. If such a body exists, it is unique up to a translation. For references and further information, we refer to
[
20
, Sect. 8.2]
and its Notes.

The cone-volume measure
V_{K}
of a convex body
K\in{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
is also a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. It can be defined as follows. Let
\nu_{K}(x)
be the outer unit normal vector of
K
at points
x\in\partial K
where it is unique (which is the case
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere on
\partial K
, the boundary of
K
). Then

V_{K}(\omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\langle x,\nu_{K}(x)\rangle\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}x)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\omega}h_{K}(u)\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}(K,u)

(1)

for Borel sets
\omega\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, where
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
denotes the scalar product of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
h_{K}
is the support function of
K
. The name ‘cone-volume measure’ comes from the fact that

V_{K}(\omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n}\left(\bigcup_{x\in\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}[o,x]\right),

(2)

where
[o,x]
is the closed segment with endpoints
o
and
x
. For the equivalence of these representations, we refer to
[
20
, p. 501]
.

The Minkowski type problem for cone-volume measures asks for necessary and sufficient conditions for a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
to be the cone-volume measure of some convex body. It is also known as the
L_{0}
-Minkowski problem or the logarithmic Minkowski problem. In the case of existence, also uniqueness is of interest. Early contributions to the two-dimensional case are due to Gage and Li
[
10
]
and to Stancu
[
25
,
26
]
. In
n
dimensions, Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
8
]
discovered that for even measures the so-called ‘subspace concentration condition’ is necessary and sufficient. Treatments of the discrete case were provided by Zhu
[
29
]
and by Böröczky, Hegedűs and Zhu
[
5
]
. That the subspace concentration condition is sufficient also for non-symmetric measures was proved by Chen, Li and Zhu
[
9
]
. Further investigations on cone-volume measures are
[
11
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
.

In the last decade, it has become clear that a counterpart to the set
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
of convex bodies containing the origin in the interior can be seen in the set of pseudo-cones. A pseudo-cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a nonempty closed convex set
K
not containing the origin and satisfying
\lambda K\subseteq K
for
\lambda\geq 1
. Thus, pseudo-cones are special unbounded convex sets. If
C
is the recession cone of the pseudo-cone
K
(i.e.,
C=\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:K+\lambda z\subseteq K\,\forall\lambda\geq 0\}
), we have
K\subset C
. In the following, we assume always that a pointed closed convex cone
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with interior points is given, and we will be interested in the pseudo-cones with recession cone
C
, called
C
-pseudo-cones. The set of all
C
-pseudo-cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is denoted by
ps(C)
.

A pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
is called
C
-full if
C\setminus K
is bounded, and
C
-close if
C\setminus K
has finite volume (called the covolume of
K
). Khovanskiĭ and Timorin
[
13
]
introduced
C
-full sets (under a different name) and proved complemented versions of the Aleksandrov–Fenchel, Brunn–Minkowski and Minkowski inequalities for them. A Brunn–Minkowski theory for
C
-close pseudo-cones was developed in
[
21
]
. The similarity of
ps(C)
to
{\mathcal{K}}^{n}_{(o)}
became even more evident when the existence of a counterpart to polarity, called copolarity, was noticed. In a special case it was introduced by Rashkovskii
[
19
]
. It appeared within a general study of dualities by Artstein–Avidan, Sadovsky and Wyczesany
[
2
]
and was further studied by Xu, Li and Leng
[
27
]
and by Schneider
[
23
]
.

Surface area measures and cone-volume measures of pseudo-cones can essentially be defined as for convex bodies. We denote by
C^{\circ}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 0\,\forall y\in C\}
the dual cone of
C
and need the open subsets of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
given by

\Omega_{C}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C,\qquad\Omega_{C^{\circ}}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}.

Here
{\rm int}
denotes the interior. The surface area measure of
K\in ps(C)
is defined by

S_{n-1}(K,\omega):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega))\quad\mbox{for }\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}}),

where
\mathcal{B}(X)
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets of a topological space
X
. The cone-volume measure
V_{K}
of
K
is defined by (
2
), but now only for Borel sets
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

The mappings
S_{n-1}(K,\cdot)
and
V_{K}
are again Borel measures, but in contrast to the case of convex bodies they are only defined on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, and they can be infinite, though they are finite on compact sets. Each of these measures can also be the zero measure, namely if
K=F+C
with a nonempty compact convex set
F\subset C
of dimension less than
n-1
.

The Minkowski problem for pseudo-cones asks for necessary and sufficient conditions for a Borel measure
\varphi
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
in order that there is a pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
with
S_{n-1}(K,\cdot)=\varphi
. A general answer is unknown. That finiteness and compact support are sufficient was proved in
[
21
]
. Extensions of this result to
L_{p}
versions were treated by Yang, Ye and Zhu
[
28
]
, and versions for dual curvature measures by Li, Ye and Zhu
[
15
]
. A further extension, to so-called
(p,q)
-dual curvature measures, is due to Ai, Yang and Ye
[
1
]
.

It has repeatedly been pointed out to the author, so by Jacopo Ulivelli, Vadim Semenov and Yiming Zhao, that the results from
[
21
,
22
]
for finite measures can also be deduced from known results about Monge–Ampère equations. A certain transformation is needed here. For this transformation we refer, for example, to Bakelman
[
3
, Sect. 4]
, where also pseudo-cones appear implicitly, though not under this name. For weightings as treated in the following, a direct geometric approach seems to be more natural.

The Minkowski problem for cone-volume measures of pseudo-cones can be formulated similarly. It was proved in
[
21
, Thm. 5]
that every nonzero finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is the cone-volume measure of some
C
-pseudo-cone
K
(but finiteness is not a necessary condition). That
K
is uniquely determined was proved by Yang, Ye and Zhu
[
28
, Thm. 7.3]
. The fact that no analogue of the subspace concentration condition is required, makes the Minkowski problem for cone-volume measures of pseudo-cones easier than for convex bodies; on the other hand, infinite measures cause new problems.

When the finiteness condition is dropped, the mentioned Minkowski problems are open. For surface area measures, a necessary condition, in the form of a moderate growth condition close to the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, was proved in
[
22
]
. A similar necessary condition for cone-volume measures is the subject of the following theorem. For sets
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
we denote by
\Delta(\omega)
the spherical distance of
\omega
from the boundary of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, that is

\Delta(\omega):=\inf\{\angle(u,v):u\in\omega,\,v\in\partial\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\}

(it is clear from the context whether the boundary operator
\partial
(and later
{\rm int}
) refers to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
or to
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
). Here
\angle(u,v)
denotes the angle between the vectors
u
and
v
. According to
[
23
, Sect. 2]
), we can fix a unit vector
\mathfrak{v}
with
\mathfrak{v}\in{\rm int}\,C
and
-\mathfrak{v}\in{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
. There is a constant
c
, depending only on
C
,
\mathfrak{v}
and
K
, such that

V_{K}(\omega)\leq\frac{c}{\Delta(\omega)^{n-1}}

for each compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

In general, necessary and sufficient conditions are unknown. However, and this leads us to the core of this investigation, we can look at surface area and cone-volume measures of
C
-pseudo-cones from a different point of view. For
K\in ps(C)
, the shape of
K
is strongly influenced by the shape of
C
if the distance from the origin tends to infinity. For this reason, we introduce weightings that become smaller where the measures under consideration are of lesser importance. For convex bodies, weighted Minkowski problems were considered, for example, by Livshyts
[
16
]
and by Kryvonos and Langharst
[
14
]
. It seems that for pseudo-cones, suitable weightings are even more desirable, allowing us to neglect those regions where the measures loose interest. For
C
-pseudo-cones, weighted surface area measures were treated in
[
24
]
. Underlying there was the following definition.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Theta:C\setminus\{o\}\to(0,\infty)
be continuous and homogeneous of degree
-q
, where
n-1<q<n
.

The
\Theta
-weighted surface area measure of
K\in ps(C)
is defined by

S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\omega):=\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\rm d}y)

for
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
. The following was proved in
[
24
]
. The measure
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)
is finite. Given a finite Borel measure
\varphi
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
with compact support, there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K
with
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)=\varphi
.

It is the main purpose of this paper to obtain a corresponding result for cone-volume measures. Although we partially use similar arguments, there are some crucial differences.

We use the density
\Theta
to define the
n
-dimensional
\Theta
-weighted Hausdorff measure on
{\rm int}\,C
by

\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}(\eta):=\int_{\eta}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y),\quad\eta\in\mathcal{B}({\rm int}\,C).

(3)

The
\Theta
-weighted cone-volume measure of
K\in ps(C)
is then defined by

V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega):=\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}\left(\bigcup_{x\in\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}[o,x]\right),\quad\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}}).

It is clear that
V_{K}^{\Theta}
is a measure, and it will turn out that it is finite.

Theorem 2
.

A Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is the
\Theta
-weighted cone-volume measure of some
C
-pseudo-cone if and only if it is finite.

In the next section, we collect some notation and basic results. Theorem
1
is shown in Section
3
. Section
4
proves some properties of the weighted cone-volume measures, in particular, their finiteness and a special weak continuity. The proof of Theorem
2
is then completed in Section
5
, by showing that finiteness is sufficient.

2
Preliminaries

We work in
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) with scalar product
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
(the induced norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|
), origin
o
, unit ball
B^{n}
and unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. A pointed
n
-dimensional closed convex cone
C
is given, and
C^{\circ}
is its dual cone. A unit vector
\mathfrak{v}\in{\rm int}\,C\cap{\rm int}(-C^{\circ})
is fixed.

Hyperplanes and closed halfspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are written in the form

H(u,t)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle=t\},\qquad H^{-}(u,t)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,u\rangle\leq t\}

for
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and for
t>0
we define the compact set

C^{-}(t):=C\cap H^{-}(\mathfrak{v},t).

For
K_{j}\in ps(C)
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we write
K_{j}\to K_{0}
as
j\to\infty
if there exists
t_{0}>0
with
K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{0})\not=\emptyset
for all
j
and
K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t)\to K_{0}\cap C^{-}(t)
for all
t>t_{0}
, in the sense of the ordinary convergence of convex bodies. When in the following continuity is mentioned for pseudo-cones, it refers to this convergence.

The support function of
K\in ps(C)
is defined by

h_{K}(x):=\sup\{\langle x,y\rangle:y\in K\},\quad x\in C^{\circ}.

We note that the supremum is a maximum if
x\in{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
and that
h_{K}
is bounded and non-positive (it is negative on
{\rm int}\,C
). For the latter reason, we also write

\overline{h}_{K}:=-h_{K}.

The halfspace
H^{-}(u,h_{K}(u))
is the supporting halfspace (containing
K
) of
K
with outer unit normal vector
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

We define

\varrho_{K}(v):=\min\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:\lambda v\in K\}\quad\mbox{for }v\in\Omega_{C}.

For
y\in{\rm int}\,C
there exists
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
\lambda y\in K
since
C
is the recession cone of
K
, and the minimum exists since
K
is closed. We have
\varrho_{K}(v)v\in\partial K
for
v\in\Omega_{C}
, and we define the mapping
r_{K}:\Omega_{C}\to\partial K
by

r_{K}(v):=\varrho_{K}(v)v,\quad v\in\Omega_{C}.

Further, we denote by
\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K
the set of points
x\in\partial K\cap{\rm int}\,C
where
\nu_{K}(x)
is unique. We shall use repeatedly that
\nu_{K}
is continuous on
\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K
. We define

\alpha_{K}:=\nu_{K}\circ r_{K}\quad\mbox{on }r_{K}^{-1}(\partial{\hskip 1.0pt}^{\prime}K).

The continuity of the maps
\varrho_{K}
,
r_{K}
,
\alpha_{K}
follows from well-known results for convex bodies. These maps are known, respectively, as the radial function, the radial map. and the radial Gauss map of
K
.

Let
\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be nonempty and compact. A pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
is said to be
C
-determined by
\varpi
if

K=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\varpi}H^{-}(u,h_{K}(u)).

Such a set is
C
-full, since
\varpi
is compact. The set of all
C
-pseudo-cones that are
C
-determined by
\varpi
is denoted by
{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
.

We recall the definition of Wulff shapes in the framework of
C
-pseudo-cones (see
[
21
, Sect. 5]
). If
\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is a nonempty compact set and
f:\varpi\to(0,\infty)
is a positive continuous function, we define

[f]:=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\varpi}\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle y,u\rangle\leq-f(u)\}.

Then
[f]\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
, and we call
[f]
the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\varpi,f)
. Clearly,
\overline{h}_{[f]}\geq f
on
\varpi
, and for
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
we have
[\overline{h}_{K}|_{\varpi}]=K
.

Proof of Theorem
1

Theorem
1
could be derived from a corresponding result for surface area measures, but we prefer to deduce it directly from the following lemma. This lemma was essentially obtained in
[
22
]
, but we give a simplified and clarified version of the argument leading to it, with a slightly more explicit estimate.

Lemma 1
.

Let
s>0
. There is a constant
c_{1}
, depending only on
C
and
\mathfrak{v}
, with the following property. To each compact set
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, there exists a number
t_{\omega}
, satisfying

t_{\omega}\leq\frac{c_{1}s}{\Delta(\omega)},

(4)

such that
H(u,\tau)\cap C^{-}(s)\not=\emptyset
with
u\in\omega
implies
H(u,\tau)\cap C\subset C^{-}(t_{\omega})
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a compact subset. The assertion is trivial if
\omega=\emptyset
or
\omega=\{-\mathfrak{v}\}
, so we exclude these cases. Define

t_{\omega}:=\max\{\langle x,\mathfrak{v}\rangle:x\in C\cap H(u,\tau),\mbox{ where }u\in\omega,\,H(u,\tau)\cap C^{-}(s)\not=\emptyset\}.

The maximum exists, due to the compactness of
C^{-}(s)
and
\omega
. Clearly,
t_{\omega}>s
. There is a hyperplane
H(u^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
with
u^{\prime}\in\omega
and
H(u^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\cap C^{-}(s)\not=\emptyset
, and there is a point
x\in H(u^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\cap C
such that
t_{\omega}=\langle x,\mathfrak{v}\rangle
. Necessarily,
x\in\partial C
. By the definition of
t_{\omega}
, if
H(u,\tau)
is any hyperplane with
u\in\omega
and
H(u,\tau)\cap C^{-}(s)\not=\emptyset
, then
H(u,\tau)\cap C\subset H^{-}(\mathfrak{v},t_{\omega})
.

By the choice of the hyperplane
H(u^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
, we can choose a point
y\in H(u^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\cap\partial C\cap H(\mathfrak{v},s)
. The segment
[x,o]
intersects the hyperplane
H(\mathfrak{v},s)
in a point
z
. Let

a:=\|y-z\|,\quad b:=\|z-x\|,\quad c:=\|x-y\|.

For the triangle with vertices
x,y,z
, let
\alpha
be the angle at
x
,
\beta
the angle at
y
, and
\gamma
the angle at
z
. Then

\alpha\geq\Delta(\omega).

(5)

This holds since the segment
[x,z]
lies in a supporting hyperplane of
C
with an outer normal vector
v
(hence
v\in\partial C^{\circ}
), the segment
[x,y]
lies in a hyperplane with normal vector
u^{\prime}\in\omega
, hence the angle
\alpha
is at least the angle between
u^{\prime}
and
v
, which is at least
\Delta(\omega)
.

Let
h
be the distance of
x
from the hyperplane
H(\mathfrak{v},s)
; thus
t_{\omega}=h+s
. We have

\frac{c}{\sin\gamma}=\frac{a}{\sin\alpha}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{h}{c}\leq\sin\beta.

(6)

For the latter inequality, we note that there is a point
z^{\prime}
on the line through
y
and
z
such that the triangle with vertices
x,y,z^{\prime}
has a right angle at
z^{\prime}
, hence
\|x-z^{\prime}\|/c=\sin\beta
. Since
z^{\prime}\in H({\mathfrak{v}},s)
, we have
h\leq\|x-z^{\prime}\|
. From (
6
) we obtain

h\leq\frac{a}{\sin\alpha}\sin\gamma\sin\beta.

(7)

There is a positive constant
c_{2}
depending only on
C
and
\mathfrak{v}
such that
a\leq c_{2}s
. Since
\mathfrak{v}\in{\rm int}\,C
, we have
\gamma>\pi/2
and thus
\alpha<\pi/2
, hence
\sin\alpha\geq 2\alpha/\pi
. Therefore, it follows from (
7
) and (
5
) that

h\leq\frac{c_{2}s}{\sin\alpha}\leq\frac{c_{2}s}{\Delta(\omega)}\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}

and hence also

t_{\omega}\leq\frac{c_{1}s}{\Delta(\omega)}

with a constant
c_{1}
depending only on
C
and
\mathfrak{v}
, as stated.
∎

The following is Lemma 6 from

Lemma 2

Let

.

We need also Lemma 5 from

from

Lemma 3
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
. Let
\omega\subseteq\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a nonempty Borel set and
g:\omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded and measurable. Then

\int_{\omega}g(u)\,S^{\Theta}_{n-1}(K,{\rm d}u)=\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}g(\alpha_{K}(v))\Theta(r_{K}(v))\frac{\varrho^{n-1}_{K}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K}(v)\rangle|}\,{\rm d}v.

We can deduce properties of the weighted cone-volume measures from those of the weighted surface area measures, by using the following relation between them. It is reminiscent of (
1
).

Lemma 4
.

For
K\in ps(C)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
we have

V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega)=\frac{1}{n-q}\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{K}\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot).

Proof.

Let
K\in ps(C)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
. Let
M(K,\omega)
be defined by (
8
). We use polar coordinates and the homogeneity of
\Theta
to obtain

\displaystyle V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M(K,\omega)}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{M(K,\omega)}(rv)\Theta(rv)r^{n-1}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{K}^{-1}(\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega))}\Theta(v)\int_{0}^{\varrho_{K}(v)}r^{n-1-q}\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-q}\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\Theta(v)\varrho_{K}^{n-q}(v)\,{\rm d}v.

Lemma
3
with
g:=\overline{h}_{K}|_{\omega}
gives

\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{K}(u)\,S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,{\rm d}u)=\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))\Theta(r_{K}(v))\frac{\varrho_{K}^{n-1}(v)}{|\langle v,\alpha_{K}(v)\rangle|}\,{\rm d}v.

Here

\overline{h}_{K}(\alpha_{K}(v))=|\langle\varrho_{K}(v)v,\alpha_{K}(v)\rangle|

(10)

and
\Theta(r_{K}(v))=\varrho_{K}(v)^{-q}\Theta(v)
, therefore

\int_{\omega}\overline{h}_{K}\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,\cdot)=\int_{\alpha_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}\Theta(v)\varrho_{K}^{n-q}(v)\,{\rm d}v=(n-q)V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega)

by (
9
), as stated.
∎

Lemma 5

For

Lemma 6
.

Let
\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be a nonempty, compact subset, and let
K_{j}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then
K_{j}\to K_{0}
as
j\to\infty
implies the weak convergence
V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}V_{K_{0}}^{\Theta}
.

Proof.

Let
f:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded, continuous function. It follows from Lemma
4
that

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}f\,{\rm d}V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}=\frac{1}{n-q}\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}f\overline{h}_{K_{j}}\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{j},\cdot).

As
j\to\infty
, we have
\overline{h}_{K_{j}}\to\overline{h}_{K_{0}}
uniformly (as can be deduced from
[
20
, Lem. 1.8.14]
), and
S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{j},\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{0},\cdot)
by Lemma
2
.
Therefore,

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}f\,{\rm d}V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}\to\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}f\,{\rm d}V_{K_{0}}^{\Theta}\quad\mbox{as }j\to\infty,

and the assertion follows.
∎

We shall need the following consequence of this lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Let
K_{j}\in ps(C)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and let
\omega\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be nonempty and compact. Suppose that
t>0
satisfies
\nu_{K_{0}}^{-1}(\omega)\subset{\rm int}\,C^{-}(t)
and

\emptyset\not=K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t)\to K_{0}\cap C^{-}(t)\quad\mbox{as }j\to\infty.

(11)

Then the restriction of
V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}
to
\omega
converges weakly to the restriction of
V_{K_{0}}^{\Theta}
to
\omega
.

Proof.

Since
\nu_{K_{0}}^{-1}(\omega)\subset{\rm int}\,C^{-}(t)
and
\omega
is compact, we can choose a compact set
\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
with
\omega\subset{\rm int}\,\varpi
and
\nu_{K_{0}}^{-1}(\varpi)\subset{\rm int}\,C^{-}(t)
. By the convergence (
11
) we have
\nu_{K_{j}}^{-1}(\varpi)\subset{\rm int}\,C^{-}(t)
for sufficiently large
j
, and after changing the notation we can assume that this holds for all
j
.

For
K\in ps(C)
we define

K^{(\varpi)}:=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\varpi}H^{-}(u,h_{K}(u)),

so that
K^{(\varpi)}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
. We have

\nu_{K^{(\varpi)}}^{-1}(\omega)=\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega).

(12)

By Lemma 2 of
[
24
]
and its proof, the convergence (
11
) implies that

K_{j}^{(\varpi)}\to K_{0}^{(\varpi)}\quad\mbox{as }j\to\infty.

The assertion now follows from (
12
) and Lemma
6
.
∎

5
Sufficiency of finiteness

For
K\in ps(C)
, we define the
\Theta
-weighted covolume
of
K
by

V_{\Theta}(K):=V_{K}^{\Theta}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})=\int_{C\setminus K}\Theta(y)\,\mathcal{H}^{n}({\rm d}y).

To show that finiteness of
\varphi
is a sufficient condition in Theorem
2
, we apply first a variational argument, using Wulff shapes. We need Lemma 11 of
[
24
]
, which we reformulate below. We use that
\overline{h}_{K}
for
K\in ps(C)
is bounded away from
0
on a compact set
\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\emptyset\not=\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
be compact and
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
. Let
f:\varpi\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous, and let
[\overline{h}_{K}|_{\varpi}+tf]
be the Wulff shape associated with
(C,\varpi,\overline{h}_{K}|_{\varpi}+tf)
for
|t|\leq\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon>0
is so small that
\overline{h}_{K}|_{\varpi}+tf>0
. Then

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{V_{\Theta}([\overline{h}_{K}|_{\varpi}+tf])-V_{\Theta}(K)}{t}=\int_{\varpi}f(u)\,S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K,{\rm d}u).

To complete now the proof of Theorem
2
, we assume that a finite Borel measure
\varphi
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
is given. If
\varphi
is the zero measure, then
V_{K}^{\Theta}=\varphi
can be satisfied if, for example, we choose
K=C+z
with
z\in{\rm int}\,C
. Therefore, we assume in the following that
\varphi
is not the zero measure.

The proof that
\varphi
can be realized as
V_{K}^{\Theta}
for some pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
proceeds in two steps. The first step assumes that
\varphi
has compact support
\omega
and finds the solution
K\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega)
by solving an extremum problem and applying the variational Lemma
8
. The second step applies this to an increasing sequence
(\omega_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of compact sets with union
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and employs the Blaschke selection theorem and the choice of a diagonal sequence.

The first step can be found in
[
21
, Sect. 9]
for unweighted surface area measures and in
[
24
, Sect. 7]
for
\Theta
-weighted surface area measures.
For unweighted cone-volume measures, both steps were carried out in
[
21
, Sects. 11, 12]
, and the second step for unweighted surface area measures appears in
[
22
, Sect. 3]
. Therefore, we need not reproduce the full proof here, but we give only a sketch and point out where different arguments are required. A major difference to
[
21
]
is the use of Lemma
8
. Aleksandrov’s original variational lemma, after which it is modeled, used in its proof (which is reproduced in
[
20
, Sect. 7.5]
) inequalities for mixed volumes, thus restricting its generalizability. Fortunately, Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
12
, Lem. 4.3]
found a different approach, which allowed several extensions of the lemma.

We assume, first, that
\varphi
is a nonzero finite Borel measure with support contained in the compact set
\varpi\subset\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Temporarily we assume that
\varphi(\varpi)=1
. The functional to be maximized is defined by

\Phi(f):=V_{\Theta}([f])^{-1/(n-q)}{\rm exp}\,\int_{\varpi}\log f\,{\rm d}\varphi,\quad f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\varpi),

where
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\varpi)
is the set of positive continuous functions on
\varpi
. This functional is continuous and homogeneous of degree zero, as follows from Lemma 5 in
[
21
]
, the properties of the function
\Theta
and from
\varphi(\varpi)=1
. As in the proof of Theorem 4 in
[
21
]
, one shows that
\Phi
attains a maximum on
\{\overline{h}_{L}|_{\varpi}:L\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi),\,V_{\Theta}(L)=1\}
at
\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\varpi}
for some set
K_{0}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\varpi)
with
V_{\Theta}(K_{0})=1
, and that this is also the maximum of
\Phi
on
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\varpi)
.

Let
f
be a continuous function on
\varpi
. The function

h_{t}:=\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\varpi}+tf\overline{h}_{K_{0}}|_{\varpi}\quad\mbox{for }|t|\leq\varepsilon

belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}(\varpi)
if
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small. Hence, the derivative of
\Phi(h_{t})
at
t=0
(which exists by Lemma
8
) is equal to zero, which together with Lemma
8
and
V_{\Theta}([h_{0}])=V_{\Theta}(K_{0})=1
leads to

\left[-\frac{1}{n-q}\int_{\varpi}f\overline{h}_{K_{0}}\,{\rm d}S_{n-1}^{\Theta}(K_{0},\cdot)+\int_{\varpi}f\,{\rm d}\varphi\right]{\rm exp}\,\int_{\varpi}\log\overline{h}_{K_{0}}\,{\rm d}\varphi=0.

In view of Lemma
4
this is equivalent to

\int_{\varpi}f\,{\rm d}V_{K_{0}}^{\Theta}=\int_{\varpi}f\,{\rm d}\varphi.

Since this holds for all continuous functions
f
on
\varpi
, we deduce that
V_{K_{0}}^{\Theta}=\varphi
.

If
\varphi(\varpi)=1
is not satisfied, then a suitable dilate
K
of
K_{0}
satisfies
V_{K}^{\Theta}=\varphi
, since
V_{K}^{\Theta}
is homogeneous in
K
of degree
n-q\not=0
.

For the second step, we assume now that
\varphi
is a nonzero, finite Borel measure on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. We choose a sequence
(\omega_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of compact subsets of
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
with
\varphi(\omega_{1})>0
,
\omega_{j}\subset{\rm int}\,\omega_{j+1}
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\omega_{j}=\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Then we define
\varphi_{j}(\omega):=\varphi(\omega\cap\omega_{j})
for
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As shown above, for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
K_{j}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega_{j})
with
V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}=\varphi_{j}
.

The proof can be completed as that of Theorem 5 in
[
21
]
, once we have shown that there are two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, independent of
j
, such that the distance
b(K)
of
K
from the origin satisfies

c_{1}<b(K_{j})<c_{2}\quad\mbox{for all }j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(13)

For the left inequality of (
13
), we use the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Let
K\in ps(C)
satisfy
b(K)=1
. Let
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
be a Borel set with
\Delta(\omega):=\tau>0
. Then there is a constant
c
, depending only on
C,\Theta,\tau
, such that
V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega)<c
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)
. There is a supporting hyperplane
H
of
K
at
x
with
u\in\omega
. Since
b(K)=1
, the hyperplane
H
meets the ball
B^{n}
. Therefore, there is a constant
R
, depending only on
C
and
\tau
, such that
H\cap C\subset RB^{n}
. Thus,
\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)\subset RB^{n}
. This implies that

V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega)=\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}(M(K,\omega))<\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}(RB^{n})=:c,

where
c<\infty
depends only on
C,\tau,\Theta
.
∎

Let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We have
V_{K}^{\Theta}(\omega_{1})=\varphi(\omega_{1})>0
. Since
b(b(K_{j})^{-1}K_{j})=1
, we get, with a constant
c>0
depending only on
C,\Theta,\omega_{1}
,

c>V_{b(K_{j})^{-1}K_{j}}^{\Theta}(\omega_{1})=b(K_{j})^{-(n-q)}V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}(\omega_{1})

and hence

b(K_{j})>\left(\frac{\varphi(\omega_{1})}{c}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-q}},

where the right-hand side is independent of
j
.

9
is modeled after Lemma 9 in

To show the right-hand inequality of (

\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}
, defined by (

), is homogeneous of degree

, hence we can choose a number

with
\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}(c_{2}B^{n})>\varphi(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
. Suppose that
b(K_{j})\geq c_{2}
for some

. Then
C\cap c_{2}B^{n}\subset C\setminus K_{j}
, hence we obtain

\varphi(\omega_{j})=V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}(\omega_{j})=V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})=\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}(C\setminus K_{j})>\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}^{n}(c_{2}B^{n})>\varphi(\omega_{j}).

This is a contradiction.

Lemma 10
.

If
K\in ps(C)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
, then

\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)\subset\frac{b(K)}{\sin\Delta(\omega)}B^{n}.

To complete now the proof of Theorem
2
, we combine some previous arguments. Precisely as in the proof of Theorem 5 in
[
21
]
, one shows that there are a pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
and an increasing sequence
(t_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
t_{k}\uparrow\infty
such that, after a change of notation, the pseudo-cones
K_{j}\in{\mathcal{K}}(C,\omega_{j})
satisfy
V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}=\varphi_{j}
and

\emptyset\not=K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{k})\to K\cap C^{-}(t_{k})\quad\mbox{as }j\to\infty,\,\mbox{for each }k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We fix a number
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Lemma
10
and the boundedness of
(b_{K_{j}})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
there is a number
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\nu_{K_{j}}^{-1}(\omega_{i})\subset C^{-}(t_{m})\quad\mbox{for }j\geq i.

For
j\geq i
, the restrictions to
\omega_{i}
(denoted by
\cdot\,\rule{0.28453pt}{5.69046pt}\rule{5.69046pt}{0.28453pt}\,\omega_{i}
) satisfy

\varphi\,\rule{0.28453pt}{5.69046pt}\rule{5.69046pt}{0.28453pt}\,\omega_{i}=\varphi_{j}\,\rule{0.28453pt}{5.69046pt}\rule{5.69046pt}{0.28453pt}\,\omega_{i}=V_{K_{j}}^{\Theta}\,\rule{0.28453pt}{5.69046pt}\rule{5.69046pt}{0.28453pt}\,\omega_{i}=V_{K_{j}\cap C^{-}(t_{m})}^{\Theta}\,\rule{0.28453pt}{5.69046pt}\rule{5.69046pt}{0.28453pt}\,\omega_{i}.

and

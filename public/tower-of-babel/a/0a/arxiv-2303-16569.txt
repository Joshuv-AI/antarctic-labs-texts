Abstract
. Given an arbitrary Coxeter system ( W , S ) (W,S) and a nonnegative integer m m , the m m -Shi arrangement of ( W , S ) (W,S) is a subarrangement of the Coxeter hyperplane arrangement of ( W , S ) (W,S) . The classical Shi arrangement ( m = 0 m=0 ) was introduced in the case of affine Weyl groups by Shi to study Kazhdan-Lusztig cells for W W . As two key results, Shi showed that each region of the Shi arrangement contains exactly one element of minimal length in W W and that the union of their inverses form a convex subset of the Coxeter complex. The set of m m -low elements in W W were introduced to study the word problem of the corresponding Artin-Tits (braid) group and they turn out to produce automata to study the combinatorics of reduced words in W W . In this article, we generalize and extend Shi’s results to any Coxeter system for any m m : (1) the set of minimal length elements of the regions in a m m -Shi arrangement is precisely the set of m m -low elements, settling a conjecture of the first and third authors in this case; (2) the union of the inverses of the ( 0 0 -)low elements form a convex subset in the Coxeter complex, settling a conjecture by the third author, Nadeau and Williams.

Introduction

Let

be a Coxeter system with length function

By stating our results in the context of chambered sets, we are able to supply simple uniform conditions whose validity determines whether the results apply in any particular situation without having to discuss the examples individually. In any case, a natural category of chambered sets forms part of rich structure observed by the first author, involving various natural monoidal categories and their actions, bicategories etc associated to Coxeter groups, and it is natural to present their definition initially in the context which motivated it and to which that structure has further applications.

Let us discuss a bit of history about the
m
-Shi arrangement. In
[
40
,
41
]
, Shi introduced the Shi arrangement
{\operatorname{Shi}}(W,S)={\operatorname{Shi}}_{0}(W,S)
in the case of irreducible affine Weyl groups to study Kazhdan-Lusztig cells for
W
. Several surprising connections with Shi arrangements for affine Weyl groups have been studied, from
ad
-nilpotent ideals of Borel subalgebras
[
11
]
to Catalan arrangements
[
5
,
3
]
; see also
[
27
]
and the references within. In
[
41
]
, Shi proves a conjecture by Carter on the number of sign-types of an affine Weyl group. In order to prove that conjecture, Shi enumerates the number of regions in
{\operatorname{Shi}}_{0}(W,S)
, which turns out to amount to
(h+1\big)^{n}
, where
h
is the Coxeter number of the underlying finite Weyl group
W_{0}
of
(W,S)
and
n
is the rank of
W_{0}
. In order to do so, Shi proves that each region of the Shi arrangement contains a unique minimal element and that the union of the chambers corresponding to the inverses of those minimal elements is a convex subset of the Euclidean space. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are a generalization of both results to arbitrary Coxeter systems. Notice that in the case of affine Coxeter systems and for
m=0
, Theorem
1.1
was proven by Chapelier-Laget and the second author in
[
12
]
, while for rank
3
and
m=0
it was proven by Charles in
[
13
]
.

After the first version of this manuscript was published on the ArXiv, Przytycki and Yau informed us that they found, in the case
m=0
, a proof of Theorem
1.1
(1) using the techniques Przytycki developed with Osajda to prove the biautomaticity of Coxeter groups
[
37
]
; they also prove in
[
39
]
that this set of minimal length elements for
{\operatorname{Shi}}_{0}(W,S)
form a Garside shadow (but not that it is equal to
L_{0}
).

As far as we know, the
m
-(extended) Shi arrangements
were defined for affine Coxeter systems by Athanasiadis
[
4
, Theorem 4.3]
. In that article, Athanasiadis proved for Coxeter systems of types

,
B
,
C
,
D
,

that the number of regions in
{\operatorname{Shi}}_{m}(W,S)
is
\big((m+1)h+1\big)^{n}
(the
generalized Catalan numbers
). Extended Shi arrangements were further studied in
[
5
,
6
,
49
,
2
]
. In his thesis
[
44

, Thiel gives a direct proof by extending Shi’s result to any

[
45
, §11]
.

Theorem
1.1
shows that Thiel’s minimal elements for

are precisely the

(Thiel

If

2.1.
Combinatorics of reduced words

We say that a word
s_{1}\cdots s_{k}
(
s_{i}\in S
) is a
reduced word for
w\in W
if
w=s_{1}\cdots s_{k}
and

k=\ell(w)
. For
u,v,w\in W
, we adopt the following terminology:

•

u
is a prefix of
w
if a reduced word for
u
can be obtained as a prefix of a reduced word for
w
;

•

v
is a suffix of
w
if a reduced word for
u
can be obtained as a suffix of a reduced word for
w
;

•

w=uv
is a
reduced product
if
\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
.

Observe that for a product
w=uv
, with
w,u,v\in W
, we have the following equivalences:
w=uv
is a reduced product if and only if
u
is a prefix of
w
, if and only if
v
is a suffix of
w
. In this case, the concatenation of a reduced word for
u
with a reduced word for
v
gives a reduced word for
w
. More generally, we say that:

•

w=u_{1}\cdots u_{k}
is a
reduced product
if
\ell(w)=\ell(u_{1})+\cdots+\ell(u_{k})
,
u_{i},w\in W
.

The empty product (the identity
e
) and the product of one element (
k=1
above) are said to be reduced products. Obviously, a reduced product
w=u_{1}\cdots u_{k}
is a reduced word if and only if
u_{i}\in S
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

2.2.
Weak and Bruhat orders

This suffix/prefix terminology is best embodied by the
weak order
. The
right weak order
is the poset
(W,\leq_{R})
defined by
u\leq_{R}w
if
u
is a prefix of
w
. The right weak order gives a natural orientation of the right Cayley graph of
(W,S)
: for
w\in W
and
s\in S
, we orient the edge
w\to ws
if
w\leq_{R}ws
. The example of the infinite dihedral Coxeter group is given in Figure
4

Similarly, the
left weak order
is the poset
(W,\leq_{L})
defined by
v\leq_{L}w
if
v
is a suffix of
w
, which induces a natural orientation of the left Cayley graph of
(W,S)
.

Finally, recall that the
Bruhat order
is the poset
(W,\leq)
defined as follows:
u\leq w
if and only if a word for
u
can be obtained as a subword of a reduced word for
w

2.3.
Root system and reflections

Recall that a quadratic space
(V,B)
is a real vector space
V

B

O_{B}(V)
is the group consisting of all invertible linear maps that preserve

B(\alpha,\alpha)\not=0

s_{\alpha}(v)=v-2\frac{B(\alpha,v)}{B(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha,

for all

is a faithful representation of

is mapped into the set of

\Delta=\{\alpha_{s}\,|\,s\in S\}
(
s=s_{\alpha_{s}}
). In a geometric representation of

, the

-orbit

is a

with

\Phi^{+}=\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi}(\Delta)
and

\Phi^{-}=-\Phi^{+}
, where
\operatorname{cone}(X)
is the set of nonnegative linear combination of vectors in

and
\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi}(X)=\operatorname{cone}(X)\cap\Phi
; see
[
31

for more details. For any
w\in W
and

ws_{\beta}w^{-1}=s_{w(\beta)}
, which induces a bijection between

and the

T=\{s_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Phi^{+}\}=\bigcup_{w\in W}wSw^{-1}.

2.4.
Depth of positive roots

The

is the function

\operatorname{dp}(\beta)=\min\{\ell(g)\mid g(\beta)\in\Delta\}.

Observe that

for all

(1)

\operatorname{dp}(s(\beta))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{dp}(\beta)-1&\textrm{if }B(\alpha_{s},\beta)>0\\
\operatorname{dp}(\beta)&\textrm{if }B(\alpha_{s},\beta)=0\\
\operatorname{dp}(\beta)+1&\textrm{if }B(\alpha_{s},\beta)<0\end{array}\right.

(1) The depth statistic was originally defined in

2.6.
Reflections and the depth of positive roots

We recall two results that provide palindromic reduced words for a reflection in
T
in relation with the depth of the associated positive root. The following first proposition goes back to Springer
[
42
, Proposition 1]
, see also
[
16
, Lemma 2.7]
; we provide a proof for convenience.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
t\in T
and chose a reduced word
t=s_{1}\cdots s_{m}
. Then there is an integer
k
such that
\ell(t)=m=2k+1
. Furthermore,
t=s_{1}\cdots s_{k}s_{k+1}s_{k}\cdots s_{1}
is a (palindromic) reduced word for
t
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
such that
t=s_{\beta}
. Since
\beta=w(\alpha_{s})
for some
w\in W
and
s\in S
, we have
t=s_{\beta}=wsw^{-1}
. Therefore
\epsilon(t)=-1
, where
\epsilon:W\to\{\pm 1\}
is the signature of
(W,S)
. Therefore
\ell(t)
is odd since
\epsilon(t)=(-1)^{\ell(t)}
. Then there is
0\leq k<m
such that
m=\ell(t)=2k+1
.

First, set
x=s_{1}\cdots s_{k+1}\leq_{R}t
. Since the word
s_{1}\cdots s_{k+1}
is reduced (as a prefix of the given reduced word for
t
), we have:

\displaystyle\ell(tx)=\ell(t^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell(s_{m}\cdots s_{1}s_{1}\cdots s_{k+1})=\ell(s_{m}\cdots s_{k+2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m-1-k=2k+1-1-k=k

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle k+1=\ell(x).

Therefore
\beta\in\Phi(x)
. Now, by Proposition
2.2
(1), there is
1\leq i\leq k+1
such that
\beta=s_{1}\cdots s_{i-1}(\alpha_{s_{i}})
. Therefore

t=s_{\beta}=s_{1}\cdots s_{i-1}s_{i}s_{i-1}\cdots s_{1},

implying

. So

and

is a reduced word for

.
∎

The next consequence relates the length of a reflection with the depth of its associated positive root.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
and
g\in W
such that
g(\beta)=\alpha_{r}\in\Delta
with

and
\ell(g)=\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
. Then
\ell(s_{\beta})=2\operatorname{dp}(\beta)+1
and the product
s_{\beta}=g^{-1}rg
is reduced.

Proof.

On the one hand, choose a reduced word
s_{\beta}=s_{1}\cdots s_{k}s_{k+1}s_{k}\cdots s_{1}
, by Proposition
2.3
. Therefore the prefix
s_{1}\cdots s_{k}s_{k+1}
is also a reduced word, hence
s_{1}\cdots s_{k}(\alpha_{s_{k+1}})\in\Phi^{+}
. Since
s_{\beta}=s_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}(\alpha_{s_{k+1}})}
, we have
\beta=s_{1}\cdots s_{k}(\alpha_{s_{k+1}})
and
\operatorname{dp}(\beta)\leq k
. On the other hand, by assumption, we have
s_{\beta}=s_{g^{-1}(\alpha_{r})}=g^{-1}rg
. So:

2k+1=\ell(s_{\beta})\leq 2\ell(g)+1=2\operatorname{dp}(\beta)+1\leq 2k+1

Therefore we have
\ell(s_{\beta})=2\operatorname{dp}(\beta)+1
and the product
s_{\beta}=g^{-1}rg
is reduced (and
\ell(g)=\operatorname{dp}(\beta)=k
).
∎

Corollary 2.5

Let
\beta\in\Phi^{+}

2.8.
Reflection subgroups and the Bruhat order

We end this section by recalling some useful facts about reflection subgroups and, in particular, about maximal dihedral reflection subgroups. We follow the exposition given in
[
24
, §2.8]

A
reflection subgroup

W^{\prime}
of
W
is a subgroup
W^{\prime}=\langle s_{\beta}\,|\,\beta\in A\rangle
generated by the reflections associated to the roots in some
A\subseteq\Phi^{+}
. Write

(2)

\Phi_{W^{\prime}}:=\{\beta\in\Phi\,|\,s_{\beta}\in W^{\prime}\}\ \textrm{ and }\ \Delta_{W^{\prime}}:=\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\,|\,\Phi(s_{\alpha})\cap\Phi_{W^{\prime}}=\{\alpha\}\}.

The first author shows in
[
16
]
that
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}
is a root system in
(V,B)
with simple system
\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
, called the
canonical simple system of
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}
. Therefore the pair
(W^{\prime},\chi(W^{\prime}))
is a Coxeter system, with
canonical simple reflections

\chi(W^{\prime}):=\{s_{\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}\},

and with corresponding positive roots
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}^{+}=\Phi_{W^{\prime}}\cap\Phi^{+}
; see also
[
20
]
(both notions depend on
(W,S)
and not just
W
). In particular:

(3)

B(\alpha,\beta)\leq 0,\ \forall\alpha\not=\beta\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}.

Observe also that
S\cap W^{\prime}\subseteq\chi(W^{\prime})
.

Coset representatives

We are now interested in decomposing elements of
W
with respect to the cosets
W^{\prime}w
for
w\in W
. The set of
minimal length right coset representatives
for
W^{\prime}
in
W
is:

X_{W^{\prime}}:=\{v\in W\,|\,\ell(s_{\beta}v)>\ell(v),\forall\beta\in\Phi_{W^{\prime}}\}=\{v\in W\,|\,\Phi(v)\cap\Phi_{W^{\prime}}=\emptyset\}.

Any coset
W^{\prime}w
in
W
contains an element of minimal length, which is in
X_{W^{\prime}}
. For an element
u\in W^{\prime}
, the inversion set
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}(u)
of
u
in
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}
satisfies
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}(u)=\Phi(u)\cap\Phi_{W^{\prime}}
; the following result is known (see
[
17
, 3.3(ii),3.4]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
W^{\prime}
be a reflection subgroup of
W
and
w\in W
. Write
w=uv
with
(u,v)\in W^{\prime}\times X_{W^{\prime}}
. We have:

(1)

\Phi(w)\cap\Phi_{W^{\prime}}=\Phi_{W^{\prime}}(u)
;

(2)

\ell(s_{\beta}w)<\ell(w)
if and only if
\ell_{W^{\prime}}(s_{\beta}u)<\ell_{W^{\prime}}(u)
for all
\beta\in\Phi_{W^{\prime}}
.

The second statement of the above proposition is referred to as
“functoriality of the Bruhat graph”
(for inclusions of reflection subgroups; see
[
17
,
18
]
). This means that there is an isomorphism of edge-labelled graph between the Bruhat graph of
W^{\prime}
and the Bruhat graph of
W
restricted to
W^{\prime}w
, for any
w\in W
; see
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
. In particular, if
\leq_{W^{\prime}}
denotes the Bruhat order on
W^{\prime}
, we have:

(4)

u_{1}\leq_{W^{\prime}}u_{2}\implies u_{1}v\leq u_{2}v,\quad\textrm{for all }u_{i}\in W^{\prime},\ v\in X_{W^{\prime}}.

The converse of this statement is not true in general; a counterexample can be seen in type
I_{2}(4)
with simple reflections
s,t
and
W^{\prime}=\{s,tst\}
.

This functoriality property implies also that the elements of
X_{W^{\prime}}
are precisely those elements
v
of
W
which are of minimum length in their coset
W^{\prime}v
, and that any element
w\in W
can be uniquely expressed in the form
w=uv
where
u\in W^{\prime}
and
v\in X_{W^{\prime}}
; note that this product is not reduced in general (it is a reduced product whenever
W^{\prime}
is a standard parabolic subgroup). Functoriality of the Bruhat graph also implies the following useful alternative characterization of
X_{W^{\prime}}
:

X_{W^{\prime}}=\{v\in W\,|\,\ell(s_{\beta}v)>\ell(v),\forall\beta\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}\}.

2.9.
Maximal dihedral reflection subgroups

A reflection subgroup
W^{\prime}
of rank
2
is well-known to be isomorphic to a dihedral group and is so called a
dihedral reflection subgroup
. This following result gives a criterion for comparing depths of roots.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
. Assume there is a dihedral reflection subgroup
W^{\prime}
such that such that
\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{W^{\prime}}\setminus\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
, then
\operatorname{dp}(\alpha)<\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
,
\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{W^{\prime}}\setminus\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
and
(W^{\prime},\chi(W^{\prime}))
is a Coxeter system of rank
2
, it is known that
s_{\alpha}<_{W^{\prime}}s_{\beta}
in the Bruhat order on
W^{\prime}
. By Eq.(
4
) (functoriality of Bruhat graphs), we have
s_{\alpha}<s_{\beta}
, since
s_{\alpha},s_{\beta}\in W^{\prime}
and
e\in X_{W^{\prime}}
. In particular,
\ell(s_{\alpha})<\ell(s_{\beta})
. By Proposition
2.4
, we conclude that
\operatorname{dp}(\alpha)<\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
.
∎

A dihedral reflection subgroup
W^{\prime}
is a
maximal dihedral reflection subgroup
if it is not contained in any other dihedral reflection subgroup but itself. This is equivalent to the following condition on the root subsystem
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}
:

\Phi_{W^{\prime}}=\mathbb{R}\Delta_{W^{\prime}}\cap\Phi.

Recall that for
X\subseteq V
,
\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi}(X)=\operatorname{cone}(X)\cap\Phi
. The following useful result gives the form of inversion sets in maximal dihedral reflection subgroup.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
W^{\prime}
be a maximal dihedral reflection subgroup. The inversion set of
u\in W^{\prime}
,
u\not=e
, is of the form

\Phi_{W^{\prime}}(u)=\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi}(\alpha,\beta)
with
\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{W^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that inversion sets in dihedral groups are of the form: for
u\in W^{\prime}
,
u\not=e
,
\Phi_{W^{\prime}}(u)=\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi_{W^{\prime}}}(\alpha,\beta)
, with
\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}_{W^{\prime}}
. By definition of maximal dihedral reflection subgroups we have:

\displaystyle\Phi_{W^{\prime}}(u)=\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi_{W^{\prime}}}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{cone}(\alpha,\beta)\cap\Phi_{W^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{cone}(\alpha,\beta)\cap\mathbb{R}\Delta_{W^{\prime}}\cap\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{cone}(\alpha,\beta)\cap\Phi,\ \textrm{ since $\operatorname{cone}(\alpha,\beta)\subseteq\mathbb{R}\Delta_{W^{\prime}}$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{cone}_{\Phi}(\alpha,\beta).\qed

Any dihedral reflection subgroup is contained in a unique maximal dihedral reflection subgroup.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
such that
\mathbb{R}\alpha\not=\mathbb{R}\beta
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}
the unique maximal dihedral reflection subgroup containing the reflections
s_{\alpha}
and
s_{\beta}
. For simplicity, if
s=s_{\alpha}\in T
and

, we write
\mathcal{M}_{s,t}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Remark 2.11
.

(1)

We have
\Phi_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}}=(\mathbb{R}\alpha\oplus\mathbb{R}\beta)\cap\Phi
.

(2)

The finite maximal dihedral reflection subgroups of

are precisely the finite parabolic subgroups of rank

distinct such that the order

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

\beta=-w(\alpha_{s})\in\Phi^{R}(w)
. Since

, there is

is a reduced product. So

Remark 3.10

(On computing the short inversion graph)
.

Let
w\in W
. The computation of
\Phi^{1}(w)

verifies

. However, computing the short inversion graph of

is more difficult. In this article, we used SageMath
[

]
and the definition to compute the short inversion graph of

, then:

(i)

If

is infinite (see for instance
[

Our algorithm is not efficient and can be improved. In §

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
w\in W
. The map
\alpha\mapsto-w^{-1}(\alpha)
induces an isomorphism between the digraph
(\Phi^{1}(w),\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w})
and
(\Phi^{1}(w^{-1}),\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w^{-1}})^{\operatorname{op}}
. In particular
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
if and only if
-w^{-1}(\beta)\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w^{-1}}-w^{-1}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

The map
\alpha\mapsto-w^{-1}(\alpha)
is a bijection between
\Phi(w)
and
\Phi(w^{-1})
; indeed:

\Phi(w^{-1})=\Phi^{+}\cap w^{-1}(\Phi^{-})=w^{-1}(w(\Phi^{+})\cap\Phi^{-})=-w^{-1}(w(\Phi^{-})\cap\Phi^{+})=-w^{-1}(\Phi(w)).

Now
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
if and only if
-w^{-1}(\beta)\in\Phi^{1}(w^{-1})
since

\ell(s_{\beta}w)-\ell(w)=\ell(w^{-1}s_{\beta})-\ell(w^{-1})=\ell(s_{-w^{-1}(\beta)}w^{-1})-\ell(w^{-1}).

Since the role of
w
and
w^{-1}
are interchangeable, it remains only to show that if
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
then
-w^{-1}(\beta)\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w^{-1}}-w^{-1}(\alpha)
. Let
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
. Write
W^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}
, then it is well-known that
\mathcal{M}_{-w^{-1}(\alpha),-w^{-1}(\beta)}=w^{-1}W^{\prime}w
. By definition, we have to show that
-w^{-1}(\alpha)\notin\Delta_{w^{-1}W^{\prime}w}
. We know that
\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
and
\beta\notin\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
by definition. Write
\Delta_{W^{\prime}}=\{\alpha,\gamma\}
, then there is
a,b>0
such that
\beta=a\alpha+b\gamma
. Therefore
\alpha=1/a\beta+b/a(-\gamma)
. Notice that

by Proposition
3.2
(1). Hence
w^{-1}(\gamma)=-w^{-1}(-\gamma)\in\Phi^{+}\cap\Phi_{w^{-1}W^{\prime}w}=\Phi^{+}_{w^{-1}W^{\prime}w}
and

-w^{-1}(\alpha)=1/a(-w^{-1}(\beta))+b/a(-w^{-1}(-\gamma)),

with

3.4.
Short inversion posets

For
w\in W
, we define the relation
\preceq_{w}
to be the transitive and reflexive closure of
\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}
, which turns out to be a partial order on
\Phi^{1}(w)
.

Proposition 3.13
.

The relation
\preceq_{w}
is a partial order on
\Phi^{1}(w)
. Moreover, for any reduced word
w=s_{1}\cdots s_{k}
consider the following total order
\leq
on
\Phi(w)
:

\alpha_{s_{1}}<s_{1}(\alpha_{s_{2}})<\cdots<s_{1}\cdots s_{k-1}(\alpha_{s_{k}})

Then
\alpha\preceq_{w}\beta
implies
\alpha\leq\beta
and
\operatorname{dp}(\alpha)\leq\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
.

Proof.

By definition of
\preceq_{w}
, one just has to show that
\preceq_{w}
is antisymmetric. This is a direct consequence of the ‘moreover’ statement. Notice first that the statement on the depth statistic is a direct consequence of transitivity and Proposition
3.8
.

Let
w=s_{1}\cdots s_{k}
be a reduced word and define the following total order on
\Phi(w)
:

\alpha_{s_{1}}<s_{1}(\alpha_{s_{2}})<\cdots<s_{1}\cdots s_{k-1}(\alpha_{s_{k}})

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
such that
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
. We show that
\alpha<\beta
. By definition
\alpha\in\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}}
and
\beta\notin\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}}
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}}
the other simple root. So
\alpha^{\prime}\notin\Phi(w)
by Proposition
3.2
.
Let
1\leq i\leq k
such that
\beta=s_{1}\cdots s_{i-1}(\alpha_{s_{i}})
. Write
u=s_{1}\cdots s_{i}\leq_{R}w
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}\notin\Phi(u)
, we must have
\alpha\in\Phi(u)
by Proposition
3.2
again. Therefore there is
j<i
such that
\alpha=s_{1}\cdots s_{j-1}(\alpha_{s_{j}})
and so
\alpha<\beta
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

(1) The relation
\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}
is not the cover relation for
\preceq_{w}
. In fact, if
\beta
covers
\alpha
in
(\Phi^{1}(w),\preceq_{w})
, then
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
but the converse is not necessarily true; see for instance Example
3.15
below.

(2) In the proof of Proposition
3.13
, the total order on
\Phi(w)
is in fact the restriction of an
admissible order
on
\Phi^{+}
to
\Phi(w)
. Admissible orders on
\Phi^{+}
are in bijection to
reflection orders
, which plays a role in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory; see for instance
[
8
, §5.2]
or
[
22
, §2.1]
for more details and references.

and

Remark 3.16
.

Let

. In
(\Phi^{1}(w),\preceq_{w})
, the elements of
\Phi^{L}(w)
are minimal and therefore incomparable. By Proposition
3.12
, the elements of
\Phi^{R}(w)
are maximal in
(\Phi^{1}(w),\preceq_{w})
, and hence also incomparable.

We state now the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
w\in W
. For the poset
(\Phi^{1}(w),\preceq_{w})
, the minimal elements are the left-descent roots in
\Phi^{L}(w)
and the maximal elements are the right-descent roots in
\Phi^{R}(w)
. In other words, for any
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
there is
\alpha\in\Phi^{L}(w)
and
\gamma\in\Phi^{R}(w)
such that
\alpha\preceq_{w}\beta\preceq_{w}\gamma
.

We dedicate the remainder of this section to the proof of this theorem.

3.5.
Short inversions and reduced products

The following two results are critical to prove Theorem
3.17
.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
x,w\in W
and
\alpha,\gamma\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\Phi(x)
.

(1)

We have
\alpha\in\Phi(xw)
if and only if
x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi(w)
.

(2)

If
xw
is a reduced product and
\alpha\in\Phi^{1}(xw)
then
x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi^{1}(w)
.

(3)

If
xw
is a reduced product and
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{xw}\gamma
then
x^{-1}(\alpha)\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}x^{-1}(\gamma)
.

Proof.

(1). First, notice that by assumption
x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi^{+}
and

(xw)^{-1}(\alpha)=w^{-1}x^{-1}(\alpha)=w^{-1}(x^{-1}(\alpha)).

Therefore:

\alpha\in\Phi(xw)\iff w^{-1}(x^{-1}(\alpha))\in\Phi^{-}\iff x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi(w).

(2). Assume that
\alpha\in\Phi^{1}(xw)
. By (1) we have
x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi(w)
. Therefore

\ell(s_{x^{-1}(\alpha)}w)=\ell(x^{-1}s_{\alpha}xw)<\ell(w),

and
\ell(x^{-1}s_{\alpha}xw)\leq\ell(w)-1
. Now, by classical properties of the length function we obtain:

\ell(x^{-1}s_{\alpha}xw)\geq\ell(xw)-\ell(x^{-1}s_{\alpha})\geq\ell(xw)-\ell(x^{-1})-1=\ell(w)-1;

the last equality is a consequence of
xw
being a reduced product. Therefore
\ell(s_{x^{-1}(\alpha)}w)=\ell(x^{-1}s_{\alpha}xw)=\ell(w)-1
and
x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi^{1}(w)
.

(3). Set
W^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\gamma}
. So
\mathcal{M}_{x^{-1}(\alpha),x^{-1}(\gamma)}=x^{-1}W^{\prime}x
. Thanks to (2), we just have to prove that
x^{-1}(\gamma)
is not a simple root of
x^{-1}W^{\prime}x
. Since
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{xw}\gamma
,
\alpha,\gamma\in\Phi^{1}(xw)
by definition and
\alpha\in\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
by Proposition
3.2
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}
be the other simple root in
\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
. Since
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{xw}\gamma
,
\gamma\notin\Delta_{W^{\prime}}
therefore
\alpha^{\prime}\notin\Phi(xw)
by Proposition
3.2
(1). Since
xw
is reduced,
\Phi(x)\subseteq\Phi(xw)
by Proposition
2.2
, and so
\alpha^{\prime}\notin\Phi(x)
. Therefore
x^{-1}(\alpha^{\prime})\in\Phi^{+}
. So
x^{-1}(\Delta_{W^{\prime}})\subseteq\Phi^{+}
and therefore
x^{-1}(\Delta_{W^{\prime}})=\Delta_{x^{-1}W^{\prime}x}
by maximality of
W^{\prime}
. Hence
x^{-1}(\gamma)\notin\Delta_{x^{-1}W^{\prime}x}
and
x^{-1}(\alpha)\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}x^{-1}(\gamma)
.
∎

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
x,w\in W
and
\beta\in\Phi(w)
. Write
w^{\prime}=s_{\beta}w
and assume that
xw
and
xw^{\prime}
are reduced products. Then
x(\beta)\in\Phi(xw)
. Furthermore

\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
if and only if
x(\beta)\in\Phi^{1}(xw)
.

Proof.

Write
\alpha=x(\beta)
. Since
xw
is a reduced product, we have
\Phi(xw)=\Phi(x)\sqcup x(\Phi(w))
by Proposition
2.2
. Hence
\alpha\in\Phi(xw)\setminus\Phi(x)
. By Proposition
3.18
, we have
\alpha=x(\beta)\in\Phi^{1}(xw)
implies
\beta=x^{-1}(\alpha)\in\Phi^{1}(w)
. Conversely, if
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
, then
\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(s_{\beta}w)=\ell(x^{-1}s_{\alpha}xw)=\ell(w)-1
. Therefore, since
xw
and
xw^{\prime}
are reduced products, we have:

\ell(xw^{\prime})=\ell(x)+\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(x)+\ell(w)-1=\ell(xw)-1.

Finally, we obtain:

\ell(s_{\alpha}xw)=\ell(xx^{-1}s_{\alpha}xw)=\ell(xs_{\beta}w)=\ell(xw^{\prime})=\ell(xw)-1,

which means that
\alpha=x(\beta)\in\Phi^{1}(xw)
.
∎

3.6.
Some edges in short inversion graphs

The key to prove Theorem
3.17
is to explicitly construct, for
w\in W
and for each
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)\setminus\Phi^{L}(w)
, a short inversion
\alpha\in\Phi^{1}(w)
such that
\alpha\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
. For such a
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)\setminus\Phi^{L}(w)
, the idea consists in considering
g\in W
such that
g(\beta)\in\Delta
and
\ell(g)=\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
, which exists by definition of the depth statistic. The first proposition below handles the case where
D_{L}(w)\cap D_{R}(g)
is not empty.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
w\in W
and
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
with
\operatorname{dp}(\beta)>0
. Then
\alpha_{s}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
for all

such that
\operatorname{dp}(s(\beta))<\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
. In this case,
\operatorname{dp}(\alpha_{s})=0<\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
.

Proof.

Let
s\in D_{L}(w)
such that
\operatorname{dp}(s(\beta))<\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
. In particular,
\alpha_{s}\in\Phi^{L}(w)\subseteq\Phi_{1}(w)
and
\alpha_{s}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{s},\beta}}
, since
S\cap\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{s},\beta}\subseteq\chi(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{s},\beta})
. Since
\operatorname{dp}(s(\beta))<\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
, we have
B(\alpha_{s},\beta)>0
by Eq. (
1
). So
\beta\notin\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{s},\beta}}
by Eq (
3
); hence
\alpha_{s}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\beta
.
∎

Example 3.21

(Continuation of Example
3.9
)
.

Here
w=312121321
in
W
of type

. The relation
\alpha_{3}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\dot{\prec}$\cr\cr}}}_{w}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}

.

The second case is more subtle: assume now that
sw
is a reduced product for

. So there is

such that
xw
is a reduced product; take such a maximal

with respect to
\leq_{L}
. We have to deal with two cases:
x=g
and
x<_{L}g
. Before dealing with these cases, we need a lemma.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
w\in W
and
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)
and write
w^{\prime}=s_{\beta}w
. Let
g\in W
such that
g(\beta)\in\Delta
and
\ell(g)=\operatorname{dp}(\beta)
. For
any
x\leq_{L}g
such that
xw
is a reduced product, the product
xw^{\prime}
is also reduced.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\ell(x)
. If
\ell(x)=0
there is nothing to show. Now let
s\in D_{L}(x)
and write
x=sx^{\prime}
, which is a reduced product. Notice that
x^{\prime}<_{L}x=sx^{\prime}\leq_{L}g
. On one hand, by Corollary
2.5
, we have:

(\star)\qquad\ell(s_{x(\beta)})=\ell(ss_{x^{\prime}(\beta)}s)=\ell(s_{x^{\prime}(\beta)})-2.

On the other hand, since
x^{\prime}w
is a suffix of
xw
,
x^{\prime}w
is also a reduced product. Therefore,
x^{\prime}w^{\prime}
is a reduced product by induction. Assume by contradiction that
xw^{\prime}=sx^{\prime}w^{\prime}
is not a reduced product, that is,
sx^{\prime}w^{\prime}<x^{\prime}w^{\prime}
. We use Lemma
2.12

z=sx^{\prime}w^{\prime}=xw^{\prime}
and
t=x^{\prime}s_{\beta}x^{\prime-1}=s_{x^{\prime}(\beta)}
. We have therefore:

z=sx^{\prime}w^{\prime}<sz=x^{\prime}w^{\prime}<x^{\prime}w=tx^{\prime}w^{\prime}=tsz<xw=stsz.

Since
x^{\prime}w
and
x^{\prime}w^{\prime}
are reduced products and
\beta\in\Phi^{1}(w)

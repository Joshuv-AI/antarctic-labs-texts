Global weak solutions in nonlinear 3D thermoelasticity
Tomasz Cieślak, Boris Muha, Srđan Trifunović

Abstract
. Here we study a nonlinear thermoelasticity hyperbolic-parabolic system describing the balance of momentum and internal energy of a heat-conducting elastic body, preserving the positivity of temperature. So far, no global existence results in such a natural case were available. Our result is obtained by using thermodynamically justified variables which allow us to obtain an equivalent system in which the internal energy balance is replaced with entropy balance. For this system, a concept of weak solution with defect measure is introduced, which satisfies entropy inequality instead of balance and has a positive temperature almost everywhere. Then, the global existence, consistency and weak-strong uniqueness are shown in the cases where heat capacity and heat conductivity are both either constant or non-constant. Let us point out that this is the first result concerning global existence for large initial data in nonlinear thermoelasticity where the model is in full accordance with the laws of thermodynamics.

2.
Comparison with known results and discussion

In this section, we compare our result with the known literature. The study of existence of solutions to systems of thermoelasticity dates back to 1960s, however the initial work was related to the linear simplification (linear thermoelasticity is addressed in detail shortly after). Nonlinear systems of elasticity require more sophisticated methods. Under some particular choices of nonlinear couplings
b(\theta,\mathbf{u}_{tx})
replacing the term
-\mu\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}
in the second equation of (
1
), smooth, classical solutions were known in the literature in the spatially one-dimensional setting locally in time for any data and globally for small data. This was investigated by various authors
and one of the pioneer works was Slemrod’s paper
[
34
]
. Still, assumptions required to guarantee existence of smooth solutions with nonnegative temperature were so specific, that the simplest system (
1
) was not covered. See the discussion in
[
10
]
and results in
[
5
,
18
,
27
]
. The classical smooth local-in-time solutions with nonnegative temperature were obtained recently in
[
10
]
. Let us also recall earlier similar results in
[
20
,
28
]
. Still, even in 1D case, the problem of existence of global-in-time solutions is widely opened. In 1D, one could mention the global existence of measure-valued solutions in
[
10
, Theorem 2]
. The regularity of such a solution is very weak and there seems no hope in that case for any sort of weak-strong uniqueness.

Recently, a notable attention is being given to problems in adiabatic thermoelasticity – the system in which the heat flux is neglected, a valid approximation in thermodynamical systems in which processes happen in a small time interval and thus not allow for heat flux to take effect. From a mathematical point of view, this is a purely hyperbolic system. Adiabatic thermoviscoelastic system was studied by C. Christoforou and A. Tzavaras in
[
7
]
. They use the method of relative entropy in order to prove the zero viscosity limit, thus going from thermoviscoelasticity with heat flux to adiabatic thermoelastic system. Note that the existence of viscous solutions is not shown. Their next results obtained together with Galanopoulou in
[
8
,
9
]
concern some adiabatic thermoelastic problems. In the first paper, a measure-valued solution to the thermoelastic problem is obtained. It requires the use of Young measures as well as an embedding of the considered system into the hyperbolic conservation laws framework. It requires some structural assumptions (see
[
8
, Remark 1, p. 6182]
). It seems that the solution obtained in
[
9
]
, again a measure-valued one, requires similar strong structural assumptions. The studies of weak-strong uniqueness of the weak solutions obtained in the above papers were performed in
[
17
]
.

Let us comment that the question concerning regularity of the obtained solution is not clear. One cannot a priori exclude the finite-time blowup of first derivatives, see
[
13
]
.

Finally, let us mention that related system of thermoviscoelasticity was also extensively studied, see e.g.
[
12
,
29
]
,
[
31
, Chapter 12]
and references within. However, in this case the system is of parabolic - parabolic type and therefore analysis of such system is much different form the analysis presented here.

2.1.
Linear thermoelasticity

Under the assumption that
\theta
is close to a state
\theta_{0}=const
, the system of equations
\eqref{system0}_{1,2}
can be reduced to a linear one (see for instance
[

):

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{tt}=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\mathbf{u}-\mu\theta I),\\
\theta_{t}-\Delta\theta=-\mu\theta_{0}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}.\end{cases}

This is a well-known system which has been studied extensively by many authors. To the best of our knowledge the first result was due to Dafermos
[
11
]
, followed by plethora of other works, see e.g.
[
19
]
,
[
22
, Chapter 7]
,
[
23
, Chapter 3]
,
[
24
]
, and references within.

Let us point out some important properties of
(
14
)
. First of all, the equation
\eqref{linearTh}_{2}
is actually an entropy equation, the entropy is
\theta+\mu\theta_{0}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}
, the entropy flux is
-\nabla\theta
, while the entropy production is zero. To obtain the corresponding heat equation, one multiplies
\eqref{linearTh}_{2}
with
\theta
which gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\theta^{2})_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta(\theta^{2})+|\nabla\theta|^{2}=-\mu\theta_{0}\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}.

From a physical point of view, there are three major issues here - the entropy production is zero, there is a dissipation coming from temperature
|\nabla\theta|^{2}
and the system allows temperature to be negative (the maximum principle isn’t satisfied). Moreover, this model predicts that temperature will oscillate around (or simply stay close to) a stationary state, which is not physical, since mechanical energy tends to irreversibly transfer to heat, thus increasing it in the process.

While this linear system has nice mathematical properties and serves as a good model for the study of stability of thermoelastic bodies, it is physical relevant only in certain regimes under quite restrictive assumptions. There have been efforts throughout the last four decades to study more nonlinear models, but most of them are restricted to special 1D cases or the developed theory requires special forms of nonlinearities. In this paper, we are finally able to bridge this gap and introduce a meaningful notion of a weak solution for a model in nonlinear thermoelasticity, which is in full agreement with the laws of thermodynamics. Moreover, these solutions are global-in-time for large data, coincide with the regular ones (provided such exists) and are also consistent in a sense that if they are smooth, they satisfy the equations pointwise. Finally, let us point out that the results also hold in lower dimensions 1 and 2.

3.
Physical derivation

The present section is devoted to the physical derivation of our model as well as some related models. When deriving the model, we obtain candidates for natural variables in which a model should be examined. This way we are led to the formal version of the important estimates on the one hand, and to the natural framework in which the weak solution is defined on the other hand.

3.1.
Derivation of the model

We start with a system of equations modeling the balance of momentum and internal energy

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\equiv 1,\quad\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\theta,\nabla\mathbf{u}),\quad e=e(\theta,\nabla\mathbf{u}),\quad\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}(\theta,\nabla\theta).

3.2.
Prescribing the constitutive functions

First, we will assume that the deformation is small, which leads to the following assumption

\displaystyle e_{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u})=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}.

Next, it is standard to assume that the stress due to heat expansion is of the following linear form
\theta s_{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u})_{\mathbf{F}}=\mu\theta I
, where
\mu>0
, which directly leads to

\displaystyle s_{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u})=\mu\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},

since
(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})_{\mathbf{F}}=I
. Note that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}
represents the linearization of Jacobian and thus approximates the volume, which is consistent with the natural increase of the entropy with the volume. Next, the internal energy flux satisfies the Fourier law

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}(\theta,\nabla\theta)=-\kappa(\theta)\nabla\theta,

where
\kappa(\theta)>0
is the heat conductivity. Finally, we assume that the body is fixed at the boundary and the internal energy flux through the boundary is zero:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\partial_{n}\theta=0,\quad\mbox{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega.

Supplementing with initial data, this leads to the following problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{tt}=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\mathbf{u}-\mu\theta I),&\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
c_{V}(\theta)\theta_{t}-\nabla\cdot(\kappa(\theta)\nabla\theta)=-\mu\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t},&\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\mathbf{u}=0,~\partial_{n}\theta=0,&\quad\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{u}_{0},~\mathbf{u}_{t}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{v}_{0},~\theta(0,\cdot)=\theta_{0},&\end{cases}

where
c_{V}(\theta):=e_{1}(\theta)_{\theta}
is the heat capacity. It remains to prescribe
c_{V}(\theta)
and
\kappa(\theta)
.

3.2.1.
High temperature model

For many homogeneous materials, the heat capacity is known to be almost constant at high temperatures due to the Dulong-Petit law (see
[
32
, Section 2]
). On the other hand, heat conductivity tends to show little change at high temperatures in many relevant materials
3
3

3

For example crystals and glasses.
(see
[
30
]
). This leads to the first model:

\displaystyle c_{V}(\theta)=1,\quad\kappa(\theta)=1.

3.2.2.
Low temperature model

At low temperatures, the heat capacity for non-magnetic materials increases as
\theta^{3}
due to the Debye law (see
[
32
, Section 2.2]
). Moreover, the heat conductivity at low temperatures also tends to increase as
\theta^{3}
(see
[
2
, Pages 504, 505]
). To keep the result general, the following is chosen:

(34)

\displaystyle c_{V}(\theta)=1+\alpha\theta^{\alpha-1},\quad\kappa(\theta)=1+\theta^{\beta},

where
\alpha>1
and
\beta>0
. Here, the constant part was added due to mathematical reasons. In particular, the constant in
c_{V}(\theta)
ensures that
\ln\theta
will be bounded, so the temperature will not vanish a.e, while the constant part in
\kappa(\theta)
is added to provide enough control over
\ln\theta
. The conditions
\alpha>1
and
\beta>0
are chosen so that
c_{V}(\theta)
and
\kappa(\theta)
are growing functions.

Remark 4.1

Note that we are actually using the heat equation in approximate problem, while later we will switch to the entropy equation in order to pass to the limit

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
({\bf u}^{n},\theta^{n})\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;V_{n})\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
be a solution to the approximate problem given by Definition

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\theta^{n}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|{\bf u}^{n}_{t}(t)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla{\bf u}^{n}(t)|^{2}=E^{n}_{0},\quad\text{ for a.a. }t\in[0,T],

where

is the energy of the initial data projected onto finite-dimensional subspace

Proof.

We take
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}={\bf u}_{t}^{n}
in (
35

\phi=1
in (

. Notice that the terms on the right hand sides cancel.
∎

First we prove a version of the standard maximum principle result for parabolic equations which we will use to prove strict positivity of the temperature.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
and
\theta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
be the solution to the following parabolic problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\theta_{t}-\Delta\theta=-a\theta,&\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{n}\theta=0,&\quad\text{ on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\theta(0)=\theta_{0}^{n}>0.&\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\theta(t,{\bf x})\geq\left(\min_{{\bf x}\in\Omega}\theta_{0}^{n}({\bf x})\right)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\|a(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}ds\right).

Proof.

We define
C_{0}=\min_{{\bf x}\in\Omega}\theta_{0}^{n}({\bf x})
and

\displaystyle d(t,{\bf x})=\theta(t,{\bf x})-C_{0}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\|a(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}ds\right).

By straightforward calculation

satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle d_{t}-\Delta d+ad=C_{0}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\|a(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}ds\right)\underbrace{(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}-a)}_{\geq 0}.

Therefore

(38)

\displaystyle d_{t}-\Delta d+ad\geq 0,\quad d(0)\geq 0.

We multiply (
38

to get:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|d^{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla d^{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq-a\|d^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Since
d(0)\geq 0
, we have
d^{-}(0)=0
and therefore by the Gronwall inequality we get

which finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 1
.

For every

there exists a solution

to the approximate problem in the sense of Definition

\theta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

Proof.

We prove existence by using Schaefer’s fixed point theorem. We define the fixed point set:

X_{n}=C^{1}([0,T];V_{n}).

We equip

with the following norm:
\|{\bf u}\|_{X_{n}}=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\{\|{\bf u}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\|{\bf u}_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\}

The fixed point mapping
\mathcal{A}
is defined in the following way. Let

. We define
\theta({\bf u})
by solving (

on the right hand side. More precisely,
\theta({\bf u}^{n})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta({\bf u})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\theta({\bf u})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mu\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\theta({\bf u})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

However, since

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{n}\|{\bf u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Note that the constant in (

\displaystyle\|\theta({\bf u})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C_{n}(\|{\bf u}\|_{X_{n}}),

is a monotone function. Now,
\mathcal{A}{\bf u}

Continuity of

5.1.1.

Before we start, let us point out that in order to solve

[

[

Now, similarly as in previous section, we introduce a basis

. The problem of interest is the following:

Definition 5.1

(Approximate problem)
.

We say that

where

and

and

Lemma 5.2

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in C^{2}(0,T;V_{n})
and
\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}
be a sub and super solutions to the equation
\eqref{APP:nonlin}_{2}
, that is they satisfy
\eqref{APP:nonlin}_{2}
where
"="
is replaced with
"\leq"
and
"\geq"
, respectively. Denote

then, one has

First, it is easy to see that the following inequality holds

\displaystyle(e_{1}(\underline{\theta})-e_{1}(\overline{\theta}))_{t}-\Delta(K(\underline{\theta})-K(\overline{\theta}))

\displaystyle\leq-\mu(\underline{\theta}-\overline{\theta})\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}-\delta(\underline{\theta}^{2}-\overline{\theta}^{2})+\delta(\underline{\theta}^{-2}-\overline{\theta}^{-2}).

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}^{+}(a):=\begin{cases}0,&\quad a\leq 0,\\
1,&\quad a>0,\end{cases}

we multiply
(

)
with

to obtain

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}^{+}(e_{1}(\underline{\theta})-e_{1}(\overline{\theta}))\Big[(e_{1}(\underline{\theta})-e_{1}(\overline{\theta}))_{t}-\Delta(K(\underline{\theta})-K(\overline{\theta}))\Big]

\delta

\mathbf{u}
and upper and lower bounds for
\underline{\theta}
and
\overline{\theta}

are all increasing, one has

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}^{+}(e_{1}(\underline{\theta})-e_{1}(\overline{\theta}))={\rm sgn}^{+}(K(\underline{\theta})-K(\overline{\theta}))={\rm sgn}^{+}(\underline{\theta}-\overline{\theta}),

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}^{+}(e_{1}(\underline{\theta})-e_{1}(\overline{\theta}))\big[e_{1}(\underline{\theta})-e_{1}(\overline{\theta})\big]_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-{\rm sgn}^{+}(K(\underline{\theta})-K(\overline{\theta}))\Delta\big[K(\underline{\theta})-K(\overline{\theta})\big]

, and noticing that for any function

that

(

)

5.1.3.
Solving the approximate problem

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

Proof.

Before we start, in order to write
\eqref{APP:nonlin}_{2}
as a quasilinear parabolic PDE, we divide

by

to obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{t}-\frac{(1+\theta^{\beta})}{1+\alpha\theta^{\alpha-1}}\Delta\theta+\frac{\mu\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}-\beta|\nabla\theta|^{2}\theta^{\beta-1}+\delta\theta^{2}-\delta\theta^{-2}}{1+\alpha\theta^{\alpha-1}}=0.

Now, for any
\omega>0
, in order to ensure uniform parabolicity (at least for a fixed
\omega
), we introduce

\displaystyle f_{\omega}(x):=\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+\omega^{2}}}{1+\omega\sqrt{x^{2}+\omega^{2}}},

and use it to approximate some functions in the equation. This will be denoted with the notation
[\cdot]_{\omega}:=f_{\omega}(\cdot)
(for example
[\theta]_{\omega}=f_{\omega}(\theta)
). Note that
\frac{\omega}{1+\omega^{2}}\leq f_{\omega}(x)<\frac{1}{\omega}
and

\displaystyle-1<f_{\omega}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\omega^{2}}(1+\omega\sqrt{x^{2}+\omega^{2}})}<1,

for all

. We aim to solve the following system in the classical sense:

(65)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\theta_{t}-a(\theta)\Delta\theta+b(t,x,\theta,\nabla\theta)=0,\\
&a(\theta)\partial_{n}\theta=0,\\
&\theta(0,\cdot)=\theta_{0,\omega}^{n},\end{cases}

where
\theta_{0,\omega}^{n}
is a regularization of
\theta_{0}^{n}
and

\displaystyle a(\theta):=\frac{[1+\langle\theta\rangle_{\omega}^{\beta}]_{\omega}}{[1+\alpha\langle\theta\rangle_{\omega}^{\alpha-1}]_{\omega}},

\displaystyle b(t,x,\theta,\nabla\theta):=

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\mu\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}(t,x)-\beta|\nabla\theta|^{2}f_{\omega}^{\prime}(1+\langle\theta\rangle_{\omega}^{\beta})(\langle\theta\rangle_{\omega}^{\beta})^{\prime}+\delta\theta^{2}-\delta\frac{1}{\theta^{2}+\omega^{2}}}{[1+\alpha\langle\theta\rangle_{\omega}^{\alpha-1}]_{\omega}},

with

being the regularization of
x\mapsto|x|^{s}
, for
s>0
. Since the functions
a
and
b
satisfy all the necessary properties, the classical solution to this problem follows by the classical quasilinear parabolic theory (see
[
21
, Chapter VI, Theorem 7.1]
or
[
16
, Theorem 10.24]
). Now, by multiplying
\eqref{app:system}_{1}
with
[1+\alpha\langle\theta\rangle_{\omega}^{\alpha-1}]_{\omega}
, one has

\displaystyle e_{\omega}(\theta)_{t}-\Delta K_{\omega}(\theta)+\mu\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}+\delta\theta^{2}-\delta\frac{1}{\theta^{2}+\omega^{2}}=0,

where

\displaystyle e_{\omega}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}[1+\alpha\langle x\rangle_{\omega}^{\alpha-1}]_{\omega}\ dx,\quad K_{\omega}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}[1+\langle x\rangle_{\omega}^{\beta}]_{\omega}\ dx.

This equation has the same structure as the original one
\eqref{APP:nonlin}_{2}
and allows us to prove the comparison principle and the estimates in the same way as in lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
. Passing to the limit in

, we obtain a solution in

∎

\displaystyle(\theta^{n})^{\alpha-1}\to\theta^{\alpha-1},\quad\text{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Moreover, from
(
67
)
and
(
68
)
, one has that
\ln\theta^{n}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
and
\nabla\ln\theta\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, so by imbedding
\ln\theta^{n}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
which gives us

\displaystyle\ln\theta^{n}\to\ln\theta,\quad\text{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

The above two convergences give us

\displaystyle s_{1}(\theta^{n})\to s_{1}(\theta),\quad\text{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Next, since
\nabla\ln\theta^{n}
and
\nabla((\theta^{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}})
are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, one has

\displaystyle\nabla\ln\theta^{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla\ln\theta\text{ and }\nabla((\theta^{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}})\rightharpoonup\nabla(\theta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}),\quad\text{in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

On the other hand, due to
(
67
)
and
(
69
)
, by interpolation, one can conclude that
(\theta^{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\in L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, for some
p>2
, so

\displaystyle(\theta^{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\to\theta^{\frac{\beta}{2}},\quad\text{in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Combining the previous two convergences, one has

\displaystyle\nabla\ln\theta^{n}+(\theta^{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\nabla((\theta^{n})^{\frac{\beta}{2}})=\frac{\kappa(\theta^{n})\nabla\theta^{n}}{\theta^{n}}\rightharpoonup\frac{\kappa(\theta)\nabla\theta}{\theta},\quad\text{in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega).

Finally, due to
(
68
)
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\delta\theta\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{n}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\delta\frac{1}{(\theta^{n})^{2}}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\delta^{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{(\theta^{n})^{3}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{3}},

so
\delta\theta^{n}
and
\delta\frac{1}{(\theta^{n})^{2}}
vanish as
n\to\infty
. Thus, we can pass to the limit in the approximate entropy balance equation
(
57
)
and the total energy balance
(
58
)
and conclude the desired result. Note that the
\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\theta^{n})^{2}
is positive, so it doesn’t affect the limiting energy inequality and therefore doesn’t need to vanish.

Consistency

5.4.
Weak-strong uniqueness

For the sake of generality of the proof, we will restrain from writing the explicit forms of functions
e_{1},s_{1}
and
\kappa
throughout the majority of the proof, and only use their properties when they are needed. As in Section
4.3
, the following proof also follows the ideas from
[
15
]
.

Now, let us introduce the
relative entropy
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\Theta):=e_{1}(\theta)-e_{1}(\Theta)-\Theta(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\Theta))

\displaystyle=\theta+\theta^{\alpha}-\Theta-\Theta^{\alpha}-\Theta\Big(\ln\theta-\ln\Theta+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(\theta^{\alpha-1}-\Theta^{\alpha-1}\big)\Big),

and for any smooth functions
\mathbf{U}\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and
\Theta\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)
with
\Theta>0
,
\partial_{n}\Theta=0
on
(0,T)\times\partial\Omega
, the
relative entropy inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\Theta)(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}_{t}-\mathbf{U}_{t}|^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{U}|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta^{2}}\Theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\Theta)(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}_{t}-\mathbf{U}_{t}|^{2}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{U}|^{2}(0)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}-\mathbf{U}_{t})\cdot\big(-\mathbf{U}_{tt}+\Delta\mathbf{U}-\mu\nabla\Theta\big)-\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\Theta)\nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}_{t}

(72)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\Theta)\right)\Theta_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\Theta.

5.4.1.
Any weak solution satisfies the relative entropy inequality

First, in the same way as in
(
52
)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\theta+\theta^{\alpha})(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}_{t}-\mathbf{U}_{t}|^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{U}|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}-\mathbf{U}_{t})\cdot\big(-\mathbf{U}_{tt}+\Delta\mathbf{U}-\mu\nabla\Theta\big)

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}-\mathbf{U}_{t})\cdot\nabla\Theta+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\theta\nabla\cdot\mathbf{U}_{t}

(73)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(\theta_{0}+\theta_{0}^{\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{V}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{0}-\nabla\mathbf{U}_{0}|^{2},

where
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathbf{u}_{t}(0,\cdot)
and
\mathbf{V}_{0}=\mathbf{U}_{t}(0,\cdot)
. Next, in
(
12
)
we choose
\phi=\chi_{[0,t]}\Theta
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}s_{1}(\theta)\Theta_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\Theta

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\nabla\Theta+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta^{2}}\Theta

(74)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}s_{1}(\theta(t))\Theta(t)-\int_{\Omega}s_{1}(\theta_{0})\Theta(0),

and since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}s_{1}(\theta)\Theta_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\Theta))\Theta_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}s_{1}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\Theta))\Theta_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big((s_{1}(\Theta)\Theta)_{t}-e_{1}(\Theta)_{t}\big),

we conclude that (
72
) holds.

5.4.2.
Proof of weak-strong uniqueness

Now, if
(\tilde{\mathbf{u}},\tilde{\theta})
is a classical solution to the same problem with
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{0}=\mathbf{v}_{0}
,
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}
and
\tilde{\theta}_{0}=\theta_{0}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}|^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\tilde{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta^{2}}\tilde{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq-\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})\right)\tilde{\theta}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}.

First, we want to transform the RHS. We start with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})\right)\tilde{\theta}_{t}+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})\right)\tilde{\theta}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\underbrace{s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}\tilde{\theta}_{t}}_{=s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{t}}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})\right)\tilde{\theta}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})(s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{t}+\mu\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}).

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})\nabla\tilde{\theta}}{\tilde{\theta}}\right)+\frac{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})|\nabla\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{\tilde{\theta}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta})\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}\frac{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})}{\tilde{\theta}}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})\frac{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}}{\tilde{\theta}^{2}},

we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}|^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\tilde{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{\theta^{2}}\tilde{\theta}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta})\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}\frac{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})}{\tilde{\theta}}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})\frac{\kappa(\tilde{\theta})|\nabla\tilde{\theta}|^{2}}{\tilde{\theta}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})\right)\tilde{\theta}_{t}.

At this point, we need some specific form for
\kappa(\theta)
in order to obtain good estimates with the right signs. Therefore, we choose
\kappa(\theta)=1+\theta^{2}
. Now, we calculate the terms corresponding to the constant part of
\kappa

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\ln\theta|^{2}\tilde{\theta}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}}{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta})\cdot\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})|\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\ln\theta|^{2}\tilde{\theta}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\theta}\nabla\ln\theta\cdot\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta\nabla\ln\theta-\tilde{\theta}\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta})\cdot\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})|\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\ln\theta-\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\theta}-\theta)\nabla\ln\theta\cdot\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})|\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\ln\theta-\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\theta}-\theta)\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}\cdot(\nabla\ln\theta-\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}).

Similarly, corresponding to the second component
-\theta^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\theta|^{2}\tilde{\theta}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\theta\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta})\cdot\nabla\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})|\nabla\tilde{\theta}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\theta}-\theta)\nabla\tilde{\theta}\cdot(\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta}).

Thus, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}_{t}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{t}|^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\tilde{\mathbf{U}}|^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\tilde{\theta}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\ln\theta-\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\tilde{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})\right)\tilde{\theta}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\theta}-\theta)\nabla\tilde{\theta}\cdot(\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta})-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\theta}-\theta)\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}\cdot(\nabla\ln\theta-\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}||\tilde{\theta}_{t}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}|(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}C(\underline{\theta})\big(||\nabla\tilde{\theta}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)\int_{\Omega}|\theta-\tilde{\theta}|^{2}

(75)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\underline{\theta}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\theta-\nabla\tilde{\theta}|^{2}+\frac{\underline{\theta}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\ln\theta-\nabla\ln\tilde{\theta}|^{2}.

Note that it is enough to prove

\displaystyle|(s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})\quad\text{ and }\quad|\theta-\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}),

for
C>0
that doesn’t depend on
t
, since this implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})(s)~ds,

and the conclusion follows as in section
4.3.2
. The following result will be useful:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be two
C^{2}
functions and
s\in[a,b]
. If:

(1)

f(s)=g(s)=f^{\prime}(s)=g^{\prime}(s)=0
,

(2)

f^{\prime\prime}(s)>0
,

(3)

f(x)>0
, on
[a,b]\setminus\{s\}
,

then there is a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle f(x)\geq c|g(x)|,\quad\text{on }[a,b].

Proof.

By Taylor’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle f(x)=\underbrace{f(s)+f^{\prime}(s)(x-s)}_{=0}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}(x-s)^{2}+r_{f}(x)(x-s)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{f}(x)\right)(x-s)^{2},\quad x\in[a,b],

where
r_{f}
is a continuous function such that
r_{f}(s)=0
. Now, since
f>0
on
[a,b]\setminus\{s\}
, one has
\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{f}(x)>0
on
[a,b]\setminus\{s\}
, while the continuity of
r_{f}
and
r_{f}(s)=0
implies that
\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{f}(x)>0
on entire
[a,b]
. Denote
m=\min\limits_{x\in[a,b]}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{f}(x)
. Applying Taylor’s theorem to
g
, in the same way we have

\displaystyle g(x)=\left(\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{g}(x)\right)(x-s)^{2},\quad x\in[a,b],

so denoting
M=\max\limits_{x\in[a,b]}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{g}(x)\right|
and choosing
c=\frac{m}{M}
, we conclude

\displaystyle c|g(x)|\leq cM(x-s)^{2}=m(x-s)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2}+r_{f}(x)\right)(x-s)^{2}=f(x),

and the proof is finished.
∎

Now, applying this lemma

\displaystyle|s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}),\quad\text{for }\theta\in\left[\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}\right].

Note that here for each fixed
\tilde{\theta}
there a constant
c_{\tilde{\theta}}
for which this inequality holds, so our
C>0
is chosen as
\max\limits_{\tilde{\theta}\in[\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}]}c_{\tilde{\theta}}
. Next, we aim to control the function
|s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|
outside of
\left[\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}\right]
. First,

\displaystyle|s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|\leq C(1+|\ln(\theta)|)\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}),\quad\text{for }\theta\in\left[0,\underline{\theta}\right],

since the term
\ln(\theta)
has a negative sign in
\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta})
and the other terms are bounded, while for
\theta\geq\overline{\theta}
the function
\theta^{\alpha}
dominates
\theta^{\alpha-1}
and
\ln\theta
so

\displaystyle|s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|\leq C(1+e_{1}(\theta))\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}),\quad\text{for }\theta\geq\overline{\theta},

which together imply

\displaystyle|s_{1}(\theta)-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})_{\theta}(\theta-\tilde{\theta})-s_{1}(\tilde{\theta})|\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}).

Next, to control
|\theta-\tilde{\theta}|^{2}
, we start with

(76)

\displaystyle|\theta-\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}),\quad\text{for }\theta\in[0,\underline{\theta}]\cup[\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}],

by Lemma
5.8

and

\displaystyle|\theta-\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\leq C(1+\theta^{2})\leq C(1+e_{1}(\theta))\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}),\quad\text{for }\theta\geq\overline{\theta},

where we have used the growth condition
\alpha\geq 2
, so combining the above inequalities implies

\displaystyle|\theta-\tilde{\theta}|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(\theta\big|\tilde{\theta}).

Thus, the proof is finished.

Lemma
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and let
p\in(1,\infty]
. Assume that
\theta\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{M}(\Omega))
, and that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\mathbf{u}_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\mu\int_{0}^{T}{}_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)}\langle\theta,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{C_{0}(\Omega)}=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{v}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi},

for all test function
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in H^{1}(0,T;C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega))
,
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}(T)=0
, where
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is given. Then,
\mathbf{u}_{t}\in C_{w}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
.

Proof.

First, let us prove that . Since
\theta\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{M}(\Omega))
, one has from the equation for every
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{tt}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right|\leq\big(||\mathbf{u}||_{L^{p}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}+||\theta||_{L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega))}\big)||\boldsymbol{\varphi}||_{L^{p^{*}}(0,T;W^{2,\infty}(\Omega))},

so
\mathbf{u}_{tt}\in L^{p^{*}}(0,T;[C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{\prime})
, and consequently
\mathbf{u}_{t}\in C(0,T;[C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{\prime})
.

Now, for an arbitrary
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
||\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}-\boldsymbol{\varphi}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
. Then, for almost all
t_{1},t_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{2}))\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{2}))\cdot(\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n})+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{2}))\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n},

so

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t_{2}\to t_{1}}\left|\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{2}))\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{t_{2}\to t_{1}}\left|\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{2}))\cdot(\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n})\right|+\underbrace{\lim\limits_{t_{2}\to t_{1}}\left|\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{1})-\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{2}))\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}\right|}_{=0}

\displaystyle\leq 2||\mathbf{u}_{t}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}||\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{n}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Now, we can give meaning to
\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{t}(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}
for all
t
by constructing a sequence of
t_{n}
such that
\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}
is finite for all
n
and
t_{n}\to t
as
n\to\infty
. From the above inequality,
\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}
forms a Cauchy sequence which converges to a unique limit
a_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\in\mathbb{R}
for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
, independent of the choice of the sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Now, since
\left|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}_{t}(t_{n})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right|\leq C||\varphi||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, one has that
|a_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}|\leq C||\varphi||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. This combined with linearity of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\mapsto a_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}
allows us to identify
a_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}
with a function
\mathbf{u}_{t}(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, by the Riesz theorem. The proof is now finished.

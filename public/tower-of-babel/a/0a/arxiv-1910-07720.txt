Riemann-Hilbert approach to the modified nonlinear Schrödinger equation with non-vanishing asymptotic boundary conditions
Yiling YANG, Engui FAN

Abstract
The modified nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation was proposed to describe the nonlinear propagation of the Alfven waves and the femtosecond optical pulses in a nonlinear single-mode optical fiber. In this paper, the inverse scattering transform for the modified NLS equation with non-vanishing asymptotic boundary at infinity is presented. An appropriate two-sheeted Riemann surface is introduced to map the original spectral parameter k k into a single-valued parameter z z . The asymptotic behaviors, analyticity and the symmetries of the Jost solutions of Lax pair for the modified NLS equation, as well as the spectral matrix are analyzed in details. Then a matrix Riemann-Hilbert (RH) problem associated with the problem of nonzero asymptotic boundary conditions is established, from which N N -soliton solutions is obtained via the corresponding reconstruction formulae. As an illustrate examples of N N -soliton formula, two kinds of one-soliton solutions and three kinds of two-soliton solutions are explicitly presented according to different distribution of the spectrum. The dynamical feature of those solutions are characterized in the particular case with a quartet of discrete eigenvalues. It is shown that distribution of the spectrum and non-vanishing boundary also affect feature of soliton solutions. Finally, we analyze the differences between our results and those on zero boundary case. Keywords: the modified NLS equation; Lax pair; inverse scattering transformation; Riemann-Hilbert problem; N N -soliton solution.

In this article, we consider the modified NLS equation (
1.3

The structure of this work is the following. In section 2, we introduce an appropriate two-sheeted Riemann surface is introduced to
map the original spectral parameter

In Section 3 and Section 4, starting from the Lax pair of the modified NLS equation, we construct Jost solutions and spectral matrix.

In section 6, we analyze asymptotic behaviors of the Jost solution, scattering matrix and reflection coefficients.
In Section 7, we discuss the discrete spectrum and the residue conditions to analyze poles for meromorphic matrices appearing in the RH problem.
In Section 8, we establish reconstruction formula between solution of the modified NLS equation and the RH problem.

The affects of distribution of the spectrum on soliton solutions are analyzed.

Jost Solutions

From eigenvalues
\pm ik\lambda
, we can get the eigenvector matrix of
X_{\pm}
and
T_{\pm}
as

Y_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{u_{\pm}}{z}\\
-\frac{u_{\pm}^{*}}{z}&1\end{array}\right)=I+\frac{1}{z}\sigma_{3}Q_{\pm},

(3.1)

by which
X_{\pm}
and

are diagonalized simultaneously

X_{\pm}=Y_{\pm}(-ik\lambda\sigma_{3})Y_{\pm}^{-1},\hskip 14.22636ptT_{\pm}=Y_{\pm}\left[-ik\lambda\sigma_{3}(2\alpha k^{2}-4\alpha-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{\alpha})\right]Y_{\pm}^{-1}.

Direct computation shows that

\det(Y_{\pm})=1+\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{z^{2}}\triangleq\gamma,

(3.3)

and

Y_{\pm}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-\frac{u_{\pm}}{z}\\

(3.4)

Substituting (
3.2
) into (
2.5

(Y_{\pm}^{-1}\psi)_{x}=-ik\lambda\sigma_{3}(Y_{\pm}^{-1}\psi),\hskip 14.22636pt(Y_{\pm}^{-1}\psi)_{t}=-ik\lambda\left(2\alpha k^{2}-4\alpha-\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{\alpha}\right)\sigma_{3}(Y_{\pm}^{-1}\psi),

4
Scattering Matrix

Since
{\rm tr}X={\rm tr}T=0
in (
2.4
), then by using Able formula, we have

(\det\phi_{\pm})_{x}=(\det\phi_{\pm})_{t}=0.

(4.1)

Again by using the relation

\det(\varphi_{\pm})=\det(\phi_{\pm}e^{-i\theta(z)\sigma_{3}})=\det(\phi_{\pm}),

we get
(\det\varphi_{\pm})_{x}=(\det\varphi_{\pm})_{t}=0
, which means that
\det(\varphi_{\pm})
is independent of

\det\varphi_{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\det(\varphi_{\pm})=\det Y_{\pm}=\gamma\neq 0,\hskip 14.22636ptz\in D^{+}\cup D^{-},

(4.2)

which implies that
\varphi_{\pm}
are inverse matrices.

Since

are two fundamental matrix solutions of the Lax pair (
2.4

and
\phi_{-}

\phi_{+}(x,t,z)=\phi_{-}(x,t,z)S(z),

where

is called scattering matrix and (
4.2
) implies that

Denoting the scattering matrix by

)

\displaystyle\phi_{+,1}=s_{11}(z)\phi_{-,1}+s_{21}(z)\phi_{-,2},\hskip 14.22636pt\phi_{+,2}=s_{12}(z)\phi_{-,1}+s_{22}(z)\phi_{-,2},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{+,1}=s_{11}(z)\varphi_{-,1}+s_{21}(z)e^{-2i\theta}\varphi_{-,2},\hskip 14.22636pt\varphi_{+,2}=s_{12}(z)e^{2i\theta}\varphi_{-,1}+s_{22}(z)\varphi_{-,2},

\rho(z)=\frac{s_{21}(z)}{s_{11}(z)},\hskip 14.22636pt\tilde{\rho}(z)=\frac{s_{12}(z)}{s_{22}(z)}.

Solving above four linear systems (
4.4

\displaystyle s_{11}(z)=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\phi_{+,1},\phi_{-,2})}{\gamma}=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\varphi_{+,1}\varphi_{-,2})}{\gamma},

(4.7)

\displaystyle s_{12}(z)=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\phi_{+,2},\phi_{-,2})}{\gamma}=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\varphi_{+,2},\varphi_{-,2})}{e^{2i\theta}\gamma},

(4.8)

\displaystyle s_{21}(z)=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\phi_{-,1},\phi_{+,1})}{\gamma}=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\varphi_{-,1},\varphi_{+,1})}{e^{-2i\theta}\gamma},

(4.9)

\displaystyle s_{22}(z)=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\phi_{-,1},\phi_{+,2})}{\gamma}=\frac{{\rm Wr}(\varphi_{-,1},\varphi_{+,2})}{\gamma},

(4.10)

which together with analyticity of
\varphi_{\pm}

8.1
The Riemann-Hilbert Problem

Proposition 4
.

Define the sectionally meromorphic matrices

M(x,t,z)=\Bigg\{\begin{array}[]{ll}M^{+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\dfrac{\varphi_{+,1}}{s_{11}},&\varphi_{-,2}\\
\end{array}\right),&\text{as }z\in D^{+},\\

\end{array}\right),&\text{as }z\in D^{-},\\
\end{array}

then a multiplicative matrix Riemann-Hilbert problem is proposed:

\bullet

M(x,t,z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma
and has single poles.

\bullet
Jump condition

M^{-}(x,t,z)=M^{+}(x,t,z)(I-G(x,t,z)),\hskip 14.22636ptz\in\Sigma,

(8.2)

(8.3)

\bullet
Asymptotic behaviors

\displaystyle M(x,t,z)\sim e^{i\nu_{-}\sigma_{3}}+O(z^{-1}),\hskip 14.22636ptz\rightarrow\infty,

(8.4)

\displaystyle M(x,t,z)\sim\frac{1}{z}e^{i\nu_{-}\sigma_{3}}\sigma_{3}Q_{-}+O(1),\hskip 14.22636ptz\rightarrow 0.

(8.5)

Proof.

The analyticity of
M_{\pm}
can be find out from the analyticity of the
\varphi_{\pm}
and
S(z)
.
From (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{-,2}(x,t,z)

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\rho}(z)\varphi_{-,1}(x,t,z)+\frac{\varphi_{+,2}(x,t,z)}{s_{22}(z)},

(8.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{+,1}(x,t,z)}{s_{11}(z)}

\displaystyle=(1-\rho(z)\tilde{\rho}(z))\varphi_{-,1}(x,t,z)+\rho(z)\frac{\varphi_{+,2}(x,t,z)}{s_{22}(z)},

(8.7)

which leads to the jump condition (
8.2
).

With the asymptotic behaviors of the Jost solution (

) and scattering matrix (

), we can derive that

M^{+}(x,t,z)\sim\frac{1}{z}e^{i\nu_{-}\sigma_{3}}\sigma_{3}Q_{-}+O(1),\ \ z\rightarrow 0,

(8.8)

M^{-}(x,t,z)\sim\frac{1}{z}e^{i\nu_{-}\sigma_{3}}\sigma_{3}Q_{-}+O(1),\ \ z\rightarrow 0.

(8.9)

Similarly, we can get another asymptotic behavior (

8.3
Trace formula and theta condition

Define two functions
\beta^{\pm}(z)
as follow

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta^{+}(z)=s_{11}(z)\prod_{n=1}^{2N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{*2}}{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{2}}e^{i\nu_{0}},\\
&\beta^{-}(z)=s_{22}(z)\prod_{n=1}^{2N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{2}}{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{*2}}e^{-i\nu_{0}},\end{split}

(8.20)

which implies the relation
\beta^{+}(z)\beta^{-}(z)=s_{11}(z)s_{22}(z)
and
\beta^{\pm}(z)\rightarrow 1,\ z\rightarrow\pm\infty
.

From the analyticity of the scattering matrix, we see that the above functions are analytic and have no zeros in
D^{+}
and
D^{-}
respectively.

From
\det S(z)=1
, we obtain that

\frac{1}{s_{11}s_{22}}=\frac{s_{11}s_{22}-s_{12}s_{21}}{s_{11}s_{22}}=1-\rho(z)\tilde{\rho}(z)=1+\rho(z)\rho^{*}(z^{*}),

so that

\beta^{+}(z)\beta^{-}(z)=\frac{1}{1+\rho(z)\rho^{*}(z^{*})},\hskip 14.22636ptz\in\Sigma.

Taking logarithms to the above relation and using the Plemelj’ formula, we have

\log\beta_{\pm}(z)=\mp\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\log[1+\rho(\zeta)\rho^{*}(\zeta^{*})]}{\zeta-z}d\zeta,\hskip 14.22636ptz\in D^{\pm}.

(8.21)

Substituting them back into (
8.20
) leads to

s_{11}(z)=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\log[1+\rho(\zeta)\rho^{*}(\zeta^{*})]}{\zeta-z}d\zeta\right]\\
\prod_{n=1}^{2N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{2}}{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{*2}}e^{-i\nu_{0}},\hskip 14.22636ptz\in D^{+},\\

(8.22)

s_{22}(z)=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\log[1+\rho(\zeta)\rho^{*}(\zeta^{*})]}{\zeta-z}d\zeta\right]\prod_{n=1}^{2N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{*2}}{z^{2}-\zeta_{n}^{2}}e^{i\nu_{0}},\hskip 14.22636ptz\in D^{-}.

(8.23)

The above formulas are called trace formulas, which express the analytic scattering coefficient in terms of
the discrete eigenvalues and the reflection coefficient.

Taking the limit
z\rightarrow 0
in (
8.22
), and using the asymptotic behavior of the scattering matrix (
6.14
) we then obtain the following theta condition

\arg\left(\frac{u_{-}}{u_{+}}\right)+2\nu_{0}=8\sum_{n=1}^{2N_{1}+N_{2}}\arg\zeta_{n}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\log[1+\rho(\zeta)\rho^{*}(\zeta^{*})]}{\zeta}d\zeta.

(8.24)

Introduction

In classical Galois theory there is the well-known construction of the general polynomial equation over
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
The coefficients of the general equation are (up to sign) the elementary symmetric polynomials in
n
indeterminates
x_{1}
, …,
x_{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Every algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
defined by a polynomial

of degree

for
x_{1}
, …,
x_{n}
in the general equation.
The case of the symmetric group was generalized by Emmy Noether in
[
Noe17
]
, which led to new problems in invariant theory (e.g., Noether problem).

In an analogous way a general linear differential equation with differential Galois group the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(C)
over an algebraically closed field

Extend the linear action of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(C)
on the vector whose coordinates are differential indeterminates

, …,
y_{n}
to
the differential field of rational functions

Introducing a new differential indeterminate

, …,

[

0\,=\,Y^{(n)}+c_{n-1}Y^{(n-1)}+\ldots+c_{0}Y\,:=\,\frac{w(Y,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})}{w(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})}.

In fact,

are differentially algebraically independent
generators of the fixed field of

under

of
\mathbb{Q}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

Every Picard-Vessiot extension of a differential field

2.
The Structure of the Classical Groups

Let
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(C)
be one of the classical groups of Lie rank
l
.
We denote by

\Phi
the root system of the corresponding type of
G
and
by
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}\}

a basis of
\Phi
with simple roots
\alpha_{i}
. Each root in
\Phi

can be written as a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of the
simple roots with all coefficients negative or positive. This
yields a disjoint decomposition of
\Phi
into a set of positive and
negative roots, i.e.

\Phi\,=\,\Phi^{+}\cup\Phi^{-}.

For a root
\alpha\in\Phi
we write
\height(\alpha)
for the height of
\alpha
,
i.e. the sum of all coefficients in the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination with
respect of
\Delta
.
Further denote by
m
the cardinalities of
\Phi^{+}
and
\Phi^{-}
.
We enumerate the positive roots as
\alpha_{1}
, …,
\alpha_{l}
,
\alpha_{l+1}
, …,
\alpha_{m}

in such a way that
\alpha_{1}
, …,
\alpha_{l}
are the simple roots introduced above and

\height(\alpha_{r})\leq\height(\alpha_{s})
for all
r\leq s
.

We denote by
\mathcal{W}
the Weyl group of
\Phi
. It is generated by the
l

simple reflections
w_{\alpha_{i}}
for the simple roots
\alpha_{i}

of
\Delta
. Every

can be written as a product of
simple reflections and if this expression is minimal we call
the number of simple reflections the length
l(w)
of
w
. The length
coincides with the number of positive roots
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}

with
w(\alpha)
negative. There is a unique element
\overline{w}
of
\mathcal{W}

of maximal length and this element maps all positive roots to negative
ones and vice versa.
We write
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(C)
for the Lie algebra of
G
and we let

\mathfrak{h}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}

consisting of diagonal matrices. Let

\mathfrak{g}\,=\,\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

be a Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

denotes the one-dimensional root space corresponding to the root

\alpha
of
\Phi
. In accordance with this decomposition we fix a
Chevalley basis

(2)

\{H_{i},X_{\alpha}\mid 1\leq i\leq l,\ \alpha\in\Phi\},

where
\mathfrak{h}=\langle H_{1},\dots,H_{l}\rangle
and

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\langle X_{\alpha}\rangle
.

We denote by
T
the maximal torus of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}

and for a root
\alpha\in\Phi
we denote by
U_{\alpha}
the
root group whose Lie algebra is the root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Let

\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\rightarrow U_{\alpha},\ X_{\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{j!}X_{\alpha}^{j}

be the exponential map from the root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
to the root group
U_{\alpha}
of
G
. For
x\in C
we write
u_{\alpha}(x)
for

\exp(xX_{\alpha})
.
Let
U^{+}
(respectively
U^{-}
) be the maximal unipotent subgroup whose Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{u}^{+}
(respectively
\mathfrak{u}^{-}
)
is the direct sum of all root spaces that are indexed by the positive
(respectively negative) roots. Further we denote
by
B^{+}
(respectively
B^{-}
) the Borel subgroup of
G

with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{b}^{+}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{+}

(respectively
\mathfrak{b}^{-}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{-}
).
Clearly we have

and
B^{+}=TU^{+}

(respectively

and

).
Moreover,
to shorten notation we will omit the plus sign in

The following theorems provide a normal form for elements of

3.
Gauge Equivalence

For
g\in G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
we denote by
\Ad(g)
the automorphism

\Ad(g):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},\ X\mapsto gXg^{-1},

i.e.,

G

\mathfrak{g}

\partial_{F}
the logarithmic derivative

Two matrices

g.A_{1}:=\Ad(g)(A_{1})+\ell\delta(g)=A_{2}.

The action of

\mathfrak{g}(F)
by gauge transformation partitions
\mathfrak{g}(F)
into orbits depending on the differential field
F
.
We will use the following two remarks to describe the images of the adjoint action and the logarithmic derivative, respectively. Together they enable us to
connect gauge transformation of an element of

by a root group element with the root structure.

.

For linearly independent

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(u_{\beta}(x))(X_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{q}c_{\beta,\alpha,i}x^{i}X_{\alpha+i\beta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(u_{\beta}(x))(H_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle X_{\beta},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(u_{\beta}(x))(X_{-\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{-\beta}+xH_{\beta}-x^{2}X_{\beta},

.

Let

5.
Resolving Weyl Group Elements and their Associated Differential Systems

Let
\mathbf{a}^{+}=(a^{+}_{1},\dots,a^{+}_{m})
,
\mathbf{a}^{0}=(a^{0}_{1},\dots,a^{0}_{l})
and

\mathbf{a}^{-}=(a^{-}_{1},\dots,a^{-}_{m})
be differential indeterminates over
C
and, using the notation introduced in Section
2
, define
a general element of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by

(4)

A:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{+}_{i}X_{\alpha_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{-}_{i}X_{-\alpha_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{l}a^{0}_{i}H_{i}.

For further differential indeterminates
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
over
C
let
u_{-\alpha_{i}}(b_{i})
be the image
of
b_{i}X_{-\alpha_{i}}
under the exponential map, that is
u_{-\alpha_{i}}(b_{i})
is a parametrization of the
root group
U_{-\alpha_{i}}
.
We fix
w\in\mathcal{W}
. Let

be the indices such that

w(\Phi^{+})\cap\Phi^{-}=\{-\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,-\alpha_{i_{k}}\},

i.e., exchanging the roles played by

and

in Theorem
2.2
,
(U^{-})_{w}^{\prime}

We set

and define

(5)

u_{w}(\mathbf{b}_{w})=u_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}(b_{i_{1}})\cdots u_{-\alpha_{i_{k}}}(b_{i_{k}}).

Gauge transformation of

by
n(w)u_{w}(\mathbf{b}_{w})
yields

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(n(w)u_{w}(\mathbf{b}_{w})).A&=&\Ad(n(w)u_{w}(\mathbf{b}_{w}))(A)+\ell\delta(n(w)u_{w}(\mathbf{b}_{w}))\\[5.0pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}f^{+}_{w,i}X_{\alpha_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}f^{-}_{w,i}X_{-\alpha_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{l}f^{0}_{w,i}H_{i}\,,\end{array}

where

,
f^{-}_{w,i}
and

are certain differential polynomials in

We define the differential system

The system expresses conditions for the vanishing of

modulo the differential ideal

of

, …,

\overline{f}^{0}_{w,i}
denote the images of

,

in the residue class ring.

The differential ideal

of

is prime.
Indeed, we gauge transform by root group elements belonging to negative roots. Let

be one of them and let

be the coefficient of some basis element of height

in the linear representation of

(6)

u_{-\alpha_{i_{j-1}}}(b_{i_{j-1}})\dots u_{-\alpha_{i_{1}}}(b_{i_{1}}).A\,.

By Remark
3.1
the effect of the adjoint action on

is that
we potentially add to

a monomial in

and other coefficients of (

) which belong to basis elements for roots of height greater than

.
Further, Remark
3.2
implies that the effect of the logarithmic derivative of

is that we add the derivative of
b_{i_{j}}
to the coefficient of the basis element
X_{-\alpha_{i_{j}}}
in the linear representation of (

is that we add to the coefficient

differential monomials in the indeterminate

and in those

which are of height greater than

.
We conclude inductively that for each basis element

,

or

of

, the differential indeterminate which is the corresponding coefficient in (

only linearly with constant coefficient.
Finally gauge transforming (

is only a permutation of the entries and so

, …,

can be solved
for pairwise distinct differential indeterminates.

Lemma 5.1
.

The differential ideal
I_{w}
is prime.

We compute a Thomas decomposition
S_{w}^{1}
, …,
S_{w}^{r}
of

with respect to an elimination ranking satisfying

\{\mathbf{b}_{w}\}\gg\{\mathbf{a}^{+},\,\mathbf{a}^{-},\,\mathbf{a}^{0}\,\}\,.

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
let
I_{w}^{i}
be the differential ideal of

C\{\mathbf{a}^{+},\mathbf{a}^{-},\mathbf{a}^{0},\mathbf{b}_{w}\}

generated by the left hand sides of the equations in
S_{w}^{i}
, and define
q_{w}^{i}
as the product of the initials and separants of all these generators. Then, by Proposition
4.5
, we have

I_{w}=\left(I_{w}^{1}:(q_{w}^{1})^{\infty}\right)\cap\ldots\cap\left(I_{w}^{r}:(q_{w}^{r})^{\infty}\right).

Since
I_{w}
is prime, there exists a unique
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such
that
I_{w}=I_{w}^{j}:(q_{w}^{j})^{\infty}
. Hence, the simple system
S_{w}^{j}
is generic
in the sense that the solution set of
S_{w}^{j}
is a
dense subset of the solution set of
S_{w}
(cf. also
[
Rob14
, Subsect. 2.2.3]
).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{w}
be the subset of
S_{w}^{j}
consisting of the equations and inequations
which only involve
\mathbf{a}^{+}
,
\mathbf{a}^{-}
,
\mathbf{a}^{0}
and their derivatives.
Let
E_{w}
be the differential ideal of
C\{\mathbf{a}^{+},\mathbf{a}^{-},\mathbf{a}^{0}\}
which is generated by the left hand sides of the equations

,
and define
q
as the product of the initials and separants of these generators.
Because of the choice of elimination ranking we have

\left(I_{w}^{j}:(q_{w}^{j})^{\infty}\right)\cap C\{\mathbf{a}^{+},\mathbf{a}^{-},\mathbf{a}^{0}\}\,=\,E_{w}:q^{\infty}.

In principle, this approach allows to decide effectively whether gauge transformation by an element of a Bruhat cell defined by
w
into normal form is possible by checking whether
E_{w}:q^{\infty}
is the zero ideal. Moreover, by trying the finitely many elements of
\mathcal{W}
, a feasible one for gauge transformation to normal form can be found if one exists. In fact, we are going to predict such a feasible element and hence prove that gauge transformation to normal form is possible.

Definition 5.2
.

For
w\in\mathcal{W}
we consider the two conditions:

(A)

\exists\,\Psi\subseteq\Phi^{-}\mbox{ such that }w(\Psi)=\Phi^{+}\setminus\Delta

and

(B)

w(\Phi^{+})\cap\Phi^{-}\supseteq\Psi\,.

An element
w\in\mathcal{W}
is called

if it satisfies

) and (

).

If
\Psi
as above exists, then it is uniquely determined by

, namely as

\Psi=w^{-1}(\Phi^{+}\setminus\Delta).

In particular, we have
|\Psi|=m-l
.

Proposition 5.3
.

For every classical group
G
the associated Weyl group
\mathcal{W}
contains a resolving element.

Proof.

Since the root system of a classical group is irreducible, there is a unique Weyl group element
\overline{w}\in\mathcal{W}
which maps
\Phi^{+}
to
\Phi^{-}
. As a consequence we have
\overline{w}(\Delta)=\Delta^{-}
and
because
\overline{w}
has order two, it follows that
\overline{w}(\Phi^{-})=\Phi^{+}

and
\overline{w}(\Delta^{-})=\Delta
. Define
\Psi:=\Phi^{-}\setminus\Delta^{-}
.
One easily checks that
\Psi
and
\overline{w}
satisfy Condition (
A
) and Condition (
B
).
∎

Definition 5.4
.

A ranking
>
on
C\{\mathbf{a}^{+},\mathbf{a}^{-},\mathbf{a}^{0},\mathbf{b}\}

is said to be
adapted to the root system
if the following three conditions are satisfied.

(a)

The ranking

is an elimination ranking satisfying
\{\mathbf{b}\}\gg\{\mathbf{a}^{+},\,\mathbf{a}^{-},\,\mathbf{a}^{0}\}
.

(b)

The restriction of

(c)

For all

,

we have

b_{i}>b_{j}\quad\Rightarrow\quad|\height(-\alpha_{i})|\geq|\height(-\alpha_{j})|\,.

Obviously a ranking that is adapted to the root system exists.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{W}
be resolving and assume that the ranking
>
is adapted
to the root system. Then we have:

(a)

The differential system

(b)

To begin with we compute the logarithmic derivative of

\mathbf{b}

\Phi

and

Now let

In this section we construct
a differential field extension

Let
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be the differential field generated by
\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}
and

\{\overline{g}^{-}_{i}\in\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}\mid i\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\}\}

over
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}_{2}\langle\mathbf{\overline{t}}\rangle=\mathcal{L}

and the elements
\mathbf{\overline{t}}
are differentially algebraically independent over
\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
Seib
, Lemma 6.8]
(Transformation Lemma) that the matrix

A(\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{+},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0})
is gauge equivalent
by a unipotent matrix
u\in U^{-}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\{\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}\})

to a matrix
A_{G}(\mathbf{\overline{t}})
with
\mathbf{\overline{t}}=(\overline{t}_{1},\dots,\overline{t}_{l})
in

\mathcal{L}_{1}\langle\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}\rangle^{l}
. More precisely, since

u
is unipotent with entries in
\mathcal{L}_{1}\{\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}\}
, the inverse of
u
also has entries in

\mathcal{L}_{1}\{\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}\}
and so we have that

\overline{t}_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}\{\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}\}
for all

1\leq i\leq l
. This shows
(a)
.

In the proof of the Transformation Lemma we gauge transform
A(\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{+},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0})
successively with products of root group elements corresponding to roots of height
-1,\dots,-m
. The effect on
A(\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{+},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0})
is that we successively eliminate all entries corresponding
to roots of height
0,-1,-2,\dots,-(m-1)
except the complementary roots.
In each step the parametrization of the product of root group elements of height
-k
depends linearly on the entries which we are going to eliminate, i.e. the entries corresponding to the roots of height
-(k-1)
. The only non-zero entries in
A(\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{+},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0})
which correspond to positive roots are the entries belonging to the simple roots. In each step the effect of the adjoint action coming from the positive simple roots is only the elimination described above and all other contributions of the adjoint action stem from entries belonging to the negative roots.
One proves now inductively on
-k=-1,\dots,-m
the following claim: After performing step
-k
an entry corresponding to a root of height

h
does not involve indeterminates among the
\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}
corresponding to roots of height less than
h
nor a proper derivative of

\overline{g}^{-}_{h}
, but the degree of this entry in
\overline{g}^{-}_{h}
is equal to one and the coefficient is constant.
In each step of the induction one concludes first with Remark
3.1
and
3.2
that to entries of height
less than or equal to
-k
only differential polynomials in indeterminates
\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}
and those of
\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}
which correspond to roots of height greater than
-k
are added. Secondly one concludes with Remark
3.1
and
3.2
that to entries of height
greater than
-k
nothing is added.
Since the complementary roots, which among themselves are ordered by height, are the only ones which are not eliminated we conclude statement
(b)
.

Next we prove that
\mathcal{L}_{2}\langle\mathbf{\overline{t}}\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{1}\langle\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}\rangle=\mathcal{L}
. We need to show
that
\mathcal{L}_{2}\langle\mathbf{\overline{t}}\rangle
contains all
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}},\dots,\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{l}}
.
By statement
(b)
we can successively express the elements
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}},\dots,\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{l}}
in terms of
\mathbf{\overline{t}}
and

\{\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}\}\setminus\{\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}},\dots,\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{l}}\}

over
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Indeed,
\overline{t}_{1}
depends linearly on
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}}
and does not involve any other indeterminate

\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{2}},\dots,\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{l}}
nor any proper derivative of
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}}
and so we can express

\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}}
as stated. The expression
\overline{t}_{2}
does not involve any of the indeterminates
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{3}},\dots,\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{l}}
nor a
derivative of
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{2}}
and
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{2}}
appears linearly in it. Using the expression of

\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}}
obtained before we can write
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{2}}
as stated.
Continuing in this way we get that all
\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{1}},\dots,\overline{g}^{-}_{i_{l}}
are contained in
\mathcal{L}_{2}\langle\mathbf{\overline{t}}\rangle
.

Since
\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}
,
\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}
are differentially algebraically independent over
\mathcal{L}_{1}
by Lemma
6.5
and we already proved that

\mathcal{L}_{2}\langle\mathbf{\overline{t}}\rangle\,=\,\mathcal{L}_{1}\langle\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0}\rangle\langle\overline{g}^{-}_{i}\in\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}\mid i\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\}\rangle\langle\mathbf{\overline{t}}\rangle\,=\,\mathcal{L}_{1}\langle\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{0},\mathbf{\overline{g}}^{-}\rangle\,,

it follows from the invariance of
the differential transcendence degree that
\mathbf{\overline{t}}
are differentially algebraically independent over
\mathcal{L}_{2}
.
∎

K(r^{(j)}\mid 0\leq j\leq d-1)
-linearly dependent, i.e.

with

∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
a
,
b\in E[r_{1},r_{2}]\subset E\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
.
Then in

the indeterminate

does not appear if and only if

.

Proof.

The derivatives of
a
and

are

\displaystyle a^{\prime}\!

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\!r_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{r_{1}}(a)+g_{a},

\displaystyle b^{\prime}\!

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\!r_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{r_{1}}(b)+g_{b},

where

,

and

.
This yields

a^{\prime}b-ab^{\prime}\,=\,r_{1}^{\prime}\big(\partial_{r_{1}}(a)b-a\partial_{r_{1}}(b)\big)+(g_{a}b-ag_{b}).

We consider

as elements of the differential ring

with

\partial_{r_{1}}
.
Then, recognizing the coefficient of

if and only if

are

-linearly dependent, i.e.

with
e\in E(r_{2})
.
∎

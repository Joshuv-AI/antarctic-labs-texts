Charm and beauty isolation from heavy flavor decay electrons in p+p and Pb+Pb collisions at s NN \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}} = 5.02 TeV at LHC
Dongsheng Li, Fan Si, Yidan Zhao, Pengyu Zhou, Yifei Zhang, Xiujun Li, Chengxi Yang

Abstract
We present an analysis on the heavy flavor hadron decay electrons with charm and beauty contributions decomposed via a data driven method in p+p and Pb+Pb collisions at s NN \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}} = 5.02 TeV at LHC. The transverse momentum p T p_{\mathrm{T}} spectra, nuclear modification factor R AA R_{\mathrm{AA}} and azimuthal anisotropic flow v 2 v_{2} distributions of electrons from charm and beauty decays are obtained. We find that the electron R AA R_{\mathrm{AA}} from charm ( R AA c → e R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}} ) and beauty ( R AA b → e R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}} ) decays are suppressed at p T p_{\mathrm{T}} > > 2.0 GeV/ c c in Pb+Pb collisions, which indicates that charm and beauty interact with and lose their energy in the hot-dense medium. A less suppression of R AA b → e R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}} than R AA c → e R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}} at 2.0 < < p T p_{\mathrm{T}} < < 8.0 GeV/ c c is observed, which is consistent with the mass-dependent partonic energy loss scenario. A non-zero electron v 2 v_{2} from beauty decays ( v 2 b → e v_{2}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}} ) is observed and in good agreement with ALICE measurement. At low p T p_{\mathrm{T}} from 1.0 to 3.0 GeV/ c c , a discrepancy between RHIC and LHC results is observed with an 85% confidence level, which might be a possible hint of an energy-dependent behavior of beauty quarks reacting with the medium created in heavy-ion collisions. At 3.0 < < p T p_{\mathrm{T}} < < 7.0 GeV/ c c , v 2 b → e v_{2}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}} deviates from a number-of-constituent-quark (NCQ) scaling hypothesis, which favors that beauty quark is unlikely thermalized in heavy-ion collisions at LHC energy.

Introduction

Strong interactions among quarks and gluons by exchanging color charges are described by the theory of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). Quarks and gluons are confined in color-neutral hadrons and can be liberated under an extremely hot and dense condition such as the stage of a few microseconds after the big bang of the universe. Ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions provide such environment to create the state of matter known as quark-gluon plasma (QGP) that behaves almost as a perfect fluid
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Heavy quarks (charm and beauty) are produced mainly via initial hard scatterings in heavy-ion collisions due to their large masses. Their production yield can be evaluated by perturbative QCD. Thus, heavy quarks are believed to experience the whole medium dynamic evolution after their creation and maintain conserved quark numbers. Therefore, heavy quarks are an ideal probe to study QGP matter and sensitive to its thermal dynamic properties
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. In the past ten years, open heavy flavor hadrons have been measured in many experiments at Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) and Large Hadron Collider (LHC)
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. It was found that the nuclear modification factors (
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
) of both electrons from heavy flavor decays and open charm mesons were strongly suppressed at high transverse momentum (
p_{\mathrm{T}}
) in central heavy-ion collisions. This indicates that charm quarks significantly lose their energy when traversing the hot-dense medium. A significant elliptic flow (
v_{2}
) of open charm mesons is also observed and comparable with light flavor hadrons, which indicates that charm quark reaches thermalization in heavy-ion collisions.

Beauty, with a factor of three larger mass than charm, is predicted to lose less energy than charm in the medium created in heavy-ion collisions either with a suppression of the gluon radiation angle
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
or the elastic collisions
[
4
,
14
]
. And its
v_{2}
is probably different from those of charm quark and light flavors if it is far from thermalization. However, measurement of beauty is challenging due to its small production cross section and low hadronic decay branching ratios. Thus, in an alternative way, the beauty was commonly measured indirectly via its decay products, such as electrons, non-prompt
\mathrm{D^{0}}
and
\mathrm{J/\psi}
mesons. It was observed that the
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
of its decay daughters are less suppressed compared to those of prompt open charm mesons at
p_{\mathrm{T}}
up to
\sim
20 GeV/
c
, which is consistent with the mass hierarchy of the parton energy loss in the medium
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. Recently, a sizable electron
v_{2}
from beauty decays was also measured by ALICE
[
23
]
, which indicates strong interactions between beauty and the medium. Since charm looks no significant difference compared with light quarks in terms of
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
and
v_{2}
in RHIC and LHC energies
[
5
,
24
]
, it is crucial to study whether beauty is sensitive to the different media created at RHIC and LHC energies and behaves differently under different temperatures.

In a previous study, the charm and beauty contributions are separated from the inclusive heavy flavor electrons (HFE) in 200 GeV Au+Au collisions at RHIC
[
25
]
. It was found that the electron
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
from beauty decays is less suppressed compared to that from charm decays, which is consistent with the mass-dependent energy loss expectation. And a first zero electron
v_{2}
from beauty decays, lower than a number-of-constituent-quark (NCQ) scaling hypothesis, was observed at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, which indicates that beauty quark is unlikely thermalized and too heavy to be moved following the collective flow of lighter partons in heavy-ion collisions at RHIC energy.

In this paper, we perform an analysis in a similar way to separate charm and beauty contributions from heavy flavor decay electrons in 5.02 TeV p+p and Pb+Pb collisions. The
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
and
v_{2}
of electrons from charm and beauty decays in 5.02 TeV Pb+Pb collisions at LHC are obtained and compared with those in 200 GeV Au+Au collisions at RHIC.

Similar procedures to Ref.
[

are applied to extract the

of
\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}

The latest measurement of prompt D-meson

in 30–50% central Pb+Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 5.02 TeV by ALICE
[

is parameterized from
p_{\mathrm{T}}
= 0 to 24.0 GeV/

v_{2}=\frac{p_{0}n}{1+{\mathrm{exp}}\left(\frac{p_{1}-\left.\left(m_{\mathrm{T}}-m_{0}\right)\middle/n\right.}{p_{2}}\right)}-\frac{p_{0}n}{1+{\mathrm{exp}}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\right)}+p_{3}\left(m_{\mathrm{T}}-m_{0}\right),

(6)

where
m_{\mathrm{T}}=\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}+m_{0}^{2}}
and
m_{0}
denote the transverse and rest masses of the particle, respectively,
n
is the number of constituent quarks and
p_{i}
(
i
= 0, 1, 2, 3) are free parameters. Meanwhile, the
v_{2}
of
\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}}
(

= 3) is also obtained from Eq. (
6
) by assuming that it follows the NCQ scaling as D-mesons (
n
= 2).

The azimuthal angle (
\phi
) distributions of D-mesons and
\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}}
in each
p_{\mathrm{T}}
bin is sampled according to the input
v_{2}
with

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}N}{{\mathrm{d}}\phi}=1+2v_{2}{\mathrm{cos}}(2\phi).

(7)

Then, semileptonic decay simulations are performed with the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra and
\phi
distributions of D-mesons and
\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}}
as inputs. The output
\phi

7
) in each
p_{\mathrm{T}}
bin, and the
v_{2}
of
\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}
(
v_{2}^{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}
) and
\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}
(

\mathrm{J/\psi}
also exhibits a finite

at LHC energy, so the
\mathrm{J/\psi}

<
4.6 GeV/

\mathrm{J/\psi}

only covers the 20–40% centrality at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 5.02 TeV
[
53

and gives poor precision at mid-rapidity (

<

[

is applied instead as an estimation
[

. Then the
v_{2}
of
\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}
(

The
v_{2}
of
\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}
(

) and
\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}

\displaystyle=w_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}v_{2}^{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}+w_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}v_{2}^{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}+w_{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}v_{2}^{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}},

(8)

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{2}^{\mathrm{ince}}-\left(1-f_{\mathrm{PbPb}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}\right)v_{2}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}}{f_{\mathrm{PbPb}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}},

(9)

where
w_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}
,
w_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}
and

denote the weights by the relative yields of D-mesons,
\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}}
and
\mathrm{J/\psi}
in
\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}
calculated in Section

denotes the

of inclusive HFE from the parameterized average of the ALICE preliminary measurements (
\left|\eta\right|

<

[
55

measurements at both centrality classes and collision energies, so they are combined together to help perform a better fit and extrapolation.

The statistical and systematic uncertainties of measured D-meson
v_{2}
are quadratically summed and propagated into the uncertainties of
v_{2}^{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}
and
v_{2}^{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}
through the decay simulations, and they are correlated as a result. The absolute uncertainties (
\sigma
) of
v_{2}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}
and
v_{2}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}
are calculated with

\begin{split}&\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}}=\left(w_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}}+w_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}}\right)^{2}+w_{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}}^{2}\\
&+\left(\frac{\Delta v_{2}\mathrm{[D,\Lambda_{c}]}}{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}\right)^{2}\left(w_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}^{2}+w_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}^{2}\right)\\
&+\left(\frac{\Delta v_{2}\mathrm{[D,J/\psi]}}{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}\right)^{2}\left(w_{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}^{2}+w_{\mathrm{D\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}^{2}\right)\\
&+\left(\frac{\Delta v_{2}\mathrm{[J/\psi,\Lambda_{c}]}}{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}\right)^{2}\left(w_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}^{2}+w_{\mathrm{J/\psi\rightarrow e}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{\Lambda_{c}\rightarrow e}}^{2}\right),\end{split}

(10)

where
\Delta v_{2}\mathrm{[A,B]}=v_{2}^{\mathrm{A\rightarrow e}}-v_{2}^{\mathrm{B\rightarrow e}}
, and

\begin{split}\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}}}=&{f_{\mathrm{1b}}^{2}\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{ince}}}^{2}+f_{\mathrm{cb}}^{2}\sigma_{v_{2}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}}}^{2}}+f_{\mathrm{1b}}^{4}\left(v_{2}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}}-v_{2}^{\mathrm{ince}}\right)^{2}\sigma_{f_{\mathrm{PbPb}}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}}}^{2},\end{split}

(11)

3
Summary

This paper presents an isolation of charm and beauty contributions from heavy flavor decay electrons in p+p and Pb+Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 5.02 TeV at LHC. The
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectra,
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
at 0–10% and 30–50% centrality classes and
v_{2}
at 30–50% centrality of electrons from charm and beauty decays at mid-rapidity in Pb+Pb collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
= 5.02 TeV are reported.

The electron
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
from charm (
R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}
) and beauty (
R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}
) decays are suppressed at
p_{\mathrm{T}}

>
2.0 GeV/
c
in Pb+Pb collisions, respectively, which indicates that charm and beauty quarks interact with the hot-dense medium and lose their energy. The
R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{c\rightarrow e}}
at 2.0
<

p_{\mathrm{T}}

<
8.0 GeV/
c
and
R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}
at 4.0
<

p_{\mathrm{T}}

<
8.0 GeV/
c
are more suppressed at 0–10% centrality than at 30–50% centrality, which suggests that heavy quarks lose more energy in the medium created in more central collisions. However, at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}

<
4.0 GeV/
c
,
R_{\mathrm{AA}}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}
shows no significant centrality dependence. A less suppression of electron
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
from beauty decays compared with that from charm decays in the whole
p_{\mathrm{T}}
region from 2.0 to 8.0 GeV/
c
is observed, which is consistent with the mass-dependent partonic energy loss mechanism. A non-zero electron
v_{2}
from beauty decays (
v_{2}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}
) is observed and consistent with the ALICE measurement in the overlapping
p_{\mathrm{T}}
region. At low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
from 1.0 to 3.0 GeV/
c
, a discrepancy between results in 200 GeV Au+Au collisions and in 5.02 TeV Pb+Pb collisions is observed with an 85% confidence level, which might be a hint that beauty quark reacts differently to the two different media created in the heavy-ion collisions with two very different collision energies. At 3.0 GeV/
c

<

p_{\mathrm{T}}

<
7.0 GeV/
c
,
v_{2}^{\mathrm{b\rightarrow e}}
deviates from a NCQ scaling hypothesis, which favors that beauty quark is unlikely thermalized even in such high energy heavy-ion collisions at LHC. The
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
and
v_{2}
results of electrons with charm and beauty contribution separation could provide a new reference for theoretical models to extract the beauty diffusion coefficient with further theoretical calculations.

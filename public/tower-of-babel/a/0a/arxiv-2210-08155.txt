A Conformal Mean Value Theorem for Solutions of the Ultrahyperbolic Equation
Guillem Cobos, Brendan Guilfoyle

Abstract
. Asgeirsson’s theorem establishes a mean value property for solutions of the ultrahyperbolic equation. In the case of four variables, it states that the integrals of a solution over certain pairs of conjugate circles are the same. In this paper, the invariance of the four dimensional ultrahyperbolic equation under conformal maps of the pseudo-Euclidean space of signature 2+2 is used to get the most general version of the mean value theorem. The name non-degenerate conjugate conics is used for the most general pairs of curves over which solutions of the ultrahyperbolic equation enjoy the mean value property. These are proven to be pairs of conic sections, so that, in addition to conjugate circles which were known to exist, the picture is completed by finding mean value theorems over conjugate hyperbolae, conjugate parabolae, and line-empty pairs. In addition, Fritz John established a link between conjugate circles and the two rulings of a hyperboloid of revolution. The line incidence property of doubly ruled surfaces is used to prove a one-to-one correspondence between non-degenerate conjugate conics and pairs of rulings of doubly ruled surfaces in Euclidean 3-space.

1.
Introduction and results

A differentiable function
u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
of four real variables is said to be a solution of the ultrahyperbolic equation if it satisfies

(1.1)

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{4}^{2}}=0.

This equation is the compatibility condition for a function on line space to be the integral over lines of a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. The X-ray transform, in which a real-valued function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is mapped to its integral over lines, is thus overdetermined, and the equation arises in tomography where one seeks to invert the X-ray transform on line data
[
3
]

[
7
]

[

]

[
12
]
.

In a previous paper
[
5
]
, the authors considered the flat ultrahyperbolic equation (
1.1
) as the Laplace equation of the canonical conformally flat neutral metric on the space of oriented lines, utilizing the ambient conformal geometry to analyze its solutions. Conformal transformations of this metric correspond to projective transformations of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, thus linking the geometries in the two spaces. While many of these geometric ideas can be extended to higher dimension, the case of 4-dimensions is of particular interest, both for its physical significance and mathematical elegance.

As a partial differential equation, the ultrahyperbolic equation is neither elliptic nor hyperbolic and many standard PDE techniques originating in the standard Laplace equation or wave equation do not apply. Asgeirsson
[
2
]
showed, however, that solutions of the ultrahyperbolic equation do satisfy a mean value property. In particular,

(1.2)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(a+r\cos\theta,b+r\sin\theta,c,d)\ d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(a,b,c+r\cos\theta,d+r\sin\theta)\ d\theta,

for all
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
. The mean value theorem itself has applications in image reconstruction
[
6
]
.
The intention of this paper is to determine the most general pair of curves over which the mean value theorem holds for solutions of the ultrahyperbolic equation. This extends both the work of Fritz John
[
11
]
and of the authors
[
5
]
.

John
[
11
]
claims that one can obtain the most general form of Asgeirsson’s theorem by applying a linear map leaving the neutral quadratic form

||\underline{x}||^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}-x_{4}^{2},

invariant. However, we will show that this can in fact be improved by the use of
conformal
maps of the associated pseudo-Riemmanian metric
g=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}-dx_{3}^{2}-dx_{4}^{2}
. Throughout the notation
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
will be used for the pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g)
.

Setting
a=b=c=d=0
and
r=1
in equation (
1.2
) we obtain a pair of circles

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=\{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},0,0)\ |\ \alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=1\}\

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{(0,0,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\ |\ \beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}=1\},

referred to as
standard conjugate circles
. By applying conformal maps to the pair of curves
S_{0}
and
S_{0}^{\perp}
one obtains new pairs of curves over which a mean value theorem holds for solutions of the ultrahyperbolic equation. Following John, such pairs of curves arising as the conformal image of
S_{0},S_{0}^{\perp}
are called
non-degenerate conjugate conics
.

Given a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics
S,S^{\perp}
there always exists a unique pair of affine
2
-planes
\pi,\pi^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
such that
S
is contained in
\pi
and
S^{\perp}
is contained in
\pi^{\perp}
. We provide a classification of non-degenerate conjugate conics depending on the signature of the induced metric on the containing planes by the pseudo-Riemmanian metric
g

[
5
, Definition 3.2]
. Here a plane is said to be
definite
,
indefinite
or
degenerate
according to whether the metric induced on the plane by the ambient metric is definite, indefinite or degenerate, respectively.

The following notation is used to refer to the hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
centered at
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and with radius-square
\pm r^{2}
,

H_{\underline{a},\pm r^{2}}:=\{\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\ |\ ||\underline{x}-\underline{a}||^{2}=\pm r^{2}\}.

The notation
C_{\underline{a}}
is used to refer to the hypersphere of zero radius
r=0
with center at
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
.
With that, all pairs of non-degenerate conjugate conics are listed in the following result:

Theorem 1.1

(Classification of non-degenerate conjugate conics according to the containing planes)
.

Let
S,S^{\perp}
be two non-degenerate conjugate conics. Then they correspond to either one of the following:

•

Case 1
.
Line-Empty Pairs
:
S
is a line and
S^{\perp}
is at infinity (or vice versa).

\begin{cases}$S$\text{ is a line.}\\
S^{\perp}=\emptyset\end{cases}

•

Case 2
.
S
and
S^{\perp}
are contained in complementary affine planes
\pi
and
\pi^{\perp}
, respectively.

(i)

Circles
in definite planes:

\begin{cases}S=\pi\cap H_{\underline{p},\pm r^{2}}\\
S^{\perp}=\pi^{\perp}\cap H_{\underline{p},\mp r^{2}},\end{cases}

where
\pi,\pi^{\perp}
are definite affine planes in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
intersecting at
\underline{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and
r^{2}\neq 0
.

(ii)

Hyperbolae
in indefinite planes:

\begin{cases}S=\pi\cap H_{\underline{p},\pm r^{2}}\\
S^{\perp}=\pi^{\perp}\cap H_{\underline{p},\mp r^{2}},\end{cases}

where
\pi,\pi^{\perp}
are indefinite affine planes in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
intersecting at
\underline{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and
r^{2}\neq 0
.

(iii)

Parabolae
in degenerate planes:

\begin{cases}S=\pi\cap C_{\underline{\tilde{p}}}\\
S^{\perp}=\pi^{\perp}\cap C_{\underline{p}},\end{cases}

where
\pi,\pi^{\perp}
are non-intersecting degenerate affine planes in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and
\underline{p}\in\pi,\underline{\tilde{p}}\in\pi^{\perp}
are null-separated points.

The proof relies on the characterization of non-degenerate conjugate conics as intersections of hyperspheres in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. Through the use of polyspherical coordinates of hyperspheres it is possible to identify a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics
S,S^{\perp}
with a pair of complementary indefinite linear
3
-dimensional subspaces
V,V^{\perp}
in a six-dimensional space of neutral signature
(3,3)
, denoted
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
.

The planes
\pi,\pi^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
containing the conics are then identified with the intersections of
V\cap\mathcal{P}
and
V^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{P}
respectively, where
\mathcal{P}
is a special degenerate
5
-subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
containing the polyspherical coordinates of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. The different possibilities for the signature of the metric induced on the spaces
V\cap\mathcal{P},V^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{P}
make up the different cases for the non-degenerate conjugate conics.

Altogether, one obtains the conformal version of Asgeirsson’s mean value theorem for solutions of the ultrahyperbolic equation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u
be a solution of the ultra-hyperbolic equation. Let
S,S^{\perp}
be a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}
. Whenever the integrals are defined, we have

\int_{S}u\ dl=\int_{S^{\perp}}u\ dl,

where
dl
is the line element induced on the curves
S,S^{\perp}
by the flat metric
g
.

In
[
11
]
the first link was made between the ultrahyperbolic equation and straight lines in space. In particular, the variables
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
are interpreted as local coordinates in the manifold of straight lines through Plücker coordinates
[
11
, pg. 303]
, and solutions of equation (
1.1
) are shown to be line integrals of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In other words, the ultrahyperbolic equation is the compatibility condition that a line function arising as a line integral of a scalar function in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
must satisfy.

Asgeirsson’s relation (
1.2
) has a special interpretation when
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
are seen as line coordinates in the way proposed by John. The curves
S_{0},S_{0}^{\perp}
over which this mean value property holds correspond to the two families of generating lines of the one-sheeted hyperboloid of revolution

H_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
given by
x^{2}+y^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(1+z^{2})
. Similarly, non-degenerate conjugate conics are shown to correspond to the pairs of families of lines of non-planar doubly ruled surfaces in
3
-space.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
S,S^{\perp}
be two curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
representing the two one-parameter families of lines
L,L^{\perp}
in
3
-space. Then
S,S^{\perp}
are a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
if and only if
L
and
L^{\perp}
are the two families of generating lines of a non-planar doubly ruled surface in
3
-space.

In Section 2, it is shown that a solution of the ultrahyperbolic equation has the mean value property over any pair of curves
S,S^{\perp}
that arise as the image of
S_{0},S_{0}^{\perp}
under an arbitrary conformal mapping of
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. This proves Theorem
1.2
.

Section 3 contains a formal treatment of conjugate conics as objects in the conformal geometry of the pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. A complete list of all non-degenerate conjugate conics is given, proving Theorem
1.1
.

In Section 4, the one-to-one correspondence between pairs of non-degenerate conjugate conics and pairs of families of generating lines of doubly ruled surfaces in
3
-space is proven, as per Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Conformal extension of Asgeirsson’s mean value theorem

Define
S_{0},S_{0}^{\perp}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}
to be the pair of circles

S_{0}:=\{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},0,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}\ |\ \alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=1\},

and

S_{0}^{\perp}:=\{(0,0,\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}\ |\ \beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}=1\}.

In this dimension the original mean value theorem of Asgeirsson can be stated:

Theorem 2.1

(Asgeirsson’s Theorem
[
2
]
)
.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a solution of
\Delta u=0
. Then the following integral equation holds

\int_{S_{0}}u\ dl=\int_{S_{0}^{\perp}}u\ dl,

where
dl
represents the line element induced by the flat metric
g=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}-dx_{3}^{2}-dx_{4}^{2}
.

Asgeirsson also proves a similar result over pairs of circles whose center is not necessarily the origin and of arbitrary radius. John mentions in
[
11
]
that an Asgeirsson’s theorem in its most general form can be obtained by applying isometries of the neutral metric to those circles. However, in the present paper a more general way of extending Asgeirsson’s theorem is considered through the use of conformal isometries of the metric.

The flat Laplace operator
\Delta
is the ultrahyperbolic operator which acts on twice continuously differentiable functions
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\Delta u:=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{4}^{2}}.

The ultrahyperbolic equation (
1.1
) can be seen as the Laplace equation
\Delta u=0
in the pseudo-Riemmanian manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\equiv(\mathbb{R}^{4},g)
. It is well-known that solutions of the Laplace equation show conformal invariance, in the sense that if
f:\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
is a conformal diffeomorphism

g(df(v),df(w))=\lambda g(v,w)\qquad v,w\in T\mathbb{R}^{2,2},

with conformal factor
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{2,2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then
\lambda(u\circ f)
solves the Laplace equation if and only if
u
does
[
5
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Conformal invariance of the ultrahyperbolic equation (
1.1
) can be exploited to extend the mean value relation stated originally by Asgeirsson:

Proposition 2.2
.

[
5
, Proposition 2.12]

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a solution of
\Delta u=0
and
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2,2}
be a conformal map. Then

\int_{f(S_{0})}u\ dl=\int_{f(S_{0}^{\perp})}u\ dl,

where
dl
represents the line element induced by the flat metric
g
.

The purpose of the present paper is to the determine the most general image of the circles
S_{0},S_{0}^{\perp}
under a conformal map of
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. Any pair of curves
S,S^{\perp}
arising in this manner is called a pair of
non-degenerate conjugate conics
, and according to Proposition
2.2
, a mean value relation for solutions of equation (
1.1
) will hold over it. Once these have been classified as in Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
follows.

3.
Pseudo-conformal geometry of
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}

Consider the affine space
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
endowed with the bilinear form

\langle\underline{x},\underline{x}^{\prime}\rangle:=x_{1}x_{1}^{\prime}+x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}-x_{3}x_{3}^{\prime}-x_{4}x_{4}^{\prime},

for all
\underline{x},\underline{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and associated quadratic form of signature
(2,2)

||\underline{x}||^{2}:=\langle\underline{x},\underline{x}\rangle=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}-x_{4}^{2},

for all
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
.

It is well-known that the conformal geometry of the pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
is better understood through its embedding into the null-cone in
\mathbb{R}^{p+1,q+1}

[
14
]
. As we will be working with the spaces
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
at the same time, we will use different notations to distinguish between the two spaces. Vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
will be denoted using underline notation, and boldface notation will be used for vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
. Moreover, in a slight abuse of notation, a vector
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
will often be represented in the way
\mathbf{s}=(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})
, where
\underline{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and
s_{0},s_{5}\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.1.
Diagonal polyspherical coordinates of hyperspheres

Hyperspheres are the fundamental objects of conformal geometry, in the sense that they are the most general subsets preserved under conformal maps. A hypersphere can either be:

(i).

a
proper hypersphere
given by an equation of the form
||\underline{x}-\underline{a}||^{2}=\pm r^{2}

(ii).

a
hyperplane
defined by
\langle\underline{a},\underline{x}\rangle=b

These objects can be defined in a unified way by an equation of the form

(3.1)

\mathsf{s}_{0}||\underline{x}||^{2}+2\langle\underline{\mathsf{s}},\underline{x}\rangle+2\mathsf{s}_{5}=0.

The coefficients
\underline{\mathsf{s}}=(\mathsf{s}_{1},\mathsf{s}_{2},\mathsf{s}_{3},\mathsf{s}_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
are called the
polyspherical coordinates
of the hypersphere (
3.1
). Polyspherical coordinates uniquely determine a hypersphere, but they are defined up to scaling, as multiplying equation (
3.1
) by a non-zero factor leaves the solution set invariant. Further information on polyspherical coordinates of hyperspheres can be found in
[
1
]
.

Knowing the polyspherical coordinates of a hypersphere, one can find its center and radius-square as follows:

\underline{a}=-\frac{\underline{\mathsf{s}}}{\mathsf{s}_{0}},\qquad\qquad\pm r^{2}=\frac{||\underline{\mathsf{s}}||^{2}-2\mathsf{s}_{0}\mathsf{s}_{5}}{\mathsf{s}_{0}^{2}}.

In this paper, a linear transformation of the polyspherical coordinates will be considered so that the expression on top of the radius-square is in diagonal form, i.e.
sums of squares
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
H
be the hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
given by the equation

(3.2)

(s_{0}+s_{5})||\underline{x}||^{2}-2\langle\underline{s},\underline{x}\rangle+(-s_{0}+s_{5})=0.

The coefficients
\mathbf{s}:=(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})=(s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4},s_{5})\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
are called the
diagonal polyspherical coordinates (DPC)
of the hypersphere
H
. Sometimes the notation
\mathbf{s}_{H}
will be used to emphasize the relation between the coordinates and the hypersphere.

As noted before, the solution set of equation (
3.2
) is invariant under rescaling of the numbers
(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5}
, thus the diagonal polyspherical coordinates of a hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
are regarded as points in the projective space
\mathbb{R}P^{5}
. It is useful to work with the
projective normalization condition

(3.3)

s_{0}+s_{5}=1,

as it will be shown now. If
\mathbf{s}:=(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})
are the normalized DPC of a hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
(so that
s_{0}+s_{5}=1
), the center and radius-square have simple expressions in terms of the DPC:

\underline{a}=\underline{s},\qquad\qquad\pm r^{2}=s_{0}^{2}+||\underline{s}||^{2}-s_{5}^{2}.

Consider the neutral bilinear form in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
given by

(3.4)

(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime}):=s_{0}s_{0}^{\prime}+s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}+s_{2}s_{2}^{\prime}-s_{3}s_{3}^{\prime}-s_{4}s_{4}^{\prime}-s_{5}s_{5}^{\prime}.

The associated quadratic form of neutral signature
(3,3)

(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s})=s_{0}^{2}+||\underline{s}||^{2}-s_{5}^{2},

is the expression of the radius-square of a hypersphere. Thus, the six-dimensional space of polyspherical coordinates of hyperspheres in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
is naturally endowed with a bilinear structure of neutral signature
(3,3)
. Call
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{6}
endowed with this neutral bilinear form.

Example 3.2
.

The
proper hypersphere
centered at
\underline{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
and radius-square
\pm r^{2}
of equation

H_{\underline{p},\pm r^{2}}:||\underline{x}-\underline{p}||^{2}=\pm r^{2},

has polyspherical coordinates

(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})=\left(\frac{1-||\underline{p}||^{2}\pm r^{2}}{2},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},\frac{1+||\underline{p}||^{2}\mp r^{2}}{2}\right).

The
standard positive hypersphere
in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
is given by
||\underline{x}||^{2}=1
,
and has diagonal polyspherical coordinates

(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})=(1,0,0,0,0,0).

The
standard negative hypersphere
given by
||\underline{x}||^{2}=-1
has DPC

(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})=(0,0,0,0,0,1).

Example 3.3
.

A hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}

\langle\underline{a},\underline{x}\rangle:=a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}-a_{3}x_{3}-a_{4}x_{4}=b,

has diagonal polyspherical coordinates

(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})=(-b,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},b).

Definition 3.4
.

The proper hypersphere
C_{\underline{p}}
centered at
\underline{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}

||\underline{x}-\underline{p}||^{2}=0,

and of zero radius-square
is called the
isotropic cone
centered at
\underline{p}
.
A hyperplane
H
given by equation
\langle\underline{a},\underline{x}\rangle=b
is said to be a

whenever
||\underline{a}||^{2}=0
.

Example 3.5
.

The isotropic cone
C_{\underline{p}}
has diagonal polyspherical coordinates

(3.5)

\mathbf{s}_{\underline{p}}=\left(\frac{1-||\underline{p}||^{2}}{2},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4},\frac{1+||\underline{p}||^{2}}{2}\right).

The null hyperplane

The diagonal polyspherical coordinates of null hyperplanes and isotropic cones both satisfy the quadratic condition

\mathbf{s}=(s_{0},\underline{s},s_{5})\in\mathbb{R}^{3,3}

Definition 3.6
.

Define

to be the null-cone in

, i.e.

\displaystyle Q_{1}

.

The set of isotropic cones in

is in one-to-one correspondence with the points in

.
Moreover, the map

3.2.

3.3.

Let

Proposition 3.12
.

The following orthogonality conditions hold:

(i)

(iii)

\underline{p}
and
\underline{p}^{\prime}
are null-separated, i.e.
||\underline{p}-\underline{p}^{\prime}||^{2}=0

(iv)

(v)

A hyperplane

the center
\underline{p}

\langle\underline{a},\underline{p}\rangle=b

Lemma 3.13

\underline{x}=\left(\frac{p_{2}+q_{2}}{q_{3}},\frac{-p_{1}-q_{1}}{q_{3}},\frac{p_{2}-q_{2}}{q_{3}},\frac{-p_{1}+q_{1}}{q_{3}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}.

Observe that both horizontal lines (those for which
q_{3}=0
) and improper lines are not covered by these lines coordinates, i.e. they appear to be
at infinity
in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
. The following important result explains the connection between projective line geometry and the geometry of the pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Two lines in
3
-space intersect or are parallel if and only if their corresponding vectors
\underline{x},\underline{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
defined as in equation (
4.1
) are null-separated (when defined), i.e.
||\underline{x}-x^{\prime}||^{2}=0
.

In this section, the null-separation properties between the points in non-degenerate conjugate conics are used to show their relation to the two families of generating lines of a doubly ruled surface in
3
-space.

4.1.
Null-separation property of non-degenerate conjugate conics

Lemma 4.2
.

Three isotropic cones in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
intersect on a non-degenerate conic if and only if the centers are not null-separated.

Proof.

Let
\underline{a},\underline{a}^{\prime},\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
be three non-null-separated points. Let
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
be the diagonal polyspherical coordinates of the isotropic cones
C_{\underline{a}},C_{\underline{a}^{\prime}}
and
C_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}
, respectively. From the non-null-separation of the centers it follows from Lemma
3.13
that

(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime}),(\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}),(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime})\neq 0.

As
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}
are DPC of isotropic cones we also have the nullity condition

(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s})=(\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})=(\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime})=0.

Let
G=(\mathbf{s}^{i},\mathbf{s}^{j})
. Then:

\displaystyle\Tr G

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\Det G

\displaystyle=2(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})(\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime})(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime})\neq 0.

Let
S
be the conic determined by the intersection of the three null cones. The associated
3
-subspace
V=\spn\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{s}^{\prime\prime}\}
is non-degenerate and has indefinite signature, and thus
S
is non-degenerate. Moreover, if ones relaxes the non-null-separation of the centers, the associated
3
-subspace
V
becomes degenerate, hence the converse also holds.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Two curves
S,S^{\perp}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
are non-degenerate conjugate conics if and only if for all non-null-separated points
\underline{a},\underline{a}^{\prime},\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}\in S
and non-null-separated points
\underline{b},\underline{b}^{\prime},\underline{b}^{\prime\prime}\in S^{\perp}
we have

S=C_{\underline{b}}\cap C_{\underline{b}^{\prime}}\cap C_{\underline{b}^{\prime\prime}},

and

S^{\perp}=C_{\underline{a}}\cap C_{\underline{a}^{\prime}}\cap C_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

Let
S,S^{\perp}
be a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics with respective associated
3
-subspaces
V,V^{\perp}
, and choose any three non-null-separated points
\underline{a},\underline{a}^{\prime},\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}\in S
. By Proposition
3.19
the vectors
\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}
defined as in equation (
3.5
) belong to the subspace
V^{\perp}
. Moreover, as the points are non-null-separated in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
, the corresponding vectors form a null basis of
V^{\perp}
. A point
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
will lie in the intersection
C_{\underline{a}}\cap C_{\underline{a}^{\prime}}\cap C_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}
if and only if the vector
\mathbf{s}_{\underline{x}}
is orthogonal to
\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
. Hence
\mathbf{s}_{\underline{x}}\in(V^{\perp})^{\perp}=V
and it follows that
\underline{x}\in S^{\perp}
. A similar argument shows that
S
is obtained as the intersection of three isotropic cones centered at any non-null-separated three points in
S^{\perp}
.

We now prove the converse. Let
\underline{a},\underline{a}^{\prime},\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}
be non-null-separated points in the curve
S
. It follows from Lemma
4.2
that
S^{\perp}=C_{\underline{a}}\cap C_{\underline{a}^{\prime}}\cap C_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}
is a non-degenerate conic with associated indefinite
3
-subspace
V^{\perp}=\spn\{\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}}\}
. Similarly, choosing three non-null-separated points
\underline{b},\underline{b}^{\prime},\underline{b}^{\prime\prime}\in S^{\perp}
we find that
S
is a non-degenerate conic with associated indefinite
3
-subspace
V=\spn\{\mathbf{s}_{\underline{b}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{b}^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{b}^{\prime\prime}}\}
. As the points
\underline{a},\underline{a}^{\prime},\underline{a}^{\prime\prime}
are null-separated from the points
\underline{b},\underline{b}^{\prime},\underline{b}^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
, it follows from Lemma

3.13
that the vectors
\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{a}}
are pairwise orthogonal to
\mathbf{s}_{\underline{b}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{b}^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{\underline{b}^{\prime\prime}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
. Hence the spaces
V
and
V^{\perp}
are complementary in
\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
, and it follows by Proposition
3.25
that
S
and
S^{\perp}
are a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

(Null-distance property between points of non-degenerate conjugate conics). Given a pair
S,S^{\perp}
of non-degenerate conjugate conics and two points
\underline{a}\in S
and
\underline{b}\in S^{\perp}
then
||\underline{a}-\underline{b}||^{2}=0
.

4.2.
Relation to lines of a doubly ruled surface

Definition 4.5
.

A
non-planar doubly ruled surface
is either a one-sheeted hyperboloid or a hyperbolic paraboloid. The two foliations by lines that these surfaces admit are called the two
families of generating lines
of the surface.

We use the characterization of the lines of a doubly ruled surface from
[
10
]
. The locus of lines incident to three skew lines in space is a family of lines
L
of a doubly ruled surface (a one-sheeted hyperboloid when the original lines are in general position, and a hyperbolic paraboloid when they are parallel to a fixed plane). The other family of lines
L^{\perp}
is obtained as the locus of lines incident to any three lines from
L
. Note in particular how this means that given any three skew lines in space, there is a unique doubly ruled surface containing those lines.

Proposition 4.6
.

Two one-parameter families of lines
L,L^{\perp}
in
3
-space are the two families of generating lines of a non-planar doubly ruled surface if and only if for every three skew lines
r,r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}\in L
and three skew lines
s,s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\perp}
we have

L
is the locus of lines incident to
s,s^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}
,

and

L^{\perp}
is the locus of lines incident to
r,r^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
S,S^{\perp}
be a pair of non-degenerate conjugate conics in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
and let
L,L^{\prime}
be the lines representing them.
The condition from Proposition
4.3
is equivalent to the condition from Proposition
4.6
, as null-separation of points in
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
is the same as incidence of lines they represent in
3
-space. Hence,
S,S^{\perp}
are non-degenerate conjugate conics iff they satisfy the condition from Proposition
4.3
iff
L,L^{\perp}
satisfy the condition from Proposition
4.6
iff
L,L^{\prime}
are the two families of a non-planar doubly ruled surface in
3
-space.
∎

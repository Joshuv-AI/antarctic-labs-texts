Intersection theorems for uniform subfamilies of hereditary families
Andrey Kupavskii

Abstract
. A family 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of sets is hereditary if whenever A ∈ 𝒞 A\in\mathcal{C} and B ⊂ A B\subset A , we have B ∈ 𝒞 B\in\mathcal{C} . Chvátal conjectured that the largest intersecting subfamily of a hereditary family is the family of all sets containing a fixed element. This is a generalization of the non-uniform Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem. A natural uniform variant of this question, which is essentially a generalization for the uniform Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem, was suggested by Borg: given a hereditary family 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , in which all maximal sets have size at least n n , what is the largest intersecting subfamily of the family of all k k -element sets in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} ? The answer, of course, depends on n n and k k , and Borg conjectured that for n ≥ 2 ​ k n\geq 2k the it is again the family of all k k -element sets containing a singleton. Borg proved this conjecture for n ≥ k 3 n\geq k^{3} . He also considered a t t -intersecting variant of the question. In this paper, we improve the bound on n n for both intersecting and t t -intersecting cases, showing that for n ≥ C ​ k ​ t ​ log 2 ​ n k n\geq Ckt\log^{2}\frac{n}{k} and n ≥ C ​ k ​ log ⁡ k n\geq Ck\log k the largest t t -intersecting subfamily of the k k -th layer of a hereditary family with maximal sets of size at least n n is the family of all sets containing a fixed t t -element set. We also prove a stability result.



1.
Introduction

In this note, we address a variant of the following classical problem. Assume that
\mathcal{C}\subset 2^{[N]}
is a simplicial complex (down-closed family). Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}
be intersecting. Chvátal
[
8
]
asked for the following generalization of the famous Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem: is it true that the largest such family is the family of all sets in
\mathcal{C}
that contain some fixed element? In the notation introduced below, is it true that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{C}(x)|
for some element
x\in[N]
?

A natural variant of the question by Chvátal was proposed by Borg
[
3
]
, in parallel with the uniform Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem. For a family
\mathcal{A}
, let
\mathcal{A}^{(k)}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the subfamily of all
k
-element sets. We say that a simplicial complex has rank
\geq n
if all maximal sets in
\mathcal{C}
have size at least
n
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a simplicial complex of rank
\geq n
and
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
be intersecting. Is it true that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)
for some
x\in[N]
, provided that
n\geq 2k
? Note that the conjectured bound is the same as in the uniform Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem. Borg proved it for
n>(k-1)(k^{2}+1)+k

[
3
,
6
]
. Another motivation for this question lies in the fact that it generalizes a question raised by Holroyd and Talbot
[
11
]
on intersecting families of independent sets in graphs. Holroyd and Talbot studied families of independent
k
-element sets in a fixed graph
G
and asked, for which
k
the largest intersecting family of such sets is a star. Holroyd and Talbot conjectured that it is the case if the smallest maximal independent set has size at least
2k
. The connection to the question of Borg can be readily seen if one reformulates the latter question in terms of intersecting families of
k
-element sets in the independent set complex of
G
.

A natural generalization of this question is to consider
t
-intersecting families. For
{[n]\choose k}
we have a theorem due to Frankl
[
9
]
and Wilson
[
14
]
that the largest
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\choose k}
is a family of all sets containing a set
T
of cardinality
t
, provided
n\geq(t+1)(n-t+1)
. Borg
[
3
]
conjectured that the analogue of this result holds for any simplicial complex of rank
n
, where
n\geq(t+1)(k-t+1)
. The validity of the conjecture was shown by Borg
[
3
]
for
n\geq(k-t){3k-2t-1\choose t+1}+k
and later improved by him in
[
6
]
to
n\geq(k-t)(k{k\choose t}+1)+k
. (The bound for
t=1
cited above is a particular case of the latter bound.)

For a family
\mathcal{A}\subset 2^{[n]}
, sets
B,X,Y\subset[n]
, where
X\subset Y
, and a family
\mathcal{S}\subset 2^{[n]}
, we will use the following notation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\bar{B})=

\displaystyle\{F:F\in\mathcal{A},F\cap B=\emptyset\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(B)=

\displaystyle\{F\setminus B:F\in\mathcal{A},B\subset F\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}[B]=

\displaystyle\{F:F\in\mathcal{A},B\subset F\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{S}]=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{A}[B]

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle\{A\setminus X:A\in\mathcal{A},A\cap Y=X\}

Whenever
B=\{x\}
for some element
x\in[n]
, we will write

\mathcal{A}(x)
instead of
\mathcal{A}(\{x\})
etc.

In this note, we prove the following theorem that greatly improves the bounds of Borg and brings them pretty close to the conjectured thresholds.

Theorem 1
.

Let
N,n,k,t
be positive integers that satisfy the following inequalities:
n\geq 2^{13}k\log_{2}(2k)
and
n\geq 2^{19}tk\log_{2}^{2}\frac{n}{k}.
Let
\mathcal{C}\subset 2^{[N]}
be a simplicial complex of rank
\geq n
. If
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
is
t
-intersecting then there exists a set
T
of size
t
in
[N]
such that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|
. Moreover, if
m=\min_{T\in{[N]\choose t}}|\mathcal{F}(T)\setminus\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\Big\{\max_{T\in{[N]\choose t}}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|-m2^{2^{-20}\frac{n}{k\log_{2}\frac{n}{k}}},0.6\max_{T\in{[N]\choose t}}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|\Big\}.

We note that both conditions on
n
are satisfied if one assumes
n\geq Ctk\log^{2}k
, where
C
is some constant. In order to prove this theorem, we use and refine the method of spread approximations developed by Zakharov and the author
[
12
]
.

We note that this theorem improves some of the known results on the Holroyd and Talbot conjecture. In particular, it improves the results of Frankl and Hurlbert
[
10
]
for bounded degree graphs and spider trees.

In the same vein, we can ask many other extremal questions for subfamilies of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
, such as: what is the largest subfamily avoiding intersection
t-1
? What is the largest family with no
s
-matching? The present method allows for such results.

2.
Preliminaries

A family
\mathcal{A}
is
r
-spread
if for any set
X
we have
|\mathcal{A}(X)|\leq r^{-|X|}|\mathcal{A}|
.
Given a family
\mathcal{A}\subset 2^{[n]}
of sets and
q,r\geq 1
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is
(r,q)
-spread
if for each
S\in{[n]\choose\leq q}
, the family
\mathcal{A}(S)
is
r
-spread. Note that putting
S=\emptyset
implies that
\mathcal{A}
is
r
-spread, so
(r,q)
-spreadness is a stronger condition than the usual
r
-spreadness. A set
W\subset[n]
is
p
-random
if each element of
[n]
is included in
W
independently and with probability
p
.

The following statement is a variant due to Tao
[
13
]
of the breakthrough result that was proved by Alweiss, Lowett, Wu and Zhang
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2

(
[
1
]
, a sharpening due to
[
13
]
)
.

If for some
n,r,k\geq 1
a family
\mathcal{F}\subset{[n]\leq k}
is
r
-spread and
W
is an
(m\delta)
-random subset of
[n]
, then

\Pr[\exists F\in\mathcal{F}:\ F\subset W]\geq 1-\Big(\frac{5}{\log_{2}(r\delta)}\Big)^{m}k.

We will need the following slight variant of a statement from the paper of Kupavskii and Zakharov
[
12
]
. We give a proof here for completeness.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n,k,t\geq 1
be some integers and
\mathcal{A}\subset 2^{[n]}
be an “ambient” family. Consider a family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{A}\cap{[n]\choose\leq k}
that is
t
-intersecting. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is
(r_{0},t)
-spread. Fix some
q
, and assume that the parameters satisfy the following inequalities:
r_{0}>r\geq 2q\geq 2t
and
r>2^{12}\log_{2}(2k)
.

Then there exists a
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{S}
of sets of size at most
q
and a ‘remainder’
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that

(i)

We have
\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{S}]
;

(ii)

for any
B\in\mathcal{S}
there is a family
\mathcal{F}_{B}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{B}(B)
is
r
-spread;

(iii)

For some
T
of size
t
we have
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq r^{q+1}r_{0}^{t-q-1}|\mathcal{A}(T)|
.

Proof.

Consider the following procedure for
i=1,2,\ldots
with
\mathcal{F}^{1}:=\mathcal{F}
.

(1)

Find an inclusion-maximal set
S_{i}
such that
|\mathcal{F}^{i}(S_{i})|\geq r^{-|S_{i}|}|\mathcal{F}^{i}|
;

(2)

If
|S_{i}|>q
or
\mathcal{F}^{i}=\emptyset
then stop. Otherwise, put
\mathcal{F}^{i+1}:=\mathcal{F}^{i}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{i}[S_{i}]
.

The family
\mathcal{F}^{i}(S_{i})
is
r
-spread. Indeed, arguing indirectly, assume that there is a set
S_{i}^{\prime}
disjoint from
S_{i}
violating this. We have
|\mathcal{F}^{i}(S_{i}\sqcup S_{i}^{\prime})|\geq r^{-|S_{i}^{\prime}|}|\mathcal{F}^{i}(S_{i})|\geq r^{-|S_{i}^{\prime}|-|S_{i}|}|\mathcal{F}^{i}|
, a contradiction with the maximality of
S_{i}.

Let
N
be the step of the procedure for
\mathcal{F}
at which we stop. Put
\mathcal{S}:=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{N-1}\}
. Clearly,
|S_{i}|\leq q
for each
i\in[N-1]
. The family
\mathcal{F}_{B}
promised in (ii) is defined to be
\mathcal{F}^{i}[S_{i}]
for
B=S_{i}
. Next, note that if
\mathcal{F}^{N}
is non-empty, then for any subset
T\subset S_{N}
of size
t
we have

|\mathcal{F}^{N}|\leq r^{|S_{N}|}|\mathcal{F}^{N}(S_{N})|\leq r^{|S_{N}|}|\mathcal{A}(S_{N})|\leq r^{|S_{N}|}r_{0}^{-|S_{N}|+t}|\mathcal{A}(T)|\leq r^{q+1}r_{0}^{-q-1+t}|\mathcal{A}(T)|,

where in the second to last inequality we used the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is
(r_{0},t)
-spread, and in the last inequality we used
|S_{N}|\geq q+1
and
r_{0}>r
.
We put
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{F}^{m}
. Since either
|S_{m}|>q
or
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, and, moreover,
r_{0}\geq r,
we have
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq r^{q+1}r_{0}^{t-q-1}|\mathcal{A}(T)|
.

The last and crucial thing to verify is the
t
-intersection property of
\mathcal{S}
.
Take any (not necessarily distinct)
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
and assume that
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|<t
. Recall that for both
\ell\in\{1,2\}
the family
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}:=\mathcal{F}_{S_{\ell}}(S_{\ell})
is
r
-spread. We use this in the second inequality below.

|\mathcal{G}_{1}(\bar{S}_{2})|\geq|\mathcal{G}_{1}|-\sum_{x\in S_{2}\setminus S_{1}}|\mathcal{G}_{1}(\{x\})|\geq\Big(1-\frac{|S_{2}|}{r}\Big)|\mathcal{G}_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{G}_{1}|.

In the last inequality we used that
r\geq 2q.
The same is valid for
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bar{S}_{1})
. Note that both
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{G}_{1}(\bar{S}_{2})
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bar{S}_{1})
are subfamilies of
2^{[n]\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})}.
Moreover,
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}^{\prime}
is
\frac{r}{2}
-spread for both
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Indeed, this holds because for any non-empty
Y
we have
|\mathcal{G}_{\ell}^{\prime}(Y)|\leq|\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(Y)|\leq r^{-|Y|}|\mathcal{G}_{\ell}|\leq 2r^{-|Y|}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{\ell}|\leq(r/2)^{-|Y|}|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{\ell}|
.

Next, we apply Theorem
2
. Let us put
m=\log_{2}(2k)
and
\delta=(2\log_{2}(2k))^{-1}
. Note that
m\delta=\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{r}{2}\delta>2^{10}
by our choice of
r
. Theorem
2
implies that a
\frac{1}{2}
-random subset
W
of
[n]\setminus(S_{i}\cup S_{j})
contains a set from
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{\prime}
with probability strictly bigger than

1-\Big(\frac{5}{\log_{2}2^{10}}\Big)^{\log_{2}2k}k=1-2^{-\log_{2}2k}k=\frac{1}{2}.

Consider a random partition of
[n]\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2})
into
2
parts
U_{1},U_{2}
, including each element with probability
1/2
in each of the parts. Then both
U_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, are distributed as
W
above. Thus, the probability that there is
F_{\ell}\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell}^{\prime}
, such that
F_{\ell}\subset U_{\ell}
, is strictly bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
. Using the union bound, we conclude that, with positive probability, it holds that there are such
F_{\ell}
,
F_{\ell}\subset U_{\ell},
for each
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. Fix such a choice of
U_{\ell}
and
F_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\{1,2\}
. But then, on the one hand, both
F_{1}\cup S_{1}
and
F_{2}\cup S_{2}
belong to
\mathcal{F}
and, on the other hand,
|(F_{1}\cup S_{1})\cap(F_{2}\cup S_{2})|=|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|<t
, a contradiction with
\mathcal{F}
being
t
-intersecting.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

We start by showing that Theorem
3
is relevant for the case of simplicial complexes.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset 2^{[N]}
be a simplicial complex of rank
\geq n
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
is
\big(\frac{n}{k},k\big)
-spread.

Proof.

Let us first show that for any
x\in[N]
we have that
|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)|\leq\frac{k}{n}\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
. In order to do so, we use the local LYM inequality. Consider a bipartite graph
G
with parts
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
and
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)
. We connect two vertices by an edge if one of the corresponding sets contain the other.

Take an arbitrary vertex of
G
from the first part. It has degree
1
in
G
if the corresponding set
F
contains
x
(there is only one set from
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)
contained in
F
, that is,
F\setminus\{x\}
). Note that there are exactly
|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)|
such vertices. Otherwise, it has degree at most
k
since it has
k
subsets of size
k-1
. Take an arbitrary vertex
F
from the second part. There is a maximal set
W
of size at least
n
such that
F\subset W
, and thus all sets of the form
F\cup\{w\}
,
w\in W\setminus F
, belong to
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
. Thus, it has degree at least
n-k+1
in
G
. Double counting the number of edges in the graph, we get

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)|+k(|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}|-|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)|)\geq(n-k+1)|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)|,

which is equivalent to

\dfrac{|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(x)|}{|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}|}\leq\dfrac{k}{n}.

Note that for any
Y\subset[N]
of size
\leq k
the family
\mathcal{C}(Y)
is a simplicial complex of rank
\geq n-|Y|
, and the family
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(Y)
is a family of uniformity
k-|Y|
. By what we have proved above, for any
x\in[N]\setminus Y
we have

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(Y\cup\{x\})|\leq\frac{k-|Y|}{n-|Y|}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(Y)|\leq\frac{k}{n}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(Y)|.

Applying this inequality iteratively, it is straightforward to see that for any disjoint
X,Y\subset[N]
, where
|Y|\leq k,
we have

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(X\cup Y)|\leq\Big(\frac{n}{k}\Big)^{|X|}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(Y)|.

This shows that the family
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
is
(\frac{n}{k},k)
-spread.
∎

.

Recall that a

-intersecting family

.

Let

Let

We will need the following lemma that relates the sizes of complexes and its restrictions.

Lemma 7
.

Fix some integers

. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a simplicial complex of rank

,

be a subset of size

, and

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,F\cup T)|\geq\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-s-t}\Big)^{s}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|

Put

,
T_{i}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
for

, and let us prove the following inequality for each

(1)

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,F\cup T_{i})|\geq\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-s-t}\Big)|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,F\cup T_{i-1})|.

In order to prove this, put

\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{(k)}[T]
. In what follows, we assume that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
is non-empty (otherwise, we are done). We need to show that
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|<|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}[T]\setminus\mathcal{F}|
. Note that
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|
, and thus the bound (
3
) is valid for
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|
as well.

The next argument borrows some of the ideas from the proof of Theorem
3
. Since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
is non-empty, we can find a maximal subset
U
of the ground set that satisfies
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(U)|\geq r^{-|U|}|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|
. Note that the family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(U)
is
r
-spread. Informally, our aim is to show that

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)\setminus\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)|\gg|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|.

In order to do so, we proceed in two steps. The first step is to show that
|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)|\gg|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|.
The second step is to show that a large portion of the sets in
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)
cannot belong to
\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U).

Let us do the first step. We have to consider two cases. The first is that
|U|\leq q
. In this case, we use (
3
) as an upper bound on
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|
. At the same time, Lemma
7
implies

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,U\cup T)|\geq\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-|U|-t}\Big)^{|U|}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|\geq\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-q-t}\Big)^{q}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|,

where the last inequality is due to our choice of parameters.
Thus, we get that

(4)

\frac{|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)|}{|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|}\geq 2^{2^{-19}\frac{n}{k\log_{2}\frac{n}{k}}-1}.

If
|U|\geq q+1
then we have the following analogue of (
3
):

(5)

|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|\leq r^{|U|}r_{0}^{t-|U|}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|=2^{-|U|+q+1}r^{q+1}r_{0}^{t-q-1}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|\\
\leq 2^{-|U|+q+1}2^{-2^{-19}\frac{n}{k\log_{2}\frac{n}{k}}}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|.

At the same time, Lemma
7
implies

|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,U\cup T)|\geq\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-|U|-t}\Big)^{|U|}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|\geq\\
\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-|U|-t}\Big)^{q+1}\cdot\Big(1-\frac{k-t}{n-|U|-t}\Big)^{|U|-q-1}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{(-|U|+q+1)/2}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|.

(Note that the last inequality is very loose.) Thus, we can see that (
4
) holds for
|U|\geq q+1
as well.

We go on to the second step. First, consider the family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(U)
. It is
\frac{n}{2k}
-spread. Then,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(U,T\cup U)
is
(\frac{n}{2k}-t)
-spread (cf. the proof of Theorem
3
). The families
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(U,T\cup U)
and
\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)
are cross-intersecting.
Assume that
|\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)|
. Note that
\mathcal{C}(T,T\cup U)
is a simplicial complex of rank at least
n-(t+|U|)\geq n-t-k
, and that
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)
consists of all sets of size
k-t
from that complex. Thus, by Lemma
4
,
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)
is
\frac{n-k-t}{k-t}
-spread. From here and the assumption on the size of
\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)
, we get that
\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)
is
\frac{n-k-t}{2(k-t)}
-spread. We use Theorem
2
with
m=\log_{2}(2k),\delta=\frac{1}{2\log_{2}(2k)},
and produce a random
2
-coloring of
[N]\setminus(T\cup U)
. As in the proof of Theorem
3
, we conclude that with probability
>1/2
the first color contains a set from
\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)
and with probability
>1/2
the second color contains a set from
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(U,T\cup U)
. Together, this contradicts the cross-intersecting property. (In order for these two properties to hold, we need the spreadness of both families to be large, concretely, we need
\frac{n}{2k}-t>2^{10}\delta^{-1}=2^{11}\log_{2}(2k)
and
\frac{n-k-t}{2(k-t)}>2^{11}\log_{2}(2k)
. Both inequalities are valid in our assumptions on
n
.)

That is,
|\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)|<\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)|,
and, combining with (
4
) we get that

(6)

\frac{|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)\setminus\mathcal{F}(T,T\cup U)|}{|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T,T\cup U)|}{2|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|}\geq 2^{2^{-19}\frac{n}{k\log_{2}\frac{n}{k}}-2}>1.

Denote
m:=|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|
. Then by (
4
)
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|+m-m2^{2^{-19}\frac{n}{k\log_{2}\frac{n}{k}}-2}\leq|\mathcal{C}^{(k)}(T)|-m2^{2^{-20}\frac{n}{k\log_{2}\frac{n}{k}}}
. The last inequality is valid due to our choice of
n
. This concludes the proof of the main part and the stability part of the theorem.
∎

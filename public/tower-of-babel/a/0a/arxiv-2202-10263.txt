Strong Converse for Privacy Amplification against Quantum Side Information
Yu-Chen Shen 1 , Li Gao 5 , and Hao-Chung Cheng 1,2,3,4

Abstract
. We establish a one-shot strong converse bound for privacy amplification against quantum side information using trace distance as a security criterion. This strong converse bound implies that in the independent and identical scenario, the trace distance exponentially converges to one in every finite blocklength when the rate of the extracted randomness exceeds the quantum conditional entropy. The established one-shot bound has an application to bounding the information leakage of classical-quantum wiretap channel coding and private communication over quantum channels. That is, the trace distance between Alice and Eavesdropper’s joint state and its decoupled state vanishes as the rate of randomness used in hashing exceeds the quantum mutual information. On the other hand, the trace distance converges to one when the rate is below the quantum mutual information, resulting in an exponential strong converse. Our result also leads to an exponential strong converse for entropy accumulation, which complements a recent result by Dupuis [arXiv:2105.05342]. Lastly, our result and its applications apply to the moderate deviation regime. Namely, we characterize the asymptotic behaviors of the trace distances when the associated rates approach the fundamental thresholds with speeds slower than O ⁡ ( 1 / n ) O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}}) .

Throughout the paper, we consider privacy amplification against quantum adversaries.
Suppose Alice and Eve (adversary) share a classical-quantum (c-q) state

The conventional protocol of privacy amplification is to apply a random hash function to Alice’s system.

Definition

(Strongly

A random hash functions

\displaystyle\Pr_{h}\left\{h(x)=z\;\wedge\;h(x^{\prime})=z^{\prime}\right\}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Z}|^{2}}\,.

Namely, the output

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{h}(\rho_{XE})

3.
Strong Converse for Privacy Amplification

In
[
5
, Theorem 8]
, the author prove an one-shot achievability bound of privacy amplification: letting
\rho_{XE}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\rho^{x}_{E}
be a classical-quantum state.
For any strongly
2
-universal hash functions
h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
,
the following holds for all
\alpha\in(1,2]
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\left(\mathcal{R}^{h}-\mathcal{U}\right)\rho_{XE}\right\|_{1}\leq\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(\log|\mathcal{Z}|-H^{*}_{\alpha}(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho})}.

When
\log|\mathcal{Z}|<H^{*}_{\alpha}(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}
, the exponent is positive, meaning that the trace distance exponentially decays.

Our main result in this section is to establish an one-shot strong converse, showing that the trace distance, however, converges to
1
exponentially fast whenever
\log|\mathcal{Z}|>H^{*}_{\alpha}(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho})
. This, it complements Ref.
[
5
]
in the strong converse regime.

Theorem 1

(One-shot strong converse)
.

Let
\rho_{XE}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\rho^{x}_{E}
be a classical-quantum state. For any strongly
2
-universal hash function
h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
, the following holds for all
\alpha\in(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1)
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\left(\mathcal{R}^{h}-\mathcal{U}\right)\rho_{XE}\right\|_{1}\geq 1-4\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(\log|\mathcal{Z}|-H_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}^{\downarrow}(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}\right)}.

Here,
H_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}^{\downarrow}
is defined in (
2.5
).

Moreover, the exponent
\sup_{\alpha\in(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1)}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\big(\log|\mathcal{Z}|-H_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}^{\downarrow}(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}\big)
is positive if and only if
\log|\mathcal{Z}|>H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}
.

Using the additivity of the Petz–Rényi divergence,
D_{\alpha}
, our one-shot result easily applies to the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) case where Alice and Eve holds state
\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}
with
|\mathcal{Z}^{n}|=\e^{nR}
.
Moreover, our result holds for every finite blocklength

Corollary 2

(Finite-blocklength exponential strong converse)
.

Let
\rho_{XE}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\rho^{x}_{E}
be a classical-quantum state. Then for every
n\in\mathds{N}
, the rate
R=\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{Z}|
and a strongly
2
-universal hash

be a strongly
2
-universal hash function,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\left(\mathcal{R}^{h^{n}}-\mathcal{U}^{n}\right)\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}\right\|_{1}\geq 1-4\,\mathrm{e}^{-n\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(R-H_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}^{\downarrow}(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}\right)}\ ,\ \ \ \ \alpha\in(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1),

where
\mathcal{U}^{n}
is the perfectly randomizing channel from
\mathcal{X}^{n}
to
\mathcal{Z}^{n}
.
The above trace distance converges to

4.
Application: classical-quantum wiretap channel coding

We now apply our strong converse Theorem
1
as well as Dupuis’ achievability result
[
5
]
to estimate the information leak to the eavesdropper in communication via a classical-quantum (c-q) wiretap channel.
Recall that a c-q wiretap channel
\mathcal{N}_{X\to BE}(\cdot)
from a classical system
\mathcal{X}
to the joint quantum system
BE
is defined as follows,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{X\to BE}(\cdot)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\langle x|\cdot|x\rangle\sigma_{BE}^{x}\,.

If Alice sends a classical symbol
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the channel output states received by Bob and Eve are respectively the marginal states
\sigma_{B}^{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
and
\sigma_{E}^{x}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}_{E})
. The goal of Alice is to transmit classical messages from a message set
[M]
to Bob over a c-q wiretap channel, without leaking too much information to the environment, or simply Eve.

In the protocol, we need two additional ingredients: first, a random codebook subject to
p_{X}
on
\mathcal{X}
, where
p_{X}
can be arbitrarily chosen
a priori
; second, a strongly
2
-universal hash function
h:[ML]\to[M]
accessible by both Alice and Bob such that the probability of it being
balanced
2
2

2

A hash function
h:[ML]\to[M]
is balanced if for any
m\in[M]
,
\left|\{k\in[ML]:h(k)=m\}\right|=L
.
is at least
(1-1/ML)
, which we called an
almost balanced strongly
2
-universal hash function
. The existence of latter can be obtained via a standard construction of hash function. The following presents one of such constructions.

Construction 1
.

To map from
[ML]\equiv\{0,1\}^{u}
to
[M]\equiv\{0,1\}^{v}
, we identify
[ML]
with the Galois field
GF(2^{u})
in the natural way.
Pick two (uniformly) random numbers
a,b\in GF(2^{u})
. For any
x\in[ML]
, define

(4.2)

\displaystyle h(x):=[ax+b]_{v}

where the calculation
ax+b
is done over the field
GF(2^{u})
and
[y]_{v}
denotes the first
v
bits of
y\in GF(2^{u})\cong\{0,1\}^{u}
. It is clear that when
a
is nonzero (hence invertible in a field),

|\{[ax+b]_{v}=z\}|=|\{[y]_{v}=z\}|=2^{u-v}=L\hskip 2.84544pt.

Hence construction
1
is strongly 2-universal and it has the probability of
(1-\frac{1}{ML})
being balanced, and the probability of
\frac{1}{ML}
being a uniform distribution independent of each
x
. In our protocol, the following steps are observed when the hash function is balanced.

1.

Alice uniformly chooses a message
m\in[M]
to send, i.e. the state at Alice is

(4.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{M}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in[M]}|m\rangle\langle m|\ .

2.

Alice picks a random hash function
h
in the almost balanced strongly
2
-universal family, and applies its reverse function
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{-1}
on her message
m\in[M]
as follows: for every
m\in[M]
,
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{-1}(m)=k\in{[ML]}
is uniform for
\{k:h(k)=m\}
. Alice’s state at this step is

(4.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{ML}=\frac{1}{ML}\sum_{k\in[ML]}\ket{k}\bra{k}.

3.

For each message
k\in[ML]
, Alice generates the codeword
\mathcal{C}(k)=x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
under distribution
p_{X}
and announces it publicly.
Namely, Alice’s encoder is described by
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{C}\circ\mathcal{R}_{h}^{-1}
.
Given the codebook
\mathcal{C}
, Alice’s state is now

(4.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{MLX}^{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{1}{ML}\sum_{k\in[ML]}\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes|x_{k}\rangle\langle x_{k}|.

4.

Alice transmit her codeword
x_{k}
through the c-q wiretap channel
\mathcal{N}
.
The joint state between the channel input and output is then

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{ABE}\equiv\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{MLXBE}:=\mathcal{N}^{X\to BE}\left(\rho_{MLX}^{\mathcal{C}}\right)=\sum_{k\in[ML]}\frac{1}{ML}\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes\ket{x_{k}}\bra{x_{k}}\otimes\sigma_{BE}^{x_{k}}.

5.

Upon receiving the channel output state, Bob performs a positive operator-valued measure (POVM)
\Pi:=\left\{\Pi^{x_{k}}_{B}\right\}_{k\in[ML]}
to obtain outcome
\hat{x}_{k}
. He then applies decoding
\mathcal{E}^{-1}
on
\hat{x}_{k}
to obtain the estimated message
\hat{m}\in[M]
.

When the hash function is balanced, the average error probability and distinquishability of Eve’s state via trace distance under random hash function
h
and random codebook
\mathcal{C}
is

(4.7)

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E},\Pi\right)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in[M]}\Pr(\hat{m}\neq m\mid\mathcal{E},\Pi)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in[M]}\Tr\left[\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k\in[L]}\sigma_{B}^{x_{k}}\left(\mathds{1}-\sum_{k^{\prime}\in[L]}\Pi^{x_{k^{\prime}}}_{B}\right)\right];

(4.9)

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sigma_{ME}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{M}\otimes\sigma_{E}^{\mathcal{C}}\right\|_{1}.

For achievability part, if the hash function is unbalanced, we can assume Alice just publicly announce the message. By this assumption, we have the following result.

(Secrecy exponent for wiretap channel coding)
.

Consider a classical-quantum wiretap channel
\mathcal{N}_{X\to BE}

For any integers L, M, and any prior distribution
p_{X}
on
\mathcal{X}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C},h}\left[\epsilon\left(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E},\Pi\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\e^{\sup_{\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(I^{\downarrow}_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}(X;B)_{\sigma}-\log ML\right)},

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C},h}\left[d_{1}(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E})\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\e^{\sup_{1<\alpha\leq 2}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(I^{*}_{\alpha}(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}-\log L)},

where,

\sigma_{XBE}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)|x\rangle\langle x|\otimes\sigma_{BE}^{x}
for each

being the channel output of
\mathcal{N}_{X\to BE}

and

are defined in (
2.6

The secrecy exponent

A.2

6.
Moderate Deviation Analysis

We shall now applies our
one-shot strong converse to the
moderate deviation regime

[
21
,
22
]
.
We call
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
a positive moderate deviation sequence if it satisfies

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0\ ,\ \lim_{n\to\infty}na_{n}^{2}=\infty.

In other words, the moderate deviation sequence vanishes (i.e. of order
o(1)
) but slower than
O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}})
(i.e. of order
\omega(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}})
).

For each
n\in\mathds{N}
, let us denote
R_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{Z}^{n}|
as the rate of the extracted randomness.
We have the following moderate deviation characterization for privacy amplification against quantum side information when the rate
R_{n}
approaches the first-order limit
H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}
at speed of
a_{n}
.

Proposition 9

(Moderate deviations for privacy amplification)
.

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a classical-quantum state and assume that
V(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}>0
. Suppose
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is a moderate deviation sequence. Then for any strongly
2
-universal hash function
h^{n}:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}^{n}
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{na_{n}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\left(\mathcal{R}^{h^{n}}-\mathcal{U}^{n}\right)\left(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}\right)\right\|_{1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2V(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}},&\text{ if }|\mathcal{Z}^{n}|=\e^{n(H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}-a_{n})}\\
\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{na_{n}^{2}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\left(\mathcal{R}^{h^{n}}-\mathcal{U}^{n}\right)\left(\rho_{XE}^{\otimes n}\right)\right\|_{1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2V(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}},&\text{ if }|\mathcal{Z}^{n}|=\e^{n(H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}+a_{n})}\end{cases}.

where
\mathcal{U}^{n}
is the perfectly randomizing channel from
\mathcal{X}^{n}
to
\mathcal{Z}^{n}
.

The above theorem means that the trace distance vanishes asymptotically when the rate
R_{n}
approaches
H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}
from below at speed no faster than
O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}})
.
On the other hand, in the strong converse regime where
R_{n}>H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}
, the trace distance still converges to
1
asymptotically when the rate of maximal extractable randomness approaches
H(X{\,|\,}E)_{\rho}
from above at speed no faster than
O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}})
.

Before proving Theorem

Lemma 10

(

(

(6.35)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{na_{n}^{2}}\log\left(1-\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{n},h^{n}}d_{1}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\mid\mathcal{E}^{n})\right)\geq-\frac{\log 5}{na_{n}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2V(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{a_{n}}{V(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}}}\left(1-\Upsilon\frac{a_{n}}{V(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}^{2}}\right).

Taking
n\to\infty
and using the definition of
a_{n}
, we obtain

(6.36)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{na_{n}^{2}}\log\left(1-\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{n},h^{n}}d_{1}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\mid\mathcal{E}^{n})\right)\geq\frac{1}{2V(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}}\ ,

which proves our second claim.
∎

Finally, the moderate deviation can also be applied in entropy accumulation. We omit the proof since it is similar to previous two moderate deviation analysis.

Proposition 13

(Moderate deviations for entropy accumulation)
.

Consider any strongly 2-universal hash function:
h^{n}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}^{n}
that produce
\log|\mathcal{Z}^{n}|=nR
at the output. In the protocol of entropy accumulation, the following holds:

(6.37)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{na_{n}^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\mathcal{R}^{h}(\rho_{A^{n}_{1}X^{n}_{1}E|\wt(T^{n}_{1})=w})-\frac{\mathds{1}}{2^{nR}}\otimes\rho_{X^{n}_{1}E|\wt(T^{n}_{1})=w}\right\|_{1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2V^{2}},&\!\!\!\!\text{ if }R=f(w)-a_{n}\\
\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{na_{n}^{2}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h}\left\|\mathcal{R}^{h}(\rho_{A^{n}_{1}X^{n}_{1}E|\wt(T^{n}_{1})=w})-\frac{\mathds{1}}{2^{nR}}\otimes\rho_{X^{n}_{1}E|\wt(T^{n}_{1})=w}\right\|_{1}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2V^{2}},&\!\!\!\!\text{ if }R=f(w)+a_{n}\end{cases}.

where
f(\cdot)
for the first equation is the min-tradeoff function defined in
[
20
, Definition 4.1]
, and
f(\cdot)
for the second equation is the max-tradeoff function defined in
[
20
, Definition 4.1]
.

The above result shows that by Dupuis’ result
[
5
, Theorem 9]
the information we get at the output becomes almost uncertain when the number of bits at the output approaches
f(w)
from below at speed no faster than
O(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{n}}})
, and it becomes almost certain when the number of bits approaches
f(w)
from above.

7.
Conclusions

We establish a one-shot strong converse bound for privacy amplification against quantum side information, which enjoys various advantages as the recent achievability bound by Dupuis
[
5
, Theorem 8]
. Moreover, our result extends to the large deviation regime
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
32
]
—an exponential convergence to
1
for every blocklength, and the moderate deviation regime
[
21
,
22
]
—an asymptotic behavior of trace distance when the rate of the extracted randomness approaches the quantum conditional entropy.
In a way, our result in the strong converse regime may be viewed as complementing Dupuis’ result
[
5
, Theorem 8]
in the error exponent regime.
As an application, we provide both secrecy exponent bound and exponential strong converse bound for the information leakage through a classical-quantum wiretap channel as well as for a quantum channel. Our result also applies to estimate the information loss in entropy accumulation protocol
[
20
,
5
]
, and those two applications can be also extended to the large deviation regime and moderate deviation regime characterizations.

We remark that several entropic quantities such as
H_{\alpha}^{*}
and
I_{\alpha}^{*}
do not have closed-form expressions for
\alpha\neq 1
in general. There is a recent optimization algorithm with asymptotic convergence guarantee that can be applied to compute them
[
38
]
.
It is intriguing to note that some entropic exponent functions obtained in this paper such as
H_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}
and
I_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}
have the same form as classical-quantum channel coding
[
33
,
36
]
and classical data compression with quantum side information
[
32
]
.

A.1.
Proof of a trace inequality

Proof of Lemma
3
.

We first claim the following, for all positive semi-definite operators
K,L
,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\Tr\left[K(K+L)^{-\frac{1}{2}}L(K+L)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]\leq\Tr\left[\frac{K+L-|K-L|}{2}\right].

Then, combining it with Audenaert
et al.
’s inequality
[
39
, Theorem 2]
: for all
K,L\geq 0
,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\frac{K+L-|K-L|}{2}\right]\leq\Tr\left[K^{1-s}L^{s}\right],\quad\forall s\in(0,1),

we prove Lemma
3
. To prove (
A.1
), we adapt Barnum and Knill’s proof technique in
[
40
]
and
[
41
, Theorem 3.10]
. Let
\rho_{0}=K\geq 0
,
\rho_{1}=L\geq 0
,
M:=\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}
, and let
\Pi_{0}=\{\rho_{0}\geq\rho_{1}\},\Pi_{1}=\{\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}\}=1-\Pi_{0}
be the corresponding optimal measurement
[
42
,
43
,
44
]
. Then

(A.3)

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\frac{M+|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|}{2}\right]=\Tr[\rho_{0}\Pi_{0}]+\Tr[\rho_{1}\Pi_{1}]

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for trace, we have for each
i=1,2
,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\Tr[\rho_{i}\Pi_{i}]

\displaystyle\leq\left\|M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\rho_{i}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{2}\left\|M^{\frac{1}{4}}\Pi_{i}M^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{2}.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for scalars, we have

(A.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Tr[\rho_{i}\Pi_{i}]\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\left\|M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\rho_{i}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\left\|M^{\frac{1}{4}}\Pi_{i}M^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{2}^{2}}.

The second factor on the right-hand side is bounded as follows:

(A.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\left\|M^{\frac{1}{4}}\Pi_{i}M^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Tr\left[\Pi_{i}\sqrt{M}\Pi_{i}\sqrt{M}\right]\leq\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Tr\left[\sqrt{M}\Pi_{i}\sqrt{M}\right]=\Tr[M].

On the other hand, the first factor is

(A.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\left\|M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\rho_{i}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Tr\left[\rho_{i}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{i}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]=\Tr[M]-2\Tr\left[\rho_{0}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{1}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right].

Combining (
A.3
), (
A.5
), (
A.6
), and (
A.7
) together, we obtain

(A.8)

\displaystyle 2\frac{\Tr\left[\rho_{0}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{1}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]}{\Tr[M]}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\left(1-\frac{\Tr\left[\frac{M-|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|}{2}\right]}{\Tr[M]}\right)^{2}

(A.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Tr\left[\frac{M-|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|}{2}\right]}{\Tr[M]}\left(2-\frac{\Tr\left[\frac{M-|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|}{2}\right]}{\Tr[M]}\right)

(A.10)

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\Tr\left[\frac{M-|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|}{2}\right]}{\Tr[M]},

where in the last inequality we have used

(A.11)

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\frac{M-|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|}{2}\right]=\Tr\left[\rho_{0}-\left(\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}\right)_{+}\right]\geq 0.

Then, (
A.10
) is exactly our claim (
A.1
), and hence we complete the proof.
∎

A.2.
Proof of secrecy exponent for wiretap channel coding

Proof of Theorem
4
.

The expected value (over the random codebook) of the average error probability of the protocol was stated in
[
45
, Equation (63)]
:

(A.12)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\epsilon\left(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E},\Pi\right)\right]\leq\min_{\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1}4(ML)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I^{\downarrow}_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}(X;B)_{\sigma}},

We remain to prove the upper bound on
d_{1}
.
It is sufficient to consider the event that the output of hash function is balanced. In this situation,
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{-1}
can be expressed as:

(A.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:\ket{m}\bra{m}\mapsto\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k:\mathcal{R}^{h}(k)=m}\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes\ket{x_{k}}\bra{x_{k}}\,;

(A.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-1}:\sigma\mapsto\sum_{k\in[ML]}(\bra{k}\otimes\bra{x_{k}})\sigma(\ket{k}\otimes\ket{x_{k}})\otimes\ket{h(x)}\bra{h(x)}.

The
d_{1}
norm of the channel when the hash function is balanced can be expressed as

(A.15)

\displaystyle d_{1}(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in[M]}\left\|\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k:\mathcal{R}^{h}(k)=m}\sigma_{E}^{x_{k}}-\frac{1}{ML}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in[ML]}\sigma_{E}^{x_{k^{\prime}}}\right\|_{1}

(A.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in[M]}\ket{m}\bra{m}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k\in h^{-1}(m)}\sigma_{E}^{x_{k}}\right)-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m\in[M]}\ket{m}\bra{m}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{ML}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in[ML]}\sigma_{E}^{x_{k^{\prime}}}\right)\right\|_{1}

(A.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(\mathcal{E}^{-1}-\mathcal{U}^{\mathcal{C}}\right)\left(\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{MLXE}\right)\right\|_{1}

(A.18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(\mathcal{E}^{-1}-\mathcal{U}^{\mathcal{C}}\right)\left(\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}\right)\right\|_{1}\,,

where
\mathcal{U}^{\mathcal{C}}
is the perfectly randomizing channel from
\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes\ket{x_{k}}\bra{x_{k}}
to
\ket{m}\bra{m}
for
k\in[ML]
and
m\in[M]
, and
A\equiv MLX
is a classical system. On the other hand, the probability of
h
being not balanced is at most
\frac{1}{ML}
, and the
d_{1}
norm in that case is at most 1.

Recall that any strongly
2
-universal family of hash functions is
1
-randomizing (e.g.
[
5
, Lemma 6]
). Hence, given a codebook
\mathcal{C}:[ML]\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, we now apply
[
5
, Theorem 8]
or namely (
3.1
) to obtain an upper bound to the expected value of Eve’s distinguishability with respect to the family of hash functions,

(A.19)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{h\mid\mathcal{C}}\left[d_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{X\to E}\mid\mathcal{E})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{h\mid\mathcal{C}}\left\|(\mathcal{E}^{-1}-\mathcal{U}^{\mathcal{C}})(\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE})\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{ML}

(A.20)

\displaystyle\leq\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(\log M-H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}}\right)}+\frac{1}{ML},\quad\forall\alpha\in[1,2].

Since in balanced condition, Alice sends every
k\in[ML]
to the codebook
\mathcal{C}
with equal probability, we calculate that

(A.21)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{*}(A;E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{E}\in\mathcal{S}(E)}D_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}\|\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\otimes\tau_{E}\right)

(A.22)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{E}\in\mathcal{S}(E)}D_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k}\frac{1}{ML}\sigma_{E}^{x_{k}}\left\|\frac{\mathds{1}_{A}}{ML}\otimes\tau_{E}\right.\right)

(A.23)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{E}\in\mathcal{S}(E)}D_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\left.\bigoplus\limits_{k}\frac{1}{ML}\sigma_{E}^{x_{k}}\right\|\mathds{1}_{A}\otimes\tau_{E}\right)+\log(ML)

(A.24)

\displaystyle=-H_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}+\log(ML).

Hence, expectation of Eve’s distinguishability using random codebook
\mathcal{C}
can further be written as

(A.25)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{h\mid\mathcal{C}}\left[d_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{X\to E}|\mathcal{E})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(\log M-H_{\alpha}(A{\,|\,}E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}\right)}+\frac{1}{ML}

(A.26)

\displaystyle=\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(\log M+I_{\alpha}^{*}(A;E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}-\log ML\right)}+\frac{1}{ML}

(A.27)

\displaystyle=\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(I_{\alpha}^{*}(A;E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}-\log L\right)}+\frac{1}{ML}

(A.28)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(I_{\alpha}^{*}(A;E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}-\log L\right)}.

The last inequality comes from the fact that the term
\nicefrac{{1}}{{ML}}
decays faster than the first term, i.e.

for every
\alpha\in(1,2]
and noting that

Next, invoking the concavity of the map

\mathcal{C}
(under distribution

(A.29)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C},h}\left[d_{1}(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathds{E}_{h\mid\mathcal{C}}\left[d_{1}(\mathcal{N}|\mathcal{E})\right]

(A.30)

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(I_{\alpha}^{*}(A;E)_{\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}}-\log L\right)}\right]

(A.31)

\displaystyle\leq 2\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(I_{\alpha}^{*}(A;E)_{\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}\right]}-\log L\right)}.

Moreover, we have

(A.32)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in[ML]}\frac{1}{ML}\ket{k}\bra{k}\otimes\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\sigma_{E}^{x}\right).

By simple calculation, one has

(A.33)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{*}(A{\,:\,}E)_{\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\sigma^{\mathcal{C}}_{AE}\right]}=I_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma},

Therefore,

(A.34)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C},h}\left[d_{1}(\mathcal{N}\mid\mathcal{E})\right]\leq 2\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}-\log L)},

which is our statement.

Finally, the positivity of the secrecy exponent follows from the monotone increasing of the map
\alpha\mapsto I^{*}_{\alpha}(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}
and (
2.7
).

\displaystyle\sigma_{X}\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}E)_{\sigma}}

A.4.
Proof of a concavity property

Proof of Lemma

As stated in the proof of Proposition 4-(b) in

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}^{*}(\sigma_{XE}\|\sigma_{X}\otimes\tau_{E})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\sum\limits_{x}p_{X}(x)\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha-1)D^{*}_{\alpha}(\rho_{E}^{x}\|\tau_{E})}.

(A.53)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}D_{\alpha}^{*}(\sigma_{XE}\|\sigma_{X}\otimes\tau_{E})}=\left(\sum\limits_{x}p_{X}(x)\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha-1)D^{*}_{\alpha}(\rho_{E}^{x}\|\tau_{E})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

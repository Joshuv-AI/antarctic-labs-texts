Abstract
. Non-Hermitian Wishart matrices were introduced in the context of quantum chromodynamics with a baryon chemical potential. These provide chiral extensions of the elliptic Ginibre ensembles as well as non-Hermitian extensions of the classical Wishart/Laguerre ensembles. In this work, we investigate eigenvalues of non-Hermitian Wishart matrices in the symmetry classes of complex and symplectic Ginibre ensembles. We introduce a generalised Christoffel-Darboux formula in the form of a certain second-order differential equation, offering a unified and robust method for analyzing correlation functions across all scaling regimes in the model. By employing this method, we derive universal bulk and edge scaling limits for eigenvalue correlations at both strong and weak non-Hermiticity.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Non-Hermitian Wishart matrices

For given non-negative integers
N
and
\nu
, we denote by
P
and
Q
the
N\times(N+\nu)
random matrices with independent complex or quaternionic Gaussian entries of mean
0
and variance
1/(2N)
.
These are known as the complex or symplectic rectangular Ginibre ensembles, respectively, see
[
35
,
36
]
for recent reviews.
For a given non-Hermiticity parameter
\tau\in[0,1]
, the non-Hermitian Wishart matrix is defined by

(1.1)

X:=X_{1}X_{2}^{*},\qquad\begin{cases}X_{1}=\sqrt{1+\tau}\,\,\textup{P}+\sqrt{1-\tau}\,\,\textup{Q},\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
X_{2}=\sqrt{1+\tau}\,\,\textup{P}-\sqrt{1-\tau}\,\,\textup{Q}.\end{cases}

For the external case
\tau=0
, the maximally non-Hermitian regime, the model gives a product of two rectangular Ginibre matrices, while in the opposite extremal case
\tau=1
, the Hermitian regime, the model coincides with the Laguerre unitary or symplectic ensemble (LUE or LSE)
[
55
]
, respectively.
We mention that the model (
1.1
) is also referred to as the chiral Ginibre ensemble
[
84
,
79
]
, where these models find applications in analyzing the Dirac operator spectrum of quantum chromodynamics with a chemical potential. It is also called the sample cross-covariance matrices
[
27
]
and finds applications for the analysis of time series
[
71
]
.

We study eigenvalues of non-Hermitian complex/symplectic Wishart ensembles.
As a common feature of integrable random matrices, the model (
1.1
) also enjoys a statistical physics realisation in a sense that the ensemble can be interpreted as the Coulomb gas at
\beta=2
with Dirichlet (complex) or Neumann (symplectic) boundary conditions along the real axis
[
79
,
2
,
55
]
.
To be more concrete, let us first setup some notations.
For given parameters
A>B\geq 0
, we consider the weight functions

(1.2)

\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(z):=|z|^{\nu}K_{\nu}(AN|z|)e^{BN\operatorname{Re}z},\qquad\omega^{\rm s}_{N}(z):=|z|^{2\nu}K_{2\nu}(2AN|z|)e^{2BN\operatorname{Re}z},

where
K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind

(1.3)

K_{\nu}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\nu}(z)-I_{\nu}(z)}{\sin(\nu\pi)},\qquad I_{\nu}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z/2)^{2k+\nu}}{k!\,\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}.

The parameters
A
and
B
are related with the non-Hermiticity parameter
\tau\in[0,1)
as

(1.4)

A=\frac{2}{1-\tau^{2}},\qquad B=\frac{2\tau}{1-\tau^{2}},

Then the eigenvalues
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
of the non-Hermitian Wishart matrix (
1.1
) follow the joint probability distribution

(1.5)

\displaystyle d\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\rm c}(\bm{z})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{\rm c}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(z_{j})\,dA(z_{j}),

(1.6)

\displaystyle d\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\rm s}(\bm{z})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{\rm s}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}|z_{j}-\overline{z}_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}-\overline{z}_{j}|^{2}\omega^{\rm s}_{N}(z_{j})\,dA(z_{j}),

respectively, where
dA(z)=d^{2}z/\pi
is the area measure, and
Z_{N}^{\rm c}
and
Z_{N}^{\rm s}
are the partition functions that make (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) probability measures.
Contrary to the matrix model realisation, for the ensembles (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), one can consider general real-valued
\nu
, as long as
\nu>-1
for the complex case and
\nu>-1/2
for the symplectic case. Note that a characteristic difference between (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) is the repulsion along the real axis that comes from the factor proportional to
|z_{j}-\overline{z}_{j}|^{2}
, see Figure
1
.

Due to the above Boltzmann-Gibbs measure realisations together with the standard equilibrium convergence (see e.g.
[
54
,
16
,
45
]
), the macroscopic behaviours of
\bm{z}
can be described using the logarithmic potential theory
[
82
]
.
The associated planar equilibrium measure problem was solved in
[
4
]
.
Consequently, it follows that for any fixed
\nu
, the empirical measure
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{z_{j}}
weakly converges to

(1.7)

d\mu(z)=\frac{1}{2(1-\tau^{2})}\frac{1}{|z|}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{S}(z)\,dA(z).

Here, the limiting spectrum
S
is closed by the ellipse

(1.8)

S:=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\Big(\frac{x-2\tau}{1+\tau^{2}}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{y}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big)^{2}\leq 1\Big\},

see Figure
1
.
This limiting distribution (
1.7
) provides a non-Hermitian generalisation of the classical Marchenko-Pastur law.
We mention that the results in
[
4
]
indeed cover the case
\nu=O(N)
as well.
We also refer to
[
69
]
for the universality of the limiting law (
1.7
) for the square (
\nu=0
) and complex case.

Beyond the averaged eigenvalue density, more detailed statistical behaviours of the eigenvalues are encoded in the
k
-point correlation functions

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{\rm c}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})

\displaystyle:=\frac{N!}{(N-k)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-k}}\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\rm c}(\bm{z})\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}\,dA(z_{j}),

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{\rm s}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})

\displaystyle:=\frac{N!}{(N-k)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-k}}\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\rm s}(\bm{z})\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}\,dA(z_{j}).

It is well known that the ensemble (
1.5
) forms a determinantal point process whose correlation kernel can be constructed using planar orthogonal polynomials.
Similarly, the ensemble (
1.6
) forms a Pfaffian point process, and in this case the associated correlation kernel can be constructed using planar skew-orthogonal polynomials.

It is a surprising fact, especially given the seemingly complicated forms of the weight functions (
1.2
), that one can express the associated (skew)-orthogonal polynomials in terms of the generalised Laguerre polynomial

(1.11)

L_{j}^{(\nu)}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}{(j-k)!\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}\frac{(-z)^{k}}{k!}.

Note that this shows a common feature with the extremal case when
\tau=1
, the LUE and LSE
[
1
]
.
As a consequence, by combining the results in
[
79
,
2
,
8
]
, the correlation kernels can be expressed in terms of the Laguerre polynomials, see Subsection
2.1
for more details.

In this wok, we investigate various scaling limits of correlation functions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).
For this purpose, for a given zooming point
p\in S\setminus\{0\}
, let

(1.12)

\delta\equiv\delta(p)=\frac{1}{2(1-\tau^{2})|p|}

be the mean eigenvalue density, cf. (
1.7
). Then we define the rescaled
k
-point correlation functions

(1.13)

\displaystyle R_{N,k}^{\rm c}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(N\delta)^{k}}\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{\rm c}\Big(p+\frac{\mathbf{n}(p)\,z_{1}}{\sqrt{N\delta}},\cdots,p+\frac{\mathbf{n}(p)\,z_{k}}{\sqrt{N\delta}}\Big),

(1.14)

\displaystyle R_{N,k}^{\rm s}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(N\delta)^{k}}\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{\rm s}\Big(p+\frac{\mathbf{n}(p)\,z_{1}}{\sqrt{N\delta}},\cdots,p+\frac{\mathbf{n}(p)\,z_{k}}{\sqrt{N\delta}}\Big),

where the
\mathbf{n}(p)
is the outward unit normal vector from
\partial S
at
p
, and otherwise,
\mathbf{n}(p)=1
for
p\in\mathrm{int}(S)
.

In the asymptotic analysis of the non-Hermitian Wishart models, one needs to distinguish the following two different regimes.

(i)

Strong non-Hermiticity.
This is the case that
\tau\in[0,1)
is fixed. In this case, the limiting spectrum is given by (
1.8
), which is a genuinely two-dimensional subset of
\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

Weak non-Hermiticity.
This is the case that
\tau\uparrow 1
as
N\to\infty
with a proper speed depending on the position of the zooming point. This critical regime gives rise to an interpolation between the one- and two-dimensional point processes.

Furthermore, from the viewpoint of the universality classes, it is also necessary to distinguish cases based on the zooming points where we look at the local statistics.

(a)

Singular origin.
This is the case
p=0
where the density at the origin diverges (
1.7
). Due to this fact, it gives rise to a non-standard universality classes in two-dimensional point process.

(b)

Regular bulk.
This is the case where
p
is the interior of the droplet. For the complex ensemble, one would expect the universality classes
[
66
,
20
,
62
,
6
]
to arise, and for the symplectic ensemble, one would expect the universality classes in
[
70
,
34
,
10
]
.

(c)

Regular edge.
This is the case where
p
is on the boundary of the droplet.
In this regime, one would expect the universality classes in
[
57
,
68
,
48
,
26
,
18
]
for the complex ensemble, and those in
[

,

,

]
for the symplectic ensemble.

Other than the aforementioned cases, the scaling limits of non-Hermitian Wishart ensembles remain undiscovered, despite their intrinsic interest, particularly from the viewpoint of the universality principle

In this work, we introduce a unified framework for analyzing the correlation functions (Theorem

As a consequence, we obtain the scaling limits of the complex and symplectic non-Hermitian Wishart ensembles for all unknown regimes, i.e. both strong and weak non-Hermiticity, as well as both bulk and edge scaling regimes (Theorems

1.2.
Main results

In this section, we introduce our main results.

Due to the lack of the classical Christoffel-Darboux formula for non-Hermitian random matrix ensembles, one should find a proper way of analyzing the correlation kernel.
Our first result provides a differential equation satisfied by the correlation kernels of the Wishart ensembles.
As will become clear, this turns out to be very helpful for proceeding with further asymptotic analysis.

Theorem 1.1

(
Differential equations for correlation kernels of planar Wishart ensembles
)
.

For any
\tau\in[0,1]
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following.

(i)

(Complex ensemble)

Let

(1.16)

\mathcal{K}_{N}(z,w)\equiv\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(\nu)}(z,w):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

Then we have

(1.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\bigg[(1-\tau^{2})\,z\,\partial_{z}^{2}+\Big((1-\tau^{2})(\nu+1)+2\tau z\Big)\partial_{z}+\frac{\tau^{2}z-w}{1-\tau^{2}}+(\nu+1)\tau\bigg]\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(\nu)}(z,w)\\
&=\frac{N!}{\Gamma(N+\nu)}\frac{\tau^{2N-1}}{1-\tau^{2}}\bigg[L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)-\tau^{2}L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg].\end{split}

(ii)

(Symplectic ensemble)

Let

(1.18)

\kappa_{N}(z,w)\equiv\kappa_{N}^{(\nu)}(z,w):=G_{N}(z,w)-G_{N}(w,z),

where

(1.19)

G_{N}(z,w):=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{2\nu+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(2k)!!\,\tau^{2k+1}}{2^{k}\Gamma(k+\nu+3/2)}\frac{(2j-1)!!\,\tau^{2j}}{2^{j}\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2k+1}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

Then we have

(1.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\bigg[(1-\tau^{2})z\,\partial_{z}^{2}+\Big((2\nu+1)(1-\tau^{2})+2\tau z\Big)\partial_{z}+\tau(2\nu+1)-z\bigg]\kappa_{N}^{(\nu)}(z,w)\\
&=\mathcal{K}_{2N}^{(2\nu)}(z,w)-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,N!\,\tau^{2N}}{2^{2\nu}\Gamma(N+\nu+1/2)}\,L_{2N}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(2j-1)!!\tau^{2j}}{2^{j}\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).\end{split}

Up to a proper scaling,
\mathcal{K}_{N}
in (
1.16
) is the correlation kernel of the complex Wishart ensemble, forming a determinantal point process. Similarly,
\kappa_{N}
in (
1.18
) is a pre-kernel of a
2\times 2
matrix-valued kernel of the symplectic Wishart ensemble, forming a Pfaffian point process, see Proposition
2.1
.

From the viewpoint of the special function theory, Theorem
1.1
provides certain functional identities involving the Laguerre polynomials, which are new to our best knowledge.
We stress that it is indeed far from being obvious that there exists such functional identities.

The idea of constructing a differential equation for the correlation kernel was employed by Lee and Riser in their study of the elliptic GinUE
[
74
]
, see also
[
40
]
.
Furthermore, this method proves particularly helpful in the asymptotic study of the planar symplectic ensembles.
We refer to
[
70
,
5
]
for the implementation in the study of the GinSE,
[
33
]
for its extension to the elliptic GinSE,
[
32
]
for the induced GinSE,
[
36
]
for the truncated ensemble, and
[
37
]
for the induced spherical GinSE.
In all of these works, a specific first-order differential equation for each model was constructed.

It is also worth highlighting that in the study of planar symplectic ensembles
[
5
,
33
,
32
,

]
, the inhomogeneous term in the differential equation involves the correlation kernel of their complex counterparts.
This common feature can also be observed in (
1.20
), where the term
\mathcal{K}_{2N}^{(2\nu)}
appears.
Such a relation between correlation functions of unitary and symplectic ensembles was also found in
[

,

]
for Hermitian random matrix ensembles, see also
[

]
for a recent work.
This relation was crucially used for the universality of Hermitian random matrix ensembles with symplectic symmetry

]
.

We now turn to the scaling limits of the non-Hermitian Wishart ensembles. Let us first discuss the strong non-Hermitian regime. Note that, by (
1.8
), the intersection of the droplet
S
with the real axis is given by

(1.21)

S\cap\mathbb{R}=[e_{-},e_{+}],\qquad e_{\pm}:=2\tau\pm(1+\tau^{2}).

In the asymptotic analysis of planar symplectic ensembles, the behaviour varies depending on proximity to the real axis.
Namely, the self-interaction term
|z_{j}-\overline{z}_{j}|^{2}
in (
1.6
) does not affect the local behaviour away from the real axis in the large
N
limit.
We shall focus on the real axis case for the planar symplectic ensemble.
On the other hand, for the complex ensemble, there is no speciality of the real axis and we consider the general zooming points in the complex plane.
In summary, we shall use the following terminology.

•

Bulk case:
p\in\textup{int}(S)\setminus\{0\}
for the complex ensemble,
p\in(e_{-},e_{+})\setminus\{0\}
for the symplectic ensemble.

•

Edge case:
p\in\partial S
for the complex ensemble,
p\in\{e_{-},e_{+}\}
for the symplectic ensemble.

We have the following results, which demonstrate the universal scaling limits.

Theorem 1.2

(
Bulk/edge scaling limits of planar Wishart ensembles at strong non-Hermiticity
)
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed.
Then we have the following.

(i)

(Complex ensemble)
For any
\nu>-1
, we have

(1.22)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N,k}^{\rm c}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\det\Big[K(z_{j},z_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, where

(1.23)

\displaystyle K(z,w)=K_{\rm b}(z,w):=e^{z\overline{w}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}},\qquad\textup{for the bulk case,}

(1.24)

\displaystyle K(z,w)=K_{\rm e}(z,w):=e^{z\overline{w}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}\Big(\frac{z+\overline{w}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\qquad\textup{for the edge case.}

(ii)

(Symplectic ensemble)
For any
\nu>-1/2
, we have

(1.25)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N,k}^{\rm s}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\overline{z}_{j}-z_{j})\,{\textup{Pf}}\bigg[e^{-|z_{j}|^{2}-|z_{l}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa(z_{j},z_{l})&\kappa(z_{j},\overline{z}_{l})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\kappa(\overline{z}_{j},z_{l})&\kappa(\overline{z}_{j},\overline{z}_{l})\end{pmatrix}\bigg]_{j,l=1}^{k},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, where

(1.26)

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\kappa_{\rm b}(z,w):=\sqrt{\pi}e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\operatorname{erf}(z-w),\qquad\textup{for the bulk case},

(1.27)

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\kappa_{\rm e}(z,w):=\sqrt{\pi}e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}W(f_{w},f_{z})(u)\,du,\qquad\textup{for the edge case}.

Here
W(f,g):=fg^{\prime}-gf^{\prime}
is the Wronskian and
f_{z}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{2}(z-u)).

Note that
K_{\rm b}
in (
1.23
) is the limiting bulk kernel of the GinUE
[
66
]
, whereas
K_{\rm e}
in (
1.24
) is the limiting edge kernel of the GinUE
[
57
]
.
We stress that the bulk universality of random normal matrices (i.e. (
1.5
) with a general weight function) was shown in
[
20
]
. On the other hand, the edge universality was shown in
[
68
]
.
Nonetheless, Theorem
1.2
(i) is not a direct consequence of
[
20
,
68
]
, as the weight function (
1.2
) depends highly on
N.

Regarding the symplectic ensemble, note that
\kappa_{\rm b}
in (
1.26
) coincides with the limiting bulk kernel of the GinSE
[
70
,
10
]
. Similarly,
\kappa_{\rm e}
in (
1.27
) corresponds to the limiting edge kernel of the GinSE recently found in
[
5
]
. In contrast to the random normal matrix ensemble, the universality of the planar symplectic ensemble (i.e. (
1.6
) with a general weight function) is much less developed.
To be more precise, the universal scaling limits (
1.26
) and (
1.27
) have been shown only for the GinSE
[
70
,
5
]
, elliptic GinSE
[
8
,
33
]
, spherical GinSE
[
37
]
, induced GinSE
[
32
,
36
]
, and truncated ensembles
[
72
,
36
]
.
The fundamental reason for this relatively slow progress compared to the random normal matrix model is that there is no general theory on the skew-orthogonal polynomial in the plane.
Furthermore, once one can construct the skew-orthogonal polynomials, the method for performing proper asymptotic analysis has only recently been developed.

As a side remark, let us also mention that the limiting correlation kernels of the GinUE and GinSE can be described in a unified manner.
For this purpose, we write

(1.28)

E:=\begin{cases}(-\infty,\infty)&\textup{for the bulk case,}\\
(-\infty,0)&\textup{for the edge case}.\end{cases}

Then the GinUE correlation kernels (
1.23
) and (
1.27
) can be written as

(1.29)

K(z,w)=e^{z\overline{w}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{E}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(z-\overline{w}-t)^{2}}\,dt.

Similarly, the GinSE correlation kernels (
1.26
) and (
1.27
) can be expressed in terms of the Wronskian form

(1.30)

\kappa(z,w):=\sqrt{\pi}e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\int_{E}W(f_{w},f_{z})(u)\,du.

In these unified formulas, by taking an interval of the form
E=(-\infty,a)
, one can also interpolate bulk and edge scaling limits, see e.g.
[
5
, Remark 2.3 (iii)]
.

Next, we discuss the scaling limits at weak non-Hermiticity.
Our first result gives the bulk scaling limits. For this, let us write

(1.31)

\sigma_{{\rm MP}}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{4-\xi}{\xi}}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,4]}(\xi)

for the Marchenko-Pastur law.
One may expect that the global eigenvalue density of the non-Hermitian Wishart ensemble when
\tau\uparrow 1
is close to the Marchenko-Pastur law.
We refer to
[
17
, Section 2]
for a geometric description on the asymptotic shape of the droplet in the weakly non-Hermitian (or bandlimited Coulomb gas) ensembles.
For the bulk scaling regime when
p\in(0,4)
, we have the following.

Theorem 1.3

(
Bulk scaling limits of planar Wishart ensembles at weak non-Hermiticity
)
.

Let

(1.32)

\tau=1-\frac{c^{2}}{N},\qquad c\in(0,\infty)

and define

(1.33)

E_{a}=(-2a,2a),\qquad a=a(p)=\pi c\sqrt{p}\,\sigma_{{\rm MP}}(p).

Suppose that
p\in(0,4)
. Then we have the following.

(i)

(Complex ensemble)
For any
\nu>-1
, we have

(1.34)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N,k}^{\rm c}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\det\Big[K^{c}_{\rm{b}}(z_{j},z_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, where

(1.35)

K^{c}_{\rm{b}}(z,w)=e^{z\overline{w}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{E_{a}}e^{\frac{1}{2}(z-\overline{w}-it)^{2}}\,dt.

(ii)

(Symplectic ensemble)
For any
\nu>-1/2
, we have

(1.36)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N,k}^{\rm s}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\overline{z}_{j}-z_{j})\,{\textup{Pf}}\bigg[e^{-|z_{j}|^{2}-|z_{l}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa^{c}_{\rm b}(z_{j},z_{l})&\kappa^{c}_{\rm b}(z_{j},\overline{z}_{l})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\kappa^{c}_{\rm b}(\overline{z}_{j},z_{l})&\kappa^{c}_{\rm b}(\overline{z}_{j},\overline{z}_{l})\end{pmatrix}\bigg]_{j,l=1}^{k},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, where

(1.37)

\kappa^{c}_{\rm b}(z,w):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\int_{E_{a}}e^{-u^{2}}\sin(2u(z-w))\frac{du}{u}.

The scaling limit (
1.35
) was first introduced in the serial work
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
studying the elliptic GinUE at weak non-Hermiticity.
This has been extended in
[
6
]
for the fixed trace elliptic GinUE with perturbation.
Note that under the assumption that

is an integer, Theorem
1.3
(i) was obtained in
[

, Theorem 1.7]
using the theory of Ward’s identity
[
21
,
22
]
.
Let us mention that, unlike the universality of random normal matrices at strong non-Hermiticity
[
20
,
68
]
, the universality at weak non-Hermiticity has been less developed.
Nonetheless, this universality class appears in the almost-circular ensemble
[
43
]
and near the singular boundary point
[
22
]
.
See also a recent work
[
80
]
for a non-integrable model lying in this universality class.

On the other hand, the scaling limit (
1.37
) was obtained in the work of Kanzieper
[

]
studying the weakly non-Hermitian elliptic GinSE at the origin. For the elliptic GinSE, this was extended to the whole real axis in
[

Our final results address the edge scaling limits at weak non-Hermiticity.
For this, recall that the Airy function is defined

(1.38)

\operatorname{Ai}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\Big(\frac{t^{3}}{3}+xt\Big)\,dt,

see e.g.

Note that as in the Hermitian random matrix theory, one needs to use different scaling for the edge case.

Theorem 1.4

(
Edge scaling limits of planar Wishart ensembles at weak non-Hermiticity
)
.

Let

(1.39)

\tau=1-\frac{c^{2}}{(2N)^{1/3}},\qquad c\in(0,\infty).

and
p=(1+\tau)^{2}
. Then we have the following.

(i)

(Complex ensemble)

For any
\nu>-1

(1.40)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N,k}^{\rm c}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\det\Big[K^{c}_{\rm{e}}(z_{j},z_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}

(1.41)

\begin{split}K^{c}_{\rm{e}}(z,w)&=2c^{2}\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-(\operatorname{Im}z)^{2}-(\operatorname{Im}w)^{2}}\\
&\quad\times\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}c^{3}(z+\overline{w}-2u)+\frac{c^{6}}{6}}\operatorname{Ai}\Bigl(\sqrt{2}c(z-u)+\frac{c^{4}}{4}\Bigr)\operatorname{Ai}\Bigl(\sqrt{2}c(\overline{w}-u)+\frac{c^{4}}{4}\Bigr)du.\end{split}

(ii)

(Symplectic ensemble)

For any
\nu>-1/2
, we have

(1.42)

\lim_{N\to\infty}R_{N,k}^{\rm s}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\overline{z}_{j}-z_{j})\,{\textup{Pf}}\bigg[e^{-|z_{j}|^{2}-|z_{l}|^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa^{\widetilde{c}}_{\rm e}(z_{j},z_{l})&\kappa^{\widetilde{c}}_{\rm e}(z_{j},\overline{z}_{l})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\kappa^{\widetilde{c}}_{\rm e}(\overline{z}_{j},z_{l})&\kappa^{\widetilde{c}}_{\rm e}(\overline{z}_{j},\overline{z}_{l})\end{pmatrix}\bigg]_{j,l=1}^{k},

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, where

and

(1.43)

\kappa^{c}_{\rm e}(z,w):=\sqrt{\pi}e^{z^{2}+w^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}W(f_{w,c},f_{z,c})(u)\,du,

and

(1.44)

f_{z,c}(u):=2c\int_{0}^{u}e^{c^{3}(z-t)+\frac{c^{6}}{12}}\operatorname{Ai}\Bigl(2c(z-t)+\frac{c^{4}}{4}\Bigr)\,dt.

For the elliptic GinUE, the edge scaling limit at weak non-Hermiticity was first obtained by Bender in

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we compile integrable structures of the non-Hermitian Wishart ensembles and asymptotic behaviours of the generalised Laguerre polynomials.

2.1.
Planar (skew)-orthogonal Laguerre polynomials

In this subsection, we recall the planar (skew)-orthogonal polynomial formalism for determinantal/Pfaffian structures of the non-Hermitian Wishart ensembles.
Let

(2.1)

p_{n}(z)=(-1)^{n}n!\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{N}\Bigr)^{n}L_{n}^{(\nu)}\Big(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Big)

be the scaled monic Laguerre polynomial.
It was shown in
[
79
,
2
]
that the family
\{p_{n}\}
satisfies the planar orthogonality

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{n}(z)\overline{p_{m}(z)}\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(z)\,dA(z)=h_{n}\,\delta_{n,m},\qquad h_{n}=\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{2}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(n+\nu+1)}{N^{\nu+2n+2}},

where
\omega^{\rm c}
is the weight function in (
1.2
).
Note that in the maximally non-Hermitian limit
\tau\to 0
, the orthogonal polynomial
p_{n}
becomes a monomial. This is consistent with the rotational symmetry of the potential (
1.2
) for
\tau=0
. In this case, some more explicit computations become possible. For instance, the fluctuation of the spectral radius was investigated in
[
46
]
.

Next, let us recall the skew-orthogonal polynomial formalism due to Kanzieper
[
70
]
.
Recall that the skew-symmetric inner product is given by

(2.3)

\langle h|g\rangle_{\rm s}:=\int(\overline{z}-z)\omega_{N}^{\rm s}(z)\Big(h(z)\overline{g(z)}-\overline{h(z)}g(z)\Big)\,dA(z).

Here, one can also consider a general weight function.
By definition, the family of skew-orthogonal polynomials
\{q_{k}\}
is characterized by

(2.4)

\langle q_{2k+1}|q_{2l}\rangle_{\rm s}=-\langle q_{2l}|q_{2k+1}\rangle_{\rm s}=r_{k}\delta_{kl},\qquad\langle q_{2k+1}|q_{2l+1}\rangle_{\rm s}=\langle q_{2l}|q_{2k}\rangle_{\rm s}=0,

where
r_{k}
is called the skew-norm.
(Note that the skew-orthogonal polynomials are not uniquely determined by this condition.) The construction of skew-orthogonal polynomials for a given weight function remains open in general. Nonetheless, a certain construction has been addressed in a recent work
[
8
]
when the associated planar orthogonal polynomial satisfies the three-term recurrence relation.
(See
[
70
,
2
]
for earlier works on special cases.)
In our present case with the weight function (
1.2
), it follows from
[
8
, Example A.3]
that the family

(2.5)

\displaystyle q_{2k+1}(z)=

\displaystyle-(2k+1)!\Bigl(\frac{\tau}{2N}\Bigr)^{2k+1}L_{2k+1}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2N}{\tau}z\Bigr)

(2.6)

\displaystyle q_{2k}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2k}k!\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}{(2N)^{2k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\tau^{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{2^{2j}j!\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2N}{\tau}z\Bigr).

form skew-orthogonal polynomials associated with the weight
\omega^{\rm s}_{N}(z)
with the skew-norm

(2.7)

r_{k}=\frac{(1-\tau^{2})^{2}(2k+1)!\Gamma(2k+2\nu+2)}{(2N)^{4k+2\nu+4}}.

Let us also mention that the partition functions
Z_{N}^{\rm c}
and
Z_{N}^{\rm s}
in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) can be written in terms of the product of orthogonal and skew-orthogonal norms.

Using these polynomials together with the general theory on determinantal/Pfaffian point process
[
35
,
36
]
, we have the following integrable structure of the
k
-point correlation functions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).

Proposition 2.1

(
Determinantal/Pfaffian point processes
)
.

For any
\tau\in[0,1]
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following.

(i)

(Complex ensemble)
We have

(2.8)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{\rm c}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\det\Big[\mathbf{K}_{N}^{\rm c}(z_{j},z_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},\qquad\mathbf{K}_{N}^{\rm c}(z,w):=\sqrt{\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(z)\omega^{\rm c}_{N}(w)}S_{N}(z,\overline{w}),

where

(2.9)

S_{N}(z,w):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{p_{j}(z)p_{j}(w)}{h_{j}}=\frac{2N^{\nu+2}}{1-\tau^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}w\Bigr).

(ii)

(Symplectic ensemble)
We have

(2.10)

\mathbf{R}_{N,k}^{\rm s}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\overline{z}_{j}-z_{j}){\textup{Pf}}\Big[\mathbf{K}_{N}^{\rm s}(z_{j},z_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},

where

(2.11)

\mathbf{K}_{N}^{\rm s}(z,w):=\sqrt{\omega^{\rm s}_{N}(z)\omega^{\rm s}_{N}(w)}\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(z,w)&{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(z,\overline{w})\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\overline{z},w)&{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(\overline{z},\overline{w})\end{pmatrix},\qquad{\bm{\varkappa}}_{N}(z,w):={\bm{G}}_{N}(z,w)-{\bm{G}}_{N}(w,z).

Here,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad{\bm{G}}_{N}(z,w):=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{q_{2k+1}(z)q_{2k}(w)}{r_{k}}\\
&=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}(2N)^{2\nu+3}}{2^{2\nu+1}(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(2k)!!\tau^{2k+1}}{2^{k}\Gamma(k+\nu+3/2)}\frac{\tau^{2j}(2j-1)!!}{2^{j}\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2k+1}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2N}{\tau}z\Bigr)L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2N}{\tau}w\Bigr).\end{split}

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the following section, let us here recall some identities of the generalised Laguerre polynomials.
They satisfy the recurrence relations

(2.13)

\displaystyle L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)=L_{k}^{(\nu+1)}(z)-L_{k-1}^{(\nu+1)}(z),

(2.14)

\displaystyle kL_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)=(k+\nu)L_{k-1}^{(\nu)}(z)-zL_{k-1}^{(\nu+1)}(z).

Furthermore it satisfies the differentiation rules:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)=-L_{k-1}^{(\nu+1)}(z),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\bigl(z^{\nu}L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)\bigr)=(k+\nu)z^{\nu-1}L_{k}^{(\nu-1)}(z).

Combining these, it also follows that

(2.17)

z\partial_{z}^{2}L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)+(\nu+1-z)\partial_{z}L_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)+kL_{k}^{(\nu)}(z)=0.

2.2.
Strong asymptotics of the generalised Laguerre polynomials

To describe the asymptotic behaviour of planar orthogonal polynomials, it is convenient use the conformal mappings associated with the droplet (
1.8
).
Let

(2.18)

\phi(z):=\Bigl(z+\frac{\tau^{2}}{z}\Bigr)+2\tau

be the (shifted) Joukowsky transform
\phi:\mathbb{C}\backslash{\rm clo}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}\backslash S
. The inverse map
\psi:\mathbb{C}\backslash S\to\mathbb{C}\backslash{\rm clo}(\mathbb{D})
is given by

(2.19)

\psi(z):=\frac{z-2\tau}{2}\Bigl(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{4\tau^{2}}{(z-2\tau)^{2}}}\Bigr).

The Schwarz function
\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{C}\backslash[0,4\tau]\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(2.20)

\mathcal{S}(z):=\overline{\phi(1/\overline{\psi(z)})}=\frac{(z-2\tau)}{2}\bigg(\tau^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}+\Bigl(\tau^{2}-\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\Bigr)\sqrt{1-\frac{4\tau^{2}}{(z-2\tau)^{2}}}\bigg)+2\tau.

Furthermore, by
[
4
, Eq.(3.11)]
, we have

(2.21)

\mathcal{S}(z)=\frac{z}{A^{2}}\bigl(2C(z)+B\bigr)^{2},\quad z\in S^{\mathrm{c}}.

where
A
and
B
are given by (
1.4
) and

(2.22)

C(z)=\frac{A}{4}\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{\tau}\frac{z-\sqrt{(z-2\tau)^{2}-4\tau^{2}}}{z}

is the Cauchy transform of the measure
\mu
in (
1.7
), see
[
4
, Eq.(3.29)]
.
Using the Schwarz function (
2.20
), the domain of (
2.19
) can be extended so that
\psi:\mathbb{C}\backslash[0,4\tau]\to\mathbb{C}\backslash{\rm clo}(B_{\tau}(0))
, where
B_{R}(w)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-w|<R\}
.

[
88
]
.
In particular, for the planar orthogonal polynomial, the asymptotic behaviours depend on the regions separated by the limiting skeleton, see Figure

Lemma 2.2

(
Exponential regime
)
.

Fix
\tau\in[0,1)
,
\nu>-1
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(2.23)

L_{N+r}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{N}{\tau}z\Bigr)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi N}}\frac{(-1)^{N+r}}{\tau^{N+r}}\frac{\psi(z)^{r+\frac{\nu}{2}}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(z)}}{z^{\nu/2}}e^{Ng_{\tau}(z)}\Bigl(1+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\Bigr)\Bigr),

uniformly over any compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\backslash[0,4\tau]
, where
g\equiv g_{\tau}
is defined by

(2.24)

g_{\tau}(z):=\frac{2z}{z+\sqrt{(z-2\tau)^{2}-4\tau^{2}}}+\log\Big(\frac{z-2\tau+\sqrt{(z-2\tau)^{2}-4\tau^{2}}}{2}\Big).

We mention that the
g
-function (
2.24
) is indeed a general concept typically encountered within the framework of Riemann-Hilbert analysis.
This function represents the (complexified) logarithmic energy of the limiting empirical measure of zeros of orthogonal polynomials, which in our present case, is a scaled Marchenko-Pastur law supported on
[0,4\tau]
.

By
[
17
, Lemma B.1]
and
[
88
, Theorem 2.4]
, we have the following asymptotic behaviour.

Lemma 2.3

(
Oscillatory regime
)
.

For a fixed small
\delta>0
, we define

B_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\delta<\operatorname{Re}z<1-\delta,-\delta<\operatorname{Im}z<\delta\}.

Fix
\tau\in[0,1)
and
\nu>-1
. Then, for
z\in B_{\delta}
, we have

(2.25)

3.1.
Differential equation of the complex ensemble

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(i).

Let us make use of the transformation

(3.1)

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}^{(\nu)}(z,w):=e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(\nu)}(z,w),

where
\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(\nu)}
is given by (
1.16
).
Then it suffices to show that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\bigg[(1-\tau^{2})^{2}z\,\partial_{z}^{2}+(1-\tau^{2})^{2}(\nu+1)\partial_{z}-w\bigg]\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)\\
&=e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\frac{N!\,\tau^{2N-1}}{\Gamma(N+\nu+1)}\bigg[(N+\nu)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)-\tau^{2}(N+\nu)\,L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg].\end{split}

By differentiating
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)
with respect to the variable
z
and using the differentiation rule (
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle\quad(1-\tau^{2})e^{-\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\partial_{z}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)

\displaystyle=-(1-\tau^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j-1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j-1}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

Furthermore, it follows from the recurrence relation (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle\quad(1-\tau^{2})e^{-\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\partial_{z}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)-\frac{N!\,\tau^{2N-1}}{\Gamma(N+\nu+1)}L_{N-1}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(j+1)!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j+1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

Note here that by using (
2.14
),

\displaystyle\quad w\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(j+1)!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j+1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

On the other hand, by (
2.16
), we have

\displaystyle\quad e^{-\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\partial_{z}\bigg[\frac{N!\,\tau^{2N-1}}{\Gamma(N+\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{\nu+1}L_{N-1}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{\nu}\frac{N!}{\Gamma(N+\nu+1)}\frac{\tau^{2N-2}}{1-\tau^{2}}\bigg(-N\tau^{2}L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)+(N+\nu)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{w}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{-\nu}e^{-\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\partial_{z}\bigg[w\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{\nu+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=(1-\tau^{2})\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j-1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)+\frac{z}{\tau}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j-1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(j+1)!\,\tau^{2j+1}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j+1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr).

Combining all of the above, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad e^{-\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\partial_{z}\Bigl[w\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{\nu+1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+2)}L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{w}{\tau(1-\tau^{2})}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{\nu}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{j!\,\tau^{2j}}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{j}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)-\frac{N!\,\tau^{2N}}{\Gamma(N+\nu+1)}L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg].

As a consequence together with (
2.14
), it follows that

\displaystyle\quad(1-\tau^{2})\Bigl(z\partial_{z}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)+(\nu+1)\partial_{z}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)\Bigr)-\frac{w}{1-\tau^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{N}(z,w)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}(z+w)}\frac{N!}{\Gamma(N+\nu+1)}\frac{\tau^{2N-1}}{1-\tau^{2}}(N+\nu)\bigg[L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)-\tau^{2}L_{N}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{N-1}^{(\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{w}{\tau}\Bigr)\bigg].

This completes the proof.
∎

Note that by change of variables, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\Bigl[\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{mN}z\partial_{z}^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{(1-\tau^{2})(m\nu+1)}{mN}+2\tau z\Bigr)\partial_{z}+mN\frac{\tau^{2}z-w}{1-\tau^{2}}+(m\nu+1)\tau\Bigr]S_{mN}^{(m\nu)}(z,w)\\
&=\frac{2(mN)^{m\nu+2}}{(1-\tau^{2})^{2}}\frac{(mN)!\,\tau^{2mN-1}}{\Gamma(mN+m\nu)}\bigg[L_{mN-1}^{(m\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{mN}{\tau}z\Bigr)L_{mN}^{(m\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{mN}{\tau}w\Bigr)-\tau^{2}L_{mN}^{(m\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{mN}{\tau}z\Bigr)L_{mN-1}^{(m\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{mN}{\tau}w\Bigr)\bigg].\end{split}

In the latter analysis, we shall use this equation frequently for
m=1,2
.

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{(2k)!!\,(2j-1)!!\,\tau^{2k+2j+1}}{2^{k+j}\,\Gamma(k+\nu+3/2)\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2k}^{(2\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2w}{\tau}\Bigr).

\displaystyle L_{2k}^{(2\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)

which gives

\displaystyle\partial_{z}f_{N}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(2k)!\tau^{4k+1}}{2^{2k}\Gamma(k+\nu+3/2)\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}L_{2k}^{(2\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{2k}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2w}{\tau}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{2\nu+1}\Bigl(\partial_{z}f_{N}(z,w)+2\tau f_{N}(z,w)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{-2\nu}\partial_{z}\bigg[\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)^{2\nu+1}\Bigl(\partial_{z}f_{N}(z,w)+2\tau f_{N}(z,w)\Bigr)\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(2N)!!\tau^{2N-1}}{2^{N-3}\Gamma(N+\nu+1/2)}L_{2N}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(2j-1)!!\tau^{2j}}{2^{j}\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2w}{\tau}\Bigr),

where we used (

.

), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}f_{N}(w,z)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(2k)!!(2j-1)!!\tau^{2k+2j+3}}{2^{k+j}\Gamma(k+\nu+3/2)\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2k+1}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2w}{\tau}\Bigr)L_{2j}^{(2\nu+1)}\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(2N)!!\tau^{2N}}{2^{N-1}\Gamma(N+\nu+1/2)}L_{2N}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2z}{\tau}\Bigr)\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{(2j-1)!!\tau^{2j}}{2^{j}\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}L_{2j}^{(2\nu)}\Bigl(\frac{2w}{\tau}\Bigr).

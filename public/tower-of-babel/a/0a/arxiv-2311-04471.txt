1.
Introduction

Our paper concerns the following Lane-Emden system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=\frac{|v|^{p-1}v}{[\ln(e+|v|)]^{\varepsilon}}&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v=\frac{|u|^{q-1}u}{[\ln(e+|u|)]^{\varepsilon}}&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u,v=0&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
,

\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter,

p
,
q
lying on the critical
Sobolev hyperbola

(1.5)

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{N-2}{N},

which has been introduced by Mitidieri
[
42
]
.
The Lane-Emden system is used in physics to model spatial phenomena in a variety of biological and chemical fields.
The origin of this concept dates back to the papers
[
16
,
48
]
for
several considerations and motivations behind them.

In the case
\varepsilon=0
,
u=v
and
p=q
, (
1
) reduces to
the celebrated Lane-Emden-Fowler problem

(1.6)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u\ \ &{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\
u=0\ \ &{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

which plays a central role in the development of methods of nonlinear analysis in the last four decades.
Since the compactness of Sobolev’s embedding holds,
there exist at least one positive solution and infinitely many sign-changing solutions to problem (
1.6
) provided that

1<p<p_{S}=\begin{cases}+\infty&\mathrm{if\penalty\ }N\leq 2,\\
\frac{N+2}{N-2}&\mathrm{if\penalty\ }N\geq 3.\end{cases}

While for
p\geq\frac{N+2}{N-2}
,
the Pohozaev identity
[
49
]
showed that problem (
1.6
) has no positive solution
if the domain
\Omega
is strictly starshaped.
Kazdan and Warner
[
35
]
established infinitely many radial solutions in an annulus domain.
For the critical case
p=\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, Bahri and Coron obtained the existence of a positive solution of (
1.6
) if the domain
\Omega

has nontrivial reduced homology with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficient.
If the domain
\Omega
has small holes, the size of the hole effects the number of sign-changing solutions of problem (
1.6
), see
[
25
,
45
]
.

The natural counterpart of (
1.6
) for elliptic systems is the following Lane-Emden system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=|v|^{p-1}v&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v=|u|^{q-1}u&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u,v=0&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
p
,
q>1
.
Compared to the single case,
since the quadratic
part of the functional of system (
1
) is strongly indefinite,
which leads to the mountain pass theorem do not work.
However, there are some ways to get the existence of
solutions, such as the linking theorem, see
[
3
,
22
,
32
]
and references therein.
Mitidieri
[
43
,
42
]
pointedx out that the Sobolev critical exponent
p_{S}
should be
played by the so-called Sobolev hyperbola (
1.5
),
which is crucial for the existence of solutions.
By the work of
[
42
]
,
the generalized Pohozaev identity for (
1
) can be written as

(1.11)

\Big(\frac{N}{p+1}-\alpha\Big)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p+1}dx+\Big(\frac{N}{q+1}-(N-2-\alpha)\Big)\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q+1}dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}d\sigma,

for every
\alpha>0
.
When
\alpha=\frac{N}{p+1}
and
(p,q)
lies on or above the critical hyperbola, that is

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}\leq\frac{N-2}{N},

The identity (
1.11
) gives the nonexistence of positive solutions on star shaped domains.
Then, the works in
[
5
,
31
]
show that the condition of existence theory is (
p,q
) satisfying the so called subcriticality, that is

(1.12)

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{N-2}{N}.

It may hold that
p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}<q
for
N\geq 3
,
then the energy functional does not be well defined on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
In a variational setting, the existence of sign-changing solution has been studied under a variety of assumptions in a large number of papers,
[
12
,
7
]
for a single
equation, for systems, see
[
15
,
13
,
26
,
10
]
,
in particular,
[
6
]
for Hénon-Lane-Emden system.

System (
1
) with critical nonlinearity has been extensively studied in the past
decade years and many results are known regarding existence, multiplicity and concentration phenomena.
One direction is the nearly critical hyperbola,
by the moving plane method,
Guerra
[
26
]
obtained the uniform boundedness of least energy solutions to (
1
) on convex domains under that
p\in(\frac{2}{N-2},\frac{N+2}{N-2}]
and

(1.13)

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q_{\varepsilon}+1}=\frac{N-2}{N}+\varepsilon,

for small
\varepsilon>0
.
Observe that (

1.12

when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

1.5
).
Then, Choi and Kim
[
11

removed the convexity assumption,
and they obtained the similar results
by local Pohozaev-type identities and sharp pointwise estimates of the solutions.
Jin and Kim
[
34
]
extended the Coron’s result
[
17
]
to (
1
) with critical hyperbola (
1.5
) provided that
\Omega
has a sufficiently small hole,
they constructed a family of positive solutions concentrating
around the center of the hole by a perturbation argument.
In the supercritical case, more precisely,
\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}\to\frac{N-k-2}{N-k}
,

\frac{N-k-2}{N-k}<\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}<\frac{N-2}{N}

Another direction,

[

By Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method,

-\Delta v=|u|^{q-1}u+\varepsilon(\beta_{2}u+\alpha v)&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]

where

Meanwhile, they also obtained the existence of multiplicity results for a slightly subcritical system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=v^{p-\alpha\varepsilon}&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v=u^{q-\beta\varepsilon}&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u,v>0&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]

Moreover, due to the local Pohozaev identities, Guo et al.
[

obtained the non-degeneracy of these blowing-up solutions,

Under the subcritical (

[

Many important contributions have been made towards the solution of system (

[

for Lane-Emden system with Neumann boundary conditions and many others.

The above cases with power type nonlinearity have been extensively

2.
Scheme of the proof

In this section, we are devoted to find a solution to system (
1
).
Recall that

\frac{1}{p^{*}}:=\frac{p}{p+1}-\frac{1}{N}=\frac{1}{q+1}+\frac{1}{N}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{q^{*}}:=\frac{q}{q+1}-\frac{1}{N}=\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{N},

so the exponents
p^{*}
and
q^{*}
are the Hölder’s conjugates of each other.
By the Sobolev embedding theorem, it holds

\begin{cases}\dot{W}^{2,\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{1,p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
\dot{W}^{2,\frac{q+1}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{1,q^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{cases}

For any smooth domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we set

X_{p,q}=\Big(\dot{W}^{2,\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)\cap\dot{W}_{0}^{1,p^{*}}(\Omega)\Big)\times\Big(\dot{W}^{2,\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)\cap\dot{W}_{0}^{1,q^{*}}(\Omega)\Big),

and

(2.1)

X_{p,q,\varepsilon}=\left\{(u,v)\in X_{p,q}:u\in L^{q+1-\varepsilon}(\Omega),v\in L^{p+1-\varepsilon}(\Omega)\right\},

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|=

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{X_{p,q,\varepsilon}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)}+\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{p+1-\varepsilon}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{q+1-\varepsilon}(\Omega)},

and
the orthogonal is taken with respect to the scalar
product in

(2.3)

\langle(u,v),(w,z)\rangle=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\nabla w+\nabla v\nabla z)dx.

It will be useful to rewrite system (
1
) in a different setting.
Let
\mathcal{I}^{*}:L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow X_{p,q,\varepsilon}

be the adjoint operator of the immersion
\mathcal{I}:X_{p,q,\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\Omega)\times L^{q+1}(\Omega)
,
that is, for
(u,v)\in L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)
,

(w,z)=\mathcal{I}^{*}(u,v)
if and only if

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=w&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
-\Delta v=z&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u=v=0&\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}u(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)w(y)dy,\\
v(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)z(y)dy,\end{cases}\quad\mbox{for}\ x\in\Omega.

Then, by the Calderón-Zygmund estimate, it holds

(2.4)

\|\mathcal{I}^{*}(u,v)\|\leq C|(u,v)|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for}\ (u,v)\in L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega),

for some constant
C>0
.
Moreover, we define

(2.5)

|(u,v)|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)}=|u|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\Omega)}+|v|_{L^{\frac{q+1}{q}}(\Omega)}.

Using these definitions and notations,
system (
1
) turns out to be equivalent to
finding solutions to the following equation

(w,z)=\mathcal{I}^{*}[f_{\varepsilon}(u),g_{\varepsilon}(v)],

where

(2.6)

f_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{[\ln(e+|u|)]^{\varepsilon}}\quad\mbox{and}\ \ g_{\varepsilon}(v)=\frac{|v|^{q-1}v}{[\ln(e+|v|)]^{\varepsilon}}.

We analyze the Green’s function denoting by
G(x,y)
for Laplace operator in
\Omega
with zero Dirichlet
boundary condition,
and to construct an approximate function for problem (
1
),
let
H(x,y)
be the regular part of
G(x,y)
.
Then

G(x,y)=\frac{\gamma_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}-H(x,y),

for
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
such that
x\neq y
,
where
\gamma_{N}=\frac{1}{(N-2)|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|}>0
and

|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|=2\pi^{N/2}/\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the origin.
In addition, we introduce a function
\tilde{G}:\Omega\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\begin{cases}-\Delta\widetilde{G}(x,y)=G^{p}(x,y)\quad&\text{for}\penalty\ x\in\Omega,\\
\widetilde{G}(x,y)=0\quad&\text{for}\penalty\ x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and its regular part
\tilde{H}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{H}(x,y)=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{N,p}}{|x-y|^{(N-2)p-2}}-\tilde{G}(x,y),

where
\tilde{\gamma}_{N,p}=\frac{\gamma_{N}^{p}}{[(N-2)p-2][N-(N-2)p]}
.

),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}\bigg|\Big(V_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)^{p}\ln\ln\Big(e+V_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)\bigg|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}\bigg|\Big[V_{i}^{p}+O\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)\Big]\ln\ln\Big(e+V_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)\bigg|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx

(A.41)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{i}}\bigg|V_{i}^{p}\ln\ln\Big(e+V_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)\bigg|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx+C\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}\int_{B_{i}}V_{j}\bigg|\ln\ln\Big(e+V_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)\bigg|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{B_{i}-\xi_{i}}{\mu_{i}}}\bigg|V^{p}_{1,0}(y)\bigg(\ln|\ln\mu_{i}^{-\frac{N}{p+1}}|+\ln\Big[1+\frac{\ln\Big(e^{1-\frac{N}{p+1}|\ln\mu_{i}|}+V_{1,0}(y)\Big)}{\frac{N}{p+1}|\ln\mu_{i}|}\Big]\bigg)\bigg|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dy+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\ln|\ln\mu|)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}.

Regarding the second term in (
A
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}\int_{B_{i}}V_{j}\bigg|\ln\ln\Big(e+V_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}V_{j}\Big)\bigg|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dx=O\Big((\ln|\ln\mu|)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\Big).

Thus,

(A.42)

\Big|f_{\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}PV_{i}\Big)-f_{0}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}PV_{i}\Big)\Big|_{\frac{p+1}{p}}=O\Big(\varepsilon\mu^{\frac{Np}{p+1}}(\ln|\ln\mu|)\Big).

Putting (

)-(
A.42
) and (
2.4
) together, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bigg[f_{\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}PV_{i}\Big)-f_{0}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}PV_{i}\Big)\bigg](P\Phi_{jh}-\Phi_{jh})dx=O\Big(\varepsilon\mu^{\frac{Np}{p+1}}(\ln|\ln\mu|)\Big).

∎

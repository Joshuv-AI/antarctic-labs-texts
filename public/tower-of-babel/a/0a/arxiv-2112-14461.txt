Abstract
. We characterise the Weyl-Hörmander symbol classes S ⁡ ( M , g ) S(M,g) via the growth of the action of the corresponding Ψ \Psi DOs on time-frequency shifts of a single test function. For this purpose, we introduce a geometric short-time Fourier transform which is well-suited for the analysis of S ⁡ ( M , g ) S(M,g) . We define new modulation spaces and achieve the characterisation of the Weyl-Hörmander classes by showing that they are intersections of such modulation spaces suitable for the time-frequency characterisation.

The general idea in the proof of the main result of the article is to extend the time-frequency analysis to the Weyl-Hörmander classes. However, the techniques from

3.1.
Essentially uniformly confined families of symbols

We introduce the key ingredient for the geometric generalisation of the symplectic short-time Fourier transform. It consists of families of Schwartz functions which “keep track of the growth of the metric”; they are going to be the analogue of the windows in the classical STFT.

We start with two technical results. Their proofs are straightforward and we omit them (cf. Remark

s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{s}}(W\times W)\times\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(W),\quad(G,\boldsymbol{\varphi})\mapsto\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G.

(iv)

Assume that

is smooth and satisfies the following condition: for every
S\in W
there exists a
g
-admissible weight
M_{S}
such that the function

belongs to
S(M_{S},g)
. If
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r)
satisfies the condition in Proposition

(iii)
, then
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}
uniquely extends to a continuous mapping
\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(W\times W)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(W)
.

Proof.

We first address
(i)
. Proposition
3.11

(ii)
verifies that (
3.17
) is separately continuous. Since both spaces in the domain of (
3.17
) are barrelled,
[
33
, Theorem 5, p. 159]
verifies that (
3.17
) is hypocontinuous. The validity of (
3.18
) for
f\in\mathcal{S}(W)
is a consequence of (
3.15
) and the general case follows from the density of
\mathcal{S}(W)
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(W)
and Proposition
3.11

(i)
. We turn our attention to
(ii)
. The fact that (
3.19
) is strongly measurable follows from the comments before Remark
3.10
. The rest of
(ii)
follows from Corollary
3.16
and a straightforward computation. To prove
(iii)
, let
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary but fixed. For
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r)
, setting
\varphi_{X}:=\boldsymbol{\varphi}(X)
,
X\in W
, and employing the same technique as in the proof of Proposition
3.11

(i)
, one can show that

\left|\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{T_{j};Y}\right)(e^{2\pi i[\Xi,Y]}\varphi_{X}(Y))\right|\\
\leq\frac{C_{1}\|\varphi_{X}\|^{(k+l)}_{g_{X},U_{X,r}}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(\Xi))^{k/2}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{kN_{0}/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}g_{0}(T_{j})^{1/2}}{(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(Y-U_{X,r}))^{l/2}}.

Let
\displaystyle G\in\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longleftarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{s}}(W\times W)
. Employing (
A.9
) and (
3.10
), we deduce

\frac{(1+g_{0}(Y))^{l/2}\left|\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{T_{j}}\right)\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G(Y)\right|}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}g_{0}(T_{j})^{1/2}}\\
\leq C_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k+l)}_{g,r}\int_{W\times W}|G(X,\Xi)|(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(\Xi))^{k/2}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{(kN_{0}+l(N_{0}+1)+2nN_{0})/2}dXd\Xi,

which implies the claim in
(iii)
.

We now address
(iv)
. For every
S_{1},S_{2}\in W
, it holds that
g_{X}(S_{1},S_{2})=(g_{X}(S_{1}+S_{2})-g_{X}(S_{1})-g_{X}(S_{2}))/2
. Let

, the condition in
(iv)
gives the following bound for some

:

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}_{T}g_{X}(E_{j},E_{l})|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}g_{X}(T)^{k/2}(M_{E_{j}+E_{l}}(X)+M_{E_{j}}(X)+M_{E_{l}}(X))

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}g_{X}(T)^{k/2}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{m_{0}},

for all

,

|\partial^{k}_{T}|g_{X}||\leq\tilde{C}^{\prime}g_{X}(T)^{k/2}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{2nm_{0}},\quad X,T\in W.

The Faá di Bruno formula (see
[

together with (

) yield (for

)

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}_{T}(|g_{X}|^{1/2})|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\sum_{l=1}^{k}|g_{X}|^{-(2l-1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\ldots+k_{l}=k\\
k_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}|\partial^{k_{j}}_{T}|g_{X}||

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{2}g_{X}(T)^{k/2}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{2nm_{0}k}\sum_{l=1}^{k}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{(2l-1)nN_{0}}

Now,
[

verifies the following bound for

:

\left|\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\partial_{T_{j}}\right)|g_{X}|^{1/2}\right|\leq C^{\prime}_{3}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{2nm_{0}k+2knN_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}g_{X}(T_{j})^{1/2},\;\;X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\in W.

be a bounded subset of
\mathcal{S}(W)
. The above estimate together with Proposition

(iii)
imply that

. For any

, we have

, which proves the claim in

∎

Remark 3.18
.

Given any Hörmander metric

, one can always find a smooth Hörmander metric
\tilde{g}
which satisfies the condition in Proposition

(iv)
and is equivalent to

(i.e., there exists

such that

,

). In fact, the smooth Hörmander metric

constructed in Example

(ii)
is equivalent to

and (

) proves that for each

, the function

Remark 3.19
.

The Hörmander metrics of the commonly used calculi almost always satisfy the assumption in Proposition
3.17

(iv)
(like the Shubin calculus, the SG-calculus, the Hörmander

-calculus etc.). In fact, if

and

.

4.
Geometric modulation spaces

In this section, we define a class of generalised modulation spaces with the help of the geometric short-time Fourier transform which we introduced in Subsection
3.2
. Besides being of independent interest, they will play a crucial role in the proof of the main result.

We start by introducing the following class of admissible weights. A positive measurable function
\eta:W\times W\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is called a
uniformly admissible weight with respect to the metric

g
if there are constants
\tilde{C}\geq 1
,
\tilde{r}>0
and
\tilde{\tau}\geq 0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle g_{X}(X-Y)\leq\tilde{r}^{2}\Rightarrow\tilde{C}^{-1}\eta(Y,\Xi)\leq\eta(X,\Xi)\leq\tilde{C}\eta(Y,\Xi),\quad X,Y,\Xi\in W,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\eta(X+X^{\prime},Y)\leq\tilde{C}\eta(X,Y)(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}))^{\tilde{\tau}},\quad X,X^{\prime},Y\in W,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\eta(X,Y+Y^{\prime})\leq\tilde{C}\eta(X,Y)(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(Y^{\prime}))^{\tilde{\tau}},\quad X,Y,Y^{\prime}\in W.

We call any number
\tilde{r}>0
for which (
4.1
) holds true a
slow variation constant
for
\eta
and we call any number
\tilde{\tau}\geq 0
for which (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) hold true a
temperance constant
for
\eta
. In such case, we call the pair
(\tilde{r},\tilde{\tau})

admissibility constants
for
\eta
.

Remark 4.1
.

The reason for this notation is the following. If
M
is a
g
-admissible weight with admissibility constants
r
and
N
, then the function
(X,Y)\mapsto M(X)
is uniformly admissible with respect to
g
with admissibility constants
(r,N)
.

Notice that (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) imply that the functions
(X,Y)\mapsto|g_{X}|
and
(X,Y)\mapsto|g^{\sigma}_{X}|
are uniformly admissible with respect to
g
with admissibility constants
(r_{0},2nN_{0})
.

Remark 4.2
.

If
g
is the standard Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, then
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{4n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is uniformly admissible for
g
if and only if
\eta
is moderate with respect to the Beurling weight
(1+|\cdot|)^{\tau}
, for some
\tau\geq 0
(cf.
[
25
, Definition 11.1.1, p. 217]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta
and
\eta_{1}
be two uniformly admissible weights with respect to
g
with admissibility constants
(\tilde{r},\tilde{\tau})
and
(\tilde{r}_{1},\tilde{\tau}_{1})
, respectively. Then
\eta\eta_{1}
is uniformly admissible weight with respect to
g
with admissibility constants
(\min\{\tilde{r},\tilde{r}_{1}\},\tilde{\tau}+\tilde{\tau}_{1})
. Furthermore,
\eta^{s}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, is also uniformly admissible with respect to
g
with admissibility constants
(\tilde{r},s\tilde{\tau})
when
s\geq 0
and
(\tilde{r},|s|\tilde{\tau}(N_{0}+1))
when
s<0
.

Proof.

The only non-trivial part is to verify (
4.2
) for
\eta^{s}
,
s<0
, with the constant
|s|\tilde{\tau}(N_{0}+1)
in place of
\tilde{\tau}
. This follows from the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\eta(X,Y)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\eta(X+X^{\prime},Y)(1+g^{\sigma}_{X+X^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}))^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\eta(X+X^{\prime},Y)(1+C_{0}g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime})(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}))^{N_{0}})^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}C_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}\eta(X+X^{\prime},Y)(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}))^{\tilde{\tau}(N_{0}+1)}.

∎

Let
w
be a positive measurable function on
W\times W
which satisfies (
2.1
). For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we denote by
L^{p,q}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)
the Banach space of all measurable functions
f
on
W\times W
which satisfy

\left(\int_{W}\left(\int_{W}|f(X,\Xi)|^{p}w(X,\Xi)^{p}dv_{g}(X)\right)^{q/p}d\Xi\right)^{1/q}<\infty

(with the obvious modifications when
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
). Similarly, we denote by
\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)
the Banach space of all measurable functions
f
on
W\times W
which satisfy

\left(\int_{W}\left(\int_{W}|f(X,\Xi)|^{q}w(X,\Xi)^{q}d\Xi\right)^{p/q}dv_{g}(X)\right)^{1/p}<\infty

(again, with the obvious modifications when
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
). When
g
is the Euclidean metric, we will simply denote these spaces by
L^{p,q}_{w}(W\times W)
and
\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{w}(W\times W)
, respectively. Clearly,

L^{p,p}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)=\widetilde{L}^{p,p}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)=L^{p}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda),\quad p\in[1,\infty].

We have the following continuous inclusions for all
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
:

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longleftarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{\infty}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{s}}(W\times W)\subseteq L^{p,q}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)\subseteq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{-s}}(W\times W),

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longleftarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{\infty}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{s}}(W\times W)\subseteq\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{w}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)\subseteq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{-s}}(W\times W).

When
p<\infty
and
q<\infty
, the inclusions are also dense. If
\eta
is uniformly admissible weight with respect to
g
, than (cf. Lemma
4.3
),

\eta(X,Y)^{\pm 1}\leq C\eta(0,0)^{\pm 1}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(X))^{N}(1+g^{\sigma}_{0}(Y))^{N},\quad X,Y\in W,

for some
C,N>0
; consequently, it satisfies (
2.1
).

The generalised modulation spaces which we are going to define will be modelled on
\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)
rather than on
L^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)
. As it turns out, the former spaces are better suited as co-domains and domains for the GSTFT and its adjoint.

Anticipating what follows, we prove the following result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\eta
be a uniformly admissible weight with respect to
g
with slow variation constant
\tilde{r}
and let
0<r^{\prime}_{0}\leq\min\{r_{0},\tilde{r}\}
. For any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r^{\prime}_{0})
, the mapping

\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)\rightarrow\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda),\quad G\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G,

is well-defined and continuous. Furthermore, for each
p,q\in[1,\infty]
there exist
C\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

(4.4)

\|\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G\|_{\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|G\|_{\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)},

for all
G\in\widetilde{L}^{p,q}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r^{\prime}_{0})
.

Before we prove Theorem
4.4
, we show the following special case.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\eta
,
\tilde{r}
and
r^{\prime}_{0}
be as in Theorem
4.4
. For any
p\in[1,\infty]
and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r^{\prime}_{0})
, the mapping

L^{p}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)\rightarrow L^{p}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda),\quad G\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G,

is well-defined and continuous. Furthermore, for each
p\in[1,\infty]
there exist
C\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

(4.5)

\|\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G\|_{L^{p}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|G\|_{L^{p}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)},

for all
G\in L^{p}_{\eta}(W\times W,dv_{g}d\lambda)
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r^{\prime}_{0})
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\tau}\geq 0
be a temperance constant for
\eta
. Let
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r^{\prime}_{0})
and set
\varphi_{X}:=\boldsymbol{\varphi}(X)
,
\psi_{X}:=\boldsymbol{\psi}(X)
,
X\in W
. In view of Proposition
3.11

(i)
and Proposition
3.17

(i)
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}:\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{-s}}(W\times W)\rightarrow\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{\infty}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{-s}}(W\times W)

is well-defined and continuous. For
G\in\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
s\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{-s}}(W\times W)
, we infer

\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G(X,\Xi)=\langle G,|g|^{1/2}\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}(e^{2\pi i[\Xi,\cdot]}\psi_{X})}\rangle.

Notice that
\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}(e^{2\pi i[\Xi,\cdot]}\psi_{X})(Z_{1},Z_{2})}=\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\varphi_{Z_{1}}(X,\Xi-Z_{2})
and consequently,

|\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G(X,\Xi)|\leq\int_{W\times W}|G(Z_{1},\Xi-Z_{2})||\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\varphi_{Z_{1}}(X,Z_{2})||g_{Z_{1}}|^{1/2}dZ_{1}dZ_{2},\;\;X,\Xi\in W.

We make the following

Claim.
For every
N>0
there are
C\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

|\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\varphi_{Z_{1}}(X,Z_{2})||g_{Z_{1}}|^{1/2}\leq C\|\varphi_{Z_{1}}\|^{(k)}_{g_{Z_{1}},U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}}\|\psi_{X}\|^{(k)}_{g_{X},U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}}(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(Z_{2}))^{-N}(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(Z_{2}))^{-N}\\
\cdot(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{-N}(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{-N},

for all
X,Z_{1},Z_{2}\in W
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\operatorname{Conf}_{g}(W;r^{\prime}_{0})
.

We postpone its proof for later and continue with the proof of the proposition. Let
X^{\prime}\in U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}
and
Z^{\prime}_{1}\in U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}
be arbitrary. Then

\displaystyle\eta(X,\Xi)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\eta(X^{\prime},\Xi)\leq\tilde{C}^{2}\eta(Z^{\prime}_{1},\Xi)(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z^{\prime}_{1}}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1}))^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}\tilde{C}^{3}\eta(Z_{1},\Xi)(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1}))^{\tilde{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}\tilde{C}^{4}\eta(Z_{1},\Xi-Z_{2})(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(Z_{2}))^{\tilde{\tau}}(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1}))^{\tilde{\tau}}.

As
X^{\prime}\in U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}
and
Z^{\prime}_{1}\in U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}
are arbitrary, we deduce

\eta(X,\Xi)\leq C_{0}^{\tilde{\tau}}\tilde{C}^{4}\eta(Z_{1},\Xi-Z_{2})(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(Z_{2}))^{\tilde{\tau}}(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{\tilde{\tau}}.

We employ the bounds in the Claim to infer that there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G(X,\Xi)|\eta(X,\Xi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|\psi_{X}\|^{(k)}_{g_{X},U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}}\int_{W\times W}|G(Z_{1},\Xi-Z_{2})|\eta(Z_{1},\Xi-Z_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(Z_{2}))^{-n-1}(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(Z_{2}))^{-n-1}(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{-(2n+1)(N_{0}+1)}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{-(2n+1)(N_{0}+1)}dZ_{1}dZ_{2},

for all
X,\Xi\in W
. Let
X^{\prime}\in U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}
and
Z^{\prime}_{1}\in U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}
be arbitrary. We have

\displaystyle g_{X}(X-Z_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 3g_{X}(X-X^{\prime})+3g_{X}(Z^{\prime}_{1}-Z_{1})+3g_{X}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 3r^{\prime 2}_{0}+3C_{0}g_{X^{\prime}}(Z^{\prime}_{1}-Z_{1})+3g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 3r^{\prime 2}_{0}+3C_{0}^{2}g_{Z^{\prime}_{1}}(Z^{\prime}_{1}-Z_{1})(1+g^{\sigma}_{X^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1}))^{N_{0}}+3g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 3r^{\prime 2}_{0}+3r^{\prime 2}_{0}C_{0}^{N_{0}+3}(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1}))^{N_{0}}+3g^{\sigma}_{X}(X^{\prime}-Z^{\prime}_{1}),

and consequently,

(4.8)

\displaystyle g_{X}(X-Z_{1})\leq 3(2r^{\prime 2}_{0}+1)C_{0}^{N_{0}+3}(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{N_{0}+1}.

Analogously,

(4.9)

\displaystyle g_{Z_{1}}(X-Z_{1})\leq 3(2r^{\prime 2}_{0}+1)C_{0}^{N_{0}+3}(1+g^{\sigma}_{Z_{1}}(U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}-U_{Z_{1},r^{\prime}_{0}}))^{N_{0}+1}.

First we prove the claim in the proposition when

and
p=\infty
. When

, (

) and (
4.8
) together with the identity

imply

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}G(X,\Xi)|\eta(X,\Xi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|\psi_{X}\|^{(k)}_{g_{X},U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}(W\times W)}\int_{W\times W}\frac{|g_{X}|^{1/2}|g^{\sigma}_{X}|^{1/2}dZ_{1}dZ_{2}}{(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(Z_{2}))^{n+1}(1+g_{X}(X-Z_{1}))^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=C_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|\psi_{X}\|^{(k)}_{g_{X},U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}(W\times W)}\int_{W}\frac{|g_{X}|^{1/2}dZ_{1}}{(1+g_{X}(Z_{1}))^{n+1}}\int_{W}\frac{|g^{\sigma}_{X}|^{1/2}dZ_{2}}{(1+g^{\sigma}_{X}(Z_{2}))^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|^{(k)}_{g,r^{\prime}_{0}}\|\psi_{X}\|^{(k)}_{g_{X},U_{X,r^{\prime}_{0}}}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}(W\times W)},

Lemma A.1

for each fixed

and

\displaystyle\inf_{j\in\tilde{J}_{1;X},\,k\in\tilde{J}_{2;Y}}f_{j,k}(X,Y)

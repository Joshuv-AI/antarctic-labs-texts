A strong approximation in L 2 L^{2} for the solutions of the Maxwell system with highly oscillating periodic coefficients.


Abstract
We consider a Maxwell system on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with periodic and highly oscillating coefficients. It is known that the solutions converge in the weak- ∗ \ast topology of L ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , L 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) ) L^{\infty}(0,T;\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})) to the solution of a similar problem with constant coefficients given as the H H -limits of the electric permittivity and the magnetic permeability respectively, i.e. the limit in the sense of the homogenization of linear elliptic equations with varying coefficients. However, it is not true that the elliptic corrector also provides a corrector for the solution of the Maxwell system, i.e. an approximation of the solutions in the strong topology of L 2 L^{2} . We shall prove that the oscillations in the space variable also produce oscillations in the time variable. We get a corrector consisting of adding to the elliptic corrector the sum of infinitely plane waves in the fast variable. Note that related results have been previously proved for the wave equation. Our proof is based on the two-scale convergence theory for almost periodic functions. One of the novelties is to show how this contains the classical Block decomposition.

3
Position of the problem and main results.

Our aim in the present section is to get a corrector result for the homogenization of the Maxwell system
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with periodic coefficients. Namely, we are interested in obtaining an approximation in the strong
L^{2}
-topology of the solutions of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}B_{\varepsilon}+{\rm curl\,}_{x}E_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}-{\rm curl\,}_{x}\big(Q^{-1}\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)B_{\varepsilon}\Big)=g_{\varepsilon}\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle(B_{\varepsilon})_{|t=0}=B^{0}_{\varepsilon},\ (E_{\varepsilon})_{|t=0}=E^{0}_{\varepsilon}\ \hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

The coefficients
P
and
Q
are assumed to satisfy:

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle P,\,Q\in L^{\infty}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})^{3\times 3}\ \hbox{ hermitian}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\exists\,\alpha>0,\ \min\Big\{P\xi\cdot\overline{\xi},\ Q\xi\cdot\overline{\xi}\Big\}\geq\alpha|\xi|^{2},\ \forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{3},\ \hbox{a.e. in }\,Y\end{array}

(3.2)

Observe that assuming

f_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad{\rm div}_{x}B^{0}_{\varepsilon}=0,

defining
\rho_{\varepsilon}
as the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}={\rm div}_{x}g_{\varepsilon},\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{|t=0}={\rm div}_{x}\Big(P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E^{0}_{\varepsilon}\Big),\ \hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

and applying the divergence (
3.1
), we get, thanks to initial conditions,

{\rm div}_{x}B_{\varepsilon}=0,\ \ {\rm div}_{x}\big(P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E_{\varepsilon}\big)=\rho_{\varepsilon}\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

The magnetic field
B_{\varepsilon}
and the electric field

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{U}+i\xi\times P^{-1}\hat{F}+{\rm curl}_{y}(P^{-1}\hat{F})=U\ \hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hat{F}-i\xi\times Q^{-1}\hat{U}-{\rm curl}_{y}(Q^{-1}\hat{U})=F\ \hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\end{array}\right.

with
\hat{F}=P\hat{V}
,
F=PV
and observing that the operator

{\cal B}:D(B)\subset\big[L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}\big]^{2}\to\big[L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}\big]^{2}
defined by

D({\cal B})=\Big\{(U,F)\in\big[L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}\big]^{2}:\ {\rm curl}_{y}(Q^{-1}U),{\rm curl}_{y}(P^{-1}F)\in L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}\Big\},

{\cal B}(F,U)=\Big(i\xi\times P^{-1}F+{\rm curl}_{y}(P^{-1}F),-i\xi\times Q^{-1}U-{\rm curl}_{y}(Q^{-1}U)\Big),\quad\forall\,(F,U)\in D({\cal B}),

has dense domain and is closed and anti-hermitic with respect to the scalar product in
\big[L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}\big]^{2}
defined by

\big((U_{1},F_{1}),(U_{2},F_{2})\big)=\int_{Y^{3}}\big(Q^{-1}U_{1}\cdot\overline{U}_{2}+P^{-1}F_{1}\cdot F_{2}\big).

This implies that
{\cal B}
is maximal monotone and then the existence of
(\hat{U},\hat{V})
.

Let us prove that
{\cal A}
is compact. We consider a bounded sequence
(U_{n},V_{n})
in
W_{\xi}
. Using that
(\hat{U}_{n},\hat{V}_{n}):={\cal A}(U_{n},V_{n})
satisfies

\int_{Y^{3}}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{U}_{n}\cdot\overline{\hat{U}_{n}}+P\hat{V}_{n}\cdot\overline{\hat{V}_{n}}\big)dy=\int_{Y^{3}}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{U}\cdot\overline{\hat{U}}+P\hat{V}\cdot\overline{\hat{V}}\big)dy

we get that
(\hat{U}_{n},\hat{V}_{n})
is also bounded in
W_{\xi}
. From definition of
{\cal A}
, we also have that

{\rm curl}_{y}\hat{V}_{n}
,
{\rm curl}_{y}(Q^{-1}\hat{U}_{n})
are bounded in
L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}
. On the other hand, using a Hodge decomposition for
Q^{-1}\hat{U}_{n}
and
\hat{V}_{n}
, we have the existence of
C>0
,

\hat{W}_{n},\hat{Z}_{n}\in H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})^{3}
and
\hat{w}_{n},\hat{z}_{n}\in H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})
such that

Q^{-1}\hat{U}_{n}=\hat{W}_{n}+\nabla_{y}\hat{w}_{n},\quad\hat{V}_{n}=\hat{Z}_{n}+\nabla_{y}\hat{z}_{n},\quad{\rm div}_{y}\hat{W}_{n}={\rm div}_{y}(Q^{-1}\hat{Z}_{n})=0,

(3.33)

with

\|\hat{W}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(Y^{3})^{3}}+\|\hat{w}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(Y^{3})}\leq C\Big(\|Q^{-1}\hat{U}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}}+\|{\rm curl}_{y}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{U}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}}\Big),

\|\hat{Z}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(Y^{3})^{3}}+\|\hat{z}_{n}\|_{H^{1}(Y^{3}))}\leq C\Big(\|\hat{V}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}}+\|{\rm curl}_{y}\hat{V}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}}\Big).

Using (
3.33
) and
(\hat{U}_{n},\hat{V}_{n})\in W_{\xi}
we get

{\rm div}_{y}(Q\nabla_{y}\hat{w}_{n})=-i\xi\cdot\hat{U}_{n}-{\rm div}_{y}(Q\hat{W}_{n}),\quad{\rm div}_{y}(P\nabla_{y}\hat{z}_{n})=-i\xi\cdot P\hat{V}_{n}-{\rm div}_{y}(P\hat{Z}_{n}),

By Rellich-Kondrachov’s compactness theorem
\hat{W}_{n}
,
\hat{Z}_{n}
are compact in
L^{2}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})
and then
the right-hand sides of these equalities are compact in
H^{-1}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})
. Thus, by Lax-Milgram’s theorem
\hat{w}_{n}
and
\hat{z}_{n}
are compact in
H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})
. Returning to (
3.33
) we conclude that
\hat{U}_{n}
,
\hat{V}_{n}
are compact in
L^{2}(Y^{3})^{3}
. This proves the compactness of
{\cal A}
.

In order to prove that the spectral values of

are a sequence of eigenvalues with finite dimension which tends to infinity it is enough to observe that

with

Thus, the result follows from the Fredholm theory.

of (
3.36
) we can use that by Proposition
3.12
, the functions
\hat{B}
,
\hat{E}
admit the decomposition

\hat{B}=\sum_{\xi\in Y^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{i(\lambda^{\xi}_{j}s+\xi\cdot y)}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}^{\xi}}a^{\xi}_{jl}(t,x)\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}(y),\quad\hat{E}=\sum_{\xi\in Y^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{i(\lambda^{\xi}_{j}s+\xi\cdot y)}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}^{\xi}}a^{\xi}_{jl}(t,x)\Psi^{\xi}_{jl}(y).

(3.47)

Taking in (
3.36
)

(U,V)=e^{i(\lambda^{\xi}_{j}s+\xi\cdot y)}\big(\Phi^{\xi}_{jl},\Psi^{\xi}_{jl}\big),

we deduce that for every
\xi\in Y^{3}
and every
j\geq 1
, the coefficients
a^{\xi}_{jl}
are the solutions of the finite-dimensional hyperbolic linear system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}a^{\xi}_{jl}+\sum_{q=1}^{m^{\xi}_{j}}b^{\xi}_{jlq}\cdot\nabla_{x}a^{\xi}_{jq}=f_{jl}^{\xi}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle(a^{\xi}_{jl})_{|t=0}=M_{y}\big(e^{-i\xi\cdot y}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}^{0}\cdot\overline{\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}}+P\hat{E}^{0}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{\xi}_{jl}}\big)\big),\end{array}\right.\qquad 1\leq l\leq m_{j},

(3.48)

with

f_{jl}^{\xi}=M_{sy}\big(e^{-i(\lambda^{\xi}_{j}s+\xi\cdot y)}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{f}\cdot\overline{\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}}+\hat{g}\cdot\overline{\Psi^{\xi}_{jl}}\big)\big),

(3.49)

b^{\xi}_{jlq}=\int_{Y}\big(\Psi_{jq}^{\xi}\cdot\overline{Q^{-1}\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}}-Q^{-1}\Phi^{\xi}_{jq}\cdot\overline{\Psi_{jl}^{\xi}}\big)dy.

(3.50)

Since by (
3.20
),
(\hat{B}^{0},\hat{E}^{0})
satisfies
{\rm div}_{y}\hat{B}^{0}={\rm div}_{y}(P\hat{E}^{0}),
we can apply Proposition
3.12
to deduce that
(\hat{B}^{0},\hat{E}^{0})
can be decomposed as

(\hat{B}^{0},\hat{E}^{0})=\sum_{\xi\in Y^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{i\xi\cdot y}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}^{\xi}}\alpha_{jl}^{\xi}\big(\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}(y),\Psi^{\xi}_{jl}(y)\big),

with

\alpha^{\xi}_{jl}=M_{y}\Big(e^{-i\xi\cdot y}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}^{0}\cdot\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}+P\hat{E}^{0}\cdot\Psi^{\xi}_{jl}\big)\Big)=(a^{\xi}_{jl})_{|t=0}.

This proves that

\big(\hat{B}(0,x,0,y),\hat{E}(0,x,0,y)\big)=\sum_{\xi\in Y^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}^{\xi}}a^{\xi}_{jl}(0,x)e^{i\xi\cdot y}\big(\Phi^{\xi}_{jl}(y).\Psi^{\xi}_{ij}(y)\big)=\big(\hat{B}^{0},\hat{E}^{0}\big),

and then it allows us to write the initial conditions for
\hat{B}
and
\hat{E}
in the simple form

(\hat{B})_{t=0,s=0}=\hat{B}^{0},\quad(\hat{E})_{t=0,s=0}=\hat{E}^{0}.

(3.51)

3.15
, we can now obtain the limit problem of (

). This is given by Theorem

. Before, we need

We define the homogenized matrices corresponding to

P_{H}\zeta=\int_{Y}P(\zeta+\nabla Z_{\zeta})\,dy,\quad Q_{H}\eta=\int_{Y}Q(\eta+\nabla W_{\eta})\,dy,\quad\quad\forall\,\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(3.52)

with

Theorem 3.19
.

In the conditions of Theorem
3.15
, defining

f,g,B^{0},(PE)^{0}
by (

P_{H}
,

B_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}B,\ \ E_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}E\quad\hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3},

(3.53)

with
B,E
the solutions of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle B,E\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\partial_{t}B+{\rm curl}\,E=f\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle P_{H}\partial_{t}E-{\rm curl}\,(Q^{-1}_{H}B)=g\ \hbox{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle B_{|t=0}=B^{0},\ P_{H}E_{|t=0}=(PE)^{0}\ \hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{array}\right.

(3.54)

Moreover, the functions the functions
\hat{B}
,

in Theorem
3.15
are related with

,

by

B=M_{sy}\hat{B},\quad E=M_{sy}\hat{E},\quad Q_{H}^{-1}B+\nabla_{y}W_{Q_{H}^{-1}B}=Q^{-1}M_{s}\hat{B},\quad E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E}=M_{s}E,

(3.55)

with
W_{Q_{H}^{-1}B}
, and

are defined by (
3.30
).

Proof.
Due to the relationship between weak convergence and two-scale convergence (see Theorem
3.4
) and
B_{\varepsilon},E_{\varepsilon}
bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}
, we have that (
3.53
) holds with

B=M_{sy}\hat{B}
,
E=M_{sy}\hat{E}
. On the other hand, statement 5 in Proposition
3.12
implies

M_{s}\hat{B}=Q(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}}),\quad M_{s}\hat{E}=E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E},

(3.56)

with
\eta_{B}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}
and
W_{\eta_{B}}
,
Z_{E}
defined by (

). Namely, defintion (
3.52
) of

provides

B=\int_{Y^{3}}Q(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}})dy=Q_{H}\eta_{B}\Longrightarrow\eta_{B}=Q_{H}^{-1}B.

(3.57)

Taking in (
3.36
)
U=Q(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})
,
V=\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta}
, with
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
W_{\eta},Z_{\zeta}\in H^{1}_{\sharp}(Y^{3})
, related by (

), we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{Y^{3}}\Big(Q(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}})\cdot\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}+P(E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E})\cdot\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}\int_{Y^{3}}\Big((E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E})\times\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}-(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}})\times\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\int_{Y^{3}}\Big(M_{s}\hat{f}\cdot\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}+M_{s}\hat{g}\cdot\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy,\end{array}

(3.58)

combined with the initial conditions

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{Y^{3}}\Big(Q(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}})\cdot\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}+P(E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E})\cdot\overline{(E+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\int_{Y^{3}}\Big(\hat{B}^{0}\cdot\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}+P\hat{E}^{0}\cdot\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy,\quad\hbox{ for }t=0.\end{array}

(3.59)

The equations (
3.30

and

3.52

,

\int_{Y^{3}}\Big(Q(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}})\cdot\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}+P(E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E})\cdot\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy=B\cdot\overline{\eta}+P_{H}E\cdot\overline{\zeta}.

Using also that by (
3.57

\int_{Y^{3}}\Big((E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E})\times\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}-(\eta_{B}+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta_{B}})\times\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy=E\times\overline{\eta}-Q_{H}^{-1}B\times\overline{\zeta},

that
{\rm div}_{y}\hat{f}={\rm div}_{y}\hat{g}=0
, and that definitions (

and

\int_{Y^{3}}\Big(M_{s}\hat{f}\cdot\overline{(\eta+\nabla_{y}W_{\eta})}+M_{s}\hat{g}\cdot\overline{(\zeta+\nabla_{y}Z_{\zeta})}\Big)dy=f\cdot\overline{\eta}+g\cdot\overline{\zeta},

3.58

(3.61)

Remark 3.20
.

Theorem
3.19
is known (see e.g.
[
22
]
, Chapter 7,
[
24
]
). It can be easily obtained using for example the two-scale convergence theory for periodic functions (
[
1
]
,
[
18
]
). Assuming that the right-hand sides
f_{\varepsilon}
,
g_{\varepsilon}
are of the form

f_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\hat{f}(t,x,x/\varepsilon),\quad g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\hat{g}(t,x,x/\varepsilon),

(3.62)

with
f,g
periodic (or almost periodic) in the quick variable
y=x/ep
, it can be formally obtained seeking an ansatz for
(B_{\varepsilon},E_{\varepsilon})
of the form

B_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\sim\check{B}\Big(t,x,{x\over\varepsilon}\Big)+\varepsilon\check{B}^{1}\Big(t,x,{x\over\varepsilon}\Big)+\cdots,\quad E_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\sim\check{E}\Big(t,x,{x\over\varepsilon}\Big)+\varepsilon\check{E}^{1}\Big(t,x,{x\over\varepsilon}\Big)+\cdots.

(3.63)

Observe that contrarily to the ansatz in Remark
3.16
, we are assuming that the functions
B_{\varepsilon}
and
E_{\varepsilon}
do not oscillate in the time variable. This may seem natural because if
f_{\varepsilon}
and
g_{\varepsilon}
satisfy (
3.62
), then neither coefficients nor the right-hand sides in (
3.3
) oscillate in the time variable. The corresponding functions
\check{B}
and
\check{E}
must be obtained from
\hat{E}
and
\hat{B}
by
\check{B}=M_{s}\hat{B}
and
\check{E}=M_{s}\hat{E}
. Thus, using (
3.55
) we get

\check{B}=Q\big(Q_{H}^{-1}B+\nabla_{y}W_{Q_{H}^{-1}B}\big),\quad\check{E}=E+\nabla_{y}Z_{E}.

(3.64)

As it is usual in classical homogenization these functions depend locally on the limits
B
and
E
of the sequences
B_{\varepsilon}
and
E_{\varepsilon}
in the sense that for a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the values of
\check{B}(t,x,.)
and
\check{E}(t,x,.)
only depend on
B(t,x)
and
E(t,x)
. If
\check{B}
and
\check{E}
effectively provide an approximation in the strong topology of
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}
for
B_{\varepsilon}
and
E_{\varepsilon}
(corrector result) then we must have
\hat{B}=\check{B}
and
\hat{E}=\check{E}
which is equivalent to have in (
3.47
) that all the functions
a_{jl}^{\xi}
vanish except those corresponding to
\xi=0
and
\lambda_{j}^{\xi}=0
. Taking into account (
3.48
) where, thanks to (
3.62
), the coefficients
f^{\xi}_{jl}
vanish, we deduce that
(\hat{B}^{0},\hat{E}^{0})
must be orthogonal for the scalar product given by (
3.25
) to all the functions
(\Phi^{\xi}_{jl},\Psi^{\xi}_{jl})
corresponding to
\xi\not=0
or
\xi=0
,
\lambda^{\xi}_{j}\not=0
, i.e. by statement 5 in Proposition
3.12
they must satisfy

\hat{B}^{0}=Q\big(Q_{H}^{-1}B^{0}+\nabla_{y}W_{Q_{H}^{-1}B^{0}}\big),\quad\hat{E}^{0}=E^{0}+\nabla_{y}Z_{E^{0}},

(3.65)

This holds if we assume that
B_{\varepsilon}^{0}
and
E_{\varepsilon}^{0}
satisfy (in addition to (
3.11
)) that

{\rm curl}\big(Q\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)^{-1}B^{0}_{\varepsilon}\big)\ \hbox{ is compact in }H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3},\quad{\rm div}\big(P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E^{0}_{\varepsilon}\big)\ \hbox{ is compact in }H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(3.66)

which would be the equivalent in our context to the well posed conditions in
[
15
]
for the homogenization of the wave equation. However (
3.65
) does not hold even if we assume that
B^{0}_{\varepsilon}
,
E^{0}_{\varepsilon}
do not depend on
\varepsilon
. This means that contrarily to assumption (
3.63
) the solutions of (
3.1
) oscillate in time even if the right-hand sides do not.

Observe that while
\check{B}
,
\check{E}
can be locally obtained from
E
and
B
, the remaining terms in decomposition (
3.47
) of
\hat{E}
,
\hat{B}
, which solve (
3.48
), depend non-locally on the initial conditions and the right-hand sides.

The following theorem combined with Proposition
3.7
provides now a corrector result for the solution of (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.21
.

In the conditions of Theorem
3.15
. If the convergences in (
3.17
) hold in the strong two-scale convergence sense, then convergences (
3.35
) also hold in the strong two-scale convergence sense.

Proof.
Integrating twice in time (
3.6
) applied to (
3.1
), we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(Q^{-1}\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)B_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{B_{\varepsilon}}+P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{E_{\varepsilon}}\Big)dxdt\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle={T\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(Q^{-1}\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)B^{0}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{B^{0}_{\varepsilon}}+P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E^{0}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{E^{0}_{\varepsilon}}\Big)dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle+Re\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(T-t)\Big(Q^{-1}\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)f_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{B_{\varepsilon}}+g_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{E_{\varepsilon}}\Big)dxdt.\end{array}

Since we are assuming that the convergences in (
3.17
) hold in the strong two-scale sense, we can use (
3.35
) to deduce

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(Q^{-1}\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)B_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{B_{\varepsilon}}+P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{E_{\varepsilon}}\Big)dxdt\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle={T\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{y}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}^{0}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}^{0}}+P\hat{E}^{0}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}^{0}}\big)dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle+Re\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(T-t)M_{sy}\Big(Q^{-1}\hat{f}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}}+\hat{g}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}}\Big)dxdt.\end{array}

(3.67)

On the other hand, taking in (
3.36
)
U=\hat{B}
and
V=\hat{E}
, and taking the real part, we get (the functions
\hat{B}
and
\hat{E}
are not the necessary regular but the result can be obtained by using an approximation of them)

{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{sy}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}}+P\hat{E}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}}\big)dxdt=Re\int_{0}^{t}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{sy}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{f}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}}+\hat{g}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}}\big)dxdt.

Integrating twice this inequality and using the boundary conditions in (
3.36
) combined with (
3.51
), we also get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{sy}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}}+P\hat{E}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}}\big)dxdt\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle={T\over 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{s}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}^{0}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}^{0}}+P\hat{E}^{0}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}^{0}}\Big)dx+Re\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(T-t)M_{sy}\Big(Q^{-1}\hat{f}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}}+\hat{g}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}}\Big)dxdt.\end{array}

By (
3.67
), this proves the convergence of the energies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(Q^{-1}\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)B_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{B_{\varepsilon}}+P\big({x\over\varepsilon}\big)E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{E_{\varepsilon}}\Big)dxdt={1\over 2}\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{sy}\big(Q^{-1}\hat{B}\cdot\overline{\hat{B}}+P\hat{E}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}}\big)dxdt,

and then the strong two-scale convergence of
B_{\varepsilon}
and
E_{\varepsilon}
.

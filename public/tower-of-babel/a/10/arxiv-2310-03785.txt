Electromagnetic fields in compact binaries: post-Newtonian wave generation and application to double white dwarfs systems Preprint: DESY-23-074
Quentin Henry, François Larrouturou, Christophe Le Poncin-Lafitte

Abstract
The aim of this work is twofold: (i) to properly define a wave-generation formalism for compact-supported sources embedded in Einstein-Maxwell theory, relying on matched post-Newtonian and multipolar-post-Minkowskian expansions; (ii) to apply this formalism (which is valid for any type of post-Newtonian sources) to the case of two stars with constant and aligned magnetic dipoles, by computing the fluxes of energy and angular momentum to the next-to-leading order, as well as the gravitational amplitude modes. Assuming eccentric orbits, we derive the evolution of orbital parameters, as well as the observables of the system, notably the gravitational phase for quasi-circular orbits. Finally, we give some numerical estimates for the contribution of the magnetic dipoles for some realistic systems.



I
Introduction

High accuracy analytic predictions are a milestone of the signal analysis for gravitational-wave (GW) detectors, notably when it comes to observing the long inspiral phase of coalescing compact objects.
If such objects, typically neutron stars (NS) and white dwarfs (WD), are far from being the main source of events of the current LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA network
Abbott et al. 2021
, they will be a major source for the next generations of detectors, such as the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA)
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
, or the Einstein Telescope (ET)
ET: 2022
.
Indeed, those instruments will resolve tens of thousands of galactic binaries, composed primarily of WD and/or NS
Hils et al. 1990
;
Timpano et al. 2006
.
It is thus crucial to provide accurate analytic templates for those sources, notably taking into account physical effects beyond the commonly used spinning point-particle approximation.
Among those effects are for instance the tidal response of the stars, that have been treated to high accuracy in
Henry et al. 2020a
;
Henry et al. 2020b
, or the presence of strong magnetic fields, that can be as intense as
10^{9}\,G
for white dwarfs and
10^{12}\,G
for neutron stars
Ferrario and Wickramasinghe 2006
;
Ferrario et al. 2020
.
Such fields can induce an orbital decay, and shift the frequency of the gravitational wave emitted by the binary
Bourgoin et al. 2021
;
Bourgoin et al. 2022a
;
Bourgoin et al. 2022b
;
Bourgoin et al. 2022c
;
Carvalho et al. 2022
;
Savalle et al. 2023
;
Aykroyd et al. 2023
.
The LISA experiment will resolve a large number of double WD
Korol et al. 2018
;
Korol et al. 2022
, and some of those systems will even be used for the calibration of the instrument
Stroeer and Vecchio 2006
;
Kupfer et al. 2018
;
Finch et al. 2023
.
It seems therefore important to provide accurate analytic waveform templates that incorporate the electromagnetic structure of WD and NS, which is the purpose of this work.

The templates that will be used for the detection and characterization of the inspiral phase of galactic binaries are mainly based on post-Newtonian results
Mangiagli et al. 2019
, relying on both weak-field and slow-motion approximations (see
e.g.

Blanchet 2014
;
Buonanno and Sathyaprakash 2014
;
Porto 2016
for reviews).
In such a framework, it is customary to distinguish between a “conservative” sector, describing the motion of the two objects
via
a set of Noetherian quantities, and a “dissipative” one, depicting the fluxes at spatial infinity.
Both sectors are linked by the famous flux-balance equations, that describe the loss of energy
E
and angular momentum
J^{i}
of the system as

\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\rangle=-\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}J^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\rangle=-\langle\mathcal{G}^{i}\rangle\,,

(1)

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}^{i}
are respectively the energy and angular momentum fluxes, as detected by an observer at spatial infinity and brackets
\langle\cdot\rangle
denote the usual orbital average.
We let the interested reader refer to
Blanchet and Faye 2019
for the flux-balance equations associated with the other Noetherian quantities,
i.e.
linear momentum and center-of-mass position.
In the case of a binary system bearing electric charges, the left-hand sides of the flux-balance equations have been widely studied, see
e.g.

Julié 2018
;
Khalil et al. 2018
;
Patil 2020
;
Gupta 2022
;
Martínez 2022
;
Martinez 2023
but, to the best of our knowledge, a proper treatment of the right-hand sides is missing.
Moreover, and as previously argued, going beyond the simple electric charge case will be important for future GW detectors.
In this spirit, we have achieved in
Henry et al. 2023
the construction of the conservative sector including electromagnetic (EM) effects, and computed the Noetherian energy and angular momentum at next-to-leading orders for a binary system of stars bearing magnetic dipoles.
The present work is the continuation of this effort, and we aim at properly defining the corresponding dissipative sector, and build a consistent wave-generation formalism, taking electromagnetic effects into account.

The computation of PN gravitational waveforms relies on matched post-Newtonian and multipolar-post-Minkowskian expansions.
This “PN-MPM” framework was developed in
Blanchet and Damour 1986
;
Blanchet 1987
;
Blanchet and Damour 1988
;
Blanchet and Damour 1992
;
Blanchet 1998a
and summarized
e.g.
in
Blanchet 2014
.
It enabled to reach high precision in the analytic determination of the gravitational waveform: the phase is currently known at 4.5PN order
Blanchet et al. 2023a
, the gravitational flux for generic orbits and the dominant gravitational modes, at 4PN order
Blanchet et al. 2023b
, and the other modes, at 3.5PN order
Faye et al. 2012
;
Henry 2023
;
Henry et al. 2022
(we recall that the
n
PN order corresponds to a
(v/c)^{2n}
correction beyond the leading order, where
v
is the typical velocity of the virialized binary system and
c
the speed of light).
Besides the PN-MPM formalism, two frameworks have also been developed to deal with the wave generation, namely the direct integration of the relaxed equations
Epstein and Wagoner 1975
and effective field theory
Goldberger and Rothstein 2006
;
Ross 2012
.
They both have reached the 2PN precision for point-particle
Will and Wiseman 1996
;
Leibovich et al. 2020
.
The aim of the present work is thus to construct a proper PN-MPM formalism including electromagnetic effects, and to apply it to derive the waveform emitted by a binary of stars bearing magnetic dipoles.

The plan of this paper is as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we present the construction of the PN-MPM wave-generation formalism including electromagnetic effects, and compute the first non-linearities (the so-called
tails
) in Sec.
III
.
Then, we apply our formalism to the case of two spinless stars bearing constant and aligned magnetic moments in Sec.
IV
, and derive the various fluxes, the evolution of orbital parameters for elliptic trajectories, as well as the gravitational phase and modes for quasi-circular orbits.
We also give numerical estimates for those quantities, before concluding in Sec.
V
.
App.
A
presents the construction of the electromagnetic fluxes.
Lengthy expressions are displayed in App.
B
, and stored in an ancillary file
Anc
.

II
Wave-generation formalism

The aim of this section is to build a wave-generation formalism in the Einstein-Maxwell theory, directly inspired from the usual PN-MPM framework used in GR
Blanchet and Damour 1986
;
Blanchet 1987
;
Blanchet and Damour 1988
;
Blanchet and Damour 1992
;
Blanchet 1998a
(see
Blanchet 2014
for a review).
In such framework, the behavior of the fields at future null infinity is parametrized by some
radiative
multipole moments, from which the fluxes of energy and angular momentum, as well as the gravitational amplitude modes, are derived.
Those radiative moments are linked to the
source
multipole moments, describing the matter distribution, by taking into account the non-linearities arising during the propagation of the waves towards spatial infinity.

As clear from the analysis of the generic structure of the fields in the exterior zone, performed in Sec
II.2
, three steps are required to construct a proper PN-MPM wave-generation formalism: (i) expressing the
source
moments in terms of the matter distribution, as done in Sec.
II.3
; (ii) taking into account the non-linearities
via
an iteration scheme and extracting the
radiative
moments, as discussed in Sec.
II.4
; (iii) relating those moments to the modes and fluxes, as presented in Sec.
II.5
.

II.2
Generic structure of the fields in the exterior zone

The equations of motion (
2
) are formally solved by the application of a retarded three-dimensional Green function

\displaystyle h^{\mu\nu}=\frac{16\pi G}{c^{4}}\,\Box_{\text{ret}}^{-1}\tau^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{4G}{c^{4}}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}^{\prime}\,\frac{\tau^{\mu\nu}\left(\bm{x}^{\prime},t-|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|/c\right)}{|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|}\,,

(8a)

\displaystyle A_{\mu}=-\frac{4\pi G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}}\,\Box_{\text{ret}}^{-1}\iota_{\mu}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}^{\prime}\,\frac{\iota_{\mu}\left(\bm{x}^{\prime},t-|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|/c\right)}{|\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|}\,,

(8b)

but those integrals are too complicated to be solved directly.
As we are interested with radiation towards spatial infinity, we will consider the problem in the
exterior

i.e.
the region of space outside the matter distribution.
In such region of space, both fields

and

coincide with their multipolar expansions,
\mathcal{M}\left(h^{\mu\nu}\right)
and
\mathcal{M}\left(A_{\mu}\right)
(as those are formal series).
Moreover, we have by construction
T^{\mu\nu}=J_{\mu}=0

As multipolar expansions commute with products and derivatives, we then have to solve a simpler, multipolar expanded, version of the equations of motion (
2
)

\displaystyle\Box\,\mathcal{M}\left(h^{\mu\nu}\right)=\mathcal{M}\left(\Box h^{\mu\nu}\right)=\mathcal{M}\left(\Lambda^{\mu\nu}\right)=\Lambda^{\mu\nu}\left[\mathcal{M}\left(h^{\mu\nu}\right),\mathcal{M}\left(A_{\mu}\right)\right]\,,

(9a)

\displaystyle\Box\,\mathcal{M}\left(A_{\mu}\right)=\mathcal{M}\left(\Box A_{\mu}\right)=\mathcal{M}\left(\Phi_{\mu}\right)=\Phi_{\mu}\left[\mathcal{M}\left(h^{\mu\nu}\right),\mathcal{M}\left(A_{\mu}\right)\right]\,.

(9b)

9

8

II.3
Expression of the source moments

As the source moments encompass the information on the matter system, we need to express the linear (homogeneous) solution in terms of the matter distribution.
The aim is twofold: (i) decomposing the quantities
F_{L}^{\mu\nu}
and
G_{\mu}^{L}
entering (
11
) as simple, irreducible STF
source
(and
gauge
) moments; (ii) matching those moments to the matter distributions
\tau^{\mu\nu}
and
\iota_{\mu}
.

The wave equation for
h^{\mu\nu}
in terms of
\tau^{\mu\nu}
(
2
) is the same as the one used in pure GR.
Indeed, in the metric sector, the only difference between this work and usual GR computations is the explicit expression of
\tau^{\mu\nu}
in terms of the fields.
Therefore, the STF reduction and matching of the moments in terms of
\tau^{\mu\nu}
will be exactly the same as the usual one, derived in
Blanchet 1998a

The linear solution is thus parametrized by six sets STF moments as

(12)

This generic homogeneous solution is split in two sectors.
The
canonical

bears the two propagating degrees of freedom of GR, and is written in terms of two sets of

moments,
\{\mathrm{I}_{L},\mathrm{J}_{L}\}

;
Pirani 1964
;
Thorne 1980
.
Its expression can be found
e.g.
in Eq. (36) of
Blanchet 2014
.
The second sector,

Next, it is common knowledge that an vector field obeying a
U(1)
symmetry only propagates two degrees of freedom, whereas the linear solution (
19
) contains three sets of moments.
Thus, in the same spirit as the splitting of
h_{\text{hom}}^{\mu\nu}
(
12
),
A_{\mu}^{\text{hom}}
can be rearranged into a “canonical” EM field, parametrized by two sets of STF
source
moments,
\{\mathrm{E}_{L},\mathrm{B}_{L}\}
, of respective electric and magnetic parities, and a
U(1)
generator, parametrized by a set of STF
gauge
moments,
\{\mathrm{K}_{L}\}

A^{\text{hom}}_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{\text{can}}\left[\mathrm{E}_{L},\mathrm{B}_{L}\right]+\partial_{\mu}\theta\left[\mathrm{K}_{L}\right]\,,

(20)

where we pose

\displaystyle A^{\text{can}}_{0}=-\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\partial_{L}\tilde{\mathrm{E}}_{L}\,,

(21a)

\displaystyle A^{\text{can}}_{i}=-\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\bigg[\partial_{L-1}\dot{\tilde{\mathrm{E}}}_{iL-1}+\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\,\partial_{jL-1}\,\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{kL-1}\bigg]\,,

(21b)

\displaystyle\theta=-\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\partial_{L}\tilde{\mathrm{K}}_{L}\,.

(21c)

The signs and the powers in
c
have been fixed to recover the usual results for constant charges and dipoles
Jackson 1999
.
Comparing with Eq. (
19
), one can read

\mathrm{E}_{L}=-\frac{R_{L}}{c}-\frac{1}{\ell+1}\,\frac{\dot{T}^{(-)}_{L}}{c^{2}}\,,\qquad\mathrm{B}_{L}=-\frac{\ell+1}{\ell}\,T^{(0)}_{L}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{K}_{L}=\frac{1}{\ell+1}\,T^{(-)}_{L}\,.

(22)

Let us note that for
\ell=0
, the linear gauge condition (
17
) simply yields
\dot{\mathrm{E}}=0
, which is nothing but the conservation of the electric monopole: the electric charge defined in our formalism is of the ADM type.
In order to reinforce this fact and avoid confusion, we will denote in the following
\mathrm{Q}=\mathrm{E}
.

II.3.2
Matching the source and gauge moments

Once the EM field has been decomposed in irreducible STF moments, one needs to link those moments to the parameter of the matter distribution, through the powerful
matching
procedure
Blanchet 1998a
.
This procedure relies on a few hypothesis, presented
e.g.
in Sec. 4.1 of
Blanchet 2014
: (i) the problem can be split into two zones, that partially overlap; (ii) the fields admit a well-defined PN expansion in the near-zone; (iii) the fields admit a well-defined multipolar expansion in the exterior zone.
Under those hypothesis, one can match the two expansions in the overlapping zone, and derive an explicit expression for the moments in terms of the source of the wave equation.

Hopefully, the structure of our problem is identical to the pure GR one, and all the hypothesis are verified.
Indeed, the first hypothesis is satisfied as long as the matter distribution has a compact support, which is one of the assumptions of this work.
The second one has been verified by the study of the conservative sector, presented in
Henry et al. 2023
.
As for the last one, it directly follows from Eq. (
9
).
Therefore, one can apply the method exposed in
Blanchet 1998a
;
Blanchet 2014
to the EM field and derive

G^{L}_{\mu}=\underset{B=0}{\text{PF}}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\,\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\,\hat{x}_{L}\,\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\delta_{\ell}(z)\,\bar{\iota}_{\mu}\left(\bm{x},t+zr/c\right)\,,

(23)

where the bar denotes a PN expansion (
i.e.
a formal
c\to\infty
series), and we have introduced

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\delta_{\ell}(z)\,\bar{\iota}_{\mu}\left(\bm{x},t+zr/c\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(2\ell+1)!!}{2^{\ell+1}\,\ell!}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\ell}\,\bar{\iota}_{\mu}\left(\bm{x},t+zr/c\right)

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(2\ell+1)!!}{(2k)!!\,(2\ell+2k+1)!!}\left(\frac{r}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{2k}\bar{\iota}_{\mu}\left(\bm{x},t\right)\,.

Combining Eqs. (
22
), (
15
) and (
23
), it finally comes

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{L}=-\underset{B=0}{\text{PF}}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\left\{\delta_{\ell}(z)\,\hat{x}_{L}\,\frac{\bar{\iota}_{0}}{c}+\frac{2\ell+1}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+3)\,c^{2}}\,\delta_{\ell+1}(z)\,\hat{x}_{iL}\,\dot{\bar{\iota}}_{i}\right\}\,,

(25a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{L}=\varepsilon_{ab\langle i_{\ell}}\,\underset{B=0}{\text{PF}}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\,\delta_{\ell}(z)\,\hat{x}_{L-1\rangle a}\,\bar{\iota}_{b}\,,

(25b)

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{L}=\frac{2\ell+1}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+3)}\,\underset{B=0}{\text{PF}}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\mathrm{d}z\,\delta_{\ell+1}(z)\,\hat{x}_{iL}\,\bar{\iota}_{i}\,.

(25c)

Those expression are in full agreement with the results of
Damour and Iyer 1991
, that were derived in the framework of non-relativistic electromagnetism (corresponding to setting
\Phi_{\mu}=0
).

II.4
Multipolar-post-Minkowskian iteration scheme and radiative moments

Once the linear solutions
h_{\text{hom}}^{\mu\nu}
and
A^{\text{hom}}_{\mu}
are decomposed in irreducible STF moments, one can turn towards the non-linear sectors of the solutions (
10
).
In order to do so, we proceed
via
a post-Minkowskian expansion

\mathcal{M}\left(h^{\mu\nu}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}G^{n}h_{(n)}^{\mu\nu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}\left(A_{\mu}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}G^{n}A_{(n)\,\mu}\,,

(26)

where the linear orders
h_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}
and
A_{(1)\,\mu}
are naturally given by the homogeneous solutions (
12
) and (
20
).
Note that we combine multipolar and post-Minkowskian expansions, hence the name of the iteration scheme.

II.4.1
Sketch of the iteration procedure

The procedure is conceptually very simple: the vacuum equations (
9
) are also expanded in a post-Minkowskian fashion, and solved order by order in
G
.
Obviously, we cannot provide here the full solution: for each practical computation, one has to determine by dimensional analysis which interactions will play a role and compute them using techniques developed notably in
Blanchet and Damour 1992
;
Blanchet 1998b
;
Blanchet et al. 2022
;
Trestini and Blanchet 2023
.
Note that the main technical difference between our case and the pure GR computations is due to the non-linear nature of the gauge condition for
A_{\mu}
.
Nevertheless, verifying the gauge condition on the
n
th PM order
A_{(n)\,\mu}
only requires the knowledge of lower-order solutions
\{h_{(m<n)}^{\mu\nu},A_{(m<n)\,\mu}\}
, already required to compute
A_{(n)\,\mu}
.
Therefore this non-linear gauge fixing is not a conceptual difficulty by itself.
We provide in Sec.
III
a practical example of such a computation, with the determination of the first non-linearities, namely the
tails
.
An interesting fact is that, due to the form of the interaction term
\Lambda_{\text{EM}}^{\mu\nu}
(
5a
), the EM corrections to the gravitational moments
\{\mathrm{I}_{L},\mathrm{J}_{L}\}
involve at least two EM moments.
For instance, the lowest order EM interactions correcting the mass quadrupole
\mathrm{I}_{ij}
are given by
\mathrm{Q}\times\mathrm{E}_{ij}
and
\mathrm{E}_{i}\times\mathrm{E}_{j}
, which both enter at a relative 2.5PN order (as shown by a rapid dimensional analysis).

Note also that, when dealing wih pure GR, it is customary to first let apart the source-to-source interactions and to focus on computing the interactions between gauge and source moments.
Indeed, such interactions can be recast in the form of a physically equivalent description in terms of only two sets of
canonical
moments
Blanchet 1996
;
Blanchet et al. 2008
;
Blanchet et al. 2022
.
As the structure of the linear EM field (
20
) is very similar to the one of the linear metric (
12
), it is expected that a similar physical equivalence exists.
Nevertheless, the difference between source and canonical moments enter at relative 2.5PN for the gravitational moments
Blanchet 1996
, and also for the EM ones.
Indeed, rapid dimensional and parity analysis show that the lowest-order gauge corrections to
\mathrm{E}_{i}
(resp.
\mathrm{B}_{i}
) are given by the interactions
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{K}_{i}
,
\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{E}_{i}
and
\mathrm{Q}\times\mathrm{W}_{i}
(resp.
\mathrm{W}\times\mathrm{B}_{i}
and
\mathrm{K}\times\mathrm{J}_{i}
), which are all multiplied by a factor
G/c^{5}
.
Such precision is beyond the scope of this work, and the gauge moment will play no role in the practical computations of Sec.
IV
.
We thus let the proper definition of
canonical
EM moments to future works.

II.4.2
Asymptotic structure of the fields

Once multipolar-post-Minkowskian expanded metric and EM fields have been constructed up to the required order, one has to extract the
radiative
moments.
Again, the procedure for the metric is similar to the pure GR case, and we let the reader refer to Sec. 3 of
Blanchet 2014
for details.
We will here concentrate on the EM case, and extract its leading behavior at future null infinity
r\to\infty
, with
u=t-r/c
constant.
Using the relation

\partial_{L}\bigg[\frac{F(t-r/c)}{r}\bigg]=\frac{(-)^{\ell}\,n_{L}}{c^{\ell}\,r}\,F^{(\ell)}(t-r/c)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

(27)

the behavior of the linear solution (
21
) is easily extracted

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\text{lin}}=-\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}\,r}\sum_{\ell}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}n_{L}E_{L}^{(\ell)}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(28a)

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\text{lin}}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}\,r}\sum_{\ell}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[n_{L-1}E_{iL-1}^{(\ell)}-\frac{\ell}{c(\ell+1)}\,\varepsilon_{iab}n_{aL-1}B_{bL-1}^{(\ell)}\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(28b)

where the moments are evaluated at
u
.
We recall that the gauge moments
\{\mathrm{K}_{L}\}
represent a
U(1)
symmetry and, as such, play no physical role.
If the linear solution is perfectly regular at infinity, this will not be the case of the non-linear interactions, that will bear (powers of) logarithms.
Indeed, the most generic structure of a MPM iterated solution at future null infinity is of the type
Blanchet 2014

A_{\mu}\sim\sum_{\ell,p}\frac{\ln^{p}r}{r}\,n_{L}\,\alpha_{\ell,p}(u)+o\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(29)

where
p\geq 0
and the “small remainder” translates the polylogarithmic behavior at lower orders.
In order to properly define radiative moments, we need to eliminate those logarithms.

II.4.3
Radiative moments

In pure GR, the study of the asymptotic structure of the space-time has revealed that this polylogarithmic behavior is an artifact of the harmonic coordinates, and there exist “radiative” coordinate systems in which the metric is free of it
Penrose 1963
;
Penrose 1965
.
The most famous ones are the Bondi coordinates system
Bondi et al. 1962
;
Sachs 1962
and the Newman-Unti one
Newman and Unti 1963
, but the class of radiative coordinates is quite large
Papapetrou 1969
;
Madore 1970a
;
Madore 1970b
.
Naturally, there exist generic constructions relating harmonic and Bondi-Newman-Unti coordinate systems exist
Blanchet 1987
;
Blanchet et al. 2021
;
Blanchet et al. 2023c
(see
Trestini et al. 2023
for a practical implementation at the state-of-the art 4PN precision).

Our claim here is that there exists a radiative coordinate system
(T,X^{i})
in which both the metric and the EM field are free of polylogarithmic structure.

H^{\mu\nu}\sim\sum_{\ell}\frac{N_{L}}{R}\,H_{L}(U)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\sim\sum_{\ell}\frac{N_{L}}{R}\,A_{L}(U)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right)\,,

(30)

with naturally
U=T-R/c
.
Such claim is corroborated by the analysis of the Sec.
III
, were we show that, under the coordinate change (
53
) both the metric and the EM field takes the form (
30
).

In such coordinate system, one can project the metric
H^{\mu\nu}
in a traceless and transverse (TT) gauge
Thorne 1980

H_{0\mu}^{\text{TT}}=N_{i}H_{ij}^{\text{TT}}=H_{ii}^{\text{TT}}=0
as

H_{ij}^{\text{TT}}\equiv-\frac{4G}{c^{2}\,R}\,\mathcal{P}^{\text{TT}}_{ijab}\sum_{\ell}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[N_{L-2}\mathrm{U}_{abL-2}-\frac{2\ell}{c(\ell+1)}\,N_{cL-2}\varepsilon_{cd(a}\mathrm{V}_{b)dL-2}\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right)\,,

(31)

where we introduced the TT projector
\mathcal{P}^{\text{TT}}_{ijab}=\perp_{i(a}\perp_{b)j}-\frac{1}{2}\perp_{ij}\perp_{ab}
with
\perp_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-N_{ij}
.
The two sets of moments
\{\mathrm{U}_{L},\mathrm{V}_{L}\}
entering the asymptotic metric are naturally dubbed mass and current
radiative
moments.

As for the EM field, let us project it on a transverse basis, defined by
A_{0}^{\text{T}}=N_{i}A_{i}^{\text{T}}=0
, as

A_{i}^{\text{T}}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}\,R}\perp_{ij}\sum_{\ell}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[N_{L-1}\mathrm{Q}_{jL-1}-\frac{\ell}{c(\ell+1)}\,\varepsilon_{jab}N_{aL-1}\mathrm{H}_{bL-1}\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right)\,,

(32)

where similarly, we have introduced two sets of
radiative
electric and magnetic moments,
\{\mathrm{Q}_{L},\mathrm{H}_{L}\}
.
Looking back at Eq. (
28
), it comes as expected

\mathrm{Q}_{L}=\mathrm{E}_{L}^{(\ell)}+\mathcal{O}(G)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{H}_{L}=\mathrm{B}_{L}^{(\ell)}+\mathcal{O}(G)\,,

(33)

where the remainder encrypts all non-linearities, and notably the tails computed in Sec.
III
.

II.5
Computation of the modes and fluxes

II.5.1
Gravitational sector

The detailed derivation of the energy and angular momentum fluxes associated with the asymptotic metric (
31
) can be found in
Thorne 1980
, and they read

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{G}{c^{2\ell+1}}\frac{(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{(\ell-1)\ell\,\ell!(2\ell+1)!!}\bigg[\mathrm{U}_{L}^{(1)}\mathrm{U}_{L}^{(1)}+\frac{4\ell^{2}}{c^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}\,\mathrm{V}_{L}^{(1)}\mathrm{V}_{L}^{(1)}\bigg]\,,

(34a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{i}_{\text{GW}}=\varepsilon_{iab}\,\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{G}{c^{2\ell+1}}\frac{(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{(\ell-1)\,\ell!(2\ell+1)!!}\bigg[\mathrm{U}_{aL-1}\mathrm{U}_{bL-1}^{(1)}+\frac{4\ell^{2}}{c^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}\,\mathrm{V}_{aL-1}\mathrm{V}_{bL-1}^{(1)}\bigg]\,.

(34b)

From the asymptotic metric (
31
), one can also extract the observable gravitational modes,
h_{\ell\mathrm{m}}
, by projecting
H_{ij}^{\text{TT}}
onto the basis of polarization
\{+,\times\}
and on spin-weighted spherical harmonics,
Y_{-2}^{\ell\mathrm{m}}
(following the conventions of
Blanchet et al. 2008
;
Faye et al. 2012
) as

h_{+}-\mathrm{i}h_{\times}=\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\sum_{\mathrm{m}=-\ell}^{\ell}h_{\ell\mathrm{m}}\,Y_{-2}^{\ell\mathrm{m}}\,.

(35)

In the particular case of planar orbits, as will be considered in Sec
IV
, the gravitational modes are linked to the radiative moments by the relations
Faye et al. 2012

\displaystyle h_{\ell\mathrm{m}}=-\frac{2G}{Rc^{\ell+2}\,\ell!}\sqrt{\frac{(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{\ell(\ell-1)}}\,\alpha_{L}^{\ell\mathrm{m}}\,\mathrm{U}_{L}\,,

\displaystyle\text{if}\ \ell+\mathrm{m}\ \text{is even}\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle h_{\ell\mathrm{m}}=-\frac{4G\,\mathrm{i}}{Rc^{\ell+3}\,\ell!}\sqrt{\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{(\ell-1)(\ell+1)}}\,\alpha_{L}^{\ell\mathrm{m}}\,\mathrm{V}_{L}\,,

\displaystyle\text{if}\ \ell+\mathrm{m}\ \text{is odd}\,.

(36b)

Introducing a fixed orthonormal basis
(\bm{n}_{0},\bm{\lambda}_{0},\bm{l})
where
\bm{l}
is normal to the orbital plane, together with
\bm{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}=(\bm{n}_{0}+\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}_{0})/\sqrt{2}
, the projector
\alpha_{L}^{\ell\mathrm{m}}
is explicitly given by

\alpha_{L}^{\ell\mathrm{m}}=\frac{(-)^{\mathrm{m}}\,2^{\frac{2+|\mathrm{m}|}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}\,\ell!}{\sqrt{(2\ell+1)(\ell+\mathrm{m})!\,(\ell-\mathrm{m})!}}\,\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}^{\langle M}l^{L-M\rangle}\,,

(37)

where the overbar denotes complex conjugation.

II.5.2
Electromagnetic sector

As discussed in App.
A.1
, the energy loss can be expressed by the angular average flux of the Landau-Lifshitz pseudotensor
\tau^{\mu\nu}
. At leading order in
1/R
, the EM sector of this tensor reduces to the usual Poynting vector
\Pi_{i}=c\,F^{\text{T}}_{ij}F^{\text{T}}_{j0}
. The EM energy flux then reads

\mathcal{F}_{\text{EM}}=\frac{c^{4}}{4\pi G\,\alpha_{\text{EM}}}\lim_{R\to\infty}\left[\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\Omega\,R^{2}\,N_{a}\Pi_{a}\right]=\frac{c^{3}}{4\pi G\,\alpha_{\text{EM}}}\lim_{R\to\infty}\left[\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\Omega\,R^{2}\,\dot{A}_{i}^{\text{T}}\dot{A}_{i}^{\text{T}}\right]\,,

(38)

where we used the relation
\partial_{j}A^{\text{T}}_{i}=-N_{j}\dot{A}^{\text{T}}_{i}/c+\mathcal{O}(1/R)
together with the transversity.
Plugging the asymptotic expression for the EM field (
32
), and performing the angular integrals with the help of the formula (
83
), it comes

\mathcal{F}_{\text{EM}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3+2\ell}}\frac{\ell+1}{\ell\,\ell!(2\ell+1)!!}\bigg[\mathrm{Q}_{L}^{(1)}\mathrm{Q}_{L}^{(1)}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{c^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}\,\mathrm{H}_{L}^{(1)}\mathrm{H}_{L}^{(1)}\bigg]\,.

(39)

This result is consistent with the computation of
Ross 2012
, and the canonical Larmor formula
Jackson 1999
is recovered in the case of a simple electric dipole.
Moreover, to emphasize the gauge invariance of the flux, a derivation relaxing the transverse gauge condition is presented in App.
A.2
and fully agree with our result (
39
).

As for the angular momentum flux, it requires more care, just as in the gravitational case (see
e.g.

Bonga and Poisson 2019
;
Blanchet and Faye 2019
;
Ashtekar et al. 2020
;
Veneziano and Vilkovisky 2022
;
Manohar et al. 2022
;
Riva et al. 2023
for subtleties linked to the definition of an angular momentum flux in GR).
As discussed in App.
A.1
, it is also defined with respect to the EM sector of the Landau-Lifshitz pseudotensor (
5a
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\text{EM}}^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\,\lim_{R\to\infty}\left[\varepsilon_{iab}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\Omega\,R^{3}N_{ac}\,\Lambda_{\text{EM}}^{bc}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{4}}{4\pi G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}\,\lim_{R\to\infty}\left[\varepsilon_{iab}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\Omega\,R^{3}\,N_{ac}\,\bigg(F_{bd}F_{cd}-F_{0b}F_{0c}\bigg)\right]\,.

But, as clear from the
R^{3}
factor, its evaluation requires the knowledge of the sub-dominant
\mathcal{O}(1/R^{2})
order in the asymptotic expansion of the field (
32
).
This is quite similar to what is happening in pure GR, see
e.g.

Thorne 1980
, and calls for a full BMS-type analysis of the asymptotic structure of the fields.
Instead of performing such tedious computation, we follow the spirit of
Blanchet and Faye 2019
and provide in App.
A.3
a derivation leading to the result

\mathcal{G}^{i}_{\text{EM}}=\varepsilon_{iab}\,\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3+2\ell}}\frac{\ell+1}{\ell!(2\ell+1)!!}\bigg[\mathrm{Q}_{aL-1}\mathrm{Q}_{bL-1}^{(1)}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{c^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}\,\mathrm{H}_{aL-1}\mathrm{H}_{bL-1}^{(1)}\bigg]\,.

(41)

Note that, in the case of a rotating electric dipole, we recover the results of
Epp et al. 2022
;
Landau and Lifschits 1975
.

Together with the gravitational fluxes (
34
), those fluxes enter the balance equations for the energy and angular momentum (
1
), that allow to derive the secular evolution of orbital quantities, see Sec.
IV.3
.
In the case of quasi-circular orbits, they are key ingredient to derive the gravitational phase
\phi=\int\!\!\mathrm{d}t\,\omega
(where
\omega
is the gravitational frequency), as presented in Sec.
IV.4
.
Interestingly, the two fluxes (
39
) and (
41
) start formally at the same order than the gravitational ones, namely 2.5PN.
This is a strong difference with the results of the conservative sector, where the EM effects enter formally as 1PN corrections to the Noetherian quantities
Henry et al. 2023
.

III
Computation of the leading tail effects

The leading corrections to the fluxes derived in the previous section are the so-called
tail
interactions.
Those interactions depict the scattering of a propagating moment (
\mathrm{I}_{L}
,
\mathrm{J}_{L}
,
\mathrm{E}_{L}
or
\mathrm{B}_{L}
) onto the static structure of the space-time, described by the constant (ADM) mass
\mathrm{M}
.
The gravitational tails
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{I}_{L}
and
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{J}_{L}
are known since a long time
Blanchet and Damour 1989
;
Blanchet and Damour 1992
;
Blanchet and Schaefer 1993
;
Blanchet 1995
(see also
Poisson 1993
for a derivation using Schwarzschild coordinates, and
Trestini et al. 2023
for a computation in a radiative coordinate system), and we will not redo it here.
Instead we will focus on the leading EM tails, namely the interaction of the EM
\ell=1
moments
\mathrm{E}_{i}
and
\mathrm{B}_{i}
with the ADM mass
\mathrm{M}
, and will explicitly apply the MPM iteration scheme pictured in Sec.
II.4.1
to those interactions.
4
4

4

Note that the interaction between the constant charge
\mathrm{Q}
and the gravitational dipole
\mathrm{I}_{i}
formally enters at the same order than the tail
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{E}_{i}
. Nevertheless, we work in the center-of-mass frame, in which
\mathrm{I}_{i}=0
, so we will discard this interaction.

III.1
The electro-magnetic tail effect

The MPM iteration method
Blanchet and Damour 1992
is a two-steps procedure, described in details
e.g.
in Sec. 2 of
Blanchet 2014
.
First, one computes a
particular
solution of the sourced wave equation, by applying the Hadamard-regularized propagator.
But, due to the regularization procedure, this particular solution may not obey the gauge condition, even if the source does
5
5

5

Note that a similar departure from gauge can also happens in the EFT framework, where the computation has to be corrected by a removal of
anomalies

Almeida et al. 2023
.
and one needs to correct for it.
Therefore, the second step is to add a proper
homogeneous
solution that corrects for the violation of the gauge condition.
The sum of those two solutions is the correct value of the field, from which the radiative moments can be extracted (after the suitable coordinate change, as explained in Sec.
II.4.3
).

III.1.1
Computation of the particular solution

In what follows, we aim to compute the quadratic interactions
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{E}_{i}
and
\mathrm{M}\times\mathrm{B}_{i}
, by solving the PM-expanded vacuum equation (
9b
).
The first step is naturally to construct the non-linear source
\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{\mu})
, by injecting the MPM expansions of the fields into the expression of
\Phi_{\mu}
.
Selecting only the
\mathrm{M}
sector of the linear metric (
12
) and the appropriate sectors of the linear EM field (
21
), we need to consider

\displaystyle h^{00}_{\mathrm{M}}=-\frac{4G\mathrm{M}}{c^{2}\,r}\,,

\displaystyle h^{0i}_{\mathrm{M}}=0\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad h^{ij}_{\mathrm{M}}=0\,.

(42a)

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\mathrm{E}_{i}}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}}\,\partial_{i}\tilde{\mathrm{E}}_{i}\,,

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\mathrm{E}_{i}}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}}\,\dot{\tilde{\mathrm{E}}}_{i}\,,

(42b)

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\mathrm{B}_{i}}=0\,,

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\mathrm{B}_{i}}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{2c^{4}}\partial_{j}\tilde{\mathrm{B}}_{i|j}\,,

(42c)

where we recall that we employ the shortcut (
13
), and have used the notation of

IV.5
Numerical applications

One of the motivations for this work is to estimate the significance of the magnetic contribution to the dissipative sector.
For instance, it has been shown in
Savalle et al. 2023
that EM effects on eccentric orbits were not negligible for LISA data analysis, when accumulating a sufficient observation time.
Indeed, eccentricity affects each harmonics differently, breaking the degeneracy and making the presence of magnetic dipoles possibly detectable.
However, the analysis done in
Savalle et al. 2023
relies solely on the leading order of the Peters & Mathews formulas for point-particles
Peters and Mathews 1963
;
Peters 1964
, neglecting EM effects in the dissipative sector.
This approach is not formally consistent in a PN sense.
In the present work, we have generalized the Peters & Mathews formulas by including the magnetic dipoles to NLO, see Eqs. (
104

Savalle et al. 2023

We can estimate the order of magnitude of the newly computed EM terms for realistic systems, by comparing them to the pure point-particle contribution.
To this end, we choose an astrophysically motivated model, that relates the magnitude of the magnetic dipole of a star to its magnetic field at its surface and its radius as
Pablo et al. 2019

But, if our formulas (

This is due to the fact that, at the periastron of a highly eccentric orbit, the weak-field condition is naturally invalid.
Thus, the enhancement of magnetic effects for high eccentricities is spurious.
Furthermore, when the two stars are close enough, depicting the magnetic interaction with dipoles is no more possible, as other effects (such mass exchange or common magnetic envelop) enter the picture.

Finally, let us comment on the application to binaries of neutron stars.

\sim 10^{3}

Thus, in our setup of constant and aligned magnetic dipoles, the EM contributions to the decay of orbital parameters for binaries of neutron stars systems are expected to be negligible.

V
Summary and conclusion

Including electromagnetic effects in the templates used by future gravitational-wave detectors, such as LISA or ET, will be important, notably for an accurate characterization of white dwarfs binaries
Bourgoin et al. 2021
;
Bourgoin et al. 2022a
;
Bourgoin et al. 2022b
;
Bourgoin et al. 2022c
;
Carvalho et al. 2022
;
Savalle et al. 2023
;
Aykroyd et al. 2023
.
In this spirit, a proper post-Newtonian treatment of the motion of two stars with EM dipoles has been done in
Henry et al. 2023
, and the usual Noetherian quantities (energy, angular momentum,
etc.
) have been derived.
Pushing further in this direction, the present work tackles the problem of the generation of gravitational-wave by such binaries.

Relying on matched multipolar-post-Minkowskian and post-Newtonian expansions, we first concentrate on the near-zone behavior of the fields, and derive
source
and
gauge
moments (
25
), that entirely describe the physics of the matter distribution.
From those moments, we iteratively construct non-linear
radiative
moments that parametrize the values of the fields at future null infinity, (
31
) and (
32
).
This asymptotic parametrization allows us to extract the fluxes of energy and angular momentum (
34
), (
39
) and (
41
), as well as the gravitational modes, parametrizing the waveform as detected by an observer at infinity.
As a proof of concept, we apply the iteration scheme to explicitly derive the EM
tail
,
i.e.
the leading order non-linear correction, of the two lowest-order EM radiative moments (
55
).
We emphasize that this formalism is valid for any type of post-Newtonian, compact-supported source (
i.e.
enjoying weak field and small velocities).
It can thus be applied to other astrophysical systems containing EM effects within the PN regime, for instance non-spherical rotating neutron stars, supernovae or even systems containing non-ultrarelativistic accretion disks.

Next, we apply this formalism to the concrete case of a binary composed of stars bearing magnetic dipoles.
Under the assumption of constant and aligned magnetic dipoles, we derive the fluxes and modes for generic orbits.
Enforcing eccentric orbits, we derive the evolution of orbital parameters (eccentricity, semi-major axis and frequency) (
104
), (
106
) and (
108
), and provide the EM corrections to the gravitational modes to the next-to-leading order.
For the specific case of quasi-circular orbits, we derive the correction to the gravitational phase.
All those results are collected in the ancillary file
Anc
.

Specifying realistic astrophysical configurations, we perform numerical estimates that show the magnitude of the magnetic contribution to the radiated gravitational waves, and dissipative dynamics.
Notably, we find that the ratio of the magnetic effects
vs.
the pure point-particle terms in the secular evolution of the orbital elements are at most
\sim 10^{-6}
. This implies that the model used in the study
Savalle et al. 2023
would yield the qualitatively similar conclusions if it were to be consistent in a post-Newtonian sense.
However, and similarly to
Henry et al. 2023
, we find that the leading magnetic corrections are smaller than a 1PN point-particle effect, but bigger than a 2PN one, making the magnetic effects roughly comparable to a 1.5PN quantity.

If the present work formally tackles the problem of post-Newtonian gravitational wave generation in an Einstein-Maxwell framework, the efforts in building realistic waveform models including EM effects can naturally be improved.
In fact, our model assumes constant and aligned magnetic dipoles.
If such configuration is indeed an equilibrium state
Aykroyd et al. 2023
, it would be quite interesting to apply our formalism to systems with non-constant and/or non-aligned dipoles.
In such configurations, we could have quantitative changes, as the leading order in the EM fluxes (
39
) and (
41
) is vanishing due to the constant dipole hypothesis.
This requires to fix the dynamics of the dipoles, which could be done for instance by coupling them to the spins of the stars. This is left for future work.

where we have used the fact that the moments
\{\mathrm{Q}_{L},\mathrm{H}_{L}\}
are STF and the integration formula

(83)

A.3
Angular momentum flux

Let us now turn to the more subtle derivation of the angular momentum flux.
As clear from the
R^{3}
appearing in its expression (
77b
), and as discussed in lengths in
e.g.

Thorne 1980
;
Bonga and Poisson 2019
;
Blanchet and Faye 2019
, its computation requires the knowledge of the sub-leading terms
\mathcal{O}(1/R^{2})
of the radiative field (
54
).
The canonical way of proceeding involves a full analysis of the asymptotic structure at future null infinity, and is plagued by subtleties, see
e.g.

Bonga and Poisson 2019
.
Instead, we adopt a more pragmatic approach, following the spirit of
Blanchet and Faye 2019
.
We start by computing the “source” flux,
i.e.
the dominant PM order of the flux, by using the linearized metric (
21
).
This partial flux will be written in terms of (derivatives) of the source moments
\mathrm{E}_{L}
and
\mathrm{B}_{L}
, hence the name.
In order to reconstruct the “full” flux from this source flux, one should in principle add all higher PM orders
via
tedious computations.
Nevertheless, using the argument of
Blanchet and Faye 2019
, we claim that this source flux has the same structure that the “radiative” flux, under the naive replacements
\big(\mathrm{E}_{L}^{(\ell)},\mathrm{B}_{L}^{(\ell)})\to(\mathrm{Q}_{L},\mathrm{H}_{L})
, see Eq. (
33
).
This method, although not a formal proof, has the advantage of the simplicity: the linear metric (
21
) is easily expanded at sub-leading order, using

\hat{\partial}_{L}\bigg[\frac{F(t-r/c)}{r}\bigg]=\frac{(-)^{\ell}\hat{n}_{L}}{r\,c^{\ell}}\bigg[F^{(\ell)}(t-r/c)+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}\,\frac{c}{r}\,F^{(\ell-1)}(t-r/c)\bigg]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right)\,.

(84)

Injecting it into the field strength, it comes

\displaystyle F_{0i}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}\,r}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[\perp_{ij}n_{L-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell+1)}_{jL-1}-\frac{\ell}{c(\ell+1)}\,\varepsilon_{iab}n_{aL-1}\mathrm{B}^{(\ell+1)}_{bL-1}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}\,r^{2}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}\,n_{L-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{iL-1}-\frac{(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{2}\,n_{iL}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{L}-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2c}\,\varepsilon_{iab}n_{aL-1}\mathrm{B}^{(\ell)}_{bL-1}\bigg]\,,

(85a)

\displaystyle F_{ij}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{4}\,r}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[n_{jL-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell+1)}_{iL-1}-n_{iL-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell+1)}_{jL-1}-\frac{\ell\,n_{aL-1}}{c(\ell+1)}\,\big(\varepsilon_{iab}n_{j}-\varepsilon_{jab}n_{i}\big)\mathrm{B}^{(\ell+1)}_{bL-1}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3}\,r^{2}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{c^{\ell}\,\ell!}\bigg[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}\bigg(n_{jL-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{iL-1}-n_{iL-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{jL-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\bigg(\frac{2\ell}{(\ell+1)c}\,\varepsilon_{ija}+\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{2c}(\varepsilon_{iab}n_{jb}-\varepsilon_{jab}n_{ib})\bigg)n_{L-1}\mathrm{B}^{(\ell)}_{aL-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{(\ell+1)c}\bigg(\varepsilon_{iab}\mathrm{B}^{(\ell)}_{jbL-2}-\varepsilon_{jab}\mathrm{B}^{(\ell)}_{ibL-2}\bigg)n_{aL-2}\bigg]\,.

(85b)

The flux (
77b
) thus becomes

\mathcal{G}_{\text{EM}}^{i}=\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{4\pi c^{3}}\sum_{\ell,k}\frac{\ell+1}{c^{\ell+k}\,\ell!\,k!}\varepsilon_{iab}\Bigg[\mathrm{E}_{L}^{(\ell)}\mathrm{E}_{bK-1}^{(k+1)}+\frac{k\,\ell}{c^{2}(k+1)(\ell+1)}\mathrm{B}_{L}^{(\ell)}\mathrm{B}_{bK-1}^{(k+1)}\Bigg]\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\Omega\,n_{aK-1}n_{L}\,.

(86)

Using the integration formula

\varepsilon_{iab}\,\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{L}\mathrm{E}^{(k+1)}_{bK-1}\,\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\Omega\,n_{aK-1}n_{L}=\frac{4\pi\,\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\,\delta_{k\ell}\,\varepsilon_{iab}\,\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{aL-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell+1)}_{bL-1}\,,

(87)

it comes

\mathcal{G}_{\text{EM}}^{i}=\varepsilon_{iab}\,\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{G\alpha_{\text{EM}}}{c^{3+2\ell}}\frac{\ell+1}{\ell!\,(2\ell+1)!!}\Bigg[\mathrm{E}^{(\ell)}_{aL-1}\mathrm{E}^{(\ell+1)}_{bL-1}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{c^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}\,\mathrm{B}^{(\ell)}_{aL-1}\mathrm{B}^{(\ell+1)}_{bL-1}\bigg]\,.

(88)

Under the replacement
\big(\mathrm{E}_{L}^{(\ell)},\mathrm{B}_{L}^{(\ell)})\to(\mathrm{Q}_{L},\mathrm{H}_{L})
, this result gives Eqs. (
41
), that we have used in this work.
Note however that when applying our formalism to a concrete case in Sec.
IV
, we have only pushed the accuracy at NLO and the non-linearities did not contribute.
In this precise case, the “source” and “full” fluxes are naturally identical.

As discussed in Sec.
IV.4
, the balance equations (
1
) are degenerate in the case of quasi-circular orbits.
Thus, the phase (
71
) can also be obtained in terms of the balance equation for the angular momentum,
i.e.

\phi=-\frac{c^{3}}{Gm}\int\!\!\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{x^{3/2}}{\mathcal{G}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}J}{\mathrm{d}x}\,,

(89)

where
\mathcal{G}=|\mathcal{G}^{i}|
and
J=|J^{i}|
.
Implementing this formula, we recover exactly the previous result (
72
).
This derivation can be seen as a check regarding the definition of the radiated EM angular momentum.
Indeed, the general definition of the GW angular momentum (
34b
) in terms of the radiative moments was, of course, already known and its explicit value contains also magnetic contributions which combine to those of the EM flux to give the expected result.
The fact that both derivations agree thus strongly suggests that the adopted definition is correct.
As a sidenote, it is also remarkable that the link between the coefficients of
\mathcal{F}_{\text{EM}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\text{EM}}
is the same as the proportionality coefficients of those between
\mathcal{F}_{\text{GW}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\text{GW}}
.

On generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials Thanks: *corresponding authors
Taekyun Kim*, DAE SAN KIM*, Hye Kyung Kim*

Abstract
. We introduce the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials as a degenerate version of the Euler-Genocchi polynomials. In addition, we introduce their higher-order version, namely the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order α \alpha , as a degenerate version of the generalized Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order α \alpha . The aim of this paper is to study certain properties and identities involving those polynomials, the generalized falling factorials, the degenerate Euler polynomials of order α \alpha , the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, and the ‘alternating degenerate power sum of integers’.

1.
Introduction

Explorations for various degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials have drawn the attention of many mathematicians in recent years, which were initiated by Carlitz’s work in [4,5]. Many interesting results were obtained by exploiting different tools such as generating functions, combinatorial methods,
p
-adic analysis, umbral calculus, differential equations, probability theory, operator theory, analytic number theory and quantum physics (see [8-10,12-14,16-21]).

Belbachir et al. introduced the Euler-Genocchi polynomials in [2,3] and Goubi generalized them to the generalized Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
in [7]. Here we introduce degenerate versions for both of them. Namely, we introduce the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials as a degenerate version of the Euler-Genocchi polynomials. In addition, we introduce their higher-order version, namely the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
, as a degenerate version of the generalized Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
. The aim of this paper is to study certain properties and identities involving those polynomials, the generalized falling factorials, the degenerate Euler polynomials of order
\alpha
, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, and the ‘alternating degenerate power sum of integers’ (see (
30
)). The novelty of this paper is that it is the first paper that introduces the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials and the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
, as degenerate versions of the polynomials introduced earlier in [2,3,7].

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section1, we recall the degenerate exponentials, the degenerate Euler polynomials, the degenerate Euler polynomials of order
\alpha
, and the degenerate Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
. Also, we remind the reader of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second and the incomplete Bell polynomials. Section 2 is the main result of this paper. We introduce the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
, as a generalization of both the degenerate Euler polynomials and the degenerate Genocchi polynomials. In Theorem 1, the generalized falling factorials
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are expressed in terms of
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
. A distribution property is derived for
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
in Theorem 2. Then the generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
,
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)
, are introduced as a higher-order version of
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
. We deduce a simple expression for
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,-m)}(x)
, with
m
a positive integer in Theorem 3. In Theorems 4 and 5, we observe certain relations between
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)
and the degenerate Euler polynomials of order
\alpha
,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)
. In Theorem 6,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)
are expressed in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
. In Theorem 7,
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are represented in terms of
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)
and
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
. In Theorem 8, we obtain an identity involving
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)
,
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
, and
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha-1)}(x)
. Let
T_{k,\lambda}(n)
denote the ‘alternating degenerate power sum of integers.’ In Theorem 9, we get a representation of
T_{k,\lambda}(n)
in terms of
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
. Finally, we get an expression of
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)
in terms of
T_{k,\lambda}(n)
and
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
in Theorem 10. In the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

It is well known that the Euler polynomials are defined by

(1)

\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-22]).

The Genocchi polynomials are given by

(2)

\frac{2t}{e^{t}+1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-22]),

When
x=0
,
G_{n}=G_{n}(0)
are called the Genocchi numbers.

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]),

where the generalized falling factorials are given by

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1).

In [4,5], Carlitz introduced the degenerate Euler polynomials which are given by

\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the degenerate Euler numbers.

For any nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, the degenerate Euler polynomials of order
\alpha
are defined by

\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5]).

When
x=0
,
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(0)
are called the degenerate Euler numbers of order
\alpha
.

Recently, the degenerate Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
are defined by

\Big(\frac{2t}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18-22]).

When
x=0
,
G_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=G_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(0)
are called the degenerate Genocchi numbers of order
\alpha
.

In particular,
\alpha=1
,
G_{n,\lambda}(x)=G_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x)
are called the degenerate Genocchi polynomials.

For
n\geq 0
, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are introduced by Kim-Kim as

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9]),

where

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1).

For
k\geq 0
, the incomplete Bell polynomials are defined by

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,14,15,17]),

where

B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n-k+1}=k\\
l_{1}+2l_{2}+\cdots+(n-k+1)l_{n-k+1}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{n-k+1}!}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\bigg)^{l_{1}}\cdots\bigg(\frac{x_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\bigg)^{l_{n-k+1}}.

2.
Generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi numbers and polynomials

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
r\geq 0
, we consider the
generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials
given by

(3)

\frac{2t^{r}}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that
A_{0,\lambda}^{(r)}=A_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=\cdots=A_{r-1,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=0
.

When
x=0
,
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}=A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(0)
are called the
generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi numbers
. Observe that

(4)

A_{n,\lambda}^{(0)}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x),\quad A_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x)=G_{n,\lambda}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
3
), we have

(5)

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x+1)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}(1)_{n-l,\lambda}A_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

By (
3
), we get

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{1}{2t^{r}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}A_{l,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)+1\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}A_{l+r,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{(l+r)!}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{m,\lambda}}{m!}t^{m}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n+r}{l+r}\frac{n!}{(n+r)!}(1)_{n-l,\lambda}A_{l+r,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n+r,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{n!}{(n+r)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n+r}{l+r}(1)_{n-l,\lambda}\frac{A_{l+r}^{(r)}(x)}{(n+r)_{r}}+\frac{A_{n+r,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)}{(n+r)_{r}}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
6
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\frac{1}{2(n+r)_{r}}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n+r}{l+r}(1)_{n-l,\lambda}A_{l+r,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)+A_{n+r,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\bigg).

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{2t^{r}}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{2t^{r}}{e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)+1}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{l}e_{\lambda}^{l+x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(mt)^{r}}{(e_{\lambda/m}(mt)+1)}\frac{1}{m^{r}}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{l}e_{\lambda/m}^{\frac{l+x}{m}}(mt)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{r}}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{l}\frac{2(mt)^{r}}{e_{\lambda/m}(mt)+1}e_{\lambda/m}^{\frac{l+x}{m}}(mt)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{r}}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda/m}^{(r)}\Big(\frac{l+x}{m}\Big)m^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m^{n-r}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{l}A_{n,\lambda/m}^{(r)}\Big(\frac{l+x}{m}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
7
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
, we have

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=m^{n-r}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{l}A_{n,\lambda/m}^{(r)}\Big(\frac{l+x}{m}\Big).

For nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
r\geq 0
, we consider the
generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
which are given by

(8)

t^{r}\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}=A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(0)
are called the
generalized degenerate Euler-Genocchi numbers of order
\alpha
.

From (
8
), we note that

(9)

\displaystyle A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{k,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)_{n-k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{n-k,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Let
\alpha=-m\ (m\in\mathbb{N})
. Then, by (
8
), we get

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,-m)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{r}}{2^{m}}(e_{\lambda}(t)+1)^{m}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{t^{r}}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}e_{\lambda}^{k+x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(k+x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n+r}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(k+x)_{n-r,\lambda}(n)_{r}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
10
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,-m)}(x)=\frac{(n)_{r}}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(k+x)_{n-r,\lambda}.

When
x=0
, we have

(11)

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,-m)}=\frac{(n)_{r}}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(k)_{n-r,\lambda}.

From (
9
) and (
11
), we have

(12)

\displaystyle A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,-m)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{n-k,\lambda}^{(r,-m)}(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{j}(j)_{n-k-r,\lambda}(n-k)_{r}(x)_{k,\lambda}.

By (
8
), we get

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=t^{r}\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}(n)_{r}\mathcal{E}_{n-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Theorem 4
.

For
n,r\geq 0
with
n\geq r
, we have

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)=(n)_{r}\mathcal{E}_{n-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x).

In particular, for
x=0
, we get

(14)

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}=(n)_{r}\mathcal{E}_{n-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}.

By (
9
) and (
14
), we get

(15)

\displaystyle A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{n-k,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-r}\binom{n}{k}A_{n-k,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-r}\binom{n}{k}(n-k)_{r}\mathcal{E}_{n-k-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}=(n)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r}\binom{n-r}{k}\mathcal{E}_{n-k-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}

(16)

\displaystyle=(n)_{r}(x)_{n-r,\lambda}+(n)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r-1}\binom{n-r}{k}\mathcal{E}_{n-k-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
15
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For any nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
n,r\geq 0
with
n\geq r
, we have

A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)=(n)_{r}(x)_{n-r,\lambda}+(n)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r-1}\binom{n-r}{k}\mathcal{E}_{n-k-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}.

Let
f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
, where
a_{0}\neq 0
.

For any nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle f^{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\bigg)^{\alpha}=\bigg(a_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\bigg)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{\alpha}{k}a_{0}^{\alpha-k}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\alpha)_{k}a_{0}^{\alpha-k}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\bigg)^{k}.

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\alpha)_{k}a_{0}^{\alpha-k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{\alpha}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\alpha)_{k}a_{0}^{\alpha-k}B_{n,k}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From the definition of degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, we have

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(j)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(j)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
18
), we have

S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(j)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

Also, we have

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{i,\lambda}}{i!}t^{i}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\big((1)_{1,\lambda},(1)_{2,\lambda},\dots,(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
19
), we have

(20)

S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=B_{n,k}\big((1)_{1,\lambda},(1)_{2,\lambda},\dots,(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big).

We note that

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(e_{\lambda}(t)+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(1)_{l,\lambda}\frac{t^{l}}{l!},

and

(22)

\displaystyle B_{n,k}\Big(\frac{(1)_{1,\lambda}}{2},\frac{(1)_{2,\lambda}}{2},\dots,\frac{(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}}B_{n,k}\big((1)_{1,\lambda},\dots,(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k).

From the definition of degenerate higher-order Euler numbers, we note that

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{2}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)+1\big)\Big)^{-\alpha}=\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}(1)_{i,\lambda}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\Big)^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-\alpha)_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{i,\lambda}}{i!}t^{i}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-\alpha)_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\big((1)_{1,\lambda},(1)_{2,\lambda},\dots,(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-\alpha)_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{k}B_{n,k}\big((1)_{1,\lambda},(1)_{2,\lambda},\dots,(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-\alpha)_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{k}B_{n,k}\big((1)_{1,\lambda},(1)_{2,\lambda},\dots,(1)_{n-k+1,\lambda}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-\alpha)_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k).

From Theorem 5, we have

(24)

\displaystyle A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=(n)_{r}(x)_{n-r,\lambda}+(n)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r-1}\binom{n-r}{k}\mathcal{E}_{n-k-r,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(n)_{r}(x)_{n-r,\lambda}+(n)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r-1}\binom{n-r}{k}(x)_{k,\lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k-r}(-\alpha)_{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{j}S_{2,\lambda}(n-k-r,j)

\displaystyle=(n)_{r}(x)_{n-r,\lambda}+(n)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-r-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k-r}\binom{n-k}{k}(-\alpha)_{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{j}S_{2,\lambda}(n-k-r,j)(x)_{k,\lambda},

and

(25)

\displaystyle A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}=(n)_{r}\sum_{j=1}^{n-r}(-\alpha)_{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{j}S_{2,\lambda}(n-r,j),

where
n,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n>r\geq 0
.

Replacing
n
by
n+r
in (
24
), we get

(26)

A_{n+r,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)=(n+r)_{r}(x)_{n,\lambda}+(n+r)_{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}(-\alpha)_{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{j}\binom{n+r-k}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n-k,j)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 1).

Thus, we have

(27)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\frac{1}{(n+r)_{r}}A_{n+r,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}(-\alpha)_{j}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{j}\binom{n+r-k}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n-k,j)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 1).

Therefore, by (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\frac{1}{(n+r)_{r}}A_{n+r,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}(-\alpha)_{j}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{j}\binom{n+r-k}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n-k,j)(x)_{k,\lambda}.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
, we have

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}\Big(\frac{x+k}{m}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}^{(r,\alpha)}\Big(\frac{x+k}{m}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}t^{r}\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{\frac{x+k}{m}}(t)=\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}t^{r}e_{\lambda}^{\frac{x}{m}}(t)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{\frac{k}{m}}(t)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha}m^{r}\Big(\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{r}e_{m\lambda}^{x}\Big(\frac{t}{m}\Big)\frac{1+e_{\lambda}(t)}{1+e_{\lambda}^{1/m}(t)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\Big)^{\alpha-1}m^{r}\Big(\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{r}e_{m\lambda}^{x}\Big(\frac{t}{m}\Big)\frac{2}{1+e_{m\lambda}\big(\frac{t}{m}\big)}

Abstract
. This paper focuses on properties of ∂ \partial -biLipschitz mappings which were recently introduced by Bulter. We establish several characterizations for the class of ∂ \partial -biLipschitz mappings between domains in quasiconvex metric spaces. As an application, we show that a locally quasisymmetric equivalence between uniform metric spaces is quasimöbius, quantitatively.

1.
Introduction and main results

In order to explain the connections between the uniformizations of Gromov hyperbolic spaces established by Bonk et al.
[
1
]
and Bulter
[
5
]
(see also
[
19
]
), Bulter recently introduced the following class of
\partial
-biLipschitz mappings in
[
4
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
L\geq 1
and
0<\lambda<1
. A homeomorphism
f:\,\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
between incomplete, locally complete and connected metric spaces is called
\partial
-
Lipschitz
with data
(L,\lambda)
if for each
x\in\Omega
and for all
y,z\in B(x,\lambda d_{\Omega}(x))
,

\frac{d^{\prime}(f(y),f(z))}{d_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f(x))}\leq L\frac{d(y,z)}{d_{\Omega}(x)}.

Moreover,
f
is called
\partial
-
biLipschitz
with data
(L,\lambda)
if both
f
and its inverse
f^{-1}
are
\partial
-Lipschitz with data
(L,\lambda)
. Here
d_{\Omega}(x)
denotes the distance between
x
and the metric boundary
\partial\Omega
for all
x\in\Omega
, and

B(x,\lambda d_{\Omega}(x))=\{z\in X:\;d(z,x)<\lambda d_{\Omega}(x)\}.

The local completeness ensures that
d_{\Omega}(x)
does not vanish.

Several important conformal transformations are
\partial
-biLipschitz mappings (cf.
[
4
]
). Examples of such mappings include Riemann mappings from the unit disc onto simply connected plane domains, sphericalization and inversion operations in metric spaces setting suggested independently by Bonk and Kleiner
[
2
]
and Buckley et al.
[
3
]
, and conformal deformations introduced by Bonk et al.
[
1
]
and Bulter
[
5
]
. More generally, Bulter proved that a homeomorphism between uniform spaces is
\partial
-biLipschitz if and only if it is biLipschitz with respect to the quasihyperbolic metric, see
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
.

This work is a continuation of
[
4
]
. We investigate new characterizations of
\partial
-biLipschitz mappings between domains (open and connected nonempty subsets) in quasiconvex metric spaces. For more investigations concerning properties of quasiconformal mappings in this setting, one may refer
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
20
]
.

Obviously, the uniformity property of metric spaces implies the quasiconvexity. Note that a metric space
(X,d)
is called
quasiconvex
if there is a constant
c\geq 1
such that for each
x,y\in X
, there is a curve
\gamma
in
X
joining
x
and
y
whose length
\ell(\gamma)
satisfies the condition
\ell(\gamma)\leq cd(x,y).
It is worth mentioning that the class of quasiconvex spaces is very large which includes convex domains, domains
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with its length metric associated to the Euclidean metric, uniform spaces, Ahlfors’ regular spaces admitting Loewner conditions introduced by Heinonen and Koskela
[
7
]
, and more generally doubling metric measure spaces which satisfy Poincaré inequalities
[
12
]
.

First, we establish the following characterizations of
\partial
-Lipschitz mappings. The terminology appeared in Theorem
1.2
and in the rest of this section will be explained in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
are
c
-quasiconvex and complete metric spaces, that
G\subsetneq X
and
G^{\prime}\subsetneq X^{\prime}
are domains, and that
f:G\to G^{\prime}
is a homeomorphism. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

f
is
\partial
-Lipschitz with data
(L,\lambda)
;

(2)

f
is
(\theta,t_{0})
-relative with
\theta(t)=c_{1}t
;

(3)

f
is
\varphi
-semisolid with
\varphi(t)=c_{2}t
.

Note that the parameters depend only on each other and
c
.

Remark 1.3
.

The class of relative mappings between domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was introduced by Gehring in his study of quasiconformal mappings. Originally, solid mappings were investigated by Gehring and Osgood
[
6
]
in proving that quasiconformal mappings in Euclidean spaces are solid mappings and the converse is not true, see also
[
14
]
. In
[
15
]
, Väisälä established the theory of freely quasiconformal mappings in Banach spaces by using the quasihyperbolic metric and solid mappings. Recently, Huang et al.
[
10
]
studied the relationships between solid mappings and (weakly) quasisymmetric mappings in quasiconvex and complete metric spaces.

Using Theorem
1.2
, we obtain many characterizations for the class of
\partial
-biLipschitz mappings between domains in quasiconvex metric spaces. The following theorem is an improvement of
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
, where the domains are assumed to be uniform.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
are
c
-quasiconvex and complete metric spaces,
G\subsetneq X
and
G^{\prime}\subsetneq X^{\prime}
are domains, and that
f:G\to G^{\prime}
is a homeomorphism. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

f
is
\partial
-biLipschitz with data
(L,\lambda)
;

(2)

f
is
M
-biLipschitz with respect to the quasihyperbolic metrics;

(3)

f
and
f^{-1}
are
(\theta,t_{0})
-relative with
\theta(t)=c_{1}t
;

(4)

f
and
f^{-1}
are
(L_{1},\lambda_{1})
-locally biLipschitz;

(5)

f
and
f^{-1}
are
(\eta,q)
-locally quasisymmetric with
\eta(t)=c_{2}t
.

Note that the parameters depend only on each other and
c
.

Remark 1.5
.

The class of locally biLipschitz mappings mentioned in Theorem
1.4
(
4
) was introduced by Bonk et al.
[
1
]
, where they demonstrated that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Gromov hyperbolic space category with biLipschitz mappings as morphisms and uniform space category with quasisimilarity mappings as morphisms. For more investigations about these mappings, see
[
18
,
20
]
.

As mentioned before, Bulter used
\partial
-biLipschitz mappings to investigate the relationships between lots of conformal deformations on uniform metric spaces, such as the uniformization processes of Gromov hyperbolic spaces in
[
1
,
5
,
19
]
and the sphericalization and inversion deformations in
[
2
,
3
]
. In particular, he obtained the following:

Theorem A.

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
)

Suppose that
(\Omega,d)
and
(\Omega^{\prime},d^{\prime})
are locally compact and
A
-uniform metric spaces. If
f:\,\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is
\partial
-biLipschitz with data
(L,\lambda)
, then
f
is
\theta
-quasimöbius with
\theta
depending only on
A
,
\lambda
, and
L
.

The class of uniform Euclidean domains was independently introduced by Jones in
[
11
]
and by Martio and Sarvas in
[
13
]
. It is known that this notation plays an important role in geometric function theory and analysis in metric spaces, see
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
17
,
19
]
and the references therein. In
[
15
]
, Väisälä proved that a quasiconformal mapping between uniform Euclidean domains is quasimöbius. A well-known fact is that quasiconformal mappings and their inverses are both locally quasisymmetric.

It is natural to consider whether Theorem A holds or not for the class of locally quasisymmetric equivalence mappings. We say that a homeomorphism between two incomplete and locally complete metric spaces is locally quasisymmetric equivalence if the mapping and its inverse are both locally quasisymmetric. As an application of Theorem
1.4
, we provide an affirmative answer to this question by showing the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A\geq 1
,
0<q<1
and
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
a homeomorphism. Suppose that
(\Omega,d)
and
(\Omega^{\prime},d^{\prime})
are locally compact and
A
-uniform metric spaces. If a homeomorphism
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
and
f^{-1}
are both
(\eta,q)
-locally quasisymmetric, then
f
is
\theta
-quasimöbius, where
\theta
depends only on
A
,
q
, and
\eta
.

Remark 1.7
.

(a)
Theorem
1.6
is a generalization of Theorem A and the result of Väisälä in
[
15
]
. Indeed, one observes from Theorem
1.4
that
\partial
-biLipschitz mappings are
(\eta,q)
-locally quasisymmetric mappings with a special control function
\eta
.

(b)
Our method of proof of Theorem
1.6
is also different from that of
[
4
,
15
]
. In
[
15
]
, the tools of conformal modulus and
n
-Lebesgue measures are needed to show that uniform domains satisfy a quasiextremal distance condition in the sense of Gehring and Martio. In order to establish Theorem A, Bulter made use of the unbounded uniformizations of Gromov hyperbolic spaces associated to a class of conformal densities via Busemann functions, see
[
5
,
19
]
. In particular, he proved that the identity mapping between any two deformed uniform spaces is quasimöbius.

In this paper we do not use the results established in
[
5
]
, but apply a result of quasisymmetric mappings from local to global given in
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
and also the metric spaces sphericalization transformations introduced in
[
3
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall necessary definitions and preliminary results. The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are given in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

2.
Preliminaries and notations

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For a bounded set
E\subset X
, the diameter of
E
is denoted by
{\operatorname{diam}}(E)
. The open (resp. closed) metric ball with center
x\in X
and radius
r>0
is denoted by

B(x,r)=\{z\in X:\;d(z,x)<r\}\;\;(\mbox{resp.}\;\;\overline{B}(x,r)=\{z\in X:\;d(z,x)\leq r\}),

and the metric sphere by
S(x,r)=\{z\in X:\;d(z,x)=r\}.

A geodesic arc
\gamma
joining
x
and
y
in
X
is a continuous map
\gamma:\,I=[0,l]\to X
from an interval
I
to
X
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(l)=y
and
d(\gamma(t),\gamma(t^{\prime}))=|t-t^{\prime}|
for all
t,t^{\prime}\in I
. If
I=[0,\infty)
, then
\gamma
is called a geodesic ray. We say that
X
is geodesic if every pair of points can be joined by a geodesic arc. The space
X
is called rectifiably connected if each pair of points in
X
can be connected by a curve
\gamma
with
\ell(\gamma)<\infty
, where
\ell(\gamma)
is the length of
\gamma
.

2.1.
Quasihyperbolic metric and uniform space

Let
(\Omega,d)
be a rectifiably connected, locally complete and incomplete metric space, where the identity mapping
{\operatorname{id}}:(\Omega,d)\to(\Omega,\ell)
is a homeomorphism and
\ell
is the length metric of
\Omega
with respect to
d
. Here
\Omega
is incomplete if its metric boundary
\partial\Omega=\bar{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
is non-empty, where
\bar{\Omega}
designates the metric completion of
\Omega
.
Note that every domain
G\subsetneq X
in a quasiconvex complete space equipped with the metric
d
is such a space.

For all
x,y\in\Omega
, the number

k_{\Omega}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}\frac{ds}{d_{\Omega}(z)}

is called
the quasihyperbolic distance
between
x
and
y
in
\Omega
, where the infimum is taken over all rectifiable curves in
\Omega
joining
x
and
y
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A\geq 1
. The space
\Omega
is called
A
-
uniform
if for each
x,y\in\Omega
, there is a curve
\gamma
joining
x
and
y
such that

(1)

\ell(\gamma)\leq Ad(x,y)
,

(2)

\min\{\ell(\gamma[x,z]),\ell(\gamma[z,y])\}\leq Ad_{\Omega}(z)
,

where
\gamma[x,z]
is the subcurve of
\gamma
between
x
and
z
. The curve
\gamma
is called an
A
-uniform curve.

The following facts about the quasihyperbolic metric are needed for our purpose.

Lemma B.

(
[
10
, Lemma 3.8]
)

Let
(X,d)
be a
c
-quasiconvex and complete metric space and let
G\subsetneq X
be a domain.

(1)

For all
x,y\in G
,

d(x,y)\leq(e^{k_{G}(x,y)}-1)d_{G}(x);

(2)

Suppose that
x,y\in G
and either
d(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{3c}d_{G}(x)
or
k_{G}(x,y)\leq 1
. Then

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{G}(x)}\leq k_{G}(x,y)\leq 3c\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{G}(x)}.

Given a Euclidean domain
G
with
x\in G
, we know that the ball
B(x,r)
lies in
G
for
0<r<d_{G}(x)
. However, this result is not true in the setting of metric spaces, see
[
10
, Example 3.1]
. In
[
9
]
, Huang et al. established the following:

Lemma C.

(
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
)

Suppose that
X
is a
c
-quasiconvex and complete metric space and
G\subsetneq X
is a domain. Let
x\in G
and
0<r\leq\frac{2}{2+c}d_{G}(x)
. Then
B(x,r)\subset G
.

2.2.
Mappings

Definition 2.2
.

Let
L\geq 1
,
C\geq 0
and let
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a homeomorphism. A map
f:\,X\to X^{\prime}
between metric spaces is called

(1)

(L,C)
-
quasi-isometric
if for all
x
,
y\in X
,

L^{-1}d(x,y)-C\leq d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))\leq Ld(x,y)+C.

If
C=0
, then
f
is called
L
-
biLipschitz
.

(2)

\eta
-
quasisymmetric
if
f
is a homeomorphism and for every distinct points
x,a,b\in X
, we have

\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(a))}{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(b))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d(x,a)}{d(x,b)}\right).

(3)

\eta
-quasimöbius if
f
is a homeomorphism and for any distinct points
x,y,z,w\in X
, we have

\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(z))d^{\prime}(f(y),f(w))}{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))d^{\prime}(f(z),f(w))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d(x,z)d(y,w)}{d(x,y)d(z,w)}\right).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
L\geq 1
,
0<q<1
and let
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a homeomorphism. A homeomorphism
f:\,\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
between incomplete, locally complete and rectifiably connected metric spaces is called

(1)

(\eta,q)
-
relative
if for each
x\in\Omega
and for all
y\in B(x,qd_{\Omega}(x))
, we have

\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f(x))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{\Omega}(x)}\right).

(2)

\eta
-
semisolid
if for all
x,y\in\Omega
,

k_{\Omega^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq\eta(k_{\Omega}(x,y)).

(3)

(L,q)
-
locally biLipschitz
if for each
x\in\Omega
, there is a constant
C_{x}
(depending on the point
x
) such that for all
y,z\in B(x,qd_{\Omega}(x))
, we have

L^{-1}C_{x}d(y,z)\leq d^{\prime}(f(y),f(z))\leq LC_{x}d(y,z).

(4)

(\eta,q)
-
locally quasisymmetric
if for every
x\in\Omega
, the restriction
f|_{B(x,qd_{\Omega}(x))}
of
f
on
B(x,qd_{\Omega}(x))
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric.

Finally, we record an auxiliary result concerning the quasisymmetry from local to global between uniform spaces in a quantitative way.

Lemma D.

(
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
)

Let
A\geq 1
,
0<q<1
, and let
\eta:\,[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a homeomorphism. Suppose that
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
is a homeomorphism between bounded, locally compact, and
A
-uniform spaces. If
f
and
f^{-1}
are both
(\eta,q)
-locally quasisymmetric, then
f
is
\eta_{0}
-quasisymmetric if and only if there is a number
C_{0}\geq 1
and a point
w\in\Omega
such that

{\rm diam}(\Omega)\leq C_{0}d_{\Omega}(w)
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega^{\prime})\leq C_{0}d_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{\prime}(f(w)).
The parameters
\eta_{0}
and
C_{0}
depend on each other and also on
\eta
,
q
, and
A
.

3.
Proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.4

In this section, we assume that
(X,d)
and
(X^{\prime},d^{\prime})
are
c
-quasiconvex and complete metric spaces, that
G\subsetneq X
and
G^{\prime}\subsetneq X^{\prime}
are domains, and that
f:G\to G^{\prime}
is a homeomorphism.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We divide the proof of Theorem
1.2
into several lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
L\geq 1,

0<\lambda<1
, and
f
is
\partial
-Lipschitz with data
(L,\lambda).
Then
f
is
(\theta,t_{0})
-relative with
\theta(t)=Lt
and
t_{0}=\lambda
.

Proof.
Let
x\in G
and
y\in B(x,\lambda d_{G}(x))
. Since
f
is
\partial
-Lipschitz, we have

\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{G^{\prime}}(f(x))}\leq L\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{G}(x)},

which implies that
f
is
(\theta,t_{0})
-relative with
\theta(t)=Lt
and
t_{0}=\lambda
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
0<t_{0}\leq 1,

c_{1}\geq 1
, and
f
is
(\theta,t_{0})
-relative with
\theta(t)=c_{1}t.
Then
f
is
\varphi
-semisolid with
\varphi(t)=c_{2}t
, where
c_{2}
depends only on
c_{1}
,
t_{0}
, and
c
.

Proof.
Put

t_{1}=\min\left\{\log\left(\frac{t_{0}}{2}+1\right),\log\left(1+\frac{1}{3c_{1}c}\right)\right\}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,c_{2}=\frac{24cc_{1}}{t_{1}}.

We first establish the following:

Claim 3.1
.

If
x,y\in G
with
k_{G}(x,y)\leq t_{1}
, then
k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq 6cc_{1}k_{G}(x,y)
.

Fix
x,y\in G
with
k_{G}(x,y)\leq t_{1}
. It follows from Lemma B(
1
) that

d(x,y)\leq(e^{t_{1}}-1)d_{G}(x)\leq\min\left\{\frac{t_{0}}{2},\frac{1}{3cc_{1}}\right\}d_{G}(x).

Since
f
is
(\theta,t_{0})
-relative with
\theta(t)=c_{1}t
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{G^{\prime}}(f(x))}\leq\theta\Big(\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{G}(x)}\Big)=c_{1}\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{G}(x)}\leq\frac{1}{3c}.

Now we obtain from (
1
) and Lemma B(
2
) that

(2)

\displaystyle k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq 3c\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{G^{\prime}}(f(x))}\leq 1.

Again using Lemma B(
2
), we see from (
1
) and (
2
) that

k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq 3c\frac{d^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{G^{\prime}}(f(x))}\leq 3cc_{1}\frac{d(x,y)}{d_{G}(x)}\leq 6cc_{1}k_{G}(x,y),

and therefore, Claim
3.1
is proved.

Next, we show that

k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq c_{2}k_{G}(x,y)

holds for all
x,y\in G
.

Fix
x,y\in G
. If
k_{G}(x,y)\leq t_{1}
, then the required assertion follows from Claim
3.1
. So it suffices to consider the case that
k_{G}(x,y)>t_{1}
. By
[
10
, Lemma 3.9]
, the space
(G,k_{G})
is
2
-quasiconvex. Therefore, there exists a curve
\gamma
in
G
joining
x
and
y
such that

(3)

\displaystyle\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)\leq 2k_{G}(x,y),

where
\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)
denotes the length of
\gamma
in the quasihyperbolic metric.
Take a sequence of points
x=x_{0}
,
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,
\ldots
,
x_{n-1}
in
\gamma
satisfying

k_{G}(x_{i},x_{i-1})=t_{1}
and
k_{G}(x_{n-1},y)\leq t_{1},

for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
, and denote
x_{n}=y
. Then we know from Claim
3.1
that
n\geq 2
and
k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x_{i}),f(x_{i-1}))\leq 6cc_{1}t_{1}\leq 6cc_{1}

for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Thus, one finds that

(4)

\displaystyle k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x_{i}),f(x_{i-1}))\leq 6cc_{1}n\leq 12cc_{1}(n-1).

It follows from the inequality (
3
) that
(n-1)t_{1}\leq\ell_{k_{G}}(\gamma)\leq 2k_{G}(x,y),

which, together with (
4
), implies
k_{G^{\prime}}(f(x),f(y))\leq 24cc_{1}t_{1}^{-1}k_{G}(x,y).
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
f
is
\varphi
-semisolid with
\varphi(t)=c_{2}t
for some
c_{2}\geq 1
, then
f
is
\partial
-Lipschitz with data
(L,\lambda)
, where
L
and
\lambda
depend only on
c_{1}
and
c
.

Proof.
Let
\lambda=1/(36c^{2}c_{2})
and
L=24cc_{2}
. For all
x\in G
and
y,z\in B(x,\lambda d_{G}(x))
, we have by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle d(y,z)\leq\frac{1}{18c^{2}c_{2}}d_{G}(x),

and

(5)

\displaystyle d_{G}(y)\geq d_{G}(x)-d(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{2}d_{G}(x).

Therefore, we have

d(y,z)\leq d_{G}(y)/(9c^{2}c_{2})
. Using Lemma B(
2
), we obtain

(6)

4.1.
Gromov hyperbolic spaces

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
Fix a base point

. For

(x|y)_{w}=\frac{1}{2}\big(d(x,w)+d(y,w)-d(x,y)\big)

for all

[
1
,
4
,
19
]
. In particular, a direct computation gives the following elementary fact: For all

, we have

(18)

(x|y)_{o}+(z|u)_{o}-(x|z)_{o}-(y|u)_{o}=(x|y)_{w}+(z|u)_{w}-(x|z)_{w}-(y|u)_{w}.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

-hyperbolic space, and

. Following
[
1
]
, we say that

is

-roughly starlike with respect to a distinguished point

, there is a geodesic ray

emanating from

such that

.

Lemma E.

(
[
1
]
)

Let
(\Omega,d)
be a locally compact and

-uniform metric space and

its quasihyperbolic metric. Then there are constants

depending only on

so that

is a proper, geodesic
\delta
-hyperbolic metric space. If, in addition,

\displaystyle d_{w_{1},\varepsilon}(w_{1})\geq\frac{1}{2e}{\rm diam}(\Omega_{w_{1},\varepsilon}).

To prove that
f:(\Omega,d)\to(\Omega^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is
\theta
-quasimöbius, we need the following:

I:

The identity mapping
\varphi_{1}:(\Omega,d)\to(\Omega,d_{w_{0},\varepsilon})
is
\eta_{1}
-quasisymmetric.

II:

It follows from Lemma
4.2
that the identity mapping
\varphi_{2}:(\Omega,d_{w_{0},\varepsilon})\to(\Omega,d_{w_{1},\varepsilon})
is
\theta_{0}
-quasimöbius with
\theta_{0}
depending only on
A
.

III:

The induced mapping

g:=f\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1}:(\Omega,d_{w_{1},\varepsilon})\to(\Omega^{\prime},d^{\prime})

is
\eta_{2}
-quasisymmetric.

After that, we see that
f=g\circ\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}
is
\theta
-quasimöbius, because the composition of quasisymmetric mapping and quasimöbius mapping is also quasimöbius.

So in the remaining part, we only need to prove the assertions
I
and
III
.

For
I
, we check that the identity mapping
\varphi_{1}:(\Omega,d)\to(\Omega,d_{w_{0},\varepsilon})
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma D. By (
24
) and (
25
), it suffices to demonstrate that both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{1}^{-1}
are locally quasisymmetric. Note that this desired claim follows from
Lemma F and Theorem
1.4
. Therefore, by Lemma D, the assertion
I
is valid.

Finally, it remains to prove
III
. We shall use Lemma D to show that
g
is
\eta_{2}
-quasisymmetric. From (
24
) and (
26
), we only need to verify that both
g
and
g^{-1}
are locally quasisymmetric. Since
f
and
f^{-1}
are both
(\eta,q)
-locally quasisymmetric, we are left to demonstrate that the identity mapping

\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}:(\Omega,d)\to(\Omega,d_{w_{1},\varepsilon})

and its inverse are both locally quasisymmetric. This is because the composition of locally quasisymmetric mappings is also locally quasisymmetric, see
[
10
, Theorem 1.12]
.

This can be seen as follows. Because
\Omega^{\prime}
is a bounded
A
-uniform metric space, it follows from Lemma E that
(\Omega^{\prime},k^{\prime})
is
K^{\prime}
-roughly starlike with respect to
w_{1}^{\prime}
, where
K^{\prime}
depends only on
A
. Moreover, because the rough starlikeness is preserved under quasi-isometric mappings by
[
19
, Lemma 4.10]
, we see from Lemma
4.3
that
(\Omega,k)
is
K
-roughly starlike with respect to
w_{1}
, where
K
depends only on
A
,
q
, and
\eta
. This fact, combined with Lemma F(b), ensures that the identity mapping
\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}:(\Omega,d)\to(\Omega,d_{w_{1},\varepsilon})
is
M_{1}
-biLipschitz with respect to the quasihyperbolic metrics, where
M_{1}
depends only on
A
,
q
, and
\eta
. It follows from Theorem
1.4
that both
\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}
and its inverse are
(\eta_{1},q_{1})
-locally quasisymmetric, where
q_{1}
and
\eta_{1}
depend on
A
,
q
, and
\eta
.
The proof is complete.
∎

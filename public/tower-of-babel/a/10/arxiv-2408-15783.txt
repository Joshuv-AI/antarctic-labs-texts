BOUNDS FOR EIGENVALUE SUMS OF SCHRODINGER OPERATORS WITH COMPLEX RADIAL POTENTIALS
Jean-Claude Cuenin, Solomon Keedle-Isack

Abstract
. We consider eigenvalue sums of Schrödinger operators − Δ + V -\Delta+V on L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) with complex radial potentials V ∈ L q ​ ( ℝ d ) V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , q < d q<d . We prove quantitative bounds on the distribution of the eigenvalues in terms of the L q L^{q} norm of V V . A consequence of our bounds is that, if the eigenvalues ( z j ) (z_{j}) accumulate to a point in ( 0 , ∞ ) (0,\infty) , then ( Im ⁡ z j ) (\operatorname{Im}z_{j}) is summable. The key technical tools are resolvent estimates in Schatten spaces. We show that these resolvent estimates follow from spectral measure estimates by an epsilon removal argument.

1.
Introduction and main results

We consider Schrödinger operators
H=-\Delta+V
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with complex-valued potentials
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
q<\infty
.
The spectrum of
H
consists of
[0,\infty)
together with a discrete set of eigenvalues
z_{j}
.
We are interested in quantitative bounds on the
z_{j}
that depend only on an
L^{q}
norm of the potential.

1.1.
Bounds for single eigenvalues

Laptev and Safronov
[
22
]
conjectured that, in
d\geq 2
dimensions, any non-positive eigenvalue
z
of
H
satisfies the bound

\displaystyle|z|^{\gamma}\leq D_{\gamma,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)|^{\gamma+\frac{d}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

(1)

for
0<\gamma\leq d/2
, and with
D_{\gamma,d}
independent of
V
and
z
. Prior to this, Abramov-Aslanyan-Davies
[
1
]
had shown that (
1
) holds if
d=1
and
\gamma=1/2
. For
d\geq 2
, the Laptev–Safronov conjecture was proved for
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
by Frank
[
16
]
and disproved for
\gamma>1/2
by Bögli and the first author
[
4
]
. The conjecture is true for
radial
potentials in the range
0<\gamma<d/2
, as proved by Frank–Simon
[
20
]
, and it fails for
\gamma\geq d/2
by a counterexample of Bögli
[
3
]
. There are some further refinements and generalisations of (
1
) (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
and references therein), and there is by now a more or less complete picture of bounds of the type (
1
) for a single eigenvalue
z
.

1.2.
Bounds for eigenvalue sums

The situation for eigenvalue sums is considerably less well understood. The starting point is the celebrated Lieb–Thirring inequality for
real-valued potentials
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|z_{j}|^{\gamma}\leq L_{\gamma,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{\gamma+d/2}\mathrm{d}x,

(2)

which holds for
\gamma\geq 1/2
if
d=1
and
\gamma>0
if
d\geq 2
. Frank–Laptev–Lieb–Seiringer
[
18
]
proved that, for
\gamma\geq 1
, the inequality (
2
) holds for all eigenvalues outside a fixed cone,
|\operatorname{Im}z_{j}|\geq\kappa\operatorname{Re}z_{j}
, with a constant that blows up as
\kappa\to 0
at a rate
\kappa^{-\gamma-d/2}
. In
d=1
, the blowup rate was shown to be optimal by Bögli
[
3
]
. Averaging the bound of Frank–Laptev–Lieb–Seiringer with respect to
\kappa
, Demuth–Hansmann-Katriel
[
11
]
proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|z_{j}|^{\gamma}\left(\frac{\delta(z_{j})}{|z_{j}|}\right)^{\gamma+d/2+\varepsilon}\leq C_{\gamma,d,\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{\gamma+d/2}\mathrm{d}x,

(3)

where
\delta(z):=\operatorname{dist}(z,[0,\infty))
,
\gamma\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
. They also posed the question
[
13
]
whether (
3
) is true for
\varepsilon=0
. For
d=1
, Bögli and Štampach
[
5
]
answered this question in the negative.

A rather different set of results concerning eigenvalue sums have been established by Frank–Sabin
[
19
]
and Frank
[
17
]
. These bounds are of the (scale-invariant) form

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}|z_{j}|^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\delta(z_{j})}{|z_{j}|}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{\gamma/\alpha}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{\gamma+d/2}\mathrm{d}x.

(4)

It would be too technical for this introduction to state the precise values of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d
for which (
4
) holds, so we will only remark some general features of these bounds.
1

[
15
]
.

•

If
0<\gamma\leq 1/2
(
\gamma=1/2
if
d=1
) then
\beta=1
. This means that if the eigenvalues accumulate in
(0,\infty)
then they are summable, i.e.,
(\operatorname{Im}z_{j})\in\ell^{1}
.

•

In all known bounds, we have
\gamma/\alpha<1
. In other words, a sum is bounded by a
power
of an integral strictly greater than
1
. This means that there is a loss of
locality
, which is a crucial feature of the Lieb–Thirring inequality (
2
).

•

One particular bound from
[
17
]
for
d=1
states that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\delta(z_{j})^{\beta}\right)^{1/(2\beta)}\leq C_{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)|\mathrm{d}x

(5)

holds with
\beta=1
. The first author showed in
[
9
]
that the inequality fails for
\beta<1
, so the bound (
5
) is optimal.

The example of
[
9
]
also shows that one can have unexpectedly many eigenvalues compared to the number of resonances. In higher dimensions, it is an open problem whether there are Schrödinger operators with complex potentials
that have significantly more eigenvalues than their real counterparts.

1.3.
Main results

Our main contribution is to improve the results of Frank
[
17
]
, concerning bounds of the type (
4
), under the assumption that
V
is
radial
. As we already mentioned, radial potentials satisfy better estimates in the case of individual eigenvalues. To the best of our knowledge, such an effect has not been observed so far for sums of eigenvalues.

Theorem 1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
q\in(\frac{d+1}{2},d)
,
p>\frac{(d-1)q}{d-q}
. Then for radial
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the eigenvalues
z_{j}
of
H
satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\delta(z_{j})|z_{j}|^{p(1-\frac{d}{2q})-1}\right)^{q/p}\leq C_{p,q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{q}\mathrm{d}x.

(6)

In particular, if
(z_{j})
accumulates to a point in
(0,\infty)
, then
(\operatorname{Im}z_{j})\in\ell^{1}
.

Remark 1
.

i) Frank-Sabin
[
19
]
proved the case
q\in(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d+1}{2}]
with the same accumulation rate.

ii) Frank
[
17
]
proved similar bounds for
q>\frac{d+1}{2}
, but with a slower accumulation rate. Moreover, Frank’s bounds distinguish between eigenvalues lying in a disk around the origin and eigenvalues lying outside this disk.

iii) The overall powers of
|z_{j}|
in the bounds of Frank–Sabin
[
19
]
and Frank
[
17
]
are always negative, whereas ours are positive.

iv) Since
q/p<(d-q)/(d-1)<1
, the bound (
6
) exhibits the same non-locality as those in
[
19
]
,
[
17
]
. We conjecture that the exponent
q/p
in (
6
) cannot be increased, i.e. that the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\delta(z_{j})^{\beta}|z_{j}|^{\beta p(1-\frac{d}{2q})-1}\right)^{q/(\beta p)}\leq C_{p,q,\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{q}\mathrm{d}x

(7)

fails for
\beta<1
. We leave it as an open problem to find a counterexample.

v) Another interesting question is whether one can dispense with the radiality assumption and instead replace the right-hand side of (
6
) by a constant multiple of the mixed norm

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{\omega\in S^{d-1}}|V(r\omega)|^{q}r^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r.

(8)

Such generalizations for bounds of single eigenvalues appear in
[
20
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1
follows the same general strategy as in
[
19
]
,
[
17
]
. It is based on the identification of eigenvalues of the Schrödinger operator
H
with zeroes of an analytic function (a regularized determinant). This method was
pioneered by Demuth–Katriel
[
14
]
and Borichev–Golinskii–Kupin
[
6
]
and extended by Demuth–Hansmann–Katriel
[
11
,
12
]
. The method rests upon a remarkable generalization of Jensen’s inequality for analytic functions, due to
[
6
]
, for functions that blow up at some points of the boundary. We will appeal to the quantitative version of Frank
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
, but the special case
\varrho=0
there is essentially contained in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 16]
. It corresponds to a blow-up at a single point (
z=0
). The main new technical ingredient in our proof is a uniform resolvent bound in trace ideals.

Theorem 2
.

Let
d/2\leq q<d
and
p>\frac{(d-1)q}{d-q}
.
Then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
and for all radial functions
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|W_{1}(-\Delta-z)^{-1}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{p,q,d}|z|^{\frac{d}{2q}-1}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2q}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2q}}.

(9)

Remark 2
.

i) Theorem
2
extends the uniform resolvent estimates of Frank–Sabin
[
19
, Theorem 12]
from
q\leq(d+1)/2
to
q<d
under the assumption of radial symmetry.

ii) Our Schatten exponent
p
coincides with that of
[
19
]
and is optimal in the range
q\leq(d+1)/2
, as shown in
[
19
, Theorem 6]
. The resolvent bounds of Frank–Sabin hold for arbitrary potentials, not just radial potentials. However, their counterexample is radial and the argument remains valid for
q>(d+1)/2
, so the same example shows optimality of the Schatten exponent
p
in Theorem
2
.

iii) The Schatten norm bound in Theorem
2
is crucial for applications involving sums of eigenvalues. For bounds involving only individual eigenvalues, a weaker bound for the operator norm would suffice. By Hölder’s inequality, the operator norm bound is equivalent to an
L^{p}\to L^{p^{\prime}}
bound for
(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
. In the range
d/2\leq q\leq(d+1)/2
, this is a special case of the uniform resolvent bounds of Kenig–Ruiz–Sogge
[
21
]
.
The idea of using uniform resolvent bounds in the context of eigenvalue bounds for Schrödinger operators with complex potentials is due to Frank
[
16
]
.

iv) For radial potentials, Frank–Simon proved uniform
L^{p}\to L^{p^{\prime}}
bounds (equivalently, bounds with the operator norm instead of a Schatten norm in the left hand side of (
9
)) in the optimal range
d/2\leq q<d
. Thus, one of our main contributions is to upgrade their bounds to stronger trace ideal bounds.

2.
Spectral Measure Estimate

Let
E(\lambda)=\mathbf{1}_{[0,\lambda]}(\sqrt{-\Delta})
. The spectral function can be factorized as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}E(\lambda)/\mathrm{d}\lambda=c_{d}\lambda^{d-2}\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\mathcal{E}(\lambda)^{*},

(10)

where
\mathcal{E}(\lambda):L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the Fourier extension operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\lambda)g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda x\cdot\xi}g(\xi)\mathrm{d}S(\xi),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\lambda>0,

(11)

and where
\mathrm{d}S
denotes induced Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. If
\lambda=1
, we will write
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}(1)
.

The main result of this section is the following precursor to Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
1\leq q<d
and
p>\frac{(d-1)q}{d-q}
.
Then for all
\lambda>0
and for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{2q}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|W_{1}(\mathrm{d}E(\lambda)/\mathrm{d}\lambda)W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{p,q,d}\lambda^{\frac{d}{q}-2}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2q}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2q}}.

(12)

Proof.

By scaling, we may assume without loss of generality that
\lambda=1
.
By (
10
) and a
TT^{*}
argument (to reduce to the case
W_{1}=\overline{W_{2}}
), it then suffices to prove

\displaystyle\|W\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{p,q,d}\|W\|_{L^{2q}}^{2},\quad\forall W\in L_{\rm rad}^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(13)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}V\mathcal{E}^{*}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{p,q,d}\|V\|_{L^{q}}\quad\forall V\in L_{\rm rad}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(14)

To prove (
14
), we set

\displaystyle\Sigma:=\mathcal{E}^{*}V\mathcal{E}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}).

We use
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{k}
to denote the
k
-th degree spherical harmonics and solid spherical harmonics respectively. Since
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{k}
, we can decompose
\Sigma
into an orthogonal sum
\Sigma=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\Sigma_{k}
, where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}:=\mathcal{E}^{*}V\mathcal{E}|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}:\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}.

(15)

The fact that
\Sigma_{k}
maps
\mathcal{H}_{k}
to itself follows from corresponding mapping properties of the Fourier transformation. More precisely, it follows from
[
23
, Thm. 3.10]
that if
f\in(L^{1}\cap L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f(x)=f_{0}(|x|)P(x/|x|)
, where
P\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
, then
\widehat{f}(\xi)=F_{0}(|\xi|)P(\xi)
, where

\displaystyle F_{0}(r)=2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k}r^{-(d+2k-2)/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{0}(s)J_{(d+2k-2)/2}(2\pi rs)s^{(d+2k)/2}\mathrm{d}s,

and
J_{\nu}
is a Bessel function.
Note that
P
is homogeneous of degree
k
, i.e.
P(\xi)=r^{k}P(\omega)
,
r=|\xi|
,
\omega=\xi/r
. We set
H=P|_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
\widetilde{f_{0}}(r):=r^{k}f_{0}(r)
. Then we have

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\widetilde{F_{0}}(r)H(\omega)
, with

\displaystyle\widetilde{F_{0}}(r)=2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k}r^{-(d-2)/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f_{0}}(s)J_{(d+2k-2)/2}(2\pi rs)s^{d/2}\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, the Fourier transform of the function
x\mapsto\widetilde{f_{0}}(|x|)H(x/|x|)
, where
H\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
, is the function
\xi\mapsto\widetilde{F_{0}}(r)H(\omega)
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{*}(x\mapsto\widetilde{f_{0}}(|x|)H(x/|x|))(r\omega)=\widetilde{F_{0}}(1)H(\omega)=\langle\varphi_{k},\widetilde{f_{0}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{d-1}\mathrm{d}r)}H(\omega),

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(s):=2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k}J_{(d+2k-2)/2}(2\pi s)s^{-d/2+1}.

A straightforward calculation shows that, for
H\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}H=\lambda_{k}H,\quad\lambda_{k}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\varphi_{k}(s)|^{2}v(s)s^{d-1}\mathrm{d}s\right),\quad v(|x|):=V(x).

Hence,
\Sigma_{k}
is a multiple of the identity
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
, with eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
of multiplicity
\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}\approx k^{d-2}
. By Hölder,

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|J_{(d+2k-2)/2}(2\pi s)|^{2}w(s)s\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|J_{(d+2k-2)/2}(2\pi s)|^{2p}s^{d-1+q^{\prime}(2-d)}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{q}s^{d-1}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/q}.

If we set
\varrho=d-1+q^{\prime}(2-d)
, then the conditions of Lemma
4
below are satisfied for
d\geq 2
and

\displaystyle q^{\prime}>\max\left\{\frac{d}{d-1},\frac{d-\frac{2}{3}}{d-\frac{4}{3}}\right\}=\frac{d}{d-1}\iff q<d.

(16)

Hence, for
q<d
, we obtain

\lambda_{k}\lesssim\max\left\{k^{-q^{\prime}+\varrho+1},k^{-\frac{2q^{\prime}}{3}+\varrho+\frac{1}{3}}\right\}^{1/q^{\prime}}\|V\|_{L^{q}}.

By orthogonality, we then have

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\Sigma_{k}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}}^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}\lambda_{k}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{d-2}\lambda_{k}^{p}\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{q}}^{p},

provided that
q<d
and

\displaystyle d-2+\max(-q^{\prime}+\varrho+1,-\frac{2}{3}q^{\prime}+\varrho+\frac{1}{3})\frac{p}{q^{\prime}}<-1\iff p>\frac{(d-1)q}{d-q}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

In the previous proof we used
the following lemma concerning bounds on integrals of Bessel functions. It is a special case of a result of Barcelo et al.
[
2
]
. The specific bound we require is stated below and can be inferred from
[
20
, Lemma A.3]
.

Lemma 4
.

For
\varrho\in\mathbb{R}
and
p>\varrho+1
,
\frac{2p}{3}>\varrho+\frac{1}{3}
, we have

\int_{1}^{\infty}|J_{\mu}(2\pi s)|^{2p}s^{\varrho}\,ds\lesssim\max\left\{\mu^{-p+\varrho+1},\mu^{-\frac{2p}{3}+\varrho+\frac{1}{3}}\right\}.

for all
\mu>1/2
,
p\neq 2
. If
p=2
then the bound gains an extra factor of
\log\mu
.

3.
Resolvent estimates

3.1.
Naive bound

In the following, we use the abbreviation
R_{0}(z):=(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
.
To prove the resolvent estimate (
9
), we can assume
|z|=1
(by scaling). We only consider the case
\operatorname{Re}z>0
, since the case
\operatorname{Re}z\leq 0
is much easier.
We split
R_{0}(z)=R_{0}(z)^{\rm high}+R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}
, where
R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}
has Fourier multiplier
(\xi^{2}-\operatorname{Re}z)^{-1}\chi(\xi/\sqrt{\operatorname{Re}z})
and
\chi
is a smooth, radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that equals
1
on
B(0,2)
and is supported on
B(0,4)
. Again, we only consider
R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}
since the estimate for
R_{0}(z)^{\rm high}
is much easier. Observing that

\displaystyle W_{1}R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}W_{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(\lambda/\sqrt{\operatorname{Re}z})}{\lambda^{2}-z}W_{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}E(\lambda)}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}W_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

(17)

and using the spectral measure bound (
12
), we find

\displaystyle\|W_{1}R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim|\log|\operatorname{Im}z||\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2q}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2q}}.

(18)

This is almost the desired bound, up to a logarithm. At this point we note that, due to the spectral localization
\chi
, we may assume that
W
is constant on the unit scale. Hence, in the following, we will assume that
W_{1},W_{2}
are supported on a union of
c
-cubes where
c\ll 1
.

3.2.
Smoothing

The first step is to trade the logarithmic loss in
|\operatorname{Im}z|
into a logarithmic loss involving the size of the supports of
W_{1},W_{2}
.
Hence, we will temporarily assume
\operatorname{supp}(W_{1}),\operatorname{supp}(W_{2})\subset B_{R}
, where
B_{R}
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
R\gg 1
(the center of the ball will not be important). The spatial localization to
B_{R}
smooths off the integrand in (
17
) on the
1/R
scale, i.e., when using the triangle inequality,
|\lambda^{2}-z|
may be replaced by
|\lambda^{2}-z|+1/R
. To make this rigorous, observe that, by the convolution theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}}m(D)\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}}=\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}}m_{R}(D)\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}}

whenever
m(D)
is a Fourier multiplier, where
m_{R}:=\varphi_{R}\ast m
,
\varphi_{R}(\xi):=R^{d}\varphi(R\xi)
and
\varphi
is a Schwartz function such that
\widehat{\varphi}=1
on
B(0,2)
.
The previous argument then yields

\displaystyle\|W_{1}R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim(\log R)|\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2q}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2q}}.

(19)

3.3.
Removal of the logarithm

To remove the
\log R
factor, we partly follow the strategy of the first author and Merz
[
10
, Sect. 7.2]
. The idea
is reminiscent of an ‘epsilon removal
lemma’ of Tao
[
26
]
in the context of Fourier restriction theory.
Compared to
[
10
]
, the strategy is easier to explain in our setting since the potential is not random. Thus, we will only need to deal with the bilinear operator
(W_{1},W_{2})\mapsto W_{1}R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}W_{2}
, as opposed to multilinear operators with more than one factor of
R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}
, which had to be considered in
[
10
]
. On the other hand, we work with Schatten norms, whereas
[
10
]
deals with operator norms.

To illustrate the method, we will first prove a version without the radial symmetry assumption.

Proposition 5
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
1\leq p_{0},q_{0}<\infty
, and assume that the estimate

\displaystyle\|W_{1}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p_{0}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C(\log R)^{s}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2q_{0}}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2q_{0}}}

(20)

holds for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported on a finite union of
c
-cubes contained in balls of radius
R
(different balls are allowed) and for some
s>0
. Then, for
1\leq q<q_{0}
and
1\leq p<p_{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1/p-1/p_{0}}{1/q-1/q_{0}}<\frac{1-1/p_{0}}{1-1/q_{0}},

(21)

there exists a constant
C_{p,q,s,d}
such that

\displaystyle\|W_{1}R_{0}(z)^{\rm low}W_{2}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{p,q,s,d}\|W_{1}\|_{L^{2q}}\|W_{2}\|_{L^{2q}}

(22)

for all
W_{1},W_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
with
|z|=1
.

Remark 3
.

By
[
19
, Theorem 2]
, the assumed bound (
20
) is satisfied iff
q_{0}\leq(d+1)/2
and
p_{0}\leq(d-1)q/(d-q)
. The resolvent bound (
22
), including the endpoint
q=q_{0}
, follows from
[
19

Abstract
. We obtain some important fundamental inequalities concerning the long time behavior of high order derivatives for solutions of some dissipative systems in terms of their L 2 L^{2} algebraic decay. Some of these inequalities have not been observed in the literature even for the fundamental Navier-Stokes equations.To illustrate this new approach, we derive bounds for the asymmetric incompressible fluids equations, where an improved inequalities established for the micro-rotational field. Finally, we show how this technique can be applied for other dissipative systems.

1.
Introduction

Asymptotic inequalities for dissipative systems are a topic of high interest, specially in the context of fluid mechanics models, such as the Navier-Stokes equations and related systems, like the asymmetric incompressible fluids equations (micropolar Navier-Stokes), among others. Here, we derive some new inequalities for high order derivatives of solutions for such systems.
The method used here is, by itself, interesting, since it is a direct method based on energy inequalities. Even tough it is direct, it depends on very subtle and technical arguments, which may eventually be used in different contexts. To describe it, we show how to obtain bounds for solutions of the so called asymmetric, also known as micropolar, fluids system

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla\>\!{\sf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\>\!\mu+\chi\>\!)\,\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,+\,\chi\,\nabla\times{\bf w},

\displaystyle{\bf w}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\bf w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\;\!\,\Delta\mbox{\bf w}\,+\,\kappa\,\nabla\>\!(\>\!\nabla\cdot\;{\bf w}\>\!)\,+\,\chi\,\nabla\times\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,-\,2\,\chi\,{\bf w},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

with suitable initial data. We note that the results obtained here
for the linear velocity

\mathbf{u}
in system above above also hold for solutions of the classical Navier-Stokes equations.
In particular, as far as we know, the results on the large time dynamics given in Theorem
1.3
below are new even
for solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations.

In 1984, Kato
[
24
]
and Masuda
[
32
]
showed that solutions
\mathbf{u}
of the Navier-Stokes
equations satisfy
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, finally solving an open problem left
by Leray
[
26
]
. Since then, several works have made considerable progress in this direction (see e.g
[
35
,
40
,
39
,
41
,
42
]
). In particular, the well-known Fourier Splitting method, developed by M. E. Schonbek
[
38
,
39
,
42
]
, is a powerful tool to prove that the
L^{2}
norm of solutions for viscous conservation laws and for Navier-Stokes equations decay with algebraic rate depending on the initial data.
Recently, Hagstrom et al.
[
21
]
derived an asymptotic inequality for arbitrary order spatial derivatives of Leray solutions for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\leq 4
. This technique may also be extended to dissipative systems with linear damping, such as the asymmetric fluids system, as we show in details here (see Theorem
1.1
).
The bounds for the linear velocity
\mathbf{u}
are the same as for solutions of classical Navier-Stokes equations obtained in
[
21
]
. The bounds for the rotational velocity
\mathbf{w}
are stronger, as can be seen
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

Firstly introduced by Eringen in
[
15
]
, the asymmetric fluids model is a generalization of the classical Navier-Stokes equations that takes into account the microstructure of the fluid particles. It describes flows of fluids whose particles undergo translations and rotations as well. Asymmetric fluids flows are governed by the equations

(1.1a)

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}\;\!+\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,+\;\!\nabla\>\!{\sf{p}}\;=\;(\>\!\mu+\chi\>\!)\,\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,+\,\chi\,\nabla\times{\bf w},

(1.1b)

{\bf w}_{t}\;\!+\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\bf w}\;=\;\gamma\;\!\,\Delta\mbox{\bf w}\,+\,\kappa\,\nabla\>\!(\>\!\nabla\cdot\;{\bf w}\>\!)\,+\,\chi\,\nabla\times\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\,-\,2\,\chi\,{\bf w},

(1.1c)

\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)=0,

with initial data

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}:=(\>\!\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},{\bf w}_{0})\in{\bf L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\!\times\!{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and

\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\cdot,t)-\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0\mbox{, as }t\to 0,

where
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}:=(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{$\bf w$})
.
In system (
1.1
), the constants

\mu,\gamma,\kappa>0
are kinematic and spin viscosities and
\chi>0
is the vortex viscosity. The functions

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)
,
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}=\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)
,
and
\sc{p}=\sc{p}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)

are the flow velocity, micro-rotational velocity and the total pressure, respectively, for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here,

\!\;\!\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

denotes the
space
of solenoidal fields

\>\!\mbox{\bf v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})\!\>\!\in\mbox{\boldmath$L$}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\equiv L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}\!\>\!

with

\nabla\!\cdot\mbox{\bf v}\!\;\!=0

in the distributional sense. For a discussion of the physical meaning and the derivation of system (
1.1
), see
[
13
,
15
,
31
]
. Observe that taking
\mbox{$\bf w$}=0
in system (
1.1
), one obtains the classical incompressible Navier-Stokes equations.

Consider the linear system obtained simply by dropping out the nonlinear terms from system (
1.1
)

(1.2a)

\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{u}$}_{t}\;\!+\,\nabla\>\!{\sf{\bar{p}}}\;=\;(\>\!\mu+\chi\>\!)\,\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{u}$}\,+\,\,\chi\,\nabla\times{\bf\bar{w}},

(1.2b)

{\bf\bar{w}}_{t}\;\!+\,\gamma\;\!\,\Delta{\bf{\bar{\mbox{$\bf w$}}}}\,+\,\kappa\,\nabla\>\!(\>\!\nabla\cdot\;{\bf\bar{w}}\>\!)\,+\,\chi\,\nabla\times\,\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{u}$}\,-\,2\,\chi\,{\bf\bar{w}},

(1.2c)

\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\bar{u}$}(\cdot,t)=0.

Our main result compares the evolution of solutions
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})
for the nonlinear initial value problem (
1.1
) with the evolution
of solutions
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}}=(\mathbf{\bar{u}},\mathbf{\bar{w}})

for the linear problem (
1.2
) with the same initial conditions.

For convenience, define
\nu:=\min\{\mu,\gamma\}
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{0}(\alpha):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{\alpha}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\alpha\geq 0

We assume that

(1.3)

\lambda_{0}(\alpha)<\infty,

\text{for some }\,\,\alpha\geq 0
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[

1

34

We state our results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\in{\bf L}^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. If

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\cdot,t)=(\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t),\mathbf{w}(\cdot,t))

is a weak solution of system (
1.1
) with initial data
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}
, then

(1.4a)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{\alpha+\frac{m}{2}}\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq K_{\alpha,m}\;\;\nu^{-m/2}\;\;\lambda_{0}(\alpha)

and, for the micro-rotational field
\mathbf{w}
,

(1.4b)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\alpha+\frac{m+1}{2}}\|D^{m}\mbox{$\bf w$}(\cdot,t)\|_{{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}\leq\frac{K_{\alpha,m+1}}{2}\;\;\nu^{-\frac{m+1}{2}}\;\;\lambda_{0}(\alpha)

for every
\alpha\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
, where

(1.5)

K_{\alpha,m}=\min_{\delta>0}\Bigg\{\delta^{-1/2}\prod_{j=0}^{m}(\alpha+\frac{j}{2}+\delta)^{1/2}\Bigg\}.

By the Calderón-Zygmund theory, one directly obtains the following high order inequality for the pressure:

Corollary 1.2
.

The pressure
p(\cdot,t)
satisfies

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{2\alpha+\frac{m}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}\|D^{m}p(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{m}\;\nu^{-(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{3}{4})}\,\lambda_{0}(\alpha)\,^{2},

where
C_{m}
is a positive constant depending only on
m
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

Lemma 1

For all integers

, we get

\begin{split}t^{\alpha+\beta+m/2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t/2}\|e^{c\,\Delta(t-s)}D^{m}\left[\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\right]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\\
\leq c^{-(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{5}{4})}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|t^{5/4+m/2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t/2}(t-s)^{-5/4-m/2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\\
\leq c^{-(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{5}{4})}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|2^{5/4+m/2}=c^{-(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{5}{4})}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|2^{\alpha+\beta+m/2}.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of inequality (
1.6a
). ∎

4.2.
Proof of inequality (
1.6b
)

Using Lemma
3
,
we get, for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{$\bf w$}}(\cdot,t)
, the bound

(4.2)

\begin{split}t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\|D^{m}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{$\bf w$}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}e^{-2\chi(t-t_{0})}\|D^{m}e^{c\,\Delta(t-t_{0})}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{$\bf w$}}(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
+t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}\|e^{c\,\Delta(t-s)}D^{m}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{$\bf w$})(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\\
+\chi t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}\|e^{c\,\Delta(t-s)}D^{m+1}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds.\end{split}

As in the proof of inequality (
1.4b
) (see bound (
3.10
)), all the terms in the right hand side of inequality (
4.2
) tend to
0
as
t
tends to infinity except for the last one. Hence, this is the term we will focus our attention. By inequality (
1.6a
), one has

\begin{split}\chi t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}\|e^{\gamma\Delta(t-s)}D^{m+1}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\\
\leq\chi t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}\|D^{m+1}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\\
\leq(C(\nu,\chi,\alpha,m+1)+\epsilon)\chi t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}s^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m+1}{2}}ds\\
=(C(\nu,\chi,\alpha,m+1)+\epsilon)\chi t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\bigg[\int_{t_{0}}^{\sigma t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}s^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m+1}{2}}ds\\
+\int_{\sigma t}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}s^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m+1}{2}}ds\bigg]\\
\leq(C(\nu,\chi,\alpha,m+1)+\epsilon)\chi t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\bigg[e^{-2\chi t(1-\sigma)}\int_{t_{0}}^{{\sigma}t}s^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m+1}{2}}ds\\
+({\sigma}t)^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{{\sigma}t}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}ds\bigg]\\
\leq(C(\nu,\chi,\alpha,m+1)+\epsilon)\chi\bigg[t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}e^{-2\chi t(1-\sigma)}\bigg(2\frac{t_{0}^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m-1}{2}}-({\sigma}t)^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m-1}{2}}}{2\alpha+2\beta+m-1}\bigg)\\
+\sigma^{-\alpha-\beta-\frac{m+1}{2}}\bigg(\frac{1-e^{-2\chi t(1-\sigma)}}{2\chi}\bigg)\bigg].\end{split}

Letting, in this order,
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, taking the
\limsup
as
t\to\infty
and letting
{\sigma}\to 1^{-}
, we get

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\alpha+\beta+\frac{m+1}{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-2\chi(t-s)}\|e^{\gamma\Delta(t-s)}D^{m+1}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\leq\frac{1}{2}C(\nu,\chi,\alpha,m+1).

which leads to the desired inequality (
1.6b
). ∎

5.
Additional comments and the fundamental inequality for other related systems

We present some direct consequences and extensions of the results previous results.
We also apply the previous arguments to quickly obtain results for other related systems.

5.1.
Direct consequences and extensions

From the results above, we can immediately get, through standard interpolations, some general inequalities.
Precisely,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\alpha+\frac{s}{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\min_{m>s}\,[\,K_{\alpha,m}\,]^{\frac{s}{m}}\nu^{-\frac{m}{s}}\lambda_{0}(\alpha),

\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\alpha+\frac{m}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq(K_{\alpha,m}\,\nu^{-\frac{m}{2}})^{1/4}(K_{\alpha,m+2}\,\nu^{-\frac{m}{2}-1})^{3/4}\,\lambda_{0}(\alpha),

and

\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\alpha+\frac{m}{2}+\frac{3(p-2)}{4p}}\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{\nu,m,\alpha,p}\,\lambda_{0}(\alpha)\mbox{,\,\, for all }2\leq p<\infty.

Remark 5.1
.

Similar versions of the inequalities above can be obtained for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{$\bf w$}}
. It is also important to note that the main theorems
1.1
and
1.3
imply the following interesting decays:

(5.1)

\|D_{t}^{k}D_{x}^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=O(t^{-\alpha-k-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{3(p-2)}{4p}}),

\|D_{t}^{k}D_{x}^{m}\mbox{$\bf w$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=O(t^{-\alpha-k-\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{3(p-2)}{4p}}),

\|D_{t}^{k}D_{x}^{m}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{\boldmath$z$}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=O(t^{-\alpha-\beta-k-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{3(p-2)}{4p}}),

\|D_{t}^{k}D_{x}^{m}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mbox{$\bf w$}}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=O(t^{-\alpha-\beta-k-\frac{m+1}{2}-\frac{3(p-2)}{4p}}),

where
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
\beta
is as in Theorem
1.3
.

5.2.
Other Systems

As mentioned in the introduction, the technique presented can be used to obtain similar estimates for other dissipative type problems. Let us now give some examples.

5.2.1.
Dissipative linear system

Consider

(5.2)

v_{t}=\mathbb{L}v

where
\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}
is a pseudodifferential operator with symbol
M(\xi)
such that

M(\xi)=P^{-1}(\xi)D(\xi)P(\xi),\mbox{ \,\,\,\,}\xi-a.e.,

where
P(\xi)\in O(3)
and
D(\xi)_{ij}=-c_{i}|\xi|^{2\gamma}\delta_{ij}
,
for
c_{i}>c>0
and
0<\gamma\leq 1
. For a study on the decay rates of solutions for these types of equations involving the
decay character
, see
[
3
,
1
,
34
]
. In fact, this approach assures that condition
\lambda_{0}(\alpha)<\infty
(condition (
1.3
))
for certain
\alpha>0
depending on the initial data.

Let
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}
. Applying
\Lambda^{m\gamma}
in equation (
5.2
) and taking the dot product with
(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\Lambda^{m\gamma}v
, we get

(5.3)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{m\gamma}}=(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\langle|\xi|^{m\gamma}\hat{v},M|\xi|^{m\gamma}\hat{v}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
=-(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\langle(-D)^{1/2}P|\xi|^{m\gamma}\hat{v},(-D)^{1/2}P|\xi|^{m\gamma}\hat{v}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
=-(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-D)^{1/2}P|\xi|^{m\gamma}\hat{v}|^{2}d\xi\\
\leq-C(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2(m+1)\gamma}|\hat{v}|^{2}d\xi=-C(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{(m+1)\gamma}}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(5.4)

\begin{split}(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{m\gamma}}+2C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|v(s)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{(m+1)\gamma}}ds\\
\leq(2\alpha+m+\delta)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m-1+\delta}\|v(s)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{m\gamma}}ds.\end{split}

Using the same induction argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, one has

(5.5)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\alpha+m/2}\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{m\gamma}}\leq K_{\alpha,m}C^{-m/2}\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\alpha}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

where

(5.6)

K_{\alpha,m}=\min_{\delta>0}\Bigg\{\delta^{-1/2}\prod_{j=0}^{m}(\alpha+\frac{j}{2}+\delta)^{1/2}\Bigg\}.

5.2.2.
Tropical climate model equations

Consider the system

(5.7a)

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-\mu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p+\nabla\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$})=0,

(5.7b)

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{t}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}-\nu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$v$}+\nabla\theta+(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0,

(5.7c)

\theta_{t}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\theta-\eta\Delta\theta+\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0,

(5.7d)

\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0,

where
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t),u_{3}(x,t))
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=(v_{1}(x,t),v_{2}(x,t),v_{3}(x,t))
are the barotropic mode and the first baroclinic mode of the velocity, respectively;
\theta=\theta(x,t)
and
p=p(x,t)
represent the scalar temperature and scalar pressure, respectively. The coefficients
\mu
,
\nu
, and
\eta
are nonnegative.
System (
5.7
) was first introduced in
[
16
]
without any dissipation terms (
\mu=\nu=\eta=0
). For a study on its regularity and well-posedness, see
[
28
,
10
,
11
,
9
,
8
,
43
,
27
]
.

Following the same techniques presented in the proof of Theorem
1.1
one can obtain the inequality

(5.8)

\begin{split}(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|(D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$v$},D^{m}\theta)(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\\
(2-\epsilon)\min{(\mu,\nu,\eta)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(s-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|(D^{m+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},D^{m+1}\mbox{\boldmath$v$},D^{m+1}\theta)(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds\\
\leq(2\alpha+m+\delta)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(s-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|(D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$v$},D^{m}\theta)(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},\end{split}

for each integer
m\geq 0
. Through the same induction argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, one gets

(5.9)

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{\alpha+\frac{m}{2}}\|(D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$v$},D^{m}\theta)(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
\leq K_{\alpha,m}(\min\{\mu,\nu,\eta\})^{-m/2}\;\;\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{\alpha}\|(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\theta)(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

where

(5.10)

K_{\alpha,m}=\min_{\delta>0}\Bigg\{\delta^{-1/2}\prod_{j=0}^{m}(\alpha+\frac{j}{2}+\delta)^{1/2}\Bigg\}.

5.2.3.
Navier-stokes with rotational framework

Consider the system

(5.11)

\begin{split}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\omega\mathbb{J}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p=&\mu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\mbox{ \,\,\,\, in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=&0\mbox{ \,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\, in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,0)=&\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{split}

where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is the Coriolis parameter and
\mathbb{J}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}:={\bf e}_{3}\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
is the Coriolis force acting on the fluid. Observe that
\mathbb{J}u=(-u_{2},u_{1},0)
and therefore this term does not affect the energy inequality obtained for
u
. Following the same procedure as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we get

(5.12)

\begin{split}(t-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+(2-\epsilon)\nu\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(s-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m+\delta}\|D^{m+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\\
\leq(2\alpha+m+\delta)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(s-t_{0})^{2\alpha+m-1+\delta}\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

for each integer
m\geq 0
. Using the same induction argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, one has

(5.13)

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{\alpha+\frac{m}{2}}\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq K_{\alpha,m}\mu^{-m/2}\;\;\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,t^{\alpha}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\end{split}

where

(5.14)

K_{\alpha,m}=\min_{\delta>0}\Bigg\{\delta^{-1/2}\prod_{j=0}^{m}(\alpha+\frac{j}{2}+\delta)^{1/2}\Bigg\}.

For the existence of a global mild solution assuming small data in
H^{1/2}
, see
[
23
]
.
The equations (
5.11
) are also related to the Navier-Stokes flow past rotating obstacles (see e.g.
[
17
]
).

5.2.4.
Magneto-micropolar equations

When the magnetic field is taken into account in system (
1.1
), we get the so called magneto-micropolar system

(5.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p&=(\mu+\chi)\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\chi\nabla\times\mbox{$\bf w$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\\
\partial_{t}\mbox{$\bf w$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{$\bf w$}&=\gamma\Delta\mbox{$\bf w$}+\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mbox{$\bf w$})+\chi\nabla\times\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-2\chi\mbox{$\bf w$},\\
\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$b$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$b$}&=\nu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$b$}+(\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\cdot\nabla)\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\\
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)&=\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(\cdot,t)\,=0,\end{aligned}\right.

with initial data

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}=(\>\!\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},{\bf w}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{0})\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\!\times\!L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The results on eventual smoothness, existence and uniqueness for the micropolar fluids system also hold for the magneto-micropolar system (
5.15
). One can derive results completely analgous to Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, where, in this case,

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\cdot,t):=(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{$\bf w$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})(\cdot,t)
.

5.2.5.
Other Parabolic Systems

Let
f_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3,4
,
1\leq j\leq N
, denote smooth vector fields with symmetric Jacobian
A_{j}(u)=D_{u}f_{j}(u)
. Assume
|f_{j}(u)|\leq C|u|^{2}
. Consider the parabolic system

(5.16)

u_{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{j}(u)D_{j}u+D_{j}(A_{j}(u)u)=Pu

where the first order terms have anti-symmetric form and the linear constant coefficient operator
P
satisfies

P=\sum_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}D_{j}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}V_{ij}D_{i}D_{j},

\hat{P}(k)+\hat{P}^{*}(k)\leq-c|k|^{2}I,

for some
c>0
. Since

\int u^{T}D_{j}(A_{j}(u)u)dx=-\int(D_{j}u^{T})A_{j}(u)udx

and
A_{j}=A^{T}_{j}
, an analogous reasoning as used in Theorem
1.1
can be used to show that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\alpha+m/2}\|D^{m}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\,C\lambda_{0}(\alpha),

where
\lambda_{0}(\alpha):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{\alpha}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

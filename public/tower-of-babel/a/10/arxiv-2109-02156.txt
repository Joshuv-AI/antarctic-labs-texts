Linking disjoint axis-parallel segments into a simple polygon is hard too
Rain Jiang, Kai Jiang, Minghui Jiang

Abstract
Deciding whether a family of disjoint axis-parallel line segments in the plane can be linked into a simple polygon (or a simple polygonal chain) by adding segments between their endpoints is NP-hard.



1
Introduction

Given a family
\mathcal{S}
of
n
closed line segments in the plane,

Simple Circuit
(respectively,
Simple Path
)
is the problem of deciding whether
these segments can be linked into
a simple polygon (respectively, a simple polygonal chain)
by adding segments between their endpoints.

Rappaport
[
4
]
proved that
Simple Circuit
is NP-hard
if the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are allowed to intersect at their common endpoints,
and asked whether the problem remains NP-hard when the segments are disjoint.
Later, Bose, Houle, and Toussaint
[
2
]
asked
whether the related problem
Simple Path
is NP-hard
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint.
Later, Tóth
[
6
]
asked about the complexity of
Simple Path
again,
with a special interest in the case
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are both disjoint and axis-parallel.

Recently, Akitaya et al.
[
1
]

proved that
Simple Circuit
is NP-hard
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint and have only four distinct orientations.
Subsequently, the authors of this paper came up with a similar construction
[
3
]
,
and proved that both
Simple Circuit
and
Simple Path
are NP-hard,
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint and have only four distinct orientations.
Akitaya et al.
[
1
]
asked whether
Simple Circuit
is NP-hard
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint and axis-parallel.

In this paper,
we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Simple Circuit
and
Simple Path
are both NP-hard
even if the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint and axis-parallel.

We prove the theorem in two steps.
First, we modify the construction in Rappaport’s proof
of NP-hardness of
Simple Circuit
on not necessarily disjoint axis-parallel segments
[
4
]

to show that the problem remains NP-hard on disjoint axis-parallel segments.
Next, we modify the construction further
to prove that
Simple Path
is also NP-hard on disjoint axis-parallel segments.

Some of the ideas behind our construction in this paper
are also used in our previous construction
[
3
]
;
interested readers may read the short proof there as a warm-up exercise.
Following the same setup there,
we briefly review Rappaport’s proof in the following,
which is based on a polynomial reduction from the NP-hard problem

Hamiltonian Path
in planar cubic graphs.

For any family
\mathcal{S}
of closed segments in the plane,
denote by
V(\mathcal{S})
the set of endpoints of the segments in
\mathcal{S}
.
For any two endpoints
p
and
q
in
V(\mathcal{S})
,
we call the open segment
pq
a
visibility edge

if it does not intersect any closed segment in
\mathcal{S}
.

Given a planar cubic graph
G
with
n\geq 4
vertices,
the reduction
[
4
]
first obtains a rectilinear planar layout of the planar graph
using an algorithm of Rosenstiehl and Tarjan
[
5
]
,
then constructs a family
\mathcal{S}
of
O(n)
axis-parallel segments
following the rectilinear planar layout,
such that
G
admits a Hamiltonian path
if and only if
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
by adding visibility edges between endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S})
.
The segments in
\mathcal{S}
are axis-parallel and interior-disjoint,
but may intersect at common endpoints.
Each endpoint in
V(\mathcal{S})
is incident to at most one horizontal segment
and at most one vertical segment in
\mathcal{S}
.
Since the rectilinear planar layout
has width at most
2n-4
and height at most
n

[
5
]
,
all coordinates of endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S})
are integers of magnitude

O(n^{2})
;
indeed a closer look at the construction
[
4
, Figure 8]

shows that

V(\mathcal{S})\subseteq[1,22n^{2}]\times[1,11n]
.
The reduction is hence strongly polynomial.
Consequently,
Simple Circuit
is strongly NP-hard,
on not necessarily disjoint axis-parallel segments.

2
Modification for
Simple Circuit

To show that
Simple Circuit
remains strongly NP-hard on disjoint segments,
we will transform the family
\mathcal{S}
of interior-disjoint axis-parallel segments,
which Rappaport constructed,
into a family
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of disjoint axis-parallel segments
in polynomial time,
such that
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
This transformation can be viewed as a reduction from
Simple Circuit
on one type
of input to the same problem on another type of input.

Lemma 1
.

With
\delta=1/(c\cdot n^{2})
for a sufficiently large integer constant

outside the gadget through the gap between

Proof.

Refer to Figure
3
.
Let
u=(6,4)
, which is collinear with
o^{\prime}=(-2,0)
and
o^{\prime\prime}=(0,1)
.
Note that

|uu^{\prime}|=\delta
,

|o^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}|>6
,
and
\angle o^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}u>\pi/2
.
Thus

\angle uo^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}<\tan\angle uo^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}<\frac{|uu^{\prime}|}{|o^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}|}<\frac{\delta}{6}.

Consider the ray that starts from
o^{\prime\prime}
and goes through
o^{\prime}
.
Rotate this ray clockwise about
o^{\prime\prime}
for a positive angle
till it goes through another integer point
p
in
V(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.
The area of the triangle
o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}p
is at least
\frac{1}{2}

since all three endpoints have integer coordinates.
Recall that the endpoints of all segments in
\mathcal{S}
are in the range

[1,22n^{2}]\times[1,11n]
,
and is scaled by a factor of
42

by the transformation from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
Thus we have

|po^{\prime\prime}|<42\cdot(22n^{2}+11n)<50\cdot 33n^{2}
.
Also note that
|o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}|<3
.
Thus

\angle o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}p>\sin\angle o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}p=\frac{2\cdot\mathrm{area}(o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}p)}{|o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}|\cdot|po^{\prime\prime}|}>\frac{1}{3\cdot 50\cdot 33n^{2}}>\frac{1}{5000n^{2}}.

Let
\delta=1/(c\cdot n^{2})
for a sufficiently large integer
c>0
such that

\angle uo^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}<\angle o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}p
.
Then the ray
u^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}
splits the angle
\angle o^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}p
.
Thus the cone with the angle
\angle o^{\prime}u^{\prime}o^{\prime\prime}
does not contain
in its interior any integer endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.
Thus
u^{\prime}
cannot see any integer endpoints through the gap between
o^{\prime}
and
o^{\prime\prime}
.

Let

, which is collinear with
(17,18)
and
(20,19)
.
Then
|vv^{\prime}|=\delta
.
By a similar analysis, we can also guarantee that

v^{\prime}
cannot see any integer endpoints from other gadgets through the gap
between
(17,18)
and
(20,19)
.

Moreover,
for an intersection of the orientation as illustrated in Figure
2

the slope of a viewing line through

and its gap is around
\frac{1}{2}
,
and the slope of a viewing line through
v^{\prime}
and its gap is around
\frac{1}{3}
.
With
\delta=1/(c\cdot n^{2})
for a sufficiently large integer
c>0
,
no two endpoints

or

from two different gadgets can see each other,
because the eight narrow ranges of slopes of viewing lines,
as illustrated by the red lines in Figure

are disjoint for intersections of different orientations.

The following lemma shows that our local transformation preserves linkability:

Lemma 2
.

\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygon.

Proof.

We first prove the direct implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
For each visibility edge of this polygon between two endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S})
,
we add a visibility edge between the corresponding endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.
For each endpoint
o
incident to two segments
oa
and
ob
in
\mathcal{S}
,
we link the segments in the corresponding gadget in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

following the alternating path as illustrated in Figure
2
right.
Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is also linked into a simple polygon.

We next prove the reverse implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
Refer to Figures

Modification for
Simple Path

To prove that
Simple Path
is also NP-hard,
we use almost the same transformation from

except two changes:

1.

Increase the initial scaling factor from

80
,
and correspondingly decrease the distance
\delta

when constructing the gadgets, to ensure that Lemma

still holds.

2.

Select an arbitrary gadget,
and replace it by an extended gadget of the same orientation.
For example, if the gadget is as illustrated in Figure

A closer look at the construction

\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

We next prove the reverse implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

As before, the linking of the segments in each ordinary gadget

Limiting behavior in growth of Bulgarian solitaire orbits
Nhung Pham

Abstract
The Bulgarian Solitaire rule induces a finite dynamical system on the set of integer partitions of n n . Brandt [ 2 ] characterized and counted all cycles in its recurrent set for any given n n , with orbits parametrized by necklaces of black and white beads. However, the transient behavior within each orbit has been almost completely unknown. The only known case is when n = ( k 2 ) n=\binom{k}{2} is a triangular number, in which case there is only one orbit. Eriksson and Jonsson [ 3 ] gave an analysis for convergence of the structure as k k grows, and to what extent the limit applied to the finite case. In this article, we generalize the convergent structure for orbits of Bulgarian Solitaire system for any n n . For necklaces of the form ( B W ) k = B W B W ⋯ (BW)^{k}=BWBW\cdots , we give the precise limit of the generating functions as k k grows. For other necklaces, we prove that the generating functions are rational and provide a bound for their denominator and numerator degrees.

Introduction and Notation

1.1
The Bulgarian Solitaire system

The game of Bulgarian Solitaire (BS) was introduced by Martin Gardner in 1983. The original game starts with
45
cards divided into a number of piles. Now keep repeating the
Bulgarian Solitaire moves
: in each turn, take one card from each pile and form a new pile. The game ends when the sizes of the piles are not changed by performing the moves. Surprisingly, it turns out that regardless of initial state of the game, it must end in a finite number of moves at the state with one pile of one card, one pile of two cards, …, and one pile of nine cards. The rule was then generalized for any
n
as the Bulgarian Solitaire operation
\beta
on the set of partition
\mathcal{P}(n)\coloneqq\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m}):\text{length }l(\lambda)=m,\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{m}>0\text{ are integers},\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{m}=n\}
. The operation
\lambda\rightarrow\beta(\lambda)
is described as: in each step, take one from each part, form a new part and put the parts in weakly decreasing order. In addition, the game can be described in terms of Young diagrams: in each turn, remove the longest column and reinsert it as a new row into the diagram. An example is shown in Figure
1

Our main results concern its generating function, which is defined as the following polynomial in

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{N}}x^{\mathrm{level}(\lambda)}.

Note that Brandt’s bijection implies that the Bulgarian solitaire system on
\mathcal{P}(n)
for

\mathcal{O}_{W^{k+1}}

\Delta_{k}
, and for any partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{W^{k+1}}

\mathrm{level}(\lambda)
is the distance in the tree from
\lambda
to the staircase
\Delta_{k}
. Eriksson and Jonsson prove
[

converges to the subsequence of evenly-indexed Fibonacci numbers

, with the generating function

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{D}_{W^{k}}(x)=\dfrac{(1-x)^{2}}{1-3x+x^{2}}=:H_{W}(x).

(3)

Eriksson and Jonsson also showed that for
\mathcal{O}_{W^{k+1}}
, the sizes of levels

in the Bulgarian solitaire game tree coincide with those of an object that they called the

after pruning an appropriate branch (described later in this section).

We generalize this, describing the limit of the level sizes for arbitrary

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a power series

2
The rules for producing the forest
\mathcal{F}_{P}

The idea for the
P-quasi-infinite forest
is that each root
\gamma^{(t)}
is actually an infinite periodic binary vector whose consecutive segments
(\gamma^{(t)}_{mp+1},\ldots,\gamma^{(t)}_{(m+1)p})
of length
p
each forms a copy of
\gamma^{(t)}
. The reason for this beginning will be explained in the next section. This infinite vector guarantees that we will not encounter negative parts in difference labellings when performing the
R_{j}
operations as in the finite cases. However, we cannot write out infinite-length vectors for the elements in the forest. Instead, we start with only one block and add more blocks as one goes further down the tree
G_{t}
and applies the operators
R_{j}
. In fact, each segment
(\gamma^{(t)}_{mp+s},\ldots,\gamma^{(t)}_{(m+1)p+s-1})
is a copy of the root
\gamma^{(t+s)}
for any nonegative integers
m,s
.

In this section, for a vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
and
j\leq k
, we write
\lambda[j:k]=(\lambda_{j},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
. Also denote
\lambda_{j}=\lambda[1:j]
and
\lambda[j:]=(\lambda_{j},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
.

As part of the rules, we will want to maintain the property that every
\lambda
in the forest
\mathcal{F}_{P}
has a
tail
(= final segment of length
p
) exactly matching one of the roots
\gamma^{(t)}
, although the subscript
t
may not match the subscript
t^{\prime}
of the tree
G_{t^{\prime}}
which contains
\lambda
. Assume in the rules below that
\lambda
’s tail matches the root
\gamma^{(t)}
.

Rules for
\lambda\xrightarrow{i}R_{i}(\lambda)
in the tree
G_{t}
in general quasi-infinite forest - that is,
\lambda_{i}
is bracketed and
R_{i}
is played:

1.

If
\lambda_{i}
is in the tail (among the lowest
p
parts), append to
\lambda
so that
\lambda[i:]=\gamma^{(s)}
for some (unique)
s
. Replace
\lambda[i:]
by
\gamma^{(s+1)}
and bracket the tail as in the root
\gamma^{(s+1)}
.

Before discussing the bracketings, we prove a general property:

\lambda_{j_{1}}
was negative at the start. We obtain a contradiction.
∎

(in the necklace
N=P^{k}
) is large enough.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
\lambda=\small\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\gamma^{(t)}\\
\gamma^{(t)}\\
\beta\end{matrix}
is a difference labelling in
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{k}}
, where
\alpha,\beta
are some difference labellings, that
l(\alpha)=a
and that
\lambda_{a+1}
is playable. Then

R_{a+1}(\lambda)=\small\begin{matrix}\alpha+1\\
\gamma^{(t+1)}\\
\gamma^{(t)}[2:]\\
\beta^{\prime}\end{matrix}

(5)

and the
\gamma^{(t+1)}
segment is bracketed similarly to the root.

Proof.

We have
l(\gamma^{(t)})=p
and note that
\beta
is not necessarily non-negative. Let
n=pk-1
. The partition corresponding to
\lambda
is

\Lambda=\lambda^{+}=\lambda+\Delta_{n}

of length
l\geq a+2p
. So
\lambda_{a+1}
is playable if and only if
\Lambda_{a+1}=\gamma^{(t)}_{1}+n-a\geq l-1
, then
1\geq\gamma^{(t)}_{1}\geq l-n+a-1
. Playing
\lambda_{a+1}
adds
1
to each of the entries of both
\alpha^{+}
and
\alpha
, erases
\lambda_{a+1}
and keeps the rest of
\lambda
, thus we obtain (
5
). Let
\Psi=R_{a+1}(\Lambda)
and
\psi=R_{a+1}(\lambda)
, so
l(\Psi)=\gamma^{(t)}_{1}+n-a
. Now in the root
\gamma^{(t+1)}
, the part
\gamma^{(t+1)}_{j}
is bracketed if and only if

\gamma^{(t+1)}_{j}+(p-j)\geq\begin{cases}p-1\qquad\text{ if }\gamma^{(t)}_{1}=1\\
p-2\qquad\text{ if }\gamma^{(t)}_{1}=0\end{cases},

thus
\gamma^{(t+1)}_{j}\geq j-2+\gamma^{(t)}_{1}
. We show the same
j^{th}
part of the
\gamma^{(t+1)}
segment is bracketed in
\psi
. Actually, that part is
\psi_{a+j}
, and

\Psi_{a+j}=\gamma^{(t+1)}_{j}+n-a-j+1\geq\gamma^{(t)}_{1}+n-a-1=l(\Psi)-1.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda=R_{\sigma}((\gamma^{(t)})^{k})
, for some
(\gamma^{(t)})^{k}
in
\mathcal{C}_{P^{k}}
and some arbitrary playing sequence
\sigma
of length
l
. If
k
is large enough, then
\lambda
is of the form
\lambda=\small\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\gamma^{(s)}\\
\gamma^{(s)}\\
\beta\end{matrix}
for some
1\leq s\leq p
and
l(\alpha)+1\geq\sigma_{l}
. Moreover,
\alpha
is determined uniquely for all large
k
including the brackets, and if rule
1
applies to
R_{\sigma_{l}}
then the first
\gamma^{(s)}
is bracketed similarly to the root
\gamma^{(s)}
as in Remark
4.1
.

Proof.

Take
k\geq l+2
, and we start with
(\gamma^{(t)})^{k}=\small\begin{matrix}\gamma^{(t)}\\
\gamma^{(t)}\\
\beta\end{matrix}=\small\begin{matrix}\gamma^{(t)}_{1}\\
\gamma^{(t+1)}\\
\gamma^{(t+1)}\\
\beta^{\prime}\end{matrix}=\small\begin{matrix}\gamma^{(t)}_{1}\\
\gamma^{(t)}_{2}\\
\gamma^{(t+2)}\\
\gamma^{(t+2)}\\
\beta^{\prime\prime}\end{matrix}
, in which at most
3
first parts are playable by Proposition
4.1
. In each play, if rule
1
applies, then Proposition
4.3
guarantees the next state has the same form as in the proposition because in each play we only lose at most
1
block of
\gamma^{(s)}
for some
s
. Otherwise, rule
2
applies which does not change the tail (consisting of blocks of
\gamma^{(s)}
). Proposition
4.2
shows that rule
2
also determines the playable parts in the next state. The conclusion follows for all trees rooted at
(\gamma^{(t)})^{h}
for
h\geq k
, since
\sigma
only takes place in the first
k
blocks.
∎

Question 4.3
.

Why can we restrict the infinite vector to some finite prefix without losing any playable parts?

Answer 4.3
.

We defined earlier that
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{\infty}}

=\lim_{k\to\infty}

\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{k}}
. Because of Proposition
4.4
, we see that for a playing sequence
\sigma
and
k
large enough, the elements
\lambda_{h}=R_{\sigma}((\gamma^{(t)})^{h})
for
h\geq k
share the same prefix
\lambda=\small\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\gamma^{(s)}\end{matrix}
as in the statement of the proposition. Thus we can represent all those
\lambda_{h}
’s by that prefix
\lambda
in
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{\infty}}
. Actually,
\lambda
is in the form
\small\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\gamma^{(s)}\\
\gamma^{(s)}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}
so that rule
1
works for playing a part in the ”tail” of
\lambda
.
Rule
2
also works since each element
\lambda
has a tail of length
p\geq 3
and by Proposition
4.2
, the number of new bracketed parts is at most
3
, thus the lower parts are not affected.

The conclusion of this section is that, the finite reverse BS forest
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{k}}
coincides with the infinite digraph
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{\infty}}
obtained from the forest
\mathcal{F}_{P}
(by modifying any branch of
\mathcal{F}_{P}
as in Section
1
) up to at least level
k
. Therefore, we confirm that
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{\infty}}

=\lim_{k\to\infty}

\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{k}}
.

5
The limiting generating function for general
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{k}}
by level sizes

We recall our second main theorem from the Introduction.

Theorem
1.2
.

For primitive necklaces
P
with
\absolutevalue{P}\geq 3
, there is a power series
H_{P}
in
\mathbb{Z}[[x]]
such that the sequence of generating functions

(\mathcal{D}_{P^{k}})_{k=0}^{\infty}

converges to
H_{P}
coefficient-wise. Moreover,
H_{P}
is a rational function having denominator polynomial of degree at most
\absolutevalue{P}
and numerator degree at most
2\absolutevalue{P}
.

We also recall a property that was mentioned in Section
4
:

Claim 5.1
.

Each
\gamma^{(t)}\in\mathcal{C}_{P}
has at most
3
playable parts. Moreover, if
\gamma^{(t)}_{p}=1
, then
\gamma^{(t)}_{2}
is playable if and only if
\gamma^{(t)}_{2}=1
.

The next claim points out a “special” element in the recurrent set.

Claim 5.2
.

For any primitive necklace
P
of length at least
3
, there is at least one
\gamma^{(t)}\in\mathcal{C}_{P}

(and possibly more than one) of the form
\small\begin{matrix}\langle{\sigma}\rangle\\
0\\
\vdots\end{matrix},
where
\sigma\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

There are two cases:

1.

P
has two consecutive black beads, that means any
\gamma^{(t)}\in\mathcal{C}_{P}
has either two consecutive
1^{\prime}s
or a
1
at the top and a
1
at the bottom. Let
\gamma^{(t)}
be of the form
\,\small\begin{matrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
1\end{matrix}\,
, exists or else
P=B^{p}
not primitive. Thus
\gamma^{(t)}_{1}+p-1=p>p-1=l(\gamma^{(t)})-1
, so it is playable. Also, Claim
5.1
admits that
\gamma^{(t)}_{2}
is not playable.

2.

P
has two consecutive white beads, consider
\gamma^{(t)}=\small\begin{matrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
1\end{matrix}
. Thus
\gamma^{(t)}_{1}
is playable and Claim
5.1
admits that
\gamma^{(t)}_{2}
is not playable.

∎

The third claim regards a special situation that separates types of playing sequences:

Claim 5.3
.

Let
S
be the subtree
S
of the forest
\mathcal{F}_{P}
which is rooted at the element

\lambda=\small\begin{matrix}\lambda[:j]\\
\langle{\lambda_{j+1}}\rangle\\
0\\
\vdots\end{matrix}=\small\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\gamma^{(t)}\end{matrix}=\small\begin{matrix}\alpha\\
\langle{\sigma}\rangle\\
0\\
\vdots\\
1\end{matrix}

where
\alpha=\lambda[:j]
has
\alpha_{j}\geq 1
, with
\sigma\in\{0,1\}
and
\small\begin{matrix}\langle{\sigma}\rangle\\
0\\
\vdots\\
1\end{matrix}=\gamma^{(t)}
chosen as in Claim
5.2
. Then
S
has growth function

h(x)=A(x)+B(x)g_{t}(x)

where
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree at most
j
.

Proof.

If
\langle{\sigma}\rangle
is bracketed, any parts of
\alpha
are bracketed, due to rule
3
. Since
\lambda_{j}=\alpha_{j}\geq 1>=0=\lambda_{j+2}
and
\sigma
is the only playable part in the segment
\gamma^{(t)}
, by the proof of Proposition
4.2
and rule
1
, the part
\lambda_{j+2}=\gamma^{(t)}_{2}=0
cannot be playable any time before
\sigma
is played, and the same for any entries below it. Let
\delta
be a playing sequence starting at
\lambda
. Let
ind_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t)
be the index of
\sigma
in
R_{\delta[:(t-1)]}(\lambda)
. We have two cases:

1.

Play
\lambda\xrightarrow{\delta}R_{\delta}(\lambda)
where
\delta_{i}\leq ind_{\sigma}^{\delta}(i)
for all
i
. Clearly,
l(\delta)\leq j
because we delete the played entry in each operation, by rule
2
. Let
A(x)
be the growth function for this set of elements obtained from performing such playing sequences.

2.

Part
\sigma
is played at some time; assume
\lambda\xrightarrow{\delta}R_{\delta}(\lambda)
where
\delta_{i}=ind_{\sigma}^{\delta}(i)
. Because
\alpha_{j}\geq 1
, the rule
3
confirms that in any states before playing
R_{\delta_{i}}
, the immediate part above the tail segment
\gamma^{(t)}
is at least
1
. Hence
R_{\delta[:i]}(\lambda)
results in
\small\begin{matrix}\beta\\
\gamma^{(t+1)}\end{matrix}
, in which the lowest part of
\beta
is at least

and we claim that
\beta
has

parts. The reason for this claim is because we erase one part of

cannot make the

at top of

. From then, if any

The playing sequences

forms a copy of

, while playing entries of

Proof of
Theorem
1.2
.

We only need to connect the dots by proving that every branch of the tree
G_{s}
rooted at
\gamma^{(s)}
will eventually hit the element of the form
\lambda
as in Claim
5.3
. In
\mathcal{C}_{P}
,
one cycles the positions of the
1
’s to obtain the successive elements
\gamma^{(s)}
, so in any
G_{s}
with
s\neq t
, the first part
\gamma^{(t)}_{1}
is at some position
\gamma^{(s)}_{j+1}
(
1\leq j\leq p-1
). We can append some entries at the end of
\gamma^{(s)}
so that the tail is
\gamma^{(t)}
. Hence the Claim

applies, with

at most
p-1
.

Furthermore,
g_{t}(x)=1+xg_{t+1}(x)
because
\gamma^{(t)}_{1}
is the only playable part in
\gamma^{(t)}
. Now there are polynomials

of degree at most

implying there are polynomials
P,Q
of degree at most
p
such that

g_{t+1}(x)=\dfrac{M(x)}{N(x)}.

Then
g_{t}(x)
is rational with denominator of degree at most
p
and numerator of degree at most

. Specifically,

g_{t}(x)=\dfrac{N(x)+xM(x)}{N(x)}.

Any other
g_{s}(x)

is of the form

g_{s}(x)=A(x)+B(x)g_{t}(x)=\dfrac{AN+BN+xBM}{N},

where

are polynomials of integer coefficients of degree at most

. Thus any such
g_{s}
is rational with denominator of degree at most
p
and numerator of degree at most
2p-1
. Let
g
be the growth function of the
P
quasi-infinite forest, then
g=\sum_{s=1}^{p}g_{s}
is rational with denominator
Q(x)
and numerator of degree at most
2p-1
.

Now we want to discard a copy of the forest coming from the branches
[1\ldots]
in each tree
G_{s}
to get the limit generating function

of
\mathcal{O}^{op}_{P^{\infty}}
. Thus

g(x)=H_{P}(x)+xg(x)

and then

H_{P}(x)=(1-x)g(x)

a rational function with denominator of degree at most
p
and numerator of degree at most
2p
.

∎

The precise limiting generating functions

and

were computed in detail the author’s bachelor thesis

\displaystyle H_{BWW}(x)=H_{BBW}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-x)\dfrac{x^{3}-3x^{2}-4x-3}{2x^{3}+x^{2}-1},

\displaystyle H_{BWWW}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-x)\dfrac{x^{5}+8x^{4}-3x^{3}-8x^{2}-6x-4}{6x^{4}+4x^{3}+x^{2}-1},

\displaystyle H_{BBBW}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-x)\dfrac{x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1}{3x^{4}+2x^{3}+x^{2}-1},

\displaystyle H_{BWWWW}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-x)\dfrac{2x^{6}+16x^{5}-12x^{4}-23x^{3}-16x^{2}-8x-5}{12x^{5}+8x^{4}+2x^{3}-1}.

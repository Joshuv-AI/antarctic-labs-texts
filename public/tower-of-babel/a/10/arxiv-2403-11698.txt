Spin-Orbital Ordering in Alkali Superoxides
Kohei Shibata, Makoto Naka, Harald O. Jeschke, Junya Otsuki

Abstract
Akali superoxides A O 2 \text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ( A = Na A=\textrm{Na} , K, Rb, Cs), due to an open p p shell of the oxygen ion O 2 \text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} - with degenerate π \pi orbitals, have spin and orbital degrees of freedom. The complex magnetic, orbital, and structural phase transitions observed experimentally in this family of materials are only partially understood. Based on density functional theory, we derive a strong-coupling effective model for the isostructural compounds A O 2 \text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ( A = K A=\textrm{K} , Rb, Cs) from a two-orbital Hubbard model. We find that CsO 2 \text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} has highly frustrated exchange interactions in the a a - b b plane, while the frustration is weaker for RbO 2 \text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and KO 2 \text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . We solve the resulting Kugel-Khomskii model in the mean-field approximation. We show that CsO 2 \text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} exhibits an antiferro-orbital (AFO) order with the ordering vector 𝒒 = ( 1 , 0 , 0 ) \bm{q}=(1,0,0) and a stripe antiferromagnetic order with 𝒒 = ( 1 / 2 , 0 , 0 ) \bm{q}=(1/2,0,0) , which is consistent with recent neutron scattering experiments. We discuss the role of the π \pi -orbital degrees of freedom for the experimentally observed magnetic transitions and interpret the as-yet-unidentified T s2 = 70 T_{\mathrm{s2}}=70 K transition in CsO 2 \text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} as an orbital ordering transition.



I
Introduction

\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a unique molecule that possesses a magnetic moment
S=1
on its own.
Solid oxygen, in which
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules are aggregated by the van der Waals force, exhibits a variety of electronic properties such as antiferromagnetism, metal-to-insulator transition
Freiman and Jodl 2004
, and superconductivity
Shimizu et al. 1998
under temperature and pressure variations.

Another interesting system composed of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules is an ionic crystal, where
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules act as electron acceptors for the counter metal ions. Alkali superoxides
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
A=\textrm{Na}
, K, Rb, Cs) are famous examples of such compounds
Hesse et al. 1989
.
The
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-
ion has three electrons in the anti-bonding
\pi_{g}^{\ast}
orbitals, which consist of two orbital states with symmetries similar to
d_{zx}
and
d_{yz}
. Hence, one hole per
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-
molecule having spin
S=1/2
and orbital degrees of freedom dominate the low-temperature physical properties.
As a consequence, spin and orbital physics as in
d
electron systems are expected.

Electronically, alkali superoxides exhibit insulating behavior for all temperatures.
Since the unit cell contains an odd number of electrons, the insulating behavior is ascribed to the Coulomb repulsion.
A first-principles assessment with dynamical mean-field theory for
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
concluded that
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is indeed a Mott insulator
Kováčik et al. 2012
.
This indicates that
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
A=\textrm{K}
, Rb, Cs) are strongly correlated electron systems consisting of
\pi
electrons.

Three compounds
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, and
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with the exception of
\text{NaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
take the same crystal structure at room temperature
Zumsteg et al. 1974
;
Bösch and Känzig 1975
;
Ziegler et al. 1976
;
Labhart et al. 1979
;
Miyajima
.
The temperature variation of crystal structure and magnetic properties is summarized in Fig.
1
.
Above 400 K,
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
exhibits the cubic NaCl-type (
Fm\bar{3}m
, no. 225) crystal structure, in which
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules are disoriented (phase I).
At around room temperature, the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules are oriented parallel to the
c

\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
becomes tetragonal (

a=b<c
.
Figure

Phases below 200 K are material dependent, although there is a tendency that a smaller alkali radius leads to a lower symmetry.

\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
undergoes two steps of symmetry lowering to monoclinic at
T=196
K and to triclinic at
T\simeq 10
K

.

\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

The magnetic structure of

For
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

On the other hand,
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
shows peculiar magnetic properties.
The susceptibility
\chi(T)

\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

down to

Miyajima et al. 2018

It is reported that

;
Miyajima et al. 2018
.
At
T=T_{\mathrm{N}}=9.6

Labhart et al. 1979
.
Recent neutron scattering experiments revealed a stripe-type magnetic structure
Nakano et al. 2023
;
Ewings et al. 2023
. Two experiments proposed different propagation vectors
\bm{q}=(0,1/2,0)

Nakano et al. 2023
and
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)

a<b
.

These experimental results demonstrate the diverse structural and magnetic properties in
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
A=\textrm{K}
, Rb, Cs).
For a comprehensive understanding, the following two issues need to be addressed:
(i) What is the relevant microscopic control parameter that governs the physical properties in
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
?
An apparent parameter that systematically changes for different
A
atoms is the lattice parameter, which increases in the order of K, Rb, and Cs. However, it is highly nontrivial why the short-range correlations are clearly observed only in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which has the largest
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
–
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
distance.
We thus raise a more specific issue: (ii) What is the electronic state of
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
? The role of the orbitals in the magnetic properties is of particular interest.

Theoretical studies have addressed the electronic structure in
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Kováčik and Ederer 2009
;
Ylvisaker et al. 2010
;
Wohlfeld et al. 2011
;
Kováčik et al. 2012
and
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Solovyev 2008
;
Nandy et al. 2010
;
Kim et al. 2010
;
Kim and Min 2014
;
Sikora et al. 2020
.
Regarding the correlated magnetic behavior in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, Riyadi
et al.
proposed a zigzag orbital ordered state
Riyadi et al. 2012
.
By assuming only hopping between
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-
\pi_{g}^{*}
and Cs-
5p
, they argue that superexchange interaction is allowed only on a one-dimensional zigzag path in the
a
-
b
plane.
The magnetic properties have been investigated by NMR
Klanjšek et al. 2015
, electron paramagnetic resonance
Knaflič et al. 2015
, and high-field magnetization measurement
Miyajima et al. 2018
.

In this paper, we derive an effective spin-orbital model for
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
based on first-principles calculations.
We will demonstrate that geometrical frustration is the key element that constitutes a difference between
A
atoms: The frustration plays a crucial role in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
but is less important in
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
With a mean-field calculation, we will propose an alternative type of orbital order in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
that leads to the magnetic state with the experimentally observed stripe AFM order.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
We first derive the electronic structure of
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and an approximate tight-binding model in Sec. II.
Using perturbation theory, we derive an effective model describing intersite spin and orbital interactions in Sec. III.
Possible spin and orbital phase transitions are identified using the mean-field (MF) approximation in Sec. IV.
Based on these results, we discuss implications for
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in Sec. V.
Results are summarized in Sec. VI.

\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is characterized by two-dimensional frustration between
l=a
and
a+b
bonds. This frustration is largest in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and tends to get weaker for smaller alkali ions.

III
Strong-coupling effective model

Since
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
A=\textrm{K}
, Rb, Cs) are Mott insulators
Kováčik et al. 2012
, we employ a strong-coupling effective model that describes intersite exchange interactions between localized spin and orbital degrees of freedom.
We first compare two kinds of exchange interactions, and then derive an explicit form of the Hamiltonian.

III.1
Comparison between two perturbation processes for the exchange interactions

There are two perturbation processes that give rise to intersite exchange interactions between
\pi_{g}^{*}
electrons on the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
ions.
One is the second-order process of the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
–
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
hopping, and the other is the fourth-order superexchange process of the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
–
\text{Cs}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
hopping.
The coupling constant in the second-order process,
J^{(2)}
, is estimated to be

\displaystyle J^{(2)}\sim\frac{t^{2}}{U},

(5)

where
U
is the Coulomb repulsion between two electrons on the same orbital.
On the other hand, the coupling constant in the fourth-order process through
\text{Cs}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions,
J^{(4)}
, is estimated to be

\displaystyle J^{(4)}\sim\frac{(t_{\textrm{Cs-O}_{2}})^{4}}{U\Delta_{5p}^{2}},

(6)

where
t_{\textrm{Cs-O}_{2}}
is the hopping amplitude between the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-
\pi_{g}^{*}
orbital and the Cs-
5p
orbital.
We estimated
t_{\textrm{Cs-O}_{2}}
by projecting the energy dispersion in Fig.
4
onto an eight-band tight-binding model consisting of
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-
\pi_{g}^{*}
,
\pi_{u}
,
\sigma_{g}
, and Cs-
5p
orbitals.
We thus concluded that
t_{\textrm{Cs-O}_{2}}
is the same order as
t
in the two-band model, namely,
t\sim t_{\textrm{Cs-O}_{2}}
.
Hence, the ratio between
J^{(2)}
and
J^{(4)}
is estimated as

\displaystyle\frac{J^{(4)}}{J^{(2)}}\sim\frac{t^{2}}{\Delta_{5p}^{2}}\sim 10^{-4}.

(7)

Here, we used
t\sim 0.1
eV (Table
2
) and
\Delta_{5p}\sim 8
eV (Fig.
4
).
Therefore, we consider from now on only the second-order process from the direct
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
–
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
hopping, neglecting the fourth-order superexchange process through Cs.

III.2
Derivation of the interactions

In order to derive the spin-orbital exchange interactions, we begin with the two-orbital Hubbard model consisting of the
\pi_{g}^{*}
orbitals,
\pi_{a}
and
\pi_{b}
. The Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\sum_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\sigma}t^{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}_{ij}(c^{{\dagger}}_{i\gamma\sigma}c_{j\gamma^{\prime}\sigma}+\textrm{H.c.})+U\sum_{i\gamma}n_{i\gamma\uparrow}n_{i\gamma\downarrow}

\displaystyle+U^{\prime}\sum_{i\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}}n_{i\gamma}n_{i\gamma^{\prime}}+J_{\mathrm{H}}\sum_{i\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}}c^{{\dagger}}_{i\gamma\sigma}c^{{\dagger}}_{i\gamma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i\gamma\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i\gamma^{\prime}\sigma}

\displaystyle+J^{\prime}_{\mathrm{H}}\sum_{i\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}}c^{{\dagger}}_{i\gamma\uparrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{i\gamma\downarrow}c_{i\gamma^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{i\gamma^{\prime}\uparrow},

(8)

where

c_{i\gamma\sigma}
is the annihilation operator for site
i
, orbital
\gamma
, and spin
\sigma
, and

n_{i\gamma\sigma}=c^{{\dagger}}_{i\gamma\sigma}c_{i\gamma\sigma}
is the number operator.
The first term represents the electron hopping between site
i
and site
j
.
The symbol
\langle ij\rangle
stands for the pairs of neighboring
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
sites shown in Fig.
5
.
The second and third terms represent the intra-orbital and inter-orbital Coulomb repulsion, respectively.
The fourth and fifth terms represent the Hund’s rule coupling and the pair hopping interaction, respectively.

We consider the Mott insulating state with the occupation number
n=3
per site.
For convenience, we treat this as
n=1
from now on, using a hole picture.
Note that the relation between orbital states and lattice distortion is reversed in the hole picture, though the effective Hamiltonian derived below is the same for
n=1
and
n=3
.
The local electronic degrees of freedom in the Mott insulating state are described by the spin and orbital (pseudospin) operators defined by

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\gamma\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i\gamma\sigma}^{{\dagger}}\bm{\sigma}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i\gamma\sigma^{\prime}},

(9)

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\sigma}c_{i\gamma\sigma}^{{\dagger}}\bm{\sigma}_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}c_{i\gamma^{\prime}\sigma},

(10)

where
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma^{x},\sigma^{y},\sigma^{z})
is the Pauli matrix.
The eigenstates of
T^{z}
are
\ket{\pi_a}
and
\ket{\pi_b}
orbitals, which extend along the
a
and
b
axes, respectively [Fig.
2
(b)].
On the other hand, the eigenstates of
T^{x}
describe
\ket{\pi_{a+b}}
and
\ket{\pi_{a-b}}
orbitals that extend to the diagonal direction [Fig.
2
(b)], because the (
\pi_{a+b}
,
\pi_{a-b}
) basis is obtained by linear combination of (
\pi_{a}
,
\pi_{b}
) as given in Eq. (
1
).
In general, we can describe the orbital state in an arbitrary direction by rotating
T^{z}
and
T^{x}
in the pseudospin space as

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{z}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=T^{z}\cos\varphi+T^{x}\sin\varphi,

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{x}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=-T^{z}\sin\varphi+T^{x}\cos\varphi.

(11)

Figure
7
shows the variation of the eigenstates of the operators
\widetilde{T}^{z}(\varphi)
and
\widetilde{T}^{x}(\varphi)
. The orbital state is rotated by
\varphi/2
around the
c

In second-order perturbation theory around the atomic limit with respect to the hopping, we can derive Kugel-Khomskii (KK) interactions
Kugel’ and Khomskiĭ 1982
for alkali superoxides. We present only the result below, and refer readers to Refs.
Maekawa et al. 2004
;
Igoshev et al. 2023
for general derivation of the KK interaction. The effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
for the
n=1
subspace of the
\pi_{g}^{\ast}
system is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{1}^{l}\left(\frac{3}{4}+\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\right)\left\{\left[1+\left(r^{l}\right)^{2}\right]\left(\frac{1}{4}-\tau_{i}^{l}\tau_{j}^{l}\right)-r^{l}\left(\tau_{i}^{l+}\tau_{j}^{l-}+\tau_{i}^{l-}\tau_{j}^{l+}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{2}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\right)\left\{\left[1+\left(r^{l}\right)^{2}\right]\left(\frac{1}{4}-\tau_{i}^{l}\tau_{j}^{l}\right)+r^{l}\left(\tau_{i}^{l+}\tau_{j}^{l-}+\tau_{i}^{l-}\tau_{j}^{l+}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{2}^{{}^{\prime}l}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\right)\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\tau_{i}^{l}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\tau_{j}^{l}\right)+\left(r^{l}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\tau_{i}^{l}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\tau_{j}^{l}\right)-r^{l}\left(\tau_{i}^{l+}\tau_{j}^{l+}+\tau_{i}^{l-}\tau_{j}^{l-}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-2\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{3}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\right)\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}+\tau_{i}^{l}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\tau_{j}^{l}\right)+\left(r^{l}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\tau_{i}^{l}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\tau_{j}^{l}\right)+r^{l}\left(\tau_{i}^{l+}\tau_{j}^{l+}+\tau_{i}^{l-}\tau_{j}^{l-}\right)\right],

(12)

where
l
is the bond index that takes the values
a
,
b
,
a+b
,
a-b
,
BC
, or
\overline{\textrm{BC}}
, depending on the combination of

and

(Fig.

Here, we introduced

and

and

(see below).
The coupling constants
J_{1}^{l},J_{2}^{l},J_{2}^{{}^{\prime}l}
, and

are given by

\displaystyle J_{1}^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(t_{\pi}^{l})^{2}}{U^{{}^{\prime}}-J_{\mathrm{H}}},

\displaystyle J_{2}^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(t_{\pi}^{l})^{2}}{U^{{}^{\prime}}+J_{\mathrm{H}}},

\displaystyle{J_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(t_{\pi}^{l})^{2}}{U-J_{\mathrm{H}}^{{}^{\prime}}},

\displaystyle J_{3}^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(t_{\pi}^{l})^{2}}{U+J_{\mathrm{H}}^{{}^{\prime}}}.

(13)

The operators
\tau^{l}
and
\tau^{l\pm}
are bond-dependent orbital operators defined by

\displaystyle\tau^{l}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{T}^{z}(2\varphi_{l}),

\displaystyle\tau^{l\pm}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{T}^{x}(2\varphi_{l})\pm iT^{y},

(14)

where

is the azimuthal angle around the

\varphi_{a}=0
,

,
\varphi_{b}=\pi/2
, and
\varphi_{a-b}=\varphi_{\mathrm{\overline{BC}}}=3\pi/4
.
The coupling constants satisfy
J_{1}^{l}>J_{2}^{l}\simeq J_{2}^{{}^{\prime}l}>J_{3}^{l}
in a typical choice of parameters. Details are given in Appendix
A
.

Equation (

\delta

.
Without

terms, namely, inserting

-

Kugel’ and Khomskiĭ 1982
;
Feiner et al. 1997
;
Khaliullin and Oudovenko 1997
;

;

;

.
We note that the definition of the bond-dependent orbital operator

in Eq. (

orbital systems, since the rotation of the
\pi_{g}^{*}
orbitals follows the rule in Eq. (

orbitals.
The KK-type interaction for
\pi
electron systems has also been derived in the context of organic conductors
Naka and Ishihara 2010
;
Hotta 2010
and
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

;

For the

The difference between

(

Mean-field calculations

IV.1
Calculation details

We apply the mean-field (MF) approximation to search for possible phase transitions emerging in the effective model in Eq. (
12

The order parameters include
\expectationvalue*{T_i^{\xi}}
,
\expectationvalue{S_i^z}
, and
\expectationvalue*{S_i^z T_i^{\xi}}

V
Discussion

V.1
Role of the orbital degree of freedom

In this section, we first discuss the role of the orbital degree of freedom in our results.
In particular, we focus on phase C (stripe-AFM + 3D-AFO order), which corresponds to the orthorhombic phase of CsO
2
, and consider the origin of the phase transitions.
The exchange interactions on all three kinds of bonds are relevant.
The order of their strengths is
|J^{\mathrm{BC}}|>|J^{a}|\simeq|J^{a+b}|
as shown in Fig.
6
.
The leading interaction
J^{\mathrm{BC}}
leads to the 3D-AFO ordered state as demonstrated in the orbital-only model in Appendix
B
.
The spin correlations are then considered on top of the 3D-AFO ordered state.

Figure 18:
Values of the coupling constants
J_{ij}
in units of
t^{2}/U
in the effective Heisenberg model in Eq. (
17
). The orange and blue indicate AFM and FM interactions, respectively.
(a) 3D-AFO ordered state, (b) disordered state.
The parameter set for the orthorhombic
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in (ii) of Table
2
was used.

For this purpose, we derive the effective spin-spin interactions represented by the Heisenberg Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{spin}}=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{ij}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j},

(17)

by eliminating the orbital operators from
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
in Eq. (
12
).
We estimate
J_{ij}
by replacing the orbital operators
T_{i}^{z}
and
T_{i}^{x}
with their expectation values. In the 3D-AFO ordered state in phase C, for example,
T_{i}^{x}
is replaced by
+1/2
or
-1/2
depending on the site and
T_{i}^{z}
is replaced by 0 for all sites (the canting of the orbital is ignored). Figure
18
(a) shows the exchange interactions
J_{ij}
obtained in the 3D-AFO ordered state.
The leading interaction turns out to be the AFM interaction on the diagonal bond in the
a
-
b
plane (
l=a+b
), which favors the stripe-AFM ordering
Jin et al. 2013
;
Glasbrenner et al. 2015
.
For comparison, we estimated
J_{ij}
in the disordered state by replacing all orbital operators with zero in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
The result is presented in Fig.
18
(b). The strengths of
J_{ij}
in the disordered state are simply determined by the hopping amplitude. Therefore, the
l=\textrm{BC}
bond has the largest AFM interaction, which does not enhance the stripe-AFM order. The comparison between Figs.
18
(a) and (b) clearly demonstrates that the orbital order in the 3D-AFO ordered state is relevant for the emergence of the stripe-AFM state. Sensitivity of the magnetic order to the presence or absence of orbital order has been observed for
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Nandy et al. 2010
.

Finally, we consider the direction of the stripe-AFM state under the orthorhombic distortion.
The external orthorhombic distortion with
a<b
tilts the
\pi_{a+b}
and
\pi_{a-b}
orbitals to the
a
axis (we again note that we are considering holes).
Therefore, the interaction on the
a
bond becomes predominant over the
b
bond since the
a
(
b
) bond is described more by
\pi
(
\delta
) hopping under the distortion.
The KK mechanism explains the AF spin configuration on the ferro-orbital configuration on the
a
bond, which leads to the stripe-AFM order with the translation vector
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
.

V.2
Implication for experiments

V.2.1
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Recent neutron scattering experiments for
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reported the stripe-AFM order with propagation vector
\bm{q}=(0,1/2,0)

Nakano et al. 2023
or
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)

Ewings et al. 2023
in the orthorhombic structure with
a<b
.
Our results for phase C and other phases having the stripe-AFM ordered configuration exhibit
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
because the KK interaction on the
l=a
bond favors
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
over
\bm{q}=(0,1/2,0)
as discussed above.

The DFT estimate is located in phase C but close to phases A, B, I, and F
′
in the phase diagram in Fig.
15
. Among those nearby phases, I and F
′
have the stripe-AFM configuration. However, it is not a pure stripe-AFM order but a stack of the stripe-AFM and the 2D-AFM orders.
In these phases, neutron scattering experiments should observe not only
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
but also
\bm{q}=(1/2,1/2,0)
. Since the peak at
\bm{q}=(1/2,1/2,0)
has not been observed, we exclude phases I and F
′
and propose only phase C as a candidate for
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

We turn our attention to the finite-temperature properties in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
Experimentally, there are three phase transitions,
T_{\mathrm{s1}}
,
T_{\mathrm{s2}}
, and
T_{\mathrm{N}}
, in phases II–III (Fig.
1
).
On the other hand, we obtained two phase transitions in phase C of our model: the stripe-AFM transition at
T_{\mathrm{M}}
and the 3D-AFO order transition at
T_{\mathrm{O}}
[Fig.
17
(a) for tetragonal structure and Fig.
17
(b) for orthorhombic structure].
The energy unit
J\equiv t^{2}/U
is estimated to be
J\approx 21
K using the DFT value
t=81
meV for the orthorhombic
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in (ii) of Table
2
and
U=3.55
eV
Solovyev 2008
. Hence,
T_{\mathrm{M}}
and
T_{\mathrm{O}}
in Fig.
17
(b) are converted to
T_{\mathrm{M}}\approx 8.4
K and
T_{\mathrm{O}}\approx 59
K.
We identify
T_{\mathrm{M}}
with
T_{\mathrm{N}}
since the calculated magnetic structure and the transition temperature is consistent with the experiment.
We further identify
T_{\mathrm{O}}
with
T_{\mathrm{s2}}
as presented in Fig.
19
since the AFO ordered state for
T_{\mathrm{M}}<T<T_{\mathrm{O}}
does not give rise to a global lattice distortion as shown in Fig.
17
(a).
The experimental structural phase transition from tetragonal to orthorhombic at
T=T_{\mathrm{s1}}
is ascribed to an origin that is not considered in our effective model, e.g., the Jahn-Teller effect. Describing this transition would require a model that also includes lattice degrees of freedom.

Figure 19:
Comparison between the experimental and theoretical finite-
T
phase diagrams.

In this scenario, the magnetic properties observed for
T_{\mathrm{N}}<T<T_{\mathrm{s2}}
should be explained by the 3D-AFO ordered state.
Experimentally, the temperature dependence of the susceptibility for
T_{\mathrm{N}}<T<T_{\mathrm{s2}}
is well fitted by the Bonner-Fisher function
Riyadi et al. 2012
;
Miyajima et al. 2018
, which was developed to fit the one-dimensional Heisenberg model. In our model with
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
–
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
hopping, none of the ordered states in Fig.
11
gives a one-dimensional hopping path.
We note that even the stripe-AFO ordered state proposed in Ref.
Riyadi et al. 2012
(e.g.,
z=0
plane in phase F) is not one-dimensional in the presence of the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
–
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
direct hopping.
Considering the fact that the Bonner-Fisher curve of the susceptibility can be observed in a wide range of Heisenberg models
Zheng et al. 2005
,
we expect that the susceptibility could be reproduced by the frustrated Heisenberg model on top of the 3D-AFO order as presented in Fig.
18
(a).
For this, strong correlations and thermal fluctuations beyond the MF approximation need to be included which is beyond the scope of the present study.

The neutron diffraction experiment in Ref.
Ewings et al. 2023
also suggests doubling of the unit cell along the
a
axis for
T\leq T_{\rm s2}
,
which is attributed to displacements of Cs and O
2
ions along the
b
axis with the propagation vector
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
.
On the other hand, in the present calculation, the stripe-AFO order with
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
has not been obtained within the realistic parameter range.
Therefore, this structural change is expected to originate from the instability of the background lattice system rather than the correlated
\pi
-electron system.

Finally, we refer to the experimental indication for the stripe-AFM + 3D-AFO ordered state of phase C in CsO
2
.
As shown in Fig.
17
, the orbital order parameter
\expectationvalue{T^z}_{\mathrm{F}}
corresponding to the orthorhombic distortion is enhanced below
T_{\rm M}
, accompanied by the development of the stripe-AFM order.
This is because in phase C the orbital on each site tends to cant uniformly towards the
a
axis
to gain the AFM exchange interaction along the
a
axis, parallel to the spin propagation vector.
This canting of the orbital moment can be detected as the elongation and contraction of the
a
and
b
axes, respectively, as the temperature decreases through
T_{\rm N}
.
This prediction provides a good validation of our scenario in experiments.

V.2.2
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Regarding
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, the spin structure has not been resolved experimentally (apart from a study of oxygen deficient
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Riyadi et al. 2011
). Our results in Fig.
16
predict phase B, namely, the 2D-AFM order with
\bm{q}=(1/2,1/2,0)
on top of the 3D-AFO order, which is the same orbital order as in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. In
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, magnetic order occurs in the monoclinic phase which is beyond the scope of the present study. A previous theoretical study
Wohlfeld et al. 2011
discussed the 3D-AFO + 2D-AFM order (phase D in Fig.
10
) for
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. It is close to our DFT estimates, but in-plane hopping would need to be stronger for its realization.

VI
Summary

We investigated the spin-orbital order in
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
A=\textrm{Cs}
, Rb, K) using a strong-coupling effective model derived based on first-principles calculations.
Relevant interactions between the
\pi_{g}^{\ast}
orbitals on the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecule are up to third neighbor for tetragonal and up to fourth neighbor for orthorhombic structures. It is common to all
A
atoms that the interaction between the corner and body-center sites (
l=\textrm{BC}
) is the largest.
The difference in
A
atoms can be seen in the
a
-
b
plane.
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
has highly frustrated interactions between the
a
(
b
) bond and the diagonal
a+b
bond, while
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{KO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
have weaker frustration.
We conclude that a relevant microscopic control parameter that distinguishes the low-temperature properties in
\text{A\/O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is the magnitude of the geometrical frustration in the
a
-
b
plane (
\phi
in our notation).

The MF calculations for the strong-coupling effective model reveal possible ground states in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Based on this, we propose a 3D-AFO order of
\pi_{a+b}
and
\pi_{a-b}
orbitals with the ordering vector
\bm{q}=(1,0,0)
below
T=T_{\mathrm{s2}}\simeq 70
K. This orbital order is caused by the leading interaction on the BC bond. The subleading interactions (those in the
a
-
b
plane) determine the spin structure. The stripe-AFM state with
\bm{q}=(1/2,0,0)
is realized due to the frustrated interactions in the
a
-
b
plane. We predict that the canting of the orbital moment towards the
a
axis, which is consistent with the orthorhombic distortion with
a<b
, is enhanced, i.e., the lattice distortion increases, by the stripe-AFM order below
T_{\mathrm{N}}=9.6
K.
For
\text{RbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we predict the 2D-AFM order with
\bm{q}=(1/2,1/2,0)
due to weaker frustration.

The peculiar magnetic properties below
T=T_{\mathrm{s2}}
in
\text{CsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
remain unsolved in the present MF calculations. Our results suggest that the temperature dependence of the susceptibility fitted by the Bonner-Fisher curve should be explained by another mechanism such as geometrical frustration in systems with two or three dimensions. The fact that the observed magnetic moment is considerably reduced from
S=1/2

Nakano et al. 2023
;
Ewings et al. 2023
indicates the importance of correlations in the magnetic properties, which is left for future work.

Appendix B
Orbital-only model

The effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
in Eq. (
12
) consists of the spin and orbital operators. Here, we consider an orbital-only model by setting
\bm{S}_{i}=0
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
Figure
21
shows the ground-state phase diagram of the orbital-only model in the
(\phi,\theta)
plane.
This figure explains the tendency of the orbital order in Fig.
10
.

Figure 21:
The ground-state phase diagram and orbital configuration in the orbital-only model. The parameters are the same as in Fig.
10
.

There are three phases.
The 3D-AFO state of the
(\pi_{a+b},\pi_{a-b})
orbital is stabilized due to the interaction between the corner site and the body-center site, which is dominant near
\theta=0
.
The region near
\theta=90^{\circ}
is divided into two phases with a two-dimensional character.
The 2D-AFO state of the
(\pi_{a},\pi_{b})
orbital is stabilized below
\phi\simeq 53^{\circ}
by the interaction along the
a
axis and
b
axis.
The diagonal interaction in the
a
-
b
plane, which is dominant near
\phi=90^{\circ}
, stabilizes the stripe-AFO order of the
(\pi_{a+b},\pi_{a-b})
orbital.

In the main text,

Figure
22

The cut of Fig.

10

It turns out that

Comparison between Fig.

This tendency can be understood as follows. As

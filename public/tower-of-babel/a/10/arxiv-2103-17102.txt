1.
Introduction

The celebrated Beurling theorem (see
[
5
]
) gives the structure of
shift invariant subspaces in the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
over the unit disc:
A non-zero closed subspace
\mathcal{S}
of

H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant for
M_{z}
if and only if there exists an
inner function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\mathcal{S}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

In particular, we can represent
\mathcal{S}
as

\mathcal{S}=\mathop{\oplus}_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}(\mathcal{S}\ominus z\mathcal{S}).

A proper closed subspace
\mathcal{Q}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is said to be a Jordan block of

H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if
\mathcal{Q}
is
M_{z}^{*}
-invariant. Now
\mathcal{Q}
is
M_{z}^{*}
-invariant if and only if
\mathcal{Q}^{\perp}
is
M_{z}
-invariant. Thus by Beurling’s theorem, there exists
an inner function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
\mathcal{Q}^{\perp}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
Therefore the Jordan block of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is determined by
\theta
and is given by
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Now we are going to define Jordan block of the Hardy space over the unit polydisc.
It is well known that the
n
-tuple
of multiplication operators
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
on the Hardy space

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
n>1
over the unit polydisc is doubly commuting. A proper closed subspace
\mathcal{Q}

of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is said to be a Jordan block of

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
n>1
if
\mathcal{Q}
is invariant under the adjoint of
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})

and the
n
-tuple
(P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{1}}|_{\mathcal{Q}},\ldots,P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{n}}|_{\mathcal{Q}})
is doubly commuting.
Firstly, Douglas and Yang (see
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
)
started the study of Jordan block
of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
over the bidisc.
After that, Izuchi, Nakazi and Seto
[
21
]

classified the Jordan block of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Recently, J. Sarkar
[
26
]

has given a complete characterization of the Jordan block of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
:
A subspace
\mathcal{Q}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a Jordan block if and only if

\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{n}}
, where
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{i}}

is a Jordan block of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

The structure of joint invariant subspaces in the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
over the bidisc
and in general the Hardy space over the polydisc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
n>1
is very complex. That is because of the subtleties of theory of holomorphic functions in several variables as well as the difficulty associated with the structure of

n
-tuples,
n>1
, of commuting isometries on Hilbert spaces.
There are several mathematicians like Agrawal,
Clark, and Douglas
[
2
]
, Ahern and Clark
[
3
]
, Guo

[
12
,
13
]
, Guo, Sun, Zheng and Zhong
[
14
]
,
Rudin
[
27
]
, Izuchi
[
16
]
, Mandrekar

[
24
]
etc. (also see
[
18
,
17
,
19
,
7
]
and the references therein)
have studied the structure
of joint invariant subspaces.
The four
operators
R_{z_{1}},R_{z_{2}}
,
C_{z_{1}}
, and
C_{z_{2}}
play an important role in finding the structure of (joint) invariant subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
,
where

R_{z_{1}}=M_{{z_{1}}}|_{\mathcal{S}}
and
R_{z_{2}}=M_{{z_{2}}}|_{\mathcal{S}}
,
C_{z_{1}}=P_{\mathcal{S}^{\perp}}M_{{z_{1}}}|_{\mathcal{S}^{\perp}}
,
and
C_{z_{2}}=P_{\mathcal{S}^{\perp}}M_{{z_{2}}}|_{\mathcal{S}^{\perp}}
.
For example,
Mandrekar
[
24
]
showed that if the commutator
[R_{z_{1}},R_{{z_{2}}}^{*}]=0
,
then
\mathcal{S}=\varphi H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
for some inner function
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
.
Again if
[C_{z_{1}},C_{{z_{2}}}^{*}]=0

or
\mbox{rank}[C_{z_{1}},C_{{z_{2}}}^{*}]=1
,
then the structure of
\mathcal{S}
is known (see
[
21
]
).
But for
\mbox{rank}[C_{z_{1}},C_{{z_{2}}}^{*}]>1
,
the structure of
\mathcal{S}
is not known.
Motivated by the above fact, Izuchi et. al. (see
[
20
]
) firstly introduced
the concept of
mixed invariant
subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
:
A closed subspace

\mathcal{S}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
is said to be
mixed invariant
if

M_{z_{1}}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{S}
and
M^{*}_{z_{2}}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{S}
.
Clearly, if
\mathcal{S}
is
joint
(M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}}^{*})
invariant, then
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})\ominus\mathcal{S}
is again a joint

(M_{z_{1}}^{*},M_{z_{2}})
invariant.
Thus a natural question arises in the setting of the Hardy space over the unit polydisc:

What is the structure of

doubly commuting mixed invariant
subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
n>1
?

More precisely, if a proper closed subspace
\mathcal{Q}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for
n>1
is invariant
under

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}},M^{*}_{z_{k+1}},\ldots,M^{*}_{z_{n}})
and the
n
-tuple

(P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{1}}|_{\mathcal{Q}},\ldots,P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{n}}|_{\mathcal{Q}})
is doubly commuting, then
what is the explicit structure of
\mathcal{Q}
?

In this paper we will give a complete characterization of

doubly commuting mixed invariant

subspaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
n>1
.
We also find an explicit representation for the commutant of an
n
-tuple of
doubly commuting shifts.
Moreover, a representation of a large class of bounded operators
which intertwine with some doubly commuting shifts and co-shifts
on the Hardy space over the polydisc is obtained.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2 we give some
basic definitions and results on the Hardy space over the
polydisc. In Section 3, we find an explicit representation for the commutant
of an
n
-tuple of doubly commuting shifts and also a
representation for the commutant of a doubly commuting tuple of shifts and co-shifts
on the Hardy space over the polydisc.
In Section 4, we completely classify
doubly commuting mixed invariant
subspaces
of the Hardy spaces over the polydisc. Finally, we discuss some applications in Section 5.

2.
Preliminaries

For
n\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{D}^{n}=\mathbb{D}\times\dots\times\mathbb{D}
be the open unit polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Throughout the paper we use the notation
\bm{z}
for the

n
-tuple
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Also for any
multi-index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we write
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
be two separable complex Hilbert spaces.
The set of all bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
is denoted
by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
. If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}
, then we shall denote it by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

instead of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
.
A contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
(that is,
\|Th\|\leq\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
)
is said to be a pure contraction if
T^{*m}\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty

in the strong operator topology. Also
an isometry
V
on
\mathcal{H}
(that is,
V^{*}V=I_{\mathcal{H}}
)
is said to be pure or shift if
V^{*m}\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty

in the strong operator topology (see
[
15
]
,
[
25
]
).

The
Hardy space

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
over the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is the Hilbert space of all
holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that

\|f\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\left(\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|f(re^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,re^{i\theta_{n}})|^{2}~d{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

where
d{\theta}
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on the
n
-dimensional
torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the distinguished boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.
It is a well known and important reproducing kernel Hilbert space
corresponding to the Szegö kernel
\mathbb{S}_{n}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
where

\mathbb{S}_{n}(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-z_{j}\bar{w}_{j})^{-1}\quad\quad(\bm{z},\bm{w}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Then one can see that

\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\mathop{\sum}_{0\leq|\bm{k}|\leq n}(-1)^{|\bm{k}|}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\bar{\bm{w}}^{\bm{k}},

where
|\bm{k}|=\mathop{\sum}_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}
and
0\leq k_{j}\leq 1

for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

We can extend this definition to a Hilbert space valued Hardy space also. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space, and the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space over
\mathbb{D}^{n}
be denoted by
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is
the
\mathcal{E}
-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
-valued kernel function

(\bm{z},\bm{w})\mapsto\mathbb{S}_{n}(\bm{z},\bm{w})I_{\mathcal{E}}\quad\quad(\bm{z},\bm{w}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

In the sequel,
we shall identify the vector valued Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
with
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{E}
by the help of the canonical unitary
U:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{E}
defined by

U(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta)=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\otimes\eta\quad\quad(\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},\eta\in\mathcal{E}).

One can easily observe that
the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
n>1
over the unit polydisc can be identified
via the unitary map
U:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
where

{U}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}z_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}})=z^{k_{1}}\otimes z^{k_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes z^{k_{n}},

with the
n
-fold Hilbert space tensor product of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

on the unit disc.
Also we can realize
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as the vector-valued
Hardy space
H^{2}_{{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-1})}}(\mathbb{D})
over the unit disc.
This identification will be helpful to understand the structure of invariant subspaces
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Let
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
denote the
n
-tuple of multiplication operators on

H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
by the coordinate functions
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,
that is,

(M_{z_{j}}f)(\bm{w})=w_{j}f(\bm{w}),

for all
f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
. It is easy to see that
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
is an
n
-tuple of
doubly commuting
shifts on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Evidently, the shift
M_{z_{j}}
on

H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
can be identified with
M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}}

on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{E}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. This canonical identification will be
used throughout the paper.
We recall that a closed subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

is called an invariant subspace for

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
if

M_{z_{j}}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{S}

for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Let
I
be any nonempty and proper subset of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
We say a closed subspace
\mathcal{Q}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is
mixed invariant
if

M_{z_{j}}(\mathcal{Q})\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
for

j\in I
, and
M_{z_{j}}^{*}(\mathcal{Q})\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
for
j\in I^{c}
.
Without loss of generality,
we say that
\mathcal{Q}
is
mixed invariant

under the multiplication operators
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})

if
M_{z_{j}}(\mathcal{Q})\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
for

1\leq j\leq k
and
M_{z_{j}}^{*}(\mathcal{Q})\subseteq\mathcal{Q}
,
k+1\leq j\leq n

for some integer
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. In addition,
\mathcal{Q}
is said to be

doubly commuting mixed invariant
if the
n
-tuple

(P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{1}}|_{\mathcal{Q}},\ldots,P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{n}}|_{\mathcal{Q}})
is doubly commuting.
We denote
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{D})
as the Banach
algebra of all
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
-valued bounded analytic functions on the
open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
25
]
).

3.
commutant of a doubly commuting shifts

In this section we obtain an explicit representation
of the commutant of an
n
-tuple of doubly commuting shifts.
We also find a representation of a large class of
bounded operators which intertwine with shifts and adjoint of
shifts, that is, co-shifts. As a by-product we give some examples of

mixed invariant
subspaces
in the Hardy space over the polydisc.

Let
m,n
be positive integers. For the sake of simplicity we denote
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
by
H_{m}
. Here we use a similar technique used in our earlier paper (see
[
22
]
).
We firstly denote
z_{j}
as the coordinate function on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for
j=1,\ldots,n

and
w_{i}
as the coordinate function on
H_{m}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
We then identify the Hardy space

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
as
H_{m}
-valued Hardy space
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
over
\mathbb{D}^{n}

via canonical unitary. With this identification the multiplication operators
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},\ldots,M_{z_{n+m}})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
can be represented by
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},M_{\kappa_{1}},\ldots,M_{\kappa_{m}})

on
H^{2}_{H{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where
\kappa_{i}
is defined by

\kappa_{i}(\bm{z})=M_{w_{i}},

for all
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
.
It is evident that
\kappa_{i}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(H{{}_{m}})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a constant function and the multiplication operator

M_{\kappa_{i}}
on
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is defined by

M_{\kappa_{i}}f=\kappa_{i}f\quad\quad(f\in H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

is a shift on
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

We now identify
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
with
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes H_{m}
by the canonical unitary map

\hat{U}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes H_{m}
defined by

\hat{U}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}\cdots z_{n+m}^{k_{n+m}})=(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}})\otimes(w_{1}^{k_{n+1}}\cdots w_{m}^{k_{n+m}}),

for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n+m}\geq 0
. Then one can easily see that

\hat{U}M_{z_{j}}=(M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{H_{m}})\hat{U}\qquad(j=1,\ldots,n)

and

\hat{U}M_{z_{n+i}}=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes K_{i})\hat{U}\qquad(i=1,\ldots,m),

where
K_{i}=M_{w_{i}}
.
Thus the
(n+m)
-tuples
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},\ldots,M_{z_{n+m}})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
and

(M_{z_{1}}\otimes I_{H_{m}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}\otimes I_{H_{m}},I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes K_{1},\ldots,I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes K_{m})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes H_{m}
are unitarily equivalent.

We now define a unitary map
\tilde{U}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes H_{m}\rightarrow H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

\tilde{U}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}\otimes f)=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}f,

for all
k_{j}\geq 0
and
f\in H_{m}
. Then

\tilde{U}(M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{H_{m}})=M_{z_{j}}\tilde{U}\qquad(j=1,\ldots,n).

For
\kappa_{i}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(H_{m})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
and each operator
M_{\kappa_{i}}:H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

defined by

(M_{\kappa_{i}}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}f))(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}(K_{i}f),

for all
k_{j}\geq 0
,
f\in H_{m}
and
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, is a shift on

H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. It also follows that

\tilde{U}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\otimes K_{i})=M_{\kappa_{i}}\tilde{U}.

Therefore by setting

U=\tilde{U}\hat{U}
,
we see that
U:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})\rightarrow H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is
a unitary operator satisfying

UM_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}U,

and

UM_{z_{n+i}}=M_{\kappa_{i}}{U},

for all
j=1,\ldots,n
and
i=1,\ldots,m
.

To summarize this, we have the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers and
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
be the Hardy space over the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n+m}
.
Then the
(n+m)
-tuples of multiplication operators
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},\ldots,M_{z_{n+m}})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
and

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},M_{\kappa_{1}},\ldots,M_{\kappa_{m}})
on
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
are unitarily
equivalent, where
\kappa_{i}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(H_{m})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the
constant function defined by

\kappa_{i}(\bm{z})=M_{w_{i}}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{m}),

for all
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
i=1,\ldots,m
.

We now return to the representation of the commutant for a tuple of doubly commuting shifts.
Let
n
be a fixed positive integer.
Recall that for
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and for any
multi-index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,
we write
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}
. Also for an
n
-tuple of
commuting shifts
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
,
V^{\bm{k}}
denotes
V_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots V_{n}^{k_{n}}
.
It is also well known (see
[
8
]
) that
if
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of doubly
commuting shifts on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, then

\mathcal{H}=\displaystyle{\oplus_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}}V^{\bm{k}}\mathcal{W}},

where
\mathcal{W}=\displaystyle\cap_{j=1}^{n}Ker(V_{j}^{*})
.
We now define a map
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Pi(V_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots V_{n}^{k_{n}}\eta)=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}\eta\qquad(k_{j}\geq 0,\eta\in\mathcal{W}).

Then
\Pi
is clearly a unitary. Moreover,

\Pi V_{j}=M_{z_{j}}\Pi\quad\mbox{for}~j=1,\ldots,n.

It is well known that if a bounded operator
X\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
commutes with the
multiplication operators
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, i.e.,

XM_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}X
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, then

X=M_{\Theta}
for some
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(see
[
4
]
).
These are called analytic Toeplitz operators which is an important class of operators.
Also the commutant of
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
can be represented as
the commutant of
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Hence the commutant of
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
can be written
as the form
M_{\Phi}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where

\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{W})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. The question is
what is the explicit representation of
\Phi
?
Here we have the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of doubly
commuting shifts on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
\Pi
be the above unitary
defined in (
3.1
).
Then

TV_{j}=V_{j}T\quad\mbox{~for all ~}j=1,\ldots,n

if and only if
\Pi T\Pi^{*}=M_{\Phi}
for some

\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{W})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and

\Phi(\bm{z})=P_{\mathcal{W}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-z_{1}V_{1}^{*})^{-1}\ldots(I_{\mathcal{H}}-z_{n}V_{n}^{*})^{-1}T\mid_{\mathcal{W}},

where
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
\mathcal{W}=\cap_{j=1}^{n}Ker(V_{j}^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then
h
can be written as

h=\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}V^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}}},

for some
\eta_{{\bm{k}}}\in\mathcal{W}
,
{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}

(as
\mathcal{H}=\displaystyle{\oplus_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}}V^{\bm{k}}\mathcal{W}}
).
Applying
P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*{\bm{m}}}
to both sides and since

\mathcal{W}=\cap_{j=1}^{n}Ker(V_{j}^{*})
, we have
\eta_{{\bm{m}}}=P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*{\bm{m}}}h
for all
{\bm{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
.
Therefore, for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

(3.2)

h=\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}V^{\bm{k}}P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*\bm{k}}h}.

Now let
TV_{j}=V_{j}T
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then there exists a bounded analytic function

\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{W})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that
\Pi T\Pi^{*}=M_{\Phi}
. For each
\bm{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{W}
we
have

\begin{split}\Phi(\bm{w})\eta&=(M_{\Phi}\eta)(\bm{w})\\
&=(\Pi T\Pi^{*}\eta)(\bm{w})\\
&=(\Pi T\eta)(\bm{w}),\end{split}

as
\Pi^{*}\eta=\eta
. Using (
3.2
), we have

T\eta=\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}V^{\bm{k}}P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*\bm{k}}T\eta},

it follows that

\begin{split}\Phi(\bm{w})\eta&=(\Pi\left[\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}V^{\bm{k}}P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*\bm{k}}T\eta}\right])(\bm{w})\\
&=\left[\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*\bm{k}}T\eta}\right](\bm{w})\\
&=\left[\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}{\bm{w}}^{\bm{k}}P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*\bm{k}}T\eta}\right]\\
&=P_{\mathcal{W}}\left[\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}{\bm{w}}^{\bm{k}}V^{*\bm{k}}T\eta}\right]\\
&=P_{\mathcal{W}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-w_{1}V_{1}^{*})^{-1}\cdots(I_{\mathcal{H}}-w_{n}V_{n}^{*})^{-1}T\eta.\end{split}

Therefore

\Phi(\bm{z})=P_{\mathcal{W}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-z_{1}V_{1}^{*})^{-1}\cdots(I_{\mathcal{H}}-z_{n}V_{n}^{*})^{-1}T|_{\mathcal{W}}\quad\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}),

as required. We omit the sufficient part as it holds trivially.

This completes the proof.

Remark 3.1
.

In the proof we have used the standard projection
formula

I_{\mathcal{H}}=\displaystyle{\mbox{SOT}-{\sum_{\bm{k}}V^{\bm{k}}P_{\mathcal{W}}V^{*\bm{k}}}}
.
Note that the function
\Phi

defined in Lemma
3.2
is well defined and a
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{W})
-valued
holomorphic function in the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
as
\|w_{j}V_{j}^{*}\|=|w_{j}|\|V_{j}\|<1

for all
w_{j}\in\mathbb{D}
. Now if
T
commutes with a shift
V_{1}
, then from the
above result, we have the representation

\Phi(z)=P_{\mathcal{W}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-zV_{1}^{*})^{-1}T|_{\mathcal{W}}\quad\quad(z\in\mathbb{D},\mathcal{W}=Ker(V_{1}^{*}))

which was obtained in our previous paper
[
23
]
.

We are now in a position to find the explicit representation
of the commutant of a tuple of doubly commuting shifts and co-shifts.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
T
satisfies

TM_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}T\quad\mbox{for~}j=1,\ldots,n

and

TM_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}=M_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}T\quad\mbox{for ~}i=1,\ldots,m

if and only if
T=M_{\Phi}
, where
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(H_{m})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and

\Phi(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}M_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}^{*}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}},

and

\Theta_{\bm{k}}(\bm{w})=\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}{\langle M^{\bm{k}}_{\bm{z}}\bm{1},T\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}\rangle_{H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}}

for all
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
\bm{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{m}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
T
is a bounded linear operator on
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
satisfying

TM_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}T\quad\mbox{for~}j=1,\ldots,n

and

TM_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}=M_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}T\quad\mbox{for~}i=1,\ldots,m.

From the first identity, we have
T=M_{\Phi}
, where
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(H_{m})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Let

\Phi(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}}T_{\bm{k}}{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\quad({\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{D}^{n},~T_{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{m})).

Now
H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},~\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}\}
.
Since
T
satisfies

TM_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}=M_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}T\quad\mbox{for~}i=1,\ldots,m,

we have

\displaystyle TM_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})=TM_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\bm{z}^{0})

\displaystyle=(\sum_{\bm{k}}T_{\bm{k}}{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}})M^{*}_{w_{i}}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}(T_{\bm{k}}M_{w_{i}}^{*}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})){\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}

and

\displaystyle M_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}T(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})=M_{\kappa_{i}}^{*}(\sum_{\bm{k}}T_{\bm{k}}{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}})(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})=\sum_{\bm{k}}(M_{w_{i}}^{*}T_{\bm{k}}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})){\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}.

From the above we have,

T_{\bm{k}}M_{w_{i}}^{*}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})=M_{w_{i}}^{*}T_{\bm{k}}(\bm{w}^{\bm{r}})

for all
\bm{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{m}
and
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. Therefore

T_{\bm{k}}M_{w_{i}}^{*}=M_{w_{i}}^{*}T_{\bm{k}}~~~\mbox{ i.e.,}~~~M_{w_{i}}T_{\bm{k}}^{*}=T_{\bm{k}}^{*}M_{w_{i}}

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. Hence
T_{\bm{k}}^{*}=M_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}

for some
\Theta_{\bm{k}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
for all
{\bm{k}}
. Therefore

\Phi(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}}M^{*}_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}}\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Now we will find
\Theta_{\bm{k}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
explicitly:

We know in view of Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\Phi(\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\sum_{\bm{k}}(P_{H_{m}}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\bm{k}}T\mid_{H_{m}}){\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}}.

Hence

M^{*}_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}=P_{H_{m}}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\bm{k}}T\mid_{H_{m}},

for all
\bm{k}
.
We shall now calculate each
\Theta_{\bm{k}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
.
Suppose
\Theta_{\bm{k}}(\bm{w})=\sum_{\bm{l}}a_{\bm{l}}\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\langle(\sum_{\bm{l}}a_{\bm{l}}\bm{w}^{\bm{l}})\bm{1},\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\langle M_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}\bm{1},\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{1},M_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}^{*}\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{1},P_{H_{m}}M_{\bm{z}}^{*\bm{k}}T\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\langle M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{1},T\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle_{H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}.

Since
\langle\bm{w}^{\bm{l}},\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle=0
for
\bm{l}\neq\bm{r}

and
\bm{w}^{0}=\bm{1}
, a constant function on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{m})
, from the above we have

a_{\bm{r}}=\langle M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{1},T\bm{w}^{\bm{r}}\rangle_{H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}

for all
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}
. Thus

\Phi(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}}M^{*}_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}}\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

where

\Theta_{\bm{k}}(\bm{w})=\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{l}}\langle M_{\bm{z}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{1},T\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}\rangle_{H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}.

The converse follows easily. This finishes the proof.

We know that if
T\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
such that
TM_{z}=M_{z}T
, then

\overline{ran}(T)
is an
M_{z}
-invariant subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
In other words, for any
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
,
\overline{ran}(M_{\varphi})

is an
M_{z}
-invariant subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Similarly
let
T\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m}))
.
Suppose that

TM_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}T\quad\mbox{and}\quad TM_{z_{n+i}}^{*}=M_{z_{n+i}}^{*}T,

for
j=1,\ldots,n
and
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then

\overline{ran}(T)
is invariant under

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},M_{z_{n+1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n+m}}^{*})

on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n+m})
. Thus from the above theorem,
we can readily find some examples of
mixed invariant
subspaces.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
. Then
\overline{ran}(T)

is a mixed invariant subspace under

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}},M_{\kappa_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{\kappa_{m}}^{*})

if

T=M_{\Phi}
, where
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(H_{m})}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is given by

\Phi(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}M_{\Theta_{\bm{k}}}^{*}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}},

and

\Theta_{\bm{k}}(\bm{w})=\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{m}}{\langle M^{\bm{k}}_{\bm{z}}\bm{1},T\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}\rangle_{H^{2}_{H_{m}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\bm{w}^{\bm{l}}}

for all
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\bm{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{m}
.

4.
Doubly commuting mixed invariant subspaces

In this section we obtain an explicit representation of

doubly commuting mixed invariant subspaces
.
We start with a known result (see (
[
22
]
) for more details).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and

M_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
be an isometric multiplier.
Then
\dim(\mathcal{F})\leq\dim(\mathcal{E})
.

The following result is required to prove our main results.
The idea of the proof comes from a paper of Douglas and Foias
[
9
]
.
This result may be known to the experts but here our proof is
direct and it is useful as an independent result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
be an
M_{z_{j}}^{*}
-invariant subspace
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
and
T:\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

be an isometry such that
TM_{z_{j}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{Q}}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}T
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
.
Then there exists a one dimensional Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, a subspace of
\mathcal{E}

such that
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and a constant unitary multiplier

M_{\Theta}:H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

such that
T=M_{\Theta}|_{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{Q}
is an
M_{z_{j}}^{*}
-invariant subspace for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
, the space

\mathcal{M}=\overline{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\mathcal{Q}:{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\}

is
M_{z_{j}}
-reducing subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
. We know that
any
(M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
reducing subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is of the form
H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

for some closed subspace
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}
of
\mathcal{E}
. Thus
\mathcal{M}=H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Firstly, our aim is to extend the operator
T:\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
to

\tilde{T}:H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that
\tilde{T}
is an isometry
and
M_{z_{j}}^{*}\tilde{T}=\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}^{*}
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
.

It is easy to see that the set
\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta:{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\eta\in\mathcal{Q}\}
is a total set in
H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Consider

\mathcal{L}=\mbox{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta:{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\eta\in\mathcal{Q}\}.

Let
F
be a finite set in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
. We define
\tilde{T}
on
\mathcal{L}
as

\tilde{T}(\sum_{\bm{k}\in F}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in F}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},

where
\alpha_{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\eta_{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{Q}
.
We shall firstly show that the map
\tilde{T}
is well defined on
\mathcal{L}
. To do that we consider
the following.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}
be two finite sets in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
, and

\eta_{\bm{k}},\zeta_{\bm{m}}\in\mathcal{Q}
. Then

(4.1)

\displaystyle<\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bar{\beta_{\bm{m}}}<\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>.

Now for each fixed
{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}
and
{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}
,
let
{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
and
{\bm{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
.
Consider
I=\{j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}:k_{j}\geq m_{j}\}
.
Using the fact that
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
is a
tuple of doubly commuting isometries and
T
is an isometry, we obtain

\displaystyle<\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\prod_{j=1}^{n}M_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\prod_{j=1}^{n}M_{z_{j}}^{m_{j}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\prod_{j\in I}M_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}-m_{j}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\prod_{j\in I^{c}}M_{z_{j}}^{m_{j}-k_{j}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\prod_{j\in I^{c}}M_{z_{j}}^{*(m_{j}-k_{j})}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\prod_{j\in I}M_{z_{j}}^{*(k_{j}-m_{j})}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<T\prod_{j\in I^{c}}M_{z_{j}}^{*(m_{j}-k_{j})}\eta_{\bm{k}},T\prod_{j\in I}M_{z_{j}}^{*(k_{j}-m_{j})}\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\prod_{j\in I^{c}}M_{z_{j}}^{*(m_{j}-k_{j})}\eta_{\bm{k}},\prod_{j\in I}M_{z_{j}}^{*(k_{j}-m_{j})}\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\prod_{j\in I}M_{z_{j}}^{(k_{j}-m_{j})}\eta_{\bm{k}},\prod_{j\in I^{c}}M_{z_{j}}^{(m_{j}-k_{j})}\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\prod_{j\in I}{z_{j}}^{m_{j}}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}{z_{j}}^{k_{j}}(\prod_{j\in I}{z_{j}}^{(k_{j}-m_{j})})\eta_{\bm{k}},\prod_{j\in I}{z_{j}}^{m_{j}}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}{z_{j}}^{k_{j}}(\prod_{j\in I}{z_{j}}^{(m_{j}-k_{j})})\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}},\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\zeta_{\bm{m}}>.

Thus from the above equation (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle<\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}},\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}>

\displaystyle=<\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}},\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}{\beta_{\bm{m}}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\zeta_{\bm{m}}>.

In particular for any finite set
F
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}.

Suppose that

\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\zeta_{\bm{m}}.

Then from the above equality (
4.2
), it is easy to see that

\|\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}}-\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}\|^{2}=\|\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}}-\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\zeta_{\bm{m}}\|^{2}=0.

Therefore

\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}T\zeta_{\bm{m}}.

Hence from the definition of
\tilde{T}
, we have

\tilde{T}(\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in F_{1}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta_{\bm{k}})=\tilde{T}(\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in F_{2}}\beta_{\bm{m}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}\zeta_{\bm{m}}).

This proves that
\tilde{T}
is well defined on
\mathcal{L}
. From equation (
4.2
),
we can conclude that
\tilde{T}
is
a bounded as well as norm preserving linear operator on
\mathcal{L}
.

Now
{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,

\eta\in\mathcal{Q}
, and for each
j=1,2,\cdots,n
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta)=\tilde{T}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j}^{k_{j}+1}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}\eta)=(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j}^{k_{j}+1}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}})T\eta=z_{j}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta=M_{z_{j}}\tilde{T}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta).

Also if
k_{j}\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}^{*}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta)=\tilde{T}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j}^{k_{j}-1}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}\eta)=(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j}^{k_{j}-1}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}})T\eta=M_{z_{j}}^{*}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta)=M_{z_{j}}^{*}\tilde{T}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta).

If
k_{j}=0
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}^{*}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta)=\tilde{T}(z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j-1}^{k_{j-1}}z_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}\eta)

\displaystyle=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j-1}^{k_{j-1}}z_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}(TM_{z_{j}}^{*}\eta)

\displaystyle=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{j-1}^{k_{j-1}}z_{j+1}^{k_{j+1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}(M_{z_{j}}^{*}T\eta)

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}T\eta)

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}\tilde{T}(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta).

The above fact implies that

\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}\tilde{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{L}}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}\tilde{T}

for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
.
Now the norm preserving operator
\tilde{T}
on
\mathcal{L}
can be extended uniquely by continuity (again denoted by same
\tilde{T}
)
to the closure of
\mathcal{L}
( i.e.
\bar{\mathcal{L}}=H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
) such that

\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}=M_{z_{j}}\tilde{T}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tilde{T}M_{z_{j}}^{*}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}\tilde{T}

for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
. This
imlplies that
\tilde{T}
is a constant isometric multiplier
from
H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
to
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Therefore from Lemma
4.1
, we have

\dim(\tilde{\mathcal{E}})=1
.
Hence
\tilde{T}=M_{\Theta}
, where
\Theta({\bm{z}})=\Theta(0)
is
a unitary from
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
to
\mathbb{C}
for
{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Also

T=\tilde{T}|_{\mathcal{Q}}=M_{\Theta}|_{\mathcal{Q}}.

This finishes the proof.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
be a fixed integer.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}

be a joint
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}},M_{z_{k+1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})

invariant subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
is some inner function
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{j}}
is either a Jordan block or the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Let

\displaystyle V_{j}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{S}}M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}~~~\mbox{for~}1\leq j\leq n.

Then
(V_{1},\cdots,V_{n})
is a doubly commuting tuple, where
V_{j}
is a pure
isometry for
1\leq j\leq k
and
V_{j}
is a pure contraction for
k+1\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

is a joint
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}},M_{z_{k+1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})

invariant subspace, where
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
is some inner function
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{j}}
is either a Jordan block or the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Clearly,

V_{j}=P_{\mathcal{S}}M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}=M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}
for
1\leq j\leq k

is a pure isometry and
V_{j}=P_{\mathcal{S}}M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}
for
k+1\leq j\leq n

is a pure contraction.

Now
\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
is a doubly commuting invariant subspace for
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}})
.
Therefore
V_{i}V_{j}=V_{j}V_{i}
and
V_{i}V_{j}^{*}=V_{j}^{*}V_{i}
for
1\leq i<j\leq k
.
Again
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}
is a
doubly commuting invariant subspace for
(M_{z_{k+1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})
.
Thus
V_{i}V_{j}=V_{j}V_{i}
and
V_{i}V_{j}^{*}=V_{j}^{*}V_{i}

for
k+1\leq i<j\leq n
.
Also

V_{i}V_{j}^{*}=M_{z_{i}}M_{z_{j}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{S}}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}M_{z_{i}}|_{\mathcal{S}}=V_{j}^{*}V_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq k
and for
k+1\leq j\leq n
.

To prove
V_{i}V_{j}=V_{j}V_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and for
k+1\leq j\leq n
,
we use the standard result of the tensor product of Hilbert spaces:
Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then

P_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}}(h_{1}\otimes h_{2})=P_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}h_{1}\otimes P_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}h_{2}.

Let
f\in\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
and
g_{l}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{l}}
for
l=1,\ldots,n-k
. Then

\displaystyle V_{i}V_{k+l}(f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k})

\displaystyle=V_{i}P_{\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}}(f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{z}g_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k})

\displaystyle=V_{i}(f\otimes P_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}}(g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes zg_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k}))

\displaystyle=M_{z_{i}}(f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{l}}}zg_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k})

\displaystyle=z_{i}f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{l}}}zg_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k}

\displaystyle=P_{\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}}(z_{i}f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes zg_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k})

\displaystyle=V_{k+l}V_{i}(f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{l}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n-k}).

This completes the proof.

We record the following known result on the analytic model
for a doubly commuting
n
-tuple of operators (see
[
6
]
).
Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
be a doubly commuting pure
n
-tuple
of operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Define the defect operator for the
n
-tuple
T
as

D_{T^{*}}=(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-T_{j}T_{j}^{*}))^{1\over 2}.

and

\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=\overline{ran}(D_{T^{*}}).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
be a doubly commuting pure
n
-tuple
of operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
on

H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is the minimal isometric dilation of
T
.
That is there exists a joint
(M_{z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})

invariant subspace
\mathcal{Q}
of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

T_{j}{\cong}P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z_{j}}\mid_{\mathcal{Q}}\quad(\mbox{for all~}j=1,\ldots,n)

and

H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\overline{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\mathcal{Q}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\}.

Using the above theorem, we characterize a doubly commuting tuple
of shifts and pure contractions.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
be a fixed integer.
Let
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{k},T_{{k+1}},\ldots,T_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of doubly commuting
operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
T_{j}
is shift for
1\leq j\leq k
and
T_{j}
is
a pure contraction for
k+1\leq j\leq n
. Then
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{k},T_{k+1},\ldots,T_{n})

on
\mathcal{H}
is unitarily eqivalent to

(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}},I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes\tau_{1},\ldots,I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes\tau_{n-k})

on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
,
where
\mathcal{W}=\cap_{j=1}^{k}Ker(T_{j}^{*})
and
\tau_{j}
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{W}

for
1\leq j\leq n-k
.

Proof.

Consider the map
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
defined by

\Pi(\prod_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}^{m_{j}}\eta)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}^{m_{j}}\eta,

where
\eta\in\mathcal{W}
and
m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then clearly
\Pi

is unitary and
\Pi T_{j}=M_{z_{j}}\Pi
for
1\leq j\leq k
. Now we know that
if
C\in\mathcal{B}({H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{k})})
such that
M_{z_{j}}C=CM_{z_{j}}
and
M_{z_{j}}^{*}C=CM_{z_{j}}^{*}

for
1\leq j\leq k
, then
C=M_{\tau}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes\tau
, where
\tau\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{W})
.
Moreover, if
C
is pure then
\tau
is pure. Thus

\Pi T_{k+j}\Pi^{*}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes\tau_{j},

where
\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{W})
is a pure contraction for
1\leq j\leq n-k
.
Since
T_{k+i}T_{k+j}=T_{k+j}T_{k+i}
and
T_{k+i}^{*}T_{k+j}=T_{k+j}T_{k+i}^{*}
,
it readily follows that

\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i}\mbox{~~and ~~}\tau_{i}^{*}\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i}^{*}

for
1\leq i<j\leq n-k
.

Now we are in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
be a fixed integer.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a joint
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}},M_{z_{k+1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})
invariant
subspace and

\displaystyle V_{j}

\displaystyle=P_{{\mathcal{S}}}M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}\quad\mbox{for~~}j=1,\ldots,n.

If
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
is doubly commuting, then

\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}

for some
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
inner function
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{j}}
either a Jordan block or the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for

j=1,\ldots,n-k
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}
is
a joint
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{k}},M_{z_{k+1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*})
invariant subspace
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,

V_{j}=P_{{\mathcal{S}}}M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}=M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}

for
j=1,\ldots,k
is shift and

V_{j}=P_{{\mathcal{S}}}M_{z_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}

for
j=k+1,\ldots,n

is a pure contraction. Hence by the above Lemma
4.5
,
there exists a unitary

\Pi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
defined by

\Pi(\prod_{j=1}^{k}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\eta)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}^{m_{j}}\eta\qquad(\eta\in\mathcal{W})

such that

\displaystyle\Pi V_{j}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}\Pi\quad\mbox{for~}j=1,\ldots,k

\displaystyle\Pi V_{k+i}

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes\tau_{i})\Pi\quad\mbox{for~}i=1,\ldots,n-k

where
\mathcal{W}=\cap_{j=1}^{k}Ker(V_{j}^{*})
and
\tau_{i}

is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{W}
with

\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i}\mbox{~~and ~~}\tau_{i}^{*}\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i}^{*}

for
1\leq i<j\leq n-k
. Now from Theorem
4.4
, we have

\tau^{*}=(\tau_{1}^{*},\ldots,\tau_{n-k}^{*})
on
\mathcal{W}
is unitarily equivalent to

(M^{*}_{w_{1}}|_{\mathcal{Q}},\ldots,M^{*}_{w_{n-k}}|_{\mathcal{Q}})
, where
\mathcal{Q}
is a
joint
(M_{w_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{w_{n-k}}^{*})
invariant subspace of

H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{\tau^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
and

\mathcal{D}_{\tau^{*}}=\overline{ran}(\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}(I_{\mathcal{W}}-\tau_{i}\tau_{i}^{*}))^{1\over 2}
.
Let

U:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{\tau^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
be a
unitary such that

U\tau_{i}^{*}=M_{w_{i}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{Q}}U\quad\mbox{for}~i=1,\ldots,n-k.

Thus we have a unitary map
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes U^{*}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes{\mathcal{W}}

such that

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes U^{*})(M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{Q}})=(M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes U^{*})

and

(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes U^{*})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes M_{w_{i}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{Q}})=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes\tau_{i}^{*})(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes U^{*})

for
j=1,\ldots,k
and
i=1,\ldots,n-k
.

Let
\iota:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
be the inclusion map.
Therefore we have an operator

\tilde{\Pi}=\iota\circ\Pi^{*}\circ(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes U^{*}):H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})

which is an isometry as each one is so and also satisfying

\tilde{\Pi}(M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{Q}})=(M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})\tilde{\Pi}\quad\mbox{for~}j=1,\ldots,k

and

\tilde{\Pi}(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes M_{w_{i}}^{*}|_{{\mathcal{Q}}})=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})}\otimes M_{w_{i}}^{*})\tilde{\Pi}\quad\mbox{for~}i=1,\ldots,n-k.

Then from the first equality, we have
\tilde{\Pi}
is an inner multiplier, say,

\tilde{\Pi}=M_{\Phi},

for some
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Q},H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k}))}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
.
Again the other equality gives

\Phi(e^{it})M_{w_{j}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{Q}}=M_{w_{j}}^{*}\Phi(e^{it}),\quad j=1,\ldots,n-k.

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
e^{it}=e^{it_{1}}\cdots e^{it_{k}}
. Since
\Phi(e^{it})
is isometry almost everywhere (a.e.)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the
k
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{k}
, from the above Lemma
4.2
,

\Phi(e^{it}):\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\quad(e^{it}\in\mathbb{T}^{k}\mbox{~a.e.})

is the restriction of a constant isometric multiplier
from
H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
to
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
, where

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
is a one-dimensional space.
Since
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
and
\mathbb{C}

are one dimensional spaces, the space of all constant multipliers in

H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}},\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})

is also one dimensional. Hence there exists a unitary multiplier

M_{{X}}:H_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})

such that

\Phi(e^{it})=\Theta(e^{it})M_{X}|_{\mathcal{Q}}\quad(\mbox{~a.e. on~}\mathbb{T}^{k})

for some scalar
\Theta(e^{it})
. Since
\Phi
is analytic,

\Theta
is also analytic on
\mathbb{D}^{k}
.
Also
|\Theta(e^{it})|=1
as
M_{X}|_{\mathcal{Q}}
and
\Phi(e^{it})

are isometries a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
.
Hence
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
is an inner function.
Thus the inner multiplier
M_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\rightarrow H^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})}(\mathbb{D}^{k})
factors as

M_{\Theta}\otimes M_{X}|_{\mathcal{Q}}
from
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes{\mathcal{Q}}
to
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes{H^{2}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
.
Therefore

\mathcal{S}=ran(M_{\Phi})=M_{\Phi}H^{2}_{\mathcal{Q}}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes M_{X}(\mathcal{Q}).

Since
M_{X}:H^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
is a constant unitary,
it intertwines with
M_{w_{i}}^{*}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-k
.
Hence
M_{X}(\mathcal{Q})
is a closed doubly commuting invariant subspace of

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n-k})
. Therefore
from the result
[
26
]
,

M_{X}(\mathcal{Q})
is of the form

M_{X}(\mathcal{Q})=\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{j}}
is either a Jordan block or the Hardy space

H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
j=1,\ldots,n-k
.
Thus

\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{k})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{\theta{n-k}}.

This finishes the proof.

5.
Some Applications

In this section we give some concrete examples
of
mixed invariant
subspaces.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the unit circle and
K(\cdot,z)
be the Szegö kernel on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
We consider the examples
of
mixed invariant
subspaces in the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
over the
bidisc for the sake of simplicity.
We can identify
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
as
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
Let
(\alpha_{n})\subset\mathbb{D}
be a sequence such that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}1-|\alpha_{n}|^{2}<\infty
.
Consider for
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{Q}_{N}=\mbox{span}\{K(\cdot,{\alpha_{1}}),\ldots,K(\cdot,{\alpha_{N}})\}

and

\mathcal{S}_{N}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{N}.

Then clearly
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is invariant under
(M_{z}\otimes I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})},I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\otimes M_{z}^{*})
.
Also

(\mathcal{S}_{N}\ominus z_{1}\mathcal{S}_{N})=(\mathcal{S}_{N}\ominus(M_{z}\otimes I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})})\mathcal{S}_{N})=(H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus zH^{2}(\mathbb{D}))\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{N}=\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathcal{Q}_{N}.

Thus
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is an example of a
mixed invariant subspace with
\dim(\mathcal{S}_{N}\ominus z_{1}\mathcal{S}_{N})=N

for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
if we take

\mathcal{Q}=\overline{\mbox{span}}\{K(\cdot,{\alpha_{i}}):i\in\mathbb{N}\},

then
\mathcal{S}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{Q}
is a mixed invariant subspace with

\dim(\mathcal{S}\ominus z_{1}\mathcal{S})=\infty
. Note that here
\mathcal{Q}
is a proper
subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as

\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{z-\alpha_{j}}{1-\bar{\alpha_{j}}z}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\perp}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\mathcal{Q}
.

In particular, we have an explicit representation of
the Beurling-Lax-Halmos’s inner function
\Theta
and the mixed invariant subspace

\mathcal{S}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{S}\ominus z_{1}\mathcal{S}
is one dimensional.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H^{2}_{H_{n}}(\mathbb{D})
be
a closed subspace, and let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{S}\ominus z\mathcal{S}
. Suppose
\mathcal{S}
is invariant
under
(M_{z},M_{\kappa_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{\kappa_{n}}^{*})
.
Then
\dim(\mathcal{W})=1
if and only if

\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

where
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C},H_{n})}(\mathbb{D})
is an inner multiplier given by

\Theta(z)=\psi(z)K(\cdot,(\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)},\ldots,\overline{\varphi_{n}(z)}))\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

\varphi_{j},\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and
|\varphi_{j}(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and

{|\psi(e^{i\theta})|^{2}}={\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\varphi_{j}(e^{i\theta})|^{2})}\mbox{~~a.e. on~~}\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}_{H_{n}}(\mathbb{D})
which
is invariant under
(M_{z},M_{\kappa_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{\kappa_{n}}^{*})
.
Consider

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=M_{z}|_{\mathcal{S}}~~~\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle V_{j}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{S}}M_{\kappa_{j}}|_{\mathcal{S}}\mbox{~~for~}j=1,\ldots,n.

Since
V
is a shift and
V_{j}^{*}
commute with
V
, the tuple

(V,V_{1}^{*},\ldots,V_{n}^{*})
on
\mathcal{S}
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{z},M_{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,M_{\varphi_{n}})

on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{S}\ominus z\mathcal{S}
and from Remark
3.1
,

\varphi_{j}(w)=P_{\mathcal{W}}(I_{\mathcal{S}}-wV^{*})^{-1}V_{j}^{*}|_{\mathcal{W}}\quad(w\in\mathbb{D},~j=1,\ldots,n).

Since
\mathcal{S}
is joint invariant under
(M_{z},M_{\kappa_{1}}^{*},\ldots,M_{\kappa_{n}}^{*})

and
\dim(\mathcal{W})=1
, using the result (
[
22
]
, Theorem 3.2 ),
we have an isometry

M_{\Theta}:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{H_{n}}(\mathbb{D})\mbox{~~such that~~}\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}{(\mathbb{D})},

where
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C},H_{n})}(\mathbb{D})
is an inner multiplier
and
M^{*}_{\kappa_{j}}M_{\Theta}=M_{\Theta}M_{\varphi_{j}}
for some
\varphi_{j}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})

for
1\leq j\leq n
.
Therefore, for each
z\in\mathbb{D}

M^{*}_{z_{j}}\Theta(z)=\Theta(z)\varphi_{j}(z)=\varphi_{j}(z)\Theta(z)

for
1\leq j\leq n
.
This implies
\Theta(z)\cdot 1=\Theta(z)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is an eigenvector for the operator

M_{z_{j}}^{*}

with eigenvalue
\varphi_{j}(z)
. Hence

|\varphi_{j}(z)|<1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
1\leq j\leq n
.
That means for each
z\in\mathbb{D}

\Theta(z)\in\ker(M_{z_{j}}-\overline{\varphi_{j}(z)}I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})})^{*}\quad\mbox{for all~}1\leq j\leq n.

This implies

\Theta(z)\in\mbox{span}\displaystyle\{K(\cdot,(\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)},\ldots,\overline{\varphi_{n}(z)})\}
,
where

K(\cdot,(\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)},\ldots,\overline{\varphi_{n}(z)})(\bm{w})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\varphi_{j}(z)w_{j}}\qquad(\bm{w}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Therefore

\Theta(z)=\psi(z)K(\cdot,(\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)},\ldots,\overline{\varphi_{n}(z)})\mbox{~~for some~~}\psi.

Using the fact that

\|K(\cdot,(\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)},\ldots,\overline{\varphi_{n}(z)})\|^{2}={1\over{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\varphi_{j}(z)|^{2})}},

we have from the above identity

|\psi(z)|^{2}=\|\Theta(z)\|^{2}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\varphi_{j}(z)|^{2})}.

It is easy to see that
\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
as

|\psi(z)|\leq\|\Theta(z)\|\leq 1
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Since
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C},H_{n})}(\mathbb{D})
is an inner multiplier, by taking the
radial limit, we have

{|\psi(e^{i\theta})|^{2}}={\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\varphi_{j}(e^{i\theta})|^{2})}\mbox{~~a.e. on~~}\mathbb{T}.

Also

\mathcal{S}=\Theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

where

\Theta(z)=\psi(z)K(\cdot,(\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)},\ldots,\overline{\varphi_{n}(z)}))\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

The converse follows easily.

This completes the proof.

The above result is a generalization of the result on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
over the bidisc
by Izuchi et al.(see Theorem 3.2 ,
[
20
]
).

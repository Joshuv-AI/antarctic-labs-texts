Quantum conditional entropy from information-theoretic principles
Sarah Brandsen, Isabelle Jianing Geng, Mark M. Wilde, Gilad Gour

Abstract
We introduce an axiomatic approach for characterizing quantum conditional entropy. Our approach relies on two physically motivated axioms: monotonicity under conditional majorization and additivity. We show that these two axioms provide sufficient structure that enable us to derive several key properties applicable to all quantum conditional entropies studied in the literature. Specifically, we prove that any quantum conditional entropy must be negative on certain entangled states and must equal − log ⁡ d -\log d on d × d d\times d maximally entangled states. We also prove the non-negativity of conditional entropy on separable states, and we provide a generic definition for the dual of a quantum conditional entropy. Finally, we develop an operational approach for characterizing quantum conditional entropy via games of chance, and we show that, for the classical case, this complementary approach yields the same ordering as the axiomatic approach.



I
Introduction

Conditional entropy quantifies the uncertainty of a bipartite state shared between Alice and Bob, given access to Bob’s system only. Previous work on conditional entropy for quantum bipartite states has demonstrated that, unlike classical conditional entropy, quantum conditional entropy can be negative
Cerf_1997
and achieves its minimum on maximally entangled states. The initial discovery that quantum conditional entropy could be negative was so surprising that various conjectures about its meaning were put forth by the authors of
Cerf_1997
, such as its potential relation to “anti-qubits,” which are quanta of negative information. The most satisfying information theoretic interpretation of quantum conditional entropy and its negativity is given by the state merging protocol of
Horodecki2005
, which demonstrates that conditional entropy is the rate at which maximally entangled Bell states need to be consumed in order transfer Alice’s share of a bipartite state to Bob, who possesses the other share of the state. The importance of the existence of negative conditional entropy is also reflected in the fact that there is an information quantity, called coherent information
PhysRevA.54.2629
, dedicated to measuring how negative the conditional entropy of a bipartite quantum state is.
Multiple previous works have investigated topics related to conditional entropy
Liu_2018
;
Patro_2017
;
Capel_2018
;
Gour5
;
Kuznetsova2010QuantumCE
;
doi:10.1063/1.5027495
;
PhysRevA.99.062119
;
Gour
;
PhysRevA.104.012417
and have found additional applications of conditional entropy to quantum cryptographic protocols
Brown_2021
.

The central role of conditional entropy in quantum information theory motivates the need for an axiomatic approach that defines the set of all possible quantum conditional entropies. Previous literature has extensively studied axiomatic derivations for other entropies
e13111945
;
e10030261
, beginning with approaches deriving the Shannon entropy
6773024
and progressing to a complete axiomatic approach for classical entropies and relative entropies
gour2021entropy
. More recently, an axiomatic approach for entropy based on core-concavity was developed and connections were made between such core-concave functions and classical conditional entropy
9064819
.

In this work, we start with a minimal set of assumptions with strong information-theoretic motivation and demonstrate that these simple assumptions are sufficient for recovering key properties of quantum conditional entropy, such as its negativity for maximally entangled states, non-negativity for separable bipartite states, invariance under local unitaries, and reduction to an entropy function on uncorrelated systems.
At the core of our approach, we fully characterise the set of channels that are conditional entropy non-decreasing, and we require that conditional entropy be monotonic under the action of such channels. More specifically, we require that any conditional entropy non-decreasing channel maps a state of maximal entropy on Alice’s side to an output state that also has maximal entropy on Alice’s side, a condition which is satisfied by
conditionally unital
channels. Likewise, given that conditional entropy can be understood as measuring as how much information Bob has about Alice’s system, any conditional entropy non-decreasing channel cannot “leak” information from Alice’s system to Bob’s system. Thus, conditional entropy non-decreasing channels must also satisfy the semi-causality condition outlined in
Beckman_2001
. In other words, we identify the set of conditional entropy non-decreasing channels with channels that are both conditionally unital and semi-causal (CUSC).

This gives rise to a natural majorization relation where
\sigma_{AB}\precsim_{A}\rho_{AB}
(i.e.
\rho_{AB}
has less uncertainty than
\sigma_{AB}
with respect to system
A
) if
\sigma_{AB}
can be obtained by applying a CUSC channel to
\rho_{AB}
. Any conditional entropy is thus required to be monotonic under this partial ordering which we call
conditional majorization
.
In
Gour
a different definition of conditional majorization (restricted to classical systems) was given, however, we prove that the quantum conditional majorization defined in this paper yields the exact same ordering as in
Gour
on (bipartite) classical systems.

An alternative, operationally motivated means for characterizing uncertainty is to utilize games of chance. It was found in
brandsen2021entropy
that games of chance provide an operational interpretation to conditional majorization for classical bipartite states. Games of chance are ideal for studying uncertainty, as the probability of winning a game with a given physical system depends solely on the uncertainty of the system’s output. Such games of chance thus lead to a natural partial ordering between channels, where state a
\rho_{AB}
is “less uncertain” than state
\sigma_{AB}
if
\rho_{AB}
performs at least as well as
\rho_{AB}
for any game of chance. In this work, we extend previous results by providing games of chance capable of characterising uncertainty of quantum bipartite states.

Lastly, we provide an alternative approach for characterising the entropy of a quantum channel
Gour_2021
, which is operationally motivated by games of chance. Our results reflect that entanglement is a resource
Chitambar_2019
;
Sparaciari2020firstlawofgeneral
, as entanglement-preserving unitary channels strictly outperform entanglement-breaking channels. We additionally compute the reward function for key quantum gambling games with several special classes of quantum channels, such as unitary, amplitude damping, depolarizing, measurement, and dephasing channels.

III
Axiomatic Approach to Quantum Conditional Entropy

We begin by completely characterising the set of bipartite quantum channels which are conditional entropy non-decreasing. A bipartite quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
is conditional entropy non-decreasing if for any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, the state
\mathcal{N}(\rho_{AB})
has at least as much conditional entropy.

Since conditional entropy measures the uncertainty of system
A
when one is given access to system
B
, evidently allowing information to leak from system
A
to system
B
could decrease conditional entropy. This gives rise to the semi-causality requirement, which is defined as follows

Definition 1
.

A bipartite channel
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}
is
A\not\to B^{\prime}
semi-causal if any channel
\mathcal{M}_{A\rightarrow A}
that Alice applies to her system cannot be detected by Bob. Formally, this requirement can be stated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{M}_{A\rightarrow A}=\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}},

(2)

for all
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{CPTP}\left(A\rightarrow A\right)
and where
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}
indicates that the channel output in system
A
is traced out.

1
for a depiction of the semi-causality requirement.

\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}

for every state
\rho_{AB}

Finally, we reframe the definition of semi-causality in terms of Choi matrices

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\in\text{CPTP}(AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime})
. If
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}
is
A\not\to B^{\prime}
semi-causal, then its Choi matrix
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{AB\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}
satisfies

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{ABB^{\prime}}=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes J^{\mathcal{N}}_{BB^{\prime}}

(4)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{A^{\prime}\rightarrow A}
be the completely randomising channel which maps any input to the maximally mixed state
\mathbf{u}_{A}
. Since
\mathcal{N}
is semi-causal, then

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\tilde{A}}\left[\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A\rightarrow A}\right]=\text{Tr}_{\tilde{A}}\left[\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\right]

(5)

and
J^{\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{R}}_{ABB^{\prime}}=J^{\mathcal{N}}_{ABB^{\prime}}
. We note that the Choi matrix for
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{R}
is then equivalent to the Choi matrix for
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}
and find

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{ABB^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{|A|}\sum_{k,l=1}^{|B|}\ket{ik}\!\!\bra{jl}_{AB}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{R}\left(\ket{i}\!\!\bra{j}\right)\otimes\ket{k}\!\!\bra{l}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sum_{k,l=1}^{|B|}\ket{k}\!\!\bra{l}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}\left(I_{A}\otimes\ket{k}\!\!\bra{l}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes M

(6)

where
M=\sum_{k,l=1}^{|B|}\ket{k}\!\!\bra{l}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow B^{\prime}}\left(I_{A}\otimes\ket{k}\!\!\bra{l}\right)
. Upon taking the partial trace of
A
over both sides, then
M=J^{\mathcal{N}}_{BB^{\prime}}
and the theorem follows.
∎

In addition to semi-causality, any entropy non-decreasing channel must map states of maximal conditional entropy (i.e. states of the form
\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
) to other states of maximal conditional entropy. We define channels satisfying this condition to be conditionally unital

Definition 2
.

A bipartite channel
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow{AB^{\prime}}}
is a conditional unital channel if for all
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}\left(B\right)
, there exists a state
\sigma_{B^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{D}\left(B^{\prime}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B^{\prime}}.

(7)

We can also reframe the definition of a conditional unital channel in terms of Choi matrices.

Lemma 3
.

\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CPTP}(AB\rightarrow\tilde{A}B^{\prime})
is conditional unital if and only if its Choi matrix
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{AB\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}
satisfies

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}=J^{\mathcal{N}}_{BB^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\tilde{A}}.

(8)

Proof.

Recall that the definition of a conditional unital channel can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\Big[J^{\mathcal{N}}_{AB\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\left(\rho_{B}\right)^{T}\otimes I_{\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\right)\Big]

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\Big[J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\left(\left(\rho_{B}\right)^{T}\otimes I_{\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\right)\Big]

(10)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B^{\prime}}.

(11)

We now multiply both sides by
|A|\eta_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}
where
\eta_{\tilde{A}}
is traceless and Hermitian and
\omega_{B^{\prime}}
is a Hermitian matrix. It follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\Big[\left(I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B^{\prime}}\right)\left(\eta_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}\right)\Big]

(12)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\Big[\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\Big[J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{B}^{T}\otimes I_{\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\right)\Big]\left(\eta_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}\right)\Big]

(13)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\Big[J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{B}^{T}\otimes\eta_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}\right)\Big].

(14)

From this, it follows that for any orthonormal basis
\{\rho_{B}^{(i)}\}_{i}
of Hermitian operators, any orthonormal basis
\{\eta_{\tilde{A}}^{\left(j\right)}\}_{j}
of traceless Hermitian operators, and any orthonormal basis
\{\omega_{B^{\prime}}^{(k)}\}_{k}
of Hermitian operators, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\Big\{\rho_{B}^{(i)}\otimes\eta_{\tilde{A}}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}^{\left(k\right)}\Big\}_{i,j,k}

(15)

spans the orthogonal complement to
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}
.

We note that
\mathbf{u}_{\tilde{A}}
is the only matrix (up to a scalar) which is orthogonal to all traceless Hermitian matrices
\{\eta_{\left(\tilde{A}\right)}^{\left(j\right)}\}
. Thus,
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}
is spanned by
\{\rho_{B}^{\left(i\right)}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}^{\left(k\right)}\}
, so

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}c_{i,j,k}\rho_{B}^{\left(i\right)}\otimes\omega_{B^{\prime}}^{\left(k\right)}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\tilde{A}}

(16)

\displaystyle=J^{\mathcal{N}}_{BB^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\tilde{A}}

(17)

This concludes the proof.
∎

Having motivated the need for a conditional entropy non-decreasing channel to be semi-causal and conditional unital, we denote the set of all conditional unital,
A\not\to B
semi-causal channels in
\operatorname{CPTP}\left(AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}\right)
as
\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}\right)
. Then bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
is less uncertain than state
\sigma_{AB}
if one can obtain
\sigma_{AB}
using
\rho_{AB}
and a CUSC channel. This gives rise to the following pre-order.

Definition 3

(Conditional Majorization)
.

Consider two bipartite states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
. We say that
\rho_{AB}
conditionally majorizes
\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
with respect to system
A
, and write

\rho_{AB}\succsim_{A}\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

(18)

if either
|A^{\prime}|\geq|A|
and there exists an isometry
\mathcal{V}\in{\rm CPTP}(A\to A^{\prime})
, and
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}\right)
, such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\mathcal{V}_{A\to A^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)\;,

(19)

or
|A|\geq|A^{\prime}|
and there exists an isometry
\mathcal{U}\in{\rm CPTP}(A^{\prime}\to A)
, and
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}\right)
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{A^{\prime}\to A}\left(\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\right)=\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{AB}\right).

(20)

The isometries introduced in the definition above enable us to compare between bipartite states of different dimensions. Note that also majorization between two probability vectors of the same dimension can be extended to vectors of different dimensions in this way (by adding zero components to the vector with the smaller dimension so that the vectors become of equal dimension). Such extension is motivated by the fact that entropy functions don’t change under such embedding (see further discussion of it in
gour2021entropy
).
We are now ready to define conditional entropy based on the pre-order induced by conditional majorization.

Definition 4
.

A nonzero function
\mathbf{H}:\bigcup_{A,B}\mathfrak{D}\left(AB\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a quantum conditional entropy if it satisfies the following monotonicity and additivity constraints

1.

For all
\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
,
\rho_{AB}
, if
\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\precsim_{A}\rho_{AB}
, then
\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq\mathbf{H}\left(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime}\right)_{\sigma}
.

2.

\mathbf{H}\left(AA^{\prime}|BB^{\prime}\right)_{\rho\otimes\tau}=\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho}+\mathbf{H}\left(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime}\right)_{\tau}
for all states
\rho_{AB}
and
\tau_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
.

Note that a conditional entropy
\mathbf{H}
is defined on all density matrices in all finite dimensions. In particular, if
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
and if
|B|=1
then we write

\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho}=\mathbf{H}(A)_{\rho}\;,

(21)

where
\mathbf{H}(A)_{\rho}
is an entropy function; that is, it is additive under tensor products and monotonic under mixing (i.e. majorization).

If a given function
\mathbf{H}
satisfies the above conditions, then
\alpha\mathbf{H}
is also a conditional entropy for any
\alpha>0
. To eliminate this extra degree of freedom due to scaling, we consider only normalised conditional entropy functions such that when
|A|=2
and
|B|=1
(i.e. the bipartite state is a single qubit), then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\frac{I}{2}}=H\!\left(\frac{I}{2}\right)=1.

(22)

where
H
is the Shannon entropy. The above three axioms (i.e. including the normalization condition) are sufficient to guarantee several key properties of conditional entropy. We start by showing that conditional entropies reduce to entropies on uncorrelated states.

Lemma 4
.

Conditional entropy reduces to an entropy function on uncorrelated states. That is, for any product state
\omega_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}
,

\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\omega_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}}=\mathbf{H}(A)_{\omega}.

(23)

Proof.

Suppose that
\rho_{AB}=w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}
is a product state. Let
\mathcal{E}_{B\to B}\in\operatorname{CPTP}\left(B\to B\right)
be the completely randomizing channel on system
B
. Then it follows that
\mathcal{I}_{A\to A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{B\to B}\in\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AB\to AB\right)
and that

\mathcal{I}_{A\to A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{B\to B}\left(w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}\right)=w_{A}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{B}.

(24)

Moreover, let
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}^{B}
be the replacement channel on system
B
, which takes any quantum state to
\tau_{B}
. Then

\mathcal{I}_{A\to A}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{B\to B}\left(w_{A}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{B}\right)=w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}\ .

(25)

Thus,

w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}\succsim_{A}w_{A}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{B}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{A}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{B}\succsim_{A}w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}

(26)

which implies, by the monotonicity property of the conditional entropy, that

\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}}=\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{w_{A}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{B}}.

(27)

Thus,
\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}}
depends on
w_{A}
only. Moreover, the function
w_{A}\mapsto\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{w_{A}\otimes\tau_{B}}
satisfies the three axioms of entropy
Gour5
and therefore can be considered as an entropy of
w_{A}
.
∎

The same statement also holds when system
|B|
is trivial, i.e., when
|B|=1
.

Unlike entropy, quantum conditional entropy can be negative. In the following theorem, we find a lower bound for the conditional entropy and show that only entangled states can have negative conditional entropy.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be any conditional entropy, and let
A
and
B
be two Hilbert spaces. Then for any
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}\left(AB\right)
the conditional entropy is lower bounded as,

-\log\min\left(|A|,|B|\right)\leq\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho},

(28)

The lower bound is achieved when
\rho_{AB}=\phi^{+}_{AB}
is the maximally entangled state. Additionally, if
\rho_{AB}
is a separable (non-entangled) state, then
\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Given that
\phi^{+}_{AB}
can be used to teleport an arbitrary quantum state, it follows that there exists some channel
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AB\rightarrow AB\right)
such that
\rho_{AB}=\mathcal{N}\left(\phi^{+}_{AB}\right)
. It immediately follows that for any
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}\left(AB\right)
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}_{AB}}\leq\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho_{AB}}

(29)

Thus, it is sufficient to demonstrate that
\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}}
is lower bounded by
-\text{log}\left(\min\{|A|,|B|\}\right)
. To this aim, we define
d\triangleq|A|=|B|
and consider the state
\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}
where
|A_{2}|=d
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\left(AA_{2}|B\right)_{\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}\left(AA_{2}|BB_{2}\right)_{\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}B_{2}}}

(30)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}}+\mathbf{H}\left(A_{2}|B_{2}\right)_{\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}B_{2}}}

(31)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}}+\mathbf{H}\left(\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}}+\text{log}\left(d\right),

(33)

where the first line follows by introducing a trivial system
B_{2}
such that
|B_{2}|=1
and the second follows from conditional entropy.

We now demonstrate that
\mathbf{H}\left(AA_{2}|B\right)_{\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}}\leq 0
by demonstrating the existence of a channel
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AA_{2}B\rightarrow AA_{2}\right)
such that
\mathcal{N}\left(\phi^{+}\otimes\mathbf{u}\right)=\ket{\psi}\!\!\bra{\psi}_{AA_{2}}
for some pure state
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}\left(AA_{2}\right)
. Consider the Choi matrix

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}}_{AA_{2}B\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d^{2}}\phi_{AB}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes I_{A_{2}}\otimes\psi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}^{\left(j\right)}

(34)

where
\{\ket{\phi}_{AB}^{\left(j\right)}\}|_{j=1}^{d^{2}}
is an orthonormal basis of
AB
consisting of maximally entangled states and where we set
\phi_{1}=\phi^{+}
. Likewise,
\{\ket{\psi_{j}}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}\}|_{j=1}^{d^{2}}
is an orthonormal basis of
AA_{2}
.

Evidently,
\mathcal{N}
is trace-preserving and conditional unital (as
J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}=I_{B\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}=J^{\mathcal{N}}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}
). Additionally,
\mathcal{N}
is trivially semi-causal as subsystem
B
is traced out. From this, it follows that
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CUSC}\left(AA_{2}B\rightarrow\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}\right)
and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\left(AA_{2}|B\right)_{\phi^{1}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{H}\left(\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}|\tilde{B}\right)_{\mathcal{N}\left(\phi^{1}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\right)}

(35)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}\left(\mathcal{N}\left(\phi^{1}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\right)\right)

(36)

where
\tilde{B}
is a trivial subsystem. Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{AA_{2}B}\left[J^{\mathcal{N}}_{AA_{2}B\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}\left(\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\otimes I_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}\right)\right]

(38)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{AA_{2}B}\!\Bigg[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d^{2}}\phi_{AB}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes I_{A_{2}}\otimes\psi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}^{\left(j\right)}\right)\phi^{+}_{AB}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\otimes I_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}\Bigg]

(39)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{AA_{2}B}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d^{2}}\phi_{AB}^{\left(j\right)}\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\otimes\psi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}^{\left(j\right)}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{AA_{2}B}\Big[\phi^{\left(1\right)}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}\otimes\psi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}^{\left(1\right)}\Big]

(41)

\displaystyle=\phi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}^{\left(1\right)},

(42)

which is evidently a pure state. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\left(AA_{2}|B\right)_{\phi^{1}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{A_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{H}\left(\ket{\psi}\!\!\bra{\psi}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}_{2}}\right)

(43)

\displaystyle=0.

(44)

Upon combining everything, we see that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{H}\left(A|B\right)_{\phi^{+}_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{2}}

(45)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}_{AB}}+\text{log}|A|

(46)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

(47)

from which it follows that
\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\phi^{+}}=-\text{log}|A|
and therefore
-\text{log}\left(\{|A|,|B|\}\right)
lower bounds any density matrix
\rho_{AB}
.

Finally, we demonstrate that if
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}\left(AB\right)
is a separable state, then
\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq 0
. This follows from noting that any separable state
\rho_{AB}
may be written as

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\sum_{j=1}p_{j}\psi_{A}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes\phi_{B}^{\left(j\right)},

(48)

where
\{\psi_{A}^{\left(j\right)}\}_{j}
and
\{\phi_{B}^{\left(j\right)}\}_{j}
are sets of pure states. It follows that the CUSC channel

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB}\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}\mathcal{U}_{A\rightarrow A}^{\left(j\right)}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{B\rightarrow B}^{\left(j\right)}

(49)

satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho_{AB}

(50)

for a choice of unitaries
\{\mathcal{U}_{A}^{\left(j\right)}\}
such that
\mathcal{U}^{j}\ket{0}=\ket{\phi_{A}^{\left(j\right)}}
and a choice of unitaries
\{\mathcal{V}_{B}^{\left(j\right)}\}
such that
\mathcal{V}^{\left(j\right)}\ket{0}=\ket{\psi_{B}^{\left(j\right)}}
. Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{B}}

(51)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\mathcal{N}\left(\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{A}\otimes\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0}_{B}\right)}

(52)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho_{AB}}.

(53)

This concludes the proof.
∎

Although conditional entropies are positive on all separable states, this does not mean that the conditional entropies are only positive on separable states. In fact, some entangled states have positive conditional entropy on
all
choice of conditional entropy functions. The following lemma provides a simple criterion for finding states with positive conditional entropy.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a conditional entropy, and let
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}\left(AB\right)
with
d\coloneqq|A|=|B|
be a density matrix whose eigenvalues are less than or equal to
\frac{1}{d}
, and whose marginal state on system
B
is a maximally mixed state, i.e.,
\rho^{B}=\mathbf{u}_{B}
. Then, the conditional entropy on
\rho
is non-negative, i.e.,

\mathbf{H}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq 0\ .

(54)

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}B\to AB^{\prime}}
is a
\tilde{A}\nrightarrow B
semi-causal channel as shown in Fig.
2
and suppose
|B|=1
. For a semi-causal channel
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}B\to AB^{\prime}}
such that

\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}B\to AB^{\prime}}=\mathcal{E}_{R\tilde{A}\to A}\circ\mathcal{F}_{B\to RB^{\prime}}\ ,

(55)

\mathcal{F}
is required to be an isometry. When
|B|=1
,
\mathcal{F}
is always an isometry. In this case, the channel
\mathcal{F}
could be viewed as a bipartite state on system
RB^{\prime}
. Let this state be the maximally entangled state
\phi_{RB^{\prime}}^{+}
and let
|R|=|B^{\prime}|
. Then the resulting channel
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}
takes the form

\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}\left(\omega_{A}\right)=\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}\left(\omega_{A}\otimes\phi_{RB^{\prime}}^{+}\right)\quad\forall\omega\in\mathfrak{D}\left(\tilde{A}\right)\ .

(56)

Note that the channel
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}
is not necessarily conditional unital. It can be shown that channel
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}
is conditional unital iff the channel
\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}
satisfies

\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\phi_{RB^{\prime}}^{+}\right)=\mathbf{u}_{AB^{\prime}}\ .

(57)

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}
be a quantum channel that satisfies (
57
). Let
\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}
be the quantum state such that

\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}=\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{A}\to AB^{\prime}}\left(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{\tilde{A}}\right)\ ,

(58)

where
|1\rangle
is a pure state in
\tilde{A}
. We then have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\left(A|B^{\prime}\right)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}\left(A|B^{\prime}\right)_{\mathcal{N}\left(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|\right)}

(59)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{H}\left(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{\tilde{A}}\right)

(60)

\displaystyle=0.

(61)

To simplify the notation, we rename system
B^{\prime}
as
B
. Since
|R|=|B|=|\tilde{B}|
, we rename
R
as
\tilde{B}
. Then, the channel
\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}R\to A}
becomes
\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}B\to A}
, and its Choi matrix is denoted by
J^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}A}
. By (
57
), we have

J^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}}=I_{A\tilde{B}}\quad\text{and}\quad J^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{B}A}=I_{\tilde{B}A}\ .

(62)

Now, let
\rho_{AB}
be as in the lemma, and define the channel
\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}B\to A}
via its Choi matrix

J^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}A}\coloneqq|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\left(d\rho_{A\tilde{B}}\right)+\sum_{x=2}^{d}|x\rangle\!\langle x|_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\frac{I_{A\tilde{B}}-d\rho_{A\tilde{B}}}{d-1}\ .

(63)

Note that the matrix above is positive semidefinite since
I_{A\tilde{B}}\geq d\rho_{A\tilde{B}}
and it satisfies the two conditions in (
62
) since
\rho_{B}=\mathbf{u}_{B}
. With this choice of
\mathcal{E}
we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{AB}=\mathcal{E}_{\tilde{A}R\to A}\left(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\phi_{RB^{\prime}}^{+}\right)

(64)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}}\left[\left(J^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}A}\otimes I^{B}\right)\left(|1\rangle\!\langle 1|_{\tilde{A}}\otimes\left(\phi_{\tilde{B}B}^{+}\right)^{T_{\tilde{B}}}\otimes I_{A}\right)\right]

(65)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{\tilde{B}}\left[\left(\rho_{A\tilde{B}}\otimes I_{B}\right)\left(\Phi_{\tilde{B}B}\right)^{T_{\tilde{B}}}\otimes I_{A}\right]

(66)

\displaystyle=\rho_{AB},

(67)

where
T_{\tilde{B}}
is the partial transposition map on system
\tilde{B}
. This completes the proof that the conditional entropy of
\sigma_{AB}
is non-negative.
∎

IV
Examples of Conditional Entropies and Duality Relations

We now demonstrate that any quantum relative entropy can be used to generate a corresponding quantum conditional entropy.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a quantum relative entropy. Then the function

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\downarrow}\left(A\big|B\right)_{\rho}\triangleq\mathrm{log}\big|A\big|-\mathbf{D}\left(\rho_{AB}\big\|\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)

is a quantum conditional entropy.

Proof.

First, we demonstrate that
\mathbf{H}
satisfies the monotonicity property of conditional entropy. Let
\mathcal{N}\in\text{CUSC}(AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
and consider the bipartite density matrix
\rho_{AB}
. We begin by considering the case where
A=A^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\downarrow}(A\big|B)_{\mathcal{N}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\text{log}|A|-\mathbf{D}\left(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{AB})\big\|\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\mathcal{N}(\rho_{AB})\right]\right)

(68)

and from using the fact that
\mathcal{N}
is semi-causal and conditionally unital, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\mathcal{N}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)\right]

(69)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

(70)

from which we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\downarrow}(A\big|B)_{\mathcal{N}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\text{log}|A|-\mathbf{D}\left(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{AB})\big\|\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\right)

(71)

\displaystyle\geq\text{log}|A|-\mathbf{D}\left(\rho_{AB}\big\|\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)

(72)

where the last line follows from the data processing inequality.

We now prove that any entropy
\mathbf{H}
satisfying the form given in the theorem statement is invariant under isometries. Consider any isometry channel
\mathcal{V}\in\text{CPTP}(A\rightarrow A^{\prime})
and let
C
represent a Hilbert space where
|C|=|A^{\prime}|-|A|
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\downarrow}\left(A\big|B\right)_{\mathcal{V}(\rho)}=\text{log}|A^{\prime}|-\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{V}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})\big\|\mathbf{u}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\rho_{B})

(73)

Clearly, if
\mathcal{V}
is unitary such that
A\simeq A^{\prime}
, then
\mathbf{H}(A^{\prime}\big|B)_{\mathcal{V}(\rho)}=\mathbf{H}(A\big|B)_{\rho}
. Then w.l.o.g. we assume that

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle=\rho_{AB}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{CB}

(74)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{AB}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}_{CB}\end{pmatrix}

(75)

since the conditional entropy is invariant under unitaries. We can additionally rewrite
\mathbf{u}_{A^{\prime}}=t\mathbf{u}_{A}\oplus(1-t)\mathbf{u}_{C}
where
t\triangleq\frac{|A|}{|A^{\prime}|}
. From this, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{V}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})\big\|\mathbf{u}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\rho_{B})

(76)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}\left(\rho_{AB}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{CB}\big\|\left(t\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)\oplus\left(\left(1-t\right)\mathbf{u}_{C}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)\right)

(77)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}\left(\rho_{AB}\big\|\left(t\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)\right)

(78)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}\left(\rho_{AB}\big\|\left(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)\right)-\text{log}(t)

(79)

such that upon substitution, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\downarrow}\left(A\big|B\right)_{\mathcal{V}(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\text{log}|A^{\prime}|-\mathbf{D}\left(\rho_{AB}\big\|\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)+\text{log}(t)

(80)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}\left(A\big|B\right)_{\rho}

(81)

The additive property of
\mathbf{H}
follows immediately from the additive property of
D
. Finally, when
\mathbf{u}=\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\left(A\big|B\right)_{\mathbf{u}}=\text{log}(2)-\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{u}\|\mathbf{u})=1

(82)

and so normalisation is also satisfied.
∎

It follows that the von-Neumann conditional entropy is a conditional entropy under our axiomatic approach. The von-Neumann conditional entropy is defined as

\displaystyle H\left(A\big|B\right)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\triangleq\text{log}|A|-D(\rho_{AB}\big|\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

(83)

where
D
is the Umegaki relative entropy such that for any
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
, then

\displaystyle D(\rho_{AB}\big|\sigma_{AB})=\text{Tr}\left[\rho_{AB}\left(\text{log}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)-\text{log}\left(\sigma_{AB}\right)\right)\right]

(84)

Finally, we define the dual of a conditional entropy function and demonstrate that the conditional von Neumann entropy is self-dual.

Definition 5
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a conditional entropy. For any density matrix
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
with a purification
\varphi\in\mathfrak{D}(ABC)
where
\rho_{AB}=\text{Tr}_{C}(\varphi_{ABC})
the dual of
\mathbf{H}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{dual}}\left(A\ \big|\ B\right)_{\rho}\triangleq-\mathbf{H}\left(A\ \big|\ C\right)_{\rho}

(85)

Let
H
be the conditional von-Neumann entropy. Then it follows from the relation
H\left(A\big|B\right)_{\varphi}+H\left(A\big|C\right)_{\varphi}=0
that

\displaystyle H^{\text{dual}}\left(A\big|B\right)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-H(A\big|C)_{\varphi}

(86)

\displaystyle=H\left(A\big|B\right)

(87)

and therefore the conditional von-Neumann entropy is self-dual.

V
Quantum conditional majorization reduces to classical conditional majorization

In
Gour
, a pre-order
\succsim_{C}
between two joint probability distributions (i.e. classical states) denotes
classical conditional majorization
. In this section, we demonstrate that the axiomatic approach to quantum conditional majorization defined in the previous section is a generalization of classical conditional majorization. To this aim, we demonstrate that when we restrict the underlying bipartite quantum states to classical states, the quantum conditional majorization is equivalent to the classical conditional majorization.

Suppose the system
A\coloneqq X
is classical, we call a channel
\mathcal{M}\in
CPTP
(XB\to XB^{\prime})

conditionally doubly stochastic
(CDS in short) if it has the following form

\mathcal{M}_{YB\to YB^{\prime}}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{D}_{X\to X}^{(j)}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}

(88)

where each
\mathcal{D}{(j)}
is a classical doubly stochastic channel and each
\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}
is a completely positive (CP) map such that
\sum_{j}\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}
is CPTP.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose all the systems involved are classical with
A\coloneqq X
,
B\coloneqq Y
, and
B^{\prime}\coloneqq Y^{\prime}
. Then we have

\operatorname{CDS}(XY\to XY^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{CUSC}(XY\to XY^{\prime})

(89)

Proof.

Recall that if
\mathcal{M}\in\text{CDS}(XY\rightarrow XY^{\prime})
, there must exist a set of classical doubly stochastic channels
\{\mathcal{D}^{(j)}\}
and a quantum instrument
\{\mathcal{F}_{Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}}^{(j)}\}
s.t.

\mathcal{M}_{AB\to AB^{\prime}}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{D}_{A\to A}^{(j)}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}

(90)

Then for any input of the form
\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{AB\to AB^{\prime}}(\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\mathcal{D}_{A\to A}^{(j)}(\mathbf{u}_{A})\otimes\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\sigma_{B})

(91)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sum_{j}\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\sigma_{B})

(92)

and therefore
\mathcal{M}
is conditionally unital. (Note that the third line of the above follows from the fact that
\mathcal{D}^{(j)}
is doubly stochastic, so
\mathcal{D}^{(j)}(\mathbf{u}_{A})=\mathbf{u}_{A}
for all
j
.)

Finally, we show that
\mathcal{M}
is semi-causal as follows:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{A}

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{T}_{A\rightarrow A}\right]=\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\sum_{j}\mathcal{D}_{A\to A}^{(j)}\circ\mathcal{T}_{A\rightarrow A}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}\right]

(93)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\mathcal{D}_{A\to A}^{(j)}\circ\mathcal{T}_{A\rightarrow A}\right]\otimes\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}

(94)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\mathcal{D}_{A\to A}^{(j)}\right]\otimes\mathcal{F}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{(j)}

(95)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{A}\left[\mathcal{M}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}\right]

(96)

Given that
\mathcal{M}
is conditionally unital and semi-causal, then the theorem statement follows.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
\rho_{XY}
and
\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}
, be classical bipartite states such that
A=X
,
B=Y
, and
B^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}
are all classical systems. Then
\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}\precsim\rho_{XY}
if and only if
\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}\precsim_{C}\rho_{XY}
where
\precsim_{C}
is the classical conditional majorization defined in
Gour
.

Proof.

It then immediately follows from lemma
6
that
\rho_{XY}\succsim\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}
as in definition 4.

To show the other direction, we suppose that
\rho_{XY}\succsim\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}
as in definition 4. That is, there exists
\mathcal{N}\in\operatorname{CUSC}(XY\to XY^{\prime})
such that

\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}=\mathcal{N}_{XY\to XY^{\prime}}(\rho_{XY})\ .

(97)

Since
\mathcal{N}
is semi-causal, it has the form

\mathcal{N}_{XY\to XY^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}_{X\to X}^{(j)}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{Y\to Y^{\prime}}^{(j)}\ ,

(98)

where for each
j\in[K]
,
\mathcal{E}^{(j)}\in\text{CPTP}(X\to X)
and
\mathcal{F}^{(j)}\in\text{CP}(Y\to Y^{\prime})
with
\sum_{j}\mathcal{F}_{j}\in\text{CPTP}(Y\to Y^{\prime})
. Hence,

\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}_{X\to X}^{(j)}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{Y\to Y^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\rho_{XY})\ .

(99)

Since
\rho,\sigma
are classical, we have the identification
\rho_{XY}\cong P
and
\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}\cong Q
. Thus, the above equation can be expressed as

Q=\sum_{j=1}^{k}E^{(j)}PR^{(j)}\ ,

(100)

where for each
j\in[k]
,
E^{(j)}
is the transition matrix of
\mathcal{E}^{(j)}
, and where
R^{(j)}
is the transpose of transition matrix of
\mathcal{F}^{(j)}
. Hence, each
E^{(j)}
is column stochastic and
R\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{k}R^{(j)}
is row stochastic. Let
\{\mathbf{p}_{y}\}_{y\in[n]}
be the columns of
P
, and observe that in components form, the above relation can be expressed as

q_{xw}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{y=1}^{n}(E^{(j)}\mathbf{p}_{y})_{x}r_{yw}^{(j)}\ .

(101)

So far we only used the constraint that
\mathcal{N}
is semi-causal. The other constraint that
\mathcal{N}
is conditionally unital implies that if
p_{xy}=\frac{1}{m}p_{y}
then
q_{yw}=\frac{1}{m}q_{w}
. Therefore, for simplicity we take
p_{xy}=\frac{1}{m}\delta_{y}{y_{0}}
for some fixed
y_{0}\in[n]
. Then the above equation becomes

\frac{1}{m}q_{w}=\sum_{j}(E^{(j)}\mathbf{u})_{x}r^{(j)}_{y_{0}w}\ .

(102)

Summing over
x
on both sides of the equation, and using the fact that each
E^{(j)}
is column stochastic implying that
E^{(j)}\mathbf{u}
is a probability vector, we have

q_{w}=\sum_{j}r_{y_{0}w}^{(j)}\ .

(103)

Note that we assumed that
y_{0}
was fixed, and therefore as a result the equation above does not imply that
q_{w}
is independent of
y_{0}
if we allow
y_{0}
to vary. In other words, each value of
y_{0}
corresponds to a different
P
matrix and therefore possibly resulting in a different
Q
matrix. For simplicity, we rename
y_{0}
as
y
and denote

q_{w}=t_{yw}\coloneqq\sum_{j}r^{(j)}_{yw}\ .

(104)

By definition, the matrix
T=(t_{yw})
is row-stochastic, and with the above notations , we can express (
102
) as

\mathbf{u}=\sum_{j}\frac{r_{yw}^{(j)}}{t_{yw}}E^{(j)}\mathbf{u}\quad y\in[n],\ w\in[\ell]\ .

(105)

Therefore, the matrices

D_{(y,w)}\coloneqq\sum_{j}\frac{r_{yw}^{(j)}}{t_{yw}}E^{(j)}\quad y\in[n],\ w\in[\ell]

(106)

are all doubly stochastic. To see this, observe that for each
y
and
w
, the set
\{\frac{r_{yw}^{(j)}}{t_{yw}}\}
forms a probability distribution over
j
, and the fact that each
E^{(j)}
is column stochastic implying that
D_{(y,w)}
is column stochastic. Moreover, the relation
\mathbf{u}=D_{(y,w)}u
implies that
D_{(y,w)}
are row stochastic. Finally, for general probability matrices
P=[\mathbf{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{p}_{n}]
and
Q=[\mathbf{q}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{q}_{n^{\prime}}]
, we conclude that (
101
) is equivalent to

\mathbf{q}_{w}=\sum_{y=1}^{n}t_{yw}D_{(y,w)}\mathbf{p}_{y}\quad\forall w\in[n^{\prime}]\ .

(107)

The above relation is precisely the condition given in lemma 3 of
Gour
. Therefore, we conclude that
\rho_{XY}\succsim\sigma_{XY^{\prime}}
implies classical conditional majorization.
∎

VI
Connection to Games of Chance

VI.1
Games of Chance for Classical Bipartite States

In
brandsen2021entropy
, games of chance were utilized to find an ordering between classical bipartite states, and it has been demonstrated that such games of chance are an alternate, operational approach for characterising classical majorisation. We aim to likewise construct a set of quantum games of chance which provide an operational meaning for majorisation of quantum bipartite states.

Firstly, we briefly review the games of chance introduced in
brandsen2021entropy
for classical bipartite states. We first note that any classical bipartite state may be represented as a probability distribution of the form
\mathbf{p}=\{p_{yz}\}
where the corresponding state in quantum notation is
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{y,z}p_{y,z}\ket{y}\!\!\bra{y}\otimes\ket{z}\!\!\bra{z}
. Each game is fixed by a classical channel
\mathcal{T}
with transition probabilities
\{t_{w|z^{\prime}}\}
, as well as the bipartite state
\mathbf{p}
which is being used for the game.

The player is only allowed to access subsystem
B
(corresponding to the value of
z
), and the goal is for the player to correctly guess the value of
y
. The family of gambling games includes all possible gambling games that incorporate a correlated source. Given access to
z
, the player chooses
z^{\prime}=f(z)
and the host subsequently selects
w
from the conditional distribution
\mathcal{T}
. In general, the player will choose
f(z)
based on their knowledge of
z
, as well as the fixed distributions
\{p_{yz}\}
and
\{t_{w|z^{\prime}}\}
. Finally, once
w
is selected, the player communicates a set
\mathcal{S}
of
w
guesses to the host and wins if and only if
y\in\mathcal{S}
. The game is depicted in Fig.
3
.

\mathcal{T}

\text{Prob}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{p})

\mathcal{T}
with state
\mathbf{p}
.

VI.2
Extension of gambling games to quantum bipartite states

In this section, we build on the framework for classical games of chance to develop the most general games of chance for quantum bipartite states (and respectively quantum channels). The only restriction on such games is that the ordering induced by these families of games of chance must reasonably correspond to a measure of uncertainty. For example, states which are equivalent up to a unitary must have equivalent performance in these games of chance.

First, we provide a straightforward extension of the classical gambling games, and demonstrate that this direct extension is insufficient to capture necessary quantum effects such quantum entanglement being a resource. Next, we provide a broader set of games which still reduces to the same ordering on classical states, but which leads to negative entropy for maximally entangled states.

To generalize the classical gambling game, we first transform Bob’s observation of the classical output
z
into a POVM implemented by Bob on system
B
. Evidently,
y
can be written in quantum notation as
\ket{y}\!\!\bra{y}
such that a measurement in the computational basis allows perfect observation of
y
.

Likewise, Bob’s choice of
w
guesses is equivalent to sending an ordered list of all potential outcomes and winning if
y
is in the first
w
elements of the list. In turn, this can be transformed into performing an ordered measurement on system
A
with the output being transmitted to the host. For example, if their first guess is
2
, the second guess is
1
, and the third guess is
3
, then the they would implement the ordered measurement
\left\{\ket{2}\!\!\bra{2},\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1},\ket{3}\!\!\bra{3}\right\}
on Alice’s system. This game is depicted in Fig.

\hat{\Pi}^{*}_{z,w}\triangleq\big\{\ket{0}\!\!\bra{0},\ket{1}\!\!\bra{1}\big\}

To break this equivalency, we now introduce a more sophisticated game which allows an adversarial player to “scramble” the state of system
A
with some probability
p

G=(\mathcal{T},\mathbf{p})
by the classical channel
\mathcal{T}
with transition matrix is
T=(t_{w|z^{\prime}})_{w,z^{\prime}}
, and the probability
p
of adversarial involvement. The specific steps of the game are as follows:

1.

Alice and Bob are given a classical description of the state they share as the entries of the corresponding density matrix
\rho_{AB}
.

2.

The host generates
x\in\{0,1\}
from the probability distribution
\mathbf{p}=\left(p,1-p\right)
, where
p\in[0,1]
. If
x=0
, then the adversary is allowed to interfere by implementing a measurement
\hat{\Pi}^{*}
on system
A
.

3.

Bob is told which measurement the adversary implemented (if any), but is not told the result. He is then allowed to perform any measurement
\hat{\Pi}^{\prime}=\{\Pi^{{}^{\prime}(z)}_{B}\}
on system
B
and is told the measurement result
z
.

4.

Bob can choose any
z^{\prime}=f\left(z\right)
to input to the classical channel
\mathcal{T}
with corresponding transition matrix
T=(t_{w|z^{\prime}})_{w,z^{\prime}}
. The host draws
w
from
\{t_{w|z^{\prime}}\}

5.

Given
z
and
w
, Alice implements a final rank-one projective measurement
\hat{\Pi}(z,w)=\{\Pi^{(k)}(z,w)\}|_{k}
on system
A
. The rank-one requirement follows from noting that if Alice were allowed to choose a trivial measurement such as
\{I,0,\ldots,0\}
, she would win any game with certainty.

6.

Alice and Bob win if the outcome
y
is less than
w

In Appendix
A
, we demonstrate that the ordering induced by this extended quantum gambling game on classical bipartite states reduces to classical conditional majorisation. In the following, we will describe the reward function for the above games of chance and demonstrate that the induced partial ordering between quantum bipartite states distinguishes between entangled and unentangled pure states. Additionally, in the case where system
B
is trivial, the ordering reduces to standard majorisation.

First, we denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T},p}
the total expected reward for playing the game corresponding to
\mathcal{T}
and probability distribution
\mathbf{p}=\{p,1-p\}
. Evidently, since
X
is drawn before the players are allowed to make any moves, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T},p}=p\times\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T},0}+(1-p)\times\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T},1}

(108)

In other words, the total expected reward is a convex combination of the total expected reward when the adversary is allowed a measurement and the total expected reward when the adversary is not allowed a measurement.

Suppose that Alice and Bob have a fixed strategy- namely, that Bob implements measurement
\hat{\Pi}^{{}^{\prime}}
, selects
z^{\prime}=f(z)
, and Alice implements the optimal measurement
\hat{\Pi}(z,w)
. We first consider the case where
p=0
, i.e. there is no adversary. Then no action is taken prior to Bob’s first measurement
\hat{\Pi}^{\prime}
on subsystem
B
, and the probability of observing a measurement result corresponding to
\Pi_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}}(z)
is

\displaystyle P\left(z\big|\hat{\Pi}_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\left[\rho_{AB}\Pi_{\text{B}}^{\prime(z)}\right].

(109)

The corresponding post-measurement state is

\displaystyle\rho_{z}\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\text{B}}^{\prime},\rho_{AB}\right)\triangleq\frac{\Pi_{\text{B}}^{\prime(z)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}(z)}}{P\left(z\big|\hat{\Pi}_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)}.

(110)

In
any
game, Alice will always choose
\hat{\Pi}_{A}(z,w)
to maximize the reward. Namely, she will measure in the eigenbasis of
\rho_{z}(\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}},\rho_{AB})
with the first measurement element corresponding to the eigenvector of
\rho_{z}(\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}},\rho_{AB})
with the largest eigenvalue and so on. Then the success probability for this fixed strategy is

\displaystyle\sum_{z}P(z\big|\hat{\Pi}^{\prime})\sum_{k=1}^{w}t_{w|f(z)}\left\|\Pi_{\text{A}}^{(k)}(z,w)\text{Tr}_{B}\left[\rho_{z}\!\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\text{B}}^{\prime},\rho_{AB}\right)\right]\right\|

(111)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z}P(z\big|\hat{\Pi}^{\prime})\sum_{w}t_{w|f(z)}\left\|\text{Tr}_{B}\left[\frac{\Pi_{\text{B}}^{\prime(z)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}(z)}}{P\left(z\Big|\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{\prime}\right)}\right]\right\|_{(w)}

(112)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z}\sum_{w}t_{w|f(z)}\left\|\text{Tr}_{B}\left[\Pi_{\text{B}}^{\prime(z)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right\|_{(w)}

(113)

Naturally, Bob will wish to choose the remaining game components (that is,
\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
f
) to maximize their reward, s.t. the final reward function for the game
G=\{\mathcal{T},p=0\}
may then be expressed as

\displaystyle R_{G}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)

\displaystyle\triangleq\underset{\hat{\Pi}^{{}^{\prime}},f}{\text{max}}\left(\sum_{z,w}t_{w|f(z)}\left\|\text{Tr}_{B}\left[\Pi_{\text{B}}^{\prime(z)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{\text{B}}^{{}^{\prime}(z)}\right]\right\|_{(w)}\right)

(115)

Now suppose the adversarial player is allowed to interfere with probability p. If the adversary chooses measurement
\hat{\Pi}_{A}^{*}=\{\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\}|_{j}
, then the starting state
\rho_{AB}
will be transformed to
\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}
. Thus, following an adversarial measurement, Alice and Bob will effectively be playing the quantum gambling game with the transformed state
\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}
and will have an expected reward of
R_{\mathcal{T},0}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)
.

The adversary will wish to choose the “worst case”
\hat{\Pi}_{A}^{*}
to minimize Alice and Bob’s reward, leading to the following reward function for a general quantum bipartite gambling game

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},p}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)

\displaystyle\triangleq p\times\underset{\hat{\Pi}_{A}^{*}}{\text{min}}\left(R_{\mathcal{T},0}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)\right)

(116)

\displaystyle+(1-p)R_{\mathcal{T},0}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)

(117)

Finally, we are ready to introduce conditional majorisation for quantum bipartite states based on games of chance.

Definition 6
.

The state
\rho_{AB}
majorizes
\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}
with respect to subsystem
A
, based on quantum conditional gambling games, denoted as
\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}\precsim_{\left(A\right)}\rho_{AB}
, if

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},p}\left(\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}\right)\leq R_{\mathcal{T},p}\left(\rho_{AB},U\right)

(118)

for all
0\leq p\leq 1
,
\mathcal{T}=\{t_{w^{\prime}|z^{\prime}}\}
,
w^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,|A|\}
, and
z^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\max\left(|B|,|B^{\prime}|\right)\}
.

It follows immediately from the definition of the reward function that it is sufficient to restrict to the set of games for which
p\in\{0,1\}
, namely, games in which the adversary either always interferes or never interferes. In the remainder of this work we restrict to this set unless otherwise specified.

One key property of this partial ordering is that maximally entangled pure states outperform tensor product pure states.

Lemma 7
.

Consider the case in which
d=|A|=|A^{\prime}|=|B|
and
d\geq 2
. Then any tensor product state
\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}=\sigma_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B^{\prime}}
is majorized by
\phi_{AB}^{(+)}
, namely,

\displaystyle\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}\prec_{\left(A\right)}\phi_{AB}^{(+)}.

(119)

Proof.

First, we demonstrate that
R_{\mathcal{T},p}\left(\phi_{AB}^{(+)}\right)=1
for all
\mathcal{T}
and all
p
. To do this, it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},0}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\rho_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)=1

(120)

for all
\mathcal{T}
and all potential adversarial projective measurements
\Pi_{A}^{*}
. Since the adversarial measurement is projective, there exists some basis
\{\ket{\psi_{j}}\}|_{j=1}^{n}
and some partition of that basis
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
s.t.

\displaystyle\Pi_{A}^{*(k)}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\ket{\psi_{j}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{j}}

(121)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\phi_{AB}^{(+)}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j,k,\ell}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\ket{k}\!\!\bra{\ell}_{A}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\otimes\ket{k}\!\!\bra{\ell}_{B}

(122)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\sum_{k,\ell}\ket{\psi_{z_{1}}}\!\!\braket{\psi_{z_{1}}|k}\!\!\braket{\ell|\psi_{z_{2}}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z_{2}}}\otimes\ket{k}\!\!\bra{\ell}_{B}

(123)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\ket{\psi_{z_{1}}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z_{2}}}\otimes\sum_{k,\ell}\braket{\psi_{z_{1}}|k}\!\!\braket{\ell|\psi_{z_{2}}}\ket{k}\!\!\bra{\ell}_{B}

(124)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\ket{\psi_{z_{1}}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z_{2}}}_{A}\otimes\ket{\psi_{z_{1}}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z_{2}}}_{B}

(125)

Suppose that Bob’s strategy is then to implement the measurement
\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{\prime}=\{\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}\}|_{z}
on system
B
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\rho_{z}\left(\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}},\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\phi_{AB}^{(+)}\left(\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)^{{\dagger}}\right)

(127)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\ket{\psi_{z_{1}}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z_{2}}}_{A}\otimes\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\braket{\psi_{z}|\psi_{z_{1}}}\!\!\braket{\psi_{z_{2}}|\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}}{d\times P\left(z\Big|\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)}

(128)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}\otimes\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}}{d\times\frac{1}{d}}=\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}\otimes\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}

(129)

Finally, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},p}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\phi_{AB}^{(+)}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)\leq R_{\mathcal{T},0}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\phi_{AB}^{(+)}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)

(130)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z}P(z\big|\hat{\Pi}^{\prime})\left\|\text{Tr}_{B}\left[\rho_{z}\!\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\text{B}}^{\prime},\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\phi_{AB}^{(+)}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)\right]\right\|_{(1)}

(131)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z=1}^{d}\frac{1}{d}\left\|\ket{\psi_{z}}\!\!\bra{\psi_{z}}_{A}\right\|_{(1)}=1

(132)

To prove the theorem statement, we now need to demonstrate that there exists some game
G
such that
R_{G}\left(\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}\right)<1
. Consider the game for which
p=1
and set
\mathcal{T}=\{\delta_{w=1|z^{\prime}}\}
such that
w=1
with certainty. If
\hat{\Pi}_{A}^{*}
is a rank-one measurement in the basis which is mutually unbiased with respect to the eigenbasis of
\sigma_{A}
, then
\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}=\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}
and we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},1}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\sigma_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)

(133)

\displaystyle\geq R_{\mathcal{T},0}\left(\sum_{j}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\sigma_{AB}\Pi_{A}^{*(j)}\right)

(134)

\displaystyle=\max_{\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}}}\sum_{z}P(z\big|\hat{\Pi}^{\prime})\left\|\text{Tr}\left[\rho_{z}\left(\hat{\Pi}_{\text{B}}^{\prime},\mathbf{u}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\right)\right]\right\|_{(1)}

(135)

\displaystyle=\max_{\hat{\Pi}_{B}^{{}^{\prime}}}\sum_{z}\text{Tr}\left[\Pi_{B}^{{}^{\prime}(z)}\sigma_{B}\right]\left\|\mathbf{u}_{A}\right\|_{(1)}=\frac{1}{d}

(136)

Given that
\frac{1}{d}<1
, the statement of the lemma follows.
∎

Finally, we demonstrate that when system
B
is trivial, the ordering reduces to standard majorisation between quantum states.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\vec{\lambda}(\rho)
denote the vector whose elements consist of the eigenvalues of
\rho
. Then for any states
\rho_{A}
and
\sigma_{A}
,
\sigma_{A}\precsim_{(A)}\rho_{A}
if and only if

\displaystyle\vec{\lambda}(\sigma_{A})\precsim\vec{\lambda}(\rho_{A})

(138)

where in the above,
\precsim
represents standard majorisation.

Proof.

Both
\rho_{A}
and
\sigma_{A}
can be viewed as bipartite states with a trivial
B
subsystem (i.e.
|B|=1
.) Evidently, the expected reward for a given
\mathcal{T}
and games where
p=0
is then

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},0}(\rho_{A})

\displaystyle=\max_{z^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{w}t_{w|z^{\prime}}\left\|\rho_{A}\right\|_{(w)}\right)

(139)

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},0}(\sigma_{A})

\displaystyle=\max_{z^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{w}t_{w|z^{\prime}}\left\|\sigma_{A}\right\|_{(w)}\right)

(140)

and by considering games where
w
is fixed, the “only if” direction of the theorem statement follows.
It remains to show that
\vec{\lambda}(\sigma_{A})\precsim\vec{\lambda}(\rho_{A})
is a sufficient condition when games of chance with
p=1
are included. In this case, the adversarial measurement
\hat{\Pi}_{A}^{*}
will be in a basis which is mutually unbiased with respect to
\sigma_{A}
(or respectively
\rho_{A}
). Thus, for both games, the state after the adversarial measurement is
\mathbf{u}_{A}
. Then

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{T},1}(\rho_{A})=R_{\mathcal{T},0}(\mathbf{u}_{A})=R_{\mathcal{T},1}(\sigma_{A})

(142)

and so the ordering is unchanged by including games where
p>0
. The theorem statement then follows.
∎

VII
Quantum Channel Gambling Games

In previous work involving gambling games for classical channels
brandsen2021entropy
, the output of the classical channel was observed directly by the host. In the quantum case, where outputs are no longer necessarily orthogonal, the observation process is now represented through the projective quantum measurement
\{\Pi_{x}\}_{x=1}^{n}
. One natural extension of the classical gambling game introduced in
brandsen2021entropy
is to determine each game with a tripartite state
\rho_{ABX}
.

The corresponding game setup is then as follows:

1.

The player is given knowledge of state
\rho_{ABX}
and chooses a pre-processing channel
\mathcal{E}
on subsystem
A

2.

Channel
\mathcal{N}
is implemented on subsystem
A
. From here on, we denote this as
\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{ABX}\right)\right)
with the understanding that the channel action is only on system
A
.

3.

The player chooses a rank-one measurement on system
AB
. The player’s optimal choice will always be to measure in the ordered eigenbasis of
\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)
.

4.

The measurement outcome
y
is obtained and the player wins with a reward of
1
if
y\leq x
and loses with a reward of
0
else

We note that the rank-one restriction above is necessary in order to prevent the player from cheating with a trivial measurement (e.g.,
\{I,0,0,\ldots\}
) which would allow the player to win any game with any channel, thus preventing any meaningful ordering between quantum channels. This setup is depicted in Figure 1.

The resulting expected reward for channel
\mathcal{N}
and distribution
\mathbf{p}
becomes

\displaystyle R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{E}}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\mathcal{N}\left(\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\Big\|_{\left(x\right)}\right)\right)

(143)

where
\big\|\cdot\big\|_{\left(x\right)}
is the Ky-Fan
x
-norm.

Based on these games of chance, we can define a majorization relation between quantum channels.

Definition 7
.

We denote the case where channel
\mathcal{M}_{C\rightarrow D}
is majorized by channel
\mathcal{N}_{C^{\prime}\rightarrow D^{\prime}}
according to quantum games of chance as
\mathcal{M}\precsim_{q}\mathcal{N}
. Equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\precsim_{q}\mathcal{N}\leftrightarrow R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\leq R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)\ \ \text{for all}\ \ \rho_{ABX}

(144)

where it is sufficient to bound the dimensions of systems
A,B
by
\max\left(|C|,|C^{\prime}|\right)
. Likewise, the dimension of system
X
can be bounded by
\max\left(|C|,|C^{\prime}|\right)\times\min\left(|D|,|D^{\prime}|\right)
.

The bound for the dimension of
X
follows by noting that
\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)
has at most
|D^{\prime}|\times|B|
nonzero eigenvalues and likewise
\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)
has at most
|D|\times|B|
nonzero eigenvalues. Since
|B|\leq\max\left(|C|,|C^{\prime}|\right)
, the bound on
X
immediately follows.

Finally, we define the entropy of a quantum channel to be a function which is monotonic under channel ordering, namely

Definition 8
.

A non-zero function,

H:\underset{A,B}{\bigcup}\mathrm{CPTP}(A\rightarrow B)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

where the union is over all finite quantum systems
A
and
B
, is a quantum channel entropy if it satisfies the following two conditions:

1.

It is monotonic under channel majorization; i.e. given quantum channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, then

H(\mathcal{M})\geq H(\mathcal{N})\ \ \text{if}\ \ \mathcal{M}\precsim\mathcal{N}

(145)

2.

It is additive under tensor products; i.e.

H(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{M})=H(\mathcal{N})+H(\mathcal{M})

(146)

for all quantum channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
.

Operational Interpretation and Results for Special Classes of Channels

We begin by showing that when
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
correspond to states or classical channels, respectively, the quantum channel majorization defined by gambling games reduces to the standard vector majorization and the classical channel majorization introduced by our previous work
brandsen2021entropy
respectively.

Lemma 9
.

If
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
are replacement channels which output states
\rho_{\mathcal{N}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{M}}
respectively, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\precsim_{q}\mathcal{N}\ \ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \ \vec{\lambda}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{M}}\right)\precsim\vec{\lambda}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{M}}\right)

(147)

where
\vec{\lambda}\left(\rho\right)
denotes the ordered eigenvalues of
\rho
.

Proof.

See Appendix
B
.
∎

Previous work introduced classical gambling games parameterized by a correlated source
T=\left(t_{wz}\right)
where
t_{wz}
represents the joint probability
\operatorname{Pr}\left(W=w,Z=z\right)
. The expected reward for playing a
T
-game with classical channel
\mathcal{N}
with transition probability matrix elements
\{p_{y|x}\}
was likewise defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prob}_{T}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{z=1}\max_{x}t_{w,z}\sum_{y=1}^{w}p_{y|x}^{\downarrow}

(148)

where
p^{\downarrow}
indicates that
\{p_{y|x}\}|_{y}
is ordered such that
p_{1|x}\geq p_{2|x}\geq\ldots p_{n|x}
for all
x
.

We now demonstrate that when
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are both classical, the ordering induced by quantum gambling games is equivalent to the ordering induced by classical gambling games.

Theorem 4
.

If
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
are both classical channels, then

\displaystyle R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\leq R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)\ \ \forall\ \rho_{ABX}

(149)

if and only if

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prob}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)\leq\operatorname{Prob}_{T}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)\ \ \forall\ \ T=\left(t_{wz}\right).

(150)

Proof.

See Appendix
C
.
∎

We now provide an operational interpretation for quantum channel majorization.

Theorem 5
.

If
\mathcal{M}=\sum_{z=1}^{s}p_{z}\mathcal{V}_{z}\circ\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}_{z}
for some set of isometries
\mathcal{V}_{z}
, some set of preprocessing quantum processes
\mathcal{E}_{z}
, and some probability distribution
\{p_{1},\ldots p_{s}\}
, then:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\precsim\mathcal{N}

(151)

Proof.

We can demonstrate the theorem statement by substituting
\mathcal{M}=\sum_{z=1}^{s}p_{z}\mathcal{V}_{z}\circ\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}_{z}
into the expression for the reward function as follows:

\displaystyle R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{M}\right)=\max_{\mathcal{E}}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\Big\|_{x}\right)

(152)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{E}}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\sum_{z=1}^{s}p_{z}\mathcal{V}_{z}\circ\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}_{z}\left(\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\right)\Big\|_{x}\right)

(153)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\mathcal{E},z}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\mathcal{V}_{z}\circ\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\Big\|_{x}\right)

(154)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{E},z}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\Big\|_{x}\right)

(155)

\displaystyle=R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)

(156)

This concludes the proof.
∎

It follows immediately from the above Theorem that two channels which are equivalent up to a unitary will have equal performance for any game of chance. We now show that for a fixed input and output system, unitaries are the “least noisy” channel.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{A\rightarrow A}
be a quantum channel, and let
\mathcal{U}_{A\rightarrow A}
be any unitary channel. Then

\displaystyle R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{U}\right)\geq R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)\ \ \forall\rho_{ABX}

(157)

Additionally,
\mathcal{U}_{C\rightarrow\text{C}}\prec\mathcal{U}_{C^{\prime}\rightarrow C^{\prime}}
if
|C|\leq|C^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

We first show that unitary channels outperform any other channel of the same dimension.

\displaystyle R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{E}}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\mathcal{N}\circ\mathcal{E}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\Big\|_{x}\right)

(158)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)\Big\|_{x}\right)

(159)

\displaystyle=\max_{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\Big\|U\tilde{\mathcal{E}}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\left(x\right)}\right)U^{{\dagger}}\Big\|_{x}\right)

(160)

\displaystyle=R_{\rho_{ABX}}\left(\mathcal{U}\right).

(161)

The statement that higher dimensional unitary channels outperform lower dimensional unitary channels follow by setting
\rho_{AB}=\ket{\Phi_{AB}^{+}}\!\!\bra{\Phi_{AB}^{+}}
where
|A|=|B|=|C^{\prime}|
(namely, the dimensions of systems
A
and
B
are set to be equal to the input dimension of the higher dimensional unitary).

Thus,
\mathcal{U}_{C^{\prime}\rightarrow C^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{AB}\right)
will result in a pure state and perfect reward for any game. On the other hand, any game played with
\mathcal{U}_{C\rightarrow C}
will require some choice of preprocessing channel
\mathcal{E}_{C^{\prime}\rightarrow C}
, which is necessarily entanglement breaking.
∎

XI
Conclusions

In this work, we demonstrate that the set of
all
conditional entropy non-decreasing channels is equivalent to the set of conditionally unital, semi-causal (CUSC) channels. We then require quantum conditional entropy to be monotonic under the action of CUSC channels as well as satisfying simple axioms such as additivity and normalisation. This minimalist approach is sufficient to guarantee negativity of conditional entropy for maximally entangled states, and additionally is sufficient demonstrate that conditional entropy is non-negative for separable bipartite states. We discuss applications of this axiomatic approach to classical bipartite states and demonstrate that the resulting conditional majorization matches the classical conditional majorization first defined in
Gour
. Finally, we develop an operational approach for characterising entropy via games of chance, and apply this approach to both quantum bipartite states and quantum channels respectively.

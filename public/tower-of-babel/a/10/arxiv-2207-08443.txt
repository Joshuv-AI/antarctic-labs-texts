1.
Introduction

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a complex Banach space
X
and denote by
-A
its generator. We say that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded if
\mathbb{C}_{-}\subseteq\rho(A)
(the resolvent set of
A
) and for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

\left\|R(-\lambda,A)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{\text{Re}(\lambda)},

where, as usual,
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ \text{Re}(\lambda)>0\right\}
and
R(\mu,A)=(\mu-A)^{-1}
is the resolvent of
A
at
\mu\in\rho(A)
.
Recall that if
X
is a Banach lattice, we say that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is positive if, for every
t\geq 0
,
T_{t}
is a positive operator, that is

\forall t\geq 0,\ 0\leq f\in X\implies 0\leq T_{t}f.

More generally, we say that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is individually eventually positive if for every
x\in X_{+}
(the positive cone of
X
), there exists
t_{x}\geq 0
such that, for every
t\geq t_{x}
,
T_{t}x
is positive. We say that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly eventually positive if there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
such that for every
t\geq t_{0}
,
T_{t}
is positive. There are examples of concrete differential equations associated to operators, such as certain Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators, which generate uniformly eventually positive but not positive
C_{0}
-semigroups. See
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
for examples and complementary informations.

In this paper, a Banach lattice is the complexification of a real Banach lattice. We denote by
X_{\mathbb{R}}
its real part, so that, by abusing the notation,
X=X_{\mathbb{R}}+iX_{\mathbb{R}}
. We refer to
[
1
, Section C-I 7.]
and
[
15
, Section 2.2]
for more details. In such space, the positive cone
X_{+}
spans the entire space
X
. Examples of Banach lattices are
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu),1\leq p\leq+\infty
for some measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
and the space
\mathcal{C}(K)
where
K
is a compact space.
In fact, the space
L^{1}
on one hand, and
\mathcal{C}(K)
on the other hand, are, respectively, examples of Banach lattices called
(\text{AL})
and
(\text{AM})
-spaces. A Banach lattice
X
is an
(\text{AL})
-space if the norm on
X
satisfies

\forall x,y\in X_{+},\ \|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|,

that is, the norm is additive on the positive cone of
X
. A Banach lattice
X
is an
(\text{AM})
-space if the norm on
X
satisfies

\forall x,y\in X_{+},\ \|\sup(x,y)\|=\sup(\|x\|,\|y\|).

If
X
is a real
(\text{AM})
-space, then, by
[
15
, Proposition 1.4.7]
,
X^{*}
is an
(\text{AL})
-space. Moreover, by
[
15
, Section 2.2]
, the dual of the complexification of
X
can be identified with the complexification of
X^{*}
. In particular, if
X
is a complex
(\text{AM})
-space, its dual space is an
(\text{AL})
-space. We will use this fact in Section
4
.

In this paper, we give an estimate for the growth rate of
\|T_{t}\|
when
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded and eventually positive on a Banach lattice
X
where
X
is either an
L^{p}
-space, an
(\text{AL})
-space or an
(\text{AM})
-space. Note that the Kreiss boundedness condition corresponds to the first order estimate in the Hille-Yosida theorem which characterizes bounded semigroups. When
X
is finite dimensional, a Kreiss bounded semigroup is bounded but this is not the case if
X
is infinite dimensional, even when
X
is a Hilbert space, see
[
10
]
.

Denote by
\omega_{0}(T)
the growth rate of
T=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that is,
\omega_{0}(T)
is the infimum over all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
for which there exists
M\geq 1
such that, for every
t\geq 0,\ \|T_{t}\|\leq Me^{\omega t}
. If
B
is a closed operator on
X
, we define its spectral bound
s(B)
by

s(B)=\sup\left\{\text{Re}(\lambda)\ :\ \lambda\in\sigma(B)\right\}.

It is well known that we have

-\infty\leq s(-A)\leq\omega_{0}(T)<+\infty

and that the inequality
s(-A)\leq\omega_{0}(T)
can be strict, see
[
12
, Example 4.2]
. However, for a uniformly eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p},\,1\leq p<+\infty
, we have equality, see
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
18
]
. We also have equality if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is individually eventually positive on an
(\text{AM})
-space, see
[
3
, Theorem 4]
. For a Kreiss bounded semigroup, we have, by definition, that
\sigma(A)\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
so that
s(-A)\leq 0
. Hence, if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is positive on
L^{p}
or on an
(\text{AM})
-space, then
\omega_{0}(T)\leq 0
which implies that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
cannot have an exponential growth.

Our main results are the following. First, we prove that if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Kreiss bounded and uniformly eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p},\,1<p<+\infty
, then
\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{t}{\log(t)^{\max\{1/p,1/p^{\prime}\}}}\right).
Second, if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Kreiss bounded and individually (respectively uniformly) eventually positive on an
(\text{AL})
-space (resp. on an
(\text{AM})
-space),
we prove the stronger estimate
\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{1-\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
This result improves earlier estimates such as in
[
16
]
, where the authors prove that if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss and positive on
X=L^{p}
or
C_{b}(\Omega)
,
\|T_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}(t)
. Note that it is important for
X
to be any of the spaces defined above. For example, in
[
10
, Example 3.1]
, the authors construct a positive Kreiss bounded semigroup with exponential growth. However, when
X
is a Hilbert space, it has been proven recently by the first author of this paper that a Kreiss bounded semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
(not necessarily positive) on
X
satisfies the estimate
\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(t/\sqrt{\log(t)}\right)
, see
[
2
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
., we prove that an individually eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded if and only if it is Cesàro bounded, that is, for every
x\in X
and every
t>0
,

\frac{1}{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq C\|x\|.

This allows to verify any of the boundedness properties defined above using either the resolvent or the semigroup. We also prove that they are equivalent to stronger conditions, namely, the uniform Kreiss boundedness and the strong Cesàro boundedness properties, and equivalent to a weaker condition, the Abel boundedness property.

Next, in a third section, we prove the estimate for the growth rate of a uniformly eventually positive Kreiss bounded semigroup on
L^{p}
. In order to do so, we first obtain an auxiliary estimate in Proposition
3.2
which can be applied to the semigroup and the adjoint semigroup. Those inequalities together will allow us to get the desired estimate for the norm of
T_{t}
to obtain our first main result, Theorem
3.1
. We then discuss the discrete case, namely, we obtain a bound for the growth rate of the powers of a single positive operator, see Theorem
3.7
.

Finally, in a fourth section, we first prove the estimate for the growth rate of an individually eventually positive Kreiss bounded semigroup on an
(\text{AL})
-space using simple estimates and deduce a similar estimate for uniformly eventually positive Kreiss bounded semigroups on
(\text{AM})
-spaces, using a duality argument.

2.
Kreiss and Cesàro conditions for
C_{0}
-semigroups

The following definitions are well known and studied in the discrete case, that is, when working with the powers of a single operator, see for instance
[
5
]
and the references therein.
We start by giving the appropriate definitions of Kreiss and Cesàro boundedness properties in the setting of
C_{0}
-semigroups.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
and let
-A
be its generator. We say that :

(1)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded if
\mathbb{C}_{-}\subseteq\rho(A)
and for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

\left\|R(-\lambda,A)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{\text{Re}(\lambda)}.

(2)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Abel bounded if
\mathbb{R}_{-}\subseteq\rho(A)
and for every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\left\|R(-\mu,A)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{\mu}.

(3)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly Kreiss bounded if, for every
\alpha>0,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and every
t>0
,

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\beta s}e^{-\alpha s}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq\frac{C\left\|x\right\|}{\alpha}.

(4)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Cesàro bounded if, for every
x\in X
and every
t>0
,

\frac{1}{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq C\|x\|.

(5)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is strongly Cesàro bounded if for every
x\in X,x^{*}\in X^{*}
and every
t>0
,

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle|ds\leq C\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

Here,
C
denotes a constant which only depends on the semigroup.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
, with generator
-A
. If
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly Kreiss bounded, then it is Kreiss bounded.

Proof.

We know that
z\mapsto R(z,A)
is analytic on
\mathcal{U}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},\ \text{Re}(z)<-\omega_{0}(T)\}
and that for every
z\in\mathcal{U}
and every
x\in X
,

R(z,A)x=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{zs}T_{s}xds.

Define
f:z\mapsto\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{zs}T_{s}ds
. Let us show that
f
extends to an analytic function on
\mathcal{V}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},\ \text{Re}(z)<0\}
. Let
\mu>0
and let us extend
f
on
\{\text{Re}(z)<-\mu\}
. Let
z=\alpha+i\beta
with
\alpha<-\mu
. For
R>0
, integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}e^{zs}T_{s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}e^{-\mu s}e^{(\alpha+\mu)s}e^{i\beta s}T_{s}ds

\displaystyle=\left[e^{-\mu s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}e^{i\beta t}T_{t}dt\right)\right]_{0}^{R}+\mu\int_{0}^{R}\left(e^{-\mu s}\int_{0}^{s}e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}e^{i\beta t}T_{t}dt\right)ds

\displaystyle=e^{-\mu R}\left(\int_{0}^{R}e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}e^{i\beta t}T_{t}dt\right)+\mu\int_{0}^{R}\left(e^{-\mu s}\int_{0}^{s}e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}e^{i\beta t}T_{t}dt\right)ds.

The uniform Kreiss bounded property of
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
implies that the first term converges to
0
as
R\to+\infty
and that the integral from the second term will converge. Taking
R\to+\infty
yields

f(z)=\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(e^{-\mu s}\int_{0}^{s}e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}e^{i\beta t}T_{t}dt\right)ds.

It is easy to check that this expression is analytic on
\{z=\alpha+i\beta,\ \text{Re}(z)<-\mu\}
. Since this holds for any
\mu>0
, we deduce that
f
extends analytically on
\mathcal{V}
. Moreover, for every
z\in\mathcal{U}

-f(z)(zI-A)=-(zI-A)f(z)=I

and this identity extends to
\mathcal{V}
, that is, for every
z\in\mathcal{V}
,
z\in\rho(A)
and
f(z)=-R(z,A)
. Finally, for
x\in X
and
\text{Re}(z)<-\mu
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\|R(z,A)x\|

\displaystyle=\|f(z)x\|

\displaystyle\leq\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu s}\left\|\int_{0}^{s}e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}e^{i\beta t}T_{t}xdt\right\|ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mu s}ds\right)\dfrac{C\left\|x\right\|}{-\alpha-\mu}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{C\left\|x\right\|}{-\alpha-\mu}.

Letting
\mu\to 0
yields
\|R(z,A)x\|\leq\dfrac{C\left\|x\right\|}{-\text{Re}(z)}
, and this holds true for every
z\in\mathcal{V}
. This shows that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly Kreiss bounded.

(2)

There exists
C>0
such that for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, any
t>0
and any
x\in X
,

\frac{1}{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{irs}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq C\left\|x\right\|.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly Kreiss then for each
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
(e^{irt}T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is also uniformly Kreiss with same constant, so it suffices to show
(2)
with
r=0
.
Let
t>0
and
\alpha>0
. By integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha s}e^{-\alpha s}T_{s}xds

\displaystyle=e^{\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha s}T_{s}xds-\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\alpha u}T_{u}xdu\right)ds.

Hence

\frac{1}{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq\frac{Ce^{\alpha t}\left\|x\right\|}{\alpha t}+\frac{C(e^{\alpha t}-1)\left\|x\right\|}{\alpha t}.

Finally, choosing
\alpha=\frac{1}{t}
yields

\frac{1}{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq C(2e-1)\left\|x\right\|.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: let
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Write
R_{t}=e^{i\beta t}T_{t}
. The same computations as above give

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha s}e^{i\beta s}T_{s}xds=e^{-\alpha t}\int_{0}^{t}R_{s}xds+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}R_{u}xdu\right)ds.

Therefore, if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisifies
(2)
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha s}e^{i\beta s}T_{s}xds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq Cte^{-\alpha t}\|x\|+C\alpha\|x\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha s}sds

\displaystyle=Cte^{-\alpha t}\|x\|+C\alpha\|x\|\left(-\dfrac{te^{-\alpha t}}{\alpha}+\dfrac{1-e^{-\alpha t}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{C\|x\|}{\alpha}(1-e^{-\alpha t})

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C\|x\|}{\alpha}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
. Then,
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is strongly Cesàro bounded if and only if there exists
C>0
such that for each measurable function
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
,
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

(1)

\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds\right\|\leq C\left\|x\right\|,\quad\forall x\in X.

Proof.

Assume
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is strongly Cesàro bounded. we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\|x^{*}\right\|=1}\Big|\Big\langle\Big(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}ds\Big)x,x^{*}\Big\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\|x^{*}\right\|=1}\Big|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\epsilon(s)T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\left\|x^{*}\right\|=1}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle\big|ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|x\right\|.

For the converse, we fix
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
and define
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
by
\epsilon(s)=\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle}}{\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle}
when
\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle\neq 0
and
\epsilon(s)=1
when
\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle=0
. The function
\epsilon
is measurable and we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\langle T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle\big|ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\epsilon(s)T_{s}x,x^{*}\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds,x^{*}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|x\right\|\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

This completes the proof.
∎

The following proposition shows that the four notions defined in Definition

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an individually eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach lattice
X
. Then the following assertions are all equivalent.

(i)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Cesàro bounded,

(ii)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is strongly Cesàro bounded,

(iii)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly Kreiss bounded,

(iv)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded.

(v)

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Abel bounded.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
For
x\in X_{+}
, we let
t_{x}\geq 0
be such that for every
t\geq t_{x}
,
T_{t}x
is positive. Let
M_{x}=\sup_{u\in[0,t_{x}]}\ \|T_{u}\|
. Let
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
be measurable. Since, for
t\geq t_{x}
,
T_{t}x
is positive and
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Cesàro bounded we have, for
t\geq t_{x}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t_{x}}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds+\frac{1}{t}\int_{t_{x}}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq M_{x}\|x\|+\left\|\frac{1}{t}\Big|\int_{t_{x}}^{t}\epsilon(s)T_{s}xds\Big|\right\|

\displaystyle\leq M_{x}\|x\|+\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{t_{x}}^{t}|T_{s}x|ds\right\|

\displaystyle=M_{x}\|x\|+\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{t_{x}}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|

\displaystyle=M_{x}\|x\|+\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t_{x}}T_{s}xds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(2M_{x}+C)\left\|x\right\|.

We deduce that a similar estimate holds for a general
x\in X
.
It follows from the uniform boundedness principle and Proposition
2.4
that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is strongly Cesàro bounded.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
follows from Remark
2.5
(1).

(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)
follows from Proposition
2.2
.

(iv)\Rightarrow(v)
follows from the definitions.

(v)\Rightarrow(iv)
First, note that
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
holds true in the case of an individually eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup. Next, apply
[
1
, Corollary 1.3]
and
[
1
, Corollary 1.4]
to obtain
\mathbb{C}_{-}\subseteq\rho(A)
as well as the desired estimate for the resolvent of the generator of
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

(iv)\Rightarrow(i)
Let
x\in X_{+}
. By
[
1
, Definition 2.8]
, we have
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|<+\infty.

We may now apply the uniform boundedness principle and this yields
(i)
.
∎

3.
Growth rate of uniformly eventually positive Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups on
L^{p}
-spaces

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and let
L^{p}
be the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
. In this section, we study the growth rate of
\|T_{t}\|
when
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a uniformly eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}
. We recall that when
1<p<+\infty
,
L^{p}
is reflexive so it follows from
[
11
, Page 9, 1.13]
that
(T_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p^{\prime}}
. Moreover, this semigroup is uniformly eventually positive. The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Kreiss bounded and uniformly eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Then, as
t\to+\infty
,

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{t}{\log(t)^{\max\{1/p,1/p^{\prime}\}}}\right).

First, we need the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a uniformly eventually positive Cesàro bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for every
x\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and every
t\geq 1
,

(2)

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}ds\leq Ct^{p}\left\|x\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

Proof.

Let
t_{0}
be such that
T_{t}
is positive for every
t\geq t_{0}
.
We make slight changes to the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2]
to be able to use the Cesàro boundedness property. Let
t>2t_{0}+1
and
x\in L^{p}(\Omega)_{+}
. Let
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}([2t_{0}+1,t];\mathbb{R})
be nonnegative and such that
\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
and extend
h
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
by setting
0
outside of
[2t_{0}+1,t]
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{2t_{0}+1}^{t}h(s)T_{s}fds

\displaystyle=\int_{2t_{0}+1}^{t}\left(\int_{s-t_{0}-1}^{s-t_{0}}du\right)h(s)T_{s}xds

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t-t_{0}}\left(\int_{t_{0}+u}^{t_{0}+u+1}h(s)T_{s}xds\right)du

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t-t_{0}}T_{u}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+1}h(s+u)T_{s}xds\right)du.

Define
f:=\displaystyle\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+1}(T_{s}x)^{p}ds\right)^{1/p}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Let
M=\sup_{u\in[t_{0},t_{0}+1]}\ \|T_{u}\|
. By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\|f\|^{p}=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+1}\|T_{s}x\|^{p}ds\leq M^{p}\|x\|^{p}.

Now, note that, by Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+1}h(s+u)T_{s}xds\leq f

so that, by positivity of
(T_{r})_{r\geq t_{0}}
, we have

\int_{2t_{0}+1}^{t}h(s)T_{s}xds\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t-t_{0}}T_{u}fdu.

Taking the supremum over such
h
and applying
[
18
, Lemma 1]
yields

\left(\int_{2t_{0}+1}^{t}(T_{s}x)^{p}ds\right)^{1/p}\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t-t_{0}}T_{u}fdu.

We integrate the previous inequality over
\Omega
and use the Cesàro boundedness property to obtain

\int_{2t_{0}+1}^{t}\|T_{s}x\|^{p}ds\leq\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t-t_{0}}T_{u}fdu\right\|^{p}\leq C^{\prime p}t^{p}\|f\|^{p}\leq C^{\prime p}M^{p}t^{p}\|x\|^{p},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The previous proposition is in fact much easier for the special case
p=1
. Indeed, if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a positive and Cesàro bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
, then, for a positive
x\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
,

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|ds=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle T_{s}x,1\right\rangle ds=\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds,1\right\rangle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq Ct\|x\|.

The same applies to a uniformly eventually positive semigroup by splitting up the integral into two integrals.

Next, we prove that the estimate obtained in the previous proposition allows us to obtain the desired estimate for
\|T_{t}\|
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a reflexive Banach space
X
and assume that there is a function
f:(1,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R_{+}}
and a constant
C\geq 0
such that, for any
t\geq 2
and any
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|^{p}ds\right)^{1/p}\leq f(t)\left\|x\right\|\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T^{*}_{s}x^{*}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq Ct\left\|x^{*}\right\|.

Then, as
t\to+\infty
,
\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{f(t)}{\log(t)^{1/p}}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
t>2
and
2\leq\alpha<\beta\leq t
. For any
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle(\beta-\alpha)|\langle T_{t}x,x^{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\langle T_{t-s}x,T_{s}^{*}x^{*}\rangle ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\left\|T_{t-s}x\right\|^{p}ds\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\left\|T_{s}^{*}x^{*}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\beta\|x^{*}\|\left(\int_{t-\beta}^{t-\alpha}\left\|T_{u}x\right\|^{p}du\right)^{1/p}.

After taking the supremum over
\{\left\|x^{*}\right\|=1\}
, we get

\frac{(\beta-\alpha)^{p}}{\beta^{p}}\left\|T_{t}x\right\|^{p}\leq C^{p}\int_{t-\beta}^{t-\alpha}\left\|T_{u}x\right\|^{p}du.

Now we set
L:=\left\lfloor\log(t)/log(2)\right\rfloor
. For
0\leq l\leq L-1
, the latter inequality applied to
\beta=2^{l+1}
and
\alpha=2^{l}
reads

\left\|T_{t}x\right\|^{p}\leq 2^{p}C^{p}\int_{t-2^{l+1}}^{t-2^{l}}\left\|T_{u}x\right\|^{p}du.

Summing this inequality over
l
now gives

L\left\|T_{t}x\right\|^{p}=\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\left\|T_{t}x\right\|^{p}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}2^{p}C^{p}\int_{t-2^{l+1}}^{t-2^{l}}\left\|T_{u}x\right\|^{p}du\leq 2^{p}C^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{u}x\right\|^{p}du.

Hence, the assumption on
T_{t}
yields, for
t>2
,

\left\|T_{t}x\right\|^{p}\leq\frac{2^{p}C^{p}f(t)^{p}\|x\|^{p}}{L}

so that

\left\|T_{t}\right\|\leq\frac{2Cf(t)}{L^{1/p}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{f(t)}{\log(t)^{1/p}}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The same proof applies if we permute the role of
T_{t}
and
T_{t}^{*}
. In particular, if for every
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|^{p}ds\leq Ct^{p}\left\|x\right\|^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T^{*}_{s}x^{*}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}ds\leq Ct^{p^{\prime}}\left\|x^{*}\right\|^{p^{\prime}},

then

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{t}{\log(t)^{\max\{1/p,1/p^{\prime}\}}}\right).

We can now prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Since
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is uniformly eventually positive and Kreiss bounded,
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Cesàro bounded and by Proposition
3.2
,
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies (
2
). Moreover
(T_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}
is a uniformly eventually positive Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
so it is Cesàro bounded and, again, by Proposition
3.2
,
(T_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies (
2
) with
p^{\prime}
instead of
p
. Finally, by Remark
3.5
, we get the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

One can define, more generally, the notion of
\alpha
-Kreiss bounded semigroups. Namely, we say that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
\alpha
-Kreiss bounded for some
\alpha\geq 0
if, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

\left\|R(-\lambda,A)\right\|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\text{Re}(\lambda)^{\alpha}}+1\right).

If
\alpha=1
, it is straightforward to check that this is equivalent to the definition of Kreiss boundedness given in Definition
2.1
.
Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be
\alpha
-Kreiss bounded and positive on
L^{p}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
.

(1)

If
0\leq\alpha<1
then,
[
9
, Page 90, Remark 1.22]
together with
[
20
, Theorem 1]
show that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is in fact exponentially stable.

(2)

If
\alpha>1
, then adapting the results of this paper (replacing
t
by
t^{\alpha}
in various places and examining the proof of Lemma
3.4
) allow us to get

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(t^{\alpha}\right),\ t\geq 1,

retrieving
[
16
, Theorem 1.1 (3)]
. We leave the details to the reader.

Let us now discuss the discrete analogue of Theorem
3.1
. Let
T
be a bounded operator on a Banach space
X
. We say that
T
is Cesàro bounded if

\sup_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{n}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}\right\|<+\infty.

Similarly, one can define, for
T
(or for the discrete semigroup
(T^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
), the notion of (absolute, uniform) Kreiss boundedness. We refer to
[
6
]
for the relevant definitions. Following Section
2
of this paper, one can prove that for an individually eventually positive operator
T
on
L^{p}
, these notions are equivalent. Moreover, it is straightforward to adapt the results of Section
3
so that we can get the following result.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
T
be a uniformly eventually positive Cesàro bounded
on
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then,

\left\|T^{n}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{n}{\log(n)^{\max\{1/p,1/p^{\prime}\}}}\right).

Remark 3.8
.

This theorem improves
[
6
, Corollary 3.9]
where the growth rate that is obtained is
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(n/\sqrt{\log(n)}\right)
.

Question 3.9
.

(1)

In Theorem
3.1
, we assumed that the semigroup is uniformly eventually positive and the same assumption was made in
[
18
]
to prove the equality between the growth bound and the spectral
bound of its generator. It is not known whether this equality holds if we assume that the semigroup is individually eventually positive. Hence, it is natural to ask if Theorem
3.1
remains true if we replace ”uniformly eventually positive” by ”individually eventually positive.

(2)

In view of Section
4
, we can also ask whether the estimate in Theorem
3.1
can be improved. For example, if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a uniformly eventually positive Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}
,
1<p<+\infty
, do we have
\|T_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(t^{1-\epsilon}\right)
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
?

4.
Growth rate of individually eventually positive Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups on
(\text{AL})
-spaces and
(\text{AM})
-spaces

In this section, we estimate the growth rate of Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups on
(\text{AL})
and
(\text{AM})
-spaces. We will also make the assumption that the semigroups are individually eventually positive on
(\text{AL})
-spaces, and uniformly eventually positive on
(\text{AM})
-spaces.

Let
X
be a (real of complex) Banach lattice. Recall that
X
is an
(\text{AL})
-space if its norm satisfies the following additive property

(3)

\forall x,y\in X_{+},\ \left\|x+y\right\|=\left\|x\right\|+\left\|y\right\|.

Typical examples of
(\text{AL})
-spaces are the
L^{1}
-spaces. In fact, it is known that every
(\text{AL})
-space is isomorphic (as a Banach lattice) with a suitable
L^{1}(\mu)
, see
[
13
]
.

Next, we say that
X
is an
(\text{AM})
-space if its norm satisfies

\forall x,y\in X_{+},\ \left\|\sup{(x,y)}\right\|=\sup{(\left\|x\right\|,\left\|y\right\|)}.

For example, if
K
is compact, the space
\mathcal{C}(K)
, or any sublattice, is an
(\text{AM})
-space. As recalled in the introduction, the dual of an
(\text{AM})
-space is an
(\text{AL})
-space.

We first establish an estimate for the growth rate of certain
C_{0}
-semigroups on
(\text{AL})
-space, see Theorem
4.1
. Then, by a duality argument, we obtain, in Theorem
4.6
, a similar estimate for the growth of certain semigroups on
(\text{AM})
-spaces.

We will assume that all the Banach lattices are complex but it is easy to obtain similar results for real Banach lattices, see Remark
4.5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an individually eventually positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
an
(\text{AL})
-space
X
. Assume that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Kreiss bounded.
Then, there exists
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that, as
t\to+\infty
,

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{1-\epsilon}).

Let us start with the following lemma. It is slightly more general than we really need but that can be of independent interest.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
. Assume that there is an increasing function
f:[1,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
such that,

(4)

\forall x\in X,\ \exists K_{x}>0,\ \forall t\geq 1,\ \int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|ds\leq K_{x}f(t)\left\|x\right\|.

Define
F
on
(2,+\infty)
by
F(u)=\displaystyle\int_{2}^{u}\dfrac{ds}{f(s)}
. Then, there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(f(t)e^{-F(t)/C}\right).

Proof.

Fix a unit vector
x\in X
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The continuity of
s\mapsto\|T_{s}x\|
ensures the existence of
t_{n}\in[n,n+1]
such that

\|T_{t_{n}}x\|=\int_{n}^{n+1}\|T_{s}x\|ds.

Let
1\leq j\leq n
. We have

(5)

\displaystyle\|T_{t_{n}}x\|\leq\|T_{t_{n}-t_{n-j}}\|\|T_{t_{n-j}}x\|.

and since
j-1\leq t_{n}-t_{n-j}\leq j+1
, we have, by letting
M=\sup_{u\in[0,2]}\ \|T_{u}\|
,

\|T_{t_{n}-t_{n-j}}\|=\|T_{(t_{n}-t_{n-j})-(j-1)}T_{j-1}\|\leq M\|T_{j-1}\|.

Hence, by (
5
),

\|T_{n+1}x\|\leq M\|T_{t_{n}}x\|\leq M\|T_{j-1}\|\|T_{t_{n-j}}x\|.

From now on, assume that for every
k,\|T_{k}\|>0
, otherwise the result is trivial. Summing the latter inequality from
j=1
to
n
gives

\|T_{n+1}x\|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{\|T_{i}\|}\leq M\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|T_{t_{k}}x\|=M\int_{0}^{n}\|T_{s}x\|ds\leq MK_{x}f(n)\|x\|.

The uniform boundedness principle and the fact that

\|T_{n+1}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\dfrac{1}{\|T_{i}\|}\leq\|T_{n+1}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{\|T_{i}\|}+2M,

give the existence of a constant
C
such that

\|T_{n+1}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\dfrac{1}{\|T_{i}\|}\leq Cf(n).

By (the proof of)
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
, we get that

\|T_{n+1}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(f(n)e^{-F(n+1)/C}\right).

Finally, if
t\geq 2
, we let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
t\in[n,n+1]
so that we have

\|T_{t}\|=\|T_{t-n}T_{n}\|\leq M\|T_{n}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(f(t)e^{-F(t)/C}\right).

This concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

An individually eventually positive Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on a
(\text{AL})
-space
X
satisfies the assumption (
4
) of Lemma
4.2
with
f(s)=s
. Indeed, let
x\in X_{+}
and let
t_{x}\geq 0
be such that, for every
t\geq t_{x}
,
T_{t}x\in X_{+}
. Property (
3
) implies that

\int_{t_{x}}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|ds=\left\|\int_{t_{x}}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|.

Let
M_{x}=\sup_{u\in[0,t_{x}]}\ \|T_{u}\|
. By Proposition
2.6
,
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is Cesàro bounded. Hence, there is a constant
C\geq 0
such that
\left\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq Ct\|x\|
. In particular, for every
t\geq t_{x}
,

\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}xds-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq(Ct+M_{x}t_{0})\|x\|\leq(C+M_{x})t\|x\|.

Hence, for
t\geq t_{x}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|ds=\int_{0}^{t_{x}}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|ds+\left\|\int_{t_{x}}^{t}T_{s}xds\right\|\leq(C+2M_{x})t\|x\|.

If
1\leq t\leq t_{x}
, we simply have

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|T_{s}x\right\|ds\leq M_{x}t\|x\|.

This shows (
4
) for
x\in X_{+}
and since
X_{+}
spans
X
, we obtain (
4
) for every
x\in X
.

Now, we can apply Lemma
4.2
and, for this choice of function
f
and using the same notation as in the Lemma, we have
F(u)=\log(u)-\log(2)
so that

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}\left(f(t)e^{-F(t)/C}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(t^{1-1/C}\right),

which finishes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Conversely, for every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, it is easy to construct a weighted translation semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
(see
[
4
, Corollary 2.3]
for the discrete case) which is Kreiss bounded and such that
\|T_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{1-\epsilon})
.

As in Section
3
, we can prove the following discrete analogue (a similar result had been obtained in
[
14
, Theorem 2]
) of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
T
be an individually eventually positive Kreiss bounded operator on an
(\text{AL})
-space.
Then, there exists
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\left\|T^{n}\right\|=\mathcal{O}(n^{1-\epsilon}).

Remark 4.5
.

It is clear from the proof of Theorem
4.1
that if
X
is a real
(\text{AL})
-space and
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
an individually eventually positive and Cesàro bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
, the conclusion that
\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{1-\epsilon})

remains true. The same remark applies to the discrete case in Theorem
4.4
.

We can now prove a similar estimate for the growth rate of
C_{0}
-semigroups on
(\text{AM})
-spaces. We thank Johen Glück for his precious help in devising this proof.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
X
be an
(\text{AM})
-space and let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a uniformly eventually positive and Kreiss bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
.
Then, there exists
\epsilon\in(0,1)
such that, as
t\to+\infty
,

\left\|T_{t}\right\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{1-\epsilon}).

Proof.

Let
T:=T_{1}
. First, we show that
T
is Cesàro bounded (as a bounded operator), that is

\exists C>0,\ \forall x\in X,\ \forall N\in\mathbb{N},\ \left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}T^{k}x\right\|\leq CN\|x\|.

Let
t_{0}\geq 0
be such that for every
t\geq t_{0},T_{t}
is a positive operator. Let
x\in X_{+}
. By
[
17
, Proposition II.7.6]
, every nonempty relatively compact subset of
X
has a supremum in
X
, so
the compact set
\{T_{t}x,t\in[t_{0};t_{0}+1]\}\subset X_{+}
has a supremum
y\in X_{+}
.
Let
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
N_{0}\geq t_{0}+1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k\geq N_{0}
. Notice that for every
s\in[k-t_{0}-1,k-t_{0}]
, we have

0\leq T_{k}x=T_{s}T_{k-s}x\leq T_{s}y,

from which we deduce that

0\leq T^{k}x=T_{k}x=\int_{k-t_{0}-1}^{k-t_{0}}T_{k}xds\leq\int_{k-t_{0}-1}^{k-t_{0}}T_{s}yds.

Let
N\geq N_{0}
. We sum the last inequality over
k=N_{0},\ldots,N
to obtain

0\leq\sum_{k=N_{0}}^{N}T^{k}x\leq\sum_{k=N_{0}}^{N}\int_{k-t_{0}-1}^{k-t_{0}}T_{s}yds=\int_{N_{0}-t_{0}-1}^{N-t_{0}}T_{s}yds.

Hence, by Cesàro boundedness of
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and similar computations as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, there exists a constant
K
such that

\left\|\sum_{k=N_{0}}^{N}T^{k}x\right\|\leq\left\|\int_{N_{0}-t_{0}-1}^{N-t_{0}}T_{s}yds\right\|\leq KN\left\|y\right\|.

From this inequality, we easily deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}\dfrac{1}{N}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}T^{k}x\right\|<+\infty.

The same holds true for a general
x\in X
so, by the uniform boundedness principle, we conclude that
T
is Cesàro bounded.

Now, note that
T^{*}
is also Cesàro bounded and uniformly eventually positive on
X^{*}
which is an
(\text{AL})
-space. Hence, by Theorem
4.4
,
\left\|T^{n}\right\|=\left\|(T^{*})^{n}\right\|=\mathcal{O}(n^{1-\epsilon})
. Since
T=T_{1}
, it is now straightforward to see that we have the desired growth rate for
T_{t}
.

∎

A complete set of Lorentz-invariant wave packets and modified uncertainty relation
Kin-ya Oda, Juntaro Wada

Abstract
We define a set of fully Lorentz-invariant wave packets and show that it spans the corresponding one-particle Hilbert subspace, and hence the whole Fock space as well, with a manifestly Lorentz-invariant completeness relation (resolution of identity). The position-momentum uncertainty relation for this Lorentz-invariant wave packet deviates from the ordinary Heisenberg uncertainty principle, and reduces to it in the non-relativistic limit.

Introduction

Wave packets are one of the most fundamental building blocks of quantum field theory. We never observe a plane-wave state of, say, zero and infinite uncertainties of momentum and position, respectively. The plane-wave construction necessarily yields a square of the energy-momentum delta function in the probability, which hence is always divergent and is
more a mnemonic than a derivation
(quoted from Sec. 3.4 in textbook
[

However so far, it has been widely believed that there are no intrinsically new phenomena appearing from a wave-packet construction, but recent developments imply that it might play important roles in vast areas of science; see e.g. references in Introduction in Ref.

4
Uncertainty relations

We show how the uncertainty relation changes for the Lorentz-invariant wave packet.
We study the momentum and position uncertainties in the first two subsections and then discuss the uncertainty relation in the next. Lastly, we comment on the time-energy uncertainty.

4.1
Momentum (co)variance

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{\mathcal{O}}}

6
Summary and discussion

We have proposed a Lorentz-invariant generalization of the Gaussian wave packet. This Lorentz-invariant wave packet has more natural dependence on the central position
X
and momentum
P
than the coherent state in the position-momentum space: The dependence is holomorphic through the variable
X+i\sigma P
if we further generalize it for a time-like off-shell momentum
P
.

We have obtained the wave function for the Lorentz-invariant wave packet in an closed analytic form, as well as their inner products. The wave function is localized in space but not in time, while its width becomes larger and larger as the time is more and more apart from
X^{0}
.

We have computed the expectation value and (co)variance of momentum for this state in a closed analytic form, and those of position in the saddle-point approximation.
They reduce to the minimum position-momentum uncertainty of the corresponding Gaussian wave packet in the non-relativistic limit.
The time-energy uncertainty takes the same minimum value at the leading order in the large width expansion.

We have managed to obtain the completeness relation for these Lorentz-invariant wave packets in a manifestly Lorentz-invariant fashion.
It would be interesting to use the complete set of the Lorentz-invariant wave packets instead of the Gaussian ones in the decay and scattering processes analyzed so far.
It would be worth applying to the wave packets in neutrino physics too.

\hat{D}\hat{D}^{\dagger}=\hat{D}^{\dagger}\hat{D}=\hat{1}
, and that

\displaystyle\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-{\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|^{2}\over 4\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}}e^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}e^{-{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}}=e^{\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|^{2}\over 4\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}e^{-{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}}e^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}},

(173)

\displaystyle\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\over 4\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\beta}\right),

(174)

where we have defined
\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|^{2}:=\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|^{2}
.
We then get

\displaystyle\Braket{\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\boldsymbol{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\Bra{\varphi}\hat{D}\!\left(-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)\Ket{\varphi}=e^{-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\over 4\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}\Bra{\varphi}\hat{D}\!\left(-\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)\Ket{\varphi}=e^{-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\over 2}e^{-{\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}-\boldsymbol{\beta}\right|^{2}\over 2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-{\left|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right|^{2}+\left|\boldsymbol{\beta}\right|^{2}\over 4\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}}e^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}.

(175)

The ground state of the harmonic oscillator
\Ket{\varphi}
is given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\Ket{\varphi}

\displaystyle=0.

(176)

That is,
\Ket{\varphi}=\bigl.\Ket{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\bigr|_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{0}}
, namely

\displaystyle\Braket{\boldsymbol{x}|\varphi}

\displaystyle={1\over\left(\pi\sigma\right)^{d\over 4}}e^{-{\boldsymbol{x}^{2}\over 2\sigma}},

\displaystyle\Braket{\varphi|\boldsymbol{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\pi\over\sigma\right)^{d\over 4}e^{-{\sigma\over 2}\boldsymbol{p}^{2}}.

(177)

From the commutator

\displaystyle\left[\widehat{a}_{i}\,,\,\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{1\over n!}\left[\widehat{a}_{i}\,,\,\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\right)^{n}\right]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{1\over n!}n{\alpha_{j}\bigl[\widehat{a}_{i}\,,\,\widehat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\bigr]\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cdot\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\over 2\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right),

(178)

we see that
\hat{a}_{i}\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\Ket{\varphi}=\alpha_{i}\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\Ket{\varphi}
, namely,

\displaystyle\Ket{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\Ket{\varphi}

(179)

and that the similarity transformation of the annihilation operator results in its displacement:

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{\dagger}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}\hat{D}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\hat{a}}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

(180)

In this sense,
\Ket{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is the ground state of the harmonic oscillator displaced by
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
.

Appendix B
Master integral

We encounter an integral of the form

\displaystyle I\!\left(\check{\Xi}\right)

\displaystyle=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{p}\over 2E_{\boldsymbol{p}}}e^{p\cdot\check{\Xi}}

(181)

where
p
is on-shell and
\check{\Xi}
is an arbitrary complex
D
-vector of mass dimension
-1
.
For a massive particle
p^{2}=-m^{2}<0
, it is more convenient to use the
D
-velocity
u:=p/m
as an integration variable. Hereafter, we always take
u
“on-shell”,
u^{2}=-1
and
u^{0}=\sqrt{1+\boldsymbol{u}^{2}}
, unless otherwise stated.
Let us define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\!\left(\Xi\right)

\displaystyle:=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2u^{0}}e^{u\cdot\Xi}=\int\text{d}^{D}u\,\delta\!\left(u^{2}+1\right)\theta\!\left(u^{0}\right)e^{u\cdot\Xi},

(182)

where
u
is on-shell and off-shell for the first and second integrals, respectively, and

\Xi
is a dimensionless complex
D
-vector.
Trivially substituting
\Xi=m\check{\Xi}
, we get

\displaystyle I\!\left(\check{\Xi}\right)

\displaystyle=m^{d-1}\mathcal{I}\!\left(m\check{\Xi}\right).

(183)

So far we have not put any kind of on-shell condition on
\Xi
, and hence

\displaystyle{\partial\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-{\Xi_{\mu}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|},

(184)

where
\left\|\Xi\right\|:=\sqrt{-\Xi^{2}}=\sqrt{\left(\Xi^{0}\right)^{2}-\boldsymbol{\Xi}^{2}}
as given in the main text.

We write
\Re\Xi=:\mathcal{P}
and
\Im\Xi=:\mathcal{Q}
, which later will correspond to some momentum and position, respectively:

\displaystyle\Xi=\mathcal{P}+i\mathcal{Q}.

(185)

Trivially,
\Xi^{2}=\left(\mathcal{P}+i\mathcal{Q}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{P}^{2}+2i\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}^{2}
.

B.1
Evaluation of master integral

We focus on the case of
\mathcal{P}
being timelike,
\mathcal{P}^{2}=-\left(\mathcal{P}^{0}\right)^{2}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{2}<0
, and future-oriented,
\mathcal{P}^{0}>0
, so that there exists a (proper orthochronous) Lorentz transformation
\Lambda
to the “rest frame”
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}=\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{0},\boldsymbol{0}\bigr)
with
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{0}=\left\|\mathcal{P}\right\|>0
such that
\Lambda\mathcal{P}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}
.
For a given
\mathcal{P}
, concrete form of
\Lambda
is, in the matrix notation,

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}U^{0}&-\boldsymbol{U}^{\text{t}}\\
-\boldsymbol{U}&\widehat{1}+\left(U^{0}-1\right)\boldsymbol{\hat{U}}\boldsymbol{\hat{U}}^{\text{t}}\end{bmatrix},

(186)

where
U:=\mathcal{P}/\left\|\mathcal{P}\right\|
,

\boldsymbol{\hat{U}}:=\boldsymbol{U}/\left|\boldsymbol{U}\right|
,

\widehat{1}
is the
d
-dimensional unit matrix, and

U^{0}=\sqrt{1+\boldsymbol{U}^{2}}
;
see e.g. Ref.
[
28
]
.
We write
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}:=\Lambda\mathcal{Q}
and
\widetilde{\Xi}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}+i\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}
. Note that
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}}=0
implies
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\Xi}}=i\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}
.
On the other hand, we leave
\mathcal{Q}
to be an arbitrary real
D
vector.

We change the integration variable to
\widetilde{u}:=\Lambda u
. Using the Lorentz invariance of the integration measure etc. as well as
\left(\Lambda^{-1}\widetilde{u}\right)\cdot\Xi=\widetilde{u}\cdot\left(\Lambda\Xi\right)=\widetilde{u}\cdot\widetilde{\Xi}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{D}\widetilde{u}\,\delta\!\left(\widetilde{u}^{2}+1\right)\theta\!\left(\widetilde{u}^{0}\right)e^{\widetilde{u}\cdot\widetilde{\Xi}}.

(187)

Using
\widetilde{u}\cdot\widetilde{\Xi}=-\widetilde{u}^{0}\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\Xi}}=-\widetilde{u}^{0}\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}+i\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}
and renaming
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
by
\boldsymbol{u}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2\sqrt{1+\boldsymbol{u}^{2}}}e^{-\sqrt{1+\boldsymbol{u}^{2}}\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}+i\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{d-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}{\sin^{d-2}\theta\,\text{d}\theta\over\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\!\left(d-1\over 2\right)/\Gamma\!\left(d\over 2\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\textsf{u}^{d-1}\text{d}\textsf{u}\over 2\sqrt{1+\textsf{u}^{2}}}e^{-\sqrt{1+\textsf{u}^{2}}\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}+i\textsf{u}\left|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\right|\cos\theta},

(188)

where
\Omega_{d-1}=2\pi^{d\over 2}/\Gamma\!\left(d\over 2\right)
is the area of a unit
\left(d-1\right)
-sphere (boundary of unit
d
-ball).

We follow Ref.
[
23
]
in the following.
The angular integral reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{d-2}\theta\,\text{d}\theta\,e^{i\textsf{u}\left|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\right|\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(d-1\over 2\right)\left(2\over\textsf{u}\bigl|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr|\right)^{d-2\over 2}J_{d-2\over 2}\!\left(\textsf{u}\bigl|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr|\right),

(189)

where
J
is the Bessel function of the first kind.
(For
d=3

2\sin\!\left(\textsf{u}\bigl|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr|\right)/\textsf{u}\bigl|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr|

Now

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle={\Omega_{d-1}\Gamma\!\left(d\over 2\right)\over 2}\left(2\over\bigl|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr|\right)^{d-2\over 2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\varepsilon^{2}-1\right)^{d-2\over 2}\text{d}\varepsilon\,J_{d-2\over 2}\!\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-1}\bigl|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigr|\right)e^{-\varepsilon\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}},

(190)

where
\varepsilon:=\sqrt{1+\textsf{u}^{2}}
is a rescaled energy.
We use the second formula of Eq. (6.645) in Ref.
[
29

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\text{d}x\left(x^{2}-1\right)^{\nu\over 2}e^{-\alpha x}J_{\nu}\!\left(\beta\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\over\pi}\beta^{\nu}\left(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\right)^{-{2\nu+1\over 4}}K_{2\nu+1\over 2}\!\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}\right),

(191)

applicable for
\Re\alpha>0
.
Setting
\alpha=\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}
,
\beta=\left|\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}\right|
, and
\nu={d-2\over 2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\!\left(\Xi\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d-1\over 2}\bigl({\left\|\Xi\right\|}\bigr)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{{d-1\over 2}}},

(192)

where we used

\displaystyle\bigl(\widetilde{\Xi}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+2i\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{0}-\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{0}\bigr)^{2}+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}}^{2}=-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{2}-2i\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}=-\widetilde{\Xi}^{2}=-\Xi^{2}=\left\|\Xi\right\|^{2}.

(193)

The limit

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\to\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{\pi\over 2}{e^{-\left\|\Xi\right\|}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d\over 2}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\!\left(1\over\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\right),

(194)

whereas
\left\|\Xi\right\|\to 0
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\to\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{2^{d-3\over 2}\Gamma\!\left(d-1\over 2\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1}}\bigl(1+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\bigr)+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\ln\left\|\Xi\right\|\right).

(195)

Recall that we are assuming

Throughout this paper, we choose to place a branch-cut for a square root, say

For

In particular we may use the following limit for

Then for the expression (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\Im\left(-\Xi^{2}\right)=\Im\left(-\widetilde{\Xi}^{2}\right)=2\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}^{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{0}.

To summarize, no ambiguity arises from the branch cut as long as

B.2
Derivative of master integral

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\!\left(\Xi\right)

\displaystyle:={\partial^{n}\over\partial\Xi_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial\Xi_{\mu_{n}}}\mathcal{I}\!\left(\Xi\right).

(201)

We note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{n}}\!\left(\Xi\right)

\displaystyle=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2u^{0}}e^{u\cdot\Xi}u^{\mu_{1}}\cdots u^{\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{D}u\,\delta\!\left(u^{2}+1\right)\theta\!\left(u^{0}\right)e^{u\cdot\Xi}u^{\mu_{1}}\cdots u^{\mu_{n}},

(202)

where
u
is on-shell and off-shell in the first and second integrals, respectively.
Using

\displaystyle{\text{d}\mathcal{I}\over\text{d}\left\|\Xi\right\|}

\displaystyle=-\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d+1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{{d-1\over 2}}},

\displaystyle{\text{d}^{2}\mathcal{I}\over\text{d}\left\|\Xi\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}\left[-{K_{d+1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d+1\over 2}}+{K_{d+3\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1\over 2}}\right],

(203)

and

\displaystyle{\partial\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-{\Xi^{\mu}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|},

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi_{\mu}\partial\Xi_{\nu}}

\displaystyle=-{1\over\left\|\Xi\right\|}\left(\eta^{\mu\nu}-{\Xi^{\mu}\Xi^{\nu}\over\Xi^{2}}\right),

(204)

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\mu}={\partial\mathcal{I}\over\partial\Xi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d+1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{{d+1\over 2}}}\Xi^{\mu},

(205)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\mu\nu}={\partial^{2}\mathcal{I}\over\partial\Xi_{\mu}\partial\Xi_{\nu}}

\displaystyle={\partial^{2}\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi_{\mu}\partial\Xi_{\nu}}{\text{d}\mathcal{I}\over\text{d}\left\|\Xi\right\|}+{\partial\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi_{\mu}}{\partial\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi_{\nu}}{\text{d}^{2}\mathcal{I}\over\text{d}\left\|\Xi\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}\left[\eta^{\mu\nu}{K_{d+1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{{d+1\over 2}}}-{\Xi^{\mu}\Xi^{\nu}\over\Xi^{2}}{K_{d+3\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1\over 2}}\right].

(206)

By construction, we have an identity

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{I}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{I},

(207)

namely,

\displaystyle\left(d+1\right){K_{d+1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|}-K_{d+3\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=-K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right),

(208)

which is exactly the identity that the modified Bessel function satisfies.

B.3.1
Saddle-point method

We compute
\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\!\left(\Xi\right)
in the large
\left\|\Xi\right\|
expansion. Concretely, we compute the following integral using the saddle-point method in the limit
\lambda\gg 1
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\!\left(\lambda\Theta\right)

\displaystyle=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2u^{0}}e^{\lambda u\cdot\Theta}{1\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{n}}=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2}\,e^{F},

(211)

where

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:=\lambda u\cdot\Theta-\left(n+1\right)\ln u^{0}.

(212)

Note that
\Theta
is “off-shell” in the sense that
\Theta^{0}
is treated as an independent variable.
For reference, we list the derivatives of exponent before setting to the saddle point:

\displaystyle{\partial F\over\partial u^{i}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\Theta_{i}-{u_{i}\over u^{0}}\Theta^{0}\right)-\left(n+1\right){u_{i}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{2}},

(213)

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{ij}\left(\lambda{\Theta^{0}\over u^{0}}+{n+1\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)+u_{i}u_{j}\left(\lambda{\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{3}}+2{n+1\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{4}}\right),

(214)

\displaystyle{\partial^{3}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{ij}u_{k}+\delta_{jk}u_{i}+\delta_{ki}u_{j}\right)\left({\lambda\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{3}}+{2\left(n+1\right)\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{4}}\right)-u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}\left({3\lambda\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{5}}+{8\left(n+1\right)\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{6}}\right),

(215)

\displaystyle{\partial^{4}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{jk}\delta_{i\ell}+\delta_{ki}\delta_{j\ell}\right)\left({\lambda\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{3}}+{2\left(n+1\right)\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\delta_{ij}u_{k}u_{\ell}+\delta_{jk}u_{i}u_{\ell}+\delta_{ki}u_{j}u_{\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}u_{j}u_{k}+\delta_{j\ell}u_{i}u_{k}+\delta_{k\ell}u_{i}u_{j}\right)\left({3\lambda\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{5}}+{8\left(n+1\right)\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+u_{i}u_{j}u_{k}u_{\ell}\left({15\lambda\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{7}}+{48\left(n+1\right)\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{8}}\right),

(216)

and the eigenvalues of the Hessian (
214
) are
\left(d-1\right)
-fold degenerate

-\lambda{\Theta^{0}\over u^{0}}-{n+1\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{2}}

and a single

-{\lambda\Theta^{0}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{3}}+\left(n+1\right)\left({1\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{2}}-{2\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{4}}\right)
.

To obtain the saddle point, we solve

\displaystyle{\partial F\over\partial u^{i}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\Theta_{i}-{u_{i}\over u^{0}}\Theta^{0}\right)-\left(n+1\right){u_{i}\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0.

(217)

We put an ansatz

\displaystyle u_{*}^{i}

\displaystyle=\Theta^{i}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{C_{n}\over\lambda^{n}}

(218)

to get the solution

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle={1\over\left\|\Theta\right\|},

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=-{n+1\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}},

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle={\left(n+1\right)^{2}\over 2\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}}\left(1+{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=-{\left(n+1\right)^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}},

(219)

etc.
28
28

28

Without large
\lambda
expansion, we may directly put
u^{i}=C\Theta^{i}
:

\displaystyle\lambda\left(1-C{\Theta^{0}\over\sqrt{1+C^{2}\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}}}\right)-\left(n+1\right){C\over 1+C^{2}\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}}

\displaystyle=0.

We may write down analytic solutions for
C
explicitly, but we show the results for large
\lambda
since the analytic one is too lengthy.

Then at the saddle point,

\displaystyle e^{F_{*}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left(\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\Theta^{0}\right)^{n+1}\left(1+{\left(n+1\right)^{2}\over 2\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right).

(220)

Around the saddle point, we expand the integrand for large
\lambda
:

\displaystyle e^{F}

\displaystyle=e^{F_{*}+{1\over 2}\Delta u_{i}M_{ij}\Delta u_{j}}\Bigg[1+{1\over 3!}{\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\Delta u_{i}\Delta u_{j}\Delta u_{k}+{1\over 4!}{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\Delta u_{i}\Delta u_{j}\Delta u_{k}\Delta u_{\ell}

\displaystyle\phantom{=e^{F_{*}+{1\over 2}\Delta u_{i}M_{ij}\Delta u_{j}}\Bigg(}+{1\over 2\,3!3!}\left({\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\Delta u_{i}\Delta u_{j}\Delta u_{k}\right)^{2}+\cdots\Bigg],

(221)

where
we have shifted the variables as
\Delta\boldsymbol{u}:=\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{*}

and have neglected terms of order
1/\lambda^{2}
, with
\Delta\boldsymbol{u}
being counted as
1/\sqrt{\lambda}
.
The derivatives at the saddle point are

\displaystyle\left.{\partial^{2}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}}\right|_{*}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\left(\delta_{ij}-{\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)-\left(n+1\right)\left(\delta_{ij}-{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}{\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\delta_{ij}\left(1+{n+1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}+\cdots\right)+\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|{\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\left(1+{n+1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right),

(222)

\displaystyle\left.{\partial^{3}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\right|_{*}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{ij}\Theta_{k}+\delta_{jk}\Theta_{i}+\delta_{ki}\Theta_{j}\right)\left(\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right)+\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\Theta_{k}\left(-3\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}+\cdots\right),

(223)

\displaystyle\left.{\partial^{4}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\right|_{*}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{jk}\delta_{i\ell}+\delta_{ki}\delta_{j\ell}\right)\left({\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\delta_{ij}\Theta_{k}\Theta_{\ell}+\delta_{jk}\Theta_{i}\Theta_{\ell}+\delta_{ki}\Theta_{j}\Theta_{\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\Theta_{j}\Theta_{k}+\delta_{j\ell}\Theta_{i}\Theta_{k}+\delta_{k\ell}\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\right)\left({3\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\Theta_{k}\Theta_{\ell}\left({15\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{6}}+\cdots\right).

(224)

Let
M
be the Hessian at the saddle point:

\displaystyle M_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\left.{\partial^{2}F\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}}\right|_{*}.

(225)

We write the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of
M
as

\displaystyle MV^{(n)}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{(n)}V^{(n)},

(226)

in which
n
is not summed.
Then we may diagonalize
M
as

\displaystyle R^{\text{t}}MR

\displaystyle=\widetilde{M}

(227)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda^{(1)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&-\lambda^{(d)}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}V^{(1)}&\cdots&V^{(d)}\end{bmatrix}.

(228)

Here,
R
is a complex orthogonal matrix
R^{\text{t}}R=1
.

Now we may change the variables as

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\Delta u_{i}M_{ij}\Delta u_{j}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{M}_{ij}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}=-{\lambda^{(n)}\over 2}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{n}^{2},

(229)

with

\displaystyle\Delta u_{i}

\displaystyle=R_{ij}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}.

(230)

Then we obtain

\displaystyle{1\over 3!}{\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\Delta u_{i}\Delta u_{j}\Delta u_{k}

\displaystyle={1\over 3!}\widetilde{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k},

(231)

\displaystyle{1\over 4!}{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\Delta u_{i}\Delta u_{j}\Delta u_{k}\Delta u_{\ell}

\displaystyle={1\over 4!}\widetilde{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\ell},

(232)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{ij}\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}+\delta_{jk}\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}+\delta_{ki}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}\right)\left(\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right)+\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}\left(-3\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}+\cdots\right),

(233)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{ij}\delta_{k\ell}+\delta_{jk}\delta_{i\ell}+\delta_{ki}\delta_{j\ell}\right)\left({\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\delta_{ij}\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}\widetilde{\Theta}_{\ell}+\delta_{jk}\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}\widetilde{\Theta}_{\ell}+\delta_{ki}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}\widetilde{\Theta}_{\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}+\delta_{j\ell}\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}+\delta_{k\ell}\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}\right)\left({3\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}\widetilde{\Theta}_{\ell}\left({15\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{6}}+\cdots\right),

(234)

Because our Hessian takes the form

\displaystyle M_{ij}

\displaystyle=A\delta_{ij}+B\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j},

(235)

with

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\left(1+{n+1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}+\cdots\right),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\left(1+{n+1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right),

(236)

we obtain

\displaystyle M_{kl}R_{ki}R_{lj}

\displaystyle=A\delta_{ij}+B\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j},

(237)

where
\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}:=\Theta_{j}R_{ji}
, that is,
\Theta_{i}=R_{ij}\widetilde{\Theta}_{j}
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&(k\neq d),\\
\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}}&(k=d).\end{cases}

(238)

Then we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}

\displaystyle=3\left(\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}^{2}+\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{2}\right)\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}}\left(\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{3}\left(\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\right)^{3\over 2}\left(-3\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}+\cdots\right),

(239)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=3\left(\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}^{2}+\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{2}\right)^{2}\left({\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad-6\left(\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}^{2}+\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{2}\right)\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\left({3\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{4}\left(\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\right)^{2}\left({15\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{6}}+\cdots\right),

(240)

where we have decomposed as
\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}=\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}+\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\parallel}
with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\widetilde{\Delta u}_{d-1}\\
0\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\parallel}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}\end{bmatrix},

(241)

and used

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}^{2}+\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{2},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}}.

(242)

For later convenience, we define

\displaystyle I\!\left(a,b\right)

\displaystyle:=\int\text{d}^{d}\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\,e^{-{1\over 2}\lambda_{l}\widetilde{\Delta u_{l}}^{2}}\left(\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}_{\perp}^{2}\right)^{a}\left(\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\parallel}^{2}\right)^{b}.

(243)

The result is

\displaystyle I\!\left(2,0\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}\,{d^{2}-1\over\lambda_{\perp}^{2}},

\displaystyle I\!\left(2,1\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}\,{d^{2}-1\over\lambda_{\perp}^{2}}{1\over\lambda_{\parallel}},

(244)

\displaystyle I\!\left(1,1\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}\,{d-1\over\lambda_{\perp}\lambda_{\parallel}},

\displaystyle I\!\left(1,2\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}\,{d-1\over\lambda_{\perp}}{3\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}},

(245)

\displaystyle I\!\left(0,2\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}\,{3\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}},

\displaystyle I\!\left(0,3\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}\,{15\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{3}},

(246)

where we have used, say,

\displaystyle I\!\left(2,0\right)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\text{d}x_{i}\right)\text{d}y\,e^{-{1\over 2}\left(\lambda_{\perp}\sum_{j}x_{j}^{2}+\lambda_{\parallel}y^{2}\right)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}x_{k}^{4}+\sum_{k\neq\ell}x_{k}^{2}x_{\ell}^{2}\right),

(247)

as well as the one-dimensional integrals:
\int\text{d}x\,e^{-{\lambda\over 2}x^{2}}x^{2}={\sqrt{2\pi}\over\lambda^{3/2}}
,
\int\text{d}x\,e^{-{\lambda\over 2}x^{2}}x^{4}={3\sqrt{2\pi}\over\lambda^{5/2}}
, and
\int\text{d}x\,e^{-{\lambda\over 2}x^{2}}x^{6}={15\sqrt{2\pi}\over\lambda^{7/2}}
.

In the expansion (
221
), the cubic and quintic integrals vanish, while the quartic and hexic ones become

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{d}\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\,e^{-{1\over 2}\lambda_{l}\widetilde{\Delta u_{l}}^{2}}\widetilde{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\quad={3\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\left(I\!\left(2,0\right)+2I\!\left(1,1\right)+I\!\left(0,2\right)-{6\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\bigl(I\!\left(1,1\right)+I\!\left(0,2\right)\bigr)+{5\left(\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\right)^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}I\!\left(0,2\right)\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad=\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}{3\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\Bigg[\left({d^{2}-1\over\lambda_{\perp}^{2}}+{2\left(d-1\right)\over\lambda_{\perp}\lambda_{\parallel}}+{3\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}}\right)-{6\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\left({d-1\over\lambda_{\perp}\lambda_{\parallel}}+{3\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}}\right)+{\left(\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\right)^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}{15\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}}+\cdots\Bigg],

(248)

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{d}\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\,e^{-{1\over 2}\lambda_{l}\widetilde{\Delta u_{l}}^{2}}\left(\widetilde{\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=9\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\lambda^{2}{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{4}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}\Bigg(I\!\left(2,1\right)+2{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}I\!\left(1,2\right)+{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{4}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}I\!\left(0,3\right)\Bigg)+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad=9\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\lambda^{2}\left(2\pi\over\lambda_{\perp}\right)^{d-1\over 2}\sqrt{2\pi\over\lambda_{\parallel}}{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{4}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}\Bigg({d^{2}-1\over\lambda_{\perp}^{2}\lambda_{\parallel}}+2{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}{3\left(d-1\right)\over\lambda_{\perp}\lambda_{\parallel}^{2}}+{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{4}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{4}}{15\over\lambda_{\parallel}^{3}}\Bigg)+\cdots.

(249)

As said above,
\widetilde{\partial^{6}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{l}\partial u^{m}\partial u^{n}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{l}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{m}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{n}
is
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\lambda^{-2}\right)
and does not contribute to the order of our interest.

Concretely, the eigenvalues of
M
are
\left(d-1\right)
-fold degenerate
-\lambda_{\perp}
and a single
-\lambda_{\parallel}
, where

\displaystyle\lambda_{\perp}

\displaystyle:=\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\left[1+{n+1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle\text{for $\lambda^{(n)}$ with $n=1,\dots,d-1$},

(250)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\parallel}

\displaystyle:=\lambda{\left\|\Theta\right\|^{3}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\left[1+{n+1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left(1+{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle\text{for $\lambda^{(n)}$ with $n=d$}.

(251)

Keeping the lowest order terms for large
\lambda
in Eq. (
248
), we get

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{d}\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\,e^{-{1\over 2}\lambda_{l}\widetilde{\Delta u_{l}}^{2}}\widetilde{\partial^{4}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}\partial u^{\ell}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\right)^{d\over 2}{3\Theta^{0}\over\lambda}\left(d+2\right)\left({d+4\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}-{4\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}}\right)+\cdots,

(252)

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{d}\widetilde{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\,e^{-{1\over 2}\lambda_{l}\widetilde{\Delta u_{l}}^{2}}\left(\widetilde{\partial^{3}F_{*}\over\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}\partial u^{k}}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{i}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{j}\widetilde{\Delta u}_{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\right)^{d\over 2}9(d+2)(d+4){\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}}{1\over\lambda\Theta^{0}}+\cdots.

(253)

To summarize,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{(n)}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\left(2\pi\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|\right)^{d\over 2}e^{-\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left(\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\Theta^{0}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[1+{1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left({d\left(d-2\right)\over 8}+{n\over 2}\left(-d+\left(n+1\right){\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)\right)+\cdots\right],

(254)

and hence

\displaystyle{1\over\mathcal{I}_{(0)}}{\partial\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\lambda\partial\Theta^{i}}

\displaystyle={\Theta_{i}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left(\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\Theta^{0}\right)^{n}\left[1+{1\over 2\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left(d-n\left(d+2\right)+n\left(n+1\right){\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left(1\over\lambda^{2}\right)\right],

(255)

\displaystyle{1\over\mathcal{I}_{(0)}}{\partial^{2}\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\lambda^{2}\partial\Theta^{i}\partial\Theta^{j}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\Theta^{0}\right)^{n}\Bigg[{\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}}+{1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left\{\delta_{ij}+{\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}}\left[d+1-{n\over 2}\left(d+4-\left(n+1\right){\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(1\over\lambda^{2}\right)\Bigg],

(256)

where we used

\displaystyle{\partial\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\partial\Theta^{i}}

\displaystyle={\partial\sqrt{\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}}\over\partial\Theta^{i}}=-{\Theta^{i}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|}.

(257)

B.3.2
Non-relativistic expansion without using saddle-point method

Instead of using the saddle-point method, we may obtain the integral (
209
) by the non-relativistic expansion, which provides a non-trivial consistency check.

In the non-relativistic limit
\Re\Xi^{0}\gg\left|\Re\boldsymbol{\Xi}\right|\geq 0
, the dominant contribution to the integral would be from the non-relativistic velocity
\left|\boldsymbol{u}\right|\ll 1
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\!\left(\Xi\right)

\displaystyle=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2u^{0}}e^{u\cdot\Xi}{1\over\left(u^{0}\right)^{n}}

\displaystyle=\int{\text{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{u}\over 2u^{0}}e^{u\cdot\Xi}\left(1-{n\over 2}\boldsymbol{u}^{2}+{n\left(n+2\right)\over 8}\left(\boldsymbol{u}^{2}\right)^{2}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)-{n\over 2}{\partial^{2}\over\partial\Xi^{i}\partial\Xi^{i}}\mathcal{I}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)+{n\left(n+2\right)\over 8}{\partial^{4}\over\partial\Xi^{i}\partial\Xi^{i}\partial\Xi^{j}\partial\Xi^{j}}\mathcal{I}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1\over 2}}\left[1-{n\over 2}\left({d\,K_{d+1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)}+{\boldsymbol{\Xi}^{2}K_{d+3\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{2}K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)}\right)+\cdots\right].

(258)

where we used

\displaystyle{\partial\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi^{i}}

\displaystyle=-{\Xi_{i}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|},

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\left\|\Xi\right\|\over\partial\Xi^{i}\partial\Xi^{j}}

\displaystyle=-{\delta_{ij}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|}-{\Xi_{i}\Xi_{j}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{3}}.

(259)

In the above,
\Xi^{0}
are written in terms of
\left\|\Xi\right\|
and
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
.

Now we show results in the non-relativistic expansion with large
\left\|\Xi\right\|
, leaving the overall function that cancels out in
{1\over\mathcal{I}_{(0)}}{\partial\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\partial\Xi^{i}}
, etc.:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{(n)}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1\over 2}}\left(1-{dn\over 2\left\|\Xi\right\|}+\cdots\right),

(260)

\displaystyle{\partial\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\partial\Xi^{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1\over 2}}{\Xi_{i}\over\left\|\Xi\right\|}\left(1+{d-n\left(d+2\right)\over 2\left\|\Xi\right\|}+\cdots\right),

(261)

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\partial\Xi^{i}\partial\Xi^{j}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{d-1\over 2}{K_{d-1\over 2}\!\left(\left\|\Xi\right\|\right)\over\left\|\Xi\right\|^{d-1\over 2}}{1\over\left\|\Xi\right\|}\left[\delta_{ij}+{1\over\left\|\Xi\right\|}\left({d-n\left(d+2\right)\over 2}\delta_{ij}+\Xi_{i}\Xi_{j}\right)+\cdots\right].

(262)

As a cross check, we put
\Xi=\lambda\Theta
and take the saddle-point limit for large
\lambda
:

\displaystyle{1\over\mathcal{I}_{(0)}}{\partial\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\lambda\partial\Theta^{i}}

\displaystyle={\Theta_{i}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left[1+{d-n\left(d+2\right)\over 2\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(1\over\lambda^{2}\right)\right],

(263)

\displaystyle{1\over\mathcal{I}_{(0)}}{\partial^{2}\mathcal{I}_{(n)}\over\lambda^{2}\partial\Theta^{i}\partial\Theta^{j}}

\displaystyle={\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}}+{1\over\lambda\left\|\Theta\right\|}\left(\delta_{ij}+{\Theta_{i}\Theta_{j}\over\left\|\Theta\right\|^{2}}\left(d+1-{n\over 2}\left(d+4\right)\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\!\left(1\over\lambda^{2}\right).

(264)

We can confirm that these results coincide with the saddle-point ones (
255
) and (
256
) if we take the NR limit
{\left\|\Theta\right\|\over\Theta^{0}}\to 1
and
{\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{2}\over\left(\Theta^{0}\right)^{2}}\to 0
there.

Abstract

1
Introduction

Consider an arbitrary irrational number
\alpha
. Denote by
a_{i},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
the partial quotients of its continued fraction expansion

\alpha=[a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]=a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\ldots}},\penalty\ a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z},\penalty\ a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\penalty\ i=1,2,\ldots.

(1)

Let us denote by
\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}
the
n
-th convergent fraction to
\alpha
, i.e.
\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}:=[a_{0};a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
. Define
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}:=\{q_{1},q_{2},\ldots\}
to be the sequence of denominators of the convergents to
\alpha
.
The irrationality measure function of
\alpha
is defined as

\psi_{\alpha}(t):=\min\limits_{1\leqslant q\leqslant t,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\|q\alpha\|,\,\,\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,\,\,\,\|x\|=\min\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-n|.

By Lagrange’s theorem on best approximations (see
[
3
]
),
\psi_{\alpha}(t)
is a piecewise constant function, moreover

\psi_{\alpha}(t)=|q_{n}\alpha-p_{n}|=\|q_{n}\alpha\|\,\,\,\,\,\text{
for}\,\,\,\,\,q_{n}\leqslant t<q_{n+1}.

Let

\alpha_{n}:=[a_{n};a_{n+1},\ldots],\quad\alpha_{n}^{*}:=[0;a_{n},a_{n-1},\ldots,a_{1}].

We say that two irrational numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
are
equivalent
if
\alpha_{n}=\beta_{m}
for some
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. It is well known fact, usually called Perron’s formula, that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
one has

\|q_{n}\alpha\|=\frac{1}{q_{n}(\alpha_{n+1}+\alpha_{n}^{*})}.

See
[
1
]
, Appendix 1 for details. The Lagrange constant of an irrational number
\alpha
is defined as

\lambda(\alpha):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}(t\cdot\psi_{\alpha}(t))^{-1}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}(\alpha_{t+1}+\alpha_{t}^{*}).

(2)

The set of values
\lambda(\alpha)
as
\alpha
runs through the set of all irrational numbers forms the
Lagrange spectrum
\mathbb{L}

\mathbb{L}:=\{\lambda\penalty\ |\penalty\ \exists\alpha\in\mathbb{R\setminus Q}\colon\lambda=\lambda(\alpha)\}.

(3)

One can find a nice and detailed survey of the results about the Lagrange spectrum up to 1990s in a book
[
1
]
.

Remark.
Some authors, for example
[
6
]
, define the Lagrange constant
\lambda(\alpha)
equal to
\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}t\cdot\psi_{\alpha}(t)
. Therefore, the Lagrange spectrum, defined by these authors, contains the reciprocals of the elements of
\mathbb{L}
from our definition.

In
[
6
]
Moshchevitin considered an irrationality measure function

\psi^{[2]}_{\alpha}(t):=\min\limits_{1\leqslant q\leqslant t,q\in\mathbb{Z},q\notin\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\|q\alpha\|.

(4)

related to so-called second best approximations. Obviously,
\psi^{[2]}_{\alpha}(t)
is also a non-increasing peacewise constant function. It is connected with the properties of best approximations by fractions, whose denominators are not the denominators of the convergents to
\alpha
. Also, in
[
6
]
an analogue of Lagrange constant was defined

\lambda^{[2]}(\alpha):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}(t\cdot\psi^{[2]}_{\alpha}(t))^{-1}

(5)

and the corresponding spectrum

\mathbb{L}_{2}:=\{\lambda\penalty\ |\penalty\ \exists\alpha\in\mathbb{R\setminus Q}\colon\lambda=\lambda^{[2]}(\alpha)\}

(6)

was considered. The structure of
\mathbb{L}_{2}
in some sense is similar to the structure of
\mathbb{L}
. The smallest elements of
\mathbb{L}_{2}
form a countable discrete set and
\mathbb{L}_{2}
contains a Hall’s ray i.e.
\exists\mu:(\mu,+\infty)\in\mathbb{L}_{2}
.

However, in the present paper we show that, unlike the classical Lagrange spectrum
\mathbb{L}
, whose discrete part is connected with the properties of so-called Markoff triples, the discrete part of
\mathbb{L}_{2}
has a short and simple description. We prove that if a real number
x
belongs to the discrete part of
\mathbb{L}_{2}
, then
x
is equivalent to one of the following numbers:
[1;\overline{1}]
,
[1;\overline{1,1,3}]
or
[1;\overline{1,1,1,1,3,(1,1,3)_{2k-1}}]
, where
k\geqslant 1
. We write
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})_{m}
if the pattern
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is repeated
m
times and
\overline{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}
if this pattern is repeated infinitely many times.

It turns out that the discrete part of
\mathbb{L}_{2}
is similar to the discrete part of so-called Dirichlet spectrum
\mathbb{D}
, which is defined as follows. Denote

d_{n}(\alpha):=[a_{n};a_{n-1},\ldots,a_{1}][a_{n+1};a_{n+2},\ldots]

and

d(\alpha):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}d_{n}(\alpha).

Then
\mathbb{D}
is defined as a set of values taken by
d(\alpha)
as
\alpha
runs through the set of all irrational numbers. Spectrum
\mathbb{D}
was studied by several authors (see for example
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
). It was shown by Lesca
[
4
]
that if
d(\alpha)<2+\sqrt{5}
, which is the accumulation point of the discrete part of
\mathbb{D}
, then
\alpha
is equivalent either to
[1;\overline{1}]
or to
[1;\overline{(1)_{2k-1},2}]
for some
k\geqslant 1
. Our proof is similar to his argument.

), we deduce that in order to obtain (

) it suffices to show that

\frac{\langle 1,1,(3,1,1)_{2k+1}\rangle}{\langle 1,1,1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+2}\rangle}>\sqrt{\frac{8}{3}}.

As
k\geqslant m+1
, it is enough to verify the inequality

\frac{\langle 1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+3}\rangle}{\langle 1,1,1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+2}\rangle}>\sqrt{\frac{8}{3}}

(24)

for all
m\geqslant 0
. As

\langle 1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+3}\rangle\geqslant\langle 1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+2}\rangle\langle 3,1,1\rangle=7\langle 1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+2}\rangle

and

\langle 1,1,1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+2}\rangle\leqslant 3\langle 1,1,(3,1,1)_{2m+2}\rangle,

the inequality (
24
) holds and the lemma is proven.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Lemmas
4
and
5
, we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose that
\lambda_{2}<\lambda^{[2]}(\alpha)<\lambda_{\infty}
for some irrational
\alpha=[a_{0};a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\ldots]
. Then
\alpha\sim\xi_{n}
for some
n\geqslant 3
.

5
Lower estimates

In this section we show that
\lambda^{[2]}(\xi_{i})=\lambda_{i}
and thus complete the proof of Theorem
1
. First, we prove two technical lemmas.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\alpha=[a_{0};a_{1},\ldots]
be an arbitrary irrational number. If
a_{n}
is the middle ’’3’’ in the pattern
311\boldsymbol{3}113
, then

\max(\varkappa^{1}_{n}(\alpha),\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha),\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha))<1.04.

Proof.

As the function
\varkappa^{1}_{n}(\alpha_{n},\alpha^{*}_{n-1})
is decreasing on both arguments and

\alpha_{n}\geqslant[3;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],\quad\ \alpha^{*}_{n-1}\geqslant[0;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],

we see that

\varkappa^{1}_{n}(\alpha)\leqslant\frac{[3;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]+[0;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]}{(1+[0;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}])([3;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]-1)}\approx 1.031440.

(25)

The quantities
\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha)
and
\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)
are estimated in a similar way. As

\alpha_{n+1}\leqslant[1;1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],\quad\alpha^{*}_{n}\leqslant[0;3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],

we get the following estimate

\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha)\leqslant\frac{[1;1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]+[0;3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]}{(1-[1;1,\overline{3,1,1,1}])(1+[0;3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}])}\approx 1.031440.

(26)

Finally, as

\alpha_{n}\leqslant[3;1,1,3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],\quad\ \alpha^{*}_{n-1}\leqslant[0;1,1,3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]

we see that

\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)\leqslant\frac{[3;1,1,3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]+[0;1,1,3,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}]}{4}\approx 1.030785.

(27)

Combining the estimates (
25
), (
26
), and (
27
) yields the statement of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
\alpha=[a_{0};a_{1},\ldots]
be an arbitrary irrational number. If
a_{n}
is the middle ’’3’’ in the pattern
31111\boldsymbol{3}113
or
31111\boldsymbol{3}113
, then

\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)>\max(\varkappa^{1}_{n}(\alpha),\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha)).

Proof.

We will give a proof for the pattern
31111\boldsymbol{3}113
only, as the proof for the second pattern is exactly same. One can easily see that
\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)>\varkappa^{1}_{n}(\alpha)
if and only if the inequality

(1+\alpha^{*}_{n-1})(\alpha_{n}-1)>4

(28)

holds. As

\alpha_{n}\geqslant[3;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],\quad\alpha^{*}_{n-1}\geqslant[0;1,1,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],

we have the following estimate

(1+\alpha^{*}_{n-1})(\alpha_{n}-1)\geqslant([1;1,1,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}])([2;1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}])\approx 4.120747

and the inequality (
28
) holds.

Using the the obvious properties
\alpha_{n}=a_{n}+1/\alpha_{n+1}
and
\alpha^{*}_{n}=1/(a_{n}+\alpha^{*}_{n-1})
and the fact that
a_{n}=3
, one can transform
\varkappa^{2}_{n}
and
\varkappa^{4}_{n}
as follows:

\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha)=\frac{3\alpha_{n+1}+\alpha_{n+1}\alpha^{*}_{n-1}+1}{(2+\alpha^{*}_{n-1})(\alpha_{n+1}+1)},\quad\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)=\frac{3\alpha_{n+1}+\alpha_{n+1}\alpha^{*}_{n-1}+1}{4\alpha_{n+1}}.

Hence
\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)>\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha)
if and only if

\alpha_{n+1}\alpha^{*}_{n-1}+\alpha^{*}_{n-1}-2\alpha_{n+1}+2>0.

(29)

We know that

\alpha_{n+1}\geqslant[1;1,\overline{3,1,1,1}],\quad\alpha^{*}_{n-1}\leqslant[0;1,1,1,1,\overline{3,1,1,1}].

Substituting these estimates to (
29
), we can see that the inequality is satisfied and therefore
\varkappa^{4}_{n}(\alpha)>\varkappa^{2}_{n}(\alpha)
. Lemma is proven.
∎

Corollary 3
.

\lambda^{[2]}(\xi_{i})=\lambda_{i}
.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemmas
6
and
7
.
∎

Corollary 4
.

\lambda_{\infty}\in\mathbb{L}_{2}
.

Proof.

Consider

\alpha=[0;(3,1,1)_{2n_{1}+1},3,1,1,1,1,(3,1,1)_{2n_{2}+1},3,1,1,1,1,(3,1,1)_{2n_{3}+1},\ldots],

(30)

where
n_{i}
is an arbitrary sequence of natural numbers that tends to infinity. By Lemmas
6
and
7
,
\lambda^{[2]}(\alpha)=\lambda_{\infty}
.
∎

Thus we completed the proof of Theorem
1
. Also, from (
30
) one can see that the set of real numbers satisfying
\lambda^{[2]}(\alpha)=\lambda_{\infty}
has continuum many elements. Note that the set of numbers satisfying
\lambda^{[2]}(\alpha)=\lambda_{n}
for each
n
is countable as all such numbers are equivalent to
\xi_{n}
.

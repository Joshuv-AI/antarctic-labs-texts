2-Arc-transitive Cayley graphs on alternating groups
Jiangmin Pan, Binzhou Xia, Fugang Yin

Abstract
. An interesting fact is that most of the known connected 2 2 -arc-transitive nonnormal Cayley graphs of small valency on finite simple groups are ( A n + 1 , 2 ) (\mathrm{A}_{n+1},2) -arc-transitive Cayley graphs on A n \mathrm{A}_{n} . This motivates the study of 2 2 -arc-transitive Cayley graphs on A n \mathrm{A}_{n} for arbitrary valency. In this paper, we characterize the automorphism groups of such graphs. In particular, we show that for a non-complete ( G , 2 ) (G,2) -arc-transitive Cayley graph on A n \mathrm{A}_{n} with G G almost simple, the socle of G G is either A n + 1 \mathrm{A}_{n+1} or A n + 2 \mathrm{A}_{n+2} . We also construct the first infinite family of ( A n + 2 , 2 ) (\mathrm{A}_{n+2},2) -arc-transitive Cayley graphs on A n \mathrm{A}_{n} . Key words: 2-arc-transitive; Cayley graph; alternating group; automorphism group MSC2020: 05E18, 05C25

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, simple and undirected.
For a positive integer
s
, an
s
-arc
of a graph is a
(s+1)
-tuple of vertices
(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{s})
where
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq s-1
and
v_{i-1}\neq v_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq s-1
.
Let
\Gamma
be a graph and let
G
be a subgroup of the full automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
.
The graph
\Gamma
is said to be
(G,s)
-arc-transitive
if

is transitive on the set of
s
-arcs.
For short, we say that
\Gamma
is
s
-arc-transitive
if it is
(\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma),s)
-arc-transitive, and
arc-transitive
if it is
(\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma),1)
-arc-transitive.
Note that for regular graphs
s
-arc-transitivity with
s\geq 2
implies
(s-1)
-arc-transitivity.
Tutte
[
25
]
in 1947 proved that there exist no finite
s
-arc-transitive cubic graphs for
s\geq 6
.
Since this remarkable result,
s
-arc-transitive graphs have attracted considerable attention in the literature.

Let
H
be a group and let

be an inverse-closed nonempty subset of
H\setminus\{1\}
. The
Cayley graph

on
H
with

is defined to be the graph with vertex set

such that

are adjacent if and only if

.

Some special classes of
2
-arc-transitive Cayley graphs have been classified or characterized in the literature. See Alspach, Conder, Marušič and Xu
[
1
]
for
2
-arc-transitive Cayley graphs on cyclic groups, Ivanov and Praeger
[
14
]
and Li and Pan
[

]
for
2
-arc-transitive Cayley graphs on abelian groups, Marušič and Du
[
9
]
for
2
-arc-transitive Cayley graphs on dihedral groups, and the recent work of Li and Xia
[
19
]
on
2
-arc-transitive Cayley graphs on solvable groups.

For a Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(H,S)
, denote by
R_{H}(H)
the subgroup of
\mathrm{Sym}(H)
induced by the right multiplication of
H
on itself. Clearly,

is a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cay}(H,S))
. The Cayley graph
\mathrm{Cay}(H,S)
is said to be

if

is a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cay}(H,S))
, and is said to be

otherwise. Nonnormal Cayley graphs are believed to be rare, and it is conjectured by Xu
[
28
]
that almost all connected Cayley graphs are normal. Moreover, it is well known that if
\mathrm{Cay}(H,S)
is a normal Cayley graph on

, then
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cay}(H,S))=R_{H}(H){:}\mathrm{Aut}(H,S)
where

\mathrm{Aut}(H,S):=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)\mid S^{\sigma}=S\},

see Godsil
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
.
Hence the symmetries of normal Cayley graphs can be well understood, and consequently more attention in the literature are on nonnormal ones
while investigating Cayley graphs.

The Jordan-Hölder theorem shows that all finite groups can be viewed as group extensions of simple groups (namely their compositor factors).
This fundamental theorem increases the interest of the study of Cayley graphs on simple groups.
Fang, Praeger and Wang
[
10
]
gave a general description of the structures of automorphism groups
of nonnormal Cayley graphs on nonabelian simple groups,
and
s
-arc-transitive nonnormal Cayley graphs on nonabelian simple groups of certain valencies have been explicitly characterized.
Let
\Gamma
be a connected nonnormal
s
-arc-transitive Cayley graph on a

Based on a so called ‘dual action’ given in Li

This motivates a natural problem as follows.

Problem A.

Study
s
-arc-transitive nonnormal Cayley graphs on alternating groups for arbitrary valency.

In this paper, we give a characterization of the graphs in Problem A with
s=2
. For a group
G
, the socle
\mathrm{Soc}(G)
of
G
is the product of all minimal normal subgroups of
G
. For a
G
-vertex transitive graph
\Gamma
and a normal subgroup

of
G
, the quotient graph
\Gamma_{M}
associated with

is a graph with the set of orbits of
M
as vertex set, and two orbits
O_{1},O_{2}
are adjacent if there is an edge in
\Gamma
with two endpoints in

respectively. The graph

is called a

of
\Gamma_{M}
if the valency
\mathsf{Val}(\Gamma)=\mathsf{Val}(\Gamma_{M})

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
(G,2)
-arc-transitive Cayley graph on an alternating group
H\cong\mathrm{A}_{n}
with
n\geq 5
and
H\leq G
, and let
\alpha
be a vertex of
\Gamma
. Suppose that
H
is not normal in

. Then one of the following holds:

(a)

\mathrm{Soc}(G)=\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
,
\mathrm{Soc}(G)_{\alpha}
is a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
.

(b)

G
has a normal subgroup
N\cong\mathrm{A}_{n+2}
such that
H<N
,
\Gamma
is
(N,2)
-arc-transitive,
N_{\alpha}
is a sharply
2
-transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{A}_{n+2}
, and
N_{\alpha}
acts faithfully on
\Gamma(\alpha)
; in particular,
\mathsf{Val}(\Gamma)=n+2
is a prime power.

(c)

\Gamma=\mathsf{K}_{m}
with
m=n!/2
, and either
(\mathrm{Soc}(G),\mathrm{Soc}(G)_{\alpha})=(\mathrm{A}_{m},\mathrm{A}_{m-1})
or
(G,G_{\alpha},n)=(\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(59),\mathrm{AGL}_{1}(11),5)
.

(d)

G
has a nontrivial maximal intransitive normal subgroup
M
such that
G/M
is almost simple,
\Gamma_{M}
is
(G/M,2)
-arc-transitive, and
\Gamma
is a normal cover of
\Gamma_{M}
; moreover, one of the following is true, where

is a vertex of
\Gamma_{M}
:

(d.1)

\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)=\mathrm{A}_{n}
such that

is a power of
2
, and
\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)_{v}
contains a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)
.

(d.2)

\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)=\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
, and
\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)_{v}
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
;

(d.3)

\Gamma_{M}=\mathsf{K}_{m}
,
\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)=\mathrm{A}_{m}
such that
m
is the index of a subgroup of
\mathrm{A}_{n}
, and
\mathrm{Soc}(G/M)_{v}=\mathrm{A}_{m-1}
.

A group

is said to be

if
\mathrm{Soc}(G)
is a nonabelian simple group. For Problem A, if we impose that the
2
-arc-transitive automorphism group of the graph is almost simple, then we have the following corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
(G,2)
-arc-transitive Cayley graph on an alternating group
H\cong\mathrm{A}_{n}
with
n\geq 5
and
H\leq G
, and let
\alpha
be a vertex of
\Gamma
. Suppose that
G
is almost simple with socle

. Then one of the following holds:

(a)

T=\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
and

is a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{A}_{n+1}
;

(b)

T=\mathrm{A}_{n+2}
such that
\Gamma
is
(T,2)
-arc-transitive,

is a sharply
2
-transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{A}_{n+2}
and acts faithfully on
\Gamma(\alpha)
, and
\mathsf{Val}(\Gamma)=n+2

(c)

\Gamma=\mathsf{K}_{n!/2}
.

As introduced above, there are a number of
(\mathrm{A}_{n+1},2)
-arc-transitive Cayley graphs on
\mathrm{A}_{n}

-arc-transitive Cayley graph on

In this paper, we construct the first infinite family of

-arc-transitive Cayley graphs on

with vertex stabilizer a subgroup of

Construction 1.3

Let

be the filed automorphism of

.

Let

The following result shows that the

Theorem 2.1

Let
\Gamma
be a connected

-arc-transitive graph with

has a normal subgroup

Moreover,

, and

.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph and let
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
. For adjacent vertices

and
\beta
of
\Gamma
,
let
G_{\alpha}^{[1]}
be the kernel of

acting on
\Gamma(\alpha)
,
and let
G_{\alpha\beta}^{[1]}=G_{\alpha}^{[1]}\cap G_{\beta}^{[1]}
.
The following theorem is a well-known result of Weiss
[
26
]
on

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
(G,2)
-transitive graph with

, and let
\alpha
and
\beta
be adjacent vertices of

Then one of the following holds:

(a)

G_{\alpha\beta}^{[1]}=1

G_{\alpha}^{[1]}\cong(G_{\alpha}^{[1]})^{\Gamma(\beta)}\trianglelefteq G_{\alpha\beta}^{\Gamma(\beta)}\cong G_{\alpha\beta}^{\Gamma(\alpha)}

(b)

is a nontrivial

\mathsf{Val}(\Gamma)=(p^{df}-1)/(p^{f}-1)
.

The following lemma is also well known. We include a proof here as it is not lengthy.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected graph, let
G
be a vertex-transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
, and let
\alpha\in V(\Gamma)
.
Then every composition factor of
G_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to a section of
G_{\alpha}^{\Gamma(\alpha)}
.

Proof.

Let
d
be the diameter of
\Gamma
.
For
i\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}
, let
V_{i}
be the set of vertices of distance
i
from
\alpha
in
\Gamma
,
let
U_{i}=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{i}
, and
let
K_{i}
be the kernel of
G_{\alpha}
acting on
U_{i}
.
By the connectivity of
\Gamma
we have
U_{d}=V(\Gamma)
, and thus

1=K_{d}\trianglelefteq K_{d-1}\trianglelefteq\dots\trianglelefteq K_{1}\trianglelefteq K_{0}=G_{\alpha}.

Moreover, for
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,

K_{i-1}/K_{i}\cong K_{i-1}^{V_{i}}.

Let
\Delta
be an orbit of
K_{i-1}
on
V_{i}
, and let
\gamma\in\Delta
.
Then there exists
\beta\in U_{i-1}
such that
\gamma\in\Gamma(\beta)
.
It follows that
\gamma^{k}\in\Gamma(\beta^{k})=\Gamma(\beta)
for each
k\in K_{i-1}
.
Hence
\Delta=\gamma^{K_{i-1}}\subseteq\Gamma(\beta)
, and so
K_{i-1}^{\Delta}
is a homomorphic image of
K_{i-1}^{\Gamma(\beta)}
.
Note that
K_{i-1}^{\Gamma(\beta)}\leq G_{\beta}^{\Gamma(\beta)}\cong G_{\alpha}^{\Gamma(\alpha)}
since
G
is vertex-transitive.
We then conclude that
K_{i-1}^{\Delta}
is (isomorphic to) a section of
G_{\alpha}^{\Gamma(\alpha)}
.
Since
\Delta
is an arbitrary orbit of
K_{i-1}
on
V_{i}
,
this implies that
K_{i-1}/K_{i}\cong K_{i-1}^{V_{i}}
is a section of
G_{\alpha}^{\Gamma(\alpha)}
.
Thus every composition factor of
G_{\alpha}
is a section of
G_{\alpha}^{\Gamma(\alpha)}
.
∎

-transitive groups are known (see for instance

.

2.2.
Coset graphs and orbital graphs

Coset graph and orbital graph are two useful tools to construct and understand general arc-transitive graphs.

Let
G
be a group,
K
a core-free subgroup of
G
(namely,
K
contains no nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
) and
g\in G\setminus K
. Define the
coset graph

\mathrm{Cos}(G,K,g)
to be a graph with vertex set
[G:K]
(the set of right cosets of
K
in
G
) such that
Kx
is adjacent to
Ky
with
x,y\in G
if and only if
yx^{-1}\in KgK
. The following assertion is due to Sabidussi
[
24
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected
G
-arc-transitive graph of valency
d
, and let
\alpha\in V(\Gamma)
.
Then
\Gamma\cong\mathrm{Cos}(G,K,g)
for
K=G_{\alpha}
and some
2
-element
g
satisfying:

g\in\mathbf{N}_{G}(K\cap K^{g}),\ \ g^{2}\in K,\ \ \langle K,g\rangle=G,\ \ |K|/|K\cap K^{g}|=d.

(1)

In particular, if
\beta\in\Gamma(\alpha)
then
\langle G_{\alpha},\mathbf{N}_{G}(G_{\alpha\beta})\rangle=G
.

Conversely, if
H
is a core-free subgroup of a group
X
and
x
is an element of
X

such that the triple
(X,H,x)

(
as
(G,K,g)
there
)
satisfies (
1
),
then
\mathrm{Cos}(X,H,x)
is a connected
X
-arc-transitive graph of valency
d
.

Let
G
be a transitive permutation group on a set
\Omega
. Then
G
naturally acts on
\Omega\times\Omega
by

(\alpha,\beta)^{g}=(\alpha^{g},\beta^{g})\ \ \text{for}\ g\in G\ \text{and}\ \alpha,\beta\in\Omega.

The orbits of
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
are called orbitals of
G
.
By the transitivity of
G
, each orbital of
G
corresponds to a orbit of
G_{\alpha}
on
\Omega
, which is called a
suborbit
of
G
.
Let
\Delta
be a suborbit of
G
, and let
\beta\in\Delta
.
We say that
\Delta
is
nontrivial
if
\Delta\neq\{\alpha\}
, and
\Delta
is
self-paired
if
(\alpha,\beta)^{G}=(\beta,\alpha)^{G}
. If
\Delta
is nontrivial, then the associated
orbital graph

\Gamma(\Delta)
of
\Delta
is the graph with vertex set
\Omega
and edge set
\{\alpha,\beta\}^{G}:=\{\{\alpha^{g},\beta^{g}\}:g\in G\}
.
Clearly, if
\Delta
is nontrivial and self-paired, then
\Gamma(\Delta)
is
G
-arc-transitive.
Conversely, every
G
-arc-transitive graph can arise in this way.
Note that if
\Gamma(\Delta)
is connected, then
\langle G_{\alpha},g\rangle=G
for any
g\in G
such that
\alpha^{g}=\beta
.

The next lemma will be needed in Section
3
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G=\mathrm{A}_{n}
be a transitive permutation group on a set
\Omega
, where
n\geq 5
, and let
\alpha\in\Omega
.
Suppose that
G_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{A}_{n-2}
or
\mathrm{S}_{n-2}
.
Then there is no connected
(G,2)
-arc-transitive associated orbital graph for any non-trivial suborbit of
G
on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that there exists a connected
(G,2)
-arc-transitive associated orbital graph
\Gamma
with a suborbit
\Delta
of
G
on
\Omega
.
Then
\Delta
is nontrivial and self-paired.

First assume that
G_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{A}_{n-2}
.
Then the action of
G

Some technical lemmas

Let

\Omega

Recall that

\Omega

if

\alpha\in\Omega

A nontrivial cyclic subgroup
\langle g\rangle

of

if and only if

can be expressed as a disjoint product
of
s

such that
st=|\Omega|
and

.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G
be a permutation group on a set
\Omega
,
let
H
be a subgroup of
G
,
and let
H_{\alpha}
be the stabilizer of a point
\alpha\in\Omega
in
H
.
If
H_{\alpha}
contains an element acting fixed-point-freely on
\Omega\setminus\{\alpha\}
,
then
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H_{\alpha})\leq G_{\alpha}
.
In particular, if
H
is
2
-transitive then
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H_{\alpha})\leq G_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathbf{N}_{G}(H_{\alpha})
, and let
h\in H_{\alpha}
such that
h
has no fixed-point on
\Omega\setminus\{\alpha\}
.
Then
\mathsf{Fix}(h)=\{\alpha\}
, and so
\mathsf{Fix}(h^{g})=\{\alpha^{g}\}
.
Since
h^{g}\in(H_{\alpha})^{g}=H_{\alpha}
, we have
\alpha\in\mathsf{Fix}(h^{g})
.
It follows that
\alpha\in\{\alpha^{g}\}
.
Hence
\alpha^{g}=\alpha
, that is,
g\in G_{\alpha}
.
Thus
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H_{\alpha})\leq G_{\alpha}
, as required.

Now suppose that
H
is
2
-transitive.
Then
H_{\alpha}
is transitive on
\Omega\setminus\{\alpha\}
.
By a theorem of Jordan, there exits
h\in H_{\alpha}
such that
h
has no fixed-point on
\Omega\setminus\{\alpha\}
.
This implies
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H_{\alpha})\leq G_{\alpha}
by the above conclusion.
∎

Recall that a
section
of a group
G
is a quotient of a subgroup of
G
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
B=M{:}H
where
H=\mathrm{A}_{n}
with
n\geq 9
. Suppose that
|M|_{r}<r^{n-2}
for each prime divisor
r
of
|M|
and that
M
has no section isomorphic to
H
. Then
B=M\times H
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
B\neq M\times H
. Let

1=M_{0}<M_{1}<\dots<M_{s}=M\lhd B

be a normal series of
B
such that
M_{i+1}/M_{i}
is minimal normal in
B/M_{i}
for each
0\leq i\leq s-1
.
Since
B\neq M\times H
, there exists
0\leq j\leq s-1
such that
M_{j+1}H\neq M_{j+1}\times H
but
M_{i}H=M_{i}\times H
for each
0\leq i\leq j
. Since
M\cap H=1
, we have
M_{j}H/M_{j}\cong H\cong\mathrm{A}_{n}
and
M_{j+1}H/M_{j}=(M_{j+1}/M_{j}){:}(M_{j}H/M_{j})
.
If the conjugation action of
M_{j}H/M_{j}
on
M_{j+1}/M_{j}
is trivial, then
M_{j+1}H/M_{j}=(M_{j+1}/M_{j})\times(M_{j}H/M_{j})
and so
M_{j}H\lhd M_{j+1}H
.
If this is the case, then since
M_{j}H=M_{j}\times H
and
M
has no section isomorphic to
\mathrm{A}_{n}
, it follows that
H
is characteristic in
M_{j}H
and thus normal in
M_{j+1}H
, which implies that
M_{j+1}H=M_{j+1}\times H
, a contradiction.
Hence the conjugation action of
M_{j}H/M_{j}
on
M_{j+1}/M_{j}
is nontrivial, and is thus faithful by the simplicity of
M_{j}H/M_{j}
.
Therefore,
\mathrm{A}_{n}\cong M_{j}H/M_{j}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(M_{j+1}/M_{j})
.

Write
M_{j+1}/M_{j}=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{m}
with
T_{1}\cong\cdots\cong T_{m}\cong T
for some simple group
T
. If
T\cong\mathrm{C}_{p}
for some prime
p
, then
\mathrm{A}_{n}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{C}_{p}^{m})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{m}(p)
, and by
[
15
, Proposition 5.3.7]
we have
m\geq n-2
, which yields that
|M|_{p}\geq|M_{j+1}/M_{j}|=p^{m}\geq p^{n-2}
, contradicting the condition of the lemma. Hence
T
is a nonabelian simple group. Then since
\mathrm{A}_{n}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(T^{m})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(T)\wr\mathrm{S}_{m}
and
M
has no section isomorphic to
\mathrm{A}_{n}
, we derive that
\mathrm{A}_{n}\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T)^{m}=1
and so
\mathrm{A}_{n}\lesssim\mathrm{S}_{m}
. This implies
n\leq m
. Then for any prime divisor
r
of
|T|
we have
|M|_{r}\geq|T|^{m}\geq r^{m}\geq r^{n}
, again contradicting the condition of the lemma.
∎

Let
G
be a
2
-transitive group of degree
n
not containing
\mathrm{A}_{n}
.
Pyber
[
23
]
gave the bound
|G|\leq n^{c\log^{2}n}
for some constant
c>0
, and in particular,
|G|\leq n^{8\lceil 4\log n\rceil\log n}
if
n\geq 400
, where
\log n
means logarithm to the base
2
.
His proof does not rely on the classification of finite simple groups (CFSG for short), and he noted that one can prove
|G|\lessapprox n^{(1+o(1))\log n}
by using CFSG. In the following lemma we shall prove that
|G|<n(n-1)2^{n-4}
. Computation shows that

n(n-1)2^{n-4}<n^{8\lceil 4\log n\rceil\log n}\text{ for }n<168840.

Thus if we use Pyber’s result, we would still need to investigate
2
-transitive groups of degree less than
168840
.
Hence for the convenience of the proof we shall use CFSG.
Then the list of
2
-transitive groups can be found in
[
3
, Tables 7.3 and 7.4]
.
Note that a
2
-transitive group is affine or almost simple.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be a
2
-transitive permutation group with degree
n\geq 11
not containing
\mathrm{A}_{n}
. Then
|G|<n(n-1)2^{n-4}
.

Proof.

Computation shows that
n^{1.5\log n}<n(n-1)2^{n-4}
for
n\geq 31
.
Hence for
n\geq 31
it suffices to show
|G|\leq n^{1.5\log n}
.
By the classification of
2
-transitive groups, it is straightforward to verify the conclusion for
n\leq 30
.
Thus we assume
n\geq 31
for the rest of the proof.

Case 1
:
G
is affine.
Then
G\leq\mathrm{AGL}_{d}(p)
for some prime
p
and integer
d
such that
n=p^{d}
, and so
d+1=\log_{p}n+1\leq\log n+1<1.5\log n
.
Consequently,

|G|\leq|\mathrm{AGL}_{d}(p)|=p^{d}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(p^{d}-p^{i})<p^{d}\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}p^{d}=p^{d(d+1)}=n^{d+1}<n^{1.5\log n}.

Case 2
:
\mathrm{Soc}(G)=\mathrm{PSL}_{d}(q)
with
n=(q^{d}-1)/(q-1)
, where
d\geq 2
and
q=p^{e}
for some prime
p
and integer
e
.
If
d=2
, then

|G|\leq e(d,q-1)|\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)|=q(q+1)(q-1)e\leq q(q+1)2^{q-3}=n(n-1)2^{n-4}.

Now let
d\geq 3
.
Then
|G|\leq 2e(d,q-1)|\mathrm{PSL}_{d}(q)|
. The candidates of the pair
(d,q)
such that
31\leq n\leq 63
are
(5,2)
,
(6,2)
,
(4,3)
,
(3,5)
,
(3,7)
, and direct calculation shows that the lemma is true for these candidates. For
n\geq 64
, since
n>q^{d-1}
, we have
d+2<\log_{q}n+3\leq\log n+3\leq 1.5\log n
and
q^{(d-1)(d+2)}<n^{d+2}<n^{1.5\log n}
, which leads to

|G|\leq 2eq^{d-1}\prod_{i=0}^{d-2}(q^{d}-q^{i})<2eq^{d-1}\prod_{i=0}^{d-2}q^{d}=2eq^{d^{2}-1}\leq q^{d^{2}}<q^{(d-1)(d+2)}<n^{1.5\log n}.

Case 3
:
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{2d}(2)
with
n=2^{2d-1}+2^{d-1}
or
2^{2d-1}-2^{d-1}
, where
d\geq 3
.
If
d=3
, then
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{6}(2)
, and so
|G|<31^{1.5\log 31}\leq n^{1.5\log n}
as
n\geq 31
.
If
d\geq 4
, then
n\geq 2^{2d-1}-2^{d-1}>2^{2d-2}
and

, which implies that

|G|=2^{d^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(2^{2i}-1)<2^{d^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}2^{2i}=2^{d(2d+1)}\leq 2^{(2d-2)\cdot(d+2)}<n^{d+2}<n^{1.5\log n}.

Case 4
:
\mathrm{Soc}(G)=\mathrm{PSU}_{3}(q)
with
n=q^{3}+1
, where
q=p^{e}\geq 4
for some prime
p
and integer
e
.
In this case, we have

|G|\leq 2(3,q+1)e|\mathrm{PSU}_{3}(q)|=2eq^{3}(q^{3}+1)(q^{2}-1)<q^{9}<(q^{3}+1)^{3}=n^{3}<n^{1.5\log n}.

Case 5
:
\mathrm{Soc}(G)=\mathrm{Sz}(q)
with
n=q^{2}+1
, where
q=2^{2e+1}\geq 8
for some integer
e
.
Then

|G|\leq|\mathrm{Sz}(q)|(2e+1)=(2e+1)q^{2}(q^{2}+1)(q-1)<q^{6}<(q^{2}+1)^{3}=n^{3}<n^{1.5\log n}.

Case 6
:
\mathrm{Soc}(G)=\mathrm{Ree}(q)
and
n=q^{3}+1
, where
q=3^{2e+1}\geq 27
for some integer

.
In this case,

|G|\leq|\mathrm{Ree}(q)|(2e+1)=(2e+1)q^{3}(q^{3}+1)(q-1)<q^{8}<(q^{3}+1)^{3}=n^{3}<n^{1.5\log n}.

For

not in any of Cases 1–6, by the classification of

-transitive groups, there are finitely many candidates for

∎

g
has order
2
, the same order as
\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
, it follows that
g=\tau
. However, this leads to
\omega^{-1}=\omega^{g}=\omega^{\tau}=\omega^{q}
, which implies that
\omega
has order dividing
q+1
, contradicting
\langle\omega\rangle=M_{0}=\mathrm{GL}_{1}(q^{2})
.

Thus we conclude that
g\notin N
. As a consequence,
K^{g}\neq K
, and so
K\cap K^{g}<K
.
From
g\in\mathbf{N}_{G}(K_{0})
we deduce that
K\cap K^{g}\geq K_{0}
.
Then since
K_{0}
is maximal in
K
, we conclude that
K\cap K^{g}=K_{0}
.
Hence the action of
K
on
[K:K\cap K^{g}]
is permutation isomorphic to the natural action of
K
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{A\Gamma L}_{1}(q^{2})
, which is
2
-transitive. In particular,
\Gamma
has valency
|K:K\cap K^{g}|=|K:K_{0}|=q^{2}
.

Since
\tau
is an involution with
\mathrm{Fix}(\tau)=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it is a product of
(q^{2}-q)/2
transpositions.
From the condition
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
we derive that
(q^{2}-q)/2
is odd.
Thus
\tau\notin\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, and so
|K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|=|K|/2=q^{2}(q^{2}-1)
.
Since
\omega
is a
(q^{2}-1)
-cycle with
q
odd, we have
\omega\notin\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
.
Hence
K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\geq\mathrm{Soc}(K){:}\langle\omega^{2},\omega\tau\rangle
, which in conjunction with
|K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|=q^{2}(q^{2}-1)=|\mathrm{Soc}(K){:}\langle\omega^{2},\omega\tau\rangle|
implies that

K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=\mathrm{Soc}(K){:}\langle\omega^{2},\omega\tau\rangle.

Since
1^{\omega}
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
and
q
is odd, the orbits of
1^{\omega}
and
(1^{\omega})^{q}
under
\langle\omega^{2}\rangle
, respectively, are the orbits of
\langle\omega^{2}\rangle
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
.
Then since
(1^{\omega})^{q}=1^{\omega\tau}
, we obtain
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}=1^{\langle\omega^{2}\rangle}\cup(1^{\omega\tau})^{\langle\omega^{2}\rangle}
, which implies that
\langle\omega^{2},\omega\tau\rangle
is transitive on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
.
Therefore, the action of
\mathrm{Soc}(K){:}\langle\omega^{2},\omega\tau\rangle
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is
2
-transitive, and so is the action of
K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
on
[K:K\cap K^{g}]
.

Since
G=K\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, the right multiplication action of
\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
on
[G:K]
is transitive.
This together with the
2
-transitivity of
K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
on
[K:K\cap K^{g}]
implies that
\Gamma
is
(\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}),2)
-arc-transitive.
Since
K_{0,1}=\langle\tau\rangle
, we have

K\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})_{0,1}=\langle\tau\rangle\cap\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})_{0,1}=1.

Observe that
|K||\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})_{0,1}|=2q^{2}(q^{2}-1)|\mathrm{A}_{q^{2}-2}|=|G|
.
We then conclude that
\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})_{0,1}
acts regularly on
[G:K]
, and so
\Gamma
is a Cayley graph on
\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})_{0,1}\cong\mathrm{A}_{q^{2}-2}
.

Now we prove that
\Gamma
is connected, that is,
\langle K,g\rangle=G
. Let
X=\langle K,g\rangle
. Then
X
is
2
-transitive on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Since
\tau\notin\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
and
\tau\in K\leq X
, the group
X
is not contained in
\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
. Then by the classification of
2
-transitive groups, either
X=\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
, or
X\leq Y:=\mathrm{AGL}_{2f}(p)
. Suppose for a contradiction that the latter occurs. Then
g\in X_{0}\leq Y_{0}
and
K_{0}\leq X_{0}\leq Y_{0}
. As
g
normalizes
M_{0}
, it follows that
g\in\mathbf{N}_{Y_{0}}(M_{0})
. However, since
M_{0}
is a Single cycle in
Y_{0}=\mathrm{GL}_{2f}(p)
, we see from
[
12
, Theorem 7.3]
that
\mathbf{N}_{Y_{0}}(M_{0})=\mathrm{\Gamma L}_{1}(q^{2})=N_{0}
, and so
g\in N_{0}
, contradicting the conclusion that
g\notin N
.
Thus
X=\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=G
, which implies that
\Gamma
is connected. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

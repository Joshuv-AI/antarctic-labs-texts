Abstract
. We introduce a new class of quasi-linear parabolic equations involving nonhomogeneous degeneracy or/and singularity ∂ t u = [ | D ​ u | q + a ⁡ ( x , t ) ​ | D ​ u | s ] ​ ( Δ ​ u + ( p − 2 ) ​ ⟨ D 2 ​ u ​ D ​ u | D ​ u | , D ​ u | D ​ u | ⟩ ) , \partial_{t}u=[|Du|^{q}+a(x,t)|Du|^{s}]\left(\Delta u+(p-2)\left\langle D^{2}u\frac{Du}{|Du|},\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right\rangle\right), where 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , − 1 < q ≤ s < ∞ -1<q\leq s<\infty and a ⁡ ( x , t ) ≥ 0 a(x,t)\geq 0 . The motivation to investigate this model stems not only from the connections to tug-of-war like stochastic games with noise, but also from the non-standard growth problems of double phase type. According to different values of q , s q,s , such equations include nonhomogeneous degeneracy or singularity, and may involve these two features simultaneously. In particular, when q = p − 2 q=p-2 and q < s q<s , it will encompass the parabolic p p -Laplacian both in divergence form and in non-divergence form. We aim to explore the from L ∞ L^{\infty} to C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} regularity theory for the aforementioned problem. To be precise, under some proper assumptions, we use geometrical methods to establish the local Hölder regularity of spatial gradients of viscosity solutions.

1.
Introduction

Let
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball with radius
r
centered at the origin and
Q_{r}=B_{r}\times(-r^{2},0]
. In this paper, we are concerned with the interior regularity for viscosity solutions to the following quasi-linear parabolic equation with nonhomogeneous degeneracy or/and singularity

\partial_{t}u=[|Du|^{q}+a(x,t)|Du|^{s}]\Delta_{p}^{N}u\quad\textmd{in }Q_{1},

(1.1)

where
1<p<\infty
,
-1<q\leq s<\infty
,
0\leq a(x,t)\in C^{1}(Q_{1})
, and
\Delta_{p}^{N}
denotes the normalized
p
-Laplace operator defined by

\Delta_{p}^{N}u=\Delta u+(p-2)\left\langle D^{2}u\frac{Du}{|Du|},\frac{Du}{|Du|}\right\rangle=\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{|Du|^{2}}\right)u_{ij}.

Here the summation convention is exploited and
Du
is the gradient of
u
in the spatial variable
x
. Throughout this paper,
u_{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}u
,
u_{ij}=\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u
,
D_{x,t}u=(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{x_{1}}u,\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}}u)^{T}
.

Over the last decade, a linkage between the stochastic tug-of-war games and nonlinear equations of
p
-Laplacian type, starting with the pioneering papers of Peres-Schramm-Sheffield-Wilson
[
52
]
and Peres-Sheffield
[
53
]
, has received lots of attention. For the parabolic scenario, Manfredi-Parviainen-Rossi
[
45
]
proved that the solutions to

\partial_{t}u=\Delta_{p}^{N}u

(1.2)

could be derived as the limits of value functions for tug-of-war games with noise if the parameter controlling the size of the possible steps tends to zero. We remark that the normalized
p
-Laplacian can be regarded as the 1-homogeneous version of standard
p
-Laplacian or as a mixture of the Laplacian and normalized infinity Laplacian,
\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u=|Du|^{-2}\langle D^{2}uDu,Du\rangle
. The lower regularity for solutions of the homogeneous or nonhomogeneous elliptic normalized
p
-Laplace equation was obtained in
[
44
,
55
]
(see
[
8
,
9
,
29
]
for the parabolic analogue). The first contribution on the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for such equations is due to the seminal work of Jin-Silvestre
[
36
]
, where they established the interior Hölder gradient estimates of solutions to (
1.2
). This result was extended to the inhomogeneous parabolic normalized
p
-Laplacian in
[
3
]
. For the inhomogeneous elliptic counterpart

-\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f(x)\quad\textmd{in }\Omega,

Attouchi-Parviainen-Ruosteenoja
[
4
]
showed that the solutions are locally
C^{1,\alpha}
regular under the condition that
f(x)\in L^{q}
with
q\leq\infty
possessing a suitably large lower bound; see also
[
10
]
for the case that
f(x)\in L(n,1)
, where
L(n,1)
denotes the standard Lorentz space. In addition, the existence of viscosity solutions to (
1.2
) has been obtained in
[
8
,
9
,
29
]
by using approximation techniques that are different from the game-theoretic arguments
[
45
]
. Juutinen
[
37
]
investigated the asymptotic behavior for (
1.2
). For more results on the stochastic tug-of-war game and the
p
-Laplacian operators, see for instance
[
40
,
42
,
46
,
54
]
.

On the other hand, equation (
1.1
) is motivated by the double phase problems as well. We observe that equation (
1.1
) is a new model of quasi-linear parabolic equations featuring a nonhomogeneous degenerate or/and singular term modelled on the double phase integrand

H(x,t,\xi):=|\xi|^{p}+a(x,t)|\xi|^{q},\quad\quad a(x,t)\geq 0,\quad 1<p\leq q.

(1.3)

In the elliptic case (i.e., the function
a
is independent of
t
), from a variational point of view, (
1.3
) is closely related to the following energy functional

u\mapsto\int(|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q})\,dx,

(1.4)

which was originally introduced by Zhikov
[
57
,
58
]
in the context of homogenization and Lavrentiev phenomenon. Such functionals can provide useful models for describing the behaviours of strongly anisotropic materials. More precisely, considering two diverse materials with hardening exponents
p
and
q
separately, the modulating coefficient
a(\cdot)
determines the geometry of the mixture composed of the two, according to whether
x
belongs to the zero set
\{a(x)=0\}
or not. These functionals with non-standard growth conditions

u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}F(x,u,Du)\,dx,\quad\nu|\xi|^{p}\leq F(x,u,\xi)\leq L(|\xi|^{q}+1),

have been a surge of interest. In the autonomous case that energy density
F(x,u,Du)\equiv F(Du)
, the regularity theory for minima of such functionals is by now well-known from the prominent works of Marcellini
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
. The investigation of double phase functional (
1.4
) has been continued in a series of nice papers by Colombo, Mingione et al. For instance, the local
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for minimizers of functional (
1.4
) was obtained in
[
11
,
19
,
20
]
under some hypotheses that
a(x)
is Hölder continuous and the magnitude of the difference
q-p\geq 0
is suitably small. Whereafter, the Calderón-Zygmund type estimates for the weak solutions to

\operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(x)|Du|^{q-2}Du)=\operatorname{div}(|F|^{p-2}F+a(x)|F|^{q-2}F)

were proved in
[
14
,
21
,
25
]
. See also
[
26
]
for the manifold constrained problem,
[
16
]
for the obstacle problem,
[
31
]
for the equivalence of weak and viscosity solutions and
[
17
]
for the potential theory. More results can be found in
[
7
,
18
,
30
,
43
]
and reference therein. Very recently, De Filippis
[
24
]
established the quantitative gradient bounds for weak solutions to the following parabolic double phase equations

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du+a(x,t)|Du|^{q-2}Du)=0.

The Harnack’s type inequality for this equation was also derived in
[
13
]
. However, as far as we know, the relevant regularity results regarding the parabolic double phase models are vary rare in the literature.

Influenced by the aforementioned works, we in the present paper introduce a new model (
1.1
) by combining the normalized
p
-Laplacian operator with the double phase gradient-diffusion. It is worthwhile mentioning that problem (
1.1
) exhibits some novel and intriguing characteristics. In the cases that
0<q\leq s
and
-1<q\leq s<0
, (
1.1
) possesses the nonhomogeneous degeneracy and singularity, respectively. Furthermore, (
1.1
) has both singularity and degeneracy when
-1<q<0<s
. In particular, equation (
1.1
) incorporates
p
-Laplacian in divergence form together with
p
-Laplacian in non-divergence form simultaneously provided that
q=p-2
and
s>q
, that is,

\partial_{t}u-\operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)-a(x,t)|Du|^{s}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0.

Meanwhile, equation (
1.1
) is a natural extension of canonical quasi-linear parabolic equations with singularity or degeneracy, whose highly celebrated prototype is

\partial_{t}u-|Du|^{q}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0.

(1.5)

Imbert-Jin-Silvestre
[
34
]
showed the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions
u
to (
1.5
) in
Q_{1}
, which states that

\|Du\|_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C

and

\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{1/2}}}{{t\neq s}}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x,s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}}\leq C.

Later, for the nonhomogeneous analogue,

\partial_{t}u-|Du|^{q}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f(x,t),

the local
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions was completed under the assumption that
f
is continuous and bounded; see
[
2
]
for the degenerate case
q\geq 0
and
[
6
]
for the singular case
-1<q<0
. Additionally, several extra aspects of such equations have already been explored as well, such as existence and uniqueness of solutions
[
15
,
27
]
, the comparison principles
[
32
,
50
]
, Aleksandrov-Bakelman-Pucci type estimate
[
1
]
, parabolic Harnack’s inequality
[
51
]
. For the related regularity results in the elliptic context, we refer to
[
5
,
12
,
35
]
and the references therein.

In this work we make use of a unified geometrical method developed by Jin-Silvestre
[
36
]
and Imbert-Jin-Silvestre
[
34
]
to study the interior Hölder continuity for the spatial gradient of solutions to (
1.1
). The strategies of the proof concentrate mainly on verifying that the oscillation of gradient is declining in a shrinking sequence of parabolic cylinders, and then reducing the iterative step to a dichotomy between two cases: either the gradient
Du
stays close to a fixed unit vector
e
for most points
(x,t)
(in measure), or it does not, and at last patching these two alternatives together. We shall first prove the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for solutions to (
1.1
) with the strong restriction that
\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
is small (less than 1). Then, by employing a scaling technique, we infer the
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for solutions of (
1.1
) under the assumption that
a(x,t)\in C^{1}(Q_{1})
. Due to the presence of coefficient
a(x,t)
and the fact that the nonhomogeneous
(q,s)
-growth gradient-diffusion terms are intertwined in equation (
1.1
), the theoretical analysis in the current study is radically much more challenging than the previous ones. The significant distinctions and difficulties are as follows. First, in order to obtain the improvement of oscillation for
|Du|
, it is indispensable to incorporate more terms involving
Da(x,t)
when we differentiate the regularized equation (
3.1
) in
x
-variable. Therefore, in comparison to the proof of Lemma 4.1 in
[
34
]
, we need additional elaborate analyses and construct a much more complicated auxiliary function. Second, the comparison principle (Proposition
3.5
) cannot plainly follow from the known results due to the presence of
a(x,t)
. We have to meticulously apply the information from the maximum principle for semicontinuous functions, together with properties such as the local Lipschitz continuity with respect to the matrix square root. Moreover, the Lipschitz regularity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) plays a rather crucial role in the proof. To the best of our knowledge, the comparison principle is new, which is also of independent interest. Finally, in order to establish the Lipschitz estimates for equation (
3.1
) in the spatial variable, we shall employ Ishii-Lions’ method twice: we first use it to deduce the solutions are Hölder continuity in
x
-variable, and then we rely on this Hölder regularity and use the Ishii-Lions’ method again to show the Lipschitz estimates.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first recall the definition of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) and then give the main result derived in this paper. Section
3
contains the Lipschitz continuity in the spatial variables, the Hölder continuity in the time variable as well as two important properties of viscosity solutions. Section
4
is devoted to establishing the Hölder estimates on the spatial gradients of solutions, which is the most technically challenging part. We complete the proof of comparison principle (Proposition
3.5
) in Section
5
. The technical proof of the Lipschitz continuity in the spatial variables (Lemma
3.1
) is postponed to Section
6

7

4.11
.

2.
Main result

The aim of this paper is to establish the interior Hölder estimates for spatial gradients of solutions to problem (
1.1
). To this end, the following hypotheses will be in force. We first assume that

1<p<\infty.

(2.1)

Furthermore, concerning the nonhomogeneous degeneracy or/and singularity term appearing in (
1.1
), we shall require that the exponents
q,s
fulfill

-1<q\leq s<\infty,

(2.2)

and that the modulating coefficient
a(\cdot)
is such that

0<a^{-}:=\inf_{Q_{1}}a(x,t)\leq a(x,t)\leq a^{+}:=\sup_{Q_{1}}a(x,t)<\infty,

(2.3)

and

a(x,t)\in C^{1}(Q_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad A:=\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}<\infty.

(2.4)

Throughout this article, the assumptions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are always supposed to hold.

In the degenerate case (i.e.,
q\geq 0
), the definition of viscosity solutions is straightforward. Nonetheless, the formulations of defining viscosity solutions cannot be displayed specifically for the case when singularity occurs (i.e.,
-1<q<0
). Hence we here adopt the same notion of viscosity solutions as the one utilized in
[
38
]
to provide a unified way of defining solutions in the degenerate and singular conditions. Next let us recall the definition of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1

(viscosity solution)
.

A finite almost everywhere and lower semicontinuous function
u:Q_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a viscosity supersolution to (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
, if whenever
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(Q_{1})
are such that
u-\varphi
attains a local minimum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
and moreover
D\varphi(x,t)\neq 0
for
x\neq x_{0}
, then we obtain

\limsup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})}}{{x\neq x_{0}}}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)-[|D\varphi(x,t)|^{q}+a(x,t)|D\varphi(x,t)|^{s}]\Delta_{p}^{N}\varphi(x,t)\right)\geq 0.

A finite almost everywhere and upper semicontinuous function
u:Q_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is a viscosity subsolution to (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
, if whenever
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(Q_{1})
are such that
u-\varphi
reaches a local maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
and moreover
D\varphi(x,t)\neq 0
for
x\neq x_{0}
, then we derive

\liminf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})}}{{x\neq x_{0}}}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)-[|D\varphi(x,t)|^{q}+a(x,t)|D\varphi(x,t)|^{s}]\Delta_{p}^{N}\varphi(x,t)\right)\leq 0.

A function
u
is called a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) if and only if it is both viscosity super- and subsolution.

Remark 2.2
.

When
D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})\neq 0
, these limits above are explicit,

\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})-[|D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})|^{q}+a(x_{0},t_{0})|D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})|^{s}]\Delta_{p}^{N}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq(\leq)0.

In addition, when
0\leq q\leq s
, the condition that
D\varphi(x,t)\neq 0
for
x\neq x_{0}
can be removed actually. For example, if
D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})=0
, after careful computations, the supremum limit turns into

(i)

the case that
q=s=0
,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})-(1+a(x_{0},t_{0}))\left(\mathrm{tr}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))+(p-2)\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))\right)\geq 0,&\text{{}}p\geq 2,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})-(1+a(x_{0},t_{0}))\left(\mathrm{tr}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))+(p-2)\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))\right)\geq 0,&\text{{}}p\in(1,2).\end{cases}

(ii)

the case that
0=q<s
,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})-\left(\mathrm{tr}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))+(p-2)\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))\right)\geq 0,&\text{{when}}\quad p\geq 2,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})-\left(\mathrm{tr}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))+(p-2)\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}))\right)\geq 0,&\text{{when}}\quad p\in(1,2).\end{cases}

(iii)

the case that
0<q\leq s
,

\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0.

Here
\mathrm{tr}(N)
is the trace of matrix
N
, and
\lambda_{\rm min}(N)
(
\lambda_{\rm max}(N)
) denotes the minimum (maximum) eigenvalue of
N
.

The infimal limit in definition can be tackled analogously.

Now we are in position to state our main contribution of this work.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let the conditions (
2.1
)–(
2.4
) be in force. Suppose that
u
is a bounded viscosity solution to equation (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
. Then there are two constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>0
, both depending upon

\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{1/2}}}{{t\neq s}}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x,s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}}\leq C.

3.
Lower regularity for solutions

In order to circumvent some technical difficulties created by the lack of smoothness of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
), we first study the regularized equation below

\partial_{t}u=\big[(|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)u_{ij}

(3.1)

in
Q_{1}
, where
0<\varepsilon<1
. Then we devote to obtaining uniform estimates with respect to
\varepsilon
so that we could pass to the limit in the end.

In this section, we are going to show the Lipschitz continuity in the spatial variables and the Hölder continuity in the time variable. Now we first present the Lipschitz estimates independent of
\varepsilon
on solutions to equation (
3.1
). However, the proof of this lemma is rather long and delicate, which was postponed to Section
6
. It is worth mentioning that, from the proof below, we can easily find that the Lipschitz estimates also hold true for
\varepsilon=0
. We state this result as follows.

Lemma 3.1

(Local Lipschitz estimates in
x
-variable)
.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and the assumptions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) be in force. Let
u
be a smooth solution to (
3.1
) in
Q_{1}
. Assume that
a(x,t)\geq a^{-}>0
and
a(x,t)
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
x
-variable, that is, there exists a constant
C_{\rm lip}>0
, independent of
t
-variable, such that
|a(x,t)-a(y,t)|\leq C_{\rm lip}|x-y|
. Then for all
r\in(0,\frac{7}{8}]
, there holds that

|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C|x-y|

for
(x,t),(y,t)\in\overline{Q_{r}}
, where
C>0
depends on
n,p,q,s,a^{-},C_{\rm lip}
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
.

Remark 3.2
.

From the proof of this lemma in Section
6
, we can see the explicit dependencies of the above constant
C
with

C:=C(n,p,q,s)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\left[1+\left(\frac{C_{\rm lip}}{a^{-}}\right)^{2}\right].

If
a(x,t)
is supposed to be of class
C^{1}(\overline{Q_{1}})
in the previous lemma, then the constant
C_{\rm lip}>0
can be replaced by
A:=\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
.

Based on the Lipschitz estimates above and a simple comparison argument, we can demonstrate that the solutions to (
3.1
) are Hölder continuous in
t
, which will be utilized in Lemma
4.8
below.

Lemma 3.3

(Local Hölder estimates in
t
-variable)
.

Suppose that
u
is a smooth solution of (
3.1
) in
Q_{1}
with
0<\varepsilon<1
. Let
a(x,t)
satisfy that
|a(x,t)-a(y,t)|\leq C_{\rm lip}|x-y|
in
Q_{1}
. Then under the assumptions (
2.1
)–(
2.3
), the following estimates hold:

(i)

for
0\leq q\leq s
,

\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{3/4}}}{{t\neq s}}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x,s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C;

(ii)

for
-1<q\leq s<0
or
-1<q<0\leq s
,

\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t),(x,s)\in Q_{3/4}}}{{t\neq s}}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x,s)|}{|t-s|^{\frac{1}{\beta(1+s)-s}}}\leq C,

where
\beta=\frac{q+2}{q+1}
and
C
depends on
n,p,q,s,a^{-},a^{+},C_{\rm lip}
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\geq 2
be determined later, according to three different scenarios, i.e.,
-1<q\leq s<0
,
-1<q<0\leq s
and
0\leq q\leq s
. For all
t_{0}\in\left[-\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2},0\right)
and
\eta>0
, we now assert that there are two constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
such that

u(x,t)-u(0,t_{0})\leq\eta+L_{1}(t-t_{0})+L_{2}|x|^{\beta}=:\varphi(x,t)

(3.2)

for any
\overline{B_{3/4}}\times[t_{0},0]
. We first select
L_{2}\geq 2(\frac{4}{3})^{\beta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
such that (
3.2
) holds for
x\in\partial B_{3/4}
, and in turn take
L_{2}
such that (
3.2
) holds for

. That is to say, we can choose properly such
L_{2}>0
that (
3.2
) does hold on the boundary of
\overline{B_{3/4}}\times[t_{0},0]
. Indeed, due to
u
is Lipschitz continuous in the spatial variables, we may take

L_{2}\geq\frac{\|Du\|^{\beta}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}}{\eta^{\beta-1}}

to guarantee that

\eta+L_{2}|x|^{\beta}\geq\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}|x|

by Young’s inequality, which implies that (

. Here we note that
\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}
is bounded depending on

and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
. In the rest of proof, we fix

Next, we are ready to select
L_{1}
such that
\varphi(x,t)
is a supersolution to certain equations. Inequality (

) then follows by the comparison principle. The remaining proof is completed under three diverse cases.

Case 1.

-1<q\leq s<0
. We shall show that
\varphi(x,t)
is a supersolution to equation (
3.1
), that is,

\partial_{t}\varphi-\big[(|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}}{|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}\geq 0.

(3.3)

We first calculate

D\varphi=\beta L_{2}|x|^{\beta-2}x,

D^{2}\varphi=\beta L_{2}|x|^{\beta-2}I+\beta(\beta-2)L_{2}|x|^{\beta-2}\frac{x}{|x|}\otimes\frac{x}{|x|},

where it is easy to see that
D^{2}\varphi
is a positive definite matrix and

\|D^{2}\varphi\|\leq\beta(\beta-1)L_{2}|x|^{\beta-2}.

(3.4)

Here
\xi\otimes\xi
is the matrix with entries
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
for a vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We next evaluate

\displaystyle\quad\big[(|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}}{|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)(|D\varphi|^{q}+a^{+}|D\varphi|^{s})\|D^{2}\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(L^{1+q}_{2}|x|^{q(\beta-1)+\beta-2}+L^{1+s}_{2}|x|^{s(\beta-1)+\beta-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(L^{1+q}_{2}+L^{1+s}_{2})

by taking
\beta\geq\frac{q+2}{q+1}(>2)
, where
C
depends only on
n,p,q,s,a^{+}
. Thereby, in order to assure (
3.3
), we need to fix
L_{1}=C(L^{1+q}_{2}+L^{1+s}_{2})
.

Finally, applying the comparison principle together with the choices of
L_{1},L_{2}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\quad u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\eta+L_{1}(t-t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\eta+C\left(\eta^{1-\beta}\|Du\|^{\beta}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}+2(4/3)^{\beta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1\right)^{1+q}(t-t_{0})

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\eta^{1-\beta}\|Du\|^{\beta}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}+2(4/3)^{\beta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1\right)^{1+s}(t-t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\eta+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+q)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}\eta^{(1-\beta)(1+q)}|t-t_{0}|+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+s)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}\eta^{(1-\beta)(1+s)}|t-t_{0}|

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1)^{1+s}|t-t_{0}|.

We now pick
\eta=|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma}
with
0<\gamma<1
to be fixed later. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\quad u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma}+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+q)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma(1-\beta)(1+q)+1}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+s)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma(1-\beta)(1+s)+1}+C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1)^{1+s}|t-t_{0}|.

Then
\gamma
can be chosen as

\begin{cases}\gamma(1-\beta)(1+q)+1-\gamma\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\gamma(1-\beta)(1+s)+1-\gamma\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
0<\gamma<1,\end{cases}

which leads to

\gamma\leq\frac{1}{\beta(1+s)-s}(<1)

by noting that
-1<q\leq s<0
. As has been stated above, we could determine
\beta=\frac{q+2}{q+1}
and
\gamma=\frac{1}{\beta(1+s)-s}
. We then get the desired result for the case that
-1<q\leq s<0
.

Case 2.

-1<q<0\leq s
. Similarly to Case 1, we get

\displaystyle\quad\big[(|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}}{|D\varphi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)(|D\varphi|^{q}+a^{+}(|D\varphi|^{s}+1))\|D^{2}\varphi\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(L^{1+q}_{2}|x|^{q(\beta-1)+\beta-2}+L_{2}|x|^{\beta-2}+L^{1+s}_{2}|x|^{s(\beta-1)+\beta-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(L^{1+q}_{2}+L_{2}+L^{1+s}_{2})

by taking
\beta\geq\frac{q+2}{q+1}
, where
C
depends only on
n,p,q,s
and
a^{+}
. Hence we can choose

to ensure (
3.3
).

Then through the comparison principle and the choices of

, it yields that

\displaystyle\quad u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\eta+L_{1}(t-t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\eta+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+q)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}\eta^{(1-\beta)(1+q)}|t-t_{0}|+C\|Du\|^{\beta}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}\eta^{1-\beta}|t-t_{0}|

\displaystyle\quad+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+s)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}\eta^{(1-\beta)(1+s)}|t-t_{0}|+C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1)^{1+s}|t-t_{0}|.

Let
\eta=|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma}
. The above display then becomes

\displaystyle\quad u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma}+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+q)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma(1-\beta)(1+q)+1}+C\|Du\|^{\beta}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma(1-\beta)+1}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|Du\|^{\beta(1+s)}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}|t-t_{0}|^{\gamma(1-\beta)(1+s)+1}+C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1)^{1+s}|t-t_{0}|.

We shall pick

such that

\begin{cases}\gamma(1-\beta)(1+q)+1-\gamma\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\gamma(1-\beta)+1-\gamma\geq 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\gamma(1-\beta)(1+s)+1-\gamma\geq 0,\end{cases}

i.e.,

Consequently, we can fix

and

0\leq q\leq s

\partial_{t}\varphi-\big[(|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}\geq 0.

\partial_{t}\varphi-a_{ij}(x,t)\varphi_{ij}\geq 0,

and

actually. We fix

u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})\leq\eta+C(\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}+1)^{s}(\eta^{-1}\|Du\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1)|t-t_{0}|.

we derive

\displaystyle u(0,t)-u(0,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}+1)^{\frac{s}{2}+1}|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|Du\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{7/8})}+1)^{s}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+1)|t-t_{0}|.

4.

4.1.
Hölder regularity of spatial gradients in the case that

Lemma 4.1
.

Let the conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) be in force. Assume that
u
is a smooth solution of (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
|Du|\leq 1
in

. For each

and
\mu>0
, if

and

\tau_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{4})

and

Proof.

Set

a_{ij}(x,t,\eta)=\big[(|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\eta_{i}\eta_{j}}{|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

(4.3)

with
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

a_{ij,m}(x,t,\eta):=\frac{\partial a_{ij}(x,t,\eta)}{\partial\eta_{m}},

where
\eta_{i}
denotes the
i

\eta
. By differentiating equation (
3.1
) in

, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u_{k})

\displaystyle=a_{ij}(x,t,Du)(u_{k})_{ij}+a_{ij,m}(x,t,Du)u_{ij}(u_{k})_{m}

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{k}a(x,t)(|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)u_{ij}.

In the rest of proof, let

b_{ij}(Du)=(|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{|Du|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

We further have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(Du\cdot e-l)

\displaystyle=a_{ij}(x,t,Du)(Du\cdot e-l)_{ij}+a_{ij,m}(x,t,Du)u_{ij}(Du\cdot e-l)_{m}

\displaystyle\quad+Da\cdot eb_{ij}(Du)u_{ij}.

Let

By direct calculation, it yields that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h

\displaystyle=a_{ij}(x,t,Du)h_{ij}+a_{ij,m}(x,t,Du)u_{ij}h_{m}+2Da\cdot Dub_{ij}(Du)u_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad-2a_{ij}(x,t,Du)u_{ki}u_{kj}.

For
\rho=\frac{l}{4}

.
In the region

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w

\displaystyle=a_{ij}(x,t,Du)w_{ij}+a_{ij,m}(x,t,Du)u_{ij}w_{m}+Da\cdot(e+2\rho Du)b_{ij}(Du)u_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad-2\rho a_{ij}(x,t,Du)u_{ki}u_{kj}.

(4.4)

Observe that

\displaystyle\quad a_{ij,m}(x,t,\eta)

\displaystyle=\big[q(|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\eta_{m}+sa(x,t)(|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\eta_{m}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\eta_{i}\eta_{j}}{|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

(4.5)

4.8
) is reformulated as

\partial_{t}w\leq\tilde{a}_{ij}w_{ij}+C_{1}(l)|Dw|^{2}+\overline{c},

with
\overline{c}\equiv C_{2}(l)H^{2}
. Set

W=1-l+\rho+\overline{c},

and

U=\frac{1}{\nu}\left(1-e^{\nu(w-\overline{c}t-W)}\right)\quad\text{with }\nu>0.

We could determine
\nu>0
, which depends on
n,p,q,s,a^{+}
and
l
, such that

\partial_{t}U\geq\tilde{a}_{ij}U_{ij}\quad\text{in }Q_{1}

in the viscosity sense. Obviously,
U\geq 0
in
Q_{1}
.

If
Du\cdot e\leq l
, then it follows from the assumption in the statement that

|\{(x,t)\in Q_{1}:U\geq\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\}|>\mu|Q_{1}|.

Thereby, we can infer from Proposition 2.3 in
[
36
]
that there are two constants
\tau_{0},\gamma_{0}>0
such that

U\geq\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\gamma_{0}\quad\text{in }Q_{\tau_{0}},

where
\tau_{0}
depends only on
n,\mu
and
\gamma_{0}
depends on
n,\mu,p,q,s
and
a^{+}
. Moreover, since
w-\overline{c}t\leq W
, we could readily get

U\leq W-w+\overline{c}t.

Thus, in
Q_{\tau_{0}}
we have

Du\cdot e+\rho|Du|^{2}\leq 1+\rho-\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\gamma_{0}+\overline{c}+\overline{c}t,

which leads to

Du\cdot e+\rho(Du\cdot e)^{2}\leq 1+\rho-\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\gamma_{0}+\overline{c}.

It yields that

Du\cdot e\leq\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4\rho(1+\rho-\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\gamma_{0}+\overline{c})}}{2\rho}\quad\text{in }Q_{\tau_{0}}.

If

H<(C_{2}^{-1}(l)\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\gamma_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}=:\kappa(\leq 1),

which implies that
\overline{c}<\nu^{-1}(1-e^{\nu(l-1)})\gamma_{0}
, then there holds that

Du\cdot e\leq 1-\delta\quad\text{in }Q_{\tau_{0}},

where
\delta>0
depends on
n,p,q,s,a^{+},\mu
and
l
. Briefly, when the upper bound on
|Da(x,t)|
is small enough depending on
n,p,q,s,a^{+},\mu
and
l
, we arrive at
Du\cdot e\leq 1-\delta
in
Q_{\tau_{0}}
. Finally, we select

\tau=\begin{cases}\tau_{0}(1-\delta)^{\frac{q}{2}}&{\text{if }q\geq 0},\\[5.69054pt]
\tau_{0}&{\text{if }-1<q<0}\end{cases}

such that
Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}\subset Q_{\tau_{0}}
. We now complete the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Observe that the selection of
\tau
and
\delta
above implies that

\tau<(1-\delta)^{\frac{q}{2}}\quad\text{when }q\geq 0.

In the rest of this work, we shall choose such smaller

\tau<(1-\delta)^{1+q}\quad\text{for any }q\geq-1.

(4.9)

If Lemma
4.1
holds true in all directions

, then it in effect indicates a reduction in the oscillation of
Du
in a smaller parabolic cylinder. This content is stated by the forthcoming corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let the conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) be in force. Suppose that
u
is a smooth solution of (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
|Du|\leq 1
in
Q_{1}
. For each
l\in(0,1)
and
\mu>0
, if
0\leq a(x,t)\in C^{1}(Q_{1})
and
\|Da\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\kappa
, where
\kappa\in(0,1]
is a small enough quantity depending on
n,p,q,s,a^{+},\mu
and
l
, then there exist
\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
\delta>0
that both depend upon
n,p,q,s,a^{+},\mu
and

such that for all nonnegative integer

if

\left|\left\{(x,t)\in Q_{\tau^{i}}^{(1-\delta)^{i}}:Du\cdot e\leq l(1-\delta)^{i}\right\}\right|>\mu\left|Q_{\tau^{i}}^{(1-\delta)^{i}}\right|

(4.10)

for all
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
i=0,1,2,\cdots,k
, then one has

|Du|<(1-\delta)^{i+1}

in
Q_{\tau^{i+1}}^{(1-\delta)^{i+1}}
for

.

Remark 4.4
.

Notice that we could further impose on
\delta
that
\delta<\frac{1}{2}
and
\delta<1-\tau
.

Proof.

Argue by induction. If
i=0
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
Du\cdot e<1-\delta
in
Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}
for all
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, which leads to
|Du|<1-\delta
in
Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}
. We now suppose that this claim holds true for
i=0,1,2,\cdots,k-1
. Next, we shall verify it for
i=k
. Define

w(x,t)=\frac{1}{\tau^{k}(1-\delta)^{k}}u(\tau^{k}x,\tau^{2k}(1-\delta)^{-kq}t),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{1}.

We can readily check that
w
solves in the viscosity sense

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w=\big[(|Dw|^{2}+\hat{\varepsilon}^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+\hat{a}(x,t)(|Dw|^{2}+\hat{\varepsilon}^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{w_{i}w_{j}}{|Dw|^{2}+\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right)w_{ij}

in
Q_{1}
, where

\hat{a}(x,t)=(1-\delta)^{k(s-q)}a(\tau^{k}x,\tau^{2k}(1-\delta)^{-qk}t)\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(1-\delta)^{2k}}.

Moreover, there holds that

|Dw|\leq 1\quad\text{in }Q_{1}

and

|\{(x,t)\in Q_{1}:Dw\cdot e\leq l\}|>\mu|Q_{1}|\quad\text{for all }e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

by the induction assumption. Additionally, note that
\varepsilon\leq(1-\delta)^{k}
. Applying Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

Dw\cdot e\leq 1-\delta\quad\text{in }Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}\quad\text{for all }e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

which implies that
|Dw|\leq 1-\delta
in
Q_{\tau}^{1-\delta}
. Rescaling back, it yields that

|Du|<(1-\delta)^{k+1}\quad\text{in }Q_{\tau^{k+1}}^{(1-\delta)^{k+1}}.

We conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In order to derive the reduction of oscillation of
|Du|
, we ask that
\|Da\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
is smaller than
\kappa
(
\kappa\leq 1
sufficiently small) in Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
. In fact, we can suppose initially that
\|D_{x,t}a\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\kappa
. These two lemmas still hold, when
\|Da\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\kappa
is substituted by
\|D_{x,t}a\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\kappa
in Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
.

If the iteration above can be carried out infinitely, then we will readily conclude the Hölder continuity of
Du
at the origin
(0,0)
. Nevertheless, unless
Du(0,0)=0
, the iteration shall stop unavoidably at some step, that is, for some nonnegative integer
k
the condition (
4.10
) is not true in some direction
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. In this case, we will infer that
u
can be approximated by a linear function, then making use of a conclusion on regularity of small perturbation solutions from
[
56
]
to prove the Hölder regularity for
Du
. Now we first study how the solution is close to a linear function.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let the conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) be in force. Suppose that
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
is a smooth solution to (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
|Du|\leq M
in
Q_{1}
. Let
0\leq a(x,t)\leq a^{+}
. If for any
t\in[-1,0]
it holds that

\mathrm{osc}_{B_{1}}u(\cdot,t)\leq L

with
L
being a positive constant, then

\mathrm{osc}_{Q_{1}}u(x,t)\leq\begin{cases}CL&{\text{if }q\geq 0},\\[5.69054pt]
C(L+L^{1+q}+L^{1+s})&{\text{if }-1<q<0},\end{cases}

where
C>0
depends upon
n,p,q,s,a^{+}
and
M
.

Proof.

This proof is similar to that of Lemma 4.4 in
[
34
]
. We only give the sketch of proof here.
If
q\geq 0
, for
a_{ij}
defined in (
4.3
) we can find that

|a_{ij}|\leq\overline{\Lambda}:=\big[(1+M^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a^{+}(1+M^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\max\{1,p-1\}.

So this claim can be concluded by the same proof of Lemma 4.3 in
[
36
]
.

When
-1<q<0
, we define the comparison functions as follows

\overline{w}(x,t)=\overline{b}+\Lambda L^{1+q}t+2L|x|^{\beta},

\underline{w}(x,t)=\underline{b}-\Lambda L^{1+q}t-2L|x|^{\beta},

where
\beta=\frac{q+2}{q+1}
and
\Lambda
will be determined later. Here
\overline{b}
is chosen so that
\overline{w}(\cdot,-1)\geq u(\cdot,-1)
in
B_{1}
and
\overline{w}(\overline{x},-1)=u(\overline{x},-1)
at some point
\overline{x}\in\overline{B_{1}}
. Correspondingly,
\underline{b}
is chosen so that
\underline{w}(\cdot,-1)\leq u(\cdot,-1)
in
B_{1}
and
\underline{w}(\underline{x},-1)=u(\underline{x},-1)
at some point
\underline{x}\in\overline{B_{1}}
. Then
\overline{b}-\underline{b}\leq L+2\Lambda L^{1+q}
.
By direct computations,

\displaystyle\quad\big[(|D\overline{w}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(|D\overline{w}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\overline{w}_{i}\overline{w}_{j}}{|D\overline{w}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\overline{w}_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq\big[((2L\beta|x|^{\beta-1})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)((2L\beta|x|^{\beta-1})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]2pn\beta(\beta-1)L|x|^{\beta-2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\beta)^{q+1}(\beta-1)pn\big[1+a^{+}(1+(2L\beta)^{2})^{\frac{s-q}{2}}\big]L^{q+1}.

Hence, if we choose

\Lambda=(2\beta)^{q+1}(\beta-1)pn\big[1+a^{+}(1+(2L\beta)^{2})^{\frac{s-q}{2}}\big]+1,

then
\overline{w}
is a strict supersolution to (
3.1
). Analogously,
\underline{w}
is a strict subsolution. Next, we can prove that
\overline{w}\geq u\geq\underline{w}
in
Q_{1}
. For the details, one can see
[
34
, Lemma 4.4]
. Finally we have

\mathrm{osc}_{Q_{1}}u(x,t)\leq\overline{b}-\underline{b}+4L\leq 2\Lambda L^{q+1}+5L=:C(L+L^{1+q}+L^{1+s}).

The proof now is completed.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
0<\sigma<\frac{1}{8}
. Let the conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) be in force. Suppose that
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
is a smooth solution to (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
in
Q_{1}
. Let
0\leq a(x,t)\leq a^{+}
. If for any
t\in[-1,0]
it holds that

\mathrm{osc}_{x\in B_{1}}(u(x,t)-x\cdot e)\leq\sigma,

then one has

\mathrm{osc}_{(x,t)\in Q_{1}}(u(x,t)-x\cdot e)\leq C\sigma,

where
C>0
depends only on
n,p,q,s
and
a^{+}
.

Proof.

Denote

\overline{w}(x,t)=\overline{b}+x\cdot e+\Lambda\sigma t+2\sigma|x|^{2},

\underline{w}(x,t)=\underline{b}+x\cdot e-\Lambda\sigma t-2\sigma|x|^{2},

with
\Lambda>0
to be fixed later. Here, the choices of
\overline{b},\underline{b}
are the same as that in the proof of Lemma
4.6
. Then we get
\overline{b}-\underline{b}\leq(2\Lambda+1)\sigma
. Owing to

, by simple calculation, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\leq|D\overline{w}(x,t)|,|D\underline{w}(x,t)|\leq\frac{3}{2},\quad\text{for }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1}}.

Therefore, it follows that

a_{ij}(x,t,D\overline{w}(x,t))\leq A_{0}I

and

a_{ij}(x,t,D\underline{w}(x,t))\leq A_{0}I,

where the notation
a_{ij}
is from (
4.3
), and the constant
A_{0}
depends on
p,q,s,a^{+}
.

We next pick

and then show that

\underline{w}\leq u\leq\overline{w}

in

. For the details, we refer to
[
34
, Lemma 4.5]
. Finally, we arrive at

\mathrm{osc}_{(x,t)\in Q_{1}}(u(x,t)-x\cdot e)\leq\sup_{Q_{1}}(\overline{w}-x\cdot e)-\inf_{Q_{1}}(\underline{w}-x\cdot e)\leq\overline{b}-\underline{b}+4\sigma\leq(2\Lambda+5)\sigma.

We now finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let the assumptions (
2.1
)–(
2.3
) be in force and let
\omega

|D_{x,t}a(x,t)|\leq 1

Set

Thereby, it yields that

|\{x\in B_{1}:|Du(x,t)-e|>\varepsilon_{0}\}|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}},

(4.11)

for any

\displaystyle\quad\mathrm{osc}_{B_{1/2}}(u(x,t)-e\cdot x)

\mathrm{osc}_{B_{1}}u(\cdot,t)\leq 2

Proposition 4.9
.

. Suppose that

is a smooth solution to (
3.1
) in

. Then there are

(small) and

Note that

is also a solution to (

.
∎

Once we show that

is close to some linear function, then the Hölder regularity for

follows from the above proposition. From Remark

, we have known that
\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
is less than

(

), so in Proposition

we may replace
\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
by

so that

do not depend on

.

In the sequel, we shall give a uniformly

).

Theorem 4.10

(
A priori
Hölder gradient estimate)
.

Let the assumptions (
2.1
)–(
2.3
) be in force and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\kappa
, where
\kappa\in(0,1]
is a small constant depending on
n,p,q,s,a^{-},a^{+}
. Assume that
u
is a smooth solution of (
3.1
) satisfying
|Du|\leq 1
in
Q_{1}
. Then there are two constants
\alpha,C>0
, both of which depend on
n,p,q,s,a^{-}
and
a^{+}
, such that the following estimates hold:

|Du(x,t)-Du(y,s)|\leq C\big(|x-y|^{\alpha}+|t-s|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}\big)

and

|u(x,t)-u(x,s)|\leq C|t-s|^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}

for any
(x,t),(y,s),(x,s)\in Q_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

This proof is similar to that of Theorem 4.8 in
[
34
]
. However, for the sake of completeness and convenience, we give the details of proof here. We first demonstrate the Hölder continuity of
Du
at
(0,0)
and the Hölder continuity of
u
in
t
at (0,0). Then, by standard translation arguments, the interior Hölder regularity follows.

We choose
\omega
as the one in Proposition
4.9
with
\|D_{x,t}a(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
replaced by
1
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
. And then for this
\omega
we pick two quantities
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}>0
small so that Lemma
4.8
holds true. Now we fix

l=1-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{|Q_{1}|}.

Here we observe that if for arbitrary
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

|\{(x,t)\in Q_{1}:Du\cdot e\leq l\}|\leq\mu|Q_{1}|,

then

|\{(x,t)\in Q_{1}:|Du-e|>\varepsilon_{0}\}|\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

This estimate will be used later.

Let
\tau
and
\delta
be two positive constants coming from Corollary
4.3
. By
[\log\varepsilon/\log(1-\delta)]
, we mean the integer part of
\log\varepsilon/\log(1-\delta)
. Let
k
be either
[\log\varepsilon/\log(1-\delta)]
or the minimum nonnegative integer that makes condition (
4.10
) false, whichever is smaller. Then it follows from Corollary
4.3
that

|Du(x,t)|\leq(1-\delta)^{m}\quad\text{in }Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}

for
m=0,1,\cdots,k
. When

(x,t)\in Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}\setminus Q_{\tau^{m+1}}^{(1-\delta)^{m+1}},

we know that

|x|\geq\tau^{m+1}\quad\text{or}\quad|t|\geq(1-\delta)^{-(m+1)q}\tau^{2(m+1)}.

Thus, by taking
\alpha=\frac{\log(1-\delta)}{\log\tau}
, it yields that

|x|^{\alpha}\geq(1-\delta)^{m+1}\quad\text{or}\quad|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}\geq(1-\delta)^{(m+1)}.

Then

|Du(x,t)|\leq(1-\delta)^{m}\leq C\left(|x|^{\alpha}+|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}\right)

with
C=\frac{1}{1-\delta}
in
Q_{\tau^{m}}^{(1-\delta)^{m}}\setminus Q_{\tau^{m+1}}^{(1-\delta)^{m+1}}
. Hence, for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|\leq(1-\delta)^{k}
,

|Du(x,t)-\xi|\leq(1-\delta)^{m}+(1-\delta)^{k}\leq C\left(|x|^{\alpha}+|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha q}}\right)

in

.

Next we give some notations that will be utilized later. Denote

F_{1}(\xi,M):=|\xi|^{q}\left(\mathrm{tr}M+(p-2)\left\langle M\frac{\xi}{|\xi|},\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right\rangle\right),

\displaystyle F_{2}(x,t,\xi,M)

\displaystyle:=a(x,t)|\xi|^{s}\left(\mathrm{tr}M+(p-2)\left\langle M\frac{\xi}{|\xi|},\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A(x,t,\xi)M),

where

A(x,t,\xi):=a(x,t)|\xi|^{s}\left(I+(p-2)\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\otimes\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right)

with
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T},M\in Sym(n)
.

Let

\eta_{j}:=D_{x}\Psi_{j}=-D_{y}\Psi_{j}=j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}(x_{j}-y_{j}).

It is essential that
\eta_{j}
is nonzero, which allows us to exploit jets. Because
u
is a subsolution and
v
is a strict supersolution, we arrive at

-\partial_{s}\Psi_{j}-F_{1}(\eta_{j},Y_{j})-F_{2}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j},Y_{j})>0

and

\partial_{t}\Psi_{j}-F_{1}(\eta_{j},X_{j})-F_{2}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j},X_{j})\leq 0

by (
5.3
) and (
5.4
). Subtracting these two inequalities above, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<-\partial_{s}\Psi_{j}-\partial_{t}\Psi_{j}+F_{1}(\eta_{j},X_{j})-F_{1}(\eta_{j},Y_{j})+F_{2}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j},X_{j})-F_{2}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

(5.7)

First, notice that

-\partial_{s}\Psi_{j}=j(t_{j}-s_{j})=\partial_{t}\Psi_{j},

then we get

J_{1}=0.

Second, through the increasing monotonicity of
F_{1}(\xi,M)
with respect to the second variable
M
, and applying
X_{j}\leq Y_{j}
, we obtain

J_{2}=F_{1}(\eta_{j},X_{j})-F_{1}(\eta_{j},Y_{j})\leq 0.

In turn, we are going to estimate the third term
J_{3}
, which is the most delicate part of the proof. Since
a(x,t)>0
, the matrix
A(x,t,\xi)
is positive definite so that it has matrix square root denoted by
A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,t,\xi)
. By
A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}(x,t,\xi)
, we mean the
k
-th column of
A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,t,\xi)
. Then it yields that

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j})X_{j})-\mathrm{tr}(A(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})Y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=1}X_{j}A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j})\cdot A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j})-\sum^{n}_{k=1}Y_{j}A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})\cdot A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})

\displaystyle\leq Cj|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j})-A^{\frac{1}{2}}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cj|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}}{(\lambda_{\rm min}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j}))+\lambda_{\rm min}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})))^{2}}\|A(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j})-A(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})\|^{2}_{2},

(5.8)

where the penultimate inequality is derived by (
5.6
) and the last inequality is obtained from the local Lipschitz continuity of
M\mapsto M^{\frac{1}{2}}
(see
[
33
, page 410]
). Here
\lambda_{\rm min}(M)
stands for the smallest eigenvalue of a symmetric
n\times n
matrix
M
. Let us mention that the inequality similar to (
5
) can be found in
[
39
, page 1484]
.

We proceed with evaluating

\displaystyle\quad\|A(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j})-A(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|(a(x_{j},t_{j})-a(y_{j},s_{j}))|\eta_{j}|^{s}\left(I+(p-2)\frac{\eta_{j}}{|\eta_{j}|}\otimes\frac{\eta_{j}}{|\eta_{j}|}\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\eta_{j}|^{s}|a(x_{j},t_{j})-a(y_{j},s_{j})|(\sqrt{n}+|p-2|).

(5.9)

In addition,

\begin{split}&\lambda_{\rm min}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j}))=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm min}(A(x_{j},t_{j},\eta_{j}))\geq\min\{1,\sqrt{p-1}\}|\eta_{j}|^{\frac{s}{2}}\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})},\\
&\lambda_{\rm min}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(y_{j},s_{j},\eta_{j}))\geq\min\{1,\sqrt{p-1}\}|\eta_{j}|^{\frac{s}{2}}\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})}.\end{split}

(5.10)

Merging (
5.10
), (
5
) with (
5
), we finally have

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cj|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}(\sqrt{n}+|p-2|)^{2}}{\min\{1,p-1\}|\eta_{j}|^{s}(\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})}+\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})})^{2}}|\eta_{j}|^{2s}|a(x_{j},t_{j})-a(y_{j},s_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})}+\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})}\right)^{-2}j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}|\eta_{j}|^{s}(|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}+|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2}),

where we have used the assumption that
a(x,t)
is Lipschitz continuous in
\Omega_{T}
, which implies that
|a(x,t)-a(y,s)|\leq C\sqrt{|x-y|^{2}+|t-s|^{2}}
. Thus (
5
) becomes

0<C\left(\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})}+\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})}\right)^{-2}j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}|\eta_{j}|^{s}(|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}+|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2})=:H_{j}.

We now verify that
H_{j}
tends to 0 as
j\rightarrow\infty
, which leads to a contradiction. Next, we split the proof into two cases.

Observe that

\begin{split}u(x_{j},t_{j})-v(x_{j},t_{j})&\leq\max_{\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T)}\{u(x,t)-v(x,t)\}\\
&\leq u(x_{j},t_{j})-v(y_{j},s_{j})-\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l}-\frac{j}{2}(t_{j}-s_{j})^{2}.\end{split}

This leads to

\displaystyle\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l}+\frac{j}{2}(t_{j}-s_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq v(x_{j},t_{j})-v(y_{j},s_{j})

\displaystyle\rightarrow v(\hat{x},\hat{t})-v(\hat{x},\hat{t})=0,

(5.11)

as
j\rightarrow\infty
, where we have utilized the fact that
v
is continuous in
\Omega_{T}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\quad u(x_{j},t_{j})-v(y_{j},s_{j})-\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l}-\frac{j}{2}(t_{j}-s_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T)\times[0,T)}\left\{u(x,t)-v(y,s)-\frac{j}{l}|x-y|^{l}-\frac{j}{2}(t-s)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq u(x_{j},t_{j})-v(x_{j},s_{j})-\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-x_{j}|^{l}-\frac{j}{2}(t_{j}-s_{j})^{2},

i.e.,

v(x_{j},s_{j})-v(y_{j},s_{j})\geq\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l}.

By virtue of the uniform Lipschitz continuity of
v
in the spatial variables, we have

\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l}\leq C|x_{j}-y_{j}|

and further

\frac{j}{l}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-1}\leq C.

Hence it follows that

|\eta_{j}|\leq Cl.

If
s\geq 0
, then it follows from (
5
),
l>2
and
|\eta_{j}|\leq Cl
that

H_{j}=C\left(\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})}+\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})}\right)^{-2}|\eta_{j}|^{s}(j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l}+j|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2})\rightarrow 0

by sending
j\rightarrow\infty
.

If
-1<s<0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle H_{j}

\displaystyle=C\left(\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})}+\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})}\right)^{-2}|\eta_{j}|^{s+1}\frac{j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-2}}{j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-1}}(|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}+|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2})

\displaystyle=C\left(\sqrt{a(x_{j},t_{j})}+\sqrt{a(y_{j},s_{j})}\right)^{-2}(|\eta_{j}|^{s+1}|x_{j}-y_{j}|+|\eta_{j}|^{s+1}|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{-1}).

Due to
|\eta_{j}|
is bounded and
s+1>0
, it yields that

|\eta_{j}|^{s+1}|x_{j}-y_{j}|\rightarrow 0

by
j\rightarrow\infty
, as
x_{j},y_{j}\rightarrow\hat{x}
. Furthermore, using (
5
), we can justify the following limit,

\displaystyle|\eta_{j}|^{s+1}|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{2}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(j|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-1}|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{\frac{2}{s+1}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\frac{-1}{s+1}}\right)^{s+1}

\displaystyle=\left(j|t_{j}-s_{j}|^{\frac{2}{s+1}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{l-1-\frac{1}{s+1}}\right)^{s+1}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0

as
j\rightarrow\infty
, where we need to notice that
\frac{2}{s+1}>2
by
-1<s<0
, together with
l-1-\frac{1}{s+1}>0
by
l>\frac{s+2}{s+1}
. In conclusion, we have proved that
H_{j}
converge to 0 as
j\rightarrow\infty
. The proof now is completed.
∎

We now conclude this section with stability properties of viscosity solution.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\{u_{i}\}
be a sequence of viscosity solutions to (
3.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
with
\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0
such that
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
. Suppose that
u_{i}
converges to
u
locally uniformly in
\Omega_{T}
. Then
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
.

Proof.

We only prove that
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
1.1
). The case of subsolution then follows in a similar way. Let
\varphi\in C^{2}(Q_{1})
be such that
u-\varphi
reaches a local minimum at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{T}
and moreover
D\varphi(x,t)\neq 0
for
x\neq x_{0}
. Taking into account that
u_{i}
converges to
u
locally uniformly, we can find a sequence
\{(x_{i},t_{i})\}\subset\Omega_{T}
satisfying
(x_{i},t_{i})\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})
as
i\rightarrow\infty
, such that
u_{i}-\varphi
attains a local minimum at
(x_{i},t_{i})
. Since
u_{i}
is a viscosity supersolution to (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\partial_{t}\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})-\left[(|D\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})|^{2}+\varepsilon_{i}^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x_{i},t_{i})(|D\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})|^{2}+\varepsilon_{i}^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})+(p-2)\frac{D^{2}\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})D\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})\cdot D\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})}{|D\varphi(x_{i},t_{i})|^{2}+\varepsilon_{i}^{2}}\right).

Furthermore, on account of
(x_{i},t_{i})\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})
, we hence conclude that

0\leq\limsup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,t)\rightarrow(x_{0},t_{0})}}{{x\neq x_{0}}}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi-[|D\varphi|^{q}+a(x,t)|D\varphi|^{s}]\left(\mathrm{tr}D^{2}\varphi+(p-2)\left\langle D^{2}\varphi\frac{D\varphi}{|D\varphi|},\frac{D\varphi}{|D\varphi|}\right\rangle\right)\right),

which implies that
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
1.1
).
∎

6.
Proof of Lipschitz continuity of solutions

This section is devoted to showing the Lipschitz continuity of solutions to (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
, that is Lemma
3.1
. Our proof follows roughly the similar lines as the one in
[
2
]
. We divide the proof of Lemma
3.1
into two steps. In the first step, we will make use of Ishii-Lions’ method to infer the Hölder continuity of solutions in spatial variables. Subsequently, the Hölder continuity shall be improved into the Lipschitz continuity by employing again the Ishii-Lions’ method in the second step.

To begin with, we prove the
C^{0,\gamma}
estimates on solutions with respect to
x
-variable for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 6.1

(Local Hölder estimates)
.

Let the conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) be in force. Let
u
be a bounded viscosity solution to (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
in
Q_{1}
. Assume that
a(x,t)\geq a^{-}>0
and
a(x,t)
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
x
-variable. Then for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there is a positive constant
C
depending only on
n,p,q,s,\gamma
, such that

|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\left(1+\frac{C_{\rm lip}}{a^{-}}\right)|x-y|^{\gamma}

for all
x,y\in B_{\frac{15}{16}}
and
t\in\left(-\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^{2},0\right]
. Here
C_{\rm lip}
is the same as the one in Lemma
3.1
.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0},y_{0}\in B_{\frac{15}{16}}
and
t_{0}\in\left(-\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^{2},0\right)
. We are ready to prove that there exist two suitable constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
such that

L:=\sup_{(x,t),(y,t)\in\overline{Q_{\frac{15}{16}}}}(u(x,t)-u(y,t)-L_{1}\phi(|x-y|)-\Psi(x,y,t))\leq 0,

where

\Psi(x,y,t)=\frac{L_{2}}{2}|x-x_{0}|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|y-y_{0}|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}|t-t_{0}|^{2}

with
\phi(r):=r^{\gamma}
. Thriving for a contradiction. We suppose that
L>0
and
(\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{t})\in\overline{B_{\frac{15}{16}}}\times\overline{B_{\frac{15}{16}}}\times\left[-\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^{2},0\right]
denotes a point reaching the maximum. By
L>0
, we know that
\overline{x}\neq\overline{y}
. Choosing

L_{2}\geq\frac{32\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}}{(\min\{\mathrm{dist}((x_{0},t_{0}),\partial Q_{15/16}),\mathrm{dist}((y_{0},t_{0}),\partial Q_{15/16})\})^{2}},

we get

|\overline{x}-x_{0}|+|\overline{t}-t_{0}|\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}}{L_{2}}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{dist}((x_{0},t_{0}),\partial Q_{15/16})}{2}

and

|\overline{y}-y_{0}|+|\overline{t}-t_{0}|\leq\frac{(\mathrm{dist}(y_{0},t_{0}),\partial Q_{15/16})}{2},

so that
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in B_{15/16}
and
\overline{t}\in\left(-\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^{2},0\right)
. In addition, if
L_{1}
is large enough, we then find that

|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|\leq\left(\frac{2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}}{L_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

is sufficiently small, which is crucial and will be used later.

By Jensen-Ishii’s lemma (see
[
23
, Theorem 8.3]
), there are

(\sigma+L_{2}(\overline{t}-t_{0}),\eta_{1},X+L_{2}I)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{2,+}u(\overline{x},\overline{t}),

(\sigma,\eta_{2},Y-L_{2}I)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{2,-}u(\overline{y},\overline{t}),

where

\displaystyle\eta_{1}=L_{1}\phi^{\prime}(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}+L_{2}(\overline{x}-x_{0}),

\displaystyle\eta_{2}=L_{1}\phi^{\prime}(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}-L_{2}(\overline{y}-y_{0}).

By choosing
L_{1}\geq C(\gamma)L_{2}
large enough, there holds that

\frac{L_{1}}{2}\gamma|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-1}\leq|\eta_{1}|,|\eta_{2}|\leq 2L_{1}\gamma|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-1}.

(6.1)

By means of Jensen-Ishii’s lemma
[
22
, Theorem 12.2]
, we could take
X,Y\in Sym(n)
such that for any
\tau>0
satisfying
\tau Z<I
, it holds that

-\frac{2}{\tau}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&\\
&I\end{array}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\\
&-Y\end{array}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Z^{\tau}&-Z^{\tau}\\
-Z^{\tau}&Z^{\tau}\end{array}\right),

(6.2)

where

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=L_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\otimes\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}+\frac{L_{1}\phi^{\prime}(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\left(I-\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\otimes\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\right)

\displaystyle=L_{1}\gamma|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}\left(I+(\gamma-2)\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\otimes\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\right)

and

Z^{\tau}=(I-\tau Z)^{-1}Z.

Here
(I-\tau Z)^{-1}
stands for the inverse of the matrix
I-\tau Z
. We now pick
\tau=\frac{1}{2L_{1}\gamma|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}}
such that

Z^{\tau}=2L_{1}\gamma|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}\left(I-2\frac{2-\gamma}{3-\gamma}\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\otimes\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}\right).

Furthermore, for
\xi=\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}
we get

\langle Z^{\tau}\xi,\xi\rangle=2\gamma\frac{\gamma-1}{3-\gamma}L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}<0.

(6.3)

It follows from (
6.2
) that

X\leq Y

and

\|X\|,\|Y\|\leq 4\gamma L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}.

(6.4)

We next introduce a notation. Let

A^{\varepsilon}(\eta)=I+(p-2)\frac{\eta}{(|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\otimes\frac{\eta}{(|\eta|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

It is easy to recognize that the eigenvalues of
A^{\varepsilon}(\eta)
belong to
\left(\min\{1,p-1\},\max\{1,p-1\}\right)
. Since
u
is a viscosity solution of (
3.1
), we will obtain the following viscosity inequalities

\sigma+L_{2}(\overline{t}-t_{0})-\left[(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(\overline{x},\overline{t})(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1})(X+L_{2}I))\leq 0

and

\sigma-\left[(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(\overline{y},\overline{t})(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})(Y-L_{2}I))\geq 0.

Thus

\displaystyle\quad L_{2}(\overline{t}-t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1})(X+L_{2}I))-(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})(Y-L_{2}I))

\displaystyle\quad+a(\overline{x},\overline{t})\left[(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1})(X+L_{2}I))-(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})(Y-L_{2}I))\right]

\displaystyle\quad+(a(\overline{x},\overline{t})-a(\overline{y},\overline{t}))(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})(Y-L_{2}I))

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

(6.5)

We first evaluate
J_{3}
as

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=(a(\overline{x},\overline{t})-a(\overline{y},\overline{t}))(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}[\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})Y)-L_{2}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2}))]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm lip}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|C_{0}(s)\gamma^{s}L^{s}_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\gamma-1)s}[n\|A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})\|\|Y\|+L_{2}(n+|p-2|)]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm lip}C_{0}(s)\gamma^{s}L^{s}_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\gamma-1)s+1}[4\gamma n\max\{1,p-1\}L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}+L_{2}(n+|p-2|)],

where we have used the inequalities (
6.1
) and (
6.4
) and the fact that
a(x,t)
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
x
-variable.

We rewrite
J_{1}
as

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1})(X-Y))+(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{tr}((A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1})-A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2}))Y)

\displaystyle\quad+[(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}-(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}]\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2})Y)

\displaystyle\quad+L_{2}\left[(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1}))+(|\eta_{2}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{2}))\right]

\displaystyle=:J_{1,1}+J_{1,2}+J_{1,3}+J_{1,4}.

By (
6.1
), it is easy to get

J_{1,4}\leq 2n\gamma^{q}C(q)\max\{1,p-1\}L_{2}L^{q}_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\gamma-1)q}.

In view of (
6.2
), we know that all the eigenvalues of
X-Y
are non-positive and at least one eigenvalue denoted by
\overline{\lambda}(X-Y)
is smaller than
8\gamma\frac{\gamma-1}{3-\gamma}L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle J_{1,1}

\displaystyle\leq(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(A^{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1}))\lambda_{i}(X-Y)

\displaystyle\leq(|\eta_{1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\min\{1,p-1\}\overline{\lambda}(X-Y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(q)(\gamma L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-1})^{q}\min\{1,p-1\}8\gamma\frac{\gamma-1}{3-\gamma}L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\gamma-2}

\displaystyle=8\gamma^{1+q}C_{1}(q)\min\{1,p-1\}\frac{\gamma-1}{3-\gamma}L^{1+q}_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\gamma-1)(q+1)-1}.

To estimate
J_{1,2}

, and for all
t\in[-1,0]

F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,DW_{y},D^{2}W_{y})\leq-1\quad\text{in }B_{1}.

Proof.

Let
y\in\partial B_{1}
,
f(r)=\sqrt{(r-1)_{+}}
and
g_{y}(x)=f(|x-2y|)
. Here
(r-1)_{+}:=\max\{r-1,0\}
. By direct calculation, then for
x\in B_{1}
and
t\in[-1,0]
, we derive

\displaystyle\quad F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,Dg_{y},D^{2}g_{y})

\displaystyle=\left[(f^{\prime 2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(f^{\prime 2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]\left[\left(1+(p-2)\frac{f^{\prime 2}}{f^{\prime 2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{|x-2y|}f^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left[(f^{\prime 2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(f^{\prime 2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right](r-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{\min\{1,p-1\}}{4}(r-1)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{(r-1)^{-1}}{4}+\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}(r-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{\min\{1,p-1\}}{4}(r-1)^{-1}\right),

if we choose
r>1
sufficiently close to 1, where
r:=|x-2y|
. Therefore, there exists
\delta>0
, that depends only on
n,p,q
, such that for
x\in B_{1}\cap B_{1+\delta}(2y)
and
t\in[-1,0]
it holds that

F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,Dg_{y},D^{2}g_{y})\leq-1.

Set

h_{y}(x)=b\left(2^{\sigma}-\frac{1}{|x-2y|^{\sigma}}\right)

with

\sigma=\frac{2n}{\min\{1,p-1\}}+2\quad\text{and}\quad b>0.

Clearly,
h_{y}(x)\geq b(2^{\sigma}-1)
in
B_{1}
. Also, for
x\in B_{1}
and
t\in[-1,0]
, using the choice of
\sigma
and
1<r<3
, we get

\displaystyle\quad F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,Dh_{y},D^{2}h_{y})

\displaystyle=b\left[(b^{2}\sigma^{2}r^{-2\sigma-2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(b^{2}\sigma^{2}r^{-2\sigma-2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\left(1+\frac{(p-2)\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}b^{-2}r^{2\sigma+2}}\right)\sigma(-\sigma-1)r^{-\sigma-2}+(n-1)\sigma r^{-\sigma-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq b\left[(b^{2}\sigma^{2}r^{-2\sigma-2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(b^{2}\sigma^{2}r^{-2\sigma-2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma r^{-\sigma-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{b}{2}\sigma r^{-\sigma-2}(b^{2}\sigma^{2}r^{-2\sigma-2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}-\frac{b^{1+q}}{2}3^{-\sigma-2-q(\sigma+1)}\sigma^{1+q}&{\text{if }q\geq 0},\\[5.69054pt]
-\frac{b}{2}3^{-\sigma-2}(1+\sigma^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma&{\text{if }-1<q<0}.\end{cases}

Then we take
b<1
satisfying

b\left(2^{\sigma}-\frac{1}{|1+\delta|^{\sigma}}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}.

Thanks to
g_{y}(y)=0
and
h_{y}(y)>0
, the function

W_{y}(x)=\begin{cases}h_{y}(x)&{\text{if }x\in\overline{B_{1}},|x-2y|\geq 1+\delta},\\[5.69054pt]
\min\{g_{y}(x),h_{y}(x)\}&{\text{if }x\in\overline{B_{1}},|x-2y|\leq 1+\delta}\end{cases}

consists with
g_{y}
in a neighborhood of
y
, and, by the selection of
b
, consists with
h_{y}
when
x\in\overline{B_{1}}
and
|x-2y|\geq 1+\tilde{\delta}
for some
\tilde{\delta}\in(0,\delta)
. Furthermore,

F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,DW_{y},D^{2}W_{y})\leq-C

for
x\in B_{1}
and
t\in[-1,0]
, where
C>0
depends only upon
n,p
and
q
. We conclude this proof through multiplying a large positive constant to
W_{y}
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

For each
(y,\tau)\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}
, there is
W_{y,\tau}\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
satisfying
W_{y,\tau}(y,\tau)=0
,
W_{y,\tau}>0
in
\overline{Q_{1}}\setminus\{(y,\tau)\}
as well as

\partial_{t}W_{y,\tau}-F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,DW_{y,\tau},D^{2}W_{y,\tau})\geq 1\quad\text{in }Q_{1}.

Proof.

For
\tau\geq-1
and
y\in\partial B_{1}
, we can construct

W_{y,\tau}(x,t)=\frac{(t-\tau)^{2}}{2}+2W_{y}(x),

which is a desired function apparently. Here
W_{y}(x)
comes from Lemma
7.1
. If
\tau=-1
and
y\in B_{1}
, define

W_{y,\tau}(x,t)=B(t+1)+|x-y|^{\iota}

with

\iota=\max\left\{2,\frac{q+2}{q+1},\frac{s+2}{s+1}\right\}.

By virtue of the definition of
\iota
, we now evaluate

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{t}W_{y,\tau}-F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,DW_{y,\tau},D^{2}W_{y,\tau})

\displaystyle=B-\left[(\iota^{2}|x-y|^{2(\iota-1)}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a(x,t)(\iota^{2}|x-y|^{2(\iota-1)}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left((n-1)\iota+\iota(\iota-1)+(p-2)\frac{\iota^{2}|x-y|^{2(\iota-1)}}{\iota^{2}|x-y|^{2(\iota-1)}+\varepsilon^{2}}\iota(\iota-1)\right)|x-y|^{\iota-2}

\displaystyle\geq B-\left[(\iota^{2}|x-y|^{2(\iota-1)}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}+a^{+}(\iota^{2}|x-y|^{2(\iota-1)}+\varepsilon^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\right]\iota(n-1+p(\iota-1))|x-y|^{\iota-2}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}B-\big[(16+1)^{\frac{q}{2}}+a^{+}(16+1)^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]2(n-1+p)&{\text{if }q\geq 0},\\[5.69054pt]
B-\big[\iota^{q}|x-y|^{q(\iota-1)+\iota-2}+a^{+}\iota^{s}|x-y|^{s(\iota-1)+\iota-2}\big]\iota(n-1+p(\iota-1))&{\text{if }-1<s<0},\\[5.69054pt]
B-\big[\iota^{q}|x-y|^{q(\iota-1)+\iota-2}+a^{+}(\iota^{2}2^{2(\iota-1)}+1)^{\frac{s}{2}}\big]\iota(n-1+p(\iota-1))&{{}\text{others}},\end{cases}

which leads to

\partial_{t}W_{y,\tau}-F_{\varepsilon}(x,t,DW_{y,\tau},D^{2}W_{y,\tau})\geq B-C,

where
C>0
depends on
n,p,q,s
and
a^{+}
. From that, we can see that
W_{y,\tau}
will be a desired function as well, if we pick
B=C+1
.
∎

Recall that
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}
and
a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
with
a
and
b
being two real numbers. With Lemma
7.2
in hand, we can deduce the following result. The proof is the same as that of Theorem A.3 in
[
34
]
, so we omit it here.

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose that
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}})
is a solution to (
3.1
) with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Let
\varphi:=u\mid_{\partial_{p}Q_{1}}
and
\rho
be a modulus of continuity of
\varphi
. Then there is another modulus of continuity
\tilde{\rho}
, which depends on
n,p,q,s,a^{+}
and
\rho
, such that, for any
(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1}}
and
(y,s)\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}
,

|u(x,t)-u(y,s)|\leq\tilde{\rho}(|x-y|\vee\sqrt{|t-s|})

holds true.

As a consequence, merging Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
with Corollary
7.3
, we could arrive at the boundary estimates (Proposition
4.11
) by following the proof of Proposition 2.5 in
[
36
]
. We omit the detailed proof.

Semi-infinite quantum wells in a position-dependent mass background
C. Quesne

Abstract
By using a point canonical transformation starting from the constant-mass Schrödinger equation for the Morse potential, it is shown that a semi-infinite quantum well model with a non-rectangular profile associated with a position-dependent mass that becomes infinite for some negative value of the position, while going to a constant for a large positive value of the latter, can be easily derived. In addition, another type of semi-infinite quantum well associated with the same position-dependent mass is constructed and solved by starting from the Rosen-Morse II potential instead of the Morse one.



Keywords: Schrödinger equation; position-dependent mass; quantum well; point canonical transformation

2
The Jafarov and Nagiyev semi-infinite quantum well and its derivation by the PCT technique

In Ref.
[
29
]
, Jafarov and Nagiyev considered the PDM Schrödinger equation

\left(-\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{M(x)}\frac{d}{dx}+V_{\rm eff}(x)\right)\psi(x)=E\psi(x),

(1)

where
M(x)
and
V_{\rm eff}(x)
are defined by

M(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{a}\right)^{-2},\quad V_{\rm eff}(x)=\frac{1}{4}M(x)\omega^{2}x^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{a^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}}{(x+a)^{2}},\quad-a<x<+\infty,

(2)

with
a>0
.
1
1

1

Note that we have adopted here units wherein
\hbar=2m_{0}=1
in the original paper.
Since the mass and the potential go to
+\infty
for
x\to-a
, the definition of the latter may be completed by setting
V_{\rm eff}(x)=+\infty
for
x\leq-a
. On the other hand, for
x\to+\infty
, it is clear that
V_{\rm eff}(x)\to\frac{1}{4}\omega^{2}a^{2}
. The potential is therefore characterized by an infinite wall on the left and a finite wall on the right. Hence, it is a semi-infinite quantum well with a non-rectangular profile.

At this stage, one may observe that the BenDaniel-Duke form
[
32
]
of the kinetic energy operator adopted in (
1
) is only a special case of the von Roos general two-parameter form
[
33
]
. Other orderings of the mass and the differential operator, such as the Zhu-Kroemer
[
34
]
or the Mustafa-Mazharimousavi
[
35
,
36
]
ones, which pass the de Souza Dutra and Almeida test
[
37
]
, might have been chosen, but, as shown elsewhere
[
19
]
, in general they do not change the results much.

In Ref.
[
29
]
, the bound-state solutions of eq. (
1
) were shown to correspond to the energies

E_{n}=\omega\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{a^{2}}n(n+1),\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,N,\qquad N<\frac{1}{2}(\omega a^{2}-1).

(3)

The corresponding wavefunctions were obtained in the form

\psi_{n}(x)\propto(x+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega a^{2}+n}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega a^{3}}{2(x+a)}\right)L_{n}^{(\omega a^{2}-2n-1)}\left(\frac{\omega a^{3}}{x+a}\right),

(4)

expressed in terms of Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)
and vanishing at
x=-a
and
x\to+\infty
. This form was then transformed into

\psi_{n}(x)\propto(x+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega a^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega a^{3}}{2(x+a)}\right)y_{n}\left(2\frac{x+a}{\omega a^{3}};-\omega a^{2}\right),

(5)

where
y_{n}(z;\alpha)
denotes a Bessel polynomial. It is this latter form whose normalization coefficient was calculated and that was shown to reduce to the well-known wavefunctions of the constant-mass harmonic oscillator in the
a\to+\infty
limit. For
E>\frac{1}{4}\omega^{2}a^{2}
, eq. (
1
) also has a continuous spectrum, whose wavefunctions were briefly discussed in
[
29
]
.

These results may be alternatively obtained by using the PCT technique applied to a constant-mass Schrödinger equation
[
16
]

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{du^{2}}+U(u)\right)\phi(u)=\epsilon\phi(u)

(6)

for an appropriately chosen potential
U(u)
, defined on a finite or infinite interval. Such a method uses a change of variable

u(x)=\bar{a}v(x)+\bar{b},\qquad v(x)=\int^{x}\sqrt{M(x^{\prime})}\,dx^{\prime},

(7)

and a change of function

\phi(u(x))\propto[M(x)]^{-1/4}\psi(x).

(8)

Here,
\bar{a}
and
\bar{b}
are assumed to be two real parameters. The potential
V_{\rm eff}(x)
, defined on a possibly different interval, and the bound-state energies
E_{n}
of the PDM Schrödinger equation are given in terms of the potential
U(u)
and the bound-state energies
\epsilon_{n}
of the constant-mass one by

V_{\rm eff}(x)=\bar{a}^{2}U(u(x))+\frac{M^{\prime\prime}}{4M^{2}}-\frac{7M^{\prime 2}}{16M^{3}}+\bar{c},

(9)

and

E_{n}=\bar{a}^{2}\epsilon_{n}+\bar{c},

(10)

where a prime denotes derivation with respect to
x
and
\bar{c}
is some additional real constant. From (
8
), the corresponding bound-state wavefunctions are given by

\psi_{n}(x)=\lambda[M(x)]^{1/4}\phi_{n}(u(x))

(11)

provided they are normalizable on the definition interval of
x
. Here
\lambda
is some constant which may arise from the change of normalization when going from
u
to
x
.

For the mass chosen in (
2
), one finds that the mass-dependent term in (
9
) is given by

\frac{M^{\prime\prime}}{4M^{2}}-\frac{7M^{\prime 2}}{16M^{3}}=-\frac{1}{4a^{2}}.

(12)

and that the change of variable (
7
) writes

u(x)=\bar{a}a\log(x+a)+\bar{b}.

(13)

On assuming

\bar{a}=\frac{1}{a},\qquad\bar{b}=0,

(14)

the latter reduces to

u(x)=\log(x+a)\qquad\text{or}\qquad e^{u(x)}=x+a.

(15)

From (
15
), it looks appropriate to assume for the potential
U(u)
in the starting constant-mass Schrödinger equation (
6
) some function of the real exponential
e^{u}
. Let us consider the Morse potential
[
30
]
, which may be written as
[
31
]

U(u)=B^{2}e^{-2u}-B(2A+1)e^{-u},\qquad-\infty<u<+\infty,

(16)

where
A
and
B
are two positive constants. Its bound-state eigenvalues are given by

\epsilon_{n}=-(A-n)^{2},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,n_{\rm max},\qquad n_{\rm max}<A,

(17)

and the corresponding eigenfunctions are

\phi_{n}(u)={\cal N}_{n}\exp[-(A-n)u-Be^{-u}]L_{n}^{(2A-2n)}(2Be^{-u}),

(18)

with a normalization constant

{\cal N}_{n}=(2B)^{A-n}\left(\frac{n!(2A-2n)}{\Gamma(2A+1-n)}\right)^{1/2}.

(19)

On applying eqs. (
9
), (
12
), (
14
), and (
15
), potential (
16
) gets transformed into

V_{\rm eff}(x)=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\left(\frac{B^{2}}{(x+a)^{2}}-\frac{B(2A+1)}{x+a}\right)-\frac{1}{4a^{2}}+\bar{c},

(20)

which reduces to the potential
V_{\rm eff}(x)
given in (
2
), provided we choose

A=\frac{1}{2}(\omega a^{2}-1),\qquad B=\frac{1}{2}\omega a^{3},\qquad\bar{c}=\frac{1}{4}\omega^{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{4a^{2}}.

(21)

From (
10
) and (
17
), its bound-state energies turn out to be given by (
3
) with
N=n_{\rm max}
, as it should be. From (
11
), the corresponding wavefunctions are obtained in the form

\psi_{n}(x)=N_{n}(x+a)^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega a^{2}+n}\exp\left(-\frac{\omega a^{3}}{2(x+a)}\right)L_{n}^{(\omega a^{2}-2n-1)}\left(\frac{\omega a^{3}}{x+a}\right)

(22)

with

N_{n}=\lambda\sqrt{a}\,{\cal N}_{n}

(23)

and

{\cal N}_{n}=(\omega a^{3})^{\frac{1}{2}(\omega a^{2}-1)-n}\left(\frac{n!(\omega a^{2}-1-2n)}{\Gamma(\omega a^{2}-n)}\right)^{1/2},

(24)

coming from (
19
) and (
21
). The remaining undetermined constant
\lambda
can be easily found by making the change of variable
e^{u}=x+a
in the normalization integral for
\psi_{n}(x)
and using the known normalization coefficient
{\cal N}_{n}
of
\psi_{n}(u(x))
. The result is
\lambda=1/\sqrt{a}
, so that
N_{n}
of eq. (
22
) simply reduces to
{\cal N}_{n}
given in (
24
).

We have therefore proved that the bound states of the semi-infinite quantum well with a non-rectangular profile of
[
29
]
can be easily derived from those of the constant-mass Morse potential. Its continuous spectrum eigenfunctions might also be obtained from those of the latter.

3
Construction of another semi-infinite quantum well by the PCT technique

In the present section, we will assume the same mass
M(x)
as in (
2
), which implies that eqs. (
12
) and (
13
) remain valid. With the same choice as in (
14
), we then get eq. (
15
).

Another potential for which the constant-mass Schrödinger equation is exactly solvable and that can be written in terms of the exponential
e^{u}
is the Rosen-Morse II potential
[
38
]

U(u)=-A(A+1)\sech^{2}u+2B\tanh u,\qquad-\infty<u<+\infty,\qquad B<A^{2},

(25)

whose bound-state energies and wavefunctions are given by
[
31
]

\epsilon_{n}=-(A-n)^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{(A-n)^{2}},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,n_{\rm max},\qquad n_{\rm max}<A-\sqrt{|B|},

(26)

and

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle={\cal N}_{n}(1-\tanh u)^{\left(A-n+\frac{B}{A-n}\right)/2}(1+\tanh u)^{\left(A-n-\frac{B}{A-n}\right)/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times P_{n}^{\left(A-n+\frac{B}{A-n},A-n-\frac{B}{A-n}\right)}(\tanh u),

(27)

where
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z)
denotes a Jacobi polynomial and
{\cal N}_{n}
is a normalization coefficient given by
[
39
]

{\cal N}_{n}=2^{n-A}\left(\frac{n!\Gamma(2A-n+1)[(A-n)^{2}-B^{2}/(A-n)^{2}]}{(A-n)\Gamma(A+1+B/(A-n))\Gamma(A+1-B/(A-n))}\right)^{1/2}.

(28)

In the special case where
B=0
, eqs. (
25
)–(
28
) reduce to

U(u)=-A(A+1)\sech^{2}u,\qquad-\infty<u<+\infty,

(29)

\epsilon_{n}=-(A-n)^{2},\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,n_{\rm max},\qquad n_{\rm max}<A,

(30)

\phi_{n}(u)=\bar{{\cal N}}_{n}(\sech u)^{A-n}C_{n}^{\left(A-n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}(\tanh u),

(31)

\bar{{\cal N}}_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(2A-2n+1)}{2^{A-n}\Gamma(A-n+1)}\left(\frac{(A-n)n!}{\Gamma(2A-n+1)}\right)^{1/2},

(32)

where the Jacobi polynomials with equal parameters have been expressed in terms of Gegenbauer ones using eq. (22.5.20) of Ref.
[
40
]
and the new normalization factor
\bar{\cal N}_{n}
combines the factor occurring in the latter equation with the normalization factor (
28
).

On applying eqs. (
9
), (
12
), (
14
), and (
15
) to potential (
29
), we obtain

V_{\rm eff}(x)=V_{0}\left(\frac{1}{[(x+a)^{2}+1]^{2}}-\frac{1}{(x+a)^{2}+1}\right),\qquad-a<x<+\infty,

(33)

where we have set

\bar{c}=\frac{1}{4a^{2}},\qquad V_{0}=\frac{4A(A+1)}{a^{2}}>0.

(34)

We note that

\lim_{x\to-a}V_{\rm eff}(x)=\lim_{x\to+\infty}V_{\rm eff}(x)=0

(35)

and we complete the definition of
V_{\rm eff}(x)
by setting

V_{\rm eff}(x)=+\infty\qquad\text{for}\qquad x<-a,

(36)

which agrees with the behaviour of the mass
M(x)
. On the interval
(-a,+\infty)
, the potential is negative and has a minimum given by

V_{\rm eff}(x_{\rm min})=-\frac{V_{0}}{4}\qquad\text{for}\qquad x_{\rm min}=-a+1.

(37)

It is therefore another semi-infinite quantum well associated with the mass defined in (
2
). An example of such a potential is displayed in Fig. 1.

From eq. (
34
), it turns out that
A
may be written as

A=\nu-\frac{1}{2},\qquad\nu\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+a^{2}V_{0}},

(38)

so that on using eqs. (
10
), (
14
), and (
34
), we obtain that the bound-state spectrum of
V_{\rm eff}(x)
is given by

E_{n}=-\frac{1}{a^{2}}(\nu-n)(\nu-n-1),\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,n_{\rm max},

(39)

while the corresponding wavefunctions can be expressed as

\psi_{n}(x)=N_{n}\frac{(x+a)^{\nu-n-1}}{[(x+a)^{2}+1]^{\nu-n-\frac{1}{2}}}C_{n}^{(\nu-n)}\left(\frac{(x+a)^{2}-1}{(x+a)^{2}+1}\right),\qquad-a<x<+\infty.

(40)

Provided
n_{\rm max}
is restricted to values such that
n_{\rm max}<\nu-1
, these wavefunctions vanish for
x\to-a
and
x\to\infty
, as it should be. Their normalization coefficient is given by

N_{n}=\lambda\sqrt{a}2^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}-n}\bar{\cal N}_{n},

(41)

where the remaining undetermined constant
\lambda
can be obtained by making the change of variable
e^{u}=x+a
in the normalization integral of
\psi_{n}(x)
on
(-a,+\infty)
. The result reads
\lambda=1/\sqrt{a}
, so that

N_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(2\nu-2n)}{\Gamma(\nu-n-\frac{1}{2})}\left(\frac{n!}{(\nu-n-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(2\nu-n)}\right)^{1/2}.

(42)

(44)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{3}\frac{(x+1)^{1/2}}{[(x+1)^{2}+1]^{3}}[(x+1)^{4}-3(x+1)^{2}+1],

(45)

corresponding to the energies
E_{0}=-\frac{35}{4}
,
E_{1}=-\frac{15}{4}
, and
E_{2}=-\frac{3}{4}
, respectively.

4
Conclusion

In the present paper, we have first shown that the PCT method applied to the constant-mass Schrödinger equation for the Morse potential allows us to easily retrieve some of the results of Ref.
[
29
]
. In addition, we have proved that normalized bound-state wavefunctions can be directly expressed in terms of Laguerre polynomials, without having to transform them in terms of Bessel polynomials.

In a second step, we have derived another example of semi-infinite quantum well by starting from the Rosen-Morse II potential with
B=0
and applying the same technique.

Considering some other exactly solvable potentials with constant mass as starting potential with the PDM defined in (
2
) may be an interesting topic for future investigation.

The Vertical Recursive Relation of Riordan Arrays and Their Matrix Representation
Tian-Xiao He

Abstract
A vertical recursive relation approach to Riordan arrays is induced, while the horizontal recursive relation is represented by A A - and Z Z -sequences. This vertical recursive approach gives a way to represent the entries of a Riordan array ( g , f ) (g,f) in terms of a recursive linear combinations of the coefficients of g g . A matrix representation of the vertical recursive relation is also given. The set of all those matrices forms a group, called the quasi-Riordan group. The extensions of the horizontal recursive relation and the vertical recursive relation in terms of c c - and C C - Riordan arrays are defined with illustrations by using the rook triangle and the Laguerre triangle. Those extensions represent a way to study nonlinear recursive relations of the entries of some triangular matrices from linear recursive relations of the entries of Riordan arrays. In addition, the matrix representation of the vertical recursive relation of Riordan arrays provides transforms between lower order and high order finite Riordan arrays, where the m m th order Riordan array is defined by ( g , f ) m = ( d n , k ) m ≥ n , k ≥ 0 (g,f)_{m}=(d_{n,k})_{m\geq n,k\geq 0} . Furthermore, the vertical relation approach to Riordan arrays provides a unified approach to construct identities. AMS Subject Classification: 05A15, 05A05, 15B36, 15A06, 05A19, 11B83. Key Words and Phrases: Riordan array, Riordan group, quasi-Riordan array, vertical recursive relation, A A -sequence, Z Z -sequence, rook triangle, Laguerre triangle, Fuss-Catalan numbers and matrix, Catalan numbers and matrix, ( c ) (c) -Riordan arrays, ( C ) (C) -Riordan arrays.

A vertical recursive relation view of Riordan arrays

In a Riordan array
(g,f)=(d_{n,k})_{n,k\geq 0}

0

d_{n,k}=[t^{n}]gf^{k}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}f_{j}[t^{n-j}]gf^{k-1}=\sum^{n-k+1}_{j=1}f_{j}d_{n-j,k-1}

(12)

\displaystyle d_{1,1}=f_{1}d_{0,0},

\displaystyle d_{2.1}=f_{2}d_{0,0}+f_{1}d_{1,0},\,\,d_{2,2}=f_{1}d_{1,1},

\displaystyle d_{3,1}=f_{3}d_{0,0}+f_{2}d_{1,0}+f_{1}d_{2.0},\,\,d_{3,2}=f_{2}d_{2,1}+f_{1}d_{1,1},\,\,d_{3,3}=f_{1}d_{2,2},\ldots

For the Pascal triangle

In general, consider a board that represents a full or a part chess board. Let

C

m\geq 2
. If
m=1

for
k\geq 2
, and

. Hence, the coefficient
r_{n,k}
of the roof polynomial

is referred to as the number of ways in which
k

full chess board such that no two rooks capture each other. The rook polynomials for
1\times 1

,
3\times 3
,
4\times 4
, and
5\times 5
full boards are, respectively,

\displaystyle r_{1}(x)=x+1,

\displaystyle r_{2}(x)=2x^{2}+4x^{1}+1,

\displaystyle r_{3}(x)=6x^{3}+18x^{2}+9x+1,

\displaystyle r_{4}(x)=24x^{4}+96x^{3}+72x^{2}+16x+1,

\displaystyle r_{5}(x)=120x^{5}+600x^{4}+600x^{3}+200x^{2}+5x+1.

In a given board C, suppose we choose a particular square denoted as
(\ast)
. Let
D
denote the board obtained from
C
by deleting the row and column containing the square
(\ast)
, and let
E
be the board obtained from
C
by deleting only the square
(\ast)

for the board

is given by

.

Denote the

n\times n

\displaystyle r(E_{n},x)=

\displaystyle r(C_{n+1},x)-xr(C_{n},x)=r_{n+1}(x)-xr_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n+1}_{k=0}\frac{(n+1)!}{k!}\binom{n+1}{k}x^{n+1-k}-x\sum^{n}_{k=0}\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n}{k}x^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n+1}_{k=0}\frac{n!}{k!}\left((n+1)\binom{n+1}{k}-\binom{n}{k}\right)x^{n+1-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{k=0}\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{n-k+1}-1\right)x^{n+1-k}+1

(39)

Let

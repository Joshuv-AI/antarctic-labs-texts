A Study on degenerate Whitney numbers of the first and second kinds of Dowling lattices
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM

Abstract
. Dowling constructed Dowling lattice Q n ​ ( G ) Q_{n}(G) , for any finite set with n n elements and any finite multiplicative group G G of order m m , which is a finite geometric lattice. He also defined the Whitney numbers of the first and second kinds for any finite geometric lattice. These numbers for the Dowling lattice Q n ​ ( G ) Q_{n}(G) are the Whitney numbers of the first kind V m ​ ( n , k ) V_{m}(n,k) and those of the second kind W m ​ ( n , k ) W_{m}(n,k) , which are given by Stirling number-like relations. In this paper, by ‘degenerating’ such relations we introduce the degenerate Whitney numbers of the first kind and those of the second kind and investigate, among other things, generating functions, recurrence relations and various explicit expressions for them. As further generalizations of the degenerate Whitney numbers of both kinds, we also consider the degenerate r r -Whitney numbers of both kinds.

1.
Introduction

Let
(L,\leq)
be a finite lattice. This means that it is a finite poset such that every pair
x,y
of elements in
L
has a supremum
x\vee y
and an infimum
x\wedge y
. It has a greatest element
\hat{1}
and a least element
\hat{0}
.
L
is said to satisfy the chain condition if all maximal chains in any interval
[x,y]=\left\{z\in L|x\leq z\leq y\right\}
have the same length. If
L
satisfies the chain condition, then the rank
\rho(x)
of an element
x\in L
is the length of a maximal chain in
[\hat{0},x]
. The rank of a finite lattice
L
is the maximum of lengths of all chains in
L
. An atom of a finite lattice
L
is an element of rank 1, and
L
is said to be atomic if every element of
L
is the join of some atoms. A finite lattice
L
is semimodular if
x\vee y
covers both
x
and
y
whenever both
x
and
y
cover
x\wedge y
. Here, for
x,y\in L
,
y
is said to cover
x
iff
x<y
and
[x,y]=\left\{x,y\right\}
. For more details on lattices, one refers to [24].

A finite lattice
L
is geometric if it is a finite semimodular lattice which is also atomic. Dowling [8] constructed an important finite geometric lattice
Q_{n}(G)
out of a finite set of
n
elements and a finite group
G
of order
m
, called Dowling lattice of rank
n
over a finite group of order
m
. Let
X
be a finite set of
n
elements and let
G
be a finite multiplicative group of order
m
. Let
Q_{n}^{\prime}(G)
be the set of all partial
G
-partitions of
X
. Here a partial
G
-partition
\alpha=\left\{a_{j}:A_{j}\rightarrow G|j=1,2,\dots,r\right\}
is a collection of functions where
A_{j}
’s are nonempty disjoint subsets of
X
with
\cup_{j=1}^{r}A_{j}\subseteq X.
Let
\beta=\left\{b_{l}:B_{l}\rightarrow G|l=1,2,\dots,s\right\}
be another partial
G
-partition of
X
. Then we write
\alpha\leq\beta
if, for every
b_{l}\in\beta
, there exist a nonempty subset
\alpha_{l}
of
\alpha
and elements
g_{j}\in G
such that
b_{l}=\sum_{a_{j}\in\alpha_{l}}g_{j}a_{j}
. This means that
B_{l}=\cup_{a_{j}\in\alpha_{l}}A_{j}
, and the restriction of
b_{l}
to
A_{j}
is given by
g_{j}a_{j}
, namely
b_{l}(x_{i})=g_{j}a_{j}(x_{i})
, for all
x_{i}\in A_{j}
. Then
\leq
defines a preorder on
Q_{n}^{\prime}(G)
, so that the order relation, defined by
\alpha\sim\beta
iff
\alpha\leq\beta
and
\beta\leq\alpha
, gives an equivalence relation on
Q_{n}^{\prime}(G)
. Now, the Dowling lattice
Q_{n}(G)
of rank
n
over a finite group of order
m
is the quotient poset
Q_{n}^{\prime}(G)/\sim
, with the order relation
[\alpha]\leq[\beta]
iff
\alpha\leq\beta
, where
[\alpha]
denotes the equivalence class containing
\alpha
. Then it was shown that
Q_{n}(G)
is a finite geometric lattice of rank
n
(see [8,Theorem 3]) which satisfies the chain condition (see [8 ,Theorem 1]). We let the interested reader refer to [8] for the details on the construction of
Q_{n}(G)
and its many fascinating properties.

For a finite geometric lattice
L
of rank
n
, Dowling [8] defined the Whitney numbers
V_{L}(n,k)
of the first kind by

V(n,k)=V_{L}(n,k)=\sum_{\rho(x)=n-k}\mu(\hat{0},x),

and the Whitney numbers
W_{L}(n,k)
of the second kind by

W(n,k)=W_{L}(n,k)=\sum_{\rho(x)=n-k}1,

where
\rho
is the rank function and
\mu:L\times L\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is the Möbius function given by

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)=0,\quad\mathrm{for\,\,all}\,\,x\nleq y\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,L,

\displaystyle\mu(x,x)=1,\quad\mathrm{for\,\,all}\,\,x\in L,

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)=-\sum_{x\leq z<y}\mu(x,z),\quad\mathrm{for\,\,all}\,\,x<y\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,L.

If
L
is the Dowling lattice
Q_{n}(G)
of rank
n
over a finite group
G
of order
m
, then the Whitney numbers of the first kind
V_{Q_{n}(G)}(n,k)
and the Whitney numbers of the second kind
W_{Q_{n}(G)}(n,k)
are respectively denoted by
V_{m}(n,k)
and
W_{m}(n,k)
. These notations are justified, since the Whitney numbers of both kinds depend only on the order
m
of
G
.
In Corollary 6.1 of [8], it was shown that, for any fixed group
G
of order
m
, the Whitney numbers
V_{m}(n,k)
and
W_{m}(n,k)
satisfy the following Stirling number-like relations:

(1)

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)m^{k}\bigg(\frac{x-1}{m}\bigg)_{k},

(2)

\displaystyle m^{n}\bigg(\frac{x-1}{m}\bigg)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m}(n,k)x^{k},

where
(x)_{0}=1
,
(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1)
.

Equivalently, the relations (
1
) and (
2
) are respectively given by

(3)

\displaystyle(mx+1)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},

(4)

\displaystyle m^{n}(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m}(n,k)(mx+1)^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,4,8]).

Either from (
1
) and (
2
) or from (
3
) and (
4
), it is evident that the Whitney numbers satisfy the orthogonality realtions.

The aim of this paper is to introduce the degenerate Whitney numbers
V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)
of the first kind and the degenerate Whitney numbers
W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)
of the second kind as degenerate versions of the Whitney numbers of both kinds of Dowling lattice
Q_{n}(G)
of rank
n
over a finite group
G
of order
m
and investigate some combinatorial properties of those numbers, including generating functions, recurrence relations and various explicit expressions. As further generalizations of the degenerate Whitney numbers of both kinds, the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the first kind
V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
and those of the second
W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
are studied and their connections with the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers are observed. As immediate applications, it is expected that these numbers or their polynomial extensions will appear in the expressions of the probability distributions of appropriate random variables. Here we remark that the study of degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials, which was initiated by Carlitz in [6,7], regained the interests of some mathematicians and yielded many interesting results. For some of these, one refers to [1,11-19,22 and references therein]. Here we would like to mention only two things. In [12], starting from the question that what if we replace the usual exponential function appearing in the generating functions of Sheffer sequences by the degenerate exponential function in (
12
), we were led to introduce the notions of degenerate Sheffer sequences and
\lambda
-Sheffer sequences. Also, it is worthwhile to note that studying degenerate versions can be done not only for polynomials but also for transcendental functions like gamma functions (see [15]).

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall some definitions, including Dowling polynomials, Tanny-Dowling polynomials, Stirling numbers of both kinds and Bell polynomials. And then we recall degenerate exponential functions, degenerate logarithms, degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds, degenerate Bell polynomials and higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials. Section 2 is the main results of this paper. In Section 2, we introduce the degenerate Whitney numbers of the second kind
W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)
(see (
22
)) and its polynomial extensions, namely the degenerate Dowling polynomials
D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)
(see (
27
)), and derive, among other things, their generating functions and explicit expressions for them when
m=1
, and their recurrence relations. In addition, we deduce various explicit expressions for the degenerate Whitney numbers of the second kind and a Dobinski-like formula for the degenerate Dowling polynomials. Then we introduce the degenerate Whitney numbers of the first kind
V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)
by considering the inversion formula of (
32
). We derive the generating function, a recurrence relation and various explicit expressions for them. Further, we introduce degenerate Tanny-Dowling polynomials and get the generating function of them. In Section 3, as further generalizations of the degenerate Whitney numbers of both kinds, we introduce the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers
W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
of the second kind (see (
69
)) and the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers
V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
of the first kind (see (
68
)), for any positive integer
r
and derive their generating functions. Moreover, for
m=1
, we note the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the second kind and those of the first kind are respectively given by the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind and those of the second kind. In the final Section 4, we get expressions for the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler numbers in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds.

For
n\geq 0
, Dowling polynomials are defined by

(5)

D_{m}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,4]).

When
x=1
,
D_{m}(n)=D_{m}(n,1)
are called the Dowling numbers.

As is known, Tanny-Dowling polynomials are given by

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{m}(n,x)\

\displaystyle=\ \int_{0}^{+\infty}D_{m}(n,\mathcal{E}x)e^{-\mathcal{E}}d\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}k!W_{m}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4]).

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(7)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,2,5,9,10,21]),

and the Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(8)

x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,5,9,21-23]).

The Bell polynomials are defined by

(9)

\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

The Fubini polynomials are given by

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}\ =\ \int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\mathcal{E}x(e^{t}-1)}e^{-\mathcal{E}}d\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(\mathcal{E}x)e^{-\mathcal{E}}d\mathcal{E}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
9
), we get

(11)

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!S_{2}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,13]).

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential functions are defined by

(12)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)\ =\ (1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}\ =\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}{t^{n}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,13-15]),

where
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda)
,
(n\geq 1)
.

When
x=1
, we denote
e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
simply by
e_{\lambda}(t)
.
Let
\log_{\lambda}(t)
be the compositional inverse of
e_{\lambda}(t)
, called the degenerate logarithms. Then we have

(13)

\log_{\lambda}t=\frac{1}{\lambda}(t^{\lambda}-1),\quad\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]).

In [11], the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(14)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)(x)_{l,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

As an inversion formula of (
14
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(15)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]).

The degenerate Bell polynomials are introduced by Kim-Kim-Dolgy as

(16)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

Thus, we note that

(17)

\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

When
x=1
, we denote
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(1)
simply by
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}
.

From (
14
) and (
15
), we note that

(18)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(19)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]).

In [6,7], Carlitz considered the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials of order
r
and the degenerate Euler polynomials of order
r
, respectively given by

(20)

\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{r}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(21)

\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{r}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=B_{n}^{(r)}(x)\displaystyle
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x)=E_{n}^{(r)}(x)
, where
B_{n}^{(r)}(x)
are the Bernoulli polynomials of order
r
, and
E_{n}^{(r)}(x)
are the Euler polynomials of order
r
.
When
r=1
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x)=\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)
, and
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)
are respectively called the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and the degenerate Euler polynomials. For
x=0
, we let
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(r)},\ \mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(0)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(r)},\ \beta_{n,\lambda}(0)=\beta_{n,\lambda}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(0)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}
.

2.
degenerate Whitney numbers of Dowling lattices

In light of (
3
), we consider the degenerate Whitney numbers of the second kind given by

(22)

(mx+1)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, we observe that

(23)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{mx+1}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(mx+1)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)m^{k}(x)_{k}.

On the other hand,

(24)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{mx+1}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ e_{\lambda}(t)\cdot e_{\lambda}^{mx}(t)\ =\ e_{\lambda}(t)\cdot\big(e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1+1\big)^{x}

\displaystyle=\ e_{\lambda}(t)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1\big)^{k}(x)_{k}.

Therefore, by (
23
) and (
24
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
k\geq 0
, we have

e_{\lambda}(t)\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1}{m}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In [11], we easily get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k+1}\bigg)\ =\ \frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}(t)\frac{1}{1+\lambda t}.

Thus, we note that

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\big(1+\lambda t\big)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\big(S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)+\lambda nS_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From Theorem 1 and (
25
), we have

(26)

W_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)+\lambda nS_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1),

where
n,k\geq 0
with
n\geq k
.

Corollary 2
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with
n\geq k
, we have

W_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)+\lambda nS_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1).

In view of (
5
), we define the degenerate Dowling polynomials as

(27)

D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

When
x=1
,
D_{m,\lambda}(n,1)=D_{m,\lambda}(n)
are called the degenerate Dowling numbers.

Then, by Theorem 1 and (
27
), we get

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ e_{\lambda}(t)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1}{m}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ e_{\lambda}(t)e^{x(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1}{m})}.

Therefore, we obtain the generating function of degenerate Dowling polynomials.

Theorem 3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

e_{\lambda}(t)e^{x(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1}{m})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
16
), we can easily derive the following equation

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \frac{d}{dt}e^{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}\ =\ \frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, we have

(30)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}(x)=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Here we note that (
30
) corresponds to the fact that
S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,0)=0
, for
n\geq 0
.

Using (
26
), we have

(31)

\displaystyle D_{1,\lambda}(n)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\big\{S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)+n\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1)\big\}

\displaystyle=\ \mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}+n\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\ =\ \mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}+n\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)

\displaystyle=\ \mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}+n\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}.

Corollary 4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{1,\lambda}(n)=\mathrm{Bel}_{n+1,\lambda}+n\lambda\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}.

As an inversion formula of (
22
), we define the degenerate Whitney numbers of the first kind as

(32)

m^{n}(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)(mx+1)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
32
), we note that

(33)

\displaystyle(1+mt)^{x}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n}m^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)(mx+1)_{k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)(mx+1)_{k,\lambda}.

On the other hand, with the help of (
13
) we have

(34)

\displaystyle(1+mt)^{x}=(1+mt)^{x+\frac{1}{m}}(1+mt)^{-\frac{1}{m}}=(1+mt)^{\frac{mx+1}{m}}(1+mt)^{-\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle\ =\ e_{\lambda}^{mx+1}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+mt)^{\frac{1}{m}}\big)(1+mt)^{-\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}(1+mt)^{\frac{1}{m}}\bigg)^{k}(1+mt)^{-\frac{1}{m}}(mx+1)_{k,\lambda}.

From (
12
), we note that

e_{m}(t)=(1+mt)^{\frac{1}{m}},\quad e_{m}^{-1}(t)=(1+mt)^{-\frac{1}{m}}.

Therefore, by (
33
) and (
34
), we obtain the generating function of the degenerate Whitney numbers of the first kind.

Theorem 5
.

For
k\geq 0
, we have

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}e_{m}(t)\big)^{k}e_{m}^{-1}(t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}V_{m,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
22
), we get

(35)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)m^{k}(x)_{k}\ =\ (mx+1)_{n,\lambda}\ =\ (mx+1)_{n-1,\lambda}\big(mx+1-\lambda(n-1)\big)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k)m^{k}(x)_{k}\big(m(x-k)+mk+1-\lambda(n-1)\big)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k)m^{k+1}(x)_{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k)\big(mk+1-\lambda(n-1)\big)m^{k}(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\big\{W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k-1)+(mk+1)W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k)-\lambda(n-1)W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k)\big\}m^{k}(x)_{k}.

Therefore, by (
35
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with
n\geq k
, we have

W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)=W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k-1)+(mk+1)W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k)-\lambda(n-1)W_{m,\lambda}(n-1,k).

From (
32
), we have

(36)

\displaystyle\ =\ m^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)\bigg(\frac{-x-1}{m}\bigg)_{k,\frac{\lambda}{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{n-k}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)(-1)^{k}\langle x+1\rangle_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{n-k}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\langle x\rangle_{i,\lambda}\langle 1\rangle_{k-i,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=i}^{n}(-1)^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}m^{n-k}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)\langle 1\rangle_{k-i,\lambda}\bigg)(-1)^{i}\langle x\rangle_{i,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
58
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 19
.

For
0\leq i\leq n
, we have

V_{m,\lambda}(n,i)=\sum_{k=i}^{n}(-1)^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}m^{n-k}S_{1,\frac{\lambda}{m}}(n,k)\langle 1\rangle_{k-i,\lambda}.

From (
55
), we note that

(59)

\displaystyle m^{n}\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg)_{n}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}(n,i)(x+1)_{i,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}(n,i)\sum_{k=0}^{i}\binom{i}{k}(1)_{i-k,\lambda}(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{i}{k}V_{m,\lambda}(n,i)(1)_{i-k,\lambda}\bigg)(x)_{k,\lambda}.

On the other hand,

(60)

\displaystyle m^{n}\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg)_{n}\

\displaystyle=\ m^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda/m}(n,k)\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg)_{k,\frac{\lambda}{m}}

\displaystyle=\ m^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda/m}(n,k)\frac{1}{m^{k}}(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{n-k}S_{1,\lambda/m}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
59
) and (
60
), we obtain the inversion formula of Theorem 19.

Theorem 20
.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

m^{n-k}S_{1,\lambda/m}(n,k)=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}V_{m,\lambda}(n,i)(1)_{i-k,\lambda.}

For
0\leq k\leq n
, by Theorem 12, we get

(61)

\displaystyle W_{m+1,\lambda}(n,k)\ =\ \frac{1}{k!(m+1)^{k}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}\big(l(m+1)+1\big)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{(m+1)^{n-k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}\bigg(l+\frac{1}{m+1}\bigg)_{n,\frac{\lambda}{m+1}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{(m+1)^{n-k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}\bigg(l+\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{m(m+1)}\bigg)_{n,\frac{\lambda}{m+1}}

\displaystyle=\ (m+1)^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(-1)^{k-l}}{(m+1)^{k}{k!}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\bigg(l+\frac{1}{m}\bigg)_{n-j,\frac{\lambda}{m+1}}\bigg(\frac{-1}{m(m+1)}\bigg)_{j,\frac{\lambda}{m+1}}

\displaystyle=\ (m+1)^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(-1)^{k-l}}{(m+1)^{k}k!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\frac{(lm+1)_{n-j,\frac{m}{m+1}\lambda}}{m^{n-j}}\bigg(\frac{-1}{m(m+1)}\bigg)_{j,\frac{\lambda}{m+1}}

\displaystyle=\ (m+1)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\frac{m^{k}}{(m+1)^{k}m^{n-j}}\bigg(-\frac{1}{m(m+1)}\bigg)_{j,\frac{\lambda}{m+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\frac{1}{k!m^{k}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}(lm+1)_{n-j,\frac{m}{m+1}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ (m+1)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\frac{m^{k}}{(m+1)^{k}m^{n-j}}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{(m(m+1))^{j}}\langle 1\rangle_{j,m\lambda}W_{m,\frac{m}{m+1}\lambda}(n-j,k)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{(m+1)^{k}m^{n-k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}W_{m,\frac{m}{m+1}\lambda}(j,k).

Therefore, by (
61
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 21
.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

W_{m+1,\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{(m+1)^{k}m^{n-k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}W_{m,\frac{m}{m+1}\lambda}(j,k).

From (
27
) and Theorem 21, we can derive the following equation:

(62)

\displaystyle D_{m+1,\lambda}(n,x)\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m+1,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{k}m^{n-k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}W_{m,\lambda}(j,k)\bigg)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(j,k)\bigg(\frac{m}{m+1}x\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}\bigg(j,\frac{m}{m+1}x\bigg).

Here
W_{m,\lambda}(j,k)=0
, if
k>j
.

Therefore, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 22
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{m+1,\lambda}(n,x)=\frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}\bigg(j,\frac{m}{m+1}x\bigg).

Remark.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{m+1,\lambda}(n,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!W_{m+1,\lambda}(n,x)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg(\frac{k!}{(m+1)^{k}m^{n-k}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}W_{m,\lambda}(j,k)\bigg)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!W_{m,\lambda}(j,k)\bigg(\frac{m}{m+1}x\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{m^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{n-j}(m+1)^{j}\langle 1\rangle_{n-j,m\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{m,\lambda}\bigg(j,\frac{m}{m+1}x\bigg).

Now, we observe from Theorem 13 that

(63)

\displaystyle D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}W_{m,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}m^{i-k}(1)_{n-i,\lambda}S_{2,\lambda/m}(i,k)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}m^{i}(1)_{n-i,\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{i}S_{2,\lambda/m}(i,k)\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}m^{i}(1)_{n-i,\lambda}\mathrm{Bel}_{i,\lambda/m}\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg).

Therefore, by (
63
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 23
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{m,\lambda}(n,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}m^{i}(1)_{n-i,\lambda}\mathrm{Bel}_{i,\lambda/m}\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg).

From the definition of
\lambda
-falling factorial sequences, we have

(64)

\displaystyle(t)_{n,\lambda}\

\displaystyle=\ (t-1+1)_{n,\lambda}\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}(t-1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)_{k-j,\lambda}(t)_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}(t)_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}(-1)^{k-j}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}\binom{k}{j}\bigg)(t)_{j,\lambda}.

In the same way, we also have

(65)

\displaystyle(t)_{n,\lambda}\

\displaystyle=\ (t+1-1)_{n,\lambda}\ =\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}(t+1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(1)_{k-j,\lambda}(t)_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}(1)_{k-j,\lambda}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}(-1)^{n-k}\bigg)(t)_{j,\lambda}.

Thus, from (
64
) and (
65
) we have the following lemma.

Lemma 24
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}=\delta_{n,j}=\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}(1)_{k-j,\lambda}.

Suppose that
\displaystyle a_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}b_{k,\lambda}\displaystyle
. Then, by Lemma 24, we have

(66)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}a_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(1)_{k-j,\lambda}b_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{n-k}(1)_{k-j,\lambda}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\bigg)b_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ b_{n,\lambda}.

Conversely, we assume that
\displaystyle b_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}a_{k,\lambda}
. Then, by Lemma 24, we note that

(67)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}b_{k,\lambda}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}a_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=j}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\bigg)a_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ a_{n,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
66
) and (
67
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 25
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

a_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(1)_{n-k,\lambda}b_{k,\lambda}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ b_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}a_{k,\lambda}.

From Theorem 23 and Theorem 25, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 26
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

m^{n}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda/m}\bigg(\frac{x}{m}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}D_{m,\lambda}(k,x).

3.
Degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of Dowling lattices

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of the first kind
V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
and those of the second
W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)
, respectively defined by

(68)

m^{n}(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(mx+r)_{k,\lambda},

and

(69)

(mx+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

We remark that
r
-Whitney numbers and their applications were studied by several authors (see [3,4,20]). Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=V_{m}^{(r)}(n,k)\displaystyle
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=W_{m}^{(r)}(n,k)\displaystyle
are respectively the
r
-Whitney numbers of the first kind and those of the second kind.
From (
68
), we note that

(70)

\displaystyle(1+mt)^{x}\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n}m^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(mx+r)_{k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)(mx+r)_{k,\lambda}.

On the other hand,

(71)

\displaystyle(1+mt)^{x}\

\displaystyle=\ (1+mt)^{x+\frac{r}{m}}(1+mt)^{-\frac{r}{m}}\ =\ (1+mt)^{\frac{mx+r}{m}}(1+mt)^{-\frac{r}{m}}

\displaystyle=\ e_{\lambda}^{mx+r}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+mt)^{\frac{1}{m}}\big)(1+mt)^{-\frac{r}{m}}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}(1+mt)^{\frac{1}{m}}\Big)^{k}(1+mt)^{-\frac{r}{m}}\bigg)(mx+r)_{k,\lambda}.

By (
70
) and (
71
), we get the generating function of the degenerate
r
-Whitney numbers of first kind given by

(72)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}e_{m}(t)\Big)^{k}e_{m}^{-r}(t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}V_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
k
is a nonnegative integer.

From (
69
), we have

(73)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{mx+r}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(mx+r)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\ =\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}m^{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)(x)_{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}m^{k}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)(x)_{k}.

On the other hand,

(74)

e_{\lambda}^{mx+r}=e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\big(e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1+1\big)^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\bigg)(x)_{k}.

By (
73
) and (
74
), we get

(75)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}^{m}(t)-1}{m}\bigg)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}W_{m,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

It is known that the unsigned degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(76)

\langle x+r\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]),

where
\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+n-1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

From (
76
), we have

(77)

(1-t)^{-r}\frac{1}{k!}\big(-\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
k
is a nonnegative integer (see [18]).

By (
77
) and (
72
), we easily get

(78)

\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}V_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=(1+t)^{-r}\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
78
), we have

(79)

V_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)=(-1)^{n-k}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda},\quad V_{1,\lambda}^{(0)}=S_{1,\lambda}(n,k),\quad V_{m,\lambda}^{(1)}(n,k)=V_{m,\lambda}(n,k).

In
[
18
]
, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(80)

(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
80
), we can easily derive the generating function of the degenerate
r
-Stirling number of the second kind given by

(81)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
k
is a nonnegative integer (see
[
15
]
).

From (
75
) and (
81
), we have

W_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n,k)={n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda},\quad W_{1,\lambda}^{(0)}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),\quad W_{m,\lambda}^{(1)}(n,k)=W_{m,\lambda}(n,k).

4.
Further remarks

From (
20
), we have

(82)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\

\displaystyle=\ \bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{k!}{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\big((e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)+1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{k!}{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{l}}{l!}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{k!}{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l+k,k)\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{l+k}}{(l+k)!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l+k,k)\frac{l!k!}{(l+k)!}\frac{1}{l!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l+k,k)\frac{1}{\binom{l+k}{l}}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{l+k}{l}^{-1}S_{1,\lambda}(l+k,k)S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
21
), we obtain

(83)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\

\displaystyle=\ \bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{\alpha}\ =\ \bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}{2}+1\bigg)^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\binom{\alpha+l-1}{l}\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}{2}\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{l}\binom{\alpha+l+1}{l}l!\frac{1}{l!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{l}\binom{\alpha+l+1}{l}l!\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{l}\binom{\alpha+l-1}{l}l!S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We summarize our results in (
82
) and (
83
) as a theorem.

Theorem 27
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k)}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{l+k}{k}^{-1}S_{1,\lambda}(l+k,k)S_{2,\lambda}(n,l),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{l}\binom{\alpha+l-1}{l}l!S_{2,\lambda}(n,l).

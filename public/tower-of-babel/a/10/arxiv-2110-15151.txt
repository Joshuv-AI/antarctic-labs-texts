Haar averaged moments of correlation functions and OTOCs in Floquet systems
Ewan McCulloch

Abstract
Scrambling and thermalisation are topics of intense study in both condensed matter and high energy physics. Random unitary dynamics form a simple testing-ground for our theoretical understanding of these processes. In this work, we derive exact expressions for the large q q limiting behaviour of a selection of n n -point correlation functions, out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs), and their moments. In the process we find a general principle that breaks OTOCs into small and easy to calculate pieces, and which can likely be deployed in a more general context.

Correlators and contours

In this manuscript, an
n
-point correlation function is given by

\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle=\langle Z(t_{1})\cdots Z(t_{n})\rangle=\frac{1}{q}\Tr[Z(t_{1})\cdots Z(t_{n})],

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{t}=\left(t_{1},\cdots,t_{N}\right)

\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})=Z(t_{1})\cdots Z(t_{n})

n

U

\mathcal{U}(q)

unitary matrices), and where no consecutive times are equal (including the first and final times, which are consecutive due to the cyclic property of the trace). Two correlators with times
\boldsymbol{t}
and
\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime}
are identical if the two sequences
\boldsymbol{t}
and
\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime}
equal up to a cyclic permutation. Therefore, without loss of generality, we assume that
t_{1}\leq t_{i}
for all

A correlator
\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle
is called contour-ordered for a contour
\mathcal{C}
if the sequence of times
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})
, is contour ordered on
\mathcal{C}
. An example is given below for contours with only a single forward and backward segment and for contours with two forward and backward segments. The later is the type of contour that out-of-time-ordered (OTO) correlators live on, and hence is referred to as a contour of OTO type. The former is referred to as a contour of time-ordered (TO) type.

Figure 1:
Contour ordering for TO type contours and OTO type contours.

Correlators that are contour ordered for a TO (OTO) type contour are referred to as TO (OTO) correlators. In the following, we assume that the number of instances
\mathcal{N}_{U}
of the Haar random unitary
U
appearing in an expression to be averaged does not scale with the dimension
q
and find large
q
asymptotics. For TO correlators, this simply means that
(\max(t_{i})-\min(t_{i}))
is finite and fixed while we probe large
q
. For OTO correlators, the number of instance of
U
is bounded above by
2(\max(t_{i})-\min(t_{i}))
.

4
Higher moments

In this section we generalise the analysis in section
3
and determine bounds on the scaling behaviour (of Haar average) of the products of
p
correlators.

Theorem 2
.

The Haar average of a product of
p
correlators
\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{1})\rangle\cdots\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{p})\rangle
, with differing consecutive times
t^{(i)}_{j}\neq t^{(i)}_{j-1},\ t^{(i)}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}

\int dU\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{1})\rangle\cdots\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{p})\rangle=\order{1/q^{2\lfloor p/2 \rfloor}}\quad\textrm{as $q\to\infty$}.

(19)

Proof.

The Haar average of a product of correlation functions,
\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{1})\rangle\cdots\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{p})\rangle
, is written

\int dU\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{1})\rangle\cdots\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{p})\rangle=\int dU\prod^{p}_{i=1}\langle Z(t^{(i)}_{1})\cdots Z(t^{(i)}_{n_{i}})\rangle.

(20)

As before, define the differences
x^{(i)}_{j}=t^{(i)}_{j+1}-t^{(i)}_{j}
with
x^{(i)}_{n_{i}}=t^{(i)}_{1}-t^{(i)}_{n_{i}}
such that
x^{(i)}_{j}\neq 0
and
\sum_{j}x^{(i)}_{j}=0
. The number of instances of

appearing in the product of

. Using the same trick as in section

(representing the Haar integral over an auxiliary unitary as a weighted sum over permutations), we find

\int dU\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{1})\rangle\cdots\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{p})\rangle=\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{M}}\frac{Wg(q,\tau\sigma^{-1})}{q^{p}}H(\tau)\int dU\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\includegraphics[height]{Manuscript/Diagrams/Weingarten_formula_trace_diagrams3-NEW}}

where

and the trace diagram
H(\tau)
is as in Eq.
8
, but with

legs in total. We define the permutation

\pi_{i}=(M_{i-1}+n_{i},M_{i-1}+1,M_{i-1}+2,\cdots,M_{i-1}+n_{i}-1)

Product of two correlation functions

In the previous sections we have found the bounds on the scaling behaviour of Haar averaged correlators and products of correlators as
q\to\infty
. In this section we present results that also predict the proportionality constants for the Haar average of a product of two correlators.

Theorem 3
.

The Haar average of a product of ATO’s
\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle
and
\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})\rangle^{*}
, where
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})
and
\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime}=(t^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,t_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime})

where
\delta^{\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime},\alpha(\boldsymbol{t})}=1
if the sequences of times
\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime}
and
\alpha(\boldsymbol{t})
are equal up to a global time translation and where
\alpha(\boldsymbol{t})
is the cyclic permutation
\alpha
of the sequence
\boldsymbol{t}
. The delta constraint is zero otherwise.

This result can be equivalently states as

\int dU\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})\rangle^{*}=\frac{S(\boldsymbol{t})}{q^{2}}\tilde{\delta}^{\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{t}}+\order{1/q^3},

(25)

\tilde{\delta}^{\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{t}}

\boldsymbol{t}

is a symmetry factor that counts the number of cyclic symmetries

\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle

, while for OTO correlators

An equivalent representation of the product of correlators is given by

\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})\rangle^{*}=\langle\prod_{i}(ZU^{x_{i}})\rangle\langle\prod_{j}(ZU^{y_{j}})\rangle^{*}

. Similarly,

and the edge case

. By assumption, all consecutive times differ so that all
x_{i}\neq 0
and
y_{j}\neq 0
. By using the left and right invariance of the Haar measure once again, we write the Haar averaged expression as

\int dU\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t})\rangle\langle\mathcal{Z}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\prime})\rangle^{*}=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{\sigma,\tau\in S_{N}}Wg(\sigma\tau^{-1})\tilde{G}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\sigma)H(\tau),

(27)

\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
,
\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n^{\prime}})

is zero unless
N
is even, in the remainder of this proof we will assume this is so. This proof differs from the previous proofs in that we choose not to shift the permutation
\sigma

\pi

\tilde{G}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\sigma)

\tilde{G}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\sigma)=\int dU\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\includegraphics[height]{Manuscript/Diagrams/prod_corr_G_trace_diagram.png}}.

(28)

Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated a selection of
n
-point correlation functions with random unitary Floquet dynamics, finding the large
q
scaling behaviour (of the Haar average) of these correlators and their higher moments. For the second moment we have found the precise proportionality constant, given by the degree of cyclic symmetry of the correlator. We then gave an exact expression for the large
q
behaviour of a class of OTOCs that prove important in circuit averaging problems in random Floquet circuits
[
33
]
. In doing so, we developed a diagrammatic scheme in which leading order contributions are easily identified and evaluated. Using this methodology, one could generalise this further by relaxing the restrictions on the form of OTOCs considered and by investigating correlators with different time-orderings.

1.
Main results

Let
E=BP\langle n\rangle
denote the Johnson-Wilson spectrum (
[
5
]
) localized at a prime
p
, which satisfies that

E_{*}=\pi_{*}(E)={\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]
, with
|v_{i}|=2(p^{i}-1)
. Our motivating example is the case
p=2
,
n=1
, when
E
is the spectrum
ku
for connective complex
K
-theory, localized at 2. Our main result is an isomorphism between certain
E
-cohomology groups and the Pontryagin dual of
E
-homology groups. We require that
E_{*}(X)
is locally finite, which means that for each
i
, the
E_{*}
-module generated by
E_{i}(X)
is finite. If
M
is an
R
-module, we denote by
M^{\vee}
the right
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
. Localized at
p
, we prefer to write
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
as
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\infty}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
E=BP\langle n\rangle
and
E_{*}(X)
is locally finite,
there is an isomorphism of right
E_{*}
-modules

E^{*}(X)\approx(E_{*}(\Sigma^{D+n+1}X))^{\vee},

where
D=\sum|v_{i}|=2((p^{n+1}-1)/(p-1)-(n+1))
.

We prove this result using a Universal Coefficient Theorem and the following algebraic result, which we prove in Section
2
. If
M
is a graded module,
\Sigma^{D}M
denotes the graded module obtained from
M
by increasing gradings by
D
. Our Ext groups are in the category of graded modules, and the second superscript refers to the grading.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R={\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
|x_{i}|
positive integers, and let
D=\sum|x_{i}|
. If
M
is a locally finite graded
R
-module, there is an isomorphism of graded right
R
-modules

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{s}(M,R)\approx\begin{cases}\Sigma^{D}M^{\vee}&s=n+1\\
0&s\neq n+1.\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By
[
7
, Corollary, p.257]
, if
E
is an
A_{\infty}
ring spectrum, there is a Universal Coefficient spectral sequence

\operatorname{Ext}_{E_{*}}^{s,t}(E_{*}X,E_{*})\Rightarrow E^{s+t}X.

By
[
1
, Corollary 3.5]
,
BP\langle n\rangle
is an
A_{\infty}
ring spectrum. By Theorem
1.2
with
R=E_{*}
, the spectral sequence must collapse, as it is confined to a single value of
s
, and the
E_{\infty}
groups are as claimed.

In Section
3
, we illustrate Theorem
1.1
for a portion of
ku_{*}(K_{2})
with
K_{2}=K({\mathbb{Z}}/2,2))
, localized at 2. Here we state the application of Theorem
1.1
to this case as a corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

There is an isomorphism of right
ku_{*}
-modules
ku^{*}(K_{2})\approx(ku_{*}(\Sigma^{4}K_{2}))^{\vee}
.

Observe also that the case
n=0
of Theorem
1.1
is the usual Universal Coefficient Theorem when
H_{*}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)})
is finite.

After a version of this paper was placed on the
arXiv
, John Greenlees pointed out to the author that Theorem
1.1
could apparently be deduced using concepts of duality in stable homotopy theory, and quickly prepared a short manuscript (
[
4
]
) which did so, at least when
n\leq 2
. A result using Brown-Comenetz duality (
[
6
, Corollary 9.3]
) is closely related. We feel that the elementary nature of our presentation lends worth to our paper.

The author is grateful to Andy Baker, John Greenlees, Andrey Lazarev, Doug Ravenel, Chuck Weibel, and Steve Wilson for helpful suggestions.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The following result is certainly well-known. Here
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic integers.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
R={\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
|x_{i}|
positive integers, and let
D=\sum|x_{i}|
. In the category of graded
R
-modules

(2.2)

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{s,t}({\mathbb{Z}}/p,R)=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}/p&(s,t)=(n+1,D)\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

(2.3)

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{s}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\infty},R)=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&(s,t)=(n+1,D)\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
be the chain complex
C_{1}\to C_{0}
with
C_{1}
and
C_{0}
free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}
-modules of rank 1 and grading 0 with generators
g_{0}
and
\iota_{0}
, respectively, and
d(g_{0})=p\iota_{0}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the chain complex
C_{i,1}\to C_{i,0}
with
C_{i,1}
and
C_{i,0}
free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}[x_{i}]
-modules of rank 1 with generators
g_{i}
and
\iota_{i}
, respectively, and
d(g_{i})=x_{i}\iota_{i}
. Here
|\iota_{i}|=0
and
|g_{i}|=|x_{i}|
. Then
{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathcal{C}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}_{n}
is a chain complex of free
R
-modules with
H_{j}({\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{Z}}/p
for
j=0
, and 0 for
j>0
, by the Künneth Theorem. Thus
{\mathbb{C}}
is an
R
-resolution of
{\mathbb{Z}}/p
. Hence
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{s}({\mathbb{Z}}/p,R)
is the
s^{\text{th}}
cohomology group of the dual complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}({\mathbb{C}},R)
, which is the tensor product,
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{*}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}_{n}^{*}
, of the dual complexes. The cohomology group is nonzero only when
s=n+1
, where it is
{\mathbb{Z}}/p
, dual to
g_{0}\otimes g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n}
, in grading
D
.

For the second result, we replace
\mathcal{C}_{0}
by a chain complex
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
which has
C_{1}^{\prime}
and
C_{0}^{\prime}
free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}
-modules with generators indexed by positive integers,
g^{\prime}_{j}
and
\iota^{\prime}_{j}
, respectively, with
d(g^{\prime}_{j})=\iota^{\prime}_{j}-p\iota^{\prime}_{j+1}
. Then
H_{0}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})={\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\infty}
is the nonzero homology group, and
H^{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is the nonzero cohomology group. The rest of the proof follows as in the previous paragraph.

Delta Characters in positive characteristic and Galois Representations
Sudip Pandit, Arnab Saha

Abstract
. In this article we develop the theory of δ \delta -characters of Anderson modules. Given any Anderson module E E (satisfying certain conditions), using the theory of δ \delta -geometry, we construct a canonical z z -isocrystal 𝐇 δ ​ ( E ) {\mathbf{H}_{\delta}(E)} with a Hodge-Pink structure. As an application, we show that when E E is a Drinfeld module, our constructed z z -isocrystal 𝐇 δ ​ ( E ) {\mathbf{H}_{\delta}(E)} is weakly admissible given that a δ \delta -parameter is non-zero. Therefore the equal characteristic analogue of the Fontaine functor associates a local shtuka and hence a crystalline z z -adic Galois representation to the δ \delta -geometric object 𝐇 δ ​ ( E ) {\mathbf{H}_{\delta}(E)} . It is also well known that there is a natural local shtuka attached to E E . In the case of Carlitz modules, we show that the Galois representation associated to 𝐇 δ ​ ( E ) {\mathbf{H}_{\delta}(E)} is indeed the usual one coming from the Tate module. Hence this article further raises the question of how the above two apparently different Galois representations compare with each other for a Drinfeld module of arbitrary rank.

Introduction

The theory of arithmetic jet spaces, developed by Buium, is inspired by
the differential algebra theory over function fields. In differential algebra,
let
K
be a function field
with a fixed derivation
\partial
on it. For any
K
-algebra
B
we denote

{D}_{n}(B)
the ring of truncated polynomials of degree
n
with
coefficients in
B
. Then
{D}_{n}(B)
naturally inherits a
K
-algebra
structure by the Hasse-Schmidt differentiation map
[
22
]
.
When the fixed derivation
\partial

on
K
is non-zero, the
K
-algebra structure on
{D}_{n}(B)
described
as above is not the usual scalar multiplication.
Given a (group) scheme
G
defined over
K
with fixed
derivation
\partial
on it, one defines the
n
-th jet space functor
J^{n}G

whose

B
-points are
G({D}_{n}(B))
. Then the functor

J^{n}G
is representable by a (group)
scheme and
\{J^{n}G\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
form a system of schemes which is known
as the
canonical prolongation sequence
associated to
G
. In fact, if the
fixed derivation
\partial
on
R
is zero, then
J^{1}G
is the tangent
bundle of
G
.

The global sections
\mathcal{O}(J^{n}G)
can be thought of as the ring of the
n
-th
order differential functions of
G
. For instance, if

G
is an elliptic curve,
then one can show that there always exists a differential function
\Theta\in\mathcal{O}(J^{2}G)
which is also a morphism of group schemes from
J^{2}G
to the
additive group scheme
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. Such a
\Theta
is called a differential
character and is also known as Manin character. As for example, if
G
is given
by the Legendre equation
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-t)
defined over the function field
K=\mathbb{C}(t)
with derivation
\partial=\frac{d}{dt}
on it, then

\Theta(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime})=\frac{y}{2(x-t)^{2}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left[2t(t-1)\frac{x^{\prime}}{y}\right]+2t(t-1)x^{\prime}\frac{y^{\prime}}{y^{2}}

where
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime}
are local coordinates of
J^{2}G
. The existence of
such a
\Theta
is due to the Picard-Fuchs equation. Given a
K
-rational
point
P\in G(K)
, using the derivation
\partial
, we can consider its
canonical lift
\nabla:G(K)\rightarrow J^{2}G(K)
given by
\nabla(x,y)=(x,y,\partial x,\partial y,\partial^{2}x,\partial^{2}y)
. However

\nabla
is only a map at the level of
K
-points and not of schemes.
The composition
\Theta\circ\nabla:G(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}(K)
is then
a group homomorphism, and hence the torsion points of
G(K)
are in the
kernel of the map since
\mathbb{G}_{a}(K)
is torsion free. Such a character
\Theta

was applied by Manin to prove the Lang-Mordell conjecture for abelian varieties
over function fields
[
28
]
. Later, Buium gave an independent proof
by using other methods but still using the Manin map
[
10
]
.

The theory of arithmetic jet spaces over number rings, as
developed by Buium, also respects similar
lines. Here the notion of derivation on the base ring is replaced by a

\pi
-derivation
\delta
which naturally arises from the
\pi
-typical Witt
vectors. The analogy of
\pi
-derivations with the usual derivations is as
follows:
note that giving a derivation on a ring

is equivalent to giving a ring
homomorphism
B\rightarrow{D}_{1}(B)
. In the same essence, giving a
\pi
-derivation
on a ring

is equivalent to giving a ring map

where

is the ring of
\pi
-typical Witt vectors of length 2. As for example,
if

-adically completed

and

-th power Frobenius endomorphism of

. Such a

-derivation

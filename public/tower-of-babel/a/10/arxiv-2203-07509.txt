Rank Two Approximations of 2 × 2 × 2 2\times 2\times 2 Tensors over ℝ \mathbb{R}
David Warren Katz

Abstract
We provide a coordinate-free proof that real 2 × 2 × 2 2\times 2\times 2 rank three tensors do not have optimal rank two approximations with respect to the Frobenius norm. This result was first proved in [ 1 , Thm. 8.1] by considering the GL ​ ( V 1 ) × GL ​ ( V 2 ) × GL ​ ( V 3 ) {\text{GL}(V^{1})\times\text{GL}(V^{2})\times\text{GL}(V^{3})} orbit classes of V 1 ⊗ V 2 ⊗ V 3 {V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}} and the 2 × 2 × 2 2\times 2\times 2 hyperdeterminant. Our coordinate-free proof expands on the result in [ 1 ] by developing a proof method that can be generalized more readily to higher dimensional n 1 × n 2 × n 3 n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3} tensor spaces.

Contraction Maps of Tensors

Let
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
be finite
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
, and
n_{3}
-dimensional real vector spaces, and let
V^{1*}
,
V^{2*}
, and
V^{3*}
denote their dual spaces. The tensor
{\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v^{1}_{i}\otimes v^{2}_{i}\otimes v^{3}_{i}\in V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}}
induces the three linear maps

\displaystyle V^{1*}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\Pi_{1}(\tau)}V^{2}\otimes V^{3}

\displaystyle V^{2*}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\Pi_{2}(\tau)}V^{1}\otimes V^{3}

\displaystyle V^{3*}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\Pi_{3}(\tau)}V^{1}\otimes V^{2}

\displaystyle v^{1*}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ v^{1*}(v^{1}_{i})\ v^{2}_{i}\otimes v^{3}_{i},

\displaystyle v^{2*}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ v^{2*}(v^{2}_{i})\ v^{1}_{i}\otimes v^{3}_{i},

and

\displaystyle v^{3*}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ v^{3*}(v^{3}_{i})\ v^{1}_{i}\otimes v^{2}_{i}.

These maps are called the mode-
1
, mode-
2
, and mode-
3
contraction maps of
\tau
, respectively.

Theorem 1
.

The mode-
i
contraction map
\Pi_{i}
is well-defined.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\sum_{t=1}^{r}v^{1}_{t}\otimes v^{2}_{t}\otimes v^{3}_{t}}

{=\sum_{t=1}^{s}w^{1}_{t}\otimes w^{2}_{t}\otimes w^{3}_{t}}
for vectors
v^{i}_{t}
,
w^{i}_{t}

\in V^{i}
. We need to show that

\Pi_{i}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}v^{1}_{t}\otimes v^{2}_{t}\otimes v^{3}_{t}\right)(v^{i*})=\Pi_{i}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{s}w^{1}_{t}\otimes w^{2}_{t}\otimes w^{3}_{t}\right)(v^{i*})

for every
v^{i*}\in V^{i*}
for
i=1,2
and
3
. Choose bases
\{e^{i}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{n_{i}}
of
V^{i}
with corresponding dual bases
\{e^{i*}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{n_{i}}
for each
i
. It is sufficient to show that

\Pi_{1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}v^{1}_{t}\otimes v^{2}_{t}\otimes v^{3}_{t}\right)(e^{1*}_{u})=\Pi_{1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{s}w^{1}_{t}\otimes w^{2}_{t}\otimes w^{3}_{t}\right)(e^{1*}_{u})

for each
e^{1*}_{u}
in our dual basis. Let
a^{i}_{t,u}
and
b^{i}_{t,u}
be scalars such that
{v^{i}_{t}=\sum_{u=1}^{n_{i}}a^{i}_{t,u}e^{i}_{u}}
and
{w^{i}_{t}=\sum_{u=1}^{n_{i}}b^{i}_{t,u}e^{i}_{u}}
for all
i
and
t
. It follows that
\sum_{t=1}^{r}v^{1}_{t}\otimes v^{2}_{t}\otimes v^{3}_{t}=\sum_{t,i,j,k=1}^{r,n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}a^{1}_{ti}a^{2}_{tj}a^{3}_{tk}\ e^{1}_{i}\otimes e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}
, which must equal
\sum_{t=1}^{s}w^{1}_{t}\otimes w^{2}_{t}\otimes w^{3}_{t}=\sum_{t,i,j,k=1}^{s,n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}b^{1}_{ti}b^{2}_{tj}b^{3}_{tk}\ e^{1}_{i}\otimes e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}
, so
\sum_{t=1}^{r}a^{1}_{ti}a^{2}_{tj}a^{3}_{tk}

=\sum_{t=1}^{s}b^{1}_{ti}b^{2}_{tj}b^{3}_{tk}
for all
i,j,k
. Hence,

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}v^{1}_{t}\otimes v^{2}_{t}\otimes v^{3}_{t}\right)(e^{1*}_{u})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t,j,k=1}^{r,n_{2},n_{3}}a^{1}_{tu}a^{2}_{tj}a^{3}_{tk}\ e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{2},n_{3}}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}a^{1}_{tu}a^{2}_{tj}a^{3}_{tk}\right)\ e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{2},n_{3}}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{s}b^{1}_{tu}b^{2}_{tj}b^{3}_{tk}\right)\ e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t,j,k=1}^{s,n_{2},n_{3}}b^{1}_{tu}b^{2}_{tj}b^{3}_{tk}\ e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{s}w^{1}_{t}\otimes w^{2}_{t}\otimes w^{3}_{t}\right)(e^{1*}_{u}).

∎

The relationship between the contraction maps of a tensor generalizes, in a coordinate-free way, the fundamental relationship between the rows and columns of a matrix to hypermatrices. The rank of a two-fold tensor
\tau\in V^{1}\otimes V^{2}
is equal to the dimension of the image of
\Pi_{1}(\tau)
, which is also equal to the dimension of the image of
\Pi_{2}(\tau)
. We now use contraction maps to show when tensors in the form (
1
) are rank three. First, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

Let
V^{1}
and
V^{2}
be finite dimensional real vector spaces, let
\{x_{i}^{1}\}_{i=1}^{r}
and
\{y^{1}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
be linearly independent subsets of
V^{1}
, and let
\{x^{2}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
and
\{y^{2}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
be linearly independent subsets of
V^{2}
. If
{\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{1}\otimes x_{i}^{2}}={\sum_{i=1}^{s}y_{i}^{1}\otimes y_{i}^{2}}
, then
r=s
.

Proof.

Since
\{x_{i}^{1}\}_{i=1}^{r}
is independent, we can choose
\{x_{j}^{1*}\}_{j=1}^{r}\subseteq V^{1*}
such that
x^{1*}_{j}(x_{i}^{1})=\delta_{ij}
. By taking the mode-
1
contraction of the tensor with respect to each of the two representations, it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ x^{1*}_{j}(x_{i}^{1})\ x_{i}^{2}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{s}x^{1*}_{j}(y^{1}_{i})\ y^{2}_{i}\ \text{ for }j=1,2,\dots,r.

This implies
x^{2}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}x^{1*}_{j}(y^{1}_{i})\ y^{2}_{i}
for
j=1,2,\dots,r
, so each
x^{2}_{j}\in\langle y^{2}_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{s}
. Thus, we conclude that the linear space
\langle x^{2}_{j}\rangle_{j=1}^{r}
is a subset of
\langle y^{2}_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{s}
. Similarly,
\langle y^{2}_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{s}\subseteq\langle x^{2}_{j}\rangle_{j=1}^{r}
, so by the independence of each set,
{r=s}
. ∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
be finite dimensional real vector spaces. If the sets
\{x^{1}_{1},x^{1}_{2}\}
,
\{x^{2}_{1},x^{2}_{2}\}
, and
\{x^{3}_{1},x^{3}_{2}\}
in
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
, respectively, are linearly independent, then the tensor

\displaystyle\beta\ =\ x^{1}_{2}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x_{1}^{3}\ +\ x_{1}^{1}\otimes x_{2}^{2}\otimes x_{1}^{3}\ +\ x_{1}^{1}\otimes x_{1}^{2}\otimes x_{2}^{3}

is rank three.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
\beta
were rank less than three. That is, suppose that

\displaystyle\beta\ =\ y^{1}_{1}\otimes y^{2}_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{3}\ +\ y_{2}^{1}\otimes y_{2}^{2}\otimes y_{2}^{3}

for some vectors
y^{i}_{j}
. Since
\im\Pi_{i}(\beta)=\langle x^{i}_{1},x^{i}_{2}\rangle=\langle y^{i}_{1},y^{i}_{2}\rangle
for each
i
, it follows that the sets
\{y^{i}_{1},y^{i}_{2}\}
are independent for each
i
. Let
\{x^{1*}_{1},x^{1*}_{2}\}
be the dual basis of the basis
\{x^{1}_{1},x^{1}_{2}\}
of the subspace
\langle x^{1}_{1},x^{1}_{2}\rangle
of
V^{1}
. By considering the mode-
1
contraction of both representations of
\beta
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}(\beta)(x^{1*}_{2})=x^{1*}_{2}(x^{1}_{2})x^{2}_{1}\otimes x_{1}^{3}\ +\ x^{1*}_{2}(x_{1}^{1})x_{2}^{2}\otimes x_{1}^{3}\ +\ x^{1*}_{2}(x_{1}^{1})x_{1}^{2}\otimes x_{2}^{3}\ =\ x^{1*}_{2}(y^{1}_{1})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y_{1}^{3}\ +\ x^{1*}_{2}(y_{2}^{1})y_{2}^{2}\otimes y_{2}^{3}.

This implies that

\displaystyle x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}=x^{1*}_{2}(y^{1}_{1})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1*}_{2}(y_{2}^{1})y^{2}_{2}\otimes y^{3}_{2}.

Since the sets
\{y^{i}_{1},y^{i}_{2}\}
are independent, Lemma
2
implies that either
x^{1*}_{2}(y^{1}_{1})=0
or
x^{1*}_{2}(y_{2}^{1})=0
. We consider the case when
x^{1*}_{2}(y_{2}^{1})=0
, and leave the remaining similar case to the reader. It follows that

\displaystyle\im\Pi_{2}(\ \Pi_{1}(\beta)(x^{1*}_{2})\ )=\im\Pi_{2}(x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1})=\im\Pi_{2}(\ x^{1*}_{2}(y^{1}_{1})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{1}\ ),

which implies that
\langle x^{2}_{1}\rangle=\langle y^{2}_{1}\rangle
. Let
k
be a scalar such that
y^{2}_{1}=kx^{2}_{1}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\im\Pi_{1}(\ \Pi_{1}(\beta)(x^{1*}_{2})\ )=\im\Pi_{1}(x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1})=\im\Pi_{1}(\ x^{1*}_{2}(y^{1}_{1})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{1}\ ),

so
\langle x^{3}_{1}\rangle=\langle y^{3}_{1}\rangle
. Finally, we derive a contradiction by considering the mode-
2
contraction of both representations of
\beta
.

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}(\beta)(x^{2*}_{2})=\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}

\displaystyle\ =\ x^{2*}_{2}(y^{2}_{1})y^{1}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{2*}_{2}(y^{2}_{2})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ x^{2*}_{2}(kx^{2}_{1})y^{1}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{2*}_{2}(y^{2}_{2})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ x^{2*}_{2}(y^{2}_{2})y^{2}_{1}\otimes y^{3}_{2}.

However, this implies that
y^{3}_{2}\in\langle x^{3}_{1}\rangle
. This is a contradiction, since we have already shown that
y^{3}_{1}\in\langle x^{3}_{1}\rangle
and the set
\{y^{3}_{1},y^{3}_{2}\}
is independent. We leave it to the reader to check the similar case of
x^{1*}_{2}(y^{1}_{1})=0
.
∎

We have now shown that when
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
are finite dimensional real vector spaces and the sets

\displaystyle\beta=\ x^{1}_{2}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x_{1}^{3}\ +\ x_{1}^{1}\otimes x_{2}^{2}\otimes x_{1}^{3}\ +\ x_{1}^{1}\otimes x_{1}^{2}\otimes x_{2}^{3}

is rank three. However, it is also the limit of the sequence of rank at most two tensors

\tau_{n}=\ n\ (x_{1}^{1}+\frac{1}{n}x_{2}^{1})\otimes(x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}x_{2}^{2})\otimes(x_{1}^{3}+\frac{1}{n}x_{2}^{3})\ -\ nx_{1}^{1}\otimes x_{1}^{2}\otimes x_{1}^{3},\ n\in\mathbb{N}

in the norm topology. Hence, such tensors do not have optimal rank two approximations. We next characterize optimal rank two

approximations geometrically, and show that, in fact,
\it{all}
rank three

A Characterization of Optimal Rank Two Approximations

The set of simple tensors is a variety, and it is called the Segre variety. It is the image of the map

\displaystyle V^{1}\times V^{2}\times V^{3}\

\displaystyle\rightarrow\ V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}

\displaystyle v^{1}\times v^{2}\times v^{3}\

\displaystyle\mapsto\ v^{1}\otimes v^{2}\otimes v^{3}.

The Segre variety of the
n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}
tensor space
{V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}}
is denoted
X^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
or simply
X
when the dimensions are clear from context. The tangent space of the Segre variety at a point
\upsilon
is denoted as
T_{\upsilon}(X)
, and is characterized in
[
3
]
as in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
be finite
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
,
n_{3}
-dimensional real vector spaces, respectively, and let
x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}
be a rank one tensor in the Segre variety
X
. The tangent space of
X
at
x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}
is the space of all tensors in the form

\displaystyle x^{1}_{2}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{2}\otimes x^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{2}

(19)

for some

,
x^{2}_{2}\in V^{2}
, and
x^{3}_{2}\in V^{3}
.

Tensors in the form of (
19
) are precisely the tensors that we previously showed were limit points of the sequence (
) of tensors of rank at most two. This gives us geometric insight into the tensor rank-jumping phenomenon. Secant lines of the Segre variety contain rank at most two tensors, and tangent lines are limits of secant lines. Thus, rank three tensors on lines tangent to the Segre variety are limit points of rank at most two tensors.

The

If
\upsilon

must be orthogonal to the tangent space of

at

Theorem 6
.

Let
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and

be finite

,
n_{2}
,

-dimensional real vector spaces, respectively, and let

be a rank two tensor in

. The tangent space of
\sigma_{2}(X)
at
\upsilon
is the space of all tensors in the form

\displaystyle x^{1}_{3}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{3}\otimes x^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{3}\ +\ x^{1}_{4}\otimes x^{2}_{2}\otimes x^{3}_{2}\ +\ x^{1}_{2}\otimes x^{2}_{4}\otimes x^{3}_{2}\ +\ x^{1}_{2}\otimes x^{2}_{2}\otimes x^{3}_{4}

(20)

for some

,
x^{2}_{3},x^{2}_{4}\in V^{2}
, and

.

Until now, we have worked with

tensors. The next theorem, however, is our first statement that must be restricted to

tensors.

Theorem 7
.

Let
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
be
2
-dimensional real vector spaces, and let
\tau\in{V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}}
be of rank greater than two. If
\upsilon\in{V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}}
is an optimal rank two approximation of
\tau
with respect to an inner product norm, then
\im\Pi_{i}(\upsilon)
is not dimension two for some

or

.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
\im\Pi_{i}(\upsilon)

and

It follows that the set

is a basis of
{V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}}

c_{ijk}
such that
\tau=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2,2,2}c_{ijk}\ x^{1}_{i}\otimes x^{2}_{j}\otimes x^{3}_{k}
. By multilinearity,

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=(c_{111}x^{1}_{1}+c_{211}x^{1}_{2})\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes c_{121}x^{2}_{2}\otimes x^{3}_{1}\ +\ x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{1}\otimes c_{112}x^{3}_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\ c_{122}x^{1}_{1}\otimes x^{2}_{2}\otimes x^{3}_{2}\ +\ x^{1}_{2}\otimes c_{212}x^{2}_{1}\otimes x^{3}_{2}\ +\ x^{1}_{2}\otimes x^{2}_{2}\otimes(c_{221}x^{3}_{1}+c_{222}x^{3}_{2}).

\upsilon
by hypothesis.
∎

P-Norms and Optimal Rank Two Approximations

Once bases of vector spaces
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
are chosen, we can define explicit norms on the tensor space
{V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}}
. Choose basis
\{e^{i}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{i}}
of
V^{i}
for
i=1
,
2
, and
3
, and denote the corresponding dual basis as
\{e^{i*}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{i}}
also for
i=

1
,
2
, and
3
. Let
B
denote the collection of these bases. For
\tau\in V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}
, we define the following class of norms for any positive integer
p
.

\|\tau\|_{B,p}\ =\ \left(\sum_{i,j,k=1,1,1}^{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}|a_{ijk}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\tau=\sum_{i,j,k}a_{ijk}\ e^{1}_{i}\otimes e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}
. Similarly, for
\kappa\in V^{r}\otimes V^{s}
and
v^{r}\in V^{r}
for
1\leq r,s\leq 3
, we define

\displaystyle\|\kappa\|_{B,p}\ =\ \left(\sum_{i,j=1,1}^{n_{r},n_{s}}|a_{ij}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\text{ and }\ \|v^{r}\|_{B,p}\ =\ \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{r}}|a_{i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
{\kappa=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\ e^{r}_{i}\otimes e^{s}_{j}}
and
{v^{r}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\ e^{r}_{i}}
. These norms are a convenient choice for working in tensor spaces as they work well with contraction maps.

Theorem 8
.

Let
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
be finite
n_{1}
,
n_{2}
, and
n_{3}
-dimensional real vector spaces, and let
B
be the collection of bases of
V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and
V^{3}
defined above. For
\tau\in V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}
,

\displaystyle\|\tau\|_{B,p}^{p}\ =\ \sum_{t=1}^{n_{i}}\ \|\Pi_{i}(\tau)(e^{i*}_{t})\|_{B,p}^{p}

for any mode-
i
contraction
\Pi_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we prove the theorem for the the mode-
1
contraction. Suppose
\tau={\sum_{s=1}^{r}v^{1}_{s}\otimes v^{2}_{s}\otimes v^{3}_{s}}
for some vectors
v^{i}_{s}
, and let
a^{i}_{s,t}
be scalars such that
{v^{i}_{s}=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{i}}a^{i}_{s,t}e^{i}_{t}}
for all
i
and
s
. It follows that

\sum_{s=1}^{r}v^{1}_{s}\otimes v^{2}_{s}\otimes v^{3}_{s}\ =\ \sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{r}a^{1}_{si}a^{2}_{sj}a^{3}_{sk}\right)\ e^{1}_{i}\otimes e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\tau\|_{B,p}^{p}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}\left|\sum_{s=1}^{r}a^{1}_{si}a^{2}_{sj}a^{3}_{sk}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{2},n_{3}}\left|\sum_{s=1}^{r}a^{1}_{si}a^{2}_{sj}a^{3}_{sk}\right|^{p}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\|\Pi_{1}(\tau)(e^{1*}_{i})\|_{B,p}^{p}.

∎

Furthermore, when
p=2
, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{B,2}
is induced by the inner product

\displaystyle\langle\tau,\upsilon\rangle_{B,2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}a_{ijk}b_{ijk},

where
\tau={\sum_{ijk}a_{ijk}\ e^{1}_{i}\otimes e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}}
and
\upsilon=\sum_{ijk}b_{ijk}\ e^{1}_{i}\otimes e^{2}_{j}\otimes e^{3}_{k}
. The norm
\|\cdot\|_{B,2}
is called the Frobenius norm with respect to bases
B
.

Theorem 9
.

The Frobenius inner product has the following property on rank one tensors.

\displaystyle\langle v^{1}_{1}\otimes v^{2}_{1}\otimes v^{3}_{1}\mid v^{1}_{2}\otimes v^{2}_{2}\otimes v^{3}_{2}\rangle_{B,2}\ =\ \langle v^{1}_{1}\mid v^{1}_{2}\rangle_{B,2}\ \langle v^{2}_{1}\mid v^{2}_{2}\rangle_{B,2}\ \langle v^{3}_{1}\mid v^{3}_{2}\rangle_{B,2}

for any rank one tensors
v^{1}_{1}\otimes v^{2}_{1}\otimes v^{3}_{1},\ v^{1}_{2}\otimes v^{2}_{2}\otimes v^{3}_{2}\in V^{1}\otimes V^{2}\otimes V^{3}
.

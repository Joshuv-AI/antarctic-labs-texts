New analytical and geometrical aspects on Trudinger-Moser type inequality in 2D
Natalino Borgia, Silvia Cingolani, Gabriele Mancini

Abstract
. The present survey is devoted to results on Trudinger-Moser inequalities in two dimension. We give a brief overview of the history of these celebrated inequalities and, starting from the geometric problem that motivated Moser’s original work, we discuss the connection between Onofri’s inequality for the unit sphere and sharp inequalities on Euclidean domains. Finally, we present recent results and new insights into nonlocal interaction energy functionals in two dimension, involving logarithmic kernels.



1.
Introduction

If
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the Sobolev space

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a borderline case for the Sobolev embedding since it is
continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\Omega)
for any

1\leq q<\infty
, but

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\not\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega).

If
\Omega
is the unit disk, a counterexample is given by the function

u(x)=\ln(1-\ln|x|).

In 1967, Trudinger
[
86
]
proved that
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is embedded into the Orlicz space

L_{\phi}(\Omega)
corresponding to the Young measure
\phi(t)=e^{t^{2}}-1
(see also Yudovich
[
90
]
and Pohozaev
[
73
]
). In 1971, Moser
[
68
]
established a celebrated result which we recall for the two-dimensional case.

Theorem 1.1

(
Trudinger-Moser inequality in 2D
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain. Let
\mathcal{H}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ |\ \lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}\leq 1\}
, where
\ \lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert_{2}
denotes the usual
L^{2}
norm. Then

1)

\displaystyle\forall\,u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
:
\quad e^{u^{2}}\in L^{1}(\Omega).

2)

There exists a constant
C\geq 1
depending on the dimension only, such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi u^{2}}\,dx\leq C.

3)

If
\alpha>4\pi,
then

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u^{2}}\,dx=+\infty.

From Theorem
1.1
, it follows that
the maximal allowed growth is of exponential type and that
for any
p>1
there exists a sequence
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon>0}
of functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}=1
,
verifying
\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi pu_{\epsilon}^{2}}\,dx\to+\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0
. On the other hand, for all
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi pu^{2}}\,dx<\infty
for all
p>0
.

In 1985, Lions
[
57
]
proved that if
(u_{\epsilon})
is a sequence
of functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}=1
such that
u_{\epsilon}\to u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then for any
p<1/(1-\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}_{2})
it results

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi pu_{\epsilon}^{2}}\,dx<+\infty.

Of course this fact gives extra information if the sequence
u_{\epsilon}
has a weak limit
u_{0}\neq 0
.

A challenging problem on Trudinger-Moser inequalities was whether the extremal functions exist or not. It has remained open for quite some years.
In 1986,
Carleson and Chang
[
21
]
proved that
the supremum is attained in
(\ref{dis})
if
\Omega=B_{1}
is the unit disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
by using a symmetrization argument.
Successively
Struwe
[
78
]
showed that
the supremum is attained in
(\ref{dis})
if
\Omega
is close to a ball in the sense of measure. Flucher
[
44
]
extended this result to arbitrary
bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
using conformal deformations.

In 2004 Adimurthi and Druet
[
3
]

highlighted that the result of Lions
[
57
]
suggested

(1.2)

C_{\alpha}(\Omega):=\sup_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\|\nabla u\|_{2}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi u^{2}(1+\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{2})}dx<+\infty

for some
\alpha>0
.
In fact, they proved that if
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, denoted by

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian with respect to the Dirichlet boundary condition, it results that
for any

0\leq\alpha<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
,
C_{\alpha}(\Omega)<+\infty
. On the contrary, for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
,
C_{\alpha}(\Omega)=+\infty
.
These results in
[
3
]
add new information when the weak limit of
u_{\epsilon}
is
0
.
A stronger version of
(\ref{disadi1})
was obtained by Tintarev
[
84
]
and existence of extremal functions was proved by Yang
[
88
]
. We point out that the existence of extremal functions for these improved inequalities is an extremely delicate problem. For example, the supremum in (
1.2
) is attained for small values of
\alpha
, but it is not attained when
\alpha
is close to
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, see
[
64
]
. In general, even small perturbations of the exponential term in (
1.1
) may affect the attainability of the supremum, as it is pointed out by
[
76
]
and clarified by
[
61
]
and
[
46
]
. We refer to
[
83
]
for recent developments on the subject.

As regards Trudinger-Moser inequality
(\ref{dis})
on unbounded domains, we mention
for the subcritical growth
\alpha<4\pi
the results by Cao
[
19
]
and Adachi, Tanaka
[
1
]
(see also
[
38
]
).
In the critical case
\alpha=4\pi
, the inequality with the sharp exponent (proved by Moser) fails on the whole plane. Ruf (see
[
77
]
and
[
55
]
) proved that
there exists a constant
d>0
such that, for any
\Omega
unbounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.3)

\sup_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\bigl(e^{4\pi u^{2}}-1\bigr)dx\leq d,

where
\|u\|=\bigr(\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2})dx\bigl)^{1/2}
denotes the usual Sobolev norm on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

The supremum is attained if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
55
,
47
]
) and the inequality is sharp: for any growth
e^{\alpha u^{2}}
with
\alpha>4\pi
, the corresponding supremum equals
+\infty
. We mention
[
55
]
for a general dimension and
[
45
]
for
a more recent improvement of the inequality on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Furthermore an equivariant version of (
1.3
) has been obtained in
[
22
]
and sharp weighted Trudinger-Moser type inequalities were proved in
[
41
,
18
]
.
For unbounded domains with
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)>0
, bounds on the full Sobolev norm are unnecessary, as one can prove that

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{H}}\int_{\Omega}(e^{4\pi u^{2}}-1)\,dx<\infty,

where
\mathcal{H}
is defined as in Theorem
1.1
, see
[
63
]
and
[
9
]
.

Even if in this survey we focus on inequalities for
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, it is important to mention the work of Adams
[
2
]
, who proved similar inequalities for domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
whenever
kp=n
. We also refer to
[
65
]
for Trudinger-Moser-Adams type inequalities on fractional order Sobolev spaces. Concerning the study of extremal functions for this more general family of inequalities, we refer to
[
58
,
37
,
62
]
and references therein.

Trudinger-Moser type inequalities have also been widely studied for Sobolev spaces on Riemannian surfaces and manifolds. Indeed, as we discuss in the next section, they have important applications to prescribed curvature problems in conformal geometry. A general formulation of Trudinger-Moser-Adams inequalities for compact Riemannian manifolds was given by Fontana in
[
43
]
( see also
[
52
,
53
]
for related results). Recently subcritical and critical Trudinger-Moser inequalities are proved for complete noncompact Riemannian manifolds in
[
54
]
.

The singular Trudinger–Moser inequality and its extremal functions were considered in
[
26
,
85
,
4
,
34
,
48
,
89
]
and generalizations to the whole space in

[
88
,
56
]
.

The aim of the present survey is two-fold. On the one hand we discuss some connections between different notions of Trudinger-Moser inequality in 2D, announcing some forthcoming results in
[
15
]
. In particular, in section
2
, starting from geometrical and physical applications, we introduce an inequality due to Onofri
[
70
]
(see (
2.5
)) for Sobolev spaces on the unit sphere and we discuss its connection with similar inequalities for domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

On the other hand we present some recent results and new insights into
nonlocal interaction energy functionals in two dimension, arising from mathematical
models for chemotaxis
[
40
]
, in the statistical mechanics of selfgravitating clouds
[
82
,
87
]

and in the description of vortices in turbulent Euler flows
[
17
]
.
In particular, in section 3, we state some
Trudinger-Moser type inequalities with logarithmic kernel in
[
32
]
and announce some forthcoming developments in
[
33
]
.

2.
Onofri-type inequalities and geometric connections

The Trudinger-Moser inequality has applications to several mathematical fields ranging from mathematical physics to differential geometry and calculus of variations.
Among the reasons that led Moser to improve Trudinger’s result, it is important to mention Nirenberg’s problem on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which consists in describing the set of smooth functions that can be obtained as Gaussian curvature of a Riemannian metric conformally equivalent to the standard spherical metric.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth surface, we recall that two Riemannian metrics
g_{0}
and
g
on
\Sigma
are said to be pointwise conformally equivalent if there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that
g=e^{u}g_{0}
on
\Sigma
. In this setting, the following identity holds:

\Delta_{g_{0}}u+2K_{g}e^{u}=2K_{g_{0}},

where
\Delta_{g_{0}}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Sigma
, and
K_{g}
and
K_{g_{0}}
are the Gaussian curvatures of
\Sigma
corresponding respectively to
g
and
g_{0}
. When
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
g_{0}
is the standard spherical metric, then a function
K\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is the Gaussian curvature of a metric which is pointwise conformally equivalent to
g_{0}
if and only if there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying

(2.1)

\Delta_{g_{0}}u+2Ke^{u}=2\quad\mbox{ on }\quad(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0}).

It is possible to give a variational formulation of (
2.1
) by considering the functional

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{g_{0}}u|^{2}_{g_{0}}\,dv_{g_{0}}+2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u\,dv_{g_{0}}-8\pi\ln\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Ke^{u}\,dv_{g_{0}}\right),

which is well defined in

\mathcal{A}=:\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})\quad|\quad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Ke^{u}\,dv_{g_{0}}>0\right\},

provided
{\displaystyle\max_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}K>0}
. A function
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
is a weak solution to (
2.1
) if and only if it is a critical point of
\Phi
and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Ke^{u}dv_{g_{0}}=4\pi.

As an analog of Theorem
1.1
for the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
, Moser proves that there exists a constant
S>1
such that for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
with
\|\nabla_{g_{0}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})}\leq 1
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}e^{4\pi{(u-\overline{u})^{2}}}dv_{g_{0}}\leq S,\quad\text{where}\quad\overline{u}:=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u\,dv_{g_{0}}.

As in the Euclidean inequality (
1.1
), the exponent
4\pi
in (
2.2
) is sharp. As a straightforward consequence of (
2.2
) and Young’s inequality, one can prove the weaker inequality:

(2.3)

\ln\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}e^{u}\,dv_{g_{0}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{g_{0}}u|_{g_{0}}^{2}\,dv_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u\,dv_{g_{0}}+{\ln S},

for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0}),
where
S
is the constant appearing in (
2.2
), and the coefficient
\frac{1}{16\pi}
is sharp. In particular, (
2.3
) shows that
\Phi
is bounded from below in
\mathcal{A}
. While this is not sufficient to apply standard minimization techniques (actually, it can be shown that minimum points for
\Phi
in
\mathcal{A}
do not exist unless
K
is constant), the existence of critical points for
\Phi
can be obtained via improved versions of (
2.3
) under suitable assumptions on
K
. For example, in
[
69
]
Moser proves that if
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
is even, then (
2.3
) can be replaced by the stronger inequality:

(2.4)

\ln\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}e^{u}\,d\nu_{g_{0}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{g_{0}}u|_{g_{0}}^{2}\,d\nu_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u\,d\nu_{g_{0}}+C,

for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
K\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is even, the restriction of
\Phi
to the space of even functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
with zero average is coercive, and one can find a critical point of
\Phi
by means of Palais symmetric criticality principle. This lead to one of first general existence results for the Nirenberg problem: if
K\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is an even function and
\displaystyle{\max_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}K>0}
, then there exists a solution of (
2.1
). Over the years, many improved versions of (
2.3
) have been discovered. For example, we mention that (
2.3
) can be improved for functions with vanishing barycenter (see
[
6
]
and
[
60
]
) or for functions such that mass of the measure
e^{u}dv_{g_{0}}
is spread in at least two disjoint open subsets of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(see
[
27
,
59
]
). We refer to
[
12
,
50
,
23
,
24
,
79
]
and references therein for further results concerning Nirenberg’s problem on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Another interesting aspect of (
2.3
) is that while the coefficient
\frac{1}{16\pi}
is sharp, the constant
\ln S
is not. Indeed, Onofri in
[
70
]
proved that

\inf_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})}\Phi=0

and he classified all minimum points of
\Phi
. Namely, we have

(2.5)

\ln\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}e^{u}\,dv_{g_{0}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{g_{0}}u|_{g_{0}}^{2}\,dv_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u\,dv_{g_{0}},

for any
\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
, and equality holds if and only if
u=\ln|J_{\psi}|+C
, where
\psi:\mathbb{S}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
is a conformal diffeomorphism of
(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
,
J_{\psi}
denotes the Jacobian of
\psi
, and
C\in\mathbb{R}
. In
[
39
]
, (
2.5
) is obtained as limit of Sobolev’s inequalities for
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
p\nearrow 2
. Onofri’s inequality (
2.5
) plays a role in spectral analysis for the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Indeed, thanks to Polyakov’s formula (see
[
74
,
75
,
71
,
72
]
), we have

\frac{\det(-\Delta_{g})}{\det(-\Delta_{g_{0}})}=e^{-\frac{1}{24}\Phi(u)},

for any metric
g=e^{u}g_{0}
with
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. In particular, Onofri’s inequality (
2.5
) implies that, in the conformal class of
g_{0}
, the determinant of the Laplace-Beltrami operator is maximized by
g_{0}
.

An analog of (
2.5
) for the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be deduced from the work of Carleson and Chang
[
21
]
. As a crucial step in their proof of the existence of extremal functions for (
1.1
), they prove an inequality which is equivalent to:

(2.6)

\ln\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx\right)<\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+1,

for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
. Again, this inequality is sharp in the sense that

\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})}\left(\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\ln\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx\right)\right)=-1,

and

\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})}\left(\alpha\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\ln\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx\right)\right)=-\infty,

for any
\alpha<\frac{1}{16\pi}
. More generally, it was proved in
[
17
]
(see also
[
25
]
) that if
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary, then for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

(2.7)

\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}e^{u}\,dx\right)\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+1+{4\pi\sup_{\Omega}\gamma(x)+\ln\frac{\pi}{|\Omega|}},

where
\gamma
is Robin’s function of
\Omega
, which is defined for
x\in\Omega
as
\gamma(x)=H_{x}(x)
, where
H_{x}
is the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\Delta H_{x}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
H_{x}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x-y|&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Differently from (
2.6
), (
2.7
) is not always sharp. Indeed, denoting

I(\Omega):=\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\ln\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}e^{u}\,dx\right)\right),

there are domains
\Omega
(see
[
16
, Section 7]
) for which

(2.8)

I(\Omega)>-1-{4\pi\sup_{\Omega}\gamma(x)-\ln\frac{\pi}{|\Omega|}}.

The domains for which (
2.7
) is sharp are known in the literature as domains of first kind, while the ones for which (
2.8
) holds are known as domains of second kind. Inequality (
2.7
) plays a crucial role in the study of the mean field equation

(2.9)

-\Delta u=\frac{\rho e^{u}}{\int_{\Omega}e^{u}\,dx}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

which appears in the statistical mechanic description of vortex formations in 2d-models for turbulent flows in fluid dynamics (see e.g.
[
49
,
17
]
). We refer to
[
28
,
29
,
59
,
7
,
8
]
for existence results for (
2.9
) and related problems.

In
[
68
]
Moser explicitly states that it seems impossible to deduce (
1.1
) from (
2.2
) or (
2.2
) from (
1.1
). The proofs of the two inequalities are somehow independent, although they are based on similar arguments relying on symmetrization techniques.
It turns out that a more explicit connection exists between (
2.5
) and (
2.6
). Indeed, one can show that (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are equivalent by exploiting the conformal equivalence between
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}
minus one point. In the remaining part of this section, we sketch a simple argument to deduce (
2.6
) from (
2.5
), which is inspired by
[
48
]
, where the authors consider generalized versions of (
2.6
) and (
2.5
) involving singular weights. In a forthcoming paper
[
15
]
, we will give a complete proof of the equivalence between (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) and investigate extensions to dimension
n\geq 3
(see
[
36
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
51
]
).

In the following, we assume that (
2.5
) holds in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
. We take a function
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
, and we prove that (
2.5
) holds. We denote by
N=(0,0,1)
the north pole of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and we let
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{N\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the standard stereographic projection. We shall consider the function

v_{0}(x)=2\ln\left(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\right).

We stress that
v_{0}
satisfies

(2.10)

-\Delta v_{0}=2e^{v_{0}}\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Moreover, for any
f:\mathbb{S}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0}),
we have that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f\,dv_{g_{0}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(f\circ\pi^{-1})\,e^{v_{0}}\,dx.

For any
r>0
, we define
u_{r}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle u_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle u\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)-v_{0}(x)&\text{if $|x|\leq r$,}\\
-2\ln\left(\frac{2}{1+r^{2}}\right)&\text{if $|x|>r$,}\end{cases}

and
\widehat{u}_{r}:\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}u_{r}\circ\pi(x)&\text{ if }x\neq N\\
-2\ln\left(\frac{2}{1+r^{2}}\right)&\text{ if }x=N.\end{cases}

Observe that
{\widehat{u}_{r}}
is constant in a neighborhood of
N
and
{\widehat{u}_{r}}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
. Then, we can apply (
2.5
) to get:

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}e^{{\widehat{u}_{r}}}\,dv_{g_{0}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{g_{0}}{\widehat{u}_{r}}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\,dv_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\widehat{u}_{r}}\,dv_{g_{0}}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}e^{\widehat{u_{r}}}\,dv_{g_{0}}=\frac{r^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx+\frac{1+r^{2}}{4}.

Similarly, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\widehat{u}_{r}}\,dv_{g_{0}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B_{r}}u_{r}e^{v_{0}}\,dx+2-\ln 4+o_{r}(1),

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}\,dx=2\ln r-1+o_{r}(1),

where
o_{r}(1)\to 0
as
r\to+\infty
.
Using (
2.10
) and (
2.12
) and (
2.11
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|\nabla_{g_{0}}{\widehat{u}_{r}}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\,dv_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{B_{r}}\left|\nabla u_{r}\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B_{r}}u_{r}e^{2v_{0}}dx+2\ln r-1+o_{r}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{D}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\widehat{u}_{r}}\,dv_{g_{0}}+\ln\left(\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right)+1+o_{r}(1).

Summing up, we have shown that

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx+\frac{1+r^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{D}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\,dx+1+o(1).

Passing to limit as
r\to+\infty,
we conclude

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx\right){<}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}e^{u}\,dx+1\right)\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\,dx+1.

Hence
u
satisfies (
2.6
).

3.
Trudinger-Moser type inequalities with logarithmic kernels

In this section we consider some nonlocal interaction functionals, which naturally
arise in 2D mathematical models for chemotaxis and in other physical contexts, as statistical dynamics, vortex theory.

These two-dimensional problems have remained for a long time a quite open field of study, due to the difficulties to handle changing sign kernels, not bounded from above and below. In literature the first results are just numerical. Recently variational results have been employed to treat integro-differential equations, involving logarithmic convolution potentials,
where classical PDE theory fails because of the presence of the non-locality
(see

[
13
,
14
,
10
,
30
,
31
,
42
,
66
,
67
,
80
,
81
]
).

In particular
we consider the following class of free energy functionals

u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy,

where

\Omega=B_{1}
is the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

and

F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
grows exponentially.

In
[
32
]
we faced the following challenging questions:

•

Moser-Trudinger type inequalities in presence of a logarithmic convolution potential;

•

characterization of the critical nonlinear growth rates for these inequalities;

•

associated Euler-Lagrange equation;

•

qualitative properties of the maximizers;

•

symmetry and uniqueness results.

Here we give some outlines of the results and the proofs
in
[
32
]
. Firstly we consider the problem of maximizing
the quantity

(3.1)

\Phi(u):=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy

among functions
in the set

{\mathcal{H}}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})\>:\>|\nabla u|_{2}\leq 1\}.

We make the following general assumption on
F
:

(A_{0})

F:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is even, continuous, and strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
. Moreover, there exist constants
\alpha,c>0
with

(3.2)

F(t)\leq ce^{\alpha t^{2}}\qquad\text{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Since the logarithmic kernel changes sign, it is not a priori clear if the double integral has a well-defined value.
To clarify this point, we split the kernel
\ln\frac{1}{|\,\cdot\,|}
into its positive and negative part and define the functionals

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln^{+}\!\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy\in[0,+\infty],

\displaystyle\Phi_{-}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln^{+}\!|x-y|\,F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy\in[0,+\infty],

where
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
, and
\ln^{+}=\max\{\ln,0\}
.
By the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality,

\int_{B_{1}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}dx<\infty\qquad\text{for any $u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})$ and any $\alpha>0$.}

By the growth condition

F(t)\leq ce^{\alpha t^{2}}\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

we infer

F(u)\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any

u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
,
s\in[1,\infty)
.
Since

\ln^{+}\frac{1}{|\cdot|}\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\qquad\text{for $s\in[1,\infty)$,}

choosing
s=2
, we deduce by Young convolution inequality that
\ln^{+}\frac{1}{|\cdot|}*F(u)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
thus
\Phi_{+}(u)<\infty
, for every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
.

Moreover, we have

[\ln^{+}|\cdot|*F(u)](x)=\int_{B_{1}}\ln^{+}|x-y|F(u(y))\,dy<\ln 2|F(u)|_{1}\qquad\text{ $\forall x\in B_{1}$}

and therefore
\ln^{+}|\cdot|*F(u)\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
and thus
\Phi_{-}(u)<\infty
for every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
.

Taking into account that

\displaystyle\ln\frac{1}{|\cdot|}=\ln^{+}\frac{1}{|\cdot|}-\ln^{+}{|\cdot|},

we have

\Phi(u):=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln\!\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy=\Phi_{+}(u)-\Phi_{-}(u)\in]-\infty,\infty[

for every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
.

Now in order to study the maximization problem related to the quantity

\Phi(u):=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln\!\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy

within the set

{\mathcal{H}}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})\>:\>|\nabla u|_{2}\leq 1\}.

we need to distinguish different forms of asymptotic growth of the nonlinearity
F
. As in Trudinger-Moser inequality, the value
4\pi
will play a key role.

Firstly we notice that if
F
has a subcritical exponential growth
\alpha<4\pi
,
an immediate proof of the claim

m_{1}(F):=\sup_{u\in{\mathcal{H}}}\Phi(u)<\infty

can be given using the logarithmic Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}f(x)f(y)\,dxdy\leq\frac{|f|_{1}}{2}\Bigl(|f|_{1}\bigl(c_{0}+\bigl|\ln|f|_{1}\bigr|\bigr)+\int_{B_{1}}f\ln f\,dx\Bigr),

which holds for any a.e. non-negative function
f\in L^{1}(B_{1})
with
f\ln f\in L^{1}(B_{1})
, see
[
11
, Theorem 2]
.

Indeed, let

u\in{\mathcal{H}}\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
, and let

v:=F(u)
.
Then
v\leq c_{1}e^{\alpha u^{2}}
,
\alpha<4\pi
,
c_{1}>0

and

v\ln v\leq c_{1}e^{\alpha u^{2}}\Bigl(\alpha u^{2}+\ln c_{1}\Bigr)\leq c_{2}e^{4\pi u^{2}}

with
c_{2}>0
. By the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality,
we have

|v|_{1}\leq c_{1}\int_{B_{1}}e^{4\pi u^{2}}dx\leq c_{1}c(B_{1})=:c_{2},\quad\qquad\int_{B_{1}}v\ln v\,dx\leq c_{2}c(B_{1})=:c_{3}.

Combining with the
logarithmic Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality we infer

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}v(x)v(y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|v|_{1}}{2}\Bigl(|v|_{1}\bigl(c_{0}+\bigl|\ln|v|_{1}\bigr|\bigr)+\int_{{B_{1}}}v\ln v\,dx\Bigr)\leq\frac{c_{2}}{2}\bigl(c_{2}\bigl({c_{0}}+|\ln c_{2}|\bigr)+c_{3}\bigr)<\infty.

For general
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
, the same inequality follows by approximation and
m_{1}(F)<\infty
.
This argument does not apply if
F
is critical, so it does not allow for sharp critical growth rates.

In
[
32
]
we define some critical rates for the Log-TM inequality.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary function. We say that
g
has

(i)

at most
\beta
-critical growth if
|g(s)|\leq c\ e^{4\pi s^{2}}(1+|s|)^{\beta}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
with some constant
c>0
;

(ii)

at least
\beta
-critical growth if there exist
s_{0},c>0
with the property that

|g(s)|\geq c\ e^{4\pi s^{2}}|s|^{\beta}\qquad\text{for $|s|\geq s_{0}$.}

In
[
32
]
we obtained the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
F
satisfies
(A_{0})
.

(1)

If
F
has at most
\beta
-critical growth
for some
\beta\leq-1
, then

m_{1}(F):=\sup_{u\in{\mathcal{H}}}\Phi(u)<\infty.

(2)

If
F
has at most

\beta
-critical growth for some
\beta<-1
, then
m_{1}(F)
is attained, and every maximizer for
\Phi
in
{\mathcal{H}}
is, up to sign, a radial and radially decreasing function in
B_{1}
.

(3)

If
F
has at least
\beta
-critical growth for some
\beta>-1
, then
m_{1}(F)=\infty
.

In order to maximize
\Phi

among the functions in
{\mathcal{H}}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})\>:\>|\nabla u|_{2}\leq 1\}
,
we consider

{\mathcal{H}}^{*}:=\{u^{*}\>:\>u\in{\mathcal{H}}\}

the corresponding Schwartz symmetrized set.
Here
u^{*}
is nonnegative, radial and nonincreasing in the radial variable.

By the Pólya-Szegö inequality,
for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we have
u^{*}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

|u^{*}|_{p}=|u|_{p},\quad|\nabla u^{*}|_{2}\leq|\nabla u|_{2},\qquad\qquad\text{ $\forall p\in[2,+\infty[$.}

Consequently, we have
u^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}
if
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and so

{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\subset{\mathcal{H}}.

Now
by Riesz’s rearrangement inequality, for every
u\in{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln^{+}\!\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,F(u(x))F(u(y))\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln^{+}\!\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,[F(u(x))]^{*}[F(u(y))]^{*}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\ln^{+}\!\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,[F(u^{*}(x))][F(u^{*}(y))]\,dxdy=\Phi_{+}(u^{*})

Therefore it is immediate that

m_{1}(F):=\sup_{{\mathcal{H}}}\Phi(u)\leq m_{1}^{+}(F):=\sup_{{\mathcal{H}}}\Phi_{+}=\sup_{u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}}\Phi_{+}(u).

In
[
32
]
we prove that

\sup_{u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}}\Phi_{+}(u)<+\infty

and thus the the following proposition holds.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
F
satisfies
(A_{0})
and has at most
\beta
-critical growth for some
{\beta\leq-1}
. Then we have

m_{1}(F):=\sup_{u\in{\mathcal{H}}}\Phi(u)<\infty

We emphasize the optimality of the growth exponent
\beta=-1
for the finiteness of
m_{1}(F)
. Indeed we can define
the Moser type functions
u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2

(3.3)

u_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\ln(1/|x|)}{(\ln n)^{1/2}}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{4\ln n}\bigr)^{1/2},\quad\ \frac{1}{n}\leq|x|\leq 1,

(3.4)

u_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{(\ln n)^{1/2}}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{4\ln n}\bigr)^{1/2},\quad\ 0\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{n}.

A standard computation gives the following blow-up estimates.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
F
satisfies
(A_{0})
and has at least
\beta
-critical growth for some

\beta>-1
. Then, if
u_{n}
defined as in (
3.3
)-(
3.4
), we have
u_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
and
\Phi(u_{n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Finally, using Concentration and Compactness Principle by Lions
[
57
]
, we can recognize the existence of estremals for the Log-TM maximization problem when the parameter
\beta<-1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
F
satisfies
(A_{0})
and has at most
\beta
-critical growth with
\beta<-1
. Then the value

m_{1}(F)<\infty
is attained by a function
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a maximizing sequence in
{\mathcal{H}}
for
m_{1}(F)
. We may assume that
u_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
, we may also assume that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})\qquad\text{with $u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}$.}

Proof.

For reader’s convenience we give a sketch of the proof in
[
32
]
.
By Lions
[
57
]
, we have two possibilities. Either

i)

u=0
or

ii)

u\not=0
, and
\int_{B_{1}}e^{(4\pi+t)u_{n}^{2}}\ dx
is bounded for some
t>0
and thus

e^{4\pi u_{n}^{2}}\to e^{4\pi u^{2}}\quad\hbox{in}\ \ L^{1}(B_{1}).

Assume first that
i)
holds. In this case, we can prove that

m_{1}(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(u_{n})=\Phi(0).

On the other hand,
if
F
satisfies
(A_{0})
, it follows from that

\displaystyle\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=2(2\pi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}rF(u(r))\ln\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\rho F(u(\rho))d\rho dr

\displaystyle>2(2\pi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}rF(u(0))\ln\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\rho F(u(0))d\rho dr=\Phi(0)

for every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})\setminus\{0\}
. Hence

m_{1}(F)\in\bigl(\Phi(0),\infty\bigr]

and therefore
m_{1}(F)
is not attained at
u=0
. So this case does not occur.

It remains to consider the case where
(u_{n})_{n}
satisfies
ii)
.

Set
v_{n}:=F(u_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
v:=F(u)
.

By
ii)
,
\int_{B_{1}}e^{(4\pi+t)u_{n}^{2}}\ dx
is bounded for some
t>0
and thus
v_{n}
is bounded in
L^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with

s_{0}=1+\frac{t}{4\pi}>1
.
Moreover, since

v_{n}\to v\qquad\text{in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$,}

interpolation yields that

v_{n}\to v
in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1\leq s<s_{0}
.
Finally we conclude that

m_{1}(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(u_{n})=\Phi(u)

so
m_{1}(F)
is attained at
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
.
∎

Furthermore it is possible to derive qualitative property of the maximizer.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
F
satisfy
(A_{0})
, and let
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
be a maximizer for
\Phi\big|_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Then, up to a change of sign, we have
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
, and

u
is strictly positive in
B_{1}
.

Moreover we can prove that the extremals are solutions of an Euler-Lagrange equation under some additional regularity properties on the function
F
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose that
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
(A_{0})
, and suppose that
f:=F^{\prime}
satisfies

f(t)\leq ce^{\alpha t^{2}}\qquad\text{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$ with constants $c,\alpha>0$.}

Suppose furthermore that
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
is a maximizer of
\Phi\big|_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. Then there exists

\theta>0

such that
u
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation in weak sense, i.e.,

\int_{B_{1}}\nabla u\nabla\phi\,dx=\theta\int_{B_{1}}\ln\frac{1}{|\cdot|}*\bigl(1_{B_{1}}F(u)\bigr)f(u)\phi\,dx

for all
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
.
Moreover,
u\in W^{2,p}(B_{1})\cap C^{1,\sigma}(\overline{B_{1}})
for all
p<\infty
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
.

In the recent paper
[
33
]
,
we focus on the case
F
has at most
\beta=-1
critical growth and
we turn to the question whether the supremum is attained in the case of critical growth.
In some sense we tackle the open problem if there is a Log-Counterpart of Carleson-Chang paper.

We recall that a sequence
(u_{n})\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a normalized concentrating sequence if

i)

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}=1

ii)

u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

iii)

there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
s.t.
\forall\rho>0,\int_{\Omega\setminus{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx\to 0
.

We also remark that
(u_{n})\subset H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
is a normalized concentrating sequence, we can suppose that
x_{0}=0
and
by symmetrization
u_{n}=u_{n}^{*}
is radially symmetric decreasing function.

In
[
33
]
we give some answers to the above open problem, studying the behaviour of the nonlocal interaction functional on the concentrating sequences in the spirit of
the
celebrated paper

[
35
]
.

Remark 3.8
.

In the case of unbounded domains, the Trudinger-Moser type inequalities in
[
32
]
are also proved for the entire domain
\mathbb{R}^{2}
under an additional assumption

(A_{1})

F:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies
(A_{0})
, and
F(t)=O(|t|)
as
t\to 0
.

Remark 3.9
.

As written in
[
32
]
, it is still an open problem to derive Trudinger-Moser inequalities with Log-potentials for different geometry of bounded domains in critical cases. The existence of maximizers may also depend on the domain.
It is still open the uniqueness, up to sign and translation, of maximizers of
\Phi|_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

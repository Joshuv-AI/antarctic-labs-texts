A formula for the n t ​ h n^{th} derivative of the quotient of two functions
Christos Xenophontos

Abstract
As the title suggests, we give a formula for the n t ​ h n^{th} derivative of a quotient of two functions, analogous to Leibniz’s formula for the product. This particular note has remained unpublished since 2007 (available only my website), however it has received several citations (e.g. [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] ). As a result, it now appears on ArXiv for future reference.



In most calculus courses one encounters Leibnitz’s formula for the
n^{\text{th}}
derivative of the
product
of two functions, but when it comes
to the
quotient
this is seldomly discussed.

Does such a formula even exist? In answering this question, we may first
attempt to answer the simpler query: what is the formula for the
n^{\text{th}}
derivative of
1/f(x)
? The answer to the latter question was given in

[
4
]
, even though the notation used makes the formula difficult to use.
The former, and original, question was answered in
[
5
]
using a linear
algebra approach which ultimately gave a rather beautiful formula involving
the determinant of a certain matrix. As the author of that article admitted,
the interest in the particular determinantal formula “lies
mainly in the structured it revealed”
[
5
]
. Here we
built upon the ideas in
[
5
]
to obtain a more usable version of the
formula for the
n^{\text{th}}
derivative of the quotient of two functions
u(x)
and
v(x)
, that is analogous to Leibnitz’s formula.

As in
[
5
]
, writing
y=u/v
and using Leibnitz’s formula we have

u^{(r)}=\left(yv\right)^{(r)}=\underset{j=0}{\overset{r}{\sum}}\frac{r!}{(r-j)!j!}y^{(j)}v^{(r-j)},

where
u^{(r)}
denotes
d^{r}u/dx^{r}
for
r=0,1,2,....
From the above
expression we get

\frac{u^{(r)}}{r!}\underset{j=0}{=\overset{r}{\sum}}\frac{y^{(j)}}{j!}\frac{v^{(r-j)}}{(r-j)!},

which, for
r=0,1,2,...,n
is nothing but the lower triangular linear system
of
n+1
equations in the unknowns
y,\frac{y^{(1)}}{1!},\frac{y^{(2)}}{2!},...,\frac{y^{(n)}}{n!},
shown below:

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}v&0&0&\ldots&0\\
\frac{v^{(1)}}{1!}&v&0&\ldots&0\\
\frac{v^{(2)}}{2!}&\frac{v^{(1)}}{1!}&v&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\frac{v^{(n)}}{n!}&\frac{v^{(n-1)}}{(n-1)!}&\ldots&\frac{v^{(1)}}{1!}&v\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
\frac{y^{(1)}}{1!}\\
\frac{y^{(2)}}{2!}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{y^{(n)}}{n!}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
\frac{u^{(1)}}{1!}\\
\frac{u^{(2)}}{2!}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{u^{(n)}}{n!}\end{array}\right].

In
[
5
]
the above linear system was solved using Cramer’s rule to obtain
a determinantal formula for
y^{(n)}
. If, instead, we take advantage of the
lower triangular structure of the linear system and use back substitution to
solve it we have

\frac{y^{(n)}}{n!}=\frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{u^{(n)}}{n!}-\underset{j=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{v^{(n+1-j)}}{(n+1-j)!}\frac{y^{(j-1)}}{(j-1)!}\right),

from which the following formula arises:

\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{(n)}=\frac{1}{v}\left(u^{(n)}-n!\underset{j=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{v^{(n+1-j)}}{(n+1-j)!}\frac{\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{(j-1)}}{(j-1)!}\right).

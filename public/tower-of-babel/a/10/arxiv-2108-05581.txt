On the Fine-Grained Complexity of the Unbounded SubsetSum and the Frobenius Problem
Kim-Manuel Klein University of Kiel kmk@informatik.uni-kiel.de

Abstract
Consider positive integral solutions x ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 n + 1 x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1} to the equation a 0 ​ x 0 + … + a n ​ x n = t a_{0}x_{0}+\ldots+a_{n}x_{n}=t . In the so called unbounded subset sum problem, the objective is to decide whether such a solution exists, whereas in the Frobenius problem, the objective is to compute the largest t t such that there is no such solution. In this paper we study the algorithmic complexity of the unbounded subset sum, the Frobenius problem and a generalization of the problems. More precisely, we study pseudo-polynomial time algorithms with a running time that depends on the smallest number a 0 a_{0} or respectively the largest number a n a_{n} . For the parameter a 0 a_{0} , we show that all considered problems are subquadratically equivalent to ( m ​ i ​ n , + ) (min,+) -convolution, a fundamental algorithmic problem from the area of fine-grained complexity. By this equivalence, we obtain hardness results for the considered problems (based on the assumption that an algorithm with a subquadratic running time for ( m ​ i ​ n , + ) (min,+) -convolution does not exist) as well as algorithms with improved running time. The proof for the equivalence makes use of structural properties of solutions, a technique that was developed in the area of integer programming. In case of the complexity of the problems parameterized by a n a_{n} , we present improved algorithms. For example we give a quasi linear time algorithm for the Frobenius problem as well as a hardness result based on the strong exponential time hypothesis.

Introduction

Consider the integer program

\displaystyle a_{0}x_{0}+\ldots+a_{n}x_{n}

\displaystyle=t

(1)

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}

a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

Having only a single constraint, (
1
) is the most basic integer program in standard form. In this paper we consider fundamental algorithmic problems regarding (
1

We call the numbers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
the

and define the set of item sizes

by
S=\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\}
. Throughout the paper we assume that the items are sorted by
a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{n}
and that
gcd(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})=1
(this property is not really necessary as one can always divide by the gcd). We call

the
target value
and we say that

is a feasible target value for given

if (
1

In the

problem the objective is to decide if (
1

S
and target
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
if
t
is feasible.
Formally it is defined by

Unbounded SubsetSum

Input:
Items
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Objective:
Decide whether (1) is feasible.

By a classical dynamic program that is being taught in undergraduate, the problem can be solved in time

9
]

In a related problem, the objective is to compute the largest

such that (
1

Frobenius

Input:
Items
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Objective:
Compute the largest
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

The solution to the problem is called the

a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
are clear from the context, we will only write

. For this problem, the property that

F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})
would not be finite otherwise. It is easy to see that
F
is finite if
gcd(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})=1

.

A natural generalization of both problems is a problem that computes the so called
\emph{residuetable}
. The residue table was first considered by Brauer and Shockley
[
11
]
already in 1962 in order to solve

AllTargets

Input:
Items
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Objective:
Compute
a[0],\ldots,a[n-1]
, such that

\displaystyle a[i]=\min\{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid t\equiv i\bmod a_{0}\text{ and $t$ is a feasible solution of (\ref{IP1})}\}.

Having a solution of

a[i]
in the sequence

can be accessed in constant time). This is because

\displaystyle t\text{ is feasible}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad t\geq a[t\bmod a_{0}].

Note that in the case that
t>a[t\bmod a_{0}]
a solution for target
t
can be obtained by adding multiples of
a_{0}
to the solution for target
a[t\bmod a_{0}]
(which by definition exists). By the same argumentation, the Frobenius number can be determined from the residue table
a
in
O(a_{0})
time. The Frobenius number

is

\displaystyle F=\max_{i}\{a[i]\}-a_{0}.

Many algorithms in the literature that solve
Unbounded SubsetSum
or
Frobenius
actually solve the more general problem of
AllTargets
and then extract the respective solution for
Frobenius
or
Unbounded SubsetSum
from the table. In this context one might ask if the

problem is actually computationally equivalent to
Unbounded SubsetSum
and

or if it is harder to compute the more general problem of determining the complete residue table. We have a partial answer on this question in this paper.

In general,

and
Frobenius
are classical (weakly) NP-complete problems
[
26
,
28
]
, that is, if we assume that the given numbers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},t
are encoded in binary. In this paper we study pseudo-polynomial time algorithms with a running time that depends on the smallest item size
a_{0}
or the largest item size
a_{n}
.

1.1
Related Results

There are two different communities involved in studying the
Unbounded SubsetSum
and the
Frobenius
problem.
On the one hand, there is the community that considers variants of subset sum problems.
On the other hand there is the community which studies the
Frobenius
problem from various angles, which includes bounds for the Frobenius number, algorithmic complexity of the problem as well as practical fast algorithms.

Subset Sum

One of the most fundamental problems in computed science is the (bounded) subset sum problem where the objective is to find a subset of a given (multi-)set of
n
items such that there total sum equals
t
. This is equivalent to an integer program as in (
1
), where additional upper bounds on the variables are given.
Unbounded SubsetSum
is in this sense a special case of the (bounded) subset sum problem, where the multiplicities of each item size are sufficiently large, i.e.
\geq t
.
Using a classical dynamic program by Bellman from 1957, the (bounded) subset sum problem can be solved in time
O(nt)
. This algorithm was improved by Koiliaris and Xu
[
24
]
who gave an algorithm with a running time of
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{n}t)
(omitting logarithmic factors) and then further by a breakthrough result of Bringmann
[
12
]
who gave an algorithm with a running time of
\tilde{O}(n+t)
. In that work, Bringmann also developed an algorithm for
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of
O(t\log t)
.
Jansen and Rohwedder
[
22
]
showed later that there is an algorithm with a running time
O(a_{n}\log^{2}a_{n})
for
Unbounded SubsetSum
for the stronger parameter
a_{n}
. Assuming the strong exponential time hypothesis (SETH), it was shown that the existing algorithms are actually optimal as Abboud et al.
[
1
]
proved that there is no algorithm for subset sum or
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of
O(t^{1-\epsilon})
for any
\epsilon>0
.

The complexity of
Unbounded SubsetSum
regarding the parameter
a_{0}
was open so far. In the hard instance that was used in
[
1
]
all items
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
and
t
differ only by a factor of
2
and therefore only an algorithm with a running time of
O(a_{0}^{1-\epsilon})
can be excluded.

The Frobenius Problem

The
Frobenius
problem is an intensively studied problem in the literature. Since we can not cover all its aspects here, we refer to the monograph of Ramirez-Alfonsin
[
2
]
for a general overview. The book covers over 400 sources that are concerned with the problem.

In the case that
n=2
, the Frobenius number
F
can be easily determined by the formula
F=a_{0}a_{1}-a_{0}-a_{1}
. However for
n>2
there is no formula but several upper and lower bounds are known for
F
(see for example
[
6
,
19
]
). A bound that we make use of in this paper is due to Erdös and Graham
[
18
]
which states that

\displaystyle F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\leq\frac{2a_{n-1}a_{n}}{n+1}-a_{n}=O\big(\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n}\big).

As mentioned, the
Frobenius
problem is NP-complete and hence we can not expect a polynomial time algorithm. However, many pseudo-polynomial time algorithms have been studied having a running time which depends on the input parameters.
The most relevant result for our paper here is the result of Böcker and Lipták
[
10
]
. They developed an algorithm solving
AllTargets
and therefore also
Frobenius
and
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of
O(na_{0})
. Based on the Round Robin procedure, they presemt a very elegant dynamic program computing the residue table. By this, Böcker and Lipták improved upon several known existing pseudo-polynomial time algorithms for the
Frobenius
problem (see Chapter 1 in
[
2
]
).
The algorithm of Nijenhuis
[
27
]
for example has a running time of
O(a_{0}(n+\log a_{0}))
. Nijenhuis used Dijkstras algorithm in the weighted Cayley graph of the group
(\mathbb{Z}_{a_{0}},+)
to compute the residue table. The performance of several pseudo-polynomial time algorithms for the problem has been tested in practice
[
8
]
.

The mentioned pseudo-polynomial time algorithms are efficient in the case that the input numbers are small. There are also algorithms for
Frobenius
in the case that
n
is small. For example, for three coins, Greenberg
[
20
]
developed an algorithm with a running time of
O(\log a_{0})
. Kannan
[
23
]
showed in the end that there is a polynomial time algorithm for any constant
n
. Other practically efficient algorithms have been developed that are efficient if
n
is small (see for example
[
15
,
29
]
).

2.1
Structural Properties of Solutions

Before we show Theorem
1
, we prove a structural result regarding the existence of solutions of (
1
) with a specific shape. The shown structural property will then be of use in Theorem
2
.

The proof of the structural result relies on a technique that was developed in the area of integer programming showing the existence of solutions of bounded support
[
16
]
. The support
supp(x)
of a solution
x
is the set of non-zero components.

Lemma 1
.

Assuming that (
1
) is feasible, then there exists a solution
x
of (
1
) with

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+1)\leq a_{0}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
be a solution of (
1
) that is lexicographically maximal. We will show that for this solution
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+1)\leq a_{0}
holds.

Suppose by contradiction that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+1)>a_{0}
. By the pigeonhole principle there exist two distinct subvectors
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\leq x
with

\displaystyle a^{T}x^{\prime}\equiv a^{T}x^{\prime\prime}\bmod a_{0}

and hence
a^{T}x^{\prime}=a^{T}x^{\prime\prime}+ka_{0}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
k\geq 0
(otherwise we would exchange
x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime\prime}
). If
k=0
we assume that
x^{\prime}
is lexicographically smaller than
x^{\prime\prime}
.

The solution
x
can now be modified to obtain a solution
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
by

\displaystyle y=x+x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}+ke_{0}.

The solution
y
is feasible since

\displaystyle t=a^{T}x=a^{T}x+a^{T}(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}+ke_{0})=a^{T}y,

as by definition

\displaystyle a^{T}(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}+ke_{0})=a^{T}(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+ka_{0}=0,

holds, where
e_{0}
is the
0
-th unit vector. Furthermore
y\geq 0
holds as
x-x^{\prime}\geq 0
and therefore we can conclude that
y
is a feasible solution of IP (
1
).

If
k>0
then
y_{0}=x_{0}+k
and hence the solution
y
is lexicographically larger than
x
, which is a contradiction to the assumption.
If
k=0
and hence
x^{\prime}
is lexicographically smaller than
x^{\prime\prime}
there exists a component
i\in(supp(x^{\prime})\cup supp(x^{\prime\prime}))
such that
x^{\prime\prime}_{i}>x^{\prime}_{i}
and
x^{\prime}_{j}=x^{\prime\prime}_{j}
holds for all
j<i
. However, this implies again that
y
is lexicographically larger than
x
which is a contradiction to the assumption. Therefore
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+1)\leq a_{0}
holds.
∎

The following corollary shows the existence of a solution of (
1
) with bounded support. It follows directly from the previous lemma. The same statement was previously already shown by Aliev et al.
[
3
]
using a different proof technique. In their proof they use a geometric volume argument in combination with Minkowski’s first theorem from the geometry of numbers. Before that Aliev et al.
[
4
]
had also shown a weaker bound of
\log(a_{n})+1
.

Corollary 1
.

There exists a solution
x
of (
1
) with

\displaystyle|supp(x)|\leq\log(a_{0})+1.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
there exists a solution
x
of (
1
) with

\displaystyle 2^{|supp(x)\setminus\{0\}|}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+1)\leq a_{0}.

We obtain that
|supp(x)|\leq\log(a_{0})+1
.
∎

2.2
Solving
AllTargets
by
MinConv

In order to solve
AllTargets
we use a min-convolution operation, where the indices of the sequences are computed
\bmod n
.
Let the
modulo-min-convolution

a\oplus_{n}b
of two sequence
(a[i])_{i=0}^{n-1}
and
(b[i])_{i=0}^{n-1}
be defined by

\displaystyle(a\oplus_{n}b)[k]=\min_{i+j\equiv k\bmod n}a[i]+a[j].

The modulo-min-convolution can easily be computed using the classical convolution on modified sequences
\bar{a}
and
\bar{b}
of length
2n
defined by

\displaystyle\bar{a}[i]=a[i\bmod n]\quad\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,2n-1

\displaystyle\bar{b}[i]=a[i\bmod n]\quad\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,2n-1.

We compute the sequence
\bar{c}
using classical min-convolution, i.e.

\displaystyle\bar{c}=\bar{a}\oplus\bar{b}.

The sequence
c=a\oplus_{n}b
is then obtained by

\displaystyle c[i]=\min\{\bar{c}[i],\bar{c}[i+n]\}\quad\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,n-1.

Applying the mod-min-convolution repeatedly to a sequence
(a[i])_{i=0}^{n-1}
yields a sequence
a^{*}
with

\displaystyle a^{*}=a\oplus_{n}a\oplus_{n}\cdots,

where
a^{*}=a^{*}\oplus_{n}a
holds. For this sequence there exist no indices
i,j
with
a^{*}[i]+a^{*}[j]<a^{*}[i+j\bmod n]
. In this sense
a^{*}
is the
transitive mod-min-convolution closure
of the sequence
a
.
It is easy to see that a solution to the
AllTargets
problem is actually the transitive mod-min-convolution closure of the sequence
(a[i])_{i=0}^{a_{0}-1}
defined by

\displaystyle a[i]=\min\{a_{j}\mid a_{j}\equiv i\bmod a_{0}\}.

In the following theorem we show that
a^{*}
can be computed by using only
\lceil\log\log a_{0}\rceil
many mod-min-convolution operations.

Theorem 2
.

An algorithm for
MinConv
with a running time of
T(n)
implies an algorithm with a running time of

\displaystyle\lceil\log\log a_{0}\rceil\cdot T(a_{0})+O(a_{0}\log a_{0})

for
AllTargets
.

Proof.

Define the set
V
, which consists of the set of item sizes
S
and multiples of
a_{i}\in S
which are powers of
2
, i.e.

\displaystyle V=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq\log(a_{0})}2^{j}a_{i}\right)

Obviously, every element of
V
is a feasible target value and also every sum of elements of
V
is a feasible target value.

Define the sequence
A
of length
a_{0}
by

\displaystyle A[i]=\min\{t\in V\mid t\equiv i\bmod a_{0}\}.

The sequence
A
can be computed in
O(a_{0}\log a_{0})
time in a straightforward way. Starting with
a_{1}
, each item
a_{i}
is iteratively multiplied by
2
and the value
2^{j}a_{i}
is stored in
A[2^{j}a_{i}\bmod a_{0}]
if it is smaller than its current content (the sequence is initialized by infinity values).

Compute the sequence

\displaystyle A^{*}=\underbrace{A\oplus_{a_{0}}\ldots\oplus_{a_{0}}A}_{\lfloor\log a_{0}\rfloor}

which is obtained by a
\lfloor\log a_{0}\rfloor
-fold mod-convolution of
A
.

Claim:
The sequence
A^{*}
is the solution to the
AllTargets
problem.

Clearly, each element
A^{*}[i]
is a feasible target value with
A^{*}[i]\equiv i\bmod a_{0}
as it is contained in the sum of elements of
V
.

On the other hand, let
t_{i}
be the minimum feasible target value with
t_{i}\equiv i\bmod a_{0}
.
By Lemma
1
we know that for each
t_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
there exists a solution
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(x_{i}+1)\leq\log a_{0}
(note that
x_{0}=0
otherwise,
t_{i}
would not be minimal). This implies that
t_{i}=x_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}x_{n}
can be written by the sum of

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(x_{i}+1)\leq\log a_{0}

many elements of
V
(by binary encoding, each multiplicity
x_{i}
of an element
a_{i}
is contained in the sum of at most
\log(x_{i}+1)
multiplicity elements
2^{j}a_{i}
contained in
V
). Therefore, every
t_{i}
is contained in the
\lfloor\log a_{0}\rfloor
-fold addition of
V
, i.e.
t_{i}\in\underbrace{V\oplus_{a_{0}}\ldots\oplus_{a_{0}}V}_{\lfloor\log a_{0}\rfloor}
which is equivalent to
\lfloor\log a_{0}\rfloor
-fold Min-Convolution of
A
and hence
t_{i}=A^{*}[i]
which proves the claim.

By using a binary exponentiation argument we can reduce the number of calls to the mod-convolution procedure further and compute
A^{*}
more efficiently. Therefore, define

\displaystyle B^{(i)}=\underbrace{A\oplus_{a_{0}}\ldots\oplus_{a_{0}}A}_{2^{i}}.

The sequences
B^{(i)}
can be computed efficiently using
i
calls to the mod-convolution procedure by
B^{(1)}=A
and
B^{(i)}=B^{(i-1)}\oplus_{a_{0}}B^{(i-1)}
for
i>1
. Finally,
A^{*}=B^{r}
is obtained for
r\geq\lceil\log\log a_{0}\rceil
. Note that as the entries of
A
are minimal, we have that
A^{*}\oplus_{a_{0}}A=A^{*}
and therefore it does not hurt to apply too many mod-convolutions on
A
.
∎

Using the famous algorithm of Williams
[
30
]
to solve
MinConv
in time
\frac{n^{2}}{2^{\Omega(\log n)^{1/2}}}
yields improved algorithms for the considered problems. By this we improve upon the algorithm of Böcker and Lipták

in the case that the number of items

for any constant

(note that the term

grows asymptotically faster than any polylogarithmic function).

Corollary 2

The

, the

and the

problem can be solved with a running time of

Furthermore, it is known that

2.3
Hardness of
Unbounded SubsetSum
and
Frobenius

In the following we show hardness of the
Unbounded SubsetSum
and the
Frobenius
problem by a reduction from
SubAdditivity Testing
, a problem that is known to be subquadratically equivalent to
MinConv
[
14
]
. The problem of
SubAdditivity Testing
is to decide for a given sequence
(a[i])_{i=0}^{n-1}
if there exist indices
i,j
such that
a[i]+a[j]<a[i+j\bmod n]
. We consider the modulo version of the problem which we call
Mod-SubAdditivity Testing
.

Mod-SubAdditivity Testing

Input:
Sequence
(a[i])_{i=0}^{n-1}

Objective:
Decide whether there exist indices
i,j
such that
a[i]+a[j]<a[i+j\bmod n]
.

In other words, the problem is to decide if a given sequence
a
is idempotent under a mod-min-convolution operation, i.e. if
a\oplus_{n}a=a
.

It is easy to see that
Mod-SubAdditivity Testing
is subquadratically equivalent to
SubAdditivity Testing
.

Proposition 1
.

If there exists an algorithm with a running time of
T(n)
for
SubAdditivity Testing
then there exists an algorithm with a running time of
T(2n)+O(n)
for
Mod-SubAdditivity Testing
and vice versa.

Proof.

Given is a sequence
(a[i])_{i=0}^{n-1}
in the
Mod-SubAdditivity Testing
problem. Construct a sequence
(b[i])_{i=0}^{2n-1}
of double length defined by

\displaystyle b[i]=a[i\bmod n]\quad\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,2n

There exist indices
i,j
such that
b[i]+b[j]<b[i+j]
if and only if there exist indices
i,j\leq n
with
a[i]+a[j]<a[i+j\bmod n]
. Therefore, an algorithm of running time
T(n)
for the
SubAdditivity Testing
problem implies an algorithm of running time
T(n)+O(n)
for the
Mod-SubAdditivity Testing
problem.

On the other hand, let
(a[i])_{i=1}^{n}
be a sequence in the
SubAdditivity Testing
problem. Construct a sequence
(b[i])_{i=1}^{2n}
of double length defined by

\displaystyle b[i]=\begin{cases}a[i]&\text{ for $i\leq n$}\\
-\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

There exist indices
i,j
such that
b[i]+b[j]<b[i+j]
if and only if there exist indices
i,j\leq n
with
a[i]+a[j]<a[i+j]
. Therefore, an algorithm of running time
T(n)
for the
Mod-SubAdditivity Testing
problem implies an algorithm of running time
T(n)+O(n)
for the
SubAdditivity Testing
problem.
∎

3
The Complexity of Parameterization by
a_{n}

In this section, we present algorithms for
Unbounded SubsetSum
,
Frobenius
and
AllTargets
parameterized by the largest item size
a_{n}
. An algorithm for
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of
O(a_{n}\log^{2}a_{n})
was already known. The algorithm by Jansen and Rohwedder
[
22
]
solves general feasibility IPs for given matrix
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}
and vector
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
of the form

\displaystyle Ax=b

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}

with a running time of
O((\sqrt{m}\left\|A\right\|_{\infty})^{m}\log\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}\cdot\log(\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|b\right\|_{\infty}))+O(nm)
. Using this algorithm for
m=1
in combination with the proximity result of Eisenbrand and Weismantel
[
17
]
in order to reduce
t
(i.e.
t\leq a_{n}^{2}
) leads to the mentioned running time for solving
Unbounded SubsetSum
.

In Section
3.1
we describe a modified and simplified version of the algorithm by Jansen and Rohwedder
[
22
]
for solving
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of
O(a_{n}\log^{2}a_{n})
. In contrast to their algorithm, our algorithm computes all feasible target values for a certain fixed set of intervals of length
a_{n}
. This has the advantage that our algorithm is able to detect if the Frobenius number of the instance is exceeded and terminates. By this we do not need to use the integer programming proximity to reduce the target value
t
and instead use a bound on the Frobenius number which is in general better. Therefore, our modified algorithm improves upon the running time if
F<a_{n}^{2}
. However, the main reason why we present this version of the algorithm is that we can show in Section
3.2
, that our modified algorithm can be easily adapted to compute a solution of the
Frobenius
problem.

In the algorithm, we compute the
sumset

S+T
of two sets
S,T\subset\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\displaystyle S+T=\{s+t\mid s\in S,t\in T\}.

For two sets
S\subseteq I\cap\mathbb{Z}
and
T\subseteq I^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Z}
that are contained in intervals
I,I^{\prime}
of size
n
, it is well known that the sumset
S+T
can be computed in time
O(n\log n)
. This can done by a reduction to boolean convolution which can be solved by an FFT approach (see also
[
22
,
12
]
).

Define the intervals

\displaystyle I^{(j)}=((j-1)a_{n},ja_{n}]

and let
A^{(j)}
be the set of all feasible target values within the interval
I^{(j)}
, i.e.

\displaystyle A^{(j)}=\{t\in I^{(j)}\mid\text{where (\ref{IP1}) is feasible}\}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle|A^{(1)}|<\ldots<|A^{(f)}|<|A^{(f+1)}|=n

holds, where
f
is the largest number such that
|A^{(f)}|<n
.
Each target value in the interval
I^{(f+1)}
is feasible, which implies that each target value in
|A^{(k)}|
for
k>f
has to be feasible as well.

In the following lemma we state a central property of the feasibility sets
A^{(i)}
that will be essential in the following algorithm as it allows to compute feasibility sets from other feasibility sets with smaller indices. A similar recursive argument was used in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2
.

For every
i,j\geq 1
the following equation holds:

\displaystyle A^{(i+j)}=(A^{(i)}+A^{(j)}+S)\cap I^{(i+j)}

Proof.

Let
t\in A^{(i+j)}
be a feasible target value. By definition, there exists a solution
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}a_{i}=t\leq(i+j)a_{n}
.
There exist subvectors
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\leq x
such that
x^{\prime}+x^{\prime\prime}+e_{k}=x
for some unit vector
e_{k}
and

\displaystyle t^{\prime}:=\sum_{l=0}^{n}x^{\prime}_{l}a_{l}<

\displaystyle~ia_{n},

\displaystyle t^{\prime\prime}:=\sum_{k=l}^{n}x^{\prime\prime}_{l}a_{l}<

\displaystyle~ja_{n},

\displaystyle t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}+a_{k}=

\displaystyle~t,

holds. Hence there exists a split of this sum into
t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}+a_{k}=t
for some
a_{k}\in S
,
t^{\prime}\in A^{(i)}
and
t^{\prime\prime}\in A^{(j)}
.
∎

Solving

Based on Lemma

\displaystyle O\Big(a_{n}\log a_{n}\cdot\log\big(\min\{\frac{t}{a_{n}};\frac{F}{a_{n}}\}\big)\Big)=O(a_{n}\log a_{n}\cdot\log(\frac{a_{n}}{n})).

Correctness

To show the correctness of Algorithm

The algorithm of Bringmann
[

The correctness of the other feasibility sets

AllTargets
can be solved in time

\displaystyle O(a_{n}^{3/2}\log^{1/2}a_{n}).

Proof.

Case:
n<(a_{n}\log a_{n})^{1/2}

In this case the algorithm of Böcker and Lipták
[
10
]
is used with a running time of

\displaystyle O(na_{0})=O(a_{n}^{3/2}\log^{1/2}a_{n}).

Case:
n\geq(a_{n}\log a_{n})^{1/2}

In this case the sets
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(1)},A^{(f+1)}
are computed with
|A^{(f)}|<n
and
|A^{(f+1)}|=n
, which requires a running time of
O(f\cdot a_{n}\log a_{n})
. By definition we know that
F\in A^{(f)}
and hence
f\leq F/a_{n}
. Using the bound by Erdös and Graham
[
18
]
yields

\displaystyle O(f\cdot a_{n}\log a_{n})=O(\frac{F}{a_{n}}\cdot a_{n}\log a_{n})=O(\frac{a_{n}^{2}\log a_{n}}{n})=O(a_{n}^{3/2}\log^{1/2}a_{n}).

∎

3.4
Hardness of Parameterization by
a_{n}

In a recent result by Abboud et al.
[
1
]
it was shown that under the SETH there exists no algorithm for
bounded subset sum
with a sublinear running time in
t
. Even more, the theorem also excludes the existence of an algorithm with a running time for example of the form
t^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\sqrt{n}}
. In their reduction they are not making use of the upper bounds of the variables which implies that the same hardness also holds for
Unbounded SubsetSum
. This hardness statement for
Unbounded SubsetSum
was made precise in
[
22
]
.

Theorem 8

(
[
1
]
,
[
22
]
)
.

Assuming SETH, then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that there exists no algorithm for
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of

\displaystyle O(t^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta n}).

Note that we can in general assume
t\geq a_{n}
as items with a size
>t
can be removed from the instance. This implies that the same hardness statement can be made for the parameter
a_{n}
instead of
t
, which shows that Algorithm
1
is (apart from logarithmic factors) nearly optimal under the assumption of the SETH.
In the following, we will show that we can derive the same hardness statement for
Frobenius
which shows that Algorithm
2
is nearly optimal.

In a classical paper by Ramirez-Alfonsin
[
28
]
it was shown that the
Frobenius
problem is NP-hard. The author gave a computational reduction from
Unbounded SubsetSum
which
showed that
Unbounded SubsetSum
can be solved for a given target value
t
by solving only at most four instances of
Frobenius
. In those instances the following item sizes were used:

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{i}=2a_{i}\qquad\qquad\text{ for }0\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{n+1}=2F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})+1

(3)

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{n+2}=F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n},\bar{a}_{n+1})-2t

The central theorem that is being used in the reduction is the following:

Theorem 9

(Ramirez-Alfonsin
[
28
]
)
.

For given
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
t\leq F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})
there exists a solution of (
1
) if and only if

\displaystyle F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n},\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n+2})<F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n},\bar{a}_{n+1}).

Based on the above theorem one can solve
Unbounded SubsetSum
by first computing whether
t>F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, in which case
t
is feasible and in the case that
t\leq F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
the algorithm computes
F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n},\bar{a}_{n+1},\bar{a}_{n+2})
and
F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n},\bar{a}_{n+1})
in order to decide by Theorem
9
if
t
is feasible.

The reason that we can not directly use this reduction in combination with Theorem
8
is that the item sizes defined in (
3.4
) are potentially larger than
a_{n}
or
t
. This is because
F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})
can only be bounded by a quadratic term in
a_{n}
. In the following theorem we show how to avoid this quadratic dependency. By adding only
O(\log t)
many additional items we can show that the Frobenius number of the new instance can be bounded by
O(t\log t)
.

Theorem 10
.

Assuming SETH, for any
\epsilon
there exists a
\delta>0
such that
Frobenius
can not be solved in time

\displaystyle O(a_{n}^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta n}).

Proof.

Assume that there exists an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
for
Frobenius
. Then we can use it to develop an algorithm
\mathcal{B}
for
Unbounded SubsetSum
solving an instance
\mathcal{I}
with given target value
t
and item sizes
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
. Without loss of generality, we thereby assume that all item sizes are less than
t
, i.e.
a_{n}\leq t
. Define a new instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
of
Unbounded SubsetSum
with additional item sizes

\displaystyle a_{n+i}=Ra_{0}+(2^{i}\bmod a_{0})\qquad\text{ for }0\leq i\leq\log a_{0},

where
R\in\mathbb{Z}
is the smallest value with
Ra_{0}>t
.
The instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
has
\log a_{0}\leq\log t
additional items and each item is bounded in size by
Ra_{0}+\log a_{0}\leq 2t+\log a_{0}\leq 3t
.
Since the newly defined item sizes exceed
t
, we know that
t
is a feasible target value for instance
\mathcal{I}
if and only if
t
is a feasible target value for the instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.

Claim:
The Frobenius number of instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is bounded by

\displaystyle F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n^{\prime}})<O(t\log t).

Consider a target value
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
t^{\prime}>3t\log t
. We will see that
t^{\prime}
can be written by a subset of the items
a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n^{\prime}}
and multiples of the item
a_{0}
.
Let
t^{\prime\prime}\equiv t^{\prime}\bmod a_{0}
and let
I
be the set of indices with a
1
in the binary encoding of
t^{\prime\prime}
. Then

t^{\prime\prime}\equiv t^{\prime}\equiv\sum_{i\in I}2^{i}\equiv\sum_{i\in I}a_{t+i}\bmod a_{0}
.
Since each
a_{t+i}\leq 3t
we know that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}a_{t+i}\leq 3t\log t\leq t^{\prime}

and hence

\displaystyle t^{\prime}=Ka_{0}+\sum_{i\in I}a_{t+i}

for some
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
which proves the claim.

Knowing that the Frobenius number of instances
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is bounded by
O(t\log t)
, we can use the reduction of
[
28
]
to show the SETH based hardness for
Frobenius
. Algorithm
5
solves
Unbounded SubsetSum
by using
Frobenius
as a subroutine.

Algorithm 5

Solving
Unbounded SubsetSum

[
28
]

1.

If
t>F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
return that
t
is feasible.

2.

Compute item sizes
\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}+2}
.

3.

Return that
t
is feasible if

\displaystyle F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}},\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}+1},\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}+2})<F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}},\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}+1}).

Otherwise return that
t
is not feasible.

Since
F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n^{\prime}})
is bounded by
O(t\log t)
, by definition of the item sizes in (
3.4
) the item sizes
\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}+2}
are also bounded by
O(t\log t)
. Note that according to
[
28
]
we have
F(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n^{\prime}})=4F(\bar{a}_{0},\ldots,\bar{a}_{n^{\prime}+1})
.

By Theorem
8
, we know that for an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that there is no algorithm with a running time
O(t^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta n})
for
Unbounded SubsetSum
. Here, we can assume that
\delta<\epsilon
as the non-existence of an algorithm with a running time of
O(t^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta n})
for
\delta\geq\epsilon
excludes the existence of an algorithm with improved running time of
O(t^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\epsilon n})
.

Assume now that there exists an algorithm with a running time of
O(a_{n}^{1-2\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta n})
for
Frobenius
. Then by the above construction we know that there also exists an algorithm for
Unbounded SubsetSum
with a running time of

\displaystyle O((t\log t)^{1-2\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta(n+\log t)})

\displaystyle=O((t\log t)^{1-2\epsilon}\cdot t^{\delta}\cdot 2^{\delta n})

\displaystyle=O((t\log t)^{1-2\epsilon+\delta}\cdot 2^{\delta n})

\displaystyle\overset{\delta<\epsilon}{=}O(t^{1-\epsilon}\cdot 2^{\delta n}).

However, this contradicts the assumption that no such algorithms exists for
Unbounded SubsetSum
.
∎

4
Conclusion

We prove nearly matching upper and lower bounds for
Unbounded SubsetSum
,
Frobenius
and
AllTargets
parameterized by
a_{0}
and
a_{n}
respectively. The only major gap left in the open is regarding the complexity of
AllTargets
parameterized by
a_{n}
. To us it seems that an algorithm beating the running time of
O(a_{n}^{3/2})
is possible but very sophisticated techniques are required, like an improvement to
MinConv
for the case that the entries of the sequence are bounded or sophisticated structural properties to the solution space are needed. An algorithm with a running time of
O(M^{1-\epsilon}a_{n})
for
MinConv
with entries bounded by
M
for example would improve upon the complexity of
AllTargets
. A hardness result showing that
AllTargets
can not be solved in linear time would also be very interesting as this would imply that the complexity of a parameterization of
a_{0}
and
a_{n}
of the considered problems would behave differently.

Furthermore, it would also be interesting to improve upon the logarithmic factors for the algorithms parameterized by
a_{n}
or to show that this is not possible under certain hardness assumptions. Note that a running time of
O(a_{n}\log a_{n})
is already necessary to apply a single FFT to an interval
I
of length
a_{n}
.

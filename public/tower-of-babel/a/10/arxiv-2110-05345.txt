A Note on Twisted Crossed Products and Spectral Triples
P. Antonini, D. Guido, T. Isola, A. Rubin

Abstract
. Starting with a spectral triple on a unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra A A with an action of a discrete group G G , if the action is uniformly bounded (in a Lipschitz sense) a spectral triple on the reduced crossed product C ∗ C^{*} -algebra A ⋊ r G A\rtimes_{r}G is constructed in [ 23 ] . The main instrument is the Kasparov external product. We note that this construction still works for twisted crossed products when the twisted action is uniformly bounded in the appropriate sense. Under suitable assumptions we discuss some basic properties of the resulting triples: summability and regularity. Noncommutative coverings with finite abelian structure group are among the most basic, still interesting, examples of twisted crossed products; we describe their main features.

Introduction

Spectral triples on
C^{*}
-algebras are a central notion in noncommutative geometry, being modeled on the geometric structure codified by the properties of a Dirac type operator on a smooth manifold. The range of applicability
of this paradigm is vast, going from the foundational example of Spin manifolds to foliated manifolds, group
C^{*}
-algebras, quantum groups, quantum deformations and fractals. This list is not exhaustive and we address the reader to
[
10
,
20
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
, the recent works
[
9
,
40
]
and the references therein.

Following the stream pursued by many authors
[
23
,
35
,
7
,
28
]
, in this paper we construct and study spectral triples on reduced twisted crossed products
A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}_{r}G
, where
A
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra,
G
a discrete group and
(\rho,\sigma)
a twisted action in the sense of Busby and Smith
[
8
]
. Two facts brought us to consider twisted crossed products: on the one hand, the crossed product construction is fundamental in the theory of
C^{*}
-algebras and in
noncommutative topology since it replaces the operation of forming a quotient when this is a singular, bad-behaved space.
On the other hand, the study of noncommutative coverings with finite abelian structure group (that two of the authors developed in
[
1
]
as
building blocks
of solenoidal noncommutative metric spaces) brings naturally to the conclusion that in the case of finite abelian groups, any twisted crossed product is a regular covering and any regular covering is a twisted crossed product (Theorem
5.15
, previously proven with a different language in
[
44
]
). This makes the problem of the construction of spectral triples on crossed products a natural subject of interest both for the creation of a new large class of examples and for the analysis of the Connes axioms in the case of noncommutative quotient manifolds
[
12
,
11
,
13
]
.

In constructing a spectral triple on a twisted crossed product, we follow, as in
[
23
]
, the guiding principle of the Kasparov external product, combining the given Dirac operator on
A
with a length function on the group. We have decided to utilize matrix valued length functions because, as pointed out in
[
23
]
, these turn out to be useful in the procedure of iterating crossed products.

We pass now to describe the content and the results of the paper giving more details and some comments.

In section 2 we follow
[
8
,
34
,
6
,
41
]
and give a concise account on the basic theory of twisted actions of discrete groups on unital
C^{*}
-algebras, twisted covariant representations and the twisted crossed product construction.
The choice of considering the unital case is dictated by the interest in constructing unital spectral triples and simplifies a bit the presentation.

In section 3 we construct spectral triples on twisted crossed products. We begin by discussing generalities about spectral triples and translation bounded and proper matrix valued length functions. Here and in the following
V
will always denote a finite dimensional Hermitian vector space so that matrix valued length functions will be maps

\bm{\ell}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{B}_{h}(V)
on the discrete group
G
and valued in the finite dimensional selfadjoint operators.
Such maps have been
introduced in
[
23
]
and generalise scalar length functions on groups. With them we can create spectral triples on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}G
of the form
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
with Dirac operator having as coefficients
elements of
\mathbb{B}_{h}(V)
.
The triple
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
is the prototypical triple on a crossed product and we study its summability properties.
To do so, we first propose
(definition
3.4
)
the notion of polynomial growth for a discrete (finitely generated) group with respect to a length function which is matrix valued. This naturally extends the usual concept of polynomial growth with respect to some word metric.
We then show (proposition
3.5
) that
the triple

(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
is
finitely summable if and only if the group has polynomial growth with respect to
\bm{\ell}
.

The construction of spectral triples on twisted crossed products is the content of Theorem
3.7
and generalizes the one in
[
23
]
.
Given a group with a proper, translation bounded matrix valued length function
(G,\bm{\ell})
, a twisted action
(\rho,\sigma)
of
G
on the
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and a spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D)
on
A
, our basic assumptions (summarized in the setup
3.1
) to extend this triple to
A\rtimes_{r}^{(\rho,\sigma)}G
are the following:

(1)

the twisted action is smooth. This means that the twisting pair
(\rho,\sigma)
is valued in the smooth algebra,

(2)

a uniform boundedness condition for the action:

\sup_{x\in G}\|[D,\pi(\rho_{x}(a)\sigma_{x,y})]\|<\infty

is satisfied for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
y\in G
.

The second condition above is the twisted version of the one appearing in
[
23
]
; it
ensures boundedness of the commutators between the Dirac operator and the elements of the (smooth) twisted convolution algebra. Moreover, we prove that if the starting triple on
A
is finitely summable and the group has polynomial growth with respect to the length function, then also the extended triple is finitely summable. The abscissa of convergence is bounded by the sum of the abscissa of convergence of the starting triple and the group growth.

In the remaining part of the section we examine the particularly favorable, still interesting, situation in which the representation
\pi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)
associated to the starting triple
(A,H,D)
is also twisted covariant with respect to the twisted action
\rho
of
G
on
A
and the Dirac operator has bounded commutators with the unitaries
U_{g}
implementing the group action on
H
.
Under this assumption, we describe what we call the
equivariant construction
. This was considered in remark 2.9 in
[
23
]
,
where it was related to the equivariant Kasparov product.
In this case a second construction of a spectral triple on a twisted crossed product can be given. It differs from the previous one only in the representation so that, under a condition of uniform boundedness of the commutators between the Dirac operator and the unitaries
U_{g}
, this second construction is a bounded perturbation of the first one (up to unitary equivalence). In particular the
K
-homological information carried by the two is unchanged.
Using the natural morphism
\phi_{A}^{r}:A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G\rightarrow(A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G)\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G)
,
we notice
(proposition
3.11
)
that the spectral triple obtained in the equivariant case can be seen as the restriction of an external product of two spectral triples: the first one is a triple on the algebra
B
given by the image of the twisted crossed product under the integrated form of the starting representation, and the second one is the (standard) spectral triple
(C^{*}_{r}(G),\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
.

In section 4 we continue the study of the spectral triple constructed in the equivariant case. Since the triple is a restriction of an exterior product and the product of regular triples is regular, we can examine its regularity properties. After giving some generalities about regularity of spectral triples, we prove theorem
4.5
giving sufficient conditions ensuing the regularity of the spectral triple in the equivariant construction.
The regularity of a spectral triple being a strong property, these assumptions are, though natural, quite restrictive and demand a strict compatibility
between
D
and the group action. Nevertheless, interesting examples can be found even in the case of ordinary (untwisted) crossed products.

Section 5 is devoted to our main example of a twisted crossed product: noncommutative coverings with finite abelian structure group, which consist of an action of a finite abelian group
G
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
. Then we say that
B

covers
the algebra of fixed points
A:=B^{G}
. We study in detail the structure of these noncommutative coverings, reserving particular attention to the notion of
rank-
1
regularity
, a property formulated in terms of the spectral subspaces of the action, that was previously called
regularity
in
[
1
]
. In particular, we relate it with the concept of freeness of the group action and we give examples. When the action is
rank-
1
regular, a twisted action of the dual group
\widehat{G}
can be found so that
B\cong A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}\widehat{G}
. The last section gives an example of the construction of a spectral triple on a twisted crossed product.

Appendix A contains some facts about the infinitesimal order of compact operators that have been used in section
3
, and Appendix B deals with the notion of regularity for spectral triples.

2.
Twisted Crossed Products

We briefly recall some basic facts about twisted crossed products following mainly
[
8
,
34
,
6
,
41
]
. We denote by
A
a separable unital
C^{*}
-algebra and by
G
a discrete group with neutral element
e
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
twisting pair

\left(\rho,\sigma\right)
for
A
and
G
is a couple of maps
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\textup{Aut}(A)
and
\sigma\colon G\times G\rightarrow U(A)
satisfying:

(1)

\rho_{x}(\sigma_{y,z})=\sigma_{x,y}\sigma_{xy,z}\sigma_{x,yz}^{*}

(2)

\rho_{x}\circ\rho_{y}=\operatorname{Ad}(\sigma_{x,y})\circ\rho_{xy}

(3)

\sigma_{x,e}=\sigma_{e,x}=1
,
\rho_{e}=\operatorname{id}_{A}
.

for all
x,y,z\in G
. We call
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
a
twisted dynamical system
. The map
\sigma
is called a
2-cocycle
with values in
U(A)
.

Given a twisted dynamical system
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
, we define a twisted convolution
\ast
-algebra structure on the space

C_{c}(G,A)

of the finitely supported functions from
G
to
A
by

(f\star g)(x)=\sum_{y\in G}f(y)\rho_{y}(g(y^{-1}x))\sigma_{y,y^{-1}x},\quad f^{*}(x)=\sigma_{x,x^{-1}}^{*}\rho_{x}(f(x^{-1})^{*}),

for
f,g\in C_{c}(G,A)
.
In terms of the
\delta
-functions, for
x,y\in G
we have:

(a\delta_{x})\star(b\delta_{y})=a\rho_{x}(b)\sigma_{x,y}\delta_{xy},\quad(a\delta_{x})^{*}=\sigma^{*}_{x^{-1},x}\rho_{x^{-1}}(a^{*})\delta_{x^{-1}}.

Such rules extend to
L^{1}(G,A)
making it the Banach
\ast
-algebra
L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
twisted covariant representation
of
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
on a Hilbert space
H
is a couple
(\pi,U)
, where
\pi\colon A\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)
is a non degenerate representation and
U:G\rightarrow U(H)
satisfies

(1)

U_{x}U_{y}=\pi(\sigma_{x,y})U_{xy}
,

(2)

\pi\circ\rho_{x}=\operatorname{Ad}(U_{x})\circ\pi,

for every
x,y\in G
.

It follows from the first relation that
U_{e}=\operatorname{Id}
. We have a
1-1
correspondence between twisted covariant representations and non degenerate representations of
L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
: to the twisted covariant representation
(\pi,U)
one associates its integrated form

\Pi:L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)
such that

\Pi\Big(\sum_{x\in G}a_{x}\delta_{x}\Big):=\sum_{x\in G}\pi(a_{x})U_{x}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.3
.

A twisted crossed product for
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
is the following universal object: a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
equipped with a unital morphism
i_{A}:A\rightarrow B
and a map
i_{G}:G\rightarrow U(B)
such that

(1)

The couple
(i_{A},i_{G})
is covariant in the sense that

\quad i_{G}(x)i_{G}(y)=i_{A}(\sigma_{x,y})i_{G}(xy)\quad\textrm{and}\quad i_{A}\circ\rho_{x}=\operatorname{Ad}(i_{G}(x))\circ i_{A}\quad

for every
x,y\in G.

(2)

For every covariant representation
(\pi,U)
of
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
on the Hilbert space
H
there is a non degenerate representation
\pi\times U:B\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)
making the diagram

\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{U}
\scriptstyle{i_{G}}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi\times U}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\scriptstyle{i_{A}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{B}(H)}

commute.

A twisted crossed product exists and is unique up to isomorphisms which take into account also the couple
(i_{A},i_{G})
(see
[
34
]
). It will be denoted with
A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G
or, when the context is clear, just with
A\rtimes G
. Moreover, under our assumptions (making the algebra
L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
unital), it is generated by the image of the integration map

i_{A}\times i_{G}:L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma)\rightarrow A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G,\quad i_{A}\times i_{G}\left(\sum_{x\in G}a_{x}\delta_{x}\right)=\sum_{x\in G}i_{A}(a_{x})i_{G}(x)

and coincides with the enveloping
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma))
. In particular the representation
\pi\times U
induced by
any covariant representation is equal, on the image of
i_{A}\times i_{G}
, to the representation (
2.1
) and will be called the
integrated form
of
(\pi,U)
as well.

Example 2.4

(Clifford Algebras)
.

As proved in
[
2
]
, we can regard (complex) Clifford algebras as twisted crossed products of
\mathbb{C}
with a suitable finite group. Indeed,
\mathbb{C}\ell_{n}
is the complex algebra generated by the skew-adjoint anti-commuting elements
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
such that
e^{2}_{i}=-1
and graded in the standard way. Consider the cyclic multiplicative group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and let
g=-1
be its generator; any element in
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
is of the form
x=(g^{x_{1}},\dots,g^{x_{n}})
where
x_{i}=0,1\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Define

\sigma_{n}(x,y)=(-1)^{\sum_{j<i}x_{i}y_{j}}

for
x,y\in G
and let
\mathbb{C}\rtimes^{\textup{id},\sigma_{n}}G
be the twisted group algebra for the trivial action of
G
on
\mathbb{C}
. Then the map
\mathbb{C}\rtimes^{\textup{id},\sigma_{n}}G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\ell_{n}
, given on a basis by

(g^{x_{1}},\dots,g^{x_{n}})\longmapsto(ie_{1})^{x_{1}}\cdots(ie_{n})^{x_{n}},

is an isomorphism of
C^{*}
-algebras.

\Box

We will be concerned with the reduced twisted crossed product which is defined by a particular class of representations.
One starts with a faithful representation
\pi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)
, then a canonical
covariant representation
(\widetilde{\pi},\widetilde{R})
on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(G,H)
is defined by

\widetilde{\pi}(a)(\xi\otimes\delta_{x})=\pi(\rho_{x}(a))\xi\otimes\delta_{x},\quad\widetilde{R}_{x}(\xi\otimes\delta_{y})=\pi(\sigma_{yx^{-1},x})\xi\otimes\delta_{yx^{-1}}

given on simple tensors of
\ell^{2}(G,H)\simeq H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)
and for
a\in A
.
This is the right induced covariant representation;
its integrated form
\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{R}:A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G,H))
reads as

\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{R}\Big(\sum_{x\in G}a_{x}\delta_{x}\Big)(\xi\otimes\delta_{y})=\sum_{x\in G}\pi(\rho_{yx^{-1}}(a_{x})\sigma_{yx^{-1},x})\xi\otimes\delta_{yx^{-1}},

on the elements
\sum_{x\in G}a_{x}\delta_{x}\in L^{1}(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
. It is called the regular representation of the twisted crossed product.

Definition 2.5
.

The reduced twisted crossed product

A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}_{r}G
(or just
A\rtimes_{r}G
)
is the
C^{*}
-algebra image of the regular representation.

It can be shown that the reduced crossed product is independent of the choice of the faithful representation
\pi

[
34
, remark 3.12]
.

We have presented the theory with the right induced representations as in
[
8
,
34
]
. However, for the construction of spectral triples it will be useful, to reconcile with the existing literature, to consider a different version of the regular representation. This will be obtained by conjugation with the unitary involution
V_{G}\colon\ell^{2}(G,H)\rightarrow\ell^{2}(G,H)
given by
V_{G}(\xi\otimes\delta_{x})=\xi\otimes\delta_{x^{-1}}
. We get in this way,
out of the faithful representation
\pi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)
,
the representation

\Pi:A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G\rightarrow\ell^{2}(G,H),
given by

\Pi(a\delta_{x})(\xi\otimes\delta_{y})=\pi(\rho_{y^{-1}x^{-1}}(a)\sigma_{y^{-1}x^{-1},x})\,\xi\otimes\delta_{xy},

(2.2)

which is the integrated form of
a covariant representation
(\widetilde{\pi},\widetilde{L})
with

\widetilde{\pi}(a)(\xi\otimes\delta_{x})=\pi(\rho_{x^{-1}}(a))\xi\otimes\delta_{x},\quad\widetilde{L}_{x}(\xi\otimes\delta_{y})=\pi(\sigma_{y^{-1}x^{-1},x})\xi\otimes\delta_{xy}.

(2.3)

So in the following
we shall consider the reduced crossed product as defined by
\Pi
and call it the left regular representation (induced by
\pi
).
This version of the reduced crossed product is of course isomorphic to the one given before.

Observe that the previous construction of (
\widetilde{\pi}
,
\widetilde{L}
) can be done even though the representation
\pi
is not faithful. However, in this case, it does not define the reduced crossed product.

3.
Spectral Triples on Twisted Crossed Products

In this section we show how to construct a spectral triple on a twisted crossed product
A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}_{r}G
starting from a spectral triple on

satisfying some assumptions.
The idea, as in

]
, is to borrow the Dirac operator from an unbounded representative for the external Kasparov product with a spectral triple on the group algebra

which is constructed with a (matrix valued) length function.

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

An

An

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}(\Dom{D})\subseteq\Dom{D}

(4)

3.1.
Length Functions and Spectral Triples on Groups

Let

be a finite dimensional Hilbert space
(usually taken even dimensional)

Definition 3.2
.

A function

{\bm{\ell}}(g)=0

(3)

Example 3.3
.

Let us construct such functions on the group

1

on a group
G
we define an odd spectral triple

(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}}).

(3.2)

The representation is obtained by amplification of the left regular representation
\lambda_{g}\delta_{x}=\delta_{gx}
; thus

\lambda(\delta_{g})(\delta_{x}\otimes v):=\delta_{gx}\otimes v

while the Dirac operator is just the multiplication action of
\bm{\ell}

initially defined on a dense subspace; concretely

M_{\bm{\ell}}(\delta_{x}\otimes v)=\delta_{x}\otimes\bm{\ell}(x)v

on
\mathbb{C}G\otimes V
(algebraic tensor product).
The assumption (
1
) in definition

3.2
implies that
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
is non-degenerate.

The eigenvectors of the Dirac operator for the spectral triple
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
are the vectors
\delta_{g}\otimes v_{j}(g)\in\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V
, where the
v_{j}(g)
’s are the eigenvectors of
\bm{\ell}(g)
. The family of singular values (with multiplicity) for
M_{\bm{\ell}}
is then given by

\{s_{j}(g),(g,j)\in G\times\{1,\dots,\dim V\}\}
.

Let us now discuss when the spectral triple
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
is finitely summable. Let us set

B_{n}=\{g\in G:\min\operatorname{Sp}(|{\bm{\ell}}(g)|)\leq n\}.

Note that the translation function
\bm{\ell}
being proper,
\#B_{n}<\infty
. In particular
G
is at most countable.

Definition 3.4
.

We shall say that
G
has polynomial growth w.r.t. the function
\bm{\ell}
if
\#B_{n}
grows at most polynomially for
n\to\infty
. In this case we call
growth of
G
the number

d_{G}=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(\#B_{n})}{\log n},

(cf. e.g
[
33
]
for the case of the word length for finitely generated groups).

For any
g\in G
, we denote by
s_{j}(g)
,
j=1,\dots\dim V
, the singular values (with multiplicity) of
\bm{\ell}(g)
and set

\Sigma_{n}\coloneqq\{(g,k)\in G\times\{1,\dots,\dim V\}:s_{k}(g)<n\}.

On the one hand
\Sigma_{n}\subset B_{n}\times\{1,\dots,\dim V\}
, therefore
\#\Sigma_{n}\leq\#B_{n}\cdot\dim V
.
On the other hand, for any
g\in B_{n}
there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,\dim V\}
such that
(g,k)\in\Sigma_{n+1}
, therefore
\#B_{n}\leq\#\Sigma_{n+1}
. This implies that

\displaystyle d_{G}=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(\#\Sigma_{n})}{\log n}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The spectral triple
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
is finitely summable iff
G
has polynomial growth w.r.t. the proper translation bounded function
\bm{\ell}
. In this case,
\textup{abs}(\zeta_{D})
coincides with the growth of
G
.

Proof.

By definition,

\zeta_{M_{\bm{\ell}}}(s)=\tr\big((I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})^{-s/2}\big)
and

\textup{abs}(\zeta_{M_{\bm{\ell}}})=\inf\{s\geq 0:\zeta_{M_{\bm{\ell}}}(s)<\infty\}.

Therefore
the equality

\textup{abs}(\zeta_{M_{\bm{\ell}}})=\displaystyle\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(\lambda_{1/n}((I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})^{-1/2}))}{\log n}

follows by theorem
A.1
.
We then observe that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1/n}((I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\#\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\mu_{k}((I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})^{-1/2})>1/n\}

\displaystyle=\#\{(g,k)\in G\times\{1,\dots,\dim V\}:\sqrt{1+s_{k}(g)^{2}}<n\}

and, since
|M_{\bm{\ell}}|\leq(I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})^{1/2}\leq I+|M_{\bm{\ell}}|
,

\#\Sigma_{n-1}\leq\#\{(g,k)\in G\times\{1,\dots,\dim V\}:\sqrt{1+s_{k}(g)^{2}}\leq n\}\leq\#\Sigma_{n}.

Finally,

d_{G}=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(\#\Sigma_{n})}{\log n}=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(\lambda_{1/n}((I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})^{-1/2}))}{\log n}=\textup{abs}(\zeta_{M_{\bm{\ell}}}).

∎

3.2.
Spectral Triples From Twisted Actions

Let
G
be a discrete group with a twisted action
(\rho,\sigma)
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. We assume that a spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D)
with corresponding representation

\pi:A\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)

has been fixed on
A
and that
\pi
is faithful.
Here
\mathcal{A}\subset A
is a smooth dense subalgebra. We give the following definitions generalising the untwisted case
[
23
]
.

Definition 3.6
.

We say that the twisted action
(\rho,\sigma)
is
smooth
if it restricts to a twisted action on
\mathcal{A}
. In other words
\rho:G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
and
\sigma:G\times G\rightarrow U(\mathcal{A})
.

We consider the following setup for the construction of spectral triples.

Setup 3.1
.

Assume we have fixed:

(1)

a spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D)
on the
C^{*}
-algebra
A
.

(2)

A twisted smooth action
(\rho,\sigma)
on
A
such that

\sup_{x\in G}\|[D,\pi(\rho_{x}(a)\sigma_{x,y})]\|<\infty

(3.3)

for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
y\in G
.

(3)

A proper translation bounded length function
{\bm{\ell}}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{B}_{h}(V)
.

Condition (
3.3
) generalizes to the twisted case the one used in
[
23
]
which, when the triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D)
satisfies the Lipschitz condition, corresponds to a metrically
equicontinuous action
.

If the spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D,\pi)
is odd,
consider the amplification
\pi\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}(G,V)}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(H\otimes\ell^{2}(G,V))
of the representation. Then we have the (left) induced representation
\Pi
of
A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G
on
H\otimes\ell^{2}(G,V)\cong\ell^{2}(G,V\otimes H)
. From formula (
2.2
) it follows

\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})(\xi\otimes\delta_{y}\otimes v)=\pi\big(\rho_{y^{-1}x^{-1}}(a_{x})\sigma_{y^{-1}x^{-1},x}\big)\xi\otimes\delta_{xy}\otimes v

for the basic elements
a_{x}\delta_{x}
,
for
v\in V
and
\xi\in H
.
We are ready to construct the spectral triple following
[
23
]
. Notice that if the twisted action is smooth it makes sense to consider the twisted convolution algebra
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
dense in the reduced twisted crossed product.

Theorem 3.7
.

Under the assumptions in setup
3.1
:

(1a)

[Odd to even case]
. Assume that the spectral triple on
A
is odd. Then we have an induced even spectral triple

(C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A}),{\widetilde{H}},\widetilde{D})

on
A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}_{r}{G}
with
\widetilde{H}=(H\otimes\ell^{2}({G})\otimes V)\oplus(H\otimes\ell^{2}({G})\otimes V)
, the representation is the direct sum
\Pi\oplus\Pi
and the Dirac operator has the form

\widetilde{D}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&D\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}-i1_{{H}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}\\
D\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}+i1_{{H}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}&0\end{matrix}\right).

(3.4)

This spectral triple is non-degenerate provided
(\mathcal{A},H,D,\pi)
is non-degenerate.

(1b)

[Even to odd case]
. If the spectral triple on
A
is even with
{H}={H}^{+}\oplus{H}^{-}
, representation
\pi=\pi^{+}\oplus\pi^{-}
and
D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{array}\right)

then we can construct an odd spectral triple

(C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})\,\widetilde{{H}},\widetilde{D})

on
A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}_{r}G
with Hilbert space
\widetilde{{H}}=({H}^{+}\otimes\ell^{2}({G})\otimes V)\oplus({H}^{-}\otimes\ell^{2}({G})\otimes V)
and the representation
\Pi
is induced by
\pi
. It is diagonal whose components are induced by
\pi^{\pm}
. The Dirac operator is:

\widetilde{D}=\left(\begin{matrix}1_{{H}^{+}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}&D^{-}\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}\\
D^{+}\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}&-1_{{H}^{-}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}\end{matrix}\right).

(3.5)

This spectral triple is non-degenerate if the triple on
A
is.

(2)

[Finite summability]
. If the triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)
is finitely summable and
G
has polynomial growth w.r.t. the translation bounded proper function
\bm{\ell}
, then also the spectral triple

A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}_{r}G
is finitely summable and

\textup{abs}(\zeta_{\widetilde{D}})\leq\textup{abs}(\zeta_{D})+d_{G}.

(3.6)

If
\lim_{n}\frac{\log(\#B_{n})}{\log n}
exists, then the equality in (
3.6
) holds true.

We notice that if the group
G
is finitely generated and
\bm{\ell}(g)
is given by the word length, then
d_{G}
does not depend on the choice of the generators and the limit
\lim_{n}\frac{\log(\#B_{n})}{\log n}
exists.

Proof.

We write the proof for the “odd to even”  case, the other one being similar.
As in
[
23
]
, most of the properties will follow because we are following the prescription for the exterior Kasparov product.
In particular the Dirac operator is a sum
\widetilde{D}=D_{1}+iD_{2}
with the two blocks

D_{1}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&D\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}\\
D\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad D_{2}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1_{{H}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}\\
1_{{H}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}&0\end{matrix}\right).

The smooth algebra
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
preserves the domain of
\widetilde{D}
and it allows us to estimate the commutators

[D_{1},\Pi\oplus\Pi(a)]
and
[D_{2},\Pi\oplus\Pi(a)],

for
a\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
separately. Of course we just have to compute these commutators for
a=a_{x}\delta_{x}
. It follows by a straightforward computation that

[D_{1},\Pi\oplus\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})]\left(\begin{matrix}\xi\otimes\delta_{y}\otimes v\\
\eta\otimes\delta_{z}\otimes w\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\big[D,\pi(\rho_{z^{-1}x^{-1}}(a_{x})\sigma_{z^{-1}x^{-1},x})\big]\eta\otimes\delta_{xz}\otimes w\\
\big[D,\pi(\rho_{y^{-1}x^{-1}}(a_{x})\sigma_{y^{-1}x^{-1},x}\big]\xi\otimes\delta_{xy}\otimes v\end{matrix}\right)

which is bounded by condition (
3.3
). Concerning the second commutator we have

[D_{2},\Pi\oplus\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})]=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\big[1_{H}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}},\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})\otimes 1_{V}\big]\\
\big[1_{H}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}},\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})\otimes 1_{V}\big]&0\end{array}\right).

Now

\big[1_{H}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}},\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})\otimes 1_{V}\big](\xi\otimes\delta_{y}\otimes v)=\Pi(a_{x}\delta_{x})(\xi\otimes\delta_{y})\otimes(\bm{\ell}(xy)-\bm{\ell}(y))v

which is bounded by the traslation boundedness of the length function.
Compactness of the resolvent and non degenerateness follow from the corresponding statements for the Kasparov exterior product
[
5
]
.

We finally prove the summability properties. We shall use the notations and results of Appendix
A
.
Let us observe that, if
T
is a positive invertible operator with compact inverse and if we denote by
N_{t}(T)
the number (with multiplicity) of the eigenvalues of
T
lower than
t
, then we have

N_{t}(T)=\#\{n\geq 0:\mu_{n}(T^{-1})>t\}
and
o(T^{-1})=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(N_{t}(T))}{\log t}
.
Therefore

\textup{abs}(\zeta_{\widetilde{D}})=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(N_{t}((I+\widetilde{D}^{2})^{1/2}))}{\log t}.

Since
\widetilde{D}^{2}=\big(I\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2}+D^{2}\otimes I\big)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

and the eigenvalues of
I\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2}+D^{2}\otimes I
are
\mu^{2}+\nu^{2}
where
\mu
, resp.
\nu
is a singular value of
M_{\bm{\ell}}
, resp.
D
, we have

N_{t}((I+\widetilde{D}^{2})^{1/2}))=2N_{t}\big(\sqrt{I\otimes(I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})+D^{2}\otimes I}\big)

and

\begin{split}N_{t/\sqrt{2}}(\sqrt{I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2}})N_{t/\sqrt{2}}(\sqrt{I+D^{2}})&\leq N_{t}\big(\sqrt{I\otimes(I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2})+D^{2}\otimes I}\big)\\
&\leq N_{t}(\sqrt{I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2}})N_{t}(\sqrt{I+D^{2}}),\end{split}

hence

\displaystyle\textup{abs}(\zeta_{\widetilde{D}})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(N_{t}((I+\widetilde{D}^{2})^{1/2}))}{\log t}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\Bigg(\frac{\log(N_{t}(\sqrt{I+M_{\bm{\ell}}^{2}}))}{\log t}+\frac{\log(N_{t}(\sqrt{I+D^{2}}))}{\log t}\Bigg)\leq d_{G}+\textup{abs}(\zeta_{D})

where the equality holds if any of the limits exists.
∎

3.3.
The Equivariant Construction

In the construction of Subsection
3.2
, the representation of the twisted crossed product is obtained by starting from just a representation of the algebra on
H
(via the induced representation). If we further have a twisted covariant representation on the same Hilbert space, then we can construct a
K
-homologically equivalent spectral triple. This follows remark
2.9
in
[
23
]
and is related to the
equivariant
Kasparov product.

Let
(\pi,U)
be a twisted covariant representation of
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
on a Hilbert space
H
and suppose that
\pi
is faithful. Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space; this space will play a role only after proposition
3.8
. Combining with the left regular representation of the group, we get a new covariant representation
(\hat{\pi},\hat{L})
of
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
on
H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V
as follows:

\begin{cases}\hat{\pi}(a)(\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v)=\pi(a)\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v\\
\hat{L}_{h}(\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v)=U_{h}\xi\otimes\delta_{hg}\otimes v\end{cases}

(3.7)

for
a\in A
,
\xi\in H
,
v\in V
and
g,h\in G
. It is immediate to check that
(\hat{\pi},\hat{L})
is twisted covariant. Consider now the unitary operator
W\colon H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V\rightarrow H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V
defined by

W(\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v)=\pi\left(\sigma_{g,g^{-1}}^{*}\right)U_{g}\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v.

(3.8)

Recall the twisted covariant representation
(\widetilde{\pi},\widetilde{L})
(equations (
2.3
)) whose integrated form is the left regular representation of
A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G
. We consider in the following the covariant couple
(\widetilde{\pi}\otimes\operatorname{Id},\widetilde{L}\otimes\operatorname{Id})
on
H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V
. With a slight abuse of notation we continue to call it
(\widetilde{\pi},\widetilde{L})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

The operator
W
satisfies

W\widetilde{\pi}(a)W^{*}=\hat{\pi}(a)
and
W\widetilde{L}_{h}W^{*}=\hat{L}_{h}
. In other words
(\hat{\pi},\hat{L})
is unitarily equivalent to
(\widetilde{\pi},\widetilde{L})
. Since
\pi
is injective, the integrated form
\hat{\pi}\times\hat{L}
descends to a representation of the reduced twisted crossed product.

Proof.

The first equality is a straightforward computation:

\begin{split}W\widetilde{\pi}(a)W^{*}(\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v)&=\pi(\sigma_{g,g^{-1}}^{*})\left[U_{g}\pi(\rho_{g^{-1}}(a))U_{g}^{*}\right]\pi(\sigma_{g,g^{-1}})\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v\\
&=\pi(\rho_{gg^{-1}}(a))\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v=\pi(a)\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v\\
&=\hat{\pi}(a)(\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v).\end{split}

We prove the second one by showing that
W\widetilde{L}_{h}=\hat{L}_{h}W
. Thus

W\widetilde{L}_{h}(\xi\otimes\delta_{x}\otimes v)=\pi(\sigma^{*}_{hx,x^{-1}h^{-1}})U_{hx}\pi(\sigma_{x^{-1}h^{-1},h})\xi\otimes\delta_{hx}\otimes v

and

\hat{L}_{h}W(\xi\otimes\delta_{x}\otimes v)=U_{h}\pi(\sigma^{*}_{x,x^{-1}})U_{x}\xi\otimes\delta_{hx}\otimes v.

It is immediate to check that these two vectors are equal using the fact that
(\pi,U)
is a twisted covariant representation. Indeed for every
x,y\in G
we have
\pi(\sigma_{x,y})=U_{x}U_{y}U_{xy}^{*}
. In other words the two vectors are converted in quantities involving only the unitaries
\{U_{g},U_{g}^{*}\}_{g\in G}
and they coincide. The rest of the proof follows from the fact that
\widetilde{\pi}\times\widetilde{L}
is the defining representation of the reduced crossed product.
∎

We pass now to describe spectral triples.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
be a twisted dynamical system with
A
a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. A spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D,\pi)
on
A
is
twisted

G
-
equivariant
or simply
G
-equivariant, if there exists a unitary map
U\colon G\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(H)
such that:

(1)

(\pi,U)
is a twisted covariant representation of
(A,G,\rho,\sigma)
on
H
.

(2)

The operators
U_{g}
leave the domain of
D
invariant for all
g\in G
.

(3)

The commutator
[D,U_{g}]
extends to a bounded operator on
H
for every
g\in G
.

Given an odd twisted equivariant spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D,\pi)
,
and a proper translation bounded length function
{\bm{\ell}}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{B}_{h}(V)
,
we can define a spectral triple on
A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G
as follows: the dense subalgebra is
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
, the Hilbert space is
\widetilde{H}=(H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V)\oplus(H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V)
and the representation is two copies of the integrated form of (
3.7
) after being extended trivially on the
V
factor.
The Dirac operator is defined as in (
3.4
).
To relate this construction to the triple defined in Subsection
3.2
, we begin with the observation that the two representations supporting the triples are unitarily equivalent, the unitary being
\mathcal{W}:=W\oplus W
with
W
taken from proposition
3.8
.
Now the same unitary conjugates
\widetilde{D}
to an operator
which on simple tensors operates as

\mathcal{W}\widetilde{D}\mathcal{W}^{*}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v\\
\eta\otimes\delta_{h}\otimes w\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\pi(\sigma_{h,h^{-1}}^{*})U_{h}DU_{h}^{*}\pi(\sigma_{h,h^{-1}})\eta\otimes\delta_{h}\otimes w-i\eta\otimes\delta_{h}\otimes\bm{\ell}(h)w\\
\pi(\sigma_{g,g^{-1}}^{*})U_{g}DU_{g}^{*}\pi(\sigma_{g,g^{-1}})\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes v+i\xi\otimes\delta_{g}\otimes\bm{\ell}(g)v\end{array}\right).

In particular, if
[D,U_{g}]
is uniformly bounded in
g\in G
, then the twisted action is automatically equicontinuous in the sense of (
3.3
). Furthermore, as
U_{h}U_{h^{-1}}=\pi(\sigma_{h,h^{-1}})
by definition, the family

\big\{[D,\pi(\sigma_{h,h^{-1}})]\big\}_{h\in G}

is uniformly bounded and
\mathcal{W}\widetilde{D}\mathcal{W}^{*}
is a bounded perturbation of
\widetilde{D}
. Therefore, the two operators lead to the same

K
-homology class. Analogous computations can be performed for the “even to odd case”.

Summing up
: starting from an odd (twisted) equivariant spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D,\pi)
with covariant representation
(\pi,U)
on
H
, we define a triple

(C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A}),\widetilde{H},\widetilde{D},\hat{\pi}\times\hat{L})
on the twisted crossed product

A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G
.
Here
\widetilde{H}\coloneqq(H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V)\oplus(H\otimes\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V)
, the representation

\hat{\pi}\times\hat{L}:C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(\widetilde{H})
is obtained by doubling the representation

\hat{\pi}\times\hat{L}(a_{h}\delta_{h})(\xi\otimes\delta_{x}\otimes v)=\pi(a_{h})U_{h}\xi\otimes\delta_{hx}\otimes v

(3.9)

and the Dirac operator is:

\widetilde{D}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&D\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}-i1_{{H}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}\\
D\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes 1_{V}+i1_{{H}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}&0\end{matrix}\right).

(3.10)

If we instead, begin with an even equivariant spectral triple

(\mathcal{A},H^{+}\oplus H^{-},D)
with
representation
\pi=\begin{pmatrix}\pi^{+}&0\\
0&\pi^{-}\end{pmatrix}

and operator

D=\begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix},

on the algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we can, in a similar way construct an odd spectral triple with Hilbert space

\widetilde{H}:=(H^{+}\otimes\ell^{2}({G})\otimes V)\oplus(H^{-}\otimes\ell^{2}({G})\otimes V)

carrying a representation obtained specializing formula (
3.9
) separately to the components
\pi^{\pm}

and operator

\widetilde{D}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{H^{+}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}&D^{-}\otimes I_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes I_{V}\\
D^{+}\otimes I_{\ell^{2}({G})}\otimes I_{V}&-I_{H^{-}}\otimes M_{\bm{\ell}}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.11)

At the end of this section we show that in the equivariant case, the constructed spectral triple can be understood as the restriction of an exterior product of spectral triples.

Let us specify what we mean by restriction for spectral triples: fix a spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},H,D)
on the unital
C^{*}
–algebra
A
;
let
(\mathcal{B},B)
be a pair consisting of a unital
C^{*}

B
with a fixed dense
*
-subalgebra
\mathcal{B}
.
Here
\mathcal{B}

\ast
-homomorphism

mapping
\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{A}

g

In this situation, composing the representation of
A
with

we obtain
a
spectral triple
(\mathcal{B},H,D)
.

Definition 3.10
.

We call the spectral triple
(\mathcal{B},H,D)
the
pullback
spectral triple of
(\mathcal{A},H,D)
under
g
. If
g

.

We pass now to the construction of a morphism:

\phi_{A}^{r}:A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G\longrightarrow A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G)

such that
\phi_{A}^{r}(a\delta_{g})=a\delta_{g}\otimes\delta_{g}
(minimal tensor product).
This map is the twisted version of the one used in
[

,
3
]
. Integrating
(\pi,U)
defines a non degenerate representation
\pi\times U:A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}G\longrightarrow\mathbb{B}(H)

B\subset\mathbb{B}(H)
be the (separable)
C^{*}
-algebra image of
\pi\times U
.
We get a non degenerate morphism

\psi_{A}:A\rtimes_{r}^{\rho,\sigma}G\longrightarrow B\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G)

(again with respect to the minimal tensor product).
The analytical details will be included in the proof of the next proposition.
The algebra
{B}
carries a spectral triple

(\mathcal{B},H,D)

with the same
D
as
A
and smooth algebra
\mathcal{B}
given by finite sums
\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}U_{g}
with

Regularity of the Equivariant Construction

In the equivariant case, by proposition

We begin with the following obvious observation.

Proposition 4.1

In the context of definition

∎

Let us pass now to discuss the regularity property of the spectral triples on group algebras constructed with matrix valued length functions.

Example 4.2
.

Consider the spectral triple
(\mathbb{C}G,\ell^{2}(G)\otimes V,M_{\bm{\ell}})
. In this case the operator
\left\lvert M_{\bm{\ell}}\right\rvert
is the (self-adjoint extension of the) multiplication operator by

\begin{split}[\left\lvert M_{\bm{\ell}}\right\rvert,\lambda_{g}](\delta_{h}\otimes v)&=\left\lvert M_{\bm{\ell}}\right\rvert(\delta_{gh}\otimes v)-\lambda_{g}\delta_{h}\otimes\left\lvert\bm{\ell}(h)\right\rvert v\\
&=\delta_{gh}\otimes\left(\left\lvert\bm{\ell}(gh)\right\rvert-\left\lvert\bm{\ell}(h)\right\rvert\right)v.\end{split}

(4.1)

It is known that for any couple of square matrices

we have

\left\lVert\left\lvert S\right\rvert-\left\lvert T\right\rvert\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert S-T\right\rVert

(4.2)

This property, weaker than the regularity, is called

is Lipschitz regular for every translation bounded length function

In general, the left regular representation
\lambda_{g}
does not lie in the domain of the derivation

for

as the elements

do not commute with each other. If this is the case, then

and this is bounded by (

). Understanding when the commutator

lies in the domain of
\delta^{k}

\begin{split}\delta([M_{\bm{\ell}},\lambda_{g}])(\delta_{h}\otimes v)&=\left\lvert M_{\bm{\ell}}\right\rvert\delta_{gh}\otimes\left(\bm{\ell}(gh)-\bm{\ell}(h)\right)v-[M_{\bm{\ell}},\lambda_{g}]\left\lvert M_{\bm{\ell}}\right\rvert(\delta_{h}\otimes v)\\

\end{split}

which is in general not bounded. However if we make the technical assumptions

[\bm{\ell}(g),|\bm{\ell}(h)|]=0,\quad\forall g,h\in G

then

If the length function

. Here

is the balanced tensor product and the image of the multiplication map from

and

.

Moreover if the action is free any

We say that an element

-module

. Analogously, an element

in a left
A
-module

is a generator if

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let

,
B
and

as above. Then

(1)

If the action is free and, for any

,

has a generator
\mu_{k}
as a right

-module, then any
\mu_{k}
has a right-inverse in

.

(2)

If the action is free and, for any

,

has a generator
\mu_{k}
as a left

\mu_{k}
has a left-inverse in

.

Proof.

(1)
:
Since

is a generator for

as a right

has a representative in

of the form

∎

The following Lemma is probably well-known, we include the proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
C
be a
C^{*}
-algebra,

a left-invertible element. Then

has a polar decomposition

with

,

an invertible positive element and

isometric.

Let us identify

with an algebra of operators acting on a Hilbert space

, and let

. Since

has a left inverse, it is injective, hence

∎

Theorem 5.8
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

For any
k\in\widehat{G}
,
B_{k}
contains an element which is unitary in
B
.

(2)

For any
k\in\widehat{G}
,
B_{k}
contains an element which is invertible in
B
.

(3)

For any
k\in\widehat{G}
,
B_{k}
is a free, rank-1, right
A
-module.

(4)

For any
k\in\widehat{G}
,
B_{k}
is a free, rank-1, left
A
-module.

(5)

For any
k\in\widehat{G}
,
B_{k}
contains an element which is a generator both as a right and left
A
-module, and the action is free.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is obvious.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: the map
a\in A\mapsto\mu_{k}a\in B_{k}
is a right
A
-module isomorphism. As for the freeness, we have to show that, for any
k\in\widehat{G}
, the
A
-bimodule map

\begin{matrix}p:&B_{-k}&\otimes_{A}&B_{k}&\to&A\\
&b_{-k}&\otimes&b_{k}&\mapsto&b_{-k}b_{k}\end{matrix}

is a bijection.
Such a map is always injective so we just have to prove surjectivity.
Setting
a=\mu_{k}^{-1}\mu_{-k}^{-1}
, we have
a\in A
and
p(\mu_{-k}\otimes\mu_{k}a)=1
, which implies that
p
is surjective.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: if
\Phi:A\to B_{k}
is a right
A
-module isomorphism, namely
\Phi(a_{1})a_{2}=\Phi(a_{1}a_{2})
,
\mu_{k}:=\Phi(1)
is clearly a generator of
B_{k}
as a right
A
-module.
Then, according to Lemma
5.6
,
\mu_{k}
has a right-inverse, hence
\mu_{k}^{*}
has a left inverse in
B_{k}
and, by Lemma
5.7
,
\mu_{k}=hv^{*}
with
v,h\in B
,
v
isometric and
h
invertible. Indeed,
h\in A
and
v_{k}^{*}\in B_{k}
.
We observe that
I-vv^{*}
is in
A
, and
\Phi(I-vv^{*})=\mu_{k}(I-vv^{*})=hv^{*}(I-vv^{*})=0
. Since
\Phi
is an
A
-module isomorphism, we get
I-vv^{*}=0
, namely
v
and
v^{*}
are unitary.

By the arguments above,
(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)
and
(2)\Rightarrow(5)
are obvious.

(5)\Rightarrow(2)
: Let
\mu_{k}\in B_{k}
be a generator of
B_{k}
both as a right and left
A
-module. By Lemma
5.6
,
\mu_{k}
has a right-inverse and a left-inverse, namely it is invertible.
∎

Definition 5.9
.

We say that the finite covering
G\curvearrowright B\supset A
is

if one of the equivalent conditions of theorem
5.8
is satisfied. In this case, the action is said to be

. Any map

with
{\mu}_{j}\in B_{j}

.

Remark 5.10
.

(i)
Condition (1) in theorem

has been called regularity in

[
1
]
and it has been proved to imply freeness by making use of the Elwood condition.

(ii)

(iii)

.

Example 5.12
.

We consider here a free
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on the Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}
such that the spectral subspace
B_{-}
has a generator as a right
A
-module, but contains no invertible elements. Standard references for the basic properties of the Toeplitz algebra are
[
17
]
and
[
4
]
.

Let us define the self-adjont unitary operator
u
on the Hardy space
H^{2}
that, on the orthonormal basis
z^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, acts as
uz^{n}=(-1)^{n}z^{n}
, and denote by
\sigma
the action on
\mathcal{T}
given by

\sigma(b)=ubu
. This defines a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on
\mathcal{T}
. Denoting by
T_{f}
the Toeplitz operator associated with the function
f\in C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C})
,
we get
\sigma(T_{z})=-T_{z}
therefore, by known properties of Toeplitz operators,
\sigma(T_{z^{n}})=(-1)^{n}T_{z^{n}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Setting
(s(f))(z)=f(-z)
for any function in
C(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C})
, we deduce

\sigma(T_{f})=T_{s(f)}
. Now if
b\in B_{+}
,
b=T_{f}+K
with

a compact operator, we have
b=\sigma(b)=\sigma(T_{f})+uKu=T_{s(f)}+uKu
. By the uniqueness of the decomposition, we obtain
\sigma(T_{f})=T_{f}
. An analogous result holds for
b\in B_{-}
.

Moreover, since
T_{z}
is an isometry, any element in
b\in B_{-}
can be written as
(bT_{z}^{*})T_{z}
with
bT_{z}^{*}\in B_{+}
, namely
T_{z}
generates
B_{-}
as a left
A
-module. Analogously,
T_{z}^{*}
generates
B_{-}
as a right
A
-module.

We now prove that,
for any Fredholm, Toeplitz operator
T_{f}\in B_{+}
,
\operatorname{Ind}(T_{f})
is even,
and,
for any Fredholm, Toeplitz operator
T_{f}\in B_{-}
,
\operatorname{Ind}(T_{f})
is odd.
Indeed,
\sigma(T_{f})=T_{f}
implies
s(f)=f
. As a consequence, setting
\varphi(t)=f(e^{it})
,
we have
\varphi(t+\pi)=\varphi(t)
, namely
\varphi
follows the same path twice, hence

has an even winding number and
T_{f}
has an even index.
If
\sigma(T_{f})=-T_{f}
then
T_{f}=T_{z}^{*}T_{zf}
with

, hence
\operatorname{Ind}(T_{f})=\operatorname{Ind}(T_{z}^{*})+\operatorname{Ind}(T_{zf})
is odd.

Finally, if
b=T_{f}+K
is invertible, it is Fredholm with index 0, hence
T_{f}
has index zero, which imply that
b\not\in B_{-}
.

As for the freeness of the action, we observe that, for any
b\in B_{+}
,

is in
B_{+}
, hence
p(T_{z}^{*}\otimes T_{z}^{*}a)=b
, that is

is surjective

\Box

Let’s examine the commutative case. We consider a compact space

for

Recall that a covering of topological space is regular when its group of automorphisms acts transitively on every fiber. To help the comparison with the algebraic case, we shall call it
topologically regular.

Assume that

is Hausdorff, connected and locally path connected, then

being its group of automorphisms. Indeed if the action is free, the Hausdorff assumption combined with the finiteness of

In this case, i.e when

with the

is the quotient space

with respect the the right diagonal action

for every

. The following facts are well known but it is interesting to specialize them to the rank-

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
X
be compact Hausdorff, connected and locally path connected.
Then the covering
C(Y)\subset C(X)\curvearrowleft G
is rank-
1
regular if and only if it is topologically regular and
all the associated bundles

are trivial:

(the isomorphism is not required to preserve the flat structure).

If all the spaces are reasonably good (say

[c_{1}(V_{j})]\in H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Assume first that we have a rank-
1
regular covering with invertibles
\mu_{j}
,
j=1,...,n
. To prove that the covering is topologically regular we just have to prove that the action is free in the topological sense. This is immediate because

implies

\langle j,g\rangle\mu_{j}(x)=(\gamma_{g}\mu_{j})(x)=\mu_{j}(x\cdot g)=\mu_{j}(x)

for every

. This is impossible unless

The viceversa is clear once we observe that the topologically regular assumption makes the construction of the vector bundles

possible. Any invertible
\mu_{j}
corresponds to a trivialization. About Chern classes we refer to
[
27
]
.
∎

Notice that if

. In particular the integer classes

are torsion classes. It follows that if

]
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
G\curvearrowright B\supset A
be a finite rank-1 regular noncommutative covering with
G
abelian. Any frame
\mu:\widehat{G}\longmapsto U(B)
determines a twisting pair
(\rho,\sigma)
by

{\rho}_{j}(a)={\mu}_{j}a{\mu}_{j}^{*},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sigma_{j,k}={\mu_{j}}\mu_{k}\,\mu_{j+k}^{*},\quad j,k\in\widehat{G}

together with an isomorphism
\Phi:A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}\widehat{G}\rightarrow B
which on the generators is given by

\Phi(a_{j}\delta_{j})=a_{j}\mu_{j}.

This isomorphism is equivariant with respect to the natural (dual) action of

G
on
A\rtimes^{\rho,\sigma}\widehat{G}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
(\rho,\sigma)
is a twisting pair. Moreover
since the group is finite we don’t have to worry about completions and the maximal reduced crossed product coincides with the reduced one. Let’s check that
\Phi
is a morphism of
C^{*}
-algebras. Then it will be invertible thanks to the rank-
1
regular property with
\Phi^{-1}(b)
given by writing
b=\sum_{k\in\widehat{G}}b_{k}\mu_{k}
.
We have

\begin{split}\Phi(a_{j}\delta_{j}\star b_{k}\delta_{k})&=\Phi(a_{j}\rho_{j}(b_{k})\sigma_{j,k}\delta_{j+k})=a_{j}(\mu_{j}b_{k}\mu_{j}^{*})({\mu_{j}}\mu_{k}\,\mu_{j+k}^{*})\mu_{j+k}=\Phi(a_{j}\mu_{j})\Phi(b_{k}\mu_{k})\end{split}

and

\Phi((a_{j}\delta_{j})^{*})=\Phi(\sigma_{-j,j}^{*}\rho_{-j}(a_{j}^{*})\delta_{-j})=\mu_{e}\mu_{j}^{*}\mu_{-j}^{*}(\mu_{-j}a_{j}^{*}\mu_{-j}^{*})\mu_{-j}=\mu_{j}^{*}a_{j}^{*}=\Phi(a_{j}\delta_{j})^{*}.

Concerning the statement about the equivariance, this follows from

\Phi(\gamma_{g}(a_{j}\delta_{j}))=\Phi(\langle j,g\rangle a_{j}\delta_{j})=\langle j,g\rangle a_{j}\mu_{j}=\gamma_{g}(a_{j}\mu_{j})=\gamma_{g}(\Phi(a_{j}\delta_{j})).

Here we denote with
\gamma_{g}
both the actions.
∎

Appendix A

Infinitesimal order of compact operators

Let
T
be a positive compact operator acting on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, denote by
\{\mu_{n}(T)\}_{n\geq 0}
the non-increasing sequence (with multiplicity) of its eigenvalues and set

\lambda_{t}(T)\coloneqq\#\{n\geq 0:\mu_{n}(T)>t\}

for
t>0
, cf. e.g.
[
19
]
.
The following holds:

Theorem A.1
.

\inf\{s>0:\tr T^{s}<\infty\}=\left(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\mu_{n}(T))}{\log(1/n)}\right)^{-1}=\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\log(\lambda_{t}(T))}{\log(1/t)}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\lambda_{1/n}(T))}{\log n}.

The first equality in the statement has been proved in
[
22
]
theorem 1.4. The second equality has been proved in
[
21
]
proposition 1.13 for general semifinite von Neumann algebras, we give a Hilbert space proof here for the sake of simplicity. We first need a lemma.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a right-continuous, non-increasing, piecewise-constant function.

(1)
If
\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)=0
and the set of discontinuity points consists of an unbounded increasing sequence
x_{n}
, then

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(1/f(t))}{\log t}=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(x_{n}))}{\log x_{n}},\quad\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(1/f(t))}{\log t}=\liminf_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(x_{n}))}{\log x_{n+1}}.

(2)
If
\lim_{t\to 0}f(t)=+\infty
and the set of discontinuity points consists of an infinitesimal decreasing sequence
x_{n}
, then

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\log f(t)}{\log 1/t}=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log f(x_{n})}{\log 1/x_{n-1}},\quad\liminf_{t\to 0}\frac{\log f(t)}{\log 1/t}=\liminf_{n}\frac{\log f(x_{n})}{\log 1/x_{n}}.

Proof.

(1) Let
t_{k}
be an increasing sequence such that
\exists\lim_{k}\frac{\log(1/f(t_{k}))}{\log t_{k}}
.
Possibly passing to a subsequence, we may assume that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is at most one
k
such that
x_{n}\leq t_{k}<x_{n+1}
, denote by
n_{k}
the indices for which

x_{n_{k}}\leq t_{k}<x_{n_{k}+1}
. Since
f(t)
is constant in
[x_{n_{k}},x_{n_{k}+1})
and
1/\log t
is decreasing, we have, for any
y_{n}\in[x_{n},x_{n+1})
such that
y_{n_{k}}\geq t_{k}
, the inequalities

\frac{\log(1/f(y_{n_{k}}))}{\log y_{n_{k}}}\leq\frac{\log(1/f(t_{k}))}{\log t_{k}}\leq\frac{\log(1/f(x_{n_{k}}))}{\log x_{n_{k}}}.

On the one hand we get

\lim_{k}\frac{\log(1/f(t_{k}))}{\log t_{k}}\leq\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(x_{n_{k}}))}{\log x_{n_{k}}}\leq\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(x_{n}))}{\log x_{n}}\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(1/f(t))}{\log t}.

On the other hand,

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log(1/f(t))}{\log t}\leq\liminf_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(y_{n}))}{\log y_{n}}\leq\liminf_{k}\frac{\log(1/f(y_{n_{k}}))}{\log y_{n_{k}}}\leq\lim_{k}\frac{\log(1/f(t_{k}))}{\log t_{k}}.

Finally, by choosing
y_{n}
close enough to
x_{n+1}
so that

\frac{\log x_{n+1}}{\log y_{n}}\to 1
, we get

\liminf_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(y_{n}))}{\log y_{n}}=\liminf_{n}\frac{\log(1/f(x_{n}))}{\log x_{n+1}}.

Since
t_{k}
may be chosen as to reach the
\liminf
or the
\limsup
, part (1) follows.

As for part (2), let
t_{k}
be a decreasing sequence such that
\exists\lim_{k}\frac{\log(f(t_{k}))}{\log 1/t_{k}}
. As above, we may assume that, for a suitable subsequence
x_{n_{k}}
,
x_{n_{k}}\leq t_{k}<x_{n_{k}-1}
, and consider a sequence
y_{n}\in[x_{n},x_{n-1})
such that
y_{n_{k}}>t_{k}
.
Again,

\frac{\log f(x_{n_{k}})}{\log 1/x_{n_{k}}}\leq\frac{\log f(t_{k})}{\log 1/t_{k}}\leq\frac{\log f(y_{n_{k}})}{\log 1/y_{n_{k}}}.

The rest of the proof follows as in part (1).
∎

Proof of theorem
A.1
.

During this proof, we suppress the dependence on
T
.
We first extend the sequence
n\geq 0\to\mu_{n}

25

24
]

We recall some basic facts

]

Definition B.3

The

B.2

[
43
]

Definition B.4

The space of
\Delta

[

]

In particular,

\Delta=1+D^{2}

D\in\textup{Op}^{1}(\Delta)
. Indeed for any
\xi\in W^{\infty}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert D\xi\right\rVert^{2}_{W^{s}}&=\langle D^{2}(1+D^{2})^{s}\xi,\xi\rangle=\langle\mathfrak{b}(D)^{2}(1+D^{2})^{s+1}\xi,\xi\rangle\\
&=\left\lVert\mathfrak{b}(D)^{2}\Delta^{\frac{s+1}{2}}\xi\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert\mathfrak{b}(D)\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert_{W^{s+1}}^{2}\end{split}

where
\mathfrak{b}(D)=D(1+D^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is the bounded transform of
D
. Now define the
\mathbb{N}
-filtered algebra
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\operatorname{End}(W^{\infty})
inductively by:

(1)

\mathcal{E}^{0}
is the subalgebra generated by
\pi(\mathcal{A})
and
[D,\pi(\mathcal{A})]
,

(2)

\mathcal{E}^{1}=\mathcal{E}^{0}+[\Delta,\mathcal{E}^{0}]+\mathcal{E}^{0}[\Delta,\mathcal{E}^{0}]
,

(3)

\mathcal{E}^{k}=\mathcal{E}^{k-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{E}^{j}\mathcal{E}^{k-j}+[\Delta,\mathcal{E}^{k-1}]+\mathcal{E}^{0}[\Delta,\mathcal{E}^{k-1}]
, for
k\geq 2
.

Theorem B.11
.

The product of regular spectral triples is regular.

Proof.

The algebra of generalised differential operators sufficing for the regularity of the product is obtained by forming the product of any of the two algebras of the corresponding factors. The product of these operators has to take into account the gradings.
Indeed to treat the even and odd case at the same footing, graded tensor products and multigradings (see

For the odd
\times

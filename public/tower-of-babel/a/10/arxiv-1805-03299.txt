Matricial Closure
Frank Murphy-Hernandez, Francisco Raggi, José Ríos

Abstract
. In this paper we build, for any natural n n , a closure operator relative to the n − n- matrix endofunctor over the category of rings with 1 1 . We analyze which properties are invariant under this operator. This operator tends to be decisive, in the sense that the operator preserves the property or loses the property for all rings.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 06C20; Secondary 06A15, 46M15

1.
Introduction

The matricial closure is, at the same time, a generalization and a particular case of the ultramatricial algebras. An ultramatricial algebra is a countable direct limit of a direct system of matrix algebras over a fixed
K
. We study just one case of this kind of direct limits, that is the sense of the particular case, but over any ring, that is the sense of the generalization.

We prove that the matricial closure is a closure operator over the category of rings, when the category of rings is thought as a big preorder with preorder induced by the monomorphisms. We define the matricial closure as an operator in the category of rings, also we prove that the matricial closure is an endofunctor. We see that the closure properties are fulfilled by a monic natural transformation in the case of the inflatory property and by a natural isomorphism in the case of the idempotent property.

We show that the matricial closure commutes with certain categorical constructions as the group ring and the polynomial ring. Then we study two functors that arise naturally between their categories of left modules. The first one is the induced by the ring morphism of the inflatory property and the second one is the direct limit of Morita equivalences. In general the two functors are not equivalences, but the second one preserves certain properties as to be simple or to be faithfull.

We study the lattice of ideals of the matricial closure. We give a lattice isomorphism between the lattice of two-sided ideals of the original ring and the lattice of two-sided ideals of its matricial closure. We show that the lattice of left (right) ideals tends to grow a lot. We analyze some chain conditions that the matricial closure of a ring never fulfills and some dimensions that the matricial closure of a ring never have, making it a pathological ring in general. We study the behavior of the matricial closure with respect to the Jacobson radical, prime rings, semiprime rings, V-rings, von Neumann regular rings and the invariant basis number.

3.
MATRICIAL CLOSURE

3.1.
Definition

Let
n
be a positive integer, we define the assignation
M_{n}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
given by: for any ring
R
we assign it
M_{n}(R)
and for any ring morphism
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
,
M_{n}(f):M_{n}(R)\longrightarrow M_{n}(S)
is given by: for any
A\in M_{n}(R)
,
M_{n}(f)(A)_{ij}=f(A_{ij})
with
1\leq i,j\leq n

Proposition 1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Then
M_{n}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
is a functor.

Proof.

Obviously
M_{n}
sends rings into rings, what is left is to verify that sends morphisms into morphisms. Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a ring morphism,
A,B\in M_{n}(R)
and
1\leq i,j\leq n
. First

\displaystyle M_{n}(f)(A+B)_{ij}

\displaystyle=f((A+B)_{ij})

\displaystyle=f(A_{ij}+B_{ij})

\displaystyle=f(A_{ij})+f(B_{ij})

\displaystyle=M_{n}(f)(A)_{ij}+M_{n}(f)(B)_{ij}

which means that
M_{n}(f)(A+B)=M_{n}(f)(A)+M_{n}(f)(B)
. Second

\displaystyle M_{n}(f)(AB)_{ij}

\displaystyle=f((AB)_{ij})

\displaystyle=f(\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{ik}B_{kj})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(A_{ik})f(B_{kj})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_{n}(f)(A)_{ik}M_{n}(f)(B)_{kj}

\displaystyle=(M_{n}(f)(A)M_{n}(B))_{ij}

which means that
M_{n}(f)(AB)=M_{n}(f)(A)M_{n}(f)(B)
. Finally
M_{n}(f)(I_{n})_{ij}=f((I_{n})_{ij})
=f(\delta_{ij})=\delta_{ij}=(I_{n})_{ij}
.

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
and
g\colon S\longrightarrow T
be ring morphisms,
A\in M_{n}(R)
and
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Then
M_{n}(gf)(A)_{ij}=g(f(A_{ij}))=g(M_{n}(f)(A)_{ij})=M_{n}(g)(M_{n}(f)(A))_{ij}
, which means that
M_{n}(gf)=M_{n}(g)M_{n}(f)
. Also it is noticed that
M_{n}(1_{R})=1_{M_{n}(R)}
. ∎

Closure Properties

Proposition 4

(Inflatory)
.

Let

Proof.

It is a general fact that, in a direct limit, if the transition maps in the inductive limit are monomorphisms then the structural maps of the direct limit are monomorphisms.

In particular we will denote by

Proposition 6

(Idempotent)

Let

Proof.

We notice that the functor

\displaystyle\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(MC_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}-)

The direct system created for a ring

Proposition 21
.

be a positive integer, let
R
be a ring and let
I
be a left (right, two-sided) ideal of

. Then

is a left (right, two-sided) ideal of

Proof.

First we name

the canonical inclusions of the matricial closure of a left (right, two-sided) ideal with
k
a natural and
\beta_{k}^{m}\colon M_{n^{k}}(I)\longrightarrow M_{n^{m}}(I)
the connection morphisms with
k\leq m
natural numbers. Let
w_{k}(X)\in MC_{n}(I)
and
i_{m}^{n}(A)
with
X\in M_{n^{k}}(I)
and

. We begin with the case when

, in this case we put
i_{m}^{n}(A)w_{k}(X)\colon=w_{m}(A\beta_{k}^{m}(X))
and when

becomes a left ideal of

. In the same manner for right and two-sided ideal.
∎

The next proposition tell us that the matricial closure behaves like the matrix ring respect to the two-sided ideals.

Proposition 22
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
R
be a ring. Then there is a lattice isomorphism between the two-sided ideals of
R
and the two-sided ideals of
MC_{n}(R)
.

Proof.

We take a two-sided ideal
I
of

and define the next subset of
R
,

is a two-sided ideal of

. Next, we call
\{e^{k}_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
the canonical basis of

we remark that
(Ae^{k}_{rs})_{ij}=A_{ir}\delta_{sj}
and

, where

. Let

for some

and some natural

X=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}X_{ij}e^{n^{m}}_{ij}

For all

w_{m}(e^{n^{m}}_{i1})w_{m_{ij}}(Y^{ij})w_{m}(e^{n^{m}}_{1j})=w_{m}(e^{n^{m}}_{11})Y^{ij}_{11}

This equality and the fact that

is a two-sided ideal mean that

. So, without lost of generality, we may put

for all

. Now, as a simple fact:

e^{n^{m}}_{i1}Y^{ij}e^{n^{m}}_{1j}=X_{ij}e^{n^{m}}_{ij}

for all

. Applying

and summing all, we get

\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}w_{m}(e^{n^{m}}_{i1})w_{m}(Y^{ij})w_{m}(e^{n^{k}}_{1j})=w_{m}(\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}X_{ij}e^{n^{m}})=w_{m}(X)

is a two-sided ideal of

, then
w_{m}(X)\in I
. Now, let
w_{m}(X)\in I
for some
X\in M_{n^{k}}(R)
and some natural

. We take

X=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{n^{m}}X_{ij}e^{n^{m}}_{ij}

If we observe that

so

for any

. So we have that the assignation

from the ideals of

is onto. It is easy to observe that is also into and monotone, so it is a lattice isomorphism.
∎

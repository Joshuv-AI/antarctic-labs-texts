BKL bounces outside homogeneity: Gowdy symmetric spacetimes
Warren Li

Abstract
We study the phenomenon of bounces, as predicted by Belinski Khalatnikov and Lifshitz (BKL), as an instability mechanism within the setting of the Einstein vacuum equations in Gowdy symmetry. In particular, for a wide class of inhomogeneous initial data we prove that the dynamics near the t = 0 t=0 singularity are well–described by ODEs reminiscent of Kasner bounces. Unlike previous works regarding bounces, our spacetimes are not necessarily spatially homogeneous, and a crucial step is proving so-called asymptotically velocity term dominated (AVTD) behaviour, even in the presence of nonlinear BKL bounces and other phenomena such as spikes. (A similar phenomenon involving bounces and AVTD behaviour, though not spikes, can also be seen in our companion paper [ 19 ] , albeit in the context of the Einstein–Maxwell–scalar field model in surface symmetry.) One particular application is the study of (past) instability of certain polarized Gowdy spacetimes, including some Kasner spacetimes. Perturbations of such spacetimes are such that the singularity persists, but the intermediate dynamics – between initial data and the singularity – feature BKL bounces.

1.2
Asymptotics towards the singularity

As mentioned above, solutions to the Gowdy symmetric system (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) arising from regular initial data exist globally in time
t\in(0,+\infty)
. Armed with this global existence result, a reasonable goal is to understand in furher detail the behaviour of solutions as one approaches the
spacelike singularity
at
t=0

For instance, one would like to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the functions
P(t,\theta)
and

in the limit
t\to 0

t=0

\theta

[
16
]

\displaystyle P(t,\theta)=-V(\theta)\log t+\Phi(\theta)+o(1),

(1.9)

\displaystyle Q(t,\theta)=q(\theta)+t^{2V(\theta)}[\Psi(\theta)+o(1)],

(1.10)

where

are regular functions of
\theta
. Upon substituting into the equations (
1.3
)–(
1.4
), one sees that these expansions are self-consistent, at least under the assumption that for each
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, one of the following holds:

(i)

The coefficient
V(\theta)
satisfies
0<V(\theta)<1
, or

(ii)

V(\theta)>0
, but
\partial_{\theta}q(\theta)=0
, or

(iii)

V(\theta)<1
, but
\Psi(\theta)=0
.

Note
: in the polarized Gowdy case with
Q\equiv 0
, any value of
V(\theta)
is permitted.

Deriving asymptotics of the form (
1.9
)–(
1.10

[
8

26

27
]

Theorem 1.0
.

Let

1.4

-t\partial_{t}P(t,\theta)\to V(\theta)

Suppose moreover that either

\displaystyle e^{2P}t\partial_{t}Q(t,\theta)\to r(\theta),\qquad e^{2P}(Q(t,\theta)-q(\theta))\to\frac{r(\theta)}{2V(\theta)}.

Note that these imply (

Under the same assumptions, if

In a context where (

1.4
Relationship to BKL and the Kasner map

We put our results in the context of the physics and mathematics literature regarding spacelike singularities arising in solutions of the Einstein equations. While celebrated “singularity theorems” of Penrose
[
23
]
and Hawking
[
15
]
asser that “singularities” are a robust prediction of Einstein’s equations (
1.1
), it is a known shortcoming that these theorems do not prove singularity formation in the sense of blow-up, but instead (causal) geodesic incompleteness, and thus provide no qualitative or quantitative description of spacetimes near their singular, or incomplete, boundaries.

Our approach is instead to outline the heuristics of Belinski, Khalatnikov and Lifshitz (often abbreviated to BKL) in their investigation of near-singularity solutions to Einstein’s equation
[
18
,
3
,
2
,
4
]
.
[
18
]
proposes the following ansatz for singular solutions to the Einstein equations: a spacetime
\mathcal{M}^{1+3}=(0,T)\times\mathbf{\Sigma}^{3}
with singularity located at
\{0\}\times\mathbf{\Sigma}
has spacetime metric
\mathbf{g}
with leading order expansion:

\mathbf{g}=-d\tau^{2}+\sum_{I=1}^{3}\tau^{2p_{I}(x)}\mathbf{\omega}^{I}(x)\otimes\mathbf{\omega}^{I}(x)+\cdots.

(1.16)

The
p_{I}(x)
are functions on
\mathbf{\Sigma}
known as
Kasner exponents
, while
\{\mathbf{\omega}^{I}(x)\}
is a frame of
1
-forms on
\mathbf{\Sigma}
. Naïvely inserting these into (
1.1
), BKL determine that the Kasner exponents must satisfy the
Kasner relations
:

\sum_{I=1}^{3}p_{I}(x)=1,\quad\sum_{I=1}^{3}p_{I}^{2}(x)=1.

(1.17)

Note (
1.17
) implies that aside from the exceptional case where
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=(1,0,0)
and permutations thereof, exactly one of the
p_{I}(x)
must be negative.
A consistency check using the Einstein equations suggests that for the ansatz (
1.16
) to remain valid all the way to
t=0
, the 1-form
\omega^{I}
associated to the negative exponent
p_{I}
must be integrable in the sense of Frobenius, meaning
\omega^{I}\wedge d\omega^{I}=0
. On top of the Kasner relations (
1.17
), this integrability condition provides another obstruction to the validity of (
1.16
)
6
6

6

In the original paper of Khalatnikov and Lifshitz
[
18
]

[
3

this observation waas instead understood as an instability; this is the modern viewpoint.

In
[
3
]
BKL then give heuristics suggesting what happens if this integrability condition fails. In
[
3
]
, BKL assume
Asymptotically Velocity Term Dominated (AVTD)
behaviour, meaning that ‘spatial derivatives’ are subdominant in comparison to
\partial_{\tau}
-derivatives. This has the effect that dynamics in the future light cones emanating from distinct points
(0,p)\in\{0\}\times\mathbf{\Sigma}
on the singularity are decoupled.

With this assumption they then provide a computation suggesting that in such a light cone, the ansatz (
1.16
) is valid for
\tau\gg\tau_{B}
for
\tau_{B}
some critical time, but for
\tau\ll\tau_{B}
the metric transitions to a form that again resembles (
1.16
) but with Kasner exponents
p_{I}(x)
replaced by new exponents
\acute{p}_{I}(x)
. Between these regimes, the spacetime undergoes a nonlinear transition from one regime to another, often denoted in the literature as a
BKL
or
Kasner bounce
. Typically these nonlinear transitions, or bounces, then cascade indefinitely, giving rise to BKL’s
chaotic and oscillatory approach to singularity
.

Within this computation, BKL even propose a formula describing how the Kasner exponents change during the transition: if
p_{2}<0
and
\omega^{2}\wedge d\omega^{2}\neq 0
then

\acute{p}_{1}=\frac{p_{1}+2p_{2}}{1+2p_{2}},\quad\acute{p}_{2}=-\frac{p_{2}}{1+2p_{2}},\quad\acute{p}_{3}=\frac{p_{3}+2p_{2}}{1+2p_{2}}.

(1.18)

To relate this to our results in Gowdy symmetry, we need to apply a change of variables from the areal time coordinate

1.2

1.16

\tau\sim t^{\frac{V^{2}+3}{4}}
. We then formally set

,

and
\omega^{3}=d\delta

1.0

1.2

1.16

While

are closed and thus integrable in the sense of Frobenius, one checks that
\omega^{2}\wedge d\omega^{2}=q^{\prime}(\theta)d\sigma\wedge d\theta\wedge d\delta
. Hence the consistency check fails if

and

. From (

if and only if

We review the heuristics regarding how the bounce map (

In the mathematical literature, progress regarding spacelike singularities and the BKL ansatz has largely regarded the “subcritical” setting, where either the integrability condition

Regarding the study of nonlinear bounces, to the best of the author’s knowledge all previous work concerns only spatially homogeneous spacetimes, where the Einstein equations reduce an exact system of nonlinear ODEs. 1.5
Sketch of the proof

The proofs of the stability result Theorem
1.1
and the bounce result Theorem
1.2
are done in tandem and there are three major steps. Recalling the main evolution equations (
1.3
)–(
1.4
), we caricature these steps as follows:

Step 1:

Analysis in the spatially homogeneous case:
In this step, we ignore certain terms in (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) involving
\partial_{\theta}
-derivatives which one expects to become negligible as
t\to 0
. We do, however, keep terms involving the expression
e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q
. The result is a nonlinear ODE system for the quantities
-t\partial_{t}P
and
e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q
; one then analyzes this system to determine bounds for these quantities.

Step 2:

Linearization of the ODE system:
In the second step, we consider the
best possible behaviour
of the terms involving further
\partial_{\theta}
-derivatives i.e. the non-spatially homogeneous corrections. We do this by taking commuting
\partial_{\theta}
with the ODE system in Step 1, resulting in a new
linear
ODE system for new quantities whose coefficients are given by the solution (i.e. some orbit) of the ODE system in Step 1.

Step 3:

Energy estimates:
Finally, one must ensure that we can close our argument without loss of derivatives (necessary due to the existence of top order terms such as
\partial_{\theta}^{2}P
in (
1.3
).) One achieves this via
L^{2}
energy estimates, which are allowed to blow up but only at a controlled rate as
t\to 0
.

We now explain in a little more detail how these steps apply to the Gowdy symmetric system. For Step 1, with
\mathscr{P}=-t\partial_{t}P
and

1.19
) via phase plane analysis, as we see in Figure
2
below. The direction of the arrows in Figure

is with respect to
t\downarrow 0
.

N
is the maximum regularity index for which one applies the energy estimate. This means that one may interpret the ignored terms in Step 1 and Step 2 as negligible errors, as claimed.

To apply Steps 1 to 3 to the nonlinear problem, one uses a standard bootstrap argument. In our proof, we shall actually perform Step 3 first. That is, one bootstraps

1.1
, while the bounce result Theorem
1.2
follows from more detailed ODE analysis.

Theorem 2.2

(Bounces)
.

Let
(P,Q)
be a solution to the Gowdy symmetric system (
1.3
)–(
1.4
), arising from initial data
(P_{D},Q_{D},\dot{P}_{D},\dot{Q}_{D})
obeying (
2.3
)–(
2.6
) and
0<t_{0}\leq t_{*}
as in Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\gamma:(0,t_{0}]\to(0,t_{0}]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

t

\mathscr{P}_{\gamma}(t)=-t\partial_{t}P(\gamma(t))
and
\mathscr{Q}_{\gamma}(t)=e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q(\gamma(t))

\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{P}}(t),\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{Q}}(t)
depending on
\gamma

such that
\gamma(t)\to(0,\theta_{0})
as

. Further,
\mathscr{Q}_{\gamma}(t)
converges to

converges to

(i)

does not converge to

and moreover

\left|\mathscr{P}_{\gamma,\infty}-\min\{\mathscr{P}_{\gamma}(t_{0}),2-\mathscr{P}_{\gamma}(t_{0})\}\right|\leq C\cdot(t_{0}^{\upgamma^{\prime}}+\mathscr{Q}^{2}_{\gamma}(t_{0}))^{1/2}.

(2.10)

(ii)

where

is chosen appropriately.

A corollary of Theorem

is the following stability / instability statement regarding

Gowdy solutions.

Corollary 2.3

(Stability / Instability)
.

Let
P(t,\theta)
be a
smooth
solution to the polarized Gowdy equation (
1.8
), arising from initial data given by
(P_{D},\dot{P}_{D})
at
t=t_{1}>0
. By Theorem
1.0
, there exists smooth
V,\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
P(t,\theta)=-V(\theta)\log t+\Phi(\theta)+o(1)
as
t\to 0
.

Suppose that
0<V(\theta)<2
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for (possibly non-polarized) perturbations of this data with
\|(\tilde{P}_{D},\tilde{Q}_{D},\tilde{\dot{P}}_{D},\tilde{\dot{Q}}_{D})-(P_{D},0,\dot{P}_{D},0)\|_{(H^{N+1})^{2}\times(H^{N})^{2}}\leq\varepsilon
, then for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small the perturbed solution still has
-t\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t,\theta)\to\tilde{V}(\theta)
pointwise as
t\to 0
, for
\tilde{V}
satisfying
0<\tilde{V}(\theta)<2
.

Next, suppose further that
\frac{1}{2}<V(\theta)<\frac{3}{2}
for the original polarized solution. Then for perturbations as above, we moreover have that
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{Q}(t,\theta)\to\partial_{\theta}\tilde{q}(\theta)
uniformly for some
C^{1}
function
\tilde{q}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, for any
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
,
\varepsilon
may be chosen small enough depending on
\tilde{\varepsilon}
such that:

(i)

|\tilde{V}(\theta)-\min\{V(\theta),2-V(\theta)\}|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}
if
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{q}(\theta)\neq 0
, while

(ii)

|\tilde{V}(\theta)-V(\theta)|\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}
if
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{q}(\theta)=0
.

Remark
.

We consider Corollary
2.3
a stability / instability result in the sense that while the perturbed spacetime retains the spacelike singularity and curvature blowup, in the case where the original
V(\theta)
has
1<V(\theta_{0})<2
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
a generic unpolarized perturbation will have a corresponding
\hat{V}(\theta_{0})
with
\hat{V}(\theta_{0})\approx 2-V(\theta_{0})
, which is “far away” from
V(\theta_{0})
.

Our result applies in particular when the background unperturbed spacetime is an exact Kasner spacetime with
P(\theta)=-V\log t
where
0<V<2
(and Kasner exponents given by (
1.14
)). When
1<V<2
, this gives a precise instability mechanism for a certain range of Kasner exponents. Note our methods do not allow us to access this mechanism outside this range, especially regarding spatially inhomogeneous perturbations.

Our instability is triggered when
\partial_{\theta}q(\theta)\neq 0
, while if
\partial_{\theta}q(\theta)=0
and
\frac{1}{2}<V(\theta)<\frac{3}{2}
our result suggests that the instability is suppressed. Note that in light of
[
27
]
it is true that for an open and dense subset of perturbations, the instability is indeed triggered at all but finitely many
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
; at the remaining points we leave open the possibility of spikes.

The energy estimate hierarchy

In this section, we derive energy estimates for

To handle “error” terms in the hierarchy (where the precise coefficients arising in the commuted equations are not crucial), it is useful to introduce the following schematic notation: expressions such as

\sum_{k_{p}+k_{1}+\ldots+k_{i}=K}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}f*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{1}}g*\cdots*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{i}}g

such that
i\geq 1
,
k_{p}\geq 1
and
k_{j}\geq 1
for all

and
k_{p}+k_{1}+\ldots+k_{i}=K
. We emphasize that

In the event that any index e.g.

4.1

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(P,Q)
be a solution to the Gowdy symmetric system (

)–(

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle(t\partial_{t})^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P-t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mkern-48.0mu2\,\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\,e^{2P}\left((t\partial_{t}Q)^{2}-(t\partial_{\theta}Q)^{2}\right)+2e^{2P}(t\partial_{t}Q)(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)+2e^{2P}(t\partial_{\theta}Q)(t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)

\displaystyle\mkern-36.0mu+t^{2}e^{2P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,k_{r}<K\\

\displaystyle\mkern-36.0mu+t^{2}e^{2P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,k_{r}<K\\

\|\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_b'}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+C_{*}^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}(e^{P}t\partial_{t}Q)\|_{L^{2}})\cdot(\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}t\partial_{t}Q)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{K-k}.

\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}t\partial_{t}Q)\|_{L^{\infty}}
, one therefore deduces that

\|\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_b}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{C_{*},K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)}.

(4.4)

The estimate for (
4.2c
) is essentially the same; we first rewrite (
4.2c
) in the schematic form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0,0\leq k_{r},k_{s}<K\\
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}+k_{r}+k_{s}=K\end{subarray}}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{1}}P*\cdots*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{i}}P*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{r}}(e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q)*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{s}}(e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q),

(4.4c’)

then use the product estimate Lemma
3.2
along with the forthcoming Lemma
4.2
to express the
L^{2}
norm of this in terms of familiar quantities:

\|\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_c'}}\|\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{P}(t\partial_{\theta}^{k+1}Q)\|_{L^{2}})\cdot(\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{K-k}

(4.5)

We then combine with the definition of the energies
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)
, and use the bootstrap assumption (
B3
) to estimate
\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q)\|_{L^{\infty}}
; one yields

\|\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_c}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{C_{*},K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)}.

(4.6)

Now, to conclude, for
0\leq K\leq N
we write

t\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left((t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)^{2}+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P)^{2}+(\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)^{2}\right)\right)\\[6.00006pt]
=(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)\left[(t\partial_{t})^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P-t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K+2}P+\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\right]+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P)^{2}+\partial_{\theta}\left(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\cdot t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P\right).

Integrating over
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
so that the last term vanishes, and inserting the commuted wave equation, one has the following identity for the
t\frac{d}{dt}
derivative of the energy:

t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{P}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)\left(\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_a}}+\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\right)+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P)^{2}\right]\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)\left(\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_b}}+\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_c}}\right)\right]\,dx.

From the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
)–(
B2
), it is clear that
\|\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_a}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 10C_{*}^{2}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)}
. Using Cauchy–Schwarz, the first integral in the above expression can thus be bounded as:

\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)\left(\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_a}}+\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\right)+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P)^{2}\right]\,dx\right|\leq(10C_{*}^{2}+9)\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t).

On the other hand, the latter integral can be estimated using (
4.4
) and (
4.6
); one yields that for some
C^{(K)}>0
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}\phi)\left(\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_b}}+\text{\eqref{eq:P_wave_K_c}}\right)\right]\,dx\right|\leq\sqrt{C^{(K)}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{-(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)}\cdot\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)}.

Combining these and applying Young’s inequality, Proposition
4.1
follows, with
2A_{*}=10C_{*}^{2}+10
.
∎

We end this subsection with the promised Lemma
4.2
, used to relate
\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}(e^{P}t\partial_{t}Q)\|_{L^{2}}
to
\|e^{P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k}Q\|_{L^{2}}
in the estimate (
4.3
), and
\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}(e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q)\|_{L^{2}}
to
\|e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}^{k+1}Q\|_{L^{2}}
in (
4.5
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a sufficiently regular function, with
|e^{P}f|\leq C_{*}
. Then for
P:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
regular function, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one has

\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}(e^{P}f)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+C_{*})\sum_{j=0}^{k}(\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{P}\partial_{\theta}^{j}f\|_{L^{2}})\cdot(\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}f)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{k-j}.

(4.7)

Proof.

We prove (
4.7
) using induction on
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The base case
k=1
follows from

|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}f)|\leq|\partial_{\theta}P|\cdot|e^{P}f|+|e^{P}\partial_{\theta}f|\leq C_{*}\cdot|\partial_{\theta}P|+|e^{P}\partial_{\theta}f|.

Now, let us suppose that (
4.7
) holds for
k\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
. To go from
k=K
to
k=K+1
, we use our schematic notation to expand
\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}(e^{P}f)
, and write

\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}(e^{P}f)=\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P\cdot e^{P}f+e^{P}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}f+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}+k_{f}=K+1\end{subarray}}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{1}}P*\cdots*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{j}}P*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{f}}(e^{P}f),

(4.8)

where crucially each of the
k_{1},\ldots,k_{j},k_{f}
will not exceed
K
. Therefore, one may apply the product estimate Lemma
4.2
to find

\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}+k_{f}=K+1\end{subarray}}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{1}}P*\cdots*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{j}}P*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{f}}(e^{P}f)\right\|_{L^{2}}\\[3.99994pt]
\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{K}(\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}(e^{P}f)\|_{L^{2}})\cdot(\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}f)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{K+1-j}.

We now apply the induction hypothesis to change
\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}(e^{P}f)\|_{L^{2}}
to
\|e^{P}\partial_{\theta}^{j}P\|_{L^{2}}
. We thus deduce

\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{j}+k_{f}=K+1\end{subarray}}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{1}}P*\cdots*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{j}}P*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{f}}(e^{P}f)\right\|_{L^{2}}\\[3.99994pt]
\lesssim(1+C_{*})\sum_{j=0}^{K}(\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{P}\partial_{\theta}^{j}f\|_{L^{2}})\cdot(\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}f)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{K+1-j}.

On the other hand, the first two terms on the right hand side of (
4.8
) have
L^{2}
norm bounded by
C_{*}\|\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{P}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}f\|_{L^{2}}
. This proves the estimate (
4.7
) for
k=K+1
and completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.2
Energy estimates for
Q

We now prove the analogous energy estimate for
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q}(t)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(P,Q)
be a solution to the Gowdy symmetric system (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) obeying the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
)–(
B4
). Then there exists a constant
A_{*}
depending only on
C_{*}
, as well as a constant
C^{(K)}
depending on
C_{*}
and the regularity index
K\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
, such that

\left|t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{Q}^{(K)}(t)\right|\leq(2A_{*}+2C_{*}t^{\upgamma})\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}C^{(K)}t^{-2(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t),

(4.9)

where it is understood that the final term is absent if
K=0
.

Proof.

Commuting the
Q
wave equation (
1.4
) with
\partial_{\theta}^{K}
, one yields for
1\leq K\leq N
:

\displaystyle(t\partial_{t})^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q-t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mkern-72.0mu-2t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\cdot t\partial_{t}Q-2t\partial_{t}P\cdot t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q+2t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P\cdot t\partial_{\theta}Q+2t\partial_{t}P\cdot t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q

(4.10a)

\displaystyle\mkern-36.0mu+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{p}+k_{q}=K\end{subarray}}\left(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}P*t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}}Q+t\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}+1}P*t\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}+1}Q\right).

(4.10b)

The base case
K=0
is just the equation (
1.4
) and will be easier to deal with. Note it again crucial that neither of
k_{p},k_{q}
is allowed to equal
K
.

In light of the
e^{2P}
weight appearing in
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q}(t)
in Definition
3.1
, we will be required to estimate
e^{P}
times the right hand side of this equation in
L^{2}
. For the lower order term (
4.10b
), we use a similar method to the estimate for (
4.2b
) in Proposition
4.1
. For the first term in (
4.10b
), it is convenient to write instead

e^{P}\sum_{k_{p}+k_{q}=K}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}P*t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}}Q=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 0\\
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}+k_{p}+k_{q}=K\end{subarray}}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{1}}P*\cdots*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{i}}P*t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}P*\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}}(e^{P}t\partial_{t}Q)

Then one may estimate this in
L^{2}
in the same way as the term (
4.2b’
), eventually getting

\left\|e^{P}\sum_{k_{p}+k_{q}=K}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}P*t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}}Q\right\|\\[3.99994pt]
\lesssim_{C_{*}}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k}P\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k}Q\|_{L^{2}})(\|\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\theta}(e^{P}t\partial_{t}Q)\|_{L^{\infty}})^{K-k}

Thereby using Definition
3.1
and the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
)–(
B4
), we get

\left\|e^{P}\sum_{k_{p}+k_{q}=K}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}P*t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}}Q\right\|\lesssim_{C_{*},K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{-(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)}.

The same method allows us to estimate the second term in (
4.10b
) in the same way, thus

\|e^{P}\text{\eqref{eq:Q_wave_K_b}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{C_{*},K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{-(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)}.

(4.11)

To conclude, we first write the derivative identity; for
0\leq K\leq N
:

t\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{2P}(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)^{2}+e^{2P}t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)^{2}\right)\right)\\[6.00006pt]
=e^{P}(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)\,e^{P}\left[(t\partial_{t})^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q-t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K+2}Q\right]+\partial_{\theta}\left(e^{2P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\cdot t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q\right)\\[6.00006pt]
+t\partial_{t}P\cdot e^{2P}(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)^{2}+(1+t\partial_{t}P)e^{2P}t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)^{2}-2t\partial_{\theta}P\cdot e^{2P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\cdot t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q.

Next, integrating this over
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, one deduces that

t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[e^{P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\left(e^{P}\text{\eqref{eq:Q_wave_K_a}}+t\partial_{t}P\cdot e^{P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\right)+(1+t\partial_{t}P)e^{2P}t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)^{2}\right]\,dx\\[6.00006pt]
-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}2t\partial_{\theta}P\cdot e^{P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\cdot e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)\left(e^{P}\text{\eqref{eq:Q_wave_K_b}}\right)\right]\,dx.

Finally, using the expression for (
4.10a
), the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
) and (
B2
), and the expression for
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)
in Definition
3.1
, one may check that the first line of the right hand side is bounded by say
(10C_{*}^{2}+9)\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)
.

On the other hand, by using the bootstrap assumption (
B3
) for the second integral and (
4.11
) for the third, one yields:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}2t\partial_{\theta}P\cdot e^{P}t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\cdot e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q\,dx\right|\leq 2C_{*}t^{\upgamma}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)\left(e^{P}\text{\eqref{eq:Q_wave_K_b}}\right)\right]\,dx\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}t^{(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)}.

Therefore applying Young’s inequality, one deduces (
4.9
) for
2A_{*}=10(C_{*}^{2}+1)
.
∎

4.3
The energy hierarchy

We now use Propositions
4.1
,
4.3
together with the initial data assumption (
2.5
)–(
2.6
), to show that the total energy of order
K
,
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)
, grows at most polynomially in
t
as
t\downarrow 0
, and moreover that the rate of blow-up depends linearly in
K
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(P,Q)
be a solution to the Gowdy symmetric system (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) in the interval
t\in[t_{b},t_{0}]
, such that the solution obeys the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
)–(
B4
). Assuming also the bounds (
2.5
)–(
2.6
) for the initial data, then there exist a constant
A_{*}
depending only on
C_{*}
, as well as constants
C^{(K)}
depending on
C_{*}
,
K
and
\upzeta
, such that for
0\leq K\leq N
, the total energy
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)
satisfies the bound:

\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)\leq C^{(K)}\,t^{-2A_{*}-2K(1-\upgamma)}.

(4.12)

Proof.

Combining Propositions
4.1
and
4.3
, it is straightforward to show that for some
A_{*}>0
depending only on
C_{*}
and constants
C^{(K)}>0
(we allow
A_{*}
to differ from the previous propositions), one has the following derivative estimate:

\left|t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)\right|\leq(2A_{*}+2C_{*}t^{\upgamma})\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)+C^{(K)}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}r^{-(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t).

As we integrate “backwards” i.e. towards
r=0
, the derivative estimate we actually use is the following:

t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)\geq-(2A_{*}+2C_{*}t^{\upgamma})\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)-C^{(K)}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}r^{-\upgamma(K-k)}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t).

In fact, using the integrating factor
t^{2A_{*}}
, which is
crucially independent of
K
, we write:

t\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)\right)\geq-2C_{*}t^{\upgamma}\left(t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)\right)-C^{(K)}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}t^{-2(1-\upgamma)(K-k)}\left(t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t)\right).

(4.13)

We will now use (
4.13
) and induction on
K\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
that

t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)\lesssim t^{-2(1-\upgamma)K},

(4.14)

where the implied constant is now allowed to depend on
C_{*}
,
K
and
\upzeta
. This is equivalent to (
4.12
). Note that the dependence on
\upzeta
comes from the fact that the initial data bound (
2.5
)–(
2.6
) implies that

\sum_{k=0}^{N}t_{0}^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(t_{0})\leq\upzeta.

(4.15)

For the base case
K=0
, one simply applies Grönwall’s inequality to (
4.13
) for
K=0
; then for
t\in[t_{b},t_{0}]

t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(0)}(t)\leq\exp(F(t_0, t))\cdot t_{0}^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(0)}(t_{0}),\quad\text{ where }\quad F(s_{a},s_{b})=\int^{s_{a}}_{s_{b}}2C_{*}\tilde{t}^{\upgamma}\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}.

Since
F(s_{a},s_{b})
is uniformly bounded for
s_{a},s_{b}\in[t_{b},t_{0}]
, it follows from the initial data bound (
4.15
) that (
4.14
) holds for
K=0
.

Moving onto the induction step, assume that (
4.14
) holds for
0\leq K<\bar{K}\leq N
; we wish to prove it also holds for
K=\bar{K}
. Applying Grönwall’s inequality to (
4.13
) for
K=\bar{K}
, we have that

t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(\bar{K})}(t)\leq\exp(F(t_0, t))\cdot t_{0}^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(\bar{K})}(t_{0})+\int^{t_{0}}_{t}\exp( F(\tilde{t}, t))C^{(\bar{K})}\sum_{k=0}^{\bar{K}-1}\tilde{t}^{-2(1-\upgamma)(\bar{K}-k)}\tilde{t}^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(\tilde{t})\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}.

It therefore follows from the initial data bound (
4.15
) and the inductive hypothesis for
\tilde{t}^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(\tilde{t})
that

t^{2A_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{(\bar{K})}(t)\lesssim\upzeta+\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\sum_{k=0}^{\bar{K}-1}\tilde{t}^{-2(1-\upgamma)(\bar{K}-k)}\cdot\tilde{t}^{-2\upgamma k}\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}\lesssim t^{-2(1-\upgamma)\bar{K}}

as required. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.4
An auxiliary energy estimate

In order to recover precise asymptotics for
Q(t,\theta)
as
t\to 0
we also make use of the following energy estimate for the energy
\mathcal{E}_{Q,u}^{(K)}(t)
, see Definition
3.1
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(P,Q)
be a solution to the Gowdy symmetric system (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) obeying the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
)–(
B4
). Then there exists a constant
A_{*}
depending only on
C_{*}
, as well as a constant
C^{(K)}
depending on
C_{*}
and the regularity index
K\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
such that

\left|t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{Q,u}^{(K)}(t)\right|\leq 2A_{*}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q,u}(t)+C^{(K)}\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q,u}(t)}\cdot\left(t^{-(1-\upgamma)}\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}^{(K-1)}_{Q,u}(t)}+t^{\upgamma^{\prime}}\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}^{(K)}(t)}\right).

(4.16)

With initial data as in Proposition
4.4
, one can moreover show that for some
C^{(K)}>0
,

\mathcal{E}_{Q,u}^{(K)}(t)\leq C^{(K)}t^{-2A_{*}-2K(1-\upgamma)}.

(4.17)

Here
C^{(K)}
may also depend on the initial data
(Q_{D},\dot{Q}_{D})
.

Proof.

For the auxiliary estimate we again commute the wave equation (
1.4
) with
\partial_{\theta}^{K}
, though we shall group terms in a slightly different way to (
4.10a
) and (
4.10b
). We have:

\displaystyle(t\partial_{t})^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q-t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q

\displaystyle=-2t\partial_{t}P\cdot t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q+2t\partial_{\theta}P\cdot t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q

(4.18a)

\displaystyle\mkern-36.0mu+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{p}+k_{q}=K\\
0\leq k_{q}<K\end{subarray}}\left(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}}P*t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}}Q+t\partial_{\theta}^{k_{p}+1}P*t\partial_{\theta}^{k_{q}+1}Q\right).

(4.18b)

The difference between this and (
4.10a
)–(
4.10b
) is that the top order term containing
P
(i.e.
k_{p}=K
) is now included in (
4.18b
). We shall now estimate (
4.18b
) in
L^{2}
without the
e^{P}
weight.

Using Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
1\leq k_{p}\leq K
, one finds

\|\text{\eqref{eq:Qu_wave_K_b}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P\|_{L^{2}}\|t\partial_{t}Q\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}P\|_{L^{\infty}}\|t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K-1}Q\|_{L^{2}}\\[3.99994pt]
+\|t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P\|_{L^{2}}\|t\partial_{\theta}Q\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|t\partial_{\theta}^{2}P\|_{L^{\infty}}\|t\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\|_{L^{2}}.

For the first and third terms on the right hand side, the
L^{2}
norm is controlled by
\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{P}(t)
, while the
L^{\infty}
norm is controlled via the bootstrap assumptions (
B1
) and (
B2
), which together give
\|t\partial_{t}Q\|_{L^{\infty}},\|t\partial_{\theta}Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{*}^{2}t^{\upgamma}
. For the second and fourth terms, the
L^{2}
norm is controlled by
\mathcal{E}^{(K-1)}_{Q,u}(t)
, while the
L^{\infty}
norms are controlled using (
B3
)–(
B4
).

Combining all of these will yield that

\|\text{\eqref{eq:Qu_wave_K_b}}\|\lesssim t^{\upgamma^{\prime}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{P}(t)}+t^{-(1-\upgamma)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(K-1)}_{Q,u}(t)}.

(4.19)

Continuing, we write down the derivative identity:

t\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left((t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)^{2}+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)^{2}+(\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)^{2}\right)\right)\\[6.00006pt]
=(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)\left[(t\partial_{t})^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q-t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K+2}Q+\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\right]+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)^{2}+\partial_{\theta}\left(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\cdot t^{2}\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q\right).

Integrating over
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, one thus yields that

t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)\left(\text{\eqref{eq:Qu_wave_K_a}}+\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q\right)+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}Q)^{2}\right]\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left[(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}Q)\cdot\text{\eqref{eq:Qu_wave_K_b}}\right]\,dx.

Using the bootstrap assumptions (

) and (

4.19
) to deal with (

Inserting the bound (

t\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q,u}(t)\geq-2A_{*}\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q,u}(t)-2C^{(K)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{(K)}_{Q,u}(t)}\cdot\left(t^{-(1-\upgamma)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{Q,u}^{(K-1)}(t)}+t^{\upgamma^{\prime}-A_{*}-K(1-\upgamma)}\right).

Equivalently,

The integrated estimate (

6.1

Lemma 6.1
.

For
t_{0}\leq t_{*}<1
sufficiently small, let

\displaystyle t\partial_{t}\mathscr{P}=\mathscr{Q}^{2}-\mathscr{R}^{2}+\mathscr{E}_{1},

(6.1)

(6.2)

\displaystyle t\partial_{t}\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{P}\mathscr{R}+\mathscr{E}_{3}.

Proof.

We proceed via using a continuity / bootstrap argument, with bootstrap assumption which is exactly (

For the improvement step, we use an approximate monotonicity property of the ODE system; let

\mathscr{K}\coloneqq(\mathscr{P}-1)^{2}+\mathscr{Q}^{2}+\mathscr{R}^{2}.

(6.5)

Using (

(6.6)

Similarly using our assumptions on initial data we may choose
\updelta
sufficiently small such that

\mathscr{K}(t_{0})\leq(1-\upgamma^{\prime\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left((1-\tfrac{\upgamma+\upgamma^{\prime\prime}}{2})^{2}+(1-\upgamma^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right).

(6.7)

Combining (
6.6
) and (

\mathcal{K}(t)\leq(1-\tfrac{\upgamma+\upgamma^{\prime\prime}}{2})^{2}<(1-\upgamma)^{2}
for

For the second inequality in (
6.4

6.3

\mathscr{P}\geq\upgamma

while

for some

\displaystyle 2\geq\int_{t}^{t_{0}}t\partial_{t}P(\tilde{t})\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{t}\in B}t\partial_{t}P(\tilde{t})\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}+\int_{t\not\in B}t\partial_{t}P(\tilde{t})\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}\geq\mu(B)\cdot\frac{1}{32}(\upgamma^{\prime\prime}-\upgamma)^{2}-C\upgamma^{\prime-1}t_{*}^{\upgamma^{\prime}}.

Here

. Let us choose

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mathbf{L}}(t)\|_{op}\leq 10,\qquad\text{ whenever }\,|\mathscr{Q}|>\frac{1}{4}(\upgamma^{\prime\prime}-\upgamma)

(6.14)

\displaystyle\text{where }B=\left\{t\in[t_{b},t_{0}]:|\mathscr{Q}(t)|>\frac{1}{4}(\upgamma^{\prime\prime}-\upgamma)\right\}\text{ has }\mu(B)\leq 128(\upgamma^{\prime\prime}-\upgamma)^{-2}.

6.13

\mathbf{x}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}\\
\mathscr{M}\\
\mathscr{N}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{e}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{E}_{4}\\
\mathscr{E}_{5}\\
\mathscr{E}_{5}\end{bmatrix}.

So that we further rewrite the ODE system (

. Using the positivity of

, together with the assumed bounds on the error terms

(6.15)

\displaystyle\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{2}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{L}}(\tilde{t})\|_{op}\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t\in[t_{2},t_{1}]\setminus B}\|\tilde{\mathbf{L}}\|_{op}\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}+\int_{t\in[t_{2},t_{1}]\cap B}\|\tilde{\mathbf{L}}\|_{op}\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}(1-\upgamma)\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}+\int_{t\in B}10\,\frac{d\tilde{t}}{\tilde{t}}

Thus Grönwall’s inequality in integral form implies that

By (

holds everywhere, we can bound the integrand here by:

\beta(\tilde{t})\cdot\|\tilde{\mathbf{L}}(\tilde{t})\|_{op}\cdot\exp( \int^{\tilde{t}}_{t} \| \tilde{\mathbf{L}}(\tilde{\tilde{t}}) \| \, \frac{d \tilde{\tilde{t}}}{\tilde{\tilde{t}}} )\lesssim\beta(\tilde{t})\cdot\left(\frac{\tilde{t}}{t}\right)^{1-\upgamma}\lesssim\upzeta\left(\frac{\tilde{t}}{t_{0}t}\right)^{1-\upgamma}+\updelta\tilde{t}^{2\upgamma^{\prime}-\upgamma}t^{-1+\upgamma}.

Therefore, using that

. By the definition of
\mathbf{x}
, and since the

and
\ell^{\infty}
norms on

are uniformly equivalent, the lemma follows.

converges to

, it will be helpful to use (
7.8
) from the proof of Theorem
2.1
, or rather its generalization to

|\partial_{\theta}Q(\gamma(t))-\partial_{\theta}q(\theta_{0})|\lesssim_{\upsigma}t^{\upsigma},\qquad\text{ for any }\upsigma<3\upgamma^{\prime}-1.

Note that this generalization, where
\gamma
is allowed to be any timelike curve rather than only a constant
\theta
-curve, is proved easily using previous methods. In (ii), we may insert
\partial_{\theta}q(\theta_{0})=0
, and therefore
|\partial_{\theta}Q(\gamma(t))|\lesssim t^{\upsigma}
.

Furthermore, from
-t\partial_{t}P\leq 2-\upgamma
it holds that
e^{P}\leq t^{-2+\upgamma}
, therefore one has
|e^{P}t\partial_{\theta}Q(\gamma(t))|\lesssim t^{-1+\upgamma+\upsigma}
. But by the definition of
\mathscr{Q}_{\gamma}(t)
, this means that
|\mathscr{Q}_{\gamma}(t)|\lesssim t^{-1+\upgamma+\upsigma}
. Note that so long as
\upgamma>\frac{1}{2}
,
\upgamma^{\prime}<\upgamma
and
\upsigma<3\upgamma^{\prime}-1
may be chosen such that
-1+\upgamma+\upsigma>0
. In the case of (ii), we therefore have from (
2.9
) that

|t\partial_{t}\mathscr{P}_{\gamma}(t)|\lesssim t^{2(-1+\upgamma+\upsigma)}+t^{\upgamma^{\prime}}.

For convenience, let use choose
\sigma=\upgamma^{\prime}-\upgamma+\frac{1}{2}
, where
\upgamma>\upgamma^{\prime}>\frac{3}{2}-\upgamma
. Then
t^{2(-1+\upgamma+\upsigma)}=t^{2\upgamma^{\prime}-1}
, and integrating the above immediately yields (
2.11
).
∎

8.2
Proof of Corollary
2.3

Lastly, we shall apply our Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
to prove the stability / instability corollary. Let
P(t,\theta)
be as stated. By Theorem
1.0
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
the convergence
-t\partial_{t}P(t,\theta)\to V(\theta)
holds in the
C^{k}
norm.

Since we are assuming
0<V(\theta)<2
, since
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
it holds that there exists
\upgamma\in(0,1)
such that
\upgamma<V(\theta)<2-\upgamma
. In fact, by the above convergence we can find
\upgamma,\upgamma^{\prime},\upgamma^{\prime\prime}
and
t_{0}>0
such that for
N
chosen (depending on
\upgamma
) as in Theorem
2.1
, we have that for
0<t\leq t_{0}
and all
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
:

0<\upgamma^{\prime}<\upgamma<\upgamma^{\prime\prime}<-t\partial_{t}P(t,\theta)<2-\upgamma^{\prime\prime}<2-\upgamma<2-\upgamma^{\prime}<2,

and there exists some
\upzeta>0
so that for all
0<t\leq t_{0}
:

\mathcal{E}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K=0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left((t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)^{2}+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}P)^{2}+t^{2\upgamma}(\partial_{\theta}^{K}P)^{2}\right)\,d\theta\leq\frac{\upzeta}{2}.

(This follows because in fact
\mathcal{E}(t)\to\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K=0}^{N}\int(\partial_{\theta}^{K}V)^{2}\,d\theta
as
t\to 0
.)

We note that
t_{0}
above may differ from the initial data time
t_{1}>0
in the statement of Corollary
2.3
. This is mitigated a standard
Cauchy stability
argument, for any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that a perturbation of size
\varepsilon
at
t=t_{1}
implies a perturbation of size
\varepsilon_{0}
at
t=t_{0}
i.e.
\|(\tilde{P}_{D},\tilde{Q}_{D},\tilde{\dot{P}}_{D},\tilde{\dot{Q}}_{D})-(P_{D},0,\dot{P}_{D},0)\|_{(H^{N+1})^{2}\times(H^{N})^{2}}\leq\varepsilon
implies
\|(\tilde{P}(t_{0},\theta),\tilde{Q}(t_{0},\theta),t\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t_{0},\theta),t\partial_{t}\tilde{Q}(t_{0},\theta))-(P(t_{0},\theta),0,t\partial_{t}P(t_{0},\theta),0)\|_{(H^{N+1})^{2}\times(H^{N})^{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Due to this we may instead consider perturbations of initial data as perturbations at time
t=t_{0}
.

Thereby by choosing
\varepsilon
(and thus
\varepsilon_{0}
) small enough one can guarantee that

(1+t\partial_{t}\tilde{P})^{2}(t_{0},\theta)+(e^{\tilde{P}}t_{0}\partial_{\theta}\tilde{P})^{2}(t_{0},\theta)\leq(1-\upgamma^{\prime\prime})^{2},\quad|e^{\tilde{P}}t\partial_{t}\tilde{Q}(t_{0},\theta)|\leq\upzeta t_{0}^{\upgamma},\quad e^{\tilde{P}}(t_{0},\theta)\leq\upzeta t_{0}^{\upgamma}

as well as the energy bound

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K=0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left((t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}\tilde{P})^{2}+t^{2}(\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}\tilde{P})^{2}+t^{2\upgamma}(\partial_{\theta}^{K}\tilde{P})^{2}+e^{2\tilde{P}}(t\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}^{K}\tilde{Q})^{2}+e^{2\tilde{P}}(t\partial_{\theta}^{K+1}\tilde{Q})^{2}\right)d\theta\leq\upzeta.

That is, the perturbed data at
t=t_{0}
satisfies the assumptions (
2.3
)–(
2.6
). Moreover,
t_{0}
can be chosen small enough so that
0<t_{0}\leq t_{*}=t_{*}(\upgamma,\upzeta)
, so that one may apply Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.

The remainder of the argument is then a direct application of these theorems. Applying Theorem
2.1
to the perturbed data, it is clear that
-t\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t,\theta)
converges to some
\tilde{V}(\theta)
pointwise, with
\upgamma\leq\tilde{V}(\theta)\leq 2-\upgamma
.

In the setting where
\frac{1}{2}<V(\theta)<\frac{3}{2}
, we are allowed to in fact choose
\upgamma>\upgamma^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}
. By the final statement of Theorem
2.1
,
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{Q}\to\partial_{\theta}\tilde{q}(\theta)
uniformly as claimed. We next apply Theorem
2.2
. In the case that
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{q}(\theta_{0})\neq 0
, Theorem
2.2
(i) implies that for
\gamma
the timelike curve with constant
\theta=\theta_{0}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{V}(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma,\infty}=\min\{-t\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t_{0},\theta_{0}),2+t\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t_{0},\theta_{0})\}+O(t_{0}^{\upgamma^{\prime}/2})+O(e^{\tilde{P}}t_{0}\partial_{\theta}\tilde{Q}(t_{0},\theta_{0}))

\displaystyle=\min\{-t\partial_{t}P(t_{0},\theta_{0}),2+t\partial_{t}P(t_{0},\theta_{0})\}+O(t_{0}^{\upgamma^{\prime}/2})+O(\varepsilon_{0})=\min\{V(\theta_{0}),2-V(\theta_{0})\}+O(t_{0}^{\upgamma^{\prime}/2})+O(\varepsilon_{0}).

Since we have license to choose
t_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
small depending on
\tilde{\varepsilon}
, Corollary
2.3
(i) follows.

Similarly, in the case that
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{q}(\theta_{0})=0
, Theorem
2.2
(ii)implies that for the same timelike curve
\gamma
,

\displaystyle\tilde{V}(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathscr{P}}_{\gamma,\infty}=-t\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t_{0},\theta_{0})+O(t_{0}^{2\upgamma^{\prime}-1})

\displaystyle=-t\partial_{t}P(t_{0},\theta_{0})+O(t_{0}^{2\upgamma^{\prime}-1})+O(\varepsilon_{0})=V(\theta_{0})+O(t_{0}^{2\upgamma^{\prime}-1})+O(\varepsilon_{0}).

Again choosing
t_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
appropriately small yields Corollary
2.3
(ii). ∎

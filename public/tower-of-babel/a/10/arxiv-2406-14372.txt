Ring-LWE based Encrypted Controller with Unlimited Number of Recursive Multiplications and Effect of Error Growth
Yeongjun Jang, Joowon Lee, Seonhong Min, Hyesun Kwak, Junsoo Kim, Yongsoo Song

Abstract
In this paper, we propose an encrypted dynamic controller that executes an unlimited number of recursive homomorphic multiplications on a Ring Learning With Errors (Ring-LWE) based cryptosystem without bootstrapping. The proposed controller exhibits lower computational complexity compared to existing encrypted controllers implemented on LWE based schemes due to the polynomial structure of Ring-LWE. However, the structural difference introduces additional difficulties in analyzing the effect of error growth; Ring-LWE based schemes inject multiple error coefficients when encrypting a single message, which accumulate under recursive homomorphic multiplications. We show that their effect on the control performance can be arbitrarily bounded by the closed-loop stability, thus recovering the performance of the unencrypted controller. Furthermore, a novel method to “pack” a vector into a polynomial is presented, which enhances computational and memory efficiency when applied to the proposed encrypted controller. The effectiveness of the proposed design is demonstrated through numerical simulations.



I
Introduction

There is a growing emphasis on securing networked control systems from cyber attacks that could cause significant physical damage

[
1
]
.
The urgency of this matter has been amplified by the recent development of attack methodologies that exploit disclosed control signals and/or model knowledge to avoid detection

[
2
]
.
One of the initial countermeasure was to encrypt the control signals before transmission, which are then decrypted at the controller for computation.
However, since the controller resides at the cyber layer in networked control systems, the decrypted signals and the parameters of the controller remain vulnerable to adversaries.
In this context, the notion of encrypted control
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
has been introduced.
Encrypted controllers utilize homomorphic encryption (HE) to operate directly over encrypted data without decryption, thereby enabling all control signals and parameters to remain encrypted throughout the cyber-layer.

When running dynamic controllers over encrypted data, it is essential to account for effect of error growth caused by the recursive update of the (encrypted) state.
This is because most HE schemes that support both addition and multiplication over encrypted data are based on the Learning With Errors (LWE) problem
[
6
]
.
LWE based schemes inject random errors during encryption to ensure security that could accumulate and contaminate the message part under recursive operations.
The bootstrapping technique
[
7
]
could be employed to refresh these errors, but it is not yet considered practical for real-time control systems.

The effect of error growth has been addressed from a control-theoretic perspective in
[
8
]
.
It has been shown that the closed-loop stability of the plant and the
controller can suppress the effect of error growth on the control performance, even though the encrypted controller performs recursive multiplications on a LWE based scheme.
However, the multiplication technique
[
9
]
used in
[
8
]
demands high computational resources and a large amount of memory usage.

In fact, there is an algebraic variant of LWE, called Ring-LWE
[
10
]
, which is more efficient in terms of computation and memory, and offers the same level of security.
In this regard, widely used HE libraries such as Microsoft SEAL
[
11
]
, HElib
[
12
]
, and Lattigo
[
13
]
are built upon Ring-LWE based cryptosystems.
Such efficiency comes from their structure based on polynomial rings; however, this also introduces a further challenge regarding the effect of error growth.
Whereas LWE based schemes inject errors as scalars, Ring-LWE based schemes inject errors as polynomials, where each coefficient is a scalar error.
These error coefficients accumulate under recursive multiplications (see (
17
)).

Instead of analyzing the effect of such error growth, recent studies
[
14
,
15
]
on utilizing Ring-LWE based cryptosystems to encrypt dynamic controllers avoid recursive multiplications with the help of re-encryption, which requires an extra communication line between the plant and the controller.
To the best of our knowledge,
a method to perform an unlimited number of recursive homomorphic multiplications on a Ring-LWE based cryptosystem without bootstrapping has not yet been reported in literature.

In this paper, we propose an encrypted dynamic controller that executes an unlimited number of recursive multiplications on a Ring-LWE based cryptosystem without bootstrapping.
In the proposed design, only the error coefficients in the constant terms affect the control performance.
It is shown that their effects can be arbitrarily bounded by means of the closed-loop stability, even when the
non-effective
error coefficients in the non-constant terms overflow the bounded message space of the Ring-LWE based scheme.
For multiplications, we employ the external product of
[
16
]
, called the “Ring-GSW (Gentry-Sahai-Waters) scheme,”
which offers lower computational complexity than the multiplication technique on LWE based schemes used in
[
8
]
.
It is also worth noting that the proposed controller’s ability to perform recursive multiplications obviates the need of re-encryption, in order to refresh grown errors.

Furthermore, we propose and apply
a novel method to encode a vector into a polynomial—such method is called “packing” in cryptography—that accelerates computation and improves memory efficiency.
The proposed packing algorithm assigns each element of a vector as the coefficient to the designated term of a polynomial.
This enables multiple scalar operations to be replaced by a single polynomial operation.
We present an encrypted controller equipped with the packing algorithm and again analyze the effect of error growth, which can also be arbitrarily bounded.
The efficiency of utilizing the packing algorithm is analyzed and demonstrated through numerical simulations.

II-A

Ring-LWE based cryptosystem

This subsection briefly explains the Ring-LWE based cryptosystem
[
10
,
16
]
used in this paper.
The scheme involves two encryption algorithms, namely the Ring-LWE encryption and the Ring-GSW encryption.
We will describe each encryption algorithm and illustrate how to perform
additions and multiplications over encrypted data.

To describe the scheme, the following definitions are introduced.
For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
R:=\mathbb{Z}[X]/\langle X^{N}+1\rangle
be the ring of integer polynomials modulo
X^{N}+1
, whose elements are represented by integer polynomials of degree less than
N
.
For
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
R_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}_{q}[X]/\langle X^{N}+1\rangle
be the residue ring of
R
modulo
q
.
Every element of the ring
R_{q}
is represented by a polynomial of degree less than
N
, whose coefficients belong to the set
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
.

The addition and multiplication of polynomials
{\mathrm{a}}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\mathrm{a}}_{i}X^{i}\in R_{q}
and
{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\mathrm{b}}_{i}X^{i}\in R_{q}
are defined by

\begin{split}{\mathrm{a}}(X)+{\mathrm{b}}(X)&:=\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left({\mathrm{a}}_{i}+{\mathrm{b}}_{i}\right)X^{i}\!\!\mod q,\\
{\mathrm{a}}(X)\cdot{\mathrm{b}}(X)&:=\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}{\mathrm{c}}_{i}X^{i}\!\!\mod q,\end{split}

(1)

where
{\mathrm{c}}_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{i}{\mathrm{a}}_{j}{\mathrm{b}}_{i-j}-\sum_{j=i+1}^{N-1}{\mathrm{a}}_{j}{\mathrm{b}}_{N+i-j}
.
These operations can be interpreted as general addition and multiplication of real polynomials, where
X^{N}
is regarded as
-1
and the coefficients are mapped to the set
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
via the modulo operation.
Although the modulo operation is embedded in the definitions given in (
1
), we often deliberately indicate it and write
{\mathrm{a}}(X)+{\mathrm{b}}(X)\!\!\mod q
or
{\mathrm{a}}(X)\cdot{\mathrm{b}}(X)\!\!\mod q
for readers who are not familiar with operations on polynomial rings.

]
.
Ciphertexts (encrypted data) are written in bold type to distinguish them from plaintexts (messages to be encrypted).

•

•

(
\mathtt{Key\ generation}

•

(
\mathtt{Ring\text{-}LWE\ encryption}

Then, the Ring-LWE encryption of a plaintext

{\mathsf{Enc}}({\mathrm{m}}):=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathrm{sk}}\cdot{\mathrm{a}}+{\mathrm{m}}+{\mathrm{e}}\\
{\mathrm{a}}\end{bmatrix}\!\!\mod q\in R_{q}^{2}.

•

(
\mathtt{Decryption}
)
\mathsf{Dec}:R_{q}^{2}\rightarrow R_{q}
. The decryption of a ciphertext
{\mathbf{c}}\in R_{q}^{2}

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}}):=\begin{bmatrix}1\!\!&-{\mathrm{sk}}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{c}}\!\!\mod q\in R_{q}.

•

(
\mathtt{Homomorphic\ addition}
)
\oplus:R_{q}^{2}\times R_{q}^{2}\rightarrow R_{q}^{2}

, and

Proposition 1

The following properties hold:

1.

For any
{\mathrm{m}}\in R_{q}
,
\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\mathrm{m}}))={\mathrm{m}}+{\mathrm{e}}\!\!\mod q
for some
{\mathrm{e}}\in R_{q}
such that
\|{\mathrm{e}}\|\leq\sigma
.

2.

For any
{\mathbf{c}}_{1}\in R_{q}^{2}
and
{\mathbf{c}}_{2}\in R_{q}^{2}
,
\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}}_{1}\oplus{\mathbf{c}}_{2})=\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}}_{1})+\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}}_{2})\!\!\mod q
.

3.

For any
{\mathrm{k}}\in R_{q}
and
{\mathbf{c}}\in R_{q}^{2}
,
\mathsf{Dec}({\mathrm{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{c}}\!\!\mod q)={\mathrm{k}}\cdot\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}})\!\!\mod q
.
\square

The property 2) of Proposition
1
is known as the additively homomorphic property of HE.
Along with the property 1), it signifies how the injected errors accumulate under homomorphic additions;
suppose
{\mathrm{e}}_{1}\in R_{q}
and
{\mathrm{e}}_{2}\in R_{q}
are the errors injected during the encryption of
{\mathrm{m}}_{1}\in R_{q}
and
{\mathrm{m}}_{2}\in R_{q}
, respectively.
It follows that

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}({\mathrm{m}}_{1})\oplus{\mathsf{Enc}}({\mathrm{m}}_{2}))\!=\!({\mathrm{m}}_{1}+{\mathrm{m}}_{2})+({\mathrm{e}}_{1}+{\mathrm{e}}_{2})\!\!\!\mod q,

where the errors are added together.

Remark 1

In practice, an encrypted message can be recovered without error as follows:
For
{\mathrm{m}}\in R_{q}
and
{\mathbf{c}}\in R_{q}^{2}
, let the encryption and decryption algorithms be modified as
{\mathsf{Enc}}_{\mathsf{L}}({\mathrm{m}}):={\mathsf{Enc}}({\mathrm{m}}/{\mathsf{L}}\!\!\mod q)

\mathsf{Dec}_{\mathsf{L}}({\mathbf{c}}):=\left\lceil{\mathsf{L}}\cdot\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}})\right\rfloor\!\!\mod q,

where

If
{\mathsf{L}}
is chosen sufficiently small such that

, then

\mathsf{Dec}_{\mathsf{L}}({\mathsf{Enc}}_{\mathsf{L}}({\mathrm{m}}))=\left\lceil{\mathsf{L}}\cdot\left({\mathrm{m}}/{\mathsf{L}}+{\mathrm{e}}\!\!\mod q\right)\right\rfloor={\mathrm{m}},

\|{\mathrm{m}}\|<{\mathsf{L}}q/2-1/2
.
\square

•

(
\mathtt{Setup}
) Choose

•

(
\mathtt{Ring\text{-}GSW\ encryption}

To define the external product, we begin by introducing the decomposition of a Ring-LWE ciphertext.

\mathtt{Ring\text{-}LWE\ ciphertext\ decomposition}

For

is defined by

{\mathsf{D}}({\mathbf{c}}):=[\mathbf{\bar{c}}_{0};\cdots;\mathbf{\bar{c}}_{d-1}]\in R_{q}^{2d}.

(3)

•

(
\mathtt{External\ product}
)
\boxdot:R_{q}^{2\times 2d}\times R_{q}^{2}\rightarrow R_{q}^{2}

Proposition 2

For any
{\mathrm{M}}\in R_{q}
and
{\mathbf{c}}\in R_{q}^{2}

\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}({\mathrm{M}})\boxdot{\mathbf{c}})={\mathrm{M}}\cdot\mathsf{Dec}({\mathbf{c}})+\Delta\!\!\mod q

such that

.
\square

It follows from the definitions of
{\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}
and

\begin{split}\mathsf{Dec}({\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}({\mathrm{M}})\boxdot{\mathbf{c}})&=\begin{bmatrix}1\!&\!-{\mathrm{sk}}\end{bmatrix}({\mathrm{M}}\cdot\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{L}){\mathsf{D}}({\mathbf{c}})\!\!\mod q\\

Here,
\begin{bmatrix}1\!\!&-{\mathrm{sk}}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{L}
is a

\|\begin{bmatrix}1\!&\!-{\mathrm{sk}}\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{L}\|\leq 2d\sigma

Since

This concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

II-B

Problem formulation

Consider a discrete-time plant written by

\begin{split}x_{p}(t+1)&=Ax_{p}(t)+Bu(t),\quad x_{p}(0)=x_{p}^{\mathsf{ini}},\\
y(t)&=Cx_{p}(t),\end{split}

(6)

where

is the state with the initial value
x_{p}^{\mathsf{ini}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}}
,
u(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the input, and

is the output.

Suppose that a controller stabilizing the plant (

) has been designed as

\begin{split}x(t+1)&=Fx(t)+Gy(t),\quad x(0)=x^{\mathsf{ini}},\\
u(t)&=Hx(t),\end{split}

(7)

where
x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the state with the initial value
x^{\mathsf{ini}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
, and the control parameters are given as

F\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n\times n},\quad G\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{n\times p},\quad\text{and}\quad H\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{m\times n}.

It has been studied in
[
17
]
that the state matrix, such as

of (

), needs to be an integer matrix, in order to implement a dynamic system over encrypted data.
Subsequently, there have been studies
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
on converting a given controller to have an integer state matrix, while preserving its control performance
1
1

1

The conversion is always possible through approximation
[
18
]
. Other results
[
19
,
20
]
avoid such approximation under some systematic assumptions.
.
Throughout this paper, we assume that such conversion has been conducted a priori.
Moreover, the elements of

The objective is to implement (

) as an encrypted controller, which satisfies the followings:

•

The encrypted controller performs an unlimited number of recursive homomorphic multiplications, on the Ring-LWE based cryptosystem described in Section

.

•

It operates for an infinite time horizon, without bootstrapping or any other methods to refresh the (encrypted) state.

The performance of the proposed controller is equivalent to that of (

III
Encrypted Controller

In this section, we present a method to operate the controller (
7
) using the Ring-LWE based scheme.
It is shown that the effect of error growth is suppressed by the closed-loop stability, enabling the proposed encrypted controller to execute an unlimited number of recursive homomorphic multiplications without the use of bootstrapping.
Additionally, we provide an explicit guideline for selecting parameters to achieve (

).

III-A

Encrypted controller design

To implement the operations of (
7
) over encrypted data, we utilize the homomorphic properties stated in Propositions
1
and
2
.
Since the plaintext space of the Ring-LWE based cryptosystem is a polynomial ring, each component of the matrices and vectors in (
7
) is regarded as a constant polynomial.

During the offline procedure, the control parameters—
F
,
G
, and
H
of (
7
)—and the initial state
x^{\mathsf{ini}}
of the controller are quantized and encrypted as follows;
let
{\mathsf{r}}>0
be the quantization step size for the plant output at the sensor.
As the elements of
G
,
H
, and
x^{\mathsf{ini}}
belong to
{\mathbb{Q}}
, a scale factor
1/{\mathsf{s}}\in\mathbb{N}
can be chosen such that

\displaystyle G/{\mathsf{s}}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times p},

\displaystyle H/{\mathsf{s}}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n},

\displaystyle x^{\mathsf{ini}}/({\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{s}})

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(9)

We also introduce a parameter
1/{\mathsf{L}}\in\mathbb{N}
, in order to deal with the effect of errors as illustrated in Remark
1
.
Then, the control parameters and the initial state are encrypted as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{F}}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}(F\!\!\!\mod q),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}(G/{\mathsf{s}}\!\!\!\mod q),

(10)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Enc}}^{\prime}(H/{\mathsf{s}}\!\!\!\mod q),

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathsf{ini}}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Enc}}(x^{\mathsf{ini}}/({\mathsf{r}}{\mathsf{s}}{\mathsf{L}})\!\!\!\mod q).

The rationale behind this is that the external product between a Ring-GSW ciphertext and a Ring-LWE ciphertext returns a Ring-LWE ciphertext,
as the product between a matrix and a vector returns a vector.

The encrypted control system operates as follows:
At each time step
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
of the online procedure, the sensor quantizes the plant output
y(t)
as

\bar{y}(t):=\left\lceil\frac{y(t)}{{\mathsf{r}}}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}^{p},

(11)

and then encrypts it as

{\mathbf{y}}(t):={\mathsf{Enc}}(\bar{y}(t)/{\mathsf{L}}\!\!\!\mod q)\in R_{q}^{2p},

(12)

which becomes the input of the encrypted controller.

IV
Encrypted Controller with Packing

In this section, we propose a novel packing algorithm that encodes a vector into a polynomial, which significantly reduces the number of operations required for a matrix-vector multiplication.
Then, we present an encrypted controller that integrates the packing algorithm, offering improved computational and memory efficiency compared to the encrypted controller (
13

We again analyze and show that the effect of error growth can be arbitrarily bounded by means of the closed-loop stability.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have introduced an encrypted dynamic controller that executes an unlimited number of recursive homomorphic multiplications on a Ring-LWE based cryptosystem without bootstrapping.
By deriving a controller over
{\mathbb{R}}
whose performance is equivalent to that of the encrypted controller, we have shown that the effect of error growth can be arbitrarily bounded by means of the closed-loop stability.
We have established a sufficient condition on the parameters
\{q,{\mathsf{r}},{\mathsf{s}},{\mathsf{L}}\}
under which the proposed encrypted controller guarantees a desired control performance.
Furthermore, we have proposed and applied a novel packing algorithm that leads to improved computational and memory efficiency.

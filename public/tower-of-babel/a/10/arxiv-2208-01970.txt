Harnack inequality for solutions of the p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) -Laplace equation under the precise non-logarithmic Zhikov’s conditions
Igor I. Skrypnik, Yevgeniia A. Yevgenieva

Abstract
We prove continuity and Harnack’s inequality for bounded solutions to the equation div ( | ∇ u | p ⁡ ( x ) − 2 ∇ u ) = 0 , p ( x ) = p + L log ⁡ log ⁡ 1 | x − x 0 | log ⁡ 1 | x − x 0 | , L > 0 , {\rm div}\big(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\,\nabla u\big)=0,\quad p(x)=p+L\frac{\log\log\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}}{\log\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}},\quad L>0, under the precise non-logarithmic condition on the function p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) . Keywords: p ⁡ ( x ) p(x) -Laplace equation, non-logarithmic conditions, continuity of solutions, Harnack’s inequality. MSC (2010) : 35B09, 35B40, 35B45, 35B65.

Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
.
In this paper we are concerned with elliptic equations of the type

{\rm div}\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla u)=0,\quad x\in\Omega.

(1.1)

We suppose that the functions
\mathbf{A}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

are such that
\mathbf{A}(\cdot,\xi)
are Lebesgue measurable for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
\mathbf{A}(x,\cdot)
are continuous for almost all

We assume also that the following structure conditions are satisfied

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(x,\xi)\,\xi

\displaystyle\geqslant K_{1}\,|\xi|^{p(x)},

(1.2)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{A}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{2}\,|\xi|^{p(x)-1},

where

are positive constants,
p(x)=p+p(|x-x_{0}|),\quad p(|x-x_{0}|)=L\dfrac{\log\log\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}}{\log\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}}

The aim of this paper is to establish basic qualitative properties such as
continuity of bounded solutions and Harnack’s inequality
for non-negative bounded solutions to equation (

The case when condition (

logarithmic case. It turns out that such non-logarithmic condition is a precise condition for the smoothness of finite functions in the corresponding Sobolev space
W^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)

Zhikov

\osc\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\,p(x)\leqslant L\,\frac{\log\log\frac{1}{r}}{\log\frac{1}{r}},\quad 0<L\leqslant\frac{p}{n}.

We would like to mention the approach taken in this paper. To prove the interior continuity we use
De Giorgi’s approach. Let us consider the standard De Giorgi’s class
DG_{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
of functions

which corresponds to

\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\,|\nabla(u-k)_{\pm}|^{p(x)}\,\zeta^{q}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\bigg(\frac{u-k}{r\sigma}\bigg)_{\pm}^{p(x)}\,dx,\quad k\in\mathbb{R}^{1},\quad\sigma\in(0,1),

(1.15)

B_{16r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and
\zeta(x)
is the correspondent cut-off function for the ball
B_{16r}(x_{0})
. Using the Young inequality,
by conditions (
1.4
) we have

\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\,|\nabla(u-k)_{\pm}|^{p_{-}}\,\zeta^{q}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\mu(r)\,\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\bigg(\frac{u-k}{r}\bigg)_{\pm}^{p_{-}}\,dx+\\
+\gamma\big|B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{(u-k)_{\pm}>0\}\big|,\quad p_{-}:=\min\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}p(x).

This estimate leads us to condition (
1.6
) (see, e.g.
[

,
47
]
). It is easy to see that condition (
1.6
) fails for the function

\mu(r)=L\log\log\frac{1}{r}
. To avoid this, using the Young inequality and our choice of

we rewrite inequality
(
1.15
) as

\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\,\bigg(\frac{M_{\pm}(k,r)}{r}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)}|\nabla(u-k)_{\pm}|^{p}\,\zeta^{q}\,dx\leqslant\\
\leqslant\gamma\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\bigg(\frac{M_{\pm}(k,r)}{r}\bigg)^{p}\int\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{(u-k)_{\pm}>0\}}\bigg(\frac{M_{\pm}(k,r)}{r}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)}\,dx,\quad M_{\pm}(k,r):=\sup\limits_{B_{r}(x_{0}}(u-k)_{\pm}.

(1.16)

It appears that the weight
\bigg(\dfrac{M_{\pm}(k,r)}{r}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)}
satisfies the Muckenhoupt type properties. In Section

we define the correspondent weighted De Giorgi’s classes by inequalities (

for the functions which belong to these classes.

The main difficulty arising in the proof of the Harnack type inequalities is related to the so-called theorem on the expansion of positivity.
Roughly speaking, having information on the measure of the ”positivity set” of
u
over the ball

B_{r}(\overline{x})\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0})
:

\left|\left\{x\in B_{r}(\overline{x}):u(x)\geqslant m\right\}\right|\geqslant\alpha(r)\,|B_{r}(\overline{x})|,\quad\alpha(r)=\gamma^{-1}\exp(-\mu^{\beta}(r)),

with some
r>0
,
m>0
and
\gamma>1
, and using the standard De Giorgi’s or
Moser’s arguments, we inevitably arrive at the estimate

u(x)\geqslant\gamma^{-1}\,\exp\big(-\gamma\exp(\mu^{c}(r))\big),\ \ x\in B_{2r}(\overline{x}),

with some
\gamma
,
c>1
. This estimate leads us to condition (
1.6
)
(see, e.g.
[
41
,
47
]
). Note that we can not use the classical approach of Krylov and Safonov
[
31
]
,
DiBenedetto and Trudinger
[
19
]
, as it was done in
[
9
]
under the logarithmic conditions. We also can not use the local clustering lemma of DiBenedetto, Gianazza and Vespri
[
17
]
(see also
[
18
,
50
]
). Difficulties arise not only due to the constant
\alpha(r)
which depends on
r
, but also when an additional term, that couldn’t be estimated, occurs during the process of iteration from
B_{r}(\bar{x})
to
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
. To overcome it, we use a workaround that goes back
to Mazya
[
38
]
and Landis
[
33
,
34
]
papers.

We will demonstrate our approach on the
p
-Laplacian. Fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
and let
0<r<\rho
,

E\subset B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0})
,
B_{16\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and consider solution

v:=v(x,m)
of the following problem:

{\rm div}\big(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\big)=0,\quad x\in\mathcal{D}:=B_{16\rho}(x_{0})\setminus E,

(1.17)

v-m\psi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathcal{D}),

(1.18)

where
m>0
is some fixed number, and
\psi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{16\rho}(x_{0}))
,
\psi=1
on
E
.

By the well-known estimate (see e.g.
[
23
]
) we have

\min\limits_{B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}v\geqslant\gamma^{-1}\,m\,\bigg(\frac{C_{p}(E)}{\rho^{n-p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

where
C_{p}(E)
is a capacity of the set
E
.
By the Poincare inequality from the previous we obtain

\min\limits_{B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}v\geqslant\gamma^{-1}\,m\,\bigg(\frac{|E|}{\rho^{n}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

(1.19)

Let
u
be a non-negative bounded super-solution to the
p
-Laplace equation in
\Omega
and construct the set

E(\rho,m):=B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\{u>m\},\quad 0<m<\sup\limits_{\Omega}u.

Consider also a solution
v
of the problem (
1.17
), (
1.18
) with
E
replaced by
E(\rho,m)
.
Then since
u\geqslant v
on
\partial\mathcal{D}
, by the maximal principle and by (
1.19
) we obtain

m(2\rho):=\min\limits_{B_{2\rho}(x_{0})}u\geqslant\gamma^{-1}\,m\,\bigg(\frac{|E(\rho,m)|}{\rho^{n}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

which by standard arguments yields for any
\theta\in(0,p-1)

|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|^{-1}\,\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\,u^{\theta}\,dx=|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|^{-1}\,\theta\,\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}E(\rho,m)\,m^{\theta-1}\,dm\leqslant m^{\theta}(2\rho)+\\
+\gamma m^{p-1}(2\rho)\int\limits_{m(2\rho)}^{\infty}\,m^{\theta-p}\,dm\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{p-1-\theta}\,m^{\theta}(2\rho),

from which the weak Harnack type inequality follows.

In Sections
3,4
we adapt this simple idea to the case of
p(x)
-Laplacian with non-logarithmic growth.
The weight

\bigg(\frac{M(\rho_{1})}{\rho_{1}}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)},\quad M(\rho_{1}):=\sup\limits_{B_{16\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\rho_{1}}(x_{0})}\,v,\quad\rho<\rho_{1}<16\rho

which naturally arises in the proof of Theorem
1.2
also satisfies the Muckenhoupt type conditions.

\frac{1}{\log\log\dfrac{1}{16\rho}}+C_{3}\,L\,\frac{\log\log\dfrac{1}{16\rho}}{\log\dfrac{1}{16\rho}}\leqslant 1,

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is almost standard. For fixed
\sigma\in(0,1/8),s\in(3/4\rho,7/8\rho),k>0
and

j=0,1,2,...
set
k_{j}:=k-k2^{-j},\rho_{j}:=s(1-\sigma+\sigma 2^{-j}),\bar{\rho}_{j}:=\dfrac{1}{2}(\rho_{j}+\rho_{j+1}),B_{j}:=B_{\rho_{j}}(x_{0}),\\
\bar{B}_{j}:=B_{\bar{\rho}_{j}}(x_{0})
and let
M_{0}:=\max\limits_{B_{0}}u,M_{\sigma}:=\max\limits_{B_{\infty}}u
. Denote by
\zeta_{j}
a non-negative piecewise smooth cutoff function in
\bar{B}_{j}

that equals one on
B_{j+1}
, such that
|\nabla\zeta_{j}|\leqslant\gamma\dfrac{2^{j}}{\sigma\rho}.
Set also

w_{0}(x):=\bigg(1+\dfrac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)}
and
w_{0}(F):=\int\limits_{F}\,w_{0}(x)\,dx
. Evidently we have

\bigg(\dfrac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)}\leqslant w_{0}(x)\leqslant\gamma\bigg(\dfrac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{p(|x-x_{0}|)}
,
if
M_{0}\geqslant\rho_{0}
.

Note that similarly to (
2.5
), (
2.6
) there hold

\gamma^{-1}\bigg(1+\frac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{-tp(\rho_{0})}\leqslant|B_{0}|^{-1}\,\int\limits_{B_{0}}w^{-t}_{0}(x)\,dx\leqslant\gamma\bigg(1+\frac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{-tp(\rho_{0})},t>0,

(4.7)

\gamma^{-1}\bigg(1+\frac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{tp(\rho_{0})}\leqslant|B_{0}|^{-1}\,\int\limits_{B_{0}}w^{t}_{0}(x)\,dx\leqslant\gamma\bigg(1+\frac{M_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\bigg)^{tp(\rho_{0})},\quad t>0,

(4.8)

provided that

\frac{1}{\log\log\dfrac{1}{\rho_{0}}}+t\,\bar{C}\,L\,\log(1+\frac{M}{\rho_{0}})\frac{\log\log\dfrac{1}{\rho_{0}}}{\log^{2}\dfrac{1}{\rho_{0}}}\leqslant 1,

(4.9)

where
\bar{C}=\max(C,C_{1})
and
C
,
C_{1}
are the constants defined in Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
. Therefore Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
continue to hold in
B_{0}
with
w_{\pm}(x,u,k,r)
replaced by
w_{0}(x)
.

Furthter we will assume that
M_{0}\geqslant\rho_{0}.
Test identity (
1.7
) by
\varphi=(u-k_{j+1})_{+}\,\zeta^{q}_{j}
, then

\int\limits_{\bar{B}_{j}}\,|\nabla(u-k_{j+1})_{+}|^{p(x)}\,\zeta^{q}_{j}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\,2^{j\gamma}\,\int\limits_{\bar{B}_{j}}\bigg(\frac{u-k_{j+1}}{\sigma\rho}\bigg)_{+}^{p(x)}\,dx\leqslant\\
\leqslant\gamma\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\frac{2^{j\gamma}}{\rho^{p}}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}}\,w_{0}(x)\,(u-k_{j})_{+}^{p}\,dx.

From this by the Young inequality, assuming that
k>\varepsilon_{0}\,M_{0}
,
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
is small enough, we obtain

\int\limits_{\bar{B}_{j}}\,w_{0}(x)\,|\nabla(u-k_{j+1})_{+}|^{p}\,\zeta^{q}_{j}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\bigg(\frac{M_{0}}{\rho}\bigg)^{p}\,\int\limits_{\bar{B}_{j}\cap\{u>k_{j+1}\}}\,w_{0}(x)\,dx+\\
\gamma\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\frac{2^{j\gamma}}{\rho^{p}}\,\,\int\limits_{B_{j}}w_{0}(x)\,(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{p}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,2^{\char 17\relax\gamma}\bigg(\frac{M_{0}}{\rho\,k}\bigg)^{p}\,\int\limits_{B_{j}\cap\{u>k_{j}\}}\,w_{0}(x)\,(u-k_{j})^{p}_{+}\,dx+\\
+\gamma\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\frac{2^{j\gamma}}{\rho^{p}}\,\,\int\limits_{B_{j}}w_{0}(x)\,(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{p}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\,\varepsilon^{-\gamma}_{0}\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\frac{2^{j\gamma}}{\rho^{p}}\,\,\int\limits_{B_{j}}w_{0}(x)\,(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{p}\,dx,

provided that (
4.9
) holds. From this similarly to (
3.8
) we obtain

M_{\sigma}\leqslant\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}_{0}\,M_{0}+\gamma\,\varepsilon^{-\gamma}_{0}\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\bigg([w_{0}(B_{0})]^{-1}\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,w_{0}(x)\,u^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.10)

Let us estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.10
), using Lemma
2.1
we obtain for any
0<\theta<p

and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

\bigg([w_{0}(B_{0})]^{-1}\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,w_{0}(x)\,u^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant M^{1-\frac{\theta}{2p}}_{0}\,\bigg([w_{0}(B_{0})]^{-1}\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,w_{0}(x)\,u^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\\
\leqslant\varepsilon M_{0}+\gamma\varepsilon^{-\gamma}\bigg([w_{0}(B_{0})]^{-1}\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,w_{0}(x)\,u^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{2}{\theta}}\leqslant\\
\leqslant\varepsilon M_{0}+\gamma\varepsilon^{-\gamma}[w_{0}(B_{0})]^{-\frac{2}{\theta}}\bigg(\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,w^{2}_{0}(x)\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\,\bigg(\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,u^{\theta}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leqslant\\
\leqslant\varepsilon M_{0}+\gamma\varepsilon^{-\gamma}\,\bigg(\rho^{-n}\,\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,u^{\theta}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}},

which together with (
4.10
) yield

M_{\sigma}\leqslant(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}_{0}+\varepsilon)\,M_{0}+\gamma\,\varepsilon^{-\gamma}_{0}\,\varepsilon^{-\gamma}\,\sigma^{-\gamma}\,\bigg(\rho^{-n}\,\int\limits_{B_{0}}\,u^{\theta}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

Choosing
\varepsilon_{0}
,
\varepsilon
small enough, iterating this inequality and taking into account our choices we arrive at

\max\limits_{B_{\rho/2}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\gamma\,\bigg(\rho^{-n}\int\limits_{B_{0}}u^{\theta}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+\gamma\,\rho,

provided that (
4.9
) is valid. This proves inequality (
1.11
).

Collecting estimates (
1.9
), (
1.11
) with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}(p-1),
we arrive at

\max\limits_{B_{\rho/2}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\gamma\,\big(\min\limits_{B_{\rho/2}(x_{0})}u+\rho\big),

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

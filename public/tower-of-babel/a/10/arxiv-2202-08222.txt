Braid orbits and the Mathieu group M 23 M_{23} as Galois group
Frank Häfner

Abstract
At present, the inverse Galois problem over ℚ \mathbb{Q} is unsolved for the Mathieu group M 23 M_{23} . Here an overview of the current state in realizing M 23 M_{23} as Galois group using the rigidity method and the action of braids is given. Computing braid orbits for M 23 M_{23} revealed new invariants of the action of braids in addition to Fried’s lifting invariant. These invariants can be used to construct generic braid orbits and more Galois realizations over ℚ \mathbb{Q} for the Mathieu group M 24 M_{24} , but until now did not lead to success for realising M 23 M_{23} as Galois group over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Thus M 23 / ℚ M_{23}/\mathbb{Q} remains open. Finally, heuristics for searching suitable class vectors with regard to the realization of groups as Galois groups are given.

2
The action of braids

For a finite group
G
, a generating system
\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})\in G^{m}
satisfying
the relation
\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}=\iota
is called a generating
m
-system of
G
.
The set of all generating
m
-systems of
G
is denoted by

\Sigma_{m}(G)=\{(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})\in G^{m}\mid\langle\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}\rangle=G,\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}=\iota\}.

The group
Inn(G)
of inner automorphisms of
G
acts on
\Sigma_{m}(G)
by simultaneous conjugation.
The set of classes
[\underline{\sigma}]=[\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}]
is denoted by

\Sigma^{i}_{m}(G)=\Sigma_{m}(G)/Inn(G).

For non-trivial conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
of
G
,
(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
is called a

class vector
of length
m
of
G
and the following sets can be defined:

\Sigma(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})=\{(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})\in\Sigma_{m}(G)\mid\sigma_{i}\in C_{i},i=1,\dots,m\}

and

\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})=\Sigma(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})/Inn(G).

A conjugacy class
C
of
G
is called
rational
if the values of the complex irreducible characters
of
G
on
C
are rational, i.e. if

\mathbb{Q}_{C}=\mathbb{Q}(\{\chi(C)\mid\chi\in Irr(G)\})=\mathbb{Q}

and a class vector is called
rational
if all of its conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
are rational.
Further, a class vector is called
symmetric
if
C_{1}=C_{2}=\dots=C_{m}
.
Finally, the number of elements in
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
is denoted by
l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
.

Let
H_{m}=\langle\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\dots,\beta_{m}\rangle
with the relations

\beta_{i}\beta_{j}=\beta_{j}\beta_{i},2\leq i<j\leq m,j-i\neq 1

(2.1)

\beta_{i}\beta_{i+1}\beta_{i}=\beta_{i+1}\beta_{i}\beta_{i+1},2\leq i\leq m-1

(2.2)

\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{m-1}\beta_{m}^{2}\beta_{m-1}\cdots\beta_{2}=\iota

(2.3)

be the
full Hurwitz braid group
.
Then
H_{m}
acts on
\Sigma^{i}_{m}(G)
by

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{i}}=[\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{i-2},\sigma_{i-1}\sigma_{i}\sigma^{-1}_{i-1},\sigma_{i-1},\sigma_{i+1},\dots,\sigma_{m}]

(2.4)

for
i=2,\dots,m
, see
[
17
, III, 1.2, Theorem 1.6 ff.]
and the action of
\beta_{i}^{-1}
is given by

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{i}^{-1}}=[\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{i-2},\sigma_{i},\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{i-1}\sigma_{i},\sigma_{i+1},\dots,\sigma_{m}]

(2.5)

for
i=2,\dots,m
.

For a class vector
(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
and
\pi\in S_{m}
, define
(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\pi}=(C_{\pi(1)},\dots,C_{\pi(m)})
and

(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{sy}=\{(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\pi}\mid\pi\in S_{m}\}.

Then
H_{m}
already acts on the set

\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{sy}=\bigcup_{\pi\in S_{m}}{\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\pi}}\subseteq\Sigma^{i}_{m}(G).

Define
\rho_{m}
to be the induced permutation representation of
H_{m}
on the set
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{sy}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
m\geq 3
, then the action of
\epsilon_{m}=\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{m}\in H_{m}

on
[\underline{\sigma}]\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{sy}
is given by

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\epsilon_{m}}=[\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3},\dots,\sigma_{m},\sigma_{1}].

Proof.

We have

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\epsilon_{m}}=[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{m}}=

[\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3},\dots,\sigma_{m}]^{\beta_{3}\cdots\beta_{m}}=

[\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{4},\dots,\sigma_{m}]^{\beta_{4}\cdots\beta_{m}}=

\dots

[\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\dots,\sigma_{1}\sigma_{m}\sigma_{1}^{-1},\sigma_{1}]=

[\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3},\dots,\sigma_{m},\sigma_{1}].

∎

The kernel of the natural projection

\pi_{m}:H_{m}\rightarrow S_{m},\pi_{m}(\beta_{i})=(i-1,i),i=2,\dots,m

is called the
pure Hurwitz braid group
and denoted by
B_{m}=kernel(\pi_{m})
.
We have

B_{m}=\langle\beta_{ij}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq m\rangle

with

\beta_{ij}=\beta_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots\beta_{j-1}^{-1}\beta_{j}^{2}\beta_{j-1}\cdots\beta_{i+1},1\leq i<j\leq m,

B_{m}\vartriangleleft H_{m}
and
H_{m}/B_{m}\cong S_{m}
.
The group
B_{m}
already acts on
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
. See also
[
17
, III]
.
Again we denote the induced permutation representation of this action with
\rho_{m}
as
it is the restriction of
\rho_{m}
from above to
B_{m}
.
For later usage, we state

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\rho:H_{m}\rightarrow S_{k}
be any permutation representation of degree
k
, then

(a)

\rho(B_{m})\leq A_{k}

(b)

\rho(H_{m})/\rho(B_{m})\cong U_{m}
with
U_{m}\leq S_{m}

Proof.

We have
sign(\rho(\beta_{ij}))=1
for
1\leq i<j\leq m
, thus
\rho(\beta_{ij})\in A_{m}
and (a) follows.
Part (b) comes from
H_{m}/B_{m}\cong S_{m}
.
∎

For
m\geq 3
, the center
Z(H_{m})
of
H_{m}
has order
2
and is generated by

\Delta_{m}=\epsilon_{m}^{m}=(\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{m})^{m},

see
[
17

.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
m\geq 3
, then
Z(H_{m})=\langle\Delta_{m}\rangle
acts trivial on
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
.

Proof.

We have
[\underline{\sigma}]^{\Delta_{m}}=[\underline{\sigma}]^{\epsilon_{m}^{m}}=[\underline{\sigma}]

by Proposition
2.1
.
∎

The next proposition allows us to deduce
\pi_{m}(\beta)\in S_{m}
for
\beta\in H_{m}
from its action
on
[\underline{\sigma}]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
m\geq 3
,
\beta\in H_{m}
and
[\underline{\sigma}]\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
.
Then we have

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta}\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\pi_{m}(\beta)^{-1}}

with the natural projection
\pi_{m}
.

Proof.

Proposition
2.4
is true for

\beta\in\{\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\dots,\beta_{m},\beta_{2}^{-1},\beta_{3}^{-1},\dots,\beta_{m}^{-1}\}

by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
For
i,j\in\{2,3,\dots,m\}
and
[\underline{\sigma}]\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
we have

\pi_{m}(\beta_{i}\beta_{j})=(i-1,i)(j-1,j)=\begin{cases}(i-1,i)(j-1,j)&|i-j|>1\\
(j-1,j,j+1)&i=j+1\\
(i-1,i+1,i)&j=i+1\\
\iota&i=j\end{cases}

(2.6)

and

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{i}\beta_{j}}\in\begin{cases}\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{(j-1,j)(i-1,i)}&|i-j|>1\\
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{(j-1,j+1,j)}&i=j+1\\
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{(i-1,i,i+1)}&j=i+1\\
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\iota}&i=j\end{cases}

(2.7)

Thus Proposition
2.4
is true for
\beta=\beta_{i}\beta_{j}
and
i,j\in\{2,3,\dots,m\}

and we get

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{i}\beta_{j}}\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{\pi_{m}(\beta_{j})\pi_{m}(\beta_{i})}.

In an analogous manner we can verify Proposition
2.4
for the elements

\beta_{i}^{-1}\beta_{j},\beta_{i}\beta_{j}^{-1},\beta_{i}^{-1}\beta_{j}^{-1},i,j\in\{2,3,\dots,m\}
.
Now the proof can be extended to

\beta=\beta_{k_{1}}^{e_{1}}\cdots\beta_{k_{l}}^{e_{1}}
with
e_{i}=\pm 1

using
\pi_{m}(\beta_{i})=\pi_{m}(\beta_{i})^{-1}
and

[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{i}},[\underline{\sigma}]^{\beta_{i}^{-1}}\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})^{(i-1,i)}

for
i=2,\dots,m
.
∎

Example 2.1
.

From Proposition
2.1
follows

Heuristics for searching class vectors

In this section, let
m\geq 4
. For realizing finite groups
G
as Galois groups using braid action,
we need
B_{m}
-orbits
Z\subseteq\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
of
G
with
g_{Z}=0

or small values of
g_{Z}
and
g_{Z^{sy}}=0
for
m=4
.
The formula for the computation of
g_{Z}
is

g_{Z}=1-|Z|+\frac{1}{2}((m-1)\cdot|Z|-\sum\limits_{j=2}^{m}z_{1j})

with
z_{1j}
,
j=2,\dots,m
the number of cycles of
\rho_{m}(\beta_{1j})
on
Z
.
We have already seen in section
3
that the length of
cycles of
\rho_{m}(\beta_{1j})
,
j=2,\dots,m
is bounded above by orders of elements in
G
.
Thus, if
|Z|
increases,
z_{1j}
,
j=2,\dots,m
must increase.
For a finite group
G
, let

{\bar{o}}_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in G}o(\sigma)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\limits_{C\in cl(G)}o(C)|C|\in\mathbb{Q}

be the average element order of
G
where the second sum runs over all conjugacy classes
C
in
G
.
Setting
|Z|={\bar{o}}_{G}\cdot x
, assuming
\lim\limits_{|Z|\to\infty}z_{1j}=x
,
j=2,\dots,m

and using the formula for
g_{Z}
leads to

{\tilde{g}}_{Z}=\frac{(m-3){\bar{o}}_{G}-m+1}{2{\bar{o}}_{G}}|Z|+1

and

\lim\limits_{|Z|\to\infty}\frac{{\tilde{g}}_{Z}}{|Z|}=\frac{(m-3){\bar{o}}_{G}-m+1}{2{\bar{o}}_{G}}=l_{m,G}.

. For simplicity, we use the rounded values

,

and

.

With increasing orbit size

, the ratio
\frac{g_{Z}}{|Z|}
gets closer to

.
Thus we may state

Conjecture 14.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
Z(G)=I
, then

\lim\limits_{|Z|\to\infty}\frac{g_{Z}}{|Z|}=l_{m,G}

and
g_{Z}
increases linearly with
|Z|
.

The linear growth of
g_{Z}
tells us to look for small
B_{m}
-orbits
Z\subseteq\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
.

If
B_{m}
acts transitively on
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
, we have a single
B_{m}
-orbit
Z

with
|Z|=l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
.
This motivates to look at the growth of
l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
for increasing dimension
m
.
As there are no non-trivial class vectors with
m=1
and class vectors with
m=2
lead to cyclic groups
G
,
we always assume
m\geq 3
.

Proposition 14.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
Z(G)=I
,
(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
a class vector of
G

with
m\geq 6
and
3\leq n\leq m-3
, then

l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})\geq|G|\cdot l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\cdot l^{i}(C_{n+1},\dots,C_{m}).

Proof.

Without restriction we may assume
l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\cdot l^{i}(C_{n+1},\dots,C_{m})>0
.
For
[\underline{\sigma}]\in S=\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
and
[\underline{\tau}]\in T=\Sigma^{i}(C_{n+1},\dots,C_{m})

we have

[\underline{\sigma},\underline{\tau}]=[\sigma_{1}^{\gamma},\dots,\sigma_{n}^{\gamma},\tau_{n+1}^{\eta},\dots,\tau_{m}^{\eta}]\in\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})

for all
\gamma\in G
and all
\eta\in G
.
Setting
\underline{\omega}_{\kappa}=[\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n},\tau_{n+1}^{\kappa},\dots,\tau_{m}^{\kappa}]

with
\kappa\in G
and

W_{[\underline{\sigma}],[\underline{\tau}]}=\{[\underline{\omega}_{\kappa}]\mid\kappa\in G\}\subseteq\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m}),

we get
|W_{[\underline{\sigma}],[\underline{\tau}]}|=|G|
. The set

Y_{S,T}=\bigcup_{[\underline{\sigma}]\in S,[\underline{\tau}]\in T}W_{[\underline{\sigma}],[\underline{\tau}]}\subseteq\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})

is a disjoint union and has therefore cardinality

|Y_{S,T}|=\sum_{[\underline{\sigma}]\in S,[\underline{\tau}]\in T}W_{[\underline{\sigma}],[\underline{\tau}]}=\sum_{[\underline{\sigma}]\in S,[\underline{\tau}]\in T}|G|=|G|\cdot|S|\cdot|T|

Finally, we get

l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})\geq|Y_{S,T}|=|G|\cdot l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\cdot l^{i}(C_{n+1},\dots,C_{m}).

∎

Considering all non-trivial splittings of
(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
and using
[
17
, I, Corollary 5.6]
leads to

Corollary 14.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
Z(G)=I
,
(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
a class vector of
G

with
m\geq 6
and
3\leq n\leq m-3
, then

\lfloor n(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})\rfloor\geq l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})\geq|G|\cdot\sum_{n=3}^{m-3}l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\cdot l^{i}(C_{n+1},\dots,C_{m}).

Using Proposition
14.1
we get

Proposition 14.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
Z(G)=I
and

l^{i}(D_{1},\dots,D_{n})\geq 1
for all class vectors
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n})
of
G
of length
n

with
n\in\{n_{0},n_{0}+1,\dots,2\cdot n_{0}-1\}
for some integer
n_{0}\geq 3
, then

l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})\geq{|G|}^{k-1}

holds for all class vectors of length
m
with
m=k\cdot n_{0}+l
,
l\in\{0,1,\dots,n_{0}-1\}
and
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
m=k\cdot n_{0}
with
k\geq 2
and choose
n=n_{0}
. Now we can apply Proposition
14.1
and get

l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{k\cdot n_{0}})\geq|G|\cdot l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n_{0}})\cdot l^{i}(C_{n_{0}+1},\dots,C_{k\cdot n_{0}})\geq

|G^{2}|\cdot l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n_{0}})\cdot l^{i}(C_{n_{0}+1},\dots,C_{2\cdot n_{0}})\cdot l^{i}(C_{2\cdot n_{0}+1},\dots,C_{k\cdot n_{0}})\geq...

After
k-1
steps we get

l^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{k\cdot n_{0}})\geq{|G|}^{k-1}\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}{l^{i}(C_{l\cdot n_{0}+1},\dots,C_{(l+1)\cdot n_{0}})}

with

l^{i}(C_{l\cdot n_{0}+1},\dots,C_{(l+1)\cdot n_{0}})\geq 1
for

by assumption.

For
m=k\cdot n_{0}+l
with

Corollary 15.1
.

If Conjecture
15.1
is true and

, then
\rho_{4}(B_{4})\cong G_{q}
.

Proof.

We have
\rho_{4}(B_{4})\cong C_{2}\cong S_{2}
for

. The transitive subgroups of
S_{3}
are
A_{3}\cong C_{3}

and

itself, thus
\rho_{4}(B_{4})\cong G_{3}
for

q\mid m

and
\rho_{4}(B_{4})\cong G_{q}
follows.
∎

16
Conclusion

In inverse Galois theory, the action of the pure Hurwitz braid group
B_{m}
on classes of generating systems
in
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
for a finite group
G
is a major tool for realising
G
as Galois group
over rational function fields.
At present, little is known about this action. Here, some generic
B_{m}
-orbits have been found and the
B_{m}
-action
on these orbits could be computed completely. Besides Fried’s lifting invariant in
[
3
]
and
the theorem of Conway and Parker in
[
17
, III, Theorem 6.10]
and
[
17
, Corollary 6.11]
,
this is a step towards a better understanding of the
B_{m}
-action. We can now explain the occurrence of
certain
B_{m}
-orbits in
\Sigma^{i}(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
in several cases. These
B_{m}
-orbits originate from
fixed points under the
B_{m}
-action resp.
H_{m}
-action of finite subgroups of
H_{m}
.

Until now, applying this knowledge to the Mathieu group
M_{23}
to find suitable
B_{m}
-orbits
that can be used to realize
M_{23}
as Galois group over rational function fields with field of constants
\mathbb{Q}

did not lead to success. One reason is that
M_{23}
has no
(2,3)
-generating systems.
Thus the problem, whether
M_{23}
occurs as Galois group over
\mathbb{Q}
still remains open.

We have seen that symmetric class vectors often lead to small
B_{m}
-orbits, so we should consider them.
For
M_{23}
and
m>4
, these class vectors are a challenge for currently available computers.

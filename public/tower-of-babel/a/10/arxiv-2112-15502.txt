Multiple-point residue formulas for holomorphic maps
Gergely Bérczi and András Szenes

Abstract
. We develop a new approach to the study of the multipoint loci of holomorphic maps between complex manifolds. We relate the k k -fold locus to the curvilinear component of the Hilbert scheme of k k points on the source space of the map, and using equivariant localisation, we derive a closed iterated multipoint residue formula. Our work is motivated by ideas and conjectures of M. Kazarian and R. Rimányi on residual polynomials.



1.
Introduction

Let
f:M\to N
be a holomorphic map between two complex manifolds of
dimensions
m\leq n
. A point
p
of
M
is called a
k
-fold point
of

f
if the fiber
f^{-1}f(p)
contains at least
k
points. For sufficiently
generic
f
the closure of the
k
-fold point locus represents a cohomology
class, and a
multiple-point formula
or
k-fold point formula

is a universal polynomial in the Chern classes of
f
representing this cycle.
Understanding the geometric origins of these universal polynomials, and finding
explicit formulas for their coefficients is one of the central problems of
enumerative geometry and global singularity theory.

Another somewhat simpler problem is the identification of the class of
the locus
\Sigma_{\alpha}
in
M
where
f
has a given singularity type
\alpha
. A
(contact) singularity type is determined by a nilpotent algebra
A
, and the
corresponding locus
\Sigma_{A}
in
M
is where
f
has local algebra
A
, see

[
1
]
. Assuming
M

is compact and
f
is sufficiently generic, the closure of
\Sigma_{A}
is an
analytic subvariety. Transplanting Thom’s pioneering work
[
49
]
in the
50’s into the complex/holomorphic world, one can say that the Poincaré dual
class
[\Sigma_{A}]\in H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is a polynomial

\mathrm{Tp}_{A}^{m\to n}(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
in the Chern classes
c_{i}(f)
of the difference
bundle
TM-f^{*}TN
. These expressions are called
the Thom polynomials of
the
singularity
; their full description has been a major open problem ever since
then, see
[
43
,
30
,
6
,
16
]
. The theory of Thom polynomials provides a
unified approach to many enumerative problems in algebraic geometry, which
often can be reformulated as problems of counting singularities of certain maps
of algebraic varieties.

In most of the enumerative problems one needs, however, an extension of Thom
polynomial theory to the case of multisingularities. This natural, but
significantly harder version studies the locus of points
p
in
M
where
f^{-1}f(p)
has

k
points with given singularity type at each point.
In the present paper, we study the simplest multisingularity problem, the
multipoint case, when
p
is a generic point of
f
, i.e., when
f
is a
local immersion of
M
into
N
at
p
.

A key difference between the theories is that Thom polynomials are expressed in
terms of the Chern classes
c_{i}(f)=c_{i}(TM-f^{*}TN)
, which we will informally
call
local
, while the formulas expressing the classes of the
multipoint loci involve more general classes of the form
f^{*}f_{*}c_{i}(f)
,
which we will call informally
global
. The very first example is the
double point formula:

f^{*}f_{*}[1]-c_{n-m}(f).

Multisingularity theory is a classical subject in enumerative geometry, and its
roots go back to the works of Plücker, and Salmon in the XIX. century (cf. for
an account in Kleiman
[
31
]
).
A major impetus to the subject was given by the work of Kleiman

[
32
,
33
]
, who developed several methods to approach the
subject: the

iteration principle
and the method of
Hilbert schemes
. His
work however applied to the rather restricted set of corank-1 functions only.
This assumption implies, in particular, the vanishing of a large
set of characteristic classes of the map
f
, and thus the resulting
formulas coincide with the conjectured general answer modulo an ideal only.

A breakthrough in the theory occurred following the work of Kazarian

[
28
]
and Rimányi
[
44
]
.
In,
[
28
,
29
]
Kazarian proposed a sieve-type multipoint formula (cf.
(
1
)), where the kernel of the sieve (which he named the

residual polynomial
) is a polynomial depending only on the local
classes. Experimenting with the restriction equations, Rimányi in
[
37
]
then
conjectured that the residual polynomials are simply the Thom polynomials for
the
A_{k}
singularities with appropriate shifts in the depth and codimension.
Assuming the sieve formula,
Rimányi and Marangell
[
37
]
also derived formulas for quadruple
points of
f
.

In
[
26
]
, it was suggested that the right approach to the
general
case lies in a refined theory of intersection on singular spaces.
In the present paper we describe such an approach and, in fact give an exact
meaning to Kazarian’s sieve formula in this framework, and then prove Rimányi’s
conjecture.

Let us describe our main result in some detail.
Let
M_{k}\subset M
and
N_{k}=f(M_{k})\subset N
denote the
k
-fold locus, and
\bar{m}_{k}\in H^{*}(M),\bar{n}_{k}=k\cdot f_{*}m_{k}\in H^{*}(N)
the multipoint classes. Note that the factor
k
comes from the degree of the map
f
on the
k
-fold locus. The
k
-fold locus in
M
and
N
have natural multiplicities, and we introduce

m_{k}=(k-1)!\cdot\bar{m}_{k}\ \ \ \text{ and }n_{k}=k!\cdot\bar{n}_{k},

where
n_{k}=f_{*}m_{k}
holds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a sufficiently generic holomorphic map between the complex manifolds
M
and
N
and
k>0
and integer. The
k
-fold locus class in
N
can be written as

(1)

n_{k}=\sum_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{s}=k}{k\choose i_{1}\ i_{2}\ \ldots\ i_{s}}S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\ldots S_{i_{s}}

where
S_{q}=f_{*}R_{q}
holds for the so-called residual polynomial

, which has the following closed expression for
1\leq q\leq k
:

R_{q}=\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{(-1)^{q-1}Q_{q-1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{q-1})\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq q-1}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{i+j\leq l\leq q-1}(z_{i}+z_{j}-z_{l})(z_{1}\ldots z_{q-1})^{m-n+1}}\prod_{j=1}^{q-1}c_{f}\left(\frac{1}{z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}

where
c_{f}\left(\frac{1}{z_{j}}\right)=1+\frac{c_{1}(TM-f^{*}TN)}{z_{j}}+\frac{c_{2}(TM-f^{*}TN)}{z_{j}^{2}}+\ldots+
is the total Chern class of
f
at
1/z_{j}
.

Explanations:

•

By sufficiently generic map we mean a stable map which Thom-Boardman transversal.

•

The iterated residue
\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}
is equal to
(-1)^{q-1}
times the
coefficient of
(z_{1}\ldots z_{q-1})^{-1}
in the Laurent expansion of the rational
expression in the domain
z_{1}\ll\ldots\ll z_{q-1}
and finally
Q_{q-1}
is a
polynomial invariant of Morin singularities.

•

n_{k}
is a polynomial in
c_{i}(f)
and the Landweber-Novikov classes
s_{J}(f)
on the target manifold
N
, where for
J=(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots)

s_{J}(f)=f_{*}(c_{1}(f)^{j_{1}}c_{2}(f)^{j_{2}}\ldots)

In
[
6
]
we proved that for a generic map
f:M\to N
the Thom polynomial of the
A_{k-1}
Morin singularity is

\mathrm{Tp}_{k-1}^{m\to n}=\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}Q_{k-1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-1})\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k-1}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{i+j\leq l\leq k-1}(z_{i}+z_{j}-z_{l})(z_{1}\ldots z_{k-1})^{m-n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}c_{f}\left(\frac{1}{z_{j}}\right)dz_{i}

Note the only difference between the two expressions is the power of
z_{1}\ldots z_{k-1}
in the denominator, which is equivalent to increasing the codimension of
the map
f
by
1
.This immediately implies Rimányi’s conjecture.

Theorem 1.2
.

The residual polynomial
R_{q}^{m\to n}
for a generic map
f:M\to N
is given by the Thom polynomial of
A_{q-1}
singularity in codimension
n-m-1
, that is

R_{q}^{m\to n}=\mathrm{Tp}_{A_{q-1}}^{m\to n-1}.

Our approach relies on integration over Hilbert schemes of points, which goes
back to our work on the Thom polynomials of
A_{k}
-singularities. The
fundamental steps are as follows:

•

The description of the class of the
k
-point loci as a generalized
tautological integral on the Hilbert scheme of
k
points on
M
.

•

Introducing a K-theoretic sieve which, emulating Kazarian’s sieve
(
1
),
localizes this integral in the neighborhood of the punctual locus of the
Hilbert scheme.

•

A vanishing result, which eliminates the integral of all but the
last element (the kernel) of the sieve.

•

The reduction of this integral to an integral over the curvilinear locus
of the Hilbert scheme.

•

An evaluation of this curvilinear integral using the techniques of

[
6
]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows: we begin with a detailed overview of
our strategy in §
2
; after giving the necessary background on
singularities in §
3
, we explain the basic structural results on
Thom polynomials and multipoint formulas in §
4
. Next, we
turn to our main geometric object, the Hilbert scheme of
k
points on
M
,
for which
we give the necessary background in §
5
. In §
6

we state the integral formula over the Hilbert scheme of points, and explain
the proof for
k=2
and
k=3
.
This is followed by the most technical part of the paper: the introduction of a local model of the Hilbert scheme in §
7

and the fully nested Hilbert scheme in §
8
. The last section,
§
9
contains the necessary technical results on equivariant
localisation.
The last three sections contain the proof of the main theorems: the integral
formula on Hilbert schemes, first in a basic form and then in an enhanced
push-forward version, and finally the multipoint formula.

2.
Overview of the strategy

In this section we give a brief outline of our strategy in order to explain the main ideas. The used concepts, terminology and their technical background is only briefly explained here, and the detailed definitions will be given in subsequent sections. This brief overview is intended to be a short guide to help the reader in following our argument.

2.1.
Reformulation of the problem with Hilbert schemes

Let

be a generic map between smooth compact complex manifolds of dimension
\dim(M)=m,\dim(N)=n

3.1.
Singularities and stability notions

We introduce the notation
J(m)=J(m,1)
for the space of germs of holomorphic functions
({\mathbb{C}}^{m},0)\to({\mathbb{C}},0)

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
at the origin,

is the algebra of power series without a constant term. Let

be the space of
k
-jets of holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
near the

by the ideal of those

Our basic object is

k
-jets of holomorphic

({\mathbb{C}}^{m},0)\to({\mathbb{C}}^{n},0)

\dim J_{k}(m,n)=n\binom{m+k}{k}-n
. We will call the elements of

as map-jets of order
k

One can compose map-jets via substitution and elimination of terms
of degree greater than
k

(13)

J_{k}(m,n)\times J_{k}(n,p)\to J_{k}(m,p),\;\;(\Psi_{2},\Psi_{1})\mapsto\Psi_{2}\circ\Psi_{1}.

When
k=1

may be identified with
n
-by-
m

matrices, and (
13
) reduces to multiplication of matrices. By
taking the linear parts of jets, we obtain a map

\mathrm{Lin}:J_{k}(m,n)\to\,{\rm Hom}\,({\mathbb{C}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}^{n}),

which is compatible with the compositions (
13
) and matrix
multiplication. The set

\mathrm{Diff}_{k}(m)=\{\Delta\in J_{k}(m,m);\;\mathrm{Lin}(\Delta)\text{ invertible}\}.

is an algebraic group under the composition map (
13
), and this gives the
so-called

action (also called

-action) of the group

[(\Delta_{L},\Delta_{R}),\Psi]\mapsto\Delta_{L}\circ\Psi\circ\Delta_{R}^{-1}

Singularity theory, in the sense that we are considering here, studies the left-right-invariant algebraic
subsets of

. A natural class of such invariant subsets are given by jets with the same local algebra; the local algebra (or nilpotent algebra) of the jet

is defined as

the algebra of function germs modulo the
ideal generated by the components of

. This is a finitely generated nilpotent algebra, whose dimension over
{\mathbb{C}}
is called the multiplicity of
f
. We call the singularity isolated, if
\mu(f)<\infty)
. For isolated singularities the multiplicity counts the maximum number of points in a fiber of
f
at a neighborhood of the singularity, that is

\dim(A_{f})+1=\max_{0<|y|<\epsilon}(\#f^{-1}(y))

Now let
A
be a finitely generated nilpotent algebra, and consider the set

(14)

\Theta_{A}^{m\to n}=\{(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\in J_{k}(m,n);\;A_{f}\cong A\}

Note that

is
\mathrm{Diff}_{k}(m)\times\mathrm{Diff}_{k}(n)
-invariant, and it serves as a prototype of singularity classes. A key observation though is that two map-jets with
the same nilpotent algebra may be in different

-orbits. However, there is an algebraic group action on

whose orbits are
exactly the sets

for various nilpotent algebras

. The so-called contact equivalence or

This can be also thought as a group action: the group

\mathscr{K}=\mathrm{Diff}_{k}(m)\times J_{k}({\mathbb{C}}^{m},\mathrm{GL}(n))

((\alpha,M)\cdot f=M\cdot(f\circ\alpha^{-1})

3.2.
Stratifications of maps

Consider a holomorphic map
f:M\to N
. We give a short survey on stability, transversality and stratifications of maps, essential to state the structural theorems in the next section. We refer
[
1
]
for details.

Two maps

are said to be

-equivalent
if there exist diffeomorphisms

such that

The map

is said to be

if any map that is close enough (together with its derivatives) to

-equivalent to

The set

of all

-jets of smooth maps of a fixed manifold

-jets of maps. The

-jet extension

is a map

which sends each point of

into the

-jet of

at

(and hence

A

of a smooth manifold is a finite union of pairwise-disjoint smooth path-connected manifolds with the property that the closure of any stratum consists of that stratum and a finite union of strata of lower dimensions. A map is
transverse to a stratified submanifold
if it is transverse to each of its strata. Thom’s Transversality Theorem (
[
1
]
) implies that the set of transverse maps is the intersection of countable number of open, dense subsets.

Let

be strata of a stratification such that

.
Whitney’s condition A
requires that if a)

and a sequence

converging to

converges in some Grassmannian to a limit

then

.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that

is a closed subset endowed with a stratification satisfying Whitney condition A. Then the set of maps

whose

Existence of good approximation of smooth maps does not imply that Thom-Boardman maps form a dense open subset of the space
J_{k}(M,N)
of
k
-jets of maps from
M
to
N
. Thom’s Transversality Theorem (see e.g.
[
1
]
) implies that this set is residual. But Wilson
[
52
]
] showed that the Thom-Boardman maps form an open subset of
J(M,N)
, if and only if either
m\leq n
and
3m-4<2n
or
m>n
and
2n<m+4
. These numerical conditions hold precisely if
\mathrm{codim}(\Sigma^{2})>m
, that is, there are no corank-
2
singularities of
f
(such maps are also called corank
1
maps or Morin maps). Moreover, If these numerical conditions hold, then a map is Thom-Boardman if and only if its germ is stable at each point.

We now prove some simple structural statements about the Thom-Boardman classification. Recall that the Thom-Boardman classes
\Sigma^{I}
do not give a stratification of the jet space
J_{k}(M,N)
; Porteous pointed out the existence of generic smooth maps
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{5}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{5}
with singularities of type
\Sigma^{2}
and
\Sigma^{1,1,1,1}
, both of dimension
1
, such that
\Sigma^{2}(f)
contains some isolated points in the closure of
\Sigma^{1,1,1,1}(f)
. In general,
\Sigma^{i}\nsubset\overline{\Sigma^{1,\ldots,1}}
holds, but if the number of
1
’s is not more then
i
, then one contains the other according to the next Proposition.

Proposition 3.16
.

For a Boardman symbol
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
and a Thom-Boardman map
f:M\to N
we have

\Sigma^{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{k}}(f)\subset\overline{\Sigma^{i_{1},\ldots i_{k}}(f)}

Proof.

First we prove the statement for
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})=(1,\ldots,1)
. The Thom-Boardman class
\Sigma^{1,1,\ldots 1}
is equal to the Morin contact singularity class:

\Sigma^{1,1,\ldots 1}=\{f\in J_{k}(m,n):A_{f}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}[t]/t^{k}\}\subset J_{k}(m,n).

The test curve model introduced in
[
6
,
3
]
says that

f\in\Sigma^{1,1,\ldots 1}\Leftrightarrow\exists\gamma\in J_{k}^{\mathrm{reg}}(1,m)\text{ such that }f\circ\gamma=0

that is,
\Sigma^{1,\ldots,1}
is the set of those
k
-jets of germs on
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
at the origin which vanish on some regular curve
\gamma
; we call such a

a test
curve. Test curves of germs are generally not unique: if
\gamma
is a test
curve for
f\in\Sigma^{1,1,\ldots 1}
, and
\varphi\in J_{k}^{\mathrm{reg}}(1,1)
is a
polynomial reparametrisation of
{\mathbb{C}}
at the origin, then
\gamma\circ\varphi
is an other test curve of
\Psi
:

\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

f\circ\gamma=0\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \ f\circ(\gamma\circ\varphi)=0

In fact, we get all test curves of
f
in this way if the

, holds: the linear part of
f
has

1
-dimensional kernel. For a given
\gamma\in J_{k}^{\mathrm{reg}}({1},{m})
let

denote the set of
solutions of the equation
f\circ\gamma=0
, that is,

(17)

\mathscr{S}^{n}_{\gamma}=\left\{f\in J_{k}({m},{n}),f\circ\gamma=0\text{ up to order }i\right\}.

The equation

reads as follows for

(18)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime})=0

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime\prime})+f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=0

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime})+2f^{\prime\prime}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})+f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=0

These are linear in

, hence

is a linear subspace of codimension

\mathrm{Grass}_{\mathrm{codim}=kn}(J_{k}(m,n))
, whose orthogonal,

. In fact,

(\mathscr{S}^{n}_{\gamma})^{\perp}=(\mathscr{S}^{1}_{\gamma})^{\perp}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

holds. The map

\gamma\mapsto(\mathscr{S}^{1}_{\gamma})^{\perp}

is

-orbits into the Grassmannian

\phi^{\mathrm{Grass}}:J_{k}^{\mathrm{reg}}({1},{m})/J_{k}^{\mathrm{reg}}(1,1)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Grass}_{k}(J_{k}(m,1)^{*}).

This is given by

\phi(\gamma^{\prime},\ldots,\gamma^{(k)})=\mathrm{Span}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}+(\gamma^{\prime})^{2},\ldots,\gamma^{(k)}+\sum_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{k}=k}(\gamma^{\prime})^{i_{1}}\cdots(\gamma^{(k)})^{i_{k}})

and the closure of the image is

.
Hence a generic point in

has the form

Now let

.

We will use the test curve model introduced in this proof for the description of the curvilinear Hilbert scheme, see Theorem

.

We finish this overview on Thom-Boardman classes with a general structure theorem on singularities of maps. Let

be a Thom-Boardman map and let

.

Assume that

4.
Thom polynomials and multisingularity classes

A natural generalisation of the multipoint problem addresses cohomological counts of multisingularity loci for generic maps. In this paper we only deal with the multipoint situation, but we give here a short survey on multisingularities to provide a wider context of the problem. In a forthcoming paper we address the multisingularity question. Thom polynomials are multisingularity classes for monosingularity types, and the conjecture of Rimányi formulates the nontrivial fact that Thom polynomials are the building blocks of multisingularity classes.

4.1.
Thom polynomials

Let
f:M\to N
be a complex analytic map between two complex manifolds,
M
and
N
, of dimensions
m\leq n
. We say that
p\in M
is a
singular
point of
f
if the rank of the differential
df_{p}:T_{p}M\to T_{f(p)}N
is less than
m
. Recall that a singularity class
O\subset J_{k}(m,n)
is a subset invariant under left-right equivalence.
For a singularity
O
and generic
holomorphic map
f:M\to N
, we can define the set

\Sigma_{O}(f)=\{p\in M;\;f_{p}\in O\},

which is independent of
coordinate choices on
M,N
. Assuming
M

is compact and
f
is sufficiently generic,
\Sigma_{O}(f)
is an analytic subvariety of
M
giving a Poincaré dual class
[\Sigma_{O}(f)]\in H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. This problem was first formulated by René Thom (cf.
[
49
,
21
]
) in the category of smooth varieties and smooth maps; in this case cohomology with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients is used. Thom discovered
that to every singularity
O
one can associate a bivariant characteristic class
\tau_{O}
, which, when evaluated on the pair
(TM,f^{*}TN)
produces the Poincaré dual class
[\Sigma_{O}(f)]
. One of the consequences of this result is that the class
[\Sigma_{O}(f)]
depends only on the homotopy class of
f
.

The structure of the
\mathrm{Diff}_{m}\times\mathrm{Diff}_{n}
-action on
J_{k}(m,n)
is rather complicated; even the parametrization of the orbits is difficult. However, as we saw in the oprevious section, there is a simple invariant on the space of orbits: to each map-germ
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, we can associate its local algebra
A_{f}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
defined as the quotient of the algebra of power series with no constant term by the ideal generated by the pull-back subalgebra
f^{*}(y_{1}\ldots,y_{n})
. This algebra
A_{f}
is trivial if the map-germ
f
is nonsingular, and it
\mathscr{A,K}
-invariant. Hence, for a fixed nilpotent algebra
A
with
n
generators one can associate the invariant subset

\Sigma_{A}=\{f\in J(m,n):A_{f}\simeq A\}
,
the set of jets with local algebra isomorphic to
A
. The
Thom principle
in this holomorphic setting (cf
[
27
,
6
]
) for
\mathscr{O}=\Sigma_{A}
tells that there is a jet order
k
depending only on
A
such that

[\Sigma_{A}(f)]=\mathrm{eP}[\Sigma_{A},J_{k}(m,n)](f^{*}TN,TM),

is obtained by substituting the Chern roots of
TM,f^{*}TN
into the
\mathrm{GL}(m)\times\mathrm{GL}(n)
-equivariant dual of
\Sigma_{A}
in
J_{k}(m,n)
. This equivariant dual sits in the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{S_{m}\times S_{n}}
of bi-symmetric polynomials on
m+n
variables. Damon and Ronga
[
10
,
46
]
proved that
[\Sigma_{A}(f)]
depends on
TM,f^{*}TN
only through the Chern classes of the difference bundle
TM-f^{*}TN
, and hence
[\Sigma_{A}(f)]=\mathrm{Tp}_{A}(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
is a polynomial in these classes, which we call the
Thom polynomial of
A
. Calculation of these polynomials remains a major open problem ever since then, see e.g
[
1
,
43
,
27
,
45
]
.

Toy example: the singularity locus.
The very first example should be the cohomological locus of singulariites, i.e the locus of points where
f
is not of maximal rank.

\mathrm{Sing}(f)=\{p\in M:\mathrm{rank}(d_{p}f)<m\}

This locus corresponds to the algebra
A_{1}={\mathbb{C}}[t]/t^{2}
and
f
has the same singularity locus as its first jet, so we can take
k=1
. Then

\Sigma_{A_{1}}=\{A\in J_{1}(m,n):A_{f}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}[t]/t^{2}\}=\{A\in\,{\rm Hom}\,({\mathbb{C}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\dim\ker A=1\}=\\
=\{A\in\,{\rm Hom}\,({\mathbb{C}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}^{n}):\exists!v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n},\,v\neq 0:\,Av=0\}

Then
\,{\rm Hom}\,({\mathbb{C}}^{m},{\mathbb{C}}^{n})=({\mathbb{C}}^{n})^{*}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is acted on by
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
. Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
and
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
denote the torus weights for the maximal tori
T^{m}\subset\mathrm{GL}(m)
and
T^{n}\subset\mathrm{GL}(n)
, respectively.
Then the
T^{m}\times T^{n}
torus weight under the left-right action on the
(i,j)
entry is
\theta_{j}-\lambda_{i}
.
We have a natural
T^{m}
-equivariant fibration
\pi:\overline{\Sigma_{A_{1}}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}
and the fiber over a point
[v]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}
is the
linear subspace
W_{v}=\{A;\;Av=0\}\subset\Sigma_{A_{1}}
.

The torus fixed-points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}
correspond to the coordinate axes, let
p_{1}\ldots p_{m}
denote these. The tangent weights at
p_{i}
are

\{\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{i};\;s\neq i\}
. The fiber at
p_{i}
is the set of matrices
A
with all entries in the

i
th column vanishing. This is a linear subspace in
\,{\rm Hom}\,(m,n)
, whose equivariant dual is the product of the normal weights:

\mathrm{eP}[W_{i},\,{\rm Hom}\,(m,n)]=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\theta_{j}-\lambda_{i})

Hence the
T^{m}\times T^{n}
-equivariant dual of
\Sigma_{A_{1}}
is given by the Atiyah-Bott formula:

\mathrm{Tp}(\Sigma_{A_{1}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\theta_{j}-\lambda_{i})}{\prod_{s\neq i}(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{i})}=\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{q=0}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+q\theta_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+q\lambda_{i})}\,\frac{dq}{q^{n-m+2}}=c_{n-m+1}(TM-f^{*}TN)

Here we used the simple but crucial observation that the residues of the rational differential form

-\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\theta_{j}-z)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-z)}\,dz.

at finite poles:
\{z=\lambda_{i};\;i=1\ldots m\}
exactly recover the
terms of the Atiyah-Bott sum. Apply the residue theorem, and change variables
z=-1/q
.

4.2.
Multisingularity classes

To every multisingularity class
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})
one can assign the corresponding multisingularity cycle
M(\alpha)
of a given map
f:M\to N
of relative dimension
l=\dim(N)-\dim(M)
. This cycle is defined as a subvariety in the Cartesian product
M^{s}=M\times\ldots\times M
, given as the closure of the set of tuples
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in M^{s}
of pairwise distinct points,
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
for
i\neq j
, such that
f(x_{1})=\ldots=f(x_{s})
and
f
has a singularity of the given type
\alpha_{i}
at
x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. Under some genericity condition
M(\alpha)
has the expected dimension equal to
\dim(M)-\mathrm{codim}\alpha_{i}-(r-l)l
, where
\mathrm{codim}\alpha_{i}
is the expected codimension of the cycle of the local singularity
\alpha_{i}
in the source manifold
M
of the map and is equal to the true codimension of the variety of this singularity in the space of jets of the maps.

Let us consider the natural projections
p_{M}:M(\alpha)\to M
and
p_{N}:M(\alpha)\to N
defined by the projection on the first factor of the Cartesian product
M^{s}
and by the map
f
, respectively. If
f
is proper, then the images of these projections are closed singular subvarieties in
M
and
N
, respectively. The theory of characteristic classes of multisingularities describes the cohomology classes
\overline{m}_{\alpha}\in H^{*}(M)
and
\overline{n}_{\alpha}\in H^{*}(N)
, dual to the images
p_{M}(M(\alpha))\subset M
and
p_{N}(M(\alpha))
, respectively (regarded as singular reduced subvarieties).

We often consider the classes
m_{\alpha}
and
n_{\alpha}
with their natural multiplicities, namely, we set

m_{\alpha}=\#\mathrm{Aut}(\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{s})\bar{m}_{\alpha},n_{\alpha}=\#\mathrm{Aut}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})\bar{n}_{\alpha}

where
\#\mathrm{Aut}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})=\#\mathrm{Aut}(\alpha)
is the number of permutations
\sigma\in S_{s}
such that
\alpha\sigma(i)=\alpha(i)
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. If
\alpha
contains
k_{j}
singularities of the same type
\beta_{j},j=1,2,\ldots
with pairwise distinct
\beta_{j}
, then
\#\mathrm{Aut}(\alpha)=k_{1}!\cdot k_{2}!\cdots
. These multiplicities are also equal to the degrees of the map of the multisingularity cycle
M(\alpha)
onto its image under the action of the projections
p_{M}
and
p_{N}
, respectively.
We note that the map
f
induces a map
p_{M}(M(\alpha))\to p_{N}(M(\alpha))
. The degree of this map is equal to the number of singularities
\alpha_{1}
in the tuple
\alpha=(\alpha)_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
, hence
n_{\alpha}=f_{*}m_{\alpha}\in H^{*}(N)
,
where
f_{*}
is the Gysin homomorphism (which is well defined if the map f is proper).

Kazarian
[
29
]
formulates a conjecture which expresses the classes
m_{\alpha}
and
n_{\alpha}
in terms of some characteristic classes related to the map

. These classes include, in particular, the Chern classes
c_{i}(TM)\in H^{*}(M)
and

themselves of the manifolds. One can obtain more classes from these by applying the pull-back and push-forward homomorphisms

The relative Chern classes

M
.

•

The Landweber-Novikov classes on the target manifold

For a multisingularity

the residual polynomial

is the polynomial in the classes

equal to the constant term of the polynomial

in the variables

5.
Hilbert schemes on smooth varieties

5.1.
Components and punctual components

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
m
and let

\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)=\{\xi\subset X:\dim(\xi)=0,\mathrm{length}(\xi)=\dim H^{0}(\xi,\mathscr{O}_{\xi})=k\}

denote the Hilbert scheme of
k
points on
X
parametrizing all length-
k
subschemes of
X
. For
p\in X
let

\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(X)=\{\xi\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X):\mathrm{supp}(\xi)=p\}

denote the
punctual Hilbert scheme
consisting of subschemes supported at
p
. Let

\rho:\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)\to\,{\rm Sym}\,^{k}X,\ \xi\mapsto\Sigma_{p\in X}\mathrm{length}(\mathscr{O}_{\xi,p})p

denote the Hilbert-Chow morphism. Then
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)_{p}=\rho^{-1}(kp)
.

The Hilbert scheme of
k
points on smooth surfaces is a
2k
dimensional nonsingular variety
[
17
]
. It occupies a central position in many important branches of mathematics and mathematical physics. In particular, it plays a pivotal role in enumerative geometry, see e.g
[
41
]
. The cohomological intersection theory of the Hilbert scheme of points on surfaces can be approached from several different directions: 1) via the inductive recursions set up in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
; 2) using Nakajima calculus
[
41
,
20
,
35
]
or more recently 3) virtual localisation on Quot schemes
[
19
,
38
]
.
Lehn’s conjecture

[
35
]
on top Segre numbers of tautological line bundles incapsulates the complexity of this theory.

While Hilbert schemes on surfaces are well-understood and broadly studied, the Hilbert scheme of points on manifolds of dimension three or higher are extremely wild objects with several unknown irreducible components and bad singularities
[
50
]
, and our undertanding of them is very limited.

In enumerative geometry applications we are mostly interested in the
geometric component
(also called the main component, or smoothable component) of the Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)
. This component, which we denote by
\mathrm{GHilb}^{k}(X)
is the closure of the open locus formed by the reduced points in
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)
, that is, those supported at
k
distinct points on
X
. This is an irreducible, but highly singular component of dimension
km
. Ekedahl and Skjelnes
[
11
]
constructs the geometric component as a certain blow-up along an ideal of
\,{\rm Sym}\,^{k}X
–this is the generalisation of the classical result of Haiman
[
22
]
on surfaces.

In this paper we develop iterated residue formulas for certain
tautological integrals
on the geometric component. The idea is to reduce integration to the
punctual curvilinear component
, which, in turn, is a projective compactification of a non-reductive quotient: the moduli of
k
-jets in
X
up to polynomial reparametrisatons.
A subscheme
\xi\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(X)
is called curvilinear if
\xi
is contained in some smooth jet of a curve
\mathscr{C}_{p}\subset X
at
p
. Equivalently,
\xi
is curvilinear if
\mathscr{O}_{\xi}
is isomorphic to the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[z]/z^{k}
.
The
punctual curvilinear locus
at
p\in X
is the set of curvilinear subschemes supported at
p
:

(20)

\mathrm{Curv}^{k}_{p}(X)=\{\xi\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(X):\xi\subset\mathscr{C}_{p}\text{ for some smooth curve }\mathscr{C}\subset X\}=\\
=\{\xi\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(X):\mathscr{O}_{\xi}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}[z]/z^{k}\}

We call the closure
\mathrm{CHilb}^{k}_{p}(X)=\overline{\mathrm{Curv}^{k}_{p}(X)}
the
punctual curvilinear component
supported at
p
.
\mathrm{CHilb}_{p}^{k}(X)
is an irreducible component of the punctual Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(X)
of dimension
(k-1)(m-1)
. On a smooth surface

,
\mathrm{CHilb}^{k}_{p}(S)=\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(S)
according to Brianchon. Moreover,

\mathrm{GHilb}^{k}_{p}(S)=\mathrm{Hilb}_{p}^{[k]}(S)\cap\mathrm{GHilb}^{k}(S)

is irreducible and equal to the full punctual Hilbert scheme. However, for
\dim(X)\geq 3

\mathrm{GHilb}^{k}_{p}(S)
is typically not irreducible; its components are called the
smoothable components
of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{p}^{k}(X)
. The punctual curvilinear component
\mathrm{CHilb}^{k}_{p}(X)
is a smoothable component. On the other hand, the Iarrobino-type punctual components
[
23
]
are not necessarily smoothable: their dimension can be higher than
km
. Description of the smoothable components is a hard problem and is unknown in general. It was an open question until recently whether there exist divisorial components of
\mathrm{GHilb}^{k}_{p}(S)
, that is, components
C_{p}\subset R_{p}^{[k]}
whose sweep
\cup_{p\in X}C_{p}
over
X
is a divisor of the good component. The dimension of this
C_{p}
is necessarily
m(k-1)-1
. Erman and Velasco in
[
15
]
give affirmative answer to this question when
d\geq 4,k\geq 11
. A beautiful illustration of the components and structure of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)
can be found in the PhD thesis of Jelisiejew
[
24
]
, called the
’Bellis Hilbertis’
.

To sum up, when
\dim(X)\geq 3
, and the number of points
k
is large enough, the punctual Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}_{p}(X)
is not necessarily irreducible or reduced, and it has smoothable and non-smoothable components. Among the smoothable components, there is a distinguished one, the curvilinear component, we will call all other smoothable components
exotic components
.

6.
Residue formula for tautological integrals on Hilbert schemes

A central result of this paper is an iterated residue formula for certain tautological integrals on the main component

Before presenting the proof of Theorem

Proof.

Let

be the Hilbert-Chow morphism, and introduce the notation

and observing that we can rewrite the second term as follows:

\int_{\mathrm{GHilb}^{2}(M)}\Phi(V^{[1,1]})=\int_{M\times M}\Phi\left(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\right).

As to the first term, we note that the integrand may be represented as a form supported in a neighborhood of

, where

\int_{\mathrm{GHilb}^{2}(M)}\left[\Phi(V^{[2]})-\Phi(V^{[1,1]})\right]=\int_{\nabla_{0}}\left[\Phi(V^{[2]})-\Phi(V^{[1,1]}\right]=\int_{\mathrm{CHilb}^{2}_{0}}\frac{\Phi(V^{[2]})-\Phi(V^{[1,1]})}{u}

Now we use the family version of Kalkman’s formula: we represent
\mathrm{CHilb}^{2}_{0}
as a

quotient of the normal bundle
\nabla_{M}
of
\Delta M\subset M\times M
. We lift the bundles
V^{[2]}
and
V^{[1,1]}
to equivariant bundles

and

\nabla_{M}

u

, and then the formula reads

\int_{\mathrm{CHilb}^{2}_{0}}\frac{\Phi(V^{[2]})-\Phi(V^{[1,1]})}{u}=\int_{\Delta M}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{z=\infty}\frac{\Phi(V_{z}^{[2]})-\Phi(V_{z}^{[1,1]})}{u_{z}\cdot c_{m}(\nabla^{z}_{\Delta M})},

where all the objects under the integral sign are restricted to
\Delta M
, the zero-section of
\nabla_{M}

M

•

•

V_{z}^{[1,1]}=V^{[1,1]}

•

•

, where the

Thus we have

\int_{\mathrm{CHilb}^{2}_{0}}\frac{\Phi(V^{[2]})-\Phi(V^{[1,1]})}{u}=\int_{\Delta M}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{z=\infty}\frac{\Phi(V\oplus V(z))-\Phi(V^{[1,1]})}{z\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z+\lambda_{i})}

and this completes the proof.

8.
Fully nested Hilbert schemes

The central object in our proof of the main theorems is a generalisation of the standard nested Hilbert schemes of points. Recall the space

\mathrm{Hilb}^{[1,2,\ldots,k]}(X)=\{(\xi_{1}\subset\xi_{2}\subset\ldots\subset\xi_{k})|\xi\subset X,\dim H^{0}(\xi_{i})=i\}\subset X\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{2}(X)\times\ldots\times\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}(X)

9.
Equivariant localisation and multidegrees

This section is a brief of equivariant cohomology and localisation. For
more details, we refer the reader to Berline–Getzler–Vergne
[
7
]
and Bérczi–Szenes
[
6
]
.

Let
{K}\cong U(1)^{m}
be the maximal compact subgroup of

T\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}
, and denote by
\mathfrak{t}
the Lie algebra of
{K}
.
Identifying
T
with the group
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, we obtain a canonical basis of the weights of
T
:

\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

For a manifold
M
endowed with the action of
{K}
, one can define a
differential
d_{K}
on the space
S^{\bullet}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}(M)^{K}
of polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{t}
with values
in
{K}
-invariant differential forms by the formula:

[d_{K}\alpha](X)=d(\alpha(X))-\iota(X_{M})[\alpha(X)],

where
X\in\mathfrak{t}
, and
\iota(X_{M})
is contraction by the corresponding
vector field on
M
. A homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
with
values in
r
-forms is placed in degree
2d+r
, and then
d_{K}
is an
operator of degree 1. The cohomology of this complex–the so-called equivariant de Rham complex, denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{T}(M)
, is called the
T
-equivariant cohomology of
M
. Elements of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(M)
are therefore polynomial functions
\mathfrak{t}\to\Omega^{\bullet}(M)^{K}
and there is an integration (or push-forward map)
\int:H_{T}^{\bullet}(M)\to H_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{point})=S^{\bullet}\mathfrak{t}^{*}
defined as

(\int_{M}\alpha)(X)=\int_{M}\alpha^{[\mathrm{dim}(M)]}(X)\text{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{t}

where
\alpha^{[\mathrm{dim}(M)]}
is the differential-form-top-degree part of
\alpha
. The following proposition is the Atiyah-Bott-Berline-Vergne localisation theorem in the form of
[
7
]
, Theorem 7.11.

Theorem 9.1

((Atiyah-Bott
[
2
]
, Berline-Vergne
[
8
]
))
.

Suppose that
M
is a compact complex manifold and
T
is a complex torus acting smoothly on
M
, and the fixed point set
M^{T}
of the
T
-action on M is finite. Then for any cohomology class
\alpha\in H_{T}^{\bullet}(M)

\int_{M}\alpha=\sum_{f\in M^{T}}\frac{\alpha^{[0]}(f)}{\mathrm{Euler}^{T}(T_{f}M)}.

Here
\mathrm{Euler}^{T}(T_{f}M)
is the
T
-equivariant Euler class of the tangent space
T_{f}M
, and
\alpha^{[0]}
is the differential-form-degree-0 part of
\alpha
.

The right hand side in the localisation formula considered in the fraction field of the polynomial ring of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{point})=H^{\bullet}(BT)=S^{\bullet}\mathfrak{t}^{*}
(see more on details in Atiyah–Bott
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
). Part of the statement is that the denominators cancel when the sum is simplified.

9.1.
Equivariant Poincaré duals and multidegrees

Restricting the equivariant de Rham complex to compactly supported (or quickly
decreasing at infinity) differential forms, one obtains the compactly
supported equivariant cohomology groups
H^{\bullet}_{{K},\mathrm{cpt}}(M)
. Clearly

The Rossmann equivariant localisation formula is an improved version of the Atiyah-Bott/Berline-Vergne localisation for singular varieties sitting in a smooth ambient space.

.

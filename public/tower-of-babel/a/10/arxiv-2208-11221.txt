Multinomial Cluster-Weighted Models for High-Dimensional Data
Kehinde Olobatuyi, Oludare Ariyo

Abstract
Modeling of high-dimensional data is very important to categorize different classes. We develop a new mixture model called Multinomial cluster-weighted model (MCWM). We derive the identifiability of a general class of MCWM. We estimate the proposed model through Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm via an iteratively reweighted least squares (EM-IRLS) and Stochastic Gradient Descent (EM-SGD). Model selection is carried out using different information criteria. Various Adjusted Rand Indices are considered as a different measure of accuracy. The clustering performance of the proposed model is investigated using simulated and real datasets. MCWM shows excellent clustering results via performance measures such as Accuracy and Area under the ROC curve. Keyword: Expectation Maximization algorithm, Stochastic Gradient Descent, Iteratively Re-weighted Least Square.

Background History

In model-based clustering and classification, cluster-weighted model (CWM) is a convenient approach when the random vector of interest consists of a response variable
Y
and a set
p
of explanatory variables
X
. CWM is an input-output inference framework based on the probability density estimation of a joint set of input feature and output target data. A broad family of cluster weighted models assumes that the components of the conditional distributions belong to the exponential family and the covariates are either continuous or finite discrete or both (
[
Ingrassia et al., 2015
]
). Different types of exponential families such as Gaussian, Binomial, Poisson, and
t
distributions have been considered for conditional distributions.
t
-CWM is introduced by
[
Ingrassia et al., 2012
]
, and subsequently the twelve mixture models with random covariates are introduced in
[
Ingrassia et al., 2014
]
. A polynomial Gaussian CWMs to capture the non-elliptical data is introduced in
[
Punzo, 2014
]
. The motivation of developing a family of CWMs is to solve the problem of assignment independence found in the finite mixture of linear regressions (
[
Hennig, 2000
]
). However, the applicability of CWMs for multi-class in high-dimensional space still remains an open research problem.
In this paper, we develop multinomial CWM to generally classify multi-class response data into different categories. We give an extensive derivation of the identifiability condition of the proposed model. To perform a maximum likelihood estimation of the proposed model, we derive the Expectation-Maximization algorithm from analytical and stochastic view such as iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) and Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) to make EM scalable for large dataset. For the first time we apply the variant of CWM to image classification problem, taking CWM from the perspective of regression to a classification.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we give a detailed explanation of the proposed model. The identifiability of the proposed model is derived in Sect.
2.1
. In Sect.
2.2
, we derive the estimation of the model parameters via EM algorithm. Furthermore, in Sect.
3
we present an experiment on simulated data and discuss convergence criterion. Sect.
4
shows the application of MCWMC on real datasets. Finally, in Sect.
5
, we draw some limitations and the main conclusions of this work.

Model specification

Let
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
be a pair of random vector
\mathbf{X}
of dimension
d
and the multiclass response variable
\mathbf{Y}
of dimension
J
defined on
\mathcal{D}
with a joint probability
p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.
\mathbf{X}
takes values in some space
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathrm{R}^{d}
and
\mathbf{Y}
takes values in
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathrm{R}^{J}
. Thus,
\b{x},\b{y}\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{d+J}
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be partitioned into
G
disjoint groups, say
\mathcal{D}_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathcal{D}_{G}
, i.e.
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\cdot\cdot\cdot\cup\mathcal{D}_{G}
. MCWM decomposes the joint probability
p(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
as follows:

p(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}p(\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{X},\mathcal{D}_{g})p(\mathbf{X}|\mathcal{D}_{g})\pi_{g},

(1)

where
p(\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{X},\mathcal{D}_{g})
is the conditional density of the multi-class response variable
\mathbf{Y}
given the predictor vector
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathcal{D}_{g}
,
p(\mathbf{X}|\mathcal{D}_{g})
is the probability density of
\mathbf{X}
given
\mathcal{D}_{g}
,
\pi_{g}=p(\mathcal{D}_{g})
is the mixing weight of
\mathcal{D}_{g}
, with constraints
\pi_{g}>0
and
\sum_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}=1
,
g=1,...,G
.

We take the conditional density of multiclass response variable to be a Multinomial distribution
\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{X},\mathcal{D}_{g}\sim Multi(\mathbf{\phi}_{jg},...,\mathbf{\phi}_{Jg})
, and the marginal density to be a Gaussian distribution
\mathbf{X}|\mathcal{D}_{g}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d}(\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g})
. Thereafter, we shall write
p(\mathbf{X}|\mathcal{D}_{g})=\psi_{d}(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g})
and
p(\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{X},\mathcal{D}_{g})=p(\mathbf{Y};\mathbf{\Phi}_{g})
,
g=1,..,G
, where the conditional densities are based on the nonlinear mappings which we will define later. Thus, Equation
1
becomes:

p(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y};\mathbf{\Theta})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}p(Y_{1}=y_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,Y_{J}=y_{J}|\mathbf{\phi}_{jg},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathbf{\phi}_{Jg})\psi_{d}(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g}),

(2)

where
\mathbf{\Theta}=(\mathbf{\Omega},\vec{\mu},\mathbf{\Sigma},\vec{\pi})
denotes the set of all model parameters,
\mathbf{\Phi}=(\mathbf{\phi}_{jg},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathbf{\phi}_{Jg})\in\mathrm{R}^{d\times J\times G}
denotes the coefficient of the local model, location parameter
\vec{\mu}\in\mathrm{R}^{d\times G}
, and
\mathbf{\Sigma}
is the positive definite covariance matrix.
Equation (
2
) is referred to as Multinomial CWM and the posterior is given by

p(\mathcal{D}_{g}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\frac{\pi_{g}p(Y_{1}=y_{1},...,Y_{J}=y_{J}|\mathbf{\phi}_{jg},...,\mathbf{\phi}_{Jg})\psi_{d}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g})}{\sum_{k=1}^{G}\pi_{k}p(Y_{1}=y_{1},...,Y_{J}=y_{J}|\mathbf{\phi}_{jk},...,\mathbf{\phi}_{Jk})\psi_{d}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\mu}_{k},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{k})}

(3)

In Equation (
3
),
\psi(.)
denotes the Gaussian density, the number of free parameters is
df=(GJd)+G[d(d+1)/2]+Gd+(G-1)
, and
G
is the number of groups.

We relate the expected value of
\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{\phi}_{j}
to the covariates
\mathbf{X}
through the relation
f_{jg}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\beta}_{jg})=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{i}\mathbf{\beta}_{jg}
, where

f_{jg}(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{\beta}_{jg})=\log\frac{\phi_{ijg}}{\phi_{i1g}}=\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{i}\mathbf{\beta}_{jg}.

(4)

In Equation
4

\beta_{0jg}
is an intercept and
\mathbf{\beta}_{1jg}
is a vector of regression coefficients, for
j=2,...,J
. We note that the intercept
\beta_{0jg}
is implicit in Equation (
4
) and the model matrix
\mathbf{X}
includes a column of ones.
Therefore, if
J=2
the MCWM reduces to Binomial CWM as a special case. The model used to predict the probabilities of different outcomes is given:

\mathbf{\phi}_{jg}=\frac{\exp\{f_{jg}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\beta}_{jg})\}}{1+\sum_{j=2}^{J}\exp\{f_{jg}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\beta}_{jg})\}}

(5)

Equation
5
can be written as the original probabilities
\phi_{jg}
rather than the log-odds by setting
\phi_{1g}=0
.

2.1
Identifiability

Sufficient identifiability conditions imply that any mixture distribution function from the specified model class can be uniquely parameterized, i.e. the parameters can be uniquely determined given infinitely many observations. On the contrary, if a mixture distribution is unidentifiable the parameters cannot be uniquely determined. Identifiability problems of finite mixture model can be categorized into
trivial
and
generic
problems
[
Fruhwirth-Schnatter, 2006
]
. Trivial identifiability is a result of empty components with the same parameters and can be avoided by restraining the feasible parameter space
\omega
to
\tilde{\omega}

\subset

\omega
,
\forall\hskip 3.61371pt\Theta\in\tilde{\omega}
:
\pi_{g}>0,\hskip 3.61371ptg=1,...,G
.

[
Teicher, 1963
]
,
[
Blischke, 1964
]
, and
[
Titterington et al., 1985
]
argued that mixtures of binomial distributions with generic problem are identifiable if the condition
M\geq 2G-1
is fulfilled, where
M
denotes the number of repetitions for a given individual. The restriction is necessary and sufficient for the model class of all mixtures with a maximum of
G
components. The result obtained by
[
Lindsay, 1995
]
for general class of mixtures of discrete exponential densities with
M+1
point supports the same condition applied for mixtures of multinomial distributions (
[
Grun, 2002
]
;
[
Elmore and Wang, 2003
]
). Moreover,
[
Hennig, 2000
]
showed that full rank covariance matrix is insufficient for identifiability of mixture of Gaussian regression models.

We establish the identifiability conditions and theorem for MCWM in order to estimate the parameters of model in Equation (
2
). Consider a parametric class of density function

\mathcal{F}=\{f(\mathbf{x};\psi):\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\psi\in\Psi\}

(6)

and the class of finite mixture of functions in

,

\mathcal{H}=\biggl\{h(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\varphi}):h(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\varphi})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}f(\mathbf{x};\vec{\Psi}_{g})\pi_{g},\hskip 7.22743pt\text{with}\hskip 7.22743pt\pi_{g}>0\hskip 7.22743pt\text{and}\hskip 7.22743pt\sum_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}=1,

f(.;\psi_{g})\in\mathcal{F},g=1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,G,\mathbf{\Psi}_{g}\neq\mathbf{\Psi}_{s}\hskip 3.61371pt\text{for}\hskip 3.61371ptg\neq s,G\in\mathcal{N},\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\mathbf{\varphi}\in\mathbf{\Theta}\biggr\}

(7)

This class is identifiable if for any two members of
\mathcal{H}
such as

h(\mathbf{x};\varphi)=\sum_{g=1}^{G}f(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\psi}_{g})\pi_{g},\hskip 7.22743pt\text{and}\hskip 7.22743pth(\mathbf{x};\tilde{\mathbf{\varphi}})=\sum_{v=1}^{\tilde{G}}f(\mathbf{x};\tilde{\mathbf{\psi}}_{s})\tilde{\pi}_{s}.

The equality

, such that

. The class of the MCWM is given by

\mathcal{P}=\biggl\{p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\mathbf{\varphi}):p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y};\mathbf{\varphi})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}F(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\beta}^{g}_{j})p(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g})\pi_{g},\hskip 7.22743pt\text{with}\hskip 7.22743pt\pi_{g}>0,\sum_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}=1,

\mathbf{\beta}_{j}^{g}\neq\mathbf{\beta}_{j}^{s}\hskip 7.22743pt\text{for}\hskip 7.22743ptg\neq s,(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{Y},\mathbf{\varphi}=\{\mathbf{\beta}_{j}^{g},\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g},\pi_{g};g=1,...,G\}\in\mathbf{\Theta},G\in\mathcal{N}\biggr\}

We provide the sufficient conditions for

p(.)

Theorem

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the class defined in Equation (
2

with probability one such that the mixture of multinomial distributions

\sum_{g=1}^{G}F(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x};\mathbf{\theta}^{g})\gamma_{g}(\mathbf{x})

(10)

where

, where

are positive weights summing to one for each

is identifiable in

for all

there exists a non-empty

which is a subset of

\sum_{i}\sum_{j}M_{ij}\geq 2G-1\hskip 7.22743pt\forall\hskip 7.22743pti\in\tilde{\mathbf{I}}_{g}.

The condition in Equation

The EM Algorithms for Parameter Estimation

Let
(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{1}),...,(\mathbf{x}_{N},\mathbf{y}_{N})
be a sample drawn from model

p(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y};\mathbf{\Theta})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}p(Y_{1}=y_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,Y_{J}=y_{J}|\mathbf{\phi}_{jg},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\mathbf{\phi}_{Jg})\psi_{d}(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g}),

The corresponding likelihood, for a fixed number of components
G
, is given by

L(\mathbf{\Theta})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}p(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{i};\big|\mathbf{\Theta})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}p(\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{\phi}_{ig})\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{i}|\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma_{g}})

(12)

Define
z_{i}=(z_{i1},...,z_{iG})^{\prime}
, with
z_{ig}=1
if
(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\prime})^{\prime}
comes from
\mathcal{D}_{g}

\big\{\big(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\prime}\big)^{\prime};i=1,...,N\big\}

L_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{\Theta})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{g=1}^{G}\pi_{g}^{z_{ig}}p(\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{\phi}_{ig})^{z_{ig}}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{i}|\vec{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma_{g}})^{z_{ig}}

z_{ig}
denotes the
gth

13

l_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{\Theta})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\bigg[z_{ig}\ln p(\mathbf{y}_{i}|\mathbf{\phi}_{ig})+z_{ig}\big\{\ln\pi_{g}+\ln\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{i}|\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma_{g}})\big\}\bigg]

(14)

l_{1c}(\mathbf{\Omega})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{g=1}^{G}z_{ig}\ln\bigg\{\prod_{j=1}^{J}\phi_{ijg}^{y_{ij}}\bigg\}

(15)

l_{2c}(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{g=1}^{G}z_{ig}\ln\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{i};\vec{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g})

(16)

l_{3c}(\vec{\pi})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{g=1}^{G}z_{ig}\ln\pi_{g}

(17)

where
\mathbf{\Omega}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{\beta}_{01g}^{\prime},...,\mathbf{\beta}_{1Jg}^{\prime})^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{k}=(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}_{1},...,\mathbf{k}^{\prime}_{G})^{\prime}
with
\mathbf{k}_{g}=(\mathbf{\mu}_{g},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{g})^{\prime}
.

Z
is a matrix whose rows are vectors of ones and zeroes, one at the position of the group and zero everywhere else.

We use EM algorithm

in the unobserved data

(18)

(x_{i},y_{i})^{\prime}
belong to the

for

and
z_{ig}^{(q)}
for

Q(\mathbf{\tau};\mathbf{\tau}^{(q)})=Q_{1}(\mathbf{\Omega};\mathbf{\tau}^{(q)})+Q_{2}(\mathbf{k};\mathbf{\tau}^{(q)})+Q_{3}(\mathbf{\pi};\mathbf{\tau}^{(q)})

On the M-step, at the

Maximization of

The updated estimates

Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we introduced a new cluster weighted models called a Multinomial CWM for clustering data with multiclass response variables. We used the softmax function as the probability of the multinomial distribution. MCWM extended preexisting Binomial CWM for binary response variables
[
Ingrassia et al., 2015
]
. Different from the previous work in the field of cluster weighted models, MCWM allows modeling of multinomial response variables. Furthermore, we described through a simulation study an EM algorithm via Iteratively Re-weighted Least Squares for parameter estimation. To investigate the performance of the model, several performance metrics were used such as confusion matrix, Receiver’s Operating Characteristics (ROC), and ARI with its variants. We also showed through different eight information criteria that the MCWM is able to discover the mixture components hidden in the data. Following the conditions of identifiability, we can conclude that MCWM is identifiable. We provided different supporting plots such as the classification plots, confusion matrix plots, and ROC plot. We also evaluated the performance of the proposed model on two real datasets. We showed that Multinomial CWM has higher accuracy compared to other models for the data.

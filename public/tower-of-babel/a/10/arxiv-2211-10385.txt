Introduction

For a natural number

A

Daisies were first introduced by Bollobás, Leader and Malvenuto in
[

-daisies, i.e., the maximum number of edges that an

-daisy. In this paper we will study the Ramsey number

-daisy. The number

is defined

(r,m,k)
-daisy.

Those numbers were already studied in
[
5

\displaystyle R_{r-k}(\lceil m/(k+1)\rceil-k)\leqslant D_{r}(m,k)\leqslant R_{r}(m)+k,

(1)

where

A natural question raised in

\displaystyle t_{r-2}(c_{1}m^{2})\leqslant R_{r}(m)\leqslant t_{r-1}(c_{2}m),

Let

3.1.
Erdős–Hajnal–Rado stepping-up lemma

For instructional purposes, we will briefly go over the stepping-up lemma in
[

4
]
using our notation. For

, let
N=R_{k-1}((n-k+4)/2)-1
and
\varphi:[N]^{(k-1)}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be a coloring of the
(k-1)
-tuples in
[N]
with no monochromatic subset of size
(n-k+4)/2
. Our goal is to find a coloring
\psi:[2^{N}]^{(k)}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
with no monochromatic subset of size
n
. This will give us that
R_{k}(n)>2^{N}=2^{R_{k-1}((n-k+4)/2)-1}
.

Fix an edge
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in[2^{N}]^{(k)}
and let
\delta_{i}=\delta(x_{i},x_{i+1})
. We describe the coloring
\psi
by the structure of

and the coloring of the vertical projection
\varphi
of

in the following way

\displaystyle\psi(X)=\begin{cases}0,&\quad\text{if }\delta_{k-3}>\delta_{k-2}<\delta_{k-1}\\

\varphi(\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k-1}\}),&\quad\text{otherwise if $|\delta(X)|=k-1$}\\
0,&\quad\text{otherwise if $|\delta(X)|<k-1$.}\end{cases}

Suppose by contradiction that
\psi
contains a monochromatic subset

of size

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists an interval
I
of
Y

|I|\geqslant(n-k+6)/2

is a
1
-comb

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
Y
is monochromatic of color

. Write

and let
\delta^{Y}_{i}=\delta(y_{i},y_{i+1})
for

. Since all edges in

, then for any edge
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in Y^{(k)}
we do not have that

\displaystyle\delta(x_{k-3},x_{k-2})<\delta(x_{k-2},x_{k-1})>\delta(x_{k-1},x_{k}).

(3)

In particular, by taking the edge
\{y_{\ell-k+1},\ldots,y_{\ell}\}
, inequality (

\delta^{Y}_{\ell-3}<\delta^{Y}_{\ell-2}>\delta^{Y}_{\ell-1}

A standard calculus argument says that between two local minimums there is always a local maximum. Therefore, the sequence
\{\delta^{Y}_{i}\}_{i=k-3}^{n-1}

3.2.
Overview of the proof

In order to obtain a lower bound for simple daisies, we will define a variant of the stepping-up lemma described in the previous subsection. Suppose for a moment that our goal is to avoid a monochromatic simple
(r,m,k)
-daisy with in
[2^{N}]
with
|K_{0}|=k_{0}
and
|K_{1}|=k_{1}
fixed. Then for every edge

of the daisy, we know that the petal of size

of

is

. That is, we know the exact location of the petal prior defining the coloring in our stepping-up lemma. In this case a natural way to define the coloring would be to just assign for every edge

Unfortunately, in the original problem we want to avoid all possible monochromatic simple

-daisies, which means that we need to avoid simple daisies for all the values of

and
|K_{1}|
. The obstruction now is that the location of the petal within the edge is no longer clear to us. To fix that we are going to pre-process our potentially monochromatic simple daisy (Lemma

) to satisfy the following property: Every petal

for every maximal comb in

and use those colorings to define a coloring for

.

Some technical challenges remain. By Proposition
2.7
, the maximal combs in

do not need to be disjoint. Therefore, it might happen that for the coloring

different maximal combs interfere with each other. To solve that we need to construct a careful coloring taking the issue into consideration. In Section

we provide an analysis showing that distinct maximal combs do not interfere with each other in our coloring. Section

is devoted to the pre-processing described in the last paragraph. One of the consequences of the section is that for an edge

is essentially determined by a unique maximal comb inside of it. Finally, we finish the proof in Section

, by showing, similarly as in Subsection

, that a monochromatic simple daisy in

corresponds to a monochromatic simple daisy in the vertical coloring of

One of the main consequences of our pre-processing is that it allows us to identify certain closed and non-closed intervals in an arbitrary edge of

. To be more precise, given an edge

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{V^{\prime},M^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\{I:\>\text{$I$ is an interval in $V^{\prime}$ and either $M^{\prime}\subseteq I$ or $M^{\prime}\cap I=\emptyset$}\}

\displaystyle=\{I:\>\text{$I$ is an interval in $X$ and either $P\subseteq I$ or $P\cap I=\emptyset$}\}

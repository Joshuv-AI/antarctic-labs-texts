1.
Introduction

In this paper we construct regular models of hyperelliptic curves over discrete valuation rings with residue characteristic different from
2
. The understanding of regular models is essential to describe the arithmetic of curves and for example finds application in the study of the Birch & Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture over global fields.

1.1.
Overview

Let
K
be a complete discretely valued field, with ring of integers
O_{K}
.
Given a connected smooth projective curve
C/K
, a
regular model
of
C
over
O_{K}
is an integral regular proper flat scheme
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow O_{K}
of dimension
2
with generic fibre isomorphic to
C
. The main result of this work can be presented as follows:

Suppose that the residue characteristic of
K
is not
2
. Let
C/K:y^{2}=f(x)
be a hyperelliptic curve. From the MacLane clusters for
f
we determine a regular model of
C
over
O_{K}
with strict normal crossings.

The
MacLane clusters
for a separable polynomial
f\in K[x]
are a new notion we introduce in this paper (see §
1.2
for more details). It has connections with other objects used for the study of regular models: clusters
[
M2D2
]
, rational clusters
[
Mus1
]
, Newton polytopes
[
Dok
]
, and MacLane valuations
[
OW
]
. Like (rational) clusters, MacLane clusters define nice and clear invariants from which one can give a result in a closed form. In fact, one can see that rational clusters are MacLane clusters of degree
1
. On the other side, the construction of our model can be implemented from the algorithmic nature of the approaches based on Newton polytopes and MacLane valuations.

4.

Let
f\in K[x]
be a separable polynomial and let
c_{f}\in K
be its leading term. Assume
f/c_{f}\in O_{K}[x]
and write
\mathfrak{R}
for the sets of roots of
f
in
\bar{K}
.
If
C/K
is a hyperelliptic curve, it is always given by an equation
y^{2}=f(x)
, where
f\in K[x]
is as above.

Definition 4.1
.

A
MacLane cluster
(for
f
) is a pair
(\mathfrak{s},v)
where
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
, and
v
is a MacLane pseudo-valuation such that

(1)

\mathfrak{s}=D_{v}\cap\mathfrak{R}\neq\varnothing
;

(2)

if
\mathfrak{s}=D_{w}\cap\mathfrak{R}
for a MacLane valuation
w>v
then
\deg w>\deg v
.

If
v
is a MacLane valuation then
(\mathfrak{s},v)
is said
proper MacLane cluster
. The degree of
(\mathfrak{s},v)
is
\deg v
. The
degree
, a
centre
and the
radius
of a MacLane cluster
(\mathfrak{s},v)
are the degree, a centre and the radius of
v
, respectively.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},v)
be a MacLane cluster. Note that by definition

(i)

\mathfrak{s}
is
G_{K}
-invariant,

(ii)

v
determines
\mathfrak{s}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
MacLane cluster picture
of
f
is the combinatorial data consisting of the collection of all MacLane clusters for
f
together with their radii. We will denote by
\Sigma_{f}^{M}
the set of all MacLane clusters for
f
.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a MacLane pseudo-valuation
v\in\hat{}\mathbb{V}_{M}

defines a MacLane cluster

(\mathfrak{s},w)\in\Sigma_{f}^{M}
, if
w=v
(and
\mathfrak{s}=D_{v}\cap\mathfrak{R}
).

Definition 4.5
.

We write
(\mathfrak{t},w)\subseteq(\mathfrak{s},v)
if
w\geq v
.
If
(\mathfrak{t},w)\subsetneq(\mathfrak{s},v)
is maximal, we write
(\mathfrak{t},w)<(\mathfrak{s},v)
and
v=P(w)
, and refer to
(\mathfrak{t},w)
as
a child
of
(\mathfrak{s},v)
, and to
(\mathfrak{s},v)
as
the parent
of
(\mathfrak{t},w)
. A proper MacLane cluster
(\mathfrak{s},v)
with no proper child of degree

is said

.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},v),(\mathfrak{t},w)\in\Sigma_{f}^{M}
such that
\mathfrak{s}\subsetneq\mathfrak{t}
. Then
(\mathfrak{s},v)\subsetneq(\mathfrak{t},w)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq D_{v}\cap D_{w}
either
D_{v}\subsetneq D_{w}
or
D_{w}\subseteq D_{v}
. But

D_{v}\cap\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{s}\subsetneq\mathfrak{t}=D_{w}\cap\mathfrak{R},

so
D_{v}\subsetneq D_{w}
. Thus
w>v
.
∎

[
KW
, Proposition 2.26]
shows that the meet of any two MacLane pseudo-valuations

and
w
exists; it will be denoted by

. Hence

is the maximal MacLane pseudo-valuation
\leq v
and
\leq w

\hat{}\mathbb{V}_{M}
with

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},v)
,
(\mathfrak{t},w)\in\Sigma_{f}^{M}

\mathfrak{s}\wedge\mathfrak{t}=D_{v\wedge w}\cap\mathfrak{R}

(\mathfrak{s},v)
and
(\mathfrak{t},w)

Proof.

We only need to show that

v^{\prime}>v\wedge w

v^{\prime}\not\leq w

If

But then

,

∎

Let
F\in K[x]
be a monic irreducible factor of

. Let

be the MacLane pseudo-valuation with

(Theorem

). We also denote

by

where

is any root of

. For any non-empty

-invariant subset

, define

. Then

. Let

be the irreducible monic factors of

. Define

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
v\in\hat{}\mathbb{V}_{M}
and let
\mathfrak{s}=D_{v}\cap\mathfrak{R}\neq\varnothing
.
Then

. In particular,

is a MacLane cluster.

Proof.

The set
\mathfrak{s}
is
G_{K}
-invariant, as so are

and
\mathfrak{R}
. Let
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
be the irreducible factors of
g_{\mathfrak{s}}
as above. Let
\mathfrak{s}_{i}

. Suppose

. Then

, so

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

, for some

. Let

. Proposition

for some

.

The argument above holds in particular when

.

∎

Proposition 4.10
.

The set
\Sigma_{f}^{M}
under the partial order

Proof.

Let

. By Remark

(ii)

inverting partial orders by definition. Hence it suffices to show that

for any monic irreducible factor

of

∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},v)
be a MacLane cluster. Then
|\mathfrak{s}|\geq\deg v
. Furthermore,
|\mathfrak{s}|>\deg v
if and only if
(\mathfrak{s},v)
is proper.

Proof.

First note that
v\leq v_{\mathfrak{s}}
by Lemma
4.8
. Then Lemma
2.25
implies

\deg v\leq\deg v_{\mathfrak{s}}\leq\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}\deg v_{r}=\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}|G_{K}\cdot r|\leq|\mathfrak{s}|.

If
|\mathfrak{s}|=\deg v
, then
\mathfrak{s}=G_{K}\cdot r
for some (any)

, and
\deg v=\deg v_{\mathfrak{s}}
. It follows from Lemma

that

. Hence

is not proper.

If

is not proper, that is

, then

for some (any)

. In particular,
\mathfrak{s}=\deg v_{r}=\deg v
.
∎

Remark 4.12

(Alternative definition for MacLane clusters)
.

Let
\Sigma
be the set of pairs
(\mathfrak{s},n)
, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{s}=D_{v}\cap\mathfrak{R}\neq\varnothing
for some MacLane pseudo-valuation
v
of degree

. It follows from Remark

(2) and Theorem

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},v)
be a MacLane cluster. Then
\lambda_{v}=\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(\phi(r))
for any centre
\phi
of
v
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(\phi(r))
. Since
\mathfrak{s}\subset D_{v}=D(\phi,\lambda_{v})
, we have
\lambda\geq\lambda_{v}
. Suppose
\lambda>\lambda_{v}
. Let
w=[v,w(\phi)=\lambda]
. Then
w>v
and
\deg w=\deg\phi=\deg v
. But
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{R}\cap D_{w}

for our choice of

. This contradicts the fact that
(\mathfrak{s},v)
is a MacLane cluster (Definition
4.1
2
).
∎

Notation 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset K[x]
to be the subset of monic irreducible polynomials. For any

, denote by

the set

.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},v)
be a proper MacLane cluster. Then

\lambda_{v}=\max_{g\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq\deg v}}\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(g(r)).

Proof.

Let
d=\deg v
. By Lemma
4.13
we only need to show that
\lambda_{v}\geq\max_{g\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}}\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(g(r))
. Suppose not. Then there exists a polynomial
g\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq d}
such that
\lambda:=\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(g(r))>\lambda_{v}
. Let
w\in\hat{}\mathbb{V}_{M}
such that
D_{w}=D(g,\lambda)
(Theorem
2.21
). Then
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq D_{w}\cap\mathfrak{R}
. By Lemma
2.22
we have
\deg w\leq\deg g\leq\deg v
and
w(g)\geq\lambda
. Since
\mathfrak{s}\subset D_{w}\cap D_{v}
, either
D_{v}\subseteq D_{w}
or

by Remark
2.18
(2). If

, then

and

, a contradiction, since

is a MacLane cluster. So
D_{v}\subseteq D_{w}
, that is

. Hence
v(g)\geq w(g)\geq\lambda>\lambda_{v}
. This gives a contradiction since
v(g)\leq\lambda_{v}
by Lemma
2.26
.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
v\in\hat{}\mathbb{V}_{M}
and
\mathfrak{s}=D_{v}\cap\mathfrak{R}

\textstyle\lambda_{v}=\max_{g\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq\deg v}}\min_{r\in\mathfrak{s}}v_{K}(g(r)).

By Lemma
4.9
, there exists a MacLane pseudo-valuation

with

be the radius of

. Then Lemma

implies

. But this is possible only if

, by Lemma

.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
v\neq v_{0}
be a MacLane valuation,
\phi
a strong key polynomial over

and

,

.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Let

.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
F\in O_{K}[x]
monic and irreducible. Let

such that
v\leq v_{F}
, e.g. when

and

. Then

(v\wedge w)(F)=\min\{v(F),w(F)\}

In particular, if

, then

.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows from the proof of
[
KW
, Proposition 2.26]
, defining

. Suppose

. If

∎

Lemma 4.20
.

Let

. Then

v(f)=v_{K}(c_{f})+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{P},\,F\mid f}\deg F\cdot\frac{\lambda_{v\wedge v_{F}}}{\deg(v\wedge v_{F})}=v_{K}(c_{f})+\sum_{r\in\mathfrak{R}}\frac{\lambda_{v\wedge v_{r}}}{\deg(v\wedge v_{r})}.

Recall

. Then

and the factors

in the product belong to

. It suffices to show that

for all

. Let
F\in\mathcal{P}\cap O_{K}[x]
. By Lemma

, the polynomial

. In particular,

\frac{(v\wedge v_{F})(F)}{\deg F}=\frac{\lambda_{v\wedge v_{F}}}{\deg(v\wedge v_{F})}.

by Theorem

. Since

shows

∎

4.1.
Newton polygons

Let
v
be a MacLane valuation and
\phi\in\mathrm{KP}({v})
. Recall the definition of the Newton polygon
N_{v,\phi}(f)
.

Definition 4.21
.

The
principal Newton polygon
N_{v,\phi}^{-}(f)
is formed by the edges of
N_{v,\phi}(f)
with slope
<-v(\phi)
.

For any edge
L
of
N_{v,\phi}^{-}(f)
with slope
-\lambda
, define the MacLane valuation
v_{L}=[v,v_{L}(\phi)=\lambda]
. Then
L=L_{v_{L}}(f)
(Notation
2.36
). Denote by
\lambda_{L}
the radius of
v_{L}
.

The aim of this subsection is proving the following theorem.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{V}_{M}
and
\phi\in\mathrm{KP}({v})
as above.

(i)

If
(\mathfrak{t},w^{\prime})
is a MacLane cluster with centre
\phi^{\prime}\sim_{v}\phi
satisfying
w^{\prime}(\phi)<\infty
, then
N_{v,\phi}^{-}(f)
has an edge
L
of slope
-w^{\prime}(\phi)
and
t_{v_{L}}=|\mathfrak{t}|/\deg\phi
.

(ii)

Conversely, for every edge
L
of
N_{v,\phi}^{-}(f)
there is a MacLane cluster
(\mathfrak{t},w_{L})
with
w_{L}\geq v_{L}
,
\deg w_{L}=\deg\phi
,
w_{L}(\phi)=\lambda_{L}
and
|\mathfrak{t}|=t_{v_{L}}\deg\phi
.

In case
(ii)
, if there exists a proper
(\mathfrak{s},w)\in\Sigma_{f}^{M}
with
w=[v,w(\phi)=\lambda]
,
\lambda\geq\lambda_{L}
, then
w_{L}=v_{L}
.

We first recall the following result from
[
FGMN
]
.

Theorem 4.23

(
[
FGMN
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

Let
F\in O_{K}[x]
be a monic irreducible polynomial and
r\in\bar{K}
a root of
F
. Then
\phi\mid_{v}F
if and only if
v_{K}(\phi(r))>v(\phi)
.
Moreover, if this condition holds, one also has:

(1)

Either
F=\phi
, or
N_{v,\phi}(F)
consists of one edge of slope
-v_{K}(\phi(r))
.

(2)

d:=\deg F/\deg\phi\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
F\sim_{v}\phi^{d}
.

Lemma 4.24
.

Let
w=[v,w(\phi)=\lambda]
be an augmentation of
v
. Let
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}
be the set of roots
r
of
f
satisfying
v_{K}(\phi(r))=\lambda
. Then
|\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}|/\deg\phi=t_{w}-t^{0}_{w}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can suppose
f
monic. If
\lambda=\infty
, then
|\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}|=\mathrm{ord}_{\phi}(f)
and the equality
|\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}|/\deg\phi=t_{w}-t^{0}_{w}
follows from the definition of
t^{0}_{w},t_{w}
. Hence suppose
\lambda<\infty
.

We first show the statement for
f=F
irreducible. In this case either
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}=\varnothing
or
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{R}
. Suppose
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{R}
, which means
v_{K}(\phi(r))=\lambda>v(\phi)
for any (some)
r\in\mathfrak{R}
. Since
F\neq\phi
(otherwise
\phi(r)=0
), Theorem
4.23
implies that
L_{w}(F)=N_{v,\phi}(F)
,
t^{0}_{w}=0
and
t_{w}=\deg F/\deg\phi=|\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}|/\deg\phi
. Now suppose that
L_{w}(F)
is an edge of
N_{v,\phi}(F)
. So
t_{w}\geq 1
. We want to show
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}\neq\varnothing
. Let
t=t_{w}
. Expand

F=\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}\phi^{j},\qquad a_{j}\in K[x],\,\deg a_{j}<\deg\phi,\,a_{d}\neq 0.

By definition of
L_{w}(f)
we have
w(a_{j}\phi^{j})\geq w(a_{t}\phi^{t})
for all
j
. Therefore

v(a_{t}\phi^{t})=w(a_{t}\phi^{t})-t(\lambda-v(\phi))<w(a_{j}\phi^{j})-j(\lambda-v(\phi))=v(a_{j}\phi^{j})

for all
j<t
. In particular,
v(a_{0})>v(F)
, so
\phi\mid_{v}F
. Theorem
4.23
then implies that
-\lambda=-v_{K}(\phi(r))
for any
r\in\mathfrak{R}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}\neq\varnothing
.

Let
f\in O_{K}[x]
be any monic separable polynomial. Write
f=F_{0}\cdots F_{t}
, with
F_{j}\in O_{K}[x]
monic irreducible. Denote by
\mathfrak{R}_{j}
the set of roots of
F_{j}
and by
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,j}
the elements
r\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}
satisfying
v_{K}(\phi(r))=\lambda
. Clearly

\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda}=\bigsqcup_{j}\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda,j}
. Moreover, from
[
FGMN
, Corollary 2.7]
, we have

L_{w}(f)=L_{w}(F_{0})+\dots+L_{w}(F_{t})

(see before
[
FGMN
, Corollary 2.7]
for a definition of
+
).
The lemma then follows from the first part of the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.25
.

Let
w=[v,w(\phi)=\lambda]
be an augmentation of
v
and let
\mathfrak{s}=D_{w}\cap\mathfrak{R}
. Then
t_{w}=|\mathfrak{s}|/\deg\phi
.

Proof.

By definition
t_{w}=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}(t_{w^{\prime}}-t^{0}_{w^{\prime}})
, where
w^{\prime}=[v,w^{\prime}(\phi)=\lambda^{\prime}]
. Lemma
4.24
implies

t_{w}\deg\phi=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}\left|\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\right|=\bigg|\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\bigg|=|\mathfrak{s}|,

where
\mathfrak{s}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
is the set of roots
r
of
f
satisfying
v_{K}(\phi(r))=\lambda^{\prime}
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.22
.

Proof of Theorem
4.22
.

(i)
. Let
(\mathfrak{t},w)
be a cluster with centre
\phi^{\prime}\sim_{v}\phi
and
w(\phi)<\infty
. In particular,
\deg\phi=\deg\phi^{\prime}
.
Let
\lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}=\min_{r\in\mathfrak{t}}v_{K}(\phi(r))\geq w(\phi)
. Consider the MacLane valuation
w_{\mathfrak{t}}=[v,w_{\mathfrak{t}}(\phi)=\lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}]
. Then
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq D_{w_{\mathfrak{t}}}\cap\mathfrak{R}
. By Remark
2.18
(2) and Theorem
2.21
, either
w_{\mathfrak{t}}>w
or
w_{\mathfrak{t}}\leq w
. By definition of MacLane cluster we have
w_{\mathfrak{t}}\leq w
. But then
\lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}\leq w(\phi)
. Thus
\lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}=w(\phi)
. Furthermore,

\mathfrak{t}\subseteq D_{w_{\mathfrak{t}}}\cap\mathfrak{R}\subseteq D_{w}\cap\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{t},

and so
\mathfrak{t}=D_{w_{\mathfrak{t}}}\cap\mathfrak{R}
.
Then Lemma
4.24
implies that
L_{w_{\mathfrak{t}}}(f)
is an edge of
N_{v,\phi}(f)
. The equality
t_{w_{\mathfrak{t}}}\deg\phi=|\mathfrak{t}|
follows from Proposition
4.25
.

(ii)
. Let
L
be an edge of
N_{v,\phi}^{-}(f)
. Let
\mathfrak{t}=D_{v_{L}}\cap\mathfrak{R}
. From Lemma
4.24
and Proposition
4.25
it follows that

|\mathfrak{t}|=t_{v_{L}}\cdot\deg\phi\quad\text{ and }\quad\min_{r\in\mathfrak{t}}v_{K}(\phi(r))=\lambda_{L}.

By Lemma
4.9
there exists a unique MacLane pseudo-valuation
w_{L}\geq v_{L}
such that
\deg w_{L}=\deg v_{L}=\deg\phi
and
(\mathfrak{t},w_{L})
is a cluster. In particular,
w_{L}(\phi)=\lambda_{L}
as

\lambda_{L}=v_{L}(\phi)\leq w_{L}(\phi)\leq\min_{r\in\mathfrak{t}}v_{K}(\phi(r))=\lambda_{L}.

there exists a proper MacLane cluster
(\mathfrak{s},w)
with
w=[v,w(\phi)=\lambda]
,
\lambda\geq\lambda_{L}
. Then
w\geq v_{L}
and so
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
. Furthermore,
\deg w_{L}=\deg v_{L}=\deg w
; hence, by definition of cluster, if
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{t}
then
w=v_{L}=w_{L}
. So suppose
\mathfrak{s}\subsetneq\mathfrak{t}
. It follows from Lemma
4.6
that
(\mathfrak{s},w)\subsetneq(\mathfrak{t},w_{L})
.
Since
\phi
is centre of
w
, Lemma
2.25
implies that
\phi
is also a centre of
w_{L}
. But we have already showed
w_{L}(\phi)=\lambda_{L}
, so
w_{L}=v_{L}
as required.
∎

4.2.

In this subsection we will see that there is a close relationship between certain children

We will need the following result.

Theorem 4.26

(
[

Appendix A
Pseudo-valuations

Let
A
be an integral domain (with identity). Let
\hat{}\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
. The ordering and the group law on
\mathbb{Q}
are canonically extended to the set
\hat{}\mathbb{Q}

Definition A.1
.

A map

is called

(
of

(a)

v(ab)=v(a)+v(b)
,

(b)

v(a+b)\geq\min\{v(a),v(b)\}
,

for any

.
A pseudo-valuation
v
is said
valuation
if it also satisfies

(c)

v(a)=\infty
if and only if
a=0
;

we call it
infinite pseudo-valuation
otherwise.

Definition A.2
.

Let

be a pseudo-valuation.

•

The
valuation group
of a pseudo-valuation

, denoted
\Gamma_{v}
, is the subgroup generated by the subset
v(A)\cap\mathbb{Q}
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that if

.

•

v
is
discrete

such that

. If that happens, then

is said ramification index of

.

•

The

of a pseudo-valuation

is the set of

with

.

•

The
residue ring
of
v
is the quotient of
O_{v}
by the prime ideal

consisting of the elements

with

.

•

If

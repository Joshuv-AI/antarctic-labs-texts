A blow-up method to prescribed mean curvature graphs with fixed boundaries
Hengyu Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we apply a blow-up method of Schoen and Yau in [ 39 ] to study a large class of prescribed mean curvature (PMC) Dirichlet problems in n ⁡ ( n ≥ 2 ) n(n\geq 2) -dimensional Riemannian manifolds. In this process we establish curvature estimates for almost minimizing PMC hypersurfaces, using an approach of Schauder estimates from Simon [ 41 ] . We define an Nc-f domain, where f f is a given function generating from the PMC equation. Combining this condition with a sufficiently mean convex assumption the blow-up method yields corresponding solutions to these PMC Dirichlet problems. Such Nc-f assumption is almost optimal by an example. An application of our result into the PMC Plateau problem is also presented.

Introduction

In this paper we will study the prescribed mean curvature (PMC) graph in Riemannian manifolds under a fixed Dirichlet data, i.e, the Dirichlet problem of some types of PMC equations from a geometrical viewpoint.

The first motivation comes from the connection between PMC equations and the PMC Plateau problem of surfaces in 3-manifolds by Gulliver-Spruck
[
20
]
and Giusti
[
17

. Combining their work together, in 3-manifolds, the existence of PMC graphs is closely related to the existence of PMC disks (with fixed Jordan curve). See Theorem

The second motivation of our study is the PMC graph of functions to solve (
1.1

The existence of the solution to (

However, this explanation is little discussed in literature. Within our knowledge, most conditions on the underlying domain for the PMC equations (

A key observation of this method is that the non-existence of apparent horizons in the underlying domain implies the global existence of a Jang graph over this domain under the natural setting in general relativity. \langle\vec{v}_{E},X^{p}_{m}\rangle(q)\geq\frac{1}{2}

As a result

\vec{v}_{E}=\frac{-g^{ij}v_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}+X^{p}_{m}}{\sqrt{1+g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}}},\quad\langle\vec{v}_{E},X^{p}_{m}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}}}

with respect to the coordinate
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{m-1}\}
.

By (
3.17
) and (
3.20
), there is a constant

such that

(3.22)

\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}v_{i}^{2}\leq C_{3}

where
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m-1},v(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m}))
belongs to
\partial E\cap B_{\rho_{2}}(p)
. Thus
\partial E\cap B_{\rho_{2}}(p)
contains a graph of

over a domain

(3.23)

where

only depends on

. Applying the coordinate in Lemma

,

satisfies the following mean curvature type equation

(3.24)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}}}(g^{ij}-\frac{v^{i}v^{j}}{1+g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}})v_{ij}=f(y,v,\frac{-g^{ij}v_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}+X^{p}_{m}}{\sqrt{1+g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}}})

over the domain

C^{-1}I\leq g^{ij}(y,v)\leq CI,\quad\rho_{1}^{1+\alpha}||g_{ij}(y,v)||_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq C\text{ on }U_{C_{4}}

is
f(p,\vec{v})
satisfying

(see Definition

, Remark

and Lemma

(3.26)

||f||_{C^{1,\alpha}(T_{u}W)}\leq\nu

for

. Notice that the normal vector

,
p_{i}=-g^{ij}v_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}}},i=1,\cdots,m-1

4.
An auxiliary Dirichlet problem

In this section we use the Perron method to study the following Dirichlet problem

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{H}(u)&=0\quad\text{ on }\Omega\\
u&=\psi\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{split}\right.

where

(4.2)

\mathcal{L}_{H}(u):=-div(\frac{Du}{\omega})+H_{1}(x,u,-\frac{Du}{\omega},\frac{1}{\omega})+H_{2}(x,u,-\frac{Du}{\omega},\frac{1}{\omega})\frac{1}{\omega}

\omega=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}
,
\Omega
is a domain in an
n(\geq 2)
dimensional Riemannian manifold
N
,
M
is the product manifold
N{\mkern-1.0mu\times\mkern-1.0mu}\mathbb{R}
and
H_{i}(x,z,X,r)
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
function on
TM
for
i=1,2
with
(x,z)\in M
and
X+r\partial_{r}\in T_{(x,z)}M
with
X\in T_{x}N
.

The main result of this section is stated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix two constants
\beta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2,\alpha}
domain. Let
H_{1}(x,z,X,0)=f(x,z,X)
for any
x\in N,z\in\mathbb{R},X
in
TN
. Suppose

(4.3)

\frac{\partial H_{1}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial H_{2}}{\partial z}\geq\beta>0

and

(4.4)

H_{\partial\Omega}(x)\geq\max\{f(x,\psi(x),\gamma(x)),-f(x,\psi(x),-\gamma(x))\}\quad x\in\partial\Omega

Here
\gamma
is the outward normal vector of
\partial\Omega
. Then there is a unique solution to the Dirichlet problem (
4.1
) in
C^{3,\alpha}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
for any
\psi
in
C(\partial\Omega)
.

Remark 4.2
.

The Dirichlet problem (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) can be used to blow up Jang equations. See Sakovich
[
36
, section 4]
for more references.

Remark 4.3
.

Bergner
[
3
]
first considered the general form of (
4.1
) in Euclidean spaces with only requiring that
\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial z}
is non-negative. His smallness condition upon
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
and
\Omega
guarantees the a priori
C^{0}
estimate of the solution to (
4.1
) is uniformly bounded. In our setting the strict monotone condition (
4.3
) gives the corresponding a priori estimate. An improvement of the boundary condition in
[
3
]
was later given by Marquardt
[
35
]
.

The closed version of Theorem
4.1
is stated as follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
\Omega
is a closed
n(\geq 2)
-dimensional manifold without boundary and (
4.3
) holds. Then there is a unique solution to the problem
\mathcal{L}_{H}(u)=0
on
\Omega
in
C^{3,\alpha}(\Omega)
.

We did not consider the variational approach in
[
18
]
since the graph of the solution to the Dirichlet problem (
4.1
) may not be written as the critical point of any area-type functional. The continuous method in
[
16
, section 11.3]
did not work in our case because we can not keep (
4.4
) unchanged in the continuous process even if the
C^{0}
estimate of the solution to (
4.1
) is available by the maximum principle. Therefore the Perron theory used by Eichmair
[
10
]
may be the only choice to show Theorem
4.1
.

4.1.
The Maximum Principle

We collect two well-known maximum principles about mean curvature equations for later use.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose
G(x,u,Du)
is a
C^{1}
function on
N{\mkern-1.0mu\times\mkern-1.0mu}\mathbb{R}{\mkern-1.0mu\times\mkern-1.0mu}TN
satisfying
\partial_{u}G(x,u,Du)\geq 0
. Suppose
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
. Define the elliptic operator

(4.5)

Lu:=-div(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}})+G(x,u,Du)\text{ on }\Omega

If
Lu_{1}(x)\geq Lu_{2}(x)
on
\Omega
and
u_{1}(x)\geq u_{2}(x)
on
\partial\Omega
,
u_{1}(x)\geq u_{2}(x)
on
\Omega
. Moreover
u_{1}(x_{0})=u_{2}(x_{0})
for some point
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
u_{1}(x)\equiv u_{2}(x)
.

Recall that for a hypersurface
\Sigma
with its normal vector
\vec{v}
, its mean curvature
H_{\Sigma}
is defined by
div(\vec{v})
.

Theorem 4.6

(Proposition 4,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain with
C^{2}
boundary. Let
G:TN\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
function. Suppose

(1)

\Sigma
is a
C^{2}
hypersurface in the complement of
\Omega
;

(2)

\Sigma
is tangent to
\partial\Omega
at a point
p
in the interior of
\Sigma
and
\partial\Omega
;

(3)

in an embedded ball
B
containing
p
, for any
q\in B
,
H_{\Sigma}(q)\leq G(q,\vec{v}_{1}(q))
,
H_{\partial\Omega}(q)\geq G(q,\vec{v}_{2}(q))
;

where
\vec{v}_{1}
and
\vec{v}_{2}
denote the normal vector of
\Sigma
and
\partial\Omega
respectively satisfying
\vec{v}_{1}(p)=\vec{v}_{2}(p)
pointing into
\Omega
.

Then
\Sigma
coincides with
\partial\Omega
in a neighborhood of
p
.

Remark 4.7
.

The condition
\vec{v}_{1}(p)=\vec{v}_{2}(p)
is essential because two unit spheres in Euclidean spaces can touch each other at a common point
p
with
\vec{v}_{1}(p)=-\vec{v}_{2}(p)
.

4.2.
The Perron Theory

We use the following definitions of the Perron method from Eichmair
[
10

is defined by (

) satisfying (

),

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded domain. Fix any

. For any

\bar{\Omega}
there is a continuous positive function

such that the Dirichlet problem

Proof.

Fix any
x\in\bar{\Omega}
. First we assume that
\psi
is in
C^{2,\alpha}(\bar{B}_{r}(x))
. For any
s\in(0,1)
, let
v_{s}\in C^{3,\alpha}(B_{r}(x))\cap C(\bar{B}_{r}(x))

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{split}-div(\frac{Du}{\omega})+s\{G(x,u,-\frac{Du}{\omega},\frac{1}{\omega})\}&=0\quad\text{ on }B_{r}(x)\\

where
G(p,Y)
be a
C^{1,\alpha}
function on

given by

with

and

for

.
Since
|\psi|\leq c
and
\frac{\partial H_{1}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial H_{1}}{\partial z}\geq\beta
, by applying the maximum principle

\max_{\bar{B}_{r}(y)}|v_{s}|<c_{0}:=c_{0}(\mu_{0},\beta,c)

Here

(4.8)

s|H_{1}(x,v_{s},-\frac{Dv_{s}}{\omega},\frac{1}{\omega})+H_{2}(x,v_{s},-\frac{Dv_{s}}{\omega},\frac{1}{\omega})\frac{1}{\omega}|\leq c_{1}=:c_{1}(c_{0},\mu_{0}),

where

for any

\bar{\Omega}
is compact, there is a continuous function

depending on

satisfies

(4.9)

Therefore there is a positive constant

such that for any
s\in(0,1)
.

(4.10)

\begin{split}s\mathcal{N}_{W}(G)\leq\mu\end{split}

where

By (
4.9

4.8
) and Theorem

in
\Gamma_{s,\pm}
. Here

. Because

on

this implies that

(4.13)

\max_{\partial B_{r}(x)}|Dv_{s}|\leq c_{3}=:c_{3}(\kappa,\nu)

for any

. By (

\max_{\bar{B}_{r}(y)}|Dv_{s}|\leq c_{4}=:c_{4}(c_{3},\mu)

Applying the classical Schauder estimate the

.

5.
The Nc-f property

In this section we first discuss the relationship between the Nc-f property and the Ricci assumptions of a domain. Then we construct two examples. The first one says that a Nc-f domain can contain a minimal hypersurface when
f\equiv 0
. The second one says that if the Nc-f assumption is almost optimal. That is if it is removed, there are a compact manifold such that there is no solution to corresponding PMC equations.

Definition 5.1

( the Nc-f property)
.

Fix
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
N
is a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Suppose

is a
C^{1,\alpha}
function in the tangent bundle

and

is a
C^{2,\alpha}
domain with embedded boundary. We say that

has the Nc-f property if there is no Caccioppoli set

in the closure of
\Omega
satisfying

(1)

for any
p\in\partial E
,
E
is a
\Lambda
-perimeter minimizer (see Definition
2.4
) in a neighborhood of
p
for some positive constant
\Lambda
;

(2)

the regular part

(see Definition
2.4
) is

, embedded and orientable with its mean curvature equal to

for any

in

(
-f(x,-\vec{v}(x))
for any
x
in
reg(\partial E)
);

Here
\vec{v}
is the outward normal vector of
x
in

, the mean curvature is defined as

.

Remark 5.2
.

In this definition we do not require that

.

In the case

with mean curvature

or
-f(x,-\vec{v}(x))
for all

is the outward normal vector of

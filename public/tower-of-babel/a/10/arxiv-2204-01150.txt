Practical exponential stability of a robust data-driven nonlinear predictive control scheme
Mohammad Alsalti, Victor G. Lopez, Julian Berberich, Frank Allgöwer, Matthias A. Müller

Abstract
We provide theoretical guarantees for recursive feasibility and practical exponential stability of the closed-loop system of a discrete-time multi-input multi-output full-state feedback linearizable nonlinear system when controlled by a robust data-driven nonlinear predictive control scheme. Two sources of uncertainties are considered: the mismatch due to the basis function approximation as well as output measurement noise. This technical report serves as a supplementary material to [ 1 ] .



I
Introduction

In
[
2
]
, we considered a discrete-time multi-input multi-output (DT-MIMO) full-state feedback linearizable nonlinear systems and provided a data-based representation of their trajectories using a single, persistently exciting input-output trajectory and a set of basis functions that only depend on input and noisy output data. In general, the combination of basis functions serves as an approximation and not as an exact decomposition of the unknown nonlinearities. Furthermore, the collected output data is generally contaminated by noise. Therefore, we proposed in
[
1
, Section 3.2]
a robust data-driven nonlinear predictive control scheme that stabilizes a fixed set-point of a DT-MIMO full-state feedback linearizable system despite unknown, but uniformly bounded, basis function approximation error and output noise. This supplementary report provides the theoretical analysis of recursive feasibility and practical exponential stability of this scheme. The proof is conceptually similar to the one presented in
[
3
]
for DT-LTI systems. This is because feedback linearizable systems can be expressed as linear systems in transformed coordinates. However, additional challenges imposed by the nonlinear nature of the problem as well as having two sources of uncertainties require careful investigation which we provide in this report.

The goal of this report is to show that the control scheme (

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{continuity_bnd_on_u},\eqref{output_candidate_at_t}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)\left(K_{\gamma}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{[t,t+L-1]}\\
\Xi_{[t,t+L-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{v}_{1,[t-d_{1},t+L-d_{1}-1]}\\
\vdots\\
{v}_{m,[t-d_{m},t+L-d_{m}-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{continuity_bnd_on_u}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)\left(K_{\gamma}\Gamma_{v}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{v}_{1,[t-d_{1},t-1]}\\
\vdots\\
{v}_{m,[t-d_{m},t-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{v}_{1,[t,t+L-d_{1}-1]}\\
\vdots\\
{v}_{m,[t,t+L-d_{m}-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{y_for_proofsA}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)\left(K_{\gamma}\Gamma_{v}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{y}_{1,[t,t+d_{1}-1]}\\
\vdots\\
{y}_{m,[t,t+d_{m}-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{v}_{1,[t,t+L-d_{1}-1]}\\
\vdots\\
{v}_{m,[t,t+L-d_{m}-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)\left(K_{\gamma}\Gamma_{v}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{[t,t+L-1]}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ctrb_argument_t}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)\left(K_{\gamma}\Gamma_{v}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\Gamma_{v}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)((K_{\gamma}+1)\Gamma_{v}+1)\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

For an upper bound on
\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
, we use (
29
) as follows

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}H_{L+d_{\textup{max}}}(\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{E}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{D}(\omega^{\textup{d}}))\\
H_{1}(\Xi_{[0,N-L-d_{\textup{max}}]}^{\textup{d}})\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{D}({\omega})\\
{\Xi}_{t-d_{\textup{max}}}\end{bmatrix},

(33)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}H_{L+d_{\textup{max}}}(\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{E}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{D}(\omega^{\textup{d}}))\\
H_{1}(\Xi_{[0,N-L-d_{\textup{max}}]}^{\textup{d}})\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}_{\coloneqq c_{\textup{pe}}}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{D}({\omega})\\
{\Xi}_{t-d_{\textup{max}}}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(34)

As we will see later in the proof, the constant
c_{\textup{pe}}
is desired to be small. In
[
3
]
, it was observed that large magnitudes of
\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}}
correspond to smaller values of
c_{\textup{pe}}
. Such observation cannot, in general, be made in the current nonlinear setting since this depends on the basis functions, which in turn depend not only on the input but on the state as well. However, such behavior was observed for some choice of
\Psi
, e.g., monomial functions in the input (
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{2},...
) where there is a direct relationship between
\Psi
and
\mathbf{u}
. To obtain a bound on
\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
, we notice that the rightmost term on the RHS of (
33
) can be upper bounded as follows

\displaystyle\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{D}({\omega})\\
{\Xi}_{t-d_{\textup{max}}}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{D}({\omega})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert{\Xi}_{t-d_{\textup{max}}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

(35)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+4\left\lVert\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+4\left\lVert\hat{D}({\omega})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert{\Xi}_{t-d_{\textup{max}}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

where the inequality was obtained by applying the triangular inequality to the first term along with
(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+4b^{2}+4c^{2}
for
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Notice that the second and third terms are bounded by Assumption
1
and boundedness of the noise, respectively, while the first term can be bounded as follows (by Lipschitz continuity of the basis functions)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)-\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{0})+\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{2}

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)-\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Psi}\left\lVert\Xi\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{2},

where
K_{\Psi}
is the Lipschitz constant of the basis functions w.r.t. the second argument in the compact subset
\Omega
and
\Xi=\Xi_{[t-d_{\textup{max}},t+L-1]}
as denoted earlier. Notice that the term
\mu\coloneqq\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\mathbf{0})\right\rVert_{2}
is uniformly bounded since it corresponds to evaluating the basis functions at the candidate inputs (which belong to the compact set
\mathcal{U}
by (
14g
)). The bound (
36
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Psi}\left\lVert\Xi_{[t-d_{\textup{max}},t+L-1]}\right\rVert_{2}+\mu

(37)

\displaystyle=K_{\Psi}\left(\left\lVert\Xi_{[t-d_{\textup{max}},t-1]}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\Xi_{[t,t+L-1]}\right\rVert_{2}\right)+\mu.

By linearity and observability of System (
3
), there exists a
\Gamma_{\Xi}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{[t-d_{\textup{max}},t-1]}\\
\Xi_{[t-d_{\textup{max}},t-1]}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{\Xi}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(38)

Hence, by (
38
) and the controllability arguments made in (
24
), one can rewrite (
37
) as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Psi}\left(\sqrt{\Gamma_{\Xi}}+\sqrt{\Gamma_{v}}\right)\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}+\mu.

(39)

Plugging these bounds back into (
35
), and using (
12
) and boundedness of the noise, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)+\hat{D}({\omega})\\
{\Xi}_{t-d_{\textup{max}}}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

(40)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(K_{\Psi}\left(\sqrt{\Gamma_{\Xi}}+\sqrt{\Gamma_{v}}\right)\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}+\mu\right)^{2}+4r(L+d_{\textup{max}})\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}((\varepsilon^{*})^{2}+(K_{w}w^{*})^{2})+\Gamma_{\Xi}\left\lVert{\Xi}_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\mu^{2}+4r(L+d_{\textup{max}})\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}((\varepsilon^{*})^{2}+(K_{w}w^{*})^{2})+\left(8K_{\Psi}(\Gamma_{\Xi}+\Gamma_{v})+\Gamma_{\Xi}\right)\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

where the last inequality was obtained by making use of
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Plugging this into (
34
), we obtain an upper bound on
\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\textup{pe}}\mu_{1}+c_{\textup{pe}}\mu_{2}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

(41)

where
\mu_{1}\coloneqq 4\mu^{2}+4r(L+d_{\textup{max}})\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}((\varepsilon^{*})^{2}+(K_{w}w^{*})^{2})
and
\mu_{2}\coloneqq 8K_{\Psi}(\Gamma_{\Xi}+\Gamma_{v})+\Gamma_{\Xi}
.
Similarly, we use the definition of
\sigma^{\prime}(t)
in (
31
) to provide an upper bound on its norm. First, recall that

\sigma^{\prime}_{\Psi}(t)=\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)-\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}(t))+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),{\Xi})+\hat{D}({\omega})-H_{L+d_{\textup{max}}}(\hat{E}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{D}(\omega^{\textup{d}}))\alpha^{\prime}(t),

(42)

which, by Assumption
1
as well as the boundedness of the output noise and local Lipschitz continuity of the basis functions, can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}_{\Psi}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\Xi)-\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}(t))\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t),{\Xi})\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\hat{D}({\omega})\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert H_{L+d_{\textup{max}}}(\hat{E}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{D}(\omega^{\textup{d}}))\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}

(43)

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Psi}\left\lVert\Xi-\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}\varepsilon^{*}+\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}K_{w}w^{*}+(\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}\varepsilon^{*}+\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}K_{w}w^{*})\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Psi}w^{*}+(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}(1+\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1}),

for
K_{\Psi}>0
. Similarly, for
\sigma_{\Xi}^{\prime}(t)
we have

\left\lVert\sigma_{\Xi}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq w^{*}(1+\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1}).

(44)

Therefore, we have the following bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max\{\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty},\left\lVert\sigma_{\Xi}^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\}

(45)

\displaystyle\leq K_{\Psi}w^{*}+(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}(1+\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1}).

Clearly, this implies that (
14f
) is satisfied. To bound
V_{t}
in (
23
), we need an upper bound on
\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. For that we write

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left((r+n)(L+d_{\textup{max}})+n\right)\left\lVert\sigma^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}

(46)

\displaystyle\leq\left((r+n)(L+d_{\textup{max}})+n\right)\left(K_{\Psi}w^{*}+(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}(1+\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left((r+n)(L+d_{\textup{max}})+n\right)\left(2(K_{\Psi}w^{*})^{2}+2(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}(2+2\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=2\left((r+n)(L+d_{\textup{max}})+n\right)\left((K_{\Psi}w^{*})^{2}+2(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}(1+\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{1}^{2})\right)

where an upper bound on
\left\lVert\alpha^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
can be obtained from (
41
). Finally, we substitute (
32
),(
41
),(
46
) back into (
23
) to obtain the following result

\displaystyle V_{t}\leq c_{4}\left\lVert\Xi_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+c_{5}

(47)

where

\displaystyle c_{4}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lambda_{\max}(Q,R)((K_{\gamma}+1)\Gamma_{v}+1)+c_{3}\lambda_{\textup{max}}(P)

\displaystyle+\left(\lambda_{\alpha}\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}+2\lambda_{\sigma}\left((r+n)(L+d_{\textup{max}})+n\right)\left((K_{\Psi}w^{*})^{2}+2(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)\right)c_{\textup{pe}}\mu_{2},

\displaystyle c_{5}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\lambda_{\sigma}\left((r+n)(L+d_{\textup{max}})+n\right)\left((K_{\Psi}w^{*})^{2}+2(\varepsilon^{*}+K_{w}w^{*})^{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\right)+\lambda_{\alpha}\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}c_{\textup{pe}}\mu_{1}.

Notice that
c_{5}
has the property that it goes to zero as
\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}\to 0
.

VI
Recursive feasibility

In this section, we show that if the robust data-driven nonlinear predictive control scheme (
14
) is feasible at time
t
, then it is feasible at time
t+d_{\textup{max}}
, given that
\varepsilon^{*}
and
w^{*}
are small enough. Theorem
1
below follows similar ideas as in
[
3
, Proposition 1]
. Showing recursive feasibility for all time
t\in\mathbb{N}
will follow in Section
VII
when it is shown that the sub-level set defined by
V_{ROA}
is invariant.

Theorem 1
.

For any
V_{ROA}>0
, there exists an
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
and
\bar{w}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon^{*}\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
and
w^{*}\leq\bar{w}
, if
V_{t}\leq V_{ROA}
for
t\geq 0
, then the optimization problem (
14
) is feasible at time
t+d_{\textup{max}}
.

Proof.

Since
V_{t}\leq V_{ROA}
, then
J^{*}_{t}\leq V_{ROA}
and, hence, the optimization problem (
14
) is feasible at time
t
. Let
\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{*}(t),\mathbf{\bar{y}}^{*}(t),\alpha^{*}(t),\sigma^{*}(t)
be the optimal solutions of (
14
) at time
t
. We propose the following candidate solution for the input sequence at time
t+d_{\textup{max}}
for a
d_{\textup{max}}-
step predictive control scheme

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[-d_{\textup{max}},-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{*}_{[0,d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t)=\mathbf{{u}}_{[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]},

(48)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[0,L-d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{*}_{[d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t),

where the instances
[-d_{\textup{max}},-1]
are fixed by the initial conditions (
14c
) at
t+d_{\textup{max}}
, while the instances from
[0,L-d_{\textup{max}}-1]
are specified as the shifted, previously optimal, input sequence.
As for the candidate output sequence, we use the output which would have been obtained by applying
\mathbf{\bar{u}}_{[0,L-1]}^{*}(t)
at time
t
to the system, i.e.,
\breve{y}_{i,[t,t+L+d_{i}-1]}
, as follows

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,[-d_{\textup{max}},-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pc2_ini}}}{{=}}\tilde{y}_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}=\underbrace{y_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}}_{=\breve{y}_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}}+w_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]},

(49)

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,[0,L+d_{i}-d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle=\breve{y}_{i,[t+d_{\textup{max}},t+L+d_{i}-1]}.

Recall that
\bar{y}_{i,[L,L+d_{i}-1]}^{*}(t)=0
by the terminal constraints of the previous iteration (i.e., at time
t
). Therefore, the following inequality holds by Lemma
1

\displaystyle\left|\breve{y}_{i,t+k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{P}^{k+d_{\textup{max}}-d_{i}}(K_{\Xi})\Big(\varepsilon^{*}(1+\left\lVert\alpha^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{1})+(1+K_{w})w^{*}\left\lVert\alpha^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{1}+(1+\left\lVert\mathcal{G}\right\rVert_{\infty})\left\lVert\sigma^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\Big),\quad\textup{for }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{[L,L+d_{i}-1]}.

(50)

As
\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}\to 0
, the RHS of (
50
) goes to zero as well since
\left\lVert\sigma^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}
is bounded by (
14f
) while
\left\lVert\alpha^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{1}
remains bounded by
\lambda_{\alpha}\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}\left\lVert\alpha^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq J^{*}(t)\leq V_{ROA}
, i.e.,

\left\lVert\alpha^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{N-L-d_{\textup{max}}+1}\left\lVert\alpha^{*}(t)\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{N-L-d_{\textup{max}}+1}\sqrt{\frac{V_{ROA}}{\lambda_{\alpha}\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}}}.

This implies that the corresponding state
\breve{\Xi}_{t+L}=\begin{bmatrix}\breve{y}_{1,[t+L,t+L+d_{1}-1]}^{\top}&\dots&\breve{y}_{m,[t+L,t+L+d_{m}-1]}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
approaches zero as the bounds
\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}\to 0
. Denote by
\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}_{k}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
the
k-
th instant of the state candidate solution predicted at time
t+d_{\textup{max}}
, and notice that the choice of candidate outputs
\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,[L-d_{\textup{max}},L+d_{i}-d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})=\breve{y}_{i,[t+L,t+L+d_{i}-1]}
implies that
\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}_{L-d_{\textup{max}}}(t+d_{\textup{max}})=\breve{\Xi}_{t+L}
, and hence
\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}_{L-d_{\textup{max}}}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
also approaches zero as
\max\{\varepsilon^{*},w^{*}\}\to 0
. Since System (
3
) is controllable, there exists a synthetic input
\mathbf{v}_{[t+L,t+L+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}
, and hence
\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[L-d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
, e.g., a deadbeat controller, that brings the state
\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}_{[L-d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
and the corresponding outputs
\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,[L-d_{\textup{max}},L+d_{i}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
to zero in
d_{\textup{max}}
steps while satisfying the following inequality (cf. (
24
))

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[L-d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\gamma}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{[t+L,t+L+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}\\
\bar{\Xi}^{\prime}_{[L-d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq K_{\gamma}\Gamma_{v}\left\lVert\breve{\Xi}_{t+L}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(51)

Notice that the effect of
\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[L-d_{\textup{max}},L-d_{i}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
(and the corresponding
\mathbf{v}_{[t+L,t+L+d_{\textup{max}}-d_{i}-1]}
), appears at the
i-
th output
d_{i}
instances afterwards. However, it only affects those outputs for which
d_{i}\neq d_{\textup{max}}
. In particular

\begin{bmatrix}\bar{y}_{i,L+d_{i}-d_{\textup{max}}}^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}})\\
\vdots\\
\bar{y}_{i,L-1}^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}})\end{bmatrix}={v}_{i,[t+L,t+L+d_{\textup{max}}-d_{i}-1]}.

(52)

Finally, the terminal constraints fix the last
d_{i}
instances of each output according to (
14d
). To sum up, the candidate solutions for the input and output sequences at time
t+d_{\textup{max}}
take the following form

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[-d_{\textup{max}},-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pc2_ini}}}{{=}}\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{*}_{[0,d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t)=\mathbf{{u}}_{[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]},

(53)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[0,L-d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pre_u_candidate_tdmax}}}{{=}}\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{*}_{[d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t),

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}_{[L-d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle:\textup{deadbeat controller that satisfies }\eqref{ctrb_argument_tdmax},

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,[-d_{\textup{max}},-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pc2_ini}}}{{=}}\tilde{y}_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}=\underbrace{y_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}}_{=\breve{y}_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]}}+w_{i,[t,t+d_{\textup{max}}-1]},

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,[0,L+d_{i}-d_{\textup{max}}-1]}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pre_y_candidate_tdmax}}}{{=}}\breve{y}_{i,[t+d_{\textup{max}},t+L+d_{i}-1]},

\displaystyle\bar{y}_{i,[L+d_{i}-d_{\textup{max}},L-1]}^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pre_y_di_not_dmax}}}{{=}}{v}_{i,[t+L,t+L+d_{\textup{max}}-d_{i}-1]},\quad\textup{ for }d_{i}\neq d_{\textup{max}},

\displaystyle\bar{y}_{i,[L,L+d_{i}-1]}^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pc2_term}}}{{=}}\mathbf{0}.

Next, we choose
\alpha^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}})
as

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}})=\begin{bmatrix}H_{L+d_{\textup{max}}}(\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{E}(\mathbf{u}^{\textup{d}},\tilde{\Xi}^{\textup{d}})+\hat{D}(\omega^{\textup{d}}))\\
H_{1}(\Xi_{[0,N-L-d_{\textup{max}}]}^{\textup{d}})\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}}),\breve{\Xi})+\hat{E}(\mathbf{\bar{u}}^{\prime}(t+d_{\textup{max}}),\breve{\Xi})+\hat{D}(\breve{\omega})\\
{\Xi}_{t}\end{bmatrix},

(54)

where

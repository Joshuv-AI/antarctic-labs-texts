Energy-balance for the incompressible Euler equations with stochastic forcing
Shyam Sundar Ghoshal, Animesh Jana, Barun Sarkar

Abstract
We establish energy-balance for weak solutions of the stochastically forced incompressible Euler equations, enjoying Hölder regularity C α \displaystyle C^{\alpha} , α > 1 / 3 \displaystyle\alpha>1/3 . It is well known as the Onsager’s conjecture for the deterministic incompressible Euler equations, which describes the energy conservation of weak solutions having Hölder regularity C α \displaystyle C^{\alpha} , α > 1 / 3 \displaystyle\alpha>1/3 . Additionally, we obtain energy-balance for the inhomogeneous incompressible Euler system driven by cylindrical Wiener process.

Incompressible Euler

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dv_{x}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}=0.

(1.2)

Inhomogeneous incompressible Euler

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\rho+\dv_{x}(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})+\dv_{x}\left(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{x}p\,\mathrm{d}t=\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(1.4)

Preliminaries

In this present article, we consider that weak solutions possess Besov regularity, defined as follows: let
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle 1\leq q<\infty
. Then for an open bounded set
\displaystyle\Pi\subset\bar{\Pi}\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
, Besov space
\displaystyle B_{q}^{\alpha,\infty}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a subset of
\displaystyle L^{q}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\displaystyle f\in{B_{q}^{\alpha,\infty}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}^{n})}
if the following seminorm is finite,

|f|_{B_{q}^{\alpha,\infty}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{0\neq\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\zeta+\Pi\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{\|f(\cdot+\zeta)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\Pi,\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{|\zeta|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

We note that
\displaystyle C^{\alpha}(\Pi)\subset B^{\alpha,\infty}_{q}(\Pi)
for
\displaystyle 1\leq q<\infty
.

The cylindrical Wiener process
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
considered here are defined over a separable Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
with respect to a filtered probability space
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
where
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a complete and right-continuous filtration.
\displaystyle W_{t}
can be the formally written as

W_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e_{j}W^{j}_{t},

(2.1)

where,
\displaystyle\{e_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
and
\displaystyle\{W^{j}_{t}\}_{j\geq 1}
is a sequence of mutually independent
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
-valued Brownian motions with respect to
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. We describe the diffusion coefficients
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
respectively.

Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then we define the noise coefficient
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}):\mathcal{U}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}})e_{j}:=\mathbb{G}_{j}(\cdot,\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(\cdot)).

The coefficients
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{j}=\mathbb{G}_{j}(x,\mathrm{\textbf{v}}):\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are
\displaystyle C^{1}
- functions that satisfy uniformly in
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{G}_{j}(\cdot,0)=0,\\
&|\nabla_{\mathrm{\textbf{v}}}\mathbb{G}_{j}|\leq g_{j},\ \ \text{[where sequence $\displaystyle\{g_{j}\}\subset(0,\infty)$ ]}\\
&\sum_{j\geq 1}g_{j}^{2}<\infty.\end{split}

(2.2)

If
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}
satisfies (
2.2
) and
v
are
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
- progressively measurable
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
- valued process such that

\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\times[0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\right),

then the following integral is well-defined

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\ dW_{s}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{G}_{j}(\cdot,\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\ dW^{j}_{s}.

Let
\displaystyle\rho\in\ L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
\displaystyle\rho\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\textbf{m}:=(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then we define the diffusion coefficient
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\textbf{m}):\mathcal{U}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\textbf{m})e_{j}:=\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}(\cdot,\rho(\cdot),\textbf{m}(\cdot)),

where coefficients
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}=\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}(x,\rho,\textbf{m}):\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are
\displaystyle C^{1}
- functions and there exists a non-negative real numbers sequence
\displaystyle\{\tilde{g}_{j}\}
such that the following holds uniformly in
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}(\cdot,0,0)=0,\ \ \ |\partial_{\rho}\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}|+|\nabla_{\textbf{m}}\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}|\leq\tilde{g}_{j}\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \sum_{j\geq 1}{\tilde{g}_{j}}^{2}<\infty.\end{split}

(2.3)

Note that, if
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
satisfies (
2.3
) and
\displaystyle\rho,(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})
are
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
- progressively measurable
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
- valued process such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\times[0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\right)\mbox{ and }(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\times[0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\right),

then the following is a well-defined
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
- martingale in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\mathrm{d}W_{s}:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{j}(\cdot,\rho,\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\mathrm{d}W^{j}_{s}.

The infinite sum in (
2.1
) does not converge in probabilistic sense as a random variable in
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
. But, we can construct an auxiliary space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0}\supset\mathcal{U}
, where the sum converges. Define the space

\mathcal{U}_{0}:=\left\{u=\sum_{j\geq 1}u_{j}e_{j};\ \sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{j^{2}}<\infty\right\},

with the following norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}^{2}:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{j^{2}}.

Note that the embedding
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{0}
is Hilbert-Schmidt. The trajectories of
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}\subset C([0,T];\mathcal{U}_{0})
,
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
- a.s. In this article, the separable Hilbert space is chosen to be
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

3
Energy-balance for the stochastically forced incompressible Euler
equation

This section deals with energy-balance for the incompressible Euler equation driven by cylindrical Wiener process.

Definition 3.1

(pathwise weak solutions of incompressible Euler
equations with stochastic forcing)
.

Let
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space with right-continuous filtration,
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a cylindrical Wiener process with respect to filtration
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}
satisfy condition (
2.2
). We say that
\displaystyle(\mathrm{\textbf{v}},p,\tau)
is a pathwise weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), if the following holds:

1.

\displaystyle\tau>0
is an a.s. strictly positive
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
-stopping time.

2.

For each
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\displaystyle t\mapsto\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,dx\in C([0,\tau))
and the process
\displaystyle t\mapsto\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,dx
, is progressively measurable with respect to filtration
\displaystyle(\mathfrak{F}_{t})
, such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\in C\left([0,\tau);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)\cap L^{\infty}([0,\tau)\times\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

3.

For each
\displaystyle\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
\displaystyle t\mapsto\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\phi p(\cdot,t)\,dx
is progressively measurable with respect to filtration
\displaystyle(\mathfrak{F}_{t})
, such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle p\in L^{2}\left([0,\tau)\times\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}\right).

4.

The following equation holds
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle\forall\,\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(t,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

5.

For all
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
the following equation holds
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(t,\cdot)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(0,\cdot)\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left[(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(x)\dv_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Theorem 3.2

(Energy-balance for the stochastically forced incompressible Euler
equations)
.

Let
\displaystyle(\mathrm{\textbf{v}},p,\tau)
be a pathwise weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), as in definition
3.1
and

\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\in L^{3}\left([0,\tau);B_{3}^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})\right).

Then, for
\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
, the following Energy-balance equation holds,
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
- a.s., for each
\displaystyle t\in[0,\tau)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(0,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x))(e_{k})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x)\cdot\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}.\end{split}

(3.1)

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

After mollifying (

)-(
1.2
) in space, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}+\left[\dv_{x}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})+\nabla_{x}p^{\epsilon}\right]\mathrm{d}t=\dv_{x}\left[(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})-(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\right]\mathrm{d}t+\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dv_{x}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}=0.

(3.3)

Strong solution of (

), for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau
,
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(-\left[\dv_{x}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})+\nabla_{x}p^{\epsilon}\right]+\dv_{x}\left[(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})-(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\right]\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Writing Itô’s formula for
\displaystyle r\mapsto\|r\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(0,x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle 2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\,,\,\left[\dv_{x}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})+\nabla_{x}p^{\epsilon}\right](s,x)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle 2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\,,\,\dv_{x}\left[(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})-(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\right](s,x)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s

&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\cdot\dv_{x}\left[(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})-(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\right](s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\

(3.4)

As
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
, by lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\\
&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(0,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(0,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(3.5)

Then, consider the integrand of the 2nd term of r.h.s. of (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\cdot\left[\dv_{x}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})+\nabla_{x}p^{\epsilon}\right](s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \nabla_{x}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x):(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \dv_{x}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\,p^{\epsilon}(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x-0\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\dv_{x}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\ |\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\\
&=0.\end{split}

(3.6)

By Commutator estimate lemma
2.3
, as
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
, 3rd term of r.h.s. of (
3.4
)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\cdot\dv_{x}\left[(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon})-(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}})^{\epsilon}\right](s,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\to 0,\,\mathbb{P}-\mbox{a.s.}

(3.7)

Again, by lemma
2.2
, as
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
, 4th term of the r.h.s. of (
3.4
)

\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x))^{\epsilon}(e_{k})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\to\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x))(e_{k})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s,\,\mathbb{P}-\mbox{a.s.}

(3.8)

At the end, as
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
, by lemma
2.1
, 5th term of r.h.s. of (
3.4
)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\cdot\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x))^{\epsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\to\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}2\ \mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x)\cdot\mathbb{G}(\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,x))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},

(3.9)

converges in mean square, hence in probability. Therefore, combining (
3.5
)-(
3.9
) in (
3.4
), as
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
, for a subsequence, we obtain energy-balance equation (
3.1
), in
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
- a.s. sense, for each
\displaystyle t\in[0,\tau)
. This concludes the proof.

∎

4
Energy-balance for the stochastically forced inhomogeneous incompressible Euler
equation

In this section, we prove energy-balance equation for the inhomogeneous incompressible Euler equation driven by the cylindrical Wiener process.

Definition 4.1

(pathwise weak solutions of inhomogeneous incompressible Euler
equations with stochastic forcing)
.

Let
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space with right-continuous filtration,
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a cylindrical Wiener process with respect to the filtration
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{G}}
satisfy condition (
2.3
).
\displaystyle(\rho,\mathrm{\textbf{v}},p,\tau)
is called a pathwise weak solution of (
1.3
)-(
1.5
), if the following holds:

1.

\displaystyle\tau>0
is an a.s. strictly positive
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
-stopping time.

2.

The density
\displaystyle\rho
is progressively measurable with respect to the filtration
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
. There exists
\displaystyle\bar{r}>0
such that the following holds for
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\rho\geq\bar{r}\mbox{ and }\ \rho\in C\left([0,\tau);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left([0,\tau)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\right).

3.

For each
\displaystyle\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\displaystyle t\mapsto\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x\in C([0,\tau))
and the process
\displaystyle t\mapsto\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x
, is progressively measurable with respect to the filtration
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
, such that for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau
,
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\in C\left([0,\tau);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left([0,\tau)\times\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

4.

The pressure
\displaystyle p
is progressively measurable with respect to the filtration
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
and following holds
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

p\in C\left([0,\tau);L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left([0,\tau)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\right).

5.

For all
\displaystyle\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}x
, is progressively measurable with respect to the filtration
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
, such that the following equation holds
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho(t,\cdot)\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho(0,\cdot)\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(s,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s.

6.

The following equation holds
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
\displaystyle\forall\,\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(t,\cdot)\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(0,\cdot)\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left[\left(\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\otimes\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\right):\nabla_{x}\Phi\,+p\,\dv_{x}\Phi\right]\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\cdot\Phi\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

7.

The following equation holds
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
\displaystyle\forall\,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for
\displaystyle 0\leq t<\tau
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}(t,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Theorem 4.2

(Energy-balance for the stochastically forced inhomogeneous incompressible Euler
equation)
.

Let
\displaystyle(\rho,\mathrm{\textbf{v}},p,\tau)
be a pathwise weak solution of (
1.3
)-(
1.5
), as in definition
4.1
and

(\rho,\mathrm{\textbf{v}},p)\in L^{3}\left([0,\tau);B_{3}^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})\right).

Then, for
\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
, the following Energy-balance equation holds,
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
- a.s., for all
\displaystyle\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\infty)
and
\displaystyle\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, for each
\displaystyle t\in[0,\tau)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\theta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}|^{2}\right)\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\theta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left[\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathrm{\textbf{v}}|^{2}+p\right)\right]\cdot\nabla_{x}\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\theta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{2\rho}|\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})(e_{k})|^{2}\,\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\theta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{\textbf{v}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbb{G}}(\rho,\rho\mathrm{\textbf{v}})\,\Psi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}W_{s}=0.\end{split}

(4.1)

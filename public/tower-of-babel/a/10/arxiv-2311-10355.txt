Abstract
. We consider a slightly subcritical elliptic system with Dirichlet boundary conditions and a non-power nonlinearity in a bounded smooth domain. For this problem, standard compact embeddings cannot be used to guarantee the existence of solutions as in the case of power-type nonlinearities. Instead, we use the dual method on Orlicz spaces, showing that our problem possesses a mountain pass type solution. Keywords: mountain pass solutions, critical Sobolev exponent, dual method MSC2020: 35B38, 35B33, 35J47, 35J67.



1.
Introduction

Let us consider the system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=\frac{u^{p}}{\big(\ln(e+u)\big)^{\alpha}}\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&-\Delta u=\frac{v^{q}}{\big(\ln(e+v)\big)^{\beta}}\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&u>0,\,v>0\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&u=v=0\ \hbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
, is a bounded domain of class
C^{2},

p,q>0
,
\alpha\leq p,\ \beta\leq q,
and
(p,q)
may belong to the critical hyperbola; specifically, either

(1.2)

1>\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{N-2}{N},

or

(1.3)

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{N-2}{N},\qquad\frac{\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{\beta}{q+1}>0.

We want to find a solution
(u,v)
of (
1.1
), positive in both components.

Problems of type (
1.1
) has been considered by several authors, we refer to
[
3
,
9
,
11
,
17
]
. In

[
3
]
the authors study the case (
1.2
) and
\alpha=\beta=0
.
Whereas in
[
9
, Theorem 2.7]
the authors study related nonlinearities when the pair of exponents
(p,q)
lies below the critical Sobolev hyperbola (
1.2
), using variational approaches.
In
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
, using an Orlicz-space approach, the authors study the existence of solutions of (
1.1
) when the pairs of exponents
(p,q)
,
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfy (
1.3
), in particular
(p,q)
lies on the critical Sobolev hyperbola, with
p,q>1
; they do not cover the case
p,q>0
.
While in
[
17
]

the authors establish a-priori
L^{\infty}
bounds when the pair of exponents
(p,q)
lies on the critical Sobolev hyperbola (
1.3
),
1<p,q<\infty
, and
\displaystyle\alpha,\,\beta>2/(N-2).

Concerning one single equation, we can mention
[
5
]
establishing
L^{\infty}
a priori bounds when
\alpha>2/(N-2)
,
[
8
]
analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the solutions as
\alpha\to 0
, and
[
10
]

including a changing sign weight.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
p,q>0
,
\alpha\leq p,\ \beta\leq q,
and either
(p,q)
satisfy (
1.2
) or
(p,q)
,
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfy (
1.3
).
Then Problem (
1.1
) possesses a solution of mountain pass type.

The proof relies on the dual method which allows to reduce the existence of solutions to the problem (
1.1
) to finding critical points of an energy functional defined on a suitable Orlicz space, whose accurate choice is the main novelty of the present paper.

Remark 1.2
.

We would like to comment on condition (
1.3
). At this aim we
point out that Theorem
1.1
also applies to the case of the single equation, namely
p=q=\frac{N+2}{N-2}

and
\alpha=\beta>0
. In particular, the problem

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\frac{u^{p}}{\big(\ln(e+u)\big)^{\alpha}}\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&u>0\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&u=0\ \hbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

has always a positive solution. On the other hand, it is known that Pohozaev’s identity
[
21
]
ensures the non-existence of positive solutions on star-shaped domains to equation (
1.4
) whenever
\alpha\leq 0,
see Remark (
4.3
).

In a similar way, it could be interesting to prove the criticality of our condition
1.3
. Indeed, a question naturally arises:
if
p
and
q
lie on the critical hyperbola and

\frac{\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{\beta}{q+1}\leq 0

does the system (
1.1
) have any positive solutions on a star-shaped domain?
In Theorem
4.2
we give a partial answer when both
\alpha
and
\beta
are not positive. The general case remains open.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we describe the dual method which allows us to consider
p,\ q>0
. Roughly speaking, this method consists in taking the inverse of the Laplace operator, and defining the inverse of the nonlinearities. In Section
3
we prove our main result, that the associated energy functional, defined in a suitable product of Orlicz spaces, has a mountain pass geometry.
Section
4
is devoted to analyze the criticality of the condition
1.3
.
Finally, Section
5
summarize the theory of Orlicz spaces needed for our purposes.

2.
The dual method

2.1.
The variational formulation

Given any
s\in(1,\infty)
, we introduce
the operator
\mathtt{K}:=(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{s}(\Omega)\to W^{2,s}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,s}(\Omega)
which is defined as

\mathtt{K}(f):=u\qquad\mbox{if and only if}\quad-\Delta u=f\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega,\ u=0\ \hbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.

Next, we set

(2.1)

a(t):=\frac{t^{p}}{\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\alpha}},\qquad\mbox{and}\quad b(t):=\frac{t^{q}}{\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\beta}}\ \hbox{ with}\ t\geq 0,

and consider their primitive functions:

(2.2)

A(t):=\int\limits_{0}^{t}a(s)\,ds\qquad\mbox{and}\quad B(t):=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(s)\,ds\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq 0.

If
\alpha\leq p
and
\beta\leq q
, the functions
a
,
b
are strictly increasing and so invertible in
[0,+\infty)
.
Let us denote by

(2.3)

\tilde{a}:=a^{-1}\qquad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{b}:=b^{-1}\quad\text{the inverse functions.}

If we set

f=a(u)
and
g=b(v)
or equivalently
u=\tilde{a}(f)
and
v=\tilde{b}(g),
then problem (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{b}(g)=\mathtt{K}(f)\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&\tilde{a}(f)=\mathtt{K}(g)\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega\\
&f>0,\,g>0\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega.&\end{aligned}\right.

Let us introduce their primitive functions:

(2.5)

\widetilde{A}(t):=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\tilde{a}(s)\,ds\qquad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{B}(t):=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\tilde{b}(s)\,ds,

for all
t\geq 0
, and denote
L^{\tilde{A}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\tilde{B}}(\Omega)
the
Orlicz spaces

associated to the functions
{\widetilde{A}}
and
{\widetilde{B}}
respectively, cf. definition
5.3
and Remark
5.7
. Observe that, since
\tilde{a}
and
\tilde{b}
are continuous and increasing the functions
\tilde{A}
are
\tilde{B}
are
\mathcal{N}
- functions, cf. definition
5.1
. We endowed the space
L^{\tilde{A}}(\Omega)
(resp.
L^{\tilde{B}}(\Omega)
) with either the
Luxembourg norm
\|\cdot\|_{(\tilde{A})}
or the
Orlicz norm

\|\cdot\|_{\tilde{A}}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{(\tilde{B})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\tilde{B}}
), see definitions (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) for those norms. From Lemma
5.5
and Proposition
5.10
(i), the spaces
L^{\tilde{A}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\tilde{B}}(\Omega)
are complete with both the Orlicz or the Luxemburg norm.

Problem (
2.4
) has a
variational structure
. Indeed, let us denote by
X
the Banach space

X=L^{\tilde{A}}(\Omega)\times L^{\tilde{B}}(\Omega)

endowed with the norm
\|(f,g)\|_{X}:=\|f\|_{\tilde{A}}+\|g\|_{\tilde{B}}
.

By the regularity result
Theorem
5.29
, the operator
{\mathtt{K}}
sends
L^{\tilde{A}}(\Omega)
into
W_{0}^{1,{\widetilde{A}}}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,{\tilde{A}}}(\Omega)
, cf. Definition
5.19
of the
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

W_{0}^{1,H}(\Omega)
and
W^{2,H}(\Omega)
, when
H
is equal to either
\tilde{A}
or
\tilde{B}
.
Thus for any
(f,g)\in X
,
({\mathtt{K}}(f),{\mathtt{K}}(g))\in W^{2,{\tilde{A}}}(\Omega)\times W^{2,{\tilde{B}}}(\Omega).

By a
solution

(u,v)
of (
1.1
) we mean a pair
(u,v)\in\big(W_{0}^{1,{\widetilde{A}}}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,{\tilde{A}}}(\Omega)\big)\times\big(W_{0}^{1,{\widetilde{B}}}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,{\tilde{B}}}(\Omega)\big)
satisfying (
1.1
) in the weak sense.

Moreover, for
(p,q),\ (\alpha,\beta)
satisfying either (
1.2
),
or (
1.3
),
we have that the embeddings
W^{2,{\tilde{A}}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{B}(\Omega)
and
W^{2,{\tilde{B}}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{A}(\Omega)
,
are continuous and compact, see Lemma
2.8
.
Consequently,
f\,{\mathtt{K}}(g)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
g\,{\mathtt{K}}(f)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, see the 2nd Holder’s inequality (
5.5
) in Proposition
5.10
. Furthermore, it follows from the definition of
\mathtt{K}
, that

(2.6)

\int\limits_{\Omega}f\,{\mathtt{K}}(g)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\,{\mathtt{K}}(f)\cdot\nabla\,{\mathtt{K}}(g)\,dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}g\,{\mathtt{K}}(f)\,dx.

For convenience, let us extend
a
,
\tilde{a}
,
b
,
\tilde{b}
in the whole
\mathbb{R}
as odd functions, and extend
A
,
\widetilde{A}
,
B
and
\widetilde{B}
as even functions. We next introduce the
C^{1}
-functional
J:X\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(2.7)

J(f,g):=\int\limits_{\Omega}\widetilde{A}(f)\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\widetilde{B}(g)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\big(f\,\mathtt{K}(g)+g\,\mathtt{K}(f)\big)\,dx

whose derivative at
(f,g)\in X
is equal to

J^{\prime}(f,g)[\psi_{1},\psi_{2}]=\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tilde{a}(f)\psi_{1}\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\widetilde{b}(g)\psi_{2}\,dx\right)-\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathtt{\psi}_{1}K(g)+\int\limits_{\Omega}\psi_{2}\,\mathtt{K}(f)\,dx\right),

for all
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in X.

Trivially, the equations (
2.4
) are the Euler-Lagrange equations associated to the action functional
J
.

2.2.
On the nonlinearities

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)
There exists two constants
c_{1},\ c_{2}>0
, only dependent of
p,
and
\alpha,

such that

c_{1}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\leq\tilde{a}(t)\leq c_{2}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}},\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq 0.

(ii)
There exists two constants
C_{1},\ C_{2}>0
only dependent of
p,
and
\alpha,

C_{1}\,t^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\leq\widetilde{A}(t)\leq C_{2}\,t^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}},\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq 0.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

(i)
By definition,

a\Big(t\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\Big)=t^{p}\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\alpha}\left[\ln\left(e+t\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\right)\right]^{-\alpha}.

Now, checking that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\displaystyle\ln\left(e+t\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\right)}{\ln(e+t)}=1,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\ln\left(e+t\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\right)}{\ln(e+t)}=1,

and since that quotient of logarithms is a continuous, non vanishing function for
t\geq 0
,
we can conclude that there exists two constants
d_{1},\ d_{2}>0
such that

d_{1}t^{p}\leq a\Big(t\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\Big)\leq d_{2}t^{p},\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq 0,

and since
\tilde{a}
is increasing,

\tilde{a}(d_{1}t^{p})\leq t\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\leq\tilde{a}(d_{2}t^{p}),\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq 0.

Now, firstly denoting by
s=d_{1}t^{p}
, secondly using that

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\displaystyle\ln\left(e+t^{1/p}\right)}{\ln(e+t)}=1,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\ln\left(e+t^{1/p}\right)}{\ln(e+t)}=1,

and thirdly, denoting by
s=d_{2}t^{p}
, we obtain (
2.8
).

(ii) Given
\gamma>0,
and
\nu\geq-\gamma
, set

\sigma_{\gamma,\nu}(t)=\sigma(t):=t^{\gamma}\,\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\nu},\qquad\mbox{for}\quad t>0,

then

\sigma^{\prime}_{\gamma,\nu}(t)=\sigma^{\prime}(t)=t^{\gamma-1}\,\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\nu}\left[\gamma+\nu\frac{1}{\ln(e+t)}\,\frac{t}{(e+t)}\right].

Observe that

a(t)=\sigma_{p,-\alpha}(t),

and that

(2.9)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{ta^{\prime}(t)}{a(t)}=p.

Moreover, if
\nu\geq-\gamma,
then
\sigma
is increasing.

Set

h(t):=\frac{1}{\ln(e+t)}\frac{t}{(e+t)}>0,\qquad\mbox{for}\quad t>0.

Trivially,
\lim_{t\to 0}h(t)=0,

\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)=0,
and there exists a unique

t^{*}>0
such that

\ h(t)\leq h(t^{*})=\frac{e}{e+t^{*}}<1,\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Hence

(2.10)

\left(\gamma+\frac{|\nu|\,e}{e+t^{*}}\right)^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}_{\gamma,\nu}(t)\leq t^{\gamma-1}\,\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\nu}\leq\left(\gamma-\frac{|\nu|\,e}{e+t^{*}}\right)^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}_{\gamma,\nu}(t),

and observe that whenever
\gamma>1
,
t^{\gamma-1}\,\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\nu}=\sigma_{\gamma-1,\nu}(t)
, so

\left(\gamma+\frac{|\nu|\,e}{e+t^{*}}\right)^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}_{\gamma,\nu}(t)\leq\sigma_{\gamma-1,\nu}(t)\leq\left(\gamma-\frac{|\nu|\,e}{e+t^{*}}\right)^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}_{\gamma,\nu}(t).

By definition of
\widetilde{A}
(see (
2.2
)), using (
2.8
), and (
2.10
),
we can write

\widetilde{A}(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\tilde{a}(s)\,ds\leq c_{2}\int\limits_{0}^{t}s^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\ln(e+s)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\,ds\leq C_{2}\sigma_{\frac{p+1}{p},\frac{\alpha}{p}}(t)=C_{2}t^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\,\big(\ln(e+t)\big)^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}.

Likewise is obtained the reverse inequality.
∎

2.3.
On the space

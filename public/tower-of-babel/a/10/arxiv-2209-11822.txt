On p p -adic Measures for Quaternionic Modular Forms
Yubo Jin

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to study the special values of the standard L L -functions for quaternionic modular forms using the doubling method. We obtain an integral representation for the L L -function twisted by a character following the work of Böcherer-Schmidt ( [ 4 ] ) on Siegel modular forms. As an application, we construct the p p -adic measure interpolating certain special L L -values.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the special
L
-values of quaternionic modular forms and construct the
p
-adic measure. The main strategy is to study the
L
-functions utilizing an integral representation involving the Siegel Eisenstein series and the properties of special
L
-values then follow from the explicit Fourier expansion of Siegel Eisenstein series. For Siegel modular forms there are two main approaches to obtain the integral representation and construct
p
-adic measures. One is the Rankin-Selberg method (e.g.
[
8
]
), the other is the doubling method (e.g.
[
4
]
). We will mainly follow the doubling method in
[
4
]
to get the integral representation for twisted
L
-functions.

Let
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}\zeta\oplus\mathbb{Q}\xi\oplus\mathbb{Q}\zeta\xi
be a quaternion algebra with
\zeta^{2}=\alpha,\xi^{2}=\beta,\zeta\xi=-\xi\zeta
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
. The modular forms studied in this paper are defined on the algebraic group

G=\{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2n}(\mathbb{B}):g\phi g^{\ast}=\phi\},\phi=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\epsilon\\
1_{n}&0\end{array}\right],

with
\epsilon=\pm 1
. Let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{R})
. It is well known that
G(\mathbb{R})/K
is a Riemannian manifold and is actually Hermitian if
\epsilon=-1,\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{H}
or
\epsilon=1,\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Here
\mathbb{H}
is the Hamilton quaternion. We will restrict ourselves to above two cases in this paper which we refer as Case I and Case II. More precisely the symmetric space defined in Case I is the Type D domain in
[
14
]
and the Type C domain in Case II. When
G(\mathbb{R})/K
is a Hermitian symmetric space, the algebraicity of modular forms makes sense (see
[
16
]
). Indeed, the algebraicity for quaternionic modular forms of Case I is already studied in our previous paper
[
7
]
. But in this paper we only consider the case that the associated symmetric space has even degree, i.e. is a ‘tube’ domain. Instead of defining algebraic modular forms using CM points as in
[
7
, Section 5.2]
, we have an easier description via Fourier expansions. In particular, we have a well-defined action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
on modular forms in terms of the action on the Fourier coefficients. This will allow us to obtain a more precise algebraic result in Proposition
6.2
. That is we can not only show that the special
L
-values are algebraic up to some constants but also describe the action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
.

After reviewing the preliminary of quaternionic modular forms and their standard
L
-functions in Section
2
, the main computations are carried out in Section
3
,
4
,
5
. Let

be a cusp form on degree
n

\mathfrak{g}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\times\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
deriving form the pullbacks of certain Siegel Eisenstein series on
\mathcal{H}_{2n}
along
\mathcal{H}_{n}\times\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathcal{H}_{2n}

such that its Fourier coefficients have natural

is same as the one in
[

]
. Indeed, it is same as the twisted Eisenstein series there except for the slight difference for local sections at bad places away from

. However, the global computations of the inner product

in

•

We remark that the
p
-adic aspects of the quaternionic modular forms are not as well understood as those of Siegel modular forms and Hermitian modular forms. For example, one can expect the result in
[

[

can be generalized to quaternionic case, the Hida theory as in
[
11
]
has not established in our case since the Shimura varieties involved in quaternionic case are not of PEL-type. The
p

Quaternionic Modular Forms and
L

In this section we review basics of quaternionic modular forms and their (standard)
L
-functions. The references for this kind of modular forms includes
[
6
,
13
]
for Case I and
[

,

]
for Case II. For preliminaries on quaternion algebras the reader can refer to

2.1.
Quaternion algebra and algebraic groups

We start by fixing some notations for quaternion algebras and our algebraic groups.

A quaternion algebra is a central simple algebra of dimension four over
\mathbb{Q}
. Picking up a basis, we can write it in the form

\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}\zeta\oplus\mathbb{Q}\xi\oplus\mathbb{Q}\zeta\xi,

where

\zeta^{2}=\alpha,\xi^{2}=\beta,\zeta\xi=-\xi\zeta,

with
\alpha,\beta
nonzero squarefree integers. The main involution of
\mathbb{B}
is given by

\overline{\cdot}:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{B}:a+b\zeta+c\xi+d\zeta\xi\mapsto\overline{a+b\zeta+c\xi+d\zeta\xi}=a-b\zeta-c\xi-d\zeta\xi.

We remind the reader that we will also use the same notation
\overline{\cdot}
for usual complex involution if its meaning is clear from the context. The trace and norm are defined by
\mathrm{tr}(x)=x+\overline{x},N(x)=x\overline{x}
for
x\in\mathbb{B}
. As usual we denote
M_{n}(\mathbb{B})
as a set of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{B}
. For
X\in M_{n}(\mathbb{B})
, we write
X^{\ast}=\text{${}^{t}\!\overline{X}$},\hat{X}=(X^{\ast})^{-1}
for the conjugate transpose and its inverse (if makes sense). The determinant
\det
and trace
\mathrm{tr}
for matrices in
M_{n}(\mathbb{B})
will mean the reduced norm and reduced trace. We use the same notations
\det,\mathrm{tr}
for usual determinant and trace of matrices whose entries are complex numbers. Denote

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{B})=\{g\in M_{n}(\mathbb{B}):\det(g)\neq 0\},\quad\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{B})=\{g\in M_{n}(\mathbb{B}):\det(g)=1\}.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be the adele ring of
\mathbb{Q}
. For a place
v
we mean either a finite place corresponding to a prime or the archimedean place
\infty
. The set of finite places is denoted as
\mathbf{h}
. We write
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{h}}\mathbb{R}
with finite adeles
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{h}}
and
x=x_{\mathbf{h}}x_{\infty}
with
x\in\mathbb{A},x_{\mathbf{h}}\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{h}},x_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix embeddings
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
and set
\mathbb{B}_{v}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{v}
. The previous definition of trace, norm, determinant naturally extends locally and adelically. Fix a maximal order
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathbb{B}
and set
\mathcal{O}_{v}=\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. For a place
v
we call
v
splits if
\mathbb{B}_{v}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
. If this is the case we fix an identification
\mathfrak{i}_{v}:\mathbb{B}_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
and assume
\mathfrak{i}_{v}(\mathcal{O}_{v})=M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{v})
for finite places. Write
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}=\mathbb{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
for
v=\infty
. We call
\mathbb{B}
definite if
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}=\mathbb{H}
is non-split and indefinite if otherwise. Here
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{j}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{ij}
with
\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=-1,\mathbf{ij=-ji}
is the Hamilton quaternion algebra.

We consider following two algebraic groups.

(Case I)
\mathbb{B}
is definite.

G:=G(\mathbb{Q}):=G_{n}(\mathbb{Q}):=\{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2n}(\mathbb{B}):gJ_{n}g^{\ast}=J_{n}\},\quad J_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1_{n}\\
1_{n}&0\end{array}\right].

(Case II)
\mathbb{B}
is indefinite.

G:=G(\mathbb{Q}):=G_{n}(\mathbb{Q}):=\{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2n}(\mathbb{B}):gI_{n}g^{\ast}=I_{n}\},\quad I_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1_{n}\\
1_{n}&0\end{array}\right].

We also define (connected) similitude groups

(Case I)

\tilde{G}:=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{B}):gJ_{n}g^{\ast}=\nu(g)J_{n},\det(g)=\nu(g)^{n},\nu(g)\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}\}.

(Case II)

\tilde{G}:=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{B}):gI_{n}g^{\ast}=\nu(g)I_{n},\det(g)=\nu(g)^{n},\nu(g)\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}\}.

Fix an integral ideal
\mathfrak{n}=(N)
of
\mathcal{O}
generated by
N=\prod_{v}p_{v}^{n_{v}}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define open compact subgroups
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
and
K^{0}(\mathfrak{n})
of
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{h}})
by

K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}):=\prod_{v}K_{v}=\prod_{v}\left\{\gamma:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\in G(\mathcal{O}_{v}):\gamma\equiv\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\ast&\ast\\
0_{n}&\ast\end{array}\right]\text{ mod }p_{v}^{n_{v}}\right\},

K^{0}(\mathfrak{n}):=\prod_{v}K_{v}:=\prod_{v}\left\{\gamma=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\in G(\mathcal{O}_{v}):\gamma\equiv\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\ast&0_{n}\\
\ast&\ast\end{array}\right]\text{ mod }p_{v}^{n_{v}}\right\}.

Denote
\Gamma_{0}(N):=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})
,
\Gamma^{0}(N):=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap K^{0}(\mathfrak{n})
. We have

(2.1)

G(\mathbb{A})=G(\mathbb{Q})G(\mathbb{R})K_{0}(\mathfrak{n})=G(\mathbb{Q})G(\mathbb{R})K^{0}(\mathfrak{n}).

In Case II, this simply follows from the strong approximation. In Case I, the strong approximation does not hold in general. But for subgroups
K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),K^{0}(\mathfrak{n})
, above relation follows by the weak approximation and
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
.

2.2.
Symmetric spaces

Define
S:=S(\mathbb{Q}):=\{X\in M_{n}(\mathbb{B}):X^{\ast}=-\epsilon X\}
for the (additive) algebraic group of hermitian matrices (resp. skew-hermitian matrices) with
\epsilon=-1
(resp.
\epsilon=1
) in Case I (resp. Case II). We use
S^{+}
(resp.
S_{+}
) denote the subgroup of
S
consisting of positive definite (resp. positive) elements. The symmetric space is defined as

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{n}:=\{z=x+iy\in S(\mathbb{R})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}:x\in S(\mathbb{R}),y\in S^{+}(\mathbb{R})\},

with the action of
\tilde{G}(\mathbb{R})
given by

g=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\times z\mapsto(az+b)(cz+d)^{-1}.

Set
\mu(g,z):=cz+d
and
j(g,z):=\det[\mu(g,z)]
for the automorphy factor. Put
\delta(z):=\det(y)^{1/2}
so
\delta(gz)=|j(g,z)|^{-1}\delta(z)
.

For Case I, the symmetric space is same as the quaternionic upper half plane in
[
6
]
and we denote
z_{0}:=i\cdot 1_{n}
for the origin of
\mathcal{H}
. By embedding the quaternion algebra
\mathbb{H}
into
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
one can show that this domain is of type D domain in
[
14
]
, see also
[
7
, Section 2.2]
for more realization of this space.

For Case II, since
\mathbb{B}
is indefinite we have an isomorphism
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}\cong M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For example, without lossing generality, write
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}\zeta\oplus\mathbb{R}\xi\oplus\mathbb{R}\zeta\xi
with
\zeta^{2}=1,\xi^{2}=-1,\zeta\xi=-\xi\zeta
. The isomorphism can be given by

\mathfrak{i}:\mathbb{B}_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad x=a+b\zeta+c\xi+d\zeta\xi\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a+b&c+d\\
-c+d&a-b\end{array}\right].

Clearly
\mathfrak{i}(x^{\ast})=-J_{1}\text{${}^{t}\!\mathfrak{i}(x)$}J_{1}
. Extending this isomorphism entries by entries we obtain

\mathfrak{i}:M_{n}(\mathbb{B})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M_{2n}(\mathbb{R}),\quad X=(x_{ij})\mapsto(\mathfrak{i}(x_{ij})).

We have
\mathfrak{i}(X^{\ast})=-J_{n}^{\prime}\text{${}^{t}\!\mathfrak{i}(X)$}J_{n}^{\prime}
where
J_{n}^{\prime}=\mathrm{diag}[J_{1},...,J_{1}]
with
n
copies. This induces the identification

\iota:G(\mathbb{R})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}(2n)(\mathbb{R})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{4n}(\mathbb{R}):gJ_{2n}\text{${}^{t}\!g$}=J_{2n}\},

given by
g\mapsto\mathrm{diag}[1,-J_{n}^{\prime}]\mathfrak{i}(g)\mathrm{diag}[1,J_{n}^{\prime}]
. It is well known that
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)(\mathbb{R})
acts on Siegel upper half plane

\mathfrak{H}_{2n}=\{z=x+iy\in M_{2n}(\mathbb{R})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}:\text{${}^{t}\!z$}=z,y>0\},

by

g=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\times z\mapsto(az+b)(cz+d)^{-1}.

We fix an identification

\iota:\mathcal{H}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{H}_{2n},z=x+iy\mapsto\mathfrak{i}(x)J_{n}^{\prime}+i\mathfrak{i}(y)J_{n}^{\prime},

satisfying
\iota(gz)=\iota(g)\iota(z)
for
g\in G(\mathbb{R}),z\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
. The origin
z_{0}
of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is chosen as
z_{0}:=i\cdot\xi
so that
\iota(z_{0})=i\cdot 1_{2n}
.

2.3.
Definition of modular forms

Fix an integer
N
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
. The group
\tilde{G}
acts on function
f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
by

(f|_{\kappa}\gamma)(z)=\det(\gamma)^{\kappa/2}j(\gamma,z)^{-\kappa}f(\gamma z),\quad\gamma\in\tilde{G},z\in\mathcal{H}.

Definition 2.1
.

A holomorphic function
f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a modular form for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of weight
\kappa
if

f|_{\kappa}\gamma=f\text{ for all }\gamma=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{\gamma}&b_{\gamma}\\
c_{\gamma}&d_{\gamma}\end{array}\right]\subset\Gamma.

Space of such functions is denoted as
M_{\kappa}(\Gamma)
.

Denote
e(z):=\mathrm{exp}(2\pi iz)
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Set
\lambda:=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}
for Case I and
\lambda:=\mathrm{tr}
for Case II and
\lambda(x+iy):=\lambda(x)+i\lambda(y)
. For
f\in M_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
and
\gamma\in G
, there is a Fourier expansion of the form

(f|_{\kappa}\gamma)(z)=\sum_{\tau\in S_{+}}c(\tau,\gamma,f)e(\lambda(\tau z)).

We say that
f
is a cusp form if
c(\tau,\gamma,f)=0
for any
\gamma
unless
\tau\in S^{+}
. The subspace of cusp forms is denoted as
S_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. When
\gamma=1
we omit
\gamma
in the formula and write

f(z)=\sum_{\tau\in\Lambda}c(\tau)e(\lambda(\tau z)),

where more precisely,
\Lambda=\{\tau\in S:\lambda(\tau s)\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for any }s\in S(\mathcal{O})\}
is a lattice in
S_{+}
. This condition is equivalent to saying that
\tau_{ii}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
2\tau_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{\#}
. Here
\mathcal{O}^{\#}
is the dual lattice of
\mathcal{O}
respect to
\lambda
.

Let

\mathbf{d}z=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta(z)^{-2n+1}dz&\text{ Case I, }\\
\delta(z)^{-2n-1}dz&\text{ Case II.}\end{array}\right.

be the invariant differential of
\mathcal{H}
. Here
dz=dxdy
is the usual Euclidean measure. For
f,h\in M_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
the Petersson inner product is defined as

(2.2)

\langle f,g\rangle:=\langle f,h\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}:=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathcal{H}}f(z)\overline{h(z)}\delta(z)^{2k}\mathbf{d}z,

whenever the integral converges. For example, the integral converges when one of
f,h
is a cusp form.

We also need the adelic definition of modular forms. Let
\mathfrak{n}=(N)
be the integral ideal in
\mathcal{O}
generated by
N
. An (adelic) modular form is defined as a holomorphic function
\mathbf{f}:G(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\mathbf{f}(\alpha gkk_{\infty})=j(k_{\infty},z_{0})\mathbf{f}(g),

for
\alpha\in G,k\in K,k_{\infty}\in K_{\infty}
. Here
K
is an open compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbf{h}})
and
K_{\infty}
is the maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{R})
fixing the origin
z_{0}
. Denote
\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}(K)
for the space of such functions and
\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}(K)
be the subspace of cusp forms. We mainly consider the group
\Gamma_{0}(N),\Gamma^{0}(N),K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}),K^{0}(\mathfrak{n})
in this paper. By (
2.1
), two definitions of modular forms are related by

\mathcal{M}_{\kappa}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))\cong M_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{0}(N)),\quad\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}(K_{0}(\mathfrak{n}))\cong S_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{0}(N)),\quad\mathbf{f}\leftrightarrow f

Now let

be compatible doubling maps as in (

4.1.
Preliminaries

We start by recalling some notations about multi-linear algebra used in
[
9
, III.6]
. Set
N=\{1,...,n\}
. For
0\leq p\leq n
, we denote

N_{p}:=\{a=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<...<a_{n}\}\subset N\}

for the set of all subsets of
N
with
p
elements. We view
M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C})=\{(A_{a,b})_{a,b\in N_{p}}\}
as a set of matrices whose entries are labeled by subsets
a,b\in N_{p}
of
N
with
p
elements. Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and for
0\leq h\leq n
, we define
A^{[p]}
and
\mathrm{Ad}^{[p]}A
in
M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C})
by setting their
(a,b)
-entries as

\displaystyle(A^{[p]})_{a,b}

\displaystyle=|A|^{a}_{b}:=\det((A_{i,j})_{i\in a,j\in b}),

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Ad}^{[p]}A)_{a,b}

\displaystyle=\epsilon(a,N\backslash a)\epsilon(b,N\backslash b)|A|^{N\backslash b}_{N\backslash a},

where for two subsets
a^{\prime}=\{a_{1}^{\prime},...,a_{p}^{\prime}\}
,
a^{\prime\prime}=\{a_{1}^{\prime\prime},...,a_{q}^{\prime\prime}\}
of
N
with
a_{1}^{\prime}<...<a_{p}^{\prime}
and
a_{1}^{\prime\prime}<...<a_{q}^{\prime\prime}
we write
\epsilon(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
for the sign of permutation which takes the
(p+q)
-tuple
\{a^{\prime}_{1},...,a_{p}^{\prime},a_{1}^{\prime\prime},...,a_{p}^{\prime\prime}\}
into its natural order. For
0\leq p,q\leq n
, we define the
\sqcap
-multiplication by

\displaystyle\sqcap:M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C})\times M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
q\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\to M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p+q\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C}),

\displaystyle(A\sqcap B)_{a,b}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}p+q\\
p\end{subarray}\right)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=a^{\prime}\cup a^{\prime\prime}\\
b=b^{\prime}\cup b^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\epsilon(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})\epsilon(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime})A_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}B_{a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime}}.

This multiplication is bilinear, commutative and associative whose properties are recorded in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
A,B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
C\in M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C})
,
D\in M_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
q\end{subarray}\right)}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

(1)

A^{[p]}=A\sqcap...\sqcap A
with
p
copies,

(2)

(A+B)^{[p]}=\sum_{a+b=p}\left(\begin{subarray}{c}p\\
a\end{subarray}\right)A^{[a]}\sqcap B^{[b]}
,

(3)

A^{[p+q]}(C\sqcap D)=(A^{[p]}C)\sqcap(A^{[q]}D)
,

(4)

(C\sqcap D)A^{[p+q]}=(CA^{[p]})\sqcap(DA^{[q]})
,

(5)

(\mathrm{Ad}^{[p]}A)A^{[p]}=\det(A)1_{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p\end{subarray}\right)}
.

We remark that for our purpose we actually hope to define
A^{[p]}
,
\mathrm{Ad}^{[p]}A
and
\sqcap
-multiplication for matrices with quaternion entries. The above definitions with
\mathbb{C}
replaced by other ring still make sense but the properties in above lemma no longer hold in general for non-commutative rings. Here we will not try to study these multi-linear algebra in the non-commutative setting. Instead, we will embed
\mathbb{H}
into
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and consider all matrices with entries in
\mathbb{H}
as complex matrices.

Write
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{j}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mathbf{ij}
with
\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=-1
,
\mathbf{ij}=-\mathbf{ji}
for the Hamilton quaternion algebra. Define an embedding

\mathfrak{i}:\mathbb{H}\to M_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad x=a+b\mathbf{i}+c\mathbf{j}+d\mathbf{ij}\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a+bi&c+di\\
-c+di&a-bi\end{array}\right].

Extending this map entries by entries provides an embedding

\mathfrak{i}:M_{n}(\mathbb{H})\to M_{2n}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad x=(x_{ij})\mapsto(\mathfrak{i}(x_{ij})).

One checks that
\mathfrak{i}
induces an isomorphism

\mathfrak{i}:M_{n}(\mathbb{H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C}):=\{x\in M_{2n}(\mathbb{C}):J_{n}^{\prime}x=\overline{x}J_{n}^{\prime}\},

where

with
n

J_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right]
. We thus have identifications

\displaystyle G_{n}(\mathbb{R})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}G_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle:=\{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{4n}(\mathbb{C}):gJ_{2n}g^{\ast}=J_{2n},J_{2n}^{\prime}g=\overline{g}J_{2n}^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle S_{n}(\mathbb{R})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}S_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle:=\{x\in M_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C}):x^{\ast}=x\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{H}_{2n}

\displaystyle:=\{z=x+iy:x,y\in M_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C}),x^{\ast}=x,y^{\ast}=y>0\}

\displaystyle:=\{z\in M_{2n}(\mathbb{C}):J_{n}^{\prime}z=\text{${}^{t}\!z$}J_{n},i(z^{\ast}-z)>0\}.

Here the last map is given by
z=x+iy\mapsto\mathfrak{i}(x)+i\mathfrak{i}(y)
and we set
z^{\ast}:=J_{n}^{\prime}\text{${}^{t}\!z$}J_{n}^{\prime}=x^{\ast}+iy^{\ast}
for

. The group
G_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
\mathfrak{H}_{2n}
by

g=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\times z\mapsto(az+b)(cz+d)^{-1}

which is compatible with the above identifications and the action of
G_{n}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. The doubling embedding
G_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\times G_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\to G_{4n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{H}_{2n}\times\mathfrak{H}_{2n}\to\mathfrak{H}_{4n}
defined as in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) are also compatible with the above identifications. We will construct differential operators on

have similar transformation properties as (
4.1
) with respect to this doubling embedding.

Over
\mathfrak{H}_{4n}
there are basic differential operators
\partial_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathfrak{Z}_{ij}}
. We write elements
\mathfrak{Z}\in\mathcal{H}_{2n}
and these differential operators into matrices

\mathfrak{Z}=(\mathfrak{Z}_{ij})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}z_{1}&z_{2}\\
z_{3}&z_{4}\end{array}\right],\qquad\partial=(\partial_{ij})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{1}&\partial_{2}\\
\partial_{3}&\partial_{4}\end{array}\right],

with relations

J_{n}^{\prime}z_{1}=\text{${}^{t}\!z$}_{1}J_{n}^{\prime},\qquad J_{n}^{\prime}z_{4}=\text{${}^{t}\!z$}_{4}J_{n}^{\prime},\qquad J_{n}z_{3}=\text{${}^{t}\!z$}_{2}J_{n},

Let

\tau_{3}&\tau_{4}\end{array}\right]\in S_{4n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}).

For

4.2.

We now start the construction of differential operators. The construction is similar to
[
2
]
for symplectic groups with

replaced by

For nonnegative integers

, put

(4.2)

\displaystyle\delta(p,a,b)=z_{2}^{[a]}\text{${}^{t}\!\partial_{4}$}^{[a]}\sqcap((1_{n}^{[p]}\sqcap z_{2}^{[b]}\text{${}^{t}\!\partial$}_{2}^{[b]})(\mathrm{Ad}^{[b+p]}\text{${}^{t}\!\partial$}_{1})\text{${}^{t}\!\partial_{3}$}^{[b+p]}).

For nonnegative integer

put

For

the straightforward computations show that

\displaystyle\delta(p,a,b)f

\displaystyle=\left(\tau^{[a+b]}z_{2}^{[a+b]}\text{${}^{t}\!(\partial_{4}^{[a]}\sqcap\partial_{3}^{[b]}\text{${}^{t}\!\tau$}^{-[b]}\partial_{2}^{[b]})\sqcap\text{${}^{t}\!\partial_{3}$}^{[p]}$}\right)f.

\displaystyle\Delta(p,q)f

\displaystyle=\left(\text{${}^{t}\!\partial_{3}$}^{[p]}\sqcap\tau^{[q]}z_{2}^{[q]}\text{${}^{t}\!(\partial_{4}-\partial_{3}\text{${}^{t}\!\tau$}^{-1}\partial_{2})^{[q]}$}\right)f.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
\mathfrak{H}_{4n}
. Let
V
be the operator defined by

(f|V)\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}z_{1}&z_{2}\\

z_{2}&z_{1}\end{array}\right]\right),

then

(4.3)

\Delta(p,q)(f|V)=(\Delta(p,q)f)|V.

Proof.

This is an analogue of the computations in
[
2
, page. 87]
. It suffices to prove for certain test functions

f_{\mathfrak{T}}(\mathfrak{Z})=e^{\lambda(\mathfrak{T}\mathfrak{Z})}=e^{\lambda(\tau_{1}z_{1}+\tau_{2}z_{3}+\tau_{3}z_{2}+\tau_{4}z_{4})},\,\mathfrak{T}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau_{1}&\tau_{2}\\
\tau_{3}&\tau_{4}\end{array}\right],

with
\det(\tau_{1})\neq 0
. We calculate that

(\Delta(p,q)f)|V=\left(\tau_{2}^{[p]}\sqcap\tau_{1}^{[q]}z_{3}^{[q]}(\tau_{4}-\tau_{3}\tau_{1}^{-1}\tau_{2})^{[q]}\right)f.

On the other hand,
(f_{\mathfrak{T}}|V)(\mathfrak{Z})=e^{\lambda(\tau_{1}z_{4}+\tau_{2}z_{2}+\tau_{3}z_{3}+\tau_{4}z_{4})}
, so

\Delta(p,q)(f|V)=\left(\tau_{3}^{[p]}\sqcap\tau_{4}^{[q]}z_{2}^{[q]}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\tau_{4}^{-1}\tau_{3})^{[q]}\right)f.

The lemma then follows from properties of

and the fact

if

.
∎

Keep considering

as a test function with

Decompose

\displaystyle h(z_{4})

\displaystyle=\det(z_{4})^{2n-\kappa}e^{\lambda((\tau_{3}\tau_{1}^{-1}\tau_{2}-\tau_{4})z_{4}^{-1})}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

.

Let

Differential operators

We now treat Case I and II together. For any

and summarize our construction of differential operators in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.6
.

There is a differential operator
\mathfrak{D}_{\alpha}
on
\mathcal{H}_{2n}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that for any integer
\kappa>2n
and any holomorphic function

and

we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{\kappa}(f|V)

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{D}_{\kappa}f)|V,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{\kappa}(f|_{\kappa}1\times\gamma)

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{D}_{\kappa}f)|_{\kappa+1}1\times\gamma,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{\kappa}(f|_{\kappa}\gamma\times 1)

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{D}_{\kappa}f)|_{\kappa+1}\gamma\times 1.

Proof.

, denote

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}:|x|_{p}\leq 1\}.

A
p
-adic distribution on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
is a continuous linear functional
\mu:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\mathbb{C}_{p})\to\mathbb{C}_{p}
. To construct a
p
-adic distribution it is enough to define
\mu
on all sets of form
a+L\mathbb{Z}_{p}
satisfying

\mu(a+L\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}\mu(a+bL+pL\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

For such
\mu
and a locally constant function
f
, we denote
\mu(f)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}fd\mu
. A
p
-adic distribution is called a
p
-adic measure if it is bounded.

We will follow the strategy of constructing
p
-adic measures in
[
8
]
. That is we first define a
p
-adic distribution interpolate the special
L
-values. Then check the distribution satisfying the Kummer congruences so the
p
-adic measure exist. Finally, since the
p
-adic measure is uniquely determined by its value at all primitive Dirichlet character of
p
-power conductor, we conclude that the
p
-adic distribution we constructed is the desired
p
-adic measure. We recall the criterion of Kummer congruences as follow.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\{f_{i}\}\subset C(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
be a dense system of continuous functions,
\{a_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
a system of elements. Then the existence of p-adic measure
\mu:C(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}f_{i}d\mu=a_{i}

is equivalent to the following Kummer congruences: for an arbitrary choice of elements

almost all of which vanish

\sum_{i}b_{i}f_{i}(y)\in p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}\text{ for all }y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\Longrightarrow\sum_{i}b_{i}a_{i}\in p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}.

Recall that
\epsilon=-1
in Case I and
\epsilon=1
in Case II. For a function
\mathfrak{g}(z,w)
on

let

\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{g})=\frac{\left\langle\left\langle\mathfrak{g}(z,-\overline{w}),f_{0}\left|{}_{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\epsilon\\
1&0\end{array}\right]\right.\right\rangle^{w}_{\Gamma^{0}(N^{2}p)},f_{0}\left|{}_{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&N^{2}p\end{array}\right]\right.\right\rangle^{z}_{\Gamma^{0}(N^{2}p)}}{\langle f_{0},f_{0}\rangle^{2}_{\Gamma_{0}(N^{2}p)}}.

In particular, taking

Appendix A
A Transformation Property of Differential Operators

In the appendix, we prove a transformation property of differential operators for hermitian matrices in the quaternionic case which will be used in Lemma
4.4
. The proofs here are almost same as the computations in
[
3
]
.

We keep our notations as in Section
4
. Recall that the space of quaternionic hermitian matrices

S_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\{y\in M_{n}(\mathbb{H}):y^{\ast}=y\}

is identified with a subspace of complex hermitian matrices

S_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})=\{y\in M_{2n}(\mathbb{C}):y^{\ast}=y,J_{n}^{\prime}y=\overline{y}J_{n}^{\prime}\}.

Denote
\partial_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{ij}}
for basic differential operators and write them in a matrix
\partial=(\partial_{ij})
. For a (complex) hermitian matrix
\tau\in M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
and
0\leq p\leq 2n
we have

\partial^{[p]}e^{\mathrm{tr}(\tau y)}=(\text{${}^{t}\!\tau$}-J_{n}^{\prime}\tau J_{n}^{\prime})^{[p]}e^{\mathrm{tr}(\tau y)},\qquad y\in S_{2n}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}).

Our computations will based on this basic identity and this cause the difference with
[
3
]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
y\in S^{\prime}_{2n}(\mathbb{R})
is positive definite. The for
0\leq q\leq 2n
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
we have

\partial^{[q]}\det(y)^{s}=C_{q}(s)\det(y)^{s}\cdot\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{-[q]},

where

C_{q}(s):=s\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)...\left(s+\frac{q-1}{2}\right).

Proof.

The proof is same as
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
. It suffices to prove for
s=-2n
. Starting with
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]
,

\det(y)^{-2n}=\int_{M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})}e^{-\pi\mathrm{tr}(x^{\ast}yx)}dx,

and applying
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
we have

\displaystyle\partial^{[q]}\det(y)^{-2n}

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})}\partial^{[q]}e^{-\pi\mathrm{tr}(x^{\ast}yx)}dx

\displaystyle=(-\pi)^{q}\int_{M_{2n}(\mathbb{C})}(\text{${}^{t}\!(xx^{\ast})$}-J_{n}^{\prime}xx^{\ast}J_{n}^{\prime})^{[q]}e^{-\pi\mathrm{tr}(x^{\ast}yx)}dx

\displaystyle=(-\pi)^{q}\sum_{a+b=q}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
a\end{array}\right)\frac{a!}{\pi^{a}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2n\\
a\end{array}\right)\frac{b!}{\pi^{b}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2n\\
b\end{array}\right)\det(y)^{-2n}(\text{${}^{t}\!y$}-J_{n}^{\prime}yJ_{n})^{[-q]}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q}q!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}4n\\
q\end{array}\right)\det(y)^{-2n}\cdot 2^{-q}\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{-[q]}

\displaystyle=(-2n)(-2n+\frac{1}{2})...(-2n+\frac{q-1}{2}).

as desired.
∎

We now prove the transformation property under
y\mapsto y^{-1}
.

Proposition A.2
.

For
0\leq q\leq 2n
, we consider the operator
D:=\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{[q]}\partial^{[q]}
and the operator
\widehat{D}
defined by
\widehat{D}(f):=D(\widehat{f})(Y^{-1})
with
\widehat{f}(y):=f(y^{-1})
. Then

\widehat{D}=(-1)^{q}\det(y)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}D^{\ast}\det(y)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}}

with
D^{\ast}=\text{${}^{t}\!($}y^{[q]}\cdot\text{${}^{t}\!\partial$}^{[q]})
.

Proof.

The proof is same as
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
. Denote

L:=\widehat{D}(f)(y),\qquad R:=(-1)^{q}\det(y)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}y^{[q]}\cdot\text{${}^{t}\!\partial$}^{[q]}\det(y)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}}f.

It suffices to prove
L=\text{${}^{t}\!R$}
for
f(y):=\det(y)^{2n}e^{\pi\mathrm{tr}(\tau y)}
with
\tau
(complex) hermitian and positive definite.

We calculate that

R=(-1)^{q}f(y)y^{[q]}\sum_{a+b=q}\pi^{b}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
a\end{array}\right)C_{q}(2n-\frac{q-1}{2})y^{-[a]}\sqcap(\text{${}^{t}\!\tau$}-J_{n}^{\prime}\tau J_{n}^{\prime})^{[b]}.

The same computations in
[
3
, Theorem 3.6]
for
L
givens

\partial^{[q]}(\widehat{f})(y^{-1})=(-\pi)^{q}2^{-q}f(y)C(q)\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{[q]}\cdot\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{-[a]}\sqcap(\text{${}^{t}\!\tau$}-J_{n}^{\prime}\tau J_{n}^{\prime})^{[b]}

where the factor

\displaystyle C(q)

\displaystyle=2^{-q}\sum_{q_{1}+q_{2}=q}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
q_{1}\end{array}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}+b_{1}=q_{1}\\
a_{2}+b_{2}=q_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{1}!}{\pi^{a_{1}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{1}\\
a_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2n-b_{1}\\
a_{1}\end{array}\right)\frac{a_{2}!}{\pi^{a_{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2n-b_{2}\\
a_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a+b=q}\pi^{-a}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
a\end{array}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}+a_{2}=a\\
b_{1}+b_{2}=b\end{subarray}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
a_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b\\
b_{1}\end{array}\right)D_{a_{1}}(-2n+b_{1})D_{a_{2}}(-2n+b_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a+b=q}\pi^{-a}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q\\
a\end{array}\right)2^{b}D_{a}(-4n+b-1).

Here we are denoting

as in
[
3
]
. Comparing

and note that

we obtain

as desired.
∎

As a corollary we have (

\partial^{[q]}(\widehat{f})=(-1)^{q}\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{-[q]}\det(y)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(\partial^{[q]}(f\cdot\det(y)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}})\right)(y^{-1})\cdot\text{${}^{t}\!y$}^{-[q]}.

Extending to the whole quaternionic upper half plane
\mathcal{H}_{n}

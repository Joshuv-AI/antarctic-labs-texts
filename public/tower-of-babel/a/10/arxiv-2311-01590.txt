Traveling wave solutions to the free boundary incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with Navier boundary conditions Thanks: I. Tice was supported by an NSF Grant (DMS #2204912).
Junichi Koganemaru, Ian Tice

Abstract
. In this paper we study traveling wave solutions to the free boundary incompressible Navier-Stokes system with generalized Navier-slip conditions. The fluid is assumed to occupy a horizontally infinite strip-like domain that is bounded below by a flat rigid surface and above by a moving surface. We assume that the fluid is acted upon by a bulk force and a surface stress that are stationary in a coordinate system moving parallel to the fluid bottom, and a uniform gravitational force that is perpendicular to the flat rigid surface. We construct our solutions via an implicit function argument, and show that as the slip parameter shrinks to zero, the Navier-slip solutions converge to solutions to the no-slip problem obtained previously.

1.
Introduction

The construction of traveling wave solutions to the inviscid, incompressible equations of fluid dynamics is a classical subject in mathematics with a rich history. In comparison, progress on the corresponding viscous problems began quite recently: the series of papers
[
18
,
19
,
29
,
30
]
developed a well-posedness theory for the free surface Navier-Stokes equations modeling incompressible fluids in a horizontally infinite strip-like domain of finite depth, subject to sources of applied force and stress. In each of these papers, the fluid is assumed to obey the standard no-slip boundary condition at its lower boundary with a flat, rigid floor. The purpose of this paper is to continue the study of this type of problem by incorporating the more general Navier-slip condition, which allows the fluid to slip along the bottom boundary, and show that a generic well-posedness theory persists. The slip boundary condition, first proposed by Navier
[
23
]
in 1832, asserts that the tangential fluid velocity at the fluid bottom is proportional to the tangential stress experienced by the fluid. The ratio of the tangential stress to the tangential fluid velocity is referred to as the slip parameter. We will prove that not only are traveling wave solutions also generic under the Navier-slip conditions, but that one recovers the no-slip solutions in the limit as the characteristic slip parameter goes to zero.

1.1.
Problem formulation

In this paper we consider a single layer of viscous, incompressible fluid evolving in a horizontally infinite strip-like domain, bounded below by a flat, rigid surface and above by a free moving surface that can be described by the graph of a continuous function, in dimensions
n\geqslant 2
. Even though the only physically relevant cases are when
n=2,3
, the analysis in this paper can be applied more generally to higher dimensions as well. Since our primary interest is the construction of traveling wave solutions, we will skip the somewhat lengthy discussion of the formulation of the fully dynamic problem and the subsequent reformulation under a traveling wave ansatz and jump straight to the traveling wave problem; these omitted details can be found in the introduction of
[
18
]
. The equations for a traveling wave solution to the free boundary Navier-Stokes system are

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge S(q,v)-\gamma e_{1}\cdot\nabla v+v\cdot\nabla v+\mathfrak{g}(\nabla^{\prime}\eta,0)=\mathfrak{f},&\text{in}\;\Omega_{b+\eta}\\
\diverge v=0,&\text{in}\;\Omega_{b+\eta}\\
-\gamma\partial_{1}\eta+\nabla^{\prime}\eta\cdot v^{\prime}=v_{n},&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b+\eta}\\
S(q,v)\mathcal{N}=(-\sigma\mathcal{H}(\eta)I+\mathcal{T})\mathcal{N},&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b+\eta}\\
-\alpha(S(q,v)e_{n})^{\prime}=[A(v)]^{\prime},&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
v_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.\end{cases}

We now explain all the terms appearing in the system (
1.1
). The fluid occupies the unknown domain
\Omega_{b+\eta}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 0<x_{n}<b+\eta(x^{\prime})\}
, where
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to(-b,\infty)
is the unknown free surface function and
b>0
is the equilibrium depth of the fluid. The graph
\Sigma_{b+\eta}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x_{n}=b+\eta(x^{\prime})\}
is the unknown upper boundary of the fluid, while the trivial graph
\Sigma_{0}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x_{n}=0\}
denotes its fixed, rigid lower boundary. See Figure
1
for a graphical depiction of the fluid domain.

Figure 1.
A sample portion of the unknown fluid domain in dimension
n=3

The fluid’s velocity field and pressure are denoted here by
v:\Omega_{b+\eta}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
q:\Omega_{b+\eta}\to\mathbb{R}
, and together they determine the viscous stress tensor

(1.2)

S(q,v)=qI_{n\times n}-\mu\mathbb{D}v=qI_{n\times n}-\mu(\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

with the viscosity coefficient
\mu>0
. We emphasize, though, that the pressure
q
is not really the fundamental fluid mechanical pressure, but rather a “good” pressure unknown obtained by subtracting off a variant of the hydrostatic pressure (see
[
18
]
for details). The parameter
\mathfrak{g}>0
is the strength of the gravitational field, and the term
\mathfrak{g}(\nabla^{\prime}\eta,0)
corresponds to the gravitational force the fluid experiences, after the aforementioned reformulation of the pressure unknown. Without loss of generality, we henceforth assume the convenient normalization
\mu=\mathfrak{g}=1
.

The parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is the traveling wave speed, and its specific appearance in (
1.1
) corresponds to solutions to the dynamic problem that are stationary in a coordinate frame moving with velocity
\gamma e_{1}
. The applied bulk force
\mathfrak{f}:\Omega_{b+\eta}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the applied surface stress
\mathcal{T}:\Sigma_{b+\eta}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\sym}
are given data that are responsible for inducing the motion of the fluid. The term
\mathcal{N}=(-\nabla^{\prime}\eta,1)
denotes the non-unit normal vector field to
\Sigma_{b+\eta}
, while the term
-\sigma\mathcal{H}(\zeta)
corresponds to surface tension on
\Sigma_{b+\eta}
, with
\sigma>0
denoting the coefficient of surface tension and
\mathcal{H}(\eta)=\diverge^{\prime}(\nabla^{\prime}\eta/\sqrt{1+\left|\nabla^{\prime}\eta\right|^{2}})
denoting the mean-curvature operator.

The system (
1.1
) is obtained from the incompressible Navier-Stokes system. The first two equations in (
1.1
) correspond to the balance of momentum and conservation of mass. The third equation is the kinematic boundary condition describing the evolution of the free surface. The fourth equation is called the dynamic boundary condition, as it encodes the balance of forces on the free surface. The fifth and sixth equations constitute a general nonlinear version of the Navier-slip condition, which we now elaborate on. The sixth equation is called the no-penetration condition, and it requires that the fluid is not able to detach from or pass through
\Sigma_{0}
. Unlike in the case of the no-slip boundary condition, the fluid is allowed to have a nontrivial tangential component on
\Sigma_{0}
, which is described as “slip.” However, slip comes at a price: it generates a tangential stress on the fluid that opposes the motion, which one should think of as being analogous to the way that air resistance is modeled in standard Newtonian point-particle mechanics. The precise form we impose in the fifth equation is (using the sixth)

(1.3)

[A(v)]^{\prime}=-\alpha(S(q,v)e_{n})^{\prime}=-\alpha(qe_{n}-\mathbb{D}ve_{n})^{\prime}=\alpha(\mathbb{D}ve_{n})^{\prime}=\alpha(\nabla^{\prime}v_{n}+\partial_{n}v^{\prime})=\alpha\partial_{n}v^{\prime}

for a given smooth “slip parameter”
\alpha>0
and “slip function”
A:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying (for technical reasons we will discuss later)

(1.4)

\displaystyle A(0)=0,\;A(w)\cdot w>0\text{ for }w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\text{ and }A(w)\cdot w\geqslant\theta\left|w\right|^{2}\text{ for }w\in B(0,\delta)\setminus\{0\},

where
\delta,\theta>0
are fixed constants. One should then think of (
1.3
) as a one-parameter family (indexed by
\alpha
) of nonlinear Robin boundary conditions with the extreme case
\alpha=0
recovering the no-slip condition since then (
1.3
) requires
0=A(v)^{\prime}
on
\Sigma_{0}
, which together with the condition
v_{n}=0
and (
1.4
) implies that
v=0
on
\Sigma_{0}
. The most common form of the Navier-slip condition in the literature is in a linear form, in which
A
is a linear map (often just the identity); we have included the nonlinear form for the sake of generality, and our analysis certainly handles the standard linear case.

1.2.
Previous work

The Navier-slip condition was first proposed by Navier
[
23
]
and it is now used to model a wide range of physical phenomena, including liquid-solid contact lines (we refer to Dussan’s survey
[
8
]
) and flows through irregular surfaces (see, for instance, the work of Gérard-Varet and Masmoudi
[
14
]
). It also plays a crucial role in the analysis of collisions in fluid-solid systems: see, for example,
[
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
28
]
. The slip phenomenon has also been empirically observed in recent experiments; we refer to the survey of Neto et al.
[
24
]
and the references therein for a review of these results.

The well-posedness of the Navier-Stokes system with Navier-slip boundary conditions has been investigated by several authors. Solonnikov-Ščadilov
[
27
]
studied the 3D linearized stationary Navier-Stokes system and proved the existence of weak solutions as well as their regularity. Beirão da Veiga
[
4
]
studied the stationary problem on the half space and proved strong regularity up to the boundary. Ferreira
[
10
]
studied the inhomogeneous system on bounded space-time domains and proved the existence of weak solutions. Masmoudi-Rousset
[
22
]
proved uniform in time bounds with respect to the viscosity parameter. Kelliher
[
17
]
studied the 2D equations on bounded domains and proved that 2D Navier-slip solutions with sufficiently smooth initial velocities converge to the no-slip solutions as the slip parameter goes to zero. Murata-Shibata
[
26
]
studied the compressible variant with slip boundary conditions on bounded domains and proved a global in time unique existence theorem for small data.

The dynamical stability of Navier-slip solutions has also been studied by various authors. Li-Pan-Zhang
[
20
]
studied the stability of steady state solutions to the 3D incompressible problem on bounded domains. Ding-Lin
[
7
]
studied the stability of the Couette flow in 2D, and Li-Zhang
[
21
]
studied the stability of Couette flow in 3D, and separately they proved that the Couette flow is asymptotically stable under small perturbations with various conditions on the slip parameter and viscosity.

The well-posedness of the traveling wave problem for the free boundary Navier-Stokes system first appeared in the recent work of Leoni-Tice
[
19
]
. This work was extended to periodic and tilted fluid configurations by Koganemaru-Tice
[
18
]
. Stevenson-Tice
[
29
]
studied multi-later configurations
[
29
]
, the vanishing wave speed limit
[
30
]
, and the compressible traveling wave problem
[
31
]
. Similar well-posedness result for the traveling wave formulation of the Muskat problem were obtained by Nguyen-Tice
[
25
]
.

1.3.
Reformulation in a fixed domain

The fluid domain
\Omega_{b+\eta}
is one of the unknowns in (
1.1
), so it is convenient to recast the system in a fixed domain. We choose the equilibrium domain
\Omega:=\Omega_{b}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,b)
for this, and write
\Sigma_{b}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x_{n}=b\}
for the flat upper boundary.
The reformulation is achieved by introducing the flattening map
\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, associated to any
\eta\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\eta>-b
, defined via

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n}+\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi(x_{n})),

where
\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
is some fixed function that it is a monotone and satisfies
\varphi=0
on
(-\infty,b/4]
and
\varphi=1
on
[3b/4,\infty)
. By construction, we have that
\mathfrak{F}(\overline{\Omega_{b}})=\overline{\Omega_{b+\eta}}
,
\mathfrak{F}(\Sigma_{b})=\Sigma_{b+\eta}
, and
\mathfrak{F}=I
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(-\infty,b/4)
, which in particular means that
\mathfrak{F}
is the identity on
\Sigma_{0}
. Moreover, it’s easy to see that if
\left\|\eta\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}}
is sufficiently small then
\mathfrak{F}
is a diffeomorphism.

We compute

(1.6)

\displaystyle\nabla\mathfrak{F}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}&0_{(n-1)\times 1}\\
\nabla^{\prime}\eta(x^{\prime})^{T}\varphi(x_{n})&1+\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{n})\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }(\nabla\mathfrak{F})^{-\intercal}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}&-\frac{\nabla^{\prime}\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi(x_{n})}{1+\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{n})}\\
0_{1\times(n-1)}&\frac{1}{1+\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{n})}.\end{pmatrix}.

We then define
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
via
\mathcal{A}(x)=(\nabla\mathfrak{F})^{-\intercal}(x)
and
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
via

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(x)=\det\nabla\mathfrak{F}(x)=1+\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{n})\text{ and }\mathcal{K}(x)=\frac{1}{J(x)}=\frac{1}{1+\eta(x^{\prime})\varphi^{\prime}(x_{n})}.

Then we define the
\mathcal{A}
-dependent differential operators:
(\nabla_{\mathcal{A}}f)_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{ij}\partial_{j}f,

(X\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{A}}u)_{i}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}X_{j}\mathcal{A}_{jk}\partial_{k}u_{i}
,
\diverge_{\mathcal{A}}X=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{ij}\partial_{j}X_{i}
,
(\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}u)_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{ik}\partial_{k}u_{j}+\mathcal{A}_{jk}\partial_{k}u_{i}
,
S_{\mathcal{A}}(p,u)=pI-\mu\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}u
,
\diverge_{\mathcal{A}}S_{\mathcal{A}}(p,u)=\nabla_{\mathcal{A}}p-\mu\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}u-\mu\nabla_{\mathcal{A}}\diverge_{\mathcal{A}}u
,
(\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}u)_{i}=\sum_{j,k,m=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{j,k}\partial_{k}(\mathcal{A}_{jm}\partial_{m}u_{i})
.

Next, we introduce the new unknowns
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
p:\Omega\to\mathbb{R},
and
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
via
u=v\circ\mathfrak{F}
,
p=q\circ\mathfrak{F}
, and
f=\mathfrak{f}\circ\mathfrak{F}
. This yields the reformulation of (
1.1
):

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge_{\mathcal{A}}S_{\mathcal{A}}(p,u)-\gamma e_{1}\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{A}}u+u\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{A}}u+(\nabla^{\prime}\eta,0)=\mathfrak{f}\circ\mathfrak{F},&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\diverge_{\mathcal{A}}u=0,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
-\gamma\partial_{1}\eta-u\cdot\mathcal{N}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
S_{\mathcal{A}}(p,u)\mathcal{N}=\left[-\sigma\mathcal{H}(\eta)I+\mathcal{T}\circ\mathfrak{F}\right]\mathcal{N},&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
\alpha[S_{\mathcal{A}}(p,u)\nu]^{\prime}=[A(u)]^{\prime},&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
u_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.\end{cases}

We now turn to a brief discussion of our strategy for proving this theorem. There are essentially two key difficulties that must be dealt with. The first comes from the fact that we want to fix the stress-force tuple
(\mathcal{T},T,\mathfrak{f},f)
and produce a family of solutions
(u_{\alpha},p_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})
to (
1.10
), parameterized by
\alpha\in(0,1)
. This is certainly plausible within the context of Theorem
1.1
, but there is nothing within the statement of that result that can guarantee that the tuple remains within the open set of data that yields solutions. Indeed, in principle the open set could shrink dramatically as
\alpha\to 0
, making it impossible to employ a fixed data tuple in the limiting argument. Provided this problem can be dispatched, we then arrive at the second: we need to establish
\alpha
-independent estimates for the solutions
(u_{\alpha},p_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})
in order to invoke weak compactness results.

We resolve both of these problems by combining a careful analysis of the linearization of (
1.10
) with some nonlinear tricks. In the linear analysis we achieve
\alpha
-independent estimates by focusing on the linearization (
2.1
) with
l=0
. This is only reasonable insofar as we can encode
l=0
in the nonlinear problem, which means that the fifth equation in (
1.10
) must already be linear. To enforce this we require that
A
itself is linear and that the matrix
\mathcal{A}
is the identity in a neighborhood of
\Sigma_{0}
. The latter condition is the motivation for the introduction of the cutoff
\varphi
in the definition of the flattening map
\mathfrak{F}
; unfortunately, its presence here requires us to retool many previously established results.

In order to enforce the linear slip condition in an implicit function theorem argument we then need to build this condition into the domain of the nonlinear map. For any fixed value of
\alpha
this is easy, but we need to do this for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which means the linear subspace obeying the
\alpha
-slip condition changes as a function of
\alpha
. This then requires us to develop a special version of the implicit function theorem capable of handling maps
f_{\alpha}:X\times Y_{\alpha}\to Z
defined over a one-parameter family of Banach spaces. We prove this variant of the implicit function theorem in Appendix
A.3
and demonstrate that with uniform control over the derivatives of the nonlinear solution operator with respect to the parameter
\alpha
, we may also deduce uniform control over the norms of solutions obtained via the parameter-dependent implicit function theorem. This is a stronger mandate than that from the standard implicit function theorem, so we must then verify these conditions in our linear analysis. This turns out to be doable but somewhat tricky because it requires determining the asymptotics of an implicit Fourier multiplier as a function of
\alpha
.

1.5.
Notational conventions and outline of the article

We will frequently use the “horizontal” Fourier transform for functions on

\hat{f}(\xi,x_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})e^{-2\pi ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi}\;dx^{\prime}

, a non-empty open set

, and a finite-dimensional inner product space
V
, we write
H^{k}(U;V)
for the usual
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space; when
U=\mathbb{R}^{d}
we extend to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};V)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
in the usual way. For
s\geqslant 0
and a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};V)
, we write
f\in\dot{H}^{-1}
to mean that the
\dot{H}^{-1}
-seminorm of
f
defined via
[f]_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\xi\right|^{-2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi
is finite. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a real Banach space
V
, and a nonempty open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geqslant 1
, we define the space
C^{k}_{b}(U;V)
of
k-
times continuous differentiable maps from
U\to V
with all derivatives bounded. We also define the space
C_{0}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d};V)\subset C^{k}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};V)
to be the closed subspace of
f
such that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)=0
for all
\left|\alpha\right|\leqslant k
.

2.
The
\gamma
-Stokes equations with stress boundary conditions

In this section our goal is to study the solvability of the linear problem

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u=f,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\diverge u=g,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
S(p,u)e_{n}=k,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
\left[\alpha S(p,u)e_{n}+\beta u\right]^{\prime}=l,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
u_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\end{cases}

with a given data tuple
(f,g,k,l)
and parameters
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Due to techniques we will employ later, it will be convenient to have access to a well-posedness theory over both the reals and the complex numbers. As such, throughout this section and the next we let
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
denote either field, and we develop a well-posedness theory generically over
\mathbb{F}
. Recall that when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
and
X
is a complex Hilbert space, the Riesz map is a linear isomorphism from
X
to
X^{\overline{*}}
, where the latter denotes the anti-linear functionals on
X
. We will use this notation a few times throughout this section.

We begin our analysis by fixing some notation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be defined as per Section
1.1
. For
\mathbb{R}\ni s>1/2
, we define the spaces
{}_{\text{tan}}H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})=\{u\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}):u_{n}\rvert_{\Sigma_{0}}=0\}
and
{}_{\text{tan}}H_{\sigma}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})=\{u\in{}_{\text{tan}}H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}):\diverge u=0\}
.
We equip these spaces with the standard
H^{s}
-norm, and note that since these spaces are closed subspaces of the Hilbert space
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
, they inherit the natural Hilbert structure. If in addition
\mathbb{R}\ni s>3/2
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the space
{}_{\alpha-\text{tan}}H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})=\{u\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}):u_{n}\rvert_{\Sigma_{0}}=0,[-\alpha\mathbb{D}ue_{n}+\beta u]^{\prime}\rvert_{\Sigma_{0}}=0\}
,
which is a closed subspace of
{}_{\text{tan}}H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
and thus inherits the natural Hilbert structure from
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
as well.

In order to produce weak solutions to the system (
2.1
) we will first need some functional analytic tools in
{}_{\text{tan}}H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
. We begin with a version of Korn’s inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have that
\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|\mathbb{D}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma_{0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{0})}
for
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Consequently,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}=\sqrt{\left\|\mathbb{D}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma_{0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}},

generates the standard
H^{1}
topology on the space
{}_{\text{tan}}H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
.

Proof.

The second assertion follows from the first bound and standard trace theory. To prove the first, it suffices to prove the result when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, as the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
can then be recovered by applying the real result to the real and imaginary parts of
u
. Assume
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
.

Consider a rectangle
Q=\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:\left|x^{\prime}\right|_{\infty}<1\}\times(0,b)
. The standard Korn inequality in Lipschitz domains (see, for instance, Lemma IV.7.6 in
[
5
]
) shows that
\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}(Q)}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\left\|\mathbb{D}u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}
. We claim that

(2.3)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\lesssim\left\|\mathbb{D}u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\left\|\Tr_{\partial Q_{0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial Q_{0})},

where
\partial Q_{0}=\partial Q\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
. Indeed, if not then we can produce a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset H^{1}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}=1
,
\left\|\mathbb{D}u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}<1/k
, and
\left\|\Tr_{\partial Q_{0}}u_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial Q_{0})}<1/k
. Then, by compactness, there exists
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}=1
such that up to passing to a subsequence,
\mathbb{D}u_{k}\to\mathbb{D}u=0
and
\Tr_{\partial Q_{0}}u_{k}\to\Tr_{\partial Q_{0}}u=0
as
k\to\infty
. Since
\mathbb{D}u=0
, we then have that
u(x)=a+Bx
for a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
B
skew-symmetric, but since
\Tr_{\partial Q_{0}}u=0
we deduce that
a=0
and
B=0
. Thus
u=0
, and we contradict the identity
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q)}=1
, proving the claim.

With (
2.3
) in hand, we write
\Omega
as a countable almost disjoint union (null intersections along the boundary) of rectangles of the form
Q_{l}=\{x\in\Omega:\left|x^{\prime}-l\right|_{\infty}\leqslant 1\}
for
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. Since each
Q_{\ell}
is a translation of the rectangle
Q
from above, (
2.3
) allows us to bound

(2.4)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{l})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}\left(\left\|\mathbb{D}u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{l})}^{2}+\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma_{0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}((\partial Q_{\ell})_{0})}^{2}\right)=\left\|\mathbb{D}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma_{0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{0})}^{2}.

This is the desired bound.
∎

The next result provides a right inverse to the divergence operator.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a linear and continuous mapping
\Pi:L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to{}_{0}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
such that
\diverge\Pi g=g
for all
g\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
. In particular, for all
g\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
we have
\left\|\Pi g\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma 2.1 in
[
19
]
and Lemma 2.2 in
[
29
]
.
∎

We next prove a Helmholtz decomposition of
{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Define the bounded linear operator
Q:L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{F}^{n})
via

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{v}=\left(Qp,v\right)_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})}\;\text{for all}\;v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}).

Then
Q
has closed range, and
(\Range Q)^{\perp}={}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Consequently, we have the orthogonal decomposition

(2.6)

\displaystyle{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})={}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\oplus_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\Range Q.

Proof.

We first show that
Q
has closed range. To do so, we first note that for all
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
we have the bound
\left\|Qp\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{n,p}\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. On the other hand, by Lemma
2.3
there exists a
v_{0}\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
such that
\diverge\overline{v_{0}}=p
and
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}}
. Therefore by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{v_{0}}=\left(Qp,v_{0}\right)_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}\leqslant\left\|Qp\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|Qp\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This implies that
\left\|p\right\|\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|Qp\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}
, thus we have the equivalence of norms
\left\|Qp\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\asymp\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}}
for all
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
. This immediately implies that
Q
has closed range, and so
{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})=\Range Q\oplus_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\left(\Range Q\right)^{\perp}
. It remains to show that
\left(\Range Q\right)^{\perp}={}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. If
v\in\left(\Range Q\right)^{\perp}
, then
\left(Qp,v\right)_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=(p,\diverge v)_{L^{2}}=0
for all
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
. Thus we must have
\diverge\overline{v}=0

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e., which implies that
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. If
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
, then
\left(Qp,v\right)=\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{v}=0
for any
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
, which implies that
v\in\left(\Range Q\right)^{\perp}
. This shows that
(\Range Q)^{\perp}={}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega,\mathbb{F}^{n})
as desired. Since the range of
Q
is closed, the Helmholtz decomposition (
2.6
) follows.
∎

This gives us an immediate corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}\in\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\right)^{\overline{*}}
be such that
\langle\Lambda_{1},v\rangle=0
for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Then there exists unique
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
such that
\langle\Lambda_{1},v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{v}
for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Moreover, we have the estimate
\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|\Lambda_{1}\right\|_{\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}\right)^{\overline{*}}}
.

Proof.

First we suppose that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and let
\Lambda\in\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{*}
be such that it vanishes on solenoidal fields. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists
w\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\langle\Lambda,v\rangle=\left(w,v\right)_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}
for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\left\|w\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}\right)^{\overline{*}}}
. Then for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
(w,v)_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\langle\Lambda,v\rangle=0
, thus
w\in\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\perp}
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
w\in\Range Q
, therefore there exists a
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
such that
Qp=w
. So we have
\langle\Lambda,v\rangle=\left(Qp,v\right)_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{v}
for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with the estimate

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|Qp\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\left\|w\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}\right)^{\overline{*}}}.

Moreover,
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is unique since
Q
is surjective.

Now we consider the case when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. If we have an antilinear functional
\Lambda\in\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})\right)^{\overline{*}}
vanishing on solenoidal fields, we can define the
\mathbb{R}
-linear functionals
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Re}},\Lambda_{\operatorname{Im}}\in\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{\overline{*}}
via
\langle\Lambda_{\operatorname{Re}},v\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\langle F,v\rangle
and
\langle\Lambda_{\operatorname{Im}},v\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\langle F,iv\rangle
for any
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Note that if
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\langle\Lambda,v\rangle=0
by assumption, so it follows that
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Re}},\Lambda_{\operatorname{Im}}
vanishes on real-valued solenoidal fields. Thus when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, there exist unique
q,r\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
such that for all
v,w\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}[\langle\Lambda,v+iw\rangle]

\displaystyle=\langle\Lambda_{\operatorname{Re}},v\rangle+\langle\Lambda_{\operatorname{Im}},w\rangle=\int_{\Omega}q\diverge v+r\diverge w=\operatorname{Re}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(q+ir\right)\overline{\diverge\left(v+iw\right)}\right].

Now define
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
via
p=q+ir
, and for any
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})
we write it as
u=v+iw
. Then

(2.10)

\langle\Lambda,u\rangle=\operatorname{Re}[\langle\Lambda,v+iw\rangle]+i\operatorname{Im}[\langle\Lambda,v+iw\rangle]=\operatorname{Re}[\langle\Lambda,v+iw\rangle]+i\operatorname{Re}[-i\langle\Lambda,v+iw\rangle]\\
=\operatorname{Re}[\langle\Lambda,v+iw\rangle]+i\operatorname{Re}[\langle\Lambda,-w+iv\rangle]=\operatorname{Re}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(q+ir\right)\overline{\diverge\left(v+iw\right)}\right]+i\operatorname{Re}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(q+ir\right)\overline{\diverge\left(-w+iv\right)}\right]\\
=\operatorname{Re}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(q+ir\right)\overline{\diverge\left(v+iw\right)}\right]+i\operatorname{Im}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(q+ir\right)\overline{\diverge\left(v+iw\right)}\right]=\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{u}.

∎

With these preliminary results in hand, we now turn to the question of weak solvability of (
2.1
). We first set some notation.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\ni s\geqslant 0
,
\mathbb{R}\ni\alpha>0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be positive definite. We define the map
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:{}_{\Tan}H^{s+3/2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}
via

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p),v\right\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu}{2}\mathbb{D}u:\mathbb{D}\overline{v}-p\diverge\overline{v}-\gamma\partial_{1}u\cdot\overline{v}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\beta u\cdot\overline{v}.

Given
F\in({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}
and
g\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
, we say that
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
and
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
are weak solutions to (
2.1
) if
\diverge u=g
and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p),v\right\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\left\langle F,v\right\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}.

This notation allows us to efficiently state our weak well-posedness result.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\ni\alpha>0
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be positive definite. Define
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
via
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)=(\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p),\diverge u)
, where
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is defined in (
2.11
). Then
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We first define the map
B_{\alpha}:{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\to\mathbb{F}
via

(2.13)

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu}{2}\mathbb{D}u:\mathbb{D}\overline{v}-\gamma\partial_{1}u\cdot\overline{v}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\beta u\cdot\overline{v},

which is clearly well-defined and continuous. Note that if
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
, then integration by parts shows that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}u\cdot\overline{u}=-\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\overline{\partial_{1}u}=-\overline{\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}u\cdot\overline{u}}\implies\operatorname{Re}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}u\cdot\overline{u}=0.

Thus by the Korn’s inequality from Lemma
2.2
, and using
\alpha>0
and the fact that
\beta
is positive definite, we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left|B_{\alpha}(u,u)\right|\geqslant\operatorname{Re}B_{\alpha}(u,u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{D}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\beta u\cdot\overline{u}\gtrsim\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}},

which shows that
B_{\alpha}
is
{}_{\Tan}H^{1}
-coercive. Since
{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
is a closed subspace of
{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
,
B_{\alpha}
is a well-defined, continuous, coercive functional that is bilinear when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and sesquilinear when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.

Let
(F,g)\in({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
and define the functional
\Lambda_{\alpha}\in\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}\right)^{\overline{*}}
via
\langle\Lambda_{\alpha},v\rangle=-B_{\alpha}(\Pi g,v)+\langle F,v\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}}}
, where
\Pi:L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to{}_{0}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
is the right inverse of the divergence operator introduced in Lemma
2.3
. By applying the standard Lax-Milgram theorem when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and the anti-dual Lax Milgram theorem (see, for instance, Theorem A.5 of
[
29
]
) when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, there exists a unique
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
such that
B_{\alpha}(u,v)=\langle\Lambda_{\alpha},v\rangle
for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}_{\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
, obeying the estimate

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\lesssim\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}}\lesssim_{\alpha,n,b}\left\|F\right\|_{\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}\right)^{\overline{*}}}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Furthermore, by Corollary
2.5
there exists a unique
p\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
such that

(2.17)

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(u,v)=-B_{\alpha}(\Pi g,v)+\langle F,v\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}+\int_{\Omega}p\diverge\overline{v}

for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. This shows that
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u+\Pi g,p)=(F,g)
, so
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is surjective.

On the other hand, if
(u,p)\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
such that
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)=(F,g)
, first we can use the Helmholtz decomposition (
2.6
) to write
u=w+\Pi g
. Then if we use
v=\Pi p
in the definition of the map
\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
from (
2.11
), we arrive at the estimate

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left\|p\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|\Lambda\right\|_{\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}\right)^{\overline{*}}}\lesssim_{\alpha,n,b}\left\|u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}+\left\|F\right\|_{\left({}_{\Tan}H^{1}\right)^{\overline{*}}}.

The injectivity of
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
then follows from the estimates (
2.16
) and (
2.18
).
∎

Next we combine the weak isomorphism with standard elliptic regularity to arrive at our well-posedness result.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
s\geqslant 0
and assume
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is positive definite. For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the bounded linear operator
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{F}^{n-1})
via
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)=\left(\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u,\diverge u,S(p,u)e_{n},\left[\alpha S(p,u)e_{n}+\beta u\right]^{\prime}\right)
. Then
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is an isomorphism for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.7
and the regularity theory for elliptic systems (see, for instance,
[
3
]
).
∎

Next we prove an important result that will be essential in the analysis to follow. We show that the weak solution map
\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
and the strong solution map
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
commute with tangential multipliers, as defined in Definition
A.4
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
s\geqslant 0
and suppose
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{C})
. Consider the tangential multiplier
M_{\omega}
defined via Definition
A.4
. If
(F,g)\in({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
and
(u,p)=\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(F,g)
, then
(M_{\omega}u,M_{\omega}p)=\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(M_{\omega}F,M_{\omega}g)
. Furthermore, if
f\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
,
g\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})
,
k\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})
,
l\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{F}^{n-1})
, and we set
(u,p)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(f,g,k,l)
, then
(M_{\omega}u,M_{\omega}p)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(M_{\omega}f,M_{\omega}g,M_{\omega}k,M_{\omega}l)
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{C}),(F,g)\in({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
and
(u,p)=\chi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(F,g)
. We first note that by the definition of
M_{\omega}
on
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
, the multiplier
M_{\omega}
commutes with differential operators and therefore we immediately have
M_{\omega}g=M_{\omega}\diverge u=\diverge M_{\omega}u
. We then note that by the definition of
M_{\omega}
on
({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}
, we may compute for all
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{n})

(2.19)

\left\langle M_{\omega}F,v\right\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\left\langle F,M_{\overline{\omega}}v\right\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu}{2}\mathbb{D}M_{\omega}u:\mathbb{D}\overline{v}-M_{\omega}p\diverge\overline{v}-\gamma\partial_{1}M_{\omega}u\cdot\overline{v}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\beta M_{\omega}u\cdot\overline{v}\\
=\langle\mathfrak{L}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(M_{\omega}u,M_{\omega}p),v\rangle.

Combining these then shows that
(M_{\omega}u,M_{\omega}p)=\chi_{\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(M_{\omega}F,M_{\omega}g)
. Next we note that again that by the definition of
M_{\omega}
on
H^{s}(U;\mathbb{F}^{k})
and
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{F}^{k})
for
\Sigma\in\{\Sigma_{b},\Sigma_{0}\}
and
k\geqslant 1
, the tangential multiplier
M_{\omega}
commutes with differential operators and therefore

(2.20)

M_{\omega}f=M_{\omega}(\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u)=\diverge M_{\omega}S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}M_{\omega}u=\diverge S(M_{\omega}p,M_{\omega}u)-\gamma\partial_{1}M_{\omega}u,\;\\
M_{\omega}k=M_{\omega}S(p,u)e_{n}=S(M_{\omega}p,M_{\omega}u)e_{n},\;M_{\omega}l=M_{\omega}\left[\alpha S(p,u)e_{n}+\beta u\right]^{\prime}=\left[\alpha S(M_{\omega}p,M_{\omega}u)e_{n}+\beta M_{\omega}u\right]^{\prime}.

Thus,
(M_{\omega}u,M_{\omega}p)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(M_{\omega}f,M_{\omega}g,M_{\omega}k,M_{\omega}l)
.
∎

We conclude this section deriving an
\alpha
-independent estimate for the operator from Theorem
2.8
, assuming that
l=0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. We only focus on the case when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, as we only consider real-valued solutions later.

Proposition 2.10
.

Suppose
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is positive definite. Let
\mathbb{R}\ni s\geqslant 0
,
f\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
g\in H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, and
k\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that if
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
p\in H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
satisfy (
2.1
) with
l=0
, then

(2.21)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}}+\left\|p\right\|_{H^{s+1}}\leqslant C\left(\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b})}\right).

Proof.

Throughout the proof we will use the operators
\mathfrak{J}^{t}_{M}
defined in Lemma
A.3
. Suppose
(u,p)\in{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is the solution to (
2.1
) with
f\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),g\in H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,
k\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
l=0
. Then we note by Theorem
2.9
, for any
M>0
the tuple
(\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u,\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p)

\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the solution to (
2.1
) with data
\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}f\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}g\in H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,
\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k

\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
l=0
.

We may then use
\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
as a test function in the weak formulation (
2.12
) to obtain

(2.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu}{2}\left|\mathbb{D}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\beta\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u:\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u=\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{J}^{s}_{M}f:\mathfrak{J}^{s+2}_{M}u-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k:\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u+\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}g.

Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
, by Lemmas
2.2
and
A.3
and trace theory, we have that there exists constants
c_{1},c_{2}
independent of
\alpha
and
M
such that

(2.23)

c_{1}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}^{2}\leqslant\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s}_{M}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+2}_{M}u\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k\right\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}g\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leqslant c_{2}\left(\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s}_{M}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k\right\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}^{2}\right)+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{c_{1}}{2}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By absorbing the last term on the right side of (
2.23
) and again using Lemma
A.3
, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.24)

\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\lesssim\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s}_{M}f\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k\right\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}+\varepsilon\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}+\varepsilon\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Next, we seek to derive a priori estimates on the pressure. By Lemma
2.3
there exists
v_{0}\in{}_{0}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
such that
\diverge v_{0}=\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p
and
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{{}_{0}H^{1}}\lesssim_{n,b}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}
. Using
v_{0}
in the weak formulation (
2.12
) with the same data, we find that there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}=\mathfrak{C}(\mu,\gamma,s,b)>0
independent of
\alpha
and
M
such that

(2.25)

\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mu}{2}\mathbb{D}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u:\mathbb{D}v_{0}-\gamma\partial_{1}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\cdot v_{0}-\mathfrak{J}^{s}_{M}f:\mathfrak{J}^{1}v_{0}+\int_{\Sigma_{b}}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k:v_{0}\\
\leqslant\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{{}_{0}H^{1}}+\left|\gamma\right|\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s}_{M}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{{}_{0}H^{1}}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}k\right\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{{}_{0}H^{1}}\\
\leqslant\mathfrak{C}\left(\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}^{2}+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus by another absorption argument we find that

(2.26)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})},

where the universal constant is independent of
\alpha
and
M
. By combining (
2.24
) and (
2.26
) we may then choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that

(2.27)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}_{M}p\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}.

Since the universal constant in (
2.27
) is independent of
M
, we may apply the monotone convergence theorem to conclude that

(2.28)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}u\right\|_{{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}+\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}p\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})}.

Standard elliptic regularity results (see
[
2
]
, for instance) then show that

(2.29)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+2}}+\left\|\nabla p\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma_{b}}u\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b})}+\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma_{0}}u\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{0})}.

Lemma
A.3
, the identity (
A.5
), and trace theory show that
\Sigma\in\{\Sigma_{b},\Sigma_{0}\}
,

(2.30)

\displaystyle\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma}u\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma)}=\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}\Tr_{\Sigma}u\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Sigma)}=\left\|\Tr_{\Sigma}\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}u\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\left\|\mathfrak{J}^{s+1}u\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Thus by combining (
2.28
), (
2.29
), and (
2.30
) we find that

(2.31)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s+2}}+\left\|p\right\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})},

where the universal constant in (
2.31
) is uniform over
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
∎

Combining Theorem
2.8
and Proposition
2.10
gives us the following corollary.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
s\geqslant 0
and assume
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is positive definite. For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we define the bounded linear operator
\Theta_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:{}_{\alpha-\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})

via
\Theta_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)=\left(\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u,\diverge u,S(p,u)e_{n}\right)
. Then
\Theta_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is an isomorphism for all

. Furthermore, there exists a constant
M>0
such that

(2.32)

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\left\|\Theta_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}({}_{\alpha-\Tan}H^{s+2}\times H^{s+1};H^{s}\times H^{s+1}\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b}))}\leqslant M.

Proof.

The fact that
\Theta_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is an isomorphism follows immediately from the definition of the space
{}_{\alpha-\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
recorded as the second item of Definition

and Theorem

, and (

) follows immediately from Proposition
2.10
.
∎

3.
The overdetermined
\gamma
-Stokes problem

Our goal in this section is to extend the linear analysis of the system (
2.1
) to the overdetermined variant

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u=f,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\diverge u=g,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u_{n}=h,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
S(p,u)e_{n}=k,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
[\alpha S(p,u)e_{n}+\beta u]^{\prime}=l,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
u_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\end{cases}

obtained from (
2.1
) by appending the equation
u_{n}=h
on
\Sigma_{b}
.

3.1.
The specified divergence problem and the divergence-trace compatibility condition

In this subsection we establish results concerning the specified divergence problem

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge u=g,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u_{n}=h,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
u_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0},\end{cases}

over
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
. The system (
3.2
) is overdetermined in the sense that a non-trivial compatibility condition needs to be satisfied by the data
g
and
h
. We record this condition below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
and let
g=\diverge u\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
and
h=u_{n}\rvert_{\Sigma_{b}}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})
. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle h(\cdot)-\int_{0}^{b}g(\cdot,x_{n})\;dx_{n}\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{F})\text{ and }\left[h-\int_{0}^{b}g(\cdot,x_{n})\;dx_{n}\right]_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\leqslant 2\pi\sqrt{b}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Theorem 3.1 in
[
19
]
establishes this for
u
that entirely vanish on
\Sigma_{0}
. However, an inspection of the proof there shows that it really only requires
u_{n}=0
on
\Sigma_{0}
, so the same argument proves the result for
u\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
.
∎

The next result constructs a right inverse to (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})=\{(g,h)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}):\left\|(g,h)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty\},
where
\left\|(g,h)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}
is defined via
\left\|(g,h)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\left[h-\int_{0}^{b}g(\cdot,x_{n})\;dx_{n}\right]^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-1}}
. There exists a bounded linear operator
G:\mathcal{H}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})\to{}_{0}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
such that
u=G(g,h)
satisfies (
3.2
).

Proof.

This is Proposition 2.4 in
[
29
]
.
∎

3.2.
Adjoint problem analysis

Now we are ready to study the
\mathbb{R}
-solvability of the system (
3.1
). We first record its formal adjoint, the underdetermined problem (here in homogeneous form)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge S(q,v)+\gamma\partial_{1}v=0,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\diverge v=0,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
(S(q,v)e_{n})^{\prime}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
[\alpha S(q,v)e_{n}^{\prime}+\beta^{T}v]^{\prime}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
v_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.\end{cases}

We note in particular that since
\beta w\cdot w=\beta^{T}w\cdot w
for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\beta^{T}
is positive definite whenever
\beta
is. As a consequence, we can augment the third equation with the extra condition
S(p,u)e_{n}\cdot e_{n}=\psi
for arbitrary
\psi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b})
in order to parameterize the solution space via the isomorphism
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}
from Theorem
2.8
.

Throughout the rest of this subsection we aim to develop the asymptotics of some special functions associated to the map
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}
from Theorem
2.8
, which we call the normal stress to solution map. First we define symbols of the pseudodifferential operator associated to this map.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be positive definite, and
s\in[-1,\infty)
. We define the
normal stress to velocity
and the
normal stress to pressure
maps to be the bounded linear maps
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})\to H^{s+2}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}\right)
and
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})\to H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F})
defined via
(\mathcal{U}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi),\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi))=\Phi^{-1}_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}(0,0,\psi e_{n},0),
where
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}
is the isomorphism from Theorem
2.8
. In other words,
(\mathcal{U}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi),\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi))
is the unique solution to the adjoint problem (
3.4
) for a given
\psi\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

There exist bounded, measurable functions
V_{\alpha,\beta},Q_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,b]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\overline{V_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},\gamma)}=V_{\alpha,\beta}(-\xi,x_{n},\gamma),\overline{Q_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},\gamma)}=Q_{\alpha,\beta}(-\xi,x_{n},\gamma)
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
a.e., and for
s\in[-1,\infty)
and all
\psi\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})
, we have
\widehat{\mathcal{U}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi)}(\xi,x_{n})=V_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},-\gamma)\hat{\psi}(\xi)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi)}(\xi,x_{n})=Q_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},-\gamma)\hat{\psi}(\xi)
. Moreover, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left|V_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},\gamma)\right|\leqslant c(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

We note that for fixed
x_{n}\in[0,b]
, the map
\psi\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi)(\cdot,x_{n})
is a bounded linear translation-invariant map between
H^{s+1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{F})
and
H^{s+3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{F}^{n})
and the map
\psi\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\psi)(\cdot,x_{n})
is a bounded linear translation-invariant map between
H^{s+1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{F})
and
H^{s+1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{F})
by Theorem
2.8
. Thus the existence of
V_{\alpha,\beta}
,
Q_{\alpha,\beta}
, and the estimate (
3.5
) is guaranteed by Proposition
A.2
. Since
\psi
is assumed to be real-valued,
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha,\gamma},\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\gamma}
are also real-valued, thus it follows that
\overline{V_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},\gamma)}=V_{\alpha,\beta}(-\xi,x_{n},\gamma),\overline{Q_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,x_{n},\gamma)}=Q_{\alpha,\beta}(-\xi,x_{n},\gamma)
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. The estimate (
3.5
) follows from trace theory and the estimate (
A.2
) recorded as a part of Proposition
A.2
.
∎

We then define
m_{\alpha,\beta}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
via

(3.6)

\displaystyle m_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,\gamma)=V_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,b,\gamma)\cdot e_{n},

which can be viewed as the symbol of the normal stress to Dirichlet pseudodifferential operator
\psi\mapsto u_{n}\rvert_{\Sigma_{b}}
.

Recall that by Theorem
2.7
and trace theory, we have the equivalence
\|\psi\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\asymp\|u\|_{{}_{0}H^{1}}+\|p\|_{L^{2}}
. The next theorem shows that if we weaken the control of
\psi
at low frequencies on the Fourier side, we then have a norm equivalence without the pressure term. Now note in particular that the constant appearing in (
3.8
) can be made to be uniform in the parameter
\alpha
if
\alpha\in(0,1)
. First we need some notation.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\},\mathbb{R}\ni\alpha>0
. For
s\geqslant-1
, we define
\mathscr{O}:H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{F}^{n-1})\to({}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}
by the action on
v\in{}_{\Tan}H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{F}^{n})
via

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathscr{O}(k,l),v\right\rangle_{({}_{\Tan}H^{1})^{\overline{*}},{}_{\Tan}H^{1}}=\left\langle k,v\rvert_{\Sigma_{b}}\right\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}},H^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left\langle l,v^{\prime}\rvert_{\Sigma_{0}}\right\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}},H^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where
\left\langle k,v\rvert_{\Sigma_{b}}\right\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}},H^{\frac{1}{2}}}
denotes the dual paring between
k\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})=(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n}))^{\overline{*}}
and
v\rvert_{\Sigma_{b}}\in H^{1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F}^{n})
, and similarly
\left\langle l,v^{\prime}\rvert_{\Sigma_{0}}\right\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}},H^{\frac{1}{2}}}
denotes the dual paring between
l\in(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{F}^{n-1}))^{\overline{*}}
and
v^{\prime}\rvert_{\Sigma_{b}}\in H^{1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{F}^{n-1})
. Clearly,
\mathscr{O}
is bounded and linear.

We can now state and prove the previously mentioned result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose
\mathbb{R}\ni\alpha>0,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is positive definite, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\psi\in H^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})
and consider
(u,p)=\chi_{\beta^{T},-\gamma}^{-1}(\mathscr{O}(\psi e_{n},0),0)
. The following hold.

(1)

There exists a constant
c>0
such that

(3.8)

(2)

Proof.

\displaystyle\left|\xi\right|^{2}\lesssim\left|(1+4\pi^{2}\left|\xi\right|^{2}\sigma)\operatorname{Re}\overline{m_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,-\gamma)}\right|=\left|\operatorname{Re}\rho_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\xi)\right|\lesssim\left|\rho_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(\xi)\right|.

(
3.37
) then follows by combining (

), and (

). We also note that by the third item of Lemma

, the constants appearing in the estimates above can be chosen to be independent of

The third item follows from a similar set of arguments.

3.3.
Data compatibility and the associated isomorphism

Now we are ready to discuss compatibility conditions associated to the solvability of (
2.1
). To do so we first define some spaces associated to the data.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
s\geqslant 0
.

(1)

We define the Hilbert space

(3.45)

\mathcal{Y}^{s}=\big\{(f,g,h,k,l)\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\\
\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\mid\left\|(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{s}}<\infty\big\},

where we equip
\mathcal{Y}^{s}
with the norm defined via

(3.46)

\displaystyle\left\|(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{s}}^{2}=\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}}^{2}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}^{2}+\left\|l\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}^{2}+\left[h-\int_{0}^{b}g(\cdot,x_{n})\;dx_{n}\right]_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}.

(2)

We define the Hilbert space

(3.47)

\mathcal{Z}^{s}=\big\{(f,g,h,k)\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid\left\|(f,g,h,k)\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{s}}<\infty\big\},

where we equip
\mathcal{Z}^{s}
with the norm defined via

(3.48)

\displaystyle\left\|(f,g,h,k)\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{s}}^{2}=\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}^{2}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}}^{2}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}^{2}+\left\|l\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}^{2}+\left[h-\int_{0}^{b}g(\cdot,x_{n})\;dx_{n}\right]_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}.

Next we define the bilinear maps associated to the data spaces
\mathcal{Y}^{s}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{s}
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\ni\alpha>0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be positive definite,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{R}\ni s\geqslant 0
. We define the bilinear map

(3.49)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:[H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{R}^{n-1})]\times[H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})]\to\mathbb{R}

via

(3.50)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}((f,g,h,k,l),\psi)=\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot v-gq)-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}(k\cdot v-h\psi)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}l\cdot v^{\prime},

where
(v,q)=\Phi^{-1}_{\alpha,-\gamma,\beta^{T}}(0,0,\psi e_{n},0)\in{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is the unique solution to the normal stress problem (
3.4
) guaranteed by Theorem
2.8
. Since
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}^{-1}
is an isomorphism, we have that
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is continuous. We define the left kernel of
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
as

(3.51)

\overleftarrow{\ker}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}=\{(f,g,h,k,l)\in H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\\
\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\mid\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}((f,g,h,k,l),\psi)=0\;\forall\psi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\}.

Since
\overleftarrow{\ker}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is a closed subspace of
H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{R}^{n-1})

inheriting the topology of
\mathcal{Y}^{s}
, we may regard

as a Hilbert space equipped with the inner product coming from
\mathcal{Y}^{s}
.

Now we are ready to record the isomorphism associated to the overdetermined problem (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\ni\alpha>0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be positive definite,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}\ni s\geqslant 0
. Consider the bounded linear map
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{Y}^{s}\cap\overleftarrow{\ker}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
defined via
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)=\mathcal{P}(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p),u_{n})
, where
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is defined in Theorem
2.8
and
\mathcal{P}:H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{Y}^{s}
is a permutation map defined via
\mathcal{P}(f,g,k,l,h)=(f,g,h,k,l)
. Then
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

To prove the first item, we first show that the map
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is well-defined. Let
(u,p)\in{}_{\Tan}H^{s+2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
. By Theorem
2.8
, Lemma
3.1
and trace theory, we have
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)\in\mathcal{Y}^{s}
. To show that
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)\in\overleftarrow{\ker}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
, we let
(f,g,k,l,h)=\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)
, and for any
\psi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})
we let
(v,q)=\Phi^{-1}_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}(0,0,\psi e_{n},0)
. Then

(3.52)

\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}((f,g,h,k,l),\psi)=\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot v-gq)-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}(k\cdot v-h\psi)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}l\cdot v^{\prime}\\
=\int_{\Omega}(\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u)\cdot v-(\diverge u)q-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}S(p,u)e_{n}\cdot v-u\cdot\psi e_{n}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}[\alpha S(p,u)e_{n}+\beta u]^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}\\
=\int_{\Omega}u\cdot(\diverge S(v,q)+\gamma\partial_{1}v)+p\diverge v+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}u^{\prime}\cdot[\alpha S(q,v)e_{n}+\beta^{T}v]^{\prime}=0.

This shows that the map
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u,p)
is well-defined, and it is clearly linear and bounded.

The injectivity of
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
follows from Theorem
2.8
. To prove that
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is surjective, for any
(f,g,h,k,l)\in\mathcal{Y}^{s}\cap\overleftarrow{\ker}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
we let
(u,p)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(f,g,k,l)
, and for any
\psi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})
we let
(v,q)=\Phi^{-1}_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}(0,0,\psi e_{n},0)
. Since
(f,g,h,k,l)\in\overleftarrow{\ker}\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
, we then have

(3.53)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}u_{n}\psi=\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot v-gq)-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}k\cdot v+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}l\cdot v^{\prime}=-\int_{\Sigma_{0}}h\psi,

and so
u_{n}=h
on
\Sigma_{b}
. This shows that
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is surjective, and the desired conclusion follows.

To prove the second item we follow a similar set of arguments as above, where we use the isomorphism
\Theta_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
in place of
\Phi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
, Corollary
2.11
in place of Theorem
2.8
,the bilinear map
\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
in place of
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
and the Hilbert space
\mathcal{Z}^{s}
in place of
\mathcal{Y}^{s}
. The fact that the operator norm of
\Psi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is independent of
\alpha
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
follows from Proposition
2.10
.
∎

Next we would like to introduce a quantitative way of measuring how close a data tuple
(f,g,h,k,l)
is to being compatible. To do so we introduce the linear map
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:H^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+3/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{0};\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\to L^{2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})
induced by the bilinear map
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
. The induced linear map
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is defined via

(3.54)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l),\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}((f,g,h,k,l),\psi),

where we use the canonical injection
i:H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{F})
to identify
\psi
with an element of
L^{2}
. First we show that
\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)
commutes with tangential multipliers defined in Definition
A.4
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Suppose
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{C})
and consider the tangential multiplier
M_{\omega}
defined in Definition
A.4
. Then

(3.55)

\displaystyle M_{\omega}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)=\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(M_{\omega}f,M_{\omega}g,M_{\omega}h,M_{\omega}k,M_{\omega}l).

Proof.

For a given
\psi\in H^{s+1/2}(\Sigma_{b};\mathbb{R})
we define
(v,q)=\Phi^{-1}_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}(0,0,\psi e_{n},0)
. Then by Theorem
2.9
, we have

(3.56)

\left\langle M_{\omega}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l),\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l),M_{\omega}\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}((f,g,h,k,l),M_{\omega}\psi)\\
=\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot M_{\omega}v-gM_{\omega}q)-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}(k\cdot M_{\omega}v-hM_{\omega}\psi)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}l\cdot M_{\omega}v^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}(M_{\omega}f\cdot v-M_{\omega}gq)\\
-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}(M_{\omega}k\cdot v-M_{\omega}h\psi)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}M_{\omega}l\cdot v^{\prime}=\langle\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(M_{\omega}f,M_{\omega}g,M_{\omega}k,M_{\omega}l,M_{\omega}h),\psi\rangle.

Since this is true for all

, the desired conclusion follows.

∎

and

Theorem 3.14

Suppose

(1)

(2)

If

, then

and there exists a constant

for which

(3.58)

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,0)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}\cap H^{s+3/2}}\leqslant c\left\|(f,g,h,k)\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}^{s}}.

(3)

Furthermore, there exists a constant
c>0
for which the estimate (
3.57
) holds for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In other word, the constant

can be chosen to be independent of
\alpha
if
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

We first note that the second item follows immediately from the first item. To prove the first item, we first note that by Proposition
3.13
,

(3.59)

\left\|\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}\cap H^{s+3/2}}\lesssim\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}}\\
=\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}+\left\|\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}f,M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}g,M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}k,M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}l,M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}h)\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}}\\
\lesssim_{\alpha}\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}\\
+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}l\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)^{c}}}h\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}}\\
\lesssim_{\alpha}\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\left\|k\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}+\left\|l\right\|_{H^{s+1/2}}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s+3/2}}.

To arrive at the desired estimate it then suffices to control
\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}
. We note that for any
\psi\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R})
, we may let
(v,q)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}^{-1}(0,0,\psi e_{n},0)
and compute

(3.60)

\int_{B(0,1)}\mathscr{F}[\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l)](\xi)\cdot\mathscr{F}[\psi](\xi)\;d\xi=\langle M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l),\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
=\langle\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(f,g,h,k,l),M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}((f,g,h,k,l),M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\psi)\\
=\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}v-gM_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}q)-\int_{\Sigma_{b}}(k\cdot M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}v-hM_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\psi)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}l\cdot M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}v^{\prime}\\

Therefore,

(3.61)

+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}(q-\psi)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\psi\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\left[h-\int_{0}^{b}g\right]_{\dot{H}^{-1}},

where we have used the second estimate in (
3.17
) on
V_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi,0,-\gamma)
to handle to integral involving

.

By Theorem
2.9
, we have
(M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}v,M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}q)=\Phi_{\alpha,\beta^{T},-\gamma}^{-1}(0,0,M_{\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,1)}}\psi e_{n},0)

(3.62)

(3.63)

To prove the third item, we note that if

then

does not appear in (

and Theorem

4.
Linear analysis with
\eta

In this section we would like to establish the
\mathbb{R}
-solvability of the
\gamma-
Stokes system with gravity capillary boundary conditions

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge S(p,u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u+(\nabla^{\prime}\eta,0)=f&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\diverge u=g,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u_{n}+\gamma\partial_{1}\eta=h,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
S(p,u)e_{n}+\sigma\Delta^{\prime}\eta e_{n}=k,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
(\alpha S(p,u)e_{n})^{\prime}+\beta u^{\prime}=l,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
u_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.\end{cases}

4.1.
Preliminaries

First, we introduce the container space for the free surface function
\eta
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
0\leqslant s\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the specialized anisotropic Sobolev space
X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to consist of
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
such that
\hat{f}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
and

(4.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{X^{s}}^{2}:=\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\xi_{1}^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{4}}{\left|\xi\right|^{2}}\left|\hat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi+\int_{B(0,1)^{c}}(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2})^{s}\left|\hat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi<\infty.

The following proposition summarizes the important properties of this space.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathbb{R}\ni s\geqslant 0
and
d\geqslant 1
. The following hold.

(1)

X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a separable Hilbert space, and if
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
s<t
, then we have the continuous inclusion
X^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(2)

If
d=1
, we have
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{s}}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X^{s}}
are equivalent norms. For
d\geqslant 2
, we have the continuous inclusion
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(3)

If
s\geqslant 1
, then
\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{H^{s-1}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{X^{s}}
for
f\in X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular, the map
\nabla:X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous.

(4)

For every
f\in X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t>0
, we can write
f=f_{l,t}+f_{h,t}
, where
f_{l,t}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\mathbbm{1}_{B(0,t)}\mathscr{F}[f]]\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f_{h,t}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}[\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,t)}\mathscr{F}[f]]\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, we have the estimates

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\|f_{l,t}\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leqslant k}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{l,R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|f_{l,t}\right\|_{X^{s}}\;\text{for each}\;k\in\mathbb{N}\;\text{and}\;\left\|f_{h,t}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left\|f_{h,t}\right\|_{X^{s}}.

(5)

If
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s>k+d/2
, then we have the continuous inclusion
X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow C^{k}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
.

(6)

If
s>d/2
, then for any

Suppose

(1)

(2)

(3)

Furthermore, there exists a constant

for which

Proof.

To prove the first and second items, we first note that by Proposition 3.13 in
[
18
]
and standard trace theory, the maps
\Upsilon_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma}
are well-defined and continuous. To show that
\Upsilon_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma}
is injective, we suppose
(u,p,\eta)\in\mathcal{X}^{s}
and
\Upsilon_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma}\left(u,p,\eta\right)=0
. We note that if

, then

and

. Therefore
\Upsilon_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma}(u,p,\eta)=0
if and only if

satisfies

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\diverge S(\tilde{p},u)-\gamma\partial_{1}u=0,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
\diverge u=0,&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
S(\tilde{p},u)e_{n}=(\eta-\sigma\Delta^{\prime}\eta)e_{n},&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
u_{n}+\gamma\partial_{1}\eta=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{b}\\
\left[\alpha S(p,u)e_{n}+\beta u\right]^{\prime}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}\\
u_{n}=0,&\text{on}\;\Sigma_{0}.\end{cases}

We note that by Tonelli’s theorem, Parseval’s theorem, and the fifth item of Theorem
4.2
we have
\hat{u}(\xi,\cdot)\in H^{s}((0,b);\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
\widehat{\tilde{p}}(\xi,\cdot)\in H^{1}((0,b);\mathbb{C})
, for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. By the second item in Theorem
4.2
,
\hat{\eta}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{R})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2})^{(s+5/2)/2}d\xi;\mathbb{R})
. Thus, we may apply the horizontal Fourier transform to (
4.11
) to deduce for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,

satisfies

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(-\partial_{n}^{2}+4\pi^{2}\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)w^{\prime}+2\pi i\xi q-2\pi i\xi_{1}\gamma w^{\prime}=0,&\text{in}\;(0,b)\\
\left(-\partial_{n}^{2}+4\pi^{2}\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)w_{n}+\partial_{n}q-2\pi i\xi_{1}\gamma w_{n}=0,&\text{in}\;(0,b)\\
2\pi i\xi\cdot w^{\prime}+\partial_{n}w_{n}=0,&\text{in}\;(0,b)\\
-\partial_{n}w^{\prime}-2\pi i\xi w_{n}=0,&\text{for}\;x_{n}=b\\

w_{n}=0,&\text{for}\;x_{n}=0.\end{cases}

For a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

and

Using (

4.15

By taking the real part of this expression and applying the coercivity condition (

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\left(\left\|Df_{\alpha}(0)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha};Y)}+\left\|Df_{\alpha}(0)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y;X_{\alpha})}+\sup_{z\in B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\varepsilon)}\left\|D^{2}f_{\alpha}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(X;Y)}\right)\leqslant C.

Then the following hold.

(1)

There exists a
\delta>0
such that for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists an open set
V_{\alpha}
such that
B_{X_{\alpha}}\left(0,\frac{\delta}{3C^{2}}\right)\subset V_{\alpha}\subset B_{X}(0,\delta)
,
f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})=B_{Y}\left(0,\frac{\delta}{2C}\right)
, and the restriction
f_{\alpha}\rvert_{V_{\alpha}}:V_{\alpha}\to f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.

(2)

There exists a constant
K>0
such that

(A.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\left(\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}(V_{\alpha};Y)}+\left\|f_{\alpha}^{-1}\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}(f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha});X_{\alpha})}\right)\leqslant K.

(3)

We have
f_{\alpha}\in C^{1}_{b}(V_{\alpha};Y)
and
f_{\alpha}^{-1}\in C^{1}_{b}(f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha});X_{\alpha})
. Furthermore,
Df_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha};Y)
is a linear homeomorphism for every
x\in V_{\alpha}
and
Df_{\alpha}^{-1}(y)\in\mathcal{L}(Y;X_{\alpha})
is a linear homeomorphism for every
y\in f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})
, and the two are related via

(A.10)

\displaystyle Df_{\alpha}^{-1}(y)=(Df_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha}^{-1}(y)))^{-1}

for every
y\in f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})
.

(4)

If
f\in C^{k}(U_{\alpha};Y)
for some
k\geqslant 2
, then
f\in C^{k}(V_{\alpha};Y)
and
f\in C^{k}(f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha});X_{\alpha})
.

A depiction of the sets
V_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})
and the
\alpha

B_{X}(0,\delta/(3C)^{2})

Proof.

It suffices to only prove the first two items as the third and fourth items follow from the standard inverse function theorem applied to each

[
1
]
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
we consider the function
F_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to X
defined via
F_{\alpha}(x)=x-Df_{\alpha}(0)^{-1}f_{\alpha}(x)
. Then for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
F_{\alpha}\in C^{2}(U_{\alpha};X)
,
F_{\alpha}(0)=0
, and
DF_{\alpha}(0)=0
. Furthermore, by applying the mean value inequality over
B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\varepsilon)
and (
A.8

x\in B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\varepsilon)

(A.11)

\displaystyle\left\|DF_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leqslant\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left\|D^{2}F_{\alpha}(tx)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(X_{\alpha})}\left\|x\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leqslant\left\|Df_{\alpha}(0)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y;X_{\alpha})}\left\|D^{2}f_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(X_{\alpha};Y)}\left\|x\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leqslant C^{2}\left\|x\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}.

By (
A.8
), we may choose
\delta>0
sufficiently small and independent of
\alpha

\delta<(2C^{2})^{-1}
and
\left\|DF_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
for all

. By the mean value inequality, we also have

(A.12)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{\alpha}(x)-F_{\alpha}(y)\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leqslant\left\|x-y\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\sup_{z\in B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta)}\left\|DF_{\alpha}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha})}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\|x-y\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\;\text{for all}\;x,y\in B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta).

Fix
y\in B_{Y}(0,\delta(2C)^{-1})
and define the function
h_{\alpha}:B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]\to B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta)\subset B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]

h_{\alpha}(x)=Df_{\alpha}(0)^{-1}(y+Df_{\alpha}(0)F_{\alpha}(x))
, where
B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]
denotes the closed ball in
X_{\alpha}
with radius
\delta
. To check that the map is well-defined, we note that since
F_{\alpha}(0)=0
, by the writing
h_{\alpha}(x)=Df_{\alpha}(0)^{-1}y+F_{\alpha}(x)
and using (
A.12

\left\|h_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leqslant C\left\|y\right\|_{Y}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}<\delta
for all
x\in B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]
. This shows that the map is well-defined. Next we note that since
h_{\alpha}
and
F_{\alpha}
differ by a constant, by the estimate on
DF_{\alpha}
we also have
\left\|Dh_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{\alpha})}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
for all
x\in B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]
, which implies that
h_{\alpha}
is a contraction on the complete metric space
B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]
. Therefore by the contraction mapping theorem, there exists a unique
x\in B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta]
for which
h_{\alpha}(x)=x
, but since
h_{\alpha}(B_{X_{\alpha}}[0,\delta])\subseteq B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta)

x=h_{\alpha}(x)\in B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta)
. Since
h_{\alpha}(x)=x

f_{\alpha}(x)=y

f_{\alpha}(x)=y
.

Now we define the set
V_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}^{-1}(B_{Y}(0,\delta(2C)^{-1}))\cap B_{X}(0,\delta)

B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta)\subset B_{X}(0,\delta)

\leqslant\frac{3}{2}C\left\|x-y\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\;\text{for all}\;x,y\in B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta).

In particular, since
f_{\alpha}(0)=0
we have
\left\|f_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{Y}\leqslant 3C/2\left\|x\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}
for all
x\in B_{X}(0,\delta)
. This implies the inclusion
f_{\alpha}(B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta(3C)^{-2}))\subseteq B_{Y}(0,\delta(2C)^{-1})
, and subsequently
B_{X_{\alpha}}(0,\delta(3C)^{-2})\subseteq V_{\alpha}
.

By the contradiction mapping argument above, the restriction
f_{\alpha}\rvert_{V_{\alpha}}:V_{\alpha}\to f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})=B_{Y}(0,\delta(2C)^{-1})
is invertible. Next we note that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in V_{\alpha}
, we have

(A.14)

\left\|x_{1}-x_{2}\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leqslant\left\|F_{\alpha}(x_{1})-F_{\alpha}(x_{2})\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}+\left\|Df_{\alpha}(0)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y;X)}\left\|f_{\alpha}(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\\
\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{1}-x_{2}\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}+C\left\|f_{\alpha}(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}.

This then implies

(A.15)

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\alpha}^{-1}(y_{1})-f_{\alpha}^{-1}(y_{2})\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}=\left\|x_{1}-x_{2}\right\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leqslant 2C\left\|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\right\|_{Y}=2C\left\|y_{1}-y_{2}\right\|_{Y},

for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})=B_{Y}(0,\delta(2C)^{-1})
. From (
A.13
) and (
A.15
) we may conclude that
f_{\alpha}\rvert_{V_{\alpha}}:V_{\alpha}\to f_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha})
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism and the estimate (
A.9
) holds.

∎

Now we are ready to prove a parameter dependent implicit function theorem.

Theorem A.6
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be Banach spaces over
\mathbb{F}
and let
\{Y_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\subset Y
be a one-parameter family of closed subspaces of
Y
. We equip the Cartesian products
X\times Y,X\times Z
with the
\infty
-norm defined via
\left\|(x,x^{\prime})\right\|_{X\times X^{\prime}}=\max\{\left\|x\right\|_{X},\left\|x^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{\prime}}\}
, and we equip the Cartesian products
X\times Y_{\alpha}
with the norm inherited from
X\times Y
.

For all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we suppose
U_{\alpha}\subseteq X\times Y_{\alpha}
is a non-empty open set containing
0
,
f_{\alpha}\in C^{2}(U_{\alpha};Z)
,
f_{\alpha}(0,0)=0
,
D_{2}f_{\alpha}(0,0)\in\mathcal{L}(Y;Z)
is a linear homeomorphism, and there exists a constant
C>2
and a non-empty open set
E_{\alpha}\subseteq U_{\alpha}
containing 0 such that

(A.16)

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\left(\left\|Df_{\alpha}(0,0)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X;Y)}+\left\|Df_{\alpha}(0,0)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y;X)}+\sup_{(x,y)\in E_{\alpha}}\left\|D^{2}f_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X;Y)}\right)\leqslant C,

Then there exists a
\delta_{1}>0
such that for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
g_{\alpha}\in C^{2}_{b}(B_{X}(0,\delta_{1});Y_{\alpha})\cap C_{b}^{0,1}(B_{X}(0,\delta_{1});Y_{\alpha})
such that the following hold.

(1)

g_{\alpha}(0)=0
and
(x,g_{\alpha}(x))\in B_{X}(0,\delta_{1})\times B_{Y_{\alpha}}(0,\delta_{1})\subseteq U_{\alpha}
for all
x\in B_{X}(0,\delta_{1})
.

(2)

f_{\alpha}(x,g_{\alpha}(x))=0
for all
x\in B_{X}(0,\delta_{1})
, and if
(x,y)\in B_{X}(0,\delta_{1})\times B_{Y_{\alpha}}(0,\delta_{1})
satisfy
f_{\alpha}(x,y)=0
, then
y=g_{\alpha}(x)
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
M>0
for which

(A.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\sup_{x\in B_{X}(0,\delta_{1})}\left\|g_{\alpha}(x)\right\|_{Y}\leqslant M.

Proof.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
consider the function
F_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to X\times Z
defined via
F_{\alpha}(x,y)=(x,f_{\alpha}(x,y))

F_{\alpha}\in C^{2}(U_{\alpha};X\times Z)
and
DF_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{L}(X\times Y_{\alpha};X\times Z)

(A.18)

\displaystyle DF_{\alpha}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{X}&0_{Y_{\alpha}}\\
D_{1}f_{\alpha}(x,y)&D_{2}f_{\alpha}(x,y)\end{pmatrix}.

Since
D_{2}f_{\alpha}(0,0)
is a linear homeomorphism, we readily conclude that
DF_{\alpha}(0,0)
is also a linear homeomorphism. Thus, we may apply the standard inverse function theorem to conclude that
F_{\alpha}
is a local
C^{2}
-diffeomorphism around 0. Note that
DF_{\alpha}
is then locally invertible with

(A.19)

\displaystyle(DF_{\alpha}(x,y))^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{X}&0_{Y_{\alpha}}\\
-(D_{2}f_{\alpha}(x,y))^{-1}D_{1}f_{\alpha}(x,y)&(D_{2}f_{\alpha}(x,y))^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

for all
(x,y)
in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
(0,0)
. Combining the expression (
A.19
) with (
A.16
), we may then conclude that
F_{\alpha}
also satisfies the estimate (
A.8
) and the rest of the hypothesis of Theorem

\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}>0

such that we have

and

is a

-diffeomorphism and a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism. Furthermore, the
C^{0}_{b}(V_{\alpha};X\times Z)
norm of
F_{\alpha}
and the
C^{0}_{b}(F_{\alpha}(V_{\alpha});X\times Y_{\alpha})

F^{-1}_{\alpha}
are independent of
\alpha
.

A.4.
Smoothness of composition operators between Sobolev spaces

In this subsection we record composition results involving the flattening map
\mathfrak{F}
defined via (
1.5
).

Theorem A.7
.

Let
\mathbb{N}\ni k\geqslant 1+\lfloor n/2\rfloor
,
d\geqslant 1
, and
m\in\{0,1,2\}
. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
be as in (
1.5
), and for every
\eta\in X^{k+1/2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
define the map
\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
via
\mathfrak{F}(x)=x+\varphi(x_{n})\eta(x^{\prime})e_{n}
. Then there exists a
0<\delta_{*}<1
for which the map
\Lambda:H^{k+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times B_{X^{k+1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\mathbb{R})}(0,\delta)\to H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{d})

defined via
\Lambda(f,\eta)=f\circ\mathfrak{F}
is well-defined and the following hold.

(1)

For all
m\in\{0,1,2\}
,
\Lambda
is continuous.

(2)

If
m=1
, then
\Lambda
is
C^{1}
and satisfies
D\Lambda(f,\eta)(f_{1},\eta_{1})=(\partial_{n}f\circ\mathfrak{F})\varphi\eta_{1}+f_{1}\circ\mathfrak{F}
.

(3)

If
m=2
, then
\Lambda
is
C^{2}
and satisfies
D^{2}\Lambda(f,\eta)[(f_{1},\eta_{1}),(f_{2},\eta_{2})]=(D^{2}f\circ\mathfrak{F})(\varphi\eta_{1}e_{n},\varphi\eta_{2}e_{n})+(\partial_{n}f_{1}\circ\mathfrak{F})\varphi\eta_{2}+(\partial_{n}f_{2}\circ\mathfrak{F})\varphi\eta_{1}
.

Proof.

The first and second items follow from Theorem 5.20 in
[
19
]
, and a close examination of the proof therein shows that the argument can be extended prove the third item.

∎

Next we prove a variant of Theorem
A.7
for compositions between
C^{k}_{b}
functions and Sobolev functions.

Theorem A.8
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an extension domain and assume
\mathbb{N}\ni k\geqslant 2+\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor,m\in\{0,1,2\}
. Let
f\in C^{k+1+m}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and assume
f(0)=0
if
\Omega
has infinite measure. Then the map
\Lambda_{f}:H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined via
\Lambda_{f}(u)=f\circ u
is well-defined and
C^{m}
.

Proof.

We prove this in four steps.

Step 1
: A multiplier estimate. We first prove via finite induction the statement
\mathbb{P}_{j}
for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant k
, where
\mathbb{P}_{j}
denotes the proposition that for all
f\in C^{j+1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u,v\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have the a priori estimate

(A.21)

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)v\right\|_{H^{j}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{j}_{b}}\langle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{j}}\;\text{for all \;}u,v\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\langle\cdot\rangle=(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2})^{1/2}
denotes the Japanese bracket.

In the case when
j=0
, by the supercritical Sobolev embedding
H^{1+\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow C^{0}_{b}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the assumption that
f\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the estimate (
A.21
) is satisfied trivially. Thus
\mathbb{P}_{0}
holds.

Next we proceed inductively and suppose that
\mathbb{P}_{l}
holds for all
0\leqslant l\leqslant j\leqslant k-1
and consider the case
j+1\leqslant k
. We first note that for any
1\leqslant p\leqslant n
and
u,v\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(A.22)

\partial_{p}\Lambda_{f}(u)=\sum_{q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)\partial_{p}(u)_{q},\;\partial_{p}(\Lambda_{f}(u)v)=\Lambda_{f}(u)\partial_{p}v+\sum_{q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)\partial_{p}(u)_{q}v.

Then by the supercritical Sobolev embedding, applying the estimate (
A.22
) from the induction hypothesis on
f\in C_{b}^{j+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\partial_{q}f\in C^{j+1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the standard Sobolev product estimate with the fact that
k-1\geqslant 1+\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor
, we have

(A.23)

\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)v\right\|_{H^{j+1}}\lesssim\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)v\right\|_{H^{0}}+\sum_{p=1}^{n}\lVert\partial_{p}(\Lambda_{f}(u)v)\rVert_{H^{j}}\lesssim\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)v\right\|_{H^{0}}+\sum_{p=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)\partial_{p}v\right\|_{H^{j}}\\
+\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)\partial_{p}(u)_{q}v\right\|_{H^{j}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{0}}+\left\|f\right\|_{C^{j}_{b}}\langle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{j+1}}+\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{j+1}_{b}}\langle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j}\left\|\partial_{p}(u)_{q}v\right\|_{H^{j}}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{0}}+\left\|f\right\|_{C^{j}_{b}}\langle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{j+1}}+\left\|f\right\|_{C^{j+1}_{b}}\langle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{j}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{j+1}_{b}}\langle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j+1}\left\|v\right\|_{H_{j+1}},

which shows
\mathbb{P}_{j+1}
holds. This completes the induction argument.

Step 2
: A difference estimate. Next we use the multiplier estimate from the previous step to prove the statement
\mathbb{Q}_{j}
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant k
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{j}
denotes the proposition that for all
f\in C^{j+1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u,v\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the difference
\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\in H^{j}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies

(A.24)

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{j}}\to 0\;\text{if}\;v\to u\;\text{in}\;H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To prove
\mathbb{Q}_{j}
for each admissible
j
we proceed by finite induction again.

In the case when
j=0
, we note that by applying the mean value inequality we may deduce that

(A.25)

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{0}}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{H^{0}}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{H^{k}}.

Thus
\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\in H^{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{0}}\to 0
as
v\to u\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus
\mathbb{Q}_{0}
holds.

Next we suppose that
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
holds for all
0\leqslant l\leqslant j\leqslant k-1
and consider the case
j+1\leqslant k
. We note that (
A.22
) implies that for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant n
,

(A.26)

\partial_{p}(\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v))=\sum_{q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)\partial_{p}(u)_{q}-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(v)\partial_{p}(v)_{q}\\
=\sum_{q=1}^{n}\left(\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(v)\right)\partial_{p}(u)_{q}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(v)\partial_{p}(u-v)_{q}\;\text{for all}\;u,v\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

thus by (
A.25
), the multiplier estimate (
A.21
), the induction hypothesis and basic product estimates, we have

(A.27)

\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{j+1}}=\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{0}}+\sum_{p=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{p}(\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v))\right\|_{H^{j}}\\
\lesssim\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{0}}+\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left\|\left(\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(v)\right)\partial_{p}(u)_{q}\right\|_{H^{j}}+\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(v)\partial_{p}(u-v)_{q}\right\|_{H^{j}}\\
\lesssim\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{0}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\sum_{q=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(v)\right\|_{H^{j}}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{q}f\right\|_{C^{j}_{b}}\langle\left\|v\right\|_{H^{k}}\rangle^{j}\left\|u-v\right\|_{H^{j+1}}.

This shows that
\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\in H^{j+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{j+1}}\to 0
if
v\to u\in H^{j+1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Thus
\mathbb{Q}_{j+1}
holds and the induction argument is complete.

Step 3
: Well-definedness and continuity. Next we utilize the result from the previous step to show that
\Lambda_{f}:H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined and continuous. We note that in the case when
\Omega
has infinite measure, using the additional assumption
f(0)=0
we may apply the mean value inequality to deduce that

(A.28)

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda_{f}(u)\right|=\left|f(u)-f(0)\right|\leqslant\left\|Df\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}}\left|u\right|\;\text{for all}\;u\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which in turn implies that

(A.29)

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)\right\|_{H^{0}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{0}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\;\text{for all}\;u\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In the case when
\Omega
has finite measure, the estimate (
A.29
) also holds. In either case, we may use
v=0\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the estimate (
A.27
) for
j+1=k
from the previous step and (
A.29
) to immediate deduce that

(A.30)

\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)\right\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)\right\|_{H^{0}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\sum_{q=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(0)\right\|_{H^{k-1}}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{q}f\right\|_{C^{j}_{b}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\sum_{q=1}^{n}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(0)\right\|_{H^{k-1}}+\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{k}}\;\text{for all}\;u\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
\partial_{q}f\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, from
\mathbb{Q}_{k-1}
we know that
\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(0)\in H^{k-1}
for all
1\leqslant q\leqslant n
, therefore
\Lambda_{f}(u)\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
u\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. From
\mathbb{Q}_{k}
we also have
\left\|\Lambda_{f}(u)-\Lambda_{f}(v)\right\|_{H^{k}}\to 0
if
v\to u
in
H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, thus we may conclude that the map
\Lambda_{f}:H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined and continuous.

Step 4:
Continuous differentiability.
To conclude the proof we show that
\Lambda_{f}
is
C^{m}
given
f\in C^{k+1+m}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
m\in\{1,2\}
. In the case when
m=1
, we note that by the fundamental theorem of calculus for all
u,v\in H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(A.31)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{f}(u+v)-\Lambda_{f}(u)-\sum_{q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)(v)_{q}=\underbrace{\sum_{q=1}^{n}(v)_{q}\int_{0}^{1}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u+tv)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)\;dt}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{1}},

and thus by the fact that
H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an algebra and applying the statement
\mathbb{Q}_{k}
from the induction argument above to
\partial_{q}f\in C_{b}^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

(A.32)

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\right\|_{H^{k}}}{\left\|v\right\|_{H^{k}}}\lesssim\sum_{q=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u+tv)-\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)\right\|_{H^{k}}\;dt\to 0\;\text{as}\;\left\|v\right\|_{H^{k}}\to 0.

Thus we may conclude that
\Lambda_{f}
is differentiable when
m=1
and

(A.33)

\displaystyle D\Lambda_{f}(u)(v)=\sum_{q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}(u)(v)_{q}.

Since
D\Lambda_{f}(u)
is in terms
\Lambda_{\partial_{q}f}
which satisfies (
A.24
), we may then conclude that
D\Lambda_{f}
is continuously differentiable.

To conclude in the case of
m=2
, we note that by (
A.33
) and the fundamental theorem of calculus again we have

(A.34)

\displaystyle D\Lambda_{f}(u+w)(v)-D\Lambda_{f}(u)(v)-\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}(u)(v)_{p}(w)_{q}=\underbrace{\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}(v)_{p}(w)_{q}\int_{0}^{1}\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}(u+tw)-\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}(u)\;dt}_{:=\mathcal{R}_{2}}.

Using the fact that
H^{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an algebra and applying the statement
\mathbb{Q}_{k}
from the induction argument above to
\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f\in C_{b}^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we then have

(A.35)

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{k})}}{\left\|w\right\|_{H^{k}}}\lesssim\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}(u+tw)-\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}(u)\right\|_{H^{k}}\;dt\to 0\;\text{as}\;\left\|w\right\|_{H^{k}}\to 0.

This shows that
\Lambda_{f}
is twice-differentiable with

(A.36)

\displaystyle D^{2}\Lambda_{f}(u)(v,w)=\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}(u)(v)_{p}(w)_{q}.

Since
\Lambda_{\partial_{p}\partial_{q}f}
satisfies (
A.24
), we may then conclude that
\Lambda_{f}
is
C^{2}
when
m=2
.

∎

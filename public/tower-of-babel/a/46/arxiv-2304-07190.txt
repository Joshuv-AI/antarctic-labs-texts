Abstract
. We prove two completeness results for Kleene algebra with tests and a top element, with respect to guarded string languages and binary relations. While the equational theories of those two classes of models coincide over the signature of Kleene algebra, this is no longer the case when we consider an additional constant “top” for the full element. Indeed, the full relation satisfies more laws than the full language, and we show that those additional laws can all be derived from a single additional axiom. We recover that the two equational theories coincide if we slightly generalise the notion of relational model, allowing sub-algebras of relations where top is a greatest element but not necessarily the full relation. We use models of closed languages and reductions in order to prove our completeness results, which are relative to any axiomatisation of the algebra of regular events. For one of our constructions, we extend the concept of finite monoid recognisability to guarded-string languages; this device makes it possible to obtain a PSpace algorithm for the equational theory of binary relations.

4.
Relational models

Given a set
X
, a
relation on
X
is a set of pairs of elements from
X
. We let
R,S
range over such relations, whose set is written
\mathcal{P}\left({X{\times}X}\right)
, and we write
x\mathbin{R}y
for
\langle{x,y}\rangle\in R
.
Given a function
p\colon X\to\mathsf{At}
,
relations on
X
form an
S_{\top}
-algebra with the operations defined as follows:

\displaystyle R+S

\displaystyle\triangleq R\cup S

\displaystyle R\cdot S

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\langle{x,z}\rangle\mid\exists y\in X,~x\mathbin{R}y~\land~y\mathbin{S}z\right\}

\displaystyle R^{*}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\langle{x_{0},x_{n}}\rangle\mid\exists n\in\mathbb{N},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},~\forall i<n,~x_{i}\mathbin{R}x_{i+1}\right\}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\triangleq\emptyset

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\langle{x,x}\rangle\mid x\in X\right\}

\displaystyle\top

\displaystyle\triangleq X\times X

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\langle{x,x}\rangle\mid p(x)=\alpha\right\}

In words,

is set-theoretic union,
\cdot
is relational composition,
\cdot^{*}
is reflexive transitive closure,

,

and

are the empty, identity and full relations, respectively.
The function

is only used to define the constants

describes a partition of

, using atoms to name the equivalence classes. The relation
\alpha
consists of the sub-identity relation selecting precisely the elements whose equivalence class is named
\alpha
.

We write

for the class of all

-algebras of the above shape.

Remark 8
.

Note that this definition covers the standard way of interpreting Kleene algebra with tests expressions into relations on a set

2
. If we start from a set

as above when setting

gives them back their original status of variables.

,

5.
Relations with a greatest element

A
generalised
S_{\top}
-algebra of relations
is an
S
-subalgebra
A
of an algebra of relations such that
A
has a greatest element, seen as an
S_{\top}
-algebra by using this greatest element for the constant
\top
. We write
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
for the class of all generalised
S_{\top}
-algebras of relations.

Intuitively,
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
consists of models of binary relations where
\top
is not necessarily the full relation, only a greatest element.
As an example, consider relations
R
over the natural numbers such that
i\leq j
whenever
i\mathbin{R}j
. Those form an
S
-algebra with greatest element the order relation
\leq
itself, which is not the full relation.

In the literature,
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
is sometimes preferred over
\mathsf{REL}
because it is closed under taking subalgebras and products, and actually forms a quasivariety
[
AM11
]
. (In contrast, it is not clear whether
\mathsf{REL}
is closed under products: the two obvious ways of embedding a pair of relations into a new relation fail to preserve either union or top—
\mathsf{REL}
as defined here is not closed under taking subalgebras either, but defining it in such a way would not change the results from the present paper.)

The equational theory of
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
differs from that of
\mathsf{REL}
. For instance, the previous example of ordered relations shows that
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}\not\models x\leq x\cdot\top\cdot x
. Indeed, for
x=\left\{\langle{0,1}\rangle\right\}
,
x\cdot\top\cdot x
is empty since
\top
does not relate
1
to
0
.

We show below that the equational theory of
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
actually coincides with that of
\mathsf{GSL}
, and can thus be axiomatised by
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
.

Proposition 26
.

Every member of
\mathsf{GSL}
embeds into a member of
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
.

Proof 5.1
.

We adapt the technique used by Pratt for Kleene algebras (without top)
[
Pra80
, third page]
and later reused by Kozen and Smith for Kleene algebras with tests
[
KS96
, Lemma 5]
.
For a set
X
, let
M(X)
be the set of relations
R
on
\mathcal{GS}_{X}
such that for all guarded strings
u,v
,
u
is a prefix of
v
whenever
u\mathbin{R}v
. The
S
-operations on relations restrict to
M(X)
, so that
M(X)
is an
S
-algebra, and setting
\top\triangleq\left\{\langle{u,u\diamond v}\rangle\mid u,v\in\mathcal{GS}_{X}\right\}
turns it into a member of
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
.
We embed the member
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{GS}_{X})
of
\mathsf{GSL}
into
M(X)
as follows:

\displaystyle\iota\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{GS}_{X})\to M(X)

\displaystyle L\mapsto\left\{\langle{u,u\diamond v}\rangle\mid u\in\mathcal{GS}_{X},~v\in L\right\}

The function
\iota
is easily shown to be an
S_{\top}
-algebra homomorphism, and it is injective (since, e.g.,
L=\left\{\alpha u\mid\langle{\alpha,\alpha u}\rangle\in\iota(L)\right\}
).

Note that it is crucial that we consider
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
rather than
\mathsf{REL}
here: the above construction would not give an
S_{\top}
-algebra homomorphism if we were not restricting to relations of a certain shape:
\top
would not be preserved.

Corollary 27
.

For all expressions, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{GSL}\models e=f\iff\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}\models e=f\iff\mathsf{KAT}_{T}\vdash e=f

Proof 5.2
.

That
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}\models e=f
entails
\mathsf{GSL}\models e=f
is a direct consequence of Proposition
26
. That
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}\vdash e=f
entails
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}\models e=f
follows from the soundness of
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
axioms w.r.t.
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
. We conclude by Theorem
6
.

Similarly to
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
, we can define a class
\mathsf{GSL^{\prime}}
of
S_{\top}
-algebras which is closed under taking subalgebras and where
\top
is not necessarily the full language. However, unlike with
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
and
\mathsf{REL}
, the equational theory of
\mathsf{GSL^{\prime}}
coincides with that of
\mathsf{GSL}
(and
\mathsf{REL^{\prime}}
). Indeed the axioms of
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
remain sound for
\mathsf{GSL^{\prime}}
.

6.
Conclusion

We have proved completeness of two axiomatic systems about regular expressions with tests and top,
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
and
\mathsf{KAT}_{F}
, with respect to guarded string language models and relational models, respectively.
We have established that the corresponding equational theories are
PSpace
-complete, and that they can be reconciled by allowing relational models where top is only a maximal element, not necessarily the full relation.

For
\mathsf{KAT}_{F}
, we have proved a graph-theoretical characterisation of the equational theory of binary relations, we have established a relationship between this graph-theoretical characterisation and a notion of closed guarded string language, and we have used an extension of the theory of finite monoid recognition for guarded string languages.

Related work

Zhang et al. gave a completeness result for
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
, in terms of guarded string languages
[
ZdAG22
, Theorem 9]
. They observed that this axiomatisation is incomplete for
\mathsf{REL}
, that it does not suffice to properly express
incorrectness triples
, and they left the existence of a complete axiomatisation for relational models open.
Our Theorem
24
gives a positive answer to this question.

For the theory
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
, the main completeness results of Zhang et al.
[
ZdAG22
, Theorems 7 and 9]
are wrong: the model of guarded strings they designed equates too many expressions (namely,
\Sigma^{*}
and
\top
—see Remark
7
).
Our Theorem
6
uses a slightly different language model and yields a linear reduction from
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
to
\mathsf{KAT}
, so that, e.g.,
[
ZdAG22
, Theorem 10]
about the complexity of
\mathsf{KAT}_{T}
remains true.

Zhang et al. also gave a completeness result w.r.t. generalised relational models
[
ZdAG22
, Theorem 8]
. Their proof is problematic because it relies on their Theorem 7, but the key idea remains valid: adapting Pratt’s trick to embed language models into relational ones. We use the very same technique to obtain Corollary
27
.

We recently proved a completeness result for
\mathsf{KAT}_{F}

[
PRW24
, Section 7]
, w.r.t. a notion of closed language defined differently than in the present work. There the emphasis is on modularity, complexity aspects are not considered, and KAT with a top element is an example among others. The closed language model defined there is most probably equivalent to the one we use in the present paper (using arguments likes the one developed in
[
PRW24
, Appendix C]
for plain KAT, which we would like to generalise in the future).
The two completeness proofs are rather different. The present one is more direct, uses guarded strings and finite monoids, and yields a
PSpace
algorithm. In contrast, the one in
[
PRW24
]
avoids guarded strings but requires more general results about Kleene algebra with hypotheses, and does not give any reasonable algorithm. Our characterisations of the equational theory of
\mathsf{REL}
(Theorem
9
, Proposition
13
) also lie out of the scope of
[
PRW24
]
.

Future work

A Hoare triple
\left\{\alpha\right\}e\left\{\beta\right\}
for partial correctness can be encoded in KAT as an equation

\alpha\cdot e\cdot\lnot\beta=0

[
Koz00
]
. Since hypotheses of the more general shape
e=0
can be incorporated into the equational theory of KAT
[
Coh94
,
HK02
]
, one can automate reasoning about partial correctness
[
Pou13
]
.

Zhang et al.
[
ZdAG22
]
have shown how to encode an incorrectness triple
[\alpha]e[\beta]
as an inequation
\beta\leq\top\cdot\alpha\cdot e
. A natural question is whether such hypotheses can also be eliminated in
\mathsf{KAT}_{F}
, in order to automate reasoning about incorrectness triples.
The modular tools we developed in
[
PRW21
,
PRW24
]
could prove useful, provided we find a way to extract efficient algorithms from the resulting reductions.

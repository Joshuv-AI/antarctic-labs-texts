1.
Introduction

Hilbert asked whether there exists a deterministic algorithm for determining the existence of integral solutions to diophantine equations with integer coefficients. In other words, the problem asks whether there exists a Turing machine which takes as input a finite set of polynomial equations over
\mathbb{Z}
and decides whether they have nontrivial solutions in
\mathbb{Z}
. If no such Turing machine does exist, then it is said that Hilbert’s tenth problem is unsolvable. For further details, we refer to
[
Shl07
]
. A celebrated result due to Matiyasevich
[
Mat70
]
, building upon previous work of Davis, Putnam and Robinson
[
DPR61
]
, shows that Hilbert’s tenth problem for diophantine equations with integer coefficients is unsolvable. One of the main open problems in the area is to study the generalization of Hilbert’s tenth problem to number rings. For a number field
L
, generally this is studied by showing that
\mathbb{Z}
is a

integrally diophantine
subset of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, in the sense of Definition
2.2
. It is then known that
\mathcal{O}_{L}
inherits the unsolvability of Hilbert’s tenth problem from
\mathbb{Z}
. The following conjecture predicts that Hilbert’s tenth problem is unsolvable for all rings of integers of number fields.

Conjecture 1.1

(Denef-Lipshitz)
.

For any number field
L
,
L/\mathbb{Q}
is an integrally diophantine extension.

Classical results in the field show that Conjecture
1.1
above
is true for the following classes of number fields
L
:

•

The conjecture holds when
L
is either totally real or a quadratic extension of a totally real number field (cf.
[
Den80
,
DL78
]
),

•

L
has exactly one complex place (cf.
[
Phe88
,
Shl89
,
Vid
]
),

•

L/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian (cf.
[
SS89
]
),

•

[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=4
,
L
is not totally real and
L/\mathbb{Q}
has a proper intermediate field (cf.
[
DL78
]
).

Poonen
[
Poo02
]
, Cornelissen-Pheidas-Zahidi
[

]
and Shlapentokh

subsequently established a geometric criterion for Conjecture

. In establishing some new cases of Conjecture

Let

1.2.
Method of proof

Let us briefly describe the method of proof of the Theorem
1.4
. Let
E_{/K}
be an elliptic curve and
p
be a prime such that
E
has good ordinary reduction at the primes of
K
above
p
. Let
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
be the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E
over
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
. We show that if there exists an elliptic curve
E
for which the following conditions hold

(1)

E
has good ordinary reduction at the primes
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{*}
,

(2)

\operatorname{rank}E(K)>0
,

(3)

the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is a cofinitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module, and

(4)

the
\lambda
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is equal to
\operatorname{rank}E(K)
,

then,
K_{n}/\mathbb{Q}
is integrally diophantine for all
n
. The proof relies on basic properties of the
\lambda
-invariant and Theorem
1.2
. We refer to Theorem
4.1
and its proof for further details. In order to construct an elliptic curve
E_{/K}
such that the above conditions are satisfied, we exploit the Iwasawa theory of congruences between elliptic curves, initiated by Greenberg-Vatsal
[
GV00
]
and further refined by Kundu-Ray
[
KR22
, section 6]
. This refinement does not only generalize the results of Greenberg and Vatsal to non-cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions, but it gives a more accurate description of the primes that actually contribute to the change in
\lambda
-invariant. This optimal set of primes is the set
\Sigma(E_{1})\cup\Sigma(E_{2})
, and for the example considered in section
5
, this set is strictly smaller than the set of all primes of bad reduction for one or both elliptic curves. Condition (
5
) stipulates that each of primes
v\in\Sigma(E_{1})\cup\Sigma(E_{2})
must be finitely decomposed in
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
, which is the case for the example considered. It is thus ideal for the set
\Sigma(E_{1})\cup\Sigma(E_{2})
for which this assumption is made, to be as small as possible.

We consider two elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
defined over
K
that are
p
-congruent satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.4
. Since
\operatorname{rank}E_{1}(K)=0
, and the
p
-primary part of
\Sh(E_{1}/K)
is finite (in fact, it is assumed to be zero), it follows that the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E_{1}
over
K
is finite. By a well known result of Greenberg that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is a cotorsion module over the Iwasawa algebra (cf. Proposition
2.1
). Furthermore, the assumptions (
6
)-(
8
) imply that the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
are zero (cf. Corollary
4.6
). Such a criterion relies on a formula for the Euler characteristic over the anticyclotomic extension, due to Van Order
[
vO15
]
. At this step, it is crucial for
E_{1}
to have rank
0
over
K
. We set
E=E_{2}
, which is an elliptic curve with positive rank over
K
, and show that the above conditions are satisfied. The congruence with the rank
0
elliptic curve
E_{1}
, along with the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
imply that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is a cofinitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module satisfying the three conditions above. At this step we make use of Theorem
3.6
, which essentially extends the results of
[
KR22
, section 6]
to

.

1.3.
Outlook/ future directions

Our results show that Iwasawa theory can be used as a novel tool to prove new interesting cases of Conjecture
1.1
. It is natural to extend such investigations to more general contexts that involve
p
-adic Lie extensions of dimension
d>1
. Let us illustrate this by considering a special case: the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
-extension
\widetilde{K}_{\infty}
of an imaginary quadratic field
K
. For
n\geq 0
, let
\widetilde{K}_{n}
denote the
n
-th layer, i.e., the extension of
K
, contained in
\widetilde{K}_{\infty}
for which
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}_{n}/K)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
p
. When the root number of the Hasse-Weil L-function of
E_{/K}
is
+1
, then, a result of Cornut and Vatsal
[
CV07
]
shows that the Mordell-Weil rank of
E(\widetilde{K}_{n})
is bounded as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Suppose that
\operatorname{rank}E(K)>0
and the root number of
E_{/K}
is equal to
+1
. Following Mazur and Rubin, we say that the Mordell-Weil rank is
diophantine stable
in
\widetilde{K}_{\infty}
if

\operatorname{rank}E(\widetilde{K}_{n})=\operatorname{rank}E(K)

for all
n
. Then, from Theorem
1.2
, it is evident that this property implies that Conjecture
1.1
is satisfied for all number fields
\widetilde{K}_{n}
. Generalized upper bounds for the asymptotic growth of the rank of the Mordell–Weil group of an abelian variety in a tower of number fields contained in a
p
-adic Lie extension, were proven by Harris
[
Har00
]
. The precise order of growth in Mordell-Weil ranks is however not well understood
[
HL21
, Question following Proposition 4.7]
. It is our expectation that the search for diophantine stability in the context of Iwasawa theory would lead to interesting theoretical and computational developments.

1.4.
Organization

Including the introduction, the paper consists of five sections. In section

1.4
through an explicit example, and prove Theorem
1.5
as a consequence.

Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves

In this section, we review some necessary background. For a more comprehensive introduction to the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves, the reader is referred to
[
Coa00
, Chapter 1]
. Throughout,
p
is an odd prime number and
E
shall denote an elliptic curve over a number field
K
. We assume that
E
has good ordinary reduction at the primes
v|p
. We fix an algebraic closure
\bar{K}
of
K
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the
p
-adic integers. An infinite Galois extension
K_{\infty}/K
is said to be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension if there is a topological isomorphism of groups
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, and set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)
. The
Iwasawa algebra
is the completed group ring defined as follows

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}].

Fixing a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
yields the usual isomorphism
\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
with the ring of integral
p
-adic power series. This isomorphism identifies the group-like element
\gamma
with
(T+1)
.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, set
K_{n}
to denote the
n
-th layer of the associated tower. This is the extension
K_{n}/K
such that
K_{n}\subset K_{\infty}
and
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n}/K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, there is an infinite tower of number fields

K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\dots\subset K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}\subset\dots,

where
K=K_{0}
. We shall refer to such a tower as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
K
.

Let
F/K
be a number field extension. Given a finite prime
v
of
K
, set
v(F)
to denote the set of primes of
F
that lie above
v
. We set

J_{v}(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{w\in v(F)}H^{1}(F_{w},E)[p^{\infty}],

and note that there is a natural map

\iota_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}(\bar{F}/F,E[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow J_{v}(E/F)

which factors through the restriction maps

H^{1}(\bar{F}/F,E[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w\in v(F)}H^{1}(F_{w},E[p^{\infty}]).

The
p
-primary Selmer group of
E/F
is defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(\bar{F}/F,E[p^{\infty}])\xrightarrow{\iota}\prod_{v}J_{v}(E/F)\right),

where the map
\iota
is induced by the maps
\iota_{v}
, and the product ranges over all primes
v
of
K
.

We set
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
to denote the direct limit
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})
, with respect to restriction maps. Let
S
be a set of primes of
K
containing the primes above
p
and the primes at which
E
has bad reduction. Denote by
K_{S}
the maximal algebraic extension of
K
in which all primes
v\notin S
are unramified. For
v\in S
, set
J_{v}(E/K_{\infty})
to be the direct limit
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{v}(E/K_{n})
. The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
is described as follows

(2.1)

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(K_{S}/K_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}J_{v}(E/K_{\infty})\right).

Given a module
M
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we set
M^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
to denote its
Pontryagin dual
. A module
M
over
\Lambda
is said to be cofinitely generated (resp. cotorsion) over
\Lambda
if
M^{\vee}
is finitely generated (resp. torsion) over
\Lambda
. We discuss conditions for
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
to be cotorsion module over
\Lambda
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
and let
p
be an odd prime number. Let
K_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
. Assume that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

E
has good ordinary reduction at the primes
v|p
,

(2)

the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
is finite.

Then the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
is a cotorsion module over
\Lambda
.

Proof.

The above result is
[
Gre01
, Corollary 4.9]
.
∎

The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
is cofinitely generated over
\Lambda
, and we may define the associated Iwasawa invariants
\mu_{p}(E/K_{\infty})
and
\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\infty})
as follows. Setting
M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
, the module
M^{\vee}
is a finitely generated torsion module over
\Lambda
. A map
M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
of
\Lambda
-modules is a pseudo-isomorphism if the kernel and cokernel are both finite. We depict this relation by writing
M_{1}\sim M_{2}
. A polynomial
f(T)\in\Lambda
is said to be distinguished if it is monic and its non-leading coefficients are all divisible by
p
. By the structure theorem for finitely generated torsion modules over
\Lambda

[
Was97
, Theorem 13.12]
,

(2.2)

M^{\vee}\sim\Lambda^{r}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{n_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{m_{j}})\right),

where
r,s,t,n_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
f_{j}(T)
are all irreducible distinguished polynomials. The
\mu
-invariant is defined as follows

\mu_{p}(E/K_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}&0\text{ if }s=0;\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\text{ if }s>0.\\
\end{cases}

The
\lambda
-invariant is defined as follows

\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}&0\text{ if }t=0;\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{t}m_{j}\operatorname{deg}f_{j}(T)\text{ if }t>0.\\
\end{cases}

Suppose that
M
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
, i.e.,
r=0
. It is easy to see that
\mu_{p}(E/K_{\infty})=0
if and only if
M^{\vee}
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Furthermore, if these equivalent conditions are satisfied, then,

(2.3)

\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\infty})=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(M^{\vee}\right).

From here on in, we assume that
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
is an imaginary quadratic field in which
p
splits into primes
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{*}
. Here,
D>0
is a squarefree integer. There are precisely two
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
K
that are Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, namely, the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
and the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
. The cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension is abelian over
\mathbb{Q}
, while the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension is pro-dihedral over
\mathbb{Q}
. Given a finite prime
v
, set
v(K_{\operatorname{anti}})
to denote the set of primes
w
of
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
that lie above
v
.

It conveniences us to work with Selmer groups over
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
defined by Greenberg’s local conditions. It follows (for instance) from
[
KR22
, Lemma 6.5]
that this definition coincides with that of (
2.1
). Given a finite prime
v\nmid p
of
K
, set

\mathcal{H}_{v}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{w\in v(K_{\operatorname{anti}})}H^{1}(K_{\operatorname{anti},w},E)[p^{\infty}].

Here,
K_{\operatorname{anti},w}
is taken to be the union of local fields
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}K_{n,w}
. Let
v|p
be a prime, and
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T_{p}(E)
be the
p
-adic Tate-module of
E
, viewed as a module over
\operatorname{G}_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v})
. Since
E
is assumed to have good ordinary reduction at
v
,
T
fits into a short exact sequence of
\operatorname{G}_{v}
-modules

0\rightarrow T^{+}\rightarrow T\rightarrow T^{-}\rightarrow 0.

The modules
T^{\pm}
are free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules of rank
1
, and the action of
\operatorname{G}_{v}
on
T^{-}
is unramified. We set
D_{v}
to denote the
p
-primary
\operatorname{G}_{v}
-module defined by
D_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T^{-}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
. For
w\in v(K_{\operatorname{anti}})
, set

L_{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(K_{\operatorname{anti},w},E[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{w},D_{v})\right),

where
\operatorname{I}_{w}
is the inertia subgroup of the absolute Galois group of
K_{\operatorname{anti},w}
. The map above is induced from the natural map
E[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow D_{v}
obtained by tensoring
T\rightarrow T^{-}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. With respect to the above notation, for
v|p
, we define the local Greenberg Selmer condition as follows

\mathcal{H}_{v}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{w\in v(K_{\operatorname{anti}})}H^{1}(K_{\operatorname{anti},w},E[p^{\infty}])/L_{w}.

Note that this product can be infinite. The Selmer group is then described as follows

(2.4)

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(K_{S}/K_{\operatorname{anti}},E[p^{\infty}])\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}\mathcal{H}_{v}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\right\}.

2.2.
Integrally diophantine extensions and Shlapentokh’s criterion

In this section, we briefly recall the notion of an integrally diophantine extension. For further details, the reader is referred to
[
Shl07
, Section 1.2]
. Let
A
be a commutative ring with identity, and
A^{n}
be the free
A
-module of rank
n
, consisting of tuples
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
with entries in
A
. Let
m
and
n
be positive integers and let
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in A^{m}
. Denote by
(a,b)\in A^{n+m}
the tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
. Given a finite set of polynomials,
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
, we set

\mathcal{F}(a;F_{1},\dots,F_{k})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{b\in A^{m}\mid F_{i}(a,b)=0\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,k\}.

Definition 2.2
.

A subset
S
of
A^{n}
is a
diophantine subset
of
A^{n}
if for some
m\geq 1
, there are polynomials
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\in A[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
such that
S
consists of all
a\in A^{n}
for which the set
\mathcal{F}(a;F_{1},\dots,F_{k})
is nonempty.

Definition 2.3
.

An extension of number fields
L/K
is said to be
integrally diophantine
if
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a diophantine subset of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Let
L^{\prime},L
and
K
be number fields such that

L^{\prime}\supseteq L\supseteq K.

Suppose that
L^{\prime}/L
and
L/K
are integrally diophantine extensions. Then, it is a well known fact that
L^{\prime}/K
is an integrally diophantine extension. Indeed, this is a special case of
[
Shl07
, Theorem 2.1.15]
.

3.
Iwasawa theory of congruences

Building on the results of Greenberg-Vatsal
[
GV00
]
and Kundu-Ray
[
KR22
]
, we describe the effect of congruences between elliptic curves on Selmer groups over anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. The results apply in what is called the
definite case
, i.e., the setting in which the Selmer groups are cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra. The results shall be applied to a congruence between an elliptic curve of rank
0
and an elliptic curve of positive rank. In the setting in which we work, both the Selmer groups over
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
shall be cofinitely generated as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules. We extend the results of
[
KR22
, section 6]
(which are proven for
p\geq 5
) to include the case
p=3
as well. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be elliptic curves defined over
K
. We let
E_{i}[p]
denote the
p
-torsion subgroup of
E_{i}(\bar{K})
, equipped with the natural action of the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{G}_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
. We say that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are
p
-congruent if
E_{1}[p]
is isomorphic to
E_{2}[p]
as modules over
\operatorname{G}_{K}
. Let
\bar{\rho}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{K}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
denote the Galois representation associated to
E_{i}[p]
. Assume that
p
splits in
K
, and let
\Sigma_{p}=\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}^{*}\}
be the primes of
K
that lie above
p
. We assume that
E_{1}(K)[p]=E_{2}(K)[p]=0
. In other words, the Galois modules
E_{i}[p]
do not contain the trivial module as a submodule. Note that this condition is automatically satisfied when the Galois representation
\bar{\rho}_{i}
is irreducible. Since it is assumed that
\bar{\rho}_{1}
is isomorphic to
\bar{\rho}_{2}
, it follows that
E_{1}(K)[p]=0
if and only if
E_{2}(K)[p]=0
. We assume that both elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have good ordinary reduction at all primes
v\in\Sigma_{p}
. Let
\mathfrak{T}
be the set of primes
v
of
K
such that either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
(or both) have bad reduction at
v
. Set
K^{\prime}
to denote
K(\mu_{p})
.

Definition 3.1
.

For
i=1,2
, the set
\Sigma(E_{i})
consists of primes
v\in\mathfrak{T}
such that the following conditions is satisfied.

(1)

If the elliptic curve
E_{i}
has good reduction at
v
, then,
p\mid\#E_{i}(\mathbb{F}_{v})
.

(2)

If the elliptic curve
E_{i}
has bad reduction at
v
, and
p\geq 5
, then
E_{i}
has split multiplicative reduction at some prime
w|v
of
K^{\prime}
.

(3)

If the elliptic curve
E_{i}
has bad reduction at
v
, and
p=3
, then, at some prime
w|v
of
K^{\prime}
, the elliptic curve
E_{i}
has either split multiplicative reduction or has additive reduction of type
\rm{IV}
or
\rm{IV}^{*}
.

We set
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Sigma(E_{1})\cup\Sigma(E_{2})
and
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathfrak{T}\cup\Sigma_{p}
.

Note that
\Sigma
is a subset of
S
. For
i=1,2
, we define the imprimitive Selmer group as follows

(3.1)

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(K_{\operatorname{anti}},E_{i}[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S\backslash\Sigma}\mathcal{H}_{v}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\right).

In other words, we relax the local conditions at the primes
v\in\Sigma
. There is one crucial difference between the cyclotomic and anticyclotomic extensions. It is easy to see that all primes are finitely decomposed in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension (cf.
[
Bri07
, Proposition 2]
). This is not the case for other
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. Let
\ell
be a prime number and
v|\ell
be a prime of
K
that lies above
\ell
. By an application of class field theory,
v
is infinitely decomposed in
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
if and only if
\ell\neq p
and
\ell
is inert or ramified in
K
(cf.
[
Bri07
, p.2132, ll. 8-10]
). Throughout this section, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 3.2
.

Assume that all primes
v\in\Sigma
are finitely decomposed in
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
. Equivalently, we assume that
\ell
splits into a product of two primes of
K
for every prime
\ell
divisible by some
v\in\Sigma
.

For
v\in S
, we refer to
[
KR22
, section 6]
for the definition of the local condition
\mathcal{H}_{v}(E[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
.
The
residual imprimitive Selmer group
is defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(K_{S}/K_{\operatorname{anti}},E[p^{\infty}])\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S\backslash\Sigma}\mathcal{H}_{v}(E[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\right\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
v\in S\backslash\Sigma
, the natural map

(3.2)

\mathcal{H}_{v}(E[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{v}(E[p^{\infty}]/K_{\operatorname{anti}})[p]

is injective.

Proof.

When
p\geq 5
, the above result is
[
KR22
, Lemma 6.10]
. Therefore, we consider the case when
p=3
.

First, we assume that
v|p
. In this case, the result follows from part (1) in the proof of Lemma 6.10 from
loc. cit
. Next, suppose that
v\nmid p
is a prime at which
E
has good reduction. The injectivity of the map follows from part (2) in the proof of the above mentioned Lemma. The only case to consider therefore is when
v\nmid p
is a prime at which
E
has bad reduction. Recall that
K^{\prime}=K(\mu_{p})
, and set
K^{\prime}_{\operatorname{anti}}=K^{\prime}\cdot K_{\operatorname{anti}}
. It follows from the proof of (3) of Lemma 6.10 of
loc. cit.
that the map (
3.2
) is injective if
E(K^{\prime}_{\operatorname{anti},w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]=0
for all primes
w^{\prime}|v
of
K^{\prime}_{\operatorname{anti}}
. Let
w
be a prime of
K^{\prime}
such that
w|v
. Let
w^{\prime}|w
be a prime of
K^{\prime}_{\operatorname{anti}}
. Since
K^{\prime}_{\operatorname{anti},w^{\prime}}/K^{\prime}_{w}
is a pro-
p
extension, it follows from
[
KR22
, Lemma 6.9]
that

E(K^{\prime}_{w})[p^{\infty}]=0\Rightarrow E(K^{\prime}_{\operatorname{anti},w^{\prime}})[p^{\infty}]=0.

Therefore, it suffices to show that
E(K^{\prime}_{w})[p^{\infty}]=0
for all
w|v
of
K^{\prime}
. We recall part (3) of the Definition
3.1
. Note that in this case
p=3
,
v\notin\Sigma
and
E
is assumed to have bad reduction at
v
. Therefore,
E
has non-split multiplicative reduction or additive reduction of Kodaira type
I\notin\{\rm{IV},\rm{IV}^{*}\}
at all primes
w|v
of
K^{\prime}
. Let
E_{0}(K^{\prime}_{w})
be the subgroup of
E
consisting of points of non-singular reduction. It follows from the proof of
[
HM99
, Proposition 5.1 (iii)]
that
E_{0}(K^{\prime}_{w})[p^{\infty}]=0
. It suffices to show that
p\nmid[E(K^{\prime}_{w})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{0}(K^{\prime}_{w})]
for all primes
w|v
of
K^{\prime}
. Since
p=3
, and
E
has non-split multiplicative reduction or additive reduction of Kodaira type
I\notin\{\rm{IV},\rm{IV}^{*}\}
, the result follows from
[
Sil94
, 4th row of Table 4.1, p. 365]
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
E
be either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
and recall that
\Sigma=\Sigma(E_{1})\cup\Sigma(E_{2})
. Assume that
E(K)[p]=0
. Then, the natural map

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})[p]

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The result follows verbatim from the proof of
[
KR22
, Proposition 6.11]
and Lemma
3.3
.
∎

We introduce some further notation. Let
E
be either
E_{1}
or
E_{2}
. Recall that by the Assumption
3.2
, each prime
v\in\Sigma
is finitely decomposed in
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
. For
v\in\Sigma
, let
\ell
be the prime number for which
v|\ell
. Note that
\ell\neq p
, since both elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have good reduction at all primes
v\in\Sigma_{p}
. Denote by
v^{*}
the conjugate prime, so that
\ell\mathcal{O}_{K}=vv^{*}
. We identify
K_{v}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, and set
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\operatorname{nr}}
to denote the unramified
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. Recall that
s_{v}
is the number of primes of
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
that lie above
v
. We note that
[
Bri07
, Theorem 2, p. 2133]
gives an explicit algorithm to compute
s_{v}
. Let
\sigma_{E}^{(v)}
denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-corank of
\mathcal{H}_{v}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
, for
v\in\Sigma
. Recall that
d_{v}(E)
is the multiplicity of
\ell^{-1}
as a root of the local
L
-factor
P_{v}(X)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
v\in\Sigma
, the
\mu
-invariant of
\mathcal{H}_{v}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is
0
and
\sigma_{E}^{(v)}=s_{v}d_{v}(E)
.

Proof.

The above result follows from the proof of
[
GV00
, Proposition 2.4]
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
K
be an imaginary quadratic field in which
p
splits. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two elliptic curves defined over
K
. Assume that the following conditions hold

(1)

Both elliptic curves have good ordinary reduction at the primes
v\in\Sigma_{p}
.

(2)

The elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are
p
-congruent. In other words,
E_{1}[p]
and
E_{2}[p]
are isomorphic as modules over
\operatorname{G}_{K}
.

(3)

We require that
E_{1}(K)[p]=E_{2}(K)[p]=0
. (This assumption is clearly satisfied when the residual representations
\bar{\rho}_{i}
on
E_{i}[p]
are irreducible as modules over
\operatorname{G}_{K}
.)

(4)

Assumption
3.2
is satified. In other words, all primes
v\in\Sigma
(cf. Definition
3.1
) are finitely decomposed in
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
.

Then, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{2}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu=0
. Furthermore, suppose that indeed both Selmer groups
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
are cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu=0
. Then, we have that

\lambda_{p}(E_{2}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})-\lambda_{p}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=\sum_{v\in\Sigma}\left(\sigma_{E_{1}}^{(v)}-\sigma_{E_{2}}^{(v)}\right)=\sum_{v\in\Sigma}s_{v}\left(d_{v}(E_{1})-d_{v}(E_{2})\right).

Proof.

When
p\geq 5
, the above result follows from
[
KR22
, Theorem 6.14, Proposition 6.19]
. When
p=3
, the same proof applies verbatim from Proposition
3.4
, the key ingredient being Lemma
3.3
. We provide a sketch of the proof.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated
\Lambda
-module with
\mu
-invariant
\mu(M)
and
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda(M)
. Then, we recall that there is a decomposition map (
2.2
)

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{\vee}\rightarrow\Lambda^{r}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{n_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{m_{j}})\right)

which is a map of
\Lambda
-modules whose kernel and cokernel are finite. The
\mu
-invariant
\mu(M)
is defined to be the sum
\sum_{i}n_{i}
and the
\lambda
-invariant is
\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{j}m_{j}\operatorname{deg}f_{j}
.
It follows that

M\text{ is cotorsion with }\mu(M)=0\Leftrightarrow M\text{ is cofinitely generated as a }\mathbb{Z}_{p}\text{-module.}

Moreover, if these conditions are satisfied, then,

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M=\lambda(M).

We note that
M
is cofinitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module if and only if
M[p]
is finite. Assume that
M
has no proper
\Lambda
-submodules of finite index, then,
M^{\vee}
has no non-zero finite
\Lambda
-submodules. Then, since the kernel of
\Phi
is finite, it must be
0
. Since
M^{\vee}
is torsion, it follows that
r=0
, and hence the target of
\Phi
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
\lambda(M)
. Since
\operatorname{cok}\Phi
is finite and
\Phi
is injective, it follows that
M^{\vee}
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank equal to
\lambda(M)
. In particular, we find that

\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}M[p]=\lambda(M).

We specialize the above discussion to the
\Sigma
-imprimitive Selmer groups

M_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}}).

It follows from Proposition
3.4
that

M_{i}[p]\simeq\operatorname{Sel}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}[p]/K_{\operatorname{anti}}).

It thus follows that
M_{1}[p]\simeq M_{2}[p]
, for further details, cf.
[
KR22
, Lemma 6.12]
. Take
\mu_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
(resp.
\lambda_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
) to denote
\mu(M_{i})
(resp.
\lambda(M_{i})
). It is shown that
M_{i}^{\vee}
has no non-zero finite
\Lambda
-submodules, cf.
[
KR22
, Proposition 6.18]
. It follows from the discussion above that

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\text{ is cotorsion with }\mu_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=0\\
\Leftrightarrow&\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E_{2}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\text{ is cotorsion with }\mu_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{2}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=0.\end{split}

Assume that the above equivalent conditions are satisfied. The equality
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}M_{1}[p]=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}M_{2}[p]
implies that

\lambda_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{1}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=\lambda_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{2}/K_{\operatorname{anti}}).

Next, we relate the imprimitive Iwasawa invariants
\mu_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
and
\lambda_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
to the primitive Iwasawa invariants
\mu_{p}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
and
\lambda_{p}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
. There is a natural short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Sigma}\mathcal{H}_{v}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\rightarrow 0,

cf.
[
KR22
, Eqn. (6.1) and Lemma 3.4]
. Proposition
3.5
asserts that the
\mu
-invariant of
\mathcal{H}_{v}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is
0
and
\sigma_{E_{i}}^{(v)}=s_{v}d_{v}(E_{i})
. It follows that

\mu_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=\mu_{p}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})

and

\begin{split}\lambda_{p}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=&\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}^{\Sigma}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\\
=&\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})+\sum_{v\in\Sigma}\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{v}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})\right)\\
=&\lambda_{p}(E_{i}/K_{\operatorname{anti}})+\sum_{v\in\Sigma}\sigma_{E_{i}}^{(v)}.\end{split}

From the above relations, we obtain the result.
∎

4.
A general criterion

Let
p
be an odd prime,
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
be an imaginary quadratic field in which
p
splits, and
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
denote the anticyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we recall that
K_{n}
is
n
-th layer in
K_{\operatorname{anti}}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{*}
be the primes of
K
that lie above
p
.

Theorem 4.1
.

With respect to notation above, suppose that there exists an elliptic curve
E_{/K}
such that all of the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

the elliptic curve
E
has good ordinary reduction at the primes
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{*}
,

(2)

the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
with
\mu_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=0
,

(3)

the rank of the Mordell-Weil group
E(K)
is positive,

(4)

\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=\operatorname{rank}E(K)
.

Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}
.

Proof.

It follows from the structure theory of
\Lambda
-modules that condition (
2
) above implies that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module and

\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})^{\vee}\right).

The kernel of the natural restriction map

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})

is finite (cf.
[
Gre01
, Theorem 4.1]
), and therefore,

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})^{\vee}\right)\leq\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}}).

Note that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})
fits into a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(K_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})\rightarrow\Sh(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0,

where
\Sh(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is the
p
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E/K_{n}
. Therefore, we deduce that for all
n\geq 0
,

\operatorname{rank}E(K_{n})\leq\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})^{\vee}\right)\leq\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}}).

On the other hand, it is assumed that
\lambda_{p}(E/K_{\operatorname{anti}})=\operatorname{rank}E(K)
. Therefore, we find that
\operatorname{rank}E(K_{n})=\operatorname{rank}E(K)
. Since,
\operatorname{rank}E(K)>0
, it follows from Theorem
1.2
that
K_{n}/K
is integrally diophantine. Since
K/\mathbb{Q}
is integrally diophantine, it follows that for all
n
,
K_{n}/\mathbb{Q}
is integrally diophantine. Therefore, for all
n
, Hilbert’s tenth problem has a negative answer for
\mathcal{O}_{K_{n}}
.
∎

In order to construct an elliptic curve
E
that satisfies the above conditions, we begin with two elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
that are both defined over the rational numbers. We assume that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are
p
-congruent, i.e., the Galois modules
E_{1}[p]
and
E_{2}[p]
are isomorphic.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and

and
\mathfrak{p}^{*}

and

1. Introduction

The Bateman function, defined by the integral

k_{\nu}(x)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos(x\tan u-\nu u)\,du,

(1.1)

was introduced by Bateman in 1931
[
3
]
as a solution of the ordinary differential equation

xu^{\prime\prime}(x)=(x-\nu)u(x)

for non-negative integer values of
\nu

arising in von Kármán’s theory of turbulent flows. An analogous integral, appearing in the problem of surface waves, was introduced by Havelock
[
4
]
as

h_{\nu}(x)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin(x\tan u-\nu u)\,du.

(1.2)

An excellent and detailed survey of the properties of these two functions, including certain generalisations and integrals, has recently been given by Apelblat, Consiglio and Mainardi
[
2
]
. Expressions for
k_{n}(x)
when
n=0,1,2,\ldots
are listed in
[
2
, §§2, 3]
and involve
e^{-x}
multiplied by a polynomial in
x
of degree
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}n
for even values of
n
and the modified Bessel function of the second kind for odd values of
n
. Expressions for
h_{n}(x)
are more complicated
[
2
, §3]
and involve the logarithmic integral li
(e^{x})
, where li
(z)=\int_{0}^{z}dt/\log\,t
.

The Bateman function can be expressed in terms of the confluent hypergeometric function
U(a,b,z)
, which is given in

[
1
, p. 510]
as

k_{\nu}(x)=\frac{e^{-x}}{\Gamma(1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)}\,U(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu,0,2x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)}\,W_{\nu/2,1/2}(2x)\qquad(x>0),\

(1.3)

where
W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)
is the Whittaker function
[
6
, p. 334]
. In the case of negative values of
x
, we have

\displaystyle k_{\nu}(-x)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos(x\tan u+\nu u)\,du

(1.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!e^{-x}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu\,U(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu,0,2x)\qquad(x>0),

from which it is seen that
k_{2n}(-x)=0
,
n=0,1,2,\ldots\
.

The asymptotic expansions of
k_{\nu}(x)
for
x\to\pm\infty
and fixed order
\nu
follow from (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) by using the well-known expansion for
U(a,b,z)

[
6
, (13.7.10)]
to find

k_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{(2x)^{\nu/2}e^{-x}}{\Gamma(1+\nu/2)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)_{k}(1-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)_{k}}{k!(2x)^{k}}\qquad(x\to+\infty)

(1.5)

k_{\nu}(-x)\sim(2x)^{-\nu/2}e^{-x}\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)_{k}(1+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\nu)_{k}}{k!(2x)^{k}}\qquad(x\to+\infty),

(1.6)

where
(a)_{k}=\Gamma(a+k)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol. The expansions of the Havelock function for
x\to\pm\infty
and fixed
\nu
are given by (for details see Appendix A)

h_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{2}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\nu)}{x^{k}},\qquad h_{\nu}(-x)\sim\frac{2}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k-1}c_{k}(\nu)}{x^{k}}

(1.7)

as
x\to+\infty
, where an exponentially small contribution of
O(e^{-x})
has been neglected in each case. The first two coefficients are
c_{0}(\nu)=1
,
c_{1}(\nu)=\nu
; the coefficients
c_{k}(\nu)
are listed in the appendix for
k\leq 8
.
It is seen that the two functions have completely different asymptotic behaviours: the Bateman function is
exponentially decaying as
x\to\pm\infty
whereas the Havelock function possesses an algebraic decay of O
(x^{-1})
.

In this paper we consider the asymptotic expansions of
k_{\nu}(x)
and
h_{\nu}(x)
of large argument and order. We shall suppose throughout that
x
and
\nu
are real variables and, in the case
x>0
, set
\nu=ax
, where
a>0
is a fixed parameter. In the case of
k_{\nu}(x)
, it would, of course, be possible to exploit the known asymptotics of the Whittaker function
W_{\kappa,\mu}(x)
for large
\kappa>0
and finite
\mu
uniformly valid in
x\in[\delta,\infty)

[
5
, p. 412, Ex. 7.3]
; see also
[
6
, §13.21]
. However, the resulting expansion and coefficients are complicated and we find it easier here to derive the asymptotics of
k_{\nu}(x)
and
h_{\nu}(x)
, and hence also

k_{\nu}(\nu)
and
h_{\nu}(\nu)
, directly by the method of steepest descents applied to the integrals (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
In Sections 2 and 3, we present the details for the case
x\to+\infty
with
\nu=O(x)
and in Section 4 the case
x\to-\infty
with
\nu=O(|x|)
. In Section 5 we display numerical results confirming the accuracy of the different expansions obtained.

is taken over the image of this path in the lower half plane to produce the conjugate result.

Then

\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}e^{x\psi(u)}du=\frac{1}{\pi i}\biggl\{\int_{OA}+\int_{AB}\biggr\}e^{x\psi(u)}\,du,

where

I_{OA}=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{i|u_{0}|}e^{x\psi(u)}du=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{|u_{0}|}e^{-x(\tanh y-ay)}dy.

We now introduce the new variable
w
in a similar manner to that in §3.1 to find

I_{OA}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{w_{0}}e^{-\lambda w}\frac{dy}{dw}\,dw=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{k}(a)\int_{0}^{w_{0}}e^{-\lambda w}w^{2k}dw,

where now
\lambda=x(1-a)
and
w_{0}:=(\tanh|u_{0}|-a|u_{0}|)/(1-a)
. Evaluation of the integrals in terms of the (lower) incomplete gamma function
\gamma(\alpha,z)=\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t}t^{\alpha-1}dt
(
\Re(\alpha)>0
) then yields

I_{OA}=\frac{1}{\pi\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{k}(a)}{\lambda^{2k}}\,\gamma(2k+1,x\Psi(a)),

(3.4)

with
\lambda w_{0}=x\Psi(a)
, where
\Psi(a)
is defined in (
2.6
). A similar argument for the integral involving factor
\exp\{-x\psi(u)\}
taken along the path
[0,-i|u_{0}|]
shows that

\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{-i|u_{0}|}e^{-x\psi(u)}du=-I_{OA}.

(3.5)

The determination of the expansion of
I_{AB}
is similar to that described in §2.2, with one important difference.
Because we have the difference between the contributions from the steepest descent paths
AB
(with
\exp\{x\psi(u)\}
) and
CD
(with
\exp\{-x\psi(u)\}
), we find

I_{AB}-I_{CD}=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{AB}e^{x\psi(u)}du-\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{CD}e^{-x\psi(u)}du

=\frac{e^{-x\Psi(a)}}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xw^{2}}\biggl(\frac{du}{dw}-{\overline{\frac{}{}}{du}{dw}}\biggr)dw,

where the expansion of
du/dw
given in (
2.7
) now has to include the odd-order powers of
w
since integration is along only half of the steepest descent path.
Thus we have

\frac{du}{dw}=\frac{(1-a)^{-1/4}}{\sqrt{a}}\biggl\{\sum_{k\geq 0}{\hat{A}}_{2k}(a)w^{2k}+i\sum_{k\geq 0}(-)^{k}{\hat{A}}_{2k+1}(a)w^{2k+1}\biggr\},

where the first few odd-order coefficients are:

{\hat{A}}_{1}(a)=\frac{3a-2}{3a(1-a)},\quad{\hat{A}}_{3}(a)=\frac{4(9a-4)}{135a^{2}(1-a)^{5/2}},\quad{\hat{A}}_{5}(a)=\frac{32-120a+144a^{2}+189a^{3}}{2835a^{3}(1-a)^{4}},

{\hat{A}}_{7}(a)=\frac{8(16-84a+180a^{2}-207a^{3}+270a^{4})}{25515a^{4}(1-a)^{11/2}},

{\hat{A}}_{9}(a)=\frac{(-35968+242784a-692064a^{2}+1077948a^{3}-1020600a^{4}+1403325a^{5}+400950a^{6})}{37889775a^{5}(1-a)^{7}}~.

(3.6)

Then

I_{AB}-I_{CD}\sim\frac{2(1-a)^{-1/4}}{\pi\sqrt{a}}\,e^{-x\Psi(a)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-)^{k}{\hat{A}}_{2k+1}(a)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xw^{2}}w^{2k+1}dw

=\frac{(1-a)^{-1/4}}{\pi\sqrt{ax}}e^{-x\Psi(a)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}k!{\hat{A}}_{2k+1}(a)}{x^{k}}.

(3.7)

Collecting together the results in (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.7
), we finally obtain:

Theorem 5

\!\!\!.
The asymptotic expansion of
h_{\nu}(x)
for
x\to+\infty
with
\nu=ax
,
a<1
is

h_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{(1-a)^{-1/4}}{\pi\sqrt{ax}}\,e^{-x\Psi(a)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}k!{\hat{A}}_{2k+1}(a)}{x^{k}}\hskip 113.81102pt

\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{2}{\pi x(1-a)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{k}(a)}{x^{2k}}\,\frac{\gamma(2k+1,x\Psi(a))}{(1-a)^{2k}},

(3.8)

where
\Psi(a)
is defined in (
2.9
). The coefficients
{\hat{A}}_{k}(a)
and
C_{k}(a)
are defined in (
2.3
), (
3.6
) and (
3.2
).

[0,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi]

OABC

\!\!\!.
The asymptotic expansions of

and

,
a>0

\displaystyle k_{\nu}(-x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{OA}+I_{OA^{\prime}}+I_{BC}+I_{B^{\prime}C}

\displaystyle\frac{2(1+a)^{-1/4}}{\sqrt{\pi ax}}\,e^{-x\Omega(a)}\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}\,\frac{{\hat{A}}_{2k}(a)}{x^{k}}

6. Concluding remarks

Asymptotic expansions have been derived for the Bateman and Havelock functions
k_{\nu}(x)
and
h_{\nu}(x)
for
x\to\pm\infty
when the order
\nu=a|x|
, with the parameter
a>0
, by application of the method of steepest descents.
In the case
x\to+\infty
, three different cases arise according as
a<1
,
a=1
and
a>1
. In the case
x\to-\infty
. a single expansion holds for
a>0
. The coefficients in these expansions are obtained using an inversion procedure (essentially Lagrange inversion). It is worth mentioning that, although the complexity of the coefficients rapidly increases with increasing order
k
, in the case of specific numerical values for
a=\nu/x

it is possible to generate many more coefficients than those displayed here.
Numerical results employing the coefficients listed in the paper show excellent agreement with the numerically computed values of
k_{\nu}(\pm x)
and
h_{\nu}(\pm x)
.

In the case of the Bateman function, a uniform approximation valid when
a\simeq 1
and
x>0
can be obtained in terms of the Airy function Ai from (
1.3
) and
[
5
, p. 412, Ex. 7.3]
. If we define the quantity
\zeta
by

\displaystyle\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{2}{3}}$}\zeta^{3/2}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\sqrt{1-a}-a\,\mbox{arctanh}\sqrt{1-a}\qquad(0<a\leq 1,\ \zeta\geq 0)

\displaystyle\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{2}{3}}$}(-\zeta)^{3/2}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!a\,\arctan\sqrt{a-1}-\sqrt{a-1}\qquad(a\geq 1,\ \zeta\leq 0),

we obtain the approximation

k_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{2}{a^{1/2}x^{1/3}}\biggl(\frac{\zeta}{1-a}\biggr)^{1/4}\mbox{Ai}(x^{2/3}\zeta)\qquad(x\to+\infty),

(6.1)

when
\nu=ax
with
a\simeq 1
. Use of the asymptotic behaviour of Ai(
z)
given by

\mbox{Ai}(z)\sim\frac{e^{-z}}{2\sqrt{\pi}z^{1/4}},\qquad\mbox{Ai}(-z)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}z^{1/4}}\,\cos\biggl(\frac{2}{3}z^{3/2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\biggr)\qquad(z\to+\infty),

combined with Ai
(0)=\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{3}}$})/(2\cdot 3^{1/6}\pi)
and the fact that
\zeta/(1-a)\to 1
as
a\to 1^{-}
, shows that (
6.1
) reduces to the leading terms of the expansions given Theorems 1, 2 and 3 when
a>1
,
a<1
and
a=1
.

Appendix A: Asymptotic expansion of
h_{\nu}(\pm x)
for
x\to+\infty
and finite
\nu>0

We deform the integration path
[0,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi]
in (
1.2
) into the path depicted in Fig. 2(b), where with
x\to+\infty
,
\nu
fixed the parameter
a=\nu/x\to 0
. The saddle point
u_{0}=i\,\mbox{arctanh}\,\sqrt{1-a}\sim i\log\,(2/\sqrt{a})
as
a\to 0
. Then the Havelock function is

h_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\{e^{i(x\tan u-\nu u)}+e^{-i(x\tan u-\nu u)}\}\,du.

(A.1)

Consider the integral involving the first exponential in (
A.1
) taken round the path
OAB
in Fig. 2(b):

\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}e^{i(x\tan u-\nu u)}du=I_{OA}+I_{AB},

where, from (
3.7
),
I_{AB}=O(e^{-x\Psi(a)})=O(e^{-x})
as
a\to 0
. The integral along the positive imaginary axis becomes

I_{OA}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{|u_{0}|}e^{-x\tanh s+\nu s}ds=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{w_{0}}e^{-xw+\nu{\footnotesize\mbox{arctanh}}\,w}\,\frac{dw}{1-w^{2}},

where we have made the substitution
s=\tanh w
and
w_{0}=\sqrt{1-a}<1
. Using the expansion

\frac{e^{\pm\nu{\footnotesize\mbox{arctanh}}\,w}}{1-w^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\pm)^{k}c_{k}(\nu)}{k!}\,w^{k}\qquad(|w|<1),

where the first few coefficients
c_{k}(\nu)
are

\displaystyle c_{0}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\quad c_{1}(\nu)=\nu,\quad c_{2}(\nu)=2+\nu^{2},\quad c_{3}(\nu)=\nu(8+\nu^{2}),

\displaystyle c_{4}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 24+20\nu^{2}+\nu^{4},\quad c_{5}(\nu)=\nu(184+40\nu^{2}+\nu^{4}),

\displaystyle c_{6}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 720+784\nu^{2}+70\nu^{4}+\nu^{6},\quad c_{7}(\nu)=\nu(8448+2464\nu+112\nu^{4}+\nu^{6}),

\displaystyle c_{8}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 40320+52352\nu^{2}+6384\nu^{4}+168\nu^{6}+\nu^{8},

we find

I_{OA}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\nu)}{k!}\int_{0}^{w_{0}}e^{-xw}w^{k}dw\sim\frac{1}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\nu)}{x^{k}}\qquad(x\to+\infty).

In the final step the upper limit
w_{0}
has been replaced by
\infty
.

The integral involving the second exponential in (
A.1
) taken along the mirror image in the lower-half plane of the path
OAB
in Fig. 2(b) yields the same result. Hence we obtain the expansion

h_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{2}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}(\nu)}{x^{k}}\qquad(x\to+\infty,\ \nu\ \mbox{fixed}).

(A.2)

The asymptotic expansion of
h_{\nu}(-x)
for

with

fixed follows the same procedure to yield

h_{\nu}(-x)\sim\frac{2}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k-1}c_{k}(\nu)}{x^{k}}\qquad(x\to+\infty,\ \nu\ \mbox{fixed}),

(A.3)

where in both (

Setting

which yields the steepest descent paths in the form

\tanh\eta=\pm\sqrt{\frac{\tan\xi-a\xi+c}{\tan\xi(1+(a\xi-c)\tan\xi)}}~.

(B.1)

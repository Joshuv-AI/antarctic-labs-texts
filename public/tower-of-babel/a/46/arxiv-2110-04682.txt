Abstract
. We give a description of the formal neighborhoods of the components of the boundary divisor in the Deligne-Mumford moduli stack ℳ ¯ g \overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g} of stable curves in terms of the extended clutching construction that we define. This construction can be viewed as a formal version of the analytic plumbing construction. The advantage of our formal construction is that we can control the effect of changing formal parameters at the marked points that are being glued. As an application, we prove that the infinitesimal neighborhood of the boundary component Δ 1 , 1 \Delta_{1,1} in ℳ ¯ 2 \overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{2} is canonically isomorphic to the infinitesimal neighborhood of the zero section in the normal bundle. As another application, we show how to study the period map near the boundary components Δ g 1 , g 2 \Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}} in terms of the coordinates coming from our extended clutching construction.



1.
Introduction

Let
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
be the moduli stack of stable curves of genus
g\geq 2
. Consider the component
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}

of the boundary divisor corresponding to reducible curves with components of genus
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, where
g=g_{1}+g_{2}
.
We are interested in a description of the
n
th order thickening
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
of
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}
in
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
for any
n\geq 1
.

Recall that there is a natural “clutching” surjective finite morphism

\xi_{g_{1},g_{2}}:\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1},1}\times\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{2},1}\to\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}

which for
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
is generically an isomorphism (see
[
1
, ch. XII, Prop. 10.11]
).

For every
n\geq 1
, let us set
S_{n}=\operatorname{Spec}(\Z[q]/(q^{n+1}))
.
Let
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g,1}\to\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,1}
denote the moduli space of stable curves of genus
g
with one marked point together with a formal
parameter at the marked point.

We show that the clutching morphism extends naturally to a collection of compatible morphisms

\xi_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}:\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{1},1}\times\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{2},1}\times S_{n}\to\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)},

for
n\geq 0
.
The corresponding family of stable curves is defined by gluing the constant deformations of the complements
to the marked points with the standard deformation of the node
x_{1}x_{2}=q
.
This can be viewed as an algebraic analog of the
plumbing construction
, well known in the Teichmüller space approach to the moduli of curves
(see
[
3
]
).

The price one pays for getting these extended morphisms is the need to fix formal parameters to be glued.
However, we show that these choices can be controlled in a way compatible with the morphisms
\xi_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
.
The setup for this is the well known action of the Lie algebra of vector fields on the moduli spaces
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g,1}
,
or more precisely of the ind-group
{\mathcal{G}}
whose
R
-points are continuous automorphisms of
R(\!(t)\!)
(see
[
5
]
,
[
2
, Sec. 17.3]
).

We construct a group scheme

{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
over
S_{n}
together with an embedding

{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}\hookrightarrow({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}})_{S_{n}},

so that we can consider the induced action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
on

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{1},g_{2},n}:=\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{1},1}\times\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{2},1}\times S_{n}.

Our main result (see Theorem
1.1
below) states that the extended clutching morphism
\xi_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}

factors through the quotient by
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
and that the map from this quotient to the
n
th neighborhood of
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}

is generically a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-torsor for
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
(there is an extra double covering in the case
g_{1}=g_{2}
).

The same construction works for the component
\Delta_{0}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
of the boundary divisor corresponding to curves with a non-separating node.
Namely, the clutching morphism

\xi_{0}:\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g-1,2}\to\Delta_{0}

extends to a collection of morphisms

\xi_{0}^{(n)}:\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g-1,2}\times S_{n}\to\Delta_{0}^{(n)},

where we consider the moduli space with choices of parameters at both marked points.
We still have the action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g-1,2}
, so that
\xi_{0}^{(n)}
factors through the quotient.

To formulate the statement more precisely let us introduce some notation.
Let
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\operatorname{irr},(\infty)}_{g_{1},g_{2},n}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{1},g_{2},n}
be the open locus corresponding to pairs of irreducible curves, and let

U\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}\subset\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
be the corresponding open locus in
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
corresponding to curves with only one separating node.
In the case
g_{1}=g_{2}=g/2
we consider the étale double coverings

{\mathcal{E}}_{g/2,g/2}\to U\Delta_{g/2,g/2},\ \ {\mathcal{E}}_{g/2,g/2}^{(n)}\to U\Delta_{g/2,g/2}^{(n)}

corresponding to a choice of one of the components of the stable curve with a separating node.
Similarly, we consider the open loci
{\mathcal{M}}^{(\infty)}_{g-1,2}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g-1,2}
of smooth curves,

U\Delta_{0}^{(n)}\subset\Delta_{0}^{(n)}
of curves with only one node, and
the étale double coverings

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\to U\Delta_{0},\ \ {\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{(n)}\to U\Delta_{0}^{(n)}

corresponding to ordering the preimages of the node in the normalization.
For
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
we set
{\mathcal{E}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}=U\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
.
Note that in the terminology of
[
1
]
these stacks correspond to choices of a
\Gamma
-structure, where
\Gamma
is the graph with two vertices marked by
g_{1}
and
g_{2}

and an edge between them, in the case of
{\mathcal{E}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}
, and
\Gamma
is the graph with the single vertex and a loop in the case of
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The morphism
\xi_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
(resp.,
\xi_{0}^{(n)}
)
factors through a morphism

[\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g_{1},g_{2},n}/{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}]\to\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}

(resp.,

[\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(\infty)}_{g-1,2}/{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}]\to\Delta_{0}^{(n)}
).
The restriction of this morphism,

[\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\operatorname{irr},(\infty)}_{g_{1},g_{2},n}/{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}]\to U\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}

(resp.,
[{\mathcal{M}}^{(\infty)}_{g-1,2}/{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}]\to U\Delta_{0}^{(n)}
)
is canonically identified with the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-torsor over
{\mathcal{E}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(n)}
corresponding to the pull-back of

{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta)
on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
.

As an application, in the case
g_{1}=g_{2}=1
we can identify the
n
th infinitesimal neighborhood of the boundary divisor with the one in the normal line
bundle.
In the case
g_{1}=1
,
g_{2}>1
, we get a presentation of the
1
st infinitesimal neighborhood as a quotient by
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\rtimes{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let us work over
\operatorname{Spec}\Q
.

(i) Let us denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
the pull-back of the normal bundle to
{\mathcal{E}}_{1,1}\to\Delta_{1,1}

(resp.,
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\to U\Delta_{0}
).
Then there is a natural isomorphism of
{\mathcal{E}}_{1,1}^{(n)}
(resp.,
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{(n)}
)
with the
n
th infinitesimal neighborhood of the zero section in the line bundle
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
over
{\mathcal{E}}_{1,1}
(resp., over
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
).

(ii) For any
g\geq 3
, there is a natural identification

U\Delta_{1,g-1}^{(1)}\simeq[(\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{1,1}^{(1)}\times\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g-1,1}^{\operatorname{irr},(2)}\times\operatorname{Spec}(\Q[q]/(q^{2})))/({\mathbb{G}}_{a}\rtimes{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2})],

where
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g-1,1}^{\operatorname{irr},(2)}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g-1,1}^{(2)}
is the locus of irreducible curves. Here
we use natural actions of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
on
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{1,1}^{(1)}\times\operatorname{Spec}(\Q[q]/(q^{2})))
and on
{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,1}^{(2)}

by infinitesimal translations and by the changes of the formal parameter, and the natural rescalings of parameters by
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2}
.

The extended clutching morphisms provide convenient “gluing” coordinates in the formal neighborhood of the components of the boundary divisor in
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
. To illustrate this
we show how to compute the expansion in powers of
q
(the gluing coordinate), defined on the formal neighborhood of
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}
,
of the morphism

\pi_{*}\omega_{C/{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\C_{C/{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}

used to define the period map (see Section
5
). Here
\pi:C\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is the universal curve. In particular, we get explicit formulas for the expansion modulo
q^{g_{1}+g_{2}+2}
, and in the case
g_{1}=g_{2}=1
,
a recursive procedure for computing the complete
q
-expansion.

The results of this paper admit counterparts for the moduli spaces of stable supercurves that will be treated elsewhere.
In the super case the “gluing coordinates” near the components of the boundary divisors are useful for studying the polar behavior of the superanalog of
the Mumford’s isomorphism and of the superstring supermeasure.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
we define and study the group scheme
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
which acts on deformations
of the node singularity. In Sec.
3
we define the extended clutching construction. In Sec.
4
we prove Theorems
1.1

and
1.2
. Finally, in Sec.
5
we study the period map near the boundary divisor
\Delta_{g_{1},g_{2}}
using the coordinates given by the extended clutching construction.

2.
Changes of coordinates on a node deformation

2.1.
The gluing group scheme

For any commutative ring
R

let us denote by
\operatorname{Aut}R[\![t]\!]
the group of continuous automorphisms of
\operatorname{Aut}R[\![t]\!]
, identical on
R
.
Similarly we denote by
\operatorname{Aut}R(\!(t)\!)
the group of continuous automorphisms of
R(\!(t)\!)
,
identical on
R
.
The Lie algebra of this group functor is
{\mathcal{W}}
, the Witt algebra over
\Z
.
It has a topological basis
L_{n}=z^{n+1}\frac{d}{dz}
,
n\in\Z
, and the bracket is given by

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}

(so an element of
{\mathcal{W}}
is an infinite series
\sum_{n\geq-N}c_{n}L_{n}
).

Let us consider the induced
\Z(\!(q)\!)
-linear bracket on
{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\Z(\!(q)\!)
.
We are interested in a completion of a certain
\Z[\![q]\!]
-lattice in
{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\Z(\!(q)\!)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let us consider the
\Z[\![q]\!]
-submodule
{\mathcal{W}}^{0}\subset{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\Z(\!(q)\!)
with the basis

(M_{n})_{n\in\Z}
, where

M_{n}=L_{n}\text{ for }n\geq 0,\ \ M_{-n}=q^{n}L_{-n}\text{ for }n>0.

We also set
{\mathcal{W}}^{0}_{n}:={\mathcal{W}}^{0}/q^{n+1}{\mathcal{W}}^{0}
. Now let
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}
(resp.,
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}_{n}
) denote the completion of
{\mathcal{W}}^{0}
(resp.,
{\mathcal{W}}^{0}_{n}
)
with respect to the filtration by
\Z[\![q]\!]
-submodules generated by
M_{n}
with
|n|>N
, where
N=1,2,\ldots
.

In other words, in
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}
we allow infinite sums in both directions,
\sum_{i\in\Z}c_{i}M_{i}
. Using the relation

[M_{i},M_{-j}]=q^{\min(i,j)}(i+j)M_{i-j},\text{ for }i\geq 0,j\geq 0,

it is easy to see that the Lie bracket on
{\mathcal{W}}^{0}
induces a well defined Lie bracket on
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}
.
We are mostly interested in the induced Lie algebra structure on
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}_{n}
, linear over
A_{n}:=\Z[q]/(q^{n+1})
.

Note that we have a natural involution preserving the bracket,

\kappa:\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}\to\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}:\kappa(M_{n})=-M_{-n}\text{ for }n\in\Z.

(2.1)

Also, the Lie algebra
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}_{n}
has a natural equivariant structure with respect to rescaling of
q
.
In other words, if we think of
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}_{n}
as a sheaf of Lie algebras over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{q}
then
it has a natural
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant structure. Namely, for any
A_{n}
-algebra
R
and any unit
\lambda\in R^{*}
,
we denote by
R(\lambda q)
the same ring
R
with the new
A_{n}
-algebra structure that replaces
q\in R
by
\lambda q
.
Then we have an action of
\lambda
,

\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}\otimes_{A_{n}}R\rTo{\alpha_{\lambda}}\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}\otimes_{A_{n}}R(\lambda q)

such that
\alpha_{\lambda}(M_{n})=\lambda^{n}M_{n}
for
n\geq 0
and
\alpha_{\lambda}(M_{n})=M_{n}
for
n<0
.

Given a
\Z[q]/(q^{n+1})
-algebra
R
, we are going to show that
the natural Lie action of
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}^{0}_{n}
on
R(\!(t)\!)
(where
M_{i}
with
i\leq-n-1
acts trivially) integrates to an action of a certain
group scheme.

For a
\Z[q]/(q^{n+1})
-algebra
R
let us set

A(R):=R[\![t_{1},t_{2}]\!]/(t_{1}t_{2}-q).

Definition 2.2
.

We define the group

{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
as the group of continuous automorphisms
\alpha
of
A(R)
, identical on
R
, such that

\alpha(t_{1})=t_{1}\cdot u,\ \ \alpha(t_{2})=t_{2}\cdot u^{-1},

for some unit
u
in
A(R)
.

The fact that the condition defining
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
is closed under product is easy to check:
if

\beta(t_{1})=t_{1}\cdot v,\ \ \beta(t_{2})=t_{2}\cdot v^{-1},

then

(\alpha\circ\beta)(t_{1})=t_{1}\cdot(u\cdot\alpha(v)),\ \ (\alpha\circ\beta)(t_{2})=t_{2}\cdot(u^{-1}\cdot\alpha(v)^{-1}),

and we observe that
u\cdot\alpha(v)
is again a unit in
A(R)
.

We have a natural involution

\kappa:{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)\to{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R):\alpha\mapsto\sigma\alpha\sigma^{-1},

where
\sigma
swaps
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
.

In the case
n=0
, i.e.,
q=0
, the group
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},0}(R)
is generated by two commuting subgroups,

\operatorname{Aut}R[\![t_{1}]\!]
and
\operatorname{Aut}R[\![t_{2}]\!]
(given by the condition that
u
depends only on
t_{1}
or only on
t_{2}
),
which intersect by
R^{*}
. In fact, it is easy to see that there is an isomorphism of groups

{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},0}(R)\simeq\operatorname{Aut}_{1}R[\![t_{1}]\!]\times R^{*}\times\operatorname{Aut}_{1}R[\![t_{2}]\!],

where
\operatorname{Aut}_{1}R[\![t]\!]\subset\operatorname{Aut}R[\![t]\!]
denotes the subgroup of automorphisms
\alpha
such that
\alpha(t)\equiv t\mod(t^{2})
.

We have a natural homomorphism of
R
-algebras

\iota:A(R)\to R(\!(t_{1})\!)\oplus R(\!(t_{2})\!):t_{1}\mapsto(t_{1},q/t_{2}),\ t_{2}\mapsto(q/t_{1},t_{2}).

(2.2)

Note that this is well-defined since
q^{n+1}=0
in
R
.

Furthermore, it is easy to see that there is a natural identification
A(R)[t_{i}^{-1}]\simeq R(\!(t_{i})\!)
,
for
i=1,2
, so that
\iota
can be identified with the map

A(R)\to A(R)[t_{1}^{-1}]\oplus A(R)[t_{2}^{-1}].

Lemma 2.3
.

(i) The morphism
\iota
is injective (in fact, a direct summand as an
R
-submodule), and
we have an inclusion

R[\![t_{1}]\!]t_{1}^{n+1}\oplus R[\![t_{2}]\!]t_{2}^{n+1}\subset\operatorname{im}(\iota).

(ii)
If an element
u\in A(R)^{*}
satisfies
t_{1}\cdot u=t_{1}
,
t_{2}\cdot u^{-1}=t_{2}
, then
u=1
.
The functor
R\mapsto{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
is represented by a flat group scheme
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
over
S_{n}=\operatorname{Spec}(\Z[q]/(q^{n+1}))

(which as a scheme is isomorphic to
the product of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
with the infinite dimensional affine space over
S_{n}
). One has

{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n+1}\times_{S_{n+1}}S_{n}\simeq{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}.

Proof.

(i) First we observe
\iota(x_{1}^{i})=(x_{1}^{i},0)
,
\iota(x_{2}^{i})=(0,x_{2}^{i})
for
i\geq n+1
, which implies the required inclusion
into
\operatorname{im}(\iota)
. Thus, it is enough to prove that the map

\overline{\iota}:R[t_{1},t_{2}]/(t_{1}^{n+1},t_{2}^{n+1},t_{1}t_{2}-q)\to P:=R(\!(t_{1})\!)/R[\![t_{1}]\!]t_{1}^{n+1}\oplus R(\!(t_{2})\!)/R[\![t_{2}]\!]t_{2}^{n+1},

induced by
\iota
is injective. Next, let
P^{\prime}
be the
R
-submodule of
P
with the basis

(1,0)
,
(x_{1}^{-i},x_{2}^{-i})_{i\leq 1}
. Then the
R
-module
P/P^{\prime}
is free of finite rank, and it is enough to prove that the map

R[t_{1},t_{2}]/(t_{1}^{n+1},t_{2}^{n+1},t_{1}t_{2}-q)\to P/P^{\prime}

of free
R
-modules is an isomorphism. But this is true, since it becomes an isomorphism modulo
q
, and
q
is nilpotent.

(ii)
Indeed, for such an element we see that
u-1
goes to zero under the localization with respect to
t_{1}
and under
the localization with respect to
t_{2}
. Hence,
\iota(u-1)=0
, so
u=1
.
It follows that the elements of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
are in bijection with
A(R)^{*}
.
Note that an element
u
of
A(R)
is a unit if and only if it has form

u_{0}+a_{1}t_{1}+a_{2}t_{1}^{2}+\ldots+b_{1}t_{2}+b_{1}t_{2}^{2}+\ldots,

where
u_{0}\in R^{*}
,
a_{i}\in R
,
b_{i}\in R
. This gives an isomorphism of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
with the product of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and an infinite-dimensional
affine space over
S_{n}
. The last assertion is clear.
∎

It is clear that any
\alpha\in{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
induces well defined homomorphisms

\alpha_{i}
of
A(R)[t_{i}^{-1}]\simeq R(\!(t_{i})\!)
, so that

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\circ\iota=\iota\circ\alpha.

In this way we get an injective morphism of group ind-schemes

{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)\to({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}})_{S_{n}}(R)=\operatorname{Aut}R(\!(t_{1})\!)\times\operatorname{Aut}R(\!(t_{2})\!):\alpha\mapsto(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}).

(2.3)

More explicitly, if
\alpha(t_{1})=t_{1}\cdot u(t_{1},t_{2})
then

\alpha_{1}(t_{1})=t_{1}\cdot u(t_{1},q/t_{1}),\ \ \alpha_{2}(t_{2})=t_{2}\cdot u(q/t_{2},t_{2})^{-1}.

In particular, this map defines an action of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
on
R(\!(t_{1})\!)
and on
R(\!(t_{2})\!)
.
We can think of this geometrically as two actions of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}
on the punctured formal disk.

Example 2.4
.

For
r\in R
, we can consider the element
\alpha\in{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},1}(R)

\alpha_{1}(t_{1})=t_{1}+rt_{1}^{2},\ \ \alpha_{2}(t_{2})=t_{2}-qr

(i) The Lie algebra of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}

(ii) There are natural embedding of groups

corresponding to the cases when the unit
u\in A(R)
depends only on
t_{1}
, only on

, or is in

, respectively.
The product map

\operatorname{Aut}_{1}R[\![t_{1}]\!]\times R^{*}\times\operatorname{Aut}_{1}R[\![t_{2}]\!]\to{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)

is an isomorphism of schemes.
Consider the subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}_{n}(R[\![t]\!],n)\subset\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(R[\![t]\!])

.
Then the map

\operatorname{Aut}_{n}(R[\![t_{1}]\!])\times\operatorname{Aut}_{n}(R[\![t_{2}]\!])\to{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)

Proof.

(i) We have to consider elements of

2.2.
Isomorphisms between node deformations

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose
q,q^{\prime}\in R
are two nilpotent elements. Then any continuous isomorphism of
R
-algebras,

\alpha:R[\![x_{1},x_{2}]\!]/(x_{1}x_{2}-q^{\prime})\to R[\![x_{1},x_{2}]\!]/(x_{1}x_{2}-q)

sending
(x_{1})
to
(x_{1})
and
(x_{2})
to
(x_{2})
, is a composition of an isomorphism

x_{1}\mapsto\lambda x_{1},\ x_{2}\mapsto x_{2},

for uniquely defined
\lambda\in R^{*}
, where
q^{\prime}=\lambda q
, followed by
an automorphism from
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha((x_{1}x_{2}))=(x_{1}x_{2})
, we have the equality of ideals
(q)=(q^{\prime})
, so
\alpha
induces an automorphism of

R/q[\![x_{1},x_{2}]\!]/(x_{1}x_{2})
.

By assumption, we have

\alpha(x_{1})=x_{1}u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),\ \ \alpha(x_{2})=x_{2}u_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}).

Reducing modulo
q
, we deduce that
u_{1}=\lambda_{1}\mod(x_{1},x_{2})
,
u_{2}=\lambda_{2}\mod(x_{1},x_{2})
, where
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
project to units in
R/(q)
.
Hence,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are units in
R
, and so
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are units in
A(R)
.
Post-composing
\alpha
with an element of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
corresponding to
u_{2}
, we obtain an isomorphism

\alpha^{\prime}(x_{1})=x_{1}u(x_{1},x_{2}),\ \ \alpha^{\prime}(x_{2})=x_{2},

where
u=u_{1}u_{2}
is a unit in
A(R)
.
Let us write

u=\lambda+x_{1}f_{1}(x_{1})+x_{2}f_{2}(x_{2}),

where
\lambda\in R^{*}
.
Then the condition

q^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}(x_{1})\alpha^{\prime}(x_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}u=qu

implies that

q^{\prime}=\lambda q,\ \ qf_{1}=0,\ \ qf_{2}=0.

It follows that

x_{1}x_{2}f_{2}=0,

so we can replace
u
with
u^{\prime}=\lambda+x_{1}f_{1}(x_{1})
and still have
\alpha^{\prime}(x_{1})=x_{1}u^{\prime}
.
Since we also have

x_{2}x_{1}f_{1}=0,

if we write
u^{\prime}=\lambda\cdot u_{1}
, then we get

x_{2}\cdot(u_{1}-1)=0.

Hence,

x_{2}\cdot u_{1}^{-1}=x_{2}+x_{2}(u_{1}^{-1}-1)=x_{2},

and the automorphism
x_{1}\mapsto x_{1}\cdot u_{1}
,
x_{2}\mapsto x_{2}
belongs to
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
.
Thus,
\alpha^{\prime}
is the composition of the isomorphism
x_{1}\mapsto\lambda x_{1}
,
x_{2}\mapsto x_{2}
with an element of
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\operatorname{glue}},n}(R)
,
as claimed.

To prove the uniqueness of
\lambda
we have to check that if

\lambda\cdot x_{1}=x_{1}\cdot u,\ \ x_{2}=x_{2}\cdot u^{-1}

for some unit
u
in
A(R)
and some
\lambda\in R^{*}
, then
\lambda=1
.
Writing

u=\lambda_{1}+x_{1}f_{1}(x_{1})+x_{2}f_{2}(x_{2}),

where
\lambda_{1}\in R^{*}
, we get that

\lambda\cdot x_{1}=\lambda_{1}+x_{1}^{2}f_{1}(x_{1})+qf_{2}(x_{2}),

which implies that
f_{1}=0
. Hence
u^{-1}
has form

u^{-1}=\lambda_{1}^{-1}+x_{2}\widetilde{f}_{2}(x_{2}).

But then the condition
x_{2}=x_{2}\cdot u^{-1}
implies that
\widetilde{f}_{2}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=1
. Hence
u^{-1}=1
and
\lambda=1
.
∎

Step 3

3.3.
Computing the determinant line bundle

Recall that the boundary line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta)
on

is defined as the determinant line bundle

which is a special case of a general construction of

We will compute the pair

for the family corresponding to the extended clutching construction.

Proposition 3.5
.

For the family
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}(\widetilde{C}_{0},p_{1},p_{2},x_{1},x_{2};q)
over
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, there is a natural trivialization

\tau:R\rTo{\sim}{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathcal{C}}/\operatorname{Spec}(R)}

such that
\theta=\tau(q)
.

Proof.

We can compute the object
R\pi_{*}[\Omega_{{\mathcal{C}}/S}\to\omega_{{\mathcal{C}}/S}]
using Cech resolutions

\Omega_{{\mathcal{C}}/S}\to\Omega_{U_{1}/S}\oplus\Omega_{U_{2}/S}\to\Omega_{U_{12}/S},

\omega_{{\mathcal{C}}/S}\to\omega_{U_{1}/S}\oplus\omega_{U_{2}/S}\to\omega_{U_{12}/S}.

Since the maps
\Omega_{U_{1}/S}\to\omega_{U_{1}/S}
and
\Omega_{U_{12}/S}\to\omega_{U_{12}/S}
are isomorphisms, we get
a natural quasi-isomorphism

R\pi_{*}(\Omega_{C/S}\to\omega_{C/S})\to[\Omega_{U_{2}/S}\to\omega_{U_{2}/S}].

Hence,

{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathcal{C}}/S}\simeq\det\bigl([\Omega_{U_{2}/S}\to\omega_{U_{2}/S}][1]\bigr)

and
\theta
is the determinant of the map
A\to B
, where
[A\to B]
is a complex of projective finitely generated
R
-modules
quasi-isomorphic to
[\Omega_{U_{2}/S}\to\omega_{U_{2}/S}]
.

Now using the coordinates on

, where
{\mathcal{A}}=R[\![x_{1},x_{2}]\!]/(x_{1}x_{2}-q)
, we get

\Omega_{U_{2}/S}={\mathcal{A}}\cdot dx_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}\cdot dx_{2}/(x_{1}dx_{2}+x_{2}dx_{1}),

It is easy to see that we have decompositions of

∎

5.3.
Differentials near a separating node

Let us consider the extended clutching construction for a pair of curves with formal parameters at marked points,
(C_{1},p_{1},x_{1})

(C_{2},p_{2},x_{2})

Here we assume that the formal parameters

Let

We want to construct a special basis of the bundle

(5.3)

.

Proposition 5.5
.

(i) For every regular differential

(ii) For

\Phi_{2}:H^{0}(C_{2},\omega_{C_{2}})\to H^{0}(\omega_{{\mathcal{C}}/S_{N}})

Proof.

(i) We solve the equations (

.
Modulo

,

and

Assume now

Since

and
\omega_{2}(x_{2})

(ii) We just have to check that for these

(iii) Note that

5.4.
Separating node boundary in
g=2
moduli

Now we specialize to the case
g_{1}=g_{2}=1
.

First of all, for
g=1
we can express all objects in Sec.
5.2
in terms of elliptic functions.
Let
C=\C/(\Z+\Z\tau)
be an elliptic curve with
0
as the marked point.
The canonical formal parameter of Lemma
5.2
(iii) is just the coordinate
z
coming from the complex plane, with

\alpha[0]=dz
.

The rational functions
f[-n]
,
n\geq 2
(with a particular normalization of additive constants), are expressed in terms of
the rescaled derivatives of the Weierstrass
\wp
-function:

f[-n]=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\wp^{(n-2)}(z)-\frac{c_{n-2}}{n-1}=\frac{1}{z^{n}}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{m\geq 1}{m+n-2\choose m}\frac{c_{m+n-2}}{n-1}z^{m},

where

\wp(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+c_{2}z^{2}+c_{4}z^{4}+\ldots

(where
c_{i}=c_{i}(\tau)
and
c_{i}=0
for odd
i
and
c_{0}=0
).
The above normalization of
f[-n]
satisfies
f[-n]=z^{-n}+O(z)
.
Hence, the rational differentials
\omega[-n]
are given by

\omega[-n]=f[-n]dz.

The basis of
H^{1}(C,\C)\simeq\omega_{C}(C\setminus p)/d{\mathcal{O}}(C\setminus p)
is given by

\alpha[0]=dz
and
\omega[-2]=\wp(z)dz
.
Note that for
n\geq 3
we have

\omega[-n]\equiv-\frac{c_{n-2}}{n-1}\cdot dz

in
H^{1}(C,\C)
.

Now we consider the extended clutching applied to the elliptic curves
C_{1}=\C/(\Z+\Z\tau_{1})
and
C_{2}=\C/(\Z+\Z\tau_{2})
with the origins as the marked points.

We claim that
\Phi_{1}(dx_{1})
can be found in the form

\Phi_{1}(dz)=([1+\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{2n}(q)f[-2n](x_{1},\tau_{1})]dx_{1},-q[f[-2](x_{2},\tau_{2})+\sum_{n\geq 2}b_{2n}(q)f[-2n](x_{2},\tau_{2})]dx_{2}),

for some series
a_{2n}(q)
,
n\geq 1
and
b_{2n}(q)
,
n\geq 2
, depending on
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
.
Indeed, we have

\phi_{1}(x_{1})=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{2n}(q)f[-2n](x_{1},\tau_{1})_{>0},\ \ \phi_{2}(x_{2})=-qf[-2](x_{2},\tau_{2})_{>0}-q\sum_{n\geq 2}b_{2n}(q)f[-2n](x_{2},\tau_{2})_{>0},

and equations (
5.3
) can be rewritten as

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{2n}(q)}{x_{1}^{2n}}=-\frac{q}{x_{1}^{2}}\phi_{2}(\frac{q}{x_{1}})=\frac{q^{2}}{x_{1}^{2}}\bigl(f[-2](\frac{q}{x_{1}},\tau_{2})_{>0}+\sum_{n\geq 2}b_{2n}(q)f[-2n](\frac{q}{x_{1}},\tau_{2})_{>0}\bigr),

\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{b_{2n}(q)}{x_{2}^{2n}}=\frac{1}{x_{2}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{2n}(q)f[-2n](\frac{q}{x_{2}},\tau_{1})_{>0}.

These are equivalent to the following recursion for
(a_{2n})_{n\geq 1}
,
(b_{2n})_{n\geq 2}

b_{2n}=q^{2n-2}\sum_{m\geq 2}a_{2m}\cdot{2m+2n-4\choose 2n-2}\frac{c_{2m+2n-4}(\tau_{1})}{2m-1},\text{ for }n\geq 2,

with the initial condition
a_{2}=0
.
This recursion has a unique solution, which proves our claim.

For example, we have

a_{4}\equiv c_{2}(\tau_{2})q^{4}+4c_{4}(\tau_{1})c_{2}(\tau_{2})c_{4}(\tau_{2})q^{10}\mod q^{12},\ a_{6}\equiv c_{4}(\tau_{2})q^{6}\mod q^{12},\ a_{8}\equiv c_{6}(\tau_{2})q^{8}\mod q^{12},

(resp.,

(resp.,

The map
\Pi
sends the differential

Thus, modulo

\displaystyle\Pi(dx_{1},0)\equiv

\displaystyle([1-\frac{c_{2}(\tau_{1})c_{2}(\tau_{2})}{3}q^{4}-\frac{c_{4}(\tau_{1})c_{4}(\tau_{2})}{5}q^{6}-\frac{c_{6}(\tau_{1})c_{6}(\tau_{2})}{7}q^{8}-\frac{4}{3}c_{2}(\tau_{1})c_{4}(\tau_{1})c_{2}(\tau_{2})c_{4}(\tau_{2})q^{10}]dx_{1},

\displaystyle-q\omega[-2]+[\frac{2}{3}c_{4}(\tau_{1})c_{2}(\tau_{2})^{2}q^{7}+2c_{6}(\tau_{1})c_{2}(\tau_{2})c_{4}(\tau_{2})q^{9}]dx_{2}).

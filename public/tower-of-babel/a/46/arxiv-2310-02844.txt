The heart fan of an abelian category
Nathan Broomhead, David Pauksztello, David Ploog, Jon Woolf

Abstract
. We apply convex geometry (cones, fans) to homological input (abelian categories, hearts of bounded t-structures) to construct a new invariant of an abelian category, its heart fan. This can be viewed as a ‘universal phase diagram’ for Bridgeland stability conditions with the given heart. When the abelian category is the module category of a finite-dimensional algebra, the heart fan is complete and contains the g g -fan as the subfan of full-dimensional cones. The heart fan is also closely related to the wall-and-chamber structure for King semistability.

Introduction

Much of homological algebra is phrased in terms of abelian and triangulated categories, and these are widely used in algebraic geometry, the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras, symplectic geometry and constructible topology. We apply convex geometry to study abelian and triangulated categories. In the first three sections we explain how to naturally construct a convex cone
C(\mathsfit{H})
— the
heart cone
— from an abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
and a fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
— the
heart fan
— from the heart
\mathsfit{H}
of a bounded t-structure (henceforth, a ‘bounded heart’ or often just ‘heart’) in a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
.

The following result defines the heart cones and the heart fan they make up in the simplest situation in which the abelian and triangulated categories have Grothendieck groups that are free of finite rank; later, we weaken this requirement by fixing a homomorphism from the Grothendieck group to a finite rank free abelian group
\Lambda
.
There is a standard partial order on bounded hearts in which
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{H}
given by inclusion of the co-aisles of the associated t-structures.

Theorem A

(Theorems
3.7
and
5.3
)
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be a bounded heart in a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
. Assume that the Grothendieck group
\Lambda\coloneqq K(\mathsfit{H})
is free of finite rank and let
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\coloneqq{\mathrm{Hom}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})
.

(1)

The set
C(\mathsfit{H})=\{v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\mid v([h])\geq 0~\forall h\in\mathsfit{H}\}
is a closed, strictly convex cone in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
.

(2)

There is a fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
generated by
\{C(\mathsfit{K})\mid\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}\}
.

(3)

The fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
does not depend on the ambient triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
.

(4)

If
\mathsfit{H}
is a length category then the fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is complete and the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is finite.

(ii)

Every heart
\mathsfit{K}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
is length.

(iii)

There are finitely many hearts
\mathsfit{K}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
.

While the definition of the heart fan in Theorem
A
is correct, we in fact start out integrally, i.e. with monoids in a lattice. Moreover, we define a preliminary convex-geometric object, cofans, which can be seen as fans before dualisation.
We construct the heart fan in a three-step process: we first define the
integral heart cofan

\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
which induces the
real heart cofan

\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H})
and finally by dualisation the (real) heart fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
.

Cofans occur implicitly in toric geometry where, however, there is no need to stress them: cofan and associated fan determine each other if all cones are polyhedral. This breaks already in our most basic examples, leading us to develop cofans. Readers only interested in the heart fan can make a beeline for it by jumping to Theorem-Definition
3.7
where we give a self-contained proof that it is indeed a fan, possibly after looking at our
notation
and Definition
2.2
.

We propose cofans as the natural convex-geometric object associated to
c
-vectors appearing in cluster theory and representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras.
In representation theory,
c
-vectors correspond to the dimension vectors of two-term simple-minded collections and the heart cofan dualises to (a completion of) the
g
-fan; see Section
8
. However, our construction does not require that the corresponding silting theory exists, giving us a notion of a ‘virtual
g
-fan’ when it does not.
In particular, because our construction applies to any bounded heart, it applies more generally than to our main examples, which come from quivers with relations and coherent sheaves on smooth projective varieties.
By analogy with toric geometry, the
c
-vector side is algebraic and the
g
-vector side geometric: the homological algebra is on the
c
-side, the geometric objects such as fans and stability spaces are on the
g

Motivation and relation to other constructions

Our first observation was that the dual cones of nearby tilted hearts fit neatly into a fan, akin to how fans of toric varieties arise.
From that initial insight we set out to explore the connection between homological algebra and convex geometry systematically. Our work is also motivated by connections to current research which are explained in Sections

Further work

In a sequel to this article, we construct a ‘multifan’
\Delta(\mathsfit{D})
for a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
. Intuitively a multifan should be thought of as a ‘fan over a vector space’ rather than a ‘fan in a vector space’; multifans formalise the gluing of compatible fans. To briefly explain, consider again the examples in Figure
1
. As is well-known,
\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H}_{1})\cong\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H}_{2})\eqqcolon\mathsfit{D}
. In particular, the ray corresponding to
\coh(\mathbb{P}^{1})
occurs in the heart fan of
\mathsfit{H}_{1}
, and is the highlighted diagonal ray emanating from the origin to the top left. In fact, the violet sections in each heart fan are identified under a basis change in
K(\mathsfit{D})
. The multifan is obtained by identifying common heart cones in the disjoint union of the heart fans of all bounded hearts of
\mathsfit{D}
.

In addition, we define a tangent multifan
T\Delta(\mathsfit{D})
by gluing together the tangent spaces of each heart cone. The tangent multifan describes the local combinatorics and geometry of
\Delta(\mathsfit{D})
. Its realisation as a space can be interpreted as the ‘space of lax stability functions’ on all hearts in
\mathsfit{D}
; here by ‘lax’ we mean that the stability function may map non-zero objects of the heart to zero. The Bridgeland stability space
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathsfit{D})
embeds as the open subset consisting of the stability functions with support and Harder–Narasimhan properties.

We want to draw attention to Parth Shimpi’s upcoming
[
Sh24+
]
on contraction algebras of 3-fold flops. Part of his work is the computation of heart fans in Grothendieck rank 3.

Notation and terminology.

Fix a lattice, i.e. a free abelian group of finite rank,
\Lambda
. Denote by
\Lambda^{*}={\mathrm{Hom}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})
the dual lattice, by
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}=\Lambda^{*}\otimes\mathbb{R}={\mathrm{Hom}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})
the associated real vector space and its dual respectively.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be a category having a Grothendieck group
K(\mathsfit{H})
, e.g.
\mathsfit{H}
is abelian or has a triangulated structure.
We call
\mathsfit{H}
a
lattice category
if
K(\mathsfit{H})
is a lattice, i.e. a finite rank, free abelian group.
Throughout, we fix a homomorphism
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
.
If
\mathsfit{H}
is a lattice category,
\lambda
will be taken as identity unless explicitly stated otherwise.
In many other settings, a natural choice is the numerical Grothendieck group,
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})/\ker(\chi)
, obtained by factoring out the kernel of the Euler pairing
\chi
. This makes sense when
\chi
is well defined and its left and right kernels agree; see
[
Ta18
]
for details.
The datum
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
is inspired by the theory of stability conditions.

By a
category over
\Lambda
, we mean
\mathsfit{H}
together with a homomorphism
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
. This is occasionally denoted
\mathsfit{H}/\Lambda
, trusting that no confusion with quotient categories is possible.

Elements of
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
are denoted
v,w
or similar.
We write
\lambda(h)
for
\lambda([h])
where
h\in\mathsfit{H}
and
[h]
is its class in
K(\mathsfit{H})
, and often suppress
\Lambda
and especially
\lambda
from notation. In particular, we write
v(h)\coloneqq v(\lambda([h]))
for
v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
and
h\in\mathsfit{H}
.

In the examples, varieties and algebras are over a field
{\mathbf{k}}
which we assume for convenience to be algebraically closed although this assumption can be weakened in places. By a ‘module’ we always mean a finite-dimensional left module.

1.
Convex geometry: cones, faces, fans, cofaces and cofans

Here we introduce the convex-geometric notions required later on; this section contains no homological algebra. We work integrally, i.e. with cones in a finite rank lattice
\Lambda
. In classical language, this means that our cones are rational. However, extra care is needed because the cones need not be finitely generated.
We first introduce terminology for faces and fans; this is in parallel with the classical notions, e.g. from toric variaties or
g
-fans. Starting in Subsection
1.4
, we introduce the notions of
coface
and
cofan
which are new.

1.1.
Cones

An
integral cone
in
\Lambda
is a non-empty subset
\sigma\subseteq\Lambda
which is closed under sums, i.e.
\sigma+\sigma\subseteq\sigma
.
In other language, a cone is a submonoid of
\Lambda
but we prefer the more geometric term ‘integral cone’; see Remark
1.1
. When the ambient lattice is clear from the the context, we just say ‘cone’.
We write
\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda)
for the set of cones in
\Lambda
.

Minkowski sum and difference
\sigma\pm\sigma^{\prime}
of cones
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
are again cones.
The
\mathbb{Z}
-linear hull of
\sigma
is the subgroup
\sigma-\sigma
and the
dimension

\dim(\sigma)\coloneqq\mathrm{rk}\,(\sigma-\sigma)
is its rank. A cone is
full
if
\dim(\sigma)=\mathrm{rk}\,(\Lambda)
.

A cone
\sigma
is
strictly convex
if it contains no non-zero subgroup, i.e.
\sigma\cap(-\sigma)=\{0\}
.
The (integral) cone
generated
by a subset
S\subseteq\Lambda
is the set

E(S)\coloneqq\bigg\{\sum_{i=0}^{m}n_{i}s_{i}\mid n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},s_{i}\in S,m\in\mathbb{N}\bigg\}

of non-negative integral combinations of elements of
S
. A cone is
polyhedral
if it is generated by a finite subset and
smooth
if it is generated by a subset of a basis of
\Lambda
.

Given a subset
S\subseteq\Lambda
, its
dual cone
is
\mathbb{D}(S)\coloneqq\{v\in\Lambda^{*}\mid v|_{S}\geq 0\}
; this is indeed a cone in the dual lattice
\Lambda^{*}={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})
. Our unusual notation is motivated by the adjointness properties of Subsection
1.6
. The
orthogonal
of
S
is the subgroup
S^{\perp}=\{v\in\Lambda^{*}\mid v|_{S}=0\}\subseteq\Lambda^{*}
. We set
C(S)_{\mathbb{Z}}\coloneqq\mathbb{D}(E(S))\subseteq\Lambda^{*}
to be the dual of the integral cone generated by
S
.

Remark 1.1.

In convex geometry, a cone is a subset
\sigma
of an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space that is closed under sums and under non-negative scalars, i.e.
\sigma+\sigma\subseteq\sigma
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\sigma\subseteq\sigma
. For distinction with integral cones, we call these
real cones
in this article.
If a lattice spanning the vector space is specified then a real cone is called
rational
if it is generated by a subset of the lattice.

We denote by
\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}})
the set of real cones in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
. The convex hull of an integral cone
\sigma\subseteq\Lambda
is the real cone
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\conv(\sigma)\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. Real cones of this form are precisely the rational cones in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. All statements in this section have obvious analogues for real cones; see Subsection
1.8
for more details.
The real cone generated by a subset
S
is denoted
E(S)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

1.2.
Faces

A
face

\tau
of a cone
\sigma
is a subcone closed under summands, i.e. a non-empty subset
\tau\subseteq\sigma
such that if
a,b\in\sigma
then
a+b\in\tau\iff a,b\in\tau
. This implies
0\in\tau
. The face relation is reflexive, anti-symmetric and transitive. Let
\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda)
denote the poset whose elements are the cones in
\Lambda
with
\tau\leq\sigma
whenever
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
. A
facet
is a face of codimension 1.

For a cone
\sigma
let
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)=\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda)_{\leq\sigma}
be the subposet consisting of
\sigma
and all its faces. Any intersection of faces of a cone is again a face. The infimum of
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
is
\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}
. Given a subset
\alpha\subseteq\sigma
of a cone, the
minimal face containing
\alpha
, denoted
\mathrm{mf}(\alpha)
, is the intersection of all faces of
\sigma
containing
\alpha
. The supremum of two faces
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
is the minimal face containing
\tau+\tau^{\prime}
. In particular,
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
is an order-theoretic lattice. The following explicit description of the minimal face will be useful later.

Lemma 1.2
.

The minimal face in
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
containing a subcone
\alpha\subseteq\sigma
is
\mathrm{mf}(\alpha)=\sigma\cap(\alpha-\sigma)
.

Proof.

As
\alpha\subseteq\sigma
are cones, so are
\alpha-\sigma
and
\sigma\cap(\alpha-\sigma)
. To check that
\sigma\cap(\alpha-\sigma)
is a face of
\sigma
, let
b,b^{\prime}\in\sigma
with
b+b^{\prime}=a-c
where
a\in\alpha
and
c\in\sigma
. Then
b=a-(b^{\prime}+c)\in\alpha-\sigma
and similarly for
b^{\prime}
. Hence
\sigma\cap(\alpha-\sigma)\leq\sigma
is a face.

Suppose
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
containing
\alpha
. If
b=a-c\in\sigma\cap(\alpha-\sigma)
then
b+c=a\in\alpha\subseteq\tau
. Hence
b\in\tau
and so
\sigma\cap(\alpha-\sigma)\subseteq\tau
. Thus it is the minimal face containing
\alpha
.
∎

Remark 1.3.

The
minimal face
of a cone
\sigma
is
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}\coloneqq\mathrm{mf}(0)=\sigma\cap(-\sigma)
. This is the maximal subgroup contained in
\sigma
. The cone
\sigma
is strictly convex if and only if
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}=\{0\}
.

1.3.
Fans

Definition 1.4.

A
fan

\Delta
in
\Lambda
is a set of cones in
\Lambda
such that (a)
\Delta
is closed under taking faces and (b) any two cones in
\Delta
intersect in a common face.

A fan
\Delta
in
\Lambda
is called

•

polyhedral
or
smooth
if all cones in
\Delta
have this property;

•

finite
if
\Delta
is a finite set of cones;

•

complete
if the union of all cones in
\Delta
is
\Lambda
.

Example 1.5.

If
\sigma
is a cone then
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
is a fan. It is polyhedral (and also finite) or smooth precisely when
\sigma
is so. Its support is the cone
\sigma
; it is complete only when
\sigma=\Lambda
.

Remark 1.6.

Any fan
\Delta
is generated by its maximal cones, i.e.
\Delta=\bigcup_{\sigma\in M}\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
where
M\subseteq\Delta
is the subset of maximal cones with respect to the face relation. Conversely, given a set
N
of cones in
\Lambda
, the union
\bigcup_{\sigma\in N}\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
is a fan if the intersection of any two cones in
N
is a common face. It suffices to check this for any two generating cones in
N
.

1.4.
Cofaces

We introduce the cofaces of a cone as localisations at a subcone: a commutative monoid with the cancellation property, e.g. a cone, naturally embeds in its (Grothendieck) group of differences.

The terminology ‘coface’ is motivated by Lemma
1.10
below which shows that every coface is the localisation at a face, and yields a bijection between the faces and cofaces of a cone.

Definition 1.7.

A
coface
of
\sigma
is the localisation
\sigma-\kappa
of
\sigma
at a subcone
\kappa
. We write
\sigma-\kappa\preccurlyeq\sigma
.

While faces are subsets of a cone, cofaces are supersets: the coface relation
\sigma\preccurlyeq\sigma^{\prime}
implies an inclusion in the reverse order
\sigma\supseteq\sigma^{\prime}
.
In Lemma
1.9
, we will show that the coface relation
\preccurlyeq
partially orders the cones in
\Lambda
. Thus we have two posets,

\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda)\coloneqq(\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda),\leq)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{Cones}_{c}(\Lambda)\coloneqq(\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda),\preccurlyeq)

with the same underlying set, ordered respectively by the face and coface relations.
In analogy with the subposet of faces, we also define the subposet of cofaces of the cone
\sigma
:

\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)=\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda)_{\leq\sigma}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)\coloneqq\mathsf{Cones}_{c}(\Lambda)_{\preccurlyeq\sigma}

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
\kappa\subseteq\sigma
be cones in
\Lambda
. Localisation at
\kappa
induces a poset isomorphism

\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)_{\supseteq\kappa}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma-\kappa),\quad\tau\mapsto\tau-\kappa

from the poset of faces of
\sigma
containing
\kappa
to that of faces of
\sigma-\kappa
. The inverse is
\nu\mapsto\nu\cap\sigma
.

Proof.

Given faces
\alpha
,
\beta
with

\kappa\subseteq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\sigma
,
we first show
\alpha-\kappa\leq\beta-\kappa
. This implies that the map of the lemma is well defined (use
\beta=\sigma
) and monotone.
The inclusion
\alpha-\kappa\subseteq\beta-\kappa
is obvious.
If
(b-k)+(b^{\prime}-k^{\prime})=a-k^{\prime\prime}
for some
a\in\alpha
,
b,b^{\prime}\in\beta
and
k,k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\in\kappa
then
b+b^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime}=a+k+k^{\prime}\in\alpha
. Hence
b,b^{\prime}\in\alpha
and
b-k,b^{\prime}-k^{\prime}\in\alpha-\kappa
, and thus
\alpha-\kappa\leq\beta-\kappa
.

Now let
\alpha\leq\sigma-\kappa
; we will show
\alpha=(\alpha\cap\sigma)-\kappa
. Clearly
\alpha\cap\sigma\leq\sigma
is a face. If
k\in\kappa
and
a\in\alpha\cap\sigma
then
k+(a-k)=a\in\alpha
and so
k,a-k\in\alpha
. Hence
\kappa\subseteq\alpha\cap\sigma
, and also
(\alpha\cap\sigma)-\kappa\subseteq\alpha
.
For the reverse inclusion, suppose
a\in\alpha
. Then
a=b-k
for some
b\in\sigma
and
k\in\kappa
. Then
a+k\in\alpha\cap\sigma
, so
a=(a+k)-k\in(\alpha\cap\sigma)-\kappa
.

Finally, we show
\alpha=(\alpha-\kappa)\cap\sigma
for any face
\alpha
of
\sigma
containing
\kappa
. Clearly
\alpha\subseteq(\alpha-\kappa)\cap\sigma
. In the other direction, if
a\in(\alpha-\kappa)\cap\sigma
then
a+k\in\alpha
for some
k\in\kappa
, and thus
a\in\alpha
. Hence
(\alpha-\kappa)\cap\sigma\subseteq\alpha
and we have equality.
∎

Lemma 1.9
.

The set
\mathsf{Cones}_{c}(\Lambda)
of cones in
\Lambda
with the coface relation
\preccurlyeq
is a partial order.

Proof.

The relation
\preccurlyeq
is reflexive because
\sigma=\sigma-\{0\}\preccurlyeq\sigma
. It is anti-symmetric because if
\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau
then
\sigma\supseteq\tau
. Finally, it is transitive because if
\sigma^{\prime}=(\sigma-\kappa)-\nu
for cones
\kappa\subseteq\sigma
and
\nu\subseteq\sigma-\kappa
then
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma-\sigma\cap(-\sigma^{\prime})
. To see this note first that
\sigma^{\prime}\supseteq\sigma-(\sigma\cap(-\sigma^{\prime}))
. Then for the other containment, choose
a^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\prime}
and write it in the form

a^{\prime}=(a-k)-(b-l)=(a+l)-(b+k)

where
a,b\in\sigma
and
k,l\in\kappa
with
b-l\in\nu
. Then observe that
b+k=(b-l)+(k+l)\in-\sigma^{\prime}
, because
\nu,\kappa\subseteq-\sigma^{\prime}
. Hence
b+k\in\sigma\cap(-\sigma^{\prime})
and so
a^{\prime}=(a+l)-(b+k)\in\sigma-\sigma\cap(-\sigma^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
\kappa\subseteq\sigma
be cones in
\Lambda
. Then
\sigma-\kappa=\sigma-\mathrm{mf}(\kappa)
is the localisation at the minimal face
\mathrm{mf}(\kappa)
of
\sigma
containing
\kappa
, and the following map is a poset isomorphism:

\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma){}^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma),\quad\tau\mapsto\sigma-\tau.

The inverse map is
\kappa\mapsto\kappa_{\mathrm{min}}\cap\sigma
where
\kappa_{\mathrm{min}}=\kappa\cap(-\kappa)
is the minimal face of
\kappa
.

Proof.

Clearly
\sigma-\kappa\subseteq\sigma-\mathrm{mf}(\kappa)
. Given
a\in\mathrm{mf}(\kappa)
, there is
b\in\sigma
with
a+b\in\kappa
by Lemma
1.2
. Therefore
-a=b-(a+b)\in\sigma-\kappa
. Hence
\sigma-\mathrm{mf}(\kappa)\subseteq\sigma-\kappa
and we have equality. This shows that the map is surjective.

Suppose
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
with
\sigma-\tau=\sigma-\tau^{\prime}
. Then for any
t\in\tau
there are
t^{\prime}\in\tau^{\prime}
and
a\in\sigma
with
-t=a-t^{\prime}
. Hence
t+a=t^{\prime}\in\tau^{\prime}
and so
t\in\tau^{\prime}
because
\tau^{\prime}
is a face. Thus
\tau\subseteq\tau^{\prime}
and by symmetry
\tau=\tau^{\prime}
. Thus the map is bijective.

The map is monotone because
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
implies
\sigma-\tau^{\prime}=(\sigma-\tau)-\tau^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\sigma-\tau
. To obtain the inverse note that if
\kappa=\sigma-\tau
then
\kappa_{\mathrm{min}}=(\sigma-\tau)_{\mathrm{min}}=\tau-\tau
by Lemma
1.8
. Since
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
we conclude
\tau=\kappa_{\mathrm{min}}\cap\sigma
. This map is also monotone.
∎

Lemma 1.11
.

The posets
\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda)
and
\mathsf{Cones}_{c}(\Lambda)
have height
\mathrm{rk}\,(\Lambda)
, i.e. the longest chains have
\mathrm{rk}\,(\Lambda)+1
elements.

Proof.

For
\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda)
this follows because a proper face has strictly lower dimension. To see this suppose
\tau\leq\sigma
and
\dim\tau=\dim\sigma
. Then
\tau-\tau
is a finite index subgroup of
\sigma-\sigma
. Thus, given
a\in\sigma
there are
b,b^{\prime}\in\tau
such that
na=b-b^{\prime}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Rearranging,
na+b^{\prime}=b\in\tau
and since
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
we conclude that
a\in\tau
. Therefore
\tau=\sigma
.

For
\mathsf{Cones}_{c}(\Lambda)
it follows from the above and Lemma
1.10
. (Note that the dimension of a coface is the same as that of the ambient cone.)
∎

1.5.
Cofans

These are the coface-analogues of fans: a cofan is a collection of cones that is (a) closed under cofaces and such that (b) the sum of two cones is a coface of each — it is the equivalent of a fan with ‘face’ replaced by ‘coface’ and ‘intersection’ replaced by ‘sum’. We rephrase this definition below using a formulation more convenient for proofs.

Definition 1.12.

A
cofan
in
\Lambda
is a set
\nabla
of cones in
\Lambda
such that

(a)

if
\tau
is a face of
\sigma\in\nabla
then
\sigma-\tau\in\nabla
;

(b)

if
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\nabla
then
\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma-\tau
for a face
\tau\leq\sigma
.

Example 1.13.

If
\sigma
is a cone in
\Lambda
then
\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)
is a cofan: it is closed under localisation at faces because the coface relation is transitive. If
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\leq\sigma
are faces then the sum

(\sigma-\tau)+(\sigma-\tau^{\prime})=\sigma-(\tau+\tau^{\prime})=\sigma-\mathrm{mf}(\tau+\tau^{\prime})=(\sigma-\tau)-(\mathrm{mf}(\tau+\tau^{\prime})-\tau)

is a coface of
\sigma-\tau
, as required for (b). Here
\mathrm{mf}(\tau+\tau^{\prime})-\tau
is a face of
\sigma-\tau
by Lemma
1.8
since
\mathrm{mf}(\tau+\tau^{\prime})\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)_{\supseteq\tau}
.

Remark 1.14.

Any cofan
\nabla
is generated by its maximal cones, i.e.
\nabla=\bigcup_{\sigma\in M}\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)
where
M\subseteq\nabla
is the subset of cones which are maximal for the coface relation. Under inclusion of subsets, these are the minimal cones in
\nabla
. Conversely, a set
N
of cones in
\Lambda
generates a cofan
\bigcup_{\sigma\in N}\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)
if, and only if, the sum of any two cones in
N
is a common coface.

Lemma-Definition 1.15
.

If
\nabla
is a cofan in
\Lambda
then
\Delta\coloneqq\bigcup_{\sigma\in\nabla}\mathsf{Faces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
is a fan in
\Lambda^{*}
. We call
\Delta
the
associated fan
.

Proof.

Since the face relation is transitive,
\Delta
contains all faces of its cones. To show that it is a fan it therefore suffices to show that any two cones intersect in a common face. In fact it suffices to check this for maximal cones, i.e. cones which are not proper faces of other cones; see Remark
1.6
. Maximal cones have the form
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in\nabla
. Given
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\nabla
, their intersection is

\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\mathbb{D}(\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\tau)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\tau^{\perp}

for some face
\tau\leq\sigma
by the cofan property. Therefore the intersection is a common face as required.
∎

We define the following attributes of cofans by analogy with fans. A cofan
\nabla
in
\Lambda
is

•

polyhedral
if each cone in
\nabla
is polyhedral;

•

smooth
if each cone in
\nabla
is a coface of a smooth cone (not necessarily in
\nabla
);

•

complete
if for each
v\in\Lambda^{*}
there is some
\sigma\in\nabla
with
v|_{\sigma}\geq 0
;

•

finite
if
\nabla
is a finite set of cones.

If
\nabla
is polyhedral then the dual cone
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
of each
\sigma\in\nabla
is polyhedral (by Gordan’s Lemma), and therefore the associated fan
\Delta
is polyhedral. Likewise the associated fan of a smooth cofan is smooth. If
\nabla
is complete then so is
\Delta
.
The associated fan of a finite cofan may be infinite.

Fans can be pulled back, and cofans pushed forward, along homomorphisms.

Lemma 1.16
.

Let
f\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
be a homomorphism of lattices. Then

(1)

If
\nabla
is a cofan in
\Lambda
then
f(\nabla)=\{f(\sigma)\mid\sigma\in\nabla\}
is a cofan in
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
\Delta^{\prime}
is a fan in
\Lambda^{\prime}
then
f^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})=\{f^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime})\mid\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}\}
is a fan in
\Lambda
.

(3)

If
\Delta
is the fan associated to
\nabla
then
(f^{*})^{-1}(\Delta)
is the fan associated to
f(\nabla)
.

Proof.

The image and preimage of a cone under a homomorphism are also cones. Moreover, it is easy to check that the image of a coface is a coface of the image, and that the preimage of a face is a face of the preimage. In fact, more generally,

\mathsf{Cofaces}(f(\sigma))=f(\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{Faces}(f^{-1}(\sigma))=f^{-1}(\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)).

For the first, note that if
\tau\leq f(\sigma)
then
f(\sigma)-\tau=f(\sigma-f^{-1}(\tau)\cap\sigma)
is the image of a coface of
\sigma
. For the second, suppose
\tau\leq f^{-1}(\sigma)
is a face. Then we claim that
\tau=f^{-1}(\mathrm{mf}(f(\tau)))
is the preimage of the minimal face of
\sigma
containing
f(\tau)
. First note that
\tau=f^{-1}(f(\tau))
because
\tau
contains the maximal subgroup in
f^{-1}(\sigma)
, and this contains
\ker f
. Then, using the formula for the minimal face from Lemma
1.2
, we have

f^{-1}(\mathrm{mf}(f(\tau)))=f^{-1}(\sigma\cap(f(\tau)-\sigma))=f^{-1}(\sigma)\cap f^{-1}(f(\tau)-\sigma)=f^{-1}(\sigma)\cap(\tau-f^{-1}(\sigma)).

This is the minimal face of
f^{-1}(\sigma)
containing
\tau
, i.e.
\tau
itself as claimed.
Therefore
f(\nabla)
is closed under taking cofaces, and
(f^{-1})(\Delta^{\prime})
closed under taking faces.

To show that sums in
f(\nabla)
are common cofaces, let
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\nabla
and
\tau\leq\sigma
with
\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma-\tau
. Then we have

f(\sigma)+f(\sigma^{\prime})=f(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})=f(\sigma-\tau)=f(\sigma)-f(\tau).

Thus
f(\nabla)
is a cofan.

To show that intersections in
(f^{-1})(\Delta^{\prime})
are common faces, take
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}
and compute

f^{-1}(\sigma)\cap f^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime})=f^{-1}(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})=f^{-1}(\tau)

for some face
\tau\leq\sigma
. Thus
f^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})
is a fan.

Finally, since
\mathbb{D}(f(\sigma))=(f^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
for any cone
\sigma
, the fan associated to
f(\nabla)
is indeed

\bigcup_{\sigma\in\nabla}\mathsf{Faces}\big(\mathbb{D}(f(\sigma))\big)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\nabla}\mathsf{Faces}\big((f^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))\big)=(f^{*})^{-1}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\nabla}\mathsf{Faces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma)).\qed

1.6.
Left adjoint to dualisation

One reason we consider two different partial orders (face and coface relations) on the set of cones in
\Lambda
is that the dualisation map

\mathbb{D}\colon\mathsf{Cones}_{c}(\Lambda)\to\mathsf{Cones}_{f}(\Lambda^{*}),\quad\sigma\mapsto\mathbb{D}(\sigma)=\{v\in\Lambda^{*}\mid v|_{\sigma}\geq 0\}

is monotone.
It also takes sums to intersections, i.e. preserves limits, but (nevertheless) does not have a left adjoint. However, its restriction
\mathbb{D}\colon\nabla\to\Delta
to a map from a cofan to its associated fan is well defined (denoted by the same symbol) and does have a left adjoint

\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}\colon\Delta\to\nabla,\quad\tau\mapsto\min\{\sigma\in\nabla\mid\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\}

which we call the
lowest coface map
.
The existence of the minimum defining
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)
is the key part of the following result; we include the cofan in the notation because the adjoint depends upon it.
Neither of the dualisation maps is injective or surjective in general.

Proposition 1.17
.

Let
\nabla
be a cofan with associated fan
\Delta
. Then:

(1)

The map
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}
is left adjoint to
\mathbb{D}
.

(2)

If
\nabla^{\prime}\subseteq\nabla
is a subcofan then
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla^{\prime}}=\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}|_{\Delta^{\prime}}
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is the fan associated to
\nabla^{\prime}
.

(3)

If
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in\nabla
then
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)=\sigma-\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Fix
\tau\in\Delta
. To define
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}
, we first find
\sigma\in\Delta
such that
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
is minimal with
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
. We then show that among the
\sigma\in\nabla
with the same dual cone
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
there is a minimal such.

By definition
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in\nabla
. If
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma^{\prime})
too then
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\mathbb{D}(\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})
and
\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}\in\nabla
as cofans are closed under sums. Thus we can construct a descending chain of cones in
\Delta
of which
\tau
is a face. By Lemma
1.11
, this chain terminates. Therefore we can find
\sigma\in\nabla
such that
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
is minimal with
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
.

If
\sigma^{\prime}\in\nabla
has
\mathbb{D}(\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
then
\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
is a common coface, and
\mathbb{D}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
. Thus we can construct a descending chain of cofaces of
\sigma
with the same dual. Again by Lemma
1.11
, this chain must terminate. This shows that
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}
exists. It is immediate that
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)\preccurlyeq\sigma\iff\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
, so that the maps are adjoint.

The description
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)=\min\{\sigma\in\nabla\mid\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\}
, together with the fact that cofans are closed under taking cofaces, implies
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla^{\prime}}=\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}|_{\Delta^{\prime}}
for any subcofan
\nabla^{\prime}
of
\nabla
.

For the last part, suppose
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
and consider the subcofan
\nabla^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)
. If
\kappa
is a face of
\sigma
with
\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\kappa^{\perp}
then
\kappa\subseteq\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}
. Since
\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
we get the desired formula:

\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)=\mathbb{L}_{\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)}(\tau)=\min\{\sigma-\kappa\mid\tau\leq\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)\}=\sigma-\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}.

∎

1.7.
Exposed faces, dual faces and dual face fans

An adjunction between posets is known as a
Galois connection
. Every Galois connection restricts to an order isomorphism between the image subposets, called a Galois correspondence. To describe these for the adjunction between a cofan and its associated fan, we need the following definitions. Readers mostly interested in the (co)fan of an abelian category may proceed to Section
2
.

We discuss dual face fans for two reasons: First, we don’t have categorical descriptions for the cones in a heart fan but we do for the associated dual face fan (which is a subset of the heart fan); see Remark
3.13
. This is used in Remark
3.14
(thick label map) and Section
7
. Second, we want to study how the standard case of polyhedral fans unfolds in greater generality.

Definition 1.18.

An
exposed face
of a cone
\sigma
is a subset
\sigma\cap v^{\perp}
for some
v\in\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
.
Let
\mathsf{ExFaces}(\sigma)
be the subposet of
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
consisting of exposed faces.

A
dual face
of
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
is a subset of the form
\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)
for some face
\kappa\leq\sigma
.
Let
\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
be the subposet of
\mathsf{Faces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
of dual faces; it depends on
\sigma
, and not just on its dual
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
.

The sets of exposed and dual faces are closed under intersection. For exposed faces this is because
(\sigma\cap v^{\perp})\cap(\sigma\cap w^{\perp})=\sigma\cap(v+w)^{\perp}
for
v,w\in\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
. For dual faces it follows because

\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)\cap\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}((\sigma-\kappa)+(\sigma-\kappa^{\prime}))=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-(\kappa+\kappa^{\prime}))

for faces
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
of
\sigma
.

An exposed face is a face, but not necessarily conversely; see Example
2.12
. By the first part of the next lemma, every dual face is exposed; in Example
1.22
we will see that every dual face is dual to the localisation at an exposed face. However,
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
can have exposed faces which are not dual faces; see Example
4.12
.

Lemma 1.19
.

Let
\sigma\subseteq\Lambda
be a cone,
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma
subcones and
\tau\leq\sigma
a face. Then:

(1)

The dual face
\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap k^{\perp}
for some
k\in\kappa
.

(2)

Dual faces
\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa^{\prime})
agree if, and only if, the minimal exposed faces containing
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
agree.

(3)

There is a finite chain
\tau=\sigma_{n}<\cdots<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{0}=\sigma

with each
\sigma_{i}<\sigma_{i-1}
an exposed face.

Proof.

Parts (1) and (2) both follow from the fact that exposed faces are closed under pairwise intersection and form a finite height poset, hence are closed under arbitrary intersection since descending chains must stabilise.
For (1), let
\kappa\subseteq\sigma
be a subcone; then

\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\kappa^{\perp}=\bigcap_{k\in\kappa}\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap k^{\perp}=\bigcap_{i=0}^{m}\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap k_{i}^{\perp}=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap(k_{0}+\cdots+k_{m})^{\perp}

for some
k_{0},\ldots,k_{m}\in\kappa
. In particular, every dual face is exposed.

For (2) write
\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\kappa^{\perp}=\{v\in\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\mid\kappa\subseteq\sigma\cap v^{\perp}\}
. Thus
\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa^{\prime})
precisely when the sets of exposed faces containing
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
agree. Since these sets are closed under arbitrary intersection, this is equivalent to asking that the minimal exposed faces containing
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
are the same.

For (3), if
\tau=\sigma
we are done, so we assume
\tau
is a proper face. It suffices to construct a proper
exposed
face
\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
containing
\tau
, and then proceed inductively starting with
\tau\leq\sigma^{\prime}
. The process terminates because
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
has finite height. When
\tau<\sigma
is a proper face then

\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\tau^{\perp}=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\tau)\neq\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\sigma)=\sigma^{\perp}.

Choose
v\in\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\tau^{\perp}
with
v\notin\sigma^{\perp}
so that

\sigma^{\prime}\coloneqq\sigma\cap v^{\perp}
is a proper exposed face containing
\tau
.
∎

It follows from the above lemma that
\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
is a subposet of
\mathsf{ExFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
. Let
\mathsf{CoExFaces}(\sigma)\coloneqq\{\sigma-\kappa\mid\kappa\in\mathsf{ExFaces}(\sigma)\}
be the subposet of
\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)
consisting of the localisations at exposed faces of the cone
\sigma
. We refer to these as
co-exposed faces
.

Corollary 1.20
.

For any cofan
\nabla
, the adjunction
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}\dashv\mathbb{D}
restricts to an isomorphism between the images of
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}
, and there is a commutative diagram of monotone maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigcup\limits_{\mathclap{\sigma\in M}}\mathsf{CoExFaces}(\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\Delta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}(\nabla)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigcup\limits_{\mathclap{\sigma\in M}}\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma)).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}}

where
M\subseteq\nabla
is the subset of maximal cones for the coface relation.

Proof.

Any Galois connection restricts to a Galois correspondence between the images; in our case this yields the above diagram.

The equality
\mathbb{D}(\nabla)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in M}\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
follows because any cone in
\nabla
is a coface of some maximal cone. For the other equality, note similarly that any
\tau\in\Delta
is a face of
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in M
. Thus
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)=\sigma-\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}\in\mathsf{CoExFaces}(\sigma)
by the last part of Proposition
1.17
and Lemma
1.19
(1).
Now consider a co-exposed face
\sigma-\kappa
. By the same reasoning as above
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\sigma-\kappa^{\prime}
for some exposed face
\kappa^{\prime}
of
\sigma
. But then
\kappa^{\prime}=\kappa
by the last part of Lemma
1.19
because

\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)

by the adjunction triangular identity.
Therefore every co-exposed face of
\sigma
is in the image of
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}
.
∎

Remark 1.21.

We refer to
\mathbb{D}(\nabla)
as the
dual face fan
associated to the cofan
\nabla
. It is a set of dual cones in
\Lambda^{*}
which is

(a)

closed under taking dual faces and such that

(b)

the intersection of any two cones is a common dual face of each.

The first follows because if
\sigma\in\nabla
and
\kappa\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
then
\sigma-\kappa\in\nabla
; the second because if
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\nabla
then
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\cap\mathbb{D}(\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma+\sigma^{\prime})=\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)=\mathbb{D}(\sigma^{\prime}-\kappa^{\prime})
for some faces
\kappa\leq\sigma
and
\kappa^{\prime}\leq\sigma^{\prime}
. In general
\mathbb{D}(\nabla)
has fewer cones than
\Delta
but the same support:

\bigcup_{\sigma\in\nabla}\mathbb{D}(\sigma)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Delta}\sigma
.

Example 1.22 (Coface cofans).

The simplest example is the cofan
\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)
from a single cone
\sigma
. The associated fan is
\mathsf{Faces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
and
\mathbb{L}_{\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma)}(\tau)=\sigma-\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}
.

The corresponding dual face fan is
\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
and the Galois connection restricts to an isomorphism
\mathbb{D}\colon\mathsf{CoExFaces}(\sigma)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
. Composing with localisation is injective by Lemma
1.10
and surjective by definition of co-exposed face, hence yields an isomorphism

\mathsf{ExFaces}(\sigma){}^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathsf{DualFaces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))
,
\kappa\mapsto\mathbb{D}(\sigma-\kappa)

with inverse
\tau\mapsto\sigma\cap\tau^{\perp}
.

The Galois connection is a poset isomorphism, and so agrees with the Galois correspondence, precisely when all faces of
\sigma
and of
\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
are exposed.

Example 1.23 (Polyhedral cofans: toric varieties and
g
-fans).

The situation is simpler for a polyhedral cofan
\nabla
, i.e. a cofan all of whose cones are polyhedral. Every face of a polyhedral cone is exposed and the dual of a polyhedral cone is again polyhedral. Hence the fan
\Delta
associated to
\nabla
is polyhedral as well. Therefore
\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\tau)=\mathbb{D}(\tau)
for
\tau\in\Delta
, and
\mathbb{D}
is involutive. The Galois connection and correspondence coincide, and the commuting diagram of Corollary
1.20
boils down to the single poset isomorphism

\mathbb{D}\colon\mathbb{L}_{\nabla}(\Delta)=\nabla\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\Delta=\mathbb{D}(\nabla)
.

The associated fan construction is the reverse of the process used to construct a toric variety: there one starts with a finite polyhedral fan, constructs the associated cofan of monoids in the dual lattice, and glues together the spectra of the algebras generated by these monoids to obtain the toric variety. The polyhedral condition ensures that the algebras are finitely generated, and the finiteness that the variety is quasi-compact.
Thus one can construct a toric variety directly from a finite polyhedral cofan; the associated fan of the cofan is the fan of the toric variety. More generally, one can construct a toric scheme from an arbitrary cofan.

The
g
-fan of a finite-dimensional algebra is polyhedral, and even smooth. The cofan underlying the
g
-fan is explained in algebraic terms in Section
8
where we also show that the heart fan of the module category is a completion of the
g
-fan, which need no longer be polyhedral.

1.8.
Real cofans and fans

Classical fans of rational cones are the real versions of all of the above. Let
\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}})
be the set of real cones in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
, i.e. subsets closed under addition and scalar multiplication by elements of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
; see Remark
1.1
. The notions defined in this section for integral cones extend to real cones.
For example, the dual of a real cone
\sigma\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
is

\mathbb{D}(\sigma)\coloneqq\{v\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\mid v|_{\sigma}\geq 0\}
.
This is a closed cone and, by the Hyperplane Separation Theorem,
\mathbb{D}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))=\overline{\sigma}
.
Given a subset
S\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
, the dual of the real cone
E(S)_{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted
C(S)\coloneqq\mathbb{D}(E(S)_{\mathbb{R}})
.
A cone in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by a subset of an
\mathbb{R}
-basis is called
simplicial
.

Integral and real cones are compatible in a strong sense; this simple behaviour arises because
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a cone in
\mathbb{R}
with only itself and
\{0\}
as faces. The compatibility arises from a Galois connection between cones in
\Lambda
and in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
ordered by inclusion:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Cones}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)_{\mathbb{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\cap\Lambda}

where
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\conv(\sigma)
is the convex hull of
\sigma
in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. The unit of this adjunction is the inclusion
\sigma\subseteq\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\cap\Lambda
of an integral cone in its
saturation

\mathrm{sat}(\sigma)\coloneqq\{a\in\Lambda\mid na\in\sigma\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\}
,
and the counit is the inclusion
(\tau\cap\Lambda)_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\tau
of the
rationalisation
of a real cone. The Galois connection therefore restricts to a correspondence between saturated cones (i.e. those equal to their saturation) in
\Lambda
and rational cones in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The following lemma is immediate.

Lemma 1.24
.

The left adjoint
\sigma\mapsto\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}
preserves cofaces and is monotone for the coface relation; the right adjoint
\tau\mapsto\tau\cap\Lambda
preserves faces and is monotone for the face relation.

Lemma 1.25
.

If
\sigma
is a cone in
\Lambda
then there is a commutative diagram of poset isomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma_{\mathbb{R}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma){}^{\mathrm{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Cofaces}(\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}){}^{\mathrm{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau\,\mapsto\,\sigma-\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau\,\mapsto\,\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu\,\mapsto\,\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}-\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma-\tau\,\mapsto\,(\sigma-\tau)_{\mathbb{R}}}

Proof.

The vertical maps are poset isomorphisms by Lemma
1.10
, and its analogue for real cones which has verbatim the same proof. The diagram commutes because
(\sigma-\tau)_{\mathbb{R}}=\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}-\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}
. Hence it suffices to show that
\tau\mapsto\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}
is a well-defined poset isomorphism
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)\to\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma_{\mathbb{R}})
.

First we verify the map is well defined, i.e. that
\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}
is a face of
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}
. If
\tau=\sigma\cap v^{\perp}
for some
v\in\mathbb{D}(\sigma)
is an
exposed
face then
\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}=(\sigma\cap v^{\perp})_{\mathbb{R}}=\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\cap v^{\perp}
is an exposed face of
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}
. The general case follows inductively because by Lemma
1.19
(3) for any face
\tau
there is a finite chain
\tau=\sigma_{n}<\cdots<\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{0}=\sigma
in
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
in which
\sigma_{i}
is an exposed face of
\sigma_{i-1}
. Since the map preserves inclusions it is monotone for the face relation because a face of a cone is also a face of any subcone containing it.

We claim the inverse is
\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma_{\mathbb{R}})\to\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
,
\kappa\mapsto\kappa\cap\sigma
.
It is easily seen to be monotone. Moreover, each face of a rational cone must be rational: if some positive real combination of lattice elements in a cone lies in a face, then each of those lattice elements lies in that face too. It follows that
\kappa=(\kappa\cap\sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Now consider
\tau\in\mathsf{Faces}(\sigma)
. The composite
\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}\cap\Lambda
is the saturation of
\tau
. Hence
\tau=(\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}\cap\Lambda)\cap\sigma=\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}\cap\sigma
. This completes the proof.
∎

The next result shows that any integral cofan has a real version whose cones are simply the convex hulls of the cones of the cofan; we call this the
realification
of the integral cofan. Likewise, any real fan has an integral version, but in this case we do not give an explicit description of the cones as this will depend on whether the fan has irrational cones or not.

Corollary 1.26
.

If
\nabla
is a cofan in
\Lambda
then
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\{\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\sigma\in\nabla\}
is a cofan in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
.

If
\Delta
is a fan in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
then
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\tau\in\Delta}\mathsf{Faces}(\tau\cap\Lambda)
is a fan in
\Lambda
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.25
the set of cones
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}
is closed under cofaces. Moreover, sums of its cones are common cofaces because
\sigma_{\mathbb{R}}+\tau_{\,\mathbb{R}}=(\sigma+\tau)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}
is a cofan in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
.

By construction
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a set of cones in
\Lambda
closed under faces. To show it is a fan, it suffices to check that the intersection of any two maximal cones is a common face. This follows from Lemma
1.24
as
(\tau\cap\Lambda)\cap(\tau^{\prime}\cap\Lambda)=(\tau\cap\tau^{\prime})\cap\Lambda
and
\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}
is a common face of
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
.
∎

Example 1.27.

Consider the cofan
\nabla=\{\sigma,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
where
\sigma=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid n>\sqrt{2}m\}
. The associated fan
\Delta=\mathsf{Faces}(\mathbb{D}(\sigma))=\{0\}
but the fan
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}
associated to the realification
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}
is generated by the ray through
(-\sqrt{2},1)
.
This shows that one cannot, in general, construct the associated real fan
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}
from the associated integral fan
\Delta
.

Remark 1.28.

The real analogue of Lemma
1.16
about the functoriality of fans and cofans under a base change
f\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
holds, with the same proof, for real fans and cofans and a linear map
V\to V^{\prime}
.
Moreover, this is compatible with passing to or from the real version when
f_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq f\otimes\mathbb{R}\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
in the sense that

(1)

f(\nabla)_{\mathbb{R}}=f_{\mathbb{R}}(\nabla_{\mathbb{R}})
for an integral cofan
\nabla
in
\Lambda
,

(2)

f^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Z}})=f_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Z}}
for a real fan
\Delta^{\prime}
in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
.

Remark 1.29 (integral cofans and real fans).

Starting from an integral cofan
\nabla
, we have defined the real cofan
\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}
and their associated fans
\Delta
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}
. Out of these four objects, only two appear in the subsequent sections: the integral cofan of a heart
\mathsfit{H}
that for emphasis we denote
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
and the associated fan of the real cofan that we denote
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
. Our philosophy is that the integral cofan carries all the relevant information and is the most basic object whereas the real fan is the familiar convex-geometric object.

2.
Effective cone and heart cone of an abelian category

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an abelian category and
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
a homomorphism to a lattice, i.e. a free abelian group of finite rank. We call this setup an ‘abelian category over a lattice’ and write
\mathsfit{H}/\Lambda
.

Remark 2.1.

Classes
[h]\in K(\mathsfit{H})
play a prominent role in this article. The
null subcategory
is
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}\coloneqq\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid 0=[h]\in K(\mathsfit{H})\}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
. It can happen that
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}=\mathsfit{H}
, e.g. whenever
\mathsfit{H}
allows countable coproducts, using a variant of the Eilenberg swindle. The categories we have in mind feature
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}=0
instead, but see Example
2.7
for an exception.
Our main examples are:

•

\mathsfit{H}=\mathsf{mod}(A)
, the category of finitely generated left
A
-modules over a finite-dimensional algebra
A
. In this case,
\Lambda\coloneqq K(\mathsfit{H})
is a free abelian group generated by the pairwise non-isomorphic simple
A
-modules. A module
M\in\mathsfit{H}
with
0=[M]\in K(\mathsfit{H})
has vanishing dimension vector, hence
M=0
.
The same reasoning applies to any length category
\mathsfit{H}
.

•

\mathsfit{H}=\coh(X)
, the category of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective variety
X
.
Then
K(X)=K(\mathsfit{H})
may have infinite rank or torsion; let
\Lambda\coloneqq N(X)
be the numerical Grothendieck group, i.e. the quotient of
K(X)
by the radical of the Euler pairing. This is a lattice; see
[
Ta17
]
for a generalisation. A coherent sheaf
F\in\mathsfit{H}
with
0=[F]\in N(X)
has vanishing Hilbert polynomial by the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem, forcing
F=0
. In particular,
N{H}=0
.

Definition 2.2.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an abelian category over a lattice
\Lambda
. We call

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}ll}E(\mathsfit{H})&\coloneqq\{a_{1}\lambda(h_{1})+\cdots+a_{n}\lambda(h_{n})\mid n\in\mathbb{N},a_{i}\in\mathbb{N},h_{i}\in\mathsfit{H}\}\subseteq\Lambda&\text{the \emph{{effective cone}} of $\mathsfit{H}$ and}\\
C(\mathsfit{H})&\coloneqq\{v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\mid v(h)\geq 0\text{ for all }h\in\mathsfit{H}\}\subseteq{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}&\text{the \emph{{heart cone}} of $\mathsfit{H}$.}\end{array}

Remark 2.3.

In words,
E(\mathsfit{H})
is the
integral
cone generated by the image of
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
.
The real version of the effective cone is its convex hull
E(\mathsfit{H})_{\mathbb{R}}=\conv(E(\mathsfit{H}))
inside
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. This is a linearisation, and often drastic simplification, of
E(\mathsfit{H})
itself.
The heart cone
C(\mathsfit{H})
is the
real
cone that is dual to the (real or integral) effective cone. In the notation of Section
1
, this is
C(\mathsfit{H})=\mathbb{D}(E(\mathsfit{H})_{\mathbb{R}})
.
Our choice to denote the integral cones
E(\mathsfit{H})
and the real cones
C(\mathsfit{H})
follows the philosophy outlined in Remark
1.29
.

We write
v|{H}\geq 0
to mean
v\in C(\mathsfit{H})
, expanding on the notational shortcut
v(h)=v(\lambda([h]))
. Hence we can rewrite the heart cone as

C(\mathsfit{H})=\{v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\mid v|{H}\geq 0\}
.

The terminology ‘effective cone’ is inspired by effective divisors or cycles in algebraic geometry.
We say ‘heart cone’ as the abelian categories will occur as hearts in triangulated categories.

If
\mathsfit{H}
is a lattice category, i.e.
K(\mathsfit{H})
is a lattice, its effective cone is just the Grothendieck monoid. These are subtle invariants. For example, Shunya Saito shows in
[
Sa22
]
that if
X
is a noetherian scheme then the Grothendieck monoid of
\coh(X)
determines the topological space underlying
X
.

Effective cones need be neither polyhedral nor strictly convex; see Examples
2.5
and
2.7
.
However, if
\mathsfit{H}
is a lattice category of finite length with the standard choice
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})
then
E(\mathsfit{H})
is smooth, so in particular polyhedral and strictly convex; see Proposition
2.9
.

Below,
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H},\lambda}\coloneqq\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid 0=\lambda(h)\in\Lambda\}
is the
null subcategory
of
\mathsfit{H}
as a category over
\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an abelian category over
\Lambda
.

(1)

If
E(\mathsfit{H})
is strictly convex then the null subcategory
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H},\lambda}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathsfit{H}
.

(2)

If
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
is surjective then
E(\mathsfit{H})
is full and
C(\mathsfit{H})
is strictly convex.

Proof.

(1) Assume
E(\mathsfit{H})
is strictly convex. Let
h\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H},\lambda}
and
0\to h^{\prime}\to h\to h^{\prime\prime}\to 0
be a short exact sequence. Then
\lambda(h^{\prime})+\lambda(h^{\prime\prime})=\lambda(h)=0
and strict convexity of
E(\mathsfit{H})
implies
\lambda(h^{\prime})=\lambda(h^{\prime\prime})=0
. Thus
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H},\lambda}
is closed under subobjects and quotients. As
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H},\lambda}
is always closed under extensions, it is a Serre subcategory.

(2) Assume
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
is surjective. Then
E(\mathsfit{H})
is a full cone because the images
\lambda(\mathsfit{H})
span
\Lambda
, and hence its integral dual cone is strictly convex and so is its convex hull
C(\mathsfit{H})
.
∎

The next examples exhibit effective cones that are not strictly convex or polyhedral. Moreover, distinct hearts inside a derived category can have the same effective or heart cone.

Example 2.5 (projective line).

Consider the category
\mathsfit{H}=\coh(\mathbb{P}^{1})
of coherent sheaves on the projective line. Then
\Lambda\coloneqq K(\mathsfit{H})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and we fix
[\mathcal{O}],[\mathcal{O}_{p}]
as an orthonormal basis, i.e.
[A]=(\mathrm{rk}\,(A),\deg(A))
for
A\in\mathsfit{H}
. Here
\mathcal{O}_{p}
denotes the length one skyscraper sheaf at a closed point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is the structure sheaf. We use the basis to identify
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{*}
.

As there is an infinite chain of line bundles, the effective cone
E(\mathsfit{H})
is the set of lattice points in a half-plane missing a boundary ray:
\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid m>0\text{ or }m=0,n\geq 0\}
. This cone is not polyhedral. The heart cone
C(\mathsfit{H})
is the ray
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. See Example
2.8
for a generalisation.

Example 2.6 (curves tilted in points).

Let
\mathsfit{H}=\coh(X)
be the category of coherent sheaves on a smooth, projective curve
X
over its numerical Grothendieck group
\Lambda\coloneqq N(X)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\lambda(A)=(\mathrm{rk}\,(A),\deg(A))
, as in Remark
2.1
.
Fix a subset
M\subseteq X
of closed points.

Let
\mathsfit{T}_{\!M}={\langle\mathcal{O}_{p}\mid p\in M\rangle}
be the subcategory generated by skyscraper sheaves at points in
M
and

\mathsfit{F}_{M}={\langle\mathrm{Pic}(X),\mathcal{O}_{q}\mid q\notin M\rangle}
be generated by all line bundles and the skyscraper sheaves at the remaining points.
Then
(\mathsfit{T}_{\!M},\mathsfit{F}_{M})
is a torsion pair, see Appendix
A.4
, and gives rise to a new heart

\mathsfit{H}_{M}\coloneqq\mathsfit{F}_{M}[1]*\mathsfit{T}_{\!M}

in
\mathsf{D}^{b}(X)
, see Appendix
A.6
.
Moreover,
(\mathsfit{T}_{\!M},\mathsfit{F}_{M})
is a
cotilting torsion pair
in
\mathsfit{H}
, that is each object of
\mathsfit{H}
is a quotient of an object of
\mathsfit{F}_{M}
.
This implies that
\mathsfit{H}_{M}
is the heart of a faithful bounded t-structure, i.e.
\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H}_{M})\cong\mathsf{D}^{b}(X)
; see
[
BvdB03
, Prop. 5.4.3]
.
For later reference, we introduce the following notation and terminology:

\displaystyle\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathsfit{H}_{X},

\displaystyle\text{the \emph{{reversed geometric heart}}};

\displaystyle\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathsfit{H}_{M},

\displaystyle\text{a \emph{{mixed geometric heart}} where $\varnothing\neq M\subsetneq X$}.

We don’t specify the subset
M
as it will never matter precisely which points are chosen.

Let
p,q\in X
be linearly equivalent points, i.e.
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-q)
. If moreover
p\in M
,
q\notin M
then
\mathcal{O}_{p},\mathcal{O}_{q}[1]\in\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(X)
.
The short exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{p}\to 0
and

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-q)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{q}\to 0
show

[\mathcal{O}_{p}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{q}[1]]=0\in K(\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(X))
. Thus the null subcategory
\mathsfit{N}_{\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(X),\lambda}
is non-zero and not closed under direct summands, so not a Serre subcategory.

Example 2.7 (projective line tilted in points).

We combine the previous two examples.
The Auslander–Reiten quiver of
\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
looks like that of
\coh(\mathbb{P}^{1})
, except that the skyscraper sheaves sit before the line bundles. In
\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
, only some of the skyscraper sheaves are tilted before the line bundles. The notation is supposed to suggest that all/some skyscraper sheaves appear at the opposite end of the Auslander–Reiten quiver of the category.

The categories
\coh(\mathbb{P}^{1}),\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
and
\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
have different effective cones

•

E(\coh{\mathbb{P}^{1}})=\{(m,n)\mid m>0\text{ or }m=0,n\geq 0\}
,

•

E(\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=\{(m,n)\mid m\geq 0\}
,

•

E(\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=\{(m,n)\mid m>0\text{ or }m=0,n\leq 0\}
,

but the same heart cone
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
.
The cone
E(\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))
is not strictly convex as it contains the subgroup
n=0
. It is the effective cone of infinitely many distinct hearts in
\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
.
The null subcategory,
\mathsfit{N}_{\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})}
, of
\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
mentioned in Remark
2.1
is non-zero.

Example 2.8 (coherent sheaves).

Let
X
be a connected, smooth and projective variety of dimension
d
and
\mathsfit{H}=\coh(X)
the category of coherent sheaves on
X
.
As in Remark
2.1
,
\Lambda=N(X)
is the numerical Grothendieck group.
Let
D\subset X
be an effective ample divisor and
Z\subseteq X
a closed subset of positive dimension; we can assume that
D
intersects
Z
transversally. The classes of the sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(nD)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
give an entire coset
[\mathcal{O}_{Z}]+\mathbb{Z}[D]
in the effective cone
E(\mathsfit{H})
. In contrast, if
p\in X
is a point then only the monoid
\mathbb{N}[\mathcal{O}_{p}]
lies in
E(\mathsfit{H})
. It follows that the heart cone
C(\mathsfit{H})\cong\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot\mathrm{rk}\,
is one-dimensional, generated by the rank function.

This example shows that the heart cones of smooth projective varieties are not interesting in themselves, as they are independent of the geometry. Example
2.10
below shows that the heart cones of finite-dimensional algebras are always orthants, likewise regardless of the algebra. These cones become interesting when studied as part of the heart fans in Subsection
3.2
.

If
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})
, full cones correspond to an algebraic setting. An abelian category is
algebraic
if it is noetherian and artinian, and has finitely many simple objects up to isomorphism.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an abelian lattice category. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The full subcategory
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}=\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid[h]=0\}
is Serre and
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}
is algebraic.

(ii)

The effective cone
E(\mathsfit{H})
is smooth and full.

(iii)

The heart cone
C(\mathsfit{H})
is full.

Proof.

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
:
Suppose
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}
is a Serre subcategory and
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}
is algebraic. The quotient functor induces a canonical isomorphism
K(\mathsfit{H})=K(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}})
identifying the effective cones of
\mathsfit{H}
and
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}
. Since
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}
is algebraic, the classes of its simple objects form a basis of
K(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}})=K(\mathsfit{H})
. The effective cone
E(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}})=E(\mathsfit{H})
is generated by this basis, so is smooth and full.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
:
By definition, a smooth, full cone is generated by a basis. Its dual cone is generated by the dual basis, hence is also smooth and full.

(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)
:
If the heart cone
C(\mathsfit{H})
is full then its dual cone
\mathbb{D}(C(\mathsfit{H}))
is strictly convex, hence so is the real subcone
E(\mathsfit{H})_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathbb{D}\mathbb{D}(E(\mathsfit{H}))
and then the integral cone
E(\mathsfit{H})
is too. By Lemma
2.4

\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}=\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H},\id}
is a Serre subcategory.

Now fix
h\in\mathsfit{H}
. Any subobject of
h
has class in
E(\mathsfit{H})\cap([h]-E(\mathsfit{H}))
. Since
E(\mathsfit{H})
is strictly convex, there are only finite many lattice points in this region, hence only finitely many possible classes for subobjects. Therefore the class of any ascending or descending chain of subobjects is eventually constant, and the quotients of successive terms in the chain are eventually in
\mathsf{N}_{\mathsfit{H}}
. We conclude that

Remark 2.16.

3.
Cofan and fan of a bounded heart

Let
\mathsfit{D}
be a triangulated category over a lattice
\Lambda

\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{D})\to\Lambda

Henceforth, a

For any heart

\Lambda

The set

\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}\iff\mathsfit{K}\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsfit{H}[n]*\cdots*\mathsfit{H}\iff{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathsfit{D}}(\mathsfit{K}[{>}0],\mathsfit{H})=0.

Happel–Reiten–Smalø tilting yields a bijection

between torsion pairs
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
in
\mathsfit{H}
and hearts

, see
[
Po03
, Lemma 1.1.2]
or Appendix

.

In this section we first define the heart cofan
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
, an integral cofan, i.e. a collection of integral cones (monoids) in the lattice
\Lambda
; see Subsection
1.5
for the basic notion. Afterwards, we define the heart fan

, a real fan in the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space

, i.e. a collection of convex cones. One can define an integral fan

Readers interested in the heart fan only may jump to Subsection

3.2.
The heart fan

We define the heart fan from HRS-tilting and show that it is the fan associated to the real heart cofan. For readers only interested in the heart fan, we give a self-contained proof that
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is indeed a fan.
Recall from Definition
2.2
that the heart cone of a heart
\mathsfit{K}
is

C(\mathsfit{K})=\{v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\mid v|{K}\geq 0\}

this is the dual cone of the real effective cone

Theorem-Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}

\mathsfit{D}
over
\Lambda

(1)

associated to the real cofan
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H})

(2)

(3)

Proof.

This follows from Lemma

3.1
about the heart cofan
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
. We give a standalone proof that
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})

There are unique torsion pairs

\displaystyle v\in C(\mathsfit{K})\cap C(\mathsfit{K}^{\prime})

\mathsfit{T}^{\prime}\cap\mathsfit{F}

Remark 3.8.

By Theorem
3.1
, the heart cofan
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
can be constructed from
\mathsfit{H}
, without recourse to any ambient triangulated category, as the cofan consisting of all the cofaces of the cones

3.3.
The dual face heart fan

By Lemma
2.15
, we have an explicit, categorical description of all cofaces of effective cones
E(\mathsfit{K})
by face pairs, hence of the entire heart cofan. We do not have a similar description for all faces of heart cones and this is one reason why we consider the cofan as the starting object.
However, we have a categorical description of the dual faces of heart cones, leading us to discuss the dual face heart fan. This circumstance is independent of whether we use integral or real cones; as we defined heart fans to consist of real cones, we will follow suit for their dual face subsets.

Dual faces were introduced in Definition
1.18
. Given an abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
over a lattice
\Lambda
and a Serre subcategory
\mathsfit{S}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
, the
induced sublattice

\Lambda{S}
is the image of the composite
K(\mathsfit{S})\to K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
. The quotient
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{S}
is a category over the lattice
\Lambda/\Lambda{S}
via the induced homomorphism
K(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{S})=K(\mathsfit{H})/K(\mathsfit{S})\to\Lambda/\Lambda{S}
.
First we observe that a dual face of a heart cone is the heart cone of a quotient category:

Lemma 3.9
.

The dual face of
C(\mathsfit{H})
corresponding to a Serre subcategory
\mathsfit{S}
of
\mathsfit{H}
is the heart cone of the quotient
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{S}
, considered as a cone in
\Lambda^{*}
via the canonical map
(\Lambda/\Lambda{S})^{*}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{*}
:

\mathbb{D}(E(\mathsfit{H},\mathsfit{S})_{\mathbb{R}})=\mathbb{D}(E(\mathsfit{H})_{\mathbb{R}}-E(\mathsfit{S})_{\mathbb{R}})=C(\mathsfit{H})\cap E(\mathsfit{S})^{\perp}=C(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{S}).

Proof.

The first equality is merely the definition of the coface
E(\mathsfit{H},\mathsfit{S})
.
The middle equality is immediate; this dual face is the image of
C(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{S})=\mathbb{D}(E(\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{S})_{\mathbb{R}})
under the dual
(\Lambda/\Lambda{S})^{*}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{*}
of the canonical projection map
\Lambda\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda/\Lambda{S}
.
∎

By Example
1.22
, the dual faces of a heart cone are in bijection with the exposed faces of the effective cone and therefore also in bijection with certain face subcategories.

Definition 3.10.

Let
\mathsfit{H}

be a over the lattice
\Lambda

(1)

The

of

Example 4.7 (projective line).

Let
\mathsfit{H}=\coh(\mathbb{P}^{1})
and write
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})=\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{O}]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{O}_{p}]
as in Example
2.5
.
Let
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
be a torsion pair in
\mathsfit{H}
.
If
\mathsfit{T}
contains all line bundles then
\mathsfit{T}=\mathsfit{H}
.
If
\mathsfit{T}
contains no line bundles, then it is of the form
\mathsfit{T}_{\!M}={\langle\mathcal{O}_{q}\mid q\in M\rangle}
for a subset
M\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Tilting preserves the heart cone:
C(\mathsfit{H})=C(\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=C(\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))
; see Example
2.6
.

Thus assume
\mathsfit{T}
contains a line bundle
\mathcal{O}(n)
of minimal degree
n
. Then all skyscraper sheaves are in
\mathsfit{T}
because torsion classes are closed under quotients. Hence
\mathsfit{T}
includes the line bundles
\mathcal{O}(n+1),\mathcal{O}(n+2),\ldots
as torsion classes are closed under extensions. Write
\mathsfit{T}_{\!n}={\langle\mathcal{O}(n),\mathcal{O}_{x}\rangle}
for this torsion class. The corresponding torsionfree class is
\mathsfit{F}_{n}={\langle\mathcal{O}(m)\mid m<n\rangle}
. Denote by
\mathsfit{K}_{n}
the heart obtained from the positive tilt of
\mathsfit{H}
at
(\mathsfit{T}_{\!n},\mathsfit{F}_{n})
. The simple objects of
\mathsfit{K}_{n}
are
\mathcal{O}(n)
and
\mathcal{O}(n-1)[1]
. Therefore the effective cone
E(\mathsfit{K}_{n})
is generated by
(1,n)
and
(-1,1-n)
and its dual cone
C(\mathsfit{K}_{n})
has generators
[-n,1]
and
[1-n,1]
.

All this implies that the maximal cones of the fan
\Delta(\mathbb{P}^{1})
are the opposite rays
C(\mathsfit{H})
and
C(\mathsfit{H}[1])
together with a series of 2-dimensional cones
C(\mathsfit{K}_{n})
. These fill out a half-plane in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
.
The fan
\Delta(\mathbb{P}^{1})
is incomplete because
v(\mathcal{O}_{x})\geq 0
for any heart
\mathsfit{K}
and any
v\in C(\mathsfit{K})
.
Moreover, the tilted hearts from Example
2.6
form an infinite family with the same heart fan:
\Delta(\coh(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=\Delta(\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))=\Delta(\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1}))
.

Example 4.8 (elliptic curve).

Let
\mathsfit{H}=\coh(X)
for an elliptic curve
X
. The Grothendieck group
K(X)=K(\mathsfit{H})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathrm{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus X
is large. Let
\Lambda=N(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the numerical Grothendieck group, i.e. the quotient of
K(X)
by the kernel of the Euler pairing, and let

\lambda\colon K(X)\to N(X)
,
\lambda(A)=(\mathrm{rk}\,(A),\deg(A))
.
We are going to show that the heart fan consists of all rays in the upper half-plane, together with the origin:

\Delta(X)=\{e^{i\pi\theta}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\theta\in[0,1]\}\cup\{\{0\}\}
.

Denote the slope of a coherent sheaf by
\mu(A)=\deg(A)/\mathrm{rk}(A)\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
, and consider the full subcategories
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}=\coh(X)_{\mu}
of semistable sheaves of slope
\mu
. Two general facts about semistable sheaves are:
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}
is a finite length abelian category; these categories are ordered:
{\mathrm{Hom}}(A_{1},A_{2})=0
if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are semistable with
\mu(A_{1})>\mu(A_{2})
.
Peculiar to the elliptic curve case is that then
{\mathrm{Hom}}(A_{2},A_{1})\neq 0
holds; moreover, indecomposable sheaves are semistable, and
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}\cong\mathsfit{H}_{\infty}
for all
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
.
As
\mathsfit{H}_{\infty}
is the subcategory of torsion sheaves, splitting as a direct sum indexed by points of
X
, each
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}
is a direct sum of rank one tubes. See
[
Po03
, II.14]
for details.

For
real
numbers
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, the additive subcategories
\mathsfit{T}_{\!\delta}={\langle\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}\mid\mu>\delta\rangle}
and
\mathsfit{F}_{\delta}={\langle\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}\mid\mu\leq\delta\rangle}
define mutually different torsion pairs in
\mathsfit{H}
. Let
\mathsfit{K}_{\delta}=\mathsfit{F}_{\delta}[1]*\mathsfit{T}_{\!\delta}
be the corresponding tilts of
\mathsfit{H}
.
The real cone
E(\mathsfit{K}_{\delta})_{\mathbb{R}}
is a half-plane containing
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
whose boundary line has slope
\delta
and so the heart cone
C(\mathsfit{K}_{\delta})=\mathbb{D}(E(\mathsfit{K}_{\delta})_{\mathbb{R}})
is a ray of slope
-1/\delta
in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This extends to
\delta=\pm\infty
with
\mathsfit{K}_{-\infty}=\mathsfit{H}=\mathsfit{K}_{\infty}[-1]
and
C(\mathsfit{K}_{-\infty})=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\{0\}=-C(\mathsfit{K}_{\infty})
.
We are going to explain that these are all heart cones in
\Delta(X)
. They do fill the upper half-plane with rays.

So let
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
be an arbitrary torsion pair in
\mathsfit{H}
with tilted heart
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
. Let
A\in\mathsfit{T}
be an indecomposable object. The category
\mathsfit{T}
is closed under quotients and extensions, hence contains the top of
A
and thus the entire tube of
A
. Moreover, for any
\mu>\mu(A)
, there are non-zero morphisms to objects in
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}
and in particular to the simple objects of
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}
which are surjections. Hence
\mathsfit{T}
contains all
\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}
with
\mu>\mu(A)
. Put
\delta\coloneqq\inf\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}\mid\mathsfit{T}\cap\mathsfit{H}_{\mu}\neq 0\}
. The real effective cone
E(\mathsfit{K})_{\mathbb{R}}
is a half-plane with the same slope as
E(\mathsfit{K}_{\delta})_{\mathbb{R}}
. The boundary of
E(\mathsfit{K})_{\mathbb{R}}
depends on whether some, all or no tubes from
\mathsfit{H}_{\delta}
belong to
\mathsfit{T}
, as in Example
2.6
. Regardless of this, the heart cone is always the same ray
C(\mathsfit{K})=C(\mathsfit{K}_{\delta})
of slope
-1/\delta
.

Example 4.9.

We continue the previous example: let
N(X)=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\Gamma\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}
,
(r,d)\mapsto d

E(\mathsfit{H}/\Gamma)_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\Delta(\mathsfit{H}/\Gamma)
is a complete fan on
\mathbb{R}
although
\mathsfit{H}
is not a length category. This is an illustration of general lattice change as in Proposition
3.15
(2).

Example 4.10 (wild quivers).

The heart fan of the elliptic curve contains a region entirely filled by rays. This behaviour occurs regularly, e.g. for all path algebras of wild quivers; see
[
DHKK14
, Prop. 3.32]
. It is related to having a dense region in the phase diagram; see Section
6
.

Specifically, and nice to draw due to their rank 2 Grothendieck groups, this holds for the
n
-Kronecker quivers
Q_{n}
with two vertices and
n\geq 3
parallel arrows; see
[
Ch13
]
. Let
\mathsfit{H}_{n}=\mathsf{mod}({\mathbf{k}}Q_{n})
. By Kac’s Theorem, the class in
K(\mathsfit{H}_{n})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of an indecomposable representation of
Q_{n}
is a positive root
(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
of the associated Euler form

q(a,b)=a^{2}-nab+b^{2}
.
The real roots
q(a,b)=1
correspond to indecomposables in the postprojective and preinjective components of the Auslander–Reiten quiver (there is one indecomposable up to isomorphism for each positive real root).
Let
P_{i}
be the indecomposable objects in the postprojective component with
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
being the indecomposable projectives with respective classes
(0,1)
and
(1,n)
. The Auslander–Reiten sequences

0\to P_{i}\to P_{i+1}^{n}\to P_{i+2}\to 0

imply that
[P_{i}]=(a_{i},a_{i+1})
where the
a_{i}
satisfy the recurrence
a_{i+2}=na_{i+1}-a_{i}
with
a_{0}=0
,
a_{1}=1
. There is a dual picture for the preinjective component where
[I_{i}]=(a_{i+1},a_{i})
.

The imaginary roots
q(a,b)<0
correspond to indecomposable objects in the regular component (there are infinitely many indecomposables for each positive imaginary root). Rays through positive imaginary roots are dense in the cone

n-\sqrt{n^{2}-4}\leq 2x/y\leq n+\sqrt{n^{2}-4}.

The sequences
a_{i+1}/a_{i}
and
a_{i}/a_{i+1}
converge to the roots
(n\pm\sqrt{n^{2}-4})/2
of
x^{2}-nx+1
.

If
n=2
then
a_{i}=i
and the classes of every regular module lie on the ray of slope 1. For
n>2
, the classes of the regular modules lie in a cone with non-empty interior.

Example 4.11 (coherent sheaves).

Let
X
be a connected, smooth and projective variety,
\mathsfit{H}=\coh(X)
the category of coherent sheaves on
X
and
\Lambda=N(X)
the numerical Grothendieck group of
X
as in Example
2.8
. Since
\mathsfit{H}
is noetherian, all torsion pairs in
\mathsfit{H}
are of the form
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{T}^{\perp})
where
\mathsfit{T}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
is an additive subcategory closed under quotients and extensions. If
\mathsfit{T}\neq 0
then it must contain the skyscraper sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{p}
of a point
p\in X
, hence the effective cone of the tilted heart
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{T}^{\perp}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
contains the point class
[\mathcal{O}_{p}]\in E(\mathsfit{K})
(as
X
is connected, all points have the same class in
N(X)
). Choose an orthonormal basis for
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=N(X)\otimes\mathbb{R}
containing the point class and denote its dual basis vector by
v_{0}\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
. Then
C(\mathsfit{K})
is contained in the half-space of
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
given by
v_{0}\geq 0
. With
C(\mathsfit{H}[1])=-C(\mathsfit{H})
being a ray by Example
2.8
, this shows that the support of the heart fan
\Delta(X)
is contained in a half-space.

A line bundle on
X
induces an exact autoequivalence of
\coh(X)
and hence, according to Proposition
3.16
(2), an automorphism of the fan
\Delta(X)
. In this way, the quotient group
\mathrm{Pic}(X)/\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
acts faithfully on the fan. For example, the action of
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
on
\Delta(\mathbb{P}^{1})
fixes the two rays
\pm C(\mathsfit{H})
and acts transitively on the remaining maximal cones: twisting by
\mathcal{O}(1)
maps
C(\mathsfit{K}_{i})\mapsto C(\mathsfit{K}_{i+1})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, using the notation of Example
4.7
.

Example 4.12 (an infinite-dimensional semisimple algebra and non-dual faces).

Let
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsf{mod}({\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbb{Z}})
be the semisimple category with simple objects
S_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and define the homomorphism
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
by
\lambda(S_{i})\coloneqq(i+1,1)
and
\lambda(S_{-i})\coloneqq(1,1+i)
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Subsets
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
are in bijection with torsion classes in
\mathsfit{H}
; the latter are
{\langle S_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle}
. The corresponding tilt
\mathsfit{K}_{I}
of
\mathsfit{H}
is also semisimple with simple objects
S_{i}
for
i\in I
and
S_{i}[1]
for
i\notin I
. It follows that
C(\mathsfit{K}_{\mathbb{Z}\backslash I})=-C(\mathsfit{K}_{I})
. For example
C(\mathsfit{K}_{\mathbb{Z}})=C(\mathsfit{H})
is the first quadrant and
C(\mathsfit{K}_{\varnothing})=C(\mathsfit{H}[1])
is the third quadrant. The only other non-zero heart cones are those of the hearts
\mathsfit{K}_{\leq n}\coloneqq\mathsfit{K}_{I}
where
I=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\mid i\leq n\}
and
\mathsfit{K}_{>n}\coloneqq\mathsfit{K}_{I}
where
I=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\mid i>n\}
. Here

C(\mathsfit{K}_{\leq n})
is generated by
[n,1]
,
[n-1,1]
if
n<0
and by
[-1,n+1]
,
[-1,n+2]
if
n\geq 0
, and
C(\mathsfit{K}_{>n})=-C(\mathsfit{K}_{\leq n})
. In all other cases
C(\mathsfit{K}_{I})=0
; this does not contradict Proposition
2.9
because
\Lambda\neq K(\mathsfit{K}_{I})
.

The effective cone
E(\mathsfit{H})=\{a,b>0\}\cup\{0\}
is not polyhedral, and the heart fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
contains non-dual faces. Hence the dual face fan
\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H}))
of Remark
3.13
is a proper subset of
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
. The latter contains four extra cones, namely the rays through
[\pm 1,0]
and
[0,\pm 1]
, which are faces of
C(\mathsfit{H})
or
C(\mathsfit{H}[1])
but not dual faces. The four rays are shown in red in Figure
2
.

5.
Finite heart fans

In this section we answer the natural question of when a heart fan is complete and finite, in the context of an
algebraic
category.
Other heart fans can be finite because of a particular choice of lattice; see Example
4.9
for a complete fan of non-length category.
The subsequent sections do not depend on the results in this one, so it can be safely skipped.

We begin with a technical result which is required for the following corollary. It applies for example if
\mathsfit{H}
is an algebraic category. The full-dimensionality of
C(\mathsfit{K})
is crucial: if
\mathsfit{H}
is the module category of the Kronecker quiver, then one possible tilt is the mixed geometric heart
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsf{coh}^{{\Join}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
of Example
2.7
which has
\mathsfit{N}{K}\neq 0
; however
C(\mathsfit{K})
is not full in this case.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
and
\mathsfit{K}
be hearts in
\mathsfit{D}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
. Assume that
K(\mathsfit{D})
is a lattice. If
C(\mathsfit{K})
is full and
\mathsfit{N}{H}=0
then
\mathsfit{N}{K}=0
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
be the torsion pair in
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsfit{T}*\mathsfit{F}
such that
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
is its tilt. Assume
\mathsfit{N}{K}\neq 0
, i.e. there is
0\neq k\in\mathsfit{K}
with
0=[k]\in K(\mathsfit{K})
. The decomposition sequence of
k\in\mathsfit{K}
with respect to the torsion pair
(\mathsfit{F}[1],\mathsfit{T})
is
0\to f[1]\to k\to t\to 0
, where
f\in\mathsfit{F}
and
t\in\mathsfit{T}
.
The decomposition is nontrivial because
[k]=0
, and
[f]=0
or
[t]=0
in
K(\mathsfit{H})=K(\mathsfit{K})
only when
f=0
or
t=0
by
\mathsfit{N}{H}=0
.
Then the effective cone
E(\mathsfit{K})
contains the opposite generators
[f[1]]=[k]-[t]=-[t]
and
[t]
, so is not strictly convex, contradicting Proposition
2.9
as
C(\mathsfit{K})
was assumed to be full.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Suppose
K(\mathsfit{D})
is a lattice and
\mathsfit{H}
is a heart with
\mathsfit{N}{H}=0
. Then
\mathsfit{K}\mapsto C(\mathsfit{K})
is a bijection between algebraic hearts
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
and full cones in
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
.

Proof.

As
\mathsfit{D}
is a lattice category, so is every bounded heart in
\mathsfit{D}
. Thus, by Proposition
2.9
,
C(K)
is full if and only if
\mathsfit{N}{K}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathsfit{K}
and
\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{N}{K}
is algebraic. Since
\mathsfit{N}{H}=0
, Lemma
5.1
implies
\mathsfit{N}{K}=0
too. Hence
C(\mathsfit{K})
is full if and only if
\mathsfit{K}
is algebraic. Finally, by Remark
3.12
,
C(\mathsfit{K})=C(\mathsfit{K}^{\prime})
for algebraic hearts if and only if
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{K}^{\prime}
.
∎

The following result is a natural generalisation of finiteness and completeness results for
g
-fans of finite-dimensional algebras, see Remark
8.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an algebraic heart and
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The heart fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is finite.

(2)

Each heart
\mathsfit{K}
in
\mathsfit{D}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
is algebraic.

(3)

There are finitely many hearts
\mathsfit{K}
in
\mathsfit{D}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
.

Proof.

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
: First we show that each facet
\tau
of a full cone
\sigma
in
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is contained in another full cone. Since
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is complete we can choose a sequence in
\lvert\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\rvert\backslash\sigma
converging to a point in the relative interior of
\tau
. Since
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is finite we can pass to a subsequence lying in a single cone
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
which is not a face of
\sigma
. It follows that
\tau\cap\sigma^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. Since the intersection is a face of each, and contains a point of the relative interior of
\tau
, we deduce that
\tau
is a proper face of
\sigma^{\prime}
. Therefore
\sigma^{\prime}
is a full cone.

Next we show that the set of algebraic hearts between
\mathsfit{H}[1]
and
\mathsfit{H}
is closed under irreducible simple tilts (which do not result in hearts outside this interval). Suppose
\mathsfit{K}
is an algebraic heart between
\mathsfit{H}[1]
and
\mathsfit{H}
and
s
is a simple object of
\mathsfit{K}
. Let
\mathsfit{S}
be the Serre subcategory generated by
s
. Then
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})=C(\mathsfit{K})\cap[s]^{\perp}
is a facet of
C(\mathsfit{K})
. By the first part there is another full cone containing this facet, and by Corollary
5.2
this cone has the form
C(\mathsfit{K}^{\prime})
for a unique algebraic heart
\mathsfit{K}^{\prime}
between
\mathsfit{H}[1]
and
\mathsfit{H}
. Choose
v
in the relative interior of the facet. By Corollaries
3.3
and
3.11
, the intersection
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}=\mathsfit{H}\cap\mathsf{thick}(\mathsfit{S})
is either
\mathsfit{S}
or
\mathsfit{S}[-1]
, depending on whether
s\in\mathsfit{H}
or
s[-1]\in\mathsfit{H}
. In each case the only torsion pairs in
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
are the trivial ones with vanishing torsion or torsionfree class. So Corollary
3.3
implies there are precisely two heart cones containing
v
, corresponding to a heart and a simple tilt of that heart. Therefore
\mathsfit{K}^{\prime}
is either the positive or negative tilt of
\mathsfit{K}
at
s
, and in particular this tilt is algebraic.

Finally, we show that every heart
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
between
\mathsfit{H}[1]
and
\mathsfit{H}
is algebraic. Since
\mathsfit{H}
is algebraic either
\mathsfit{F}=0
, in which case
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{H}
and there is nothing to prove, or
\mathsfit{F}
contains a simple object,
s
, of
\mathsfit{H}
. Let
\mathsfit{H}_{1}
be the positive tilt of
\mathsfit{H}
at
s
. By the above
\mathsfit{H}_{1}
is algebraic, and by construction
\mathsfit{H}_{1}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}_{1}
because
\mathsfit{F}\supseteq{\langle s\rangle}
. Repeating, we construct a sequence of algebraic hearts
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\cdots>\mathsfit{H}_{1}>\mathsfit{H}_{0}\eqqcolon\mathsfit{H}
. Since each corresponds to a distinct full cone in the heart fan, and there are only finitely many such cones, the sequence must terminate with
\mathsfit{H}_{n}=\mathsfit{K}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,
\mathsfit{K}
is algebraic.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
: Consider the poset of hearts between
\mathsfit{H}
and
\mathsfit{H}[1]
. By assumption each of these hearts is algebraic. It follows that each element has valency the rank of
K(\mathsfit{H})
because the covering relations in the poset correspond to the irreducible simple tilts. If the length of any chain in the poset is also bounded then the poset is finite as claimed.

Each chain in the poset induces a sequence
(\mathsfit{T}_{i},\mathsfit{F}_{i})
of torsion pairs in
\mathsfit{H}
with
\mathsfit{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathsfit{F}_{1}\subseteq\cdots
. Since
\mathsfit{H}
is length,
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})\coloneqq(\bigcap_{i}\mathsfit{T}_{i}\,,\,\bigcup_{i}\mathsfit{F}_{i})
is also a torsion pair because
\mathsfit{T}
is closed under extensions and quotients,
\mathsfit{F}
is closed under extensions and subobjects, and
\mathsfit{T}={}^{\perp}\mathsfit{F}
. The corresponding heart
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
is, by assumption, algebraic. Therefore each of its finitely many simple objects is either in
\mathsfit{T}
or
\mathsfit{F}[1]
. It follows that each is in either
\mathsfit{T}_{i}
or
\mathsfit{F}_{i}[1]
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
\mathsfit{K}\subseteq\mathsfit{F}_{i}[1]*\mathsfit{T}_{i}
are nested bounded hearts, and are therefore equal. It follows that the chain has finite length.

(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)
: The heart fan consists of finitely many heart cones and their faces. In particular, there are finitely many full heart cones, whose union is the support of the heart fan. It follows that each cone in the heart fan is a face of one of these full cones. By Proposition
2.9
each full cone is simplicial, in particular has finitely many faces. Therefore the heart fan is finite.
∎

6.
Stability conditions and the universal phase diagram

The main result of this section is an interpretation of the heart fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
as a ‘universal phase diagram’ for stability functions on
\mathsfit{H}
with the HN property. We first recall standard notions of stability theory in the abelian setting and then specify what we mean by phase diagram.

6.1.
King and Bridgeland stability

Alastair King’s notion depends on a vector in the real dual of the Grothendieck group; Tom Bridgeland’s depends on one in the complex dual.

Definition 6.1.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an abelian category and
v\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(K(\mathsfit{H}),\mathbb{R})
.

(1)

h\in\mathsfit{H}
is
v
-semistable
if
v(h)=0
and
v(h^{\prime})\leq 0
for all subobjects
h^{\prime}\subseteq h
.

(2)

h\in\mathsfit{H}
is
v
-stable
if it is
v
-semistable and if
h^{\prime}\hookrightarrow h
,
v(h^{\prime})=0
implies
h^{\prime}=0
or
h^{\prime}=h
.

(3)

\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
denotes the full subcategory of
v
-semistable objects in
\mathsfit{H}
.

This is
[
Ki94
, Def. 1.1]
although for compatibility with
[
Br17
]
we have reversed the sign convention.
The subcategory
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
is wide, i.e. closed under extensions, kernels and cokernels. The simple objects of
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
are precisely the
v
-stable objects of
\mathsfit{H}
.

Fix a surjective group homomorphism
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
onto a lattice. Let
\mathbb{H}_{-}\coloneqq\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{R}_{<0}=\{me^{\pi i\varphi}\in\mathbb{C}\mid\varphi\in(0,1],m>0\}
be the upper half-plane extended by the negative real axis. A
stability function
on
\mathsfit{H}
is a group homomorphism
Z\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
with
Z(\lambda(h))\in\mathbb{H}_{-}
for all
0\neq h\in\mathsfit{H}
; we write
Z(h)\coloneqq Z(\lambda(h))
. The
phase

\varphi=\varphi(h)\in(0,1]
of
0\neq h\in\mathsfit{H}
is defined by
Z(h)=me^{\pi i\varphi}\in\mathbb{C}
. An object
h\in\mathsfit{H}
is
Z
-semistable
if
\varphi(h^{\prime})\leq\varphi(h)
for all non-zero subobjects
h^{\prime}\hookrightarrow h
.
The full subcategory
P(\varphi)
of
Z
-semistable objects of phase
\varphi
is a wide abelian subcategory of
\mathsfit{H}
; it is called the
slice
of phase
\varphi
.
Simple objects of
P(\varphi)
are called
Z
-stable
.
If
\psi>\varphi
then
{\mathrm{Hom}}(P(\psi),P(\varphi))=0
.
For an interval
I\subseteq(0,1]
let
P(I)
be the extension closure of the subcategories
P(\varphi)
with
\varphi\in I
. We abuse notation by writing
P(a,b)
for
P((a,b))
and so on.

A stability function
Z
has the
Harder–Narasimhan (HN) property
if for each
h\in\mathsfit{H}
there is a finite chain of inclusions
0=h_{0}\hookrightarrow h_{1}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow h_{n}=h
such that (a) each quotient
0\neq h_{i}/h_{i-1}\in P(\varphi_{i})
for some
\varphi_{i}\in(0,1]
and (b)
\varphi_{1}>\varphi_{2}>\cdots>\varphi_{n}
.

6.2.
Phase diagrams

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be a heart in a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
and
Z\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
a stability function on
\mathsfit{H}
.
We define an open half-plane in the
\mathbb{R}
-dual vector space
\mathbb{C}^{*}={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R})
— not to be confused with the multiplicative group
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
— by

\mathbb{H}^{*}\coloneqq\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\mid w(e^{\pi i\varphi})=0,w(ie^{\pi i\varphi})>0\ \text{some}\ \varphi\in(0,1]\}.

This dual half-plane comes with a map
\theta\colon\mathbb{H}^{*}\to(0,1]
defined by
e^{\pi i\theta(w)}\in\ker(w)
.

For any
v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
, there is a subcategory
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
of
v
-semistable objects as in Definition
6.1
. (Technically we should write
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v\lambda}
but as elsewhere we suppress the map
\lambda\colon K(\mathsfit{H})\to\Lambda
.) The two notions of semistable subcategories agree under the dual map
Z^{*}\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\to{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
:

Lemma 6.2
.

The subcategory
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{Z^{*}(w)}=P(\theta(w))
for any
w\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
.

Proof.

Put
v\coloneqq Z^{*}(w)\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
and
\theta\coloneqq\theta(w)\in(0,1]
.
By Definition
6.1
, a non-zero object
h\in\mathsfit{H}
is
v
-semistable if
v(h)=0
and
v(h^{\prime})\leq 0
for all non-zero subobjects
h^{\prime}\subseteq h
. Since
w\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
and
Z
is a stability function on
\mathsfit{H}
this is equivalent to
\varphi(h)=\theta
and
\varphi(h^{\prime})\leq\theta
for all non-zero subobjects
h^{\prime}\subseteq h
, i.e. it is equivalent to
h\in P(\theta)
being
Z
-semistable.
∎

Definition 6.3.

The
phase diagram
of the stability function
Z\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
is the map

\Phi_{Z}\colon\mathbb{H}^{*}\to\mathsf{Wide}{(\mathsfit{H})},\quad w\mapsto P(\theta(w)).

Remark 6.4.

The map
\Phi_{Z}
records
Z
-semistable objects of each phase in
(0,1]
and is a stronger invariant than the ‘naive phase diagram’
\{\varphi\in(0,1]\mid P(\varphi)\neq 0\}
, tracking non-zero slices.

Recall the thick label map

\Theta\colon\lvert\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\rvert\to\mathsf{Thick}{(\mathsfit{D})}
,
v\mapsto\mathsf{thick}(\mathsf{ker}_{\mathsfit{K}}(v))

from Remark
3.14
, defined by choosing a heart
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
with
v\in C(\mathsfit{K})
, where
\mathsf{ker}_{\mathsfit{K}}(v)=\{k\in\mathsfit{K}\mid v(k)=0\}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
Z
be a stability function on
\mathsfit{H}
with the HN property.
Then the phase diagram of
Z
factorises over the support of the heart fan of
\mathsfit{H}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Wide}{(\mathsfit{H})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}\lvert\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\rvert}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta\cap\mathsfit{H}}

i.e.
\Phi_{Z}(w)=\Theta(Z^{*}(w))\cap\mathsfit{H}
for all
w\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
P
be the slicing induced by
Z
on
\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H})
. As
Z
satisfies the HN property, for any
\psi\in(0,1)
there are two torsion pairs

\mathsfit{H}=P(\psi,1]*P(0,\psi]=P[\psi,1]*P(0,\psi)
,
and therefore the positive tilts
P(\psi,\psi+1]
and
P[\psi,\psi+1)
of
\mathsfit{H}=P(0,1]
exist.

If
w\in\mathbb{H}^{*}
then
w(e^{i\pi\theta(w)})=0
and
w(ie^{i\pi\theta(w)})>0
, hence
Z^{*}(w)=w\circ Z
is in the heart cone of the positive tilt

\mathsfit{K}\coloneqq P[\theta(w),\theta(w)+1)
of
\mathsfit{H}
, and so is in the support
\lvert\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\rvert
. Moreover
\mathsf{ker}_{\mathsfit{K}}(Z^{*}(w))=P(\theta(w))
. Thus
\Theta(Z^{*}(w))=\mathsf{thick}(P(\theta(w)))
, hence
\Theta(Z^{*}(w))\cap\mathsfit{H}=P(\theta(w))
.
∎

Remark 6.6.

The previous result can be viewed as a description of ‘half’ of the pullback of the heart fan along
Z^{*}\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\to{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
. The other ‘half’ is similarly related to
Z
-semistable objects for the opposite ordering on the real numbers.

There is an essentially equivalent description of ‘half’ of the push-forward of the heart cofan along
Z\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
. Its cones in the half-space
\varphi\leq\arg(z)\leq\varphi+1
are
Z(P(\varphi,\varphi+1])
, its coface
Z(P[\varphi,\varphi+1])
and when
\varphi>0
also
Z(P[\varphi,\varphi+1))
. These are the same if
P(\varphi)=0
, but are otherwise distinct.

\mathsfit{K}
with heart cone generated by
[0,1]
and
[-1,1]
.
The simple objects of
\mathsfit{K}
are
S_{2}[1]
and

. By a phase gap argument as in
[
BCPW24
, Ex. 2.9]
, one can show that the simple tilt of
\mathsfit{K}
at
E
is not algebraic.

We prove that the remaining region in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
is filled by 1-dimensional heart cones.
Consider the stability function
Z\colon\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
Z(S_{1})=-1
and
Z(S_{2})=i
. Because
\mathsfit{H}
is algebraic,
Z
satisfies the support and HN properties. If

lies in the region between the ray
e^{3i\pi/4}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and the negative real axis, and one can check that any subrepresentation has smaller phase so that

is
Z
-semistable.

Fix
\varphi=p/q\in[3/4,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
where

, and choose an indecomposable

with
\arg Z(M)=\pi\varphi

Define a torsion pair
(\mathsfit{T}_{\!\varphi},\mathsfit{F}_{\varphi})

\mathsfit{F}_{\varphi}\coloneqq P(\varphi,1]*{\langle M\rangle}

The effective cone of the associated tilt

.
Therefore, the heart cone is

\varphi
.
By Theorem

At its boundary, we get a heart

is a face of

Moreover,

Finally, we present a heart

with

\mathsfit{K}^{\prime\prime}
be the tilt of
\mathsfit{H}

The torsion pairs

is not.

Example 6.8 (smooth projective curve).

.

7.
The wall-and-chamber structure of an algebraic heart

We relate the heart fan of an
algebraic
abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
to the wall-and-chamber structure of
[
BST19
]
defined via King semistability (Definition
6.1
). More precisely, we use the dual face heart fan
\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H}))
of Subsection
3.3
. It is complete by Remark
1.21
and Theorem
3.7
, i.e.
\lvert\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H}))\rvert=\lvert\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\rvert={\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
where
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})
and
\mathsfit{D}=\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H})
.

We begin by showing that among all kernel pairs describing the same cone in the dual face heart fan there is a unique one closest to the reference heart
\mathsfit{H}
. Recall from Definition
3.10
that a kernel pair
(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})\in\mathsf{KerPairs}_{\Lambda\!}(\mathsfit{H})
consists of a heart
\mathsfit{K}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
and a face subcategory
\mathsfit{S}=\mathsf{ker}_{\mathsfit{H}}(v)=\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid v(h)=0\}
for some
v\in C(\mathsfit{H})
.
Below,
C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
denotes the relative interior of
C(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
, i.e. the interior of the cone inside its linear hull.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\mathsfit{H}
be an algebraic abelian category and
\Lambda=K(\mathsfit{H})
its Grothendieck group. For each
v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
there is a unique kernel pair
(\mathsfit{K}^{v},\mathsfit{S}^{v})
in
\mathsfit{D}=\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H})
such that

(1)

C(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
is the minimal dual face containing
v
,

(2)

\mathsfit{K}^{v}
is minimal amongst hearts
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
with
v\in C(\mathsfit{K})
and

(3)

\mathsfit{S}^{v}=\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}\cap\mathsfit{K}^{v}
is the subcategory of
v
-semistable objects in
\mathsfit{H}
.

Moreover, if
w\in C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
then
(\mathsfit{K}^{w},\mathsfit{S}^{w})=(\mathsfit{K}^{v},\mathsfit{S}^{v})
.

We call kernel pairs of the form
(\mathsfit{K}^{v},\mathsfit{S}^{v})

distinguished
.
Recall the surjection from kernel pairs to the dual face heart fan

\mathsf{KerPairs}_{\Lambda\!}(\mathsfit{H})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H}))
,
(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})\mapsto C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
;
see Remark
3.13
. By the proposition, this map has a section sending a dual cone
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})\in\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H})
to the distinguished kernel pair
(\mathsfit{K}^{v},\mathsfit{S}^{v})
with
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})=C(\mathsfit{K}^{v}/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
or, equivalently,
v\in C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsfit{H}=\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}*\mathsfit{F}_{v}
be the torsion pair of Lemma
3.2
, i.e.
\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}\coloneqq\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid v(h^{\prime\prime})\geq 0~\forall h\twoheadrightarrow h^{\prime\prime}\}
and

\mathsfit{F}_{v}\coloneqq\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid v(h^{\prime})<0~\forall\,0\neq h^{\prime}\hookrightarrow h\}.

Let
\mathsfit{K}^{v}\coloneqq\mathsfit{F}_{v}[1]*\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
be the tilted heart; by construction
v\in C(\mathsfit{K}^{v})
.
The subcategory

\mathsfit{S}^{v}\coloneqq\{t\in\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}\mid v(t)=0\}=\{k\in\mathsfit{K}^{v}\mid v(k)=0\}\subseteq\mathsfit{K}^{v}

is the face subcategory of the exposed face
E(\mathsfit{K}^{v})\cap v^{\perp}
, i.e. a kernel subcategory of
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
. Then
C(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
is a dual face of
C(\mathsfit{K}^{v})
containing
v
, and is minimal with this property.

If
v\in C(\mathsfit{K})
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
then
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
for a torsion pair
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsfit{T}*\mathsfit{F}
where
v|{F}\leq 0
.
Considering a decomposition of an object of
\mathsfit{F}_{v}
with respect to
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
and using that
\mathsfit{F}_{v}
is closed under subobjects, we get
\mathsfit{F}_{v}\subseteq\mathsfit{F}
, and hence
\mathsfit{K}^{v}\leq\mathsfit{K}
. Thus
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
is minimal amongst hearts
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
with
v\in C(\mathsfit{K})
. In particular,
v
determines
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
uniquely.

To show that
\mathsfit{S}^{v}\subseteq\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
is the subcategory of
v
-semistable objects, let
h\in\mathsfit{S}^{v}
and
h^{\prime}\hookrightarrow h
be a subobject. Then
0\leq v(h/h^{\prime})=v(h)-v(h^{\prime})=-v(h^{\prime})
by
h\in\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
, and hence
h\in\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
.
Conversely if
h\in\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
then it has a short exact decomposition sequence
0\to t\to h\to f\to 0
in
\mathsfit{H}
with
t\in\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
and
f\in\mathsfit{F}_{v}
. Since
h
is semistable, we have
v(t)\leq 0
for its subobject
t
. On the other hand,
v(t)\geq 0
for all objects of
\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
. Therefore
v(t)=0
and
v(f)=v(h)-v(t)=0
too, so that
f=0
by definition of
\mathsfit{F}_{v}
. Hence
h=t\in\mathsfit{S}^{v}
.

Finally, if
C(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
is the minimal dual face containing
w
then
\mathsfit{K}^{w}=\mathsfit{K}^{v}
by minimality. Thus
\mathsfit{S}^{w}=\mathsfit{S}^{v}
too, as each is the kernel subcategory of the dual face
C(\mathsfit{K}^{v}\!/\mathsfit{S}^{v})
.
∎

Remark 7.2.

If the minimal dual face containing both
v
and
w
is
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
for some
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
then the subcategories of semistable objects
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}=\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{w}
are the same. The converse does not hold in general; for example
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}=0
for any
v
in the interior of a full cone in
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
.

Example 7.3 (distinguished kernel pair in Kronecker quiver heart fan).

Consider the heart fan of the module category over the Kronecker quiver,
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsf{mod}({\mathbf{k}}(\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet))
.
This fan contains a limiting ray denoted
C(\mathsfit{K}_{\infty})
in Example
4.6
. This ray is the dual cone of the infinitely many hearts of
\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H})
listed in Example
2.7
, among them the reversed geometric heart
\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
.

The distinguished kernel pair
(\mathsfit{K}^{v},\mathsfit{S}^{v})
of Proposition
7.1
for
0\neq v\in C(\mathsfit{K}_{\infty})
is
\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
with its face subcategory of (shifted) torsion sheaves: as the geometric and mixed hearts are positive tilts of
\mathsf{coh}^{{\ltimes}\!}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
, this heart is indeed minimal amongst
\mathsfit{K}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
and
v\in C(\mathsfit{K})
.

Lemma 7.4
.

An object of
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
is stable if and only it is simple as an object of
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
.

Proof.

Suppose
h\in\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
. If
0\to h^{\prime}\to h\to h/h^{\prime}\to 0
is short exact in
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
then, since
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}=\mathsfit{S}^{v}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
, both
h^{\prime}
and
h/h^{\prime}
are in
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
so the sequence is also short exact in
\mathsfit{H}
. It follows that if
h
is stable it is simple in
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
.

Conversely, if
h^{\prime\prime}
is a subobject of
h
in
\mathsfit{H}
with
v(h^{\prime\prime})=0
then there is a short exact sequence
0\to h^{\prime\prime}\to h\to h/h^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
\mathsfit{H}
with all terms in
\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
, which is therefore also short exact in
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
. Thus if
h
is simple in
\mathsfit{K}^{v}
then it is stable.
∎

7.1.
Wall-and-chamber structures

Introduced in
[
BST19
]
for finite-dimensional algebras, we explain how to reconstruct walls and chambers from the heart fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
of an algebraic abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
. The
stability space

[
BST19
, Def. 3.2]
of an object
h\in\mathsfit{H}
is

\mathcal{D}(h)\coloneqq\{v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\mid h\in\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}\}.

This is a
wall
if
\codim_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\mathcal{D}(h)=1
. We write
\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\bigcup_{h\neq 0}\mathcal{D}(h)
for the union of stability spaces of non-zero objects. A
chamber
is a connected component of the complement
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\backslash\smash{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
.
The
wall-and-chamber structure
is the set of stability spaces
\mathcal{D}(h)
for indecomposable objects
h
together with the set of chambers.
While walls are defined integrally in
\Lambda^{*}
(more precisely, a wall
\mathcal{D}(h)
is determined by
\mathcal{D}(h)\cap\Lambda^{*}
), chambers are only defined in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
.

Theorem 7.5
.

The stability space
\mathcal{D}(h)
of
h\in\mathsfit{H}
is a rational, polyhedral cone. Moreover,

\Delta(h)\coloneqq\{C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})\mid(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})\text{ distinguished kernel pair with }h\in\mathsfit{S}\}

is a subset of
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
forming a dual face fan with support

\lvert\Delta(h)\rvert=\mathcal{D}(h)
.

The chambers are the open cones
C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})
for the algebraic hearts
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
, and each chamber is the interior of a full, smooth cone.

Remark 7.6.

This provides an alternative proof of
[
BST19
, Prop. 3.15]
and
[
As21
, Thm. 13.7]
for
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsf{mod}(A)
for a finite-dimensional algebra
A
which combined show that the chambers are in natural bijection with
\tau
-tilting pairs, i.e. with algebraic hearts in the interval between
\mathsfit{H}
and
\mathsfit{H}[1]
. Always
\lvert\Delta(h)\rvert\subseteq\lvert\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\rvert\cap[h]^{\perp}
but
\Delta(h)
need not contain all dual faces in this hyperplane section of the heart fan; it only contains those in heart cones
C(\mathsfit{K})
where
h\in\mathsfit{K}
.

Proof.

Stability spaces are rational, polyhedral cones:

The set
S\coloneqq\{[h^{\prime}]\mid h^{\prime}\subseteq h\}\subset K(\mathsfit{H})
of classes of subobjects of
h
is finite because
\mathsfit{H}
is a length category, and therefore it generates a rational, polyhedral cone
E(S)_{\mathbb{R}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence its dual cone
C(S)\subset{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
is rational, polyhedral as well (this is Gordan’s Lemma in convex geometry), as is
\mathcal{D}(h)=-C(S)\cap[h]^{\perp}
.

\Delta(h)
is a dual face subfan:

Let
(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})
be a distinguished kernel pair with
h\in\mathsfit{S}
. Then
h
is
v
-semistable for any
v\in C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
by Proposition
7.1
; as semistability is a closed condition, this holds for any
v\in C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
.
Hence every dual face of
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
in
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
is also in
\Delta(h)
. So
\Delta(h)
is a dual face fan, and it is a subset of
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
anyway.

Stability space as support:

The support
\lvert\Delta(h)\rvert\subseteq{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
is the union of all cones in
\Delta(h)
. We have
\mathcal{D}(h)\supseteq\lvert\Delta(h)\rvert
by the previous step.
Conversely, suppose
v\in\mathcal{D}(h)
. Then
h
is
v
-semistable and, as
\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H}))
is complete, there is a minimal dual face
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
containing
v
where
(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})
is a distinguished kernel pair with
h\in\mathsfit{S}=\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}
by Proposition
7.1
, and hence
\mathcal{D}(h)\subseteq\lvert\Delta(h)\rvert
.

Chambers are interiors of full cones:

Suppose
\mathsfit{K}
is an algebraic heart with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
. Then
C(\mathsfit{K})
is a full cone, so the relative interior
C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})\subseteq{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}\backslash\mathcal{D}
is an open subset.
Each proper face of
C(\mathsfit{K})
has the form
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
for
\mathsfit{S}\neq 0
and thus is contained in
\mathcal{D}
. Hence
C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})
is a chamber.

Conversely, suppose
v\in{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
is in a chamber
\mathcal{C}
. As
\mathbb{D}(\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H}))
is complete,
v
is contained in a minimal dual face
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})
where
(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})
is a distinguished kernel pair with
\mathsfit{S}=\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{v}=0
. It suffices to show that
C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})
is open in
{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
for then
\mathsfit{K}
is algebraic by Proposition
2.9
and Lemma
5.1
, and
\mathcal{C}=C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})
by the first part.

Let
U\subset{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}
be a convex open subset with
v\in U\subset\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\mathsfit{S}^{w}=\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{w}=0
for all
w\in U
.
We claim
\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muw}}=\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
for all
w\in U
. Suppose contrariwise
\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muw}}\neq\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
for some
w\in U
. As
\mathsfit{H}
is algebraic, we may assume
t\in\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}\backslash\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muw}}
, and that every proper quotient of
t
is in
\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muw}}
, i.e.
v(t)\geq 0>w(t)
and
v(t^{\prime\prime}),w(t^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for every proper quotient
t^{\prime\prime}
of
t
. Thus there is
u\in U
on the line segment between
v
and
w
with
u(t)=0
and
u(t^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for all quotients
t^{\prime\prime}
of
t
. Then
u(t^{\prime})=u(t)-u(t/t^{\prime})=-u(t/t^{\prime})\leq 0
for all subobjects
t^{\prime}
of
t
, hence
0\neq t\in\mathsfit{H}^{\mathsf{\mkern 1.0muss}}_{u}
, contradicting
u\in U\subset\mathcal{C}
. We conclude that
\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muw}}=\smash{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu\mathsfit{T}}_{\mkern-6.0muv}}
for all
w\in U
after all. Therefore
\mathsfit{K}^{w}=\mathsfit{K}
for all
w\in U
, i.e.
U\subset C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})
. Thus
C^{\circ}(\mathsfit{K})
is open and we are done.
∎

Definition 7.7.

The
stability fan
of
\mathsfit{H}
is the dual face fan

\Delta^{\mathsf{\mkern-3.0mus}}(\mathsfit{H})\coloneqq\{C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})\in\Delta(\mathsfit{H})\mid(\mathsfit{K},\mathsfit{S})\text{ kernel pair with }\mathsfit{S}\neq 0\}.

Remark 7.8.

It suffices to use the set of distinguished kernel pairs in the definition. The stability fan is a dual face fan by Proposition
7.1
, and is the union of the dual face fans
\Delta(h)
for
0\neq h\in\mathsfit{H}
. Its support
\lvert\Delta^{\mathsf{\mkern-3.0mus}}(\mathsfit{H})\rvert=\bigcup_{h\neq 0}\mathcal{D}(h)

Remark 7.10.

The previous example may suggest that the stability fan is the heart fan with all full cones and all non-rational cones removed. This is not true in general: if
Q=Q^{\prime}\amalg Q^{\prime\prime}
is the disjoint union of the
A_{2}

\Delta({\mathbf{k}}Q)=\Delta({\mathbf{k}}Q^{\prime})\times\Delta({\mathbf{k}}Q^{\prime\prime})

The product of a non-rational ray from
\Delta({\mathbf{k}}Q^{\prime\prime})

\mathsfit{S}

8.
g
-fans

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field
{\mathbf{k}}
. We write
\Delta(A)
for the heart fan of the category of finite-dimensional
A
-modules
\mathsf{mod}(A)
over
\Lambda\coloneqq K(A)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The main result of this section is that the
g
-fan of
A
is the subfan of
\Delta(A)
consisting of the full cones and their faces.
In order to define the
g
-fan of
A
we first recall some silting theory.

8.1.
Projective-simple duality

We may assume that
A
is basic, i.e.
A\cong P(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus P(n)
, where the
P(i)
are pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable projective
A
-modules.
We consider two Hom-finite triangulated categories associated to
A
together with their Grothendieck groups:

\displaystyle\mathsfit{D}^{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathsf{K}^{b}(\mathsf{proj}(A))

the perfect derived category of
A
,

\displaystyle\mathsfit{D}^{b}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsf{mod}(A))

the bounded derived category of
A
,

\displaystyle K(A)

\displaystyle\coloneqq K(\mathsfit{D}^{b})=K(\mathsf{mod}(A))

the Grothendieck group of
A
,

\displaystyle K^{\text{split}}(A)

\displaystyle\coloneqq K(\mathsfit{D}^{p})=K(\mathsf{proj}(A))

\displaystyle\text{the split Grothendieck group of $A$}.

The classes
[P(1)],\ldots,[P(n)]
form a basis of
K^{\text{split}}(A)
and the classes of simple modules
[S(1)],\ldots,[S(n)]
form a basis of
K(A)
. These bases are dual under the non-degenerate pairing

K^{\text{split}}(A)\times K(A)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(P,M)\mapsto\chi(P,M)=\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A}(P,M).

Having set
\Lambda\coloneqq K(A)
, we identify
\Lambda^{*}=K^{\text{split}}(A)
.

8.2.
Two-term (pre)silting objects

Consider the full subcategory of two-term complexes of projective
A
-modules,
\mathsfit{C}\coloneqq\mathsfit{K}^{[-1,0]}(\mathsf{proj}(A))=\mathsf{add}(A)*\mathsf{add}(A)[1]\subset\mathsfit{D}^{p}
. It is an extriangulated category with
K(\mathsfit{C})=K(\mathsfit{D}^{p})
.
A complex
P_{\bullet}\in\mathsfit{C}
is called

•

(two-term)
presilting
if
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathsfit{D}^{p}}(P_{\bullet},P_{\bullet}[{>}0])=0
;

•

(two-term)
silting
if it is presilting and the thick subcategory generated by
P_{\bullet}
is
\mathsfit{D}^{p}
.

While we don’t use the adjective ‘two-term’, we stress that we are working in
\mathsfit{C}
throughout, so that our complexes have non-zero terms in homological degrees
1
and
0
only.

8.3.
g
-vectors,
g
-cones and the
g
-fan

The
g
-vector
of
P_{\bullet}\in\mathsfit{C}
is its class
[P_{\bullet}]\in K(\mathsfit{C})
; writing
P_{\bullet}=(P_{1}\to P_{0})
and decomposing
P_{0}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P(i)^{a_{i}}
and
P_{1}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P(i)^{b_{i}}
, this is the classical
g^{P_{\bullet}}=(a_{1}-b_{1},\ldots,a_{n}-b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong K(\mathsfit{C})
, identified using the basis
P(1),\ldots,P(n)
.

For
P_{\bullet}\in\mathsfit{C}
presilting with indecomposable summands
Q^{1}_{\bullet},\ldots,Q^{t}_{\bullet}
, the
g
-vectors
[Q^{1}_{\bullet}],\ldots,[Q^{t}_{\bullet}]
are linearly independent by
[
DIJ19
]
and generate the
g
-cone

C(P_{\bullet})=\{a_{1}[Q^{1}_{\bullet}]+\cdots+a_{t}[Q^{t}_{\bullet}]\mid a_{i}\geq 0\}\subset{\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}=K(\mathsfit{C})\otimes\mathbb{R}.

They form a basis if
t=n
, i.e. if
P_{\bullet}
is silting.

The
g
-fan

\Delta_{A}^{g}\coloneqq\{C(P_{\bullet})\mid P_{\bullet}\in\mathsfit{C}\text{ is presilting}\}

of a finite-dimensional algebra
A
was introduced in
[
DIJ19
, §6]
.
For comparison with the heart fan, we note that any fan
\Delta
has a subfan
\Delta^{\mathrm{full}}
generated by its full cones and for the heart fan
\Delta(A)
this is

\Delta(A)^{\mathrm{full}}=\{\kappa\in\Delta(A)\mid\exists\sigma\in\Delta(A)\text{ full},\kappa\leq\sigma\}=\{C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})\in\Delta(A)\mid\mathsfit{K}\text{ algebraic}\},

where the identification of full cones with algebraic hearts follows from Proposition
2.9
together with Lemma
5.1
.
The
g
-fan coincides with the full subfan of the heart fan
\Delta(A)
:

Theorem 8.1
.

The
g
-fan of the finite-dimensional algebra
A
is
\Delta_{A}^{g}=\Delta(A)^{\mathrm{full}}
.

Remark 8.2.

In Theorem
3.7
, we showed that the heart fan of any length category is complete. Thus the heart fan of
\mathsf{mod}(A)
is a natural completion of the
g
-fan.
The following finiteness conditions are all equivalent for a finite-dimensional algebra
A
:

(1)

the heart fan is finite;

(2)

the
g
-fan is finite, i.e.
A
is
\tau
-tilting finite (the set of silting complexes in
\mathsfit{C}
is finite);

(3)

the
g
-fan is complete;

(4)

heart fan and
g
-fan coincide;

(5)

all two-term hearts relative to
\mathsfit{H}
are algebraic;

(6)

there are finitely many two-term hearts relative to
\mathsfit{H}
.

Theorem
8.1
shows

1

\Longrightarrow

2
and

3

\Longrightarrow

4

\Longleftrightarrow

5
,
where the last equivalence uses that full heart cones correspond to algebraic hearts by Corollary
5.2
.

2

\Longleftrightarrow

3
is
[
As21
, Thm. 4.7]
,

5

\Longrightarrow

6
follows from
[
AIR14
, Thms. 2.7 & 3.2]
and
[
KY14
, Thm. 6.1]
using

[
DIJ19
, Thm. 3.8]
,
and

6

\implies

1
is just the heart fan definition.

Of these conditions,
1
,
5
,
6
don’t refer to the algebra and so make sense for arbitrary abelian categories.
For algebraic abelian categories, they are indeed equivalent by Theorem
5.3
.

Remark 8.3.

Lutz Hille
[
Hi06
]
defined a fan using classical (partial) tilting modules over a finite-dimensional algebra.
His fan comprises a collection of subcones of the effective cone of the module category, and therefore sits in the positive orthant.
Despite a similar homological approach, Hille’s construction is unrelated to ours and the
g
-fan.

To prove the proposition, we need to describe the connection of silting theory to Serre subcategories via simple-minded collections and
c
-vectors.

8.4.
Koenig-Yang correspondences and Serre subcategories

In
[
KY14
]
, Steffen Koenig and Dong Yang obtain correspondences between algebraic hearts
\mathsfit{K}
in
\mathsfit{D}^{b}
and silting subcategories of
\mathsfit{D}^{p}
which restrict to two-term versions by
[
BY13
]
. Recall that
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}\}
is a
simple-minded collection
in a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear triangulated category if (a)
{\mathrm{Hom}}^{<0}(X_{i},X_{j})=0
for all
i,j
and (b)
{\mathrm{Hom}}(X_{i},X_{i})={\mathbf{k}}
,
{\mathrm{Hom}}(X_{i},X_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
and (c) the collection generates the triangulated category; see
[
KY14
, §3.2]
.

Proposition 8.4

(
[
KY14
, Thm. 6.1]
&
[
BY13
, Cor. 4.3]
)
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra and
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsf{mod}(A)
. There are bijections between the following sets:

(1)

silting objects in
\mathsfit{C}
, up to additive equivalence;

(2)

algebraic hearts
\mathsfit{K}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
;

(3)

simple-minded collections in
\mathsfit{H}[1]*\mathsfit{H}
.

The map from (2) to (3) sends
\mathsfit{K}\mapsto\{\text{simple objects of }\mathsfit{K}\}
; the inverse map from (3) to (2) sends a simple-minded collection
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}\mapsto{\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\rangle}
, its extension closure in
\mathsfit{D}^{b}
.
The objects in (1) and (3) obey a
generalised projective-simple duality
: let
P_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}Q^{i}_{\bullet}
be a silting object and
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
the corresponding simple-minded collection, then

{\mathrm{Hom}}(Q^{i}_{\bullet},X_{i})={\mathbf{k}}
and
{\mathrm{Hom}}(Q^{i}_{\bullet},X_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
.
Moreover,
\mathsfit{K}\simeq\mathsf{mod}({\mathrm{End}}(P_{\bullet}))
.
By Proposition
8.4
and
[
GL91
, Prop. 5.3]
, there are bijections

\{\text{direct summands of }P_{\bullet}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\{\text{subsets of }\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\{\text{Serre subcategories of }\mathsfit{K}\}.

8.5.
c
-vectors

Following
[
As20
, p. 5035]
, if
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
is a simple-minded collection, then the
c
-vector
of
X_{i}
is its class
[X_{i}]\in K(A)
. Expressed in the basis of
K(A)
of simple modules, this gives the classical
c^{X_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Since
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
is a complete list of non-isomorphic simple objects for an algebraic heart in
\mathsfit{D}^{b}
, the
c
-vectors
\{[X_{1}],\ldots,[X_{n}]\}
form a basis of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
.
By generalised simple-projective duality
\chi([Q^{i}_{\bullet}],[X_{j}])=\delta_{ij}
, and the
c
-vector basis is dual to the
g
-vector basis of
\Lambda^{*}
coming from the silting object
P_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}Q^{i}_{\bullet}
corresponding to
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
.

Remark 8.5 (Heart cofans as
c
-vector cofans).

Let
\mathsfit{H}
and
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
be algebraic hearts. The simple objects in
\mathsfit{K}
form a simple-minded collection in
\mathsf{D}^{b}(\mathsfit{H})
and their classes generate the integral cone
E(\mathsfit{K})
; hence the heart cofan
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
or its real version
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{R}}(\mathsfit{H})
can be thought of as convex-geometric objects corresponding to
c
-vectors. This is possible even if the projective objects necessary to define
g
-vectors don’t exist.
Therefore, the heart cofan
\nabla_{\!\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsfit{H})
may be considered as the ‘
c
-cofan’ and the corresponding dual fan
\Delta(\mathsfit{H})
as a ‘virtual
g
-fan’.

We now assemble the ingredients from silting theory to prove Theorem
8.1
.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Let
\mathsfit{K}
be an algebraic heart with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
, write
P_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}Q^{i}_{\bullet}
for the corresponding two-term silting complex in
\mathsfit{C}
, and
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
for the corresponding two-term simple-minded collection in
\mathsfit{H}[1]*\mathsfit{H}
under the bijections in Proposition
8.4
. Then
C(\mathsfit{K})=C(P_{\bullet})
follows from the definitions and the duality
\chi([Q^{i}_{\bullet}],[X_{j}])=\delta_{ij}
.

Now, suppose
\mathsfit{S}\subseteq\mathsfit{K}
is a Serre subcategory. By
[
GL91
, Prop. 5.3]
, we can re-order the two-term simple-minded collection
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
so that
\mathsfit{S}={\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}\rangle}
for some
1\leq t\leq n
.
We order the summands of the corresponding two-term silting complex
P_{\bullet}
accordingly and write
Q_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{i=t+1}^{n}Q^{i}_{\bullet}
.
It follows immediately from the generalised projective-simple duality that
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})=C(Q_{\bullet})
using
C(\mathsfit{K}/\mathsfit{S})=C(\mathsfit{K})\cap E(\mathsfit{S})^{\perp}
from Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Appendix A
Homological algebra

A good source emphasising the similarity of abelian and triangulated categories is
[
BR07
, Ch. I]
.
Categories are assumed to be essentially small, i.e. having a set of objects up to isomorphism. Subcategories are assumed to be full and strict, i.e. closed under isomorphisms.
The
n
-fold shift (or: translation or suspension) functor of triangulated categories is denoted by
[n]
.

A.1.
Abelian categories.

An abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
is called
(of finite) length
if each object of
\mathsfit{H}
admits a finite filtration with simple factors. Equivalently,
\mathsfit{H}
is noetherian (all ascending chains of subobjects stabilise) and artinian (all descending chains stabilise). If in addition
\mathsfit{H}
has finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects, it is called
algebraic
.

A non-empty full subcategory
\mathsfit{U}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
is
wide
if it is closed under kernels, cokernels and extensions, and is
Serre
if it is closed under subobjects, quotients and extensions. The quotient category
\mathsfit{H}/\mathsfit{U}
by a Serre subcategory
\mathsfit{U}
is abelian.
Denote the sets of Serre and wide subcategories by
\mathsf{Serre}(\mathsfit{H})\subseteq\mathsf{Wide}{(\mathsfit{H})}
respectively.
In Definition
2.13
, we introduce
\mathsf{Serre}_{\Lambda\!}(\mathsfit{H})
as the subset of face subcategories.

A.2.
Triangulated categories.

Let
\mathsfit{D}
be a triangulated category.
A subcategory
\mathsfit{U}
of
\mathsfit{D}
is called
thick
if it is a triangulated subcategory of
\mathsfit{D}
and is closed under direct summands. We denote the set of thick subcategories by
\mathsf{Thick}{(\mathsfit{D})}
. If
S
is any collection of objects of
\mathsfit{D}
then we denote by
\mathsf{thick}(S)
the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathsfit{D}
containing
S
.

A.3.
Extension closure.

Let
\mathsfit{C}
be an abelian or triangulated category, so we can speak of extensions in
\mathsfit{C}
.
The
ordered extension closure

\mathsfit{M}_{1}*\mathsfit{M}_{2}
of subcategories
\mathsfit{M}_{1},\mathsfit{M}_{2}\subseteq\mathsfit{C}
is the subcategory of all extensions of objects of
\mathsfit{M}_{2}
by objects of
\mathsfit{M}_{1}
.
E.g. for
\mathsfit{C}
triangulated
\mathsfit{M}_{1}*\mathsfit{M}_{2}
consists of
c\in\mathsfit{C}
sitting in exact triangles
m_{1}\to c\to m_{2}\to m_{1}[1]
with
m_{1}\in\mathsfit{M}_{1},m_{2}\in\mathsfit{M}_{2}
.

The
extension closure
of a subcategory
\mathsfit{M}\subseteq\mathsfit{C}
is
{\langle M\rangle}\coloneqq\mathsfit{M}*\mathsfit{M}
. For
\mathsfit{H}\subset\mathsfit{C}
a heart in a triangulated category and
\mathsfit{M}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
, the two notions of
{\langle\mathsfit{M}\rangle}
agree.

A.4.
Torsion pairs.

Two full subcategories
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
of an abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
form a
torsion pair
if (a)
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})=0
and (b)
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsfit{T}*\mathsfit{F}
. In this case,
\mathsfit{T}
is called the
torsion class
and
\mathsfit{F}
the
torsionfree class
of the pair.
Torsion classes are closed under quotients and extensions; torsionfree classes are closed under subobjects and extensions.
If
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
is a torsion pair then
\mathsfit{F}=\mathsfit{T}^{\perp}\coloneqq\{h\in\mathsfit{H}\mid{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{T},h)=0\}
and
\mathsfit{T}={}^{\perp}\mathsfit{F}
; e.g.
\mathsfit{F}\subseteq\mathsfit{T}^{\perp}
by (a) and
\mathsfit{F}\supseteq\mathsfit{T}^{\perp}
by (b).

If
\mathsfit{H}
is noetherian and
\mathsfit{T}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
is a subcategory closed under quotients and extensions then
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{T}^{\perp})
is a torsion pair. Dually, if
\mathsfit{H}
is artinian and
\mathsfit{F}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}
closed under subobjects and extensions then
({}^{\perp}\mathsfit{F},\mathsfit{F})
is a torsion pair. An example is
\mathsfit{F}={\langle s\rangle}
for a simple object
s\in\mathsfit{H}
.

A.5.
T-structures.

Two subcategories
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
, called
aisle
and
co-aisle
of a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
form a
t-structure
if (a)
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})=0
and (b)
\mathsfit{C}=\mathsfit{T}*\mathsfit{F}
and (c)
\mathsfit{T}[1]\subseteq\mathsfit{T}
. The t-structure is
bounded
if moreover (d)
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathsfit{T}[n]=\mathsfit{D}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathsfit{F}[n]
.
Any t-structure
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
on
\mathsfit{D}
, bounded or not, induces an abelian subcategory of
\mathsfit{D}
as
\mathsfit{H}\coloneqq\mathsfit{T}\cap\mathsfit{F}[1]
called the
heart
. If the t-structure is bounded then
\mathsfit{T}
and
\mathsfit{F}
can be reconstructed from the heart
\mathsfit{H}
as
\mathsfit{T}={\langle\mathsfit{H}[\mathit{\geq}0]\rangle}
and
\mathsfit{F}={\langle\mathsfit{H}[\mathit{<}0]\rangle}
. In this article,
heart
always means the
heart of a bounded t-structure
.

Setting
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})\geq(\mathsfit{T}^{\prime},\mathsfit{F}^{\prime})\iff\mathsfit{F}\supseteq\mathsfit{F}^{\prime}
defines a partial order on the bounded t-structures of a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
.
Equivalently,

\mathsfit{H}\geq\mathsfit{H}^{\prime}\iff{\langle\mathsfit{H}[{\leq}0]\rangle}\supseteq{\langle\mathsfit{H}^{\prime}[{\leq}0]\rangle}

gives a partial order on hearts in
\mathsfit{D}
.
The convention we follow here makes
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{H}
.

A heart
\mathsfit{K}
is called
two-term relative to
\mathsfit{H}
if
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
.
The next fact is well known.

Lemma A.1
.

For bounded hearts
\mathsfit{H}
and
\mathsfit{K}
in
\mathsfit{D}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
;

(ii)

\mathsfit{K}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}[1]*\mathsfit{H}
;

(iii)

{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{H}[{\geq}2],\mathsfit{K})=0
and
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{K}[{\geq}1],\mathsfit{H})=0
.

Proof.

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
: The inequalities say
\mathsfit{K}\subseteq{\langle\mathsfit{K}[{\leq}0]\rangle}\subseteq{\langle\mathsfit{H}[{\leq}1]\rangle}
and
\mathsfit{K}\subseteq{\langle\mathsfit{K}[{\geq}0]\rangle}\subseteq{\langle\mathsfit{H}[{\geq}0]\rangle}
, hence
\mathsfit{K}\subseteq{\langle\mathsfit{H}[1],\mathsfit{H}\rangle}
. To show
\mathsfit{H}*\mathsfit{H}[1]\subseteq\mathsfit{H}[1]*\mathsfit{H}
, take
d\in\mathsfit{H}*\mathsfit{H}[1]
, i.e.
d
is the cone of a morphism
\alpha\colon h_{1}\to h_{0}
in
\mathsfit{H}
. The octahedral axiom for the composition
h_{1}\to\mathrm{im}(\alpha)\to h_{0}
produces the exact triangle
\ker(\alpha)[1]\to d\to\mathrm{coker}(\alpha)\to\ker(\alpha)[2]
, exhibiting
d\in\mathsfit{H}[1]*\mathsfit{H}
.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
:
\mathsfit{H}
is a heart, so
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{H}[{\geq}1],\mathsfit{H})=0
; with
\mathsfit{K}\subseteq\mathsfit{H}[1]*\mathsfit{H}
this gives
(iii)
.

(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)
: We have
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{H}[{\geq}2],\mathsfit{K}[{\leq}0])=0
, so that
{\langle\mathsfit{K}[{\leq}0]\rangle}\subseteq(\mathsfit{H}[{\geq}2])^{\perp}={\langle\mathsfit{H}[{\leq}1]\rangle}
, where the last equality holds because
\mathsfit{H}
is a bounded heart. Hence,
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}
.
Similarly,
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathsfit{K}[{\geq}1],\mathsfit{H}[{\leq}0])=0
gives
{\langle\mathsfit{H}[{\leq}0]\rangle}\subseteq(\mathsfit{K}[{\geq}1])^{\perp}={\langle\mathsfit{K}[{\leq}0]\rangle}
and
\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
.
∎

A.6.
Tilting.

Let
\mathsfit{D}
be a triangulated category,
\mathsfit{H}
a heart in
\mathsfit{D}
and
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
a torsion pair in the abelian category
\mathsfit{H}
, so that
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsfit{T}*\mathsfit{F}
. From this setup we get two new hearts of
\mathsfit{D}
as

\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}\text{, the \emph{{positive tilt}} of $\mathsfit{H}$};\qquad\mathsfit{F}*\mathsfit{T}[-1]\text{, the \emph{{negative tilt}} of $\mathsfit{H}$}.

The terminology is justified by
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}\geq\mathsfit{H}\geq\mathsfit{F}*\mathsfit{T}[-1]\geq\mathsfit{H}[-1]
.
Positive/negative tilts are also called left/right.
This fact is crucial for us, see
[
Po03
, Lemma 1.1.2]
:
If
\mathsfit{K}
and
\mathsfit{H}
are two hearts in a triangulated category
\mathsfit{D}
with
\mathsfit{H}[1]\geq\mathsfit{K}\geq\mathsfit{H}
, then there is a unique torsion pair
(\mathsfit{T},\mathsfit{F})
on
\mathsfit{H}=\mathsfit{T}*\mathsfit{F}
with
\mathsfit{K}=\mathsfit{F}[1]*\mathsfit{T}
; it is given by
\mathsfit{T}=\mathsfit{H}\cap\mathsfit{K}
and
\mathsfit{F}=\mathsfit{H}\cap\mathsfit{K}[-1]
.

The
positive irreducible simple tilt
of an artinian category
\mathsfit{H}
at a simple object
s\in\mathsfit{H}
is given by the torsion pair
({}^{\perp}s,{\langle s\rangle})
.

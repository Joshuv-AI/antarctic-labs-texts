Abstract
. Elliott and Halberstam proved that ∑ p < n 2 ω ⁡ ( n − p ) \sum_{p<n}2^{\omega(n-p)} is asymptotic to ϕ ⁡ ( n ) \phi(n) . In analogy to the Erdős–Kac Theorem, Elliott conjectured that if one restricts the summation to primes p p such that ω ⁡ ( n − p ) ≤ 2 ​ log ​ log ⁡ n + λ ​ ( 2 ​ log ​ log ⁡ n ) 1 / 2 \omega(n-p)\leq 2\log\log n+\lambda(2\log\log n)^{1/2} then the sum will be asymptotic to ϕ ( n ) ∫ − ∞ λ e − t 2 / 2 d t / 2 ​ π \phi(n)\int_{-\infty}^{\lambda}e^{-t^{2}/2}dt/\sqrt{2\pi} . We show that this conjecture follows from the Bombieri–Vinogradov Theorem. We further prove a related result involving Poisson–Dirichlet distribution, employing deeper lying level of distribution results of the primes.



1.
Introduction and motivation

Let
\omega(m)=\sum_{p\mid m}1
be the prime divisor function. Elliott and Halberstam proved that
[
9
]

(1.1)

\sum_{p<n}2^{\omega(n-p)}=\phi(n)+O\left(\frac{n}{\log n}(\log\log n)^{2}\right)

holds for
n\geq 3
. Here and later
p
denotes a prime and
\phi
is Euler’s totient function. Elliott gave a talk at the meeting held in Urbana–Champaign, June 5–7, 2014, in memory of Paul and Felice Bateman, and Heini Halberstam, where he revisited his works with Halberstam. A telegraphic representation of the talk was published, which contains the following conjecture
[
10
, Conj. B]
: for each real
\lambda
,

(1.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<n\\
\omega(n-p)\leq 2\log\log n+\lambda(2\log\log n)^{1/2}\end{subarray}}2^{\omega(n-p)}\to\frac{\phi(n)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\lambda}e^{-u^{2}/2}du

as
n\to\infty
. In this note we establish the conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

The asymptotic relation (
1.2
) holds for each real
\lambda
.

Theorem
1.1
can be restated as follows. Let
X_{n}
be a random variable taking the value
m\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with probability proportional to
2^{\omega(m)}\mathbf{1}_{n-m\textup{ a prime}}
. Then

(1.3)

\frac{\omega(X_{n})-2\log\log n}{(2\log\log n)^{1/2}}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}N(0,1),\qquad n\to\infty,

where
N(0,1)
is the standard gaussian distribution and the arrow indicates convergence in distribution. The proof of Theorem
1.1
will follow from

Proposition 1.2
.

Fix
k,B\geq 1
. For any
1\leq R\leq n^{1/100}
and
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
we have

(1.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq R,\,a\in S\\
\omega(a)\leq k\\
(a,n)=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(a)\bigg|\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }X_{n})-\frac{2^{\omega(a)}}{a}\bigg|\ll_{k,B}\frac{(\log\log n)^{3}+\log R}{\log n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq R,\,a\in S\\
\omega(a)\leq k\\
(a,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(a)}{a}+\frac{1}{\log^{B}n}.

The reduction of Theorem
1.1
to Proposition
1.2
is explained in §
3
. The proof of Proposition
1.2
is given in §
4
and uses the classical tools utilized in
[
9
]
(the Brun–Titchmarsh and Bombieri–Vinogradov Theorems). Proposition
1.2
has little content when
\log R\asymp\log n
. This is remedied by the following proposition, proved using recent work on primes in arithmetic progressions
[
6
]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

There is an absolute constant
\eta>0
with the following property. For every
k\geq 1
,

(1.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq n^{\eta}\\
\omega(a)\leq k\\
(a,n)=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(a)\bigg|\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }X_{n})-\frac{2^{\omega(a)}}{a}+\frac{2D_{a}}{\phi(a)\log n}\bigg|\ll_{k}\frac{(\log\log n)^{C_{k}}}{\log n}

where
C_{k}
is a positive constant depending only on
k
,

(1.6)

D_{a}:=\sum_{d\mid a^{\infty}}g(d)\log d,\qquad g(d):=\prod_{p^{k}\mid\mid d}(-1)^{k-1}(p-2)(p-1)^{-k}.

Since
\sum_{d\mid a^{\infty}}|g(d)|=2^{\omega(a)-\mathbf{1}_{2\mid a}}
, we have

(1.7)

|D_{a}|\leq\sum_{p\mid a}\sum_{i\geq 1}\log(p^{i})\sum_{p^{i}\mid\mid d\mid a^{\infty}}|g(d)|\leq 2^{\omega(a)}\sum_{p\mid a}\sum_{i\geq 1}\log(p^{i})|g(p^{i})|\ll 2^{\omega(a)}\log a.

Proposition
1.3
is proved in §
5
.
The lower order term in (
1.5
) leads to the following application. Given an integer
m>1
we write it as

m=\prod_{i=1}^{\Omega(m)}p_{i}(m)
where
\Omega(m)
is the number of prime factors of
m
(multiplicity counted) and
p_{1}(m)\geq\ldots\geq p_{\Omega(m)}
. We define

(1.8)

V(m):=\left(\frac{\log p_{i}(m)}{\log m}\right)_{i\geq 1}

where
p_{i}(m):=1
for
i>\Omega(m)
. This vector sums to
1
and its entries are nonincreasing. Let
\textup{PD}(\theta)
be the Poisson–Dirichlet distribution with parameter
\theta
(see Chapter 1 of
[
5
]
for its definition).

Corollary 1.4
.

If
V(X_{n})
tends in distribution to
\textup{PD}(\theta)
for some
\theta>0
then
\theta=2
.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{n}
be the random variable taking the value
m\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with probability proportional to
2^{\omega(m)}
. As observed by Elliott
[
10
]
,

(1.9)

\frac{\omega(\widetilde{X}_{n})-2\log\log n}{(2\log\log n)^{1/2}}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}N(0,1),\qquad n\to\infty.

See §
6
for a self-contained proof of (
1.9
). As shown in
[
8
]
,
V(\widetilde{X}_{n})
tends to
\textup{PD}(2)
. We expect Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.4
to hold because
X_{n}
is distributed like
\widetilde{X}_{n}
, only with support restricted to shifted primes which are believed to behave in most ways like random integers.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In the next short section we show how Corollary
1.4
follows from Proposition
1.3
. Afterwards in §
3
we reduce the conjecture of Elliott we prove, Theorem
1.1
to Proposition
1.2
. In §

we show how Proposition
1.2

1.1
is complete. In the penultimate §

[

1.3

1.9

2.
Proof of Corollary
1.4

We now show how Corollary
1.4
follows from Proposition
1.3
. Suppose
V(X_{n})\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\textup{PD}(\theta)
, and in particular
V(X_{n})_{i}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\textup{PD}(\theta)_{i}
for all
i
. Since
2^{\omega(n-p)}=n^{o(1)}
, we have
\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\leq T)\ll T/n^{1+o(1)}
, so that
\log X_{n}/\log n
tends to
1
in probability. This implies
\widetilde{V}(X_{n})_{i}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\textup{PD}(\theta)_{i}
for
\widetilde{V}(X_{n})_{i}:=\log p_{i}(X_{n})/\log n
. By
[
12
, Thm. 2.1]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{V}(X_{n})_{i}\in[a,b]}=\theta\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(1-t)^{\theta-1}}{t}dt

for all
0<a<b<1
(the sum appears infinite but can be truncated at
i\leq\lfloor 1/a\rfloor
since
\sum_{i\geq 1}\widetilde{V}(X_{n})_{i}\leq 1
). This is simplified as

(2.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{p\in[n^{a},n^{b}]}\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(p^{k}\text{ divides }X_{n})=\theta\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(1-t)^{\theta-1}}{t}dt.

Since
\mathbb{P}(d\text{ divides }X_{n})\ll n^{o(1)}/d
, (
2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{n^{a}\leq p\leq n^{b}}\mathbb{P}(p\text{ divides }X_{n})=\theta\int_{a}^{b}\frac{(1-t)^{\theta-1}}{t}dt.

By Proposition
1.3
with
k=1
we have

(2.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{n^{a}\leq p\leq n^{b}}\mathbb{P}(p\text{ divides }X_{n})=2\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1-t}{t}dt

by Mertens’ theorem
[
13
, Thm. 2.7(d)]
, as long as
b<\eta
. This is consistent with (
2.2
) only if
\theta=2
.

Remark 2.1
.

More generally, let
k\geq 1
and a continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
such that the (closure of the) support of
f
is
\subseteq\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}):x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}>0,\,x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}<\eta\}
. Then with additional work one can compute

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\text{ distinct}}f(V(X_{n})_{j_{1}},\ldots,V(X_{n})_{j_{k}})

using Proposition
1.3
and show that it coincides with

\mathbb{E}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\text{ distinct}}f(L_{j_{1}},\ldots,L_{j_{k}})

where
(L_{1},L_{2},\ldots)
is distributed according to
\textup{PD}(2)
. In the language of Bharadwaj and Rodgers
[
3
]
, this means we can compute the correlation functions of large prime factors of
X_{n}
and show they tend to those of the Poisson–Dirichlet
process with parameter
\theta=2
, at least for test functions with restricted support.

3.
Reduction

We now reduce Theorem
1.1
to Proposition
1.2
. In other words, we make precise the well-known principle (see for example
[
11
]
) that Erdős–Kac type results can be deduced from level of distribution information.

Let

(3.1)

C_{k}:=\mathbb{E}X^{k},\qquad C^{\prime}_{k}:=\mathbb{E}|X|^{k},

where
X\sim N(0,1)
. It is known that for (
1.3
) to hold it suffices to show that

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\omega(X_{n})-2\log\log n}{(2\log\log n)^{1/2}}\right)^{k}\to C_{k},\qquad n\to\infty,

holds for every integer
k\geq 1

[
7
, Ch. 3.3.5]
. To approach (
3.2
) we use

Proposition 3.1
.

[
11
]

Let
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be an arithmetic function and
\mathcal{P}
be a squarefree integer. Let
h\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a multiplicative function with
h(p)\leq p
for
p|\mathcal{P}
. For
a|\mathcal{P}
define
r_{a}
via

\sum_{m\leq n:\,a\mid m}f(m)=\frac{h(a)}{a}\sum_{m\leq n}f(m)+r_{a}.

Let

\mu_{\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}\frac{h(p)}{p},\qquad\sigma^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}\frac{h(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{h(p)}{p}\right).

Uniformly for
1\leq k\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{2/3}
we have

(3.3)

\sum_{m=1}^{n}f(m)\big(\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{1}_{p\mid m}-\mu_{\mathcal{P}}\big)^{k}=\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{k}\sum_{m=1}^{n}f(m)\big(C_{k}+O((k^{3/2}\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1})^{1+\mathbf{1}_{2\mid k}}C^{\prime}_{k})\big)+O\bigg(\mu_{\mathcal{P}}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(a)\leq k\\
a\mid\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}|r_{a}|\bigg),

where
C_{k}
,
C^{\prime}_{k}
are given by (
3.1
).

For
f\equiv 1
Proposition
3.1
is
[
11
, Prop. 3]
and the proof works the same way for general
f\geq 0
. We derive from Proposition
3.1
the following criterion, to be proved below.

Corollary 3.2
.

For every sufficiently large
n
let
Y_{n}
be a random variable taking values in
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and set

I_{n}:=(\exp(\exp(\log^{1/3}\log n)),n^{\exp(-\log^{1/3}\log n)}),\quad T:=n^{1/\log^{1/3}\log n},\quad\mathcal{P}:=\prod_{p\in I_{n}}p.

Fix
k\geq 1
. Suppose that

(3.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(a)\leq k\\
a\mid\prod_{p<T}p/\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }Y_{n})=o\big((\log\log n)^{k/2}\big)

as
n\to\infty
, and that for some multiplicative function
g\colon\mathbb{N}\to[0,1]
,

(3.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(a)\leq k\\
a\mid\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\bigg|\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }Y_{n})-g(a)\bigg|=o\big((\log\log n)^{-k/2}\big)

as
n\to\infty
. If
\sum_{p\leq x}g(p)=\theta\log\log x+O(1)
for some
\theta>0
and
\sum_{p\leq x}g^{2}(p)=O(1)
then

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\frac{\omega(Y_{n})-\theta\log\log n}{(\theta\log\log n)^{1/2}}\bigg)^{k}\to C_{k}

as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

We consider a given
n
and apply Proposition
3.1
with
f(m)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}=m)
,
h(m)=g(m)m
. Define

(3.7)

\omega_{\mathcal{P}}(n)=\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}\mathbf{1}_{p\mid n},\quad\mu=\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}g(p),\quad\sigma^{2}=\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}g(p)(1-g(p)),\quad R_{a}=\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }Y_{n})-g(a).

We find that

(3.8)

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}=m)\left(\frac{\omega_{\mathcal{P}}(m)-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^{k}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}=m)\big(C_{k}+O(\sigma^{-1-\mathbf{1}_{2\mid k}})\big)+O\bigg(\frac{\mu^{k}}{\sigma^{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(a)\leq k\\
a\mid\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}|R_{a}|\bigg)

holds for every fixed
k\geq 1
(implied constants may depend on
k
). We claim

(3.9)

\mu,\sigma^{2}=\theta\log\log n+O(\log^{1/3}\log n).

Indeed, by our assumptions on
\sum_{p\leq x}g(p)
and
\sum_{p\leq x}g^{2}(p)
,

0\leq\mu-\sigma^{2}=\sum_{p\mid\mathcal{P}}g^{2}(p)\leq\sum_{p\leq n}g^{2}(p)=O(1)

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{p<n^{\exp(-\log^{1/3}\log n)}}g(p)-\sum_{p\leq\exp(\exp(\log^{1/3}\log n))}g(p)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\theta\log\log(n^{\exp(-\log^{1/3}\log n)})+O(1)-\theta\log\log(\exp(\exp(\log^{1/3}\log n)))+O(1)

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\theta\log\log n+O(\log^{1/3}\log n).

We express (
3.8
) as

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\omega_{\mathcal{P}}(Y_{n})-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^{k}-C_{k}\ll(\log\log n)^{-1/2}+(\log\log n)^{k/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(a)\leq k\\
a\mid\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}|R_{a}|.

By (
3.5
), the right-hand side of (
3.13
) goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
. We have the identity

\frac{\omega(Y_{n})-\theta\log\log n}{(\theta\log\log n)^{1/2}}=A_{1}A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4}+A_{5}

for

(3.14)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma}{(\theta\log\log n)^{1/2}},\qquad A_{2}=\frac{\omega_{\mathcal{P}}(Y_{n})-\mu}{\sigma},\qquad A_{3}=\frac{\mu-\theta\log\log n}{(\theta\log\log n)^{1/2}},

(3.15)

\displaystyle A_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{p\in[2,T)\setminus I_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{p\mid Y_{n}}}{(\theta\log\log n)^{1/2}},\qquad A_{5}=\frac{\sum_{p\in[T,n]}\mathbf{1}_{p\mid Y_{n}}}{(\theta\log\log n)^{1/2}}

for
T
and
I_{n}
as in the statement of the corollary. By (
3.9
), the (constant) random variables
A_{1}
and
A_{3}
tend to
1
and
0
, respectively. We have observed
\mathbb{E}A_{2}^{k}\to C_{k}
by (
3.13
). An integer
m
can have at most
\log m/\log T
prime factors of size at least
T
, implying
A_{5}\ll(\log\log n)^{-1/6}
and so
\mathbb{E}A_{5}^{k}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. To handle
A_{4}
we use (
3.4
) which yields

(3.16)

\mathbb{E}A_{4}^{k}=\frac{\sum_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\in[2,T)\setminus I_{n}}\mathbb{P}([p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}]\textup{ divides }Y_{n})}{(\theta\log\log n)^{k/2}}\ll\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega(a)\leq k\\
a\mid\prod_{p<T}p/\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(a\text{ divides }Y_{n})}{(\log\log n)^{k/2}}=o(1).

We have shown
\mathbb{E}|A_{3}+A_{4}+A_{5}|^{k}\to 0
and
\mathbb{E}(A_{1}A_{2})^{k}\to C_{k}
. By Hölder, (

4.
Proof of Proposition
1.2

We have
\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }X_{n})=A_{a}(n)/A_{1}(n)
where

(4.1)

A_{a}(n):=\sum_{p<n:\,a\mid n-p}2^{\omega(n-p)}.

Using (
1.1
) we see that Proposition
1.2
will follow if we show

(4.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq R,\,a\in S\\
\omega(a)\leq k\\
(a,n)=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(a)\bigg|A_{a}(n)-\phi(n)\frac{2^{\omega(a)}}{a}\bigg|\ll_{k,B}\frac{\phi(n)\log R+n(\log\log n)^{2}}{\log n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq R,\,a\in S\\
\omega(a)\leq k\\
(a,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(a)}{a}+\frac{n}{\log^{B}n}.

We write
2^{\omega(n-p)}
as a divisor sum using
2^{\omega(m)}=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu^{2}(d)
and
\mu^{2}(d)=\sum_{e^{2}\mid d}\mu(e)
and separate into different ranges:

2^{\omega(m)}=\sum_{m=ds}\mu^{2}(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=ds\\
d\leq D\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(d)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=ds\\
D<s<m/D\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(d)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=e^{2}ds\\
s\leq D\\
e\leq V\end{subarray}}\mu(e)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=e^{2}ds\\
s\leq D\\
e>V\end{subarray}}\mu(e),

with the choices

(4.3)

V=\log n\qquad\text{and}\qquad D=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{R^{2}\exp((\log\log n)^{2})},

and
m
is equal to

. We split
A_{a}(n)
accordingly:

Proposition 5.5

Lemma 6.1
.

For positive integers
1\leq a\leq n
such that
\omega(a)\leq k
we have

\mathbb{P}(a\textup{ divides }\widetilde{X}_{n})=\frac{2^{\omega(a)}}{a}\bigg(1-\frac{\log a}{\log n}+O_{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\log n}+\sum_{p\mid a}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)\bigg).

Proof.

Let
S_{a}(n):=\sum_{a\mid r\leq n}2^{\omega(r)}
. We have

(6.1)

\displaystyle S_{a}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq n/a}2^{\omega(am)}=\sum_{d\mid a^{\infty}}2^{\omega(ad)}\sum_{m^{\prime}\leq n/(ad),\,(m^{\prime},a)=1}2^{\omega(m^{\prime})}=A+B

We have proved

(6.2)

\left|S_{a}(n)-2^{\omega(a)}S_{1}(n/a)\right|\leq\sum_{1<d\mid a^{\infty}}(2^{\omega(ad)}+2^{\omega(a)+\omega(d)})S_{1}(n/(da)).

We divide (

[

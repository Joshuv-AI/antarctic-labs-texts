L p L^{p} -Strong solution for the stationary exterior Stokes equations with Navier boundary condition
Dhifaoui Anis

Abstract
. This paper treats the stationary Stokes problem in exterior domain of ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} with Navier slip boundary condition. The behavior at infinity of the data and the solution are determined by setting the problem in L p L^{p} -spaces, for p > 2 p>2 , with weights. The main results are the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions of the corresponding system.

We consider a simply-connected bounded domain

(1.1)

\begin{split}-\nu\Delta\textit{{u}}+\nabla\pi&=\textit{{f}}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\mathrm{div}\,\textit{{u}}&=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\end{split}

\begin{split}\textit{{u}}\cdot\textit{{n}}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Gamma,\end{split}

\begin{split}\textit{{u}}_{\tau}=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\Gamma,\end{split}

2.2.
Weighted Sobolev spaces

In order to control the behavior at infinity of our functions and distributions we use for basic weights the quantity
\rho(\textbf{{x}})=(1+r^{2})^{1/2}
which is equivalent to
r
at infinity, and to one on any bounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we introduce

W_{k}^{0,p}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),\,\rho^{k}u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\Big\},

which is a Banach space equipped with the norm:

\|u\|_{W_{k}^{0,p}(\Omega)}=\|\rho^{k}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

For any non-negative integers
m
, real numbers
p>1
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We define the weighted Sobolev space for
3/p+k\notin\{1,\cdots,m\}

W_{k}^{m,p}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega);\,\forall\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}:\,0\leq|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|\leq m,\,\rho^{k-m+|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|}D^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\Big\}.

It is a reflexive Banach space equipped with the norm:

\|u\|_{W_{k}^{m,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{0\leqslant|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|\leqslant m}\|\rho^{k-m+|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|}D^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/p}.

We define the semi-norm

|u|_{W_{k}^{m,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|=m}\|\rho^{k}D^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/p}.

Let us give some examples of such space that will be often used in the remaining of this work.

1)

For

, we have

\displaystyle{W_{k}^{1,p}(\Omega)}:={\{}

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega);\,\rho^{k-1}u\in L^{p}(\Omega),\,\rho^{k}\,\nabla\,u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\}

2)

For
m=2
, we have

W_{k+1}^{2,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in{W_{k}^{1,p}(\Omega)},\rho^{k+1}\nabla^{2}u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\right\},

2.5.

Theorem 2.9

3.
Strong solutions for the exterior Stokes problem

In this section, we are interested in the following problem:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{T})\begin{cases}-\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}+\nabla\,{\pi}=\textbf{{f}}\quad and\quad\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{u}}={\chi}\qquad\quad\text{ in }\,\Omega,\\
\quad\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=g\,\,\,\,and\quad 2[\mathrm{\textbf{D}}(\textbf{{u}})\textbf{{n}}]_{\tau}+\alpha\textbf{{u}}_{\tau}=\textbf{{h}}\quad\text{ on }\,\Gamma.\par\end{cases}

In this part, we investigate the well-posedness of strong solutions in

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k+1}^{p}(\Omega)=\Big\{(\textbf{{u}},{\pi})\in W^{2,p}_{k+1}(\Omega)\times{{W}}^{1,p}_{k+1}(\Omega);\,

\displaystyle-\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}+\nabla\,{\pi}=0,\,\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{u}}=0\text{ in }\,\Omega\text{ and }\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0,\,\,2[\mathrm{\textbf{D}}(\textbf{{u}})\textbf{{n}}]_{\tau}+\alpha\textbf{{u}}_{\tau}=0\,\text{ on }\,\Gamma\Big\}.

The characterization of the kernel

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider an exterior domain
\Omega
with boundary
\Gamma
of class
\mathcal{C}^{2.1}
. We have

\mathcal{N}_{k+1}^{p}(\Omega)=\left\{(\textbf{{v}}-\boldsymbol{\lambda},{\theta}-\mu);\quad(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\mu)\in N_{[1-k-3/p]}\right\},

where

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\,\textbf{{v}}+\nabla\,{\theta}=\boldsymbol{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{v}}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\[4.0pt]

Let us assume that

. It follows from [Theorem 3.9,
[
5
]
], that there exists a unique solution

It follows that
({\textbf{{v}}}-\widetilde{\textbf{{u}}},{\theta}-\widetilde{\pi})
belongs to

\left(W^{2,2}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})+{{W}}^{2,p}_{k+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\right)\times\left(W^{1,2}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})+W^{1,p}_{k+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\right)

Thus,

belongs to

, its restriction to

∎

(\mathcal{S}_{T})
when

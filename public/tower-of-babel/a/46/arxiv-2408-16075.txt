Topological superconductivity from repulsive interactions in twisted WSe 2
Daniele Guerci, Daniel Kaplan, Julian Ingham, J. H. Pixley, Andrew Millis

Abstract
The recent observation of superconductivity in twisted bilayer WSe 2 raises intriguing questions concerning the origin and the properties of superconducting states realized in bands with non-trivial topological properties and repulsive electron-electron interactions. Using a continuum band structure model, we analyze a mechanism for Coulomb interaction-driven superconductivity in twisted bilayers of WSe 2 . We discuss the symmetries and the phenomenological properties of the resulting superconducting phases and their evolution with interlayer potential difference, tunable via an out of plane electric field. The pairing strength is a non-monotonic function of interlayer potential, being larger at intermediate values due to mixing of singlet and triplet pairing. In contrast, at larger interlayer potential, the pairing tendency is suppressed due to enhanced Coulomb repulsion. The superconducting state is chiral in a large regime of parameters and undergoes a transition to a nodal nematic superconductor at a critical potential difference. The chiral state, characterized by an intervalley-symmetric superposition of triplet and singlet pairs, is classified as a topological superconductor within the Altland-Zirnbauer class C. At zero interlayer potential difference, the superconducting state is instead of class D, which hosts Majorana zero modes, making it a promising candidate for applications in quantum computation.

The mixed singlet-triplet nature of the chiral superconductor has important consequences for the edge theory. In the zero displacement field case, the topological superconductor in class D hosts protected Majorana edge modes

\bm{\eta}=(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)^{T}

Conclusions.—
We have demonstrated that in tWSe
2
, an electronic mechanism for superconductivity leads to attraction with dominant superconducting channels
E^{-}
and subleading
A^{-}
, both originating from inversion (
I
) odd (triplet) instabilities at vanishing displacement field.
Competing
E^{-}
and
A^{-}
symmetry instabilities were also identified within a spin fluctuation mechanism
[
83
]
, while an
A^{-}
is consistent with a phonon-mediated mechanism
[
81
]
.
The two-dimensional nature of the
E^{-}
representation gives rise to competing chiral (gapped) and nematic (nodal) superconducting ordering, both described at finite displacement field by an intervalley symmetric superposition of
S^{z}=0
triplet and singlet channels
[
77
]
.
Through variational calculations, we showed that the chiral superconductor is the lowest energy solution over a wide range of displacement fields and belongs to the C Altland-Zirnbauer class, hosting Dirac edge states that can be probed via STM and thermal transport measurements. At a critical displacement field, we find a transition from the chiral state to the nematic state, which is time-reversal symmetric and topologically trivial. This transition results in distinctive low-energy Leggett modes of the relative phase of the two-component order parameter
[
123
]
.

In both the experiments
[
38
,
39
]
, superconductivity is seen to be enhanced at finite displacement field – consistent with our proposal of mixed singlet-triplet pairing at finite
u_{D}
. In Ref.
[
38
]
, superconductivity extends down to zero displacement fields, where the superconducting state hosts Majorana zero modes
[
55
]
.
We remark that the chiral superconductor despite breaking TRS is valley symmetric (
S^{z}=0
) and cannot be detected by probes only sensitive to valley polarization.

Independent of the precise superconducting order parameter symmetry, unique features of this system suggest an interesting interplay with magnetic fields. The spin-valley locking implies that the in-plane Zeeman coupling is negligible, while the orbital coupling introduced by the minimal substitution
\hat{\bm{k}}_{t/b}\to\hat{\bm{k}}_{t/b}\pm e\bm{B}_{\parallel}d/2\hbar
, with
d
thickness of the bilayer, acts as a pair-breaking term and for large
\bm{B}_{\parallel}
might lead to the formation of a pair density wave. An estimate of this effect can be obtained by noting that
d\approx 0.7
nm and
a_{\rm M}\approx 4.4
nm, which yields a momentum shift of
a_{\rm M}d{eB_{\parallel}/\hbar}\approx 5\times 10^{-3}
for a magnetic field of
1
Tesla.
On the other hand, a magnetic field
\bm{B}_{z}
perpendicular to the bilayer strongly suppresses the intervalley symmetric superconducting state.

The prediction of a chiral superconducting ground state tunable with displacement field will allow for the observation of the superconducting diode effect (SDE)
[
124
,
125
]
. The state predicted here realizes the minimal requirements by breaking in-plane rotational symmetries, inversion, and time reversal in the superconducting state. This ground state differs from the scenario of a Josephson diode
[
126
,
127
]
, as the intrinsic effect (junction-free) probes the symmetry breaking of the superconducting ground state. Time-reversal symmetry breaking in the normal state (a necessary condition) can be achieved through via a Zeeman field
[
128
]
or the introduction of a magnetic substrate
[
129
]
.
The diode effect manifests in the measurement of the
j_{+}
,
j_{-}
which stand for the forward current (along the
\bm{a}_{1}
direction, for example) and reverse current, respectively. The diode efficiency
\eta=(|j_{+}|-|j_{-}|)/(|j_{+}|+|j_{-}|)\neq 0
can then directly estimate the degree of TRS breaking and the size of the chiral gap that is formed. Recent proposals for the detection of the SDE without reaching critical currents may apply here as well
[
130
]
.

The importance of our purely electronic mechanism for superconductivity in tWSe
2
can be directly tested by controlling the Coulomb interaction via the distance from metallic gates. Increasing this distance should enhance the attractive interaction, thereby increasing
T_{c}
. Finally, incorporating phonons could induce attraction in the
s
-wave channel, which is
C_{3z}
-invariant, thereby enhancing the
A^{-}
superconducting instability. Future research directions include a detailed analysis of retardation effects and competing instabilities.

\Delta_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=-\frac{T}{A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}\sum_{i\epsilon}V_{s^{\prime}s,s_{1}s_{2}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})G_{s_{1}}(\bm{k}^{\prime},i\epsilon)G_{s_{2}}(-\bm{k}^{\prime},-i\epsilon)\Delta_{s_{1}s_{2}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(9)

We have introduced the pairing interaction:

\begin{split}V_{s_{1}s_{2},s_{3}s_{4}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})&=\delta_{s_{1}s_{4}}\delta_{s_{2}s_{3}}\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g},\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}s_{3}}\Lambda^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}s_{4}}.\end{split}

(10)

Performing the Matsubara sum we obtain:

I_{s_{1}s_{2}}(\bm{k})=T\sum_{i\epsilon}G_{s_{1}}(\bm{k},i\epsilon)G_{s_{2}}(-\bm{k},-i\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2(\xi_{\bm{k}s_{1}}+\xi_{-\bm{k}s_{2}})}\left[\tanh\frac{\beta\xi_{\bm{k}s_{1}}}{2}+\tanh\frac{\beta\xi_{-\bm{k}s_{2}}}{2}\right],

(11)

and redefining
\Delta_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=\phi_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})/\sqrt{I_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})}
we arrive at the integral eigenvalue equation

\lambda\phi_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sum_{s_{1}s_{2}}K_{ss^{\prime},s_{1}s_{2}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(12)

where

K_{s_{1}s_{2},s_{3}s_{4}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=-\sqrt{I_{s_{1}s_{2}}(\bm{k})}V_{s_{2}s_{1},s_{3}s_{4}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\sqrt{I_{s_{3}s_{4}}(\bm{k}^{\prime})}/A.

(13)

To solve the gap equations we fix the time reversal gauge, by explicitly enforcing the relation between
\ket{\bk\uparrow}
and
\ket{-\bk\downarrow}
, through:

z_{-\bm{g}\uparrow}(-\bm{k})=z^{*}_{\bm{g}\downarrow}(\bm{k}),

(14)

where
z_{\bm{g}\sigma}(\bm{k})
is the Fourier amplitude
z_{\bm{g}\sigma}(\bm{k})=\innerproduct{\bm g}{u_{\bk\sigma}}
. The time reversal symmetry gauge is then imposed building the basis such that for any
\bm{k}
the spin
\downarrow
is computed from the
\uparrow
sector through Eq. (
14
).

The projected screened Coulomb interaction takes the form in Eq. (

). Within the time reversal symmetric gauge we decomposed the form factors as
\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\sigma}=X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}+i\sigma Y^{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}
and we obtained:

V_{s_{1}s_{2},s_{3}s_{4}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=\sum_{\mu\nu=0,z}V_{\mu\nu}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\sigma^{\mu}_{s_{1}s_{4}}\sigma^{\nu}_{s_{2}s_{3}},

(15)

where:

\displaystyle V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}},

(16)

\displaystyle V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=i\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}},

(17)

\displaystyle V_{0z}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=i\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}Y^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}},

(18)

\displaystyle V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=-\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}Y^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}},

(19)

and
X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=(\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\uparrow}+\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\downarrow})/2
,
Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=-i(\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\uparrow}-\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\downarrow})/2
.
As a result of the time reversal symmetry we have:

X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=X^{-\bm{k}^{\prime},-\bm{k}}_{\bm{g}},\quad Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=-Y^{-\bm{k}^{\prime},-\bm{k}}_{\bm{g}},

(20)

which directly implies

\displaystyle V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=V_{00}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}),\quad V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=V_{zz}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}).

(21)

Finally, we have the mixed terms
V_{0z}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})
and
V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})
which satisfies the following conditions:

V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=-V_{0z}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}).

(22)

An additional symmetry which is present also for finite displacement field is
C_{3z}
. The threefold rotational symmetry acts on the Hamiltonian as
C_{3z}H_{\sigma}(\bm{k})C^{-1}_{3z}=H_{\sigma}(C_{3z}\bm{k})
leading to the relation
C_{3z}\ket{u_{\bk\sigma}}=\ket{u_{C_{3z}\bk\sigma}}
with direct implications on the matrix elements
\Lambda^{C_{3z}\bm{k},C_{3z}\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\sigma}=\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{C^{-1}_{3z}\bm{g}\sigma}
. This result combined with the symmetry properties of the screened Coulomb potential
V_{C_{3z}\bm{g},C_{3z}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=V_{\bm{g},\bm{g}^{\prime}}(C_{3z}\bm{q})
leads to the relation:

V_{\mu\nu}(C_{3z}\bm{k},C_{3z}\bm{k}^{\prime})=V_{\mu\nu}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(23)

the scattering vertex is invariant under the threefold rotation symmetry. This property directly implies that the eigenspectrum of the pairing Kernel operator can be classified according to the symmetry properties under
C_{3z}
. The presence of time reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}
protects two-fold degeneracies as
\mathcal{T}
is off-diagonal in subspaces of eigenstates transforming non trivially under
C_{3z}
.

Finally, we discuss the role of the extra symmetries valid only at vanishing displacement field. The first one is inversion
I
implying that
\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\sigma}=\Lambda^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}\sigma}
. Additionally, due to inversion symmetry we find
V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=V_{\bm{g}^{\prime}\bm{g}}(\bm{q})
. Combining the latter identity with time reversal symmetry we find
X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=\real\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\uparrow}
and
Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=\imaginary\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\uparrow}
. Furthermore, we have:

V_{00}(-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime})=\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g},\bm{g}^{\prime}}(-\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{-\bm{g}^{\prime},-\bm{g}}(-\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}}X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}=V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(24)

similarly we find
V_{zz}(-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime})=V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})
. This latter relation implies that, in the symmetric inversion case
V_{00/zz}
cannot mix the triplet and singlet sectors, as expected from the different parity under inversion of the two pairing channels.
Finally, we have

V_{0z}(-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime})=V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(25)

C_{2y}
sends
\uparrow\to\downarrow
and
\bm{k}\to(-k_{x},k_{y})
imposing the relation
\Lambda^{C_{2y}\bm{k},C_{2y}\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}\uparrow}=\Lambda^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{C_{2y}\bm{g}\downarrow}
which implies
X^{C_{2y}\bm{k},C_{2y}\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{C_{2y}\bm{g}}
and
Y^{C_{2y}\bm{k},C_{2y}\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}}=-Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{C_{2y}\bm{g}}
. As a result, we find:

\displaystyle V_{00/zz}(C_{2y}\bm{k},C_{2y}\bm{k}^{\prime})=V_{00/zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}),\quad V_{0z/z0}(C_{2y}\bm{k},C_{2y}\bm{k}^{\prime})=-V_{0z/0z}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(26)

Interestingly, the components
V_{0z}
and
V_{z0}
are odd under
C_{2y}
and must necessarily shows nodes in the mini Brillouin zone. This is also expected since these two components mix triplet and singlet with opposite angular momenta and display vortices.

B.2
Properties of the particle-particle Kernel

In this section we discuss the symmetries of the pairing kernel. We start from the
u_{D}=0
limit where the kernel enjoys the point group
D_{3d}
. Then, we move to the more general case of a finite displacement field where the symmetries reduce to
C_{3z}
and
T

B.2.1
Vanishing displacement field

To start with we notice that in the presence of inversion symmetry
I
the dressed Coulomb potential satisfies the relation
V_{\bm{g},\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=V_{\bm{g}^{\prime},\bm{g}}(\bm{q})
which is directly inherited from
\Pi_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{q})
. Furthermore, in this limit
\xi_{\bm{k}\sigma}=\xi_{\bm{k}}
is spin independent which implies:

I_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=\frac{1}{2\xi_{\bm{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta\xi_{\bm{k}}}{2}=F_{\bm{k}}.

(27)

Without, loss of generality we write:

\lambda\phi_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}}}V_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}\phi_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(28)

where we have explicitly imposed the constraint imposed by the spin conservation. Focusing on the spin
S^{z}=0
sector, we decompose the amplitude
\phi_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{k})
as:

\phi(\bm{k})=\begin{bmatrix}0&\psi(\bm{k})+d^{z}(\bm{k})\\
-\psi(\bm{k})+d^{z}(\bm{k})&0\end{bmatrix},

(29)

with
\psi(\bm{k})
even function of
\bm{k}
and
d^{z}(\bm{k})
odd. For
\psi(\bm{k})
we have:

\begin{split}\lambda\psi(\bm{k})=&-\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}}}\left[V_{\uparrow\downarrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})+V_{\downarrow\uparrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}\psi(\bm{k}^{\prime})\\
&-\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}}}\left[V_{\uparrow\downarrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{\downarrow\uparrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}d^{z}(\bm{k}^{\prime}),\end{split}

(30)

Employing the decomposition (
16
), we find:

\frac{V_{\uparrow\downarrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})+V_{\downarrow\uparrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2}=V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}),\quad\frac{V_{\uparrow\downarrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{\downarrow\uparrow}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2}=V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{0z}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(31)

Using the inversion symmetry of the model
X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}}_{\bm{g}}=X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}
and
Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}}_{\bm{g}}=Y^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}}

\displaystyle V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{z0}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\left[Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}^{\prime}}X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}}-X^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}^{\prime}}Y^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}}\right]

(32)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}Y^{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{-\bm{g}^{\prime}}X^{-\bm{k},-\bm{k}^{\prime}}_{\bm{g}^{\prime}}\left[V_{\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{\bm{g}^{\prime}\bm{g}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]=0.

(33)

As expected from the opposite inversion eigenvalues in table
1
, we find that the singlet
\psi(\bm{k})
and triplet
d^{z}(\bm{k})
are decoupled:

\lambda\psi(\bm{k})=-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}}}\left[V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}\psi(\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(34)

Similarly, in the triplet sector we find:

\lambda d^{z}(\bm{k})=-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}}}\left[V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}d^{z}(\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(35)

We now compute numerically the spectral properties of the kernel
K_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}=-\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}}}\left[V_{00}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})-V_{zz}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\right]\sqrt{F_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}/A
. In the limit of vanishing displacement field
u_{D}=0
, the point group of the continuum theory is
D_{3d}

1

E_{g/u}

S^{z}=0

In the two-dimensional degenerate subspace

(36)

where

(37)

Under

\displaystyle C_{3z}d^{z}_{1}(\bm{k})=d^{z}_{1}(C_{3z}^{-1}\bm{k})=-\frac{d_{1}(\bm{k})}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}d_{2}(\bm{k})}{2},\quad C_{3z}d^{z}_{2}(\bm{k})=d^{z}_{2}(C_{3z}^{-1}\bm{k})=-\frac{d_{2}(\bm{k})}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}d_{1}(\bm{k})}{2}.

\eta_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(42)

To determine the ordering in the two-fold degenerate manifold we perform a variational calculation finding the best BCS wavefunction
\ket{\Psi}
optimizing the energy of the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\rm eff}
.
The energy of the mean-field ansatz is given by:

E_{\Psi}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\sigma}\left[\epsilon_{\bm{k}\sigma}+\delta\epsilon_{\bm{k}\sigma}\right]\langle c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}c_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\langle c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}\Delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k})c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,

(47)

where the second contribution takes into account the condensate energy.
The Bogoliubov de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian of the
S^{z}=0
superconductor in the basis
\Psi_{\bm{k}}=[c_{\bm{k}\uparrow},c_{\bm{k}\downarrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}]^{T}
reads:

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm BdG}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\Psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\xi}_{\bm{k}}&\hat{\Delta}(\bm{k})\\
\hat{\Delta}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})&-\hat{\xi}_{-\bm{k}}\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{\bm{k}},

(48)

where
\hat{\xi}_{\bm{k}}={\rm diag}[\xi_{\bm{k}\uparrow},\xi_{\bm{k}\downarrow}]
and the self-consistent pairing gap introduced in the Hamiltonian (
48
) which gives:

\delta\epsilon_{\bm{k}\sigma}=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}V_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k})\langle c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\sigma^{\prime}}c_{-\bm{k}\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle,\quad\Delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}V_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})\langle c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma}c_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle.

(49)

We initialize the solution from two different ansatz: the first corresponding the nematic state
\bm{\eta}=|\bm{\eta}|(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)
obtained imposing
\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}
. The second describes a chiral state where we allow the coefficients
\bm{\eta}
to develop a complex amplitude. Fig.
S7
shows the order parameter developed at self-consistency in the two different cases. One clearly see that the chiral state is characterized by a full gap in the spectrum while the nematic state has a line of nodes. The nematic phase has been obtained by enforcing that
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
are both real, the self-consistent solution selects a linear combination of the two components with
\varphi=\arctan\eta_{2}/\eta_{1}=\pi n/3
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The chiral superconductor is characterized by the particle-hole symmetry
\mathcal{C}=i\tau^{y}\sigma^{y}\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}(\bm{k})\mathcal{C}^{-1}=-H_{\rm BdG}(-\bm{k})
, with
\tau^{x}
in the particle-hole basis consistent with a D superconductor of the Altland-Zirnbauer class
[
118
,
119
]
. This can be readily understood noticing that the BdG Hamiltonain decouples in two blocks
[c_{\bm{k}\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}]^{T}
and
[c_{\bm{k}\downarrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}\uparrow}]^{T}
. Within each subspace, the effectively spinless Hamiltonian reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}(\bm{k})=\sum_{s=\pm}\frac{\tau^{0}+s\tau^{z}}{2}\left[\xi_{\bm{k}}\sigma^{z}+\real d^{z}(\bm{k})\sigma^{x}-\imaginary d^{z}(\bm{k})\sigma^{y}\right]

(50)

leading to a pseudospin vector
\bm{n}(\bm{k})=[\real d^{z}(\bm{k}),-\imaginary d^{z}(\bm{k}),\xi_{\bm{k}}]/\sqrt{\xi^{2}_{\bm{k}}+|d^{z}(\bm{k})|^{2}}

At a finite displacement field, the pairing function
\Delta(\bm{k})
does not have a well defined inversion symmetry as it is characterized by the mixing of spin
S^{z}=0
(intervalley) triplet and singlet channels. The resulting BdG Hamiltonian reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm BdG}(\bm{k})=\sum_{s=\pm}\frac{\tau^{0}+s\tau^{z}}{2}h_{s}(\bm{k}),\quad h_{\pm}(\bm{k})=\xi_{\bm{k}\uparrow/\downarrow}+\left[d^{z}(\bm{k})\mp\psi(\bm{k})\right]\sigma^{+}+h.c..

(51)

The winding of the pseudospin vector
\bm{n}_{+}(\bm{k})
is displayed in Fig.
S8

\displaystyle P_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\rm mBZ}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|d^{z}(\bm{k})|^{2},\quad P_{s}=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\rm mBZ}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|\psi(\bm{k})|^{2},\quad\mathcal{N}=\int_{\rm mBZ}\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}\left[|d^{z}(\bm{k})|^{2}+|\psi(\bm{k})|^{2}\right].

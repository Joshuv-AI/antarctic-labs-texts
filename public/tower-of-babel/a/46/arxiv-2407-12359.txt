Beyond Energy: Teleporting Current, Charge, and More
Kazuki Ikeda ( {uCJK} 池田一毅)

Abstract
As an homage to Quantum Energy Teleportation , we generalize the idea to arbitrary physical observables, not limited to energy, and prove a rigorous upper bound on the activated (“teleported”) quantity. The essence of this protocol is a quantum feedback control with respect to the entangled ground state of a quantum many-body system. To demonstrate the concept, we explore a (1+1)-dimensional chiral Dirac system and execute the protocol for the electric current and charge. One of the most significant results is the creation of long-range correlations across the system after applying control operations only to one local site. Consequently but surprisingly, the induced charge susceptibility fully reconstructs the phase diagram, despite the model initially having no charge. Moreover, we find an activation of novel chiral dynamics induced by feedback control operations, which can be experimentally confirmed using trapped ions and neutral atoms.

Chiral Maxwell Demon
.— Quantum feedback control protocols can be regarded as a modern, practical embodiment of Maxwell’s demon

The chirality of a Dirac fermion in the model (
1
) can be measured by the projection operator:

P(b)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-(-1)^{b}\gamma_{5}\right),

(2)

where
b=0
for the right-handed mode and
b=1
for the left-handed mode. The chiral Maxwell Demon (CMD) is characterized by its ability to measure the chirality of fermions and execute a control protocol. Conducting the chirality measurements, the CMD records the outcomes as a bit-string
b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{k}
.

In the protocol, the CMD involves spacially separated Alice (detector) and Bob (controller). Let
\ket{gs}
be the ground state of the system and
\rho_{0}=\ket{gs}\bra{gs}
the corresponding density matrix. Alice measures her local qubit of
\ket{gs}
by
P_{n_{A}}(b)
and obtains either
b=0
or
1
. Repeating the measurements, Alice creates a mixed state
\rho_{\text{post}}=\sum_{b}P_{n_{A}}(b)\rho_{0}P_{n_{A}}(b)
, and therefore excites her local energy:
\langle\Delta E_{n_{A}}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\text{post}}H_{n_{A}}]-\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{0}H_{n_{A}}]>0
.

Receiving Alice’s outcome
b
, Bob applies a control operation
U_{n_{B}}(b)=e^{-b\gamma^{1}_{n_{B}}}
to his qubit. Then the CMD statistically obtains the feedback-controlled (FC) state:

\rho_{\text{FC}}=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}U_{n_{B}}(b)P_{n_{A}}(b)\rho_{0}P_{n_{A}}(b)U^{\dagger}_{n_{B}}(b).

(3)

The CMD is fully consistent with information thermodynamics. Let
X
be the system after the measurement and
X^{\prime}
be the evolution of
X
after feedback. The second law of thermodynamics is equivalent to
[
35
,
42
]

S(X^{\prime})-S(X)\geq I(X^{\prime}:A)-I(X:A),

(4)

where
S(\cdot)
is the von Neumann entropy of
\cdot\in\{X,X^{\prime}\}
and
I(\cdot:A)
is the mutual information between
\cdot
and Alice’s memory
A
. In our case, both L.H.S and R.H.S of (
4
) are negative, which confirms that
X
receives a feedback from Alice. This clarifies the relation between the Demon and the QET protocol. See also
SM
.

The upper bound of teleportation
.—
Applying
\rho_{\text{FC}}
to an arbitrary observable
\mathcal{O}
would activate some amount, which we call
teleportation
of
\mathcal{O}
following QET. Here we provide a general concise answer (
8
) to
Question
2
: the upper bound of the teleported amount, which has remained an open question, particularly in the context of QET.

We define the difference between the two expectation values of
\mathcal{O}
evaluated by two density operators
\rho,\sigma
:

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\mathcal{O}]-\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma\mathcal{O}].

(5)

Then we note that the upper bound of the trace norm of
\Delta\mathcal{O}
is related to the fidelity in such a way that

\|\Delta\mathcal{O}\|\leq\|\rho-\sigma\|\|\mathcal{O}\|\leq 2\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)}\|\mathcal{O}\|,

(6)

where
F
is the fidelity between two density matrices
\rho,\sigma
:

F(\rho,\sigma)=\left(\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\right)^{2}.

(7)

Since
|\langle\Delta\mathcal{O}\rangle|=|\mathrm{Tr}[\Delta\mathcal{O}]|\leq\|\Delta\mathcal{O}\|
, the following inequality holds and gives the upper bound of teleportaiton:

|\langle\Delta\mathcal{O}\rangle|\leq 2\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)}\|\mathcal{O}\|.

(8)

To observe waves without the effect of collision between Alice and Bob’s waves, we need to take a larger lattice having a long quantum coherence time.

The main contributions of our work to QET, and more broadly to quantum feedback and control in quantum many-body systems, can be summarized as follows:

I.4
Comment on the Maxwell Demon and QET

The ground state of the minimal model is written as

\ket{gs}=\alpha\ket{00}+\beta\ket{11}.

(27)

Before the measurement, her memory and the system is completely irrelevant so the mutual information is
I(X:A)=0
.

Suppose Alice performs measurement using
P_{0}(b)=\frac{1}{2}(1+(-1)^{b})X_{0})
to the first qubit. We write
\ket{gs}
in
X
-basis as follows:

\ket{gs}=\ket{+}\otimes\frac{\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}+\ket{-}\otimes\frac{\alpha\ket{0}-\beta\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}

(28)

Therefore she can obtain
\ket{+}
or
\ket{-}
with probability
p_{\pm}=1/2
. The entropy is

S(X)=-p_{+}\log(p_{+})-p_{-}\log(p_{-})=\log 2.

(29)

If there is no error in the measurement, the mutual information is
I(X:A)=S(X)=\log 2
.

Now let Alice make a feedback to the system, where Bob applies
U_{1}(b)=\cos\phi I+(-1)^{b}i\sin\phi Y
to his qubit. After her measurement, the sate collapse either
\ket{+}
or
\ket{-}
. Suppose she observes
b=0
. Then the state is projected to

\ket{\psi}=\ket{+}\otimes\frac{\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}

(30)

with probability 1 and the feedback changes the state into

\ket{\psi^{\prime}}=\ket{+}\otimes U_{1}(b=0)\left(\frac{\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

(31)

with probability 1. Therefore the entropy of the system after the feedback is 0 and the mutual information is
I(X:A)=0
.

As a result, the consumption of entropy
\Delta S(X)=-\log 2
before and after the feedback is equal to the change in the mutual information
\Delta I(X:A)=-\log 2
. In general,
\Delta S(X)\geq\Delta I(X:A)
holds, which is called the second law of thermodynamics
[
35
,
42
]
. In a thermal system with a heat bath at temperature
T
, work
W=-k_{B}T\Delta I(X:A)
would be extracted.

One can also discuss this process with a quantum circuit where Alice’s measurement is postponed to the end of the circuit (see Fig.1 in
[
9
]
). In such a case, the control operation is performed first and the initial state is mapped into

\ket{gs}\to\ket{\psi^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{+}\otimes U_{1}(b=0)\left(\frac{\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{-}\otimes U_{1}(b=1)\left(\frac{\alpha\ket{0}-\beta\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(32)

Again Alice observes
\ket{\pm}
with equal probability
1/2
and the system evolves in the same way.

I.5
Upper bound on teleportation: Entropy vs Fidelity

Here we give a general remark on the upper bound of teleportation (
8
). So far, the known best upper bound was given by the entanglement entropy between Alice’s subsystem and its complement
S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho_{0})

[
69
]
in such a way that

\frac{E^{2}_{n_{B}}}{4\|H_{n_{B}}\|^{2}}\leq S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho).

(33)

However, in general, the upper bound of
S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho_{0})
is determined by the area of
A
(the number of qubits in
A
), the dimension of the system and the energy gap between the lowest energy and the 1st excited energy. Therefore
S_{A\bar{A}}
can be larger than 1, depending on the systems and the parameters. In particular, when a system undergoes a quantum phase transition, it diverges at a critical point and becomes very large around it. Of course, the energy is finite around such a critical point, so the teleported energy is much lower than the entanglement entropy. In fact, the theoretically rigorous upper bound given by the fidelity
1-F(\rho_{0},\rho_{\text{FC}})
(
8
) guarantees that the ratio
E_{n_{B}}/4\|H_{n_{B}}\|
does not exceed 1. A closer look at Figs.
2
and
3
clarifies that
1-F(\rho_{0},\rho_{\text{FC}})
can be greater or smaller than
S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho_{0})
, depending on the points.

Here we obtain the state-of-the-art upper bound of the teleported quantity. For a general observable
\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}
at Bob’s site, we can still apply the discussion in
[
69
]
:

\frac{|\langle\Delta\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\rangle-\langle\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\rangle|^{2}}{4\|\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\|^{2}}\leq S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho_{0}),

(34)

where
\langle\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\rangle=\sum_{b}p_{A}(b)\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{0}U^{\dagger}_{n_{B}}(b)\mathcal{O}U_{n_{B}}(b)]
with the probability
p_{A}(b)
that
b
is observed by Alice. When the sign of
\langle\Delta\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\rangle
is opposite to the sign of
\langle\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\rangle
, we obtain a new upper bound:

\frac{|\langle\Delta\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\rangle|^{2}}{4\|\mathcal{O}_{n_{B}}\|^{2}}\leq\min\{S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho_{0}),1-F(\rho_{0},\rho_{\text{FC}})\}.

(35)

In particular, when
O_{n_{B}}
is Bob’s local Hamiltonian, the previous upper bound (
33
) is now updated as follows:

\frac{E^{2}_{n_{B}}}{4\|H_{n_{B}}\|^{2}}\leq\min\{S_{A\bar{A}}(\rho_{0}),1-F(\rho_{0},\rho_{\text{FC}})\}.

(36)

General extension of the teleportation to arbitrary coordinates

As outlined in the main text, we provide an elaboration on the scenario where Alice and Bob execute the protocol on physical qubits or more general combinations of qubits. Specifically, they perform operations on neighboring qubits that consist of a Dirac fermion, which inherently possesses two components. For the sake of simplicity and manageable scale in the primary analysis, the operations were confined to only one component.

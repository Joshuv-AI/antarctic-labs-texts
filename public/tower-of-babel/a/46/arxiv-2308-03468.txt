The mass density contrast in perturbed Friedman-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker cosmologies
Edward Malec

Abstract
We analyze the evolution of the mass density contrast in spherical perturbations of flat Friedman-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker cosmologies. Both dark matter and dark energy are included. In the absence of dark energy the evolution equation coincides with that obtained by Bonnor within the “Newtonian cosmology”.



I
Introduction

We shall analyze the evolution of perturbations of flat FLRW spacetimes using the
1+3

The original aim of this paper was just to find the general relativistic version of the well known result of Bonnor

III
The Friedman type solution

Assuming that matter consists of dust and imposing in addition homogeneity on slices of constant time
t
,

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}+\varrho_{\Lambda}=\frac{3}{8\pi}H^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{dH}{dt}=4\pi\varrho_{0}

The extrinsic curvatures of this solution are equal to the Hubble parametr

IV
Evolution of small spherical inhomogeneities in a FLRW universe

We assume that the background (Friedman-type) universe is dotted by isolated, locally isotropic mass density perturbations
\delta\varrho
, so that the mass density is split into the background part
\varrho_{0}
and the perturbation
\delta\varrho

\varrho=\varrho_{0}+\delta\varrho

p=c^{2}_{s}\delta\varrho

N
tends to 1 and the spatial part of the metric is approaching the background metric
a^{2}\left(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\right)

Pelykh

The areal velocity
U=\partial_{0}R/N=R(\mathrm{trK}-\mathrm{K^{r}_{r}})/2

U=H(t)R+\delta_{U},

(18)

where
H(t)
is the Hubble constant at the time

We need initial data — for the areal velocity

14

IV.1
The extrinsic curvature

The first part of the calculation is actually exact — we do not need the assumption of small perturbations in order to get the trace of the extrinsic curvature

Evolution of the mass density contrast

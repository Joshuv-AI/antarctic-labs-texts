Entropy of a unitary operator on l 2 ​ ( ℕ 0 ) l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
Andrey Chernyshev

Abstract
In this article we continue to study the concept of entropy introduced in [ 4 ] , [ 15 ] - [ 17 ] . We calculate entropy for a wider class of finite-dimensional operators in comparison with [ 15 ] . We also approximate the entropy of a unitary operator on l 2 ​ ( ℕ 0 ) l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0}) by the entropy of finite-dimensional operators. Finally we calculate the entropy of several operators on l 2 ​ ( ℕ 0 ) l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0}) .

Introduction and main definitions

In classical dynamics, chaos is associated with such concepts as non-integrability, positivity of the Lyapunov exponents, symbolic dynamics, positivity of topologic and metric entropy. There are many attempts to propose analogous definitions for quantum chaos
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
]
but here the situation is not so clear. In this article we measure quantum chaos by using a certain quantities similar to the metric entropy. Since quantum evolution is unitary, we mainly discuss the entropy of the unitary operator.

Let
{\cal X}
be a non-empty set, and let
{\cal B}
be a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets
X\subset{\cal X}
. Consider the measure space
({\cal X},{\cal B},\mu)
, where
\mu
is a probability measure,
\mu({\cal X})=1
. We say that
\chi=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{J}\}
is a partition of
{\cal X}
if

\displaystyle Y_{j}\in{\cal B},\qquad\mu\Big({\cal X}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}Y_{j}\Big)=0,\qquad\mu(Y_{j}\cap Y_{k})=0,\qquad j\neq k.

We say that
\kappa=\{X_{0},\ldots,X_{K}\}
is a subpartition of
\chi=\{Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{J}\}
if for any
k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}
there exist
j\in\{0,\ldots,J\}
such that
\mu(X_{k}\setminus Y_{j})=0.
We also say that
\kappa
is a refinement of the partition
\chi
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
be an endomorphism of the space
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B},\mu)
. This means that for any
X\in\mathcal{B}
the set
F^{-1}(X)
(the full preimage of
X
) lies in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mu(X)=\mu(F^{-1}(X)).
Invertible endomorphisms are called automorphisms. Let
\textrm{End}(\mathcal{X})
denote the semigroup of all endomorphisms of the space
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B},\mu)
. There are two standart constructions associated with an arbitrary
F\in\textrm{End}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
.

(1). Each
F
generates an isometry (a unitary operator if
F
is an automorphism)
U_{F}
on the space
L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
(the Koopman operator):

L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)\ni f\mapsto U_{F}f=f\circ F,\qquad U_{F}=\textrm{Koop}(F).

(2). For any
F\in End(\mathcal{X},\mu)
one can calculate its metric entropy (also known as the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy)
h(F)
.

Let us recall how metric entropy can be constructed for an arbitrary endomorphism. Let
\mathcal{S}_{n,K}
be the set of all mappings
\{0,\ldots,n\}\to\{0,\ldots,K\}
. For any partition
\chi=\{X_{0},\ldots,X_{K}\}
and for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,K}
we define a measurable set
\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}\subset\mathcal{X}
by

\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}=F^{-n}(X_{\sigma(n)})\cap\ldots\cap F^{-1}(X_{\sigma(1)})\cap X_{\sigma(0)}.

Define

h_{F}(\chi,n+1)=-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,K}}\mu(\mathbf{X}_{\sigma})\log\mu(\mathbf{X}_{\sigma}).

It is well known (see
[
10
]
) that
h_{F}
is subadditive as a function of the second argument:
h_{F}(\chi,n+m)\leq h_{F}(\chi,n)+h_{F}(\chi,m).
Therefore there exists the limit

h_{F}(\chi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}h_{F}(\chi,n).

It was proved in
[
10
]
that the function
h_{F}(\chi)
does not decrease as the partition
\chi
is refined. The metric entropy of the endomorphism
F
is defined by

h(F)=\sup_{\chi}h_{F}(\chi).

In
[
4
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
the question was raised about defining a function
\mathfrak{h}
on the semigroup
\textrm{Iso}(L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu))
of isometric operators such that the diagram

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&\textrm{End}(\mathcal{X},\mu)&\\
h\swarrow&&\searrow\textrm{Koop}\\
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{h}}}{{\longleftarrow}}&\textrm{Iso}(L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu))\end{array}

is commutative.

An entropy of a unitary operator
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
was constructed in
[
4
,
16
,
17
]
by using the concept of the
\mu
-norm.

Consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathcal{X},\mu)
with the scalar product and norm

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}f\overline{g}\,d\mu,\qquad\|f\|=\sqrt{\langle f,g\rangle}.

Let
{\cal L}({\cal H})
denote the semigroup of bounded linear operators on
{\cal H}
. For any
W\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
let
\|W\|
be its
L^{2}
operator norm:

\|W\|=\sup_{\|f\|=1}\|Wf\|.

For any
X\in\mathcal{B}
, consider the orthogonal projection

\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H},\qquad\mathcal{H}\ni f\mapsto\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X}f=\mathbf{1}_{X}\cdot f,

where
\mathbf{1}_{X}
is the indicator of the set
X
.

Let
W
be a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
. For any partition
\chi=\{Y_{0},...,Y_{J}\}
of the set
\mathcal{X}
, we define

\mathcal{M}_{\chi}(W)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mu(Y_{j})\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{Y_{j}}\|^{2}.

(1.1)

We define the
\mu
-norm of the operator
W
by the equation:

\|W\|_{\mu}=\inf_{\chi}\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\chi}(W)}.

(1.2)

The group of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\chi=\{X_{0},\ldots,X_{K}\}
be a partition of
\mathcal{X}
, and let
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,K}
. In
[
16
]
the following definition of entropy
\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U)
of an operator
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
was proposed. Consider

\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}=\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X_{\sigma(n)}}U\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X_{\sigma(n-1)}}U\ldots U\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X_{\sigma(0)}}.

(1.3)

By definition, put

\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U,\chi,n)=-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,K}}\|\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{\mu}\log\|\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{\mu},

\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U,\chi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U,\chi,n),\qquad\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U)=\sup_{\chi}\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U,\chi).

(1.4)

If the limit (
1.4
) exists, then
\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U)
is called the entropy of the operator
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
. It is known
[
16
]
that
\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U_{F})=h(F)
for
U_{F}=\textrm{Koop}(F)
where F is arbitary authomorphism of
({\cal X},{\cal B},\mu)
. Unfortunately it is unknown whether the limit (
1.4
) always exist. Is the function
\mathfrak{h}_{1}(U,\chi)
monotone as the partition is refined? Another definition of the entropy
\mathfrak{h}(U)
of an operator
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
was proposed in
[
4
,
15
]
(see also Section
4
) such that the equation
\mathfrak{h}(U_{F})=h(F)
holds for any Koopman operator
U_{F}=\textrm{Koop}(F)
. The construction of the entropy
\mathfrak{h}(U)
is based on the fact that in several important situations there is a natural way to associate with any
U\in{\cal U}_{\mu}({\cal H})
a bistochastic operator
{\bf B}=b(U)
. Then it is possible to propose a definition of the entropy
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
ideologically close to constructions in
[
6
,
8
]
. The disadvantage of the second definition of the entropy
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
is that the mapping
U\mapsto b(U)
has been defined only for some special classes of unitary operators.

In general, the entropy means how much information is lost when applying a unitary operator. There is another interpretation in finite-dimensional case
{\cal X}<\infty
. In this case it is possible to describe a subgroup of operators with zero entropy explicitly. Thus this entropy in a certain sense means the distance to this subgroup.

In the literature there exist several attempts to extend the concept of the measure entropy to quantum systems, see
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
12
]
and many others. In
[
1
]
several mutual relations between
these approaches are given. In
[
6
,
8
]
a construction for the measure entropy is proposed
for doubly stochastic (bistochastic) operators on various spaces of functions on a measure
space.

In
[
15
]
entropy
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
of the finite-dimensional unitary operator
U\in{\cal U}(\mathbb{C}^{J})
is calculated by

{\mathfrak{h}}(U)=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{J-1}\alpha_{k}{\bf B}_{jk}\log{\bf B}_{jk},\qquad{\bf B}=b(U),

(1.5)

where

\alpha_{k}=\big({\cal P}_{\text{Ker}({\bf B}-I)}\mu\big)_{k},\qquad\mu=(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{J-1}).

The equation (
1.5
) motivates the problem of approximation of the entropy
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
,
U\in{\cal U}_{\mu}({\cal H})
by the entropy
{\mathfrak{h}}(U_{J})
defined by (
1.5
) of a finite-dimensional approximation
U_{J}\in{\cal L}(\mathbb{C}^{J})
.

In this paper, we propose some process of finite-dimensional approximation of the unitary operator
U\in{\cal U}_{\mu}({\cal H})
. In the other words, we construct some finite-dimensional sequence
\{U_{J}\}_{J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
which converges (in some sense) to
U\in{\cal U}_{\mu}({\cal H})
. We also establish some connection between the sequence
\{{\mathfrak{h}}(U_{J})\}_{J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
.

Consider
{\cal X}=\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
. Let
\mu_{j}>0
be the measure of
j
-th point,
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j}=1.
Then
{\cal H}=l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)
with the scalar product and norm

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j}x_{j}\overline{y}_{j},\qquad\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}.

(1.6)

We also consider the space
l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
with the norm

\|x\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|x_{j}|,\qquad x\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0}).

Let
(W_{jk})_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the representation of the operator
W
in the basis
\{e_{j}=(0,\ldots,\mu_{j}^{-1/2},\ldots)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Below, we establish the following facts.

(1). The
\mu
-norm of an operator
W\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
is calculated in Section
2
by

\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{k}|W_{kj}|^{2}.

In Section
2
we also prove that for arbitrary partitions
\chi=\{X_{n}\}_{n=0}^{p}
and
\kappa=\{Y_{k}\}_{k=0}^{q}
of the set
{\cal X}

\|W\|_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\|\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{n}}W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{Y_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mu}.

(2). In Section
3
we introduce semigroups of contraction
{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}({\cal H})
and semibistochastic
{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
operators by

{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}({\cal H})=\{U\in{\cal L}({\cal H})\colon\forall\;x\in{\cal H},\quad\|Ux\|\leq\|x\|\},

{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})=\bigg\{{\bf B}=({\bf B}_{jk})_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in{\cal L}(l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0}))\colon{\bf B}_{jk}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{jk}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{jk}\leq 1\bigg\}.

We establish that
{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is a closed semigroup. We use
\mu
-norm to construct the mapping
b\colon{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}({\cal H})\to{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. Suppose
U\in{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}({\cal H})
. Using the mapping
b
, we get
{\bf B}=b(U)\in{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. We consider the following sequences

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\qquad a_{\alpha}^{k}(U)=\sum_{j_{k-1},\ldots,j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j_{k-1}j_{k-2}}{\bf B}_{j_{k-2}j_{k-3}}\ldots{\bf B}_{j_{0}\alpha},

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad u_{\alpha}^{n}(U)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{\alpha}^{k}(U),\qquad u_{\alpha}(U)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}u_{\alpha}^{n}(U).

We prove that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the sequence of operators

{\bf B}_{n,\alpha}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{\alpha}^{n-1-j}(U){\bf B}^{j}.

strongly converges to
u_{\alpha}(U){\cal P}_{{\text{Ker}}({\bf B}-I)}
, where
{\cal P}_{{\text{Ker}}({\bf B}-I)}
is the projection on
{\text{Ker}}({\bf B}-I)
, i.e

\forall x\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\bf B}_{n,\alpha}x-u_{\alpha}(U){\cal P}_{{\text{Ker}}({\bf B}-I)}x\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}=0.

(3). Section
4
introduces the definition of entropy of a unitary operator
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
. We establish that for any
F\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)\colon

{\mathfrak{h}}(U)=h(F)={\mathfrak{h}}_{1}(U)
.

(4). In Section
5
we prove that the function
{\mathfrak{h}}(U,\chi)
is monotone as the partition is refined. More precisely, let
\kappa
be a subpartition of the partition
\chi
. Then
\mathfrak{h}(U,\chi,n)\leq\mathfrak{h}(U,\kappa,n)
.

(5). In Section
6
we study the finite-dimensional case. Consider the following scalar product and norm on
\mathbb{C}^{J}\colon

\langle x,y\rangle_{J}=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\mu_{j}x_{j}\overline{y_{j}},\qquad\|x\|_{J}=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_{J}}.

Suppose
U\in{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{J})
,
\mu=(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{J-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{J}
,
e=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{C}^{J}
. We prove that its entropy is calculated by

{\mathfrak{h}}(U)=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{J-1}\big({\cal P}^{T}_{\text{Ker}({\bf B}-I)}e\big)_{j}{\bf B}_{jk}\big({\cal P}_{\text{Ker}({\bf B}-I)}\mu\big)_{k}\log{\bf B}_{jk},\quad b(U)={\bf B},

(1.7)

(6). In Section

{\mathfrak{h}}(U)

(i) Suppose

and
{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}\in{\cal U}(\mathbb{C}^{J})
are diagonal operators. Then
{\mathfrak{h}}({\cal D}_{1}U{\cal D}_{2})={\mathfrak{h}}(U)
.

(ii) Suppose

,

. Then
{\mathfrak{h}}(U_{F}^{-1}UU_{F})={\mathfrak{h}}(U)
. Informally speaking, this means that measure preserving coordinate changes on
{\cal X}
preserve
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
.

(iii) We establish the specific operators with zero entropy.

(7). In Section

{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
. Suppose
U\in{\cal U}_{\mu}({\cal H})
. Consider the following operators
p_{J}\colon l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)\to\mathbb{C}^{J}
,

defined by

\displaystyle p_{J}\colon(x_{0},\ldots,x_{J-1},x_{J},\ldots)\mapsto(x_{0},\ldots,x_{J-1}),

\displaystyle q_{J}\colon(x_{0},\ldots,x_{J-1})\mapsto(x_{0},\ldots,x_{J-1},0,\ldots).

The sequence
\{U_{J}\in{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{J})\}_{J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is constructed by the unitary operator

so that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{J}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{J}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{J}}

\{U_{J}\}_{J\in\mathbb{C}^{J}}
strongly converges to

. This sequence generates the sequence
\{{\mathfrak{h}}(U)\}_{J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

{\mathfrak{h}}(U_{J})

(8). In Section

we define the number
{\mathfrak{H}}(U)
by

{\mathfrak{H}}(U)=\lim_{J\to\infty}{\mathfrak{h}}(U_{J}).

Then we establish some connection between the numbers
{\mathfrak{h}}(U)
and
{\mathfrak{H}}(U)
. In particular, we obtain the following statements

(i) Let

be a diagonal operator. Then

(ii) Let

(iii) We also consider a strict contraction operator

. We prove that for this operator:

.

(vi) The equation

2.2
Computation of the
\mu
-norm

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
W\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. Then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\{j\}}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{k}|W_{kj}|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

\big(W\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}x\big)_{k}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}W_{k\alpha}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\{j\}}\big)_{\alpha\beta}\;x_{\beta}=W_{kj}x_{j}.

Thus we have

\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\{j\}}x\|^{2}=\langle W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\{j\}}x,\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\{j\}}x\rangle=|x_{j}|^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{k}|W_{kj}|^{2}\leq\|x\|^{2}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{k}|W_{kj}|^{2}.

To conclude the proof, it remains to note that equality is achieved on the following vector:
\mathcal{H}\ni x_{*}=(0,\ldots,0,\mu_{j}^{-1/2},0,\ldots),

\|x_{*}\|=1.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
W\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{k}|W_{kj}|^{2},\qquad\|W\|_{\mu}\leq\|W\|<\infty.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
\chi=\{Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
be an arbitrary partition of
{\cal X}
. Then

\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\chi}(W)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mu(Y_{j})\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{Y_{j}}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mu(Y_{j})\|W\|^{2}=\|W\|^{2}<\infty.

Let
\chi_{n}=\{\{0\},\{1\},\ldots,\{n\},X_{n+1}\}
be the partition of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
X_{n+1}=\{n+1,n+2,\ldots\}
. Using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}=\inf_{\chi}\mathcal{M}_{\chi}(W)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{M}_{\chi_{n}}(W).

Thus we have

\mathcal{M}_{\chi_{n}}(W)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mu_{j}\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{\{j\}}\|^{2}+M_{n+1}\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X_{n+1}}\|^{2},

where
M_{n+1}=\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}
. Since for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:
\|W\widehat{\mathbf{1}}_{X_{n+1}}\|\leq\|W\|<\infty
and
M_{n+1}\to 0

(n\to\infty)
, it follows that (
2.1
). ∎

2.3
Koopman operator

Let the map
F\colon\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
be an authomorphism (
F\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)
).
Consider the Koopman operator
U_{F}\colon x\mapsto x\circ F
on
l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)
, where
x\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0},\mu)
. Then

(U_{F})_{jk}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{j}}}\delta_{F(j)k}.

(2.2)

In the other words, the Koopman operator
U_{F}
permutes points with the same measures. Consider sets
A_{j},\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\alpha_{j}\in(0,1),\quad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\alpha_{j}\leq 1,\qquad A_{j}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\colon\mu_{k}=\alpha_{j}\}.

It is clear that
A_{j}\cap A_{k}=\emptyset
for
j\neq k
and
\#A_{j}<\infty
. Let
F
be an automorphism. Then
F^{-1}(A_{j})=A_{j}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\colon

F^{-n}(A_{j})=A_{j}
. Thus we have for all
j\in A_{n}

\mu_{j}=\mu_{F^{-1}(j)}.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
U_{F}
be a Koopman operator. Consider the ordered collection of the diagonal operators
{\bf G}=\{\widehat{g_{0}},\ldots,\widehat{g_{n}}\}
. Then

\|\widehat{g_{0}}U_{F}\widehat{g_{1}}\ldots U_{F}\widehat{g_{n}}\|_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|(g_{0})_{j}|^{2}|(g_{1})_{F(j)}|^{2}\ldots|(g_{n})_{F^{n}(j)}|^{2}\mu_{F^{n}(j)}.

Proof.

Let us calculate the elements of the matrix
\widehat{g_{0}}U_{F}\widehat{g_{1}}\ldots U_{F}\widehat{g_{n}}
,

\begin{split}\big(\widehat{g_{0}}U_{F}\widehat{g_{1}}\ldots U_{F}\widehat{g_{n}}\big)_{jk}&=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\widehat{g_{0}})_{j}(U_{F})_{j\alpha_{1}}(\widehat{g_{1}})_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots(U_{F})_{\alpha_{n-1}k}(\widehat{g_{n}})_{k}\\
&=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\widehat{g_{0}})_{j}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\alpha_{1}}}{\mu_{j}}}\delta_{F(j)\alpha_{1}}(\widehat{g_{1}})_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{\alpha_{n-1}}}}\delta_{F(\alpha_{n-1})k}(\widehat{g_{n}})_{k}\\
&=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{j}}}(\widehat{g_{0}})_{j}(\widehat{g_{1}})_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots(\widehat{g_{n}})_{k}\delta_{F(j)\alpha_{1}}\ldots\delta_{F(\alpha_{n-1})k}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{F^{n}(j)}}{\mu_{j}}}(g_{0})_{j}(g_{1})_{F(j)}\ldots(g_{n})_{F^{n}(j)}\delta_{F^{n}(j)k}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\widehat{g_{0}}U_{F}\widehat{g_{1}}\ldots U_{F}\widehat{g_{n}}\|_{\mu}^{2}&=\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j}\frac{\mu_{F^{n}(j)}}{\mu_{j}}|(g_{0})_{j}|^{2}|(g_{1})_{F(j)}|^{2}\ldots|(g_{n})_{F^{n}(j)}|^{2}\delta_{F^{n}(j)k}\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|(g_{0})_{j}|^{2}|(g_{1})_{F(j)}|^{2}\ldots|(g_{n})_{F^{n}(j)}|^{2}\mu_{F^{n}(j)}.\end{split}

∎

2.4
Additivity

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
W\in{\cal L}({\cal H})
and let
\chi=\{X_{n}\}_{n=0}^{p}
and
\kappa=\{Y_{k}\}_{k=0}^{q}
be an arbitrary partitions of the set
{\cal X}
. Then

\|W\|_{\mu}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\|\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{n}}W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{Y_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mu}.

Proof.

Let us calculate the entries of the matrix
\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{n}}W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{Y_{k}}

\big(\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{n}}W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{Y_{k}}\big)_{jm}={\bf 1}_{X_{n}}(j)W_{jm}{\bf 1}_{Y_{k}}(m),\quad j,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Using Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\|\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{n}}W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{Y_{k}}\|_{\mu}^{2}&=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j}{\bf 1}_{X_{n}}(j)|W_{jm}|^{2}{\bf 1}_{Y_{k}}(m)\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j}\sum_{n=0}^{p}{\bf 1}_{X_{n}}(j)|W_{jm}|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{q}{\bf 1}_{Y_{k}}(m)\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j}|W_{jm}|^{2}=\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}.\end{split}

∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Lemma
2.5
implies the left and right additivity,

\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{n}}W\|^{2}_{\mu},\qquad\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mu}.

Proof.

We obtain the first or the second equality in the corollary if we set
\kappa=\{\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
or
\chi=\{\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
, respectively, in Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Note that the left additivity for arbitrary probability space
({\cal X},{\cal B},\mu)
was
established in
[
17
]
, where the following inequality was also obtained:

\|W\|^{2}_{\mu}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|W\widehat{{\bf 1}}_{X_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mu}.

It is not clear whether the right additivity holds for any probability space
({\cal X},\mu)
.

3.2
Definition and properties of
{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
{\bf B}\colon l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\to l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is a semibistochastic operator (
{\bf B}\in{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
) if

\begin{split}&(1).\qquad{\bf B}_{jk}\geq 0,\quad j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
&(2).\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{jk}\leq 1,\qquad\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{jk}\leq 1.\end{split}

(3.5)

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
{\bf B}\in{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. Then
\|{\bf B}\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}\leq 1.

Proof.

Let
x\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. Consider

\|{\bf B}x\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\big|({\bf B}x)_{j}\big|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}|x_{\alpha}|.

Since

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}|x_{\alpha}|\leq\|x\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}<\infty,

if follows that

\|{\bf B}x\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}|x_{\alpha}|=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}|x_{\alpha}|\leq\|x\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}.

∎

.

Let

b(U)\in{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

Proof.

Note that

Using Lemma
8.4

Lemma 3.5
.

{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is a closed semigroup.

Proof.

Let
A,B\in{\mathcal{SB}}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. Consider

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(AB)_{jk}&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}A_{j\alpha}B_{\alpha k}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}A_{j\alpha}B_{\alpha k}\\
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(AB)_{jk}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}A_{j\alpha}B_{\alpha k}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}B_{\alpha k}\leq 1.\end{split}

Analogously, we can prove that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(AB)_{kj}\leq 1
. Suppose that
B_{n}\to B
, i.e
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|B_{n}-B\|_{\l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}=0.
Therefore, we have the weak convergence, i.e. for all
f\in\big(\l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\big)^{*}\cong l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}f\big((B_{n}-B)x\big)=0.

(3.6)

Consider the following linear functional
f\in\big(\l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\big)^{*}

y=(1,1,\ldots)\in l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}_{0}),\quad f_{y}(x)=(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}x_{k}.

Using (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle\big|f_{y}\big((B_{n}-B)e_{\alpha}\big)\big|=\Big|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}({\bf B}_{n})_{j\alpha}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}\Big|<\varepsilon,

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\leq-\varepsilon+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}({\bf B}_{n})_{j\alpha}<\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}<\varepsilon+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}({\bf B}_{n})_{j\alpha}\leq\varepsilon+1.

Then
0\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bf B}_{j\alpha}\leq 1.
Consider the sequence
\{B_{n}^{T}\colon(B_{n}^{T})_{jk}=(B_{n})_{kj}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Suppose

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|B_{n}^{T}-B^{\prime}\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}=0.

Consider the lineal functional
f_{k}\in\big(\l^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\big)^{*}
defined by

Hence, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{k}(B^{T}_{n}e_{j})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(B^{T}_{n})_{kj}=(B^{\prime})_{kj}.

On the other hand, we have

Therefore
B^{\prime}=B^{T}
and

5
Monotonicity of
{\mathfrak{h}}

Before proving the main assertion of this section, we prove two auxiliary lemmas. Consider a mapping
p\colon\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}\to\mathbb{Z}_{k}
such that

p(j)=j\quad\text{if}\quad j\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}\quad\text{and}\quad p(k+1)=k.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k}
,
A_{\sigma}=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k+1}\;|\;p\circ\lambda=\sigma\}
. Then

\mathcal{S}_{n,k+1}=\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k}}A_{\sigma}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in A_{\sigma^{\prime}}\cap A_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\text{ for }\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma^{\prime\prime}.

Then
\sigma^{\prime}=p\circ\lambda=\sigma^{\prime\prime}.
Therefore,
A_{\sigma^{\prime}}\cap A_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}=\varnothing.

Let us prove the following inclusion:

\mathcal{S}_{n,k+1}\subset\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k}}A_{\sigma}.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k+1}
,
M=\{m\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\;|\;\lambda(m)=k+1\}.
By definition, we put
\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n,k}
so that

\sigma(m)=\begin{cases}k,\quad m\in M\\
\lambda(m),\qquad m\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\setminus M.\end{cases}

Evidently, for any
m\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\colon

p\circ\lambda(m)=\sigma(m)
. The inverse inclusion

\mathcal{S}_{n,k+1}\supset\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n,k}}A_{\sigma}

holds by the definition of the set
A_{\sigma}
. Lemma is proved.
∎

Let
\kappa=\{Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{k+1}\}
be a subpartition of the partition

.

\sum_{\lambda\in A_{\sigma}}\mathcal{I}_{U}(\mathbf{G}_{\lambda}(\kappa))=\mathcal{I}_{U}(\mathbf{G}_{\sigma}(\chi)).

Let
M=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\colon\sigma(m)\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\}
. Then

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{U}(\mathbf{G}_{\sigma}(\chi))&=\sum_{j_{0},\ldots,j_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j_{n}}|U_{j_{n}j_{n-1}}\ldots U_{j_{1}j_{0}}|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X_{\sigma(n)}}(j_{n})\ldots\mathbf{1}_{X_{\sigma(0)}}(j_{0})\\
&=\sum_{j_{0},\ldots,j_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mu_{j_{n}}|U_{j_{n}j_{n-1}}\ldots U_{j_{1}j_{0}}|^{2}\prod_{m\in M}\mathbf{1}_{Y_{\sigma(m)}}(j_{m})\prod_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{J}\setminus M}\mathbf{1}_{X_{k}}(j_{m})\\

6

Let
\mathbb{C}^{J}
be Hilbert space with the scalar product and norm

\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\mu_{j}x_{j}\overline{y_{j}},\qquad\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}.

By definition, we put

{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{J})=\{U\in{\cal L}(\mathbb{C}^{J})\colon\ \|Ux\|\leq\|x\|\}.

(6.1)

(6.2)

Theorem 6.1

Consider the following partition of

{\cal I}_{U}({\bf G}_{\sigma})={\cal I}_{U}({\bf G}_{\sigma}(\chi_{\odot}))={\bf B}_{\sigma(n)\sigma(n-1)}\ldots{\bf B}_{\sigma(1)\sigma(0)}\mu_{\sigma(0)},\qquad\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n,J-1}.

Using Lemma

and (

\begin{split}-{\mathfrak{h}}(U,\chi_{\odot},n)&=\sum_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n,J-1}}{\cal I}_{U}({\bf G}_{\sigma})\log{\cal I}_{U}({\bf G}_{\sigma})\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{0},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{J-1}{\bf B}_{j_{n}j_{n-1}}\ldots{\bf B}_{j_{1}j_{0}}\mu_{j_{0}}\log{\bf B}_{j_{k}j_{k-1}}+\\

\begin{split}S_{k}&=\sum_{j_{0},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{J-1}{\bf B}_{j_{n}j_{n-1}}\ldots{\bf B}_{j_{k+1}j_{k}}{\bf B}_{j_{k}j_{k-1}}{\bf B}_{j_{k-1}j_{k-2}}\ldots{\bf B}_{j_{1}j_{0}}\mu_{j_{0}}\log{\bf B}_{j_{k}j_{k-1}}\\

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}^{J-1}{\bf B}_{\alpha\beta}\Bigg(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{\alpha}^{n-k}(U){\bf B}^{k-1}\Bigg)_{\beta\gamma}\mu_{\gamma}\log{\bf B}_{\alpha\beta}\\

By Lemma
3.8
, so that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}^{J-1}u_{\alpha}(U){\bf B}_{\alpha\beta}\big({\cal P}_{{\text{Ker}}({\bf B}-I)}\big)_{\beta\gamma}\mu_{\gamma}\log{\bf B}_{\alpha\beta}\\

By using (

\begin{split}u_{\alpha}(U)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{k-1},\ldots,j_{0}=0}^{J-1}{\bf B}_{j_{k-1}j_{k-2}}{\bf B}_{j_{k-2}j_{k-3}}\ldots{\bf B}_{j_{0}\alpha}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\big({\bf B}^{k-1}\big)_{j\alpha}\\

∎

7

7.1
Multiplication by diagonal operator

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose
U\in{\mathcal{CU}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{J})
,
{\cal D}_{1},{\cal D}_{2}\in{\cal U}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{J})
are diagonal operators. Then

{\mathfrak{h}}({\cal D}_{1}U{\cal D}_{2})={\mathfrak{h}}(U).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{1}=\text{diag}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{J-1}),\qquad|a_{j}|=1,\quad j\in\{0,\ldots,J-1\},

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{2}=\text{diag}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{J-1}),\qquad|b_{j}|=1,\quad j\in\{0,\ldots,J-1\}.

Thus we have

({\cal D}_{1}U{\cal D}_{2})_{jk}=a_{j}U_{jk}b_{k},\qquad b({\cal D}_{1}U{\cal D}_{2})=\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{k}}|a_{j}|^{2}|U_{jk}|^{2}|b_{k}|^{2}=\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{k}}|U_{jk}|^{2}=b(U).

The result is

j\in\{0,\ldots,J-1\},\quad u_{j}({\cal D}_{1}U{\cal D}_{2})=u_{j}(U),\qquad v_{j}({\cal D}_{1}U{\cal D}_{2})=v_{j}(U).

This completes the proof of Lemma
7.1
.
∎

7.2

Lemma 7.2
.

. Let

Using (

), we get

\begin{split}\big(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F}\big)_{jk}&=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}=0}^{J-1}(U^{-1}_{F})_{j\alpha_{1}}U_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}(U_{F})_{\alpha_{2}k}\\
&=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}=0}^{J-1}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{F^{-1}(j)}}{\mu_{j}}}\delta_{F^{-1}(j)\alpha_{1}}U_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{\alpha_{2}}}}\delta_{F(\alpha_{2})k}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{j}}\frac{\mu_{F^{-1}(j)}}{\mu_{F^{-1}(k)}}}U_{F^{-1}(j)F^{-1}(k)},\qquad j,k\in\{0,\ldots,J-1\}.\end{split}

If we combine this with definition of the mapping

(see Section
3.1
). For any

\big(b(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F})\big)_{jk}=\frac{\mu_{F^{-1}(j)}}{\mu_{F^{-1}(k)}}|U_{F^{-1}(j)F^{-1}(k)}|^{2}={\bf B}_{F^{-1}(j)F^{-1}(k)}.

Using equations (

) and (

Since

Therefore

(7.2)

Analogously

\begin{split}\big(b^{j}(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F})\big)_{st}&=({\bf B}^{j})_{F^{-1}(s)F^{-1}(t)}\\

&=\big(b^{j}(U)\big)_{F^{-1}(s)F^{-1}(t)},\qquad s,t\in\{0,\ldots,J-1\}.\end{split}

Thus we have for any

\begin{split}v_{s}(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F})&=\big({\cal P}_{\text{Ker}(b(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F})-I)}E_{\mu}\big)_{s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b^{j}(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F})E_{\mu}\bigg)_{s}\\

&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\big(b^{j}(U)\big)_{F^{-1}(s)k}\mu_{F(k)}.\end{split}

\begin{split}v_{s}(U^{-1}_{F}UU_{F})&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\big(b^{j}(U)\big)_{F^{-1}(s)k}\mu_{k}\\

(7.3)

Combining Theorem

\begin{split}{\mathfrak{h}}(U_{F}^{-1}UU_{F})&=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{J-1}u_{j}(U_{F}^{-1}UU_{F})b_{jk}(U_{F}^{-1}UU_{F})v_{k}(U_{F}^{-1}UU_{F})\log b_{jk}(U_{F}^{-1}UU_{F})\\

7.3

On Cubical Sets of Quivers and Digraphs
Rolando Jimenez, Vladimir Vershinin, Yuri Muranov

Abstract
. The singular cubical homology theory for the category of quivers or digraphs can be constructed similarly to the classical singular homology theory for topological spaces. The case of digraphs and quivers differs from the topological case due to the possibility of using a large number of non-isomorphic line digraphs that correspond to the unit interval in algebraic topology. In this paper we introduce several different notions of quiver realizations of a cubical set and we describe relations between them. We also define various singular cubical homology theories on quivers and digraphs. Moreover, using quiver realizations of cubical sets we define a collection of path homology theories on the category of cubical sets and we describe their properties.



Keywords:
box category, cubical set, cubical set of a quiver, homology of digraph, homology
of quiver, singular cubical homology, topological realization of cubical set, digraph realization of
cubical set, quiver realization of cubical set, path homology, homology of cubical set

]
,
[

]
,
[

Every cubical set

In the present paper, we introduce various notions of a quiver realization of a cubical set and various singular
cubical sets of quivers and digraphs, and we describe relations between introduces objects. Using this approach we
construct several singular and path homology theories on the categories of cubical sets, quivers and digraphs.

In Section

In Section

Preliminaries

Recall the notions of the
box category
and
cubical set

[
2
,
5
,
15
]
. For
n\geq 1

\mathbb{I}^{n}
be the
n

\mathbb{I}=\{0,1\}
, and let
\mathbb{I}^{0}=\{0\}
be the one-element set.

Definition 2.1
.

The
box category

\Box
has objects
\mathbb{I}^{n}
for
n\geq 0
and morphisms of this category can be obtained by composition of
face inclusions

\delta_{i}^{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{I}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{I}^{n}\ \text{given by}\ \delta_{i}^{\alpha}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},\alpha,x_{i},\dots x_{n-1}),\ \alpha\in\mathbb{I}

where
(1\leq i\leq n)\&(n\geq 2)
and
\delta_{1}^{\alpha}(0)=\alpha
for
n=1

and
projections

\sigma_{i}\colon\mathbb{I}^{n}\to\mathbb{I}^{n-1}\ \text{given by}\ \sigma_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots x_{n})

where
n\geq 2
,
1\leq i\leq n
, and
\sigma_{1}(x_{1})=0
for
n=0
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Any morphism in
\Box
has a unique
expression as a composition

\delta_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots\delta_{i_{k}}^{\alpha_{k}}\sigma_{j_{1}}\dots\sigma_{j_{l}}\ \text{where}\ i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k},\ j_{1}\leq\dots\leq j_{l}.

There are the following relations between morphisms of the category
\Box
:

\delta_{j}^{\beta}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}=\delta_{i}^{\alpha}\delta_{j-1}^{\beta}\ \ \text{for}\ i<j,

\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j+1}\ \ \text{for}\ i\leq j,

\sigma_{j}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}\sigma_{j-1}&\text{for}\ i<j\\
\delta_{i-1}^{\alpha}\sigma_{j}&\text{for}\ i>j\\
\operatorname{Id}&\text{for}\ i=j.\end{cases}

Definition 2.3
.

A
cubical
set
is a functor
K\colon\Box^{op}\to\operatorname{\bf Set}

and a morphism of cubical sets is a natural transformation of functors. We denote by
\mathbb{Cub}
the category of cubical sets.

It follows from Definition
2.3
that a cubical set
K
is defined by the family of sets

\{K_{n}=K(\mathbb{I}_{n})\,|\,n\geq 0\}
, the face maps

(1)

\partial^{\alpha}_{i}=K\left(\delta^{\alpha}_{i}\right)\colon K_{n}\to K_{n-1}\ (\alpha\in\mathbb{I},n\geq 1,i=1,\dots,n)

and the degeneracy maps

(2)

\varepsilon_{i}=K(\sigma_{i})\colon K_{n-1}\to K_{n}\ (n\geq 1,\ i=1,\dots,n)

which satisfy the following relations

(3)

\begin{matrix}\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\partial_{j}^{\beta}=\partial_{j-1}^{\beta}\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\ \ \text{for}\ i<j,\\
\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{j+1}\varepsilon_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ i\leq j,\\
\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\varepsilon_{j}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{j-1}\partial_{i}^{\alpha}&\text{for}\ i<j\\
\varepsilon_{j}\partial_{i-1}^{\alpha}&\text{for}\ i>j\\
\operatorname{Id}&\text{for}\ i=j.\end{cases}\\
\end{matrix}

We note that morphisms of cubical sets are maps
of sets
K_{n}\to K_{n}^{\prime}
for all
n
which commute with face and degeneracy maps.

Let
T=[0,1]
be the unit interval and
T^{n}
be the standard
n
-cube for
n\geq 1
and
T^{0}=\{0\}
. Then the maps

\delta_{i}^{\alpha}\colon T^{n-1}\to T^{n}

and

\sigma_{i}\colon T^{n}\to T^{n-1}

are defined similarly to definition
2.1
and they satisfy the equalities of
Proposition
2.2
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
topological realization

|K|_{\mathbb{Top}}
of a cubical set

K\colon\Box^{op}\to\mathbb{Set}
is the quotient topological space

|K|_{\mathbb{Top}}=\{\coprod_{n}K_{n}\times T^{n}\}/\sim

where the set
K_{n}
is equipped with the discrete topology,
T^{n}
is the
n
-cube and the equivalence relation is generated by

(\partial^{\alpha}_{i}(x),v))\sim(x,\delta_{i}^{\alpha}(v))\ \text{for}\ x\in K_{n},v\in T^{n-1}

and

(\varepsilon_{i}(y),w)\sim(y,\sigma_{i}(w))\ \text{for}\ y\in K_{n-1},w\in T^{n}.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
2
, Pr. 10.1.12]
The topological space

|K|_{\mathbb{Top}}
has a natural structure of a
CW
-complex having one
n
-cell for each non-degenerate
n
-cube.
The topological realization
|\ |_{\mathbb{Top}}
is the functor from the category
\mathbb{Cub}
of cubical sets to the category
\mathbb{CW}
of

CW
-complexes and cell-maps.

Now we recall the standard notions of the graph theory which we need in the paper (see,

9
]
).

Definition 2.6
.

A directed graph (digraph)

G=\left(V_{G},E_{G}\right)
consists of a set
V_{G}
of
vertices
and a
subset
E_{G}\subset\{V_{G}\times V_{G}\setminus\operatorname{diagonal}\}
of ordered
pairs
(v,w)
of vertices which are called
arrows
. The ordered pair of vertices
(v,w)
is denoted by

v\to w
. The vertex
v=\mbox{orig }(v\rightarrow w)
is called
the
origin of the arrow
and the vertex
w=\mbox{end}(v\rightarrow w)

is called the
end of the arrow
.

For
v,w\in V_{G}
, we write
v\,\overrightarrow{=}w
if either
v=w
or
v\rightarrow w
. A digraph
H
is a
subgraph
of a digraph
G
and we write
H\subset G
if
V_{H}\subset V_{G}
and
E_{H}\subset E_{G}
. A
directed path

in a digraph
G
is a sequence of vertices
a_{i}\in V_{G}\,(0\leq i\leq n)
such that
(a_{i}\to a_{i+1})\in E_{G}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
. The number
n
of arrows

a_{i}\to a_{i+1}
fitting into the path is called the
length
of the path. The vertex
a_{0}
is
the origin of the path
and the vertex
a_{n}

is
the end of the path
.

Definition 2.7
.

A
digraph map

f\colon G\to H
is given by a map

f\colon V_{G}\rightarrow V_{H}
between the sets of vertices such that
v\overrightarrow{=}w
in
G
implies
f\left(v\right)

\overrightarrow{=}f\left(w\right)
in
H
. We call the map
f

non-degenerate on an arrow

(v\to w)\in E_{G}
if
(f(v)\to f(w))\in E_{H}
.

All digraphs with digraph maps form the
category of digraphs
,
which is denoted by
\mathbb{D}
.

Definition 2.8
.

For two digraphs
G
and
H
define the
Box product

\Pi=G\Box H
as the digraph with the set of
vertices
V_{\Pi}=V_{G}\times V_{H}
and the set of arrows
E_{\Pi}
given
by the rule

(x,y)\rightarrow(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\ \ \text{if }x=x^{\prime}\text{
and }y\rightarrow y^{\prime}\text{, or }x\rightarrow x^{\prime}\text{ and }y=y^{\prime},

where
x,x^{\prime}\in V_{G}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in V_{H}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Two digraph maps
f,g\colon G\rightarrow H
are

called
homotopic
if there exists a line
digraph
I_{n}
with
n\geq 1
and a digraph map

F\colon G\Box I_{n}\rightarrow H

such that

F|_{G\Box\{0\}}=f\ \ \text{and}\ \ F|_{G\Box\{n\}}=g

where we identify
G\Box\{0\}
and
G\Box\{n\}
with
G
in a natural way. In this case we shall write
f\simeq g
. The map
F
is called a

homotopy
between
f
and
g
. In the case
n=1
we refer to the map
F
as an
one-step homotopy
.

Definition 2.10
.

Digraphs
G
and
H
are called
homotopy equivalent

if there exist maps

f:G\rightarrow H,\ \ \ g:H\rightarrow G

such that

f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{id}_{H},\ \ \ \ \ g\circ f\simeq\operatorname{id}_{G}.

In this case we shall write
H\simeq G
and the maps
f
and
g

are called
homotopy inverses
of each other.

All digraphs with homotopic classes of digraph maps form the
homotopy category of digraphs
which is denoted by
\mathbb{HoD}

[
9
]
.

3.
Category of quivers and
\Box
-categories of digraphs

We define a category of quivers
\mathbf{Q}
in such a way that the category of digraphs
\mathbf{D}
will be a subcategory of
\mathbf{Q}
(see, for example,
[
3
,
18
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

A
quiver

Q=(V,E,s,t)
consists of a set of vertices

V
, a set of edges (arrows)
E
, and two maps
s,t\colon E\rightarrow V
. The vertex
s(a)\in V

is called the start vertex of
a
, and the vertex
t(a)
is called the end vertex of
a
.

The map

f:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
of quivers is given by a pair of maps

f_{V}\colon V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
and

f_{E}\colon E\rightarrow E^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime}
such that for every
a\in E
only one of the following two conditions:

i)
f_{E}(a)\in E^{\prime}
and
f_{V}(s(a))=s^{\prime}(f_{E}(a))
,
f_{V}(t(a))=t^{\prime}(f_{E}(a))
,

ii)
f_{E}(a)=v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
and
f_{V}(s(a))=f_{V}(t(a))=v^{\prime}

is satisfied. We denote by
\mathbf{Q}
the category of quivers and their maps.

Every digraph
G=(V,E)
defines a quiver
Q=(V,E,s,t)
, where
s(v\to w)=v,t(v\to w)=w
for
(v\to w)\in E
.
Every map
f\colon G\to G^{\prime}
of digraphs defines the map
f=(f_{V},f_{E})
of the corresponding quivers as follows.
The map
f_{V}
coincides with the map
f\colon V\to V^{\prime}
on the set of quiver vertices and

f_{E}(v\to w)=\begin{cases}f(v)\to f(w)&\text{for}\ f(v)\neq f(w),\\
f(v)&\text{for}\ f(v)=f(w).\\
\end{cases}

Hence, we have the inclusion of categories
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{Q}
.

Let us fix
n\geq 0
and denote by
I_{n}
a digraph with the set of
vertices
V=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and such that for
i=0,1,\dots n-1
, there is exactly one arrow
i\to i+1
or
i+1\to i

and there are no other arrows. Such digraph is called a
line
digraph and a
direct line
digraph if additionally
all arrows are of the form of
i\to i+1
. We denote the line digraph
0\to 1
by
I
. Define a category
\mathbb{I}
of
line digraphs and digraph maps. It has a subcategory
\mathbb{DI}
of directed line digraphs.

Let us fix a line digraph
I_{k}(k\geq 1)
. For
n\geq 0
, we define an
n
-cube digraph

I^{n}_{k}
as
follows. For
n=0
we put
I^{0}_{k}=\{0\}
— one-vertex digraph and for
n\geq 1
,

I^{n}_{k}
is given by

I^{n}_{k}=\underbrace{I_{k}\Box I\Box I_{k}\Box\dots\Box I_{k}}_{n-times}.

Note, that for
n\geq 0
, every digraph morphism
\tau\colon I_{k}\to I_{m}
induces a digraph morphism

\tau^{n}\colon I_{k}^{n}\to I_{m}^{n}
.

For
n\geq 2,\ 1\leq i\leq n
, and
\alpha\in\{0,k\}\subset V_{I_{k}}
, we define the
following inclusion
\delta_{i}^{\alpha}\colon I^{n-1}_{k}\rightarrow I^{n}_{k}
of the face of digraphs given on the set of vertices by

(1)

\begin{matrix}\delta_{i}^{\alpha}(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})=(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},\alpha,c_{i},\dots,c_{n})\ \text{for}\ \ c_{j}\in V_{I_{k}}.\end{matrix}

We define also
\delta_{1}^{\alpha}\colon I_{k}^{0}=\{0\}\to I_{k}^{1}=I_{k}
by
\delta_{1}^{\alpha}(0)=\left(\alpha\right)
.

For
n\geq 2
and
1\leq i\leq n
, let us consider the natural projection

\sigma_{i}\colon I^{n}_{k}\to I^{n-1}_{k}
on the
i
-face
I^{n-1}_{k}
given on the set of vertices by

(2)

\sigma_{i}(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})=(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},c_{i+1},\dots,c_{n})\ \text{for}\ \ c_{j}\in V_{I_{k}},

and let

\sigma_{1}\colon I^{1}_{k}\to\{0\}
be given on the set of vertices by
\sigma_{1}(c_{1})=0
for
c_{1}\in V_{I_{k}}
.
For
k\geq 1
denote by
\mathbb{D}_{k}
the category consisting of
n
-cubes
I^{n}_{k}
and maps obtained by compositions of the maps
\delta_{i}^{\alpha},\sigma_{i}
defined above.

Proposition 3.2
.

For fixed
k\geq 1
and every line digraph
I_{k}
, the category
\mathbb{D}_{k}
,
with objects
I^{n}_{k}
and morphisms generated by
\sigma_{i}
,
\delta_{i}^{\alpha}
, is isomorphic to the
\Box
-category.

Since we have the inclusion of categories
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{Q}
, we can consider every digraph as a quiver
if this not leads to confusion. In order to introduce the notion of homotopy in
\mathbf{Q}
we reformulate Definition
2.9
for this category.

Definition 3.3
.

For a line digraph
I_{k}\,(k\geq 0)
and a quiver
Q=(V,E,s,t)
we define the
Box product

\Pi=I_{k}\Box\,Q=(V_{\Pi},E_{\Pi},s_{\Pi},t_{\Pi})
as the quiver with the set of
vertices
V_{\Pi}=V_{I_{k}}\times V_{H}
, the set of arrows

E_{\Pi}=\{(v,a)|v\in V_{I_{k}},a\in E\}\cup\{[(v,w)\to(v^{\prime},w)]\,|\,(v\to v^{\prime})\in E_{I_{k}},w\in V\},

and the maps
s_{\Pi},t_{\Pi}
given
by the rule

\begin{matrix}s_{\Pi}(v,a)=(v,s(a)),\ t_{\Pi}(v,a)=(v,t(a)),\\
s_{\Pi}[(v,w)\to(v^{\prime},w)]=(v,w),\ t_{\Pi}[(v,w)\to(v^{\prime},w)]=(v^{\prime},w).\end{matrix}

where
v,v^{\prime}\in V_{I_{k}},a\in E,(v\to v^{\prime})\in E_{I_{k}},w\in V
.

Definition 3.4
.

Two quiver maps
f,g\colon Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
are

called
homotopic
if there exists a line
digraph
I_{k}
and a quiver map

F\colon I_{k}\Box Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}

such that

F|_{\{0\}\Box Q}=f\ \ \text{and}\ \ F|_{\{k\}\Box Q}=g

where we identify
\{0\}\Box Q
and
\{k\}\Box Q
with
Q
in a natural way. In this case we shall write
f\simeq g
. The map
F
is called a

homotopy
between
f
and
g
. In the case
n=1
we call the map
F
a
one-step homotopy
.

Definition 3.5
.

A quiver
Q=(V,E,s,t)
is
simple
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

i)
s(a)\neq t(a)
for every arrow
a\in E
,

ii) ordered pairs of vertices
(s(a),t(a))
and
(s(b),t(b))
are different for every two different arrows
a,b\in E
.

Remark 3.6
.

It is clear that every simple quiver
Q=(V,E,s,t)
defines a digraph
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
where
V_{G}=V
,
E_{G}=\{s(a)\to t(a)\,|\,a\in E\}
.

4.
Singular cubical set of a quiver and the quiver realization of a cubical set

Now, for every quiver
Q
and every line digraph
I_{k}
, we define a cubical set which functorially depends of
Q
and
I_{k}
.
More precisely, every line digraph
I_{k}
defines a cubical set
S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)
consisting in dimension

n\geq 0
of singular digraph cubes which are given by quiver maps
\varphi\colon I_{k}^{n}\to Q
. Any morphism of quivers
f\colon Q\to Q^{\prime}
induces
the morphism
f_{\Box}=f\varphi\colon S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)\to S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q^{\prime})
, and any morphism
\tau\colon I_{k}\to I_{m}
induces the morphism

\tau^{\Box}=\varphi\tau\colon S_{I_{m}}^{\Box}(Q)\to S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)
.
Hence singular cubical sets of quivers give a collection of covariant functors from the category
\bf Q
to the category
\mathbb{Cub}
and a collection of contravariant functors
from the category
\mathbb{I}
to the category
\mathbb{Cub}
. We describe relations between obtained functors and give applications of these results to the category of digraphs
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{Q}
.

Remark 4.1
.

For a topological space
X
, the singular cubical set
S^{\Box}(X)
is given by singular topological cubes in
X
(see
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
). The definition of singular cubical set
S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)
of a quiver
Q
given below is similar to the definition
S^{\Box}(X)
of a topological space
X
. Recall that for a topological space
X
the topological realization
\left|S^{\Box}(X)\right|_{\mathbb{Top}}
and
X
are week homotopy equivalent.

Fix a line digraph
I_{k}\ (k\geq 1)
. For
n\geq 0
,
a singular
n
-cube in a quiver
Q
is a quiver map
\phi\colon I^{n}_{k}\to Q
. For any quiver
Q
denote by
S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)=\left\{\left[S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)\right]_{n}\left|\,\right.n\geq 0\right\}
the set of all singular cubes
\phi\colon I^{n}_{k}\to Q\,(n\geq 0)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
Q
be a quiver. For
n\geq 1
define the face morphism

\partial_{i}^{\alpha}\colon\left[S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)\right]_{n}\to\left[S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)\right]_{n-1}
by
\partial_{i}^{\alpha}(\phi)=\phi\delta^{\alpha}_{i}
and define the degeneracy morphism
\varepsilon_{i}\colon\left[S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)\right]_{n-1}\to\left[S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)\right]_{n}
by

\varepsilon(\phi)=\phi\sigma_{i}
. Thus
S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}(Q)
is a cubical set.
In such a way, for any line digraph
I_{k}
, we obtain a singular cubical functor

S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}\colon\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Cub}.

The restriction of this functor to the subcategory of digraphs
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{Q}
provides
a functor
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{Cub}
which we continue to denote
S_{I_{k}}^{\Box}
.

Remark 4.3
.

In the case of directed line digraphs singular cubes were introduced in
[
7
]
and
[
14
]
for the construction of singular cubical homology groups and cell singular cubical homology groups of digraphs.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any quiver

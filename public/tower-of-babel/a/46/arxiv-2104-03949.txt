Stabilization by transport noise and enhanced dissipation in the Kraichnan model
Benjamin Gess, Ivan Yaroslavtsev

Abstract
Zusammenfassung. Stabilization and sufficient conditions for mixing by stochastic transport are shown. More precisely, given a second order linear operator with possibly unstable eigenvalues on a smooth compact Riemannian manifold, it is shown that the inclusion of transport noise can imply global asymptotic stability. Moreover, it is shown that an arbitrary large exponential rate of convergence can be reached, implying enhanced dissipation. The sufficient conditions are shown to be satisfied by the so-called Kraichnan model for stochastic transport of passive scalars in turbulent fluids. In addition, an example is given showing that it can be sufficient to force four modes in order to induce stabilization.

1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{M}
be a
d
-dimensional
C^{\infty}
-smooth connected compact Riemannian manifold. In this work, we consider the following linear second order stochastic PDE with transport noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{M}
,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}+A\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k},\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\circ\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}&=(Tu_{t}+Cu_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
u_{0}&=u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M}),\end{cases}

where

(W^{k})_{k\geq 1}
are independent real-valued Brownian motions,
A\geq 0
,
C\in\mathbb{R}
,

(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are divergence-free
C^{\infty}
-smooth vector fields and

(1.2)

Tf:=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\Bigl\langle\chi_{m},\nabla\bigl\langle\chi_{m},\nabla f\bigr\rangle\Bigr\rangle,\;\;\;f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),

is a second-order strictly elliptic operator with
(\chi_{m})_{m=1}^{n}
divergence-free, smooth tangent vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
.
A model case is given by
T=\Delta
, and
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Stochastic equations of the form (
1.1
) describe the evolution of a passive scalar in a turbulent fluid, see, for example
[
64
,
31
]
. In particular, for a certain choice of
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
equation (
1.1
) corresponds to the
Kraichnan model
from turbulence theory, see Section
5
below. Another example of (
1.1
) arises in magnetohydrodynamics, for example studied by Baxendale and Rozovskii in
[
13
]
, see Example
6.2
below. In the latter case, the corresponding sum over
k
in (
1.1
) is finite.

The motivation of the present work is twofold: The first aim is to prove the possibility of stabilization by noise for stochastic PDE with transport noise. The second aim is the derivation of sufficient conditions on the coefficients
\sigma_{k}
in (
1.1
) to imply a.s. exponential mixing for (
1.1
). We will next describe each of these aspects in some more detail.

The possibility of stabilizing linear SDE by noise was analyzed by Arnold, Crauel, and Wihstutz in
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also
[
3
]
). More precisely, let
n\geq 2
and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a self-adjoint linear operator with a negative trace,
\text{tr}\;T<0
. Then, if
T
has unstable eigenvalues, the solution to

\,\mathrm{d}x_{t}=Tx_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t
blows up exponentially in time. The results of
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
imply that the inclusion of linear noise can induce global asymptotic stability, in the sense that there exist skew-symmetric matrices
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
A>0
such that the solution
x_{t}
to

(1.3)

\,\mathrm{d}x_{t}+A\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}D_{k}x_{t}\circ dW^{k}_{t}=Tx_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,\;\;\;t\geq 0,

a.s. exponentially decays with an exponential rate arbitrarily close to
\text{tr}\;T
for
A
large enough. The natural question of the possibility of an extension to infinite dimensions was posed by Capiński in the late 80s. Choosing
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
T=\Delta+C
, in (
1.1
)
we have, informally,
\text{tr}\;T=-\infty
. Consequently, one may conjecture that a choice of diffusion coefficients
\sigma_{k}
in (
1.1
) is possible, implying an exponential rate of decay for the solution to (
1.1
) with exponential rate arbitrarily close to
-\infty
. Following Flandoli and Luo
[
37
]
this problem is called
Capiński’s conjecture
.

The first result of this work demonstrates that, if the sum of the coefficients
\sigma_{k}
in (
1.1
) is smooth, this conjecture can be deduced by combining results by Dolgopyat, Kaloshin and Koralov
[
27
]
with a recent argument by Bedrossian, Blumenthal and Punshon-Smith
[
17
]
and a stopping time argument (see Section
4
below). With an eye on the Kraichnan model considered below, we include the case of irregular coefficients
\sigma_{k}
, thereby identifying and including the optimal range of regularities for which these arguments can be used (cf. also p.4 below). For irregular coefficients the results of
[
27
]
are not directly applicable, but have to be carefully modified and generalized.

More precisely, we deduce that the following sufficient conditions on the coefficients
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, inspired by the ones used in
[
27
,
14
]
, imply stabilization by noise. In an informal form, the assumptions read

(a)

The coefficients
(\sigma_{k}(x_{1}),\sigma_{k}(x_{2}))_{k\geq 1}
are strictly elliptic for any off-diagonal point
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{M}^{2}
. This can be relaxed to the Hörmander condition if the sequence
(\sigma_{k})
is finite, see Section
6
below.

(b)

The coefficients
(\tilde{\sigma}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of the normalized tangent flow, see (
3.1
) below, are strictly elliptic.

(c)

Summability and Hölder continuity of the series
\sum_{k\geq 1}\sigma_{k}
and their first and second derivatives.

The rigorous form of the conditions

(a)–(c)
are given in full detail in assumptions
(A)–(C)
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfy the conditions
(A)–(C)
and let
T
be a second-order strictly elliptic operator defined by (
1.2
). Then there exist
A_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{0}>0
such that for any
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0}]
and
A\geq A_{0}
there is
D^{A,\gamma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\sup_{A\geq A_{0}}\mathbb{E}(D^{A,\gamma})^{p}<\infty
for any
1\leq p<\tfrac{9d\gamma_{0}}{4\gamma}
such that the solution
(u_{t})
of (
1.1
) satisfies a.s.

(1.4)

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leq A^{2}D^{A,\gamma}e^{(C-\gamma A^{2})t}\|u\|_{L^{2}},\;\;\;t>0.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
4
below.

As addressed in detail below, we verify the conditions
(A)–(C)
in two particular examples: First, for the
Kraichnan model
arising in the analysis of turbulent fluids, for which the series in (
1.1
) is infinite, causing limited regularity of the stochastic flow. Second, for a two-dimensional example due to Baxendale and Rozovskii. In this example, only four modes have to be forced, that is
\sigma_{k}\equiv 0
for
k\geq 5
, to induce stabilization by noise.

We note the relation to the enhancement of diffusive mixing studied in the deterministic setting by Constantin, Kiselev, Ryzhik, and Zlatož in
[
25
]
, where necessary and sufficient conditions on the coefficient
\sigma
are derived, implying that the solution
u_{t}
of the PDE

(1.5)

\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}=\Delta u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-A\langle\sigma,\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\;\;\;t\geq 0,

converges to zero in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})

for any fixed
t>0
as
A\to\infty
.

We next address the second main aspect of this work: stochastic and turbulent mixing of passive scalars. Let
u_{0}:\mathcal{M}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be the density of a solute in a solvent with dynamics on
\mathcal{M}
governed by a divergence-free vector field
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathcal{M}\to T\mathcal{M}
. Then, the density
u_{t}
of the solute at time
t\geq 0
is given as the solution to the transport equation

(1.6)

\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}+\langle\sigma(t),\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

The concept of mixing measures how “well-spread” the density
u_{t}
is.
Mixing of passive scalars can be understood as weak-
L^{2}
convergence of the density
u_{t}
to its mean
\bar{u}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}u_{0}
. For the relation to notions of mixing in dynamical systems theory see e.g.
[
80
]
. This weak-
L^{2}
convergence is equivalent to convergence of
u_{t}
to
\bar{u}
in the Sobolev space
H^{-s}
for any
s>0
(see e.g.
[
60
,
80
]
). In the deterministic setting, the analysis of necessary and sufficient conditions on the drift field
\sigma
implying mixing and rates of mixing for (
1.6
) has attained considerable interest in the literature, see, for example
Alberti, Crippa, and Mazzucato
[
1
]
and the references therein.

More recently, the case of a solvent in a stochastic fluid has been considered by
Bedrossian, Blumenthal, and Punshon-Smith in
[
17
,
16
,
15
]
. In this case, the flow
\sigma
in (
1.6
) is chosen as the solution to the two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equation with random excitation of small modes, and it is shown that for a.e. realization (
1.6
) becomes mixing. With an eye on mixing in turbulent fluids, in the present work, the solution to the stochastic Navier-Stokes equation is replaced by the so-called Kraichnan model.
In a turbulent regime, based on the statistics provided by the Kolmogorov turbulence theory (see, for example,
[
64
, Section 4]
), the Kraichnan model (see
[
50
]
and Section
5
below) describes turbulent fluids by means of a random field, rapidly decorrelating in time and decorrelating in space in accordance to the Kolmogorov statistics. More precisely, in this case
we have
\sigma(t):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sigma_{k}\dot{W}^{k}_{t}
for a certain choice of coefficients
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, determining the spatial decorrelation. Hence, (
1.6
) becomes a stochastic transport equation

(1.7)

\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k}(t),\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\circ\,\mathrm{d}{W}^{k}_{t}=0.

The second result of this work proves a.s. exponential mixing for (
1.7
) under the same assumptions
(a)–(c)
.

These conditions are shown to be satisfied by the Kraichnan model in regimes corresponding to non-smooth coefficients in (
1.7
), while still offering sufficient regularity to be accessible by an analysis of the Lyapunov exponents and stochastic flows: More precisely, we require, roughly speaking,
\sum_{k\geq 1}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{k}):\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to T(\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M})
to be
C^{2+\beta}
for some
\beta>0
. This is the optimal range for the argument used in the present work, since for
\beta<0
the Lagrangian dynamics lack uniqueness and branching of particles occurs (see
[
56
, Theorem 9.4 and 10.1]
). In addition,
[
8
, Corollary 3.3 and p. 331]
implies that the top Lyapunov exponent diverges to infinity for
\beta\searrow 0
. We note that the Kraichnan model corresponding to Kolmogorov turbulence leads to
\sum_{k\geq 1}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{k})
being at most in
C^{4/3}
, i.e.
\beta=-\frac{2}{3}
above.

The results of the present work also includes the viscous case, for
\kappa\geq 0
,

(1.8)

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k},\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\circ\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}\!\!\!&=\kappa Tu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,\\
u_{0}&=u.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfy the conditions
(A)–(C)
and let
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the solution of (
1.8
) with
u
being mean-zero in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{M})
for some
s\in(0,1+\beta/2)
. Then there exist
\kappa_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{0}>0
such that for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
and
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0})
there exists
D_{\kappa,\gamma}:\Omega\to[1,\infty)
so that a.s.

(1.9)

\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-s}}\leq D_{\kappa,\gamma}e^{-\gamma st}\|u\|_{H^{s}},\;\;\;t>0.

Moreover,
\sup_{\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]}\mathbb{E}|D_{\kappa,\gamma}|^{p}<\infty
for any
1\leq p<\frac{9d\gamma_{0}}{2s\gamma}
.

We next sketch some aspects of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. By rescaling, (
1.1
) is equivalent to (
1.8
), see Subsection
4
below, with
A\to\infty
corresponding to
\kappa\to 0
. Next, we consider the Lagrangian level, that is the characteristics of (
1.8
) and show that the corresponding two-point motion Markov semigroup
(P^{(2),\kappa}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is ergodic. More precisely, we show that there exists
p>0
and
\alpha>0
such that for any bounded mean-zero continuous
\psi:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
we have that

(1.10)

|P^{(2),\kappa}_{t}\psi(x,y)|\leq Ce^{-\alpha t}d(x,y)^{-p}\|\psi\|_{\infty},\;\;\;t\geq 0.

Inequality (
1.10
) will be shown by means of a quantified form of Harris’ ergodic theorem, see Theorem
2.3
below. The verification of the assumptions of this results require results on stochastic flows provided by Kunita
[
53
]
and Baxendale and Stroock
[
14
]
, and a simple but important trick from
[
27
, p. 9]
. Finally, using (
1.10
) we show mixing for (
1.8
) in the form of (
1.9
),
by using the methods developed by Bedrossian, Blumenthal, and Punshon-Smith in
[
17
,
16
]
. A simple PDE argument then allows to convert
H^{-s}
-decay of the solution of (
1.8
) to the desired Theorem
1.1
(see Subsection
4
). In addition, in Section
4
and Theorem
1.2

L^{p}
-estimates for
D^{A,\gamma}
in Theorem
1.1
are provided.

Further comments on the literature

We collect here further references to the literature on synchronization/stabilization by noise, mixing of passive scalars, and turbulent mixing of passive scalars. Since the literature is extensive, we restrict to literature particularly relevant to this work, referring to the references in the cited works for a more extensive and complete overview.

Stabilization and synchronization by noise for stochastic PDE has been considered, for example in
[
35
,
40
,
39
,
17
,
74
]
, with related work on the level of SDE in
[
34
,
75
,

,

,
5
,
3
]
and for iterated function systems in
[
43
]
and references therein. For applications to sampling of invariant measures we refer to
[
59
]
.

Lyapunov exponents of stochastic flows and their relation to asymptotic properties of the two-point motion have been addressed in depth in the works
[
27
,
14
,
8
,
11
,
10
,
4
,
7
,
49
]
and references therein.

In parallel to the completion of this work, mixing properties for stochastic PDE falling into the general class of (
1.1
) have been analyzed in
[
33
,
32
]
by entirely different methods.
In comparison to the results obtained in the present work, the methods of
[
33
,
32
]
require not only to increase the amplitude of the noise
A
in (
1.1
) but also to choose a particular scaling of the noise coefficients
\sigma_{k}
in (
1.1
) to obtain an arbitrarily high exponential rate of mixing. In other words, given an exponential rate of mixing
\lambda
, the choice of the noise coefficients
\sigma_{k}
in
[
33
,
32
]
will depend on this rate
\lambda
, while the present work shows that
\sigma_{k}
can be chosen fixed and only the amplitude
A
has to be chosen large. In this sense the results of the present work are closer to the way problems are formulated in
[
25
]
for the deterministic case (
1.5
), identifying sufficient conditions for diffusion coefficients
\sigma_{k}
to imply arbitrarily fast mixing.
On the other hand, the results of
[
33
]
allow to include the Kraichnan model for general
\alpha>0
due to
[
33
, Remark 1.2]
, which does not follow from Theorem
1.1
. A second difference is that by the arguments of
[
33
]
no exponential mixing for large times as in Theorem
1.2
can be obtained, but only weaker statements on finite time horizons, see
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Mixing of passive scalars driven by solutions to the stochastic Navier-Stokes equation has been shown in
[
15
]
. An essential difference to the present contribution is that the passive scalar equation in
[
15
]
takes the form of a random PDE, while (
1.1
) is a stochastic PDE. As a consequence, the Langragian system corresponding to (
1.1
) is Markovian, while the one in
[
15
]
only becomes Markovian when coupled with the active stochastic PDE (see e.g.
[
15
, Lemma 7.3]
). Therefore, in the present work we can rely on Harnack inequalities for Kolmogorov equations in finite dimensions, whereas
[
15
]
has to rely on more specific arguments to take care of the infinite dimensionality of the system. This makes possible covering less regular coefficients in the present work. A second difference to the line of arguments developed in
[
15
]
is the construction of a
\kappa
-independent Lyapunov function together with the continuity of the two-point motion in
\kappa
(see Subsections
3.2
and
3.3
), replacing the
\kappa
-dependent Lyapunov function in
[
15
]
. At the same time, the stochastic PDE nature of (
1.1
) also causes additional challenges: While
[
15
]
may prove the time and spatial regularity of solutions to the passive scalar equation in a pathwise manner (see e.g.
[
17
, proof of Lemma 6.11]
), the present work has to rely on probabilistic calculus in form of Kunita’s theory (see
[
53
]
and Lemma
3.9
). Finally, the present paper extends these considerations to the manifold setting.

In comparison with Baxendale and Stroock
[
14
]
, the key difference is that in the present work we include irregular coefficients in the sense that the coefficients of the Fokker-Planck equation corresponding to (
1.8
) are assumed only to have
C^{2+\beta}
regularity, see Proposition
3.13
).

The existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Kraichnan model in the irregular regime has been analyzed in the works
[
62
,
56
,
29
]
. For related work on well-posedness by noise we refer to
[
31
,
36
]
.

For physical background on mixing by stochastic transport and the Kraichnan model we refer to
[
22
,
64
,
38
,
29
,
24
]
and the references therein.

There is a large body of works addressing the mixing of passive scalars by deterministic transport. We refer to
[
25
,
60
,
1
,
65
,
63
,
85
]
and the references therein. In
[
68
]
the effect of diffusion on mixing is analyzed. It is observed that in certain cases, a too strong diffusion decreases the mixing effect of transport. This is in line with the results of this work which apply for diffusion intensity

in (

Preliminaries

In this section we introduce notations and conventions used throughout the paper.

For

we write

,

and

hold simultaneously.

We set
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
,

we let

be the vector norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
to be the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

to be a function identically equal to

.

Further, we set
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
to be the Banach space of bounded linear operators acting from a Banach space

to a Banach space

endowed with the operator norm

. In particular,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We let
\mathbb{E}_{A}\xi_{t}
be the expectation of

, where

.
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}
denotes

.

Let
d\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{M}
be a

-dimensional compact

-smooth connected Riemannian manifold with the Riemannian metric tensor

(see e.g.
[
57

). We will additionally assume that

. Due to the Nash embedding theorem (see

denotes

. The tangent bundle is

For each

we set

to be the unit sphere of

. We also define

. Note that

can be endowed with a Riemannian metric called the

. Then

.

Analogously to

, Section 3]
define

\Phi(x,v)=(x,\exp_{x}(v)),\;\;\;x\in\mathcal{M},\;\;\;v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M},

Remark 2.1
.

As

\delta_{0}>0

This means that for any continuous

(\overline{\Omega},\overline{\mathcal{F}},\overline{\mathbb{P}})
(see Section
2
).

Fix any
p\in(0,p^{*}]
. From Proposition
3.20
and the fact that
\widetilde{V}_{p}
is in the domain of
L^{(2)}
we conclude that for any
t\geq 0

(3.58)

\begin{split}e^{\Lambda(p)t}P^{(2)}_{t}\widetilde{V}_{p}-\widetilde{V}_{p}&=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Lambda(p)s}P^{(2)}_{s}(\Lambda(p)+L^{(2)})\widetilde{V}_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Lambda(p)s}P^{(2)}_{s}c_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s=c_{p}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Lambda(p)s}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=c_{p}\frac{e^{\Lambda(p)t}-1}{\Lambda(p)}\leq c_{p}\frac{e^{\Lambda(p)t}}{\Lambda(p)}.\end{split}

Therefore
P^{(2)}_{t}\widetilde{V}_{p}\leq e^{-\Lambda(p)t}\widetilde{V}_{p}+\widetilde{C}_{p}
, where
\widetilde{C}_{p}:=\tfrac{c_{p}}{\Lambda(p)}
, and in particular, if we consider

V_{p}(x,y):=d(x,y)^{-p},\;\;\;(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}^{c},

then by (
3.36
) and thanks to the fact that
P^{(2)}_{t}
is nonnegative we get that

(3.59)

P^{(2)}_{t}V_{p}\leq K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t}V_{p}+\widetilde{C}_{p}K.

Let us show that an analogue of (
3.59
) holds for
P^{(2),\kappa}
with constants independent of
\kappa
for
\kappa
small enough.

Proposition 3.25
.

For any
t>0
there exist
\kappa_{0}>0
and
C_{p,t}>0
such that for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
one has that

(3.60)

P^{(2),\kappa}_{t}V_{p}\leq 2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t}V_{p}+C_{p,t}.

Beweis.

By (
3.32
) and the definition of
V_{p}
we have that for any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}^{c}

\displaystyle|(P^{(2),\kappa}_{t}-P^{(2)}_{t})V_{p}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=|\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}V_{p}(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})-V_{p}(x_{t},y_{t})|

\displaystyle=|\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})^{-p}-d(x_{t},y_{t})^{-p}|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}\bigl|d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})-d(x_{t},y_{t})\bigr|\bigl(d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})^{-p-1}+d(x_{t},y_{t})^{-p-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}\bigl(d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})-d(x_{t},y_{t})\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\cdot\Bigl(\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})^{-2p-2}+d(x_{t},y_{t})^{-2p-2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thanks to (
3.17
)

\Bigl(\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})^{-2p-2}+d(x_{t},y_{t})^{-2p-2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{p}d(x,y)^{-p-1}.

Fix
\kappa_{0}>0
to be chosen at the end of the proof. Let us bound
\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}\bigl(d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})-d(x_{t},y_{t})\bigr)^{2}
. As by Lemma
3.9

(\phi^{\mu^{2}}(x))_{x\in\mathcal{M},\mu\in[0,\sqrt{\kappa_{0}}]}
is a stochastic flow of
C^{1}
-diffeomorphisms on
\mathcal{M}\times[0,\sqrt{\kappa_{0}}]
, and as
\mathcal{M}\times[0,\sqrt{\kappa_{0}}]
is a compact, there exists
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that with probability at least
1-\delta_{\varepsilon}
for any
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
with
d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
and for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
one has that

(3.61)

|d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})-d(x_{t},y_{t})|\leq(\|D\phi^{\kappa}_{t}-D\phi_{t}\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon^{1/4})d(x,y),

where
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\kappa_{0}\to 0
. To this end first fix
\kappa_{0}>0
and let
\overline{\Omega}_{1}\subset\overline{\Omega}
be the set of all
\overline{\omega}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
with
d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
one has that
(x^{\kappa}_{t}(\overline{\omega}))_{\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]}
and
(y^{\kappa}_{t}(\overline{\omega}))_{\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]}
are all in the same local chart. Assume that
\varepsilon
is so small that any
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
with
d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
are in the same local chart as well. Then by (
3.18
) one can show that
\delta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=1-\mathbb{P}(\overline{\Omega}_{1})\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\kappa_{0}\to 0
.

Next, for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
by (
3.18
) and by the fact that
(x,y)\mapsto d(x,y)
is
C^{\infty}
we have that for some universal
C_{\mathcal{M}}

(3.62)

\bigl|d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})-|y^{\kappa}_{t}-x^{\kappa}_{t}|\bigr|\leq C_{\mathcal{M}}d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})^{2}\leq C_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{C}d(x,y)^{3/2},

where
\widetilde{C}
is as in (
3.18
). Let
\overline{\Omega}_{2}:=\overline{\Omega}_{1}\cap\{C_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{C}\varepsilon^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon^{1/4}/2\}
and let
\delta_{\varepsilon}:=1-\mathbb{P}(\overline{\Omega}_{2})
. Then it remains to notice that
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\kappa_{0}\to 0
and that

(3.63)

\begin{split}\bigl||y^{\kappa}_{t}&-x^{\kappa}_{t}|-|y_{t}-x_{t}|\bigr|\leq|y^{\kappa}_{t}-x^{\kappa}_{t}-(y_{t}-x_{t})|\\
&=\Bigl|\int_{0}^{d(x,y)}\langle D\phi^{\kappa}_{t}(\zeta(a)),\zeta^{\prime}(a)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}a-\int_{0}^{d(x,y)}\langle D\phi_{t}(\zeta(a)),\zeta^{\prime}(a)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}a\Bigr|\\
&\leq\|D\phi^{\kappa}_{t}-D\phi_{t}\|_{\infty}d(x,y),\end{split}

where
\zeta:[0,d(x,y)]\to\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{\infty}
geodesic mapping within one chart such that
\zeta(0)=x
and
\zeta(d(x,y))=y
with
|\zeta^{\prime}(a)|=1
, so (
3.61
) follows from (
3.62
) and (
3.63
).

Therefore thanks to (
3.61
)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{E}}_{x,y}\bigl(d(x^{\kappa}_{t},y^{\kappa}_{t})

\displaystyle-d(x_{t},y_{t})\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\lesssim}}(\sqrt{\delta_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon^{1/4}+\mathbf{1}_{[\varepsilon,\infty)}(d(x,y)))d(x,y)^{2}+\overline{\mathbb{E}}\|D\phi^{\kappa}_{t}-D\phi_{t}\|_{\infty}^{2}d(x,y)^{2}

\displaystyle=d(x,y)^{2}(\sqrt{\delta_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon^{1/4}+\mathbf{1}_{[\varepsilon,\infty)}(d(x,y))+\overline{\mathbb{E}}\|D\phi^{\kappa}_{t}-D\phi_{t}\|_{\infty}^{2})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\lesssim}}d(x,y)^{2}(\sqrt{\delta_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon^{1/4}+\mathbf{1}_{[\varepsilon,\infty)}(d(x,y))+\kappa_{0}^{\beta/2}),

where
(i)
holds by (
3.17
) and Hölder’s inequality and
(ii)
follows from (
3.19
). Now, via choosing optimal
\varepsilon
and
\kappa_{0}
small enough one can obtain that

|(P^{(2),\kappa}_{t}-P^{(2)}_{t})V_{p}(x,y)|\leq\begin{cases}K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t}d(x,y)^{-p},\;\;\;&d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon,\\
C_{p,t},\;\;\;&d(x,y)>\varepsilon,\end{cases}

for some
C_{p,t}>0
, so (
3.60
) holds true.
∎

Later one needs
C_{p,t}
being independent of
t>0
. This can be done via the following corollary.

Corollary 3.26
.

Fix
t^{*}>0
so that
2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}}<1
and
\kappa_{0}
as in Proposition
3.25
corresponding to
t^{*}
. Then there exists
C_{p}>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
and
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]

(3.64)

P^{(2),\kappa}_{nt^{*}}V_{p}\leq(2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}})^{n}V_{p}+C_{p},

where
C_{p}:=\tfrac{C_{p,t^{*}}}{1-2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}}}
.

Beweis.

It is sufficient to notice that by induction and the fact that
P^{(2),\kappa}_{nt^{*}}=(P^{(2),\kappa}_{t^{*}})^{n}

P^{(2),\kappa}_{nt^{*}}V_{p}\leq(2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}})^{n}V_{p}+C_{p,t^{*}}\bigl(1+2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}}+\ldots+(2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}})^{n-1}\bigr),

so the desired follows.
∎

Thanks to Harris’ Theorem
2.3
we can conclude the following.

Proposition 3.27
.

Fix
p^{*}>0
as in Proposition
3.20
.
Then for any
0<p<p^{*}
there exist
t_{0},\kappa_{0},C,\hat{\alpha}>0
such that for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
, for any
\psi:\mathcal{D}^{c}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}}\psi(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0
, and for any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
we have that

(3.65)

|P^{(2),\kappa}_{nt_{0}}\psi(x,y)|\leq Ce^{-\hat{\alpha}nt_{0}}d(x,y)^{-p}\|\psi\|_{\infty},\;\;\;n\geq 1.

Beweis.

Let us start by noticing that
\mu\otimes\mu
is an invariant measure of
(x^{\kappa},y^{\kappa})
on
\mathcal{D}^{c}

as
t\mapsto(x^{\kappa},y^{\kappa})
satisfies the following SDE
defined on
\mathcal{D}^{c}
by

\mathrm{d}\binom{x_{t}^{\kappa}}{y_{t}^{\kappa}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{\sigma_{k}(x_{t}^{\kappa})}{\sigma_{k}(y_{t}^{\kappa})}\circ\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k}+\sqrt{2\kappa}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{\chi_{m}(x_{t}^{\kappa})}{\chi_{m}(y_{t}^{\kappa})}\circ\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{W}_{t}^{m},

which contains only divergence-free vector fields and hence preserves
\mu\otimes\mu
a.s. by Remark
3.7
.
Hence in order to show (
3.65
) one needs only to check the conditions of Theorem
2.3
(in this case the unique invariant measure would be
\mu\otimes\mu
), i.e. we need to show that for
\kappa_{0}
being from Proposition
3.25
and for
t_{0}=t^{*}
as in Corollary
3.26
for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]

\mathcal{P}=P_{t^{*}}^{(2),\kappa}
satisfies the conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
2.3
with constants independent of
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
.

The condition
(i)
is satisfied by Corollary
3.26
with constants
\gamma:=2K^{2}e^{-\Lambda(p)t^{*}}
and
C:=C_{p}
. Let us now check the condition
(ii)
, i.e. that there exist
\eta>0
,
z_{*}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}
, and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.66)

\inf_{z\in\{V_{p}\leq R\}}P_{t^{*}}^{(2),\kappa}\mathbf{1}_{A^{\prime}}(z)>\eta(\mu\otimes\mu)(A^{\prime}),

for any
A^{\prime}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(z_{*})
. Choose
R>1
to be such that

\{V_{p}<R\}=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}^{c}:d(x,y)>R^{-1/p}\}

is nonempty.
Then the condition follows from Harnack-type inequalities presented in Proposition
A.4
, since for any
\kappa\geq 0
the transition probability function
p^{\kappa}:(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{D}^{c}\times\mathcal{D}^{c}
corresponding to
(x^{\kappa},y^{\kappa})
satisfies the following equation for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{D}^{c})

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{D}^{c}}\left[\frac{\partial u(t,(x,y))}{\partial t}+L^{(2),\kappa}u(t,(x,y))\right]p^{\kappa}(t,\cdot,(x,y))\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0,

where

L^{(2),\kappa}u:=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}^{2}+\kappa\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{\chi_{m}}{\chi_{m}}^{2}\right)u,

see (
2.2
). Note that the second- and first-order coefficients, as well as linear term of
L^{(2),\kappa}
are continuous in
\kappa
. Moreover, by the condition
(C)
the derivatives of the second- and first-order coefficients and by the condition
(A)
the ellipticity constant on any given compact
K\subset\mathcal{D}^{c}
are continuous in
\kappa
as well. Therefore one can apply Proposition
A.4
for
p^{\kappa}
with setting the corresponding compact
K
to be
\{V_{p}\leq R\}
so that due to the continuity in
\kappa
one is able to assume that both
a_{K}
and
b_{K}
are independent on
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
, hence the corresponding Harnack constant
c_{t^{*},K,a_{K},b_{K}}
is independent of
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
and thus (
3.66
) holds true.
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
3.5

We now present the proof of Theorem
3.5
.
To this end it is sufficient to show the following proposition, connecting ergodicity of the two-point motion and mixing.

Proposition 3.28
.

Assume that the condition
(C)
is satisfied and that in addition
there exist
t_{0},\kappa_{0},C,\hat{\alpha}>0
such that for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
with
\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=0
we have that

(3.67)

\int_{\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}}\bigr|\mathbb{E}f(\phi^{\kappa}_{nt_{0}}(x))f(\phi^{\kappa}_{nt_{0}}(y))\bigl|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\leq Ce^{-\hat{\alpha}nt_{0}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2},\;\;\;n\geq 1.

Let
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the solution of (
3.9
) with
u
being mean-zero in
H^{s}(\mathcal{M})\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{M})
for some
s\in(0,1+\beta/2)
. Then there exist
\kappa_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{0}>0
independent of
s
and
u
such that for any
\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]
and
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{0})
there exists
D_{\kappa,\gamma}:\Omega\to[1,\infty)
so that

(3.68)

\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-s}}\leq D_{\kappa,\gamma}e^{-\gamma st}\|u\|_{H^{s}},\;\;\;t>0.

Moreover,
\sup_{\kappa\in[0,\kappa_{0}]}\mathbb{E}|D_{\kappa,\gamma}|^{p}<\infty
for any
1\leq p<\tfrac{9d\gamma_{0}}{2\gamma s}
.

Note that (
3.67
) is a direct consequence of (
3.65
) as for any
0<p<d
one has
\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d(x,y)^{-p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)<\infty
.

The proof of Proposition
3.28
is inspired by Section 2.1 and Section 7 from
[
17
]
. In the first step of the proof, we restrict to integer times, relying on their countability. This step exploits in an essential way the ergodicity of the two-point motion (
3.67
)
and allows to conclude for large orders
s
. Using an interpolation argument, in the second step, this is extended to general order
s
. In the third step, the result is extended to all times, by means of local-in-time regularity preservation by the stochastic flow. Together with moments bounds, derived in step five, the proof is finished in the final step.

Step 1: Integer times and large
s

First, similarly to
[
17
, Section 2.1]
we prove the proposition for integer times
n
.
To this end we notice that for any
n\geq 1
, for any zero mean
f,g\in C(\mathcal{M})
, and for any fixed
0<\hat{\gamma}<\hat{\alpha}/2

(here we set for simplicity that
t_{0}

equals 1)

(3.69)

\begin{split}\overline{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl\{\Big|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}&\mu(x)\Big|>e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}\Bigr\}\leq e^{2\hat{\gamma}n}\mathbb{E}\Bigl|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Bigr|^{2}\\
&=e^{2\hat{\gamma}n}\int_{\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}}\tilde{f}(x,y)P^{(2)}_{n}\tilde{g}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\lesssim}}e^{-(\hat{\alpha}-2\hat{\gamma})n}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{2},\end{split}

where
\tilde{f}(x,y)=f(x)f(y)
,
\tilde{g}(x,y)=g(x)g(y)
, and where
(*)
follows from (
3.67
). By using the fact that the embedding
H^{s}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
is continuous for
s
big enough we can conclude that

\overline{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl\{\Big|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big|>e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}\Bigr\}\lesssim e^{-(\hat{\alpha}-2\hat{\gamma})n}\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

so by Borell-Cantelli lemma we have that

\Bigl|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x,\overline{\omega}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Bigr|\lesssim_{\overline{\omega},f,g}e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}

for a.e.
\overline{\omega}\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Unfortunately, this logic does not seem to lead to (
3.13
) as there we need a constant independent of
f\in H^{s}
(recall that

\|g\|_{H^{-s}}=\sup_{0\neq f\in H^{s}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}fg/\|f\|_{H^{s}}

for any
g\in H^{-s}
). So we will proceed via an orthonormal basis of
H^{s}
. Fix any
s_{0}>2d
and
f,g\in H^{s_{0}}
. Let
(\hat{e}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of mean zero functions on
\mathcal{M}
consisting of all eigenvectors of
-\Delta
and let
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be the corresponding eigenvalues (see Section
2
). Without loss of generality let
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be nondecreasing. Then by Weyl’s law (see e.g.
[
71
]
) we have that

(3.70)

\lambda_{k}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}}k^{2/d},\;\;\;k\geq 1;

we also know that
\|\hat{e}_{k}\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}}\lambda_{k}^{d/4}
(see e.g.
[
77
,
28
]
). Therefore we can conclude that

(3.71)

\|\hat{e}_{k}\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}}k^{1/2},\;\;\;k\geq 1.

Moreover, for each
k,k^{\prime}\geq 1
analogously to (
3.69
) by (
3.71
) we have that

\overline{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl\{\Big|\int_{\mathcal{M}}\hat{e}_{k}(x)\hat{e}_{k^{\prime}}(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big|>e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}(kk^{\prime})^{\frac{s_{0}}{d}-1}\Bigr\}\\
\lesssim e^{-(\hat{\alpha}-2\hat{\gamma})n}(kk^{\prime})^{2-\frac{2s_{0}}{d}}\|\hat{e}_{k}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\hat{e}_{k^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim e^{-(\hat{\alpha}-2\hat{\gamma})n}(kk^{\prime})^{3-\frac{2s_{0}}{d}},

so by Borell-Cantelli lemma and the fact that
\sum_{n,k,k^{\prime}\geq 1}e^{-(\hat{\alpha}-2\hat{\gamma})n}(kk^{\prime})^{3-\frac{2s_{0}}{d}}<\infty
there exists a.s. finite
\widehat{D}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by

(3.72)

\widehat{D}:=1\vee\sup_{n,k,k^{\prime}\geq 1}\frac{|\int_{\mathcal{M}}\hat{e}_{k}(x)\hat{e}_{k^{\prime}}(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)|e^{\hat{\gamma}n}}{(kk^{\prime})^{\frac{s_{0}}{d}-1}},

so that
|\int_{\mathcal{M}}\hat{e}_{k}(x)\hat{e}_{k^{\prime}}(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)|\leq\widehat{D}e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}(kk^{\prime})^{\frac{s_{0}}{d}-1}
a.s. In particular, if
f
and
g
have the following expansions

f=\sum_{k\geq 1}f_{k}\hat{e}_{k}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;g=\sum_{k\geq 1}g_{k}\hat{e}_{k},

then we get that a.s. for any
n\geq 1

\Bigl|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Bigr|\leq\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\geq 1}|f_{k}||g_{k^{\prime}}|\Big|\int_{\mathcal{M}}\hat{e}_{k}(x)\hat{e}_{k^{\prime}}(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big|\\
\leq\widehat{D}e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{\frac{s_{0}}{d}-1}|f_{k}|\sum_{k^{\prime}\geq 1}k^{\prime\frac{s_{0}}{d}-1}|g_{k^{\prime}}|\lesssim\widehat{D}e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}\|f\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\|g\|_{H^{s_{0}}},

where for the last estimate we used that by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, (
2.3
), and (
3.70
)

\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{\frac{s_{0}}{d}-1}|f_{k}|\leq\|(k^{\frac{s_{0}}{d}}|f_{k}|)_{k\geq 1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|(k^{-1})_{k\geq 1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\eqsim\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

Thus (
3.13
) follows for integer
t=n
and any fixed
s_{0}>2d
.

Step 2: Integer times and arbitrary
s

In order to move to a general
s>0
notice that
S:g\mapsto S(g):=g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(\cdot))
is a bounded linear operator from
H^{s_{0}}
to
H^{-s_{0}}
with the norm bounded from above by
\widehat{D}e^{-\hat{\gamma}n}
a.s.; at the same time this is an isometry in
L^{2}
by the fact that
\phi^{\kappa}_{t}
is
\mu
-measure preserving thanks to Remark
3.7
. Hence thanks to the Riesz-Thorin interpolation argument (see e.g.
[
45
, Section C]
) we can extend (
3.13
) to any
0<s\leq 2d
(and hence to any
s>0
) still with
t
being integer so that

\|S\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{s},H^{-s})}\leq\|S\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{s_{0}},H^{-s_{0}})}^{\frac{s}{s_{0}}}\|S\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}^{1-\frac{s}{s_{0}}}=\|S\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{s_{0}},H^{-s_{0}})}^{\frac{s}{s_{0}}},

in particular

(3.73)

\Bigl|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{n}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Bigr|\lesssim_{s}\widehat{D}^{\frac{s}{s_{0}}}e^{-\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}n}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{H^{s}},

where
\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}=\frac{s\hat{\gamma}}{s_{0}}
(one can assume
s_{0}=3d
for simplicity).

Step 3: Arbitrary times

Fix
s\in(1,1+\beta/2)
, the case
s\in(0,1]
can be done analogously. Let us now treat general
t\geq 0
. This can be done similarly to
[
17
, Section 7.2]
: it is enough to notice that for a.e.
\overline{\omega}\in\overline{\Omega}

(3.74)

\Bigl|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(\phi^{\kappa}_{t}(x,\overline{\omega}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Bigr|\lesssim_{s}\widehat{D}(\overline{\omega})^{\frac{s}{s_{0}}}e^{-\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}n}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g(\phi^{\kappa}_{\hat{t}}(\cdot,\theta_{n}\overline{\omega}))\|_{H^{s}},

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\hat{t}\in[0,1)
are such that
t=n+\hat{t}
and where
\theta_{n}
is as defined in Section
2
, see (
2.5
). Then thanks to (

4.

In this section we prove Theorem

Corollary 4.1
.

Let

(4.1)

Moreover,

Beweis.

It follows from (

a.s.

(4.2)

\begin{split}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&-\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle u_{s}(x)\sigma_{k}(x),\nabla u_{s}(x)\rangle_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\circ\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\\

where the integrals

are well-defined as
u_{s}
is an element of
H^{1}(\mathcal{M})
for any
s>0
a.s. (see
[
61
, Theorem 4.2.4]
), so

a.s., and as

for any
k\geq 1
.
The fact that
\circ\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}
-integrals of (
4.2
) exist and are summable follows from the condition
(C)
and the fact that by

61

As the coefficients
\sigma_{k}
’s are divergence-free, by approximating

by smooth functions in

thanks to the divergence theorem on manifolds (see
[

[

so a.s.

(4.3)

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\kappa\sum_{m=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\|\chi_{m}u_{s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

hence by Lemma
3.3

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=-\kappa\sum_{m=1}^{n}\|\chi_{m}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\eqsim-\kappa\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

Therefore (

D^{\prime}_{\kappa,\gamma}(\omega)\eqsim\sqrt{\gamma}C^{2}D_{\kappa,\gamma,\tau}(\omega),\;\;\;\omega\in\Omega.

5.
Regular Kraichnan model on
\mathbb{T}^{d}

The
Kraichnan model
(a.k.a. the
RDT model
for “Rapid Decorrelation in Time”) was introduced by Kraichnan in
[
50
]
and by Kazantsev in
[
48
]
in the context of turbulence in fluids. We also refer to
[
64
]
for further details. The classical Kraichnan model defines an
isotropic flows
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or
\mathbb{S}^{d}
), while the Kraichnan model on the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
was introduced in
[
24
, Section 3]
as follows. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we set, for
i,j=1,\ldots,d
,

(5.1)

\begin{split}D(x,y)(e_{i},e_{j})&=\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k}(x),e_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{k}(y),e_{j}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}}\Bigl[(1-\wp)\delta_{ij}-(1-\wp d)\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{2}}\Bigr]e^{iz\cdot(x-y)}\hat{d}(|z|),\end{split}

where the parameter
\wp
measures the compressibility of the flow (see e.g.
[
38
, Section 2]
). Since we are considering incompressible flows, corresponding to
\sigma_{k}
being divergence-free (see e.g.
[
54
, §10]
), we have
\wp=0
.
Following
[
64
, pp. 343, 426, and 432]
the coefficient
\hat{d}(|z|)
is chosen as
\hat{d}(|z|)\eqsim\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}
for some
\alpha>2
.

In terms of the coefficients
\sigma_{k}
this choice correlation function
D(x,y)
corresponds to choosing
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
as
(\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0},1\leq\ell\leq d-1}
, where for any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and for any pair
(z,-z)
from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}
with
z
lexicographically dominating
-z

(5.2)

\begin{split}\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}(x)&=a^{\ell}_{z}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\cos(z\cdot x),\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\hat{e}_{-z}^{\ell}(x)&=a^{\ell}_{z}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\sin(z\cdot x),\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{split}

with
(a^{\ell}_{z})_{1\leq\ell\leq d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
being an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement to
z
. Indeed, in this case for any
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle D(x,y)(e_{i},e_{j})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k}(x),e_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{k}(y),e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}}\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\bigl[\cos(z\cdot x)\cos(z\cdot y)+\sin(z\cdot y)\sin(z\cdot y)\bigr]\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}\langle a^{\ell}_{z},e_{i}\rangle\langle a^{\ell}_{z},e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\frac{1}{4}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}}\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\cos\bigl(z\cdot(x-y)\bigr)\Bigl[\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle-\frac{\langle z,e_{i}\rangle\langle z,e_{j}\rangle}{|z|^{2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}}\Bigl[\delta_{ij}-\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{2}}\Bigr]e^{iz\cdot(x-y)}\frac{1}{8|z|^{d+\alpha}},

where
(*)
follows from the fact that
(a^{1}_{z},\ldots,a^{d-1}_{z},z/|z|)
forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, we obtain (
5.1
) with
\hat{d}(|z|)=\frac{1}{8|z|^{d+\alpha}}
as required.

We further notice that,

(5.3)

\langle D\sigma_{k},\sigma_{k}\rangle=0,\;\;\;\;\;k\geq 1,

since

(5.4)

\begin{split}\langle D\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell},\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}\rangle&=a^{\ell}_{z}\langle a^{\ell}_{z},z\rangle\frac{1}{2|z|^{d+\alpha}}\sin(z\cdot x)\cos(z\cdot x)=0,\\
\langle D\hat{e}_{-z}^{\ell},\hat{e}_{-z}^{\ell}\rangle&=-a^{\ell}_{z}\langle a^{\ell}_{z},z\rangle\frac{1}{2|z|^{d+\alpha}}\sin(z\cdot x)\cos(z\cdot x)=0.\end{split}

This implies that the Stratonovich SDE (
1.1
) coincides with the Itô one by
[
72
, Theorem V.26]
. Note that (
5.3
) holds for general isotropic flows on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, see
[
12
, (3.7)]
and compare Remark
5.1
.

Remark 5.1

((An-)isotropy in the Kraichnan model)
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or
\mathbb{S}^{d}
) the Kraichnan model is known to be governed by isotropic flows, i.e. stochastic flows defined analogously (
3.5
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}^{d}
) with
D(x,y):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sigma_{k}(x)\otimes\sigma_{k}(y)
depending only on
|x-y|
, see, for example
[
64
, Section 4.2.2]
and
[
56
,
12
,
83
,
73
,
84
]
. For the Kraichnan model over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(the box case) the phenomenon of anisotropy appears (see
[
24
,
21
,
55
]
). Indeed, any isotropic flow on the torus is necessarily trivial: If
D(x,y)
depends only on
|x-y|
then it is invariant under rotations. Now applying rotations with center
\{0\}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
any point on the torus can be moved arbitrarily close to
\{0\}
. Hence, continuity of
D(x,y)
implies that
D
has to be a constant.

Theorem 5.2
.

Consider the Kraichnan model on the
d
-dimensional torus with spatial decorrelation parameter
\alpha>2
, that is, the stochastic transport equation

(5.5)

\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}+A\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k},\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\circ\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}=0,

with
\sigma_{k}
chosen as in (
5.2
). Then, (
5.5
) is exponentially mixing, in the sense of Theorem
1.1
.
Moreover, the viscous Kraichnan model

(5.6)

\,\mathrm{d}u_{t}+A\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle\sigma_{k},\nabla u_{t}\rangle_{T\mathcal{M}}\circ\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}=\Delta u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,\

satisfies enhanced diffusion, in the sense of Theorem
3.5
.

Beweis.

The proof consists in verifying the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
for the Kraichnan model, that is, the conditions
(A)–(C)
from Section
1
.

(A)
: Fix
x\neq y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Fix
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}
lexicographically dominating
-z
such that

\langle z,x-y\rangle\neq 2\pi n,\;\;\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Note that in this case for any
\ell

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}(x)\\
\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}(y)\\
\end{array}\right)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a^{\ell}_{z}\cos(z\cdot x)\\

\end{array}\right)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{e}_{-z}^{\ell}(x)\\
\hat{e}_{-z}^{\ell}(y)\\

generate

as then

as the set

is dense in the unit sphere of

(if
z
does not satisfy (

), then a small perturbation of
Cz
for big

does). Therefore

span

and hence

is satisfied.

(B)
: To this end let us actually write down what are
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}
’s. By (

) we have that
(\tilde{\sigma}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
coincides with
(\tilde{e}_{z}^{\ell})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0},1\leq\ell\leq d-1}
, where for any

, for any

, and for any pair
(z,-z)
from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}
with
z
lexicographically dominating
-z

\begin{split}\tilde{e}_{z}^{\ell}(x,v)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\sin(z\cdot x)\langle z,v\rangle\Bigl[a^{\ell}_{z}-v\langle v,a^{\ell}_{z}\rangle\Bigr],\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\;\;v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\\
\tilde{e}_{-z}^{\ell}(x,v)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\cos(z\cdot x)\langle z,v\rangle\Bigl[v\langle v,a^{\ell}_{z}\rangle-a^{\ell}_{z}\Bigr],\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\;\;v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.\end{split}

Fix
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
We need to show that for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
w\in v^{\perp}
satisfying
|u|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1
there exists

and

such that

. This follows from the fact that if

dominates lexicographically
-z
, then

\langle e_{z}^{\ell}(x),u\rangle+\langle\tilde{e}_{z}^{\ell}(x,v),w\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}\bigl[\cos(z\cdot x)\langle a^{\ell}_{z},u\rangle+\sin(z\cdot x)\langle z,v\rangle\langle a^{\ell}_{z},w\rangle\bigr],

Linear combinations of these numbers generate both
\langle a^{\ell}_{z},u\rangle
and
\langle z,v\rangle\langle a^{\ell}_{z},w\rangle
. So if

, then it is sufficient to choose some

. If

, then

.

(C)
: First note that by (

) we have that
\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle D\sigma_{k},\sigma_{k}\rangle=0
, so this function is in

for some
\beta\in(0,1]
. Next notice that

\|\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}\|_{\infty}\eqsim\frac{1}{|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}},\;\;\;\;\|D\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}\|_{\infty}\eqsim\frac{1}{|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-1}},\;\;\;\;\|D^{2}\hat{e}_{z}^{\ell}\|_{\infty}\eqsim\frac{1}{|z|^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}-2}},

hence

(5.8)

\begin{split}\sum_{k\geq 1}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|D\sigma_{k}\|_{\infty}^{2}&+\|D\sigma_{k}\|_{\infty}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\sigma_{k}\|_{\infty}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{\infty}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\eqsim_{d}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}}\frac{1}{|z|^{d+\alpha-2}}\eqsim_{d}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0},|z|_{\infty}=n}\frac{1}{n^{d+\alpha-2}}\\

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\|(\sigma_{k}(y)-\sigma_{k}(x))\otimes(\sigma_{k}(y)-\sigma_{k}(x))-\langle D\sigma_{k}(x),y-x\rangle\otimes\langle D\sigma_{k}(x),y-x\rangle\|

6.
Hörmander conditions

In this section we show that, in the case of smooth diffusion coefficients, the ellipticity conditions
(A)
for the two-point motion and
(B)
for the normalized tangent flow can be relaxed to the Hörmander condition, as it was also done in the classical work
[

by Baxendale and Stroock (see also
[

,

(A’)

There is a
K>0
such that
\sigma_{k}\equiv 0
for all
k>K
and that for all

such that

we have that

{\rm Lie}\left(\binom{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\binom{\sigma_{K}}{\sigma_{K}}\right)\binom{x_{1}}{x_{2}}=T_{x_{1}}\mathcal{M}\times T_{x_{2}}\mathcal{M}.

(B’)

There is a
K>0
such that
\sigma_{k}\equiv 0
for all
k>K
and that for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
v\in S_{x}\mathcal{M}
we have that

{\rm Lie}\left(\binom{\sigma_{1}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{1}},\ldots,\binom{\sigma_{K}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{K}}\right)\binom{x}{v}=T_{x}\mathcal{M}\times T_{v}(S_{x}\mathcal{M}).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfy the conditions
(A’),(B’),(C)
. Then the results of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
hold.

Beweis.

First, one can impose the condition
(B’)
instead of

in Subsection
3.1
as it was done e.g. in
[

]
. Next, Proposition

with the conditions
(A’)
and
(B’)
instead of

and

was shown on

.
The proof then follows the lines of the proofs of Theorem

and Theorem

. Since the sequence
(\sigma_{k})
is finite and each
\sigma_{k}

(A)
does not hold. Indeed, for any fixed
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
with
x=(x^{1},x^{2})
and
y=(y^{1},y^{2})
one has that

(6.1)

\binom{\sigma_{1}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(y)}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\sin x^{1}\\
0\\
\sin y^{1}\end{pmatrix},\;\;\binom{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{2}(y)}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\cos x^{1}\\
0\\
\cos y^{1}\end{pmatrix},

(6.2)

\binom{\sigma_{3}(x)}{\sigma_{3}(y)}=\begin{pmatrix}\sin x^{2}\\
0\\
\sin y^{2}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\;\;\binom{\sigma_{4}(x)}{\sigma_{4}(y)}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos x^{2}\\
0\\
\cos y^{2}\\
0\end{pmatrix},

which span
\mathbb{R}^{4}
if and only if

(6.3)

x^{1}\neq y^{1}+\pi k\;\;\text{or}\;\;x^{2}\neq y^{2}+\pi k\;\;\text{for all}\;\;k\in\{0,1\}.

Indeed, in this case both (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) span two-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
which are orthogonal to each other.
Hence, the strict ellipticity condition
(A)
for the two point motion is not satisfied.

However, we next show that
(A’)
can be verified. To this end fix
x
and
y
so that (
6.3
) is satisfied and let us exemplarily compute one of the two-point Lie brackets

\displaystyle\left[\binom{\sigma_{1}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(y)},\binom{\sigma_{3}(x)}{\sigma_{3}(y)}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle D\binom{\sigma_{1}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(y)},\binom{\sigma_{3}(x)}{\sigma_{3}(y)}\right\rangle-\left\langle D\binom{\sigma_{3}(x)}{\sigma_{3}(y)},\binom{\sigma_{1}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(y)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sin x^{1}\cos x^{2}\\
-\cos x^{1}\sin x^{2}\\
\sin y^{1}\cos y^{2}\\
-\cos y^{1}\sin y^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

In a similar way, the first-order Lie brackets generate the vectors

\begin{pmatrix}\cos x^{1}\cos x^{2}\\
\sin x^{1}\sin x^{2}\\
\cos y^{1}\cos y^{2}\\
\sin y^{1}\sin y^{2}\end{pmatrix},\;\;\begin{pmatrix}\sin x^{1}\sin x^{2}\\
\cos x^{1}\cos x^{2}\\
\sin y^{1}\sin y^{2}\\
\cos y^{1}\cos y^{2}\end{pmatrix},\;\;\text{and}\;\;\begin{pmatrix}\cos x^{1}\sin x^{2}\\
-\sin x^{1}\cos x^{2}\\
\cos y^{1}\sin y^{2}\\
-\sin y^{1}\cos y^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Together with (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) these vectors span
\mathbb{R}^{4}
unless
x^{1}=y^{1}
and
x^{2}=y^{2}
, i.e. unless
x=y
.
Therefore, the desired result follows from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Abstract

1.
Introduction

In the recent paper
The third homology of

SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
(
[
10

H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),\mathbb{Z})
by calculating the surjective homomorphism
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathbb{Q})

Let
F
be a field, there is a natural induced map from
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})
to the indecomposable quotient,
K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(F)
, of the third Quillen
K
-group of

[
6
]

[
4
]
, Johan Dupont and Chih-Han Sah showed that this map is an isomorphism for the case of
F=\mathbb{C}
(and more generally
F^{\times}=(F^{\times})^{2}
). Furthermore, in
[
5
]
, Chih-Han Sah proved this result for the case of
\mathbb{R}
. However, for general fields, the map
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(F)
has a large kernel (
[
8
]
). In fact,
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})
is naturally a module over the integral group ring
R_{F}:=\mathbb{Z}[F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}]
of the group of square classes of the field
F
and the above homomorphism is a homomorphism of
R_{F}
-modules if
K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(F)
is given the trivial module structure. In (
[
8
]
), Hutchinson shows that the action of
R_{F}
on
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})
is highly nontrivial for fields with (many) discrete valuations. When
F^{\times}
acts nontrvially on
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})
, the map
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(F)
has a nontrivial kernel. Mirzaii has shown (
[
3
]
) that the kernel of the induced homomorphism
H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})_{F^{\times}}\rightarrow K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(F)
consists at most of
2
-primary torsion. In other words
\mathrm{Ker}\left(H_{3}\left(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)\rightarrow K_{3}^{\mathrm{ind}}(F)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)
is
\mathcal{I}_{F}H_{3}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z})
(up to some
2
-torsion), where

is the augmentation ideal of
R_{F}
.

For

where

, the main proposition of this article (see Proposition

\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
p=N(\alpha)=(R(\alpha))^{2}+p\ell(I(\alpha))^{2}
. It follows that
p|R(\alpha)
and from above, we get our result.

\Box

6.
The main theorem

Let
F
be a number field. We consider
\Pi
, the set of discrete valuations on
F
. As we seen, the maps
S_{p}
induce

\overline{S}:H_{3}\left(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)_{0}\cong\mathcal{I}_{F}\mathcal{RP}_{+}(F)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\rightarrow\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\Pi}\mathcal{P}(k(\upsilon_{p}))\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\{p\}

In
[
10
]
, Hutchinson proved this is an isomorphism for
F=\mathbb{Q}
. Our main theorem (see Proposition
6.10
) is that it is an isomorphism for
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-m})
for
m\in\{2,3,,7,11\}
.
Furthermore we will prove a slightly weaker statement for the case
m=-1
. In all cases, we will use the local-global
principle for characters; i.e. we will prove that

\overline{S}_{\chi}:H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},\mathcal{I}_{F}\mathcal{RP}_{+}(F)\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)_{\chi}\rightarrow\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\Pi}\left(\mathcal{P}(k(\upsilon_{p}))\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\{p\}\right)_{\chi}

is an isomorphism for all
\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}}
.

We will use the following observation repeatedly below:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be an
R_{F}
-module. Let
\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}}
, let
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
. Suppose there exist

such that
\langle a\rangle
acts as
\varepsilon
on

and
\chi(\langle a\rangle)=-\varepsilon
. Then
M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]_{\chi}=0

\textbf{\emph{Proof}}.
Let
m\in M
. In

we get

-\varepsilon m_{\chi}=\chi(\langle a\rangle)m_{\chi}=\langle a\rangle m_{\chi}=\varepsilon m_{\chi}
.

Then
2\varepsilon m_{\chi}=0
. Therefore
m_{\chi}=0
for all
m_{\chi}\in M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]_{\chi}
.

\Box

To start with, we consider the trivial character
\chi_{0}

Next, we consider characters
\chi
satisfying

. Let us recall that
\langle-1\rangle
acts as multiplication by

on

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-m})
be an imaginary quadratic number field with

and

. Also let
\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of

We have the following cases:

.

\langle-1\rangle

b)

If

. It follows that

. Clearly we have that

\langle\omega^{2}\rangle=\langle 1\rangle

\Box

Now let
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-m})
be an imaginary quadratic number field with

and let
\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}}
. If

for some

\left(\mathcal{P}(k(\upsilon_{p}))\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\{p\}\right)_{\chi}=0
.

If the class number of

It follows that

\widehat{\frac{F^{\times}}{(F^{\times})^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}}}\cong\widehat{\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\Pi}p^{\mathbb{Z}/2}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}\left(\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p\in\Pi}p^{\mathbb{Z}/2},\mu_{2}\right)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Sets}}(\Pi,\mu_{2})

.

•

Lemma 6.3
.

Let

. Let

\left(\mathcal{P}(k(\upsilon_{p}))\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\{p\}\right)_{\chi}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{P}(k(\upsilon_{p}))\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\{p\},&\hbox{$\chi=\chi_{p}$;}\\

If

\Box

\Box

x=\frac{a}{b}
with
a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
, we will prove that
[x]_{\chi}=0
by induction on
\mathrm{min}(N(a),N(b))=m
. If
m=1
, then
a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F}
or
b\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F}
. Thus either
x\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
or
x^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Therefore, it follows from the previous paragraph that
[x]_{\chi}=0
.

Since
[x]_{\chi}=-\left[\frac{1}{x}\right]_{\chi}
, we can suppose that

N(b)<N(a)
. Now we suppose the results holds if norm
m=N(b)\leq n
. We will
prove it for
\mathrm{min}(N(a),N(b))=N(b)=n+1
. Note that there exist

q,r\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
such that
a=qb+r
and
N(r)<N(b)
. Taking
t=q

from Proposition
6.7
, we obtain that

[x]_{\chi}=\left[\frac{r}{b}\right]_{\chi}
. Note that

\mathrm{min}(N(r),N(b))=N(r)\leq n
. Hence by induction hypothesis we have
that
[x]_{\chi}=0
.

\Box

Lemma 6.9
.

If
f:M\rightarrow N
is a map of
R_{F}
-modules and if
f_{\chi}:M_{\chi}\rightarrow N_{\chi}
is an isomorphism for all
\chi\in\widehat{\frac{F^{\times}}{(F^{\times})^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}}}
, then the induced map

H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)\rightarrow H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},N\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)

is an isomorphism.

\textbf{\emph{Proof}}.
Let
\chi\in\widehat{\frac{F^{\times}}{(F^{\times})^{2}}}
. If
\chi\in\widehat{\frac{F^{\times}}{(F^{\times})^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}}}
, then
H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)_{\chi}=M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]_{\chi}\cong N\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]_{\chi}=H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},N\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)_{\chi}
.

Otherwise,
\chi(u)=-1
for some
u\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. But the element
\langle u\rangle
acts trivially on
H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)
and
H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},N\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)
. By Lemma
6.1

H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},M\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)_{\chi}=0=H_{0}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F},N\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)_{\chi}
.

The result follows by the local-global principle for characters (Proposition
3.2
).

\Box

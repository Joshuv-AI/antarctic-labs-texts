Traveling waves for nonlinear Schrödinger equations
Laura Baldelli, Bartosz Bieganowski, Jarosław Mederski

Abstract
. We look for traveling wave solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a subsonic speed, covering several physical models with Sobolev subcritical nonlinear effects. Our approach is based on a variant of Sobolev-type inequality involving the momentum and we show the existence of its minimizers solving the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. MSC 2010: Primary: 35J20, 35J10, 35C07. Key words: Traveling waves, Schrödinger equation, Gross-Pitaevskii equation, Pohožaev constraint, concentration compactness, non-vanishing.



Introduction

In this paper, we study the existence of nontrivial finite energy traveling waves moving with subsonic speed for a class of nonlinear Schrödinger equations of the type

(1.1)

i\partial_{t}\Phi=\Delta\Phi+F(|\Phi|^{2})\Phi\quad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},

where
d\geq 3
,
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies the ”boundary condition”
|\Phi|\to r_{0}
as
|x|\to\infty
, with
r_{0}>0
and
F
is a real-valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
F(r_{0}^{2})=0
.
If (
1.1
) is defocusing, that is if
F^{\prime}(r_{0}^{2})<0
, a simple scaling, see
[
17
]
, enables us to assume
r_{0}=1
and
F^{\prime}(r_{0}^{2})=-1
, so that the sound velocity at infinity associated to (
1.1
) is
v_{s}=r_{0}\sqrt{-2F^{\prime}(r_{0}^{2})}=\sqrt{2}
.
Equations of this type appear in mathematical
physics and have been derived as models of several physical phenomena such as superconductivity, superfluidity in Hellium II, Bose-Einstein condensates as well as dark optical solitons
[
1
,
13
,
12
]
.

The Gross-Pitaevskii equation is a particular case of (
1.1
) with
F(s)=1-s
and it was deeply studied in the literature, see
[
2
,
17
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
16
]
and references therein. Recall that nontrivial finite energy traveling waves for
F(s)=1-s
in dimension
d=1
are unique up to rotation and translation and are given by

\Phi_{c}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2-c^{2}}{2}}\tanh\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2-c^{2}}}{2}x\Big)+i\frac{c}{\sqrt{2}}

for
c\in(0,\sqrt{2})
.
\Phi_{c}
are called
dark solitons
,
\Phi(x,t)=\Phi_{c}(x-ct)
solves (
1.1
) and observe that
|\Phi(x,t)|\to 1
as
|x|\to\infty
.

In the present paper, we are focused on
traveling wave
solutions which are solutions to (
1.1
) of the form

(1.2)

\Phi(x,t)=1+u(x_{1}-ct,y)

with
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,
x=(x_{1},y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
d\geq 2
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
describes the
speed
.
By the ansatz (
1.2
) the equation for the profile
u
is given by

(1.3)

-ic\partial_{x_{1}}u+\Delta u+(1+u)F(|1+u|^{2})=0\quad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The study of finite energy traveling solutions for (
1.1
) has a long story. In the particular case of the Gross-Pitaevskii equation, this problem was first studied in the papers by Putterman and Roberts
[
12
]
, where formal calculations and numerical analysis gave rise to a set of conjectures regarding existence, asymptotic behavior and stability of finite energy traveling waves: the so-called Jones-Putterman-Roberts program.
In particular, the existence of finite energy traveling waves is expected if and only if the propagation velocity is subsonic, namely if
c\in(0,\sqrt{2})
.
In the last years, much progress has been made to give rigorous proofs of those conjectures. Nonexistence of nontrivial finite energy traveling waves for supersonic speed was proved by Gravejat in
[
10
]
, for general nonlinearities see
[
16
]
.
Concerning the asymptotics of finite energy solutions, for any
d\geq 2
, finite energy solutions of (
1.3
) converge at infinity to a fixed complex number of modulus
1
.
We recall the existence results for small values of
c
, see
[
4
]
in dimension 2
and
[
6
]
in dimension 3, but a complete existence result in the sub-sonic case remained for many years as a standing open problem. Finally, Mariş proved in
[
17
]
the existence
result for any subsonic speed in dimension
d\geq 3
. His approach is, summing up, to minimize the Lagrangian under a Pohožaev-type constraint. Once this is accomplished, Mariş proves that
the corresponding Lagrange multiplier is
0
, concluding the proof. This approach works also for more general nonlinearities with nonvanishing conditions at infinity, such as cubic-quintic nonlinearity. As commented in
[
17
]
, this minimization approach breaks
down in dimension
2
because of different scaling properties: the infimum is
0
and is never attained. Moreover Chiron and Mariş
[
9
]
have considered also travelling waves with at the fixed momentum.
Another approach is due to Bellazzini and Ruiz
[
2
]
and is based on the approximation domains and result of Mariş in dimension 2 and 3 were recovered. Let us also mention that Mariş and Mur
[
18
]
have recently considered periodic traveling waves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The aim of this work is to consider more general growth assumptions at infinity concerning
F
in comparison to
[
17
]
in case
d\geq 4
, and provide a direct and simpler variational approach based on a Sobolev-type inequality involving the momentum, see (
1.5
) below. Moreover, in contrary to
[
17
]
, we no longer require regularity of minimizers on the Pohožaev manifold to solve (
1.3
) (cf. Remark
3.4
), and we show that only the nonvanishing analysis is sufficient to establish the existence of travelling waves instead of the full concentration-compactness. We also point out that, under condition (F2), the bootstrap argument is no longer available.

Concerning the nonlinearity
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
we consider the following general assumptions:

(F1)

F\in{\mathcal{C}}[0,\infty)
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
in a neighborhood of
1
,
F(1)=0
and
F^{\prime}(1)=-1
,

(F2)

\limsup_{s\to\infty}|F(s)|/s^{\frac{2^{*}-2}{2}}=0
,

where
2^{*}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent.
In particular, (F2) is in the spirit of Berestycki-Lions
[
3
]
.

Letting
V(s):=\int_{s}^{1}F(t)\,dt
for
s\geq 0
, we can define the
energy
and the Lagrangian of the problem consider, respectively as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(|1+u|^{2})\,dx,\quad u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle E_{c}(u)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(u)+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(|1+u|^{2})\,dx+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u),\quad u\in\mathcal{X},

where
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
stands for the completion of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the norm

\|u\|=|\nabla u|_{2}
,

(1.4)

{\mathcal{X}}:=\big\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\;|1+u|-1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big\},

and
{\mathcal{Q}}(u)
is the first component of the
momentum
defined as

{\mathcal{Q}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle i\partial_{x_{1}}u,u\rangle\,dx.

Here and in what follows
|\cdot|_{k}
denotes the
L^{k}
-norm,
k\in[1,\infty]
and
\langle f,g\rangle=\Re(f)\Re(g)+\Im(f)\Im(g)
, with
\Re(\cdot),\Im(\cdot)
the real and the imaginary part, respectively. Observe that, in view of (F1) and (F2),
{\mathcal{E}}(u)<\infty
if and only if
u\in{\mathcal{X}}
.
It is clear that
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a well defined functional in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, but in general there exist functions
u\in{\mathcal{X}}\setminus H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\langle i\partial_{x_{1}}u,u\rangle\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore a proper definition of the momentum in
{\mathcal{X}}
was proposed in
[
17
, Section 2]
and then
|{\mathcal{Q}}(u)|<\infty
for
u\in{\mathcal{X}}
.

Now we state the
Sobolev-type inequality
involving the momentum
{\mathcal{Q}}
for
d\geq 4
as follows. Let
S_{c}\in\mathbb{R}
be the largest possible constant such that

(1.5)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{d-1}{d-3}}\geq-S_{c}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{x_{1}}u|^{2}+V(|1+u|^{2})\,dx+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u)\right)

holds for all
u\in{\mathcal{X}}
, where
x=(x_{1},y)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Our main result can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that (F1), (F2) hold and let
c\in(0,\sqrt{2})
,
d\geq 4
. There holds.

(a)
S_{c}>0
.

(b)
If
(u_{n})\subset{\mathcal{X}}
is a sequence such that

\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{d-1}{d-3}}}{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{x_{1}}u_{n}|^{2}+V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})\,dx+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u_{n})}\to-S_{c}.

as
n\to\infty
,
then there is
(x_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, passing to a subsequence,

(u_{n}(\cdot+x_{n}))
is weakly convergent in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to a minimizer
u
of (
1.5
), i.e. the equality in (
1.5
) holds.

(c) If
u
is a minimizer of (
1.5
), then (up to some rescaling)
u
is a weak solution to (
1.3
).

Problem (
1.3
) was solved in
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
under a slightly more restrictive growth condition at infinity, namely

\limsup_{s\to\infty}|F(s)|/s^{p_{0}}=0

for some
p_{0}<\frac{2^{*}-2}{2}
. This assumption was crucial, in particular, for carrying out the bootstrap argument.
We present a different technique to prove Theorem
1.1
, which is based on a variant of Lions’ lemma based on the nonvanishing property from
[
17
]
– see Lemma
1.2
below, and on the Sobolev-type inequality. The variational approach seems to be simpler and more direct.

If
d=3
, then we no longer have a Sobolev-type inequality, however, recall that in
[
17
, Theorem 6.2]
a minimizer of
E_{c}
on the Pohožaev manifold

{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{0\}\ :\ \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{x_{1}}u_{n}|^{2}+V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})\,dx+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u_{n})=0\right\}

has been found, which solves (
1.3
), however it is not clear whether our approach can be applied in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

In what follows we state the following Lions’ type lemma (cf.
[
15
]
) which will be useful to prove the non-triviality of the solution given in the main result of the paper.

First, we introduce the Ginzburg-Landau energy as follows. Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be an odd function that
\varphi(s)=s
for
s\in[0,2]
,
\varphi(s)=3
for
s\geq 4
, and
0\leq\varphi^{\prime}(s)\leq 1
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for
u\in{\mathcal{X}}
we set

E_{GL}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u|)-1\right)^{2}\,dx.

Note that the function space
\mathcal{X}
in (
1.4
), as observed by
[
17
]
, for
d\geq 3
satisfies

\mathcal{X}=\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ :\ \varphi^{2}(|1+u|)-1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}=\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ :\ E_{GL}(u)<\infty\}.

Observe that for
d=3
,
\mathcal{X}
is not a vector space.
Moreover, if
d\geq 3
, then
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{X}
and the Lagrangian
E_{c}(u)
is well-defined for
u\in\mathcal{X}
.
While, for
d\in\{3,4\}
,
\mathcal{X}
can be also written as

\mathcal{X}=\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ :\ |1+u|^{2}-1\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\},

see also
[
11
]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Assume
d\geq 3
and let
(u_{n})\subset\mathcal{X}
be a sequence such that
(E_{GL}(u_{n}))
is bounded and

(1.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B(y,1)}|u_{n}|^{2}+\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\,dx=0.

Then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}E_{c}(u_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}E_{GL}(u_{n})+c\mathcal{Q}(u_{n})\geq 0
for any
c\in(0,\sqrt{2})
.

Remark 1.3
.

Recall that the classical version of the Lions’ lemma
[
15
]
states that if
(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B(y,1)}|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0,

then

(1.7)

u_{n}\to 0\quad\hbox{in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for
2<p<2^{*}
,
[
15
]
. Observe that, in a similar way as in Lemma
1.2
, we can replace (
1.7
) by the equivalent condition

\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(u_{n})\geq 0
, where

J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p}\,dx

is the energy functional associated to the problem

-\Delta u+u=|u|^{p-2}u
. Indeed,
note that if
(u_{n})
is such a sequence as above and (
1.7
) holds, then clearly
\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(u_{n})\geq 0
. On the other hand, suppose that
(u_{n})
is such a sequence as above, but
|u_{n}|_{p}
is bounded away from
0
passing a subsequence. Then

t_{n}^{p-2}:=(|\nabla u_{n}|_{2}^{2}+|u_{n}|_{2}^{2})/|u_{n}|_{p}^{p}
is bounded and bounded away from
0
. Observe that
J^{\prime}(t_{n}u_{n})(t_{n}u_{n})=0
,
(p^{1/(p-2)}t_{n}u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B(y,1)}|p^{1/(p-2)}t_{n}u_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0.
Then

J(p^{1/(p-2)}t_{n}u_{n})=-\frac{p^{2/(p-2)}}{2}(|\nabla(t_{n}u_{n})|_{2}^{2}+|t_{n}u_{n}|_{2}^{2})\leq-c<0

for a constant
c>0
, which contradicts the statement
\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(p^{1/(p-2)}t_{n}u_{n})\geq 0
.

In what follows
\lesssim
denotes the inequality up to a multiplicative constant. Moreover
C>0
denotes a generic constant that may vary from one line to another.

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H(u_{n})-H(m_{n}(y))|_{2,B(y,1)}\lesssim\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}-m_{n}(y)|_{2,B(y,1)}\to 0^{+}

again thanks to (
2.4
). Hence
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H(m_{n}(y))|_{2,B(y,1)}\to 0
and therefore
h_{n}\to 0^{+}
.

Hence, we may assume that
0<h_{n}<1
for any
n\geq 1
. Let
v_{n}
be the minimizer of
G_{h_{n}}^{u_{n}}
given by Lemma
2.2
(ii), it satisfies

-\Delta v_{n}+H(v_{n})+\frac{1}{h_{n}^{2}}\varphi^{\prime}(|v_{n}-u_{n}|^{2})(v_{n}-u_{n})=0

in the sense of distributions.

In view of the elliptic regularity, we follow arguments presented in
Step 2
and
Step 3
from
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
and we obtain that
v_{n}
are locally uniformly Hölder continuous with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
, namely there are
R>0
,
C_{*}>0
such that for any
n
there holds
|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|\leq C_{*}|x-y|^{1/2}
if
|x-y|<R
. Now, put

\delta_{n}:=\left||v_{n}+1|-1\right|_{\infty}.

Using Lipschitz-continuity of
z\mapsto(\varphi^{2}(|1+z|)-1)^{2}
, (
2.1
) and boundedness of
E_{GL}(u_{n})
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B(x,1)}\left|\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+v_{n}(y)|)-1\right)^{2}-\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}(y)|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(x,1)}|v_{n}(y)-u_{n}(y)|\,dy\lesssim|v_{n}-u_{n}|_{2,B(x,1)}\lesssim|v_{n}-u_{n}|_{2}\lesssim h_{n}^{2/d}.

Since
d\geq 3
, and using (
2.4
) we get that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B(x,1)}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+v_{n}(y)|)-1\right)^{2}\,dy\to 0.

Choose
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\left||v_{n}(x_{n})+1|-1\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta_{n}.

Then - from the local uniform Hölder continuity,
|v_{n}(x_{n})-v_{n}(x)|\leq C_{*}|x_{n}-x|^{1/2}
if
x\in B(x_{n},R)
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
R<1
. Set

r_{n}:=\min\left\{R,\left(\frac{\delta_{n}}{4C_{*}}\right)^{2}\right\}.

Then, if
|x_{n}-x|<r_{n}
, we get

\Big|\left||v_{n}(x_{n})+1|-1\right|-\left||v_{n}(x)+1|-1\right|\Big|\leq|v_{n}(x_{n})-v(x)|\leq C_{*}|x_{n}-x|^{1/2}<C_{*}r_{n}^{1/2}\leq\frac{\delta_{n}}{4}.

Hence

\left||v_{n}(x)+1|-1\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta_{n}

if
x\in B(x_{n},r_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B(x_{n},1)}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+v_{n}(y)|)-1\right)^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{n},r_{n})}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+v_{n}(y)|)-1\right)^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(x_{n},r_{n})}\eta\left(\frac{\delta_{n}}{4}\right)\,dy\gtrsim\eta\left(\frac{\delta_{n}}{4}\right)r_{n}^{d},

where
\eta
is given by

(2.6)

\eta(s):=\inf\{(\varphi^{2}(\tau)-1)^{2}:|1-\tau|\geq s\}.

Hence
\delta_{n}\to 0
.

Now we claim that

E_{GL}(v_{n})+c{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n})\geq 0

for large
n
. Fix
n
sufficiently large so that
1-\delta\leq|v_{n}+1|\leq 1+\delta
and
\delta<1
(note that
\delta
doesn’t depend on
n
). From Lemma
2.1
there are real valued functions
\rho_{n},\theta_{n}
such that
\rho_{n}-1\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\theta_{n}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
1+v_{n}=\rho_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}}
and

{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{n}^{2}-1)\frac{\partial\theta_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}\,dx.

Thus

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}(1-\delta)|{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1-\delta)\left|\frac{\partial\theta_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}\right|_{2}|\rho_{n}^{2}-1|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-\delta)^{2}\left|\frac{\partial\theta_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}\right|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\rho_{n}^{2}-1|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n}^{2}|\nabla\theta_{n}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(\rho_{n}^{2}-1)^{2}\,dx=E_{GL}(v_{n}).

Hence, for sufficiently large
n
and
|c|<\sqrt{2}
we get

E_{GL}(v_{n})+c{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n})\geq 0.

Now (
2.2
) implies that

\left|{\mathcal{Q}}(u_{n})-{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n})\right|\lesssim\left(h_{n}^{2}+(E_{GL}(u_{n}))^{2/d}h_{n}^{4/d}\right)^{1/2}E_{GL}(u_{n})\lesssim\left(h_{n}^{2}+M^{2/d}h_{n}^{4/d}\right)^{1/2}M\to 0,

where,
M:=\sup_{n}E_{GL}(u_{n})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\quad E_{c}(u_{n})=E_{GL}(u_{n})+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|-1\right)^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\underbrace{E_{GL}(v_{n})+c{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n})}_{\geq 0}+\underbrace{c({\mathcal{Q}}(u_{n})-{\mathcal{Q}}(v_{n}))}_{\to 0}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dx.

It is sufficient to show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dx\to 0.

Take any
\varepsilon>0
. Lemma
2.3
implies that

\left|V(|1+z|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(|1+z|^{2}-1\right)^{2}\right|\leq\varepsilon\left(|1+z|^{2}-1\right)^{2}

for
\left||1+z|-1\right|\leq\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\delta\leq 1
and therefore

\left|V(|1+z|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+z|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\leq\varepsilon\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+z|)-1\right)^{2}.

for
\left||1+z|-1\right|\leq\delta
. Thus

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\{||1+u_{n}|-1|\leq\delta\}}\left|V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\{||1+u_{n}|-1|\leq\delta\}}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon M.

Define
w_{n}:=||1+u_{n}|-1|
. Then
w_{n}\leq|u_{n}|
,
w_{n}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\lesssim 1.

In particular, from the continuity of the embedding
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(w_{n})
is bounded in
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Note that from (
2.3
),

\left|V(|1+z|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+z|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\leq\varepsilon\left||1+z|-1\right|^{2^{*}}+C_{\varepsilon}\left||1+z|-1\right|^{q}.

Thus

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\{||1+u_{n}|-1|>\delta\}}\left|V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\{w_{n}>\delta\}}|w_{n}|^{2^{*}}\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\{w_{n}>\delta\}}|w_{n}|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|w_{n}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\{w_{n}>\delta\}}|w_{n}|^{2^{*}}\,dx\right)^{q/2^{*}}|\{x\ :\ w_{n}(x)>\delta\}|^{1-q/2^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|w_{n}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}+C_{\varepsilon}C_{S}^{q}|\nabla w_{n}|_{2}^{q}|\{x\ :\ w_{n}(x)>\delta\}|^{1-q/2^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\underbrace{|w_{n}|_{2^{*}}^{2^{*}}}_{\mathrm{bounded}}+C_{\varepsilon}C_{S}^{q}M^{q/2}|\{x\ :\ w_{n}(x)>\delta\}|^{1-q/2^{*}},

where we note that
2<q<2^{*}
. We will show that for any
\delta>0

|\{x\ :\ w_{n}(x)>\delta\}|\to 0\quad\mbox{as }n\to\infty.

By contradiction, assume that (up to a subsequence),

|\{x\ :\ w_{n}(x)>\delta_{0}\}|\geq\gamma>0

for some
\delta_{0}
. Since
|\nabla w_{n}|_{2}
stays bounded, then - from Lieb’s Lemma (
[
14
, Lemma 6]
,
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
)

\beta:=\inf_{n}\left|\{x\ :\ w_{n}(x)>\delta_{0}/2\}\cap B(y_{n},1)\right|>0

for some
y_{n}
. However, then if
w_{n}(x)>\delta_{0}/2
, then

\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\geq\eta(\delta_{0}/2)>0,

where
\eta
is defined by (
2.6
). Hence

\int_{B(y_{n},1)}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\,dx\geq\eta(\delta_{0}/2)\beta>0

which is a contradiction with (
2.4
). This, by (
2.7
), proves that

\int_{\{||1+u_{n}|-1|>\delta\}}\left|V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dx\to 0

for any
\delta>0
and therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|V(|1+u_{n}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}(|1+u_{n}|)-1\right)^{2}\right|\,dx\to 0.

and the proof is completed.

\Box

3.
Variational approach and proof of Theorem

Before giving the proof of Theorem

, we state some preliminary results.
Let
\sigma>0

of the form

\mathcal{M}=\big\{u\in{\mathcal{X}}\setminus\{0\}\ :\ P_{c}(u)=0\big\},

where

\displaystyle P_{c}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{d-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}E_{c}(u_{1,\sigma})|_{\sigma=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{d-3}{2(d-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{x_{1}}u|^{2}+V(|1+u|^{2})\,dx+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{d-3}{2(d-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u|^{2}\,dx-L(u),

and

(3.1)

Observe that

\displaystyle P_{c}(u_{1,\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{d-3}{2(d-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u_{1,\sigma}|^{2}\,dx+\!\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{x_{1}}u_{1,\sigma}|^{2}\!+\!V(|1+u_{1,\sigma}|^{2})\,dx+c{\mathcal{Q}}(u_{1,\sigma})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{d-3}\biggl[\frac{d-3}{2(d-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u|^{2}\,dx-\sigma^{2}L(u)\biggr].

We will define

, the projection of

onto
\mathcal{M}
as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

and

Then
\mathcal{U}\neq\emptyset
and for any

We only need to prove that

, which is equivalent to find

.
From

, there exists a continuous map

from

Moreover we observe that arguing as in

Note that, for any

Thus, if

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

. For every

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}u|^{2}\,dx\geq K_{0}T_{c}^{2/(d-1)}L(u)^{(d-3)/(d-1)},

Take

\displaystyle T_{c}

\displaystyle t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle i\partial_{x_{1}}(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv),v\rangle+\langle i\partial_{x_{1}}v,\widetilde{u}_{n}\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle i\partial_{x_{1}}(\widetilde{u}+tv),v\rangle+\langle i\partial_{x_{1}}v,\widetilde{u}\rangle\,dx.

Moreover

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{x_{1}}(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)|^{2}-|\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}t|\partial_{x_{1}}v|^{2}+2\Re(\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{u}_{n}\partial_{x_{1}}v)\,dx

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}t|\partial_{x_{1}}v|^{2}+2\Re(\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{u}\partial_{x_{1}}v)\,dx.

In view of (F1) and (F2), note that there is a constant
C>
such that

|F(|1+s|^{2})||1+s|\leq C\big(||1+s|-1|+|s|^{2^{*}-1}\big)

for
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
by the mean value theorem, there is
\theta\in[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\big|V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv|^{2})-V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2})\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|F(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+\theta tv|^{2})\right||1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+\theta tv||tv|

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\big||1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+\theta tv|-1\big||tv|+|\widetilde{u}_{n}+\theta tv|^{2^{*}-1}|tv|\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\big||1+\widetilde{u}_{n}|-1\big||tv|+|tv|^{2}+(|\widetilde{u}_{n}|+|tv|)^{2^{*}-1}|tv|\big).

Since
(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}|-1)\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(\widetilde{u}_{n})\subset L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are bounded,
we infer that the family

\left\{V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv|^{2})-V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2})\right\}

is
uniformly integrable and by the Vitali’s convergence theorem

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv|^{2})-V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,v}V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv|^{2})-V(|1+\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(|1+\widetilde{u}+tv|^{2})-V(|1+\widetilde{u}|^{2})\,dx.

Therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(L(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)-L(\widetilde{u}_{n})\big)=L(\widetilde{u}+tv)-L(\widetilde{u}).

Note that
(L(\widetilde{u}_{n}))
is bounded since
(\widetilde{u}_{n})\subset{\mathcal{M}}
and

E_{c}(\widetilde{u}_{n})=\frac{1}{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx=\frac{2}{d-3}L(\widetilde{u}_{n}).

Passing to a subsequence, let us define
A:=\lim_{n\to\infty}L(\widetilde{u}_{n})
, hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}L(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)=A+L(\widetilde{u}+tv)-L(\widetilde{u}).

Moreover

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}

\displaystyle\biggl[L(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)^{(d-3)/(d-1)}-L(\widetilde{u}_{n})^{(d-3)/(d-1)}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\biggl[(A+L(\widetilde{u}+tv)-L(\widetilde{u}))^{(d-3)/(d-1)}-A^{(d-3)/(d-1)}\biggr]

Now we can pass to the limit for
t\to 0
, since
L
is differentiable in the
v
-direction, and we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\biggl[L(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)^{(d-3)/(d-1)}-L(\widetilde{u}_{n})^{(d-3)/(d-1)}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{d-3}{d-1}(A+L(\widetilde{u}+tv)-L(\widetilde{u}))^{-2/(d-1)}(A+L(\widetilde{u}+tv)-L(\widetilde{u}))^{\prime}\mid_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{d-3}{d-1}A^{-2/(d-1)}L^{\prime}(\widetilde{u})v.

Since
(\widetilde{u}_{n})\subset\mathcal{M}
and
(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)\subset\mathcal{U}
, we can apply Proposition
3.2
with
u=\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv
and
u=\widetilde{u}_{n}
, so that we get

\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)|^{2}-|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{1}{t}K_{0}T_{c}^{2/(d-1)}\biggl[L(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)^{(d-3)/(d-1)}-L(\widetilde{u}_{n})^{(d-3)/(d-1)}+o_{n}(1)\biggr]

Now, letting
n\to\infty
, and then letting
t\to 0
, in view of (
3.7
) we get

(3.8)

\lim_{t\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)|^{2}-|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{d-3}{d-1}K_{0}T_{c}^{2/(d-1)}A^{-2/(d-1)}L^{\prime}(\widetilde{u})v.

On the other hand,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}(\widetilde{u}_{n}+tv)|^{2}-|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2\langle\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}_{n},\nabla_{y}tv\rangle+t^{2}|\nabla_{y}v|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2\langle\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u},\nabla_{y}tv\rangle+t^{2}|\nabla_{y}v|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2\langle\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u},\nabla_{y}v\rangle\,dx.

Using (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and recalling the definitions of
A
and
K_{0}
, we finally get

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u},\nabla_{y}v\rangle\,dx\geq\frac{d-3}{d-1}K_{0}T_{c}^{2/(d-1)}A^{-2/(d-1)}L^{\prime}(\widetilde{u})v=L^{\prime}(\widetilde{u})v.

Since
v
is arbitrarily, we get

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u},\nabla_{y}v\rangle\,dx=L^{\prime}(\widetilde{u})v

for any
v\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
which means that
\widetilde{u}
is a weak solution to (
1.3
) and (c) is proved.

We will show that
\widetilde{u}\in W^{2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
q<\infty
. Indeed, note that since
\widetilde{u}
is a weak solution to (
1.3
), following
[
17
, Lemma 5.5]
and introducing
\widetilde{w}:=e^{\frac{-icx_{1}}{2}}(1+\widetilde{u})
we find that
\widetilde{w}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a distributional solution to

\displaystyle-\Delta\widetilde{w}-\frac{c^{4}}{4}\widetilde{w}=F\left(|\widetilde{w}|^{2}\right).

From Brezis-Kato’s result
[
7
]
,
[
19
, Lemma B.3]
, we obtain that
\widetilde{w}\in W^{2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
q<\infty
. Then also
\widetilde{u}\in W^{2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This regularity is enough to show that
\widetilde{u}
satisfies the Pohožaev identity, namely
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

Note that, from the proof above, it follows that
T_{c}
and
S_{c}^{(d-3)/(d-1)}
are attained, namely
T_{c}=E_{c}(\widetilde{u})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx=S_{c}^{(d-3)/(d-1)}
.
Indeed, since
(\widetilde{u}_{n})\subset\mathcal{M}
is a minimizing sequence for
T_{c}
, by the weak convergence
\widetilde{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we get

E_{c}(\widetilde{u})=\frac{1}{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx\leq E_{c}(\widetilde{u}_{n})\to T_{c}.

Moreover by the Pohožaev identity,
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathcal{M}}
and

T_{c}\leq E_{c}(\widetilde{u}),

thus the equality holds and
E_{c}(\widetilde{u})=T_{c}
, so that also
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{y}\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx=S_{c}^{(d-3)/(d-1)}
and
L(\widetilde{u})=-1
. Hence also
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}
.

Finally, observe that if
u
is a minimizer of (
1.5
), then by the above considerations, we get that
\widetilde{u}:=m_{{\mathcal{M}}}(u)
is a solution and we complete the proof.

\Box

Remark 3.4
.

Observe that in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we do not require any regularity properties and the bootstrap argument to solve (
1.3
), which appears to be difficult to carry out under the general growth condition (F2). However, we note that the solutions must belong to
W^{2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
q<\infty
in view of Brezis-Kato’s theorem, and this fact is only crucial for proving that a solution lies in
\mathcal{M}
.

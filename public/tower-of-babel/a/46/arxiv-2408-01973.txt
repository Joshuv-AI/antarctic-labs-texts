Abstract
. It is well known that the Navier-Stokes equations have unique global strong solutions for standard domains when initial data are small in L σ n L^{n}_{\sigma} . Global well-posedness has been extended to rough initial data in larger critical spaces. This paper explores the global strong solvability of the smooth exterior domain problem for initial data that is small in some critical spaces larger than L σ n L^{n}_{\sigma} . 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: primary 76D05, secondary 35Q30. Keywords and phrases: Navier-Stokes, exterior domain, critical space, rough data.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider initial-boundary value problem for Navier-Stokes equations in exterior domain
\Omega
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\partial_{t}{u}-\Delta{u}+\nabla p=-(u\cdot\nabla)u(=-{\rm div}({u}\otimes{u}))\,\,\mbox{ in }\,\,\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\mbox{\rm div}\,{u}=0\,\,\mbox{ in }\,\,\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\,\,\mbox{ for }\,\,t>0,\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{u}(x,t)=\,\,0\mbox{ for }t>0,\\
{u}|_{t=0}={u}_{0}\,\,\mbox{ in }\,\,\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here,
{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n})
and
p
denote unknown velocity and pressure, respectively, while
{u}_{0}
is a given initial velocity.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}
be the continuous projection operator onto solenoidal vector fields
and
A=-{\mathbb{P}}\Delta
.
The classical system (
1
) can be reduced into the integral equation

u(t)=e^{-tA}u_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{-tA(t-\tau)}{\mathbb{P}}(u\cdot\nabla u)(\tau)d\tau.

(1.6)

A solution
in
L^{q}(0,T;L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega))

satisfying (
1
) in distributional sense is called very weak solution and a solution
in
C(0,T;X)

satisfying (
1.6
) is called a mild solution in
X
.
Here,
X
denotes some Banach space with
{\mathbb{P}}X\subseteq X
.
It is well known that very weak solutions, mild solutions and the strong solutions coming from the same initial data coincide with each other as far as they are in
C(0,T;L^{p}_{\sigma}),p\geq n
(see
[
1
]
).
In this sense, we will not make a distinction between the types of solutions without further mention.

The solvability of the Navier-Stokes equations in the whole space has been well known for the initial data in
L^{p}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for any
p\geq n
(see
[
3
,
5
,
12
,
24
]
and references therein). The well-posedness for the data in
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
have been studied also for the other standard domains such as half spaces, bounded domains and exrerior domains (see
[
27
,
35
]
for the half spaces,
[
19
,
26
,
36
]
for bounded domains,
[
23
]
for the exterior domains). In those literatures, local solvability has been studied for
u_{0}\in L^{p}_{\sigma},p\geq n
, and global solution has been obtained if
u_{0}
is small in
L^{n}_{\sigma}
.

Rough computation shows that

\|e^{-(t-\tau)A}{\mathbb{P}}(u\cdot\nabla)u(\tau)\|_{L^{p}}\leq c(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2p}}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{p}}^{2},

and the right hand side is integrable in
\tau
variable for
p>n
but not for
p=n
.
In this sense, the space
L^{n}_{\sigma}
is critical.
The space
L^{n}_{\sigma}
is invariant under the scaling

\lambda v(\lambda x)
.
In general, scaling invariant spaces are called critical spaces.

If the data
u_{0}
is in the critical space
L^{n}_{\sigma}
, then we could obtain a closed form

\|u\|_{K}\leq c\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{n}}+\|u\|_{K}^{2}\big),

where
\|u\|_{K}=\sup_{0\leq t<T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{n}}+\sup_{0\leq t<T}t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2p}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}},p>n
.
With this argument T, Kato
[
24
]
showed the global in time solvability for the data small in
L^{n}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

The same argument can be applicable for any domain as far as
L^{r}-L^{p}
estimates for the Stokes semigroup operators have been obtained. Also, it can be extended to any critical space
X
if we could derive the estimates

\|e^{-tA}u_{0}\|_{K}\leq c\|u_{0}\|_{X}.

M. Cannone
[
6
]
showed the global solvability for the data small in the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,\infty,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),p>n
.
Observe that
|x|^{-1}\in\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),p>n
but
|x|^{-1}\notin L^{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
(See also
[
25
]
for the data in even the weaker space
{\rm BMO}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
).

The same result could be expected for the domains with non empty boundaries.
However, the non local properties of the Besov norm make it difficult to show

\|e^{-tA}u_{0}\|_{K}\leq c\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,\infty,\sigma}(\Omega)}\mbox{ when }\Omega\neq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Fortunately, the Stokes flow in the half space has explicit solution representation consisting of the compositions of the heat semigroup operators, Riesz operators and their convolutions. This helps M. Cannone, F. Planchon and M. Schonbek
[
7
]
to prove the global strong solvability
for the data small in
\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,\infty,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
,
p>n
.

Poincare’s inequality holds for the functions in bounded domains. This leads to an exponential decay estimate of the Stokes operator in a bounded domain and helps
H. Amann
[
1
]
to show the global strong solvability
for the data small in little Nikolskii space
{n}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,0,\sigma}(\Omega),p>n
.
Here
{n}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,0,\sigma}
is litte Nikolskii space (see
[
1
]
) satisfying the property
L^{n}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\subset{n}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)\subset B^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,\infty,\sigma}(\Omega),\,p>n

See also
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
for the data in
B^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,q,\sigma}(\Omega),\,\frac{n}{p}+\frac{2}{q}\leq 1,\,n<p<\infty,\,2<q\leq\infty
.

In
[
1
]
, the local strong solvability for
u_{0}\in{n}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,0,\sigma}(\Omega)
has been proved for any standard domains such as the whole space, the half space, bounded domains and exterior domains. However, global solvability has been shown for such domains except exterior domains.
M.-H. Ri, P. Zhang and Z. Zhang

[
29
]
proved the local and global solvability for the data in critical space

B^{0}_{n,\infty,\sigma}(\Omega)
, where exterior domains is not still inclued.

There are no such advantages in the exterior domain problem, and our aim of this paper is to obtain analogous result for the exterior domain problem.
Nonetheless, Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{1}_{p}(\Omega)\subset L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(\Omega),\,\,p<n
still holds for exterior domain. This let us have the interpolation properties

(L^{p}(\Omega),\dot{H}^{1}_{p}(\Omega))_{\alpha,1}=\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,1}(\Omega)
and
(L^{p}(\Omega),\dot{H}^{1}_{p,0}(\Omega))_{\alpha,1}=\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,1,0}(\Omega)
, for
p<n
.
Applying interpolation properties to
L^{p}-L^{r}
estimates of the Stokes operator we can derive the Stokes estimate from
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,1,0}(\Omega)
to
L^{r}
for
p<n
.
Making use of the duality argument we will obtain the estimate

\|e^{-tA}u_{0}\|_{K}\leq c\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\alpha}_{p,\infty,\sigma}(\Omega)},\quad p>\frac{n}{n-1}.

See Section
2
for the definitions of the function spaces mentioned in the above and in the below.

2.
Homogeneous spaces and their properties

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}},1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and let
\Omega
be domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We denote by
H^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
the usual (inhomogeneous) Bessel potentials and by
B^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)
Besov spaces.
We denote by
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
the set of infinitely differentiable functions compactly supported in
\Omega
. The completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
H^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
and
B^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)
are denoted by
H^{s}_{p,0}(\Omega)
and
B^{s}_{p,q,0}(\Omega)
, respectively. For the details of the definitions of the (inhomogeneous) spaces and thier properties see
[
1
,
2
,
37
]
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the Schwartz space, by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the set of tempered distributions.
Now, we introduce the homogeneous function spaces.
Homogeneous spaces are defined via Littlewood-Paley decompositions. Fix
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{1}(B_{1})
with
{\rm supp}\,\varphi\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ :2^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
,
\varphi(\xi)\geq 0
,
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-k}\xi)=1(\xi\neq 0)
.
Let
\ \varphi_{k},\ \psi_{k}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi_{k})=\varphi(2^{-k}\xi),\ {\mathcal{F}}(\psi_{k})=1-\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-l}\xi)
.
Here
{\mathcal{F}}(f)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The usual homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is defined by the set of functions
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
with
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\|\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Big(|\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{F}}(\phi_{j}*f\Big)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty
, and the usual homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is defined by the set of functions
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
with
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\Big(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|2^{sj}\phi_{j}*f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty
.
Then, the homogeneous spaces is semi-normed space and that
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=0
or
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=0
if and only if
f
is a polynomial. The usual homogeneous spaces is undersood as a quotient spaces modulo the spaces of the polynomials, and there holds several theories.
See
[
2
]
for the details of the definitions of the homogeneous spaces and its properties.

The definition of the usual homogeneous spaces by the qoutient spaces are not so realistic when we try to extend the notions to general domains.
H. Bahouri, J.Y-. Chemin and R. Danchin
[
9
]
introduced a modified definitions of the homogeneous spaces as follows:

\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}:\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\},\quad\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}:\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\},

where
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:\ \lim_{j\rightarrow-\infty}\|\psi_{j}*f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\rightarrow 0\}
. We observe that
S_{h}^{\prime}
does not contain any polynomials and that
\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
are normed spaces. In particular,
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset S_{h}^{\prime}
for sny
1\leq p<\infty
.

The followings are the known facts for the (modified) homogeneous spaces(see
[
9
]

for the homogeneous Besov spaces and
[
17
]
for the homogeneous Sobolev spaces):

Proposition 2.1
.

(i)

The (modified) homogeneous spaces are Banach sapces for
s\in{\mathbb{R}},1<p<\infty,\,1\leq q<\infty
with

[s<\frac{n}{p}]
or
[s=\frac{n}{p}
and
q=1]
.

(ii)

Define
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}:\ 0\notin\mbox{\rm supp}({\mathcal{F}}(f))\}.

Then, for any
1<p<\infty,\,1\leq q<\infty,\,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
is dense in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Let

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=
completion of
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
in
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Then

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subsetneq\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(iii)

For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
1<p<\infty,\,1\leq q<\infty
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and for all
u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|D^{m}_{x}u\|_{\dot{H}^{s-m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\partial_{x_{k}}^{m}f\|_{\dot{H}^{s-m}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|D^{m}_{x}u\|_{\dot{B}^{s-m}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\partial_{x_{k}}^{m}f\|_{\dot{B}^{s-m}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Here,

.

In particular,

\dot{H}^{0}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

(iv)

Let

and

. Then, the following embedding holds:

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\quad L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset\dot{H}^{-s}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\ \mbox{ for }q\in[1,r],

(v)

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
1<p<\infty
. Then,

\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

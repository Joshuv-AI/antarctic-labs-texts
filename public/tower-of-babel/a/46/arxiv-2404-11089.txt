Global solutions for semilinear parabolic evolution problems with Hölder continuous nonlinearities
Bogdan–Vasile Matioc, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. It is shown that semilinear parabolic evolution equations u ′ = A + f ⁡ ( t , u ) u^{\prime}=A+f(t,u) featuring Hölder continuous nonlinearities f = f ⁡ ( t , u ) f=f(t,u) with at most linear growth possess global strong solutions for a general class of initial data. The abstract results are applied to a recent model describing front propagation in bushfires and in the context of a reaction-diffusion system.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35A01; 35K58; 35Q92

1.
Introduction

For semilinear parabolic problems

u^{\prime}=Au+f(t,u)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)=u^{0}\,,

(1.1)

involving a generator
A
of an analytic semigroup on a Banach space
E_{0}
and a locally Lipschitz continuous nonlinearity
f
, there is a well-established theory
for well-posedness based on Banach’s fixed point theorem, e.g. see
[
1
, Section 12]
and the references therein.
The situation is different if
f=f(t,u)
is not locally Lipschitz with respect to
u
and less seems to be known.
If compactness properties are available, (versions of) Schauder’s fixed point theorem may be used to derive existence
– but not uniqueness – results, see e.g. the textbooks
[
9
,
10
]
or also
[
3
]
.
We also refer to
[
13
]
for the treatment of non-Lipschitz semilinear parabolic scalar equations by comparison principle arguments.

The aim of this paper is to establish the existence of strong solutions to (
1.1
)
in the context of nonlinearities
{f=f(t,u)}
with at most linear growth in
u
, which are not necessarily locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to the variable
u
,
under optimal (i.e. low) regularity assumptions on the initial value.

To be more precise, in the following let
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
be two Banach spaces over
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
with compact, continuous, and dense embedding

E_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mbox{\mbox{\raisebox{3.1298pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\subset\hskip-2.16238pt\subset$}}\hskip-4.83694pt$\longrightarrow$}}}\,E_{0}\,.

For each
\theta\in(0,1)
, let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
be an arbitrary admissible interpolation functor of
exponent
\theta
(see
[
2
, I. Section 2.11]
) and denote by
{E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}}
the corresponding interpolation space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
.
Then, the embeddings

E_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mbox{\mbox{\raisebox{3.1298pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\subset\hskip-2.16238pt\subset$}}\hskip-4.83694pt$\longrightarrow$}}}\,E_{\theta}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mbox{\mbox{\raisebox{3.1298pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\subset\hskip-2.16238pt\subset$}}\hskip-4.83694pt$\longrightarrow$}}}\,E_{\vartheta}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mbox{\mbox{\raisebox{3.1298pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\subset\hskip-2.16238pt\subset$}}\hskip-4.83694pt$\longrightarrow$}}}\,E_{0}\,,\qquad 0<\vartheta<\theta<1\,,

are all dense and compact.
We fix an infinitesimal generator
A:E_{1}\to E_{0}
of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup
{\{e^{tA}\,:\,t\geq 0\}}\subset{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}

and assume that
f:[0,T]\times E_{\xi}\to E_{\gamma}
for some
{\gamma,\,\xi\in[0,1)}
and
{T\in(0,\infty)}
.

We first point out the following result guaranteeing the global existence
of (at least) a global strong solution to (
1.1
) for initial data
u_{0}\in E_{\alpha}
for which

f(0,u_{0})
is defined, that is, for
\alpha\in[\xi,1)
, under the general assumption that
f(t,u)
grows at most linearly in
u
and is continuous
(along with an additional Hölder continuity property in the limiting case
\gamma=0
).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\gamma,\,\xi\in[0,1)
and assume that
f\in C\big([0,T]\times E_{\xi},E_{\gamma}\big)
is such that there is a constant
C>0
with

\|f(t,u)\|_{\gamma}\leq C\big(1+\|u\|_{\xi}\big)\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\quad u\in E_{\xi}\,.

(1.2)

If
\gamma=0
assume additionally that there exists
\vartheta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for each
R>0
there is a constant
{K(R)>0}
with

\displaystyle\|f(t,u)-f(s,v)\|_{0}\leq K(R)\big(|t-s|^{\vartheta_{0}}+\|u-v\|_{\xi}^{\vartheta_{0}}\big),\qquad t,s\in[0,T]\,,\quad u,v\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\xi}}(0,R)\,.

(1.3)

Then, if
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[\xi,1)
, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) admits a solution

u\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{\alpha}\big)\,.

The main purpose of this paper is to extend the existence theory for the evolution problem (
1.1
) by providing a global strong solution to (
1.1
)
for a general initial datum
u^{0}
in the ambient space
E_{0}
.
To this end we require, additionally to being linearly bounded in
u
, that
f
is locally Hölder continuous with respect to
u
.
More precisely, we fix

0\leq\gamma<1\,,\qquad 0<\xi<\min\Big\{1,\frac{1}{q}\Big\}\,,\qquad 0<\vartheta_{j}\leq q_{j}\,,\quad 1\leq j\leq m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\,,

(1.4a)

where

q:=\max\{q_{j}\,:\,1\leq j\leq m\}>0\,,

and assume that
f\in C\big((0,T]\times E_{\xi},E_{\gamma}\big)
is such that there exists a positive constant
C>0
with

\displaystyle\|f(t,u)\|_{\gamma}\leq C\big(1+\|u\|_{\xi}\big)

(1.4b)

and

\displaystyle\|f(t,u)-f(t,v)\|_{\gamma}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(1+\|u\|_{\xi}^{q_{j}-\vartheta_{j}}+\|v\|_{\xi}^{q_{j}-\vartheta_{j}}\big)\|u-v\|_{\xi}^{\vartheta_{j}}

(1.4c)

for all
{t\in(0,T]}
and
u,v\in E_{\xi}
.
In the particular case
\gamma=0
we additionally require (similarly as in Proposition
1.1
, but in a slightly weaker form) that there exists
\vartheta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for each
{R>0}
there is a constant
K(R)>0
with

\|f(t,u)-f(s,u)\|_{0}\leq K(R)\,|t-s|^{\vartheta_{0}},\qquad t,s\in(0,T]\,,\quad u\in E_{\xi}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{0}}(0,R)\,.

(1.5)

Our main result then reads:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose (
1.4
) and if
\gamma=0
also assume (
1.5
).
Then, given
u^{0}\in E_{0}
,
the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) possesses a global strong solution

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\,.

Furthermore, if
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\min\{1,1+\gamma-\xi\})
,
then
C\big([0,T],E_{\alpha}\big)
.

Theorem
1.2
provides strong solutions assuming minimal regularity on the initial value.
In particular,
f(0,u^{0})
needs not be defined under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
(in contrast to Proposition
1.1
).
For this reason, if
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}\setminus E_{\xi}
, the continuity in
E_{\alpha}
of the solution at
t=0
is guaranteed only for possibly restricted
\alpha
.

Remarks 1.3
.

(i)
If
1\leq\vartheta_{j}\leq q_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
, then
f=f(t,u)
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
u
and the classical theory on well-posedness for semilinear parabolic equations applies, see e.g.
[
1
]
for a general treatment (or
[
11
]
for a similar approach to the one chosen herein requiring minimal regularity assumptions on the initial value).

(ii)
In general, however, the nonlinearity
f=f(t,u)
in (
1.1
) is not locally Lipschitz continuous in
u
as
the exponents
\vartheta_{j}
in (
1.4c
) are allowed to be less than
1
.
Thus, our approach to Proposition
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, relying on the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem, does not provide uniqueness of solutions.

(iii)
The linear bound (
1.4b
) is not in contradiction with assumption (
1.4c
) (even if
q
is possibly larger than
1
).
This is well exemplified in Section
3
by application (
3.4
) with
\nu=1
.

Combining Proposition
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we may establish a global existence result for
{T=\infty}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\gamma,\,\xi\in[0,1)
,
f\in C\big([0,\infty)\times E_{\xi},E_{\gamma}\big)
, and assume that for each
T>0
there exists a constant
C(T)>0
with

\displaystyle\|f(t,u)\|_{\gamma}\leq c(T)(1+\|u\|_{\xi})\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\quad u\in E_{\xi}\,.

(1.6)

(a)
Let
\gamma>0
. Then (
1.1
) has a global strong solution

u\in C^{1}\big((0,\infty),E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,\infty),E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,\infty),E_{\alpha}\big)

provided either
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[\xi,1)
, or
f
satisfies (
1.4
) for some
T=T_{0}>0
and
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[0,\min\{1,1+\gamma-\xi\})
.

(b)
Let
\gamma=0
and suppose
there is
\vartheta_{0}\in(0,1)
and for
T,R>0
there is
K=K(T,R)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f(t,u)-f(s,v)\|_{0}\leq K\big(|t-s|^{\vartheta_{0}}+\|u-v\|_{\xi}^{\vartheta_{0}}\big)\,,\qquad t,s\in[0,T]\,,\quad u,v\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\xi}}(0,R)\,.

(1.7)

Then (
1.1
) has a global strong solution

u\in C^{1}\big((0,\infty),E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,\infty),E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,\infty),E_{\alpha}\big)

provided either
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[\xi,1)
, or

f
satisfies (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) for some
T=T_{0}>0
and
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[0,1-\xi)
.

The proofs of Proposition
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
, and Corollary
1.4
are presented in Section
2
.
In Section
3
we apply the abstract results to models describing front propagation in bushfires and to reaction-diffusion models.

2.
Proof of the main results

We provide first the details of the proof Theorem
1.2
(for which the arguments are a bit more delicate)
and subsequently only sketch the proofs of Proposition
1.1
and Corollary
1.4
.

Since
A
is the generator of the strongly continuous analytic semigroup
\{e^{tA}\,:\,t\geq 0\}
, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) can be formulated (under suitable assumptions on
u
and
f
, see e.g.
[
2
, II. Remarks 2.1.2 (a)]
) as the fixed point equation

u(t)=e^{tA}u^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)A}f(\tau,u(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

(2.1)

which we shall solve using the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Assuming the premises of Theorem
1.2
, we set

\vartheta:=\min\{\vartheta_{j}\,:\,1\leq j\leq m\}>0

and choose
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\xi<\mu<\min\Big\{1,\frac{1}{q}\Big\}\,.

(2.2)

We denote by

X_{T}:=C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\xi})

the Banach space consisting of all functions
{u\in C((0,T],E_{\xi})}
such that

t^{\mu}u(t)\rightarrow 0
in
E_{\xi}
as
t\rightarrow 0
, which is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{T}}:=\sup\big\{t^{\mu}\,\|u(t)\|_{\xi}\,:\,t\in(0,T]\big\}\,.

Our goal is to prove that the function
{F:X_{T}\to X_{T}}
,
defined by

F(u)(t):=e^{tA}u^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)A}f(\tau,u(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\quad u\in X_{T}\,,

(2.3)

satisfies the hypotheses of the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem
[
8
, Theorem 11.3]
and thus admits a fixed point in
X_{T}
, which is then shown to be a global strong solution to (
1.1
).

Well-definedness.
In order to establish that
F(u)\in X_{T}
for each
u\in X_{T}
, we first recall from
[
2
, II. Lemma 5.1.3]
the estimates

\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}+t^{\alpha-\beta_{0}}\,\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\beta},E_{\alpha})}\leq M\,,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T\,,

(2.4)

for
\theta\in[0,1]
and
0\leq\beta_{0}\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1

with
\beta_{0}<\beta
if
0<\beta<\alpha<1
, where
M=M(T)
depends also on these parameters.

Given
u,\,v\in X_{T}
with
\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq L
and
\|v\|_{X_{T}}\leq L
(for some arbitrary
L>0
), assumption (
1.4c
) implies

\displaystyle\|f(t,u(t))-f(t,v(t))\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq c(L)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(1+t^{-\mu(q_{j}-\vartheta_{j})}\big)\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{\xi}^{\vartheta_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq c(T,L)\,t^{-\mu q}\,\|u-v\|_{X_{T}}^{\vartheta}\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,

(2.5)

while (
1.4b
) yields

\displaystyle\|f(t,u(t))\|_{\gamma}\leq c(T,L)\,t^{-\mu}\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,.

(2.6)

Fix
u\in X_{T}
with
\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq L
(for some arbitrary
L
).
Let
\eta\in(0,1)
be given and define
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(\eta)
as

\gamma_{0}:=0
if
\gamma=0
and
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\min\{\gamma,\,\eta\})
if
\gamma>0
.

(2.7)

In view of (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|F(u)(t)\|_{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\eta})}\,\|u^{0}\|_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-\tau)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\eta})}\,\|f(\tau,u(\tau))\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq ct^{-\eta}\,\|u^{0}\|_{0}+c(T,L)\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\gamma_{0}-\eta}\tau^{-\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq c(T,L)\big(t^{-\eta}+t^{1+\gamma_{0}-\eta-\mu}{\sf B}(1+\gamma_{0}-\eta,1-\mu)\big)

(2.8)

for
t\in(0,T]
, where
{\sf B}
denotes the Beta function.
Therefore, for
\eta=\xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle t^{\mu}\|F(u)(t)\|_{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq c(T,L)\big(t^{\mu-\xi}+t^{1+\gamma_{0}-\xi}\big)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,

(2.9)

and the right hand side converges to zero as
t\to 0
since
\xi<\mu<1
.

Given
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
, set
u_{\varepsilon}(t):=u(t+\varepsilon)
for
t\in[0,T-\varepsilon]
.
Then,
u_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,T-\varepsilon],E_{\xi})
and the continuity of
f
implies
f(\cdot+\varepsilon,u_{\varepsilon})\in C([0,T-\varepsilon],E_{\gamma})
.
Moreover, the definition of
F(u)
entails

F(u)(t+\varepsilon)=e^{tA}F(u)(\varepsilon)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)A}f(\tau+\varepsilon,u_{\varepsilon}(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad t\in[0,T-\varepsilon]\,.

(2.10)

Hence,
[
2
, II.Theorem 5.3.1]
yields

F(u)(\cdot+\varepsilon)\in C\big((0,T-\varepsilon],E_{\theta}\big)\,,\qquad\theta\in(0,1)\,,\quad\varepsilon\in(0,T)\,,

that is,

F(u)\in C\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\,,\quad\theta\in(0,1)\,.

(2.11)

Together with (
2.9
) we conclude
F(u)\in X_{T}
.

Continuity.

Given
u,\,v\in X_{T}
with
\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq L
and
\|v\|_{X_{T}}\leq L
, we obtain in view of (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), similarly as above, that

\displaystyle\|F(u)(t)-F(v)(t)\|_{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-\tau)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\xi})}\,\|f(\tau,u(\tau))-f(\tau,v(\tau))\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq c\|u-v\|_{X_{T}}^{\vartheta}{\sf B}(1+\gamma_{0}-\xi,1-\mu q)t^{1+\gamma_{0}-\xi-\mu q},\qquad t\in(0,T],

(2.12)

with
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(\xi)
as in (
2.7
).
Since
\mu q<1
and
\xi<\mu<1
, we get

\|F(u)-F(v)\|_{X_{T}}\leq c_{1}(T,L)\,\|u-v\|_{X_{T}}^{\vartheta}\,.

This proves in particular the (Hölder) continuity of
F
.

Compactness.
We show that
F:X_{T}\to X_{T}
is compact.
Let therefore
(u_{k})_{k}
be a bounded sequence in
X_{T}
and choose
L>0
such that
\|u_{k}\|_{X_{T}}\leq L

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We prove that
(F(u_{k}))_{k}
is relatively compact in
X_{T}
by applying the
Arzelà-Ascoli theorem to the sequence
(v_{k})_{k}\subset C([0,T],E_{\xi})
defined as

v_{k}(t):=t^{\mu}F(u_{k})(t)\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Note that
v_{k}(0)=0
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
(v_{k}(t))_{k}
is bounded in
E_{\eta}
for
\eta\in(\xi,\mu]
by (
2.8
),
the compactness of the embedding
E_{\eta}\hookrightarrow E_{\xi}
ensures that
(v_{k}(t))_{k}
is relatively compact in
E_{\xi}
for each
{t\in[0,T]}
.

We next prove that the sequence
(v_{k})_{k}
is equi-continuous.
Given
0<s<t\leq T
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|v_{k}(t)-v_{k}(s)\|_{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq(t^{\mu}-s^{\mu})\|F(u_{k})(t)\|_{\xi}+s^{\mu}\int_{s}^{t}\|e^{(t-\tau)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\xi})}\,\|{f(\tau,u_{k}(\tau))}\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

(2.13a)

\displaystyle+s^{\mu}\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{(t-\tau)A}-e^{(s-\tau)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\xi})}\,\|{f(\tau,u_{k}(\tau))}\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+s^{\mu}\,\|(e^{tA}-e^{sA})u^{0}\|_{\xi}=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\,.

In the following, we simply write
c
for positive constants
c=c(T,L)
depending on
T
and
L
.
From (
2.9
), we obtain

\|F(u_{k})(t)\|_{\xi}\leq ct^{-\xi}\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\,,

and therefore

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq ct^{-\xi}(t^{\mu}-s^{\mu})\leq ct^{-\xi}(t-s)^{\mu}\leq c(t-s)^{\mu-\xi}\,.

(2.13b)

Using (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), we deduce

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq cs^{\mu}\int_{s}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\gamma_{0}-\xi}\,\tau^{-\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq cs^{\mu}(t-s)^{1+\gamma_{0}-\xi}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\gamma_{0}-\xi}(r(t-s)+s)^{-\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq c(t-s)^{1+\gamma_{0}-\xi}\,.

(2.13c)

Let now
\eta\in(\xi,\mu)
be fixed and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(\eta)
as in (
2.7
).
Since

\displaystyle\|e^{(t-s)A}-1\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\eta},E_{\xi})}\leq c(t-s)^{\eta-\xi}

(2.13d)

due to
[
2
, II. Theorem 5.3.1]
(with
f=0
therein),
we use (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) to derive

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq s^{\mu}\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{(t-s)A}-1\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\eta},E_{\xi})}\,\|e^{(s-\tau)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\eta})}\|f(\tau,u_{k}(\tau))\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq cs^{\mu}(t-s)^{\eta-\xi}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{\gamma_{0}-\eta}\,\tau^{-\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq cs^{1+\gamma_{0}-\eta}{\sf B}(1+\gamma_{0}-\eta,1-\mu)\,(t-s)^{\eta-\xi}

\displaystyle\leq c\,(t-s)^{\eta-\xi}\,.

(2.13e)

Finally, from (
2.13d
) and (
2.4
) we get

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq s^{\mu}\|e^{(t-s)A}-1\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\eta},E_{\xi})}\,\|e^{sA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\eta})}\|u^{0}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq cT^{\mu-\eta}(t-s)^{\eta-\xi}\,\|u^{0}\|_{0}\,.

(2.13f)

We thus conclude from (
2.13
) that the sequence
(v_{k})_{k}
is equi-continuous.
The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem now ensures (up to a subsequence) that
v_{k}\to v

in
{C([0,T],E_{\xi})}
for some function
v
with
v(0)=0.

Defining
u(t):=t^{-\mu}v(t),

t\in(0,T]
, we obtain
{F(u_{k})\to u}
in
X_{T}
.
This establishes the compactness of
F
.

A priori bound.
We prove that the set

S:=\big\{u\in X_{T}\,:\,\text{$u=\lambda F(u)$ for some $\lambda\in[0,1]$}\big\}

is bounded in
X_{T}
. To this end we infer from (
1.4b
) and (
2.4
) that for
u\in S
and
t\in(0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\xi}

\displaystyle\leq\|F(u)(t)\|_{\xi}\leq\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\xi})}\,\|u^{0}\|_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-\tau)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\xi})}\,\|f(\tau,u(\tau))\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq ct^{-\xi}+c\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\gamma_{0}-\xi}\big(1+\|u(\tau)\|_{\xi}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq ct^{-\mu}+c\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\gamma_{0}-\xi}\|u(\tau)\|_{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,

with
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(\xi)
defined as in (
2.7
).
The singular Gronwall inequality
[
2
, II.Corollary 3.3.2]
now implies the existence of a constant
K=K(T)>0

such that

\|u(t)\|_{\xi}\leq Kt^{-\mu}\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,

which entails the boundedness of
S
in
X_{T}
.

Existence of a solution.

Due to the above considerations, we may apply Leray-Schauder’s fixed point theorem
[
8
, Theorem 11.3]

to deduce that there exists
u\in X_{T}
such that
F(u)=u
.
It remains to prove that
u
is a strong solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

To this end, we note that if
u_{0}\in E_{\alpha}
and
\alpha\in[0,\min\{1,1+\gamma-\xi\})
, then
{u\in C([0,T],E_{\alpha})}
.
Indeed, the continuity of
u
on
(0,T]
is established in (
2.11

Since

,

\|f_{\nu}(t,u_{1})-f_{\nu}(t,u_{2})\|_{E_{\gamma}}\leq C(T)\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\vartheta_{1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{q_{2}-\vartheta_{2}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\vartheta_{2}}+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\vartheta_{3}}\big)

with

\vartheta_{1}:=1=:q_{1}\,,\quad 0\leq\vartheta_{2}:=\nu-1\leq q_{2}:=1\,,\quad 0\leq\vartheta_{3}:=2-\nu=:q_{3}\leq 1\,,

while,
for
\nu=1
, we have

\|f_{1}(t,u_{1})-f_{1}(t,u_{2})\|_{E_{\gamma}}\leq C(T)\big(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\vartheta_{1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{q_{1}-\vartheta_{1}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{E_{\xi}}^{\vartheta_{2}}\big)

with

\vartheta_{1}:=1=:q_{1}\,,\quad 0\leq\vartheta_{2}:=4\varepsilon/n\leq q_{2}:=1+\vartheta_{2}\,.

The largest value
q
of the exponents
q_{j}
is easily computed as

q:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1\,,&\nu\in(1,2]\,,\\[4.30554pt]
1+4\varepsilon/n\,,&\nu=1\,,\end{array}\right.

and, since
n\geq 2
and
2\varepsilon<1/7
, it holds that
\xi<1/q=\min\big\{1,1/q\big\}
.

This shows that assumption (
1.4
) is fulfilled in the context of (
3.7
) for each
T>0
.
We may thus apply Corollary
1.4
(a) with

\alpha:=\varepsilon\in[0,1/2-\varepsilon)=[0,\min\{1,1+\gamma-\xi\})
and deduce, for each
{u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}=L_{2}(\Omega)}
, the existence of a global strong solution

u\in C^{1}\big((0,\infty),E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,\infty),E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,\infty),E_{\alpha}\big)

to (
3.7
).

Regarding the uniqueness claim for
\nu=2
stated in (i), let
u_{1},\,u_{2}
be solutions to (
3.4
) as found in Theorem
3.1
such that
u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)
and choose an arbitrary
T>0
.
Using the Lions-Magenes lemma, see
[
4
, Theorem II.5.12]
,
we have for
0<\tilde{t}<t\leq T

\displaystyle\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(t)\|_{2}^{2}-\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(\tilde{t})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\tilde{t}}^{t}\|\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\tilde{t}}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}-u_{2})(\tau)\big[f_{2}(\tau,u_{1}(\tau))-f_{2}(\tau,u_{2}(\tau))\big]\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\tau\,.

Since
\|f_{2}(\tau,u_{1}(\tau))-f_{2}(\tau,u_{2}(\tau))\|_{2}\leq C(T)\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}
for
\tau\in(0,T]
due to Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
,
Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality, and the previous estimate lead, after taking the limit
\tilde{t}\to 0
, to

\displaystyle\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C(T)\int_{0}^{t}\|(u_{1}-u_{2})(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T\,.

The desired assertion follows now from Gronwall’s lemma.

Finally, let
\Theta\in C^{\vartheta}([0,\infty),L_{2}(\Omega))
and
\omega\in C^{\vartheta}([0,\infty),L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for some
\vartheta\in(0,1)
and fix
{\delta\in(0,1)}
.
Since
f(\cdot,u)\in C([\delta,\infty),L_{2}(\Omega))
,
u(\delta)\in H^{3/2}_{0}(\Omega)
, and
\Delta\in\mathcal{H}(H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),L_{2}(\Omega))
, we infer from
[
2
, II.Theorem 5.3.1]
that

u\in C^{\rho}([\delta,\infty),E_{\xi})
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
.
This property together with Lemma
3.3
-Lemma
3.5
show that
f(\cdot,u)\in C^{\rho^{\prime}}([\delta,\infty),L_{2}(\Omega))
for some
{\rho^{\prime}\in(0,\rho)}
.
In view of
[
2
, II.Theorem 1.2.1]
we get
u\in C^{1}\big((\delta,\infty),L_{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big((\delta,\infty),H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\big)

and, since
\delta\in(0,1)
is chosen arbitrarily, this establishes (ii),
and the proof of Theorem
3.1
is complete.∎

3.3.
Dynamics of an isothermal, autocatalytic chemical reaction scheme

We consider a mathematical model for the dynamics of an isothermal, autocatalytic chemical
reaction scheme with termination, taking place in an unstirred environment and undergoing
molecular diffusion investigated in a one-dimensional setting in
[
12
]
.
The governing equations are summarized by the system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u

\displaystyle=-u_{+}^{\mu}v_{+}^{\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptt>0\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

(3.8a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\Delta v

\displaystyle=u_{+}^{\mu}v_{+}^{\beta}-av_{+}^{\theta}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptt>0\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

(3.8b)

where
u
and
v
are concentrations of the
reactant and the autocatalyst, respectively,
a
is a (positive) constant, and the constants
\mu,\,\beta,\,\theta\in(0,1]
are assumed to satisfy
\mu+\beta\leq 1
.
As before, we set
{r_{+}:=\max\{0,r\}}
for
{r\in\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, is an open and bounded set with smooth boundary and outward unit normal
\nu
.

We supplemented (
3.8a
)-(
3.8b
) by homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=0\qquad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\,,\quad t>0\,,

(3.8c)

and impose the initial condition

u(0,x)=u^{0}(x)\,,\quad v(0,x)=v^{0}(x)\,,\qquad x\in\Omega\,.

(3.8d)

In the context of the evolution problem (
3.8
) we chose the Hilbert spaces
H^{2\theta}_{N}(\Omega)
, which are defined by (
3.2
) with
{\mathcal{B}u=\partial_{\nu}u}
, to establish the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu,\,\beta,\,\theta\in(0,1]
satisfy
\mu+\beta\leq 1.

Then, given
u^{0}\in L_{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a global strong solution to (
3.8
) such that

(u,v)\in C^{1}\big((0,\infty),L_{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)\cap C\big((0,\infty),H^{2}_{N}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)\cap C\big([0,\infty),L_{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)\,.

(3.9)

Proof.

Set

E_{0}:=L_{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\,,\qquad E_{1}:=H^{2}_{N}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})\,,

and
\xi:=\gamma:=0
.
The evolution problem (
3.8
) may be recast
as the semilinear parabolic Cauchy problem

w^{\prime}=Aw+f(w)\,,\quad t>0\,,\qquad w(0)=w^{0}:=(u^{0},v^{0})\,,

(3.10)

where

A:=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&\Delta\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\,,

see
[
2
, I. Theorem 1.6.1]
and (
3.1
)-(
3.2
), and
f:E_{0}\to E_{0}
is given by

f(w):=(-u_{+}^{\mu}v_{+}^{\beta},u_{+}^{\mu}v_{+}^{\beta}-av_{+}^{\theta}),\qquad w=(u,v)\in E_{0}\,.

In fact, using Hölder’s inequality, for
w=(u,v)\in E_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\|v_{+}^{\theta}\|_{2}\leq|\Omega|^{(1-\theta)/2}\|v\|_{2}^{\theta}\leq|\Omega|^{(1-\theta)/2}\|w\|_{E_{0}}^{\theta}\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|u_{+}^{\mu}v_{+}^{\beta}\|_{2}\leq|\Omega|^{(1-\beta-\mu)/2}\|u\|_{2}^{\mu}\|v\|_{2}^{\beta}\leq|\Omega|^{(1-\beta-\mu)/2}\|w\|_{E_{0}}^{\mu+\beta}\,,

which shows that
f
is well-defined and satisfies

\|f(w)\|_{E_{0}}\leq C\big(1+\|w\|_{E_{0}}\big),\qquad w\in E_{0}\,.

(3.12)

Moreover, (
3.11
) together with the inequality

|a_{+}^{\vartheta}-b_{+}^{\vartheta}|\leq|a-b|^{\vartheta}\,,\qquad a,\,b\in\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\vartheta\in(0,1]\,,

shows that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
w,\,\tilde{w}\in E_{0}
we have

\|f(w)-f(\tilde{w})\|_{E_{0}}\leq C(\|w-\tilde{w}\|_{E_{0}}^{\theta}+\|w\|_{E_{0}}^{\mu}\|w-\tilde{w}\|_{E_{0}}^{\beta}+\|w\|_{E_{0}}^{\beta}\|w-\tilde{w}\|_{E_{0}}^{\mu})\,.

(3.13)

In view of (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we may thus apply Corollary
1.4
(b) (with
\alpha=\xi=\gamma=0
) in the context of the evolution problem (
3.10
) to deduce, for each
w^{0}\in E_{0}
,
the existence of a strong solution to (
3.8
) with the required regularity
property (
3.9
).
∎

There are other examples to which the abstract results Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.4
apply, e.g., to reaction–diffusion equations of the form

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=u|u|^{p-1}\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

with
p\in(0,1)
, see
[
5
]
and the references therein.
In this case, the function
f:L_{2}(\Omega)\to L_{2}(\Omega)
with
{f(u):=u|u|^{p-1}}
is linearly bounded
and uniformly
p
–Hölder continuous.

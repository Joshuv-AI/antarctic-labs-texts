From homogeneity to discrete homogeneity
Vitalij A. Chatyrko and Alexandre Karassev

Abstract
. This is a survey of recent and classical results concerning various types of homogeneity, such as n n -homogeneity, discrete homogeneity, and countable dense homogeneity. Some new results are also presented, and several problems are posed.

1.
Homogeneous spaces

Definition 1.1
.

A topological space
X
is called homogeneous if for any points
x,y
of
X
there exists a homeomorphism
f:X\to X
such that
f(x)=y
.

Note that any homogeneous space is
similar
around all its points from the topological point of view.

Example 1.2
.

The following spaces are not homogeneous:

(a)

Any space which has both isolated points and non-isolated points.

(b)

Any closed bounded interval of the real line
\mathbb{R}
.

(c)

Any space
X
which has points
p,q
such that

\mathrm{ind}\,_{p}X\neq\mathrm{ind}\,_{q}X
.

Example 1.3
.

The following spaces are homogeneous.

(a)

Any discrete topological space.

(b)

The real line
\mathbb{R}
and its subspace
\mathbb{Q}
of rational numbers.

(c)

The n-dimensional sphere
S^{n},n\geq 1
.

(d)

The Sorgenfrey line
\mathbb{S}
(the set of real numbers with the base of topology provided by intervals
[a,b)
).

The following statement is obvious.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\{X_{\alpha},\alpha\in A\}
, is a family of homogeneous spaces then the topological product

\prod_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}
is also a homogeneous space.

Example 1.5
.

The following are examples of homogeneous spaces which are homeomorphic to products of homogeneous spaces.

(a)

The space
\mathbb{P}
of irrational numbers which is homeomorphic to
Z^{\omega}
, where
Z
is the space of integer numbers.

(b)

The Cantor set
\mathbb{C}
(since
\mathbb{C}
is homeomorphic to
D^{\omega}
, where
D
is the two-points discrete space).

(c)

The topological products
\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{P}
.

Example 1.6
.

The following spaces are also homogeneous:

(a)

The Hilbert cube
[0,1]^{\omega}
.

(b)

Erdös spaces
E(\mathbb{Q})
and
E(\mathbb{P})

(where
E(\mathbb{X})=\{\overline{x}\in l_{2}:x_{i}\in\mathbb{X}\subset\mathbb{R}\}
and
l_{2}
is the real separable Hilbert space).

(c)

Any topological group
G
.

Note that the spaces
\mathbb{S}
,
[0,1]^{\omega}
, and
S^{n},n\neq 1,3
, are not topological groups unlike to other examples mentioned above.

2.
Generalizations of homogeneity

Remark 2.1
.

One can substitute in Definition
1.1
the points
x,y
with the sets
A,B
from a given non-empty class
\mathcal{C}
of subsets of
X
, and require the homeomorphism
f\colon X\to X
to be such that
f(A)=B
.

Note the following natural restrictions on the sets
A
and
B
for such a homeomorphism to exist:
A\simeq B
and
X\setminus A\simeq X\setminus B
, where
\simeq
means homeomorphic. In particular,

|A|=|B|
and
|X\setminus A|=|X\setminus B|.

Further, we will discuss stronger forms of homogeneity based on Remark
2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. In 1954 Burgess
[
Bu
]
called a topological space
X

n
-homogeneous
if
for any two subsets
A
and
B
of
X
of cardinality
n
there exists a homeomorphism
f\colon X\to X
, such that
f(A)=B
. Note that the
1
-homogeneous spaces are homogeneous spaces in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Further, he called
X

strongly
n
-homogeneous
if
any bijection
f\colon A\to B
between subsets
A
and
B
of
X
of cardinality
n
extends to a homeomorphism
f\colon X\to X
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
n
be an integer
>1
and
X
a topological space. Then the following is valid.

(a)

If
X
is strongly
n
-homogeneous then
X
is strongly
(n-1)
-homogeneous.

(b)

If
X
is strongly
n
-homogeneous then
X
is
n
-homogeneous.

(c)

If
X
is
n
-homogeneous then
X
is
1
-homogeneous, i.e. homogeneous (see
[
Bu
, Theorem 1]
).

Question 2.3
.

Are there an integer
n\geq 3
and a
n
-homogeneous space which is not
2
-homogeneous?

Example 2.4
.

(a)

The real line
\mathbb{R}
is
n
-homogeneous for every integer
n\geq 1
, strongly

2
-homogeneous, and not strongly
3
-homogeneous.

(b)

The circle
S^{1}
is
n
-homogeneous for every integer
n\geq 1
, strongly

3
-homogeneous, and not strongly
4
-homogeneous.

(c)

Any connected
k
-dimensional manifold (in particular, the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and the sphere
S^{k}
),
k\geq 2,
is strongly
n
-homogeneous for every integer
n\geq 1

(see
[
Ba
, Theorem 14]
).

Let us add that
if
X
is an
n
-homogeneous compact metric connected space then
X
is strongly

n
-homogeneous or it is the circle
S^{1}

[
U
, Theorem 3.11]
, and
there is a connected Lindelöf space that is
n
-homogeneous for every positive integer
n
and not strongly
2
-homogeneous
[
vM2
, Example]
.

Question 2.5
.

Is there strongly
4
-homogeneous space which is not strongly
5
-homogeneous?

Example 2.6
.

The topological union
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}
is
1
-homogeneous
but not
2
-homogeneous.

Question 2.7
.

Is there
2
-homogeneous space which is not
3
-homogeneous?

It is well-known that any finite dimensional metrizable locally connected locally compact group is a Lie group
[
MZ
]
and hence if it is connected and has dimension
\geq 2
then it is strongly
n
-homogeneous for any
n
. However, the answer to the following question is unknown to the authors.

Question 2.8
.

Are there connected and locally connected topological groups which are not
n
-homogeneous for some
n>1
?

3.
A generalization of the (strong)
n
-homogeneity

A family of subsets
\mathcal{D}
of a topological space
X
is

discrete
if each point
x\in X
has a neighborhood
Ox
which intersects at most one set from
\mathcal{D}
. If each element of
\mathcal{D}
is a singleton, we obtain the definition of
a discrete subset
of
X

as the union of these singletons.

Note that each discrete subset of
X
is closed, and any finite subset of
X
is discrete.

In 2022 the authors
[
ChK1
]
suggested the following notions.

A Hausdorff topological space
X
is called

•

discrete homogeneous
(
DH
), if for any two discrete subsets
A
and
B
of
X
with
|A|=|B|
there exists a homeomorphism

f
of
X
onto itself such that
f(A)=B
;

•

strongly discrete homogeneous
(
sDH
), if for any two discrete subsets
A
and
B
of
X
and any bijection
f\colon A\to B
this bijection extends to a homeomorphism of the whole
X
onto itself.

Some simple facts from
[
ChK1
]
are mentioned next.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space. Then the following is valid.

(a)

If
X
is a
sDH
space then
X
is a
DH
space.

(b)

If
X
is a
sDH
space then
X
is strongly
n
-homogeneous for every
n\geq 1
.

(c)

If
X
is a
DH
space then
X
is
n
-homogeneous for every
n\geq 1
.

Example 3.2
.

(a)

Any infinite discrete space is strongly
n
-homogeneous for every
n\geq 1
but not a
DH
-space, and hence not an
sDH
space.

(b)

The real line
\mathbb{R}
is
n
-homogeneous for every
n\geq 1
but not a
DH
-space.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space. Then:

(a)

X
is a
sDH
-space iff it is strongly
n
-homogeneous for every
n\geq 1
;

(b)

X
is a
DH
-space iff it is
n
-homogeneous for every
n\geq 1
.

Example 3.4
.

The circle
S^{1}
is
DH
but not
sDH
.

Question 3.5
.

Does there exist a noncompact metric space which is
DH
but not
sDH
?

4.
Zero-dimensional
sDH
spaces

In 1981 van Mill introduced
the strongly homogeneous spaces
as those all nonempty clopen subspaces of which are homeomorphic. Any connected space is evidently strongly homogeneous. Therefore this concept is of interest only in the case of disconnected spaces.

The following result was obtained by van Mill.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
vM1
, Theorem 3.1]
) Every homeomorphism between two closed nowhere dense subsets of a strongly homogeneous separable metrizable zero-dimensional space
X
can be extended to an automorphism of
X
. In particular, the space
X
is homogeneous.

Note that the spaces
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{P}
,
{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathbb{Q}
,
{\mathbb{P}}\times\mathbb{Q}
are strongly homogeneous
[
vM1
]
, separable metrizable and zero-dimensional. Since each discrete subset of a space without isolated points is closed and nowhere dense we get that the spaces
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{P}
,
{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathbb{Q}
,
{\mathbb{P}}\times\mathbb{Q}
are sHD.

In 2022 the authors
[
ChK1
]
defined
a property
\mathcal{D}
as follows.
A Hausdorff space
X
possesses the property
\mathcal{D}
if for any proper clopen homeomorphic subspaces
F
and
G
of
X
the subspaces
X\setminus F
and
X\setminus G
are also homeomorphic.

Note that the spaces
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{P}
,
{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathbb{Q}
,
{\mathbb{P}}\times\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{S}
have the property
\mathcal{D}
.
Observe also that all mentioned above spaces are paracompact, zero-dimensional in the sense of dimension
\mathrm{ind}\,
(i.e. they have topology bases consisting of clopen sets) and homogeneous.
Furthermore, there are other zero-dimensional in the sense of dimension
\mathrm{ind}\,
, paracompact, homogeneous spaces possessing property
\mathcal{D}
(one of them is a van Douwen’s subgroup
H
of the circle group from
[
vD
]
which is not strongly homogeneous).

Recall that a topological space
X
is called
collectionwise normal
if
X
is
T_{1}
and for every discrete family
\{F_{s}\}_{s\in S}
of closed subsets of
X
there exists a discrete family
\{V_{s}\}_{s\in S}
of open subsets of
X
such that
F_{s}\subset V_{s}
for every
s\in S
.

It is a well-known fact that every paracompact space is collectionwise normal.

Theorem 4.2
.

(
[
ChK1
, Theorem 4.2]
) Let
X
be a collectionwise normal zero-dimensional in the sense of
\,\mathrm{ind}\,
homogeneous topological space with the property
\mathcal{D}
. Then
X
is
sDH
.

In particular, the spaces
\mathbb{S}
and
H
are sDH.

Since each zero-dimensional in the sense of dimension
\mathrm{ind}\,
first countable strongly homogeneous space is homogeneous, we have the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

(
[
ChK1
, Corollary 2.7]
) Let
X
be a collectionwise normal first countable strongly homogeneous topological space with
\mathrm{ind}\,X=0.
Then
X
is sDH.

Note that
\mathbb{X}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{X}
, whenever
X
is
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{P}
,
{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathbb{Q}
,
{\mathbb{P}}\times\mathbb{Q}
and
n
is any poitive integer.
Since
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is not normal for
n>1
,
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is not homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}
for
n>1
.

Proposition 4.4
.

(
[
ChK1
, Proposition 4.4]
) For each
n>1
the space
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is not
DH
.

We do not know if
H\simeq H^{2}
.

Are

(s)DH

Note that
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
and
\mathbb{P}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{\omega}
but

Are

Since

Do

The following notion from

]
was motivated by Theorem 4.1.

Let
X
be a topological space and the class of all closed nowhere dense subsets of

is not empty.
The space

is called

nowhere dense homogeneous
(NDH), if for any two closed nowhere dense subsets
F
and

of

Theorem 4.1 implies the following.

Proposition 4.8
.

Every strongly homogeneous separable metrizable zero-dimensional space is NDH.

Corollary 4.9

The spaces

\mathbb{Q}

{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathbb{Q}

The following proposition together with Corollary

Proposition 4.10
.

Let

Proof.

Note that the class of all closed nowhere dense subsets of

.

Are the Sorgenfrey line

.

Let

5.
Non-zero-dimensional
sDH

Proposition 5.1
.

(
[
ChK1
, Propositions 2.14 and 2.15]
) Every finite-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n},n>1,
is
an sDH space.

Proposition 5.2
.

Every finite-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2,
is not NDH.

Proof.

To see that consider the closed nowhere dense subset
X=(\{0\}\cup\{1\}\cup\{2\})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and a homeomorphism
f:X\to X
such that
f(0,\overline{x})=(0,\overline{x})
,

f(1,\overline{x})=(2,\overline{x})
and
f(2,\overline{x})=(1,\overline{x})
for any
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. It is easy to see that

cannot be extended to an automorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In fact, if such automorphism
\bar{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
exists and

\{0\}\times R^{n-1}
and
\{1\}\times R^{n-1}

then
\bar{f}(I)\cap\bar{f}(\{2\}\times R^{n-1})\neq\emptyset
. We have a contradiction.
∎

.

(

The separable Hilbert space

Proof.

Represent

6.
Charakterization of
sDH
(DH) connected metrizable manifolds of dimension

.

Note that every connected compact metrizable
k
-manifold,

, is

(see Example

In 2023 the authors
[
ChK2
]
introduced the following notions.

A space is called
generalized continuum
if it is locally compact,
\sigma
-compact, non-compact, connected, and locally connected Hausdorff space.

Let
X
be a generalized continuum.
There exists a sequence (generally speaking, not unique) of compact subsets
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
X
such that
X_{n}\subset{\rm int}X_{n+1}
for all
n=1,2,\dots
and
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}=X
.

Further,
by
an end
of
X
with respect to
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
we mean a sequence
\{Y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that for all
n
the set
Y_{n}
is a non-empty connected component of
X\setminus X_{n}
and
Y_{n+1}\subset Y_{n}
.

Note that the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as well as the product
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
are generalized
continua. Moreover, the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
has one end and the space
S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
has two ends.

This distinction can be extended to the following fact.

Theorem 6.1
.

(
[
ChK2
, Theorem 4.3]
) A connected non-compact metrizable manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
is
sDH
(
DH
) iff
it has one end.

.

Let
X
be a metrizable strongly locally homogeneous generalized continuum with one end. Is
X
(s)DH?

7.
Other generalization of homogeneity

A separable space
X
is called CDH if for any two countable dense subsets
A,B
of
X
there exists a homeomorphism
f:X\to X
such that
f(A)=B
.

The CDH-spaces do not need to be homogeneous (for example, the topological sum
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{P}
) but if a CDH space is connected then it is homogeneous. In 2012 van Mill
[
vM2
]
proved that connected CDH spaces are even n-homogeneous for each positive integer
n
, and there exists a connected Lindelöf CDH space
M
which is not strongly
2
-homogeneous.

Question 7.1
.

Is the space
M

DH
?

In 1975 Ungar
[
U
]
proved for a locally compact separable metrizable space
X
such that no finite set separates it the equivalence of the following conditions:

(a)

X
is CDH,

(b)

X
is
n
-homogeneous for every positive integer
n
,

(c)

X
is strongly
n
-homogeneous for every positive integer
n
.

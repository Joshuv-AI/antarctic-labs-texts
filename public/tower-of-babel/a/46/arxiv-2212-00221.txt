2.
Convex sets and polyhedra

A subset
X
of a real vector space
V
is
convex

if
\lambda_{1}\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{x}_{2}\in X

for all vectors
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in X
and all nonnegative
numbers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
such that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
.
The intersection of convex subsets of
V
is a convex set in
V
.
For example, for every positive integer
k
, the set

\Delta_{k}=\left\{\mathbf{\lambda}=\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{k}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{k}:\mathbf{\lambda}\geq\mathbf{0}\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1\right\}

is a convex subset of
\mathbf{R}^{k}
.
The set
X
is convex if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\in X

for all
k\geq 1
,
(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{k})\in X^{k}
,
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Delta_{k}
.
We call
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\in X
a
convex combination

of the
k
-tuple
(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{k})
.

The intersection of convex subsets of a real vector space
V
is convex.
The
convex hull
of a nonempty subset
W

of
V
is the intersection of all convex subsets of
V
that contain
W
.
This is the set
X=\conv(W)
of all convex combinations of
k
-tuples of elements of
W

for all
k\geq 1
.
For
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, the standard basis vector
\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{k}
is the vector
whose
j
th coordinate is 1 and
whose
i
th coordinate is 0 for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}\setminus\{j\}
.
The set
\Delta_{k}
is the convex hull of the set
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{k}\}
.
We define
\conv(\emptyset)=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

The
closed half-space

in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
defined by the vector
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and the scalar
b\in\mathbf{R}

is the set

H(\mathbf{a},b)=\left\{\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:\mathbf{a}^{t}\mathbf{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\leq b\right\}.

If
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in H(\mathbf{a},b)
and if
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
, then

\mathbf{a}^{t}\left(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{x}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{y}\right)=\lambda_{1}\mathbf{a}^{t}\mathbf{x}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{a}^{t}\mathbf{y}\leq\lambda_{1}b+\lambda_{2}b=b

and so
\lambda_{1}\mathbf{x}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{y}\in H(\mathbf{a},b)
.
Thus, every closed half-space is convex.

If
\mathbf{a}\neq 0
, then
H(\mathbf{a},b)\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}
and
H(\mathbf{a},b)\neq\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
We have

H(\mathbf{0},b)=\emptyset
if
b<0
and
H(\mathbf{0},b)=\mathbf{R}^{n}
if
b\geq 0
.

A
polyhedron
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is the intersection
of a finite number of closed half-spaces, that is, a set
P
of vectors

\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn
whose coordinates satisfy
a finite number
m
of linear inequalities of the form
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{j}\leq b_{i}
.
These inequalities define the
m\times n
matrix
A=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i,j}\end{matrix}\right)

and the vector
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallbm\in\mathbf{R}^{m}
, which
generate the polyhedron
P
as follows:

P=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}\right\}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}H(\mathbf{a}_{i},b_{i})

where
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{t}
is the
i
th row vector of
A
.
If
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in P
and if
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1

with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
, then

A\left(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{x}_{2}\right)=\lambda_{1}A\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda_{2}A\mathbf{x}_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}\mathbf{b}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}

and so every polyhedron is convex.

The inequality
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\geq b
is equivalent to the inequality

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-a_{j})x_{j}\leq-b
, and the equation
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}=b

is equivalent to the two inequalities
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\leq b

and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-a_{j})x_{j}\leq-b
.
Thus, the set of vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
that satisfy a finite number of
inequalities of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\leq b
, a finite number of
inequalities of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}\geq b
, and a finite number of equations

\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}=b
is a polyhedron.

Lemma 1
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be real vector spaces and let
T:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
be a linear transformation.
If
X
is a convex subset of
V
, then
Y=T(X)
is a convex subset of
V^{\prime}
.
In particular, the projection of a convex set in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a convex set.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\in Y

and
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\Delta_{2}
.
There exist
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in X
such that
T(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\mathbf{y}_{1}
and
T(\mathbf{x}_{2})=\mathbf{y}_{2}
.
If
X
is convex, then
\lambda_{1}\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{x}_{2}\in X
and so

\lambda_{1}\mathbf{y}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{y}_{2}=\lambda_{1}T\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}\right)+\lambda_{2}T\left(\mathbf{x}_{2}\right)=T\left(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{x}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{x}_{2}\right)\in T(X)=Y.

Thus,
Y
is convex.

Every projection
\pi_{n,k}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n-k}
is a linear transformation
and so the projection of a convex set is convex.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 1
.

The projection of a polyhedron is a polyhedron.

Proof.

Identity (
1
) implies that it suffices to prove the Theorem for
n\geq 2
and
k=1
.
The projection
\pi_{n,1}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
is defined by

\pi_{n,1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Let
P=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}\}
be the polyhedron in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
defined by the
m\times n
matrix
A=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i,j}\end{matrix}\right)

and the vector
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallbm\in\mathbf{R}^{m}
.
We have
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in P
if and only if the following
m

\displaystyle a_{1,1}x_{1}+a_{1,2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{1,n}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq b_{1}

\displaystyle a_{2,1}x_{1}+a_{2,2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{2,n}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq b_{2}

\displaystyle a_{m,1}x_{1}+a_{m,2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{m,n}x_{n}

\displaystyle\leq b_{m}

Partition the set
S=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
as follows:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\{p\in S:a_{p,1}>0\}

\displaystyle S_{-1}

\displaystyle=\{q\in S:a_{q,1}<0\}

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=\{r\in S:a_{r,1}=0\}.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in P
. If
p\in S_{1}
, then

x_{1}\leq-\frac{a_{p,2}}{a_{p,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{p,n}}{a_{p,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{p}}{a_{p,1}}.

If

, then

-\frac{a_{q,2}}{a_{q,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{q,n}}{a_{q,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q,1}}\leq x_{1}.

For all

and

-\frac{a_{q,2}}{a_{q,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{q,n}}{a_{q,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q,1}}\leq x_{1}\leq-\frac{a_{p,2}}{a_{p,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{p,n}}{a_{p,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{p}}{a_{p,1}}

and so

\left(\frac{a_{p,2}}{a_{p,1}}-\frac{a_{q,2}}{a_{q,1}}\right)x_{2}+\cdots+\left(\frac{a_{p,n}}{a_{p,1}}-\frac{a_{q,n}}{a_{q,1}}\right)x_{n}\leq\frac{b_{p}}{a_{p,1}}-\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q,1}}.

For all

we have

a_{r,2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{r,n}x_{n}\leq b_{r}.

Thus, if
\mathbf{x}\in P
, then the vector
\pi_{n,1}(\mathbf{x})
is in the polyhedron
defined by the linear inequalities (
2
)
and (
3
) for all
p,q,
and
r
.
There are
|S_{1}|\ |S_{-1}|+|S_{0}|
such inequalities.

Conversely, if the vector
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
satisfies

)
and (

) for all

and

-\frac{a_{q,2}}{a_{q,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{q,n}}{a_{q,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q,1}}\leq-\frac{a_{p,2}}{a_{p,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{p,n}}{a_{p,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{p}}{a_{p,1}}

for all

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{a_{q,2}}{a_{q,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{q,n}}{a_{q,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q,1}}\right)\leq\min_{p\in S_{1}}\left(-\frac{a_{p,2}}{a_{p,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{p,n}}{a_{p,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{p}}{a_{p,1}}\right).

The left side of this inequality is

If

is any real number such that

\displaystyle\max_{q\in S_{-1}}

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{a_{q,2}}{a_{q,1}}x_{2}-\cdots-\frac{a_{q,n}}{a_{q,1}}x_{n}+\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q,1}}\right)

4.
Convex cones

A
cone
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a nonempty set
C

such that
\lambda\mathbf{w}\in C
for all

\mathbf{w}\in C
and
\lambda\geq 0
.
If
\mathbf{w}\in C
, then
\mathbf{0}=0\mathbf{w}\in C
and so every cone contains the zero vector.
The set
\{\mathbf{0}\}
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a cone. The cone
C
is bounded if and only if
C=\{\mathbf{0}\}
.

A
convex cone

in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a cone that is convex.
The set
\{\mathbf{0}\}
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a convex cone.
If the cone
C
is convex, then for all
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
we have

\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=2\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right)\in C.

Conversely, if
C
is a cone such that
\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C

for all
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
, then for all
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\Delta_{2}

we have
\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w},\lambda_{2}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
and so
\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
.
Thus, the cone
C
is convex if and only if
\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C

for all
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
.

The union of cones is a cone, but the union of convex cones is not necessarily convex.
For example, the union of two distinct one-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
(that is, two lines passing through the origin) is a cone that is not convex.

The vector space
\mathbf{R}^{n^{2}}
can be viewed as the set of
n\times n
matrices.
An
n\times n
matrix
A
is
positive semidefinite

if
\mathbf{x}^{t}A\mathbf{x}\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
. The set of positive semidefinite matrices
is a convex cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n^{2}}
.

A
conic combination
of a finite sequence
of vectors
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n})
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a vector of the form

\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}\mathbf{w}_{n}

for some
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.
A nonempty set
\mathcal{W}
of vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{n}

conically generates
the cone
C

if
C
is the set of all conic combinations
of finite sequences of elements of
\mathcal{W}
.
We write
C=\cone(\mathcal{W})
if
C
is the cone generated by
\mathcal{W}
.
A cone is
finitely generated

if it is the set of all conic combinations of a finite set of vectors.
The zero cone
C=\{\mathbf{0}\}
is the cone generated by the empty set of vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 2
.

The cone generated by a nonempty set of vectors is convex.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a nonempty set of vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and let
C=\cone(\mathcal{W})
.
The set
C
is nonempty because
\mathcal{W}
is nonempty.
A conic combination of conic combinations of finite sequences of vectors in
\mathcal{W}

is also a conic combination of a finite sequence of vectors in
\mathcal{W}
, and so
C
is a cone.
In particular, if
\mathbf{w}\in C
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
, then
\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in C
.
Thus,
C
is convex. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
W
be an
n\times m
matrix .
The set

\displaystyle C(W)=\left\{W\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\right\}

is a convex cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let
\mathbf{w}_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
be the the
j
th column vector of the matrix
W

and let
\mathcal{W}=\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{m}\}
.
We have

\displaystyle C(W)

\displaystyle=\left\{W\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{y_{1}\mathbf{w}_{1}+\cdots+y_{m}\mathbf{w}_{m}:y_{j}\geq 0\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,m\right\}

and so
C(W)
is the set of all conic combinations of the sequence of vectors

(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{m})
, that is,
C(W)=\cone(\mathcal{W})
.
By Theorem
2
, the cone
C(W)
is convex.
This completes the proof.
∎

A
polyhedral cone
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a
polyhedron that is a cone.

Lemma 2
.

For every positive integer
m
and every
m\times n
matrix
A
, the polyhedron

P(A)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}

is a polyhedral cone.
Every polyhedral cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is of this form for some matrix
A
.

Proof.

If
A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}
, then
A(\lambda\mathbf{x})=\lambda A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}

for all
\lambda\geq 0
and so the polyhedron

\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}
is a cone.

Let
A=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i,j}\end{matrix}\right)
and
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallbm
.
Suppose that the polyhedron
P=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}\right\}

is a cone.
We have
\mathbf{0}\in P
and so
\mathbf{0}=A\mathbf{0}\leq\mathbf{b}
.
Therefore,
A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}
implies
A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}

and
P
contains the set
\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}
.

If
P
contains a vector
\mathbf{x}
such that
A\mathbf{x}\not\leq\mathbf{0}
,
then the
i
th coordinate of the vector
A\mathbf{x}
is positive for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
This coordinate is
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{j}
and satisfies the inequality

0<\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{j}\leq b_{i}.

Because
P
is a cone, for all
\lambda>0
we have
\lambda\mathbf{x}\in P

and so
\lambda A\mathbf{x}=A(\lambda\mathbf{x})\leq\mathbf{b}
.
The
i
th coordinate of the vector
\lambda A\mathbf{x}
satisfies the inequality

0<\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{j}\leq b_{i}

for all
\lambda>0
, which is absurd. Therefore,

P=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be real vector spaces and let
T:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
be a linear transformation.
If
X
is a cone in
V
, then
Y=T(X)
is a cone in
V^{\prime}
.
In particular, the projection of a cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a cone.

Proof.

Let
C
be a cone in
V
. If
\mathbf{w}\in T(C)
, then
\mathbf{w}=T(\mathbf{v})
for some
\mathbf{v}\in V
.
For all
\lambda\geq 0
we have
\lambda\mathbf{v}\in C
and so

\lambda\mathbf{w}=\lambda T(\mathbf{v})=T(\lambda\mathbf{v})\in T(C)

and so
T(C)
is a cone.

Every projection
\pi_{n,k}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n-k}
is a linear transformation
and so the projection of a cone is a cone.
This completes the proof.
∎

x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{p+n}
.
Let
I_{n}
be the
n\times n
identity matrix and
\mathbf{0}_{p,n}
the
p\times n
zero matrix.

Theorem 4

(Weyl)
.

Every finitely generated convex cone is a polyhedral cone.

Proof.

Let
C
be the convex cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
generated by the finite set of vectors

\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{p}\}
.
Let
W
be the
n\times p
matrix whose column vectors are
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{p})
.
Then

C=\left\{W\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\right\}.

Consider the set

P=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{y}\\
W\mathbf{y}\end{matrix}\right):\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p},\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\right\}\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{p+n}.

We have

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{y}\\
\mathbf{x}\end{matrix}\right):\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p},\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0},\mathbf{x}=W\mathbf{y}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{y}\\
\mathbf{x}\end{matrix}\right):\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p},\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n},-\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{0},W\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0},-W\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{R}^{p+n}:\tilde{W}\mathbf{z}\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}

where
\tilde{W}
is the
(p+2n)\times(p+n)
matrix written in block form as

\tilde{W}=\left(\begin{matrix}-I_{p}&\mathbf{0}_{p,n}\\
W&-I_{n}\\
-W&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right).

It follows from Lemma
2
that
P
is a polyhedral cone.

Let
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
The projection
\pi_{p+n,p}:\mathbf{R}^{p+n}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n}
is
defined by
\pi_{p+n,p}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{y}\\
\mathbf{x}\end{matrix}\right)=\mathbf{x}
.
We have the cone

\pi_{p+n,p}(P)=\left\{W\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\right\}=C.

By Theorem
1
, the set

C=\pi_{p+n,p}(P)
is a polyhedron, and so
C
is a polyhedral cone.
This completes the proof.
∎

The following result is an example of what is called a “theorem of the alternative.”

Theorem 5

(Farkas lemma)
.

Let
W
be an
n\times p
matrix and let
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
Exactly one of the following two statements holds:

(a)

There is a vector
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}
such that

\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\qqand W\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{b}.

(b)

There is a vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that

\mathbf{v}^{t}W\leq\mathbf{0}^{t}\qqand\mathbf{v}^{t}\mathbf{b}>0.

Proof.

Suppose that alternative (a) holds and that
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}
satisfies

\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}
and
W\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{b}
. If
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and

\mathbf{v}^{t}W\leq\mathbf{0}^{t}
, then

\mathbf{v}^{t}\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{v}^{t}\left(W\mathbf{y}\right)=\left(\mathbf{v}^{t}W\right)\mathbf{y}\leq 0

and so alternative (b) does not hold.

Let
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{p}
be the column vectors of the matrix
W

and let

C(W)=\{W\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}

be the convex cone
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
generated by the finite sequence
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{p})
.
By Weyl’s theorem (Theorem
4
),
the finitely generated convex cone
C(W)
is a polyhedral cone and so
there is an
m\times n
matrix
A
such that

C(W)=P(A)=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}.

If alternative (a) does not hold, then
\mathbf{b}\notin C(W)

and so
A\mathbf{b}\not\leq\mathbf{0}
.
This means that the
i
th coordinate of the vector
A\mathbf{b}
is positive
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,n})
be the
i
th row of the matrix
A

and let
\mathbf{v}=(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,n})^{t}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
The
i
th coordinate of
A\mathbf{b}
is
\mathbf{v}^{t}\mathbf{b}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}b_{j}

and so
\mathbf{v}^{t}\mathbf{b}>0
.

For all
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, the
j
th column vector
\mathbf{w}_{j}=W\mathbf{e}_{j}

belongs to the convex cone
C(W)
, and so
A\mathbf{w}_{j}\leq\mathbf{0}
.
The vector
A\mathbf{w}_{j}
is the
j
th column vector of the matrix
AW
.
Therefore, the
m\times h
matrix
AW
is nonnegative.
Because
\mathbf{v}^{t}
is the
i
th row of
A
,
it follows that
\mathbf{v}^{t}W
is the
i
th row of
AW
and so
\mathbf{v}^{t}W\leq\mathbf{0}^{t}
.
Thus, alternative (b) holds if alternative (a) does not hold.
This completes the proof of the Farkas lemma.
∎

5.
A duality

Associated to every

Theorem 6
.

Let
A
be an
m\times n
matrix and let
B
be an
n\times p
matrix. If

(7)

P(A)=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}=\{B\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}=C(B)

then

(8)

P(B^{t})=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:B^{t}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}=\{A^{t}\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}=C(A^{t}).

Proof.

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
, the
j
th column vector of
B
is

\mathbf{b}_{j}=B\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\{B\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}

and so

\mathbf{b}_{j}\in\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}

by equation (
7

The vector inequalities
A\mathbf{b}_{j}\leq\mathbf{0}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}

imply the matrix inequality
AB\leq\mathbf{0}
. The transpose of the matrix
AB

is also nonpositive:

B^{t}A^{t}=(AB)^{t}\leq\mathbf{0}.

It follows that for all
\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}
we have

B^{t}\left(A^{t}\mathbf{y}\right)=\left(B^{t}A^{t}\right)\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{0}

and so

C(A^{t})=\{A^{t}\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}\subseteq\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:B^{t}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}=P(B^{t}).

We must prove the reverse inclusion:
P(B^{t})\subseteq C(A^{t})
.
Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
with
B^{t}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}
.
If
A^{t}\mathbf{y}\neq\mathbf{x}
for all
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}
with
\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}
,
then the Farkas Lemma (Theorem
5
) implies that there exists a vector

\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that

(9)

(A\mathbf{v})^{t}=\mathbf{v}^{t}A^{t}\leq\mathbf{0}^{t}\qqand\mathbf{v}^{t}\mathbf{x}>0.

The inequalities
(A\mathbf{v})^{t}\leq\mathbf{0}^{t}
and

are equivalent.
Equation (
7
) implies
\mathbf{v}=B\mathbf{y}
for some
\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}
.
The inequalities
\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{y}^{t}\geq\mathbf{0}^{t}
are equivalent.
From
\mathbf{y}^{t}\geq\mathbf{0}^{t}
and

\mathbf{v}^{t}\mathbf{x}=(B\mathbf{y})^{t}\mathbf{x}=\left(\mathbf{y}^{t}B^{t}\right)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}^{t}\left(B^{t}\mathbf{x}\right)\leq 0

which contradicts (

P(B^{t})=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:B^{t}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}\subseteq\{A^{t}\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}=C(A^{t}).

This completes the proof.

We can now prove the converse of Weyl’s Theorem.

Theorem 7

(Minkowski)
.

Every polyhedral cone is a finitely generated convex cone.

Proof.

Let
A
be an
m\times n
matrix.
Consider the polyhedral cone

.
By Weyl’s theorem, the cone
C(A^{t})
is polyhedral and so
there is an
n\times p
matrix
B
such that

C(A^{t})=\{A^{t}\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0}\}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:B^{t}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{0}\}=P(B^{t}).

Theorem
6
implies that

conically generated by the finite sequence of vectors

\left(\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}_{1}\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}_{k}\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}_{k+1}\\
0\end{matrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}_{h}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle\widehat{P}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}_{j}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{b}_{j}\\
0\end{matrix}\right):\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{h}\geq 0\right\}.

By Weyl’s theorem (Theorem
4
)
and by relation (
10
), the finitely generated convex
cone
\widehat{P}
is a polyhedral cone, and so there is an
\ell\times n
matrix
A
and
a vector
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{R}^{\ell}
such that the
\ell\times(n+1)

matrix
(A|\mathbf{b})
satisfies

(11)

\widehat{P}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}:(A|\mathbf{b})\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}:A\mathbf{x}\leq-t\mathbf{b}\right\}.

Let
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{k})
be a finite sequence of vectors
that convexly generates the polytope
Q

and let
(\mathbf{w}_{k+1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{h})
be a finite sequence of vectors
that conically generates the cone
C
.
If
\mathbf{x}\in Q+C
, then

\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{Q}+\mathbf{x}_{C}

with

\mathbf{x}_{Q}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in Q\qqand\mathbf{x}_{C}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in C

and

\lambda_{j}\geq 0\quad\text{for all $j\in\{1,\ldots,k,k+1,\ldots,h\}$}\qqand\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}=1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}_{Q}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}_{C}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
\\
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\end{matrix}\right)+\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\in\widehat{P}.

It follows from (
11
) that
A\mathbf{x}\leq-\mathbf{b}

and so
C+Q\subseteq\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq-\mathbf{b}\right\}
.

Similarly, if
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
A\mathbf{x}\leq-\mathbf{b}
,
then
\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)\in\widehat{P}
and there exist
nonnegative numbers
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{h}

such that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\end{matrix}\right)+\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

It follows that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}=1
and so

\mathbf{x}_{Q}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in Q\qqand\mathbf{x}_{C}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in C.

Thus,
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{Q}+\mathbf{x}_{C}\in Q+C
and

\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq-\mathbf{b}\right\}\subseteq Q+C
.
This proves that

Q+C=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq-\mathbf{b}\right\}

and so the sum of a polytope and a finitely generated convex cone is a polyhedron.

Conversely, we shall prove that every polyhedron is the sum of a finitely generated
convex cone and a polytope.
Let
P
be a polyhedron in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. There is an
m\times n
matrix

A=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{i,j}\end{matrix}\right)
and a vector
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{R}^{m}

such that

P=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}\}.

Consider the
(m+1)\times(n+1)
matrix

\widehat{A}=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1,1}&a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,n}&-b_{1}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2,2}&\cdots&a_{2,n}&-b_{2}\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
a_{m,1}&a_{m,2}&\cdots&a_{m,n}&-b_{m}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&-1\end{matrix}\right)

By Lemma
2
,
the set
\widehat{P}
in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
defined by

(12)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
t\end{matrix}\right):\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n},t\geq 0,A\mathbf{x}\leq t\mathbf{b}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}:\widehat{A}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\leq\mathbf{0}\right\}

is a polyhedral cone.
We have
\mathbf{x}\in P
if and only if
A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}

if and only if
\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)\in\widehat{P}

and so

P=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)\in\widehat{P}\right\}.

By Minkowski’s theorem (Theorem
7
), the polyhedral
cone
\widehat{P}
is a finitely generated convex cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,
and so there is an
(n+1)\times h
matrix
W
such that

\widehat{P}=\{W\mathbf{y}:\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{h}\text{ and }\mathbf{y}\geq 0\}.

The set
\widehat{P}
is conically generated by the set of columns of
W
.
For
j\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
, let
\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
t_{j}\end{matrix}\right)
be the
j
th column of
W
,
where
\mathbf{w}_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
t_{j}\in\mathbf{R}
.
We have
t_{j}\geq 0
because
\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
t_{j}\end{matrix}\right)=W\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\widehat{P}
.

Modify the matrix
W
as follows:
If
t_{j}>0
, then multiply the
j
th column of
W
by
1/t_{j}>0
. If
t_{j}=0
, do not
change the
j
th column of
W
.
We obtain a new matrix, which we shall also denote by
W
, whose
j
th column
is of the form
\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
\varepsilon_{j}\end{matrix}\right)
with
\varepsilon_{j}=0
or 1.
Because the columns of the original matrix
W
have only been multiplied by positive numbers,
the new matrix
W
still conically generates
\widehat{P}
.
Renumber the vectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{h}
so that

\{\mathbf{w}_{j}:\varepsilon_{j}=1\}=\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{k}\}\qqand\{\mathbf{w}_{j}:\varepsilon_{j}=0\}=\{\mathbf{w}_{k+1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{h}\}.

Let
Q
be the polytope in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
convexly generated by the set
\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{k}\}

and let
C
be the convex cone in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
conically generated by the set
\{\mathbf{w}_{k+1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{h}\}
.
Let
Q=\{\mathbf{0}\}
if
k=0
, that is,
\{\mathbf{w}_{j}:\varepsilon_{j}=1\}=\emptyset
,
and let
C=\{\mathbf{0}\}
if
k=h
.

We have
\mathbf{x}\in P
if and only if
\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)\in\widehat{P}
if and only if
there is a nonnegative vector
\mathbf{\lambda}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{h}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=W\mathbf{\lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
\\
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\end{matrix}\right)+\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

It follows that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}=1
and so

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in Q\qqand\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in C.

We obtain

\mathbf{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in Q+C.

Thus,
P\subseteq Q+C
.

Now we prove that
Q+C\subseteq P
.
If
\mathbf{x}\in Q+C
, then
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{Q}+\mathbf{x}_{C}
, where

\mathbf{x}_{Q}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in Q\qqand\mathbf{x}_{C}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}\in C

and
\mathbf{\lambda}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{h}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{h}

is a nonnegative vector such that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}=1
.
Then

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}_{Q}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{x}_{C}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{h}\lambda_{j}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbf{w}_{j}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle\in\widehat{P}

and so
\mathbf{x}\in P
. Thus,
C+Q\subseteq P
.
It follows that
P=C+Q
and so every polyhedron is the sum of a finitely generated
convex cone and a polytope.
This completes the proof.
∎

Optimal boundary regularity and Green function estimates for nonlocal equations in divergence form
Minhyun Kim, Marvin Weidner

Abstract
. In this article we prove for the first time the C s C^{s} boundary regularity for solutions to nonlocal elliptic equations with Hölder continuous coefficients in divergence form in C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} domains. So far, it was only known that solutions are Hölder continuous up to the boundary, and establishing their optimal regularity has remained an open problem in the field. Our proof is based on a delicate higher order Campanato-type iteration at the boundary, which we develop in the context of nonlocal equations and which is quite different from the local theory. As an application of our results, we establish sharp two-sided Green function estimates in C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} domains for the same class of operators. Previously, this was only known under additional structural assumptions on the coefficients and in more regular domains.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this work is to establish sharp two-sided Green function estimates and the optimal
C^{s}
boundary regularity for solutions to nonlocal elliptic equations posed in
C^{1,\alpha}
domains. We are interested in integro-differential operators

(1.1)

\displaystyle Lu(x)=2\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x)-u(y))K(x,y)\textnormal{\,d}y

with kernels
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
satisfying a natural symmetry and uniform ellipticity condition of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lambda|x-y|^{-n-2s}\leq K(x,y)=K(y,x)\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{-n-2s}

for some
s\in(0,1)
with
2s<n
and
\Lambda\geq\lambda>0
. We assume throughout this paper that the kernels
K
are locally Hölder continuous of order
\sigma\in(0,s)
in the following sense: given a set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, (which will typically be the domain where the equation is posed)

(1.3)

\displaystyle|K(x+h,y+h)-K(x,y)|\leq\Lambda\frac{|h|^{\sigma}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal{A}\text{ and }h\in B_{1}.

Due to the symmetry of
K
, such operators
L
are naturally associated to variational functionals and often referred to as
nonlocal operators in divergence form
. They can be considered as nonlocal counterparts of second-order operators in divergence form
-\dvg(A(x)\nabla u(x))
with
A\in C^{\sigma}
.

1.1.
Two-sided Green function estimates

Solutions to equations of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lu&=f&&\text{in }\Omega,\\

\displaystyle u(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)f(y)\textnormal{\,d}y,

where

denotes the Green function associated to

and

The study of the Green function for nonlocal operators

[

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

Our proof of the
C^{s}
boundary regularity in
Theorem 1.6
is based on a nonlocal version of the Morrey–Campanato theory at the boundary. Such technique has already been established for nonlocal equations in the context of interior regularity (see for instance
[
KMS15
,
KNS22
]
) and in this paper we prove similar estimates at the boundary.

Obtaining boundary regularity estimates for nonlocal problems is much more difficult than for local equations due to the following reasons:

•

For local equations it is sufficient to establish the boundary regularity in flat domains and to deduce it in general domains with a suitable (local) change of variables. Such an argument does not work for nonlocal equations.

•

Solutions to nonlocal equations behave like
d_{\Omega}^{s}
near
\partial\Omega
. Thus, derivatives of solutions explode at the boundary, and hence it seems hopeless to obtain higher order regularity estimates (of order
s
) by differentiation of the equation. Due to the same reason, one cannot approximate solutions by linear functions at the boundary.

Our proof of
Theorem 1.6
consists of several steps:

The proof of
Theorem 1.7
relies on a Morrey-type iteration argument. Given any point
x_{0}\in\Omega\cap B_{1/2}
, we consider the “frozen” operator
L_{x_{0}}
which is of the same shape as (
1.1
) but has the kernel

(1.9)

\displaystyle K_{x_{0}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}[K(x_{0}+x-y,x_{0})+K(x_{0}+y-x,x_{0})].

Note that
K_{x_{0}}
only depends on
x-y
and therefore,
L_{x_{0}}
is translation invariant. Then, we compare the solution
u
with its
L_{x_{0}}
-harmonic replacements
v
in
\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})
for all scales
R\in(0,1/2)
. For a fixed
R\in(0,1/2)
,
v
is given as the solution to

(1.10)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} L_{x_{0}}v&=0&&\text{in }\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}),\\
v&=u&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})).\end{aligned}\right.

Since the
C^{s}
boundary regularity for
v
is already known (see
[
FRRO24a
,
ROW24
]
), we can transfer the regularity from
v
to
u
by establishing suitable comparison estimates for
[u-v]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
(see
4.1
). This way, we derive the following nonlocal Morrey-type estimate, which we state here for simplicity only in case
f=0
(see also
5.4
and (
5.5
)):

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})\leq c\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\varepsilon}+R^{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R,x_{0})+cR^{n+s}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 0<\rho<R.

Here,
\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})
denotes the nonlocal excess functional

(1.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0}):=\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\rho^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;\rho,x_{0}).

The first summand takes into account the behavior of solutions to nonlocal equations at the boundary. The second summand is defined as

\displaystyle\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;\rho,x_{0})=\rho^{2s-\sigma}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u(y)||y-x_{0}|^{-n-2s+\sigma}\textnormal{\,d}y,

and it is more difficult to grasp since it reflects upon three different aspects of the problem at hand:

•

The position of the ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
relative to
\partial\Omega
is taken into account through the prefactor
\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}
.

•

The tail captures the information on
u
at intermediate scales
B_{1}\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{0})
, thereby reflecting the nonlocality of the problem.

•

The weight and scaling of the tail keep track of the
C^{\sigma}
regularity of the kernel
K
.

A key difficulty in the proof of (
1.11
) is to incorporate the tail term into the estimate, making it a purely nonlocal result. Although tail terms commonly appear in the study of nonlocal equations (see
[
DCKP14
,
DCKP16
,
KW24
]
) and have also been treated before in iterative schemes (see
[
KNS22
,
BDL+25
]
), we believe that
\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}
is quite special in the sense that the order of the weight matches the regularity of the coefficients. This aspect is crucial to us since it allows us to profit from the regularity of
K
also on intermediate scales. To prove interior regularity, there is no need to introduce
\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}
since solutions enjoy regularity of an order higher than
C^{s}
(see
[
KNS22
, Lemma 3.5]
).

The Morrey-type estimate (
1.11
) is a key component in the proof of
Theorem 1.7
. Indeed, by combining it with interior regularity results, a standard iteration argument directly establishes the
C^{s-\varepsilon}
regularity of
u
.

1.3.2.
Upgrade to
C^{s}
regularity

Theorem 1.7
is a crucial ingredient in the proof of
Theorem 1.6
. However, upgrading the
C^{s-\varepsilon}
regularity to the optimal
C^{s}
regularity is far from trivial (and this is crucially needed to get the sharp Green function estimates). In fact, it lies in the nature of perturbation arguments such as the ones we develop in this article, that through the comparison with the prototype (translation invariant) equation one always loses a (fractional) derivative of arbitrarily small order
\varepsilon
. In other words, it is impossible to improve the exponent
n-\varepsilon
in (
1.11
) (or any other excess decay estimate of similar type)
unless
v
is more regular than
C^{s}
. However, since the
C^{s}
regularity is optimal
for translation invariant problems, in fact,
v/d_{\Omega}^{s}
does not even have to be continuous (see
[
ROW24
]
), new ideas are required in order to prove
Theorem 1.6
. This is in stark contrast to the local case.

The first key idea to establish the optimal
C^{s}
regularity in
Theorem 6.1
is to prove an
expansion of order
s+\varepsilon
at boundary points
of
\Omega
with
\varepsilon>0
for translation invariant equations.
Such an expansion was already proved in
[
ROW24
]
but only in terms of a one-dimensional barrier
b_{x_{0}}
that solves an equation in the half-space and heavily depends on its orientation. Since this would not allow us to prove
C^{s}
regularity in more general domains, we slightly improve the result in
[
ROW24
]
as follows.

For any frozen operator
L_{x_{0}}
(more precisely, for any locally frozen operator
\widetilde{L}_{x_{0}}
given by (
6.12
)) we construct a barrier function
\psi_{x_{0}}\in C^{s}
that takes into account the geometry of
\Omega
and is comparable to
d_{\Omega}^{s}
, by an application of the results in
[
ROW24
]
. Then, we show that solutions
v
to (
1.10
) satisfy an expansion in terms of
\psi_{x_{0}}
(see
6.2
) of the following form for some
q_{z}\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle|v(x)-q_{z}\psi_{x_{0}}(x)|\leq c|x-z|^{s+\varepsilon}\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall z\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega\text{ and }x\in\Omega\cap B_{R/2}(x_{0}).

A main achievement of the current article is to show that this expansion provides enough higher order information about the boundary behavior of
v
and that it actually implies
C^{s}
regularity for
u
. This goes by a higher order nonlocal Campanato-type estimate in terms of the function
u/\psi_{x_{0}}
, which we state here, again, for simplicity only in case
f=0
(see also
6.7
):

(1.13)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n+\varepsilon}\Psi_{\sigma}(u;R,x_{0})+cR^{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R,x_{0})+cR^{n+s}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here,
\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})
denotes the higher order nonlocal excess functional

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})&:=\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{\psi_{x_{0}}}-\left(\frac{u}{\psi_{x_{0}}}\right)_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\\
&\quad+\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\rho^{n}\mathrm{Tail}_{\sigma,B_{1}}\left(u-\psi_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{u}{\psi_{x_{0}}}\right)_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})};\rho,x_{0}\right).\end{split}

Note that the quotients
u/\psi_{x_{0}}
and
v/\psi_{x_{0}}
do not solve any reasonable equation, which is why the proof of (
1.13
) is quite involved and differs significantly from the ones in
[
KNS22
]
.

Another key difficulty is to deduce the
C^{s}
regularity of
u
from the Campanato-type estimate (
1.13
). In fact, since the functions
\psi_{x_{0}}
heavily depend on
x_{0}
and are themselves only in
C^{s}
it is not possible to deduce any regularity of
u
directly from (
1.13
). We solve this problem by proving in
6.4
that for any
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega\cap B_{1/2}
, it holds

(1.15)

\displaystyle[\psi_{x_{0}}-\psi_{y_{0}}]_{C^{s-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega\cap B_{1/2}})}\leq c|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{\sigma}.

The proof of this result requires a considerable amount of work and goes by extending some of the arguments in the proof of
Theorem 1.7
to equations with nonlocal operators on the right-hand side.

Thanks to (
1.15
) we are able to replace
u/\psi_{x_{0}}
in the Campanato excess decay estimate (
1.13
) by
u(x)/\psi_{x}(x)
. This way, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(x\mapsto\frac{u(x)}{\psi_{x}(x)}\right)\in C^{\varepsilon},

see
Theorem 6.8
, and in particular it implies the optimal
C^{s}
regularity of
u
.

1.3.3.
Higher regularity for homogeneous kernels

Finally, let us mention that we also obtain higher order fine boundary asymptotics under additional structural assumptions on
K
. Recall that for translation invariant operators one can prove higher regularity of
u/d_{\Omega}^{s}
if
K
is homogeneous, i.e.
K(h)=a(h/|h|)|h|^{-n-2s}
for some function
a:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to[\lambda,\Lambda]
(see
[
ROS16b
,
ROS16a
,
ROS17
,
ARO20
]
). The following result shows that the same higher order asymptotics hold true when the frozen kernels
K_{x_{0}}
are homogeneous:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
\alpha,\sigma\in(0,s)
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain and let
L,K,\lambda,\Lambda
be as in (
1.1
), (
1.2
). Assume that (
1.3
) holds with
\mathcal{A}=B_{1}
and that
K_{x_{0}}
is homogeneous for any
x_{0}\in\Omega\cap B_{1}
. Let
f
be such that
d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\in L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{1})
for some
p\in(0,s]
and
q\in(\frac{n}{p},\infty]
.
Let
u
be a weak solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lu&=f&&\text{in }\Omega\cap B_{1},\\
u&=0&&\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u/d_{\Omega}^{s}\in C^{\min\{\alpha-\varepsilon,\sigma,p-\frac{n}{q}\}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{1})
and

\displaystyle\|u/d_{\Omega}^{s}\|_{C^{\min\{\alpha-\varepsilon,\sigma,p-\frac{n}{q}\}}(\overline{\Omega\cap B_{1/2}})}\leq c\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{1})}\right)

for some constant
c>0
, depending only on
n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\alpha,\sigma,p,q,\varepsilon
, and
\Omega
.

The following is a natural class of kernels satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem 1.8
. Take

\displaystyle K(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-2s}a_{x}((x-y)/|x-y|)+|x-y|^{-n-2s}a_{y}((x-y)/|x-y|)

and
a_{x}(h)=a_{x}(-h)
with
0<\lambda\leq a(h)\leq\Lambda<\infty
for every
h\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Note that
K
does not possess the non-divergence form symmetry. Moreover, we believe this class of kernels to be natural since kernels of similar structure have already been considered in the literature (see
[
BKS19
,
FRRO24b
]
).

The proof of
Theorem 1.8
is less technical than the one of
Theorem 6.1
. In fact, in the framework of
Theorem 1.8
one can establish a similar higher order Campanato-type estimate as in (
1.13
) and directly deduce regularity of
u/d_{\Omega}^{s}
from it since in this case the corresponding barrier functions
\phi_{x_{0}}
satisfy
\phi_{x_{0}}/d_{\Omega}^{s}\in C^{\alpha}
due to
[
ROS17
]
, see Section
8
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect several definitions of function spaces, define weak solutions, introduce some notions, list functional inequalities, and provide auxiliary lemmas that will be used throughout the paper. Recall that we always assume
s\in(0,1)
,
n>2s
,
\Lambda\geq\lambda>0
, and that
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Throughout this paper, we use the following notation:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})=\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0}),\qquad(u)_{\Omega}={\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{\Omega}|u|\textnormal{\,d}x=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}|u|\textnormal{\,d}x,\qquad(u)_{\rho,x_{0}}=(u)_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}.

2.1.
Function spaces and weak solutions

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be with
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}
and define

\displaystyle H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):[u]_{H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\textnormal{\,d}y\textnormal{\,d}x<\infty\right\}

to be a fractional Sobolev space equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})}=\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+[u]_{H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

If
\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}
, we write
H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})=H^{s}(\Omega)
and define
H^{s}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u\equiv 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}
. Given a kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
satisfying (
1.2
), we define for measurable functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{K}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))K(x,y)\textnormal{\,d}y\textnormal{\,d}x,

provided that it is finite. Note that
\mathcal{E}^{K}(u,v)
is finite when
u,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and that it gives rise to an integro-differential operator
L
given by (
1.1
) via the relation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{K}(u,\varphi)=\langle Lu,\varphi\rangle\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{s}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In order to define weak solutions, we also recall the so-called
tail space

L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is given by

\displaystyle L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{n+2s}}\textnormal{\,d}y<\infty\right\}.

It is well known that
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that the
tail
, defined by

\displaystyle\tail(u;R,x_{0})=R^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+2s}}\textnormal{\,d}y,

is finite for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
if
u\in L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The tail is introduced to capture the nonlocality of solutions to nonlocal equations with kernels
K
.

By using the relation (
2.1
), we define weak solutions to nonlocal equations with respect to
L
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in(H_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\ast}
. We say that
u
is a (weak)
subsolution
to
Lu\leq f
in
\Omega
if there exists a domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
such that
u\in H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})\cap L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{K}(u,\varphi)\leq\langle f,\varphi\rangle

for all nonnegative functions
\varphi\in H^{s}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that
u
is a (weak)
supersolution
to
Lu\geq f
in
\Omega
if (
2.2
) holds with
u
replaced by
-u
. Moreover, we say that
u
is a (weak)
solution
to
Lu=f
in
\Omega
if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution.

We require weak solutions to be in
H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})
instead of
H^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
in order to be able to analyze their boundary regularity. This condition is very natural, see also the discussion in
[
KL23
, p.5-6]
. Note also that if
u
is a solution to
Lu=f
in
\Omega
with zero exterior condition
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, then
u\in H^{s}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and so
u
itself is an admissible test function.

As we mentioned in the introduction, we need another type of tail capturing the decay of
K-K_{x_{0}}
. For this purpose, we introduce the following
\sigma
-tail
for
\sigma\in(0,s)
:

\displaystyle\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})=R^{2s-\sigma}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}|u(y)||y-x_{0}|^{-n-2s+\sigma}\textnormal{\,d}y.

Note that
\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\leq R^{-\sigma}\tail(u;R,x_{0})<\infty
if
u\in L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that we have the following trivial estimate:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\tail(u;R,x_{0})\leq\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})+R^{2s}\tail(u;1,0).

2.2.
Functional inequalities

This section is devoted to several functional inequalities that will be useful throughout the paper. We begin with the (fractional) Poincaré inequality.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
Pon04
, Corollary 2.1]

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
. Then there exists
c=c(n,s)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-(u)_{R,x_{0}}|^{2}\textnormal{\,d}x\leq cR^{2s}[u]^{2}_{H^{s}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

for any
u\in H^{s}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
.

The following property, which is an easy consequence of the triangle inequality, will also be used: for any
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
:

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{\Omega}|u-(u)_{\Omega}|\textnormal{\,d}x\leq 2{{\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{\Omega}|u-c|\textnormal{\,d}x}.

The (fractional) Poincaré–Wirtinger-type inequality for functions having fat zero level sets is known.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
Coz17b
, Lemma 4.7]

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
. Let
u\in H^{s}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
be such that
|\{u=0\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|\geq c_{0}|B_{R}(x_{0})|
for some
c_{0}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\leq cR^{s}[u]_{H^{s}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

for some
c=c(n,s,c_{0})>0
.

We have the following version of the (fractional) Hardy inequality, where
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are the usual fractional Sobolev spaces with integrability
p\geq 1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain such that
\Omega^{c}
satisfies the measure density condition, i.e. there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\inf_{x_{0}\in\partial\Omega}\inf_{r>0}\frac{|B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|}\geq c_{0}.

(In particular,
\Omega
can be any bounded Lipschitz domain.)
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|^{p}\textnormal{\,d}x\leq c[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for any
u\in W^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
c=c(n,s,p,c_{0})>0
.

Proof.

For any
x\in\Omega
there exists
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
such that
d_{\Omega}(x)=|x-x_{0}|
. We then have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{\textnormal{\,d}y}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\geq\int_{B_{d_{\Omega}(x)}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega}\frac{\textnormal{\,d}y}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\geq\frac{|B_{d_{\Omega}(x)}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|}{(2d_{\Omega}(x))^{n+sp}}\geq cd_{\Omega}^{-sp}(x)

for some
c=c(n,s,p,c_{0})>0
. Since
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega^{c}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|^{p}\textnormal{\,d}x\leq c\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{\textnormal{\,d}y}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\right)\textnormal{\,d}x=c\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\textnormal{\,d}y\textnormal{\,d}x\leq c[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p},

as desired.
∎

2.3.
Auxiliary lemmas

We also have the following auxiliary estimate.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lipschitz domain,
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
R>0
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}d_{\Omega}^{-\varepsilon}\textnormal{\,d}x\leq cR^{n-\varepsilon}

for some
c=c(n,\varepsilon,\Omega)>0
.

Proof.

Note that in case
d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\geq 2R
, the result is immediate since
d_{\Omega}^{-\varepsilon}\leq R^{-\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{R}(x_{0})
. Hence, we assume from now on that
d_{\Omega}(x_{0})<2R
.
By applying
[
FRRO24a
, Lemma B.2.4]
with
\gamma:=-\varepsilon
and
\beta:=-n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}(x_{0})}d^{-\varepsilon}_{\Omega}\textnormal{\,d}x=\int_{\Omega_{1}(x_{0})\setminus B_{d_{\Omega}(x_{0})/2}(x_{0})}d^{-\varepsilon}_{\Omega}\textnormal{\,d}x+\int_{B_{d_{\Omega}(x_{0})/2}(x_{0})}d^{-\varepsilon}_{\Omega}\textnormal{\,d}x\leq c(1+d_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{n-\varepsilon})

for some constant
c>0
, depending only on
n,\varepsilon
, and
\Omega
.
Hence, an application of the previous estimate to
\tilde{\Omega}=R^{-1}(\Omega-x_{0})
shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}d^{-\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(x)\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle=R^{n}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}\cap B_{1}}d^{-\varepsilon}_{\Omega-x_{0}}(Rx)\textnormal{\,d}x=R^{n-\varepsilon}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}\cap B_{1}}d^{-\varepsilon}_{\tilde{\Omega}}(x)\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq cR^{n-\varepsilon}(1+d_{\tilde{\Omega}}(0)^{n-\varepsilon})\leq cR^{n-\varepsilon}(1+R^{\varepsilon-n}d_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{n-\varepsilon})\leq cR^{n-\varepsilon},

where we used that
d_{\Omega-x_{0}}(R\cdot)=Rd_{\tilde{\Omega}}
and
d_{\Omega}(x_{0})<2R
.
∎

The following iteration lemma is crucial in the Morrey–Campanato theory.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A,B,\alpha,\beta,R_{0}>0
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
, and assume
\alpha>\beta
.
Let
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a function satisfying

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)\leq A\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}+\varepsilon\right]\Phi(R)+BR^{\beta}\quad\text{for all }0<\rho<R\leq R_{0}.

Assume that for any
\tau\in(0,1)
, there exists
c_{0}=c_{0}(\tau)>0
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)\leq c_{0}\Phi(\bar{\rho})\quad\text{for all }0<\bar{\rho}\leq R_{0}\text{ and }\rho\in[\tau\bar{\rho},\bar{\rho}].

Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0},c>0
such that if
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)\leq c\left[\frac{\Phi(R)}{R^{\beta}}+B\right]\rho^{\beta}\quad\text{for all }0<\rho<R\leq R_{0}.

The constants
\varepsilon_{0}
and
c
depend on
A,\alpha,\beta
, and
c_{0}(\tau)
for some
\tau=\tau(A,\alpha,\beta)
.

Proof.

The proof goes as in
[
GM12
, Lemma 5.13]
, with the only difference that we use (
2.7
) instead of the assumption that
\Phi
is non-decreasing in the last display.
∎

Throughout this paper, we will apply
2.6
with
\Phi_{\sigma}(u,R):=\Phi_{\sigma}(u,R,x_{0})
and
\Psi_{\sigma}(u,R):=\Psi_{\sigma}(u,R,x_{0})
, which are defined in (
1.12
) and (

), respectively. It will require a significant amount of work to verify that
\Phi_{\sigma}(u,R)
and
\Psi_{\sigma}(u,R)
satisfy (
2.6
) when
u
is a solution to (
1.4
). However, verifying (
2.7
) is a straightforward consequence of the triangle inequality:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lipschitz domain and
u,\psi\in H^{s}(\Omega|\Omega^{\prime})\cap L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Assume that
0\leq\psi\leq c_{0}d_{\Omega}^{s}
in
\Omega_{1/4}
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1/8}
and
0<\bar{\rho}<\frac{1}{8}
. Then, for any
\tau\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in[\tau\bar{\rho},\bar{\rho}]
it holds

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})\leq c\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho}),\qquad\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})\leq c\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})

for some
c=c(n,s,c_{0},\tau)>0
.

Proof.

For
\Phi_{\sigma}
, we compute

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho)&\leq\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})+\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\rho^{n+2s-\sigma}\int_{B_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+2s-\sigma}}\textnormal{\,d}y\\
&\leq\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})+\frac{1}{\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{s}}\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}|u(y)|\textnormal{\,d}y\\
&\leq\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})+c\frac{\max\{\bar{\rho},d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{s}}{\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{s}}\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}y\\
&\leq c\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})\end{split}

for some
c=c(s,\tau)>0
.
For
\Psi_{\sigma}
, we proceed as follows, using (
2.4
):

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\rho)&\leq 2\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})+\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\rho^{n+2s-\sigma}\int_{B_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)-\psi(y)(u/\psi)_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}|}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+2s-\sigma}}\textnormal{\,d}y\\
&\leq 2\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})+\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}|u(y)-\psi(y)(u/\psi)_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\textnormal{\,d}y\\
&\leq 2\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho})+c\frac{\max\{\bar{\rho},d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{s}}{\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{s}}\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)-\psi(y)(u/\psi)_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}|}{\psi(y)}\textnormal{\,d}y\\
&\quad+\left(\int_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}\frac{\psi(y)}{\max\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{s}}\textnormal{\,d}y\right){\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{\psi}-\left(\frac{u}{\psi}\right)_{\Omega_{\bar{\rho}}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\\
&\leq c\Psi_{\sigma}(u;\bar{\rho}),\end{split}

where
c=c(n,s,c_{0},\tau)>0
.
∎

3.
Auxiliary results for weak solutions

In this section, we provide several auxiliary results for weak solutions to
Lu=f
in
\Omega
. The following lemma contains an important estimate for source terms
f
, satisfying
d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\in L^{q}(\Omega_{1})
, which will be used throughout the paper. Let us point out that such a class in particular includes source terms
f
that are in
L^{q}(\Omega_{1})
, and also source terms that might explode near
\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}
like
d_{\Omega}^{p-s}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain such that
\Omega^{c}
satisfies the measure density condition (
2.5
) with
c_{0}>0
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
R>0
, and let
f
be such that
d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\in L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))
for some
p\in(0,s]
and
q\in(\frac{n}{s+p},\infty]
. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}|fw|\textnormal{\,d}x\leq c|\{w\not=0\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p}{n}-\frac{1}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
w\in H^{s}_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
c=c(n,s,p,q,c_{0})>0
.
In particular, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}|fw|\textnormal{\,d}x\leq cR^{\frac{n}{2}+p-\frac{n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
w\in H^{s}_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
c=c(n,s,p,q,c_{0})>0
.

Proof.

By using Hölder’s inequality and the Hardy inequality (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}fw\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}^{s-p}}\right|^{\frac{2n}{n-2p}}\textnormal{\,d}x\right)^{\frac{n-2p}{2n}}|\{w\not=0\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p}{n}-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}[w]_{W^{s-p,\frac{2n}{n-2p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\{w\not=0\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p}{n}-\frac{1}{q}},

Here, we also used that that
q>\frac{n}{s+p}>\frac{2n}{n+2p}
, since
2s<n
. Then, since
w=0
outside
\Omega_{R}(x_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{s-p,\frac{2n}{n-2p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c[w]_{W^{s-p,\frac{2n}{n-2p}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}+c\left(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})^{c}}\frac{|w(x)|^{\frac{2n}{n-2p}}}{|x-y|^{n+(s-p)\frac{2n}{n-2p}}}\textnormal{\,d}y\textnormal{\,d}x\right)^{\frac{n-2p}{2n}}

\displaystyle\leq c[w]_{W^{s-p,\frac{2n}{n-2p}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}+cR^{-s+p}\|w\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2p}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

Moreover, we have the embedding

(3.3)

\displaystyle H^{s}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))\hookrightarrow W^{s-p,\frac{2n}{n-2p}}(B_{2R}(x_{0})),

which follows from
[
Tri06
, Equation (1.301)]
, using that the indices of the two fractional Sobolev spaces coincide, i.e.
s-p-(n/\frac{2n}{n-2p})=s-\frac{n}{2}
. Thus, the embedding (
3.3
) and
2.3
show that

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{s-p,\frac{n-2p}{2n}}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}+R^{-s+p}\|w\|_{L^{\frac{n-2p}{2n}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\left([w]_{H^{s}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}+R^{-s}\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\right)\leq c[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which proves (
3.1
). The second claim (
3.2
) follows once again from the fact that
q>\frac{n}{s+p}>\frac{2n}{n+2p}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The only property we need for
f
throughout this paper is the inequality (
3.2
). Thus, one may introduce a function space

\displaystyle X(\Omega_{1})=\left\{f\in(H_{\Omega_{1}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{\ast}:\|f\|_{X(\Omega_{1})}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X(\Omega_{1})}=\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega_{1/2},0<R<\frac{1}{2}}\,\sup_{w\in H^{s}_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\{w\not=0\}\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p}{n}-\frac{1}{q}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}fw\textnormal{\,d}x}{[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},

and take

instead of

with
d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\in L^{q}(\Omega_{1})
in our main theorems.

We have the following Caccioppoli-type inequality up to the boundary.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
and
f
be given as in
3.1
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
R>0
. Assume that
K
satisfies (
1.2
) and let

be a solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lu&=f&&\text{in }\Omega_{R}(x_{0}),\\

Then

\displaystyle[u]_{H^{s}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\leq cR^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+cR^{\frac{n}{2}-s}\tail(u;R,x_{0})+cR^{\frac{n}{2}+p-\frac{n}{q}}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}

The proof goes as in

, however since

The local boundedness estimate of solutions

to (

[

or

Lemma 3.4

Let

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\leq cR^{-n}\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+\tail(u;R,x_{0})+cR^{2s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}

for some

Proof.

The proof goes by a standard nonlocal De Giorgi iteration at the boundary, following for instance
[
KKP16
, Theorem 5]
(see also
[
KL23
, Theorem 3.1]
for a proof via Moser iteration). First, from Hölder’s inequality, the fractional Sobolev embedding, the Caccioppoli inequality (see
[

), and (

), we obtain the following gain of integrability estimate:

\displaystyle\|w_{\pm,k}^{2}\|_{L^{1}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq|A_{\pm,k}(r+\rho,x_{0})|^{\frac{2s}{n}}\|w_{\pm,k}^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-2s}}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq c|A_{\pm,k}(r+\rho,x_{0})|^{\frac{2s}{n}}\rho^{-2s}\|w_{\pm,k}^{2}\|_{L^{1}(B_{r+\rho}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\quad+c|A_{\pm,k}(r+\rho,x_{0})|^{\frac{2s}{n}}\left(\frac{r+\rho}{\rho}\right)^{n}\rho^{-2s}\|w_{\pm,k}\|_{L^{1}(B_{r+\rho}(x_{0}))}\tail(w_{\pm,k};r+\rho,x_{0})

\displaystyle\quad+c|A_{\pm,k}(r+\rho,x_{0})|^{1+2\left(\frac{s+p}{n}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}^{2},

where we denote

and

, where

,

. From here, we can derive an iterative scheme, following
[
KKP16
, Theorem 5]
, where the additional summand, coming from the source term

can be treated in a standard way (see for instance
[
Sch20
, Chapter 11]
), using only that

, by assumption. This yields the desired result.
∎

A crucial tool is the comparison estimate between solutions

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lu&=f&&\text{in }\Omega_{1},\\

We provide three estimates for

in the rest of this section. Let us begin with the energy estimate.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain such that

\displaystyle[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{2\sigma}\left([u]_{H^{s}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+R^{n-2s}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u-(u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})};R,x_{0})^{2}\right)

Note that by construction,

\displaystyle|K_{x_{0}}(x,y)-K(x,y)|\leq\Lambda\frac{\min\{|x-x_{0}|^{\sigma}+|y-x_{0}|^{\sigma},1\}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}.

\displaystyle\lambda[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

For

\displaystyle|K_{x_{0}}(x,y)-K(x,y)|\leq c|y-x_{0}|^{-n-2s+\sigma}\quad\forall x\in B_{R}(x_{0}),\penalty\ y\in B_{1}\setminus B_{2R}(x_{0}),

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}|u(x)-u(y)||w(x)|\frac{1}{|y|^{n+2s}}\textnormal{\,d}y\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}+\tail(u;1,0)\right)\|w\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{\frac{n}{2}+s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right)[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Finally, for
I_{5}
we use (
3.2
) and obtain

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{\frac{n}{2}+p-\frac{n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Note that the measure density condition (
2.5
) of
\Omega^{c}
is used here.
This proves the desired result after summing up all the previous estimates, and dividing by
[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
∎

Let us present two applications of the previous lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the same situation as in
4.1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|w\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}&\leq cR^{\sigma}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\right)\\
&\quad+cR^{n+2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+R^{-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)\end{split}

for some
c=c(n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\sigma,p,q,c_{0})>0
.

Proof.

By using
4.1
,
3.3
, and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{2\sigma}\left([u]_{H^{s}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+R^{n-2s}(u)_{R,x_{0}}^{2}+R^{n-2s}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+cR^{n+2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+R^{-2(s-p)-\frac{2n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq cR^{2\sigma-2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{4R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+R^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+cR^{n+2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+R^{-2(s-p)-\frac{2n}{q}}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}^{2}\right).

Moreover, since
\Omega_{8R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega_{1}
,
3.4
shows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{4R}(x_{0}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{-n}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{8R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n}\tail(u;4R,x_{0})+R^{n+2s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{-n}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n}\tail(u;R,x_{0})+R^{n+2s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)^{2}.

Combining these two estimates and using again (
2.3
) yield

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}&\leq cR^{-n+2\sigma-2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+cR^{n+2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+R^{-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

The desired result now follows from (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

In the same situation as in
4.1
, it holds that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x&\leq cR^{\sigma}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}R^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\right)\\
&\quad+cR^{n+s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+R^{-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)\end{split}

for some
c=c(n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\sigma,p,q,c_{0})>0
.

Proof.

In case
B_{2R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\not=\emptyset
, we observe by using Hölder’s inequality,
2.4
, and (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{n}\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}^{s}}\right|^{2}\textnormal{\,d}x\leq cR^{n}[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{2\sigma}\left(R^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n-s}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+cR^{2n+2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+R^{-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d^{s-p}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right)^{2}.

Since
d_{\Omega}\leq 3R
in
\Omega_{R}(x_{0})
and
\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}\leq 2R
, we obtain the desired estimate (
4.8
) in this case.

In case
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\geq d_{\Omega}(x_{0})+R\geq d_{\Omega}\geq d_{\Omega}(x_{0})-R\geq\frac{1}{2}d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0}).

By using
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq cd_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{-s}\|w\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{\sigma}\left(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+d_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{-s}R^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+cd_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{-s}R^{n+2s}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+R^{-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right).

Therefore, (
4.9
) together with
R\leq\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}=d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
proves (
4.8
) in this case.
∎

5.
C^{s-\varepsilon}
boundary regularity in flat Lipschitz domains via Morrey theory

The goal of this section is to prove the
C^{s-\varepsilon}
boundary regularity result for solutions to nonlocal equations in flat Lipschitz domains from
Theorem 1.7
. To prove it, we develop a nonlocal Morrey theory at the boundary.

5.1.
Morrey estimate in the translation invariant case

The goal of this subsection is to prove the following nonlocal Morrey-type estimate for solutions to translation invariant problems.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Lipschitz domain with Lipschitz constant
\delta>0
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
R>0
. Let
L
be a translation invariant operator with kernel
K
satisfying (
1.2
) and let
v
be a solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lv&=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{R}(x_{0}),\\
v&=0&&\text{in }B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

For given
\varepsilon\in(0,s)
, there exist
c,\delta_{0}>0
, depending only on
n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\varepsilon
, and
\Omega
, such that if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}R^{n}\tail(v;R,x_{0})\right)

for any
0<\rho<R
.

In order to prove
5.1
, we recall the following result from
[
ROW24
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Omega,x_{0},R
, and
L
be given as in
5.1
and let
v
be a solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lv&=f&&\text{in }\Omega_{R}(x_{0}),\\
v&=0&&\text{in }B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))
. For given
\varepsilon\in(0,s)
, there exist
c,\delta_{0}>0
, depending only on
n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\varepsilon
, and
\Omega
, such that if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, then

(5.1)

\displaystyle[v]_{C^{s-\varepsilon}(\overline{B_{R/2}(x_{0})})}\leq cR^{-(s-\varepsilon)}\left(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}+\tail(v;R,x_{0})+R^{2s}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}\right).

Proof.

The result was proved for distributional solutions in
[
ROW24
, Theorem 6.8]
(see also
[
FRRO24a
, Proposition 2.5.4]
for an analogous result for homogeneous kernels
K
) in case
R=1
. The result for general
R
follows immediately by rescaling. Note that in particular, it holds for weak solutions by
[
FRRO24a
, Lemma 2.2.32]
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

5.2
also holds for operators that are only translation invariant in a ball, as in (
6.12
). Indeed, if
\widetilde{L},L
are operators with kernels
\widetilde{K},K
, both satisfying (
1.2
), where
K
is translation invariant and
\widetilde{K}(x,y)=K(x,y)
for all
x,y
with
|x-y|<2R
, and
v
is a solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \widetilde{L}v&=f&&\text{in }\Omega_{R}(x_{0}),\\
v&=0&&\text{in }B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

then
v
solves
Lv=(L-\widetilde{L})v+f
in
\Omega_{R}(x_{0})
. Note that we have for
x\in\Omega_{R}(x_{0})

\displaystyle|(L-\widetilde{L})v(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)||K(x,y)-\widetilde{K}(x,y)|\textnormal{\,d}y

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}(|v(x)|+|v(y)|)|x_{0}-y|^{-n-2s}\textnormal{\,d}y

\displaystyle\leq cR^{-2s}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}+cR^{-2s}\tail(v;R,x_{0}).

Hence, by applying
5.2
, we get the exact same estimate (
5.1
) for
v
. This result will be used in Section
6
and applied to
\widetilde{K}_{x_{0}}
defined in (
6.12
).

As a consequence, we are in a position to show the Morrey-type estimate at the boundary for solutions to translation invariant problems.

Proof of
5.1
.

Let
\delta_{0}
be the constant given in
5.2
and assume
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
.
Note that we may assume without loss of generality that
\rho\leq R/8
.
Let us first prove the desired result for balls
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
such that
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\not=\emptyset
.
Since
v
vanishes on the boundary of
\Omega
and since, by assumption, for any
x\in B_{\rho}(x_{0})
, the projection to
\partial\Omega
is inside
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{2\rho}(x_{0})}d_{\Omega}^{-\varepsilon}(x)\sup_{y\in\Omega_{2\rho}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{|x-y|^{s-\varepsilon}}\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{2\rho}(x_{0})}d_{\Omega}^{-\varepsilon}\textnormal{\,d}x\right)[v]_{C^{s-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega_{2\rho}(x_{0})})}\leq c\rho^{n-\varepsilon}[v]_{C^{s-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega_{R/4}(x_{0})})},

where we applied
2.5
in the last estimate. Next, using the
C^{s-\varepsilon}
-boundary estimate from
5.2
, the local boundedness estimate (
3.4
), and the fact that
d_{\Omega}\leq 2R
in
\Omega_{R}(x_{0})
, we deduce

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x&\leq cR^{-(s-\varepsilon)}\rho^{n-\varepsilon}\left(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R/2}(x_{0}))}+\tail(v;R/2,x_{0})\right)\\
&\leq cR^{-s}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\varepsilon}\left(\|v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n}\tail(v;R,x_{0})\right)\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+R^{n-s}\tail(v;R,x_{0})\right).\end{split}

The desired result follows from (
5.2
) and the fact that
\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}=R
in case
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\neq\emptyset
.

Next, let us assume that
B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. In that case, we have

\displaystyle\frac{5}{4}d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\geq d_{\Omega}(x_{0})+\rho\geq d_{\Omega}\geq d_{\Omega}(x_{0})-\rho\geq\frac{3}{4}d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }B_{\rho}(x_{0}).

Note that if also
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}=\emptyset
, then by the local boundedness estimate (
3.4
) we obtain

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x&\leq cd_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{-s}\rho^{n}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\\
&\leq cd_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{-s}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n}\left(\|v\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+R^{n}\tail(v;R,x_{0})\right)\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\varepsilon}\left(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}R^{n}\tail(v;R,x_{0})\right),\end{split}

where we used in the last step that
d_{\Omega}\leq d_{\Omega}(x_{0})+R\leq 2d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
.

If
B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\not=\emptyset
, then we apply (
5.2
) with
\rho:=\rho
and
R:=d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
, and then observe that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{d_{\Omega}(x_{0})}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{2d_{\Omega}(x_{0})}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x.

The desired result follows from (
5.2
) applied with
\rho:=2d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
and
R:=R
.

Finally, note that if
B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\not\subset\Omega
and also
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}=\emptyset
, then we can just apply (
5.4
) first, and then use (
5.2
).
∎

5.2.
Regularity up to the boundary

In this section, we prove
Theorem 1.7
.

First, we establish the following Morrey-type estimate for solutions with respect to
L
, having
C^{\sigma}
continuous coefficients. Recall that the excess functional
\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho):=\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho,x_{0})
is given by (
1.12
) in the next lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that we are in the same setting as in
Theorem 1.7
. Then, there exist
c,\delta_{0}>0
, and
R_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{16})
, depending only on
n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\sigma,p,q,\varepsilon
, and
\Omega
, such that if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, then the following holds true:
For any
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1/2}
and
0<\rho\leq R\leq R_{0}
it holds that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho)\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n+\min\{p-\frac{n}{q},-\varepsilon\}}\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)+c\rho^{n+\min\{p-\frac{n}{q},-\varepsilon\}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right).

Proof.

Let
\delta_{0}
be the constant given in
5.1
and assume
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
.
We claim that for any
x_{0}\in\Omega_{1/2}
and
0<\rho\leq R<\frac{1}{16}
it holds

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(u;\rho)\leq c\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+R^{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)+cR^{n+s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right).

The desired result follows immediately from (
5.5
) by an application of the standard iteration lemma in
2.6
(with
\alpha:=n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
\beta=:n+\min\{p-\frac{n}{q},-\varepsilon\}<\alpha
, and recalling (
2.8
)).

Let
v
and
w
be solutions to (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively. As a consequence of
5.1
and
4.3
, we obtain using also (
2.3
) and the fact that
u=v
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega_{R}(x_{0})

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x&\leq\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\Phi_{\sigma}(v;R)+cR^{n+s}\tail(u;1,0)+\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)+cR^{n+s}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+c\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\\
&\leq c\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+R^{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)+cR^{n+s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right).\end{split}

From here, it remains to show that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\max&\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\rho^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;\rho,x_{0})\\
&\leq c\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+R^{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)+cR^{n+s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right).\end{split}

To prove this, let us first consider the case
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\not=\emptyset
. Then, we take
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{-m}R\leq\rho<2^{-m+1}R
and compute

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\{\rho,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}\rho^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;\rho,x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq c(2^{-m}R)^{n-s}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(2^{-m}R)^{2s-\sigma}\int_{B_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{k-m-1}R}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+2s-\sigma}}\textnormal{\,d}y

\displaystyle\quad+c(2^{-m}R)^{n-s}2^{-m(2s-\sigma)}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Note that for
I_{2}
, we can simply estimate since
\sigma<s
:

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n+s-\sigma}R^{n-s}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;R,x_{0})\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R).

For
I_{1}
, we deduce, using that
d_{\Omega}\leq 2(2^{k-m}R)
in
B_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})
and applying (
5.6
):

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c(2^{-m}R)^{-s}\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-k(n+2s-\sigma)}\int_{B_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})}|u|\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-k(n+s-\sigma)}\int_{\Omega_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{s}}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-k(n+s-\sigma)}\left[2^{(k-m)(n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}+R^{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)

\displaystyle\quad+c\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-k(n+s-\sigma)}R^{n+s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+R^{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)+cR^{n+s-(s-p)-\frac{n}{q}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|d_{\Omega}^{s-p}f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{1})}\right).

Here, the sums
\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-k(s-\sigma+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}
and
\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-k(n+s-\sigma)}
are finite since
\sigma<s
. This shows (
5.7
) in case
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\not=\emptyset
.

Next, we assume that
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. The proof of (
5.7
) goes by the exact same arguments as before, with the only difference that we have now
\max\{2^{k-m}R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}=\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}=d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
for every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}
and thus also
d_{\Omega}\leq 2d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
in
B_{2^{k-m}R}(x_{0})
, and that we use (
5.3
) instead of (
5.2
).

If
B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}\not=\emptyset
, we apply (
5.7
) with
\rho:=\rho
and
R:=d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
, then observe that

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}(x_{0})^{n-s}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(u;d_{\Omega}(x_{0}),x_{0})\leq c\Phi_{\sigma}(u;2d_{\Omega}(x_{0})),

and then use (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) with
\rho:=2d_{\Omega}(x_{0})
and
R:=R
to deduce the desired result.

Finally, note that if
B_{4\rho}(x_{0})\not\subset\Omega
and also
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega^{c}=\emptyset
, then we can just apply (
5.4
) first, and then use (
5.6
) and (
5.7
).
This establishes (
5.7
).
∎

Before we prove
Theorem 1.7
, we need the following consequence of the interior regularity for solutions to nonlocal equations with Hölder continuous coefficients (see

Theorem 6.1

Let

6.3.

In this section we establish our main result

Having at hand the functions

and

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,s)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha s)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a

Let

be a translation invariant operator with kernel

satisfying (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} Lv&=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{R}(x_{0}),\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{\psi}-\left(\frac{v}{\psi}\right)_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{n+\varepsilon}\left[\int_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{\psi}-\left(\frac{v}{\psi}\right)_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x+\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}R^{n}\mathrm{Tail}\left(v-\psi(v/\psi)_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})};R,x_{0}\right)\right],\end{split}

, and

Proof.

We may assume that
\rho\leq R/4
. Let us consider the case

Let
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let us denote by

the factor from the expansion in (

) corresponding to the projection of

to

for the function

6.2
(iii). Then, we estimate by using (

) and (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v}{\psi}-\left(\frac{v}{\psi}\right)_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{v-c_{0}\psi}{\psi}(x)-\left(\frac{v-c_{0}\psi}{\psi}\right)_{\Omega_{\rho}(x_{0})}\right|\textnormal{\,d}x

in

Let

7.2.

Before we prove the Hopf lemma (

Lemma 7.5

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(me_{n})\geq c\varepsilon^{-s}m^{s}\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall m\in(0,\varepsilon/2]

Thus, we have since

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma}(u;R)

\displaystyle\leq cR^{n}+cR^{n+\min\{\sigma-\varepsilon_{0},\alpha-\varepsilon_{0},p-\frac{n}{q}\}}+c(u/\phi)_{\Omega_{R}(x_{0})}\max\{R,d_{\Omega}(x_{0})\}^{-s}R^{n}\tail_{\sigma,B_{1}}(\phi;R,x_{0})

where we used in the last step that we have since for any

Lemma 9.3

Let

and

]

.

Let

,

), (

). Assume that (

) holds with

.

The proof goes by combination of (

) and

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\leq cR^{-1}\left(R^{-n}\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}+\tail(u;R,x_{0})\right),

where
c>0
depends only on
n,s,\lambda,\Lambda
, and
\sigma
.

Remark 9.8
.

The only reason why we exclude the case
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
from
1.2
is due to the lack of a suitable analog of
9.7
in the literature. We believe that in case
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, one can establish
9.7
for
K
satisfying (
1.2
), and (
1.3
) with
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and in addition that for some
\gamma>1-2s
:

(9.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{y\in B_{2\rho}(x)\setminus B_{\rho}(x)}|K(x,h+z)-K(x,z)|\leq\Lambda|h|^{\gamma}\rho^{-n-2s-\gamma}\quad\forall\rho>0\text{ and }|h|\leq\rho.

Proving it rigorously would be well beyond the scope of this paper. Hence, we only provide a sketch of the proof. We follow the strategy in
[
FRRO24b
, Theorem 4.2]
in the case
\beta=2s+\gamma>1
. Using the pointwise assumptions (
1.3
) and (
9.7
) on
K
instead of the integrated assumptions in
[
FRRO24b
]
, we obtain the desired estimate for
u\in C^{2s+\gamma}(B_{R}(x_{0}))\cap L^{1}_{2s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\tail(u;R,x_{0})
instead of the weighted
L^{\infty}
norm. Note that the global nonlocal energy from
[
FRRO24b
, Definition 4.1]
is not required to be finite for this proof, since it is based on rewriting
L
as a nonsymmetric operator in non-divergence form, which can be evaluated in the classical sense. Finally, we can drop the assumption
u\in C^{2s+\gamma}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
by an approximation argument, which goes in a similar way as in
[
FRRO24a
, Chapter 3.4.1]
but adapted to divergence form operators (see also
[
FR24
, Section 3]
).

and

.

Proof of
1.2
.

First, let us assume that
|x-y|\leq d_{\Omega}(x)
. Then, an application of
9.7
with
u:=G(\cdot,y)
,
R:=|x-y|/2
, and
x_{0}:=x
(this is possible by
9.2
) yields

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq c|x-y|^{-1}\left(|x-y|^{-n}\|G(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{1}(B_{|x-y|/2}(x))}+\tail(G(\cdot,y),|x-y|/2,x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq c|x-y|^{-1}\left(|x-y|^{-n}\|G(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{1}(B_{2|x-y|}(x))}+\tail(G(\cdot,y),2|x-y|,x)\right).

Clearly, using (
9.2
) with
\beta=s

\displaystyle|x-y|^{-n}\|G(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{1}(B_{2|x-y|}(x))}\leq|x-y|^{-n}\|G(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{1}(B_{3|x-y|}(y))}\leq c\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n+2s}.

Moreover, by using (
9.2
) with
\beta=s
again and observing that
|z-y|\geq|z-x|-|x-y|\geq|x-y|
for
z\in\Omega\setminus B_{2|x-y|}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\tail(G(\cdot,y),2|x-y|,x)

\displaystyle\leq c|x-y|^{2s}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{2|x-y|}(x)}\left(\frac{1}{|z-y|^{n-2s}}\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}^{s}(y)}{|z-y|^{n-s}}\right)|x-z|^{-n-2s}\textnormal{\,d}z

\displaystyle\leq c|x-y|^{2s}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2s}}\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}^{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{n-s}}\right)\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{2|x-y|}(x)}|x-z|^{-n-2s}\textnormal{\,d}z

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n+2s}.

Altogether, this shows

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|\leq c\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n+2s-1}\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall x,y\in\Omega\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ with }|x-y|\leq d_{\Omega}(x).

Next, let us consider the case
|x-y|>d_{\Omega}(x)
. In this case, we apply
9.7
with
u:=G(\cdot,y)
,
R:=d_{\Omega}(x)/2
, and
x_{0}:=x
. This yields

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|\leq c(d_{\Omega}(x))^{-1}\left(\|G(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{\Omega}(x)/2}(x))}+\tail(G(\cdot,y),d_{\Omega}(x)/2,x)\right).

We have by (
9.2
):

\displaystyle\|G(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{\Omega}(x)/2}(x))}\leq cd_{\Omega}^{s}(x)\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n+s}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\tail(G(\cdot,y),d_{\Omega}(x)/2,x)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\Omega}^{2s}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{|x-y|/2}(x)}G(z,y)|x-z|^{-n-2s}\textnormal{\,d}z

\displaystyle\quad+d_{\Omega}^{2s}(x)\int_{B_{|x-y|/2}(x)\setminus B_{d_{\Omega}}(x)/2}G(z,y)|x-z|^{-n-2s}\textnormal{\,d}z=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

For
T_{1}
, we follow the proof of
[
KKL23
, (4.14)-(4.16)]
but use (
9.2
) instead of (
1.6
) to obtain

\displaystyle T_{1}\leq cd_{\Omega}^{2s}(x)\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n}\leq cd_{\Omega}^{s}(x)\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n+s}.

For
T_{2}
, we compute, in case
d_{\Omega}(y)\geq|x-y|
, using that
|x-y|\leq 2|z-y|
and
d_{\Omega}(z)\leq d_{\Omega}(x)+|x-z|
for any
z\in B_{|x-y|/2}(x)\setminus B_{d_{\Omega}}(x)/2
:

\displaystyle T_{2}\leq cd_{\Omega}^{2s}(x)|x-y|^{-n+s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{d_{\Omega}(x)/2}(x)}\frac{d_{\Omega}^{s}(x)+|x-z|^{s}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\textnormal{\,d}z\leq cd^{s}_{\Omega}(x)|x-y|^{-n+s}.

A similar computation as before yields in case
d_{\Omega}(y)<|x-y|
for
T_{2}
:

\displaystyle T_{2}\leq cd_{\Omega}^{2s}(x)d_{\Omega}^{s}(y)|x-y|^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{d_{\Omega}(x)/2}(x)}\frac{d_{\Omega}^{s}(x)+|x-z|^{s}}{|x-z|^{n+2s}}\textnormal{\,d}z\leq cd^{s}_{\Omega}(x)d^{s}_{\Omega}(y)|x-y|^{-n}.

Altogether, we have proved

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)|\leq cd_{\Omega}^{s-1}(x)\left(1\wedge\frac{d_{\Omega}(y)}{|x-y|}\right)^{s}|x-y|^{-n+s}\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall x,y\in\Omega\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ with }|x-y|>d_{\Omega}(x).

∎

Families of Geodesic Orbit Spaces and Related Pseudo–Riemannian Manifolds Thanks: Research partially supported by a Simons Foundation grant
Joseph A. Wolf

Abstract
. Two homogeneous pseudo–riemannian manifolds ( G / H , d ​ s 2 ) (G/H,ds^{2}) and ( G ′ / H ′ , d ​ s ′ 2 ) (G^{\prime}/H^{\prime},ds^{\prime 2}) belong to the same real form family if their complexifications ( G ℂ / H ℂ , d ​ s ℂ 2 ) (G_{\mathbb{C}}/H_{\mathbb{C}},ds_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}) and ( G ℂ ′ / H ℂ ′ , d ​ s ℂ ′ 2 ) (G_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}/H_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime},ds_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime 2}) are isometric. The point is that in many cases a particular space ( G / H , d ​ s 2 ) (G/H,ds^{2}) has interesting properties, and those properties hold for the spaces in its real form family. Here we prove that if ( G / H , d ​ s 2 ) (G/H,ds^{2}) is a geodesic orbit space with a reductive decomposition 𝔤 = 𝔥 + 𝔪 \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m} , then the same holds all the members of its real form family. In particular our understanding of compact geodesic orbit riemannian manifolds gives information on geodesic orbit pseudo–riemannian manifolds. We also prove similar results for naturally reductive spaces, for commutative spaces, and in most cases for weakly symmetric spaces. We end with a discussion of inclusions of these real form families, a discussion of D’Atri spaces, and a number of open problems.

2.
The Moduli Spaces

The moduli spaces
\Omega

\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}
will allow us to carry the

GO

real and complex polynomials

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ by }\varphi(\xi,\alpha,\zeta)=\langle[\xi+\alpha,\zeta]_{\mathfrak{m}},\xi\rangle-c\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ by }\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi,\alpha,\zeta)=\langle[\xi+\alpha,\zeta]_{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}},\xi\rangle-c\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle

to
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}

\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\neq 0

only when
\xi
is null. Note that
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi,\alpha,\zeta)

is linear both in
\zeta
and in
\alpha
, and if

it is quadratic in

\xi
. Define subvarieties

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Omega=\{(\xi,\alpha)\in(\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{h})\mid\varphi(\xi,\alpha,\zeta)=0\text{ for every }\zeta\in\mathfrak{m}\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}=\{(\xi,\alpha)\in(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}})\mid\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi,\alpha,\zeta)=0\text{ for every }\zeta\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}\}.

As one might guess from the notation we have

.

The real affine variety
\Omega=\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}\cap(\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m})
, and
it is a real form of the complex affine variety
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}
. In other
words
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of
\Omega
.
In particular, if
f
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}

f|_{\Omega}\equiv 0
, then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

As
\varphi
and
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}
are linear in
\zeta

replace the “every
\zeta

2.2
)
by “
\{\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{\ell}\}
” where

\{\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{\ell}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{m}
. Now

\Omega
is defined by the
\ell

on
\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{h}
,
and
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by the

\varphi_{j;\mathbb{C}}:(\xi,\alpha)\mapsto\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi,\alpha,\zeta_{j})

We start by pinning down the moduli spaces

From the definitions

In the notation of

(

)

In the notation of
(
2.2
)

4.
Naturally Reductive Spaces

A homogeneous space
(G/H,ds^{2})
with a reductive decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}
,
{\rm Ad}(H)\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
, is called
naturally reductive

if

(4.1)

\text{if }\xi\in\mathfrak{m}\text{ then }t\mapsto\exp(t\xi)H\text{ is a complete geodesic in }(G/H,ds^{2}).

The Lie algebra formulation of (
4.1
) is

(4.2)

\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{m}},\zeta\rangle+\langle\eta,[\xi,\zeta]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=0\text{ for all }\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{m}.

The case
\zeta=\xi
is
\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{m}},\zeta\rangle+\langle\eta,[\xi,\zeta]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{m}},\xi\rangle+\langle\eta,[\xi,\xi]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle=\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{m}},\xi\rangle

spaces. Or one can see this by noting that (
4.1
) is the case

\alpha=0
of (
1.2
).

As in (
2.1
) one has corresponding polynomials

(4.3)

\displaystyle\psi:\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ by }\psi(\xi,\eta,\zeta)=\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{m}},\zeta\rangle+\langle\eta,[\xi,\zeta]_{\mathfrak{m}}\rangle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ by }\psi_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi,\eta,\zeta)=\langle[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}},\zeta\rangle+\langle\eta,[\xi,\zeta]_{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}}\rangle.

As in (
2.2
) those polynomials define corresponding moduli spaces

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Psi=\{(\xi,\eta,\zeta))\in(\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m})\mid\psi(\xi,\eta,\zeta)=0\}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathbb{C}}=\{(\xi,\eta,\zeta)\in(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{C}})\mid\psi_{\mathbb{C}}(\xi,\eta,\zeta)=0\}.

Then we have the analog of Proposition
2.3
:

Proposition 4.5
.

The real affine variety
\Psi=\Psi_{\mathbb{C}}\cap(\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m})
, and
it is a real form of the complex affine variety
\Psi_{\mathbb{C}}
. In other
words
\Psi_{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of
\Psi
.
In particular, if
f
is a holomorphic function on
\Psi_{\mathbb{C}}
and

f|_{\Psi}\equiv 0
, then
f\equiv 0
.

We reformulate the definition (

Proposition 4.6

In the notation of
(
4.4
)
,

(G/H,ds^{2})
is a naturally reductive space if and only if

(\xi,\eta,\zeta))\in(\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{m})
.
The crown
{\bf{cr}}\{\{(G/H,ds^{2})\}\}

Now we combine Propositions

In the notation of
(

)

(G/H,ds^{2})

is a naturally reductive space. In particular, if

7.
D’Atri Spaces

We say that

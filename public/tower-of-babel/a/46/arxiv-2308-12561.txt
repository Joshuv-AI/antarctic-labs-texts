A Theory of γ \gamma -factors for G 2 × GL r G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
Wee Teck Gan and Gordan Savin

Abstract




1.
Introduction

As the title of this paper indicates, our purpose is to develop a theory of local
\gamma
-factors

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)

for irreducible representations
\pi\otimes\tau
of the group
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
over a local field
F
, associated to the tensor product of the 7-dimensional representation of the Langlands dual group
G_{2}^{\vee}=G_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
and the
r
-dimensional standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}^{\vee}=\mathrm{GL}_{r}({\mathbb{C}})
. The reader may be expecting that this involves the discovery of an ingenious global zeta integral (in the sense of Tate’s thesis) which represents the relevant L-function and the proof of a local functional equation for the corresponding local zeta integral which can then be used to define the desired
\gamma
-factor. Such a reader will be disappointed, for this is not the route we take. Rather, we define these
\gamma
-factors by establishing a functorial lifting from
G_{2}
to the group
\mathrm{GL}_{7}
, associated to the embedding

G_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{7}({\mathbb{C}})

of dual groups, and then defining the
\gamma
-factor for
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
as the Rankin-Selberg
\gamma
-factor of
\mathrm{GL}_{7}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
. The functorial lifting needed for this definition is provided by the exceptional theta correspondences that we established in
[
GaSa
]
, together with the local lifting from
\mathrm{Sp_{6}}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{7}
(which is a special case of results of Arthur
[
Ar
]
).

Now the knowledgeable reader will no doubt question the utility of such an approach, so let us elaborate further. Any such potential theory of
\gamma
-factors is expected to satisfy a list of properties
[
Sh
,
LR
]
. The most important of these properties can be loosely summarized as:

•

Unramified property
: the
\gamma
-factors for unramified representations must be the expected ones, in the sense that they should agree with what is provided by the unramified local Langlands correspondence.

•

Multiplicativity
: the
\gamma
-factors should satisfy an inductive property with respect to parabolic induction. In particular, the
\gamma
-factors depend only on the cuspidal support of the representations and the
\gamma
-factor of non-supercuspidal representations can be deduced from simpler
\gamma
-factors of supercuspidal representations on proper Levi subgroups.

•

Global property
: over a number field
k
, the
\gamma
-factors can be used to provide the global functional equation for the corresponding global L-function of cuspidal representations.

Equally importantly, the above properties uniquely characterize the relevant
\gamma
-factors.

Given this, one would naturally ask if the
\gamma
-factors for
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
whose definition we outlined above satisfy such a list of properties and is uniquely characterized by it.
This is in fact the main result of this paper. More precisely, we show in Theorem
2.4
that the
\gamma
-factors for
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
that we defined satisfy a list of properties as above, with the caveat that the global property above is only established for a subset of cuspidal representations, instead of all of them. This may seem a less-than-ideal result, but Theorem
2.4
further asserts that this weaker list of properties already characrerizes the local
\gamma
-factors. Hence, if there is indeed a theory of
\gamma
-factors for
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
, the one we developed here is the only possible candidate. Whether it satisfies the full list of expected properties (for all cuspidal representations) remains to be seen.

While Theorem
2.4
provides a satisfactory justification for our definition of
\gamma
-factors, one may ask whether such a construction lends itself readily to concrete applications.
If one is thinking about arithmetic applications such as relating L-values to period integrals, then our construction indeed has very little to offer in that direction. However, the main motivation for constructing this theory of
\gamma
-factors is its use in characterizing the local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for
G_{2}
that we established in
[
GaSa2
]
.
See
[
GaSa2
, Main Theorem (ix) and (x)]
.

Using the same strategy, we also define the twisted adjoint
\gamma
-factor

\gamma(s,\pi,{\rm Ad}\times\chi,\psi)

for
\pi\otimes\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1})
and show that they are characterized by an analogous list of properties. We also deduce that the LLC for
G_{2}
respects these
\gamma
-factors as well.

2.
\gamma
-Factors for
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}

In this section, we give the full details of our construction of a theory of
\gamma
-factors for
G_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
outlined in the introduction.

2.1.
Recollection of
[
GaSa
]

We first recall some results from
[
GaSa
]
over a nonarchimedean local field
F
.
The main results of
[
GaSa
]
can be summarized as the construction of a map

{\rm Lif}:\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{7})

which is given in the following commutative diagram:

\begin{CD}{}\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2}){}\\
\Big\|\\
\mathrm{Irr}(PD^{\times})@<{\theta}<{}<\mathrm{Irr}^{\spadesuit}(G_{2})\sqcup\mathrm{Irr}^{\heartsuit}(G_{2})@>{\theta}>{}>\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{PGSp_{6}})\\
@V{}V{\rm JL}V@V{}V{\rm Lif}V@V{}V{\rm rest}V\\
\mathrm{Irr}_{ds}(\mathrm{PGL}_{3})@>{\boxplus}>{}>\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{7})@<{\rm Art}<{}<\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{Sp_{6}})/_{\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}}\end{CD}

Here,

•

the two
\theta
’s refer to the local theta correspondence;

•

D
is a cubic division
F
-algebra, so that
PD^{\times}
is an inner form of
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
;

•

\mathrm{Irr}^{\spadesuit}(G_{2})
and
\mathrm{Irr}^{\heartsuit}(G_{2})
are the subsets of
\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2})
which participate in the theta correspondence with
\mathrm{PD}^{\times}
and
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
respectively.

•

JL refers to the Jacquet-Langlands transfer from
PD^{\times}
to
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
;

•

rest refers to the restriction of representations of
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
to
\mathrm{Sp_{6}}
;

•

Art refers to the functorial transfer of irreducible representations from
\mathrm{Sp_{6}}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{7}
established by Arthur
[
Ar
]
;

•

\boxplus:\mathrm{Irr}_{ds}(\mathrm{PGL}_{3})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{7})
is the map

\tau\mapsto\tau\times 1\times\tau^{\vee}.

2.2.
Definition of
\gamma
-factors

With the above preparation, we can now define the local
\gamma
-factors.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi:F\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.
For
\pi\in{\rm Irr}(G_{2})
and
\rho\in{\rm Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})
, set

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi):=\gamma(s,{\rm Lif}(\pi)\times\rho,\psi).

where the
\gamma
-factor on the RHS is the Rankin-Selberg
\gamma
-factor defined by Jacquet-PS-Shalika and Shahidi
[
GeSh
]
.

2.3.
Compatibility with LLC

In
[
GaSa2
, Main Theorem (ix) and §3.4]
, we have shown that the LLC for
G_{2}
established there respects the above
\gamma
-factor:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field.
For
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2})
and
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})
, one has

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)=\gamma(s,({\rm std}\circ\phi_{\pi})\otimes\phi_{\rho},\psi)

where
\phi_{\pi}:WD_{F}\longrightarrow G_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\phi_{\rho}:WD_{F}\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{r}({\mathbb{C}})
are the L-parameters of
\pi
and
\rho
respectively and
{\rm std}:G_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{7}({\mathbb{C}})
is the standard degree 7 irreducible representation of
G_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
.

The reader will no doubt complain that our construction of the LLC map in
[
GaSa2
]
and our definition of the
\gamma
-factor here makes the above theorem almost a formality, which would not be inaccurate description. But the main question is then whether this
\gamma
-factor can be characterized by a list of properties independent of the LLC.
Our main result, Theorem
2.4
below, gives an affirmative answer.
Before coming to that, we define
\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)
in the archimedean setting for the sake of completeness.

2.4.
Archimedean case

In the archimedean setting, the (known) LLC allows one to define

{\rm Lif}:\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{7}).

More precisely, if
\phi_{\pi}
is the L-parameter of
\pi
, then
{\rm Lif}(\pi)
is characterized by

\phi_{{\rm Lif}(\pi)}={\rm std}\circ\phi_{\pi}.

If
\phi_{\rho}
denotes the L-parameters of
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})
, then we set:

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi):=\gamma(s,{\rm Lif}(\pi)\times\rho,\psi)=\gamma(s,\phi_{\pi}\otimes\phi_{\rho},\psi).

where the second
\gamma
-factor is that defined by Jacquet-PS-Shalika and Shahidi, and for the third
\gamma
-factor, we have regarded
\phi_{\pi}
and
\phi_{\rho}
as
7
-dimensional and
r
-dimensional representations respectively.

The main difference with the non-archimedean case is that we have used the LLC instead of the theta correspondence to define the lifting map
{\rm Lif}
.

2.5.
Cuspidal support

Returning to the setting of a non-archimedean local field
F
, we shall now investigate the behaviour of cuspidal support under the map
{\rm Lif}
.

Let
\mathcal{SC}(G)
denote the set of cuspidal supports of non-supercuspidal representations for the group
G
:

\mathcal{SC}(G)=\{[M,\tau]:\text{$M$ is a proper Levi subgroup of $G$ and $\tau\in\mathrm{Irr}_{sc}(M)$}\}

where the pairs
[M,\tau]
are taken up to
G(F)
-conjugacy.

For the group
G_{2}
, the cuspidal supports fall into 3 families, as there are 3 conjugacy classes of parabolic subgroups:

•

[T,\chi]
with
T
a maximal torus;

•

[M,\tau]
, with
M\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}
the Levi subgroup of a Heisenberg maximal parabolic subgroup
P
;

•

[L,\tau]
, with
L\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}
the Levi subgroup of a non-Heisenberg maximal parabolic subgroup
Q
.

Then we define

f:\mathcal{SC}(G_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{SC}(\mathrm{GL}_{7})

as follows:

•

f(T,\chi)=(T^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
, where
T^{\prime}
is the maximal diagonal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{7}
and
\chi^{\prime}
is given as follows. Let us identify the character
\chi
with a triple

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
of characters of
F^{\times}
with
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}=1
, as in
[
GaSa
, Pg. 16]
. Then

\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{1}\times\chi_{2}\times\chi_{3}\times 1\times\chi_{3}^{-1}\times\chi_{2}^{-1}\times\chi_{1}^{-1}.

•

f(M,\tau)=(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}^{3},\,\tau\otimes\tau^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{\tau}\otimes\omega_{\tau}^{-1}\otimes 1)

•

f(L,\tau)=(\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathcal{L},\,\tau\otimes\tau^{\vee}\otimes\rho_{\tau}),

where
(\mathcal{L},\rho_{\tau})
is the cuspidal support of the representation
{\rm Ad}(\tau)
of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
.
If
\tau
is a non-dihedral supercuspidal representation, then the adjoint lift
{\rm Ad}(\tau)
is a supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
. On the other hand, if
\tau
is dihedral, then
{\rm Ad}(\tau)
is non-supercuspidal and its
cuspidal support can be a maximal proper Levi subgroup subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
or even the maximal torus, depending on whether
\tau
is dihedral with respect to 1 or 3 quadratic fields.

Having introduced the map
f
, the following result follows from the explicit theta correspondence established in
[
GaSa
]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

For any non-supercuspidal
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2})
, one has

\mathcal{SC}({\rm Lif}(\pi))=f(\mathcal{SC}(\pi)).

In particular, if
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
have the same supercuspidal support, then so do
{\rm Lif}(\pi)
and
{\rm Lif}(\pi^{\prime})
.

2.6.
Properties and characterization of
\gamma
-factor

The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 2.4
.

The local
\gamma
-factors defined above for non-archimedean local fields satisfy and are uniquely characterized by the following properties:

(i)
\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)
depends only on the cuspidal support of
\pi
and
\rho
. More precisely,

(a)

If
\pi
is a subquotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G_{2}}\chi
, with
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
and
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3}=1
, then

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)=\gamma(s,\rho,\psi)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\gamma(s,\chi_{i}\times\rho)\cdot\gamma(s,\chi_{i}^{-1}\times\rho)\right).

(b)

If
\pi
is a subquotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G_{2}}\tau
, then

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)=

\gamma(s,\tau\times\rho,\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\tau^{\vee}\times\rho,\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\omega_{\tau}\times\rho,\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\omega_{\tau}^{-1}\times\rho,\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\rho,\psi).

(c)

If
\pi
is a subquotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{Q}^{G_{2}}\tau
, then

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)=\gamma(s,\tau\times\rho,\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\tau^{\vee}\times\rho,\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\rho_{\tau}\times\rho,\psi).

(d)

If
\rho
is a subquotient of
\rho_{1}\times\rho_{2}
, then

\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho_{1},\psi)\cdot\gamma(s,\pi\times\rho_{2},\psi).

In particular, the
\gamma
-factors for unramified representations are compatible with the unramified LLC by (a). Moreover, the
\gamma
-factors satisfy the property of multiplicativity.

(ii) Suppose we have the following data:

•

k
is a totally real number field with adele ring
{\mathbb{A}}
;

•

\psi:k\backslash{\mathbb{A}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is a nontrivial additive character;

•

\mathbb{O}
is an (not necessarily split) octonion
k
-algebra with automorphism group
G=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O})
, which is of type
G_{2}
.

•

\Pi
is a cuspidal representation of
G
such
\Pi_{v}
is spherical for all real places
v
where
G(k_{v})
is split.

•

\Sigma
is a cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
;

•

S
is a finite set of places
v
of
k
containing all archimedean ones and such that
\Pi_{v}
,
\Sigma_{v}
and
\psi_{v}
are unramified for
v\notin S
.

Suppose further that one of the two conditions holds:

(a)

\Pi
has a nonzero cuspidal global theta lift to the split group
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
.

(b)

\Pi
has a nonzero theta lift to

\mathbb{PD}^{\times}
for some central simple
k
-algebra
\mathbb{D}
of degree
3
(possibly split).

Then
L^{S}(s,\Pi\times\Sigma)
has meromorphic continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}
and satisfies the functional equation

L^{S}(1-s,\Pi\times\Sigma^{\vee})=\left(\prod_{v\in S}\gamma(s,\Pi_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})\right)\cdot L^{S}(s,\Pi\times\Sigma).

Before proving the theorem, we make two remarks:

Remarks:

(i) It is curious to note that Theorem
2.4
does not include the archimedean case. The main reason is that we are not entirely sure if
the key property of multiplicativity is satisfied. Namely if
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are constituents of the same principal series representation, does one have

\gamma(s,\phi_{\pi_{1}}\otimes\phi_{\rho},\psi)=\gamma(s,\phi_{\pi_{2}}\otimes\phi_{\rho},\psi)?

The two papers
[
A1
,
A2
]
of Martin Andler come very close to giving an affirmative answer to this question, but seem to be not as definitive as one might hope. This is an issue which certainly deserves to be definitively resolved, but we will not discuss it further here.

(ii) The properties stated in Theorem
2.4
are the usual ones used for characterizing
\gamma
-factors, except that the global property (ii) is not the most general. One would like to have the global property (ii) for all cuspidal representations
\Pi
of
G_{2}
.
One reason for this more restricted result is due to the fact that the relevant archimedean exceptional theta correspondences are not fully understood. Another is that the global lifting from
G_{2}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{7}
cannot be fully achieved by the exceptional theta correspondences considered here.
On the other hand, the uniqueness part of Theorem
2.4
, proved under this more constrained global property, is thus stronger than the usual version and implies that the theory of
\gamma
-factors developed here is the only possible candidate for such a theory.

2.7.
Proof of Expected Properties

We now show that the
\gamma
-factors defined in Definition
2.1
satisfy properties (i) and (ii) of the theorem.
The properties in (i) follow from the definition of the
\gamma
-factors, by using Proposition
2.3
and the multiplicativity of the Rankin-Selberg
\gamma
-factors.

Let us now place ourselves in the setting of (ii), so that
\Pi
is a cuspidal representation of
G=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O})
over
k
. We consider the situations (a) and (b) in turn.

(a)

Suppose first that we are in the situation (a) in (ii) of the theorem, so that

\Pi
has nonzero cuspidal global theta lift
\Theta(\Pi)
on
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
.

The local theta correspondence at the real places
v
such that
G(k_{v})
is compact is completely understood by the work of Gross-Savin
[
GrSa
]
.
The local theta lift of an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of compact
G_{2}
is the holomorphic discrete series of
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}(\mathbb{R})
with the associated infinitesimal character. If
G(k_{v})
is split and
\Pi_{v}
is spherical then
\Theta(\Pi_{v})
is a finite length module with unique (spherical) quotient by Loke-Savin
[
LoSa
]
. In these two cases
the local theta correspondence is functorial with respect to the inclusion
G_{2}(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spin}_{7}(\mathbb{C})
.

In view of the above,
\Pi^{\prime}=\Theta(\Pi)
is
irreducible and
\Pi^{\prime}_{v}\cong\theta(\Pi_{v})
at all places.
Let
\mathcal{A}(\Pi^{\prime})
denote its Arthur transfer to
\mathrm{GL}_{7}
.
Thus, we see that at all places
v
of
k
,

\mathcal{A}(\Pi^{\prime})_{v}\cong{\rm Lif}(\Pi_{v}).

In particular,

\gamma(s,\Pi_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})=\gamma(s,\mathcal{A}(\Pi^{\prime}_{v})\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})

and

L^{S}(s,\Pi\times\Sigma)=L^{S}(s,\mathcal{A}(\Pi^{\prime}_{v})\times\Sigma).

so the desired global functional equation follows from that for the Rankin-Selberg L-functions for
\mathcal{A}(\Pi^{\prime})\otimes\Sigma
on
\mathrm{GL}_{7}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
.

(b)

Suppose now that we are in situation (b) in (ii) of the theorem.
In this case
\Pi
has a global theta lift
\Theta(\Pi)
on
\mathrm{Aut}(D)
.

At real places, the Howe duality theorem holds for spherical representations by results of Loke-Savin
[
LoSa
]
. In particular,
\Theta(\Pi)
is irreducible and all its
local components are isomorphic to
\theta(\Pi_{v})
. At any real place
v
,
the L-parameters of
\theta(\Pi_{v})
and
\Pi_{v}
are related by the inclusion

\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow G_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of dual groups.

The lift of
\Pi
to
\mathbb{P}\mathbb{D}^{\times}
is obtained by restricting
\Theta(\Pi)
to
\mathbb{P}\mathbb{D}^{\times}
. Let
\Pi^{\prime}
be any irreducible summand.
Then
\Pi^{\prime}
has a Jacquet-Langlands transfer
\mathrm{JL}(\Pi^{\prime})
to
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
. At all places
v
, we thus have

\gamma(s,\Pi_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})=\gamma(s,\psi_{v})\cdot\gamma(s,\mathrm{JL}(\Pi^{\prime})_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})\cdot\gamma(s,\mathrm{JL}(\Pi^{\prime})^{\vee}_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v}).

Now the desired global functional equation for
\Pi\times\Sigma
follows from that for the Rankin-Selberg L-function of
JL(\Pi^{\prime})\times\Sigma
on
\mathrm{GL}_{3}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r}
.

The completes the proof that the
\gamma
-factors defined in Definition
2.1
satisfy properties (i) and (ii) of the theorem.

2.8.
Proof of Uniqueness

Suppose that we have two systems of invariants
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
as in the theorem.
By properties (a), (b) and (c) in (i), we deduce the equality

\gamma_{1}(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)=\gamma_{2}(s,\pi\times\rho,\psi)

when
\pi
is non-supercuspidal. When
\pi
is supercuspidal, property (d) of (i) reduces us to showing the above equality when

\rho
is supercuspidal as well. For the supercuspidal case, we shall exploit the global property (ii). Let
k
be a totally real field such that
k_{v_{0}}\cong F
for some place
v_{0}
.
Let
\Sigma
be a cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
such that
\Sigma_{v_{0}}\cong\rho
.
There are now two cases:

Case (i)
:
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{sc}^{\heartsuit}(G_{2}(F))
. Thus the theta lift
\theta(\pi)
to
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
is non-zero. It is a tempered representation, since this
theta correspondence preserves tempered representations. Let
P=MN
be a Siegel parabolic in
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
.
Open
M
-orbits on
\hat{N}
are parameterized by quaternion algebras.
Since
\theta(\pi)
is tempered, there exists a quaternion algebra
B
such that

\theta(\pi)_{N,\psi_{B}}\neq 0
, where
\psi_{B}
is a character of
N
in the
M
-orbit parameterized by
B
.
(Indeed, non-trivial representations of
\mathrm{Sp_{6}}
such that twisted
N
-coinvaraints vanish for characters in open orbits are theta lifts from
\mathrm{O}(2)

[
Li
]
, and these are not tempered.)
Let
O
be an octonion algebra over
F
, so that

\mathrm{Aut}(O)
is the split group of type
G_{2}
. Then
B\hookrightarrow O
, embedding unique up to conjugation. Then
\pi
has a non-trivial

\mathrm{Aut}(B\hookrightarrow O)\cong SL_{1}(B)
-invaraint linear form. Pick a totally definite octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}
over
k
, that is,
\mathbb{O}_{v}
is definite for all real places
v
.
Pick a totally definite quaternion algebra
\mathbb{B}
such that
\mathbb{B}_{v_{0}}\cong B
. Then
\mathbb{B}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O}
.
By a result of Prasad-Schulze-Pillot
[
PSP
]
, one can find an
automorphic representation
\Pi
of
G=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O})
such that

•

\Pi_{v_{0}}\cong\pi
;

•

\Pi_{v}
is a spherical representation for all finite
v\neq v_{0}
;

•

\Pi
has a nonvanishing global
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{B}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{O})
-period.

Now
\Pi
has a non-zero lift to
\mathrm{PGSp_{6}}
, since it has a non-vanishing
\psi_{\mathbb{B}}
-Fourier coefficient, see
[
GrSa
]
. Assume that
\theta(\pi)
is supercuspidal. Then
\Theta(\Pi)
is a cupidal automorphic representation and
we can apply the global functional equation (assumption (ii) (a) in the theorem)
to the two system of invariant
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, we obtain:

L^{S}(1-s,\Pi\times\Sigma^{\vee})=\left(\prod_{v\in S}\gamma_{1}(s,\Pi_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})\right)\cdot L^{S}(s,\Pi\times\Sigma)

and

L^{S}(1-s,\Pi\times\Sigma^{\vee})=\left(\prod_{v\in S}\gamma_{2}(s,\Pi_{v}\times\Sigma_{v},\psi_{v})\right)\cdot L^{S}(s,\Pi\times\Sigma).

Since

\gamma

\gamma_{1}(s,\pi\times\sigma,\psi)=\gamma_{2}(s,\pi\times\sigma,\psi).

If

the local component

is a supercuspidal representation
that lifts to a supercuspidal representation of

Observe that we have so far proved uniqueness of gamma factors for all

\mathrm{PGL}_{3}

\Pi

Proof.

We have
\pi=\theta(\tau)
for

. Let
P=MN
be a Heisenberg maximal parabolic in
G
. Open
M
-orbits of characters of

of

in the

.
Then we know that
\tau
has a nonzero

-invariant linear form.
Let

be a cubic field extension of
k

. Let
v_{1}
be any other place such that

is a field. Let

e\hookrightarrow\mathbb{D}
.

3.

2.4
. In particular, in the situations of Theorem
2.4
(ii), the global partial adjoint L-function has meromorphic continuation and satisfies the analogous global functional equation with these local
\gamma
-factors.

Using the fact that the LLC for

\gamma

.

The twisted adjoint
\gamma

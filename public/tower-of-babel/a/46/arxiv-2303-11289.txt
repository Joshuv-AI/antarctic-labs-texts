The Porous Medium Equation: Large Deviations and Gradient Flow with Degenerate and Unbounded Diffusion
Benjamin Gess, Daniel Heydecker

Abstract
. The problem of deriving a gradient flow structure for the porous medium equation which is thermodynamic , in that it arises from the large deviations of some microscopic particle system is studied. To this end, a rescaled zero-range process with jump rate g ⁡ ( k ) = k α , α > 1 g(k)=k^{\alpha},\alpha>1 is considered, and its hydrodynamic limit and dynamical large deviations are shown in the presence of both degenerate and unbounded diffusion. The key superexponential estimate is obtained using pathwise discretised regularity estimates in the spirit of the Aubin-Lions-Simons lemma. This allows to exhibit the porous medium equation as the gradient flow of the entropy in a thermodynamic metric via the energy-dissipation inequality.

(Large Deviations Principle)

A regularity-based approach to the replacement lemma

We now turn to the main technical tool which will be needed in the derivation of Theorems
1
-
2
. As is common in the hydrodynamic limits and large deviations of lattice gasses, we will need to take a limit of terms similar to
\int_{0}^{t}\langle(\Delta\varphi),(\eta^{N}_{s})^{\alpha}\rangle ds
which are nonlinear in the particle configuration
\eta^{N}_{s}
, and where convergence of
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
is not sufficient; this is usually called a
replacement lemma
or
superexponential estimate
(e.g.
[
48
, Theorem 3.1]
, Theorem 2 of Benois, Kipnis and Landim
[
6
]
, or Theorem 2 in Kipnis, Olla and Varadhan
[
47
]
).

As already remarked, the usual approach to the superexponential estimate in the previously cited works cannot be applied for the rescaled ZRP, and the difficulties correspond exactly to the features of degenerate and unbounded diffusivity that we aim to study. Firstly, the presence of degenerate diffusivity leads to a vanishing minimum rate; if one attempts to carry out the usual argument leading to the one-block estimate, the control provided by the Dirichlet form degenerates as
N\to\infty
, and the requisite compactness is lost. At the same time, the superlinear growth of the local jump rate
(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}
means that the functionals
\langle\Delta\varphi,(\eta^{N}_{t})^{\alpha}\rangle
could diverge in the limit
N\to\infty
for a nontrivial set of times
t
. Since the local jump rate
(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}
corresponds to the mobility
u^{\alpha}
in the limit, this is the possibility of the explosion of the total mobility described above (
1.1
). In order to establish the superexponential estimate, we must establish a sufficiently strong integrability estimate to show that such divergences occur at most for a small set of times, and in fact the contributions to the error from any small space-time region are small, with superexponentially high probability.

The proof developed in this work is inspired by techniques in the theory of stochastic partial differential equations
[
22
,
31
,
32
]
and the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma
[
4
,
53
,
68
]
. For the case of a lattice-based model, as we deal with here, the situation is complicated by the fact that the regularity is measured by different functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}
for each
N
, whose
\Gamma
-convergence in the limit is not immediate. This issue further cannot be remedied by a convenient choice of
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}
, since these are dictated by the fundamental regularity and cannot be freely chosen.

Before stating the theorem, let us first introduce the entropy dissipation which is central to the argument. It is a classical computation that, along solutions to (
1.1
)
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}(u_{t})=-\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{t})\leq 0

(1.11)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u)=\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|\nabla(u^{\alpha/2})|^{2}(x)dx.

This estimate is also central to the theory of (

[

(1.12)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\mathcal{H}(u_{t})+\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{t})dt\leq\mathcal{H}(u_{0})+c\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.\end{split}

In particular, solutions to (
1.6

g
always take values in the space
{\mathcal{R}}\subset L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
of paths which are continuous in

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{s})ds<\infty
, see (

Theorem 3

(Large Deviations Weak-to-Strong Principle)
.

Suppose the scaling relation (

i).

(Open Sets Version) For any

(1.15)

\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{U}\right)\leq-z.

(Almost-Sure-Convergence Version) For

\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}H\left({\rm Law}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\eta^{N}_{\bullet}]\right|\left.{\rm Law}_{\mathbb{P}}[\eta^{N}_{\bullet}]\right)<\infty

Having achieved the probabilistic parts of the strategy, we turn to the derivation of a gradient flow structure, hence resolving the problem described below (
1.1

[

1
]

We consider the space of absolutely continuous measures
\mathcal{M}_{ac,\lambda}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

\mathbb{T}^{d}

\lambda>0

(1.17)

\mathcal{A}(u_{\bullet})=\frac{1}{2}\inf\left\{\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}^{2}\right\}.

In this definition, the infimum runs over all

\partial_{t}u_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\frac{1}{2}u^{\alpha/2}_{t}\theta_{t})=0

Theorem 4
.

Let

2. Relation to the Zero-Range Process
The limits of the zero-range process for general jump rates, assuming bounded diffusivity, as well as fluctuation theorems about these limits, have been widely studied. The hydrodynamic limit, see
[
48
]
, is a nonlinear parabolic equation
\partial_{t}u=\Delta\Phi(u)
, with globally bounded and locally nondegenerate diffusivity
\sup_{\rho}\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)<\infty,\inf_{\rho\leq M}\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)>0
. Let us cite the works by Menegaki
[

and Menegaki and Mouhot

for a more recent hydrodynamic limit with an explicit rate of convergence. Equilibrium large deviations for a variant of the zero-range process have been studied by Bernardin, Gonçalves, Jiménez-Oviedo and Scotta in
[

[
50
]
and Landim and Mourragui
[
51
]
. Quastel, Rezakhanlou and Varadhan
[

found the analagous rate function for the simple symmetric exclusion process.

As already mentioned below Theorem

, an important difference to previous works is that we obtain a full large deviation principle, with matching upper and lower bounds and the infimum of (

) running over the whole open set
\mathcal{U}
, rather than being restricted to

for a class of ‘good’ paths

. This is achieved through two steps, namely the exclusion of paths outside

by Theorem

as the lower semicontinuous envelope of a restriction

(Proposition

[

]
. This property is known for relatively few of the models whose large deviations have been studied. In the case

]
, the rate function is globally convex and such approximations can be obtained by convolution, and in works on reaction-diffusion systems
[

,
11
,
52

, the rate is a pertubation of a convex functional by a lower-order term. In
[

8

In other works, for example Quastel and Yau
[

, the rate function is defined as a lower semicontinuous envelope, so that this property holds by definition, but lack a corresponding explicit characterisation. A counterexample in a different setting, where the naïve rate function does not coincide with the lower semicontinuous envelope of its restriction, has been found by the second author

5. PME as a gradient flow

As discussed in the introduction, the problem of identifying a thermodynamic gradient flow structure for (
1.1
) has stood open since the works of Brézis and Otto
[
12
,
60
]
. Theorem
4
is one resolution of this problem by extending, to the case of degenerate and unbounded diffusivity, the connection between large deviations and gradient flow
[
1
,
56
,
30
]
, and more specifically the connection via the EDI
[
2
,
21
]
.

We first start by recalling the state of the art in the nondegenerate and boundedly diffusive setting. In
[
21
]
, for nondegenerate and sublinear nonlinearity
\Phi(u)
replacing
u^{\alpha}
in (
1.1
), it is shown how the identity analogous to (
1.19
) arises from large deviations of the ZRP with Lipschitz jump rates, and how this identity corresponds to the EDI. The corresponding gradient flow is in a Riemannian structure determined by the underlying ZRP, which is defined by modifying the Otto calculus
[
60
]
by replacing the weight
u
by
\Phi(u)
in the Riemannian tensor. This Riemannian structure leads to a global metric which may be defined in the same way as the Benamou-Brenier formulation for the Wasserstein distance: One defines the action
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}
of a curve in the same way as (
1.17
), with
\Phi(u)
in place of
u^{\alpha}
, and defines the distance between two profiles
u_{0},u_{1}
as the infimal action
\mathcal{A}_{\Phi}(u_{\bullet})
of all curves starting at
u_{0}
and ending at
u_{1}
. The modified Otto calculus also permits to identify the action as
\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}g_{u_{t}}(\dot{u}_{t},\dot{u}_{t})dt
, for a Riemannian metric
g_{u}
for which the tangent space may be identified in the same way as (
1.20
). For these cases, where a global metric structure may be defined, the identity corresponding to (
1.19
) is the entropy dissipation identity, which gives an equivalent formulation of the gradient flow property
[
3
,
65
,
66
,
30
]
. In addition, in these cases further connections between the large deviations rate function and gradient flow structure are possible, for instance the convergence of a JKO scheme
[
45
,
1
,
56
]
.

Let us now contrast this nondegenerate and boundedly diffusive case with the case of degenerate and unbounded diffusivity of interest here. Replacing the nonlinearity
\Phi(u)
in the arguments of Dirr et al.
[
21
]
by the porous medium nonlinearity
u^{\alpha},\alpha>1
, introduces both degenerate diffusivity at
u=0
, and unbounded diffusivity as
u\to\infty
. As a result, the formal Riemann structure no longer gives a well-defined global geometry: The distance between two profiles may become zero, as a result of unbounded diffusivity, or infinite, as a result of degeneracy. For completeness, the formal Riemannian metric and gradient flow structure are recalled in Appendix
A
.
As a result, characterisations of the gradient flow involving the metric, such as the JKO scheme, are no longer meaningful.

The approach in Theorem
4
therefore consists of rigorously validating the EDI via the connection to the rate function, extending the connection
[
21
,
1
,
2
,
56
]
, and in this way we resolve the problem of selecting a gradient flow corresponding to a microscopic model for the PME.

The second part of Theorem
4
shows that the gradient of the entropy in the formal Otto calculus is indeed an element of the tangent space according to (
1.20
). The final part of the theorem makes the formal computation above (
1.11
) precise for solutions to the PME (
1.1
) no weaker than the solution concept of
[
31
]
.

Notably, Theorem
4
even extends what is known in the setting of nondegenerate and bounded diffusivity, since the formal calculations of
[
1
,
21
]
are implicitly restricted to fluctuations where each
u_{t}
is regular enough to satisfy a ‘chain rule for the entropy’, see the display above
[
21
, Equation (2.3)]
and Erbar
[
27
, Proposition 4.1]
for a similar calculation in a different context. The argument in Section
11
circumvents these difficulties by using the large deviations principle and the machinery of the skeleton equation (
1.6
) from
[
31
]
, thereby removing any additional assumptions on the path
u
aside from the finiteness of the objects in (
1.19
).

Gradient flow structures for the PME (
1.1
) are well-known: Brézis
[
12
]
formulated it as the gradient flow of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(u)=\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int u^{\alpha+1}
with respect to a flat
H^{-1}
-metric, and Otto
[
60
]
showed that is the gradient flow of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\alpha}(u)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int u^{\alpha}
with respect to a geometry inducing the Wasserstein
2
distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}
. As already remarked, the microscopic model studied here is not the only model which leads to the PME (
1.1
) as a macroscopic limit, and other choices of the underlying particle system may lead to different large deviation rate functions, and hence other gradient flow formulations. In particular, the energy functional
\alpha\mathcal{H}
and the geometry defined by
\mathcal{A}
should be thought of as intrinsic
to the class of particle systems whose large deviations are given by Theorem
2
, and not to the PME (
1.1
).

3.1.
Formal Definitions.

We now give some technical definitions, which allow us to give precise statements of the main results, as well as fixing some frequently used notation.

1. Lebesgue Spaces on the Discrete Lattice and Torus; Particle State Space.

We will frequently work with Lebesgue spaces on the discrete lattice, which we define so as to be included in
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. For each
N
, let
(c^{N}_{x},x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})
be a partition of
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
into translations of
[0,N^{-1})^{d}
, with centres
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
, and let
\mathcal{B}_{N}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{c^{N}_{x},x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\}
. We then take
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}):=L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}},\mathcal{B}_{N})
to be the
p
-integrable functions on the whole torus
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
which are measurable with this
\sigma
-algebra, and hence constant on each
c^{N}_{x}
. This is trivially isometrically isomorphic to the usual meaning of
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})
, and this viewpoint is helpful in that we can view
\eta^{N}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})
as
either
a function on the lattice (for example, using Sobolev inequalities on the lattice in Lemma
5.3
), or as a function on the whole torus
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
. We will indicate
\cdot_{\geq 0}
the subset of a Lebesgue space consisting of nonnegative functions.

We now define the state space for the particle configurations as

X_{N}:=\left\{\eta^{N}\in L^{1}_{\geq 0}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}):\eta^{N}(x)\in\chi_{N}\mathbb{N}\text{ for all }x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\right\}

which corresponds to the informal description of the ZRP by setting
\eta^{N}=k\chi_{N}
on
c^{N}_{x}
to represent
k
particles of height
\chi_{N}
at the lattice site
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. The identification of
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})\subset L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
above has the immediate benefit that all particle state spaces are embedded into a common space, namely
L^{1}_{\geq 0}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. For
a\in[0,\infty)
, we write
X_{N,a}
for the subset

(3.1)

X_{N,a}:=\left\{\eta^{N}\in X_{N}:\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\eta^{N}dx\leq a\right\}.

2. Generator and Exponentially Transformed Generator.

We now make precise the definition of the particle dynamics by specifying the generator, for
F_{N}:X_{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, by

(3.2)

\mathcal{L}_{N}F_{N}(\eta^{N}):=\frac{dN^{2}}{\chi_{N}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p^{N}(x,y)(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}\left(F_{N}(\eta^{N,x,y})-F_{N}(\eta^{N})\right)

where
\eta^{N,x,y}
is the particle configuration obtained by moving one particle from site
x
to site
y
:

(3.3)

\eta^{N,x,y}=\eta^{N}-\chi_{N}e^{N}_{x}+\chi_{N}e^{N}_{y}

where
e^{N}_{x}
is the indicator function for the cube
c^{N}_{x}
. It is immediate to check that the rates of
\mathcal{L}_{N}
are bounded on each space
X_{N,a}
, and these spaces are invariant because
\mathcal{L}_{N}F_{N}\equiv 0
for any functional of the form
F_{N}(\eta^{N}):=f_{N}(\langle 1,\eta^{N}\rangle)
. It follows that
\mathcal{L}_{N}
generates a Markovian semigroup, that for each initial measure the generator corresponds to a unique-in-law process
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
, and that the process is globally defined.

We will frequently work with exponential martingales constructed by the (nonlinear) exponentially transformed generator, see the book of Feng and Kurtz
[
34
]
. For a functional
F_{N}:X_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, the action of the exponentially transformed generator is defined by

(3.4)

\mathcal{G}_{N}F_{N}(\eta^{N}):=\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}e^{-N^{d}F_{N}(\eta^{N})/\chi_{N}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{N}e^{N^{d}F_{N}/\chi_{N}}\right)(\eta^{N})

which is defined such that

(3.5)

\exp\left(\frac{N^{d}}{\chi_{N}}\left(F_{N}(\eta^{N}_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{N}F_{N}(\eta^{N}_{s})ds\right)\right)

is a nonnegative local martingale, and hence a supermartingale.

4. Entropy and Entropy Dissipation.

For

\mathcal{H}_{\rho}(u):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\left(\frac{u(x)}{\rho(x)}\log\frac{u(x)}{\rho(x)}-\frac{u(x)}{\rho(x)}+1\right)dx

\begin{split}{\mathcal{R}}:&=\left\{u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}:u^{\alpha/2}\in L^{2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}],\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))\right\}\\
&=\left\{u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}:\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{s})ds<\infty\right\}\end{split}

where

is defined above and we have used the definition (

. Let us remark, for future reference, that we have the equality

\begin{split}{\mathcal{R}}&=\left\{u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}:u^{\alpha/2}\in L^{2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}],H^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))\right\}\\
&=\left\{u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}\cap L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}):\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{s})ds<\infty\right\}\end{split}

by using the fact that

3.2.
Equilibrium & Slowly Varying Local Equilibrium Distributions

As is common in the theory of the zero-range process, a straightforward computation shows that the process
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
admit a one-parameter family of reversible invariant measures, which we write as

\Pi^{N}_{\rho}(\eta^{N}):=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\pi^{N}_{\rho}(\eta^{N}(x)),\qquad\rho>0,\eta^{N}\in X_{N}

Here,

, we have

(5.20)

\|\eta^{N,\delta}\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\beta}\leq C(1+a)(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}))

for some
\beta>\alpha
, and the same extends to
\|\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}\|_{L^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{\beta}
by Jensen’s inequality.

Step 1. From
\eta^{N,\delta}
to
\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

We begin with the observation that, for any
\alpha\geq 1
and for all
w,z\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
,

(5.21)

(w^{\alpha/2}-z^{\alpha/2})^{2}\geq\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}\delta^{|\alpha-2|}(z-w)^{2}

which implies that

(5.22)

\mathcal{D}_{2,N}(\eta^{N,\delta})\leq C\delta^{-|\alpha-2|}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N})

for some
C=C(\alpha)
independent of
N,\delta,r
. We now apply the case
\alpha=2
to
\eta^{N,\delta}
to find

(5.23)

\left\|\eta^{N,\delta}-\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}\leq C\delta^{-|\alpha-2|}(1+a)r^{\gamma_{0}}\left(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N})\right)

where
\gamma_{0}
is the exponent in the case
\alpha=2
.

Step 2. From
\eta^{N,\delta}
to
\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}
in
L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

We now convert (
5.23
) to an estimate in
L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, differentiating between the cases
\alpha\leq 2,\alpha>2
. In either case, we will prove that

(5.24)

\begin{split}\left\|\eta^{N,\delta}-\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}&\leq C(1+a)\delta^{-\lambda_{1}}r^{\lambda_{2}}(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}))\end{split}

for some exponents
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
depending only on
\alpha
.

2a. Case 1:
\alpha\leq 2
.

In the case
\alpha\leq 2
, the monotonicity of
L^{p}
norms implies that (
5.23
) still holds with the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
norm in place of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
. The claim (
5.24
) follows by raising both sides to the power of
\frac{\alpha}{2}\leq 1
.

2b. Case 2:
\alpha>2
.

In this case, we recall that
\eta^{N,\delta}
and
\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}
are bounded in
L^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
by (
5.20
). The claim now follows by recalling that
\alpha\in(2,\beta)
and using the interpolation

(5.25)

\begin{split}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}&\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\frac{2(\beta-\alpha)}{\beta-2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\frac{\beta(\alpha-2)}{\beta-2}}\end{split}

for
f\in L^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

Step 3. From
\eta^{N}
to
\eta^{N,\delta}
in
L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

Finally, let us estimate the error induced by the truncation. Using a Chebychev estimate for the truncation at
\delta^{-1}
, we estimate

(5.26)

\|\eta^{N,\delta}-\eta^{N}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq\delta^{\alpha}+\delta^{\beta-\alpha}\left\|\eta^{N}\right\|_{L^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\beta}

and by Jensen’s inequality the same extends to
\|\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}-\overline{\eta}^{N,r}\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
. Recalling (
5.20
) again, we find that

(5.27)

\begin{split}&\left\|\eta^{N,\delta}-\eta^{N}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}+\left\|\overline{\eta}^{N,\delta,r}-\overline{\eta}^{N,r}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\leq C(1+a)(\delta^{\alpha}+\delta^{\beta-\alpha})(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N})).\end{split}

Step 4. Reassembly and Conclusion.

Gathering (
5.24
) and (
5.27
), we find that for any
\delta<1
,

(5.28)

\left\|\eta^{N}-\overline{\eta}^{N,r}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}\leq C(1+a)(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}))\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\delta^{\beta-\alpha}+\delta^{-\lambda_{1}}r^{\lambda_{2}}\right).

We now take
\delta=r^{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\min(\alpha,\beta-\alpha)+\lambda_{1}}}\in(0,1)
to conclude the claim for a new exponent
\gamma
. ∎ The final lemma we will need before turning to the proof of Lemma
5.1
establishes a consistency between the averaging
\eta^{N}\mapsto\overline{\eta}^{N,r}
on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
and the continuum averaging
u\mapsto\overline{u}^{r}
.

Lemma 5.5

(Convergence of local averages)
.

Let
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{D}_{N}
be a sequence converging, in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
, to
u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
. Then for any
r>0
,

(5.29)

\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\left|\overline{\eta}^{N,r}_{t}(x)-\overline{u}^{r}_{t}(x)\right|\to 0.

Proof.

Firstly, the assumption
u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
and the convergence
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\to u_{\bullet}
in
\mathbb{D}
, it follows from definition of the topology on
\mathbb{D}
that

(5.30)

\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\left|\langle\varphi,\eta^{N}_{t}-u_{t}\rangle\right|\to 0

for any
\varphi\in C({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. It follows that the masses
\langle 1,\eta^{N}_{t}\rangle
,
\langle 1,u_{t}\rangle
are bounded uniformly in
N
and
t
. Since
\overline{\eta}^{N,r}
is defined to be constant on the boxes
c^{N}_{x},x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
and
\overline{u}^{r}_{t}
is uniformly continuous in
x
, it is now sufficient to prove (
5.29
) with the supremum restricted to
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. We next observe that, for
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
,

(5.31)

\overline{\eta}^{N,r}(x)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{r,N}}\left\langle\varrho_{r}(\cdot-x),\eta^{N}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\kappa_{r,N}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\varrho_{r}(y-x)\eta^{N}(y)dy.

Let us clarify that the third expression is the definition of the second, but this is not the definition of
\overline{\eta}^{N,r}(z)
for
z\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\setminus\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. Since the functions
\varrho_{r}(\cdot-x)
are continuous, we apply (
5.30
) for each
x
to see that

(5.32)

\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\left|\langle\varrho_{r}(\cdot-x),\eta^{N}_{t}-u_{t}\rangle\right|\to 0.

Next, observe that the family
(\varrho_{r}(\cdot-x))_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}
is compact in the uniform topology by Arzelà-Ascoli, and the aforementioned bound on the mass implies the continuity of
x\mapsto\langle\varrho_{r}(\cdot-x),\eta^{N}_{t}\rangle
, uniformly in
N
and
t
. It therefore follows that the convergence in (
5.32
) is further uniform in
t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
, that is,

(5.33)

\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\left|\langle\varrho_{r}(\cdot-x),\eta^{N}_{t}-u_{t}\rangle\right|\to 0.

Finally, the normalisation
\kappa_{r,N}\to 1
is independent of
x
, and returning to (
5.31
) we conclude that

(5.34)

\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left|\overline{\eta}^{N,r}_{t}(x)-\overline{u}^{r}_{t}(x)\right|\to 0

and the proof is complete. ∎

We may now assemble the various parts to prove Lemma
5.1
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

First of all, we observe that
u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
and the convergence in
\mathbb{D}
imply that
a=\sup_{N}\sup_{t}\langle 1,\eta^{N}_{t}+u_{t}\rangle<\infty
, and the boundedness of
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
in
L^{\beta}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
follows immediately from Proposition
5.3
. It is readily checked that this norm is lower semicontinuous for the topology of
\mathbb{D}
, which also implies that
u_{\bullet}\in L^{\beta}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

Step 1. Strong Convergence in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

We next show how to improve the convergence to norm convergence in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. Fix
\epsilon>0
, and observe that for all
t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}
such that
u_{t}\in L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
,

(5.35)

\left\|\overline{u}^{r}_{t}-u_{t}\right\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\to 0

by standard properties of convolutions. Thanks to the gain of integrability, this holds for almost all
t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}
, and the left-hand side is dominated by
2^{\alpha}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}\in L^{1}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}])
, so by dominated convergence it holds for all
r>0
small enough that

(5.36)

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\left\|\overline{u}^{r}_{t}-u_{t}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}dt<\epsilon^{\alpha}.

We now apply Lemma
5.4
to see that, for all
t
,

(5.37)

\|\overline{\eta}^{N,r}_{t}-\eta^{N}_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}\leq C(1+a)r^{\gamma}(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{t}))

for some
\gamma>0
. Integrating in time, we conclude that

(5.38)

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\left\|\overline{\eta}^{N,r}_{t}-\eta^{N}_{t}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}dt\leq C(1+a)r^{\gamma}\left({t_{\mathrm{fin}}}+\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{s})ds\right)

and the last factor is at most
{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}+\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{\bullet})
, which is bounded in
N
. In particular, we may choose
r>0
small enough that (
5.36
) holds and that the right-hand side of (
5.38
) is at most
\epsilon^{\alpha}
for all
N
. Since
u_{\bullet}
was assumed to be in
\mathcal{C}
, it follows from
5.5
that

(5.39)

\|\overline{\eta}^{N,r}_{\bullet}-\overline{u}^{r}_{\bullet}\|_{L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\to 0.

Combining with (
5.36
,
5.38
), we finally conclude that

(5.40)

\limsup_{N}\left\|\eta^{N}_{\bullet}-u_{\bullet}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq 2\epsilon.

and since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, the step is complete.

Step 2.
\Gamma
-lower bound.

We next prove the
\Gamma
-lower bound (
5.4
). First, because
u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
,
\eta^{N}_{t}\to u_{t}
in the weak-
\star
topology of
L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
for all
t
, and by lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{H}
in this topology,

(5.41)

\sup_{t}\mathcal{H}(u_{t})\leq\liminf_{N}\sup_{t}\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N}_{t}).

For the other term, set
\xi^{N}_{t}(x):=(\eta^{N}_{t}(x))^{\alpha/2}
and
v_{t}(x):=u_{t}(x)^{\alpha/2}
. From step 1,
\xi^{N}\to v
strongly in
L^{1}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, and hence strongly in
L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
for almost all
t\in[0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]
. By the well-known
\Gamma
-convergence
\Gamma-\lim\mathcal{D}_{2,N}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\hskip 2.84544pt\cdot\hskip 2.84544pt\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}
in this topology, it follows that for almost all
t
,

(5.42)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{t})=\frac{2}{\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{2}(v_{t})\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}\hskip 2.84544pt\liminf_{N}\mathcal{D}_{2,N}(\xi^{N}_{t})=\liminf_{N}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{t}).

Integrating in time and using Fatou’s lemma, it follows that

(5.43)

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{t})dt\leq\liminf_{N}\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{t})dt

and combining with (
5.41
), the claim (
5.4
) follows.

Step 3. Construction of
\mathbb{D}
-Open Neighbourhoods.

We now deduce (
5.5
-
5.6
) by contradiction from steps 1-2.

3a. Proof of (
5.5
).

Let us fix
u_{\bullet},\lambda,\mathcal{V}
as in the claim, and suppose for a contradiction that no such
\mathcal{U}
exists. Recalling that the topology of
\mathbb{D}
is induced by a metric
[
42
]
, there exists a decreasing sequence
\mathcal{U}_{k}
of
\mathbb{D}
-open sets whose intersection is the singleton
\{u_{\bullet}\}
. By the contradiction assumption applied to
\mathcal{U}_{k}
, we can choose an increasing sequence
N_{k}\to\infty
and
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\in(\mathcal{U}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{V})\cap\mathbb{D}_{N_{k}}
enjoying the uniform bound
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N_{k}}(\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet})\leq\lambda
. By the choice of
\mathcal{U}_{k}
, we have
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\to u_{\bullet}
in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
, and it follows by step 1 that
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\to u_{\bullet}
in the topology of
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. On the other hand, this is absurd, since
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\not\in\mathcal{V}
for any
k
, and we conclude by contradiction that (
5.5
) holds.

3b. Proof of (
5.6
).

The other case is similar. If we assume for a contradiction that (
5.6
) is false, the same choice of
\mathcal{U}_{k}
produces
N_{k}\to\infty
,
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{D}_{N_{k}}
with
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\to u_{\bullet}
in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
, while
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N_{k}}(\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet})\leq\lambda
. This is the setting of step 2, and we conclude that
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(u_{\bullet})<\infty
, in contradiction to the hypothesis of the claim. ∎

Remark 5.6
.

Exactly the same arguments also establish a continuum version of all the same results. Replacing Lemma
5.4
, for
u\in L^{1}_{\geq 0}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
with
u^{\alpha/2}\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and bounded mean
\langle 1,u\rangle\leq a
,

(5.44)

\|\overline{u}^{r}-u\|_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\alpha}\leq C(1+a)r^{\gamma}(1+\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u))

for the same
\gamma>0
. Overall, if
u^{(n)}_{\bullet}\to u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
relative to the topology of
\mathbb{D}
and
\limsup_{n}\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u^{(n)}_{s})ds<\infty
, then
u^{(n)}_{\bullet},u_{\bullet}
are bounded in
L^{\beta}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, for the same
\beta>\alpha
, that
\|u^{(n)}_{\bullet}-u_{\bullet}\|_{L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\to 0
and that

(5.45)

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u_{s})ds\leq\liminf_{n}\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(u^{(n)}_{s})ds.

6.
Fundamental
a priori
Large Deviations Estimate

In this section we will prove an
a priori
estimate for the particle process, which we will extensively use in the sequel. We will see below that the scaling hypothesis (
1.2
) allows us to find a genuinely probabilistic estimate on the discrete entropy dissipation at the level of realisations
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}(\omega)
, rather than the analytic cotravellers given in terms of
\text{Law}(\eta^{N}_{t})
. Together with Lemma
5.1
, we will then be able to prove Theorem
3
.

The strategy will be to consider the exponential martingale
\mathcal{Z}^{N}_{t}
associated to
\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N}_{t})
according to the general construction (
3.5
). The key computation, which we make precise in Lemma
6.1
below, relates
\mathcal{G}_{N}(\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{H})
, for
\mathcal{G}_{N}
the nonlinear generator (
3.4
), to the discrete entropy dissipation
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}
defined at (
5.2
). This will allow us to find large-deviation estimates on
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{\bullet})
in Lemma
6.2
, which we use to show a time-continuity property and exponential tightness in Lemma
6.3
.

We work under the standing assumption (
1.13
), which immediately implies that

(6.1)

\limsup_{a\to\infty}\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\eta^{N}_{0}\not\in X_{N,a}\right)=-\infty

where
X_{N,a}
is the space of configurations with mass bounded by
a
, defined in (
3.1
).

The key estimate is as follows:

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists an absolute constant
C
such that, for all
N<\infty
and all
\eta^{N}\in X_{N}
,

(6.2)

\mathcal{G}_{N}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{H}\right)(\eta^{N})\leq-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N})+C\left(N^{2}\chi_{N}^{\min(\alpha/2,1)}\right)\left(1+\left\|\eta^{N}\right\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right).

In particular, if (
1.2
) holds, then for any
\vartheta\in(0,\alpha/2)
and
a\geq 1
, we can find
C=C(\vartheta,a,\alpha)
such that, whenever
\eta^{N}\in X_{N,a}
,

(6.3)

\mathcal{G}_{N}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{H}\right)(\eta^{N})\leq-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\vartheta\right)\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N})+C.

Proof.

We start by applying the definition (
3.4
) to find

(6.4)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{G}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{H}\right)(\eta^{N})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\cdot\frac{dN^{2}}{\chi_{N}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p^{N}(x,y)(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}\left(e^{N^{d}\alpha(\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N,x,y})-\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N}))/2\chi_{N}}-1\right)\end{split}

where we understand the summand to be
0
if
\eta^{N}(x)=0
. Recalling the notation
\Psi(u):=u\log u
, the difference appearing in the exponent is

(6.5)

\begin{split}&\frac{N^{d}}{\chi_{N}}\left(\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N,x,y})-\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N})\right)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{\Psi(\eta^{N}(x)-\chi_{N})-\Psi(\eta^{N}(x))+\Psi(\eta^{N}(y)+\chi_{N})-\Psi(\eta^{N}(y))}{\chi_{N}}.\end{split}

Substituting everything back into (
6.4
) and simplifying,

(6.6)

\mathcal{G}_{N}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{H}\right)(\eta^{N})=\frac{d}{N^{d-2}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p^{N}(x,y)g^{N}_{x,y}[\eta^{N}]

where we define
g^{N}_{x,y}[\eta^{N}]
to be the contribution from jumps from
x
to
y
:

(6.7)

g^{N}_{x,y}:=g^{N}_{x,-}g^{N}_{y,+}-(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}

where we factorise the gain term as

(6.8)

g^{N}_{x,-}:=(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\Psi(\eta^{N}(x)-\chi_{N})-\Psi(\eta^{N}(x))}{\chi_{N}}\right)\right)

which we understand to be
0
whenever
\eta^{N}(x)=0
, and

(6.9)

g^{N}_{y,+}:=\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\Psi(\eta^{N}(y)+\chi_{N})-\Psi(\eta^{N}(y))}{\chi_{N}}\right)\right).

The first goal is to prove that, for some absolute constant
C
,

(6.10)

\begin{split}g^{N}_{x,y}\leq&(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}\left((\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2}-(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}\right)\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+C\chi_{N}^{\min(\alpha/2,1)}(1+(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha}).\end{split}

This will be the goal of steps 1-3. Once we have obtained this, we will post-process the inequality into the claim (
6.2
) in step 4.

Step 1. Analysis of
g^{N}_{x,-}
.

Recalling that
\eta^{N}(x)
is always a multiple of
\chi_{N}
, we split the argument into cases. If
\eta^{N}(x)=0
, then (
6.10
) is immediate and there is nothing to prove. In the case
\eta^{N}(x)\geq 2\chi_{N}
, we write

(6.11)

\begin{split}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\Psi(\eta^{N}(x)-\chi_{N})-\Psi(\eta^{N}(x))}{\chi_{N}}\right)\right)&=\frac{(\eta^{N}(x)-\chi_{N})^{\alpha(\eta^{N}(x)-\chi_{N})/2\chi_{N}}}{(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha\eta^{N}(x)/2\chi_{N}}}\\
&=(\eta^{N}(x))^{-\alpha/2}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(x)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\eta^{N}(x)}{\chi_{N}}-1\right)}.\end{split}

Still assuming
\eta^{N}(x)\geq 2\chi_{N}
, we rewrite the second factor as

(6.12)

\begin{split}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(x)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\eta^{N}(x)}{\chi_{N}}-1\right)}&=\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\eta^{N}(x)}{\chi_{N}}-1\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(x)}\right)\right).\end{split}

By Taylor expanding
z\mapsto\ln(1-z),z\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
about
z=0
, we write

(6.13)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\eta^{N}(x)}{\chi_{N}}-1\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(x)}\right)&=-1+\frac{\lambda^{N}_{x,-}\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(x)}\end{split}

for some
\lambda^{N}_{x,-}
enjoying a bound
0\leq\lambda^{N}_{x,-}\leq C
uniform in
N,x,\eta^{N}
. It follows that, for
\eta^{N}(x)\geq 2\chi_{N}
,

(6.14)

\begin{split}g^{N}_{x,-}-e^{-\alpha/2}(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}&=(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}e^{-\alpha/2}\left(\exp\left(\frac{\chi_{N}\lambda^{N}_{x,-}}{\eta^{N}(x)}\right)-1\right)\\
&=:r^{N}_{x,-}e^{-\alpha/2}.\end{split}

The same conclusion (
6.14
) also holds for the case
\eta^{N}(x)=\chi_{N}
, now taking
\lambda^{N}_{x,-}=\frac{\alpha}{2}
. Finally, for suitable constants
C
, we can bound the remainder above in the case
\alpha\in[1,2)
by

(6.15)

r^{N}_{x,-}\leq\chi_{N}^{\alpha/2}C\sup_{t\geq 1}\left(t^{\alpha/2}(e^{t^{-1}}-1)\right)=C\chi_{N}^{\alpha/2}

and if
\alpha\geq 2
then

(6.16)

r^{N}_{x,-}\leq C\chi_{N}(\eta^{N}(x))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}.

Step 2. Analysis of
g^{N}_{y,+}
.

We follow the same argument for
g^{N}_{y,+}
, distinguishing between the cases
\eta^{N}(y)\geq\chi_{N},\eta^{N}(y)=0
. For the case
\eta^{N}(y)\geq\chi_{N}
, the estimate
\ln(1+x)\leq x
produces

(6.17)

\begin{split}g^{N}_{y,+}&=(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\eta^{N}(y)}{\chi_{N}}+1\right)\log\left(1+\frac{\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(y)}\right)\right)\\
&\leq(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2}e^{\alpha/2}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{\chi_{N}}{\eta^{N}(y)}\right)=:e^{\alpha/2}\left((\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2}+r^{N}_{y,+}\right).\end{split}

As in step 1, if

we bound the remainder by

(6.18)

For the excluded case

so that (
6.18
,
6.19
) apply unconditionally, up to increasing the constant

Step 3. Reassembly

We now multiply the bounds found in the previous steps. In either case

&=:(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}\left((\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha/2}-(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha/2}\right)+r^{N}_{x,y}\end{split}

where we consolidate all terms involving at least one of

into a single error

6.10
) now reduces to finding a suitable bound for

. For

&\leq C\chi_{N}^{\alpha/2}\left(1+(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha}\right).\end{split}

In the case
\alpha\geq 2
, we combine (
6.16
,
6.19

(6.23)

\begin{split}r^{N}_{x,y}&\leq C\chi_{N}\left((\eta^{N}(x)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}(\eta^{N}(y))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\left(1+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\right)\eta^{N}(x))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+C^{2}\chi_{N}^{2}\left((\eta^{N}(x)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(1+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\right)\right)\\
&\leq C\chi_{N}\left((1+((\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha}\right)\end{split}

possibly up to a new choice of

Step 4. Summing over

We now substitute (

&\hskip 85.35826pt=:-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}).\end{split}

Meanwhile, the sum in the error term produces

\begin{split}&\frac{dC\chi_{N}^{\min(1,\alpha/2)}}{N^{d-2}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}p^{N}(x,y)(1+(\eta^{N}(x))^{\alpha}+(\eta^{N}(y))^{\alpha})\\

Step 5. Interpolation.

We now post-process (

Lemma 6.3

Under the assumptions of the previous lemma:

The processes

\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\not\in\mathcal{K}\right)\leq-z.

For any

.

Let us define the sets

(6.42)

By the Dunford-Pettis Theorem

We will now prove, for every

, that

(6.44)

\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(q^{N}_{\varphi}(\delta)>\epsilon\right)=-\infty

where

Point i) will then follow from general considerations, see Feng and Kurtz
[

. Using Lemma
3.1
, there exists a finite constant

such that, for all

Let us fix

\epsilon>0

\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})

for

Using the hypothesis (

) to control the first term, we may thus transfer Lemma
6.2
to
\mathbb{Q}
to find

(7.8)

\limsup_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{Q}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{\bullet})>M\right)=0.

Recalling that
\eta_{\bullet}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-almost sure limit in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
on a subsequence
N_{k}\to\infty
, we now argue that
\mathbb{Q}(\eta_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}})=1
. To see this, it follows from (
7.8
) that there exists an increasing function
\vartheta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, with
\vartheta(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, such that

\sup_{N}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\vartheta\left(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{\bullet})\right)\right]<\infty.

Extending
\vartheta
by
\vartheta(\infty):=\infty
, it follows from monotonicity and (
5.4
) in Lemma
5.1
that,
\mathbb{Q}
-almost surely,

\vartheta\left(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(\eta_{\bullet})\right)\leq\liminf_{k}\vartheta\left(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N_{k}}(\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet})\right)

and the right-hand side has finite expectation by Fatou. It follows that the left-hand side is almost surely finite, whence
\mathbb{Q}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}(\eta_{\bullet})<\infty)=1
and the claim follows.

We now prove convergence in probability in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
on the subsequence
N_{k}
. Fix
\epsilon,\delta>0
, and choose
M,a
such that

(7.9)

\limsup_{N}\mathbb{Q}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,N}(\eta^{N}_{\bullet})>M\right)<\delta;\qquad\limsup_{N}\mathbb{Q}\left(\eta^{N}_{0}\not\in X_{N,a}\right)<\delta.

Thanks to Lemma
5.4
, we may now choose
r\in(0,1)
small enough that, for the constant
C
in (
5.18
),
C(1+a)r^{\gamma}({t_{\mathrm{fin}}}+M)<\epsilon^{\alpha}
, so that

(7.10)

\limsup_{N}\mathbb{Q}\left(\left\|\eta^{N}_{\bullet}-\overline{\eta}^{N,r}_{\bullet}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}>\epsilon\right)<2\delta.

By Remark
5.6
,
\overline{\eta}^{r}\to\eta
in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
,
\mathbb{Q}
-almost surely, so by making
r>0
smaller if necessary, we can further arrange that

(7.11)

\mathbb{Q}(\|\overline{\eta}^{r}_{\bullet}-\eta_{\bullet}\|_{L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}>\epsilon)<\delta.

By Lemma
5.5
, it holds
\mathbb{Q}
-almost surely that
\overline{\eta}^{N_{k},r}_{\bullet}\to\overline{\eta}^{r}_{\bullet}
in
L^{\infty}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, and hence also in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. Combining everything, it follows that

\limsup_{N_{k}}\mathbb{Q}\left(\left\|\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}-\eta_{\bullet}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}>3\epsilon\right)<4\delta

and we conclude the convergence
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}\to\eta_{\bullet}
in
\mathbb{Q}
-probability in the topology of
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. The conclusion of
\mathbb{Q}
-almost sure convergence on a subsequence is elementary.∎

8.
Hydrodynamic Limit

We now prove Theorem
1
, based on the general tools we have already developed. We fix
u_{0}\in L^{1}_{\geq 0}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on the underlying probability space as in the statement of the theorem, and let
u_{\bullet}
be the unique solution to the PME (
1.1
) starting at
u_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be as given, and for all
M,a
, let us define new measures
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}
by conditioning on the event

(8.1)

A^{N}_{M,a}:=\left\{\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N}_{0})\leq M,\langle 1,\eta^{N}_{0}\rangle\leq a\right\}.

We will now prove that, under these conditioned measures,
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\to u_{\bullet}
in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathbb{P}
-probability, using the well-known technique of proving compactness and identifying all possible limit points. Finally, we will remove the conditioning, to recover statements in terms of
\mathbb{P}
. For now, let us fix
M,a
.

Step 1. Tightness.

For each
M,a
, the measures
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}
trivially satisfy the hypothesis (
1.13
), and so Lemma
6.3
shows that
{\rm Law}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}}[\eta^{N}_{\bullet}]
are exponentially tight, and hence tight, relative to the topology of
\mathbb{D}
.

Step 2. Identification of Limits.

Let us now prove that, if a subsequence
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet}
converges in distribution under
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}
to
\eta_{\bullet}
relative to the topology of
\mathbb{D}
, then
\eta_{\bullet}
takes values in the set
\mathcal{S}(u_{0})
of solutions to the PME (
1.1
) starting at
u_{0}
. Since we know from Proposition
3.4
that
\mathcal{S}(u_{0})=\{u_{\bullet}\}
is a singleton, we will then conclude that

{\rm Law}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}}[\eta^{N}_{\bullet}]\to\delta_{u_{\bullet}}

weakly as measures on
\mathbb{D}
.

Thanks to Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we may realise all
\eta^{N_{k}}_{\bullet},\eta_{\bullet}
on a common probability space
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
such that
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\to\eta_{\bullet}
in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost surely. By Theorem
3
ii),
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}(\eta_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}})=1
and, passing to a further subsequence,
\eta^{N^{\prime}_{k}}_{\bullet}\to\eta_{\bullet}
in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
,
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost surely; to ease notation, we will continue to denote the subsequence by
\eta^{N}_{\bullet}
. For each
N
in the subsequence, we write

(8.2)

\langle\varphi,\eta^{N}_{t}\rangle=\langle\varphi,\eta^{N}_{0}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{N}F^{\varphi}_{N}(\eta^{N}_{s})ds+M^{{N},\varphi}_{t}

where
M^{{N},\varphi}
is a
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-martingale, starting at zero. We now deal with the terms one by one; the left-hand side, and the first term of the right-hand side, converge to
\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle,\langle\varphi,\eta_{0}\rangle
respectively, uniformly in
t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}
, thanks to convergence in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
and the fact that
\eta_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
almost surely. The jumps of
M^{{N},\varphi}
are of size at most
\lesssim\frac{\chi_{N}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}}{{N}^{d+1}}
and occur at rate at most
\lesssim\frac{{N}^{2}}{\chi_{N}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}(\eta^{N}_{t}(x))^{\alpha}
, which gives the elementary bound on the quadratic variation

(8.3)

\left[M^{N,\varphi}\right]_{t}\leq C\frac{\chi_{N}N^{2}}{N^{2d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}(\eta^{N}_{s}(x))^{\alpha}ds=C\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\eta^{N}_{s}\right\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}ds

for some
C=C(\varphi)
. Thanks to the second part of Lemma
6.2
, it follows that, for all
\delta>0
,

(8.4)

\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}\left(\left[M^{N,\varphi}\right]_{{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}>\delta\right)\to 0

and hence
M^{N,\varphi}_{\bullet}
converges in
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-probability to
0
. Concerning the integral term, we use Lemma
3.1
and the second part of Lemma
6.2
again to find the
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost sure convergence

(8.5)

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\left|\mathcal{L}_{N}F_{N}(\eta^{N}_{s})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\Delta\varphi(x)(\eta^{N}_{s}(x))^{\alpha}dx\right|ds\to 0.

Meanwhile, by the almost sure convergence in
L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
obtained from Theorem
3
ii), we have
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost surely,

(8.6)

\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\Delta\varphi(x)(\eta^{N}_{s}(x))^{\alpha}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\Delta\varphi(x)(\eta_{s}(x))^{\alpha}dx\right|ds\to 0.

Combining the previous two displays and returning to (
8.2
), we conclude that,
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost surely, for all
t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}
,

(8.7)

\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle=\langle\varphi,\eta_{0}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\Delta\varphi)(x)(\eta_{s}(x))^{\alpha}dxds.

By taking an intersection over a countable dense set
\varphi_{n}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, we may extend this to holding,
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost surely, for all
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
simultaneously. We have now checked that
\eta_{\bullet}
satisfies Definition
3.2
with
g=0
, and hence conclude that,
\mathbb{Q}_{M,a}
-almost surely,
\eta_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{S}(u_{0})
, which completes the step.

Step 3. Removal of Conditioning

From steps 1-2 it follows that, for any
\mathcal{U}\ni u_{\bullet}
open in the topology of
\mathbb{D}
and
M,a
sufficiently large,

(8.8)

\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}\left(\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\not\in\mathcal{U}\right)\to 0.

To finish the theorem we now need to replace
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}
with
\mathbb{P}
in the above statement. Let us fix
\epsilon>0
. Thanks to the hypotheses (
1.3
) and the observation that this implies (
6.1
), we can find
M,a
sufficiently large that the conditioning in (
8.1
) satisfies

(8.9)

\limsup_{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\left(A^{N}_{M,a}\right)^{\mathrm{c}}\right)=\limsup_{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{H}(\eta^{N}_{0})>M\text{ or }\langle 1,\eta^{N}_{0}\rangle>a\right)<\epsilon.

It now follows that

(8.10)

\begin{split}\limsup_{N}\mathbb{P}\left(\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\not\in\mathcal{U}\right)&\leq\limsup_{N}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\left(A^{N}_{M,a}\right)^{\mathrm{c}}\right)+\mathbb{P}^{N}_{M,a}\left(\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\not\in\mathcal{U}\right)\right]<\epsilon\end{split}

and, since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, the proof is complete. ∎

9.

Equipped with the variational formulation Lemma

Lemma 9.1
.

Assume (

), fix

\mathbb{P}

2

(9.1)

\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\eta^{N}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{U}\right)\leq-z.

Proof.

Fix
u_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}}
and
z<\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(u_{\bullet})

so that
z+6\epsilon<\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(u_{\bullet})

3.5

\psi\in C({\mathbb{T}^{d}}),\varphi\in C^{1,2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})

(9.2)

\Xi_{0}(\psi,u_{0},\rho)+\Xi_{1}(\varphi,u_{\bullet})>z+6\epsilon.

With this

(9.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Z}^{N}_{t}:&=\exp\bigg(\frac{N^{d}}{\chi_{N}}\bigg(\langle\psi,\eta^{N}_{0}\rangle-\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\rho(x)(e^{\psi(x)/\alpha}-1)dx+\langle\varphi_{t},\eta^{N}_{t}\rangle-\langle\varphi_{0},\eta^{N}_{0}\rangle\\
&\hskip 128.0374pt-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\varphi_{s},\eta^{N}_{s}\rangle ds-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{N}F_{N}^{\varphi_{s}}(\eta^{N}_{s})ds\bigg)\bigg)\end{split}

where, for each

is the evaluation
\eta^{N}\mapsto\langle\varphi_{s},\eta^{N}\rangle

3.1

(9.4)

\left|\mathcal{G}_{N}F_{N}^{\varphi_{t}}(\eta^{N}_{t})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\Delta\varphi_{t}(x)+|\nabla\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2})(\eta^{N}_{t}(x))^{\alpha}dx\right|\leq\theta_{N}\left\|\eta^{N}_{t}\right\|_{L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{\alpha}

for some deterministic

; applying Lemma

, it follows that the events

(9.5)

A^{1}_{N,\epsilon}=\left\{\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\left|\mathcal{G}_{N}F_{N}^{\varphi_{t}}(\eta^{N}_{t})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\Delta\varphi_{t}(x)+|\nabla\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2})({\eta}^{N}_{t}(x))^{\alpha}dx\right|dt>\epsilon\right\}

(9.6)

\limsup_{N}\frac{\chi_{N}}{N^{d}}\log\mathbb{P}(A^{1}_{N,\epsilon})\leq-z.

Next, noting that the map
\mathbb{D}\to(L^{1}_{\geq 0}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}),d),v_{\bullet}\mapsto v_{0}

\mathbb{D}
-open neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}_{1}
of

contained in

(9.7)

\begin{split}&\left\{v_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{D}:\hskip 2.84544pt\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}|\langle\varphi,u_{t}-v_{t}\rangle|<\epsilon,\hskip 2.84544pt\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{s}\varphi_{s},u_{s}-v_{s}\rangle ds\right|<\epsilon\right\}\\
&\hskip 199.16928pt\cap\left\{v_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{D}:\hskip 2.84544pt|\langle\psi,u_{0}-v_{0}\rangle|<\epsilon\right\}\end{split}

3

(9.9)

\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}_{1}\cap\mathcal{U}_{2}
, and consider the event

(9.10)

Proof of Lemma

Step 1. Construction of a change of measure.

For the initial data, let us pick

and set

\mathbb{E}\left[\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}[\eta^{N}_{0}=\eta^{N}]Y^{N}_{0}\right]={\Pi}^{N}_{u_{0},M}(\eta^{N})

to be the functional given by, for

N

, and observing that

(10.10)

\sup_{N}\left\|\hbox{\rm 1}{\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{\rm I}_{\{Y^{N}_{0}\neq 0\}}\sup_{t\leq{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}|\widehat{F}_{N}(\eta^{N}_{t},J^{N}_{t})|\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\leq C.

Meanwhile, a calculation analagous to Lemma

11.
From Large Deviations to Gradient Flow

We now prove Theorem
4
as a consequence of the large deviations principle and the microscopic reversibility. This proof was inspired by a similar proof of the Boltzmann
H
-Theorem in kinetic theory by the second author
[
40
, Section 6.5]
. We recall the notation
L^{2}_{t,x}:=L^{2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}
from the introduction.

Proof.

We divide into steps. Firstly, the equality is trivial if
u_{\bullet}\not\in{\mathcal{R}}
, since both sides are infinite. On the left-hand side, the property that
\mathcal{J}=\infty
outside
{\mathcal{R}}
follows from the definition of
\mathcal{J}
as already remarked in the discussion under (
3.7
-
3.8
). On the right-hand side, if
\mathcal{A}(u_{\bullet})<\infty
, then
u_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
, so the finiteness of the right-hand side implies that
u_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}}
according to the definition (
3.7
). Next, assuming that
u_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}}
, we observe that
\mathcal{A}(u_{\bullet})<\infty
if and only if
\mathcal{J}(u_{\bullet})<\infty
. Indeed, the assumption
u_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}}
implies
\nabla u^{\alpha/2}\in L^{2}_{t,x}
, and it is straightforward to verify that the map

\theta\mapsto g=\nabla u^{\alpha/2}+\frac{\theta}{2}

is a bijection of
L^{2}_{t,x}
onto itself, and from the set of competitors
\theta
for
\mathcal{A}
onto the set of competitors
g
for
\mathcal{J}
. In particular, the existence of a competitor in
L^{2}_{t,x}
for either problem implies the same for the other problem, and hence the finiteness of the corresponding infimum. We may therefore restrict attention to the case

Step 1. Definition of Time-Reversal

Let us define
\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
by the time reversal

(11.1)

(\mathcal{T}\eta)_{t}=\eta_{({t_{\mathrm{fin}}}-t)-}.

The detailed balance condition for

implies that

and hence that

satisfy the same large deviation principle as

. On the other hand,

is a continuous involution, and so the contraction principle (see Theorem 4.2.1 in the book of Dembo and Zeioutini

) implies that

have a large deviation principle with rate function

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{T}}_{\rho}(u_{\bullet})&=\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(v_{\bullet}):\mathcal{T}v_{\bullet}=u_{\bullet}\right\}=\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(\mathcal{T}u_{\bullet}).\end{split}

Since rate functions are unique
[
34
, Remark 3.1]
, we conclude that, for all

,

\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(u_{\bullet})=\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(\mathcal{T}u_{\bullet}).

Let us stress that this is the key equality on which the proof rests; although the objects involved in the statement of the theorem do not involve probability, this equality has a natural probabilistic meaning, and indeed we are not aware of any proof of it which does not involve the large deviations.

To ease notation, it will be helpful to also have a time reversal operation
\mathcal{T}
on

, let us define

is readily seen to be an isometry of

.

where in both of the final lines we have recalled the fact that
p^{N}
is the nearest neighbours transition matrix to turn the sum over
y
with respect to
dp^{N}(x,\cdot)
into a sum over tangent vectors. We observe that

(B.4)

\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x+l)-\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x)=N^{d}\int_{c^{N}_{0}}(\varphi(x+l+z)-\varphi(x+z))dz

and recalling that
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, we Taylor expand to second order. We write
\theta_{N}
for errors, potentially with additional arguments
x,(x,l),(x,l,z)
, which are
\mathfrak{o}(1)
(uniformly in all arguments) and which may change from line to line. We find

(B.5)

\begin{split}\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x+l)-\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x)&=N^{d}\int_{c^{N}_{0}}\left(\nabla\varphi(x+z)\cdot l+\frac{1}{2}l\cdot\nabla^{2}\varphi(x+z)l+\theta_{N}(x,l,z)N^{-2}\right)dz\\[4.30554pt]
&=\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot l+\frac{1}{2}l\cdot\nabla^{2}\varphi(x)l+\theta_{N}(x,l)N^{-2}\end{split}

whence

(B.6)

\begin{split}e^{\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x+l)-\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x)}-1&=\varphi(x+l)-\varphi(x)+\frac{1}{2}(\varphi(x+l)-\varphi(x))^{2}+\theta_{N}(x,l)N^{-2}\\[4.30554pt]
&=\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot l+\frac{1}{2}l\cdot\nabla^{2}\varphi(x)l+\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot l\right)^{2}+\theta_{N}(x,l)N^{-2}.\end{split}

For fixed
x
, we sum over
l\in\{\pm e_{i}/N,1\leq i\leq d\}
. By symmetry,
\sum_{l}l=0
and the first-order terms vanish, and the remaining terms produce

(B.7)

\begin{split}\sum_{l\in\{\pm e_{i}/N\}}(\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x+l)-\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x))=N^{-2}\left(\Delta\varphi(x)+\theta_{N}(x)\right).\end{split}

and

(B.8)

\begin{split}\sum_{l\in\{\pm e_{i}/N\}}(e^{\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x+l)-\overline{\varphi}^{N}(x)}-1)=N^{-2}\left(\Delta\varphi(x)+|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}+\theta_{N}(x)\right).\end{split}

Substituting into (
B.2
) and (
B.3
) respectively, we find the claimed result, with
\Delta\varphi,\nabla\varphi
replaced by versions which are piecewise constant on each box
c^{N}_{x}
. Using continuity to control the difference, both claims follow.
∎

We divide the proof into steps for clarity.

Step 1. Lower Bound of (

We will first show that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\inf\left\{\left\|g\right\|_{{L^{2}_{t,x}}}^{2}:u_{\bullet}\text{ solves (\ref{eq: Sk})}\right\}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\geq\sup_{\varphi\in C^{1,2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\Xi_{1}(\varphi,u_{\bullet}).\end{split}

If the set of

solves (

\begin{split}\langle\varphi_{{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}},u_{{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\rangle=\langle\varphi_{0},u_{0}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\langle\partial_{t}\varphi_{t},u_{t}\rangle&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\Delta\varphi_{t}(x)u_{t}(x)^{\alpha}\\

so that

\begin{split}\Xi_{1}(\varphi,u_{\bullet})=&\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\left(g_{t}(x)u^{\alpha/2}_{t}(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi_{t}(x)-\frac{1}{2}u_{t}(x)^{\alpha}|\nabla\varphi_{t}(x)|^{2}\right)dtdx\\

Step 2. Upper Bound of (

To complement the previous step, we now fix

. If the right-hand side of (

) is infinite then both expressions in (

\frac{1}{2}\left\|g\right\|_{{L^{2}_{t,x}}}^{2}\leq\sup_{\varphi\in C^{1,2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\Xi_{1}(\varphi,u)

) is an equality. We define a nonnegative-semidefinite bilinear form
\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle_{u_{\bullet}}
on

by

Let us briefly emphasise that this is well-defined, since we assumed
u_{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{R}}
and
{\mathcal{R}}\subset L^{\alpha}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
by (
3.8

as the completion of

\varphi\in C^{1,2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, we can write

as

(B.24)

\Xi_{1}(\varphi,u_{\bullet})=L_{u_{\bullet}}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\langle\varphi,\varphi\right\rangle\right\rangle_{u_{\bullet}}

with

\begin{split}L_{u_{\bullet}}\varphi:=\langle\varphi_{{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}},u_{{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}}\rangle&-\langle\varphi_{0},u_{0}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\langle\partial_{t}\varphi_{t},u_{t}\rangle\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\Delta\varphi_{t}(x)u_{t}(x)^{\alpha}dtdx.\end{split}

Since we assumed the finiteness of the supremum, it follows that

(B.26)

\begin{split}\sup_{\varphi\in C^{1,2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\Xi_{1}(\varphi,u_{\bullet})&=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{1,2}([0,{t_{\mathrm{fin}}}]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\left\{L_{u_{\bullet}}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\langle\varphi,\varphi\right\rangle\right\rangle_{u_{\bullet}}\right\}\\

As already remarked,

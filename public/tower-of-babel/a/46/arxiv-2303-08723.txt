Ms.FPOP : An Exact and Fast Segmentation Algorithm With a Multiscale Penalty Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Liehrmann Arnaud, Rigaill Guillem

Abstract
Given a time series in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with a piecewise constant mean and independent noises, we propose an exact dynamic programming algorithm to minimize a least square criterion with a multiscale penalty promoting well-spread changepoints. Such a penalty has been proposed in Verzelen et al. (2020), and it achieves optimal rates for changepoint detection and changepoint localization. Our proposed algorithm, named Ms.FPOP , extends functional pruning ideas of Rigaill (2015) and Maidstone et al. (2017) to multiscale penalties. For large signals, n ≥ 10 5 n\geq 10^{5} , with relatively few real changepoints, Ms.FPOP is typically quasi-linear and an order of magnitude faster than PELT . We propose an efficient C++ implementation interfaced with R of Ms.FPOP allowing to segment a profile of up to n = 10 6 n=10^{6} in a matter of seconds. Finally, we illustrate on simple simulations that for large enough profiles ( n ≥ 10 4 n\geq 10^{4} ) Ms.FPOP using the multiscale penalty of Verzelen et al. (2020) is typically more powerfull than FPOP using the classical BIC penalty of Yao 1989.

2.1
Functional Pruning Optimal Partioning (
FPOP
)

To better explain

we first review some of the key elements of

3

FPOP
introduces for every change

its best cost as function of the last parameter
\mu

t

\widetilde{f}_{t,s}(\mu)
.
Formally this is:

\widetilde{f}_{t,s}(\mu)=F_{s}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=s+1}^{t}(y_{i}-\mu)^{2}+\alpha,\text{ }\text{ }\text{ with }\text{ }\text{ }\widetilde{f}_{t,t}(\mu)=F_{t}+\alpha\text{ }\text{ }\text{ and }\text{ }\text{ }F_{0}=-\alpha.

(5)

\widetilde{f}_{t,s}(\mu)
is a second degree polynomial in

defined by three coefficients :

with

and

a_{0}=F_{s}+\alpha+\displaystyle\sum_{i=s+1}^{t}y_{i}^{2}

The update of these coefficients is straightforward using the following formula:

\widetilde{f}_{t,s}(\mu)=\widetilde{f}_{t-1,s}(\mu)+(y_{t}-\mu)^{2}.

(6)

At each time step

,

updates the minimum of all

The key idea behind

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\{s\leq t|Z_{t,s}^{*}\neq\emptyset\}
where the “living-set” of change

. Given those definitions we have
\widetilde{F}_{t}(\mu)=\min_{s\in\mathcal{F}_{t}}\ \left\{\widetilde{f}_{t,s}(\mu)\right\}
. In other words,

is pruned as soon as its “living-set” is empty, which is justified because

Z_{t,s}^{*}\supset Z_{t+1,s}^{*}.\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{and }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }Z_{t,s}^{*}=\emptyset\implies Z_{t+1,s}^{*}=\emptyset\ .

(7)

Note that we can then retrieve

by minimizing
\widetilde{F}_{t}(\mu)
on
\mu
.

5
Discussion

Extending Functional Pruning Techniques to the Multiscale Penalty

In section
2.2
we have explained how to extend functional pruning techniques to the case of multiscale penalty. In Figures
1
and
3
we have seen that for large signals (
n\geq 10^{5}
) with few changepoints,
Ms.FPOP
is an order of magnitude faster than
Ms.PELT
(which relies on inequality based pruning, see
A
). Even when the number of changepoints increased linearly with the size of the data,
Ms.FPOP
was still faster than
Ms.PELT
.

The main update rule
12
of our dynamic programming algorithm suggests to compare each candidate change
s
with a set of future candidate changes
s^{\prime}
. As we have seen in
D
, the strategy of randomly drawing one
s^{\prime}
according to a uniform distribution is the best strategy and allows us to tackle large signals. It is likely that uniform sampling is not optimal. The algorithm alternates between good draws (leading to a strong reduction of
Z_{t,s}
or even the pruning of
s
) and bad draws (leading to a weak reduction
Z_{t,s}
). On average this is sufficient but improvements are possible. In particular the study of
h(t,s,s^{\prime})=\log(\frac{t-s^{\prime}}{t-s})
(see Assumption A1), suggests disfavoring
s^{\prime}
that are too recent or that have been compared recently.

Calibration of
\gamma
and
\beta
from the Multiscale Penalty

The least-squares estimator with multiscale penalty proposed by
[
17
]
involves two constants
\gamma
and
\beta
that still need to be investigated. Using signals simulated under the null hypothesis (no changepoint) we have seen that it is possible to find a pair of constants
\gamma=9
and
\beta=2.25
for which
Ms.FPOP
controls
R_{>0}
. Under this setting we have shown on
hat
(see section
4.3.1
) and
step
(see Figure
10
) simulations that
Ms.FPOP
is more powerful than
FPOP
on segmentations with well-spread changepoints. This difference of power grows with
n
. For segmentation with small segments
FPOP
is more powerful
Ms.FPOP
when
n
is small (
\approx 10^{3}
), but for larger
n
(
\geq 10^{4}
) this difference disappears.

We also tested
Ms.FPOP
on the benchmark proposed in
[
24
]
. The performances of
Ms.FPOP
are not so good on the original benchmark containing mostly small profiles with small segments but much better for an extended benchmark with larger profiles (see section
4.3.2
).

Without additional work on the calibration of the constants, we would thus recommend using
Ms.FPOP
for large profiles (
\geq 10^{4}
).

Unknown Variance

All our simulations have been done on signals with known variance,
\sigma^{2}
. However, in real-world situations, this may not always be the case. One approach is to estimate
\sigma^{2}
and then plugging-in it in the problem,
i.e
scaling the signal or the penalty by
\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}
or
\sigma^{2}
, respectively. A robust estimate of
\sigma^{2}
can be obtained by calculating the variance of
\Delta_{Y}=Y_{i+1}-Y_{i}
using either the median absolute deviation or the estimator suggested in
[
26
]
. As an alternative,
[
17
]
pointed out that one could calibrate the multiplicative constant
L
of the penalized least-squares estimator using the slope heuristic
[
27
]
. Investigating the performances of these various approaches is outside the scope of this paper.

Appendix A
PELT
for Multiscale Penalized Likelihood

Following the notation of the PELT paper
[
20
]
the cost of a segment from
s+1
to
s^{\prime}
,
s+1:s^{\prime}
is defined as
\mathcal{C}_{s+1:s^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=s+1}^{s^{\prime}}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s+1:s^{\prime}})^{2}-\beta g(s^{\prime}-s).

In what follow we consider three time points
s<s^{\prime}<t
. Let
\ell=s^{\prime}-s
denote the length of the sequence of observations between time
s
and
s^{\prime}
and
\ell^{\prime}=t-s^{\prime}
denote the length of the sequence of observations between time
s^{\prime}
and
t
.

The key condition to apply the PELT algorithm
[
20
]
is that up to a constant
K
adding a changepoints always reduce the cost, that is :

Assumption 2
.

\mathcal{C}_{s+1:s^{\prime}}+\mathcal{C}_{s^{\prime}+1:t}+K\leq\mathcal{C}_{s+1:t}

(18)

The following lemma ensure that such
K
exists for any
n
and provide explicit values for
K
in general and if
g
is concave.

Lemma 2
.

(a) For any function
g
from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
,
\beta\geq 0
, and any
n
, Assumption
2
is true at least for
K=2\beta\min_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\{g(\ell)\}-\beta\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\{g(\ell)\}
.
(b) If
g
is concave the condition is true for
K=-\beta g(2)+2\beta g(1).

Proof.

We first note that

m_{n}=\underset{1\leq\ell<n}{\min}\left\{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell^{\prime}<n\\
\ell+\ell^{\prime}\leq n\end{subarray}}{\min}\left\{g(\ell)+g(\ell^{\prime})-g(\ell+\ell^{\prime})\right\}\right\}

is well defined as the minimum of a finite set.
By definition of
m_{n}
we thus have, for any
1\leq s<s^{\prime}<t\leq n
and for any
K<\beta m_{n}
, that

\displaystyle-\beta g(s^{\prime}-s)-\beta g(t-s^{\prime})+K

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\beta g(t-s)

Combining this with

\displaystyle\sum_{i=s+1}^{s^{\prime}}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s+1:s^{\prime}})^{2}+\sum_{i=s^{\prime}+1}^{t}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s^{\prime}+1:t})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=s+1}^{t}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s+1:t})^{2}.

we recover that equation (
2
) is true for any
K<\beta m_{n}.

Now for any
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}
in
\{1,\ldots,n\}^{2}
such that
\ell+\ell^{\prime}\leq n
we have

2\min_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\{g(\ell)\}-\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\{g(\ell)\}\leq g(\ell)+g(\ell^{\prime})-g(\ell+\ell^{\prime}).

Hence we get

2\min_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\{g(\ell)\}-\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\{g(\ell)\}\leq m_{n},

and we recover (a).

In case
g
is concave using the technical lemma
3
two times we get :

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell^{\prime}<n\\
\ell+\ell^{\prime}\leq n\end{subarray}}{\min}\left\{g(\ell)+g(\ell^{\prime})-g(\ell+\ell^{\prime})\right\}=g(\ell)+g(1)-g(\ell+1)

(19)

and

\underset{1\leq\ell<n}{\min}\left\{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell^{\prime}<n\\
\ell+\ell^{\prime}\leq n\end{subarray}}{\min}\left\{g(\ell)+g(\ell^{\prime})-g(\ell+\ell^{\prime})\right\}\right\}=2g(1)-g(2)

For example, if
g=\log
we get
K=-\beta\log(2)

∎

Lemma 3
.

If
g
is concave then for any
\delta>0
, the function
h:x\rightarrow g(x+\delta)-g(x)
is non increasing.

Proof.

Consider any
\delta^{\prime}>0
.
We have

x+\delta=(1-\alpha)x+\alpha(x+\delta+\delta^{\prime})
for
\alpha=\delta/(\delta+\delta^{\prime})
and similarly

x+\delta^{\prime}=(1-\alpha^{\prime})x+\alpha^{\prime}(x+\delta+\delta^{\prime})
with
\alpha^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}/(\delta+\delta^{\prime}).

Using concavity we have

\displaystyle g(x+\delta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)g(x)+\alpha g(x+\delta+\delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle g(x+\delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha^{\prime})g(x)+\alpha^{\prime}g(x+\delta+\delta^{\prime}).

Suming these two lines and noting that
\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}=1
we get

g(x+\delta)-g(x)\geq g(x+\delta^{\prime}+\delta)-g(x+\delta^{\prime})

∎

Appendix B
Adaptative
PELT
for Concave Multiscale Penalty

In the following lemma we show that for our multiscale penalty assuming the function
g
is concave the constant
K
in theorme 3.1 of
[
20
]
can be chosen adaptively to the length of the last segment.

Lemma 4
.

If
g
is concave and
\beta\geq 0.

then if at time
s^{\prime}
we have,

F_{s}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{s^{\prime}}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s+1:s^{\prime}})^{2}-\beta g(\ell)+K_{s^{\prime}-s=\ell}\geq F_{s^{\prime}},

with
K_{\ell}=\beta(g(\ell)+g(1)-g(\ell+1))

then for any time
t
larger than
s^{\prime}
we have :

F_{s}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{t}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s+1:t})^{2}-\beta g(\ell+\ell^{\prime})\geq F_{s^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=s^{\prime}+1}^{t}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{s^{\prime}+1:t})^{2}-\beta g(\ell^{\prime}),

and thus
for any time
t\geq s^{\prime}
, a change at
s
can never be optimal. Taking
g=\log
we get
K_{\ell}=-\beta\log(\frac{1}{\ell}+1)\leq-\beta\log(2)
.

Proof.

We follows the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
[
20
]
using the fact that if
g
is concave then equation
19
is true.
∎

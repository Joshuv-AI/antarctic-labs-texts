Abstract
. We prove that there is an action of the cyclic group 𝐂 2 \mathbf{C}_{2} on the 10 10 -dimensional Floyd manifold which turns it into a conjugation manifold. The submanifold of fixed points is the 5 5 -dimensional Floyd manifold, whose cohomology is isomorphic to that of the large one, scaled down by dividing the cohomological degree by a factor two.

1.
Introduction

In
[
1
]
Floyd showed, among many other things, that there exist only two non-trivial cobordism classes that contain manifolds
with three cells, and that they lie in dimensions
10
and
5
. He also produced representatives of these classes,
which we name here
F_{10}
and
F_{5}
.
His computations show that the mod
2
cohomology of the larger one has generators
1,u_{4},u_{6},u_{10}
in degrees

0
,
4
,
6
, and
10
respectively, whereas the smaller one looks like a divided by
2
version with generators
1,v_{2},v_{3},v_{5}

in degrees
0
,
2
,
3
, and
5
respectively. Moreover, the product of the two
middle generators give the top one, so that the abstract “halving isomorphism” between
\mathrm{H}^{*}(F_{10};\mathbb{F}_{2})
and

\mathrm{H}^{*}(F_{5};\mathbb{F}_{2})
is actually compatible with the cup product. More is true since Floyd also identified the action of the Steenrod
algebra and computed that
Sq^{2}u_{4}=u_{6}
and
Sq^{4}u_{6}=u_{10}
, an analogous result being true for
F_{5}
.
Hence the halving isomorphism is also compatible with the action of the Steenrod algebra.

From the point of view of the theory of conjugation spaces introduced by Hausmann, Holm, and Puppe in
[
3
]
, and
further developments on the action of the Steenrod algebra by Franz and Puppe,
[
2
]
, this remarkable behavior looks
like an algebraic shadow of the fact that the larger manifold
F_{10}
could be a conjugation manifold.

We give here an affirmative answer to this conjecture, after several attempts.
This problem appears as an open question in our previous article on the subject,
[
7
]
, where
this was only proved for the suspension spectrum in the stable homotopy category.
The main problem is to endow
F_{10}
with an action by the group of two elements
\mathbf{C}_{2}
in such a way that it becomes a conjugation space
with fixed points
F_{5}
.

Theorem
4.7
.

The Floyd manifold
F_{10}
is a smooth conjugation manifold with fixed points
F_{5}
.

The strategy is to analyze carefully Floyd’s surgery construction and perform it equivariantly. For this first step we use Lück and Uribe’s
theory of equivariant principal bundles,
[
4
]
. This yields an action of
\mathbf{C}_{2}
on
F_{10}
. The second and final step consists
then in verifying the “cohomological purity” in the genuine equivariant stable sense as explained in our joint work with Ricka,
[
6
]
. A
key ingredient here is Clover May’s splitting result,
[
5
]
.

Construction of Floyd’s manifolds

We will go through a very careful analysis of Floyd’s construction
[

Let

If

\tau(x\theta)=\tau(x)\overline{\theta}\ \ \hbox{\rm for all}\ x\in S^{3}

3.
The surgery step

We will use the framework developed by Lück and Uribe in
[
4
]
to prove that the normal bundle to
S^{\rho}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{F_{10}}
is equivariantly trivial. We will work with the associated frame bundle, as it is a principal
GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})
-bundle
whose triviality is equivalent to that of the normal bundle.

Because we have split the fibration
\widetilde{F}_{10}\rightarrow S^{\rho}
by choosing an
S^{1}
-fixed point in
\mathbb{H}P^{2}
,
the normal bundle to the splitting
S^{\rho}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{F}_{10}
is given by the vertical tangent bundle in the fibration,
that is the tangent bundle to the fiber
\mathbb{H}P^{2}
. So, if
T\widetilde{F_{10}}
is the tangent bundle,
our model for the normal bundle is
T_{V}\widetilde{F_{10}}
, the rank
8
vertical bundle, which fiberwise is the kernel of
the projection to
S^{\rho}
. The group
\mathbf{C}_{2}
acts by diffeomorphisms on
\widetilde{F_{10}}
, and by equivariance, preserves this bundle:
for
\gamma\in\mathbf{C}_{2}
, the tangent map
T\gamma
acts on the left on
T_{V}\widetilde{F_{10}}
. The associated frame bundle

Fr(T_{V}\widetilde{F_{10}})
has as model the bundle one obtains by considering fiberwise, over a point
x\in S^{\rho}
,

\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{8},T_{xV}\widetilde{F_{10}}),

the space of linear isomorphisms out of
\mathbb{R}^{8}
. The canonical left action of
GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})
induces then a right action
by contravariance on
Fr(T_{V}\widetilde{F_{10}})
. Precomposition commutes with the postcomposition
\mathbf{C}_{2}
-action induced by
the tangent action on
T_{xV}\widetilde{F_{10}}
. Hence the associated frame bundle is an equivariant bundle without intertwine.

We first need to fix a set
\mathcal{R}
of local isotropy representations, satisfying Condition (H) of Lück-Uribe,
[
4
, Definition 6.1]
.
This ensures that the classification problem of the normal bundle is a purely homotopical one by
[
4
, Theorem 10.1]
, which
means that the equivariant triviality of the bundle will be a consequence of the null-homotopy of the classifying map.

Locally, in a neighborhood of the fixed point that was chosen to split the fibration, the vertical direction looks like
a quadruple of complex numbers
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})
on which
\mathbf{C}_{2}
acts by complex conjugation as described in the previous section.
Hence the representation of
\mathbf{C}_{2}
is given by a homomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathbf{C}_{2}\rightarrow GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})
conjugate
to
I_{4}\oplus(-I_{4})
.

At a non-fixed point, the isotropy subgroup is per force the trivial group and the representation the trivial one.
We thus set
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\{(1_{\mathbf{C}_{2}},triv),(\mathbf{C}_{2},\alpha)\}
, and close it under conjugation by elements in
GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})
to form
a family
\mathcal{R}
. This is, in the terminology of
[
4
, Definition 3.4]
, the family of representations associated to
S^{\rho}
.
Notice that the passage from pre-families to families is here effortless: the group
\mathbf{C}_{2}
is so small that two representations thereon either
coincide or coincide only on the trivial element. Because
\mathbf{C}_{2}
is finite and
GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})
is a quasi-connected Lie group,
by
[
4
, Theorem 6.3]
, condition (H) is satisfied for the family
\mathcal{R}
.
These choices define a classifying space
B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{R})
, see
[
4
, Theorem 11.4]
, which
we write
B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))
for short. The isomorphism classes of equivariant bundles over
S^{\rho}
with isotropy representations in
\mathcal{R}
are in bijection
with the set of equivariant homotopy classes into
B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))
. Hence, to our bundle corresponds a (homotopy class of)
\mathbf{C}_{2}
-equivariant
map
f\colon S^{\rho}\rightarrow B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))
. We analyze this map by decomposing
S^{\rho}
into the fixed equator
S^{1}
and two
2
-cells,
the hemispheres, that are permuted. Our aim is to extend this map to a map
F
defined on the whole ball
B^{\rho}
whose boundary is
S^{\rho}
.
We will do this by first extending over the fixed equatorial disk, and then filling-in the two remaining spheres.
Since we are only interested in the triviality of our bundle, we will make our computations in the reduced setting, i.e.
we consider
f
as an element in the set of pointed homotopy classes:

[S^{\rho},B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))]^{\mathbf{C}_{2}}

Our first step is to restrict our equivariant bundle to the fixed circle and show that it is trivial.

Lemma 3.1
.

The classifying map
f
extends to a map
f^{\prime}\colon S^{\rho}\cup D^{2}\rightarrow B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

By Lück-Uribe’s results, the restriction of
f
to
S^{1}
amounts to having a non-equivariant bundle on
S^{1}
but with a reduced structural group.
More precisely, since
S^{1}
is fixed under the
\mathbf{C}_{2}
-action, its image lies in the fixed points of the equivariant classifying space.
By
[
4

, these fixed points are

\displaystyle B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))^{\mathbf{C}_{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\coprod_{\alpha\in Hom_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))/GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})}BC_{GL_{8}(\mathbb{R})}(\alpha).

Since up to conjugacy we only have one representation in
\mathcal{R}
, let us fix as a representative the representation that sends
\tau\in\mathbf{C}_{2}
to
\left(\begin{matrix}I_{4}&0\\
0&-I_{4}\end{matrix}\right)
.
By direct computation

and the trivial
\dashv
fixed points adjunction yields an isomorphism

S^{1}

S^{\rho}
is non-equivariantly two times some bundle.
So let

be a classifying map of the latter bundle. This means that a classifying map for the double

\scriptstyle{\Delta}
\textstyle{S^{2}\times S^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Lemma 3.2
.

The classifying map
f
extends to a map
F\colon B^{\rho}\rightarrow B(\mathbf{C}_{2},GL_{8}(\mathbb{R}))

Proof.

Observe that

is equivariantly homotopic to

By the free

Through this isomorphism

corresponds to its restriction to the union of the northern hemisphere of

and the equatorial disc.
However, by construction, a classifying map for this bundle is given by

. In particular, this is a classifying map of twice a bundle.
We use now Floyd’s original argument to see that this bundle is trivial (it is twice something in
\mathbb{Z}/2
).
Therefore
f^{\prime}
is null-homotopic and thus extends to
B^{\rho}
.
∎

As an immediate consequence we have our desired result that will be used to do the surgery step:

Theorem 3.3
.

The equivariant normal bundle to
S^{\rho}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{F_{10}}
is equivariantly trivial, i.e. the total space of the fibration is equivariantly homeomorphic to
S^{\rho}\times\mathbb{R}^{4\rho}
. ∎

4.
The Floyd manifold
F_{10}
is a conjugation manifold

We are now ready to perform Floyd’s construction of the manifolds
F_{5}
and
F_{10}
simultaneously and equivariantly
on the larger one.
By Theorem
3.3
, we learn that the equivariant tubular neighborhood of
S^{\rho}
is equivariantly
homeomorphic to
S^{\rho}\times D(4\rho)
with boundary
S^{\rho}\times S^{4\rho-1}
. We now remove the interior and glue back a copy of

, thus killing the sphere

to get

with a
\mathbf{C}_{2}
-action.
Observe that on the fixed point subset, we replaced
S^{1}\times D(4)
, a tubular neighbourhood of
S^{1}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{F_{5}}
,
by
D(2)\times S^{3}
, i.e. we performed a standard non-equivariant surgery and get by Floyd’s argument his manifold
F_{5}
.
In particular, by construction, we have:

(F_{10})^{\mathbf{C}_{2}}=F_{5}

To prove our main result is now, we will verify the homotopical criterion for being a conjugation space from
[

Let
\operatorname{H\underline{\mathbf{F}}}
denote the genuine
\mathbf{C}_{2}
-equivariant spectrum associated to the constant functor with values

Theorem 4.1
.

[

A compact equivariant space

such that

X\wedge\operatorname{H\underline{\mathbf{F}}}\simeq\bigvee_{i\in I}S^{n_{i}\rho}\wedge\operatorname{H\underline{\mathbf{F}}}

The starting point to prove this is the very nice structural theorem of C. May
[

]

[

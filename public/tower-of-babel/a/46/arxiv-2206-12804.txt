On the Euler-Poincaré characteristics of a simply connected rationally elliptic CW-complex
Mahmoud Benkhalifa

Abstract
. For a simply connected rationally elliptic CW-complex X X , we show that the cohomology and the homotopy Euler-Poincaré characteristics are related to two new numerical invariants namely η X \eta_{X} and ρ X \rho_{X} which we define using the Whitehead exact sequences of the Quillen and the Sullivan models of X X .



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
55P62

1.
introduction

A simply connected CW-complex
X
is called rationally elliptic if both the graded vector spaces
H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\pi_{*}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
are finite dimensional. To such a space
X
are attached two numerical invariants namely the cohomology Euler characteristic
\chi_{H}
and the homotopy Euler characteristic
\chi_{\pi}
defined by

\chi_{H}=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}H^{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\quad,\quad\chi_{\pi}=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}.

Computing
\chi_{H}
and
\chi_{\pi}
and studying their properties is a major task in rational homotopy theory. For instance, it is well-known that (see
[
9
]
, Prop.
\left.32.10\right)

•

\chi_{H}\geq 0
and
\chi_{\pi}\leq 0.

•

\chi_{H}>0
if and only if
\chi_{\pi}=0.

If
X^{[i]}
denotes the
i^{\text{th }}
Postnikov section of
X
and
X^{i}
denotes its
i^{\text{th }}
skeleton, then we introduce two new numerical homotopy invariants namely

\rho_{X}=1+\sum_{i\geq 2}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}H^{i}(X^{[i-2]};{\mathbb{Q}})\quad,\quad\eta_{X}=1+\sum_{i\geq 2}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}\Gamma_{i}(X),

where
\Gamma_{i}(X)=\operatorname{ker}(\pi_{i}(X^{i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(X^{i},X^{i-1})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}})
for
i\geq 2
.

By exploiting the well-known properties of a rationally elliptic CW-complex
X
as well as the virtue of the Whitehead exact sequences associated respectively to the Sullivan model and the Quillen model of
X
, we prove the following

Theorem 1
.

If
X
is a rationally elliptic CW-complex
X
, then

(1)

H^{i}(X^{[i-2]};{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\Gamma_{i-1}(X)
for all
i\geq 2;

(2)

\rho_{X}=\eta_{X}>0;

(3)

Either
\rho_{X}=\chi_{H}
or
\rho_{X}=-\chi_{\pi}
;

(4)

If
X
is
2
–connected, then
\Gamma_{i}(X)=\pi_{i}(X^{i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
for all
i\geq 2
.

As a corollary of theorem 1, we deduce the following result

Corollary 2
.

Let
X
be a rationally elliptic CW-complex
X
. We have the following

(1)

If
H^{2i}(X^{[2i-2]};{\mathbb{Q}})=0
for every
i\geq 2
, then
X
is rationally an odd-dimensional sphere.

(2)

If
X
is
2
-connected and
\pi_{2i-1}(X^{2i-2})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=0
for every
i\geq 1
, then
X
is rationally an odd-dimensional sphere.

A special class of rationally elliptic CW-complexes is formed by the
F_{0}
-spaces. Such CW-complex is characterised by the fact that its homotopy Euler characteristics vanishes (see definition
3.14
). Thus, from the main theorem of this paper we show

Corollary 3
.

If
X
is a
2
-connected
F_{0}
-space, then

\pi_{2i+1}(X^{2i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=H^{2i+2}(X^{[2i]},{\mathbb{Q}})=0\quad,\quad\forall i\geq 1.

We show our results using standard tools of rational homotopy theory by working algebraically on the models of Quillen and Sullivan of
X
. We refer to
[
9
]
for a general introduction to these techniques. Recall that every simply connected space of finite type has a corresponding differential commutative cochain algebra called the Sullivan model of
X
, unique up to isomorphism, that encodes the rational homotopy of
X
. Dually, every simply connected space
X
has a differential graded Lie algebra (DGL for short), called the Quillen model of
X
and unique up to isomorphism, which determines completely the rational homotopy type of
X
.

The paper is organised as follows. In section 2, we recall the definitions of the Whitehead exact sequences associated with the Quillen and the Sullivan models of an rationally elliptic CW-complex as well as we formulate and prove all the results in an algebraic setting. In section 3, we give a mere transcription of the above results in the topological context.

2.
Whitehead exact sequences in rational homotopy theory

2.1.
Whitehead exact sequence associated with a DGL

Let
W=(W_{\geq 1})
be a finite dimensional graded vector space over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and let
(\mathbb{L}(W),\delta)
be a DGL. We define the linear maps

j_{i}:H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i}))\longrightarrow W_{i}\quad,\quad b_{i}:W_{i}\longrightarrow H_{i-1}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1})),

(1)

by setting
j_{i}([w+y])=w
and
b_{i}(w)=[\delta(w)]
, were
[\delta(w)]
denotes the homology class of
\delta(w)
in the sub-Lie algebra
\mathbb{L}_{i-1}(W_{\leq i-1}).
Recall that if
x\in H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i}))
, then
x=[w+y]
, where
w\in W_{i},y\in\mathbb{L}_{i}(W_{\leq i-1})
and
\delta(w+y)=0
.

To every DGL
(\mathbb{L}(W),\delta)
we can assign (see
[
4
,
6
,
8
]
for more details) the following long exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow W_{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{i+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma_{i}\rightarrow H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W))\rightarrow W_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

(2)

called the Whitehead exact sequence of
(\mathbb{L}(W),\delta)
, where

\Gamma_{i}=\operatorname{ker}(j_{i}:H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i}))\rightarrow W_{i})\quad,\quad i\geq 2.

(3)

Remark 2.1
.

First, it is clear that if
H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i}))=0
, then
\Gamma_{i}=0
.

Next, if
(\mathbb{L}(W),\delta)
satisfies
W_{1}=0
, then

\Gamma_{i}=H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,i\geq 2.

(4)

Indeed; let us consider the following long exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow H_{i+1}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i})/\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))\rightarrow H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))\rightarrow H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i}))\rightarrow\cdots

which is associated with the following short exact of chain complexes

(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}),\delta)\mapsto(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i}),\delta)\rightarrow(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i})/\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}),\widetilde{\delta}),

where
(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i})/\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}),\widetilde{\delta})
is the quotient chain complex and the differential
\widetilde{\delta}
is induced by
\delta.
Now clearly, we have
(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i})/\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))\cong W_{i}
and as
W_{1}=0
it follows that
H_{i+1}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i})/\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))=0.
As a result we obtain (
4
).

2.2.
Whitehead exact sequence associated with a Sullivan algebra

A Sulivan algebra
(\Lambda V,\partial)
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is simply connected if
V^{1}=0.
Let
(\Lambda(V^{\leq i-2}),\partial)
be the sub algebra generated by the graded vector apace
V^{\leq i-2}.
For simplicity let us write

L^{i}=H^{i}(\Lambda(V^{\leq i-2}))\quad,\quad i\geq 2.

(5)

The Whitehead exact sequence of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is defined as follows (see
[
4
,
5
,
9
]
for more details)

\cdots\rightarrow H^{i}(\Lambda V)\rightarrow V^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b^{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}L^{i+1}\longrightarrow H^{i+1}(\Lambda V)\longrightarrow V^{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b^{i+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

(6)

Here
b^{i}(v)=[\partial(v)]
, where
[\partial(v)]
is the cohomology class of the cycle
\partial(v)
in
\Lambda(V^{\leq i-2})
.

2.3.
Elliptic algebras

A Sullivan algebra
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is called elliptic if

\operatorname{dim}H^{*}(\Lambda V)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{dim}H^{i}(\Lambda V)<\infty\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\operatorname{dim}V=\sum_{i\geq 2}\operatorname{dim}V^{i}<\infty.

Let us call
n=\max\left\{i:H^{i}(\Lambda V)\neq 0\right\}
the formal dimension of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
. For an elliptic Sullivan algebra
(\Lambda V,\partial)
, we define the following two numbers

\displaystyle\chi_{\Lambda V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{dim}H^{2i}(\Lambda V)-\sum_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{dim}H^{2i+1}(\Lambda V)=H^{\rm{even}}(\Lambda V)-H^{\rm{odd}}(\Lambda V),

\displaystyle\chi_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}\operatorname{dim}V^{2i}-\sum_{i\geq 1}\operatorname{dim}V^{2i+1}=\operatorname{dim}V^{\rm{even}}-\operatorname{dim}V^{\rm{odd}}.

The following are some important properties of elliptic Sullivan algebras.

Theorem 2.2
.

([4], §32) Suppose
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is elliptic of formal dimension
n.
Then

(1)

\operatorname{dim}V^{\text{odd }}\geq\operatorname{dim}V^{\text{even }};

(2)

V^{i}=0
, for
i\geq 2n
;

(3)

V^{i}=0
, for
i>n
and
i
even;

(4)

There is only one non-trivial
V^{i}
, for
i>n
and
i
odd. Necessary, we have
\operatorname{dim}V^{i}=1;

(5)

\chi_{\Lambda V}\geq 0
and
\chi_{V}\leq 0.
Moreover
\chi_{\Lambda V}=0\Longleftrightarrow\chi_{V}<0.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is elliptic of formal dimension
n.

(1)

For every even number i such that
i>n+1
, we have
L^{i}=0
;

(2)

For every odd number i such that i
>n+1
we have
L^{i}\cong V^{i-1}
;

(3)

For every i such that
i>2n
, we have
L^{i}=0
.

Proof.

As
(\Lambda V,\partial)
has formal dimension
n
, it follows that
H^{i}(\Lambda V)=0
for
i>n
. Therefore, by theorem
2.2
the Whitehead exact sequence of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
can be written as

H^{n}(\Lambda V)\rightarrow V^{n}\rightarrow L^{n+1}\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots\to 0\rightarrow V^{2n-1}\to L^{2n}\rightarrow 0\rightarrow V^{2n}=0\to L^{2n+1}\to 0

Consequently, the three assertions of proposition
2.3
are easily derived.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\Lambda V,\partial)
be an elliptic Sullivan algebra. Using (
5
), we define

\rho_{\Lambda V}=1+\operatorname{dim}L^{\text{even }}-\operatorname{dim}L^{\text{odd }},

where

\operatorname{dim}L^{\text{even }}=\operatorname{dim}L^{4}+\operatorname{dim}L^{6}+\operatorname{dim}L^{8}+\dots

\operatorname{dim}L^{\text{odd }}=\operatorname{dim}L^{5}+\operatorname{dim}L^{7}+\operatorname{dim}L^{9}+\dots

Note that from (
5
), we deduce that
L^{i}=0
for
i=2,3
.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is elliptic of formal dimension
n
, then we have

0\leq\rho_{\Lambda V}-\sum_{i=4}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}\leq 2.

(7)

Proof.

First by (
4
) of proposition
2.3
, we have

\rho_{\Lambda V}=1+\sum_{i=4}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}=1+\sum_{i=4}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}+\sum_{i=n+2}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}.

Next, let us focus on the integer
\sum_{i=n+2}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}.
Using the relations (3) and (4) of theorem
2.2
and (2) of proposition
2.3
, we deduce that

-1\leq\sum_{i=n+2}^{2n}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}\leq 1.

Therefore

\sum_{i=4}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}\leq\rho_{\Lambda V}\leq\sum_{i=4}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}+2,

as wanted.
∎

The next result shows the relationship between the
\rho_{\Lambda V},\chi_{\Lambda V}
and
\chi_{V}.

Theorem 2.6
.

If
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is an elliptic Sullivan algebra, then
\rho_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{\Lambda V}-\chi_{V}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the Whitehead exact sequence of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
given in (
6
)

\cdots\rightarrow H^{i}(\Lambda V)\rightarrow V^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b^{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}L^{i+1}\longrightarrow H^{i+1}(\Lambda V)\longrightarrow V^{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b^{i+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

Let us write
H^{*}=H^{*}(\Lambda V)
. For every
i\geq 2
, let

V^{i,1}=\operatorname{ker}b^{i}\quad,\quad V^{i,2}=\operatorname{im}b^{i}\quad,\quad L^{i+1,1}=\operatorname{im}i^{i+1}.

The exactness of the above sequence implies that

V^{i}\cong V^{i,1}\oplus V^{i,2}\quad,\quad L^{i+1}\cong V^{i,2}\oplus L^{i+1,1}\quad,\quad H^{i+1}\cong L^{i+1,1}\oplus V^{i+1,1}.

(8)

Using the direct summands in (
8
) we deduce the following

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}V^{3}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}H^{3}+\operatorname{dim}V^{3,2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}H^{3}+\operatorname{dim}L^{4}-\operatorname{dim}L^{4,1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}H^{3}+\operatorname{dim}L^{4}-\operatorname{dim}H^{4}+\operatorname{dim}V^{4,1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}H^{3}+\operatorname{dim}L^{4}-\operatorname{dim}H^{4}+\operatorname{dim}V^{4}-\operatorname{dim}V^{4,2}.

Iterating the above process and taking into consideration that
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is elliptic which implies that this process must stop, we get the following formula

\operatorname{dim}V^{3}=-\sum_{i\geq 3}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}H^{i}+\sum_{i\geq 4}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}+\sum_{i\geq 4}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}V^{i},

which implies

\sum_{i\geq 3}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}H^{i}-\sum_{i\geq 3}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}V^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 4}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i}.

(9)

As
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is simply connected, it follows that
H^{0}\cong{\mathbb{Q}}
,
H^{1}=0
and
H^{2}\cong V^{2}
. Therefore the relation (
9
) becomes

(\operatorname{dim}H^{\text{even }}-\operatorname{dim}H^{\text{odd }})-(\operatorname{dim}V^{\text{even }}-\operatorname{dim}V^{\text{odd }})=1+\sum_{i\geq 4}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}L^{i},

implying
\rho_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{\Lambda V}-\chi_{V}
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

If
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is an elliptic Sullivan algebra, then
\rho_{\Lambda V}>0.
Moreover if
\chi_{\Lambda V}>0
, then
\rho_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{\Lambda V}
.

Proof.

As
\rho_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{\Lambda V}-\chi_{V}
, the relation (5) of theorem
2.2
implies that
\rho_{\Lambda V}\geq 0.
Now if we assume that
\rho_{\Lambda V}=0
, then we derive that
\chi_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{V}
. Again by the relation (5) of theorem
2.2
, on one hand we have
\chi_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{V}=0
and on the other hand we have
\chi_{V}<0
which is impossible. Thus,
\rho_{\Lambda V}>0
.

Now if
\chi_{\Lambda V}>0
, then the relation (4) of theorem
2.2
implies that
\chi_{V}=0.
Hence
\rho_{\Lambda V}=\chi_{\Lambda V}-\chi_{V}=\chi_{\Lambda V}
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

If
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is an elliptic Sullivan algebra, then
L^{\rm{even}}\geq L^{\rm{odd}}
. Moreover, if
L^{\rm{even}}=0
, then
\operatorname{dim}H^{*}(\Lambda V)=\operatorname{dim}V^{*}+1
.

Proof.

By combining definition
2.4
and corollary
2.7
, we obtain

\rho_{\Lambda V}=1+\operatorname{dim}L^{\text{even }}-\operatorname{dim}L^{\text{odd }}>0\Longrightarrow L^{\rm{even}}\geq L^{\rm{odd}}.

Thus, if
L^{\rm{even}}=0
, then
L^{\rm{odd}}=0
implying that
L^{i}=0\,,\,\forall i\geq 2
. Finally, from the Whitehead exact sequence (
6
) of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
, we deduce that

H^{i}(\Lambda V)\cong V^{i}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall i\geq 2.

(10)

Consequently, if
n
is the formal dimension of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
, then
V^{i}=0\,,\,\forall i>n
and we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}H^{*}(\Lambda V)\hskip-5.69054pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\Lambda V)+\operatorname{dim}H^{1}(\Lambda V)+\operatorname{dim}H^{2}(\Lambda V)+\cdots+\operatorname{dim}H^{n}(\Lambda V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt1+0+\operatorname{dim}V^{2}+\operatorname{dim}V^{3}+\cdots+\operatorname{dim}V^{n}=1+\operatorname{dim}V^{*}

as desired.
∎

3.
Topological applications

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex.
For every
i\geq 2
, we define the vector space

\Gamma_{i}(X)=\operatorname{ker}(\pi_{i}(X^{i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(X^{i},X^{i-1})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}).

(11)

Here
X^{i}
denotes the
i^{\text{th }}
-skeleton of
X
.

Thus, if
(\mathbb{L}(W),\delta)
denotes the Quillen model of
X
, then by virtue of the properties of this model, we obtain the following identifications (valid for any
i\geq 2
)

\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\cong H_{i-1}(\mathbb{L}(W))\quad,\quad H_{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong W_{i-1}\quad,\quad\Gamma_{i}(X)\cong\Gamma_{i-1},

(12)

where
\Gamma_{i}
is defined in (
3
). Therefore the Whitehead exact sequence (
2
) of this model can be written as

\cdots\longrightarrow H_{i+1}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{i+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma_{i}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

(13)

where
h_{i}
is the Hurewicz homomorphism.

Remark 3.1
.

Firstly, since
(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}),\delta)
can be chosen as the Quillen model of the skeleton
X^{i}
(see
[
9
]
, pp. 323) implying that

\pi_{i}(X^{i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\cong H_{i-1}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall i\geq 2

and based on remark
2.1
and the identifications (
12
), it follows that

\pi_{i}(X^{i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=0\Longrightarrow\Gamma_{i}(X)=0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall i\geq 2

(14)

Secondly, if the space
X
is
2
-connected, then by remark
2.1
we deduce that

\Gamma_{i}\cong H_{i}(\mathbb{L}(W_{\leq i-1}))\cong\pi_{i+1}(X^{i})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\quad,\quad i\geq 2,

and the Whitehead exact sequence (
13
) becomes

\cdots\longrightarrow H_{i+1}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{i+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi_{i}(X^{i-1})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow H_{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

Dually, if
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is the Sullivan model of

, then we have

H^{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H^{i}(\Lambda V)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,H^{i+1}(X^{[i-1]};{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H^{i+1}(\Lambda V^{\leq i-1}),

V^{i}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}};{\mathbb{Q}}),

(15)

where
X^{[i]}
denotes the
i^{\text{th }}
Postikov section of
X.
Therefore, the Whitehead exact sequence of this model can be written as

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{Q}})\overset{b^{i}}{\rightarrow}H^{i+1}(X^{[i-1]};{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow H^{i+1}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{i+1}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow\cdots

(16)

Recall that a simply connected CW-complex
X
is rationally elliptic if

\operatorname{dim}H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})=\sum_{i\geq 0}\operatorname{dim}H^{i}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})<\infty\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\,\operatorname{dim}\pi_{*}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=\sum_{i\geq 2}\operatorname{dim}\pi_{i}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}<\infty

Note that, by virtue of the Sullivan model,

is rationally elliptic if and only if its Sullivan model is elliptic. In this case the formal dimension of

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a rationally elliptic CW-complex. We define

\eta_{X}=1+\mathrm{dim}\,\Gamma_{\rm{even}}(X)-\mathrm{dim}\,\Gamma_{\rm{odd}}(X)

where
\Gamma_{*}(X)
is defined in (

)

Definition 3.3
.

If
X
is a rationally elliptic CW-complex and
(\Lambda V,\partial)
its Sullivan model, then we define

, where

Subsequently, we need the following proposition showing that

Proposition 3.4
.

If

H^{i+1}(X^{[i-1]};{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\text{\rm{Hom}}(\Gamma_{i}(X),{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad i\geq 2.

Applying the exact functor

and by comparing the sequences

.
∎

The next result establishes that the two numerical invariants
\eta_{X}
and
\rho_{Y}
are equal although they are defined differently.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
X
is a rationally elliptic CW-complex, then
\eta_{X}=\rho_{X}
.

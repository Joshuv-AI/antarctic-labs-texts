Introduction

Let
A
and
B
be sets of integers. The sumsets and difference sets are defined by

A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\},\quad A-B=\{a-b:a\in A,b\in B\}

respectively. For any integer
t,
we define the sets

t+A=\{t\}+A,\quad t-A=\{t\}-A.

For
h\geq 2
, we denote by
hA
the
h
-fold sumset of
A
, which is the set of all integers
n
of the form
n=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{h}
, where
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{h}
are elements of
A
and not necessarily distinct.

In
[
4
,
3
]
, Nathanson proved the following fundamental beautiful
theorem on the structure of
h
-fold sumsets.

Nathanson’s Theorem A.
Let
A=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
be a finite set of integers such that

0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\gcd(A)=1.

Let
h_{1}=(k-1)(a_{k}-1)a_{k}+1
. There are nonnegative integers
c_{1}
and
d_{1}
and
finite sets
C_{1}
and
D_{1}
with
C_{1}\subseteq[0,c_{1}-2]
and
D_{1}\subseteq[0,d_{1}-2]

such that

hA=C_{1}\cup[c_{1},ha_{k}-d_{1}]\cup(ha_{k}-D_{1})

for all
h\geq h_{1}
.

Later, Wu, Chen and Chen
[
6
]
improved the lower bound of
h_{1}
to

\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}-k
. Recently, Granville and Shakan
[
1
]
, and Granville and Walker
[
2
]

gave some further results on this topic.

Let
A
be a set of integers. For every positive integer
h,
the
h
-fold representation function
r_{A,h}(n)
counts the number of representations of
n
as the sum of
h
elements of
A.
Thus,

r_{A,h}(n)=\sharp\left\{\left(a_{j_{1}},\ldots,a_{j_{h}}\right)\in A^{h}:n=\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{j_{i}}\text{ and }a_{j_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq a_{j_{h}}\right\}.

For every positive integer
t,
let
(hA)^{(t)}
be the set of all integers
n
that have at least
t
representations as the sum of
h
elements of
A,
that is,

(hA)^{(t)}=\left\{n\in\mathbf{Z}:r_{A,h}(n)\geq t\right\}

Recently, Nathanson
[
5
]
found that the sumsets
(hA)^{(t)}
have the same structure as the sumset

hA
and proved the following theorem.

Nathanson’s Theorem B.
Let
k\geq 2
, and let
A=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
be a finite set of integers
such that
0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{k}
and
\gcd(A)=1
. For every positive integer

t
, let
h_{t}=(k-1)(ta_{k}-1)a_{k}+1
. There are nonnegative integers
c_{t}
are
d_{t}

and finite sets
C_{t}
and
D_{t}
with
C_{t}\subseteq[0,c_{t}-2]
and
D_{t}\subseteq[0,d_{t}-2]

such that

(hA)^{(t)}=C_{t}\cup[c_{t},ha_{k}-d_{t}]\cup(ha_{k}-D_{t})

for all
h\geq h_{t}
.

In this paper, motivated by the idea of Wu, Chen and Chen
[
6
]
, we improved the lower bound of

in Nathanson’s Theorem B.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 2,
and let
A=\left\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\right\}
be a finite set of integers such that

0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{k}\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{gcd}(A)=1

For every positive integer

(1.1)

for all

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
and let
A=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
be a set of integers satisfying

0=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{k}
and
\gcd(A)=1.
For every positive integer
t
, let

h_{t}=\sum_{i=2}^{k}(ta_{i}-1)-1
and

If
c^{\prime}_{t}-a_{k}<n<c^{\prime}_{t}
, then there exist at least
t
distinct nonnegative

k
-tuples
(x_{1,s},x_{2,s},\ldots,x_{k,s})~(1\leq s\leq t)
satisfying

\displaystyle n=x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k,s}a_{k}

and
x_{1,s}+x_{2,s}+\cdots+x_{k,s}\leq h_{t}
for
s=1,2,\ldots,t
.

Proof.

Since
\gcd\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\right)=1,
there exist integers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
such that

n=x_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}a_{k}.

For any positive integer
s
,
[(s-1)a_{2},sa_{2}-1]
is a complete residue system modulo
a_{2}
. Hence
there exists an integer
q
such that

x_{1}=a_{2}q+x_{1,s}
with
(s-1)a_{2}\leq x_{1,s}\leq sa_{2}-1
.
This gives

n=x_{1,s}a_{1}+\left(a_{1}q+x_{2}\right)a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k}a_{k}.

Let
x^{\prime}_{2}=a_{1}q+x_{2}
. Similarly, there exists an integer
q^{\prime}
such that

x^{\prime}_{2}=a_{3}q^{\prime}+x_{2,s}
with
(s-1)a_{3}\leq x_{2,s}\leq sa_{3}-1
.
Now we have

n=x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}x_{2}+(a_{2}q^{\prime}+x_{3})a_{3}+\cdots+x_{k}a_{k}.

By continuing this process, we obtain

n=x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k,s}a_{k}

with
(s-1)a_{i+1}\leq x_{i,s}\leq sa_{i+1}-1
for
i=1,\cdots,k-1
and
x_{k,s}
is some integer.
Hence, for any integer
s\in[1,t]
, we have

0\leq x_{i,s}\leq ta_{i+1}-1.

Since
n>c^{\prime}_{t}-a_{k}
, it follows that

\displaystyle x_{k,s}a_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\left(x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k-1,s}a_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n-\left(ta_{2}-1\right)a_{1}-\cdots-\left(ta_{k}-1\right)a_{k-1}=n-c^{\prime}_{t}>-a_{k},

and then
x_{k,s}>-1
. Noting that
x_{k,s}
is an integer, we have
x_{k,s}\geq 0
.
By the bound of
x_{i,s}
, the following nonnegative
k
-tuples

(x_{1,s},x_{2,s},\ldots,x_{k-1,s},x_{k,s})\quad(1\leq s\leq t)

are distinct.

Next, we shall prove that
x_{1,s}+x_{2,s}+\cdots+x_{k,s}\leq h_{t}
for
s=1,2,\ldots,t
.

For any integer
s\in[1,t]
, let
x_{1,s}+x_{2,s}+\cdots+x_{k,s}=u_{s}
.
Since
n<c^{\prime}_{t}
, it follows that

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k,s}a_{k}

\displaystyle=x_{1,s}a_{1}+\cdots+x_{k-1,s}a_{k-1}+\left(u_{s}-x_{1,s}-x_{2,s}-\cdots-x_{k-1,s}\right)a_{k}

\displaystyle=u_{s}a_{k}-x_{1,s}\left(a_{k}-a_{1}\right)-\cdots-x_{k-1,s}\left(a_{k}-a_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq u_{s}a_{k}-\left(ta_{2}-1\right)\left(a_{k}-a_{1}\right)-\cdots-\left(ta_{k}-1\right)\left(a_{k}-a_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=u_{s}a_{k}-a_{k}\left[\left(ta_{2}-1\right)+\cdots+\left(ta_{k}-1\right)\right]+a_{1}\left(ta_{2}-1\right)+\cdots+a_{k-1}\left(ta_{k}-1\right)

\displaystyle=u_{s}a_{k}-\left(h_{t}+1\right)a_{k}+c^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle>u_{s}a_{k}-\left(h_{t}+1\right)a_{k}+n.

Hence
u_{s}a_{k}-\left(h_{t}+1\right)a_{k}<0
, and then
u_{s}<h_{t}+1
. Therefore,
u_{s}\leq h_{t}
.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
c^{\prime}_{t}
and
h_{t}
be defined in Lemma
2.2
. Then

c^{\prime}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{i}\left(ta_{i+1}-1\right)\in\left(\left(h_{t}+1\right)A\right)^{(t)}.

Proof.

For

, let

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{t}=\left(ta_{2}-1\right)a_{1}+\cdots+\left(ta_{k}-1\right)a_{k-1}=p_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+p_{k-1}a_{k-1}.

Noting that

p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k-1}=\sum_{i=2}^{k}(ta_{i}-1)=h_{t}+1,

we have
c^{\prime}_{t}\in\left(h_{t}+1\right)A
.

Moreover, for any integers
r\in[0,t-1]
, we have

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(ta_{i+1}-1\right)a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left((t-r)a_{i+1}-1\right)a_{i}+r\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}a_{i}a_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((t-r)a_{2}-1\right)a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}((t-r)a_{i+1}-1+ra_{i-1})a_{i}+ra_{k-1}a_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle p_{1,r}a_{1}+p_{2,r}a_{2}+\cdots+p_{k-1,r}a_{k-1}+p_{k,r}a_{k},

where
p_{1,r}=(t-r)a_{2}-1
,
p_{k,r}=ra_{k-1}
and
p_{i,r}=(t-r)a_{i+1}-1+ra_{i-1}~(2\leq i\leq k-1)
.
Hence
p_{i,r}\geq 0
for all
i\in[1,k]
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i,r}

\displaystyle=(t-r)a_{2}-1+(t-r)a_{3}-1+ra_{1}+\cdots+(t-r)a_{k}-1+ra_{k-2}+ra_{k-1}

\displaystyle=h_{t}+1-r\left(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}\right)+r\left(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{t}+1-r\left(a_{k}-a_{1}\right)\leq h_{t}+1.

Thus,
r_{A,{h_{t}+1}}(c^{\prime}_{t})\geq t
, and so
c^{\prime}_{t}\in\left(\left(h_{t}+1\right)A\right)^{(t)}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

,

be positive integers with

For any positive integer

Proof.

Suppose that

. Let

be a solution of the equation (

). Then
all the integer solutions of the equation (

) is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x=x_{0}+ka_{2},\\
y=y_{0}-ka_{1},\end{cases}~k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.2)

In order to

and
y\geq 0
, we only need

and

, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{-1-x_{0}}{a_{2}}<k<\frac{y_{0}+1}{a_{1}}.

(2.3)

Since

\displaystyle\frac{y_{0}+1}{a_{1}}-\frac{-1-x_{0}}{a_{2}}=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{1}x_{0}+a_{2}y_{0}}{a_{1}a_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+n}{a_{1}a_{2}}>\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+ta_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}}{a_{1}a_{2}}=t,

there exist at least
t
integers

such that (
2.3
) holds.

Therefore, the equation (
2.1
) has at least
t
nonnegative integer solutions.

Now suppose that
l=ta_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}
. Then

(ta_{2}-1,-1)
is a solution of (
2.1
). Take
x_{0}=ta_{2}-1
and

.
Then (

) becomes

\begin{cases}x=ta_{2}-1-ka_{2},\\

Since

and

Hence there exist at most

This completes the proof of Lemma

(x_{1,s},x_{2,s},\ldots,x_{k,s})~(1\leq s\leq t)
such that

a=x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k,s}a_{k}

and

x_{1,s}+x_{2,s}+\cdots+x_{k,s}\leq h.

By Lemma 2, assume that
0\leq x_{i,s}\leq ta_{i+1}-1
for

.
If
x_{k,s}\leq 0
, then by (3) we have

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leq x_{1,s}a_{1}+x_{2,s}a_{2}+\cdots+x_{k-1,s}a_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq a_{1}\left(ta_{2}-1\right)+\cdots+a_{k-1}\left(ta_{k}-1\right)

Therefore

.

Proof of Remark
1.1
.

,

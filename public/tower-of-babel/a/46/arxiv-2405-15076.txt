Refined conjectures on Fitting ideals of Selmer groups over 𝐙 p 2 \mathbf{Z}_{p}^{2} -extensions
Cédric Dion

Abstract
. Let p > 3 p>3 be a prime number and K K be an imaginary quadratic field where p p splits. Let K ∞ K_{\infty} be the 𝐙 p 2 \mathbf{Z}_{p}^{2} -extension of K K and let K n K_{n} be a finite subextension of K ∞ / K K_{\infty}/K . Let E E be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at p p . Under some hypotheses, we show that the Mazur–Tate element attached to E E over K n K_{n} by S. Haran generates the Fitting ideal of the dual Selmer group of E E over K n K_{n} .

Introduction

{\mathrm{Sel}}(\mathbf{Q}^{(n)},E[p^{\infty}])\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ker\left(H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbf{Q}^{(n)},E[p^{\infty}])\to\prod_{v}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{(n)},E[p^{\infty}])}{E(\mathbf{Q}_{v}^{(n)})\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\right)

\theta^{\prime}_{n+1}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{a\in(\mathbf{Z}/p^{n+1}\mathbf{Z})^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}}\left[\frac{a}{p^{n+1}}\right]^{+}\cdot\sigma_{a}

\Lambda_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}[{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}/\mathbf{Q})]

Conjecture 1.1

(Weak Mazur–Tate conjecture)
.

Assume that

\theta_{n}(f)\in\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n}}\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}({\mathrm{Sel}}(\mathbf{Q}^{(n)},E[p^{\infty}]),\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})\right).

A stronger form of this conjecture formulated by Kurihara
[
15

Conjecture 1.2

(Strong Mazur–Tate conjecture)
.

Assume that
a_{p}\not\equiv 1\bmod p

E

(\theta_{n}(f))=\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n}}\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}({\mathrm{Sel}}(\mathbf{Q}^{(n)},E[p^{\infty}]),\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})\right).

The strong form of the Mazur–Tate conjecture was proven by Kim and Kurihara in
[

Theorem 1.3

(Kim-Kurihara, Strong Mazur–Tate conjecture)
.

Let

be an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at an odd prime
p
. Let
\overline{\rho}
be the residual Galois representation attached to

\overline{\rho}
is surjective if
E
is non-CM. Assume that
a_{p}(E)\not\equiv 1\bmod p
and that
p
does not divide the Tamagawa number of
E
. Further assume that the Iwasawa main conjecture over
\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}
holds. Then,

(\theta_{n}(f))=\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n}}\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}({\mathrm{Sel}}(\mathbf{Q}^{(n)},E[p^{\infty}]),\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})\right).

Lemma 4.6

[
11
, §1.3]
for a reference on the topic). In
[
11
, discussion after Corollary 2.4]
, it is shown that the determinant of such matrices is given by

\mathrm{det}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1-\alpha_{v}^{-1}\chi_{i}(\sigma_{v})\right).

Since the
\chi_{i}(\sigma_{v})
are
k

For

has

6.

6.1.
Fitting ideals

Let
R
be a ring and
M
be a finitely presented
R
-module. Let

R^{s}\xrightarrow{h}R^{r}\to M\to 0

be a presentation of
M
. The zeroth Fitting ideal of
M
over
R
, denoted
\mathrm{Fitt}_{R}(M)
, is the ideal of
R
generated by the
r\times r
minors of the matrix
h
. The Fitting ideal does not depend of the choice of presentation for
M
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely presented
R
-module. If
I\subset R
is an ideal, then

\mathrm{Fitt}_{R/I}(M/IM)=\pi\left(\mathrm{Fitt}_{R}(M)\right)

where
\pi:R\to R/I
is the natural quotient map.

Proof.

See
[
18
, 4, Appendix]
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

If
R\to R^{\prime}
is a ring map, then
\mathrm{Fitt}_{R^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{R}R^{\prime})
is the ideal of
R^{\prime}
generated by the image of
\mathrm{Fitt}_{R}(M)
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
is right exact.
∎

6.2.
Definition

Definition 6.3
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring. A complex
M^{\bullet}
of
R
-modules is perfect if there exists a quasi-isomorphism
P^{\bullet}\to M^{\bullet}
, where
P^{\bullet}
is a bounded complex of projective
R
-modules of finite type. Denote by
D_{\mathrm{parf}}(R)
the category of perfect
R
-modules. One says that
M^{\bullet}\in D_{\mathrm{parf}}(R)
has perfect amplitude contained in
[a,b]
(denoted
M^{\bullet}\in D_{\mathrm{parf}}^{[a,b]}(R)
) if the complex
P^{\bullet}
above can be chosen in such a way that
P^{i}=0
for every
i<a
and
i>b
.

Definition 6.4
.

Suppose that
M^{\bullet}\in D_{\mathrm{parf}}(R)
is represented by a complex

M^{\bullet}=\cdots\to M^{0}\to M^{1}\to M^{2}\to\cdots

of finitely generated free
R
-modules such that
M^{N}=M^{-N}=0
for all sufficiently large integers
N
. We define the Euler-Poincaré characteristic
\chi(M^{\bullet})
as

\chi(M^{\bullet})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}(-1)^{n}\mathrm{rank}_{R}(H^{n}(M)).

6.3.
Selmer complexes for elliptic curves

Recall that
\Sigma
is a finite set of places of
K
containing the places at infinity and the ones dividing
N_{E}p
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the set of primes dividing
N_{E}
. For
v
a prime in
K
, let
I_{v}\subseteq{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{K_{v}}/K_{v})
be the inertia group at
v
.
We denote by
\widetilde{{\mathbf{R}}\Gamma}_{f}(G_{K,\Sigma},A,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
the Selmer complex defined by using the Greenberg local conditions for
E

[
19
]
and we denote its cohomology by
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{i}(G_{K,\Sigma},A,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
. We define
\widetilde{H}^{i}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
to be
\widetilde{H}_{f}^{i}(G_{K,\Sigma},T\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\Lambda^{\iota},\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
where the local datum
\Delta_{\text{Gr}}
is induced by the filtration
T^{+}\otimes\Lambda^{\iota}\subset T\otimes\Lambda^{\iota}
. Then, Nekovář duality theorem
[
19
, 8.9.6.2]
provides us with a canonical isomorphism

(3)

\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})^{\iota}\cong\widetilde{H}_{f}^{1}(G_{K,\Sigma},A,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})^{\vee}.

Proposition 6.5
.

For every prime
v\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, suppose that
A^{G_{v}}=0
. Then, we have

\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}}))=\mathrm{char}_{\Lambda}(\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})).

Proof.

By the definition of the Selmer complex
\widetilde{{\mathbf{R}}\Gamma}_{f}(G_{K,\Sigma},A,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
, we have an isomorphism

\widetilde{H}^{0}_{f}(G_{K,\Sigma},A,\Delta_{\mathrm{Gr}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\ker\left(H^{0}(G_{K,\Sigma},A)\to\prod_{v\in\Sigma}H^{0}(G_{K_{v}},A)\right).

This shows that
\widetilde{H}^{0}_{f}(G_{K,\Sigma},A,\Delta_{\mathrm{Gre}})=0
. Let
v
be a prime of
K
and let
\mathrm{Tam}_{v}(T,(\varpi))
be the local Tamagawa factor as defined in
[
19
, Definition 7.6.10.1]
. If
A^{G_{v}}=0
, then we have by
[
19
, 7.6.10.12]
that
\mathrm{Tam}_{v}(T,(\varpi))=0
. Thus, it follows by
[
19
, Proposition 9.7.7 (iii)]
that

(4)

\widetilde{{\mathbf{R}}\Gamma}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(G_{K,\Sigma},T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})\in D_{\mathrm{parf}}^{[1,2]}(\Lambda).

Furthermore,
[
19
, Theorem 8.9.15]
gives us that the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of
\widetilde{{\mathbf{R}}\Gamma}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(G_{K,\Sigma},T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
is

\mathrm{rank}_{\Lambda}(T\otimes\Lambda^{\iota})-\sum_{v\in\{{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}\}}[K_{v}:\mathbf{Q}_{p}]\mathrm{rank}_{\Lambda}(T_{v}^{+}\otimes\Lambda^{\iota}).

By the definition of
T_{v}^{+}
and the fact that
p
splits in
K
, we get

(5)

\chi(\widetilde{{\mathbf{R}}\Gamma}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(G_{K,\Sigma},T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}}))=0.

By combining (
4
) and (

), there exists an exact sequence of
\Lambda
-modules

0\to\widetilde{H}^{1}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})\to P\to P\to\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})\to 0,

where

is a free
\Lambda
-module of finite rank. The proof of
[
5
, Corollary 2.10]
shows that
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
is
\Lambda
-torsion if and only if
\widetilde{H}^{1}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})=0
, in which case

\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}}))=\mathrm{char}_{\Lambda}(\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})).

If
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})
is not torsion,

\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}}))=0=\mathrm{char}_{\Lambda}(\widetilde{H}^{2}_{f,\mathrm{Iw}}(K_{\infty}/K,T,\Delta_{\text{Gr}})).

∎

7.
Refined Mazur–Tate conjecture

We now prove the main result of this note.

Let
\mathfrak{f}={\mathfrak{p}}^{n+1}{\mathfrak{q}}^{m+1}
be a modulus. Since taking
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K_{\infty}/K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{f}))
-invariants is the same as considering the part of
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A)
killed by
(\omega_{n}(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}-1),\omega_{m}(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{q}}}-1))
, the control theorem (
3.2
) can be reformulated as

(6)

{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A)[\omega_{n}(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}-1),\omega_{m}(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{q}}}-1)]\cong{\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{f}),A)

provided that
E(K)[p]
is trivial, that
a_{p}\not\equiv 1\bmod p
and that
E(K_{v})[p]
is trivial for all
v\in\Sigma^{\prime}
. Put
\Lambda_{n,m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}[{\mathrm{Gal}}(K^{\prime}({\mathfrak{p}}^{n+1}{\mathfrak{q}}^{m+1})/K)]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that the Iwasawa main conjecture
1.4
holds. Assume that the ratio of periods
\Omega_{\mathrm{Loe}}/\langle f,f\rangle_{N_{E}}
is a
p
-adic unit. Suppose that
E(K)[p]
is trivial, that
E(K_{v})[p]
is trivial for all
v\in\Sigma^{\prime}
and that
a_{p}\not\equiv 1\bmod p
. then,

\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n,m}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K^{\prime}({\mathfrak{p}}^{n+1}{\mathfrak{q}}^{m+1}),A))=(\Theta_{n,m})

as ideals in
\Lambda_{n,m}
for
n+m\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the Iwasawa main conjecture
1.4
, proposition
5.1
and proposition
6.6
, we have an equality of ideals

(7)

\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A))=\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A))=(L_{p}^{\mathrm{Hi}}(f_{\alpha}/K))=(L_{p,\Delta}(f))

inside
\Lambda
. Take the projection to
\Lambda_{n,m}
on both side of (
7
) and use lemma
6.1
to get

(\Theta_{n,m}^{S})=\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n,m}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A)/(\omega_{n}(\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}-1),\omega_{m}(\gamma_{\mathfrak{q}}-1))\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A)).

Finally, since Mazur–Tate elements and
p
-stabilized Mazur–Tate elements generate the same ideal by theorem
4.10
, we get

\displaystyle(\Theta_{n,m})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n,m}}({\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K_{\infty},A)[\omega_{n}(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}-1),\omega_{m}(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{q}}}-1)]^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n,m}}({\mathrm{Sel}}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K^{\prime}({\mathfrak{p}}^{n+1}{\mathfrak{q}}^{m+1}),A)^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{n,m}}(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{Gr}}(K^{\prime}({\mathfrak{p}}^{n+1}{\mathfrak{q}}^{m+1}),A))

where the second equality follows by Mazur’s control theorem (
6
).
∎

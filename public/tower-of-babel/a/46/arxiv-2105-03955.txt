Abstract
. We characterize the Lie groups with finitely many connected components that are O ⁡ ( u ) O(u) -bilipschitz equivalent (almost quasiisometric in the sense that the sublinear function u u replaces the additive bounds of quasiisometry) to the real hyperbolic space, or to the complex hyperbolic plane. The characterizations are expressed in terms of deformations of Lie algebras and in terms of pinching of sectional curvature of left-invariant Riemannian metrics in the real case. We also compare sublinear bilipschitz equivalence and coarse equivalence, and prove that every coarse equivalence between the logarithmic coarse structures of geodesic spaces is a O ⁡ ( log ) O(\log) -bilipschitz equivalence. The Lie groups characterized are exactly those whose logarithmic coarse structure is equivalent to that of a real hyperbolic space or the complex hyperbolic plane. Finally we point out that a conjecture made by Tyson about the conformal dimensions of the boundaries of certain hyperbolic buildings holds conditionally to the four exponentials conjecture.

2.3.1.
On cone dimension

We have seen that the covering dimension of (moving) cones is an efficient tool to discriminate between the Examples

Beyond geometric models of polynomially growing groups

2.4.
Coarse structures

In the 1930s, Weil abstracted the notion of a uniform structure from the topology of locally compact groups.
Coarse structures are large-scale counterparts of uniform structures; they were introduced by Roe in the 1990s.
We recall below the definition of a coarse space.

Let
X
be a set. The square
X\times X
is a groupoid for the composition law
(x_{0},x_{1})\circ(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{0},x_{2})
and
(x_{0},x_{1})^{-1}=(x_{1},x_{0})
for
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\in X
. For
E,F\subseteq X\times X
, define
E\circ F=\{e\circ f:e\in E,f\in F\}
and
E^{-1}=\{e^{-1}:e\in E\}
.

Definition 2.13

(
[
Roe03
, Definition 2.3]
)

A collection
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}(X\times X)
is called a coarse structure if it contains the diagonal
\Delta_{X\times X}
, is stable by composition, inverse, taking subsets, and taking finite unions; the subsets
E\in\mathcal{E}
are called entourages.

A coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
is called monogenic if it is generated by a single entourage, that is if there exists
E\in\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathcal{E}
is smallest among all coarse structures containing
E
. Note that this notion has no analog among uniform structures.

Definition 2.14

(Coarse equivalence)
.

Given two coarse spaces
(X,\mathcal{E}_{X})
and
(Y,\mathcal{E}_{Y})
and a map
\phi:X\to Y
, we say that
\phi
is coarse if

(
2.14

for all

B\times B\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}\implies\phi^{-1}(B)\times\phi^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
and

(

for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{X}
,
(\phi\times\phi)(E)\in\mathcal{E}_{Y}
, where
\phi\times\phi(x,y)=(\phi(x),\phi(y))
.

A pair of coarse maps
\{\phi:X\to Y
,
\psi:Y\to X\}
realizes a coarse equivalence if the graphs of
\phi\circ\psi
and

are both contained in entourages of the coarse structures.

3.1.

The cohomology groups used in this paper are obtained by direct methods (i.e. by somewhat explicit computations of derivative, cocycles and coboundaries).
We summarize them below.

A.1.

\displaystyle=(-Y^{\mu}\wedge X^{\mu}-S^{\ast}\wedge T)\otimes Y_{\alpha}-X^{\alpha}\wedge T^{\ast}\otimes T

Proof.

The whole computation being of little interest, let us explain in detail only how one computes
d_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}
,
d_{\mathbf{C}}Y_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and
d(X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast})
as a sample of the techniques employed. Applying (
3.11
),

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(X_{\mu\ell})

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[X_{\mu},X_{\ell}]+[X_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}X_{\ell}]-[X_{\ell},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}X_{\mu}]

\displaystyle=[X_{\mu},\delta_{\beta\ell}X_{\alpha}]-[X_{\ell},\delta_{\beta\mu}X_{\alpha}]=0;

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(Y_{\mu\ell})

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[Y_{\mu},Y_{\ell}]+[Y_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}Y_{\ell}]-[Y_{\ell},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}Y_{\mu}]=0;

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(X_{\mu}\wedge Y_{\ell})

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[X_{\mu},Y_{\ell}]+[X_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}Y_{\ell}]-[Y_{\ell},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}X_{\mu}]

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\mu\ell}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}T-\delta_{\beta\mu}[Y_{\ell},X_{\alpha}]=\delta_{\alpha\ell}\delta_{\beta\mu}T;

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(X_{\mu}\wedge S)

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[X_{\mu},S]+[X_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}S]-[S,X_{\alpha}^{\beta}X_{\mu}]

\displaystyle=X_{\alpha}^{\beta}X_{\mu}-[S,\delta_{\beta\mu}X_{\alpha}]=\delta_{\beta\mu}(X_{\alpha}-X_{\alpha})=0,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(X_{\mu}\wedge T)

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[X_{\mu},T]+[X_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}T]-[T,X_{\alpha}^{\beta}X_{\mu}]=-[T,\delta_{\beta\mu}X_{\alpha}]=0.

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(Y_{\mu}\wedge S)

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[Y_{\mu},S]+[Y_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}S]-[S,X_{\alpha}^{\beta}Y_{\mu}]=X_{\alpha}^{\beta}Y_{\mu}=0.

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(Y_{\mu}\wedge T)

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[Y_{\mu},T]+[Y_{\mu},X_{\alpha}^{\beta}T]-[T,X_{\alpha}^{\beta}Y_{\mu}]=0;

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}(S\wedge T)

\displaystyle=-X_{\alpha}^{\beta}[S,T]+[S,X_{\alpha}^{\beta}T]-[T,X_{\alpha}^{\beta}S]=-2X_{\alpha}^{\beta}T=0,

which yields the expression of
d_{\mathbf{C}}X_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.
Applying
\widetilde{J}
produces

:

Now for
d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}(X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast})(X_{\mu}\wedge S)
, using the observation that
\ker S^{\ast}=\mathfrak{s}_{1}\oplus s_{2}=[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}]
, we can reduce the number of terms needed for the computations of

is not a factor. The remaining terms are:

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}(X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast})(X_{\mu}\wedge S)

\displaystyle=[X_{\mu},X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast}S]=0;

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}(X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast})(Y_{\mu}\wedge S)

\displaystyle=[Y_{\mu},X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast}S]=[Y_{\mu},X_{\alpha}]=-\delta_{\alpha\mu}T;

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\mathbf{C}}(X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast})(S\wedge T)

\displaystyle=-[T,X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast}S]+[S,X_{\alpha}\otimes S^{\ast}T]=0.

∎

Quasi-streamwise vortices and enhanced dissipation for the incompressible 3D Navier-Stokes equations
In-Jee Jeong, Tsuyoshi Yoneda

Abstract
We consider the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations under the following 2 + 1 2 2+\frac{1}{2} -dimensional situation: vertical vortex blob (quasi-streamwise vortices) being stretched by two-dimensional shear flow. We prove enhanced dissipation induced by such quasi-streamwise vortices.



1
Introduction

One of the most important law in the study of developed turbulence is
Kolmogorov’s
4/5
-law, and in the derivation of this
law, a significant assumption is the zeroth law (see Frisch
[
1
]
for example).
The zeroth law of turbulence states that, in the limit of vanishing viscosity, the rate of kinetic energy dissipation for solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations becomes nonzero.
To formulate this law, let us recall the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations on
\mathbb{T}^{3}:=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{3}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\cdot\nabla u^{\nu}+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u^{\nu}+f,\\
\nabla\cdot u^{\nu}=0,\\
u^{\nu}(t=0)=u^{\nu}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\nu>0
is the viscosity and
u^{\nu}:\mathbb{T}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},p:\mathbb{T}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the velocity and pressure of the fluid, respectively. Here
f:\mathbb{T}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is some external force. Assuming that the solution is sufficiently smooth, taking the dot product of the equation with
u^{\nu}
and integrating over the domain
\mathbb{T}^{3}
gives the energy balance:

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\|u^{\nu}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f(t)\cdot u^{\nu}(t)dx-\nu\|\nabla u^{\nu}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(1.2)

The zeroth law then postulates that, under the normalization
\|u_{0}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}=1
, the mean energy dissipation rate does not vanish as
\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}
:

\begin{split}\liminf_{\nu\rightarrow 0}\nu\langle|\nabla u^{\nu}|^{2}\rangle>0,\end{split}

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
usually denotes some
ensemble
or long-time, space averages.
Laboratory experiments and numerical simulations of turbulence both confirm the above zeroth law
[
6
]
.
Keeping this classical physical phenomena in mind, in this paper,
we compare the Navier-Stokes flow and the heat flow with the same initial data, and show that vortex stretching is enhancing the viscosity dissipation. This could be a good example clarifying a mechanism of the actual zeroth law.
Here, by the heat flow, we shall mean the unique
L^{2}
solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{\nu,heat}=\nu\Delta u^{\nu,heat},\\
u^{\nu,heat}(t=0)=u^{\nu}_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

in
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,3/4)
, there is a sequence of smooth initial data
\{u_{0}^{\nu_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with
\nu_{n}=2^{-2n}
and
\|u_{0}^{\nu_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}=1
such that, for the solution
u^{\nu_{n}}
to the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) with zero external force, we have

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}\int_{0}^{\nu_{n}^{2\alpha/3}}\|\nabla u^{\nu_{n}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\geq c

(1.4)

for some absolute constant
c>0
,
whereas for the solution to the heat equation (
1.3
), we have

\nu_{n}\int_{0}^{\nu_{n}^{2\alpha/3}}\|\nabla u^{\nu_{n},heat}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C\nu_{n}^{2\alpha/3}(1+\nu_{n}^{1-2\max\{1/2,\alpha\}}),

(1.5)

with some absolute constant
C>0
, so that the right hand side vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Recalling the energy balance (
1.2
), (
1.4
) implies that at least a fixed portion of the initial
L^{2}
energy for
u^{\nu_{n}}
is lost in the
O(\nu_{n}^{2\alpha/3})
-timescale. Moreover, the comparison between (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) simply tells us that viscosity alone is not enough to obtain such an enhanced dissipation.

Remark 1.2
.

With a different construction, the same authors considered the zeroth law under the
2+\frac{1}{2}
-dimensional Navier-Stokes flow: small-scale horizontal vortex blob being stretched by large-scale, anti-parallel pairs of vertical vortex tubes
[
3
]
. It is inspired by the direct numerical simulations of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations by Goto, Saito and Kawahara
[
2
]
. They have found that sustained turbulence in a periodic box consists of a hierarchy of antiparallel pairs of vortex tubes.

Remark 1.3
.

In this paper, we set a sequence of initial data tending to highly oscillating two-dimensional shear flow, adding oscillating vorticity to the vertical direction.
Let us mention a recent numerical simulation which have inspired this construction of the initial data. Recently, using direct numerical simulations of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations, Motoori and Goto
[
5
]
considered the generation mechanism of turbulent boundary layer. They found that small-scale vortices in the log layer are generated predominantly by the stretching in a strain-rate field at larger scale rather than by the mean-flow stretching. On the other hand, large-scale vortices, namely, vortices as large as the height of the
99\%
boundary layer thickness are stretched and amplified directly by the mean flow (we call “qusi-streamwise vortices”).
In particular large-scale vortices tend to align with the stretching direction of the mean flow, which is inclined at nearly
\pi/4
from the streamwise direction.
We are inspired by the generation mechanism of this quasi-streamwise vortices.

Notations.
We write
A\lesssim B
if there exists some constant
C>0
independent of
\nu
such that
A\leq CB
.
Then, we say
A\approx B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. Finally we write
A\simeq B
if
A/B\rightarrow 1
as
\nu\rightarrow 0
.
We use the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the standard
L^{2}
inner product:

\begin{split}\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f(x)g(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Here
f,g
are real-valued functions. We also have
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle f,f\rangle
.

2
Proof of the main theorem

We consider solutions of the following form

\begin{split}u^{\nu}(t,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=u^{L,\nu}(t,x_{2})+u^{S,\nu}(t,x_{1},x_{2})\end{split}

where
u^{L,\nu}
and
u^{S,\nu}
only have nontrivial first and third components, respectively. Furthermore, the solution depends only on
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. It is well known that these assumptions (so-called
2+\frac{1}{2}
dimensional flow) propagate by the Navier-Stokes equations (cf.
[
4
]
). With some abuse of notation, we shall identify
u^{L,\nu}
and
u^{S,\nu}
with their single nontrivial component, and drop the dependence of the solution on
\nu
. Then, it is not difficult to see that the 3D Navier-Stokes equations reduce to two
scalar
equations

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u^{L}=\nu\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}u^{L}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u^{S}+u^{L}\partial_{x_{1}}u^{S}=\nu(\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{2})u^{S}.\end{split}

2.1
Choice of
u^{L}

We take
u_{0}^{L}
to be a rescaling of
smoothed triangular
wave

\begin{split}u_{0}^{L}(x_{2})=T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}).\end{split}

(2.1)

To be precise, we take
T
to be a
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
-smooth function satisfying

T(z)=z\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\left[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4}\right],

and
T(z)=-T(1/2+z)
. It is not difficult to see that

\begin{split}\|u_{0}^{L}\|_{L^{2}}\approx 1.\end{split}

We may expand
T
in sine series: we have

\begin{split}T(z)=\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}\sin(2(2i-1)\pi z),\end{split}

where
\{a_{i}\}_{i}
satisfies
|a_{i}|\lesssim_{\delta}|i|^{-\delta}
for any
\delta\geq 0
.
Here, it is assumed that
0<\nu\leq 1
is given in a way that
\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is an integer. Then,
u^{L}(t,x_{2})
is given by the solution to the one dimensional heat equation with initial data
u_{0}^{L}
:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u^{L}=\nu\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}u^{L}.\end{split}

From the explicit formula for the initial data in sine series, we have that the solution is

\begin{split}u^{L}(t,x_{2})=\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}\sin\left(2(2i-1)\pi\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}\right)\exp\left(-{4\pi^{2}}(2i-1)^{2}t\right).\end{split}

We shall compare it with

\begin{split}\overline{u}^{L}(t,x_{2}):=u_{0}^{L}(x_{2})\exp\left(-{4\pi^{2}}t\right).\end{split}

Then by direct computation (using
\{a_{i}\}_{i}
is rapidly decaying) we see that

\begin{split}\|u^{L}(t)-\overline{u}^{L}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ct,\end{split}

(2.2)

for
t\geq 0
with
C>0
independent of
\nu>0
. Indeed,

\begin{split}&u^{L}(t,x_{2})-\overline{u}^{L}(t,x_{2})=\\
&\sum_{i\geq 2}a_{i}\sin\left(2(2i-1)\pi\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}\right)\left(\exp\left(-{4\pi^{2}}(2i-1)^{2}t\right)-\exp\left(-{4\pi^{2}}t\right)\right)\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}|u^{L}(t,x_{2})-\overline{u}^{L}(t,x_{2})|\leq C\sum_{i\geq 2}|a_{i}|((2i-1)^{2}-1)t\leq Ct.\end{split}

for any
x_{2}
. The advantage of
\bar{u}^{L}
over
u^{L}
is that
\bar{u}^{L}
is exactly linear in some region of
\mathbb{T}
, which makes it easy to analyze the corresponding transport operator.

Remark 2.1
.

We see that

\|\nabla u^{L,heat}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla u_{0}^{L}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\nu^{-1}.

Thus

\nu\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}\|\nabla u^{L,heat}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\lesssim{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}.

2.2
Choice of
u^{S}

For
\alpha\in(0,3/4)
, we assume for simplicity that
\nu^{-\alpha}
is an integer (otherwise, we can simply replace
\nu^{-\alpha}
with its integer part in the following argument). Then we set

\begin{split}u^{S}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sin(\nu^{-\alpha}x_{1})\phi(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}).\end{split}

(2.3)

Here
\phi\geq 0
is a smooth function supported in
(0,1/4)
with
\phi=1
in
(1/8,3/16)
. Note that

\begin{split}\|u_{0}^{S}\|_{L^{2}}\approx 1.\end{split}

Remark 2.2
.

We see that

\|\nabla u^{S,heat}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla u^{S}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\nu^{-2\max\{1/2,\alpha\}}.

Thus

\nu\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}\|\nabla u^{S,heat}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\lesssim\nu^{1+2\alpha/3-2\max\{1/2,\alpha\}}.

We first consider the linear advection-diffusion equation by rescaled and smoothed triangular wave, possibly with an additional term
f
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\overline{u}^{S}+\overline{u}^{L}\cdot\nabla\overline{u}^{S}=\nu\Delta\overline{u}^{S}+f,\\
\overline{u}^{S}(t=0)=u^{S}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

Explicitly, the transport operator is given by

\begin{split}\overline{u}^{L}\cdot\nabla=e^{-c_{0}t}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\partial_{x_{1}},\quad c_{0}=4\pi^{2}.\end{split}

Let us consider the ansatz

\begin{split}\overline{u}^{S}(t,x_{1},x_{2})=e^{-a(t)}\sin\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\phi(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}).\end{split}

(2.5)

Here,
a(t)
is a continuous function of time which will be determined below.
We shall now see that under an appropriate choice of
a(t)
, (
2.5
) provides an
approximate
solution to (
2.4
) with
f=0
. Let us compute the error.
Taking a time derivative to (
2.5
),

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\overline{u}^{S}=-a^{\prime}(t)\overline{u}^{S}\\
&-e^{-a(t)-c_{0}t}\nu^{-\alpha}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\cos\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\phi(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\overline{u}^{S}+\overline{u}^{L}\cdot\nabla\overline{u}^{S}=-a^{\prime}(t)\overline{u}^{S}.\end{split}

On the other hand, applying spatial derivatives to (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}\partial_{x_{1}x_{1}}\overline{u}^{S}=-\nu^{-2\alpha}\overline{u}^{S},\end{split}

\begin{split}\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{u}^{S}&=e^{-a(t)}\cos\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\times\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \left(-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu^{-\alpha}T^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\right)\phi(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\\
&\qquad+e^{-a(t)}\sin\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}\overline{u}^{S}&=-\overline{u}^{S}\times\left(-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu^{-\alpha}T^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\right)^{2}\\
&\qquad+e^{-a(t)}\cos\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\times\\
&\qquad\ \ \ \ \left(-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}\nu^{-1}\nu^{-\alpha}T^{\prime\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\right)\phi(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\\
&\qquad+2e^{-a(t)}\cos\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\times\\
&\qquad\ \ \ \ \ \left(-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu^{-\alpha}T^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\right)\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\\
&\qquad+e^{-a(t)}\sin\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\nu^{-1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}).\end{split}

Recalling that

\begin{split}T^{\prime}(z)=1\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\left[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4}\right],\end{split}

we have that
T^{\prime}=1
and
T^{\prime\prime}=0
on the support of
\phi
. With these observations,
\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}\overline{u}^{S}
is simply

\begin{split}\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}\overline{u}^{S}&=-\overline{u}^{S}\frac{(1-e^{-c_{0}t})^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\nu^{-1-2\alpha}\\
&\qquad-2\nu^{-\alpha-1}e^{-a(t)}\cos\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\times\\
&\qquad\qquad\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}\phi^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\\
&\qquad+\nu^{-1}e^{-a(t)}\sin\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\phi^{\prime\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}).\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\Delta\overline{u}^{S}=(\partial_{x_{1}x_{1}}+\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}})\overline{u}^{S}=-\left(\nu^{-2\alpha}+\frac{(1-e^{-c_{0}t})^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\nu^{-1-2\alpha}\right)\overline{u}^{S}+g,\end{split}

where

\begin{split}g&=-2\nu^{-\alpha-1}e^{-a(t)}\cos\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\times\\
&\qquad\qquad\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}\phi^{\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})\\
&\qquad+\nu^{-1}e^{-a(t)}\sin\left(\nu^{-\alpha}(x_{1}-\frac{1-e^{-c_{0}t}}{c_{0}}T(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}))\right)\phi^{\prime\prime}(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2}).\end{split}

Hence, defining
a(t)
to be the solution to the ODE

\begin{split}a^{\prime}(t)&=\nu^{1-2\alpha}+\frac{(1-e^{-c_{0}t})^{2}}{c_{0}^{2}}\nu^{-2\alpha},\\
a(0)&=1,\end{split}

we have that (
2.5
) is a solution to

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\overline{u}^{S}+\overline{u}^{L}\cdot\nabla\overline{u}^{S}=\nu\Delta\overline{u}^{S}-\nu g.\end{split}

(2.6)

Remark 2.3
.

Observe that for
O(1)
-small
t>0
, we have

\begin{split}a^{\prime}(t)\simeq\nu^{1-2\alpha}+t^{2}\nu^{-2\alpha}\end{split}

and hence

\begin{split}a(t)\simeq 1+\nu^{1-2\alpha}t+\frac{t^{3}}{3}\nu^{-2\alpha}.\end{split}

This shows that for
\overline{u}^{S}
, loss of energy occurs within an
O(\nu^{\frac{2}{3}\alpha})
-timescale. Here, it is required that

\nu^{1-2\alpha}t\lesssim 1

for
t=\nu^{\frac{2}{3}\alpha}
,
which is equivalent to

\alpha<\frac{3}{4}
.
Indeed, when
0<\alpha<\frac{3}{4}
, we have that
\exp(-a(t))\gtrsim 1
for
0\leq t\leq\nu^{2\alpha/3}
, and a direct computation gives

\nu\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}\|\partial_{x_{2}}\bar{u}^{S}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\gtrsim\nu\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}t^{2}\nu^{-1-2\alpha}dt\approx 1.

(2.7)

2.3
Difference estimate

We consider the solution of

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u^{S}+u^{L}\cdot\nabla u^{S}=\nu\Delta u^{S}\end{split}

(2.8)

with initial data
u^{S}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sin(\nu^{-\alpha}x_{1})\phi(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{2})=\overline{u}^{S}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})
and compare it with
\overline{u}^{S}
from (
2.5
): the difference
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}=\overline{u}^{S}-u^{S}
satisfies the equation

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}+\overline{u}^{L}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{\epsilon}+(u^{L}-\overline{u}^{L})\cdot\nabla u^{S}=\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}-\nu g.\end{split}

(2.9)

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product with
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\langle\nu g,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\rangle-\langle(u^{L}-\overline{u}^{L})\cdot\nabla u^{S},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\rangle\end{split}

We easily estimate

\begin{split}\left|\langle(u^{L}-\overline{u}^{L})\cdot\nabla u^{S},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\rangle\right|&\lesssim\|u^{L}-\overline{u}^{L}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x_{1}}u^{S}\|_{L^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim t\|\partial_{x_{1}}u^{S}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim t\nu^{-\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Moreover, observing that
\|\nu g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1+t\nu^{-\alpha}
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\lesssim(1+t\nu^{-\alpha})\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.10)

Therefore,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1+t\nu^{-\alpha},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t+t^{2}\nu^{-\alpha}.\end{split}

(2.11)

Finally, we have after integrating (
2.10
) in
t\in[0,{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}]
and applying (
2.11
),

\begin{split}\nu\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt&\lesssim\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}\left|\langle\nu g,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\rangle\right|+\left|\langle(u^{L}-\overline{u}^{L})\cdot\nabla u^{S},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\rangle\right|dt\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}}t(1+t\nu^{-\alpha})^{2}dt\lesssim\nu^{4\alpha/3}+\nu^{2\alpha/3}.\end{split}

(2.12)

2.4
Completion of the proof

We are now in a position to complete the proof. Let us briefly note that strictly speaking, we only have
\|u^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\approx 1
while the statement of the Theorem requires
\|u^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. This can be fixed with a simple rescaling of
u^{\nu}
.

By (
2.7
) from Remark
2.3
and (
2.12
),
we have

\begin{split}\nu\int_{0}^{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}\|\nabla u^{\nu}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt&\geq\nu\int_{0}^{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}\|\nabla u^{S,\nu}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\\
&\geq\nu\int_{0}^{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}\|\partial_{x_{2}}u^{S,\nu}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\\
&\gtrsim\nu\int_{0}^{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}\|\partial_{x_{2}}\bar{u}^{S,\nu}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt-\nu\int_{0}^{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}\|\partial_{x_{2}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\\
&\gtrsim 1-{\nu^{2\alpha/3}}.\end{split}

This gives the enhanced dissipation statement (
1.4
). For the heat flow case, combining Remark
2.1
and Remark
2.2
, we immediately obtain the desired estimate.

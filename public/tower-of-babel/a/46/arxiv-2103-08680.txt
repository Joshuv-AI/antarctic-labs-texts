Anti- k T k_{T} jet function at next-to-next-to-leading order Preprint: DESY 21-032
Hao-Yu Liu, Xiaohui Liu, Sven-Olaf Moch

Abstract
Jets constructed via clustering algorithms (e.g., anti- k T k_{T} , soft-drop) have been proposed for many precision measurements, such as the strong coupling α s \alpha_{s} and the nucleon intrinsic dynamics. However, the theoretical accuracy is affected by missing QCD corrections at higher orders for the jet functions in the associated factorization theorems. Their calculation is complicated by the jet clustering procedure. In this work, we propose a method to evaluate jet functions at higher orders in QCD. The calculation involves the phase space sector decomposition with suitable soft subtractions. As a concrete example, we present the quark-jet function using the anti- k T k_{T} algorithm with E-scheme recombination at next-to-next-to-leading order.

III.2.3
Jet algorithm and subtraction terms

In this work, we are always interested in
k_{T}
-type jet algorithms, which involves the comparison of the metrics

\displaystyle\rho_{ij}=\min\left[p_{T,i}^{-2\alpha},p_{T,j}^{-2\alpha}\right]\frac{\Delta R_{ij}^{2}}{R^{2}}\,,\quad\rho_{i}=p_{T,i}^{-2\alpha}\,,

(29)

for some parameter
\alpha
and
\alpha=1
defines the anti-
k_{T}
jet algorithm.

In the small jet radius limit, or in the collinear limit, we can approximate
p_{T,i}=z_{i}\,p_{T}
up to power corrections.
Furthermore, if we demand all three partons to be grouped into the same jet,
we will require in the last stage of the clustering,

\displaystyle\Delta R_{\tilde{ij},k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\eta_{\tilde{ij},k}^{2}+\Delta\phi_{\tilde{ij},k}^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2(\cosh\Delta\eta_{\tilde{ij},k}-\cos\Delta\phi_{\tilde{ij},k})\leq R^{2}\,,

where we have assumed that the partons
i
and
j
are clustered first into the pseudo-particle
\tilde{ij}
,
which then clusters with parton
k
to form the final jet.

Within the E-scheme recombination, the four-momentum of the pseudo particle
\tilde{ij}
is given by
p^{\mu}_{\tilde{ij}}=p^{\mu}_{i}+p^{\mu}_{j}
and thus

\displaystyle 2p_{\tilde{ij}}\cdot p_{k}=2p_{k,T}(m_{\tilde{ij},T}\cosh\Delta\eta_{\tilde{ij},k}-p_{\tilde{ij},T}\cos\Delta\phi_{\tilde{ij},k})\,

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{k,T}\left(\sqrt{p^{2}_{\tilde{ij},T}+s_{ij}}\cosh\Delta\eta_{\tilde{ij},k}-p_{\tilde{ij},T}\cos\Delta\phi_{\tilde{ij},k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{k,T}p_{\tilde{ij},T}(\cosh\Delta\eta_{\tilde{ij},k}-\cos\Delta\phi_{\tilde{ij},k})+p_{k,T}\frac{s_{ij}}{p_{\tilde{ij},T}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(1+{\cal O}\left(s_{ij}/p^{2}_{{\tilde{ij}},T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2p_{T}^{2}(z_{i}+z_{j})z_{k}(\cosh\Delta\eta_{\tilde{ij},k}-\cos\Delta\phi_{\tilde{ij},k})

\displaystyle+\frac{z_{k}z_{i}z_{j}}{(z_{i}+z_{j})}\frac{s_{ij}}{z_{i}z_{j}p_{T}^{2}R^{2}}p_{T}^{2}R^{2}\,.

\displaystyle c_{ijk}\equiv\left(z_{i}\tilde{s}_{ik}+z_{j}\tilde{s}_{jk}\leq z_{i}+z_{j}+\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{z_{i}+z_{j}}\tilde{s}_{ij}\right)\,,

In the earlier history of the clustering, in order to have

\displaystyle\rho_{ij}<\min\left[\rho_{ik}\,,\rho_{jk}\,,\rho_{i}\,,\rho_{j}\,,\rho_{k}\right]\,,

The jet clustering conditions can be expressed as constraints in the phase space integrals. For case I, we have

\displaystyle d\Phi^{\text{I}}_{3}=d\Phi_{3}\,\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<\left(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}}\right)^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{jk}\right)\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<\left(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}}\right)^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{ik}\right)\,

\displaystyle\times{\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<\left(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}}\right)^{2\alpha}\right)}\theta\left(c_{ijk}\right)\theta(z_{i}\leq z_{j}\leq z_{k})\,,

(34)

where

is the three-body phase space given in the previous

is given in Eq. (

\theta
-functions in the first line due to the

\epsilon
in the exponent in Eq. (

), associated with the

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x_{3}\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,x_{2}^{-1-a_{2}\epsilon}x_{3}^{-1-a_{3}\epsilon}\,,

(35)

and

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x_{3}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,x_{2}^{-1-a_{2}\epsilon}x_{3}^{-1-a_{3}\epsilon}\theta(x_{3}^{\alpha}-x_{2})\,.

Apparently we can safely Laurent-expand the integrand
x_{2}^{-1-a_{2}\epsilon}x_{3}^{-1-a_{3}\epsilon}
in

to perform the integration.
This naive expansion is not allowed in
I_{2}
, since the correct power of
\epsilon
for

is not
-a_{3}\epsilon

but
-(a_{3}+a_{2}\alpha)\epsilon
, which can be easily seen by integrating out
x_{2}
. To avoid this complication, we introduce a subtraction term, to remove all possible the
z_{j}\to 0
singularities by taking the
z_{j}\to 0
limit in the clustering condition to find

\displaystyle d\Phi^{\text{I}}_{3,sub.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\Phi_{3}\,\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<z_{j}^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{jk}\right)\,\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{jk}\leq 1\right)\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ik}\leq 1\right)

(37)

\displaystyle\times\theta(z_{i}\leq z_{j}\leq z_{k})\,,

where we have used the fact that as
z_{j}\to 0
,
\tilde{s}_{ij}\to 0
as forced by the
\theta
-functions, and therefore

\tilde{s}_{jk}\to\tilde{s}_{ik}
. With the subtraction, the integral

(d\Phi_{3}^{I}-d\Phi^{I}_{3,sub.})|{\cal M}|^{2}
will be free of the
z_{j}\to 0
poles. The term
d\Phi^{I}_{3,sub.}\,|{\cal M}|^{2}
alone still contains the
z_{j}\to 0
singularity and therefore the potential
\epsilon
fractional power modification due to
\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<z_{j}^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{jk}\right)
. But we will see later that when combined with other subtraction terms introduced soon, all such dangerous
\theta
-functions go away.

We have, for case II and case III, the phase space integrals

\displaystyle d\Phi^{\text{II}}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\Phi_{3}\left[1-\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<\left(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}}\right)^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{ik}\right)\right]\theta(\tilde{s}_{ik}<1)

(38)

\displaystyle\times\theta(\tilde{s}_{ik}\leq\tilde{s}_{jk})\theta\left(c_{ikj}\right)\theta(z_{i}\leq z_{j}\leq z_{k})\,,

and

\displaystyle d\Phi_{3}^{\text{III}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\Phi_{3}\times\left[1-\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<\left(\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}}\right)^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{jk}\right)\right]\theta(\tilde{s}_{jk}<1)

(39)

\displaystyle\times\theta(\tilde{s}_{jk}<\tilde{s}_{ik})\theta\left(c_{jki}\right)\theta(z_{i}\leq z_{j}\leq z_{k})\,,

respectively. Here for later use, we have relabeled subscripts
i
,

and

to stick to the case that
z_{i}\leq z_{j}\leq z_{k}
, but with

,

clustered first in case II and

and

first in case III. This is always
allowed since eventually we sum over all possible orders of
z_{i}
’s and all possible clustering histories. Following the same logic as in case I, we construct the subtraction terms for case II

\displaystyle d\Phi^{\text{II}}_{3,sub.}\,=\,d\Phi_{3}\left[1-\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<z_{j}^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{jk}\right)\right]\theta(\tilde{s}_{ik}\leq\tilde{s}_{jk})

\displaystyle\times\,\theta(\tilde{s}_{ik}<1)\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{jk}<1\right)\theta(z_{i}<z_{j}<z_{k})\,,\qquad

(40)

and case III

\displaystyle d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{\text{III}}\,=\,d\Phi_{3}\left[1-\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ij}<z_{j}^{2\alpha}\tilde{s}_{jk}\right)\right]\theta(\tilde{s}_{jk}<\tilde{s}_{ik})

(41)

respectively, to remove the

singularities.

The phase space integral with the jet algorithm implemented reads

\displaystyle d\Phi_{\rm alg.}|{\cal M}|^{2}=\sum_{i=\text{I}}^{\text{III}}\left(d\Phi_{3}^{i}-d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}\right)|{\cal M}|^{2}+\,d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}|{\cal M}|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 21.52771pt+\left(\text{all $z_{i}$, $z_{j}$, $z_{k}$ orders}\right)\,,

(42)

and we note that by construction, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sum_{i={\text{I}}}^{\text{III}}d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}=d\Phi_{3}\theta(\tilde{s}_{jk}<1)\theta\left(\tilde{s}_{ik}<1\right)\,\theta(z_{i}<z_{j}<z_{k})\,,

where all
\theta
-function constraints involving powers of
z_{j}^{\alpha}
cancel in the sum.
Therefore, the phase space integration does not induce any modifications
of the fractional powers in
\epsilon
as
z_{j}\to 0
.

However at this stage, the phase space parameterization in Eq. (
III.2.2
) is still not sufficient to isolate all singularities within
|{\cal M}|^{2}
. Particularly in
\sum_{i={\text{I}}}^{\text{III}}d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}
, a further ordering between the variables
x_{2}
and
x_{4}
is required to fully disentangle the poles generated by

s_{ijk}=z_{i}z_{k}\tilde{s}_{ik}+z_{j}z_{k}\tilde{s}_{jk}+z_{i}z_{j}\tilde{s}_{ij}\to 0
.
To avoid such an ordering, we introduce instead a subtraction term in the matrix element
to regulate the single soft limit, as
x_{4}\to 0
, of
|{\cal M}|^{2}
.
Thus, a natural choice for the subtraction term
|{\cal M}|_{sub.}^{2}
is

\displaystyle|{\cal M}|_{sub.}^{2}=\lim_{x_{4}\to 0}|{\cal M}|^{2}=T_{j}\cdot T_{k}\,\frac{s_{jk}}{s_{ij}s_{ik}}P^{(0)}_{a\to jk}\,,

(44)

which is nothing but the product of the LO eikonal factor and the LO splitting kernel.

The final phase space integration suitable for a numerical evaluation is thus given by

\displaystyle d\Phi_{\rm alg.}|{\cal M}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=\text{I}}^{\text{III}}d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}|{\cal M}|_{sub.}^{2}

(45)

\displaystyle+\left(d\Phi_{3}^{i}-d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}\right)|{\cal M}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\,\sum_{i=\text{I}}^{\text{III}}d\Phi_{3,sub.}^{i}(|{\cal M}|^{2}-|{\cal M}|_{sub.}^{2})

\displaystyle+\left(\text{all $z_{i}$, $z_{j}$, $z_{k}$ orders}\right)\,.

Similar subtraction counter terms can be constructed for the WTA scheme recombination as well as other jet algorithms such as the Cambridge-Aachen algorithm and thus used to compute the soft-drop groomed jet function. Further applications to obtain the semi-inclusive jet function
Kang:2016mcy
;
Dai:2016hzf
at
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
are also straightforward.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we have developed a method to calculate the jet functions, which
arise in the factorization of cross sections with exclusive jets of small radius
R
,
with a jet clustering procedure up to NNLO.
Explicit results at NNLO have been presented, for the first time, for the
quark-jet function with the anti-
k_{T}
jet algorithm.
The new two-loop contribution to the anti-
k_{T}
quark-jet function provides the
missing input for the evaluation of the factorization theorem in
Eq. (
1
) to NNLO and for resummation beyond NLL accuracy.
This will substantially improve the precision of predictions for
observables with jets as hard probes.
The computational framework is not limited to anti-
k_{T}
E-scheme jets
but readily applicable to many other jet observables up to NNLO,
such as the semi-inclusive jet function, the WTA jet and the soft-drop groomed
jet radius
R_{g}
and the jet momentum fraction
z_{g}
. In addition, the obtained two-loop quark-jet function will serve as an important building block
when using the factorization in Eq. (
1
) for the development of a colorful
q_{T}
-subtraction scheme,
which is applicable to both, calculations at fixed order in perturbation theory up to NNLO and to parton shower simulations.

In summary, given the broad interest in jets from both the high-energy particle and the nuclear communities,
we expect the results to have numerous phenomenological applications at
colliders, such as the LHC and a future EIC for the study of
jet cross sections, TMDs and in addressing the spin structure of the nucleon.

Appendix B
The leading
\epsilon
-poles and large logarithms from the RGE

Up to non-global contributions, the jet function
J
satisfies the RGE,

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}J}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=\left(2\Gamma[\alpha_{s}]L+\gamma_{J_{q}}\right)J\,,

(69)

where
\Gamma
is the cusp anomalous dimension, cf. e.g.
Moch:2004pa
,

\displaystyle\Gamma=\sum_{n=0}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{n+1}\Gamma_{n}\,,\quad\quad\mbox{with}\quad\quad\Gamma_{0}=4C_{F}\,,\quad\quad\Gamma_{1}=4C_{F}\left[\left(\frac{67}{9}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\right)C_{A}-\frac{20}{9}T_{F}N_{F}\right]\,,

(70)

and
\gamma_{J_{q}}
is the anomalous dimension of the quark-jet,

\displaystyle\gamma_{J_{q}}=\sum_{n=0}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{n+1}\gamma_{n}\,,\quad\quad\mbox{with}\quad\quad\gamma_{0}=6C_{F}\,.

(71)

Using the fact that
\mathrm{d}\log\mu=\beta^{-1}[\alpha_{s}]\,\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}
, with
\beta[\alpha_{s}]=-2\alpha_{s}\sum_{n=0}\beta_{n}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{n+1}
,
the RGE can be solved to find

\displaystyle J(\mu)=J(\mu=p_{T}R)\exp\left[2\int_{\alpha_{s}(p_{T}R)}^{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}\frac{\Gamma[\alpha_{s}]}{\beta[\alpha_{s}]}\int_{\alpha_{s}(p_{T}R)}^{\alpha_{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}}{\beta[\alpha_{s}^{\prime}]}\,+\int_{\alpha_{s}(p_{T}R)}^{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}\frac{\gamma_{J_{q}}[\alpha_{s}]}{\beta[\alpha_{s}]}\right]\,.

(72)

Expanding the solution to

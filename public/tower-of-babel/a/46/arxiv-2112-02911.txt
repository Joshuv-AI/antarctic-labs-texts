Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group with a subgroup
H
which satisfies

H<N_{G}(H)<N_{G}(N_{G}(H))\unlhd G\mbox{ and }|N_{G}(N_{G}(H))|=p^{3}

(1)

where
p

In this article we focus on association schemes axiomatizing
some properties derived from the orbitals of the action of
G
on

.

We shall recall some facts on coherent configurations obtained from a permutation group.
Let
G
be a permutation group of a finite set
\Omega
.
Then
G
acts on
\Omega\times\Omega
by its entry-wise action, i.e.,

(\alpha,\beta)^{x}:=(\alpha^{x},\beta^{x})\mbox{ for $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega$ and $x\in G$.}

We denote the set of orbits of the action of
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
by
\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)
and
\mathrm{Inv}(G)=(\Omega,\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G))
, which satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

The diagonal relation
1_{\Omega}
is a union of elements of
\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)
;

(ii)

For each
s\in

\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)
we have
s^{\ast}\in

\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)

where
s^{\ast}:=\{(\alpha,\beta)\mid(\beta,\alpha)\in s\}
;

(iii)

For all
s,t,u\in

\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)
we have
\sigma_{s}\sigma_{t}=\sum_{u\in S}c_{st}^{u}\sigma_{u}

for
c_{st}^{u}\in\mathbb{N}
uniquely determined by
s,t,u

where
\sigma_{u}

is the adjacency matrix of
u
,
i.e.,
(\sigma_{u})_{\alpha,\beta}=1
if
(\alpha,\beta)\in u
and
(\sigma_{u})_{\alpha,\beta}=0
if
(\alpha,\beta)\not\in u
.

A
coherent configuration
is a pair
(\Omega,S)
of a finite set
\Omega

and a partition
S
of
\Omega\times\Omega

which satisfies the conditions obtained from the above by replacing
\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)
by
S
.
We say that a coherent configuration
(\Omega,S)
is
schurian
if
S=

\mathrm{Orb}{{}_{2}}(G)
for some permutation group
G
of
\Omega
, and it is
homogeneous
or
an
association scheme
if
1_{\Omega}\in S
(see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
for its background).

Suppose that
G
has a subgroup
H
which satisfies (
1
).
Then
|H|=p
,
|N_{G}(H)|=p^{2}
and for each
g\in G
we have the following:

(i)

|HgH|/|H|\in\{1,p\}
and
|N_{G}(H)gN_{G}(H)|/|N_{G}(H)|\in\{1,p\}

(ii)

|HgH|/|H|=1

;

(iii)

|N_{G}(H)gN_{G}(H)|/|N_{G}(H)|=1

;

(iv)

N_{G}(N_{G}(H))
is the smallest normal subgroup of

containing

Since
G
acts faithfully and transitively on the set of right cosets of

in

(\Omega,S)

where

(ii)

(iii)

(see Section 2,

The following is our main result:

Theorem 1.1

Let
(\Omega,S)
be an association scheme with
{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S)<{\mathbf{O}}^{\mathrm{\theta}}(S)

such that
n_{s}\in\{1,p\}
for each
s\in S

n_{{\mathbf{O}}^{\mathrm{\theta}}(S)}=p^{2}

is a prime.
Then
|\Omega|\leq p^{2}(p+1)
.

In
[
5
]
one can find a criterion on association schemes
whose thin residue

induces the subschemes isomorphic to either

C_{p^{2}}
,
C_{p}\times C_{p}
or
C_{p}\wr C_{p}
.

Here we denote
(G,\mathrm{Inv}(G))
by
G
when
G
acts on itself by its right multiplication and
we denote the wreath product of one scheme
(\Delta,U)
by another scheme
(\Gamma,V)
by
(\Delta,U)\wr(\Gamma,V)
, i.e.,

(\Delta,U)\wr(\Gamma,V):=(\Delta\times\Gamma,\{1_{\Gamma}\otimes u\mid u\in U\}\cup\{v\otimes U\mid v\in V\setminus\{1_{\Gamma}\}\})

where

1_{\Gamma}\otimes u:=\{((\delta_{1},\gamma),(\delta_{2},\gamma))\mid(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\in u,\gamma\in\Gamma\}

v\otimes U:=\{((\delta_{1},\gamma_{1}),(\delta_{2},\gamma_{2}))\mid\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\Delta,(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in v\}.

For the case of

is schurian.
For the case of

under the assumption that

for each

.
For the case of

we had no progression for the last five years.

In
[
7

all association schemes of degree 27 are
classified by computational enumeration, and there are three pairs of

These examples had given an impression that we need some complicated combinatorial argument to enumerate

with

Throughout this section, we assume that
(\Omega,S)

[
10
]

\Omega

. Furthermore,

For all

.
It is easily verified that

Now we define the
complex product
on the power set of

For all subsets
T
and

TU:=\{s\in S\mid c_{tu}^{s}>0\>\>\mbox{for some $t\in T$ and $u\in U$}\}

The singleton

The following equations are frequently used without any mention:

Lemma 2.4

Let

be a coherent configuration. Then for all

(i)

;

|\{t\in S\mid t\in rs\}|\leq\mathsf{gcd}(n_{r},n_{s})
.

For

be an association scheme and

.

Proof of the main theorem

Let
(\Omega,S)
be a coherent configuration whose distinct fibers are
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\ldots,\Omega_{m}
.
For all integers

with
1\leq i,j\leq m
we set

S_{ij}:=S_{\Omega_{i},\Omega_{j}}\mbox{ and $S_{i}:=S_{ii}$.}

Throughout this section we assume that

(\Omega_{i},S_{i})\simeq C_{p}\wr C_{p}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
where
p
is a prime and

C_{p}\wr C_{p}
is the unique up to isomorphism non-thin
p
-scheme of degree
p^{2}
.

For
s\in S
we say that
s
is
regular
if
ss^{\ast}s=\{s\}
and
we denote by
R
the set of regular elements in
S

[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.5

For each regular element
s\in S_{ij}
with
n_{s}=p
we have

\sigma_{s}\sigma_{s^{\ast}}=p(\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})}\sigma_{t})
and
\sigma_{s^{\ast}}\sigma_{s}=p(\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{j})}\sigma_{t})
.

In particular,
ss^{\ast}={\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})
and
s^{\ast}s={\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{j})
.

Proof 3.1

Notice that
\{1_{\Omega_{i}}\}\subsetneq ss^{\ast}\subset S_{i}
and
ts=\{s\}
for each
t\in ss^{\ast}
.
Since
\{t\in S_{i}\mid ts=\{s\}\}
is a closed subset of valency at most
n_{s}
, it follows from
(\Omega_{i},S_{i})\simeq C_{p}\wr C_{p}
that

ss^{\ast}={\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})
, and hence for each
t\in ss^{\ast}
we have
n_{t}=1
and

c_{ss^{\ast}}^{t}=c_{st}^{s}n_{s^{\ast}}/n_{t^{\ast}}=p.

This implies that
\sigma_{s}\sigma_{s^{\ast}}=p(\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})}\sigma_{t})
.
By symmetry,
\sigma_{s^{\ast}}\sigma_{s}=p(\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{j})}\sigma_{t})
.

Lemma 3.6

For each non-regular element
s\in S_{ij}
with
n_{s}=p
we have

\sigma_{s}\sigma_{s^{\ast}}=p\sigma_{1_{\Omega_{i}}}+\sum_{u\in S_{i}\setminus{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})}\sigma_{u}
and

\sigma_{s^{\ast}}\sigma_{s}=p\sigma_{1_{\Omega_{j}}}+\sum_{u\in S_{j}\setminus{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{j})}\sigma_{u}
.

Proof 3.2

Notice that
\{t\in S_{i}\mid ts=\{s\}\}=\{1_{\Omega_{i}}\}
, otherwise,
s
is regular or
n_{s}=p^{2}
, a contradiction.
This implies that the singletons
ts
with
t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})
are distinct elements of valency
p
.
Since

p^{2}=|\Omega_{j}|=\sum_{s\in S_{ij}}n_{s}\geq\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})}n_{ts}=p+p+\cdots+p=p^{2},

it follows that
{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})s=S_{ij}
.

We claim that
S_{i}\setminus{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})\subseteq ss^{\ast}
.
Let
u\in S_{i}\setminus{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})
.
Then there exists
t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})
such that
u\in tss^{\ast}
since
u\in S_{ij}s^{\ast}={\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})ss^{\ast}
.
This implies that
u=t^{\ast}u\subseteq t^{\ast}(tss^{\ast})=ss^{\ast}
.

By the claim with
p^{2}=n_{s}n_{s^{\ast}}=\sum_{t\in S_{i}}c_{ss^{\ast}t}n_{t}

and
c_{ss^{\ast}1_{\Omega_{i}}}=n_{s}=p
we have the first statement,
and the second statement is obtained by symmetry.

For the remainder of this section we assume that
n_{s}=p
for each
s\in\bigcup_{i\neq j}S_{ij}
.

Lemma 3.7

The set
\bigcup_{s\in R}s
is an equivalence relation on
\Omega
.

Proof 3.3

Since
1_{\Omega_{i}}\in S_{i}\subseteq R
for
i=1,2\ldots,m
,
\bigcup_{s\in R}s
is reflexive.
Since
ss^{\ast}s=\{s\}
is equivalent to
s^{\ast}ss^{\ast}=\{s^{\ast}\}
,
\bigcup_{s\in R}s
is symmetric.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega_{i}
,
\beta\in\Omega_{j}
and
\gamma\in\Omega_{k}
with
r(\alpha,\beta),r(\beta,\gamma)\in R
.
Then we have

r(\alpha,\gamma)r(\alpha,\gamma)^{\ast}\subseteq r(\alpha,\beta)r(\beta,\gamma)r(\beta,\gamma)^{\ast}r(\alpha,\beta)^{\ast}.

If one of
r(\alpha,\beta)
,
r(\beta,\gamma)
is thin, then
r(\alpha,\gamma)r(\alpha,\gamma)^{\ast}\subseteq{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})
, and hence
r(\alpha,\gamma)\in R
. Now we assume that both of them are non-thin.
Since
r(\beta,\gamma)r(\beta,\gamma)^{\ast}={\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{j})=r(\alpha,\beta)^{\ast}r(\alpha,\beta)
, it follows that

r(\alpha,\gamma)r(\alpha,\gamma)^{\ast}\subseteq r(\alpha,\beta)r(\alpha,\beta)^{\ast}={\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i}).

Applying Lemma
3.5
and
3.6
we obtain that
r(\alpha,\gamma)
is regular, and hence

\bigcup_{s\in R}s
is transitive.

Lemma 3.8

The set
\bigcup_{s\in N}s
is an equivalence relation on
\Omega

where
N:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}\cup(S\setminus R)
.

Proof 3.4

Since
1_{\Omega_{i}}\in S_{i}\subseteq N
for
i=1,2\ldots,m
,
\bigcup_{s\in N}s
is reflexive.
By Lemma
3.7
,
\bigcup_{s\in R}
is symmetric, so that
\bigcup_{s\in N}s
is symmetric.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega_{i}
,
\beta\in\Omega_{j}
and
\gamma\in\Omega_{k}
with
r(\alpha,\beta),r(\beta,\gamma)\in N
.
Since
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}\subseteq R
, it follows from Lemma
3.7

that it suffices to show that

r(\alpha,\gamma)\in S\setminus R

under the assumption
that

r(\alpha,\beta),r(\beta,\gamma)\in S\setminus R
with
i\neq k
.

Suppose the contrary, i.e.,
r(\alpha,\gamma)\in R
.
Then, by Lemma
3.7
,
S_{ik}\subseteq R
.
Since

r(\alpha,\beta)r(\beta,\gamma)\subseteq S_{ik}\subseteq R,

it follows that

{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})r(\alpha,\beta)r(\beta,\gamma)=r(\alpha,\beta)r(\beta,\gamma).

On the other hand, we have

{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{i})r(\alpha,\beta)r(\beta,\gamma)=S_{ij}r(\beta,\gamma)=S_{ik}.

Thus,
r(\alpha,\beta)r(\beta,\gamma)=S_{ik}
.
Since
i\neq k
, each element of
S_{ik}
has valency
p
, and hence,

\sigma_{s_{1}}\sigma_{s_{2}}=\sum_{u\in S_{ik}}\sigma_{u}

where
s_{1}:=r(\alpha,\beta)
and
s_{2}:=r(\beta,\gamma)
.
By Lemma
3.6
,

p^{2}=\langle{\sigma_{s_{1}}\sigma_{s_{2}},\sigma_{s_{1}}\sigma_{s_{2}}}\rangle=\langle{\sigma_{s_{1}}^{\ast}\sigma_{s_{1}},\sigma_{s_{2}}\sigma_{s_{2}}^{\ast}}\rangle=p^{2}+p(p-1),

a contradiction where
\langle{\>\>,\>\>}\rangle
is the inner product defined by

\langle{A,B}\rangle:=1/p^{2}\mathrm{tr}(AB^{\ast})\>\>\mbox{for all $A,B\in M_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})$}.

Therefore,
\bigcup_{s\in N}s
is transitive.

Lemma 3.9

We have either
R=S
or
N=S
.

Proof 3.5

Suppose
R\neq S
.
Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
with
r(\alpha,\beta)\in R
.
Since
R\neq S
, there exists
\gamma\in\Omega
with
r(\alpha,\gamma)\in N
.
Notice that
r(\beta,\gamma)\in R\cup N
.
By Lemma
3.7
,
r(\beta,\gamma)\in N
, and hence, by Lemma

,

r(\alpha,\beta)\in R\cap N=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}.

Since
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
are arbitrarily taken, it follows that

R=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}
and

.

Lemma 3.10

Suppose that
S=N
and
s_{1}\in S_{ij}
,
s_{2}\in S_{jk}
and
s_{3}\in S_{ik}

\sigma_{s_{1}}\sigma_{s_{2}}=\sigma_{s_{3}}(\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{k})}a_{t}\sigma_{t})

\sum_{t\in{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{k})}a_{t}^{2}=2p-1

Proof 3.6

Since
s_{1}s_{2}\subseteq S_{ij}=s_{3}{\mathbf{O}}_{\mathrm{\theta}}(S_{k})

for some non-negative integers

.
Since

Expanding

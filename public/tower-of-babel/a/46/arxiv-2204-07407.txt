A new entanglement measure based dual entropy
Xue Yang, Yan-Han Yang, Li-Ming Zhao, Ming-Xing Luo

Abstract
Quantum entropy is an important measure for describing the uncertainty of a quantum state, more uncertainty in subsystems implies stronger quantum entanglement between subsystems. Our goal in this work is to quantify bipartite entanglement using both von Neumann entropy and its complementary dual. We first propose a type of dual entropy from Shannon entropy. We define S t S^{t} -entropy entanglement based on von Neumann entropy and its complementary dual. This implies an analytic formula for two-qubit systems. We show that the monogamy properties of the S t S^{t} -entropy entanglement and the entanglement of formation are inequivalent for high-dimensional systems. We finally prove a new type of entanglement polygon inequality in terms of S t S^{t} -entropy entanglement for quantum entangled networks. These results show new features of multipartite entanglement in quantum information processing.

II
The total entropy of von Neumann entropy and its complementary dual

Consider a discrete random variable
X
on sample set
\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\}
. Its probability distribution is given by
p_{i}=Pr(X=x_{i})
. This implies a self-information about each event
x_{i}
as
I(X=x_{i})=-\log_{2}p_{i}
. The average information of the random variable
X
is then known as Shannon entropy
Shannon
defined by

\displaystyle H(X)=-\sum^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}\log_{2}p_{i}.

(4)

This gives the uncertainty which has before the statistical experiment. Now, for a given event
x_{i}
one may define a new binomial random variable
X_{i}\sim\{p_{i},1-p_{i}\}
, that is, by regarding all the events except for
x_{i}
as a complementary dual event of
x_{i}
. In this case, we get its self-information as
-\log_{2}(1-p_{i})
. This follows a new binary entropy as

\displaystyle H(X_{i})=-p_{i}\log_{2}p_{i}-(1-p_{i})\log_{2}(1-p_{i})

(5)

for
i=1,\cdots,n
. Moreover, all the complementary dual information are framed into an entropy function as

\displaystyle\overline{H}(X)=-\sum^{n}_{i=1}(1-p_{i})\log_{2}(1-p_{i}).

(6)

This is firstly introduced as “extropy”, a complementary dual of Shannon entropy
Lad2015
.

In classical information theory, the intuitive link between the uncertainty and information is that the greater the uncertainty of the given distribution, the more information gain from learning the outcome of the experiment. This means the Shannon entropy only presents the information all we know concerning which event will occur while ignores the information of its complement. However, the extropy (
6
) can make up for this weakness. Thus, we redefine the entropy of the random variable
X
as

\displaystyle H^{t}(X)=\sum^{n}_{i=1}H(X_{i})=H(X)+\overline{H}(X).

(7)

Example 1
. Consider a random variable
X
with probability distribution
p_{i}=Pr(X=x_{i})
associated with
X=x_{i},i=1,2,3
, where
0\leq p_{i}\leq 1
and
\sum^{3}_{i=1}p_{i}=1
. The Shannon entropy is given by

\displaystyle H(X)=-\sum^{3}_{i=1}p_{i}\log_{2}p_{i},

(8)

while the total entropy is shown as

\displaystyle H^{t}(X)=-\sum^{3}_{i=1}p_{i}\log_{2}p_{i}-(1-p_{i})\log_{2}(1-p_{i}).

(9)

As its illustrated in Fig.
1
, the total entropy
H^{t}(X)
shows more uncertainty beyond Shannon entropy.

In quantum scenarios, inspired by the von Neumann entropy we can define the quantum total entropy for a given quantum state.

Definition 1

The total entropy of a quantum state
\rho
on
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
{\cal H}
is defined by

\displaystyle S^{t}(\rho)=-{\rm{Tr}}[\rho\log_{2}\rho+(\mathbbm{1}-\rho)\log_{2}(\mathbbm{1}-\rho)],

(10)

where
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity matrix.

For a given state
\rho
, the spectrum decomposition is given by
\rho=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
, where
\lambda_{i}
are eigenvalues associated the orthogonal eigenvectors
|\psi_{i}\rangle
. With this decomposition the
S^{t}
-entropy is rewritten into

\displaystyle S^{t}(\rho)=-\sum^{d}_{i=1}(\lambda_{i}\log_{2}\lambda_{i}+(1-\lambda_{i})\log_{2}(1-\lambda_{i})).

(11)

This states that the
S^{t}
-entropy of a density matrix
\rho
is equal to the classical
S^{t}
-entropy of the probability distribution
\vec{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{d})
from the its eigenvalues.

. Consider a

-qubit Heisenberg model with a random magnetic field in the

\displaystyle+0.2\vec{\sigma}_{3}\cdot{}\vec{\sigma}_{6}+0.6\vec{\sigma}_{5}\cdot{}\vec{\sigma}_{6}+\sum_{i=1}^{6}h_{j}\sigma_{j}^{z},

Intuitively, the von Neumann entropy of a bipartite state

\{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle\}

. Now, for each measurement outcome

For a quantum system described by a density operator
\rho
, a general entropy function

may be defined as

\displaystyle S_{f}(\rho)={\rm Tr}f(\rho)=\sum_{i}f(\lambda_{i}),

(14)

where

is a smooth concave function satisfying

,
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of

. This includes the von Neumann entropy with

\displaystyle f(\lambda)=-\lambda\ln\lambda.

(15)

While the Tsallis entropy

\displaystyle f(\lambda)=\frac{(\lambda-\lambda^{q})}{q-1},

(16)

where

and

. Instead, the present

\displaystyle f(\lambda)=-\lambda\ln\lambda-(1-\lambda)\ln(1-\lambda).

(17)

This allows us construct one-parameter total entropy based on the Tsallis entropy and its complementary dual (see Appendix B).

Next, we show that the present
S^{t}
-entropy satisfies the following properties.

Lemma 1

Let
\rho_{AB}
be any density operator of a composite system
AB
, and
\varrho_{A(B)}
be the reduced density operator of the subsystem
A(B)
. The total entropy
S^{t}
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

Non-negativity
: The total entropy satisfies
0\leq S^{t}(\rho)\leq d\log_{2}d-(d-1)\log_{2}(d-1)
.

(ii)

Concavity
: For any convex combination
\rho=\sum^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}\rho_{i}
, the total entropy satisfies

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}S^{t}(\rho_{i})\leq S^{t}(\rho).

(18)

(iii)

Symmetry
: For any pure state
\rho_{AB}
the total entropy satisfies
S^{t}(\varrho_{A})=S^{t}(\varrho_{B})
.

(iv)

Invariance
: The total entropy is invariant under any unitary transformation, that is,
S^{t}(\rho)=S^{t}(U\rho U^{\dagger})
for any
U\in\mathbb{SU}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
.

(v)

Subadditivity
: For any product state
\rho=\varrho_{A}\otimes\varrho_{B}
, the total entropy satisfies

\displaystyle S^{t}(\rho)<S^{t}(\varrho_{A})+S^{t}(\varrho_{B})

(19)

and

\displaystyle S^{t}(\rho)\geq\max\{S^{t}(\varrho_{A}),S^{t}(\varrho_{B})\}.

(20)

(vi)

Boundedness
: For any convex combination
\rho=\sum^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}\rho_{i}
, the total entropy satisfies

\displaystyle S^{t}(\rho)\leq H^{t}(X),

(21)

where
X
is the classical random variable with distribution
\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\}
.

The proof of Lemma 1 is shown in Appendix A.

The
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement

Let
{\cal S}^{AB}={\cal S}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
be the set of density matrices acting on Hilbert space

. A function

(E1)

E(\rho)=0
if and only if

is a separable state;

(E2)

Invariance under local unitary transformations:
E(\rho)=E((U_{A}\otimes U_{B})\rho(U^{{\dagger}}_{A}\otimes U^{{\dagger}}_{B}))
for any local unitary transformations
U_{A}\in\mathbb{SU}(\mathcal{H}_{A}),U_{B}\in\mathbb{SU}(\mathcal{H}_{B})

(E3)

Non-increasing on average under local operations and classical communication (LOCC) operations that is,
\sum_{j}p_{j}E(\sigma_{j})\leq E(\rho)
, where
\Lambda^{LOCC}
is an arbitrary LOCC operation defined by
\Lambda^{LOCC}(\rho)=\sum_{j}p_{j}\sigma_{j}
.

(E4)

Convexity:
E(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}E(\rho_{i})
for any
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}
, where
\{p_{i}\}
is a probability distribution, and
\rho_{i}
’s are density matrices.

-entropy entanglement from the total quantum entropy.

III.1
The
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement

For a pure state
|\Phi\rangle_{AB}
on
d\times d
dimensional Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, the
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement is defined as

\displaystyle E_{t}(|\Phi\rangle_{AB})=\frac{1}{r}S^{t}(\varrho_{A}),

(22)

where

is a normalized factor and
\varrho_{A}={\rm Tr}_{B}(|\Phi\rangle_{AB}\langle\Phi|)
denotes the reduced density operator of the subsystem
A
.

For a bipartite mixed state
\rho_{AB}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, the
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement is given via the convex-roof extension as

\displaystyle E_{t}(\rho_{AB})=\inf_{\{p_{i},|\Phi_{i}\rangle\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{t}(|\Phi_{i}\rangle_{AB}),

(23)

where
E_{t}(|\Phi_{i}\rangle_{AB})=S^{t}(\varrho^{i}_{A})
is the
S^{t}
-entropy of the subsystem
A
for the pure state
\rho^{i}_{AB}=|\Phi_{i}\rangle_{AB}\langle\Phi_{i}|
, and the infimum is taken over all the possible pure-state decompositions of
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|
with
p_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
. The quantity
E_{t}(\rho)
has defined an entanglement measure for any bipartite state.

Theorem 1

For an arbitrary finite-dimensional quantum state
\rho
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, the quantity
E_{t}
is a bipartite entanglement measure.

Proof
. For any density matrix
\rho
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
, it follows that
E_{t}(\rho)\geq 0
from the non-negativity in Lemma 1 and Eq.(
23
), herein, the equality holds if and only if
\rho
is separable. This has proved the condition (E1).

Note that the quantum
S^{t}
-entropy is invariant under local unitary transformations from Lemma 1, this follows the condition (E2).

The concavity of
S^{t}
-entropy in Lemma 1 assures the monotonicity under average local operations and classical communication (LOCC). Here, we firstly prove the monotonicity for pure states, i.e., when
\rho
and
\sigma_{j}
are all pure states. From the definition of the quantum
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement in Eq.(
22
), we have

\displaystyle E_{t}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{t}(\rho^{A})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{t}(\sum_{j}p_{j}\sigma^{A}_{j})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}p_{j}S^{t}(\sigma^{A}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}p_{j}E_{t}(\sigma_{j}),

(25)

where the inequality (
24
) is from the concavity of the quantum
S^{t}
-entropy in Lemma 1. The definition of the
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement in Eq.(
22
) implies Eq.(
25
). According to Ref.
Vidal2000
, the monotonicity for mixed states can be inherited from the monotonicity of pure states via the convex roof extension. This has proved the condition (E3).

Finally, the convexity (E4) is followed directly from the fact that all the entanglement measures constructed via the convex roof extension are convex

.

III.2
Analytic formula for two-qubit states

In this subsection, we provide an analytic formula of the
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement for two-qubit systems. For any bipartite pure state
|\Phi\rangle_{AB}
on Hilbert space

\displaystyle C(|\Phi\rangle_{AB})=\sqrt{2(1-{\rm Tr}(\varrho^{2}_{A}))},

For a mixed state
\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle C(\rho_{AB})=\inf_{\{p_{i},|\Phi_{i}\rangle\}}\sum_{i}p_{i}C(|\Phi_{i}\rangle_{AB}),

where the infimum takes over all the possible pure-state decompositions of

Interestingly, for a two-qubit mixed state

\displaystyle C(\rho)=\max\{0,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}\},

VI
Conclusion

Entanglement entropy exhibits qualitatively different behaviours from that of classical entropy, and provides a valuable tool for describing the features of many-body states. More importantly, the entanglement entropy measures how a subsystem is entangled with other systems in a given system. In an operational manner, the degree of entanglement in a pure many-body state is quantified by the entanglement entropy of its subsystems. However, until now, most of the entanglement entropies ignore the information of its complement of subsystems. Our consideration in this paper is to propose a new measure from non Neumann entropy and its complement entropy to characterize entanglement. This kind of entanglement shows new features beyond previous entanglement measures. So far, we have considered some special entanglement. A natural problem is to investigate the monogamy relationship for general entanglements, specially for high-dimensional systems.

In conclusion, we proposed the total entropy of the von Neumann entropy and its complementary dual which not only reveals the information of one subsystem but also its complementary statistics. We defined the bipartite
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement based on the total entropy and provided the analytic formula in two-qubit systems. We also considered the monogamy property of qubit systems and higher-dimensional systems in terms of the
S^{t}
-entropy entanglement. We obtained a polygon monogamy inequality for arbitrary quantum networks consisting of bipartite entangled pure states. This reveals an intriguing feature of high-dimensional entanglement going beyond any qubit entanglement. These results are interesting in quantum entanglement theory.

(iii) Suppose a pure state

(v) From Ref.

(vi) Consider a given mixed state

Appendix B
The measure based the total entropy of Tsallis entropy and its complementary dual

For the probability distribution
\textbf{p}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}\}
of random variable
X
, the Tsallis entropy
Tsallis1988
is defined as

\displaystyle T_{q}({\textbf{p}})=-\sum^{n}_{i=1}p_{i}\ln_{q}p_{i}

(98)

with one parameter
q
as an extension of shannon entropy. This relation gives the arithmetic mean of the information
-\ln_{q}p_{i}
, with its corresponding probability
p_{i}
, where
q
logarithm function is defined by

\displaystyle\ln_{q}(x)=\frac{1-x^{1-q}}{q-1}

(99)

for any negative real numbers
x
and
q
. The complementary dual of the Tsallis entropy is defined as

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{q}({\textbf{p}})=-\sum^{n}_{i=1}(1-p_{i})\ln_{q}(1-p_{i}).

(100)

Similar with Eq.(
7
), we define the total entropy of Tsallis entropy and its complementary dual as

\displaystyle T^{t}_{q}({\textbf{p}})=\sum^{n}_{i=1}T_{q}(p_{i},1-p_{i}),

(101)

where
T_{q}(p_{i},1-p_{i})
are the sum of the Tsallis entropy and its complementary dual of a discrete random variable
X
with a probability distribution
\{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}\}
.
T^{t}_{q}
is also viewed as entropy of the binomial random variable
X_{i}\sim\{p_{i},1-p_{i}\}
with a probability distribution
\{p_{i},1-p_{i}\}
.

The quantum entropy of a density operator equals the classical entropy of
the probability vector formed by its eigenvalues, thus we give the following definition.

Definition 2

The total entropy of the Tsallis entropy and its complementary dual of a quantum state
\rho
on
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
{\cal H}
is defined by

\displaystyle T^{t}_{q}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-{\rm Tr}\rho^{q}-{\rm Tr}({\mathbbm{1}-\rho})^{q}+{\rm Tr}({\mathbbm{1}-\rho)}}{q-1},

(102)

which corresponds to with the form (
14

\displaystyle f(p)=\frac{(p-p^{q})+1-p-(1-p)^{q}}{(q-1)}.

(103)

Here, the function

satisfying
f(0)=f(1)=0

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(p)=-q[(1-x)^{q-2}+p^{q-2}]<0

(104)

Mirror symmetry for Nahm branes
Emilio Franco, Marcos Jardim

Abstract




Abstract.
The Dirac–Higgs bundle is a hyperholomorphic bundle over the moduli
space of stable Higgs bundles of coprime rank and degree. We provide
an algebraic generalization to the case of trivial degree and the
rank higher than
1
. This allow us to generalize to this case the
Nahm transform defined by Frejlich and the second named author,
which, out of a stable Higgs bundle, produces a vector bundle with
connection over the moduli space of rank
1
Higgs bundles. By
performing the higher rank Nahm transform we obtain a
hyperholomorphic bundle with connection over the moduli space of
stable Higgs bundles of rank
n
and degree
0
, twisted by the
gerbe of liftings of the projective universal bundle.

Such hyperholomorphic vector bundles over the moduli space of stable
Higgs bundles can be seen, in the physicist’s language, as

\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes twisted by the above mentioned gerbe. We refer to these
objects as Nahm branes. Finally, we study the behaviour of Nahm branes
under Fourier–Mukai transform over the smooth locus of the Hitchin
fibration, checking that the resulting objects are supported on a
Lagrangian multisection of the Hitchin fibration, so they describe
partial data of
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-branes.

Keywords.
Higgs bundles, mirror symmetry, Fourier–Mukai transform,
Nahm transform

1. Introduction

1.1. Context

The Nahm transform was originally described as a geometrical correspondence between solutions of the self-duality Yang–Mills equations (also known as instantons) in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
which are invariant under dual groups of translations
[
ADHM78
,
Nah82
,
Nah84
,
Hit83
,
CG84
,
BvB89
,
Nak93
,
Jar02
]
. In
[
Jar04
]
, the second-named author reviewed the Nahm transform in several situations and gave an interpretation as a nonlinear version of the Fourier transform which, given a family of self-dual connections over a spin four-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, produces a vector bundle with connection over the parametrizing space of the family. Such bundle is constructed by considering, at each point of the parametrizing space, the cokernel of the associated Dirac operator. The connection is hyperkähler whenever both varieties, the base manifold and the parametrizing space, are hyperkähler.

The study of instantons that are invariant under translations in two directions led Hitchin to introduce Higgs bundles in
[
Hit87a
]
as solutions of the dimensional reduction to a Riemann surface of the self-dual connections in
4
dimensions. It turns out that the moduli space
\mathrm{M}
of Higgs bundles has a very rich geometry; in particular, it can be constructed as a hyperkähler quotient in the context of gauge theory
[
Hit87a
,
Sim94
,
Sim95
,
Don87
,
Cor88
]
inheriting a hyperkähler structure. When the rank and the degree are coprime, all semistable Higgs bundles are stable, and Hausel
[
Hau98
]
showed that a universal bundle exists. In degree
0
, however, there is no universal bundle over the stable locus
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
or even over any other Zariski open set of the moduli space (see Ramanan
[
Ram73
]
and Drezet and Narasimhan
[
DN89
]
for a proof in the case of vector bundles that extends naturally to Higgs bundles). There exists nonetheless a local universal bundle in the étale topology of the moduli space of stable Higgs bundles
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
, as indicated by Simpson
[
Sim95
]
.

Another important feature of the moduli space
\mathrm{M}
of stable Higgs bundles with coprime rank and degree is the existence of the so called
Dirac–Higgs bundle
, originally considered by Hitchin and studied in detail by Hausel in
[
Hau98
]
. Later, Blaavand
[
Bla15
]
extended the construction of the Dirac–Higgs bundle to the moduli space of parabolic Higgs bundles.

One way to describe the Dirac–Higgs bundle is as the hyperholomorphic bundle on
\mathrm{M}
obtained via the Nahm transform, as defined in
[
FJ08
]
, associated to the universal bundle. To be more precise, the Nahm transform of a Higgs bundle is defined by considering the index bundle associated to the family obtained by twisting the original Higgs bundle with the universal family of rank 1 Higgs bundles. This transform underlies the Fourier–Mukai transform for Higgs bundles defined by Bonsdorff
[
Bon06
]
, which also equipped it with an autodual connection
[
Bon10
]
.

Another interesting feature of the moduli space
\mathrm{M}
of Higgs bundles is that it admits a fibration
\mathrm{M}\to B
over a vector space, becoming an algebraically completely integrable system
[
Hit87b
]
which is known as the
Hitchin system
. The notion of Higgs bundles generalizes naturally to any structure group
G
. It was shown in
[
HT03
,
DG02
,
DP12
]
that Hitchin systems for Langlands dual structure groups,
G
and
G^{L}
, are dual, satisfying thereby the requirements of being
Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (SYZ) mirror partners

[
SYZ96
]
, which allows for the identification of T-duality with mirror symmetry between them. Since the group
G=\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
is Langlands self-dual, we obtain a self-dual Hitchin system in this case, which is the one that we study in this paper.

The rich geometry of the moduli space of Higgs bundles
\mathrm{M}
makes it an object of interest for theoretical physics. In
[
BJSV95
,
HMS95
]
it was shown that the dimensional reduction of an
N=4
Super Yang–Mills theory in
4
dimensions gives a
2
dimensional sigma model with hyperkähler target
\mathrm{M}
, and, hence, S-duality in the former becomes T-duality (mirror symmetry) in the latter. This was the starting point for the ground-breaking article by Kapustin and Witten
[
KW07
]
(see also
[
Wit18
]
), where they relate the Geometric Langlands Conjecture and S-duality in the original
N=4
super Yang–Mills theory. Following Kapustin and Witten, a
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-brane is a pair consisting of a hyperkähler submanifold and a hyperholomorphic vector bundle. Similarly, a
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-brane is given by a submanifold which is complex Lagrangian with respect to the first Kähler structure, and a flat vector bundle. In String Theory, branes are geometrical objects that encode the Dirichlet boundary conditions, and mirror symmetry
[
KW07
,
Wit18
]
predicts a 1-1 correspondence between
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes on the moduli space of
G
-Higgs bundles and
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-branes on its
G^{L}
counterpart.

Motivated by this context, many authors have studied branes in moduli spaces of Higgs bundles, see for instance
[
Hit16
,
Hit17
,
Gai18
,
BS14
,
BS16
,
BS19
,
BG15
,
HS18
,
BCFG19
,
Bra17
,
FP17
,
FGOP
,
HMP
]
and
[
AFES18
]
for a survey on this topic, and the geometry of these objects has been intensively described
[
BS16
,
BGH16
,
BGH19
,
GW20
,
Bai18
,
Bai20
,
CFF19
,
Sch12
]
. More generally, due to their intrinsic geometric interest, one can also study hyperkähler and complex Lagrangian submanifolds on other classes of hyperkähler manifolds, like quiver varieties
[
FJM17
,
HS19
]
, and moduli spaces of stable sheaves on symplectic surfaces
[
FJM19
]
.

1.2. Our constructions

In this paper we generalize the constructions of the Dirac–Higgs bundle and the Nahm transform of a stable Higgs bundle to the case of trivial degree and rank higher than
1
. This provides a class of (space filling)
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes that we transform under Fourier–Mukai, obtaining a partial description of the mirror dual
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-brane.

In the case of coprime rank and degree, the universal Higgs bundle plays a central role in the construction of the Dirac--Higgs bundle. In our case, however, there is no universal Higgs bundle at hand, not even locally. To surpass this obstacle, we consider the gerbe of liftings of the projective universal bundle
1
1

1

We are grateful to the anonymous referee for suggesting this approach.
, and we introduce the notions of sheaves twisted and shifted by such gerbe. We can then construct the Dirac–Higgs bundle as a vector bundle twisted by our gerbe, showing that it is equipped with a hyperholomorphic connection. The techniques used in the construction of the Dirac–Higgs bundle can then be applied to define the Nahm transform of a stable Higgs bundle which is, again, a bundle twisted by our gerbe and equipped with a hyperholomorphic connection. This constitutes a family of
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes (one for each stable Higgs bundle) which we call
Nahm branes
.

The second step is to study the behaviour of these Nahm branes under mirror symmetry. We work over the smooth locus of the Hitchin fibration, where mirror symmetry is expected to be realized via Fourier–Mukai transform. We check that the transformed sheaf is supported on a complex Lagrangian multisection of the Hitchin fibration. This is part of the data of a
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-brane, providing evidence for the existence of a correspondence between
\mathrm{(BBB)}
and
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-branes conjectured by Kapustin and Witten.

1.3. Organization of the paper

In Sections
2.1
and
2.2
we review the properties of the theory of Higgs bundles and the Hitchin system. In Section
2.3
we survey, after restricting ourselves to the smooth locus of the Hitchin fibration, the Fourier–Mukai transform, which is expected to realize mirror symmetry in this context. In Section
2.4
we review gerbes and the notions of sheaves twisted by a gerbe, we also describe an example of gerbe over the moduli space of Higgs bundles of particular importance for us, the gerbe of liftings of the projective universal bundle. In Section
2.5
we review the Dirac–Higgs bundle in general, and we provide an algebraic construction over the moduli space of Higgs bundles with
0
degree as a bundle twisted by the gerbe of liftings. In Section
3
we study the behaviour of spectral data of Higgs bundles under tensorization, crucial for understanding our generalization of the Nahm transform of a stable Higgs bundle to rank higher than
1
, which we achieve in Section
4
, thereby constructing Nahm
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes. In Section
5.1
we adapt the Fourier–Mukai transform for sheaves twisted by a gerbe, in order to study in Sections
5.2
and
5.3
, respectively, the transform of the Dirac–Higgs bundle and of the Nahm brane associated to any stable Higgs bundle.

2. Geometry of the Hitchin system

2.1. Non-abelian Hodge theory

In this section we introduce the moduli space of Higgs bundles, an object with an extremely rich geometry. In particular, it is equipped with a hyperkähler structure.

Let us consider a smooth projective curve
X
over
\mathbb{C}
of genus
g\geq 2
. Denote by
\mathbb{E}
the unique up to isomorphism
C^{\infty}
-bundle of rank
n
over
X
and consider a Hermitian metric
h
on it. Denote by
\mathcal{G}
the Gauge group of unitary automorphism of
\mathbb{E}
preserving the metric, and its complexification,
\mathcal{G}{C}
, parametrizing all automorphisms of
\mathbb{E}
. Recall that
\mathbb{E}
equipped with a Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}_{E}
gives rise to a holomorphic vector bundle
E
. Out of
\overline{\partial}_{E}
and the metric
h
, one can construct
\partial_{E}
and
\partial_{E}+\overline{\partial}_{E}
is a unitary connection on
E
.

A
Higgs pair
of rank
n
on
X
is pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
where
\overline{\partial}_{E}
is a Dolbeault operator on
\mathbb{E}
fixing an integrable complex structure on it, and
\varphi
is an element of
{}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))
. Note that the space
\mathcal{A}
of Higgs pairs is an affine space modeled in the infinite dimensional vector space
{}_{X}^{0,1}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))\oplus{}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))
.

A
Higgs bundle
over
X
is a Higgs pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
satisfying
\overline{\partial}_{E}\,\varphi=0
(hence
\partial_{E}\,\varphi^{*}=0
, where
\varphi^{*}
is the adjoint of
\varphi
with respect to the metric
h
). Equivalently, a Higgs bundle is a pair
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
, where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
, and
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes K_{X})
is a holomorphic section of the endomorphisms bundle, twisted by the canonical bundle
K_{X}
. Let us write
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
for the
\mathcal{G}{C}
-invariant subset parametrizing Higgs bundles.

Recall from
[
Hit87a
,
Sim94
,
Nit91
]
that a Higgs bundle
\mathcal{E}
is said to be
(semi)stable
if every proper, non-trivial,
\varphi
-invariant sub-bundle
F\subset E
satisfies

\frac{\deg F}{\mathrm{rk}F}<\penalty\ (\leq)\penalty\ \frac{\deg E}{\mathrm{rk}E}.

In addition,
\mathcal{E}
is
polystable
if it is a direct sum of semistable bundles
\mathcal{E}_{i}=(E_{i},\varphi_{i})
, all with the same slope
\deg E_{i}/\mathrm{rk}E_{i}
. It is possible to construct
[
Hit87a
,
Sim94
,
Sim95
,
Nit91
]
the moduli space,

\mathrm{M}=\mathcal{B}\big/\hskip-3.44444pt\big/\mathcal{G}{C},

of rank
n
and degree
0
semistable Higgs bundles on
X
. The locus of stable Higgs bundles
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}\subset\mathrm{M}
is a dense open subset identified with the quotient under
\mathcal{G}{C}
of
\mathcal{B}^{\,\mathrm{st}}\subset\mathcal{B}
, the set of stable Higgs bundles. When we need to specify the rank
n
we shall write
\mathrm{M}_{n}
and
\mathrm{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{st}}
.

Non-abelian Hodge theory establishes the existence of a homeomorphism
[
Hit87a
,
Sim94
,
Sim95
,
Don87
,
Cor88
]
between
\mathrm{M}
and the moduli space of flat connections of rank
n
. This is a consequence of the construction of these moduli spaces as a hyperkähler quotient of the space of Higgs pairs
\mathcal{A}
by the gauge group
\mathcal{G}{C}
of complex automorphisms of
\mathbb{E}
. Tangent to any Higgs pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
we can consider its infinitesimal deformations
\dot{\alpha}\,d\overline{z}\in{}_{X}^{0,1}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))
and
\dot{\varphi}\,dz\in{}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))
, with
z
being a holomorphic coordinate of the base curve. Also, we consider
\dot{\alpha}^{*}\,dz\in{}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))
and
\dot{\varphi}^{*}\,d\overline{z}\in{}_{X}^{0,1}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{E}))
to be infinitesimal deformations of
\partial_{E}
and
\varphi^{*}
respectively. The hyperkähler structure on
\mathcal{A}
is given by the following metric on this space,

(2.1)

\widetilde{g}\left((\dot{\alpha}_{1},\dot{\varphi}_{1}),(\dot{\alpha}_{2},\dot{\varphi}_{2})\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\dot{\alpha}_{1}^{*}\dot{\alpha}_{2}+\dot{\alpha}_{2}^{*}\dot{\alpha}_{1}+\dot{\varphi}_{1}\dot{\varphi}_{2}^{*}+\dot{\varphi}_{2}\dot{\varphi}_{1}^{*}\right)dz\wedge d\overline{z},

and the complex structures
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{1}
,

\widetilde{\Gamma}^{2}
, and
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{3}=\widetilde{\Gamma}^{1}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{2}
. We denote by

\widetilde{\omega}^{j}(\cdot,\cdot)=\widetilde{g}(\cdot,\widetilde{\Gamma}^{j}(\cdot))

the associated Kähler forms and consider the symplectic
forms
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{j}=\widetilde{\omega}^{j+1}+\,\mathrm{i}\,\widetilde{\omega}^{j-1}
which are holomorphic for the corresponding

\widetilde{\Gamma}^{j}
.

From each of the Kähler forms
\widetilde{\omega}^{j}
one can construct a moment map
\mu^{j}
. The space of Higgs bundles
\mathcal{B}
can be identified with
(\mu^{2})^{-1}(0)\cap(\mu^{3})^{-1}(0)
inside
\mathcal{A}
. Then, after an infinite-dimensional version of Kempf–Ness theorem,
\mathrm{M}
can be identified with the hyperkähler quotient

\mathrm{M}\cong(\mu^{1})^{-1}(0)\cap(\mu^{2})^{-1}(0)\cap(\mu^{3})^{-1}(0)\big/\mathcal{G}.

The
\mathcal{G}
-invariant complex structures
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{j}
descend naturally to complex structures
\Gamma^{j}
on
\mathrm{M}
. Also, the
2
-forms
\widetilde{\omega}^{j}
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{j}
are
\mathcal{G}
-invariant, so they provide naturally the Kähler forms
\omega^{j}
on
\mathrm{M}
, and the holomorphic symplectic forms
\Lambda^{j}
.

Given a Higgs bundle
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
, consider the complex

C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathrm{End}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[\cdot,\varphi]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes K_{X},

which induces the following exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}^{0}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathrm{End}(E))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes K_{X})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathrm{End}(E))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes K_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}^{2}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[\cdot,\varphi]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\cdot,\varphi]}

where
\mathbb{H}^{p}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
are the hypercohomology groups for the complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is stable,
\mathbb{H}^{0}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})\cong\mathbb{H}^{2}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})\cong\mathbb{C}
so
\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
has fixed dimension
2n^{2}(g-1)+2
and, furthermore, it can be identified with the tangent space
T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}=\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
. Then,
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
is smooth, and since
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}\subset\mathrm{M}
is a dense open subset, one has that

\dim\mathrm{M}=2n^{2}(g-1)+2.

Thanks to Serre duality,
\varphi\in H^{0}(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes K_{X})
can also be regarded as an element of the dual space
H^{1}(\mathrm{End}(E))^{*}
; one can define a 1-form
\theta
on
\mathrm{M}^{\rm st}
as the composition of the map
\eta:\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})\to H^{1}(\mathrm{End}(E))
and the pairing with
\varphi
,
i.e.

\theta(v)=\langle\varphi,\eta(v)\rangle
, for each
v\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
. Hence,
d\theta
is proportional to the holomorphic symplectic form
\Lambda^{1}
, obtained from
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{1}
.

2.2. The Hitchin fibration

Besides its hyperkähler nature, the moduli space of Higgs bundles is equipped with the structure of an algebraically completely integrable system by means of the spectral correspondence that we revise
[
Hit87b
,
BNR89
,
Sim95
]
in this section.

Let
(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
be a basis of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-invariant polynomials with
\deg(q_{i})=i
. The
Hitchin fibration
is the dominant morphism

\begin{array}[]{cccc}h:&\mathrm{M}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}&B:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{\otimes i})\\
&(E,\varphi)&\longmapsto&\left(q_{1}(\varphi),\dots,q_{n}(\varphi)\right),\end{array}

and we refer to
B
as the
Hitchin base
.
Consider the total space
\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
of the canonical bundle, and the obvious algebraic surjection
p:\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\to X
; let
\lambda
be the tautological section of the pullback bundle
p^{*}K_{X}\to\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
. Given an element
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in B
we construct the associated
spectral curve

S_{b}\subset\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
by considering the vanishing locus of the section

\lambda^{n}+p^{*}b_{1}\lambda^{n-1}+\dots+p^{*}b_{n-1}\lambda+p^{*}b_{n}\in H^{0}(X,p^{*}K_{X}^{\otimes n}).

Restricting
p
to
S_{b}
yields a finite morphism
p_{b}:S_{b}\to X
of degree
n
.

One can further consider the pull-back of
p^{*}K_{X}^{\otimes n}
to the product
\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\times B
, which is naturally equipped with a section obtained by pull-back of
\lambda
. Seeing the
b_{i}
as coordinates in
B
, one obtains a second section of our bundle, whose vanishing locus provides naturally a family of spectral curves
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\times B
for which we naturally have that
\mathcal{S}\cap(\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\times\{b\})=S_{b}
. Restricting the projection
p\times\mathbf{1}_{B}:\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\times B\to X\times B
, we obtain a finite morphism of degree
n
:

(2.2)

p:\mathcal{S}\to X\times B.

For every
b\in B
, the corresponding spectral curve
S_{b}
belongs to the linear system
|nX|
, and, by Bertini’s theorem, it is generically smooth and irreducible. Furthermore, since the canonical divisor of the symplectic surface
\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
is zero, the genus of
S_{b}
is given by

(2.3)

d:=g\left(S_{b}\right)=1+n^{2}(g-1).

Thanks to Riemann–Roch theorem,
p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S_{b}}
is a degree

-(n^{2}-n)(g-1)
vector bundle of rank
n
. This motivates the notation

\delta:=(n^{2}-n)(g-1).

Following
[
BNR89
]
, we consider the push-forward

(2.4)

E_{L}:=p_{*}L

of a torsion free sheaf
L
on
S_{b}
of rank
1
and degree
\delta
, which is a vector bundle on
X
of rank
n
and degree
\delta+\deg(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S_{b}})=0
. We consider as well the multiplication by the restriction to
S_{b}
of tautological section,
\lambda_{b}:\mathcal{O}_{S_{b}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S_{b}}\otimes p^{*}K_{X}
. Note that this induces the following twisted endomorphism of
L

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\psi_{b}:&L&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}&L\otimes p^{*}K_{X}\\
&s&\longmapsto&s\otimes\lambda_{b}.\end{array}

whose push-forward returns the Higgs field

(2.5)

\varphi=p_{*}\psi_{b}:E_{L}\to E_{L}\otimes K_{X},

so that
b=h(\varphi)
. Thanks to the spectral correspondence
[
Hit87b
,
BNR89
,
Sim95
,
Sch98
,
dCat17
]
, each Hitchin fibre is identified with the compactified Jacobian of the corresponding spectral curve

h^{-1}\left(b\right)\cong\overline{\Jac}^{\,\delta}(S_{b}).

Fixing a point
x_{0}\in X
in our curve, we construct a smooth section
\hat{\sigma}:B\to\overline{\Jac}^{\,\delta}_{B}(S)
by considering for every
b
the line bundle
p_{b}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(x_{0})^{\otimes(n-1)(g-1)}
on
S_{b}
. Such choice induces the following identification

(2.6)

\overline{\Jac}^{\,\delta}_{B}(\mathcal{S})\cong\overline{\Jac}^{\,0}_{B}(\mathcal{S}).

2.3. Fourier–Mukai transform and mirror symmetry for the Hitchin system

A very active field of research is the occurrence of mirror symmetry phenomena between Higgs moduli spaces of pairs of Langlands dual groups. In the so-called semi-classical limit, mirror symmetry is expected to be realised via a Fourier–Mukai transform relative to the Hitchin fibration. In this section we review this correspondence within the framework of the moduli space of Higgs bundles for
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
, which is Langlands self-dual. Hence, the mirror of
\mathrm{M}
is (conjecturally) itself. Even if the Fourier–Mukai transform extends out of the locus of smooth Hitchin fibres
[
Ari13
,
MRV19a
,
MRV19b
]
, we restrict here to the original construction of Mukai over (families of) abelian varieties. We do so because the locus of smooth Hitchin fibres is dense in the Higgs moduli space, hence the study of the duality there is enough for our purposes.

Let us denote by

Note that all the points of

is contained in the stable locus,

By the autoduality of smooth relative jacobians, we know that

We will study mirror symmetry in the sense of Strominger–Yau–Zaslow

has a section

, it is well known (see
[

\mathcal{P}\to\Jac_{B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\times_{B^{\prime}}\Jac_{B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})^{\vee}.

With it, we can consider the relative Fourier–Mukai transforms

(2.9)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}R\check{\mathscr{F}}:&D^{b}_{B^{\prime}}(\Jac^{\delta}_{B^{\prime}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}))\cong D^{b}_{B^{\prime}}(\mathrm{M}^{\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}&D^{b}_{B^{\prime}}(\Jac^{\delta}_{V}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})^{\vee})\cong D^{b}_{B^{\prime}}(\mathrm{M}^{\prime})\\

After
[
Muk81
]

R\hat{\mathscr{F}}\circ R\check{\mathscr{F}}=[d]\circ\left(\mathrm{1}_{\Jac}^{-1}\right)^{*},

where
\mathrm{1}_{\Jac}^{-1}
denotes the involution given by inverting elements on each
\Jac^{\delta}(S_{b})
under the group structure (recall (

)), and

is defined in (
2.3
).

We say that a sheaf

on

is

-WIT
(after
Weak Index Theorem
) if its image under

is a complex supported in degree

. Let us denote by

the category of
\ell
-WIT sheaves. It follows from (

) that the Fourier–Mukai transform
R\hat{\mathscr{F}}
induces an equivalence of categories

R\hat{\mathscr{F}}:\mathrm{Wit}_{\ell}\left(\Jac_{B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Wit}_{d-\ell}\left(\Jac_{B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\right)

with inverse
R\check{\mathscr{F}}
.

2.4. Flat unitary gerbes and the universal bundle

When the rank and the degree are coprime, the moduli space of stable Higgs bundles is fine and the universal bundle plays an important role in the construction of the Dirac–Higgs bundle (see Section
2.5
below). In our case, with the degree being trivial, there is no universal bundle at hand, not even over the stable locus or any other Zariski open subset of the moduli space (this was proven for vector bundles by Ramanan
[
Ram73
]
and reproved by Drezet and Narasimhan
[
DN89
]
but a similar discussion holds for Higgs bundles). The best we have at hand is a local universal bundle in the étale topology constructed by Simpson
[
Sim95
]
.

Since our universal bundle only exists locally, we need to introduce gerbes. We refer to
[
Hit01
]
for a nice introduction to gerbes. Given an algebraic variety
Y
, denote by
\mathrm{Tors}(\mathbf{U}(1),Y)
the group of
\mathbf{U}(1)
-torsors over
Y
,
i.e.
the group of flat unitary line bundles on
Y
. A
flat unitary gerbe in the étale topology
on
Y
is a sheaf of categories in the étale topology over
Y
such that its restriction to each étale open subset
Y^{\prime}\to Y
is a torsor for the group
\mathrm{Tors}(\mathrm{U}(1),Y^{\prime})
. Given an étale covering
\{Y_{i}\to Y\}_{i\in I}
, a gerbe provides a category (a groupoid indeed) for every
Y_{i}
, the natural transformations of these categories in the intersections
Y_{ij}:=Y_{i}\times_{Y}Y_{j}
are realized via tensoring by flat unitary line bundles
L_{ij}\to Y_{ij}
. Therefore, a gerbe defines a set of flat unitary line bundles over the intersections
\{L_{ij}\to Y_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
such that
L_{ij}\cong L_{ji}^{-1}
and over the triple intersections
Y_{ijk}:=Y_{i}\times_{Y}Y_{j}\times_{Y}Y_{k}
, one has that
\zeta_{ij}^{*}L_{ij}\otimes\zeta_{jk}^{*}L_{jk}\otimes\zeta_{ki}^{*}L_{ki}
is isomorphic to the trivial bundle on
Y_{ijk}
, being
\zeta_{ij}
the obvious projection
Y_{ijk}\to Y_{ij}
.

If
\beta
is a flat unitary gerbe in the étale topology and
\{Y_{i}\to Y\}_{i\in I}
is an étale cover of
Y
, a
\beta
-twisted sheaf

\mathbf{F}
is a set of sheaves
\{F_{i}\to Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that on each intersection
\zeta_{i}^{*}F_{i}\cong L_{ij}\otimes\zeta_{j}^{*}F_{j}
, where
\zeta_{i}
denotes the projection
Y_{i}\times_{Y}Y_{j}\to Y_{i}
. In this case, we also say that the cover
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is
fine enough
for
\mathbf{F}
. A
\beta
-twisted sheaf
\mathbf{F}
is a
\beta
-twisted vector bundle
of rank
n
if all the
F_{i}
are locally free sheaves of rank
n
. We say that
\boldsymbol{\nabla}=\{\nabla_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a connection on the
\beta
-twisted vector bundle
\mathbf{F}
if
\nabla_{i}
is a connection on each of the
F_{i}
satisfying the compatibility relations
\zeta_{i}^{*}\nabla_{i}\cong\mathrm{1}_{L_{ij}}\otimes\zeta_{j}^{*}\nabla_{j}+\nabla_{ij}\otimes\mathrm{1}_{\zeta_{j}^{*}F_{j}}
, where
\nabla_{ij}
is the flat unitary connection naturally defined on
L_{ij}
via the flat unitary gerbe
\beta
. Given two
\beta
-twisted sheaves
\mathbf{F}_{1}=\{F_{1,i}\to Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\mathbf{F}_{2}=\{F_{2,i}\to Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
that are fine enough for the same étale covering, a morphism
\boldsymbol{\psi}:\mathbf{F}_{1}\to\mathbf{F}_{2}
of
\beta
-twisted sheaves is a collection of morphisms of sheaves
\{\psi_{i}:F_{1,i}\to F_{2,i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfying
\zeta_{i}^{*}\psi_{i}\cong\mathrm{1}_{L_{ij}}\otimes\zeta_{j}^{*}\psi_{j}
for any
i,j\in I
. In a similar way, the notions of quotient of sheaves, complex of sheaves and cohomology generalize naturally to the context of
\beta
-twisted sheaves. If
f:Z\to Y
is a morphism of algebraic varieties,
\beta
a flat unitary gerbe in the étale topology over
Y
and
\mathbf{F}
a
\beta
-twisted sheaf on
Y
, we define
f^{*}\mathbf{F}
to be
\{f_{i}^{*}F_{i}\to Y_{i}\times_{Y}Z\}
, where
f_{i}:Y_{i}\times_{Y}Z\to Y_{i}
is the projection to the first factor and we note that
\{Y_{i}\times_{Y}Z\to Z\}_{i\in I}
is an étale covering. Similarly, for a
f^{*}\beta
-twisted sheaf
\mathbf{F}
on
Z
with a fine enough étale covering of the form
\{Y_{i}\times_{Y}Z\to Z\}_{i\in I}
, we set
f_{*}\mathbf{F}:=\{f_{i,*}F_{i}\to Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Thanks to the projection formula and base-change theorems,
f_{*}\mathbf{F}
is a
\beta
-twisted sheaf as

\displaystyle(\zeta_{i}\times_{Y}\mathrm{1}_{Z})^{*}\left(f_{i,*}F_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\cong f_{ij,*}\left(\zeta_{i}^{*}F_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\cong f_{ij,*}\left(L_{ij}\otimes\zeta_{j}^{*}F_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\cong f_{ij,*}\left(f_{ij}^{*}L_{ij}\otimes\zeta_{j}^{*}F_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\cong L_{ij}\otimes f_{ij,*}\zeta_{j}^{*}F_{j}

\displaystyle\cong L_{ij}\otimes(\zeta_{j}\times_{Y}\mathrm{1}_{Z})^{*}f_{i,*}F_{j},

where
f_{ij}
denotes the projection
Y_{ij}\times_{Y}Z\to Y_{ij}
.

Given a
\beta
-twisted sheaf
\mathbf{F}_{1}
and vector bundle
F_{2}
over
Y
, we define
\mathbf{F}_{1}\otimes F_{2}
to be the
\beta
-twisted sheaf
\{F_{1,i}\otimes y_{i}^{*}F_{2}\to Y_{i}\}_{i\in I}
where we denote by
y_{i}
the étale maps
Y_{i}\to Y
.

As we said at the beginning of this section, Simpson
[
Sim95
, Theorem 4.7 (4)]
ensures the existence of local universal Higgs bundles
\left(U_{i},\Phi_{i}\right)\to X\times Z_{i}
for a certain étale covering
\{Z_{i}\to\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}\}_{i\in I}
of the stable locus. By universality, there exists a line bundle over each intersection
L_{ij}\to Z_{i}\times_{Y}Z_{j}
satisfying
U_{i}\cong\pi_{ij}^{*}L_{ij}\otimes U_{j}
, where
\pi_{ij}
is the obvious projection
X\times Z_{i}\times_{Y}Z_{j}\to Z_{i}\times_{Y}Z_{j}
. This defines a flat unitary gerbe in the étale topology over
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
that we denote by
\beta
for the rest of the paper. After having set our gerbe
\beta
, observe that
\mathbf{U}=\{U_{i}\to X\times Z_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a
\pi_{\mathrm{M}}^{*}\beta
-twisted vector bundle over
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
, where
\pi_{\mathrm{M}}:X\times\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}\to\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
is the obvious projection. In view of this, we refer to

\left(\mathbf{U},\boldsymbol{\Phi}\right)=\left\{\left(U_{i},\Phi_{i}\right)\to X\times Z_{i}\right\}_{i\in I},

as the
universal
\pi_{\mathrm{M}}^{*}\beta
-twisted Higgs bundle
. This object will be crucial in our description of the Dirac Higgs bundle which we shall address in the next subsection.

We finish this section adapting Kapustin–Witten’s definition of a
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-brane
[
KW07
]
to this context. Suppose that
Y
is equipped with a hyperkähler structure, we say that
(\mathbf{F},\boldsymbol{\nabla})
is a
space filling
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-brane
if
\mathbf{F}
is a
\beta
-twisted vector bundle and
\boldsymbol{\nabla}
is a connection which is hyperholomorphic,
i.e.
is
(1,1)
with respect to the three complex structures of
Y
.

2.5. The Dirac–Higgs bundle

Hitchin (see
[
Hit02
]
for instance) constructed the Dirac–Higgs bundle over the moduli space of Higgs bundle with coprime rank and degree, showing that it can be equipped with a hyperholomorphic structure. The Dirac–Higgs bundle was used by Hausel
[
Hau98
]
to study the cohomology of this moduli space. Blaavand
[
Bla15
]
studied this object over the moduli space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
, showing that it exists only as a differential object. In a local sense, Hitchin’s proof of the existence of a hyperholomorphic structure on the Dirac–Higgs bundle extends to this case, allowing us to produce
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes out of it.

Our ultimate goal is to study the mirror dual of
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes constructed out of the Dirac–Higgs bundle. For this task, we shall Fourier–Mukai transform the sheaves underlying these
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes. As the Fourier–Mukai transform is an algebraic device, we need an algebraic construction of the Dirac–Higgs bundle, which we address in this section. To surpass the non-existence of a universal bundle, we make use of the flat unitary gerbe
\beta
defined in the étale topology of
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
.

Let us fix a Hermitian metric on the rank
n
topologically trivial
C^{\infty}
-bundle
\mathbb{E}
over
X
. Associated to it, consider the vector space (of infinite dimension)

\underline{\Omega}:={}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathbb{E})\oplus{}_{X}^{0,1}(\mathbb{E}),

which comes equipped with a natural metric. Given a Higgs bundle
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
supported on
\mathbb{E}
, we write
\overline{\partial}_{E}
for the associated Dolbeault operator and
\partial_{E}
for the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection constructed with the metric and
\overline{\partial}_{E}
. Hitchin introduced in
[
Hit02
]
the following
Dirac–Higgs operators

\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{E}&-\varphi\\
\varphi^{*}&-\overline{\partial}_{E}\end{pmatrix}\,:\begin{pmatrix}{}_{X}^{0}(\mathbb{E})\\
{}_{X}^{0}(\mathbb{E})\end{pmatrix}\longrightarrow\underline{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}{}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathbb{E})\\
{}_{X}^{0,1}(\mathbb{E})\end{pmatrix}

and

\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\partial}_{E}&\varphi\\
\varphi^{*}&\partial_{E}\end{pmatrix}:\,\underline{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}{}_{X}^{1,0}(\mathbb{E})\\
{}_{X}^{0,1}(\mathbb{E})\end{pmatrix}\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}{}_{X}^{1,1}(\mathbb{E})\\
{}_{X}^{1,1}(\mathbb{E})\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{p}(\mathcal{E})
denote the hypercohomology groups of the complex of sheaves
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\rightarrow}}E\otimes K_{X}
. The key fact about such operators is that

\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})\oplus\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{E})

and

(2.12)

\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{E}),

see
[
Hit02
, Section 7]
or
[
Bla15
, Lemma 2.4.1 and Remark 2.4.2]
. Hausel proved in
[
Hau98
, Corollary 5.1.4]
that if
\mathcal{E}
is a nontrivial stable Higgs bundle of degree 0,
i.e.

\mathcal{E}\neq(\mathcal{O}_{X},0)
, then
\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{E})=0
, so that
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}=0
.
Since the index of
\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}
is
-2n(g-1)
, see
[
Bla15
, Lemma 2.1.8]
or
[
FJ08
, page 1226]
, we conclude that

(2.13)

\dim\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}=2n(g-1)

whenever
\mathcal{E}
is a semistable Higgs bundle of degree 0 of rank
n
without trivial factors. Furthermore, the vector space
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}
does not depend upon the choice of a representative within the S-equivalence class
[\mathcal{E}]
of
\mathcal{E}
.

Applying the Dirac operators pointwise in
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{st}}
one obtains the corresponding morphism of trivial bundles

\mathscr{D}^{*}:\underline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{st}}\longrightarrow\left({}_{X}^{1,1}(\mathbb{E})\oplus{}_{X}^{1,1}(\mathbb{E})\right)\times\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{st}}.

After (
2.13
), the kernel
\ker\mathscr{D}^{*}
defines a
C^{\infty}
vector bundle of rank
2n(g-1)
over
\mathcal{B}^{\,\mathrm{st}}
. If ever
\ker\mathscr{D}^{*}
descends from
\mathcal{B}^{\,\mathrm{st}}
to give a vector bundle on
\mathrm{M}^{\,\mathrm{st}}
we call the resulting object the
Dirac–Higgs bundle
. When our moduli space is equipped with a universal family, the Dirac–Higgs bundle is defined as the pull-back of
\mathcal{D}
under the section
\mathrm{M}^{\,\mathrm{st}}\to\mathcal{B}^{\,\mathrm{st}}
obtained from the universal family. The rank one case, where
\mathrm{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{st}}=\mathrm{M}_{1}
, is one of the few cases were the construction of the Dirac–Higgs bundle is possible, and it was achieved by the second named author in
[
FJ08
]
.

For
n>1
(and trivial degree) we have already seen that no universal bundle only exists, not even Zariski locally. As we have seen in Section
2.4
, for general rank, the best we can obtain is the
\pi_{M}^{*}\beta
-twisted universal bundle

over the moduli space of stable Higgs bundles

. We can now define the family of Dirac-type operators

\mathscr{D}_{(U_{i},\Phi_{i})}^{*}:\underline{\Omega}\times Z_{i}\longrightarrow\left({}_{X}^{1,1}(\mathbb{E})\oplus{}_{X}^{1,1}(\mathbb{E})\right)\times Z_{i},

given by

. Recalling (

), let us denote, for every

, the rank

algebraic sub-bundle of
\underline{\Omega}\times Z_{i}

and consider

\mathbf{D}:=\left\{D_{i}\to Z_{i}\right\}_{i\in I},

which we shall call the
\beta
-twisted Dirac–Higgs bundle
.

Adapting the work of Hausel
[

, one can describe the

\beta
-twisted Dirac–Higgs bundle in terms of the universal bundle
by means of (

\beta
-twisted Dirac–Higgs bundle is, indeed, a
\beta
-twisted

Proposition 2.1
.

Consider the obvious projections
\pi_{\mathrm{M}}:X\times\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}\to\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
and
\pi_{X}:X\times\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}\to X

\beta
-twisted

\beta
-twisted bundle over
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}

isomorphic to

(2.15)

\mathbf{D}\cong\mathbb{R}^{1}\pi_{\mathrm{M},*}\left(\mathbf{U}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\Phi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{U}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}K_{X}\right).

Proof.

Assuming the isomorphism (
2.15
), it follows from
the projection formula that
\mathbf{D}
is a
\beta
-twisted bundle over

\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
. We then focus on the proof of this isomorphism. Considering
each local universal bundle
(U_{i},\Phi_{i})
over the étale open open
subset
Z_{i}\to\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}
and recall from Section
2.4
that
we denoted
\pi_{\mathrm{M},i}:X\times Z_{i}\to Z_{i}
. Note that

\mathbb{R}^{0}\pi_{\mathrm{M},i,*}\left(U_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{i}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}K_{X}\right)=\mathbb{R}^{2}\pi_{\mathrm{M},i,*}\left(U_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{i}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}K_{X}\right)=0

Globally, one gets (

), and the proof is completed.
∎

One can also define a connection on the
\beta
-twisted Dirac–Higgs

\mathbf{D}=\left\{D_{i}\to Z_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}
. Consider the

, where

is the sheaf of

. Denote by

and the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{i}:\underline{\Omega}\times Z_{i}\to D_{i}
defined by the natural metric
on
\underline{\Omega}\times Z_{i}

and note that this defines a connection on the
\beta
-twisted
Dirac–Higgs bundle
\mathbf{D}
,

that we call the

connection. The importance of this

(1,1)
with respect to all complex structures, see in

One can check that

and

-brane on

3. Tensorization and spectral data

The morphism between Higgs moduli spaces that one obtains by
considering the tensor product with a particular Higgs bundle will be
crucial in our description of the Nahm transform of high rank that we
provide in Section
4
below. In this section we
explore the behaviour of the spectral data under tensorization,
generalizing partial results established in
[
BS16

for Higgs bundles of rank
2
and
4

5.3
, during our study of the
mirror branes dual to the ones we obtain after Nahm transform of high
rank.

As it is useful for the purpose of the remaining sections, we add to the notation related to the moduli space of Higgs bundles a sub-index indicating the rank.

Let us introduce in this section the tensorization of two Higgs bundles
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
and
\mathcal{F}=(F,\phi)

It is well known that, if

and

\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tau^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}:&\mathrm{M}_{m}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathrm{M}_{nm}\\
&[\mathcal{F}]&\longmapsto&[\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}].\end{array}

Remark 3.1
.

Note that
\tau^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
is hyperholomorphic, meaning that it is a holomorphic morphism between
(\mathrm{M}_{m},\Gamma_{m}^{i})
and
(\mathrm{M}_{nm},\Gamma_{nm}^{i})
for each of the
i=1

2
or
3

\tau^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}

i=1

i=2

(E^{\prime},\nabla_{E^{\prime}})
corresponding to
\mathcal{E}
under the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence, and observe that
\tau^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
, in the complex structure
\Gamma_{2}

(F^{\prime},\nabla_{F^{\prime}})
corresponding to
\mathcal{F}

(E^{\prime}\otimes F^{\prime},\nabla_{E^{\prime}}\otimes\mathrm{1}_{F^{\prime}}+\mathrm{1}_{E^{\prime}}\otimes\nabla_{F^{\prime}})

\tau^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
is holomorphic for
i=1
and

4. Nahm transform of high rank

In
[
Jar04
]
, the Nahm transform is constructed for any a family of self-dual connections over a spin four-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature. The construction produces a vector bundle with connection over the parametrizing space of the family once we consider, at each point of the parametrizing space, the cokernel of the associated Dirac operator. It is also possible to define naturally a connection on this bundle. The Nahm transform for Higgs bundles is defined in
[
FJ08
]
considering, for each stable Higgs bundle, the family obtained by twisting with the universal family of rank
1
Higgs bundles. Hence, for each stable Higgs bundle, we obtain a Hermitian connection over
\mathrm{M}_{1}
of type
(1,1)
with respect to the complex structures
\Gamma^{1}_{1}
,
\Gamma^{2}_{1}
and
\Gamma^{3}_{1}
.

Here, we generalize the previous construction to moduli spaces of stable Higgs bundles of arbitrary rank. The main difference with the rank
1
case relies in the fact that there is no universal bundle over
X\times\mathrm{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{st}}
for
n>1
, not even Zariski locally. Therefore, as we did in Section
2.5
, we shall work with the gerbe
\beta_{n}
in the étale topology of
\mathrm{M}_{n}
, and make use of the definition of
\beta_{n}
-twisted bundles.

Fix a Higgs bundle
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
of rank
n
and degree
0
, supported on the Hermitian
C^{\infty}
vector bundle
\mathbb{E}_{n}
. For every rank
m
Higgs bundle
\mathcal{F}=(F,\phi)
with
\deg(F)=0
, supported on the Hermitian
C^{\infty}
vector bundle
\mathbb{E}_{m}
, we can consider the Higgs bundle
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}=(E\otimes F,\varphi\otimes\mathrm{1}_{F}+\mathrm{1}_{F}\otimes\phi)
on
\mathbb{E}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{E}_{m}\cong\mathbb{E}_{nm}
. If
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are semistable, it is well known that
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}
is semistable, although, for
m>1
, such correspondence is no longer valid when we replace semistability with stability.

We recall that a crucial step in the construction of the Dirac–Higgs bundle is Hausel’s vanishing statement
[
Hau98
, Corollary 5.1.4]
which ensures that
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}=0
whenever
\mathcal{E}
is stable and different from the trivial Higgs bundle of degree 0 (where by trivial Higgs bundle we mean the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{X},0)
given by the trivial line bundle and zero Higgs field). Note that
\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X},0)=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X},0)=\mathbb{C}
. Since we intend to study the Dirac operator over the locus of Higgs bundles obtained as a tensor product, we need to study first whether or not one can generalize Hausel’s vanishing statement to this locus. This justifies the following definition.

If
\mathcal{E}
be a semistable Higgs bundle of degree 0, let
{\rm gr}(\mathcal{E})=\oplus_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j}
be the associated graded object, where

(\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{l})
are the stable factors of its Jordan–Hölder
filtration; we say that
\mathcal{E}
is
without trivial factors

if none of these factors
\mathcal{G}_{j}
is the trivial
Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{O}_{X},0)
. Clearly, every nontrivial stable
Higgs bundle is without trivial factors.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a semistable Higgs bundle of degree 0. If
\mathcal{E}
is
without trivial factors, then

\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{E})=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}=\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{E}))\simeq\oplus_{j}\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{j}),

where
\mathcal{G}_{j}
are the stable factors of the Jordan–Hölder
filtration of
\mathcal{E}
. In addition,
\mathcal{E}
is without trivial
factors if and only if
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}
is a semistable Higgs bundle of degree 0 and let

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{E}

be its Jordan–Hölder filtration, and let
\mathcal{G}_{j}=\mathcal{E}_{j}/\mathcal{E}_{j-1}
be
its factors. We prove the first claim by induction on the length
l

of the Jordan–Hölder filtration.

If
l=1
, then
\mathcal{E}
is stable and the claim is just the corollary due to Hausel mentioned above. For the induction step, assume that the claim holds when the
l=k-1
and consider the short exact sequence of Higgs bundles

0\to\mathcal{E}_{l-1}\to\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{G}_{l}\to 0.

If
\mathcal{E}
is without trivial factors, then
\mathcal{G}_{l}
is a nontrivial stable Higgs bundle, and
\mathcal{E}_{l-1}
is also without trivial factors, so that, by the induction hypothesis

\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{l-1})=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{l-1})=\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G}_{l})=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{l})=0.

The associated long exact sequence in hypercohomology then yields

\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{E})=0\penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{l-1})\oplus\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{l})=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{l}\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{j}).

In particular, we also conclude that if
\mathcal{E}
is without trivial factors, then
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}=0
.

For the converse statement, assume that one of the factors of the Jordan–Hölder filtration, say
\mathcal{G}_{j}
with
j\in\{1,\dots,l\}
, is trivial. Considering the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}_{j-1}\to\mathcal{E}_{j}\to\mathcal{G}_{j}\to 0,

we obtain a surjective map
\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{j})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{j})=\mathbb{C}
, thus
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}\neq 0
. The monomorphism
\mathcal{E}_{j}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}
then provides an injective map
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}_{j}}\hookrightarrow\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}
, proving that
\ker\mathscr{D}_{\mathcal{E}}\neq 0
as well.
∎

5. Mirror branes

5.1. A Fourier–Mukai transform for bundles twisted by gerbes

The goal of this section is to describe the mirror partners of the Nahm
\mathrm{(BBB)}
-branes we have constructed in previous sections. We shall do that by Fourier–Mukai transforming the Dirac–Higgs bundle and the bundles we obtain from the high rank Nahm transform. Before that, we first need to adapt the Fourier–Mukai transform to the setting of vector bundles twisted with a gerbe, a task that we address in this subsection. This requires the introduction of some notation.

Let
Y\to V
be a smooth
V
-variety equipped with a section
\hat{\sigma}:V\to Y
. In that case (see
[
BLR90
, 8.2, Proposition 4]
), there exists a relative Jacobian
\Jac^{0}_{V}(Y)
and let us consider

to be a torsor for
\Jac^{0}_{V}(Y)

\{V_{i}\to V\}_{i\in I}
of

and observe that
\{\zeta_{i}:Y\times_{V}V_{i}\to Y\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\zeta_{i}^{\vee}:Y^{\vee}\times_{V}V_{i}\to Y^{\vee}\}_{i\in I}
are étale coverings of, respectively,
Y
and
Y^{\vee}
. Let
\beta
be a flat unitary gerbe on the étale topology over
Y
giving the set of flat unitary line bundles
\{L_{ij}\to Y\times_{V}V_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
over the intersections
(Y\times_{V}V_{i})\times_{V}(Y\times_{V}V_{j})\cong Y\times(V_{i}\times_{V}V_{j})=Y\times_{V}V_{ij}

\check{\sigma}_{ij}:V_{ij}\to\Jac^{0}_{V_{ij}}(Y)
which can be understood as

. By abuse of notation, we still denote them by

\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i}
.

We can now provide a result analogous to Proposition
5.3
for
\widehat{\mathbf{E}}^{\prime}_{m}:=\widehat{\mathbf{E}}_{m}|_{\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}}
. We recall the
\beta_{m}
-shifted closed embedding
\mathfrak{i}_{m}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}
defined in Section
5.1
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
be a stable Higgs bundle with spectral data
L_{\mathcal{E}}\to S_{\mathcal{E}}
such that
S_{\mathcal{E}}
is reduced. Consider the
\beta_{m}
-shifted push-forward under
\mathfrak{i}_{m}
,

\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m}:=\mathfrak{i}_{m,*}\left(\widetilde{p}_{m}^{*}K_{X}\otimes(-1)^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}}\right).

Then,
\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m}
is a
\beta_{m}
-shifted
0
-WIT sheaf on
\Jac_{B^{\prime}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{S})^{\vee}
and

\widehat{\mathbf{E}}^{\prime}_{m}\cong R\check{\mathscr{F}}^{\beta_{m}}_{m}\left(\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m}\right).

Proof.

Recalling Proposition
4.3
, we proceed as in the proof of Proposition
5.3
with the appropriate modifications. We have

\displaystyle\widehat{E}^{\prime}_{m,i}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{R}^{1}\pi_{\Jac,*}\left(U^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{m,i}\otimes\mathrm{1}+\mathrm{1}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}U^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}E\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}K_{X}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{R}^{1}\pi_{\Jac,*}\left((\mathrm{1}_{\Jac}\times p_{m,i})_{*}P^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{m,i}\otimes\mathrm{1}+\mathrm{1}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathrm{1}_{\Jac}\times p_{m,i})_{*}P^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}(E\otimes K_{X})\right)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{R}^{1}\pi_{\Jac,*}\left((\mathrm{1}_{\Jac}\times p_{m,i})_{*}\left(P^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{S}\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\mathrm{1}_{\Jac}\times\lambda_{m,i})\otimes\mathrm{1}+\mathrm{1}\otimes\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{S}\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}P^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{S}\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}(E\otimes K_{X})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{R}^{1}\widetilde{\pi}_{\Jac,*}\left(P^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\widetilde{\pi}_{S}^{*}\left(\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{1}\otimes\lambda_{m,i}+\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}\varphi\otimes\mathrm{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}E\otimes\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}K_{X}\right)\right).

Using a (vertical) spectral sequence similar to the one that appears in Proposition
5.3
, one can show

\widehat{E}^{\prime}_{m,i}\cong R^{0}\widetilde{\pi}_{\Jac,*}\left(P^{\prime}_{m,i}\otimes\widetilde{\pi}_{S}^{*}\left(\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}K_{X}\otimes(-1)^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E},i}|_{\Xi_{m,i}^{\mathcal{E}}}\right)\right),

since
\mathrm{1}\otimes\lambda_{m,i}+\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}\varphi\otimes\mathrm{1}
is again injective.

Next, using the description of the Poincaré bundle
\mathbf{P}_{m}
in terms of
\mathcal{P}_{m}
given in Section
2.4
, the projection formula and base change theorems for the various morphisms of diagram (
5.5
), one obtains

\widehat{\mathbf{E}}^{\prime}_{m}\cong R^{0}\check{\mathscr{F}}_{m}^{\beta_{m}}\left(R^{0}\imath_{m,i,*}\left(\widetilde{p}_{m}^{*}K_{X}\otimes(-1)^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\Xi_{m}^{\mathcal{E}}}\right)\right).

Finally, as in the last part of the proof of Proposition
5.3
, due to the fact that
\Xi_{m,i}^{\mathcal{E}}
is a finite
2nm(g-1)
-cover of
B^{\prime}_{m}
,
R\check{\mathscr{F}}_{m}^{\beta_{m}}\left(\imath_{m,i,*}\left(\widetilde{p}_{m,i}^{*}K_{X}\otimes(-1)^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{E}}|_{\Xi_{m,i}^{\mathcal{E}}}\right)\right)
is a complex supported in degree
0
.
∎

Thanks to Theorem
5.5
and (
2.11
) we can describe the Fourier–Mukai transform restricted to the smooth locus of the Hitchin fibration
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}\subset\mathrm{M}_{m}
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
d_{m}=1+m^{2}(g-1)
. The
\beta_{m}
-twisted bundle
\widehat{\mathbf{E}}^{\prime}_{m}
is a
\beta_{m}
-twisted
d_{m}
-WIT sheaf satisfying

R\hat{\mathscr{F}}_{m}^{\beta_{m}}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{E}}^{\prime}_{m}\right)\cong\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m},

where

\mathrm{supp}\left(\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m}\right)=\mathfrak{i}_{m}\left(-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}\right).

We study now its support.

Theorem 5.7
.

The support of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m}
is a
\beta_{m}
-shifted
2nm(g-1)
-section of the Hitchin fibration and it is Lagrangian with respect to
\Lambda_{m}^{1}
.

Proof.

Recall the étale covering
\{V_{m,i}\to B^{\prime}_{m}\}_{i\in I}
of the locus of smooth spectral curves which is fine enough for the gerbe
\beta_{m}
. This induces an étale covering
\left\{z_{i}:\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}\times_{B^{\prime}}V_{m,i}\to\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}\right\}_{i\in I}
. Since
\mathfrak{i}_{m}
is a
\beta_{m}
-shifted embedding and
\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
is a
2nm(g-1)
-cover of
B^{\prime}_{m}
, we have that
\mathfrak{i}_{m}(-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m})
is a
\beta_{m}
-shifted
2nm(g-1)
-section of
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}\to B^{\prime}_{m}
. It remains to proof that it is Lagrangian with respect to
\Lambda^{1}_{m,i}:=z_{i}^{*}\Lambda^{1}_{m}
.

Recalling Corollary
5.6
, we now address the proof that

\mathfrak{i}_{m}(-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m})
is Lagragian with respect to
\Lambda_{m}^{1}
. Recall from Section
2.1
, that
\Lambda_{m}^{1}
is defined as the exterior derivative
d\theta
of a certain
1
-form
\theta
. Then, we see that
\imath_{m,i}(-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i})
is Lagrangian with respect to
\Lambda^{1}_{m,i}
if and only if
\theta_{i}:=z_{i}^{*}\theta
is a constant 1-form along
\imath_{m,i}(-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i})
for all
i\in I
. Since
\imath_{m,i}
is an embedding, it suffices to prove that
\imath_{m,i}^{*}\theta_{i}|_{-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i}}
is constant.

We recall the definition of
\theta
. We recall that
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}\subset\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{st}}_{m}
so all the points are smooth and represented by the stable Higgs bundle
\mathcal{E}=(E,\varphi)
, the tangent space is
T_{\mathcal{E}}\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m}=\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
, which comes naturally equipped with the map
t:\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})\to H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
. By Serre duality, the Higgs field
\varphi\in H^{0}(\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes K_{X})
is an element of the dual space of
H^{1}(\mathrm{End}(E))
and recall that we defined
\theta(v)=\langle\varphi,t(v)\rangle
, for each
v\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
.

We now study the description of
\theta_{i}
over
\Jac_{V_{m,i}}^{\,\delta_{m}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m,i})
. By the spectral correspondence, given the
spectral data
L\to S_{m,b}
, one has that
E=(p_{b})_{*}L
and

\varphi=(p_{b})_{*}\lambda_{b}
where
\lambda_{b}:L\to L\otimes p_{b}^{*}K_{X}
is given by tensoring by the restriction to
S_{m,b}
of the
tautological section
\lambda:\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\to p^{*}K_{X}
. Note that

p_{b}^{*}K_{X}
is a sub-sheaf of the canonical bundle
K_{S_{m,b}}
, then

\lambda_{b}
gives naturally an element of
H^{0}(S_{m,b},K_{S_{m,b}})
. The isomorphism
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}_{m,i}\cong\Jac_{V_{m,i}}^{\,\delta_{m}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m,i})^{\vee}\cong\Jac_{V_{m,i}}^{\,\delta_{m}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m,i})
, given by the push-forward under
p_{b}
and
autoduality, provides as well the isomorphism between

\mathbb{H}^{1}(C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})}(L,L)
and between

H^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{S_{m,b}}(L,L)\cong H^{1}(S_{m,b},\mathcal{O}_{S_{m,b}})
. Then, we can express
\theta_{i}(v)
to be
\langle\lambda_{b},t^{\prime}(v)\rangle
given by Serre duality, where now
v\in T_{L}\Jac_{V_{m,i}}^{\,\delta_{m}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m,i})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})}(L,L)
, the section
\lambda_{b}\in H^{0}(S_{m,b},K_{S_{m,b}})
is defined
by the restriction of the tautological section
\lambda:\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\to p^{*}K_{X}
to
S_{m,b}\subset\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
, and

t^{\prime}:\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})}(L,L)\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{S_{m,b}}(L,L)\cong H^{1}(S_{m,b},\mathcal{O}_{S_{m,b}})

is the projection to those deformations that preserve the support.

Note that, for every
L_{1},L_{2}\in\Jac(S_{m,b})
, one has naturally that

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{S_{m,b}}(L_{1},L_{1})\cong H^{1}(S_{m,b},\mathcal{O}_{S_{m,b}})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{S_{m,b}}(L_{2},L_{2}).

We observe that
\theta_{i}
is a 1-form which is constant along the fibres
\Jac^{\delta_{m}}(S_{m,b})
. On the other hand, the 1-form
\imath_{m,i}^{*}\theta_{i}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m,i}
depends on the embedding
d\imath_{m,i}:T_{s}S_{m,b}\hookrightarrow T_{\mathcal{O}(s-s_{i})}\Jac^{\delta_{m}}(S_{m,b})\cong H^{1}(S_{m,b},\mathcal{O}_{S_{m,b}})
. Recall that
\imath_{m,i}
sends the point
s\in S_{m,b}
to the line bundle whose meromorphic sections have pole at
s\in S_{m,b}
and a zero at
s_{i}
. Since Serre duality
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:H^{0}(K_{S_{m,b}})\times H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S_{m,b}})\to\mathbb{C}
sends
\langle\lambda,\xi\rangle
to the sum of residues of the meromorphic differential
\lambda\xi
, one has that

\imath_{m,i}^{*}\theta_{i}|_{s}\cong\imath_{m,i}^{*}\langle\lambda_{b},\cdot\rangle|_{s}\cong\lambda_{b}(s).

So,
\imath_{m,i}^{*}\theta_{i}
is the one form defined by the tautological section
\lambda:\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})\to p^{*}K_{X}
.

Obviously, the tautological section
\lambda
restricted to
X\times\{0\}\subset\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
, is the zero section. Recall that we have
defined
-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i}
as the intersection of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E},i}
and

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{m,i}
inside
\mathrm{Tot}(K_{X})
. But this is equivalent to the
intersection of
\Sigma_{m,i}^{\mathcal{E}^{*}}
with
X\times\{0\}
. Therefore,
\lambda
is constantly
0
along
-\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i}
,
that is
\imath_{m,i}^{*}\theta_{i}|_{\Xi^{\mathcal{E}}_{m,i}}=0
, and this
concludes the proof.
∎

We finish placing this statement in the context of mirror symmetry.

Corollary 5.8
.

The
\beta_{m}
-shifted coherent sheaf
\widecheck{\mathfrak{E}}^{\prime}_{m}
admits a

\beta_{m}
-shifted
\mathrm{(BAA)}
-brane structure.

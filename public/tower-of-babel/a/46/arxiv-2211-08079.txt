Introduction

For an abelian variety
X
,
we have a notion of dual
\widehat{X}
.
It is an abelian variety
which is the moduli space
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
of numerically trivial line bundles
on
X
, and
X
is the moduli space of
numerically trivial line bundles on
\widehat{X}

via the universal family
{\mathcal{P}}
:
We have an isomorphism

X\to\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\widehat{X})
by sending
x\in X
to

{\mathcal{P}}_{|\{x\}\times\widehat{X}}
.
We call this property Fourier-Mukai duality in
[
21
]
, since it was a key property
of Mukai’s construction of
an equivalence

{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(\widehat{X})

[
14
]
.
It is now known that
Fourier-Mukai duality holds for many moduli spaces
X^{\prime}
of stable sheaves
on abelian surfaces, K3 surfaces and elliptic surfaces
X
, if
\dim X=\dim X^{\prime}

[
2
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
18
]
.
In
[
21
]
, we explained Fourier-Mukai duality on K3 surfaces by using Bridgeland stability conditions.
In this note, by introducing a stability condition,
we shall give a similar explanation for an elliptic surface
(Theorem
2.6
).
our argument is the same as in
[
21
]
.
As an application, we shall recover a result in
[
16
]
that
a suitable twisted stability for a semi-stable 1-dimensional sheaf
is preserved under a Fourier-Mukai transform
by using Bridgeland stability condition (see Proposition
3.2
).
As another motivation of this note,
we discuss the relation of walls for Bridgeland stability and
\lambda
-stability
in
[
22
]
when
X
is an elliptic K3 surface.

Let us briefly explain the organization of this note.
In sections
1.1
and
1.2
,
we recall Bridgeland stability conditions.
In section
1.3
,
we introduce a symmetric pairing on
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

which is preserved under Fourier-Mukai transforms.
By using our pairing, we introduce a stability condition, and show that it is equivalent to
a geometric stability condition constructed by Arcara and Bertram
[
1
]
(Proposition
1.10
).
We next study the Fourier-Mukai transform of our stability condition in section
2
, and prove that
the obtained stability condition is equivalent to
a geometric stability condition (Proposition
2.3
).
Then Theorem
2.6
easily follows.
All these arguments are the same as in
[
21
]
.
In section
3
and section
4
,
we give applications.

For related results, we would like to remark that Lo and Martinez
[
10
]
obtained almost all results in this note
for the Fourier-Mukai transform between a Weierstrass elliptic surface and
its compactfied relative Picard scheme.

1.
Stability conditions and Fourier-Mukai transforms on an elliptic surface.

1.1.
Stability conditions

Let us briefly recall some notation on stability conditions.
For more details, see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.
A stability condition
\sigma=(Z_{\sigma},{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma})

on
{\bf D}(X)

consists of a group homomorphism
Z_{\sigma}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\mathbb{C}}

and a slicing
{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}
of
{\bf D}(X)
such that if

0\neq E\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)
then

Z_{\sigma}(E)=m(E)\exp(\pi\sqrt{-1}\phi)

for some
m(E)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
.
The set of stability conditions has a structure of complex
manifold.
We denote this space by
\operatorname{Stab}(X)
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
\sigma
-semi-stable object
E
of phase
\phi
is
an object of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)
.
If
E
is a simple object of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)
, then

E
is
\sigma
-stable.

By
[
5
, Prop. 5.3]
,
giving a stability condition
\sigma
is
the same as giving a bounded
t
-structure on
{\bf D}(X)

and a stability function
Z_{\sigma}
on its heart
{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma}

with the Harder-Narasimhan property.
For
\sigma=(Z_{\sigma},{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma})
,
we have the relation
{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma}={\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}((0,1])
,
where
{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}((0,1])
is the subcategory of
{\bf D}(X)

generated by semi-stable objects
E\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)
with

\phi\in(0,1]
.
Since the pair
(Z_{\sigma},{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma})

defines a stability condition,
we also use the symbol
\sigma=(Z_{\sigma},{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma})

to denote a stability condition.
For a
\sigma
-semi-stable object
E\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)
, we set
\phi_{\sigma}(E)=\phi
.
For
E\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}((0,1])
,
we also set
\phi_{\sigma}(E)\in(0,1]

by
Z_{\sigma}(E)=m(E)\exp(\pi\sqrt{-1}\phi_{\sigma}(E))
.

For an equivalence
\Phi:{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(X^{\prime})
,
we have an isomorphism
\Phi:\operatorname{Stab}(X)\to\operatorname{Stab}(X^{\prime})

such that

\Phi(\sigma)

(\sigma\in\operatorname{Stab}(X))

is a stability condition given by

(1.1)

\begin{split}Z_{\Phi(\sigma)}=&Z_{\sigma}\circ\Phi^{-1}:{\bf D}(X^{\prime})\to{\mathbb{C}},\\
{\mathcal{P}}_{\Phi(\sigma)}(\phi)=&\Phi({\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi)).\end{split}

We also have an action of the universal covering

\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
on

\operatorname{Stab}(X)
.
Since
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
,
we have an injective homomorphism

{\mathbb{C}}\to\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Thus
we have an action of
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
on

\operatorname{Stab}(X)
.
For a stability condition
\sigma\in\operatorname{Stab}(X)
,

\lambda(\sigma)
is given by

(1.2)

\begin{split}Z_{\lambda(\sigma)}=&\exp(-\pi\sqrt{-1}\lambda)Z_{\sigma}\\
{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda(\sigma)}(\phi)=&{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(\phi+\mathrm{Re}\lambda).\end{split}

1.2.
Geometric stability conditions

Bridgeland
[
6
]
and Arcara and Bertram
[
1
]

constructed a stability condition

\sigma_{(\beta,\omega)}=(Z_{(\beta,\omega)},{\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,\omega)})

which is characterized
by the stability of
k_{x}
(
x\in X
), where

Z_{(\beta,\omega)}(\bullet)=\langle e^{\beta+\sqrt{-1}\omega},\operatorname{ch}(\bullet)\rangle:{\bf D}(X)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is the stability function and

{\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,\omega)}

is an abelian category which is a tilting of
\operatorname{Coh}(X)

by a torsion pair
({\mathcal{T}}_{(\beta,\omega)},{\mathcal{F}}_{(\beta,\omega)})
:

(i)

{\mathcal{T}}_{(\beta,\omega)}
is a full subcategory of
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
generated by
torsion sheaves and
\mu
-stable sheaves
E
with

(c_{1}(E)-\operatorname{rk}E\beta,\omega)>0
and

(ii)

{\mathcal{F}}_{(\beta,\omega)}
is a full subcategory of
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
generated by

\mu
-stable sheaves
E
with

(c_{1}(E)-\operatorname{rk}E\beta,\omega)\leq 0
.

This kind of stability conditions are called geometric.
To be more precise,
for a geometric stability condition

\sigma=(Z_{\sigma},{\mathcal{A}}_{\sigma})
,
we require that

\mathrm{Re}\mho,\mathrm{Im}\mho
span a positive definite
2-plane of
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
,
where
Z_{\sigma}(\bullet)=\langle\mho,\bullet\rangle

with
\mho\in H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
.
Up to the action of
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
,
there is
(\beta,\omega)
such that

\sigma=\sigma_{(\beta,\omega)}
.
We shall use this fact for a stability condition
on an elliptic surface (see subsection
1.4
in particular Lemma
1.11
).

1.3.
Mukai pairing and Fourier-Mukai transform on an elliptic surface.

Let
\pi:X\to C
be an elliptic surface over a curve
C

and
f
a fiber of
\pi
.
Let

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}:=H^{0}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus H^{4}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

be the algebraic part of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.
We define
\varrho_{X}\in H^{4}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
by

\int_{X}\varrho_{X}=1
.
Then we have

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}={\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\varrho_{X}.

For
u=x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}\varrho_{X}
(
x_{0},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
x_{1}\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
),
we set
u^{*}:=x_{0}-x_{1}+x_{2}\varrho_{X}
.

Definition 1.2
.

(1)

We define the Mukai pairing
\langle\;\;,\;\;\rangle
on

H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}

by

(1.3)

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{X}u^{*}v=(x_{1}\cdot y_{1})-x_{0}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{0},\;u=x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}\varrho_{X},v=y_{0}+y_{1}+y_{2}\varrho_{X},

where
(x_{1}\cdot y_{1})
is the intersection pairing of
\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(2)

For
E\in{\bf D}(X)
, we define the Mukai vector of
E
by

(1.4)

v(E):=\operatorname{ch}E(1+\tfrac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}{2}\varrho_{X})\in H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}.

Lemma 1.3
.

For
E,F\in{\bf D}(X)
,

(1.5)

-\langle v(E),v(F)\rangle=\chi(E,F(\tfrac{1}{2}K_{X})).

Proof.

(1.6)

\chi(E,F(\tfrac{1}{2}K_{X}))=\int_{X}(\operatorname{ch}E)^{*}\operatorname{ch}Fe^{\frac{1}{2}K_{X}}\operatorname{td}_{X}=\int_{X}v(E)^{*}v(F)=-\langle v(E),v(F)\rangle.

∎

Definition 1.4
.

Let
v\in H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}
be a Mukai vector (of a coherent sheaf).

(1)

For an ample divisor
H
on
X
and
\beta\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
let
M_{H}^{\beta}(v)
be the moduli space of

S
-equivalence classes of

\beta
-twisted semi-stable sheaves
E
with
the Mukai vector
v(E)=v

and
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}^{\beta}(v)^{ss}
the moduli stack of

\beta
-twisted semi-stable sheaves
E
with
v(E)=v
.

(2)

For a stability condition
\sigma
,
we denote the moduli stack of
\sigma
-semi-stable objects
E
with

v(E)=v
by
{\mathcal{M}}_{\sigma}(v)
,
where we usually choose

\phi:=\mathrm{Im}(\log Z_{\sigma}(v))/\pi\in(-1,1]
.
We also denote the moduli scheme by
M_{\sigma}(v)
if it exists.

M_{H}^{\beta}(v)
is a projective scheme.
If
H
is general in the ample cone,

M_{H}^{\beta}(v)
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{H}^{\beta}(v)^{ss}
are
independent of the choice of
\beta
.

Definition 1.5
.

For elliptic surfaces
X\to C
,
Y\to C

and
{\bf P}\in{\bf D}(X\times Y)
,
let
\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}:{\bf D}(X)\to{\bf D}(Y)
be an integral functor
whose kernel is
{\bf P}
:

(1.7)

\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}}(E)={\bf R}p_{Y*}(p_{X}^{*}(E)\otimes{\bf P}),\;\;E\in{\bf D}(X).

We also set

(1.8)

v({\bf P}):=\operatorname{ch}({\bf P})p_{X}^{*}((1+\tfrac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}{2}\varrho_{X}))p_{Y}^{*}((1+\tfrac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{Y})}{2}\varrho_{Y}))\in H^{*}(X\times Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

As a consequence of Grothendieck Riemann-Roch theorem, we have the following.

Lemma 1.6
.

For
\Phi:=\Phi_{X\to Y}^{{\bf P}^{\vee}}
,
we have a commutative diagram

(1.9)

\begin{CD}{\bf D}(X)@>{\Phi}>{}>{\bf D}(Y)\\
@V{v}V{}V@V{}V{v}V\\
H^{*}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}@>{\overline{\Phi}}>{}>H^{*}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})_{\operatorname{alg}}\end{CD}

where

(1.10)

\overline{\Phi}(\alpha):=p_{Y*}(v({\bf P}^{\vee})p_{X}^{*}(e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}K_{X}})p_{X}^{*}(\alpha)).

Lemma 1.7
.

Assume that
\Phi
is a relative Fourier-Mukai transform, that is,

{\bf P}_{|X\times\{y\}}

(y\in Y)
is a stable 1-dimensional sheaf on a fiber of
\pi
.
Then
for
E\in{\bf D}(X)
,

\overline{\Phi}(v(E)e^{\lambda f})=e^{\lambda f}\overline{\Phi}(v(E))
.

Proof.

By

(1.11)

(\operatorname{ch}E)e^{\lambda f}=\operatorname{ch}E+\lambda\operatorname{ch}(E_{|f}),

we get

(1.12)

v(E)e^{\lambda f}=v(E)+\lambda v(E_{|f}).

Then we see that

(1.13)

\begin{split}v(\Phi(E))e^{\lambda f}=&v(\Phi(E))+\lambda v(\Phi(E)_{|f})\\
=&v(\Phi(E))+\lambda v(\Phi(E_{|f}))\\
=&\overline{\Phi}(v(E))+\overline{\Phi}(\lambda v(E_{|f}))\\
=&\overline{\Phi}(v(E)e^{\lambda f}).\end{split}

∎

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\Phi
be an equivalence in Lemma
1.7
. Then

\overline{\Phi}
preserves

\langle\;\;,\;\;\rangle
.
Thus

\langle\overline{\Phi}(v(E_{1})),\overline{\Phi}(v(E_{2}))\rangle=\langle v(E_{1}),v(E_{2})\rangle,\;E_{1},E_{2}\in{\bf D}(X).

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\Phi}:{\bf D}(Y)\to{\bf D}(X)
be a quasi-inverse of
\Phi
.
For
E\in{\bf D}(X)
and
F\in{\bf D}(Y)
, by using Lemma
1.7
,
we get

(1.14)

\begin{split}\chi(E,\widehat{\Phi}(F)(\tfrac{1}{2}K_{X}))=&\chi(E(-\tfrac{1}{2}K_{X}),\widehat{\Phi}(F))\\
=&\chi(\Phi(E(-\tfrac{1}{2}K_{X})),F)\\
=&\chi(\Phi(E)(-\tfrac{1}{2}K_{Y}),F)\\
=&\chi(\Phi(E),F(\tfrac{1}{2}K_{Y})).\end{split}

Hence our claim holds.
∎

1.4.
A stability condition on an elliptic surface.

Let
\pi:X\to C
be an elliptic surface over a curve
C
.
We shall introduce a stability condition which has a better behavior than
the stability condition
\sigma_{(\beta,\omega)}
in subsection
1.2

under the Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi
.

Definition 1.9
.

For
(\beta,\omega)\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\times\operatorname{Amp}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
with

(\omega^{2})>\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,
we set

\widehat{Z}_{(\beta,\omega)}(E):=\langle e^{\beta+\sqrt{-1}\omega},v(E)\rangle,\;E\in{\bf D}(X)

and set

(1.15)

\widehat{\sigma}_{(\beta,\omega)}:=(\widehat{Z}_{(\beta,\omega)},{\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,\omega)}).

We shall prove that
\widehat{\sigma}_{(\beta,\omega)}
is equivalent to the stability condition

\sigma_{(\beta,\omega^{\prime})}
by the action of
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
.
For
H\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, we set
\omega:=tH
and

s:=\sqrt{t^{2}-\frac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}{(H^{2})}}
, where
t^{2}-\frac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}{(H^{2})}>0
.
Let
(T,f)\in\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
be an element such that

T\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
acts on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}={\mathbb{R}}+{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{-1}
as

(1.16)

T(x+y\sqrt{-1})=x+\tfrac{s}{t}y\sqrt{-1}

and the increasing function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
f(n)=n
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then we get the following result.

Proposition 1.10
.

(T,f)\cdot(Z_{(\beta,sH)},{\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,sH)})=(\widehat{Z}_{(\beta,tH)},{\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,tH)})
.
In particular
\widehat{\sigma}_{(\beta,\omega)}
is a stability condition such that

k_{x}

(x\in X)
are stable objects of phase 1.

Proof.

We set
\sigma^{\prime}:=(T,f)\cdot\sigma_{(\beta,sH)}
.

(1.17)

\widehat{Z}_{(\beta,tH)}(E)=T^{-1}\circ Z_{(\beta,sH)}(E).

We set

(1.18)

\operatorname{ch}(E)=e^{\beta}(r+\xi+a\varrho_{X}),r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\;\xi\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{R}},a\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Then

(1.19)

\begin{split}\widehat{Z}_{(\beta,tH)}(E)=&\langle e^{\beta}(1+\sqrt{-1}tH-\tfrac{t^{2}(H^{2})}{2}\varrho_{X}),e^{\beta}(r+\xi+a\varrho_{X})(1+\tfrac{\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}{2}\varrho_{X})\rangle\\
=&\frac{r}{2}(t^{2}(H^{2})-\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}))-a+\sqrt{-1}t(H\cdot\xi)\\
=&\frac{r}{2}s^{2}(H^{2})-a+\sqrt{-1}\frac{t}{s}(sH\cdot\xi)\\
=&T^{-1}\circ Z_{(\beta,sH)}.\end{split}

By the definition of
(T,f)
, we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}((0,1])={\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma_{(\beta,sH)}}((0,1])={\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,sH)}={\mathcal{A}}_{(\beta,tH)}
.
Hence the claim holds.
∎

6
, Prop. 10.3]
.

.

Let

be a

-divisor with

2.
Fourier-Mukai duality

2.1.
A Fourier-Mukai transform on an elliptic surface.

Let
\pi:X\to C
be an elliptic surface over a curve
C

and
f
a fiber of
\pi
.
Let
H
be a relatively ample
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor such that

(H\cdot f)=1
.
Let
v_{0}=\eta+b\varrho_{X}
be a primitive and isotropic Mukai vector
such that
\eta=r_{0}f
, that is,
\eta
is a nef and effective divisor with

(\eta^{2})=0
.
Replacing
H
by
H+xf
, we may assume that

(H^{2})=0
.
We shall consider
\beta
-twisted stability with respect to

H_{n}:=H+nf

(n\gg 0)
.
We can take
e^{\beta}
such that
\langle e^{\beta},v_{0}\rangle=0
.
Indeed we can choose
y

satisfying

\langle e^{\beta+yH_{n}},v_{0}\rangle=\langle e^{\beta},v_{0}\rangle-y(H\cdot\eta)=0
.
Then
v_{0}=\eta e^{\beta}
.
Assume that
X^{\prime}:=M_{H_{n}}^{\beta}(v_{0})
is a fine moduli
scheme consisting of
\beta
-twisted stable sheaves. Then

X^{\prime}
is an elliptic surface with a fibration

\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to C
. We denote a fiber of
X^{\prime}\to C
by
f^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

{\mathcal{M}}_{H+nf}^{\beta}(v_{0})^{ss}
depends only on

H
and

\beta\mod{\mathbb{Q}}f+{\mathbb{Q}}H
.
In particular
{\mathcal{M}}_{H+nf}^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}}(v_{0})^{ss}={\mathcal{M}}_{H+nf}^{\beta}(v_{0})^{ss}
.

Proof.

Let
E
be a purely 1-dimensional sheaf with
v(E)=v_{0}
.
For an exact sequence

0\to E_{1}\to E\to E_{2}\to 0,

0=(c_{1}(E)\cdot f)=(c_{1}(E_{1})\cdot f)+(c_{1}(E_{2})\cdot f)

implies
that
(c_{1}(E_{1})\cdot f)=(c_{1}(E_{2})\cdot f)=0
.
Hence
(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot(H+yf))

(i=1,2)
is independent of
y
.
Since

\frac{\chi(e^{\beta+xH+yf},E_{i})}{(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot(H+nf))}=\frac{\chi(e^{\beta+xH+yf},E_{i})}{(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot H)}=\frac{\chi(e^{\beta},E_{i})}{(c_{1}(E_{i})\cdot H)}-x,

the twisted semi-stability is independent of the choice of
x,y
.
∎

For a universal family
{\mathcal{E}}
,
we have a Fourier-Mukai transform

\Phi:=\Phi_{X\to X^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}[2]}
.
We set
v_{0}^{\prime}:=v({\mathcal{E}}_{|\{x\}\times X^{\prime}}^{\vee}[1])=-\overline{\Phi}(\varrho_{X})
and
H^{\prime}:=c_{1}(\overline{\Phi}(r_{0}e^{\beta}))
.
Then
c_{1}(v_{0}^{\prime})=r_{0}f^{\prime}
,
({H^{\prime}}^{2})=0
and
(H^{\prime}\cdot f^{\prime})=1
.

Since
\langle\overline{\Phi}(He^{\beta})^{2}\rangle=0
and

\langle\overline{\Phi}(He^{\beta}),\varrho_{X^{\prime}}\rangle=(H\cdot\eta)=r_{0}
,
there is
\beta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{NS}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}
with

\overline{\Phi}(He^{\beta})=-r_{0}e^{\beta^{\prime}}
.
Then we have

(2.1)

For a primitive Mukai vector

let

(4.1)

For

[f]\in\overline{P}

Lemma 4.1

Let

be a wall for

(-2)

Proof.

Let

(v-u)^{\perp}=u^{\perp}
.
By
[
3

\langle u^{2}\rangle=0

0<\langle v,u\rangle\leq\ell

(iii)

By

(\xi,f)=0

Assume that

Since

\langle v,u\rangle>\ell

Then we get

\langle u^{2}\rangle=0

\langle u^{2}\rangle=-2

In this case

and

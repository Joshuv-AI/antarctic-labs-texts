Measurement-Device-Independent Detection of Beyond-Quantum State
Baichu Yu, Masahito Hayashi

Abstract
In quantum theory, a quantum state on a composite system of two parties realizes a non-negative probability with any measurement element with a tensor product form. However, there also exist non-quantum states which satisfy the above condition. Such states are called beyond-quantum states, and cannot be detected by standard Bell tests. To distinguish a beyond-quantum state from quantum states, we propose a measurement-device-independent (MDI) test for beyond-quantum state detection, which is composed of quantum input states on respective parties and quantum measurements across the input system and the target system on respective parties. The performance of our protocol is independent of the forms of the tested states and the measurement operators, which provides an advantage in practical scenarios. We also discuss the importance of tomographic completeness of the input sets to the detection.



I
introduction

Bell test is a very important type of protocol to study the theoretical and practical problems of quantum information. Standard Bell test contains two spatially separated parties, each of which are given some classical inputs and required to generate corresponding classical outputs. The result of the standard Bell test only depends on the generated input-output statistics, which are referred to as correlations, and no knowledge about the experimental setups or the physical theory is required. Such a property is called device-independent (DI). The DI property of Bell test makes it a convenient scenario for studying theories which could be more general than quantum mechanics. Some of these theories are proposed based on physical principles
Linden et al. 2007
;
Pawłowski et al. 2009
;
Navascués and Wunderlich 2010
, some of them are based on more general mathematical frameworks
Navascués et al. 2015
;
Barnum et al. 2010
;
Arai et al. 2023
.

General Probabilistic Theory (GPT)
Plávala 2023
is a theory equipped with general states and measurements, which together produce probability distribution for experimental outcomes. GPT requires only two weak assumptions: the independence between the choice of state and the choice of measurement, and the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d) property of data generated by repeating experiment
Grabowecky et al. 2022
, therefore it includes quantum theory. Due to its generality, GPT is used to discuss the mathematical structure
Arai and Hayashi 2023a
and information processing properties
Barrett 2007
;
Dall’Arno et al. 2017
of quantum theory. In particular, there are models which possess the same local structure as quantum theory, but has different global structure
Stevens and Busch 2014
;
Arai et al. 2019
;
Aubrun et al. 2022
;
Arai and Hayashi 2023a
.

Then a natural question would be whether we can identify quantum mechanics with the correlations generated in Bell-type DI scenarios. The negative answer was shown by the existence of a family of GPT states which is more general than quantum states, but produces only quantum correlations in DI Bell tests
Barnum et al. 2010
;
Arai et al. 2023
. Such states are locally quantum and generate valid probability distribution under local (separable) measurements, therefore are called positive over all pure tensors (POPT) states
Kläy et al. 1987
;
Barnum et al. 2005
. The set of POPT states includes the set of quantum states, and also some non-quantum states which have negative eigenvalues.
In the following, we refer to such non-quantum POPT states as beyond-quantum states.
Briefly speaking, DI Bell tests can not detect beyond-quantum states
on the composite system of
A
and
B

due to the following reasons.
Any beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{nq}
can be
obtained by performing a positive trace-preserving map

\Gamma_{A}

on
A
to a quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{q}
.
The dual map
\Gamma_{A}^{*}
of the positive map
\Gamma_{A}
is also a positive map, which turns a
Positive-Operator-Valued Measurement (POVM) element

M^{A}_{a}
on
A

into another POVM element
\Gamma_{A}^{*}(M^{A}_{a})
on
A
.
Therefore, the correlations produced by
the pair of a beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{nq}

and a local measurement
\{M^{A}_{a}\otimes M^{B}_{b}\}_{a,b}

can be simulated by the correlations produced by
the pair of the above quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{q}

and a local measurement
\{\Gamma_{A}^{*}(M^{A}_{a})\otimes M^{B}_{b}\}_{a,b}
.
That is, if the measurement operators can not be certified,
the correlation produced by measuring a beyond quantum
state cannot be distinguished from the correlation
produced by measuring a quantum state.

In order to detect/identify beyond-quantum states with local measurements, we need some protocol which is more restrictive on the measurement operators than DI Bell test. A straightforward idea is to consider device-dependent (DD) protocol, where the form of the measurement operators are known
Arai et al. 2023
.
However, we do not always have so much knowledge on measurement devices, e.g.,
sometimes the measurement devices can not be trusted. In those cases, the soundness of the result can not be guaranteed. Therefore we consider a type of an intermediate protocol between DI and DD ones,
called a measurement-device-independent (MDI) protocol.
The key property of an MDI protocol is that it transfers the trust on measurement devices into the trust on state preparations, such that the soundness of the protocol is independent of the knowledge of measurement operators. Consequently, MDI protocols are widely used in tasks such as quantum key distribution (QKD)
Lo et al. 2012
;
Liu et al. 2013
;
Curty et al. 2014
and entanglement detection
Branciard et al. 2013
;
Rosset et al. 2018
;
Guo et al. 2020
.

The aim of this paper is
to propose an MDI protocol for beyond-quantum state detection, which is composed of
quantum input states on respective parties and
quantum measurements across the input system and the target system on respective parties as Fig.
1
.
To address the requirement for our protocol,
we will first discuss the following three criteria about the performance of the beyond-quantum state detection protocol, which we name as completeness, universal completeness and soundness,
while the words completeness and soundness were originally introduced in the context of
the verification
Hayashi and Hajdušek 2018
;
Li et al. 2023
.
Based on these concepts, we will propose our MDI detection protocol
for beyond-quantum state.

II
Relation with existing protocols

First, the studies

Branciard et al. 2013
;
Buscemi 2012
;
Abiuso et al. 2021

introduced a MDI Bell test
as a type of a generalized Bell test
by replacing the classical inputs of the standard Bell test by quantum input states.
MDI Bell test is not fully DI since it assumes quantum theory locally and requires the knowledge (trust) on the quantum input states. The additional assumptions provides a MDI Bell test some advantages over DI Bell test, e.g. it does not suffer the locality or detection loophole
Branciard et al. 2013
;
Rosset et al. 2018
. Moreover, it certifies certain properties of measurement operators (such as entanglement).

In a recent work, it was shown that a MDI Bell test can be used to detect beyond-quantum states
Lobo et al. 2022
. More specifically, the authors proved that for any given beyond-quantum state, it is possible to construct a MDI witness, which is a linear function of the correlations

\{p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})\}_{a,b,x,y}
. The construction of the witness is dependent on the form of the tested state and the measurement operators. However, in a practical MDI Bell test, the form of the tested state or measurement operators is usually not assumed to be known. In that case, the proper witness for detection can not be determined, which may greatly influence the detectability (the ability of detecting a given beyond-quantum state) of the protocol.

The difference between our protocol and that in
Lobo et al. 2022
lies in the method of processing the experimental correlations.
In our protocol, the detectability depends only on the experimental correlations, but not the forms of the tested state and the measurement operators. Such property provides our protocol an advantage when
the tested state and the measurement operators are unknown.
Here, we examine whether the existing detection protocols for beyond-quantum state satisfy the above criteria, and what assumptions they require.
For the device-dependent protocol proposed in Ref.
Arai et al. 2023
,
the knowledge of the forms of the measurement operators
is required to satisfy soundness, and
the form of the tested state is additionally required to satisfy completeness.
For the MDI protocol proposed in Ref.
Lobo et al. 2022
,
the knowledge of the form of quantum input states is required to satisfy soundness,
and
the knowledge of the forms of the tested state and the measurement operators is additionally required to satisfy completeness.
For our protocol, while the knowledge of the form of quantum inputs
is also required to satisfy soundness,
only the knowledge of the local dimensions of the tested state is additionally required to satisfy completeness. Also, since our protocol satisfies completeness without the knowledge of the tested state and the measurement operators, it satisfies universal completeness. In Table

III
Beyond-quantum state and MDI protocol

At first we briefly introduce the setting of a GPT model in the form used in Ref.
Arai et al. 2023
.

Definition 4
.

A model of GPT is defined by a tuple
G=(\mathcal{V},\langle,\rangle,\mathcal{C},u)
, where
(\mathcal{V},\langle,\rangle),\mathcal{C},u
are a real-vector space with inner product, a proper cone, and an order unit of
C^{*}
respectively.

Definition 5
.

The space
S(G)
given the GPT model
G=(\mathcal{V},\langle,\rangle,\mathcal{C},u)
is defined as

S(G):=\{\rho\in\mathcal{V}|\langle\rho,u\rangle=1\}.

(1)

Definition 6
.

A measurements is a family
\{M_{i}\}
satisfying
M_{i}\in C^{*}
and
\sum_{i}M_{i}=I
. Here
i
is called the outcome of a measurement, and the probability of obtaining outcome
i
is
p_{i}=\langle\rho,M_{i}\rangle
.

In this work we consider a less general but very practical GPT model, where each measurement operator can be written as a Hermitian matrix, or equivalently,
dim\mathcal{V}=d^{2}
for some integer
d>1
, then each state can be written as a Hermitian matrix of unit trace, and the inner product takes the form of trace
Gleason 1975
;
Busch 2003
;
Arai and Hayashi 2023b
. Also, in this work,
our scenario has two spatially separated experimenters, Alice and Bob, who can only perform local measurements. Let
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\mathcal{H}_{S})
denote the set of Hermitian matrices on a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{S}
, sometimes we also simply write it as
\mathcal{L}_{S}
and write
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{V})
as
\mathcal{L}_{SV}
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{+}
denote the positive semi-definite matrices among the corresponding set
\mathcal{L}
. Then the GPT model we consider in this work can be written as
G=(\mathcal{L}_{AB},\textrm{Tr},SEP(A:B)^{*},I)
, where
SEP(A:B)^{*}:=(\mathcal{L}_{A}^{+}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{B}^{+})^{*}
is the cone of (unnormalized) POPT states.

Now we formally introduce a MDI protocol and a MDI witness as its special case.
Their aim is to distinguish
two sets:
the set
\mathcal{Q}_{AB}
of quantum states in
\mathcal{L}_{AB}

and
the set
\mathcal{S}_{AB}
of states in
SEP(A:B)^{*}
, i.e.,
to discriminate
an element of
\mathcal{S}_{AB}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{AB}

from the set
\mathcal{Q}_{AB}
.

Similar to entanglement detection
Branciard et al. 2013
,
a MDI protocol
for a joint state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{Q}_{AB}
across Alice’s and Bob’s systems is given as follows.
In each round, Alice and Bob are given one of the quantum states in the sets

\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\}_{x}
,
\{\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{y}
on
auxiliary systems
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
, respectively.
They make quantum measurements

M_{A^{\prime}A}=\{M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}\}_{a}
and
M_{BB^{\prime}}=\{M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}\}_{b}
on system
A^{\prime}A
and
BB^{\prime}
respectively, obtaining outcomes
a
and
b
. The generated correlations is obtained by

p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})=\textrm{Tr}(\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\rho_{AB}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\cdot M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}\otimes M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}).

(2)

A MDI protocol outputs a certain outcome depending only on
the correlations
P:=\{p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})\}_{a,b,x,y}
.
During the process, Alice and Bob is allowed to communicate classically, and they can also post-process their measurement correlations. These actions do not affect the soundness of the detection.

A MDI witness is a special type of a MDI protocol,
and is constructed by using the correlations as below:

\mathcal{W}_{\beta}(P)=\sum_{x,y,a,b}\beta_{x,y,a,b}p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}),

(3)

where
\beta=(\beta_{x,y,a,b})_{x,y,a,b}

are some real coefficients dependent on
x,y,a,b
. When the input set
\mathcal{I}:=\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}
forms a tomographically complete set on
\mathcal{L}_{AB}
,
a MDI witness can correspond to an entanglement witness as follow: for any entanglement witness
W\in\mathcal{L}_{AB}
, it can be spanned using
\tau_{x,A}:=\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{T}}
,
\tau_{y,B}:=\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{T}}
as

W=\sum_{x,y}\alpha_{x,y}\tau_{x,A}\otimes\tau_{y,B}

(4)

where
\alpha_{xy}
are some real coefficients.
To recover the above entanglement witness
W
from
a MDI witness, we fix
one element
a_{0}
among Alice’s outcomes
and one element
b_{0}
among Bob’s outcomes,
and choose the coefficients
\beta_{x,y,a,b}
to be

\beta_{x,y,a,b}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{x,y},&\hbox{when }a=a_{0},b=b_{0}\\
0,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(5)

Then, we have
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}(P)\geq 0

when the tested state
\rho_{AB}
is separable. Also, when measurement operators take the form
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a_{0}}=\outerproduct{\Phi_{A'A}}{\Phi_{A'A}}
, where

\ket{\Phi_{A'A}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}\ket{i}\ket{i}

is the maximally entangled state in
\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}
, and analogously
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b_{0}}=\outerproduct{\Phi_{BB'}}{\Phi_{BB'}}
, we have

\mathcal{W}_{\beta}(P)=\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}\Tr(W\rho_{AB})

Branciard et al. 2013
. Therefore entanglement is detected if

\mathcal{W}_{\beta}(P)<0
.

In Ref.
Lobo et al. 2022
, the authors showed that MDI witness can also be used to detect beyond-quanutm states. The central point is that there exists a positive semi-definite matrix

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textrm{Tr}\rho_{AB}Y&<0,\\
\textrm{Tr}\sigma_{AB}Y&\geq 0\hbox{ for }\forall\sigma_{AB}\in\mathcal{Q}_{AB}.\end{split}

(6)

We will refer to such matrices

hereinafter. By substituting the entanglement witness

analogously, the MDI entanglement witness in (
3

becomes a MDI beyond-quantum state witness. Ref.
Lobo et al. 2022
reveals the strong potential MDI protocol has on detecting beyond-quantum states, but there are still problems to address when we come to a more practical scenario. More specifically, to witness a beyond-quantum state with (
3

\beta_{x,y,a,b}
is dependent on the knowledge of the form of the tested state
\rho_{AB}
and the measurement operators
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}
and
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
.

To see this dependence, for example, we focus on a two-qubit system
with two Bell states

\ket{\Phi_{+}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{00}+\ket{11})
,

\ket{\Psi_{-}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{01}-\ket{10})

and the partial transposition

\Gamma_{A}:=\textrm{T}_{A}\otimes I_{B}

on subsystem
A
, where

\textrm{T}_{A}
expresses the transposition
on subsystem
A
.
The witness
Y_{0}=\outerproduct{\Psi_{-}}{\Psi_{-}}

of
\rho_{0}:=\Gamma_{A}(\outerproduct{\Phi_{+}}{\Phi_{+}})

can be recovered by a MDI witness as follows.
When we choose
\beta_{x,y,a,b}
as (
5
) with
\alpha_{x,y}
satisfying

Y_{0}=\sum_{x,y}\alpha_{x,y}\tau_{x,A}\otimes\tau_{y,B}

(7)

and define the correlations
P=\{p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})\}_{a,b,x,y}
by (

)
with the measurement operators

,
our MDI witness

,

.
However, under the same setting (input set and measurement operators),
the same MDI witness

can not detect the beyond-quantum state

.
In practical MDI protocols, it is more common that we only know the local dimensions of

Inspired by a previous work
Rosset et al. 2018

We will show that this method satisfies universal completeness with only the knowledge about the dimensions of the local systems, and its detection is not influenced by the knowledge of the states and measurement operators.

IV
MDI protocol with SDP

An important observation which inspires our approach is that, although the knowledge of the tested state and the measurement operators are assumed to be known when constructing a MDI witness, neither of them is actually necessary. By processing Eq. (
2
) we obtain the relation

\begin{split}p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})=\Tr_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}(\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\Pi_{ab}),\ \forall a,b,x,y,\end{split}

(8)

where

\Pi_{ab}=\Tr_{AB}(I_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\rho_{AB}\otimes I_{B^{\prime}}M^{a}_{A^{\prime}A}\otimes M^{b}_{BB^{\prime}})

(9)

is some operator corresponding to
a,b
in
\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
. Eq. (
8
) allows us to reduce the detection of beyond-quantumness onto a set
\{\Pi_{ab}\}_{a,b}
of operators,
where each
\Pi_{ab}
could be understood as an effective state (unnormalized), and
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}
serve as effective measurement operators. It can be obtained from Eq. (
9
) that
\Pi_{ab}\geq 0
when
\rho_{AB}\geq 0
. Therefore if any
\Pi_{ab}
is found to be non-positive semi-definite, the tested state is beyond-quantum.

When the form of the tested state or the measurement operators are not known,
the form of
\Pi_{ab}
are also not known. In such a case, we propose
an SDP for beyond-quantum state detection as below:

\displaystyle\text{minimise:}

\displaystyle c_{ab}

(10)

\displaystyle\text{such that:}

\displaystyle c_{ab}=\text{Tr}X_{ab}^{-}

\displaystyle X_{ab}=X_{ab}^{+}-X_{ab}^{-}

\displaystyle X_{ab}^{+}\geq 0,X_{ab}^{-}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}(X_{ab}\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})=p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}),\forall x,y.

SDP (
10
) aims to reconstruct the least negative operators
X_{ab}\in\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
(with respect to the sum of negative eigenvalues) which can generate the experimental correlations via Eq. (
8
). It is not difficult to see that
c_{ab}>0
if and only if the projection of
\Pi_{ab}
onto the subspace spanned by
\{\tau_{x}\otimes\tau_{y}\}_{x,y}

is non-positive semi-definite. Therefore if there exists a pair of outcomes
(a,b)
such that
c_{ab}>0
, we conclude that the tested state is beyond-quantum.
In SDP (
10
), only the knowledge of the form of inputs
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}
is used together with the correlations to obtain the result
because
c_{ab}
is given as a function of inputs
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}
,
\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}
, and
the observed correlation
p(a,b|\tau_{x,A^{\prime}},\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})
.
The detectability of our method is independent of the knowledge of the tested state and the measurement operators since it exhausts all possible operators which can generate the correlations. So our method is also optimal in the sense that if it does not detect the beyond-quantumness with given correlations, no other method can detect it.

\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\}_{x}
and
\{\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{y}

Without loss of generality,
we can always assume that the dimension of the auxiliary systems is the same as the corresponding local systems, i.e.,

and
d_{B}=d_{B^{\prime}}
.

Theorem 1
.

When

M_{A^{\prime}A}
and
M_{BB^{\prime}}

are quantum measurements
on system
A^{\prime}A
and
BB^{\prime}
,
and

\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\}_{x}
and
\{\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{y}
are chosen to be sets of states in

and

and

Proof.

If the tested state
\rho_{AB}
is a quantum state, any effective states
\Pi_{ab}
is positive semi-definite. Since the constraints of SDP (

This indicates that

Proof.
We will first show that when the measurement operators

Generalized Bell measurement (GBM) operators has the form
M^{a}_{A^{\prime}A}=\outerproduct{\Lambda_a}{\Lambda_a}

\ket{\Lambda_a}=U_{A^{\prime}}^{a}\otimes I_{A}\ket{\Phi_{A'A}}

\ket{\Phi_{A'A}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i}^{d_{A}}\ket{i}\ket{i}
is the maximally entangled state. Then we have

M^{a}_{A^{\prime}A}=\frac{1}{d_{A}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{A}}U_{A^{\prime}}^{a}\outerproduct{i}{j}U_{A^{\prime}}^{a\dagger}\otimes\outerproduct{i}{j},

(11)

and
M^{b}_{BB^{\prime}}
can be obtained analogously. Since any state
\rho_{AB}
can be written in the same local basis as

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{d}a_{i,j,k,l}\outerproduct{i}{j}\otimes\outerproduct{k}{l}.

(12)

Substituting Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) into Eq. (
9
), we obtain the relation

\Pi_{ab}=\frac{1}{d_{A^{\prime}}d_{B^{\prime}}}U_{A^{\prime}}^{a}\otimes U_{B^{\prime}}^{b}\rho_{AB}^{T}U_{A^{\prime}}^{a\dagger}\otimes U_{B^{\prime}}^{b\dagger}

(13)

which indicates that
\Pi_{ab}
has the eigenvalues proportional to the eigenvalues of
\rho_{AB}
(note that
\rho_{AB}^{T}
here is a density matrix in
\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
).

Now we consider the case where the measurement operators take the form of more general pure states. Let the measurement operator
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}=\outerproduct{\psi_{a}}{\psi_{a}}
, where
\ket{\psi_{a}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}^{d}
is an arbitrary state with Schmidt rank
d_{A}
. Since
\ket{\psi_{a}}
can be written in Schmidt decomposition form as

\ket{\psi_{a}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{A}}a_{i}\ket{u_{i}}_{A^{\prime}}\ket{v_i}_{A},

(14)

it is always possible to find a proper generalized Bell state
\ket{\Lambda_a}_{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i}^{d_{A}}\ket{u_{i}}_{A^{\prime}}\ket{v_i}_{A}
and a positive semi-definite matrix
X_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}}
with the form

X_{a}=x_{i}\ket{u_{i}}_{A^{\prime}}\bra{u_{i}}_{A^{\prime}},

(15)

where
x_{i}=\sqrt{d_{A}}a_{i}
, such that

\ket{\psi_{a}}=X_{a}\otimes I_{A}\ket{\Lambda_a}_{\psi},

(16)

Since
\ket{\psi_a}_{\psi}
has Schmidt rank
d_{A}
, that is, all
a_{i}
are nonzero,
X_{a}
is invertible. Analogously we can obtain invertible positive semi-definite matrix
X_{b}
for a measurement operator
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
which has Schmidt rank
d_{B}
. Then we have

\Pi_{ab}=X_{a}U_{A^{\prime}}^{a}\otimes X_{b}U_{B^{\prime}}^{b}\rho_{AB}^{T}U_{A^{\prime}}^{a\dagger}X_{a}^{\dagger}\otimes U_{B^{\prime}}^{b\dagger}X_{b}^{\dagger},

(17)

where
U_{A^{\prime}}^{a}
is the local unitary that transforms a maximally entangled state
\ket{\Phi_{A'A}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{A}}}\sum_{i}^{d_{A}}\ket{v_i}\ket{v_i}
into generalized Bell state
\ket{\Lambda_a}_{\psi}
, i.e.
\ket{\Lambda_a}_{\psi}=U_{A^{\prime}}^{a}\otimes I_{A}\ket{\Phi_{A'A}}
.
U_{B^{\prime}}^{b}
is defined analogously.
In such a case,
\Pi_{ab}\geq 0
if and only if
\rho_{AB}\geq 0
.

Since the reconstructed
X_{AB}
will be exactly the same as
\Pi_{ab}
when
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\}_{x}
and

\{\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{y}
are tomographically complete sets. When
\rho_{AB}
is beyond-quantum,
\Pi_{ab}
has a negative eigenvalue, which means that
X_{ab}^{-}
must have positive eigenvalues, and we will obtain
c_{ab}>0
.∎

A natural question would be: is it always necessary to use tomographically complete input sets to attain universal completeness? We will discuss this question in the next section.

V
Necessary condition for universal completeness

In this section we show that it is necessary to use tomographically complete input sets on the composite system
{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}

to attain universal completeness
when the local dimensions of Alice and Bob is the same
(d_{A}=d_{B})
.
The local dimension is denoted by
d
.
We will construct a special subset
S
of beyond-quantum states,
for which the protocol is complete if and only if the input sets is tomographically complete. That is, it is impossible to detect all states in
S
unless the input set is tomographically complete.

In our protocol, instead of directly dealing with the tested state, we witness the beyond-quantumness of the effective states
\Pi_{ab}
with certain input set is
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}
using SDP (
10
). Therefore the minimum number of input states needed to detect a beyond-quantum state is determined by the minimum number of input states needed to detect the negativity of at least one effect state. More formally,
we say that
an input set
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}
detects
a beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}

when there exist two positive semi-definite operators

M^{a}_{A^{\prime}A}\in\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}A}
and
M^{b}_{BB^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{L}_{BB^{\prime}}

such that the effective state
\Pi_{ab}
generated by Eq. (
9
) satisfies
the following.
(i)
\Pi_{ab}
is a (unnormalized) beyond-quantum state.
(ii) There exists a witness
Y
of
\Pi_{ab}
, i.e.,
a Hermitian matrix
Y
satisfying (
6
),
with the form

Y=\sum_{x,y}\alpha_{xy}\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}

(18)

with certain coefficients
\alpha_{xy}
.
Conversely, a beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}
can not be detected if
no effective state
\Pi_{ab}
generated by
\rho_{AB}

satisfies the above two conditions.
For the simplicity of the following discussion, we first define two terminologies.

Definition 7
.

A family
S
of beyond-quantum states is called demanding
when the following condition holds:
an input set
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}

detects any state
\rho_{AB}\in S

if and only if the input set
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}

is tomographically complete.

Definition 8
.

A family
S
of beyond-quantum states
is called witness-demanding if the following condition holds:
For any element
\rho_{AB}\in S
, there exists
a witness
Y
of
\rho_{AB}
to satisfy Eq. (
18
)
if and only if the input set
\{\tau_{x,A}\otimes\tau_{y,B}\}_{x,y}
is tomographically complete.

We discuss the relation between demanding and witness-demanding as following.

Theorem 3
.

If a family
S
of beyond-quantum states
is witness-demanding, it is also demanding.

Proof.

Suppose
S
is not demanding.
Then, there exists a tomographically

incomplete
input set
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}

to detect any element
\rho_{AB}\in S
.
That is, for every
\rho_{AB}\in S
, there exist two positive semi-definite operators

M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}A}
and
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}\in\mathcal{L}_{BB^{\prime}}

such that the effective state
\Pi_{ab}
generated via Eq. (
9
) satisfies
the following:
(i)
\Pi_{ab}
is a (unnormalized) beyond-quantum state.
(ii) There exists a witness
Y
of
\Pi_{ab}
which satisfies (
18
) with the input set
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}
.

For operators
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}A}
, we can define the CP map
L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a}
from
\mathcal{L}_{h}(A)
to
\mathcal{L}_{h}(A^{\prime})
satisfying

L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a}(W_{A})=\Tr_{A}M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}(I_{A^{\prime}}\otimes W_{A})

(19)

for
W_{A}\in\mathcal{L}_{h}(A)
.

L_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
can be defined analogously.
We also define

L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a*}
and
L_{BB^{\prime}}^{b*}
as the adjoint maps of

L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a}
and
L_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
respectively.

Then for every
\rho_{AB}\in S
, we can define the Hermitian matrix

Y^{*}:=\sum_{x,y}\alpha_{xy}L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a*}(\tau_{x,A^{\prime}})\otimes L_{BB^{\prime}}^{b*}(\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})
. And we have

\begin{split}&\Tr\rho_{AB}Y^{*}\\
=&\sum_{x,y}\alpha_{xy}\Tr[\rho_{AB}L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a*}(\tau_{x,A^{\prime}})\otimes L_{BB^{\prime}}^{b*}(\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})]\\
=&\sum_{x,y}\alpha_{xy}\Tr[L_{AA^{\prime}}^{a}\otimes L_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}(\rho_{AB})\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}]\\
=&\Tr\Pi_{ab}Y<0.\end{split}

(20)

Eq. (
20
) shows that
Y^{*}
is a witness of
\rho_{AB}
. However, the set
\{L_{AA^{\prime}}^{*}(\tau_{x,A^{\prime}})\otimes L_{BB^{\prime}}^{*}(\tau_{y,B^{\prime}})\}_{x,y}
is tomographically
incomplete
on

\mathcal{L}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
since
\{\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}\otimes\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}\}_{x,y}
is tomographically
incomplete
.
Hence, the relation (
20
) contradicts the assumption that
S
is witness-demanding. Therefore
\rho_{AB}
must be demanding.
∎

We define the set
{\cal P}({\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}})

of pure states
|\psi\rangle
on
{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}}

whose maximum Schmidt coefficient
\mathop{\rm sc}(\rho)

is not greater than
\sqrt{3/2d}
.

Lemma 1
.

The linear space spanned by

{\cal P}({\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}})

equals the set
{\cal L}_{h}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
of Hermitian matrices on
{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

We define the operators
X,Z
on
{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle X:=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|j+1\rangle\langle j|,\quad Z:=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{j}|j\rangle\langle j|,

(21)

with
\omega:=e^{2\pi i/d}
.
We define
|\Phi\rangle:=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}|jj\rangle

and

|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle:=X^{j}Z^{k}\otimes I_{B^{\prime}}|\Phi\rangle
.
For
(j,k)\neq(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
and
c\in(0,1)
, we define

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|\sqrt{c}}\rangle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-c}|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle+\sqrt{c}|\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle

(22)

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|i\sqrt{c}}\rangle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-c}|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle+i\sqrt{c}|\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle.

(23)

The set
\{|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle\}
forms an orthogonal basis of
{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}}
.
Hence, it is sufficient to show the following:
for
j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}
,
there exist real numbers
c\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|\sqrt{c}}\rangle\langle\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|\sqrt{c}}|,|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|i\sqrt{c}}\rangle\langle\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|i\sqrt{c}}|

\displaystyle\in{\cal P}({\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}}).

(24)

More specifically,
since
\{|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle\}_{j,k}

forms an orthogonal basis of
{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}}
, and
\{\outerproduct{\Phi_{j,k}}{\Phi_{j,k}}\}_{j,k}\subset{\cal P}({\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}})

the set
{\cal D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})

of the diagonal matrices in
\mathcal{L}_{h}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
are included in the linear span of

{\cal P}({\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}})
. The quotient space
{\cal L}_{h}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})/{\cal D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})

\{|\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\Phi_{j,k}|+|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}|\}_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\cup\{i|\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\Phi_{j,k}|-i|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle\langle\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}|\}_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}

{\cal L}_{h}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})/{\cal D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
includes all off-diagonal elements, the other matrices in
\mathcal{L}_{h}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
are equivalent to the matrices in (
24
)
in the sense of this quotient space. Therefore
\mathcal{L}_{h}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
equals to the linear span of

{\cal P}({\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes{\cal H}_{B^{\prime}})
if (
24
) is satisfied.

We choose
c\in(0,1)
as

\sqrt{c}+\sqrt{1-c}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}
.
Then, we show that
the maximum Schmidt coefficients of

|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|\sqrt{c}}\rangle

and
|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|i\sqrt{c}}\rangle

are upper bounded by
(\sqrt{c}+\sqrt{1-c})/\sqrt{d}=\sqrt{3/2d}

as follows.
We choose
the simultaneous Schmidt basis

\{|v_{l,A}\rangle\}_{l=0}^{d-1}
,

\{|v_{l,B}\rangle\}_{l=0}^{d-1}
for
the vectors

|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle
and
|\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle
as
Hiroshima and Hayashi 2004

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j,k}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{i\theta_{1}(l)}|v_{l,A},v_{l,B}\rangle,

(25)

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{i\theta_{2}(l)}|v_{l,A},v_{l,B}\rangle.

(26)

Although
\theta_{1}(l)

Hence,

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|\sqrt{c}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(\sqrt{1-c}e^{i\theta_{1}(l)}+\sqrt{c}e^{i\theta_{2}(l)})|v_{l,A},v_{l,B}\rangle

\displaystyle|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|i\sqrt{c}}\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(\sqrt{1-c}e^{i\theta_{1}(l)}+i\sqrt{c}e^{i\theta_{2}(l)})|v_{l,A},v_{l,B}\rangle.

the maximum Schmidt coefficient of

Similarly,
since
|\sqrt{1-c}e^{i\theta_{1}(l)}+i\sqrt{c}e^{i\theta_{2}(l)}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}

the maximum Schmidt coefficient of

|\Phi_{j,k,j^{\prime},k^{\prime}|i\sqrt{c}}\rangle

Hence, we have shown that condition (
24

1

For

Now we discuss how many input states are required to at least detect one beyond-quantum state, and how to choose the input states in such a circumstance. We present a result in 2-dimensional case.

Theorem 5
.

Let
d_{A}=d_{B}=2
, and let
\{\cal{Z}\}
be the set of all matrices which are unitarily equivalent to the Pauli matrix
\sigma_{z}
.
If there exists a matrix
Z\in\cal{Z}
such that
\Tr(Z\tau_{x,A'})=0
for all input states
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}
, no beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}_{AB}
can be detected by the MDI protocol. The theorem works analogously for input states
\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}
.

Theorem
5
implies that, in order that our MDI protocol detects at least one beyond-quantum state with
d_{A}=d_{B}=2
,
Alice and Bob need to input at least 3 quantum states, respectively.
Additionally, the chosen input states should be linearly independent and non-orthogonal.

VI
conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we have proposed a practical MDI test to detect beyond-quantum states, which further shows the power of MDI protocol on such a task.
Compared to the previous work
Lobo et al. 2022
which also applies a MDI Bell test for the same task,
our method processes the experimental correlations in an optimal way, therefore greatly improves the detectability in practical scenarios where the form of the tested state, the measurement operators,
nor the input states is not known.
More specifically, our method attains the universal completeness only with the natural assumption of knowing the local dimensions of the tested system, instead of the tested states.

Also, due to the advantage that the detection result of our protocol is independent of the form of tested state and measurement operators,
we are able to study the importance of having tomographically complete quantum input set.
We have shown that, under the typical case where
d_{A}=d_{B}
,
the universal completeness can be attained
if and only if the input sets are tomographically complete.
We have also clarified the importance of
the choice of the input states
when the input set is tomographically incomplete.
We have presented an example in
d_{A}=d_{B}=2
cases to support this importance. All the above results are possible to be extended into continuous variable case based on the method proposed in Ref.
Abiuso et al. 2021
. We leave such extension for further work.

Further, as a byproduct,
using the relation between entangled states and beyond-quantum states,
we can derive an interesting observation for
MDI entanglement detection as follows.
In
d_{A}=d_{B}=2
case,
any beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{bq}

can be described by using
an entangled state
\rho_{AB}^{E}

with partial transposition, namely,
\rho_{AB}^{bq}=\Gamma_{A}\otimes I_{B}(\rho_{AB}^{E})
.
Then, their respective effective states
\Pi^{bq}_{ab}
and
\Pi_{ab}^{E}
have the relation

\Pi^{bq}_{ab}=\Gamma_{A}\otimes I_{B}(\Pi_{ab}^{E})

under the same measurement operators
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}
and
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
.
Therefore,
universal completeness in entangled state detection implies universal completeness in beyond-quantum state detection.
This implication yields that
the input set needs to be tomographically complete to attain universal completeness in entangled state detection
in the
d_{A}=d_{B}=2
case.
A similar kind of observation can be expected in more general cases.

Appendix A
Proof that LOCC will not produce false detection

Since a multi-round LOCC is composed of one-way LOCC
Chitambar et al. 2014
, it is sufficient to prove that any one-way LOCC does not produce false detection when the tested state is quantum. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{X})
denote the set of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}_{X}
. Any one-way LOCC operated on a tested system
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
can be represented as a quantum instrument composed of
n
completely positive (CP) maps
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i=1,2,...,n}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{i}^{A}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{i}^{B}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{i}^{A}
is a CP map on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
and
\mathcal{P}_{i}^{B}
is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
. Note that the condition applies to the case where the communication is from Alice to Bob. In the opposite direction case,
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{i}^{A}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{i}^{B}
.

(ii)
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a CPTP map.

For such a one-way LOCC, we have the following relation:

\begin{split}&\Tr[\mathcal{E}^{A^{\prime}A}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{BB^{\prime}}_{j}(I\otimes\rho_{AB}\otimes I)\cdot M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}\otimes M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}]\\
=&\Tr[\tau_{x}\otimes\rho_{AB}\otimes\tau_{y}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{A^{\prime}A*}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{BB^{\prime}*}_{j}(M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}\otimes M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b})]\\
=&\Tr(\tau_{x}\otimes\rho_{AB}\otimes\tau_{y}\cdot\hat{M}_{A'A}^{a(i)}\otimes\hat{M}_{BB'}^{b(j)})\end{split}

(30)

where
\mathcal{E}^{A^{\prime}A*}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}^{BB^{\prime}*}_{j}
are the adjoint CP maps of
\mathcal{E}^{A^{\prime}A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{F}^{BB^{\prime}}_{j}
, they map POVM operators
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}

and
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
into
\hat{M}_{A^{\prime}A}^{a(i)}
and
\hat{M}_{BB^{\prime}}^{b(j)}
, which are also POVM operators. Therefore the correlations generated with such a one-way LOCC can be represented as

p(a,b|x,y)=\Tr(\hat{\Pi}_{ab}^{i,j}\tau_{x}\otimes\tau_{y}),

(31)

where

\hat{\Pi}_{ab}^{i,j}=\Tr_{AB}(I\otimes\rho_{AB}\otimes I\cdot\hat{M}_{A^{\prime}A}^{a(i)}\otimes\hat{M}_{BB^{\prime}}^{b(j)}).

(32)

Eq. (
32

\rho_{AB}
is quantum,
\hat{\Pi}_{ab}^{i,j}
is positive semi-definite for every pair of outcomes
(a,b)

of SDP (
10

\rho_{AB}

Proof of theorem

Let
\Gamma_{A^{\prime}}
be the partial transposition on
A^{\prime}
.
Then, we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

When the dimensions of systems
A
and
B
are
d_{A}=d_{B}=2
,
any beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{bq}
in
\mathcal{S}_{AB}

can be written with a form

\rho_{AB}^{bq}=\Gamma_{A}(\rho_{1})+\rho_{2}
, where

\rho_{1}
and

{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

The proof of lemma
3
is essentially shown in Proposition 11 of Ref.
Arai et al. 2023

2\times 2
-dimensional case, the set of all extremal beyond-quantum states is given as
\{\Gamma(\rho)|\rho\text{ is pure entangled quantum states}\}\cup\{\rho|\rho\text{ is pure quantum states}\}
.

Now we come to the proof of theorem
5
.

Proof of theorem
5
.

As the assumption, we choose
a matrix
Z\in\cal{Z}

such that
\Tr(Z\tau_{x,A'})=0
for all input states
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}
.
Matrix
Z
can be represented as
Z=U\sigma_{2}U^{\dagger}
with a properly chosen local unitary
U
on
{\cal H}_{A^{\prime}}

(note that we denote Pauli matrices
I
,
\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{y}
,
\sigma_{z}
by
\sigma_{i}\ (i=0,1,2,3)
for simplicity).
Given a beyond-quantum state
\rho_{AB}^{bq}\in\mathcal{S}_{AB}

and measurement operators
M_{A^{\prime}A}^{a}
and
M_{BB^{\prime}}^{b}
,
a corresponding effective state

\Pi_{ab}\in\cal{L}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
is defined via Eq. (
9
).
In our MDI protocol, we use SDP (
10
) to find the least negative Hermitian matrix
X_{ab}
satisfying

\Tr(\tau_{x,A'}\otimes\tau_{y,B'} X_{ab})=\Tr(\tau_{x,A'}\otimes\tau_{y,B'} \Pi_{ab})

(33)

for all
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}

If for any possible effective state
\Pi_{ab}

\rho_{AB}^{bq}

33

When
\Pi_{ab}

3

W_{ab,1}
and

W_{ab,2}
on

(U^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})\Pi_{ab}(U\otimes I_{B})=\Gamma_{A}(W_{ab,1})+W_{ab,2}

The positive semi-definite matrix

W_{ab,1}=\sum_{kj=0}^{3}g_{kj}\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{j}.

(34)

\Gamma_{A}(W_{ab,1})=\sum_{k=0,1,3}\sum_{j=0}^{3}g_{kj}\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{j}-2\sum_{j=0}^{3}g_{2j}\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}.

(35)

\displaystyle(U^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})\hat{\Pi}_{ab}(U\otimes I_{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}(W_{ab,1})+2\sum_{j=0}^{3}g_{2j}\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}+W_{AB,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{AB,1}+W_{AB,2}\geq 0.

\displaystyle\Tr(\tau_{x,A'}\otimes\tau_{y,B'})2(U\otimes I_{B})\sum_{j=0}^{3}g_{2j}\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}(U^{\dagger}\otimes I_{B})

\displaystyle\Tr(\tau_{x,A'}\otimes\tau_{y,B'} \hat{\Pi}_{ab})=\Tr(\tau_{x,A'}\otimes\tau_{y,B'} \Pi_{ab})

for all
\tau_{x,A^{\prime}}

\tau_{y,B^{\prime}}

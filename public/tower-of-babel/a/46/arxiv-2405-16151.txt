Abstract
. We study the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process on the integer lattice. Under suitable constraints on the strength of the weak asymmetry of the dynamics, we prove moderate deviation principles for the fluctuation fields when the process starts from stationary measures. As an application, we obtain sample path moderate deviation principles for the occupation time of the process in one dimension.



1.
Introduction

The exclusion process is one of the most widely studied models in statistical physics and in the theory of interacting particle systems. In the dynamics, particles perform random walks on some graph subject to the exclusion rule, that is, there is at most one particle at each site. The theory of hydrodynamic limits studies the behaviors of large systems of interacting particle systems at a macroscopic scale. Law of large numbers, fluctuations and large deviation principles from hydrodynamic limits have been widely investigated so far, see
[
13
]
and references therein. Moderate deviations studies probabilities of rare events occurring with intermediate frequency, and fills the gap between large deviations and fluctuations
[
4
, Section 3.7]
. As far as we know, moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits was only considered in
[
8
,
23
,
24
]
for the symmetric simple exclusion process, and in
[
22
]
for a specific Ginzburg-Landau model.

One of the main purposes of this article is to study moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits for a more general class of exclusion processes,
i.e.
, the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process (WASEP) on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, where particles jump to the left at rate
1/(2d)
and to the right at rate
1/(2d)+\alpha n^{-\beta}/d
in the
i
-th direction for
1\leq i\leq d
. Here,
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
are two parameters, and
n
is the scaling parameter which will go to infinity. Notice that when
\alpha=0
, we recover the standard symmetric simple exclusion process. Under diffusive scaling (time sped up by
n^{2}
) and suitable constraints on
\beta
, we prove moderate deviation principles for the density fluctuation fields of the process when starting from the stationary measure, see Theorem
2.1
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
follows the ideas in
[
8
]
. For the upper bound, we investigate an exponential martingale associated with the density fluctuation fields of the process; for the lower bound, a perturbation of the WASEP is considered. Compared with
[
8
]
, due to the asymmetric part of the dynamics, we need to prove an extra super-exponential estimate, see Proposition
3.2
.

As an application of Theorem
2.1
, we further investigate occupation times of the WASEP. Stationary fluctuations for the occupation times, or more generally the additive functionals, of the exclusion process have been widely studied, see
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. We refer the readers to
[
15
]
for an excellent review of this topic. Large deviations for the occupation times of the exclusion process was considered in
[
3
,
16
]
. Very recently, moderate deviations for the occupation times were proved at any fixed time in the symmetric simple exclusion process
[
7
]
and in the Kawasaki dynamics with mixing conditions
[
9
]
. Under suitable constraints on
\beta
, we prove in Theorem
2.2
sample path moderate deviation principles for the occupation time of the WASEP in one dimension.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem
2.2
is to relate the occupation time to the density fluctuation fields of the WASEP. Then by Theorem
2.1
and the contraction principle, we obtain a variational formula as the moderate deviations rate functional of the occupation time, which can be solved explicitly.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we state the model and our main results. Moderate deviations upper and lower bounds from hydrodynamic limits are proved respectively in Sections
3
and
4
. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to the proof of moderate deviations for the occupation time.

2.
Model and results

The state space of the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process (WASEP) is
\mathcal{X}_{d}:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, whose elements are denoted by
\eta=\{\eta_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. We call a function
f
on
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
is local if its value depends on
\eta
only through a finite number of coordinates. Fix parameters
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
. The infinitesimal generator of the process is
L_{n}=n^{2}(L_{s}+\alpha n^{-\beta}L_{a})
, where for local functions
f:\mathcal{X}_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle L_{s}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big],

\displaystyle L_{a}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big].

Here,
n
is the scaling parameter,
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
after swapping the values of
\eta_{x}
and
\eta_{y}
, that is,
(\eta^{x,y})_{z}=\eta_{x}
if
z=y
,
=\eta_{y}
if
z=x
, and
=\eta_{z}
otherwise.

For a constant
\rho\in[0,1]
, let
\nu_{\rho}
be the Bernoulli product measure on
\mathcal{X}_{d}
with particle density
\rho
,

\nu_{\rho}(\eta_{x}=1)=\rho,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

It is well known that
\nu_{\rho}
,
\rho\in[0,1]
, are reversible for the generator
L_{s}
, and invariant for
L_{a}
, see
[
17
, Chapter VIII]
.

Throughout this article, we fix
\rho\in(0,1)
, and let
\eta^{n}(t)
be the process with generator
L_{n}
and with initial distribution
\nu_{\rho}
. Without confusion, we write
\eta(t)
for
\eta^{n}(t)
to make notation short. Fix
T>0
a time horizon. Denote by
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}
the probability measure on the path space
{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}}_{d})
associated with the law of the process
\{\eta(t)\}
, and by
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}
the corresponding expectation.

2.1.
Density fluctuation fields

In this subsection, we state the moderate deviation principles (MDP) from hydrodynamic limits. The rescaled density fluctuation field
\mu^{n}_{t}
of the process, which acts on Schwartz functions
H\in{\mathcal{S}}:={\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is defined as

\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle=\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}(t)-\rho)H(\tfrac{x-v_{n}tm}{n}),

where

v_{n}:=\frac{\alpha(1-2\rho)}{d}n^{2-\beta},\quad m=(1,1,\ldots,1).

For two real numbers
a,b
, denote
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\},a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
. We make the following assumptions on
\beta
and
a_{n}
.

Assumption 2.1
.

Assume

n^{d/2}\sqrt{\log n}\ll a_{n}\ll n^{d}\wedge n^{d+\beta-1}.

Assume further that

•

for
d=1
,

\beta>\frac{2}{3},\quad a_{n}\gg n^{1-\tfrac{\beta}{2}};

•

for
d=2
, there exists some
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
such that

\beta>\frac{1}{2},\quad a_{n}\gg n^{2-\beta}(\log n)^{\tfrac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{0}}};

•

for
d\geq 3
,

\beta>\tfrac{1}{2},\quad a_{n}\gg n^{d-\beta}.

Next, we introduce the moderate deviations rate function. For
H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the dual of the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}
, define the linear functional
l(\cdot,\cdot)
as

l(\mu,H)=\left\langle\mu_{T},H_{T}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mu_{0},H_{0}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mu_{s},\left(\partial_{s}+(1/2d)\Delta\right)H_{s}\rangle ds.

Above,
H_{t}(u)=H(t,u)
for
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}
is the Laplacian operator. The rate function
\mathcal{Q}(\mu):=\mathcal{Q}_{dyn}(\mu)+\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{dyn}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sup_{H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\{l(\mu,H)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{0}\left(\mu_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\{\left\langle\mu_{0},\phi\right\rangle-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right\},

where
\chi(\rho)=\rho(1-\rho)
, and

\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(u)]^{2}\,du\,ds,\quad\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(u)^{2}du.

We state the first main result of this article.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the sequence of processes
\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the MDP with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}
and with rate function
\mathcal{Q}(\mu)
. More precisely, for any closed set
C\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, and for any open set
O\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\big(\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}\in C\big)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in C}\mathcal{Q}(\mu),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\big(\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}\in O\big)\geq-\inf_{\mu\in O}\mathcal{Q}(\mu).

2.2.
Additive functionals

As an application of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the MDP for the additive functionals of the WASEP in dimension
d=1
. For any local function
f
on
{\mathcal{X}}_{1}
, define

\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f)=\frac{n}{a_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\{f(\eta(s))-\tilde{f}(\rho)\}ds,

where
\tilde{f}(\rho)=E_{\nu_{\rho}}[f]
. When
f(\eta)=\eta_{0}
, we simply write
\Gamma^{n}_{t}=\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f)
the occupation time of the WASEP. Invariance principles for the above additive functional was proved by Gonçalves and Jara in
[
10
]
. In particular, if
\beta>1
or
\rho=1/2
, then the limit is given by
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)
multiple of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
3/4
.

Now, we introduce the rate function for the MDP of the additive functional
\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f)
. Let
\{B_{t}^{3/4}:t\geq 0\}
be the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
3/4
, which has the following representation:

B_{t}^{3/4}=\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)dB_{s},

where
\{B_{t}\}
is the standard one dimensional Brownian motion, and

K(t,s)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}x^{-\frac{3}{4}}dx}}s^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{s}^{t}(u-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}u^{\frac{1}{4}}du,

see
[
18
, Proposition 2.5, Chapter 2]
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be the set of continuous functions
\gamma:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
\dot{\gamma}\in L^{2}([0,T])
satisfying that

\gamma(t)=\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)\dot{\gamma}(s)ds,\quad t\in[0,T].

Finally, if
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)\neq 0
, define

I_{\rm path}(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\dot{\gamma}(t)^{2}dt,\quad&\text{if }\;\gamma\in{\mathcal{H}};\\
+\infty,\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\quad I_{f}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)^{2}}I_{\rm path}(\gamma),

where
\sigma^{2}=4\sqrt{2}\chi(\rho)/(3\sqrt{\pi})
is the limiting variance of the occupation time, see
[
12
]
. Notice that
I_{\rm path}
is the large deviations rate function of the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
3/4
, see
[
5
, Theorem 3.4.12]
for example.

The following is the second main result of this article.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
d=1
and Assumption
2.1
hold. Assume further that
\rho=1/2
if
2/3<\beta\leq 1
.
If
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)\neq 0
, then the sequence
\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f):0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the MDP with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}/n
and with rate function
I_{f}(\cdot)
.
More precisely, for any closed set
C\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, and for any open set
O\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\big(\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f):0\leq t\leq T\}\in C\big)\leq-\inf_{\gamma\in C}I_{f}(\gamma),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\big(\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f):0\leq t\leq T\}\in O\big)\geq-\inf_{\gamma\in O}I_{f}(\gamma).

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: upper bound

3.1.
An exponential martingale

In this subsection, we introduce the following exponential martingale, which plays an important role when proving the upper bound (
2.1
). For
H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by Feynman-Kac formula
[
13
, Appendix 1.7]
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)=\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H_{t}\big\rangle-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}(\partial_{s}+L_{n})e^{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}ds\Big\}

is a martingale. We want to write the above martingale as a functional of the density fluctuation field.

In the following computations, we simply write
\eta_{x}=\eta_{x}(s)
when there are no confusions. By Taylor’s expansion and summation by parts formula,

(3.1)

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}n^{2}L_{s}e^{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[e^{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})(H_{s}(\frac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\frac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}))}-1\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2d}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\Delta_{n}H_{s}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{4dn^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})^{2}\big(\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big)^{2}+O_{H}\Big(\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d+1}}\Big)\Big\}.

Above,
O_{H}(a_{n}/n^{d+1})
is bounded by
C(H)a_{n}/n^{d+1}
which vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
since
a_{n}\ll n^{d}
, and
\nabla_{n,i}
and
\Delta_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Delta_{n,i}
are the discrete gradient and Laplacian respectively,

\displaystyle\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})

\displaystyle=n\big[H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big],

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})

\displaystyle=n^{2}\big[H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})+H_{s}(\tfrac{x-e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-2H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big].

Similarly,

(3.2)

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}\alpha n^{2-\beta}L_{a}e^{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha n^{2-\beta}}{d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\Big[e^{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(H_{s}(\frac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\frac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}))}-1\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})-\chi(\rho)]\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})+O_{H}(n^{-\beta})\Big\}.

We introduced
\chi(\rho)
in the last line since
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})=0
. Finally,

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}\partial_{s}e^{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\partial_{s}{H}_{s}\big\rangle-\frac{\alpha(1-2\rho)n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\eta_{x}-\rho]\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\Big\}.

To replace the above discrete gradient and Laplacian by the continuous ones, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r_{n}\gg a_{n}
be a sequence of real numbers, and let
f:\mathcal{X}_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be some local function. Then, for any
H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big(\tau_{x}f(\eta(s))-\tilde{f}(\rho)\big)H(s,\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty,

where
\tilde{f}(\rho)=E_{\nu_{\rho}}[f]
.

Proof.

By Markov’s inequality, we only need to show that for any
A>0
,

(3.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\frac{Aa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big(\tau_{x}f(\eta(s))-\tilde{f}(\rho)\big)H(s,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big|ds\Big\}\Big]=0.

By Jensen’s inequality and stationary of the process, the above expectation is bounded by

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dsE_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\Big|\frac{ATa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big(\tau_{x}f(\eta)-\tilde{f}(\rho)\big)H(s,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big|\Big\}\Big].

Using the basic inequalities
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
and

(3.4)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log(x_{n}+y_{n})=\max\Big\{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log x_{n},\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log y_{n}\Big\},

we can remove the absolute value inside the expectation. Since
\nu_{\rho}
is a product measure and
e^{x}\leq 1+x+(x^{2}/2)e^{|x|}
, for
n
large enough, we can bound the expectation by

\displaystyle\prod_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big(E_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{C(f)ATa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}r_{n}}\big(\tau_{x}f(\eta)-\tilde{f}(\rho)\big)H(s,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\Big]\Big)^{1/C(f)}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\frac{C(f,A,T,H)a_{n}^{4}}{n^{2d}r_{n}^{2}}\Big)^{n^{d}/C(f,H)}.

Thus, the limit on the left side of (
3.3
) is bounded by

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{C(f,A,T,H)a_{n}^{2}}{r_{n}^{2}}=0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Using the above lemma, we have

(
3.1
)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2d}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\Delta H_{s}\big\rangle+\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2dn^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big)^{2}+o_{H}(n)+\varepsilon^{n}_{1,H}(s)\Big\},

(
3.2
)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})-\chi(\rho)]\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})+o_{H}(n)+\varepsilon^{n}_{2,H}(s)\Big\}.

Above,
o_{H}(n)
denotes some constant depending on
H
and vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and for
j=1,2
, for any
\delta>0
,

(3.5)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{n}_{j,H}(s)ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

Since

(3.6)

\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})-\chi(\rho)-(1-2\rho)(\eta_{x}-\rho)=-(\eta_{x}-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}-\rho)+\rho(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}}),

by summation by parts formula and Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})-\chi(\rho)-(1-2\rho)(\eta_{x}-\rho)]\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})=-Q^{n}_{s}(H)+\varepsilon^{n}_{3,H}(s),

where
\varepsilon^{n}_{3,H}(s)
satisfies the estimates in (
3.5
), and

Q^{n}_{s}(H)=\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}(s)-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)-\rho)\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}).

To sum up, we have shown that

(3.7)

{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)=\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big(\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H_{t}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\big(\partial_{s}+\tfrac{1}{2d}\Delta\big)H_{s}\big\rangle ds\\
-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(u)\big)^{2}du\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}Q^{n}_{s}(H)ds+o_{H}(n)t+\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{n}_{H}(s)ds\Big)\Big\},

where
\varepsilon^{n}_{H}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\varepsilon^{n}_{j,H}(s)
.

The next result states that the time integral of
Q^{n}_{s}(H)
is superexponentially small uniformly in time as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}Q^{n}_{s}(H)ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

To prove the above result, we need to introduce more notation. For an integer
\ell>0
, let
p_{\ell}
be the uniform measure on
\Lambda_{\ell}^{d}:=\{0,1,\ldots,\ell-1\}^{d}
, and let
q_{\ell}
be the convolution of
p_{\ell}
with itself,

p_{\ell}(x)=\ell^{-d}\mathbf{1}\{x\in\Lambda_{\ell}^{d}\},\quad q_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p_{\ell}(x-y)p_{\ell}(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Notice that
q_{\ell}
is supported in
\Lambda_{2\ell-1}^{d}
. Let
\delta_{0}
be the Dirac measure at the origin. The following result was proved in
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists some function
\Phi_{\ell}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\delta_{0}(z)-q_{\ell}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\{\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{i})-\Phi_{\ell}(z-e_{i},e_{i})\}.

Moreover,
\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{i})=0
for
z\notin\Lambda_{2\ell-1}^{d}
, and there exists some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(d)
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{i})^{2}\leq C_{0}g_{d}(\ell),

where

g_{d}(\ell)=\begin{cases}\ell,\quad&\text{if }d=1,\\
\log\ell,\quad&\text{if }d=2,\\
1,\quad&\text{if }d\geq 3.\end{cases}

For
\eta\in{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
, define
\eta^{\ell}_{x}
as the spatial average of
\eta_{x+\cdot}
with respect to the weight function
q_{\ell}
,

\eta_{x}^{\ell}=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\eta_{x+z}q_{\ell}(z),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Finally, for an integer
\ell>0
, let

Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)=\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}(s)-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}(s)-\rho)\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}).

To prove Proposition
3.2
, we shall take
\ell=\ell(n)\leq n
such that

(3.8)

\ell^{d}g_{d}(\ell)\ll n^{2\beta},\quad\frac{n^{2d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\ll\ell^{d}.

Such
\ell
exists by Assumption
2.1
. Indeed, let

M_{n}=\begin{cases}n^{\beta}\wedge n,\quad&\text{if }d=1,\\
n^{2\beta/d}\wedge n,\quad&\text{if }d\geq 2,\end{cases},\quad m_{n}=\frac{n^{2}}{a_{n}^{2/d}}.

Then, Assumption
2.1
ensures that
M_{n}\gg m_{n}
and that for
d=2
,

\Big(\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}\Big)^{1-\varepsilon_{0}}\gg\log M_{n}.

Thus, one can take
\ell(n)=M_{n}^{\varepsilon_{0}}m_{n}^{1-\varepsilon_{0}}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

By (
3.4
), we only need to prove that, for any
\delta>0
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}[Q^{n}_{s}(H)-Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)]ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

We remark that the first constraint on
\ell
in (
3.8
) is used to prove (
3.9
), while the second one is used to prove (
3.10
).

We first prove (
3.9
). By Markov’s inequality, we only need to show that for any
A>0
,

(3.11)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{Aa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}[Q^{n}_{s}(H)-Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)]ds\Big|\Big\}\Big]=0.

Let
t_{i}=iT/n^{d+1}
for
0\leq i\leq n^{d+1}
. Since

|Q^{n}_{s}(H)-Q_{s}^{n,\ell}(H)|\leq\frac{C(H,d)n^{d+1-\beta}}{a_{n}},

we can replace the supremum over time
0\leq t\leq T
by over
\{t_{i}\}
in (
3.11
) with an error bounded by
C(H,A,d)/(a_{n}n^{\beta})
. Thus, we only need to show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq i\leq n^{d+1}}\Big|\frac{Aa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}[Q^{n}_{s}(H)-Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)]ds\Big|\Big\}\Big]=0.

Since for any
K
random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{K}
,

(3.12)

\log E\big[\exp\{\max_{1\leq i\leq K}X_{i}\}\big]\leq\log\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}E\big[\exp\{X_{i}\}\big]\Big)\leq\log K+\max_{1\leq i\leq K}\log E\big[\exp\{X_{i}\}\big],

and
a_{n}\gg n^{d/2}\sqrt{\log n}
, we can first put the supremum over
0\leq i\leq n^{d+1}
outside, then remove the absolute value, and finally only need to prove that

(3.13)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leq i\leq n^{d+1}}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{Aa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}[Q^{n}_{s}(H)-Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)]ds\Big\}\Big]=0.

By Feynman-Kac formula
[
13
, Appendix 1.7]
,

(3.14)

\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{Aa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}[Q^{n}_{s}(H)-Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)]ds\Big\}\Big]\leq\frac{\alpha An^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}ds\\
\sup_{f:\;\nu_{\rho}{\rm-density}}\Big\{\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}-\eta_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})f(\eta)d\nu_{\rho}-\frac{dn^{d+1+\beta}}{\alpha Aa_{n}}D(\sqrt{f})\Big\},

where the supremum is over local functions
f\geq 0
on
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
such that
E_{\nu_{\rho}}[f]=0
, and
D(\sqrt{f})
is the Dirichlet form of the function
f
with respect to the generator
L_{s}
under
\nu_{\rho}
,

D(\sqrt{f})=E_{\nu_{\rho}}[\sqrt{f}(-L_{s})\sqrt{f}]=\frac{1}{4d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\nu_{\rho}}\big[\big(\sqrt{f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})}-\sqrt{f(\eta)}\big)^{2}\big].

Since

\displaystyle\eta_{x+e_{i}}-\eta_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\eta_{x+e_{i}+z}[\delta_{0}(z)-q_{\ell}(z)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\eta_{x+e_{i}+z}[\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{j})-\Phi_{\ell}(z-e_{j},e_{j})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}[\eta_{x+e_{i}+z}-\eta_{x+e_{i}+z+e_{j}}]\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{j}),

by changing of variables
\eta\mapsto\eta^{x+e_{i}+z,x+e_{i}+z+e_{j}}
and Young’s inequality, the integral inside the supremum in (
3.14
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},z\in\Lambda_{2\ell-1}^{d}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{j})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)[\eta_{x+e_{i}+z}-\eta_{x+e_{i}+z+e_{j}}][f(\eta)-f(\eta^{x+e_{i}+z,x+e_{i}+z+e_{j}})]d\nu_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4B}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},z\in\Lambda_{2\ell-1}^{d}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\Phi_{\ell}(z,e_{j})^{2}\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})^{2}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{d}}\big[\sqrt{f(\eta)}+\sqrt{f(\eta^{x+e_{i}+z,x+e_{i}+z+e_{j}})}\big]^{2}d\nu_{\rho}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{B}{4}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},z\in\Lambda_{2\ell-1}^{d}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathcal{X}}_{d}}\big[\sqrt{f(\eta)}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{x+e_{i}+z,x+e_{i}+z+e_{j}})}\big]^{2}d\nu_{\rho}

for any
B>0
. By Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
f
is a
\nu_{\rho}
-density, the last expression is bounded by

\frac{C(H,d,C_{0})}{B}n^{d}g_{d}(\ell)+Bd^{2}(2\ell)^{d}D(\sqrt{f}).

Taking
B
such that

Bd^{2}(2\ell)^{d}=\frac{dn^{d+1+\beta}}{\alpha Aa_{n}},

we bound the limit on the left side of (
3.13
) by

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C(H,d,C_{0},A)\frac{\ell^{d}g_{d}(\ell)}{n^{2\beta}}=0,

thus proving (
3.13
).

Now, we prove (
3.10
). As before, we only need to prove that, for any
A>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{Aa_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\big|Q^{n,\ell}_{s}(H)\big|ds\Big\}\Big]=0.

By Jensen’s inequality, stationary of the process and (
3.4
), we only need to show that

(3.15)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}ds\\
E_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{ATa_{n}\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{dn^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}-\rho)\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\Big\}\Big]\Big)=0.

By definition,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}^{\ell}-\rho)\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}+z}^{\ell}-\rho)p_{\ell}(z-y)p_{\ell}(y)\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}).

Making the change of variables
z\mapsto z-x-e_{i},y\mapsto y-x-e_{i}
, the last line equals

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)p_{\ell}(y-x-e_{i})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\Big)\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta^{\ell}_{z}-\rho)p_{\ell}(z-y)\Big).

Notice that if
|y-y^{\prime}|:=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|y_{i}-y^{\prime}_{i}|>5\ell
, then for
\ell
large enough,

\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)p_{\ell}(y-x-e_{i})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\Big)\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta^{\ell}_{z}-\rho)p_{\ell}(z-y)\Big)

and

\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)p_{\ell}(y^{\prime}-x-e_{i})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\Big)\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta^{\ell}_{z}-\rho)p_{\ell}(z-y^{\prime})\Big)

are independent under
\nu_{\rho}
. By Hölder’s inequality, the expectation in (
3.15
) is bounded by

\prod_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Big(E_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{ATa_{n}\alpha n^{1-\beta}d(5\ell)^{d}}{n^{d}}\\
\times\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)p_{\ell}(y-x-e_{i})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\Big)\Big(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta^{\ell}_{z}-\rho)p_{\ell}(z-y)\Big)\Big\}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{d(5\ell)^{d}}}.

We say a random variable
X
is sub-Gaussian of order
\sigma^{2}
if

\log E[e^{\theta X}]\leq\frac{\theta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2},\quad\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, one can easily check that
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)p_{\ell}(y-x-e_{i})\partial_{u}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})
and
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta^{\ell}_{z}-\rho)p_{\ell}(z-y)
are both sub-Gaussian of order
C(H)\ell^{-d}
. Since by Assumption
2.1
,
a_{n}\ll n^{d+\beta-1}
, using
[
11
, Lemma F.8]
, for
n
large enough, the expectation in (
3.15
) is bounded by

C(A,T,H,\alpha,d)^{\frac{n^{d}}{\ell^{d}}}.

Thus, the limit in (
3.15
) is bounded by

C(A,T,H,\alpha,d)\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{2d}}{\ell^{d}a_{n}^{2}}=0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Proof of the upper bound

In this subsection, we prove the moderate deviations upper bound (
2.1
). The strategy is as follows: we first prove the upper bound for compact sets in
{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
, and then prove the exponential tightness of the sequence
\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}
. Finally, from the above two results, we conclude the proof of the upper bound for closed sets in
{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
immediately.

We start with the former. Let
K\subset{\mathcal{D}}([0,T];{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
be compact and
H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By (
3.7
) and Proposition
3.2
, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\mu^{n}\in K\Big)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\mu^{n}\in K,|{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{T}(H)|\leq\delta\}}\Big],

where

(3.16)

{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{t}(H)=\int_{0}^{t}Q^{n}_{s}(H)ds+o_{H}(n)t+\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{n}_{H}(s)ds.

On the event
\{\mu^{n}\in K,|{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{T}(H)|\leq\delta\}
, for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}
is bounded from above by

\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\sup_{\mu\in K}\Big(-l(\mu,H)+\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\delta-\mu_{0}(\phi)\Big)\Big\}\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\mu_{0}(\phi)\Big\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\mu^{n}\in K\Big)\leq-

\displaystyle\inf_{\mu\in K}\Big(l(\mu,H)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\mu_{0}(\phi)\Big)

\displaystyle+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\mu_{0}(\phi)\Big\}\Big]+\delta.

For the initial density fluctuation field, since
a_{n}\ll n^{d}
, by Taylor’s expansion,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\mu^{n}_{0}(\phi)\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\log E_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(x/n)\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\log\Big(1+\frac{a_{n}^{2}\chi(\rho)}{2n^{2d}}\phi(x/n)^{2}+O_{\phi}(a_{n}^{3}/n^{3d})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\chi(\rho)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

By first letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
, and then optimizing over
H\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\mu^{n}\in K\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\sup_{H\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\atop\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\inf_{\mu\in K}\Big\{l(\mu,H)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\mu_{0}(\phi)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Big\}.

Notice that the term inside the above brace is continuous and linear in
\mu
for fixed
H,\phi
, and continuous and concave in
H,\phi
for fixed
\mu
. Thus, by the Minimax theorem (see
[
8
]
for example), we can exchange the order of the above supremum and infimum. This concludes the proof of the upper bound (
2.1
) for compact sets.

It remains to prove that the sequence
\{\mu^{n}\}
is exponentially tight, which holds true once we can show the following two estimates:

(i)

for any
H\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

(3.18)

\limsup_{A\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle|>A\Big)=-\infty;

(ii)

for any
H\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.19)

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t+\tau}-\mu^{n}_{\tau},H\big\rangle|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty,

where
{\mathcal{T}}
is the set of all stopping times bounded by
T
.

For any
H\in{\mathcal{S}}
, we first rewrite (
3.7
) as

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)=

\displaystyle\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\tfrac{1}{2d}\Delta H\big\rangle ds

\displaystyle-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(u)\big)^{2}du\,ds+{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{t}(H),

where
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{t}(H)
is introduced in (
3.16
). Since the penultimate term on the right hand side is bounded by
C(H,d)t
, it satisfies the estimates in (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) obviously.
The second and the last terms on the right hand side also satisfy the above estimates by (
3.17
) and Proposition
3.2
. For the third one on the right hand side, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
H\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{A\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H\big\rangle ds\Big|>A\Big)=-\infty,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\Big|\int_{s}^{s+t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{r},H\big\rangle dr\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

The proof of the above lemma is the same as
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
, and thus is omitted.

It remains to deal with the martingale term. By Markov’s inequality,

\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}|\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)|>A\Big)\leq-A+\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)|\Big].

By (
3.7
), for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.21)

\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)^{\lambda}\Big]\leq C(H)|\lambda|(|\lambda|+1)ta_{n}^{2}/n^{d}.

Then, by Doob’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)|^{2}\Big]\leq 4\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{2}\Big]\leq 4e^{C(H)Ta_{n}^{2}/n^{d}}.

Thus,

\limsup_{A\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}|\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t}(H)|>A\Big)=-\infty.

For the estimate in (
3.19
), for any
\varepsilon>0
and for any
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}|\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t+\tau}(H)-\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{\tau}(H)|>\varepsilon a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\varepsilon+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\Big({\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{t+\tau}(H)/{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{\tau}(H)\Big)^{\lambda}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\varepsilon+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\Big({\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{\delta+\tau}(H)/{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{\tau}(H)\Big)^{2\lambda}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda\varepsilon+C(H)\lambda(\lambda+1)\delta.

Above, we used Doob’s inequality and (
3.21
). We conclude the proof by first letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
and then
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: lower bound

To prove the lower bound from hydrodynamic limits, we consider the following perturbation of the generator
L_{n}
. For any
H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define
L_{n}^{H}=n^{2}(L_{n,s}^{H}+\alpha n^{-\beta}L_{n,a}^{H})
, where for local functions
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle L_{n,s}^{H}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\big(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}}\big)\big(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big)\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big],

\displaystyle L_{n,a}^{H}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\big(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big)\big\}\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big].

Notice that we introduced a spatial translation to the function
H
in the above definition. For
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define
\nu_{\rho}^{n,\phi}
as the product measure on
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
with marginals

\nu_{\rho}^{n,\phi}(\eta_{x}=1)=\rho+\frac{\chi(\rho)a_{n}}{n^{d}}\phi(\tfrac{x}{n}),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Last, we denote by
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
the probability measure on
{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}}_{d})
induced by the process with generator
L_{n}^{H}
starting from the measure
\nu_{\rho}^{n,\phi}
, and by
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}
the corresponding expectation. By
[
13
, Proposition 7.3, Appendix 1]
, we have

(4.1)

\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}}{d\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}}={\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)\frac{d\nu_{\rho}^{n,\phi}}{d\nu_{\rho}}.

The following result concerns hydrodynamic limits for the measure
\mu^{n}=\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}
under
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
, whose proof is presented in Subsection
4.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, the sequence of measures
\{\mu^{n}_{t}(du):0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
converges in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
-probability to the absolutely continuous measure
\{\mu(t,u)du:0\leq t\leq T\}
, where
\mu(t,u)
is the unique weak solution to

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mu=\frac{1}{2d}\Delta\mu-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{d}\Delta H,\quad&\text{in}\quad(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\mu(0,\cdot)=\chi(\rho)\phi(\cdot),\quad&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

Here, we say
\mu
is a weak solution to (
4.2
) if for any
G\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for any
0<t\leq T
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(t,u)G_{t}(u)du=\chi(\rho)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(u)G_{0}(u)du\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(s,u)\Big(\partial_{s}+\frac{1}{2d}\Delta\Big)G_{s}(u)\,du\,ds+\frac{\chi(\rho)}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(u)\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(u)\,du\,ds.

Actually,
\mu
has the following explicit expression: for any
t\geq 0
and for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(4.3)

\mu(t,u)=\chi(\rho)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t/d}(u-v)\phi(v)dv-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{v_{i}}p_{(t-s)/d}(u-v)\partial_{v_{i}}H(s,v)\,dv\,ds,

where
p_{t}(u)
is the transition probability of the standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion

p_{t}(u)=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\Big\{-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{2t}\Big\}.

Before proving the lower bound, we also need to introduce some properties of the rate function
{\mathcal{Q}}
. For
H,G\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define the scalar product

[H,G]=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{u_{i}}H(t,u)\partial_{u_{i}}G(t,u)\,du\,dt.

Define further the equivalence relation
G\sim H
if and only if
[H-G,H-G]=0
. Last, let
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
be the Hilbert space defined as the completion of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})/\sim
with respect to the scalar product
[\cdot,\cdot]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)<+\infty
, then there exist
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}(\mu_{0})=\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},\quad{\mathcal{Q}}_{dyn}(\mu)=\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}[H,H].

Moreover,
\mu
is the unique weak solution of (
4.2
).

The proof of the above result uses Riesz representation theorem, and is standard, see
[
8
, Lemma 5.1]
for example. Indeed, if
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)<+\infty
, then there exists
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
such that

\big\langle\mu_{0},\psi\big\rangle=\chi(\rho)\big\langle\phi,\psi\big\rangle,\;\forall\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\quad l(\mu,G)=\chi(\rho)[H,G],\;\forall G\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1},

from which the above lemma follows easily. For this reason, we omit the proof.

Now, we are ready to prove the lower bound.

Proof of the lower bound (
2.2
).

Let
O
be an open set of
{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
. We only need to show that for any
\mu\in O
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}(\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}\in O)\geq-{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu).

Without loss of generality, we can assume
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)<+\infty
; otherwise, the last inequality is trivial. Let
\phi=\phi(\mu)
and
H=H(\mu)
be identified as in Lemma
4.2
. For
\delta>0
, choose
\phi^{\delta}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),H^{\delta}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\|\phi^{\delta}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\|H^{\delta}-H\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}=0.

Above,
\|H\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}:=\sqrt{[H,H]}
. Then, by (
4.3
) and Lemma
4.2
,
\mu^{\delta}=\mu^{\delta}(\phi^{\delta},H^{\delta})
converges, as
\delta\rightarrow 0
, to
\mu
in
{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
, and
\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu^{\delta})={\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)
. Thus, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
\mu^{\delta}\in O
with
\phi^{\delta}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),H^{\delta}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

-{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu^{\delta})\geq-{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)-o_{\mu}(\delta),

where
o_{\mu}(\delta)
is some constant depending on
\mu
and converges to zero as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. Therefore, we only need to show that, for
\delta
small enough,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}(\{\mu^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}\in O)\geq-{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu^{\delta}).

Since
\mu^{\delta}\in O
for
\delta
small enough, to make notations short, let us denote
\mu^{\delta}
by
\mu
, and then
\phi=\phi(\mu)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H=H(\mu)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By (
4.1
), we first write

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}(\mu^{n}\in O)=\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}}{d\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}}\mathbf{1}\{\mu^{n}\in O\}\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\frac{d\nu^{n}_{\rho}}{d\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}}\Big|\mu^{n}\in O\Big]\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}(\mu^{n}\in O).

Then, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}(\mu^{n}\in O)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\Big|\mu^{n}\in O\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{d\nu^{n}_{\rho}}{d\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}}\Big|\mu^{n}\in O\Big]+\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}(\mu^{n}\in O)=:\sum_{j=1}^{3}A^{n}_{j}.

The term
A^{n}_{3}
is simpler: by Theorem
4.1
, under
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
,
\mu^{n}\rightarrow\mu\in O
in probability as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A^{n}_{3}=0.

For
A^{n}_{1}
, by (
3.7
) and (
3.16
),

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}=-\Big(\ell(\mu^{n},H)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}[H,H]+{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{T}(H)\Big).

By Lemma
4.3
below,
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{T}(H)
converges in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
-probability to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Together with Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, the last line converges, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
-probability to

-\Big(\ell(\mu,H)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}[H,H]\Big)=-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}[H,H]=-{\mathcal{Q}}_{dyn}(\mu).

Last, by dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A^{n}_{1}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}(\mu^{n}\in O)}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\mathbf{1}\{\mu^{n}\in O\}\Big]=-{\mathcal{Q}}_{dyn}(\mu).

It remains to deal with
A^{n}_{2}
. Since
\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}
and
\nu^{n}_{\rho}
are both product measures,

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{d\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}}{d\nu^{n}_{\rho}}=\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\{\eta_{x}\log\Big(1+\frac{(1-\rho)a_{n}}{n^{d}}\phi(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)+(1-\eta_{x})\log\Big(1-\frac{\rho a_{n}}{n^{d}}\phi(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)\Big\}.

Since
|\log(1+x)-x+x^{2}/2|\leq Cx^{3}
for
x
small, the last line equals

\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(\tfrac{x}{n})-\frac{1}{2n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\big(\eta_{x}(1-\rho)^{2}+(1-\eta_{x})\rho^{2}\big)\phi(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}+O_{\phi}(a_{n}/n^{d}).

Notice that

\eta_{x}(1-\rho)^{2}+(1-\eta_{x})\rho^{2}=(1-2\rho)(\eta_{x}-\rho)+\chi(\rho).

Thus,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{d\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}}{d\nu^{n}_{\rho}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(\tfrac{x}{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{(2\rho-1)}{2n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(\tfrac{x}{n})^{2}-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+o_{\phi}(n),

where
o_{\phi}(n)
is a constant depending on
\phi
and vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Under
\nu_{\rho}^{n,\phi}
, the last expression converges in probability to
\chi(\rho)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}/2
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
As we dealt with
A^{n}_{1}
, by dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A^{n}_{2}=-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=-{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}(\mu_{0}).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
4.1
. By Dynkin’s martingale formula, under
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
, for any
G\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,2}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle M^{n}_{t}(G)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},G_{t}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},G_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{s}+L^{H}_{n})\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle ds,

(4.6)

\displaystyle M^{n}_{t}(G)^{2}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\{L^{H}_{n}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle^{2}-2\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle L^{H}_{n}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle\big\}ds

are both martingales.

We first calculate the time integral in (
4.5
). For the part associated to the symmetric generator
L^{H}_{n,s}
,

\displaystyle n^{2}L^{H}_{n,s}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle=\frac{n^{2}}{2da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}))\Big\}

\displaystyle\times(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\big(G_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big).

Using the basic inequality
|e^{x}-1-x|\leq(x^{2}/2)e^{|x|}
, and the summation by parts formula, the term on the right-hand side of the last expression equals

(4.7)

\frac{1}{2da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\Delta_{n}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\\
+\frac{1}{2dn^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})^{2}\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})+o_{H,G}(n),

where
o_{H,G}(n)
is some constant depending on
H,G
and vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Similarly, for the asymmetric part,

\alpha n^{2-\beta}L^{H}_{n,a}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle\\
=\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}))\Big\}\nabla_{n,i}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}).

Using the inequality
|e^{x}-1|\leq|x|e^{|x|}
, the last line equals

(4.8)

\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})-\chi(\rho)\big)\nabla_{n,i}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}s}{n})+o_{H,G}(n).

By Lemma
3.1
, we can replace
\nabla_{n,i}G_{s}
by
\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}
in the last expression, producing an error term
\varepsilon^{n}_{4,G}(s)
that is super-exponentially small under the measure
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}
. For the time derivative,

\partial_{s}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle=\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\partial_{s}G_{s}\big\rangle-\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(1-2\rho)(\eta_{x}-\rho)\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}).

By (
3.6
), summation by parts formula and Lemma
3.1
,

(\partial_{s}+\alpha n^{2-\beta}L^{H}_{n,a})\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},G_{s}\big\rangle\\
=\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\partial_{s}G_{s}\big\rangle-\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}-\rho)\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})+\varepsilon^{n}_{5,G}(s)+o_{H,G}(n),

where
\varepsilon^{n}_{5,G}(s)
satisfies the estimates in (
3.5
). To sum up, the time integral in (
4.5
) equals

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\Big(\partial_{s}+\frac{1}{2d}\Delta_{n}\Big)G_{s}\big\rangle ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2dn^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)^{2}\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})ds\\
&-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}(s)-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)-\rho)\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})ds\\
&+o_{H,G}(n)+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=4,5}\varepsilon^{n}_{j,G}(s)ds.\end{split}

A simple calculation also shows that the time integral in (
4.6
) equals

\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\frac{1}{2da_{n}^{2}}(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s))^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{dn^{\beta}a_{n}^{2}}\eta_{x}(s)(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s))\Big)\\
\times\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)\big(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}-v_{n}sm}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\big)\big\}(\nabla_{n,i}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n}))^{2}ds.

By Doob’s inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, since
a_{n}\gg n^{d/2}
,

(4.10)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}M^{n}_{t}(G)^{2}\Big]\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{C(H,G)T^{2}n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}=0.

To deal with (

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\mathcal{D}}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}}_{d})
satisfy

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}({\mathcal{A}})=-\infty.

Then,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}({\mathcal{A}})=0.

Proof.

For any two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on the same space, recall that the relative entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
is defined as

H(\mu|\nu)=\int\log\Big(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\Big)d\mu

if
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
, and
H(\mu|\nu)=+\infty
otherwise.

We claim that

(4.11)

H(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}|\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,0})=H(\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}|\nu_{\rho})\leq\frac{K_{0}a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}

for some constant
K_{0}=K_{0}(\phi)>0
. Indeed, by (
4.4
),

\Big|\log\frac{d\nu_{\rho}^{n,\phi}}{d\nu_{\rho}}-\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(x/n)\Big|\leq C(\phi)\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}.

Thus,

\Big|H(\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}|\nu_{\rho})-\int\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(x/n)d\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}\Big|\leq C(\phi)\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}.

We conclude the proof by noting that

\Big|\int\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\rho)\phi(x/n)d\nu^{n,\phi}_{\rho}\Big|=\frac{a_{n}^{2}\chi(\rho)}{n^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\phi(x/n)^{2}\leq C(\phi)\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}.

By entropy inequality (see
[
13
, Proposition 8.2, Appendix 1]
),

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}({\mathcal{A}})\leq\frac{\log 2+H(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}|\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,0})}{\log[1+1/\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,0}({\mathcal{A}})]}.

Thus, we conclude the proof once we can show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,0}({\mathcal{A}})=-\infty.

To prove it, by (
4.1
), Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (
3.21
),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,0}({\mathcal{A}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)\mathbf{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{2a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{2}\big]+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{2a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}({\mathcal{A}})=-\infty.

The following statements hold.

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}[J(\mu^{n}_{t})]\leq\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}H(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}|\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,0})+\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,0}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}J(\mu^{n}_{t})\Big\}\Big].

. By the definition of
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,0}
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (
3.21
),

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,0}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}J(\mu^{n}_{t})\Big\}\Big]=\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}J(\mu^{n}_{t})\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{d}}{2a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{T}(H)^{2}\Big]+\frac{n^{d}}{2a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{2a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}J(\mu^{n}_{t})\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C(H)T+\frac{n^{d}}{2a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{2a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}J(\mu^{n}_{t})\Big\}\Big].

It remains to deal with the second term on the right side in the last expression. Since
\nu_{\rho}
is invariant for the WASEP and is a product measure, the measure
\mu^{n}_{t}
satisfies the large deviations principle with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}
and with rate function

I_{0}(\mu)=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{S}}}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(u)\mu(du)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log M_{\rho}(f(u))du\Big\},

where, for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

M_{\rho}(\theta)=E_{\nu_{\rho}}[e^{\theta(\eta_{0}-\rho)}]=\rho e^{\theta(1-\rho)}+(1-\rho)e^{-\theta\rho}.

Moreover, the rate function
I_{0}
is equal to

(4.12)

I_{0}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\mu(u))du,\quad&\text{if }\mu(du)=\mu(u)du,\\
+\infty,\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
h
is the Legendre transform of
M_{\rho}
,

h(\gamma)=\sup_{\theta}\big\{\theta\gamma-\log M_{\rho}(\theta)\big\}.

Thus, by Laplace-Varadhan Theorem (see
[
13
, Theorem 3.1, Appendix 2]
),

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{2a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{2a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}J(\mu^{n}_{t})\Big\}\Big]\leq\sup_{\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}}\big\{J(\mu)-I_{0}(\mu)/2\big\}.

Formally, taking
J=I_{0}/2
, the last line is zero, and thus

E_{Q^{*}}[I_{0}(\mu_{t})]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}[I_{0}(\mu^{n}_{t})]\leq 2[K_{0}+C(H)T].

Thus, by Fubini’s Theorem,

E_{Q^{*}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}I_{0}(\mu_{t})dt\Big]=\int_{0}^{T}E_{Q^{*}}[I_{0}(\mu_{t})]dt\leq 2[K_{0}T+C(H)T^{2}].

Together with (
4.12
),
Q^{*}
concentrates on measures which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure for
t\in[0,T]
almost everywhere. We conclude the proof by changing the measure
\mu_{t}(du)
is a time set of measure zero if necessary.

(3) To characterize the limit point, for any
G\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,2}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, by (
4.5
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big(

\displaystyle\Big|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},G_{t}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},G_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\Big(\partial_{s}+\frac{1}{2d}\Delta_{n}\Big)G_{s}\big\rangle ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2dn^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)^{2}\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\alpha n^{1-\beta}}{da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\eta_{x}(s)-\rho)(\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)-\rho)\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(\tfrac{x-v_{n}sm}{n})ds\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=0.

By Lemmas
4.3
,
3.1
, and Proposition
3.2
, we could replace
\Delta_{n}G_{s}
by
\Delta G_{s}
in the third term inside the absolute value, replace
\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)^{2}
by
2\chi(\rho)
in the fourth one, and the last term inside the absolute value vanishes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
-probability.
Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big(\Big|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},G_{t}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},G_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\Big(\partial_{s}+\frac{1}{2d}\Delta\Big)G_{s}\big\rangle ds\\
-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{u_{i}}H_{s}(u)\partial_{u_{i}}G_{s}(u)duds\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

In this section, we assume
d=1
. The following lemma states that if
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)\neq 0
, then we only need to consider the MDP for the occupation time.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)\neq 0
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\Gamma^{n}_{t}(f)-\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\rho)\Gamma^{n}_{t}\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

The above result was proved in
[
7
, Section 5]
without the supremum over time inside the parenthesis. However, since
a_{n}\gg\sqrt{n\log n}
, by dividing the time interval
[0,T]
into small time intervals as in Proposition
3.2
, it is not hard to extend the result to be uniform in time. For this reason, we omit the proof.

In the rest of this section, we prove the sample path MDP for the occupation time. In subsection
5.1
, we show the sequence
\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
is exponentially tight. Subsections
5.2
and
5.3
are devoted to the proof of the finite-dimensional MDP of the occupation time. We conclude the proof of the sample path MDP in Subsection
5.4
. Finally, in Subsection
5.5
, we calculate a related variational problem.

5.1.
Exponential tightness

The exponential tightness of the sequence
\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
follows directly from the following result and Lemma
3.4
. Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
have support contained in
(0,1)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(u)du=1
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, define
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(u)=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi(u/\varepsilon)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume
\beta>1
or
\rho=1/2
. Then, for any
\delta>0
,

(5.1)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\Gamma^{n}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

Proof.

For any configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, let
(\eta\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{x}
be the discrete convolution of
\eta
with
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
,

(\eta\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y}\eta_{x+y}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y/n),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then, it suffices to show that for any
\delta>0
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{n}{a_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}[\eta_{0}(s)-(\eta(s)\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{0}]ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\frac{n}{a_{n}}\big[(\eta(s)\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{0}-\rho\big]-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle\Big\}ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty.

We first prove (
5.3
). Notice that

\frac{n}{a_{n}}\big[(\eta(s)\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{0}-\rho\big]-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle=\frac{-v_{n}s}{na_{n}}\sum_{y}(\eta_{y}(s)-\rho)\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\theta_{n}(y))+O_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}(1/a_{n})

for some
\theta_{n}(y)
between
y/n
and
(y-v_{n}s)/n
. Thus, (
5.3
) holds if
\beta>1
by Lemma
3.1
, and also holds if
\rho=1/2
since
v_{n}=0
in this case.

The proof of (
5.2
) is similar to (
3.9
), so we only sketch it. By Chebyshev’s inequality, we only need to show that, for any
A>0
,

(5.4)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|a_{n}A\int_{0}^{t}[\eta_{0}(s)-(\eta(s)\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{0}]ds\Big|\Big\}\Big]=0.

Take
t_{i}=iT/n
for
0\leq i\leq n
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, since
a_{n}\gg\sqrt{n\log n}
, by Feynman-Kac formula, the last limit is bounded by

(5.5)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,T\sup_{f:\nu_{\rho}{\rm-density}}\Big\{\frac{An}{a_{n}}\int[\eta_{0}-(\eta\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{0}]fd\nu_{\rho}-\frac{n^{3}}{a_{n}^{2}}D(\sqrt{f})\Big\}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\eta_{0}-(\eta\ast\varphi^{n}_{\varepsilon})_{0}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y=0}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{z=0}^{y-1}(\eta_{z}-\eta_{z+1})\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y/n)+O_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}(1/n),

where
O_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}(1/n)\leq C/n
for some constant
C=C(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
. By making change of variables
\eta\mapsto\eta^{z,z+1}
, the first term inside the supremum in (
5.5
) equals

\frac{A}{2a_{n}}\sum_{y=0}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{z=0}^{y-1}\int(\eta_{z}-\eta_{z+1})(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{z,z+1}))\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y/n)d\nu_{\rho}+O_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}(A/a_{n}).

By Young’s inequality, for any
B>0
, the first term in the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{A}{4Ba_{n}}\sum_{y=0}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{z=0}^{y-1}\int(\sqrt{f(\eta)}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{z,z+1})})^{2}d\nu_{\rho}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{BA}{4a_{n}}\sum_{y=0}^{\varepsilon n}\sum_{z=0}^{y-1}\int(\sqrt{f(\eta)}+\sqrt{f(\eta^{z,z+1})})^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y/n)^{2}d\nu_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A\varepsilon n}{Ba_{n}}D(\sqrt{f})+\frac{C(\varphi)BAn^{2}}{a_{n}}.

Taking
B=A\varepsilon a_{n}/n^{2}
, we bound the expression in (
5.5
) by
C(\varphi)TA^{2}\varepsilon+TO_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}(A/a_{n})
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

5.2.
Finite-dimensional upper bound

In this subsection, we prove the upper bound for the MDP of the occupation time in the sense of finite-dimensional distributions. For any integer
k>0
, denote
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. For
t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{k}
, let
A=A(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})=(a(t_{i},t_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
be the
k\times k
matrix with
a(s,t)
being the covariance function of the fractional Brownian motion with parameter
3/4
,

a(s,t)=\frac{1}{2}\big(t^{3/2}+s^{3/2}-|t-s|^{3/2}\big),\quad s,t\geq 0.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix integer
k>0
. For any closed set
F_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq k
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\Gamma^{n}_{t_{i}}\in F_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big)\leq-\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}:\alpha_{i}\in F_{i},1\leq i\leq k\Big\},

where
\sigma^{2}=4\sqrt{2}\chi(\rho)/(3\sqrt{\pi})
.

Proof.

For any
\delta>0
and any set
F\subset\mathbb{R}
, let
F^{\delta}:=\{x:|y-x|\leq\delta\text{ for some }y\in F\}
be the
\delta
-dilation of the set
F
. By Lemma
5.2
, the limit on the left side in the lemma is bounded by, for any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big).

Since the mapping
\mu\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds
is continuous, by Theorem
2.1
, the last line is bounded by

(5.6)

-\sup_{\delta>0}\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

Define

(5.7)

{\mathcal{A}}=\Big\{\mu:\mu\;\text{given by \eqref{mu formula} with }\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\text{ and }H(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}H(t,\cdot)\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}),\forall t\in[0,T]\Big\}.

We claim that (
5.6
) is bounded from above by

(5.8)

-\inf_{\alpha_{i}\in F_{i},1\leq i\leq k}\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\mu\in{\mathcal{A}},\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds=\alpha_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

We then conclude the proof of the lemma by using Proposition
5.4
below.

For the claim, it is trivial if the infimum in (
5.6
) is infinity, so we only need to consider the
\mu
’s such that
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)<+\infty
. Then, by Lemma
4.2
,
\mu
is the unique weak solution to (
4.2
). For
\varepsilon>0
, define
\mu_{s}^{\varepsilon}=\mu_{s}\ast\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
as the convolution of
\mu_{s}
with
\bar{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(-\cdot)
. By definition,
\big\langle\mu_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle=\mu_{s}^{\varepsilon}(0)
. Since
{\mathcal{Q}}
is convex, we have
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu^{\varepsilon})\leq{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)
. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}\geq\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\},

which permits us to bound (
5.6
) from above by

(5.9)

-\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds\in F_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

For any
\delta>0
, for any
H\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
and any
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, by Lemma
4.2
and (
4.3
), there exists
H^{\delta}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu)\geq{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu^{\delta})-\delta,\quad\big|\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)-\mu_{s}^{\delta}(0)ds\big|\leq\delta,\;1\leq i\leq k.

Since
\mu^{\delta}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we bound (
5.9
) from above by

-\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\mu\in{\mathcal{A}},\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds\in F_{i}^{\delta},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}+\delta.

Finally, we prove the claim by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and for any
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{k}\leq T
,

(5.10)

\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\mu\in{\mathcal{A}},\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds=\alpha_{i},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

The proof of the above result is postponed to Subsection
5.5
.

5.3.
Finite-dimensional lower bound

The next result concerns the lower bound for the MDP of the occupation time in the sense of finite-dimensional distributions.

Lemma 5.5
.

Fix integer
k>0
. For any closed set
O_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq k
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\Gamma^{n}_{t_{i}}\in O_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big)\geq-\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}:\alpha_{i}\in O_{i},1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

Proof.

For any
\alpha_{i}\in O_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, and for any small
\delta>0
such that
(\alpha_{i}-\delta,\alpha_{i}+\delta)\in O_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, by Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\Gamma^{n}_{t_{i}}\in O_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big)\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\Gamma^{n}_{t_{i}}\in(\alpha_{i}-\delta,\alpha_{i}+\delta),\;1\leq i\leq k\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in(\alpha_{i}-\delta/2,\alpha_{i}+\delta/2),\;1\leq i\leq k\Big).

By Theorem
2.1
, the last expression is bounded from below by

-\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf\big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in(\alpha_{i}-\delta/2,\alpha_{i}+\delta/2),\;1\leq i\leq k\big\}.

Let
\hat{\mu}
be the minimizer of the variational problem

\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\mu\in{\mathcal{A}},\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds=\alpha_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big\}.

It is constructed in the proof of Proposition
5.4
, and satisfies that

{\mathcal{Q}}(\hat{\mu})=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

Since for any
1\leq i\leq k
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big(\hat{\mu}_{s}(0)-\big\langle\hat{\mu}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle\big)ds=0,\quad\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\hat{\mu}_{s}(0)ds=\alpha_{i},

there exists some
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,\hat{\mu})
such that for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and for any
1\leq i\leq k
,

\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\hat{\mu}_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in(\alpha_{i}-\delta/2,\alpha_{i}+\delta/2).

Therefore, for
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

\inf\big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\big\langle\mu_{s},\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\rangle ds\in(\alpha_{i}-\delta/2,\alpha_{i}+\delta/2),\;1\leq i\leq k\big\}\leq{\mathcal{Q}}(\hat{\mu}).

This implies that for any
\alpha_{i}\in O_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho}\Big(\Gamma^{n}_{t_{i}}\in O_{i},\;1\leq i\leq k\Big)\geq-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

We conclude the proof by optimizing over
\alpha_{i}\in O_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
.
∎

5.4.
Concluding the proof of Theorem
2.2

In the last three subsections, we have shown the MDP for the occupation time in the sense of finite-dimensional distributions and exponential tightness of the process
\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}
. By
[
6
, Theorem 4.28]
, the process
\{\Gamma^{n}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}
satisfies the MDP with rate function

\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sup\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}:k\geq 1,0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{k}\leq T\Big\}

for any
\alpha\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,T])
, where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha(t_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(t_{k}))^{T}
and
A=(a(t_{i},t_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
. The last supremum is exactly the large deviation rate function of the fractional Brownian motion
\{n^{-1/2}B^{3/4}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}
, and thus equals
I_{\rm path}
. This concludes the proof.

5.5.
Proof of Proposition
5.4

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step
1
.
We first show that, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

(5.11)

\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\mu\in{\mathcal{A}},\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{s}(0)ds=\alpha\Big\}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}T^{3/2}}=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}\alpha^{2}}{8\sqrt{2}T^{3/2}\chi(\rho)}.

By (
4.3
), the constraint
\int_{0}^{T}\mu_{s}(0)ds=\alpha
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\chi(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s}(v)\phi(v)\,dv\,ds-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{\prime}_{s-r}(v)\partial_{v}H(r,v)\,dv\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}p_{s}(v)ds\Big)\phi(v)\,dv-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds\Big)\partial_{v}H(r,v)\,dv\,dr.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\chi(\rho)^{2}}\leq\big\{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+[H,H]\big\}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}p_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv\,dr\Big\}.

Moreover, the equality holds at the point
(\phi^{T,\alpha},H^{T,\alpha})
, where

\phi^{T,\alpha}(v)=c_{0}\int_{0}^{T}p_{s}(v)ds,\quad\partial_{v}H^{T,\alpha}(r,v)=-c_{0}\int_{0}^{T-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds,

with the constant
c_{0}
determined by

(5.12)

\frac{\alpha}{\chi(\rho)}=c_{0}\,\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}p_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv\,dr\Big\}.

Notice that
\phi^{T,\alpha},H^{T,\alpha}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. At the end of this step, we show that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}p_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{4(2-\sqrt{2})T^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}},

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{8(\sqrt{2}-1)T^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}.

Thus,

(5.15)

c_{0}=c_{0}(T,\alpha)=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4\sqrt{2}T^{3/2}}\frac{\alpha}{\chi(\rho)}.

Then, we conclude the proof of (
5.11
) by Lemma
4.2
.

It remains to prove (
5.13
) and (
5.14
). For (
5.13
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}p_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ts}}e^{-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}(\tfrac{1}{t}+\tfrac{1}{s})}.

Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}(\tfrac{1}{t}+\tfrac{1}{s})}=\sqrt{\frac{ts}{t+s}},

the last expression equals

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+s}}=\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\big[(T+s)^{1/2}-s^{1/2}\big]=\frac{4(2-\sqrt{2})T^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}.

For (
5.14
), notice that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds\Big)^{2}dv\,dr=\int_{0}^{T}dr\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\int_{0}^{T-r}ds\int_{0}^{T-r}dt\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{v^{2}}{(ts)^{3/2}}e^{-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}(\tfrac{1}{t}+\tfrac{1}{s})}.

Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\frac{v^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\tfrac{v^{2}}{2}(\tfrac{1}{t}+\tfrac{1}{s})}=\Big(\frac{ts}{t+s}\Big)^{3/2},

the last expression equals

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}dr\int_{0}^{T-r}ds\int_{0}^{T-r}dt\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(t+s)^{-3/2}=\int_{0}^{T}dr\int_{0}^{T-r}ds\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}[s^{-1/2}-(T-r+s)^{-1/2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dr\frac{4(\sqrt{2}-1)}{\sqrt{\pi}}(T-r)^{1/2}=\frac{8(\sqrt{2}-1)T^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}.

Step
2
.
We then show that, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and for any
0\leq t\leq T
,

(5.16)

\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu):\mu\in{\mathcal{A}},\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{s}(0)ds=\alpha\Big\}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}t^{3/2}}=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}\alpha^{2}}{8\sqrt{2}t^{3/2}\chi(\rho)}.

By replacing
T
by
t
in the last step, one can prove that the left hand side is not smaller than the right hand side. For the opposite direction, define
(\phi^{t,\alpha},H^{t,\alpha})
as

(5.17)

\phi^{t,\alpha}(v)=c_{0}(t,\alpha)\int_{0}^{t}p_{s}(v)ds,\quad\partial_{v}H^{t,\alpha}(r,v)=\begin{cases}-c_{0}(t,\alpha)\int_{0}^{t-r}p^{\prime}_{s}(v)ds,\quad&0\leq r\leq t,\\
0,&r\geq t,\end{cases}

where
c_{0}(t,\alpha)
is given by (
5.15
) with
T
replaced by
t
. By the above calculations, the right hand side in (
5.16
) equals
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mu^{t,\alpha})
, where
\mu^{t,\alpha}
is defined from (
4.3
) corresponding to
(\phi^{t,\alpha},H^{t,\alpha})
. Thus, we only need to show

\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{t,\alpha}(0,s)ds=\alpha.

It is straightforward by using (
4.3
) and (
5.12
), thus concluding the proof.

Step 3.
Last, we claim that the infimum in the proposition is obtained at the point
(\hat{\phi},\hat{H})
, where

(5.18)

\hat{\phi}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\phi^{t_{i},1},\quad\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}H^{t_{i},1}.

Above, the coefficients
\{\beta_{i}\}
are determined by the constraints

\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\hat{\mu}(s,0)ds=\alpha_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq k.

Equivalently,

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu^{t_{j},1}(s,0)ds=\alpha_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq k.

Indeed, for the claim, we need to show that, for any
\mu\in{\mathcal{A}}
satisfying

\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu_{s}(0)ds=0,\quad\forall i=1,2,\ldots,k,

we have

{\mathcal{Q}}(\hat{\mu}+\mu)=\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\Big(\|\phi+\hat{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|H+\hat{H}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}^{2}\Big)\geq\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\Big(\|\hat{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|\hat{H}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}^{2}\Big)={\mathcal{Q}}(\hat{\mu}).

The above result follows directly if we can show that

\big\langle\phi,\hat{\phi}\big\rangle+[H,\hat{H}]=0.

By (
5.17
) and (
4.3
), the left hand side of the last line equals

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\int_{0}^{t_{i}}dr\partial_{v}H(r,v)\big(-c_{0}(t_{i},1)\int_{0}^{t_{i}-r}p_{s}^{\prime}(v)ds\big)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv\phi(v)\big(c_{0}(t_{i},1)\int_{0}^{t_{i}}p_{s}(v)ds\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\beta_{i}c_{0}(t_{i},1)}{\chi(\rho)}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu(s,0)ds=0,

thus concluding the proof.

Next, we show that

(5.19)

\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu^{t_{j},\alpha_{j}}(s,0)ds=\frac{\alpha_{j}a(t_{i},t_{j})}{t_{j}^{3/2}}.

By (
4.3
) and (
5.17
), the term on the left hand side equals

\displaystyle\chi(\rho)c_{0}(t_{j},\alpha_{j})\Big\{\int_{0}^{t_{i}}ds\int_{0}^{t_{j}}dr\int_{\mathbb{R}}dvp_{s}(v)p_{r}(v)+\int_{0}^{t_{i}\wedge t_{j}}dr\int_{0}^{t_{i}-r}ds\int_{0}^{t_{j}-r}d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}dvp^{\prime}_{\tau}(v)p^{\prime}_{s}(v)\Big\}.

By direct calculations, the first term inside the above brace equals

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{i}}ds\int_{0}^{t_{j}}dr\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(r+s)^{-1/2}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\big[(t_{i}+t_{j})^{3/2}-t_{i}^{3/2}-t_{j}^{3/2}\big],

and the second one equals

\int_{0}^{t_{i}\wedge t_{j}}dr\int_{0}^{t_{i}-r}ds\int_{0}^{t_{j}-r}d\tau\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\tau+s)^{-3/2}\\
=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\big[t_{i}^{3/2}+t_{j}^{3/2}+(t_{i}+t_{j}-2t_{i}\wedge t_{j})^{3/2}/2-(t_{i}-t_{i}\wedge t_{j})^{3/2}-(t_{j}-t_{i}\wedge t_{j})^{3/2}-(t_{i}+t_{j})^{3/2}/2\big].

Thus,

\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu^{t_{j},\alpha_{j}}(s,0)ds=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\chi(\rho)c_{0}(t_{j},\alpha_{j})a(t_{i},t_{j})=\frac{\alpha_{j}}{t_{j}^{3/2}}a(t_{i},t_{j}).

Let
D={\rm Diag}(d_{j})_{1\leq j\leq k}
be the diagonal
k\times k
matrix with
d_{j}=t_{j}^{-3/2}
. Then, the constraints on
\boldsymbol{\beta}
reduces to

AD\boldsymbol{\beta}=\boldsymbol{\alpha},

and thus

\boldsymbol{\beta}=D^{-1}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha},

where
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k})^{T}
.

With the above construction, by Lemma
4.2
, the infimum in the proposition equals

\displaystyle\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\big\{\big\langle\phi^{t_{i},1},\phi^{t_{j},1}\big\rangle+[H^{t_{i},1},H^{t_{j},1}]\big\}=\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}c_{0}(t_{i},1)c_{0}(t_{j},1)

\displaystyle\times\Big\{\int_{0}^{t_{i}}ds\int_{0}^{t_{j}}dr\int_{\mathbb{R}}dvp_{s}(v)p_{r}(v)+\int_{0}^{t_{i}\wedge t_{j}}dr\int_{0}^{t_{i}-r}ds\int_{0}^{t_{j}-r}d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}dvp^{\prime}_{\tau}(v)p^{\prime}_{s}(v)\Big\}.

By the above calculations, the last expression equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}a(t_{i},t_{j})t_{i}^{-3/2}t_{j}^{-3/2}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\beta}^{T}DAD\boldsymbol{\beta}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha},

thus concluding the proof.

Abstract
In the current paper the authors linked two methods in order to evaluate general n-th order tridiagonal determinants. A breakdown free numerical algorithm is developed for computing the inverse of any n × n n\times n general nonsingular tridiagonal matrix without imposing any constrains. The algorithm is suited for implementation using any computer language such as FORTRAN, PYTHON, MATLAB, MAPLE, C, C++, MACSYMA, ALGOL, PASCAL and JAVA. Some illustrative examples are presented.

Introduction

Applications of matrices in real life include physics, geology, statistics, graphics, economic, business, animation, encryption, games and construction.

Definition 1.
A band matrix
A_{n}=(t_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is called tridiagonal matrix if it takes the form:

A_{n}=[t_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}=trid(b_{i},d_{i},a_{i})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}d_{1}&a_{1}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\

0&\cdots&\cdots&0&b_{n-1}&d_{n}\\
\end{array}\right]

(1)

in which
t_{ij}=0
whenever
|i-j|>1

2
A Cost-efficient Numerical Algorithm for Evaluating General n-th Order Tridiagonal Determinants

In this section, we focus on constructing an efficient numerical algorithm for the determinant of
A_{n}
in (1). The algorithm is mainly based on the following facts:

1.

The reversal matrix
J_{n}
defined by:

J_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&0&\cdots&0&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1&0\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]

(2)

satisfies:

(i)
det(J_{n})=\pm 1
, and

(ii)
J_{n}=J_{n}^{t}=J_{n}^{-1}
.

2.

Let
M=[m_{ij}]
be an
n\times n
matrix. The rotate of
M
, denoted
M^{R}
is defined as:
M^{R}=J_{n}MJ_{n}
. If
M=M^{R}
, then
M
is called centrosymmetric matrix. It is true that
det(M)=det(M^{R})
.

To study tridiagonal matrices, it is advantageous to introduce two n-dimensional vectors,
\mathbf{c}=[c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{n}]^{t}
,
and
\mathbf{e}=[e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}]^{t}
, and two (n+1)-dimensional vectors,
\mathbf{f}=[f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}]^{t}
, and
\mathbf{g}=[g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n},g_{n+1}]^{t}
. These vectors are given by:

c_{1}=d_{1},c_{i}=d_{i}-\frac{a_{i-1}b_{i-1}}{c_{i-1}},i=2,3,\cdots,n,

(3)

e_{n}=d_{n},e_{i}=d_{i}-\frac{a_{i}b_{i}}{e_{i+1}},i=n-1,n-2,\cdots,1,

(4)

f_{0}=1,f_{i}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccccc}d_{1}&a_{1}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
b_{1}&d_{2}&a_{2}&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&b_{2}&d_{3}&a_{3}&0&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&&0&\ddots&\ddots&a_{i-1}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&b_{i-1}&d_{i}\\
\end{array}\right|,i=1,2,3,\cdots,n,

(5)

and

g_{n+1}=1,g_{i}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccccc}d_{i}&a_{i}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
b_{i}&d_{i+1}&a_{i+1}&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&b_{i+1}&d_{i+2}&a_{i+2}&0&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&&0&\ddots&\ddots&a_{n-1}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&b_{n-1}&d_{n}\\
\end{array}\right|,i=n,n-1,n-2,\cdots,1.

(6)

The four vectors are related by
[
13
]
:

f_{i}=\prod_{r=1}^{i}c_{r}=c_{i}f_{i-1},i=1,2,\cdots,n,

(7)

and

g_{i}=\prod_{r=i}^{n}e_{r}=e_{i}g_{i+1},i=n,n-1,\cdots,1

(8)

Consequently,

f_{n}=det(A_{n})=g_{1}

(9)

The principal minors
f_{i}
’s and
g_{i}
’s in (5) and (6) satisfy the second order linear difference equations:

f_{i}=d_{i}f_{i-1}-a_{i-1}b_{i-1}f_{i-2},\quad i=2,3,\cdots,n,

(10)

with
f_{0}=1,f_{1}=d_{1}
.

and

g_{i}=d_{i}g_{i+1}-a_{i}b_{i}g_{i+2},\quad i=n-1,n-2,\cdots,1.

(11)

with
g_{n+1}=1,g_{n}=d_{n}

respectively.
Note that for each
i=1,2,\cdots,n
,
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are determinants of order
i
and
n+1-i
, respectively. By using the syntax of Maple language, we have:

f_{i}=det(submatrix(A_{n},1..i,1..i))\quad\textit{and}\quad g_{i}=det(submatrix(A_{n},i..n,i..n))

It is worth mentioned that any of the following conditions is sufficient (but not necessary) to ensure that the tridiagonal matrix
A_{n}
in (1) is nonsingular.

1.

(i) All
c_{i}\neq 0,i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

2.

(ii) All
e_{i}\neq 0,i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

For example, consider the two matrices
A
and
B
given by:

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&0\\
1&1&2\\
0&2&3\\
\end{array}\right],B=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}3&2&0\\
-1&1&1\\
0&1&1\\
\end{array}\right]

For these matrices, we have

det(A)=-4\neq 0
and
det(B)=2\neq 0
although
c_{2}=e_{2}=0
.

Also, each of the following conditions is necessary and sufficient to ensure that a symmetric tridiagonal matrix of the form (1) is positive definite
[
12
]
.

1.

(i) All
c_{i}>0
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

2.

(ii) All
e_{i}>0
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

The nonsingularity of a matrix should be checked before considering the problem of computing its inverse. So, we will present two numerical algorithms for evaluating the determinant of
A_{n}
.

For both
Algorithm 1
and
Algorithm 2
, the authors successfully linked two methods together in order to evaluate n-th order tridiagonal determinants. Throughout this paper
\sum_{k+1}^{k}(.)
is assumed equal to
0
and
\prod_{k+1}^{k}(.)
is assumed equal to
1
. Also,
p_{i}=-a_{i}
and
q_{i}=-b_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
.

Algorithm 1

(To evaluate
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}=det(A_{n})
)

Input:

n
and the components of the vectors
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
, and
\mathbf{d}
.

Output:

det(A_{n})
.

Step 1:
Set
c_{1}=d_{1}
,
f_{1}=d_{1}
, and
m=1
.

Step 2:
While
m\leq n-1
and
c_{m}\neq 0
do

m=m+1
,

c_{m}=d_{m}-a_{m-1}b_{m-1}/c_{m-1}
,

f_{m}=c_{m}f_{m-1}
,

End do.

Step 3:
For
k=m+1
to
n
do

f_{k}=d_{k}f_{k-1}-a_{k-1}b_{k-1}f_{k-2}

End do.

Step 4:
Set
det(A_{n})=f_{n}
.

Algorithm 2

(To evaluate
g_{n},g_{n-1},\cdots,g_{1}=det(A_{n})
)

Input:

n
and the components of the vectors
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}
, and
\mathbf{d}
.

Output:

det(A_{n})
.

Step 1:
Set
e_{n}=d_{n}
,
g_{n}=d_{n}
, and
m=n
.

Step 2:
While
m\geq 2
and
e_{m}\neq 0
do

m=m-1
,

e_{m}=d_{m}-a_{m}b_{m}/e_{m+1}
,

g_{m}=e_{m}g_{m+1}
,

End do.

Step 3:
For
k=m-1
step
-1
to
1
do

g_{k}=d_{k}g_{k+1}-a_{k}b_{k}g_{k+2}

End do.

Step 4:
Set
det(A_{n})=g_{1}
.

The
Algorithm 1
and
Algorithm 2
will be referred to as
DETGTRIF
and
DETGTRIG
respectively.

3
The Main Result

This section is mainly devoted for developing a new efficient numerical algorithm for computing the inverse of general nonsingular tridiagonal matrices of the form (1).

Lemma 1:
For
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, we have

f_{k}g_{k+1}-d_{k}f_{k-1}g_{k+1}+f_{k-1}g_{k}=f_{n}

(12)

Proof:
for
k=1
, the left-hand side of (12) gives

L.H.S=f_{1}g_{2}-d_{1}f_{0}g_{2}+f_{0}g_{1}=(d_{1})(g_{2})-(d_{1})(1)(g_{2})+(1)g_{1}=g_{1}=f_{n}=R.H.S

Thus formula (12) holds for
k=1
.

We next assume that formula (12) holds for
k=1,2,\cdots,i-1
. That is

f_{i-1}g_{i}-d_{i-1}f_{i-2}g_{i}+f_{i-2}g_{i-1}=f_{n}

(13)

We want to prove that:

f_{i}g_{i+1}-d_{i}f_{i-1}g_{i+1}+f_{i-1}g_{i}=f_{n}

(14)

Consider the left-hand side of (13)

f_{i-1}g_{i}-d_{i-1}f_{i-2}g_{i}+f_{i-2}g_{i-1}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=f_{i-1}g_{i}-f_{i-2}(g_{i-1}+a_{i-1}b_{i-1}g_{i+1})+f_{i-2}g_{i-1}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=f_{i-1}g_{i}-a_{i-1}b_{i-1}f_{i-2}g_{i+1}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=c_{i-1}f_{i-2}e_{i}g_{i+1}-a_{i-1}b_{i-1}f_{i-2}g_{i+1}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=f_{i-2}g_{i+1}(c_{i-1}e_{i}-a_{i-1}b_{i-1})\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=c_{i-1}f_{i-2}g_{i+1}(e_{i}-\frac{a_{i-1}b_{i-1}}{c_{i-1}})\\
\hskip 56.9055pt=f_{i-1}g_{i+1}(e_{i}-d_{i}+c_{i})

Hence, we may write the formula (13) in the form:

(c_{i}-d_{i}+e_{i})f_{i-1}g_{i+1}=f_{n}

(15)

Expanding the left-hand side of (15), yields

(c_{i}f_{i-1})g_{i+1}-d_{i}f_{i-1}g_{i+1}+f_{i-1}(e_{i}g_{i+1})=f_{n}

Therefore, by using (7) and (8), we get

f_{i}g_{i+1}-d_{i}f_{i-1}g_{i+1}+f_{i-1}g_{i}=f_{n}

Consequently, the formula (15) is valid. This completes the proof.

Armed with the above result, we may now formulate the following result.

Theorem 1
[
8
]
:
Let
A_{n}
be a nonsingular tridiagonal matrix of the form (1). Then
B_{n}=A_{n}^{-1}=[\alpha_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is given by

\alpha_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{1}{c_{i}-d_{i}+e_{i}}&\textit{if}\quad i=j\\
-y_{i}\alpha_{i+1,j}&\textit{if}\quad i<j\\
-v_{i-1}\alpha_{i-1,j}&\textit{if}\quad i>j\end{array}\right.\\

(16)

where
y_{i}=\frac{a_{i}}{c_{i}},
and
v_{i}=\frac{b_{i}}{e_{i+1}},i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
.

Repeated applications of (16) yields

\alpha_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{1}{c_{i}-d_{i}+e_{i}}&\textit{if}\quad i=j\\
(\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}\frac{p_{r}}{c_{r}})\alpha_{jj}&\textit{if}\quad i<j\\
(\prod_{r=j}^{i-1}\frac{q_{r}}{e_{r+1}})\alpha_{jj}&\textit{if}\quad i>j\end{array}\right.\\

(17)

Theorem 2.
Consider the matrix
A_{n}
in (1). If
det(A_{n})\neq 0
then the inverse of the matrix
A_{n}
is given by
A_{n}^{-1}=[\alpha_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
with
\alpha_{ij}
given by:

\alpha_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}f_{i-1}g_{i+1}/f_{n}&\textit{if}\quad i=j\\
(f_{i-1}g_{j+1}/f_{n})(\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}p_{r})&\textit{if}\quad i<j\\
(f_{j-1}g_{i+1}/f_{n})(\prod_{r=j}^{i-1}q_{r})&\textit{if}\quad i>j\end{array}\right.

(18)

Proof:
By using (7) and (8), we see that

\alpha_{ii}=\frac{1}{c_{i}-d_{i}+e_{i}}=\frac{1}{\frac{f_{i}}{f_{i-1}}-d_{i}+\frac{g_{i}}{g_{i+1}}}=\frac{f_{i-1}g_{i+1}}{f_{i}g_{i+1}-d_{i}f_{i-1}g_{i+1}+f_{i-1}g_{i}}=\frac{f_{i-1}g_{i+1}}{f_{n}}

by using
Lemma 1
.

Now, if
i<j
, then

\alpha_{ij}=(\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}\frac{p_{r}}{c_{r}})\alpha_{jj}=\frac{\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}p_{r}}{\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}c_{r}}\frac{f_{j-1}g_{j+1}}{f_{n}}=\frac{\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}p_{r}}{\prod_{r=1}^{i-1}c_{r}\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}c_{r}}\frac{f_{j-1}g_{j+1}f_{i-1}}{f_{n}}\\
\hskip 14.22636pt=\frac{f_{i-1}g_{j+1}}{f_{n}}\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}p_{r}
having used (7).

Similarly, we can prove that:

For
i>j
, we have

\alpha_{ij}=\frac{f_{j-1}g_{i+1}}{f_{n}}\prod_{r=j}^{i-1}q_{r}

This completes the proof.

Following
[
7
]
, we see that

\alpha_{ij}=[g_{j+1}(\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\delta_{ik}f_{k-1}\prod_{r=k}^{j-1}p_{r})+\delta_{ij}f_{j-1}g_{j+1}+f_{j-1}(\sum_{j+1}^{n}\delta_{ik}g_{k+1}\prod_{r=j}^{k-1}q_{r})]/f_{n}

(19)

is satisfied (see also
[
2
]
).

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta symbol which is equal to
1
if
i=j
and
0
if
i\neq j
. It should be noticed that we may obtain (18) from (19).

At this point, we can introduce the new numerical algorithm for inverting any general nonsingular tridiagonal matrix
A_{n}
of the form (1).

Algorithm 3

(To find the
n\times n
inverse matrix of a general tridiagonal matrix

A_{n}
of the form (1)).

Input:
The components
a_{i},b_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
and
d_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

Output:
The inverse matrix
A_{n}^{-1}=[\alpha_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
.

Step 1:
Compute
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}
using the
DETGTRIF
algorithm. If
f_{n}=0
then print"Singular matrix" and stop.

Step 2:
Compute
g_{n+1},g_{n},\cdots,g_{1}
using the
DETGTRIG
algorithm.

Step 3:
Calculate the elements of the inverse matrix
\alpha_{ii},i=1,2,3,\cdots,n
,

\alpha_{ij},i=1,2,3,\cdots,n-1,j=i+1,i+2,\cdots,n
,

and
\alpha_{ij},j=1,2,3,\cdots,n-1,i=j+1,j+2,\cdots,n
by using (18).

The
Algorithm

3
will be referred to as
HINVGTRI
algorithm.

Using the
HINVGTRI
algorithm, a procedure has been written in Maple and can be obtained from the authors upon request.

Remark 1.
If
a_{k}=0,1\leq k\leq n-1
, then
\alpha_{ij}=0,i=1,2,\cdots,k;j=k+1,k+2,\cdots,n
. Similarly, If
b_{k}=0,1\leq k\leq n-1
, then
\alpha_{ij}=0,j=1,2,\cdots,k;i=k+1,k+2,\cdots,n
.

Remark 2.
If
f_{k}=0,1\leq k\leq n-1
, then
\alpha_{k+1,j}=\alpha_{j,k+1}=0,j=k+1,k+2,\cdots,n
. Similarly, If
g_{k}=0,2\leq k\leq n
, then
\alpha_{i,k-1}=\alpha_{k-1,i}=0,i=1,2,\cdots,k-1
.

Remark 3.
The inverse matrix
B_{n}
of the matrix
A_{n}
of the form in (1) can be written as the Hadamard product, also called element-wise product.

B_{n}=R\circ S

(20)

where
R=(r_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
and
S=(s_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
are given by:

r_{ij}=r_{ji}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}f_{i-1}g_{i+1}/\Delta&\textit{if}\quad i=j\\
f_{i-1}g_{j+1}/\Delta&\textit{if}\quad i\neq j\end{array}\right.

(21)

s_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1&\textit{if}\quad i=j\\
\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}p_{r}&\textit{if}\quad i<j\\
\prod_{r=j}^{i-1}q_{r}&\textit{if}\quad i>j\end{array}\right.

(22)

and

\alpha_{ij}=r_{ij}\times s_{ij},i,j=1,2,\cdots,n.

(23)

Note that in formula (21),
\Delta=det(A_{n})
.

Remark 4.
For centrosymmetric tridiagonal matrices, we have
f_{i}=g_{n+1-i},i=0,1,2,\cdots,n
.

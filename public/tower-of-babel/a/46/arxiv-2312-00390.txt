The Collatz map analogue in polynomial rings and in completions
Angelot Behajaina, Elad Paran

Abstract
. We study an analogue of the Collatz map in the polynomial ring R ⁡ [ x ] R[x] , where R R is an arbitrary commutative ring. We prove that if R R is of positive characteristic, then every polynomial in R ⁡ [ x ] R[x] is eventually periodic with respect to this map. This extends previous works of the authors and of Hicks, Mullen, Yucas and Zavislak, who studied the Collatz map on 𝔽 p ​ [ x ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[x] and 𝔽 2 ​ [ x ] \mathbb{F}_{2}[x] , respectively. We also consider the Collatz map on the ring of formal power series R ⁡ [ [ x ] ] R[[x]] when R R is finite: we characterize the eventually periodic series in this ring, and give formulas for the number of cycles induced by the Collatz map, of any given length. We provide similar formulas for the original Collatz map defined on the ring ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} of 2 2 -adic integers, extending previous results of Lagarias.



1.
Introduction

The classical Collatz map is defined for any natural number
n
by
T(n)=n/2
if
n
is even and
T(n)=3n+1
if
n
is odd. The Collatz conjecture states that for any
n\geq 1
we have
\big(T^{k}(n),T^{k+1}(n),\ldots\big)=\big(1,4,2,1,4,2,\ldots\big)
for some
k\geq 1
. While some progress has been made in recent years by Tao
[
10
]
, the conjecture remains wide open. For a survey on the history of this question, see
[
5
]
.

In the recent work
[
2
]
, inspired by the work
[
3
]
of Hicks, Mullen, Yucas and Zavislak, the authors consider an analogue of the Collatz map, defined on the ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
of polynomials over a prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, by:

T(f)=\begin{cases}f\cdot(x+1)-f_{0}&\textrm{if}\,\,f_{0}\neq 0,\\
\frac{f}{x}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(Here,
f_{0}
denotes the constant coefficient of
f
.) One says that a polynomial
f
is
periodic
(with respect to
T
) if there exists
k\geq 1
such that
T^{k}(f)=f
. If
f
is periodic and such a
k
is minimal, then we call the sequence
(f,T(f),T^{2}(f),\ldots,T^{k-1}(f))
a
T
-cycle
. One says that
f
is
eventually periodic
or
pre-periodic
(with respect to
T
), if there exists
m\geq 1
such that
T^{m}(f)
is periodic.

The main result of
[
2
]
is that every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
is eventually periodic with respect to
T
, in a quadratic number in
\deg(f)
of
T
-iterations, and precise description of all
T
-cycles is given, see
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
. This extends the main result of
[
3
]
, where the case of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is analyzed. In
[
2
]
the authors also considered the extension of
T
to the formal power series ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[x]]
, and determined the eventually periodic elements in this ring with respect to
T
, see
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The goal of the present work is to study the Collatz map in
R[x]
and
R[[x]]
, given by the same definition as above, where
R
is any commutative ring (possibly infinite). In the sequel, we assume
R
to be a
nonzero ring
. Concerning the polynomial ring
R[x]
, our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring of characteristic
\mathfrak{N}
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{N}=0
, then the only
T
-cycles in
R[x]
are
(0)
and those of the form
(a,ax)
, for all
0\neq a\in R
.

(2)

Assume
\mathfrak{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}>0
, where
s\geq 1
,
p_{1},\dots,p_{s}
are distinct primes, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\geq 1
. Then:

•

Every
f\in R[x]
is eventually periodic with respect to
T
.

•

Let
f=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}x^{k}\in R[x]
be a periodic polynomial with
b_{0},b_{n}\neq 0
and let
\rho_{T}(f)
denote the length of the corresponding
T
-cycle. Then

(1)

\rho_{T}(f)\mid 2\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(\deg(f))\rfloor}.

Moreover if
b_{n}
is in
R^{\times}
, then we have equality in (
1
):

\rho_{T}(f)=2\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(\deg(f))\rfloor}.

We note that in the case where
R
is finite, the fact that every polynomial in
R[x]
is eventually periodic is immediate, see our remark following Lemma
2.6
below. The case where
R
is an infinite ring of positive characteristic is more complicated and is the main novelty of Theorem
1.1
.

In
[
4
]
, Lagarias considered the extension of the classical Collatz map to the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of
2
-adic integers. He proved that under the “condensed” Collatz map:

\displaystyle\overline{T}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{f}{2}&\textrm{if}\,\,f\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\
\frac{3f+1}{2}&\textrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

the number of cycles of length
n\geq 1
is

I(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)2^{n/d},

where
\mu
denotes the Möbius function. As a consequence, he proved that
I(n)\sim\frac{2^{n}}{n}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the
2
-adic completion of
\mathbb{Z}
, and its formal analogue is the ring
R[[x]]
of formal power series (the
x
-adic completion of
R[x]
). For
R[[x]]
with
R
a finite ring, inspired by the method used in
[
4
]
, we characterize all eventually
T
-periodic elements, prove that there are finitely many cycles of any possible length for the map
T
itself, and provide a formula for the number of such cycles:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be any
finite
ring of cardinality
q
.

(1)

The eventually
T
-periodic power series in
R[[x]]
are precisely series of the form
u(1+xv)^{-1}
, where
u,v
are polynomials in
R[x]
.

(2)

Let
n\geq 1
. Then the number
Z_{n}
of
T
-cycles of length
n
in
R[[x]]
is finite. Moreover, letting
b=4q-3
,
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{b}}{2}
and
\beta=\frac{1-\sqrt{b}}{2}
, we have

Z_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)\left(\alpha^{\frac{n}{d}}+\beta^{\frac{n}{d}}\right).

Asymptotically, we have

Z_{n}\sim\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 1.3
.

The first part of Theorem
1.2
cannot be extended to the case where
R
has characteristic
0
. In fact, there are polynomials in
R[x]
that are not eventually
T
-periodic. For example, when
R=\mathbb{Z}
, we have
T^{2k}(x+1)=x+k+1
for all
k\geq 0
; hence
x+1
is not eventually
T
-periodic by the first part of Theorem
1.1
.

We note that the second part of Theorem
1.2
is novel even in the case where
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a prime field –
this question was not studied in
[
2
]
.

Lagarias’s mentioned result on the number of cycles is given for the condensed Collatz map
U
. For the original Collatz map, the authors could not find available formulas in the literature. Therefore, to complete the picture and see the analogy between
R[[x]]
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we compute here the number of cycles for the usual Collatz map
T
on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, of any given length, and obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Then the number
Z_{n}
of
T
-cycles of length
n
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is finite, given by:

\displaystyle Z_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)\left(\phi^{\frac{n}{d}}+\psi^{\frac{n}{d}}\right),

where
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and
\psi=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}
.
Asymptotically, we have

Z_{n}\sim\frac{\phi^{n}}{n},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Our proof strategy for Theorem
1.4
is very similar to the strategy we employ for
R[[x]]
.

This paper is organized as follows: In section
2
, we study the Collatz map on
R[x]
and prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
3
we study the Collatz map on
R[[x]]
and prove Theorem
1.2
. Finally, in section
4
we prove Theorem
1.4
.

2.
The Collatz map in polynomial rings

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Basic results on valuations

For any prime
p
:

•

We denote by
v_{p}
the corresponding
p
-adic valuation.

•

For any non-negative integer
n=n_{0}+n_{1}p+\dots+n_{r}p^{r}
(
r\geq 0
and
0\leq n_{i}\leq p-1
for
0\leq i\leq r
), written in base
p
, let denote
S_{p}(n)=n_{0}+\dots+n_{r}
.

Recall the following well-known result (see
[
9
]
):

Theorem 2.1

(Kummer’s theorem)
.

Let
p
be a prime. Then we have

v_{p}\left(\binom{n}{m}\right)=\frac{S_{p}(m)+S_{p}(n-m)-S_{p}(n)}{p-1},

for all integers
0\leq m\leq n
.

In the sequel, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let

{\bf 1}_{a\geq b}=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if}\,\,a\geq b,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p
be a prime. Let
a\geq n\geq 1
such that
v_{p}(a)\geq\lfloor\log_{p}(n)\rfloor+1
. Then

\displaystyle v_{p}\left(\binom{a}{n}\right)=v_{p}(a)-v_{p}(n).

Proof.

Write
n=n_{k_{0}}p^{k_{0}}+n_{k_{1}}p^{k_{1}}+\dots+n_{k_{v}}p^{k_{v}}
, where
v\geq 0
,
0\leq k_{0}<k_{1}<\dots<k_{v}
and
1\leq n_{k_{0}},\dots,n_{k_{v}}\leq p-1
. Write
a=a_{\ell_{0}}p^{\ell_{0}}+a_{\ell_{1}}p^{\ell_{1}}+\dots+a_{\ell_{w}}p^{\ell_{w}}
, where
w\geq 0
,
0\leq\ell_{0}<\ell_{1}<\dots<\ell_{w}
and
1\leq a_{\ell_{0}},\dots,a_{\ell_{w}}\leq p-1
. Note that
v_{p}(a)\geq\lfloor\log_{p}(n)\rfloor+1
is the same as
\ell_{0}\geq k_{v}+1
. Then we have

\displaystyle a-n

\displaystyle=\left(a_{\ell_{w}}p^{\ell_{w}}+\dots+a_{\ell_{0}}p^{\ell_{0}}\right)-\left(n_{k_{v}}p^{k_{v}}+n_{k_{v-1}}p^{k_{v-1}}+\dots+n_{k_{0}}p^{k_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a_{\ell_{w}}p^{\ell_{w}}+\dots+a_{\ell_{1}}p^{\ell_{1}}+(a_{\ell_{0}}-1)p^{\ell_{0}}\right)+\left(p^{\ell_{0}}-n_{k_{v}}p^{k_{v}}-n_{k_{v-1}}p^{k_{v-1}}-\dots-n_{k_{0}}p^{k_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a_{\ell_{w}}p^{\ell_{w}}+\dots+a_{\ell_{1}}p^{\ell_{1}}+(a_{\ell_{0}}-1)p^{\ell_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle+{\bf 1}_{\ell_{0}\geq k_{v}+2}\sum_{i=k_{v}+1}^{\ell_{0}-1}(p-1)p^{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{v}(p-n_{k_{i}}-1)p^{k_{i}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{v-1}{\bf 1}_{k_{j+1}\geq k_{j}+2}\sum_{i=k_{j}+1}^{k_{j+1}-1}(p-1)p^{i}+(p-n_{k_{0}})p^{k_{0}}.

A straightforward computation gives
S_{p}(a-n)=S_{p}(a)+(p-1)(\ell_{0}-k_{0})-S_{p}(n)
. From Theorem
2.1
, we get

v_{p}\left(\binom{a}{n}\right)=\frac{S_{p}(n)+S_{p}(a-n)-S_{p}(a)}{p-1}=\ell_{0}-k_{0}=v_{p}(a)-v_{p}(n),

as was to be shown.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime.
Let
\alpha\geq 1
,
r\geq 0
and
k\geq p^{r}
. Assume that

v_{p}\left(\binom{k}{j}\right)\geq\alpha,

for all
1\leq j\leq p^{r}
. Then we have
v_{p}(k)\geq\alpha+r
.

Proof.

Write
k=k_{\ell_{0}}p^{\ell_{0}}+\dots+k_{\ell_{v}}p^{\ell_{v}}
, where
v\geq 0
,
0\leq\ell_{0}<\dots<\ell_{v}
and
1\leq k_{\ell_{0}},\dots,k_{\ell_{v}}\leq p-1
. By contradiction, assume that
r>\ell_{0}
. Since
k\geq p^{r}
, we have
r\leq\ell_{v}
. Hence there exists
0\leq i\leq v-1
such that
\ell_{i}<r\leq\ell_{i+1}
. From
1\leq\sum_{j=0}^{i}k_{\ell_{j}}p^{\ell_{j}}\leq p^{r}
, we get

\alpha\leq v_{p}\left(\binom{k}{\sum_{j=0}^{i}k_{\ell_{j}}p^{\ell_{j}}}\right)=\frac{S_{p}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}k_{\ell_{j}}p^{\ell_{j}}\right)+S_{p}\left(k-\sum_{j=0}^{i}k_{\ell_{j}}p^{\ell_{j}}\right)-S_{p}(k)}{p-1}=0,

a contradiction. Consequently we have
r\leq\ell_{0}
. From the equality

\displaystyle k-p^{r}

\displaystyle=k_{\ell_{v}}p^{\ell_{v}}+\dots+k_{\ell_{0}}p^{\ell_{0}}-p^{r}

\displaystyle=k_{\ell_{v}}p^{\ell_{v}}+\dots+k_{\ell_{1}}p^{\ell_{1}}+(k_{\ell_{0}}-1)p^{\ell_{0}}+{\bf 1}_{\ell_{0}\geq r+1}\sum_{j=r}^{\ell_{0}-1}(p-1)p^{j},

we obtain

S_{p}(k-p^{r})=S_{p}(k)-1+(p-1)(\ell_{0}-r),

and hence

\alpha\leq v_{p}\left(\binom{k}{p^{r}}\right)=\frac{S_{p}(p^{r})+S_{p}(k-p^{r})-S_{p}(k)}{p-1}=\ell_{0}-r=v_{p}(k)-r.

Therefore we get
v_{p}(k)\geq\alpha+r
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{s}
(
s\geq 1
) be distinct primes and let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\geq 1
be integers. Let
k\geq n\geq 1
be integers. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The product
\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
divides
k
.

(2)

For all
1\leq i\leq s
and all
1\leq j\leq n
, we have

v_{p_{i}}\left(\binom{k}{j}\right)\geq\alpha_{i}.

In other words, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\mid\binom{k}{j},

for all
1\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

Assume that (1) holds, that is,
\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}\mid k
. Let
1\leq i\leq s
and
1\leq j\leq n
. Since
v_{p_{i}}(k)\geq\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor\geq 1+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(j)\rfloor
, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

v_{p_{i}}\left(\binom{k}{j}\right)=v_{p_{i}}(k)-v_{p_{i}}(j)\geq v_{p_{i}}(k)-\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(j)\rfloor\geq v_{p_{i}}(k)-\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor\geq\alpha_{i}.

Therefore we obtain (2).

Conversely assume that (2) holds, that is,

v_{p_{i}}\left(\binom{k}{j}\right)\geq\alpha_{i},

for all
1\leq i\leq s
and all
1\leq j\leq n
. Now let
1\leq i\leq s
. Taking
p=p_{i}
,
r=\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor
and
\alpha=\alpha_{i}
in Lemma
2.3
, and noticing that
p^{r}\leq n
, we deduce

v_{p_{i}}(k)\geq\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor.

Consequently, we obtain
\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}\mid k
. Hence (1) holds.
∎

2.2.
Main results

Let
R
be any commutative ring of characteristic
\mathfrak{N}
. Denote by
R^{\times}
its group of units. We define the
Collatz map
by:

\displaystyle T:R[x]

\displaystyle\rightarrow R[x]

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}f\cdot(x+1)-f_{0}&\textrm{if}\,\,f_{0}\neq 0,\\
\frac{f}{x}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.5
.

(1)

Let
f\in R[x]
. We say that
f
is :

•

T
-
periodic
if there exists
n\geq 1
such that
T^{n}(f)=f
. The minimal such
n
is called the
period
of
f
, and is denoted by
\rho_{T}(f)
.

•

eventually
T
- periodic
if there exist
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
such that
T^{n+m}(f)=T^{m}(f)
; that is, if some
T
-iterate of
f
is
T
-periodic.

(2)

A
T
-cycle

\mathcal{C}\subset R[x]
is the orbit of some
T
-periodic polynomial
f\in R[x]
. The cardinality
\ell(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is called the
length
of
\mathcal{C}
. In this case, we have
\ell(\mathcal{C})=\rho_{T}(f)
.

(3)

A polynomial
f\in R[x]
is
odd
(resp.,
even
) if
f(0)\neq 0
(resp.,
f(0)=0
).

We need the following two results, also established in
[
2
]
when
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}
(
p
prime); however, we omit the proofs since they are similar to the proofs in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f\in R[x]\setminus\{0\}
. The following hold:

(1)

We have
\deg(T(f))\leq\deg(f)+1
.

(2)

If
\deg(T(f))>\deg(f)
, then
f
is odd and
T(f)
is even.

(3)

We have
\deg(T^{2}(f))\leq\deg(f)
.

(4)

For all
j\geq 0
, we have
\deg(T^{2j+1}(f))\leq\deg(f)+1
and
\deg(T^{2j}(f))\leq\deg(f)
.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Lemma 2.7.]
.
∎

Note that from part
4
of Lemma
2.6
it immediately follows that if
R
is finite, then every polynomial
f\in R[x]
is eventually
T
-periodic, since in this case the space of polynomials of degree at most
\deg(f)+1
is finite.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset R[x]\setminus\{0\}
be a
T
-cycle. Then
f\in\mathcal{C}
is even if and only if
T(f)
is odd. In particular, the length
\ell(\mathcal{C})
is even and
\mathcal{C}
contains the same number of odd polynomials and even polynomials.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Lemma 2.9]
.
∎

In the sequel, we need the following intermediate map, also introduced in
[
2
, Section 2]
:

\displaystyle\Pi:R[x]

\displaystyle\rightarrow R[x]

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{f\cdot(x+1)-f_{0}}{x}.

We recall some of the properties of
\Pi
(see
[
2
, §2]
): Let
n\geq 0
. Denote by
R[x]_{\leq n}
the additive group of all polynomials of degree at most
n
. Clearly, we have
\Pi(R[x]_{\leq n})\subset R[x]_{\leq n}
. Let
\Pi_{n}
be the restriction of
\Pi
to
R[x]_{\leq n}
. We define the
R
-linear shift map

L_{n}:(b_{n},\dots,b_{0})\in R^{n+1}\mapsto(0,b_{n},\dots,b_{1})\in R^{n+1}.

By identifying
R^{n+1}
with
R[x]_{\leq n}
via
(b_{n},\dots,b_{0})\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}x^{i}
, note that
\Pi_{n}={\rm Id}_{R[x]_{\leq n}}+L_{n}
.
By
R
-linearity, for all
k\geq 0
, we have

(2)

\Pi^{k}_{n}(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}L^{j}_{n}(f),

for all
f\in R[x]_{\leq n}
.

2.2.1.
Zero characteristic

Assume
\mathfrak{N}=0
. In contrast to positive characteristic, there are only few
T
-cycles in
R[x]
:

Proposition 2.8
.

The only
T
-cycles in
R[x]\setminus\{0\}
are those of the form
(a,ax)
(for any
a\in R\setminus\{0\}
).

Proof.

It is easy to see that every
(a,ax)
(
a\neq 0
) is a
T
-cycle. Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}\subset R[x]\setminus\{0\}
be a
T
-cycle. By Lemma
2.7
, there exists an odd polynomial
f\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
d=\deg(f)
. Then for each
j\geq 0
, the polynomial
T^{2j}(f)
is odd of degree
d
and
T^{2j+1}(f)
is even of degree
d+1
(the same argument as in the beginning of the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 2.15.]
). By Lemma
2.7
, we have
\ell(\mathcal{C})=2m
for some
m\geq 1
. In particular, we have
f=T^{2m}(f)=\Pi_{d}^{m}(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}L_{d}^{j}(f)
(see (
2
)), and so
0=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m}{j}L_{d}^{j}(f)
. If
d>0
, then comparing the first coordinate in this equation, the coefficient of
x^{d}
in
f
is zero, a contradiction. Thus
d=0
and
f
is a nonzero constant. Therefore we obtain
\mathcal{C}=(f_{0},f_{0}x)
.
∎

2.2.2.
Positive characteristic

Assume
\mathfrak{N}>0
. Write
\mathfrak{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
, where
p_{1},\dots,p_{s}
are distinct primes and
n_{1},\dots,n_{s}\geq 1
. In this part,
\rho_{\Pi}(f)
denotes the period of any periodic polynomial
f\in R[x]
under the map
\Pi
.
The following result generalizes
[
2
, Corollary 2.13.]
:

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
f=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}x^{k}\in R[x]
be a
T
-periodic odd polynomial with
b_{n}\neq 0
. Then we have

(3)

\rho_{T}(f)\mid 2\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(\deg(f))\rfloor}.

Moreover if
b_{n}
is in
R^{\times}
, then (
3
) is an equality, that is,

\rho_{T}(f)=2\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(\deg(f))\rfloor}.

Proof.

By (
2
), for all
k\geq 1
, we have

(4)

\Pi^{k}_{n}(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}L^{j}_{n}(f).

In particular, for
k=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
, by Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\Pi_{n}^{k}(f)=f
; hence, we have
\rho_{\Pi}(f)|k
. Using Lemma
2.7
, we get
\rho_{T}(f)=2\rho_{\Pi}(f)
, and so we obtain (
3
).

Now assume that
b_{n}
is in
R^{\times}
. Let
k=\rho_{\Pi}(f)=\dfrac{\rho_{T}(f)}{2}
. First, we claim that
k>n
. By contradiction, assume that
k\leq n
. From
\Pi^{k}_{n}(f)=f
, using (
4
), we deduce
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}L_{n}^{j}(f)=0
, and in particular,

\begin{cases}\binom{k}{1}b_{n}=0,\\
\binom{k}{1}b_{n-1}+\binom{k}{2}b_{n}=0,\\
\dots\\
\binom{k}{1}b_{n-k+1}+\dots+\binom{k}{k}b_{n}=0.\end{cases}

Since
b_{n}
is in
R^{\times}
, we get

\binom{k}{1}=\dots=\binom{k}{k}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{N}},

and so
1=\binom{k}{k}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{N}}
, a contradiction. Consequently, we have
k>n
. Using the same argument as before, we also have

\binom{k}{1}=\dots=\binom{k}{n}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{N}}.

By Lemma
2.4
,
\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
divides
k
. Consequently, we obtain

\rho_{T}(f)=2\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}.

This completes the proof of the last statement of the proposition.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}x^{k}\in R[x]
be an odd polynomial with
b_{n}\neq 0
. Then
f
is
T
-periodic if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}b_{j}\neq 0,

for all
0\leq\ell\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
.

Proof.

We sketch a proof which is similar to the one of
[
2
, Lemma 2.15]
. Assume that
f
is
T
-periodic. Then, by degree argument,
T^{2\ell}(f)
(resp.,
T^{2\ell+1}(f)
) must be odd (resp., even) for all
\ell\geq 0
. This is equivalent to the fact that
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}b_{j}
is the constant term of
T^{2\ell}(f)
and is nonzero, for all
\ell\geq 0
. Therefore
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}b_{j}\neq 0
, for all
0\leq\ell\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
.

Conversely assume that
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}b_{j}\neq 0
for all
0\leq\ell\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
. Then

T^{2\ell}(f)=\Pi_{n}^{\ell}(f),

for all
0\leq\ell\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
; in particular, by the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
2.9
, for
k=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}+\lfloor\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rfloor}
, we have

T^{2k}(f)=\Pi_{n}^{k}(f)=f,

and so
f
is
T
-periodic. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result is established in
[
2
]
when
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. As mentionned earlier, it is easy to see that it also holds for any finite ring
R
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Every polynomial in
R[x]
is eventually
T
-periodic.

Proof.

Clearly, the zero polynomial generates the trivial cycle
\{0\}
. Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}b_{k}x^{k}\in R[x]\setminus\{0\}
, with
b_{n}\neq 0
.
By Lemma
2.6
, we have
\deg(T^{m}(f))\leq\deg(f)+1
for all
m\geq 0
. We may replace
f
by
T^{m}(f)
(
m\geq 0
) such that
T^{m}(f)
is of minimal degree in the orbit of
f
. In particular,
f
is odd. In this case, an easy induction shows that
T^{2i}(f)
is odd and
T^{2i+1}(f)
is even for all
i\geq 0
. Note that the constant term of
T^{2i}(f)=\Pi_{n}^{i}(f)
is

\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}b_{j}\neq 0,

for all
i\geq 0
. By Lemma
2.10
,
f
is
T
-periodic. This completes the proof.
∎

Together with Proposition
2.8
and Proposition

of the introduction. We note that in the case where

(

) holds without any change in the proofs, including the mentioned quadratic bound. The corresponding result is then:

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of characteristic

.

i)

For any
k\geq 1
, a polynomial
f
with

Let

T
-periodic.

Every

-periodic odd polynomial of degree

with

.

iv)

There are
q-1

2
.

v)

For

\frac{(q-1)^{p^{k}}-(q-1)^{p^{k-1}}}{p^{k}}.

i)

Let

. For an odd polynomial

(A_{k}\cdot(a_{0},\dots,a_{p^{k}-1})^{t})_{\ell+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}a_{j},

for all

(which holds over any finite field),

is

-periodic if and only if all the elements in (

is a vector in

The proof is the same as the one of

iii)

This follows from Proposition

.

3.
The Collatz map in rings of formal power series

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

3.1.
Arbitrary rings

Let
R
be any commutative ring. We consider the analogue of the Collatz map for
R[[x]]
as in
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle T\colon R[[x]]

\displaystyle\rightarrow R[[x]]

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}(x+1)f-f_{0}&\textrm{if}\,\,f_{0}\neq 0,\\
\frac{f}{x}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Our aim (Theorem
1.2
) is, in fact, when
R
is finite, to characterize eventually
T
-periodic series and also to count the number of
T
-periodic cycles of a given period.
The method we use here is inspired by
[
4
]
, where the Collatz map on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
– the arithmetic analogue of
R[[x]]
– is investigated.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{R}
be the set of all polynomials
f\in R[x]
with
f_{0}\in R^{\times}
; this set is clearly a multiplicative subset of
R[x]
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{R}
the localization of
R[x]
at
\mathcal{I}_{R}
. Since series in
\mathcal{I}_{R}
are invertible in
R[[x]]
, the morphism
i_{R}:f/g\in\mathcal{S}_{R}\hookrightarrow fg^{-1}\in R[[x]]
(
f\in R[x]
and
g\in\mathcal{I}_{R}
) is injective. In the sequel, we may identify
\mathcal{S}_{R}
with its image in
R[[x]]
. Explicitly, we have

\mathcal{S}_{R}=\left\{u(1+xv)^{-1}\mid u,v\in R[x]\right\}.

In our investigation, we shall use the following modified Collatz map:

\displaystyle\overline{T}\colon R[[x]]

\displaystyle\rightarrow R[[x]]

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{(x+1)f-f_{0}}{x}&\textrm{if}\,\,f_{0}\neq 0,\\
\frac{f}{x}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For any
v\in R
, let

p(v)=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if}\,\,v=0,\\
1&\textrm{if}\,\,v\neq 0,\end{cases}

and let

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{v}\colon R((x))

\displaystyle\rightarrow R((x))

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{(x+1)^{p(v)}f-v}{x}.

For any
f\in R[[x]]
, we define

{\bf v}^{T}_{f}=\left({\bf v}^{T}_{f}(0),{\bf v}_{f}^{T}(1),{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(2),\dots\right)=\left(T^{0}(f)(0),T^{1}(f)(0),T^{2}(f)(0),\dots\right)\in R^{\mathbb{N}}

(resp.,

{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}=({\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(0),{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(1),{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(2),\dots)=\left(\overline{T}^{0}(f)(0),\overline{T}^{1}(f)(0),\overline{T}^{2}(f)(0),\dots\right)\in R^{\mathbb{N}}.)

Also, for
n\geq 1
, let
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}=\left({\bf v}^{T}_{f}(0),\dots,{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n-1)\right)\in R^{n}
and
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}=\left({\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(0),\dots,{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(n-1)\right)\in R^{n}
. Moreover, for
n\geq 1
, let

\Omega_{n}=\left\{f\in R[[x]]\mid T^{n}(f)=f\right\},

\overline{\Omega}_{n}=\left\{f\in R[[x]]\mid\overline{T}^{n}(f)=f\right\},

and

\Omega^{*}_{n}=\left\{f\in R[[x]]\mid f\,\,\textrm{is $T$-periodic of $T$-period}\,\,n\right\}.

For
{\bf v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
(
n\geq 1
), we define the map

\overline{T}_{{\bf v}}=\overline{T}_{v_{n-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\overline{T}_{v_{0}}:R((x))\rightarrow R((x)).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
{\bf v}=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) and
f\in R[[x]]
, we have

\overline{T}_{{\bf v}}(f)=\frac{1}{x^{n}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}f-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}\right).

Proof.

Let
f\in R[[x]]
. We prove the claim by induction on the length
n
of
{\bf v}
. This is clear when
n=1
. Assume this is true for some
n\geq 1
. Let
{\bf v}=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n})\in R^{n+1}
of length
n+1
. Write
{\bf v}^{\prime}=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
. By the induction assumption, we have

\overline{T}_{{\bf v}^{\prime}}(f)=\frac{1}{x^{n}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}f-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}\right).

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{{\bf v}}(f)

\displaystyle=\overline{T}_{v_{n}}(\overline{T}_{{\bf v}^{\prime}}(f))

\displaystyle=\frac{(x+1)^{p(v_{n})}\overline{T}_{{\bf v}^{\prime}}(f)-v_{n}}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x+1)^{p(v_{n})}\left(\frac{1}{x^{n}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}f-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}\right)\right)-v_{n}}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x^{n+1}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n}p(v_{k})}f-\sum_{j=0}^{n}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n}p(v_{k})}\right).

Therefore the claim holds for
n+1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Roughly speaking, the following result asserts that any
\overline{T}
-periodic series
f
is uniquely determined by
{\bf v}_{f}^{\overline{T}}
:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\bf v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
(
n\geq 1
). Then there exists a unique
f\in\overline{\Omega}_{n}
such that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}={\bf v}
, given by:

f=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}}{(x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}-x^{n}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\overline{\Omega}_{n}
such that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}={\bf v}
. Then, we have

f=\overline{T}^{n}(f)=\overline{T}_{{\bf v}}(f),

and, by Lemma
3.1
, we get

f=\frac{1}{x^{n}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}f-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}\right),

from which we obtain

f=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}}{(x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}-x^{n}}\in\mathcal{S}_{R}.

Hence we have the unicity.

Now we are going to prove that, for

(6)

g=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}}{(x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}-x^{n}}\in\mathcal{S}_{R},

we have
\overline{T}^{n}(g)=g
(that is,
g\in\overline{\Omega}_{n}
) and that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g,n}={\bf v}
. Since
g=\overline{T}_{{\bf v}}(g)
by the above argument, it suffices to prove that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g,n}={\bf v}
. Let us prove, by induction, that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g}(\ell)=v_{\ell}
, for all
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
. For
j=0
: it is clear that
g(0)=v_{0}
, and so
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g}(0)=v_{0}
. Assume that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g}(0)=v_{0},\dots,{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g}(\ell)=v_{\ell}
, for some
0\leq\ell<n-1
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{T}^{\ell+1}(g)

\displaystyle=\overline{T}_{(v_{0},\dots,v_{\ell})}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x^{\ell+1}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}p(v_{k})}g-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{\ell}p(v_{k})}\right)\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{lem:fundiden}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}-x^{n}}\left((x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}p(v_{k})}\left(v_{\ell+1}+xA(x)\right)+x^{n-\ell-1}B(x)\right),

for some
A(x),B(x)\in R[x]
. Noticing that
n-\ell-1\geq 1
, we have
\left[\overline{T}^{\ell+1}(g)\right](0)=v_{\ell+1}
, and so
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{g}(\ell+1)=v_{\ell+1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

As a generalization of Proposition
3.2
, one may ask the following; note that it holds true for the condensed Collatz map on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Question 3.3
.

For any
{\bf v}\in R^{\mathbb{N}}
, is there
f\in R[[x]]
such that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}={\bf v}
?

Corollary 3.4
.

For all
n\geq 1
, we have
\overline{\Omega}_{n}\subset\mathcal{S}_{R}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\overline{\Omega}_{n}
. Write
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})
. By Proposition
3.2
, we have

f=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}x^{j}(x+1)^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}}{(x+1)^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(v_{k})}-x^{n}},

which clearly belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{R}
. Therefore we have
\overline{\Omega}_{n}\subset\mathcal{S}_{R}
.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

A vector
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots)\in R^{\mathbb{N}}
is called
zero dense
if it contains no consecutive nonzero coordinates; in other words, for all
i\geq 0
, we have
v_{i}=0
or
v_{i+1}=0
. A vector
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) is called
cyclically zero dense
if the infinite concatenation

(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},\dots,v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},\dots)\in R^{\mathbb{N}}

is zero dense in the preceding sense.

The following observation follows directly from the definition of
T
and
\overline{T}
:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f\in R[[x]]
. Then
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}
by inserting
0
after each nonzero coordinate. Conversely
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}
by removing the zero coordinate following any nonzero coordinate. In particular,
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}
is zero dense.

Moreover we have the following:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f\in R[[x]]
.

(1)

Let
n\geq 1
and let
s
be the number of nonzero coordinates in
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}
. Then we have
\overline{T}^{n}(f)=T^{n+s}(f)
. Moreover,
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n+s}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}
by inserting
0
after each nonzero coordinate.

(2)

Let
n\geq 1
. Assume that
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n-1)=0
and let
s
denote the number of nonzero coordinates in
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}
. Then we have
T^{n}(f)=\overline{T}^{n-s}(f)
. Moreover,
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n-s}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}
by removing the zero coordinate following any nonzero coordinate.

The following result, which follows immediately from Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.7
, is needed to count
T
-cycles in §
3.2
.

Corollary 3.8
.

For each cyclically zero dense vector
{\bf v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), there exists a unique
f\in\Omega_{n}
such that
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}={\bf v}
.

The following follows from the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
f=u(1+xv)^{-1}\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
with
u,v\in R[x]
. Then
T^{k}(f)=u_{k}(1+xv)^{-1}
(
k\geq 0
and
u_{k}\in R[x]
) where
\deg(u_{k})
is bounded as
k
increases.

3.2.
Finite rings

Assume that
R
is
finite
of cardinality
q
. We obtain the first part of Theorem
1.2
:

Theorem 3.10
.

The eventually
T
-periodic series are exactly the series in
\mathcal{S}_{R}
.

Proof.

Let
0\neq f
be an eventually
T
-periodic series. Note that
T(f)\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
implies
f\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
. Hence we may assume that
f
is
T
-periodic. Then there exists
n\geq 1
such that
T^{n}(f)=f
. Note that
T^{n}(T(f))=T(f)
and, if
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n-1)\neq 0
then
{\bf v}^{T}_{T(f)}(n-1)={\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n)=0
. Hence, up to replacing
f
by
T(f)
, we may assume
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n-1)=0
. In this case, by Lemma
3.7
, there exists
0\leq s\leq n
such that
f=T^{n}(f)=\overline{T}^{n-s}(f)
, and so
f\in\overline{\Omega}_{n-s}
. By Corollary
3.4
, we get
f\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
.

Conversely, let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{R}
. Since
R
is finite, by Lemma
3.9
, the orbit of
f
is finite, that is,
f
is eventually
T
-periodic.
∎

For
n\geq 1
, by Lemma
3.6
and Corollary
3.8
, we can identify
\Omega_{n}
with the set
\mathcal{F}_{n}
of all cyclically zero dense vectors of
R^{n}
, and so
\Omega_{n}
is finite. We shall now count the number of
T
-cycles of a given length. For
n\geq 1
, let
i_{n}=|\Omega^{*}(n)|
and
j_{n}=|\Omega(n)|
. For
n\geq 1
, we have the disjoint union
\Omega(n)=\bigsqcup_{d|n}\Omega^{*}(d)
, and so we obtain
j_{n}=\sum_{d|n}i_{n}
. Hence, for all
n\geq 1
, the Möbius inversion formula gives

(7)

i_{n}=\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)j_{\frac{n}{d}},

where
\mu
denotes the Möbius function.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
E_{n}
be the set of all zero dense vectors in
R^{n}
and let
e_{n}=|E_{n}|
. Notice that
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subset E_{n}
. For
(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})\in R^{n}
, we have
(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
if and only if
v_{n-1}=0
and
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-2})\in E_{n-1}
, or if
v_{n-1}\neq 0
,
v_{0}=v_{n-2}=0
and
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-3})\in E_{n-3}
. Hence for
n\geq 3
, we have

(8)

j_{n}=|\mathcal{F}_{n}|=e_{n-1}+(q-1)e_{n-3}.

Moreover we have
j_{1}=1
(because
\Omega_{1}=\{0\}
) and
j_{2}=2q-1
(because
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(0,a),(a,0)\mid a\neq 0\}
). We now compute
e_{n}=|E(n)|
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
b=4q-3
,
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{b}}{2}
and
\beta=\frac{1-\sqrt{b}}{2}
. Then

(9)

e_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n+2}-\beta^{n+2}}{\sqrt{b}},

for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Consider the following Fibonacci-like sequence:

(10)

\begin{cases}f_{0}=1,\\
f_{1}=q,\\
f_{n}=f_{n-1}+(q-1)f_{n-2},\,\,n\geq 2.\end{cases}

Note that the characteristic polynomial of the linear recurrence (
10
) is
X^{2}-X-(q-1)
. Since its roots are
\alpha
and
\beta
, we have

f_{n}=A\alpha^{n}+B\beta^{n}\,\,(n\geq 0),

for some
A,B\in\mathbb{C}
. Using the initial conditions

\displaystyle A+B=f_{0}=1

\displaystyle A\alpha+B\beta=f_{1}=q,

we get

A=\dfrac{q-\beta}{\sqrt{b}}
and
B=\dfrac{\alpha-q}{\sqrt{b}}
. Therefore we have

(11)

f_{n}=\frac{(q-\beta)\alpha^{n}-(q-\alpha)\beta^{n}}{\sqrt{b}}\,\,(n\geq 0).

Since
\alpha^{2}=q-\beta
and
\beta^{2}=q-\alpha
, we obtain

f_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n+2}-\beta^{n+2}}{\sqrt{b}}\,\,(n\geq 0).

To complete the proof of the lemma, it remains to prove that
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
also satisfies (
10
).
For
n=1,2
, it follows from
E_{1}=R
and
E_{2}=\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(0,a),(a,0)\mid a\in R\setminus\{0\}\}
. Let
n\geq 2
. Observe that
(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})\in E_{n}
if and only if (
v_{0}=0
and
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in\Omega_{n-1}
) or (
v_{0}\neq 0
,
v_{1}=0
and
(v_{2},\dots,v_{n-1})\in\Omega_{n-2}
). This shows that
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies (
10
).
∎

The following is the second part of Theorem
1.2
:

Proposition 3.12
.

With the same notation as in Lemma
3.11
, for any
n\geq 3
, the number of
T
-cycles of length
n
is:

(12)

Z_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)\left(\alpha^{\frac{n}{d}}+\beta^{\frac{n}{d}}\right)

Proof.

Combining (
7
) and Equation (
8
), for
n\geq 3
, we have

i_{n}=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)j_{\frac{n}{d}}=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\left(e_{\frac{n}{d}-1}+(q-1)e_{\frac{n}{d}-3}\right)

A straightforward computation using Lemma
3.11
,
\alpha^{2}+q-1=\alpha\sqrt{b}
and
\beta^{2}+q-1=-\beta\sqrt{b}
leads to:

Z_{n}=\frac{i_{n}}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)\left(\alpha^{\frac{n}{d}}+\beta^{\frac{n}{d}}\right).

∎

Corollary 3.13
.

With the same notation as in Lemma
3.11
, we have

Z_{n}\sim\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n},

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

4.
The Collatz map in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. As mentioned in the introduction, Lagarias proved in
[
4
]
that, under the “condensed” Collatz map:

\displaystyle\overline{T}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{f}{2}&\textrm{if}\,\,f\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\
\frac{3f+1}{2}&\textrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

the number of cycles of length
n\geq 1
is

I(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)2^{\frac{n}{d}}.

In this section, using the same approach as in §
3
, we determine the number of cycles of length
n\geq 1
for the classical Collatz map:

\displaystyle T\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{f}{2}&\textrm{if}\,\,f\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\
3f+1&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We shall use similar notation to that of §
3
: For any
v\in\{0,1\}
, let

(13)

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{v}\colon\mathbb{Q}_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{2}

(14)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{3^{v}f+v}{2}.

(where
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
denotes the field of
2
-adic numbers – the quotient field of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
). For any
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, let
f_{0}\in\{0,1\}
be such that
f\equiv f_{0}\pmod{2}
. For any
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we define

{\bf v}^{T}_{f}=\left({\bf v}^{T}_{f}(0),{\bf v}_{f}^{T}(1),{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(2),\dots\right)=\left(T^{0}(f)_{0},T^{1}(f)_{0},T^{2}(f)_{0},\dots\right)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}

(resp.,

{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}=({\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(0),{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(1),{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(2),\dots)=\left(\overline{T}^{0}(f)_{0},\overline{T}^{1}(f)_{0},\overline{T}^{2}(f)_{0},\dots\right)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}.)

Also, for
n\geq 1
, let
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}=\left({\bf v}^{T}_{f}(0),\dots,{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n-1)\right)\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}=\left({\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(0),\dots,{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}(n-1)\right)\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. For
n\geq 1
, let

\Omega_{n}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mid T^{n}(f)=f\right\},

\overline{\Omega}_{n}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mid\overline{T}^{n}(f)=f\right\},

and

\Omega^{*}_{n}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mid f\,\,\textrm{is $T$-periodic of $T$-period}\,\,n\right\}.

The following result, an analogue of Proposition
3.2
, was proved in
[
4
, Theorem 2.1.]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\bf v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
). Then there exists a unique
f\in\overline{\Omega}_{n}
such that
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}={\bf v}
, given by:

f=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v_{j}2^{j}3^{\sum_{k=j+1}^{n-1}v_{k}}}{2^{n}-3^{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}v_{k}}}.

Remark 4.2
.

Regarding Proposition
4.1
, although the statement of
[
4
, Theorem 2.1.]
concerns
\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we may replace
\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
without any change in the proof. (We note that there is a minor difference in terminology between here and
[
4
]
– there an element
f
is called “periodic of period
n
” if
\overline{T}^{n}(f)=f
, and
n
need not be minimal with this property.)

Concerning eventually
\overline{T}
-periodic elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, it is known that they are all rational (see
[
1
,
8
]
). Lagarias conjectured that the converse also holds (see
[
5
]
).

Conjecture 4.3

(Periodicity Conjecture, Lagarias)
.

The eventually
\overline{T}
-periodic elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
are exactly the elements of
\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Definition 4.4
.

A vector
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
is called
zero dense
if it contains no consecutive
1
’s; in other words, for all
i\geq 0
, we have
v_{i}=0
or
v_{i+1}=0
. A vector
(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) is called
cyclically zero dense
if the infinite concatenation

(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},\dots,v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},\dots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}

is zero dense in the preceding sense.

Remark 4.5
.

In symbolic dynamics, the set of zero dense sequences is called the
golden mean
shift space (see
[
6
]
).

The proofs of Lemma
4.6
, Lemma
4.7
and Proposition
4.8
below are exactly the same as the proofs of Lemma
3.6
, Lemma
3.7
and Corollary
3.8
respectively.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}
by inserting
0
after each
1
. Conversely
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}
by removing the zero coordinate following any
1
. In particular,
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}
is zero dense.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

(1)

Let
n\geq 1
and let
s
be the number of
1
in
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}
. Then we have
\overline{T}^{n}(f)=T^{n+s}(f)
. Moreover,
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n+s}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{\overline{T}}_{f,n}
by inserting
0
after each
1
.

(2)

Let
n\geq 1
. Assume that
{\bf v}^{T}_{f}(n-1)=0
and let
s
denote the number of
1
in
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}
. Then we have
T^{n}(f)=\overline{T}^{n-s}(f)
. Moreover,
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n-s}
is obtained from
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}
by removing the zero coordinate following any
1
.

Proposition 4.8
.

For each cyclically zero dense vector
{\bf v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), there exists a unique
f\in\Omega_{n}
such that
{\bf v}^{T}_{f,n}={\bf v}
.

With Lemma
4.6

4.7
and Proposition

, Lemma

Theorem

Conditional work statistics of quantum measurements
M. Hamed Mohammady, Alessandro Romito

Abstract
In this paper we introduce a definition for conditional energy changes due to general quantum measurements, as the change in the conditional energy evaluated before, and after, the measurement process. By imposing minimal physical requirements on these conditional energies, we show that the most general expression for the conditional energy after the measurement is simply the expected value of the Hamiltonian given the post-measurement state. Conversely, the conditional energy before the measurement process is shown to be given by the real component of the weak value of the Hamiltonian. Our definition generalises well-known notions of distributions of internal energy change, such as that given by stochastic thermodynamics. By determining the conditional energy change of both system and measurement apparatus, we obtain the full conditional work statistics of quantum measurements, and show that this vanishes for all measurement outcomes if the measurement process conserves the total energy. Additionally, by incorporating the measurement process within a cyclic heat engine, we quantify the non-recoverable work due to measurements. This is shown to always be non-negative, thus satisfying the second law, and will be independent of the apparatus specifics for two classes of projective measurements.

In quantum mechanics, measurements induce an unavoidable stochastic change in the state of a system, which will generally modify its energy. How the energy will change on average is well understood, and is simply given by the difference in the system’s average energy, evaluated before and after the measurement.
Quantifying the change in energy,
conditional
on observing a given measurement outcome, however, is still lacking a general answer. A well known method used to establish the energetic fluctuations due to dynamical processes, such as measurement, is the Two-Point-Measurement (TPM) protocol, which uses projective energy measurements before, and after, the dynamical process in question
[
11
]
. This protocol, however, is known to break down when the system initially has coherences with respect to its Hamiltonian
[
13
,
17
]
. An alternative approach is that used in quantum stochastic thermodynamics
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
, wherein the system follows a trajectory of pure states that are not necessarily eigenstates of the Hamiltonian. The change in energy is thus defined as the difference in expected values of the Hamiltonian at the start and end of the trajectory in question. Such an approach, however, implicitly assumes that we know which pure state the system initially occupies.

In the present paper we provide a general definition for conditional energy changes, for general quantum measurements and initial system states, as the difference in conditional energies evaluated before and after the measurement process. By imposing three minimal physical requirements on these conditional energies, we show that the most general expresion for the conditional energy after the measurement is simply the expected value of the Hamiltonian given the post-measurement state. Conversely, the conditional energy before the measurement process is shown to be given by the real component of the generalised weak value of the Hamiltonian
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
.
The energetic statistics obtained by the proposed definition generalises existing results in the literature, which are valid in specific circumstances: (i) if the measured observable involves an initial and final energy measurement, we regain the work distribution of the TPM protocol; (ii) if the observable measured is the Heisenberg-evolved Hamiltonian, the energy statistics is equivalent to the quasi-probability distribution over the random variable of work introduced in
[
13
]
; and (iii) if the measurement process first projects the system onto one of its pure state components, we obtain the definition for internal energy change along a quantum trajectory used in stochastic thermodynamics.

By evaluating the conditional energy change of both system and measurement apparatus, we obtain the full conditional work statistics of quantum measurements. We show that when the measurement process conserves the total energy, the conditional work vanishes for all measurement outcomes, and not just on average. Finally, by incorporating the measurement process within a cyclic heat engine involving a single heat bath of temperature
T
, we define the non-recoverable, or irreversible, work due to measurement. This is shown to be non-negative, thus satisfying the second law of thermodynamics. In general, the non-recoverable work will depend on the specifics of the measurement apparatus. However, we show that it becomes a system-only property for two classes of projective measurements: (i) repeatable projective measurements, which is a generalisation of ideal projective measurements (the case for ideal projective measurements has already been shown in
[
21
]
); and (ii) “noisy” projective measurements where the apparatus has the same dimension as the system. Here, a noisy projective measurement is to be understood as the necessarily non-repeatable measurement of an observable by use of a measurement apparatus that is initially prepared in a state of full-rank.

\xi(x):=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}\xi^{\prime}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}/p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\equiv\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}(x)]

\xi^{\prime}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]

3
Energy change conditional on measurement outcome

We wish to quantify the increase in energy of the system, conditional on observing outcome
x
of the POVM
M
. Before doing so, it will be instructive to consider the same question for a classical probabilistic system. Consider the time-dependent and time-independent random variables
A(t)
and
B
, respectively, with the joint probability distributions
p(a(t),b)
such that
\sum_{a(t)}p(a(t),b)=p(b)
for all
t
. The change in the expected value of
A(t)
, for
t=0
and
t=\tau
, conditional on
B=b
, can be defined as

\displaystyle\Delta\langle A(t)\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle:=\langle A(\tau)\rangle_{b}-\langle A(0)\rangle_{b}.

(3)

Here, the expected value of
A(t)
conditional on
B=b
is defined as
\langle A(t)\rangle_{b}:=\sum_{a(t)}a(t)\,p(a(t)|b)
, where
p(a(t)|b):=p(a(t),b)/p(b)=p(a(t),b)/\sum_{a(t)}p(a(t),b)
is the probability of
A(t)=a(t)
conditional on
B=b
. Clearly, averaging
\Delta\langle A(t)\rangle_{b}
with respect to the probability distribution
p(b)
yields
\sum_{b}p(b)\Delta\langle A(t)\rangle=\sum_{a(\tau)}p(a(\tau))a(\tau)-\sum_{a(0)}p(a(0))a(0)\equiv\langle A(\tau)\rangle-\langle A(0)\rangle
. If
A(t)
is the energy of the system, while
B
is another physical quantity, then Eq. (
3
) will quantify the conditional increase in energy for classical systems.

Now consider a quantum measurement process
{\mathcal{M}}
which takes place during the time interval
(0,\tau)
. We wish to quantify the increase in energy of the system, conditional on observing outcome
x
of the POVM
M
. To this end, we may define the conditional energies
E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
for
t=0
and
t=\tau
, from which the conditional increase in energy will be given as

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)

\displaystyle:=E_{x,\tau}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)-E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho).

(4)

Eq. (
4
) can be seen as a quantum analogue to Eq. (
3
). Here, we replace
A(t)
with the time-dependent Hamiltonian
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t):=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(t)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)
, where
\epsilon_{j}(t)
are the energy-eigenvalues and
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)
the corresponding spectral projections. Similarly,
B
is replaced with the POVM
M
. For the two equations to be fully analogous, however, we must obtain a quantum version of the joint probability distribution
p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)
. Prima facie, this would be determined by the Born rule, given the initial state
\rho
and the joint measurement of
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)
and
M
. However, due to contextuality,
p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)
is generally not uniquely defined, and will depend on the
order
in which we measure
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)
and
M
: if we first perform an ideal measurement of
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)
, and then measure
M
, we obtain
p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)=\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t))]
, whereas if we first measure
M
, and then
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)
, we obtain
p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]
. Note that these joint probability distributions may differ even if
\rho
is a classical probabilistic mixture of energy eigenstates – it is the quantum nature of measurements that leads to the contextuality of the joint probability distribution. We define the “classical” (non-contextual) limit as being characterised by measurement processes
{\mathcal{M}}
which satisfy
\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t))]=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]
for all
\rho,j,x
, thereby providing a uniquely defined
p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)
, so that Eq. (
4
) reduces to Eq. (
3
).

We are therefore interested in obtaining the most general expressions of
E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
, for
t=0
and
t=\tau
, that are valid for all states
\rho
and POVMs
M
, so that Eq. (
4
) will be a physically meaningful definition for conditional energy change which, in the “classical” limit, will reduce to Eq. (
3
). To this end, we impose the following three minimal requirements on
E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
:

Requirement 1
.

For all
\rho
and
{\mathcal{M}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho_{t}],

(5)

where
\rho_{0}:=\rho
, and
\rho_{\tau}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
.

Requirement 2
.

For all
\rho
and
{\mathcal{M}}
, and all ensembles
\{p_{k},\rho^{(k)}\}
satisfying
\sum_{k}p_{k}\rho^{(k)}=\rho
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\frac{p_{k}p_{\rho^{(k)}}^{M}(x)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho^{(k)})=E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho),

(6)

where
\sum_{k}p_{k}p_{\rho^{(k)}}^{M}(x)=p_{\rho}^{M}(x)
.

Requirement 3
.

If

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t))]=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]

(7)

for all
\rho
and
j
, then

\displaystyle E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(t)\frac{p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)}{p(x)},

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(t)\frac{\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]}{\sum_{j}\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]},

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]}{\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]}=:\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho(x)].

(8)

Requirement
1
is necessary for the average conditional increase in energy to equal the increase in average energy, i.e.
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho_{\tau}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]
; Requirement
2
is needed to ensure that the conditional increase in energy is not dependent on how the state
\rho
is prepared; and Requirement
3
follows from the fact that in the “classical” limit, achieved when Eq. (
7
) is satisfied, the conditional energies
E_{x,t}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
will be evaluated by sampling the energy eigenvalues by the conditional probability distribution
p(\epsilon_{j}(t)|x):=p(\epsilon_{j}(t),x)/p(x)=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]/\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]
.

Following the logic of conditional expectation values in classical probability theory, we may be tempted to define the conditional energy before the measurement,
E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
, as

\displaystyle\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{(\rho,x)}:=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(0)\frac{\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(0))]}{\sum_{j}\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(0))]},

(9)

and the conditional energy after the measurement,
E_{x,\tau}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
, as

\displaystyle\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rangle_{(\rho,x)}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(\tau)\frac{\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(\tau){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]}{\sum_{j}\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(\tau){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]},

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho(x)].

(10)

Here, Eq. (
9
) obtains the joint probabilities
p(\epsilon_{j}(0),x)
by performing an ideal measurement of
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
at
t=0
, i.e. before the measurement process
{\mathcal{M}}
. In contrast, Eq. (
3
) obtains
p(\epsilon_{j}(\tau),x)
by performing an ideal measurement of
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)
at
t=\tau
, i.e., after the measurement process
{\mathcal{M}}
. These equations trivially satisfy Requirement
3
. Moreover, as shown in Appendix (
A
), Eq. (
3
) is the most general expression for
E_{x,\tau}(\rho)
that will satisfy all three requirements. Consequently, we may safely choose

E_{x,\tau}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho):=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho(x)].

(11)

However, Eq. (
9
) does not satisfy Requirement
1
and Requirement
2
, as Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
6
) are satisfied only if either
\rho
or
M_{x}
commute with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
. In Appendix
A
we prove that the most general form of
E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
that is compatible with all three physical requirements is

\displaystyle E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=\lambda\,{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}+(1-\lambda)\,{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}^{*}

(12)

where
\lambda\in[0,1]
and

\displaystyle{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}:=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[M_{x}H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}\equiv\frac{\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho)]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

(13)

is the generalised weak value of the Hamiltonian
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
, given the initial state
\rho
, and postselected by outcome
x
of the POVM
M

[
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
.

The weak value
{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}
is generically a complex number associated with conditional observables in quantum mechanics.
While its imaginary part is a non-universal feature usually associated with the dynamics and back-action of the measurement process
[
40
]
, the real part is the universal (independent of the particular measurement implementation) response of a detector in the limit of a vanishing measurement disturbance, and can be associated with the physical estimation of conditional quantities
[
41
,
42
]
; indeed, as discussed in
[
43
]
, the real component of
{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}
can be understood as the limit of Eq. (
9
) as the disturbance of
\rho
due to the initial measurement of
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
becomes vanishingly small.

Therefore, Eq. (
12
) has an operationally meaningful interpretation when
\lambda=1/2
, in which case it reduces to the real part of the weak value, and we shall use it as the definition of the initial conditional energy in Eq. (
4
), i.e.

\displaystyle E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Re}\left({}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\mathrm{tr}[(M_{x}H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)M_{x})\rho]}{2p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}.

(14)

We note that in the stochastic thermodynamics literature
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
, the conditional change in energy along “trajectories”, which can be thought of as being determined by outcomes of a general measurement, is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{E}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x):=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho(x)]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho].

(15)

Here, the first term coincides with our definition for the conditional energy after the measurement process, i.e.
E_{x,\tau}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
. However, the second term is the average
unconditional
energy of the system prior to measurement. While Eq. (
15
) satisfies Requirement
1
, it will fail to satisfy Requirement
2
, since Eq. (
6
) will only be satisfied if
\rho
is a pure state. Moreover, Eq. (
15
) clearly fails to satisfy Requirement
3
.

In Appendix (
B
) we show how Eq. (
4
) can be generalised so as to give an additive value for conditional energy change due to any measurement
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
from the sequence of measurements
{\mathcal{M}}:=\{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
, with outcomes
x:=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
. This is done by introducing a trivial measurement
{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}
, with only one outcome, at the end of the sequence
{\mathcal{M}}
, so that
\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}}(A)]=\mathrm{tr}[A]
for any operator
A
. The conditional increase in energy due to the
i\textsuperscript{th}
measurement in the sequence can therefore be defined as
\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(x)=E_{x_{i+1},t_{i+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}}(\rho)-E_{x_{i},t_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(\rho)
, where

\displaystyle E_{x_{i},t_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(\rho):=\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(i)(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i})\rho(x_{i-1}))]\right)}{\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(i)(\rho(x_{i-1}))]}.

(16)

Here
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(i):={\mathcal{I}}_{x_{I+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}
is the instrument implemented by the measurements
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
through to
{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}
;
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i})
is the system Hamiltonian before the measurement of
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
in the sequence
{\mathcal{M}}
; and
\rho(x_{i})={\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}(\rho)/\mathrm{tr}[\mathrm{idem}]
is the state of the system after the
i
th
measurement in the sequence. Here we denote
\rho(x_{0}):=\rho
. It follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(x)=\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)
, in full agreement with the conditional energy increase when the full sequence of measurements are considered.

3.1
Relation between the proposed definition and previous work

The proposed definition of conditional energy change is rather general, and it encompasses and generalises known protocols. First, if a quantum system is measured by the instruments
{\mathcal{I}}_{(m,n)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho):=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)UP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)
, where
U
is a unitary operator while
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)

4
Work statistics for measurements

In order to define the work statistics for a measurement model
{\mathcal{M}}
, we must extend the above defined conditional energy increase to the measurement apparatus. The premeasurement and objectification steps of the measurement process can be defined by the instrument
{\mathcal{J}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho\otimes\xi):=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}
.
Therefore, by Eq. (
4
), the conditional increase in energy of the compound system
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
, given outcome
x
of the POVM
M
, is

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)){\mathcal{J}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho\otimes\xi)]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{J}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}((H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0))\rho\otimes\xi)]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}.

(17)

\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)
can be identified as the conditional work,
{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)
, if it is in principle possible for this quantity to account for all the energy change in the universe as a result of this measurement. We must therefore be able to implement an ideal measurement of the observable
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}xP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}
on the apparatus
{\mathcal{A}}
, by an inclusion of a second apparatus
{\mathcal{B}}
, such that the conditional energy change of the larger system
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}+{\mathcal{B}}
will equal that of
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
given by Eq. (
4
). This way all the energy changes due to measurement will be accounted for, and the source of this energy can be understood as originating from an (implicit) external work storage device, which is outside of the quantum description, the effect of which is the time-dependence of the Hamiltonian of
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
. In Appendix (
D
) we show that this is possible for all states
\rho
and observables
M
if and only if we ensure that
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
commutes with the Hamiltonian of
{\mathcal{A}}
when the objectification step of the measurement process is assumed to take place, which is
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)
. This is a consequence of the Wigner-Araki-Yanase (WAY) theorem
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
. Given this restriction on
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
, Eq. (
4
) becomes

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau))U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}U(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0))(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}.

(18)

The above expression implies that, if
U
commutes with the total Hamiltonian
H=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0)
, then
{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)=0
for all outcomes
x
and initial states
\rho
. This strengthens previous results, which only established that the average work vanishes when
U
and
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
commute with the total Hamiltonian
[
47
]
.

In the inset of Fig.

we plot
{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)
for a specific implementation of the sequential measurement introduced in the previous section (see Appendix (

\theta_{2}=0
.

5
Non-recoverable work and the second law of thermodynamics

In order to relate the obtained work statistics of measurement with the second law of thermodynamics, we make the measurement process cyclic by use of a single thermal bath of temperature
T
. Namely, at the end of the measurement process we must return the Hamiltonian of system and apparatus to the initial configuration
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0)
, while also returning the state of the compound system to
\rho\otimes\xi
. The average “non-recoverable” or “irreversible” work for the measurement process
{\mathcal{M}}
will therefore be given by the average work of performing the measurement,
\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x){\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)
, plus the
minimal
average work of returning the system to its initial configuration. Below, we shall show that the non-recoverable work will always be non-negative, thus satisfying the second law.

The average work
\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle
is obtained from
Eq. (
4
) to be
\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle=\mathrm{tr}[(U^{\dagger}H(\tau)U-H(0))(\rho\otimes\xi)]
, where
H(0)=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0)
and
H(\tau)=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)
. Recall that the average state of
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
at the end of the measurement process is
\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
, defined in Eq. (
2
). Denoting the average state of the system at the end of measurement as
\rho_{\tau}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}]
, and the average state of the apparatus at the end of premeasurement and objectification as respectively
\xi^{\prime}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]
and
\xi_{\tau}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}]
, the average work can be recast in terms of free energy and information related quantities as

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}+\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big),

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}-I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\prime}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big).

(19)

Here
k_{B}
is Boltzmann’s constant;
\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}
and
\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
are the increase in free energy of system and apparatus due to the transformations
\rho\mapsto\rho_{\tau}
and
\xi\mapsto\xi_{\tau}
, respectively, while
\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
is the increase in free energy of the compound system due to the transformation
\rho\otimes\xi\mapsto\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
;
I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}:=S(\rho_{\tau})+S(\xi^{\prime})-S(U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger})
is the quantum mutual information between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
after premeasurement, where
S(\rho)
is the von Neumann entropy of
\rho
;
I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\prime}:=S(\rho_{\tau})+S(\xi_{\tau})-S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}})
is the quantum mutual information between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
after the measurement process;
\mathscr{H}:=-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\,\mathrm{ln}\left({p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}\right)
is the Shannon entropy of the measurement probability distribution
p_{\rho}^{M}(x)
; and
\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}:=S(\xi^{\prime})-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)S(\xi(x))
is the Holevo information of the apparatus with respect to the ideal measurement of
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
and its state after premeasurement,
\xi^{\prime}
. See Appendix (
F
) for details.

There are two ways of returning the compound system to its initial state
\rho\otimes\xi
by use of a single thermal bath with temperature
T
: (a) first couple
{\mathcal{S}}
with the bath to realise the desired transformation on
{\mathcal{S}}
, and then repeat the process with
{\mathcal{A}}
; or (b) couple the compound system
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
with the bath to bring the total system to the desired state. In case (a) the minimum work cost is
-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
, whereas in case (b) the minimum work cost is
-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
. Both of these are achieved in the limit when the process by which the systems are coupled with the bath is quasi-static
[
48
]
. Therefore, in case (a) the non-recoverable work of measurement will be

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}},

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big),

(20)

whereas in case (b) the non-recoverable work will be

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}},

\displaystyle={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)-k_{B}T\,I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\prime}.

(21)

Since case (b) uses the correlations between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
to effect the desired transformation, whereas case (a) does not, we refer to
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
as the “inclusive” and “non-inclusive” non-recoverable work, respectively
[
49
]
.

The non-negativity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
follows from the fact that
I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}\geqslant 0
and
\mathscr{H}\geqslant\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\geqslant 0
. Similarly, the non-negativity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
can be shown by re-expressing it as
k_{B}T(S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}})-S(\rho\otimes\xi))
, and noting that the measurement process is unital
[
50
,
51
]
. Therefore, the work statistics of measurement as defined by Eq. (
4
) will obey the Kelvin statement of the second law
[
52
]
. Moreover, the non-negativity of the mutual information implies that
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)\geqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
. We note that the non-inclusive non-recoverable work of measurement is related to the work cost of measurement,
E_{\!{\,\mathrm{cost}}}
, discussed in
[
18
,
21
]
. Here, only the apparatus is returned to its initial configuration by interacting with the bath. Consequently, it is related to the non-inclusive non-recoverable work by the equality
E_{\!{\,\mathrm{cost}}}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)+\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}
.

5.1
Non-recoverable work of projective measurements

In
[
21
]
it was shown that the work cost of ideal projective measurements is given as
E_{\!{\,\mathrm{cost}}}^{\mathrm{proj}}=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\rho_{\tau}-\rho)]+k_{B}T\mathscr{H}
, i.e. it only depends on the observable measured and the system states; quantities pertaining to the apparatus do not appear (unlike the case for general observables). We may generalise this for both the inclusive and non-inclusive non-recoverable work, and for repeatable projective measurements of a sharp observable
M
. Recall that an ideal measurement of
M
is implemented by the instrument
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)=M_{x}\rho M_{x}
. A repeatable measurement of
M
can be implemented by the instrument
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)=\sum_{i}q_{i}V_{x,i}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)V_{x,i}^{\dagger}
, where
q_{i}
is a probability distribution, and
V_{x,i}
are unitaries acting non-trivially only on the subspace projected onto by
M_{x}
. Such a measurement is called repeatable because
\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)]=\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)]
, i.e. if we measure the system and obtain outcome
x
, performing the same measurement will reveal
x
with certainty. Clearly,
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}={\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}
if
V_{x,i}=\mathds{1}
for all
i
. It follows that the non-recoverable work for repeatable measurements reduces from Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
5
) to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(\mathscr{H}+S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Rep}})-S(\rho)\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}})-S(\rho)\big),

(22)

where
\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Rep}}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)
and
\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)
. Note that while the non-inclusive non-recoverable work depends on the particular instrument
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}
, the same is not true for the inclusive non-recoverable work. The reason that Eq. (
5.1
) is obtained is that for repeatable measurements,
S(\xi(x))=S(\xi)
for all
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
(see Appendix (
G
)).

As discussed in
[
53
,
22
]
, ideal (or repeatable) projective measurements require that the initial apparatus state
\xi
not have full rank. Therefore, by the third law of thermodynamics, such measurements will require infinite resources
[
53
,
54
,
55
,
56
]
. To account for this,
[
22
]
introduced measurement models of a PVM wherein the apparatus is initially in a thermal state of finite temperature. These measurements will necessarily be unrepeatable. We show in Appendix (
G
) that the non-recoverable work for projective measurements that use a full-rank apparatus of dimension
\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})=\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}})
, which we call noisy projective measurements, is of the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho)=k_{B}T\big(S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}})-S(\rho)\big).

(23)

Here, the premeasurement unitary is a SWAP operation, followed by an appropriate local unitary on
{\mathcal{A}}
. Consequently the mutual information terms in Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
5
) vanish, and we are left with
\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}=S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}})-S(\rho)
. As with the case of ideal and repeatable measurements, the non-recoverable work for such noisy measurements is purely determined by the instrument implementing
M
, and
\rho
; the non-recoverable work is independent of the apparatus state. However, since noisy measurements require the apparatus to have the same Hilbert space as the system, the independence of the non-recoverable work on the apparatus is not as general as the case for repeatable measurements.

Finally, comparing all three classes of projective measurements, we observe the following relations:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)\geqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho).

(24)

The first inequality in the top line is due to the random unitary mixing in
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}
, with the equality condition being satisfied if
V_{x,i}=V_{x}
for all
i
and
x
. Meanwhile, the second inequality in the top line follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)-{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho)=k_{B}T\,\mathscr{H}
, and so the equality condition is only obtained if
\mathscr{H}=0
. However, these inequalities are replaced with equalities on the bottom line, showing that the inclusive non-recoverable work of repeatable, ideal, and noisy measurements is identical.

6
Conclusions

In the present work we have defined the change of energy, conditional on the outcome of a general quantum measurement, as the difference in conditional energies of the system, evaluated before and after the measurement process. In order for this definition to be physically meaningful, we imposed three requirements on the definitions for the initial and final conditional energies. We showed that the most general expression for the conditional energy of the system, after the measurement process, is simply the expected value of the Hamiltonian, given the transformed state of the system after a measurement outcome. This is as one would expect. However the most general, and operationally meaningful, expression for the conditional energy of the system, evaluated before the measurement process, is not the expected value of the Hamiltonian given the initial state, but rather the real component of the weak value of the Hamiltonian. We have shown that our definition provides a unified platform which, in specific cases, reproduces previously existing results such as the TPM or quasi-probability distributions for work in isolated systems, and energy change along pure-state quantum trajectories. By extending our definition to the apparatus used to measure an observable, we determine the work statistics for general quantum measurements, and show that these vanish for all measurement outcomes when the measurement process conserves energy. Finally, to link the work distribution to the second law of thermodynamics, we characterise the non-recoverable work when the measurement process is embedded within a cyclic heat engine in contact with a single thermal bath. The non-recoverable work is non-negative, thus satisfying the second law. Moreover, we show that the non-recoverable work is purely determined by system quantities for two classes of projective measurements: repeatable projective measurements, and non-repeatable, noisy projective measurements. Here, noisy projective measurements are implemented by use of an apparatus that has the same dimension as the system, and is initially in a state of full-rank.

\rho
thus satisfying (i), and when
\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t))]=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]
, Eq. (
26
) will reduce to
\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho(x)]
, thus satisfying Requirement
3
. To see this, we note that given this relation, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(t)\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho)],

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}\epsilon_{j}(t)\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{k}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho)],

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(t)\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t))],

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(t)\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{j}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)].

(27)

Finally, in order for Eq. (
26
) to satisfy (ii), thus fulfilling all three physical requirements, we must have
\gamma=0
for
t=0
, and
\gamma=1
for
t=\tau
. This is because
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t){\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho_{\tau}]
, whereas

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\lambda\,\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho)]+(1-\lambda)\,\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho)]^{*}=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)\rho].

(28)

Therefore, the most general expression for the conditional energies which satisfy all three physical requirements are

\displaystyle E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\,\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho)]+(1-\lambda)\,\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho)]^{*}}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)},

\displaystyle\equiv\lambda\,{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}+(1-\lambda)\,{}_{x}\langle H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rangle_{\rho}^{*},

(29)

and

\displaystyle E_{x,\tau}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho(x)].

(30)

Appendix B
Conditional change in energy given multiple measurements

In many situations, a system may be subject to a sequence of measurements. However, care must be taken when considering the corresponding conditional energy changes, since these are not additive when only one measurement is considered at a time. Specifically, consider the measurement
{\mathcal{M}}:=\{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
, with the corresponding outcomes
x:=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I}
. Here, the system is sequentially measured by the observables
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
, with the recorded outcomes
x_{i}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
. Given the initial preparation of the system as
\rho
, and assuming that the Hamiltonians before and after the
i
th
measurement are
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i})
and
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i+1})
respectively, the corresponding energy change given the full sequence of measurements is given as
\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}}(x)=E_{x,t_{I+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}}(\rho)-E_{x,t_{1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}}(\rho)=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{I+1})\rho(x_{I})]-\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{1})\rho)])/\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]
, where
\rho(x_{i})
is the normalised state of
{\mathcal{S}}
after the
i
th
measurement in the sequence, and
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{I}}_{x_{I}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I}}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}
the sequential application of instruments
{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}
. Denoting
\rho\equiv\rho(x_{0})
, the sum of conditional energy changes for each measurement will be

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\Delta E_{\rho(x_{i-1})}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i+1})\rho(x_{i})]

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i})\rho(x_{i-1}))]}{\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(\rho(x_{i-1})]}\right),

\displaystyle\neq\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}}(x).

(31)

The reason for the non-additivity is two-fold. First, the final energy after measuring
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
, namely,
\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i+1})\rho(x_{i})]
, does not equal the initial energy before measuring
{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}
, namely,
\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i+1})\rho(x_{i}))])/\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}}(\rho(x_{i}))]
. Secondly, the weak value of the Hamiltonian evaluated at any point in the sequence of measurements depends on the full sequence of measurements performed afterwards, and not just the measurement performed immediately after.

In order to have an additive notion of conditional energy changes, we must extend our definition as follows: first, augment the sequence of measurements by including a trivial measurement at the end, i.e., let
{\mathcal{M}}:=\{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{I+1}
, such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}
is a trivial observable that does not reveal any information about the system, and is equivalent to not performing any measurement at all. In other words let
{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}
have only one outcome
x_{I+1}
, with the instrument satisfying
\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{I+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}}(A)]=\mathrm{tr}[A]
. The conditional energy of the system, before measuring the
i
th
observable in the sequence, can thus be given as

\displaystyle E_{x_{i},t_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(\rho):=\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{tr}[({\mathcal{I}}_{x_{I+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}})(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t_{i})\rho(x_{i-1}))])}{\mathrm{tr}[({\mathcal{I}}_{x_{I+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{I}}_{x_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}})\rho(x_{i-1}))]}.

(32)

Note that
E_{x_{I+1},t_{I+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{I+1}}(\rho)=E_{x,t_{I+1}}^{\mathcal{M}}
, and
E_{x_{1},t_{1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}(\rho)=E_{x,t_{1}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
.
By defining the change in conditional energy during the
i
th
measurement as
\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(x_{i}):=E_{x_{i+1},t_{i+1}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i+1}}(\rho)-E_{x_{i},t_{i}}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(\rho)
, we therefore obtain an additive notion of conditional energy change which satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{i}}(x_{i})=\Delta E_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}}(x)
.

For long sequences of measurements, such as those encountered in stochastic quantum thermodynamics, calculating Eq. (
32
) can be very arduous. However, as shown in Appendix (
C C.3
), if the measurement of
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
first projects
\rho(x_{i-1})
onto one of its pure state components
|{\psi(x_{i-1})}\rangle
, then Eq. (
32
) is reduced to the simple expression
\langle\psi(x_{i-1})|H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}|\psi(x_{i-1})\rangle
.

Appendix C
Limiting cases of conditional energy change

C.1
Connection to the TPM protocol

Consider the measurement model
{\mathcal{M}}
which induces the instruments
{\mathcal{I}}_{(m,n)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)UP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)
, and the associated effect operators
M_{(m,n)}:=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)UP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)
for the POVM
M
with outcomes
x:=(m,n)
. Here,
U
is a unitary operator, while
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(t)
are time-local spectral projections of a Hamiltonian
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)
, with energy eigenvalues
\epsilon_{n}(t)
. By Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
3
), the conditional change in energy for each outcome can thus be written as

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(m,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau){\mathcal{I}}_{(m,n)}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(m,n)}-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{(m,n)}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho)]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(m,n)},

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{n}(\tau)-\epsilon_{m}(0),

(33)

which is a difference in energy eigenvalues. This is the work distribution one obtains from the TPM protocol, wherein a quantum system undergoing unitary evolution
U
generated by its Hamiltonian is projectively measured by its time-local Hamiltonian at two points of its evolution. The average change in energy, over all measurement outcomes, is thus given by the probabilities
p_{\rho}^{M}(m,n):=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)UP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho]
to be

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}p_{\rho}^{M}(m,n)\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(m,n)=\sum_{m}\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)UP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)U^{\dagger}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho].

(34)

C.2
Connection to quasi-probability distributions of work in isolated systems

As is well known, Eq. (
34
) will fail to equal the increase in average energy of an isolated system as it unitarily evolves by
U
, namely
\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)U\rho U^{\dagger}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]
, except for when
\rho
commutes with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
. Let us therefore consider a different measurement model
{\mathcal{M}}
, which induces the instruments
{\mathcal{I}}_{n}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)U\rho U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)
with the associated effect operators
M_{n}:=U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)U
for the POVM
M
with outcomes
n
. This is equivalent to the ideal measurement of the Heisenberg evolved Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau):=U^{\dagger}H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)U
, with spectral projections
\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau):=U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)U
. The conditional change in energy now becomes

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau){\mathcal{I}}_{n}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(n)}-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[{\mathcal{I}}_{n}^{\mathcal{M}}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho)]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(n)},

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{n}(\tau)-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(n)},

(35)

which always averages to

\displaystyle\sum_{n}p_{\rho}^{M}(n)\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(n)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)U\rho U^{\dagger}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho].

(36)

However, this comes at the expense of the energy change to no longer necessarily be equal to the difference in energy eigenvalues when
\rho
commutes with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
. This is because unless
\rho
only has support on a single energy subspace, then the conditional initial energy in Eq. (
C C.2
) will be an average over energy eigenvalues. Indeed, in order for
\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[M_{x}H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]\right)/p_{\rho}^{M}(x)=\epsilon_{m}(0)
to obtain for any state
\rho
that commutes with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
, then it is necessary for the support of
M_{x}
to be contained in the energy subspace of
\epsilon_{m}(0)
. It will follow that
M_{x}
would be equivalent to
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)M_{x}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)
, resulting in the instruments
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)={\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0))
and, hence, the channel
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho):=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=\sum_{m}{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathcal{M}}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0))
. We may conclude that the channel
{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathcal{M}}
cannot model the unitary evolution of a system with a state
\rho
that does not commute with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)
. As such, when the state
\rho
does not commute with the Hamiltonian, it follows that the measurement will not result in an average work that equals
\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)U\rho U^{\dagger}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]
. In other words a measurement model
{\mathcal{M}}
that obtains the TPM statistics of work, when the system is prepared as a mixture of energy eigenstates, cannot give the average work as being the increase in average energy, as the system transforms unitarily, for arbitrary states. Our definition for conditional energy change is therefore in full agreement with the no-go theorem of
[
16
]
.

We now show that the generalisation of conditional energy change beyond energy eigenvalue differences is equivalent to the generalisation of probabilities to quasiprobabilities introduced in
[
13
]
. The summand in Eq. (
36
) can be written as

\displaystyle p_{\rho}^{M}(n)\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(n)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{n}(\tau)\mathrm{tr}[\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)\rho]-\sum_{m}\epsilon_{m}(0)\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho]\right),

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\epsilon_{n}(\tau)\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho]\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{m}\epsilon_{m}(0)\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho]\right),

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\tilde{p}_{(m,n)}(\epsilon_{n}(\tau)-\epsilon_{m}(0)).

(37)

On the left hand side, we have a real probability distribution
p_{\rho}^{M}(n)
, and energy change
\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(n)
which may lie outside the range of energy eigenvalue differences. Meanwhile, on the right hand side, the energy change of
{\mathcal{S}}
is defined with respect to the random variable
\epsilon_{n}(\tau)-\epsilon_{m}(0)
, which is sampled by the quasi-probability distribution
\tilde{p}_{(m,n)}:=\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[\tilde{P}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{n}(\tau)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}^{m}(0)\rho]\right)
.

C.3
Connection to quantum stochastic thermodynamics

Consider a quantum state
\rho=\sum_{m}q_{m}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi_{m}}]
, where
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi}]\equiv|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
is a projection on the vector
|{\psi}\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}
. We may define a quantum trajectory induced by the POVM
M:=\{M^{(1)},M^{(2)}\}
as the sequence of measurement outcomes
x:=(m,n)
, with
m
the outcome of an ideal projective measurement of the observable
M^{(1)}:=\sum_{m}mP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi_{m}}]
, and
n
the outcome of a subsequent measurement of a general POVM
M^{(2)}
. Given that the effect operators for the POVM
M
defined by the sequence of measurements are
M_{x}:=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi_{m}}]M_{n}^{(2)}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi_{m}}]
, the conditional initial energy of the system is given by Eq. (
3
) as

\displaystyle E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi_{m}}]M_{n}^{(2)}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\psi_{m}}]H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)},

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{m}\langle\psi_{m}|M_{n}^{(2)}|\psi_{m}\rangle\langle\psi_{m}|H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)|\psi_{m}\rangle}{q_{m}\langle\psi_{m}|M_{n}^{(2)}|\psi_{m}\rangle},

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{m}|H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)|\psi_{m}\rangle.

(38)

Therefore, the conditional initial energy of the system, along the quantum trajectory
x=(m,n)
, is the expected energy of the system when it is in the pure state
|{\psi_{m}}\rangle
that defines the starting point of the trajectory in question. We note that this result holds even when
\rho
is already a pure state
|{\psi_{m}}\rangle
. Of course, the preparation of a quantum system in a pure state can also be recast in terms of projective measurements. Cast in this light, this means that in order for
E_{x,0}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)=\langle\psi_{m}|H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)|\psi_{m}\rangle
to hold, then
M
must also account for the preparation of the system in state
|{\psi}\rangle
. This should not be too surprising, however, since when we say that we “know” that the system starts in a given pure state
|{\psi_{m}}\rangle
, operationally this means that we must have performed a projective measurement to verify this fact.

Appendix D
When extending the measurement apparatus will not change the total conditional increase in energy

Let us augment the measurement model
{\mathcal{M}}:=({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}},\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}},U,Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})
by including a measurement model for
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
. The augmented model is denoted
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}:=({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}},\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}},VU,Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})
. Here
\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}
is the initial state of apparatus
{\mathcal{B}}
, and
V
is a unitary operator that acts on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}
after the application of
U
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
. Finally,
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}xP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}
is the observable on apparatus
{\mathcal{B}}
. Denoting
H(t):=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(t)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}(t)
for
t\in\{0,\tau\}
, and remembering that
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
are models for the same POVM
M
and, hence, give the same probabilities, we obtain the conditional energy increase of the compound system by generalising Eq. (
4
) as

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[H(\tau)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}VU(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})U^{\dagger}V^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}VUH(0)(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})U^{\dagger}V^{\dagger}]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}.

(39)

We now wish to show that it is possible to have
\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}}(x)=\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}}(x)
for all states
\rho
if
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
commutes with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)
. Let us assume that this commutation relation holds. By the WAY theorem
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
, it follows that we may perform an ideal measurement of
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
by a unitary coupling
V
with an apparatus
{\mathcal{B}}
, followed by an ideal measurement of this apparatus by
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}
, such that both
V
and
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}
commute with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}(\tau)
. If these conditions, together with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}(\tau)=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}(0)
, are satisfied, then Eq. (
D
) can be shown to reduce to

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[V^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}V(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau))U(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})U^{\dagger}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

\displaystyle\,\,-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[V^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}VU(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0))(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})U^{\dagger}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)},

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathrm{tr}[V^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}VU(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})U^{\dagger}\otimes(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}(\tau)-H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}(0))\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau))U(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})U^{\dagger}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}

\displaystyle\,\,-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}U(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0))(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})U^{\dagger}]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}=\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)

(40)

for all
\rho
. In the last line, we have used the fact that
\mathrm{tr}[V^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{x}V(O_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})]=\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}O_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}]
by construction. Therefore, if
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
commutes with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)
, then it is possible to extend the measurement model while not changing the total conditional increase in energy due to measurement.

To show that
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
commuting with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)
is the only possibility for
\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}}(x)=\Delta{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)
to hold for all
\rho
, let us look at a counter example. By the WAY theorem, if
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
does not commute with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)
, then an ideal measurement of this observable precludes the possibility that
V
commutes with
H(\tau)
. As such, even if we choose
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}
to commute with
H(\tau)
, it will generally not be possible to reduce Eq. (
D
) to Eq. (
4
). In such cases, the energy change of
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
cannot account for the total energy change due to measurement.

Appendix E
Model for a sequence of measurements on a two-level system

Let us consider a two-level system
{\mathcal{S}}
with Hamiltonian
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}(P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{e}]-P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{g}])

{\mathcal{A}}

{\mathcal{B}}
, with Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\simeq\mathds{C}^{2}

{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}\simeq\mathds{C}^{4}

H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}=\mathds{1}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}

|{\theta,\pm}\rangle:=\pm\cos(\theta/2)|{g/e}\rangle+\sin(\theta/2)|{e/g}\rangle

\displaystyle U_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}:=|{\theta_{2},\pm}\rangle\otimes|{\phi}\rangle\mapsto|{\theta_{2},\pm}\rangle\otimes|{\phi_{\pm}}\rangle,

(41)

and

\displaystyle U_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{B}}}:=\begin{cases}|{g}\rangle\otimes|{1}\rangle\mapsto|{e}\rangle\otimes|{0}\rangle,\\
|{e}\rangle\otimes|{1}\rangle\mapsto|{e}\rangle\otimes|{1}\rangle,\\
|{g}\rangle\otimes|{2}\rangle\mapsto|{g}\rangle\otimes|{2}\rangle,\\
|{e}\rangle\otimes|{2}\rangle\mapsto|{g}\rangle\otimes|{3}\rangle.\end{cases}

(42)

The full premeasurement unitary is then given as
U=U_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{B}}}U_{{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}
. Let the systems

{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
be initially prepared in states
\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi}]
and
\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}=qP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}[1]+(1-q)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}[2]
, respectively. Finally, let the apparatus observables be
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{+}}]-P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{-}}]
and
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}=eP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{e}+gP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{g}
, where
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{e}=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}[0]+P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}[1]
and
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{g}=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}[2]+P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}[3]
.

This model defines the POVM
M_{m,n}
, where
M_{\pm,e}:=qP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\theta_{2},\pm}]
and
M_{\pm,g}:=(1-q)P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\theta_{2},\pm}]
, with the post-measurement states of
{\mathcal{S}}
being given as
\rho(\pm,e)=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{e}]
and
\rho(\pm,g)=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{g}]
.

By Eq. (
4
), the conditional change in energy of
{\mathcal{S}}
is given as

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(\pm,e/g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\pm}]P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{e/g}U(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})U^{\dagger}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(\pm,e/g)}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\pm}]P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{e/g}U(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})U^{\dagger}]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(\pm,e/g)},

(43)

and similarly the work is given by Eq. (
4
) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(\pm,e/g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathrm{tr}[HP_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\pm}]P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{e/g}U(\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})U^{\dagger}]}{p_{\rho}^{M}(\pm,e/g)}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\mathrm{tr}[P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\pm}]P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}^{e/g}U(H\rho\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\otimes\xi_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}})U^{\dagger}]\right)}{p_{\rho}^{M}(\pm,e/g)},

(44)

where
H=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{B}}}}
is the total Hamiltonian. It is simple to verify that when
\theta_{2}=0
, then
U
commutes with
H
, and so the work values for all four outcomes will vanish. Fig.
2
shows the conditional energy change and work statistics for this measurement model, when the initial system state is chosen as
\rho=P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[{\theta_{1},+}]
, with
\theta_{1}=\pi/2
.

Appendix F
Non-recoverable work due to measurement

Recall that at the end of the measurement process, the average state of the compound system
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
is
\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
defined in Eq. (
2
), while the average state of
{\mathcal{S}}
is
\rho_{\tau}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}]\equiv\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]\equiv\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
, and the average state of
{\mathcal{A}}
is
\xi_{\tau}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}]\equiv\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\xi(x)
. Moreover, the average state of
{\mathcal{A}}
after premeasurement is
\xi^{\prime}:=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]
. Using the fact that
\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}=\mathds{1}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
, then by Eq. (
4
) the average work, over all measurement outcomes, can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x){\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x),

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau))U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathrm{tr}[(H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0))(\rho\otimes\xi)],

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho_{\tau}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]+\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)\xi^{\prime}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0)\xi],

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau)\rho_{\tau}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)\rho]+\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)\xi_{\tau}]-\mathrm{tr}[H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0)\xi].

(45)

In the last line, we have used the fact that
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
commutes with
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau)
to infer that
\xi^{\prime}
and
\xi_{\tau}
have the same expected energies.

The free energy of a quantum state
\rho
, with respect to the Hamiltonian
H
and temperature
T
, is defined as

\displaystyle F(\rho,H,T):=\mathrm{tr}[H\rho]-k_{B}T\,S(\rho),

(46)

where
k_{B}
is Boltzmann’s constant and
S(\rho):=-\mathrm{tr}[\rho\,\mathrm{ln}\left({\rho}\right)]
is the von Neumann entropy of
\rho
. Moreover, we note that since
\xi_{\tau}
is a mixture of orthogonal states
\xi(x)
, it follows that
S(\xi_{\tau})=\mathscr{H}+\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)S(\xi(x))
, where
\mathscr{H}:=-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\,\mathrm{ln}\left({p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}\right)
is the Shannon entropy of the measurement probabilities. By using Eq. (
46
), and the aforementioned relation, we can restate Eq. (
F
) as

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}+\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(S(\rho_{\tau})+S(\xi_{\tau})-S(\rho)-S(\xi)\big)

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}+\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}+S(\xi_{\tau})-S(\xi^{\prime})\big),

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}+\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big).

(47)

Here
\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}:=F(\rho_{\tau},H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(\tau),T)-F(\rho,H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0),T)
and
\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}:=F(\xi_{\tau},H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(\tau),T)-F(\xi,H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0),T)
are the increase in free energy of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
, respectively;
I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}:=S(\rho_{\tau})+S(\xi^{\prime})-S(U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger})
is the quantum mutual information between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
after the premeasurement unitary evolution; and
\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}:=S(\xi^{\prime})-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)S(\xi(x))
is the Holevo information of the apparatus with respect to the ideal measurement of
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
and state
\xi^{\prime}
, which obeys the inequality
\mathscr{H}\geqslant\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\geqslant 0

[
50
,
51
]
. Alternatively, the average work can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}})-S(\rho\otimes\xi)\big)

\displaystyle=\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}+k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}-I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\prime}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big),

(48)

where
\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}:=F(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}},H(\tau),T)-F(\rho\otimes\xi,H(0),T)
is the increase in free energy of the compound system, where
H(t)=H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(t)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(t)
for
t=0,\tau
, and
I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\prime}:=S(\rho_{\tau})+S(\xi_{\tau})-S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}})
is the mutual information between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
at the end of the measurement process.

Returning the Hamiltonian of the compound system to
H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}(0)+H_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}(0)
, while returning the state of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
to
\rho
and
\xi
respectively, will incur a minimal work cost of
-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
and
-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
if the systems are coupled to the thermal bath individually, or collectively, respectively. In either case, the minimal work cost is achieved in the limit as the process is quasistatic
[
55
,
48
]
. Therefore, the non-inclusive non-recoverable work, which does not make use of the correlations between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
during the closing of the cycle, can be defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}},

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big),

(49)

while the inclusive non-recoverable work, which does make use of the correlations between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
, is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\rho}^{\mathcal{M}}(x)\rangle-\Delta F_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}},

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}})-S(\rho\otimes\xi)\big)\equiv{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)-k_{B}T\,I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\prime}.

(50)

We note that the non-negativity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
is guaranteed by the fact that
I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}\geqslant 0
and
\mathscr{H}\geqslant\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\geqslant 0
, while the non-negativity of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
is guaranteed by the fact that the quantum channel that implements the transformation
\rho\otimes\xi\mapsto\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}
is unital. The non-negativity of the mutual information also implies that
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)\geqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathcal{M}}(\rho)
.

Appendix G
Non-recoverable work due to projective measurements

Consider the self adjoint operator
M=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}xM_{x}
that defines a projective-valued measure on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}
. Here,
M_{x}
are projection operators of arbitrary rank. We wish to implement this by the measurement model
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}},\xi,U,Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})
, where
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{i}}]
(with
0<q_{i}<1
and
\sum_{i}q_{i}=1
) is a mixed state of rank
1\leqslant r\leqslant\mathrm{dim}({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})
. By linearity, for each
i
,
({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}},P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{i}}],U,Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})
must also be a measurement model for
M

[
37
]
. Denoting the eigenstates of
M
as
|{\psi_{x}^{\alpha}}\rangle
, with
\alpha
denoting the degeneracy, a general (albeit not the most general) prescription of premeasurement will be

\displaystyle U:|{\psi_{x}^{\alpha}}\rangle\otimes|{\phi_{i}}\rangle\mapsto|{\tilde{\psi}_{x,i}^{\alpha}}\rangle\otimes|{\phi_{x,i}}\rangle,

(51)

where
|{\phi_{x,i}}\rangle
is an eigenvector of
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
with eigenvalue
x
, while for each
i
and
x
,
\{|{\tilde{\psi}_{x,i}^{\alpha}}\rangle\}_{\alpha}
is an orthonormal set of vectors that need not be eigenstates of
M
. This is referred to as a Type-4 measurement
[
39
]
.

If upon observing outcome
x
of
M
, a subsequent measurement of
M
will yield
x
with certainty, the measurement of
M
is said to be repeatable. Given the premeasurement unitary defined in Eq. (
51
), the measurement will be repeatable if
|{\tilde{\psi}_{x,i}^{\alpha}}\rangle=V_{x,i}|{\psi_{x}^{\alpha}}\rangle
are eigenstates of
M
with eigenvalue
x
. Here,
V_{x,i}
is a unitary operator that acts non-trivially only on the support of
M_{x}
. Such a measurement model is referred to as a Type-3 measurement, and will implement the instrument
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}
defined as:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}V_{x,i}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)V_{x,i}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=M_{x}\rho M_{x}.

(52)

In the special case where, for each
i
,
V_{x,i}=\mathds{1}
, then we have
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}={\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}
, which is a Lüders instrument that implements an ideal (also known as a Type-2) measurement. If
M
is a non-degenerate observable, then the only repeatable measurements are automatically ideal. These are the standard von Neumann measurements, also known as Type-1.

If the measurement is repeatable, it follows that for every
i\neq j
, there exists a pair of vectors
|{\tilde{\psi}_{x,i}^{\alpha}}\rangle
and
|{\tilde{\psi}_{x,j}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}\rangle
with a non-vanishing inner product. As unitary operators preserve the inner product, this can only be achieved if
\langle\phi_{x,i}|\phi_{x,j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
. Therefore, the cardinality of the set of orthonormal vectors

\displaystyle\left\{|{\phi_{x,i}}\rangle:x\in\{1,\dots,|{\mathcal{X}}|\},i\in\{1,\dots,r\}\right\}

(53)

must be
r\,|{\mathcal{X}}|
, where
|{\mathcal{X}}|
is the cardinality of the outcome set
{\mathcal{X}}
. Since this value cannot exceed the dimension of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
, the rank of
\xi
must obey the inequality

\displaystyle r\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{dim}({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})}{|{\mathcal{X}}|}.

(54)

In other words,
\xi
cannot have full rank.

Let us express the initial state of the system as
\rho=\sum_{x,y,\alpha,\beta}c_{x,y,\alpha,\beta}|\psi_{x}^{\alpha}\rangle\langle\psi_{y}^{\beta}|
. Therefore, the conditional states of the compound system
{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}
, after a repeatable measurement process, will be

\displaystyle\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}U(\rho\otimes\xi)U^{\dagger}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x},

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,x,y,\alpha,\beta}\frac{q_{i}c_{x,y,\alpha,\beta}}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}|\tilde{\psi}_{x}^{\alpha}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}_{y}^{\beta}|\otimes P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x}|\phi_{x,i}\rangle\langle\phi_{y,i}|P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}^{x},

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,x,\alpha,\beta}\frac{q_{i}c_{x,x,\alpha,\beta}}{p_{\rho}^{M}(x)}|\tilde{\psi}_{x}^{\alpha}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}_{x}^{\beta}|\otimes P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{x,i}}],

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}q_{i}V_{x,i}\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x)V_{x,i}^{\dagger}\otimes P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{x,i}}],

(55)

where
\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x):={\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)/p_{\rho}^{M}(x)
. It is clear from this equation that the apparatus states representing outcome
x
will be

\displaystyle\xi(x):=\mathrm{tr}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}}[\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x)]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{x,i}}].

(56)

Therefore, the von Neumann entropy of
\xi(x)
will be equal to that of
\xi
for all
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
. This implies that the Holevo information of the apparatus, with respect to the state
\xi^{\prime}
and ideal measurement of
Z_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}
, is

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}

\displaystyle:=S(\xi^{\prime})-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)S(\xi(x)),

\displaystyle=S(\xi^{\prime})-S(\xi).

(57)

It follows that the non-inclusive non-recoverable work is, by Eq. (
5
), given as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(I_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}+\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big),

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(\mathscr{H}+S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Rep}})-S(\rho)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+k_{B}T\big(S(\xi^{\prime})-S(\xi)-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}\big),

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(\mathscr{H}+S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Rep}})-S(\rho)\big),

(58)

where
\rho^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x):={\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)/p_{\rho}^{M}(x)
and
\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Rep}}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)
. Similarly, the inclusive non-recoverable work for repeatable measurements can be obtained from Eq. (
5
) to be

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}})-S(\rho\otimes\xi)\big),

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\left(\mathscr{H}+\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))-S(\rho)-S(\xi)\right),

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\left(\mathscr{H}+\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\big(S(\rho^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))+S(\xi(x))-{\mathcal{I}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x)\big)-S(\rho)-S(\xi)\right),

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\left(\mathscr{H}+\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\big(S(\rho^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))-{\mathcal{I}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x)\big)-S(\rho)\right),

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\big(S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}})-S(\rho)\big),

(59)

where
{\mathcal{I}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x):=S(\rho^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))+S(\xi(x))-S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))
is the quantum mutual information between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
conditional on outcome
x
, and
\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)
. To see this, first note that because the states
P_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}[{\phi_{x,i}}]
are all orthogonal, the von Neumann entropy of the conditional states in Eq. (
G
) will be given as

\displaystyle S(\varrho_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}+{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))

\displaystyle=S(\xi)+\sum_{i}q_{i}S(V_{x,i}\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x)V_{x,i}^{\dagger}),

\displaystyle=S(\xi)+S(\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x)).

(60)

Therefore, since
S(\xi(x))=S(\xi)
, the conditional mutual information terms will be
{\mathcal{I}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{A}}}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x)=S(\rho^{\mathrm{Rep}}(x))-S(\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x))
.

Note that both
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)
do not depend on the apparatus specifics. However, while
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)
also depends on the particular instrument
{\mathcal{I}}_{x}^{\mathrm{Rep}}
, the same does not hold for
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)
, as the inclusive non-recoverable work will be the same for all repeatable instruments. Moreover, it follows from Uhlmann’s theorem
[
57
]
that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)-{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=k_{B}T\,\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}p_{\rho}^{M}(x)\left[S\left(\sum_{i}q_{i}V_{x,i}\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x)V_{x,i}^{\dagger}\right)-S\left(\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x)\right)\right]\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)-{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=0

(61)

with the equality condition in the first line being satisfied if and only if
V_{x,i}=V_{x}
for all
i
and
x
.

It has recently been suggested that, by the third law of thermodynamics, the preparation of the apparatus in a non-full rank state requires infinite resources and, as such, ideal (or repeatable) measurements are thermodynamically impossible
[
22
,
56
]
. To account for this, we consider measurement models where
\xi
has full-rank, i.e.
r=\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})
. We refer to such measurement models as “noisy” projective measurements.

Although the smallest dimension permissible for the apparatus is
\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})=|{\mathcal{X}}|
, wherin
|{\phi_{x,i}}\rangle=|{\phi_{x}}\rangle
for all
i
, we shall consider only the case where
\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})=\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{S}}}})
. Here,
\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}})\geqslant|{\mathcal{X}}|
, with equality only when
M
is a non-degenerate observable. The simplest premeasurement unitary which will allow for a measurement of
M
is a SWAP operator, followed by an appropriate local unitary on
{\mathcal{A}}
.

We immediately see that here, the mutual information terms in Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
5
) vanish, and so the inclusive and non-inclusive non-recoverable work will be identical. Moreover, since
S(\xi^{\prime})=S(\rho)
, and
S(\xi(x))=S(\rho^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(x))
, we may identify
\mathscr{H}-\mathscr{X}_{\!{\,{\mathcal{A}}}}=S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}})-S(\rho)
, thereby obtaining the non-recoverable work

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho)=k_{B}T\big(S(\rho_{\tau}^{\mathrm{Ideal}})-S(\rho)\big).

(62)

Comparison between Eqs. (
G
), (
G
), (
G
) and (
62
) allows us to write the following relations:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)\geqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Rep}}(\rho)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{inc.irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho).

(63)

Here, the first inequality in the top line results from Eq. (
G
), while the second inequality is a consequence of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Ideal}}(\rho)-{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{\mathrm{Noisy}}(\rho)=k_{B}T\,\mathscr{H}
.

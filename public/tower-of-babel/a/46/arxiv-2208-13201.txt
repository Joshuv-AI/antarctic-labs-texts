In the algebraic world, the

This approach was used by

In the analytical world, one is interested in the continuous fields of quantized coordinate rings, in the

To overcome this difficulty, one can look at the more general concept of higher-rank graph algebras, due to Kumjian and Pask

Roughly speaking, a higher-rank graph is a graph whose edges are classed into

In a recent advance, Olof Giselsson

The main result of this article is that the coordinate ring of any quantized compact semisimple Lie group admits a

The construction of the higher-rank graph, which is summarized below and makes use of the theory of crystal bases, will make clear the link between the algebraic and analytic approaches to the crystal limit.

The generators of the

They have a much simpler algebra structure than the algebras

In this way, our results can be seen as a non-commutative geometric counterpart of the simplifications afforded by crystal basis theory in the algebraic context.

for each
q\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
.

There is a unique
\mathbb{C}(q)
-valued inner product on
V(\lambda)
such that the
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-action is a
*
-representation and the highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}
has norm
1
, see
[
Jan96
, Lemma 9.20 c)]
. For convenience, we will refer to this as the
standard inner product
on
V(\lambda)
.

2.3.
Crystal bases

In this section we briefly recall various facts associated with Kashiwara’s theory of crystal bases
[
Kas90
,
Kas91
]
.
Our main references are the textbooks
[
HK02
]
and
[
Jan96
]
.

We need the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{E}_{i}
and
\tilde{F}_{i}
, which are algebraic renormalizations of
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
with well-defined limits at
q=0
.
They play a similar role to the operator phases of
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
in the
C^{*}
-algebraic world.
To define them, we first introduce the divided powers

\displaystyle E_{i}^{(k)}

\displaystyle:=E_{i}^{k}/[k]_{q_{i}}!,

\displaystyle F_{i}^{(k)}

\displaystyle:=F_{i}^{k}/[k]_{q_{i}}!,

where
[k]_{q}:=\frac{q^{k}-q^{-k}}{q-q^{-1}}
and
[k]_{q}!:=[k]_{q}[k-1]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}
.
The Kashiwara operators are defined by imposing that, for any weight vector
u_{0}
in an integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
V
with
E_{i}u_{0}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}:F_{i}^{(k)}u_{0}

\displaystyle\mapsto F_{i}^{(k-1)}u_{0},

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{i}:F_{i}^{(k)}u_{0}

\displaystyle\mapsto F_{i}^{(k+1)}u_{0},

with the convention
F_{i}^{(-1)}u_{0}=0
.

We can now define crystal bases.

Definition 2.2
.

A
crystal lattice
in
V
is an
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-submodule
\mathcal{L}
of
V
such that:

(1)

\mathcal{L}
is finitely generated over
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
and generates
V
as a vector space over
\mathbb{C}(q)
,

(2)

\mathcal{L}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbf{P}}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=\mathcal{L}\cap V_{\mu}
,

(3)

\tilde{E}_{i}\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{L}
and
\tilde{F}_{i}\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{L}
for all
i
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
crystal basis
of
V
is a pair
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{B})
, where
\mathcal{L}
is a crystal lattice and
\mathcal{B}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of the quotient
\mathcal{L}/q\mathcal{L}
such that:

(1)

\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{\mu}\mathcal{B}_{\mu}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{\mu}=\mathcal{B}\cap(\mathcal{L}_{\mu}/q\mathcal{L}_{\mu})
,

(2)

\tilde{E}_{i}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}
and
\tilde{F}_{i}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}
for all
i
,

(3)

for any
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
and
i
we have
b=\tilde{E}_{i}b^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow b^{\prime}=\tilde{F}_{i}b
.

In this way, we replace the action of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
on a finite-dimensional module
V
by a purely combinatorial action of the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{E}_{i},\tilde{F}_{i}
on the finite set
\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}
.
Kashiwara proved that every finite-dimensional integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module admits a crystal basis, which is unique up to isomorphism. We denote the crystal basis of the simple module
V(\lambda)
by
(\mathcal{L}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda))
.

The crystal basis is orthonormal in the following sense, compare
[
HK02
, Lemma 5.1.6]
,
[
Jan96
, Theorem 9.25]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
. Let
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{d}\}\subset\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
be a family of lifts for the crystal basis
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{d}\}
of
V(\lambda)
. Let
\langle\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}},\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\rangle
be the standard inner product on
V(\lambda)
. Then
\langle v_{a},v_{b}\rangle\in\delta_{ab}+q{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
.

As a consequence, for any
u,v\in\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
we have
\langle u,v\rangle\in{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
.

From an analytic point of view,
Lemma

2.4
means that for any
u,v\in\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
, the limit of the specialized inner products

\lim_{q\to 0}\ev_{q}\langle u,v\rangle

exists. Note that
\ev_{q}\langle u,v\rangle
does not necessarily exist for all
q>0
, but since
\langle u,v\rangle\in{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
, it at least exists in some neighbourhood of
0
.
We will use similar observations frequently in this work, and as above will use the notation
\lim_{q\to 0}
when strictly speaking we mean
\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}
.

2.4.
Tensor products

A major feature of crystal bases is the simplicity of their tensor products, and this will play a major role in what follows.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be finite-dimensional integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules with crystal bases
(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{2})
respectively.
Then
(\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes_{{\mathbf{A}_{0}}}\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{1}\times\mathcal{B}_{2})
is a crystal basis of
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
, see
[
HK02
, Theorem 4.4.1]
.
We will be lazy and write
\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}
to mean the crystal lattice
\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{2}
.

To describe the action of the Kashiwara operators we first define

\varepsilon_{i}(b):=\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\tilde{E}_{i}^{k}b\neq 0\right\},\quad\varphi_{i}(b):=\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\tilde{F}_{i}^{k}b\neq 0\right\}.

Then the action on the tensor product is given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}(b\otimes b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tilde{E}_{i}b\otimes b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)\geq\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\\
b\otimes\tilde{E}_{i}b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)<\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\end{cases},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{i}(b\otimes b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tilde{F}_{i}b\otimes b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)>\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\\
b\otimes\tilde{F}_{i}b^{\prime}&\varphi_{i}(b)\leq\varepsilon_{i}(b^{\prime})\end{cases}.

Here, as is customary, we denote the element
(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{1}\times\mathcal{B}_{2}
by
b_{1}\otimes b_{2}
, and we use the conventions
b_{1}\otimes 0=0\otimes b_{2}=0
.

3.
The quantized coordinate ring

3.1.
Definitions

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module over
\mathbb{C}(q)
and let
V^{*}=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}(q)}(V,\mathbb{C}(q))
be the dual module.
A
matrix coefficient
for the module
V
is a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
c^{V}_{f,v}
on
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
of the form

(3.1)

c^{V}_{f,v}(X):=f(Xv),\quad v\in V,\ f\in V^{*}.

The
quantized coordinate ring

\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
is the subspace of
\Hom_{\mathbb{C}(q)}(\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbb{C}(q))
spanned by the matrix coefficients of finite-dimensional integrable modules.
This is a Hopf algebra with operations obtained from those of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
by duality. Here, we use the algebraists’ convention of a non-skew pairing:

\displaystyle(\Delta(X),a\otimes b)=(X,ab),

\displaystyle(X\otimes Y,\Delta(a))=(XY,a).

Explicitly, this corresponds to the matrix coproduct on matrix coefficients:

\Delta(c^{V}_{f,v})=\sum_{i}c^{V}_{f,v_{i}}\otimes c^{V}_{f^{i},v},

where
\{v_{i}\}
and
\{f_{i}\}
are any basis and dual basis for
V
and
V^{*}
, respectively.
We denote the left and right regular representations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
on
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle X\triangleright c^{V}_{f,v}

\displaystyle=c^{V}_{f,Xv},

\displaystyle c^{V}_{f,v}\triangleleft X

\displaystyle=c^{V}_{f\circ X,v}

We also have a version of the quantized coordinate ring defined over
\mathbb{C}
for a fixed complex parameter
q\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
. This algebra will be denoted
\mathcal{O}[G_{q}]
, with apologies for the subtle difference in notation. For every
q\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
we have a partially defined specialization map

\ev_{q}:\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]\to\mathcal{O}[G_{q}],

induced by evaluation at
q
.

In
[
Kas93
]
Kashiwara introduced a crystal lattice for
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
consisting of finite sums of matrix coefficients
c^{V}_{f,v}
where
v
belongs to a crystal lattice
\mathcal{L}
for
V
and
f
belongs to
\mathcal{L}^{*}=\Hom_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}(\mathcal{L},{\mathbf{A}_{0}})\subset V^{*}
.
We denote this
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-form by
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]
.
This algebra was also studied by Iglesias in
[
Igl06
]
, where he uses the notation
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

3.2.
Star structure and compact integral form

The
*
-structure (
2.3
) on
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
induces a corresponding
*
-structure on
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
and on each
\mathcal{O}[G_{q}]
by the formula
(X,a^{*})=\overline{(S(X)^{*},a)}
.
When equipped with this
*
-structure, we denote the
*
-algebras
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
and
\mathcal{O}[G_{q}]
by
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]
and
\mathcal{O}[K_{q}]
, respectively. Again, we caution that
q^{*}=q
, and so the specialization map
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]\to\mathcal{O}[K_{q}]
is a
*
-morphism only when
q
is real.

As mentioned in the introduction, the
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-form
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]
is not stable under the
*
. This can be seen in the following example.

Example 3.1
.

Let
V
be the fundamental representation of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
over
\mathbb{C}(q)
. Let
v_{1}
be the highest weight vector for
V
and
v_{2}=Fv_{1}
. Let
\{f^{1},f^{2}\}\in V^{*}
be the dual basis for
V^{*}
. The subspace of
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]
corresponding to the fundamental representation is spanned by the matrix coefficients
u^{V}_{ij}=c^{V}_{f^{i},v_{j}}
with
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.

In this particular case,
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
V
, so upon specialization at
q\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
, the matrix coefficients
u^{V}_{ij}
coincide with the traditional generators for the operator algebraists’
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}[K_{q}]
, which in Woronowicz’s notation
[
Wor87b
]
are denoted

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=u^{V}_{11},

\displaystyle-q\gamma^{*}

\displaystyle=u^{V}_{12},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=u^{V}_{21},

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}

\displaystyle=u^{V}_{22}.

We see that
u^{V*}_{21}=-q^{-1}u^{V}_{12}
, which does not belong to
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]
.

Because of this,
we cannot simply equip the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]
with the
*
operation. Instead, we proceed as follows.

For each simple module
V(\lambda)
fix a lift of the crystal basis to a basis of weight vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i}\subset\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
, with dual basis
\{f^{i}\}_{i}\subset V(\lambda)^{*}
.
We impose that
v_{1}=v_{\lambda}
is the highest weight vector of
V(\lambda)
, and so
f^{1}=f^{-\lambda}
is the lowest weight element.

With this notation, we define the matrix coefficients

(3.3)

\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}:=c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{\lambda}},\quad\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}:=S(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{-\lambda},v_{i}}).

We also note that
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}=c^{V(\lambda)^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{i},f^{-\lambda}}
, where
\{\tilde{v_{i}}\}_{i}\subset V(\lambda)^{**}
is the dual basis to
\{f^{i}\}_{i}
.

Following Joseph
[
Jos95
, §9.1.6]
, let
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G/N^{+}]
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G/N^{-}]
be the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
generated by the matrix coefficients
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}
and
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}
, respectively (for all
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
and
i=1,\cdots,\dim V(\lambda)
). Joseph uses the notation
R_{q}[G/N^{+}]
and
R_{q}[G/N^{-}]
.
The multiplication map
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G/N^{+}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}(q)}\mathcal{O}_{q}[G/N^{-}]\to\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
is surjective, see
[
Jos95
, Proposition 9.2.2]
.

We now define
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-forms of these subalgebras.

Definition 3.2
.

We define:

•

\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{+}]
as the
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-subalgebra generated by the elements
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}
,

•

\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{-}]
as the
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-subalgebra generated by the elements
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}
,

•

\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[K]
as the
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-subalgebra generated by the elements
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}
and
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}
.

Thus, while
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
denote the same
\mathbb{C}(q)
-algebra with or without the
*
-structure, we stress that
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]
are not the same
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-subalgebra. They represent different
q=0
limits of the families of algebras
\mathcal{O}[K_{q}]=\mathcal{O}[G_{q}]
.
The notation
\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[K]
suggests that this algebra is closed under the
*
-structure, which is not immediately obvious from its definition.
However this is true, as shown in the next result.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have
(\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{+}])^{*}=\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{-}]
. Moreover

\begin{split}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i})^{*}&\equiv\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mod q\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{-}],\\
(\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i})^{*}&\equiv\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mod q\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{+}].\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\{v_{i}\}_{i}
be the lift of the crystal basis of
V(\lambda)
as above.
Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be the unique inner product on
V(\lambda)
invariant under
*
such that
(v_{1},v_{1})=1
.
Let
G^{i}_{j}=(v_{i},v_{j})
be the Gram matrix for the basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i}
.
Some linear algebra shows that we have the identity

(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{j}})^{*}=\sum_{k,l}(G^{-1})^{l}_{i}G^{j}_{k}S(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{k},v_{l}}).

Now consider the case of
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}=c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{1}}
.
We have
G^{1}_{k}=(v_{1},v_{k})=\delta_{1k}
, since different weight spaces are orthogonal under the inner product. Then we get

(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i})^{*}=\sum_{l}(G^{-1})^{l}_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{l}.

Recall from
Lemma

2.4
that the basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i}
becomes orthonormal at
q=0
, which implies that
G^{i}_{j}
and
(G^{-1})^{i}_{j}
are equal to
\delta_{ij}
modulo terms in
q{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
.
Thus we obtain the desired result for
(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i})^{*}
. The result for
(\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i})^{*}
is proven similarly, and it follows that
(\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{+}])^{*}=\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}_{q}[G/N^{-}]
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

It is not clear from the definition above whether
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[G]\subset\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]
, although we expect this to be the case. Answering this question would require a more careful study of the crystal lattice structure. Since we don’t need this property here, we won’t discuss it further.

3.3.
The gauge action

For the moment, we continue to suppose that
K
is simply connected, with rank
r
. That is, we have
T\cong\mathbb{T}^{r}
for the maximal torus and
\mathbf{P}\cong\hat{T}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for the lattice of integral weights.
The quantized coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]
is
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
-graded as follows.

Definition 3.5
.

For any
\mu\in\mathbf{P}
, we write
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]_{\mu}
for the span of all matrix coefficients
c^{V}_{f,v}
where
v\in V
is a vector of weight
\mu
, so that
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbf{P}}\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]_{\mu}
is a
\mathbf{P}
-graded algebra.

The
\mathbf{P}
-grading corresponds to the gauge action of the torus subgroup
T
,

(3.4)

z\cdot c^{V}_{f,v}=z^{\mu}c^{V}_{f,v}\quad\text{for }c^{V}_{f,v}\in\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]_{\mu},

where
z^{\mu}=e^{(\mu,\log(z))}
denotes the evaluation at
z\in T
of the unitary character associated to
\mu\in\mathbf{P}
. Geometrically, this corresponds to the realization of
K
as a principal
T
-bundle over the full flag variety
X=K/T
, and this language is extended by analogy to the quantum case. The gauge-invariant subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]
is then the quantized coordinate ring of the flag variety.

3.4.
Flag manifolds and non-simply connected groups

We finish this preliminary section with the structure theory of the quantized coordinate rings of general flag varieties. This material could be skipped on a first reading. We follow the conventions of Stokman
[
Sto03
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}_{+}
denote the Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\mathfrak{h}
and the elements
E_{1},\cdots,E_{r}
. The standard parabolic subalgebras are indexed by subsets of simple roots
S\subseteq{\bm{\Delta}}
. Specifically, we denote by
\mathfrak{p}_{S}\supseteq\mathfrak{b}_{+}
the standard parabolic which contains
F_{i}
if and only if
\alpha_{i}\in S
. The Levi factor
\mathfrak{l}_{S}
of
\mathfrak{p}_{S}
is generated by
\mathfrak{h}
and those
E_{i},F_{i}
with
\alpha_{i}\in S
.
The associated Lie subgroups of
G
will always be denoted by the corresponding uppercase letters. We write
X_{S}=G/P_{S}
for the corresponding flag variety.

Passing to the compact form, we have
X_{S}=K/K_{S}
where
\mathfrak{k}_{S}=\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{S}
. The Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}_{S}
decomposes as

\mathfrak{k}_{S}=\mathfrak{k}^{0}_{S}\oplus\mathfrak{z}_{S},

where
\mathfrak{z}_{S}
is the centre of
\mathfrak{k}_{S}
and
\mathfrak{k}^{0}_{S}
is semisimple. Then
Y_{S}=K/K^{0}_{S}
is a principal torus bundle over the flag variety
X_{S}
with fibres
Z_{S}\cong\mathbb{T}^{N}
, where
N=r-|S|
.

For instance, when
S=\emptyset
we have
K_{\emptyset}=T
and
K^{0}_{\emptyset}=\{1\}
, so the torus bundle
Y_{\emptyset}\to X_{\emptyset}
is the principal bundle
K\to K/T
with fibres
Z_{\emptyset}=T\cong\mathbb{T}^{r}
. More generally, the torus group
Z_{S}
identifies canonically with the dual torus of the abelian group
\mathbf{P}_{S^{c}}\subseteq\mathbf{P}
generated by the fundamental weights
\varpi_{i}
with
i\notin S
.

All of the above can be quantized.
We shall not spell out all the details here, but refer directly to
[
Sto03
]
. Following our above notation, we write
\mathcal{O}[X_{S,q}]
and
\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,q}]
for what Stokman
[
Sto03
]
calls
\mathbb{C}_{q}[U/K_{S}]
and
\mathbb{C}_{q}[U/K^{0}_{S}]
in Definitions 2.3(a) and Section 4, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,q}]

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathcal{O}[K_{q}]\mid T\triangleright a=\epsilon(T)a\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{k}^{0}_{S})\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}[X_{S,q}]

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathcal{O}[K_{q}]\mid T\triangleright a=\epsilon(T)a\text{ for all }T\in\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{k}_{S})\}.

The following is due to Stokman
[
Sto03
]
.
We retain the notation
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}=c^{V(\lambda)}_{f,v_{1}^{\lambda}}
,
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}=c^{V(\lambda)^{*}}_{\tilde{v},f^{1}_{\lambda}}
for the generators of
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G/N^{+}]
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G/N^{-}]
from Equation (
3.3
).
We denote the specialized versions of these elements by the same symbols, for simplicity.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{S}^{+}
denote the set of dominant weights which are non-negative integral linear combinations of the fundamental weights
\varpi_{k}
with
\alpha_{k}\in{\bm{\Delta}}\setminus S
.
The quantized coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,q}]
of the torus bundle
Y_{S}\to X_{S}
is generated as an algebra by the matrix coefficients

\left\{\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i},\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mid\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{S}\right\}.

This algebra is stable under the gauge action of
T
defined in
Section

3.3
, and the gauge-invariant subalgebra is
\mathcal{O}[X_{S,q}]
.

Proof.

In fact, Stokman
[
Sto03
, Theorem 4.1]
proves that
\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,q}]
is generated by
c^{V(\lambda)}_{f,v_{1}^{\lambda}}
and
c^{V(\lambda)^{*}}_{\tilde{v},f^{1}_{\lambda}}
with
\lambda=\varpi_{k}
for
\alpha_{k}\notin S
, so our first claim is in fact weaker. The second claim follows immediately upon observing that
\mathcal{O}[X_{S,q}]
consists of those elements of
\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,q}]
which are invariant under the action of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{h})
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The algebra
\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,q}]
is already invariant under the action of
K_{i}
for
\alpha_{i}\in S
, so the gauge action in
Theorem

3.6
is determined entirely by the action of the generators
K_{i}
with
\alpha_{i}\notin S
. Thus the relevant gauge action on

The Cartan braiding and the crystal limit

5.1.
Braiding and commutation relations

At this point, we will be obliged to enlarge our base field slightly in order to work with expressions of the form
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}
where
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}
are any integral weights. Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest positive integer such that
(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P})\subseteq\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{Z}
. Then we work over
\mathbb{C}(s)
and put
q=s^{L}
. Likewise we redefine the rings
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
,
\mathbf{A}_{\infty}
and
\mathbf{A}
with the parameter
s
in place of
q
. This has essentially no effect on the crystal theory. Rather than rewrite expressions in terms of
s
, we will write
q^{k}
where
k\in\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{Z}
.

We denote by
\hat{R}_{V,W}:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
the braiding corresponding to the finite-dimensional integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules
V
and
W
.
It is a
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module isomorphism which generalizes the classical flip map.
The braiding is not quite unique; the choice we make can be characterized uniquely by

(5.1)

\begin{split}\hat{R}_{V,W}(v\otimes w)&=q^{-(\wt(v),\wt(w))}w\otimes v+\sum_{i}w_{i}\otimes v_{i},\\
&\text{
where $\wt(w_{i})<\wt(w)$ and $\wt(v_{i})>\wt(v)$ for every $i$.
}\end{split}

Here,
\wt
denotes the weight of a weight vector.
In particular, in the case
V=V(\lambda)
and with
v_{\lambda}
a highest weight vector, we get
\hat{R}_{V,V}(v_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\lambda})=q^{-(\lambda,\lambda)}v_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\lambda}
.

The braiding can be used to give commutation relations for the elements of
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
.
Let
V
and
W
be
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules with bases
\{v_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{w_{i}\}_{i}
and denote by
\{f^{i}\}_{i}
and
\{g^{i}\}_{i}
the dual bases. Let us introduce the coefficients of
\hat{R}_{V,W}
by

\hat{R}_{V,W}(v_{i}\otimes w_{j})=\sum_{k,l}(\hat{R}_{V,W})_{ij}^{kl}w_{k}\otimes v_{l}.

Then, using the fact that the braiding satisfies
\hat{R}_{V,W}\Delta(X)=\Delta(X)\hat{R}_{V,W}
for any
X\in\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, it is easy to derive the relations

(5.2)

\begin{split}c^{V}_{f^{i},v_{k}}c^{W}_{g^{j},w_{l}}&=\sum_{a,b,c,d}(\hat{R}_{V,W}^{-1})^{ij}_{ab}(\hat{R}_{V,W})^{cd}_{kl}c^{W}_{g^{a},w_{c}}c^{V}_{f^{b},v_{d}}\\
&=\sum_{a,b,c,d}(\hat{R}_{W,V})^{ij}_{ab}(\hat{R}_{W,V}^{-1})^{cd}_{kl}c^{W}_{g^{a},w_{c}}c^{V}_{f^{b},v_{d}}.\end{split}

5.2.
The Cartan braiding

Before discussing the crystal limit of the braiding operators, let us make some observations about tensor products of irreducible crystals.

Definition 5.1
.

A finite-dimensional integrable
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
V
will be called a
product of irreducibles
if it is of the form
V(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes V(\lambda_{n})
for some
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
. Such a module contains a unique irreducible submodule of highest weight
\lambda=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}
, which we call the
Cartan component
. We refer to
\lambda
as the
highest weight
of
V
(although strictly speaking it is the largest among the highest weights of all irreducible submodules of
V
).

The same terminology will be used to refer to the associated
Cartan component
of a product of irreducible crystals.
If
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
is a product of irreducible crystals, we denote by
\eta:\mathcal{B}\sqcup\{0\}\to\{0,1\}
the indicator function of its Cartan component:

\eta(b)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $b$ is in the Cartan component},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The following well-known property of the Cartan component will occasionally be useful (we include the easy proof for completeness).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
b_{\lambda}
and
b_{w_{0}\lambda}
denote the highest and lowest weight elements, respectively, of the irreducible crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Then the tensor
c\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}}
belongs to the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
for every
c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Similarly,
b_{w_{0}\lambda}\otimes c^{\prime}
belongs to the Cartan component for every
c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
.

Proof.

The first statement follows by repeatedly applying the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{F}_{i}
to the highest weight element
b_{\lambda}\otimes b_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and using the tensor product formula (
2.5
). Similarly, the second follows by applying the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{E}_{i}
to the lowest weight element
b_{w_{0}\lambda}\otimes b_{w_{0}\lambda^{\prime}}
.
∎

We now return to the braiding.
Consider two simple modules
V(\lambda)
and
V(\lambda^{\prime})
.
We have crystal lattices of
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{\prime})
and
V(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes V(\lambda)
given by

\mathcal{L}_{V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{\prime})}:=\mathcal{L}(\lambda)\otimes_{{\mathbf{A}_{0}}}\mathcal{L}(\lambda^{\prime}),\quad\mathcal{L}_{V(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes V(\lambda)}:=\mathcal{L}(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes_{{\mathbf{A}_{0}}}\mathcal{L}(\lambda).

The braiding
\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}
does not typically map
\mathcal{L}_{V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{\prime})}
into
\mathcal{L}_{V(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes V(\lambda)}
because of the negative powers of
q
in (
5.1
).
This deficiency can be remedied by multiplying by an appropriate power of
q
, as we show in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.3
.

Given any simple modules
V(\lambda)
and
V(\lambda^{\prime})
we have

q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}(\mathcal{L}_{V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{\prime})})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{V(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes V(\lambda)}.

Moreover, the map
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}
induces a morphism of crystals

\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda).

It is an isomorphism between the Cartan components of the two crystals, and is zero on all other components.

Proof.

It is a consequence of the tensor product rule (
2.4
) for
\tilde{E}_{i}
that every highest weight element of the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
is of the form
b_{\lambda}\otimes b^{\prime}
, where
b_{\lambda}
is the highest weight element of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, and
b^{\prime}
is some element of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Let
v_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
and
w\in\mathcal{L}(\lambda^{\prime})
be lifts of
b_{\lambda}
and
b^{\prime}
, respectively, to weight vectors. Write
\mu
for the weight of
w
.

Let us write
V=V(\lambda)
and
W=V(\lambda^{\prime})
for convenience.
By the nature of the braiding described in (
5.1
), we have

(5.3)

q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,W}(v_{\lambda}\otimes w)=q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})-(\lambda,\mu)}w\otimes v_{\lambda}.

Since
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
and
\lambda^{\prime}-\mu\in\mathbf{Q}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\cdot{\bm{\Delta}}
, we have
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}-\mu)\geq 0
. Therefore, (
5.3
) is in
\mathcal{L}_{W\otimes V}
, and moreover is in
q\mathcal{L}_{W\otimes V}
unless
w
is the highest weight vector of
W
.

The images of the various highest weight elements
v_{\lambda}\otimes w
above, under repeated action of the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{F}_{i}
, generate the crystal lattice
\mathcal{L}_{V,W}
as an
{\mathbf{A}_{0}}
-module. Since
\hat{R}_{V,W}
is a morphism of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, we get

q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,W}(\tilde{F}(v_{\lambda}\otimes w))=q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})-(\lambda,\mu)}\tilde{F}(w\otimes v_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{L}_{W\otimes V}

for any word
\tilde{F}=\tilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tilde{F}_{i_{n}}
in the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{F}_{i}
.
This proves that the map
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,W}
respects the crystal lattices and induces a map of crystals.

For the final claim, note that crystal element
b^{\prime}\otimes b_{\lambda}
associated to the right hand side of (
5.3
) always belongs to the Cartan component by
Lemma

5.2
. Since the left-hand side is a highest-weight element by assumption, it belongs to the Cartan component only if
w
is of weight
\lambda^{\prime}
. It follows that the crystal morphism
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}
must vanish on all non-Cartan components. On the Cartan component, (
5.3
) gives
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,W}(v_{\lambda}\otimes v_{\lambda^{\prime}})=v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes v_{\lambda}
, from which we conclude that
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}
is an isomorphism here.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be products of irreducible modules with highest weights
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
, respectively, and let
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{B})
and
(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
be their crystal bases. The
Cartan braiding
is the morphism of crystals

\sigma=\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{B}

induced by the morphism of crystal lattices
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,V^{\prime}}:\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\mathcal{L}
.

The use of the word braiding here is a bit misleading. Firstly, braidings are supposed to be isomorphisms, while our Cartan braiding is not, see
Theorem

5.3
. More importantly, the Cartan braiding
\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}
is only defined when
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
products of irreducible modules, not on general direct sums of irreducibles. This is because we need a well-defined notion of highest weights
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
for
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
in order to define it, see below.

If
V
and
V^{\prime}
are irreducible modules, then the Cartan braiding is the crystal morphism described in
Theorem

5.3
, that is, it is an isomorphism between the Cartan components of
\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{B}
, and zero on all other irreducible components.

If
V
and
V^{\prime}
are merely products of irreducibles, then we need a few remarks to justify that the Cartan braiding is well-defined. If
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
are the highest weights of any components of
V
and
V^{\prime}
respectively, then we have
\lambda-\mu
and
\lambda-\mu^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{N}\cdot{\bm{\Delta}}
. Therefore

(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=(\lambda-\mu,\lambda^{\prime})+(\mu,\lambda^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime})+(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\geq(\mu,\mu^{\prime}),

so
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V(\mu),V(\mu^{\prime})}=q^{k}q^{(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V(\mu),V(\mu^{\prime})}
for some
k\geq 0
.
Now
Theorem

5.3
shows that
q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,V^{\prime}}:\mathcal{L}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\mathcal{L}
is well defined and descends to a morphism of crystals.
Note though that in this case, it is possible for
\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}
to be non-zero on components other than the Cartan component, as the following example shows.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{4}
, and consider
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{2})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{3})
, where
\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2},\varpi_{3}
are the fundamental weights. Then
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{2}+\varpi_{3})\oplus\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})
. One can calculate

(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2}+\varpi_{3})={\frac{3}{4}}=(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{1}).

It follows that
\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}
is non-zero on two distinct components in the triple product
\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{2})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{3})
.

On the other hand, if we consider the map
\id_{\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{2}),\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{3})}
, this will kill
\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})\subset\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{1})\otimes(\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{2})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\varpi_{3}))
. In fact, we will show that the Cartan component of any tensor product of irreducibles is characterized by the non-vanishing of every possible action of the Cartan braidings. See
Theorem

6.3
below for a precise statement.

The Cartan braiding is very easy to describe in one particular situation.

Example 5.6

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
and let
\phi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
be the unique non-trivial crystal morphism. If
b^{\lambda}_{i}\otimes b^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}
is in the Cartan component, with
\phi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(b^{\lambda}_{i}\otimes b^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j})=b^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}_{m}
, then we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathsf{f}^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}_{m}\mod{q\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathsf{v}^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}_{m}\mod{q\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]}.

Otherwise
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}\equiv 0
and
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{q\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]}

Proof.

We have
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}=c^{V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{\prime})}_{f^{i}_{\lambda}\otimes f^{j}_{\lambda^{\prime}},v^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes v^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{1}}
.
Let us write
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})=\bigsqcup_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}
for the decomposition of the tensor product crystal into irreducible components, with
\mathcal{B}_{1}
being the Cartan component. Let

and
\mathcal{L}_{n}
be the corresponding irreducible components of

and
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)\otimes_{\mathbf{A}_{0}}\mathcal{L}(\lambda^{\prime})

The vector

is the highest weight vector for the Cartan component
\mathcal{B}_{1}
.
If

Proposition 5.9
.

We have the commutation relations

\begin{split}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}&=\sum_{k,l}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\left(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime}),V(\lambda)}\right)^{ij}_{kl}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{l},\\
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}&=\sum_{k,l}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\left(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime}),V(\lambda)}\right)^{lk}_{ji}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{l},\\
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}&=\sum_{k,l}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\left(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}\right)^{ki}_{lj}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{l}.\end{split}

Moreover we have
\sum_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}=1
.

Proof.

These can be derived from the commutation relations (
5.2
) which hold in
\mathcal{O}_{q}[G]
.
Since
v_{1}
is the highest weight vector we have
(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime}),V(\lambda)}^{-1})^{cd}_{11}=q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\delta^{c}_{1}\delta^{d}_{1}
, which gives the relation for
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}
in the form

c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{1}}c^{V(\lambda^{\prime})}_{f^{j},v_{1}}=\sum_{a,b}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime}),V(\lambda)})^{ij}_{ab}c^{V(\lambda^{\prime})}_{f^{a},v_{1}}c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{b},v_{1}}.

Similarly, since
f^{1}
is the lowest weight vector we have
(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*},V(\lambda)^{*}}^{-1})^{cd}_{11}=q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\delta^{c}_{1}\delta^{d}_{1}
, which gives the relation for
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}
in the form

c^{V(\lambda)^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{i},f^{1}}c^{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{j},f^{1}}=\sum_{a,b}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*},V(\lambda)^{*}})^{ij}_{ab}c^{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{a},f^{1}}c^{V(\lambda)^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{b},f^{1}}.

It can be rewritten in terms of
\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime}),V(\lambda)}
by using the identities

(5.8)

(\hat{R}_{V^{*},W})_{ij}^{kl}=(\hat{R}_{V,W}^{-1})_{jl}^{ik},\quad(\hat{R}_{V,W^{*}}^{-1})_{ij}^{kl}=(\hat{R}_{V,W})_{jl}^{ik}.

These are valid for any
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules
V,W
(with dual bases for
V^{*},W^{*}
) and follow from the fact that the finite-dimensional
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules form a braided monoidal category (see
[
Mat19

for a proof). They imply that

(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*},V(\lambda)^{*}})^{ij}_{ab}=(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda^{\prime}),V(\lambda)})^{ba}_{ji},

which gives the relation for
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}
as stated.

Proceeding as above, we have
(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})})^{cd}_{11}=q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\delta^{c}_{1}\delta^{d}_{1}
and this gives the relation for
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j}
in the form

c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{1}}c^{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{j},f^{1}}=\sum_{a,b}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*}}^{-1})^{ij}_{ab}c^{V(\lambda^{\prime})^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{a},f^{1}}c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{b},v_{1}}.

It can be rewritten using the identity

from (
5.8
), which gives the relation as stated.

Finally we want to show that
\sum_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}=1
.
It is not difficult show that
\sum_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}
is invariant under the right action of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, which means that
\sum_{i}\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}=c\cdot 1
for some

Applying the counit to the left-hand side we have

\sum_{i}\varepsilon(\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i})=\sum_{i}\varepsilon(c^{V(\lambda)^{*}}_{\tilde{v}_{i},f^{1}})\varepsilon(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{1}})=\sum_{i}\tilde{v}_{i}(f^{1})f^{i}(v_{1})=1.

Therefore

, which gives the claimed relation.

\lim_{q\to 0}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\left(\hat{R}_{V(\lambda),V(\lambda^{\prime})}\right)_{ij}^{kl}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}(b^{\lambda}_{i}\otimes b^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j})=b^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k}\otimes b^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{l},\\
0&\text{if }\eta(b^{\lambda}_{i}\otimes b^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j})=0.\end{cases}\qed

Ultimately, we will show that

5.11
gives a complete set of generators and relations for
\mathcal{O}[K_{0}]
.

Remark 5.12
.

Note that relations (

) and (

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i})\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{j})

\displaystyle=\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{k})\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{l}),

6.
Properties of the Cartan braiding

6.1.
Hexagon and braid relations

The Cartan braiding of
Definition

5.4
inherits properties from the braiding operators
\hat{R}_{V,V^{\prime}}
as follows.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
V
,
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
be products of irreducibles with crystal bases
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{B})
,
(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})
, respectively. Then the crystal braiding maps satisfy the hexagon relations

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=(\id_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}})(\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}})(\id_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}),

and the braid relation

\displaystyle(\sigma_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle(\id_{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}})(\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}})

(6.3)

\displaystyle=(\id_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}})(\sigma_{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}})(\id_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}).

Proof.

Let
\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
be the highest weights of
V
,
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
, with crystals
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. Then the highest weight of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
is
\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. Using the hexagon relation for
\hat{R}
, we get

\displaystyle q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime})}\hat{R}_{V,V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime\prime})}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}(\id_{V^{\prime}}\otimes\hat{R}_{V,V^{\prime\prime}})(\hat{R}_{V,V^{\prime}}\otimes\id_{V^{\prime\prime}}).

This specializes at
q=0
by
Theorem

5.3
, and the specialization induces the hexagon relation (
6.1
). The other hexagon relation is proven similarly, and the braid relation follows by an analogous calculation.
∎

6.2.
Partial action of the symmetric group

Let
\mathcal{B}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{n}
be products of irreducible crystals, see
Definition

5.1
. For long tensor products of the form
\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}
, we will denote the action of the crystal braiding on two successive terms by
\sigma_{i}=\id_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\id_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}
, so that

\sigma_{i}:\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\cdots\mathcal{B}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\cdots\mathcal{B}_{i+1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{n}
be irreducible crystals. Let
s\in S_{n}
be a permutation and let
s=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
be a realization of
s
as a reduced word in the transpositions
s_{i}=(i\;\;i\!+\!1)
. Then the crystal morphism

\sigma_{s}:=\sigma_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{i_{n}}:\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\cdots\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{B}_{s(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{s(n)}

is independent of the choice of reduced word representing
s
.

Proof.

Thanks to
Theorem

5.3
, this follows from the well-known fact that any two reduced words can be linked by repeated application of the braid relations
s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}=s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}
.
∎

Note that the relation
s_{i}^{2}=\id
is not necessary in the above proof. This is important since
\sigma_{i}^{2}\neq\id
, although
\sigma_{i}^{2}
is the identity on the Cartan component. As a consequence, the map
s\mapsto\sigma_{s}
doesn’t define an action of
S_{n}
, but it does define an action between the Cartan components, thanks to the following theorem.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{n}
be irreducible crystals and let
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
for each
i
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}
,

(ii)

\sigma_{s}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0
for every
s\in S_{n}
,

(iii)

\sigma_{s_{0}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0
, where
s_{0}=\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&n\\
n&n-1&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}
is the longest permutation.

If
\mathcal{B}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{B}_{n}
are products of irreducibles, the equivalence holds if we add to (ii) and (iii) the condition that each
b_{i}
be in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}_{i}
.

Proof.

To begin with, assume that all the
\mathcal{B}_{i}
are irreducible.

For (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii), note that if
b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}
, then necessarily
b_{i}\otimes b_{i+1}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{i+1}
for every
i
. Therefore
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{i},\mathcal{B}_{i+1}}(b_{i}\otimes b_{i+1})
is non-zero and, since it corresponds to the same element of the Cartan component as
b_{i}\otimes b_{i+1}
, we have that
\sigma_{i}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})
again belongs to the Cartan component.
Inductively, any repeated composition of the
\sigma_{i}
is non-zero.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is obvious. To prove (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), we work inductively on
n
. When
n=1
or
2
, the claim is immediate. Suppose it is true for
n-1
. If
\sigma_{s_{0}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0
, write

(6.4)

s_{0}=s_{0}^{\prime}(s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\cdots s_{1})=(s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\cdots s_{1})s_{0}^{\prime\prime},

where

s_{0}^{\prime}=\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&n-1&n\\
n-1&n-2&\cdots&1&n\end{pmatrix},\hskip 8.19447pts_{0}^{\prime\prime}=\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&n-1&n\\
1&n&\cdots&3&2\end{pmatrix}

are the permutations reversing the first
n-1
and last
n-1
entries, respectively. Upon writing
s_{0}^{\prime}
and
s_{0}^{\prime\prime}
as reduced words, both expressions in (
6.4
) give a reduced expression for
s_{0}
, so we have

\sigma_{s_{0}^{\prime}}(\sigma_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{1}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}))\\
=\sigma_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{s_{0}^{\prime\prime}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}))=\sigma_{s_{0}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0.

In particular,
\sigma_{s_{0}^{\prime\prime}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0
, so by the inductive hypothesis,
b_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n}
. But also
\sigma_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{1}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0
, and repeated application of the hexagon relation (
6.1
) shows that

\sigma_{n-1}\circ\sigma_{n-2}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{1},(\mathcal{B}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n})},

so
b_{1}\otimes(b_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})
is necessarily in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes(\mathcal{B}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n})
.

This completes the proof for
\mathcal{B}_{i}
irreducible. The general case where the
\mathcal{B}_{i}
are products of irreducibles follows readily from this by naturality of the braiding, which implies that we can restrict our attention to the Cartan components in each
\mathcal{B}_{i}
.
∎

6.3.
Left and right ends

We now make a definition that is going to play a major role in the rest of this work, namely the notion of right end of a crystal element.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
with
\lambda\geq\mu
.
Considering the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, the
left end
and
right end
with respect to
\mu
are the set-theoretic maps

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{\mu}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\mu),

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{\mu}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\mu),

defined as follows. There exist unique injective morphisms of crystals

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu),

and we define
\mathsf{L}_{\mu}(b)
and
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)
to be the unique elements of
\mathcal{B}(\mu)
such that

\displaystyle b\mapsto\mathsf{L}_{\mu}(b)\otimes b^{\prime},

\displaystyle b\mapsto b^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b),

for some
b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu)
.

More generally, let
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
be a product of irreducibles of highest weight
\lambda=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}
.
If
b\in\mathcal{B}
is in the Cartan component we define
\mathsf{L}_{\mu}(b)
and
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)
as above, under the identification of the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}
with
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.
Otherwise
\mathsf{L}_{\mu}(b)=\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)=0
.

We will also consider the right ends with respect to a
family
of dominant weights, and in particular for the family of fundamental weights
{\bm{\Pi}}=(\varpi_{1},\cdots,\varpi_{r})
.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
\mathsf{S}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of dominant weights.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a product of irreducible crystals of highest weight
\lambda
with
\lambda\geq\mu_{i}
for all
i
.
Then the
\mathsf{S}
-left end
and
\mathsf{S}
-right end
maps are the set-theoretic maps

\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{S}},\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}(\mu_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{B}(\mu_{n})\sqcup\{0\}

defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{S}}(b):=\left(\mathsf{L}_{\mu_{1}}(b),\cdots,\mathsf{L}_{\mu_{n}}(b)\right),

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(b):=\left(\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{1}}(b),\cdots,\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{n}}(b)\right).

Remark 6.6
.

According to the usual conventions on crystal bases, the notations
\mathcal{B}(\mu_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu_{n})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mu_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{B}(\mu_{n})
denote the same object.
We use the former when we take into account the additional crystal structure, but the latter when regarded simply as a set.
This is the notation we use for left and right ends, since
\mathsf{L}_{\mathsf{S}}
and
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}
are only set-theoretic maps.

The rest of this section is devoted to the properties of the right end maps. We focus on the right ends since these play the main role in our setup, but similar results can of course be obtained for the left end maps.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
be dominant weights. An element
b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
belongs to the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
if and only if we have

(6.5)

\sigma_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{k}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0

for every
k\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
.
In this case, for each
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, the right end
\mathsf{R}_{\lambda_{k}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})
is equal to the rightmost tensor factor of (
6.5
).

Proof.

For
n=1
the statement is trivial, so suppose it is true for tensor products of length
n-1
. The necessity of (
6.5
) is obvious from
Theorem

6.3
, so it remains to check sufficiency.

By the inductive hypothesis,
b_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{2})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
, so by
Theorem

6.3
we have
s_{0}^{\prime}(b_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0
where
s_{0}^{\prime}
denotes the longest word of
S_{n-1}
. But now the longest word
s_{0}
of
S_{n}
satisfies

s_{0}\circ(\id\otimes s_{0}^{\prime})(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})=\sigma_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{1}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})\neq 0

by (
6.5
).
Therefore, by
Theorem

6.3
,
(\id\otimes s_{0}^{\prime})(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
, and so
b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}
as well. This proves the first statement.

Now the map from (
6.5
) gives a morphism of crystals

\sigma_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{k}:\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{k})}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{k})

with the hat denoting omission, which gives the second statement.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

We briefly mention how this connects to the theory of set-theoretic solutions to the Yang-Baxter equations, as in
[

]
.
Writing the Cartan braiding as
\sigma(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\mathsf{f}_{b}(b^{\prime})\otimes\mathsf{g}_{b^{\prime}}(b)

can be recast in the form

\mathsf{R}_{\lambda_{k}}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n})=\mathsf{g}_{b_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathsf{g}_{b_{k+1}}(b_{k}),\quad k=1,\cdots,n.

This gives the components of the map
J_{n}
introduced in
[
ESS99

(if we work with left ends instead of right ends).

Next, we discuss how right ends behave with respect to tensor products.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu\in\mathbf{P}^{+}

and let
b\otimes b^{\prime}
be in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
.

1)

If
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\mu
then
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b^{\prime})
.

2)

If
\lambda\geq\mu
then
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)\otimes b^{\prime})
. Consequently, if
\lambda^{\prime\prime}\geq\mu
and
c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})
has the same
\mu
-right end as

, then
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(c\otimes b^{\prime})
.

Proof.

1) is almost immediate from the definitions. For 2), consider the inclusion of the Cartan component
\iota:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
. We have

\textstyle{b\otimes b^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota\otimes\id}
\textstyle{b^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)\otimes b^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{2}}
\textstyle{b^{\prime\prime}\otimes b^{\prime\prime\prime}\otimes\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b)\otimes b^{\prime}),}

for some
b^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu)
and
b^{\prime\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
. The result then follows from
Proposition

6.7
. The final statement is now immediate.
∎

The next result shows that we obtain the same set of non-zero
\mathsf{S}
-right ends from any appropriate crystal.

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
\mathsf{S}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
be a family of dominant weights. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a product of irreducible crystals. Then the set of (non-zero)
\mathsf{S}
-right ends of elements of
\mathcal{B}
, namely

(6.6)

\{\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(b)\mid b\in\mathcal{B}\}\setminus\{0\}\quad\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i}),

is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{B}
, as long as the highest weight
\lambda
of
\mathcal{B}
satisfies
\lambda\geq\mu_{i}
for every
i
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
is irredudible, with
\lambda\geq\mu_{i}
for all
i
, since if
\mathcal{B}
has highest weight
\lambda
then by definition the right ends of
\mathcal{B}
are the same as those of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.

Now suppose
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda
.
Proposition

(1) shows that the
\mu_{i}
-right ends of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
are the same as the

-right ends of

-right ends of

.

7.
The higher-rank graph algebra

7.1.
Higher-rank graphs

We begin by recalling the definition of a
k
-graph from
[
KP00
]
. For this, we need to regard the abelian monoid
\mathbb{N}^{k}
as a category with one object, in which composition is given by addition.

Definition 7.1
.

A
higher-rank graph of rank
k
(or
k
-graph) is a small category
\Lambda
equipped with a functor
\mathsf{d}:\Lambda\to\mathbb{N}^{k}
satisfying the
factorization property
: for every
e\in\Lambda
, and any pair of multi-indices
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
\mathsf{d}(e)=m+n
, there are unique elements
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Lambda
with
\mathsf{d}(e_{1})=m
,
\mathsf{d}(e_{2})=n
such that
e=e_{1}\cdot e_{2}
.

The elements of
\Lambda
are called
paths
, the indices
\{1,\cdots,k\}
are referred to as
colours
, and
\mathsf{d}(e)
is called the
degree
or
coloured length
of
e
. We write
\Lambda^{n}
for the set of paths of length
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. Paths of length
0
are called
vertices
, naturally enough. Paths of length
\delta_{i}=(0,\cdots,1,\cdots,0)
, with
1
in the
i
th slot, are called
edges of colour
i
. We use the notation
\Lambda^{\neq 0}
for the set of paths of non-zero length.

The factorization property implies that for every path
e\in\Lambda
, there are unique vertices
\mathsf{r}(e)
and
\mathsf{s}(e)
in
\Lambda^{0}
, with the property that

e=\mathsf{r}(e)\cdot e\cdot\mathsf{s}(e).

This defines the
range
and
source
maps
\mathsf{r},\mathsf{s}:\Lambda\to\Lambda^{0}
.
Given a vertex
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and a multi-index
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, we write

\displaystyle v\Lambda^{n}:=\{e\in\Lambda^{n}:\mathsf{r}(e)=v\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}v:=\{e\in\Lambda^{n}:\mathsf{s}(e)=v\}.

It is common to impose the following additional hypotheses on a higher-rank graph, which will apply in our case, see
Section

7.4
.

Definition 7.2
.

A
k
-graph
\Lambda
is
row-finite
if
v\Lambda^{n}
is finite for every
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. It has
no sources
if
v\Lambda^{n}\neq\emptyset
for every
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, and
no sinks
if
\Lambda^{n}v\neq\emptyset
for every
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

7.2.
Higher-rank graph algebras

Higher-rank graph algebras were first defined as
C^{*}
-algebras, by Kumjian and Pask
[
KP00
]
. We will work initially with the algebraic version, which is due to Aranda Pino, Clark, an Huef and Raeburn
[
APCaHR13
]
, although we will modify their definition slightly to take advantage of the fact that we only work over the field
\mathbb{C}
rather than an arbitrary commutative ring with unit.

Definition 7.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a row-finite
k
-graph without sources.
The
Kumjian-Pask algebra

\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda)
is the
*
-algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by the elements
\{p_{v}\}_{v\in\Lambda^{0}}
and
\{s_{e}\}_{e\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}}
with the following relations:

(KP1)

\{p_{v}\}_{v\in\Lambda^{0}}
is a set of mutually orthogonal projections, that is
p_{v}^{*}=p_{v}=p_{v}^{2}
and
p_{v}p_{v^{\prime}}=\delta_{v,v^{\prime}}p_{v}
,

(KP2)

for all
e,e^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
with
\mathsf{s}(e)=\mathsf{r}(e^{\prime})
we have

s_{e}s_{e^{\prime}}=s_{e\cdot e^{\prime}},\quad p_{\mathsf{r}(e)}s_{e}=s_{e}=s_{e}p_{\mathsf{s}(e)},

(KP3)

for all
e,e^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
with
\mathsf{d}(e)=\mathsf{d}(e^{\prime})
we have

s_{e}^{*}s_{e^{\prime}}=\delta_{e,e^{\prime}}p_{\mathsf{s}(e)},

(KP4)

for all
v\in\Lambda^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\backslash\{0\}
we have

p_{v}=\sum_{e\in v\Lambda^{n}}s_{e}s_{e}^{*}.

Remark 7.4
.

The definition in
[
APCaHR13
]
is given in terms of paths
e\in\Lambda^{\neq 0}
and ghost paths
e^{*}\in G(\Lambda^{\neq 0})
, corresponding to generators
s_{e}
and
s_{e^{*}}
.
When working over
\mathbb{C}
there is a
*
-structure uniquely defined by
p_{v}^{*}=p_{v}
and

7.3.
Higher-rank graphs associated to quantum groups

Let
K
be a connected, simply connected compact semisimple Lie group of rank
r
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
and complexification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Our goal in this section is to define a higher-rank graph

of rank

\mathfrak{g}
.
In fact, we will make a more general construction, in anticipation of the results required for torus bundles over flag varieties, compare
Section

3.4
and in particular

Consider any
N

\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
of linearly independent dominant weights.
We refer to
\mathsf{C}
as the set of

and we blur the distinction between
\mathsf{C}
and the index set
\{1,\cdots,N\}

Then to any such choice of colours we will associate a higher-rank graph

The basic case

\mathsf{C}={\bm{\Pi}}=(\varpi_{1},\cdots,\varpi_{r})

Write
\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}:=\mathbb{N}\cdot\mathsf{C}
for the submonoid of

generated by the colours

with the monoid

via the map

\mathbb{N}^{N}\to\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}},\qquad(n_{1},\cdots,n_{N})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}\vartheta_{i}.

In this way, the degree of a path in our higher rank graph will be given by a weight in
\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
.
We also fix the dominant weight

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathsf{C}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\vartheta_{i}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}.

Definition 7.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and fix a set of colours
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
as above.
We define the pair
(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}})
as follows.

•

The
set of vertices
consists of the
\mathsf{C}
-right ends of
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
, that is

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}:=\left\{\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b)\mid b\in\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\right\}\subset\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{N}).

•

The
set of paths

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
is the set of pairs
(v,b)
, where
v=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{N})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
such that
c_{i}\otimes b
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for every colour
i
.

We define the
degree
of the path
e=(v,b)
above to be
\mathsf{d}(e)=\lambda
, where we use the identification
\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}\cong\mathbb{N}^{N}
from (

).

Some remarks are in order. Firstly, we can identify the vertex set
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
with the set
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
of paths of degree

, where

is the unique element of the trivial crystal.
Secondly, note that in the definition of the vertex set
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
, we could equally well use the set of
\mathsf{C}
-right ends of any crystal

with highest weight

, thanks to

Lemma 7.6
.

Let

and fix

with
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(c)=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{N})=v
, for some
\mu\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
with
\mu\geq\rho_{\mathsf{C}}
. Let also

for some

if and only if

is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. In this case, for every

, we have
\mathsf{R}_{\vartheta_{i}}(c_{i}\otimes b)=\mathsf{R}_{\vartheta_{i}}(c\otimes b)

Proof.

First, suppose that

\mathcal{B}(\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)

(2) shows that

Conversely, suppose that
(v,b)\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}

is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for every

We can write

\displaystyle\sigma_{|\mu|}\circ

\displaystyle\cdots\circ\sigma_{k}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{|\mu|}\otimes b)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{|\mu|}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots a_{k-1}\otimes a_{k}^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes a^{\prime}_{|\mu|-1}\otimes c_{i}\otimes b)

In particular,

Definition 7.7
.

We define the

and

as follows.

such that

.

Theorem 7.8
.

The set

becomes a higher-rank graph of rank

•

7.5
,

•

7.7

•

composition of paths

with

(v^{\prime},b^{\prime})\cdot(v,b)=(v,\phi(b\otimes b^{\prime})),

where

Proof.

First we need to show that the composition law is well-defined. Let
(v,b)
and
(v^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
with
\mathsf{r}(v,b)=\mathsf{s}(v^{\prime},b^{\prime})

Associativity follows from the associativity of the tensor product of crystals. The identity arrows are the paths of length

7.4.
Properties of the higher-rank graph

We now prove that our graphs satisfy the properties from
Definition

7.2
.

Proposition 7.10
.

The
N
-graph
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
is row-finite and has no sources and sinks.

Proof.

Row-finiteness is clear, because
v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{\lambda}\subseteq\{v\}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, which is finite.

Now let
v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
and
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
. Pick
c\in\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
with
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(c)=v
. Let
b_{\lambda}
and
b_{w_{0}\lambda}
denote the highest and lowest weight elements of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, respectively. By
Lemma

5.2
,
c\otimes b_{\lambda}
belongs to the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and so
(v,b_{\lambda})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{\lambda}v
.

Likewise,
b_{w_{0}\lambda}\otimes c
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
. Let
c^{\prime}\otimes b=\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})}(b_{w_{0}\lambda}\otimes c)
. Then
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(c^{\prime}\otimes b)=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w_{0}\lambda}\otimes c)=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(c)=v
by
Proposition

6.9
, so putting
v^{\prime}=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(c^{\prime})
we have
(v^{\prime},b)\in v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{\lambda}
.
∎

The higher rank graph
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
is compatible with a partial ordering on the vertices, as we now describe.
Firstly, every irreducible crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
admits a partial ordering by declaring that
b\leq b^{\prime}
if and only if
b=\tilde{F}b^{\prime}
where
\tilde{F}=\tilde{F}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tilde{F}_{i_{m}}
is some product of the Kashiwara operators
\tilde{F}_{i}
. Then for
N
-tuples
v=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})
and
v^{\prime}=(b_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,b_{N}^{\prime})
in
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
, we write
v\leq v^{\prime}
if and only if
b_{i}\leq b_{i}^{\prime}
for every
i
.

Proposition 7.11
.

Consider the partial order
\leq
on
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
defined above. Then:

1)

for any
e\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
we have
\mathsf{s}(e)\leq\mathsf{r}(e)
,

2)

there are unique maximal and minimal elements in
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
, namely

\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{max}}=(b_{\vartheta_{1}},\cdots,b_{\vartheta_{N}}),

\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{min}}=(b_{w_{0}\vartheta_{1}},\cdots,b_{w_{0}\vartheta_{N}}),

where
b_{\lambda}
and
b_{w_{0}\lambda}
denote the highest and lowest weight elements of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, respectively.

Proof.

1) Consider a path
e=(v,b)
with
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.
Write
v=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})
for
\mathsf{s}(e)
and
v^{\prime}=(b_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,b_{N}^{\prime})
for
\mathsf{r}(e)
. Fixing
i
, we have
b^{\prime}_{i}=\mathsf{R}_{\vartheta_{i}}(b_{i}\otimes b)
, so by Proposition
6.7
we have
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i}),\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}:b_{i}\otimes b\mapsto c\otimes b^{\prime}_{i}
for some
c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
. Considering the structure of the braiding operators
\hat{R}_{\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i}),\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}
fixed in (

), we obtain that
b_{i}\leq b_{i}^{\prime}
. The result follows.

2) Consider any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
with
\lambda\geq\mu
.
The inclusion
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\lambda-\mu)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu)
maps
b_{\lambda}
to
b_{\lambda-\mu}\otimes b_{\mu}
, from which we see that
\mathsf{R}_{\mu}(b_{\lambda})=b_{\mu}
. It follows that
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{\rho_{\mathsf{C}}})=(b_{\vartheta_{1}},\cdots,b_{\vartheta_{N}})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
, and this element is clearly maximal. The result for

is obtained in a similar way.

8.
The crystal algebra

8.1.
Definition of the algebra

In this section we introduce an abstract
*
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k}}
which is universal for the generators and relations for the crystal limit
\mathcal{O}[K_{0}]
given in
Theorem

5.11
, so that we get a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k}}\to\mathcal{O}[K_{0}]
.
It provides a convenient bridge between
\mathcal{O}[K_{0}]
and the higher-rank graph algebra
\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
Ultimately, we are going to prove that these three algebras are all
*
-isomorphic.

As in
Section

7.3
, we will anticipate the case of flag varieties by fixing a linearly independent family of dominant weights
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
and writing
\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}=\mathbb{N}\cdot\mathsf{C}
for the monoid generated by the colours.

Definition 8.1
.

We define the
crystal algebra

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
(with respect to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the colours
\mathsf{C}
) to be the unital
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by elements
\{\mathsf{f}_{b},\mathsf{v}_{b}\mid\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}},b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\}
with the following relations:

(1)

for any
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
,
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
,

(8.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}=\eta(b\otimes b^{\prime})\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime\prime}},

\displaystyle\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b}=\eta(b\otimes b^{\prime})\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime\prime}},

where we write
b\otimes b^{\prime}\mapsto b^{\prime\prime}
under the unique surjective crystal morphism
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
;

(2)

for any
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
,
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
,

(8.2)

\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}=\sum_{(c,c^{\prime})}\mathsf{v}_{c^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{c},

where the sum is over all pairs
(c,c^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\times\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
such that the condition
\sigma(c\otimes b^{\prime})=c^{\prime}\otimes b
holds;

(3)

for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
we have

(8.3)

\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}=1.

For the fundamental weights
\mathsf{C}={\bm{\Pi}}
we simply write
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{g}}
instead of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{g},{\bm{\Pi}}}
.

Remark 8.2
.

Note that, by the first relation, we could reduce the set of generators to those
\mathsf{f}_{b}
,
\mathsf{v}_{b}
with
b\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})
for
\vartheta_{i}\in\mathsf{C}
.
Hence the algebra is finitely generated.

As in Remark
5.12
, the relations (
8.1
) imply the exchange relations

(8.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}=\mathsf{f}_{c^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{c},

\displaystyle\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{v}_{c^{\prime}},

whenever
b\otimes b^{\prime}
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
and
\sigma(b\otimes b^{\prime})=c^{\prime}\otimes c
. On the other hand,
\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}=\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b}=0
if
b\otimes b^{\prime}
is not in the Cartan component.

In the case
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, the relations (
8.4
) and (
8.2
) simplify further: for any elements
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
we have

(8.5)

\begin{gathered}\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}=\eta(b\otimes b^{\prime})\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}},\qquad\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b}=\eta(b\otimes b^{\prime})\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b},\\
\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}=\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\eta(c\otimes b)\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{c}.\end{gathered}

Proposition 8.3
.

We have a
*
-structure on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
given by
\mathsf{f}^{*}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{b}
.

Proof.

The only relation of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
which needs some checking is (
8.2
).
By the properties of the Cartan braiding
\sigma
, the relation
\sigma(b\otimes b^{\prime})=c^{\prime}\otimes c
is equivalent to
\sigma(c^{\prime}\otimes c)=b\otimes b^{\prime}
. Then we have

\left(\sum_{(c,c^{\prime})}\mathsf{v}_{c^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{c}\right)^{*}=\sum_{(c,c^{\prime})}\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{c^{\prime}},

where on both sides the sum is over pairs
(c,c^{\prime})
as in (
8.2
). The result follows.
∎

We write
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}
for the crystal algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
equipped with this
*
-structure.
When
\mathsf{C}={\bm{\Pi}}
we simply write
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

The following statement now follows immediately from
Theorem

5.11
.

Proposition 8.4
.

There is a unique surjective morphism of
*
-algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k}}\to\mathcal{O}[K_{0}]
defined on generators as follows: for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
and any
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
we have

\mathsf{f}_{b_{i}}\mapsto\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}_{i}^{\lambda}),\quad\mathsf{v}_{b_{i}}\mapsto\pi_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{i}^{\lambda}),

where
\mathsf{f}_{i}^{\lambda}
and
\mathsf{v}_{i}^{\lambda}
are as in Equation (
3.3
).

With notation as in
Section

3.4
,
if
K
is a compact connected semisimple Lie group, not necessarily simply connected, with complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}
,
S\subset{\bm{\Delta}}
is a set of simple roots, and
Y_{S}=K/K^{0}_{S}
is the associated torus bundle over the flag manifold
X_{S}=K/K_{S}
, then the above morphism restricts to a surjective
*
-morphism
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}\to\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,0}]
where
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
is the family of linearly independent dominant weights which generate
\mathbf{P}^{+}_{K,S}
.

The following notation is useful.

Definition 8.5
.

Given
b=b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
, we write

\mathsf{f}_{b}:=\mathsf{f}_{b_{1}}\cdots\mathsf{f}_{b_{n}},\quad\mathsf{v}_{b}:=\mathsf{v}_{b_{n}}\cdots\mathsf{v}_{b_{1}}.

Note that
\mathsf{f}_{b}^{*}=\mathsf{v}_{b}
. We also adopt the useful convention
\mathsf{f}_{0}=\mathsf{v}_{0}:=0
.

Proposition 8.6
.

Let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})
, and let
\phi:\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{n})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n})
denote the projection onto the Cartan component. Then

\mathsf{f}_{b}=\mathsf{f}_{\phi(b)},\quad\mathsf{v}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{\phi(b)}.

In particular
\mathsf{f}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{b}=0
if
b
is not in the Cartan component.

Proof.

This follows by an inductive argument using the relations (
8.1
).
∎

Remark 8.7
.

Proposition

8.6
implies the following generalization of (
8.4
): with
b
as above, we have
\mathsf{f}_{b}=\mathsf{f}_{\sigma_{s}(b)}
and
\mathsf{v}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{\sigma_{s}(b)}
for every
s\in S_{n}
.

8.2.
Projections associated to crystal elements

Fix
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
. For any
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
we define

P_{b}:=\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}.

As we now show, for each fixed
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
,
\{P_{b}\}_{b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}
is a set of mutually orthogonal projections summing to
1
.

Proposition 8.8
.

For any
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
we have

P_{b}^{*}=P_{b},\quad P_{b}P_{b^{\prime}}=\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}P_{b},\quad\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}P_{b}=1.

Proof.

The first relation follows from the
*
-structure
\mathsf{f}^{*}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{b}
and the third relation is just the unitality relation (
8.3
).
For the second we use the relations (
8.5
).
They immediately imply
P_{b}P_{b}^{\prime}=0
for
b\neq b^{\prime}
, while for
b=b^{\prime}
we compute

\begin{split}P_{b}^{2}&=\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\eta(c\otimes b)\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{b}\\
&=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}=P_{b}.\qed\end{split}

Now consider arbitrary projections
P_{b}
and
P_{b^{\prime}}
with
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
,
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Our next goal is to show that they commute even when
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
.
First we need the following result, which will also be useful elsewhere.

Lemma 8.9
.

Let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Then we have

P_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\\
\mathsf{R}_{\lambda}(c\otimes b^{\prime})=b\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}P_{c}.

Proof.

Using the cross-relations (
8.2
) we have

P_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}=\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}=\sum_{(c,c^{\prime})}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{c^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{c},

where the sum is over all pairs
(c,c^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\times\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
such that
\sigma(c\otimes b^{\prime})=c^{\prime}\otimes b
.
For such pairs we have the relation
\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{c^{\prime}}=\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{c}
. This gives

P_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}=\sum_{c}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{c}=\sum_{c}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}P_{c}.

Finally note that the condition
\sigma(c\otimes b^{\prime})=c^{\prime}\otimes b
is equivalent to
\mathsf{R}_{\lambda}(c\otimes b^{\prime})=b
.
∎

Now we can show that the various projections commute.

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Then
P_{b}P_{b^{\prime}}=P_{b^{\prime}}P_{b}
.

Proof.

We need to show that
P_{b}P_{b^{\prime}}=P_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}
and
P_{b^{\prime}}P_{b}=\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}P_{b}
are equal.
Using the relation from
Lemma

8.9
and its adjoint we have

P_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\\
\mathsf{R}_{\lambda}(c\otimes b^{\prime})=b\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}P_{c}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}=\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}P_{b}.\qed

8.3.
Projections associated to sets of crystal elements

Knowing that the projections
P_{b}
all commute, we can associate projections to
n
-tuples of crystal elements, as in the following definition.

Definition 8.11
.

Let
\mathsf{S}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
be any family of dominant weights and let
B=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
be an
n
-tuple with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i})
for each
i
. We define the projection

P_{B}:=P_{b_{1}}\cdots P_{b_{n}}.

We will be particularly interested in the case where
\mathsf{S}=\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
is a linearly independent family of dominant weights as in
Section

3.4
, and
B=v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
is a vertex of the higher-rank graph of
Section

7.3
.

The next result generalizes
Lemma

8.9
.

Lemma 8.12
.

Let
\mathsf{S}
be as above and let
B=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i})
. Fix any
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
.
Then for any
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
we have

P_{B}\mathsf{v}_{b}=\sum_{B^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b}P_{B^{\prime}},

where the sum is over all families
B^{\prime}=(b_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,b_{n}^{\prime})
with
b_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i})
satisfying

\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{i}}(b_{i}^{\prime}\otimes b)=b_{i}

for every
i=1,\cdots,n
.

Proof.

We have
P_{B}=P_{b_{1}}\cdots P_{b_{n}}
and using
Lemma

8.9
we compute

P_{B}\mathsf{v}_{b}=\sum_{b_{1}^{\prime}:\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{1}}(b_{1}^{\prime}\otimes b)=b_{1}}\cdots\sum_{b_{n}^{\prime}:\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{n}}(b_{n}^{\prime}\otimes b)=b_{n}}\mathsf{v}_{b}P_{b_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots P_{b_{n}^{\prime}}.

This gives the result.
∎

We now show how to rewrite the projections
P_{B}
in a normal form in terms of the generators
\mathsf{v}_{b}
and
\mathsf{f}_{b}
.

Proposition 8.13
.

Let
\mathsf{S}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})
and write
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\mu_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu_{n})
. For any
B=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i})
for each
i
, we have

P_{B}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathcal{B}\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(b)=B\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}.

Proof.

We work by induction on the size
n
of
\mathsf{S}
. For
n=1
, the statement degenerates to the definition of
P_{b_{1}}
.

Now suppose it holds for all sets with
n-1
elements.
Write
\mathsf{S}_{*}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n-1})
,
\mathcal{B}_{*}=\mathcal{B}(\mu_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mu_{n-1})
and
B_{*}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n-1})
.
Using this notation and
Lemma

8.12
we obtain

P_{B}=P_{B_{*}}\mathsf{v}_{b_{n}}\mathsf{f}_{b_{n}}=\sum_{B^{\prime}_{*}}\mathsf{v}_{b_{n}}P_{B^{\prime}_{*}}\mathsf{f}_{b_{n}},

where the sum is over all
B^{\prime}_{*}=(b^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,b^{\prime}_{n-1})
with
b^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i})
satisfying
\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{i}}(b_{i}^{\prime}\otimes b_{n})=b_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,n-1
.
By the inductive assumption we know that

P_{B^{\prime}_{*}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{*}\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}_{*}}(b^{\prime})=B^{\prime}_{*}\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}},

and hence

P_{B}=\sum_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}\otimes b_{n}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}\otimes b_{n}},

where now the sum is over all
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{*}
such that
\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{i}}(\mathsf{R}_{\mu_{i}}(b^{\prime})\otimes b_{n})=b_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,n-1
. But by
Proposition

6.9
this is equivalent to
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(b^{\prime}\otimes b_{n})=B
, so we are done.
∎

Corollary 8.14
.

Let
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
be a family of linearly independent dominant weights. Fix
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
with
\lambda\geq\rho_{\mathsf{C}}
. Then for every
v=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})
with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})
, we have

P_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b)=v\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{f}_{b}.

In particular, we have
P_{v}=0
if
v\notin\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
.

Proof.

Fix
v=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})
as in the statement. Write
\lambda=\sum_{i}n_{i}\vartheta_{i}
and put
\mathsf{S}=(\vartheta_{i_{1}},\cdots,\vartheta_{i_{|\lambda|}})
, where each
\vartheta_{i}\in\mathsf{C}
appears with multiplicity
n_{i}
. Put also
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i_{|\lambda|}})
and
B=(b_{i_{1}},\cdots,b_{i_{|\lambda|}})
with the same multiplicities.

Applying
Proposition

8.13
, and using the fact that
(P_{b_{i}})^{n_{i}}=P_{b_{i}}
, we get

P_{v}=P_{B}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{b}\in\mathcal{B}\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(\tilde{b})=B\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{\tilde{b}}\mathsf{f}_{\tilde{b}}.

But
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(\tilde{b})\neq 0
if and only if
\tilde{b}
is in the Cartan component, in which case its image
b
under the projection to
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
satisfies
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{S}}(b)=B
if and only if
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b)=v
. The result follows.
∎

In
Corollary

9.2
we will show that all
P_{v}
with
v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
are non-zero.

8.4.
Graph algebra relations

Let
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
be a family of linearly independent dominant weights and consider the corresponding higher-rank graph
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
, as in
Definition

7.5
.
For any vertex
v=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})
, we have defined the projection
P_{v}=P_{b_{1}}\cdots P_{b_{N}}
.
Next, given a path
e\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
of the form
e=(v,b)
, we define

S_{e}:=\mathsf{v}_{b}P_{v},\quad S_{e}^{*}:=P_{v}\mathsf{f}_{b}.

Our goal is to show that the elements
\{P_{v},S_{e}\}
satisfy the relations of a Kumjian-Pask algebra, as in
Definition

7.3
.

Remark 8.15
.

If
(v,b)\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}\times\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
does not define an element of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
, then we get
v_{b}P_{v}=0
. To see this, note that if
v=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{N})
then by hypothesis
c_{i}\otimes b
is not in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})
for some
i
. Therefore
\mathsf{v}_{b}P_{c_{i}}=\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{c_{i}}\mathsf{f}_{c_{i}}=0
, and the claim follows.

We begin with the condition (KP1) concerning the elements
P_{v}
.

Lemma 8.16
.

The set
\{P_{v}\}_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}}
consists of mutually orthogonal projections.
Moreover we have
\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}}P_{v}=1
.

Proof.

Given a vertex
v=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
, we have the corresponding projection
P_{v}=P_{b_{1}}\cdots P_{b_{N}}
.
It easily follows from commutativity of the projections and
Proposition

8.8
that
P_{v}
is an orthogonal projection. In the same way one shows that they are mutually orthogonal, that is
P_{v}P_{v^{\prime}}=\delta_{v,v^{\prime}}P_{v}
.

It remains to show that
\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}}P_{v}=1
. It follows from
Proposition

8.8
that

\sum_{b_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1})}\cdots\sum_{b_{N}\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{N})}P_{b_{1}}\cdots P_{b_{N}}=1.

By
Corollary

8.14
we have
P_{b_{1}}\cdots P_{b_{N}}=0
unless
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{N})=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N}^{\prime})
for some
b_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N}^{\prime}
in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{N})
.
Since the latter can be identified with
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
we obtain the result.
∎

Next we look at the condition (KP2), which we divide into two parts for convenience.

Lemma 8.17
.

For any
e\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
we have

P_{\mathsf{r}(e)}S_{e}=S_{e}=S_{e}P_{\mathsf{s}(e)}.

Proof.

Consider a path
e=(v,b)
with range
\mathsf{r}(e)=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{N})
where
c_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{i})
.
Then using
Lemma

8.12
we obtain

P_{\mathsf{r}(e)}S_{e}=P_{\mathsf{r}(e)}v_{b}P_{v}=\sum_{v^{\prime}}v_{b}P_{v^{\prime}}P_{v},

where the sum is over all
v^{\prime}=(c_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,c_{N}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{N})
satisfying the condition
\mathsf{R}_{\vartheta_{i}}(c_{i}^{\prime}\otimes b)=c_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
.
Note that
v^{\prime}=v
satisfies this condition, by definition of the range map.
Then using the relation
P_{v^{\prime}}P_{v}=\delta_{v^{\prime},v}P_{v}
we obtain
P_{\mathsf{r}(e)}S_{e}=v_{b}P_{v}=S_{e}
.
The second identity follows immediately from the fact that
\mathsf{s}(e)=v
, since we have
S_{e}P_{\mathsf{s}(e)}=v_{b}P_{v}P_{v}=S_{e}
.
∎

Lemma 8.18
.

Let
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
be composable paths. Then
S_{e_{1}\cdot e_{2}}=S_{e_{1}}S_{e_{2}}
.

Proof.

Write
e_{i}=(v_{i},b_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
We have
\mathsf{s}(e_{1})=v_{1}=\mathsf{r}(e_{2})
, since
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are composable paths. Then we compute

S_{e_{1}}S_{e_{2}}=\mathsf{v}_{b_{1}}P_{v_{1}}S_{e_{2}}=\mathsf{v}_{b_{1}}P_{\mathsf{r}(e_{2})}S_{e_{2}}=\mathsf{v}_{b_{1}}S_{e_{2}},

where we have used
Lemma

8.17
. Then we obtain
S_{e_{1}}S_{e_{2}}=\mathsf{v}_{b_{1}}\mathsf{v}_{b_{2}}P_{v_{2}}
.
Recall that the composition of paths is given by
e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=(v_{2},\phi(b_{2}\otimes b_{1}))
, where
\phi:\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
denotes the projection onto the Cartan component.
Then
\mathsf{v}_{b_{1}}\mathsf{v}_{b_{2}}=\mathsf{v}_{b_{2}\otimes b_{1}}=\mathsf{v}_{\phi(b_{2}\otimes b_{1})}
and we get
S_{e_{1}}S_{e_{2}}=S_{e_{1}\cdot e_{2}}
.
∎

Next we consider the condition (KP3), concerning source projections.

Lemma 8.19
.

For any
e,e^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
with
\mathsf{d}(e)=\mathsf{d}(e^{\prime})
we have

S_{e}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}}=\delta_{e,e^{\prime}}P_{\mathsf{s}(e)}.

Proof.

Write
e=(v,b)
and
e^{\prime}=(v^{\prime},b^{\prime})
with
\mathsf{d}(e)=\mathsf{d}(e^{\prime})=\lambda
, that is
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
.
By (
8.5
) we have
f_{b}v_{b^{\prime}}=\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\eta(c\otimes b^{\prime})P_{c}
and so

(8.6)

S_{e}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}}=P_{v}f_{b}v_{b^{\prime}}P_{v^{\prime}}=\delta_{b,b^{\prime}}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\eta(c\otimes b^{\prime})P_{v}P_{c}P_{v^{\prime}}.

Recall that the projections commute and we have
P_{v}P_{v^{\prime}}=\delta_{v,v^{\prime}}P_{v}
for
v,v^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
.
Then we find that
S_{e}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}}=\delta_{e,e^{\prime}}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)}\eta(c\otimes b)P_{c}P_{v}
.
If
e\neq e^{\prime}
we are done, so it remains to deal with the case
e=e^{\prime}
.

We work by induction on the total length
|\lambda|=\sum_{i}n_{i}
where
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}\vartheta_{i}
. If
|\lambda|=0
, then we have
S^{*}_{e}S_{e}=P_{v}^{*}P_{v}=P_{v}
so the lemma holds.
Suppose the lemma holds for any
e^{\prime}
of degree
\lambda^{\prime}
with
|\lambda^{\prime}|<n
. Consider
e=e^{\prime\prime}\cdot e^{\prime}
with
e^{\prime\prime}
of length
1
and
e^{\prime}
of length
n-1
.
Note that
\mathsf{s}(e^{\prime\prime})=\mathsf{r}(e^{\prime})
since they are composable. Then we compute

\begin{split}S_{e}^{*}S_{e}&=S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}S_{e^{\prime\prime}}^{*}S_{e^{\prime\prime}}S_{e^{\prime}}=S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}P_{\mathsf{s}(e^{\prime\prime})}S_{e^{\prime}}=S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}P_{\mathsf{r}(e^{\prime})}S_{e^{\prime}}\\
&=S_{e^{\prime}}^{*}S_{e^{\prime}}=P_{\mathsf{s}(e^{\prime})}=P_{\mathsf{s}(e)},\end{split}

where we use the relation
P_{\mathsf{r}(e)}S_{e}=S_{e}
from
Lemma

8.17
.
∎

Finally we look at the condition (KP4), concerning range projections.
Recall that
v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{n}
denotes the set of paths of degree
n
and range
v
.

Lemma 8.20
.

For any
v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
we have

P_{v}=\sum_{e\in v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{n}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}.

Proof.

We identify
n\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
with the corresponding dominant weight
\lambda=\sum_{i}n_{i}\vartheta_{i}
.
Then we consider the sum
\sum_{e}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}
over all paths of degree
\lambda
and range
v
.

Let
e=(v^{\prime},b)\in v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{n}
.
From
Corollary

8.14
we have

S_{e}S_{e}^{*}=\mathsf{v}_{b}P_{v^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b^{\prime})=v^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b}.

Summing this over all such
e=(v^{\prime},b)\in v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{n}
, we get

\sum_{e=(v^{\prime},b)\in v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{n}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda),~b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b^{\prime}\otimes b)=v\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{b}\mathsf{v}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b}

Recall that
\mathsf{f}_{b^{\prime}}\mathsf{f}_{b}=0
unless
b^{\prime}\otimes b
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, in which case it is equal to
\mathsf{f}_{c}
, where
c
is the image of
b^{\prime}\otimes b
under the projection
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
. Thus the above sum becomes

\sum_{e=(v^{\prime},b)\in v\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{n}}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\\
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(c)=v\end{subarray}}\mathsf{v}_{c}\mathsf{f}_{c}=P_{v},

again by
Corollary

8.14
.
∎

Collecting all the results above, we obtain the following.

Proposition 8.21
.

There is a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}})\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}
, which on the generators is given by

p_{v}\mapsto P_{v},\quad s_{e}\mapsto S_{e}.

Proof.

The existence of the
*
-homomorphism follows from the fact that the elements
P_{v},S_{e}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}
satisfy the relations (KP1)–(KP4), as we have shown above.

To prove surjectivity, we must show that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}
is generated by the elements
S_{e}
with
e\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
and their adjoints.
Let
b\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}_{\mathsf{C}}
. From
Lemma

5.2
, we have that
b_{w_{0}\rho_{\mathsf{C}}}\otimes b
is in the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
, so putting
v^{\prime}=\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w_{0}\rho_{\mathsf{C}}})
, we have that
e=(v^{\prime},b)
defines a path in
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
. Therefore
S_{e}=\mathsf{v}_{b}P_{v^{\prime}}
is non-zero. Using
Proposition

8.8
and
Remark

8.15
, we get

v_{b}=\sum_{v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}}v_{b}P_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}\\
(v,b)\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}\end{subarray}}S_{(v,b)}.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}
is generated by the
\mathsf{v}_{b}
’s and their adjoints, the result follows.
∎

In the next section we will show that this map is an isomorphism.

9.
Crystal limits are higher-rank graph algebras

The universal maps from
Proposition

8.21
and
Proposition

8.4
yield a pair of surjective

-homomorphisms

(9.1)

\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k}}\to\mathcal{O}[K_{0}].

In this section, we will show that these maps are isomorphisms, as well as their restrictions to the subalgebras

(9.2)

\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}})\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{k},\mathsf{C}}\to\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,0}]

corresponding to the principal torus bundle over any flag variety of a connected compact semisimple group
K
.

We use the same notation as in
Section

3.2
, namely, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{P}^{+}
we fix a weight basis
\{v^{\lambda}_{i}\}_{i}
, sometimes denoted simply

, lifting the crystal
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)=\{b^{\lambda}_{i}\}_{i}

be the dual basis. We define the generating matrix coefficients

and
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}
for
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]

Lemma 9.1
.

The image of every one of the generators
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}
and
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]
from
Definition

3.2
under the representation
\pi_{0}
is non-zero.

Proof.

Let
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}
be the chosen reduced decomposition of the longest word of the Weyl group. The (partially defined) Soibelman representation

\pi_{q}=(\tilde{\pi}_{i_{1},q}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{i_{2},q}\otimes\cdots\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{i_{l},q}\otimes\chi)\circ\Delta^{(l)}:\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]\to\mathcal{B}(\mathsf{H}).

maps
\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\mathbf{A}_{0}}[K]
to
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}\mathbb{N})^{\otimes l}\otimes C(T)
.
Since
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{j}=c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{j},v_{1}}
we have

\Delta^{(l)}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{j})=\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{l}}c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{j},v_{k_{1}}}\otimes c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{k_{1}},v_{k_{2}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{k_{l}},v_{1}}.

Let
\epsilon_{T}:C(T)\to\mathbb{C}
denote the evaluation at the identity in
T
. Then

(9.3)

(\id^{\otimes l}\otimes\epsilon_{T})\circ\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}_{b_{j}})=\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{l-1}}\tilde{\pi}_{i_{1},0}(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{j},v_{k_{1}}})\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{i_{2},0}(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{k_{1}},v_{k_{2}}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{i_{l},0}(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{k_{l-1}},v_{1}}).

With respect to the standard basis for

have all coefficients equal to

4.2
, so there can be no cancellation in the sum (

). Therefore it suffices to check that at least one of the terms in the sum is non-zero.

For this, we use the notion of

, see for instance
[

, Chapter 11]
, which shows that if

is the highest weight element and

. Explicitly, these are given by

,
a_{2}=\varepsilon_{i_{2}}(\tilde{E}_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}b)
,
etc
.
Therefore

for

is a highest weight crystal element for the restriction to

associated to the simple root

. Therefore, by
Theorem

4.2
we have

\tilde{\pi}_{i_{1},0}(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{j},v_{j_{1}}})\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{i_{2},0}(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{j_{1}},v_{j_{2}}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{i_{l},0}(c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{j_{l-1}},v_{j_{l}}})\neq 0,

and so
\pi_{0}(f^{\lambda}_{j})\neq 0
.

The result for
\pi_{0}(\mathsf{v}_{b})
follows by taking adjoints.
∎

Now let
\Phi:\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}})\to\mathcal{O}[K_{0}]
denote the
*
-homomorphism of (
9.1
).

Corollary 9.2
.

For any

we have
\Phi(p_{v})\neq 0
.

Proof.

By
Corollary

8.14
, and
Proposition

8.4
, we have

\Phi(p_{v})=\sum_{i}\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i})^{*}\pi_{0}(\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}),

where the sum is over all

such that
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{i})=v
. The result now follows from
Lemma

9.1
.
∎

We are now in a position to apply the gauge invariant uniqueness theorem
[
KP00
]
, or more precisely the algebraic version due to Aranda Pino, Clark, an Huef and Raeburn
[
APCaHR13
]
.
Recall that the Kumjian-Pask algebra
\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}})
is equipped with a natural
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
-grading, see
[
APCaHR13
, Theorem 3.4]
. Explicitly, each generator
s_{e}
is given degree
d(e)
,
s_{e}^{*}
is given degree
-d(e)
and the vertex projections

are given degree
0
.

Meanwhile, the
\mathbf{P}
-grading on
\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]
from

3.5
is such that
\mathsf{f}^{\lambda}_{i}=c^{V(\lambda)}_{f^{i},v_{1}}
has degree
\lambda
and
\mathsf{v}^{\lambda}_{i}
has degree
-\lambda
. Here, as previously, we are identifying
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
with
\mathbf{P}
via
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})\mapsto\sum_{i}n_{i}\varpi_{i}
.

Moreover, the Soibelman representations

are all grading-preserving, in the following sense. By Pontrjagin duality, we have

-graded algebra in the obvious way. Examining the formula for

in

, we see that
\pi_{q}:\mathcal{O}_{q}[K]_{\mu}\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})^{\otimes l})\otimes\mathcal{O}[T]_{\mu}

, this makes

-graded algebra.

Now let

with degree

, so that

for some

. The image of

in

. Under the universal map

, which has degree

, while the projection

is grading-reversing.

Thus, equipping

with the opposite grading and taking into account

9.2
, we can immediately apply the graded-uniqueness theorem as appearing in
[

, Theorem 4.1]
. We get the following result.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let

be a compact, connected, simply connected semisimple Lie group.
The map

is an isomorphism of
*
-algebras. Consequently, we have a

-isomorphism

.

By restricting to the subalgebras spanned by elements of degree
\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
for the
N
-tuple of dominant weights
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\cdots,\vartheta_{N})
described in
Section

3.4
, we deduce the following more general result.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
K
be a compact, connected, semisimple Lie group, not necessarily simply connected.
Let
S\subseteq{\bm{\Delta}}
be a set of simple roots, with
X_{S}
be the associated flag variety and

the torus bundle over

, as described in
Section

3.4
. Let
\mathsf{C}
be the
N
-tuple of dominant weights generating the submonoid
\mathbf{P}^{+}_{K,S}
from
Theorem

3.8
.

The map
\Phi
above restricts to an isomorphism of
*
-algebras
\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}})\to\mathcal{O}[Y_{S,0}]
. The degree

\mathrm{KP}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}})_{0}
is isomorphic to

.

Taking

.

It is well-known that the family of

-algebras. The matrix coefficients

.

10.
Further properties and examples

We conclude with some additional remarks on the structure of the higher-rank graphs
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
, in particular in relation to the role of the Weyl group.
First we need the following property of the Cartan braiding, which is a consequence of a similar property for the ordinary braiding
\hat{R}
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
be irreducible crystals.
Then the condition

\sigma_{\mathcal{B}(\lambda),\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=b^{\prime}\otimes b

is equivalent to
b\otimes b^{\prime}
being in the Cartan component and
(\wt(b),\wt(b^{\prime}))=(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Consider
V=V(\lambda)
and
W=V(\lambda^{\prime})
.
Using the definition of the braiding from (
5.1
) we have
(\hat{R}_{V,W})_{ij}^{ji}=q^{-(\wt(v_{i}),\wt(w_{j}))}
for any
v_{i}\in V
and
w_{j}\in W
.
Taking into account that
(\wt(v_{i}),\wt(w_{j}))\leq(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
, we get

(10.1)

\lim_{q\to 0}q^{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}(\hat{R}_{V,W})_{ij}^{ji}=\begin{cases}1&(\wt(v_{i}),\wt(w_{j}))=(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}),\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now denote by
b_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda)
and
c_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime})
the crystal elements corresponding to
v_{i}
and
w_{j}
.
According to
Theorem

5.3
, we have
\sigma(b_{i}\otimes c_{j})=0
if
b_{i}\otimes c_{j}
is not in the Cartan component, otherwise
\sigma(b_{i}\otimes c_{j})=c_{k}\otimes b_{l}
for some
k,l
.
Then (
10.1
) shows that, in the case
(\wt(v_{i}),\wt(w_{j}))=(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
, we have
\sigma(b_{i}\otimes c_{j})=c_{j}\otimes b_{i}
.
The converse also follows in a similar way from (
10.1
).
∎

Remark 10.2
.

Suppose
b
and
b^{\prime}
are such that
\wt(b)=w\lambda
and
\wt(b^{\prime})=w\lambda^{\prime}
for some
w\in W
, where
W
denotes the Weyl group. Then
W
-invariance of the inner product gives
(\wt(b),\wt(b^{\prime}))=(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
.

As usual, let
\mathsf{C}=(\vartheta_{1},\ldots,\vartheta_{N})
be a family of dominant weights. For
w\in W
, let us write
b_{w}
, for the unique element of
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
with extremal weight
w\rho_{\mathsf{C}}
.

Proposition 10.3
.

Consider the map

W\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0},\quad w\mapsto\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w}).

Write
W_{\rho_{\mathsf{C}}}\subset W
for the stabilizer of the dominant weight
\rho_{\mathsf{C}}
.

(1)

We have an embedding of
W/W_{\rho_{\mathsf{C}}}
into
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
.

(2)

When
\mathsf{C}={\bm{\Pi}}
this is an embedding of
W
.

Proof.

(1) We have a map
W/W_{\rho_{\mathsf{C}}}\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}^{0}
, since
w\rho_{\mathsf{C}}=w^{\prime}\rho_{\mathsf{C}}
if and only if
w^{-1}w^{\prime}\in W_{\rho_{\mathsf{C}}}
.
Hence it suffices to show that
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w})\neq\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w^{\prime}})
whenever
b_{w}\neq b_{w^{\prime}}
.

Identify
\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})=\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1}+\cdots\vartheta_{N})
with the Cartan component of
\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{N})
. Then
b_{w}\in\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
corresponds to a unique element
b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N}\in\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{B}(\vartheta_{N})
.
Since
w\rho_{\mathsf{C}}=w\vartheta_{1}+\cdots+w\vartheta_{N}
has multiplicity one, being in the Weyl group orbit of
\rho_{\mathsf{C}}
, we find that
\wt(b_{i})=w\vartheta_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
.
It follows that
(\wt(b_{i}),\wt(b_{j}))=(\vartheta_{i},\vartheta_{j})
for any
i
and
j
.

According to
Proposition

6.7
, the right end
\mathsf{R}_{\vartheta_{k}}(b_{w})
is equal to the rightmost factor of
\sigma_{N-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{k}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N})

Then using

10.1
we obtain

\sigma_{N-1}\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{k}(b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N})=b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\widehat{b_{k}}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N}\otimes b_{k}.

It follows that
\mathsf{R}_{\vartheta_{k}}(b_{w})=b_{k}
for any
k=1,\cdots,N
.
Since
b_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b_{N}
is the unique element corresponding to
b_{w}
, we find that
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w})\neq\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b_{w^{\prime}})
when
b_{w}\neq b_{w^{\prime}}
.

(2) In this case the stabilizer of
\rho=\varpi_{1}+\cdots+\varpi_{r}
is trivial.
∎

We conclude with one more example corresponding to a quantum homogeneous spaces, to connect with some existing literature.

Example 10.7
.

Consider the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
and the single colour
\mathsf{C}=(\varpi_{1})
.
Geometrically this example corresponds to
Y_{S}=S^{2n-1}
being an odd-dimensional sphere and
X_{S}=\mathbb{C}P^{n-1}
, with notation as in
Section

3.4
.

In this case we have
\rho_{\mathsf{C}}=\varpi_{1}
, which obviously implies that
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{C}}(b)=b
for every
b\in\mathcal{B}(\rho_{\mathsf{C}})
.
Therefore the vertex set of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{C}}
can be identified with the weights of the minuscule representation
V(\varpi_{1})
.

Let us label the crystal basis by
\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\}
, where
b_{i+1}=\tilde{F}_{i}b_{i}
.
It is easy to see that
b_{i}\otimes b_{j}
is in the Cartan component if and only if
i\geq j
(in fact, one can generalize this to any minuscule representation).
Therefore there is a single edge from
b_{i}
to
b_{j}
for every
i\geq j
.
This graph coincides with the one given in
[
HS02
, Theorem 4.4]
(up to switching sources and ranges of edges, to match up with our conventions).

Abstract
. We show that the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L L -functions is a consequence of certain conjectural properties of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Conversely, we prove that the zeros of ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) satisfy those properties under GRH.

1.
Introduction

In a recent paper
[
1
]
, the author has shown that
all Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
are nonvanishing
in the half-plane
\sigma>\tfrac{9}{10}
if and only if
the zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
satisfy
certain distributional properties, including the classical
Riemann Hypothesis (RH). In the present paper, we extend that
result to show that the full Generalized Riemann
Hypothesis (GRH) holds if and only if the zeros of
\zeta(s)

satisfy similar criteria.

To formulate our main result, we introduce some notation.
Throughout the paper, let
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
be the space of smooth
functions
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Let
\mu
and
\phi
be the Möbius and Euler functions, respectively.
Also, let us recall the functional equation

\zeta(s)=\mathcal{X}(s)\zeta(1-s)
, where

\mathcal{X}(1-s)=\mathcal{X}(s)^{-1}=\pi^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}s)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}s)}=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\cos\tfrac{1}{2}\pi s.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis holds for all Dirichlet
L
-functions;

(ii)

The Riemann Hypothesis is true, and
for any rational number
\xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=m/q
with
0<m<q

and
(m,q)=1
, any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(1.1)

\sum_{\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma}\xi^{-\rho}\,\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi X}\Big)+\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)}C_{\mathcal{B}}X\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon},

where the sum on the left runs over all complex zeros

\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)

(
counted with multiplicity
)
,

C_{\mathcal{B}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,du
,
and the implied constant depends only on

\xi
,
\mathcal{B}
, and
\varepsilon
.

We emphasize that statement
(ii)
involves
only properties of the Riemann zeta function. In particular,

no conditions are placed on the zeros
of
non-principal
Dirichlet
L
-functions
.

[

Theorem

Notation

\mathbf{e}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi iu}

for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
. As in Theorem
1.1
,
C_{\mathcal{B}}

is used to denote the constant
\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,du
.

Throughout the paper, any implied constants in the
symbols

, etc., may depend on various parameters
(e.g.,
\xi
,

, and

2.2.
The function
\mathcal{X}(s)

For fixed
\delta>0
, Stirling’s formula for the gamma function,
namely

\Gamma(z)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,z^{z-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-z}\big\{1+O_{\delta}(|z|^{-1})\big\},

holds uniformly for all complex
z
satisfying

|z|\geqslant\delta
and
|{\rm arg}~z|<\pi-\delta
;
see, for example, Montgomery and Vaughan
[
10
, Thm. C.1]
.
From this, it is not difficult to derive the estimates

(2.1)

\mathcal{X}(1-s)=\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi/4}\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi}\Big)^{\sigma-1/2}\exp\Big(it\log\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi\mathrm{e}}\Big)\Big)\big\{1+O_{\mathcal{I}}(t^{-1})\big\}

and

(2.2)

\mathcal{X}(\overline{s})=\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi/4}\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi}\Big)^{1/2-\sigma}\exp\Big(it\log\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi\mathrm{e}}\Big)\Big)\big\{1+O_{\mathcal{I}}(t^{-1})\big\}

uniformly for all
\sigma
in a given bounded interval
\mathcal{I}

and all
t\geqslant 1
. The following lemma
is due to Gonek
[
7
, Lem. 2]
; the proof uses
(
2.1
) and the stationary phase method.
The formulation given here is a special case
of Conrey, Ghosh and Gonek
[
3
, Lem. 1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
r>0
and
\tfrac{1}{100}\leqslant c\leqslant 2
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+i}^{c+iT}r^{-s}\mathcal{X}(1-s)\,ds=\begin{cases}\mathbf{e}(-r)+E(r,c)r^{-c}&\quad\hbox{if $r\leqslant T/2\pi$},\\
E(r,c)r^{-c}&\quad\hbox{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

where

E(r,c)\ll T^{c-1/2}+\frac{T^{c+1/2}}{|T-2\pi r|+T^{1/2}}.

2.3.
Reformulation of GRH

For a given Dirichlet character
\chi\bmod q
,
its Dirichlet
L
-function
is defined
for
\sigma>1
by

L(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\chi(n)n^{-s}=\prod_{p\text{~prime}}(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.

Consider the following hypothesis about the zeros of
L(s,\chi)
:

Hypothesis
{\rm GRH}[\chi]
:

If
L(\beta+i\gamma,\chi)=0
and
\beta>0
, then
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
.

The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis is the
assertion that
{\rm GRH}[\chi]
holds for all
characters
\chi
. Note that for a principal
character
\chi_{0}\bmod q
, we have

L(s,\chi_{0})=\zeta(s)\prod_{p\,\mid\,q}(1-p^{-s}),

hence
{\rm GRH}[\chi_{0}]
is equivalent to RH. Similarly, if

\chi
is induced from a primitive character
\chi_{*}

of conductor
q_{*}>1
, then
L(s,\chi)
and
L(s,\chi_{*})
have the same zeros
in the critical strip since

L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi_{*})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\,\mid\,q\\
p\,\nmid\,q_{*}\end{subarray}}(1-\chi_{*}(p)p^{-s}),

and therefore
{\rm GRH}[\chi]
is equivalent to
{\rm GRH}[\chi_{*}]
.
From these remarks, and taking into account
the special case
\beta_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tfrac{1}{2}
of
[
1
, Lem. 5.1]
,
the next result is immediate.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis holds for all Dirichlet
L
-functions;

(ii)

The Riemann Hypothesis is true, and
for every primitive character
\chi
of modulus
q>1
,
any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

2.4.
Initial estimates

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
RH
.
For every
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, we have

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)=C_{\mathcal{B}}X+O_{\mathcal{B}}(X^{1/2}\log^{2}X).

Proof.

Under RH, the Chebyshev function

\psi(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\Lambda(n)
is known to satisfy

(2.3)

\psi(x)=x+E(x)\qquad\text{with}\quad E(x)\ll x^{1/2}\log^{2}x;

see
[
10
, Thm. 13.1]
.
Using Riemann-Stieltjes integration, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/X)\,d\psi(u)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,d\psi(Xu)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,d(Xu)+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,dE(Xu)

\displaystyle=C_{\mathcal{B}}X-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)E(Xu)\,du.

The bound in (
2.3
) implies that the last integral is

O_{\mathcal{B}}(X^{1/2}\log^{2}X)
, and the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume GRH. For any rational number
\xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=m/q
with
0<m<q

and
(m,q)=1
,
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
\varepsilon>0
,
we have

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\mathcal{B}(n/X)=\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)}C_{\mathcal{B}}X+O_{\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.3
,
this is a consequence of
[
1
, Lem. 5.2]
.
∎

3.
Twisting the von Mangoldt function

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
T\geqslant 100
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
0<\gamma\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)+\sum_{n\leqslant T/2\pi\xi}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T^{1/2}\log^{2}T.

where the first sum runs over complex zeros

\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)

(
counted with multiplicity
)
,
and the implied constant depends only on
\xi
.

Proof.

For
u>1
, let

\Sigma_{1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
0<\gamma\leqslant u\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}(1-\rho),\qquad\Sigma_{2}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<n\leqslant u\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi).

Then our goal is to show that

(3.1)

\Sigma_{1}(T)+\Sigma_{2}(T/2\pi\xi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T^{1/2}\log^{2}T.

According to
[
10
, Lemma 12.2]
,
for any real number
T_{*}\geqslant 2
, there exists
T\in[T_{*},T_{*}+1]
such that

(3.2)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma+iT)\ll\log^{2}T\qquad(-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2).

In proving (
3.1
), we can assume
that
T
has this property. Indeed, let
T_{*}\geqslant 100
be
arbitrary, and suppose
T\in[T_{*},T_{*}+1]
satisfies
(
3.1
).
By (
2.1
), we have
\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)\ll|\gamma|^{1/2}

for all complex zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
,
and therefore

\big|\Sigma_{1}(T_{*})-\Sigma_{1}(T)\big|\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
T_{*}<\gamma\leqslant T_{*}+1\end{subarray}}\big|\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)\big|\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T_{*}^{1/2}\log T_{*}

since the number of zeros

\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
with
T_{*}<\gamma\leqslant T_{*}+1

does not exceed
O(\log T_{*})
. We also have

\big|\Sigma_{2}(T_{*}/2\pi\xi)-\Sigma_{2}(T/2\pi\xi)\big|\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{*}/2\pi\xi<n\leqslant(T_{*}+1)/2\pi\xi\end{subarray}}\big|\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\big|\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\log T_{*}.

Combining the preceding two bounds and (
3.1
),
it follows that

\Sigma_{1}(T_{*})+\Sigma_{2}(T_{*}/2\pi\xi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T_{*}^{1/2}\log^{2}T_{*}.

From now on, we assume that
T
satisfies (
3.2
).
In particular,
T
is not the ordinate of a zero of
\zeta(s)
.
Put
c\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1+\frac{1}{\log T}
,
and let
\mathcal{C}
be the rectangle in
\mathbb{C}
:

c+i~~\longrightarrow~~c+iT~~\longrightarrow~~-1+iT~~\longrightarrow~~-1+i~~\longrightarrow~~c+i.

By Cauchy’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{c+i}^{c+iT}+\intop\nolimits_{c+iT}^{-1+iT}+\intop\nolimits_{-1+iT}^{-1+i}+\intop\nolimits_{-1+i}^{c+i}\bigg)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{X}(1-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\quad\text{(say)}.

We estimate the four integrals separately.

Since

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)=-\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}}\qquad(\sigma>1),

it follows that

I_{1}=-\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+i}^{c+iT}(n\xi)^{-s}\mathcal{X}(1-s)\,ds.

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we get that

I_{1}=-\sum_{n\xi\leqslant T/2\pi}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}O\bigg(}\xi^{-c}\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}T^{c-1/2}+\xi^{-c}\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{T^{c+1/2}}{|T-2\pi n\xi|+T^{1/2}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bigg)}.

The first sum is
\Sigma_{2}(T/2\pi\xi)
; hence, defining

T_{\circ}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T/2\pi\xi
and noting that

(3.3)

\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}=-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c)\ll\frac{1}{c-1}=\log T,

we see that

(3.4)

I_{1}+\Sigma_{2}(T/2\pi\xi)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T^{1/2}\log T+T^{3/2}\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}.

For the last sum, we split the integers
n\geqslant 2
into
three disjoint sets:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{n\geqslant 2:|n-T_{\circ}|>\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\circ}\},

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{n\geqslant 2:|n-T_{\circ}|\leqslant T_{\circ}^{1/2}\},

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{n\geqslant 2:T_{\circ}^{1/2}<|n-T_{\circ}|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}T_{\circ}\}.

Since
|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}\gg_{\xi}T

for each
n\in S_{1}
, we have by (
3.3
):

(3.5)

\sum_{n\in S_{1}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\frac{\log T}{T},

which is acceptable. For each
n\in S_{2}
, we have

n\asymp T_{\circ}\,\mathop{\asymp}\limits_{\xi}\,T,\qquad\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\frac{\log T}{T^{c}}\asymp\frac{\log T}{T},\qquad\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T^{-1/2}.

As
|S_{2}|\ll T^{1/2}
, it follows that

(3.6)

\sum_{n\in S_{2}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\frac{\log T}{T}.

Finally, for each
n\in S_{3}
, we again have

n\asymp T_{\circ}\,\mathop{\asymp}\limits_{\xi}\,T
, and so

\sum_{n\in S_{3}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\frac{\log T}{T}\sum_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}n\in S_{3}}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}.

The last sum is bounded by

\ll\sum_{T_{\circ}^{1/2}\leqslant k\leqslant\frac{1}{2}T_{\circ}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geqslant 1\\
k<|n-T_{\circ}|\leqslant k+1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}\ll\sum_{T_{\circ}^{1/2}\leqslant k\leqslant\frac{1}{2}T_{\circ}}\frac{1}{k}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\log T,

hence it follows that

(3.7)

\sum_{n\in S_{3}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{1}{|n-T_{\circ}|+T^{1/2}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\frac{\log^{2}T}{T}.

Combining the bounds (
3.4
)
-
(
3.7
),
we have therefore shown that

(3.8)

I_{1}=-\Sigma_{2}(T/2\pi\xi)+O_{\xi}(T^{1/2}\log^{2}T).

Next, observe that (
2.1
) yields the uniform bound

\mathcal{X}(1-(\sigma+iT))\ll T^{1/2}\qquad(-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant c).

Recalling (
3.2
), it follows that

(3.9)

I_{2}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{-1+iT}^{c+iT}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{X}(1-s)\,ds\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,T^{1/2}\log^{2}T.

Similarly, using (
2.2
) we derive that

\mathcal{X}(2-it)\ll t^{-3/2}\qquad(1\leqslant t\leqslant T).

Moreover, according to
[
10
, Lemma 12.4]
,
the following bound holds:

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(-1+it)\ll\log 2t\qquad(1\leqslant t\leqslant T).

Therefore,

(3.10)

I_{3}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{-1+i}^{-1+iT}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{X}(1-s)\,ds\ll\xi^{-1}\intop\nolimits_{1}^{T}t^{-3/2}\log 2t\,dt\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,1.

Finally, it is clear that

(3.11)

I_{4}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{1-c+i}^{c+i}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{X}(1-s)\,ds\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,1.

Combining (
3.8
)
-
(
3.11
), we obtain
(
3.1
), and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
X>100\xi^{-1}
,
we have

(3.12)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)+\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\mathcal{B}(n/X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi,\mathcal{B}}\,X^{1/2}\log^{2}X.

Proof.

Denote

F_{1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\

Applying Theorem
3.1
with
T\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2\pi\xi Xu
,
we have the uniform bound

(3.13)

F_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)+F_{2}(Xu)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,(Xu)^{1/2}\log^{2}(Xu)\qquad(u\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2},~X>100\xi^{-1}).

Using Riemann-Stieltjes integration, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{u}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)\,dF_{1}(u)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,dF_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)

\displaystyle=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)F_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)\,du,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\mathcal{B}(n/X)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u/X)\,dF_{2}(u)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,dF_{2}(Xu)

\displaystyle=-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)F_{2}(Xu)\,du,

and so the left side of (

) is equal to

-\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(u)\Big\{F_{1}(2\pi\xi Xu)+F_{2}(Xu)\Big\}\,du.

Using (
3.13
), the result follows immediately.

(m,q)=1
, a function

\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma
2.4
,

(4.1)

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\mathcal{B}(n/X)=\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)}C_{\mathcal{B}}X+O_{\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}),

and Corollary
3.2
shows that

(4.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)+\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi)\mathcal{B}(n/X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi,\mathcal{B}}\,X^{1/2}\log^{2}X.

Combining these results, we deduce the bound (
1.1
),
and this completes our proof that
(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
.

To prove the reverse implication
(ii)\Longrightarrow(i)
, assume RH
and the bound (
1.1
) for all triples

(\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon)
as above. Applying
Corollary
3.2
again, which yields the
bound (
4.2
), and using (
1.1
),
we deduce that (
4.1
) again holds.

According to Lemma
2.2
, to establish GRH, it suffices
to show that for any primitive character
\chi
modulo
q>1
,
any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(4.3)

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{q,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

For this, we invoke the following identity
(see, e.g., Bump
[
2
, Chapter 1]
):

(4.4)

\chi(n)=\frac{\chi(-1)\tau(\chi)}{q}\sum_{m\bmod q}\overline{\chi}(m)\mathbf{e}(-nm/q)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z}),

where the sum runs over any complete set of reduced residue
classes
m\bmod q
, and

\tau(\chi)
is the Gauss sum given by

\tau(\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\bmod q}\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(n/q).

Using (
4.1
) and (
4.4
), we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(-1)\tau(\chi)}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m<q\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(m)\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathbf{e}(-nm/q)\mathcal{B}(n/X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(-1)\tau(\chi)}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m<q\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(m)\bigg(\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)}C_{\mathcal{B}}X+O_{\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(-1)\tau(\chi)}{q}\frac{\mu(q)}{\phi(q)}C_{\mathcal{B}}X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m<q\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(m)+O_{q,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}).

Since
\chi
is primitive and
q>1
, it follows that
\chi

is non-principal, and thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m<q\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(m)=0.

Consequently, the first expression in the preceding estimate vanishes,
and we obtain (
4.3
) as required.
This completes our proof that
(ii)\Longrightarrow(i)
.

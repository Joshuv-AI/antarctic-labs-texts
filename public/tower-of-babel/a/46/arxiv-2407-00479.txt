Faces of quasidensity
Stephen Simons

Abstract
This paper is about the maximally monotone and quasidense subsets of the product of a real Banach space and its dual. We discuss six subclasses of the maximal monotone sets that are equivalent to the quasidense ones. We define the Gossez extension to the dual of a maximally monotone set, and give nine equivalent characterizations of an element of this set in the quasidense case. We discuss maximally monotone sets of “type (NI)” (one of the six classes referred to above) and we show that the “tail operator” is not of type (NI), but it is the Gossez extension of a maximally monotone set that is of type (NI). We generalize Rockafellar’s surjectivity theorem for maximally monotone subsets of reflexive Banach spaces to maximally monotone subsets of type (NI) of general Banach spaces. We discuss a generalization of the Brezis-Browder theorem on monotone linear subspaces of reflexive spaces to the nonreflexive situation. We also discuss briefly maximally monotone subsets of “type (D)” and “type (WD)” (two more of the six classes referred to above).

Banach space notation

Definition 2.1
.

If
X
is a nonzero real Banach space and
f\colon\ X\to\,]{-}\infty,\infty]
, we write
\hbox{\rm dom}\,f:=f^{-1}\mathbb{R}
, and say that
f
is
proper
if
\hbox{\rm dom}\,f\neq\emptyset
. If
f\colon X\to\,]{-}\infty,\infty]
is proper and
g\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, we define
f\ \square\ g\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty[
by
(f\ \square\ g)(x):=\inf\nolimits_{y\in X}\big[f(y)+g(x-y)\big]
.
f\ \square\ g
is the
inf-convolution
of
f
and
g
. We say that this inf-convolution is
exact
is the infimum is attained. In this case we write
f\ \square_{E}\ g
. So
(f\ \square_{E}\ g)(x)=\min\nolimits_{y\in X}\big[f(y)+g(x-y)\big]
. This concept will be used in Theorem
7.7
, Corollary
7.8
(b) and Theorem
9.9
and its consequences.

We write
{\cal PC}(X)
for the set of all proper convex functions from
X
into
\,]{-}\infty,\infty]
and
{\cal PCLSC}(X)
for the set of all proper convex lower semicontinuous functions from
X
into
\,]{-}\infty,\infty]
. We write
X^{*}
for the dual space of
X

(
with the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon X\times X^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
)
. If
f\in{\cal PC}(X)
then, as usual, we define the
Fenchel conjugate
,
f^{*}
, of
f
to be the function on
X^{*}
given by

x^{*}\mapsto\sup\nolimits_{X}\big[x^{*}-f\big]\qquad(x^{*}\in X^{*}).

(2.1)

We write
X^{**}
for the bidual of
X

(
with the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon X^{*}\times X^{**}\to\mathbb{R}
)
.If
f\in{\cal PC}(X)
and
f^{*}\in{\cal PCLSC}(X^{*})
, we define
f^{**}\colon X^{**}\to\,]{-}\infty,\infty]
by
f^{**}(x^{**}):=\sup_{X^{*}}\big[x^{**}-f^{*}\big]
. If
x\in X
, we write
\widehat{x}
for the canonical image of
x
in
X^{**}
, that is to say
(x,x^{*})\in X\times X^{*}\Longrightarrow\big\langle x^{*},\widehat{x}\big\rangle=\langle x,x^{*}\rangle
. If
Y\subset X
and
x\in X
, then
\mathbb{I}_{Y}(x):=0
if

and
\mathbb{I}_{Y}(x):=\infty
if

.
\mathbb{I}_{Y}
is the

.

The basic result about

is the

:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\in{\cal PCLSC}(X)
and

. Then
f^{**}(\widehat{x})=f(x)
, that is to say,
f(x)=\sup\nolimits_{x^{*}\in X^{*}}\big[\langle x,x^{*}\rangle-f^{*}(x^{*})\big]
.

Proof.

See Moreau,
[
8
, Section 5–6, pp. 26–39]
or Zălinescu,
[
20
, Theorem 2.3.3 pp. 77–78]
. This result is extended in
[
14
, Section 12, pp. 58–60]
.

3
q
,
L
and
r

We define the norm on
B=E\times E^{*}
by
\|(x,x^{*})\|:=\sqrt{\|x\|^{2}+\|x^{*}\|^{2}}
,and represent
B^{*}
by
E^{*}\times E^{**}
under the pairing

\big\langle(x,x^{*}),(z^{*},z^{**})\big\rangle:=\langle x,z^{*}\rangle+\langle x^{*},z^{**}\rangle.

Our discussions of monotonicity lead us to expressions of the form
\langle x-y,x^{*}-y^{*}\rangle
. We now introduce notations that will simplify this and similar expressions. We have the identity
\langle x-y,x^{*}-y^{*}\rangle=\langle x,x^{*}\rangle-\langle x,y^{*}\rangle-\langle y,x^{*}\rangle+\langle y,y^{*}\rangle
. We define the quadratic function
q
on
B
by
q(x,x^{*}):=\langle x,x^{*}\rangle
. Thus, if
b=(x,x^{*})
and
c=(y,y^{*})
, we have
q(b-c)=q(b)-\langle x,y^{*}\rangle-\langle y,x^{*}\rangle+q(c)
. We now deal with the middle two terms of this. Since
\langle x,y^{*}\rangle+\langle y,x^{*}\rangle=\big\langle(x,x^{*}),(y^{*},\widehat{y})\big\rangle
, we define
the linear isometry
L\colon\ B\to B^{*}
by
L(y,y^{*}):=(y^{*},\widehat{y})
, and obtain the relationship
q(b-c)=q(b)-\langle b,Lc\rangle+q(c)
. It is sometimes useful to multiply
b
and
c
by scalars, and we end up with: for all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
d,e\in B
,

q(\lambda d-\mu e)=\lambda^{2}q(d)-\lambda\mu\langle d,Le\rangle+\mu^{2}q(e).

(3.1)

A word is in order about the map
L
. If
E
is any Banach space then
\widehat{}
is the
canonical map
from
E
into
E^{**}
. If
B
is a Banach space of the special form
E\times E^{*}
then we can think of
L
as the
canonical map
from
B
into
B^{*}
.

The following
weighted parallelogram law
is an immediate consequence of this: if
\lambda+\mu=1
and
d,e\in B
then

q(\lambda d+\mu e)+\lambda\mu q(d-e)=\lambda q(d)+\mu q(e).

(3.2)

If we take
\lambda=\mu={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}
and multiply by 4, we obtain the
usual parallelogram law
:

q(d+e)+q(d-e)=2q(d)+2q(e).

(3.3)

We define the quadratic function
r
on
B
by

r:=q+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|\cdot\|^{2}.

(3.4)

The function
r
appears in the reflexive case in
[
13
, Theorem 10.3, p. 36]
and in Simons–Zălinescu
[
18
]
, with the symbol “
G
”. It was used in the nonreflexive case by Zagrodny in
[
19
]
. Since
\|L\|\leq 1
, for all
b\in B
,
|q(b)|={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}|\langle b,Lb\rangle|\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|b\|\|Lb\|\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|b\|^{2}
, consequently

0\leq r\leq\|\cdot\|^{2}\ \hbox{on}\ B.

(3.5)

Now let
d,e\in B
. Since
\|L\|\leq 1
, from (
3.1
) with
\lambda=1
and
\mu=-1

q(d+e)=q(d)+\langle d,Le\rangle+q(e)\leq q(d)+\|d\|\|e\|+q(e).

(3.6)

Since
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|d+e\|^{2}\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(\|d\|+\|e\|)^{2}\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|d\|^{2}+\|d\|\|e\|+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|e\|^{2}
, from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
),

r(d+e)\leq r(d)+2\|d\|\|e\|+r(e)\leq\|d\|^{2}+2\|d\|\|e\|+r(e).

(3.7)

It is easy to see that, for all
b,c\in B
,
\langle b,Lc\rangle=\langle c,Lb\rangle
and, for all
b\in B
,
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\langle b,Lb\rangle=q(b)
. It follows that, for all
b,c\in B
,

|q(b)-q(c)|={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}|\langle b,Lb\rangle-\langle c,Lc\rangle|={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}|\langle b-c,L(b+c)\rangle|\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|b+c\|\|b-c\|.

Consequently,

q\hbox{ is continuous}.

(3.8)

4
Monotone, Quasidense and CMQd sets

Let
A\subset B
. Obviously
A
is
monotone
in the usual sense exactly when, for all
d,e\in A
,
q(d-e)\geq 0
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A\subset B
. We say that
A
is
quasidense in
B
if,

\hbox{for all}\ b\in B,\quad\inf\nolimits r(A-b)=0.

(4.1)

Equivalently, for all
(x,x^{*})\in B
,

\inf\nolimits_{(s,s^{*})\in A}\big[{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|s-x\|^{2}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|s^{*}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\langle s-x,s^{*}-x^{*}\rangle\big]=0.

(4.2)

We shall write
“CMQd”
for “closed, monotone and quasidense”.

Theorem 4.2

(Quasidensity and maximality)
.

Let
A
be a CMQd subset of
B
. Then
A
is maximally monotone.

Proof.

Let
b\in B
and
A\cup\{b\}
be monotone. Let
\varepsilon>0
. By hypothesis, there exists
a\in A
such that
q(a-b)+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|a-b\|^{2}<\varepsilon
. Since
A\cup\{b\}
is monotone,
q(a-b)\geq 0
, and so
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|a-b\|^{2}<\varepsilon
. However,
A
is closed. Thus, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
,
b\in A
. This completes the proof of the maximality.
(
This proof is adapted from that of
[
16
, Lemma 4.7, p. 1027]
— the result appears explicitly in
[
16
, Theorem 7.4(a), pp. 1032–1033]
.
)

5
The function

We suppose for the rest of this paper that

is a maximally monotone subset of

. The main result of this section is Theorem

, in which we give a sufficient condition for

to be quasidense.

Let

(5.1)

It is clear from the continuity of

(

)

P_{M}\hbox{ is lower semicontinuous\kern 5.0ptand\kern 5.0pt}M={P_{M}}^{-1}\{0\}={P_{M}}^{-1}\big(\,]-\infty,0\,]\big).

(5.2)

We collect together in Lemma

the elementary properties of the function

Lemma 5.1
.

(a)
We have

.

(b)

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|m-b\|^{2}\leq r(m-b)+P_{M}(b)

. On the other hand, from (

(b) By virtue of (

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|m-b\|^{2}=r(m-b)-q(m-b)
and, by virtue of (

),

. This completes the proof of (b).

(c) From (

), and the parallelogram law, (

), with

and

,

\displaystyle-q(b-a)

\displaystyle\leq 4P_{M}({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}a+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}b)-q(b-a)=-4\inf\nolimits_{m\in M}q(m-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}a-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}b)-q(b-a)

\displaystyle=-\inf\nolimits_{m\in M}q(2m-a-b)-q(b-a)

\displaystyle=-\inf\nolimits_{m\in M}\big[q(2m-a-b)+q(b-a)\big]

\displaystyle=-\inf\nolimits_{m\in M}\big[2q(m-a)+2q(m-b)\big]

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that

P_{M}\ \square\ r\leq 0\ on\ B.

(5.3)

(a)
Let
c\in B
. Then
\inf\|M-c\|^{2}\leq 4P_{M}(c)
.

(b)
The set
M
is CMQd.

Proof.

(a) We can clearly suppose that
P_{M}(c)<\infty
. Let
P_{M}(c)<\Lambda<\infty
. Let
\eta\in\ ]0,1[
be chosen so small that

4\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\eta^{n}+2\sqrt{P_{M}(c)}<2\sqrt{\Lambda}.

If

then, from (
5.3
),
(P_{M}\ \square\ r)(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k})\leq 0
, and so, for all
n\geq 1
, there exists
b_{n}\in B
such that
P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k})+r(-b_{n})<\eta^{2n+2}
. Since
P_{M},r\geq 0
on
B
and
r
is even, we can choose
b_{1},b_{2}\dots\in B
inductively so that, for all
n\geq 1
,

P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k})\vee r(b_{n})=P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k})\vee r(-b_{n})<\eta^{2n+2}.

(5.4)

From (
3.4
), (
5.4
) and Lemma
5.1
(c) with
a=c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k}
and
b=c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}
, for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|b_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=r(b_{n})-q(b_{n})\leq\eta^{2n+2}+2P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k})+2P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{2n+2}+2P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k})+2\eta^{2n+2}=3\eta^{2n+2}+2P_{M}(c+\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k}).

When
n=1
, this gives us
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|b_{1}\|^{2}\leq 3\eta^{4}+2P_{M}(c)
. Multiplying this by 2,

\|b_{1}\|^{2}\leq 6\eta^{4}+4P_{M}(c)\leq 16\eta^{2}+4P_{M}(c)
and so, in this case,

\|b_{1}\|\leq 4\eta+2\sqrt{P_{M}(c)}.

(5.5)

On the other hand, when

Theorem 13.3
.

The following three conditions are equivalent:

(b)

, let

. Then Lemma
13.1
gives a net in

with the required property. If (b) is true then it is obvious that (c) is true.

Suppose, finally, that (a) is false. From Theorem
11.1
(
(b)
\Longrightarrow
(a)
)
, thereexists

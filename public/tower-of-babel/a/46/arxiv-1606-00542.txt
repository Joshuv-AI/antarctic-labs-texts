Homomorphisms from Specht Modules to Signed Young Permutation Modules This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on the Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups and Related Topics. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/symmetric-groups-2018.html
Kay Jin LIM † and Kai Meng TAN ‡

Abstract
We construct a class Θ ℛ {\Uptheta}_{\mathscr{R}} of homomorphisms from a Specht module S ℤ λ S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda} to a signed permutation module M ℤ ​ ( α | β ) M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta) which generalises James’s construction of homomorphisms whose codomain is a Young permutation module. We show that any ϕ ∈ Hom ℤ ​ 𝔖 n ⁡ ( S ℤ λ , M ℤ ​ ( α | β ) ) \phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)\big) lies in the ℚ \mathbb{Q} -span of Θ sstd {\Uptheta}_{\text{sstd}} , a subset of Θ ℛ {\Uptheta}_{\mathscr{R}} corresponding to semistandard λ \lambda -tableaux of type ( α | β ) (\alpha|\beta) . We also study the conditions for which Θ sstd 𝔽 {\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathrm{sstd}} – a subset of Hom 𝔽 ​ 𝔖 n ⁡ ( S 𝔽 λ , M 𝔽 ​ ( α | β ) ) \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big) induced by Θ sstd {\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}} – is linearly independent, and show that it is a basis for Hom 𝔽 ​ 𝔖 n ⁡ ( S 𝔽 λ , M 𝔽 ​ ( α | β ) ) \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big) when 𝔽 ​ 𝔖 n \mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n} is semisimple.

1
Introduction

Modular representation theory of finite groups, unlike its ordinary counterpart, is not at all well understood. Even for ubiquitous groups like the symmetric groups, many fundamental questions remain open. It is therefore important to understand the naturally occurring representations, as these may provide more information about the modular representation theory in general.

Among the naturally occurring representations of the symmetric groups, the Young permutation modules are perhaps the most well-known. James
[
7
]
studied these modules in detail and exploited their knowledge to obtain important information such as those about the Specht modules.

When the characteristic of the underlying field is not
2
, the signed permutation modules are a natural generalisation of the Young permutation modules. Their indecomposable summands, known as signed Young modules, are first studied by Donkin in
[
3
]
. Subsequently, signed Young modules are shown to be related to irreducible Specht modules. More specifically, Hemmer
[
6
]
showed that irreducible Specht modules are signed Young modules and, recently, Danz and the first author
[
2
]
described the label explicitly. Signed permutation modules for Iwahori–Hecke algebras of type
A
are also studied by Du and Rui in
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we construct a class
{\Uptheta}_{\mathscr{R}}
of homomorphisms from the Specht module
S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}
to the signed permutation module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)
, just like James did for the (unsigned) Young permutation module
M^{\mu}_{\mathbb{F}}
. A subset of this class, denoted
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
, is a direct generalisation of James’s semistandard homomorphisms. James’s semistandard homomorphisms form a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}\big)
, unless
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=2
and
\lambda
is
2
-singular. It is therefore natural to seek generalisation of this statement for our
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
. We show that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
is contained in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
, and provide a sufficient condition for
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
to be linearly independent when reduced modulo
p
. In particular, we prove that when
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is semisimple, with
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=p
, then reducing
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
modulo
p
does indeed give a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
.

We found many examples in our study which show that the signed permutation modules behave in a much more unpredictable way than the unsigned ones. We include some of these in Examples
3.1
,
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
.

The paper is organised as follows. In the next section, we give a quick introduction of the background.
In Section
3
, we generalise James’s construction to obtain homomorphisms from the Specht module
S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}
to the signed permutation module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)
, and in Section
4
, we look into the conditions for which a subset of our constructed homomorphisms, corresponding to the semistandard
\lambda
-tableaux of type
(\alpha|\beta)
, will be a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide the necessary background and introduce some notations that we shall use in this paper.

Throughout, we fix a field
\mathbb{F}
of arbitrary characteristic.

2.1
Symmetric groups

Let
X
be a finite set. The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{X}
on
X
is the group of bijections from
X
to
X
under composition of functions. By convention,
\mathfrak{S}_{\varnothing}
is the trivial group. When
Y
is a non-empty subset of
X
, we view
\mathfrak{S}_{Y}
as a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{X}
by identifying an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{Y}
with its extension that sends
x
to
x
for all
x\in X\setminus Y
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Define the
k
-translated subset
{X}^{+k}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
by

\displaystyle{X}^{+k}=\{x+k\colon x\in X\}.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{X}^{+k}=\big\{{f}^{+k}\colon f\in\mathfrak{S}_{X}\big\}
where
{f}^{+k}\colon{X}^{+k}\to{X}^{+k}
is defined by

\displaystyle{f}^{+k}(x+k)=f(x)+k

for all
x\in X
. Clearly,
f\mapsto{f}^{+k}
is a group isomorphism from
\mathfrak{S}_{X}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{X}^{+k}
. For any
S\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{X}
, write
{S}^{+k}
for
\{{s}^{+k}\colon s\in S\}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we write
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for
\mathfrak{S}_{\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}}
, the usual symmetric group on
n
letters.

2.2
Compositions and Young subgroups

A composition is a finite sequence of positive integers. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})
be a composition. Then
\ell(\lambda)=r
. If
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}=n
, we say that
\lambda
is a composition of
n
, and write
|\lambda|=n
. If in addition
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}
, then we call
\lambda
a partition of
n
. The unique composition (or partition) of 0 is denoted by
\varnothing
, and
\ell(\varnothing)=0
.

To a composition
\lambda
of
n
, we associate the Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{1}}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{2}}^{+s(\lambda)_{1}}\dotsm\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}}^{+s(\lambda)_{\ell(\lambda)-1}}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{2}}\times\dotsb\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}}

and
s(\lambda)_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\lambda_{i}
for all
1\leq j\leq\ell(\lambda)-1
.

Let
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
, i.e.,
\alpha
and
\beta
are compositions and
|\alpha|+|\beta|=n
. One may view
(\alpha|\beta)
as a composition of
n
by concatenating
\alpha
with
\beta
, and so we have the associated Young subgroup

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}=\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}.

2.3
Young diagrams

For a partition
\lambda
, its Young diagram
[\lambda]
is defined as

\displaystyle[\lambda]=\big\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\colon 1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda),\ 1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\big\}.

The conjugate partition of
\lambda
, denoted
\lambda^{\prime}
, is the partition whose Young diagram
[\lambda^{\prime}]=\{(i,j)\colon(j,i)\in[\lambda]\}
.

2.4
Tableaux

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
. Define formally a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}
as a bijective function
\mathfrak{t}\colon[\lambda]\to\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}
. We usually view this as a labelling of the elements of
[\lambda]
by numbers
1,2,\dotsc,n
, such that each number appears exactly once. Denote the set of
\lambda
-tableaux by
\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
.

Through a fixed
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}
we obtain a group isomorphism
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{[\lambda]}
where we identify
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
\mathfrak{t}^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{S}_{[\lambda]}
. Let
R_{[\lambda]}
and
C_{[\lambda]}
be the row and column stabilizers of
[\lambda]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle R_{[\lambda]}=\{f\in\mathfrak{S}_{[\lambda]}\colon\forall\,(i,j)\in[\lambda],\,\exists\,(i,j^{\prime})\in[\lambda],\,f(i,j)=(i,j^{\prime})\},

\displaystyle C_{[\lambda]}=\{f\in\mathfrak{S}_{[\lambda]}\colon\forall\,(i,j)\in[\lambda],\,\exists\,(i^{\prime},j)\in[\lambda],\,f(i,j)=(i^{\prime},j)\}.

Under the above group isomorphism,
R_{[\lambda]}
corresponds to the row stabilizer
R_{\mathfrak{t}}
of
\mathfrak{t}
, i.e.,
R_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t}\circ R_{[\lambda]}\circ\mathfrak{t}^{-1}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and similarly,
C_{[\lambda]}
corresponds to the column stabilizer
C_{\mathfrak{t}}
of
\mathfrak{t}
, i.e.,
C_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t}\circ C_{[\lambda]}\circ\mathfrak{t}^{-1}
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}
denote the
initial
\lambda
-tableau
, defined by
\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}(i,j)=\lambda_{1}+\dotsb+\lambda_{i-1}+j
for all
(i,j)\in[\lambda]
. Observe that
R_{\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}}=\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}
.

Post-composition of
\lambda
-tableaux by elements of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
gives a well-defined, faithful and transitive left action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
, i.e.,
\sigma\cdot\mathfrak{t}=\sigma\circ\mathfrak{t}
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
. Observe that

\displaystyle R_{\sigma\cdot\mathfrak{t}}=R_{\sigma\circ\mathfrak{t}}=(\sigma\circ\mathfrak{t})\circ R_{[\lambda]}\circ(\sigma\circ\mathfrak{t})^{-1}=\sigma\circ R_{\mathfrak{t}}\circ\sigma^{-1}=\sigma R_{\mathfrak{t}}\sigma^{-1},

and similarly,
C_{\sigma\cdot\mathfrak{t}}=\sigma C_{\mathfrak{t}}\sigma^{-1}
. In particular, the row stabilizers of the
\lambda
-tableaux are conjugate subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}
.

A
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}
is
standard
if it is increasing along each row and down each column, i.e., we have both
\mathfrak{t}(i,j)<\mathfrak{t}(i,j^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{t}(i,j)<\mathfrak{t}(i^{\prime},j)
for all
(i,j),(i,j^{\prime}),(i^{\prime},j)\in[\lambda]
with
j<j^{\prime}
and
i<i^{\prime}
. Write
\mathscr{T}_{\rm{std}}(\lambda)
for the set of all standard
\lambda
-tableaux.

Let
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
and
\lambda
be a partition of
n
. We view a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathrm{T}
of type
(\alpha|\beta)
as a colouring of the nodes of
[\lambda]
by the colours
\mathbbm{{c}}_{1},\mathbbm{{c}}_{2},\dotsc,\mathbbm{{c}}_{\ell(\alpha)},\mathbbm{{d}}_{1},\mathbbm{{d}}_{2},\dotsc,\mathbbm{{d}}_{\ell(\beta)}
such that there are exactly
\alpha_{i}
nodes of colour
\mathbbm{{c}}_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
nodes of colour
\mathbbm{{d}}_{j}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell(\alpha)
and
1\leq j\leq\ell(\beta)
. Formally,
\mathrm{T}
is a function

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}\colon\ [\lambda]\to\{\mathbbm{{c}}_{1},\mathbbm{{c}}_{2},\dotsc,\mathbbm{{c}}_{\ell(\alpha)},\mathbbm{{d}}_{1},\mathbbm{{d}}_{2},\dotsc,\mathbbm{{d}}_{\ell(\beta)}\}

such that
|\mathrm{T}^{-1}(\{\mathbbm{{c}}_{i}\})|=\alpha_{i}
and
|\mathrm{T}^{-1}(\{\mathbbm{{d}}_{j}\})|=\beta_{j}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell(\alpha)
and
1\leq j\leq\ell(\beta)
. We write
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
for the set of all
\lambda
-tableaux of type
(\alpha|\beta)
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}_{0}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
be a fixed
\lambda
-tableau (which may or may not be the initial
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}
defined above). We define the canonical
\lambda
-tableau of type
(\alpha|\beta)
associated to
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, denoted as
\mathrm{T}_{0}
, as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{0}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}&\text{if $\displaystyle{\sum^{k-1}_{a=1}\alpha_{a}<\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j)\leq\sum^{k}_{a=1}\alpha_{a}}$ for some $k$,}\\
\mathbbm{{d}}_{k}&\text{if $\displaystyle{|\alpha|+\sum^{k-1}_{b=1}\beta_{b}<\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j)\leq|\alpha|+\sum^{k}_{b=1}\beta_{b}}$ for some $k$,}\end{cases}

namely, the nodes labelled
1,\ldots,\alpha_{1}
by
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
are coloured
\mathbbm{{c}}_{1}
, those labelled
\alpha_{1}+1,\dotsc,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
are coloured
\mathbbm{{c}}_{2}
, and so on.

We also have a transitive left action on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
through
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
as follows:
\sigma\cdot\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{T}\circ(\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}\circ\sigma^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{t}_{0}
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
. Thus, if
(i,j)\in[\lambda]
is labelled
a
by
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, while the node labelled
\sigma^{-1}(a)
by
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
is coloured
\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}
(respectively,
\mathbbm{{d}}_{k}
) by
\mathrm{T}
, then
\sigma\cdot\mathrm{T}
colours
(i,j)
with
\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}
(respectively,
\mathbbm{{d}}_{k}
). Observe that under this action, the stabiliser of
\mathrm{T}_{0}
is
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
. Consequently,
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
is in a bijective correspondence with the set of left cosets of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
via
d\cdot\mathrm{T}_{0}\leftrightarrow d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
. We set

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{d}=d\cdot\mathrm{T}_{0}

so that
\mathrm{T}_{d}=\mathrm{T}_{d^{\prime}}
if and only if
d^{-1}d^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
.

We order the colours
\mathbbm{{c}}_{1},\mathbbm{{c}}_{2},\dotsc,\mathbbm{{d}}_{1},\mathbbm{{d}}_{2},\dotsc
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbbm{{c}}_{1}<\mathbbm{{c}}_{2}<\cdots<\mathbbm{{d}}_{1}<\mathbbm{{d}}_{2}<\cdots.

A
\lambda
-tableau
\mathrm{T}
of type
(\alpha|\beta)
is
row semistandard
(respectively,
column semistandard
) if every row (respectively, column) of
\mathrm{T}
is weakly increasing. More precisely,
\mathrm{T}
is row semistandard if
\mathrm{T}(i,j)\leq\mathrm{T}(i,j^{\prime})
for all
(i,j),(i,j^{\prime})\in[\lambda]
with
j<j^{\prime}
, and
\mathrm{T}
is column semistandard if
\mathrm{T}(i,j)\leq\mathrm{T}(i^{\prime},j)
for all
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j)\in[\lambda]
with
i<i^{\prime}
. Following
[
14
, Section 1.2]
(see also
[
4
, Section 4]
), we say that
a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathrm{T}
of type
(\alpha|\beta)
is
semistandard
if and only if

(i)

\mathrm{T}
is both row and column semistandard;

(ii)

\mathrm{T}(i,j)=\mathrm{T}(i,j^{\prime})
for some
(i,j),(i,j^{\prime})\in[\lambda]
with
j\neq j^{\prime}
only if
\mathrm{T}(i,j)=\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}
for some
k
;

(iii)

\mathrm{T}(i,j)=\mathrm{T}(i^{\prime},j)
for some
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j)\in[\lambda]
with
i\neq i^{\prime}
only if
\mathrm{T}(i,j)=\mathbbm{{d}}_{k}
for some
k
.

We denote the set of semistandard
\lambda
-tableaux of type
(\alpha|\beta)
by
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
.

2.5
One-dimensional representations of symmetric groups

For the remainder of this section, let
\mathcal{O}
be either
\mathbb{F}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. The signature representation of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
over
\mathcal{O}
is denoted by
\mathrm{sgn}\colon\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\{\pm 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
. We shall abuse notation and also write
\mathrm{sgn}
for the
\mathcal{O}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module associated to it. In this latter context,
\mathrm{sgn}
is
\mathcal{O}
-free of rank
1
, with basis
\{\epsilon\}
. We denote the trivial
\mathcal{O}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module that is
\mathcal{O}
-free of rank
1
as
\mathcal{O}
, with basis
\{\mathbbm{{1}}\}
. Thus,
\sigma\cdot\mathbbm{{1}}=\mathbbm{{1}}
and
\sigma\cdot\epsilon=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\epsilon
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

When
H
is a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we write the respective restricted
\mathcal{O}H
-modules as
\mathcal{O}_{H}
and
\mathrm{sgn}_{H}
. Sometimes, we shall abuse notation and write them as
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathrm{sgn}
when there is no confusion.

2.6
Young permutation modules and Specht modules

Let
\mu
be a composition of
n
. The Young permutation module
M^{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}}
is the permutation module associated to the left regular action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on the left cosets of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. In other words,
M^{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}}=\bigoplus_{d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}}\mathcal{O}(d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu})
, and
\sigma\cdot(d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu})=(\sigma d)\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
.

When
\tilde{\mu}
is a composition of
n
obtained by rearranging some parts of
\mu
, the Young subgroups
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{\tilde{\mu}}
are conjugate in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, so that
M^{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}}\cong M^{\tilde{\mu}}_{\mathcal{O}}
as
\mathcal{O}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. Given a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}
, let
d_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t}\circ\big(\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}\big)^{-1}
. Then
d_{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
d_{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}=d_{\mathfrak{t}}\circ\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}=\mathfrak{t}
. We define the polytabloid

\displaystyle e_{\mathfrak{t}}:=\sum_{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)(\sigma\cdot(d_{\mathfrak{t}}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}))\in M^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}.

The Specht module
S^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}
is defined to be the
\mathcal{O}
-submodule of
M^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}
spanned by
\{e_{\mathfrak{t}}\colon\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda)\}
. It is not difficult to see that
\tau\cdot e_{\mathfrak{t}}=e_{\tau\cdot\mathfrak{t}}
for
\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
, so that
S^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}
is an
\mathcal{O}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-submodule of
M^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Let
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{F}
and
M^{*}
denote the contragradient dual of an
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module
M
. The following isomorphism is well known (see
[
7
, Theorem 8.15]
):

\displaystyle S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}}\otimes\mathrm{sgn}\cong\big(S^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{F}}\big)^{*}.

Furthermore,
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is semisimple as an algebra if and only if either
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=0
or
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})>n
, in which case, the Specht modules
S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}}
, as
\lambda
runs over all the partitions of
n
, give a complete list of pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules.

2.7
Signed permutation modules

Let
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
. We define the signed permutation module
M_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha|\beta)
to be

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha|\beta)=\operatorname{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(\mathcal{O}\boxtimes\mathrm{sgn}),

where
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
is identified with the Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Thus, when
\Gamma
is a left transversal of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
M_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha|\beta)
has a basis
\{d\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon\colon d\in\Gamma\}
, and
\sigma\cdot(d\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon)=\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{\beta})(d^{\prime}\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon)
if
\sigma d=d^{\prime}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}
where
d^{\prime}\in\Gamma
,
(\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
.

Observe that if
\tilde{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\beta}
are compositions obtained by rearranging some parts of
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively, then
M_{\mathcal{O}}(\tilde{\alpha}|\tilde{\beta})\cong M_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha|\beta)
. Also,

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha|\beta)\otimes\mathrm{sgn}\cong\operatorname{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}((\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathrm{sgn})\boxtimes(\mathrm{sgn}\otimes\mathrm{sgn}))\cong M_{\mathcal{O}}(\beta|\alpha).

The Young permutation module
M^{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}}
, where
\mu
is a composition of
n
, in the previous subsection is isomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathcal{O}
. Thus,
M^{\mu}_{\mathcal{O}}\cong M_{\mathcal{O}}(\mu|\varnothing)
. As such, signed permutation modules generalise Young permutation modules.

Theorem 2.2

(signed Young’s rule)
.

Let
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
. The signed permutation module
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
has a Specht filtration in which, for a partition
\lambda
of
n
, the multiplicity of
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
as a factor of the filtration equals
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|
.

Dually, the signed permutation module
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
has a dual Specht filtration in which, for a partition
\lambda
of
n
, the multiplicity of
S_{\lambda,\mathbb{F}}\cong\big(S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}}\big)^{*}
as a factor of the filtration equals
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|
.

Proof.

This is essentially proved by Du and Rui in
[
4
]
; we review their proof here. In the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 5.2]
, they showed that there is a Specht filtration for the signed
q
-permutation module of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type
A
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
(where
v
is an indeterminate and
q=v^{2}
) with the correct multiplicity for each Specht factor. As such, under specialization to any field
\mathbb{F}
, we get a Specht filtration for the signed
q
-permutation module with the correct multiplicity for each Specht factor. Since the symmetric group algebra is an Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type
A
with
v=1=q
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In a private communication with the authors, Andrew Mathas constructed explicitly a Specht filtration and a dual Specht filtration of the signed permutation module, with the correct multiplicity for each Specht factor, for the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type
A
.

We record some immediate corollaries to the signed Young’s rule.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
.

(i)

[\mathfrak{S}_{n}\colon\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}]=\sum_{\lambda}|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)||\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|
, where the sum runs over all partitions
\lambda
of
n
.

(ii)

For any partition
\lambda
of
n
,
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|=|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda^{\prime},(\beta|\alpha))|
.

(iii)

Let
\tilde{\alpha}
,
\tilde{\beta}
be compositions obtained by rearranging some parts of
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively. For any partition
\lambda
of
n
,
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\tilde{\alpha}|\tilde{\beta}))|=|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|
.

(iv)

For any partition
\lambda
of
m+n
,
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha\mathbin{{\text{\footnotesize$\sqcup$}}}(1^{m})|\beta))|=|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta\mathbin{{\text{\footnotesize$\sqcup$}}}(1^{m})))|
where
\mathbin{{\text{\footnotesize$\sqcup$}}}
denotes the concatenation of compositions.

Proof.

(i) It is well known that the Specht module
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
has dimension
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)|
(see, for example,
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
). Taking the dimension of
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
and applying Theorem
2.2
thus yield part (i).

(ii) When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
, the Specht modules are the irreducible modules, so that the Specht filtration in Theorem
2.2
is in fact a composition series. Thus

\displaystyle|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|=\big[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta):S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\lambda}\big]=\big[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta)\otimes\mathrm{sgn}:S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{sgn}\big]

\displaystyle\hphantom{|\mathscr{T}_\mathrm{sstd}(\lambda, (\alpha|\beta))|= \big[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta):S_{\mathbb{Q}}^\lambda\big]}{}=\big[M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta|\alpha):S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\big]=|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda^{\prime},(\beta|\alpha))|;

here,
[M:S]
denotes the composition multiplicity of an irreducible module
S
in
M
.

(iii, iv) These are proved in a manner similar to but easier than part (ii), using the isomorphisms
M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta)\cong M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\tilde{\alpha}|\tilde{\beta})
and
M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha\mathbin{{\text{\footnotesize$\sqcup$}}}(1^{m})|\beta)\cong M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta\mathbin{{\text{\footnotesize$\sqcup$}}}(1^{m}))
respectively.
∎

3
Homomorphisms

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and
\mu
be a composition of
n
. In
[
7
, Section 13]
, James constructed for each
\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda,\mu)
an
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}\colon M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}\to M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}
, and showed that
\{\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}:=\theta_{\mathrm{T}}|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}}\colon\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,\mu)\}
is a basis for the space
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}\big)
, unless
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=2
and
\lambda
is
2
-singular. In particular, this shows that every homomorphism in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}\big)
is the restriction of a homomorphism in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}\big)
when
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
.

In this section, we shall generalise these homomorphisms to obtain homomorphisms between Specht modules and signed permutation modules. The next example shows that not every homomorphism in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
is the restriction of a homomorphism in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
, illustrating the difficulty of such generalisation.

Example 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and let
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=p
with
0<p\leq n
. Let
\lambda=(1^{n})
and
(\alpha|\beta)=(\varnothing|(n))
. Then
M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
is the regular
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, while both
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)=M_{\mathbb{F}}(\varnothing|(n))
and
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
are isomorphic to the signature representation
\mathrm{sgn}
of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Thus
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
has dimension one.

On the other hand,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n},\mathrm{sgn})
has dimension
1
with a basis
\{\theta\colon\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathrm{sgn}\}
, where
\theta(1_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}})=\epsilon
. For any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\theta(\sigma)=\sigma\cdot\theta(1_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}})=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\epsilon.

As a submodule of
M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}=\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the Specht module
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
(
\cong\mathrm{sgn}
) is generated by
\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma
. We have

\displaystyle\theta\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sigma\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\theta(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\epsilon=(n!)\epsilon=0,

since
p\leq n
. Thus
\theta|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}}=0
.

This example shows that the map
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
defined by
\theta\mapsto\theta|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}}
is not surjective in general.

As such, to generalise the homomorphisms constructed by James for signed permutation modules, we should not attempt to generalise
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}
and take its restriction to
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
, but have to generalise
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}
directly instead. In other words, we need to understand
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}(e_{\mathfrak{t}})
.

3.1
James’s construction

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and
\mu
be a composition of
n
. Let
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}\colon M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}\to M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}
be the
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism defined in
[
7
, Section 13]
. In this subsection, we study how
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}
acts on the polytabloids in
M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
.

Fix a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, so that
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,\mu)
via
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, as described in Section
2.4
.
Let
\mathrm{T}_{0}
be the canonical
\lambda
-tableau of type
\mu
associated to
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
; recall that
\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(\mathrm{T}_{0})=\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
.
Then each left coset of
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
corresponds to a
\lambda
-tableau of type
\mu
; let
d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
be the left coset corresponding to
\mathrm{T}
.
Recall also the initial
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda}
, and let
d_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}=\mathfrak{t}_{0}\circ(\mathfrak{t}^{\lambda})^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
James defined
\theta_{\mathrm{T}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}\big)
so that

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathrm{T}}(d_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}:\\
d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\subseteq R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\end{subarray}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}.

Let
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
, and let
\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t}\circ(\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}
. Then
\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot\mathfrak{t}_{0}=\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\circ\mathfrak{t}_{0}=\mathfrak{t}
, so that
e_{\mathfrak{t}}=e_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot\mathfrak{t}_{0}}=\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot e_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
. Thus

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathrm{T}}(e_{\mathfrak{t}})

\displaystyle=\theta_{\mathrm{T}}(\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot e_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}})=\theta_{\mathrm{T}}\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)(\sigma\cdot(d_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)(\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\sigma\cdot\theta_{\mathrm{T}}(d_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}))=\sum_{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\sigma\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}:\\
d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\subseteq R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\end{subarray}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}:\\
d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\subseteq\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}\sigma R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\end{subarray}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}=\sum_{d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}}a_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,d_{\mathrm{T}}}\,d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu},

where

\displaystyle a_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,d_{\mathrm{T}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}:\\
\sigma^{-1}\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}:\\
\sigma\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma).

We summarise this below.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and
\mu
be a composition of
n
, and
let
\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda,\mu)
.
The
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}\colon S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}\to M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}
constructed by James satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}(e_{\mathfrak{t}})=\sum_{d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}}a_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,d_{\mathrm{T}}}\,d\mathfrak{S}_{\mu},

where

\displaystyle a_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,d_{\mathrm{T}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}:\\
\sigma\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d_{\mathrm{T}}\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma).

3.2
Generalization of James’s construction

Fix a partition
\lambda
of
n
and a bicomposition
(\alpha|\beta)
of
n
. In this subsection, we generalise James’s construction of homomorphisms between Specht modules and Young permutation modules to obtain, for each
d
in a subset
\mathscr{R}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
to be defined below (see Definition
3.4
), a
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}\colon S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)
(see Theorem
3.3
).

As before, we fix a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, so that
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
through
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, and denote the canonical
\lambda
-tableau of type
(\alpha|\beta)
associated to
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
by
\mathrm{T}_{0}
. The following is the main theorem of this section:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
,
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
and
\Gamma
be a fixed left transversal of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. For each
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
, we have a
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}\colon S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}\to M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)
given by

\displaystyle\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}(e_{\mathfrak{t}})=\sum_{d\in\Gamma}{\swabfamily a}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,\text{d}}\,(d\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon),

where

\displaystyle{\swabfamily a}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,\text{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}:\\
\sigma\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\varepsilon_{\text{d}}\big(\sigma\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d\big),

and
\varepsilon_{\text{d}}\big(\tau\text{d}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}\big)=\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{\beta})
for
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
(\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
.

By ‘reducing modulo
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})
’ the coefficients
{\swabfamily a}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,\text{d}}
in
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}(e_{\mathfrak{t}})
, we obtain an
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}\colon S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}}\to M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
. Comparing this with
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}
in Theorem
3.2
, one can see that the former is indeed a generalisation of the latter. The appearance of the map
\varepsilon_{\text{d}}
in Theorem
3.3
also explains why our maps are indexed by elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
instead of left cosets of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
(or equivalently,
\lambda
-tableaux of type
(\alpha|\beta)
), since
\varepsilon_{\text{d}}
depends on
d
and not on
\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
. A little thought should convince the reader that to ensure that
\varepsilon_{\text{d}}
is well-defined,
d
may only run over a carefully chosen subset
\mathscr{R}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Definition 3.4
.

Define subsets of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}=\big\{d\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\colon d^{-1}R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\big\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}=\big\{d\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\colon d^{-1}C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}\big\}.

These sets
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{C}
of course depend on
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
and
(\alpha|\beta)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
d\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

(i)

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

d\in\mathscr{R}
;

(b)

\operatorname{stab}_{R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{d})\subseteq d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}d^{-1}
;

(c)

whenever
\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j^{\prime})
for some
(i,j),(i,j^{\prime})\in[\lambda]
with
j\neq j^{\prime}
, we have
\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}
for some
k
.

(ii)

If
d\in\mathscr{R}
, then
\tau d\xi\in\mathscr{R}
for all
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
.

Proof.

For part (i), firstly,

\displaystyle\operatorname{stab}_{R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{d})=R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(d\cdot\mathrm{T}_{0})=R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}d^{-1}.

This proves the equivalence of (a) and (b).

Next, observe that a transposition
(a\;\;b)
lies in
R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
if and only if
a
and
b
label nodes in the same row of
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, i.e.,
(\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}(a)=(i,j)
and
(\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}(b)=(i,j^{\prime})
for some
i
. Furthermore,
(a\;\;b)\cdot\mathrm{T}_{d}=\mathrm{T}_{d}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{d}((\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}(a))=\mathrm{T}_{d}((\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}(b))
.
Since
\operatorname{stab}_{R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{d})=R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}d^{-1}
is an intersection of conjugates of Young subgroups, it is generated by the transpositions it contains. Thus,
\operatorname{stab}_{R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{d})
is generated by

\displaystyle S=\big\{(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j)\;\;\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j^{\prime}))\colon(i,j),(i,j^{\prime})\in[\lambda],\,j\neq j^{\prime},\,\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j^{\prime})\big\}.

Now,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j)\;\;\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j^{\prime}))\in d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}d^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad{}\Leftrightarrow\big(d^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j))\;\;d^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j^{\prime}))\big)\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad{}\Leftrightarrow\exists\,(1\leq k\leq\ell(\alpha)),\ \sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}<d^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j)),\ d^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j^{\prime}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle\quad{}\Leftrightarrow\exists\,(1\leq k\leq\ell(\alpha)),\ \mathrm{T}_{0}\big((\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}\big(d^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j))\big)\big)=\mathrm{T}_{0}\big((\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}\big(d^{-1}(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j^{\prime}))\big)\big)=\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}

\displaystyle\quad{}\Leftrightarrow\exists\,(1\leq k\leq\ell(\alpha)),\ \mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j^{\prime})=\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}.

Hence (b) and (c) are equivalent.

For part (ii), observe that

\displaystyle(\tau d\xi)^{-1}R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}(\tau d\xi)\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}=\xi^{-1}\big(d^{-1}R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\big)\xi\subseteq\xi^{-1}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\xi=\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}.

∎

We have analogous statements and proofs for
\mathscr{C}
too.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
d\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

(i)

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

d\in\mathscr{C}
;

(b)

\operatorname{stab}_{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{d})\subseteq d\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}d^{-1}
;

(c)

whenever
\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathrm{T}_{d}(i^{\prime},j)
for some
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j)\in[\lambda]
with
i\neq i^{\prime}
, we have
\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathbbm{{d}}_{k}
for some
k
.

(ii)

If
d\in\mathscr{C}
, then
\tau d\xi\in\mathscr{C}
for all
\tau\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
.

Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
give the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
d\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that
\mathrm{T}_{d}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
, then
d\in\mathscr{R}\cap\mathscr{C}
.

In order to generalise the coefficient
a_{d,d_{\mathrm{T}}}
in Theorem
3.2
to
{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}
in Definition
3.9
, we need the following lemma which also explains the choice of the set
\mathscr{R}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
. There is a well-defined projection map
\pi_{\text{d}}\colon R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\to\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
defined by
\tau\text{d}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}\mapsto\xi_{\beta}
for all
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
(\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
.

Proof.

If
\tau\text{d}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}=\tau^{\prime}\text{d}\xi^{\prime}_{\alpha}\xi^{\prime+|\alpha|}_{\beta}
, then

\displaystyle\text{d}^{-1}R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\ni\text{d}^{-1}\big(\tau^{-1}\tau^{\prime}\big)\text{d}=\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}\big(\xi^{\prime}_{\alpha}\xi^{\prime+|\alpha|}_{\beta}\big)^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta},

so that
\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}\big(\xi^{\prime}_{\alpha}\xi^{\prime+|\alpha|}_{\beta}\big)^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}
since
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
, forcing
\xi_{\beta}=\xi^{\prime}_{\beta}
. The lemma thus follows.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.8
, we can make the following definition.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
d,\text{d}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
.

(i)

For
\omega\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
, let
\varepsilon_{\text{d}}(\omega):=\mathrm{sgn}(\pi_{\text{d}}(\omega))\in\{\pm 1\}
.

(ii)

Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{d,\text{d}}:=\big\{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\colon\sigma d\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\big\},\qquad{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{d,\text{d}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\varepsilon_{\text{d}}(\sigma d)\in\mathbb{Z}.

By convention, if
\Omega_{d,\text{d}}=\varnothing
then
{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}=0
.

Remark 3.10
.

We give another description of
\Omega_{d,\text{d}}
here. Restrict the left regular action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
to the subgroups
R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
, which partition
\mathfrak{S}_{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
into
R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
-orbits and into
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
-orbits respectively. Then
\sigma\in\Omega_{d,\text{d}}
if and only if
\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
\sigma\cdot(d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta})\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cdot(\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta})
. As such,
\Omega_{d,\text{d}}\neq\varnothing
if and only if
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\cap R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\neq\varnothing
. Furthermore, if
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\cap R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
contains precisely the distinct left cosets
d^{(1)}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta},d^{(2)}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta},\dotsc,d^{(r)}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
, then
\Omega_{d,\text{d}}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}\Omega^{(i)}
(disjoint union), where for each
i
,
\Omega^{(i)}=\{\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\colon\sigma\cdot d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}=d^{(i)}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\}
and is therefore a left coset of
\operatorname{stab}_{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta})=C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}d^{-1}
. In particular,
\Omega_{d,\text{d}}
is a union of some left cosets of
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}d^{-1}
.

We collect together some important properties that
{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}
satisfies:

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
d,\text{d}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
.

(i)

If
\text{d}^{\prime}=\tau\text{d}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}
and
d^{\prime}=\sigma d\eta_{\alpha}\eta_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}
for some
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
,
\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
(\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}),(\eta_{\alpha},\eta_{\beta})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
, then
\text{d}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}
and

\displaystyle{\swabfamily a}_{d^{\prime},\text{d}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{\beta})\mathrm{sgn}(\eta_{\beta}){\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}.

(ii)

If
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\cap R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}=\varnothing
or
d\notin\mathscr{C}
, then
{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}=0
.

(iii)

Suppose that
d\in\mathscr{C}
and that
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\cap R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
is a disjoint union of
r
left cosets of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
, with representatives
d^{(1)},\dotsc,d^{(r)}
. For each
i
, let
\sigma_{i}\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
such that
\sigma_{i}d\in d^{(i)}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
, and let
\varepsilon^{(i)}=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma_{i})\varepsilon_{\text{d}}(\sigma_{i}d)
. Then

\displaystyle{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}=\big|C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}d^{-1}\big|\sum_{i=1}^{r}\varepsilon^{(i)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
(ii),
\text{d}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}
. It is also easy to see that

\displaystyle\Omega_{d^{\prime},\text{d}^{\prime}}=\Omega_{\sigma d,\text{d}}=\Omega_{d,\text{d}}\sigma^{-1}.

Take
\omega\in\Omega_{d^{\prime},\text{d}^{\prime}}
, say
\omega d^{\prime}=\tau^{\prime}\text{d}^{\prime}\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}
with
\tau^{\prime}\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
(\gamma_{\alpha},\gamma_{\beta})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
. Then

\displaystyle\omega\sigma d=\tau^{\prime}\tau\text{d}\xi_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}^{-1}\big(\xi_{\beta}\gamma_{\beta}\eta_{\beta}^{-1}\big)^{+|\alpha|}

and hence we have
\varepsilon_{\text{d}^{\prime}}(\omega d^{\prime})=\mathrm{sgn}(\gamma_{\beta})=\varepsilon_{\text{d}}(\omega\sigma d)\mathrm{sgn}(\eta_{\beta})\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{\beta})
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\swabfamily a}_{d^{\prime},\text{d}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{d^{\prime},\text{d}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)\varepsilon_{\text{d}^{\prime}}(\omega d^{\prime})=\sum_{\omega\sigma\in\Omega_{d,\text{d}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\mathrm{sgn}(\omega\sigma)\varepsilon_{\text{d}}(\omega\sigma d)\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{\beta})\mathrm{sgn}(\eta_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{\beta})\mathrm{sgn}(\eta_{\beta}){\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}.

This completes the proof of part (i).

For part (ii), it is clear that
\Omega_{d,\text{d}}=\varnothing
if
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\cap R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}=\varnothing
, so that
{\swabfamily a}_{d,\text{d}}=0
in this instance. Next we assume that
d\notin\mathscr{C}
. By Lemma
3.6
(i), there exist
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j)\in[\lambda]
with
i\neq i^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{T}_{d}(i,j)=\mathbbm{{c}}_{k}=\mathrm{T}_{d}(i^{\prime},j)
for some
k
. Let
\rho=(\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i,j)\;\;\mathfrak{t}_{0}(i^{\prime},j))
. Then
\rho\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}d^{-1}
. If
\sigma\in\Omega_{d,\text{d}}
, say
\sigma d=\tau\text{d}\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}^{+|\alpha|}
with
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
(\gamma_{\alpha},\gamma_{\beta})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\beta}
, then

\Delta

Lemma 3.16
.

The map
\psi\colon\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)\to S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}
defined by
\mathfrak{t}\mapsto e_{\mathfrak{t}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module epimorphism, and
\ker(\psi)
is generated, as a
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
, by
G\cup H
, where

\displaystyle G=\big\{G_{\Delta}^{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot\mathfrak{t}\colon\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda),\ \Delta\text{ a Garnir transversal}\big\},

\displaystyle H=\{\pi\cdot\mathfrak{t}-\mathrm{sgn}(\pi)\mathfrak{t}\colon\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda),\ \pi\in C_{\mathfrak{t}}\}.

Proof.

That
\psi
is a
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module epimorphism is clear, so we only need to justify the assertion about its kernel. Let
K=\mathbb{Z}(G\cup H)
. It is straightforward to verify that both
G
and
H
are invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and that
\psi(G\cup H)=\{0\}

[
8
, 7.2.1 and Theorem 7.2.3]
, so that
K
is a
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
, and
K\subseteq\ker(\psi)
. It remains to show that
\ker(\psi)\subseteq K
.

Since
\psi(\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda))=S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-free with basis
\{e_{\mathfrak{t}}\colon\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)\}

[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, we see that
\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)=(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{t})\oplus\ker(\psi)
.

Consider
\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)/K
. For each
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda)
, let
v_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathfrak{t}+K\in\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)/K
. Then
\pi\cdot v_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathrm{sgn}(\pi)v_{\mathfrak{t}}
for any
\pi\in C_{\mathfrak{t}}
, and
G_{\Delta}^{\mathfrak{t}}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{t}}=0
for any Garnir transversal
\Delta
. Using the same argument as in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 7.2.7]
, we can show that
\{v_{\mathfrak{t}}\colon\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)\}
generates
\mathbb{Z}\mathscr{T}(\lambda)/K
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Let
x\in\ker(\psi)
. Then there exists
b_{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbb{Z}
for each
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)
such that

\displaystyle x+K=\sum_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)}b_{\mathfrak{t}}v_{\mathfrak{t}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)}b_{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}+K).

Thus there exists
k\in K
such that

\displaystyle x+k=\sum_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)}b_{\mathfrak{t}}\mathfrak{t}\in\left(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{std}}(\lambda)}\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{t}\right)\cap\ker(\psi)=\{0\}.

Hence
x+k=0
and so
x\in K
, and our proof is complete.

\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module homomorphism
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}\colon S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}\to M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)
such that
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}\circ\psi=\upvartheta_{\text{d}}
. The theorem immediately follows.
∎

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. Since

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}\cong\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda}\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\cong\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta),

each
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}
gives rise to an
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-homomorphism
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}\colon S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}\to M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
. More specifically, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
k^{\mathbb{F}}=k\cdot 1_{\mathbb{F}}\in\mathbb{F}
. Then

\displaystyle\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}(e_{\mathfrak{t}})=\sum_{d\in\Gamma}{\swabfamily a}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{t}}^{-1}d,\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}\,(d\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon).

As we mentioned in the paragraph following Theorem
3.3
, our
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}
’s generalise the
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}
’s constructed by James.

4
Basis

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and let
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
. As before, fix a
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
so that
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
through
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
. Fix a left transversal
\Gamma
of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and write

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}=\{d\in\Gamma\colon\mathrm{T}_{d}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))\}.

Recall from Corollary
3.7
that we have
\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}\subseteq\mathscr{R}\cap\mathscr{C}
. It is easy to see that
|\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}|=|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|
.
In the last section, we constructed for each
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
an
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-homomorphism
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
which generalises James’s construction of
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mu}\big)
where
\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda,\mu)
.

Let

\displaystyle{\Uptheta}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\big\{\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}\colon\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}\big\},\qquad

\displaystyle{\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\big\{\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}\colon\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}\big\},

\displaystyle{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}:=\big\{\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}\colon\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}\big\},\qquad

\displaystyle{\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathrm{sstd}}:=\big\{\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}\colon\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}\big\}.

In James’s classical case, i.e., when
\beta=\varnothing
, the
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}
’s, as
\mathrm{T}
runs over all the semistandard
\lambda
tableaux of type
\alpha
, form a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}^{\alpha}\big)
, unless
\mathbb{F}
has characteristic
2
and
\lambda
is
2
-singular (see
[
7
, Theorem 13.13]
).

In this section, we investigate the circumstances under which
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
.

4.1
Spanning

We give two examples in which there is no semistandard
\lambda
-tableau of type
(\alpha|\beta)
, but
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)\neq 0
.

Example 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\varnothing|(n))\big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},\mathrm{sgn}\big)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(\mathrm{sgn},\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}\big)^{*}\big)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(\mathrm{sgn},S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes\mathrm{sgn}\big)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(\mathbb{F},S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\big).

In
[
7
, Theorem 24.4]
, a necessary and sufficient condition in terms of the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
(as well as the partition
\lambda
) is obtained for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(\mathbb{F},S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\big)
to be non-zero. As long as
\lambda\neq(1^{n})
, there is no semistandard
\lambda
-tableau of type
(\varnothing|(n))
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime integer, and let
p:=\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})
be either odd or zero.
Let
\lambda=\big(\ell,2,1^{\ell-2}\big)
and
\alpha=(\ell)=\beta
. Clearly,
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}\big(\big(\ell,2,1^{\ell-2}\big),((\ell)|(\ell))\big)=\varnothing
.

Firstly,
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)=\operatorname{\mathrm{Ind}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{2\ell}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}}(\mathbb{F}\boxtimes\mathrm{sgn})\cong\operatorname{\mathrm{Ind}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{2\ell}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{(\ell)}\boxtimes S_{\mathbb{F}}^{(1^{\ell})}\big)
has a Specht filtration with two factors:
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{(\ell+1,1^{\ell-1})}
at the top and
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{(\ell,1^{\ell})}
at the bottom
[
9
]
, which are simple unless
p=\ell

[
10
, Theorem 2]
.
Furthermore, unless
p=\ell
, the partitions
\big(\ell,2,1^{\ell-2}\big)
,
\big(\ell+1,1^{\ell-1}\big)
and
(\ell,1^{\ell})
have distinct
p
-cores and hence the Specht modules they label lie in distinct blocks of
\mathfrak{S}_{2\ell}
by the Nakayama rule
[
8
, Corollary 6.1.42]
, so that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{2\ell}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)=0
.

On the other hand, if
p=\ell
, then
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{(\ell,1^{\ell})}
, a submodule of
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
, is a non-split extension of
D^{(\ell,2,1^{\ell-2})}
by
D^{(\ell+1,1^{\ell-1})}
. From the known radical structures of Specht modules lying in defect
2
blocks of symmetric group algebras (see
[
12
, Theorem 4.4]
and
[
1
, Proposition 6.2]
), we see that
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
has a quotient which is a non-split extension of
D^{(\ell,2,1^{\ell-2})}
by
D^{(\ell+1,1^{\ell-1})}
as well. Since all nonzero extensions between simple modules lying in defect
2
blocks of symmetric group algebras are one-dimensional
[
13
, Theorem I(5)]
, we see that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{2\ell}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)\neq 0
.

Thus,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{2\ell}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)\neq 0
if and only if
p=\ell
.

The two examples above illustrate the difficulty in determining a good sufficient condition in general for
{\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
to span
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
.

4.2
Linear independence

While James’s
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}
is always nonzero irrespective of the characteristic of the ground field, our
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}
may be zero in some characteristic in view of Lemma
3.11
(iii). As such, it is certainly possible for
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
to be linearly dependent. In fact, even when all elements of
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
are nonzero, it is still possible for
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
to be linearly dependent:

Example 4.3
.

We continue with Example
3.12
, where
\lambda=(2,1^{p+2})
and
(\alpha|\beta)=\big(\varnothing|\big(p,2^{2}\big)\big)
. Suppose that
p=\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})
is an odd prime. Then

\displaystyle-{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d_{1},d_{3}}={\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}=((p-1)!\cdot 4)\cdot 1_{\mathbb{F}}\neq 0_{\mathbb{F}},

\displaystyle{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d_{2},d_{2}}={\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d_{3},d_{2}}={\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d_{2},d_{3}}={\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d_{3},d_{3}}=0_{\mathbb{F}}.

Thus,
\hat{\upvartheta}_{d_{2}}^{\mathbb{F}}=-\hat{\upvartheta}_{d_{3}}^{\mathbb{F}}
by Lemma
3.11
(i).

In this subsection, we obtain a sufficient condition for
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
to be linearly independent.

We first introduce a pre-order
\unrhd
on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
, which induces another on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

For each
\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
, write
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})
for the multi-set associated to the
j
th column of
\mathrm{T}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle C_{j}(\mathrm{T})=\{\mathrm{T}(1,j),\mathrm{T}(2,j),\dotsc,\mathrm{T}(r,j)\}\subseteq\{\mathbbm{{c}}_{1},\mathbbm{{c}}_{2},\ldots,\mathbbm{{d}}_{1},\mathbbm{{d}}_{2},\ldots\},

where
r=(\lambda^{\prime})_{j}
. Recall that we have the total order

\displaystyle\mathbbm{{c}}_{1}<\mathbbm{{c}}_{2}<\cdots<\mathbbm{{d}}_{1}<\mathbbm{{d}}_{2}<\cdots.

Let
\mathrm{T},\mathrm{T}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
. Suppose that
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\}
and
C_{j}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\}
, where
y_{1}\leq y_{2}\leq\dotsb\leq y_{r}
and
z_{1}\leq z_{2}\leq\dotsb\leq z_{r}
. Write
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})\rhd C_{j}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
k
such that
y_{s}=z_{s}
for all
1\leq s<k
and
y_{k}>z_{k}
. We write
\mathrm{T}\unrhd\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
if and only if
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})=C_{j}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
for all
j
, or there exists
t
such that
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})=C_{j}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
for all
j<t
and
C_{t}(\mathrm{T})\rhd C_{t}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
. It is easy to check that
\unrhd
is a pre-order on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
(i.e., it is a reflexive and transitive binary relation on
\mathscr{T}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
). In addition, write
\mathrm{T}\sim\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}\unrhd\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}\unrhd\mathrm{T}
(equivalently,
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})=C_{j}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
for all
j
), and
\mathrm{T}\rhd\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}\unrhd\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
but
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}\ntrianglerighteq\mathrm{T}
(equivalently, there exists
t
such that
C_{j}(\mathrm{T})=C_{j}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
for all
j<t
and
C_{t}(\mathrm{T})\rhd C_{t}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
).

Recall that
\mathrm{T}_{d}=d\cdot\mathrm{T}_{0}
. Let
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and write
d\unrhd d^{\prime}
,
d\sim d^{\prime}
and
d\rhd d^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{d}\unrhd\mathrm{T}_{d^{\prime}}
,
\mathrm{T}_{d}\sim\mathrm{T}_{d^{\prime}}
and
\mathrm{T}_{d}\rhd\mathrm{T}_{d^{\prime}}
respectively.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
,
(\alpha|\beta)
be a bicomposition of
n
and
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

(i)

d\sim d^{\prime}
if and only if
d^{\prime}\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{T}_{d}
is row semistandard, and
d^{\prime}\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}d\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
, then either
\mathrm{T}_{d^{\prime}}=\mathrm{T}_{d}
or
\mathrm{T}_{d^{\prime}}\rhd\mathrm{T}_{d}
.

(iii)

If
\mathrm{T}_{d}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
and
{\swabfamily a}_{d^{\prime},d}\neq 0
, then
d^{\prime}\unrhd d
.

Proof.

Parts (i) and (ii) are straightforward. For part (iii), if
{\swabfamily a}_{d^{\prime},d}\neq 0
, then there exist
\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
,
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
such that
\sigma d^{\prime}=\tau d\xi
. Thus,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}\sim\sigma d^{\prime}=\tau d\xi\unrhd d

by parts (i) and (ii) since
\mathrm{T}_{d}
is, in particular, row semistandard.
∎

We can now state a sufficient condition for
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
to be linearly independent.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that
{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d},\text{d}}\neq 0
for all
\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}
. Then
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

Suppose the contrary that
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is linearly dependent, say
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\hat{\upvartheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d}_{i}}=0
, where, for each
1\leq i\leq r
,
\text{d}_{i}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
. Since the
\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}_{i}}
’s are all column semistandard and distinct, we see that
\text{d}_{i}\not\sim\text{d}_{j}
for all
i\neq j
. Thus, relabelling if necessary, we may assume that
\text{d}_{1}\ntrianglerighteq\text{d}_{i}
for all
i\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle 0=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\hat{\upvartheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d}_{i}}\right)(e_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\sum_{d\in\Gamma}{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d,\text{d}_{i}}(d\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon)=\sum_{d\in\Gamma}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d,\text{d}_{i}}\right)(d\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon),

so that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{d,\text{d}_{i}}=0
for all
d\in\Gamma
. In particular,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d}_{1},\text{d}_{i}}=0
. But for each
2\leq i\leq r
, we have
\text{d}_{1}\ntrianglerighteq\text{d}_{i}
, so that
{\swabfamily a}_{\text{d}_{1},\text{d}_{i}}=0
by Lemma
4.4
(iii), and hence
{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d}_{1},\text{d}_{i}}=0
. Consequently,
c_{1}{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d}_{1},\text{d}_{1}}=0
, contradicting
c_{1}\neq 0
and
{\swabfamily a}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\text{d}_{1},\text{d}_{1}}\neq 0
.
∎

The next lemma gives us some idea of what
{\swabfamily a}_{\text{d},\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is for
\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\text{d}\in\mathscr{R}
such that
\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
. Then

\displaystyle{\swabfamily a}_{\text{d},\text{d}}=|\operatorname{stab}_{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}})|.

Proof.

Observe that
C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\cap R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}=\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
: if
\sigma\text{d}=\tau\text{d}\xi
, where
\sigma\in C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
,
\tau\in R_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
, then
\text{d}\sim\tau\text{d}
by Lemma
4.4
(i) so that
\mathrm{T}_{\tau\text{d}}\ntriangleright\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}}
, while
\tau\text{d}\sim\text{d}
only if
\tau\text{d}\in\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}
by Lemma
4.4
(ii). Applying Lemma
3.11
(iii) completes the proof since
\operatorname{stab}_{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}})=C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}\cap\text{d}\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha|\beta}\text{d}^{-1}
.
∎

Combining the last two results, we get the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 4.7
.

If
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})\nmid|\operatorname{stab}_{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}})|
for all
\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}
, then
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is linearly independent.

4.3
Main results

Similar to how our
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}
induces
\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}^{\mathbb{F}}
, every
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
also induces
\phi^{\mathbb{F}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
. Write

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}:=\big\{\phi^{\mathbb{F}}\colon\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)\big\}.

Then
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
is a
\mathbb{F}
-subspace of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
containing
{\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathscr{R}}
– and hence
{\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
– as a subset.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}
be a linearly independent subset of
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
of size
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta)|
. Then

(i)

\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}
is a basis for
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
, and

(ii)

if
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
lies in a block of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
which is simple as an algebra, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)=\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}.

Proof.

If
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
lies in a block of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
which is simple as an algebra, then the composition multiplicity of
S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda}
in
M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)
equals
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
, which is in turn equal to
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta)|
by Theorem
2.2
. Since
\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}
is linearly independent with the same cardinality, it is a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta))
. In particular, since

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)=\mathbb{F}\text{-span}\big(\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}\big)\subseteq\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big),

we must have equality throughout, proving part (ii).

For part (i), let
\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}=\big\{\phi_{1}^{\mathbb{F}},\dotsc,\phi_{k}^{\mathbb{F}}\big\}
, where
k=|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta)|
, and let
\phi_{0}^{\mathbb{F}}\in\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
. Since
\mathbb{Q}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is semisimple as an algebra, we apply the previous paragraph and obtain that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta))
has dimension
k
. Thus there is a non-trivial relation on
\phi_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}},\phi_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}},\dotsc,\phi_{k}^{\mathbb{Q}}
, which we can write as
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}\phi_{j}^{\mathbb{Q}}=0
, where the
c_{j}
’s are coprime integers. This yields the non-trivial linear relation
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}^{\mathbb{F}}\phi_{j}^{\mathbb{F}}=0
. Since
\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}
is linearly independent, this implies that
\phi_{0}^{\mathbb{F}}
lies in the
\mathbb{F}
-span of
\Psi^{\mathbb{F}}
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

As is well-known,
S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{F}}
lies in a simple block if and only if
\lambda
is a
p
-core partition
(in other words,
\lambda
has no rimhook of size
p
) where
p=\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})
.

As an immediate corollary, we have

Corollary 4.10
.

The dimension of
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
is at most
|\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))|
.

The following is our first main result:

Theorem 4.11
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is semisimple as an algebra
(
or equivalently,
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=0
or
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})>n)
. Then
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is semisimple, every block of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is simple. Thus,
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
by Corollary
4.7
and Proposition
4.8
.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{Z}},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
. Then
\phi
lies in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.11
,
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{Q}}
is a basis for
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
, so that
\phi^{\mathbb{Q}}=\sum\limits_{\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}}c_{\text{d}}\hat{\upvartheta}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{\text{d}}
, where
c_{\text{d}}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}
. But this implies that
\phi=\sum\limits_{\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}}c_{\text{d}}\hat{\upvartheta}_{\text{d}}
.
∎

Our next main result provides a sufficient condition for
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
to be a basis for
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
when
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is not semisimple. Note that Example
4.2
shows that this condition is insufficient in ensuring that
{\Uptheta}^{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
spans
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S_{\mathbb{F}}^{\lambda},M_{\mathbb{F}}(\alpha|\beta)\big)
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})=p>0
. If no column of
\mathrm{T}
has
p
or more nodes of the same colour for every
\mathrm{T}\in\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{sstd}}(\lambda,(\alpha|\beta))
, then
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is a basis for the
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

Let
\text{d}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{sstd}}
. Observe that
p\nmid|\mathrm{stab}_{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}}}(\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}})|
, if and only if no column of
\mathrm{T}_{\text{d}}
has
p
or more nodes of the same colour. Thus, this follows from Corollary
4.7
and Proposition
4.8
immediately.
∎

In particular:

Corollary 4.14
.

Suppose that
\ell(\lambda)<\operatorname{\mathrm{char}}(\mathbb{F})
. Then
{\Uptheta}_{\mathrm{sstd}}^{\mathbb{F}}
is a basis for
\Phi^{\mathbb{F}}
.

Remark 4.15
.

Recall from Definition
3.4
that the set
\mathscr{R}
and hence the sets
\Uptheta_{\mathscr{R}}
and
\Uptheta_{\mathrm{sstd}}
depend on the fixed
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
and
(\alpha|\beta)
we chose. Suppose that we had chosen another
\lambda
-tableau
\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{\prime}
and arrived at the sets
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
,
\Uptheta_{\mathscr{R}^{\prime}}
and
\Uptheta^{\prime}{}_{\mathrm{sstd}}
. It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{\prime}=\pi\mathscr{R},\qquad\Uptheta_{\mathscr{R}^{\prime}}=\Uptheta_{\mathscr{R}},\qquad\Uptheta^{\prime}_{\mathrm{sstd}}=\Uptheta_{\mathrm{sstd}},

where
\pi=\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{\prime}\circ(\mathfrak{t}_{0})^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

On the other hand, let
\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\cup\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{s}B_{j}
(disjoint union throughout), with
A_{i},B_{j}\neq\varnothing
for all
i
and
j
. The set
\mathscr{R}
in Definition
3.4
and the homomorphisms
\upvartheta_{\text{d}}
in Section
3.2
can be generalised to yield the set
\mathscr{R}_{A|B}
and homomorphisms
\upvartheta_{\text{d}}^{A|B}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{Z}},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(A|B)\big)
, where

\displaystyle M_{\mathbb{Z}}(A|B)=\operatorname{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{A|B}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{S}_{A}}\boxtimes\mathrm{sgn}_{\mathfrak{S}_{B}}\big),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{A}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{S}_{A_{i}},\qquad\mathfrak{S}_{B}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mathfrak{S}_{B_{j}},\qquad\mathfrak{S}_{A|B}=\mathfrak{S}_{A}\mathfrak{S}_{B},

giving us a subset
\Uptheta^{A|B}_{\mathscr{R}_{A|B}}
of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big(S^{\lambda}_{\mathbb{Z}},M_{\mathbb{Z}}(A|B)\big)
.

Let
g\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and let
A_{i}^{\prime}=g^{-1}(A_{i})
,
B_{j}^{\prime}=g^{-1}(B_{j})
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
and
j=1,\ldots,s

\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}A^{\prime}_{i}\cup\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{s}B^{\prime}_{j}
(disjoint union throughout), and it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{A^{\prime}|B^{\prime}}=\mathscr{R}_{A|B}g,\qquad\Uptheta^{A^{\prime}|B^{\prime}}_{\mathscr{R}_{A^{\prime}|B^{\prime}}}=\varrho_{g}\circ\Uptheta^{A|B}_{\mathscr{R}_{A|B}},

where
\varrho_{g}
is the natural
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module isomorphism
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(A|B)\to M_{\mathbb{Z}}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})
defined by
\varrho_{g}(x\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon)=xg\otimes\mathbbm{{1}}\otimes\epsilon
for all
x\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

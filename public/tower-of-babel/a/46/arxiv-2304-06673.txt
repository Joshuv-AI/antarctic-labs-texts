Abstract
. We consider solutions satisfying the zero Neumann boundary condition and a linearized mean field game equation in Ω × ( 0 , T ) \Omega\times(0,T) whose principal coefficients depend on the time and spatial variables with general Hamiltonian, where Ω \Omega is a bounded domain in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and ( 0 , T ) (0,T) is the time interval. We first prove the Lipschitz stability in Ω × ( ε , T − ε ) \Omega\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon) with given ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 for the determination of the solutions by Dirichlet data on arbitrarily chosen subboundary of ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . Next we prove the Lipschitz stability for an inverse problem of determining spatially varying factors of source terms and a coefficient by extra boundary data and spatial data at intermediate time.

We mainly consider a linearized equation of (1.1), which is formulated
as follows.

We set

\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq d
and

\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and for
\gamma:=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{d})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{d}
we define

\partial_{x}^{\gamma}:=\partial_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\partial_{d}^{\gamma_{d}}
and

|\gamma|:=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}
.

We introduce partial differential operators of the second order
with
(x,t)
- dependent
coefficients by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&A(t)u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x,t)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}u+a_{0}(x,t)u,\\
&B(t)v:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{ij}(x,t)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}v+\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}v+b_{0}(x,t)v,\\
&A_{0}(t)u:=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}b_{\gamma}(x,t)\partial_{x}^{\gamma}u,\end{array}\right.

where
a_{ij},b_{ij}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})
and
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
,

b_{ij}=b_{ji}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

c_{0},a_{k},b_{k}\in L^{\infty}(Q)

for
0\leq k\leq d
,
b_{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(Q)
for

|\gamma|\leq 2
, and it is assumed that there exists a constant

\chi>0
such that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\chi\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}^{2},\quad\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{ij}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\chi\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $(x,t)\in Q$ and $\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

We define

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(\partial_{j}u)\nu_{i},\quad\partial_{\nu_{B}}v:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{ij}(\partial_{j}v)\nu_{i}\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

We consider a linearized mean field game system:

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u+A(t)u=c_{0}(x,t)v+F(x,t),\\
&\partial_{t}v-B(t)v=A_{0}(t)u+G(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q\end{array}\right.

with Neumann boundary condition

[formula]

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=\rho,\quad\partial_{\nu_{B}}v=\sigma\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

We emphasize that general Hamiltonians can be considered, which produces
the second-order partial differential operator

A_{0}(t)u
after the linearization.

In this article, we establish the Lipschitz stability results
for the following inverse problems. Let
\Gamma
be an arbitrarily chosen
non-empty subboundary of
\partial\Omega
,
t_{0}\in(0,T)
be arbitrarily given, and
let
u,v
satisfy (1.3) and (1.4).

Determination of state:

Determine
u,v
in

by
u|_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}
and

v|_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}
.

Inverse source problem:

In (1.1) let
F(x,t)=q_{1}(x,t)f(x)
and
G(x,t)=q_{2}(x,t)g(x)
for

(x,t)\in Q
. Then determine
f,g
in
\Omega
by data

u|_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}
,
v|_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}

and
\{u(\cdot,t_{0}),\,v(\cdot,t_{0})\}

\Omega
.

We set

\|u\|_{H^{2,1}(Q)}:=\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We first describe our main result for the state determination.

Theorem 1.

We assume that
u,v\in H^{2,1}(Q)
satisfy (1.3) and (1.4).
For arbitrarily given
\varepsilon>0
, we can find a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2,1}(\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon))}+\|v\|_{H^{2,1}(\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon))}\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}+\|\sigma\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}).

In particular, we directly see

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|G\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}+\|v\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}+\|\sigma\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}),\quad\varepsilon\leq t\leq T-\varepsilon.

We emphasize that Theorem 1 asserts unconditional stability which implies
that we do not need to impose any boundedness conditions for
u
and
v
.

The norm of boundary values
\rho
and
\sigma
is not the best possible, but
improvements require more arguments and here we do not pursue.
We can obtain a similar estimate for the case where
u,v
are given on

\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
and
\partial_{\nu_{A}}u,\partial_{\nu_{B}}v
on

\Gamma\times(0,T)
.

In Klibanov, Li and Liu
[
11
]
,
the Hölder stability is proved with data
u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v

on the whole lateral boundary
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
, which is the case of
\Gamma=\partial\Omega

By the parabolicity of the equations (1.3), extra Dirichlet data should be

Next we state our main result on the inverse source problem.
In (1.3) we assume

F(x,t)=q_{1}(x,t)f(x),\quad G(x,t)=q_{2}(x,t)g(x),\quad(x,t)\in Q,

where
q_{1},q_{2}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.
Moreover we assume

\displaystyle a_{ij},\partial_{t}a_{ij}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q}),\quad c_{0},\partial_{t}c_{0},a_{k},\partial_{t}a_{k},b_{k},\partial_{t}b_{k}\in L^{\infty}(Q)\quad\mbox{for $1\leq i,j\leq d$ and $0\leq k\leq d$},

\displaystyle b_{\gamma},\partial_{t}b_{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(Q)\quad\mbox{for $|\gamma|\leq 2$}.

We fix
t_{0}\in(0,T)
arbitrarily and we are given

u_{0}(x):=u(x,t_{0}),\quad v_{0}(x):=v(x,t_{0}),\quad x\in\Omega.

Then

Theorem 2 (global unconditional Lipschitz stability for an inverse
source problem).

Let
u,v\in H^{2,1}(Q)
satisfy (1.3),
\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v\in H^{2,1}(Q)
and

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=\partial_{\nu_{B}}v=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

We assume

[formula]

|q_{1}(x,t_{0})|,\,|q_{2}(x,t_{0})|>0\quad\mbox{for all
$x\in\overline{\Omega}$}.

Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{k=0}^{1}(D(\partial_{t}^{k}u)^{2}+D(\partial_{t}^{k}v)^{2})\right).

Here and henceforth we set

D(u):=\left(\int_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{2})dSdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We emphasize that in our stability, we do not assume neither data

u(\cdot,T)
nor
v(\cdot,0)
in
\Omega
,
nor any a priori bounds on
u,v,f,g
, but we require data
u(\cdot,t_{0})

and
v(\cdot,t_{0})
over
\Omega
at an intermediate time
t_{0}\in(0,T)
.
Our stability can be understood unconditional in the sense that
we do not need to assume any a priori boundedness conditions.

Our key is a classical Carleman estimate for a single parabolic equation
with singular weight function by Imanuvilov
[
5
]
.
The linearized mean field game equations (1.3) have two features:

•

The equation in
u
is backward and the one in
v
is forward.

•

The equation in
v
contains the second-order spatial derivatives

A_{0}(t)u
of
u
.

The mixed forward and backward equations in (1.3),
makes the direct problem such as initial boundary
value problem difficult, but thanks to the symmetry of the time
variable in the weight function, this does not matter for
Carleman estimates, our main tool.

Usually systems with coupled principal parts cause
difficulty for establishing relevant Carleman estimates.
However, in our case, although the second equation in (1.3) is coupled with
the second-order terms of
u
, the first one in (1.3) is coupled only with
zeroth order term of
v
, which enables us executing a typical argument
of absorbing the second-order terms of
u
by taking large parameters
of the Carleman estimate.

This article is composed of four sections. In Section 2, we prove
a Carleman estimate (Theorem 3) for the linearized mean field game
equations and complete the proof of Theorem 1.
Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem 2. In Section 4, we
discuss the state determination problem for the original nonlinear
mean value field equations (1.1).

2.
Carleman estimate for the mean field game equations and the proof
of Theorem 1

For an arbitrarily given subboundary
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
,
it is known (Lemma 2.3 in Imanuvilov and Yamamoto
[
6
]
,
Imanuvilov
[
5
]
) that there exists

\eta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\eta>0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$},\quad|\nabla\eta|>0\quad\mbox{on $\overline{\Omega}$},\quad\partial_{\nu_{A}}\eta\leq 0,\quad\partial_{\nu_{B}}\eta\leq 0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma$}.

We set

\varphi(x,t)=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{t(T-t)},\quad\alpha(x,t)=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}-e^{EE}}{t(T-t)}

and

t_{0}=\frac{T}{2}.

We set

M:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\|a_{ij}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{Q})}+\|b_{ij}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{Q})})+\sum_{k=0}^{d}(\|a_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+\|b_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)})+\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|b_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}.

We recall

D(u):=\left(\int_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{2})dSdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Henceforth
C>0
denotes generic constants which are independent of
s,\lambda>0

which are parameters, and we write
C(s,\lambda)
when we need to specify the
dependency.

Then we can prove

Lemma 1.

There exists a constant
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for each
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
, we can find
a constant
s_{0}(\lambda)>0
satisfying: there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}+s^{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{4}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{4}|u|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}s\varphi|\partial_{t}u+A(t)u|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{1}(\lambda,s)D(u)^{2}

for all
s>s_{0}(\lambda)
and
u\in H^{2,1}(Q)
satisfying

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
.

Here the constant
C>0
depends continuously on
M
: bound of the
coefficients and
\lambda
but
independent of
s\geq s_{0}(\lambda)
, while
\lambda_{0}>0
depends continuously
on
M
.

Proof.

By Fursikov and Imanuvilov
[
4
]
or Imanuvilov
[
5
]
, we
know

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\biggl\{\frac{1}{s\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}w|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w|^{2}\right)+s\lambda^{2}\varphi|\nabla w|^{2}+s^{3}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{3}|w|^{2}\biggr\}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}s\varphi|\partial_{t}w-A(t)w|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{1}(\lambda,s)D(w)^{2}

for
w\in H^{2,1}(Q)
satisfying
\partial_{\nu_{A}}w=0
on

\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
.

We set
u(x,t)=w(x,T-t)
for
(x,t)\in Q
.
Then, since
\alpha(x,t)=\alpha(x,T-t)
and

\varphi(x,t)=\varphi(x,T-t)
for
(x,t)\in Q
, the change of varables

t\mapsto T-t
verifies

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\biggl\{\frac{1}{s\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}\right)+s\lambda^{2}\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{3}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{3}|u|^{2}\biggr\}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}u+A(t)u|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C_{1}(\lambda,s)D(u)^{2}.

Next we have to prove the estimate with one more factor
s\varphi
.
For simplicity we consider
A(t)=\Delta
.

By
\eta(x)\geq 1
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, we have

\partial_{i}\varphi=\lambda(\partial_{i}\eta)\varphi,\quad\partial_{i}^{2}\varphi=(\lambda\partial_{i}^{2}\eta+\lambda^{2}(\partial_{i}\eta)^{2})\varphi,

and

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{t(T-t)}\right)=\frac{2(t-t_{0})}{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}.

Hence

|\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq C\varphi^{2},\quad|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\leq C\lambda\varphi,\quad|\partial_{i}^{2}\varphi|\leq C\lambda^{2}\varphi\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\lambda>0
.

We set
w:=\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}u
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{t}\varphi)u+\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{t}u,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}w=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{i}\varphi)u+\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{i}u,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w=\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u+\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}((\partial_{i}\varphi)\partial_{j}u+(\partial_{j}\varphi)\partial_{i}u)+\left(-\frac{1}{4}\varphi^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\partial_{i}\varphi)\partial_{j}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi)\right)u,

and so

|\partial_{i}w|\leq C(\lambda\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}|u|+\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{i}u|)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+\Delta w=\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{t}u+\Delta u)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{t}\varphi)u

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda\Delta\eta+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}u+\lambda\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla u\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.

Hence,

|\partial_{t}w+\Delta w|\leq C(\varphi|F|^{2}+\varphi^{3}|u|^{2}+\lambda^{4}\varphi|u|^{2}+\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}).

Consequently the already proved Carleman estimate (
[
5
]
) yields

\int_{Q}\biggl\{\frac{1}{\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}w|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w|^{2}\right)+s^{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi|\nabla w|^{2}+s^{4}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{3}|w|^{2}\biggr\}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

[formula]

\leq C\int_{Q}s\varphi|F|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}(s\varphi^{3}+s\lambda^{4}\varphi)|u|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}s\varphi|u|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD(w)^{2}.

On the other hand,

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\varphi|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\leq C(|\partial_{t}w|^{2}+\varphi^{3}|u|^{2}),\\
&\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C(|\nabla w|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\varphi|u|^{2}),\\
&\varphi|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}\leq C(|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda^{4}\varphi|u|^{2}),\end{array}\right.

so that

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}w|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w|^{2}\right)+C(\lambda^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda^{4}|u|^{2}+\varphi^{2}|u|^{2}).

Therefore, by (2.2) we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}+s^{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{4}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{4}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\biggl\{\frac{1}{\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}w|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w|^{2}\right)+\lambda^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+(\lambda^{4}+\varphi^{2})|u|^{2}\biggr\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C(s^{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi|\nabla w|^{2}+s^{2}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{2}|u|^{2}+s^{4}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{3}|w|^{2}).

In view of (2.1), we see

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}+s^{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{4}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{4}|u|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}\left\{\frac{1}{\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}w|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w|^{2}\right)+s^{2}\lambda^{2}\varphi|\nabla w|^{2}+s^{4}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{3}|w|^{2}\right\}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(\lambda^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+(\lambda^{4}+\varphi^{2})|u|^{2}+s^{2}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{2}|u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}s\varphi|F|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}(s\varphi^{3}+s\lambda^{4}\varphi)|u|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}s\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C(s,\lambda)D(w)^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(\lambda^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+(\lambda^{4}+\varphi^{2})|u|^{2}+s^{2}\lambda^{4}\varphi^{2}|u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt.

The second, the third and the fifth terms on the right-hand side can be
absorbed into the left-hand side for large
s>0
.

Moreover, we can estimate the coefficient of
D(w)^{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle s\varphi^{m}e^{2s\alpha}=s\frac{e^{m\lambda\eta(x)}}{t^{m}(T-t)^{m}}\exp\left(-2s\left(\frac{e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{t(T-t)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(\lambda)s\left(\frac{1}{t(T-t)}\right)^{m}\exp\left(-2s\frac{C_{2}(\lambda)}{t(T-t)}\right)\leq C_{1}(\lambda)s\max_{\xi\geq 0}\xi^{m}e^{-2sC_{2}(\lambda)\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}(\lambda)s\left(\frac{m}{2sC_{2}(\lambda)}\right)^{m}e^{-m}\quad\mbox{for each $m\in\mathbb{N}$},

where the maximum is taken at
\xi=\frac{m}{2sC_{2}(\lambda)}
.
Thus the proof of Lemma 1 is complete.

Proof of Theorem 1.

First we can reduce (1.4) to the case where

Indeed, by the extension theorem in Sobolev spaces (e.g.,

\widetilde{u}=\widetilde{v}=0,\quad\partial_{\nu_{A}}\widetilde{u}=\rho,\quad\partial_{\nu_{B}}\widetilde{v}=\sigma\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}

and

[formula]

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))},\quad\|\widetilde{v}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\sigma\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}U+A(t)U=c_{0}V+\{F-(\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}+A_{1}(t)\widetilde{u}-c_{0}\widetilde{v})\},\\

\partial_{\nu_{A}}U=\partial_{\nu_{B}}V=0,\quad U=u,\quad V=v\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

By (2.5), we see

\displaystyle\|F-(\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}+A_{1}(t)\widetilde{u}-c_{0}\widetilde{v})\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}+A_{1}(t)\widetilde{u}-c_{0}\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+C(\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\widetilde{v}\|_{L^{2}(Q)})\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+C(\|\rho\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}+\|\sigma\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))})

Therefore, it suffices to prove Theorem 1 by assuming that

\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)

for all

Thus Theorem 2 completes the proof of Theorem 1.

Proof of Theorem 3.

First Step: Carleman estimate for an integral operator

In this step, we prove

Lemma 3.

Let

and

. Then there exists a constant

such that

\int_{Q}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}w(x,\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}(s\varphi)^{p-1}\lambda^{-1}|w(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

for all large
s,\lambda>0
.

A similar lemma is proved in Loreti, Sforza and Yamamoto

[
17
]
where the weight is given by

e^{\lambda(\eta(x)-\beta(t-t_{0})^{2})}
. For the case
p=0
, the works
Bukhgeim and Klibanov
[
2
]
and Klibanov
[
7

used this kind of weighted estimate
for the uniqueness of inverse problems.

Proof of Lemma 3.

We divide the left-hand side and consider

\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}w(x,\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dt

and

\int^{t_{0}}_{0}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}w(x,\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dt.

We set
h(x):=e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\eta(x)}>0
. Then we can write

\alpha(x,t)=\frac{-h(x)}{t(T-t)}
in
Q
.
Moreover

\partial_{t}(e^{2s\alpha})=2s(\partial_{t}\alpha)e^{2s\alpha}=2s(-h(x))\frac{2(t-t_{0})}{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}e^{2s\alpha},

and so

[formula]

(t-t_{0})(s\varphi)^{p}e^{2s\alpha}=\frac{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}(s\varphi)^{p}}{4s(-h(x))}\partial_{t}(e^{2s\alpha}).

We note

[formula]

\frac{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}(s\varphi)^{p}}{4s|h(x)|}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}(s\varphi)^{p-1},\quad(x,t)\in Q.

Proof of (3.2).

It suffices to prove

\frac{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{|h(x)|}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda\varphi}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}\lambda\varphi}{|h(x)|}=\frac{t(T-t)\lambda e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}\leq\frac{C\lambda e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C\lambda}{e^{\lambda(2\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}-\eta(x))}-1}\leq\frac{C\lambda}{e^{\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-1},\quad(x,t)\in Q.

On the other hand,

\sup_{\lambda>0}\frac{\lambda}{e^{C_{0}\lambda}-1}<\infty,

where
C_{0}:=\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}
. Therefore,

\sup_{(x,t)\in Q}\frac{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}\lambda\varphi}{|h(x)|}<\infty,

which proves (3.2).
\blacksquare

Note

[formula]

\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)=\frac{2(t-t_{0})}{t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}e^{\lambda\eta(x)}.

Let
t_{0}<t<T
. Then by (3.1) and (3.2), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
implies

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}w(x,\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dt\leq\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(s\varphi)^{p}\left(\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)(t-t_{0})e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\left\{\left(\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{4s(-h(x))}\right\}\partial_{t}(e^{2s\alpha})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{4s(-h(x))}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}\right]^{t=T}_{t=t_{0}}+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{4s|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\left(\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t(t-2t_{0})(t-t_{0})}{s|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\left\{\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{p}{2}(s\varphi)^{p-1}\frac{(t-t_{0})e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{T}_{t_{0}}|w(x,t)|^{2}\frac{1}{\lambda}(s\varphi)^{p-1}e^{2s\alpha}dt+C\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\left(\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)(s\varphi)^{p-1}e^{2s\alpha}dt.

Here we used also (3.3),
\alpha(x,T)=-\infty
,

\frac{t(t-2t_{0})(t-t_{0})}{|h(x)|}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{|h(x)|}\leq C\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}=\frac{C}{e^{\lambda(2\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}-\eta(x))}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{e^{\lambda(2\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}-\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})})}-1}=\frac{C}{e^{\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-1}\quad\mbox{for $\lambda>1$},

which implies

[formula]

\sup_{\lambda>1}\sup_{(x,t)\in Q}\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{|h(x)|}<\infty.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right|e^{2s\alpha}dtdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{t_{0}}s^{p-1}\lambda^{-1}\varphi^{p-1}|w(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dtdx+C\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(s\varphi)^{p-1}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dtdx

for all large
s,\lambda>0
.
Choosing
s>0
and
\lambda>1
sufficiently large, we can absorb the
second term on the right-hand side into the left-hand side.
Thus

[formula]

\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{t_{0}}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right|e^{2s\alpha}dtdx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int^{T}_{t_{0}}s^{p-1}\lambda^{-1}\varphi^{p-1}|w(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dtdx.

Similarly

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{0}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}w(x,\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dt=\int^{t_{0}}_{0}(s\varphi)^{p}\left|\int^{t_{0}}_{t}w(x,\xi)d\xi\right|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\left\{\left(\int^{t_{0}}_{t}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{4s|h(x)|}\right\}\partial_{t}(e^{2s\alpha})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\int^{t_{0}}_{t}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{4s|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}\right]^{t=t_{0}}_{t=0}+\int^{t_{0}}_{0}|w(x,t)|^{2}\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t^{2}(T-t)^{2}}{4s|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\left(\int^{t_{0}}_{t}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{(s\varphi)^{p}t(t-2t_{0})(t-t_{0})}{s|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha}dt

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\left\{\int^{t_{0}}_{t}|w(x,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\frac{p}{2}(s\varphi)^{p-1}\frac{(t-t_{0})e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{|h(x)|}e^{2s\alpha}dt

Here we used (3.3), (3.4),

Therefore (3.2) verifies

Choosing

\lambda>0
large, we reach

\int_{Q}\biggl\{|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}+s^{4}\varphi^{4}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|\partial_{t}v|^{2}\biggr\}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

[formula]

\leq C\int_{Q}(s\varphi|f|^{2}+|g|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD_{0}^{2}

for all large
s>0
.

Third Step: Completion of the proof.

We note that
|\partial_{t}\alpha|\leq C|\varphi|^{2}
in
Q

with fixed large
\lambda>0
.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}s\varphi(x,t_{0})|\partial_{t}u(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx=\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\partial_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}s\varphi|\partial_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(2s\varphi(\partial_{t}u)(\partial_{t}^{2}u)+s(\partial_{t}\varphi)|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+2s\varphi\times s(\partial_{t}\alpha)|\partial_{t}u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(s\varphi|\partial_{t}u||\partial_{t}^{2}u|+s\varphi^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+s^{2}\varphi^{3}|\partial_{t}u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}((s^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{t}^{2}u|)(s^{\frac{3}{2}}\varphi|\partial_{t}u|)+(s\varphi^{2}+s^{2}\varphi^{3})|\partial_{t}u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(s^{-1}|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}+(s^{3}\varphi^{2}+s\varphi^{2}+s^{2}\varphi^{3})|\partial_{t}u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq\frac{C}{s}\int_{Q}(|\partial_{t}^{2}u|^{2}+s^{4}\varphi^{4}|\partial_{t}u|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt.

Hence, by (3.7), we have

[formula]

\int_{\Omega}s\varphi(x,t_{0})|\partial_{t}u(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}\left(\varphi|f|^{2}+\frac{1}{s}|g|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD_{0}^{2}

for all large
s>0
.

Next we can similarly estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx=\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\partial_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}v(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(2(\partial_{t}v)(\partial_{t}^{2}v)+2s(\partial_{t}\alpha)|\partial_{t}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(|\partial_{t}v||\partial_{t}^{2}v|+s\varphi^{2}|\partial_{t}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt=C\int_{Q}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{3}{4}}\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\partial_{t}^{2}v|\right)\left(s^{\frac{3}{4}}\varphi^{\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{t}v|\right)+s\varphi^{2}|\partial_{t}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}\right\}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2}+s^{2}\varphi|\partial_{t}v|^{2}+s^{\frac{3}{2}}\varphi^{2}|\partial_{t}v|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{s\varphi}|\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|\partial_{t}v|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt.

The estimate (3.7) implies

[formula]

\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}\left(\sqrt{s}\varphi|f|^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}|g|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD_{0}^{2}

for all large
s>0
.

On the other hand, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&f(x)=\frac{1}{q_{1}(x,t_{0})}\partial_{t}u(x,t_{0})+\frac{1}{q_{1}(x,t_{0})}(A_{1}(t_{0})u_{0}-c_{0}(x,t_{0})v_{0}),\\
&g(x)=\frac{1}{q_{2}(x,t_{0})}\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})+\frac{1}{q_{2}(x,t_{0})}(-A_{2}(t_{0})v_{0}-A_{0}(t_{0})u_{0}),\quad x\in\Omega\end{array}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&s\varphi(x_{0})|f(x)|^{2}\leq Cs\varphi(x,t_{0})|\partial_{t}u(x,t_{0})|^{2}+Cs\varphi(x,t_{0})\left(\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}u_{0}(x)|^{2}+|v_{0}(x)|^{2}\right),\\
&|g(x)|^{2}\leq C|\partial_{t}u(x,t_{0})|^{2}+C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}(|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}u_{0}(x)|^{2}+|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}v_{0}(x)|^{2}).\end{array}\right.

Hence (3.8) and (3.9) yield

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(s\varphi(x_{0},t)|f(x)|^{2}+|g(x)|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}(s\varphi(x,t_{0})|\partial_{t}u(x,t_{0})|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}\left(\varphi|f|^{2}+\frac{1}{s}|g|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+C\int_{Q}\left(\sqrt{s}\varphi|f|^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}|g|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+CD_{0}^{2},

that is,

\int_{\Omega}(s\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}+|g(x)|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx

[formula]

\leq C\int_{Q}\left(\sqrt{s}\varphi(x,t)|f(x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}|g(x)|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt+CD_{0}^{2}

for all large
s>0
.
Here, setting
\ell(t):=t(T-t)
, we see

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t)|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}=\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}\times\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{\varphi(x,t_{0})}e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}\times\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{4e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sh(x)\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}.

Here we set

h(x):=e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\eta(x)}>0,\quad C_{1}:=e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}>0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\varphi(x,t)|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sh(x)\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sC_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}dt\right)dx.

We will estimate

\int^{T}_{0}\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sC_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}dt
.
Indeed

\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sC_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}=0

for each fixed
t\in(0,T)\setminus\{t_{0}\}
. Next, since

\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sC_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2C_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}

for
s\geq 1
and by
\ell(t)\leq\ell(t_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\geq 1}\sup_{0<t<T}\frac{1}{\ell(t)}\exp\left(-2sC_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)\right)\leq\sup_{0<t<T}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-\frac{2C_{1}}{\ell(t)}}\right)e^{\frac{2C_{1}}{\ell(t_{0})}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi>0}(\xi e^{-2C_{1}\xi})e^{\frac{2C_{1}}{\ell(t_{0})}}<\infty.

Consequently, the Lebesgue convergence theorem yields

\int^{T}_{0}\frac{1}{\ell(t)}e^{-2sC_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(t)}-\frac{1}{\ell(t_{0})}\right)}dt=o(1)\quad\mbox{as $s\to\infty$}.

Hence,

\int_{Q}\varphi(x,t)|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt=o(1)\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx

as
s\to\infty
. By (3.10), using
\alpha(x,t)\leq\alpha(x,t_{0})
for

(x,t)\in Q
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}(s\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}+|g(x)|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}(o(\sqrt{s})\varphi(x,t_{0})|f(x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}|g(x)|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx+CD_{0}^{2}

for all large
s>0
.
Choosing
s>0
sufficiently large, we absorb the first term on the right-hand
side into the left-hand side, we reach

\int_{\Omega}(s\varphi(x_{0})|f(x)|^{2}+|g(x)|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq CD_{0}^{2},

which completes the proof of Theorem 2.

\blacksquare

4.
State determination for the nonlinear mean field game system

For the not-linearlized mean field game system (1.1), we discuss the state
determination problem. In this article, we do not consider inverse source
problems and inverse coefficient problems of determining spatially varying
factors of the coefficients such as
\kappa(x,t)
,
and we postpone them to a future work.

In (1.1), we assume that

[formula]

a\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)),\,>0\quad\mbox{on $\overline{Q}$},\quad p\in L^{\infty}(Q),\quad\kappa\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)).

Then we can prove

Theorem 4.

For
k=1,2
, let
(u_{k},v_{k})\in H^{2,1}(Q)
satisfy

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u_{k}+a(x,t)\Delta u_{k}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\kappa(x,t)|\nabla u_{k}(x,t)|^{2}+p(x,t)v_{k}=F_{k}(x,t),\\
&\partial_{t}v_{k}-\Delta(a(x,t)v_{k}(x,t))-\mathrm{div}\,(\kappa(x,t)v_{k}(x,t)\nabla u_{k}(x,t))=G_{k}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q\end{array}\right.

and

\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nu=\nabla v_{k}\cdot\nu=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

We further assume

[formula]

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{2,\infty}(\Omega))}+\|v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega))}\leq M_{1},

where
M_{1}>0
is an arbitrarily chosen constant.
Then, for arbitrarily given
\varepsilon>0
, we can find a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0

such that

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H^{2,1}(\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon))}+\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{2,1}(\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon))}\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|G_{1}-G_{2}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\nabla(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}+\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}+\|\nabla(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\times(0,T))}).

Here the constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
depends on
\varepsilon,M_{1}>0
and
the coefficients
a,p,\kappa
.

Proof of Theorem 4.

We take the difference
u:=u_{1}-u_{2}
and
v:=v_{1}-v_{2}
. Then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u+a(x,t)\Delta u(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\kappa(x,t)((\nabla u_{1}+\nabla u_{2})\cdot\nabla u)+p(x,t)v=F_{1}-F_{2},\\
&\partial_{t}v-a(x,t)\Delta v(x,t)-2\nabla a(x,t)\cdot\nabla v(x,t)-v(x,t)\Delta a(x,t)\\
-&(\kappa\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla v+(\nabla\kappa\cdot\nabla u_{1}+\kappa\Delta u_{1})v+v_{2}\kappa\Delta u+\nabla(\kappa v_{2})\cdot\nabla u)=G_{1}-G_{2},\quad(x,t)\in Q\end{array}\right.

By (4.1) and (4.3), we can find a constant
M_{2}>0
depending on

M_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{2}\kappa\nabla u_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+\|\nabla\kappa\cdot\nabla u_{1}+\kappa\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\kappa v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+\|\nabla(\kappa v_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq M_{2}.

Therefore, we can satisfy the conditions of Theorem 3 where the constants

C>0
,
\lambda
and
s_{0}(\lambda)
depend only on
M_{2},a(x,t),\kappa(x,t),p(x,t)
,
but independent of choices
u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2}
.
Thus the proof of Theorem 4 follows directly from Theorem 3.

\blacksquare

On ideals of affine semigroups and affine semigroups with maximal embedding dimension
J. I. García-García, R. Tapia-Ramos, A. Vigneron-Tenorio

Abstract
Let S ⊆ ℕ p S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p} be a semigroup, any P ⊆ S P\subseteq S is an ideal of S S if P + S ⊆ P P+S\subseteq P , and an I ⁡ ( S ) I(S) -semigroup is the affine semigroup P ∪ { 0 } P\cup\{0\} , with P P an ideal of S S . We characterise the I ⁡ ( S ) I(S) -semigroups and the ones that also are 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -semigroups. Moreover, some algorithms are provided to compute all the I ⁡ ( S ) I(S) -semigroups satisfying some properties. From a family of ideals of S S , we introduce the affine semigroups with maximal embedding dimension, characterising them and describing some families.

Introduction

An affine semigroup
S
of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
(for a non-zero natural number
p
) is a commutative additive submonoid of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
containing the zero element, and such a finite generating set exists. That is, there exists a finite subset
\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\}\subset S
satisfying
S=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}n_{i}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
. If
p=1
and generators
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
are coprime, the semigroup
S
is called numerical semigroup. Consider the non-negative integer cone generated by a set
B\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
as the set

\mathcal{C}_{B}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\mid k\in\mathbb{N},\,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0},\mbox{ and }b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B\right\}\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}.

Assume that
\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{t}\}
is the set of extremal rays of
\mathcal{C}_{S}
, and
n_{i}\in\tau_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,t
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{C}_{S}
and
S
are finitely generated if the last condition holds (see
[
3
, Corollary 2.10]
). A semigroup
S
is called simplicial if
t=p
. In general, given a cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
, a semigroup
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is called
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if
\mathcal{C}\setminus S
is a finite set, with
\mathcal{C}
equal to
\mathcal{C}_{S}
. Note that any numerical semigroup satisfies this property. Since the generators are coprime for numerical semigroups, we have that
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
is finite. For any semigroup
S
,
\mathcal{H}(S)
denotes the set
\mathcal{C}_{S}\setminus S
, whose elements are called gaps of
S
. The cardinality of its gap set is known as the genus of
S
and is denoted by g(S).
We call
i
-multiplicity of
S
, denoted by
\mathrm{mult}_{i}(S)
, the minimum element in
\tau_{i}\cap S
for the componentwise partial order in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Other invariants require considering a monomial order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, a monomial order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
is a total order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
satisfying compatibility with addition, and ensuring
0\preceq c
for any
c\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
. For example, the Frobenius element
\mathrm{Fb}_{\preceq}(S)
of a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
is defined as
\max_{\preceq}(\mathcal{C}\setminus S)
. To simplify the notation, fixed a monomial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we use the symbol
\mathrm{Fb}(S)
instead of
\mathrm{Fb}_{\preceq}(S)
. Note that
\mathrm{Fb}(S)
depends on the fixed monomial order.

In this work, we explore the properties of ideals of affine semigroups; a subset
P
of a semigroup
S
is an ideal of
S
if
P+S\subseteq P
, and observe that
P=S
if and only if
0\in P
. The ideals of affine semigroups have been widely treated in the literature (to mention some of them, see
[
1
,
16
,
17
]
), and there exists a large list of publications devoted to the study of ideals of numerical semigroups. Some examples are
[
2
,
9
,
10
,
14
]
and the references therein. A recent reference is
[
4
]
, where the property of cofiniteness of ideals in affine semigroups is characterised. In
[
10
]
, the authors introduce the concept of numerical
I(S)
-semigroup, given a numerical semigroup
S
, a numerical semigroup
P
is called
I(S)
-semigroup if
P\setminus\{0\}
is an ideal of
S
. Following this line of research, our work extends properties from numerical semigroups and their
I(S)
-semigroups and ideals to non-numerical affine semigroups, providing some results that are only satisfied by non-numerical affine semigroups. In particular, given
S
an affine semigroup, we focus on characterising its affine
I(S)
-semigroups, and assuming that
S
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, we identify those
I(S)
-semigroups that are also
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups. Moreover, we present some algorithms for computing objects related to
I(S)
-semigroups. On the one hand, for any affine semigroup
S
, we outline the description of all the
I(S)
-semigroups up to a given genus, which allows us to arrange the set of all
I(S)
-semigroups in a tree. On the other hand, for any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, we turn out our attention on determining all the
I(S)
-semigroups with a fixed Frobenius element and a fixed set of
i
-multiplicities.

We generalise to higher dimensions the concept of numerical semigroup with maximal embedding dimension by considering ideals
M+S
with
M=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\}\subset S
such that
m_{i}\in\tau_{i}\setminus\{0\}
for any
i\in[t]
. This new kind of affine ideals leads us to introduce affine semigroups with maximal embedding (MED-semigroup). A MED-semigroup is an affine semigroup such that all the elements in
\cap_{i=1}^{t}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{i})\setminus\{0\}
are minimal generators of
S
. The set
\mathrm{Ap}(S,m)
denotes the Apery set of
S
for
m\in S\setminus\{0\}
, defined as
\mathrm{Ap}(S,m)=\{s\in S\mid s-m\notin S\}
. We prove that the
I(S)
-semigroup
(M+S)\cup\{0\}
is an affine MED-semigroup. Furthermore, we characterise MED-semigroups using
I(S)
-semigroups. Our findings also provide a method for computing as many non-numerical affine MED-semigroups as desired.

Another of our objectives is to study the membership problem for an affine semigroup
S
: given an element
x
in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, checking whether
x
belongs to
S
. This is an essential problem in the context of affine semigroups. Most existing methods are related to find non-negative integer solutions to some system of linear Diophantine equations. In particular,
x\in S
means that there exist
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}n_{i}
. Several algorithms to find such a non-negative solution are shown in
[
13
]
, and the references therein. However, the computational complexity of these methods grows with the number of variables, and the cardinality of the minimal generating set of an affine semigroup can be very large. For example, for
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups, this high cardinality can be inferred from the study made in
[
5
]
. For a fixed numerical semigroup
S
, knowing its Apery set for one non-zero element in
S
, it is easy to solve the membership problem for
S
. Inspired by this idea, we provide an algorithm to solve this membership problem using Apery sets. Unfortunately, the Apery set of a non-numerical affine semigroup for one non-zero of its elements is not finite. However, the intersection of the Apery sets of some non-zero elements in
S
is. We use this fact to design an algorithm for the membership problem for any non-numerical simplicial affine semigroup.

The content of this work is organised as follows: in Section
1
, we study several properties of ideals of affine semigroups and introduce the necessary background about these ideals. Sections
2
and
3
are devoted to improve the knowledge of
I(S)
-semigroups and to describe a tree containing all the
I(S)
-semigroups. Besides, some algorithms are given to compute the sets of all
I(S)
-semigroups up to a fixed genus, with a fixed Frobenius element, and with a set of fixed
i
-multiplicities. In Section
4
, affine MED-semigroups are defined and characterised, and several families are provided. In the last section (Section
5
), we use some Apery sets to give an algorithm to solve the membership problem for affine semigroups. The results of this work are illustrated with several examples. To this purpose, we implemented all the algorithms shown in this work in some libraries developed by the authors in Mathematica
[
8
]
.

1
Ideals of affine semigroups

Let us start by introducing some notations. Moving forward, we characterise the affine ideals and those that are also
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups. For any integers
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
a\leq b
, we denote the integer interval
[a,b]
as the set
{a,a+1,\ldots,b}
, and we denote the set
[n]
as
{1,2,\ldots,n}
.
Let
B\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a non-empty set and
x,y\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, in this work, we consider the partial order
x\leq_{B}y
if
y-x\in B
. Given an affine semigroup
S\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
minimally generated by the set
\{n_{1},\ldots n_{t},n_{t+1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
, we denote by
E
and
A
the sets
\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}
and
\{n_{t+1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
, respectively. Hereafter, we assume that
n_{i}=\mathrm{mult}_{i}(S)
for any
i\in[t]
. That implies
E
is the set of
i
-multiplicities of
S
.

Recall that a subset
P
of an affine semigroup
S
is an ideal of
S
if
P+S\subseteq P
. Given a set
B
, we say that a non-empty subset
X
of
B
is
B
-incomparable if
x-x^{\prime}\notin B
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
distinct from each other (see
[
9
]
). For instance, in the context of an affine semigroup
S
, given a non-empty subset
X
of
S
, the set
Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(X)
is
S
-incomparable.
If
X
is a non-empty subset of an affine semigroup
S
, then
X+S
is an ideal of
S
. We mention this case because every ideal of
S
can be expressed in this way. This expression is not unique; that is, two different finite subsets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
S
could exist such that
X_{1}+S=X_{2}+S
. One such example is to consider
X_{2}=Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(X_{1})
. Just as it occurs for numerical semigroups, we can achieve the desired uniqueness by imposing additional conditions. The following theorem generalises to affine semigroups Theorem 5 in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
S
be an affine semigroup. Then,

\{X+S\mid X\text{ is }S\text{-incomparable}\}

is the set formed by all the ideals of
S
. Moreover, if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are different
S
-incomparable sets, then
X_{1}+S\neq X_{2}+S
.

In fact, for any ideal
P
of an affine semigroup
S
, there exists a unique
S
-incomparable subset
X
of
S
such that
P=X+S
. Using the terminology given in
[
9
]
,
X
is the ideal minimal system of generators of
P
, denoted by
imsg_{S}(P)
. The following lemma generalises to affine semigroups Proposition 6 in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
P
be an ideal of an affine semigroup
S
. Then,
imsg_{S}(P)
is equal to
Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(P)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in P
. Note that
x\in Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(P)
if and only if there does not exist
y\in P\setminus\{x\}
such that
x\leq_{S}y
, which is equivalent to
x\in imsg_{S}(P)
.
∎

If
S
is a numerical semigroup, and
P
is an ideal of
S
, then
P\cup\{0\}
is a numerical semigroup; the key of this result is that
\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid x>\min(P)+\mathrm{Fb}(S)\}\subset P
. Nevertheless, the above statement extended to affine semigroups is not true.
For example, consider
S=\mathbb{N}^{2}
and its ideal
P=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid x\neq 0\}
. Trivially,
P\cup\{0\}
is not affine. This raises the problem of determining when the ideal of a non-numerical affine semigroup provides an affine one. The next lemma solves this question. Its proof can be obtained directly from
[
3
, Corollary 2.10]
.

Lemma 3
.

Given an affine semigroup
S
, and
P
an ideal of
S
,
P\cup\{0\}
is an affine semigroup if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{P}
is an affine cone.

From this fact, we can prove the following result.

Lemma 4
.

Given an affine semigroup
S
, and
P
an ideal of
S
, then
P\cup\{0\}
is an affine semigroup and
\mathcal{C}_{P}=\mathcal{C}_{S}
if and only if there exists
Y\subset P\setminus\{0\}
such that
Y\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[t]
.

Proof.

If
P\cup\{0\}
is an affine semigroup and
\mathcal{C}_{P}=\mathcal{C}_{S}
, then
P\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[t]
. It is enough to take
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{t}\}
, with
y_{i}\notin P\cap\tau_{i}
. Conversely, if there exists
Y\subset P\setminus\{0\}
such that,
Y\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[t]
, then
\mathcal{C}_{P}=\mathcal{C}_{S}
. By applying Lemma
3
,
P\cup\{0\}
is an affine semigroup.
∎

Recall that an affine semigroup
S
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if the complement of
S
in
\mathcal{C}_{S}
is finite. Lemmas
3
and
4
can be refined to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups. Given any subset
B\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
,
\langle B\rangle
denotes the set
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\mid k,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{N},\mbox{ and }b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B\right\}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, and let
P
be an ideal of
S
. If
P\cup\{0\}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, then
\mathcal{C}_{P}=\mathcal{C}_{S}
.

Proof.

Let
P
be an ideal of
S
, then
\mathcal{C}_{P}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{S}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{P}\neq\mathcal{C}_{S}
, then there exists
\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
such that
\tau\notin\mathcal{C}_{P}=\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\rangle
. Consider
x\in P
, since
P
is an ideal of
S
, we obtain that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x+k\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}
, for some
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Taking into account
x=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\gamma_{i}\alpha_{i}
for some
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
, and by choosing
k
large enough such that
\lambda_{i}>\gamma_{i}
for all
i\in[r]
, it follows that
k\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda_{i}-\gamma_{i})\alpha_{i}
, contradicting that
\tau\notin\mathcal{C}_{P}
.
∎

Proposition 6
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, and let
P
be an ideal of
S
. Then,
P\cup\{0\}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if and only if there exists a finite set
Y\subset P\setminus\{0\}
such that
Y\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
, for all
i\in[t]
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5
, if
P\cup\{0\}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, then there exists
Y\subset P\setminus\{0\}
such that
Y\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
, for any
i\in[t]
.
Conversely, assume that there exists a finite subset
Y
of
P\setminus\{0\}
such that,
Y\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[t]
. By applying Lemma
4
,
\mathcal{C}_{P}=\mathcal{C}_{S}
, and
P\cup\{0\}
is an affine semigroup. We point out that
Y+S\subseteq P
. To prove that
\mathcal{H}(P)
is finite, and taking into account that
\mathcal{C}_{S}=S\sqcup\mathcal{H}(S)
where
\mathcal{H}(S)
is a finite subset, it suffices to show that the cardinality of
\mathcal{C}_{S}\setminus\left(Y+\mathcal{C}_{S}\right)
is finite (
\sharp(B)
denotes the cardinality of any set
B
). Let
\mathcal{C}_{S}=\langle b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\rangle
, then, for each
i\in[m]
, there exists a positive integer
k_{i}
such that
k_{i}b_{i}\in\langle Y\rangle
. Since
x\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
if and only if
x=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}b_{i}
, thus,
\sharp\left(\mathcal{C}_{S}\setminus\big(Y+\mathcal{C}_{S}\big)\right)=\sharp\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}\leq k_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[m]\}<\infty
. Hence,
P\cup\{0\}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup.
∎

2
I(S)
-semigroups and its associated tree

Let
S
be an affine semigroup. Recall that an affine semigroup
T
is an
I(S)
-semigroup of
S
if
T\setminus\{0\}
is an ideal of
S
. We can easily rewrite Proposition
6
from this definition.

Corollary 7
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, and let
T
be an
I(S)
-semigroup. Then,
T
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if and only if there exists
Y\subset T\setminus\{0\}
such that
Y\cap\tau_{i}\neq\emptyset
, for all
i\in[t]
.

As an immediate consequence of Corollary
7
, the analysis of
I(S)
-semigroups is equivalent to the study of ideals of
S
meeting the conditions outlined in Proposition
6
. Furthermore, as observed in numerical semigroups (see
[
9
]
), given a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
,
T
is an
I(S)
-semigroup if and only if
T\subseteq S\subseteq T\cup PF(T)
, where
PF(S)
is the set of pseudo-Frobenius elements defined as
\{x\in\mathcal{H}(S)\mid x+(S\setminus\{0\})\subset S\}
. In the context of
I(S)
-semigroups, we interpret Lemma
2
as follows.

Lemma 8
.

Let
S
be an affine semigroup and let
T
be an
I(S)
-semigroup. Then,
imsg_{S}(T\setminus\{0\})
is finite. Moreover,
imsg_{S}(T\setminus\{0\})=Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(msg(T))
.

Proof.

By applying Lemma
2
it is suffices to prove
Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(msg(T))\subseteq Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(T\setminus\{0\})
. Let
x\in T\setminus\{0\}
such that
x=y+s
, for some
s\in S
and
y\in T\setminus\{0\}
. If
x\notin msg(T)
, then clearly
x\notin Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(msg(T))
. If
x\in msg(T)
, then
s\in S\setminus T
and necessarily
y\in msg(T)
and thus
x\notin Minimals_{\leq_{S}}(msg(T))
.
∎

This section shows how the set of all
I(S)
-semigroups can be arranged in a tree, drawing inspiration from
[
9
]
. From now on, we fixed a monomial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, and let
\mathcal{J(S)}=\{T\mid T\text{ is an $I(S)$-semigroup}\}
.

The following results are essential to obtain the announcement tree, and it has a straightforward proof (see
[
9
, Lemma 27]
). Given
A
and
B
two subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that
A\setminus B
is finite,
\mathcal{O}_{A}(B)
is defined as
\max_{\preceq}(A\setminus B)
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and
T
be a non-proper
I(S)
-semigroup. Then,
T\cup\{\mathcal{O}_{S}(T)\}\in\mathcal{J(S)}
.

We define
G(\mathcal{J(S)})
, the associated graph to
\mathcal{J(S)}
, in the
following way: the set of vertices of
G(\mathcal{J(S)})
is
\mathcal{J(S)}
and
(T_{1},T_{2})\in\mathcal{J(S)}\times\mathcal{J(S)}
is an edge if
T_{2}=T_{1}\cup\{\mathcal{O}_{S}(T_{1})\}
. When
(T_{1},T_{2})
is an edge, we say that
T_{1}
is a child of
T_{2}
.

From Lemma 8 in
[
7
]
, we deduce that given an affine semigroup
S
and an element
x
of
S
, then
S\setminus\{x\}
is an affine semigroup if and only if
x\in msg(S)
. This characterisation can be translated to
I(S)
-semigroup as follows.

Lemma 10
.

Let
S
be an affine semigroup,
T
be an
I(S)
-semigroup, and
x\in msg(T)
. Then
T\setminus\{x\}
is an
I(S)
-semigroup if and only if
x\in imsg_{S}(T\setminus\{0\})
.

Proof.

This is followed by arguing as in
[
9
, Lemma 33]
.
∎

Theorem 11
.

For any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
,
G(\mathcal{J(S)})
is a tree with root
S
. Furthermore, the set of children of any
T\in\mathcal{J(S)}
is the set

\{T\setminus\{x\}\mid x\in imsg_{S}(T\setminus\{0\})\text{ and }x\succ\mathcal{O}_{S}(T)\}.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{J(S)}
. We
consider the sequence of
I(S)
-semigroups
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by
T_{i+1}=T_{i}\cup\{\mathcal{O}_{S}(T_{i})\}
. Considering that
S\setminus T
is finite,
a unique path exists connecting
T
with
S
, defined using the above sequence. Regarding the second assertion. If
B
a child of
T
then,
T=B\cup\{\mathcal{O}_{S}(B)\}
, thus by Lemma
10
,
B=T\setminus\{\mathcal{O}_{S}(B)\}
is an
I(S)
-semigroup and,
\mathcal{O}_{S}(B)\in imsg_{S}(T\setminus\{0\})
. By definition,
\mathcal{O}_{S}(B)\neq\mathcal{O}_{S}(T)
. If
\mathcal{O}_{S}(B)\prec\mathcal{O}_{S}(T)
, then by the maximality of
B
,
\mathcal{O}_{S}(T)\in B
, which it is not possible. Conversely, suppose that
B=T\setminus\{x\}
is an
I(S)
-semigroup with
x\succ\mathcal{O}_{S}(T)
, whence
\mathcal{O}_{S}(B)=\max_{\preceq}(S\setminus B)=\max_{\preceq}\{\max_{\preceq}(S\setminus T),x\}=x.

∎

The above result can be used to recurrently build
G(\mathcal{J(S)})
. Let us prove that it is indeed infinite.

Proposition 12
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, and
g
be an integer greater than or equal to
g(S)
. Then, at least one
I(S)
-semigroup
T
with genus
g
exists.

Proof.

Let
E_{0}=\cup_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{mult}_{i}(S)=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}
, and
T_{0}=E_{0}+S
be an
I(S)
-semigroup. If
g(T_{0})=g
we have already finished. Otherwise
g(T_{0})<g
, we consider the sequence of
I(S)
-semigroups
\{T_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

4
Ideals of semigroups and affine MED-semigroups

From now on, let
S\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be an affine semigroup with
t
extremal rays, and minimally generated by
E\sqcup A
with
E=\cup_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{mult}_{i}(S)=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}
, and
A=\{n_{t+1},\ldots,n_{r}\}
. In this section, we consider the ideals of affine semigroups
M+S
with
M=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\}
a finite subset of
S
such that
m_{i}\in\tau_{i}\setminus\{0\}
for any
i\in[t]
. This kind of ideals allows us to generalise the concept of maximal embedding dimension from numerical semigroup to affine semigroups. Let us start with some necessary definitions and results.

Recall that the Apery set of
S
respect to
m\in S
is the set
\mathrm{Ap}(S,m)=\{s\in S\mid s-m\notin S\}
. For any non-numerical affine semigroup, this set is not finite, but the intersection
\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,p_{i})
is finite for any fixed elements
p_{i}\in\tau_{i}\cap S
. Note that
A\subset\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{i})
, that is,
E\sqcup\big(\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{i})\setminus\{0\}\big)
is a generating set of
S
.

Definition 18
.

Given
S\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
an affine semigroup minimally generated by
E\sqcup A
,
S
is a maximal embedding dimension affine semigroup (MED-semigroup) if
\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{i})=A\sqcup\{0\}
.

These semigroups can be characterised by their minimal generating sets.

Proposition 19
.

The affine semigroup
S
is a MED-semigroup if and only if for any
i,j\in[t+1,r]
, there exists
k\in[t]
such that
n_{i}+n_{j}-n_{k}\in S
.

Proof.

Assume that
S
is a MED-semigroup. Thus, any non-zero
m\in S
satisfies that
m-n_{k}\notin S
for every
k\in[t]
if and only if
m\in A
. Since the elements in
A
are all minimal generators, we have that
n_{i}+n_{j}\notin\cap_{k\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{k})
for any
i,j\in[t+1,r]
.

Conversely, let
m
be an element belonging to
\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{i})
, and consider that for all
i,j\in[t+1,r]
there exists at least an integer
k\in[t]
such that
n_{i}+n_{j}-n_{k}\in S
. Hence,
m=\sum_{q=t+1}^{r}\lambda_{q}n_{q}
for some
\lambda_{t+1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
. Our hypothesis means that
\sum_{q=t+1}^{r}\lambda_{q}=1
, and so Proposition holds.
∎

The following result determines the relationship between affine MED-semigroups and the affine ideals.
In addition, this lemma provides a method to construct an arbitrary number of affine MED-semigroups.

By construction,

The next proposition shows a family of MED-semigroups determined from its minimal generating set and its associated sets
S_{i}
. Note that the fixed conditions imply that
S_{0}=\{0\}
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
S
be an affine semigroup minimally generated by
E\sqcup A
with
E=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}
, and such that for any
m\in A
,
m-n_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
for some
n_{k}\in E
. If the set
S_{k}
is a semigroup, then
S
is a MED-semigroup.

Proof.

Given any
m,n\in A
, we have that
m-n_{k},n-n_{k}\in S_{k}
. Since
S_{k}
is a semigroup,
m-n_{k}+n-n_{k}\in S_{k}
. Hence,
m+n-n_{k}\in S
. By Proposition
19
, we conclude
S
is a MED-semigroup.
∎

Example 29
.

Let
S
be again the MED-semigroup considered in Example
23
. Note that
S
satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
28
since
(8,2)-(5,1),(9,2)-(5,1),(12,3)-(5,1)\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
, and
S_{1}
is a semigroup (in particular,
S_{1}
is equal to
\mathcal{C}_{S}
).

We have seen two distinct families of MED-semigroups. As far as the authors are aware, no additional constructions have been identified. We threw the open problem of whether more distinct constructions exist beyond those already proposed for obtaining MED-semigroups.

5
Apery sets and the membership problem for affine semigroups

In this section, we show an algorithm for checking whether an integer vector belongs to an affine simplicial semigroup using some of its Apery sets. For some algorithms appearing in this work, we need to be able to compute the set
\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,m_{i})
for
M=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\}\subset S
such that
m_{i}\in\tau_{i}\setminus\{0\}
for any
i\in[t]
. To compute the intersection
\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,m_{i})
, the following lemma can be used, which has a straightforward proof.
Alternative methods using Gröbner basis techniques to compute this intersection are discussed in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
. For these methods, you have to introduce a new variable for each element in
M
which is not a minimal generator of
S
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
S
be an affine semigroup, and
M=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\}\subset S
such that
m_{i}\in\tau_{i}\setminus\{0\}
for any
i\in[t]
. Then,
\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,m_{i})=\{s\in\Gamma\mid s-m_{i}\notin S,\forall i\in[t]\}
.

Example 31
.

Let
S
be again the affine semigroup considered in Example
23
. By Lemma
30
, we have that
\mathrm{Ap}(S,(5,1))\cap\mathrm{Ap}(S,(6,2))=\{(0,0),(8,2),(9,2),(12,3)\}
. Hence, as we have just known,
S
is a MED-semigroup.

Coming back to the membership problem, note that for any
x\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
such that
x-n\notin S
for all
n\in E
, there are only two possibilities:
x\notin S
or
x\in\cap_{i\in[t]}\mathrm{Ap}(S,n_{i})
, i.e.
x
belongs to
S
. This simple fact lets us introduce Algorithm
4
for checking if an element in
\mathcal{C}_{S}
belongs to
S
.

Algorithm 4

Checking the membership problem for a simplicial affine semigroup.

Input:

A
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
minimally generated by

, and
x\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
.

Output:

True if

, false in the other case.

1

2

3

4

for

i\in[1,t]

do

5

then

7

True

9

12

False

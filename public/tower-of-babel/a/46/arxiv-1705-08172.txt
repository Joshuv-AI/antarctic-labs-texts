Abstract
. We give a description of Nurowski’s conformal structure for some examples of bracket-generating rank 2 distributions in dimension 5, aka ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) -distributions, namely the An-Nurowski circle twistor distribution for pairs of surfaces of constant Gauss curvature rolling without slipping or twisting over each other. In the case of hyperboloid surfaces whose curvature ratios give maximally symmetric ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) -distributions, we find the change of coordinates that map the conformal structure to the flat metric of Engel. We also consider a rank 3 3 Pfaffian system in dimension 5 with S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) symmetry obtained by rotating two of the 1-forms in the Pfaffian system of the spheres rolling distribution, and discuss complexifications of such distributions.

This Pfaffian system annihilates the vector fields

\displaystyle X^{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+E^{2},\qquad X^{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\cos\left(3x\right)E^{1}-3\sin\left(3x\right)E^{3},

which span the real distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Reversing signs in

and
o_{2}

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{R}}
to be the Pfaffian system given by the 1-forms

\displaystyle\tilde{o}_{1}=\sigma_{1}+\cos(3x){\rm d}q,\qquad\tilde{o}_{2}=\sigma_{2}+{\rm d}x,\qquad\tilde{o}_{3}=o_{3},

which annihilate the “sign-reversed” distribution
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The associated real metric given by

\displaystyle g_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=-2(o_{4}o_{4}+o_{5}o_{5})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{o}_{1}\tilde{o}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{o}_{2}\tilde{o}_{2}-\frac{1}{6}\tilde{o}_{3}\tilde{o}_{3}

is conformally flat.
We have the following theorem:

Theorem 4.3
.

A representative metric of Nurowski’s conformal class associated to the maximally symmetric real distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{R}}
given by the span of

\displaystyle X^{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+E^{2},\qquad X^{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\cos\left(3x\right)E^{1}-3\sin\left(3x\right)E^{3},

is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle g_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=-2(o_{4}o_{4}+o_{5}o_{5})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{o}_{1}\tilde{o}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{o}_{2}\tilde{o}_{2}-\frac{1}{6}\tilde{o}_{3}\tilde{o}_{3}

\displaystyle=\chi_{1}^{2}+\chi_{2}^{2}-(\chi_{3}^{2}+\chi_{4}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2})-\frac{1}{6}\omega_{3}^{2},

where

\tilde{o}_{1}
,
\tilde{o}_{2}
,
\tilde{o}_{3}
are 1-forms in the “sign-reversed” Pfaffian system
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, which span the complementary bundle
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\perp}
. The conformal structure
[g_{\mathbb{R}}]
is conformally flat and the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{R}}
has the split real form of
G_{2}
as its group of symmetries.

Likewise for the metric obtained in Theorem
4.3
, we are able to find an Einstein representative in the conformal class. It is given by the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a non-zero real constant and
\hat{g}=-\frac{6}{\lambda\cos(\theta)^{2}}g_{\mathbb{R}}
be the conformally rescaled metric of

in (

).
Then

. In particular,
\hat{g}
is an Einstein metric with constant scalar curvature

.

Proposition 5.2
.

The flat conformal structure

determined by

6.
Doubling the
SU(2)
symmetry

Under symmetry considerations, we can also generalise the results in Section
4
on
SU(2)
Pfaffian systems by supposing that the sphere element
o_{4}o_{4}+o_{5}o_{5}
arises from another copy of
SU(2)
. Let
(\psi,\theta,\phi,v,x,q,z)
be coordinates parametrising
S^{3}\times S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, with the left-invariant 1-forms on the second copy of
S^{3}
given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{4}

\displaystyle=\sin(v){\rm d}x-\cos(v)\sin(x){\rm d}q,

\displaystyle\sigma_{5}

\displaystyle=\cos(v){\rm d}x+\sin(v)\sin(x){\rm d}q,

\displaystyle\sigma_{6}

\displaystyle=-{\rm d}v-\cos(x){\rm d}q.

We shall take

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=\sigma_{2}+i\sigma_{1},\qquad\tau_{2}=\sigma_{2}-i\sigma_{1},\qquad\tau_{4}=\sigma_{5}+i\sigma_{4},\qquad\tau_{5}=\sigma_{5}-i\sigma_{4}.

We have

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=e^{i\psi}({\rm d}\theta-i\sin(\theta){\rm d}\phi),\qquad\tau_{2}=e^{-i\psi}({\rm d}\theta+i\sin(\theta){\rm d}\phi),

\displaystyle\tau_{4}

\displaystyle=e^{iv}({\rm d}x-i\sin(x){\rm d}q),\qquad\tau_{5}=e^{-iv}({\rm d}x+i\sin(x){\rm d}q).

Consider the rank
3
Pfaffian system on
S^{3}\times S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\eta_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}+i\sigma_{1}+\alpha e^{-i(v-\psi+z)}(\sigma_{5}+i\sigma_{4}),

\displaystyle\eta_{2}=\bar{\eta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}-i\sigma_{1}+\alpha e^{i(v-\psi+z)}(\sigma_{5}-i\sigma_{4}),

\displaystyle\eta_{3}

\displaystyle=-{\rm d}(v-\psi+z)+\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{6}.

The 1-forms annihilate the vector fields spanned by

\displaystyle\partial_{z}-\alpha(E^{1}-iE^{2})+\gamma E^{3}+e^{i(\psi-z-v)}(E^{4}-iE^{5})+(\gamma+1)E^{6}

and its complex conjuate. Here
\gamma
is a constant.
Let
M^{5}\subset S^{3}\times S^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
be the submanifold given by
\{z=v=\psi\}
, and the 1-forms when restricted to
M^{5}
give

\displaystyle\eta_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}+i\sigma_{1}+\alpha e^{-iz}(\sigma_{5}+i\sigma_{4})=\sigma_{2}+i\sigma_{1}+\alpha({\rm d}x-i\sin(x){\rm d}q),

(6.1)

\displaystyle\eta_{2}=\bar{\eta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}-i\sigma_{1}+\alpha e^{iz}(\sigma_{5}-i\sigma_{4})=\sigma_{2}-i\sigma_{1}+\alpha({\rm d}x+i\sin(x){\rm d}q),

\displaystyle\eta_{3}

\displaystyle=-{\rm d}z+\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{6}=-{\rm d}z-\cos(\theta){\rm d}\phi+\cos(x){\rm d}q,

where we have used the fact that on
M^{5}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{4}

\displaystyle=\sin(z){\rm d}x-\cos(z)\sin(x){\rm d}q,

\displaystyle\sigma_{5}

\displaystyle=\cos(z){\rm d}x+\sin(z)\sin(x){\rm d}q,

\displaystyle\sigma_{6}

\displaystyle=-{\rm d}z-\cos(x){\rm d}q.

We have the following theorem:

Theorem 6.1
.

On
M^{5}
, the
(2,3,5)
-distribution associated to the rank 3 Pfaffian system spanned by the 1-forms
\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}\}
in (
6
) is maximally symmetric when
\alpha^{2}=\frac{1}{9}
or
\alpha^{2}=9
, i.e. when
\alpha=\pm\frac{1}{3}
or
\pm 3
. In the case when
\alpha=\pm\frac{1}{3}
, a representative metric in Nurowski’s conformal structure associated to this distribution is given by

(6.2)

\displaystyle g=9\eta_{1}\eta_{2}+3\eta_{3}\eta_{3}-18\tau_{1}\tau_{2}+2\tau_{4}\tau_{5}.

In the case when
\alpha=\pm 3
, a representative metric in Nurowski’s conformal structure associated to this distribution is given by

\displaystyle g=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}+3\eta_{3}\eta_{3}+2\tau_{1}\tau_{2}-18\tau_{4}\tau_{5}.

The two metrics are equivalent via the substitution

(z,\theta,\phi,x,q)\mapsto(-z,x,q,\theta,\phi)
. Both are conformally flat with vanishing Weyl tensor. Under a conformal rescaling,
the first (resp. second) metric

\displaystyle\hat{g}=\frac{4}{\cos(\theta)^{2}}g\qquad\left(resp.~\hat{g}=\frac{4}{\cos(x)^{2}}g\right)

is Einstein with

\displaystyle\text{Ric}\left(\hat{g}\right)=\frac{1}{12}\hat{g},\qquad\left(resp.~\text{Ric}\left(\hat{g}\right)=\frac{1}{12}\hat{g}\right).

From this form of the metric, we can readily recover the 1-forms of the
SU(2)
Pfaffian system in Section
4
. The distribution spanned by the kernel of the 1-forms also annihilate the combinations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\left(\eta_{1}-\bar{\eta}_{1}\right)=\sigma_{1}-\alpha\sin(x){\rm d}q,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta_{1}+\bar{\eta}_{1}\right)=\sigma_{2}+\alpha{\rm d}x,\qquad\eta_{3}=\sigma_{3}+\cos(x){\rm d}q.

This Pfaffian system is invariant under the substitution
(x,q,\alpha)\mapsto(-x,q,-\alpha)
.

To relate the distribution back to the rolling surfaces distribution of An and Nurowski, observe that the elements given by
18\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
and
2\tau_{4}\tau_{5}
in (
6.2
) describe the metrics on a pair of spheres. The element with coefficient
2
corresponds to the sphere of radius
\sqrt{2}
while the element with coefficient
18
corresponds to the sphere of radius
3\sqrt{2}
. The ratio of their curvatures are in the form
9:1
.

Replacing
z
by
\psi
, we shall let

\displaystyle E^{4}

\displaystyle=\sin(\psi)\partial_{x}+\cos(\psi)\left(\cot(x)\partial_{\psi}-\frac{1}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q}\right),

\displaystyle E^{5}

\displaystyle=\cos(\psi)\partial_{x}-\sin(\psi)\left(\cot(x)\partial_{\psi}-\frac{1}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q}\right).

The vector fields
E^{3}
,
E^{4}
,
E^{5}
form another set of left-invariant vector fields of
SU(2)

The vector fields annihilating the 1-forms (
6

(6.3)

\displaystyle X^{1}

\displaystyle=2\cot(x)E^{3}-\alpha(E^{1}-iE^{2})+e^{-i\psi}(E^{4}-iE^{5}),

(6.4)

\displaystyle X^{2}=\bar{X}^{1}

\displaystyle=2\cot(x)E^{3}-\alpha(E^{1}+iE^{2})+e^{i\psi}(E^{4}+iE^{5}).

Using the fact that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}=\sin(\psi)E^{4}+\cos(\psi)E^{5}\text{~and~}-\frac{1}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q}-\cot(x)E^{3}=\cos(\psi)E^{4}-\sin(\psi)E^{5},

we find that

gives the vector field

\displaystyle\partial_{x}-\alpha E^{2},

while

gives the vector field

\displaystyle\partial_{q}+\alpha\sin(x)E^{1}-\cos(x)E^{3}.

This pair of vector fields describe a
su(2)
-valued connection over the sphere with coordinates
(x,q)
.

We can interpret the complex vector fields
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
that span the distribution
\mathcal{D}
as determining a non-Abelian

spin connection on a rank 2 spinor bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
over
\Sigma^{\prime 2}

(see
[

\mathcal{E}
over
\Sigma^{2}
with
\Sigma\hookrightarrow SU(2)\subset\mathcal{E}
.

We show the following:

Theorem 6.2
.

By passing to the An-Nurowski circle twistor distribution over the product of two spheres
\Sigma^{2}\times\Sigma^{\prime 2}
, the
su(2)
-valued connection over the sphere

\Sigma^{\prime 2}
with coordinates
(x,q)
given by

\displaystyle\partial_{x}-\alpha E^{2},\qquad\partial_{q}+\alpha\sin(x)E^{1}-\cos(x)E^{3},

can be rotated to a
su(2)
-valued connection over the other sphere
\Sigma^{2}
with coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
and components given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\alpha}E^{5},\qquad\partial_{\phi}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sin(\theta)E^{4}-\cos(\theta)E^{3}.

Proof.

The rotated connection can be obtained in the following way. Let
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
be the vector fields (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), which define the connection over
\Sigma^{\prime 2}
. Take the combinations

\displaystyle Y^{1}=\frac{1}{2}(e^{i\psi}X^{1}+e^{-i\psi}X^{2}),\quad Y^{2}=\frac{1}{2i}(e^{i\psi}X^{1}-e^{-i\psi}X^{2}).

Then we have the vector fields

\displaystyle-\sin(2\psi)Y^{1}+\cos(2\psi)Y^{2}={}

\displaystyle\alpha\partial_{\theta}+\cot(x)\sin(\psi)\partial_{\psi}-\cos(\psi)\partial_{x}+\frac{\sin(\psi)}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q},

\displaystyle\cos(2\psi)Y^{1}+\sin(2\psi)Y^{2}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\phi}-(\cot(x)\cos(\psi)+\alpha\cot(\theta))\partial_{\psi}

\displaystyle-\sin(\psi)\partial_{x}-\frac{\cos(\psi)}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q}.

Now let
\tilde{\psi}=-\psi
. This gives
\partial_{\tilde{\psi}}=-\partial_{\psi}
and we have

\displaystyle\sin(2\tilde{\psi})Y^{1}+\cos(2\tilde{\psi})Y^{2}={}

\displaystyle\alpha\partial_{\theta}+\cot(x)\sin(\tilde{\psi})\partial_{\tilde{\psi}}-\cos(\tilde{\psi})\partial_{x}-\frac{\sin(\tilde{\psi})}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q},

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\alpha\partial_{\theta}-E^{5},

\displaystyle\cos(2\tilde{\psi})Y^{1}-\sin(2\tilde{\psi})Y^{2}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\phi}+(\cot(x)\cos(\tilde{\psi})+\alpha\cot(\theta))\partial_{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle+\sin(\tilde{\psi})\partial_{x}-\frac{\cos(\tilde{\psi})}{\sin(x)}\partial_{q},

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\phi}+E^{4}+\alpha\cot(\theta)\partial_{\tilde{\psi}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\phi}+E^{4}-\alpha\cot(\theta)E^{3}.

This combination gives, up to proportionality, the vector fields

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\alpha}E^{5}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{\phi}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sin(\theta)E^{4}-\cos(\theta)E^{3},

which define the connection over
\Sigma^{2}
.

Analytic characterization of high dimension weighted special atom spaces
Eddy Kwessi, and, Geraldo de Souza

Abstract
Special atom spaces have been around for quite awhile since the introduction of atoms by R. Coifman in his seminal paper who led to another proof that the dual of the Hardy space H 1 H^{1} is in fact the space of functions of bounded means oscillations (BMO). Special atom spaces enjoy quite a few attributes of their own, among which the fact that they have an analytic extension to the unit disc. Recently, an extension of special atom spaces to higher dimensions was proposed, making ripe the possible exploration of the above extension in higher dimensions. In this paper we propose an analytic characterization of special atom spaces in higher dimensions.

Introduction

Let
d
be some positive integer. We define the unit disk as

\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|<1\}
, the sphere as
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|=1\}
, the polydisk and polysphere respectively as
\mathbb{D}^{d}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Atoms were introduced by Coifman in
[
5
]
as a tool to explicitly represent functions in the Hardy space
H^{p}
for
0<p\leq 1
. The following definition was proposed:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and an interval
J
of
\mathbb{R}
. An atom is a function
b
defined on the interval
J
and satisfying

1.

\left|b(\xi)\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{\left|J\right|^{1/p}}
.

2.

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi^{k}b(\xi)d\xi=0
,  for
0\leq k\leq\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]-1
, where
[x]
is the integer part of
x
.

From this definition, functions in
H^{p}(\mathbb{R})
could now be characterized via their atomic decomposition in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2

(see
[
5
]
)
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
. Then
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if there exist real
numbers
a_{i}
, atoms
b_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and absolute constants
c,C
such that such that

\displaystyle f(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}b_{i}(\xi)\quad\mbox{and}\quad c\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p}}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left|a_{i}\right|^{p}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p}}\;.

Fefferman
[
8
]
observed that this result is in fact due to the duality between
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and the space of functions of bounded means oscillations (BMO), therefore providing another proof that the dual space of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is in fact BMO. The era of the atomic decomposition therefore started. One criticism of the atomic decomposition at the time was that it was too general, making it difficult or not very useful for applications. This atomic decomposition was proved to be quite useful in harmonic analysis. However, in an attempt to answer this criticism, Richard O’Neil and Geraldo De Souza proposed an example of atoms defined on the interval
I=[0,1]
that was latter dubbed “special atoms”. This special atom has some very desirable properties as we will see in the sequel.

Definition 1.3
.

Consider
1\leq p<\infty
.

(a)

A
special atom of type 1
is a function
b:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that

b(\xi)=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\dfrac{1}{|J|^{1/p}}\left[\chi_{R}(\xi)-\chi_{L}(\xi)\right]$,&{if $\xi\in J$}\\
1,&{if $\xi\in I\backslash J$ }\end{tabular}\right.\;,

where
J
is a subinterval of
I
,
L
and
R
are the halves of
J
such that
J=L\cup R
, and
|J|
is the length of
J
.

(b)

A
special atom of type 2
is a function
c:J\to\mathbb{R}
such that

c(\xi)=\dfrac{1}{|J|^{1/p}}\left[\chi_{J}(\xi)\right]\;,

where
J
is an interval contained in
I
.

Remark 1.4
.

We observe that this definition can be extended on the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by using dyadic decomposition, see
[
1
]
.

From this definition, they introduced the
special atom space

B^{p}
(for type 1 atom) defined on
J
, but with a different norm from the
L^{p}
-norm.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. The special atom space
B^{p}
(of type 1) is defined as

B^{p}=\left\{f:I\to\mathbb{R};f(\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}b_{n}(\xi);\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\alpha_{n}\right|<\infty\right\}\;,

where the
b_{n}
’s are special atoms of type 1. The space
B^{p}
is endowed with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{B^{p}}=\inf\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\alpha_{n}\right|,

where the infimum is taken over all representations of
f
.

The weighted special atom soon followed (see for instance
[
3
]
) which gave rise a host of very interesting properties, namely the analytic characterization.

Definition 1.6
.

We define the
weighted special atom (of type 1)
on
J
as:

b_{w}(\xi)=\frac{1}{w(J)}\left[\chi_{{}_{R}}(\xi)-\chi_{{}_{L}}(\xi)\right]\;,

where

w\in L^{1}(I)\quad\mbox{with}\quad w(J)=\int_{J}w(\xi)d\xi,\quad\mbox{and $L,R$ are as in Definition \ref{defn2}}\;.

The weighted special atom space is the space
B_{w}
of functions
f
with atomic decomposition

f(\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}b_{w,n}(\xi)\;,

endowed with the Infimum norm.

The importance of this definition can not be overstated. Indeed, weighted special atom spaces are invariant under the Hilbert transform and they contain some functions whose Fourier series diverge, see
[
7
]
. One of their most applicable features is their connection to Haar wavelets, in that, a Haar wavelet function is just a special atom with weight
2^{-n/2}
,
[
13
]
. Weighted special atom spaces are also Banach equivalent to some Bergman-Besov-Lipschitz spaces (see
[
6
]
), which leads to a complete characterization of their lacunary functions, see
[
12
]
. Moreover, functions in
B_{w}
have analytic correspondences by integrating against analytic functions whose real parts coincide with the Poisson kernel. In particular,
B^{1}
is Banach equivalent to the space of analytic functions
F
on the complex unit disc for which
\displaystyle F(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{i\xi}+z}{e^{i\xi}-z}f(\xi)d\xi
. The question that was latter raised by Brett Wick in 2010 (personal communication with the first author) was whether this analytic characterization could be achieved in higher dimensions. In order to entertain such a question, one has to, for
d\geq 2
,

1.

first provide a definition of the special atom and its weighted counterpart on
I^{d}
so that its restriction to
I=[0,1]
is the original special atom.

2.

second, provide a definition of the special atom space
B^{p}
on
I^{d}
.

3.

third, verify that the Banach structure of
B^{p}
is preserved.

4.

fourth, set the conditions on the weight function
w
on
I^{d}
and define the weighted special atom space
B_{w}
on
I^{d}
.

5.

fifth, define the analytic extension
F(\bm{z})
of a function
f(\bm{\xi})\in B_{w}
for

\bm{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{d})\in\mathbb{D}^{d}
and
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{d})\in I^{d}
.

6.

sixth, verify that
B_{w}
and its analytic extension
A_{w}^{1}
are indeed Banach-equivalent under the above conditions.

Remark 1.7
.

The first step was recently accomplished in
[
13
]
. Also the requirement that by restricting to
I=[0,1]
we obtain the original special atom is for simplicity sake. The argument is important in high dimensions to prove for example in the case of Haar wavelets that we obtain an orthonormal system. However, there exist numerous ways to define atoms similar to the special atom.

We end this introductory part by recalling the definition of the weighted Lipschitz class of functions.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
w
be a weight function defined on
J=[a-h,a+h]\subseteq I
. The weighted Lipschitz class is the class of continuous functions defined as

\Lambda_{w}=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}:\left\|f\right\|_{\Lambda_{w}}=\sup_{h>0,\xi}\;\left|\frac{f(\xi+h)+f(\xi-h)-2f(\xi)}{w(J)}\right|<\infty.\right\}

For completeness, recall that for
w(t)=t
,

is the Zygmund class and for

,
\Lambda_{w}
is the Lipschitz class of order
\alpha

that the dual space

of

is
\Lambda_{w}^{\prime}=\left\{f^{\prime}:f\in\Lambda_{w}\right\}
, where

is understood in the sense of distributions.

Main results

Now we can now state our main theorem:

Main Theorem

(b)

is Banach equivalent to
A_{w}^{1}
if and only if
\displaystyle w(\bm{r}e^{i\bm{\xi}})\equiv\frac{w(1-\bm{r})}{1-\bm{r}}
is a product weight and
w\in\pazocal{D}_{1}\cap\pazocal{B}_{2}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The Main theorem essentially states that
B_{w}
and
A_{w}^{1}
are isomorphic as Banach spaces in the sense that

1.

B_{w}
and
A_{w}^{1}
are both Banach spaces,

2.

f\in B_{w}
if and only if its analytic extension
F\in A_{w}^{1}
,

3.

F\in A_{w}^{1}
if and only if
\displaystyle\lim_{\bm{r}\to 1}\mbox{Re}\;F(\bm{r}e^{i\bm{\xi}})\in B_{w}
,

4.

\left\|f\right\|_{B_{w}}\equiv\left\|F\right\|_{A_{w}^{1}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

1.

In the first part of the Main Theorem, the weight function depends only on the argument
\bm{\xi}
of
\bm{z}=\bm{r}e^{i\bm{\xi}}
, whereas in the second part, it depends only on the radius
\bm{r}
. The condition that
w\in\mathscr{B}_{2}
in the first part is weaker than the condition
w\in\pazocal{D}_{1}\cap\pazocal{B}_{2}
in the second part since per Lemma
2.6
,

w\in\pazocal{D}_{1}\cap\pazocal{B}_{2}\subseteq\mathscr{D}=\bigcup\limits_{p>1}\mathscr{B}_{p}

implies the existence of
p>1
such that
w\in\mathscr{B}_{p}
. There is no guarantee that this
p
will be 2 as in the first part. However, what the two parts have in common is the necessary condition that if
B_{w}
is contained in
A_{w}^{1}
, then the weight
w\in\mathscr{D}
.

2.

In the Main Theorem, the weight
w
is a product weight, however general weights
w
defined on
I^{d}
are not addressed in this manuscript and it would be a worthwhile future endeavor to have a holistic understanding of the role of the weight
w
.

The proof of the Main Theorem relies on some crucial lemmas that will be stated below. The first lemma shows that partial derivatives of analytic extensions of special atoms in higher dimensions are bounded. Henceforth, the constants
C
will be generic and when necessary, their dependence on an interval
J
will be specified accordingly.

Part (a): Let

be a product weight such that
w\in\mathscr{B}_{2}

B_{w}\subseteq A_{w}^{1}

are contained in
A_{w}^{1}

b_{w}(\bm{\xi})\in B_{w}
, we have
F\in A_{w}^{1}
where

F(\bm{z})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{I}P(\bm{z},\bm{\xi})b_{w}(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}.

Fix
1\leq j\leq d

d\bm{\xi}^{-j}d\bm{r}^{-j}=d\xi_{1}dr_{1}\cdots d\xi_{j-1}dr_{j-1}d\xi_{j+1}dr_{j+1}\cdots d\xi_{d}dr_{d}

From Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\left|f_{j}(\bm{z})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(J)\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|\prod_{\underset{l\neq j}{l=1}}^{d}\left|K_{2}(a_{l},h_{l},z_{l})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M(J)\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|\quad\mbox{where}\quad M(J)=C(J)C_{2}^{d-1}\;.

Also from Lemma
3.3
above,
K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})
is bounded, for all
1\leq j\leq d
.
Therefore
\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|
exists. Moreover using the definition of

in equation (
2.3

\displaystyle\left|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{1/2}M(J)\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|\;.

(3.2)

We then have that

\displaystyle\int\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}\left|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right|w(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}d\bm{r}={}

\displaystyle\int\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}\left|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right|\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}w_{j}(\xi_{j})\right)d\bm{\xi}d\bm{r}

\displaystyle\leq d^{1/2}M(J)\left(\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}\int\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|w_{j}(\xi_{j})d\xi_{j}dr_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d-1}}\left(\prod_{\underset{l\neq j}{l=1}}^{d}w_{l}(\xi_{l})\right)d\bm{\xi}^{-j}d\bm{r}^{-j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}\left(\int\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|w_{j}(\xi_{j})d\xi_{j}dr_{j}\right)<\infty\;.

This proves that
F\in A_{w}^{1}

Conversely, suppose that
F\in A_{w}^{1}
. We will show that in this case,
w\in\mathscr{B}_{2}
. As above, it suffices to consider analytic extensions
F
of weighted special atoms. Let
C>0
such that
\left\|F\right\|_{A_{w}^{1}}<C

Fix
1\leq j\leq d
. Then we know that
\left\|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right\|_{2}\geq\left\|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right\|_{\infty}\geq\left|f_{j}(\bm{z})\right|
. Therefore

\displaystyle C>\int\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}\left|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right|w(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}d\bm{r}

\displaystyle=\int\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{d}\left|f_{l}(\bm{z})\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}w(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}d\bm{r}

Part (b). Now assume
w

\displaystyle F(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{J}P(\bm{z},\bm{\xi})b_{w}(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle\int\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}\left|F^{\prime}(\bm{z})\right|\frac{w(1-\bm{r})}{1-\bm{r}}d\bm{\xi}d\bm{r}

\displaystyle\leq d^{1/2}M(J)\left(\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}\int\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|\frac{w_{j}(1-r_{j})}{1-r_{j}}d\xi_{j}dr_{j}\right)

By Lemma

\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}\int\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|K_{1}(a_{j},h_{j},z_{j})\right|\frac{w_{j}(1-r_{j})}{1-r_{j}}d\xi_{j}dr_{j}\leq\sup_{1\leq j\leq d}C(J_{j})<\infty\;.

Conversely, suppose that

It remains to show that

and

are norm-equivalent. Since

, there exists a constant

such that

. To obtain the reverse inequality, it suffices to use a simple extension of the one dimension case to obtain that the dual

of

(b):

(c) Since

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

In the proof that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{B_{w}}
is a norm, only the triangle inequality requires special care. Using the definition of the infimum, let
\epsilon>0
and let
\left\{\alpha_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\left\{\beta_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle f(\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{n}b_{w,n}(\xi)
and
\displaystyle g(\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{n}b_{w,n}(\xi)
and
\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n}\right|<\left\|f\right\|_{B_{w}}+\epsilon/2,\penalty\ \sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\beta_{n}\right|<\left\|g\right\|_{B_{w}}+\epsilon/2
.
Hence
\displaystyle(f+g)(\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n})b_{w,n}(\xi)
with
\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}\right|\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n}\right|+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\beta_{n}\right|<\infty\;.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|f+g\right\|_{B_{w}}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n}+\beta_{n}\right|\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n}\right|+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\beta_{n}\right|<\left\|f\right\|_{B_{w}}+\left\|g\right\|_{B_{w}}+\epsilon\;.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that
\left\|f+g\right\|_{B_{w}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{B_{w}}+\left\|g\right\|_{B_{w}}
.

Now, let us prove that
B_{w}
is a Banach space. It will be sufficient to show that every absolutely convergent sequence is convergent. In short, it will be enough to show that given a sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have
\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}\right\|_{B_{w}}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{B_{w}}
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a sequence
\alpha_{n_{k}}
of real numbers such that
\displaystyle f_{n}(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{n_{k}}b_{w,n_{k}}(\xi)
with
\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n_{k}}\right|<\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{B_{w}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2^{n}}
.
Therefore

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\alpha_{n_{k}}\right|<\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{B_{w}}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\epsilon}{2^{n}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{B_{w}}+\epsilon\;.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that

\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}\right\|\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{B_{w}}\;.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle P_{j}^{\prime}(z_{j},\xi_{j})=\frac{\partial P_{j}(z_{j},\xi_{j})}{\partial z_{j}}=\frac{e^{i\xi_{j}}}{(e^{i\xi_{j}}-z_{j})^{2}}
, for
j=1,2,\ldots,d
.

Let us start with
d=2
. Let
J=[a_{1}-h_{1},a_{1}+h_{1}]\times[a_{2}-h_{2},a_{2}+h_{2}]
.

Then

F(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{J}P_{1}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})b_{w}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},

and

\displaystyle f_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{J}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})b_{w}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(J)(2\pi)^{2}}[I_{1}+I_{2}-I_{2}-I_{4}]\;,

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}-h_{1}}^{a_{1}}\int_{a_{2}}^{a_{2}+h_{2}}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},\quad I_{2}=\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{1}+h_{1}}\int_{a_{2}-h_{2}}^{a_{2}}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}-h_{1}}^{a_{1}}\int_{a_{2}-h_{2}}^{a_{2}}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},\quad I_{4}=\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{1}+h_{1}}\int_{a_{2}}^{a_{2}+h_{2}}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\;.

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}-h_{1}}^{a_{1}}\int_{a_{2}}^{a_{2}+h_{2}}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}-\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{1}+h_{1}}\int_{a_{2}}^{a_{2}+h_{2}}P_{1}^{\prime}(z_{1},\xi_{1})P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-K_{1}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})M_{2}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})\;,

where

\displaystyle K_{1}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}-h_{1}}^{a_{1}}\frac{e^{i\xi_{1}}}{(e^{i\xi_{1}}-z_{1})^{2}}d\xi_{1}+\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{1}+h_{1}}\frac{e^{i\xi_{1}}}{(e^{i\xi_{1}}-z_{1})^{2}}d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\left[\frac{1}{z_{1}-e^{i(a_{1}-h_{1})}}+\frac{1}{z_{1}-e^{i(a_{1}+h_{1})}}+\frac{2}{e^{ia_{1}}-z_{1}}\right]\;.

Also,

\displaystyle M_{2}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a_{2}}^{a_{2}+h_{2}}P_{2}(z_{2},\xi_{2})d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\left[-ih_{2}+2\ln(e^{ia_{2}}-z_{2})-2\ln(e^{i(a_{2}-h_{2})}-z_{2})\right]\;.

Likewise, we have

I_{1}-I_{3}=K_{1}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})M_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})
with

M_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})=\frac{1}{i}\left[-ih_{2}-2\ln(e^{ia_{2}}-z_{2})+2\ln(e^{i(a_{2}-h_{2})}-z_{2})\right]\;.

It follows that

I_{1}-I_{4}+I_{2}-I_{3}=K_{1}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})\left[M_{2}^{\prime}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})-M_{2}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})\right]=K_{1}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})K_{2}a_{2},h_{2},z_{2}),

where

K_{2}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})=\frac{2}{i}\left[\ln(e^{i(a_{2}-h_{2})}-z_{2})+\ln(e^{i(a_{2}+h_{2})}-z_{2})-2\ln(e^{ia_{2}}-z_{2})\right]\;.

Hence

\displaystyle f_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})=C(J)K_{1}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})K_{2}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2}),

where
\displaystyle C(J)=\frac{2}{w(J)(2\pi)^{2}}
.
Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{2}(z_{1},z_{2})=C(J)K_{1}(a_{2},h_{2},z_{2})K_{2}(a_{1},h_{1},z_{1})\;.

For
d\geq 2
, we observe that the constant
C(J)
will remain the same, regardless of the variable of differentiation. Moreover, the function
K_{1}
takes as arguments
a_{j},h_{j},z_{j}
if we are differentiating with respect to
z_{j}
and
K_{2}
takes as arguments
a_{l},h_{l},h_{l}
, for all

Conclusion

We have proposed in this paper a space that acts as the analytic extension of the so-called special atom spaces in higher dimensions. What we find interesting and remarkable in these spaces is their apparent simplicity. Certainly one could think of atoms defined on intervals that are different from the characteristic functions on these intervals, say for instance polynomial type of atoms. However, the properties like orthonormality would be difficult to prove, especially in higher dimensions. The results in this paper open the door to exploring lacunary sequences in Bergman-Besov-Lipschitz spaces in higher dimensions. Another idea that is no more far-fetched in the idea of Blaschke-products in these spaces, that are non-complex in their inception but have complex extensions.

Concentration of Measure for the Graphon Particle System
Erhan Bayraktar, Donghan Kim

Abstract
We study heterogeneously interacting diffusive particle systems with mean-field type interaction characterized by an underlying graphon and their finite particle approximations. Under suitable conditions, we obtain exponential concentration estimates over a finite time horizon for both 1 and 2 Wasserstein distances between the empirical measures of the finite particle systems and the averaged law of the graphon system, extending the work of Bayraktar-Wu [ 3 ] .

Introduction

In this article, we study concentration of measures related to the graphon particle system and its finite particle approximations. This work is a continuation of earlier papers
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. A graphon particle system consists of uncountably many heterogeneous particles
X_{u}
for
u\in[0,1]
with which their interactions are characterized by a graphon. More precisely, for a fixed
T>0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the following system

\displaystyle X_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=X_{u}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\big(X_{u}(s),y\big)G(u,v)\,\mu_{v,s}(dy)\,dv+\psi\big(X_{u}(s)\big)\bigg]ds+\sigma B_{u}(t),

\displaystyle\mu_{u,t}\text{ is the probability distribution of }X_{u}(t),\text{ for every }u\in[0,1]\text{ and }t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

where
\{B_{u}\}_{u\in[0,1]}
is a family of i.i.d.
d
-dimensional Brownian motions,
\{X_{u}(0)\}_{u\in[0,1]}
is a collection of independent (but not necessarily identically distributed)
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variables with law
\mu_{u}(0)
, independent of
\{B_{u}\}_{u\in[0,1]}
for each
u\in[0,1]
, defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\},\mathbb{P})
. Two functions
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
represent pairwise interactions between the particles and single particle drift, respectively.
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a constant and
G:[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a graphon, that is, a symmetric measurable function.

Along with the graphon particle system, we introduce two finite particle systems with heterogeneous interactions, which approximate (
1.1
). For a fixed, arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each
i\in[n]:=\{1,\cdots,n\}
, we first consider the not-so-dense analogue of (
1.1
) introduced in Section 4 of
[
2
]

X^{n}_{i}(t)=X_{\frac{i}{n}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\frac{1}{np(n)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi^{n}_{ij}\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)+\psi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s)\big)\Big]ds+\sigma B_{\frac{i}{n}}(t),

(1.2)

where
\{p(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1]
is a sequence of numbers and
\{\xi^{n}_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
are independent Bernoulli random variables satisfying

\xi^{n}_{ij}=\xi^{n}_{ji},\qquad\mathbb{P}(\xi^{n}_{ij}=1)=p(n)G(\frac{i}{n},\,\frac{j}{n}),\qquad\text{for every }i,j\in[n],

independent of
\{B_{i/n},X_{i/n}(0):i\in[n]\}
. Here,
p(n)
represents the global sparsity parameter and the strength of interaction between the particles in (
1.2
) is scaled by
np(n)
, the order of the number of neighbors, as in mean-field systems on Erdös-Rényi random graphs
[
7
,
15
,
27
]
; the convergence of

as

implies that the graph is sparse, but we shall consider the not-so-dense case

of diverging average degree in the random graph.

The other finite particle approximation system is given by

\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)=X_{\frac{i}{n}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi\big(\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s),\bar{X}^{n}_{j}(s)\big)G(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})+\psi\big(\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s)\big)\Big]ds+\sigma B_{\frac{i}{n}}(t).

(1.3)

Since this system has a nonrandom coefficient for the interaction term (but still models heterogeneous interaction via the graphon), it is easier to analyze than the other finite particle system (
1.2
). We note that three systems (
1.1

1.3

Notation

Given a metric space
(S,d)
and a function

, we define

\displaystyle||f||_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\sup\{|f(x)|:x\in S\},

\displaystyle||f||_{Lip}

\displaystyle:=\sup\Big\{\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}:x,y\in S,\,x\neq y\Big\},

\displaystyle||f||_{BL}

\displaystyle:=2\big(||f(x)||_{\infty}+||f||_{Lip}\big),

and we say that

(
||f||_{BL}<\infty
), respectively. In particular,

is called
a
-Lipschitz if

Denote by

on
S

For a separable Banach space

\pi

\nu

S^{n}:=S\times\cdots\times S

||x||_{n,p}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}||x_{i}||^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

(2.11)

for some constants
L>0
and
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
. Let

be the law of

2.6
Bernstein’s inequality and

-Fourier class

When comparing two approximation systems (

) and (

of (

in terms of
\|\cdot\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}
norm, which is from Lemma 2 of
[

]
. Its proof is straightforward application of Bernstein’s inequality (Lemma

entries of the matrix

Lemma 2.9
.

For any

, we have

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}>\eta\bigg]\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\eta^{2}n^{2}p(n)}{2+\frac{\eta}{3}}\bigg).

In particular, under Assumption
2.4
, we have for every

\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}>\eta\bigg]\longrightarrow-\infty,\qquad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Lemma 2.10

(Bernstein’s inequality, Theorem 2.9 of
[
9
]
)
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots X_{k}
be independent random variables with finite variance such that
X_{i}\leq b
for some
b>0
almost surely for each
i\in[k]
. Let
v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]
, then we have

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}(X_{i})\big)\geq u\Big]\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{u^{2}}{2(v+\frac{bu}{3})}\bigg).

When the interaction function
\phi
belongs to a special class of functions, we shall see in the proof of Theorem
3.4
that the distance
W_{1}(L_{n},\bar{L}_{n})
can be easily expressed in terms of the quantity
||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}
. This observation is inspired by the work of
[
27
]
. To state more precisely, we introduce the notion of
L^{1}
-Fourier class of functions.

Definition 2.1
.

Identifying
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belongs to the
L^{1}
-Fourier class
, if there exists a finite complex measure
m_{f}
over
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
such that for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

f(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\exp\Big(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\big\langle(x,y),\,z\big\rangle\Big)m_{f}(dz).

We recall that a finite complex measure
m
over
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is a set function
m:\pazocal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of the form
m=m_{r}^{+}-m_{r}^{-}+\sqrt{-1}(m_{i}^{+}-m_{i}^{-})
, where each
m_{r}^{+},m_{r}^{-},m_{i}^{+},m_{i}^{-}
is a finite,
\sigma
-additive (nonnegative) measure over
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. We define the total mass of
m

||m||_{TM}:=m_{r}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})+m_{r}^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})+m_{i}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})+m_{i}^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

If a function
f
is an inverse
L^{1}
-transform of a function in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then
f
belongs to the
L^{1}
-Fourier class. In particular, any Schwartz function belongs to the
L^{1}
-Fourier class. An example of such function is the Kuramoto interaction; if
d=1
and
\phi(x-y)=K\sin(y-x)
for some constant
K
, then the corresponding complex measure is equal to

m_{\phi}=\frac{K}{2\sqrt{-1}}\big(\delta_{(-1,1)}+\delta_{(1,-1)}\big).

The finite system (
1.2
) of “oscillators” with the Kuramoto interaction function is studied in
[
13
]
.

4.1.1
Proof of Proposition
3.1

Let us recall the identity (
4.7
), along with the notations (
2.3
). Using Hölder’s inequality, there exists
K>0
, depending on
\phi
and
\psi
, such that for every

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|X^{n}_{i}(t)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq KT\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+KT\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{P}^{(n)}_{i,j}\Big(\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)-\phi\big(\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s),\bar{X}^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big)\Big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+KT\int_{0}^{T}\big|X^{n}_{i}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s)\big|^{2}\,ds.

Taking the expectation to the first term, and using the independence of
\{D^{(n)}_{i,j}\}_{j\in[n]}
and the boundedness of
\phi
, we have

\displaystyle KT\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big|^{2}\,ds\leq KT\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(D^{(n)}_{i,j})^{2}]\,ds\leq\frac{KT^{2}}{np(n)}.

For the second term, Hölder’s inequality and the Lipschitz continuity of
\phi
give

\displaystyle KT\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{P}^{(n)}_{i,j}

\displaystyle\Big(\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)-\phi\big(\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s),\bar{X}^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big)\Big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq KT\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(|X^{n}_{i}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s)|^{2}+|X^{n}_{j}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{j}(s)|^{2}\big)\,ds.

Combining above inequalities and averaging over
i\in[n]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{i}(t)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{KT^{2}}{np(n)}+KT\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|X^{n}_{i}(u)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(u)|^{2}\big]\,ds.

Grönwall’s inequality yields

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{n}_{i}(t)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{KT^{2}\exp(KT^{2})}{np(n)},

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W^{2}_{2}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{n}_{i}(t)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\Big|D^{(n)}_{i,j}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(s)a^{k}_{i}(z,s)b^{k}_{j}(z,s)m_{\phi_{k}}(dz)\Big|\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{Kt}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big|\langle\mathbf{a}^{k}(z,s),\,D^{(n)}\mathbf{b}^{k}(z,s)\rangle\big|\,m_{\phi_{k}}(dz)\,ds,

(4.12)

where we defined the complex vectors

\mathbf{a}^{k}(z,s):=\Big(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(s)a^{k}_{i}(z,s)\Big)_{i\in[n]},\qquad\mathbf{b}^{k}(z,s):=\big(b^{k}_{j}(z,s)\big)_{j\in[n]},\qquad\text{for each }k\in[d].

(4.13)

Since
\ell^{\infty}
-norms of these vectors are bounded by
1
, decomposing them into real and complex parts gives for each
k\in[d]

\big|\langle\mathbf{a}^{k}(z,s),\,D^{(n)}\mathbf{b}^{k}(z,s)\rangle\big|\leq 4\sup\big\{\langle\mathbf{x},D^{(n)}\mathbf{y}\rangle:\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[-1,1]^{n}\big\}=4||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}.

Thus, the right-hand side of (
4.12
) is bounded above by

\frac{Kdt^{2}}{n}\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq d}||m_{\phi_{k}}||_{TM}\Big)||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}.

For the integrals of (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we use the Lipschitz continuity of
\phi
and
\psi
, thus there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\triangle(t)\leq K\int_{0}^{t}\triangle(s)ds+\frac{||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}Kt^{2},\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

(4.14)

Grönwall’s inequality yields

\triangle(T)\leq\frac{K\|D^{(n)}\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n},

(4.15)

where
K
is now a positive constant depending on the time horizon
T
. Recalling the notation
\triangle(t)
, we obtain

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{1}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})\leq\triangle(T)\leq\frac{K||D^{(n)}||_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n},

and finally Lemma
2.9
gives the bound for the last probability of (
4.6
)

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{1}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})>\frac{a}{2}\Big]\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{a^{2}n^{2}p(n)}{8K^{2}+2aK/3}\Big).

(4.16)

For the first probability on the right-hand side of (
4.6
), Theorem
3.3
yields for every
n\geq N

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{1}(\bar{L}_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})>\frac{a}{2}\Big]\leq 2\exp\Big(-\frac{\delta a^{2}n}{16}\Big).

Thanks to Assumption
2.4
, by choosing a larger value for
N\in\mathbb{N}
than the one in Theorem
3.3
, we can make

\exp\big(-\frac{a^{2}n^{2}p(n)}{8K^{2}+2aK/3}\big)\leq\exp\big(-\frac{\delta a^{2}n}{16}\big)
for every
n\geq N
, and the assertion (
3.6
) follows.

For the result (
3.7
), we can approximate general
\phi
with those in
L^{1}
-Fourier class by the approximation method in Section 5.1.3 of
[
27
]
, to find the exponential bound for the probability
\mathbb{P}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}d_{BL}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})>a/2]
similar to (
4.16
). By recalling the fact
d_{BL}\leq W_{1}
and replacing all the
W_{1}
-metrics with the
d_{BL}
-metrics in (
4.6
), we arrive at (
3.7
).

∎

4.3.3
Proof of Theorem
3.5

It is easy to verify the
(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
-Lipschitz continuity of the map

Y\mapsto\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{Y_{i}(t)},\,\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t}\Big),

from
\Big(\big(C([0,T]:\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)^{n},\,\|\cdot\|_{n,2}\Big)
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then, for any
a>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(\bar{L}_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})>a\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(\bar{L}_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(\bar{L}_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})\big]>\frac{a}{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{P}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(\bar{L}_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})\big]>\frac{a}{2}\Big].

The first term is bounded by the right-hand side of (
3.8
) from Theorem
3.2
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, the last probability vanishes for all but finitely many
n
from Corollary
3.1
.

∎

4.3.4
Proof of Lemma
3.1

We note from (
2.3
) that
\{D^{(n)}_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
are independent zero-mean random variables and for every
i,j\in[n]

\mathbb{E}\big[(D^{(n)}_{i,j})^{2}\big]=\frac{p(n)G(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\big(1-p(n)G(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\big)}{\big(np(n)\big)^{2}}.

(4.17)

In particular, since
p(n)\leq 1
, we have
0\leq p(n)G(\frac{i}{n},\frac{j}{n})\leq 1
, and thus
\mathbb{E}\big[(D^{(n)}_{i,j})^{2}\big]\leq 1/(4n^{2}p(n)^{2})
.

Let us fix any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For arbitrary
n
-dimensional vectors
\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in[-1,1]^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\langle\mathbf{x},(D^{(n)})^{\top}D^{(n)}\mathbf{y}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(D^{(n)}\mathbf{x}\big)_{i}\big(D^{(n)}\mathbf{y}\big)_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}\big]\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}y_{j}}{4n^{2}p(n)^{2}}.

Thus, we have for fixed arbitrary
\eta>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{\|(D^{(n)})^{\top}D^{(n)}\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}>\eta\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta}{3}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}\big]\Big)>\frac{\eta}{3}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{1}{4np(n)^{2}}>\frac{\eta}{3}\bigg]=:P_{1}+P_{2}+P_{3}.

(4.18)

From Assumption
3.1
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
P_{3}
vanishes for every
n\geq N
. In the following, we find the bounds for
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. Using the distribution

D^{(n)}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\frac{1-p(n)G(i/n,j/n)}{np(n)}\leq\frac{1}{np(n)},&\text{ with probability }p(n)G(i/n,j/n),\\
\\
-\frac{1}{n}G(i/n,j/n)\geq-\frac{1}{n},&\text{ with probability }1-p(n)G(i/n,j/n),\end{cases}

for each
i,j\in[n]
, we derive for
P_{1}

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta}{3}\bigg]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta}{3n}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta}{3n}\Big|D^{(n)}_{i,j}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta p(n)}{3}\Big]+\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}<-\frac{\eta}{3}\Big]\bigg).

The summands
D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}
in the last two probabilities are independent zero-mean random variables, bounded above by
1/(np(n))
, bounded below by
-1/n
, and satisfies

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[(D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k})^{2}\big]\leq\frac{n-1}{4n^{2}p(n)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4np(n)^{2}},

from (
4.17
). From Bernstein’s inequality (Lemma
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta p(n)}{3}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{2np(n)^{4}\eta^{2}}{9+4\eta p(n)^{2}}\bigg),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,k}x_{j}y_{k}>\frac{\eta}{3}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{2np(n)^{2}\eta^{2}}{9+4\eta p(n)^{2}}\bigg),

thus

P_{1}\leq 2n^{2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{2np(n)^{4}\eta^{2}}{9+4\eta p(n)^{2}}\bigg).

(4.19)

We now compute the bound for
P_{2}
. Since we have

P_{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}\big]\Big)>\frac{\eta}{3}\bigg]

(4.20)

and the summands
\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}\big]
in the probability are independent zero-mean random variables bounded above by
5/(2np(n))^{2}
. Moreover, we easily obtain the bound
\mathbb{E}\big[(D^{(n)}_{i,j})^{4}\big]\leq 1/(np(n))^{4}
, and thus the sum of variances of summands are

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}x_{j}y_{j}\big]\Big)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(D^{(n)}_{i,j}\big)^{2}\big]\Big)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\big[(D^{(n)}_{i,j})^{4}\big]+\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[(D^{(n)}_{i,j})^{2}\big]\Big)^{2}\bigg)\leq\frac{17}{16n^{3}p(n)^{4}}.

Applying Bernstein’s inequality (Lemma
2.10
) to each probability of (
4.20
) yields

P_{2}\leq n\exp\bigg(-\frac{8n^{3}p(n)^{4}\eta^{2}}{153+20np(n)^{2}\eta}\bigg).

(4.21)

Comparing the bounds of (
4.19
), (
4.21
), modifying the value of
N
if necessary, and plugging into (
4.18
), the result follows.
∎

4.3.5
Proof of Theorem
3.6

The argument is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.4
. The triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(L_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})>a\Big]\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(\bar{L}_{n,t},\mathaccent 869{\mu}_{t})>\frac{a}{2}\Big]+\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})>\frac{a}{2}\Big].

(4.22)

Recalling the identity (
4.7
), applying a series of Hölder’s inequality, and using the Lipschitz property yield

\displaystyle\big|X^{n}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)\big|^{2}-2t\int_{0}^{t}\big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2K^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}2\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{P}^{(n)}_{i,j}\Big(\big|X^{n}_{i}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s)\big|+\big|X^{n}_{j}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{j}(s)\big|\Big)^{2}+2\big|X^{n}_{i}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s)\big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2K^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}6\big|X^{n}_{i}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(s)\big|^{2}+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big|X^{n}_{j}(s)-\bar{X}^{n}_{j}(s)\big|^{2}\,ds,\qquad\forall\,t\in[0,T].

For a fixed
t\in[0,T]
, by using the continuity of
X^{n}_{i}(\cdot)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(\cdot)
, there exists
t_{i}\in[0,t]
for each
i\in[n]
satisfying
\Box(t):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|X^{n}_{i}-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}\|^{2}_{\star,t}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big|X^{n}_{i}(t_{i})-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t_{i})\big|^{2}
. Combining with the last inequality, we have

\Box(t)\leq 2t\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(s)\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big|^{2}\,ds+16K^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}\Box(s)\,ds.

(4.23)

We recall the representations (
4.11
) and (
4.13
) and use Hölder’s inequality to derive for the first integral on the right-hand side

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{(n)}_{i,j}

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(s)\phi\big(X^{n}_{i}(s),X^{n}_{j}(s)\big)\Big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big|D^{(n)}_{i,j}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(s)a^{k}_{i}(z,s)b^{k}_{j}(z,s)\big|\,m_{\phi_{k}}(dz)\bigg|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|m_{\phi_{k}}\|_{TM}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big|\big(\mathbf{a}^{k}(z,s)\big)_{i}\,\big(D^{(n)}\mathbf{b}^{k}(z,s)\big)_{i}\big|^{2}\,m_{\phi_{k}}(dz)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|m_{\phi_{k}}\|_{TM}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big|\big(D^{(n)}\mathbf{b}^{k}(z,s)\big)_{i}\big|^{2}\,m_{\phi_{k}}(dz)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\|m_{\phi_{k}}\|_{TM}^{2}d^{2}t\frac{\|(D^{(n)})^{\top}D^{(n)}\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}.

In the last two inequalities, we used the fact that
\ell^{\infty}
-norms of two vectors
\mathbf{a}^{k}(z,s)
and
\mathbf{b}^{k}(z,s)
are bounded by
1
. Thus, from (
4.23
), there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\Box(t)\leq Kt^{2}\frac{\|(D^{(n)})^{\top}D^{(n)}\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}+Kt\int_{0}^{t}\Box(s)ds

holds for every
t\in[0,T]
and applying Grönwall’s inequality gives

\Box(T)\leq\frac{K\|(D^{(n)})^{\top}D^{(n)}\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n},

where the constant
K
now depends on
T
.

Since we have

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big|X^{n}_{i}(t)-\bar{X}^{n}_{i}(t)\big|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\Box(T)},

Lemma
3.1
shows that there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that the last probability in (
4.22
) has the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}W_{2}(L_{n,t},\bar{L}_{n,t})>\frac{a}{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\Box(T)>\frac{a^{2}}{4}\Big]\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\frac{\|(D^{(n)})^{\top}D^{(n)}\|_{\infty\rightarrow 1}}{n}>\frac{a^{2}}{4K}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 3n^{2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{a^{4}np(n)^{4}}{72K^{2}+8a^{2}K}\bigg),

for every
n\geq N
.

On the other hand, Theorem
3.5
gives the bound for the other probability in (
4.22
). By comparing both of the bounds under Assumption
3.1
(a), the assertion (
3.9
) follows. The result (
3.10
) is now clear under Assumption
3.1
(b), by setting
p(n)\equiv 1
and redefining the constants
\delta>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
appropriately.

∎

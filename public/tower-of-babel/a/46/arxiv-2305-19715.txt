Peter Schlosser

Abstract
. Superoscillations are a phenomenon in physics, where linear combinations of low-frequency plane waves interfere almost destructively in such a way that the resulting wave has a higher frequency than any of the individual waves. The evolution of superoscillatory initial datum under the time dependent Schrödinger equation is stable in free space, but in general it is unclear whether it can be preserved in the presence of an external potential. In this paper, we consider the two-dimensional problem of superoscillations interacting with a half-plane barrier, where homogenous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions are imposed on the negative x 2 x_{2} -semiaxis. We use the Fresnel integral technique to write the wave function as an absolute convergent Green’s function integral. Moreover, we introduce the propagator of the Schrödinger equation in form of an infinite order differential operator, acting continuously on the function space of exponentially bounded entire functions. In particular, this operator allows to prove that the property of superoscillations is preserved in the form of a similar phenomenon called supershift, which is stable over time.

A particular class of initial conditions are entire functions in two complex variables which grow at most exponentially at

\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\Set{F:\mathbb{C}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\text{ entire}}{\exists A,B\geq 0:\,|F(\mathbf{z})|\leq Ae^{B|\mathbf{z}|},\text{ for every }\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}},

(1.3)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}}|F_{n}(z)-F_{0}(z)|e^{-B|z|}=0.

The standard examples of superoscillatory functions, which serve as initial condition in the above Schrödinger equation are of the form

(1.4)

F_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}C_{j}(n)e^{ik_{j}(n)^{p_{1}}x_{1}+ik_{j}(n)^{p_{2}}x_{2}},\quad\mathbf{x}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\

C_{j}(n)=\binom{n}{j}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{n-j}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{j},

Definition 1.1

(Superoscillations)
.

A sequence of functions of the form

(1.5)

F_{n}(\mathbf{z})=\int_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq k_{0}}e^{i\mathbf{kz}}d\mu_{n}(\mathbf{k}),\quad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

with a common maximal frequency
k_{0}>0
and complex Borel measures
\mu_{n}
on the closed ball
\overline{B_{k_{0}}(\mathbf{0})}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
of radius
k_{0}
, is called superoscillating, if there exists some
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|\mathbf{a}|>k_{0}
, such that

(1.6)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\mathbf{z})=e^{i\mathbf{az}}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Here the products
\mathbf{kz}:=k_{1}z_{1}+k_{2}z_{2}
of real or complex vectors is understood in the usual bilinear sense.

Remark 1.2
.

We point out that any function
F_{n}
of the form (
1.5
) is automatically contained in
\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. The exponential bound is given by the estimate

|F_{n}(\mathbf{z})|\leq\int_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq k_{0}}e^{|\mathbf{kz}|}d|\mu_{n}|(\mathbf{k})\leq|\mu_{n}|\big(\overline{U_{k_{0}}(\mathbf{0})}\big)e^{k_{0}|\mathbf{z}|},\quad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

with
|\mu_{n}|
the total variation of the complex measure
\mu_{n}
. The holomorphicity also follows from this locally uniform upper bound and a version of the dominated convegence theorem, which allows to interchange derivative and integral and leads to a holomorphic function
F_{n}(\mathbf{z})
.

To study the evolution of superoscillating functions we will introduce for every

U_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{\partial z_{1}^{n_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{n_{2}}},

where, the coefficients
c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})
depend on the potential and on the auxiliary complex variables

and

These operators applied to the initial datum

\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})=U_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})F(\mathbf{z})\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}}.

As we will see the continuity on spaces of entire functions
of the above operator

is of crucial importance in the investigation of the time evolution of superoscillations.

2.
The Green’s function of the half-plane barrier

The strategy to solve the Schrödinger equation of the half-plane barrier is based on Green’s functions techniques, i.e., we write the solution of the Schrödinger
equation with initial condition
F
as an integral of the form

(2.1)

\Psi(t,\mathbf{x})=\int_{\Omega}G(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})F(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y},\quad t>0,\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega.

The Green’s function
G
for the particular problem of the half-plane barrier is calculated in
[
29
]
, and using polar coordinates
\mathbf{x}=r(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\varphi\\
\sin\varphi\end{smallmatrix})
,
\mathbf{y}=(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix})
with
r,\rho>0
,
\varphi,\theta\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2})
, explicitly given by

(2.2a)

\displaystyle G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D}}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{(r+\rho)^{2}}{4it}}}{8i\pi t}\bigg(\Lambda\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{r\rho}\,\cos(\frac{\varphi-\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)-\Lambda\bigg(-\frac{\sqrt{r\rho}\,\sin(\frac{\varphi+\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)\bigg),

(2.2b)

\displaystyle G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{N}}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{(r+\rho)^{2}}{4it}}}{8i\pi t}\bigg(\Lambda\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{r\rho}\,\cos(\frac{\varphi-\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)+\Lambda\bigg(-\frac{\sqrt{r\rho}\,\sin(\frac{\varphi+\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)\bigg).

Here the indices
D
and
N
indicate the type of boundary conditions (Dirichlet or Neumann) in (
1.1
), and for a shorter notation we used the entire function

(2.3)

\Lambda(z):=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s^{2}-2zs}ds,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

It can be shown that
\Lambda(z)=e^{z^{2}}(1-\operatorname{erf}(z))
is a modification of the well-known error function. Since we allow initial conditions
F\in\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, which may grow exponentially at
\infty
, some regularization is needed in the integral (
2.1
), such that the solution of (
1.1
) can be written as

(2.4)

\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}e^{-\varepsilon|\mathbf{y}|^{2}}G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})F(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y},\qquad t>0,\,\mathbf{x}\in\Omega.

The aim of this section is to find an absolute convergent integral representation of the wave functions in (
2.4
), which will then be needed in the sequel to prove the main results of the paper. The key ingredient will be the so called Fresnel integral technique, which roughly speaking rotates the domain of integration into the complex plane and produces in this way an absolute convergent integrand. Note that the following Lemma
2.1
is a special version of
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
, where also a proof can be found.

Lemma 2.1

(Fresnel integral)
.

Let
a>0
and
f:\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic on

\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}:=\Set{z\in\mathbb{C}}{\operatorname{Re}(z)>0},

and satisfies the estimate

(2.5)

|f(z)|\leq Ae^{B|z|},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0},

for some and
A,B\geq 0
. Then, for every
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, we get

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\varepsilon y^{2}}e^{iay^{2}}f(y)dy=e^{i\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ia(ye^{i\alpha})^{2}}f(ye^{i\alpha})dy.

The following Theorem
2.2
now uses this Fresnel integral technique, to rewrite the regularized integral (
2.4
) as an absolute convergent integral in the complex plan.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Then, for every
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
the functions
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D}}
and
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{N}}
in (
2.4
) can be written as the absolute convergent integral

(2.6)

\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})=e^{2i\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},\rho e^{i\alpha}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)F\big(\rho e^{i\alpha}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)\rho\,d\theta d\rho.

Proof.

First, we transform the integral (
2.4
) into polar coordinates, which is

(2.7)

\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}e^{-\varepsilon\rho^{2}}G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},\rho\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)F\big(\rho\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)\rho\,d\theta d\rho.

Next, we note that the function
(0,\infty)\mapsto G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x},\rho(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}))
holomorphically extends to
\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}
by simply replacing
\rho\in(0,\infty)
by

in (
2.2
). Note that it could also be extended to

, which is the maximal domain of the square root in (
2.8
), but this is not necessary for our purposes. Furthermore, we can decompose this extended Green’s function into

\displaystyle G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)

(2.8)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4it}}\underbrace{\frac{e^{-\frac{r^{2}+2rz}{4it}}}{8i\pi t}\bigg(\Lambda\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{rz}\,\cos(\frac{\varphi-\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)\mp\Lambda\bigg(-\frac{\sqrt{rz}\,\sin(\frac{\varphi+\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)\bigg)}_{=:\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\

and hence write the integral (

) as

\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\varepsilon\rho^{2}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4it}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},\rho\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)F\big(\rho\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)\rho\,d\theta d\rho.

The idea is now to apply the Fresnel integral technique of Lemma
2.1
with respect to the radial part of the above integral. To do so, we have to check if the function

(2.9)

z\mapsto\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)F\big(z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)z\,d\theta

is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}
and exponentially bounded as in (
2.5
). It is obvious that the integrand

z\mapsto\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)F\big(z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)

is holomorphic in
\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}
. Also knowing that

\theta\mapsto\frac{d}{dz}\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)F\big(z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big),

is continuous, it follows that also the integral (
2.9
) is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0}
for every fixed
t>0
,
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
. To verify the exponential bound (
2.5
) for the mapping (
2.9
), we first estimate the reduced Green’s function
\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}
by

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)\big|

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{r\operatorname{Im}(z)}{2t}}}{8\pi t}\bigg|\Lambda\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{rz}\,\cos(\frac{\varphi-\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)\mp\Lambda\bigg(-\frac{\sqrt{rz}\,\sin(\frac{\varphi+\theta}{2})}{\sqrt{it}}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi t}e^{\frac{3r|z|}{2t}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\operatorname{Re}>0},

where we used the estimate

of the function

). Since

, there exist constants

, such that

\big|F\big(z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)\big|\leq Ae^{B\sqrt{|z\cos\theta|^{2}+|z\sin\theta|^{2}}}=Ae^{B|z|},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Combining now (
2.10
), (
2.11
) as well as

, which is an immediate consequence of the power series expansion of the exponential, the integral in (
2.9
) admits the estimate

(2.12)

\bigg|\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}\widetilde{G}_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},z\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\

Hence, we verified that the assumptions of Lemma

for the mapping (

) are satisfied and so we can write the integral (

) for any

in the form

which, after substituting the defintion of

The infinite order differential operators associated

) as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=e^{2i\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},\rho e^{i\alpha}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\times\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial_{z_{1}}^{n_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}^{n_{2}}F(\mathbf{0})}{n_{1}!n_{2}!}(\rho e^{i\alpha}\cos\theta)^{n_{1}}(\rho e^{i\alpha}\sin\theta)^{n_{2}}\rho\,d\theta d\rho

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(n_{1}+n_{2}+2)i\alpha}}{n_{1}!n_{2}!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},\rho e^{i\alpha}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\times(\cos\theta)^{n_{1}}(\sin\theta)^{n_{2}}\rho^{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}d\theta d\rho\frac{\partial^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{\partial z_{1}^{n_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{n_{2}}}F(\mathbf{z})\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{\partial z_{1}^{n_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{n_{2}}}F(\mathbf{z})\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}},

using the coefficients

(3.2)

c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x}):=\frac{e^{(n_{1}+n_{2}+2)i\alpha}}{n_{1}!n_{2}!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}G_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x},\rho e^{i\alpha}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)\big)(\cos\theta)^{n_{1}}(\sin\theta)^{n_{2}}\rho^{n_{1}+n_{2}+1}d\theta d\rho.

The above computations mean, that using the

If a function

\displaystyle\leq\frac{An_{1}!n_{2}!}{r_{1}^{n_{1}}r_{2}^{n_{2}}}e^{B\sqrt{|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}}+B\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}}.

Assuming now for the moment

Using the inequality

Using now the values (

and
r_{2}

3.9

whenever
z_{1},z_{2}\neq 0

by continuity. The case
n_{1}=0
and/or
n_{2}=0
follows the same calculations with the choice

and/or
r_{2}=1
in (
3.7
).
er reduces to

For the case where both

n_{1}\neq 0

For every fixed

Using the decomposition (

For the proof of the continuity let

.

Let

4.
Time persistence of superoscillations and supershift

In this section, we investigate the evolution of superoscillating functions as initial conditions in the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
). The expectation that for any sequence
(F_{n})_{n}
of superoscillating initial conditions, the sequence of solutions
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};F_{n})
will again be superoscillating (for fixed times
t>0
), can easily be negated. In fact, one way of reasoning is that the convergence (
1.6
) of the initial conditions
F_{n}
to a plane wave
e^{iax}
implies that the solutions
converge as

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};F_{n})=\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{a}\,\cdot\,}\big),

see, e.g., the continuous dependency result in Corollary
3.3
ii). However, for
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};F_{n})
to be superoscillating, the limit function
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{a}\,\cdot\,})
has to be plane wave due to the definition in (
1.6
), which is not possible since
the boundary condition forces the wave function (or its derivative)
to vanish on
\Gamma
.
Moreover, the solution
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{a}\,\cdot\,})
will no longer be a holomorphic function in the
\mathbf{x}
-variable and hence no element in the space
\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. These considerations show that the precise mathematical notion of superoscillations is too narrow to persist in time.

This motivates the following notion of
supershift
, which basically is a replacement of the holomorphic exponentials
e^{i\mathbf{ax}}
by arbitrary continuous functions
\varphi_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})
in the Definition
1.1
of superoscillations.

Definition 4.1

(Supershift)
.

Let
X
be a metric space and

(4.1)

\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

be a family of complex valued functions such that
\mathbf{k}\mapsto\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}(s)
is continuous for every
s\in X
. We say that a sequence of the form

(4.2)

\Phi_{n}(s):=\int_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq k_{0}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}(s)d\mu_{n}(\mathbf{k}),\qquad s\in X,

for some
k_{0}>0
and complex Borel measures
\mu_{n}
on the closed ball
\overline{B_{k_{0}}(0)}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}
, admits a supershift, if there exists some
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with
|\mathbf{a}|>k_{0}
, such that

(4.3)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{n}(s)=\varphi_{\mathbf{a}}(s),\qquad s\in X,

converges uniformly on compact subsets of
X
.

Remark 4.2
.

With the special choice
X=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{z})=e^{i\mathbf{kz}}
, it turns out that the notion of supershift in Definition
4.1
is a generalization of the notion of superoscillations in Definition
1.1
. Indeed, with this choice the integrals (
1.5
) and (
4.2
) coincide and since the
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-convergence (
1.3
) is stronger than the convergence on compact sets, the convergence (
1.6
) implies the convergence (
4.3
).

In the following theorem we now prove that after the interaction of superoscillations with the half-plane barrier, the superoscillatory property turns into a supershift property, which then persists for all times
t>0
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a superoscillating sequence according to Definition
1.1
, i.e.

(4.4)

F_{n}(\mathbf{z})=\int_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq k_{0}}e^{i\mathbf{kz}}d\mu_{n}(\mathbf{k})\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{i\mathbf{az}},\quad\text{in }\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

(4.5)

Proof.

For the first identity in (

\displaystyle=U_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})\int_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq k_{0}}e^{i\mathbf{kz}}d\mu_{n}(\mathbf{k})\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{\partial z_{1}^{n_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{n_{2}}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq k_{0}}e^{i\mathbf{kz}}d\mu_{n}(\mathbf{k})\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}}

and hence the sum

Secondly, the convergence in (

In the following we consider the special case of superoscillating functions of the form (

Corollary 4.4

Let
F_{n}
be functions of the form

F_{n}(\mathbf{z})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}C_{j}(n)e^{i\mathbf{k_{j}}(n)\mathbf{z}},\quad\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

with coefficients
C_{j}(n)\in\mathbb{C}
and wave vectors
\mathbf{k_{j}}(n)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
|\mathbf{k_{j}}(n)|\leq 1
.
If

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\mathbf{z})=e^{i\mathbf{az}}

converges in
\mathcal{A}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, for some
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|\mathbf{a}|>1
, then also the sequence of solutions
\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};F_{n})
converge as

(4.7)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};F_{n})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}C_{j}(n)\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{k_{j}}(n)\,\cdot\,}\big)=\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{a}\,\cdot\,}\big),

uniformly for
(t,\mathbf{x})
in compact subsets of
(0,\infty)\times\Omega
.

For the final part of this paper let us now fix
t>0
and
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and look at equation (
4.7
) in terms of the mapping
\mathbf{k}\mapsto\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{k}\,\cdot\,})
. Then, one sees that the value of this mapping at a point
\mathbf{a}
with
|\mathbf{a}|>1
, which is located outside the unit ball is determined by only values
|\mathbf{k_{j}}(n)|\leq 1
inside the unit ball. This property looks very much like analyticity. The following proposition shows that this is indeed the case.

Proposition 4.5
.

For every fixed
t>0
,
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
, the mapping

\mathbf{k}\mapsto\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{k}\,\cdot\,}\big)\quad\text{is holomorphic on }\mathbb{C}^{2}.

Proof.

Using the representation of the wave function using the infinite order differential operator in Corollary
3.3
i), gives

\displaystyle\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}\big(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{k}\,\cdot\,}\big)

\displaystyle=U_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x})e^{i\mathbf{kz}}\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}{\partial z_{1}^{n_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{n_{2}}}e^{i\mathbf{kz}}\Big|_{\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}c_{n_{1},n_{2}}(t,\mathbf{x})(ik_{1})^{n_{1}}(ik_{2})^{n_{2}}.

Since this is an everywhere convergent power series in
\mathbf{k}
, the mapping
\mathbf{k}\mapsto\Psi_{\hskip-1.42271pt{D},{N}}(t,\mathbf{x};e^{i\mathbf{k}\,\cdot\,})
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
∎

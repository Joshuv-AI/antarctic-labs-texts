An atomic Coxeter presentation
Hankyung Ko

Abstract
. We study parabolic double cosets in a Coxeter system by decomposing them into atom(ic coset)s, a generalization of simple reflections introduced in a joint work with Elias, Libedinsky, Patimo. We define and classify braid relations between compositions of atoms and prove a Matsumoto theorem. Together with a quadratic relation, our braid relations give a presentation of nilCoxeter algebroids similar to Demazure’s presentation of nilCoxeter algebras. Our consideration of reduced compositions of atoms gives rise to a new combinatorial structure, which is equipped with a length function and a Bruhat order and is realized as Tits cone intersections in the sense of Iyama-Wemyss.

1.3.
Atoms and atomic expressions

Now suppose

(I,J)

Then

It is thus natural to ask whether there exist

By an

from a maximal cell to its codimension one face and from a codimension one face to a maximal cell,

The answer is positive and appears in a joint work with Elias-Libedinsky-Patimo
[
EKLP24
]
(see Proposition
3.10
or
[
EKLP24
, Corollary 2.17]
).
In fact,
[
EKLP24
]
shows that there is a reduced expression with the above condition and calls it an

atomic reduced expression
. To explan the terminology, let us start with atoms.

Definition 1.6
.

An
atom
(or an
atomic coset
) is
a core coset of the form
W_{K\setminus s}w_{K}W_{K\setminus t}
, where
K\subset S
is finitary and
s,t\in K
.

Here
w_{K}
denotes the maximal element in
W_{K}
, and it is straightforward that the core coset condition implies
t=w_{K}sw_{K}
.
A further justification of the definition (in addition to that they are the simplest core cosets) is given by the fact that an atom has a unique reduced expression (see Proposition
3.8
).
An
atomic (reduced) expression
then refers to a (reduced) composition of atoms, identifying the atoms with their unique reduced expressions. For example, if
{\mathtt{a}}=W_{I}w_{K}W_{J}

and
{\mathtt{b}}=W_{J}w_{L}W_{M}
are atoms, then
their unique reduced expressions are

{\mathtt{a}}\leftrightharpoons[[I\subset K\supset J]],\quad{\mathtt{b}}\leftrightharpoons[[J\subset L\supset M]]

and we have

{\mathtt{a}}\circ{\mathtt{b}}\leftrightharpoons[[I\subset K\supset J\subset L\supset M]].

Thus the forementioned result of
[
EKLP24
]
says that a core coset decomposes as a reduced composition of atoms.
In all above senses, an atom, in the study of (core) double cosets, plays the same role as what a simple reflection
s\in S
plays in Coxeter combinatorics.

1.4.
Atomic braid relations and Matsumoto’s theorem

The purpose of the current work is to understand the relations between atomic expressions. For reduced expressions, we achieve this in the atomic Matsumoto theorem:

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Two atomic reduced expressions of the same core coset are related by
atomic braid relations
.

We refer to Definition
4.10
for its definition, but an atomic braid relation is of the form

(1.5)

\underbrace{{\mathtt{a}}\circ{\mathtt{b}}^{\prime}\circ{\mathtt{a}}^{\prime\prime}\cdots}_{m}=\underbrace{{\mathtt{b}}\circ{\mathtt{a}}^{\prime}\circ{\mathtt{b}}^{\prime\prime}\cdots}_{m},

where
{\mathtt{a}},{\mathtt{b}}
are atoms; the atoms
{\mathtt{a}}^{\prime},{\mathtt{a}}^{\prime\prime},\cdots
and
{\mathtt{b}}^{\prime},{\mathtt{b}}^{\prime\prime},\cdots
are certain twists of the atoms
{\mathtt{a}}
and
{\mathtt{b}}
, respectively, determined by their position in the composition; and
m=m_{{\mathtt{a}},{\mathtt{b}}}\geq 2
is an integer (not necessarily equal to one of the entries in the Coxeter matrix for
(W,S)
). See also Proposition
4.9
and Proposition
4.18
. Here is an example in type
D_{4}
(our running example
\widetilde{S}_{3}
is unfortunately too small to have nontrivial atomic braid relations) where no twists occurs; see Examples
4.13
,
4.15
, Section
7
, Section
8.2
for examples with twists.

Example 1.9

(Example
4.14
)
.

Let
(W,S)
be of type
D_{4}
where
S=\{1,2,3,4\}
is indexed so that

, etc. for subsets of

{\mathtt{a}}\leftrightharpoons[[13\subset 123\supset 13]],\qquad{\mathtt{b}}\leftrightharpoons[[13\subset 134\supset 13]]

where the left hand side is identified with the expression

We point out a key feature of our braid relations, a feature the braid relations from

Due to this feature, the combinatorics of atomic reduced expressions very much resembles that of regular reduced expressions in a Coxeter group, bringing us a substantial computational and pedagogical advantage over the presentation in

.

1.5.
An atomic presentation

The non-reduced (atomic or not) expressions depend on different natural ways of composing double cosets in a non-reduced way. We consider two of them, namely the singular Coxeter monoid
\mathcal{SC}
studied in
[
EK23
]
(see Section
2.2
) and the nilCoxeter algebroid
\mathcal{SD}
studied in
[
EKLP23
]
(see Section
2.3
).
As is implicit above, we use the adjective ‘singular’ to mean ‘of parabolic double cosets’.
In particular
\mathcal{SC}
is the double coset analogue of the Coxeter monoid, and
\mathcal{SD}
is that of the nilCoxeter algebra, sometimes called the nilHecke algebra (our notation comes from
\mathcal{SD}
being equivalent to the category of singular Demazure operators, i.e., Demazure operators associated to parabolic double cosets; see
[
Dem73
,
EKLP23
]
). The two settings differ only in the quadratic relations, namely,
ss=s
in the Coxeter monoid and
ss=0
in the nilCoxeter algebra.
As can be guessed from the quadratic relation, it is easier to deal with non-reduced expressions in
\mathcal{SD}
: they are equal to zero.

As our second main result, we provide in Theorem
6.13
a presentation of the core cosets in
\mathcal{SD}
by generators and relations. Theorem
6.13

generalizes
the (nil)Coxeter presentation of Demazure operators
[
Dem73
, Theoreme 1 and Proposition 3]
.

We restricted ourselves to core double cosets in Sections

Let

We have

(see e.g.,

In particular, the coset

and

Then a result from
[

]
(see Proposition

p^{\core}=W_{\leftred(p)}\underline{p}W_{\rightred(p)}
is a core
(\leftred(p),\rightred(p))

(1.6)

[[I\supset\leftred(p)]]\circ M_{\bullet}\circ[[\rightred(p)\subset J]]

where
M_{\bullet}
is a reduced expression for

. This allows us to reduce problems on arbitrary double cosets to that on core cosets.

For example, if an
(I,J)
-coset

\leftred(p)=I
,

1.7.
Motivation from Lie theory

Now that we have given purely combinatorial motivations for our work, let us explain our original motivations from the study of Hecke categories.
In a joint work
[

]

The result of

2.1.
Coxeter group preliminaries

Given an element

the (Coxeter)

is the smallest length of an expression of

where

for some

A product

An expression

if every product involved in computing the expression, in any order, is reduced.
The latter condition is equivalent to the product

being reduced for each

, which also is equivalent to the length
m
of the expression being the shortest possible, i.e.,

.
The

is defined and denoted as

\leftdes(w)=\{s\in S\ |\ sw\text{ is not reduced}\},\quad\text{ resp., }\quad\rightdes(w)=\{s\in S\ |\ ws\text{ is not reduced}\}.

A subset
I\subset S
is said to be

if the parabolic subgroup
W_{I}
generated by
I
is finite.
Given finitary

, we call an element of

an
(I,J)
-coset.

An
(I,J)
-coset

has a unique maximal element, denoted by
\overline{p}
, and a unique minimal element, denoted by
\underline{p}
, with respect to the Bruhat order (see, e.g.,
[
EK23
, Lemma 2.12]
).
The
left (resp. right) redundancy
of an
(I,J)
-coset

is

(2.1)

\leftred(p)=I\cap\underline{p}J\underline{p}^{-1},\quad\text{resp.,}\quad\rightred(p)=\underline{p}^{-1}I\underline{p}\cap J.

Howlett’s theorem
[

, Lemma 2.12]
says that, when writing

as a product

,

is unique up to the action of the redundancy, that is, the surjective map

(2.2)

\displaystyle W_{I}\times W_{J}

\displaystyle\to p

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto x\underline{p}y

induces the bijections

(2.3)

(W_{I}/W_{\leftred(p)})\times W_{J}\to p\quad\text{ and }\quad W_{I}\times(W_{\rightred(p)}\backslash W_{J})\to p.

[

, Lemma 2.12]
also says that, if

and

{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t]
is core exactly when
t=w_{Is}sw_{Is}
. Thus the atoms in
(W,S)
are indexed by the pairs
(I,s)
, with
sI\subsetneq S
finitary and
s\in S\setminus I
.

Remark 3.7
.

If
{\mathtt{a}}\leftrightharpoons[I,Is,J]=[I+s-t]
is an atom, then its

reverse

{\mathtt{a}}^{-1}\leftrightharpoons[J,Is,I]=[J+t-s]
, where
t=w_{Is}sw_{Is}
, is an atom. (See
[
EK23
, Section 4.4]
for a general discussion on
reversing
double cosets.)

Another way to characterize an atom among the cosets of the form
{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t]
is that an atom does not allow for braid relations:

Proposition 3.8
.

[
EK23
, Proposition 5.14]

An atomic coset has a unique reduced expression. More precisely, a coset of the form
{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t]
is atomic if and only if
{p}
has a unique
reduced
expression; when
{p}
is not atomic the switchback relation applies.

We are interested in the composition of atoms, say

(3.1)

{\mathtt{a}}_{1}*{\mathtt{a}}_{2}*\cdots*{\mathtt{a}}_{m}.

Replacing each
{\mathtt{a}}_{i}
with its unique reduced expression given in Proposition
3.8
, we view the composition (
3.1
) as an expression in
\mathcal{SC}
. Expressions in
\mathcal{SC}
arising in this way are characterized as follows.

Definition 3.9
.

An expression of the form

(3.2)

[L_{0},I_{0},L_{1},I_{1},\cdots I_{m-1},L_{m}]=[L_{0}+s_{1}-t_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}-t_{m}]

is called an
atomic expression
if, for each
0\leq i<m
, the coset

(3.3)

{\mathtt{a}}_{i}\leftrightharpoons[L_{i},I_{i},L_{i+1}]=[L_{i}+s_{i+1}-t_{i+1}]

is core (thus an atom).

A
n atomic expression is a composition of atoms in the same way a regular expression in a Coxeter group is a product of simple reflections.
In this sense, the following result from
[
EKLP24
, Section 2.5]
says that every core coset decomposes into atoms. We slightly reformulate the result and include a proof for convenience.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
{p}
be a core
(I,J)
-coset. Then
p
has an atomic reduced expression. In fact,

(1)

For each
{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}s}\in\leftdes(\overline{{p}})\setminus I
, there is a reduced atomic expression of the form

{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}s}-t+\cdots].

(2)

For each
{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}s}\in\rightdes(\overline{{p}})\setminus J
, there is a reduced atomic expression of the form

{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+\cdots+u-{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}s}].

Remark 3.11
.

Note that

\leftdes(\overline{{p}})=I
implies
\overline{{p}}=w_{I}\in W_{I}
and thus
{p}
is a trivial
(I,I)
-coset with the empty atomic expression
[I]
.

Proof.

We prove the first claim by induction on
\overline{p}
. The second claim has a similar proof.
The base case is given by Remark
3.11
, which also allows us to assume that
I\subsetneq\leftdes(r)
.

Let
s\in\leftdes(r)\setminus I
.

[
EK23
, Proposition 4.21]
gives a reduced expression
r\leftrightharpoons[I,Is,\ldots,J]
. Let
n
be the
(Is,J)
-coset such that
r=[I,Is].n
.
The left redundancy of
n
is at most the size of
J
, so it is a proper subset of
Is
. By Proposition
3.2
, for any
t\in Is\setminus\leftred(n)
, the coset
n
has a reduced expression of the form
[Is,Is\setminus t,\ldots J]
. Let
q
be the
(Is\setminus t,J)
-coset such that
n=[Is,Is\setminus t].q
.
Then

r={\mathtt{a}}.q
for
{\mathtt{a}}\leftrightharpoons[I,Is,Is\setminus t]
. Then
{\mathtt{a}}
is an atom and

is a core coset by Lemma

. Since
\overline{q}<\overline{p}
(see Definition

), the induction step is established.
∎

5.
The atomic Matsumoto theorem

In this section we let
(W,S)
be an arbitrary Coxeter system and
I,J\subset S
be finitary subsets.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given a core
(I,J)
-coset
{p}
, any two atomic reduced expressions of
{p}
are related by a composition of atomic braid relations (associated to
(M,s,t)
for various
M\subset S
finitary and
s,t\in M
with
t\neq w_{M}sw_{M}
).

Proof.

Given two atomic expressions
L_{\bullet}
and
K_{\bullet}
of a core coset, we write
L_{\bullet}\sim K_{\bullet}
if
L_{\bullet}
and
K_{\bullet}
are related by a composition of atomic braid relations.
Recall that the
width
of an expression
[L_{0},L_{1},\cdots,L_{m}]
is the number
m
.

We proceed by induction on the minimal width of atomic reduced expressions of
{p}
. The base case
{p}\leftrightharpoons[I]
has a unique reduced expression, hence the claim of the theorem is trivially true.

Suppose that the claim is true for every core coset with an atomic reduced expression of width
<m
.
Let

(5.1)

p\leftrightharpoons L_{\bullet}=[L_{0},L_{1},\cdots,L_{m}]=[I+s-s^{\prime}+\ldots-s^{\prime\prime}]

and

(5.2)

p\leftrightharpoons K_{\bullet}=[K_{0},K_{1},\cdots,K_{l}]=[I+t-t^{\prime}+\ldots-t^{\prime\prime}]

be two atomic reduced expressions (where
l
is any positive integer).

If
s=t
, then we also have

s^{\prime}=w_{Is}sw_{Is}=t^{\prime}
since
L_{\bullet},K_{\bullet}
are atomic, thus
L_{i}=K_{i}
for
i=0,1,2
.

Since
L_{\bullet},K_{\bullet}
are reduced,

\overline{p}=w_{L_{1}}w_{L_{2}}^{-1}\overline{[L_{2},\cdots,L_{m}]}=w_{K_{1}}w_{K_{2}}^{-1}\overline{[K_{2},\cdots,K_{l}]}

holds by (
1.4
), where
\overline{M_{\bullet}}
denotes the maximal element in the coset
M_{\bullet}
expresses.
We deduce

[L_{2},\cdots,L_{m}]\leftrightharpoons[K_{2},\cdots,K_{l}]

whose sides are two atomic reduced expressions covered by the induction hypothesis.
Therefore,
the claim

L_{\bullet}\sim K_{\bullet}
follows from
[L_{2},\cdots,L_{m}]\sim[K_{2},\cdots,K_{l}]
.

It is thus enough to consider the case
s\neq t
. By
[
EK23
, Corollary 4.20]
, we have
s,t\in\leftdes(\overline{p})
and by
[
EK23
, Proposition 4.21]
there exist reduced expressions (called high roads) of the form

p\leftrightharpoons[I,Is,Ist]\circ J_{\bullet}\leftrightharpoons[I,It,Ist]\circ J_{\bullet},

where
J_{\bullet}
expresses some
(Ist,J)
-coset
{q}
.
Then we have

\underline{q}^{-1}J\underline{q}=\underline{q}^{-1}\underline{p}I\underline{p}^{-1}\underline{q}\subset W_{Ist}IW_{Ist}=W_{Ist}

where we use that
p
is core in the first equality and
\underline{q}\in W_{Ist}\underline{p}
in the inclusion.
By Kilmoyer’s theorem
[
EK23
, Lemma 2.14]
, we obtain
Ist\supset\underline{q}^{-1}J\underline{q}
and thus

\leftred(q)=Ist\cap\underline{q}^{-1}J\underline{q}=\underline{q}^{-1}J\underline{q}.

Note also that
|\leftred(q)|=|J|=|I|=|Ist|-2
. Naming
\{u,v\}=Ist\setminus\leftred(q)
, we have by Proposition
3.2

q\leftrightharpoons[Ist-u-v]\circ q^{\core}

from which it follows

(5.3)

p\leftrightharpoons[I+s+t-v-u]\circ q^{\core}

We may and do assume that
u\neq w_{Ist}^{-1}sw_{Ist}
and
v\neq w_{Ist}^{-1}tw_{Ist}
, since we can swap
u
and
v
if an equality holds.

Applying the switchback relation for
(Ist,{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}t},{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}v})
to (
5.3
), we get

(5.4)

p\leftrightharpoons[I+s{+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}t}-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}v}}-u]\circ q^{\core}\leftrightharpoons[I+s{-u_{1}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}u_{0}}-\ldots-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}u_{d}}+u_{d-1}}-u]\circ q^{\core}.

By Lemma
5.2
below, the expression
[I+s{-u_{1}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}u_{0}}-\ldots-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}u_{d}}+u_{d-1}}-u]
is atomic. Since
[I+s-s^{\prime}]
is also atomic (recall the atomic expression (
5.1
)) we have
u_{1}=s^{\prime}
.
In the rest of the proof we let
u^{\prime}:=u_{d-1}

to make the equations more readable.

Now, taking an atomic reduced expression
M_{\bullet}\leftrightharpoons q^{\core}
, we obtain the atomic expression

[L_{0},L_{1},L_{2}\cdots,L_{m}]=[I+s-s^{\prime}+\ldots-s^{\prime\prime}]\leftrightharpoons[I+s{-s^{\prime}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}t}-\ldots-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}v}+u^{\prime}}-u]\circ M_{\bullet}

and

(5.5)

[L_{2},\cdots,L_{m}]\leftrightharpoons[(Is\setminus s^{\prime})+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}t}-\ldots-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}v}+u^{\prime}-u]\circ M_{\bullet}

where the right hand side, being a subexpression of such, is an atomic reduced expression.
Then induction hypothesis gives
[L_{2},\cdots,L_{m}]\sim[(Is\setminus s^{\prime})+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}t}-\ldots-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}v}+u^{\prime}-u]\circ M_{\bullet}
, from which it follows

(5.6)

L_{\bullet}\sim[I+s{-s^{\prime}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,1}t}-\ldots-{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}v}+u^{\prime}}-u]\circ M_{\bullet}.

Since the same arguments establishes (an analogue of) (
5.6
) for
K_{\bullet}
, we
have

(5.7)

\displaystyle L_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq.indstep}\text{ for $L_{\bullet}$}}{\sim}[I+s\underbrace{-s^{\prime}+t-\ldots-v+u^{\prime}}-u]\circ M_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}switchback}}{\leftrightharpoons}[I+s\overbrace{+t-v}-u]\circ M_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\underset{downdown}{\overset{upup}{\leftrightharpoons}}[I+t\underbrace{+s-u}-v]\circ M_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\overset{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}switchback}}{\leftrightharpoons}[I+t\overbrace{-t^{\prime}+s-\ldots-u+v^{\prime}}-v]\circ M_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\underset{\eqref{eq.indstep}\text{ for $K_{\bullet}$}}{\sim}K_{\bullet},

where, by Proposition
4.18
, the middle three relations compose to an atomic braid relation. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If
u_{\bullet}
is a rotation sequence and

{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+s{-u_{1}+u_{0}-\ldots-u_{d}+u_{d-1}}-u]\circ{r}

is a core coset, then

[I+s{-u_{1}+u_{0}-\ldots-u_{d}+u_{d-1}}-u]
is an atomic expression.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, it is enough to show that
[I+s-u_{1}]
is atomic and that
[+u_{d-1}-u]
is atomic.

Let us prove that
[I+s-u_{1}]
is atomic. Suppose not. Then, by Proposition
4.5
, a switchback relation applies to
[I+s-u_{1}]
to give a reduced expression of
{p}
starting with some
[I\supset I^{\prime}]
.
Since the redundancy decreases in the first step of the latter expression (see also remark

), this contradicts

being a core coset (see e.g.,
[
EK23
, Lemma 4.25]
).

Similarly, if the subexpression

is not atomic, then remark

applies here to result in a contradictory size of the redundancy for

.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Theorem
5.1
does not say that the atomic reduced expressions are in correspondence with the reduced expressions in some Coxeter group.
Although this is true in type
A
and type

(as is proved in
[

), new structures appear in other types. See Section

.

Remark 5.4
.

Theorem

does not say that any relation between two atomic reduced expressions in

is a composition of atomic braid relations. For a core coset
{p}
and its atomic reduced expressions, it seems more reasonable to consider the rex graph whose edges are the atomic braid relations, rather than the full subgraph of the rex graph for

in

Corollary 5.5
.

All atomic reduced expressions of a given core coset are of the same width.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem

since every atomic braid relation relates expressions of the same width.
∎

The width of an atomic expression of a core coset

is an even number

, where

Corollary

says that the following is well-defined.

Definition 5.6
.

The

of a core coset
{p}
is half of the width of an atomic expression of
{p}
.

Remark 5.7
.

One may define the atomic length for all double cosets by setting the atomic length of

to be the atomic length of

, but this definition lacks desirable properties.
For example, the atomic length of

atomic.

6.
Non-braid relations

We continue letting
(W,S)
be an arbitrary Coxeter system.
Let us introduce two classes of non-braid relations between atomic expressions.

Definition 6.1
.

Associated to an atomic expression in
\mathcal{SC}
of the form

[I+s-s]
, we define the
atomic quadratic relation
as

(6.1)

[I+s\underbrace{-s+s}-s]\underset{*-quadratic}{\leftrightharpoons}[I+s-s].

Associated to an atomic expression in
\mathcal{SC}
of the form
[I+s-t]
with
s\neq t
, we define the
atomic cubic relation
as the composition (of two commuting relations)

(6.2)

[I+s\underbrace{-t+t}\overbrace{-s+s}-t]\overset{*-quadratic}{\underset{*-quadratic}{\leftrightharpoons}}[I+s-t].

The relation (
6.2
) still holds when
s=t
but is generated by (
6.1
), so we exclude such cases.

Remark 6.2
.

The left hand side of the atomic quadratic relation (
6.1
) and the atomic cubic relation (
6.2
) are indeed atomic expressions. The only nontrivial part is
[(Is\setminus t)+t-s]
in (
6.2
) being atomic, but this follows from Remark
3.7
since the latter is the
reverse
of the atomic expression
[I+s-t]
.

The atomic quadratic relation (or the absence of other quadratic relations) is motivated by the following statement from
[
EKLP24
]
(see
[
EKLP24
, Lemma 2.10, Proposition 2.20]
). We include a proof for convenience.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
{\mathtt{a}}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t]
be an atomic
(I,J)
-expression (where
J=Is\setminus t
) and
{\mathtt{b}}\leftrightharpoons[J+u-v]
be an atomic
(J,K)
-expression.

(1)

If
{p}={\mathtt{a}}*{\mathtt{b}}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t]\circ[J+u-v]=[I+s-t+u-v]
is reduced, then
{p}
is a core coset.

(2)

If the composition
{p}={\mathtt{a}}*{\mathtt{b}}
is not reduced, then
{p}
is core if and only if
I=J=K
and
s=t=u=v
, in which case
{p}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-s]
.

Proof.

The first part is a special case of Lemma
3.4
. The ‘if’ claim follows from the
*
-quadratic relation
[Is-s+s]\leftrightharpoons[Is]
which also gives
p\leftrightharpoons[I+s-s]
in this case.

Now suppose
p={\mathtt{a}}*{\mathtt{b}}\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t+u-v]
is not reduced while
p
is core. Then
q\leftrightharpoons[I+s-t+u]
is not reduced, that is,
\underline{q}<\underline{{\mathtt{a}}}
or
\leftred(q)\supsetneq\leftred({\mathtt{a}})
. The latter is not the case since
{\mathtt{a}}
is core.
Since
\underline{{\mathtt{a}}}=\underline{q}.x
for some

and since the only right descent of
\underline{{\mathtt{a}}}
is

, we have

, thus

. It follows

and

.
Finally, the

-quadratic relation gives

implying that

is an atom and thus

. The other equalities follows.
∎

Recall that by an atomic expression we mean a composition of (the unique reduced expressions of) atomic cosets, which is not necessarily reduced or core. We introduce an auxiliary definition useful for this section.

Definition 6.4
.

An atomic expression
[I_{0},\cdots,I_{2\ell}]
is said to be
admissible
if each
[I_{0}\cdots,I_{2m}]
for
m\leq\ell
expresses a core coset.

Remark 6.5
.

A reduced atomic expression
[I_{0},\cdots,I_{2\ell}]
is automatically admissible since the redundancies for all
p_{i}\leftrightharpoons[I_{0},\cdots,I_{i}]
are the same (see Lemma
3.3
).
In this case, every atomic subexpression
[I_{2k},...,I_{2m}]
expresses a core coset. In general we do not know whether an atomic subexpression of an admissible expression is also an expression of a core coset.

We then have the following partial analogue of the Coxeter presentation.

Proposition 6.6
.

A non-reduced admissible atomic expression is related to a reduced atomic expression by a composition of atomic braid
and
atomic quadratic relations
. Moreover, atomic quadratic relations may be applied only from the left hand side to the right hand side of (
6.1
).

Our proof of Proposition
6.6
uses the following analogue of the (weak) exchange property for Coxeter groups.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
{q}
be a core
(I,J)
-coset and suppose
r=q\circ[J+s]
is a (well-defined) non-reduced composition.

(1)

We have
\underline{q}>\underline{r}
.

(2)

The core coset
{q}
has an atomic reduced expression ending in
[-s]
.

Proof.

Since
{q}\subset r
we have
\underline{q}\geq\underline{r}
.
Suppose
\underline{q}=\underline{r}
. Then

\rightred(r)\underset{def}{=}Js\cap\underline{r}^{-1}I\underline{r}\underset{\underline{q}=\underline{r}}{=}Js\cap\underline{q}^{-1}I\underline{q}=Js\cap J=\rightred(q),

where we use that
{q}

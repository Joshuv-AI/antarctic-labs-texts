Inflation in f(R,T) gravity with Double-Well potential
Biswajit Deb, Atri Deshamukhya

Abstract
In this piece of work, we studied the inflation in the context of f ⁡ ( R , T ) {f(R,T)} theory of gravity. We assumed the functional form of f ⁡ ( R , T ) {f(R,T)} to be R + 16 ​ π ​ G ​ λ ​ T R+16\pi G\lambda T , where R is the Ricci scalar, T is the trace of the Energy-Momentum tensor and λ \lambda is the model parameter. The cosmological observable parameters like scalar spectral index n s n_{s} and tensor-to-scalar ratio r r are estimated for Double-Well potential. We found that for λ = 115 \lambda=115 , n s n_{s} and r r are in good agreement with Planck 2018 data. Further, considering the vacuum expectation value in Double-Well potential to be Planckian, we observed the admissible range of model parameter to be 101 < λ < 130 101<\lambda<130 for which this model remains consistent with Planck 2018 data.

2
f(R,T)
gravity in nutshell

The action in
{f(R,T)}
gravity theory as proposed by Harko
[
12
]
is given by,

S=\int\left[\frac{f(R,T)}{16\pi G}+L_{m}\right]\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x

(1)

where
{f(R,T)}
is an arbitrary function of Ricci scalar R and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\alpha\beta}
,
L_{m}
is the matter Lagrangian density, g is the metric determinant and G is the Newtonian gravitational constant. On variation of the action with respect to the metric, we obtain the
{f(R,T)}
gravity field equation as,

f_{R}(R,T)R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}f(R,T)+[g_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla^{\sigma}-\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}]f_{R}(R,T)=8\pi GT_{\alpha\beta}-f_{T}(R,T)(T_{\alpha\beta}+\Theta_{\alpha\beta})

(2)

where we have denoted
f_{R}(R,T)=\frac{\partial f(R,T)}{\partial R}
,
f_{T}(R,T)=\frac{\partial f(R,T)}{\partial T}
and defined
T_{\alpha\beta}
and
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
as,

T_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}L_{m}-2\frac{\delta L_{m}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}

(3)

\Theta_{\alpha\beta}=g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta T_{\mu\nu}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}=-2T_{\alpha\beta}+g_{\alpha\beta}L_{m}-2\frac{\delta^{2}L_{m}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\mu\nu}}

(4)

This term
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
plays a crucial role in
{f(R,T)}
gravity. Since it contains matter Lagrangian
L_{m}
, depending on the nature of the matter field, the field equation for
{f(R,T)}
gravity will be different. Besides this the functional form of
{f(R,T)}
will also change the field equation. Thus, the field equation in
{f(R,T)}
gravity depends both on the nature of matter field and choice of the function
f
.

Harko proposed four different forms of the function
{f(R,T)}
in his paper
[
12
]
, viz.:

•

f(R,T)=R+2f(T)

•

f(R,T)=f_{1}(R)+f_{2}(T)

•

f(R,T)=f_{1}(R)+f_{2}(R)f_{3}(T)

•

f(R,T^{\phi})=R+f(T^{\phi})
, where
\phi
is a self interacting scalar filed.

Apart from the above forms, Mauricio
[
14
]
proposed two other forms of
{f(R,T)}
which are as follows:

•

f(R,T)=R+\alpha RT

•

f(R,T)=R+\alpha e^{RT}
,
\alpha
is a constant.

In this work, we have considered the simplest form of
{f(R,T)}
which is
f(R,T)=R+16\pi G\lambda T
, where
\lambda
is the model parameter. In this particular case the action becomes,

S=\int\left[\frac{R}{16\pi G}+\lambda T+L_{m}\right]\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x

(5)

and the field equation will take the following form,

R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}R=8\pi GT_{\alpha\beta}^{(eff)}

(6)

where
T_{\alpha\beta}^{(eff)}
is the effective stress-energy tensor given by,

T_{\alpha\beta}^{(eff)}=T_{\alpha\beta}-2\lambda(T_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}Tg_{\alpha\beta}+\Theta_{\alpha\beta})

(7)

It is clear that when
\lambda=0
, above field equation immediately reduces to Einstein’s general field equation.

3
Inflation in
f(R,T)
gravity

To understand the cosmological implications of
{f(R,T)}
gravity, let us assume the Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walkar (FLRW) metric in spherical coordinate,

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}\left[\frac{dr^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\right]

(8)

where a(t) is the scale factor with
t
being the cosmic time. The constant k is the spatial curvature with value, +1, -1, and 0 which corresponds to close, open and flat universe respectively. The current observation shows that the present universe is spatially flat and hence we will assume k=0.

Considering the above metric, various components of Ricci tensor are as follows
[
55
]
:

\displaystyle R_{00}

\displaystyle=-3\frac{\dot{a}}{a},

\displaystyle R_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}+2\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}\right]g_{ij},

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=6\left[\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}+\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}\right]

(9)

In order to describe the inflation, we have to introduce a homogeneous scalar field
\phi
minimally coupled to gravity called inflaton
[
56
]
. Then the matter Lagrangian will take the form,

\displaystyle L_{m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi-V(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)

(10)

where
V(\phi)
is the potential whose form will be discussed later on. Then the energy-momentum tensor takes the form,

T_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+g_{\alpha\beta}[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)]

(11)

From this we can calculate the energy density
\rho
and pressure p component of
T_{\alpha\beta}
as,

T_{00}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)=\rho

(12)

T_{ij}=\left[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)\right]g_{ij}=pg_{ij}

(13)

Now we will evaluate the different components of the tensor
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
. For the matter Lagrangian given in Eq. (
10
), the tensor
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
becomes,

\Theta_{\alpha\beta}=-2\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi-g_{\alpha\beta}\left[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)\right]

(14)

and its components are as follow:

\displaystyle\Theta_{00}

\displaystyle=-T_{00}-\dot{\phi}^{2};

\displaystyle\Theta_{ij}

\displaystyle=-T_{ij}

(15)

We will now proceed to compute the components of effective energy-momentum tensor defined in Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle T_{00}^{(eff)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)=\rho^{(eff)}

(16)

\displaystyle T_{ij}^{(eff)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)-V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)g_{ij}=p^{(eff)}g_{ij}

(17)

From Eq. (
16
) and (
17
) we can derive the effective equation of state as the ratio of effective pressure and energy density,

w^{(eff)}=\frac{p^{(eff)}}{\rho^{(eff)}}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)-2V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)}{\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+2V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)}

(18)

Now, the 00 component of field Eq. (
6
) yields,

H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}(1+2\lambda)+V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)\right]

(19)

which is known as the modified Friedmann first equation. Here the Hubble parameter is defined as
H=\frac{\dot{a}}{a}
. Similarly, computation of the ij component yields,

\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left[\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)\right]

(20)

This is called the modified acceleration equation.

From this, we can obtain the expression for
\dot{H}
as,

\dot{H}=\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}-H^{2}=-\frac{8\pi G}{2}(p^{eff}+\rho^{eff})=-4\pi G\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)

(21)

Both the above Eq. (
20
) and (
21
) are known as modified Friedmann second equation.
Now, using Eq. (
16
) and (
17
) we can derive the continuity equation or the modified Klein-Gordon equation as,

\ddot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)+3H\dot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)+\frac{dV}{d\phi}(1+4\lambda)=0

(22)

Now,to check whether slow roll inflation is viable in this model, the standard slow-roll parameters need to be computed for this model. One of the standard slow-roll parameter denoted by
\epsilon

[
56
,
57
]
in terms of parameters of this model are found as: ,

\epsilon=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=\frac{3\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)}{\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+2V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)}

(23)

Accelerated expansion occurs in slow roll regime if
\epsilon<<1
, that is when the potential energy of the inflaton dominates over the kinetic energy. Under this condition the inflaton rolls down slowly and hence the name, slow-roll condition
[
56
]
. In terms of current model parameters, the first slow roll condition reads as,

\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)<<V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)

(24)

Again, the accelerated expansion must sustain for a long period of time to achieve the desired amount of e-folds and hence we need another condition to be imposed. In terms of our model parameters this reads as
[
56
]
,

\ddot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)<<|3H\dot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)|

(25)

This defines the second slow-roll condition
|\eta|<<1
,
[
56
,
57
]
as:

\eta=-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{H\dot{\phi}}

(26)

The parameters defined in Eq.
23
and
26
are often called Hubble slow-roll parameters
[
56
]
. These parameters can also be defined in terms of the potential and are called potential slow-roll parameters
\epsilon_{V}
and
\eta_{V}

[
56
,
57
]
.

We obtain the potential slow-roll parameters in the proposed model in terms of reduced Planck mass
M_{P}^{2}=\frac{1}{8\pi G}
, where
M_{P}=2.4\times 10^{18}GeV
as
[
57
]
,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\eta_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}M_{P}^{2}\frac{V^{\prime\prime}}{V}

(27)

From the above expressions, it is clear that the affect of the correction due to f(R,T) chosen has been induced in the slow-roll parameters. If
\epsilon_{V},|\eta_{V}|<<1
holds in a region of space, then slow roll inflation can happen
[
56
]
. It may be noted that the slow-roll inflation ends as soon as either of the slow roll parameters becomes of the order of unity.

Another important quantity to be studied in any inflationary model is the amount of inflation that has taken place. It is quantified by the no of e-folding that has occurred before the end of inflation and is defined by the natural logarithm of the scale factor as,

N=\ln a=\int_{\phi_{i}}^{\phi_{end}}\frac{H}{\dot{\phi}}\,d\phi\

(28)

where
\phi_{end}
is the value of the inflaton field at the end of the inflation. Under slow-roll approximation, the number of e-folding becomes,

N=\frac{1+2\lambda}{M_{P}^{2}}\int_{\phi_{end}}^{\phi_{i}}\frac{V(\phi)}{V^{\prime}(\phi)}\,d\phi\

(29)

The spectral indices and tensor-to-scalar ratio in terms of the slow-roll parameters can be written as
[
56
,
58
]
,

\displaystyle n_{s}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{d\ln(\Delta_{s}^{2})}{d\ln(k)}=-6\epsilon_{V}+2\eta_{V}

\displaystyle n_{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\ln(\Delta_{T}^{2})}{d\ln(k)}=-2\epsilon_{V}

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=16\epsilon_{V}

(30)

where
\Delta_{s}
and
\Delta_{T}
are the dimensionless scalar and tensor power spectrum respectively.

Double-Well potential popularly known as "Maxican hat" potential was first introduced by Goldstone

V(\phi)=M^{4}\left[\left(\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\right)^{2}-1\right]^{2}

[

61

\phi_{0}

10^{15}GeV

[
53
]
. A dimension-less parameter

has been introduced for convenience of computation. Fig.1 shows the variation of the potential in terms of the dimensionless parameter

5
Discussion and Conclusion

Inflation has been the most accepted theory for describing the primordial universe. Recently, inflation has been studied in the frame work of
{f(R,T)}
gravity with different potentials like quadratic potential, natural, hilltop potential and Starobinsky potential
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. Now, the functional form of
{f(R,T)}
is a bit arbitrary and model parameters can be included for fine tuning to fit the observational data. In this work we have taken the simplest yet the most used form
f(R,T)=R+16\pi G\lambda T
, where
\lambda
is the model parameter. We reviewed the slow-roll inflation in the context of
{f(R,T)}
gravity and it is found that correction due to the trace term has induced in all important equations.

Then we introduced Double-Well potential and computed the cosmological observables for the model. We found that for
\lambda=115
, the scalar spectral index is
n_{s}=0.965324
and tensor-to-scalar ratio is
r=0.000141
which are in good agreement with Planck 2018 data. Further, it is seen the with the increase in
\lambda
, tensor-to-scalar ratio decreases. So, in future if further constraints will be imposed on r then the correction from
{f(R,T)}
will help the model to fit the observational data. We then obtain the permissible range of the model parameter to be
101<\lambda<130
in order to fit the Planck 2018 data. These results are valid for this model only. For other functional form of
{f(R,T)}
result will vary.

Another important point to be discussed is that in case of Double-Well potential vacuum expectation value needs to be Super Planckian in order to match the results of the model with the observational data. But in our study we have set the vacuum expectation value at the Planck scale and obtain model results which are consistent with the observational CMB data. It implies that the inflaton vacuum expectation value in Double-Well inflation (DWI) can be reduced from Super Planckian to Planckian scale if we include a correction trace term in the theory. This
{f(R,T)}
correction helps the DWI to be consistent with the Particle physics and effective field theory.

Now as a possible extension of our work, one can study the same model for different other potentials. For a plethora of potential, one may check this reference
[
53
]
. Besides this, other forms of
{f(R,T)}
as mentioned in the section 2 may be studied with different potentials. Further, Palatini and Metric-affine versions of
{f(R,T)}
gravity may be checked to study inflation for different potentials which may generate interesting results.

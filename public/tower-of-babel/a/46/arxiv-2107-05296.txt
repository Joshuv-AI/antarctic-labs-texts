Separating LREC from LFP Thanks: Research funded in part by EPSRC grant EP/S03238X/1.
Anuj Dawar, Felipe Ferreira Santos

Abstract
LREC = \textsf{LREC}_{=} is an extension of first-order logic with a logarithmic recursion operator. It was introduced by Grohe et al. and shown to capture the complexity class L \mathrm{L} over trees and interval graphs. It does not capture L \mathrm{L} in general as it is contained in FPC —fixed-point logic with counting. We show that this containment is strict. In particular, we show that the path systems problem, a classic P \mathrm{P} -complete problem which is definable in LFP —fixed-point logic—is not definable in LREC = \textsf{LREC}_{=} . This shows that the logarithmic recursion mechanism is provably weaker than general least fixed points. The proof is based on a novel Spoiler-Duplicator game tailored for this logic.



1
Introduction

The founding result of the subject of descriptive complexity is Fagin’s characterization of
\mathrm{NP}
as the class of properties definable in existential second-order logic
[
6
]
. Similar characterizations for complexity classes below
\mathrm{NP}
are not known and remain an active area of investigation. Much attention has been devoted to the question of whether there is a logic for
\mathrm{P}
, and this is often said to be the central open question of descriptive complexity. Perhaps less well known but still wide open is the question of whether there is a logic giving a descriptive characterization of
\mathrm{L}
—the class of properties of finite structures decidable deterministically in logarithmic space.

Immerman established logical characterizations of
\mathrm{P}
and
\mathrm{L}
on
ordered
structures. The former is given by the properties definable in
LFP
—the extension of first-order logic with a least fixed-point operator (a result obtained independently by Vardi)—and the latter by
DTC
—the extension of first-order logic with a deterministic transitive closure operator. These logics are known to be strictly weaker than the corresponding complexity classes in the absence of a built-in order (see
[
14
]
for details). Moreover, on unordered structures, it is known that the expressive power of
DTC
is strictly weaker than that of
LFP

[
7
]
, whereas the corresponding question for ordered structures is equivalent to the separation of
\mathrm{L}
from
\mathrm{P}
.

Since the simplest properties separating
LFP
and
DTC
from the complexity classes
\mathrm{P}
and
\mathrm{L}
respectively are counting properties, there has also been much interest in the extensions of these logics with counting mechanisms. The logic
FPC
—fixed-point logic with counting—is widely studied (see
[
3
]
for a brief introduction). Though it is known to be strictly weaker than
\mathrm{P}
by a celebrated result of Cai, Fürer and Immerman
[
2
]
, it gives a robust definition of the class of problems in
\mathrm{P}
which are symmetrically solvable
[
1
]
and captures
\mathrm{P}
on a wide range of graph classes. Most significantly, Grohe has proved that any polynomial-time property of graphs that excludes a fixed graph as a minor is definable in
FPC

[
10
]
. Results showing that
FPC
captures
\mathrm{P}
on a class of structures
\mathcal{C}
(including Grohe’s theorem) are typically established by showing that a polynomial-time canonical labelling algorithm on the class
\mathcal{C}
can be implemented in
FPC
. Such results were established for specific classes of graphs such as trees
[
15
]
and graphs of bounded tree-width
[
9
]
before Grohe’s theorem which supersedes them all.

The situation for logics capturing
\mathrm{L}
is less clear-cut, even for classes of structures where it is known that logarithmic-space canonical labelling algorithms are possible. For instance, Etessami and Immerman
[
5
]
showed that the extension of
DTC
with counting fails to capture
\mathrm{L}
even on trees. What this suggests is that the weakness of
DTC
is not just in the lack of a means of counting but that the recursion mechanism embodied in the deterministic transitive closure operator is too weak. An interesting suggestion to remedy this is in the logic
LREC
introduced in
[
11
]
, which incorporates a rich recursion mechanism which can still be evaluated within logarithmic space. To be precise, the paper introduces two versions of the logic, one called
LREC
and the second
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
. While the first is shown to be sufficient to capture the complexity class
\mathrm{L}
on trees, it is the latter that is the more robust logic. In particular,
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
properly extends
LREC
in expressive power, is closed under first-order interpretations and (unlike
LREC
) can express undirected reachability. Moreover,
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
has also been shown to capture
\mathrm{L}
on other interesting classes of structures
[
13
]
.

It is known that
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
does not capture
\mathrm{L}
on all graphs. In particular,
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
is included in
FPC
and the CFI construction that separates
FPC
from
\mathrm{P}
can be suitably padded to give a separation of
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
from
\mathrm{L}

[
8
]
. While Grohe et al. proved that
LREC
is properly contained in
FPC
(for instance, it cannot express reachability on undirected graphs), the question was left open for the stronger logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
. We settle this question in the present paper. That is, we prove that the expressive power of
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
is strictly weaker than
FPC
. Indeed, we show that the
path systems problem
(PSP), a natural
\mathrm{P}
-complete problem that is expressible in
LFP
(even without counting) is not expressible in
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
. The result is established by first defining an Ehrenfeucht-style pebble game for the
L
-recursion quantifier used in the logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
and then deploying it on an intricate class of instances of PSP. Describing the winning Duplicator strategy in the game is challenging and takes up the bulk of the paper.

2
Preliminaries

We assume that the reader is familiar with the definitions of the
basic logics used in finite model theory, in particular first-order
logic
FO
, fixed-point logic
LFP
and their extension swith
coungint,
FOC
and
FPC
respectively. These definitions can be found in standard
textbooks
[
4
,
16
]
. We consider vocabularies with relation
and constant symbols, which we call
relational vocabularies
for
short. Formulas in a vocabulary
\sigma
are interepreted in finite

\sigma
-structures. We often use Fraktur letters

\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\ldots
for structures and the corresponding Roman
letter
A,B,\ldots
to denote the universe of the structure.

In the case of logics with counting, such as
FOC
and

FPC
, the interpretation also involves a
number domain
. The
logic allows for two kinds of variables (and more generally, two kinds
of terms) which range over elements and numbers respectively. Thus,
we consider a
\sigma
-structure
\mathfrak{A}
to be extended with a
domain of non-negative integers up to the cardinality of
\mathfrak{A}
.
Element variables are interpreted by elements of
\mathfrak{A}
and number
variables by elements of the number domain. We write
\mathcal{N}(A)
to
denote the number domain associated with the structure
\mathfrak{A}
. This
can be identified with the set
\{0,\ldots,|A|\}
and we assume this
is disjoint from the set
A
. The logics include a counting operator
and we assume a normal form where every occurrence of the counting
operator is in a formula of the form
[\#x\varphi]=\nu
where
\nu

is a number variable. This formula is to be read as saying that the
number of elements
x
satisfying
\varphi
is
\nu
.

Any formula in any of the logics defines a
query
.
If
\varphi
is a formula in vocabulary
\sigma
and
\vec{x}
is an
m
-tuple of element variables such
that all free variables of
\varphi
are among
\vec{x}
, then
\varphi(x)

defines an
m
-ary query
. This is a map taking each

\sigma
-structure
\mathfrak{A}
to an
m
-ary relation
\varphi^{\mathfrak{A}}
on
the universe of
\mathfrak{A}
containing the tuples
\vec{a}
such that

\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi[\vec{a}]
. If
m=0
and so
\varphi
is a sentence,
we call the query a
Boolean query
. A Boolean query is often
identified with a class of
\sigma
-structures. Note that the query is not completely specified by the formula
\varphi
alone, but rather by the formula along with the tuple of variables
\vec{x}
. In particular the order on the variables is given by the tuple.

To allow for formulas which may also have free number variables, we consider
numerical queries
. An
m
-ary numerical query is a map
Q
that takes any
\sigma
-structures
\mathfrak{A}
to a relation
Q(\mathfrak{A})\subseteq A^{m}\times\mathbb{N}
and is invariant under isomorphisms. The last condition means that if
\eta:A\rightarrow B
is an isomorphism from
\mathfrak{A}
to
\mathfrak{B}
, then
(\vec{a},n)\in Q(\mathfrak{A})
if, and only if,
(\eta(\vec{a}),n)\in Q(\mathfrak{B})
. Such queries arise quite naturally. As an example, the function that takes a graph
G
to the number of connected components in
G
is a
0
-ary numerical query. The function taking a graph
G
to the set of pairs
(a,n)
where
a
is a vertex in
G
and
n
the number of vertices in the connected component of
a
is a
1
-ary numerical query.

Suppose
\varphi
is a formula of a logic with counting, such as
FOC
or
FPC
, with free variables among
\vec{x}
, where
\vec{x}
contains
m_{1}
element variables and
m_{2}
number variables. Without loss of generality we assume all element variables appear in
\vec{x}
before the number variables. Interpreted in a structure
\mathfrak{A}
, the formula
\varphi
defines a relation of mixed type: a subset of
A^{m_{1}}\times\mathcal{N}(A)^{m_{2}}
. We treat the tuples of numbers as coding positive integers in a natural way. So, suppose
n=|A|
and therefore
\mathcal{N}(A)=\{0,\ldots,n\}
. For a tuple
\vec{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m_{2}})
we write
\langle\vec{r}\rangle
denote the number

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m_{2}}(n+1)^{i-1}r_{i}.

In this way, we can see
\varphi
as defining an
m_{1}
-ary numerical query which takes
\mathfrak{A}
to the set
\{(\vec{a},\langle\vec{r}\rangle)\mid\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi[\vec{a},\vec{r}]\}
.

2.1
Generalized Quantifiers and Operators

In the next subsection we define the logics
LREC
and
\textsf{LREC}_{=}

which were introduced by Grohe et al.
[
12
]
. They were
originally defined as an extension of first-order logic with a new
kind of recursion operator called
L
-recursion, designed to be
computable in logspace. We present an equivalent formulation through
a variation of generalized quantifiers in the style of Lindström.
To do this, we first briefly review interpretations and generalized quantifiers. In what follows, fix a logic
\mathcal{L}
, which can be any of the logics
FO
,
FOC
,
LFP
or
FPC
. Whenever we refer to formulas, this is to be read as formulas of
\mathcal{L}
.

Given two relational vocabularies
\sigma
and
\tau
and a positive
integer
d
, where
\tau=(R_{1},\ldots,R_{s})
contains no constant
symbols, an interpretation of
\tau
in
\sigma
of dimension
d
is a
sequence
\mathcal{I}
of formulas

(\delta(\vec{x},\vec{w}),\varepsilon(\vec{x},\vec{y},\vec{w}),\varphi_{R_{1}}(\vec{z}_{1},\vec{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{R_{s}}(\vec{z}_{s},\vec{w})),

where
\vec{x}
and
\vec{y}
are tuples of variables of length
d
,
and each
\vec{z}_{i}
is a tuple of variables of length
dr_{i}
where

r_{i}
is the arity of the relation symbol
R_{i}
. The variables
\vec{w}
are the
parameter variables
. Note that all tuples of variables may contain both element variables and number variables.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
\sigma
structure and
\vec{p}
be an interpretation for the parameter variables
\vec{w}
. The interpretation
\mathcal{I}
associates a
\tau
-structure
\mathfrak{B}
to

\mathfrak{A},\vec{p}
if there is a map
h
from
\{\vec{a}\in(A\cup\mathcal{N}(A))^{d}\mid\mathfrak{A}\models\delta[\vec{a},\vec{p}]\}
to the universe
B
of
\mathfrak{B}

such that: (i)
h
is surjective onto
B
; (ii)
h(\vec{a}_{1})=h(\vec{a}_{2})
if, and only if,
\mathfrak{A}\models\varepsilon[\vec{a}_{1},\vec{a}_{2},\vec{p}]
; and (iii)
R^{\mathfrak{B}}(h(\vec{a}_{1}),\dots,h(\vec{a}_{k}))

if, and only if,
\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi_{R}[\vec{a}_{1},\dots,\vec{a}_{k},\vec{p}]
. Note that an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
associates
a
\tau
-structure with
\mathfrak{A},\vec{p}
only if
\varepsilon
defines an
equivalence relation on
(A\cup\mathcal{N}(A))^{d}
that is a congruence with respect to the
relations defined by the formulaa
\varphi_{R}
. In such
cases, however,
\mathfrak{B}
is uniquely defined up to isomorphism and we
write
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{A},\vec{p})=\mathfrak{B}
.

There are many kinds of interpretation that are defined in the
literature. The ones we have defined here are fairly generous, in
that they allow for
relativization
,
vectorization
and

quotienting
. The first of these means that the universe of

\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{A})
is defined as a subset of
(A\cup\mathcal{N}(A))^{d}
rather
than the whole set. The formula
\delta
is the relativizing
formula. Vecotorization refers to the fact that
d
can be greater
than
1
and so
\mathcal{I}
can map
\sigma
-structures to structures that
are polynomially larger, and quotienting refers to the fact that the
universe is obtained by quotienting with the congruence relation
defined by
\varepsilon
.

Let
K
be an isomorphism-closed class of
\tau
-structures (or equivalently a
Boolean query). A standard way of extending any logic
\mathcal{L}
to obtain a minimal extension
\mathcal{L}[K]
in which the Boolean query
K
can be expressed is to adjoin to
\mathcal{L}
the Lindström quantifier
Q_{K}
, corresponding to
K
(see
[
4
, Chapt. 12]
). This allows us to write formulas of the form
Q_{K}\mathcal{I}
where
\mathcal{I}
is an interpretation as above. The quantifier
Q_{K}
binds the variables
\vec{x},\vec{y},\vec{z}_{1},\ldots,\vec{z}_{s}
appearing in
\mathcal{I}
so the free varialbles of
Q_{K}\mathcal{I}
are
\vec{w}
. The formula
Q_{K}\mathcal{I}
is true in a
\sigma
-structure
\mathfrak{A}
with an intepretation
\vec{p}
for the free variables if, and only if,
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{A},\vec{p})
is in
K
.

We generalize such quantifiers to queries that are not necessarily Boolean. In general, consider an
m
-ary numerical query
K
over
\tau
-structures. We define
\mathcal{L}[K]
, the extension of the logic
\mathcal{L}
with an operator for
K
as follows. Let
\mathcal{I}=(\delta(\vec{x},\vec{w}),\varepsilon(\vec{x},\vec{y},\vec{w}),\varphi_{R_{1}}(\vec{z}_{1},\vec{w}),\ldots,\varphi_{R_{s}}(\vec{z}_{s},\vec{w}))
be an interpretation of
\tau
in
\sigma
of dimension
d
. Let
\vec{u}
be a tuple of variables of length
md
and
\vec{s}
a tuple of number terms. Then,

[{Q_{K}}_{\vec{x},\vec{y},\vec{z}_{1},\ldots,\vec{z}_{s}}\mathcal{I}](\vec{u},\vec{s})

is a formula with free variables
\vec{w},\vec{u},\vec{s}
. This formula is true in a
\sigma
-structure
\mathfrak{A}
, with an interpretation of
\vec{p}
for
\vec{w}
,
\vec{a}
for
\vec{u}
and
\vec{r}
for
\vec{s}
if, and only if,

([\vec{a}]_{\varepsilon},\langle\vec{r}\rangle)\in K(\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{A},\vec{p})).

Here,
[\vec{a}]_{\varepsilon}
denotes the
m
-tuple of elements of
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{A},\vec{p})
obtained by taking the equivalence classes of the
m

d
-tuples that make up
\vec{a}
under the equivalence relation defined by
\varepsilon
.

2.2
LREC

=

In this subsection we define
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
, the logic in which we prove the inexpressibility result. We define the logic as an extension of
FOC
by means of a generalized operator in the sense of Section
2.1
above. This definition is superficially different from that of Grohe et al.
[
11
]
where it is defined as a restricted fixed-point operator, but the two definitions are easily seen to be equivalent.

We first define a unary numeric query
\chi
on a class of
labelled

graphs. These are directed graphs
G=(V,E)
together with a
labelling
C
that gives for each vertex
v\in V
a set of numbers

C(v)\subseteq\mathcal{N}(V)
.
For each such
(G,C)
we define

\chi(G,C)\subseteq V\times\mathbb{N}
. To do this, we first
introduce some notation. For any binary relation
R\subseteq A^{2}
,
and
a\in A
, let
aR
denote the set
\{b\in A\mid(a,b)\in R\}

and
Ra
denote
\{b\in A\mid(b,a)\in R\}
. Then
\chi
is
defined by recursion on
l
by the following rule:

(u,\ell)\in\chi(G,C):\iff\ell\geq 0\text{ and }|\{v\in uE\mid(v,\lfloor(\ell-1)/|Ev|\rfloor)\in\chi(G,C)\}|\in C(u).

The logic
LREC
is the closure of
FOC
with a generalized operator
for computing the numeric query
\chi
. Note that to get this
formally, we have to extend the idea of interpretation we had earlier
to be able to produce a
labelled
graph. In a logic with
couting, this is easily achieved as the interpretation will allow for
formulas defining relations of mixed element and number type.

To define the logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
, we considered labelled

semi-graphs
.
A semi-graph is a structure
G=(V,E,\sim)
, where
V
is a set of
nodes, and
E
and
\sim
are two binary relations on
V
. Again, we
consider a semi-graph together with a labelling
C
such that for each

v\in V
,
C(v)\subseteq\mathcal{N}(V)
.
Let
\cong
be the symmetric reflexive transitive closure of
\sim

and
[v]
denote the equivalence class of
v
under
\cong
. Write

V/_{\cong}
for set of such equivalence classes, i.e. the quotient
of
V
under this equivalence relation. We write
[G]
for
the directed graph with vertex set
V/_{\cong}
and edge set

[E]:=\{([a],[b])\mid(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in E\text{ for some }a^{\prime}\in[a]\text{ and }b^{\prime}\in[b]\}.

Write
\hat{C}
for the labelling of
[G]
given by
\hat{C}([v])=\bigcup_{u\in[v]}C(u)
. We can then define the numeric query

\hat{\chi}
on labelled semi-graphs by the condition:

\hat{\chi}(G,C)=\chi([G],\hat{C}).

The logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
is then defined as the closure of
FOC
under a
generalized operator for the numeric query
\hat{\chi}
. For
simplicity, we only allow applications of the operator to
interpretations without relativization of quotienting. We explian below
why this is no loss of generality. Indeed, it is also easily checked
that this restricted definition corresponds exactly to the original definition
of the logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
given by Grohe et al.

The syntax of the logic can then be defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

For a vocabulary
\tau
, the set of formulas of
\textsf{LREC}_{=}[\tau]
is defined by extending
\textsf{FOC}[\tau]
formula formation rules by the following rule.
Suppose
\varphi_{\sim}(\vec{u},\vec{v}),\varphi_{F}(\vec{u},\vec{v})
, and
\varphi_{C}(\vec{u},\vec{p})
are
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
formulas where
\vec{u}
,
\vec{v}
and
\vec{w}
are tuples of variables all of length
c
;
\vec{p}
is a tuple of
number
variables of length
d\leq c
and
\vec{r}
is a tuple of number variables of length
q
, then

\varphi:=[\textsf{lrec}_{\vec{u},\vec{v},\vec{p}}\varphi_{E},\varphi_{\sim},\varphi_{C}](\vec{w},\vec{r})

is an
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
formula. The free variables of the formula are those in
\vec{w}
and
\vec{r}
, along with the free variables in
\varphi_{E}
and
\varphi_{\sim}
that are not among
\vec{u}
and
\vec{v}
and those of
\varphi_{C}
that are not among
\vec{u}
and
\vec{p}
.

To define the semantics of the logic, consider the formula
\textsf{lrec}_{\vec{u},\vec{v},\vec{p}}\varphi_{E},\varphi_{\sim},\varphi_{C}](\vec{w},\vec{r})
interpreted in a
\tau
-structure
\mathfrak{A}
. Let the semigraph
G=(A^{c},E,\sim)
be given by the interpretation
\varphi_{E},\varphi_{\sim}
on
\mathfrak{A}
and
C
be the labelling defined by
\varphi_{C}
. Then the formula is satisfied in
\mathfrak{A}
with
\vec{a}
and
\vec{s}
interpreting
\vec{w}
and
\vec{r}
respectively if, and only if,
(\vec{a},\langle\vec{s}\rangle)\in\hat{\chi}(G,C)
.

Note that this amounts to extending
FOC
with a generalized operator for the numeric query
\hat{\chi}
. We have not allowed for relatavization or quotienting in the interpretations in our definition and we now explain why this involves no loss of generality. The definition of
\hat{\chi}
over the semi-graph
G
is defined by taking
\chi
over the quotient of
G
with respect to the reflexive, symmetric and transitive closure of
\sim
. Thus, we can replace a definable congruence in an interpretation by combining it with the definition of
\sim
without changing the semantics. By the same token, we can replace any relativizing formula
\delta
by incorporating it in the definition of the binary relation
E
and ensuring that only tuples that satisfy
\delta
are involved in this binary relation. It is easily seen from the definition of
\chi
that for any vertex
v
that has no
E
-neighbours, either
0
is in
C(v)
and thus all pairs
(v,\ell)
are in
\chi(G,C)
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, or 0 is not in
C(v)
and thus no such pairs
(v,\ell)
are in
\chi(G,C)
.

We define two parameters by which we measure the size of an
\textsf{LREC}_{=}

formula, rank and iteration-degree. As a base case, the rank and
iteration-degree of any atomic formula
\varphi
is 0. If
\varphi
has
rank
r
and iteration-degree
d
, then
\exists x\varphi
,
\forall x\varphi
and
[\#x\varphi]=\nu
all have rank
r+1
and iteration degree
d
Finally, if we have the formula

\varphi:=[\textsf{lrec}_{\vec{u},\vec{v},\vec{p}}\varphi_{E},\varphi_{\sim},\varphi_{C}](\vec{w},\vec{r})

where the length of
\vec{u},\vec{v}
is
c
and
\vec{p}
is
d
, then the rank of
\varphi
, denoted
\operatorname{rank}(\varphi)
, is equal to

\max[2c+\operatorname{rank}(\varphi_{\sim}),2c+\operatorname{rank}(\varphi_{E}),c+d+\operatorname{rank}(\varphi_{C})]

and if
\vec{r}
has length
q
then the iteration-degree of
\varphi
, denoted
\operatorname{deg}(\varphi)
, is equal to

\max[q,\operatorname{deg}(\varphi_{\sim}),\operatorname{deg}(\varphi_{E}),\operatorname{deg}(\varphi_{C})].

Note that the rank and iteration-degree values may be distinct from each other, and the iteration-degree need not be larger than that of sub-formulas, regardless of the form of
\varphi
. This will be important when we introduce the game for the logic.

2.3
The Path Systems Problem

In this subsection we define the path systems problem (
\mathrm{PSP}
). Moreover we will define a specific subclass of
\mathrm{PSP}
instances which we will be using to prove that
\mathrm{PSP}
is inexpressible in
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
.

The problem is defined as a class of structures in a vocabulary with a ternary relation
R
, unary relation
S
, and constant
\mathtt{t}
. Given a universe
U
and a relation
R\subseteq U^{3}
, we define the upward closure of any set
X\subseteq U
as the smallest superset
Y
of
X
such that for
a,b,c\in U
, if
a,b
is in
Y
and
(a,b,c)
is in
R
, then
c
is in
Y
.

The decision problem
\mathrm{PSP}
then consists of those structures
\mathfrak{A}
for which
\mathtt{t}^{\mathfrak{A}}
is in the upwards closure of
S^{\mathfrak{A}}
.

As we will be using
\mathrm{PSP}
to prove a separation between
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
and
LFP
, we verify that
\mathrm{PSP}
is definable in
LFP
. This is well known. For completeness, we give the defining sentence.

3
LREC
=
Game

In order to prove the inexpressibility result for
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
, we
introduce a Spoiler-Duplicator game. This game does not exactly
characterize the expressive power of the logic, but it provides a
sufficient condition for indistinguishability of structures.

The game we define is an extension of the classic
Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé game for first-order logic played on a pair
of structures
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
, where Spoiler aims to demonstrate
a difference between the two structures and Duplicator aims to
demonstrate that they are not distinguishable. Before
introducing it formally, we make some observations. Because our
logic includes counting, the games are based on bijection games, which
characterise first-order logic with counting. Thus, our games have
two kinds of moves—bijection moves, which account for counting and
ordinary quantifers, and what we call
graph moves
which account
for the
lrec
operator.

In a graph move, Spoiler chooses an interpretation which defines a
labelled semi-graph from each of
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
. An auxilliary game is then played on the
pair of semi-graphs in which Spoiler aims to show that the two
semi-graphs are distinguished by the query
\hat{\chi}
while
Duplicator attempts to hide the difference between them. At any point
in this auxilliary game, Spoiler can choose to revert to the main
game, for instance if a position is reached from where Spoiler can win
the ordinary bijection game.

It should be noted that in requiring Spoiler to provide an explicit
interpretation in the graph game, we build in definability in the
logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
into the rules of the game. In that sense, it does
not provide an independent characterization of definability.
Nonetheless, it does give a sufficient criterion for proving
undefinability. Similarly, Theorem
3.2
below only gives
one direction of the connection between the game and the logic. That
is, it shows that if there is a formula distinguishing
\mathfrak{A}
and

\mathfrak{B}
, it yields a winning strategy for Spoiler. We do not claim,
and do not need, the other direction.

A final remark is that we have, for simplicity, only defined the game
for the special case where
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
are structures over
the same universe. Again, this is sufficient for our purpose as the
structures we construct on which we play the game satisfy this
condition.

The game is parameterized by the two measures of size of formulas we
introduced in Section
2.2
: the rank and iteration
degree. To
define the game, we first introduce some notation for partial maps.

For any sets

and

with
A\subseteq B
and
C\subseteq D

, we say
f
is a
partial function
between
B
and
D
, which we can denote by
f:B\hookrightarrow D
. We say that a function
f^{\prime}:B\hookrightarrow D

extends

if
f^{\prime}|_{A}=f
. Let
\operatorname{dom}{f}
denote the set

where

is defined. For partial function

with

for

be the partial function defined on

. Let the two children of

. Then, we have

For a closed set
X\subseteq V
, we say a function
\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is

if whenever

are such that

and
E(x,z)
, then
\rho(x)=\rho(y)+\rho(z)
. If

, we say that a function
\rho^{\prime}:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}

extends

\rho
if

. The following is a useful characterization of consistent functions on closed sets.

Proposition 4.3
.

For a closed set
X
,
\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is consistent if, and only if, for every
F\subseteq X
and

such that
F
minimally encloses

, we have
\rho(x)=\sum_{y\in F}\rho(y)
.

Proof.

The direction from right to left is immediate, since for any
x,y,z\in X
with
(x,y,z)\in R_{T}
, we have that
F=\{y,z\}
minimally encloses
x
. Thus, by assumption
\rho(x)=\sum_{w\in F}\rho(w)=\rho(y)+\rho(z),
as required.

In the other direction, assume that
\rho
is consistent and suppose
F
minimally encloses
x
. Let
h=\text{height}(\overline{F})
and we proceed by induction on
h
. If
h=0
then
F=\{x\}
and
\rho(x)=\sum_{y\in F}\rho(y)
is clearly true. Suppose then the claim is true for all
F^{\prime}
with
\text{height}(\overline{F^{\prime}})\leq h
and let
F
be a set minimally enclosing
x
with
\text{height}(F)=h+1
. Let
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
be the two children of
x
and
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
the subsets of

that minimally enclose
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
respectively. Then
\sum_{y\in F}\rho(y)=\left(\sum_{y\in F_{1}}\rho(y)\right)+\left(\sum_{y\in F_{2}}\rho(y)\right)=\rho(s_{1})+\rho(s_{2})=\rho(x)
. Here the first equality holds from the fact that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
form a partition of

, the second by induction hypothesis and the third by the consistency of
\rho
.
∎

For any (not necessarily closed) set
X\subseteq V
, we say a function
\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is

\rho^{\prime}:\overline{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}

. Note that if such a

Proposition 4.4
.

For any set

,
\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is consistent if, and only if, for every
F\subseteq X
and
x\in X
such that
F
minimally encloses

, we have
\rho(x)=\sum_{y\in F}\rho(y)
.

Proof.

In one direction, suppose
\rho
is consistent and there is an

minimally enclosing

. Then this

and

is present in

and so, by the consistency of

by Proposition

.

In the other direction, suppose
\rho(x)=\sum_{y\in F}\rho(y)
for all

minimally enclosing

in

. If

is closed, there is nothing to prove by Proposition
4.3
. If not, then we can enumerate the elements of
\overline{X}

for the set

. We prove by induction on

to a function

on

We let
\rho_{0}=\rho
and the base case follows by assumption. Suppose

has been defined to satisfy the inductive hypothesis. If there is exactly one pair of elements

such that

to

, there is nothing to check as the condition is satisfied by induction hypothesis. Similarly, if

•

If

and

and

are the two children of

then

•

If

and

If

•

If

, then it must be that

The only case remaining is if both children of

, say

and

as well as its parent, say

and its sibling, say

are all in

as before as long as

. But, since

∎

The situation we are often interested in is when we have a consistent function

Definition 4.5

Proposition 4.6

Let

with

\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
be a consistent function. Suppose

is the set of elements in

which are free over

Proof.

Assume, for contradiction, that
\rho^{\prime}:=\rho\cup\sigma
is not

4.4
, there is a

with frontier

and head

such that
\rho^{\prime}(x)\neq\sum_{y\in F}\rho^{\prime}(y)
. Let

be a minimal such set. Note that

must contain an element of

, since
\rho
is assumed to be consistent. Then, it must be the case that
\rho(z)\neq 0
for some

for otherwise by definition we would have
\rho^{\prime}(z)=0
for all

\rho^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{y\in F}\rho^{\prime}(y)

Then, by Definition

, there is a

such that

. Let

be the unique subset of

and in the second, let

. In either case we have

is a proper closed connected subset of

\rho^{\prime}
at its head is not the sum of the values of

at its fronitier, contradicting the minimality of

.

\operatorname{bij}(\sigma_{i}-\rho_{i})
, and since each
g_{u}
defines a partial isomorphism over
[G_{\mathfrak{A}}]
, by repeated application of Proposition
5.1
we can show that
g(\vec{a}_{i})\cong\vec{a}_{i}
, and thus
\sigma_{i}
is a valid choice by Duplicator.

Moreover, fix some
Y\subseteq V
and let
g
now be the function induced by
\sigma_{i}\cup\eta_{Y}
. Note again that
\text{null-h}(\sigma_{i})
is no smaller than
\text{null-h}(\rho_{i})
, and thus
\text{null-h}(\sigma_{i}\cup\eta_{Y})
is no smaller than
k^{3}(3q+1)(m-1)+2k
when
|Y|
is at most
c
by Proposition
4.7
, which implies
g
defines a partial isomorphism between
[G_{\mathfrak{A}}]
and
[G_{\mathfrak{B}}]
. Therefore, Duplicator’s choices for
\eta_{Y}
are valid. Finally, we can see by a similar argument that the in-degree of
[\vec{a}_{i}]
in
[G_{\mathfrak{A}}]
is the same as the in-degree of
[f_{i}(\vec{a}_{i})]
in
[G_{\mathfrak{B}}]
. Thus we have verified that as long as
\text{null-h}(\rho_{i})
is greater than
k^{3}(3q+1)(m-1)+4k
for all
i
, the graph move only ends when the spoiler decides to reset
\rho
to
\rho_{s}
for some
s
. By our assumption on
\text{null-h}(\rho_{s})
, and the inductive hypothesis on
m-1
, Duplicator has a winning strategy when such a decision is made by Spoiler.

Therefore, if we can prove that
\text{null-h}(\rho_{i})
is greater than
h(3q+1)(m-1)+4k
for all
i
then we are done. To derive a contradiction, assume there is some
s
such that
\text{null-h}(\rho_{s})\leq k^{3}(3q+1)(m-1)+4k
. Then, let
t\leq s
be the smallest such that
\text{null-h}(\rho_{t})\leq k^{3}(3q+1)(m-1)+k^{3}
and take
A:=\{\vec{a}_{i}\mid|[F]\vec{a}_{i}|\geq p\text{ AND }0<i\leq t\}
. By our choice of
t
, we know that
\text{null-h}(\sigma_{t-1})\geq\text{null-h}(\rho_{t-1})\geq k^{3}(3q+1)(m-1)+k^{3}
and thus for
k
large enough we have
\text{null-h}(\rho_{t})>k^{3}(3q+1)(m-1)+4k
by Proposition
4.7
. It is easy to verify that
\vec{a}_{0},\vec{a}_{1},\ldots,\vec{a}_{t}
is a strongly-bounding path, so we invoke Lemma
5.4
to get that
|A|\geq 3q
(since
\text{null-h}(\rho_{1})-\text{null-h}(\rho_{t})\geq 3qk^{3}
by choice of
t
). Note that
|V\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}|
is no greater than
p^{2}
, and thus
\ell_{0}
can be at most
p^{2q}
. However, this implies that
\ell_{t-1}
can be at most
\ell_{0}/p^{|A|-1}<1
. Hence, we have a contradiction: as
\ell_{t-1}
must be 0, the graph move must terminate prior to reaching step
t
. This completes the proof.
∎

From this our main theorem follows immediately.

Theorem 5.6
.

\mathrm{PSP}
is not expressible in
\textsf{LREC}_{=}

Proof.

Let
\tau
be the vocabulary of
\mathrm{PSP}
instances and suppose for
contradiction that there is a sentence
\varphi
of
\textsf{LREC}_{=}[\tau]

that defines the positive instances. Fix
k
to be greater than the
rank of
\varphi
and
q
to be grater than
\operatorname{deg}(\varphi)
. Take
T
to
be a complete binary tree of height
n\geq k^{3}(3q+1)(k+1)
and
p

to be a prime with
p\geq n
. Fix a function
\sigma
from the
leaves
L
of
T
to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
\mathfrak{A}
be the structure

\mathcal{P}(n,p,\sigma,t)
where
t=\sum_{l\in L}\sigma(l)
and

\mathfrak{B}
to be the structure
\mathcal{P}(n,p,\sigma,t^{\prime})
where
t^{\prime}=t+1
. Then, by construction
\mathfrak{A}
is a positive instance of

\mathrm{PSP}
so
\mathfrak{A}\models\varphi
and
\mathfrak{B}
is a negative instance,
so
\mathfrak{B}\not\models\varphi
. But, by Lemma
5.5
, the
two structures are indistinguishable by
\varphi
and we have a contradiction.

∎

6
Conclusion

Over ordered structures, the logic
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
captures
\mathrm{L}
, while
FPC
captures
\mathrm{P}
. So, it is of value to investigate the relationship between the two logics. It was shown in
[
12
]
that
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
is contained in
FPC
, and the main result of this paper shows the containment is proper. Our proof here uses novel techniques that provide insight on the expressive power of
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
and some of the combinatorial properties of
\mathrm{PSP}
that make it difficult to be solved in
\mathrm{L}
.

These techniques can also be used to find further results. Firstly, the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game provides a new tool that can be used and adapted for other inexpressibility results of the logic. Secondly, we note that the combinatorial results in Section
4
use the characterization of a tree as a matroid. The results can further be generalized to a larger class of finite matroids, which would include constructing a more general definition of height for arbitrary matroids. It is not clear whether it could be extended to all classes of finite matroids however, as the argument relies on the fact that the independent sets of the matroid is described concisely in the form of the edge relation of the tree, and it is not true that all classes of finite matroids can be described by a finite vocabulary. The results of Section
5
can be generalized to larger classes of structures as well. This may for example include considering other classes of matroids as the base set following from the discussion above. Or, it may include constructing interpretations of structures other than semi-graphs, as all that is required for the result is a binary relation
\sim
, which is used to define a quotient structure of the interpretation through its transitive reflexive symmetric closure.

The Path Systems Problem is not known to be in
\mathrm{L}
, and thus the question of whether
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
is equal to
\textsf{FPC}\cap\mathrm{L}
remains open. A result that they are equal would imply that
\mathrm{L}\neq\mathrm{P}
, since
\mathrm{PSP}
is in
LFP
and hence in
FPC
. It also remains open for many classes of structures
\mathcal{C}
that are known to have logarithmic-space canonical labelling algorithms whether
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
captures
\mathrm{L}
over
\mathcal{C}
. Studying these classes may prove fruitful, as it is a direction for solving the
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
versus
\textsf{FPC}\cap\mathrm{L}
problem.

As mentioned before, it remains open whether
\mathrm{PSP}
is expressible in some logics stronger than
\textsf{LREC}_{=}
, including the logic
CLog
. Of course, one can also ask whether
\mathrm{PSP}
is in
\mathrm{L}
and this is equivalent to asking if
\mathrm{L}
is equal to
\mathrm{P}
, but the question of whether
\mathrm{PSP}
is in
CLog
does not seem to have been investigated. Another related long-standing open problem is to find a logic that captures
\mathrm{L}
over all structures.

Abstract
. The scaling entropy of a p.m.p. action is a slow-entropy type invariant that characterizes the intermediate growth of entropy in a dynamical system. An amenable group G G has a scaling entropy growth gap if the scaling entropy of any its free p.m.p. action admits a non-trivial lower bound. We prove that the group S ∞ S_{\infty} of all finite permutations has a scaling entropy growth gap as well as the Houghton group ℋ 2 \mathcal{H}_{2} . By proving this, we show that there are finitely generated amenable groups with this property.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study slow entropy-type invariants of actions of amenable groups. We focus on the invariant proposed by Vershik called
scaling entropy

[
20
,
21
,
22
]
although we also formulate our main results in terms of
slow entropy
by Katok-Thouvenot
[
6
]
and
measure theoretic complexity
by Ferenczi
[
2
]
. All these invariants characterize an intermediate growth of entropy of a system which turns out to be an intrinsic characteristic that is invariant under measure-theoretic isomorphism. We give all the necessary definitions of the scaling entropy invariant in Section
1.1
.

A question that naturally arises is what are the possible asymptotics that may appear as values of such invariants. In recent papers
[
1
,
14
,
16
,
18
,
13
]
, several attempts to answer such questions led to non-trivial applications. For the case of a single transformation, the possible values of scaling entropy were completely described in
[
11
,
25
]
. For amenable groups, this question becomes much harder. For any amenable group, it is shown in
[
18
]
that the scaling entropy of a generic p.m.p. action of such a group can not be bounded from above by a given non-trivial sequence. If the group is moreover residually finite (or, more generally, if it has a free compact action), then the scaling entropy of a generic action can not be bounded from below by a non-trivial rate. It was shown, however, that there are amenable groups that fail to satisfy the last property. Moreover, any free p.m.p. action of such a group satisfies a given lower bound on its scaling entropy. We call this property
scaling entropy growth gap
.

Examples of amenable groups with a scaling entropy growth gap provided in
[
18
]
are not finitely generated. Namely, they are the matrix groups
SL(2,{\bar{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
over the algebraic closure of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p},\ p>2
. The existence of the gap in these groups follows from Helfgott-type estimates of growth in finite linear groups (see,
[
3
,
12
]
).

In this paper, we provide an example of a
finitely generated
amenable group such that the scaling entropy of any its free p.m.p. action has scaling entropy which is bounded from below by a given rate. It turns out that such an example can be obtained from the series of Houghton groups. To prove this we first show that the infinite symmetric group has a scaling entropy growth gap.

Theorem 1
.

The group
S_{\infty}
of all finite permutations has a scaling entropy growth gap.

Note that
S_{\infty}
has only two finite-dimensional unitary representations: the sign of a permutation and the trivial one. Both of these do not distinguish even permutations and, therefore,
S_{\infty}
can not possess a free compact action. Theorem
1
gives a quantitative version of this statement: any free action of
S_{\infty}
is not only non-compact but also its scaling entropy is bounded from below by some given rate which does not depend on the action itself.

In section
3
we prove that the property of scaling entropy growth gap extends from a subgroup to the ambient group. This allows us to provide an example of a finitely generated amenable group with this property.

Proposition 2
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of an amenable group
G
and assume that
H
has a scaling entropy growth gap. Then the group
G
has a scaling entropy growth gap.

Houghton groups
\mathcal{H}_{n}
were introduced in
[
4
]
. A particular example from this series of groups, namely, the Houghton group
\mathcal{H}_{2}
consists of all bijective maps from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
, generated by translations and finite permutations. This group is amenable and is generated by the shift map and an elementary involution exchanging
0
and
1
.
Since the Houghton group
\mathcal{H}_{2}
by definition contains
S_{\infty}
we obtain the following theorem as a corollary.

Theorem 3
.

The Houghton group
\mathcal{H}_{2}
has a scaling entropy growth gap.

1.1.
Scaling entropy

As we mentioned above the main object we deal with in this paper is
the scaling entropy
of a p.m.p. action. This invariant was introduced by Vershik in
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. The main idea of Vershik is to introduce a measurable metric on a measure space and then consider the evolution of its geometrical numerical characteristics. Namely, we consider an average of the metric and the minimal number of balls (with respect to this average) of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover the entire space up to measure
\varepsilon
. We call the logarithm of this number
the
\varepsilon
-entropy
of a metric triple. The asymptotic behavior of this function, as was conjectured by Vershik and proved by Zatitskii in
[
24
]
, does not depend on the choice of the initial metric and is a measure-theoretic invariant of a system. Similar ideas were introduced by Katok-Thouvenot
[
6
]
and Ferenczi
[
2
]
and led to related slow-entropy type invariants. We refer the reader to a recent survey
[
19
]
for details on the theory of scaling entropy and a survey
[
7
]
for several other slow-entropy type invariants.

Now let us proceed with formal definitions. Let
(X,\mu)
be a standard probability space. We call measurable function
\rho\colon(X^{2},\mu^{2})\to[0,+\infty)

a measurable semimetric

if it is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality. We call
\rho
summable if
\rho\in L^{1}(X^{2},\mu^{2})
. For a positive number
\varepsilon
, we define
the
\varepsilon
-entropy
as follows. Let
k
be the minimal positive integer such that the space
X
can be decomposed into a union of measurable subsets
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
with
\mu(X_{0})<\varepsilon
and
\diam_{\rho}(X_{i})<\varepsilon
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then we define

(1)

\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon}(X,\mu,\rho)=\log_{2}k.

If there is no such
k
we say that the
\varepsilon
-entropy of the triple is infinite.
A semimetric is called
admissible
if all its
\varepsilon
-entropies are finite. It is shown in
[
10
]
that a semimetric is admissible if and only if it is separable on a subset of full measure. For any measurable partition
\xi
, there is the corresponding
cut semimetric

\rho_{\xi}
which equals
0
if two points lie in the same cell of
\xi
and equals
1
otherwise. A cut semimetric is admissible if and only if the corresponding partition is essentially countable.

Let
G
be an amenable group and
\lambda=\{F_{n}\}
be a Følner sequence. Let
\alpha\colon G\to\Aut(X,\mu)
be a p.m.p. action of
G
on a standard propbability space
(X,\mu)
. For a measurable semimetric
\rho
, we define its average over
F_{n}
:

(2)

G_{av}^{n}\rho(x,y)=\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum\limits_{g\in F_{n}}\rho(gx,gy)\quad x,y\in X.

We will also occasionally use the notation
G_{av}^{F_{n}}\rho
to emphasize the set
F_{n}
over which the average is computed. For an element
g\in G
we denote the
g

\rho
by
g^{-1}\rho
meaning that
g^{-1}\rho(x,y)=\rho(gx,gy),\ x,y\in X
. A semimetric is called
generating
if all its translations together separate points mod 0 that is for a subset
X^{\prime}
of full measure and any distinct
x,y\in X^{\prime}
there exists some
g\in G
such
g^{-1}\rho(x,y)>0
.

Then we consider the following function of two variables

(3)

\Phi_{\rho}(n,\varepsilon)=\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon}(X,\mu,G_{av}^{n}\rho).

The main idea behind the scaling entropy invariant is that the asymptotic behavior in
n
of this function does not depend on the choice of the initial semimetric
\rho
. To justify this we need to define what
the asymptotic behavior
of
\Phi_{\rho}
is. We say that two functions
\Phi,\Psi\colon\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
that decrease in their second arguments are
asymptotically equivalent
if for any
\varepsilon
there exists a
\delta>0
such that

(4)

\Phi(n,\varepsilon)\precsim\Psi(n,\delta)\text{ and }\Psi(n,\varepsilon)\precsim\Phi(n,\delta),

when
n
goes to infinity (here and in what follows, for two sequences
f
and
g
, relation
f\precsim g
means that
\limsup_{n}f(n)/g(n)<\infty
). In this case, we write
\Phi\asymp\Psi
. We denote by
{\left[\Phi\right]}
the equivalence class of function
\Phi
with respect to relation
\asymp
and call it
the asymptotic class
. We say that
\Psi

asymptotically dominates

\Phi
and write
\Phi\precsim\Psi
if for any
\varepsilon
there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\Phi(n,\varepsilon)\precsim\Psi(n,\delta)
when
n
goes to infinity. Clearly, if
\Phi\precsim\Psi
and
\Psi\precsim\Phi
hold simultaneously then
\Phi\asymp\Psi
. Relation
\precsim
agrees with the equivalence relation
\asymp
and can be naturally applied to its equivalence classes.

Theorem 4

(
[
24
,
25
]
)
.

Let
\rho
and
\omega
be two summable admissible generating semimetrics. Then the functions
\Phi_{\rho}
and
\Phi_{\omega}
are asymptotically equivalent.

Theorem
4
gives rise to the following definition.

Definition 5
.

The scaling entropy
of a p.m.p. action of an amenable group
G
is the asymptotic class
\mathcal{H}(X,\mu,G,\lambda)
of the function
\Phi_{\rho}
for some (hence for any) summable admissible generating semimetric
\rho
.

We will also use a shorter notation
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)
for the scaling entropy of action
\alpha
. Note that by definition
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)
depends on the choice of the Følner sequence
\lambda
.

For any action
\alpha
of an amenable group
G
there are two simple natural bounds for the scaling entropy (see, e. g.,
[
16
]
):

(5)

{1}\precsim\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\precsim{|F_{n}|}.

Moreover, the scaling entropy is bounded (meaning that
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\asymp 1
) if and only if the action is compact (see
[
10
,
23
]
). Equivalence
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\asymp|F_{n}|
characterizes exactly the systems with positive measure-theoretic entropy (see
[
16
]
).

For the classical case of a single transformation with
\lambda
being the sequence of standard intervals
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, it is shown in
[
11
,
15
]
that the scaling entropy
\mathcal{H}
always contains a function decreasing in
\varepsilon
and increasing and subadditive in
n
. Moreover, for any such function
\Phi
, there exists an ergodic transformation whose scaling entropy is exactly
{\left[\Phi\right]}
. Therefore, the complete description of possible values of the invariant in the classical case was given.

For a general amenable group, the exact description of the set of possible asymptotic classes that may appear as scaling entropy for some p.m.p. action is unknown. However, recently several steps were made towards understanding the properties of this set (see
[
9
,
18
]
). Some of these results led to several non-trivial applications to
universal systems
and
generic extensions
. As shown in
[
18
]
, for any amenable group, Følner sequence
\lambda=\{F_{n}\}
, and any sequence
\phi(n)=o(|F_{n}|)
there exists a free p.m.p. action
\alpha
with
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\not\precsim\phi
(see also
[
9
]
for a related result regarding slow entropy). This result can be interpreted as the absence of a gap between the maximal possible growth
|F_{n}|
and any other slower rate
\phi
. For a residually finite group, the same holds for the lower bound: for any unbounded sequence
\phi(n)
there exists a free p.m.p. action
\alpha
with
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\not\succsim\phi
. The last statement does not hold for a general amenable group, examples of such groups were given in
[
18
]
. This shows that there is a significant difference in the behavior of our invariant for different amenable groups. This paper is devoted to studying this property closely and finding more examples of such groups, in particular, finitely generated examples.

1.2.
Scaling entropy growth gap

Definition 6

(
[
18
]
)
.

We say that an amenable group
G
has
a scaling entropy growth gap
if for some (then for any) Følner sequence
\lambda=\{F_{n}\}
there exists an unbounded function
\phi(n)
such that for any essentially free p.m.p. action
\alpha
we have
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\succsim\phi
.

The correctness of this definition, that is independence of the Følner sequence, is proved in
[
18
]
. Here we can give an equivalent definition of the gap property replacing essentially free actions with essentially faithful and restricting our considerations to ergodic actions.

Proposition 7
.

Let
G
be an amenable group and
\lambda=\{F_{n}\}_{n}
its Følner sequence. Assume that there is an unbounded function
\phi(n)
such that for every ergodic essentially free p.m.p. action
\alpha
of
G
the lower bound
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\succsim\phi(n)
holds true. Then for every essentially faithful p.m.p. action
\beta
the same lower bound
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\lambda)\succsim\phi(n)
holds true.

Proof.

Let us denote by
(X,\mu)
the measure space on which
\beta
acts. Consider the infinite power
\beta^{\mathbb{N}}
of the given action. For any non-identity element
g\in G
we have
\mu(\{x\colon\beta(g)x\not=x\})>0
since
\beta
is essentially faithful. Then, clearly,
\mu^{\mathbb{N}}(\{y\in X^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\beta^{\mathbb{N}}(g)y\not=y\})=1
, hence, the action
\beta^{\mathbb{N}}
is essentially free. It is easy to see that the scaling entropy of a countable power of a given p.m.p. action is the same, that is
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\lambda)=\mathcal{H}(\beta^{\mathbb{N}},\lambda)
.

So, we may assume that
\beta
is essentially free. The scaling entropy of a system dominates the scaling entropies of its ergodic components (see
[
19
]
, the proof for amenable groups is the same) which are essentially free as well. Since almost each ergodic component
\mu_{x}
satisfies
\mathcal{H}(X,\mu_{x},G,\lambda)\succsim\phi(n)
, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\lambda)\succsim\phi(n)
.
∎

As we already mentioned, residually finite amenable groups do not have a scaling entropy growth gap since they possess essentially free compact actions which have bounded scaling entropy. An example of an amenable group with a scaling entropy growth gap is the liner group
SL(2,{\bar{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}),\ p>2
, as was shown in
[
18
]
using Helfgott’s estimates (see
[
3
,
12
]
) in finite linear groups. In this paper, we provide more examples of such groups, in particular, a finitely generated one. Namely, we show that the infinite symmetric group
S_{\infty}
of all finite permutations of integer numbers, and the Houghton group
\mathcal{H}_{2}
have a scaling entropy growth gap. It is unknown to the author if there are amenable groups without an essentially free compact action that also have no scaling entropy growth gap.

3.
Closure properties

In this section, we study several closure properties of the family of groups that have a scaling entropy growth gap. We start with the proof of Proposition
2
that gives the last remaining part of the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Let
\{W_{n}\}_{n}
be a Følner sequence in
G
and
\{F_{n}\}_{n}
a Følner sequence in
H
. Let
\phi(n)
be a corresponding absolute lower bound for scaling entropy of
(H,\{F_{n}\}_{n})
. Let
\alpha
be an essentially free p.m.p. action of
G
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
and let
\beta
be its restriction to
H
.
Let
\rho
be a measurable admissible metric on
(X,\mu)
bounded from above by
1
almost everywhere. Since
\alpha
is essentially free,
\beta
is essentially free as well. Therefore, due to Proposition
7
we have for any
\delta>0
sufficiently small

(20)

\mathbb{H}_{\delta}(X,\mu,H_{av}^{F_{n}})\succsim\phi(n).

For any integer
n
, there exits some
k_{n}
such that for any
r>k_{n}
the inequality

(21)

|F_{n}W_{r}\triangle W_{r}|<2^{-n}|W_{r}|

is satisfied. Then

(22)

\frac{1}{|W_{r}|}\sum\limits_{g\in W_{r}}g^{-1}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum\limits_{h\in F_{n}}h^{-1}\rho\leqslant\frac{1}{|W_{r}|}\sum\limits_{f\in F_{n}W_{r}}f^{-1}\rho=G_{av}^{W_{r}}\rho+l_{1},

where the term
l_{1}
is bounded in absolute value by
2^{-n}
. The last equality holds true due to inequality (
21
).
Take
\varepsilon>0
satisfying

\mathbb{H}_{4\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(H_{av}^{F_{n}}\rho)\succsim\phi(n)
. For sufficiently large
n
, the term
l_{1}
is negligible for computing
\varepsilon
–entropy of
G_{av}^{W_{r}}\rho
. Lemma
8
together with relation (
20
) give

(23)

\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon}(G_{av}^{W_{r}}\rho)\geqslant\mathbb{H}_{4\varepsilon}(G_{av}^{W_{r}}\rho+l_{1})\geqslant\mathbb{H}_{4\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(G_{av}^{F_{n}}\rho)\succsim\phi(n).

Therefore,
G
has a scaling entropy growth gap with respect to
\{W_{r}\}
and bound function
\psi(r)=\phi(n(r))
, where
n(r)
is the maximal
n
such that
k_{n}<r
.
∎

Proposition 11
.

Let
H
be a finite index subgroup of an amenable group
G
and assume that
G
has a scaling entropy growth gap. Then the subgroup
H
has a scaling entropy growth gap.

Proof.

Since
H
has a finite index, the group
G
decomposes as a finite disjoint union of right cosets. That is there are
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in G
, where
k
is the index of
H
in
G
, such that
G=\bigcup_{i}Hg_{i}
.
Let
\{W_{n}\}_{n}
be a Følner sequence in
G
which is simultaneously left and right Følner. We have

(24)

W_{n}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}(W_{n}\cap Hg_{i}).

Find an index
i_{0}
for which the set
W_{n}\cap Hg_{i}
has the largest cardinality among these intersections. By passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
i_{0}
does not depend on
n
. Let

(25)

F_{n}=W_{n}g_{i_{0}}^{-1}\cap H\subset H.

Clearly
|F_{n}|\geqslant\frac{1}{k}|W_{n}|
and, hence,
\{F_{n}\}_{n}
is a left Følner sequence in
H
since
W_{n}
is a left Følner sequence in
G
. Moreover, since
W_{n}
is also a right Følner sequence we have for any
\varepsilon>0
and any sufficiently large
n

(26)

|F_{n}g_{i}\triangle(W_{n}\cap Hg_{i})|<\varepsilon k|F_{n}|,

due to
g_{i_{0}}^{-1}g_{i}
right almost invariance of
W_{n}
. Therefore, the set

(27)

\tilde{W}_{n}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}F_{n}g_{i}

satisfies
|\tilde{W}_{n}\triangle W_{n}|=o(|W_{n}|)
and, hence, is a Følner sequence in
G
.

Now let
H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{{{\alpha}}}}}{{{\curvearrowright}}}(X,\mu)
be an essentially free action of
H
. Let
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{{{\beta}}}}}{{{\curvearrowright}}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}^{-1}(X,\mu)=(Y,\nu)
be the corresponding coinduced action of
\alpha
from
H
to
G
(see, e. g.,
[
8
]
). Let
\rho
be a bounded admissible metric on
(X,\mu)
. Then the function
\tilde{\rho}(x,y)=\rho(x_{1},y_{1})
is an admissible generating semimetric on
Y
. We have

(28)

\frac{1}{|\tilde{W}_{n}|}\sum_{g\in\tilde{W}_{n}}g^{-1}\tilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}^{-1}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{h\in F_{n}}h^{-1}\tilde{\rho}.

Clearly, for any
i=1,\ldots,k
and any
\varepsilon>0

(29)

\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon}(Y,\nu,\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{h\in F_{n}}h^{-1}\tilde{\rho})=\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon}(X,\mu,H_{av}^{F_{n}}\rho).

And, since the right hand side of (
28
) is a finite average, we have due to Lemma
9

(30)

\mathcal{H}(G,\beta,\{\tilde{W}_{n}\})\precsim\mathcal{H}(H,\alpha,\{F_{n}\}_{n}).

Since by our assumptions
G
has a scaling entropy growth gap, there exists a function
\phi(n)
tending to infinity (which depends only on the Følner sequence
\{\tilde{W}_{n}\}_{n}
) such that
\mathcal{H}(G,\beta,\{\tilde{W}_{n}\})\succsim\phi
. Then

(31)

\mathcal{H}(H,\alpha,\{F_{n}\}_{n})\succsim\phi.

Therefore,
H
has a scaling entropy growth gap as well since
\alpha
was chosen as an arbitrary essentially free action of
H
.
∎

Corollary 12
.

The infinite Alternating group
A_{\infty}
of all even permutations of integer numbers has a scaling entropy growth gap.

Proposition 13
.

Let
H
and
G
be amenable groups without a scaling entropy gap. Then the direct product
G\times H
has no scaling entropy gap.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\{W_{n}\}_{n}
be a Følner sequence in
G
and
\sigma=\{F_{n}\}_{n}
a Følner sequence in
H
. Then
\tau=\{W_{n}\times F_{n}\}_{n}
is a Følner sequence in
G\times H
. Let
\phi(n)
be a given function increasing to infinity. Find an essentially free p.m.p. action
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{{{\alpha}}}}}{{{\curvearrowright}}}(X,\mu)
satisfying
\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)\not\succsim\phi
.
There exists a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
such that for any
\Phi\in\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\lambda)
and any
\varepsilon>0

(32)

\Phi(n_{k},\varepsilon)\prec\phi(n_{k}).

Let
\lambda^{\prime}=\{W_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
,
\sigma^{\prime}=\{F_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
,
\tau^{\prime}=\{W_{n_{k}}\times F_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
, and
\tilde{\Phi}(k,\varepsilon)=\Phi(n_{k},\varepsilon)
. Find an essentially free p.m.p. action
H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{{{\beta}}}}}{{{\curvearrowright}}}(Y,\nu)
satisfying
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\sigma^{\prime})\not\succsim\phi(n_{k})
. Consider the product action
\alpha\times\beta
of
G\times H
on
(X,\mu)\times(Y,\nu)
, where
G
and
H
act independently via actions
\alpha
and
\beta
on the first and the second components respectively. For any
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\sigma^{\prime})
we have
\tilde{\Phi}+\Psi\in\mathcal{H}(\alpha\times\beta,\tau^{\prime})
. Since
\tilde{\Phi}\prec\phi(n_{k})
and
\Psi\not\succsim\phi(n_{k})
we conclude that

(33)

\mathcal{H}(\alpha\times\beta,\tau^{\prime})\not\succsim\phi(n_{k}),

and, therefore,
\mathcal{H}(\alpha\times\beta,\tau)\not\succsim\phi(n)
since
\tau^{\prime}
is a subsequence of
\tau
.
∎

4.
Remarks and open questions

4.1.
Connections to other slow entropy type invariants

The scaling entropy growth gap property can be expressed in terms of related slow entropy-type invariants. Here we give a simple alternative definition of this property in terms of slow entropy introduced in
[
6
]
by Katok and Thouvenot. We refer the reader to survey
[
7
]
for definitions of this and similar invariants.

Proposition 14
.

An amenable group
G
has a scaling entropy growth gap if and only if for some (hence for any) Følner sequence in
G
there exists a non-trivial scale
\mathbf{a}_{n}(t)
such that for any essentially free p.m.p. action
\alpha
the lower slow entropy of
\alpha
(with respect to the given Følner sequence) is positive:

\underline{ent}_{\mathbf{a}}(\alpha)>0.

The proof is straightforward and follows directly from the definitions of scaling entropy and slow entropy. See a recent survey
[
19
]
for more on the connections between these invariants. In view of Proposition
14
, one may call our property a "slow entropy growth gap". In a similar manner, one can give an alternative definition of this property via the notion of measure-theoretic complexity introduced by Ferenczi in
[
2
]
.

4.2.
Open questions

We conclude the paper with several open questions and possible directions for further investigations of the scaling entropy growth gap property.

First, there are lots of interesting amenable groups for which it is unknown to the author whether they have this property or not. The classical lamplighter group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}
is residually finite and hence has no scaling entropy growth gap, however, it is unknown if a lamplighter group
A\wr\mathbb{Z}
with non-abelian finite group
A
of the lamps has this property. Other interesting candidates are finitely generated simple amenable groups, the first examples of which were constructed in
[
5
]
.

Another question is whether the possession of essentially free compact action (residual finiteness for finitely generated groups) is the only obstruction to the scaling entropy growth gap property. For now, groups with such actions are the only examples of groups known to the author that do not have a scaling entropy growth gap.

In a recent discussion with the author, Anatoly Vershik proposed the following question which is naturally related to the property of the scaling entropy growth gap. For a given group without compact actions, what are the (essentially free) actions with
the slowest possible
scaling entropy? One may consider this question as trying to find an action of a group that is the closest in this sense to being compact. In other words, can one identify the optimal bound
\phi(n)
in Definition
6
and the actions for which it is achieved? Since the absence of compact actions is determined by the family of finite-dimensional representations of a group, this question should be related to the representation theory of the group as well. For
SL(2,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
with a specific Følner sequence this question was answered in
[
18
]
. Even for the infinite symmetric group, this question remains open.

Abstract
. Let 𝐀 \mathbf{A} be a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra (e.g. a group, loop, ring). We investigate the Boolean power 𝐃 \mathbf{D} of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} by the countable atomless Boolean algebra 𝐁 \mathbf{B} filtered at some idempotents e 1 , … , e n e_{1},\dots,e_{n} of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} . When e 1 , … , e n e_{1},\dots,e_{n} are all idempotents of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} we establish two concrete representations of 𝐃 \mathbf{D} : as the (generalized) Fraïssé limit of the class of finite direct powers of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} , and as congruence classes of the countable free algebra in the variety generated by 𝐀 \mathbf{A} . Further, for arbitrary e 1 , … , e n e_{1},\dots,e_{n} , we show that 𝐃 \mathbf{D} is ω \omega -categorical and that its automorphism group has the small index property, strong uncountable cofinality and the Bergman property. As necessary background we establish some general properties of congruences and automorphisms of filtered Boolean powers of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} by any Boolean algebra 𝐁 \mathbf{B} , including a semidirect decomposition for their automorphism groups.

1.
Introduction and main results

The purpose of this paper is to investigate filtered Boolean powers
\mathbf{D}
of a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra
\mathbf{A}
by a Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
.
On a general level, we establish some properties of congruences and automorphisms of
\mathbf{D}
.
When
\mathbf{B}
is the countable atomless Boolean algebra, we investigate the role
\mathbf{D}
plays in the variety generated by
\mathbf{A}
, and prove that it has a number of combinatorial properties:
it is
\omega
-categorical and its automorphism group has the small index property, strong uncountable cofinality and the Bergman property.

In this section we present the background and motivation for our work, introduce the concepts and notation that will be used throughout and give statements of the main results.

A
variety
is a class of algebraic structures (
algebras
for short) of the same type that is defined by
equations. By Birkhoff’s Theorem the
variety generated by a class of algebras

C
is the class of all
homomorphic images of subalgebras of products of elements in
C
. We refer to
[
8
]
for background on
general algebra.

Varieties generated by a finite simple group have been studied by Neumann
[
29
]
, Apps
[
3
]
and others,
while Apps
[
2
]
considers subgroups of Boolean products and powers.
Many of the techniques carry over from groups to Mal’cev algebras in general. Here an algebra
\mathbf{A}
is

Mal’cev
if it has a ternary term operation
m
satisfying
m(x,x,y)=y=m(y,x,x)
.
Note that groups, quasigroups, loops, rings as well as their expansions
with additional operations
are all Mal’cev.

The generalization of commutator theory from groups to algebras as described in
[
15
]
allows us to talk
about (non)-abelian algebras. A Mal’cev algebra is
abelian
if its basic operations can be represented
as affine functions over a module; otherwise we call it
non-abelian
. This is consistent with the classical
notions for groups and loops. A ring is abelian if and only if its multiplication is constant
0
.

Unless otherwise indicated,
\mathbf{A}
will always stand for a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra.
Throughout, we will use bold letters to denote algebras and other structures,
and matching standard letters will be used for the underlying universes. Thus, here,
A
would be the universe of
\mathbf{A}
.
Let
V
denote the variety generated by
\mathbf{A}
, and let
W
be the variety generated by all proper subalgebras of
\mathbf{A}
.
Then
W
is the unique maximal subvariety of
V
.
Every finite algebra in
V
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{A}^{k}\times\mathbf{B}
for
some integer
k\geq 0

and some finite
\mathbf{B}\in W
(cf.
[
29
]
for groups,
[
14
]
for functionally complete algebras).
This indicates the distinguished role the class
\{\mathbf{A}^{k}\>:\>k\geq 1\}
of finite powers of
\mathbf{A}
plays in the
structure of the finite members of
V
.
It turns out that this class has the amalgamation property, leading to the existence of a (generalized)
Fraïssé limit.

In order to be able to state this, we briefly review the standard Fraïssé theory following
[
20
]
.
Let
K
be a countable class of finitely generated structures of the same type.
Consider the following three properties that such a class may have:

Hereditary property (HP):
Every finitely generated substructure of a member of
K
is isomorphic to a member of
K
.

Joint embedding property (JEP):
For any
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in K
there exists
\mathbf{C}\in K
into which both
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
embed.

Amalgamation property (AP):
For any
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}\in K
with embeddings
\varphi\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
and
\psi\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}
there exists
\mathbf{D}\in K
and
embeddings
\varphi^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{D}
and
\psi^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}\varphi=\psi^{\prime}\psi
.

The classical Fraïssé Theorem asserts that these three conditions are equivalent to the existence of
a countable homogeneous structure whose class of finitely generated substructures is
K
. This necessarily unique
structure is known as the
Fraïssé limit
of
K
; see
[
20
, Theorem 7.1.2]
.

Returning to our situation of the variety
V
generated by
a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra
\mathbf{A}
, note that its subclass of finite algebras
has the HP but in general not the JEP or AP.
On the other hand the subclass
K=\{\mathbf{A}^{k}\>:\>k\geq 1\}

will be shown to have the JEP and AP but in general not the HP.
Under these conditions, a Fraïssé limit-like structure still exists with similar properties as the classical
Fraïssé limit.
We call this structure the
generalized Fraïssé limit
of
K
.
More details are given in Subsection
2.5
.

This generalized Fraïssé limit can be explicitly described as a filtered Boolean power of
\mathbf{A}
,
as used by Arens and Kaplansky
[
4
]
(see also
[
12
]
).
We briefly review this construction.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a Boolean algebra. The
Stone space
of
\mathbf{B}
is the set
X
of ultrafilters on
\mathbf{B}

with the topology whose basic open sets are
\{x\in X\>:\>b\in x\}
for
b\in B
.
By Stone’s Representation Theorem
\mathbf{B}
is isomorphic to the Boolean algebra of clopen sets of
X
.
An arbitrary topological space
X
is homeomorphic to the Stone space of a Boolean algebra if and only if it is compact and totally separated.
For basics on Boolean algebras we refer the reader to
[
23
, Chapter 1]
and for Stone duality to
[
23
, Chapter 3]
.

Recall that there is a unique (up to isomorphism) countable atomless Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
.
It is in fact the Fraïssé limit of all finite Boolean algebras, and will play a particularly important role in this paper.
Its Stone space is the
Cantor space

2^{\omega}
,
i.e. the unique space up to homeomorphism which is compact, Hausdorff, has no isolated points and has a countable basis of clopen sets.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a Boolean algebra with Stone space
X
, and let
\mathbf{A}
be an algebra.
The
Boolean power

\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
is the subalgebra of
\mathbf{A}^{X}
with universe

A^{\mathbf{B}}:=\{f\colon X\to A\>:\>f\text{ is continuous}\},

where
A
is endowed with the discrete topology.

Borrowing the concept from semigroups, we call an element
e
of an algebra
\mathbf{A}
an
idempotent

if
\{e\}
forms a (singleton) subalgebra of
\mathbf{A}
.
For pairwise distinct
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in X
and idempotents
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
\mathbf{A}
,
the subalgebra
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
of
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
with universe

\{f\in A^{\mathbf{B}}\colon f(x_{1})=e_{1},\dots,f(x_{n})=e_{n}\}

is a
filtered Boolean power
of
\mathbf{A}
.

In choosing our notation we have opted to follow the usage in group theory literature, for example
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
, because it emphasizes the power nature of the construction. It is, however, important not to confuse this construction with the standard direct power
\mathbf{A}^{Y}
of
|Y|
copies of
\mathbf{A}
, where
Y
is an arbitrary (unstructured) set.
Other notations used for
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
are e.g.
\mathbf{A}[\mathbf{B}]
in
[
7
,
12
]
or
\mathbf{A}[\mathbf{B}]*
in
[
8
]
.
We can now state our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev
algebra, let the idempotents of
\mathbf{A}
be
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
,
let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra,
and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
be distinct.

(1)

The class
\{\mathbf{A}^{k}\>:\>k\geq 1\}
has the JEP and AP and its generalized Fraïssé limit is isomorphic to
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
.

(2)

Let
\mathbf{F}
be the free algebra of countable rank in the variety
V
generated by
\mathbf{A}
, and let
\theta
be the smallest
congruence such that
\mathbf{F}/\theta
is in the variety
W
generated by all proper subalgebras of
\mathbf{A}
.
Then
\mathbf{F}
embeds into
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
and every
\theta
-class which is a subalgebra of
\mathbf{F}
is isomorphic to

(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
.

In the theorem above it is in fact sufficient to take
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
to be a set of orbit representatives under
\Aut\mathbf{A}
for
the idempotents in
\mathbf{A}
(see Corollary
3.8
).

Part (
1
) of Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
4
and
part (
2
) in Section
5
.

Note that when
\mathbf{A}
is a group, the filtered Boolean power arising in Theorem
1.1
(
2
) is of the form

(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x}_{e}
, where
e
is the identity of
\mathbf{A}
. This is in turn easily seen to be isomorphic to
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{R}}
where
\mathbf{R}
is the countable atomless Boolean ring without identity. (For Boolean powers over Boolean rings see
[
2
,
19
]
.)
Theorem
1.1
(
2
)
in this case gives that the kernel of the homomorphism from
\mathbf{F}
to the free group of countable rank in
W
is isomorphic to
(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x}_{e}
. This observation was made in
[
5
, p. 367-8]
and
[
6
, p. 201]
.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
(
2
),
for groups and loops and rings we obtain
the stronger result that every countable algebra
\mathbf{C}
in
V
is a split
extension of a Boolean power of
\mathbf{A}
by an algebra in
W
(Corollary
5.1
).

We also provide an illustration of Theorem
1.1
for a specific algebra with more than one idempotent.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{A}:=(\mathbb{Z}_{2},x-y+z,\cdot)
be the idempotent reduct of the field of size
2
.
The only proper subalgebras of
\mathbf{A}
are the trivial ones
\{0\}
and
\{1\}
, and
\Aut\mathbf{A}
is trivial as well.
It turns out that the variety
V
generated by
\mathbf{A}
is the class of (isomorphic copies of) filtered Boolean
powers of
\mathbf{A}
.

Every finite algebra in
V
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{A}^{k}
for some integer
k\geq 0
.
Let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra. Then the generalized Fraïssé limit of
\{\mathbf{A}^{k}\>:\>k\geq 1\}

is isomorphic to the free algebra of countable rank in
V
and to

(\mathbf{A^{B}})_{0,1}^{x_{0},x_{1}}\cong(\mathbf{A^{B}})_{0}^{x_{0}}\times(\mathbf{A^{B}})_{1}^{x_{1}}

for distinct
x_{0},x_{1}\in 2^{\omega}
.

We now investigate automorphism groups of filtered Boolean powers.

It is well-known that for any countably infinite first order structure
M
,
the group of automorphisms

G:=\Aut M
affords a natural
topology of pointwise convergence
with basic open sets the cosets of
pointwise stabilizers

G_{Y}
of finite subsets
Y
of
M
.
This makes
G
a topological (in fact, Polish) group.

The group
G
has the
small index property
(SIP) if any subgroup of index less than

2^{\aleph_{0}}
is open, that is, contains the pointwise stabilizer of some finite subset of
M
.

Note that if
\Aut M
has the SIP, then its topological structure is completely determined by its abstract
algebraic structure.
Specifically, if
N
is another countable structure and
\Aut M\cong\Aut N
as groups, then in fact
\Aut M
and
\Aut N
are isomorphic as topological groups
[
26
, Proposition 5.2.2]
.
In particular
\Aut M
has a unique Polish topology.

The
cofinality
of a group
G
is the smallest cardinality
\kappa
such that
G
is the union of a chain of length

\kappa
of proper subgroups.

The
strong cofinality
of
G
is the smallest cardinality
\kappa
such that
G
is the union of a chain

(U_{i})_{i<\kappa}
of proper subsets of
G
such that for all
i<j
we have
U_{i}\subseteq U_{j}
,

U_{i}=U_{i}^{-1}
and
U_{i}U_{i}\subseteq U_{k}
for some
k\in\kappa
.

The group
G
has the
Bergman property
if for each subset
E
of
G
such that
1\in E=E^{-1}
and

E
generates
G
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
E^{k}=G
.

Finally
G
has
ample generics
if for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
the diagonal conjugation action of
G
on
G^{k}

has a comeager orbit, that is, an orbit containing the intersection of countably many dense open subsets of
G^{k}
.

These properties are related as follows.
A group
G
has uncountable strong cofinality if and only if

G
has uncountable cofinality and the Bergman property
(cf.
[
11
, Theorem 2.2]
).
The existence of ample generics implies the SIP as shown by Hodges, Hodkinson, Lascar and Shelah
[
21
]
.
Further, if
M
is
\omega
-categorical and
\Aut M
has ample generics,
then
\Aut M
has uncountable strong cofinality by Kechris and Rosendal
[
22
]
.

The automorphism group
G
of the countable atomless Boolean algebra
(equivalently, the group of homeomorphisms of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
)
was among the first for which these
properties were established. Truss
[
32
]
showed that it has the SIP. Droste and Göbel
[
10
]

proved uncountable strong cofinality.
Later Kwiatkowska
[
24
]
subsumed these results by showing that
G
actually has ample generics.

As our second main result we show that the SIP and uncountable strong cofinality carry over from the countable atomless Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
to filtered Boolean powers of a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra by
\mathbf{B}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra, let
n\geq 0
, let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in A
be idempotents,
let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
be distinct. Then

(1)

(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
is
\omega
-categorical;

(2)

\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
has the SIP;

(3)

\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
has uncountable strong cofinality, in particular, uncountable cofinality and the Bergman property.

Theorem
1.3
(
1
) will follow from results of Macintyre and Rosenstein
[
25
]

in Section
3.5
.
We will prove items (
2
),(
3
) of Theorem
1.3
in
Sections
6
,
7
following the strategies for the countable atomless Boolean
algebra by Truss
[
32
]
and Droste and Göbel
[
10
]
, respectively.
Underpinning these results is an explicit description of
\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
as semidirect product of
the pointwise stabilizer of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in the group of homeomorphisms of
2^{\omega}

and a normal subgroup
K
, which is a certain
closure of a filtered Boolean power of
\Aut\mathbf{A}
by
\mathbf{B}

(see Theorem
3.6
and the subsequent remarks).

It remains open whether
\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
has ample generics. In particular, it is unclear whether Kwiatkowska’s
approach for the countable atomless Boolean algebra from
[
24
]
can be extended to filtered Boolean powers.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we gather general terminology and notation that is needed throughout the paper. More specialised concepts and results will be introduced where they are needed in the text.

2.1.
Notation

The set of natural numbers
\{1,2,\dots\}
will be denoted by
\mathbb{N}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we write
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
First order structures will be denoted by the boldface capital letters
\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{B}
,
\mathbf{C}
etc.,
with the corresponding letters
A
,
B
,
C
standing for their underlying sets (universes). One exception will be groups, which will be denoted by non-bold letters

G
,
H
etc.
For an algebra
\mathbf{A}
and a subset
U\subseteq A
we will denote by
\langle U\rangle
the
subuniverse
of
\mathbf{A}

generated
by
U
, i.e. the carrier set of the subalgebra of
\mathbf{A}
generated by
U
.
Topological spaces will be denoted by
X
,
Y
,
Z
etc. For a structure
\mathbf{A}
we will write
\Aut\mathbf{A}
for its automorphism group, and for a topological space
X
we will write
\Homeo X
for its group of homeomorphisms. For a permutation group acting on a set
X
and a subset
Y\subseteq X
the pointwise stabilizer of
Y
in
G
is denoted by

G_{Y}:=\{g\in G\>:\>g(x)=x\text{ for all }x\in Y\}
.

2.2.
Boolean algebras

Stone’s Representation Theorem will be used throughout the paper, usually without explicit reference. An abstract Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
will be at times identified as the algebra of clopen sets of its Stone space
X
.
It will be useful to bear in mind that
\Aut\mathbf{B}
and
\Homeo X
are naturally isomorphic to each other; see
[
23
, Theorem 8.2]
.

2.3.
Boolean powers

Our main references are
[
7
]
and
[
8
, Section IV.5]
.
Our definition follows the latter source.
In
[
7
]
instead of continuous functions from the Stone space
X
of
\mathbf{B}
into
\mathbf{A}
, the elements are taken to be
mappings from
A
to
B
. To obtain such a mapping from a continuous function
f\colon X\rightarrow A
, one just maps

a\in A
to its pre-image
f^{-1}(a)
, regarded as an element of
B
by Stone’s duality. Equipped with this it is easy to
see that the two definitions are equivalent.

Regarding
filtered
Boolean powers, there is no universally agreed terminology or notation in literature.
The concept first seems to appear in
[
4
]
where Arens and Kaplansky use it for representing rings.
In
[
13
]
Foster generalizes it to arbitrary algebras calling it Boolean extensions.
As far as we know the first use of the term filtered Boolean powers is due to Burris and Werner
[
9
]
.
This term is also preferred by Evans in his survey
[
12
]
.

2.4.
Subdirect products of Mal’cev algebras

Certain structural features of subgroups of direct products of groups generalize to Mal’cev algebras.
We present those that will be used in this paper.
First is Fleischer’s extension of Goursat’s Lemma for groups
[
18
, Theorem 5.5.1]
.

Lemma 2.1

(cf. Fleischer’s Lemma
[
8
, Lemma IV.10.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}_{1},\mathbf{A}_{2}
be algebras belonging to a variety
V
with a Mal’cev term, and
let
\mathbf{C}\leq\mathbf{A}_{1}\times\mathbf{A}_{2}
be a subdirect product of
\mathbf{A}_{1}
and
\mathbf{A}_{2}
.
Then there exist
\mathbf{D}\in V
and epimorphisms
\psi_{i}\colon\mathbf{A}_{i}\rightarrow\mathbf{D}
for
i\in[2]
such that

C=\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A_{1}\times A_{2}\colon\psi_{1}(a_{1})=\psi_{2}(a_{2})\}.

The second states that a finite subdirect power of a simple Mal’cev algebra has a very restricted form:
it is entirely determined by the projections to a subset of direct components, and these projections are pairwise independent.

Lemma 2.2

(cf. Foster–Pixley Theorem
[
8
, Corollary IV 10.2]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a simple Mal’cev algebra, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathbf{C}\leq\mathbf{A}^{n}
be a subdirect power.
Then there exist a non-empty subset
I\subseteq[n]
, a mapping
f\colon{[n]\setminus I}\rightarrow I
and automorphisms
\theta_{i}\in\Aut\mathbf{A}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I
such that

C=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}\colon a_{i}=\theta_{i}(a_{f(i)})\text{ for all }i\in[n]\setminus I\}.

In particular,
\mathbf{C}\cong\mathbf{A}^{|I|}
.

Finally, a direct power of a simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra has no skew-congruences.

Lemma 2.3

(cf.
[
16
, Lemma 9.69, Corollary 9.66]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Every congruence of the direct power
\mathbf{A}^{n}
is a product congruence,
i.e. it has the form
\theta_{1}\times\dots\times\theta_{n}
, where each
\theta_{i}
is either the total relation or the equality on
\mathbf{A}
.

2.5.
Generalized Fraïssé limits.

As mentioned in the introduction, some of our filtered Boolean powers will be representable as so called generalized Fraïssé limits of finite powers.
The need for generalization comes from the fact that the finite powers do not satisfy the hereditary property from the classical Fraïssé theory.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
20
, Exercise 7.1.11]
)
.

Let
K
be a countable class of first order structures of the same type satisfying the JEP and AP.
Then there exists a countable structure
\mathbf{D}
which satisfies the following properties:

(1)

up to isomorphism, the class of all finitely generated substructures of
\mathbf{D}
is the class of all finitely generated substructures of members of
K
;

(2)

\mathbf{D}
is a direct limit of structures in
K
; and

(3)

every isomorphism between substructures of
\mathbf{D}
that are isomorphic to some element in
K
extends
to an automorphism of
\mathbf{D}
.

We will call the structure
\mathbf{D}
in the above result the
generalized Fraïssé limit
of
K
.
Property (
3
) will be referred to as
K
-homogeneity
.

3.
Filtered Boolean powers

We collect the structural information on filtered Boolean powers and their automorphism groups that we need for
proving our main results.
Throughout this section we use the following notation unless specified otherwise:

•

\mathbf{A}
is a finite simple non-abelian Mal’cev algebra;

•

n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
;

•

e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
are idempotents of
\mathbf{A}
; they do not need to be distinct or to include all the idempotents;

•

\mathbf{B}
is a Boolean algebra with Stone space
X
; the latter may be taken to consist of the ultrafilters on
\mathbf{B}
;

•

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are pairwise distinct points in
X
, and

X^{\circ}:=X\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is equipped with the subspace topology from
X
.

3.1.
Filtered Boolean powers represented on arbitrary sets

Filtered Boolean powers can be defined using an arbitrary representation of a Boolean algebra as field of sets,
not only via its Stone space.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{B}
be a subalgebra of the Boolean algebra of subsets of a set
Y
.

(1)

For any
y\in Y
the set
y^{\prime}:=\{b\in B\>:\>y\in b\}
is an ultrafilter on
\mathbf{B}
.

(2)

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite algebra, let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in A
be idempotents, and let
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in Y

be distinct. Then the set

\displaystyle D^{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in A^{Y}\>:\>f^{-1}(a)\in B\text{ for all }a\in A,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\text{and }f(y_{i})=e_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}

is the universe of a subalgebra
\mathbf{D}^{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
of
\mathbf{A}^{Y}

isomorphic to the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{y^{\prime}_{1},\dots,y^{\prime}_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
.

Proof.

(
1
) is clear.

(
2
) For
b\in B
, let
b^{**}:=\{x\in X\>:\>b\in x\}
. Let
B^{**}:=\{c\subseteq X\>:\>c\text{ clopen}\}
.
By Stone’s Representation Theorem

\varphi\colon\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{B}^{**},\ b\mapsto b^{**},

is an isomorphism. Now

D:=\{f\in A^{Y}\>:\>f^{-1}(a)\in B\text{ for all }a\in A\}

is the universe of a subalgebra
\mathbf{D}
of
\mathbf{A}^{Y}
.
For
f\in D
define
f^{\prime}\colon X\to A
by

(f^{\prime})^{-1}(a)=(f^{-1}(a))^{**}\text{ for all }a\in A.

Equivalently, if
Y=b_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup b_{k}
(disjoint union) and
f(b_{j})=\{a_{j}\}
for
b_{j}\in B,j\in[k]
, then

X=b^{**}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup b^{**}_{k}
and
f^{\prime}(b^{**}_{j})=\{a_{j}\}
for
j\in[k]
.
Since
\varphi\colon b\mapsto b^{**}
is an isomorphism and
f(b)=f^{\prime}(b^{**})
for all
b\in B
,
it follows that

\varphi^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}},\ f\mapsto f^{\prime},

is an isomorphism.

We claim that
\varphi^{\prime}
restricts to the required isomorphism between

\mathbf{D}^{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
and
(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{y^{\prime}_{1},\dots,y^{\prime}_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
. To see that
the domain and codomain are correct,
note that for
f\in D
and
i\in[n]
,

\displaystyle f(y_{i})=e_{i}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y_{i}\in f^{-1}(e_{i})\Leftrightarrow f^{-1}(e_{i})\in y^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow y^{\prime}_{i}\in(f^{-1}(e_{i}))^{**}=(f^{\prime})^{-1}(e_{i})\Leftrightarrow f^{\prime}(y_{i}^{\prime})=e_{i}.

Hence

\varphi^{\prime}(\mathbf{D}^{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}})=(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{y^{\prime}_{1},\dots,y^{\prime}_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}},

completing the proof.
∎

3.2.
Congruences

Our description of congruences of filtered Boolean powers extends those of Boolean powers of simple algebras by
Burris
[
7
, Theorem 3.5, Corollary 3.6]
.

For
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
and
Y\subseteq X
let

\theta_{Y}:=\{(f,g)\in D^{2}\>:\>f|_{Y}=g|_{Y}\}

denote the kernel of the projection of
\mathbf{D}
to
Y
.

Lemma 3.2

(cf.
[
7
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
.

(1)

The principal congruence of
\mathbf{D}
generated by a pair
(f,g)\in D^{2}
is the kernel
of the projection of
\mathbf{D}
onto
the clopen set

b:=\{x\in X\>:\>f(x)=g(x)\}.

(2)

Every congruence
\theta
of
\mathbf{D}
is the kernel of the projection of
\mathbf{D}
onto

Y:=\bigcap_{(f,g)\in\theta}\{x\in X\>:\>f(x)=g(x)\}.

Proof.

(
1
)
Denote by
\theta
the principal congruence generated by
(f,g)
.
Note that every finite subalgebra of
\mathbf{D}
is contained in a filtered Boolean power
\mathbf{D}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{C}}
for some finite subalgebra

\mathbf{C}
of
\mathbf{B}
. Here we view
C
as a finite set of clopens on
X
and
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{C}}
as the subalgebra of
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
with the universe

A^{\mathbf{C}}=\{h\colon X\to A\>:\>h^{-1}(a)\in C\text{ for all }a\in A\}.

Now let
\mathbf{C}
be a finite subalgebra of
\mathbf{B}
such that
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{C}}
contains both
f
and
g
.
For all
x\in b
the projection of
\theta\cap(D\cap A^{\mathbf{C}})^{2}
onto
x
is contained in the equality on
A
;
else if
x\in X\setminus b
we have
f(x)\neq g(x)
, and hence the projection of
\theta\cap(D\cap A^{\mathbf{C}})^{2}
onto
x

is the total congruence on
\mathbf{A}
, as
\mathbf{A}
is simple.
By Lemma
2.3
each congruence of the finite algebra
\mathbf{D}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{C}}
is a product congruence
and uniquely determined by its projections onto the points
x\in X
. It follows that

\theta\cap(D\cap A^{\mathbf{C}})^{2}=\{(u,v)\in(D\cap A^{\mathbf{C}})^{2}\>:\>u|_{b}=v|_{b}\}.

Hence the restrictions of
\theta
and the projection kernel
\theta_{b}
onto any finite
\mathbf{D}\cap\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{C}}

containing
f
and
g
coincide.
This proves (
1
).

(
2
)
For
S
a set of clopens in
X
, we claim that

\bigvee_{b\in S}\theta_{b}=\theta_{\bigcap S}.

(3.1)

From Lemma
2.3
it is straightforward that
\theta_{b}\vee\theta_{c}=\theta_{b\cap c}
for clopens
b,c
on
X
.
Hence
(f,g)\in\bigvee_{b\in S}\theta_{b}
if and only if
f
and
g
agree on some element in the filter
F
of clopens
on
X
that is generated by
S
.
As a standard consequence of the Ultrafilter Lemma, every filter is the intersection of the ultrafilters which contain it.
By Stone’s Representation Theorem and Lemma
3.1
(
1
),
the ultrafilters of the Boolean algebra of clopens on
X
are exactly of the form

\{b\text{ clopen}\>:\>x\in b\}=:x^{\prime}
for
x\in X
.
Then
F\subseteq x^{\prime}
if and only if every
b\in F
contains
x
if and only if
x\in\bigcap S
.
Hence

F=\bigcap\{x^{\prime}\>:\>x\in\bigcap S\}=\{b\text{ clopen }\>:\>\bigcap S\subseteq b\}

and (
3.1
) follows. Since

\theta=\bigvee_{(f,g)\in\theta}\theta_{\{x\in X\>:\>f(x)=g(x)\}}

using (
1
), we obtain
\theta=\theta_{Y}
as claimed.
∎

For a subset
Y
of
X
, the quotient
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}/\theta_{Y}
is isomorphic to the restriction of
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
to
Y
.
The latter is again isomorphic to a filtered Boolean power by Lemma
3.1
.
Thus every homomorphic image of a filtered Boolean power is a filtered Boolean power again.

Lemma
3.2
actually yields a lattice isomorphism between the lattice of congruences
of the filtered Boolean power
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
and the lattice of filters contained in the intersection of ultrafilters

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
on the Boolean algebra
\mathbf{B}
. In particular every principal filter generated by some clopen

b
containing the points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
corresponds to a principal congruence on
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
generated by a pair of
functions
f,g
that coincide exactly on
b
.

3.3.
Automorphisms

In this subsection we give a detailed analysis of the automorphisms of
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
,
in terms of automorphisms of
\mathbf{A}
and of
\mathbf{B}
.
With some additional mild assumptions regarding
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
we obtain a semidirect decomposition of

\Aut\mathbf{D}
in Theorem
3.6
.
Our results generalize those for Boolean powers of groups by Boolean rings
by Apps
[
2
, Theorem C]
.

Automorphisms of
\mathbf{B}
act naturally on the ultrafilters of
\mathbf{B}
,
i.e. the points in the Stone space
X
of
\mathbf{B}
.
This correspondence witnesses the well-known fact that
\Aut\mathbf{B}\cong\Homeo X
.
Reversing it and lifting to the Boolean power leads to the following construction.
For
\psi\in(\Homeo X)_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
define

\psi^{\mathbf{D}}\colon D\to D,\ f\mapsto f\psi^{-1}.

(3.2)

Equivalently
[\psi^{\mathbf{D}}(f)]^{-1}(a)=\psi f^{-1}(a)
for all
f\in D,a\in A
.
It is easy to check that
\psi^{\mathbf{D}}
is an automorphism of
\mathbf{D}
and that

g\colon(\Homeo X)_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}\to\Aut\mathbf{D},\ \psi\mapsto\psi^{\mathbf{D}},

(3.3)

is a group embedding.

In the following lemma we show that, conversely, automorphisms of
\mathbf{D}
induce homeomorphisms on
X^{\circ}
, using the fact that every automorphism of
\mathbf{D}
induces an automorphism on the congruence lattice of
\mathbf{D}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
x\in X
, let
\pi_{x}\colon(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}\to\mathbf{A},\ f\mapsto f(x)
, be the projection at
x
,
and let
\theta_{x}:=\ker\pi_{x}
be its kernel. For any
\varphi\in\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
the following hold:

(1)

The mapping
\varphi^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}
defined by

\varphi(\theta_{x})=\theta_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}

is a homeomorphism.

(2)

For every
x\in X^{\circ}
, the mapping
\varphi_{x}\colon A\rightarrow A
defined by

\varphi_{x}\pi_{x}=\pi_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}\varphi

(3.4)

is an automorphism of
\mathbf{A}
.

(3)

For any convergent net
(y_{i})_{i\in I}
in
X^{\circ}
with limit
x_{k}
for
k\in[n]
, all cluster points of

(\varphi^{\circ}(y_{i}))_{i\in I}
are in
\{x_{\ell}\>:\>\ell\in[n],\ e_{\ell}\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})(e_{k})\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
.

(
1
)
To see that
\varphi^{\circ}
is well-defined, let
x\in X^{\circ}
.
Since
\mathbf{A}
is simple,
\theta_{x}
is a maximal congruence of
\mathbf{D}
, which
\varphi
maps to another maximal congruence

\varphi(\theta_{x})=\{(\varphi(f),\varphi(g))\in D^{2}\>:\>f(x)=g(x)\}.

By Lemma
3.2
we have a unique point
\varphi^{\circ}(x)
in
X^{\circ}
such that

\varphi(\theta_{x})=\theta_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}
as required.

Next note that
\varphi^{\circ}
is bijective with inverse
(\varphi^{-1})^{\circ}
since by
definition

\theta_{x}=\varphi^{-1}(\theta_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}).

For continuity, it suffices to show that
\varphi^{\circ}
maps any clopen subset
b
of
X
that is contained in
X^{\circ}

to such a clopen subset again.
As mentioned in the comment after Lemma
3.2
, the kernel of the projection on the clopen

X\setminus b
is the principal congruence generated by a pair of functions
f,g\in D
which coincide exactly on

X\setminus b
.
It follows that the congruence generated by
(\varphi(f),\varphi(g))
is the kernel of the projection to the clopen

\{x\in X\>:\>\varphi(f)(x)=\varphi(g)(x)\}=X\setminus\varphi^{\circ}(b).

Hence
\varphi^{\circ}(b)
is clopen in
X
and disjoint from
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

(
2
)
Let
x\in X^{\circ}
.
Note that (
3.4
) can be rewritten as

\varphi_{x}(f(x))=\varphi(f)(\varphi^{\circ}(x))\quad\text{for all }f\in D.

Suppose
f,g\in D
satisfy
f(x)=g(x)
, that is,
(f,g)\in\theta_{x}
.
Then
(\varphi(f),\varphi(g))
is in
\varphi(\theta_{x})=\theta_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}
.
Hence

\varphi_{x}(f(x))=\varphi(f)(\varphi^{\circ}(x))=\varphi(g)(\varphi^{\circ}(x))=\varphi_{x}(g(x)).

So (
3.4
) uniquely defines
\varphi_{x}
.

To check that
\varphi_{x}
is a homomorphism on
\mathbf{A}
, let
t
be a
k
-ary operation in the signature of
\mathbf{A}
and

f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in D
. Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}(t^{\mathbf{A}}(f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{k}(x)))

\displaystyle=\varphi(t^{\mathbf{D}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k}))\,(\varphi^{\circ}(x))

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{D}}(\varphi(f_{1}),\dots,\varphi(f_{k}))\,(\varphi^{\circ}(x))

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{A}}(\varphi(f_{1})(\varphi^{\circ}(x)),\dots,\varphi(f_{k})(\varphi^{\circ}(x)))

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{A}}(\varphi_{x}(f_{1}(x)),\dots,\varphi_{x}(f_{k}(x))).

Finally for the bijectivity of
\varphi_{x}
, recall that
(\varphi^{-1})^{\circ}=(\varphi^{\circ})^{-1}
.
Hence the unique mapping
(\varphi^{-1})_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}
defined by (
3.4
) is the inverse of
\varphi_{x}
.

(
3
)
The point
x_{k}
, as the limit of the net
(y_{i})_{i\in I}
, is the unique cluster point of that net.
In particular,
(y_{i})_{i\in I}
has no cluster points in
X^{\circ}
.
The mapping
(\varphi^{-1})^{\circ}
is continuous on
X^{\circ}
by (
1
), and therefore
all cluster points of the net
(\varphi^{\circ}(y_{i}))_{i\in I}
are in
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

Next from the definition of
\varphi_{x}

we see that

\varphi_{y_{i}}(f(y_{i}))=\varphi(f)(\varphi^{\circ}(y_{i}))\quad\text{for all }f\in D,\ i\in I.

Since
\lim y_{i}=x_{k}
, we have

\forall f\in D\ \exists j\in I\ \forall i\geq j:\ y_{i}\in f^{-1}(e_{k}).

Note that

y_{i}\in f^{-1}(e_{k})
implies
\varphi_{y_{i}}(f(y_{i}))\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})(e_{k})

and hence

\forall f\in D\ \exists j\in I\ \forall i\geq j:\>\varphi(f)(\varphi^{\circ}(y_{i}))\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})(e_{k}).

Since
\varphi
is an automorphism of
D
, this can be written as

\forall g\in D\ \exists j\in I\ \forall i\geq j:\>g(\varphi^{\circ}(y_{i}))\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})(e_{k}).

It follows that if
x_{\ell}
is a cluster point of
(\varphi^{\circ}(y_{i}))_{i\in I}
,
then
g(x_{\ell})=e_{\ell}
is in
(\Aut\mathbf{A})(e_{k})
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

(1)

The mapping

h^{\circ}\colon\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}\rightarrow\Homeo X^{\circ},\ \varphi\mapsto\varphi^{\circ},

is a group homomorphism.

(2)

For
\varphi\in\ker h^{\circ}
,

\varphi_{*}\colon X^{\circ}\to\Aut\mathbf{A},\ x\mapsto\varphi_{x},

is continuous.

(3)

The mapping

p\colon\ker h^{\circ}\to(\Aut\mathbf{A})^{X^{\circ}},\ \varphi\mapsto\varphi_{*},

is a group monomorphism.

Proof.

(
1
) By Lemma
3.3
(
1
),
\varphi^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}\rightarrow X^{\circ}
is the unique
mapping that satisfies
\varphi(\theta_{x})=\theta_{\varphi^{\circ}(x)}
for all
x\in X^{\circ}
.
Therefore, for
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\Aut(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
we have

\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}(\theta_{x})=\varphi_{1}(\theta_{\varphi_{2}^{\circ}(x)})=\theta_{\varphi_{1}^{\circ}\varphi_{2}^{\circ}(x)},

and it follows that
(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})^{\circ}=\varphi_{1}^{\circ}\varphi_{2}^{\circ}
, as required.

(
2
)
Let
\varphi\in\ker h^{\circ}
, that is,
\varphi^{\circ}=\mathrm{id}_{X^{\circ}}
, let
\alpha\in\Aut\mathbf{A}
and
x\in X^{\circ}
.
Then the definition of
\varphi_{x}
in Lemma
3.3
(
2
) yields

\alpha=\varphi_{x}\Leftrightarrow\alpha\pi_{x}=\pi_{x}\varphi.

(3.5)

Consequently

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\{x\in X^{\circ}\>:\>\varphi_{x}=\alpha\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in X^{\circ}\>:\>\alpha\pi_{x}=\pi_{x}\varphi\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in X^{\circ}\>:\>\alpha f(x)=\varphi(f)(x)\text{ for all }f\in D\}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\bigcap_{f\in D}\bigcup_{a\in A}\bigl((\alpha f)^{-1}(a)\cap(\varphi(f))^{-1}(a)\bigr)\Bigr]\cap X^{\circ}.

As
\alpha f
and
\varphi(f)
are both continuous on
X
, all the sets

(\alpha f)^{-1}(a)
,
(\varphi(f))^{-1}(a)
are clopen in
X
.
It follows that
\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
is closed in
X^{\circ}
. But since this is true for all
\alpha\in\Aut\mathbf{A}
,
the set
\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
is in fact clopen, completing the proof that

\varphi_{*}
is continuous.

(
3
)
Let
x\in X^{\circ}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\ker h
.
Using (
3.5
) for these maps as well as for their composition we obtain

\varphi_{x}\psi_{x}\pi_{x}=\varphi_{x}\pi_{x}\psi=\pi_{x}\varphi\psi=(\varphi\psi)_{x}\pi_{x}.

Hence
(\varphi\psi)_{x}=\varphi_{x}\psi_{x}
for all
x\in X^{\circ}
, that is,
p
is a homomorphism.

To see that
p
is injective, let
\varphi\in\ker p
, that is,
\varphi^{\circ}=\mathrm{id}_{X^{\circ}}
and

\varphi_{x}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
for all
x\in X^{\circ}
.
For
f\in D
and
x\in X^{\circ}
, using (
3.5
) we have

f(x)=\varphi_{x}(f(x))=\pi_{x}\varphi\pi_{x}^{-1}(f(x))=\pi_{x}\varphi(f)=\varphi(f)(x).

Hence
\varphi=\mathrm{id}_{D}
.
∎

In general the homeomorphism
\varphi^{\circ}
on
X^{\circ}
from Lemma
3.3

does not have a continuous extension on
X
as shown in the following.

Example 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite non-abelian simple Mal’cev algebra with an idempotent
e
,
let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and let
x_{0}:=00\dots
,
x_{1}:=11\dots
in
2^{\omega}
.
We will construct an automorphism
\varphi
of the Boolean power

\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0},x_{1}}_{e,e}
such that the homeomorphism
\varphi^{\circ}
of
X^{\circ}:=2^{\omega}\setminus\{x_{0},x_{1}\}

cannot be extended to a homeomorphism of
2^{\omega}
.

To this end, consider the following clopen sets in
2^{\omega}
:

\displaystyle b_{i}:=\{\sigma\in 2^{\omega}\>:\>\sigma_{0}=i\}\quad\text{for }i\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle b_{ij}:=\{\sigma\in 2^{\omega}\>:\>\sigma_{l}=i\text{ for }l\in[j],\sigma_{j}=1-i\}\quad\text{for }i\in\{0,1\},j\geq 1.

Note that

(\bigcup_{j\geq 1}b_{ij})\cup\{x_{i}\}=b_{i}\quad\text{for }i\in\{0,1\}.

Define
\psi:X^{\circ}\rightarrow X^{\circ}
by

\psi\colon(\underbrace{i,\dots,i}_{j},1-i,\sigma_{j+2},\sigma_{j+3},\dots)\\
\mapsto\begin{cases}(\underbrace{i,\dots,i}_{j},1-i,\sigma_{j+2},\sigma_{j+3},\dots)&\text{if }j\text{ is odd},\\
(\underbrace{1-i,\dots,1-i}_{j},i,\sigma_{j+2},\sigma_{j+3},\dots)&\text{if }j\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

In particular,

\psi(b_{ij})=\begin{cases}b_{ij}&\text{if }j\text{ is odd},\\
b_{1-i,j}&\text{if }j\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\psi
is indeed a homeomorphism of
X^{\circ}
.
But
\psi
cannot be extended to a homeomorphism of
2^{\omega}
. Indeed, supposing it could,
we would have

\displaystyle\psi(b_{0})

\displaystyle=\psi(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}b_{0i}\ \cup\>\{x_{0}\})=\bigcup_{in\in\mathbb{N}}\psi(b_{0i})\ \cup\>\{\psi(x_{0})\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}b_{0,2i-1}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}b_{1,2i}\ \cup\>\{\psi(x_{0})\}.

The set
b_{0}
is closed in
2^{\omega}
, whereas the set
\psi(b_{0})
has both
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
in its closure,
but contains only one of them, namely
\psi(x_{0})
.

Now define
\varphi\colon D\rightarrow D
by

(\varphi(f))(x):=\begin{cases}f\psi^{-1}(x)&\text{if }x\in X^{\circ},\\
e&\text{if }x\in\{x_{0},x_{1}\}.\end{cases}

(3.6)

We show that
\varphi
is an automorphism of
\mathbf{D}
and that
{\varphi^{\circ}=\psi}
.

To verify that
\varphi
is well-defined, we show that
\varphi(f)
is continuous on
2^{\omega}
.
It is certainly continuous on
X^{\circ}
as a composition of two continuous maps.
We now claim that
\varphi(f)
is also continuous at
x_{i}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
.
Since
f\in D
, the set
c:=f^{-1}(e)
is clopen in
X
and contains

3.4.
Isomorphisms

In this subsection we discuss isomorphisms between filtered Boolean powers, in the case where
\mathbf{B}
is the countable atomless Boolean algebra.
The aim is to show that in a Boolean power
\mathbf{D}=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
we can add or remove filtering idempotents which
belong to the orbits of the remaining ones
under
\Aut\mathbf{A}
without changing the isomorphism type of
\mathbf{D}
.

As a stepping stone towards the main result of this section, the following lemma establishes one important special case when
the number of filter points in a filtered Boolean power can be reduced.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be an algebra with idempotent
e
,
let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and let
x_{0}\in 2^{\omega}
.

Then
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e}\times(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e}\cong(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e}
.

Proof.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be two disjoint copies of
2^{\omega}
and fix
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for
i\in[2]
.
We will represent the two copies of
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e}
as

D_{i}:=\{f\colon X_{i}\rightarrow A\>:\>f\text{ is continuous and }f(x_{i})=e\}\quad\text{ for }i\in[2].

Consider now the space
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
with the union topology; it is homeomorphic to
2^{\omega}
.
Let
\sim
be the equivalence relation on
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
with the equivalence classes

\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
and
\{x\}
for
x\in(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\setminus\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
.
The quotient space
X:=(X_{1}\cup X_{2})/{\sim}
is again homeomorphic to
2^{\omega}
.
This is easy to show directly by verifying that it is Hausdorff, compact, has no isolated points and has a countable basis
of clopen sets. Alternatively, one can note that
\sim
is a Boolean equivalence in the sense of
[
23
]
,
and that the resulting quotient
(X_{1}\cup X_{2})/\!\sim
has a countable basis of clopen sets and no isolated points.
We will take its points to be
(X_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\})\cup(X_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}\})\cup\{x_{12}\}
and note that there are
two types of open sets:

•

U_{1}\cup U_{2}
where
U_{i}
is open in
X_{i}
and
x_{i}\not\in U_{i}
for
i\in[2]
;

•

(U_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\}\cup(U_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}\})\cup\{x_{12}\}
where
U_{i}
is open in
X_{i}
and
x_{i}\in U_{i}
for
i\in[2]
.

Let

D:=\{f\colon X\rightarrow X\>:\>f\text{ is continuous and }f(x_{12})=e\}

be yet another copy of
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e}
. We will prove the lemma by showing that

\psi\colon D_{1}\times D_{2}\rightarrow D
defined by

\psi(f_{1},f_{2})(x):=\begin{cases}f_{1}(x)&\text{if }x\in X_{1}\setminus\{x_{1}\},\\
f_{2}(x)&\text{if }x\in X_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}\},\\
e&\text{if }x=x_{12},\end{cases}

is an isomorphism.

We prove that
\psi
is well-defined, i.e. that
\psi(f_{1},f_{2})\in D
for all
f_{i}\in D_{i}
,
i\in[n]
.
Let
a\in A
.
If
a\neq e
, then

(\psi(f_{1},f_{2}))^{-1}(a)=f_{1}^{-1}(a)\cup f_{2}^{-1}(a),

where each
f_{i}^{-1}(a)
is a clopen in
X_{i}
not containing
x_{i}
.
Hence
(\psi(f_{1},f_{2}))^{-1}(a)
is clopen in
X
.
Similarly, for
a=e
we have

(\psi(f_{1},f_{2}))^{-1}(a)=(f_{1}^{-1}(e)\setminus\{x_{1}\})\cup(f_{2}^{-1}(e)\setminus\{x_{2}\})\cup\{x_{12}\},

again a clopen in
X
.
Hence indeed
\psi(f_{1},f_{2})\in D
.
It is clear from the definition that
\psi
is bijective. It is a homomorphism by the componentwise definition of
operations in
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
and
D
.
∎

We now show that any filtered Boolean power of any algebra by the countable atomless Boolean algebra is isomorphic to a Boolean power in which the filtering idempotents are in distinct
\Aut\mathbf{A}
-orbits.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite algebra,
n\leq m

and let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be orbit representatives of
idempotent elements
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
in
\mathbf{A}
under
\Aut\mathbf{A}
.
Let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in 2^{\omega}
be distinct.

Then
(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}}
is isomorphic to
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
.

Proof.

By partitioning
2^{\omega}
into disjoint clopens
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
with
x_{i}\in b_{i}
,
and recalling that all
b_{i}
are homeomorphic to
2^{\omega}
, we obtain

(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{i}}

(3.10)

where
x_{0}\in 2^{\omega}
.

Now, for every
j\in\{n+1,\dots,m\}
there exist
i\in[n]
and
\alpha\in\Aut\mathbf{A}
such that

\alpha(e_{j})=e_{i}
.
Clearly
(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{j}}\rightarrow(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{i}},\ f\mapsto\alpha f,
is an isomorphism.
Together with Lemma
3.7
this yields

(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{i}}\times(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{j}}\cong(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{i}}\times(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{i}}\cong(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{0}}_{e_{i}}.

The result follows from (

).
∎

3.5.
\omega
-categoricity

Macintyre and Rosenstein’s characterization of
\omega
-categorical filtered Boolean powers in
[
25
]

yields the following special case and in particular Theorem
1.3
(
1
).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite algebra, let
n\geq 0
, let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be idempotents of
\mathbf{A}
,
let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
be distinct.

Then
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
is
\omega
-categorical.

Proof.

Since Macintyre and Rosenstein use Stone spaces of maximal ideals instead of ultrafilters in
[
25
]
,
we change to that dual perspective in the following.
Note that the complement of an ultrafilter
x^{\prime}_{i}:=B\setminus x_{i}
is a maximal ideal of
\mathbf{B}
for
i\in[n]
.
We can identify
x^{\prime}_{i}
with the set of all clopens in the Stone space of
\mathbf{B}
that do not contain the point
x_{i}
.

Recall that the Heyting algebra of ideals of
\mathbf{B}
has operations

I\wedge J:=I\cap J,\quad I\vee J:=I+J,\quad I\rightarrow J:=\{b\in B\>:\>bI\subseteq J\}

for ideals
I,J
. For
U,V\subseteq[n]
, it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in U}x^{\prime}_{i}\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in V}x^{\prime}_{i}=\bigwedge_{i\in U\cup V}x^{\prime}_{i},

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in U}x^{\prime}_{i}+\bigwedge_{i\in V}x^{\prime}_{i}=\bigwedge_{i\in U\cap V}x^{\prime}_{i},

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in U}x^{\prime}_{i}\rightarrow\bigwedge_{i\in V}x^{\prime}_{i}=\bigwedge_{i\in V\setminus U}x^{\prime}_{i}.

So the Heyting algebra
\mathbf{H}_{0}
of ideals of
\mathbf{B}
generated by
x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n},0,B
has universe

\{\bigwedge_{i\in U}x^{\prime}_{i}\>:\>U\subseteq[n]\}\cup\{0\}.

In particular
\mathbf{H}_{0}
is finite and
\mathbf{B}/J
has finitely many atoms for any ideal
J
in
\mathbf{H}_{0}
.
Now the augmented Boolean algebra
(\mathbf{B},x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n})
is a first order structure
with operations from the Boolean algebra and unary relations that are ideals
x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n}
(see
[
25
, p. 137]
).
Then
(\mathbf{B},x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n})
is
\omega
-categorical by
[
25
, Theorem 7]
.
Hence
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
is
\omega
-categorical by
[
25
, Theorem 5]
.
∎

(a_{1},\dots,a_{u})

(\underbrace{a_{1},\dots,a_{1}}_{v_{1}\text{ times}},\dots,\underbrace{a_{u},\dots,a_{u}}_{v_{u}\text{ times}},\underbrace{e_{1},\dots,e_{1}}_{w_{1}\text{ times}},\dots,\underbrace{e_{n},\dots,e_{n}}_{w_{n}\text{ times}}).

Since
K
has the JEP and AP, by Lemma
2.4
there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) countable algebra

\mathbf{C}
such that

(i)

the set of finitely generated subalgebras of
\mathbf{C}
is the class of all finitely generated algebras that can be embedded into
algebras in
K
up to isomorphism,

(ii)

\mathbf{C}
is a direct limit of algebras in
K
, and

(iii)

every isomorphism between subalgebras of
\mathbf{C}
that are isomorphic to some element in
K

extends to an automorphism of
\mathbf{C}
(i.e.,
\mathbf{C}
is
K
-
homogeneous
).

Let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
be distinct.
To see that the filtered Boolean power
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}

is isomorphic to the generalized Fraïssé limit
\mathbf{C}

it suffices to show that
\mathbf{D}
satisfies (i), (ii), (iii).
Properties (i) and (ii) follow from the fact that
\mathbf{D}
is locally finite and that every finite subalgebra of
\mathbf{D}

is contained in a subalgebra isomorphic to some finite direct power of
\mathbf{A}
, as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
To prove (iii) we emulate
[
20
, Lemma 7.1.4]
, and show that

\mathbf{D}
is
weakly
K
-homogeneous
, in the sense that for any
u\leq v
and
any embeddings
\varphi\colon\mathbf{A}^{u}\to\mathbf{A}^{v}
and
\psi\colon\mathbf{A}^{u}\to\mathbf{D}
there exists an embedding

\psi^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{A}^{v}\to\mathbf{D}
such that
\psi^{\prime}\varphi=\psi
.
That weak
K
-homogeneity implies
K
-homogeneity is straightforward using a standard back-and-forth argument as in
[
20
, cf. Lemma 7.1.4]
.

To verify weak
K
-homogeneity, as above we may assume that

\varphi(a_{1},\dots,a_{u})=(\underbrace{a_{1},\dots,a_{1}}_{p_{1}\text{ times}},\dots,\underbrace{a_{u},\dots,a_{u}}_{p_{u}\text{ times}},\underbrace{e_{1},\dots,e_{1}}_{q_{1}\text{ times}},\dots,\underbrace{e_{n},\dots,e_{n}}_{q_{n}\text{ times}})

with multiplicities
p_{1},\dots,p_{u}\geq 1,q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\geq 0
for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{u}\in A
.
For
\psi\colon\mathbf{A}^{u}\to\mathbf{D}
there exist
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
(
i\in[u]
), automorphisms

\alpha_{ij}
of
\mathbf{A}
(
i\in[u],j\in[m_{i}]
), and a partition of
2^{\omega}
into clopens

c_{ij}
(
i\in[u],{j\in[m_{i}]}
) and
b_{i}
(
i\in[n]
) such that

\psi(a_{1},\dots,a_{u})(x)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{ij}(a_{i})&\text{if }x\in c_{ij}\text{ for }i\in[u],j\in[m_{i}],\\
e_{i}&\text{if }x\in b_{i}\text{ for }i\in[n].\end{cases}

Clearly we may assume that
m_{i}\geq p_{i}
for all
i\in[u]
. Now define an embedding
\psi^{\prime}\colon\mathbf{A}^{v}\to\mathbf{D}
as follows.
Subdivide each
b_{i}
into
q_{i}+1
clopens
b_{ij}
(
0\leq j\leq q_{i}
) with
x_{i}\in b_{i0}
.
Set

\displaystyle p_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}p_{k}+j&\text{if }i\in[u],\ j\in[p_{i}],\\
\sum_{k=1}^{i}p_{k}&\text{if }i\in[u],\ j\in[m_{i}]\setminus[p_{i}],\\
\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle q_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{u}p_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}q_{i}+j\ \text{ if }i\in[n],j\in[q_{i}].

Then define

\psi^{\prime}(a_{1},\dots,a_{v})(x)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{ij}(a_{p_{ij}})&\text{if }x\in c_{ij}\text{ for }i\in[u],j\in[m_{i}],\\
a_{q_{ij}}&\text{if }x\in b_{ij}\text{ for }i\in[n],j\in[q_{i}],\\
e_{i}&\text{if }x\in b_{i0}\text{ for }i\in[n].\end{cases}

It is straightforward to verify that
\psi^{\prime}
is indeed an embedding and that
\psi^{\prime}\varphi=\psi
.

This completes the proof of weak homogeneity of
(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
, and hence of the theorem.
∎

We conclude this section with examples showing that in general

(1)

the class
K
of finite direct powers of a finite algebra
\mathbf{A}
may not have the AP and

(2)

the
K
-homogeneous limit
\mathbf{D}
above may fail to be homogeneous.

Example 4.1
.

It is well-known and not hard to show that the class of finite abelian groups has the AP. In particular, the class of
finite direct powers of any finite abelian group has the AP.

We claim that this does not generalize to
2
-nilpotent groups. Let
\mathbf{A}=D_{8}\times C_{2}
be the direct product
of the dihedral group
D_{8}:=\langle a,b\>:\>a^{4}=b^{2}=1,a^{b}=a^{-1}\rangle
of order
8
and the cyclic group

C_{2}:=\langle-1\rangle
of order
2
.
We define embeddings
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{A}^{2}
by

\varphi_{1}(x,(-1)^{z}):=\bigl((x,1),(a^{2z},1)\bigr),\quad\varphi_{2}(x,(-1)^{z}):=\bigl((x,1),(b^{z},1)\bigr),

for
x\in D_{8},z\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Assume that there exists a group
\mathbf{B}
and embeddings
\psi_{i}\colon\mathbf{A}^{2}\to\mathbf{B}
such that

\psi_{1}\varphi_{1}=\psi_{2}\varphi_{2}
.
Note that
a^{2}=[a,b]
is a commutator and hence
\psi_{1}\varphi_{1}(1,-1)
is an element of the derived subgroup of
\mathbf{B}
.
On the other hand,
\psi_{2}\varphi_{2}(1,-1)
does not commute with
\psi_{2}\bigl((1,1),(a,1)\bigr)
and hence is not in
the center of
\mathbf{B}
. Thus
\mathbf{B}
is not
2
-nilpotent, in particular, not a direct power of
\mathbf{A}
.
So
\{\mathbf{A}^{k}\>:\>k\geq 1\}
does not have the AP.

Example 4.2
.

For a generalized Fraïssé limit that is not homogeneous for arbitrary finite substructures,
let
\mathbf{A}
be the alternating group of degree
6
.
Let
2^{\omega}=b_{1}\cup b_{2}
be a partition of
2^{\omega}
into two non-empty clopens, and let
x_{1}\in b_{1}
.
Consider the two elements
f,g
of the filtered Boolean power
\mathbf{D}=(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{1}}_{1}
defined as follows:

f(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in b_{1}\\
(1\>2\>3)&\text{if }x\in b_{2}\end{cases},\quad g(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in b_{1}\\
(1\>2\>3)(4\>5\>6)&\text{if }x\in b_{2}\end{cases}.

Each of these two elements generates a cyclic subgroup of
\mathbf{D}
of order
3
.
Since
\Aut(\mathbf{A})
is the symmetric group
S_{6}
acting by conjugation,
there is no
\alpha\in\Aut(\mathbf{A})
with
\alpha(1\>2\>3)=(1\>2\>3)(4\>5\>6)
.
By our description of automorphisms from Theorem
3.6
it follows that
there is no automorphism of
\mathbf{D}
which maps the subgroup generated by

to that generated by

.

5.
The variety generated by a simple algebra

We investigate the countable free algebra in the variety
V
generated by a finite simple non-abelian
Mal’cev algebra
\mathbf{A}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(
2
).

First we build explicit representations of finite and countable free algebras in
V
.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
x_{i}\colon A^{\mathbb{N}}\to A,\ a\mapsto a(i)
.
Then the universe of the subalgebra
\mathbf{F}:=\langle x_{i}\>:\>i\in\mathbb{N}\rangle
of

generated by the

forms the clone of term operations on
\mathbf{A}

(see
[
28
, Section 4.1]
). Hence
\mathbf{F}
is free in
V
over
\{x_{i}\>:\>i\in\mathbb{N}\}
(see
[
28
, Section 4.11]

Note that
A^{\mathbb{N}}
with the product topology is homeomorphic to the Cantor space.
Since
x_{i}
for

are continuous functions from
A^{\mathbb{N}}
to
A
, so are all the elements of
F
.
Hence
F
is contained in the Boolean power
\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}}
for
\mathbf{B}
the countable atomless Boolean algebra.

Consider the lexicographic ordering on

, where

is linearly ordered in an arbitrary way.
Let
R
be the transversal of
\Aut\mathbf{A}
orbits on

consisting of lexicographically minimal elements.
It is easy to see that:

•

For every

the restriction

is a set of orbit representatives of
A^{k}
under
\Aut\mathbf{A}
.

•

The restriction
\mathbf{F}|_{R}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{F}
.

We will also need the following explicit description of the free algebra

\mathbf{F}_{k}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\rangle
of rank

as a subalgebra of

, as well as the descriptions of the corresponding free algebras in the variety

generated by the proper subalgebras of
\mathbf{A}
. Let

Then
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{R\setminus S_{k}}
is a finite subdirect power of
\mathbf{A}
, hence isomorphic to a direct power of
\mathbf{A}

by the Foster–Pixley Theorem
(Lemma
2.2
). Clearly
\mathbf{F}_{k}
is a subdirect product of
its projections
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{R\setminus S_{k}}
and
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{S_{k}}
. Since every quotient of the former is isomorphic to a direct power
of
\mathbf{A}
again (cf. Lemma
3.2
) and the latter is in

,
the factors
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{R\setminus S_{k}}

So Fleischer’s Lemma (Lemma
2.1
)
yields that
\mathbf{F}_{k}
is the direct product of its projections to

and
S_{k}
respectively.
For
p\in R|_{[k]}
let

be a basic clopen in
R
, and
note that every element of

is constant on every

.
This in turn implies

F_{k}=\left\{f\in A^{R}\>:\>\begin{array}[]{l}f|_{S_{k}}\in F_{k}|_{S_{k}},\,f\text{ is constant on }c_{p}\\
\text{for all }p\in(R\setminus S_{k})|_{[k]}\end{array}\right\}.

(5.1)

Further
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{S_{k}}
is the greatest homomorphic image of
\mathbf{F}_{k}
that is in
W
.
Thus
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{S_{k}}
is free in
W
over the restrictions of
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
to
S_{k}
.
Let

S:=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}S_{k}=\{a\in R\>:\>F|_{a}\neq A\}.

Since

for every

,
the restriction of
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{S_{k}}
to
S
is an isomorphism.
Hence
\mathbf{F}_{k}|_{S}
is free in
W
over
\{x_{i}|_{S}\>:\>i\in[k]\}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}

and consequently
\mathbf{F}|_{S}
is free in
W
over
\{x_{i}|_{S}\>:\>i\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Since the kernel

of the restriction map of
\mathbf{F}
to

is union of the kernels of the restrictions of
\mathbf{F}_{k}

to
S_{k}
for all

, it follows that

is the smallest congruence such that
\mathbf{F}/\theta\in W
.

So let
e\in F
such that

forms a singleton subalgebra of
\mathbf{F}|_{S}
.
Then
e(S)=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
is a set of idempotents of
\mathbf{A}
containing at least one idempotent from each

-orbit.
The sets

for

partition

. Also let

.

We claim that

is the countable atomless Boolean algebra.
To see this consider an arbitrary basic clopen

.
Let
l>k
and

be such that

.
Then

We may view

(5.2)

The inclusion

For the converse, let

6.

6.1.
SIP for the punctured Cantor space

The proof of Theorem
1.3
(

group of homeomorphisms of the Cantor space

has the SIP
[

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
be distinct, let
G:=(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
,
and let
H\leq G
with
|G:H|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Then there exist
m\geq n
and a partition of
2^{\omega}
into clopen sets

b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
such that
x_{i}\in b_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
and
G_{\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}}\leq H
.

The proof makes up the rest of this subsection.
Throughout, we use the notation and assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, assume
n\geq 1
and let

X^{\circ}:=2^{\omega}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

The proof heavily relies on the corresponding results from
[
32
]
concerning the Cantor space itself (Theorem 3.7)
and
\mathbb{Q}
with a point removed (Theorem 2.7).
We do not reproduce the arguments that are essentially repetitions of those from
[
32
]
but
just point out the necessary adaptations.
Reading the original proof from
[
32
]
verbatim, with
2^{\omega}
or
\mathbb{Q}
replaced by

X^{\circ}
, and keeping in these additional adaptations, constitutes a full proof of each such result.
The arguments that are new to our situation are presented in full.

Following Truss
[
31
,
32
]
we say that a subset
b
of
2^{\omega}

abuts

of
2^{\omega}
if

x\not\in b
and

is closed in
2^{\omega}
.
We will also talk about a sequence
(b_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of subsets of

converging
to a point

all but finitely many members of the sequence are fully contained in the neighborhood.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(b_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pairwise disjoint clopen subsets of

that converges to
x_{1}

such that either (a) each

is clopen in
2^{\omega}
or (b) each
b_{i}
abuts
x_{1}
.

Then
G_{2^{\omega}\setminus b_{i}}\leq H
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(a) This is identical to the argument in the first paragraph of the proof of
[
32
, Theorem 3.7]
.
Here we can use that
G_{2^{\omega}\setminus b_{i}}\cong\Homeo 2^{\omega}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, which is a simple group
by a result of Anderson
[
1
]
.

(b) This is shown by following the proof of
[
32
, Lemma 2.5]
.
This time
G_{2^{\omega}\setminus b_{i}}\cong(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{x_{1}}
for all

, which is not simple.
Instead, by
[
31
, Theorem 3.13]
, the latter has precisely one non-trivial proper normal subgroup

However, this normal subgroup has uncountable index in

, which can be proved by following

[

, p. 499, par. 3]
. So as in
[

, Lemma 2.5]
we can show that

for some

.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

There exists a clopen set
b
of
2^{\omega}
such that the following hold:

•

b\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\{x_{1}\}
; and

•

G_{2^{\omega}\setminus c}\leq H
for every clopen set
c\subseteq b
of
2^{\omega}
not containing
x_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose the statement is not true. Then we can inductively define two sequences of clopen sets
(b_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and

(c_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
2^{\omega}
as follows.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
b_{i}
be a clopen satisfying the following properties:

•

b_{i}\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\{x_{1}\}
,

•

b_{i}
is contained in the ball of radius
1/i
around
x_{1}
with respect to any metric on

which induces the same topology,

•

b_{i}
is disjoint from each of

.

By our assumption we have a clopen

not containing

such that

.
The sequence

consists of pairwise disjoint clopen sets not containing

, which converges to

,
and such that

G_{2^{\omega}\setminus c_{i}}\nleq H
for all

. This contradicts Lemma
6.2
(a).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

There exists a clopen set

of

: thus

Claim 6.5
.

and

are clopen sets of

,

and

abut

This is identical to

∎

.

The proof of Lemma

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

We use induction on

.
The base case for

is Truss’ result
[
32
, Theorem 3.7]
.
So assume

in the following.
By Lemma
6.4
there exists a clopen
b_{1}
in
2^{\omega}
such that
b_{1}\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\{x_{1}\}
and

.
If
b_{1}=2^{\omega}
, then

and there is nothing left to prove. So suppose

.
Since

6.2.
Orbits of clopens in the punctured Cantor space

As above, let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in 2^{\omega}
and

X^{\circ}:=2^{\omega}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.
For any set
b\subseteq 2^{\omega}
we write
\cl(b)
for its
closure
in
2^{\omega}
.
For a clopen
b
in
X^{\circ}
let

\displaystyle I:=\{i\in[n]\>:\>x_{i}\in\cl(b)\},

\displaystyle I^{\prime}:=\{i\in[n]\>:\>x_{i}\in\cl(X^{\circ}\setminus b)\}.

We call the pair
(I,I^{\prime})
the
type
of
b
. Note that
I\cup I^{\prime}=[n]
but that union is not necessarily disjoint.

Let us partition the Cantor space into the disjoint union
2^{\omega}=c_{1}\cup\dots\cup c_{n}
of disjoint clopens with
x_{i}\in c_{i}
for all
i
. For any
i\in[n]
and any
j\in\mathbb{N}
let
c_{ij}
be the intersection of
c_{i}
and the ball of radius
1/j
around
x_{i}
.
The sets
c_{ij}
are clopen, and satisfy
c_{i}=c_{i1}\supseteq c_{i2}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\{x_{i}\}
,
and
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}c_{ij}=x_{i}
for all
i
.
The sets
c_{i,j+1}\setminus c_{ij}
for
i\in[n]
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
are pairwise disjoint, clopen, contained in
X^{\circ}
, and satisfy

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}c_{i,j+1}\setminus c_{ij}=x_{i}
for all
i
.

Now consider again an arbitrary clopen
b
in
X^{\circ}
.
Intersecting
b
with each
c_{i,j+1}\setminus c_{ij}
and disregarding all the empty sets, yields a partition of
b
into the disjoint union of non-empty clopens
b_{ij}
of
2^{\omega}
with
i\in[n]
and
j\in J_{i}
, where
J_{i}
is either empty, or a finite interval
[m_{i}]
or equal to
\mathbb{N}
.
The case where
J_{i}
is finite arises when
b
is disjoint from

c_{i,j+1}\setminus c_{ij}
for sufficiently large
j
, i.e. when
x_{i}\not\in\cl(b)
.
It follows that all
J_{i}
are finite precisely when
b
is closed in
2^{\omega}
, i.e. it is a clopen of
2^{\omega}
.
We can perform a similar analysis on the set
X^{\circ}\setminus b
,
and thus conclude that the following hold:

•

I=\emptyset
if and only if
b
is clopen in
2^{\omega}
;

•

I^{\prime}=\emptyset
if and only if
X^{\circ}\setminus b
is clopen in
2^{\omega}
;

•

I,I^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
if and only if there exist pairwise disjoint clopens
b_{ij}
(
i\in I
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
) and
b_{ij}^{\prime}
(
i\in I^{\prime}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
) such that

\begin{array}[]{rlrl}b&=\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I,\,j\in\mathbb{N}}b_{ij},&\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{ij}&=x_{i}\ (i\in I),\\
X^{\circ}\setminus b&=\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I^{\prime},\,j\in\mathbb{N}}b_{ij}^{\prime},&\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{ij}^{\prime}&=x_{i}\ (i\in I^{\prime}).\end{array}

(6.1)

We can characterize the orbits of clopens in
X^{\circ}
under
(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
as follows.

Lemma 6.8
.

For
I,I^{\prime}\subseteq[n]
with
I\cup I^{\prime}=[n]
, the set of clopens in
X^{\circ}
of type
(I,I^{\prime})
is an orbit under

(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
.

Proof.

Homeomorphisms from
(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
preserve types of clopens because they fix
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
Conversely, consider two clopens
b,c
of the same type
(I,I^{\prime})
.
If
I=\emptyset
then taking the union of a homeomorphism
b\to c
and a homeomorphism
2^{\omega}\setminus b\to 2^{\omega}\setminus c
that fixes
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
yields a homeomorphism
\psi\in(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
with
\psi(b)=c
.
An analogous construction deals with the case
I^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
Consider now the case where
I,I^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Decompose each of
b
and
c
according to (
6.1
);
let

b_{ij}
(
i\in I
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
),
b_{ij}^{\prime}
(
i\in I^{\prime}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
) be the clopens associated with
b
, and let

c_{ij}
(
i\in I
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
),
c_{ij}^{\prime}
(
i\in I^{\prime}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
) be those for
c
.
Take arbitrary homeomorphisms
\psi_{ij}\colon b_{ij}\to c_{ij}
(
i\in I
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
) and

\psi_{ij}^{\prime}\colon b_{ij}^{\prime}\to c_{ij}^{\prime}
(
i\in I^{\prime}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
).
Their union
\psi
extended by
\psi(x_{i})=x_{i}
(
i\in[n]
) is a homeomorphism of
2^{\omega}
because of the limit
conditions in (
6.1
), and clearly
\psi(b)=c
, completing the proof.
∎

6.3.
SIP for the filtered Boolean power

We need one more auxiliary result for the proof of Theorem
1.3
(
2
).

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite simple Mal’cev algebra with an idempotent
e_{1}
,
let
\mathbf{B}
be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, let
x_{1}\in 2^{\omega}
, let
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{1}}_{e_{1}}
,
let
h\colon\Aut\mathbf{D}\to(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{x_{1}}
be as in Theorem
3.6
(
1
), and let

g\colon(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{x_{1}}\to\Aut\mathbf{D}
be as in (
3.3
).

Any subgroup
L
of
K:=\ker h
with
|K:L|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
that is invariant under conjugation by

C:=g((\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{x_{1}})

contains

K^{\prime}:=\{\varphi\in K\>:\>\varphi_{x}\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}\text{ for all }x\in X^{\circ}\}.

Proof.

If
(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
is trivial, then so is
K^{\prime}
and the result clearly holds. So we assume that
(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}

is non-trivial for the rest of the proof.

For
\varphi\in K^{\prime}
, let
\varphi_{*}\colon X^{\circ}\to(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}},\ x\mapsto\varphi_{x}
, be as in
Lemma
3.4
(
2
).
Then
\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
is clopen in
X^{\circ}
for any
\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
by that
lemma.

We will prove the result by showing that

W:=\{\varphi\in K^{\prime}\>:\>x_{1}\in\cl(\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha))\text{ for all }\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}\}

is contained in
L
and generates
K^{\prime}
. Note that
\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset
for any

\varphi\in W
and
\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
, in particular,
\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
is clopen of type
([1],[1])
.

First we claim that

L\cap W\neq\emptyset.

(6.2)

To see this, partition
X^{\circ}
into a sequence of clopens in
2^{\omega}
converging to
x_{1}
.
Then subdivide each of the clopens into
|(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}|
non-empty clopens to obtain a partition of

X^{\circ}
into
|(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}|
disjoint sequences of clopens
b_{i\alpha}
for
i\in\mathbb{N},\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
that converge to
x_{1}
.
For any subset
T\subseteq\mathbb{N}
define
\varphi^{T}\in K^{\prime}
such that

\varphi^{T}_{x}:=\begin{cases}\alpha&\text{if }x\in b_{i\alpha}\text{ and }i\in T,\\
\mathrm{id}_{A}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of
2^{\aleph_{0}}
subsets of
\mathbb{N}
such that
T_{1}\triangle T_{2}
(the symmetric difference)
is infinite for any two distinct
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
.
To see that such
\mathcal{T}
exists, one can consider the equivalence relation on the power-set
2^{\mathbb{N}}
defined by

T_{1}\sim T_{2}\ \Leftrightarrow\ |T_{1}\Delta T_{2}|\text{ is finite},

note that its equivalence classes are all countable, and then let
\mathcal{~T}
be a set of representatives of these classes (using AC).
Since
|K:L|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
, there exist distinct
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
with

\varphi:=\varphi^{T_{1}}(\varphi^{T_{2}})^{-1}\in L
.
We claim that
\varphi\in L\cap W
.
For
i\in T_{1}\setminus T_{2}
and
\alpha\in\Aut\mathbf{A}
we have

\varphi_{x}^{T_{1}}=\alpha
and
\varphi_{x}^{T_{2}}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
for all
x\in b_{i\alpha}
.
Similarly, for
i\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}
and
\alpha\in\Aut\mathbf{A}
we have

\varphi_{x}^{T_{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
and
\varphi_{x}^{T_{2}}=\alpha^{-1}
for all
x\in b_{i\alpha^{-1}}
.
It follows that

\bigcup_{i\in T_{1}\setminus T_{2}}b_{i\alpha}\ \cup\bigcup_{i\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}}b_{i\alpha^{-1}}\subseteq\varphi_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Aut\mathbf{A}.

As
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
, the set
T_{1}\Delta T_{2}
is infinite, and so the set on the left hand side of the above inclusion has
x_{1}
in its closure, implying
\varphi\in W
, and completing the proof of the claim that
\varphi\in L\cap W
.

We show that

C\text{ acts transitively on }W\text{ by conjugation}.

(6.3)

Let
\sigma,\tau\in W
. For each
\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
the clopens

\sigma_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
and
\tau_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)

have type
([1],[1])
.
By Lemma
6.8
there exists
\psi\in(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{x_{1}}
such that

\psi(\sigma_{*}^{-1}(\alpha))=\tau_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
.
Recall that
g(\psi)=\psi^{\mathbf{D}}\in C
and
(\tau^{\psi^{\mathbf{D}}})_{x}=\tau_{\psi(x)}
.
It follows that
\tau^{\psi^{\mathbf{D}}}=\sigma
and (
6.3
) is proved.

Since
L
is normal in
\Aut\mathbf{D}
by assumption, claims (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) imply
W\subseteq L
.
It remains to show that

\langle W\rangle=K^{\prime}.

(6.4)

For
\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
and
c
clopen in
X^{\circ}
, define
\chi^{c,\alpha}\in K
by

\chi^{c,\alpha}_{x}:=\begin{cases}\alpha&\text{if }x\in c,\\
\mathrm{id}_{A}&\text{else.}\end{cases}

To show that
\chi^{c,\alpha}
is generated by
W
we distinguish two cases, depending on the type of
c
.

Case 1:
c
is clopen in
2^{\omega}
.

As in the proof of Claim (
6.2
) we partition
X^{\circ}\setminus c
into
|(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}|
sequences of clopens converging to

x_{1}
. Use these to define
\sigma,\tau\in W
with
c\subseteq\sigma_{*}^{-1}(\alpha)
,

c\subseteq\tau_{*}^{-1}(\mathrm{id})
and
\sigma_{x}=\tau_{x}
for all
x\in X^{\circ}\setminus c
.
Then
\chi^{c,\alpha}=\sigma\tau^{-1}\in\langle W\rangle
.

Case 2:
2^{\omega}\setminus c
is clopen in
2^{\omega}
.

This is analogous to Case 1.

Case 3:
c
is a disjoint union of clopens in
2^{\omega}
converging to
x_{1}
.

This time we partition
c
itself into
|(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}|
disjoint sequences of clopens
b_{i\beta}
on
2^{\omega}
for

i\in\mathbb{N},\beta\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
that converge to
x_{1}
.
Define
\sigma,\tau\in W
by

\sigma_{x}:=\begin{cases}\alpha\beta&\text{if }x\in b_{i\beta},\\
\mathrm{id}_{A}&\text{else},\end{cases}\quad\tau_{x}:=\begin{cases}\beta&\text{if }x\in b_{i\beta},\\
\mathrm{id}_{A}&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Then
\chi^{c,\alpha}=\sigma\tau^{-1}\in\langle W\rangle
.

Since every element in
K^{\prime}
is a product of elements
\chi^{c,\alpha}
for
c
clopen in
X^{\circ}
and

\alpha\in(\Aut\mathbf{A})_{e_{1}}
, it follows that
K^{\prime}
is generated by
W
.
This proves (
6.4
) and the lemma.
∎

Finally we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(
2
).

Let
\mathbf{D}:=(\mathbf{A^{B}})^{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}_{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}}
and let
H
be a subgroup of
\Aut\mathbf{D}
of index
<2^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Let
G:=(\Homeo 2^{\omega})_{\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}}
.
By Corollary

\Aut\mathbf{A}

So, by Theorem
3.6
(
1
) and (

),
\Aut\mathbf{D}
is a semidirect product of
K:=\ker h
and

C:=g(G)
.

Then
|C:H\cap C|<2^{\aleph_{0}}
. By Theorem

we have
m\geq n
and a partition of
2^{\omega}

into clopens

b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
with
x_{i}\in b_{i}
for

C^{\prime}:=\{\psi^{\mathbf{D}}\>:\>\psi\in G,\ \psi(b_{i})=b_{i}\text{ for }i\in[m]\}\leq H\cap C.

For

, we will now restrict to a copy of
\Aut(\mathbf{A}^{\mathbf{B}})^{x_{i}}_{e_{i}}
inside
\Aut\mathbf{D}
. Let

C_{i}:=\{(\psi\cup\mathrm{id}_{X\setminus b_{i}})^{\mathbf{D}}\>:\>\psi\in(\Homeo\,b_{i})_{x_{i}}\}\leq C^{\prime}

and

K_{i}:=\{\varphi\in K\>:\>\varphi_{x}=\mathrm{id}_{A}\text{ for }x\in X^{\circ}\setminus b_{i}\}.

Since

is homeomorphic to

3.6

and the complement

.
Since

H\cap K_{i}
is invariant under conjugation by
C_{i}
.

|K_{i}:H\cap K_{i}|<2^{\aleph_{0}}

Applying Lemma

as isomorphic copy of

It follows that

We complete the proof by showing that
K^{\prime}C^{\prime}\leq H
contains the stabilizer of finitely many elements in

.
Specifically,
for
a
in

, we define

(\Aut\mathbf{D})_{\{f_{a}\>:\>a\in T\}}\leq K^{\prime}C^{\prime}\leq H.

(6.5)

Consider an element in the above stabilizer decomposed as
\varphi\psi^{\mathbf{D}}

for
\varphi\in K
,
\psi\in G
.
We first show that
\psi
stabilizes each set

, so
\psi^{\mathbf{D}}\in C^{\prime}
.
Let

. By (

f_{a}=\varphi\psi^{\mathbf{D}}(f_{a})=\varphi(f_{a}\psi^{-1}).

Evaluating both sides at

and applying Lemma

(

If

and

for

So, for all

.

, contradicting our assumption.

and
\psi^{\mathbf{D}}\in C^{\prime}

Now (

a_{i}=\varphi_{x}(a_{i})\text{ for all }x\in b_{i}\cap X^{\circ},i\in[m].

If

, we obtain

for

.
If

, we obtain

for all

\varphi_{x}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
for all

Claim 7.3

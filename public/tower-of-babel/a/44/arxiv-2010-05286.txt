Restrictions of Sobolev W p 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) -spaces to planar rectifiable curves Thanks: Keywords: Traces, Extensions, Sobolev spaces, Frostman measures, Measures on curves
A. I. Tyulenev

Abstract
We construct explicit examples of Frostman-type measures concentrated on arbitrary simple rectifiable curves Γ ⊂ ℝ 2 \Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} of positive length. Based on such constructions we obtain for each p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) an exact description of the trace space W p 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) | Γ W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})|_{\Gamma} of the first-order Sobolev space W p 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) to an arbitrary simple rectifiable curve Γ \Gamma of positive length.

In fact the methods of

.
Note that this work extended the earlier results by Aronszajn

and Slobodetskii and Babich
[

in the case

,

was covered by Besov in the fundamental paper
[
3
]
.

In the case
p=\infty
, the Sobolev space
W^{1}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be identified with the space

\operatorname{LIP}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and it is known (see McShane-Whitney extension lemma in section 4.1 of
[

the restriction
\operatorname{LIP}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}

coincides with the space
\operatorname{LIP}(S)
of Lipschitz functions on
S
and that, furthermore, the
classical Whitney extension operator linearly and continuously maps the space
\operatorname{LIP}(S)

into the space
\operatorname{LIP}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see e.g.,
[
19
]
, Chapter 6).

In the case

p=1

[

the trace space on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

In the sequel we deal with the case

Recall that given

Summarizing results and methods of papers

Recently Rychkov introduced

In particular, that restrictions did not allow to attack the trace problem for Sobolev spaces

But this is not the case for

-thick sets.
Unfortunately, in this case one has to work with a sequence of measures with prescribed growth conditions instead of the only "nice measure".
Recall
[
22

that given a closed
d
-thick set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a sequence of Radon measures
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}:=\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with the following properties:

(1) for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\operatorname{supp}\mathfrak{m}_{k}=S;

(1.3)

(2) there exists a constant
C^{1}>0
such that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathfrak{m}_{k}(Q(x,r))\leq C^{1}r^{d}\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and every }r\in(0,2^{-k}];

(1.4)

(3) there exists a constant
C^{2}>0
such that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\mathfrak{m}_{k}(Q(x,2^{-k}))\geq C^{2}2^{-dk}\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in S;

(1.5)

(4) for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{k}=w_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{0}
with

and

2^{d-n}w_{k+1}(x)\leq w_{k}(x)\leq w_{k+1}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-a.e.\quad x\in S.

(1.6)

In what follows we call any sequence of Radon measures
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}

d
-regular on

.

We denote by

Finally, given a closed set

Theorem A.

Let
d\in(0,n)
and
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty)
. Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed
d
-thick set with
\mathcal{H}^{n}(S)=0
.
Given a
d
-regular sequence of measures
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}:=\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on

, a measurable function

belongs to the trace space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
if and only if
the following conditions hold:

(1)
for
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-almost every point
x\in S
it holds

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q(x,2^{-k})\cap S}|f(x)-f(z)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(z)=0;

(1.10)

(2)
there exists a number
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
such that (we set
S_{k}(\lambda):=S_{2^{-k}}(\lambda)
)

\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\},p,\lambda_{0}}[f]:=\|f|L_{p}(\mathfrak{m}_{0})\|+\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(d-n)}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda_{0})}\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\bigr)^{p}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(x)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty.

(1.11)

Furthermore, for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]

there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
p,n,d,C^{1}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}},C^{2}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}}
and

\frac{1}{C}\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\},p,\lambda}[f]\leq\|f|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\|\leq C\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\},p,\lambda}[f].

(1.12)

The extension operator
\operatorname{Ext}_{S,\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}}
is a right inverse operator
for the usual trace operator. It maps the trace
space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
to the space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
linearly and continuously.

In fact Theorem
1
is an almost immediate consequence of Theorem 2.1 from
[
22
]
. The only delicate point
which we have to mention is that condition (

) is more rough than the corresponding condition
in

because it appeals to Hausdorff measures instead of capacities.
On the other hand, our definition of the trace space given in this paper also appeals to Hausdorff measures rather than capacities (compare Definition

Hence, a careful analysis of proofs of Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.2 in

together with very well known

In practice the criterion given in Theorem
1
is not so easy to verify by the following reasons:

(1) it is difficult to check a delicate condition (
1.10
);

(2) in fact the typical construction of a
d
-regular sequence of measures
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}
given in
[
22
]

was not fully constructive. Indeed, we used the classical Frostman-type arguments which are based on an inductive algorithm and on the weak limit
procedure. In practice it is not so easy to present explicit expressions for such measures.
Hence, the construction of explicit examples of

-regular sequences of measures
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}
on different closed

-thick sets

is of great importance.

The aim of this paper is to demonstrate that one can overcome the difficulties described in items (1) and (2) above
and simplify Theorem A in the case when
S
is
a planar simple (i.e. without self-intersections) rectifiable curve

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with positive length
. In this case
\Gamma
is a
1
-thick set. We construct
the special

-regular on
\Gamma

\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is that the

have explicit expressions.
Furthermore, having at disposal such measures one can get rid of a sophisticated condition (
1.10
) in the statement of Theorem

1
.
We have to note that results obtained in this paper
are new and could not be obtained by previously known methods.

Indeed, as far as we know
explicit constructions of Frostman-type measures
on arbitrary planar rectifiable curves
\Gamma
were not
considered in the literature before. Furthermore, even in the the case of a planar simple rectifiable curve

S=\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of positive length results formulated in Theorem A could not be obtained by previously known methods.
Indeed, it was unaccessible by methods of
[
15
]
because
in the case of Sobolev spaces the corresponding trace problem was considered only for
d
-thick sets
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the additional
requirement

. On the other hand, there are planar rectifiable curves of positive length that fail to satisfy the Ahlfors-David
1
-regularity
condition (and hence, fail to satisfy the Ahlfors-David

-regularity conditions for all

) and hence do not fall into the scope of

.

Let us informally explain why in the present paper we restrict ourselves to simple rectifiable curves
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

only.

(A) In fact arguments of section 3 work for simple rectifiable curves in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2

with positive length. The only minor problem is that in the case
n>2
the corresponding expressions for measures will be more technical.
On the other hand, what is more important is the application of Theorem 3.1 in section 4 where
restriction to the dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
of the ambient space becomes essential. More precisely, as we have already mentioned any rectifiable curve of positive length
is a
1
-thick subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. But Theorem 4.1 (which is a keystone for the main result) works for
d
-thick sets
with
d\in[n-1,n]
. This obstruction justifies the working in the 2-dimensional plane.

(B) The main reason why we restrict ourselves to the case of curves instead of general
1
-thick sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
that the corresponding expressions for the Frostman-type measures concentrated on general 1-thick sets will be much less transparent. Roughly speaking the main technical
advantage of our construction which works for curves is the reduction of
1
-dimensional Frostman-type measures to the
0
-dimensional Frostman-type
measures.
Indeed, it is well known that any simple planar rectifiable curve
\Gamma

have finite number of intersections with "almost every" line parallel to the coordinate axes. This allows
to built the corresponding
0
-dimensional Frostman-type measure with a help of elementary combinatorial
arguments. Clearly, one can not hope to make a similar trick for general
1
-thick sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
because intersections
of such sets with lines can have a complicated geometry.
Indeed, the general
1
-thick sets can be composed of pieces with different dimensions.

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},2^{-k}\geq t}2^{k}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q(x,2^{-k}))\leq f^{\sharp}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}}(x,t)\leq\sup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},2^{-k}\geq t}2^{k}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q(x,2^{-k})).\end{split}

(2.5)

Proof.

We fix arbitrary
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r\in(0,1]
and set
Q=Q(x,r)
for brevity. The first inequality in (
2.5
) follows form the fact that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q)\leq\fint\limits_{Q}|f(y)-c|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(y)+\fint\limits_{Q}|-f(z)+c|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(z)\\
&\leq 2\fint\limits_{Q}|f(y)-c|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(y).\end{split}

The second inequality in (
2.5
) follows from the estimate

\begin{split}&\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\fint\limits_{Q}|f(y)-c|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(y)\leq\fint\limits_{Q}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q}f(z)\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(y)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{split}

∎

In this paper it will be convenient (in the sense that there are several definitions of Hausdorff measures
in the literature that give the same values up to some universal constants) to follow
[
5
]
(see section 1.2 therein) and define for each
d\in[0,n]
and
\delta\in(0,\infty]

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(E):=\inf\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}(\operatorname{diam}U_{i})^{d},

where the infimum is over all coverings
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
E
with
\operatorname{diam}U_{i}<\delta
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now, the
d
-Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{d}
of the set

E

is defined as
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E):=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(E)
.
By the
d
-Hausdorff content of
E
we mean
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(E)
.

The following proposition is an immediate consequence of (
1.4
) and definition of the
measure
\mathcal{H}^{d}
. We omit an elementary proof.

Proposition 2.2.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed
d
-thick set for some
d\in(0,n]
. Let
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}

be a
d
-regular sequence of measures on
S
. Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the measure
\mathfrak{m}_{k}

is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d}\lfloor_{S}
. Furthermore, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds

\mathfrak{m}_{k}(E)\leq C^{1}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}}\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)\quad\hbox{for any Borel set}\quad E\subset S.

(2.6)

Remark 2.1.
Note that the right hand side of (
2.6
) can be equal
+\infty
and hence the corresponding estimate
is trivial.

Given a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, recall that a map
f:E\to\mathbb{R}
,
E\subset\mathbb{R}
is said to have
the Lusin property

if for any set
E_{0}\subset E
of Lebesgue measure zero the image
f(E_{0})
has Lebesgue measure zero.
The following result is a particular case of Theorem 12 of
[
8
]
(see also Theorem 4.3.3 in
[
12
]
where minor modifications are required). In fact it will be a keystone
for the whole section 3 below.

Proposition 2.3.

Let a map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous, differentiable almost everywhere and has Lusin property.
Let
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]
be a measurable function.
Then for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}

\int\limits_{E}g(x)|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\,dx=\int\limits_{\Phi(E)}\sum\limits_{x\in\Phi^{-1}(y)\cap E}g(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)=\int\limits_{\Phi(E)}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Phi^{-1}(y)\cap E}g(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{0}(x)\Bigr)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y).

(2.7)

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set. We recall that given
\lambda\in(0,1)

and
t\in(0,1]
, the definition of the set
S_{t}(\lambda)
was given in the introduction.
In what follows for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set
S_{k}(\lambda):=S_{2^{-k}}(\lambda)
.
It is natural to ask whether there exists a parameter
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that the union
\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}S_{k}(\lambda)
contains
S
or equivalently

\max_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\chi_{S_{k}(\lambda)}(x)=1
for every
x\in S
?
Unfortunately, this is not the case in general.
In fact in the sequel sets with even a more restrictive properties will be important for us.
More precisely, we introduce the following concept.

Definition 2.1.

Let
d\in(0,n]
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)>0
. We say that
S

is
(d,\lambda)
-quasi-porous
if

\lim_{k\to\infty}\chi_{S_{k}(\lambda)}(x)=1\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{d}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.

(2.8)

Remark 2.2.

It is obvious that if a set
S
is
(d,\lambda)
-quasi-porous for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then it is
(d,\lambda^{\prime})
-quasi-porous
for every
\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,\lambda]
.

The following lemma gives one useful and simple sufficient conditions for a given closed set
S
to be
(d,\lambda)
-quasi-porous.

Lemma 2.1.

Let
d\in(0,n)
. Suppose that a closed set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that:

(1)

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)\in(0,\infty)
;

(2)
there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

\inf\limits_{r\in(0,1]}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q(x,r)\cap S)}{r^{d}}\geq c_{1}\quad\hbox{for every}\quad x\in S.

(2.9)

Then, there exists
\lambda_{0}(S)\in(0,1)
such that
S
is
(d,\lambda)
-quasi-porous for every
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]
.

Proof.

The proof is very close in spirit to the proof of Proposition 9.18 in
[
20
]
. We present the details for the completeness.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)\in(0,\infty)
, it is well known (see for example Theorem 1.3.9 in
[
12
]
) there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
depending only on
d
and there is a set
S^{\prime}\subset S
with

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S\setminus S^{\prime})=0
such that

\varlimsup\limits_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q(x,r)\cap S)}{r^{d}}\leq c_{2}\quad\hbox{for every}\quad x\in S^{\prime}.

(2.10)

Now we fix an arbitrary point
x_{0}\in S^{\prime}
. By (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) there is a
small
r_{0}=r_{0}(x_{0})>0
such that for any
r\in(0,r_{0})
it holds

c_{1}\leq\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q(x_{0},r)\cap S)}{r^{d}}\leq 2c_{2}.

(2.11)

Fix a sufficiently big number
N\in\mathbb{N}
, fix
r\in(0,r_{0}/2)
, subdivide the cube
Q(x_{0},r)

into
2^{Nn}
congruent cubes and choose those of them that have a nonempty intersection with
S
. Let

\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
be the family of all such chosen cubes. For each
i\in\{1,...,M\}
take an arbitrary
point
x_{i}\in Q_{i}\cap S
. Clearly, we have
Q_{i}\subset Q(x_{i},2^{-N+1}r):=Q_{i}^{\ast}
for every
i\in\{1,...,M\}
.
Hence,
S\cap Q(x_{0},r)\subset\cup_{i=1}^{M}Q_{i}^{\ast}
and the multiplicity of
the covering of
Q(x_{0},r)\cap S
by the cubes
Q_{i}^{\ast}
,
i=1,...,M
is bounded above by
5^{n}
. As a result, using
additivity of
\mathcal{H}^{d}
we get

2c_{2}r^{d}\geq\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q(x_{0},r)\cap S)\geq 5^{-n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q_{i}^{\ast}\cap S)\geq 5^{-n}c_{1}M2^{d-Nd}r^{d}.

Hence, this gives

M\leq\Bigl(\frac{2c_{2}}{c_{1}}5^{n}2^{-d}2^{(d-n)N}\Bigr)2^{nN}.

(2.12)

Since
d<n
we can take
N=N(d,n,c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{N}
big enough to deduce existence of at least one cube
Q_{i}\subset Q(x_{0},r)\setminus S
.
Hence, if we set
\lambda_{0}=1/N
we get that
x_{0}\in S_{k}(\lambda_{0})
for every
k>-\log_{2}r+1
. This observation
together with Remark
2.1

completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.3.
Clearly, each Ahlofrs-David
d
-regular set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(with
d\in(0,n)
) satisfies conditions (1) and (2) of Lemma
2.1
.
The converse is false. For example, one can consider a planar rectifiable curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with positive length.

2.2
Sobolev spaces

As usual for each
p\in[1,\infty]
, we let
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the corresponding Sobolev
space of all equivalence classes of real valued functions
F\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whose distributional partial derivatives
D^{\beta}F
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of order
|\beta|\leq 1
belong to
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
This space is normed by

\|F\|_{W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sum\limits_{|\beta|\leq 1}\|D^{\beta}F\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Recall (see e.g.,
[
1
]
, section 6.2) that given a parameter
p\in(1,n]
, for every element
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
there is
a representative

\widehat{F}

in the equivalence class of the element
F
such that
\widehat{F}
has
Lebesgue points everywhere except a set
E_{F}
of
C_{1,p}
-capacity zero. Furthermore, according to imbedding theorem of S. L. Sobolev (see e.g., Theorem 1.2.4 in
[
1
]
), given a parameter
p>n
, for every
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
there is a
continuous representative

\widehat{F}
of
F
.
In the sequel we will call
\widehat{F}
a
good representative
of the element

F
. Recall also (see Theorem 5.1.13 in
[
1
]
) that if
p\in(1,n]
,
d\in(n-p,n]
then for any given set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the condition
C_{1,p}(S)=0
implies
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)=0
. Since in this paper we mainly focus on traces of Sobolev functions to 1-dimensional path-connected sets
in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we can present a little bit more rough definition of the trace of a given Sobolev function than the corresponding one
used in
[
22
]
. More precisely, the later appeals to
C_{1,p}
-capacities instead of the Hausdorff measures. However, we believe that the using of
C_{1,p}
-capacities
is not so reasonable in the present framework. Indeed, Proposition
2.1
together with very well known relations between Hausdorff measures
and capacities shows that knowing of a given trace function everywhere except a set of
C_{1,p}
-capacity zero will be overly detailed for us.
Informally speaking, our trace criterion in Theorem 4.2 is expressed in terms of Frostman-type measures
\mu_{k}[\Gamma]
which "do not
feel" changes of a trace function on a set of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measure zero. These remarks justify the following definition.

Definition 2.2.

Let
d\in(0,n]
,
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty)
and
F\in W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
S
be a Borel set with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)>0
.
We define the trace
F|_{S}
of the element
F
to the set
S
as

F|_{S}:=\{f:S\to\mathbb{R}:f(x)=\widehat{F}(x)\hbox{ for }\mathcal{H}^{d}-\hbox{a.e. }x\in S\}.

(2.13)

We define the trace space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
of the space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}:=\{f:S\to\mathbb{R}:f=F|_{S}\hbox{ for some }F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

(2.14)

and equip it with the usual quotient-space norm, i.e.

\|f|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\|:=\inf\{\|F|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|:f=F|_{S}\}.

(2.15)

We denote by
\operatorname{Tr}|_{S}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
the corresponding
trace operator.

Remark 2.4.
Since the trace
F|_{S}
of a given Sobolev function is a class of equivalent (modulo coincidence on a set of
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-measure zero) functions
f:S\to\mathbb{R}
, the trace operator
is well defined and linear.

2.3
Rectifiable curves in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

By
a curve in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we mean the image
\Gamma
of a continuous map
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

, i.e.
\Gamma=\gamma([a,b])
. The function
\gamma
is
called a parametrization of the curve

\Gamma
.
We say that a curve
\Gamma
is
simple
if the map
\gamma
is injective.
As usual, we say that a curve
\Gamma
is
rectifiable
provided that

l(\Gamma):=\sup\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\gamma(t_{i})-\gamma(t_{i-1})\|<\infty,

(2.16)

where the supremum is taken over all tuples
\{t_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
such that

a=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{n}=b
.
The associated
length function

s_{\gamma}
is defined as

s_{\gamma}(t):=l(\gamma([a,a+t])),\quad t\in[a,b].

The properties summarised in the next proposition
are well known (see e.g.
[
7
]
, ch.5).

Proposition 2.4.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a rectifiable curve and let
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

be its parametrization. Then
\Gamma
admits the
1
-Lipschitz arc length parametrization.
More precisely, there exists the
1
-Lipschitz
map
\gamma_{s}:[0,l(\gamma)]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\gamma_{s}(\tau):=\gamma(s^{-1}_{\gamma}(\tau)),

(2.17)

where

s^{-1}_{\gamma}(\tau):=\sup\{s:s_{\gamma}(s)=\tau\}.

(2.18)

If the curve
\Gamma
is simple, then

l(\Gamma)=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Gamma).

(2.19)

It is also useful to recall the infinitesimal behavior of
\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}
.
The following property is also well known (see e.g. Lemma 3.5 in
[
5
]
)

Proposition 2.5.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a rectifiable curve. Then,

\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}(Q(x,r))}{2r}=1\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Gamma.

(2.20)

Clearly, one could assume everywhere in the sequel that any given curve
\Gamma
has a
1
-Lipschitz parametrization (for example the arc-length parametrization).
However, as we will see in section 5 in practice
it is useful to have a some sort of flexibility in the choice of parameterizations.
This fact justifies the following definition.

Definition 2.3.

Given a curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we say that a map
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

is an admissible parametrization of the curve

\Gamma

if the following conditions hold:

(1) the map
\gamma
is absolutely continuous;

(2)

\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|>0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad t\in[a,b].

(2.21)

In the sequel by
\Pi_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
we denote the
projection maps along
the
i
-th coordinate axes, i.e.
if
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
\Pi_{i}(x):=(x_{1},..,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},..,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Sometimes we will use the shorthand

\widehat{x}^{i}:=\Pi_{i}(x)
,
i=1,...,n
for brevity.
Given a parametrization
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{n}):[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the curve
\Gamma
we let
\gamma_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
denote
its projections to the
i
-th coordinate axes. Similarly, if for some
t_{0}\in[a,b]
there exists
the velocity vector

\dot{\gamma}(t_{0}):=\lim\limits_{t\to t_{0}}\frac{\gamma(t)-\gamma(t_{0})}{t-t_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(2.22)

we let
\dot{\gamma}_{i}(t_{0})
,
i=1,...,n
denote
the corresponding projections.

Definition 2.4.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a simple rectifiable curve and let
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an admissible parametrization of
\Gamma
.
Given
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, we identify
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with a hyperplane orthogonal to the
i
-th coordinate axe and define

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{i}[\Gamma](x^{\prime}):=\Pi_{i}^{-1}(x^{\prime})\cap\Gamma,\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(2.23)

Informally speaking
\mathcal{L}_{i}[\Gamma](x^{\prime})
is just an intersection of the line going through
x^{\prime}
parallel to the
i
-th coordinate axe
with
\Gamma
. We also define the Banach indicatrix functions of
\Gamma
by

\begin{split}&N_{i}[\Gamma](x^{\prime}):=\operatorname{card}\mathcal{L}_{i}[\Gamma](x^{\prime})=\operatorname{card}\{t\in[a,b]|t=(\Pi_{i}\circ\gamma)^{-1}(x^{\prime})\},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.\\
\end{split}

(2.24)

Remark 2.5.

It follows from Theorem 4.3.2 in
[
12
]
that for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
the function
N_{i}[\Gamma]\in L_{1}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
.
Hence,
N_{i}[\Gamma](x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
and almost all
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.
Furthermore, analysis of the proof of Lemma 4.1.4 in
[
12
]
allows to deduce that the functions
N_{i}[\Gamma]
are Borel.

3
Construction of the special
1
-regular sequence of measures.

As we mentioned in the introduction, every path-connected set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
consisting of more than one point is
1
-thick.
In particular, every curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
l(\Gamma)>0
is
1
-thick. Hence, there is a
1
-regular sequence of measures on
\Gamma
.
The aim of this section is a construction for
any planar simple rectifiable curve

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

with positive length
of

a special
1
-regular sequence of measures

\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}
concentrated on
\Gamma
. All constructions can be easily extended to
simple rectifiable curves in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We consider the case
n=2
just for the simplicity.

Let us briefly describe the main idea of the construction.
During the section we will assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma\subset[0,1)\times[0,1)
.
By Remark
2.3
for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}

intersections of the curve
\Gamma
with almost every lines
L^{i}
parallel to the
i
-th coordinate axe consist
of at most finite number of points. Hence, we can easily construct a special
0
-regular sequence of measures concentrated on that finite sets.
After that, taking an arbitrary admissible parametrization
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
\Gamma
and applying Proposition
2.1

we easily obtain 1-regular sequence of measures concentrated on
\Gamma
.

Let
I_{0}:=[0,1)
be a half-open unit interval. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let

\mathcal{D}_{k}:=\Bigl\{I_{k,m}:=\Bigl[\frac{m}{2^{k}},\frac{m+1}{2^{k}}\Bigr),m=0,...,2^{k}-1\Bigr\}

be the family of all dyadic half-open intervals with side length
2^{-k}
contained in
I_{0}
.

Definition 3.1.
Given a set of distinct points
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\subset I_{0}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
\operatorname{P}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N})
be the set of all probability
measures with the support
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
. In other words,
\nu\in\operatorname{P}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N})
if and only if
there exists a
density function

\alpha:\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha(x_{i})=1
such that

\nu=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\alpha(x_{i})\delta_{x_{i}},

(3.1)

where
\delta_{x_{i}}
,
i=1,...,N
are the Dirac measures concentrated at the points
x_{i}
,
i=0,...,N
.

Definition 3.2.

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\subset I_{0}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}

be an arbitrary finite set of distinct points. Let
k^{\ast}:=k^{\ast}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N})
be the minimal among all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for each of which the map sending
every point
x_{i}
to the unique dyadic interval
I_{k,m}(x_{i})\ni x_{i}
is injective.
For every
k\geq k^{\ast}
we define the family

\mathcal{F}_{k}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}):=\Bigl\{I_{k,m}:I_{k,m}\cap\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}\neq\emptyset\Bigr\}.

(3.2)

For each
i\in\{0,...,N\}
and any
k\geq k^{\ast}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N})
we denote by
I_{k,m}(x_{i})
the unique dyadic interval
in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N})
containing the point
x_{i}
.

Given an arbitrary tree
\mathcal{T}
with a root
r
we introduce the intrinsic metric
\rho
on
\mathcal{T}
making it the so-called metric tree

(\mathcal{T},\rho)
.
More precisely, given two vertices
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})
joined by some edge
e
we put
\rho(\xi,\xi^{\prime})=1
. For generic two
vertices
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})
we define

\rho(\xi,\xi^{\prime})=\inf\sum_{i}\rho(\xi_{i},\xi_{i-1})
, where the infimum is taken over all paths

\xi=:\xi_{0}\leftrightarrow....\leftrightarrow\xi_{l}:=\xi^{\prime}
joining
\xi
with
\xi^{\prime}
.
Given
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
\mathcal{V}^{i}(\mathcal{T}):=\{\xi\in\mathcal{T}:\rho(r,\xi)=i\}
.
If
\mathcal{V}^{i}(\mathcal{T})\neq\emptyset
then for any
\xi\in\mathcal{V}^{i}(\mathcal{T})
we denote by
n(\xi)

the number of edges joining
\xi
with the corresponding vertices in
\mathcal{V}^{i+1}(\mathcal{T})
.

Definition 3.3.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Given a nonempty family of dyadic intervals
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subset\mathcal{D}_{k}
, we define
the tree
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}_{k})
as the metric tree whose vertices
\mathcal{V}^{i}(\mathcal{T})
,
i=0,...,k

are naturally corresponds to all those dyadic intervals in
\mathcal{D}_{i}

Definition 3.7.

Let
\Gamma\subset[0,1)\times[0,1)
be a simple rectifiable curve with positive length. Let

be an admissible parametrization of

.
We say that the sequence of measures
\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}:=\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

is a special sequence of measures on

\Gamma

\mu_{k}[\Gamma]:=\gamma_{\sharp}(\operatorname{D}^{k}[\gamma]\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{[a,b]})\quad\hbox{for every}\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.12)

We are going to show that the measures
\mu_{k}[\Gamma]
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are well defined.
For that purpose we recall the following property. Probably it looks like a folklore but we present the proof for the completeness.

Proposition 3.2.

Let
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
,
[A,B]\subset\mathbb{R}
and
g:[a,b]\to[A,B]
be absolutely continuous. Assume that

\frac{dg}{dt}(t)>0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-a.e.\quad t\in[a,b].

(3.13)

Then the inverse function
g^{-1}:[A,B]\to[a,b]
is absolutely continuous.

Proof.

From (
3.13
) it follows that
g:[a,b]\to[A,B]
is strictly increasing.
Therefore, there exists the inverse function
g^{-1}:[A,B]\to[a,b]
which is continuous and strictly increasing. Hence, to prove that

g^{-1}:[A,B]\to[a,b]
is absolutely continuous it is sufficient to check the Lusin property.
Assume the contrary. Hence, there exists a set
E\subset[A,B]
with
\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)=0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(g^{-1}(E))>0
.
Combing this with (
3.13
) we get

r_{0}:=\int\limits_{g^{-1}(E)}\frac{dg}{dt}(t)dt>0.

(3.14)

Let
\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an arbitrary sequence of nonempty intervals such that
E\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}(A_{i},B_{i})
.
Since
g^{-1}
is strictly increasing we have a sequence of nonempty intervals
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
(a_{i},b_{i}):=g^{-1}((A_{i},B_{i}))
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly
g^{-1}(E)\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}(a_{i},b_{i})
.
Since
g
is absolutely continuous we can apply Newton-Leibniz formula and take into account (
3.14
). As a result, we have

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}|A_{i}-B_{i}|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\int\limits_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}\frac{dg}{dt}(t)dt\geq r_{0}.

(3.15)

The sequence
\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
was chosen arbitrarily. Hence, by (
3.15
) and the definition of the measure
\mathcal{H}^{1}
we conclude
that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)\geq r_{0}>0
.
This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3.

\Gamma\subset[0,1)\times[0,1)

Proof.

Since the parameterizations

&\dot{s}_{\gamma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})=\|\dot{\gamma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\|>0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad t^{\prime}\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}].\end{split}

Hence, there are continuous strictly increasing functions

not only the functions

2.1

Lemma 4.1.

Let
\Gamma\subset[0,1)\times[0,1)
be a simple rectifiable curve of positive length. Let
\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}
be the special
1
-regular sequence of measures on
\Gamma
. Let
\mu_{k}[\Gamma]=w_{k}[\Gamma]\mu_{0}[\Gamma]
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,

\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{w_{k}[\Gamma](x)}\fint\limits_{Q(x,2^{-k})}w_{k}[\Gamma](y)\,d\mu_{0}[\Gamma](y)<+\infty\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-a.e.\quad x\in\Gamma.

(4.1)

Proof.

Due to Proposition
3
we may assume that
\Gamma

is parameterized by the arc length
\gamma_{s}:[0,l(\Gamma)]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}
is a Radon measure, by Theorem
3
and Proposition
2.1

there exists a function
g[\Gamma]\in L_{1}(\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma})

such that for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e. point
x\in\Gamma
it holds (note that
w_{0}[\Gamma]\equiv 1
)

g[\Gamma](x)=\frac{d\mu_{0}[\Gamma]}{d\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}}(x)\leq C^{1}_{\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}}.

(4.2)

Step 2.
At this step we are going to show that

g[\Gamma](x)>0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Gamma.

(4.3)

By Proposition
2.3
it follows from (
4.2
) that in order to prove (
4.3
) it is sufficient to establish

\varliminf\limits_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu_{0}[\Gamma](Q(x,r))}{r}>0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Gamma.

(4.4)

Since the set
\Gamma
is connected and the map
\gamma_{s}
is the arc-length parametrisation of
\Gamma

we get for each point
x\in\Gamma
and any
r<\frac{\operatorname{diam}\Gamma}{3}

that the preimage
\gamma^{-1}_{s}(Q(x,r))

contains a closed interval
[t^{1}_{x}(r),t^{2}_{x}(r)]\ni\gamma^{-1}_{s}(x)
such that:

(1)
\gamma_{s}(t^{1}_{x}(r))=x
,
\gamma_{s}(t^{2}_{x}(r))\in\partial Q(x,r)
;

(2) it holds

|t^{1}_{x}(r)-t^{2}_{x}(r)|\geq r.

(4.5)

But then, since
\gamma_{s}
is the arc-length parametrization, taking into account Proposition
2.3
we obtain

|t^{1}_{x}(r)-t^{2}_{x}(r)|\leq l(\Gamma\cap Q(x,r))\to 0,\quad r\to 0.

(4.6)

Combining (
3.12
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) and taking into account that
\gamma_{s}
has the Lusin property (because
\gamma_{s}
is

\begin{split}&\varliminf\limits_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu_{0}[\Gamma](Q(x,r))}{r}\\
&\geq\varliminf\limits_{r\to 0}\fint\limits_{t^{1}_{x}(r)}^{t^{2}_{x}(r)}\chi_{[0,l(\Gamma)]}(\tau)\operatorname{D}^{0}[\gamma_{s}](\tau)\,d\tau=\operatorname{D}^{0}[\gamma_{s}](\gamma^{-1}_{s}(x))>0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Gamma.\end{split}

(4.7)

Step 3.

Using (
4.3
) we deduce that the measure
\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}
is
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{0}[\Gamma]
. Furthermore,
w_{0}[\Gamma](x):=1
for all
x\in\Gamma
by
Theorem
3
and definition of a
d
-regular sequence of measures given in the introduction. As a result, by (
3.21
) we have

w_{k}[\Gamma](x)\geq w_{0}[\Gamma](x)=1\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Gamma.

(4.8)

According to Theorem
3
the sequence of measures
\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}
is
1
-regular on
\Gamma
.
Hence, we can apply Proposition
2.1
with
d=1
and then use (
4.3
), (
4.8
). We get the desirable

\begin{split}&\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{w_{k}[\Gamma](x)}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x)}w_{k}[\Gamma](y)\,d\mu_{0}[\Gamma](y)\\
&=\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{w_{k}[\Gamma](x)}\frac{\mu_{k}[\Gamma](Q_{k}(x))}{\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}(Q_{k}(x))}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}\lfloor_{\Gamma}(Q_{k}(x))}{\mu_{0}[\Gamma](Q_{k}(x))}\\
&\leq\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(\frac{1}{w_{k}[\Gamma](x)}\frac{C^{1}_{\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}}}{g(x)}\Bigr)\leq\frac{C^{1}_{\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\}}}{g(x)}<+\infty\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Gamma.\end{split}

(4.9)

Theorem 4.1.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
,
d\in[n-1,n]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed
d
-thick set. Let
\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}

be a
d
-regular sequence of measures on
S
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(1)
the set
S
is
(d,\lambda)
-quasi-porous;

(2)
for
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-almost every
x\in S

\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{w_{k}(x)}\fint\limits_{Q(x,2^{-k})}w_{k}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(y)<+\infty.

(4.10)

Then, the condition
\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\},p,\lambda}[f]<+\infty
implies

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q(x,2^{-k})}|f(x)-f(y)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(y)=0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.

(4.11)

Proof.

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1.

Using Proposition
2.1
we get for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

2^{k(p-(n-d))}\Bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x))\Bigr)^{p}\leq 2^{k(d-n)}\Bigl(2f^{\sharp}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in S.

(4.12)

Step 2.
By B. Levi theorem we deduce from (
4.12
) and (
1.11
)

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{S}\Bigl[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(p-(n-d))}\chi_{S_{k}(\lambda)}(x)\Bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x))\Bigr)^{p}w_{k}(x)\Bigr]\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(x)\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(p-(n-d))}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\Bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x))\Bigr)^{p}w_{k}(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(x)\leq 2^{p}\Bigl(\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\},p,\lambda}[f]\Bigr)^{p}.\end{split}

(4.13)

Step 3.
Since the set
S
is
(d,\lambda)
-quasi-porous and
\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\},p,\lambda}[f]<+\infty

(

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(p-(n-d))}w_{k}(x)\Bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x))\Bigr)^{p}<+\infty\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.

(4.14)

In particular, this gives

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}2^{k(p-(n-d))}w_{k}(x)\Bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x))\Bigr)^{p}=0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.

(4.15)

Since
p>1
,
d\in[n-1,n]
and since
w_{k}(x)\leq 2^{k(n-d)}
for
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-a.e.
x\in S
we obtain

w^{p}_{k}(x)\leq 2^{k(p-1)(n-d)}w_{k}(x)\leq 2^{k(p-(n-d))}w_{k}(x)\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.

(4.16)

As a result, combination of (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) gives

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(w_{k}(x)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x))\Bigr)^{p}=0\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.

(4.17)

Step 4.
Clearly, the condition
\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mathfrak{m}_{k}\}p,\lambda}[f]<+\infty
implies
f\in L_{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{0})
. Hence,
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-almost every point

is a Lebesgue
point of the function

with respect to the measure

. This gives

\begin{split}&\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}|f(x_{0})-f(x)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(x)\leq\\
&\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}|f(x_{0})-f(y)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(y)+\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}|f(y)-f(x)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(y)d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(x)\\
&\leq\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}|f(y)-f(x)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(y)d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(x)\quad\hbox{for}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x\in S.\end{split}

(4.18)

Step 5.

Since
w_{k}(x)\geq 1
for
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
-a.e.
x\in S
and since
\operatorname{supp}\mathfrak{m}_{k}=S
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have by (
4.10
) and (
4.17
)

\begin{split}&\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}|f(y)-f(x)|\,d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(y)\\
&\leq\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{k}(Q_{k}(x_{0}))}{w_{k}(x_{0})\mathfrak{m}_{0}(Q_{k}(x_{0}))}\frac{w_{k}(x_{0})}{\mathfrak{m}_{k}(Q_{k}(x_{0}))}\int\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}\fint\limits_{Q_{k}(x_{0})}|f(y)-f(x)|d\mathfrak{m}_{k}(x)w_{k}(y)\,d\mathfrak{m}_{0}(y)\\
&\leq\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{k}(Q_{k}(x_{0}))}{w_{k}(x_{0})\mathfrak{m}_{0}(Q_{k}(x_{0}))}\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}w_{k}(x_{0})\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{k}}[f](Q_{k}(x_{0}))=0,\quad\mathfrak{m}_{0}-\hbox{a.e.}\quad x_{0}\in S.\end{split}

(4.19)

Combining (
4.18
) with (
4.19
) we get (
4.11
) and complete the proof.

Theorem 4.2.

Let
\Gamma\subset[0,1)\times[0,1)
be a simple rectifiable curve of positive length. Let
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

be an admissible parametrization of
\Gamma

-regular sequence of measures

\lambda_{0}:=\lambda_{0}(\Gamma)\in(0,1]

\begin{split}&\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mu_{k}\},p,\lambda_{0}}[f]:=\|f|L_{p}(\mu_{0})\|+\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\int\limits_{\Gamma_{k}(\lambda_{0})}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.\end{split}

(4.21)

Furthermore, for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]

there exists a constant

depending only on
p
,
\lambda
,
C^{1}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}
,

such that

(4.22)

and the operator
\operatorname{Ext}_{\Gamma}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})|_{\Gamma}\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined in
(

) is linear, bounded and
\operatorname{Tr}|_{\Gamma}\circ\operatorname{Ext}_{\Gamma}=\operatorname{Id}

on the space

.

Proof.

Since

By Proposition

5
Example

Note that despite the fact that for a given simple rectifiable curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with positive length
the measures
\mu_{k}[\Gamma]
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
constructed above
have explicit expressions, still a generic simple rectifiable curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be extremely complicated and
functions
\operatorname{D}^{k}[\gamma]
can oscillate wildly in general. As a result, given a
function
f\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})|_{\Gamma}
computations of
the norm
\mathcal{BN}_{\{\mu_{k}[\Gamma]\},p,\lambda}[f]
can be problematic in practice.

Below we present an illustrative example,
which on the one hand is quite simple for computations, on the other hand it exhibits the typical
effects when oscillations of a given curve
\Gamma
affect to the behavior of densities
\operatorname{D}^{k}[\gamma]

(for admissible parameterizations
\gamma
).

We restrict ourselves to the case when
\Gamma
is a graph of some locally Lipschitz nonnegative function. More precisely, we assume that
\gamma_{1}=\operatorname{id}
on
[0,1]
and
\gamma_{2}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a locally Lipschitz (i.e.
\gamma_{2}|_{[a,b]}
is Lipschitz for each
[a,b]\subset(0,1)
) function. We set
\Gamma:=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}\in[0,1],x_{2}=\gamma_{2}(x_{1})\}
. Note that in order to make our example
interesting our curve
\Gamma
should satisfy the following requirements:

a) the parametrization
\gamma
is not (globally) Lipschitzian because otherwise we fall into the scope of
[
6
]
;

b) the graph
\Gamma
fails to satisfy the Ahlfors-David 1-regularity condition
because otherwise we fall into the scope of
[
9
]
. Hence,
\gamma_{2}

should oscillate strongly.

Firstly we define

\zeta(t):=\begin{cases}&2t,\quad t\in[0,2^{-1}],\\
&2-2t,\quad t\in(2^{-1},1]\\
&0,\quad t\notin[0,1].\end{cases}

(5.1)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we set

\psi_{k}(t):=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta(2^{k}(t-\frac{m}{2^{k}})),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.2)

Let
\{c_{k}\}=\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,1)
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers and let
\{n_{k}\}=\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of nonnegative integer numbers
such that
n_{k}>k
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We define

\begin{cases}&\gamma_{1}(t)=t,\quad t\in[0,1];\\
&\gamma_{2}(t):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\chi_{[2^{-k},2^{-k+1})}(t)\psi_{n_{k}}(t),\quad t\in[0,1].\end{cases}

(5.3)

In other words, the graph
\Gamma
looks like a sequence of triangles. The amount of the congruent triangles on the interval
[2^{-k},2^{-k+1})

equals
2^{n_{k}-k}
and the height of every such triangle equals
c_{k}
.

Since
\gamma_{2}
is a continuous function, the map
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):[0,1]\to[0,1)\times[0,1)
gives a parametrization
of the simple planar curve. Clearly,
\gamma_{2}
is
locally Lipschitz
(i.e.
\gamma_{2}
is Lipschitz on any closed interval
[a,1]

with
a\in(0,1]
).
It is easy to see that the sequences
\{c_{k}\}
and
\{n_{k}\}
can be chosen in such a way that:

(1)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}2^{n_{k}-k}<\infty;

(5.4)

(2)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}c_{k}2^{n_{k}}=+\infty.

(5.5)

As a typical example one can take
c_{k}:=k^{-\alpha}2^{k-n_{k}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\alpha>1
.

From now we assume that our planar curve
\Gamma
satisfies (
5.4
)–(
5.5
).
The condition (
5.4
) is necessary and sufficient for the
rectifiability
of
\Gamma
.
This fact together with local Lipschitz property of
\gamma_{2}
implies that the parametrization

\gamma

is admissible
.
From (
5.5
) it follows that

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}2^{j}\sum\limits_{k=j}^{\infty}c_{k}2^{n_{k}-k}=+\infty.

(5.6)

Clearly (
5.6
) leads to the distortion of the second inequality in (
1.1
) with
d=1
and
S=\Gamma

(the first inequality in (
1.1
) always holds true with
S=\Gamma
and
d=1
). Hence, our curve is
locally Lipschitz and
fails to satisfy the Ahlfors-David 1-regularity condition.

Finally, (
5.5
) implies that
\gamma_{2}
is
not globally Lipschitz.

Hence, the exact description of the trace space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})|_{\Gamma}

[

in the case when either the side length

is comparable

It remains to describe porous subsets of

For each

\begin{split}&E^{l}_{k}(\lambda):=\Bigl(\gamma_{2}^{-1}((c_{l}-\frac{1-\lambda}{2^{k}},c_{l}])\cap[\frac{1}{2^{l}},\frac{2}{2^{l}})\Bigr)\bigcup\Bigl(\gamma_{2}^{-1}([0,\frac{1-\lambda}{2^{k}}))\cap[\frac{1}{2^{l}},\frac{2}{2^{l}})\Bigr)\\
&\bigcup\Bigl(\gamma_{2}^{-1}((c_{l+1}-\frac{1-\lambda}{2^{k}},c_{l}])\cap[\frac{1}{2^{l}},\frac{1}{2^{l}}+\frac{1-\lambda}{2^{k}})\Bigr)\\
&\bigcup\Bigl(\gamma_{2}^{-1}((c_{l-1}-\frac{1-\lambda}{2^{k}},c_{l}])\cap(\frac{2}{2^{l}}-\frac{1-\lambda}{2^{k}},\frac{2}{2^{l}}])\Bigr).\end{split}

(5.12)

Now for every

and

(5.13)

\Gamma_{k}(\lambda)=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in E_{k}(\lambda),x_{2}=\gamma_{2}(x_{1})\}.

Excitonic Chern insulator and kinetic ferromagnetism in MoTe 2 /WSe 2 moiré bilayer
Zhihuan Dong 1, Ya-Hui Zhang 2

Abstract
We propose a new mechanism for the quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) effect in the AB stacked MoTe 2 /WSe 2 system. Based on the observation that the inter-layer tunneling is suppressed in the AB stacking, we consider a model with two layers coupled through the Coulomb interaction. The moiré lattices of the two layers are shifted to form a honeycomb lattice. Initially, the system is in a layer-polarized Mott insulator at ν T = 1 \nu_{T}=1 . But with a displacement field, an equal number of holes and electrons are doped into the two layers respectively, forming inter-layer exciton condensation. Through mean field theory, we find p ± i ​ p p\pm ip exciton condensation in a certain parameter regime, which leads to a Chern insulator with Chern number C = ± 1 C=\pm 1 . The valleys are polarized due to the kinetic energy instead of interaction. But the polarization in the two layers can be either the same or opposite. Especially, an inter-valley-coherent (IVC) Chern insulator phase is possible, in agreement with the recent magnetic circular dichroism (MCD) measurement. Our work opens a new direction to search for a topologically non-trivial excitonic insulator with high angular momentum exciton pairing symmetry.

Kinetic magnetism and symmetry selection

The ECI phase in the above is fully valley polarized in both layers. Unlike the quantum Hall ferromagnetism, there is no Hund’s coupling or band topology in our model. Instead, the ferromagnetism is kinetically driven: with the valley polarization, the hopping of the holon
f_{i}
is larger, favored by the kinetic energy. This is similar to the celebrated Nagaoka ferromagnetism
Nagaoka 1966
in a hole-doped Mott insulator.

J_{B}
term can lead to an excitonic Chern insulator with canted spin texture and only partial valley polarization. This may be one explanation for the non-saturation of the MCD at a small magnetic field in the recent experiment
Tao et al. 2022
. However, we note that partial valley polarization can also be trivially explained by domain wall formation driven by disorder effects.

Summary
In conclusion we propose a new mechanism for the QAH effect in the Coulomb coupled moire bilayer and apply it to the MoTe
2
/WSe
2
system. In our model, the inter-layer hopping is spontaneously generated from inter-layer exciton condensation, which can have
p\pm ip
symmetry and lead to an excitonic Chern insulator (ECI). The ECI phase has valley ferromagnetism arising from kinetic energy, in contrast to the quantum Hall ferromagnetism picture for the QAH effect in graphene moiré systems
Zhang et al. 2019a
;
Repellin et al. 2020
. The valley polarizations in the two layers can be the same or opposite, depending on small perturbations away from a symmetric point where they are degenerate. Our theory opens a new direction to search for interaction-driven topological phases without single-particle band topology, for example in moiré bilayer with an hBN barrier in the middle. Excitonic insulators with
p\pm ip
pairing symmetry may also be realized in other systems outside of the moiré materials.

Details on Hartree-Fock calculation of the Hubbard model

We start with the Hubbard model on the honeycomb lattice:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=-t_{AB}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)+D\sum_{i\in A}n_{i}-D\sum_{i\in B}n_{i}

\displaystyle-t_{A}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{A}}(e^{i\phi^{A}_{ij}\tau_{z}}c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)-t_{B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}(e^{i\phi^{B}_{ij}\tau_{z}}c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)

\displaystyle+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i}n_{i}(n_{i}-1)+V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}n_{i}n_{j}+V^{\prime}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}n_{i}n_{j}

(6)

We can decouple the interaction part through mean-field ansatz:

\displaystyle H_{HF}

\displaystyle=-t_{AB}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)+D\sum_{i}(-1)^{\sigma_{z}(i)}n_{i}

\displaystyle-t_{A}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{A}}(e^{i\phi^{A}_{ij}\tau_{z}}c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)-t_{B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}(e^{i\phi^{B}_{ij}\tau_{z}}c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)

\displaystyle+U\sum_{i}(\rho_{i}c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{i,\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}^{2})-U\sum_{i}(\tau^{\mu}_{i}c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}\tau^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i,\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{i}^{\mu}\tau_{i}^{\mu})

\displaystyle+2V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(\rho_{j}c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{i,\alpha}-\chi_{ji,\beta\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{j,\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\frac{1}{2}|\chi_{ij,\alpha\beta}|^{2})

\displaystyle+2V^{\prime}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}\rho_{j}c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{i,\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}\rho_{j}

with self-consistent equations:

\displaystyle\chi_{ij,\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\langle c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{j,\beta}\rangle\;\mathrm{for\;}i,j\in\mathrm{NN}

\displaystyle\rho_{i}

\displaystyle=\langle c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{j,\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle\tau^{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle=\langle c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}\tau^{\mu}_{\alpha,\beta}c_{j,\beta}\rangle

Appendix B
Details on Schwinger boson calculation of the t-J model

In the limit of large
U
, the Hubbard model is approximated by a
t-J
model

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=-t_{A}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{A}}(e^{i\phi^{A}_{ij}\tau_{z}}c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)+D\sum_{i\in A}n_{i}

\displaystyle~~~-t_{B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}(e^{i\phi^{B}_{ij}\tau_{z}}Pc^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}P+h.c.)-D\sum_{i\in B}n_{i}

\displaystyle~~~+J_{B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}\tau^{\mu}_{i}R^{\mu\nu}_{z}(2\phi^{B}_{ij})\tau^{\nu}_{j}+(V^{\prime}-\frac{J_{B}}{4})\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}n_{i}n_{j}

\displaystyle~~~+V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}n_{i}n_{j}+V^{\prime}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{A}}n_{i}n_{j}

(9)

where
J=\frac{4t^{2}}{U}
, and
R^{\mu\nu}_{z}
is the matrix representing rotation around
z
axis, namely,
R(\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos{\phi}&\sin{\phi}&0\\
-\sin{\phi}&\cos{\phi}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
in the basis of
(\tau_{x},\tau_{y},\tau_{z})
. Note that since
A
layer has a low density
x
, the double-occupancy projection
P
is not expected to be relevant. So we have treated the A layer as free fermions instead of using the composite degrees of freedom. In the calculation, we will set the magnetization in the A layer to be polarized along
\tau^{z}
direction, which is found to hold throughout the Hartree-Fock phase diagram.

We use a fermionic holon, bosonic spinon construction for the B layer to take care of the projected operator:
c_{i\alpha}=b_{i\alpha}f_{i}
, with a constraint
n^{f}_{i}=n^{b}_{i}
.
b_{i;\alpha}
will be assumed to be condensed and just represents a magnetic order. This is just a convenient way to deal with the charge degree of freedom moving in the background of magnetic order. No exotic phase with fractionalization is targeted in our calculation. The Hamiltonian in Eq.
2
is rewritten as

H=H_{t}+H_{D}+H_{J}+H_{V}

(10)

where

\begin{split}H_{t}&=-t_{A}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{A}}(e^{i\phi^{A}_{ij}\tau_{z}}c^{\dagger}_{i;\alpha}c_{j;\alpha}+h.c.)+D\sum_{i\in A}n_{i}\\
&~~~-t_{B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}(e^{i\phi^{B}_{ij}\tau_{z}}b^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}b_{j\alpha}f^{\dagger}_{i}f_{j}+h.c.)\\
H_{D}&=D\sum_{i\in A}f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}-D\sum_{i\in B}f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}\\
H_{J}&=J\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}b^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}b_{i\beta}R^{\mu\nu}_{z}(\phi_{ij})b^{\dagger}_{j\gamma}\sigma^{\nu}_{\gamma\delta}b_{j\delta}\\
H_{V}&=V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}f^{\dagger}_{i}f_{i}f^{\dagger}_{j}f_{j}+V^{\prime}\sum_{a=A,B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{a}}n_{i}n_{j}\end{split}

The exciton insulators found in Hartree-Fock always have
\tau_{z}
polarization in the A layer. But the magnetic order of the B layer could be either
120^{\circ}
order or
\tau_{z}
polarized FM order. To study the competition between the two magnetic orders of the B phase, we adopt the following ansatz for the Schwinger boson part

\begin{pmatrix}\langle b_{i;+}\rangle\\
\langle b_{i;-}\rangle\end{pmatrix}=F_{i}

(11)

with
F^{A}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
,
F^{B}_{i}=\sqrt{n_{B}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
for the FM order. or
F^{B}_{i}=\sqrt{n_{B}}e^{\frac{i}{2}(\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{i})\tau_{z}}\begin{pmatrix}\cos{\frac{\theta}{2}}\\
\sin{\frac{\theta}{2}}\end{pmatrix}
for the canted AFM order, where
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{K}_{M}\ \mathrm{or}-\mathbf{K}_{M}
.

After the magnetic orders are decided for both layers, the charge degree of freedom is captured by a spinless model. We also label the valley polarized operator
c_{i;+}
in the A layer as
f_{i}
with
i\in A
. The kinetic term is now

\displaystyle H_{t}^{f}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a=A,B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{a}}t^{f}_{ij;a}f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{j}+D\sum_{i\in A}f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}-D\sum_{i\in B}f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}

(12)

where
t^{f}_{ij;a}=t_{a}(F_{i}^{a\dagger}e^{i\phi_{ij;a}\tau_{z}}F^{a}_{j})
.
n_{i}=f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{i}
is constrained to be the physical density. The total filling is
n_{A}+n_{B}=1
so there are electron and hole pockets in the two layers respectively.

The
H_{J}
term simply contributes energy for each magnetic pattern:

H_{J}=J\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{B}}n_{B}\bigg(\cos^{2}{\theta}+\sin^{2}{\theta}\cos{(2\phi_{ij}+\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{ij})}\bigg)

(13)

where
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{\Gamma}_{M},\mathbf{K}_{M}\;\mathrm{or}-\mathbf{K}_{M}
. Here

is the angle between
z

\vec{\tau}_{B}
, and
\phi
is the valley-contrasting hopping phase.
H_{V}^{MF}

H_{V}=-V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\chi_{ji}f^{\dagger}_{i}f_{j}+h.c.-|\chi_{ij}|^{2}

(14)

with self-consistent equation

\chi_{ij}=\langle f_{i}^{\dagger}f_{j}\rangle

(15)

The mean field energy is

\displaystyle H_{MF}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{a=A,B}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle_{a}}t_{a}(F_{i}^{a\dagger}e^{i\phi_{ij;a}\tau_{z}}F^{a}_{j})\chi_{ij}+h.c.+D\sum_{i\in A}\langle n_{i}\rangle-D\sum_{j\in B}\langle n_{j}\rangle

(16)

\displaystyle-V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}|\chi_{ij}|^{2}+J\sum_{\langle\langle jj^{\prime}\rangle\rangle_{B}}n_{B}\bigg(\cos^{2}{\theta}+\sin^{2}{\theta}\cos{(2\phi_{jj^{\prime}}+\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{jj^{\prime}})}\bigg)

AFM in the B layer and assume
\tau_{z}
FM in the A layer, after that for each magnetic order pattern we deal with

term with

in Eq.

term and compare their total energies. The phase diagram is shown in the main text.

Appendix D
Competition between the
s

In this section, we discuss why the
p

The nearest neighbor interaction is decoupled into

H_{V}=-V\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\chi_{ij}^{*}c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}f_{j\sigma}+h.c.=-V\sum_{\mathbf{k},l}\chi^{*}_{l}F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}c^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\sigma^{\nu}_{\alpha\beta}f_{j\beta}+h.c.

(24)

where
F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{\mathbf{\delta}}^{NN}e^{il\theta_{\delta}}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{\delta}}
, and
\chi_{l}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\mathbf{\delta}}^{NN}\chi_{\mathbf{\delta}}e^{-il\theta_{\mathbf{\delta}}}

The self-consistent equation

\displaystyle\chi_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega}F^{l*}_{\mathbf{k}}\langle c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega}G^{c}(\mathbf{k},i\omega)V\chi_{m}F^{m}_{\mathbf{k}}\tilde{G}^{f}(\mathbf{k},i\omega)F^{l*}_{\mathbf{k}}

(26)

\displaystyle=-\frac{V}{3}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega}G^{c}(\mathbf{k},i\omega)\tilde{G}^{f}(\mathbf{k},i\omega)F^{l*}_{\mathbf{k}}F^{m}_{\mathbf{k}}\chi_{m}

(27)

Here
G_{c}=(i\omega-\epsilon^{A}_{\mathbf{k}})^{-1}
and
\tilde{G}_{f}=(i\omega-\epsilon^{B}_{\mathbf{k}}-\Sigma_{f}(\mathbf{k},i\omega))^{-1}
. This is an eigenvalue problem. The off-diagonal(

\chi_{l}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega}G^{c}(\mathbf{k},i\omega)\tilde{G}^{f}(\mathbf{k},i\omega)|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}/3

(28)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{f(\xi_{\mathbf{k},+})-f(\xi_{\mathbf{k},-})}{\xi_{\mathbf{k},+}-\xi_{\mathbf{k},-}}|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}/3

(29)

\displaystyle=-\int^{E_{+}}_{E_{-}}d^{2}\mathbf{k}\frac{|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}/3}{\xi_{\mathbf{k},+}-\xi_{\mathbf{k},-}}

(30)

\displaystyle=-\int^{E_{+}}_{E_{-}}d^{2}\mathbf{k}\frac{|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}}{\sqrt{\delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+V^{2}|\chi_{l}|^{2}|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}/3}}

(31)

\epsilon^{A/B}_{\mathbf{k}}=\epsilon_{F}\pm\delta_{\mathbf{k}}/2

\Phi_{l}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}V\chi_{l}F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}

\frac{1}{V}=-\int^{E_{+}}_{E_{-}}d^{2}\mathbf{k}\frac{|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}}{\sqrt{\delta_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}+4\Phi_{l}^{2}(\mathbf{k})}}

(32)

This is analogous to the self-consistency equation for BCS instability. At zero temperature the logarithmic divergence of integral at
\Phi=0
indicates that an infinitesimal
V

here the weight is the density of states. This expression reduces to

\eta^{l}(\epsilon_{F})\sim\rho(\epsilon_{F})\int_{FS}d\mathbf{k}_{F}|F^{l}_{\mathbf{k}_{F}}|^{2}

(35)

in the small
k_{F}
limit where the band structure has an approximate rotational symmetry. The angular momentum channel of the condensed exciton is efficiently predicted by comparing the parameter

across all angular momentum channels

The bCI phase by Ref.

Appendix I
Excitonic topological insulator at
\nu_{T}=2

Let us also have some discussions for the total filling
\nu_{T}=n_{A}+n_{B}=2
. When
D
is large, the ground state should be a band insulator with two electrons per triangular site on the B layer while the A layer is empty. Again electrons are transferred to the A layer when
D
is decreased. Experimentally a topological insulator with helical edge modes is observed at
\nu_{T}=2
Li et al. 2021b
for intermediate values of

C=\pm 1
are occupied. Each Chern band can be described by a mean-field theory similar to our discussion at
\nu_{T}=1
and can still arise from the excitonic mechanism. Both intra-valley and inter-valley exciton condensation are possible, leading to either a quantum valley Hall (QVH) insulator or a quantum spin Hall (QSH) insulator QVH insulator can crossover to a band insulator from pure single particle physics. QSH insulator has additional inter-valley-coherent (IVC) order and maybe a parent state of superconductivity upon doping
Po et al. 2018
;
Kozii et al. 2020
;
Chatterjee et al. 2021
. We hope future experiments can distinguish these two different states.

In search of the perfect fit: interpretation, flexible modelling, and the existing generalisations of the normal distribution
Andriette Bekker, Matthias Wagener, and Mohammad Arashi

Abstract
Many generalised distributions exist for modelling data with vastly diverse characteristics. However, very few of these generalisations of the normal distribution have shape parameters with clear roles that determine, for instance, skewness and tail shape. In this chapter, we review existing skewing mechanisms and their properties in detail. Using the knowledge acquired, we add a skewness parameter to the body-tail generalised normal distribution [ 47 ] , that yields the FIN with parameters for location, scale, body-shape, skewness, and tail weight. Basic statistical properties of the FIN are provided, such as the PDF , cumulative distribution function, moments, and likelihood equations. Additionally, the FIN PDF is extended to a multivariate setting using a student t-copula, yielding the MFIN ( MFIN ). The MFIN is applied to stock returns data, where it outperforms the t-copula multivariate generalised hyperbolic, Azzalini skew-t, hyperbolic, and normal inverse Gaussian distributions. Keywords : body tail, kurtosis, maximum likelihood, multivariate, skewness, skew normal, stock returns

The process of generating a new distribution involves four steps:

1.

Investigate the functional properties of derivative kernel functions from existing distributions.

Construct a derivative kernel function with the desired functional form.

Take the indefinite integral of the derivative kernel function to obtain a new kernel function.

3.2
Cumulative distribution function

The

of the standard

is derived with the definition of a

and (

) working in a piece-wise fashion. For
z\leq 0
substituting Lemma 2, in the Appendix, we have that:

\displaystyle F(z;\alpha,\beta,\kappa)

\displaystyle=\int^{z}_{-\infty}{\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{\delta}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta\kappa},|t|^{\beta\kappa}\right)dt}

\displaystyle={\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{\delta}\int^{\infty}_{z}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta\kappa},t^{\beta\kappa}\right)dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{\delta}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta\kappa},z^{\beta\kappa}\right)-z\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta\kappa},z^{\beta\kappa}\right)\right).

(12)

For
z>0
:

F(z;\alpha,\beta,\kappa)=1-\int^{\infty}_{z}{\phi\kappa\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha\kappa}{\beta},\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)^{\beta/\kappa}\right)dt}.

(13)

Letting

, and substituting Lemma 2, in the Appendix, the integral in (
13

\displaystyle=1-\phi\kappa\int^{\infty}_{\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z}{\phi^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha\kappa}{\beta},s^{\frac{\beta}{\kappa}}\right)ds}

\displaystyle=1-\phi^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta/\kappa},\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)^{\beta/\kappa}\right)-\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta/\kappa},\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)^{\beta/\kappa}\right)\right).

(14)

Summarising from (

) and (

) we have that the

of the standard

F(z;\alpha,\beta,\kappa)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\kappa^{-1}}{\delta}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta\kappa},z^{\beta\kappa}\right)-z\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta\kappa},z^{\beta\kappa}\right)\right)&z\leq 0\\
1-\phi^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta}\kappa,\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)^{\beta/\kappa}\right)-\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha\kappa}{\beta},\left(\phi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}z\right)^{\beta/\kappa}\right)\right)&z>0\\

(15)

of

is given by the substitution of

in (

5
Concluding remarks

This chapter focuses on the analysis of characteristics such as body shape, skewness, and kurtosis that can be observed in different data sets. In particular, we explore the generalisations of the
EP
distribution for this purpose. Through an examination of existing skew
EP
distributions and skewing mechanisms, we identify the advantages and drawbacks of previous literature in this regard. In order to improve on this, we introduce the derivative kernel skewing paradigm to derive the
FIN
distribution. The
FIN
distribution has several desirable properties, including interpretable parameters, finite moments, tractable equations, a “joint" free mode, a mode equal to the location parameter, and kurtosis skewing similar to the Jones skew t and Azzalini skew normal. We also provide statistical properties such as the
PDF
,
CDF
, moments, and
ML
equations. To demonstrate the modelling performance of the
FIN
distribution, we apply it to stock returns data from five companies listed on the
NASDAQ
. Our results show that the
FIN
distribution achieves the best-fitting marginal distributions according to in-sample and out-of-sample metrics, while the dependence structure is modelled best with the t copula according to the sample metrics. In conclusion, the
FIN
distribution is a flexible model without sacrificing interpretability.

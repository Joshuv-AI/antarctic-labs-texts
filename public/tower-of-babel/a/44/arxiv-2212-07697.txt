On tetravalent half-arc-transitive graphs of girth 5 5


Abstract
A subgroup of the automorphism group of a graph Γ \Gamma is said to be half-arc-transitive on Γ \Gamma if its action on Γ \Gamma is transitive on the vertex set of Γ \Gamma and on the edge set of Γ \Gamma but not on the arc set of Γ \Gamma . Tetravalent graphs of girths 3 3 and 4 4 admitting a half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms have previously been characterized. In this paper we study the examples of girth 5 5 . We show that, with two exceptions, all such graphs only have directed 5 5 -cycles with respect to the corresponding induced orientation of the edges. Moreover, we analyze the examples with directed 5 5 -cycles, study some of their graph theoretic properties and prove that the 5 5 -cycles of such graphs are always consistent cycles for the given half-arc-transitive group. We also provide infinite families of examples, classify the tetravalent graphs of girth 5 5 admitting a half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms relative to which they are tightly-attached and classify the tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants of girth 5 5 .

Introduction

Throughout this paper graphs are assumed to be finite, simple, connected and undirected (but with an implicit orientation of the edges when appropriate). Most of the relevant group and graph theoretic concepts are reviewed in Section
2
, but for the ones that are not see
[
6
,
7
]
.

The topic of half-arc-transitive graphs and graphs admitting half-arc-transitive group actions has been extensively researched in the past decades with numerous publications and with researchers focusing on several different aspects of such graphs (see for instance
[
5
,
24
,
25
]
and the references therein). The vast majority of papers dealing with half-arc-transitive graphs focuses on examples with smallest possible valence, namely on the tetravalent ones, which is also what we do in this paper. One of the more fruitful approaches, in the context of tetravalent graphs in particular, has been studying the structure imposed on the graph by the half-arc-transitive action of the corresponding group. Namely, it is easy to see (but see for instance
[
14
]
) that a half-arc-transitive subgroup of the automorphism group of a graph induces two paired orientations of its edges. One can then study the structural properties of the given tetravalent graph induced by the so called alternating cycles (the investigation of which was initiated in
[
14
]
and recently taken further in
[
25
]
), or by the so called reachability relations (introduced in a more general context in
[
16
]
and then further studied in
[
12
,
13
]
also for infinite (di)graphs).

Investigations with respect to alternating cycles have been particularly successful, resulting in partial classifications and characterizations of such graphs (see for instance
[
14
,
17
,
21
,
26
,
29
]
), and revealing that the tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants (first studied in
[
18
]
and later analyzed in a number of papers, see for instance
[
1
,
5
,
27
,
28
]
) form an important subfamily of these graphs.

Another situation, where the local structure given by a half-arc-transitive group action proves to be very useful, is when one studies examples with small girth. It was observed back in 1997
[
19
]
that the tetravalent half-arc-transitive graphs of girth
3
naturally correspond to the
1
-regular cubic graphs (cubic graphs whose automorphism group acts regularly on the set of their arcs). Obtaining a nice characterization of half-arc-transitive tetravalent graphs of girth
4
turned out to be more challenging
[
15
]
. The weak metacirculants among them were later classified in
[
1
]
. It should be mentioned that tetravalent edge-transitive graphs of girths
3
and
4
were thoroughly investigated also in
[
22
]
.

In this paper we focus on the examples of girth
5
. More precisely, we study tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive group action. We study the possible types of
5
-cycles with respect to the induced orientation of the edges that can arise (see Section
3
for the definition) and provide a complete classification of the examples with undirected
5
-cycles (see Theorem
5.5
). Quite remarkably, it turns out that there are just two such graphs and that one of them is in fact the smallest half-arc-transitive graph (the well-known Doyle-Holt graph) while the other is a certain Rose window graph admitting two different half-arc-transitive subgroups of automorphisms giving rise to
5
-cycles of two different types.

All other tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting half-arc-transitive group actions have directed
5
-cycles in the corresponding orientations of the edges and here the situation is much more complex and diverse. For these graphs we first provide a classification of the tightly-attached examples (see Theorem
4.1
), where it is interesting to note that all such examples are in fact half-arc-transitive (that is, their full automorphism group is half-arc-transitive). The non-tightly-attached examples are then studied in Section
6
. We prove various properties of such graphs, among them the fact that in this case the
5
-cycles are all consistent and that each edge lies on at most two
5
-cycles, to name just two. We provide infinitely many examples having precisely one
5
-cycle through any given edge and also infinitely many examples having two
5
-cycles through any given edge (see Theorem
6.7
). We also show that for each of the two possibilities half-arc-transitive examples exist. Interestingly, the smallest half-arc-transitive one with two
5
-cycles through each edge is quite large with its order being
6480
. We also analyze the examples that can be found in the census of all tetravalent graphs up to order
1000
admitting a half-arc-transitive action
[
20
]
(see also
[
23
]
) to compare some other properties of such graphs. It turns out that there is a great variety in their properties (see the tables in Section
6
) which suggests that a complete classification of all tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive action is probably not feasible. We conclude the paper by an application of our results yielding a complete classification of tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants of girth
5
(see Theorem
7.2
).

Preliminaries

For a graph
\Gamma
we let
V(\Gamma)
,
E(\Gamma)
and
A(\Gamma)
be its vertex set, edge set and arc set, respectively, where an
arc
is an ordered pair of adjacent vertices. A subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is said to be
vertex-transitive
,
edge-transitive
and
arc-transitive
on
\Gamma
if the corresponding natural action of
G
on
V(\Gamma)
,
E(\Gamma)
and
A(\Gamma)
, respectively, is transitive. If
G
is vertex- and edge- but not arc-transitive on
\Gamma
, it is said to be
half-arc-transitive
on
\Gamma
. In this case
\Gamma
is said to be
G
-half-arc-transitive
. In the case that
G=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
we simply say that
\Gamma
is
half-arc-transitive
. Observe that if
\Gamma
admits a half-arc-transitive subgroup of automorphisms then
\Gamma
is either half-arc-transitive or is arc-transitive. We will often be using the well-known fact (see
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
) that if
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is half-arc-transitive on
\Gamma
then no element of
G
can interchange a pair of adjacent vertices of
\Gamma
.

As is well known (but see for instance
[
14
]
) a half-arc-transitive action of
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
induces two paired orientations of edges of
\Gamma
. If
\vec{\Gamma}
is one of the two corresponding oriented graphs with underlying graph
\Gamma
and
(u,v)
is an arc of
\vec{\Gamma}
, we say that
u
is the
tail
and
v
is the
head
of the arc
(u,v)
and indicate this by writing
u\to v
. In the case that
\Gamma
is tetravalent (which will be the case throughout this paper) each vertex is the tail of two and the head of two arcs of
\vec{\Gamma}
, thereby giving rise to
G
-alternating cycles
which are cycles of
\Gamma
for which every two consecutive edges have opposite orientations in
\vec{\Gamma}
. It was proved in
[
14
]
that all
G
-alternating cycles of a graph have the same even length, half of which is called the
G
-radius
of
\Gamma
, and that every pair of nondisjoint
G
-alternating cycles meets in the same number of vertices (called the
G
-attachment number
of
\Gamma
). If the
G
-attachment number coincides with the
G
-radius, the graph is said to be
G
-tightly-attached
(in all of the above terminology we omit the prefix
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
when
G=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
).

The tightly-attached examples were classified in
[
14
,
26
]
(but see also
[
17
,
29
]
) and in
[
18
]
it was shown that they represent Class I of the four (not disjoint) classes of a larger family of
weak metacirculants
, which are graphs admitting a transitive metacyclic group. In the case that the quotient graph with respect to the orbits of the corresponding normal cyclic subgroup of the metacyclic group is of valence
4
the graph is said to be of Class IV. The results of
[
28
]
imply that each tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculant belongs to at least one of Classes I and IV and the tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants of Class IV and girth
4
were classified in
[
1
]
.

When studying the structure of (tetravalent) graphs admitting a half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms the concept of reachability can also be useful. Here we only describe some of the corresponding notions first introduced in
[
16
]
and later further studied in
[
12
,
13
]
but see
[
16
,
12
]
for details. Let
\vec{\Gamma}
be an oriented graph (in our case we will always take one of the two
G
-induced orientations of the tetravalent graph in question where
G
will be a corresponding half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms) and let
W=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
be a walk in the underlying graph of
\vec{\Gamma}
. The
weight

\zeta(W)
of
W
(called the sum of
W
in
[
16
]
) is defined as the number of
i
,
0\leq i<m
, such that
(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in A(\vec{\Gamma})
, minus the number of
i
,
0\leq i<m
, such that
(v_{i+1},v_{i})\in A(\vec{\Gamma})
. We then say that the vertices
u
and
v
of
\vec{\Gamma}
are
R^{+}
-related
if there exists a walk
W=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
in
\vec{\Gamma}
from
u
to
v
such that
\zeta(W)=0
and for each
i
with
0\leq i\leq n
the weight of the subwalk
(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{i})
of
W
is nonnegative. The number of equivalence classes of
R^{+}
is called the
alter-perimeter
of
\vec{\Gamma}
and
\vec{\Gamma}
is said to be
alter-complete
if its alter-perimeter is
1
(that is, if all pairs of vertices are
\mathrm{R}^{+}
-related).

When studying graphs possessing a large degree of symmetry the notion of consistent cycles turns out to be useful. Here we only review the notions and results needed in this paper, but see
[
2
]
where consistent cycles in half-arc-transitive graphs were thoroughly investigated. Let
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
be a vertex-transitive subgroup of automorphisms of a graph
\Gamma
. A cycle
C
of
\Gamma
is said to be
G
-consistent
if there exists an element
g\in G
(which is said to be a
shunt
of
C
) acting as a
1
-step rotation of
C
. It is easy to see (but see
[
2
]
) that
G
has a natural action on the set of all
G
-consistent cycles of
\Gamma
and so one can speak of
G
-orbits of
G
-consistent cycles. It follows from
[
2
, Proposition 5.3]
that if
G
is a half-arc-transitive subgroup of automorphisms of a tetravalent graph then there are precisely two
G
-orbits of
G
-consistent cycles of
\Gamma
.

We conclude this section by reviewing the definition of an important family of tetravalent graphs, known as the Rose window graphs
[
30
]
, containing four infinite subfamilies of vertex- and edge-transitive (in fact arc-transitive) examples (see
[
9
]
). For an integer
n\geq 3
and integers
a,r
, where
1\leq r<n/2
and
1\leq a\leq n-1
, the
Rose window
graph
\mathrm{R}_{n}(a,r)
has vertex set
V=\{x_{i}\,|i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}\cup\{y_{i}\,|i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}
and edge set

, where

is the residue class ring modulo

.

We conclude this section by another observation that will be useful in the subsequent sections.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tetravalent graph of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive subgroup
G\leq\text{Aut}(\Gamma)
and let
\vec{\Gamma}
be one of the two
G
-induced orientations of
\Gamma
. Then the natural action of
G
on the set of all
2
-arcs of
\vec{\Gamma}
is transitive if and only if
|G_{v}|>2
for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
. Moreover, if
|G_{v}|=2
for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
then
G
has precisely two orbits on the set of all
2
-arcs of
\vec{\Gamma}
, for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
there are precisely two
2
-arcs with middle vertex
v
from each of these two
G
-orbits, and the two
2
-arcs with middle vertex
v
from the same
G
-orbit have no edges in common.

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of the fact that in
\vec{\Gamma}
each vertex
v
has two out-neighbors and two in-neighbors and that
G
acts transitively on the set of arcs of
\vec{\Gamma}
. Namely, this implies that for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
there is an element of
G_{v}
interchanging the two out-neighbors of
v
and at the same time interchanging the two in-neighbors of
v
. Therefore,
G
acts transitively on the set of
2
-arcs of
\vec{\Gamma}
if and only if there is an element of
G_{v}
fixing the two out-neighbors of
v
while interchanging the two in-neighbors of
v
. We will see that such an automorphism of
G_{v}
exists if and only if
|G_{v}|>2
. Obviously in the case of its existence,
|G_{v}|>2
holds. Let now
|G_{v}|>2
. Then there exists a nontrivial automorphism
\alpha\in G_{v}
fixing at least one of the neighbors of
v
. Since the two in-neighbors are in the same
G_{v}
-orbit and the two out-neighbors are in the same
G_{v}
-orbit, this means that
\alpha
either fixes both in-neighbors, or both out-neighbors. If it also interchanges the out-neighbors in the first case or in-neighbors in the second case, the sufficiency is proven. If not, then since
\Gamma
is connected and
\alpha\neq id
, there is some vertex fixed by
\alpha
, were
\alpha
does not fix all its neighbors, but does fix both in-neighbors or both out-neighbors. Since
G
acts transitively on the vertex set of
\Gamma
, all vertex stabilizers are isomorphic, meaning that this is also true for

, as required.

Let
\Gamma

Theorem 4.1
.

A tetravalent graph

of girth

is a tightly-attached

-half-arc-transitive graph for some subgroup

if and only if one of the following holds:

•

, the well-known Doyle-Holt graph;

•

, where

and

Moreover, all such graphs are half-arc-transitive. Furthermore, the only tetravalent graph of girth

admitting a half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms relative to which it is tightly-attached and has undirected

-cycles is the Doyle-Holt graph.

Given that there is only one tetravalent graph of girth

\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)=\mathrm{SL}(2,p)/\mathrm{Z}
, where
\mathrm{Z}=\{I,-I\}
and
I
is the identity matrix, and so we can view the elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
as matrices from
\mathrm{SL}(2,p)
with the understanding that for each
A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,p)
the matrices
A
and
-A
represent the same element of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
. We start with a preparatory lemma.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
p
be a prime with
p\equiv 1\pmod{10}
, let
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a fifth root of unity and let

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}\xi&\xi+\xi^{-1}\\
0&\xi^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

be viewed as elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
. Then
A
is an involution,
B
and
AB
are both of order
5
and
\langle A,B\rangle=\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
.

Proof.

A straightforward calculation shows that
A
is an involution (in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
) and
B
is of order
5
(recall that
\xi
is of order
5
in the multiplicative group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
, and so
1+\xi+\xi^{2}+\xi^{3}+\xi^{4}=0
). Moreover, one can verify that
(AB)^{5}=-I
(in
\mathrm{SL}(2,p)
), and so
AB
is also of order
5
in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
. Letting
G=\langle A,B\rangle
it thus remains to be verified that
G=\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
.
Observe first that

ABA=\begin{pmatrix}-\xi^{-1}&0\\
\xi+\xi^{-1}&-\xi\end{pmatrix}\notin\langle B\rangle,

showing that
G
is neither abelian nor dihedral. Since
G
contains an element of order
5
, the theorem of Dickson on subgroups of the
\mathrm{PSL}
groups (see
[
8
, Hauptsats 8.27]
) implies that if
G
is not the whole
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
then it must be isomorphic to the alternating group
A_{5}
or to a semidirect product of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{t}
for a divisor
t
of
p-1
.

It can be verified that in
A_{5}
for each pair of an involution
a
and an element
b
of order
5
such that
ab
is also of order
5
the element
ab^{2}
is necessarily of order
3
. Calculating we find that

AB^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\xi^{-2}\\
-\xi^{2}&\xi+\xi^{-1}-1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad(AB^{2})^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\xi-\xi^{-1}&-\xi^{2}(3-\xi+3\xi^{2})\\
\xi(3-\xi+3\xi^{2})&-3+6\xi+6\xi^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

and so
AB^{2}
is not of order
3
as
1-\xi-\xi^{-1}\in\{-1,1\}
contradicts the assumption that
\xi
has multiplicative order
5
. Therefore,
G
is not isomorphic to
A_{5}
.

Finally, suppose that
G
is isomorphic to a semidirect product of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{t}
for a divisor
t
of
p-1
. Then
G=\langle x,y\mid x^{t}=y^{p}=1,x^{-1}yx=y^{k}\rangle
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
k^{t}=1
. Since
G
contains the involution
A
,
G
is of even order, and so
t
is even. Moreover, as
y
is of odd order,
A
is of the form
x^{t/2}y^{j}
, where
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. But as
B=x^{i}y^{\ell}
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{t}
,
i\neq 0
, and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we see that
B
and
AB
cannot both be of order
5
(as this would force
t\mid 5i
and
t\mid 5(i+t/2)
, which clearly cannot both hold). This finally proves that
G=\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
, as claimed.
∎

In the proof of the next result we use a well-known construction of double coset graphs. For self containment we recall the definition. For a group
G
, its subgroup
H
and a subset
S
of
G
the
double coset graph

\mathrm{Cos}(G,H,S)
of
G

with respect to

H
and
S
is the graph whose vertex set is the set of all right cosets
Hg
of
H
in
G
and in which the vertices
Hg
and
Hg^{\prime}
are adjacent if and only if
g^{\prime}g^{-1}\in HSH
. It is well known and easy to see that
G
has a vertex-transitive action on
\mathrm{Cos}(G,H,S)
via right multiplication which is faithful if and only if
H
is core-free in
G
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
p
be a prime with
p\equiv 1\pmod{10}
. Then there exists a connected tetravalent arc-transitive graph of girth
5
and order
p(p^{2}-1)/4
admitting the group
\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
as a half-arc-transitive subgroup of automorphisms and each edge of this graph lies on two
5
-cycles.

Proof.

Let
G=\mathrm{PSL}(2,p)
, let
A,B\in G
be as in Lemma
6.5
and let
H=\langle A\rangle
. By Lemma
6.5
we have that
G=\langle A,B\rangle
, and so the double coset graph
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cos}(G,H,\{B,B^{-1}\})
is a connected graph of order
|G|/2=p(p^{2}-1)/4
. As explained in the paragraph preceding this proposition
G
has a faithful vertex-transitive action on
\Gamma
. For better readability of the remainder of this proof we write
a
and
b
instead of
A
and
B
, respectively.

Observe first that the neighbors of the vertex
H
are
Hb
,
Hb^{-1}
,
Hba
and
Hb^{-1}a
. Since
a
is an involution,
a
and
b
do not commute, and
ab
and
b
are both of order
5
(see Lemma
6.5
), these four cosets are pairwise distinct, and so
\Gamma
is tetravalent. In addition, right multiplication by
a
fixes
H
and interchanges
Hb
with
Hba
and at the same time
Hb^{-1}
with
Hb^{-1}a
, while right multiplication by
b
maps the edge
\{Hb^{-1},H\}
to the edge
\{H,Hb\}
. This shows that the action of
G
on
\Gamma
is edge-transitive and is thus in fact half-arc-transitive (since the order of
G
is twice the order of
\Gamma
it cannot be arc-transitive). Choose the
G
-induced orientation
\vec{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
in which
Hb
and
Hba
are out-neighbors and
Hb^{-1}
and
Hb^{-1}a
are in-neighbors of
H
. Since right multiplication by
b
is a shunt of a
5
-cycle through
(Hb^{-1},H,Hb)
while right multiplication by
ab
is a shunt of a
5
-cycle through
(Hb^{-1}a,H,Hb)
, there are thus two distinct directed
5
-cycles (with respect to
\vec{\Gamma}
- henceforth we simply speak of directed cycles) through the edge
\{H,Hb\}
.

We next prove that
\Gamma
is in fact of girth
5
. Since a
3
-cycle of
\Gamma
can only be a directed cycle (recall that no element of
G
can swap a pair of adjacent vertices) and each of the
2
-arcs
(Hb^{-1},H,Hb),(Hb^{-1}a,H,Hb)
of
\vec{\Gamma}
lies on a
G
-consistent
5
-cycle, it is clear that there are no
3
-cycles in
\Gamma
. Since
(Hb^{-1}aba,Hba,H,Hb,Hb^{-1}ab)
is a part of a
G
-alternating cycle, the
G
-alternating cycles are of length
4
if and only if
Hb^{-1}aba=Hb^{-1}ab
, which holds if and only if
b^{-1}ab
and
a
commute. A straightforward calculation shows that

b^{-1}aba=\begin{pmatrix}-2-\xi^{2}-2\xi^{3}&1+\xi^{2}\\
1+\xi^{2}&-\xi^{2}\end{pmatrix}\ \text{and}\ \begin{pmatrix}-\xi^{2}&-1-\xi^{2}\\
-1-\xi^{2}&-2-\xi^{2}-2\xi^{3}\end{pmatrix}=ab^{-1}ab.

Note that
-1-\xi^{2}\neq 1+\xi^{2}
(since
p
is odd and
\xi^{2}\neq-1
), and so the comment from the paragraph preceding Lemma 6.5 implies that these two elements of
\mathrm{PSL(2,p)}
can only be equal if
-2-\xi^{2}-2\xi^{3}=-(-\xi^{2})=\xi^{2}
. But since
1+\xi+\xi^{2}+\xi^{3}+\xi^{4}=0
and
p
is odd, this would force
\xi=-\xi^{-1}
, contradicting the fact that
\xi
is of multiplicative order
5
. Therefore, the
G
-alternating cycles of
\Gamma
are not
4
-cycles. The results of
[
15
]
(in particular, the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
) thus reveal that the only way
\Gamma
can be of girth
4
is if it has directed
4
-cycles (it is easy to see that the so called “exceptional” graphs from
[
15
]
are either bipartite or can only have directed
5
-cycles if they are of order at most
25
). Suppose there is a directed
4
-cycle through the edge
\{H,Hb\}
. It can share at most two consecutive edges with any of the above mentioned
5
-cycles through this edge given by the shunts
b
and
ab
, and so one of
Hbab\to Hb^{-1}
and
Hb^{2}\to Hb^{-1}a
must hold (see Figure
7
). In the latter case the
2
-arc
(H,Hb,Hb^{2})
lies on a directed
4
-cycle, and so applying
b^{-1}
we see that
(Hb^{-1},H,Hb)
also lies on a directed
4
-cycle, showing that
Hbab\to Hb^{-1}
does hold in any case. But then
Hb^{2}ab=Hb^{-1}
, which is impossible as
b^{2}ab^{2}=1
yields
a=b
, while
b^{2}ab^{2}=a
yields
aba=b^{-1}
, contradicting Lemma
6.5
.

5
-cycles through the edge
\{H,Hb\}
.

We finally prove that
\Gamma
is in fact arc-transitive. To this end take the matrix

C=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1-2\xi-2\xi^{2}\\
-1-2\xi-2\xi^{2}&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Calculating we find that
(1+2\xi+2\xi^{2})^{2}=-3+4\xi^{2}+4\xi^{3}
, and so
C
is invertible if and only if
-2+4\xi^{2}+4\xi^{3}\neq 0
, which is easily seen to be the case. We can thus view
C
as an element of
\mathrm{PGL}(2,p)
. Writing
c
instead of
C
it is then straightforward to verify that
c
,
ac
and
bc
are all involutions. This shows that
c
commutes with
a
and inverts
b
, and so we can let
c
act by conjugation on the vertex set of
\Gamma
. Since
c
preserves
H
and
\{b,b^{-1}\}
this is clearly an automorphism of
\Gamma
and as it fixes the vertex
H
and interchanges the neighbors
Hb
and
Hb^{-1}
, the graph
\Gamma
is indeed arc-transitive, as claimed.
∎

In view of the discussion from the paragraph following the proof of Proposition
6.4
and the fact that by the well-known Dirichlet’s prime number theorem (see for instance
[
11
, p. 167]
) there are infinitely many primes
p
with
p\equiv 1\pmod{10}
, we thus finally obtain the following.

Theorem 6.7
.

There exist infinitely many tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive group action such that each edge of the graph lies on a unique
5
-cycle, and there also exist infinitely many tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive group action such that each edge of the graph lies on precisely two
5
-cycles.

The above result shows that there is an abundance of examples of tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive group action. It thus makes sense to inspect the census of all tetravalent graphs admitting a half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms up to order
1000

[
20
]
to see how many such graphs exist and how much variety one can get while observing some of their other interesting properties.

The above data suggests that there is a great variety in examples of tetravalent graphs of girth
5
admitting a half-arc-transitive group action. It also seems to suggest that the examples with two
5
-cycles through each edge are not as common as those with a unique
5
-cycle through each edge. But the one thing that stands out the most is that (at least up to order
1000
) none of the examples with two
5
-cycles through each edge is half-arc-transitive. It thus makes sense to ask if this is the case in general. Now, suppose
\Gamma
is a tetravalent graph of girth
5
with two
5
-cycles through each edge and suppose
\Gamma
admits a half-arc-transitive subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
. Let
v
be a vertex of
\Gamma
, let
u
be one of its neighbors and choose the
G
-induced orientation
\vec{\Gamma}
such that
v\to u
. By Proposition
6.3
the
5
-cycles are
G
-consistent, and so there must exist elements
g,g^{\prime}\in G
, each of which is of order
5
and maps
v
to
u
(these are the
G
-shunts of the two
G
-consistent
5
-cycles through the edge
vu
). It is easy to see that
z=g^{\prime}g^{-1}
is an involution fixing
v
but interchanging the two out-neighbors of
v
as well as the two in-neighbors of
v
in
\vec{\Gamma}
. Therefore,
H=\langle g,z\rangle
is a half-arc-transitive subgroup of
G
. If
H\neq G
, then
G
has vertex stabilizers of order
4
and
H
is an index
2
half-arc-transitive subgroup of
G
. Moreover, if
H=G
and
G
has vertex stabilizers of order
4
then
G_{v}=\langle z,z^{\prime}\rangle
for some involution
z^{\prime}
,
z^{\prime}\neq z
, commuting with
z
. In any case, the group
H
acts half-arc-transitively on
\Gamma
, is a quotient group of the finitely presented group

\tilde{G}=\langle\tilde{z},\tilde{g}\mid\tilde{z}^{2},\tilde{g}^{5},(\tilde{z}\tilde{g})^{5}\rangle

and a vertex stabilizer in
H
is either generated by the element corresponding to
\tilde{z}
or by the element corresponding to
\tilde{z}
and some involution commuting with it.

One can thus use the
LowIndexNormalSubgroups
method (see
[
4
]
) in
Magma

[
3
]
to construct all candidates for such graphs up to some reasonably large order. It turns out that up to order
5\,000
there are
43
tetravalent graphs of girth
5
with two
5
-cycles through each edge admitting a half-arc-transitive group action (recall that there are
15
of them up to order
1000
). Of these there are
6
examples admitting only one half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms where this group has vertex stabilizers of order
2
(like the graph GHAT[480,114]), there are
8
examples admitting only one half-arc-transitive group of automorphisms where this group has vertex stabilizers of order
4
(like the graph GHAT[165,4]), while the remaining
29
examples admit two half-arc-transitive groups, a larger one with vertex stabilizers of order
4
and the smaller one being its index
2
subgroup. However, all of these
43
examples are in fact arc-transitive.

As much as the above data supports a conjecture that there are no tetravalent half-arc-transitive graphs of girth
5
with two
5
-cycles through each edge such a conjecture would be false. Namely, it turns out that there exists a tetravalent half-arc-transitive graph of order
6480
and girth
5
with two
5
-cycles through each edge and that it is the unique smallest example with this property. Its automorphism group is a solvable group of the form

(\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{4}\rtimes((\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times Q_{8})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}))\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{5}

and has vertex stabilizers of order
2
. Given that the graph can relatively easily be obtained via the above mentioned
LowIndexNormalSubgroups
method while giving the group as a finitely presented group would require a rather long list of relations, we do not spell out the automorphism group of the graph explicitly. We do mention that the radius and attachment number of the graph are
4
and
1
, respectively, and that the graph is not alter-complete.

The above data suggests many interesting questions to be considered in possible future studies. One of them is for sure the following one (note that the potential graphs giving an affirmative answer to the question necessarily have two
5
-cycles through each edge).

Question 6.8
.

Does there exist a tetravalent half-arc-transitive graph of girth
5
whose automorphism group has vertex stabilizers of order
4
? If so, what is the smallest example?

To mention just one more interesting question observe that inspecting Table
2
it appears that if a tetravalent graph
\Gamma
of girth
5
with a unique
5
-cycle through each edge admits more than one half-arc-transitive subgroup of automorphisms then it admits precisely two. It turns out that up to order
1000
for each tetravalent graph of girth
5
with a unique
5
-cycle through each edge the following holds: if this graph admits more than one half-arc-transitive subgroup of automorphisms then the vertex stabilizers in the full automorphism group are of order
4
. Of course, if this is indeed the case then any half-arc-transitive subgroup is of index
2
and is thus normal, in which case one easily shows that there can be at most two half-arc-transitive subgroups. What is more, one can show that if
H
and
G
are two half-arc-transitive subgroups (each of which is of index
2
in the full automorphism group) then the
H
-consistent cycles which are not
5
-cycles are the
G
-alternating cycles while the
G
-consistent cycles which are not
5
-cycles are the
H
-alternating cycles (which is in accordance with the data in Table
2
). However, it is not obvious why the stabilizers in the full automorphism group should be of order
4
if we have at least two half-arc-transitive subgroups. To support this we mention that, although the example GHAT[125,2] from Table
2
does admit just one half-arc-transitive subgroup of automorphisms, its full automorphism group has vertex stabilizers of order
8
, despite the fact that there is just one
5
-cycle through each edge of the graph. We thus pose the following question.

Question 6.9
.

Does there exist a tetravalent graph of girth
5
admitting more than two half-arc-transitive subgroups of its automorphism group?

7
An application: weak metacirculants of girth
5

We conclude the paper by showing how the results from the previous sections can be used to easily obtain a classification of tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants of girth
5
. It was shown in
[
28
]
that each non-tightly-attached tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculant is of Class IV. In view of Theorem
4.1
we thus only need to consider the weak metacirculants of Class IV. It was shown in
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
that the tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants of Class IV all belong to the
8
-parametric family of graphs
\mathcal{X}_{IV}(m,n;r,t;p,a;q,b)
, the definition of which we review for self completeness (but see
[
1
]
for details).

The graph
\mathcal{X}_{IV}(m,n;r,t;p,a;q,b)
, where
m\geq 5
,
n\geq 3
,
1\leq p<q<m/2
and
r,t,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are such that

\gcd(p,q,m)=1,\ \gcd(a,b,t,n)=1,\ \gcd(t,n)\neq 1,\ r^{m}=1\ \text{and}\ t(r-1)=0,

has vertex set
\{u_{i}^{j}\colon i\in\mathbb{Z}_{m},\ j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}
and adjacencies of the form

u_{i}^{j}\>\>\sim\>\>\begin{cases}u_{i+p}^{j+ar^{i}},\,u_{i+q}^{j+br^{i}}&;\quad\text{$0\leq i<m-q$, $j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}$ }\\
u_{i+p}^{j+ar^{i}},\,u_{i+q}^{j+br^{i}+t}&;\quad\text{$m-q\leq i<m-p$, $j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}$ }\\
u_{i+p}^{j+ar^{i}+t},\,u_{i+q}^{j+br^{i}+t}&;\quad\text{$m-p\leq i<m$, $j\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}.$ }\\
\end{cases}

The edges connecting vertices with subscripts of the form
i
and
i+p
(addition performed modulo
m
) are called the
p
-edges
and those connecting vertices with subscripts of the form
i
and
i+q
are
q
-edges
. It follows from
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
that each tetravalent half-arc-transitive
\mathcal{X}_{IV}(m,n;r,t;p,a;q,b)
graph admits a regular subgroup of automorphisms (generated by the automorphisms
\rho
and
\sigma
from
[
1
]
) whose two orbits on the set of the edges of the graph are the set of all
p
-edges and the set of all
q
-edges. Using our results we now show that such a graph cannot be of girth
5
.

Proposition 7.1
.

There exist no tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculants of Class IV and girth
5
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\Gamma=\mathcal{X}_{IV}(m,n;r,t;p,a;q,b)
is a tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculant of Class IV and girth
5
. Since
\mathrm{R}_{12}(5,2)
is arc-transitive while
\mathcal{X}_{o}(3,9;4)
is not of Class IV (as can easily be verified, but see for instance
[
28
]
), Theorem
5.5
implies that the
5
-cycles of
\Gamma
are all directed. As explained in the comments preceding this lemma the results of
[
1
]
show that
\Gamma
is a Cayley graph, and so Corollary
6.2
and Proposition
6.4
imply that each edge of
\Gamma
lies on a unique
5
-cycle, that the vertex stabilizers in
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
are of order
2
, and that one of the two
5
-cycles through the vertex
u_{0}^{0}
consists of five
p
-edges while the other consists of five
q
-edges. Moreover, for each vertex
v
of
\Gamma
the unique nontrivial element of the stabilizer
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)_{v}
interchanges the set of all
p
-edges with the set of all
q
-edges (as the regular group mentioned in the paragraph preceding this lemma is of index
2
in
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
).

As
1\leq p<q<m/2
, this implies that
5p=m
and
5q=2m
, forcing
q=2p
. The condition
\gcd(p,q,m)=1
thus forces
p=1
, and consequently
m=5
and
q=2
. It is not difficult to see (but see
[
1
]
) that because of
p=1
we can in fact assume that
a=0
, and so to obtain a
5
-cycle through
u_{0}^{0}
consisting of five
p
-edges we require that
t=0
. Then
\gcd(a,b,t,n)=1
implies
\gcd(b,n)=1
, and one can again easily show that we can in fact assume that
b=1
. Therefore,
\Gamma=\mathcal{X}_{IV}(5,n;r,0;1,0;2,1)
with
r^{5}=1
,
r\neq 1
(otherwise
\Gamma
would have
4
-cycles). To obtain the
5
-cycle through
u_{0}^{0}
consisting of five
q
-edges we thus require
1+r+r^{2}+r^{3}+r^{4}=0
.

Let
\tau
be the unique nontrivial automorphism fixing
u_{0}^{0}
and recall that it swaps
p
-edges with
q
-edges. Consider the
\tau
-image of the walk
(u_{0}^{0},u_{2}^{1},u_{1}^{1},u_{0}^{1},u_{2}^{2},u_{1}^{2},u_{0}^{2},u_{2}^{3},\ldots,u_{0}^{5})
of length
15
(on which every third edge is a
q
-edge and the remaining ones are
p
-edges). Fix the
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
-induced orientation of the edges of
\Gamma
in which
u_{0}^{0}\to u_{1}^{0}
. If
u_{0}^{0}\to u_{2}^{1}
, then one readily verifies that
\tau
maps
u_{0}^{1}
to
u_{2}^{-r^{2}-r^{4}}
,
u_{0}^{2}
to
u_{4}^{-r-r^{2}-2r^{4}}=u_{4}^{1+r^{3}-r^{4}}
,
u_{0}^{3}
to
u_{1}^{1-r-r^{4}}
,
u_{0}^{4}
to
u_{3}^{-r-r^{3}-r^{4}}
and finally
u_{0}^{5}
to
u_{0}^{-1-r-r^{2}-r^{3}-r^{4}}=u_{0}^{0}
. Since
u_{0}^{0}
is left fixed by
\tau
this implies
n=5
. But this contradicts the fact that
r^{5}=1
and
r\neq 1
. In an analogous way one can verify that if
u_{2}^{1}\to u_{0}^{0}
we again have that
\tau
maps
u_{0}^{5}
to
u_{0}^{0}
, which we already know cannot be the case.
∎

Together with Theorem
4.1
this yields the following result.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a tetravalent half-arc-transitive weak metacirculant of girth
5
. Then
\Gamma
is tightly-attached and is either isomorphic to the Doyle-Holt graph
\mathcal{X}_{o}(3,9;4)
or is isomorphic to
\mathcal{X}_{o}(5,r;q)
for an odd integer
r
and some
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{r}^{*}
with
q\neq\pm 1
and
1+q+q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}=0
.

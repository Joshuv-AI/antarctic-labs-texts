Non-uniform convergence of solution for the Camassa–Holm equation in the zero-filter limit
Jinlu Li, Yanghai Yu, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this short note, we prove that given initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\frac{3}{2}
and for some
T>0
, the solution of the Camassa-Holm equation does not converges uniformly with respect to the initial data in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
to the inviscid Burgers equation as the filter parameter
\alpha
tends to zero. This is a supplement to our recent result on the zero-filter limit.

Keywords:
Camassa-Holm equation; Burgers equation; Non-uniform convergence; Zero-filter limit.

MSC (2010):
35Q35.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we continue to consider the zero-filter limit
\alpha\to 0
for the Camassa–Holm equation in the Sobolev space

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}m+2m\partial_{x}u+u\partial_{x}m=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
m=(1-\alpha^{2}\partial^{2}_{x})u,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the constant
\alpha>0
is a filter parameter. When the filter parameter
\alpha=0
, Eq.(
1.1
) becomes the Burgers equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+3u\partial_{x}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.2)

The Camassa–Holm equation was firstly proposed in the context of hereditary symmetries studied in
[
14
]
and then was derived explicitly as a water wave equation by Camassa–Holm
[
3
]
. (
1.1
) is completely integrable
[
3
,
6
]
with a bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
5
,
14
]
and infinitely many conservation laws
[
3
,
14
]
. Also, it admits exact peaked
soliton solutions (peakons) of the form
u(x,t)=ce^{-|x-ct|}
with
c>0,
which are orbitally stable
[
12
]
. Another remarkable feature of the Camassa–Holm equation is the wave breaking phenomena: the solution remains bounded while its slope becomes unbounded in finite time
[
4
,
8
,
9
]
. It is worth mentioning that the peaked solitons present the characteristic for the travelling water waves of greatest height and largest amplitude and arise as solutions to the free-boundary problem for incompressible Euler equations over a flat bed, see Refs.
[
7
,
10
,
11
]
for the details.

We note that the pseudo-differential operator
(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
with the
Fourier multiplier
(1+\alpha^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{-1}
can be defined as follows

\displaystyle\quad\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}f=g*f,\quad\forall\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.3)

where
g(x):=\frac{1}{2\alpha}e^{-\frac{|x|}{\alpha}}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
*
denotes convolution, then
u=g*m
. Using this identity and applying the pseudo-differential operator
\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}
to Eq.(
1.1
), one can rewrite Eq.(
1.1
) as a quasi-linear nonlocal evolution equation of hyperbolic type, namely

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+3u\partial_{x}u=-\alpha^{2}\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}u^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.4)

Formally, as
\alpha\to 0
, the solution of the Camassa–Holm equation (
1.4
) converges to the
solution of the following Burgers equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}+3u\partial_{x}u=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.5)

The Burgers equation is perhaps the most basic example of a PDE evolution leading to shocks. Further background and motivation for the Burgers equation may be found in
[
20
,
1
,
13
,
16
,
19
,
21
]
and references therein.

Gui-Liu
[
15
]
proved that the solutions of the Camassa–Holm equation with additional dissipative term
\nu\Lambda^{\gamma}u
does converge, at least locally, to the
one of the dissipative Burgers equation as the filter parameter
\alpha
tends to zero in the lower regularity Sobolev spaces.
Recently, in
[
18
]
, we considered the zero-filter limit for the Camassa–Holm equation without dissipative term in Sobolev spaces, and proved that the solution of (
1.4
) converges to the solution of the inviscid Burgers equation (
1.5
) in the topology of Sobolev spaces. Precisely speaking,

Theorem 1.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Assume that the initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})
be the smooth solutions of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) with the initial data
u_{0}
respectively. Then there exists a time
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})>0
such that
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0}),\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];H^{s})
and

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\left\|\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}=0.

An interesting problem appears:
For any
u_{0}\in U_{R}
, in the zero-filter limit
\alpha\to 0
, whether or not the
H^{s}
-convergence

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{0})\rightarrow\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})\quad\text{in}\quad L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}

can be established
uniformly with respect to the initial data
u_{0}
?

For any
R>0
, from now on, we denote any bounded subset
U_{R}\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
by

U_{R}:=\left\{\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}):\|\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq R\right\}.

In this paper, we shall answer the above question and state our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
s>\frac{3}{2}
. For any
u_{0}\in U_{R}
, let
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{0})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})
be the solutions of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) with the same initial data
u_{0}
, respectively. Then a family of solutions
\left\{\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{0})\right\}_{\alpha>0}
to (
1.4
)

\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\alpha}:\begin{cases}U_{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}([0,T];H^{s}),\\
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{0}),\end{cases}

do not converge strongly in a uniform way with respect to initial data to the solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})
of (
1.5
) in
H^{s}
.
More precisely, there exists a sequence initial data
\{u^{n}_{0}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in U_{R}
such that for a short time
T_{0}\leq T

\liminf_{\alpha_{n}\to 0}\left\|\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\alpha_{n}}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u^{n}_{0})\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{s}}\geq\eta_{0},

with some positive constant
\eta_{0}
.

Notation
Throughout this paper, we will denote by
C
any positive constant independent of the parameter
\alpha
, which may change from line to line. The symbol
\mathrm{A}\lesssim(\gtrsim)\mathrm{B}
means that there is a uniform positive “harmless” constant
C
independent of
\mathrm{A}
and
\mathrm{B}
such that
\mathrm{A}\leq(\geq)C\mathrm{B}
, and we sometimes use the notation
\mathrm{A}\approx\mathrm{B}
means that
\mathrm{A}\lesssim\mathrm{B}
and
\mathrm{B}\lesssim\mathrm{A}
.
Given a Banach space
X
, we denote its norm by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. For
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(I;X)
the set of continuous functions on
I
with values in
X
. Sometimes we will denote
L^{p}(0,T;X)
by
L_{T}^{p}X
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the nonhomogeneous Sobolev space is defined by

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi.

We recall the classical result for later proof.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

For
s>0
,
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is an algebra.
Moreover, we have for any
u,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\big(\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\big).

In particular, for
s>\frac{1}{2}
, due to the fact
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then we have

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

For fixed
\alpha>0
, by the classical local well-posedness result, we known that there exists a
T_{\alpha}=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},s,\alpha)>0
such that the Camassa-Holm has a unique solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,T_{\alpha}];H^{s})
. Furthermore, we can obtain that
\exists\;T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}},s)>0
such that
T\leq T_{\alpha}
and there exists
C_{1}>0
independent of
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}},\quad\forall\alpha\in[0,1).

(2.6)

Moreover, if
u_{0}\in H^{\gamma}\cap H^{s}
for some
\gamma\geq s-1
, then there exists
C_{2}(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}})>0
independent of
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{\gamma}}\leq C_{2}(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}})\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{\gamma}}.

(2.7)

For more details on the proof of (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we can refer to see
[
18
]
.

Next, we establish the following proposition will play a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Assume that
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\approx 1
. Let
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\mathbf{\alpha}}(u_{0})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}^{0}(u_{0})
be the smooth solutions of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) with the same initial data
u_{0}
, respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}(\alpha,u_{0})\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq Ct^{2}\mathbf{F}(\alpha,u_{0}),

where we denote

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(\alpha,u_{0}):=-3u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0}-\alpha^{2}\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}u_{0}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\alpha,u_{0}):=\alpha\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\left(\alpha\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\right)+(\alpha+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}})\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+2}}\right).

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote
u(t)=\mathbf{S}^{\alpha}_{t}(u_{0})
.
Firstly, we need to estimate the different Sobolev norms of the term
u(t)-u_{0}
, which can be bounded by
t
multiplying the corresponding Besov norms of initial data
u_{0}
.
For
t\in[0,T]
, by the fundamental theorem of calculus in the time variable and using the product estimates from Lemma
1.1
, we obtain from (
1.4
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\left(3\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s}}+\alpha^{2}\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}u^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\left\|u\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\alpha\left\|(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s-1}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s+1}}+\alpha\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s-1}}\left\|\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}+\alpha\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\right),

(2.8)

where we have used that
H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\frac{3}{2}
.

Following the same procedure of estimates as above, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{s-1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\left(3\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s-1}}+\alpha^{2}\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}u^{2}\right\|_{H^{s-1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right\|_{H^{s-1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\left\|u\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s-1}}+\alpha\left\|(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s-1}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}+\alpha\right),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{s+1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\left(3\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s+1}}+\alpha^{2}\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}u^{2}\right\|_{H^{s+1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right\|_{H^{s+1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\left\|u\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s+1}}+\left\|(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s-1}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s+2}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\right).

(2.10)

Next, we estimate the
H^{s}
-norm for the term
u(t)-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}(\alpha,u_{0})
which can be bounded by
t^{2}

multiplying the Sobolev norms of initial data
u_{0}
. For
t\in[0,T]
, by the fundamental theorem of calculus in the time variable and using the product estimates from Lemma
1.1
again, we obtain from (
1.4
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}(\alpha,u_{0})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u-\mathbf{E}(\alpha,u_{0})\|_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\left(3\left\|u\partial_{x}u-u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}+\alpha^{2}\left\|\partial^{3}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}\left(u^{2}-u_{0}^{2}\right)\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}\left((\partial_{x}u)^{2}-(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}\right)\right\|_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\|u^{2}-u_{0}^{2}\right\|_{H^{s+1}}+\alpha\left\|(\partial_{x}u)^{2}-(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\left(\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s-1}}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\alpha\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\mathbf{F}(\alpha,u_{0}),

where we have used (
2.8
)-(
2.10
) in the last step.
Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Before proving Theorem
1.2
, we need to construct the initial data
u_{0}
. Firstly, we introduce smooth, radial cut-off functions to localize the frequency region. Precisely,
let
\widehat{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be an even, real-valued and non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfy

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=

\displaystyle 1,

if
|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{4}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=

\displaystyle 0,

if
|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Motivated by
[
17
]
, we establish the following crucial lemmas which will be used later on.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Define the high frequency function
f_{n}
and the low frequency function
g_{n}
by

\displaystyle f_{n}=2^{-ns}\phi(x)\sin\left(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\right),\quad n\gg 1,

\displaystyle g_{n}=2^{-n}\phi(x).

Then for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\phi)
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-ns},\quad\|\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n(s-1)},

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\approx 2^{n(\sigma-s)},\quad\|g_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\approx 2^{-n},

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\geq C.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
be defined as Lemma
2.1
. Assume that
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha_{n}=2^{-n}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(\phi)
such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n})\right\|_{H^{s}}\geq C.

Proof.

By the construction of
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
, one has (for more details see
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}}\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\ \frac{17}{12}2^{n}-1\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{17}{12}2^{n}+1\right\}.

By the Plancherel’s identity, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n})\right\|_{H^{s}}\approx\|g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{H^{s}}

Using Lemma
2.1
, we complete the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Now we begin to prove our main Theorem
1.2
.
Letting
\alpha_{n}=2^{-n}
.
We set the initial data
u^{n}_{0}=f_{n}+g_{n}
.
It is easy to show that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s+kn}}\lesssim 2^{kn}\quad\text{and}\quad\|g_{n}\|_{H^{s+kn}}\lesssim 2^{-n}\quad\text{for}\quad k\in\{-1,0,1,2\},

which gives directly that

\displaystyle\|u^{n}_{0}\|_{H^{s+kn}}\lesssim 2^{kn}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(0,u^{n}_{0})\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{F}(\alpha_{n},u^{n}_{0})\lesssim 1.

We decompose the solution
\mathbf{S}^{\alpha}_{t}(u_{0})
to (
1.4
) and the solution
\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u_{0})
to (
1.5
) into three parts, respectively

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})=u^{n}_{0}+\underbrace{\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}(\alpha_{n},u^{n}_{0})}_{=:\,\mathbf{I}_{1}}+t\mathbf{E}(\alpha_{n},u^{n}_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})=u^{n}_{0}+\underbrace{\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{E}(0,u^{n}_{0})}_{=:\,\mathbf{I}_{2}}+t\mathbf{E}(0,u^{n}_{0})\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(\alpha_{n},u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{E}(0,u^{n}_{0})=-2\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0})-\frac{\alpha^{2}_{n}}{2}\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0})^{2}.

Furthermore, notice that

u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0}=g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}+f_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}+u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}g_{n},

by Proposition
2.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})\right\|_{H^{s}}\geq~t\left\|\mathbf{E}(\alpha_{n},u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{E}(0,u^{n}_{0})\right\|_{H^{s}}-\left\|\mathbf{I}_{1}\right\|_{H^{s}}-\left\|\mathbf{I}_{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t\left(2\left\|\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0})\right\|_{H^{s}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}_{n}}{2}\left\|\partial_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(\partial_{x}u_{0})^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)-Ct^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle~t\left(\left\|\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial^{2}_{x}\left(1-\alpha^{2}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\right)^{-1}(g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n})\right\|_{H^{s}}-\left\|f_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\right\|_{H^{s}}-\left\|u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}g_{n}\right\|_{H^{s}}-2^{-n}\left\|(\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0})^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)-t^{2}.

(2.11)

Using Lemma
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
, after simple calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim 2^{-n(s-1)},

\displaystyle\left\|u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}g_{n}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u^{n}_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\|g_{n}\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim 2^{-n},

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0})^{2}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}u^{n}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u^{n}_{0}\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim 2^{n}(2^{-n}+2^{-n(s-1)})\lesssim 1+2^{-n(s-2)}.

Plugging the above estimates into (
2.11
) and combining Lemma
2.2
yields that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\mathbf{S}^{\alpha_{n}}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{S}^{0}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})\right\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim t\quad\text{for}\ t\ \text{small enough}.

This completes the proof of Theorem

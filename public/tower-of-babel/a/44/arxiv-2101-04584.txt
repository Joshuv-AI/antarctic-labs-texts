Sharp detection boundaries on testing dense subhypergraph
Mingao Yuan, Zuofeng Shang

Abstract
We study the problem of testing the existence of a dense subhypergraph. The null hypothesis is an Erdös-Rényi uniform random hypergraph and the alternative hypothesis is a uniform random hypergraph that contains a dense subhypergraph. We establish sharp detection boundaries in both scenarios: (1) the edge probabilities are known; (2) the edge probabilities are unknown. In both scenarios, sharp detectable boundaries are characterized by the appropriate model parameters. Asymptotically powerful tests are provided when the model parameters fall in the detectable regions. Our results indicate that the detectable regions for general hypergraph models are dramatically different from their graph counterparts.

Introduction

Suppose we observe an
undirected

m

(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
on

vertices with an edge set
\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{E}

m

In particular,
m=2
degenerates to ordinary graphs. Without loss of generality, denote
\mathcal{V}=[N]:=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}

The corresponding adjacency tensor is an
m

A\in(\{0,1\}^{N})^{\otimes m}
satisfying
A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}=1
if and only if
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}\}\in\mathcal{E}

By symmetry we mean that
A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}=A_{j_{1}j_{2}\ldots j_{m}}
whenever
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}
is a permutation of
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{m}

Given

, we are interested in testing the existence of
a dense subhypergraph, which can be formulated as a hypothesis testing problem:

H_{0}:A\sim\mathcal{H}_{m}(N,p_{0})\,\,\,\,\text{vs.}\,\,\,\,H_{1}:A\sim\mathcal{H}_{m}(N,p_{0};n,p_{1}),

(1)

where
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq 1

The null hypothesis in (
1

A
follows an
Erdös-Rényi
m
-uniform random hypergraph with edge rate
p_{0}

Bernoulli variables with
\mathbb{P}(A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}=1)=p_{0}

1

says that
A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}
for
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq N

there exists a subset
S\subset\mathcal{V}
with
|S|=n
such that
\mathbb{P}(A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}=1)=p_{1}
if the distinct vertices
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}
all belong to
S
, and
\mathbb{P}(A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}=1)=p_{0}
otherwise.
The assumption
p_{1}>p_{0}
implies that the vertices within
S
are more possibly connected. When
p_{1}=1

is called the hypergraphic planted clique (HPC),
and problem (

We borrow the terminology HPC from
[

When

4

However, it remains unknown whether sharp detection boundaries for testing

In this paper, we positively answer the above question and provide sharp
detection boundaries for testing the problem (

A statistical test

H_{0}
and
H_{1}
, respectively.
A test

is said to be asymptotically powerful (or asymptotically powerless) if

(or
\gamma_{N}(T)\to 1
).
If an asymptotically powerful test exists, we say that the dense subhypergraph is detectable; otherwise, it is undetectable.

2
Detection of HPC

Consider the problem of detecting HPC, i.e.,
p_{1}=1
in (
1
).
Define the hypergraphic clique number test (HCNT) as

\omega_{N}=\max\{0\leq k\leq N:V\subset[N],W_{V}=k^{(m)},|V|=k\}
, where

W_{V}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in V\\
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}
is the number of edges in the subhypergraph restricted to
V
.
The following theorem provides a sharp detection boundary characterized by
n,N,m,p_{0}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
. The following results hold.

(I)

All tests are asymptotically powerless if for a constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

n\leq\left[m!(1-\epsilon)\log_{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}N\right]^{\frac{1}{m-1}}.

(II)

The HCNT is asymptotically powerful if for a constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

n\geq\left[m!(1+\epsilon)\log_{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}N\right]^{\frac{1}{m-1}}.

(4)

For Part
(I)
, one can show that
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
are asymptotically mutually contiguous so that all tests are asymptotically powerless.
For Part
4
, one can show that
\gamma_{N}(\omega_{N})\to 1
so that HCNT is asymptotically powerful.
Bollobás and Erdös
[
9
]
showed that HPC can be detected through exhaustive search under (
4
), so Part
4
is nothing but a restatement of their result in hypothesis testing framework.
Part
(I)
says that this condition is necessary to ensure the existence of a successful test.

An interesting byproduct of Theorem
2.1
is to provide an understanding on the relationship between testing PC (
m=2
) and HPC (
m>2
).
Figure
1
demonstrates detection regions when
m=2,3
and
p_{0}=1/2
. The green color indicates regions where PC or HPC are detectable,
and the red color indicates regions where they are undetectable.
Interestingly,
m=3
yields a larger detectable region than
m=2
, displaying a dramatic difference between testing PC and testing HPC.

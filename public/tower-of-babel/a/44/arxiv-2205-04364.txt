Rational curves and the Hilbert Property on Jacobian Kummer varieties
Damián Gvirtz-Chen and Zhizhong Huang

Abstract
. A conjecture by Corvaja and Zannier predicts that smooth, projective, simply connected varieties over a number field with Zariski dense set of rational points have the Hilbert Property; this was proved by Demeio for Kummer surfaces which are associated to products of two elliptic curves. In this article, over a finitely generated field of characteristic zero, we establish the Hilbert Property for all Kummer surfaces associated to the Jacobian of a genus 2 2 curve. In general we prove that all Jacobian Kummer varieties associated to a hyperelliptic curve of genus ≥ 2 \geq 2 of odd degree also have the Hilbert Property.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
14G05, 11G35, 14J28

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to introduce the use of rational curves into the study of the Hilbert Property for non-unirational varieties.

1.1.
Background for the Hilbert Property

Let
X
be a quasi-projective integral variety of dimension at least one over a field
K
. Recall after
[
Ser08
, §3]
(see also
[
Col20
, §2]
) that a subset
A\subset X(K)
is
thin
if there exists a
K
-morphism
f:Y\to X
from a
K
-variety
Y
to
X
such that

•

A\subset f(Y(K))
;

•

the fibre of
f
over the generic point of
X
is finite, and
f
does not have any rational sections.

Definition 1.1

(
[
CS87
]
p. 189)
.

We say that
X
has the
Hilbert Property
(or
X
is
of Hilbert type
) if
X(K)
is not a thin set.

The field
K
is called
Hilbertian
if the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
satisfies the Hilbert Property. Not every base field is Hilbertian. The field of rational numbers, and more generally all number fields, are classically known to be Hilbertian by the Hilbert Irreducibility Theorem
[
Hil92
]
(see
[
Ser08
, Theorem 3.4.3]
). In this article, unless explicitly stated to the contrary, we will assume that
K
is a finitely generated field of characteristic
0
. These fields are known to be Hilbertian (see e.g.
[
Lan83
, Chapter 9]
and
[
FJ08
, §13.4]
).

It should be pointed out that
Definition

1.1
looks different from the one introduced later in
[
CZ17
, §1.1 Definition]
,
[
CDJLZ21
, Definition 1.1]
. We shall provide a proof (see
Proposition

2.1
and
Corollary

2.2
below) that all these definitions are actually equivalent.

It is clear from the definition that Zariski density of
X(K)
in
X
is a necessary condition for the Hilbert Property, and that the Hilbert Property is a birational invariant. Moreover, if
X
satisfies the Hilbert Property, then it is geometrically integral (see
[
Ser08
, p. 20]
).
The Hilbert Property is a specialisation of weak weak approximation (see
[
Ser08
, §3.5, Corollary 3.5.4, Definition 3.5.6]
and also
[
Col20
, §2 (AFF), Théorème 2.3]
) and provides an approach to solving the Inverse Galois Problem (see for example the book
[
Ser08
]
and notably
ibid.
, Theorem 3.5.9).

There is a well-known topological obstruction to the Hilbert Property which goes back to the Chevalley–Weil Theorem. Namely, a projective variety
X
cannot have the Hilbert Property if it is not algebraically simply connected (or equivalently for normal
X
, if its fundamental group has a proper subgroup of finite index), see
[
CZ17
, Theorem 1.4]
. Corvaja and Zannier conjecture that the converse is true.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
CZ17
]
, §2 Question-Conjecture 1)
.

If
X(K)
is Zariski dense and if
X
has a smooth, projective model over
K
which is algebraically simply connected, then X has the Hilbert Property.

While there have been quite a number of results confirming the weak weak approximation property for unirational varieties (they are simply connected by
[
Ser59
]
), as conjectured by Colliot-Thélène (see
[
CS87
]
,
[
Ser08
, Conjecture 3.5.8]
), it is an overarching challenge to establish the Hilbert Property for intermediate type varieties.
In particular Corvaja and Zannier ask for a proof that the Hilbert Property holds for Kummer surfaces with a Zariski dense set of rational points
[
CZ17
, Appendix 1]
. (See
[
HS16
, p. 817]
for a stronger conjecture of weak weak approximation on K3 surfaces and
[
HP15
]
for a consequence on the rank of quadratic twists of abelian varieties.)

1.2.
Main results and discussion

Let
H
be a smooth, projective hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 2
over a finitely generated field
K
of characteristic
0
. Let
\Jac(H)
be its Jacobian variety and let
\Kum(\Jac(H))
be the associated Kummer quotient, i.e. the quotient of
\Jac(H)
by the natural involution. The minimal desingularisation of
\Kum(\Jac(H))
is a simply connected variety of dimension
g
, called a
Jacobian Kummer variety
(see §
3.1
for details).

In dimension two, all principally polarised abelian surfaces over
K
are isomorphic to either the Jacobian of a genus
2
curve or, up to a quadratic extension, the product of two elliptic curves (see
[
GGR05
, Theorem 3.1]
). The Jacobian case is the generic one, in the sense of forming a Zariski dense open subvariety of the corresponding moduli space. Our main theorem confirms
1.2
in the outstanding Jacobian case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
H
be a smooth, projective genus
2
curve over
K
. Then the variety
\Kum(\Jac(H))
has the Hilbert Property.
1
1

1

Note that any smooth genus
2
curve is hyperelliptic.

The study of the Hilbert Property for K3 surfaces was first addressed by Corvaja and Zannier in
[
CZ17
]
, and they verified it for the diagonal quartic surface
x^{4}+y^{4}=z^{4}+w^{4}

[
CZ17
, Theorem 1.6]
using multiple elliptic fibrations. The same method was formalised and applied in the recent work of Demeio
[
Dem21
, Proposition 4.2.1]
. He establishes the Hilbert Property for the Kummer surface
\Kum(E_{1}\times E_{2})
associated to the product of two elliptic curves
E_{1},E_{2}
over
K
, under the assumption that rational points on
\Kum(E_{1}\times E_{2})
are Zariski dense. (Demeio’s theorem is stated over a number field and contains the stronger assumption that the elliptic curves have positive Mordell-Weil ranks. In fact, the proof of
[
Dem21
, Theorem 1.0.2]
works for any finitely generated field
K
of characteristic
0
and only assumes that the set of rational points is Zariski dense. This is known to hold unconditionally unless
j(E_{1})=j(E_{2})\in\{0,1728\}
, thanks to the work of Kuwata–Wang
[
KW93
]
.) The second author studied in
[
Hua21
]
various Kummer surfaces associated to torsors under the product of two elliptic curves, though the Hilbert Property for such surfaces remains still open. More recently, the first author and Mezzedimi
[
GM21
]
have shown that the Hilbert Property holds for Kummer surfaces if one allows a finite field extension of the base field
K
.

The elliptic fibrations (modulo automophisms) of a generic Jacobian Kummer surface (i.e.
\Jac(H)
has no extra endomorphisms) over an algebraically closed field have been classified by Kumar in
[
Kum14
]
. Unfortunately, in general none of these fibrations descend to
K
and thus, it is not possible to apply directly the method of
[
Dem21
]
over
K
. Instead, the novelty of our proofs of Theorem
1.3
is, amongst other things, the use of rational curves on
\Kum(\Jac(H))
.
To the authors’ knowledge, this is the first time that rational curves have been used to prove the Hilbert Property for non-unirational varieties.

In fact, our approach allows us to settle
1.2
for a family of higher-dimensional varieties.
We are not aware of any previous results on the Hilbert Property for non-unirational varieties of dimension
>2
, apart from naive constructions like product varieties.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
H
be a smooth, projective hyperelliptic curve over
K
of odd degree, i.e.,
H
is defined by an affine model
y^{2}=f(x)
with
f
separable of degree
2g+1
.
Then the variety
\Kum(\Jac(H))
has the Hilbert Property.

1.3.
Strategy and outline

In order to reduce the
a priori
unknown Hilbert Property of a higher-dimensional variety
X
to the known Hilbert Property of (possibly singular) rational subcurves on
X
, their behaviour under pullbacks along covers of
X
has to be understood. This is the criterion of
Proposition

5.1
which we formulate in a way tailored to our application to Jacobian Kummer varieties.

Let
\Alb^{1}(H)
be the (first) Albanese torsor of the hyperelliptic curve
H
, and let
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
be the associated Kummer quotient, i.e. the quotient of
\Alb^{1}(H)
by the natural involution. See §
3.2
for details. In general
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
is a non-isomorphic Galois twist of
\Kum(\Jac(H))
.
In practice, it turns out that working with
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
instead of
\Kum(\Jac(H))
is more useful for our purposes. The most general form of our main theorem is the following, from which Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
will follow as special cases.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
H
be a smooth, projective hyperelliptic curve over
K
.
Then the variety
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
has the Hilbert Property.

The variety
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
always possesses a countable family of rational curves, whose union is Zariski dense. To show that the conditions in the criterion of
Proposition

5.1
are satisfied for
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
, we prove Pullback Lemmas that deal with the three occurring types of covers of
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
:

(1)

covers that factor rationally through
\Alb^{1}(H)
;

(2)

covers that arise as Kummer quotients of étale covers of
\Alb^{1}(H)
;

(3)

covers that pull back to ramified covers of
\Alb^{1}(H)
.

While type (1) covers are relatively easy to treat, for the other types we rely on two separate methods: Type (2) covers are dealt with through a fundamental group argument supplemented by a
coprimality trick
. As for type (3) covers, we make use of the recently established
weak Hilbert Property for abelian varieties
due to Corvaja, Demeio, Javanpeykar, Lombardo and Zannier
[
CDJLZ21
]
, which guarantees the existence of many rational points avoiding these covers. To control the pullbacks of rational curves on
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
, we apply this result not to
\Alb^{1}(H)
itself but instead to the base change of
\Alb^{1}(H)
to the function field
L(H)
of
H
over some finite extension
L
of
K
.

Finally, for any given finite collection of these covers, we need to carefully match these methods together, so as to extract a subfamily of rational curves which simultaneously have no rational sections along any one of them. Thus
a fortiori
these curves contribute to many rational points not coming from these covers, thereby achieving the Hilbert Property for
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
. The deduction of the Hilbert Property for
\Kum(\Jac(H))
in the case
g=2
is based on the fact (due to Cassels and Flynn, see
Theorem

3.5
) that
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
and
\Kum(\Jac(H))
are birational to each other over
K
.

It is worth pointing out that, while the ideas of this article are applied to Jacobian Kummer varieties, we expect them to be far more fruitful beyond this particular case, especially when combined with
[
GM21
]
.

The article is structured as follows. After notation and background in §
2
, in §
3
we construct and prove properties of rational curves on
\Kum(\Jac(H))
and
\Kum(\Alb^{1}(H))
. In §
4
the crucial Pullback Lemmas are formulated for covers of types (1)-(3). These are applied in §
5
to prove the main theorems.

2.
Notation, conventions and preliminaries

A
variety
over
K
is a separated reduced
K
-scheme of dimension
>0
and of finite type. To simplify exposition, throughout the rest of this article all varieties are supposed to be integral. We write
K(X)
for the function field of
X
.

Let
\phi:Y\to X
be a morphism of varieties and let
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
be a subvariety. We say that
X^{\prime}

has a rational section along
\phi
, if the morphism
\phi|_{\phi^{-1}(X^{\prime})}:\phi^{-1}(X^{\prime})\to X^{\prime}
has a rational section, i.e. there exists an open dense subset
U^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
and
\psi:U^{\prime}\to Y
such that
\phi\circ\psi
is the identity map on
U^{\prime}
.

A
K
-variety birationally equivalent to
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{K}
is called
K
-
rational
. By a
rational curve
on a
K
-variety
X
, we mean a closed (possibly singular) subvariety on
X
which is birationally equivalent to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
. By a
hyperelliptic curve
over
K
, we mean a smooth, projective curve over
K
with a finite morphism to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
of degree
2
. (Note that a curve may be geometrically hyperelliptic but not hyperelliptic over
K
, the image of the canonical map being a conic without a
K
-rational point. This does not happen when
g
is even.)

Assume that a
K
-variety
V
is equipped with an involution
\tau:V\to V
, usually taken to be implicit from the context. Then we define the
Kummer quotient

\Kum(V)
as the (possibly singular) quotient of
V
by the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-action for which the group generator acts by
\tau
.

Let
(G,+)
be an abelian group. A
coset

{\mathcal{C}}
of
G
is of the form
g+H
where
g\in G
and
H\subset G
is subgroup.

Our next proposition says that proving the Hilbert Property boils down to a specific family of maps.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety over a field
K
. Then a subset
M\subset X(K)
is thin if and only if
there exists a finite collection
(\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to X)
, where
Y_{i}
are normal and geometrically integral varieties over
K
, and
\phi_{i}
are finite surjective
K
-morphisms of degree
[K(Y_{i}):K(X)]>1
, such that
M\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\phi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))
is not Zariski dense.

Proof.

The “if” part is clear (by considering the morphism from the disjoint union of
Y_{i}
with a Zariski closed subset of
X
to
X
).

We now prove the “only if” part. Namely, for every thin set
M\subset X(K)
, we want to construct such a finite collection
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to X
as above such that
M\setminus(\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\phi_{i}(Y_{i}(K)))
is contained in a proper Zariski closed subset.
We may therefore assume that

M=\phi(Y(K))
, where
Y
is an integral
K
-variety with
\dim Y=\dim X
, and
\phi:Y\to X
is a dominant
K
-morphism with degree
[K(Y):K(X)]>1
.
Let us fix a projective model
\overline{X}
of
X
in what follows.

By the Nagata compactification theorem (see
[
Lüt93
, Theorem 3.2]
),
\phi
extends to a proper morphism
\overline{\phi}:\overline{Y}\to\overline{X}
where
\overline{Y}
is a proper model of
Y
. Using the Stein factorization (
[
Har77
, III. Corollary 11.5]
), we can decompose
\overline{\phi}=g_{1}\circ g_{2}:\overline{Y}\to W\to\overline{X}
, where
g_{1}:\overline{Y}\to W
is proper with connected fibres and
g_{2}:W\to\overline{X}
is finite. It follows that
g_{1}
is birational, and
g_{2}
is proper by
[
Har77
, Exercise II 4.1]
) and hence it is surjective (since
\phi
is dominant). Let
Z
denotes the non-normal locus of
W
and let
h:\widehat{W}\to W
be the normalisation, which is finite birational. We may assume that
\widehat{W}(K)\neq\varnothing
and hence
\widehat{W}
is geometrically integral (see
[
Ser08
, p. 20]
). Otherwise
M
is contained in the Zariski closed subset
g_{2}(Z)
. We let
\widetilde{W}=\widehat{W}\setminus(h\circ g_{2})^{-1}(\overline{X}\setminus X)
. We claim that the morphism
h\circ g_{2}|_{\widetilde{W}}:\widetilde{W}\to X
meets our need. Indeed,
h\circ g_{2}
is finite surjective of degree
[K(W):K(X)]=[K(Y):K(X)]>1
, and
M\setminus((h\circ g_{2})(\widetilde{W}(K)))
is contained in the Zariski closed subset
g_{2}(Z)
.
∎

The proposition above motivates the following convention.

A
cover

\phi:Y\to X
between
K
-varieties is a finite surjective morphism with
Y
being normal and geometrically integral. The
degree
of
\phi
is
[K(Y):K(X)]
.

Therefore
Proposition

2.1
may be rephrased as follows:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety over a Hilbertian field
K
. Then
X
has the Hilbert Property over
K
if and only if for any finite family of covers
\phi_{i}\colon Y_{i}\to X
,
i=1,\dots,r
with
\deg(\phi_{i})>1
,
X(K)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\phi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))
is Zariski dense in
X
.

3.
Rational curves on Jacobian Kummer varieties

Let
H
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
over
K
. Recall the following classical facts about
H
, its Jacobian and Albanese varieties, and their Kummer quotients.

The hyperelliptic involution
\tau_{H}:H\to H
is defined over
K
. The Kummer quotient
C=\Kum(H)
is non-canonically isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
, and we write
\pi_{H}:H\to C
for the quotient morphism. The set
W\subset H(\overline{K})
of Weierstrass points of
H
, i.e. the fixed points under
\tau_{H}
, forms a zero-dimensional reduced
K
-subscheme of degree
2g+2
.

3.1.
Jacobian variety and rational curves on its Kummer quotient

The Jacobian
J=\Jac(H)
is a principally polarised abelian variety over
K
, even when
H(K)=\emptyset
. See e.g.
[
Mil86a
, Theorem 1.1, Summary 6.11]
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, write
[n_{J}]:J\to J
for the multiplication-by-
n
morphism, and for a closed point
x\in J
, write
t_{x}:J\to J
for the translation-by-
x
morphism. The fixed points of the involution
\tau_{J}=[(-1)_{J}]
consist precisely of the
2
-torsion
J[2]
, which is a zero-dimensional subgroup scheme of degree
2^{2g}
. Let
X=\Kum(J)
and
\pi:J\to X
be the quotient morphism. Clearly the morphisms
[n_{J}]
and
\tau_{J}
commute, so
[n_{J}]
descends to
X
and we denote the resulting morphism by
[n_{X}]
. The morphism
\pi
is étale outside the images of
J[2]
, which form the singular locus of
X
.
Blowing up these
2^{2g}
canonical singularities gives a minimal resolution
{\widetilde{X}}\to X
where
{\widetilde{X}}
is known as the
Kummer variety
of
J
. It is a smooth, projective and simply connected variety (see
[
Spa56
, Theorem 1]
).

For every
x_{0}\in H(K)
, there exists an Abel–Jacobi closed
K
-embedding

\displaystyle\iota_{x_{0}}:H

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow J,

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\langle x_{0}-x\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the class of a divisor. By the Riemann-Roch theorem, the closed points on
H
generate
J
(see
[
Har77
, IV Remark 4.10.9]
or
[
Mil86a
, §5]
).

We assume throughout the rest of §
3.1
that
H
has a
K
-rational Weierstrass point
\infty
.
We consider
H
as a subvariety of
J
via the Abel-Jacobi embedding
\iota_{\infty}
. Since the map
\tau_{J}
restricted to
H
is just
\tau_{H}
, the image of
H
under
\pi
is a smooth rational curve, which we denote by
C
. For every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, let
H_{n}=[n_{J}](H)
, the image of
H
under the map
[n_{J}]
, and let
C_{n}=\pi(H_{n})
. The curves

are rational for all

. (See e.g.
[
Huy16
, Chapter 13, Example 0.4]
.)

Lemma 3.1
.

The family
(H_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
consists of pairwise distinct curves. Consequently,
(C_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are pairwise distinct rational curves on
X
.

Proof.

Assume
H_{m}=H_{n}
for some
0<m<n
. Then there is an automorphism
\sigma
of
H_{n}
such that

[m_{J}]|_{H}=\sigma\circ[n_{J}]|_{H}.

Since
g\geq 2
, the order of
\sigma
is finite, so we may assume that
[m_{J}]|_{H}=[n_{J}]|_{H}
. It follows that
H\subset\Ker([(n-m)_{J}])\subset J
, which is impossible since the closed points of
H
generate
J
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We would like to point out an oversight in
[
Huy16
, p. 274]
, where it is stated that when
g=2
, the rational curve
C_{n}
is smooth for all

. In fact this is false for

and it is for this reason that we will need the coprimality argument in
Proposition

4.4
. If
C_{n}
were smooth, then since it is an ample geometrically irreducible divisor of

, its pullback along
[n_{J}]
would also be geometrically irreducible by
[
BL04
, Corollary 4.3.4]
. But this is clearly not the case since it is (geometrically) a sum of translates of

. See also
Remark

4.3

More concretely, let us take a look at the curve
C_{2}
. Since
H
contains
6
points of
J[2]
, then
H_{2}=[2_{J}](H)
passes through
0\in J
with multiplicity at least
6
. Hence
C_{2}
has multiplicity as least
3
at
\pi(0)
. In general, the singular locus of
H_{n}
(resp.
C_{n}
) is the closed subscheme of all points
[n_{J}]x
(resp.
[n_{X}]\pi(x)
),
x\in H
, such that there exists

different from
x
with
[n_{J}](x-y)=0
. In other words,

is in a
torsion package
of size
>1
in the sense of
[
BP01
]
. We omit the proof since this fact will not be needed.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\eta_{H}\in J_{K(H)}
be the image of the generic point of
H
under the embedding
\iota_{\infty}:H\hookrightarrow J
.
Then
\eta_{H}
is non-degenerate in
J_{K(H)}
, i.e.
{\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{H}
is Zariski dense in
J_{K(H)}
.

Proof.

Assume instead that
\eta_{H}
is contained in a proper abelian subvariety of
J_{K(H)}
. Then by the Poincaré irreducibility theorem, there exists a non-zero endomorphism
e\in\End(J_{\overline{K(H)}})
(actually in
\End(J_{K(H)})
) such that
e\eta_{H}=0
. Because
\End(J_{\overline{K(H)}})\simeq\End(J_{\overline{K}})
, see
[
CCO14
, Lemma 1.2.1.2]
, it follows that there exists
e^{\prime}\in\End(J_{\overline{K}})
such that
e^{\prime}s(x)=e^{\prime}x=0
for all
x\in H
. Thus
H
would have to be contained in a proper abelian subvariety of
J
, which is impossible again since the closed points of
H
generate
J
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For every finite index coset

,
\bigcup_{n\in{\mathcal{N}}}H_{n}
is Zariski dense in

and
\bigcup_{n\in{\mathcal{N}}}C_{n}
is Zariski dense in

.

Proof.

Write
{\mathcal{N}}=a{\mathbb{Z}}+b
where

are integers with

. Since the morphism
t_{b\eta_{H}}\circ[a_{J_{K(H)}}]:J_{K(H)}\to J_{K(H)}
is dominant, the conclusion follows directly from

3.3
.
∎

3.2.
Albanese torsor and its Kummer quotient

The zero-cycles of degree

on

form a torsor

, which is trivial if

4.
Pullbacks along covers

Throughout this section,
K
denotes a finitely generated field of characteristic zero and
X=\Kum(J)
denotes the Kummer quotient associated to the Jacobian
J
of a genus
g

H

K

.
We continue to consider

4.1.
Pullback along covers factoring through the Jacobian

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\phi:Y\to X
be a cover of degree
>1
. Suppose that the base change variety
Y\times_{X}J
is not geometrically integral. Then for every finite index coset
{\mathcal{N}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
, there exists a subcoset
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{N}}
of finite index, such that none of the rational curves
(C_{n})_{n\in{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}}
have a rational section along
\phi
over
\overline{K}
.

Proof.

We may base change everything to
\overline{K}
and we fix an algebraic closure
\Omega
of
\overline{K}(X)
. We have that
Y_{\overline{K}}\times_{X_{\overline{K}}}J_{\overline{K}}
is not geometrically integral if and only if
\overline{K}(Y)\otimes_{\overline{K}(X)}\overline{K}(J)
is not a field, or equivalently, the field extensions
\overline{K}(Y)
and
\overline{K}(J)
in
\Omega
are not linearly disjoint over
\overline{K}(X)
. Since
[\overline{K}(Y)\cdot\overline{K}(J):\overline{K}(Y)]\leq[\overline{K}(J):\overline{K}(X)]=2
, this happens if and only if
[\overline{K}(Y)\cdot\overline{K}(J):\overline{K}(Y)]<2
, or equivalently
\overline{K}(J)\subseteq\overline{K}(Y)
. It follows that, over
\overline{K}
,
\phi:Y\to X
factors as a rational map
\phi_{1}:Y\dashrightarrow J
then to
X
.

By
Lemma

3.3
, we fix a closed point
P\in H
which is non-degenerate in
J
. By
[
CDJLZ21
, Lemma 4.6]
, there exists a finite index subcoset
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{N}}
such that the set
\{nP,n\in{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}\}
does not intersect with the indeterminacy locus, say
Z\subset J
, of
\phi_{1}
. This implies that none of the curves
(H_{n})_{n\in{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}}
is contained in
Z
. If a rational curve
(C_{n})_{\overline{K}}\subset X_{\overline{K}},n\in{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}
has a rational section along
\phi
, say
\widetilde{\phi}
, then it has a rational section
\phi_{1}\circ\widetilde{\phi}
along
\pi
. This is impossible since there are no non-constant rational maps from the projective line to abelian varieties (see
[
Mil86
, Corollary 3.8]
).
∎

4.2.
Pullbacks along unramified covers

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
D
be a smooth, projective curve of genus
\geq 1
over a separably closed field. Fix a point
x
in
D
inducing the Abel–Jacobi embedding
\iota_{x}:D\hookrightarrow\Jac(D)
. Let
\pi_{1}(D,x)
(resp.
\pi_{1}(\Jac(D),0)
) be the pointed étale fundamental group of
D
(resp.
\Jac(D)
). Then the map

(\iota_{x})_{*}:\pi_{1}(D,x)\to\pi_{1}(\Jac(D),0)

induced by
\iota_{x}
is surjective. Consequently, every pullback via
\iota_{x}
of a connected finite étale covering of
\Jac(D)
remains connected as a finite étale covering over
D
.

Proof.

This is explained in
[
Mil86a
, Proposition 9.1]
.
Indeed,
(\iota_{x})_{*}
induces an isomorphism

\pi_{1}(D,x)^{\operatorname{ab}}\to\pi_{1}(\Jac(D),0),

where
\pi_{1}(D,x)^{\operatorname{ab}}
is the maximal abelian quotient of
\pi_{1}(D,x)
and in particular
(\iota_{x})_{*}
is surjective. Thus by the theory of Galois categories, all connected finite étale coverings of
\Jac(D)
have abelian Galois group, and the surjectivity of
(\iota_{x})_{*}
implies that all connected finite abelian coverings of
D
are obtained precisely by pulling back the connected finite étale coverings of
J
. See also
[
Sza09
, Proposition 5.5.4 (2)]
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If
g=2
, one may also prove
Lemma

4.2
by using the fact that the pullback of a geometrically irreducible, unibranch, ample divisor on an abelian variety along an isogeny is again geometrically irreducible. (See
[
BL04
, Corollary 4.3.4]
. Note that the statement is missing the crucial unibranch (or at the least smoothness) assumption.)

Lemma

4.2
applies to
H\hookrightarrow J
, while an analogous statement for
H_{n}
,
n>1
fails because of the non-smoothness. However, one can remedy the situation by restricting to a subfamily:

Proposition 4.4

(“Coprimality trick”)
.

Let
\phi:J^{\prime}\to J
be an unramified cover. Then
\phi^{-1}(H_{n})
are geometrically irreducible for all
n
coprime to
\deg(\phi)
.

Proof.

We may base change everything to
\overline{K}
, without affecting the geometrical irreducibility. By the Lang–Serre theorem
[
Ser88
, Chapter I §2]
,
J^{\prime}
is an abelian variety and
\phi
can be made into an isogeny. The case where
\deg(\phi)=1
is clear and so we assume from now on that
\deg(\phi)>1
.

If
n=1
, then the claim follows from
Lemma

4.2
. In particular,
\Ker(\phi)\subset\phi^{-1}(H_{1})
. In general, if
n
is coprime to
\deg(\phi)=\deg(\Ker(\phi))
, then
[n_{J^{\prime}}]|_{\Ker\phi}
is an isomorphism. Thus

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}(H_{n})

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}([n_{J}](H_{1}))

\displaystyle=[n_{J^{\prime}}](\phi^{-1}(H_{1}))+\Ker(\phi)

\displaystyle=[n_{J^{\prime}}]\left(\phi^{-1}(H_{1})+\Ker(\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=[n_{J^{\prime}}](\phi^{-1}(H_{1})),

and hence

\phi^{-1}(H_{n})
is geometrically irreducible.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to X,i\in I
be any finite collection of covers of with

such that

to

.
∎

5.
Hilbert Property for Jacobian Kummer varieties

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety over a Hilbertian field
K
. Assume that there exists an infinite set
{\mathcal{C}}
of (not necessarily smooth)

For every finite family of covers
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to X

\deg(\phi_{i})>1
, there exists a subset
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{C}}

\bigcup_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}C

and for all
C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
and

C
does not have a rational section along
\phi_{i}
.

Then

has the Hilbert Property.

Proof.

Let us fix a finite family of covers
\phi_{i}:Y_{i}\to X
,

with
\deg(\phi_{i})>1
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
be as stated in the proposition. Because the union of
C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is Zariski dense, upon replacing
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
by a subset if necessary, we may assume that for every subvariety
C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
,

is not contained in the ramification locus of any
\phi_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r
.
In view of
Proposition

2.1
, to show the Hilbert Property for
X
, it suffices to show that

\left(\bigcup_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}}C(K)\right)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\phi_{i}(Y_{i}(K))\right)

is not contained in any proper Zariski closed subset of
X
.

Assume that there exists
Z\subset X
proper Zariski closed containing all the
K
-points above. We fix
C_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
such that
C_{0}\not\subset Z
. For every

, let
{C_{ij}}
,

, be the irreducible components of
\phi_{i}^{-1}(C_{0})
.
The maps

are finite morphisms because

by assumption, if

is not thin, whence

Generalized Clifford Algebras as Algebras in Suitable Symmetric Linear Gr-Categories
Tao CHENG †‡, Hua-Lin HUANG † and Yuping YANG †

Abstract
By viewing Clifford algebras as algebras in some suitable symmetric Gr-categories, Albuquerque and Majid were able to give a new derivation of some well known results about Clifford algebras and to generalize them. Along the same line, Bulacu observed that Clifford algebras are weak Hopf algebras in the aforementioned categories and obtained other interesting properties. The aim of this paper is to study generalized Clifford algebras in a similar manner and extend the results of Albuquerque, Majid and Bulacu to the generalized setting. In particular, by taking full advantage of the gauge transformations in symmetric linear Gr-categories, we derive the decomposition theorem and provide categorical weak Hopf structures for generalized Clifford algebras in a conceptual and simpler manner.

Introduction

Clifford algebras and their generalizations have played important roles in various branches of science and engineering. Though they have been studied in depth for many years, some new ideas still get involved and novel properties and applications are found. In recent years, with the pioneering work of Albuquerque and Majid
[
1
,
2
]
, Clifford algebras can be seen as algebras in some suitable symmetric Gr-categories. Many well known results about Clifford algebras thus can be derived by some novel ideas arising from the theory of tensor categories and this viewpoint also helps to make natural generalization. Along the same line, Bulacu observed that Clifford algebras are weak Hopf algebras in the aforementioned categories and obtained other interesting properties, see
[
4
]
.

The aim of this paper is to study generalized Clifford algebras, written simply GCAs in the following, in a similar manner and extend the results of Albuquerque, Majid
[
1
,
2
]
and Bulacu
[
3
,
4
]
to the generalized setting. We provide an explicit formula of 2-cocycles to present GCAs as twisted group algebras. This helps us to put them inside suitable symmetric Gr-categories and apply the working philosophy of Albuquerque–Majid. In particular, by taking full advantage of the gauge transformations in symmetric linear Gr-categories, we derive the decomposition theorem and provide completely the categorical weak Hopf structures for GCAs in a conceptual and simpler manner.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the notion of symmetric linear Gr-categories and some examples of algebras inside them. The presentation of GCAs as explicit twisted group algebras is given in Section
3
. The well known decomposition theorem of GCAs are also derived conceptually and in a very simple manner with a help of suitable gauge transformations. Section
4
is devoted to the categorical weak Hopf structures of GCAs. The complete determination of the weak Hopf structures of GCAs in some suitable symmetric Gr-categories is translated to a much simpler situation via gauge transformations. This simplifies, improves and generalizes the main results of
[
3
,
4
]
.

Throughout the paper, let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field and let
\mathbbm{k}^{*}
denote the multiplicative group
\mathbbm{k}{\setminus}\{0\}
. Fix a positive integer
n\geq 2
. Always assume that there exists in
\mathbbm{k}
a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\omega
and
\operatorname{char}\mathbbm{k}\nmid n
. By
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1},\dots,\overline{n-1}\}
we denote the cyclic group of order
n
written additively.

2
Symmetric linear Gr-categories and algebras therein

The aim of this section is to collect some preliminary concepts and notations about symmetric linear Gr-categories and various algebraic structures within them.

2.1
Symmetric linear Gr-categories

Let
G
be a group with unit
e
and
\mathbbm{k}
a field. By
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}
we denote the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbbm{k}
-spaces graded by the group
G
. Adorned with the usual tensor product of
G
-graded vector spaces and an associativity constraint given by a normalized 3-cocycle
\Phi
on
G
, i.e., a function
\Phi\colon G\times G\times G\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}^{*}
such that for all
x,y,z,t\in G

\displaystyle\Phi(y,z,t)\Phi(x,yz,t)\Phi(x,y,z)=\Phi(x,y,zt)\Phi(xy,z,t)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\Phi(x,e,y)=1,

then
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}
becomes a tensor category which is called a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear Gr-category over
G
and is denoted by
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
in the rest of the paper. When
\Phi
vanishes, i.e.,
\Phi(x,y,z)\equiv 1
, we write simply
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}
. A linear Gr-category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
is said to be braided, if there exists a braiding given by a quasi-bicharacter
\mathcal{R}
with respect to
\Phi
, that is a function
\mathcal{R}\colon G\times G\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}^{*}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(xy,z)=\mathcal{R}(x,z)\mathcal{R}(y,z)\frac{\Phi(z,x,y)\Phi(x,y,z)}{\Phi(x,z,y)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x,yz)=\mathcal{R}(x,y)\mathcal{R}(x,z)\frac{\Phi(y,x,z)}{\Phi(y,z,x)\Phi(x,y,z)}

for all
x,y,z\in G
. Note that a necessary condition for
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
being braided is that
G
is abelian. A symmetric Gr-category is a braided Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R})
in which the braiding
\mathcal{R}
is symmetric, in other words,
\mathcal{R}(x,y)\mathcal{R}(y,x)=1
for any
x,y\in G
. Note that, symmetric braidings of
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}
are nothing other than the familiar symmetric bicharachers of
G
.

For more details on symmetric linear Gr-categories, the reader is referred to
[
6
,
7
]
.

2.2
Algebras and coalgebras in Gr-categories

An algebra in the linear Gr-category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
is an object
A
with a multiplication morphism
m\colon A\otimes A\to A
and a unit morphism
u\colon\mathbbm{k}\to A
satisfying the associativity and the unitary conditions of usual associative algebras but expressed in diagrams of the category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
. More precisely, an algebra
A
in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
is a finite-dimensional
G
-graded space
\oplus_{g\in G}A_{g}
with a multiplication
\cdot
such that

\displaystyle A_{g}\cdot A_{h}\subseteq A_{gh},\qquad(a\cdot b)\cdot c=\Phi(|a|,|b|,|c|)a\cdot(b\cdot c)

for all homogeneous elements
a,b,c\in A
. Here and below,
|a|
denotes the
G
-degree of
a
. There is also a unit
element
1
in
A
such that

\displaystyle 1\cdot a=a=a\cdot 1

for all
a\in A
. Further, if the Gr-category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
is braided with braiding
\mathcal{R}
, then we say that two homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
are commutative if

\displaystyle a\cdot b=\mathcal{R}(|a|,|b|)b\cdot a.

If any two homogeneous elements commute, then we say that
A
is a commutative algebra in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
.

Dually, a coalgebra in the linear Gr-category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
is an object
C

with a comultiplication morphism
\Delta\colon C\to C\otimes C
and a counit
morphism
\varepsilon\colon C\to\mathbbm{k}
satisfying the coassociativity and counitary
axioms in the category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
.

We remark that algebras in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
are also called
G
-graded quasialgebras in
[
1
]
. In addition, morphisms for algebras and coalgebras in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
can be defined in an obvious manner and thus we omit further details.

2.3
Twisted group algebras

Algebras in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
may be viewed as a natural generalization of the familiar twisted group algebras. Let
G
be a group and
F
a normalized 2-cochain on
G
, i.e., a function
F\colon G\times G\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}^{*}
with
F(e,x)=F(x,e)=1
. Then we can define a twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[G]
which has the same vector space as the group algebra
\mathbbm{k}[G]
but a different product twisted by
F
, namely

\displaystyle g\star h=F(g,h)gh,\qquad\forall\,g,h\in G.

Clearly,
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[G]
is an algebra in the linear Gr-category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\partial F}
, where

\displaystyle\partial F(x,y,z)=\frac{F(x,y)F(xy,z)}{F(x,yz)F(y,z)}

is the differential of
F
. Note that, the twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[G]
is associative in the usual sense if and only if
F
is a 2-cocycle, i.e.,
\partial F=0
, on
G
. Furthermore, if
G
is abelian then
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\partial F}
is symmetric with braiding given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{F}(g,h)=\frac{F(g,h)}{F(h,g)},\qquad\forall\,g,h\in G,

and
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[G]
is commutative in the symmetric Gr-category
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\partial F},\mathcal{R}_{F}\big)
.

Many interesting algebras appear as twisted group algebras. Here we recall some examples presented in
[
1
,
2
,
15
]
. Let
\mathbbm{R}
denote the field of real numbers,
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
the cyclic group of order 2, and
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}
the direct product of
n
copies of
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
. Elements of
\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}
are written as
n
-tuples of
\{0,1\}
and the group product is written as
+
. Define functions
f_{m}\colon\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
for all
1\leq m\leq 3
by

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,y)=\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i},\qquad f_{2}(x,y)=\sum_{i<j}x_{i}y_{j},\qquad f_{3}(x,y)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\rm distinct}\ i,\,j,\,k\\
i<j\end{subarray}}x_{i}x_{j}y_{k}.

1.

Let
F_{\rm Cl}\colon\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbbm{R}^{*}
be a function defined by

\displaystyle F_{\rm Cl}(x,y)=(-1)^{f_{1}(x,y)+f_{2}(x,y)}.

Then the associated twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{R}_{F_{\rm Cl}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}]
is
the well-known real Clifford algebra
{\rm Cl}_{0,n}
, see
[
2
]
for detail.
This recovers the algebra of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
when
n=1
and the algebra of quaternions
\mathbb{H}
when
n=2
.
Note that
{\rm Cl}_{0,n}
is associative in the usual sense since the function
F_{\rm Cl}
is a 2-cocycle.

2.

Assume
n\geq 3
. Define the function
F_{\mathbb{O}}\colon\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbbm{R}^{*}
by

\displaystyle F_{\mathbb{O}}(x,y)=(-1)^{f_{1}(x,y)+f_{2}(x,y)+f_{3}(x,y)}.

Then the twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{R}_{F_{\mathbb{O}}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}]
is the algebra of higher octonions
\mathbb{O}_{n}

introduced in
[
15
]
by generalizing the realization of octonions via twisted group algebras
(i.e., the case of
n=3
) observed in
[
1
]
.
Clearly, the series of algebras
\mathbb{O}_{n}
are nonassociative in the usual sense as the function
F_{\mathbb{O}}
is not a 2-cocycle.

2.4
Braided tensor products

If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are two algebras in a symmetric linear Gr-category
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
, then there is a natural braided tensor product algebra
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}A_{2}
living in same category with product given by

\displaystyle\big(a\widehat{\otimes}a^{\prime}\big)\big(b\widehat{\otimes}b^{\prime}\big)=\frac{\Phi(|a|,|a^{\prime}|,|b|)\Phi(|a||b|,|a^{\prime}|,|b^{\prime}|)}{\Phi(|a||a^{\prime}|,|b|,|b^{\prime}|)\Phi(|a|,|b|,|a^{\prime}|)}\mathcal{R}(|a^{\prime}|,|b|)a\cdot b\widehat{\otimes}a^{\prime}\cdot b^{\prime},

for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A_{1}
and
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in A_{2}
. Moreover, if
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
A_{3}
are algebras in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
, then

\big(A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}A_{2}\big)\widehat{\otimes}A_{3}
is naturally isomorphic to
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\big(A_{2}\widehat{\otimes}A_{3}\big)
. Thus in the following, for brevity we will omit the parentheses for multiple braided tensor product of algebras in symmetric tensor categories.

Dually, if
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two coalgebras in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
, then there is a braided tensor product coalgebra
C_{1}\widehat{\otimes}C_{2}
in the same category with coproduct defined by

\displaystyle\Delta\big(a\widehat{\otimes}b\big)=\frac{\Phi(|a_{1}|,|a_{2}|,|b_{1}|)\Phi(|a_{1}||b_{1}|,|a_{2}|,|b_{2}|)}{\Phi(|a_{1}||a_{2}|,|b_{1}|,|b_{2}|)\Phi(|a_{1}|,|b_{1}|,|a_{2}|)}\mathcal{R}(|a_{2}|,|b_{1}|)(a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}b_{1})\otimes(a_{2}\widehat{\otimes}b_{2}),

for all homogeneous
a\in C_{1}
,
b\in C_{2}
. Here and in the following we use Sweedler’s sigma notation
\Delta(a)=a_{1}\otimes a_{2}
for coproduct.

2.5
Weak Hopf algebras in symmetric Gr-categories

With braided tensor product, we can define Hopf algebras and their various generalizations in symmetric Gr-categories. Of course, one may even define these algebraic structures in general braided tensor categories, see
[
12
,
13
]
.
For simplicity and for our purpose, we only recall the notion of weak Hopf algebras in symmetric Gr-categories
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Call
(H,m,\mu,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
a weak Hopf algebra in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
, if

(1)

(H,m,\mu)
is an algebra in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
;

(2)

(H,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a coalgebra in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
;

(3)

\Delta\colon H\longrightarrow H\widehat{\otimes}H
is multiplicative, i.e.,
\Delta(xy)=\Delta(x)\Delta(y)
,
\forall\,x,y\in H
;

(4)

the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\varepsilon((fg)h)=\Phi(|fg_{1}|,|g_{2}|,|h|)\Phi^{-1}(|f|,|g_{1}|,|g_{2}|)\varepsilon(fg_{1})\varepsilon(g_{2}h)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\varepsilon((fg)h)}{}=\Phi(|fg_{2}|,|g_{1}|,|h|)\Phi^{-1}(|f|,|g_{2}|,|g_{1}|)\mathcal{R}(|g_{1}|,|g_{2}|)\varepsilon(fg_{2})\varepsilon(g_{1}h)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\varepsilon((fg)h)=}{}\ \forall\ \mathrm{homogeneous\ elements}\ f,g,h\in H,

(2.1)

\displaystyle(1_{11}\otimes 1_{12})\otimes 1_{2}=\Phi(|1_{1}|,|1_{2}|,|1_{(1)}|)(1_{1}\otimes 1_{2}1_{(1)})\otimes 1_{(2)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(1_{11}\otimes 1_{12})\otimes 1_{2}}{}=\Phi(|1_{1}|,|1_{2}|,|1_{(1)}|)\mathcal{R}(|1_{2}|,|1_{(1)}|)(1_{1}\otimes 1_{(1)}1_{2})\otimes 1_{(2)},

(2.2)

where we write
\Delta(1)=1_{1}\otimes 1_{2}=1_{(1)}\otimes 1_{(2)}
;

(5)

S\colon H\longrightarrow H
is a morphism satisfying

\displaystyle h_{1}S(h_{2})=\Phi(|1_{1}|,|1_{2}|,|h|)\Phi^{-1}(|1_{1}|,|h|,|1_{2}|)\mathcal{R}(|1_{2}|,|h|)\varepsilon(1_{1}h)1_{2},

\displaystyle S(h_{1})h_{2}=\Phi^{-1}(|h|,|1_{1}|,|1_{2}|)\Phi(|1_{1}|,|h|,|1_{2}|)\mathcal{R}(|h|,|1_{1}|)1_{1}\varepsilon(h1_{2}),

\displaystyle S(h)=(S(h_{1})h_{2})S(h_{3})

for all homogeneous elements
h\in H
.

Note that a weak Hopf algebra in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
is a Hopf algebra in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
if and only if the comultiplication is unit-preserving, and if and only if the counit is a morphism of algebras in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
. Moreover, if
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are (weak) Hopf algebras in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)
, then so is
H_{1}\widehat{\otimes}H_{2}
.

2.6
Gauge transformations

When considering algebras in symmetric linear Gr-categories, we are entitled to apply the powerful idea of gauge transformations invented for general quasi-Hopf algebras by Drinfeld
[
5
]
. In fact, this point of view is crucial in the pioneering works of Albuquerque and Majid
[
1
,
2
]
. For the purpose of the present paper, it suffices to recall gauge transformations in the following special situation.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and
\Phi
a 3-cocycle on
G
. It is well known that
\mathbbm{k}[G]
has a natural Hopf algebra structure. If we extend
\Phi
trilinearly to
(\mathbbm{k}[G])^{\otimes 3}
, then we have a dual quasi-Hopf algebra
(\mathbbm{k}[G],\Phi)
where
\Phi
is an associator, see
[
1
]
. In this terminology, the linear Gr-category
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
is exactly the comodule category of
(\mathbbm{k}[G],\Phi)
. By definition, a gauge transformation, or a twisting, on the dual quasi-Hopf algebra
(\mathbbm{k}G,\Phi)
is a convolution-invertible linear map
F\colon\mathbbm{k}[G]\otimes\mathbbm{k}[G]\to\mathbbm{k}
satisfying
F(g,e)=F(e,g)=1
for all
g\in G
. In other words,
F
is a bilinear expansion of a normalized 2-cochain on
G
. Given a gauge transformation
F
on
Q=(\mathbbm{k}[G],\Phi)
, one can define a new dual quasi-Hopf algebra
Q_{F}=(\mathbbm{k}[G],\Phi\partial F)
, see
[
1
]
for more details. The corresponding linear Gr-category of
(\mathbbm{k}[G],\Phi_{F})
is
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
, which is tensor equivalent to
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
. The tensor equivalence is given by
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{0},\varphi_{2})\colon\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(U)=U,\qquad\varphi_{0}=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbbm{k}},\qquad\varphi_{2}\colon\ U\otimes V\longrightarrow U\otimes V,\qquad u\otimes v\mapsto F(x,y)u\otimes v

for all
U,V
and
u\in U_{x}
,
v\in V_{y}
. The tensor equivalence
\mathcal{F}
also induces a one-to-one correspondence between the set of braidings of
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
and that of
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
. Given a braiding
\mathcal{R}
of
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
, define
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{R})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{R})(x,y)=\frac{F(x,y)}{F(y,x)}\mathcal{R}(x,y),\qquad\forall\,x,y\in G.

Then
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{R})
is a braiding of
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{0},\varphi_{2})\colon\ \big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi},\mathcal{R}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F},\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{R})\big)

is a braided tensor equivalence. The reader is referred to
[
1
,
9
,
13
]
for unexplained concepts and notations.

Clearly, if
A
is an algebra in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
, then
A_{F}:=\mathcal{F}(A)
is an algebra in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
. Note that the multiplication of
A_{F}
is given by

\displaystyle x\cdot_{{}_{F}}y=F(|x|,|y|)x\cdot y

for all homogeneous
x,y\in A
. Similarly, if
C
is a coalgebra in

\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
, then
C_{F}:=\mathcal{F}(C)
is a coalgebra in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
with twisted comultiplication

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}(u)=F(|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|)^{-1}u_{1}\otimes_{F}u_{2}

for all homogeneous
u\in C
. Moreover, if
H
is a (weak) Hopf algebra in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
, then
H_{F}:=\mathcal{F}(H)
is a (weak) Hopf algebra in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
, etc. The merit of these gauge transformations lies in that, studying an algebra
A
in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
amounts to studying the algebra
A_{F}
in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
. By choosing suitable twisting
F
, there might be a chance to gauge transform an algebra
A
in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi}
to a simpler algebra
A_{F}
in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\Phi\partial F}
.

3
GCAs as algebras in symmetric Gr-categories

In this section, we will show how to regard generalized Clifford algebras as algebras in some suitable symmetric linear Gr-categories and apply this point of view to derive, in an easy manner, the well known theorem of decomposition for GCAs via gauge transformations.

3.1
Generalized Clifford algebras

Various notions of GCAs appear in the literature, see for example
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. In the present paper, we adopt the following concept which seems to be of the most general form. Assume
q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. By definition, the generalized Clifford algebra
C^{(n)}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m})
is the associative
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra with unit
1
generated by
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m}
subject to the relations

\displaystyle e_{i}^{n}=q_{i}1,\qquad\forall\,i\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad e_{i}e_{j}=\omega e_{j}e_{i},\qquad\forall\,i>j.

3.2
Formulas of 2-cocycles

By
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
we denote the group
\underbrace{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbbm{Z}_{n}}_{m}
and by
[a]
the integer part of a rational number
a
. Let
0\leq x^{\prime}\leq n-1
denote the residue of the integer
x
divided by
n
. Elements
g\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
are written in the form
(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{m})
and the product of
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
is written as
+
.

Take a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\omega
and fix
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. Define
F\colon\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}\times\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}^{*}
by

\displaystyle F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)=\omega^{\sum\limits_{1\leq j<i\leq m}g_{i}h_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{[\frac{g_{i}+h_{i}}{n}]}

(3.1)

for all
g,h\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
. It is easy to see that
F(g,0)=F(0,g)=1
, and that

\displaystyle\partial F_{\rm GCA}(f,g,h)=\frac{F_{\rm GCA}(f,g)F_{\rm GCA}(f+g,h)}{F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)F_{\rm GCA}(f,g+h)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\partial F_{\rm GCA}(f,g,h)}{}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{[\frac{f_{i}+g_{i}}{n}]+[\frac{(f_{i}+g_{i})^{\prime}+h_{i}}{n}]-[\frac{g_{i}+h_{i}}{n}]-[\frac{f_{i}+(g_{i}+h_{i})^{\prime}}{n}]}=1.

Hence
F_{\rm GCA}
is a 2-cocycle on
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA}(g,h)=\frac{F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)}{F_{\rm GCA}(h,g)}=\omega^{\sum\limits_{1\leq j<i\leq m}(g_{i}h_{j}-g_{j}h_{i})}

(3.2)

and clearly it is a symmetric braiding in
\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}}
. By Section
2.3
,
the twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
is a commutative algebra in the symmetric Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA})
.
For the convenience of exposition, we write
u_{g}
for the element of
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
corresponding to
g\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
. Then the product on
\mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]

is given by

\displaystyle u_{g}\star u_{h}=F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)u_{g+h},

where
g,h\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
and
g+h=((g_{1}+h_{1})^{\prime},(g_{2}+h_{2})^{\prime},\dots,(g_{m}+h_{m})^{\prime})\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
.

3.3
GCAs as twisted group algebras

Keep all the notations of the previous two subsections. Now we have an explicit realization of
C^{(n)}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m})
as a twisted group algebra.

Proposition 3.1
.

\mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
and
C^{(n)}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m})
are isomorphic as associative
\mathbbm{k}
-algebras. Consequently, the latter is an algebra in the symmetric Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}^{m}_{n}},\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
C^{(n)}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m})
has a basis

\big\{e_{1}^{g_{1}}e_{2}^{g_{2}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}\,|\,g_{i}\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n},\,i=1,2,\dots,m\big\}
.
Define a map

\displaystyle\psi\colon\ \mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C^{(n)}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m}),

\displaystyle u_{g}

\displaystyle\to e_{1}^{g_{1}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}.

Clearly
\psi
is a linear isomorphism, so it remains to verify that
\psi
is also multiplicative.
By direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\psi(u_{g}\star u_{h})=\psi(F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)u_{g+h})=F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)\psi(u_{g+h})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\psi(u_g\star u_h)}{}=\omega^{\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq m}g_{j}h_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{[\frac{g_{i}+h_{i}}{n}]}e_{1}^{(g_{1}+h_{1})^{\prime}}\cdots e_{m}^{(g_{m}+h_{m})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\psi(u_g\star u_h)}{}=\big(e_{1}^{g_{1}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}\big)\big(e_{1}^{h_{1}}\cdots e_{m}^{h_{m}}\big)=\psi(u_{g})\psi(u_{h}).

Hence we have proved that
\psi
is an isomorphism of algebras.
∎

3.4
Decomposition theorem of GCAs

In this subsection, we apply Proposition
3.1
to derive the well known decomposition theorem of generalized Clifford algebras via gauge transformations.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have the following isomorphisms of algebras in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}^{m}_{n}},\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA})
:

\displaystyle C^{(n)}(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})\cong C^{(n)}(q_{1},\dots,q_{l})\widehat{\otimes}C^{(n)}(q_{l+1},\dots,q_{m})\cong C^{(n)}(q_{1})\widehat{\otimes}C^{(n)}(q_{2})\cdots\widehat{\otimes}C^{(n)}(q_{m}).

Proof.

Recall that
F_{\rm GCA}
is the function defined in (
3.1
) and

\displaystyle\mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]\cong C^{(n)}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{m})

in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}^{m}_{n}},\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA})
.
Let
F_{\rm GCA}^{-1}
be the inverse of
F_{\rm GCA}
, namely,
F_{\rm GCA}^{-1}(g,h)=\frac{1}{F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)}
,
\forall\,g,h\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
. Then
F_{\rm GCA}^{-1}
is a twisting on the dual quasi-Hopf algebra
(\mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}],\partial F_{\rm GCA})
and it induces a braided tensor equivalence:

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1},\varphi_{0},\varphi_{2}\big)\colon\ (\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},\mathcal{R})\longrightarrow(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o),

where
o(g,h)\equiv 1
. Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the inverse functor of
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
. By Section
2.6
, considering
\mathbbm{k}_{F_{\rm GCA}}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},\mathcal{R})
amounts to considering
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
. Note that
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{l}]
with
l<m
can be viewed as an algebra in

(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
by a natural manner, for example one may assign the element
(g_{1},\dots,g_{l})\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{l}
the grading

(g_{1},\dots,g_{l},0,\dots,0)\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
. Thus, by assigning appropriate gradings in this way, one has immediately in

\left(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o\right)
the following isomorphisms of algebras

\displaystyle\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]\cong\mathbbm{k}\big[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{l}\big]\otimes\mathbbm{k}\big[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m-l}\big]\cong\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}].

(3.3)

Note that the braiding
o
is trivial, hence the braided tensor product in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
is the usual tensor product and so we omit the “hats” in the preceding equation. Now by applying
\mathcal{F}
to (
3.3
), the claimed isomorphisms follow.
∎

4
Weak Hopf structures of GCAs in symmetric Gr-categories

In this section, we will show that GCAs have various weak Hopf structures if they are putting in suitable symmetric linear Gr-categories. With a help of the handy gauge transformations, we generalize and simplify the results obtained by Bulacu
[
3
,
4
]
. More precisely, according to Sections
2.6
and
3.4
the study of
C^{n}(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA})
amounts to that of
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
. Apparently, the latter situation seems much simpler. Therefore, we study the weak Hopf structures of
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
first and then translate to those of
C^{n}(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},\mathcal{R}_{\rm GCA})
via gauge transformations.

4.1
Weak Hopf structures of
\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]}
in
\boldsymbol{(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}},o)}

For a better exposition of our idea, we start with the simplest case in which
m=1
. In order to find categorical weak Hopf structures on
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]
, it suffices to determine its compatible coalgebra structures in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}},o)
. This can be done by direct computation in accordance with the axiom of weak Hopf algebras as follows.

Recall that the elements of
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}
are denoted by
\bar{i}
, i.e., integers modulo
n
, and the multiplication is written additively. According to Definition
2.1
(2), the comultiplication must be of the following form

\displaystyle\Delta(\bar{i})=\sum_{\bar{j}}\delta(\bar{j},\overline{i-j})\bar{j}\otimes\overline{i-j}.

The coassociativity of
\Delta
implies that

\displaystyle\delta(\bar{i},\overline{j+k})\delta(\bar{j},\bar{k})=\delta(\bar{i},\bar{j})\delta(\overline{i+j},\bar{k}),\qquad\forall\,\bar{i},\bar{j},\bar{k}.

In other words,
\delta
is a 2-cocycle on
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}
. In addition, the counitary condition says

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bar{0})\delta(\bar{i},\bar{0})=1=\varepsilon(\bar{0})\delta(\bar{0},\bar{i}),\qquad\forall\,\bar{i}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\varepsilon(\bar{j})=0,\qquad\forall\,\bar{j}\neq\bar{0}.

(4.1)

The multiplicity of
\Delta
impose on
\delta
the following

\displaystyle\delta(\bar{k},\overline{i+j-k})=\sum_{\bar{l}}\delta(\bar{l},\overline{i-l})\delta(\overline{k-l},\overline{j+l-k}),\qquad\forall\,\bar{i},\bar{j},\bar{k}.

(4.2)

By (
2.2
), the 2-cocycle
\delta
satisfies a further condition

\displaystyle\delta(\bar{i},\overline{j-i})=\delta(\bar{i},\overline{-i}),\qquad\forall\,\bar{i},\bar{j}.

(4.3)

Now it follows immediately from (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\delta(\bar{i},\bar{j})\equiv c,\qquad\forall\,\bar{i},\bar{j}

where
c\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
is a constant. Then by (
4.2
), we have
c=nc^{2}
, and thus
c=\frac{1}{n}
. With this, the counit is also completely determined, namely,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bar{0})=n,\qquad\varepsilon(\bar{i})=0,\qquad\forall\,\bar{i}\neq\bar{0}.

It is clear that
\varepsilon
satisfies (
2.1
). Therefore, endowed with the preceding comultiplication
\Delta
and counit
\varepsilon
, the algebra
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]
becomes a weak bialgebra in the category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}},o)
. Finally, it is not hard to observe that, if we define a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map
S\colon\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]
by
S(\bar{i})=\bar{i}
,
\forall\,\bar{i}
, then
S
satisfies the antipode axiom of weak Hopf algebras (see Definition
2.1
(5)) and
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]
becomes a weak Hopf algebra in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}},o)
.

To summarize, we have

Proposition 4.1
.

The group algebra
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}]
has a unique weak Hopf algebra structure in the Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}},o)
with comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
and antipode
S
given by

\displaystyle\Delta(\bar{i})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\bar{j}}\bar{j}\otimes\overline{i-j},\qquad\varepsilon(\bar{i})=n\delta_{\bar{i},\bar{0}},\qquad S(\bar{i})=\bar{i},\qquad\forall\,\bar{i}\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}

where
\delta_{\bar{i},\bar{0}}
is the Kronecker’s delta.

4.2
Weak Hopf structures of
\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]}
in
\boldsymbol{(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)}

Now we extend the above arguments to the general situation. Recall that the elements
g\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
are written in the form
(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{m})
and the product of
\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
is written as
+
. By a similar argument, any comultiplication of
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
is determined by a 2-cocycle
\delta\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m},\mathbbm{k}^{*})
, that is,

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{g})=\sum_{h\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}}\delta(h,g-h)u_{h}\otimes u_{g-h}

and the weak bialgebra axioms in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
will impose on
\delta
the following conditions:

\displaystyle\delta(f,g+h)\delta(g,h)=\delta(f,g)\delta(f+g,h),\qquad\forall\,f,g,h,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(0)\delta(g,0)=1=\varepsilon(0)\delta(0,g),\qquad\forall\,g\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\varepsilon(h)=0,\qquad\forall\,h\neq 0,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\delta(h,f+g-h)=\sum_{l}\delta(l,f-l)\delta(h-l,g+l-h),\qquad\forall\,f,g,h,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\delta(g,h-g)=\delta(g,-g),\qquad\forall\,g,h.

(4.7)

As before, it is clear by (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\delta(g,h)\equiv\frac{1}{n^{m}},\qquad\forall\,g,h,\qquad\varepsilon(g)=n^{m}\delta_{g,0},\qquad\forall\,g.

Again, let
S\colon\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
be the identity map. It is routine to verify that
(\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}],\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
makes a weak Hopf algebra in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
. This provides the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

The group algebra
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
has a unique weak Hopf algebra structure in the Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
with comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
and antipode
S
given by

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{g})=\frac{1}{n^{m}}\sum_{h}u_{h}\otimes u_{g-h},\qquad\varepsilon(u_{g})=n^{m}\delta_{g,0},\qquad S(g)=g,\qquad\forall\,g\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}.

4.3
Categorical weak Hopf structures of GCAs

With a help of the results in Section
3
, now we can apply the handy gauge transformation to simplify, improve and generalize the results of Bulacu
[
3
,
4
]
.

We start with the following

Corollary 4.3
.

The twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
has a unique weak Hopf algebra structure in the symmetric Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}}^{\partial F},\mathcal{R}_{F})
with comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
and antipode
S
given by

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{g})=\frac{1}{n^{m}}\sum_{h}F(h,g-h)^{-1}u_{h}\otimes u_{g-h},\!\qquad\varepsilon(u_{g})=n^{m}\delta_{g,0},\!\qquad S(g)=g,\!\qquad\forall\,g\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}.

Proof.

By applying the gauge transformation

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{0},\varphi_{2})\colon\ (\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)\longrightarrow\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}}^{\partial F},\mathcal{R}_{F}\big),

one has
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]=\mathcal{F}(\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}])
and therefore the set of weak Hopf structures of
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}}^{\partial F},\mathcal{R}_{F}\big)
is in one-to-one correspondence with that of
\mathbbm{k}[\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}]
in
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},o)
. Now the claim follows immediately by the above Theorem
4.2
.
∎

With this, we may discover the categorical weak Hopf structures in suitable symmetric Gr-categories of some well known algebras by properly specifying the twisted functions. In particular, if we take
F=F_{\rm GCA}
, then we determine the categorical weak Hopf structure of generalized Clifford algebras via Proposition
3.1
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The generalized Clifford algebra
C^{n}(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
has a unique weak Hopf algebra structure in the symmetric Gr-category
(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}},\mathcal{R}_{F_{\rm GCA}})
, where

\displaystyle F_{\rm GCA}(g,h)=\omega^{\sum\limits_{1\leq j<i\leq m}g_{i}h_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{[\frac{g_{i}+h_{i}}{n}]},

with comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
and antipode
S
given by

\displaystyle\Delta\big(e_{1}^{g_{1}}e_{2}^{g_{2}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}\big)=\frac{1}{n^{m}}\sum_{h}\omega^{-\sum\limits_{1\leq j<i\leq m}h_{i}(g_{j}-h_{j})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Delta\big(e_1^{g_1}e_2^{g_2}\cdots e_m^{g_m}\big)=}{}\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{q}_{i}e_{1}^{h_{1}}e_{2}^{h_{2}}\cdots e_{m}^{h_{m}}\otimes e_{1}^{(g_{1}-h_{1})^{\prime}}e_{2}^{(g_{2}-h_{2})^{\prime}}\cdots e_{m}^{(g_{m}-h_{m})^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\varepsilon\big(e_{1}^{g_{1}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}\big)=n^{m}\delta_{g,0},\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad S\big(e_{1}^{g_{1}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}\big)=e_{1}^{g_{1}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}},

for all
e_{1}^{g_{1}}e_{2}^{g_{2}}\cdots e_{m}^{g_{m}}\in C^{n}(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
and all
g=(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{m})
,
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{m})\in\mathbbm{Z}_{n}^{m}
, where

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{i}=\begin{cases}q_{i},&h_{i}>g_{i},\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Direct consequence of Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
4.3
.
∎

4.4
Final remarks

In fact, our above discussion can be easily extended to the full generality. Namely,
let
G
be an arbitrary finite abelian group written multiplicatively with unit
e
, let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field with
\operatorname{char}\mathbbm{k}\nmid|G|
, and let
F
be an arbitrary 2-cochain on
G
. Then the twisted group algebra
\mathbbm{k}_{F}[G]
has a unique weak Hopf structure in the symmetric Gr-category
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\partial F},\mathcal{R}_{F}\big)
with comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
and antipode
S
given by

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{u}F\big(u,u^{-1}x\big)^{-1}u\otimes u^{-1}x,\qquad\varepsilon(x)=|G|\delta_{x,e},\qquad S(x)=x,\qquad\forall\,x\in G.

If we specify
G=\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}
and
F
the Albuquerque–Majid cochain
F_{\rm CD}
of Cayley–Dickson algebras
[
1
]
, then we determine completely the weak Hopf structures of Cayley–Dickson algebras in
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}}^{\partial F_{\rm CD}},\mathcal{R}_{F_{\rm CD}}\big)
. This improves the main result of Bulacu
[
3
]
. If we specify
G=\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}
and
F=F_{\mathbb{O}}
, the Morier-Genoud–Ovsienko cochain of higher octonions
[
15
]
, then we determine completely the weak Hopf structures of higher octonions
\mathbb{O}_{n}
in the symmetric Gr-category
\big(\operatorname{Vec}_{\mathbbm{Z}_{2}^{n}}^{\partial F_{\mathbb{O}}},\mathcal{R}_{F_{\mathbb{O}}}\big)
.

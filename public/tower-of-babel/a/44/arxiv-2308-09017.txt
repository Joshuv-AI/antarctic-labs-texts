Algebra and Geometry of Irreducible toric vector bundles of rank n n on ℙ n {\mathbb{P}}^{n}
Courtney George, Christopher Manon

Abstract
. We construct a presentation for the Cox ring of the projectivization ℙ ​ ℰ {\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E} of any rank n n irreducible toric vector bundle on ℙ n {\mathbb{P}}^{n} . 1.
Introduction

A toric vector bundle
\pi:\mathcal{E}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is a vector bundle equipped with the additional information of an algebraic action by the torus
T\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
. This action is required to intertwine with the usual action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, and induce linear maps between fibers. We let
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
denote the
projectivization
of
\mathcal{E}
. Like toric varieties, projectivized toric vector bundles are a rich class of algebraic varieties which display a wide range of behavior, yet admit a combinatorial description.

In
[
Kan
]
, Kaneyama shows that any toric vector bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
of rank
<n
must split into a direct sum of line bundles, and gives a complete classification of the irreducible toric vector bundles of rank
n
. Let
\mathcal{O}(a)
for
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the line bundle of degree
a
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. Any irreducible toric vector bundle of rank
n
can be described as the cokernel
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
of a map
\mathcal{O}\to\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}\mathcal{O}(a_{i})
where
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, or as the dual
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
of such a bundle. In particular, the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and cotagent bundle
\mathcal{T}^{\vee}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
are the case
a_{j}=1
. In this paper we study the projectivizations
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
of these bundles.

1.2.
Divisors and Fujita’s conjectures

For a smooth, projective variety
X
we let
\textup{CL}(X)
denote the divisor class group, and
K_{X}
denote the canonical class of
X
. The following are Fujita’s freeness and ampleness conjectures, respectively.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
X
be smooth of dimension
n
and let
A\in\textup{CL}(X)
be ample, then

(1)

for
m\geq n+1
,
K_{X}+mA
is globally generated,

(2)

for
m\geq n+2
,
K_{X}+mA
is very ample.

By Mori’s cone theorem (
[
Laz04
, Theorem 1.5.33]
), Conjecture
1.6
can be proven for a variety
X
by showing that any Nef class on
X
is basepoint-free, and any ample class on
X
is very ample. Techniques for computing positivity properties of divisors on a projectived toric vector bundle
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
are developed in
[
GM
]
. In Section
4
we apply modifications of those techniques to compute the pseudo-effective and Nef cones, along with the effective and semiample monoids, for any
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
. We then deduce the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any
\mathbf{a}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, a divisor on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
or
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
is basepoint free if it is Nef, and very ample if it is ample. As a consequence,
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
satisfy Fujita’s freeness and ampleness conjectures.

The Fujita conjectures are known for smooth toric varieties. In the non-smooth toric case, freeness holds by work of Fujino
[
Fuj
]
, and a theorem of Payne
[
Pay06
]
establishes ampleness in the case of Gorenstein singularities. Working by way of dimension, curves satisfy both conjectures by Riemann-Roch, and results of Reider
[
Rei
]
prove the surface case. The freeness conjecture has been proved for smooth projective varieties up to dimension
5

[
EL
,
Kaw
,
YZ20
]
. For toric vector bundles, if the rank of
\mathcal{E}
is
2
, then
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is a
complexity-
1

T
-variety
, and therefore satisfies Fujita’s freeness conjecture by a result of Altmann and Ilten
[
AI
]
. We credit Altmann and Ilten for the suggestion to study the Fujita conjectures for projectivized toric vector bundles. For Mori dream spaces, Fahrner
[
Fah
]
has developed algorithms which test the freeness conjecture.

We prove Theorem
1.7
by showing that
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
both carry embeddings into split projectivized toric vector bundles in such a way that both the class groups and semiample monoids are isomorphic to those of the ambient space (Proposition
4.3
). We call a map with these properties a
neat and tidy
embedding, see Section
4
.

2.
Linear ideals and diagrams

In what follows let
E
denote the fiber of a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over the identity point of a torus
T
, thought of as a dense, open subvariety of a smooth toric variety
X(\Sigma)
. The data of a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
can be packaged in a number of ways. Kaneyama’s classification
[
Kan75
]
is by certain
\textup{GL}(E)
cocycles. Klyachko
[
Kly89
]
uses an arrangement of filtrations of
E
labelled by data from the representation theory of
T
. In
[
KMb
]
, Kaveh and the
2
nd author show that
\mathcal{E}
can be captured in a
prevaluation

\mathfrak{v}:E\to\mathcal{O}_{|\Sigma|}
where the latter denotes the integral piecewise-linear functions with a finite number of domains of linearity on the support of the fan
\Sigma
. The operations on
\mathcal{O}_{|\Sigma|}
are “multiplication,” computed as pointwise sum, and “addition,” computed as pointwise minimum. We regard
\infty
as the additive identity of
\mathcal{O}_{|\Sigma|}
, and
0
serves as the multiplicative identity. Under these operations,
\mathcal{O}_{|\Sigma|}
has the structure of a
semifield
.

Definition 2.1
.

A prevaluation
\mathfrak{v}:E\to\mathcal{O}_{|\Sigma|}
is a function which satisfies:

(1)

\mathfrak{v}(0)=\infty
,

(2)

\mathfrak{v}(Cf)=\mathfrak{v}(f)
for any
C\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
,

(3)

\mathfrak{v}(f+g)\geq\min\{\mathfrak{v}(f),\mathfrak{v}(g)\}
.

Klyachko’s compatibility conditions, and Kaneyama’s cocycle data can be wrapped up in the axioms of prevaluations, along with the requirement that there be an
linear adapted basis

\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}
for the restriction
\mathfrak{v}\!\!\mid_{\sigma}:E\to\mathcal{O}_{|\sigma|}
of
\mathfrak{v}
to each maximal face of the fan
\Sigma
. This means that there is some vector space basis
\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}\subset E
with the property that
\mathfrak{v}\!\!\mid_{\sigma}(b)
agrees with an integral linear form on
|\sigma|
, and for any
f\in E
with
f=\sum_{b_{i}\in\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}}c_{i}b_{i}
we have
\mathfrak{v}\mid_{\sigma}(f)=\min\{\mathfrak{v}(b_{i})\mid c_{i}\neq 0\}
.

Because of the adapted bases
\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}\subset E
and the smoothness of
\Sigma
, the prevaluation
\mathfrak{v}
is entirely determined by its values on the ray generators of
\Sigma
. The fact that
\mathfrak{v}
is a prevaluation implies that its specialization
\mathfrak{v}(u):E\to{\mathbb{Z}}
at a ray generator determines an integral, decreasing filtration of
E
by the spaces:

F_{r}^{u}=\{f\mid\mathfrak{v}(u)(f)\geq r\}.

The integral filtration
F^{u}
of
E
can be used to define an integral valuation on the polynomial ring
\textup{Sym}(E)
. A choice of spanning set
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\}\subset E
determines a presentation of
\textup{Sym}(E)
by a linear ideal
L\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, and by taking values on
\mathcal{B}\subset E
, the valuation defined by
F^{u}
specializes to a point
w(u)
in the tropical variety
\textup{Trop}(L)
by evaluation on the image of the generators
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
in
\textup{Sym}(E)
. The point
w(u)
completely determines
F^{u}
in that
F^{u}_{r}
is the span of those elements of
\mathcal{B}
whose
w(u)
entries are larger than
r
. Such a set always defines a flat of the matroid
\mathcal{M}(L)
determined by
L
on the set
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
.

If we take
\mathcal{B}
to be any spanning set of
E
containing adapted bases
\mathbb{B}_{\sigma}
we arrive at the characterization of toric vector bundles in
[

of points on a tropicalized linear space

to a pair

for a dual bundle. The ideal is obtained by taking

to be the relations which vanish on the set

which come from the rays

We now apply this construction to

.

Fix a face
\sigma_{i}
in
\Sigma_{n}

has adapted basis the images of the

with

Now we give a presentation of the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))

\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{T}{\mathbb{P}}^{n})
in
[
GM23
]
(this is the special case
\mathbf{a}=(1,\ldots,1)
). For toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
n
, the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
can be constructed as the algebra of invariants by the natural action of the unipotent group
U_{n-1}
of
n-1\times n-1
upper triangular matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal on the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}))
. In the case
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
, the algebra
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}))
is presented by the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},Y_{ij}\mid 0\leq j\leq n,1\leq i\leq n-1]
, which also carries an action by
U^{n-1}
. The unipotent actions extend to an action by the reductive group
\textup{GL}_{n-1}({\mathbb{C}})
, hence we obtain a surjection:

{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},Y_{ij}\mid 0\leq j\leq n,1\leq i\leq n-1]^{U_{n-1}}\to\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))\to 0.

The algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},Y_{ij}\mid 0\leq j\leq n,1\leq i\leq n-1]^{U_{n-1}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},Y_{ij}\mid 0\leq j\leq n,1\leq i\leq n-1]
is a polynomial ring in
n+1
variables over the Plücker algebra of upper-justified minors of the matrix of variables
[Y_{ij}]
. For background on the Plücker relations see
[
MS05
, Section 14.2]
. Accordingly, we can present
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))
as a quotient of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},P_{\tau},P_{0,\tau}\mid 0\leq j\leq n,\tau\subset[n]]
.

The Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))
is the image of the map
\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}:{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},P_{\tau},P_{0,\tau}]\to{\mathbb{C}}[t_{j},y_{ij}]
defined by:

\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{j})=t_{j}^{-1},

\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}(P_{\tau})=\det[y(\tau)]t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}},

\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}(P_{0,\tau})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\det[y(j,\tau)]t_{0}^{\mathbf{a}_{0}}t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}.

Here
y(\tau)
denotes the minor on the first
|\tau|
rows and the
\tau
columns of
[y_{ij}]
, and
\mathbf{a}_{\tau}
denotes the part of the tuple
\mathbf{a}
supported on
\tau
.
The quadratic Plücker relations hold among the
P_{\tau}
and
P_{0,\tau}
. Moreover, it is straightforward to very that
\sum_{j\notin\tau}x_{j}^{\mathbf{a}_{j}}P_{j\tau}=0
using the definition of
\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}
. We show that these relations suffice to present
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))
.

The proof of Theorem
3.5
involves a modification of the semigroup
GZ_{n}
of Gel’fand-Zetlin patterns with
n
rows. A Gel’fand-Zetlin pattern
g\in GZ_{n}
is an array of integers arranged in
n
rows, where the
i
-th row has
n+1-i
entries
g_{ij}
. These integers satisfy additional inequalities:
g_{ij}\geq g_{i+1,j}\geq g_{i,j+1}
. Let
GZ_{n}^{+}
be the set of patterns with
g_{1n}=0
. The generators of
GZ_{n}^{+}
are in bijection with strict subsets
\tau\subset[n]
, where the pattern
g(\tau)
corresponding to
\tau
is the unique pattern with
|\tau\cap[n-i+1]|

1
’s and
|[n-i+1]\setminus\tau|

0
’s in row
i
.

A solution to the
word problem
for a semigroup
S
with generators
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
is a minimal set of relations of the form
A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{t}}=A_{j_{1}}\cdots A_{j_{s}}
which can be used to transform an arbitrary relation to a trivial relation by substitution.

Theorem 3.5
.

The ideal
\ker(\Psi_{\mathbf{a}})
is generated by the Plücker relations among the
P_{\tau}
and
P_{0,\tau}
, along with relations of the form
\sum_{j\notin\tau}x_{j}^{\mathbf{a}_{j}}P_{j\tau}=0
.

Proof.

The argument proceeds as in
[
KM19
, Algorithm 1.8]
. We select a monomial ordering on
{\mathbb{C}}[t_{j},y_{ij}]
which satisfies
y_{ij}\prec t_{\ell}
for all
i,j,\ell
, such that the initial form
\textup{in}_{\prec}\det[y(\tau)]
is the product of the diagonal terms. This ordering also defines a partial ordering on the variables
x_{j},P_{\tau},P_{0,\tau}
. The initial forms of
\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}(x_{j}),\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}(P_{\tau}),
and
\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}(P_{0,\tau})
with respect to this ordering generate a semigroup in
GZ_{n}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
. To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that there is a generating set of the binomial relations which vanish on these initial forms which are themselves the initial forms of the described set of relations with respect to the induced partial ordering on the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{j},P_{\tau},P_{0,\tau}]
.

Now we identify the initial forms
\textup{in}_{\prec}t_{j}^{-1}
,
\textup{in}_{\prec}\det[y(\tau)]t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}
, and
\textup{in}_{\prec}\det[y(0,\tau)]t_{0}^{\mathbf{a}_{0}}t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}
with elements of the semigroup
GZ_{n}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
. The form
\textup{in}_{\prec}t_{j}^{-1}
is sent to
(0,-\mathbf{e}_{j})\in GZ_{n}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
, the form
\textup{in}_{\prec}\det[y(\tau)]t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}
is sent to
(g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j})\in GZ_{n}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
, and finally, the form
\textup{in}_{\prec}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\det[y(j,\tau)]t_{0}^{\mathbf{a}_{0}}t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}
is sent to
(g(\tau^{*}),a_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0}+\sum_{j\in\tau}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j})\in GZ_{n}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
. Here
\tau^{*}
denotes the set
\tau\cup\{\ell\}
, where
\ell
is the first element of
[n]
not in
\tau
. In particular, if
\mathbf{e}_{0}
appears in the support of
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
, where
(g(\eta),\mathbf{u})
is a generator, then the first index in the support of
\eta
is smaller than the first non-zero index of
\mathbf{u}
.

Next, we find a generating set of the prime binomial ideal which vanishes on these patterns by solving the word problem. If
[b]\subset\eta
then we have:

[0,-a_{b}\mathbf{e}_{b}][g(\eta),\sum_{j\in\eta}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]=[0,-a_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0}][g(\eta),a_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0}+\sum_{j\in\eta\setminus\{b\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]

Here
[g(\eta),\sum_{j\in\eta}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]
is the representative of
\textup{in}_{\prec}\det[y(\eta)]t^{\mathbf{a}_{\eta}}
, and
[g(\eta),a_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0}+\sum_{j\in\eta\setminus\{b\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]
is the representative of
\textup{in}_{\prec}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\det[y(j,\eta\setminus\{b\})]t_{0}^{\mathbf{a}_{0}}t^{\mathbf{a}_{\eta\setminus\{b\}}}
. We also have the standard binomial relations among the Gel’fand-Zetlin generators:

[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}][g(\eta),\sum_{i\in\eta}a_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}]=[g(\tau\cup\eta),\sum_{k\in\tau\cup\eta}a_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}][g(\tau\cap\eta),\sum_{\ell\in\tau\cap\eta}a_{\ell}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}].

These binomials hold among the initial forms of
\textup{in}_{\prec}\det[y(\tau)]t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}
; it is straightforward to verify that the versions involving
\textup{in}_{\prec}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\det[y(j,\tau)]t_{0}^{\mathbf{a}_{0}}t^{\mathbf{a}_{\tau}}
work in the same way.

The two families of binomials described lift to
\sum_{j\notin\tau}x_{j}^{\mathbf{a}_{j}}P_{\tau}=0
, and Plücker relations, respectively. Therefore, if we check that these relations suffice to generate the binomial ideal which vanishes on the initial forms, we have shown that the required relations generate
\ker(\Psi_{\mathbf{a}})
. We suppose we have two words
A_{1}\cdots A_{n}
,
B_{1}\cdots B_{n}
whose product maps to the same extended Gel’fand-Zetlin pattern. We must show that after applications of the above binomial relations, these words can be taken to a common word.

First we observe that the contributions to the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
component from either word agree, and that if
\mathbf{e}_{\ell}
appears with multiplicity
b_{\ell}
, we can divide
b_{\ell}
by
a_{\ell}
and factor the resulting number of copies of
[0,-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]
off both words. In particular, we may assume without loss of generality that the contribution of
\mathbf{e}_{\ell}
is divisible by
a_{\ell}
. Similarly, any
[0,-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]
not supported by a pattern elsewhere in the words can be read off of the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
component, and factored off both sides. Also, any
[0,-a_{\ell}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]
for
[\ell]\subset\tau
for
[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]
appearing the word can be converted to
[0,-a_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0}]
using the first relation above. So we may assume without loss of generality that both words do not contain a generator of the form
[0,-a_{\ell}\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]
for any
\ell\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
.

Now, using the Gel’fand-Zetlin binomial relations, we can ensure that the underlying Gel’fand-Zetlin patterns in both words are the same, with possibly different
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
components. Select a leading pattern on both sides, say
A_{1}=[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau\setminus\{b_{1}\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]
and
B_{1}=[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau\setminus\{c_{1}\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]
, and suppose that
b_{1}<c_{1}
. We must have that
[b_{1}]\subset\tau
and
[c_{1}]\subset\tau
. Moreover, the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
components of both words agree, so there must be some
A_{2}=[g(\eta),\sum_{k\in\eta\setminus\{c_{1}\}}a_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}]
with
[c_{1}]\subset\eta
. This means
b_{1},c_{1}\in\tau
, and
c_{1}\in[c_{1}]\subset\eta
, so also
b_{1}\in\eta
. Now we may apply the consequence of the Gel’fand-Zetlin relations:
[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau\setminus\{b_{1}\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}][g(\eta),\sum_{k\in\eta\setminus\{c_{1}\}}a_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}]=[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau\setminus\{c_{1}\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}][g(\eta),\sum_{k\in\eta\setminus\{b_{1}\}}a_{k}\mathbf{e}_{k}]
, and factor off
[g(\tau),\sum_{j\in\tau\setminus\{c_{1}\}}a_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}]
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
The Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}))

In this section we obtain a presentation the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
from a presentation of the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))
. For a general toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
over a smooth, projective toric variety
X(\Sigma)
the class group of
\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E})
is naturally a product:

\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))\cong\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\times\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}_{r}),

where
\mathcal{FL}_{r}
denotes the full flag variety of the vector space
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, see
[
GM23
, Proposition 3.5]
. For a moment we draw from representation theory and write
\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}_{r})
as
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{i}
, wnere
\omega_{i}
denotes the
i
-th fundamental weight of the reductive group
\textup{SL}_{r}
. Each positive combination
\lambda=\sum_{i}^{r-1}n_{i}\omega_{i}
corresponds to a Schur functor
\textup{S}_{\lambda}
, which is an operation which applies to both vector spaces and vector bundles. These are precisely the effective classes on
\mathcal{FL}_{r}
. If
(\mathbf{d},\lambda)\in\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
is effective, restriction of this class to the identity fiber of
\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E})
gives an effective class of
\mathcal{FL}_{r}
. The Picard variety of
\mathcal{FL}_{r}
is a point, so this implies that
\lambda
is a positive combination of the
\omega_{i}
. In this case, the global sections of
(\mathbf{d},\lambda)
can be realizes as the following section space on
X(\Sigma)
:

H^{0}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}),\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{d},\lambda))\cong H^{0}(X(\Sigma),\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{d})\otimes\textup{S}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E})).

In the special case that
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\omega_{i}
with
n_{i}=0
for
i<r-1
,
\textup{S}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E})
is of the form
\textup{Sym}^{n}(\bigwedge^{r-1}\mathcal{E})
. In this way we see that the Cox ring of
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{r-1}\mathcal{E})
is naturally the subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
supported on the subgroup
\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\times{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{r-1}\subset\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
. Finally,
\bigwedge^{r-1}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
differ by tensoring by the determinant line bundle
\det^{-1}(\mathcal{E})
, so their projectivizations agree. We summarize these observations below.

Proposition 3.6
.

Under the grading by
\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
,
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}))
is the subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
supported on the subgroup
\textup{CL}(X(\Sigma))\times{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{r-1}\subset\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}))
.

Now we apply a little bit of invariant theory to find a presentation of
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}))
.

Proposition 3.7
.

The Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
is the quotient of the polynomial ring

by the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
generated by the relations
x_{j}^{a_{j}}Z_{ik}-x_{k}^{a_{k}}Z_{ij}-x_{i}^{a_{i}}Z_{jk}
for

, and the quadratic Plücker relations on the

.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
,
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}))
is the subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))
generated by the

, along with the top Plücker generators
P_{\tau}
, where
|\tau|=n-1
. We let
Z_{ij}
denote the Plücker generator corresponding to the complement of
\{i,j\}
in
\{0,\ldots,n\}
. The
\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}_{n})
components of the
\textup{CL}(\mathcal{FL}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}))
degrees of a Plücker generator

is the fundamental weight

, where

. Consequently, any relation among the

must come from the
\omega_{n-1}
component of the ideal, which is in turn generated by the Plücker relations on the

.
∎

Remark 3.8

In the case

\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
is the tangent bundle of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, making
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
the cotangent bundle
\mathcal{T}^{\vee}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. The presentation above shows that the transformation
x_{i}\to P_{0,i+1}
,
Z_{ij}\to P_{i+1,j+1}
takes the Cox ring
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{T}^{\vee}{\mathbb{P}}^{n})
isomorphically onto the Plücker algebra of the Grassmannian variety
\textup{Gr}_{2}(n+2)
. See
[
GHPS12
]
for a different account of this isomorphism.

3.4.
The ideals
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
are well-poised

Well-poised ideals were introduced in
[
IM19
]
, where it is shown that any rational, complexity-1
T
-variety has a well-poised embedding. Operations which preserve the well-poised property, in particular GIT quotients and the construction of
T
-varieties from certain polyhedral divisors, were studied in
[
CM20
]
. The following is the main result of
[
CDM+
]
, which gives a classification of well-poised hypersurfaces.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
p=\sum_{i=1}^{m}C_{\mathbf{a}_{i}}x^{\mathbf{a}_{i}}\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, then every initial form of
p
generates a prime ideal if and only if:

(1)

the supports of the monomials
x^{\mathbf{a}_{i}}
are disjoint,

(2)

for any
i,j
, the greatest common divisor of the list
\{\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{a}_{j}\}
is
1
.

Theorem
3.9
immediately implies that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}
is well-poised. In particular,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}
is generated by
\sum_{j=0}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{i}}Y_{j}
, which has disjointly supported monomials whose exponent vectors always contain a
1
.

The Plücker ideal
I_{2,n+2}
provides another example of a well-poised ideal. The initial ideals coming from the tropical variety
\textup{Trop}(I_{2,n+2})
were first studied by Speyer and Sturmfels in
[
SS04
]
. The initial ideals of points from the interior of a maximal face of
\textup{Trop}(I_{2,n+2})
are precisely the prime binomial ideals which cut out certain toric varieties related to trivalent trees. In particular, as a consequence of
[
SS04
, Theorem 3.4]
, it is known that the maximal faces of
\textup{Trop}(I_{2,n+2})
are in bijection with trivalent trees
\mathcal{T}
with
n+2
leaves labelled in some way with the set
\{0,\ldots,n+1\}
. Let
C_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the face associated to the labelled tree
\mathcal{T}
. Let
E(\mathcal{T})
denote the set of edges of a trivalent tree
\mathcal{T}
. Let
p_{ij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{E(\mathcal{T})}
denote the indicator vector of the unique path from leaf
i
to leaf
j
in
\mathcal{T}
. We let
S_{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{E(\mathcal{T})}
be the affine semigroup generated by the indicator vectors
p_{ij}
for
0\leq i<j\leq n+1
. The following is the main result we will need it what follows.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\rho\in C_{\mathcal{T}}
, then the affine semigroup algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[S_{\mathcal{T}}]
is isomorphic to the quotient algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[P_{ij}\mid 0\leq i<j\leq n+1]/\textup{in}_{\rho}(I_{2,n+2})
.

Recall that a solution to the
word problem
for a semigroup
S
with generators
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
is a minimal set of relations of the form
A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{t}}=A_{j_{1}}\cdots A_{j_{s}}
which can be used to transform an arbitrary relation to a trivial relation. Theorem
3.10
can be restated as saying that the binomial initial forms of the Plücker generators of
I_{2,n+2}
with respect to
\rho\in C_{\mathcal{T}}
solve the word problem for
S_{\mathcal{T}}
. We show that an almost identical statement holds for the ideals
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
and a semigroup which is closely related to
S_{\mathcal{T}}
. First we define a map of polynomial rings which relates
I_{2,n+2}
to the
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
:

\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}:{\mathbb{C}}[P_{ij}\mid 0\leq i<j\leq n+1]\to{\mathbb{C}}[x_{i},Z_{j,k}\mid 0\leq i,j<k\leq n]

\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}(P_{0,i+1})=x_{i}^{a_{i}}

\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}(P_{j+1,k+1})=Z_{j,k}

The Plücker generators of
I_{2,n+2}
map precisely to the generators of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
found in Proposition
3.7
under this map. As a consequence, the prime ideal
\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
is the Plücker ideal
I_{2,n+2}
. Let
S_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{a})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathcal{T}}
be the affine semigroup generated by the indicator vectors
p_{ij}
for
0<i<j\leq n+1
, and
\frac{1}{a_{i}}p_{0i}
for
0<i\leq n+1
.

Theorem 3.11
.

The ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
is well-poised.

Proof.

There is an induced map
\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}^{*}:\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})\to\textup{Trop}(I_{2,n+2})
, which is always onto by
[
MS15
, Lemma 3.2.13]
. For
\rho=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots,z_{ij},\ldots)\in\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
the pullback map on tropical varieties gives
\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}^{*}(\rho)=(a_{0}x_{0},\ldots,a_{n}x_{n},\ldots,z_{ij},\ldots)\in\textup{Trop}(I_{2,n+2})
, which is
1-1
.

Now fix
\rho\in\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
, and suppose that
\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}^{*}(\rho)
lands in the interior of
C_{\mathcal{T}}\subset\textup{Trop}(I_{2,n+2})
. We have
\langle\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}(\textup{in}_{\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}^{*}\rho}(I_{2,n+2}))\rangle\subset\textup{in}_{\rho}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
. The initial forms of the generating set of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
are the image of the
C_{\mathcal{T}}
-initial forms of the Plücker generators of
I_{2,n+2}
, and so are contained in the set
\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}(\textup{in}_{\Phi_{\mathbf{a}}^{*}\rho}(I_{2,n+2}))
. Therefore, it suffices to show that these initial forms generate a prime ideal of height equal to the height of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
. We show this by arguing that these initial forms solve the word problem for
S_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{a})
. Let
p_{1}\cdots p_{m}=q_{1}\cdots q_{m}
be an equation in the generators of
S_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbf{a})
, in particular, the induced weighting
w
of
E(\mathcal{T})
by these two words coincide. For either word, the contribution of
\frac{1}{a_{i}}p_{0i}
modulo
a_{i}
can be read off the edge containing the
i
-th leaf of
\mathcal{T}
in its boundary. It follows that if we assume (without loss of generality) that
p_{1}\cdots p_{m}
and
q_{1}\cdots q_{m}
share no generators, the contribution of each
\frac{1}{a_{i}}p_{0i}
to each must be divisible by
a_{i}
. Now, after grouping these generators into subwords of length
a_{i}
, we have a word problem identical to that of
S_{\mathcal{T}}
. This is solvable by the initial forms
\textup{in}_{\rho}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
by construction. Finally, we observe that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
is prime and homogeneous, so the fact that any
\rho\in\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee})
has a prime initial ideal follows from this fact for a
\rho
coming from the interior of a maximal face.
∎

For a divisor class
(\alpha,\beta)\in\textup{CL}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
let
\mathcal{R}_{(\alpha,\beta)}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
denote the graded subring given by the global sections of the positive multiples of
(\alpha,\beta)
. The following is a consequence of
[
CM20
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an irreducible toric vector bundle of rank
n
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, and let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a pseudo-effective divisor class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
, then the ring
\mathcal{R}_{(\alpha,\beta)}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
has a presentation by a well-poised ideal.

For
\rho\subset|\Sigma_{n}|
a ray we let
\textup{Bl}_{\rho}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
denote the corresponding toric blow-up of projective space and
\beta_{\rho}:\textup{Bl}_{\rho}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be the blow-down map. Given a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
with
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
a Mori dream space, it is natural to ask when the projectivization of the pullback bundle
\beta_{\rho}^{*}\mathcal{E}
is also a Mori dream space. A sufficient condition for this to occur, along with a presentation of the Cox ring of
{\mathbb{P}}\beta_{\rho}^{*}\mathcal{E}
is given in
[
KMa
, Theorem 6.13]
. Roughly speaking, one tests whether or not a certain point of
\textup{Trop}(L)
derived from
\rho
belongs to a certain polyhedral complex derived from
\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I})
. If the ideal
\mathcal{I}
presenting the Cox ring of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is well-poised, this subcomplex is all of
\textup{Trop}(L)
, meaning that
{\mathbb{P}}\beta_{\rho}^{*}\mathcal{E}
is always a Mori dream space, regardless of the choice of ray
\rho
. This leads us to the following corollary.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an irreducible bundle of rank
n
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, then for any ray
\rho
,
{\mathbb{P}}(\beta_{\rho}^{*}\mathcal{E})
is a Mori dream space.

Newton-Okounkov bodies

In
[
GM

methods are described for computing global Newton-Okounkov bodies and the Newton-Okounkov bodies of divisors for a projectivized toric vector bundle

with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree
1

on

The procedure in
[

uses the prime cone method for computing Newton-Okounkov bodies introduced in
[
KM19
]
. We require a matrix which is built from a choice of points in a chosen prime cone. Suppose that the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
comes from the pair
(L,D)
with

\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})

\mathcal{I}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{r}=\mathcal{M}(L)

in the flag corresponding to

. We let

be the following matrix:

M=\begin{bmatrix}D&-I\\
E_{K}&0\\
\end{bmatrix}.

For a divisor class

and

\Delta_{C}=M\circ{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}^{m+n}

\Delta_{C}(\alpha,\beta)=M\circ P_{\alpha,\beta}.

/

Finally, for

and
\mathcal{I}
as above, there is a surjection

\phi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m},m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})=(\ldots,v_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}D_{ij},\ldots),

which takes faces of
\textup{Trop}(\mathcal{I})
into faces of
\textup{Trop}(L)

can be translated to a point in the image of

[

.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle over

-degree

, and let

The assumption that the inverse image of any

Proposition 3.15
.

For the bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}

Proof.

First, any collection

Let

4.1.
The basepoint-free monoid

For the following see

. The following is
[

, Proposition 4.1]

.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and

be as above, and suppose that
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is generated in

-degree
1
, then
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})=\bigcap_{\sigma,F}S_{p_{\sigma,F}}
.

If
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is generated in
Sym
-degree
1
, the effective monoid is spanned by the class-group degrees of the generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
. Over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, these degrees are
\deg(x_{i})=(-1,0)
and
\deg(Y_{j})=(\mathbf{d}_{j},1)
, where
\mathbf{d}_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}D_{ij}
.

We say the bundle
\mathcal{E}
is a
monomial bundle

[
GM
, Section 4.2]
if each initial ideal
\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L)
is a monomial ideal. Equivalently,
(L,D)
defines a monomial ideal when, for any facet
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the minimal face of the Gröbner fan of
L
containing the rows of
D
corresponding to the rays
\sigma(1)
is maximal. In this case, the flats
F\subset\mathcal{M}(\textup{in}_{\sigma}(L))
are complements of single elements. If
\mathcal{E}
is a bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and the monoids
S_{p_{\sigma,F}}
of a monomial bundle are generated by the classes
(-1,0)
and
(\mathbf{d}_{j},1)
for one of the generators
Y_{j}\in\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
. In particular, any
S_{p_{\sigma,F}}
in this case is freely generated.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an irreducible toric vector bundle of rank
n
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, then
\mathcal{E}
is monomial. In particular, for a facet
\sigma_{i}
in the fan of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
,

(1)

\textup{in}_{\sigma_{i}}(L_{n})=\langle y_{i}\rangle\subset{\mathbb{C}}[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}]
,

(2)

\textup{in}_{\sigma_{i}}(L_{n}^{\vee})=\langle z_{jk}\mid i\notin\{j,k\}\rangle\subset{\mathbb{C}}[z_{ij}\mid 0\leq i<j\leq n]
.

Proof.

For both classes of ideal we consider the face
\sigma_{0}
in the fan of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
spanned by the elementary basis vectors. The ideal
L_{n}
is generated by the form
y_{0}+\cdots+y_{n}
. The rows of
D_{\mathbf{a}}
corresponding to the elements of
\sigma_{0}(1)
are each of the form
(0,\ldots,a_{i},\ldots,0)
where
i\neq 0
. The initial ideal of the face of the Gröbner fan of
L_{n}
must be generated by a common refinement of the initial forms of these rows, and the only variable not given a positive weight by some row is
y_{0}
. This shows
\textup{in}_{\sigma_{0}}(L_{n})=\langle y_{0}\rangle
.

For
L_{n}^{\vee}
we use the non-negative diagram
D_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}
. Recall that the
i
-th row of this diagram weights
z_{jk}
with
a_{i}
if
i\notin\{j,k\}
and
0
otherwise. We show that
\textup{in}_{\sigma_{0}}(L_{n}^{\vee})
must containing
z_{jk}
for any
0<j<k
. This proves the lemma for dimension reasons. The initial ideal of the minimal face of the Gröbner containing the rows from
\sigma_{0}(1)
must itself be an initial ideal of the sum of any two rows. Consider the initial ideal of the sum of the
j
and
k
-th rows; this weights
z_{0k}
with
a_{j}
,
z_{0j}
with
a_{k}
and
z_{jk}
with
0
. It follows that the initial form of
z_{0k}-z_{0j}-z_{jk}
with respect to this sum is
z_{jk}
, and that
z_{jk}\in\textup{in}_{\sigma_{0}}(L_{n}^{\vee})
for all
0<j<k
.
∎

4.2.
Embeddings into toric varieties

Observe that Lemma
4.2
implies that for any irreducible toric vector bundle of rank
n
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
,
\textup{Bpf}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
is the intersection of saturated monoids, and is therefore saturated. This implies that any Nef class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is basepoint free. In this section we recover this fact and more by showing that any
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
comes with a useful embedding into a smooth, projective toric variety.

Let
\phi:X\to Z
be an embedding of a Mori dream space into a toric variety
Z
. This embedding is said to be
neat
(
[
ADHL15
, Definition 3.2.5.2]
) if it induces an isomorphism
\textup{CL}(X)\cong\textup{CL}(Z)
. We say that
\phi
is
neat and tidy
it also induces
\textup{Bpf}(X)\cong\textup{Bpf}(Z)
.

In
[
GM
, Proposition 2.6]
it is shown that if
\mathcal{E}
is a toric vector bundle corresponding to the pair
(L,D)
with
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
generated in
Sym
-degree
1
then
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
has a neat embedding into the projectivization of a split toric vector bundle. In particular, if the
Y_{j}\in\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
corresponding to the variables
y_{j}
in the polynomial ring containing
L
generate
\mathcal{R}({\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E})
, one can use the split toric vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{D}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}(D_{j})
, where
\mathcal{O}(D_{j})
is the
T
-linearized line bundle corresponding to the
j
-th column of the diagram
D
for the embedding.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an irreducible toric vector bundle of rank
n
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, then
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
has a neat and tidy embedding into the projectivization of a split toric vector bundle. As a consequence, any Nef class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is basepoint free, and any ample class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is very ample.

Proof.

We consider the pairs
(L_{n},D_{\mathbf{a}})
and
(L_{n}^{\vee},D_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime})
. In both cases, the basepoint-free monoid of the corresponding bundle
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D}
is the intersection of the monoids
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\{(-1,0),(\mathbf{d}_{j},1)\}
, where
\mathbf{d}_{j}
runs over all the classes corresponding to the columns of the diagram
D
. To prove the first statement, it suffices to show that the basepoint-free monoids of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{a}}^{\vee}
have the same description. This translates to showing that for any
y_{i}
(respectively
z_{jk}
) there is a face
\sigma_{\ell}
of the fan of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
for which
y_{i}
(respectively
z_{jk}
) is not in the initial ideal
\textup{in}_{\sigma_{\ell}}(L_{n})
(respectively
\textup{in}_{\sigma_{\ell}}(L_{n}^{\vee})
). But this is a consequence of Lemma
4.2
.

As a consequence, any ample class of
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is the restriction of any ample class from
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{V}_{D}
. Any ample line bundle on a smooth toric variety is very ample, hence any ample class on
{\mathbb{P}}\mathcal{E}
is very ample.
∎

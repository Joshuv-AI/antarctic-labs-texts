Energy-constrained LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channel
Amir Arqand, Laleh Memarzadeh, and Stefano Mancini

Abstract
We study the LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channel with energy constraint on input states. We start our analysis by focusing on the energy-constrained squashed entanglement of the channel, which is an upper bound for the energy-constrained LOCC-assisted quantum capacity. As computing energy-constrained squashed entanglement of the channel is challenging due to a double optimization (over the set of density matrices and the isometric extensions of a squashing channel), we first derive an upper bound for it, and then we discuss how tight that bound is for energy-constrained LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channel. We prove that the optimal input state is diagonal in the Fock basis. Furthermore, we prove that for a generic channel, the optimal squashing channel belongs to the set of symmetric quantum Markov chain inducer (SQMCI) channels of the channel system-environment output, provided that such a set is non-empty. With supporting arguments, we conjecture that this is instead the case for the bosonic dephasing channel. Hence, for it we analyze two explicit examples of squashing channels which are not SQMCI, but are symmetric. Through them, we derive explicit upper and lower bounds for the energy-constrained LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of the bosonic dephasing channel in terms of its quantum capacity with different noise parameters. As the difference between upper and lower bounds is at most of the order 10 − 1 10^{-1} , we conclude that the bounds are tight. Hence we provide a very good estimation of the LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of the bosonic dephasing channel.

II-C

Two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity

In this subsection, we bring to light the definition of two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity, and its energy-constrained form
[
28
,
26
]
. Then we recall its upper bound in terms of the squashed entanglement of a channel.

The performance of quantum channels for reliable quantum communication is quantified by quantum capacity when there is no extra resources, such as shared entanglement or classical communication between sender and receiver. Indeed by allowing further resources, we expect higher rates of information transmission through the channel.
When LOCC is allowed interactively between sender and receiver, the capability of channel for quantum communication is quantified by its two-way LOCC assisted quantum capacity, which is defined as follows:

Definition 4
.

The two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity
Q^{LOCC}_{S\leftrightarrow S^{\prime}}(\mathcal{N_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}})
of quantum channel
\mathcal{N_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}}
is the highest achievable rate of faithful qubit transmission (through infinitely many uses) of the channel with the assistance of unlimited two-way classical
communication
[
29
,
30
]
.

The above definition is generalized for the situations where there is an upper bound on the average input energy:

Definition 5
.

The energy-constrained two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity
Q^{LOCC}_{S\leftrightarrow S^{\prime}}(\mathcal{N}_{{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}},G,N)
of a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}
is the two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of Definition
4
, with the constraint that the average input energy per channel use (denoted by observable
G
) is not larger than
N
.

Note that we could have considered a uniform energy constraint at the input (constraining the energy of each input) instead of considering the average input energy constraint (see e.g.
[
26
]
). However, the two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity with uniform energy constraint is upper bounded by the two-way LOCC-assisted quantum capacity with average energy constraint on input. As we derive an upper bound on the latter, the results provide an upper bound for the former too.

according to

IV
Squashing channels

In this section, first, we recall the definition of quantum Markov chain (QMC) for tri-partite quantum states
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
. Then we focus on a subset of QMC states which we call symmetric quantum Markov chains (SQMC) due to a particular symmetry they have. Subsequently we define quantum Markov chain inducer (QMCI) channels, and symmetric quantum Markov chain inducer (SQMCI) channels, which are quantum channels that transform an input state to respectively QMC state and SQMC state. Then we show that
if for system-environment output of the channel, the set of SQMCI channels is not null, then
the optimal squashing channel belongs to this set of SQMCI channels.

Definition 8
.

(
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
)
A tri-partite state
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
is a quantum Markov chain (QMC) with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
if and only if there exists a recovery channel
\mathcal{R}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
, such that

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}}),

(58)

where
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}})
.

The following lemma introduces a convenient way to verify if a given tri-partite state is a QMC state:

Lemma 1
.

A tri-partite state
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
is a QMC with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
if and only if the following relation holds
[
37
]

I(S;F^{\prime}|E^{\prime})_{\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}}=0.

(59)

Lemma
1
implies that, if a tri-partite state
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
is a QMC with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
then the marginal state
\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}
is a separable state
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
. Conversely, if
\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}
is separable, there exists an extension of it

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
(
\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{E^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}})
) such that
I(S;F^{\prime}|E^{\prime})_{\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}}=0
. For an example of QMC state with order of

S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
consider the following tri-partite state

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=\sum_{n}p_{n}\ket{s_{n}}\bra{s_{n}}\otimes\ket{e_{n}}\bra{e_{n}}\otimes\eta_{n},

(60)

where
p_{n}>0
for all
n
,
\sum_{n}p_{n}=1
,
\bra{s_{n}}s_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
,
\bra{e_{n}}e_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
and
\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{F^{\prime}})
are density matrices. It is easy to see that for the state in Eq. (
60
) we have
I(S;F^{\prime}|E^{\prime})=0
. Similarly, one can show that the state

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=\sum_{n}p_{n}\ket{s_{n}}\bra{s_{n}}\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes\ket{f_{n}}\bra{f_{n}}

(61)

with
\bra{f_{n}}f_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
and
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}})
is a QMC with the order
S\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}

In the next definition, we impose more constraints on a QMC tri-partite state by demanding symmetric properties in the order the state is a QMC:

Definition 9
.

A tri-partite state
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
is a Symmetric quantum Markov chain (SQMC), if it is a QMC with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
, and
S\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}
.

According to Lemma.
1
, if the tri-partite state
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
is a SQMC state
with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
, and
S\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}
then the marginal states

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}}
and
\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}
are separable states.
Also according to Definition (
8
), it is easy to see that given a SQMC tri-partite state, there exist recovery channels
\mathcal{R}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{R^{\prime}}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
, such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}})

(62)

\displaystyle\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{R^{\prime}}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}),

(63)

where
\sigma_{SE^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}})
, and
\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{E^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}})
.
The
quantum state

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=\sum_{n}p_{n}\Omega_{n}\otimes\ket{e_{n}}\bra{e_{n}}\otimes\ket{f_{n}}\bra{f_{n}}

(64)

with
\Omega_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{S})
,
p_{n}>0,\forall n
,
\sum_{n}p_{n}=1
and

\bra{e_{n}}e_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\bra{f_{n}}f_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}

(65)

is an example of SQMC state.

In the following, we discuss channels that can convert input states to QMC or SQMC states.

Definition 10
.

A quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}
with corresponding isometry conjugation
\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
is a quantum Markov chain inducer (QMCI) channel for a bipartite initial state
\sigma_{SE}
, if the tri-partite state

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SE}).

(66)

is a QMC with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow.F^{\prime}

An example of QMCI channel for a bipartite state
\sigma_{SE}
is the one that transforms it to a product state
\sigma_{SE}\otimes\sigma_{F^{\prime}}
, a QMC state with order of
S\leftrightarrow E\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
. Such a channel is described by an isometry over the extended state:

V_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=\sum_{n,i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\ket{e_{n},\lambda_{i}}\bra{\lambda_{i}}

(67)

where
\{\ket{e_{n}}\}
forms an orthogonal set in
\mathcal{H}_{E}
and in its isometric space
\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}
, while
\{\lambda_{i},\ket{\lambda_{i}}\}
are eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the quantum state
\sigma_{F^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{F^{\prime}})
.

The definition of QMCI channels can be generalized to channels that produce QMC with symmetric order:

Definition 11
.

A quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}
with corresponding isometry conjugation
\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}

\sigma_{SE}

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SE})

(68)

is a SQMC with the order
S\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
and
S\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}\leftrightarrow E^{\prime}

For an example of SQMCI channel for separable initial state

\sigma_{SE}=\sum_{n}p_{n}\mu_{n}\otimes\ket{e_{n}}\bra{e_{n}}

(69)

with
\mu_{n}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{S})

V_{E\rightarrow EF^{\prime}}=\sum_{l}\ket{e_{l},f_{l}}\bra{e_{l}}.

\sigma_{SEF^{\prime}}=\sum_{n}p_{n}\mu_{n}\otimes\ket{e_{n}}\bra{e_{n}}\otimes\ket{f_{n}}\bra{f_{n}},

S\leftrightarrow E\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}
and
S\leftrightarrow F^{\prime}\leftrightarrow E

Proposition 2
.

Consider a channel
\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}
with an isometry conjugation
\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}}_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}E}

\sigma_{S^{\prime}E}

\mathcal{H}_{E}
is not null, it contains the optimal squashing channel.

Proof.

Consider a generic squashing channel
\mathcal{N}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}
, and
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}
a squashing channel which is SQMCI with initial state
\sigma_{S^{\prime}E}
and denote their isometry conjugations respectively by
\mathcal{V}^{{{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{V}^{{\widetilde{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}

\mathcal{U}_{E^{\prime}F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
such that

\mathcal{U}_{E^{\prime}F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{V}^{{{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=\mathcal{V}^{{\widetilde{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}

(72)

As
\mathcal{U}_{E^{\prime}F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
acts only on
\mathcal{H}_{E}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{F}
the correlation between
\sigma_{S^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{S^{\prime}})
and
\sigma_{E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{F^{\prime}})

\mathcal{U}_{E^{\prime}F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}

I(S;E^{\prime}F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{\widetilde{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}=I(S;E^{\prime}F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}.

(74)

\displaystyle I(S;E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}+I(S;F^{\prime}|E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{{\cal N}}^{sq}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}.

or as

\displaystyle I(S;F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{\widetilde{{\cal N}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}+I(S;E^{\prime}|F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{\widetilde{{\cal N}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}=

\displaystyle I(S;E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{V}^{{\widetilde{{\cal N}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE})}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}}=(\operatorname{id}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{R^{\prime}}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}})(\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}),

\sigma_{SE^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{F^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}})

\sigma_{SF^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Tr}_{E^{\prime}}(\sigma_{SE^{\prime}F^{\prime}})

By defining

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow F^{\prime}}\coloneqq\operatorname{Tr}_{E^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{R}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}},

(91)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}}\coloneqq\operatorname{Tr}_{F^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}F^{\prime}},

(92)

and according to the data processing argument, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle I(S;E^{\prime})_{(\mathcal{{M}}^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}})(\rho_{SE})}\leq I(S;F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})}

(93)

\displaystyle I(S;F^{\prime})_{(\mathcal{{M}}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow F^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}})(\rho_{SE})}\leq I(S;E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})}.

(94)

On the other hand, the action of the SQMCI channel
\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}
and its complement
\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}}
on
\rho_{SE}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})=\mathcal{(}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}}\big)(\rho_{SE})

(96)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})=\big(\mathcal{M}_{E^{\prime}\rightarrow F^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}\big)(\rho_{SE}),

(97)

Combining Eq. (
96

93

I(S;F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})}=I(S;E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})},

(98)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(I(S;F^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}^{c}_{E\rightarrow F^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})}

\displaystyle+I(S;E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})}\Big)

(99)

\displaystyle=I(S;E^{\prime})_{\mathcal{N}_{E\rightarrow E^{\prime}}(\rho_{SE})}.

(100)

∎

This proposition simplifies computing Eq.(

) when the optimal squashing channel is a SQMCI channel.

VI
Conclusion

We have analyzed the LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channel subject to energy constraints on input states.
Quantum capacity of this non-Gaussian channel is derived in
[
20
]
and our aim
here was to address the role of LOCC on the reliable rate of entanglement establishment between sender and receiver.

Although for practical reasons it is essential to know the LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of the channels
[
28
]
, there is no compact formula in terms of entropic functions for quantifying it. It was proved that squashed entanglement of a channel is an upper bound for LOCC-assisted quantum capacity
[
26
]
and secret-key agreement capacity
[
28
]
. In order to compute squashed entanglement of a channel, optimization over both the set of input states and the set of isometric extensions of quantum channels for the optimal squashing channel is required. Thus,
even computing a bound (for the LOCC-assisted quantum capacity) through such an optimization results in general is very challenging, if not impossible.

To overcome these complications for computing the upper bound for LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channel, first, we used the phase covariant property of the channel to determine the structure of the optimal input state. In other words, we prove that it is sufficient to search for the optimal input state over the set of diagonal states in the Fock basis instead of the whole set of density operators. For the optimization over the isometric extensions of the squashing channels,
we prove that it is sufficient to restrict the search to the set of SQMCI channels of the channel environment output, if this set is non-empty.This result is valid for a generic given channel. However for the bosonic dephasing channel we found arguments supporting the conjecture that the set of SQMCI squashing channels is null. Hence, we choose the explicit examples of squashing channels for bosonic dephasing channels from the set of symmetric channels.

For the explicit examples, first we considered a 50/50 beamsplitter for the squashing channel. We showed that in this case, the LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of the channel is less than or equal to the quantum capacity of a bosonic dephasing channel having the noise parameter reduced by a factor two. Furthermore, to derive the tightness possible lower bound, we used the result in
[
41
]
where a lower bound on LOCC-assisted quantum capacity is introduced in terms of reverse coherent information.
We proved that the reverse coherent information and the quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channels are equal. Hence, when LOCC is allowed, the reliable rate for sharing entanglement between the two parties increases. Therefore, taking into account the results in
[
20
]
, we provided computable upper and lower bounds for LOCC-assisted quantum capacity of bosonic dephasing channel, and we showed that this result is valid whether or not the input state is subject to energy constraints. More importantly, we showed that these bounds are tight, meaning that the quantum capacity of a bosonic dephasing channel with noise parameter
\gamma
when LOCC is allowed is very close to the quantum capacity of a bosonic dephasing channel with noise parameter
\frac{\gamma}{2}
.
In other words, with the assistance of LOCC the effective noise parameter is halved.

To extend our analysis beyond 50/50 beamsplitter squashing channel, we also discussed the case where the squashing channel is a symmetric qubit channel. As put forward in §
V-B
, although the upper bound given by the optimal qubit squashing channel is smaller than the one with the optimal one-mode Gaussian channel for some range of noise parameter, their difference is negligible.

Our results not only set as an explicit example
to confirm the importance and tightness of the upper bound in terms of squashed entanglement of the channel, but also motivates analysing the quantum capacity of other non-Gaussian channels especially with the assistance of LOCC. Additionally, it seems interesting to devote further investigations on characterizing the set of QMCI and SQMCI channels for particular classis of initial states and addressing their performance as squashing channels.

As defined in Eq. (

\mathcal{I}_{R}(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}})=Q(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}}^{\gamma}).

(135)

To this end, we first define the following function:

I_{R}(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S})\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(\rho_{S})-\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N}^{c}_{S\rightarrow E}(\rho_{S})).

(136)

Next, we prove that
I_{R}(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S})
is concave with respect to input states
\rho_{S}
. Consider the following two classical-quantum states:

\displaystyle\sigma_{XS}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{n}\ket{n}_{X}\bra{n}\otimes\rho_{S}^{(n)},

(137)

\displaystyle\tau_{XE}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{n}\ket{n}_{X}\bra{n}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{c}_{S\rightarrow E}(\rho_{S}^{(n)}),

(138)

where
\mathcal{N}^{c}_{S\rightarrow E}
is the complementary channel to the channel
\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}}
. The following inequalities hold true:

\displaystyle I(X;E)_{\tau_{XE}}

\displaystyle\leq I(X;S)_{\sigma_{XS}},

(139)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(\tau_{E})-{\mathcal{S}}(E|X)_{\tau_{XE}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}(\sigma_{S})-{\mathcal{S}}(S|X)_{\sigma_{XS}},

(140)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(S|X)_{\sigma_{XS}}-\mathcal{S}(E|X)_{\tau_{XE}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}(\sigma_{S})-\mathcal{S}(\tau_{E}).

(141)

The first inequality is due to the data processing inequality of mutual information, the second inequality follows the definition of mutual information, and the third inequality is just a rearrangement. Therefore, due to the classical-quantum nature of the states
\sigma_{XS},\tau_{XE}
in Eq. (
137
), and the last inequality of (
139
), we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{n}p_{n}\Big(

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\rho_{S}^{(n)})-\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N}^{c}_{S\rightarrow E}(\rho_{S}^{(n)}))\Big)

(143)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}(\sum_{n}p_{n}\rho_{S}^{(n)})-\mathcal{S}(\sum_{n}p_{n}\mathcal{N}^{c}_{S\rightarrow E}(\rho_{S}^{(n)})),

(144)

which is equivalent to

\sum_{n}p_{n}I_{R}(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S}^{(n)})\leq I_{R}\Big(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\sum_{n}p_{n}\rho_{S}^{(n)}\Big),

(146)

for any probability distribution
p_{n}
. As a consequence
I_{R}(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S})
is a concave function with respect to its argument
\rho_{S}
.

On the other hand, according to Eq. (
31
) the complementary channel of a bosonic dephasing channel, is invariant under the phase shift operator of Eq.(
29
).
Then, by considering the unitarily invariance property of the von-Neumann entropy along with the invariance property of the complementary channel of bosonic dephasing channel under the action of
U_{\theta}
, we conclude that:

I_{R}(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}},{\rho_{S}({\theta})})=I_{R}(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S})

(147)

where
{\rho_{S}({\theta)}:=}U_{\theta}\rho_{S}U_{\theta}
. Employing the concavity of
I_{R}(\mathcal{N}_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}},\rho_{S})
as in Eq. (
146
), and Eq. (
147
) we are led to:

I_{R}(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S})\leq I_{R}\Big(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta p(\theta){\rho_{S}({\theta})}\Big).

(148)

Taking
p(\theta)
as a flat distribution and expanding
\rho_{S}
in the Fock basis,
\rho_{S}=\sum_{m,n}\rho_{m,n}\ket{m}_{S}\bra{n}
, we have:

\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\rho_{S}({\theta})}p(\theta)d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m,n}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\rho_{m,n}e^{\operatorname{i}\theta(n-m)}\ket{n}_{S}\bra{m}.

(149)

Inserting the above result into the r.h.s. of (
148
) we end up with:

I_{R}(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\rho_{S})\leq I_{R}\Big(\mathcal{N}^{\gamma}_{S\rightarrow{S}},\sum_{n}\rho_{n,n}\ket{n}_{S}\bra{n}\Big).

(150)

Hence, the supremum in Eq. (
133
) is achieved by a mixture of Fock states
\rho^{\prime}_{S}
. In other words, the optimization in Eqs. (
133
) and (
134
) are over the same space and it proves their equality as expressed in Eq. (
135
).

Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan bias for consecutive sums of two squares
Chantal David, Lucile Devin, Jungbae Nam and Jeremy Schlitt

Abstract
. In a surprising recent work, Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan noticed how experimental data exhibits erratic distributions for consecutive pairs of primes in arithmetic progressions, and proposed a heuristic model based on the Hardy–Littlewood conjectures containing a large secondary term, which fits the data very well. In this paper, we study consecutive pairs of sums of squares in arithmetic progressions, and develop a similar heuristic model based on the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture for sums of squares, which also explain the biases in the experimental data. In the process, we prove several results related to averages of the Hardy–Littlewood constant in the context of sums of two squares.

Introduction

We study in this paper the distribution of consecutive sums of two squares in arithmetic progressions. Our work is inspired by a recent paper of Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[
LOS16
]
who proposed a heuristic model based on the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture for the distribution of consecutive primes in arithmetic progressions.

Roughly speaking, it is expected that numbers described by reasonable multiplicative constraints should be well-distributed, in short intervals and in arithmetic progressions. The case of prime numbers is of course well-studied, and this philosophy was also tested for numbers expressible as sums of two squares, as well as square-free numbers

In the case of square-free numbers, the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture is a theorem

Some unexpected irregularities in the distribution of primes in short intervals were discovered by Maier

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)\sim K\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log{x}}},

The distribution of sum of two squares in arithmetic progressions exhibit different behavior depending on the modulus

Estimates for the consecutive sums of squares (or consecutive primes) in arithmetic progressions is a very difficult question, and few results are known. For consecutive primes in arithmetic progressions, it was conjectured by Chowla that there are infinitively many primes
p_{n}
such that
p_{n+i-1}\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
for
1\leq i\leq r
, for any
(a,q)=1
and
r\geq 2
. This was proven by Shiu
[
Shi00
]
. Recent progress in sieve theory have led to a new proof of Shiu’s result
[
BFT15
]
, and Maynard has proven that the number of such primes is
\gg\pi(x)

[
May16
]
. It would be interesting to see if those recent progresses could be applied to get lower bounds for the number of successive sums of two squares
E_{n}
such that
E_{n+i-1}\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
for
1\leq i\leq r
, for any
a
and
r\geq 2
, but this question was not addressed yet in the literature.

We propose in this paper a heuristic model predicting an asymptotic for
N(x;q,(a,b))
, based on the heuristic of Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[
LOS16
]
for the case of primes, and exhibiting a similar bias.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Fix a prime
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, and
J\geq 1
. Then, for any
a\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle N(x;q,(a,a))

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{q^{2}}\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}\phi(q)}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{\log\log{x}}}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}C_{j}(\log\log x)^{\frac{1}{2}-j}\bigg)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{x}{\log{x}(\log{\log x})^{J+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

for some explicit constants
C_{j}
depending only on
q
.
For
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
a\not\equiv b\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
, we have

\displaystyle N(x;q,(a,b))

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{q^{2}}\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{\log\log{x}}}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}+\frac{C_{a,b}}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}-\frac{1}{\phi(q)\sqrt{\log{x}}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}C_{j}(\log\log x)^{\frac{1}{2}-j}\bigg)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{x}{\log{x}(\log{\log x})^{J+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

with

(2)

C_{a,b}:=\frac{1}{2K}\frac{q}{\phi(q)}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\overline{\chi}(b-a)C_{q,\chi},

where the sum is over the non-principal Dirichlet characters modulo
q
and
C_{q,\chi}
is defined in (
28
). The value of
C_{1}
is given in
Conjecture
4.3
.

Our heuristic model leading to Conjecture

follows very closely
[

Fix

\{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}

As the results of

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
be a prime.
For each Dirichlet character
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
, let
C_{q,\chi}
be defined by (
28
).
Then, for any
J\geq 1
, and
v\neq 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{h\geq 1}\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})e^{-h/H}

\displaystyle=H-\frac{2}{K\pi}\sqrt{\log\>H}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}c(j)\,(\log{H})^{1/2-j}+O\left((\log{H})^{-1/2-J}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geq 1\\
h\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})e^{-h/H}

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{q}-\frac{2}{K\pi}\sqrt{\log\>H}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{0}(j)\,(\log{H})^{1/2-j}+O\left((\log{H})^{-1/2-J}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\geq 1\\
h\equiv v\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})e^{-h/H}

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{q}+{\frac{1}{2K^{2}\phi(q)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\bar{\chi}(v)C_{q,\chi}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{1}(j)\,(\log{H})^{1/2-j}+O\left((\log{H})^{-1/2-J}\right).

The constants

are absolute while the constants

depend only on

, they can all be explicitly computed, in particular the values for

are given in (

) and (

Moreover they satisfy the relation

(3)

\displaystyle c_{0}(j)+\phi(q)c_{1}(j)=c(j),\hskip 5.69046ptj\geq 1.

, which is the analogue of the work

2.
Sums of two squares in arithmetic progressions

By a classical result of Landau
[
Lan08
]
, we have

(4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)\sim{K}\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}},

where

(5)

\displaystyle K=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\prod_{p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}(1-p^{-2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

is the Landau–Ramanujan constant.
We remark that, unlike the prime number theorem, the asymptotic above gives only the main term, and there is no simple integral similar to
{\text{li}}(x)
which approximates well the number of sums of two squares up to
x
. This is caused by the fact that the generating series for sums of two squares has an essential singularity at
s=1

4

(6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)={K}x\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{c_{j}}{(\log x)^{1/2+j}}\right)+O\left(x(\log{x})^{-3/2-J}\right).

Explicit values for the constants
c_{j}
can be found in the literature for
c_{0}=1

[
Lan08
]
,
c_{1}=0.581948659\cdots

[
Sta28
,
Sta29
,
Sha64
]
and up to
c_{15}
in
[
EG18
]
.
It is possible to get an expression for the number of sums of two squares smaller than
x
with a better error term,
but one looses the simplicity of the formula above as a sum of descending powers of

. We state this result in the next theorem, that we will prove in Section
7
. A similar expression for the number of sums of two squares exhibiting squareroot cancellation under the GRH can be found in
[

p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, it is easy to see that for
\re(s)>1
,

\displaystyle F^{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\not\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)^{-2}\prod_{p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-2}

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{4})\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{s}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\equiv 3(4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}

where
\chi_{4}
is the non-principal Dirichlet character modulo 4.
Landau
[
Lan08
]
also showed
that in a neighborhood of
s=1
,

\frac{F(s)}{s^{2}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}ia_{\ell}(1-s)^{\ell-1/2},

with

a_{0}=K\sqrt{\pi}
and

a_{1}=a_{0}(2c_{1}+1)

[
Sha64
]
. Applying Theorem
2.3
with
\kappa=1/2
, we get (
6
), using the values
a_{0},a_{1}
to get explicit values for the first two Taylor coefficients of
(s-1)^{1/2}F(s)/s
.

To introduce the congruence condition, we write for
a\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
,

(8)

\displaystyle N(x;q,a)=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n),

and we denote the generating function of
f_{\chi}(n)=\chi(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)
by
F_{\chi}(s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)n^{-s}.

For
\chi_{0}
the principal character modulo
q
and
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
, we have for
\re(s)>1
,

(9)

\begin{split}F_{\chi}^{2}(s)&=L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi_{4}\chi)\left(1-\frac{\chi(2)}{2^{s}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\equiv 3(4)}\left(1-\frac{\chi^{2}(p)}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}\\
F_{\chi_{0}}^{2}(s)&=\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{s}}\right)^{2}F^{2}(s).\end{split}

For
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
, since
F_{\chi}(s)
is analytic for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1/2
, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
that

\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)=O\left(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right),

and the theorem will follow by evaluating
\sum_{n\leq x}\chi_{0}(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)
with the Selberg–Delange method.
Let
\widetilde{b_{j}}
be the Taylor coefficients of
(s-1)^{1/2}F_{\chi_{0}}(s)/s
around
s=1
, and
\widetilde{c_{j}}
are the Taylor coefficients of
(s-1)^{1/2}F(s)/s
around
s=1
.
From (
9
), it is easy to compute

\displaystyle\widetilde{b_{0}}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{-1})\,\widetilde{c_{0}}=(1-q^{-1})\,K\sqrt{\pi}

\displaystyle\widetilde{b_{1}}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{-1})\,\widetilde{c_{1}}+\frac{\log q}{q}\,\widetilde{c_{0}}=K\sqrt{\pi}\left(\frac{\log{q}}{q}-2c_{1}(1-q^{-1})\right).

Applying Theorem
2.3
with
\kappa=1/2
, to estimate the sum
\sum_{n\leq x}\chi_{0}(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n),

and replacing in (
8
), we get
the statement of the theorem when
a\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
, with

\frac{K}{q}c_{0,a}=\frac{\widetilde{b_{0}}}{(q-1)\Gamma(1/2)},\;\;\;\;\frac{K}{q}c_{1,a}=\frac{\widetilde{b_{1}}}{(q-1)\Gamma(-1/2)}.

For
a\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
, we use the above and (
6
) to obtain

\displaystyle N(x;q,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)-\sum_{a\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)}N(x;q,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{q}x\left(\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/2}}+\left(c_{1}+\frac{\log{q}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{(\log x)^{3/2}}+\sum_{j=2}^{J}\frac{c_{j}-(q-1)c_{j,1}}{(\log x)^{1/2+j}}\right)+O\left(x(\log x)^{-3/2-J}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

3.
Hardy–Littlewood conjectures in arithmetic progressions for sum of two squares

We state in this section the analogue of the Hardy–Littlewood prime
k
-tuple conjectures for the case of sums of two squares, following
[
FKR17
]
. We also state new bounds on the average of the Hardy–Littlewood constant in this context that are useful in our heuristic for Conjecture
1.1
, but are also interesting in themselves as they are related to the distribution of gaps between sums of two squares.

For
k\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{H}=\left\{h_{1},\dots,h_{k}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, and

\displaystyle R_{k}(\mathcal{H};x):=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{1})\dots\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{k}).

In the case
\mathcal{H}=\{0\}
, we have

R_{1}(x):=R_{1}(\{0\};x)=\frac{1}{x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)\sim\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log x}}.

The philosophy of the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture is that the events
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{i})
are “independent”, and the probability that
n+h_{i}
are simultaneously sums of two squares for
1\leq i\leq k
is the product of the probabilities, which is (ignoring the small differences between
\log{n}
or
\log{n+h_{i}}
)

\left(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}\right)^{k}.

Of course, the events are not really independent, so we adjust by considering the probabilities that
n+h_{i}
are sums of two squares modulo
p
versus the probably that
k
independent integers are sums of two squares modulo
p
. To do so, for each prime
p
, we define

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{H}}(p)=\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{0\leq a<p^{\alpha}:\forall h\in\mathcal{H},a+h\equiv\square+\square\,(\mathrm{mod}\,p^{\alpha})\}}{p^{\alpha}}.

Since
\delta_{\mathcal{H}}(p)=1
for
p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
(see e.g.
[
FKR17
, Proposition 5.1]
),
we define the singular series for
\mathcal{H}=\left\{h_{1},\dots,h_{k}\right\}
by

(10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H}):=\prod_{p\not\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{H}}(p)}{(\delta_{\{0\}}(p))^{k}}.

It is proven in
[
FKR17
]
that the limit defining
\delta_{\mathcal{H}}(p)
exists, and the the Euler product converges to a non-zero limit provided that
\delta_{\mathcal{H}}(p)>0
for all
p\not\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
.

Conjecture 3.1
.

[
FKR17
, Conjecture 1.1]

Fix
k\geq 1
, and
\mathcal{H}=\left\{h_{1},\dots,h_{k}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. If
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})>0
, then

R_{k}(\mathcal{H};x)\sim\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})\left(R_{1}(x)\right)^{k}\sim\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})\left(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right)^{k}

This conjecture is still open, but it is known that
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{1})\dots\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{k})
is infinite
for
k=2,3
by the work of Hooley
[
Hoo71
,
Hoo73
]
.

It is not straightforward to give a simple formula for the singular series
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})
for a given set
\mathcal{H}
(see Section
6
), except the trivial cases
\mathfrak{S}(\emptyset)=\mathfrak{S}(\{h\})=1.
For
\mathcal{H}=\left\{0,h\right\}
, Connors and Keating
[
CK97
]
computed

(11)

\mathfrak{S}(\{0,h\})=\frac{1}{2K^{2}}W_{2}(h)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)\\
p\mid h\end{subarray}}\frac{1-p^{-v_{p}(h)-1}}{1-p^{-1}},

where

W_{2}(h)=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }2\nmid h\\
2-3\cdot 2^{-v_{2}(h)}\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

and
v_{p}
is the
p
-adic valuation.

Notice that it means that
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})>0
when
k=2
. This can also be proven for
k=3
, but for general
k
, we can find sets
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})=0
.
It is easy to see that
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{1})\dots\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{k})
is finite
when
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})=0
.

We now state a slight generalization of the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture where
n
is restricted to an arithmetic progression modulo
q
.

Conjecture 3.2
.

(Hardy–Littlewood for sums of two squares in arithmetic progressions) Fix
k\geq 1
, and
\mathcal{H}=\left\{h_{1},\dots,h_{k}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
be a prime, and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})>0
, then

\displaystyle R_{k}(\mathcal{H};x,q,a)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{1})\dots\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h_{k})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H})}{q}\left(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right)^{k}.

We remark that unlike the generalized Hardy–Littlewood conjecture of
[

4.
Heuristic for the conjecture

We now develop a heuristic leading to Conjecture
1.1
following
[
LOS16
]
.
Let
q\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
be a prime, and we recall that

N(x;q,(a,b))=\#\{E_{n}\leq x:E_{n}\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q),E_{n+1}\equiv b\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\}.

We first write

(14)

\displaystyle N(x;q,(a,b))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h)\prod_{t=1}^{h-1}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+t)).

We introduce the notation

\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)-\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log n}},

and for each fixed
h
in (
14
), we study the sum

\displaystyle S_{h}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log n}}+\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{E}}(n)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log{(n+h)}}}+\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+h)\bigg)\prod_{0<t<h}\bigg(1-\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log{(n+t)}}}-\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+t)\bigg).

If we ignore the small differences among
\sqrt{\log{n}}
,
\sqrt{\log(n+h)}
, and
\sqrt{\log{(n+t)}}
and we expand out the product, we get

\displaystyle S_{h}=\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log n}}\bigg)^{2-|\mathcal{A}|}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[1,h-1]\\
t\not\in\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\bigg(1-\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log n}}\bigg)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{A\cup T}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+t)

Finally, denoting

\alpha(n)=1-\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log n}},

and using (
12
), we conjecture that

\displaystyle S_{h}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log(n)}}\bigg)^{2-|\mathcal{A}|}\alpha(n)^{{h-1-|\mathcal{T}|}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{A\cup T}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{\mathbf{E}}(n+t)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{x}{q}\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T})\bigg(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{2+|\mathcal{T}|}\alpha(x)^{h-1-\lvert\mathcal{T}\rvert}.

We emphasize that this is a heuristic argument: in obtaining this expression for
S_{h}
, we have not paid attention to the error terms in (
12
), in particular on the
dependency on the size of the sets
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T}
and on
h
.

Summing
S_{h}
over all
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)
, this gives the conjectural estimate

(15)

N(x;q,(a,b))\sim\frac{x}{q}{\alpha(x)^{-1}}\bigg(\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{2}\mathcal{D}(a,b;x),

where

(16)

\mathcal{D}(a,b;x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,h\}}\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T})\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{|\mathcal{T}|}\alpha(x)^{{h}}.

In order to evaluate (
16
), we will use the following notations. Let

(17)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)^{h}=\left(1-\frac{K}{\sqrt{\log{x}}}\right)^{h}=e^{-h/H}\iff H=-\frac{1}{\log{\alpha(x)}},

which implies that

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\log{x}}}{K}-\frac{1}{2}+O\left((\log x)^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\log{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log{\log{x}}-\log{K}+O\left((\log x)^{-1/2}\right).

4.1.
Discarding the singular series involving larger sets

We approximate
\mathcal{D}(a,b;x)
by discarding all the singular series where
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T}
has more than 2 elements, which is justified by
Theorem
3.4
. We separate in 3 cases, depending on the possible choices for the set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{0,h\}
.
We use the notation defined in (
17
) for
H
, and the bound
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv v\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\alpha(x)^{h}h^{\ell}\ll_{\ell}H^{\ell+1}
for any
\ell\geq 0
, and
v\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\mathcal{A}=\emptyset
, then
for
k\geq 3
, we deduce from Theorem
3.4
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]\\
\lvert\mathcal{T}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{T})\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}\alpha(x)^{{h}}\ll_{k}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}h^{\frac{k}{2}+\varepsilon}\alpha(x)^{{h}}

\displaystyle\ll_{k}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}H^{1+\frac{k}{2}+\varepsilon}\ll_{k}(\log x)^{-\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

If
\mathcal{A}=\{h\}
and
\lvert\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T}\rvert\geq 3
, we have for

k\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]\\
\lvert\mathcal{T}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{T}\cup\{h\})\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}\alpha(x)^{h}

\displaystyle\approx_{k}\frac{1}{q}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subset[1,H]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k+1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})\ll_{k}\frac{1}{q}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}H^{\frac{k+1}{2}+\varepsilon}\ll_{k}\frac{1}{q}(\log x)^{-\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon},

where we are approximating the sum over
h
and
{\mathcal{T}}
of the first line as the sum over all
\mathcal{D}
of size
k+1
contained in
[1,H]
, which we then bound by Theorem
3.4
.
We obtain the same bound for
\mathcal{A}=\{0\}
using the fact that
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
is invariant by translation.

Finally, in the case
\mathcal{A}=\{0,h\}
, we introduce an extra average. Since
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
is translation invariant, we have

\sum_{s\geq 1}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{s,t_{1}+s,\dots,t_{k}+s,h+s\})e^{-{s}/{H}}=\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,t_{1},\dots,t_{k},h\})\sum_{s\geq 1}e^{-{s}/{H}}\approx\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{0,t_{1},\dots,t_{k},h\})H,

and using this, we get for

k\geq 1

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
h\equiv b-a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{T}\subset[1,h-1]\\
\lvert\mathcal{T}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{T}\cup\{0,h\})\alpha(x)^{h}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{qH}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{s\geq 1}\sum_{h\geq 1}\sum_{0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}<h}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{s,t_{1}+s,\dots,t_{k}+s,h+s\})e^{-{(s+h)}/{H}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{qH}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{0<s<t^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<t^{\prime}_{k}<h^{\prime}<2H}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\{s,t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{k},h^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle\ll_{k}\frac{1}{q}\bigg(\frac{K}{\alpha(x)\sqrt{\log x}}\bigg)^{k}(2H)^{-1+\frac{k+2}{2}+\varepsilon}\ll_{k}\frac{1}{q}(\log x)^{-\frac{k}{4}+\varepsilon}.

Discarding all the singular series where
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T}
has more than 2 elements from (
16
), and working again heuristically by ignoring the dependence on
\lvert\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{T}\rvert
in the error terms, we are led to the model

\mathcal{D}(a,b;x)=(\mathcal{D}_{0}+\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathcal{D}_{2})(a,b;x)+O_{\varepsilon}((\log x)^{-\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}),

where

\mathcal{D}_{0}(a,b;x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\

By definition we have

for all prime number
p
, thus

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq y}\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{D}}(p)}{\delta_{\{0\}}(p)^{k}}\leq 2^{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)\end{subarray}}(1+\tfrac{1}{p})^{k}\ll_{k}(\log y)^{k},

which gives

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{D})=\prod_{p\leq y}\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{D}}(p)}{\delta_{\{0\}}(p)^{k}}+O(y^{-1}(\log y)^{k-1}).

Using Lemma
6.1
and the bound
\lvert A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})\rvert\leq 2^{\frac{k}{2}}\frac{q_{1}\dots q_{k}}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}
(see (
46
)), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{D}}(p)}{\delta_{\{0\}}(p)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid p^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})+O_{k}\big(\sum_{p^{N+1}\mid q_{1}\mid p^{\infty}}\sum_{q_{2},\dots,q_{k}\mid p^{\infty}}\frac{1}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid p^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})+O_{k}\big(\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{k-1}}{p^{n}}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid p^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})+O_{k}\big(p^{-N-1}(N+2)^{k-1}\big).

Moreover, using again the bound (
46
), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq y}\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid p^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})

\displaystyle\ll_{k}\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lvert\lambda_{2}(q_{i})\rvert}{q_{i}}\frac{q_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot q_{k}}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{1}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}

(41)

\displaystyle\ll_{k}\prod_{p\leq y}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{(n+1)^{k}}{p^{n}}\leq\prod_{p\leq y}\big(1+\frac{C_{k}}{p}\big)\ll_{\varepsilon}y^{\varepsilon}

for some constant
C_{k}>0
, for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Finally,

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq y}\frac{\delta_{\mathcal{D}}(p)}{\delta_{\{0\}}(p)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})+O_{k,\varepsilon}\big(y^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid P_{y}\\
q\neq 1\end{subarray}}q^{-N-1}N^{(k-1)\omega(q)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})+O_{k,\varepsilon}\big(y^{\varepsilon}2^{-N}N^{k-1}\big).

Choosing
\frac{\log y}{\log 2}(1+\varepsilon)<N
gives (
39
).
We deduce (
40
)
from (
39
) using the formula

\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}(-1)^{\lvert\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{T}\rvert}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{T})

and the relation

A_{\{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k-1},1)=A_{\{d_{1},\dots,d_{k-1}\}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k-1})
.
∎

In particular,
taking
y=h^{k+1}
in (
40
), one has

(42)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})+o_{k}(1).

6.2.
An easier version of the main term

To continue with notation similar to
[
MS04
]
, we define

(43)

\displaystyle V_{k}(y,N,h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}\sum_{1\leq d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\leq h}A_{(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k}),

where we remark that the difference with the main term above is that
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}
do not have to be distinct.
Let us introduce some other useful notations and results from
[
MS04
]
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote

(44)

\displaystyle E_{h}(\alpha)=\sum_{d=1}^{h}e(\alpha d)\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{h}(\alpha)=\min(h,\lVert\alpha\rVert^{-1}),

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is the distance to the nearest integer, so that we have
\lvert E_{h}(\alpha)\rvert\leq F_{h}(\alpha)
.
We have (see
[
MS04
, (54)]
)

(45)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}F_{h}(\tfrac{a}{q})^{2}\ll q\min(q,h).

We will also use the following result from the work of Montgomery and Vaughan
[
MV84
]
and which is an analogue of
[
MS04
, Lemma 1]
that applies to the case of non necessarily square-free numbers.

Lemma 6.3

(Theorem 1 of
[
MV84
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and for
1\leq i\leq k
, let
q_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
G_{i}
be a
1
-periodic complex valued function.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}G_{i}(\tfrac{a_{i}}{q_{i}})\Big\rvert\leq\frac{1}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(q_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i}\\
(q_{i},a_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\lvert G_{i}(\tfrac{a_{i}}{q_{i}})\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular we deduce the bound for
A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})
that we used in the proofs of Lemma
6.1
and
6.2
:

(46)

\displaystyle\lvert A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})\rvert\leq\frac{1}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(q_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i}\\
(q_{i},a_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\lvert C(q_{i},a_{i})\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We also have a bound for
V_{k}(y,N,h)
.

Corollary 6.4
.

For any
h,y,N>0
,
one has
V_{k}(y,N,h)\ll_{k,\varepsilon}h^{\frac{k}{2}}y^{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Recall that we defined

\displaystyle V_{k}(y,N,h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})}{q_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}E_{h}\Big(\frac{a_{i}d_{i}}{q_{i}}\Big)C(q_{i},a_{i}),

where
C(q,a)=1
for odd
q
and
\lvert C(q,a)\rvert\leq 2
in general.
We use (
44
) to write

\displaystyle\lvert V_{k}(y,N,h)\rvert\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{2}{q_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}F_{h}(\tfrac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}).

Then Lemma
6.3
, (
45
) and the bound in (
6.1
) yield

\displaystyle\lvert V_{k}(y,N,h)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{k}}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{q_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i}\\
(q_{i},a_{i})=1\end{subarray}}F_{h}(\tfrac{a_{i}}{q_{i}})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{k}}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}h^{\frac{k}{2}}\ll_{k,\varepsilon}h^{\frac{k}{2}}y^{\varepsilon},

which is the bound announced.
∎

6.3.
The main estimate

We now prove the analogue of
[
MS04
, (60)]
, writing
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})
in terms of
V_{k}(y,N,h)
.
Again, the idea of the proof is very similar to the work of Montgomery and Soundararajan, except that we deal with a wider summation (namely a sum over all integers instead of a sum over square-free integers).

Lemma 6.5
.

For any
h>k\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
y=h^{k+1}
and
N\geq 4\log y
.
One has

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\left(-h\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\right)^{j}V_{k-2j}(y,N,h)+O_{k,\varepsilon}(h^{\frac{k-1}{2}}y^{\varepsilon}),

where
V_{k}(y,N,h)
is defined in (
43
), and

C(d)=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }d\text{ is odd}\\
0\text{ if }2\mid q,4\nmid d\\
4\text{ if }4\mid d.\end{cases}

Proof.

Following the arguments of
[
MS04
]
, we can prove the analogue of
[
MS04
, (52)]
in our context, which is

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}A_{\mathcal{D}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})=\sum_{\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{M}\}}w(\mathcal{P})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}C(q_{i},a_{i})\prod_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{d_{m}=1}^{h}e\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}d_{m}\right),

where the first sum is over partitions
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{M}\}
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
, and
w(\mathcal{P})
is defined in
[
MS04
, p. 17]
.

In the case of a partition
\mathcal{P}
containing at least one part of size
\geq 3
,
write
\mathcal{N}_{1}=\bigcup_{\lvert\mathcal{S}_{m}\rvert=1}\mathcal{S}_{m}
,
\mathcal{N}_{2}=\{1,\dots,k\}\smallsetminus\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
m_{2}=\lvert\{1\leq m\leq M:\lvert\mathcal{S}_{m}\rvert\geq 2\}\rvert
.
Using (
44
) and
\lvert C(q,x)\rvert\leq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}C(q_{i},a_{i})\prod_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{d_{m}=1}^{h}e\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}d_{m}\right)\Big\rvert\leq 2^{k}h^{m_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}F_{h}(\tfrac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}).

Then we apply Lemma
6.3
and the bound (
45
) to obtain that the sum above is

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k}h^{m_{2}}}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{1}}\Big(q_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i}\\
(q_{i},a_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\big(F_{h}(\tfrac{a_{i}}{q_{i}})\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{2}}\Big(q_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i}\\
(q_{i},a_{i})=1\end{subarray}}1^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k}h^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\frac{q_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot q_{k}}{[q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]},

where we used
\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert\mathcal{N}_{1}\rvert+m_{2}\leq\tfrac{k-1}{2}
when
the partition
\mathcal{P}
contains at least one part of size
\geq 3
.
Replacing this bound in (
47
) and then in (
42
), we sum over
1<q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}
as in (
42
) and use the bound (
6.1
) to obtain that the contribution of the partitions containing at least one part of size
\geq 3

in
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})
is at most
O_{k,\varepsilon}(h^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\varepsilon})
.

We now turn our attention to partitions of
\{1,\dots,k\}
with sets of size at most
2
.
The combinatorics leading to
[
MS04
, (56)]
work similarly and give

(48)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})=&\sum_{0\leq j\leq\frac{k}{2}}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\sum_{r_{1},\dots,r_{j}\mid P_{y}^{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1},\dots,b_{j}\\
1\leq b_{i}\leq r_{i},(r_{i},b_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}H\big(\tfrac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\big)\\
&\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2j+1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{j}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}+\sum_{i=2j+1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=2j+1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})C(q_{i},a_{i})}{q_{i}}\sum_{d_{i}=1}^{h}e\left(\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}d_{i}\right)\\
&+O_{k}(h^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\varepsilon})\end{split}

where

H\big(\tfrac{b}{r}\big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},q_{2}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}\in\frac{b}{r}+\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{1})C(q_{1},a_{1})\lambda_{2}(q_{2})C(q_{2},a_{2})}{q_{1}q_{2}}\sum_{d=1}^{h}e\left(\frac{b}{r}d\right).

In particular, we have

\displaystyle H(1)=\sum_{1<q\mid P_{y}^{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\\
(q,a)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{C(q,a)C(q,q-a)}{q^{2}}h=h\sum_{1<q\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(q)\phi(q)}{q^{2}},

and the contribution of the terms with all
r_{i}=1
in the sum above is

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq\frac{k}{2}}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\Big(-h\sum_{1<q\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(q)\phi(q)}{q^{2}}\Big)^{j}V_{k-2j}(y,N,h).

We now show that the contribution to (
48
) of the terms
where not all
r_{i}
are 1 can be absorbed in the error term.
Let
\ell
be the number of
i
’s for which
r_{i}>1
.
For any
\ell>0
, and any
r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell},q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}>1
up to re-ordering and applying Lemma
6.3
, we have

(49)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}\\
1\leq b_{i}\leq r_{i},(r_{i},b_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H\big(\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2j+1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{j}\frac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}+\sum_{i=2j+1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=2j+1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})C(q_{i},a_{i})}{q_{i}}\sum_{d_{i}=1}^{h}e\left(\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}d_{i}\right)\\
\ll_{k}\frac{1}{[r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell},q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}]}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big(r_{i}\sum_{1\leq b\leq r_{i},(r_{i},b)=1}\left\lvert H\big(\frac{b}{r_{i}}\big)\right\rvert^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\times\prod_{i=2j+1}^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{q_{i}}\sum_{1\leq a\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a)=1}\left\lvert F_{h}\big(\frac{a}{q_{i}}\big)\right\rvert^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

To obtain a bound for
\left\lvert H\big(\frac{b}{r}\big)\right\rvert
we proceed similarly to
[
MS04
]
which gives

\displaystyle H\big(\tfrac{b}{r}\big)

\displaystyle\ll F_{h}\big(\tfrac{b}{r}\big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1},s_{2}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
[s_{1},s_{2}]=r\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1},c_{2}\\
1\leq c_{i}\leq s_{i},(s_{i},c_{i})=1\\
\frac{c_{1}}{s_{1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{s_{2}}\in\frac{b}{r}+\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s_{1}s_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
(t,r)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(t)}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll F_{h}\big(\tfrac{b}{r}\big)\frac{1}{\phi(r)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1},s_{2}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
[s_{1},s_{2}]=r\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(s_{1})\phi(s_{2})}{s_{1}s_{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\nmid r\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{1-p^{-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}p^{-n}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll F_{h}\big(\tfrac{b}{r}\big)\frac{1}{r}\prod_{p\mid r}(1-p^{-1})^{2}(1+v_{p}(r)-v_{p}(r)p^{-1})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\nmid r\end{subarray}}\Big(1+p^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll F_{h}\big(\tfrac{b}{r}\big)\frac{d(r)}{r}\log y,

where
d(r)
is the number of divisors of
r
.
Using (
45
), this gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b\leq r\\
(r,b)=1\end{subarray}}\big\lvert H(\tfrac{b}{r})\big\rvert^{2}\leq\min(r,h)\frac{d(r)^{2}}{r}(\log y)^{2}.

Using this bound and (
45
) in (
49
) summed over all
r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell},q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}>1
divisors of
P_{y}^{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
r_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}\\
1\leq b_{i}\leq r_{i},(r_{i},b_{i})=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H\big(\tfrac{b_{i}}{r_{i}}\big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{2j+1},\dots,a_{k}\\
1\leq a_{i}\leq q_{i},(q_{i},a_{i})=1\\
\sum_{i=1}^{j}\frac{y_{i}}{r_{i}}+\sum_{i=2j+1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=2j+1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{2}(q_{i})C(q_{i},a_{i})}{q_{i}}\sum_{d_{i}=1}^{h}e\left(\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
r_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}\mid P_{y}^{N}\\
q_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell},q_{2j+1},\dots,q_{k}]}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big(h^{\frac{1}{2}}d(r_{i})\log y\Big)h^{\frac{k-2j}{2}}

\displaystyle\ll_{k}

\displaystyle h^{\frac{k+\ell-2j}{2}}(\log y)^{\ell}\sum_{m\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{1}{m}\Big(\sum_{r\mid m}d(r)\Big)^{\ell}\Big(\sum_{q\mid m}1\Big)^{k-2j}

\displaystyle\ll_{k}

\displaystyle h^{\frac{k+\ell-2j}{2}}(\log y)^{\ell}\prod_{p\mid P_{y}}\Big(1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}(n+1)^{k+\ell-2j}\big(\frac{n+2}{2}\big)^{\ell}p^{-n}\Big)\ll_{k,\ell,j,\varepsilon}h^{\frac{k+\ell-2j}{2}}y^{\varepsilon}.

Finally, summing the contribution for each
\ell\geq 0
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\left(-h\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\right)^{j}V_{k-2j}(y,N,h)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\binom{j}{\ell}\left(h\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\right)^{j-\ell}O\big(h^{\frac{k+\ell-2j}{2}}y^{\varepsilon}\big)+O_{k}(h^{(k-1+\varepsilon)/2}),

and using

\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\ll_{\varepsilon}y^{\varepsilon},

we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\left(-h\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\right)^{j}V_{k-2j}(y,N,h)+O_{k,\varepsilon}(h^{\frac{k-1}{2}}y^{\varepsilon}).

which completes the proof of Lemma
6.5
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3.4
is now relatively straightforward.
Lemma
6.5
gives
for any
h>k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
N\geq 4(k+1)\log h

that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\left(-h\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\right)^{j}V_{k-2j}(h^{k+1},N,h)+O_{k,\varepsilon}(h^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\varepsilon}).

Then the bound from Corollary
6.4
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\subseteq[1,h]\\
\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert=k\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{0}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\ll_{k,\varepsilon}\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}\binom{k}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{j!2^{j}}\left(h\sum_{1<d\mid P_{y}^{N}}\frac{C(d)\phi(d)}{d^{2}}\right)^{j}h^{\frac{k-2j}{2}+\varepsilon}+O_{k,\varepsilon}(h^{\frac{k-1}{2}+\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\ll_{k}h^{\frac{k}{2}+\varepsilon}\Big(\prod_{p<y}1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\frac{-k}{2}}\ll_{k,\varepsilon}h^{\frac{k}{2}+2\varepsilon},

which finishes the proof of Theorem
3.4
.

\displaystyle\sim\frac{H}{q}+\frac{1}{\pi K^{2}}\int_{1/2+\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{F^{\prime}(\sigma)H^{\sigma-1}+F(\sigma)H^{\sigma-1}(\log{H}-A_{q}(\sigma)/2)}{|\sigma-1|^{1/2}}d\sigma.

∎

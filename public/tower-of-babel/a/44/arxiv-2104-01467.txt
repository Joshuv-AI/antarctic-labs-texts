Factorization for entropy production of the Eikonal equation and regularity
Andrew Lorent, Guanying Peng

Abstract
The Eikonal equation arises naturally in the limit of the second order Aviles-Giga functional whose Γ \Gamma -convergence is a long standing challenging problem. The theory of entropy solutions of the Eikonal equation plays a central role in the variational analysis of this problem. Establishing fine structures of entropy solutions of the Eikonal equation, e.g. concentration of entropy measures on ℋ 1 \mathcal{H}^{1} -rectifiable sets in 2 2 D, is arguably the key missing part for a proof of the full Γ \Gamma -convergence of the Aviles-Giga functional. In the first part of this work, for p ∈ ( 1 , 4 3 ] p\in\left(1,\frac{4}{3}\right] we establish an L p L^{p} version of the main theorem of [ GL20 ] . Specifically we show that if m m is a solution to the Eikonal equation, then m ∈ B 3 ​ p , ∞ , loc 1 3 m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty,{\rm loc}} is equivalent to all entropy productions of m m being in L loc p L^{p}_{{\rm loc}} . Given the main result of [ GL20 ] , this result also shows that as a consequence of a weak form of the Aviles-Giga conjecture (namely the conjecture that all solutions to the Eikonal equation whose entropy productions are in L loc p L^{p}_{{\rm loc}} are rigid) - the rigidity/flexibility threshold of the Eikonal equation is exactly the space B 3 , ∞ , loc 1 3 B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty,{\rm loc}} . In the second part of this paper, under the assumption that all entropy productions are in L loc p L^{p}_{{\rm loc}} , we establish a factorization formula for entropy productions of solutions of the Eikonal equation in terms of the two Jin-Kohn entropies. A consequence of this formula is control of all entropy productions by the Jin-Kohn entropies in the L p L^{p} setting - this is a strong extension of the main result of [ LP18 ] .

Introduction

1.1
The Aviles-Giga functional and the Eikonal equation

The Aviles-Giga functional is a second order functional that (subject to appropriate boundary conditions) models phenomena from thin film blistering to smectic liquid crystals, and is also the most natural higher order generalization of the Cahn-Hilliard functional. It is defined as

AG_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon\left|\nabla^{2}u\right|^{2}+\frac{\left(1-\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\;dx

for
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
over a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, where
\nabla^{2}u
is the Hessian matrix of the scalar-valued function
u
and
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter. The Aviles-Giga conjecture for the
\Gamma
-limit of
AG_{\varepsilon}
is one of the central conjectures in the theory of
\Gamma
-convergence and has attracted a great deal of attention, yet remains open; see for example
[
AG87
,
AG96
,
ADLM99
,
DMKO01
,
DLO03
,
CDL07
,
Pol07
,
GL20
]
. What makes the Aviles-Giga conjecture much more challenging than the
\Gamma
-convergence of the Cahn-Hilliard functional is the cubic power scaling in the former, which makes the
BV
function theory inapplicable.

One of the foundational theorems established for the Aviles-Giga functional is the compactness
[
ADLM99
,
DMKO01
]
. Specifically, given a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}\subset W^{2,2}(\Omega)
such that
\sup_{\varepsilon}AG_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty
, it has a subsequence that converges strongly in
W^{1,3}
to some limiting function
u
(here we are stating the compactness result in
[
ADLM99
]
; the version proved in
[
DMKO01
]
is slightly different). The limiting function
u
must satisfy the Eikonal equation given by

\left|\nabla u\right|=1\quad\text{ a.e in }\Omega.

In two dimensions, the above Eikonal equation can be equivalently formulated in terms of vector fields
m:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as

|m|=1\text{ a.e.},\;\;\;\;\;\dv m=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

(1)

by identifying
m=\nabla^{\perp}u
. This formulation of the Eikonal equation in
2
D is enlightening in that one can view (
1
) as a scalar conservation law in
1
D. This was first observed by the authors of
[
DMKO01
]
, who introduced the concept of
entropies as a central tool for the analysis of the Aviles-Giga functional (the implicit use of the concept of entropies in this setting appeared in
[
JK00
,
ADLM99
]
). In
[
DMKO01
]
, entropies for the Eikonal equation (
1
) are defined as vector fields
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that
\dv\Phi(m)\equiv 0
if
m
is a smooth solution to (
1
). Such entropies can be characterized explicitly; see (
81
). This is completely analogous to entropies for hyperbolic conservation laws. For solutions
m=\nabla^{\perp}u=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\perp}
with
\sup_{\varepsilon}AG_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<\infty
, it can be shown that
\dv\Phi(m)
are finite Radon measures, called
entropy measures
, and (if
m
has the additional property that
m\in BV
) they detect the jumps in
m
. As such, the function space for the
\Gamma
-convergence of the Aviles-Giga functional is a subset of the space
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
consisting of
entropy solutions
of the Eikonal equation, i.e. weak solutions
m
to (
1
) such that
\dv\Phi(m)\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
for all entropies
\Phi
, where
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is the set of finite Radon measures on
\Omega
. It is thus natural to understand the space
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
for the purpose of the full proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence of the Aviles-Giga functional.

The lack of understanding of fine structures of the space
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
constitutes one of the major obstacles in the study of the Aviles-Giga functional. Analogous issues arise in the context of a closely related micromagnetics energy
[
RS01
,
ARS02
,
AKLR02
,
DKMO02
,
RS03
]
and in the study of large deviation
principles for some stochastic processes, where the limiting equations are
scalar conservation laws
[
BBMN10
]
. Roughly speaking it is expected that vector fields
m\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
exhibit properties similar to those enjoyed by
BV
functions and that entropy measures
\dv\Phi(m)
are concentrated on a one-dimensional rectifiable set on which
m
has left and right traces. The most progress to date in this direction is due to De Lellis and Otto
[
DLO03
]
, who showed that the points of positive one-dimensional
density of entropy measures do form an
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable set
J
. However their result leaves open concentration of entropy measures on this set
J
. Indeed, a major conjecture raised in
[
DLO03
]
is the following

Conjecture 1

(De Lellis-Otto)
.

For any
m\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
,
\dv\Phi(m)
is supported on an
\mathcal{H}^{1}

\sigma
-finite rectifiable set
J
for all entropies
\Phi
and

\dv\Phi(m)=\left[\eta\cdot\left(\Phi\left(m^{+}\right)-(\Phi\left(m^{-}\right)\right)\right]\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\lfloor J},

where
\eta
is the unit vector normal to
J
and
m^{\pm}
are the traces of
m
on the two sides of
J
.

It is expected that such concentration of entropy measures on the
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable set
J
, if resolved, will be a crucial step towards the full proof of the Aviles-Giga conjecture. Note that very recently Marconi has resolved the analogous versions of Conjecture
1
for Burgers equation
[
Mar20b
]
and the micromagnetics functional
[
Mar20a
]
using a powerful Lagrangian representation method.

1.2

On the threshold regularity for the Eikonal equation

As described in the introduction of
[
CS19
]
, a recurring theme in modern non-linear PDE is to understand the threshold between rigidity and flexibility that often occurs at the limiting regularity required for weak solutions of PDE to satisfy additional derived equations
by virtue of some form of the chain rule. In

[
CS19
]
the list of examples presented includes entropy solutions of hyperbolic conservation laws and incompressible Euler equations. The authors further note that it is an interesting future direction to understand the rigidity/flexibility threshold on Besov or Sobolev scale for non-linear PDE.

The notion of entropies for the Aviles-Giga functional and the Eikonal equation is closely connected to (and indeed was inspired by) entropies for hyperbolic conservation laws. For
|m|=1
, it is sufficient to define entropies on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. It is easy to check that the restrictions to
\mathbb{S}^{1}
of entropies defined in
[
DMKO01
]
satisfy

e^{it}\cdot\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(e^{it})=0.

(2)

Thus, following
[
GL20
]
we define the set of entropies to be

ENT:=\left\{\Phi\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{S}^{1};\mathbb{R}^{2}\right):\Phi\text{ satisfies }(\ref{cpa1.7})\right\}.

(3)

The derived equations for the Eikonal equation are therefore the equations
\dv\Phi(m)\equiv 0
for all
\Phi\in ENT
. In
[
DLI15
]
(building on previous work
[
Ign12
]
) the authors showed that for
m
satisfying the Eikonal equation (
1
), the Sobolev regularity
m\in W^{\frac{1}{3},3}_{{\rm loc}}
is sufficient to allow something like the chain rule (or more accurately a substitute for the chain rule) to hold and hence conclude that all the derived equations hold. By the fundamental result of
[
JOP02
]
, this is strong enough information to conclude that
m
is rigid in the sense that it has only vortex singularities; see Conjecture
4
below for the precise statement. On the other hand, in
[
GL20
, Theorem 2.6]
it is shown that for weak solutions
m
to the Eikonal equation (
1
), the Besov regularity
m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty,{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
is equivalent to
\dv\Phi(m)
being locally finite measures for all
\Phi\in ENT
, and thus there is no rigidity for
m
under such regularity. These results leave the question of the critical regularity threshold between rigidity/flexibility for the Eikonal equation, and our first main result provides an initial step in this direction.

Our first theorem is an
L^{p}
version of the main theorem in
[
GL20
]
. To state this result, we first make some definitions. Recall that we denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
the set of finite Radon measures on
\Omega
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, let
\|\mu\|
denote its total variation measure. Next we recall
[
GL20
, Definition 2.5]
with some extensions for our setting:

Definition 2
.

We say that a vector field
m
solving (
1
) satisfies the
kinetic formulation
if there exists a Radon measure
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
such that

e^{is}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathds{1}_{e^{is}\cdot m(x)>0}=\partial_{s}\sigma\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\right).

(4)

We call the measure
\sigma
the
kinetic measure
.

Further, we say that
m
satisfies the
L^{p}
kinetic equation
for some
1\leq p<\infty
if it satisfies (
4
) and there exists a family of measures
\left\{\sigma_{x}\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})
for
\mathcal{L}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
such that

\langle\sigma(x,s),f(s)\zeta(x)\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}}f(s)d\sigma_{x}(s)\right)\zeta(x)\,dx\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}\left(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\right),\>\zeta\in C^{0}_{c}(\Omega)

(5)

and

\nu(x):=\|\sigma_{x}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})}\in L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega).

(6)

When (
5
) and (
6
) hold, following the notation of
[
AFP00
, Definition 2.27]
, we write
\sigma=\mathcal{L}^{2}\otimes\sigma_{x}
. Our first main result is the following

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be an open set and
m:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
satisfy (
1
). Then for any
1<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
, the following are equivalent:

1.

m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty,{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
;

2.

\dv\Phi(m)\in L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
for all
\Phi\in ENT
;

3.

m
satisfies the
L^{p}
kinetic equation in the sense of Definition
2
.

Note that for
p>\frac{4}{3}
, we are unable to establish the implication
(C)\Longrightarrow(A)
in Theorem
3
. However, for
p\geq\frac{4}{3}
, knowing only two special entropy productions of
m
(those of the Jin-Kohn entropies given in (
7
) and (
8
) in the following subsection) are in
L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}
is sufficient to establish the
B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{4,\infty,{\rm loc}}
regularity for
m
(see Proposition
33
). Our interest in Theorem
3
is two-fold. Firstly, concerning Conjecture
1
, for
m\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, it is not even known if entropy measures are singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure and a proof of this fact would represent the first progress on this long standing problem. The following conjecture represents an even more accessible goal in this program:

Conjecture 4
.

Let
m:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
satisfy (
1
). Assume
\dv\Phi(m)\in L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
for some
p>1
and all
\Phi\in ENT
,
then
\dv\Phi(m)\equiv 0
for all
\Phi\in ENT
, and hence
m
is rigid, i.e.
m
is locally Lipschitz outside a locally finite set of points, and in any neighborhood containing only one singularity
m
forms a vortex.

Our Theorem
3
gives equivalent formulations for
\dv\Phi(m)\in L^{p}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
for all
\Phi\in ENT
, in particular in terms of the Besov regularity
m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty,{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
. Under this Besov regularity, many powerful tools become available. In particular, in Theorem
5
, we establish explicit formulas for entropy productions and the kinetic measure
\sigma
in terms of two special entropies. It is likely that one can extract very detailed information out of the explicit formulas, which will lead to rigidity of
m
as stated in Conjecture
4
.

Secondly, it is known that the Eikonal equation enjoys no rigidity for
m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty,{\rm loc}}
. Thus a consequence of (a) Theorem
3
and (b) a proof of Conjecture
4

would provide the threshold of rigidity/flexibility for the Eikonal equation at the Besov scale. Specifically, let
\mathcal{S}_{m}
denote the points of approximate discontinuity of
m
(see
[
AFP00
, Definition 3.63]
), then

(a)\,\&\,(b)\Longrightarrow m\in\begin{cases}B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty,{\rm loc}}(\Omega)&\mathcal{S}_{m}\text{ can be }\mathcal{H}^{1}\text{-rectifiable};\\
B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{q,\infty,{\rm loc}}(\Omega),q>3&\mathcal{S}_{m}\text{ can only be isolated points and }m\\
&\text{forms a vortex around each point in }\mathcal{S}_{m}.\end{cases}

1.3
Control of all entropies by two special ones

Our next main results explore the connections between general entropies and two special entropies. Building from earlier work of Aviles and Giga
[
AG87
]
, Jin and Kohn
[
JK00
]
introduced two fundamental entropies
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
given by

\Sigma_{1}(m)=\left(m_{2}\left(1-m_{1}^{2}-\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{3}\right),m_{1}\left(1-m_{2}^{2}-\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{3}\right)\right)

(7)

and

\Sigma_{2}(m)=\left(-m_{1}\left(1-\frac{2m_{1}^{2}}{3}\right),m_{2}\left(1-\frac{2m_{2}^{2}}{3}\right)\right).

(8)

These two entropies restricted to
\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfy (
2
), and thus belong to the set
ENT
defined in (
3
). They play fundamental roles in the
\Gamma
-limit conjecture for the Aviles-Giga functional. Indeed, the conjecture in
[
ADLM99
]
is that the
\Gamma
-limit of the Aviles-Giga functional is (up to a constant) the total mass of the entropy measure

\mu=\left\|\begin{array}[]{c}\dv\Sigma_{1}(\nabla^{\perp}u)\\
\dv\Sigma_{2}(\nabla^{\perp}u)\end{array}\right\|,

which is indeed controlled by the energy, and coincides with the cubic jump cost when
\nabla u\in BV
. A necessary condition for this
\Gamma
-limit conjecture to hold is that the two special entropy measures
\dv\Sigma_{j}(m)
control all entropy measures, and our remaining results are in this spirit.

To state our next result, for
m:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
x\in\Omega
and
0<\varepsilon<\mathrm{dist}\{x,\partial\Omega\}
, we define the function

\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-3}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\left|D^{z}m(x)\right|^{3}dz,

(9)

where we denote
D^{z}m(x)=m(x+z)-m(x)
. Let us take a moment to explain the function
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\varepsilon}
. This function arises naturally in the control of entropy productions of
m
satisfying (
1
). Indeed, letting
m_{\varepsilon}
be a regularization of
m
, direct computations show that the main contribution of
\dv\Phi(m_{\varepsilon})
comes from terms of the form
|\nabla m_{\varepsilon}|\left(1-|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)
for any
\Phi\in ENT
(see (
3
)). A commutator argument further gives
|\nabla m_{\varepsilon}|\left(1-|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\lesssim\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\varepsilon}
. On the other hand, if
m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty}
for some
p>1
, then from Lemma
9
we have
{\|\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\varepsilon}\|}_{L^{p}}\lesssim|m|_{B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty}}^{3}
. Thus, putting everything together, if
m
is sufficiently regular (which is the case if
m\in B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty}
for some
p>1
), one can pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
to deduce

{\|\dv\Phi(m)\|}_{L^{p}}\lesssim{\|\mathcal{P}_{m}\|}_{L^{p}}\lesssim|m|_{B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3p,\infty}}^{3}

(10)

for some
\mathcal{P}_{m}
which is a weak limit of
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\varepsilon}
. As such, the function
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{\varepsilon}
plays a crucial role in controlling entropy productions of
m
by its Besov norm, and we will make extensive use of it.

In
[
GL20
]
, Ghiraldin and Lamy constructed a special class of entropies
\{\Phi_{f}\}\subset ENT
parameterized by

(see (

\sigma
(recalling (

\dv\Phi_{f}(m)
and the kinetic measure

in terms of

, under suitable assumptions.

.

Let

2.1
Extension of entropies to
\overline{B}_{1}
and commutator argument

A fundamental idea that we will use throughout is to try and understand
\dv\Phi(m)
by understanding
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\dv\Phi\left(m_{\varepsilon}\right)
for a regularization
m_{\varepsilon}
of
m
. By definition, entropies are designed to provide additional derived equations via the chain rule and as such applying them to
m_{\varepsilon}
is a natural idea. However for
\Phi\in ENT
, it is necessary to extend the definition to
\overline{B}_{1}
in order to act them on
m_{\varepsilon}
. One natural way to do this is to extend
\Phi
through a
radial extension
(see (
18
)). Another way is through the original definition of entropies in
[
DMKO01
]
. Here entropies are defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and obtained from smooth functions
\varphi
via the formula

\Phi^{{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\varphi}}}(z)=\varphi(z)z+((iz)\cdot\nabla\varphi(z))iz\qquad\forall z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

The underlying principle is, through different extensions, we extract different information from the limit of
\dv\Phi\left(m_{\varepsilon}\right)
, and when pieced together, the information has strong consequences for the structure of
\dv\Phi\left(m\right)
.

In order to pass to the limit in
\dv\Phi\left(m_{\varepsilon}\right)
, an important trick is the commutator argument of
[
CET94
]
that was first introduced to our setting in
[
DLI15
]
, and used later in
[
LP18
,
GL20
,
LLP20
]
with extensions. As explained above (
10
), this commutator argument allows to control
\dv\Phi\left(m_{\varepsilon}\right)
and pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
to establish an estimate like (
10
) under sufficient regularity of
m
. This is a crucial idea in many of our arguments.

Lemma 14

(Lamy)

\langle\dv\Phi_{f}(m),\zeta\rangle=\langle\sigma(x,s),f(s)\zeta(x)\rangle\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}),\>\zeta\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega),

where

Proof.

Given Banach spaces

and

denote the space of bounded linear operators from

to

. Given

The estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\mathcal{Z}}:=\left\{T_{\zeta}:\zeta\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),{\|\zeta\|}_{\mathcal{Z}}\leq 1\right\}.

\displaystyle\langle\dv\Phi_{f}(m),\zeta\rangle\leq C{\|f\|}_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})}{\|\zeta\|}_{\mathcal{Z}}\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}),\>\zeta\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega).

\displaystyle\psi(x,s)=\sum_{j}{\mathds{1}}_{E_{j}}(s)\zeta_{j}(x),\qquad E_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\text{ Borelian, }\zeta_{j}\in\mathcal{Z},

Proof.

The proof relies on the div-curl inequality

{\left|\int E\wedge B\right|}\lesssim pp^{\prime}\left({\|E\|}_{L^{p^{\prime}}}{\|\dv B\|}_{W^{-1,p}}+{\|B\|}_{L^{p^{\prime}}}{\|\dv E\|}_{W^{-1,p}}\right),

[
GL20

and

Next we wish to estimate the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{if }k\text{ is odd},

\displaystyle\xi_{f^{1}_{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k(1-2k)(1+2k)}\varphi^{1}_{2k},

\displaystyle\xi_{f^{2}_{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k(1-2k)(1+2k)}\varphi^{2}_{2k},

(130)

where
\varphi^{j}_{k}
are the harmonic polynomials given in polar coordinates by
\varphi^{1}_{k}=r^{k}\sin(k\theta)
,
\varphi^{2}_{k}=r^{k}\cos(k\theta)
. Hence

\displaystyle\Phi^{\xi_{f^{j}_{k}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqad11},\eqref{eqad12}}{=}0\qquad\text{if }k\text{ is odd},

\displaystyle\Phi^{\xi_{f^{1}_{2k}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqad11},\eqref{eqad12}}{=}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k(1-2k)(1+2k)}\Phi^{\varphi^{1}_{2k}},

\displaystyle\Phi^{\xi_{f^{2}_{2k}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqad11},\eqref{eqad12}}{=}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k(1-2k)(1+2k)}\Phi^{\varphi^{2}_{2k}},

(131)

and it remains to compute
\Phi^{\varphi^{j}_{2k}}(e^{it})
.

For
\xi=\varphi^{1}_{k}
we find

\displaystyle\Phi^{\varphi^{1}_{k}}(e^{it})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{1}_{k}e^{it}+(-\sin t\,\partial_{1}\varphi^{1}_{k}+\cos t\,\partial_{2}\varphi^{1}_{k})ie^{it}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{appeqd3}}{=}\sin(kt)e^{it}+(-k\sin t\sin((k-1)t)+k\cos t\cos((k-1)t))ie^{it}

\displaystyle=\sin(kt)e^{it}+k\cos(kt)ie^{it}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}e^{it}\left[-e^{ikt}+e^{-ikt}+ke^{ikt}+ke^{-ikt}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left[(k-1)e^{i(k+1)t}+(k+1)e^{-i(k-1)t}\right],

(132)

and for
\xi=\varphi^{2}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\varphi^{2}_{k}}(e^{it})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{2}_{k}e^{it}+(-\sin t\,\partial_{1}\varphi^{2}_{k}+\cos t\,\partial_{2}\varphi^{2}_{k})ie^{it}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{appeqd3}}{=}\cos(kt)e^{it}+(-k\sin t\cos((k-1)t)-k\cos t\sin((k-1)t))ie^{it}

\displaystyle=\cos(kt)e^{it}-k\sin(kt)ie^{it}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{it}\left[e^{ikt}+e^{-ikt}-ke^{ikt}+ke^{-ikt}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[-(k-1)e^{i(k+1)t}+(k+1)e^{-i(k-1)t}\right].

(133)

Gathering the above we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi^{\xi_{f^{1}_{2k}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqad13},\eqref{eqad14}}{=}\frac{i(-1)^{k}}{2k}\left[\frac{e^{i(2k+1)t}}{2k+1}+\frac{e^{-i(2k-1)t}}{2k-1}\right],

\displaystyle\Phi^{\xi_{f^{2}_{2k}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eqad13},\eqref{eqad15}}{=}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k}\left[\frac{e^{i(2k+1)t}}{2k+1}-\frac{e^{-i(2k-1)t}}{2k-1}\right],

which, gathering all the cases (
k
even or odd), and recalling the expressions found in Lemma
34
for
\Phi_{f^{j}_{k}}
, proves Lemma
35
.
∎

Proof of

Lemma 36

(Ghiraldin-Lamy)
.

Let

satisfy (

). Assume
\dv\Phi_{f}(m)\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
for all

such that

\langle\dv\Phi_{f}(m),\zeta\rangle=\langle\sigma(x,s),f(s)\zeta(x)\rangle\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}),\>\zeta\in C^{0}_{c}(\Omega),

(134)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality between measures and functions.

Proof.

Since the proof follows almost exactly the same lines as that of Lemma
14
, we only sketch it briefly focusing on the differences. Letting
\mathcal{Z}=C^{0}_{c}(\Omega)
and thus
\mathcal{Z}^{*}=\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
, exactly as in the proof of Lemma
14
, the Banach-Steinhaus’ uniform boundedness principle implies the map

\displaystyle T\colon C^{0}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})\to\mathcal{Z}^{*},\quad f\mapsto\dv\Phi_{f}(m)

(135)

is a bounded linear operator.

Again by
[
DU77
, Theorem VI.2.1]
, there exists an
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)^{**}
-valued Borel measure
G
on
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
such that

\langle\varphi^{*},Tf\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}}f\,d\langle G,\varphi^{*}\rangle\qquad\forall\varphi^{*}\in\mathcal{Z}^{**}.

(136)

As in the proof of Lemma
14
, for
\psi

\displaystyle\psi(x,s)=\sum_{j}{\mathds{1}}_{E_{j}}(s)\zeta_{j}(x),\qquad E_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\text{ Borelian, }\zeta_{j}\in\mathcal{Z},

we define

\displaystyle\langle\sigma,\psi\rangle:=\sum_{j}\langle G(E_{j}),\zeta_{j}^{*}\rangle,\qquad\zeta_{j}^{*}:=(\mu\mapsto\langle\mu,\zeta_{j}\rangle)\in\mathcal{Z}^{**}.

(137)

Thus, assuming the
E_{j}
’s are disjoint and non-negligible, as in (

\displaystyle\langle\sigma,\psi\rangle\leq{\|T\|}_{\mathcal{L}(C^{0}(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z});\mathcal{Z}^{*})}{\|\psi\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})}.

After extension,
\sigma

Proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma

.

Given any open set

, recall from (

\langle\dv\Phi_{f}(m),\zeta\rangle=\iint_{U\times{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}}f(s)\zeta(x)\,d\sigma(x,s)\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}\left(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\right),\>\zeta\in C^{0}_{c}(U).

(138)

Let

\dv\Phi_{f_{j}}(m)

\mu_{m}

x_{0}

\displaystyle\left|\dv\Phi_{f_{j}}(m)(x_{0})\right|

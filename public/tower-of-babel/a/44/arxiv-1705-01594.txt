1.
Introduction

Let
R
denote a commutative Noetherian ring and let
I
be an
ideal of
R
. In
[
11
]
, Hartshorne defined an
R
-module
L
to be

I
-
cofinite
, if
\operatorname{Supp}L\subseteq V(I)
and
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,L)
is a finitely generated module
for all
i
. He posed the following question:

Whether the category
\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}

of
I
-cofinite modules is an Abelian subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules?
That is, if
f:M\longrightarrow N
is an
R
-homomorphism of

I
-cofinite modules, are
\ker f
and
{\rm coker}f

I
-cofinite?

Hartshorne gave a counterexample to show that this question has not an
affirmative answer in general, (see
[
11
, §3]
). On the positive side, Hartshorne proved that if
I
is a prime
ideal of dimension one in a complete regular local ring
R
, then the answer to his question
is yes. On the other hand, in
[
7
]
, Delfino and Marley extended this result to arbitrary
complete local rings. Kawasaki in
[
18
]
generalized the Delfino and Marley’s result
for an arbitrary ideal
I
of dimension one in a local ring
R
. Finally, Melkersson
in
[
23
]
generalized the Kawasaki’s result for all ideals of dimension one of any
arbitrary Noetherian ring
R
. More recently, in
[
5
]
as a generalization of
Melkersson’s result it is shown that for any ideals
I
in a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, the category of all
I
-cofinite

R
-modules
M
with
\dim M\leq 1
is an Abelian subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules.

Recall that, for an
R
-module
M
, the
cohomological dimension of
M
with
respect to

I
, denoted by
\operatorname{cd}(I,M)
, is the smallest integer
n\geq 0
such that
H^{i}_{I}(M)=0
for all
i>n
.

The cohomological dimension have been studied by
several authors; see, for example, Faltings
[
9
]
, Hartshorne

[
12
]
, Huneke-Lyubeznik
[
16
]
, Divaani-Aazar et al
[
8
]
, Hellus
[
13
]
, Hellus-Stückrad
[
14
]
,
Mehrvarz et al
[
22
]
, and Ghasemi et al
[
10
]
.

Recall that, for any proper ideal
I
of
R
, the

arithmetic rank
of
I
, denoted by
\operatorname{ara}(I)
, is the least number of
elements of
I
required to generate an ideal which has the same radical as
I
.

Now, let
I
be an ideal of an arbitrary Noetherian ring
R
. Kawasaki in
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]

proved that if
\operatorname{ara}(I)=1
then the category
\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}

of
I
-cofinite modules is an Abelian subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules.

It is well known that, for any proper ideal
I
of a Noetherian ring
R
we have
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)\leq\operatorname{ara}(I)
.
(See
[
6
, Corollary 3.3.3]
). In particular, for any ideal
I
with
\operatorname{ara}(I)=1
we have
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)\leq 1
.
So, as a generalization of Kawasaki’s interesting result, it is more natural that we ask the following question:

Question 1:
Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
I
be an ideal of
R
with
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)\leq 1
.
Whether the category
\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}

of
I
-cofinite modules is an Abelian subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules?

In section 2 of this paper we present an affirmative answer to the Question 1, whenever
R
is a local Noetherian ring.
In section 3 we present some equivalent conditions for the exactness of ideal transform functors. Using these results,
for any ideal
I
generated by two elements, we provide some necessary and sufficient conditions for the non-vanishing
of the local cohomology module
H^{2}_{I}(R)
. Finally, in section 4 we prepare some vanishing conditions for the Bass and
Betti numbers of some special local cohomology modules. Also, we give a formula for the cohomological dimension of some
special ideals in Noetherian domains.

Throughout this paper,
R
will always be a commutative Noetherian
ring and
I
will be an ideal of
R
. Also, for an
R
-module
M
,
\Gamma_{I}(M)
denotes the submodule of
M
consisting
of all elements annihilated by
some power of
I
, i.e.,
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(0:_{M}I^{n})
. For every
R
-module
L
, we denote by
\operatorname{mAss}_{R}L
the set of
minimal elements of
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}L
with respect to inclusion. Also, for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, we denote

\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\rm Spec}\,R:\,\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}\}
by

V(\mathfrak{a})
. Finally, for any ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
R
,
the
radical of

\mathfrak{b}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{b})
, is defined to
be the set
\{x\in R\,:\,x^{n}\in\mathfrak{b}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. For any unexplained notation and terminology we refer
the reader to
[
6
]
and
[
21
]
.

2.
A category of modules which is Abelian

The following well known lemma plays a key role in the proof of Proposition 2.2.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
I
be an ideal of
R
and
T
be an
R
-module. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The
R
-module
H^{i}_{I}(T)
is Artinian, for
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

The
R
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,T)
is Artinian, for
i\geq 0
.

(iii)

The
R
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,T)
is Artinian, for
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{cd}(I,R)
.

Proof.

See
[
24
, Theorem 5.5]
and
[
2
, Theorem 2.9]
.∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring,
I
be an ideal of
R
and
M
be an
R
-module. Then the following conditions
are equivalent:

(i)

The
R
-modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M)
are finitely generated for all
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

The
R
-modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M)
are finitely generated for all
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{cd}(I,R)
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Is clear.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
T:=D(M)
, where
D(-):={\rm Hom}_{R}(-,E)

denotes the Matlis dual functor and
E:=E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})
is the
injective hull of the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
. Then by the adjointness we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,T)\simeq D(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M))
,
for all
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{cd}(I,R)
. In particular, the
R
-modules
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,T)
are Artinian for all
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{cd}(I,R)
.
So, it follows from Lemma 2.1 and adjointness, that the
R
-modules
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,T)\simeq D(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M))
are
Artinian for all
i\geq 0
. Now, it follows from
[
19
, Lemma 1.15(a)]
that, the
R
-modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M)

are finitely generated for all
i\geq 0
,
as required.∎

The following easy consequence of Proposition 2.2 plays a key role in the proof of Theorem 2.4.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring,
I
be an ideal of
R
with
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)=n

and let
M
be an
R
-module with
\operatorname{Supp}M\subseteq V(I)
. Then the
R
-module
M
is
I
-cofinite,
if and only if, the
R
-modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M)
are finitely generated for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Proposition 2.2 and
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
.∎

The following result gives an affirmative answer to the Question 1, for the local case.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a Noetherian local ring
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
such that
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)\leq 1
. Let

\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
denote the category of
I
-cofinite

R
-modules. Then

\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
is an Abelian subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules.

Proof.

Let
M,N\in\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
and let
f:M\longrightarrow N
be
an
R
-homomorphism. It is enough to show that the
R
-modules

\ker f
and
{\rm coker}f
are
I
-cofinite.

To this end, the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\ker f\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow{\rm im}f\longrightarrow 0,

induces an exact sequence

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,{\rm im}f)\rightarrow 0,

which using
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
, implies that
the
R
-module
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,{\rm im}f)
is finitely generated. Now, the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm im}f\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow{\rm coker}f\longrightarrow 0.

induces an exact sequence

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(R/I,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(R/I,{\rm coker}f)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,{\rm im}f)

\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,{\rm coker}f)\rightarrow 0.

By
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]

the modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(R/I,N)
and
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,N)
are finitely generated
R
-modules, which implies that the

R
-modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}(R/I,{\rm coker}f)
and
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(R/I,{\rm coker}f)
are finitely generated. Therefore, it follows
from Corollary 2.3, that the

R
-module
{\rm coker}f
is
I
-cofinite. Now, the assertion follows from the following exact sequences

0\longrightarrow{\rm im}f\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow{\rm coker}f\longrightarrow 0,

and

0\longrightarrow\ker f\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow{\rm im}f\longrightarrow 0.

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a Noetherian local ring
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
such that
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)\leq 1
.
Let
\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
denote the category of
I
-cofinite modules over
R
.
Let

X^{\bullet}:\cdots\longrightarrow X^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{i+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{i+2}\longrightarrow\cdots,

be a
complex such that
X^{i}\in\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
the
i^{{\rm th}}
cohomology module
H^{i}(X^{\bullet})
is in

\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Theorem 2.4.∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring,
I
be an ideal of
R
with
\operatorname{cd}(I,R)\leq 1
and
let
M
be an
I
-cofinite
R
-module. Then, the
R
-modules

{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}(N,M)
and
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(N,M)
are
I
-cofinite, for all finitely generated
R
-modules
N
and all
integers
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

Since
N
is finitely generated it follows that,
N
has a
free resolution with finitely generated free
R
-modules. Now the
assertion follows using Corollary 2.5 and computing the
R
-modules
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{R}(N,M)
and
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(N,M)
, using this
free resolution. ∎

3.
Vanishing of the extension and torsion functors

In this section we present some equivalent conditions for the exactness of ideal transform functors.
Using these results, for any ideal
I
generated by two elements, we provide some
necessary and sufficient conditions for the non-vanishing of the local cohomology module
H^{2}_{I}(R)
.

The following lemma is needed in the proof of Proposition 3.2.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
I
be an
ideal of
R
. Let
M
be an
R
-module such that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,M)=0
, for all integers
i\geq 0
. Then

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/I,M)=0
.

Proof.

Let
I=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and let

K_{\bullet}(\underline{x},M):0\longrightarrow M\overset{f_{0}}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{k=1}^{C_{n}^{1}}M\overset{f_{1}}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{k=1}^{C_{n}^{2}}M\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{k=1}^{C_{n}^{n-1}}M\overset{f_{n-1}}{\longrightarrow}M\longrightarrow 0,

be the Koszul complex of
M
with respect to
\underline{x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.

We prove that
\lc_{i}(\underline{x};M)=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. By the definition we have

\lc_{0}(\underline{x};M)=\Coker f_{n-1}=M/IM\simeq R/I\otimes_{R}M=0.

So, we have
\Image f_{n-1}=M
.
Therefore, using the hypothesis it follows that

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,\Image f_{n-1})=0,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm each}\,\,i\geq 0.

Hence,
the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Ker{f_{n-1}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{k=1}^{C_{n}^{n-1}}M\longrightarrow\Image f_{n-1}\longrightarrow 0,

implies that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,\Ker f_{n-1})=0
, for each
i\geq 0
.
The exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Image{f_{n-2}}\longrightarrow\Ker f_{n-1}\longrightarrow\lc_{1}(\underline{x};M)\longrightarrow 0,\,\,\,\,\,\,~(3.1.1)

induces the exact sequence

R/I\otimes_{R}\Ker f_{n-1}\longrightarrow R/I\otimes_{R}\lc_{1}(\underline{x};M)\longrightarrow 0.\,\,\,\,\,\,~(3.1.2)

Now as
R/I\otimes_{R}\Ker f_{n-1}=0
, it follows from
(3.1.2)
that

R/I\otimes_{R}\lc_{1}(\underline{x};M)=0.

By
the definition of the Koszul complex we have
I\lc_{1}(\underline{x};M)=0
. Therefore, we have
\lc_{1}(\underline{x};M)\simeq R/I\otimes_{R}\lc_{1}(\underline{x};M)=0
. Now it follows from the exact sequence
(3.1.1)
that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,\Image f_{n-2})=0
, for each
i\geq 0
.
Moreover, the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Ker f_{n-2}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{k=1}^{C_{n}^{n-2}}M\longrightarrow\Image{f_{n-2}}\longrightarrow 0,

implies that

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,\Ker f_{n-2})=0
, for each
i\geq 0
. Proceeding in the same way we can see

\lc_{i}(\underline{x};M)=0
, for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

Now, since

\lc_{i}(\underline{x};M)\simeq\lc^{n-i}(\underline{x};M)
for all
0\leq i\leq n
, it follows
that

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/I,M)\simeq(0:_{M}I)\simeq\lc^{0}(\underline{x};M)\simeq\lc_{n}(\underline{x};M)=0.

∎

The following proposition plays a key role in the proof of our main results.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring,
I
be an
ideal of
R
and
M
be an
R
-module. Then the following conditions
are equivalent:

(i)

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{n}(R/I,M)=0
, for all integers
n\geq 0
.

(ii)

\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(R/I,M)=0
, for all integers
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) We argue by induction on
n
. For
n=0
, the assertion holds by Lemma 3.1.
We therefore assume, inductively, that
n>0
and the result has been proved for smaller values of
n
.
Then there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow E_{R}(M)\longrightarrow E_{R}(M)/M\longrightarrow 0.\,\,\,\,\,\,~(3.2.1)

Since, by the hypothesis we have
(0:_{M}I)=0
it follows that
(0:_{E_{R}(M)}I)=0
and hence
\Gamma_{I}(E_{R}(M))=0
.
By
[
21
, Theorem 18.5]
the
R
-module
E_{R}(M)
is isomorph with a direct sum of a family of indecomposable injective
R
-modules.
Let
\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}
be a prime ideal of
R
such that
E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}})
is a direct summand of
E_{R}(M)
. Then we have
\Gamma_{I}(E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}))=0
.
Therefore, form the fact that
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}})=\{\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}\}
it follows that
I\not\subseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}
.
So there exists an element
a\in I
such that
a\not\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then by
[
21
, Theorem 18.4(iii)]
,
multiplication by
a
is an automorphism on
E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}})
. Therefore, multiplication by
a
is an automorphism on

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}))
, for all
i\geq 0
. But, since
a\in I
it follows that, multiplication by
a
on
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}))

is the zero map, for all
i\geq 0
. Thus,
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,E_{R}(R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}))=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Since for each
i\geq 0
, the torsion functor

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,-)
commutes with the direct sums it follows that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,E_{R}(M))=0
, for all
i\geq 0
. So, from the exact
sequence
(3.2.1)
it follows that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,E_{R}(M)/M)=0
, for all
i\geq 0
. Hence, by applying the inductive hypothesis to the

R
-module
E_{R}(M)/M
we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{i+1}_{R}(R/I,M)\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,E_{R}(M)/M)=0\,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,0\leq i\leq n-1.

So, we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{R}(R/I,M)=0
. This completes the inductive step.∎

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Assume the opposite. Then there is an integer
j\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{j}(R/I,M)\neq 0
.
Let
\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Supp}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{j}(R/I,M)
. Then localizing at
\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}
, without loss of generality, we may assume that
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})

is a Noetherian local ring. Let
T:=D(M)
, where
D(-)

denotes the Matlis dual functor. Then by the adjointness we have

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,T)\simeq D(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,M))=0,

for all
i\geq 0
. Therefore, by the previous part of the proof we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,T)=0,
for each
i\geq 0
.
So, by the adjointness we have

D(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{j}(R/I,M))\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{R}(R/I,T)=0,

which implies that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{j}(R/I,M)=0
.
This is the desired contradiction.∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
I
be an ideal of
R
. Let
E
be an injective
R
-module and
K
be a submodule of
E
. Then

\frac{L}{\Gamma_{I}(L)}\otimes_{R}\frac{R}{I}=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm where}\,\,L:=\frac{E}{K}.

Proof.

In view of
[
6
, Proposition 2.1.4]
the
R
-module
E_{1}:=\Gamma_{I}(E)
is injective.
Therefore, there exists an injective submodule
E_{2}
of
E
such that
E_{1}+E_{2}=E
and
E_{1}\cap E_{2}=0
.
Since
\frac{E_{1}+K}{K}
is a submodule of
\Gamma_{I}(L)
it follows that the
R
-module
\frac{L}{\Gamma_{I}(L)}

is a homomorphic image of the
R
-module
E_{2}
. So, in order to prove the assertion it is
enough to prove that
E_{2}\otimes_{R}\frac{R}{I}=0
. So, we must prove that
E_{2}=IE_{2}
. Since
\Gamma_{I}(R)E_{2}\subseteq\Gamma_{I}(E_{2})=0
, it follows that
\Gamma_{I}(R)E_{2}=0
. Therefore, we have

E_{2}=(0:_{E_{2}}\Gamma_{I}(R))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2}).

On the other hand, by
[
6
, Lemma 2.1.1]
there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R/\Gamma_{I}(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\longrightarrow}}R/\Gamma_{I}(R),

for some element
a\in I
, which effecting the
R
-linear
exact functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,E_{2})
induces the exact sequence

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore, we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})=a\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}/aE_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle E_{2}\otimes_{R}R/Ra

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})\otimes_{R}R/Ra

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})/a\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/\Gamma_{I}(R),E_{2})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 0.

So, we have
E_{2}=aE_{2}
and hence
E_{2}=IE_{2}
.∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and let
I\subseteq J
be two ideals of
R
.
Let
M
be an
R
-module and
t\geq 2
be an integer such that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{j}(R/J,H^{i}_{I}(M))=0
for all
i>t
and all
j\geq 0
. Then we have
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{j}(R/J,H^{t}_{I}(M))=0
for
j=0,1
.

Proof.

Let

0\longrightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

be a minimal injective resolution for
M
. Set
K_{i}:={\rm ker}f_{i}
for
i\geq 0
.
By splitting this minimal injective resolution to some short exact sequences we get the isomorphisms

H^{t}_{I}(M)\simeq H^{t-1}_{I}(K_{1})\simeq H^{t-2}_{I}(K_{2})\simeq\cdots\simeq H^{2}_{I}(K_{t-2})\simeq H^{1}_{I}(K_{t-1})\simeq H^{1}_{I}(K_{t-1}/\Gamma_{I}(K_{t-1})).

Set

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian domain and let

and

be two non-zero proper

such that

Proof.

Since by the hypothesis we have

it follows that

J\not\subseteq\bigcap_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{mAss}_{R}R/I}\operatorname{\mathfrak{p}},

which implies that
J\not\subseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}
for some
\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{mAss}_{R}R/I
. Assume that
k:=0pt\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then by Grothendieck’s
Non-vanishing Theorem we have

0\neq H^{k}_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}R_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}}}(R_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}})=H^{k}_{IR_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}}}(R_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}})\simeq(H^{k}_{I}(R))_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}},

which implies that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{Supp}H^{k}_{I}(R)
. Hence we have
\operatorname{Supp}H^{k}_{I}(R)\not\subseteq V(J)
and so we have

k\in\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,:\,\,\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)\not\subseteq V(J)\}.

\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,:\,\,\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)\not\subseteq V(J)\}\neq\emptyset.

Now, set
\ell:=\max\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,:\,\,\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)\not\subseteq V(J)\}
and
t:=\operatorname{cd}(I\cap J,R)
.
Then as by
[
6

we have

and

it

and

. By the Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence for

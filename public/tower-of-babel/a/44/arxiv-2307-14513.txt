Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
R
denote a commutative Noetherian ring
(with identity) and
I
be an ideal of
R
. Also, we denote by
\mathscr{C}(R)
the category of all
R
-modules. For an
R
-module
M
, the

i
th local cohomology module of
M
with respect to
I
is
defined as:

H^{i}_{I}(M)=\underset{n\geq 1}{\varinjlim}\,\,\text{Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I^{n},M).

We refer the reader to
[
8
]
or

[
6
]
for more details about local cohomology. Hartshorne, in
[
9
]
, defined an
R
-module
M
to be

I
-
cofinite
, if
\Supp M\subseteq V(I)
and
{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/I,M)
is a finitely generated
R
-module
for all
i\geq 0
.

In the sequel, for any ideal
I
of
R
, we denote by
\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
the category of all
I
-cofinite

R
-modules. Also, we denote by
\mathscr{A}(R)
the class of all ideals
I
of
R
such that
\mathscr{C}(R,I)_{cof}
is an Abelian subcategory of
\mathscr{C}(R)
; that is, if
f:M\longrightarrow N
is an
R
-homomorphism of

I
-cofinite modules, then the
R
-modules
{\rm ker}\,f
and
{\rm coker}\,f
are
I
-cofinite, too.

One of the more elementary results concerning the finitely generated
R
-module
M
is the relation
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
. This relation easily follows from the fact that
\Supp M=V(\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)
. But, this result does not hold for cofinite
R
-modules with respect to an ideal of
R
. For example, if
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}},k)
is a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d>0
, then the
\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}
-cofinite
R
-module
\mbox{E}_{R}(k)
(the injective envelope of the residue field
k=R/\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}
of
R
) is faithful, that is
\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\mbox{E}_{R}(k)=0
, and
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\mbox{E}_{R}(k)=0
. So,
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\mbox{E}_{R}(k)=d>\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\mbox{E}_{R}(k)
.

But, we know that every finitely generated
R
-module
M
is
(0_{R})
-cofinite, where
(0_{R})
denotes the zero ideal of
R
. So, one has the equality
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/((0_{R})+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
. Furthermore, in
[
12
]
, Melkersson proved that if
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
is a Noetherian complete local ring and
I
is an ideal of
R
, then each zero-dimensional
I
-cofinite module
M
is Artinian and for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mbox{Att}\,_{R}M
we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{p})=0
. So,
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
. Therefore, according to the above, it is natural to raise the following question:

Question 1.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a Notherian complete local ring
R
and
M
an
I
-cofinite
R
-module. Then, does the equality
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
hold?

More recently, in
[
13
]
, Pirmohammadi provided a partial answer to the above question. Indeed. he proved that for any ideal
I
of a Noetherian complete local ring with
I\in\mathscr{A}(R)
and any non-zero
I
-cofinite
R
-module
M
, the equality
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
holds.

In the present paper, we provide an affirmative answer to the above question without any additional assumption. Indeed, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
I
be and ideal of a complete local ring
R
and
M
a non-zero
I
-cofinite
R
-module. Then

\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M.

Here, we should notice that the category of
I
-cofinite
R
-modules with dimension less than one is Abelian (see
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
). But, the category of
I
-cofinite
R
-modules with a dimension greater than two is not necessarily Abelian. For example, consider the following counterexample of Hartshorne: Let
R=k[[x,y,z,w]]
,
I=(x,z)
and
M=R/(xy-zw)
. Now, set
t:=xy-zw
and by the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
, one can get the following induced exact sequence:

\cdots\longrightarrow\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(R)\longrightarrow\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(M)\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\mbox{H}_{I}^{i}(R)=0
for all
i\neq 2
, one can use
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
to see that the
R
-module
\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(R)
is
I
-cofinite. However,
\mbox{coker}\,(t^{*})=\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(M)
is not
I
-cofinite (see
[
9
]
). Furthermore, using the fact that
\Supp_{R}(\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(R))=\Supp_{R}(\underset{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\bigoplus}\mbox{H}_{I}^{i}(R))=\Supp_{R}(R/I)
, implies that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}(\mbox{H}_{I}^{2}(R))=2
.

2.
results

The starting point of this section is the following main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I
be and ideal of a complete local ring
(R,\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}})
and
M
a non-zero
I
-cofinite
R
-module. Then

\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M.

Proof.

We use induction on
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M
to prove the result. First set
\mathfrak{b}:=\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M
, and then assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M=0
. Then, using
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
implies that
M
is Artinian; and thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{b})=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M
, by
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
. Now, let
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M\geq 1
and suppose that the result has been proved for all cofinite
R
-modules of dimension
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M-1.
Note that since
\Supp M\subseteq V(I+\mathfrak{b})
, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{b})\geq\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}M.
Therefore, we may and do assume that
n:=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{b})\geq 0.
Hence, there are elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
R-(I+\mathfrak{b})
such that

\operatorname{Rad}((x_{1}+(I+\mathfrak{b}),\dots,x_{n}+(I+\mathfrak{b})))=\mathfrak{m}/(I+\mathfrak{b}),

and so
\operatorname{Rad}((x_{1},\dots,x_{n})+(I+\mathfrak{b}))=\mathfrak{m}
.
By
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
,
\Gamma_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}}(M)
is an Artinian
I
-cofinite
R
-module. Therefore, by a similar argument in the first step of induction, we have
\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\Gamma_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}}(M))\supseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}
. Now, set
\overline{M}:=M/\Gamma_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}}(M)
and consider the following short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}}(M)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\overline{M}\longrightarrow 0,

which shows that the
R
-module
\overline{M}
is
I
-cofinite and
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}\overline{M}=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M
. Furthermore, since
\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{b})=\operatorname{Rad}(\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cap\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M})
and
\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\Gamma_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}}(M))\supseteq\operatorname{\mathfrak{m}}
, then by considering the following equalities:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mathfrak{b})&=\operatorname{Rad}(I+\operatorname{Rad}(\mathfrak{b}))\\
&=\operatorname{Rad}(I+\operatorname{Rad}(\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cap\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M}))\\
&=\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cap\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M})\\
&=\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M))\cap\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M})\\
&=\mathfrak{m}\cap\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M})\\
&=\operatorname{Rad}(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M}),\end{array}

one has
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{b})=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\overline{M})
. Therefore, by replacing
M
with
\overline{M}
, we may and do assume that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
, and so
\mathfrak{m}\not\in\Ass_{R}M
. Therefore, one can deduce that

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\nsubseteq\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}M}\mathfrak{p}.

So, there exists an element
b\in(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
such that
z:=x_{1}+b\notin\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}M}\mathfrak{p},
and also
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=(z,x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
. Hence, by the following equalities:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathfrak{m}/(I+\mathfrak{b})&=\operatorname{Rad}(\frac{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})+(I+\mathfrak{b})}{(I+\mathfrak{b})})\\
&=\operatorname{Rad}(\frac{(z,x_{2},\dots,x_{n})+(I+\mathfrak{b})}{I+\mathfrak{b}})\\
&=\operatorname{Rad}((z+(I+\mathfrak{b}),x_{2}+(I+\mathfrak{b}),\dots,x_{n}+(I+\mathfrak{b}))),\end{array}

one can deduce that the element
z+(I+\mathfrak{b})
is a subset of a system of parameters for
R/(I+\mathfrak{b})
.
Here, we should notice that
z\not\in(I+\mathfrak{b})
. It therefore follows from
[
15
, Proposition 15.22]
that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{b})=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(Rz+I+\mathfrak{b})+1.

Next, in view of
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
we have that the
R
-module
H^{1}_{Rz}(M)
is a non-zero
(I+Rz)
-cofinite module and
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}\mbox{H}^{1}_{Rz}(M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M-1
. Therefore, by the inductive hypothesis one has the following equality:

(2.2)

\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+Rz+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}\mbox{H}^{1}_{Rz}(M))=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}\mbox{H}^{1}_{Rz}(M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M-1.

Now, we claim that
z\not\in\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}M/(0:_{M}I^{n})}\mathfrak{p}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. To do this, contrary assume that
z\in\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}M/(0:_{M}I^{n})}\mathfrak{p}
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, there is
\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass_{R}M/(0:_{M}I^{t})
such that
z\in\mathfrak{p}
and also there is an element
\beta\in M
with
\beta\not\in(0:_{M}I^{t})
such that
\mathfrak{p}=(0:_{R}\beta+(0:_{M}I^{t}))
. Therefore
z\beta I^{t}=0
; and thus
I^{t}\beta\subseteq(0:_{M}Rz)=0
. So,
\beta\in(0:_{M}I^{t})
which is a contradiction. Now, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(0:_{M}I^{n})\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/(0:_{M}I^{n})\longrightarrow 0,

one get the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow H^{1}_{Rz}((0:_{M}I^{n}))\longrightarrow H^{1}_{Rz}(M),
which shows that
\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}H^{1}_{Rz}(M)\subseteq\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}H^{1}_{Rz}((0:_{M}I^{n})).
Since
z
is a regular element on the finitely generated
R
-module
(0:_{M}I^{n})
, then by
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
one has the equality
\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}H^{1}_{Rz}((0:_{M}I^{n}))=\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}(0:_{M}I^{n}).

Hence, by considering the following

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M&\subseteq\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}H^{1}_{Rz}(M)\\
&\subseteq\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}(0:_{M}I^{n})\\
&=\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M,\end{array}

we can get the equality
\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}H^{1}_{Rz}(M)\overset{{\dagger}}{=}\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}M
. Therefore, given the (2.1), (2.2) and
†
we have the following equalities:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(I+\mathfrak{b})&=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(Rz+I+\mathfrak{b})+1\\
&=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}R/(Rz+I+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}H^{1}_{Rz}(M))+1\\
&=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M-1+1\\
&=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}M,\end{array}

as required.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

There exists a two-dimensional Noetherian local domain
(R,\mathfrak{m})

which does not have a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module (see
[
10
, §1]
and
[
7
]
).
Therefore, by
[
4
, Corollay 3.6]
, over such a ring the
R
-module
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)
can not be finitely generated.
Now, let
x
be a nonzero element of
R
and consider the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/xR)\longrightarrow\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R),

which is induced from the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\longrightarrow R/xR\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore, one has the following isomorphism:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/xR)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(R/Rx,\mbox{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)).

If
x\in\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R))
, then by the above isomorphism
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/xR)\cong\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)

which is a contradiction by the fact that
\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)
is not finitely generated, and so we have
\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R))=0
.

Furthermore, by the isomorphism (2.3),
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(R/Rx,\mbox{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is finitely generated and also
\Supp_{R}\mbox{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\subseteq\mbox{Max}\,R\cap\mbox{V}(Rx).
Hence, by
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
one has
\mbox{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is
Rx
-cofinite. Therefore, there is the following equality:

1=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}(R/Rx)=\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}R/(Rx+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)).

But,
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)=0
, and so
\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}R/(Rx+\mbox{Ann}\,_{R}(\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R))\neq\operatorname{\mathsf{dim}}_{R}\mbox{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(R)
. It therefore follows from Theorem
2.1
that the desired ring
R
cannot be complete. So, in our main result, the completeness assumption on
R
is quite necessary.

B → K ∗ ​ M X B\to K^{*}M_{X} vs B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} as a probe of a scalar-mediator dark matter scenario
Alexander Berezhnoy a and Dmitri Melikhov a,b,c

Abstract
Recently, Belle II reported the observation of the decay B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} , M X M_{X} the missing mass, with the branching ratio much exceeding ℬ ⁡ ( B → K ​ ν ​ ν ¯ ) {\cal B}(B\to K\nu\bar{\nu}) which is the only Standard Model (SM) process contributing to this reaction. If confirmed, this might be an indication of new nonSM particles produced in this decay. One of the possible explanations of the observed effect could be light dark-matter (DM) particles produced via a scalar mediator field. We give simple arguments, that a combined analysis of the B → K ​ M X B\to KM_{X} and B → K ∗ ​ M X B\to K^{*}M_{X} reactions would be a clean probe of the scalar mediator scenario: (i) making use of an observed value ℬ ⁡ ( B → K ​ M X ) ≃ 5.4 ​ ℬ ​ ( B → K ​ ν ​ ν ¯ ) SM {\cal B}(B\to KM_{X})\simeq 5.4\,{\cal B}(B\to K\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM} and (ii) assuming that the effect is due to the light dark matter coupling to the top quark via a scalar mediator field, one finds an upper limit ℬ ⁡ ( B → K ∗ ​ M X ) < 2.8 ​ ℬ ​ ( B → K ∗ ​ ν ​ ν ¯ ) SM {\cal B}(B\to K^{*}M_{X})<2.8\,{\cal B}(B\to K^{*}\nu\bar{\nu})_{\rm SM} . Within the discussed scenario, this upper limit does not depend on the mass of the scalar mediator nor on the specific details of the unobserved dark-matter particles in the final state.

Before closing this letter, let us briefly comment on the case of the spin-1 (
V
) mediator.
Here the situation is much more involved:
The effective

Hamiltonian coincides with the
C_{7\gamma}
part of the
b\to s\gamma
effective Hamiltonian
of the Standard Model
mns1998

the effect of the propagator of the vector mediator does not drop out

For instance, for

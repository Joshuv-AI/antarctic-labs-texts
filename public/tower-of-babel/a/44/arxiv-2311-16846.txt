Normalized Ground State Solutions for Fractional Schrödinger Systems with General Nonlinearities
Hichem Hajaiej, Linjie Song

Abstract
Normalized ground state solutions (NGSS) of Schrödinger equations (SE) have attracted the attention of many research groups during the last decades. This is essentially due to their relevance in many fields in physics and engineering, where the stable and most attractive solutions happen to be the normalized ones. For a single (SE), recent developments lead to the establishment of existence and non-existence results for a wide range of natural nonlinearities in (SE) in the sub-critical, critical and super-critical regimes. However for systems of (SE), there are still many interesting open questions for basic nonlinearities. It certainly requires innovative ideas to shed some light to treat these complex situations. So far, only a very few specific nonlinearities have been addressed. Unlike the single (SE), the corresponding strict sub-additivity inequality is challenging and an improved concentration-compactness theorem is critical to treat (NGSS) of systems of (SE). The aim of this paper is to establish the existence of (NGSS) for a large class of nonlinearities. This class includes many relevant pure-power type nonlinearities and can be easily extended to Hartree type nonlinearities (and a combination of both). The presence of the fractional Laplacian adds a considerable difficulty to rule out the dichotomy. We were able to overcome this challenge and establish very general assumptions ensuring the strict subadditivity of the constrained energy functional. We believe that our approach will open the door to many unresolved problems.

Fractional Laplacian, Vectorial Schrödinger, constrained minimization problem

2
A few technical Lemmata

To investigate the constrained variational problems, we firstly give some estimates.

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that
(H_{1})
and
(H_{5})
holds true for
F
and
F^{\infty}
respectively. Then, we have the following conclusions:

(1)
J(\textbf{u})
and
J^{\infty}(\textbf{u})
are
C^{1}
functional satisfying

\displaystyle||J(\textbf{u})||_{H^{-1}}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||_{H^{\alpha}}+||u_{i}||^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}}_{H^{\alpha}}),

\displaystyle||J^{\infty}(\textbf{u})||_{H^{-1}}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||_{H^{\alpha}}+||u_{i}||^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}}_{H^{\alpha}}),\ \forall\ \textbf{u}\in H^{\alpha}.

(2) There exist two positive constants
C_{A,m,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
and
C_{A^{\prime},m,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
such that

\displaystyle J(\textbf{u})\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{i}||_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}-C_{A,m,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}},

\displaystyle J^{\infty}(\textbf{u})\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{i}||_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}-C_{A^{\prime},m,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}},\ \forall\ \textbf{u}\in H^{\alpha}.

(3)
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}>-\infty
and
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}>-\infty
. In addition, any minimizing sequence of
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
and
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
is bounded in
H^{\alpha}
.

(4) The mappings
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\mapsto I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
and
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\mapsto I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
are continuous on
(0,+\infty)^{m}
.

Proof

We only prove the assertion for
J(\textbf{u})
and
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
here. The proofs for
J^{\infty}(\textbf{u})
and
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
are similar.

First, we introduce the cut off function
\phi(x)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&|x|\leq 1,\\
0,&|x|\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

By direct calculations, there exists a constant
K>0
such that for
1\leq i\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle||\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi(u_{j})\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u})||_{2}\leq K\sum_{j=1}^{m}||u_{j}||_{2},

\displaystyle||(1-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi(u_{j}))\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u})||_{p}\leq K\sum_{j=1}^{m}||u_{j}||_{q}^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}},

where

\displaystyle p=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2N}{N+2\alpha},&N\geq 2,\\
\frac{4}{3},&N=1,\end{array}\right.\ q=\big(\frac{4\alpha}{N}+1\big)p.

For any
\psi\in H^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u}),\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi(u_{j})\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u}),\psi\rangle+\langle(1-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi(u_{j}))\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u}),\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq||\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi(u_{j})\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u})||_{2}||\psi||_{2}+||(1-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\phi(u_{j}))\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u})||_{p}||\psi||_{p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(||u_{i}||_{2}||\psi||_{2}+||u_{i}||_{q}^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}}||\psi||_{p^{*}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||_{H^{\alpha}}+||u_{i}||^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}}_{H^{\alpha}})||\psi||_{H^{\alpha}},

where
p^{*}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. The last inequality is obtained by using Sobolev embedding theorem.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle||\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u})||_{H^{-\alpha}}\leq C_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||_{H^{\alpha}}+||u_{i}||^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}}_{H^{\alpha}}).

(2.1)

Note that

\displaystyle\int F(x,\textbf{u})dx\leq A\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||_{2}^{2}+||u_{i}||^{l_{1,i}+2}_{l_{1,i}+2})\leq C_{A}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||^{2}_{H^{\alpha}}+||u_{i}||^{l_{1,i}+2}_{H^{\alpha}}).

It implies that
J(\textbf{u})
is
C^{1}
on
H^{\alpha}
and

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(\textbf{u})\textbf{v}=\int\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Lambda^{\alpha}u_{i}\Lambda^{\alpha}v_{i}dx-\int\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{i}F(x,\textbf{u})v_{i}dx,\ \forall\ \textbf{v}:=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\in H^{\alpha}.

From (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle||J^{\prime}(\textbf{u})||_{H^{-\alpha}}\leq C_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{i}||_{H^{\alpha}}+||u_{i}||^{1+\frac{4\alpha}{N}}_{H^{\alpha}}),\ \forall\ \textbf{u}\in H^{\alpha}.

(2.2)

Next, we estimate
J(\textbf{u})
.
For any
\textbf{u}\in S_{c}
, there holds

\displaystyle\int F(x,\textbf{u})dx\leq Ac+A\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{i}||_{l_{1,i}+2}^{l_{1,i}+2}.

From the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
HMWO
]
and Young’s inequality, for
1\leq i\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle||u_{i}||_{l_{1,i}+2}^{l_{1,i}+2}

\displaystyle\leq C||u_{i}||^{\frac{2\alpha(l_{1,i}+2)-Nl_{1,i}}{2\alpha}}_{2}||u_{i}||^{\frac{Nl_{1,i}}{2\alpha}}_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4Am}(||u_{i}||_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{\frac{Nl_{1,i}}{2\alpha}})^{\frac{4\alpha}{Nl_{1,i}}}+C(\frac{1}{4AmC})^{\frac{Nl_{1,i}}{Nl_{1,i}-4\alpha}}(||u_{i}||_{2}^{\frac{2\alpha(l_{1,i}+2)-Nl_{1,i}}{2\alpha}})^{\frac{4\alpha}{4\alpha-Nl_{1,i}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4Am}||u_{i}||_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}+C_{A,m}||u_{i}||_{2}^{\frac{4\alpha(l_{1,i}+2)-2Nl_{1,i}}{4\alpha-Nl_{1,i}}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle J(\textbf{u})\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{i}||_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}-Ac-C_{A,m,c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}.

It implies that
J(\textbf{u})
is bounded from below on
S_{c}
and any minimizing sequence of the constrained variational problem
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
is bounded in
H^{\alpha}
.

Now, we prove the continuity of the map
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\mapsto I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
on
(0,\infty)^{m}
. For any fixed
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
, we consider a sequence
c_{n}=(c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m})
satisfying
c_{n,i}\rightarrow c_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists
\textbf{u}_{n}=(u_{n,1},\ldots,u_{n,m})\in S_{c_{n}}
with
\int|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx=c_{n,i},i=1,\ldots,m
such that

\displaystyle I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}\leq J(\textbf{u}_{n})\leq I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}+\frac{1}{n}.

Using a similar argument as the previous one, there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{n,i}||_{H^{\alpha}}\leq K,\ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

Let
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n}=(\tilde{u}_{n,1},\ldots,\tilde{u}_{n,m})
satisfy
\tilde{u}_{n,i}=\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{n,i}}}u_{n,i},i=1,\ldots,m
. It is obvious that
\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n}\in S_{c}
. Moreover, there exists
n_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle||\textbf{u}_{n}-\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n}||_{H^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{n,i}-\tilde{u}_{n,i}||_{H^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big|\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{n,i}}}-1\big|||u_{n,i}||_{H^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq K+1,\ \forall\ n\geq n_{1}.

Due to the estimate (
2.2
), we have:

\displaystyle|J(\textbf{u}_{n})-J(\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{||\textbf{u}||_{H^{\alpha}}\leq 2K+1}||J^{\prime}(\textbf{u})||_{H^{-\alpha}}||\textbf{u}_{n}-\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n}||_{H^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|1-\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{n,i}}}|.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}\geq J(\textbf{u}_{n})-\frac{1}{n}\geq J(\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n})+C_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|1-\sqrt{\frac{c_{i}}{c_{n,i}}}|-\frac{1}{n}

which implies that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}\geq I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}.

(2.3)

On the other hand, let
\textbf{u}_{n}\in S_{c}
be a minimizing sequence satisfying
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(\textbf{u}_{n})=I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{n,i}||_{H^{\alpha}}\leq K
. Then, for any sequence
c_{n}=(c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,i})
with
c_{n,i}\rightarrow c_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, we set
\tilde{u}_{n,i}=\sqrt{\frac{c_{n,i}}{c_{i}}}u_{n,i}
. Therefores

\displaystyle I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}\leq J(\tilde{\textbf{u}}_{n})\leq J(\textbf{u}_{n})+C_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|1-\sqrt{\frac{c_{n,i}}{c_{i}}}|.

Thus,
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}\leq I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
. Combining this and (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m}}=I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}

which completes the proof.

\square

Lemma 2.2

(1)

Assume that
(H_{1})
and
(H_{2})
hold true for
F
. Then,
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}<0
.

(2)

Assume that
(H_{2})
and
(H_{5})
hold true for
F^{\infty}
. Then,
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}<0
.

Proof

Let
\phi
be a fixed radial function with
||\phi||_{2}=1
. Define

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\Phi(\lambda x)=\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}(\phi_{1}(\lambda x),\ldots,\phi_{m}(\lambda x)),

where
\phi_{i}=\sqrt{c_{i}}\phi
.
0<\lambda<1
is a small constant to be chosen later.

One can see that

\displaystyle J(\Phi_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\Phi_{\lambda}||^{2}_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}-\int F(x,\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{1}(\lambda x),\ldots,\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{m}(\lambda x))dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\xi|^{2\alpha}|\widehat{\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{i}(\lambda x)}|^{2}d\xi-\int_{|x|\geq R}F(x,\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{1}(\lambda x),\ldots,\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{m}(\lambda x))dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2\alpha}}{2}\int|\frac{\xi}{\lambda}|^{2\alpha}|\widehat{\phi}\big(\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\big)|^{2}d\big(\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\big)-\lambda^{\frac{Ns}{2}}\beta\int_{|x|\geq R}|x|^{-t}\phi_{1}^{s_{1}}(\lambda x)\cdots\phi_{m}^{s_{m}}(\lambda x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2\alpha}}{2}||\Phi||^{2}_{2}-\beta\lambda^{\frac{Ns}{2}-N+t}\int_{|y|\geq\lambda R}|y|^{-t}\phi_{1}^{s_{1}}(y)\cdots\phi_{m}^{s_{m}}(y)dy

Thus, there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle J(\Phi_{\lambda})\leq\lambda^{2\alpha}(C_{1}-\lambda^{\frac{Ns}{2}-N+t-2\alpha}C_{2}).

Note that
\frac{Ns}{2}-N+t-2\alpha<0
. Therefore, it completes the proof by choosing
\lambda<\Big(\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{\frac{Ns}{2}-N+t-2\alpha}}
.
\square

Now, we prove the sub-additivity of
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
and
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Strict sub-additivity)

(1)

Assume that
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
and
(H_{3})
hold true for
F
. Then, for any
a_{i}\in(0,c_{i}),i=1,\ldots,m
, we have
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\leq I_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}+I_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
.

(2)

Assume that
(H_{2})
,
(H_{5})
and
(H_{6})
hold true for
F^{\infty}.
Then, for any
a_{i}\in(0,c_{i}),i=1,\ldots,m
, we have
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}<I^{\infty}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}+I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
.

Proof

First, we will prove the assertion (1). Let
\textbf{u}_{n}=(u_{n,1},\cdots,u_{n,m})\in S_{a}
,
\textbf{v}_{n}=(v_{n,1},\cdots,v_{n,m})\in S_{c-a}
be bounded minimizing sequences of
I_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}
and
I_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
respectively, satisfying
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(\textbf{u}_{n})=I_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}},\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(\textbf{v}_{n})=I_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
. By a density argument, we may suppose that
u_{n,i},v_{n,i}
have compact support for all
1\leq i\leq m
. For
\textbf{u}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m})\in H^{\alpha}
, we define

J_{1}(\textbf{u}):=\frac{1}{2}\int|\Lambda^{\alpha}u_{1}|^{2}-\int F_{11}(x,u_{1},\cdots,u_{m})dx,

J_{2}(u_{2},\cdots,u_{m}):=\sum_{i=2}^{m}\frac{1}{2}\int|\Lambda^{\alpha}u_{i}|^{2}-\int F_{21}(x,u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})dx,

where
F_{11}
and
F_{21}
are given by
(H_{3})
. Set

M_{1}:=\frac{1}{a_{1}}\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{1}(\textbf{u}_{n}),

M_{2}:=\frac{1}{c_{1}-a_{1}}\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{1}(\textbf{v}_{n}).

WLOG, we assume that
M_{1}\leq M_{2}
. We denote
\tilde{v}_{n,i}(x)=v_{n,i}(x+b_{i}L)
for all
2\leq i\leq m
where
L
is given by
(H_{3})
. For any
\epsilon>0
, by chosing
b_{i}
large enough, direct calculations yield that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(u_{n,2}+\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m}+\tilde{v}_{n,m})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(u_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m})+\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,\tilde{v}_{n,m})+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(u_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m})+\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(v_{n,2},\cdots,v_{n,m})+\epsilon.

Moreover, we can choose suitably
b_{i}
and
R_{n}
such that
\cup_{i\geq 1}\mbox{supp}\ u_{n,i}\subset B_{R_{n}}
and
\cup_{i\geq 2}\mbox{supp}\ v_{n,i}\subset B_{R_{n}}^{c}
. Then by
(H_{3})
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{1}(\sqrt{\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}}u_{n,1},u_{n,2}+\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m}+\tilde{v}_{n,m})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}J_{1}(u_{n,1},\cdots,u_{n,m})

\displaystyle\leq a_{1}M_{1}+(c_{1}-a_{1})M_{2}.

Noticing that
(\sqrt{\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}}u_{n,1},u_{n,2}+\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m}+\tilde{v}_{n,m})\in S_{c}
we deduce that

\displaystyle I_{c_{1},\cdots,c_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}J(\sqrt{\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}}u_{n,1},u_{n,2}+\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m}+\tilde{v}_{n,m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(J_{1}(\sqrt{\frac{c_{1}}{a_{1}}}u_{n,1},u_{n,2}+\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m}+\tilde{v}_{n,m})+J_{2}(u_{n,2}+\tilde{v}_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m}+\tilde{v}_{n,m}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{1}(\textbf{u}_{n})+\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{1}(\textbf{v}_{n})+\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(u_{n,2},\cdots,u_{n,m})+\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{2}(v_{n,2},\cdots,v_{n,m})+\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}J(\textbf{u}_{n})+\lim_{n\to\infty}J(\textbf{v}_{n})+\epsilon.

By the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
we complete the proof of (i).

Now, we focus on studying
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
. Let
\textbf{u}_{n}\in S_{a},\textbf{v}_{n}\in S_{c-a}
be two bounded minimizing sequences of
I^{\infty}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}
and
I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
respectively. By
(H_{6})
, similar to the proof of (i), we know that
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\leq I^{\infty}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}+I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
. Furthermore, the equality infers that

\lim_{n\to\infty}F^{\infty}_{1j}(x,\textbf{u}_{n})=0,\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,m\},

implying that
\lim_{n\to\infty}F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u}_{n})=0
. This yields that
I^{\infty}_{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}}\geq 0
, in a contradiction with Lemma
2.2
(2).

\square

Lemma 2.4

Assume that
F
and
F^{\infty}
satisfy
(H_{1})
,
(H_{2})
,
(H_{3})
and
(H_{2})
,
(H_{5})
,
(H_{6})
,
(H_{7})
respectively. Then, it follows that
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\leq I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
for any
c_{i},i=1,\ldots,m
. Moreover, for any
a_{i}\in(0,c_{i}),i=1,\ldots,m
, we have
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}<I_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}+I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}
.

Proof

The result is obtained by Lemma
2.3
and hypothesis
(H_{7})
immediately.

\square

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.2
by the improved concentration-compactness principle (Lemma
1.4
). Assume that
\textbf{u}_{n}
is a minimizing sequence of the constrained variational problems
I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
. First, we will rule out vanishing by contradiction. For that, we suppose that
\textbf{u}_{n}
is vanishing. That is, for all
r<\infty
, it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{|x-y|\leq r}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx=0.

From Lemma 2.4 in
[
15
]
, one can see
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{n,i}||_{p}=0
for
p\in(2,2^{*}_{\alpha})
.

Recalling the assumption
(H_{5})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u})dx=0.

It obtains that
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n})\geq 0
which contradicts with Lemma
2.2
.

Next, we will prove that the dichotomy does not occur by contradiction. Suppose that there exists constants
a_{i}\in(0,c_{i})
and two bounded vectorial sequences
\textbf{v}_{n}=(v_{n,1},\ldots,v_{n,m}),\textbf{w}_{n}=(w_{n,1},\ldots,w_{n,m})\subset H^{\alpha}
such that for
1\leq i,j\leq m
, we have

\mbox{supp}\ v_{n,i}\bigcap\mbox{supp}\ w_{n,j}=\emptyset,

|v_{n,i}|+|w_{n,i}|\leq|u_{n,i}|.

||v_{n,i}||^{2}_{L^{2}}:=a_{n,i}\rightarrow a_{i},\quad||w_{n,i}||^{2}_{L^{2}}:=b_{n,i}\rightarrow(c_{i}-a_{i}),\ as\ n\rightarrow\infty.

||u_{n,i}-v_{n,i}-w_{n,i}||_{L^{p}}\rightarrow 0,\ \textrm{for}\ 2\leq p<2^{*}_{\alpha},

where

\displaystyle 2^{*}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\infty,&N\leq 2\alpha\\
\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha},&N>2\alpha.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\{\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{n,i},u_{n,i}\rangle-\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}v_{n,i},v_{n,i}\rangle-\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}w_{n,i},w_{n,i}\rangle\}\geq 0.

From the arguments in Lemma
2.1
, there exists a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\int(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n})-F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n}))dx|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\int(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n}+\textbf{w}_{n})dx|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{||\textbf{u}||_{H^{\alpha}}\leq 2K+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||\partial_{i}F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u})||_{2}||\textbf{u}_{n}-\textbf{v}_{n}-\textbf{w}_{n}||_{2}

\displaystyle+\sup_{||\textbf{u}||_{H^{\alpha}}\leq 2K+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||\partial_{i}F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u})||_{p}||\textbf{u}_{n}-\textbf{v}_{n}-\textbf{w}_{n}||_{p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{K}(||\textbf{u}_{n}-\textbf{v}_{n}-\textbf{w}_{n}||_{2}+||\textbf{u}_{n}-\textbf{v}_{n}-\textbf{w}_{n}||_{p^{*}}),

where
p^{*}
is the conjugate of
p
.

Then, for any given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N>0
such that

\displaystyle J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n})-J^{\infty}(\textbf{v}_{n})-J^{\infty}(\textbf{w}_{n})\geq-\epsilon,\ \forall n\geq N.

We have that

\displaystyle I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}

\displaystyle=J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n})-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq J^{\infty}(\textbf{v}_{n})+J^{\infty}(\textbf{w}_{n})-2\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq I^{\infty}_{a_{n,1},\ldots,a_{n,m}}+I^{\infty}_{b_{n,1},\ldots,b_{n,m}}-2\epsilon

for
n
large enough.

Since the map
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\mapsto I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
is continuous, let
n
go to infinity. Thanks to the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\geq I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}+I^{\infty}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}

which is a contraction with Lemma
2.3
.

Thus, we conclude that the compactness occurs. Therefore, there exists a sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for any given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
r(\epsilon)>0
with

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(y_{n},r(\epsilon))}u^{2}_{n,i}dx\geq c-\epsilon.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we can choose
z_{n}=(z_{n,1},\ldots,z_{n,m})
with
z_{n,i}\in\{NL,N=0,1,\ldots\}(1\leq i\leq m)
such that
y_{n}-z_{n}\in[0,L]^{N}
, where
L\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is such that
F^{\infty}(x+L,\textbf{u})=F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Now, define
\textbf{v}_{n}=\textbf{u}_{n}(x+z_{n})
. Note that
||\textbf{u}_{n}||_{H^{\alpha}}=||\textbf{v}_{n}||_{H^{\alpha}}
which is bounded. Therefore, there exists a subsequence
\textbf{v}_{n}
which is denoted by itself and there exists
v
satisfying
\textbf{v}_{n}\rightharpoonup\textbf{v}
in
H^{\alpha}
.

On the one hand, there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}||v_{i}||^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(0,r+\sqrt{N}L)}|v_{i}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(0,r+\sqrt{N}L)}|v_{n,i}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(z_{n},r+\sqrt{N}L)}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(y_{n},r)}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq c-\epsilon.

Due to the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}||v_{i}||^{2}_{2}\geq c.

On the other hand,
||v_{i}||^{2}_{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}||v_{n,i}||^{2}_{2}\leq c_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m.
Therefore, there holds

\displaystyle||v_{i}||^{2}_{2}=c_{i}

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||v_{i}-v_{n,i}||_{2}=0,\ 1\leq i\leq m.

Applying an interpolation theorem, we have

\displaystyle v_{n,i}\rightarrow v_{i}\ \textrm{in}\ L^{p},\ p\in[2,2^{*}_{\alpha}).

Then, we have that:

\displaystyle\int F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n})dx\rightarrow\int F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v})dx.

(3.1)

Due to the periodicity of
F^{\infty}(\cdot,\textbf{u})
, we get

\displaystyle J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n})=J^{\infty}(\textbf{v}_{n})\rightarrow I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\ \textrm{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

By the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm in
L^{2}
and (
3.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle J^{\infty}(\textbf{v})=I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}

which implies

\displaystyle v_{n,i}\rightarrow v_{i}\ \textrm{in}\ H^{\alpha},\ 1\leq i\leq m.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
. Assume that
\textbf{u}_{n}
is a minimizing sequence of the constrained variational problems of
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
. First, we will rule out vanishing by contradiction. We suppose that
\textbf{u}_{n}
is vanishing. That is, for all
r<\infty
, it follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{|x-y|\leq r}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx=0.

From Lemma 2.4 in
[
15
]
, one can see that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}||u_{n,i}||_{p}=0
for
p\in(2,2^{*}_{\alpha})
.

From the assumptions
(H_{1})
and
(H_{4})
, it follows that for any given
\delta>0
, there exists
R_{\delta}>0
such that

\displaystyle F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big[\delta(|u_{n,i}|^{2}+|u_{n,i}|^{l_{2,i}+2})+A^{\prime}(|u_{n,i}|^{\beta^{\prime}_{i}+2}+|u_{i}|^{l_{3,i}+2})\Big],\ \forall|x|\geq R_{\delta}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R_{\delta}}F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})dx\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big[\delta(||u_{n,i}||_{2}^{2}+||u_{n,i}||^{l_{2,i}+2}_{l_{2,i}+2})+A^{\prime}(||u_{n,i}||^{\beta^{\prime}_{i}+2}_{\beta^{\prime}_{i}+2}+||u_{n,i}||^{l_{3,i}+2}_{l_{3,i}+2})\Big].

(4.1)

This implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x|\geq R_{\delta}}F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})dx\leq\delta c.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R_{\delta}}F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{|x|\leq R_{\delta}}\Big(|u_{i,n}|^{2}+|u_{i,n}|^{l_{3,i}+2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\sum_{i=1}^{m}(||u_{n,i}||^{l_{3,i}+2}_{l_{3,i}+2}|R_{\delta}|^{\frac{l_{3,i}}{l_{3,i}+2}}+||u_{n,i}||^{l_{3,i}+2}_{l_{3,i}+2})\rightarrow 0,\ \textrm{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

From (
4.1
) and the arbitrariness of
\delta
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})dx=0.

Meanwhile, we notice that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(\textbf{u})=I_{c_{1}\ldots,c_{m}}<0.

Which is a contradiction.

Next, we will ruled out the dichotomy by contradiction. Suppose that there exists constants
a_{i}\in(0,c_{i})
and two bounded vectorial sequences
\textbf{v}_{n}=(v_{n,1},\ldots,v_{n,m}),\textbf{w}_{n}=(w_{n,1},\ldots,w_{n,m})\subset H^{\alpha}
such that for
1\leq i,j\leq m
, we have

\mbox{supp}\ v_{n,i}\bigcap\mbox{supp}\ w_{n,j}=\emptyset,

|v_{n,i}|+|w_{n,i}|\leq|u_{n,i}|.

\displaystyle||v_{n,i}||^{2}_{L^{2}}:=a_{n,i}\rightarrow a_{i},\quad||w_{n,i}||^{2}_{L^{2}}:=b_{n,i}\rightarrow(c_{i}-a_{i}),\ as\ n\rightarrow\infty.

(4.2)

||u_{n,i}-v_{n,i}-w_{n,i}||_{L^{p}}\rightarrow 0,\ \textrm{for}\ 2\leq p<2^{*}_{\alpha},

where

\displaystyle 2^{*}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\infty,&N\leq 2\alpha\\
\frac{2N}{N-2\alpha},&N>2\alpha.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\{\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{n,i},u_{n,i}\rangle-\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}v_{n,i},v_{n,i}\rangle-\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}w_{n,i},w_{n,i}\rangle\}\geq 0.

Then, for any given
\epsilon>0
, choosing
\delta\in(0,\epsilon)
, there exists
n_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle J(\textbf{u}_{n})-J(\textbf{v}_{n})-J^{\infty}(\textbf{w}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}u_{n,i},u_{n,i}\rangle-\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}v_{n,i},v_{n,i}\rangle-\langle(-\triangle)^{\alpha}w_{n,i},w_{n,i}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle-\int(F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{v}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n}))dx+\int(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n}))dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\delta-\int(F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{v}_{n}+\textbf{w}_{n}))dx+\int(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n}))dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\delta-\int(F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{v}_{n}+\textbf{w}_{n}))dx+\int_{|x-y_{n}|\geq R_{n}}(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n}))dx,\ \forall\ n\geq n_{2},

where
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
R_{n}
satisfies
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{n}=+\infty
.

With similar arguments as in the proof of theorem
1.2
, there exists
n_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle-\delta-\int(F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{v}_{n}+\textbf{w}_{n}))dx\geq-\epsilon,\ \forall\ n\geq n_{3}.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle J(\textbf{u}_{n})-J(\textbf{v}_{n})-J^{\infty}(\textbf{w}_{n})\geq-\epsilon+\int_{|x-y_{n}|\geq R_{n}}(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n}))dx,\ \forall\ n\geq\max\{n_{2},n_{3}\}.

From the assumption
(H_{4})
, for any given
\eta>0
, there exists
R>0
such that

\displaystyle|F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u})-F(x,\textbf{u})|\leq\eta\sum_{i=1}^{m}(|u_{i}|^{2}+|u_{i}|^{l_{2,i}+2})

for all
u
and
|x|\geq R.

Thanks to the boundness of
\{y_{n}\}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{n}=\infty
, there exists
n_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle\{x:|x-y_{n}|\geq R_{n}\}\subset\{x:|x|\geq R\},\ \forall\ n\geq n_{4}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y_{n}|\geq R_{n}}|F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n})|dx\leq\eta\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\|w_{n,i}\|_{2}^{2}+\|w_{n,i}\|_{l_{2,i}+2}^{l_{2,i}+2}),\ \forall\ n\geq n_{4}.

Combining that and the boundedness of
\{\textbf{w}_{n}\}
in
H^{\alpha}
, there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x-y_{n}|\geq R_{n}}|F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{w}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{w}_{n})|dx=0.

Therefore, it obtains that

\displaystyle I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(\textbf{u}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(J(\textbf{v}_{n})+J^{\infty}(\textbf{w}_{n})\Big)-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(I_{a_{n,1},\ldots,a_{n,m}}+I^{\infty}_{b_{n,1},\ldots,b_{n,m}}\Big)-\epsilon.

From (
4.2
) and Lemma
2.1
,
I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\geq I_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}+I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}-\epsilon
. Thanks to the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
, we get

\displaystyle I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}\geq I_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}+I^{\infty}_{c_{1}-a_{1},\ldots,c_{m}-a_{m}}.

It is a contradiction which implies that the dichotomy cannot occur.

Thus, we can conclude that the compactness occurs. Hence, there exists a sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R(\epsilon)>0
with

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(y_{n},R(\epsilon))}u^{2}_{n,i}dx\geq c-\epsilon.

(4.3)

Now we will prove the sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
is bounded. By contradiction, we suppose that
|y_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
by passing to a subsequence which is denoted by itself. We can choose
z_{n}=(z_{n,1},\ldots,z_{n,m})
satisfying
z_{n,i}\in\{NL,N=0,1,\ldots\}
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|z_{n,i}|=\infty
,
i=1,\ldots,m
such that
y_{n}-z_{n}\in[0,L]^{N}
.

Define
\textbf{v}_{n}(x)=\textbf{u}_{n}(x+z_{n}):=(v_{n,1},\ldots,v_{n,m})
. With a similar argument in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, there exists
\textbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\in H^{\alpha}
such that
v_{n,i}\rightharpoonup v_{i}
in
H^{\alpha}
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||v_{n,i}-v_{i}||_{L^{p}}=0\ \textrm{for}\ 2\leq p<2^{*}_{\alpha},\ i=1,\ldots,m.

On the other hand, note that

\displaystyle J(\textbf{u}_{n})-J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n})

\displaystyle=\int(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F(x,\textbf{u}_{n}))dx

\displaystyle=\int(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{u}_{n})-F(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n}))dx.

From the assumption
(H_{4})
, for any given
\delta>0
, there exists
R(\delta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|x-z_{n}|\geq R(\delta)}\big(F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n})-F(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n})\big)dx\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|x-z_{n}|\geq R(\delta)}\big(F^{\infty}(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n})-F(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n})\big)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{|x-z_{n}|\geq R(\delta)}(|v_{n,i}|^{2}+|v_{n,i}|^{l_{2,i}+2})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(||v_{n,i}||^{2}_{H^{1}}+||v_{n,i}||^{l_{2,i}+2}_{l_{2,i}+2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta.

The last inequality is from the Sobolev embedding and the boundedness of
\textbf{v}_{n}
in
H^{\alpha}
.

Since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|z_{n}|=\infty
, there exists
n_{R(\delta)}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|x-z_{n}|\leq R(\delta)}F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n})-F(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n})dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}|}F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n})-F(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n})dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}|}\Big(|v_{n,i}|^{2}+|v_{n,i}|^{l_{2,i}+2}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\int_{|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}|}\big(|v_{i}|^{2}+|v_{i}|^{l_{2,i}+2}\big)dx+\int_{|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}|}\big(|v_{n,i}-v_{i}|^{2}+|v_{n,i}-v_{i}|^{l_{2,i}+2}\big)dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\int_{|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}|}\big(|v_{i}|^{2}+|v_{i}|^{l_{2,i}+2}\big)dx+\int\big(|v_{n,i}-v_{i}|^{2}+|v_{n,i}-v_{i}|^{l_{2,i}+2}\big)dx\Big)

for
n\geq n_{R(\delta)}.

It obtains that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big|\int_{|x-z_{n}|\leq R}F^{\infty}(x,\textbf{v}_{n})-F(x-z_{n},\textbf{v}_{n})dx\Big|=0.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}(J(\textbf{u}_{n})-J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n}))\geq-C\delta.

Due to the arbitrariness of
\delta,
it obtains that

\displaystyle I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J(\textbf{u}_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}J^{\infty}(\textbf{u}_{n})\geq I^{\infty}_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}.

It is a contradiction with Lemma
2.4
. Hence,
\{y_{n}\}
is bounded.

Let
\rho=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|y_{n}|
. From (
4.3
), there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(0,R(\epsilon)+\rho)}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(y_{n},R(\epsilon))}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx\geq c-\epsilon.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int|u_{i}|^{2}dx\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(0,R(\epsilon)+\rho)}|u_{i}|^{2}dx=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B(0,R(\epsilon)+\rho)}|u_{n,i}|^{2}dx\geq c-\epsilon.

Due to the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
, it obtains that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int|u_{i}|^{2}dx\geq c.

From the weakly lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
norm, one can see

\displaystyle\int|u_{i}|^{2}dx\leq c^{2}_{i},\ \forall 1\leq i\leq m.

Therefore,
\textbf{u}\in S_{c}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||u_{n,i}-u_{i}||_{2}=0
,
1\leq i\leq m
. From the boundedness of
||\textbf{u}_{n}||_{H^{1}}
and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, it obtains that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||u_{n,i}-u_{i}||_{p}=0
for
p\in[2,2^{*}_{\alpha})
,
1\leq i\leq m
. It implies that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int F(x,\textbf{u}_{n})dx=\int F(x,\textbf{u})dx
and
J(\textbf{u})=I_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}}
.

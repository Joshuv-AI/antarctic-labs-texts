1.
Introduction

1.1.
A historical overview

Let

(1.1)

f=\{f_{k}(x),\quad k=1,2,\ldots\}

be an infinite sequence of real functions. Denote by
C(f)\subset\mathbb{R}
the convergence set of sequence (
1.1
), i.e. the set of points
x\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\lim f_{n}(x)
exists. A classical theorem of Hahn-Sierpinski
[
13
,
35
]
asserts that if functions (
1.1
) are continuous, then
C(f)
is a
F_{\sigma\delta}
-set, and conversely, every
F_{\sigma\delta}
-set is a convergence set for a sequence of continuous functions. The first part of this statement is immediate, since the convergence set of (
1.1
) may be written in the form

C(f)=\cap_{m=1}^{\infty}\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}\{x:\,|f_{n+k}(x)-f_{n}(x)\leq 1/m\},

and the latter is a
F_{\sigma\delta}
-set if the functions are continuous (see Definition
1.1
). The second part of the Hahn-Sierpinski theorem requires certain construction of a sequence of continuous functions for which a given
F_{\sigma\delta}
-set is a convergence set (see also
[
15
]
, page 307). Note that the complement of the convergence set
C(f)
is the divergence set of (
1.1
), which we denote by
D(f)
. So the
G_{\delta\sigma}
sets completely characterize the divergence sets of sequences of continuous functions. One can also consider unbounded divergence set
UD(f)\subset D(f)
of (
1.1
), which consists of the points
x
, satisfying
\limsup|f_{n}(x)|=\infty
. It is also known that for a set to be the
UB
-set for a sequence of continuous functions it is necessary and sufficient to be a
G_{\delta}
-set (see
[
13
,
35
,
15
]
). Hence, we have certain topological characterization of the
C
,
D
and
UD
sets in the class of sequences consisting of continuous functions.

Such characterization problems were commonly considered in many fields of analysis (Fourier series, analytic functions on the unit ball, power series, differentiation theory) and there are many published papers and open problems, some of which will be considered in the last section. Earlier reviews of problems, concerning the convergence or divergence sets of Fourier series in trigonometric, Walsh and Haar orthogonal systems the reader can find in papers
[
40
,
38
,
39
]
. We also refer the monograph
[
7
]
related to boundary exceptional sets of analytic functions.

Let us state few remarkable examples of characterization theorems below. The first one provides a complete characterization of non-differentiability points sets of continuous functions.

Theorem A

(Zahorski,
[
42
]
)
.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
can be a set of non-differentiability points of a real continuous function if and only if it is a union of a
G_{\delta}
-set and a
G_{\delta\sigma}
-null-set.

In fact, the main ingredient of this theorem is the construction of a continuous function, whose non-differentiability points set coincides with a given set, which is a union of a
G_{\delta}
-set and a
G_{\delta\sigma}
-set of measure zero. For the realization of this construction in
[
42
]
a subtle technique was used (see also
[
32
]
and
[
9
]
for alternative simplified proofs of Theorem

). The next well-known result is an extension of Kolmogorov’s celebrated theorem of
[
25
]
on an example of an integrable function, whose Fourier series is everywhere divergent.

Theorem B

(Zeller,
[
43
]
)
.

Let
E
be a
F_{\sigma}
-set in the circle
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi
. Then there exists a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
whose Fourier series convergence at any point
x\in E
and unboundedly diverges whenever
x\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
.

This theorem, combining with above mentioned general results, provides a complete characterization of
UD
-sets in the class of Fourier series of functions from
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
. Later, an analogous result for the Walsh series was proved in
[
27
,
28
]
. Note that it is an open problem to characterize

or

sets of Fourier series of

,
1<p\leq\infty
, or continuous functions (see
[
38
]
, page 107). Note that according to Carleson-Hunt
[

,
16
]
results, Fourier series of functions from
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,

converge a.e., so the devergence sets of those series are null-sets. Some partial results concerning the characterization of convergence or divergence sets of Fourier series one can find in
[
1
,
2
,

,
37
,
10
,
24
,
17
,
19
,

,
18
,
29
,

]
. Mostly, those papers provide construction of different examples of Fourier series, diverging on a given null-set and
in this context the most general is Kahane-Katznelson’s
[
17
]
theorem on existence of a continuous function, whose trigonometric Fourier series diverges on a given null-set (see also
[
23
]
, chap. II.3, for a detailed discussion of divergence sets). We also refer papers

obtained a general characterization theorems for certain operator sequences, which cover many results of above mentioned papers, providing complete characterization of convergence sets of Fourier series in Haar, Franklin systems, as well as positive order Cezaro means of trigonometric and Walsh Fourier series. We remark here that Körner

The next result provides a complete characterization of radial convergence sets of bounded analytic functions on the unit disc. It was a solution of a longstanding problem posed by Collingwood and Lohwater in

(Kolesnikov,

Let

1.2.
Main results of the paper

In the present paper we obtain complete characterization theorems for certain operator sequences living in general spaces of homogeneous type. Those generalize the results of
[
20
,
21
]
, where the operators were considered on the interval
[0,1]
. By a quasi-distance on a set
X
we mean a non-negative function
d(x,y)
defined on
X\times X
such that

(1)

d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
,

(2)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for any
x,y\in X
,

(3)

d(x,y)\leq K(d(x,z)+d(z,y))
,

where
K>0
is a constant. Such a distance defines a topology on
X
, for which the balls
B(x,r)=\{y\in X:\,d(y,x)<r\}
form a base. Namely, a set
G\subset X
in this topology is open if and only if for any point
x\in G
there exists a ball
B(x,r)\subset G
. However, the balls themselves need not to be open when
K>1
. Let
\mu
be measure on a
\sigma
-algebra in
X
, containing all Borel sets and balls, such that

(1.2)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\mu(B(x,r))<\infty.

Such a combination
(X,d,\mu)
is called a space of homogeneous type, which definition is adapted from
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Recall that a set in a topological space is of
G_{\delta}
type provided it is a countable intersection of open sets, and a set is
G_{\delta\sigma}
if it is a countable union of
G_{\delta}
-sets. The
F_{\sigma}
sets are the countable unions of closed sets, and
F_{\sigma\delta}
are the countable intersections of
F_{\sigma}
sets.

Definition 1.2
.

The distance of two sets
A
and
B
in a space of homogeneous type
X
will be denoted by

(1.3)

{\rm dist}(A,B)=\inf_{x\in A,\,y\in B}d(x,y)

The notation
A\Subset B
will stand for the relation
{\rm dist}(A,B^{c})>0
. Note that
A\Subset B
implies
B^{c}\Subset A^{c}
.

Let
L^{1}(X)
denote the space of Lebesgue integrable functions on a space of homogeneous type
(X,d,\mu)
and suppose that
M(X)
is the normed space of bounded measurable functions on
X
with the norm
\|f\|_{M}=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|
.
We consider sequences of linear operators

(1.4)

U_{n}:L^{1}(X)\to M(X),\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

which may have some of the following properties

U 1
.

\rho_{n}=\|U_{n}\|_{L^{1}\to M}<\infty
,
n=1,2,\ldots
,

U 2
.

\varrho=\sup_{n}\|U_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}\to M}<\infty
,

U 3
.

if
f\in L^{1}(X)
is constant on an open set
G\subset X
, then
U_{n}(x,f)
uniformly converges over any set
E\Subset G
.

U 4
.

if
G\subset X
is an open set with
\mu(G)<\infty
, then
U_{n}(x,\textbf{1}_{G})\to\textbf{1}_{G}(x)
almost everywhere as
n\to\infty
.

We will always suppose that
X
is a space of homogeneous type unless other assumptions are suggested. The main results of the paper read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If an operator sequence (
1.4
) satisfies U
1
-U
4
, then for any
G_{\delta\sigma}
null-set
E\subset X
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a function
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
such that

1)
\mu({\rm supp\,}f)<\varepsilon
,

2)
U_{n}(x,f)
diverges at any
x\in E
,

3)
U_{n}(x,f)\to f(x)
if
x\in X\setminus E
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let an operator sequence (
1.4
) satisfy U
1
-U
4
, and for a function
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
we have

(1.5)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\psi(t)=\infty.

Then for every
G_{\delta}
null-set
E\subset X
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a measurable function
f
such that

1)
\mu({\rm supp\,}f)<\varepsilon,\quad\int_{X}\psi(|f|)<\infty
,

2)
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|U_{n}(x,f)|=\infty
for all
x\in E
,

3)
U_{n}(x,f)\to f(x)
if
x\in X\setminus E
.

Observe that if besides of properties U
1
-U
4
we also require the continuity of
U_{n}(x,f)
as functions on

, then Theorem
1.1
gives the complete characterization of
C
and
D
sets of sequences

U_{n}(x,f)
, where

and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Similarly, Theorem
1.2
provides complete characterization of
UD
-sets of those sequences when
1\leq p<\infty
.
One can not claim for the function

in Theorem
1.2
to be bounded like in Theorem
1.1
, since in that case condition U
2
would imply the boundedness of
U_{n}(x,f)
for any

.

In the case when

coincides with
[0,1]
, Theorems

and
1.2
were proved in
[
21
]
, and we essentially use some arguments of
[
20
,
21
]
in the present work. Note that in a recent paper
[
12
]
authors consider kernel operator sequences

U_{n}(x,f)=\int_{[0,1]^{d}}K_{n}(x,t)f(t)dt

on the

[0,1]^{d}
(or
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), where the kernels

satisfy certain conditions in the spirit of an approximation of identity. Those conditions provide properties U
1
-U
3
for the operators

, but instead of U

the paper requires the convergence of

at any continuity point

. Under these requirements on

the main result of

The approach provided in the proofs of Theorems

and

enables to obtain also the following pure divergence result, where the operator sequences satisfy only conditions U

and U

. This result is a generalization of an analogous theorem of

for operators living on

.

Theorem 1.3
.

If an operator sequence (
1.4
) satisfies U
1
and U
3
, then for every null-set

, there exists a set

such that for the indicator function

we have a divergence of

. Moreover,

(1.6)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}(x,f)\geq 1,\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_{n}(x,f)\leq 0\text{ for all }x\in E.

Remark 1.1
.

With a misery change in the proofs, Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
can be stated and proved for operators

, where the parameter

varies in an infinite partially ordered set

. Namely, we can consider a partially ordered set

with a relation

, satisfying

Then given a process (or a sequence)

,

, in

, the convergence
\lim_{r\to r_{\infty}}x_{r}=a
requires the following: for any open set
G\supset a
there exists a

.

We note that Theorem

as well as the result of paper

]
generalize the results of papers

,

,

,

, where authors consider different Fourier partial sums (trigonometric, Walsh, Haar) instead of operators

2.
Examples and applications

2.1.
Approximation of the identity on metric measure spaces

Let
X
be a space of homogeneous type. A sequence of kernel functions
K_{n}(x,y)\in L^{\infty}(X\times X)\cap L^{1}(X\times X)
is said to be an approximation of the identity if it satisfies the conditions

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{X}K_{n}(x,y)dy\rightrightarrows 1\text{ as }n\to\infty,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\{y:\,d(x,y)>\delta\}}K_{n}^{*}(x,y)dy\rightrightarrows 0\text{ as }n\to\infty,\text{ for every }\delta>0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{n,x}\int_{X}|K_{n}^{*}(x,y)|dy<\infty.

where the convergence in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) holds uniformly with respect to
x
over
X
, and

K_{n}^{*}(x,y)=\sup_{t:\,d(x,t)\geq d(x,y)}|K_{n}(x,t)|.

Consider the operator sequence

(2.4)

U_{n}(x,f)=\int_{X}K_{n}(x,y)f(y)d\mu(y),

where the kernels
K_{n}
satisfy above conditions. It is known that if a space of homogeneous type satisfies the following property that the space of functions

(2.5)

C_{K}(X)=\left\{f\in C(X):\,{\rm supp\,}(f)\text{ is bounded and }\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|<\infty\right\}

is dense in
L^{1}(X)
, then operators (
2.4
) enjoy the following properties (see for example
[
6
,
5
]
):

1) If a function
f\in L^{1}(X)
is uniformly continuous (in particular is constant) on an open set
G\subset X
, then
U_{n}(x,f)\rightrightarrows f(x)
uniformly on any subset
E\Subset G
.

2) If
f\in L^{1}(X)
, then
U_{n}(x,f)\to f(x)
almost everywhere.

Obviously, such operators satisfy conditions U
1
-U
4
. Properties U
1
and U
2
immediately follow from the definition of approximation of the identity, while properties U
3
and U
4
are weakened cases of above properties 1) and 2) respectively. So we can state the following

Proposition 2.1
.

If a space of homogeneous type
X
is such that
C_{K}(X)
is dense in
L^{1}(X)
, then operators (
2.4
) obey the conditions of Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
.

2.2.
Walsh functions

We consider Walsh orthonormal system, defined on the set of all sequences

(2.6)

(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots),\quad x_{j}=0\text{ or }1,\quad j=0,1,2,\ldots.

(see
[
11
]
, chap. 1). Every such a sequence generates a series

(2.7)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{j}\cdot 2^{-j-1},

which is the dyadic decomposition of some number from
[0,1]
. We note that this correspondence is surjective, but it is not injective, since any dyadic rationals of
[0,1]
have two decompositions (
2.7
), infinite and finite. For a geometric understanding of the set of sequences (
2.6
) introduce the extended interval
[0,1]^{*}
, where each dyadic rational number
x
is doubled, generating the left point
x-
corresponding to the finite dyadic decomposition and the right point
x+
that corresponds to the infinite decomposition of
x
. Writing
x+
or
x-
for a dyadic irrational number
x
, we mean just the point
x\in[0,1]^{*}
. Define a generalized dyadic interval by

(2.8)

[a,b]=\{x+,x-\in[0,1]^{*}:\,a<x<b\}\cup\{a+,b-\},

where

(2.9)

a=\frac{j-1}{2^{k}},\quad b=\frac{j}{2^{k}},\quad 1\leq j\leq 2^{k},\quad k=0,1,\ldots.

One can easily check that the dyadic distance

(2.10)

d(x,y)=\inf\{b-a:\,[a,b]\text{ is a generalized dyadic interval},\,x,y\in[a,b]\},

between two points
x,y\in[0,1]^{*}
defines a quasi-distance on
[0,1]^{*}
. Then any set
E^{*}\subset[0,1]^{*}
has its counterpart
E\subset[0,1]
, obtained by an identifying all the pairs of points
x+,x-\in[0,1]^{*}
in one. So we define the measure of
E^{*}
to be the Lebesgue measure of the set
E
. Hence
[0,1]^{*}
equipped with such a measure becomes a space of homogeneous type. Moreover, one can easily check that
[0,1]^{*}
is a compact space (we will not need it).

To define the Walsh functions, recall the group operation on
[0,1]^{*}
, defining the sum of two sequences of type (
2.6
)
\{x_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
to be the sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
, where

z_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}1&\hbox{ if }&x_{j}+y_{j}=1,\\
0&\hbox{ if }&x_{j}+y_{j}=0\hbox{ or }2.\end{array}\right.

Now we can define the Walsh system of functions
\{w_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
on
[0,1]^{*}
. We set
w_{0}(x)\equiv 1
. For
n\geq 1
we write its dyadic representation

n=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{j}2^{j},

then define

(2.11)

w_{n}(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}}\text{ for }x=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in[0,1]^{*}.

One can see that Walsh functions are continuous in the topology of
[0,1]^{*}
. Besides, it is well-known that
(C,\alpha)
-means
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}(x,f)

\alpha>0
of the partial sums of Walsh-Fourier series satisfy properties U
1
-U
4
(see
[
11
]
, chap. 4). So we can state the following results.

Corollary 2.1
.

For a set
E\subset[0,1]^{*}
to be the divergence (unbounded divergence) set of
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}(x,f)
for a function
L^{p}([0,1]^{*})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
it is necessary and sufficient to be a
G_{\delta\sigma}
-set (
G_{\delta}
-set) in the topology of
[0,1]^{*}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The
(C,\alpha)
-means
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}
of Walsh-Fourier series can be considered as kernel operators. Nevertheless, it is known (see
[
11
]
) that the kernels of
\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}
do not form an approximation of the identity.

2.3.
Splines

Recall the definition of splines on an interval
[a,b]
. For a knot-collection
\Delta=\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n+k}\subset[a,b]
such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle t_{i}\leq t_{i+1},\quad t_{i}\leq t_{i+k},

(2.13)

\displaystyle t_{1}=\ldots=t_{k}=a,\quad t_{n+1}=\ldots=t_{n+k}=b

let denote by
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Delta)
the space of
k
-order splines with the notes
\Delta
. Those are the functions, which are polynomials of degree
\leq k-1
on each interval
[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
and has a
k-1-m_{j}
continuous derivatives at any node
t_{j}\in\Delta
of a multiplicity
m_{i}
. Let
P_{\Delta}
be the orthoprojection operator onto
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\Delta)
. Denote
|\Delta|=\max_{j}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})
. Given sequence of fixed
k
-order knot-collections
\Delta_{n}
with
|\Delta_{n}|\to 0
consider the operator sequence
P_{\Delta_{n}}
. It is known such an operator sequence satisfy properties U
1
-U
4
. Moreover, Shadrin
[
34
]
solving de Boor’s conjecture proved the uniformly boundedness of these operators on
C[a,b]
that implies
P_{\Delta_{n}}(f)\rightrightarrows f
whenever
f\in C[a,b]
. Then Passenbrunner and Shadrin in
[
31
]
proved
P_{\Delta_{n}}(f)\to f
a.e. for any
f\in L^{1}[a,b]
, where it was also proved that the kernels of operators
P_{\Delta_{n}}
form an approximation of the identity. Thus we can state the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\Delta_{n}
be a sequence of knot-collections in
[a,b]
such that
|\Delta_{n}|\to 0
. Then for a set
E\subset[a,b]
to be the divergence (unbounded divergence) set of
P_{\Delta_{n}}(f)
for a function
L^{p}[a,b]
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
it is necessary and sufficient to be a
G_{\delta\sigma}
-set (
G_{\delta}
-set) in the standard topology of
[a,b]
.

(2)

The characterization of convergence and divergence sets of

(3)

Let

be a space of homogeneous type such that

is dense in

It is known from

that for any quasi-distance

-balls become open sets there.
Using this we can prove the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

Any open set

in a space of homogeneous type permits a representation

, where each

is a closed set and

.

Proof.

Let

denote the distance corresponding to the quasi-distance

from (

). Define

It is clear that

. It remains to show that each

is closed or equivalently

is open. For any

. Then for small enough

). Thus we obtain

The following lemma is a standard property of a topology induced by a quasi-distance.

Lemma 3.2
.

If sets

and

and

Proof.

Let

be a Lipschits distance satisfying (

implies

, besides we know that each

-ball is an open set. Define an open set

\displaystyle|U_{\nu_{k}(\omega_{k})}(x,f)-k|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|U_{\nu_{k}(\omega_{k})}(x,h_{i})-1|+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}|U_{\nu_{k}(\omega_{k})}(x,h_{i})|

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq k\cdot\frac{1}{k^{2}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i^{2}}<\frac{2}{k},

which implies the unboundedly divergence of
U_{n}(x,f)
at a point

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We construct open sets

,

,

\Omega_{k}
for

. Applying Lemma
3.4
, we may choose open sets

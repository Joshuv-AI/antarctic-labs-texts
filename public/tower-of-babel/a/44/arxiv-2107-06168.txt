Reactivity of the Si(100)-2 × \times 1-Cl surface with respect to PH 3 , PCl 3 , and BCl 3 : Comparison with PH 3 on Si(100)-2 × \times 1-H
T. V. Pavlova 1,2, K. N. Eltsov 1

Abstract
Despite the interest in a chlorine monolayer on Si(100) as an alternative to hydrogen resist for atomic-precision doping, little is known about its interaction with dopant-containing molecules. We used the density functional theory to evaluate whether a chlorine monolayer on Si(100) is suitable as a resist for PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} , PCl 3 \text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} , and BCl 3 \text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} molecules. We calculated reaction pathways for PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} , PCl 3 \text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} , and BCl 3 \text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} adsorption on a bare and Cl-terminated Si(100)-2 × \times 1 surface, as well as for PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} adsorption on H-terminated Si(100)-2 × \times 1, which is widely used in current technologies for atomically precise doping of Si(100) with phosphorus. It was found that the Si(100)-2 × \times 1-Cl surface has a higher reactivity towards phosphine than Si(100)-2 × \times 1-H, and, therefore, unpatterned areas are less protected from undesirable incorporation of PH 3 \text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} fragments. On the contrary, the resistance of the Si(100)-2 × \times 1-Cl surface against the chlorine-containing molecules turned out to be very high. Several factors influencing reactivity are discussed. The results reveal that phosphorus and boron trichlorides are well-suited for doping a patterned Cl-resist by donors and acceptors, respectively.



I
Introduction

The interaction of dopant-containing molecules with the Si(100) surface is important for functionalization reactions used in many practical applications
Perrine and Teplyakov 2010
. In particular, the fabrication of nanoelectronic devices on single atoms requires functionalization of the Si(100) surface with atomic precision
Fuechsle et al. 2012
;
He et al. 2019
. To create silicon-dopant bonds in preselected areas, the resist patterned by scanning tunneling microscope (STM) lithography is dosing with dopant-containing molecules
O’Brien et al. 2001
;
Schofield et al. 2003
. One of the most important characteristics of an atomic resist is its low reactivity with respect to dopant-containing molecules
Goh et al. 2007
.

Currently, a hydrogen monolayer is used as a resist on the Si(100) surface, and phosphine serves as a donor-containing molecule
Fuechsle et al. 2012
;
He et al. 2019
;
O’Brien et al. 2001
;
Schofield et al. 2003
;
Goh et al. 2007
. Recently, a chlorine monolayer has been proposed as an alternative resist
Pavlova et al. 2018
. Although STM-lithography on the Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface has been demonstrated
Dwyer et al. 2019
;
Pavlova et al. 2020a
, the selectivity of the phosphine reaction with a chlorinated surface has been only estimated
Pavlova et al. 2018
. Besides, it can be reasonable to use trichlorides as dopant-containing molecules with Cl-resist (for example,
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

Silva-Quinones et al. 2020
;
Dwyer et al. 2021
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
) instead of trihydrides. However, it has previously been reported that the chlorinated surface is highly reactive towards
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

Silva-Quinones et al. 2020
and
\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

Soria et al. 2011
, with activation barriers for ammonia incorporation even lower than that on the bare surface. At the same time, a chlorine monolayer was successfully used as a resist for
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

Dwyer et al. 2021
, and the calculated activation barriers for the reaction of
\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
with a chlorinated surface were quite high
Lange and Schmidt 2008
. Thus, the reactivity of the Cl-terminated Si(100) surface with respect to some dopant-containing molecules is still unclear.

This work is aimed to establish the possibility of using chlorine as a resist for
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
by studying the selectivity of the reactions of such molecules with passivated and depassivated Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl. We used density functional theory (DFT) to calculate reaction pathways for
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
incorporation into the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface. As a reference, the reaction of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
with the H-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface was considered. To ensure that the selectivity of the studied reactions is high, we also calculated the adsorption and dissociation of the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules on the bare Si(100) surface and in H and Cl vacancies. The reaction pathways were considered with the removal of excess atoms into the gas phase as well as with their insertion into the surface. We found that
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
exhibit very high selectivity towards the reactions with the Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface with depassivated areas.

II
Calculation details

The spin-polarized DFT calculations were performed using the projector augmented wave method implemented in Vienna
Ab-initio
Simulation Package (VASP)
Kresse and Hafner 1993
;
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
. For the exchange-correlation potential, generalized gradient approximation (GGA) by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof (PBE)
Perdew et al. 1996
was employed. To account van der Waals corrections we used DFT-D2 method developed by Grimme
Grimme 2006
. The kinetic-energy cutoff of the plane wave basis was set up to 400 eV. The silicon surfaces were modeled by an eight layer slabs consisting of 4
\times
4 supercell and a vacuum region of about 13 Å. Chlorine or hydrogen atoms were placed on the upper face of the slabs to form a Si(100)-2
\times
1 structure, whereas H atoms saturate the dangling bonds at the bottom face of the slabs. The bottom two Si layers were frozen at their bulk position, while the coordinates of other atoms were fully relaxed until the residual forces were smaller than 0.01 eV/ Å. Brillouin zone integrations were done using a 4
\times
4
\times
1 k-point grid.

The calculated Si lattice constant (5.415 Å) is in a good agreement with the experimental value (5.416 Å). We used GGA-PBE with DFT-D2 because it is a good compromise between accuracy and computational cost to study the surface reactions considered in this work. For the accurate energy evaluation of the reaction of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules with H- and Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1, the size of the supercell is important. A larger surface supercell allows the better relaxation of the chlorine layer upon the trichloride molecule incorporation, and sixty layers in a slab are required to accurately describe the electronic structure of the Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface
Sagisaka et al. 2017
. However, we have limited the size of the supercell to 4
\times
4
\times
8 to enable calculations of reaction barriers in a reasonable time.

The activation barriers were calculated by using the nudged elastic band (NEB) method
Jónsson et al. 1998
and further validated by the climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method
Henkelman et al. 2000
. In this case, the criterion of convergence for residual forces was reduced to 0.03 eV/ Å. The number of images (including the two end points) in the NEB method ranged from seven for simple adsorption pathways to twelve for complex pathways. For further refinement calculation by the CI-NEB method, three to seven images were used, one of which was climbed to the highest saddle point.

Summarizing the results of this section, the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

-1

\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
unit becomes lower than that of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

III.2
Molecular adsorption in H and Cl vacancies

The structure with a phosphine adsorbed in a H vacancy on the Si(100)-2
\times
1-H surface is significantly less stable (adsorption energy
-0.32
eV) than that on the clean surface (
-1.14
eV). A stronger P-Si bond is formed on the clean surface due to the electrophilic nature of the down Si atom. Interestingly, the structure with
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorbed in a Cl vacancy (
-0.87
eV) is much more stable than in a H vacancy. This can be explained by the electronegativity of chlorine, which can lead to a more electrophilic Si atom on the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface than on the H-terminated one.

The adsorption of
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
in a Cl vacancy on Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl (
-0.37
eV) is less preferable than on the clean surface (
-0.94

IV
Discussion

To enable the utilizing of the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface as a resist, direct silicon-dopant bonds must be formed in the depassivated areas only. On the bare Si(100) surface, dissociative adsorption of
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
can occur spontaneously, but if
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
are molecularly adsorbed, dissociation into two fragments requires activation energy (Fig.
1
). The reaction barrier for
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
dissociation into
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and H is easily overcome at room temperature
Warschkow et al. 2016
. Consequently, the adsorbed
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule will rapidly dissociate at room temperature since it has a lower activation barrier. Taking into account that the time scale of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
dissociation is of the order of 10
\mathrm{\mu s}

Warschkow et al. 2016
, the adsorbed
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule is also expected to be short-lived at room temperature because the activation barrier for
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
dissociation is only slightly higher than for
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
. Thus, on the bare Si(100) surface, the first stage of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
dissociation into two fragments will proceed at room temperature.

To compare the resistance of a hydrogen and chlorine monolayer to the direct Si-dopant bond formation, we put together the lowest activation energies for all the considered reactions in Table
1
. At phosphine adsorption on the chlorinated surface, the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
unit formation requires an activation energy of 1.38 eV, which is approximately 0.5 eV lower than that for the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
unit on the hydrogenated surface. Thus, the Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface is more reactive to phosphine than Si(100)-2
\times
1-H. However, the replacement of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
with
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
(
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
) makes the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface more protective against Si-P (Si-B) bond formation. Indeed, the activation barrier for the
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
units insertion into Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl increases to 3.7 eV. Thus, if
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
are used as dopant-containing molecules, the chlorine monolayer protects the silicon surface from Si-P and Si-B bond formation even better than the hydrogen monolayer from Si-P bond formation at
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorption. Note that the chlorine monolayer also protects the Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface against undesirable dopant incorporation in the presence of single vacancies since molecular adsorption in vacancies is less favorable than on a clean surface.

There are several factors that govern the reactivity of the Si(100)-2
\times
1-H and Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surfaces with respect to the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules. One such factor is the energy loss at the
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, HCl, and
\text{Cl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
species desorption in the course of the molecular fragments insertion. To compare the desorption energies of these species, a 4
\times
4 supercell was prepared with one row of H-terminated dimers and another row of Cl-terminated dimers. We sequentially removed chlorine and hydrogen from the surface to form
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, HCl, or
\text{Cl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
in the vacuum gap between the slabs, keeping the distance between vacancies on the surface as large as possible. The desorption energies of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, HCl, and
\text{Cl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
obtained in this way are 2.71 eV, 3.04 eV and 5.57 eV, respectively. A comparison of the calculated desorption energies predicts that the reaction accompanied by
\text{Cl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
removal from the surface is significantly less favorable than the reaction with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
or HCl desorption. Note that excess atoms can remain on the surface rather than desorb, but the energy also increases due to defects formation. We can therefore conclude that the undesirable incorporation of the dopant-containing molecules with chlorine (such as
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
) into the Cl-terminated surface is very unlikely due to the high energy cost for
\text{Cl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
desorption or surface defect formation.

The steric effect is another factor that determines the lower reactivity of chlorine monolayer with respect to Cl-containing molecules compared to the reactivity of the hydrogen monolayer with respect to phosphine. Indeed, the calculated distance between the nearest Cl atoms on the Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface (3.83 Å) is close to the double van der Waals radius (ionic radius) of chlorine, which ranges from 3.5 to 3.8 Å
Batsanov 2001
. In addition, the
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules are larger than
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, as can be seen from the calculated P-Cl, B-Cl, and P-H bond lengths equal to 2.07 Å, 1.75 Å, and 1.43 Å, respectively. For these reasons, when we placed the
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules on Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl, in many cases they were pushed out of the surface by the repulsive forces between chlorine. Thus, the larger radius of the resist atoms, together with the size of the molecule, reduces the reactivity.

One more factor affecting reactivity is the charge distribution on the surface and in the molecules. Chlorine on the Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface takes more charge from the Si atom than hydrogen
Perrine and Teplyakov 2010
due to stronger electronegativity of Cl, which implies that Si atoms under a chlorine monolayer are more electrophilic than under a hydrogen monolayer. The adsorption of phosphorus-containing molecules is facilitated by a more electrophilic Si atom, since the
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules have a lone pair of electrons, which makes the chlorinated surface more reactive towards these molecules. On the contrary, the
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule is not polar and prefers to form a bond with a nucleophilic silicon atom. Consequently, the adsorption structure with
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
should have a lower stability than that with
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, in accordance with our calculations (Table
1
).

Recently, it was experimentally demonstrated that
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
reacts with the Cl-terminated Si(100) surface more effectively than with H-terminated Si(100) producing the direct Si-B bonds at temperatures even below 70
∘
C
Silva-Quinones et al. 2020
. Our calculations predict a low reactivity of the Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface with respect to
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and therefore seem to contradict this experiment. The contradiction can be explained by dichloride species and possible defects presented on the surfaces prepared by the wet chemistry method (as was mentioned in Ref.
Silva-Quinones et al. 2020
), which should have different reactivities than monochloride. In another recent work, the Si(100) surface covered with chlorine monolayer, which was verified by imaging the Cl-terminated Si(100) surface with an STM, was used to selectively deposit
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

Dwyer et al. 2021
. Experimental results obtained at room temperature demonstrate that the reaction proceeds selectively in the depassivated areas, in accordance with our calculations.

The selectivity of the reaction of phosphine with the lithographic patterned H-resist has been experimentally proved in the STM study
Goh et al. 2007
. After adsorption at room temperature,
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
fragments were found only within the depassivated areas. Therefore,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
are also suitable for doping patterned Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface since the activation barriers for their insertion under Cl monolayer are higher than in the case of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
on Si(100)-2
\times
1-H (Table
1
). Moreover, high activation barriers ensure that the chlorine monolayer will protect Si(100) from
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
even at higher temperatures, close to the temperature of chlorine desorption from Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl. In contrast, the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface is more reactive to phosphine than H-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1, but this does not necessarily mean that the Cl-resist is not suitable for use with phosphine. Indeed, the activation barrier of phosphine reaction with Cl-terminated silicon (1.38 eV) is higher than with the bare surface (0.66 eV), thus the chlorine monolayer can, in principle, protect Si(100) from undesirable incorporation of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
at certain parameters (temperature and phosphine dose).

V
Summary and conclusions

In summary, DFT calculations have been performed to study the selectivity of
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
,
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
reactions with the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface and
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
reaction with H-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1. Several factors have been identified that influenced the reactivity of the considered reactions. We found that the Cl-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1 surface is more reactive to phosphine than H-terminated Si(100)-2
\times
1. The Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface is the least reactive towards
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules, which allows it to use as a resist even at elevated temperatures. The reasons for the low reactivity are the steric effect and the significantly less favorable configurations with desorbed
\text{Cl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
compared to that with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
or
HCl
. Preferred adsorption of
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
on the nucleophilic rather than electrophilic Si atom explains the lower stability of the adsorption structures with
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
compared to
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
. Thus, the
\text{PCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules are well-suited for doping a selectively patterned Si(100)-2
\times
1-Cl surface with donors, and
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
with acceptors.

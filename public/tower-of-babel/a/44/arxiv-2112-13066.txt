Complexity of Bose-Hubbard Model :
Quantum Phase Transition

Abstract




Abstract

The operator approach is applied to investigate the complexity of Bose-Hubbard model. We present a systematic method to expand the quantum complexity in series of coupling constant. We first study 2-sites system. For the ground state we can find the exact value of complexity which is the summation of all order. The complexity is divergent at critical value of coupling constant that indicates the quantum phase transition at this point. We then generalize the method to the N-sites closed chain and any dimensional system. The found properties are similar to those in 2-sites system. We also study the excited state and present the general formulas of Bose-Hubbard model complexity, which shows a similar form as that in
\lambda\phi^{4}
theory studied in our previous paper.

2
Bose Hubbard Model

Quantum Phase transitions is known to occur when a coupling constant
g
in the Hamiltonian is varied across some critical value
g=g_{c}
at which the ground state dependence on
g
becomes non-analytic
[
15
,
16
]
. In this paper we are interesting in the quantum phase transition happens in a system of Bosons in the background of a periodic potential and a two-particle repulsive interaction. The Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=\int d^{d}x\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\psi^{\dagger}(x)\nabla^{2}\psi(x)\right)+\frac{{\cal U}_{0}}{2}\int d^{d}x\psi^{\dagger}(x)\psi^{\dagger}(x)\psi(x)\psi(x)

(2.1)

The constant
{\cal U}_{0}
is the short-ranged repulsion between the Bosons. At low energies, we can keep only the lowest vibrational state at each minima of
{\cal V}_{0}(x)
and the dynamics is given by the Bose Hubbard model
[
13
]
:

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J\over N}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{i}\tilde{a}_{j}+{1\over N}\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\tilde{n}_{i}+{U\over N}\sum_{i}\tilde{n}_{i}(\tilde{n}_{i}-1),~~~~~~~\tilde{n}_{i}=\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{i}\tilde{a}_{i}~~

(2.2)

Here
\langle i,j\rangle
refers to all nearest neighbor pairs,
J
is a hopping term while
U
is an on-site repulsion proportional to
{\cal U}_{0}
. The factor
{1\over N}
is used to render
{\cal H}
to be energy per site.

We consider the homogeneous case and set
\epsilon_{i}=0
at each site. This system has a superfluid-Mott insulator transition first studied by
[
17
]
. The insulating phase is characterized by zero compressibility and has a gap in the excitation spectrum. The Bose-Hubbard model can describe optical lattices and its properties, such as the excitation spectrum, had been experimentally probed
[
18
,
19
]
.

Complexity of Two-Sites Bose Hubbard Model

Hamiltonian of 2-sites Bose Hubbard Model can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{1,2}={J\over 2}(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\tilde{a}_{2}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\tilde{a}_{1})+{U\over 2}\left((\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1})^{2}+(\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2})^{2}-(\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1})-(\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2})\right)

(3.1)

in which we consider the periodic boundary as shown in figure 1.

Figure 1: Two sites Bose-Hubbard Model model. We choose the model with periodic boundary and there has two links. The associated complexity is plotted in figure 2.

Now, we define new operators
a
and
b

\displaystyle a\equiv{1\over\sqrt{2}}(\tilde{a}_{1}+\tilde{a}_{2}),~~b\equiv{1\over\sqrt{2}}(\tilde{a}_{1}-\tilde{a}_{2})

(3.2)

then, by
[\tilde{a}_{i},\tilde{a}_{j}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{i,j}
, we have commutation relations

\displaystyle[a,a^{\dagger}]={1\over 2}[\tilde{a}_{1}+\tilde{a}_{2},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{1}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{2}]={1\over 2}+{1\over 2}=1

(3.3)

\displaystyle[b,b^{\dagger}]={1\over 2}[\tilde{a}_{1}-\tilde{a}_{2},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{1}-\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{2}]={1\over 2}+{1\over 2}=1

(3.4)

\displaystyle[a,b^{\dagger}]={1\over 2}[\tilde{a}_{1}+\tilde{a}_{2},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{1}-\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{2}]={1\over 2}-{1\over 2}=0=[a^{\dagger},b]

(3.5)

\displaystyle[a,b]=[a^{\dagger},b^{\dagger}]=0

(3.6)

Thus
\{a,a^{\dagger}\}
commute with
\{b,b^{\dagger}\}
while each set has standard commutation relation. We can then define the number operators
n_{a}
and
n_{b}

\displaystyle n_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}a,~~~~n_{b}=b^{\dagger}b,~~~~~[n_{a},n_{b}]=0

(3.7)

which define the Fock space

\displaystyle n_{a}|N_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{a}|N_{a}\rangle,~~~~~n_{b}|N_{b}\rangle=N_{b}|N_{b}\rangle

(3.8)

and kinematic term
{\cal H}^{(0)}
becomes

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{(0)}_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J\over 2}(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\tilde{a}_{2}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\tilde{a}_{1})={J\over 2}(a^{\dagger}a-b^{\dagger}b)

(3.9)

To proceed we begin to calculated the interaction terms :

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2}+\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}(a^{\dagger}+b^{\dagger})(a-b)+{1\over 2}(a^{\dagger}-b^{\dagger})(a+b)=a^{\dagger}a-b^{\dagger}b=n_{a}-n_{b}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1}+\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}(a^{\dagger}+b^{\dagger})(a+b)+{1\over 2}(a^{\dagger}-b^{\dagger})(a-b)=a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b=n_{a}+n_{b}

(3.11)

and

\displaystyle(\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({1\over 2}(a^{\dagger}+b^{\dagger})(a+b)\Big)^{2}={1\over 4}\Big((a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b)+(a^{\dagger}b+ab^{\dagger})\Big)^{2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}+2n_{a}n_{b}+(a^{\dagger}bab^{\dagger}+ab^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}b)+\text{irrelevant terms}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}+2n_{a}n_{b}+(n_{a}(1+n_{b})+(1+n_{a})n_{b})+\text{irrelevant terms}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}+4n_{a}n_{b}+n_{a}+n_{b}+\text{irrelevant terms}\Big)

\displaystyle(\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({1\over 2}(a^{\dagger}-b^{\dagger})(a-b)\Big)^{2}={1\over 4}\Big((a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b)-(a^{\dagger}b+ab^{\dagger})\Big)^{2}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}+2n_{a}n_{b}-(a^{\dagger}bab^{\dagger}+ab^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}b)+\text{irrelevant terms}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}+2n_{a}n_{b}-(n_{a}(1+n_{b})+(1+n_{a})n_{b})+\text{\it irrelevant terms}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}-n_{a}-n_{b}+\text{\it irrelevant terms}\Big)

in which the ”
irrelevant terms
” are those with different power of
a
and
a^{\dagger}
and/or with different power of
b
and
b^{\dagger}
.

In this paper we investigate the quantity
\langle N_{a},N_{b}|{\cal H}_{1.2}|N_{a},N_{b}\rangle

Collect all then Hamiltonian of 2-sites Bose-Hubbard model becomes

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J\over 2}(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\tilde{a}_{2}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\tilde{a}_{1})+{U\over 2}\left((\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1})^{2}-(\tilde{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{1})+(\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2})^{2}-(\tilde{a}_{2}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{2})\right)

(3.14)

\displaystyle{J\over 2}(n_{a}-n_{b})+{U\over 2}\Big(n_{a}^{2}+n_{b}^{2}+2n_{a}n_{b}-n_{a}-n_{b}\Big)+\text{\it irrelevant terms}~~~

Complexity of Free Theory

To calculate the complexity we choose the following kinetic term as that of the reference state

\displaystyle K^{\rm(ref)}_{a^{\rm(ref)},\,b^{\rm(ref)}}

Now we see that with the replacement

\displaystyle a^{\rm(ref)}\rightarrow\sqrt{J_{a}\over J_{f}}\,a,~~b^{\rm(ref)}\rightarrow\sqrt{J_{b}\over J_{f}}\,b~~~

\displaystyle K^{\rm(\rm ref)}\rightarrow K^{\rm(tar)}

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{a,b}^{\rm(tar)}+{\cal V}_{a,b}

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{a,b}^{\rm(tar)}

3.2.1
) and final formulas in first-order transformations are

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}R^{(1)}_{a}{(N_{a},\,N_{b})}&=&{J\left(1-{U\over J}\,\left(R^{(0)}_{a}N_{a}+R^{(0)}_{b}N_{b}-1\right)\right)\over J_{f}\left(1-{U\over J_{f}}\,(N_{a}-1)\right)}\\
R^{(1)}_{b}{(N_{a},\,N_{b})}&=&{J\left(1+{U\over J}\,\left(R^{(0)}_{a}N_{a}+R^{(0)}_{b}N_{b}-1\right)\right)\over J_{f}\left(1+{U\over J_{f}}\,(N_{b}-1)\right)}\end{array}\right.~~~

The first-order square distance is

\displaystyle\Big(D^{(1)}_{(N_{a},N_{b})}\Big)^{2}=(N_{a}+1)\left(\ln\Big(\sqrt{R^{(1)}_{a}(N_{a},N_{b})}\Big)\right)^{2}+(N_{b}+1)\left(\ln\Big(\sqrt{R^{(1)}_{b}(N_{a},N_{b})}\Big)\right)^{2}~~

(3.54)

which reduce to the square distance formula of free theory in (
3.33
) for ground (i.e.
N_{a}=N_{b}=0
)

3.2.2
n’th-Order

Extending to higher-order interactions is straightforward. In terms of new variables

\displaystyle g={U\over J},~~~~\gamma={J\over J_{f}}~~~

(3.55)

the recursion relations are

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}R^{(n)}_{a}{(N_{a},\,N_{b})}&=&{\gamma\left(1-{g}\,\left(R^{(n-1)}_{a}N_{a}+R^{(n-1)}_{b}N_{b}\,-1\right)\right)\over\left(1-{g\,\gamma}\,(N_{a}-1)\right)},~~~~~~~~~~R^{(0)}_{a}={\gamma}\\
R^{(n)}_{b}{(N_{a},\,N_{b})}&=&{\gamma\left(1+{g}\,\left(R^{(n-1)}_{a}N_{a}+R^{(n-1)}_{b}N_{b}\,-1\right)\right)\over J_{f}\left(1+{g\,\gamma}\,(N_{b}-1)\right)},~~~~~~~~~~R^{(0)}_{b}={\gamma}\end{array}\right.~~

with initial values
R^{(0)}_{(a,b)}
defined in (
3.32
) with
J_{a}=J_{b}=J
.
For excited states, the
n
-order square distance is

\displaystyle\Big(D^{(n)}_{(N_{a},N_{b})}\Big)^{2}=(N_{a}+1)\left(\ln\Big(\sqrt{R^{(n)}_{a}(N_{a},N_{b})}\Big)\right)^{2}+(N_{b}+1)\left(\ln\Big(\sqrt{R^{(n)}_{b}(N_{a},N_{b})}\Big)\right)^{2}~~

(3.59)

which is the general formula of
n^{\prime}th
-order complexity of two-sites Bose-Hubbard model.

3.3
Complexity and Quantum Phase Transition

We now use above formula to investigate the quantum phase transition in two-sites Bose-Hubbard model. Consider first the ground in which
N_{a}=N_{b}=0
. Then (
3.2.2
) become

\displaystyle R^{(n)}_{a}{(0,\,0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\gamma\left(1+g\right)\over 1+g\,\gamma},~~~~~~~R^{(n)}_{b}{(0,\,0)}={\gamma\left(1-g\right)\over 1-g\,\gamma}~~~~

(3.60)

in which
g={U\over J},\,\gamma={J\over J_{f}}
, defined in (
3.55
), describes the ratio of one-site repulsion and hopping constant. We expect that complexity will be divergent at critical value
g_{c}
, which indicates the quantum phase transition occurs at this point.

To proceed we make following comments about above relation :

1.
R^{(n)}_{a,b}{(0,\,0)}
are the qualities at n’th order of interaction strength g. Thus (
3.60
) gives

\displaystyle R^{(0)}_{a,b}{(0,\,0)}={J\over J_{f}}=\gamma

(3.61)

which are those in (
3.32
). To first order (
3.60
) gives

\displaystyle R^{(1)}_{a}{(0,\,0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma+\gamma(1-\gamma)g,~~~~~~~R^{(1)}_{b}{(0,\,0)}=\gamma-\gamma(1-\gamma)g

(3.62)

and using (
3.60
) we can find any order of
R^{(n)}_{a,b}{(0,\,0)}
by series expansion of
g
.

2. In fact the formulas (
3.60
) are the exact values and we can let
n\to\infty
. Note that being able to obtain the exact formula for ground state complexity is a special property in our method. Now we see that
R^{(n\to\infty)}_{b}{(0,\,0)}
in (
3.60
) becomes zero at critical value
g=g_{c}=1
. This implies that the associated complexity,
(D^{(n\to\infty)}_{(0,0)})^{2}
, is divergent and thus it is the quantum phase transition point.

3. We plot the figure 2 to explicitly see this property.

(D^{(n\to\infty)}_{(0,0)})^{2}
on
``g"
in 2-sites Bose-Hubbard model. The divergence at
g=g_{c}=1
indicates the quantum phase transition at this point. The figure is plotted with
\gamma_{L}=1/2
while
\gamma_{R}=3/2
.

4. The value of
g
we considered is positive. Thus the factor
(1-g\,\gamma)
in formulas (
3.60
) could become zero at
g_{0}=\gamma^{-1}
which depends on the reference state parameter
\gamma
. To avoid
"g_{0}"
become a critical point the following scheme is proposed. For range of
g>g_{c}
we choose
\gamma=\gamma_{R}>1
while for range of
g<g_{c}
we choose
\gamma=\gamma_{L}<1
. Under this prescription the value of
(1-g\,\gamma)
is always positive and it remains only one critical point at
g=g_{c}
.

5. Notice that, in this way, the complexity calculated for
g>g_{c}
is that with
\gamma=\gamma_{R}>1
while the complexity calculated with
g<g_{c}
is that with
\gamma=\gamma_{L}<1
. Therefore, one could compare the magnitude of complexity between the systems of
g>g_{c}
. One could also compare the magnitude of complexity between the systems of
g<g_{c}
. However, it makes no sense to compare the magnitude of complexity between the systems of
g<g_{c}
and of systems
g>g_{c}
since the reference state parameter

is different between them. The figure 2 is plotted with
\gamma_{L}=1/2
while
\gamma_{R}=3/2
.

6. Consider next the excited ground in which
N_{a}=N_{b}=1
. For
n\geq 1
(
3.2.2
) becomes

\displaystyle R^{(n)}_{a}{(1,\,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\left(1+g(1-2\gamma)\right),~~~~~~~R^{(n)}_{b}{(1,\,1)}=\gamma\left(1-g(1-2\gamma)\right),~~~n\geq 1~~~~

(3.63)

As that in ground case, the formulas (
3.63
) are the exact values and we can let
n\to\infty
. We also see that either
R^{(n\to\infty)}_{a}{(1,\,1)}
or
R^{(n\to\infty)}_{a}{(1,\,1)}
becomes zero at critical value
g=g^{(N=1)}_{c}={\pm 1\over 1-2\gamma}
. This implies that the associated complexity,
(D^{(n\to\infty)}_{(1,1)})^{2}
, is divergent at the critical value and thus it is the quantum phase transition point. Note that the critical point is a physical property and it shall not depend on the reference state parameter
\gamma
. However, the calculated value
g^{(N=1)}_{c}
depends on
\gamma
. This feels short of author expectation. The reason may be that the model described in (
2.2
) is that keeps only the lowest vibrational state and could only describe the ground state property. The analysis in above, however, have shown a possible way to calculate excited-state complexity in other many-body models.

4
Complexity of N-Sites Bose-Hubbard Model

Hamiltonian of N-sites closed chain of Bose-Hubbard model defined in (
2.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{N}={J\over 2N}\sum_{j=0}^{N}(\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j+1}\tilde{a}_{j}+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j-1}\tilde{a}_{j})+\frac{U}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\tilde{n}_{j}(\tilde{n}_{j}-1),~~~~~~~\tilde{n}_{j}=\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\tilde{a}_{j}~~

(4.1)

in which we consider the periodic boundary as shown in figure 3.

Figure 3: Six-sites Bose-Hubbard Model model. We choose the model with periodic boundary and there has six links. The associated complexity is plotted in figure 4.

We can generalize method in previous section to define the new operators

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\,exp\Big({2\pi ik\over N}\,j\Big)\,\tilde{a}_{j},~~a^{\dagger}_{k}={1\over\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\,exp\Big({-2\pi ik\over N}\,j\Big)\,\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\,exp\Big({-2\pi ik\over N}\,j\Big)\,a_{j},~~\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{k}={1\over\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\,exp\Big({2\pi ik\over N}\,j\Big)\,a^{\dagger}_{j}

(4.3)

in which we impose a periodic boundary condition
\tilde{a}_{\rm k+N+1}=\tilde{a}_{k}
and
\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{\rm k+N+1}=\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{k}
.

Note that the relative sign between the Fourier series of
a_{k}
and
a^{\dagger}_{k}
is important to have standard commuation relation
[
6
]
. Then, above definitions leads to simple relations

\displaystyle~[\tilde{a}_{i},\tilde{a}_{j}]=[\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{i},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]=0,~~[\tilde{a}_{i},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}~~~\Rightarrow~~~[a_{i},a_{j}]=[a^{\dagger}_{i},a^{\dagger}_{j}]=0,~~[a_{i},a^{\dagger}_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle[a_{j},a^{\dagger}_{k}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{m,\ell}\,e^{{2\pi\,i(m\,j-k\,\ell)\over N}}\,[a_{m},a^{\dagger}_{\ell}]={1\over N}\sum_{m,\ell}\,e^{{2\pi\,i(m\,j-k\,\ell)\over N}}\,\delta_{m,\ell}={1\over N}\sum_{\ell}\,e^{{2\pi\,i\ell(m-k)\over N}}=\delta_{j,k}~~~~~

(4.5)

The general states can then be constructed by creation operators
a_{k}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle|{N}_{0},\,{N}_{1}\cdot\,\cdot\,\cdot\,{N}_{N-1}\rangle=a_{0}^{{N}_{0}}\,a_{1}^{{N}_{1}}\cdot\,\cdot\,\cdot\,a_{N-1}^{{N}_{N-1}}\,|0\rangle

(4.6)

due to the commutation relation between the new operators.

We now express N-sites Hamiltonian
{\cal H}_{N}
in terms of new operators
a_{j}
and
a^{\dagger}_{j}
. Kinematic term is

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j+1}\tilde{a}_{j}+\sum_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j-1}\tilde{a}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{j}\sum_{m,k}e^{{2\pi\,i(-m(j+1)+kj)\over N}}\,a^{\dagger}_{m}a_{k}+{1\over N}\sum_{j}\sum_{m,k}e^{{2\pi\,i(-m(j-1)+kj))\over N}}\,a^{\dagger}_{m}a_{k}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}e^{{-2\pi\,ik\over N}}\,a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}+\sum_{k}e^{{2\pi\,im\over N}}\,a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\,\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}

(4.8)

The kinematic term in
{\cal H}_{N}
can thus be expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{N}={J\over 2N}\left(\sum_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j+1}\tilde{a}_{j}+\sum_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j-1}\tilde{a}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}

(4.9)

which reduces to the formula of N=2 case in previous section. The interaction terms has two parts. One is

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\tilde{n}_{j}=\sum_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\tilde{a}_{j}={1\over N}\sum_{j}\sum_{m,k}e^{{2\pi\,i(-m+k)j\over N}}\,a^{\dagger}_{m}a_{k}=\sum_{m,k}\delta_{m,k}\,a^{\dagger}_{m}a_{k}=\sum_{k}n_{k}

(4.10)

Another one is

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\tilde{n}_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\tilde{a}_{j}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\tilde{a}_{j}={1\over N^{2}}\sum_{j}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta}e^{2\pi j(-\alpha+\beta-\gamma+\eta)\over N}a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}a_{\beta}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\eta}

(4.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta}a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}a_{\beta}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\eta}\,\delta_{\alpha-\beta+\gamma-\eta,0}

(4.12)

The delta function in here means that once
\alpha=\beta
then
\gamma=\eta
and, if
\alpha\neq\beta
then
\gamma\neq\eta
. In the first case we have term

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}={1\over N}\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}n_{\alpha}\,a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}

(4.13)

In the second case, following the discuss in previous section we have to choose the
relevant term
which is

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{\alpha\neq\gamma}a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}a_{\gamma}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{\alpha\neq\gamma}n_{\alpha}\,a_{\gamma}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}={1\over N}\sum_{\alpha\neq\gamma}n_{\alpha}\,(1+a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\gamma})

(4.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({N-1\over N}\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}\right)+{1\over N}\sum_{\alpha\neq\gamma}n_{\alpha}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}

Collect all and the interaction term in
{\cal H}_{N}
becomes

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{U\over N}\Big(\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}n_{\alpha}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}+\sum_{\alpha\neq\gamma}n_{\alpha}a^{\dagger}_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}-\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}\Big)+\text{\it irrelevant terms}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{U\over N}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}n_{k}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}+2\sum_{(k,j)=0}^{N-1}n_{j}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}n_{k}\Big)+\text{\it irrelevant terms}

where
(k,j)
denotes any pair at site
k
and site
j
. When N=2 above formula reduces to that of 2-sites formula in (
3.41
).

4.1
Complexity of Free Theory

Let us first discuss the case of free theory. Following the scheme detailed in previous section the target system kinematic term and reference system kinematic term are

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{\rm(tar)}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,a^{\dagger}_{k}\,a_{k}~~

(4.16)

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{\rm(ref)}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J_{f}\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)a_{k}^{\rm(ref){\dagger}}\,a_{k}^{\rm(ref)}~~

(4.17)

where
J_{f}
is the coupling strength of the reference state.

Now we see that with the replacement

\displaystyle a^{\rm(ref)}_{k}\rightarrow\sqrt{J\over J_{f}}\,a_{k}

(4.18)

one can obtain
K^{\rm(tar)}
from
K^{\rm(ref)}
, i.e.

\displaystyle K^{\rm(\rm ref)}\rightarrow K^{\rm(tar)}

(4.19)

As mentioned before, in the operator approach the gate matrix defined in (
1.1
) is constructed by the transformation from target operator to reference operator in above relation. Thus the complexity,
square distance, for the general excited state
\{{N}_{0},{N}_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot,{N}_{N-1}\}
is easy to be found by the method discussed in the 2-site case. The result is

\displaystyle D^{2}_{\{{N}_{0},{N}_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot,{N}_{N-1}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\,({N}_{k}+1)\left(\ln\Big(\sqrt{R^{(0)}_{k}}\Big)\right)^{2},~~~~~~R^{(0)}_{k}={J\over J_{f}}~~~

(4.20)

which is a simple extension of 2-sites formula in (
3.39
).

4.2
Complexity of Interacting Theory

To include the interaction we first collect the total Hamiltonian

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}J\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}+{U\over N}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}n_{k}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}+2\sum_{(k,j)=0}^{N-1}n_{j}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}\Big)

(4.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(J\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\Big(-1+n_{k}+\sum_{j\neq k}^{N-1}n_{j}\Big)\right)a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}

(4.22)

where
n_{k}=a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}
is the number operator. Then, following the scheme described in previous section we furthermore write the total Hamiltonian of target state and reference state as

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{\rm(tar)}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(J\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\Big(-1+{N}_{k}+\sum_{j\neq k}^{N-1}{N}_{j}\Big)\right)a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}

(4.23)

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{\rm(ref)}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(J_{f}\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\left(-1+{N}_{k}\right)\right)a_{k}^{\rm(ref){\dagger}}\,a_{k}^{\rm(ref)}

(4.24)

where
{N}_{k}
is an integral number defined by
n_{k}|0\rangle=|{N}_{k}\rangle
. The reference operator in above satisfies a desirable property of the reference state that it does not contain any entanglement between operators
a_{k}^{\rm(ref)}
and
a_{j}^{\rm(ref)}
if
k\neq j
, as discussed in
[
6
,
12
]
.

4.2.1
First Order

In the case of zero-order of
U
the Hamiltonian remain only the kinetic term. This is the free case. Now consider the perturbation to the complexity for the N sites Bose Hubbard Model. At the first order of
U
the transformation from target operator to reference operator is chosen as following

\displaystyle\left(J\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\Big(-1+{N}_{k}+\sum_{j\neq k}^{N-1}{N}_{j}\Big)\right)a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\left(J_{f}\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\left(-1+{N}_{k}\right)\right)a_{k}^{\rm(ref){\dagger}}\,a_{k}^{\rm(ref)}~~

Now, we notice that the factors
N_{k},\,N_{j}
are within the coupling term, i.e.
{U}
, and we only need to consider their zero-order transform. Thus, by (
4.20
), we shall put following replacement

\displaystyle N_{k}\rightarrow R^{(0)}_{k}\,N_{k}

(4.26)

into (
4.2.1
) and final formulas in first-order transformation is

\displaystyle R^{(1)}_{k}({\{N_{i}\}})

Therefore the first-order complexity or square distance is

(4.28)

The case of

above formula reduce to the square distance formula of 2-site theory in (

).

4.2.2
n’th-Order

Extending to higher-order interactions is straightforward. The recursion relation is

\displaystyle R^{(n)}_{k}({\{N_{i}\}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{J\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\Big(-1+R^{(n-1)}_{k}\,{N}_{k}+\sum_{j\neq k}^{N-1}{N}_{j}\Big)\over J_{f}\cos\left({2\pi k\over N}\right)\,+{U}\left(-1+{N}_{k}\right)}

with initial values

defined in (

) and the

\displaystyle\Big(D^{(n)}_{\{N_{i}\}}\Big)^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{N_{1}}(N_{k}+1)\,\left(\ln\Big(\sqrt{R^{(n)}_{k}({\{N_{i}\}})}\Big)\right)^{2}~~

(4.31)

These complete our derivations.

5
Concluding Remarks

We adopt the operator approach to compute the complexity of the Bose-Hubbard Model. Our prescription is to consider the value of
\langle N_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot N_{n}|{\cal H}|N_{1}\cdot\cdot\cdot N_{n}\rangle
. In this way, after proper mode expansion of
{\cal H}
, many irrelevant terms become zero and the contributions of complexity from the remained terms could be calculated exactly through a perturbation algorithm. We then obtain the general formula of complexity therein. It is interesting to see that the formula of complexity of Bose-Hubbard model is very similar to that of

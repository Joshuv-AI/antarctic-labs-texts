Large Deviations of Gaussian Neural Networks with ReLU activation
Quirin Vogel 1,2

Abstract
. We prove a large deviation principle for deep neural networks with Gaussian weights and at most linearly growing activation functions, such as ReLU. This generalizes earlier work, in which bounded and continuous activation functions were considered. In practice, linearly growing activation functions such as ReLU are most commonly used. We furthermore simplify previous expressions for the rate function and provide a power-series expansions for the ReLU case.

We mention the recent work

1.2.
Our contributions

Our main result is the LDP for the deep Gaussian neural network

in the case of linearly growing activation functions
\sigma
, such as ReLU, see Theorem
1
. This is important because ReLU is one of the activation functions most commonly used in deep neural networks. Our proof is different to
[
macci2024large
]

\sigma

1.3
) for a definition) is no longer finite for all inputs. The Gärtner--Ellis theorem is no longer applicable, as steepness may fail for some activation functions

Indeed, this is an integrable majorant given

Recall that

as

Equation (

) now follows recalling the continuity of the

map and by expanding

+\left|\sigma\left(q^{\#}N_{\beta}\right)-\sigma\left(q^{\#}_{n}N_{\beta}\right)\right|\left|\sigma\left(q^{\#}_{n}N_{\alpha}\right)\right|\,.

(2.10)

This concludes the proof of the first statement.

Next we prove the second statement. Note that we can assume that

and that hence

as

, as the statement trivially follows from dominated convergence otherwise. Note that for any

there exists

large enough such that for all

,

There exists

such that for all

(2.12)

Hence

\left|\sigma\left(q_{n}^{\#}N_{\alpha}\right)-\sigma\left(q^{\#}N_{\alpha}\right)\right|\leq\varepsilon(1+c_{+})\left|\hskip-1.12389pt\left|N\right|\hskip-1.12389pt\right|+C_{\sigma}\,,

(2.13)

for

By Young’s inequality

∎

Lemma 3.1
.

We have for
a>0
and

This follows from a change of variables

∎

As an immediate consequence, take

(3.2)

where
a
is an implicit argument of

.
Assume now that
q
diagonal with

on the diagonals. For
\underline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
\underline{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and

, write

, i.e., the number of times that

appears in the joint vector

Generalised Temperley-Lieb algebras of type G ⁡ ( r , p , n ) G(r,p,n) Thanks: The authors wish to thank the referee for their very comprehensive reading of the manuscript, and in particular for pointing out some errors, and for numerous suggestions which have significantly improved the exposition.
Gus Lehrer and Mengfan Lyu

Abstract
. In an earlier work, we defined a “generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra” T ​ L r , 1 , n TL_{r,1,n} corresponding to the imprimitive reflection group G ⁡ ( r , 1 , n ) G(r,1,n) as a quotient of the cyclotomic Hecke algebra. In this work we introduce the generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra T ​ L r , p , n TL_{r,p,n} which corresponds to the complex reflection group G ⁡ ( r , p , n ) G(r,p,n) . Our definition identifies T ​ L r , p , n TL_{r,p,n} as the fixed-point subalgebra of T ​ L r , 1 , n TL_{r,1,n} under a certain automorphism σ \sigma . We prove the cellularity of T ​ L r , p , n TL_{r,p,n} by proving that σ \sigma induces a special shift automorphism with respect to the cellular structure of T ​ L r , 1 , n TL_{r,1,n} . We also give a description of the cell modules of T ​ L r , p , n TL_{r,p,n} and their decomposition numbers, and finally we point to how our algebras might be categorified and could lead to a diagrammatic theory.

1.
Introduction

The classical Temperley-Lieb algebra was initially introduced in
[
18
]
in connection with transition matrices in statistical mechanics.
Since then, it has been linked with many diverse areas of mathematics, such as operator algebras, quantum groups, categorification and representation theory.
It can be defined in several ways, including as a quotient of the Hecke algebra of type
A
, or as an associative diagram algebra, i.e. an algebra whose elements are linear combinations of diagrams
with certain composition rules.

In
[
11
]
, we defined a generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra
TL_{r,1,n}
corresponding to the complex reflection group
G(r,1,n)
as a generalisation of the Temperley-Lieb algebras of types
A_{n-1}
and
B_{n}
.
In that work, we give a cellular structure for
TL_{r,1,n}
and find the decomposition matrix of the corresponding cell modules using the technology of KLR algebras.

We generalise that work in this paper. Specifically, we introduce a generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra
TL_{r,p,n}
which corresponds to an arbitrary imprimitive complex reflection group
G(r,p,n)
. It is defined as the fixed-point subalgebra of a suitably specialised generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra
TL_{r,1,n}
under an automorphism
\sigma_{TL}
defined in (
4.4
).

Moreover, we introduce a modified multipartition which is called a 3-dimensional multipartition in Section
5
, and construct a new cellular structure for our specialised
TL_{r,1,n}
in Theorem
7.13
. Inspired by the skew cellularity introduced by Hu, Mathas and Rostam in
[
8
]
, we show that
\sigma_{TL}
induces a shift automorphism with respect to this cellular basis in Theorem
7.25
and deduce the cellularity of
TL_{r,p,n}
in Theorem
7.31
.
Using this cellular structure of
TL_{r,p,n}
, which is inherited from
TL_{r,1,n}
, we determine the decomposition matrix of the cell modules of
TL_{r,p,n}
in Theorem
8.24
.

In §9, we indicate how our algebras might be categorified, and provide some hints for their realisation as diagram algebras.

The cyclotomic Hecke algebra
H(r,p,n)

To define the generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra

The following lemma gives a presentation of the imprimitive reflection group

Lemma 2.1
.

(cf.
[

\displaystyle s_{0}^{d}=s_{1}^{2}=s_{1}^{\prime 2}=s_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\dots=s_{n-1}^{2}=1;

\displaystyle s_{0}s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}

\displaystyle=s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}s_{0};

\displaystyle s_{0}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{0}s_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=s_{1}^{\prime}s_{0}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{0};

\displaystyle s_{0}s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{0};

\displaystyle s_{0}s_{i}=s_{i}s_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ for }i\geq 2;

\displaystyle s_{1}^{\prime}s_{2}s_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=s_{2}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{2};

\displaystyle s_{1}^{\prime}s_{i}=s_{i}s_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}=s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}

\displaystyle\text{ for }i\geq 1;

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}=s_{j}s_{i}

\displaystyle\text{ for }|i-j|\geq 2\text{ and }i,j\geq 1;

\displaystyle\underbrace{s_{0}s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}\dots}_{p+1}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{s_{1}^{\prime}s_{0}s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}s_{1}\dots}_{p+1}.

G(r,p,n)

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{p}E_{1,1}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}E_{k,k},

\displaystyle s_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\zeta E_{1,2}+\zeta^{-1}E_{2,1}+\sum_{k=3}^{n}E_{k,k}

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq i-1}E_{k,k}+E_{i+1,i}+E_{i,i+1}+\sum_{i+2\leq k\leq n}E_{k,k}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n-1

and all other entries equal to zero. Since the complex reflection group

is generated by

the complex reflection group

is a normal subgroup of

The definition of

In this section, we define the generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra

Denote by

By Theorem

,

where
\mathcal{J}_{n}(\Lambda)
is the two-sided ideal
generated by all the
e(\mathbf{i}),\mathbf{i}\in K^{n}
such that:

(4.1)

i_{1}=i_{2},j_{1}=j_{2}-1\text{ if }\mathbf{i}_{1}=(i_{1},j_{1})\text{ and }\mathbf{i}_{2}=(i_{2},j_{2})

(4.2)

(\alpha_{\mathbf{i}_{l}},\Lambda)>0\text{ for }l=1,2,3.

As
\sigma_{0}((i,j))=(i-1,j)

\displaystyle\sigma_{TL}(e((i_{1},j_{1}),(i_{2},j_{2}),\dots,(i_{n},j_{n})))

\displaystyle=e((i_{1}-1,j_{1}),(i_{2}-1,j_{2}),\dots,(i_{n}-1,j_{n}));

\displaystyle\sigma_{TL}(\psi_{i})=\psi_{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1.

We can now state the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\zeta\in R
be a primitive root of unity of order

and let

be the specialisation of the generalised Temperley-Lieb algebra

be the automorphism of

defined above, let

be the fixed point subalgebra under
\sigma_{TL}
and let

. Then we have

(4.6)

TL_{r,1,n}(q,\zeta)^{\sigma_{TL}}\cong\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\Gamma_{e,p})^{\sigma}/\mathcal{J}^{\sigma}.

Thus the fixed subalgebra

is a quotient of
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\Gamma_{e,p})^{\sigma}
, which is isomorphic to the cyclotomic Hecke algebra of type

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
TL_{r,1,n}(q,\zeta)=\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\Gamma_{e,p})/\mathcal{J}_{n}(\Lambda)
.
Let
f:\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\Gamma_{e,p})^{\sigma}/\mathcal{J}^{\sigma}

\to TL_{r,1,n}(q,\zeta)^{\sigma_{TL}}
be the map such that
f(a+\mathcal{J}^{\sigma})=a+\mathcal{J}_{n}(\Lambda)
for all
a\in\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\Lambda}(\Gamma_{e,p})^{\sigma}
. We only need to prove the surjectivity.

For any

. Thus,
a+\mathcal{J}=\sigma(a)+\mathcal{J}=\dots=\sigma^{p-1}(a)+\mathcal{J}

.

6.

In this section, we review some general results on graded skew cellular algebras, which were introduced by Hu, Mathas and Rostam in

In a similar way to cellular algebras, skew cellular algebras are defined in terms of a skew cell datum, which consists of the terms of a cell datum as well as a poset involution.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a finite poset with
\trianglelefteq

(\mathfrak{P},\trianglelefteq)
is a permutation
\sigma

\mathfrak{P}
such that

\lambda\trianglelefteq\mu\text{ if and only if }\sigma(\lambda)\trianglelefteq\sigma(\mu)

for all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{P}
. If
\sigma=\iota
is an involution, we say that
\iota
is a poset involution of
\mathfrak{P}
.

Graded skew cellular algebras are defined as follows.

Definition 6.2
.

(

8
]

R
be an integral domain and

where
(\mathfrak{P},\trianglelefteq)

together with a bijection
\iota_{\lambda}:T(\lambda)\mapsto T(\iota(\lambda))
such that

\iota_{\iota_{\lambda}}\circ\iota_{\lambda}=id_{T(\lambda)}.

The injection

\displaystyle C:\cup_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{P}}T(\lambda)\times T(\lambda)

\displaystyle\mapsto A

\displaystyle(S,T)

Let

\displaystyle\psi_{d([s])}^{*}(\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\equiv\mu}e_{\mu^{\prime}})\psi_{d([t])}\psi_{d([u])}^{*}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\equiv\lambda}e_{\lambda^{\prime}})\psi_{d([v])}

\displaystyle\psi_{d([s])}^{*}\psi_{d([t])}(\sum_{t^{\prime}\equiv t}e(t^{\prime}))(\sum_{u^{\prime}\equiv u}e(u^{\prime}))\psi_{d([u])}^{*}\psi_{d([v])}.

9.
Further developments: speculations on potential categorification and diagrammatics

The original Temperley-Lieb algebras
TL_{n}(q)
and the Temperley-Lieb algebras of “type
B
”,
TL_{n}(Q,q)
, fit into a category, where they are the algebras of endomorphisms of the
objects. These categories may be shown to be equivalent to certain categories of representations of quantum
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
(see
[
9
]
). We briefly discuss here how
our theory of generalised Temperley-Lieb algebras might be further developed in this direction.

Similarly to the categorification of
TLB_{n}(Q,q)
in
[
6
]
, we may define a Temperley-Lieb category of type

,
written
\mathbb{TL}_{r,p}
, where the objects are non-negative integers
\mathbb{N}
. Instead of the marked diagrams, we use linear combinations of standard tableau pairs as morphisms.

Let
m,n
be a pair of positive integers of the same parity. Using the notation above Definition
5.1
, denote by
\lambda_{(i_{1},l_{1}),(i_{2},l_{2})}^{[a]}
a 3D-multipartition of
m
and by
\mu_{(i_{1},l_{1}),(i_{2},l_{2})}^{[a]}
a 3D-multipartition of
n
. Let
t
and
s
be any standard tableaux of shape
\lambda
and
\mu
, respectively. Then the pair
(t,s):m\mapsto n
forms a morphism from
m
to
n
in
\mathbb{TL}_{r,p}
. Composition of two morphisms
(t,s):m\mapsto n
and
(u,v):n\mapsto l
could be defined as
(t,v):m\mapsto l
multiplied by the bilinear form
\phi_{\mu}(s,u)
in
TL_{r,p,n}
.

It is an open question whether this generalised Temperley-Lieb category can be realised as a category of some representations, or indeed whether there are interesting
functors from
\mathbb{TL}_{r,p}
to categories of representations.

The categorification above generalises that of

in

Efficiency at maximum power of a Carnot quantum information engine
Paul Fadler, Alexander Friedenberger, Eric Lutz

Abstract
Optimizing the performance of thermal machines is an essential task of thermodynamics. We here consider the optimization of information engines that convert information about the state of a system into work. We concretely introduce a generalized finite-time Carnot cycle for a quantum information engine and optimize its power output in the regime of low dissipation. We derive a general formula for its efficiency at maximum power valid for arbitrary working media. We further investigate the optimal performance of a qubit information engine subjected to weak energy measurements.

We here introduce a generalized Carnot cycle for a quantum information engine by replacing the cold heat bath of a finite-time quantum Carnot heat engine by an information reservoir. We optimize its power output and derive a general formula for the efficiency at maximum power for arbitrary working media within the framework of nonequilibrium thermodynamics in the weak dissipation regime. We obtain a Curzon-Ahlborn-like expression where the optimal cold coupling time is replaced by a new dissipation time that characterizes irreversible losses. We further illustrate our findings with the example of a qubit information engine, and obtain a microscopic expression of its efficiency at maximum power.

Reversible information engine cycle.
The reversible Carnot cycle describes the most efficient heat engine, and is thus of fundamental importance. It consists of two adiabatic and of two isothermal (expansion and compression) branches
cen01
. Its realization requires two heat baths: a hot bath from which heat is absorbed during the hot isotherm and a cold bath which takes on heat during the cold isotherm. Finite-time quantum Carnot cycles have been theoretically studied in Refs.
qua07
;
abe11
;
dan20
;
abi20
;
den21
. The first experimental implementation of a classical finite-time Carnot engine has been presented in Ref.
mar15
. We here construct a finite-time generalization of the Carnot cycle for a quantum information engine by substituting the cold heat bath (and the corresponding isotherm) by an information bath that involves measurement and subsequent outcome-dependent feedback (Fig. 1).

An important feature of this information cycle is that it is thermodynamically reversible for infinitely long cycle durations, like its thermal conterpart. In other words, each branch, including measurement and feedback, does not dissipate any irreversible entropy in that limit. We concretely impose the following three conditions on the engine cycle: (a) both measurement and feedback control are reversible, (b) the cycle is independent of the measurement outcome, meaning that measurement and feedback operation always lead to the same state, irrespective of the measurement result, and (c) the state
\rho_{\text{after}}
after measurement and feedback is a thermal state at temperature
T_{\text{after}}
with the same Hamiltonian
H
as that of the state
\rho_{\text{before}}
before the measurement.

We measure the state of the working medium of the information engine with a generalized measurement described by a set of positive operators
\{M_{i}\}
that satisfy
\sum_{i}M_{i}^{\dagger}M_{i}=I

The state after a measurement is
\rho_{i}=M_{i}\rho_{\text{before}}M_{i}^{\dagger}/p_{i}
with probability
p_{i}=\text{Tr}[M_{i}\rho_{\text{before}}M_{i}^{\dagger}]

S_{i}=-k\text{ Tr}[\rho_{i}\ln\rho_{i}

E_{i}=\text{Tr}[\rho_{i}H]
the energy of that state (

Such a generalized measurement usually leads to a classical mixtures of states, implying that entropy is irreversibly produced during the process,
S(\rho_{\text{{meas}}})>S(\rho_{\text{before}})

\rho_{\text{{meas}}}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}
is the density operator averaged over all the measurement outcomes, unless
[M_{i},\rho_{\text{before}}]=0

Since the latter state is diagonal in the energy basis after the adiabatic compression branch,

describe a nonprojective measurement of the energy of the working fluid

The average entropy change provided by the measurement is

Conclusions.

We have proposed a generalized finite-time Carnot cycle for a quantum information engine. Like the standard Carnot cycle for heat engines, it is thermodynamically reversible for large cycle durations. This cycle thus describes the most efficient quantum information engine with unit information efficiency. We have optimized its power output in the regime of low dissipation and derived a Curzon-Ahlborn-like formula for its efficiency at maximum power. This generic expression only depends on the optimal time of the hot isotherm and a new dissipation time associated with irreversible entropy production. The efficiency at maximum power was further shown to obey the general inequality
1/2<\eta^{*}<1
, independent of the microscopic details of the engine. Our results provide a theoretical basis for the optimization of information engines. We hence expect them to be important for the study of optimal quantum machines in finite-time information thermodynamics.

Appendix A
Alternative thermodynamic analysis

Instead of performing the thermodynamic analysis of the finite-time quantum information engine as done in the main text by distinguishing, on the one hand, the measurement and feedback part (step (1-2) in Fig. 1), and, on the other hand, the engine cycle seen from the standpoint of the working medium (steps (1-4) in Fig. 1), we here present an alternative, but, equivalent, description that separates the measurement in point 1 in Fig. 1 and the remaining cycle which is now considered to be operated by a general controller. The latter cycle includes the feedback part that is conditioned on the measurement outcome, as well as the two adiabats and the isotherm (steps (1-4) in Fig. 1). This derivation generalizes the one discussed in Ref.
bra15
for a different quantum information cycle through the usage of incomplete measurements.

We begin by evaluating the respective changes of energy and entropy associated with (i) the measurement and with (ii) the remaining cycle. We have

\displaystyle\Delta E^{\mathrm{meas}}(m)

\displaystyle=E[\rho_{m}]-E[\rho_{1}]

(13)

\displaystyle\Delta S^{\mathrm{meas}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m)

\displaystyle=S_{\mathrm{sys}}[\rho_{m}]-S_{\mathrm{sys}}[\rho_{1}]

(14)

for the measurement with outcome
m
, and

\displaystyle\Delta E^{\mathrm{cyc}}(m)

\displaystyle=E[\rho_{1}]-E[\rho_{m}]

(15)

\displaystyle\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m)

\displaystyle=S_{\mathrm{sys}}[\rho_{1}]-S_{\mathrm{sys}}[\rho_{m}]

(16)

for the cycle implemented by the controller.
The total entropy production during the cycle is the sum of the entropy change of the system and of the bath

\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{cyc}}(m)=\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m)+\Delta S_{\mathrm{bath}}\geq 0{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}

(17)

since the total entropy production is non-negative. We accordingly obtain

Q_{\text{h}}=Q(m)=-T{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta S_{\mathrm{bath}}}\leq T\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m).

(18)

The first law applied to the complete control operation then reads

\displaystyle\Delta E^{\mathrm{cyc}}(m)

\displaystyle=Q(m)-W(m)=Q_{\text{h}}-W,

(19)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle W(m)

\displaystyle\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}T\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m)}-\Delta E^{\mathrm{cyc}}(m).

(20)

Since the measurement is reversible, that is,
S[\rho_{m}]=S[\rho_{2}]
for all
m
,
averaging
\Delta E^{\mathrm{cyc}}(m)
yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta E^{\mathrm{cyc}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}p_{m}\left(E[\rho_{1}]-E[\rho_{m}]\right)=E[\rho_{1}]-\sum_{m}p_{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(H\rho_{m}\right)=E[\rho_{1}]-\sum_{m}p_{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(H\frac{M_{m}\rho_{1}M_{m}^{\dagger}}{p_{m}}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=E[\rho_{1}]-\mathrm{Tr}\left(H\rho_{1}\sum_{m}M_{m}^{\dagger}M_{m}\right)=E[\rho_{1}]-\mathrm{Tr}\left(H\rho_{1}\right)=0,

(22)

where we have used
[H,M_{m}]=0
and
\sum_{m}M_{m}^{\dagger}M_{m}=1
. Similarly, we have
\left\langle\Delta E^{\mathrm{meas}}\right\rangle=0
.

Since
\Delta S^{\mathrm{meas}}_{\mathrm{sys}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}=S_{\mathrm{sys}}[\rho_{2}]-S_{\mathrm{sys}}[\rho_{1}]
, we eventually arrive at

\left\langle W\right\rangle\leq-T\Delta S^{\mathrm{meas}}_{\mathrm{sys}}=-T(S[\rho_{2}]-S[\rho_{1}]).

(23)

In the low dissipation limit, we may be further write the entropy production in the form

\displaystyle\frac{\Sigma}{\tau_{h}}

\displaystyle=\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m)+\Delta S_{\mathrm{bath}}(m),

(24)

from which we find

\displaystyle Q_{\text{h}}=-Q(m)

\displaystyle=-T\Delta S_{\mathrm{bath}}(m)=T\left(\Delta S^{\mathrm{cyc}}_{\mathrm{sys}}(m)-\frac{\Sigma}{\tau_{h}}\right)

(25)

Combining everything, we finally obtain (denoting
\Delta S^{\mathrm{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}cyc}}}_{\mathrm{sys}}=\Delta S
, as in the main text)

\left\langle W\right\rangle=T\left(\Delta S-\frac{\Sigma}{\tau_{h}}\right).

(26)

This is equation (1) of the main text.

Appendix B
Measurement operators for the qubit information engine

We here explicitly derive the Kraus operators
M_{i}
for the generalized quantum measurement implemented in the two-level information engine. They have to fulfill the condition

\rho_{\mathrm{after}}=\Phi_{i}[\rho_{i}],

(27)

where the state
\rho_{\text{after}}
(after measurement and feedback) is a thermal state at effective temperature
T_{\text{after}}
and
\rho_{i}=M_{i}\rho_{\mathrm{before}}M_{i}^{{\dagger}}/p_{i}
is the state of the system after a measurement with outcome
i=(0,1)
. For
i=0
,
\Phi_{0}={I}
is the identity, whereas for
i=1
,
\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{\mathrm{flip}}
is the quantum bit flip channel.

Let us parametrize the thermal states before and after the measurement as

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{before}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\ket{1}\bra{1}+(1-\alpha)\ket{0}\bra{0},

(28)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{after}}

\displaystyle=\beta\ket{1}\bra{1}+(1-\beta)\ket{0}\bra{0}.

(29)

Since the operators
M_{i}
commute with thermal states, we can also parametrize them by their diagonal entries as

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle=x\ket{1}\bra{1}+y\ket{0}\bra{0},

(30)

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=u\ket{1}\bra{1}+v\ket{0}\bra{0}.

(31)

Neglecting arbitrary phases by choosing
(x,y,u,v)\in\mathds{R}^{+}
, which can always be done by properly adjusting the adiabatic protocol, we can eliminate the parameters
u
and
v
by using

\displaystyle\mathds{1}=M_{0}^{{\dagger}}M_{0}+M_{1}^{{\dagger}}M_{1}=\left(x^{*}x+u^{*}u\right)

\displaystyle\ket{1}\bra{1}+\left(y^{*}y+v^{*}v\right)\ket{0}\bra{0}.

(32)

We then obtain

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad v=\sqrt{1-y^{2}}.

(33)

Looking at measurement outcome
0
, we further have

\displaystyle\frac{M_{0}\rho_{\mathrm{before}}M_{0}^{{\dagger}}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left(M_{0}\rho_{\mathrm{before}}M_{0}^{{\dagger}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{after}},

(34)

or, explicitly

\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}\alpha}{x^{2}\alpha+y^{2}(1-\alpha)}\ket{1}\bra{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{y^{2}(1-\alpha)}{x^{2}\alpha+y^{2}(1-\alpha)}\ket{0}\bra{0}=\beta\ket{1}\bra{1}+(1-\beta)\ket{0}\bra{0}.

(35)

Setting the populations of the excited state to be equal, we find

\frac{x^{2}\alpha}{x^{2}\alpha+y^{2}(1-\alpha)}=\beta.

(36)

The equality of the populations of the ground state is automatically fulfilled owing to the unit trace.
On the other hand, looking at measurement outcome
1
, we have

\frac{M_{1}\rho_{\mathrm{before}}M_{1}^{{\dagger}}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left(M_{1}\rho_{\mathrm{before}}M_{1}^{{\dagger}}\right)}=\Phi_{\mathrm{flip}}[\rho_{\mathrm{after}}],

(37)

which leads to the equation

\frac{(1-x^{2})\alpha}{(1-x^{2})\alpha+(1-y^{2})(1-\alpha)}=1-\beta.

(38)

Solving the set of Eqs. (
36
) and (
38
), we obtain

\displaystyle x=\sqrt{\frac{\beta-\beta^{2}-\alpha\beta}{\alpha-2\alpha\beta}}\quad\text{and}\quad y=\sqrt{\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}\frac{1-\beta-\alpha}{1-2\beta}},

(39)

as well as

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\beta\frac{1-\beta-\alpha}{\alpha-2\alpha\beta}}\quad\text{and}\quad v=\sqrt{1-\frac{1-\beta}{1-\alpha}\frac{1-\beta-\alpha}{1-2\beta}}.

(40)

Writing further
\alpha=\frac{1}{1+e^{a}}
and
\beta=\frac{1}{1+e^{b}}
, with
a=\beta_{\text{b}}{\omega_{\text{fb}}}
and
b=\beta_{\text{a}}{\omega_{\text{fb}}}
, where
\beta_{\text{a}}=\beta_{\text{after}}
is the inverse temperature of state
\rho_{\text{after}}
and
\beta_{\text{b}}=\beta_{\text{before}}
is the inverse temperature of state
\rho_{\text{before}}
, we finally arrive at

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{e^{a+b}-1}{e^{2b}-1}},\quad\quad y=\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-a-b}}{1-e^{-2b}}},\quad\quad u=\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{a-b}}{1-e^{-2b}}}\quad\text{and}\quad v=\sqrt{\frac{e^{-a+b}+1}{e^{2b}-1}}.

(41)

Appendix C
Hot isotherm time for the qubit information engine

We next derive Eq. (10) of the main text for the duration of the hot isotherm following Refs.
gev92
;
lin03
. We begin with Eq. (9) of the main text for the average polarization
{\cal P}_{t}
in the Heisenberg picture

\dot{\left\langle{\cal P}_{t}\right\rangle}=-ae^{q\beta\omega_{t}}[2(1+e^{\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}})\left\langle{\cal P}_{t}\right\rangle+(e^{\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}}-1)].

(42)

Applying the chain rule for derivatives, we have

\dot{\left\langle{\cal P}_{t}\right\rangle}=\frac{d\left\langle{\cal P}_{t}(\omega_{t})\right\rangle}{d\omega_{t}}\frac{d\omega_{t}}{dt}.

(43)

Since the effective temperature along the isothermal branch is constant, we obtain from
\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega_{t})\right\rangle=-\tanh\left(\beta^{\prime}\omega_{t}/2\right)/2

\displaystyle\frac{d\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{t}\right\rangle}{d\omega_{t}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta^{{}^{\prime}}}{2[\cosh(\beta^{{}^{\prime}}\omega_{t})+1]}.

(44)

Combining both equations, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{t}\right\rangle}{d\omega_{t}}\frac{d\omega_{t}}{dt}=

\displaystyle-ae^{q\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}}[-(1+e^{\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}})\tanh\left(\beta^{{}^{\prime}}\omega_{t}/2\right)+e^{\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}}-1].

(45)

or, equivalently, solving for
dt

\displaystyle dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{(d\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{t}\right\rangle/d\omega_{t})}{ae^{q\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}}[-(1+e^{\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}})\tanh\left(\beta^{{}^{\prime}}\omega_{t}/2\right)+(e^{\beta_{\text{h}}\omega_{t}}-1)]}d\omega_{t},

(46)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a}\int_{x_{0}}^{x(t)}\left[e^{q\epsilon x}(e^{\epsilon x}-e^{x})(1+e^{-x})\right]^{-1}dx,

(47)

where we have introduced the new variable
x=\beta^{\prime}\omega_{t}
and defined
\epsilon=\beta_{\text{h}}/\beta^{\prime}
.
Using the expansion
e^{x}\simeq 1+x
in the high-temperature limit, we finally arrive at

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{t}}\frac{dx}{(1+q\epsilon x)(1+\epsilon x-(1+x))(1+1-x)}=\frac{\ln(\omega_{3}/\omega_{4})}{4a(1-\beta_{\mathrm{h}}/\beta^{{}^{\prime}})}.

(48)

Appendix D
Entropy production for the qubit information engine

We finally derive an expression for the parameter
\Sigma
which determines the nonequilibrium entropy production. We consider an arbitrary
N
-dimensional working fluid with Hamilton operator
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\omega_{t}\mathcal{P}=\omega_{t}\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\ket{n}\bra{n}
. We choose the global energy offset of
\mathcal{P}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{2}=2\chi.

(49)

In the high temperature limit (
\beta\lambda_{n}\omega_{t}\ll 1
), we can express the thermal state as

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\beta\omega_{t}\mathcal{P}}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left[e^{-\beta\omega_{t}\mathcal{P}}\right]}\simeq\frac{(1-\beta\omega_{t}\lambda_{N}+(\beta\omega_{t}\lambda_{N})^{2}/2)\ket{N}\bra{N}}{Z},

(50)

with the partition function

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{n}\left[1-\beta\omega_{t}\lambda_{n}+(\beta\omega_{t}\lambda_{n})^{2}/2\right]=N-\beta\omega_{t}\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}+\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{2}/2=N+\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\chi.

(51)

The occupation probabilities are therefore

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=\bra{n}\rho\ket{n}\simeq\frac{1-\beta\omega_{t}\lambda_{n}+\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}/2}{N+\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\chi}=\frac{1}{N}-\frac{\beta\omega_{t}\lambda_{n}}{N}+\frac{\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}{2N}-\frac{\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\chi}{N^{2}}.

(52)

The entropy can accordingly be written as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=-\text{Tr}[\rho\ln\rho]=-\sum_{n}P_{n}\ln P_{n}\simeq\ln N-\frac{\beta^{2}\omega_{t}^{2}\chi}{N}.

(53)

The entropy changes for an isochoric process from
\beta_{1}
to
\beta_{2}
at frequency
\omega_{t}
and an isothermal transformation from
\beta_{1}
to
\beta_{2}
at inverse temperature
\beta
thus read

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\mathrm{isochoric}}=\frac{\omega_{t}^{2}\chi}{N}\left(\beta^{2}_{2}-\beta^{2}_{1}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta S_{\mathrm{isothermal}}=\frac{\beta^{2}\chi}{N}\left(\omega^{2}_{2}-\omega^{2}_{1}\right).

(54)

The dissipation constant
\Sigma
for a two-level system (with
N=2
and
\chi=1/4
) hence follows as

\displaystyle{\Sigma}

\displaystyle={\Delta S}\,\frac{\ln(\omega_{2}/\omega_{1})}{4a}={\Delta S_{\text{isothermal}}}\,\frac{\ln(\omega_{2}/\omega_{1})}{4a}=\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\left(\omega^{2}_{2}-\omega^{2}_{1}\right)\frac{\log(\omega_{2}/\omega_{1})}{4a}.

(55)

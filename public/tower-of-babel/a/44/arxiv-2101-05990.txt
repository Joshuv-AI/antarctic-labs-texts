Quenched free energy from spacetime D-branes
Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
We propose a useful integral representation of the quenched free energy which is applicable to any random systems. Our formula involves the generating function of multi-boundary correlators, which can be interpreted on the bulk gravity side as spacetime D-branes introduced by Marolf and Maxfield in [arXiv:2002.08950]. As an example, we apply our formalism to the Airy limit of the random matrix model and compute its quenched free energy under certain approximations of the generating function of correlators. It turns out that the resulting quenched free energy is a monotonically decreasing function of the temperature, as expected.

More importantly, as emphasized in

In this paper, we propose a useful integral representation of the quenched free energy

\displaystyle\langle\log Z\rangle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\Bigl[\big\langle e^{-Zx}\big\rangle-e^{-\langle Z\rangle x}\Bigr],

2
General formula for the quenched free energy

2.1
Direct derivation of (
4
)

Let us prove our general formula (
4
) for the quenched free energy.
(
4
) is based on the following simple relation

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}(e^{-ax}-e^{-bx})=\log\frac{a}{b}.

(7)

Note that, if we take the first term of (
7
)
only, then the integral is
logarithmically divergent

\displaystyle-\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}e^{-ax}=\log(a\varepsilon)+\gamma+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),

(8)

where
\varepsilon
is a small regularization parameter and
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
However, if we compute the difference of two such integrals as in (
7
)
the divergence from
x=0
is canceled and we get the finite result (
7
).
Setting
a=Z
and
b=\langle Z\rangle
in (
7
) we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\Big(e^{-Zx}-e^{-\langle Z\rangle x}\Big)=\log\frac{Z}{\langle Z\rangle}.

(9)

Finally, taking the expectation value of the both sides of
(
9
) and using the relation
\langle 1\rangle=1
, we find
our desired result (
4
).
We emphasize that our formula (
4
)
is an exact relation and we did not use any approximation
in deriving (
4
).

Let us consider an alternative derivation of (
4
)
by expanding the quenched free energy around the annealed free energy.

\displaystyle\langle\log Z\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\log\big(\langle Z\rangle+Z-\langle Z\rangle\big)\big\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle=\log\langle Z\rangle+\Biggl\langle\log\Biggl(1+\frac{Z-\langle Z\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle}\Biggr)\Biggr\rangle

\displaystyle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\frac{\big\langle\big(Z-\langle Z\rangle\big)^{k}\big\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle^{k}}.

Then using the relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}e^{-y}y^{k}=(k-1)!,\qquad(k\geq 1),

(11)

we rewrite the summation in (
10
) as

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\frac{\big\langle\big(Z-\langle Z\rangle\big)^{k}\big\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle^{k}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}e^{-y}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-y)^{k}}{k!}\frac{\big\langle\big(Z-\langle Z\rangle\big)^{k}\big\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle^{k}}

(12)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}e^{-y}\left[\Biggl\langle\exp\Biggl(-y\frac{Z-\langle Z\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle}\Biggr)\Biggr\rangle-1\right]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}\left[\Biggl\langle\exp\Biggl(-y\frac{Z}{\langle Z\rangle}\Biggr)\Biggr\rangle-e^{-y}\right]

Finally, by rescaling the integration variable
y=\langle Z\rangle x
we arrive at
our formula (
4
).

2.2
Relation to the replica method

Let us consider a derivation of our formula (
4
)
from the replica method (
3
).
In the replica method, we need to analytically continue
the
n
-point function
\langle Z^{n}\rangle
from a positive integer
n
to
n=0
.
As pointed out in
Engelhardt:2020qpv
,
the analytic continuation of
\langle Z^{n}\rangle
is not unique
and there might be some ambiguity
in the computation of the right hand side of (
3
).
It turns out that there is a natural definition of
the analytic continuation of
\langle Z^{n}\rangle
which leads to our formula
(
4
).

In general, the correlator
\langle Z^{n}\rangle
is expanded as a combination of
the connected correlators
\langle Z^{k}\rangle_{c}
.
If we order this expansion by the number of connected components,
the first term is the totally disconnected part
\langle Z\rangle^{n}
.
The next term is
\langle Z\rangle^{n-2}\langle Z^{2}\rangle_{c}

and the coefficient of this term is
\binom{n}{2}=\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)

which is the number of ways to choose two boundaries out of
n
boundaries.
Then
\langle Z^{n}\rangle
is expanded as

\displaystyle\langle Z^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Z\rangle^{n}+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)\langle Z\rangle^{n-2}\langle Z^{2}\rangle_{c}+\cdots

(13)

\displaystyle=\langle Z\rangle^{n}\left[1+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)\frac{\langle Z^{2}\rangle_{c}}{\langle Z\rangle^{2}}+\cdots\right].

Taking the
n\to 0
limit in (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\log Z\rangle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle Z^{2}\rangle_{c}}{\langle Z\rangle^{2}}+\cdots.

(14)

In this computation, up to the overall factor

\langle Z\rangle^{n}
the
n
-dependence in (
13
)
is essentially a polynomial in
n

and hence the analytic continuation can be defined unambiguously.

To see the general structure, it is convenient to define

\displaystyle w_{n}=\frac{\langle Z^{n}\rangle_{c}}{\langle Z\rangle^{n}}.

(15)

Then the expansion (
13
)
is generalized as (see e.g.
Okuyama:2019xvg
)

\displaystyle\langle Z^{n}\rangle=\langle Z\rangle^{n}\sum_{j_{i}\geq 0~(i=2,3,\cdots)}\frac{n!}{(n-\sum_{l\geq 2}lj_{l})!}\prod_{k\geq 2}\frac{1}{j_{k}!}\left(\frac{w_{k}}{k!}\right)^{j_{k}}.

(16)

Now using the relation

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{n}\frac{n!}{(n-m)!}=(-1)^{m-1}(m-1)!,

(17)

the quenched free energy becomes

\displaystyle\langle\log Z\rangle=\log\langle Z\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{i}\geq 0~(i=2,3\cdots)\\
(j_{2},j_{3},\cdots)\neq(0,0,\cdots)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\sum_{l\geq 2}lj_{l}-1}\Bigl(\sum_{l\geq 2}lj_{l}-1\Bigr)!\prod_{k\geq 2}\frac{1}{j_{k}!}\left(\frac{w_{k}}{k!}\right)^{j_{k}}.

(18)

Again using the trick (
11
) this is rewritten as

\displaystyle\langle\log Z\rangle

\displaystyle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{i}\geq 0~(i=2,3,\cdots)\\
(j_{2},j_{3},\cdots)\neq(0,0,\cdots)\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}e^{-y}(-y)^{\sum_{l\geq 2}lj_{l}}\prod_{k\geq 2}\frac{1}{j_{k}!}\left(\frac{w_{k}}{k!}\right)^{j_{k}}

(19)

\displaystyle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y}e^{-y}\left[\exp\Biggl(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-y)^{k}\frac{w_{k}}{k!}\Biggr)-1\right]

\displaystyle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\Biggl[\exp\Biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}\langle Z^{k}\rangle_{c}\Biggr)-e^{-\langle Z\rangle x}\Biggr],

where in the last step we changed the integration variable
y=\langle Z\rangle x
.
One can see that the first term of the integrand in (
19
)
is
e^{-\mathcal{Z}(x)}
defined in (
5
), and hence
(
19
) agrees with our formula
(
6
).

To summarize, we have derived our formula (
4
)
from the replica method by using the prescription
(
17
) for the analytic continuation.
For instance, the example in (
14
) of this computation
corresponds to the
m=2
case of (
17
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{n}\frac{n!}{(n-2)!}=\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{n}n(n-1)=-1.

(20)

At fixed
m
, the factor
\frac{n!}{(n-m)!}
in (
17
)
is a polynomial in
n

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(n-m)!}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1),

(21)

and hence we believe that our prescription (
17
)
of the analytic continuation is a natural choice.

2.3
Possible interpretation of (
4
)

Our formula (
4
)
is very suggestive for a possible interpretation of the bulk spacetime
picture of the quenched free energy.
In
Marolf:2020xie
, it is discussed that the operator

e^{-Zx}
corresponds to the so-called “spacetime D-brane” (SD-brane).
Our formula (
4
) suggests that the bulk spacetime picture of
quenched free energy involves a superposition of SD-branes.
As mentioned in section
1
,
in order to obtain the quenched free energy,
we have to integrate over the parameter
x
in the SD-brane operator

e^{-Zx}
.

Recently, in
Penington:2019kki
;
Almheiri:2019qdq

the von Neumann entropy of Hawking radiation is
computed by the replica method and it is argued that the so-called replica wormholes
are essential for recovering the unitary Page curve.
In the saddle point approximation of gravitational path integral, the dominant contribution comes from either
maximally connected or maximally disconnected spacetimes
and these contributions exchange dominance around the Page time.
On the other hand, in our formula (
4
)
no such saddle point approximation is made.
In our derivation of (
4
) from the replica method,
all terms in the decomposition of
\langle Z^{n}\rangle

in (
16
) contribute to
the quenched free energy and they add up to the generating function

\langle e^{-Zx}\rangle
. The first term
\log\langle Z\rangle
in (
4
)
comes from the maximally disconnected part
\langle Z\rangle^{n}
, while
the remaining term in (
4
) is a contribution of SD-brane

e^{-Zx}

which does not have a simple interpretation as the contribution
of maximally connected part.
As we will see in section
3.3
,
the insertion of the operator
e^{-Zx}
can be interpreted as a shift of the background couplings, or
a deformation of the matrix model potential.

It is argued in
Engelhardt:2020qpv
that
the contribution of the so-called replica wormholes should be included in
the replica computation of the quenched free energy.
The second term of (
4
) represents the deviation from
the annealed free energy, which might be interpreted as the
contribution of replica wormholes. It is interesting that
even after taking the limit
n\to 0
(
3
),
infinite number of boundaries contributes to
\langle e^{-Zx}\rangle=e^{-\mathcal{Z}(x)}
in
(
5
). In other words,
we need to include a coherent superposition
\mathcal{Z}(x)
of
the infinite number of boundaries for the computation of quenched free energy.

In general,
the large
N
limit serves as a semi-classical approximation in the random matrix model.
One might think that we can see an exchange of dominance between disconnected and
connected contributions in the large
N
limit.
However, if we naively take the large
N
limit at fixed temperature,
the disconnected correlator always dominates
over the connected
correlators. Around the temperature
T\sim N^{-2/3}
, the disconnected
and connected contributions become comparable
Okuyama:2019xvg
, but this temperature is pushed to zero as
N\to\infty
which implies that the disconnected part
is always dominant at finite temperature.
As discussed in
Engelhardt:2020qpv
;
Johnson:2020mwi
,
to focus on this temperature scale
T\sim N^{-2/3}

one can take a certain double scaling limit, called
the Airy limit of random matrix model, which we will consider in the next section.

3
Quenched free energy in the Airy limit

In this section we apply our general formula (
4
)
to the Airy limit of Gaussian random matrix model.
In this case, the connected part of the
n
-point correlator

\langle Z^{n}\rangle_{c}
is known in the integral representation

.
For instance,
\langle Z^{n}\rangle_{c}

okounkov2002generating

\displaystyle\langle Z(\beta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\frac{\xi}{12}}}{\sqrt{4\pi\xi}},

(22)

\displaystyle\langle Z(\beta)^{2}\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle Z(2\beta)\rangle\text{Erf}\Biggl(\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{2}}\Biggr),

\displaystyle\langle Z(\beta)^{3}\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle Z(3\beta)\rangle\Biggl[1-12T\Bigl(\sqrt{3\xi},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigr)\Biggr],

\displaystyle T(z,a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{a}dt\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}(1+t^{2})}}{1+t^{2}}.

The parameter

in (

) is given by

\displaystyle\xi=\hbar^{2}\beta^{3},

where

In the Airy limit,

In principle one can compute

.

where

we find that the quenched free energy

However, the genus expansion is not a good approximation

3.1
Quenched free energy at genus-zero

Let us consider the genus-zero part of the expansion of

The connected correlator

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(x)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{n}}{n!}\big(\widehat{Z}\big)^{n}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}-e^{-\widehat{Z}x}\mathcal{F}.

4
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper we have studied the quenched free energy
in an ensemble average of random systems.
We found the general formula (
4
) for the quenched free energy
and argued that it can be derived by the replica method
(
3
) under a certain prescription
of the analytic continuation of
\langle Z^{n}\rangle
.
We also discussed that the bulk gravity picture of our formula (
4
)
involves the so-called spacetime D-branes
e^{-Zx}

introduced in
Marolf:2020xie
.
Then we have applied our general formula (
4
) to the Airy
limit of the Gaussian matrix model.
We found that the genus-zero approximation of the generating function

\mathcal{Z}(x)
leads to a monotonic behavior of the quenched free energy.
The inclusion of higher genus corrections to
\mathcal{Z}(x)
leads to
a diverging free energy at low temperature, but this is not a problem
of our formula (
4
);
it simply means that the genus expansion is not a good approximation
at low temperature. Instead, we found that the alternative
low temperature approximation (
27
) gives rise to a
well-behaved monotonic free energy even at low temperature.

There are many interesting open problems.
It would be interesting to generalize our analysis of the Airy case to
the matrix model of JT gravity
Saad:2019lba
.
It is speculated in
Saad:2019lba
that
the quenched free energy of JT gravity exhibits
a spin glass phase with the replica symmetry breaking at low temperature.
Based on the result in
Okuyama:2019xbv
;
Okuyama:2020ncd
,
it is argued in
Engelhardt:2020qpv
that
in the low temperature scaling limit

\displaystyle\beta\to\infty,\quad S_{0}\to\infty\quad\text{with}\quad\beta e^{2S_{0}/3}:~\text{fixed},

(95)

the quenched free energy of JT gravity reduces to that
of the Airy case.
Thus, according to the argument in
Engelhardt:2020qpv
,
we would be able to see the replica symmetry breaking
and spin glass phase in the quenched free energy of the Airy case
studied in our paper.
However, in our computation in section
3
,
it is not obvious whether the replica symmetry breaking occurs in the Airy case or not.
Instead, our analysis is based on the spacetime D-branes

e^{-Zx}
. It would be interesting to clarify
the relation between these two pictures.

Our analysis of the Airy case is limited to the genus expansion of
\mathcal{Z}(x)

in (
25
) and the low temperature approximation
of
\mathcal{Z}(x)
in (
27
).
It would be nice if we can find a closed form expression of the generating function
of the integral representation of
\langle Z^{n}\rangle_{c}
given
in
okounkov2002generating
.
We leave this as an interesting future problem.

In section
3.3
,
we have seen that
\mathcal{Z}(x)
in the Airy limit is closely related to the
generating function of the Hodge integrals studied in
dubrovin2017classical
.
However, the quenched free energy is not the generating function of the Hodge integrals
itself, but some integral transformation of it.
By introducing the chemical potential
\mu=\log x
, (
6
)
is written as

\displaystyle\langle\log Z\rangle=\log\langle Z\rangle-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu\Bigl[e^{-\mathcal{Z}(e^{\mu})}-e^{-\langle Z\rangle e^{\mu}}\Bigr].

(96)

This reminds us of the frame change in the topological string/spectral theory
(TS/ST) correspondence
(see e.g.
Marino:2015nla
for a review).
It would be interesting to understand the mathematical meaning
of this integral transformation.

In our previous paper
Okuyama:2020mhl
,
we find that the quenched free energy of Gaussian matrix model
has a finite limit at zero temperature

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to 0}F=E_{0},

(97)

and
E_{0}
is given by the average of the minimal eigenvalue of the random matrix.
Our result in the Airy case suggests that the quenched free energy
in the Airy limit also has a finite limit at zero temperature
(see Fig.
3
).
It would be interesting to compute this zero temperature value of the free energy
and clarify its physical meaning.

Finally, let us comment on the status of our formula
(
6
).
We emphasize that (
6
) is not just a formal expression,
but it is useful in practice for the actual computation of
the quenched free energy in random matrix models.
We have explicitly demonstrated this computation for the Airy case
in the genus expansion and also
in the low temperature approximation (
27
).
For the JT gravity case, as shown in

Okuyama:2019xbv
;
Okuyama:2020ncd

the deviation from the Airy limit (
95
)
is organized as a low temperature expansion
and it would be possible to compute
\mathcal{Z}(x)
of JT gravity in this expansion.
In general, the summation (
5
) over the number of boundaries
might be an asymptotic series and one might worry that

\mathcal{Z}(x)
is only defined perturbatively in the small
x
expansion.
However, in principle
\mathcal{Z}(x)
can be defined non-perturbatively
as the free energy of random matrix model with
a shifted background (
76
), as demonstrated in section
3.3
in the Airy case.
In other words,
\mathcal{Z}(x)
is the free energy of random matrix model
with a deformed matrix potential due to the insertion of the SD-brane operator

e^{-Zx}
.
This definition of
\mathcal{Z}(x)
does not rely on the small
x
expansion
in (
5
) and it may serve as a non-perturbative definition of
\mathcal{Z}(x)
,
which in turn leads to a non-perturbative definition of
\langle\log Z\rangle

via (
6
).
Instead of computing the connected correlators
\langle Z^{n}\rangle_{c}
term by term in
(
5
), we should consider the expectation value of the SD-brane operator

\langle e^{-Zx}\rangle
as a whole.
We stress that
this viewpoint is not obvious from the replica method (
3
)
and it is one of the advantages of our formalism (
6
),
at least conceptually.
One might think that the expansion (
16
) used in the replica method
breaks down when
w_{n}
’s in (
15
) become of order one. This
is indeed the case at low temperature (see (
98
)).
However, the computation in section
2.2
is just
an illustration that our formula (
4
)
can be obtained from the replica method and the final
result (
4
) makes sense without referring to the replica
method. Our formula (
4
) does not require

\langle\log Z\rangle
to be computed via the expansion (
16
);
(
4
) is well-defined even at low temperature once
\langle e^{-Zx}\rangle

is defined non-perturbatively by a shift of background (
76
).
We hope that our expression of
the quenched free energy (
4
) in terms of the SD-brane operator
e^{-Zx}

provides us with a new perspective on our understanding of random systems
and gravitational path integrals.

Appendix A
High temperature expansion in the Airy limit

In this appendix we consider the

The natural expansion parameter at high temperature is
\xi=\hbar^{2}\beta^{3}
.
For small
\xi
,
w_{n}

defined in (
15
) behaves as

\displaystyle w_{n}=\mathcal{O}(\xi^{n-1}).

(98)

Up to the order
\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})
, the expansion (
16
) reads

\displaystyle\frac{\langle Z^{n}\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle^{n}}=1+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)w_{2}+\frac{1}{6}n(n-1)(n-2)w_{3}+\frac{1}{8}n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)w_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3}).

(99)

For the Airy case,
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
are known in the closed form
okounkov2002generating
;
Beccaria:2020ykg
. From (
22
) we find

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\pi\xi}{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\xi}\text{Erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{2}}\right),

(100)

\displaystyle w_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\xi}{3\sqrt{3}}e^{2\xi}\Biggl[1-12T\Bigl(\sqrt{3\xi},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigr)\Biggr].

Plugging (
100
) into (
99

in the Airy limit

\displaystyle\frac{\langle Z^{n}\rangle}{\langle Z\rangle^{n}}=1+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\xi+\frac{n(n-1)(3n-5)(n+2)}{24}\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3}).

(101)

the result eq.(1.17) in
Beccaria:2020ykg
.
Taking the

\xi

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log(4\pi\xi)-\frac{5}{12}\xi+\frac{5}{12}\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3}),

in the small

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=u\partial_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{6}\partial_{0}^{3}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}+\hbar(\partial_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}})^{2}.

(104)

33

On this subspace (
33

\displaystyle\langle Z(\beta)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\hat{\xi}}}\exp\left(\frac{\hat{\xi}}{12}+\frac{\beta t_{0}}{1-t_{1}}\right).

\hat{\hbar}
in (

\displaystyle\partial_{1}W_{n}

\displaystyle=u\partial_{0}W_{n}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{6}\partial_{0}^{3}W_{n}

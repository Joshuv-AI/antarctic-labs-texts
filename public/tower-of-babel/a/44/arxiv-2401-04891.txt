Regularity of sets of finite fractional perimeter and nonlocal minimal surfaces in metric measure spaces
Josh Kline

Abstract
. In the setting of a doubling metric measure space, we study regularity of sets with finite s s -perimeter, that is, sets whose characteristic functions have finite Besov energy, with regularity parameter 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 and exponent p = 1 p=1 . Following a result of Visintin in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , we provide a sufficient condition for finiteness of the s s -perimeter given in terms of the upper Minkowski codimension of the regularized boundary of the set. We also show that if a set has finite s s -perimeter, then its measure-theoretic boundary has codimension s s Hausdorff measure zero. To the best of our knowledge, this result is new even in the Euclidean setting. By studying certain fat Cantor sets, we provide examples illustrating that the converses of these results do not hold in general. In the doubling metric measure space setting, we then consider minimizers of a nonlocal perimeter functional, extending the definition introduced by Caffarelli, Roquejoffre, and Savin in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , and prove existence, uniform density, and porosity results for minimizers.

1.
Introduction

In the setting of a doubling metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, we consider measurable sets
E\subset X
for which the following Besov energy is finite for some fractional parameter
0<s<1
:

\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}=\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E}(y)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

Specifically, we study regularity properties of these sets of finite
s
-perimeter and minimizers of the
s
-perimeter functional with respect to a given bounded domain
\Omega
.

In the celebrated work of Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
11
]
, it was shown that
for
p>1
, functions in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are characterized by the limiting behavior of the Besov
B^{s}_{p,p}
-energy, under suitable rescaling, as
s\to 1^{-}
. It was then shown by Dávila
[
16
]
that the same characterization holds for functions in
BV(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
p=1
. That is, given a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C_{n}P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the perimeter of
E
, given by the BV energy of
\chi_{E}
, and
C_{n}
is a dimensional constant. More recently, Di Marino and Squassina
[
17
]
proved analogous results in the setting of a complete, doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality. When
p=1
, they showed that

P(E,X)\lesssim\liminf_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}\leq\limsup_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}\lesssim P(E,X).

Hence, in both Euclidean and metric settings, sets of finite perimeter are characterized by the limiting behavior of their
s
-perimeters. For more on characterization of Sobolev and BV functions in the metric setting by nonlocal functionals, see
[
32
,
46
,
47
,
44
]
.

Federer’s characterization
[
28
]
states that
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of finite perimeter if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E)<\infty
, where
\partial^{*}E
is the measure-theoretic boundary of
E
, see Section
2.2
. In the setting of a complete, doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, the “only if” direction was proved by Ambrosio
[
3
]
, and more recently the “if” direction was shown by Lahti
[
45
]
. Here the codimension 1 Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{-1}
was used as a replacement for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, see Section
2.2
. In fact, it was shown in
[
3
,
43
]
that this characterization holds in this setting with
\partial^{*}E
replaced with a smaller subset of the boundary, namely points where
E
and its complement have lower density bounded below by a constant which depends only on the doubling and Poincaré constants, see Section
2.4
. As sets of finite perimeter are characterized both by this boundary regularity and the limiting behavior of their
s
-perimeters, we first consider in this paper the relationship between the
s
-perimeter of a set and codimension
s
measurements of its boundary.

The von Koch snowflake domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
shows that the “if” direction of Federer’s characterization does not hold in the fractional case. Indeed, it was shown in
[
48
]
that
\chi_{K}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
s\in(0,2-\log 4/\log 3)
. Since
\partial K=\partial^{*}K
and
\mathcal{H}^{\log 4/\log 3}(\partial K)<\infty
(see
[
27
]
for example), we see that
\mathcal{H}^{n-s}(\partial^{*}E)<\infty
does not imply that
E
is a set of finite
s
-perimeter in general. However, considering the upper Minkowski content rather than the Hausdorff measure provides a sufficient condition for finiteness of the
s
-perimeter. In
[
54
]
, the following fractional codimension was introduced by Visintin for bounded sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\codim_{F}(E)=\sup\{0<s<1:\chi_{E}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

and it was shown that this codimension is bounded below by the upper Minkowski codimension of the regularized boundary
\partial^{+}E
of
E
. The regularized boundary contains the measure-theoretic boundary, and any set can be modified on a set of measure zero so that its topological boundary coincides with its regularized boundary, see Lemma
2.7
. See Section
2.2
for the precise definitions of the regularized boundary and Minkowski contents and codimensions. We first generalize this result from
[
54
]
to the doubling metric measure space setting. For the following and subsequent result, we denote our ambient metric measure space by
(Z,d,\nu)
, rather than
(X,d,\mu)
, in order to avoid confusion with the hyperbolic filling, used in the same section.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a metric measure space satsifying the LLC-1 condition, with
\nu
a doubling measure. Let
E\subset Z
be a bounded measurable set, and suppose that there exists
t>0
such that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{+}E)<\infty
. Then
\chi_{E}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(Z)
for all
0<s<t
.

Here,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}
is the codimension
t
upper Minkowski content, defined in Section
2.2
, and the LLC-1 condition, defined in Section
2.1
, is a connectedness condition on
Z
. In Example
3.6
, we show that the conclusion to Theorem
1.1
may fail if
Z
does not satisfy this condition. Furthermore, by constructing certain fat Cantor sets, we show in Example
4.10
that the converse to Theorem
1.1
does not hold in general. In fact, we provide an example of a set
E\in B^{s}_{1,1}(Z)
for which
\partial^{*}E\subsetneq\partial^{+}E
and
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{*}E)=\infty
for all
0<t<1
. Thus, the converse does not hold even with the weaker conclusion of
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{*}E)<\infty
. Here
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}
is the codimension
t
lower Minkowski content, see Section
2.2
. For related results in the setting of strongly local Dirichlet metric spaces with sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates, see
[
2
, Section 5.3]
.

We then study necessary conditions for finiteness of the
s
-perimeter in the metric setting, and obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact metric measure space with
\nu
a doubling measure, and let
0<s<1
. If
E\subset Z
is such that
\chi_{E}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(Z)
, then
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{*}E)=0
.

Here,
\mathcal{H}^{-s}
is the codimension
s
Hausdorff measure, see Section
2.2
. To the best of our knowledge, this result is new even in the Euclidean setting. To prove this result, we utilize recent developments from analysis in metric spaces pertaining to the hyperbolic filling of a compact, doubling metric measure space. In
[
8
]
, it was shown that any compact, doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
can be realized as the boundary of a uniform metric measure space
X
which is doubling and supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality.
Furthermore, bounded trace and extension operators exist between the Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,p}(Z)
and the Newton-Sobolev spaces
N^{1,p}(X)
. See Theorem
2.15
and Section
2.5
below for an outline of the construction of the hyperbolic filling. To study the measure-theoretic properties of
E\subset Z
, we consider the Newton-Sobolev extension of
\chi_{E}
to the hyperbolic filling of
Z
. There, the validity of a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality provides us with useful potential theoretic tools with which to study
\partial^{*}E
. As with Theorem
1.1
, we also construct certain fat Cantor sets in Example
4.9
which illustrate that the converse of Theorem
1.2
does not hold in general. This construction also shows that Theorem
1.2
is sharp in the sense that we cannot replace
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{*}E)=0
with
\mathcal{H}^{-t}(\partial^{+}E)<\infty
. Indeed, we show in Example
4.8
that there exists a fat Cantor set
C_{a}
such that
\chi_{C_{a}}\in B^{s}_{1,1}([0,1])
, but
\mathcal{H}^{-t}(\partial^{+}C_{a})=\infty
for all
0<t<1
.

By combining Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following corollary, which generalizes the result from
[
54
]
to the metric setting and provides a new upper bound in terms of the Hausdorff codimension. See Section
2.2
and
2.6
for the precise definitions.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact metric measure space satisfying the LLC-1 condition, with
\nu
a doubling measure. Let
E\subset Z
be measurable. Then,

(1.4)

\overline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(\partial^{+}E)\leq\codim_{F}(E)\leq\codim_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial^{*}E).

In
[
48
]
, it was shown that (
1.4
) holds with equality when
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the von Koch snowflake domain. In Example
4.11
, we show that these inequalities may be strict, again by constructing a certain fat Cantor set.

In the second half of this paper, we consider a complete, doubling metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, and fix a bounded domain
\Omega\subset X
and
0<s<1
. We then study minimizers of the
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
functional, defined here in Section
2.6
, as originally introduced in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by Caffarelli, Roquejoffre, and Savin
[
15
]
. Related to problems arising in phase transitions, motion by mean curvature, and front propogation, see for example
[
1
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
53
]
, this functional measures the interaction between a set and its complement given by the
B^{s}_{1,1}
-seminorm, but excludes the interaction occurring in the complement of
\Omega
. In
[
15
]
, existence and regularity of minimizers, referred to there as
nonlocal minimal surfaces
, were studied for Lipschitz domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, it was shown that if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a minimizer, then
\partial E\cap\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-hypersurface around each of its points, outside of an
n-2
dimensional set. Since their introduction in
[
15
]
, nonlocal minimal surfaces have been studied at great length and remain a subject of active research in the Euclidean setting, see for example
[
14
,
21
,
20
,
29
,
50
]
and references therein. Of particular interest are asymptotic results as the nonlocal parameter
s
converges to either
0
or
1
and the “stickiness” phenomenon which is exhibited by nonlocal minimal surfaces, see for example
[
23
,
13
,
12
,
22
,
19
]
. For
\Gamma
-convergence results regarding the
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
functional, see
[
5
]
.

We consider this problem in the doubling metric measure space setting, and first prove an existence result via the direct method of calculus of variations in Theorem
5.9
. To do so, we first prove a compactness result in Theorem
5.2
by adapting the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 7.1]
to the metric setting. We then obtain the following uniform density result, which was established in
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
in the Euclidean setting:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a connected doubling metric measure space, let
\Omega\subset X
a bounded domain and let
0<s<1
. Then there exists a constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, such that if
E\subset X
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
, then after possibly modifying
E
on a set of measure zero, it follows that for all
x_{0}\in\partial E\cap\Omega
and
R_{0}>0
such that
B(x_{0},2R_{0})\subset\Omega
, we have

(1.6)

\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})\cap E)}{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}))}\geq\gamma_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}))}\geq\gamma_{0}.

Here, the constant
\gamma_{0}
depends only on
s
and the doubling constant of
\mu
, see (
6.3
).

The connectedness assumption on
X
can be relaxed, see Remark
6.4
. Our proof is inspired by the arguments of
[
42
, Theorem 4.2]
, where an analogous uniform density result was obtained for minimizers of the (local) perimeter functional, in the setting of a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality. There, the result is obtained via the De Giorgi method, where the Poincaré inequality plays a crucial role. Since we are dealing with the fractional case, we do not require the validity of a Poincaré inequality, instead utilizing a fractional Poincaré inequality
[
24
, Theorem 3.4]
, which is valid without additional assumptions on
X
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
, we obtain the following porosity results for minimizers. In the Euclidean setting, this was established in
[
15
, Corollary 4.3]
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space satisfying the LLC-1 condition, let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain, and let
0<s<1
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that if
E
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}^{s}_{\Omega}
, then after possibly modifying
E
on a set of measure zero, it follows that for all
x_{0}\in\partial E\cap\Omega
and
R_{0}>0
such that
B(x_{0},2R_{0})\subset\Omega,
there exist
y,z\in B(x_{0},R_{0})
such that

(1.8)

B(y,R_{0}/C)\subset E\cap\Omega\text{ and }B(z,R_{0}/C)\subset\Omega\setminus E.

The constant
C
depends only on
s
, the doubling constant of
\mu
, and the constant
C_{L}
from the LLC-1 condition.

We point out that Theorem
1.5
follows also from Theorem
1.7
.
However, to prove Theorem
1.7
, we use the uniform density result of Theorem
1.5
, and so we prove it independently. As in Theorem
1.5
, our proof is inspired by the arguments used to obtain the corresponding porosity results for minimizers of the local perimeter functional in
[
42
, Theorem 5.2]
. As before, however, the argument there utilizes the
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, which we do not assume in our case. As a corollary to Theorem
1.7
, we see that if
E
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
with
\chi_{E}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(X)
, then
\partial E\cap\Omega\subset\partial^{*}E
, and so by Theorem
1.2
, we have that
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial E\cap\Omega)=0
, see Corollary
6.10
.

The structure of the paper is as follows: in Section
2
we introduce the necessary definitions and background notions, and in Section
3
, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In Section 4, we establish examples illustrating sharpness of the results of Section
3
and that the converses of these results do not hold. In Section
5
, we prove existence results for minimizers of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
, and in Section
6
, we prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Doubling and connectedness properties

Given a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, we say that the measure
\mu
is
doubling
if there exists a constant
C_{\mu}\geq 1
such that

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\mu}\mu(B(x,r))<\infty

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
. We say that
(X,d,\mu)
is a
doubling metric measure space
if
\mu
is a doubling measure. By iterating this condition, there are constants
C_{Q}\geq 1
and
Q\geq 1
, depending only on
C_{\mu}
, such that

(2.1)

\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq C_{Q}^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{Q}

for all
x\in X
,
0<r\leq R
, and
y\in B(x,R)
. We say that
\mu
is
reverse doubling
if the opposite relationship holds, that is, if there exist constants
C_{\sigma}\geq 1
and
\sigma>0
such that for all
x\in X
,
0<r\leq R
, and
y\in B(x,R)
, we have

(2.2)

\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\leq C_{\sigma}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\sigma}.

The reverse doubling condition holds if
\mu
is doubling and
X
is connected, see for example
[
7
, Corollary 3.8]
, with constants
\sigma
and
C_{\sigma}
depending only on
C_{\mu}
. Throughout this paper, we let
C
denote a constant which depends, unless otherewise stated, only on the structural constants, such as the doubling constant for example. Its specific value is not of interest, and may vary with each occurrence, even within the same line. Given quantities
A
and
B
, we use the notation
A\simeq B
to mean that there exists such a constant
C\geq 1
such that
C^{-1}A\leq B\leq CA
. Likewise, we use
\lesssim
and
\gtrsim
if the left and right inequalities hold, respectively.

As a consequence of the doubling property, we have the following covering lemma, see for example
[
33
, Appendix B7]
and
[
37
, Section 3.3]
. This lemma holds under the weaker assumption that
(X,d)
is
metric doubling
. A metric space is metric doubling if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that any ball in the space can be covered by at most
C
balls of half the radius, see
[
35
,
37
]
for example. As doubling metric measure spaces are necessarily metric doubling, we state this lemma here for such spaces.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a countable cover
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
of
X
by balls with
\rad(B_{i})=\varepsilon
such that the collection
\{\frac{1}{5}B_{i}\}_{i}
is pairwise disjoint. Moreover,
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
has bounded overlap in the sense that for all
K>0
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
K
and
C_{\mu}
such that

\sum_{i}\chi_{KB_{i}}\leq C.

Here and throughout the paper, we denote by
\rad(B)
the radius of a ball
B
. We associate to this cover the following
Lipschitz partition of unity
, see
[
33
, Appendix B7]
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space, and let
\varepsilon>0
and
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
be as in Lemma
2.3
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for each
i
, there exists a
C/\varepsilon
-Lipschitz function
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}
satisfying
0\leq\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leq 1
,
\supt(\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon})\subset 2B_{i}
, and
\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
. Here the constant
C
depends only on
C_{\mu}
.

These preceding lemmas will be used in Section
5
to construct a
discrete convolution
of a locally integrable function
f
:

f_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i}f_{B_{i}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}.

Here and throughout the paper, we denote

f_{B}=\fint_{B}f\,d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}f\,d\mu.

At times, we will assume that
X
satisfies the following connectedness property:

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a metric space
(X,d)
is
linearly locally connected
(LLC) if there exists a constant
C_{L}\geq 1
such that for all
x\in X
and
r>0
, the following conditions hold:

(1)

Any two points in
B(x,r)
can be joined by a connected set in
B(x,C_{L}r)
.

(2)

Any two points in
X\setminus\overline{B}(x,r)
can be joined by a connected set in
X\setminus\overline{B}(x,r/C_{L}).

We say that
X
is LLC-1 if the first condition holds and LLC-2 if the second condition holds.

2.2.
Measure-theoretic boundary, Minkowski content, and Hausdorff measure

Definition 2.6
.

Let
E\subset X
. We define the
regularized boundary
of
E
, denoted
\partial^{+}E
, to be the collection of points
x\in X
such that

\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)>0\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)>0

for all
r>0
. We define the
measure-theoretic boundary
of
E
, denoted
\partial^{*}E
, to be collection of points
x\in X
such that

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}>0.

We note that
\partial^{*}E\subset\partial^{+}E\subset\partial E
, with
\partial E
being the topological boundary of
E
.

With the following lemma, we show that any measurable set can be modified on a set of measure zero so that its regularized boundary coincides with its topological boundary.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space. If
E\subset X
is measurable, then there exists
\widetilde{E}\subset X
such that
\mu(E\Delta\widetilde{E})=0
and
\partial^{+}E=\partial^{+}\widetilde{E}=\partial\widetilde{E}
.

Proof.

We define the following sets:

\displaystyle E_{-}:=\{x\in E:\exists r>0\text{ s.t. }\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)=0\}

\displaystyle E_{+}:=\{x\in X\setminus E:\exists r>0\text{ s.t. }\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)=0\}.

Since
\mu
is doubling, and so balls have positive
\mu
-measure, it follows that
E_{-}\cup E_{+}\subset\partial E
.
Let
\widetilde{E}=(E\setminus E_{-})\cup E_{+}
. It follows that
\mu(E_{-})=0=\mu(E_{+})
by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, and so
\mu(E\Delta\widetilde{E})=0
. As such, we have that
\partial^{+}E=\partial^{+}\widetilde{E}
by the definition of the regularized boundary. Since
\partial^{+}\widetilde{E}\subset\partial\widetilde{E}
, it remains to show the opposite inclusion.

Let
z\in\partial\widetilde{E}
, and let
r>0
. Then there exists
x\in B(z,r/2)\cap\widetilde{E}
. If
x\in E\setminus E_{-}
, then for all
\rho>0
, we have that

\mu(B(x,\rho)\cap\widetilde{E})=\mu(B(x,\rho)\cap E)>0,

since
\mu(E\Delta\widetilde{E})=0
. Hence,
\mu(B(z,r)\cap\widetilde{E})\geq\mu(B(x,r/2)\cap\widetilde{E})>0
. If
x\in E_{+}
, then there exists
r_{x}>0
such that
\mu(B(x,r_{x})\setminus\widetilde{E})=\mu(B(x,r_{x})\setminus E)=0
, and so we necessarily have that

\mu(B(z,r)\cap\widetilde{E})>0.

Hence, for all
r>0
, we have that
\mu(B(z,r)\cap\widetilde{E})>0
.

Likewise, since
z\in\partial\widetilde{E}
, there exists
y\in B(z,r/2)\setminus\widetilde{E}
. If
y\in E
, then we have that
y\in E_{-}
, in which case there exists
r_{y}>0
such that
\mu(B(y,r_{y})\cap\widetilde{E})=\mu(B(y,r_{y})\cap E)=0
. Then necessarily we have that
\mu(B(z,r)\setminus\widetilde{E})>0
. If
y\in X\setminus E
, then
y\in X\setminus E_{+}
, and so

\mu(B(z,r)\setminus\widetilde{E})\geq\mu(B(y,r/2)\setminus\widetilde{E})=\mu(B(y,r/2)\setminus E)>0.

Therefore, for all
r>0
, we have
\mu(B(z,r)\setminus\widetilde{E})>0
, and so
z\in\partial^{+}\widetilde{E}
.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

Let
E\subset X
, let
t>0
, and let
r>0
. We define the
codimension
t
Minkowski
r
-content
of
E
by

\mathcal{M}^{-t}_{r}(E):=\inf\left\{r^{-t}\sum_{i}\mu(B(x_{i},r)):E\subset\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i},r),\,x_{i}\in E\right\},

and we define the
codimension
t
upper and lower Minkowski contents
of
E
, respectively, by

\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(E)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{M}^{-t}_{r}(E)\quad\text{ and }\quad\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(E)=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{M}^{-t}_{r}(E).

The
upper and lower Minkowski codimensions
of
E
are defined, respectively, by

\overline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(E)=\sup\{t>0:\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(E)<\infty\}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(E)=\sup\{t>0:\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(E)<\infty\}.

Note that
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(E)\leq\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(E)
, and so
\overline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(E)\leq\underline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(E)
.
We define the
codimension
t
Hausdorff
r
-content
of
E
to be

\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{r}(E):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i}\frac{\mu(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})^{t}}:E\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{i},\,\rad(B_{i})\leq r\right\},

and likewise, we define the
codimension
t
Hausdorff measure
of
E
by

\mathcal{H}^{-t}(E)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{r}(E).

The
Hausdorff codimension
of
E
is defined by

\codim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)=\sup\{t>0:\mathcal{H}^{-t}(E)<\infty\}.

At times we will be considering Hausdorff contents and measures with respect to different references measures. In these cases we will include the reference measure as a subscript in the notation (
\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{\mu,r}(E)
and
\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{\mu}(E)
, for example). However, if the reference measure is clear from context, we will drop the reference to simplify the notation.

2.3.
Newton-Sobolev, BV, and Besov classes

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Given a family
\Gamma
of non-constant, compact, rectifiable curves, we define the
p
-modulus of
\Gamma
by

\Mod_{p}(\Gamma)=\inf_{\rho}\int_{X}\rho^{p}d\mu,

where the infimum is taken over all Borel functions
\rho:X\to[0,\infty]
such that
\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq 1
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. We refer the interested reader to
[
37
]
for more on modulus of curve families. Given a function
u:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that a Borel function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of
u
if the following holds for all non-constant, compact, rectifiable curves
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
:

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

whenever
u(x)
and
u(y)
are both finite, and
\int_{\gamma}g\,ds=\infty
otherwise. Here
x
and
y
denote the endpoints of
\gamma
. Upper gradients were first defined in
[
36
]
. We say that
g
is a
p
-weak upper gradient
of
u
if the
p
-modulus of the family of curves where the above inequality fails is zero.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X)
to be the class of all functions in
L^{p}(X)
which have an upper gradient belonging to
L^{p}(X)
, and we define

\|u\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\inf_{g}\|g\|_{L^{p}(X)},

where the infimum is taken over all upper gradients
g
of
u
. We then define an equivalence relation in
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X)
by
u\sim v
if and only if
\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X)}=0
. The
Newton-Sobolev space

N^{1,p}(X)
is then defined to be
\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X)/\sim
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}:=\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,p}(X)}
. One can similarly define
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
for any open set
\Omega\subset X
. Each
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
has a
minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
, denoted
g_{u}
, which is unique up to sets of measure zero, see for example
[
37
, Chapter 6]
. For more on
p
-modulus, upper gradients, and the Newton-Sobolev class, see
[
52
,
7
,
35
,
37
]
.

Following Miranda Jr.
[
51
]
we define the
total variation
of a function
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
, by

\|Du\|(X):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}g_{u_{i}}\,d\mu:N^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(X)\ni u_{i}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)\right\},

where
g_{u_{i}}
is an upper gradient of
u_{i}
. For
u\in L^{1}(X)
, we say that
u\in BV(X)
, that is,
u
is of
bounded variation
, if
\|Du\|(X)<\infty
. Similarly, we can define
\|Du\|(\Omega)
and
BV(\Omega)
for open sets
\Omega\subset X
. For an arbitrary set
A\subset X
, we then set

\|Du\|(A):=\inf\{\|Du\|(\Omega):A\subset\Omega,\,\Omega\text{ open}\}.

In
[
51
]
, it was shown that
\|Du\|(\cdot)
is a finite Radon measure on
X
when
u\in BV(X)
. For a measurable set
E\subset X
, we say that
E
is a set of
finite perimeter
if
\|D\chi_{E}\|(X)<\infty
, and we denote the
perimeter of
E
in
\Omega
by

P(E,\Omega):=\|D\chi_{E}\|(\Omega).

For more on BV functions in the Euclidean and metric settings, see
[
26
,
4
,
6
,
3
]
.

For
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the
Besov energy
of a function
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
by

\|u\|^{p}_{B^{s}_{p,p}(X)}:=\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

We then define the
Besov space

B^{s}_{p,p}(X)
to be the set of all functions
u\in L^{p}(X)
for which this energy is finite. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the space
B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
corresponds to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In
[
31
]
, it was shown that in doubling metric measure spaces supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality (see below),
B^{\alpha}_{p,p}(X)
coincides with the real interpolation space
(L^{p}(X),KS^{1,p}(X))_{\alpha,p}
, where
KS^{1,p}(X)
is the Korevaar-Schoen Sobolev space.

2.4.
Poincaré inequalities and consequences

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We say that
(X,d,\mu)
supports a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that

\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|d\mu\leq C\rad(B)\left(\fint_{\lambda B}g^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}

for all balls
B\subset X
and function-upper gradient pairs
(u,g)
. If
X
is a length space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality, then we may take
\lambda=1

[
34
]
. If
u
is locally integrable and possesses a
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient in
X
, then the Poincaré inequality above holds with
g_{u}
, the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
, on the right hand side, see
[
37
, Proposition 8.1.3]
.

When
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, Federer’s characterization of sets of finite perimeter holds. That is,
E\subset X
is such that
P(E,X)<\infty
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\partial^{*}E)<\infty

[
3
,
45
]
. In fact, it was shown by Ambrosio
[
3
]
(who proved the “only if” direction) and Lahti
[
43
]
(who proved the “if” direction), that there exists
\gamma>0
, depending only on the doubling and Poincaré constants, such that
P(E,X)<\infty
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\gamma}E)<\infty
. Here,
\Sigma_{\gamma}E
is the set of all
x\in X
such that

\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}\geq\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}\geq\gamma.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
and let
0<s<1
. We say that
X
supports an
(s,q,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that

\left(\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|^{q}d\mu\right)^{1/q}\leq C\rad(B)^{s}\left(\fint_{\lambda B}\int_{\lambda B}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}}{d(x,y)^{sp}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}

for all
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
and balls
B\subset X
.

The validity of a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality implies a number of strong geometric properties of
X
, and as a result, it is often assumed as a standing assumption on
X
. Under mild assumptions on
X
however, the fractional
(s,q,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality always holds:

Lemma 2.9
.

[
24
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space. Let
0<s<1
and let
1\leq p,q<\infty
be such that
q\leq p
. Then
X
supports an
(s,q,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality, with
\lambda=1
and constant
C
depending only on the
C_{\mu}
,
s
,
q
, and
p
.

If we additionally assume that
\mu
is reverse doubling, then the exponent on the left-hand side can be improved as follows:

Theorem 2.10
.

[
24
, Theorem 3.4]

Let
\mu
be reverse doubling. Let
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
be such that
sp<Q
, where
Q\geq 1
is as in (
2.1
), and let
p^{*}=Qp/(Q-sp)
. Then
X
supports an
(s,p^{*},p,p)
-Poincaré inequality with
\lambda=2
and the constant
C
depending only on
C_{\mu}
,
s
,
p
, and the constants from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

By applying this result to the case
p=1
, we obtain the following lemma, the proof of which follows mutatis mutandis from
[
42
, Lemma 2.2]
. We include the proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mu
be reverse doubling, and let
0<s<1
, and let
Q\geq 1
be as in (
2.1
). There exists
C\geq 1
such that if
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
and a ball
B\subset X
are such that

(2.12)

\frac{\mu(\{|u|>0\}\cap B)}{\mu(B)}\leq\gamma

for some
0<\gamma<1
, then

\left(\fint_{B}|u|^{Q/(Q-s)}d\mu\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}\leq C\frac{\rad(B)^{s}}{1-\gamma^{s/Q}}\fint_{2B}\int_{2B}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

Here the constant
C
depends only on
C_{\mu}
,
s
, and the constants from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
and let
B\subset X
be a ball such that (
2.12
) holds for some
0<\gamma<1
. For
x\in X
, let

g_{u,s,2B}(x):=\int_{2B}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y),

and let
t=Q/(Q-s)
.
By Minkowski’s inequality and Theorem
2.10
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}|u|^{t}d\mu\right)^{1/t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|^{t}d\mu\right)^{1/t}+|u_{B}|\lesssim\rad(B)^{s}\fint_{2B}g_{u,s,2B}\,d\mu+|u_{B}|

We then have from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle|u_{B}|\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B\cap\{|u|>0\}}|u|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\left(\int_{B\cap\{|u|>0\}}|u|^{t}d\mu\right)^{1/t}\mu(B\cap\{|u|>0\})^{1-1/t}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{1-1/t}\left(\fint_{B}|u|^{t}d\mu\right)^{1/t}.

Absorbing this term to the left hand side of the previous inequality gives

\left(\fint_{B}|u|^{t}d\mu\right)^{1/t}\lesssim\frac{\rad(B)^{s}}{1-\gamma^{1-1/t}}\fint_{2B}g_{u,s,2B}\,d\mu,

with comparison constant coming from Theorem
2.10
. Hence, this constant depends only on
C_{\mu}
,
s
, and the constants from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).
∎

2.5.
Hyperbolic fillings

For a sufficiently regular domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B^{1-\theta/p}_{p,p}(\partial\Omega)
arises naturally as the trace space of
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, where
n-\theta
is the dimension of
\partial\Omega
, see for example
[
30
,
41
]
. Such a relationship also holds between the Newton-Sobolev class
N^{1,p}(\Omega,\mu)
and
B^{1-\theta/p}_{p,p}(\partial\Omega,\nu)
when
\Omega\subset X
is a uniform domain, and
(X,d,\mu)
is a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,p)
-Poincaré inequality
[
49
]
. Here
\nu
is a measure on
\partial\Omega
which is codimension
\theta
Ahlfors regular with respect to
\mu
. That is, there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
x\in\partial\Omega
, and
0<r<2\diam(\Omega)
,

C^{-1}\nu(B(x,r)\cap\partial\Omega)\leq\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)}{(\rad(B))^{\theta}}\leq C\nu(B(x,r)\cap\partial\Omega)).

Recently, it was shown by Björn, Björn, and Shanmugalingam
[
8
]
that every compact, doubling metric measure space
(Z,d,\nu)
arises as the boundary of a uniform space
\Omega
, where the above codimensional relationship between
\nu
and the measure on
\Omega
is satisfied. It was also shown that the Besov spaces on
Z
arise as traces of the Newton-Sobolev classes on
\Omega
. The space
\Omega
was constructed using the hyperbolic filling technique, first introduced by Bonk and Kleiner
[
9
]
and Bourdon and Pajot
[
10
]
. We outline the construction from
[
8
]
as follows:

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a compact, doubling metric measure space. Fix
\alpha>1
,
\tau>1
, and
x_{0}\in Z
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, choose a maximally
\alpha^{-n}
-separated set
A_{n}\subset Z
. By scaling the metric if necessary, we may assume that
\diam(Z)<1
, and so
A_{0}:=\{x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in Z
. We define the vertex set

V=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\{(x,n):x\in A_{n}\}.

We then define the edge relationship
\sim
between vertices by

(x,n)\sim(y,m)
if and only if either
n=m
and
B_{Z}(x,\tau\alpha^{-n})\cap B_{Z}(y,\tau\alpha^{-m})\neq\varnothing
, or
|n-m|=1
and
B_{Z}(x,\alpha^{-n})\cap B_{Z}(y,\alpha^{-m})\neq\varnothing
. Thus, vertices
(x,n)
and
(y,m)
can only be joined by an edge if
|n-m|\leq 1
. Here
B_{Z}
means that we are considering the ball in
Z
. We then turn the combinatorial graph given by
V
and this edge relationship into a metric graph
X
by assigning a unit length interval to each edge.
We define the
hyperbolic filling
of
(Z,d,\nu)
to be the metric space
(X,d_{X})
, where
d_{X}
is the path metric on
X
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we define the uniformized metric
d_{\varepsilon}
on
X
by

(2.13)

d_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\varepsilon d_{X}(\cdot,v_{0})}ds,

where
v_{0}=(x_{0},0)
, and the infimum is over all curves in
X
with endpoints
x
and
y
. By choosing
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
, it was shown in
[
8
]
that
(X,d_{\varepsilon})
is a bounded uniform domain. Furthermore, denoting
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
to be the completion of
X
with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
and
\partial_{\varepsilon}X:=\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus X
, it was shown that
Z
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
\partial_{\varepsilon}X
, with constants depending only on
\alpha
and
\tau
. Moreover,
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
is geodesic. That is, every two points
x,y\in\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
can be joined by a curve
\gamma
such that
\ell(\gamma)=d_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
.

Let
\beta>0
. For each vertex
v=(z,n)\in V
, we define the weight

\hat{\mu}_{\beta}(\{v\})=e^{-\beta n}\nu(B_{Z}(z,\alpha^{-n})),

and for
A\subset X
, we define the measure
\mu_{\beta}
by

\mu_{\beta}(A)=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{w\sim v}(\hat{\mu}_{\beta}(\{v\})+\hat{\mu}_{\beta}(\{w\}))\mathcal{H}^{1}(A\cap[v,w]),

where
[v,w]
denotes the edge joining
v
to
w
. For
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
and
\beta>0
, it was shown in
[
8
]
that the metric measure space
(X,d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is doubling and supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, as does
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
, with constants depending only on
\alpha
,
\tau
,
\beta
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
. Furthermore, the following codimensional relationship holds between
\nu
and
\mu_{\beta}
:

(2.14)

\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,r))}{r^{\beta/\varepsilon}}\simeq\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r))

for all
\zeta\in\partial_{\varepsilon}X
and
0<r\leq 2\diam X_{\varepsilon}
. Here the comparison constants also depend only on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\tau
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
. It was then shown in
[
8
]
that for
1\leq p<\infty
, there exist bounded trace and extension operators between
N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
and
B^{1-\beta/(\varepsilon p)}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
. In particular, the following theorem holds, which we will utilize in Section
3
:

Theorem 2.15
.

[
8
, Theorem 12.1]

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
0<s<1
. Let
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
and choose
\beta>0
so that
s=1-\beta/(p\varepsilon)
. Then for each
f\in B^{s}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)
, there exists
u_{f}\in N^{1,p}(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
such that
u_{f}=f

\nu
-a.e. on
Z
, and

\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}g^{p}_{u_{f}}d\mu_{\beta}\lesssim\|f\|^{p}_{B^{s}_{p,p}(Z,\nu)}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}}|u_{f}|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}\lesssim\int_{Z}|f|^{p}d\nu,

where
g_{u_{f}}
is the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u_{f}
, and the comparison constants depend only on
s
,
p
,
\alpha
,
\tau
, and the doubling constant of
\nu
. Furthermore, for
\nu
-a.e.
z\in Z
, we have that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(z,r)}|u_{f}-f(z)|^{p}d\mu_{\beta}=0.

That is,
\nu
-a.e.
z\in Z
is an
L^{p}(\mu_{\beta})
-Lebesgue point of
u_{f}
.

By (
2.14
), we have the following lemmas, which relate
\nu
and codimensional Hausdorff measures on
Z
to codimensional Haudsorff measures on
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
:

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
and
\beta>0
. Let
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
be the completion with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
of the uniformized hyperbolic filling
(X,d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
of
(Z,d_{Z},\nu)
. If
z\in Z
,
\delta>0
, and
A\subset B_{Z}(z,r)\cap Z
, then

\nu(A)\lesssim\mathcal{H}^{-\beta/\varepsilon}_{\mu_{\beta},\,\delta}(A).

Proof.

Let
\{B_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence of balls
B_{k}\subset\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
with
\rad(B_{k})\leq\delta
such that
A\subset\bigcup_{k}B_{k}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
B_{k}\cap A\neq\varnothing
for all
k
. Then, for each
k
, there exists
B_{k}^{\prime}
such that
B_{k}\subset B_{k}^{\prime}
,
B_{k}^{\prime}
is centered at a point in
Z
, and
\rad(B_{k})\simeq\rad(B_{k}^{\prime})
. We then have that

\sum_{k}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{k})}{\rad(B_{k})^{\beta/\varepsilon}}\simeq\sum_{k}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{k}^{\prime})}{\rad(B_{k}^{\prime})^{\beta/\varepsilon}}\simeq\sum_{k}\nu(B_{k}^{\prime})\geq\nu(A).

Here we have used the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
, and bi-Lipschitz equivalence of
d_{\varepsilon}
and
d_{Z}
on
Z
in conjunction with (
2.14
). This completes the proof, as the cover
\{B_{k}\}_{k}
is arbitrary.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\varepsilon=\log\alpha
and
0<\beta\leq\varepsilon
. Let
\overline{X}_{\varepsilon}
be the completion with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
of the uniformized hyperbolic filling
(X,d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
of
(Z,d_{Z},\nu)
. Then

\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mu_{\beta}}|_{Z}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{-(1-\beta/\varepsilon)}_{\nu}.

Proof.

The result follows directly from (
2.14
). Note that by by the bi-Lipschitz equivalence of
d_{\varepsilon}
and
d_{Z}
on
Z
, we can equivalently define
\mathcal{H}^{-(1-\beta/\varepsilon)}_{\nu}
with respect to
d_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

2.6.
Fractional
s
-perimeter and minimizers of the
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
functional

It was shown in
[
16
]
that for a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following formula holds:

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=C_{n}P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
C_{n}
is a dimensional constant and
P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})=\|D\chi_{E}\|(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the perimeter of
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see Section
2.3
. More recently, it was shown in
[
17
]
that in a complete, doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, the following holds for measurable sets
E\subset X
:

P(E,X)\lesssim\liminf_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}\leq\limsup_{s\to 1^{-}}(1-s)\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}\lesssim P(E,X).

In this manner, when
p=1
, the Besov energy of the characteristic function of a set
E
recovers the perimeter of
E
under suitable rescaling as
s\to 1^{-}
. For
0<s<1
, we therefore define the
s
-perimeter
of a measurable set
E\subset X
, by

P_{s}(E,X):=\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}=\int_{X}\int_{X}\frac{|\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E}(y)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

If
\mu
is doubling, then we have that

(2.18)

P_{s}(E,X)\simeq\int_{E}\int_{X\setminus E}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

The
s
-perimeter generates the following notion of codimension, as originally defined in
[
54
]
:

Definition 2.19
.

Let
E\subset X
be measurable. We define the
fractional codimension
of
E
by

\codim_{F}(E)=\sup\{0<s<1:P_{s}(E,X)<\infty\}.

In Section
3
and
4
, we will study the relationship between the fractional codimension of a set and the Minkowski and Hausdorff codimensions of its boundary.

For
x,y\in X
, we define the kernel

K_{s}(x,y):=\frac{2}{d(x,y)^{s}[\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))+\mu(B(y,d(x,y)))]},

and for measurable sets
A,B\subset X
, we adopt the following notation:

L_{s}(A,B):=\int_{A}\int_{B}K_{s}(x,y)d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

By symmetry of the kernel
K_{s}
, we have that

L_{s}(A,B)=L_{s}(B,A),

and if
\mu
is doubling, then

(2.20)

L_{s}(A,B)\simeq\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x),

with comparison constants depending only on the doubling constant of
\mu
.

Given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset X
with
X\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
, and a set
E\subset X
, we define the following functional
\mathcal{J}^{s}_{\Omega}
as introduced in
[
15
]
in the Euclidean setting:

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E):=L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)+L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,\Omega\setminus E).

From (
2.18
), (
2.20
), and (
2.21
), we see that in doubling metric measure spaces, this functional incorporates much of the
s
-perimeter of
E
, but it excludes the term
L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,X\setminus(E\cup\Omega))
from the
s
-perimeter. The reason for this exclusion is that this term may be infinite, and in the following minimization problem, candidate sets will be fixed outside of
\Omega
. Therefore, this exclusion does not affect the minimization process.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain, and let
0<s<1
. Let
F\subset X
be a measurable set such that
F\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
. We say that
E\subset X
is a
minimizer
of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
if
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)<\infty
,
E\setminus\Omega=F\setminus\Omega
, and

\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)\leq\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E^{\prime})

for all
E^{\prime}\subset X
such that
E^{\prime}\setminus\Omega=F\setminus\Omega
.

This problem was introduced in the Euclidean setting in
[
15
]
, where existence and regularity of minimizers was studied.
We will study existence and regularity of minimizers in the doubling metric measure space setting in Sections
5
and
6
.

We conclude this section with a lemma concerning minimizers of the
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
functional, which can be found in
[
15
, Section 2]
. We will use this lemma to study regularity of minimizers in Section
6
.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
E
be a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
. If
A\subset(E\cap\Omega),
then

(2.24)

L_{s}(A,X\setminus E)\leq L_{s}(E\setminus A,A).

Proof.

Since
E
is a minimizer, by taking
E\setminus A
as a competitor, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E\setminus A)-\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)=L_{s}((

\displaystyle E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,X\setminus(E\setminus A))+L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,\Omega\setminus(E\setminus A))

\displaystyle-L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)-L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,\Omega\setminus E).

Since
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)<\infty
, we know that
L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,\Omega\setminus E)<\infty
. We then have that

L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,\Omega\setminus(E\setminus A))-L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,\Omega\setminus E)=L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,A)=L_{s}((E\setminus A)\setminus\Omega,A).

We also have that

L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,X\setminus(E\setminus A))=L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)+L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,A).

Substituting both of these expressions into the previous expression yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)+L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,A)+L_{s}((E\setminus A)\setminus\Omega,A)-L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)

\displaystyle=L_{s}(E\setminus A,A)+L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)-L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)

\displaystyle=L_{s}(E\setminus A,A)-L_{s}(A,X\setminus E).

Note that both
L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)
and
L_{s}((E\setminus A)\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)
are finite since
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)<\infty
. This allows us to obtain the last equality in the above expression.
∎

3.
On the boundary size of sets of finite
s
-perimeter

In this section, we let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a metric measure space, with
\nu
a doubling Borel regular measure. Our goal here is to explore the relationship between the
s
-perimeter of a subset of
Z
and the size of its boundary. Recall the definition of
s
-perimeter: for
E\subset Z
, we define
P_{s}(E,Z)=\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(Z)}
.
We begin by proving a sufficient condition, given in terms of the upper Minkowski content of the regularized boundary, guaranteeing that a set has finite
s
-perimeter. This result was first proved in the Euclidean setting by Visintin, see
[
54
, Propositions 11 and 13]
. We show here that the same result holds in doubling metric measure spaces satisfying the LLC-1 condition, see Definition
2.5
.

For ease of notation in the following proof, we define the following for measurable sets
A,B\subset Z
:

J_{s}(A,B):=\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{s}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
E\subset Z
be bounded and measurable, and suppose that there exist
t>0
such that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{+}E)<\infty
. By Lemma
2.7
and the fact that the Besov energy does not detect measure zero changes in sets, we may assume without loss of generality that
\partial^{+}E=\partial E
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
0<s<t
. By definition, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
0<r<r_{0}
, there exists a countable cover
\{B(x_{i},r)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\partial E
, with each
x_{i}\in\partial E
, such that

(3.1)

r^{-t}\sum_{i}\nu(B(x_{i},r))<\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial E)+\varepsilon.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

E_{k}:=\{z\in E:2^{-k-1}r_{0}\leq\dist(z,Z\setminus E)<2^{-k}r_{0}\},

and let
E_{0}=E\setminus\left(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}E_{k}\cup\partial E\right).
Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial E)<\infty
and
t>0
, it follows that
\nu(\partial E)=0
, and so we have that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{E}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(Z,\nu)}\simeq J_{s}(E,Z\setminus E)\simeq J_{s}(E_{0},Z\setminus E)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}J_{s}(E_{k},Z\setminus E).

For each
z\in E_{0}
, we have that
\dist(z,Z\setminus E)\geq r_{0}
, and so by doubling we have that

\displaystyle J_{s}(E_{0},Z\setminus E)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E_{0}}\int_{Z\setminus B(z_{,}r_{0})}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{s}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E_{0}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2^{m+1}r_{0})\setminus B(z,2^{m}r_{0})}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{s}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{E_{0}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(2^{m}r_{0})^{-s}d\nu(z)\simeq r_{0}^{-s}\nu(E_{0})<\infty.

Here, the finiteness is due to boundedness of
E
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have by a similar computation that

\displaystyle J_{s}(E_{k},Z\setminus E)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E_{k}}\int_{Z\setminus B(z,2^{-k-1}r_{0})}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{s}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{k}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2^{-k+m}r_{0})\setminus B(z,2^{-k-1+m}r_{0})}\frac{1}{d(z,w)^{s}\nu(B(z,d(z,w)))}d\nu(w)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\nu(E_{k})}{(2^{-k}r_{0})^{s}}.

For each
z\in E_{k}
, there exists
w\in Z\setminus E
such that
d(z,w)<2^{-k}r_{0}
. By the LLC-1 condition, there exists a connected set in
B(z,C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0})
joining
z
to
w
, where
C_{L}\geq 1
is the constant from (
2.5
). Since
z\in E_{k}\subset E
and
w\in Z\setminus E
, it follows that there exists
y\in B(z,C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0})\cap\partial E
. Therefore,

E_{k}\subset\{z\in Z:\dist(z,\partial E)<C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}\}=:\mathcal{N}_{C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}}(\partial E),

and so combining this with the previous inequality, we have

(3.4)

J_{s}(E_{k},Z\setminus E)\lesssim\frac{\nu(\mathcal{N}_{C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}}(\partial E))}{(2^{-k}r_{0})^{s}}.

By (
3.1
), there exists a cover
\{B(x_{i},2^{-k}r_{0})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\partial E
, with
x_{i}\in\partial E
, such that

(3.5)

(2^{-k}r_{0})^{-t}\sum_{i}\nu(B(x_{i},2^{-k}r_{0}))<\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial E)+\varepsilon.

We then claim that

\mathcal{N}_{C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}}(\partial E)\subset\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i},2C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}).

Indeed, if
x\in\mathcal{N}_{C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}}(\partial E)
, then there exists
y\in\partial E
such that
d(x,y)<C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}
. There also exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
y\in B(x_{i},2^{-k}r_{0})
, and so
d(x,x_{i})<(C_{L}+1)2^{-k}r_{0}\leq 2C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}
. The claim follows. Therefore, by (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and the doubling property of
\nu
, we have that

\displaystyle J_{s}(E_{k},Z\setminus E)\lesssim(2^{-k}r_{0})^{-s}\sum_{i}\nu(B(x_{i},2C_{L}2^{-k}r_{0}))

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{-k}r_{0})^{-s}\sum_{i}\nu(B(x_{i},2^{-k}r_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq(2^{-k}r_{0})^{t-s}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial E)+\varepsilon).

Since
t>s
, it follows that

\sum_{k}J_{s}(E_{k},Z\setminus E)\lesssim r_{0}^{t-s}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial E)+\varepsilon)<\infty.

Combining this with (
3
) and (
3.2
) completes the proof.
∎

Given a set

In particular, we will use the following lemma, which is a modification of

. This can be found for example in

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a geodesic space, with
\mu
a doubling measure supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, and let
0<t<1
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that if
r>0

E
and
F
are subsets of a ball
B(x,r)

satisfies

\min\{\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{r}(E),\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{r}(F)\}\geq\lambda\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{t}},

then for any function
u\in N^{1,1}(X)
such that
u=1
on
E

u=0
on
F
, and each
z\in E\cup F
such that

-Lebesgue point of

\int_{B(x,r)}g_{u}\,d\mu\geq\frac{\lambda}{C}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r},

where
g_{u}
is the minimal

u

Proof.

By truncation, we may assume that
0\leq u\leq 1
. If
u_{B(x,r)}\leq 1/2
, then for all

|u(z)-u_{B(x,r)}|\geq 1/2

u_{B(x,r)}>1/2

Let
z\in E

[x,z]
connecting
x

, then let
B_{0}:=B(x,r)
,
B_{1}:=B(x,d(x,z)/2)
, and let
B_{2}:=B(x_{2},d(x,z)/2^{2})
, where
x_{2}
is a point in
[x,z]

.
Inductively, if

B_{0}=B(x,r)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq|u(z)-u_{B(x,r)}|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|u_{B_{k+1}}-u_{B_{k}}|\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\fint_{2B_{k}}|u-u_{2B_{k}}|d\mu\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}r\fint_{2B_{k}}g_{u}d\mu.

\displaystyle\min\{\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{1}),\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{0})\}

\displaystyle=\min\{\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k})\cap E),\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k})\setminus E)\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k}))\simeq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k}))}{r_{\zeta,k}^{1-s}}.

Here we have used (
2.14
) and the choice
\beta/\varepsilon=1-s
in the last inequality. By Lemma
2.16
, (
3.10
), and the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
, we have that

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{H}^{-(1-s)}_{\mu_{\beta},\,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k}}\left(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{1}\right),\mathcal{H}^{-(1-s)}_{\mu_{\beta},\,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k}}(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{0})\right\}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\min\{\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{1}),\nu(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{0})\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{1}),\nu(B_{Z}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k})\cap E_{0})\}\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,r_{\zeta,k}))}{r_{\zeta,k}^{1-s}}\simeq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k}))}{(C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})^{1-s}}.

Note that
(\overline{X}_{\varepsilon},d_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\beta})
is geodesic, where
\mu_{\beta}
is doubling and supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, see Section
2.5
. Hence, we apply Lemma
3.7
to obtain

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})}g_{u}\,d\mu_{\beta}\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k}))}{C_{0}r_{\zeta,k}}.

Since
\{B_{\varepsilon}(\zeta,C_{0}r_{\zeta,k})\}_{\zeta\in\partial^{*}_{k}E}
covers
\partial^{*}_{k}E
, it follows from the 5-covering lemma that there exists a pairwise disjoint subcollection
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
such that
\partial^{*}_{k}E\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}5B_{i}
. Since
r_{\zeta,k}<1/(C_{0}j_{k})
, we have that
B_{i}\subset X_{j_{k}}
for each
i
. Then, by (
3.9
), the doubling property of
\mu_{\beta}
, and by
r_{\zeta,k}<\delta/(5C_{0})
, it follows that

\displaystyle 2^{-2k}\eta>\int_{X_{j_{k}}}g_{u}\,d\mu_{\beta}\geq\sum_{i\in I}\int_{B_{i}}g_{u}\,d\mu_{\beta}\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu_{\beta}(5B_{i})}{\rad(5B_{i})}\geq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mu_{\beta},\,\delta}(\partial^{*}_{k}E).

Hence, from (
3.8
) we have that

\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mu_{\beta},\,\delta}(\partial^{*}E)\leq\sum_{k=N_{\delta}}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mu_{\beta},\,\delta}(\partial^{*}_{k}E)\lesssim\eta.

As
\delta>0
and
\eta>0
are arbitrary, we have from Lemma
2.17
that

\mathcal{H}^{-s}_{\nu}(\partial^{*}E)=\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mu_{\beta}}(\partial^{*}E)=0.\qed

Corollary
1.3
now follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. As mentioned above, the first inequality in Corollary
1.3
was obtained in the Euclidean setting in
[
54
]
. While (
1.4
) may hold with equality in the above corollary, as is the case with the von Koch snowflake domain, see
[
48
]
, we provide an example in the next section showing that these inequalities may be strict.

4.
Examples

In this section, we demonstrate some sharpness of the above results, and show by example that the converses of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
do not hold in general. We do this by constructing a fat Cantor set as follows:

Let
I=[0,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
be equipped with the Euclidean distance and Lebesgue measure. For a parameter
0<a<1/3
, we remove an open interval
I_{1,1}
of length
a
from the center of
I
. Let
C_{1,1}
and
C_{1,2}
be the disjoint closed intervals which comprise
I\setminus I_{1,1}
. For the second stage of the construction, we remove an open interval
I_{2,k}
of length
a^{2}
from the center of each
C_{1,k}
,
k=1,2
. Let
\{C_{2,k}\}_{k=1}^{4}
be the remaining closed intervals. Continuing inductively in this manner, for the
j
-th stage of the construction, we remove an open interval
I_{j,k}
of length
a^{j}
from the center of each
C_{j-1,k}
,
k=1,\dots,2^{j-1}
, and we let
\{C_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{2^{j}}
be the collection of remaining closed intervals. Then for each
1\leq k\leq 2^{j}
, we have that

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{j,k})=\frac{1-3a+a(2a)^{j}}{2^{j}(1-2a)}=:c_{j}.

We then define the Cantor set
C_{a}
by

C_{a}:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=1}^{2^{j}}C_{j,k}=I\setminus\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k=1}^{2^{j-1}}I_{j,k}\right).

Since
0<a<1/3
, we see that

\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a})=\frac{1-3a}{1-2a}>0.

We now relate the Besov energy of the characteristic function of
C_{a}
to the parameter
a
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
0<a<1/3
, and let
C_{a}
be constructed as above. Then

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(2a^{1-s})^{j}

with the comparison constant depending only on
s
.

Proof.

We have that

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}=\|\chi_{I\setminus C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j-1}}\chi_{I_{j,k}}\Big\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j-1}}\|\chi_{I_{j,k}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)},

and for
1\leq j<\infty
and
1\leq k\leq 2^{j-1}
,

\|\chi_{I_{j,k}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}=2\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I\setminus I_{j,k}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx.

We have that

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I\setminus I_{j,k}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{a^{j}}\int_{a^{j}}^{1}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{s(1-s)}(a^{j(1-s)}+(1-a^{j})^{1-s}-1)\leq\frac{2a^{j(1-s)}}{s(1-s)},

and so it follows that

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\leq\frac{2}{s(1-s)}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(2a^{1-s})^{j}.\qed

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
0<s<1
, and let
0<a<1/3
be such that
a<1/2^{1+s}
. Then

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\simeq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(2a^{1-s})^{j},

with comparison constant depending only on
s
and
a
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have that

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(2a^{1-s})^{j}.

To prove the converse inequality, we have that

(4.4)

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}=2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx,

and for each
1\leq j<\infty
and
1\leq k\leq 2^{j-1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx=\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I\setminus I_{j,k}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1,\\
(l,m)\neq(j,k)\end{subarray}}^{2^{l-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx.

Fix
1\leq j<\infty
and
1\leq k\leq 2^{j-1}
. Setting

J_{1}:=\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I\setminus I_{j,k}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx
,
and

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1,\\
(l,m)\neq(j,k)\end{subarray}}^{2^{l-1}}

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1,\\
(l,m)\neq(j,k)\end{subarray}}^{2^{l-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx+\sum_{l=j+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{l-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx

\displaystyle=:J_{2}+J_{3},

we then have that

(4.5)

\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx=J_{1}-J_{2}-J_{3}.

For each
1\leq l\leq j
and
1\leq m\leq 2^{l-1}
such that
(l,m)\neq(j,k)
, it follows from the construction that there is at least one closed interval from the collection
\{C_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{2^{j}}
separating
I_{j,k}
and
I_{l,m}
. Hence

\dist(I_{j,k},I_{l,m})\geq c_{j}>a^{j},

since
0<a<1/3
, where
c_{j}
is given by (
4.1
). Letting
I_{j,k}\pm a^{j}
denote the
a^{j}
-neighborhood of the interval
I_{j,k}
, it then follows that

\displaystyle J_{1}-J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I\setminus I_{j,k}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx-\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1,\\
(l,m)\neq(j,k)\end{subarray}}^{2^{l-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{(I_{j,k}\pm a^{j})\setminus I_{j,k}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx

(4.6)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{a^{j}}\int_{a^{j}}^{2a^{j}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx=\frac{4(1-2^{-s})}{s(1-s)}a^{j(1-s)}.

To estimate
J_{3}
, fix
l\geq j+1
and consider the collection of intervals
\{I_{l,m}\}_{m=1}^{2^{l-1}}
. By the construction of
C_{a}
, it follows that for each interval
I_{l,m}
in this collection, there exist some positive integer
1\leq n_{m}\leq 2^{l}
such that there are precisely
n_{m}
closed intervals from the collection
\{C_{l,i}\}_{i=1}^{2^{l}}
between
I_{j,k}
and
I_{l,m}
, and so

\dist(I_{j,k},I_{l,m})\geq n_{m}c_{l}.

From this, we see that

\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx\leq\frac{a^{j}a^{l}}{(n_{m}c_{l})^{1+s}}.

Furthermore, for each
1\leq n\leq 2^{l}
, there are at most two intervals from the collection
\{I_{l,m}\}_{m=1}^{2^{l-1}}
which are separated from
I_{j,k}
by precisely
n
closed intervals from
\{C_{l,i}\}_{i=1}^{2^{l}}
. Hence, we overestimate
J_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=j+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{l-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{I_{l,m}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx\leq 2\sum_{l=j+1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{l}}\frac{a^{j}a^{l}}{(nc_{l})^{1+s}}=2a^{j}\sum_{l=j+1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{l}}{c_{l}^{1+s}}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{l}}\frac{1}{n^{1+s}}

We have that

\displaystyle\frac{a^{l}}{c_{l}^{1+s}}=a^{l}\left(\frac{2^{l}(1-2a)}{1-3a+a(2a)^{l}}\right)^{1+s}\leq(2^{1+s}a)^{l}\frac{1}{(1-3a)^{1+s}},

and so by the assumption that
a<\min\{1/3,1/2^{1+s}\}
, it follows that

C_{s,a}:=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{l}}{c_{l}^{1+s}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+s}}<\infty.

Therefore, we have that

(4.7)

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq 2C_{s,a}a^{j}.

Combining (
4.5
), (
4
), and (
4.7
), we have that

\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx\geq\left(\frac{4(1-2^{-s})}{s(1-s)}-2C_{s,a}a^{js}\right)a^{j(1-s)},

and so there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
s
and
a
such that

\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx\geq\frac{2(1-s^{-s})}{s(1-s)}a^{j(1-s)}=:C_{s}a^{j(1-s)}

for all
j>N
. By (
4.4
), we then have that

\displaystyle\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\geq 2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{j-1}}\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx+C_{s}\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}(2a^{1-s})^{j}.

For each
1\leq j\leq N
and
1\leq k\leq 2^{j-1}
, there exists
C_{j,k}>0
such that

2\int_{I_{j,k}}\int_{C_{a}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{1+s}}dydx\geq C_{j,k}a^{j(1-s)}.

Such a
C_{j,k}
exists since
\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a})>0
. Therefore, letting

C:=\min\{C_{s},C_{j,k}:1\leq j\leq N,\,1\leq k\leq 2^{j-1}\},

which depends only on
s
and
a
, we have that

\|\chi_{C_{a}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(I)}\geq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(2a^{1-s})^{j}.\qed

By using Proposition
4.3
, we can now provide examples illustrating the sharpness of Theorem
1.2
, and that the converses of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.1
do not hold in general.

Example 4.8
.

For each
0<a<1/3
, consider the Cantor set
C_{a}
constructed above. The regularized boundary of
C_{a}
is given by

\partial^{+}C_{a}=C_{a}.

Then,

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial^{+}C_{a})=\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a})=\frac{1-3a}{1-2a}>0,

and so
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{+}C_{a})=\infty
for all
0<s<1
. However, by Proposition
4.3
, we can choose
0<a<1/3
and
0<s<1
such that
\chi_{C_{a}}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(I)
. This shows that Theorem
1.2
is sharp in the sense that we cannot strengthen the conclusion by replacing
\partial^{*}E
with
\partial^{+}E
, or even replacing
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{*}E)=0
with
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{+}E)<\infty
.

Example 4.9
.

For each
0<a<1/3
, consider the Cantor set
C_{a}
, and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the endpoints of the open intervals
I_{j,k}
considered above. Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a})>0
, we see from the construction that

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(B(x,r)\setminus C_{a})}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(B(x,r))}=0

for all
x\in C_{a}\setminus\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}.
Therefore,
\partial^{*}C_{a}=\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, and as a countable set, it follows that

\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{*}C_{a})=0

for all
0<s<1
.

Now, fix
0<a_{0}<1/4
. We can then choose
0<s_{0}<1
sufficiently close to
1
so that
2a_{0}^{1-s_{0}}>1
. Since
a_{0}^{1/(1+s_{0})}<a_{0}^{1/2}<1/2
, the hypotheses of Proposition
4.3
are satisfied, and so
\chi_{C_{a_{0}}}\not\in B^{s_{0}}_{1,1}(I)
. However, by the above discussion, we have that
\mathcal{H}^{-s_{0}}(\partial^{*}C_{a_{0}})=0
. Thus the converse of Theorem
1.2
does not hold in general.

Example 4.10
.

Let
0<a<1/3
and
0<t<1
. For
r>0
, consider a countable cover
\{B(y_{j},r)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\partial^{*}C_{a}=\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that
y_{j}\in\partial^{*}C_{a}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
. If
z_{1}\in C_{a}\setminus\bigcup_{j}B(y_{j},r)
, then by the density of
\partial^{*}C_{a}
in
C_{a}
, we have that
\dist(z_{1},\bigcup_{j}B(y_{j},r))=0
. Hence if
z_{2}\in C_{a}\setminus\bigcup_{j}B(y_{j},r)
such that
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, then
d(z_{1},z_{2})\geq 2r
. Therefore
C_{a}\setminus\bigcup_{j}B(y_{j},r)
is a finite set. Thus, we have that

\sum_{j}r^{-t}\mathcal{H}^{1}(B(y_{j},r))\geq r^{-t}\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a}),

and so since
\{B(y_{j},r)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is arbitrary, we have that
\mathcal{M}^{-t}_{r}(\partial^{*}C_{a})\geq r^{-t}\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a})
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}(C_{a})>0
, it follows that
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{*}C_{a})=\infty
. However by Proposition
4.3
, we can choose
0<a<1/3
and
0<s<1/2
so that
\chi_{C_{a}}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(I)
. Therefore, the converse to Theorem
1.1
does not hold in general for any
0<t<1
, nor even with the weaker conclusion that
\underline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{*}E)<\infty
.

Example 4.11
.

It was shown in
[
48
]
that the von Koch snowflake domain
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is such that

\overline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(\partial^{+}K)=\codim_{F}(K)=\codim_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial^{*}K)=2-\log 4/\log 3.

That is, (
1.4
) holds with equality in some cases. However, the fat Cantor sets constructed above show that these inequalities may be strict. For example, let
a=1/4
and consider
C_{a}
. By Proposition
4.3
, we see that
\chi_{C_{a}}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(I)
if and only if
0<s<1/2
, and so
\codim_{F}(C_{a})=1/2
. However,
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial^{*}C_{a})=0
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-s}(\partial^{+}C_{a})=\infty
for all
0<s<1
. Hence, we have that

0=\overline{\codim}_{\mathcal{M}}(\partial^{+}C_{a})<\codim_{F}(C_{a})<\codim_{\mathcal{H}}(\partial^{*}C_{a})=1.

5.
Existence of minimizers of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}

Throughout this section, we assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete metric measure space, with
\mu
a doubling Borel regular measure. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain, that is, a bounded connected open set. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
X\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
. Otherwise, the existence results of this section hold trivially. Let
F\subset X
be a measurable set such that
F\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
, and let
0<s<1
. We consider the functional

\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E):=L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,X\setminus E)+L_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,\Omega\setminus E)

and the associated minimization problem defined in Section
2.6
and Definition
2.22
, and prove existence of minimizers of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
by the direct method of calculus of variations. We first note that
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to
L^{1}
-convergence by Fatou’s lemma, see
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
E_{k}
and
E
are subsets of
X
such that
\chi_{E_{k}}\to\chi_{E}
in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
as
k\to\infty
, then

\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E_{k}).

The following compactness theorem is an adaption of
[
18
, Theorem 7.1]
, proved there in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To prove the result in the doubling metric measure space setting, we will use the discrete convolution technique, obtained from the Lipschitz partition of unity given by Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of functions on
\Omega
such that for all compact sets
K\subset\Omega
,

(5.3)

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(K)}<\infty,

and also that

(5.4)

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(K)}<\infty.

Then, for any sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathcal{F}
, there exists
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and a subsequence, not relabeled, so that
f_{k}\to f
in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and pointwise
\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}\Subset\Omega
, and let
K\subset\Omega
be compact such that
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset K
. For each
0<\varepsilon<\dist(K,X\setminus\Omega)/4
, there exists by Lemma
2.3
, a countable cover
\{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
of
X
by
balls of radius
\varepsilon
with bounded overlap, such that collection
\{\frac{1}{5}B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in I}
is pairwise disjoint. Let
\{\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in I}
be a Lipschitz partition of unity subordinate to this cover, given by Lemma
2.4
.

Let
J:=\{i\in I:B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\cap K\neq\varnothing\}
. Since
K
is bounded and since
\{\frac{1}{5}B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in J}
is pairwise disjoint, the doubling property of
\mu
ensures that
J
is a finite set. Thus, there exists
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
so that, relabeling if necessary, we can denumerate this collection as follows:

\{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in J}=\{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}.

By Lemma
2.4
, it follows that for each
x\in K
,

(5.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)=1.

By the above upper bound on
\varepsilon
, we can choose a compact set
\widetilde{K}\Subset\Omega
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}2B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\subset\widetilde{K}
. For each
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we define the discrete convolution
f_{\varepsilon}
of
f
by

f_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}.

We also define the vector

R(f)=\left(\int_{B_{1}^{\varepsilon}}f\,d\mu,\dots,\int_{B_{N^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}}f\,d\mu\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\varepsilon}}.

By the bounded overlap of the cover
\{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}
and (
5.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\|R(f)\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|f|d\mu\lesssim\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}B^{\varepsilon}_{i}}|f|d\mu\leq\int_{\widetilde{K}}|f|d\mu\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\widetilde{K})}<\infty.

Therefore, we have that

R(\mathcal{F})
is a totally bounded set in
(\mathbb{R}^{N_{\varepsilon}},\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{1}})
, and so for every
\eta>0
, there exists
M_{\eta}\in\mathbb{N}
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{M_{\eta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\varepsilon}}
such that for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,M_{\eta}\}
such that

(5.6)

\|R(f)-b_{j}\|_{1}<\eta.

Let
\eta:=\varepsilon/(N_{\varepsilon}+1)
.
For each
j\in\{1,\dots,M_{\eta}\}
, let
b_{j}=(b_{1,j},\dots,b_{N_{\varepsilon},j})
, and define
\beta_{j}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\beta_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\frac{b_{i,j}}{\mu(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}.

For
f\in\mathcal{F}
, let
j\in\{1,\dots,M_{\eta}\}
be such that (
5.6
) holds. We then have that

(5.7)

\|f-\beta_{j}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leq\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(K)}+\|f_{\varepsilon}-\beta_{j}\|_{L^{1}(K)}.

By (
5.5
), we have that

\displaystyle\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}\cap K}|f-f_{\varepsilon}|d\mu\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}\cap K}|f(x)-f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)d\mu(x).

Letting
I_{k}:=\{i\in\{1,\dots,N_{\varepsilon}\}:2B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\cap B_{k}^{\varepsilon}\neq\varnothing\}
, we have that

\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}\cap K}|f-f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|d\mu\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\fint_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|f(x)-f(y)|d\mu(y)d\mu(x).

For
x\in B_{k}^{\varepsilon}
and
i\in I_{k}
, we then have by the doubling property of
\mu
that

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|f(x)-f(y)|d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{s}\frac{\mu(B(x,5\varepsilon))}{\mu(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{s}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y).

Substituting this into the previous expression, we have by bounded overlap of
\{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}
,

\displaystyle\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{s}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{s}\int_{\widetilde{K}}\int_{\widetilde{K}}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{s}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(\widetilde{K})}.

Since we have chosen
j\in\{1,\dots,M_{\eta}\}
so that (
5.6
) holds, we have from the doubling property of
\mu
and choice of
\eta
that

\displaystyle\|f_{\varepsilon}-\beta_{j}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}|f_{\varepsilon}-\beta_{j}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}|f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}-b_{i,j}/\mu(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})|\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\frac{1}{\mu(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}\left|\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}f\,d\mu-b_{i,j}\right|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\fint_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}f\,d\mu-b_{i,j}\right|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\fint_{B_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\|R(f)-b_{j}\|_{\ell^{1}}\,d\mu<N_{\varepsilon}\eta<\varepsilon.

Combining this estimate with (
5
) and (
5.7
), we have that

\|f-\beta_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq\|f-\beta_{j}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{s}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(\widetilde{K})}+\varepsilon.

Recall that the compact set
\widetilde{K}
is chosen independently of
\varepsilon>0
, and

C:=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(\widetilde{K})}<\infty

by (
5.4
).
Therefore, we have shown that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
M_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{M_{\varepsilon}}\in L^{1}(\widetilde{\Omega})
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,M_{\varepsilon}\}
such that

\|f-\beta_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\lesssim C\varepsilon^{s}+\varepsilon,

with comparison constant depending only on
s
and the doubling constant of
\mu
.
That is,
\mathcal{F}
is totally bounded, and hence precompact, in
L^{1}(\widetilde{\Omega})
, by completeness of
L^{1}(\widetilde{\Omega})
.

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathcal{F}
. Let
\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{i}
be an exhaustion of
\Omega
by an increasing sequence of open sets
\Omega_{i}\Subset\Omega
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is precompact in
L^{1}(\Omega_{1})
, there exists a subsequence
\{f_{1,k}\}_{k}\subset\{f_{k}\}_{k}
and
g_{1}
such that
f_{1,k}\to g_{1}
in
L^{1}(\Omega_{1})
as
k\to\infty
. We choose
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|f_{1,k_{1}}-g_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1})}<2^{-1}.

Inductively, for
i\geq 2
, there exists by precompactness of
\mathcal{F}
on
\Omega_{i}
, a subsequence
\{f_{i,k}\}_{k}\subset\{f_{i-1,k}\}_{k=k_{i-1}}^{\infty}
and a function
g_{i}
such that
f_{i,k}\to g_{i}
in
L^{1}(\Omega_{i})
as
k\to\infty
. Furthermore, there exists
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|f_{i,k_{i}}-g_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{i})}<2^{-i}.

Since the subsequences are nested in this manner, we have that
g_{i}=g_{i-1}

\mu
-a.e. on
\Omega_{i-1}
. We then define
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
by
f(x)=g_{i}(x)
if
x\in\Omega_{i}
. We claim that
f_{i,k_{i}}\to f
in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and pointwise
\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
. Indeed, for
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{f_{i,k_{i}}\}_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}
is a subsequence of
\{f_{i_{0},k}\}_{k}
, and
f_{i_{0},k}\to f
in
L^{1}(\Omega_{i_{0}})
as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, by the monotone convergence theorem and our choice of

, we have that

\int_{\Omega_{i_{0}}}\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}|f_{i,k_{i}}-f|d\mu=\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{i_{0}}}|f_{i,k_{i}}-f|d\mu<\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-i}<\infty.

Hence,
\sum_{i=i_{0}}^{\infty}|f_{i,k_{i}}-f|<\infty

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega_{i_{0}}
, and so
f_{i,k_{i}}\to f
pointwise
\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega_{i_{0}}
as

. Since

is arbitrary, and
\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{i}
with
\Omega_{i}\Subset\Omega
, this concludes the proof.
∎

We define the following collection of measurable sets:

\mathcal{A}(F):=\{E\subset X:E\setminus\Omega=F\setminus\Omega,\,\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)<\infty\}.

Theorem 5.9
.

If
\mathcal{A}(F)\neq\varnothing
, then there exists a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}(F)\neq\varnothing
, we have that
\beta:=\inf\{\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E):E\in\mathcal{A}(F)\}<\infty
. Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{A}(F)
such that
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E_{k})\to\beta
as
k\to\infty
. Hence we have that

\sup_{k}\left(\|\chi_{E_{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\chi_{E_{k}}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(\Omega)}\right)<\infty

as
\Omega
is bounded,
and so by Theorem
5.2
, there exists
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and a subsequence
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
, not relabeled, such that
\chi_{E_{k}}\to f

in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and pointwise
\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
as

. Thus, we can take
f=\chi_{\widetilde{E}}
for some set
\widetilde{E}\subset\Omega
.

Let
\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{i}
be an exhaustion of
\Omega
by an increasing sequence of open sets
\Omega_{i}\Subset\Omega
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\Omega
is bounded, there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{i})<\varepsilon
. We then have that

\int_{\Omega}|\chi_{E_{k}}-\chi_{\widetilde{E}}|d\mu=\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\chi_{E_{k}}-f|d\mu+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{i}}|\chi_{E_{k}}-\chi_{\widetilde{E}}|d\mu<\int_{\Omega_{i}}|\chi_{E_{k}}-f|d\mu+\varepsilon.

Since
\chi_{E_{k}}\to f
in

, we have that
\chi_{E_{k}}\to\chi_{\widetilde{E}}
in

. Letting

, we then have that
\chi_{E_{k}}\to\chi_{E}
in

, as

in

for all

. By Proposition

, it follows that

\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E_{k})=\beta<\infty.

As

, it follows that

Remark 5.10
.

We note that Theorem
1.1
gives us a sufficient condition on
\Omega
for existence of solutions. Namely, if
(X,d,\mu)
is a doubling metric measure space satisfying the LLC-1 condition, and if
\Omega\subset X
is bounded and there exists
t>s
such that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{-t}(\partial^{+}\Omega)<\infty
, then for any measurable set
F\subset X
with

, we have that

,

\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(F\setminus\Omega)=L_{s}(F\setminus\Omega,\Omega)\leq L_{s}(X\setminus\Omega,\Omega)\simeq\|\chi_{\Omega}\|_{B^{s}_{1,1}(X)}<\infty,

and so

. We compare this to the existence result from
[

]
in

.

6.
Regularity of minimizers of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}

Throughout this section, we assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, connected metric measure space, with
\mu
a doubling Borel regular measure. We let
0<s<1
and assume that
\Omega\subset X
is a bounded domain. As before, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
X\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
. Otherwise, constant functions are the only minimizers of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
, and the results in this section hold trivially. We prove uniform density estimates and porosity of minimizers of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
, analogous to the results obtained in the Euclidean setting in
[
15
, Theorem 4.1,Corollary 4.3]
. In the metric setting, similar regularity results were obtained in
[
42
, Theorems 4.2,5.2]
for minimizers of the (local) perimeter functional when the space is doubling and supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality. We emphasize here that the energy functional
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
is nonlocal, unlike in
[
42
]
.

6.1.
Uniform density estimates

In
[
42
]
, the authors obtained uniform density estimates for the (local) perimeter minimizers by means of the
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality and the De Giorgi method. Since we are concerned with the nonlocal case, we do not need to assume that
(X,d,\mu)
supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality. Instead, we will make use of the fractional Poincaré inequality given by Theorem
2.10
, which does not impose the geometric restrictions that the
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality imposes on the metric measure space. In the statement of Theorem
2.10
, it is assumed that
(X,d,\mu)
is reverse doubling; since we assume that
X
is connected and
\mu
is doubling, this condition is satisfied
[
7
, Corollary 3.8]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
E\subset X
be a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
. Modifying
E
by a set of measure zero if necessary, we may assume by Lemma
2.7
that
\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)>0
and
\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)>0
for each
x\in\partial E
and
r>0
. Note that such a modification on a set of measure zero does not affect the property of being a minimizer. Let
x_{0}\in\partial E\cap\Omega
and
R_{0}>0
be such that
B(x_{0},2R_{0})\subset\Omega
. We prove the first inequality of (
1.6
), as the second inequality follows from the first and the fact that the complement of a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
is also a minimizer. This is due to the fact that
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)=\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(X\setminus E)
, by symmetry of the kernel
K_{s}
.

Let
z\in\Omega
and
R>0
be such that
B(z,4R)\subset\Omega.

For each
0<r<R
, let

u=\chi_{B(z,r)\cap E}.

Since
X
is connected and
X\setminus\Omega\neq\varnothing
, there exists
y\in\partial B(z,3r/2)
, and so
B(y,r/2)\subset B(z,2r)
and
B(y,r/2)\cap B(z,r)=\varnothing
. Since
\mu
is doubling, it then follows that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(B(z,2r)\cap\{|u|>0\})}{\mu(B(z,2r))}\leq\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{\mu(B(z,2r))}\leq\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{\mu(B(z,r))+\mu(B(y,r/2))}\leq 1/(1+C_{\mu}^{-3})<1.

By the doubling property of
\mu
and by applying Lemma
2.11
along with (
2.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,r))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{B(z,2r)}|u|^{Q/(Q-s)}d\mu\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r^{s}}{\mu(B(z,r))}\int_{B(z,4r)}\int_{B(z,4r)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{r^{s}}{\mu(B(z,r))}\int_{B(z,r)\cap E}\int_{X\setminus(B(z,r)\cap E)}\frac{d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{r^{s}}{\mu(B(z,r))}L_{s}(B(z,r)\cap E,X\setminus(B(z,r)\cap E)).

Setting
A=B(z,r)\cap E
, we have by Lemma
2.23
that

\displaystyle L_{s}(A,X\setminus A)=L_{s}(A,X\setminus E)+L_{s}(A,E\setminus A)\lesssim L_{s}(A,E\setminus A)\leq L_{s}(A,X\setminus B(z,r)).

Substituting this into the above inequality along with (
2.20
) then yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,r))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}\lesssim\frac{r^{s}}{\mu(B(z,r))}\int_{B(z,r)\cap E}\int_{X\setminus B(z,r)}\frac{d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))}.

Setting
r_{x}:=r-d(z,x)
for each
x\in E\cap B(z,r)
, we have by the doubling property of
\mu
that

\displaystyle\int_{X\setminus B(z,r)}\frac{d\mu(y)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X\setminus B(x,r_{x})}\frac{d\mu(y)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{m+1}r_{x})\setminus B(x,2^{m}r_{x})}\frac{d\mu(y)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(r-d(z,x))^{s}}.

Substituting this into the previous expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r^{s}}\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,r))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(z,r)\cap E}\frac{1}{(r-d(z,x))^{s}}d\mu(x).

Integrating both sides of the above expression with respect to
r
, and using Tonelli’s theorem gives

\displaystyle\int_{R/2}^{R}\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r^{s}}\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,r))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{R}\int_{B(z,r)\cap E}\frac{1}{(r-d(z,x))^{s}}d\mu(x)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\int_{B(z,R)}\frac{\chi_{E}(x)\chi_{B(z,r)}(x)}{(r-d(z,x))^{s}}d\mu(x)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{B(z,R)}\chi_{E}(x)\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\chi_{B(z,r)}(x)}{(r-d(z,x))^{s}}drd\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(z,R)}\chi_{E}(x)\int_{d(z,x)}^{R}\frac{1}{(r-d(z,x))^{s}}drd\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{1-s}\mu(B(z,R)\cap E).

By the doubling property of
\mu
, we estimate the left-hand side of the above expression from below by

\displaystyle\int_{R/2}^{R}\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r^{s}}\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,r))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}dr

\displaystyle\gtrsim R^{1-s}\mu(B(z,R))\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,R/2)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,R/2))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}.

Combining this with the previous expression, we obtain

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,R/2)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,R/2))}\right)^{(Q-s)/Q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\frac{\mu(B(z,R)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,R))}

for all
z\in\Omega
and
R>0
such that
B(z,4R)\subset\Omega
, where the constant
C_{0}\geq 1
depends only on
s
and the doubling constant of
\mu
.

Now consider
x_{0}\in\partial E\cap\Omega
and
R_{0}>0
fixed at the beginning of the proof. Suppose that

(6.3)

\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})\cap E)}{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})}<\gamma_{0}:=\frac{1}{2^{Q+1}C_{0}^{Q/s}C_{Q}}.

Then, for
z\in B(x_{0},R_{0}/4)
, and
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
j\geq 2
, set
B_{j}:=B(z,R_{0}/2^{j})
. Setting
p:=Q/(Q-s)
, we iterate estimate (
6.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(B_{j}\cap E)}{\mu(B_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{0}\frac{\mu(B_{j-1}\cap E)}{\mu(B_{j-1})}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{p}\left(C_{0}\frac{\mu(B_{j-2}\cap E)}{\mu(B_{j-2})}\right)^{p^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{p+p^{2}+\cdots+p^{j-1}}\left(\frac{\mu(B(z,R_{0}/2)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z,R_{0}/2))}\right)^{p^{j-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}^{p+p^{2}+\cdots+p^{j-1}}\left(2^{Q}C_{Q}\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})\cap E)}{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}))}\right)^{p^{j-1}}\leq(2^{Q}C_{0}^{Q/s}C_{Q}\gamma_{0})^{p^{j-1}}.

By our choice of
\gamma_{0}
, we have that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\fint_{B_{j}}\chi_{E}\,d\mu=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\mu(B_{j}\cap E)}{\mu(B_{j})}=0.

Since
z\in B(x_{0},R_{0}/4)
is arbitrary, it follows from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem that
\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}/4)\cap E)=0
. However, this is a contradiction, as
x_{0}\in\partial E
, and we have assumed that
\mu(B(x_{0},R)\cap E)>0
for all
R>0
. Hence,

\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})\cap E)}{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}))}\geq\gamma_{0}.\qed

Remark 6.4
.

One can obtain the conclusion of Theorem
1.5
by replacing the assumption that

]

). Under this assumption, however, the constant

Moreover, since we are assuming that

) follows by the same argument in the proof of Theorem

and
\Omega\setminus B(z,2r)\neq\varnothing
. In this case, connectedness of
\Omega
and the argument used to obtain (
6.1
), gives

\frac{\mu(B(z,\rho/2))}{\mu(B(z,\rho))}\leq 1/(1+C_{\mu}^{-3})

for all
0<\rho<2r
. Hence, we have the fractional Poincaré inequality on the desired ball, and can apply Lemma
2.11
as we did in the above proof. For simplicity, and since we assume connectedness, via the LLC-1 condition, in Theorem
1.7
below, we have kept the assumption of connectedness in the statement of Theorem
1.5
.

6.2.
Porosity

By using Theorem
1.5
, we now prove the porosity result Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
E\subset X
be a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
, possibly modified on a set of measure zero so that the conclusion to Theorem
1.5
holds. Let
x_{0}\in\partial E\cap\Omega
and
R_{0}>0
be such that
B(x_{0},2R_{0})\subset\Omega
. We prove the first containment of (
1.8
), as the second follows from the first and the fact that the complement of a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
is a minimizer.

For each
R_{0}/2\leq r\leq R_{0}
, let

\widetilde{\rho}_{r}=\inf\{\rho>0:B(y,\rho)\setminus E\neq\varnothing\text{ for all }y\in B(x_{0},r)\cap E\}.

Let
C_{L}\geq 1
denote the constant from the LLC-1 condition, see Definition
2.5
. If
\widetilde{\rho}_{r}>r/(20C_{L})
, then there exists
y\in B(x_{0},R_{0})\cap E
such that
B(y,R_{0}/(40C_{L}))\subset E\cap\Omega
, and so we can take
C=40C_{L}
. Thus, without loss of generality, we assume that
\widetilde{\rho}_{r}\leq r/(20C_{L})
for each
R_{0}/2\leq r\leq R_{0}
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<R_{0}/(12C_{L})
, and let
\rho_{r}:=2C_{L}(\widetilde{\rho}_{r}+\varepsilon)
. By the 5-covering lemma, we can cover
B(x_{0},r)\cap E
by balls
\{B(y_{i},5\rho_{r})\}_{i}
, with
y_{i}\in B(x_{0},r)\cap E
, such that the collection
\{B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\}_{i}
is pairwise disjoint. By Theorem
1.5
and doubling, we have that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\mu(B(x_{0},r))\leq\mu(B(x_{0},r)\cap E)\leq\sum_{i}\mu(B(y_{i},5\rho_{r}))\lesssim\sum_{i}\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})).

By the definition of
\widetilde{\rho}_{r}
, there exists
z\in B(y_{i},\widetilde{\rho}_{r}+\varepsilon)\setminus E
, and by the LLC-1 condition, we can join
y_{i}
and
z
by a connected set inside the
B(y_{i},C_{L}(\widetilde{\rho}_{r}+\varepsilon))
. Therefore, there exists
z^{\prime}\in\partial E\cap B(y_{i},C_{L}(\widetilde{\rho}_{r}+\varepsilon))=\partial E\cap B(y_{i},\rho_{r}/2)
. By our choices, we have that
B(z^{\prime},\rho_{r})\subset\Omega
, and so by Theorem
1.5
and the doubling property of
\mu
,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E)}{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r}))}\gtrsim\frac{\mu(B(z^{\prime},\rho_{r}/2)\cap E)}{\mu(B(z^{\prime},\rho_{r}/2))}\geq\gamma_{0}.

Similarly, Theorem
1.5
gives us

(6.7)

\frac{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\setminus E)}{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r}))}\gtrsim\gamma_{0}.

By (
2.20
), the doubling property of
\mu
, as well as (
6.7
) and (
6.6
), it follows that

\displaystyle L_{s}(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E,\,B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\setminus E)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E}\int_{B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\setminus E}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\rho_{r}^{s}}\int_{B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E}\frac{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x,2\rho_{r}))}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\rho_{r}^{s}}\int_{B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E}\frac{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r}))}{\mu(B(x,2\rho_{r}))}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\gamma_{0}\frac{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E)}{\rho_{r}^{s}}\gtrsim\gamma_{0}^{2}\frac{\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r}))}{\rho_{r}^{s}}.

From (
6.5
), it then follows that

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}L_{s}(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E,B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\setminus E)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\rho_{r}^{s}}\sum_{i}\mu(B(y_{i},\rho_{r}))\gtrsim\gamma_{0}^{3}\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},r))}{\rho_{r}^{s}}.

By Lemma
2.23
, it follows that

L_{s}(B(x_{0},2r)\cap E,X\setminus E)\lesssim L_{s}(B(x_{0},2r)\cap E,E\setminus B(x_{0},2r)),

and so by disjointness of
\{B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\}_{i}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}L_{s}(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E,B(y_{i},\rho_{r})

\displaystyle\setminus E)\leq\sum_{i}L_{s}(B(y_{i},\rho_{r})\cap E,X\setminus E)

\displaystyle\leq L_{s}(B(x_{0},2r)\cap E,X\setminus E)

(6.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim L_{s}(B(x_{0},2r)\cap E,E\setminus B(x_{0},2r))\leq L_{s}(B(x_{0},2r),X\setminus B(x_{0},2r)).

For each
x\in B(x_{0},2r),
let
r_{x}:=2r-d(x_{0},x)
. By the doubling property of
\mu
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{X\setminus B(x_{0},2r)}\frac{d\mu(y)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X\setminus B(x,r_{x})}\frac{d\mu(y)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{m+1}r_{x})\setminus B(x,2^{m}r_{x})}\frac{d\mu(y)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(2r-d(x_{0},x))^{s}},

from which we obtain, by (
2.20
),

\displaystyle L_{s}(B(x_{0},2r),X\setminus B(x_{0},2r))

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{B(x_{0},2r)}\int_{X\setminus B(x_{0},2r)}\frac{d\mu(y)d\mu(x)}{d(x,y)^{s}\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(x_{0},2r)}\frac{1}{(2r-d(x,x_{0}))^{s}}d\mu(x).

Combining this inequality with (
6.8
) and (
6.2
), and noting that
\rho_{r}\leq\rho_{R_{0}}
by definition, we obtain by the doubling property of
\mu
,

\gamma_{0}^{3}\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}))}{\rho_{R_{0}}^{s}}\lesssim\int_{B(x_{0},2r)}\frac{1}{(2r-d(x,x_{0}))^{s}}d\mu(x)

for each
R_{0}/2\leq r\leq R_{0}
.
Integrating both sides with respect to
r
, and using Tonelli’s theorem and the doubling property of
\mu
, we have that

\displaystyle R_{0}\gamma_{0}^{3}\frac{\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0}))}{\rho_{R_{0}}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\int_{B(x_{0},2r)}\frac{1}{(2r-d(x,x_{0}))^{s}}d\mu(x)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\int_{B(x_{0},2R_{0})}\frac{\chi_{B(x_{0},2r)}(x)}{(2r-d(x,x_{0}))^{s}}d\mu(x)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{0},2R_{0})}\int_{d(x,x_{0})/2}^{R_{0}}\frac{1}{(2r-d(x,x_{0}))^{s}}drd\mu(x)\lesssim R_{0}^{1-s}\mu(B(x_{0},R_{0})).

Therefore, there exists a constant
C_{0}
depending only on
\gamma_{0}
,
s
, and the doubling constant of
\mu
such that
\rho_{R_{0}}\geq R_{0}/C_{0}
, and so
\widetilde{\rho}_{R_{0}}\geq R_{0}/(2C_{L}C_{0})
, since
0<\varepsilon<R_{0}/(12C_{L})
is arbitrary. Hence, we obtain the desired constant by setting
C:=4C_{L}C_{0}
.
∎

The conclusion of Theorem
1.7
immediately tells us that if
E
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
, modified on a set of measure zero if necessary, then
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial E\cap\Omega)<\infty
. Indeed, for
\delta>0
, consider a countable cover
\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{i}
of
\partial E\cap\Omega
such that
x_{i}\in\partial E
,
r_{i}<\delta
and
B(x_{i},2r_{i})\subset\Omega
. The 5-covering lemma then gives us a pairwise disjoint subcollection
\{B(x_{i},2r_{i})\}_{i\in I\subset\mathbb{N}}
such that
\partial E\cap\Omega\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},10r_{i})
. For each
i\in I
, there exists
y_{i},z_{i}\in B(x_{i},r_{i})
such that
B^{1}_{i}:=B(y_{i},r_{i}/C)\subset E\cap\Omega
and
B^{2}_{i}:=B(z_{i},r_{i}/C)\subset\Omega\setminus E
, by Theorem
1.7
. By the doubling property of
\mu
, it then follows that

\displaystyle\infty>\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}(E)\geq L_{s}(E\cap\Omega,\Omega\cap E)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}L_{s}(B(x_{i},2r_{i})\cap E,B(x_{i},2r_{i})\setminus E)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}L_{s}(B^{1}_{i},B^{2}_{i})\gtrsim\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i}))}{r_{i}^{s}}\geq\mathcal{H}^{-s}_{\delta}(\partial E\cap\Omega).

However, the conclusion of Theorem
1.7
together with the doubling property of
\mu
implies that
\partial E\cap\Omega\subset\partial^{*}E
. Therefore, Theorem
1.2
gives us the following stronger corollary:

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a compact metric measure space satisfying the LLC-1 condition, with
\mu
a doubling measure. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain, let
0<s<1
, and let
E
be a minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\Omega}^{s}
such that
\chi_{E}\in B^{s}_{1,1}(X)
. Then, after modifying
E
on a set of measure zero if necessary, we have that
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\partial E\cap\Omega)=0
.

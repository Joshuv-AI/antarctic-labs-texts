Abstract
. We investigate necessary and sufficient conditions for the extendibility and boundedness of Gaussian curvature, Mean curvature and principal curvatures near all types of singularities on fronts. We also study the convergence to infinite limits of these geometrical invariants and show how this is tightly related to a particular property of uniform approximation of fronts by parallel surfaces.

\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}

a

\mathbf{p}\in U

\mathbf{x}
a

The behavior of Gaussian curvature, Mean curvature and principal curvatures near non-degenerate singularities on wave fronts have been widely studied in
[

,
5
,
9
,
11
]
. However, in the degenerate case this is unknown, as well as the convergence to infinite limits of these invariants has been little explored. For this reason it is natural to wonder which properties of wave fronts determine one behavior or another on general types of singularities. Also, there is a lack of literature about the geometry of singularities of rank 0 (or corank 2) on wave fronts and our approach here allow us to study them. For a singularity
\mathbf{p}
of a wavefront
\mathbf{x}
, there exists
l>0
and a neighborhood
U_{l}
of
\mathbf{p}
such
\mathbf{y}_{l}=\mathbf{x}+l\mathbf{n}

can be hold fixed for
l

\mathbf{x}
is
parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
. We will see that, this last property is determined by the convergence to infinite limits of the geometric invariants at each type of singularity and also is related with the extendibility of the principal curvatures at singularities of rank 1.

2.
Fixing notation, definitions and some basic results

In this paper, all the maps and functions are of class
C^{\infty}
. We denote
U
and
V
open sets in
\mbox{\bbb R}^{2}
when is not mentioned anything about them. Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a smooth map, we call a
tangent moving basis
(tmb) of
\mathbf{x}
a smooth map
\mathbf{\Omega}:U\to\mathcal{M}_{3\times 2}(\mbox{\bbb R})
in which the columns
\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
of the matrix
\mathbf{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{w}_{1}&\mathbf{w}_{2}\end{pmatrix}
are linearly independent smooth vector fields and
\mathbf{x}_{u},\mathbf{x}_{v}\in\left\langle{\mathbf{w}_{1}},{\mathbf{w}_{2}}\right\rangle
, where
\left\langle{},{}\right\rangle
denotes the linear span vector space. It is known that a smooth map
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
is a frontal if and only if there exist tangent moving bases of
\mathbf{x}
locally. Since we are interested in exploring local properties of frontals, we always assume that we have a global tmb
\mathbf{\Omega}
for
\mathbf{x}
. We denote by
\mathbf{n}:=\frac{\mathbf{w}_{1}\times\mathbf{w}_{2}}{\|\mathbf{w}_{1}\times\mathbf{w}_{2}\|}
the normal vector field induced by
\mathbf{\Omega}
. Let
\mathbf{f}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{n}
be a smooth map, we denote by
D\mathbf{f}:=(\frac{\partial\mathbf{f}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}})
, the differential of
\mathbf{f}
and we consider it as a smooth map
D\mathbf{f}:U\to\mathcal{M}_{n\times 2}(\mbox{\bbb R})
. We write
D\mathbf{f}_{x_{1}}
,
D\mathbf{f}_{x_{2}}
the partial derivatives of
D\mathbf{f}
and
D\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{p}):=(\frac{\partial\mathbf{f}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(\mathbf{p}))
for
\mathbf{p}\in U
. Also, vectors in
\mbox{\bbb R}^{n}
are identified as column vectors in
\mathcal{M}_{n\times 1}(\mbox{\bbb R})
and if
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{M}_{n\times n}(\mbox{\bbb R})
,
\mathbf{A}_{(i)}
is the
i^{th}
-row and
\mathbf{A}^{(j)}
is the
j^{th}
-column of
\mathbf{A}
. The trace and adjoint of a matrix are denoted by
tr()
and
adj()
respectively.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
. Denoting
()^{T}
the operation of transposing a matrix, we set the matrices of the first and second fundamental forms:

\mathbf{I}=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\
F&G\end{pmatrix}:=D\mathbf{x}^{T}D\mathbf{x},\ \mathbf{II}=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
f&g\end{pmatrix}:=-D\mathbf{x}^{T}D\mathbf{n}.

The Weingarten matrix
\bm{\alpha}:=-\mathbf{II}^{T}\mathbf{I}^{-1}
is defined in
\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
. Also, we set the matrices:

\mathbf{I}_{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}E_{\Omega}&F_{\Omega}\\
F_{\Omega}&G_{\Omega}\end{pmatrix}:=\mathbf{\Omega}^{T}\mathbf{\Omega},\ \mathbf{II}_{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}e_{\Omega}&f_{1\Omega}\\
f_{2\Omega}&g_{\Omega}\end{pmatrix}:=-\mathbf{\Omega}^{T}D\mathbf{n},

\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}:=-\mathbf{II}_{\Omega}^{T}\mathbf{I}_{\Omega}^{-1},\ \mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega}:=D\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{\Omega}(\mathbf{I}_{\Omega})^{-1},\ \bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}:=\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega}).

If
\mathbf{x}
is a frontal and
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
, we write simply
\mathbf{\Lambda}=(\lambda_{ij})
and
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{ij})
instead of
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega}
and
\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}
when there is no risk of confusion,
\lambda_{\Omega}:=det(\mathbf{\Lambda})
and
\mathfrak{T}_{\Omega}(U)
as the principal ideal generated by
\lambda_{\Omega}
in the ring
C^{\infty}(U,\mbox{\bbb R})
. The matrix
\mathbf{\Lambda}
and
\bm{\mu}
satisfy
D\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}
and
D\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}
(see
[
6
]
), thus
\Sigma(\mathbf{x})=\lambda_{\Omega}^{-1}(0)
and
rank(D\mathbf{x})=rank(\mathbf{\Lambda})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})

.

This definition does not depend on the chosen tmb
\mathbf{\Omega}
because with another tmb
\bar{\mathbf{\Omega}}
, we have
\lambda_{\bar{\Omega}}=\rho\lambda_{\Omega}
with
\rho:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}
being a
C^{\infty}
-function that never vanish.

The following propositions were proved in

]
and we are going to use these frequently.

.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}

\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
, the
\Omega
-relative curvature and the

.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tangent moving basis of
\mathbf{x}
,
K_{\Omega}
,
H_{\Omega}
,
K
and

the

-relative curvature, the

Let

.

[

3.
The relative principal curvatures

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
, we have the following equality,
\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}=\bm{\alpha}\lambda_{\Omega}
on
\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
, in particular
\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}
has real eigenvalues.

Proof.

By proposition
2.2
,
\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{I}_{\Omega}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}
and
\mathbf{II}=\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{II}_{\Omega}
, then for
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
,
\bm{\alpha}=-\mathbf{II}^{T}\mathbf{I}^{-1}=-\mathbf{II}_{\Omega}^{T}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T})^{-1}\mathbf{I}_{\Omega}^{-1}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{-1}=\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{-1}
. Thus, we have
\bm{\alpha}\lambda_{\Omega}=\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda})=\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}
. The eigenvalues of
\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}
are real if
tr(\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega})^{2}-4det(\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega})\geq 0
. As
K_{\Omega}=det(\bm{\mu}_{\Omega})
and
H_{\Omega}=-\frac{1}{2}tr(\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega})
this is equivalent to have
H_{\Omega}^{2}-\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}\geq 0
and since
H_{\Omega}^{2}-\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}(H^{2}-K)\geq 0
on
\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
, then by continuity and the density of regular points, it follows the result on
U
.
∎

Denoting the eigenvalues of
\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}
by
-k_{1\Omega}
,
-k_{2\Omega}
, then
k_{1\Omega}
,
k_{2\Omega}
satisfy the equation
k^{2}+tr(\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}^{T})k+det(\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}^{T})=0
. Since
K_{\Omega}=det(\bm{\mu}_{\Omega})
and
H_{\Omega}=-\frac{1}{2}tr(\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega})
, we have
k^{2}-2H_{\Omega}k+K_{\Omega}=0
. Thus,

k=H_{\Omega}\pm\sqrt{H_{\Omega}^{2}-\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}}

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
, we call the functions
k_{1\Omega}:=H_{\Omega}-\sqrt{H_{\Omega}^{2}-\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}}
and
k_{2\Omega}:=H_{\Omega}+\sqrt{H_{\Omega}^{2}-\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}}
the relative principal curvatures. We also define the following functions on
U-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
:

k_{1}:=\begin{cases}H-\sqrt{H^{2}-K}&\text{if }\lambda_{\Omega}>0,\\
H+\sqrt{H^{2}-K}&\text{if }\lambda_{\Omega}<0.\end{cases}

k_{2}:=\begin{cases}H+\sqrt{H^{2}-K}&\text{if }\lambda_{\Omega}>0,\\
H-\sqrt{H^{2}-K}&\text{if }\lambda_{\Omega}<0.\end{cases}

We clarify that, the principal curvatures of
\mathbf{x}
are the functions defined by
\kappa_{-}:=H-\sqrt{H^{2}-K}
and
\kappa_{+}:=H+\sqrt{H^{2}-K}
on
U-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
.

Remark 3.1
.

k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are smooth functions on
U-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and they have similar properties to the classical principal curvatures. Also their definitions do not depend on the chosen tmb
\mathbf{\Omega}
inducing the same orientation of the normal vector field
\mathbf{n}
. If another tmb
\hat{\mathbf{\Omega}}
induces an opposite orientation of
\mathbf{n}
, then the signs of these functions are opposite as well. Observe that
k_{1}k_{2}=K
and
\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{2}=H
on
U-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
. In the case of non-degenerate singularities, if we make a suitable change of coordinates
k_{1},k_{2}
coincides with those functions defined in (
[
9
]
, equation (2.6)).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tangent moving basis of
\mathbf{x}
. Then,

(1)

for
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
,
k_{1\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}k_{1}
and
k_{2\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}k_{2}
,

(2)

for
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
,
k_{1\Omega}=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\lambda_{\Omega}k_{1}
and
k_{2\Omega}=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\lambda_{\Omega}k_{2}
.

Proof.

We have that

k_{1\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}H-\sqrt{\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}H^{2}-\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}K}=\lambda_{\Omega}H-|\lambda_{\Omega}|\sqrt{H^{2}-K}=\lambda_{\Omega}k_{1}
and similarly
k_{2\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}k_{2}
on
\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
. For
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
, by smoothness of
k_{1\Omega}
,
k_{2\Omega}
and density of
\Sigma(\mathbf{x})^{c}
,
k_{1\Omega}=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\lambda_{\Omega}k_{1}
and
k_{2\Omega}=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\lambda_{\Omega}k_{2}
.
∎

In
[
6
]
(proof of proposition 3.19) was observed that making change of coordinates
\mathbf{h}
on a frontal
\mathbf{x}
and taking
\hat{\mathbf{\Omega}}:=\mathbf{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
as tmb of
\mathbf{x}\circ\mathbf{h}
, it results with new different relative curvatures
det(\mathbf{h})(K_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h})
and
det(\mathbf{h})(H_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h})
. However, if we choose the tmb
\mathbf{\Omega}^{h}:=(\mathbf{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h})D\mathbf{h}
instead of
\hat{\mathbf{\Omega}}
when we make a change of coordenates, they remain invariant.

Proposition 3.3

(Invariance property)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a frontal,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{h}:V\to U
diffeomorphism, then the new relative curvatures of
\mathbf{x}\circ\mathbf{h}
are
K_{\Omega^{h}}=K_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
and
H_{\Omega^{h}}=H_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
. In particular,
k_{1\Omega^{h}}=k_{1\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
,
k_{2\Omega^{h}}=k_{2\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
and
\lambda_{\Omega^{h}}=\lambda_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
.

Proof.

Observe that, the matrix
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega^{h}}
induced by
\mathbf{\Omega}^{h}
is
(D\mathbf{h})^{-1}(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h})D\mathbf{h}
, then
\lambda_{\Omega^{h}}=\lambda_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
and
since
D(\mathbf{n}\circ\mathbf{h})=\mathbf{\Omega}^{h}\bm{\mu}_{\Omega^{h}}^{T}
, we have
(D\mathbf{h})^{-1}\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}^{T}(D\mathbf{h})=\bm{\mu}_{\Omega^{h}}^{T}
and therefore
K_{\Omega^{h}}=K_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
. Also,
\bm{\mu}_{\Omega^{h}}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega^{h}})=det(D\mathbf{h})D\mathbf{h}^{-1}\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega})adj(D\mathbf{h}^{-1})
and using that
tr(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C})=tr(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})
we get
H_{\Omega^{h}}=H_{\Omega}\circ\mathbf{h}
. Since,
k_{1\Omega^{h}}
and
k_{2\Omega^{h}}
are written in terms of
K_{\Omega^{h}}
and
H_{\Omega^{h}}
, the result follows for them.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
, for each
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
there exist locally an embedding
\mathbf{y}_{l}:V\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
,
\mathbf{p}\in V
, such that
D\mathbf{y}_{l}
is a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
, the matrix
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{D\mathbf{y}_{l}}
determined for this tmb is
\mathbb{I}-l\bm{\alpha}_{l}
, where
l\in\mbox{\bbb R}^{+}
and
\bm{\alpha}_{l}
is the Weingarden matrix of
\mathbf{y}_{l}
and
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix.

Proof.

For each
t\in\mbox{\bbb R}
, consider
\mathbf{y}_{t}=\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{n}
, as
D\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}
and
D\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}
we have
D\mathbf{y}_{t}=\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}+t\mathbf{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}
, then
\mathbf{y}_{t}
has a singularity at
\mathbf{q}
if and only if
det(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}+t\bm{\mu}^{T})(\mathbf{q})=0
. Making a direct computation
det(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}+t\bm{\mu}^{T})=\lambda_{\Omega}-2tH_{\Omega}+t^{2}K_{\Omega}
and now taking
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
, by corollary (
2.1
), there exist
l\in\mbox{\bbb R}^{+}
such that
det(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}+l\bm{\mu}^{T})(\mathbf{p})=-2lH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})+l^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
. Thus, there exist a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
such that
\mathbf{y}_{l}:V\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
is an embedding. Since,
D\mathbf{y}_{l}=\mathbf{\Omega}(\mathbf{\Lambda}+l\bm{\mu})
,
D\mathbf{y}_{l}
is a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
. We can assume
D\mathbf{y}_{l}
and
\mathbf{\Omega}
induce the same normal vector
\mathbf{n}
(i.e
det(\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}+l\bm{\mu}^{T})>0
on
V
), otherwise we can change the order of column in
\mathbf{\Omega}
from the beginning. Therefore, we have
D\mathbf{y}_{l}(\mathbb{I}-l\bm{\alpha}^{T}_{l})=D\mathbf{y}_{l}-lD\mathbf{n}=D\mathbf{x}=D\mathbf{y}_{l}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{D\mathbf{y}_{l}}^{T}
, thus
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{D\mathbf{y}_{l}}=\mathbb{I}-l\bm{\alpha}_{l}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{\Omega}=D\mathbf{y}_{l}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
as above, then

(1)

\mathbf{I}_{\Omega}=\mathbf{I}_{l}
,
\mathbf{II}_{\Omega}=\mathbf{II}_{l}
,
\bm{\mu}=\bm{\alpha}_{l}
and
\bm{\mu}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda})=\bm{\alpha}_{l}-lK_{l}\mathbb{I}
.

(2)

K_{\Omega}=K_{l}
,
H_{\Omega}=H_{l}+K_{l}l
and
\lambda_{\Omega}=1+2H_{l}l+K_{l}l^{2}
.

(3)

k_{1\Omega}=k_{1l}(1+lk_{2l})
and
k_{2\Omega}=k_{2l}(1+lk_{1l})
.

where
\mathbf{I}_{l}
,
\mathbf{II}_{l}
,
K_{l}
,
H_{l}
,
k_{1l}
,
k_{2l}
are first fundamental form, second fundamental form, Gaussian curvature, mean curvature and principal curvatures of
\mathbf{y}_{l}
respectively. Additionally,
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=1
if and only if
\mathbf{y}_{l}
is free of umbilical point on a neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
. Similarly,
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=0
if and only if
\mathbf{y}_{l}
has a umbilical point at
\mathbf{p}
of positive Gaussian curvature.

Proof.

(1)

Applying the definition directly we get the first three equalities. By lemma
3.1

\mathbf{\Lambda}=\mathbb{I}-l\bm{\alpha}_{l}
, then
\bm{\mu}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda})=\bm{\alpha}_{l}(\mathbb{I}-ladj(\bm{\alpha}_{l}))=\bm{\alpha}_{l}-lK_{l}\mathbb{I}
.

(2)

Using item
(1)
,
K_{\Omega}=det(\bm{\mu})=det(\bm{\alpha}_{l})=K_{l}
,
H_{\Omega}=-\frac{1}{2}tr(\bm{\mu}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda}))=-\frac{1}{2}tr(\bm{\alpha}_{l}-lK_{l}\mathbb{I})=H_{l}+K_{l}l
and
\lambda_{\Omega}=det(\mathbf{\Lambda})=det(\mathbb{I}-l\bm{\alpha}_{l})=1+2H_{l}l+K_{l}l^{2}
.

(3)

Using the formulas in definition
3.1
, item
(2)
and knowing that
k_{1l}=H_{l}-\sqrt{H_{l}^{2}-K_{l}}
,
k_{2l}=H_{l}+\sqrt{H_{l}^{2}-K_{l}}
and
K_{l}=k_{1l}k_{2l}
, a simple computation leads to item
(3)
.

For the last part, by proposition
2.3

rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=0
if and only if
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
,
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
and
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
. On the other hand these conditions are equivalent to
H_{l}(\mathbf{p})=-K_{l}(\mathbf{p})l
and
0=1-2l^{2}K_{l}(\mathbf{p})+l^{2}K_{l}(\mathbf{p})
which is equivalent to
K_{l}(\mathbf{p})=\frac{1}{l^{2}}
and
H_{l}(\mathbf{p})=-\frac{1}{l}
. Then
\mathbf{y}_{l}
has an umbilical point at
\mathbf{p}
of positive Gaussian curvature. Conversely, we have
0<K_{l}(\mathbf{p})=H_{l}(\mathbf{p})^{2}
, then
0=1+2lH_{l}(\mathbf{p})+l^{2}H_{l}(\mathbf{p})^{2}
which imply
H_{l}(\mathbf{p})=-\frac{1}{l}
and therefore
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=-\frac{1}{l}+\frac{1}{l^{2}}l=0
, by proposition
2.3

rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=0
. Equivalently
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=1
if and only if
\mathbf{p}
is not a umbilical point which is equivalent to have
\mathbf{y}_{l}
free of umbilical point on a neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with

(1)

(k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p}),k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p}))\neq(0,0)
. In particular, if
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
(resp.
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
), then
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
(resp.
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
). Also,
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})<0
(resp.
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})>0
) if and only if
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
(resp.
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
).

(2)

There is an open neighborhood
V\subset U
of
\mathbf{p}
in which one of the functions
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
has a
C^{\infty}
extension to
V
. More precisely,
k_{1}
(resp.
k_{2}

extension if only if

(resp.
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})
).

(3)

One of the functions
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
in absolute value converge to
\infty
. More precisely,
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|k_{1}|=\infty
(resp.
|k_{2}|
) if and only if
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
(resp.
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})
).

(4)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|H|=\infty
.

(5)

If
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
then
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|K|=\infty
.

Proof.

(1)

Observe that, for
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
,
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})-|H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})|
and
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})+|H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})|
. By proposition
2.3
,
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
, then just one of

,
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})
is different of zero. Thus, the sub index of
k_{i\Omega}(\mathbf{p})
corresponding to the non-zero value is determined bijectively by the sign of
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})
.

(2)

By item
(1)
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
. Let
V
a neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
such that
k_{1\Omega}\neq 0
on
V
, then by proposition
2.1
,
k_{2}=\frac{\lambda_{\Omega}k_{1}k_{2}}{\lambda_{\Omega}k_{1}}=\frac{K_{\Omega}}{k_{1\Omega}}
on
V-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
. Thus,
\frac{K_{\Omega}}{k_{1\Omega}}
is a
C^{\infty}
extension of
k_{2}
to
V
.

(3)

By item
(1)
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
. Let
V
be a neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
such that
k_{1\Omega}\neq 0
on
V
, then
k_{1}=\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{\lambda_{\Omega}}
on

. Thus, for every
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})\cap V
,
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|k_{1}|=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\frac{|k_{1\Omega}|}{|\lambda_{\Omega}|}=\infty
.

(4)

Since
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
and by proposition
2.1
,
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|H|=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\frac{|H_{\Omega}|}{|\lambda_{\Omega}|}=\infty
.

(5)

Since
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
and by proposition

,
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|K|=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}\frac{|K_{\Omega}|}{|\lambda_{\Omega}|}=\infty

∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper wavefront,
W\subset U
a compact set,
V\subset U
an open set,
a:W\to\mbox{\bbb R}
a continuous function,
\mathbf{\Omega}_{1}
and
\mathbf{\Omega}_{2}
tangent moving bases of
\mathbf{x}
, then we have:

(1)

If there exist a constant
C_{1}>0
such that
|a|\leq C_{1}|\lambda_{\Omega_{1}}|
on
W
then there exist a constant
C_{2}>0
such that
|a|\leq C_{2}|\lambda_{\Omega_{2}}|
on
W

(2)

\mathfrak{T}_{\Omega_{1}}(V)=\mathfrak{T}_{\Omega_{2}}(V)

Proof.

(1)

Setting
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{I}_{\Omega_{1}}^{-1}\mathbf{\Omega}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{\Omega}_{2}
(change of basis matrix) and
\rho=det(\mathbf{A})
, we have
\mathbf{\Omega}_{2}=\mathbf{\Omega}_{1}\mathbf{A}
, therefore
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega_{1}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\Omega_{2}}
and
\lambda_{\Omega_{1}}=\rho\lambda_{\Omega_{2}}
. Since
|a|\leq C_{1}|\lambda_{\Omega_{1}}|=C_{1}|\rho||\lambda_{\Omega_{2}}|
and choosing
C_{2}
as the maximum of

on
W
, we get the result.

(2)

Using the proof of item (1),
\lambda_{\Omega_{1}}=\rho\lambda_{\Omega_{2}}
with
\rho\neq 0
, then we have the equality.

∎

[K_{\Omega}]_{p}\in J_{\Sigma p}
.

(4)

(5)

Example 4.2

The wavefront

Remark 4.1
.

The boundedness and extendibility of Gaussian curvature are conserved under changes of coordinates in the domain, however they are not conserved making changes at co-domain. The wavefront
\mathbf{x}(u,v)=(u,2v^{3}+u^{2}v,3v^{4}+u^{2}v^{2}+u^{2})
with Gaussian curvature unbounded can be obtained from example

whose Gaussian curvatures is bounded, applying at co-domain the diffeomorphism

and

(5\Leftrightarrow 2)

•

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=1
,
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
and
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})<0
(resp.
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})>0
) then the following statements are equivalents:

(1)

\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
.

(2)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}H=\pm\infty
.

(3)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}k_{1}=\pm\infty
(resp.
k_{2}
).

(4)

There exist an open neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
, in which
\lambda_{\Omega}
does not change sign.

Proof.

The proof of
(1\Rightarrow 4)
,
(2\Leftrightarrow 3)
,
(2\Leftrightarrow 4)
is equal to corresponding ones in theorem
4.3
because this does not use the hypothesis of
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
. To prove
(3\Rightarrow 1)
, let us define
A:=\{\mathbf{q}\in U:K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0\}
. If
\mathbf{p}\notin\bar{A}
, there exist a neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{p}
in which
K_{\Omega}\equiv 0
, thus
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})+t^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})=\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})
on
W
and since
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
, using that
\lambda_{\Omega}
does not change sign on a neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
, shrinking
W
we have that
\frac{\lambda_{\Omega}}{H_{\Omega}}
does not change sign, then if
\frac{\lambda_{\Omega}}{H_{\Omega}}\geq 0
(resp.
\frac{\lambda_{\Omega}}{H_{\Omega}}\leq 0
) we have that
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0
for every
(\mathbf{q},t)\in W\times(-\epsilon,0)
(resp.
W\times(0,\epsilon)
) with an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
and from this follows the result. If
\mathbf{p}\in\bar{A}
, by hypothesis there exist an open neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{p}
such that
k_{1}>0
and
H>0
(resp. or
<0
) on
W-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and as
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
, we have that
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}|\big|_{A}=\infty
. Let
\epsilon>0
be given, there exist a open ball
B
such that
|\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}|>\epsilon
on
B\cap A
,
k_{1}>0
(resp.
<0
),
H>0
(resp.
<0
) and
\frac{\lambda_{\Omega}}{H_{\Omega}}=\frac{1}{H}>0
(resp.
<0
) on
B-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
. Since
\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}=\frac{1}{k_{1}}
on
(B\cap A)-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
, we have that
\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}\geq 0
(resp.
\leq 0
) on
B\cap A
. Now, if
(\mathbf{q},t)\in B\times(-\epsilon,0)
(resp.
B\times(0,\epsilon)
) and
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})=0
then
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})+t^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})=\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0
because
\frac{\lambda_{\Omega}}{H_{\Omega}}\geq 0
(resp.
\leq 0
) on
B
. The another option is that

, then
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})+t^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0
because this is

if and only if

or
t=\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{q})
which is impossible since that
|\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{q})|>\epsilon
and
\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{q})\geq 0

\leq 0
). It follows the result.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with

, we have that
\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{p}

.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=1
, if
\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
, then
\mathbf{p}
is a degenerate singularity.

Proof.

If we suppose that
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2})
is a non-degenerate singularity, then
\lambda_{\Omega u}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
or
\lambda_{\Omega v}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
and therefore
\lambda_{\Omega}(u,p_{2})
or
\lambda_{\Omega}(p_{1},v)
is strictly monotone as function of one variable on every sufficient small neigborhood of
\mathbf{p}
, which is contradictory, because
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
and this does not change sign by corollary
4.2
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with

\mathbf{p}

\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
.

Proof.

if
\mathbf{p}
is an isolated singularity, then there exist an open connected neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
, such that
\lambda_{\Omega}\neq 0
on

and since that
V-\{\mathbf{p}\}

\lambda_{\Omega}
does not change sign on
V
. By corollary
4.2
, it follows the result.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=1
. One of the principal curvatures
\kappa_{-},\kappa_{+}
has a

-extension to an open neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
if and only if
\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
.

Proof.

if
\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
, then by corollary
4.2

\lambda_{\Omega}\geq 0
(or
\lambda_{\Omega}\leq 0
, this case is analogous) on an open neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
, thus
k_{1}=\kappa_{-}
,
k_{2}=\kappa_{+}
on
V-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and by proposition
4.1
one of these function has a

-extension to an open neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
. Conversely, without loss of generality let us suppose that
\kappa_{-}
has a

-extension to an open neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{p}
, then
\lambda_{\Omega}
does not change sign on some neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
, otherwise there are sequences
\mathbf{a}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{b}_{n}\longrightarrow\mathbf{p}
such that

and

. Thus,

4.1
one of the limits
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|k_{1}(\mathbf{a}_{n})|
,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|k_{2}(\mathbf{b}_{n})|
is
\infty
.
∎

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=0
, then:

(1)

(k_{1\Omega},k_{2\Omega})(\mathbf{p})=(0,0)
.

(2)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|K|=\infty
.

(3)

\frac{1}{k_{1}}
and
\frac{1}{k_{2}}
have continuous extensions on a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
, which are of class
C^{\infty}
except possibly at umbilical points and singularities of rank 0 of
\mathbf{x}
.

(4)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|k_{1}|=\infty
and
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|k_{2}|=\infty
.

(5)

\frac{1}{k_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}
has a
C^{\infty}
-extension on a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
.

Proof.

(1)

By proposition
2.3
,
H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
, then
k_{1\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=H_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})-\sqrt{H_{\Omega}^{2}(\mathbf{p})-\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})}=0
. Similarly
k_{2\Omega}(\mathbf{p})=0
.

(2)

By proposition
2.3
,
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
and therefore
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|K|=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|\frac{K_{\Omega}}{\lambda_{\Omega}}|=\infty

(3)

There exist a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
such that
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
and hence
k_{1}\neq 0,k_{2}\neq 0
on
V-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
, then
\frac{1}{k_{1}}=\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}k_{2}}=\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}
and similarly
\frac{1}{k_{2}}=\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}
which are well defined on
V
. Notice that
k_{1\Omega}
and
k_{2\Omega}
may not be differentiable at umbilical points and singularities of rank 0 of
\mathbf{x}
.

(4)

By item (2), there exist a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
such that
k_{1}\neq 0,k_{2}\neq 0
on
V-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
, therefore
k_{1\Omega}\neq 0,k_{2\Omega}\neq 0
as well. Then,
k_{1}=\frac{K_{\Omega}}{k_{2\Omega}}
and
k_{2}=\frac{K_{\Omega}}{k_{1\Omega}}
on
V-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and using that
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
and item (1) we get (3).

(5)

Since
K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{p})\neq 0
, there exist an open neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
such that
K_{\Omega}
does not vanish on
V
, then
\frac{1}{k_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}=\frac{2H}{K}=\frac{2H_{\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}
on

and as
\frac{2H_{\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}
is well defined on

, this is a

-extension.

∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a proper wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=0
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
.

(2)

\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}\geq 0
and
H_{\Omega}
does not change sign on a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
.

(3)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}K=\infty
and
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}H=\pm\infty
.

(4)

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}k_{1}=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}k_{2}=\infty
or
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}k_{1}=\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}k_{2}=-\infty
.

Proof.

•

(1\Rightarrow 2)
If
\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
, then there exist
\epsilon>0
and an open connected neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
such that
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})+t^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0
for every
(\mathbf{q},t)\in V\times(0,\epsilon)
(or
V\times(-\epsilon,0)
, this case is analogous). As
K_{\Omega}\neq 0
and does not change sign on
V
(shrinking
V
if it is necessary),
k_{1\Omega}=k_{2\Omega}=0
and since
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})+t^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0
if and only if
t=\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{q})
or
t=\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}(\mathbf{q})
, we have that
\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}\leq 0
and
\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}\leq 0
on
V
. Then,
k_{1\Omega}k_{2\Omega}\geq 0
on
V
, but
-k_{1\Omega}
and
-k_{2\Omega}
are the eigenvalues of
\bm{\alpha}_{\Omega}=\bm{\mu}_{\Omega}adj(\mathbf{\Lambda})
, then
\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}=k_{1\Omega}k_{2\Omega}\geq 0
on
V
. Observe that
k_{1\Omega}
and
k_{2\Omega}
do not change sign on
V
, then
H_{\Omega}
neither.

•

(2\Rightarrow 3)
Since
\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}K
on
U-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and using that
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|K|=\infty
we get that
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}K=\infty
. On the other hand,
H^{2}\geq K
, then
\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to p}|H|=\infty
. As
H_{\Omega}
and
\lambda_{\Omega}
do not change sign on a neighborhood of
\mathbf{p}
,
H=\frac{H_{\Omega}}{\lambda_{\Omega}}
neither and we get the result.

•

(3\Rightarrow 4)
As
K
is positive near to
\mathbf{p}
then
\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}K\geq 0
and
K_{\Omega}\neq 0
on a neighborhood

of
\mathbf{p}
. Shrinking

does not change sign and
H
neither on

. Without loss of generality, let us suppose
\lambda_{\Omega}\geq 0
on

, then
k_{1}=H-\sqrt{H^{2}-K}
and
k_{2}=H+\sqrt{H^{2}-K}
and since that

does not change sign on

, one of the function
k_{1},k_{2}
neither. By this last and since that
K>0
, we have that
k_{1}>0,k_{2}>0
or
k_{1}<0,k_{2}<0
on

, then using item (3) of proposition

•

(4\Rightarrow 1)
There exist a neighborhood

of
\mathbf{p}
such that

(or
k_{1}<0,k_{2}<0
, this case is analogous) on

and

on

, then

and
\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}=\frac{1}{k_{1}}>0
on

\frac{k_{1\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}},\frac{k_{2\Omega}}{K_{\Omega}}\geq 0
on

. Choose

arbitrary and we have that
\lambda_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})-2tH_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})+t^{2}K_{\Omega}(\mathbf{q})\neq 0
for every

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}
be a wavefront,
\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{p}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
with
rank(D\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{p}))=0
. If there exist a neighborhood
V
of
\mathbf{p}
in which the only singularity of rank
0
is
\mathbf{p}
, then
\mathbf{x}
is parallelly smoothable at
\mathbf{p}
if and only if
\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}\geq 0
on a neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{p}
.

Proof.

If
\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}\geq 0
on
W
, shrinking if it is necessary we can suppose that
K\neq 0
on
W-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
and since
\lambda_{\Omega}K_{\Omega}=\lambda_{\Omega}^{2}K
, then
H^{2}>K>0
on
W-\Sigma(\mathbf{x})
. As
H_{\Omega}\neq 0
on singularities of rank 1, then
H_{\Omega}
has a isolated zero on
W\cap V
and therefore
H_{\Omega}
does not change sign. Applying the last theorem we get the result.
∎

Observe that, if we have a wavefront
\mathbf{x}:U\to\mbox{\bbb R}^{3}

\mathbf{\Omega}
a tmb of
\mathbf{x}

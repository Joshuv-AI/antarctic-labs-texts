Dynamical large deviations for the boundary driven symmetric exclusion process with Robin boundary conditions.
T. Franco, P. Gonçalves, C. Landim, A. Neumann

Abstract
. In this article, we consider a one-dimensional symmetric exclusion process in weak contact with reservoirs at the boundary. In the diffusive time-scaling the empirical measure evolves according to the heat equation with Robin boundary conditions. We prove the associated dynamical large deviations principle.



1.
Introduction

The investigation of the thermodynamic properties of stationary
nonequilibrium states of interacting particle systems has been proven
to be an important step in the understanding of nonequilibrium
phenomena and a rich source of mathematical problems
[
11
,
5
,
21
]
.

In the context of lattices gases, the empirical measure is the only
relevant thermodynamical quantity in the macroscopic description of
the system, and the thermodynamical functionals, as the free energy,
can be identified to the large deviations rate functional.

While in equilibrium the stationary state is given by the Gibbs
distribution associated to the Hamiltonian, in nonequilibrium the
construction of the stationary state requires solving a
dynamical-variational problem which defines the so-called
quasi-potential
[
19
]
.

At the beginning of this century, Derrida, Lebowitz and Speer

[
14
]
considered the one-dimensional symmetric exclusion
process in strong contact with reservoirs. In this context, the
empirical measure evolves in the diffusive time-scale according to the
heat equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions
[
22
]
. Expressing
the stationary state as a product of matrices
[
12
]
, they
obtained an explicit formula for the large deviations principle rate
functional of the empirical measure under the stationary state, the
so-called nonequilibrium free energy.

Later,
[
2
]
derived the Derrida-Lebowitz-Speer formula (in
short DLS formula) for the nonequilibrium free energy extending to
infinite dimensions the dynamical approach introduced in
[
19
]
.
More precisely, they first proved a dynamical large deviations
principle for the empirical measure for symmetric exclusion processes
in strong contact with reservoirs
[
3
]
. Denote by

I_{[0,T]}(u)
the large deviations rate function of the dynamical
large deviations principle. Hence,
I_{[0,T]}(u)
represents the cost
of observing a trajectory
u(t)
in the time-interval
[0,T]
. Let

\bar{\rho}
be the stationary profile of the hydrodynamic equation,
that is, the typical density profile under the stationary
state. Define the quasi-potential as

V(\gamma)\;=\;\inf_{T>0}\inf_{u}I_{[0,T]}(u)\;,

where the second infimum is carried over all trajectories
u(t)

connecting the stationary density profile
\bar{\rho}
to a density
profile
\gamma
in the time interval
[0,T]
:
u(0)=\bar{\rho}
,

u(T)=\gamma
. It is proven in
[
2
]
that the quasi-potential

V
coincides with the nonequilibrium free energy, i.e., that it
satisfies the DLS’ equations.

In the sequel,
[
8
,
16
]
derived a large deviations principle
for the empirical measure under the stationary state from the
dynamical one, with rate functional given by the quasi-potential.
This result was later extended to weakly symmetric exclusion processes
in strong contact with reservoirs
[
15
,
7
,
6
,
4
]
and to reaction-diffusion models
[
25
,
18
]
.

It has long been understood that these results extend to
boundary-driven one-dimensional symmetric exclusion processes in weak
contact with reservoirs
[
10
]
. But only recently, this result
appeared in
[
13
]
through the matrix ansatz product method.

In this article, we accomplish the first step in the project of
deriving the large deviations principle for the empirical measure
under the stationary state, through the dynamical approach, for
boundary-driven one-dimensional symmetric exclusion processes in weak
contact with reservoirs. The law of large numbers has been obtained in

[
1
,
20
]
. We prove here the dynamical large
deviations, while in the companion paper
[
9
]
, it is shown
that the quasi-potential satisfies the DLS’ equations obtained in

[
13
]
for this model.

The rate functional

In this section, we present some properties of the rate function

and prove Theorem

once for all, a measurable density profile
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
.

Note:
Throughout this article, given a
function

, we represent by
u_{t}
and

u(t)
the function defined on
[0,1]
and such that
u_{t}(x)=u(t,x)
.

We start with two elementary bounds. The first estimate asserts that
the cost of a trajectory in a interval
[0,T]
is bounded by the sum
of its cost in the intervals
[0,S]
and
[S,T]
. Let

\tau_{r}u:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,
r>0
, be the
function defined by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\tau_{r}u(t,x)=u(t+r,x)
. For all

\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)\,dx
in
D([0,T],{\mathscr{M}}_{\rm ac})
and
0<S<T
,

I_{[0,T]}(u)\;\leq\;I_{[0,S]}(u)\;+\;I_{[0,T-S]}(\tau_{S}u)\;.

(3.1)

The proof of this claim is elementary and left to the reader. It
relies on the fact that
\sup_{n}\{a_{n}+b_{n}\}\leq\sup_{n}a_{n}+\sup_{n}b_{n}
.

The second assertion states that the cost of a trajectory on a
subinterval of
[0,T]
is bounded by its total cost. For all

\pi(t,dx)=u(t,x)\,dx
in
D([0,T],{\mathscr{M}}_{\rm ac})
and
0<S<T
,

I_{[0,S]}(u)\;\leq\;I_{[0,T]}(u)\;.

(3.2)

To prove this claim, assume that
I_{[0,S]}(u)<\infty
,
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. The same argument applies to the case

I_{[0,S]}(u)=\infty
. By definition of the rate
function, there exists
H:[0,S]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
smooth such that

I_{[0,S]}(u)\;\leq\;J_{S,H}(u)\;+\;\varepsilon\;.

Let
\sigma_{n}:[0,T]\to[0,1]
,
n\geq 1
, be a sequence of smooth,
monotone functions such that
\sigma_{n}(t)=1
for
0\leq t\leq S
and

\sigma_{n}(t)=0
for
S+(1/n)\leq t\leq T
. Define the function
H_{n}:[0,T]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
as
H_{n}(t,x)=H(t,x)
,
0\leq t\leq S
and

H_{n}(t,x)=H(S,x)\,\sigma_{n}(t)
,
S\leq t\leq T
. Then,

J_{S,H}(u)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{T,H_{n}}(u)\;\leq\;I_{[0,T]}(u)\;,

as claimed.

A similar argument yields that the cost of a trajectory
u
in a
time-interval
[R,R+S]
is bounded by the total cost. More precisely,

I_{[0,S]}(\tau_{R}u)\;\leq\;I_{[0,T]}(u)\;.

(3.3)

for all
S>0
,
R>0
such that
R+S\leq T
.

The proof of the next result is similar to the ones of
[

, Lemma

,
[
17

. We present it here in sake
of completeness.

Lemma 3.1

Fix

We turn to the second assertion of the lemma. Fix

We turn to the second assertion. We may represent

Proposition 3.5
.

\displaystyle L_{H}(u^{\varepsilon,\delta})+B^{1}_{H^{\varepsilon,\delta}}(u)-B^{2}_{H^{\varepsilon,\delta}}(u)-R^{\varepsilon,\delta}\;\leq\;I_{[0,T]}(u)\;,

where, recall,

4.
Deconstructing the rate functional

The main result of this section, stated in Proposition
4.5

below, shows that the rate function
I_{[0,T]}(\,\cdot\,)
can
be decomposed as the sum of two rate functions. The first one measures
the cost of the trajectory due to its evolution in the bulk, while the
second one measures the costs due to the boundary evolution. This
decomposition of the rate function is the main tool in the proof that
any trajectory
u
with finite rate function can be approximated by a
sequence of regular trajectories
(u^{n}:n\geq 1)
in such a way that

I_{[0,T]}(u^{n}\,|\gamma)\to I_{[0,T]}(u\,|\gamma)
, the content of
the next section.

Weighted Sobolev spaces

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\Omega_{T}

Above and hereafter, induced means that we first declare two functions

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(\Omega_{T})

Lemma 4.1

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\Delta_{R}
the Laplacian on
[0,1]

with Robin boundary conditions, sometimes called the Robin Laplacian

[
29
, Section 4.3]
.

Fix
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and consider the eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\,\Delta f=\lambda\,f\;,\\
&(\nabla f)(0)\,=\,A^{-1}\,f(0)\;,\\
&(\nabla f)(1)\,=\,-\,B^{-1}f(1)\;.\end{aligned}\right.

(A.1)

This problem has only the trivial solution
f=0
for
\lambda\leq 0
. For
\lambda>0
, the equation
-\,\Delta f=\lambda\,f
can be
turned into a two-dimensional ODE which yields that the solutions of
(
A.1
) are given by

f(x)=a\,[\,\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}x)+b\,\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}x)\,]
for some
a
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
. The boundary conditions are
satisfied if and only if

\tan\sqrt{\lambda}\;=\;(A+B)\,\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\lambda AB-1}\;,

(A.2)

in which case
b=(A\sqrt{\lambda})^{-1}
. This identity excludes

\lambda=0
from the set of eigenvalues of the Robin Laplacian.

An analysis of (
A.2
) shows that it has a countable set of
solutions
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\{\lambda_{j}:j\geq 1\}
, where
0<\lambda_{1}
,

\lambda_{j}<\lambda_{j+1}
and
\lambda_{j}\sim j^{2}
in the sense that
there exists
0<c_{0}<c_{1}<\infty
such that

c_{0}\,j^{2}\;\leq\;\lambda_{j}\;\leq\;c_{1}\,j^{2}\;\;\text{for all $j\geq 1$}\;.

(A.3)

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\{f_{j}:j\geq 1\}
the associated orthonormal
eigenvectors, which form a basis of
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
. By the previous
analysis,

f_{j}(x)\;=\;a_{j}\,\big\{\,\cos(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}x)\;+\;\frac{1}{A\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}\,\sin(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}x)\,\big\}\;,

(A.4)

where
a_{j}
is chosen for
f_{j}
to have
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-norm equal to
1
. It
can be shown that
|a_{j}|\leq C_{0}
for all
j\geq 1
, where
C_{0}
is a
finite constant depending only on
A
and
B
. Therefore, by
(
A.3
),

\|\,f_{j}\,\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{0}\;,\quad\|\,\nabla^{n}f_{j}\,\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{0}\,(\lambda_{j})^{n/2}\;\leq\;C_{0}\,j^{n}

(A.5)

for all
j\geq 1
,
n\geq 1
.
A straightforward computation provides a formula for the Green
function of the Robin Laplacian: Let

K_{R}:[0,1]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be given by

K_{R}(x,y)\;=\;\frac{1}{1+A+B}\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &(B+1-x)\,(A+y)\;,\quad 0\leq y\leq x\leq 1\;,\\
&(B+1-y)\,(A+x)\;,\quad 0\leq x\leq y\leq 1\;.\end{aligned}\right.

(A.6)

Denote by
K_{R}
the integral operator defined by

(K_{R}f)(x)\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}K_{R}(x,y)\,f(y)\;dy\;.

Then,
K_{R}=(-\Delta_{R})^{-1}
.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
the Hilbert space obtained by
completing the space

C^{2}_{A,B}([0,1])\;=\;\big\{\,f\in C^{2}([0,1]):(\nabla f)(0)\,=\,A^{-1}\,f(0)\;,\,(\nabla f)(1)\,=\,-\,B^{-1}f(1)\,\big\}

endowed with the scalar product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}

defined by

\displaystyle\langle\,f\,,\,g\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}

\displaystyle=\;\langle\,f\,,\,(-\,\Delta_{R})g\,\rangle

(A.7)

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{A}\,f(0)\,g(0)\;+\;\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla f)(x)\,(\nabla g)(x)\;dx\;+\;\frac{1}{B}\,f(1)\,g(1)\;.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}
the norm induced by the
scalar product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}
. We have that

\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle^{2}\;,

(A.8)

for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
.

Recall from (
3.4
) the definition of the Sobolev space
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
.
The norms
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}
and

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}
are equivalent. There exist finite
constants
0<C_{1}<C_{2}<\infty
such that

C_{1}\,\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}\;\leq\;\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;\leq\;C_{2}\,\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}

(A.9)

for all
f\in C^{2}([0,1])
. In particular, the spaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
and

{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
coincide.

In terms of the
eigenfunctions
f_{k}
,

\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}\,|\,\langle\,f\,,\,f_{k}\,\rangle\,|^{2}\;.

(A.10)

Moreover, a straightforward computation yields that for all

f\in C^{2}_{A,B}([0,1])
,

\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{\infty}\;\leq\;2\,(A\vee 1)\,\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;.

(A.11)

Fix a function
f
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
. It is well known that there exists a
continuous function
f^{(c)}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
(actually Hölder
continuous,

|f^{(c)}(y)-f^{(c)}(x)|\leq\|f\|_{2}|y-x|^{1/2}
) such that
f=f^{(c)}
almost surely. Moreover, for all

h\in C^{1}([0,1])
,

\int_{0}^{1}f\,\nabla h\;dx\;=\;f^{(c)}(1)\,h(1)\;-\;f^{(c)}(0)\,h(0)\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}\nabla f\,h\;dx\;.

(A.12)

The next result provides an explicit formula for
f^{(c)}
in terms of
the eigenvectors
f_{k}
.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists a finite constant
C_{0}
such that

\sum_{k\geq 1}\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|\;\leq\;C_{0}\,\|\,f\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}

for all
f\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
. In particular,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}(\cdot)
defines a continuous
function, and, for almost all
x\in[0,1]
,

f(x)\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}(x)\;.

(A.13)

Proof.

By (
A.9
),
f
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
. By Schwarz inequality,

\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|\,\Big)^{2}\;\leq\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}\,\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\;\cdot

The second sum is finite by (
A.3
) and the first one is finite
by (
A.10
). This proves the first assertion.

Since each function
f_{k}
is continuous, and a summable sum of
uniformly bounded continuous functions is continuous,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}(\cdot)
defines a continuous
function. As
(f_{k}:k\geq 1)
forms an orthonormal basis of

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
,
f=\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}
as an
identity in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
. In particular, these functions are equal
almost everywhere.
∎

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}(P^{(R)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
the semigroup in

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
generated by the Robin Laplacian: For any function

f\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
,
t>0
,

P^{(R)}_{t}f\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}\;.

(A.14)

In particular, for each
t\geq 0
,
P^{(R)}_{t}
is a symmetric operator
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
and
P^{(R)}_{t}f\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
for all

f\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
. Moreover, as
P^{(R)}_{t}
is symmetric, by
(
A.10
),
P^{(R)}_{t}
is a contraction in
{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
and

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
:

\begin{gathered}\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-2\lambda_{k}t}\,\lambda_{k}\,|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,|^{2}\;\leq\;\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;,\\
\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-2\lambda_{k}t}\,|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,|^{2}\;\leq\;\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;.\end{gathered}

(A.15)

Let
f\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
be given by

f=\sum_{k\geq 1}\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}
. For each
t>0
, there
exists a finite constant
C_{0}(t)
such that

\displaystyle\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,\|^{2}_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{0}(t)\,\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;,\quad\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;\leq\;C_{0}(t)\,\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;.

(A.16)

Indeed, by (
A.10
) and since
P^{(R)}_{t}
is symmetric and

P^{(R)}_{t}f_{k}=e^{-\lambda_{k}t}f_{k}
,

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}\,e^{-2\lambda_{k}t}\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{0}(t)\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;=\;C_{0}(t)\,\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}

for some finite constant
C_{0}(t)
. On the other hand, by Schwarz
inequality and (
A.5
),

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,\|^{2}_{\infty}\;=\;\Big\|\,\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,f_{k}\,\Big\|^{2}_{\infty}\;\leq\;\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-2\lambda_{k}t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle^{2}\;=\;C_{0}(t)\,\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}

for some finite constant
C_{0}(t)
.

Lemma A.2
.

There exists a finite constant
C_{0}
such that

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,-\,f\,\|_{2}\;\leq\;C_{0}\,t^{1/3}\,\,\|\,f\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}

for all
t\geq 0
,
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
.

Proof.

Since
(f_{k}:k\geq 1)
is an orthonormal basis of
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
,

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,-\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}\big[\,e^{-\lambda_{k}\,t}\,-\,1\,\big]^{2}\,\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;.

Fix
k_{0}\geq 1
. Since the sequence
\lambda_{k}
increases, the
right-hand side can be bounded by

\big[\,e^{-\lambda_{k_{0}}\,t}\,-\,1\,\big]^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}-1}\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{k_{0}}}\,\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\lambda_{k}\,\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;.

The first sum is bounded by
\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}
. In view of
(
A.10
), the second one is bounded by

\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}
so that

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,-\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;\leq\;\big[\,1\,-\,e^{-\lambda_{k_{0}}\,t}\,\big]^{2}\,\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{k_{0}}}\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;.

As
1-e^{-x}\leq x
,
x>0
, and since, by (
A.9
),

\|\,f\,\|_{2}\leq C_{0}\|\,f\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}
for some
finite constant
C_{0}
,

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,-\,f\,\|^{2}_{2}\;\leq\;\Big\{\,C_{0}\,(\,\lambda_{k_{0}}\,t\,)^{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{k_{0}}}\,\Big\}\,\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;.

To complete the proof, it remains to choose
k_{0}
such that

\lambda^{-3}_{k_{0}}\sim t^{2}
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

There exists a finite constant
C_{0}
such that

\|\,P^{(R)}_{t}f\,-\,f\,\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{0}\,t^{1/5}\,\,\|\,f\,\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}

for all
t\geq 0
,
f\in C([0,1])\cap{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in[0,1]
. Since
f
is continuous, by (
A.13
) and
(
A.5
),

\big\{\,P^{(R)}_{t}f(x)\,-\,f(x)\,\big\}^{2}\;\leq\;C_{0}^{2}\,\Big(\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\big[\,1\,-\,e^{-\lambda_{k}\,t}\,\big]\,\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|\,\Big)^{2}

for some finite constant
C_{0}
. By Schwarz inequality and
(
A.10
), the right-hand side is bounded by

C_{0}^{2}\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\,\big[\,1\,-\,e^{-\lambda_{k}\,t}\,\big]^{2}\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}\,\,\big|\,\langle f\,,\,f_{k}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;=\;C_{0}^{2}\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\,\big[\,1\,-\,e^{-\lambda_{k}\,t}\,\big]^{2}\|\,f\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{R}}\;.

It remains to estimate the sum. Fix
k_{0}\geq 1
. Since the sequence

\lambda_{k}
increases, as
1-e^{-x}\leq x
,
x>0
, by (
A.3
),
the sum is less than or equal to

C\,\big[\,1\,-\,e^{-\lambda_{k_{0}}\,t}\,\big]^{2}\;+\;\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\;\leq\;C\,\Big\{\,(k^{2}_{0}\,t)^{2}\;+\;\frac{1}{k_{0}}\,\Big\}

for some finite constant
C
. It remains to choose
k_{0}
such that

k_{0}^{5}\sim t^{-2}
.
∎

Appendix B
Initial-value problems with Robin boundary conditions

We present in this section some result on the initial-boundary value
problems (
2.6
), (
7.2
). Recall the definition of the
Sobolev space
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
introduced in (
3.4
). Fix a function

\phi\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
, and consider the initial-boundary problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u\,=\,\Delta u\\
(\nabla u)(t,0)\,=\,A^{-1}\,u(t,0)\\
(\nabla u)(t,1)\,=\,-\,B^{-1}\,u(t,1)\\
u(0,\cdot)\,=\,\phi(\cdot)\;.\end{cases}

(B.1)

Definition B.1
.

A function
u
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{H}}^{1})
is said to be a
generalized solution in the cylinder
[0,T]\times[0,1]
of the
equation (
B.1
) if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}\,H_{t}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}\phi\,H_{0}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}\,\partial_{s}H_{s}\;dx

\displaystyle=\;-\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u_{s}\,\nabla H_{s}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\,\frac{1}{B}\,u_{s}(1)\,H_{s}(1)\,+\,\frac{1}{A}\,u_{s}(0)\,H_{s}(0)\,\big\}\;ds

for every
0<t\leq T
, function
H
in
C^{1,2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
.

Next result is proved in
[
1
]
. We present it here in sake
of completeness.

Theorem B.2
.

For each
\phi\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
, there exists one and only one
generalized solution to (
B.1
). The solution is smooth in

(0,\infty)\times[0,1]
and can be represented as

u(t,x)=(P^{(R)}_{t}\phi)(x)
, where
P^{(R)}_{t}
is the semigroup
associated to the Robin Laplacian. Moreover,

\min\{\,0\,,\,{\rm ess\,inf}\,\phi\,\}\,\leq\,u(t,x)\,\leq\,\max\{\,0\,,\,{\rm ess\,sup}\,\phi\,\}

(B.2)

for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]
. Finally, if
\phi(x)\leq b

for some
b>0
, then, for each
t_{0}>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such
that
u(t,x)\leq b-\varepsilon
for all

(t,x)\in[t_{0},\infty)\times[0,1]
. Analogously, if
\phi(x)\geq a

for some
a<0
, then, for each
t_{0}>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such
that
u(t,x)\geq a+\varepsilon
for all

(t,x)\in[t_{0},\infty)\times[0,1]
.

Proof.

Existence and uniqueness of generalized solutions, as well as their
representation in terms of the semigroup
P^{(R)}_{t}
is the content of
Theorems 1 and 3 in
[
26
, Section VI.2]
.

We turn to (
B.2
). Assume first that
\phi
belongs to

{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
. By (
A.9
),
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{R}
, and, by
Lemma
A.3
,
u(t)
converges to
\phi
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{\infty}([0,1])

as
t\to 0
. Since the solution is smooth in

(0,\infty)\times[0,1]
, by the maximum principle stated in Theorems
2 and 3 of
[
27
, Chapter 3]
,

\min\{\,0\,,\,\inf_{0\leq y\leq 1}u(t_{0},y)\,\}\,\leq\,u(t,x)\,\leq\,\max\{\,0\,,\,\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1}u(t_{0},y)\,\}

for all
(t,x)\in[t_{0},\infty)\times[0,1]
. Letting
t_{0}\to 0
, as

u(t_{0})
converges to
\phi
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{\infty}([0,1])
, yields
(
B.2
).

To extend this result to
\phi\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
, we consider a
sequence
\phi_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
which converges to
\phi
in

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
and such that

{\rm ess\,inf}\,\phi\,\leq\,\phi_{n}(x)\,\leq\,{\rm ess\,sup}\,\phi
for all
0\leq x\leq 1
. Denote by
u^{n}
the solution of
(
B.1
) with initial condition
\phi_{n}
. Fix
t>0
. By the
result for initial conditions in
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
,

\displaystyle\min\{\,0\,,\,{\rm ess\,inf}\,\phi\,\}\;\leq\;\min\{\,0\,,\,\inf_{0\leq y\leq 1}\phi_{n}(y)\,\}

\displaystyle\leq\;u^{n}(t,x)\;\leq\;\max\{\,0\,,\,\sup_{0\leq y\leq 1}\phi_{n}(y)\,\}\,\leq\,\max\{\,0\,,\,{\rm ess\,sup}\,\phi\,\}\;.

for all
0\leq x\leq 1
. By (
A.16
),
u^{n}(t)
converges to
u(t)

in
{\mathscr{L}}^{\infty}([0,1])
. This completes the proof of (
B.2
).

Assume that
\phi(x)\leq b
for some
b>0
. By (
B.2
),

u(t,x)\leq b
for all
t\geq 0
,
0\leq x\leq 1
. Fix
t_{0}>0
, and
assume that
\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}u(t_{0},x)=b
. As
b>0
, the boundary
conditions imply that the maximum cannot be attained at the
boundary. On the other hand, if it is attained at the interior, by
Theorem 2 of
[
27
, Chapter 3]
and by the smoothness of the
solution,
u(t,x)=b
for all
(t,x)\in(0,t_{0}]\times[0,1]
. This
is not possible at the boundary. Therefore,

\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}u(t_{0},x)<b
. By the maximum principle, this
bound can be extended to all
(t,x)\in[t_{0},\infty)\times[0,1]
.
The same argument applies to the lower bound.
∎

Let
\bar{\rho}\in{\mathscr{M}}_{\rm ac}
be the unique stationary solution of
the equation (
2.6
). That, is
\bar{\rho}
is the solution of the
elliptic equation

\begin{cases}\Delta\rho\,=\,0\\
(\nabla\rho)(0)\,=\,A^{-1}[\,\rho(0)-\alpha\,]\\
(\nabla\rho)(1)\,=\,B^{-1}[\,\beta-\rho(1)]\;.\end{cases}

(B.3)

An elementary computation yields that

\bar{\rho}
is given by

\bar{\rho}(x)\;=\;\frac{\alpha(1+B)+\beta A}{1+B+A}\;+\;\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\,x}{1+B+A}\;\cdot

Note that
\bar{\rho}
is the linear interpolation between

\bar{\rho}(-A)=\alpha
and
\bar{\rho}(1+B)=\beta
.

Definition B.3
.

Fix
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
. A function
u
in

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{H}}^{1})
is said to be a generalized solution in the
cylinder
[0,T]\times[0,1]
of the equation (
2.6
) if

u(t,x)-\bar{\rho}
is a generalized solution of the initial-boundary

) with initial condition
\gamma-\bar{\rho}
.

Therefore, a function
u
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{H}}^{1})
is a
generalized solution in the cylinder
[0,T]\times[0,1]
of the
equation (
2.6
) if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}\,H_{t}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\,H_{0}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}\,\partial_{s}H_{s}\;dx=\;-\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u_{s}\,\nabla H_{s}\;dx

\displaystyle-\;\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\,\frac{1}{B}\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,H_{s}(1)\,+\,\frac{1}{A}\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,H_{s}(0)\,\Big\}\;ds

for every
0<t\leq T
, function
H
in
C^{1,2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
.

Theorem B.4
.

Fix
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
. There exists a unique generalized
solution of (
2.6
). The solution is smooth in

(0,T]\times[0,1]
and satisfies the bounds

\min\{\,\alpha\,,\,{\rm ess\,inf}\,\gamma\,\}\,\leq\,u(t,x)\,\leq\,\max\{\,\beta\,,\,{\rm ess\,sup}\,\gamma\,\}

for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,1]
. Moreover, for all

there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varepsilon\leq u(t,x)\leq 1-\varepsilon
for all

(t,x)\in[t_{0},T]\times[0,1]
.

Proof.

The proof of this result is similar to the one of Theorem
B.2
.
∎

Fix
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, and denote by
u^{(\gamma)}
the unique
weak solution of (
2.6
) with initial condition
\gamma
.

Lemma B.5
.

There exists a
finite constant
C_{0}
, which depends only on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
A
,

B
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(\nabla u_{s})^{2}}{\sigma(u_{s})}\,dx+\;\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{t}\,\Big|\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(0)}{1-u_{s}(0)}\,\Big|\,ds

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{B}\int_{0}^{t}\,\Big|\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(1)}{1-u_{s}(1)}\,\Big|\,ds\;\leq\;C_{0}\,t\;+\;\int_{0}^{1}F_{0}(\gamma)\,dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}F_{0}(u_{t})\,dx

for all
t>0
and all
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
.

Proof.

Fix
F\in C^{2}([0,1])
, an initial profile
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, and
denote by
u
the solution of (
2.6
). Since
u
is smooth on

(0,\infty)\times[0,1]
, integrating by parts and in view of the
boundary conditions, for all
0<\delta<t<\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}F(u_{t})\,dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}F(u_{\delta})\,dx\;=\;-\,\int_{\delta}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}F^{\prime\prime}(u_{s})\,(\nabla u_{s})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\,\int_{\delta}^{t}\frac{1}{A}\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,F^{\prime}(u_{s}(0))\,ds\,-\,\int_{\delta}^{t}\frac{1}{B}\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,F^{\prime}(u_{s}(1))\,ds\;.

As
u_{\delta}
converges to
\gamma
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
, letting

\delta\to 0
yields that for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}F^{\prime\prime}(u_{s})\,(\nabla u_{s})^{2}\,dx+\;\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{A}\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,F^{\prime}(u_{s}(0))\,ds

\displaystyle+\,\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{B}\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,F^{\prime}(u_{s}(1))\,ds\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}F(\gamma)\,dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}F(u_{t})\,dx\;.

Since for each
t>0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varepsilon\leq u(s,x)\leq 1-\varepsilon
for all

(s,x)\in[t,\infty)\times[0,1]
, the previous argument can be
applied to the function
F_{0}(r)=r\log r+(1-r)\log(1-r)
. It
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(\nabla u_{s})^{2}}{\sigma(u_{s})}\,dx+\;\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{A}\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(0)}{1-u_{s}(0)}\,ds

(B.5)

\displaystyle+\,\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{B}\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(1)}{1-u_{s}(1)}\,ds\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}F_{0}(\gamma)\,dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}F_{0}(u_{t})\,dx

for all
t>0
. Clearly, for each
\varrho>0
, the function

f_{\varrho}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f_{\varrho}(r)=[\,r\,-\,\varrho\,]\,\log[\,r/(1-r)\,]
is
bounded below by a finite constant, say
-\,c_{1}(\varrho)<0
. Hence,

|\,f_{\varrho}(r)\,|\leq f_{\varrho}(r)+2c_{1}
. Therefore, there
exists a finite constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(A,B,\alpha,\beta)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(\nabla u_{s})^{2}}{\sigma(u_{s})}\,dx+\;\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{t}\,\Big|\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(0)}{1-u_{s}(0)}\,\Big|\,ds

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{B}\int_{0}^{t}\,\Big|\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(1)}{1-u_{s}(1)}\,\Big|\,ds\;\leq\;C_{0}\,t\;+\;\int_{0}^{1}F_{0}(\gamma)\,dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}F_{0}(u_{t})\,dx

for all
t>0
, as claimed.
∎

As
u_{t}
converges to
\gamma
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
, letting

t\to 0
in the previous lemma yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\Big\{\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(\nabla u_{s})^{2}}{\sigma(u_{s})}\,dx

\displaystyle+\;\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{t}\,\Big|\,[\,u_{s}(0)-\alpha\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(0)}{1-u_{s}(0)}\,\Big|\,ds

(B.6)

\displaystyle+\,\frac{1}{B}\int_{0}^{t}\,\Big|\,[\,u_{s}(1)-\beta\,]\,\log\frac{u_{s}(1)}{1-u_{s}(1)}\,\Big|\,ds\,\Big\}\;=\;0\;.

Definition B.6
.

Fix
H\in C^{0,1}([0,T]\times[0,1])
. A function
u
in

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{H}}^{1})
such that
0\leq u\leq 1
a.e.​ is said to be a
generalized solution in the cylinder
[0,T]\times[0,1]
of the
equation (
7.2
) if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}\,G_{t}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\,G_{0}\;dx\;-\;\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}\,\partial_{s}G_{s}\;dx

(B.7)

\displaystyle=\;\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}\big\{\,-\,\nabla u_{s}\,\nabla G_{s}\,+\,2\,\sigma(u_{s})\,\nabla H_{s}\,\nabla G_{s}\,\big\}\;dx

\displaystyle+\;\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{\beta,B}(u_{s}(1),H_{s}(1))\,G_{s}(1)\,+\,{\mathfrak{p}}_{\alpha,A}(u_{s}(0),H_{s}(0))\,G_{s}(0)\,\big\}\;ds

for every
0<t\leq T
and function
G
in
C^{1,2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
.

Theorem B.7
.

Fix
\gamma:[0,1]\to[0,1]
and
H
in
C^{0,1}([0,T]\times[0,1])
. There exists a unique weak solution of (
7.2
).

Proof.

Existence follows from the hydrodynamic limit of the WASEP. Uniqueness
is based on the energy estimate. Fix two initial
conditions
\gamma^{(1)}
,
\gamma^{(2)}
, and denote by
u^{(1)}
,

u^{(2)}
two weak solutions of (
7.2
) with initial conditions

\gamma^{(1)}
,
\gamma^{(2)}
, respectively.

Before presenting a rigorous argument we provide an heuristic one.
Approximate
w=u^{(2)}-u^{(1)}
by a sequence of functions
G
in

C^{1,2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
. By (
B.7
), for all
0<t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{0}^{1}w^{2}_{t}\;dx\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{0}^{1}[\,\gamma^{(2)}-\gamma^{(1)}\,]^{2}\;dx\;+\;\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla w_{s})^{2}\;dx

(B.8)

\displaystyle=\;2\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}[\,\sigma(u^{(2)}_{s})-\sigma(u^{(1)}_{s})\,]\,\nabla H_{s}\,\nabla w_{s}\;dx

\displaystyle-\;\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\,w_{s}(1)^{2}\,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\beta,B}(H_{s}(1))\,+\,w_{s}(0)^{2}\,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\alpha,A}(H_{s}(0))\,\Big\}\;ds\;,

where

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\varrho,D}(M)\,=\,D^{-1}\,\{\,\varrho\,e^{M}+(1-\varrho)e^{-M}\,\}
. As

\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\varrho,D}(M)\geq 0
, the last integral is
negative. Therefore, by Young’s inequality

2xy\leq ax^{2}+a^{-1}x^{2}
, and since
\nabla H
is uniformly
bounded and
\sigma
Lipschitz continuous,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{0}^{1}w^{2}_{t}\;dx\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{0}^{1}[\,\gamma^{(2)}-\gamma^{(1)}\,]^{2}\;dx\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla w_{s})^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{0}(H)\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}w^{2}_{s}\;dx\;.

for some finite constant
C_{0}(H)
which depends on
H
.
It remains to apply Gronwal inequality to conclude that

\int_{0}^{1}w^{2}_{t}\;dx\;\leq\;e^{C_{0}t}\,\int_{0}^{1}[\,\gamma^{(2)}-\gamma^{(1)}\,]^{2}\;dx\;,

which yields uniqueness.

We turn to a rigorous proof. Recall the notation introduced in the
proof of Lemma
3.3
. Fix a smooth function

F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and recall that
w=u^{(2)}-u^{(1)}
. As
w^{\varepsilon,\delta}
is a smooth function, for

0<t\leq T
,

\langle\,F(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t})\,\rangle\,-\,\langle\,F(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{0})\,\rangle\,=\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{s})\,\partial_{s}w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}\,dx\;.

Integrating by parts, the right-hand side becomes

\int_{0}^{1}w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}\,F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t})\;dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{1}w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{0}\,F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{0})\;dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}\,\partial_{s}F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{s})\,dx\;.

By Lemma
3.3
, actually its proof since we changed the definition
of
w^{\varepsilon}
, this expression is equal to

\int_{0}^{1}w_{t}\,F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{t})^{\varepsilon,\delta}\;dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{1}w_{0}\,F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{0})^{\varepsilon,\delta}\;dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}w_{s}\,\partial_{s}[F^{\prime}(w^{\varepsilon,\delta}_{s})^{\varepsilon,\delta}]\,dx\;+\;R_{\varepsilon,\delta}\;,

(B.9)

where for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}R_{\varepsilon,\delta}=0
.

Take
F(a)=(1/2)\,a^{2}
. Let
\phi^{(2)}
be the convolution of
\phi

with itself:

\phi^{(2)}(t)\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t-s)\,\phi(s)\;ds\;,

and set

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\phi_{\delta}^{(2)}(t)=\delta^{-1}\,\phi^{(2)}(t/\delta)
. Since
P^{(R)}_{t}
is a semigroup and since
P^{(R)}_{t}

comutes with the time convolution, for any function

f\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
,

(f^{\varepsilon,\delta})^{\varepsilon,\delta}(t,x)\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\,P^{(R)}_{2\varepsilon}f(t+s)\,](x)\,\phi_{\delta}^{(2)}(s)\;ds\;.

Therefore, the first three terms of (
B.9
) are equal to

\int_{0}^{1}w_{t}\,w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}\;dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{1}w_{0}\,w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{0}\;dx\,-\,\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{1}w_{s}\,\partial_{s}w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}\,dx\;,

with the convention, starting from this equation and up to the end of
the proof, that the superscript
\delta
represent now convolution
with
\phi_{\delta}^{(2)}
instead of
\phi_{\delta}
.

By (
B.7
), this sum is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}\big\{\,-\,\nabla w_{s}\,\nabla w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}\,+\,2\,\{\,\sigma(u^{(2)}_{s})-\sigma(u^{(1)}_{s})\,\}\,\nabla H_{s}\,\nabla w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}\,\big\}\;dx

\displaystyle-\;\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\beta,B}(H_{s}(1))\,w_{s}(1)\,w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}(1)\,+\,\overline{\ {\mathfrak{p}}}_{\alpha,A}(H_{s}(0))\,w_{s}(0)\,w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{s}(0)\ \,\big\}\;ds\;,

where
\overline{\ {\mathfrak{p}}}_{\varrho,D}(M)
has been introduced in
(
B.8
). By (
A.16
), (
A.9
), for each
\varepsilon>0
,

\nabla w^{2\varepsilon}
belongs to
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
. Therefore, as
\delta\to 0
,

\nabla w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}=(\nabla w^{2\varepsilon})^{\delta}\to\nabla w^{2\varepsilon}
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
.

On the other hand, by (
A.7
) and (
A.9
),

|\,w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}(1)\,-\,w^{2\varepsilon}_{t}(1)\,|^{2}\;\leq\;C_{0}\,\|\,w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}\,-\,w^{2\varepsilon}_{t}\,\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{1}}

for some finite constant
C_{0}
independent of
\varepsilon
and
t
. A
similar inequality holds at
x=0
. Therefore, as

\nabla w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}\to\nabla w^{2\varepsilon}
in

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
as
\delta\to 0
,

w^{2\varepsilon,\delta}_{t}(1)\to w^{2\varepsilon}_{t}(1)
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T])

as
\delta\to 0
. In conclusion, letting
\delta\to 0
, the sum
appearing in the penultimate displayed equation converges to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}\big\{\,-\,\nabla w_{s}\,\nabla w^{2\varepsilon}_{s}\,+\,2\,\{\,\sigma(u^{(2)}_{s})-\sigma(u^{(1)}_{s})\,\}\,\nabla H_{s}\,\nabla w^{2\varepsilon}_{s}\,\big\}\;dx

\displaystyle-\;\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\beta,B}(H_{s}(1))\,w_{s}(1)\,w^{2\varepsilon}_{s}(1)\,+\,\overline{\ {\mathfrak{p}}}_{\alpha,A}(H_{s}(0))\,w_{s}(0)\,w^{2\varepsilon}_{s}(0)\ \,\big\}\;ds\;.

By the first assertion of Lemma
3.2
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\nabla w^{2\varepsilon}
converges to
\nabla w
in

{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
. Therefore, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the first
line converges to

\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\int_{0}^{1}\big\{\,-\,\nabla w_{s}\,\nabla w_{s}\,+\,2\,[\,\sigma(u^{(2)}_{s})-\sigma(u^{(1)}_{s})\,]\,\nabla H_{s}\,\nabla w_{s}\,\big\}\;dx\;.

On the other hand, as
w\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{H}}^{1})
, by Lemma
A.3
,

w^{\varepsilon}_{t}(1)\to w_{t}(1)
in
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,T])
. Hence, by the
dominated convergence theorem, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the second line converges to

-\,\int_{0}^{t}\big\{\,\overline{{\mathfrak{p}}}_{\beta,B}(H_{s}(1))\,w_{s}(1)^{2}\,+\,\overline{\ {\mathfrak{p}}}_{\alpha,A}(H_{s}(0))\,w_{s}(0)^{2}\ \,\big\}\;ds\;.

This proves that equation (
B.8
) is in force and completes the
proof of the theorem.
∎

We conclude this section with a heat equation with mixed boundary
equations. Fix a function
\phi\in{\mathscr{L}}^{2}([0,1])
, and consider the
initial-boundary problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u\,=\,\Delta u\\
(\Delta u)(t,0)\,=\,A^{-1}\,\nabla u(t,0)\\
(\Delta u)(t,1)\,=\,-\,B^{-1}\,\nabla u(t,1)\\
u(0,\cdot)\,=\,\phi(\cdot)\;.\end{cases}

(B.10)

One can define generalized solutions of this problem as in Definition

B.1
and prove existence and uniqueness as stated in Theorem

B.2
. The solution can be represented as
u_{t}=P^{(M)}_{t}\phi
,
where
(P^{(M)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
represents the semigroup associated to
the Laplacian with boundary conditions

(\Delta f)(0)\,=\,A^{-1}\,(\nabla f)(0)\;,\qquad(\Delta f)(1)\,=\,-\,B^{-1}(\nabla f)(1)\;.

Denote this operator by
\Delta_{M}
. An elementary computation shows
that the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{M}
coincide with those of
\Delta_{R}
.

We claim that for all
s\geq 0
and function
f
in
C^{1}([0,1])
,

\nabla P^{(R)}_{s}f\;=\;P^{(M)}_{s}\nabla f\;.

(B.11)

To check this identity, fix
f
in
C^{1}([0,1])
, and let

u_{s}:=P^{(R)}_{s}f
. Clearly
u_{s}
is the solution of (
B.1
)
with initial condition
u_{0}=f
. Let
v_{s}:=\nabla u_{s}
, Then,
v_{s}

solves (
B.10
) initial condition
v_{0}=\nabla f
. Hence,
v_{s}
can
be represented as
v_{s}=P^{(M)}_{s}\nabla f
, that is,

P^{(M)}_{s}\nabla f=v_{s}=\nabla u_{s}=\nabla P^{(R)}_{s}f
, as
claimed.

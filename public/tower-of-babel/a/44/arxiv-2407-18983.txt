Inequalities involving Higher Degree Polynomial Functions in π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x)
Subham De

Abstract
The primary purpose of this article is to study the asymptotic and numerical estimates in detail for higher degree polynomials in π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x) having a general expression of the form, P ⁡ ( π ⁡ ( x ) ) − e ​ x log ⁡ x ​ Q ​ ( π ⁡ ( x / e ) ) + R ⁡ ( x ) \displaystyle P(\pi(x))-\frac{ex}{\log x}Q(\pi(x/e))+R(x) P P , Q Q and R R are arbitrarily chosen polynomials and π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x) denotes the Prime Counting Function . The proofs require specific order estimates involving π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x) and the Second Chebyshev Function ψ ⁡ ( x ) \psi(x) , as well as the famous Prime Number Theorem in addition to certain meromorphic properties of the Riemann Zeta Function ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) and results regarding its non-trivial zeros. A few generalizations of these concepts have also been discussed in detail towards the later stages of the paper, along with citing some important applications.

3.1
Cubic Polynomial Inequality

The statement is as follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let us consider the cubic polynomial of
\pi(x)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x):=(\pi(x))^{3}-\frac{3ex}{\log x}(\pi(x/e))^{2}+\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\pi(x/e^{2})

(7)

Given that
\pi(x)
is approximated by
\frac{x}{\log x}
with a known error term, we can hypothesize that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

(8)

Furthermore,
\mathcal{H}(x)<0
for sufficiently large values of
x
.

Proof.

A priori from the order estimate between
\pi(x)
and
\psi(x)
as defined in (
6
) (cf.
[
4
]
), we compute the indivudual terms of
\mathcal{H}(x)
as follows,

\displaystyle\pi(x/e)=\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log(x/e)}+O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log(x/e))^{2}}\right)=\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}+O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)

(9)

and,

\displaystyle\pi(x/e^{2})=\frac{\psi(x/e^{2})}{\log(x/e^{2})}+O\left(\frac{x/e^{2}}{(\log(x/e^{2}))^{2}}\right)=\frac{\psi(x/e^{2})}{\log x-2}+O\left(\frac{x/e^{2}}{(\log x-2)^{2}}\right)

(10)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle(\pi(x))^{3}=\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)\right)^{3}=\frac{(\psi(x))^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}+3\cdot\frac{(\psi(x))^{2}}{(\log x)^{3}}O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt+3\cdot\frac{\psi(x)}{(\log x)^{3}}\left(O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}+\left(O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)\right)^{3}

(11)

Further simplification yields,

\displaystyle(\pi(x))^{3}=\frac{(\psi(x))^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

(12)

Finally,

\displaystyle\frac{3ex}{\log x}(\pi(x/e))^{2}=\frac{3ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}+O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{3ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x-1)^{2}}+2\cdot\frac{\psi(x/e)}{(\log x-1)^{2}}O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)+\left(O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt=\frac{3ex(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x)(\log x-1)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

(13)

And,

\displaystyle\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\pi(x/e^{2})=\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e^{2})}{\log x-2}+O\left(\frac{x/e^{2}}{(\log x-2)^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt=\frac{3e^{2}x\psi(x/e^{2})}{(\log x)^{2}(\log x-2)}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

(14)

Combining all the terms (
9
), (
12
), (
13
) and (
14
), we obtain,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)=\left(\frac{(\psi(x))^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)\right)-\left(\frac{3ex(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x)(\log x-1)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt+\left(\frac{3e^{2}x\psi(x/e^{2})}{(\log x)^{2}(\log x-2)}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)\right)

Considering the dominant terms and the contributions of each term separately as compared to the error term, we get,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{(\psi(x))^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}-\frac{3ex(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x)(\log x-1)^{2}}+\frac{3e^{2}x\psi(x/e^{2})}{(\log x)^{2}(\log x-2)}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

(15)

Given the statement of the
Prime Number Theorem

[
9
]
[
10
]
,
\psi(x)\sim x
as
x
approaches
\infty
, thus we consider the dominant terms for sufficiently large
x
. Hence, substituting (
5
) in (
15
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}-\frac{3x^{3}}{e(\log x-1)^{3}}+\frac{3x^{2}}{(\log x-2)^{3}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt=-\frac{3x^{3}}{e(\log x-1)^{3}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

(16)

Since,
\frac{3x^{3}}{e(\log x-1)^{3}}>0
for sufficiently large
x
( observe that higher-order terms diminish as
x
grows ), the dominant term is thus negative.

In coclusion, for sufficiently large values of
x
, one shall have (
8
) to satisfy and,
\mathcal{H}(x)<0
.
∎

3.3
Application: Equivalence with Ramanujan’s Inequality

The one question which might pop up at this point is to justify the sugnificance of studying inequalities like (
17
) involving cubic polynomials in
\pi(x)
. One such application which we shall observe in this section is the equivalence of the statements of the
Cubic Polynomial Inequality
(cf. Theorem (
3.4
)) and the
Ramanujan’s Inequality
(cf. Theorem (
1.1
)).

Assume that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x):=(\pi(x))^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\pi\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)

(18)

Hence, the statement goes as follows.

Theorem 3.6
.

The Cubic Polynomial Inequality is
equivalent
to proving the Ramanujan’s Inequality
[
1
]
[
2
]
. In other words, if
\mathcal{H}(x)<0
for large
x
, then,
\mathcal{G}(x)<0
for sufficiently large
x
and vice versa.

Proof.

A priori from (
6
) of Theorem (
2.3
), we attain the derivations (
9
) and (
10
).

First, we approximate
\mathcal{H}(x)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)=\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)^{3}-\frac{3ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}\right)^{2}+\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e^{2})}{\log x-2}\right)

Ignoring higher-order error terms. Estimating
\mathcal{G}(x)
in similar manner, we obtain,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(x)=\frac{(\psi(x))^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}

First we assume , if possible that,
\mathcal{H}(x)<0
for sufficiently large values of
x
.
Given that
\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}
is the dominant term, for large
x
, thus the second term in the expression of
\mathcal{H}(x)
will dominate the first and third terms due to the
ex
factor in the numerator. Hence, to maintain the inequality, we must have,

\displaystyle\frac{(\psi(x))^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}\approx\frac{3ex}{\log x}\frac{(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x-1)^{2}}

Implying,

\displaystyle(\psi(x))^{3}\approx 3ex(\psi(x/e))^{2}(\log x)(\log x-1)^{2}

(19)

Dividing both sides by
(\psi(x))^{2}
,

\displaystyle\psi(x)\approx 3ex(\log x)(\log x-1)^{2}

N.B.
Since
\psi(x)
is much larger than
\psi(x/e)
for large
x
, this approximation holds.

As for
\mathcal{G}(x)
, again, given the dominance of
\psi(x)
,

\displaystyle\frac{(\psi(x))^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}\approx\frac{ex}{\log x}\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}

(20)

Observe that, the leading term in
\mathcal{G}(x)
is negative, implying
\mathcal{G}(x)<0
.

Conversely, consider that,
\mathcal{G}(x)<0
. This implies,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)^{2}<\frac{ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}\right)

(21)

Dividing both sides by
\left(\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}\right)
, we get,

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}<\frac{ex(\psi(x/e))}{(\log x-1)}

Evaluate
\mathcal{H}(x)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x):=\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)^{3}-\frac{3ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)+\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e^{2})}{\log x-2}\right)

Given the dominance of
\psi(x)
, we can assert that,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)^{3}<\frac{3ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)

(22)

Which simplifies to,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)^{3}\approx\frac{3ex(\psi(x/e))^{2}}{(\log x-1)^{2}}

(23)

Dividing both sides by
\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}\right)^{2}\approx 3ex(\psi(x/e))(\log x)(\log x-1)

(24)

The dominant term in (
24
) indicates that the inequality
\mathcal{H}(x)<0
holds true for large enough
x
. This completes the proof.

∎

3.4
Higher-Degree Polynomial Inequality

Theorem 3.7
.

For higher powers, let’s consider,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x):=(\pi(x))^{4}-\frac{4ex}{\log x}(\pi(x/e))^{3}+\frac{6e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}(\pi(x/e^{2}))^{2}-\frac{4e^{3}x}{(\log x)^{3}}\pi(x/e^{3})

(25)

Then, the following holds true,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(26)

and for sufficiently large
x
we have,
\mathcal{K}(x)>0
.

Proof.

A priori using the relation (
6
) (cf.
[
4
]
) from Theorem (
2.3
), along with (
9
) and (
10
),

\displaystyle\pi(x/e^{3})=\frac{\psi(x/e^{3})}{\log x-3}+O\left(\frac{x/e^{3}}{(\log x-3)^{2}}\right)

(27)

Now, we compute,

\displaystyle(\pi(x))^{4}=\left(\frac{\psi(x)}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)\right)^{4}=\frac{(\psi(x))^{4}}{(\log x)^{4}}+O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(28)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{4ex}{\log x}(\pi(x/e))^{3}=\frac{4ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e)}{\log x-1}+O\left(\frac{x/e}{(\log x-1)^{2}}\right)\right)^{3}

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt=\frac{4ex(\psi(x/e))^{3}}{(\log x)(\log x-1)^{3}}+O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(29)

Subsequently, we approximate the rest of the terms of
\mathcal{K}(x)
as follows.

\displaystyle\frac{6e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}(\pi(x/e^{2}))^{2}=\frac{6e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e^{2})}{\log x-2}+O\left(\frac{x/e^{2}}{(\log x-2)^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 150.0pt=\frac{6e^{2}x(\psi(x/e^{2}))^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}(\log x-2)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(30)

And,

\displaystyle\frac{4e^{3}x}{(\log x)^{3}}\pi(x/e^{3})=\frac{4e^{3}x}{(\log x)^{3}}\left(\frac{\psi(x/e^{3})}{\log x-3}+O\left(\frac{x/e^{3}}{(\log x-3)^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt=\frac{4e^{3}x\psi(x/e^{3})}{(\log x)^{3}(\log x-3)}+O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(31)

Combining (
27
), (
28
), (
29
), (
30
) and (
31
), and sorting out the dominant terms and their contributions towards the error term,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x)=\frac{(\psi(x))^{4}}{(\log x)^{4}}-\frac{4ex(\psi(x/e))^{3}}{(\log x)(\log x-1)^{3}}+\frac{6e^{2}x(\psi(x/e^{2}))^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}(\log x-2)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 150.0pt-\frac{4e^{3}x\psi(x/e^{3})}{(\log x)^{3}(\log x-3)}+O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(32)

A simple application of (
5
) yields,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x)=\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{4}}\left(1-\frac{4e}{\log x}+\frac{6e^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{4e^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

(33)

Since
\left(1-\frac{4e}{\log x}+\frac{6e^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{4e^{3}}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)
is positive for sufficiently large
x
(higher-order terms diminish as
x
grows), hence the dominant term is positive. Accordingly, the error term in the approximation is,

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{x^{4}}{(\log x)^{5}}\right)

In conclusion, we assert that, (
26
) indeed holds true, and
\mathcal{K}(x)>0
for sufficiently large enough
x
.
∎

4
Quadratic Form Involving Sums of Prime Counting Function

4.1
Inequality involving Weighted Sums of
\pi(x)

Theorem 4.9
.

Consider a quadratic form involving the sum of the prime counting function over smaller intervals,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\pi(x/k)\right)^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\pi(x/(ek))\right)\mbox{ , }\hskip 10.0ptn>1

(35)

For some fixed
n
, then we have the following approximation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(36)

With
\mathcal{L}(x)>0
for sufficiently large values of
x
.

Proof.

For the proof, we evaluate terms inside the summand of
\mathcal{L}(x)
, a priori using the result (
6
) (cf.
[
4
]
) in Theorem (
2.3
).

\displaystyle\pi(x/k)=\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}+O\left(\frac{x/k}{(\log x-\log k)^{2}}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle\pi(x/(ek))=\frac{\psi(x/(ek))}{\log x-\log(ek)}+O\left(\frac{x/(ek)}{(\log x-\log(ek))^{2}}\right)

(38)

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\pi(x/k)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}+O\left(\frac{x/k}{(\log x-\log k)^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 120.0pt=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}+O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/k}{(\log x-\log k)^{2}}\right)

(39)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\pi(x/(ek))=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\psi(x/(ek))}{\log x-\log(ek)}+O\left(\frac{x/(ek)}{(\log x-\log(ek))^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 150.0pt=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/(ek))}{\log x-\log(ek)}+O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/(ek)}{(\log x-\log(ek))^{2}}\right)

(40)

Squaring (
39
) gives,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\pi(x/k)\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}+O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/k}{(\log x-\log k)^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 250.0pt\approx\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}\right)^{2}

Ignoring the higher-order terms for the time being. Further computation yields,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}\right)^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/k)\psi(x/j)}{(\log x-\log k)(\log x-\log j)}

(41)

Combining (
40
) and (
41
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)\approx\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/k)}{\log x-\log k}\right)^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\psi(x/(ek))}{\log x-\log(ek)}

(42)

An important observation is that, the leading term of
\psi(x)
is
x
, so for large
x
,

\displaystyle\psi(x/k)=\frac{x}{k}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{k}}\log^{2}x\right)\mbox{ and, }\psi(x/(ek))=\frac{x}{ek}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{ek}}\log^{2}x\right)

Therefore, using the leading term approximation, we can deduce,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)\approx\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/k}{\log x-\log k}\right)^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/(ek)}{\log x-\log(ek)}

Where,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/k}{\log x-\log k}\right)^{2}\approx\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\right)^{2}\approx\left(\frac{x}{\log x}H_{n}\right)^{2}

H_{n}
denoting the
n
-th
Harmonic Number
,
H_{n}\approx\log n+\gamma
,
\gamma
being the
Euler Constant
.

As for the second term in the expression of
\mathcal{L}(x)
,

\displaystyle\frac{ex}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/(ek)}{\log x-\log(ek)}\approx\frac{ex}{\log x}\cdot\frac{x}{e\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\approx\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right)^{2}H_{n}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)\approx\left(\frac{xH_{n}}{\log x}\right)^{2}-\frac{x^{2}H_{n}}{(\log x)^{2}}\approx\frac{x^{2}(\log n)^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}-\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{2}}\approx\frac{x^{2}\log n(\log n-1)}{(\log x)^{2}}

On the other hand, analyzing the error terms from previously derived estimates, it can be deduced that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)=O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

(43)

Hence, (
36
) follows. Moreover, since the leading term
\frac{x^{2}\log n(\log n-1)}{(\log x)^{2}}
is positive for
n>1
, thus it implies that
\mathcal{L}(x)
is positive for large
x
, and the proof is complete.
∎

4.3
Logarithmic Weighted Sum Inequality

It is very much possible to improve (
36
) even further, where one can also consider the case which involves
logarithmic weights
.

Theorem 4.11
.

The following can in fact be conjectured for the logarithmic weights,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x):=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/k)}{\log(x/k)}\right)^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/(ek))}{\log(x/(ek))}\right)\mbox{ , }\hskip 10.0ptn>1

(45)

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

(46)

And,
\mathcal{F}(x)<0
for large values of
x
.

Proof.

A priori for large
x
, utilizing (
5
) (cf.
[
4
]
),

\displaystyle\psi(x/k)=\frac{x}{k}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{k}}\log^{2}\left(\frac{x}{k}\right)\right)\mbox{ and, }\psi(x/(ek))=\frac{x}{ek}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{ek}}\log^{2}\left(\frac{x}{ek}\right)\right)

Rigorously computation each and every term of
\mathcal{F}(x)
yields,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/k)}{\log(x/k)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\psi(x/k)}{(\log(x/k))^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x/k}{(\log(x/k))^{3}}\right)\right),

(47)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/(ek))}{\log(x/(ek))}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\psi(x/(ek))}{(\log(x/(ek)))^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x/(ek)}{(\log(x/(ek)))^{3}}\right)\right).

(48)

Subsequently squaring the left-hand side of (
47
),

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/k)}{\log(x/k)}\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\psi(x/k)}{(\log(x/k))^{2}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}O\left(\frac{x/k}{(\log(x/k))^{3}}\right)\right)^{2}

Using the
Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality
, and considering the main term and error terms separately,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x}{k(\log(x/k))^{2}}\right)^{2}=\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{4}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\right)^{2}=\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{4}}\left(H_{n}\right)^{2}

(49)

Where,
H_{n}
denotes the
Harmonic Number
. Using the harmonic series approximation,

\displaystyle H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}\approx\log n+\gamma.

(50)

Hence, from (
49
),

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/k)}{\log(x/k)}\right)^{2}=\frac{x^{2}\log^{2}n}{(\log x)^{4}}.

(51)

As for the error term,

\displaystyle O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/k}{(\log(x/k))^{3}}\right)=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/k}{(\log(x/k))^{3}}\right)=O\left(\frac{x\log n}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

Thus, combining all our deductions,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/k)}{\log(x/k)}\right)^{2}=\frac{x^{2}\log^{2}n}{(\log x)^{4}}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}\log^{2}n}{(\log x)^{6}}\right)

Furthermore, for the second term in (
45
), we have the following calculations,

\displaystyle\frac{ex}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/(ek))}{\log(x/(ek))}=\frac{ex}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\psi(x/(ek))}{(\log(x/(ek)))^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x/(ek)}{(\log(x/(ek)))^{3}}\right)\right).

(52)

Approximating the main term,

\displaystyle\frac{ex}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x/(ek)}{(\log(x/(ek)))^{2}}=\frac{ex^{2}}{\log x}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{ek(\log(x/(ek)))^{2}}

Using the harmonic series approximation,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{ek}\approx\frac{\log n}{e}

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\frac{ex^{2}}{\log x}\frac{\log n}{e(\log x)^{2}}=\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{3}}.

Combining all,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/k)}{\log(x/k)}\right)^{2}-\frac{ex}{\log x}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x/(ek))}{\log(x/(ek))}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}\log^{2}n}{(\log x)^{4}}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}\log^{2}n}{(\log x)^{6}}\right)-\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{3}}=-\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{3}}+O\left(\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)

Considering the dominant term. As a result, we conclude,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{4}}\right)=O\left(\frac{x^{2}}{(\log x)^{3}}\right)

for large
x
, and moreover, the dominant term,
\frac{x^{2}\log n}{(\log x)^{3}}
being always positive for
n>1
, we can thus assert that,
\mathcal{F}(x)<0
for sufficiently large values of
x
.
∎

5
A More General Framework

Given the asymptotic nature of the prime counting function
\pi(x)
, the general form of such polynomial functions can be formulated as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(x):=P(\pi(x))-\frac{ex}{\log x}Q(\pi(x/e))+R(x)

(54)

where
P
and
Q
are polynomials and
R
is a term that compensates for higher-order error terms. Subsequently, one can claim that, the error term in (
54
) might behave similarly as in the previous cases. In mathematical terms, it might very well be possible that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{d}}{(\log x)^{d+1}}\right)

(55)

for some degree
d
depending on the degrees of
P
and
Q
.

In order to justify our claim (
55
) corresponding to (
54
), let’s delve into a specific example by explicitly choosing polynomials
P
,
Q
, and
R(x)
and studying the function
\mathcal{N}(x)
for different cases explicitely.

5.1
A Typical Example I : Generalized Cubic Polynomial Inequality

We assume,

\displaystyle P(\pi(x)):=(\pi(x))^{3^{n}}\mbox{ , }\hskip 10.0ptQ(\pi(x/e)):=3(\pi(x/e))^{3^{n}-1}

and,

\displaystyle R(x):=\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}(\pi(x/e^{2}))^{3^{n}-2}

for every
n>1
. Then, in contrast to what we’ve discussed in Theorem (
3.4
), we can observe a significant change in the behaviour of the so-called
Generalized Cubic Polynomial
defined as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(x):=(\pi(x))^{3^{n}}-\frac{3ex}{\log x}(\pi(x/e))^{3^{n}-1}+\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}(\pi(x/e^{2}))^{3^{n}-2}

(56)

Furthermore, one has the following estimate for
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)
as
x
increases.

Theorem 5.13
.

A priori
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)
defined as in (
56
) for every
n>1
, we can derive that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}+1}}\right)

(57)

Furthermore,
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)>0
for sufficiently large values of
x
.

Proof.

Utilizing (
6
) in Theorem (
2.3
) as we’ve done in other cases, along with the derivations done in (
9
) and (
10
), we rigorously compute the following terms of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)
indivudually for large values of
n
and
x
,

\displaystyle(\pi(x))^{3^{n}}=\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right)^{3^{n}}+3^{n}\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right)^{3^{n}-1}O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt=\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right)^{3^{n}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}+1}}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle(\pi(x/e))^{3^{n}-1}=\left(\frac{x/e}{\log x-1}\right)^{3^{n}-1}+(3^{n}-1)\left(\frac{x/e}{\log x-1}\right)^{3^{n}-2}O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt=\left(\frac{x}{e(\log x-1)}\right)^{3^{n}-1}+O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}-1}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}}}\right)

(59)

and,

\displaystyle(\pi(x/e^{2}))^{3^{n}-2}=\left(\frac{x/e^{2}}{\log x-2}\right)^{3^{n}-2}+(3^{n}-2)\left(\frac{x/e^{2}}{\log x-2}\right)^{3^{n}-3}O\left(\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt=\left(\frac{x}{e^{2}(\log x-2)}\right)^{3^{n}-2}+O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}-2}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}}}\right)

(60)

Substituting (
58
), (
59
) and (
60
) back into
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)
yields,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)=\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right)^{3^{n}}+\frac{3e^{2}x}{(\log x)^{2}}\left(\frac{x}{e^{2}(\log x-2)}\right)^{3^{n}-2}-\frac{3ex}{\log x}\left(\frac{x}{e(\log x-1)}\right)^{3^{n}-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 250.0pt+O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}}}\left(1-\frac{3e}{e^{3^{n}-1}(\log x-1)^{3^{n}-1}}+\frac{3e^{2}}{e^{2(3^{n}-2)}(\log x-2)^{3^{n}-2}}\frac{1}{x}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 250.0pt+O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}+1}}\right)

(61)

Important to note that, for sufficiently large
x
, the dominant term on the R. H. S. of (
61
) will be
\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}}}
. The other terms will have exponentially decaying factors due to the presence of
e^{-(3^{n}-1)}
and
e^{-2(3^{n}-2)}
, making them small relative to the leading term. Therefore, the dominant term is positive, and the higher-order error terms do not affect the sign significantly.

In other words, the terms
\frac{3e}{e^{3^{n}-1}(\log x-1)^{3^{n}-1}}
and
\frac{3e^{2}}{e^{2(3^{n}-2)}(\log x-2)^{3^{n}-2}}\frac{1}{x}
on (
61
) are small for large
x
, the dominant term
\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}}}
ensures that
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)>0
. Moreover, we have,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)\approx O\left(\frac{x^{3^{n}}}{(\log x)^{3^{n}+1}}\right)

And thus the proof is complete.

∎

6
Furture Scope for Research

It can definitely be said that, the results discussed in this article serves as a mere small version of what can be considered as a plethora of possibilities that future researchers can come up with.

It is very much possible to eventually derive a similar estimate for polynomials of degree
\geq 5
in
\pi(x)
, adopting similar techniques as devised in proposing the
Cubic Polynomial Inequality
and the
Higher Degree Polynomial Inequality
. It may very well help us to establish certain conjectures and even extend the study of primes even further.

Regarding the estimates which we’ve derived for functions involving weighted sums of
\pi(x)
over small intervals, it might be interesting to study them even further by varying indivudual weights of such funtions, and study their respective sign changes over increasing values of
x
.

On the other hand, it is very much possible to produce absolutely new results from the general polynomial (
54
), by choosing
P
,
Q
and
R
appropriately. As evident from the two examples which serves as generalizations of two of the inequalities which we’ve already discussed, it’s fascinating to observe that, the sign changes of the polynomials are completely unpredictable. For example, we’ve deduced that, for higher values of
x
, the generalized cubic polynomial
\mathcal{H}_{n}(x)
as defined in (
56
) yields negative values for
n=1
(in this case, we denote
\mathcal{H}_{1}(x)
by
\mathcal{H}(x)
, as defined in (
7
)), whereas, it’s sign reverses drastically for the case,
n>1
(cf. Theorem (
5.13
)). Important to mention that, different conclusions may very well be possible for different types of examples. Although each and every such result involves some robust calculations and numerical computations, but it eventually opens up a whole new area for future researchers to explore, which might end up in enlightening us to some significant discoveries in the relevant field of Number Theory.

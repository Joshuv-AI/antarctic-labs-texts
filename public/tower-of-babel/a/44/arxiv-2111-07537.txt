Convergence analysis of a second-order accurate, linear numerical scheme for the Landau-Lifshitz equation with large damping parameters
Yongyong Cai, Jingrun Chen, Cheng Wang, Changjian Xie

Abstract
. A second order accurate, linear numerical method is analyzed for the Landau-Lifshitz equation with large damping parameters. This equation describes the dynamics of magnetization, with a non-convexity constraint of unit length of the magnetization. The numerical method is based on the second-order backward differentiation formula in time, combined with an implicit treatment of the linear diffusion term and explicit extrapolation for the nonlinear terms. Afterward, a projection step is applied to normalize the numerical solution at a point-wise level. This numerical scheme has shown extensive advantages in the practical computations for the physical model with large damping parameters, which comes from the fact that only a linear system with constant coefficients (independent of both time and the updated magnetization) needs to be solved at each time step, and has greatly improved the numerical efficiency. Meanwhile, a theoretical analysis for this linear numerical scheme has not been available. In this paper, we provide a rigorous error estimate of the numerical scheme, in the discrete ℓ ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , ℓ 2 ) ∩ ℓ 2 ​ ( 0 , T , H h 1 ) \ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell^{2})\cap\ell^{2}(0,T;H_{h}^{1}) norm, under suitable regularity assumptions and reasonable ratio between the time step-size and the spatial mesh-size. In particular, the projection operation is nonlinear, and a stability estimate for the projection step turns out to be highly challenging. Such a stability estimate is derived in details, which will play an essential role in the convergence analysis for the numerical scheme, if the damping parameter is greater than 3.

1.
Introduction

The Landau-Lifshitz (LL) equation
[
21
]
, with quasilinearity and the constraint of unit length of magnetization, describes the evolution of the magnetization in ferromagnetic materials with applications of information storage in the magnetic-based recording devices
[
24
]
. The nonlinear conservative term of the LL equation preserves the unit length of magnetization and drives the system. The remaining nonlinear part related to the harmonic mapping of the LL equation is dissipated by a factor of damping parameters.
Such a parameter plays an important role for energy evolution, which can be calculated
[
23
]
.

There have been extensive numerical works for the LL equation
[
15
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
. One of the most popular temporal discretization is the semi-implicit method
[
8
,
12
,
14
]
, which turns out to remarkably relax restrictions of temporal step-size.
For example, the linearly implicit backward Euler approach has been well studied in
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
14
]
. A combination of this numerical idea with a high-order non-conforming finite element discretization in space has been proposed and analyzed in
[
2
]
, in which a projection is applied to an approximate tangent space to the normality constraint. Moreover, a convergence analysis in both space and time has been established in
[
1
]
, by evaluating the approximated error of time derivative term which is orthogonal to the magnetization. The error estimates for linearly implicit schemes, based on either backward Euler or Crank-Nicolson method, combined with finite element/finite difference spatial discretization, have been obtained in
[
3
,
4
,
14
]
. The backward differentiation formula (BDF)-based linearly implicit methods have been analyzed in
[
1
,
8
]
, and a second-order accuracy have been rigorously proved under the same condition that temporal step-size proportional to the spatial mesh-size in both space and time.

Meanwhile, it is noticed that, all these existing numerical methods lead to an asymmetric linear system of equations with the coefficients dependent of the updated magnetization. An efficient numerical solver for such an asymmetric linear system is highly non-trivial, which usually results in a very expensive computation cost in the three-dimensional simulation. Therefore, a numerical scheme only involved with a constant coefficient linear system, so that the coefficients are independent of the updated magnetization, is highly desirable.
In fact, such a linear numerical scheme has been proposed and studied in a recent work
[
7
]
. In more details, the second-order BDF stencil is used in the temporal discretization, the perfect Laplace term (in the harmonic mapping part) is treated implicitly, while the nonlinear terms are approximated by explicit extrapolation formulas. After an intermediate magnetization vector is obtained by this linear algorithm, a projection of magnetization onto the unit sphere is applied, to satisfy the non-convex constraint of unit length. Of course, this numerical approach leads to a linear system with constant coefficients independent the updated magnetization at each time step. Because of this subtle fact, the linear numerical scheme has demonstrated great advantages in the simulation of ferromagnetic materials for large damping parameters
[
7
]
. Furthermore, extensive simulation experiments have indicated that the proposed linear numerical method preserves better stability property as the damping parameter takes large values, in comparison with all the existing works
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
6
,
8
]
, etc.

On the other hand, a theoretical analysis of the proposed linear numerical scheme has not been available, in spite of its extensive advantages in the practical computations for large damping parameters. The key theoretical difficulty is associated with the fact that, a fully explicit treatment of the nonlinear gyromagnetic term (by an extrapolation formula) breaks its (energetic) conservative feature at the numerical level, so that a direct control of this nonlinear term becomes a very challenging issue. The only hopeful approach is to control this term by the linear diffusion term in the harmonic mapping part, while the fact that the nonlinear gyromagnetic term and the linear diffusion term are updated by different temporal discretization makes this estimate highly non-trivial. In this article, we provide the convergence analysis and the optimal rate error estimate for the proposed linear numerical scheme, in the discrete
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell^{2})\cap\ell^{2}(0,T;H_{h}^{1})
norm, if the damping parameter is greater than 3. To overcome the above-mentioned difficulties, we build the stability estimate of the projection step, which will play a crucial role in the rigorous error estimate for the original error function. In particular, a standard
\ell^{2}
stability estimate for the projection step is not sufficient to recover the convergence analysis, and an
H_{h}^{1}
stability estimate turns out to be necessary at the projection step, which comes from the technique of controlling the nonlinear gyromagnetic term by the linear diffusion term. In more details, the
a-priori

W_{h}^{1,\infty}
estimate for the numerical solution and
a-priori

H_{h}^{1}
estimate for the intermediate numerical error function at the previous time step is needed in the error analysis. Meanwhile, the
a-priori

H_{h}^{1}
estimate for the numerical error for the magnetization vector can be controlled by a growth factor
1+\delta
acting on the
H_{h}^{1}
estimate of the intermediate magnetization error function, with
\delta
being arbitrary positive number. Such a
W_{h}^{1,\infty}
bound for numerical solution and
H_{h}^{1}
estimate for the intermediate numerical error function can be recovered at the next time step, with the help of the inverse inequality and a mild temporal constraint and large damping parameter (larger than three). In turn, an estimate for numerical error function becomes a straightforward consequence of an application of discrete Gronwall inequality, combined with the fine estimate of a growth factor
1+\delta
acting on the
H_{h}^{1}
estimate of the intermediate error function.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the fully discrete numerical schemes and state the main theoretical result of convergence. The detailed proof is provided in Section
3
.
Finally, some concluding remarks are made in Section
4
.

2.
The mathematical model and the numerical scheme

2.1.
The Landau-Lifshitz equation

The LL equation is formulated as

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)=-{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\times\Delta{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}+\alpha\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}+\alpha|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$m$},&\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
\partial_{\bf n}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\partial\Omega,\,t\geq 0,\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},0)=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}),&\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
x
and

are the variables of space and time, respectively,

(
d=1,2,3
, with
d
being the spatial dimension) is a bounded domain and
{\bf n}
is the unit outward normal vector along
\partial\Omega

{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}:=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})^{T}\,:\,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to S^{2}
represents the magnetization vector field with
|{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}|=1,\;\forall\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega

, and

represent the temporal derivative, the gradient and the Laplacian, respectively. The homogeneous Neumann boundary condition is considered. The first term on the right hand side of

is the damping term. In comparison with the ferromagnetic model
[
21
]
,

2.1
only includes the exchange term which poses the main difficulties in numerical analysis, as done in the literature
[

,
13
,
14
]
. To simplify the presentation, we set
\Omega=[0,1]^{d}
and consider the 3D case in this paper, while the results hold for the 1D and 2D models.

A semi-implicit numerical scheme has been proposed in
[
29
]
, and used in the numerical simulation for small damping parameter models. In more details, semi-implicit approximations are applied to the two nonlinear terms, namely
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}

\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$})

is treated implicitly, while the coefficient variables are explicitly updated via an extrapolation formula. The theoretical convergence analysis has been established in a more recent work
[
8

However, this numerical scheme involves a large linear system with time-dependent coefficients, related to the updated magnetization at each time step, and the symmetry is not available in the linear system, due to the nonlinear structure. To overcome this subtle difficulty, which leads to significant computational costs (especially in the 3D case), we make use of the alternate PDE formulation (
2.1

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+2}

The proof of the standard inverse inequality and discrete Gronwall inequality can be obtained in existing textbooks and references; we just cite the results here. In the sequel, for simplicity of notation, we will use the uniform constant

Lemma 2.1
.

(Inverse inequality)
[

,
10
,

For each vector-valued grid function

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma{h}^{-1/2}(\|{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}+\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}),\quad\|\nabla_{h}{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}\leq\gamma{h}^{-3/4}\|\nabla_{h}{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2},

The following estimate will be utilized in the convergence analysis. A rough version has been provided in a recent article

Lemma 2.2

(Discrete gradient acting on cross product)
.

For grid functions
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h},\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h},\mbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}
, we have for any
\delta>0

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\times\Delta_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h},\mbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}\times\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h},\Delta_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\right\rangle,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}({\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}_{h}\times{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}_{h})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta)\|{\mbox{\boldmath$f$}}_{h}\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}\|{\mbox{\boldmath$g$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}.

Proof.

Equality (
2.8
) has been proved in
[
8
]
, so that we only focus on the proof of (
2.9
).

At each numerical mesh cell, from
(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{\ell})
to
(\hat{x}_{i+1},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{\ell})
, the following expansion identity is valid:

(2.10)

\displaystyle D_{x}(\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\times\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell}=

\displaystyle(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell}\times(D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell}+(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell}\times(D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell},

\displaystyle\mbox{with}\,\,\,(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}((\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})_{i,j,\ell}+(\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})_{i+1,j,\ell}).

In turn, we get the following expansion, over each mesh cell:

(2.11)

D_{x}(\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\times\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})=(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})\times(D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})+(D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})\times(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}).

Subsequently, a careful application of discrete Hölder inequality reveals that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})\times(D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{2}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{2},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|(D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h})\times(A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}\cdot\|A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}\leq\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}\cdot\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4},

in which the fact that
\|A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}
,
\|A_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}
, has been applied. Then we get

(2.14)

\|D_{x}(\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\times\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})\|_{2}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{2}+\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}\cdot\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}.

The corresponding estimates in the
y
and
z
directions can be similarly derived, and the technical details are skipped for the sake of brevity.
A combination of (
2.14
) and its counterparts in
y
and
z
directions leads to

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}(\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\times\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h})\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{2}+3\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}\cdot\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+6\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}\cdot\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta)\|\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{2}+(3+9\delta^{-1})\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}\cdot\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}\|_{4}^{2},

for any
\delta>0
, in which the Cauchy inequality has been applied in the last step. Therefore, the nonlinear cross product estimate (
2.9
) has been derived. This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.

3.
The optimal rate convergence analysis: Proof of Theorem
2.1

Denote
\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)=\mathcal{P}_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}
(
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega_{h}
) and
\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t^{n})
(
n\geq 0
). Around the boundary section
z=0
, we set
\hat{z}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}h
,
\hat{z}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}h
, and we can extend the profile
\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}
to the numerical “ghost” points, according to the extrapolation formula
eq.

2.2
:

(3.1)

\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{i,j,0}=\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{i,j,1},\quad\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{i,j,N_{z}+1}=\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{i,j,N_{z}},

and the extrapolation for other boundaries can be formulated in the same manner. The proof of such an extrapolation yields a higher order
\mathcal{O}(h^{5})
approximation, instead of the standard
\mathcal{O}(h^{3})
accuracy has been applied in
[
8
]
. Also see the related works
[
25
,
28
,
27
]
in the existing literature.

Performing a careful Taylor expansion for the exact solution around the boundary section
z=0
, combined with the mesh point values:
\hat{z}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}h
,
\hat{z}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}h
, we get

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{0})

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{1})-h\partial_{z}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},0)-\frac{h^{3}}{24}\partial_{z}^{3}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},0)+\mathcal{O}(h^{5})

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{1})-\frac{h^{3}}{24}\partial_{z}^{3}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},0)+\mathcal{O}(h^{5}),

in which the homogenous boundary condition has been applied in the second step. It remains to determine
\partial_{z}^{3}\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},0)
, for which we use information from the rewritten PDE (
2.1
) and its derivatives. Applying
\partial_{z}
to the first evolutionary equation in (
2.1
) along the boundary section
\Gamma_{z}:z=0
gives

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(m_{1})_{zt}-2\alpha(m_{1}(\nabla m_{1}\cdot\nabla(m_{1})_{z}+\nabla m_{2}\cdot\nabla(m_{2})_{z}+\nabla m_{3}\cdot\nabla(m_{3})_{z}))\\
&-\alpha|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}|^{2}(m_{1})_{z}-\alpha((m_{1})_{zxx}+(m_{1})_{zyy}+\partial_{z}^{3}m_{1})\\
=&(m_{3})_{z}\Delta m_{2}+m_{3}((m_{2})_{zxx}+(m_{2})_{zyy}+\partial_{z}^{3}m_{2})\\
&-(m_{2})_{z}\Delta m_{3}-m_{2}((m_{3})_{zxx}+(m_{3})_{zyy}+\partial_{z}^{3}m_{3}),\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z}.\end{aligned}

The first, third terms, and the first two parts in the fourth term on the left-hand side of (
3.3
) disappear, due to the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition for
m_{1}
. For the second term on the left hand side, we observe that

(3.4)

\nabla m_{1}\cdot\nabla(m_{1})_{z}=(m_{1})_{x}\cdot(m_{1})_{zx}+(m_{1})_{y}\cdot(m_{1})_{zy}+(m_{1})_{z}\cdot(m_{1})_{zz}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z},

since
(m_{1})_{z}=0
on the boundary section. Similar derivations could be made to the two other terms on the left hand side:

(3.5)

\nabla m_{2}\cdot\nabla(m_{2})_{z}=0,\,\,\,\nabla m_{3}\cdot\nabla(m_{3})_{z}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z}.

Meanwhile, on the right hand side of (
3.3
), we see that the first and third terms, as well as the first two parts in the second and fourth terms, disappear, which comes from the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition for
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
. Then we arrive at

(3.6)

\alpha\partial_{z}^{3}m_{1}=-m_{3}\partial_{z}^{3}m_{2}+m_{2}\partial_{z}^{3}m_{3},\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z}.

Similarly, we are able to derive the following equalities:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\alpha\partial_{z}^{3}m_{2}=m_{1}\partial_{z}^{3}m_{3}-m_{3}\partial_{z}^{3}m_{1},\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z}

\displaystyle\alpha\partial_{z}^{3}m_{3}=m_{2}\partial_{z}^{3}m_{1}-m_{1}\partial_{z}^{3}m_{2},\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z}.

In turn, for any
\alpha>0
, we observe that the matrix
\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha&m_{3}&-m_{2}\\
-m_{3}&\alpha&m_{1}\\
m_{2}&-m_{1}&\alpha\end{array}\right)
has a positive determinant, so that the linear system (
3.6
)-(
3.7
) has only one trivial solution:

(3.8)

\partial_{z}^{3}m_{1}=\partial_{z}^{3}m_{2}=\partial_{z}^{3}m_{3}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma_{z}.

As a result, an
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{5})
consistency accuracy for the symmetric extrapolation is obtained:

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{0})=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{e}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{1})+\mathcal{O}(h^{5}),\;\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{0})=\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{1})+\mathcal{O}(h^{5}).

In other words, the extrapolation formula
eq.

3.1
is indeed
\mathcal{O}(h^{5})
accurate.

Subsequently, a detailed calculation of Taylor expansion, in both time and space, leads to the following truncation error estimate:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{\frac{3}{2}\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})-2\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+1}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})+\frac{1}{2}\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})}{{k}}\\
&=-\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}+\tau^{n+2}+\alpha\Delta_{h}\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}+\alpha|{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\nabla_{h}\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}|^{2}\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2},\,\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega_{h}^{0},\end{aligned}

where
\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}=2\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+1}-\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}
,
\tau^{n+2}\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}
and
\|\tau^{n+2}\|_{2}\leq\mathcal{C}({k}^{2}+h^{2})
. Introducing the numerical error functions

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}=\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}-\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}=\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}

eq.

2.3
-
eq.

2.4

3.10

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}}{{k}}&=-\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\left(2{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\right)\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\underline{\bm{m}}}_{h}^{n+2}\\
&+\alpha\Delta_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}+\alpha|{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\nabla_{h}\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}|^{2}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}+\tau^{n+2}\\
&+\alpha\Big({\mathcal{A}}_{h}\nabla_{h}(\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}+\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2})\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\nabla_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\Big)\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}}{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\frac{C\cdot{k}^{\frac{31}{16}}}{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C{k}^{\frac{7}{16}}\leq{k}^{\frac{3}{8}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}}{h^{\frac{3}{4}}}\leq\frac{C\cdot{k}^{\frac{11}{8}}}{h^{\frac{3}{4}}}\leq C{k}^{\frac{5}{8}}\leq{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{3}.

Subsequently, the triangle inequality yields the desired
W_{h}^{1,4}
bound for the numerical solutions
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}
and
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}
(
n\leq q+1
):

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{h}\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n}-\nabla_{h}{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}\leq\|\nabla_{h}\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}+\|\nabla_{h}{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}\leq\mathcal{C}+\frac{1}{3}.

Furthermore, we need a sharper
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}

, which will be needed in the later error analysis. The following extrapolation estimate is valid, due to the

(3.17)

\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n+2}=2\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n+1}-\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n}+{\mathcal{O}}({k}^{2}).

Meanwhile, since
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)|\equiv 1

), we conclude that

(3.18)

\|2\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n+1}-\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq 1+{\mathcal{C}}{k}^{2},\quad n\leq q+1.

Its combination with the
a-priori

\|{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq{k}+h
, for
n\leq q+1
, (as given by (

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\|2\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|2\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n+1}-\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}+\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+{\mathcal{C}}{k}^{2}+3{k}^{\frac{3}{8}}\leq\alpha_{1}:=\Big(\frac{3+\alpha}{6}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

provided that
{k}
and
h
are sufficiently small. In addition, we denote
\gamma_{0}:=\alpha-3>0
, so that
\alpha_{1}^{2}=1+\frac{1}{6}\gamma_{0}

Next, we perform a discrete
\ell^{2}

3.11

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\in\mbox{\boldmath$X$}
(

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} R.H.S.=&\left\langle-\left(2{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n}\right)\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\right\rangle\\
&-\left\langle\left(2{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\right)\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tau^{n+2},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\right\rangle\\

&+\alpha\Big\langle\Big({\mathcal{A}}_{h}\nabla_{h}(\hat{\underline{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}_{h}^{n+2}+\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2})\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\nabla_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\Big)\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\Big\rangle\\
=:&\tilde{I}_{1}+\tilde{I}_{2}+\tilde{I}_{3}-\alpha\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{I}_{5}+\tilde{I}_{6}.\end{aligned}

Then all the terms are accordingly analyzed. For the term
\tilde{I}_{1}
, a combination of the summation by parts formula by and Cauchy inequality results in

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{I}_{1}=&\left\langle-\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2},-\Delta_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\right\rangle\\

Meanwhile, an application of the cross product gradient estimate (
2.9

\delta>0
, the following inequality is valid:

(3.22)

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla_{h}\Big(\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\Big)\Big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta)\|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{4}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta)\alpha_{1}^{2}\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{4}^{2},

where

\delta
, in which the
a-priori
bound estimates (
3.15
) and (
3.19
) have been applied. The term

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}(2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n})\|_{2}^{2}\leq 6\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+3\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle(1+\delta)(6\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+3\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2})+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}),

in which the estimate (
2.19

assumption (
3.13

3.23

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{I}_{1}\leq\,&\frac{3}{2}\Big\|\nabla_{h}\Big(\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{n+2}\Big)\Big\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\|\nabla_{h}\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}\\
\leq\,&(1+\delta)\Big(\frac{3}{2}\alpha_{1}^{2}\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)\\
&+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{4}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}).\end{aligned}

For the term

, by the preliminary estimate (

) for the exact solution, we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{I}_{2}=\,&-\left\langle\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\times\Delta_{h}\hat{\underline{\bm{m}}}_{h}^{n+2},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\right\rangle\leq\,\frac{1}{2}\big[\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\|\Delta_{h}\hat{\underline{\bm{m}}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{\infty}^{2}\big]\\

For the term

In terms of

\displaystyle L.H.S.=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4{k}}\big(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|2\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}\big).

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{4{k}}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|2\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2})\\

where

Meanwhile, for the

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4{k}}\big(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|2\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2})

In particular, we observe that

(3.33)

\displaystyle\big(\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\alpha_{1}^{2}(1+\delta)-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{12}\Big)-\frac{3}{2}(1+\delta)(1+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{12})

As a result, by (

.

) (in Lemmas

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(1-{k}^{\frac{5}{4}})\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+(1-{k}^{\frac{1}{4}})\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle\Big|\langle\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{p}\rangle\Big|\leq{k}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{p}\|_{2}^{2}+{k}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2},\,\,p=n+1,n+2.

:

(3.39)

\displaystyle\|2\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+3}\|_{2}^{2}+4\langle\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2},\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+3}\rangle+4\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-8{k}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}-8{k}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-4{k}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-4{k}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+4\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+(4-12{k}^{\frac{1}{4}})\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Going back (

), we arrive at the following estimate, for

(3.40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4{k}}\big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}-\|2\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{5-13{k}^{\frac{1}{4}}}{4{k}}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}+\Big(\alpha_{2}-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{12}\Big)\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1+\delta)(1+\frac{\gamma_{0}}{12})\Big(\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\delta}\sum_{p=n}^{n+2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{p}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{p}\|_{2}^{2}\Big)+\mathcal{C}({k}^{4}+h^{4}).

Meanwhile, for the terms

-

In addition, the explicit treatment of the Laplace term, namely

Remark 3.2
.

In a recent work
[
7
]
, a rough stability estimate for the projection step, namely,

Extensive numerical experiments have demonstrated a better stability property of the temporal stencil in

4.
Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented an optimal rate convergence analysis and error estimate for a second-order accurate, linear numerical scheme to the LL equation. The second-order backward differentiation formula is applied in the temporal discretization, the linear diffusion term is treated implicitly, while the nonlinear terms are updated by a fully explicit extrapolation formula. Afterward, a point-wise projection is applied to normalize the magnetization vector. In turn, only a linear system independent of the updated magnetization needs to be solved at each time step, which has greatly improved the computational efficiency, and many great advantages of this numerical scheme have been reported in the numerical simulation with large damping parameters. The error estimate has been theoretically established in the discrete
\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell^{2})\cap\ell^{2}(0,T;H_{h}^{1})
norm, under suitable regularity assumptions and reasonable ratio between the time step-size and the spatial mesh-size. The key difficulty of the theoretical analysis is associated with the fact that the projection step is highly nonlinear and non-convex. To overcome this subtle difficulty, we build a stability estimate for the projection step, which plays a crucial role in the derivation of the convergence analysis for the numerical scheme.

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\max\Big(\frac{2}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|+|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|^{2}}\Big)\cdot\|D_{x}\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{3}{2}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}})C^{*}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}\leq(1+\frac{3}{2}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}})M\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}.

Therefore, a substitution of (
4.19
), (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) into (
4.11
) leads to

(4.22)

\displaystyle\Big\|D_{x}\frac{\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|}-D_{x}\Big(\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\frac{2\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\cdot\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|+|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|^{2}}\Big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{5}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}})\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+3M(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}).

The analysis for the last term on the right hand side of (
4.10
) can be similarly carried out; some technical details are skipped for the sake of brevity.

(4.23)

\displaystyle\Big\|D_{x}\Big(\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\frac{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}|^{2}}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|+|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|^{2}}\Big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{C}}\Big(\|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\max\Big(\frac{2}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|+|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|^{2}}\Big)\cdot\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|D_{x}\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\max\Big(\frac{1}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|+|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|^{2}}\Big)\cdot\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\max\Big(\frac{1}{(|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|+|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}|^{2})^{2}}\Big)\cdot(1+2\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}\|_{\infty})\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}\|_{4}

\displaystyle\cdot\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{C}}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}(\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4})\leq{k}^{\frac{5}{8}}(\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}).

Finally, a substitution of (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) into (
4.10
) yields

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+2{k}^{\frac{5}{8}})\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+(3M+1)(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}),\quad\mbox{so that}

\displaystyle\|D_{x}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+5{k}^{\frac{5}{8}})\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+(3M+1)^{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4})^{2}

\displaystyle+4(3M+1)\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+5{k}^{\frac{5}{8}})\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+2(3M+1)^{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta}{4}\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+16(3M+1)^{2}\delta^{-1}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\delta}{2})\|D_{x}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+(32\delta^{-1}+2)(3M+1)^{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}),

for any
\delta>0
, provided that
{k}
is sufficiently small. Similar estimates can be derived for the gradient in the
y
and
z
directions; the technical details are skipped for the sake of brevity.

(4.25)

\displaystyle\|D_{y}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\delta}{2})\|D_{y}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+(32\delta^{-1}+2)(3M+1)^{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}),

\displaystyle\|D_{z}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\delta}{2})\|D_{z}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+(32\delta^{-1}+2)(3M+1)^{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}).

Then we arrive at

(4.26)

\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\frac{\delta}{2})\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+3(32\delta^{-1}+2)(3M+1)^{2}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}).

Meanwhile, by the discrete Sobolev inequality (
2.16
) (in Lemma
2.3
), we get

(4.27)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}\leq\mathcal{C}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{4}}),

so that

(4.28)

\displaystyle 3(32\delta^{-1}+2)(3M+1)^{2}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3(32\delta^{-1}+2)(3M+1)^{2}\mathcal{C}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\delta>0,

in which the Young’s inequality has been applied in the last step. Going back (
4.26
), we obtain

(4.29)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\delta}{2})\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}\leq(1+\delta)\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2}+{\mathcal{C}}_{\delta}\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}\|_{2},

provided that
k
is sufficiently small. This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.4
. ∎

Appendix B. Proof of
2.5

Proof.

Here
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(1)}
and
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(2)}
serve as the exact solution at different time steps and
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(1)},\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(2)}\in X
are the corresponding numerical solutions.

The
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
bounds for the error functions
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(q)}
can be derived, with the help of the
a-priori
estimate (
2.20
):

(4.30)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(q)}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma h^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(q)}\|_{2}+\|\nabla_{h}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(q)}\|_{2})\leq\gamma h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot{k}^{\frac{11}{8}}\leq\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}},

\displaystyle\mbox{so that}\,\,1-\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q)}|\leq 1+\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}},\,\,\mbox{at a point-wise level, \, $q=1,2$}.

For the numerical error functions at different time steps, the decomposition (
4.5
) is still valid:

(4.31)

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(q)}=\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(q)}+\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h,c}^{(q)},\quad\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h,c}^{(q)}:=\frac{\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q)}}{|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q)}|}(1-|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q)}|).

At a fixed grid point
(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j},\hat{z}_{k})
, we look at the triangle formed by the vectors:
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q)}
,
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(q)}
,
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}
, and denote the angle between
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q))}
and
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}
as
\theta_{q}
. In turn, the estimate (
4.6
) is laid for each
\theta_{q}
:

(4.32)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(q)}|\leq 1+\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}},\,\,\,|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}|\leq\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}},\,\,\,|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}|\equiv 1,

\displaystyle 0\leq\sin\theta_{j}\leq\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}},\quad q=1,2.

Similarly, in the triangle formed by the vectors:
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(q)}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(q)}
,
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}
, two sides have equal lengths:
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(q)}|=|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}|=1
, and the angle between
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(q)}
and
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}
is exactly
\theta_{q}
. In turn, the angle between vectors
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{(q)}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(q)}
, as well as the angle between vectors
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(q)}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(q)}
is given by
\varphi^{(q)}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\theta_{q}}{2}
.

Meanwhile, we denote the angle between
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(1)}
and
\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(2)}
as
\theta^{*}
. By the
a-priori
assumption and the fact that
|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(1)}|=|\underline{\bm{m}}_{h}^{(2)}|=1
, we have an estimate for
\theta^{*}
:

(4.33)

2\sin\frac{\theta^{*}}{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{7}{8}}.

Subsequently, we denote the angle between
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}_{h}^{(1)}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(2)}
as
\phi^{*}
. By the above analyses, we see that

(4.34)

\phi^{*}=\varphi^{(2)}\pm(\theta_{1}+\theta^{*})=\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}\pm(\theta_{1}+\theta^{*}).

In either case,
\phi^{*}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}+(\theta_{1}+\theta^{*})
or
\phi^{*}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}-(\theta_{1}+\theta^{*})
, the following estimate is valid:

(4.35)

\displaystyle|\cos\phi^{*}|=

\displaystyle|\sin(\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}\pm(\theta_{1}+\theta^{*}))|\leq\sin(\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}+\theta_{1}+\theta^{*})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sin\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}+\sin\theta_{1}+\sin\theta^{*}\leq\sin\theta_{2}+\sin\theta_{1}+\sin\theta^{*}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\frac{1}{4}{k}^{\frac{7}{8}}\leq\frac{3}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}},

in which we have used the triangular inequality,
\sin(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3})\leq\sin s_{1}+\sin s_{2}+\sin s_{3}
, provided that
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}>0
are sufficiently small. As a consequence, the definition of the point-wise inner product implies the following estimate

(4.36)

\displaystyle\Big|(\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(1)}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(1)})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(2)}\Big|=

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(1)}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(1)}|\cdot|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(2)}|\cdot|\cos\phi^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(1)}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(1)}|\cdot|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(2)}|\cdot\frac{3}{4}{k}^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle k}^{\frac{1}{4}}|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(1)}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{(1)}|^{2}+{k}^{\frac{4}{5}}|\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}}_{h}^{(2)}|^{2}.

Again, this estimate is valid at a point-wise level for
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\Omega_{h}^{0}
. Therefore, a summation in space leads to the first inequality (
2.22
). ∎

Chromatic index of dense quasirandom graphs
Songling Shan

Abstract




Abstract
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with maximum degree
\Delta(G)
. A subgraph
H
of
G
is overfull if
|E(H)|>\Delta(G)\lfloor|V(H)|/2\rfloor
. Chetwynd and Hilton in 1985 conjectured that a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\Delta(G)>n/3

has chromatic index
\Delta(G)
if and only if
G
contains no overfull subgraph. Glock, Kühn and Osthus in 2016 showed that the conjecture is true for
dense quasirandom graphs with even order, and they conjectured that the same should
hold for such graphs with odd order. In this paper,
we show that the conjecture of Glock, Kühn and Osthus is affirmative.

Keywords
.
Chromatic index; overfull graph; quasirandom graph.

1
Introduction

In this paper, a graph means a simple graph;
and a multigraph may contain parallel edges but no loops.
Let
G
be a multigraph.
Denote by
V(G)
and
E(G)
the vertex set and edge set of
G
,
respectively. For
v\in V(G)
,
N_{G}(v)
is the set of neighbors of
v

in
G
, and

d_{G}(v)
, the degree of
v

in
G
, is the number of edges of
G
that are incident with
v
.
When
G
is simple,
d_{G}(v)=|N_{G}(v)|
.
For

S\subseteq V(G)
, the subgraph of
G
induced on
S
is denoted by
G[S]
, and
G-S:=G[V(G)\setminus S]
.
For notational simplicity, we write
G-x
for
G-\{x\}
.
If
F\subseteq E(G)
, then
G-F
is obtained from
G
by deleting all
the edges of
F
.
Let
V_{1},V_{2}\subseteq V(G)
be two disjoint vertex sets. Then
E_{G}(V_{1},V_{2})
is the set
of edges in
G
with one end in
V_{1}
and the other end in
V_{2}
, and
e_{G}(V_{1},V_{2}):=|E_{G}(V_{1},V_{2})|
. We write
E_{G}(v,V_{2})
and
e_{G}(v,V_{2})

if
V_{1}=\{v\}
is a singleton.
We also write
G[V_{1},V_{2}]

to denote the bipartite subgraph of
G
with vertex set
V_{1}\cup V_{2}

and edge set
E_{G}(V_{1},V_{2})
.

For two integers
p,q
, let
[p,q]=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,p\leq i\leq q\}
.
An
edge
k
-coloring
of
G
is a mapping
\varphi
from
E(G)
to the set of integers

[1,k]
, called
colors
, such that no two adjacent edges receive the same color with respect to
\varphi
.
The
chromatic index
of
G
, denoted
\chi^{\prime}(G)
, is defined to be the smallest integer
k
so that
G
has an edge
k
-coloring.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}^{k}(G)
the set of all edge
k
-colorings of
G
.
A graph
G
is
\Delta
-critical
if
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1=\Delta+1
and
\chi^{\prime}(H)<\Delta+1
for every proper subgraph
H
of
G
.
In 1960’s, Vizing
[
22
]
and, independently, Gupta
[
8
]
proved
that for all simple graphs
G
,
\Delta(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
.
This leads to a natural classification of graphs. Following Fiorini and Wilson
[
5
]
, we say a graph
G
is of
class 1
if
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
and of
class 2
if
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1
. Holyer
[
10
]
showed that it is NP-complete to determine whether an arbitrary graph is of class 1.
Nevertheless, if a graph
G
has too many edges, i.e.,
|E(G)|>\Delta(G)\lfloor|V(G)|/2\rfloor
, then we have to color
E(G)
using exactly
(\Delta(G)+1)
colors. Such graphs are
overfull
. An overfull subgraph
H
of
G
with
\Delta(H)=\Delta(G)

is called a
\Delta(G)
-overfull subgraph
of
G
.

Applying Edmonds’ matching polytope theorem, Seymour
[
16
]
showed that whether a graph
G
contains an overfull subgraph of maximum degree
\Delta(G)
can be determined in polynomial time. A number of long-standing conjectures listed in
Twenty Pretty Edge Coloring Conjectures
in
[
18
]
lie in deciding when a
\Delta
-critical graph is overfull. Chetwynd and Hilton
[
2
,
3
]
, in 1986, proposed the following
conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Overfull conjecture)
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
\Delta(G)>\frac{1}{3}|V(G)|
. Then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
if and only if
G
contains no
\Delta(G)
-overfull subgraph.

The degree condition
\Delta(G)>\frac{1}{3}|V(G)|
in the conjecture above is best possible, as seen by the
graph
P^{*}
, which is obtained from the Petersen graph by deleting one vertex. If the overfull conjecture is true, then the NP-complete problem of
determining the chromatic index becomes polynomial-time solvable
for graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)>\frac{|V(G)|}{3}
.
Despite its importance, very little is known about its truth.
It was confirmed only for graphs with
\Delta(G)\geq|V(G)|-3
by
Chetwynd and Hilton
[
3
]
in 1989. By restricting the minimum degree,
Plantholt
[
15
]
in 2004 showed that the overfull conjecture is affirmative for
graphs
G
with even order
n
and minimum degree
\delta\geq\sqrt{7}n/3\approx 0.8819n
.
The 1-factorization conjecture is a special case of the overfull conjecture, which in 2013
was confirmed for large graphs by Csaba, Kühn, Lo, Osthus and Treglown
[
4
]
. The overfull conjecture is still wide open in general, and it seems
extremely difficult even for graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)\geq|V(G)|-4
.

Recently in 2016, Glock, Kühn and Osthus
[
6
]
showed that the
overfull conjecture is true for dense quasirandom graphs of even order.
Following their definition, for the notion of quasirandomness,
the following one-sided version of
\varepsilon
-regularity will be considered. Let
0<\varepsilon,p<1
. A graph
G

on
n
vertices is called
lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular
if we have
e_{G}(S,T)\geq(p-\varepsilon)|S||T|
for all disjoint
S,T\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|,|T|\geq\varepsilon n
.
In particular, the following result was proved in
[
6
, Theorem 1.6]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
0<p<1
there exist
\varepsilon,\eta>0
such that for sufficiently
large
n
, the following holds: Suppose
G
is a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular
graph on
n
vertices and
n
is even. Moreover, assume that
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)\leq\eta n
. Then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)

if and only if
G
contains no
\Delta(G)
-overfull subgraph. Further, there is a polynomial
time algorithm which finds an optimal coloring.

Glock, Kühn and Osthus
[
6
]
conjectured that the same result as in Theorem
1.2
should hold for such graphs
G
with odd order.
We here confirm the conjecture.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
0<p<1
there exist
\varepsilon,\eta>0
such that for sufficiently
large
n
, the following holds: Suppose
G
is a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular
graph on
n
vertices and
n
is odd. Moreover, assume that
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)\leq\eta n
. Then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)

if and only if
G
is not overfull. Further, there is a polynomial
time algorithm which finds an optimal coloring.

For a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular graph with odd order
n
,
it is easy to see that for any subset
X\subseteq V(G)

with
|X|
odd and
3\leq|X|\leq n-2
, we have
e_{G}(X,V(G)\setminus X)\geq\Delta(G)
. Thus
G[X]
is not
\Delta(G)
-overfull. Therefore, the only possible
\Delta(G)
-overfull subgraph in
G
is
G
itself.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
In the next section, we list some preliminary results on
quasirandom graphs and edge colorings.
In Section 3,
we study the chromatic index of a regular
lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular star-multigraph, which is obtained
from a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular graph by adding a new vertex and some edges
between the graph and the new vertex. In the last section, we prove
Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<1/n_{0}\ll\varepsilon,\gamma\ll\alpha,p<1
, and
G
be a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular
star-multigraph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices such that
\delta(G)\geq\alpha n
.
Let
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
be disjoint and
|X|=|Y|
,
and
H
be a graph with
V(H)=X\cup Y

and

obtained from
E_{G}(X,Y)
by deleting some edges.
If
\delta(H)>\frac{1}{4}\alpha n
and each vertex

is incident with at most
\gamma n
edges from
E_{G}(X,Y)\setminus E(H)
,
then

has a perfect matching. Furthermore, a perfect matching of
H
can be found in polynomial time.

Proof
. If the multi-center of
G
is contained in
X\cup Y
, we may assume by symmetry that the multi-center is contained in
X
.
To have a uniform proof,
if the multi-center of
G
is contained in
X
, we let
x

be the multi-center; otherwise we let

be an arbitrary vertex
from
X
.

Since
\delta(H)>0
,

has in

y\in Y
. Let
X_{1}=X\setminus\{x\}
and
Y_{1}=Y\setminus\{y\}
.
It suffices to show that
H_{1}:=H[X_{1},Y_{1}]
satisfies Hall’s condition. For otherwise,
there exists
A\subseteq X_{1}
such that
B:=N_{H_{1}}(A)
satisfying
|B|<|A|
. Since
\delta(H_{1})\geq\frac{1}{4}\alpha n-1
and
|B|<|A|
, it follows that
|A|>\frac{1}{4}\alpha n-1
.
On the other hand, since
E_{H_{1}}(A,Y_{1}\setminus B)=\emptyset
,
Y_{1}\setminus B\neq\emptyset
, and
\delta(H_{1})\geq\frac{1}{4}\alpha n-1

. Let

2.2
Results on edges colorings

For any

For a vertex set

The set

\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(v)=\emptyset

for every two distinct vertices
u,v\in X
. For two distinct colors

\alpha
or

(\alpha,\beta)
-chains. Clearly, each
(\alpha,\beta)
-chain is either a path or an even cycle.
If we interchange the colors

-chain

-coloring of

. This operation is a

.
For an

-chain

to stress the endvertex

An
(\alpha,\beta)
-swap

Let

-chain of

Otherwise,

The fan argument was introduced by Vizing
[
20

Let

(F1)

For every edge

\varphi(rs_{i})\in\overline{\varphi}(s_{j})

The following result regarding a multifan can be found in

Proved by König

Theorem 2.7

Lemma 2.8

Proof
. Assume to the contrary that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\geq\Delta(G)+2
. By deleting edges and vertices from

if necessary, we may assume that
every edge

\chi^{\prime}(G-e)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
.
Assume first that there exists

.
Let

. Since

\chi^{\prime}(G-uv)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)-1

Let

By Lemma

Since

\varphi

|\overline{\varphi}(V(F))|\geq 2+(k-d_{G}(u))+d_{G}(u)=k+2

Thus we assume that every vertex of

is incident with some multiple edges.
Since

is a star-multigraph, it has a vertex

. Let

Since

Let

\chi^{\prime}(H)\leq t+1

By assigning a different color to each of the edges in

This gives a contradiction to the assumption that

.

For the complexity of edge coloring

using at most

[1,\Delta(G)+1]
.
Misra and Gries

described a polynomial time algorithm for coloring the edges of any simple graph

with at most

(\Delta(H)+1)
colors.
Thus we first edge color

.
Then we color the other edges of

greedily, which results in

\Delta(G)+1

O(|N_{G}(x)|+\Delta(G))
steps to
edge color
G[N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}]
using at most
\Delta(G)+1
colors.
If all edges of
G
are already colored, then we are done.
Otherwise, we greedily color the other edges of
G
until we encounter

uv_{1}
such that

v_{1}

do not have a common missing color with respect to the current edge coloring, say
\varphi
.
We may also assume that for any color
\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(u)

and any color
\beta\in\overline{\varphi}(v_{1})

v_{1}
are
(\alpha,\beta)
-linked.
By symmetry, assume

We construct a multifan centered at

and

. Assume

As shown in the first paragraph of

Assume fist that there exist

with

and a color

\gamma\in\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(v_{i})

we may assume

is the sequence such that
\varphi(uv_{j})\in\overline{\varphi}(v_{j-1})

for

We color the edge

using the color on

using the color on

by the color
\gamma

there exist distinct

and a color
\gamma
such that

\gamma\in\overline{\varphi}(v_{i})\cap\overline{\varphi}(v_{j})

\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(u)

As at least one of
v_{i}

and

is
(\alpha,\gamma)
-unlinked with

v_{j}
, we do an
(\alpha,\gamma)
-swap at

. (When both of

and

-unlinked with

is still a multifan with respect to

and the current edge coloring,
but

have a common missing color. We can then again color

as in the first case. Thus we can color all the remaining edges of

in this way using at most

colors. For each uncolored edge such as

,
it takes
O(|V(G)|)
steps to have it colored. As it takes

steps to
edge color

it then takes
O(|E(G)||V(G)|)

\Delta(G)+1

∎

Given an edge coloring of
G
,
since all vertices not missing a given color

in

Lemma 2.9

(Parity Lemma)
.

Let

n
-vertex multigraph and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(G)
for some integer

.
Then for any color
\alpha\in[1,\Delta]
,

|\{v\in V(G):\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(v)\}|\equiv n\pmod{2}
.

Let
G
be a multigraph,

be an integer and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(G)
.
For a subset

of

and a color

\overline{\varphi}_{X}^{-1}(i)=\{v\in X:i\in\overline{\varphi}(v)\}

e(X)=|E(G[X])|
.
An edge
k
-coloring of a multigraph

observed the following result.

Theorem 2.10

Let

be a graph with chromatic index

with
k

We will need the following weaker version of “equalized” edge

Lemma 2.11
.

Let

be a star-multigraph on

and

.
If

, and

k

then there exists an edge coloring
\varphi

using
k

|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|=|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|

and
\left||\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|-|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(j)|\right|\leq 2
. Furthermore,

\varphi
can be found in

-time.

Proof
. We first show that there exists an edge

-coloring
\varphi
of

|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|=|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|

d_{\varphi}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\left||\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|-|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|\right|

d_{\varphi}\geq 1
. By the Parity Lemma,
for each

|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|+|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|\equiv 2n\pmod{2}
. Thus
|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|-|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|\equiv 2n\pmod{2}
. Since

, we assume, by symmetry, that there exists
i\in[1,k]

|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|\equiv|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|\pmod{2}

|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|-|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|\geq 2
.
Since

\sum_{x\in A}d_{G}(x)=\sum_{y\in B}d_{G}(y)

\df(A):=\sum\limits_{v\in A}(k-d_{G}(v))=\sum\limits_{v\in B}(k-d_{G}(v))=:\df(B).

On the other hand,

\df(A)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|\quad\text{and}\quad\df(B)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|.

Therefore
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(i)|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|
.
Consequently, by the existence of

there exists

Next, we will do some Kempe changes on some
(i,j)
-chains to obtain another

-coloring
\varphi^{\prime}

\varphi

If there exist distinct

u

and

(i,j)
-linked with respect to
\varphi

\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi/P_{u}(i,j,\varphi)
.

, showing a contradiction to the choice of
\varphi
.
Thus for any two distinct

and

(i,j)
-unlinked with respect to
\varphi

and

(i,j)
-unlinked with respect to
\varphi

Thus, for every

Since
|\overline{\varphi}_{A}^{-1}(j)|+|\overline{\varphi}_{B}^{-1}(i)|\leq t_{B}-2+t_{A}-2

there exists

and

(i,j)
-linked with respect to

We then let

(p,\varepsilon)
-regular star-multigraphs

We will need the following version of Chernoff bound. (See e.g.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be mutually independent random variables that satisfy
E(X_{i})=0
and
|X_{i}|\leq 1

for each

. Then for any

\pr(|S|>a)<2e^{-a^{2}/2n}.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a positive integer

such that for all

the

be a graph on
2n

(i)

|A|=|B|
;

(ii)

|A\cap\{x_{i},y_{i}\}|=1

(iii)

Proof
. Set
A

n

(x_{i},y_{i})

and the other to

(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})

a_{i}\in A

b_{i}\in B

X_{i}=e_{G}(v,a_{i})-e_{G}(v,b_{i}).

Clearly,
X_{i}\in\{-1,0,1\}

|X_{i}|\leq 1

If
a_{i},b_{i}\in N(v)

.
If

\pr(X_{i}=1)=\pr(X_{i}=-1)=1/2

Thus

are mutually independent. Let

Then

\displaystyle\pr(|d_{A}(v)-d_{B}(v)|>n^{2/3}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pr(|S|>n^{2/3}-1)\leq\pr(|S|>0.9n^{2/3})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2e^{-0.4n^{1/3}},

where when

There are
2n

2.8
. In Step 2, the construction of the alternating paths and swaps of the colors on the paths can be done in
O(n^{3})
-time, as the total number of colors missing at vertices is
O(n^{2})
and it takes
O(n)
-time to find an alternating path for a pair of vertices
(a,b)
with a common missing color.
In Step 3, there is polynomial time algorithm (see e.g.
[
14
]
) to edge color
R_{A}
and
R_{B}
using at most
\ell
colors; then by
doing Kempe changes as in the proof of Lemma
2.8
, one can obtain
an equalized edge-coloring in polynomial time.
The remaining procedures are only about finding perfect matchings in
bipartite graphs, which can be done in polynomial time too such as
applying the Hopcroft-Karp algorithm
[
11
]
.
Thus, there is a polynomial time
algorithm that gives a 1-factorization of
G
.
∎

4
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We need the following classical result of Hakimi
[
9
]
on multigraphic degree sequence.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0\leq d_{n}\leq\ldots\leq d_{1}
be integers. Then there exists a multigraph
G

on vertices
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
such that
d_{G}(x_{i})=d_{i}

for all
i
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
is even and
\sum_{i>1}d_{i}\geq d_{1}
.

Though it is not explicitly stated in
[
9
]
, the inductive proof yields a polynomial
time algorithm which finds an appropriate multigraph if it exists.

Let
G
be a graph and
v\in V(G)
. Define
\df_{G}(v)=\Delta(G)-d_{G}(v)
to be the
deficiency
of
v
in
G
.
It is clear that
G
is overfull if and only if
|V(G)|
is odd and
\sum\limits_{v\in V(G)}\df_{G}(v)\leq\Delta(G)-2
.
The proof below follows the ideas of Glock, Kühn, and Osthus in the proof of Theorem
1.2
from
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
0<p<1
there exist
\varepsilon,\eta>0
such that for sufficiently
large
n
, the following holds: Suppose
G
is a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular
graph on
n
vertices and
n
is odd. Moreover, assume that
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)\leq\eta n
. Then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)

if and only if
G
is not overfull. Further, there is a polynomial
time algorithm which finds an optimal coloring.

Proof
. Choose constants
\varepsilon,\eta
and positive integer
n_{0}
such that
0<1/n_{0}\ll\varepsilon\leq\eta\ll p
. Let
G
be a
lower
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices such that
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)\leq\eta n
, where
n
is odd.
For any
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|=\lceil\varepsilon n\rceil
, we have
E_{G}(X,V(G)\setminus X)\geq(p-\varepsilon)(n-\varepsilon n-1)|X|
. Thus the average degree of a vertex from
X
is at least
(p-\varepsilon)(n-\varepsilon n-1)
, and do
\delta(G)\geq(p-\varepsilon)(n-\varepsilon n-1)-\eta n>\frac{p}{2}n
. We let
\alpha=p/2
and use
\alpha n
as a lower bound on
\delta(G)

in the following.

If
G
is overfull, then
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1
. Thus assume
G
is not overfull.
We will now add a new vertex to
G
to form a multigraph with even order.
Let
x
be a new vertex
and let
G^{\prime}
be obtained from
G
by adding
x
to
G

and adding some edges between
x

and vertices
y\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(y)<\Delta(G)

with the following constraints:

(1)

d_{G^{\prime}}(x)=\delta(G^{\prime})=\delta(G)
and
\Delta(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G)
;

(2)

subject to (1), at most one neighbor of
x
in
G^{\prime}
is not a maximum degree vertex of
G^{\prime}
.

Since
G
is not overfull and
n
is odd, we have
2e(G)=\sum_{y\in V(G)}d_{G}(y)\leq\Delta(G)(n-1)
. Consequently,
\sum_{y\in V(G)}\df_{G}(y)\geq\Delta(G)
.
Let
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and assume that
d_{G}(x_{1})\leq\ldots\leq d_{G}(x_{n})
.
Note that
d_{G}(x_{n})=\Delta(G)
.
Assume
s\in[1,n-1]
is the smallest integer such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\df_{G}(x_{i})\geq\delta(G)
. For each
i\in[1,s-1]
, we add exactly
\df_{G}(x_{i})
edges between
x

and
x_{i}
, and we add
\delta(G)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s-1}\df_{G}(x_{i})
edges between
x

and
x_{s}
, and call the resulting multigraph
G^{\prime}
. It is clear that
G^{\prime}

satisfies both of the constraints (i) and (ii). Since
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)\leq\eta n
,
we know that

|N_{G^{\prime}}(x)|\geq\frac{\alpha}{\eta}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\geq 3.

We claim that
G^{\prime}
contains no
\Delta(G)
-overfull subgraph.
Let
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|
odd. We show that
G[X]

is not
\Delta(G)
-overfull.
Assume first that
3\leq|X|\leq n-2
.
If
|X|<\varepsilon n+1
or
|X|>n-\varepsilon n-1
, then
e_{G}(X,V(G)\setminus X)\geq 2(\delta(G)-\varepsilon)n>\Delta(G)
.
If
1+\varepsilon n\leq|X|\leq n-\varepsilon n-1
, by the lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regularity of
G
, we have

e_{G}(X,V(G)\setminus X)\geq e_{G}(X\setminus\{x\},V(G)\setminus(X\cup\{x)\})\geq(p-\varepsilon)(|X|-1)(n-|X|-1)>\Delta(G)
. Therefore
2e(X)<\Delta(G)|X|-\Delta(G)
and so
G[X]

is not
\Delta(G)
-overfull. Thus, the only possible
\Delta(G)
-overfull subgraph of
G^{\prime}

is obtained from
G^{\prime}
by deleting a single vertex
y\in V(G)
.
Note that if
G^{\prime}-y
is
\Delta(G)
-overfull for some
y\in V(G^{\prime})
, then so is

G^{\prime}-x
as

x
has minimum degree in
G^{\prime}
.
However,
G^{\prime}-x=G
, which is not overfull by our assumption.
Thus
G^{\prime}-y
is not
\Delta(G)
-overfull for any
y\in V(G^{\prime})
.

Now
V(G^{\prime})=\{x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
Since
x
has the smallest degree in
G^{\prime}
and
G=G^{\prime}-x
is not overfull,

\sum_{i\geq 1}\df_{G^{\prime}}(x_{i})\geq\df_{G^{\prime}}(x)
. Since
|V(G^{\prime})|=n+1

is even,
\df_{G^{\prime}}(x)+\sum_{i\geq 1}\df_{G^{\prime}}(x_{i})
is even.
Then by Theorem
4.1
, there exists a multigraph
H

on
V(G)
such that
d_{H}(x)=\df_{G^{\prime}}(x)

and
d_{H}(x_{i})=\df_{G^{\prime}}(x_{i})
for each
i\in[1,n]
. This multigraph
H

will aid us to find a spanning regular subgraph of
G^{\prime}
.

Note that
\Delta(H)=\df_{G^{\prime}}(x)=\Delta(G)-\delta(G)\leq\eta n
and
H
contains isolated vertices.
Thus
\chi^{\prime}(H)\leq\Delta(H)+\mu(H)\leq 2\Delta(H)\leq 2\eta n<7\eta n/\alpha
. Hence we
can greedily partition
E(H)
into
k\leq 7\eta n/\alpha
matchings
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}

each of size at most
\alpha n/7
. Now we take out linear forests
from
G^{\prime}
by applying Lemma
2.3
with
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
.
As at most one neighbor of
x
is not a maximum degree vertex of
G^{\prime}
, it follows that

x
is adjacent in
G^{\prime}
to at most one vertex of those vertices from
M_{i}
for each
i\in[1,k]
.
More precisely, define spanning subgraphs
G_{0},\ldots,G_{k}

of
G^{\prime}
and edge-disjoint linear forests
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}

such that

(1)

G_{0}:=G^{\prime}
and
G_{i}=G_{i-1}-E(F_{i})
for
i\in[1,k]
,

(2)

F_{i}
is a spanning linear forest (each vertex of
G_{i-1}
has degree 1 or 2 in
F_{i}
) in
G_{i-1}
whose leaves are precisely the
vertices in
M_{i}
.

Let
G_{0}=G^{\prime}
and suppose that for some
i\in[1,k]
, we already defined
G_{0},\ldots,G_{i-1}

and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}
. As
\Delta(F_{1}\cup\ldots\cup F_{i-1})\leq 2(i-1)\leq 14\eta n/\alpha
,
it follows that
\delta(G_{i-1})\geq(\alpha-14\eta n/\alpha)\geq 6\alpha n/7
. Moreover,
let
\varepsilon^{\prime}=2\sqrt{14\eta/\alpha}
. Since
G^{\prime}
is lower-
(p,2\varepsilon)
-regular by Proposition
2.1
(1) and
\varepsilon^{\prime}>4\varepsilon
,
G_{i-1}

is lower-
(p,\varepsilon^{\prime})
-regular by Proposition
2.1
(2).
Note that
e_{G_{i-1}}(x,v)\leq e_{G^{\prime}}(x,v)\leq\eta n
for every
v\in V(G^{\prime})
and
|N_{G_{i-1}}(x)|\geq\frac{6\alpha/7}{\eta}\gg 3
. Thus
x
has in
G_{i-1}
at least two neighbors outside the vertices of
M_{i}
. Hence, since
M_{i}
has size at most
\alpha n/7
, we can apply Lemma
2.3
to
G_{i-1}
and
M_{i}
and obtain a spanning linear forest
F_{i}
in
G_{i-1}
whose leaves are precisely the vertices in
M_{i}
.
Set
G_{i}:=G_{i-1}-E(F_{i})
.

We claim that
G_{k}
is regular. Consider any vertex
u\in V(G_{k})
.
For every
i\in[1,k]
,
d_{F_{i}}(u)=1
if
u
is an endvertex of some edge of
M_{i}

and
d_{F_{i}}(u)=2
otherwise.
Since
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
partition
E(H)
, we know that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{F_{i}}(u)=2k-d_{H}(u)=2k-\df_{G^{\prime}}(u)
.
Thus

d_{G_{k}}(u)=d_{G^{\prime}}(u)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{F_{i}}(u)=d_{G^{\prime}}(u)-(2k-\df_{G^{\prime}}(u))=\Delta(G^{\prime})-2k.

Let
d=\Delta(G^{\prime})-2k
and
\alpha^{\prime}=6\alpha/7
. We have shown that
G_{k}
is a
d
-regular, lower-
(p,\varepsilon^{\prime})
-regular star-multigraph
with
d\geq\alpha^{\prime}n
.
Furthermore,
e_{G_{k}}(x,v)\leq\eta n
for every
v\in V(G_{k})
,

|N_{G_{k}}(x)|\geq\frac{6\alpha/7}{\eta}\gg 3
, and
|N_{G_{k}}(x)|\leq n-1
as
e_{G^{\prime}}(x,x_{n})=0
.
By Theorem
3.3
,
\chi^{\prime}(G_{k})=d
. Now we color the edges of
F_{i}

using 2 distinct colors from
[d+1,d+2k]
for each
i\in[1,k]
.
It is clear that any edge
d
-coloring of
G_{k}
together with
this coloring of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
gives an edge coloring
of
G^{\prime}
using
d+2k=\Delta(G^{\prime})
colors. As
G
is a proper subgraph of
G^{\prime}
with
\Delta(G)=\Delta(G^{\prime})
, it follows
that
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
.

We lastly check that the procedure above yields a polynomial time algorithm. Given
G
, we first check
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\df_{G}(v)
.
If it is at most
\Delta(G)-2
, then we conclude that
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1
,
and
G
can be edge colored using
\Delta(G)+1
colors in polynomial
time
[
14
]
. If
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\df_{G}(v)>\Delta(G)-2
,
then we add a new vertex
x
to
G
and form
G^{\prime}
as described in
the beginning of this proof. Then we
can construct an edge
\Delta(G^{\prime})
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
through the process. Since Theorem
4.1
, Lemma
2.3
and Theorem
3.3
give appropriate
running time statements, this can be achieved in time polynomial in
n
.
∎

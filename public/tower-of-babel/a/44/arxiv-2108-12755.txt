Abstract
For a complete connected Riemannian manifold M M let V ∈ C 2 ​ ( M ) V\in C^{2}(M) be such that μ ⁡ ( d ​ x ) = e − V ⁡ ( x ) ​ vol ⁡ ( d ​ x ) \mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)}\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm vol}}{\text{\rm vol}}{\text{\rm vol}}{\text{\rm vol}}}\nolimits(\mathrm{d}x) is a probability measure on M M . Taking μ \mu as reference measure, we derive inequalities for probability measures on M M linking relative entropy, Fisher information, Stein discrepancy and Wasserstein distance. These inequalities strengthen in particular the famous log-Sobolev and transportation-cost inequality and extend the so-called Entropy/Stein-discrepancy/Information (HSI) inequality established by Ledoux, Nourdin and Peccati (2015) for the standard Gaussian measure on Euclidean space to the setting of Riemannian manifolds.

Introduction

Let
\gamma(\mathrm{d}x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x
be the
standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote by
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the
set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The classical log-Sobolev
inequality
[
7
]
indicates that

H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{2}I(\nu\,|\,\gamma),\ \ \nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.1)

and the transportation-cost inequality
[
16
]
states that

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\gamma)^{2}\leq 2H(\nu\,|\,\gamma),\quad\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.2)

where for
\nu,\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we consider

1.

the relative entropy of
\nu
with respect to
\mu
,

H(\nu\,|\,\mu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h\log h\,\text{\rm{d}}\mu,&\text{if}\ \nu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=h(x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x),\\
\infty,\ &\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(1.3)

2.

the Fisher information of
\nu
with respect to
\mu

I(\nu\,|\,\mu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\ &\text{if}\ \nu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=h(x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x),\sqrt{h}\in W^{1,2}(\mu),\\
\infty,\ &\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(1.4)

3.

the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{2}
of
\mu
and

\nu
, i.e.

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{2}\,\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\right)^{1/2}

(1.5)

with

\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
being the set of all couplings of
\mu
and
\nu
.

Inspired by
[
12
]
, Ledoux, Nourdin and Peccati
[
8
]

established some new type of inequalities improving (
1.1
) and
(
1.2
) by adopting the Stein discrepancy

S(\nu\,|\,\gamma)
of
\nu
with respect to
\gamma
as further ingredient.
This quantity is defined as

S(\nu\,|\,\gamma):=\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\tau-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/2}

(1.6)

where
{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}
is the
n\times n
-identity matrix and

\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
the set of measurable maps

\tau\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n};\nu)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\tau,{\operatorname{Hess}}_{\varphi}\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu,\quad\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

A map
\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
is called a Stein kernel of
\nu
. In general, the set

\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
may contain infinitely many maps; for instance, for the
Gaussian measure
\gamma
,

\left\{x\mapsto\big(1+r\text{\rm{e}}^{|x|^{2}/2}\big)\,{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}\colon r\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\mathbb{S}_{\gamma}.

Recall that however the Gaussian measure

\gamma
is characterized as the only probability distribution on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\gamma=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\gamma,\quad\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Hence for
\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
it holds that
{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
if and only if
\nu=\gamma
.

This equivalence indicates that the Stein discrepancy
S(\nu\,|\,\gamma)

with respect to the Gaussian distribution
\gamma
provides a natural
measure for the proximity of
\nu
to
\gamma
and allows to quantify how
far
\nu
is away from
\gamma
. It is a crucial quantity for normal
approximations and appears implicitly in many works on Stein’s method

[
15
]
. The Stein method was initially developed to quantify
the rate of convergence in the Central Limit Theorem

[
14
]
, and has recently been extended to probability
distributions on Riemannian manifolds
[
20
]
. For Gamma
approximations the Stein discrepancy represents the bound one
customarily obtains when applying Stein’s method to measure the distance
to the one-dimensional Gamma distribution, see

[
2
,
4
,
8
,
11
]
.

Recall that the relative entropy
H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)
is another measure of the
proximity between
\nu
and
\gamma
(note that
H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)\geq 0
and

H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)=0
if and only if
\nu=\gamma
) which is moreover stronger
than the total variation distance,
2
TV
(\nu,\gamma)^{2}\leq H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)
,
see
[
21
,
8
]
.

Considering the Stein discrepancy

S(\nu\,|\,\gamma)
as a new ingredient,
according to
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
, one has the
following HSI inequality which strengthens (
1.1
):

H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)\log\bigg(1+\frac{I(\nu\,|\,\gamma)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)}\bigg),\quad\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.7)

whereas the inequality
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
,

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)\leq S(\nu\,|\,\gamma)\arccos\left(\exp\left(-\frac{H(\nu\,|\,\gamma)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)}\right)\right),\quad\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.8)

improves the transportation-cost inequality (
1.2
).
Moreover,
[
8
, Theorem 2.8]
gives the existence of a constant
C>0

such that

\left(\int|f|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(S_{p}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)+\sqrt{p}\,\Big(\int|\tau|_{\rm op}^{p/2}\mathrm{d}\nu\Big)^{1/p}\right),\quad\nu(f)=0,\ |\nabla f|\leq 1,\ \tau\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu},

(1.9)

where for
p\geq 1
, one defines

S_{p}(\nu\,|\,\gamma):=\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\tau-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/p}.

(1.10)

In particular
S_{2}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)
is the Stein discrepancy as defined above.

In
[
8
]
these inequalities have been extended to probability measures
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\text{\rm{e}}^{V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are stationary distributions of an elliptic symmetric diffusion process on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The required
assumptions are formulated in terms of conditions on the iterated Bakry-Émery operators
\Gamma_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
. It is worth mentioning that the analysis towards the HSI bound in this context
makes crucial use of the
iterated gradient
\Gamma_{3}
which is rather uncommon in the study of functional inequalities.

The aim of this paper is to put forward this framework and to investigate inequalities
of the type (
1.7
), (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) on general Riemannian manifolds.
It should be stressed that in our approach explicit Hessian estimates of the heat semigroup take over the
role of bounds on
\Gamma_{3}
.
Our results on Riemannian manifolds include the above inequalities as special cases.

We start with some basic notations.
Let
M
be a complete connected Riemannian manifold equipped with a probability measure

generated by

is symmetric on

. We denote by

the
Bakry-Émery curvature tensor.

Let

,
I(\nu\,|\,\mu)
,
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)
and
S(\nu\,|\,\mu)

for
\nu\in\mathscr{P}(M)
be defined as in (

), (
1.4
),
(
1.5
) and (
1.6
) respectively, with
(M,\mu)
replacing

, the Riemannian distance

replacing

,
and
\mathbb{S}_{\nu}
being the class of measurable 2-tensors

which are
locally integrable with respect to

such that

Assume

is non-empty, that is a Stein kernel for

In the Euclidean case

, this is ensured by the existence of a spectral gap (see

Our results on Riemannian manifolds are presented in the Sections 3, 4

In this case, for instance, we obtain inequalities of the same form as

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{K^{1/2}}\arccos\left(\exp\left(-\frac{H(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)\right),\ \ \nu\in\mathscr{P}(M),

and there exists a constant

such

\left(\int|f|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(S_{p}(\nu\,|\,\gamma)+\sqrt{p}\Big(\int|\tau|_{\rm op}^{p/2}\mathrm{d}\nu\Big)^{1/p}\right),\quad\nu(f)=0,\ |\nabla f|\leq 1,\ \tau\in\mathbb{S}_{\nu}.

2
Hessian estimate of
P_{t}

Let
(M,g)
be a
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold. We
write
\langle u,v\rangle=g(u,v)
and

|u|=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle}
for
u,v\in T_{x}M
and
x\in M
. Let

R
,
{\operatorname{Ric}}
be the Riemann curvature tensor and Ricci curvature tensor
respectively. Recall that

R\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M\otimes TM)
where

R(X,Y,Z)\equiv R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,\quad X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM),

and
{\operatorname{Ric}}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M)
given
as
{\operatorname{Ric}}(Y,Z)={\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\big(X\mapsto R(X,Y)Z\big)
.

1.

For
f,h\in C^{2}(M)
and
x\in M
, we consider the
Hilbert-Schmidt inner product of the Hessian tensors
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}
and

{\operatorname{Hess}}_{h}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f},{\operatorname{Hess}}_{h}\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(X_{i},X_{j}){\operatorname{Hess}}_{h}(X_{i},X_{j}),

where
(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
denotes an orthonormal base of

T_{x}M
. Then the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}
is given by

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}(x)=\sqrt{\langle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f},{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}.

2.

For a symmetric 2-tensor
T
and a constant
K
, we write

T\geq K
if

\displaystyle T(w,w)\geq K|w|^{2},\quad w\in T_{x}M,\ x\in M,

and
T\leq K
if

\displaystyle T(w,w)\leq K|w|^{2},\quad w\in T_{x}M,\ x\in M.

3.

Given a symmetric 2-tensor
T
, we let

T^{\sharp}\colon TM\rightarrow TM
be defined by

\displaystyle\langle T^{\sharp}(v),w\rangle=T(v,w),\quad v,w\in T_{x}M,\,x\in M.

Then
T^{\sharp}
is a symmetric endomorphism, i.e.,

\langle T^{\sharp}(w),v\rangle=\langle T^{\sharp}(v),w\rangle
for

v,w\in T_{x}M
,
x\in M
. Let

\displaystyle|T|(x)=\sup\left\{|T^{\sharp}(w)|\colon w\in T_{x}M,\ |w|\leq 1\right\},\quad x\in M.

Then, in particular,
|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|(x)
gives the operator norm of the
Hessian of a function
f
at
x
.

4.

Furthermore, denoting by Bil
(TM)
the vector bundle of bilinear
forms on
TM
, we consider

\tilde{R}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M\otimes\text{Bil}(TM))
given
by

\tilde{R}(v_{1},v_{2})=\langle R(\boldsymbol{\cdot},v_{1})v_{2},\boldsymbol{\cdot}\rangle,\quad v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}M,

and let

\displaystyle|\tilde{R}|(x)=\left||\tilde{R}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},\boldsymbol{\cdot})|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\right|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}(x)\ \mbox{for}\ x\in M\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in M}|\tilde{R}|(x).

Note that in explicit terms

\displaystyle\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in M}\left(\sum_{k,\ell}\sum_{i,j}\langle R(e_{i},v_{k})v_{\ell},e_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)^{1/2}

where
(v_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n}
and
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
denote
orthonormal bases for
T_{x}M
.

5.

For a general symmetric 2-tensor
T
, we adopt the notation

\displaystyle(RT)(v_{1},v_{2}):={\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\,\langle R(\boldsymbol{\cdot},v_{1})v_{2},T^{\sharp}(\boldsymbol{\cdot})\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle R(e_{i},v_{1})v_{2},T^{\sharp}(e_{i})\rangle,

where
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}M,\ x\in M
and
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
is an
orthonormal base of
T_{x}M
. Let

\displaystyle|R|(x)=\sup\Big\{|(RT)(v_{1},v_{2})|\colon|v_{1}|\leq 1,\ |v_{2}|\leq 1,\ |T|\leq 1\Big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|R\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in M}|R|(x).

It is easy to see that
|\tilde{R}|(x)\leq n|R|(x)
. In particular, if
\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
then
\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}<\infty
as well.

6.

In addition, let

\mathrm{d}^{*}R=-{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\,\nabla{\boldsymbol{.}}\,R,

i.e.,

(\mathrm{d}^{*}R)(v_{1},v_{2})=-{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\,\nabla{\boldsymbol{.}}\,R(\boldsymbol{\cdot},v_{1})v_{2},\quad v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}M.

Note that

\displaystyle\langle(\mathrm{d}^{*}R)(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3}\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{v_{3}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{v_{2}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{3}),v_{1}\rangle,\quad v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in T_{x}M.

7.

Finally, for
v,w\in T_{x}M
, let

\displaystyle R(\nabla V)(v,w):=R(\nabla V,v)w.

2.1
Hessian estimates of semigroup: type I

Let us introduce a first type of Hessian estimate of the heat semigroup. When
M
is Ricci parallel and the generator of the
diffusion equals half the Laplacian
\Delta
, such a type of formula
bounding the norm of the Hessian of
P_{t}f
from
above by
P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2}
, has been already given in
[
24
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Hessian estimate: type I)
.

Assume that
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K
,
\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
and

\beta:=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Let
\alpha_{1}:=\|R\|_{\infty}
and
\alpha_{2}:=\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}
. Then for
f\in C_{b}^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{K-2\alpha_{1}}{\text{\rm{e}}^{(2K-2\alpha_{1})t}-\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}}\right)^{1/2}\left((P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2})^{1/2}+\left(\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}-1}{K}\right)^{1/2}\frac{\beta}{K}(P_{t}|\nabla f|)\right).

Moreover, if
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K
, then

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{K-2\alpha_{2}}{\text{\rm{e}}^{(2K-2\alpha_{2})t}-\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}}\right)^{1/2}\left((P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2})^{1/2}+\left(\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}-1}{K}\right)^{1/2}\frac{n\beta}{K}(P_{t}|\nabla f|)\right).

(2.1)

To prove Theorem
2.1
, we first introduce a
probabilistic representation formula for
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}
. For the
semigroup
P_{t}
generated by
\Delta/2
, a Bismut type Hessian formula
has been established in
[
1
]
, which was then extended to general
Schrödinger operators on
M

[
9
,
19
]
.

Denote by
{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}_{V}={\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}+{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}^{\sharp}
the
Bakry-Émery tensor (written as endomorphism of
TM
). The damped
parallel transport
Q_{t}\colon T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}M
is defined as
the solution, along the paths of
X_{t}
, to the covariant ordinary
differential equation

\displaystyle DQ_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}_{V}Q_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad Q_{0}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},

where the covariant differential is given by
/\!/_{t}^{-1}\,D=\mathrm{d}\,/\!/_{t}^{-1}
.

For
w\in T_{x}M
, we define an operator-valued
process
W_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},w):T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}M
by

\displaystyle W_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},w):=

\displaystyle Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},Q_{r}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}))Q_{r}(w)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}\big(\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\big)\big(Q_{r}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}),Q_{r}(w)\big)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Note that
W_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},w)
is the solution to the covariant Itô
equation

\displaystyle DW_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},w)

\displaystyle=R(/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},Q_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}))Q_{t}(w)-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}(W_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+R(\nabla V))(Q_{t}(\boldsymbol{\cdot}),Q_{t}(w))\,\mathrm{d}t,

with initial condition
W_{0}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},w)=0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\rho
be the Riemannian distance to a fixed point
o\in M
.
Assume that

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\Big(\big|\mathrm{d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+R(\nabla V)\big|+|R|\Big)}{\rho^{2}}=0,

and

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq-h(\rho)\quad\mbox{for some positive function }h\in C([0,\infty))\mbox{ such that }\ \lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h(r)}{r^{2}}=0.

Then

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}(v,w)=\mathbb{E}\left[{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))+\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),W_{t}(v,w)\rangle\right].

Proof.

For fixed
T>0
, set

\displaystyle N_{t}(v,w):={\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))+\langle\nabla P_{T-t}f(X_{t}),W_{t}(v,w)\rangle.

We first recall that
N_{t}(v,w)
is a local martingale, which has been shown e.g. in
[
20
, Lemma 11.3]
. We include a proof here for the convenience of the reader.
We first observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(\Delta-\nabla V)f

\displaystyle=\left({\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\nabla^{2}-\nabla_{\nabla V}\right)\mathrm{d}f-\mathrm{d}f({\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}),

\displaystyle\nabla\mathrm{d}(\Delta f)

\displaystyle={\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\nabla^{2}(\nabla\mathrm{d}f)-(\nabla\mathrm{d}f)({\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}\odot{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}+{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}\odot{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}-2R^{\sharp,\sharp})-\mathrm{d}f(\mathrm{d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}),

\displaystyle\nabla\mathrm{d}(\nabla V(f))

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\nabla V}(\nabla\mathrm{d}f)+(\nabla\mathrm{d}f)({\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}^{\sharp}\odot{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}+{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}\odot{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}^{\sharp})+\mathrm{d}f(\nabla{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}^{\sharp}+R(\nabla V)),

where
\odot
denotes the symmetric tensor product.
Thus, for the Itô differential of
N_{t}(v,w)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}N_{t}(v,w)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}}{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f})(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))+{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}\left(\frac{D}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}\left(Q_{t}(v),\frac{D}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{t}(w)\right)\mathrm{d}t+\partial_{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f})(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt(\nabla^{2}-\nabla_{\nabla V})({\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f})(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\,\mathrm{d}t+(\nabla_{/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}}\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}(v,w))

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)(DW_{t}(v,w))+\langle D(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f),DW_{t}(v,w)\rangle+\partial_{t}(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}(v,w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt(\nabla^{2}-\nabla_{\nabla V})(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}(v,w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\scriptsize\rm m}}{=}-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}\left({\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}(v)),Q_{t}(w)\right)\mathrm{d}t-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}\left(Q_{t}(v),{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}(w))\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{d}(\Delta-\nabla V){P_{T-t}f})(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\left({\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\nabla^{2}-\nabla_{\nabla V}\right)({\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f})(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)(\mathrm{d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+R(\nabla V))(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)({\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}(W_{t}(v,w)))\,\mathrm{d}t+{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\left\{{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},R(\boldsymbol{\cdot},Q_{t}(v))Q_{t}(w))\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}(\Delta-\nabla V)P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}(v,w))\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\left({\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\nabla^{2}-\nabla_{\nabla V}\right)(\mathrm{d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}(v,w))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=0,

where
\overset{\text{\scriptsize\rm m}}{=}
denotes equality modulo differentials of local martingales,
so that
N_{t}
is a local martingale. Assume that

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\left(|\mathrm{d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+R(\nabla V)|+|R|\right)}{\rho^{2}}=0,

and

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq-h(\rho)\quad\mbox{for some positive }\ h\in C([0,\infty))\ \mbox{with}\ \ \lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h(r)}{r^{2}}=0.

Then by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|Q_{s}|^{2}\right]<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|W_{s}|^{2}\right]<\infty.

In addition,

Under our curvature assumptions these local bounds then provide global bounds

for every small

Thus the local martingale

is a true martingale on the time interval

\mathbb{E}[N_{0}]=\mathbb{E}[N_{T-\varepsilon}]

and then

{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T}f}(v,w)=\mathbb{E}\left[{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(Q_{T}(v),Q_{T}(w))+\langle\nabla f(X_{T}),W_{T}(v,w)\rangle\right]

by passing to the limit as

.
Note that since the manifold is complete,
we have by the spectral theorem

where

is the
canonical heat semigroup on

∎

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}\big(\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\big)\big(Q_{r}(v),Q_{r}(w)\big)\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\rangle.

To deal with the first term on the right hand side, we observe that

Lemma 2.3
.

Keeping the assumptions of Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},Q_{r}(v))Q_{r}(w)\Big\rangle\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}(R{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f})(Q_{s}(v),Q_{s}(w)\,\mathrm{d}s\right].

Let

It is easy to
check that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(H_{s}(v,w))

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\nabla_{/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}}(\nabla P_{t-s}f)(X_{s}),Q_{s}\int_{0}^{s}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},Q_{r}(v))Q_{r}(w)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\langle{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}(\nabla P_{t-s}f)(X_{s}),Q_{s}\int_{0}^{s}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},Q_{r}(v))Q_{r}(w)\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\Big\langle(\nabla P_{t-s}f)(X_{s}),{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}\Big(Q_{s}\int_{0}^{s}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},Q_{r}(v))Q_{r}(w)\Big)\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

Proof of Theorem

.

We begin with the following observation obtained by combining the

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),W_{t}(v,w)\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\mathrm{d}B_{r},Q_{r}(v))Q_{r}(w)\Big\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}(\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V))(Q_{r}(v),Q_{r}(w))\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\left({\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},R(\boldsymbol{\cdot},Q_{s}(v))Q_{s}(w))\right)\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big\langle\nabla f(X_{t}),Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}(\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V))(Q_{r}(v),Q_{r}(w))\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\rangle\right].

Noting that
|Q_{t}Q_{r}^{-1}|\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-K(t-r)/2}
,

|Q_{r}|\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kr/2}
, and

\displaystyle{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\left({\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f}(\boldsymbol{\cdot},R(\boldsymbol{\cdot},Q_{s}(v))Q_{s}(w))\right)

where
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
is an orthonormal base of

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|

\displaystyle\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,P_{t}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|+\|R\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ks}P_{s}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f}|\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-K(t+r)/2}\mathrm{d}r\right)P_{t}|\nabla f|

\displaystyle=\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,P_{t}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|+\frac{\beta(\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt/2}-\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt})}{K}P_{t}|\nabla f|+\|R\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ks}P_{s}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f}|\,\mathrm{d}s,\ \ t\geq 0.

Now let

\phi(r):=\text{\rm{e}}^{-K(t-r)}P_{t-r}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{r}f}|,\ \ r\in[0,t].

Applying the above estimate for
P_{r}f
instead of
P_{t}f

\displaystyle\phi(r)

By Gronwall’s lemma, this implies

\begin{split}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|&=\phi(t)\leq\bigg\{\phi(0)+\frac{\beta(\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt/2}-\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt})}{K}P_{t}|\nabla f|\bigg\}\,\text{\rm{e}}^{\|R\|_{\infty}t}\\
&=\text{\rm{e}}^{(\|R\|_{\infty}-K)t}P_{t}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|+\frac{\beta\,\text{\rm{e}}^{\|R\|_{\infty}t}\big(\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt/2}-\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\big)}{K}P_{t}|\nabla f|.\end{split}

(2.2)

On the other hand, by Itô’s formula we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}|\nabla P_{t-s}f|^{2}(X_{s})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(L|\nabla P_{t-s}f|^{2}(X_{s})-\langle\nabla P_{t-s}f,\nabla LP_{t-s}f\rangle(X_{s})\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\left({\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}(\nabla P_{t-s}f,\nabla P_{t-s}f)+|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f}|^{2}_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\right)(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\langle\nabla|\nabla P_{t-s}f|^{2}(X_{s}),/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2}-\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}|\nabla P_{t}f|^{2}\geq\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{K(t-s)}P_{s}|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t-s}f}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Combining this
with (
2.2
) for
(P_{s},P_{t-s}f)
instead of

, and

Important examples in the sequel will be Ricci parallel manifolds

\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}=0
for the

. Recall that an Einstein manifold is
Ricci parallel but in general the inverse is not true.

]
used functional inequalities for the

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{Y,Z}(X)=-R(X,Y)Z.

2.2
Hessian estimate of semigroup: type II

We now introduce a slightly different type of Hessian estimate for the
semigroup.

Theorem 2.5

(Hessian estimate: type
II)
.

Assume that
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K>0
,
\alpha_{1}:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
(or

\alpha_{2}:=\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}<\infty
) and

\beta:=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Then for
f\in C_{b}^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt/2}}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt/2}}{\sqrt{K}}\right)(P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2})^{1/2}+\frac{\beta\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt/2}}{K}(P_{t}|\nabla f|).

Moreover, if
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K
, then for
f\in C_{b}^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|_{\rm HS}\leq

3
The HSI inequality

We first recall the formula relating relative entropy and Fisher information.
From now on, we always assume that
\nu
is a distribution
which is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
such that

h:={\mathrm{d}\nu}/{\mathrm{d}\mu}\in C_{b}^{2}(M)

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}:={\operatorname{Ric}}-{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}\geq K

for some positive constant
K
. Recall that

\mathrm{d}\nu^{t}=P_{t}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
for
t>0
. Then

(i)

(Integrated de Bruijn’s formula)

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)={\rm Ent}_{\mu}(h)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\,\mathrm{d}t;

(ii)

(Exponential decay of Fisher information) for every

,

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)=I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}I(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}I_{\mu}(h).

The HSI inequality connects the entropy
H
, the Stein
discrepancy
S
and the Fisher information
I
. We first give a bound
for the Fisher information by Stein’s discrepancy
S
.
More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\nu
be a distribution satisfying
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
.
Assume that
\alpha_{1}:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
(or

. Let
\nu
be a distribution satisfying

\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}\|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}^{2}\leq\Psi(t)P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2},

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t+S(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}\int_{u}^{\infty}\Psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
(i), we have

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Combining this with the following facts:

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}I(\nu\,|\,\mu),

and

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq\Psi(t)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu),

we obtain

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\Bigg\{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t+S(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}\int_{u}^{\infty}\Psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg\}.

Remark 3.7
.

Suppose that
\beta=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty

\beta=0

combined with Corollary

(i), we get the HSI inequality

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\Bigg\{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t+nS(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}\int_{u}^{\infty}\left(\frac{K-2\|R\|_{\infty}}{\text{\rm{e}}^{(2K-2\|R\|_{\infty})t}-\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg\}.

The term

\frac{K-2\|R\|_{\infty}}{\text{\rm{e}}^{(2K-2\|R\|_{\infty})t}-\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}}

\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}

If

\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}

2.7
to establish the HSI inequality.

3.2
Case II :
\beta\neq 0
.

We start by introducing the main theorem of this subsection which also
provides a general way to the HSI inequality.

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume that
\alpha_{1}:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty,\ \beta:=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
Let
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
with
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
.

(i)

If
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{n\left(1+\varepsilon\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2\varepsilon}\left[c_{0}+\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{n\left(1+\varepsilon\right)KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}-c_{0}\right)\right],

for any
\varepsilon>0
, where

c_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon(\alpha_{1}\sqrt{K}+\beta)^{2}}{K^{3}}-1.

Moreover, if
\alpha_{1}=0
and
\beta=0
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{n}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\ln\left(1+\frac{I}{nKS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

(ii)

If
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2\varepsilon}\left[\tilde{c}_{0}+\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}-\tilde{c}_{0}\right)\right],

for any
\varepsilon>0
, where

\tilde{c}_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon(\alpha_{2}\sqrt{K}+n\beta)^{2}}{K^{3}}-1.

Moreover, if
\alpha_{2}=\beta=0
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\ln\left(1+\frac{I}{KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

Proof.

We only need to prove the first estimate. Denote again
I=I(\nu\,|\,\mu)
and
S=S(\nu\,|\,\mu)
for simplicity.
By Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)

\displaystyle\leq n\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq n\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}}{\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}+n(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Using this inequality, we need to estimate

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{A\int_{0}^{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t+B\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{K}{\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}(\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}-1)}\,\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{u}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{\frac{A(1-\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ku})+C\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ku}}{K}+B\int_{0}^{\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ku}}\frac{r}{1-r}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\},

where

\displaystyle A=I(\nu\,|\,\mu);\ \ B=n\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu);

\displaystyle C=n(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

It is easy to see that if

, then
\inf
is reached when
u
tends to
\infty
; if

A>C
however, then
\inf
is reached for
\text{\rm{e}}^{Ku}=\frac{A-C+BK}{A-C}
so that

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{C}{2K}+\frac{B}{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{A-C}{BK}\right).

We then conclude that

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{B}{2}\left[c_{0}+\Phi\left(\frac{A}{BK}-c_{0}\right)\right],

where

c_{0}=\frac{C-BK}{BK}=\frac{\varepsilon(\alpha_{1}\sqrt{K}+\beta)^{2}}{K^{3}}-1.

The proof of (ii) is the same by taking
B
as

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu),

and

as

(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{n\beta}{K}\right)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

Example 3.14
.

Let
M=\mathbb{R}
. We consider a family of diffusion operator on the line of the type

\displaystyle Lf=f^{\prime\prime}-u^{\prime}f^{\prime}

\mathrm{d}\mu=\text{\rm{e}}^{-u}\,\mathrm{d}x

We have

such that

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{2}2^{2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}K}-1+\Theta\left(\frac{\varepsilon I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\left(1+\varepsilon\right)KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta^{2}2^{2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}K}+1\right)\right].

\varepsilon=\delta^{2}2^{2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}K

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{\left(1+\delta^{2}2^{2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}K\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\delta^{2}2^{1+2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}K}\,\Theta\left(\frac{\delta^{2}2^{2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\left(1+\delta^{2}2^{2(p-1)/p}(pK)^{2/p}K\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

. Then

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}-(\delta u^{\prime\prime\prime})^{2}\geq 1

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+6a)\,S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\,\Theta\left(\frac{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\left(6a+1\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

3.10
, we have

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{\left(2\sqrt{2n(n-1)}-(n-1)\right)}{2(n-1)}\,S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\,\Theta\left(\frac{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\big(2\sqrt{2n(n-1)}-(n-1)\big)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

On the other hand, to put these results in perspective with the
method of
[
8
]
, let us first recall the necessary notions:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(f,\,g):=\langle\nabla f,\,\nabla g\rangle,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f,\,g):={\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}(\nabla f,\,\nabla g)+\langle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f},\,{\operatorname{Hess}}_{g}\rangle_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}(f,\,g):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(L\Gamma_{2}(f,\,g)-\Gamma_{2}(Lf,\,g)-\Gamma_{2}(f,\,Lg)\Big).

Adopting the approach of
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
we have the
following result.

Theorem 3.16
.

If there exist positive constants
\kappa,\rho
and
\sigma

such that

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq\rho\Gamma_{1}(f),\quad\Gamma_{3}(f)\geq\kappa\Gamma_{2}(f),\quad\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq\sigma|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|^{2}_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}},

then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2\sigma}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\,\Theta\left(\frac{\sigma\max\{\rho,\kappa\}I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\rho\kappa S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

For the general Riemmanian case, a crucial difficulty in applying Theorem
3.16
is to check the existence of a constant

\kappa>0
such that
\Gamma_{3}(f)\geq\kappa\Gamma_{2}(f)
. In the
special case
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)

\displaystyle=(n-1)|\nabla f|^{2}+|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}^{2}\geq(n-1)|\nabla f|^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}(f)

\displaystyle=(n-1)\left((n-1)|\nabla f|^{2}+|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|^{2}+2(n-1)|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}^{2}-2\langle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}(R^{\sharp,\sharp}),{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{(3(n-1)-2\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}),(n-1)\right\}\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq\left(3(n-1)-2\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}\right)\Gamma_{2}(f),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)

\displaystyle\geq|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{f}|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}^{2}.

Thus
\rho=(n-1),\sigma=1,
and

\kappa=\min\left\{(3(n-1)-2\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}),(n-1)\right\}
. If

\kappa=3(n-1)-2\sqrt{2n(n-1)}>0
, i.e.
n\geq 9
, by Theorem

3.16
, we have

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\,\Theta\left(\frac{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\left(3(n-1)-2\sqrt{2n(n-1)}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

We first observe that this inequality holds for all
n\geq 0
and when

I(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\left(3(n-1)-2\sqrt{2n(n-1)}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu);

the inequality can not become the classical log-Sobolev inequality.
In any case, our HSI inequality improves the classical log-Sobolev
inequality. In particular, for general Riemannian case, if
|R|
is
small such that
K-2\|R\|_{\infty}>0
, the HSI inequality improves the
classical HI inequality no matter whether
S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)
is
small or not.

Example 3.17
.

Let
G
be a
n
-dimensional Lie group with a bi-invariant metric

g
, and let
\mathfrak{g}
denote its Lie algebra.
Consider
L=\Delta-\nabla V

for
V\in C^{2}(M)
such that
\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)}\mathrm{d}x
. Then for

X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Y=\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]\quad\mbox{and}\ \quad R(X,Y)Z=\frac{1}{4}[Z,[X,Y]].

By the Jacobi identity, we have

\displaystyle\big(\nabla{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}+R(\nabla V)\big)(X,Y)

\displaystyle\quad=\nabla_{X}(\nabla_{Y}\nabla V)-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}^{Y}}\nabla V+R(\nabla V,X)Y

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{4}[X,[Y,\nabla V]]+\frac{1}{4}[\nabla V,[X,Y]]+\frac{1}{4}[Y,[\nabla V,X]]=0.

We conclude that if
G
is a Ricci parallel Lie group with

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K>0
and
\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
, then the inequalities
in Theorem
3.8
(i) and Theorem
3.10
(i) hold. When the
condition
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K>0
is satisfied, both of the inequalities in Theorems

3.8
and
3.10
(i) and (ii) hold true.

4
The WS inequality and HWSI inequality

Denote by
\mathscr{P}(M)
the set of probability measures on
M
. For

\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{P}(M)
the
L^{2}
-Wasserstein distance is given by

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\left(\int_{M\times M}\rho(x,y)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/2}

where
\rho
denotes the Riemannian distance on
M
and

\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
consists of all couplings of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.
The Wasserstein distance has various characterizations and plays an
important role in the study of SDEs, partial differential equations,
optimal transportation problems, etc. For more background, one may
consult
[
16
,
22
,
23
]
and the references therein.
The following Theorem describes the relationship between Wasserstein distance
and Stein discrepancy.

Theorem 4.1

(WS inequality)
.

Assume that
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K>0
,
\alpha_{1}:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
( or
\alpha_{2}:=\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}<\infty
) and

\beta:=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Then for
\nu\in\mathscr{P}(M)
satisfying

{\mathrm{d}\nu}/{\mathrm{d}\mu}\in C_{b}^{2}(M)
, we have

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\Psi(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)S(\nu\,|\,\mu),

where
\Psi
is defined by the term in (
3.5
) (and also as in (
3.2
) when
{\operatorname{Ric}}=K>0
).

Proof.

Recall that
h={\mathrm{d}\nu}/{\mathrm{d}\mu}\in C_{b}^{2}(M)
and let

\mathrm{d}\nu^{t}=P_{t}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
. By the
formula in
[
13
, Lemma 2]
or
[
21
, Theorem 24.2(iv)]
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{+}}{\mathrm{d}t}}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\nu^{t})\leq\left(\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla P_{t}h|^{2}}{P_{t}h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/2}=I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)^{1/2},

(4.1)

where
{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{+}}{\mathrm{d}t}}
stands for the upper right derivative.
On the other hand, by Theorem
3.2
,

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq\Psi(t)S(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}.

Combining this with (
4.1
), we obtain

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}(I_{\mu}(P_{t}h))^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq S(\nu\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\Psi(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t.\qed

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
\beta=0
and
\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Let

\nu\in\mathscr{P}(M)
satisfying

{\mathrm{d}\nu}/{\mathrm{d}\mu}\in C_{b}^{2}(M)
.

(i)

If
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K>0,
then

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n(2\|R\|_{\infty}-K)}{\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}-\text{\rm{e}}^{2(K-\|R\|_{\infty})t}}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\,S(\nu\,|\,\mu);

(ii)

if
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K>0,
then

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{2\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty}-K}{\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}-\text{\rm{e}}^{2(K-\|\tilde{R}\|_{\infty})t}}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\,S(\nu\,|\,\mu).

One may compare this inequality with the classical
Talagrand-type transportation cost inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2K}H(\nu\,|\,\mu).

(4.2)

We can go
further and improve this inequality to the following HWSI
inequality by assuming
\beta=0
.

Theorem 4.3

(HWSI inequality)
.

Assume that

\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\beta=0
. If
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K>0
and
\alpha:=K-2\|R\|_{\infty}>0
. Let

\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\,\mu)\leq\frac{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2K}\int_{0}^{L^{-1}\left(\frac{2KH(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\left(1-\left(\frac{y}{y+\alpha n}\right)^{K/\alpha}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

where

\displaystyle L(x)=x+Kn\int_{0}^{x}\frac{r^{K/\alpha-1}(r-x)}{(r+\alpha n)^{K/\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Remark 4.4
.

Since
L(r)\leq r
for
r\geq 0
,
this inequality improves the Talagrand quadratic
transportation cost inequality (
4.2
).

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Recall that
\mathrm{d}\nu^{t}=P_{t}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
. Then

\displaystyle H(\nu^{t}\,|\ \mu)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu}(P_{s+t}h)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Together with Proposition
3.1
this implies

\displaystyle H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{\{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ks}\,\mathrm{d}s+S(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}\int_{u+t}^{\infty}\Psi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{\{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{-Ks}\,\mathrm{d}s+S(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}\int_{u}^{\infty}\Psi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\}

If
\beta=0
,
\alpha=K-2\|R\|_{\infty}\geq 0
and
\Psi(s)=\frac{\alpha n}{\text{\rm{e}}^{Ks}(\text{\rm{e}}^{\alpha s}-1)}
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{2K}\left(1-\left(\frac{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)+\alpha nS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)^{K/\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{nS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2}\int_{0}^{\textstyle\frac{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)+\alpha nS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}}\frac{r^{K/\alpha}}{1-r}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2K}L\left(\frac{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right),

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle L(x)=x+Kn\int_{0}^{x}\frac{r^{K/\alpha-1}(r-x)}{(r+\alpha n)^{K/\alpha+1}}\,\mathrm{d}r.

It is easy to see that

L^{\prime}(x)=1-\left(\frac{x}{x+\alpha n}\right)^{K/\alpha}>0

for
x>0
. Thus
L^{-1}
exists and

\displaystyle I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)\geq S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)L^{-1}\left(\frac{2KH(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

Dividing
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu^{t})
by
t
and using the above estimate, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{+}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu^{t})

\displaystyle\leq I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)^{1/2}=\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{\sqrt{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)\sqrt{L^{-1}\left(\frac{2KH(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)}}.

Therefore, integrating both sides from
0
to
\infty
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)\sqrt{L^{-1}\left(\frac{2KH(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)}}

\displaystyle=S(\nu\,|\,\mu)\int_{0}^{\frac{H(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\sqrt{L^{-1}(2Kx)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{2K}\int_{0}^{L^{-1}\left(\frac{2KH(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}\left(1-(\frac{y}{y+\alpha n})^{K/\alpha}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y.

∎

In particular, if
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K
for some positive constant
K
,
then by Obata’s Rigidity Theorem (see
[
25
, Theorems 3.4 and 6.3]
),
M
is
isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we have

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume that
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K>0
. Let
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\mathrm{d}\mu
. Then

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\,\mu)\leq\frac{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{K^{1/2}}\arccos\left(\exp\left(-\frac{H(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)\right).

Proof.

As
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K
, we know that
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
First, we repeat the steps of the proof of Theorem
4.3
letting

\Psi(t)=\frac{K}{\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}(\text{\rm{e}}^{Kt}-1)}
.
By this and (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\nu^{t})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}\leq-\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{\sqrt{K}S(\nu\,|\,\mu)\sqrt{\exp\left(\frac{2H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)-1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\{\frac{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{K^{1/2}}\arccos\left(\exp\left(-\frac{H(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)\right)\right\}.

Consequently,

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\nu,\,\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu^{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{S(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{K^{1/2}}\arccos\left(\exp\left(-\frac{H(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)\right).\qed

5
Moment bounds and Stein discrepancy

In
[
8
]
, the authors investigate another feature of Stein’s
discrepancy applied to concentration inequalities on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is
well known that the classical log-Sobolev inequalities on the
manifolds is a powerful tool towards the invariant measure. In this
section, we continue to relate the Stein discrepancy to the concentration
inequality on a Riemannian manifold. Let

\displaystyle S_{p}(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\inf\left(\int|\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/p}.

As explained in
[
8
]
, the growth of the Stein
discrepancy
S_{p}(\nu\,|\,\mu)
in
p
entails concentration properties
of the measure
\nu
in terms of the growth of its moments. The
following result shows how to directly transfer information on the
Stein kernel to concentration properties on the manifold.

Theorem 5.1

(Moment bounds)
.

Assume that
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K>0
, and

|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}|_{{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}}^{2}\leq\Psi(t)P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2}

where
\Psi
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi^{1/2}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r<\infty.

There exists a numerical constant
C>0
such that for every

1
-Lipshitz function
f\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\int f\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0
,
and every
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\left(\int|f|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(S_{p}(\nu\,|\,\mu)+\sqrt{p}\Big(\int|\tau_{\nu}|_{\rm op}^{p/2}\mathrm{d}\nu\Big)^{1/p}\right),

where the constant
C
depends on the constants
K
,
p
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi^{1/2}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Proof.

We only prove the result for
p
an even integer, the general case
follows similarly with some further technicalities. We may also
replace the assumption
\int_{M}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu=0
by

\int_{M}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0
via a simple use of the triangle inequality.
Let
f\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be 1-Lipshitz, and assume
f
to be smooth and
bounded. Let
q\geq 1
be an integer and set

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\int_{M}(P_{t}f)^{2q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu,\quad t\geq 0.

Since
\mu(f)=0
, it follows that
\phi(\infty)=0
. Now using the
calculation with respect to the semigroup
P_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2q\int_{M}(P_{t}f)^{2q-1}LP_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle=2q\int(P_{t}f)^{2q-1}\Delta P_{t}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int\langle\tau_{\nu},{\operatorname{Hess}}((P_{t}f)^{2q})\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle=2q\int(P_{t}f)^{2q-1}\langle{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}-\tau_{\nu},{\operatorname{Hess}}(P_{t}f)\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\quad-2q(2q-1)\int_{M}(P_{t}f)^{2q-2}\langle\tau_{\nu},\nabla P_{t}f\otimes\nabla P_{t}f\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

(5.1)

Next, Theorem
2.1
implies

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}},{\operatorname{Hess}}(P_{t}f)\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}

\displaystyle\leq|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,|{\operatorname{Hess}}(P_{t}f)\|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}

\displaystyle\leq|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\left(\Psi(t)P_{t}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\Psi^{1/2}(t).

Combining these inequalities with (
5
) and observing that

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}f|\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-K/2}P_{t}|\nabla f|\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-K/2},

we arrive at

\displaystyle-\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\Psi^{1/2}(t)\int 2q|P_{t}f|^{2q-1}|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\quad+\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\int 2q(2q-1)(P_{t}f)^{2q-2}|\tau_{\nu}|_{\rm op}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Therefore, from the Young-Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle-\phi^{\prime}(t)\leq C(t)\phi(t)+D(t),

where

D(t)=\Psi^{1/2}(t)\int|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu+\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}\int\big((2q-1)|\tau_{\nu}|_{\rm op}\big)^{q}\mathrm{d}\nu

and

C(t)=\Psi^{1/2}(t)\,(2q)^{2q/(2q-1)}+\text{\rm{e}}^{-Kt}(2q)^{2q/(2q-2)}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\left(\int_{t}^{s}C(r)\,dr\right)D(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

and it follows that

\displaystyle\phi(0)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2q)^{2q/(2q-1)}}\exp{\left((2q)^{2q/(2q-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi^{1/2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)}\int|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{(2q)^{2q/(2q-2)}/K}}{(2q)^{2q/(2q-2)}}\int\left((2q-1)|\tau_{\nu}|_{\rm op}\right)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Therefore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{M}|f|^{2q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq C\left(\int|\tau_{\nu}-{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu+\int\big(2q|\tau_{\nu}|_{\rm op}\big)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right).\qed

Remark 5.2
.

We see that when
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K
, by Obata’s Rigidity Theorem
(see
[
25
, Theorem 3.4]
),
M
is
isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which implies
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K
,
\alpha_{n}=K
,
\|R\|_{\infty}=0
,
and then the constant
C
is independent of the dimension

n
. In the general case however, as
\Psi
depends on the
dimension, the constant
C
will not be dimension-free.

When
p=2
, we observe that

|\tau_{\nu}|_{\rm op}\leq 1+|\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}
which implies
that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}_{\nu}(f)\leq C(1+S(\nu\,|\,\mu)+S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)).

Thus, the Stein discrepancy
S(\nu\,|\,\mu)
with respect to the
invariant measure gives another control of the spectral properties
for log-concave measures, see
[
10
]
for the Lipshitz
characterization of Poincaré inequalities for measures of this
type.

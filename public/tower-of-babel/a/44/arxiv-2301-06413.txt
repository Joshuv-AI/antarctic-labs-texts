For

We invoke here some known arithmetic functions on

\displaystyle\mu(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if n = 1},\\

(1)

A
Dirichlet series
is a series of the form

\displaystyle f(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}j^{-s},

where

If

for all

then we have the Riemann zeta function, denoted by
\zeta.

If
f
is convergent at

, then it converges
uniformly throughout the angular region
\{s\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg(s-s_{0})|<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta\}
for every positive real number
\delta<\frac{\pi}{2}.
Consequently,
f
defines a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}_{\Re(s_{0})}
(refer to
[
13
, Chapter IX]
for the basic theory of Dirichlet series). Let
\mathcal{D}
denotes the set of all functions which are representable by a convergent Dirichlet series in some right half plane.

The following proposition describes the product of two Dirichlet series (see
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.1 and discussion prior to Theorem 4.3.4]
).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let

and
g(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}j^{-s}
be two convergent Dirichlet series on
\mathbb{H}_{\rho}.
If
g
converges absolutely on
\mathbb{H}_{\rho},
then

\displaystyle f(s)g(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{m|j}a_{m}b_{\frac{j}{m}}\Big)j^{-s},

which converges on
\mathbb{H}_{\rho}.

For an integer

let
\text{\bf gpf}(n)
denote the greatest prime factor of

For a sequence
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{j}\}_{j\geqslant 2}
of non-negative real numbers, let

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathbf{x}}:=\inf\Big\{\Re(s):\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}x_{j}j^{-s}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbf{x}}:=\inf\Bigg\{\Re(s):\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant 2\\
\tiny{\mbox{\bf gpf}}(j)\leqslant p_{n}\end{subarray}}x_{j}j^{-s}<\infty~\text{for all}~n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\Bigg\},

(2)

where

is the increasing enumeration of prime numbers. Note that

1.1.
Multipliers of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space

Let
X
be a non-empty open subset of
\mathbb{C}
and
let
H^{\infty}(X)
denote the Banach space of bounded holomorphic functions on
X
endowed with supremum norm.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of complex-valued holomorphic functions on
X.
A function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called
multiplier of
\mathcal{H}
if
\varphi\cdot f\in\mathcal{H}
for every
f\in\mathcal{H}.
Clearly,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
is an algebra. Denote by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H}),
the set of all multipliers of
\mathcal{H}.
Note that if the constant function equal to
1
belongs to
\mathcal{H},
then by
[
9
, Corollary 5.22]
,

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
is contractively contained in
H^{\infty}(X).

For a sequence
\mathbf{w}=\{w_{j}\}_{j=2}^{\infty}
of positive real numbers,
consider the weighted Hilbert space of the formal Dirichlet series

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}:=\Bigg\{f(s)=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\hat{f}(j)j^{-s}:\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbf{w}}:=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{|\hat{f}(j)|^{2}}{w_{j}}\textless\infty\Bigg\}

endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{w}}.

If
\sigma_{\mathbf{w}}<+\infty,
then
\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space associated with
\kappa_{\mathbf{w}}
given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathbf{w}}(s,u)=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}w_{j}j^{-s-\overline{u}},\quad s,u\in\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\sigma_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}}.

(3)

For every
s\in\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\sigma_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}},

\kappa_{\mathbf{w}}(\cdot,s)\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{\kappa_{\mathbf{w}}(\cdot,s)}\rangle=f(s),\quad f\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}.

(4)

Note that
\kappa_{\mathbf{w}}
converges absolutely on
\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\sigma_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}}\times\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\sigma_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}}.

The following is immediate from
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
and the discussion prior to
[
12
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}})\subseteq H^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}})\cap\mathcal{D}
and

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\infty,\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}}}\leqslant\|M_{\varphi,\mathbf{w}}\|_{\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}},\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}).

This raises the following question.

Question 1.1
.

For which sequences
\mathbf{w},
the multiplier algebra
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}})
of
\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}
is isometrically isomorphic to
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}})\cap\mathcal{D}?

In the following cases, Question
1.1
is answered affirmatively.

(i)

If
\mathbf{1}
denotes the constant sequence with value
1,
then
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{1}})
is isometrically isomorphic to
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{0})\cap\mathcal{D}
(see
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
).

(ii)

If, for some positive Radon measure
\eta
on
[0,\infty)
with

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w_{j}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}j^{-2\sigma}d\eta(\sigma),\quad j\geqslant n_{0},

(see
[
6
, Theorem 1.11]
).

(iii)

If
\mathbf{w}
is either
\{\frac{1}{d_{\beta}(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},
where

and

denotes

or

8

(iv)

If

for all integers

) satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{w_{p^{j-1}}}{w_{p^{j}}}\leqslant p^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}~\text{for all integers}~~j\geqslant 1~\mbox{and prime numbers}~p,

(6)

then

[
12
, Corollary 4.2]
).

In all the cases above, the multipliers of

are extended beyond the common domain of the functions in

The following is the main theorem of this paper, which generalizes the aforementioned results

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant 2\\
j|n\end{subarray}}j^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}w_{j}\mu\Big(\frac{n}{j}\Big)\geqslant 0,\quad n\geqslant 2,

where

) and (

), respectively.
Then
\mathcal{M}(\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}})
is isometrically isomorphic to
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}_{\frac{\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}{2}})\cap\mathcal{D}.

.

Here we make some observations:

(a)

If

then there are no non-constant multipliers of

Indeed, if

,

is entire and bounded. Hence, by the Liouville’s theorem

(b)

for an integer

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant k\\
j|n\end{subarray}}j^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}w_{j}\mu\Big(\frac{n}{j}\Big)\geqslant 0,\quad n\geqslant k,

(8)

then the conclusion of Theorem

2.

\mathscr{H}_{\mathbf{w}}

In view of Remark

The first one relates the condition (

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

Then the kernel

\displaystyle\eta(s,u)=\frac{\kappa_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}(s,u)}{\kappa_{\mathbf{1}}(s,u)},\quad s,u\in\mathbb{H}_{\beta}

Proof.

By

]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu(j)j^{-s},\quad s\in\mathbb{H}_{1}.

This, combined with

and (

\displaystyle\eta(s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}j^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}w_{j}j^{-s-\overline{u}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu(j)j^{-s-\overline{u}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant 2\\
j|n\end{subarray}}j^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}w_{j}\mu\Big(\frac{n}{j}\Big)\Big)n^{-s-\overline{u}},\quad s,u\in\mathbb{H}_{\beta}.

The desired equivalence is now immediate from

The following lemma is needed in the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 2.2
.

For

(

Let

Since

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(s)j(f)(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\Big(s+\frac{t}{2}\Big)f\Big(s+\frac{t}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant 2\\

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(s_{m})_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\in\,\text{Im}(\psi)}\!\!\!\Big(\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}p_{i_{m}}^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}s_{m}}\Big)\big(w_{{}_{\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}p_{i_{m}}^{s_{m}}}}\big)\mu\Big({\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}p_{i_{m}}^{r_{m}-s_{m}}}\Big).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geqslant 2\\
j|n\end{subarray}}j^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}}w_{j}\mu\Big(\frac{n}{j}\Big)\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{(s_{m})_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\in\,\text{Im}(\psi)}\sum_{t=1}^{\omega(n)}\Bigg(w_{p_{i_{t}}^{s_{t}}}\Big(\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}p_{i_{m}}^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}s_{m}}\Big)\mu\Big({\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}p_{i_{m}}^{r_{m}-s_{m}}}\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\omega(n)}\!\sum_{(s_{m})_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\in\,\text{Im}(\psi)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\Big(w_{p_{i_{t}}^{s_{t}}}\Big(\!\!\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}p_{i_{m}}^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}s_{m}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\mu(p_{i_{m}}^{r_{m}-s_{m}})\Big)\!\Big)

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\omega(n)}\sum_{(s_{m})_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\in\,\mathcal{U}_{n}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\Big(w_{p_{i_{t}}^{s_{t}}}\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\Big(p_{i_{m}}^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}s_{m}}\mu(p_{i_{m}}^{r_{m}-s_{m}})\Big)\Big),

\displaystyle T_{t}=\sum_{(s_{m})_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\in\,\mathcal{U}_{n}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\Big(w_{p_{i_{t}}^{s_{t}}}\prod_{m=1}^{\omega(n)}\Big(p_{i_{m}}^{-\delta_{\mathbf{w}}s_{m}}\mu(p_{i_{m}}^{r_{m}-s_{m}})\Big)\Big).

\displaystyle T_{t}\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!=

2
Hadronic insensitive observables

To explore NP emerging around the electroweak energy scale affecting the bottom to strange transitions involving leptons one is led to introduce the following class of effective operators

{\cal O}_{b_{X}\ell_{Y}}=(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{X}b)(\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}P_{Y}\ell)\ ,

(7)

which can be written as
\,{\rm SU}(2)_{L}
-invariant operators. A more general discussion can be found in
[
62
,
63
,
61
]
.
These operators are incorporated in the following effective Hamiltonian

\mathscr{H}_{\rm eff}=-V_{tb}V_{ts}^{*}\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi v^{2}}\sum_{\ell,X,Y}C_{b_{X}\ell_{Y}}{\cal O}_{b_{X}\ell_{Y}}\,+\mathrm{h.c.}\,,

(8)

where the sum runs over leptons
\ell=\{e,\mu,\tau\}
and over their chiralities
X,Y=\{L,R\}
. Additionally, it is convenient to define dimensionless coefficients
C_{I}
related to the dimensionful
c_{I}
coefficients appearing in the equivalent Lagrangian formulation

\mathscr{L}_{\rm eff}=\sum_{\ell,X,Y}c_{b_{X}\ell_{Y}}{\cal O}_{b_{X}\ell_{Y}}\ ,

(9)

and

c_{I}=V_{tb}V_{ts}^{*}\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi v^{2}}C_{I}=-\frac{C_{I}}{(36\,{\rm TeV})^{2}}\,,

(10)

where

V_{ts}=-0.0412\pm 0.0006
and

v=1/(2\sqrt{2}G_{\rm F})=(174\,{\rm GeV})^{2}
is the square of the Higgs vacuum expectation value and
G_{\rm F}
the Fermi constant.

Motivated by the SM prediction that
|C^{\rm SM}_{b_{L}\ell_{R}}|\ll|C^{\rm SM}_{b_{L}\ell_{L}}|
, since
C^{\rm SM}_{b_{L}\ell_{L}}=8.64
and
C^{\rm SM}_{b_{L}\ell_{R}}=-0.18
, as in
[
17
]
we use the chiral basis related to the standard one
[
64
]
via

\displaystyle 2C_{9}={C_{b_{L}\mu_{L+R}}},\quad 2C_{10}=-{C_{b_{L}\mu_{L-R}}},\quad 2C^{\prime}_{9}={C_{b_{R}\mu_{L+R}}},\quad 2C^{\prime}_{10}=-{C_{b_{R}\mu_{L-R}}}\ .

(11)

In the SM we have
C_{9}^{\rm SM}\approx-C_{10}^{\rm SM}
. We also use the following notation
[
17
]

\displaystyle C_{b_{L\pm R}\ell_{Y}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle C_{b_{L}\ell_{Y}}\pm C_{b_{R}\ell_{Y}},\quad C_{b_{L+R}\ell_{L\pm R}}\equiv C_{b_{L}\ell_{L}}+C_{b_{R}\ell_{L}}\pm C_{b_{L}\ell_{R}}\pm C_{b_{R}\ell_{R}},

\displaystyle C_{b_{X}(\mu-e)_{Y}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle C_{b_{X}\mu_{Y}}-C_{b_{X}e_{Y}}.

(12)

Experimental deviations from the SM predictions are encoded by splitting the
C_{b_{X}\ell_{Y}}
Wilson coefficients as follows

C_{I}=C_{I}^{SM}+\Delta C_{I}\ .

(13)

We define with
C_{I}^{SM}
the coefficients arising from the RG analysis performed by
Flavio

[
65
,
62
,
63
]
within the SM.

We further divide
\Delta C_{I}
into a part that contains potentially unknown hadronic effects
[
45
]
(not contained in
Flavio

[
65
]
) plus a genuine beyond standard model (BSM) contribution:

\Delta C_{I}=C_{I}^{H}+C_{I}^{BSM}\ .

(14)

Although, in principle, one needs a non-perturbative computation of the hadronic contributions
[
66
,
19
]
, it is a fact that any SM-induced hadronic corrections preserve lepton flavour universality (LFU) and therefore it cannot explain
R_{K}
and
R_{K^{*}}
. We use this point, in one of our analyses, to phenomenologically estimate both the hadronic contributions and the ones stemming from BSM able to explain the observed lepton flavour universality violations (LFV)s.

2.1
R_{K}
anatomy

The LHCb collaboration presented a recent measurement of
the
R_{K}
ratio (see Figure
1(a)
and Figure
1(b)
representing the leading Feynman diagrams for the relevant
B
decay) with full Run 1 and Run 2 data.
The result
[
4
]
is

R_{K}=\frac{{\rm BR}\left(B^{+}\to K^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\right)}{{\rm BR}\left(B^{+}\to K^{+}e^{+}e^{-}\right)}=0.846^{+0.042}_{-0.039}\text{(stat)}^{+0.013}_{-0.012}\text{(syst)}\qquad[1.1,6]\,\text{GeV}^{2}\ .

(15)

The central value in (
15
) is
3.5
standard deviations below the SM unity prediction.
Furthermore, the
Belle
collaboration presented a preliminary
results for this quantity, that is
[
2
]

R_{K}=1.03^{+0.28}_{-0.24}\pm 0.01\quad[1.1,6]\,\text{GeV}^{2}.

Because the experimental analysis provides results in bins of the squared invariant mass of the lepton system
q^{2}\equiv(P_{\ell^{-}}+P_{\ell^{+}})^{2}
we consider the related
q^{2}
-dependence via

R_{K}[q_{\rm min}^{2},q_{\rm max}^{2}]\equiv\frac{\int_{q_{\rm min}^{2}}^{q_{\rm max}^{2}}dq^{2}d\Gamma(B^{+}\to K^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})/dq^{2}}{\int_{q_{\rm min}^{2}}^{q_{\rm max}^{2}}dq^{2}d\Gamma(B^{+}\to K^{+}e^{+}e^{-})/dq^{2}}\,.

(16)

The energy bin reported by the experiments used in eq. (
15
) refers to

R_{K}\!\equiv\!R_{K}[1.1\,{\rm GeV}^{2},6\,{\rm GeV}^{2}]
. More precisely, the decay rate leading to eq. (
16
), in the limit of vanishing lepton masses, reads
[
64
]

\frac{d\Gamma(B^{+}\to K^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{dq^{2}}=\frac{G_{\rm F}^{2}\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}|V_{tb}V_{ts}^{*}|^{2}}{2^{10}\pi^{5}M_{B}^{3}}\lambda^{3/2}(M_{B}^{2},M_{K}^{2},q^{2})\left(|F_{V}|^{2}+|F_{A}|^{2}\right)~,

(17)

where
\lambda(a,b,c)\equiv a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-2(ab+bc+ac)
,
M_{B}\approx 5.279
GeV,
M_{K}\approx 0.494
GeV,

|V_{tb}V_{ts}^{*}|\approx 40.58\times 10^{-3}
. The form factors
F_{V/A}
collect the contribution of the standard form factors
f_{+/T}
as well as the relevant Wilson coefficients and possible non-factorazible hadronic terms

\displaystyle F_{A}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(C_{10}+C_{10}^{\prime}\right)f_{+}(q^{2})~,

(18)

\displaystyle F_{V}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C_{9}+C_{9}^{\prime})f_{+}(q^{2})+\underbrace{\frac{2m_{b}}{M_{B}+M_{K}}\left(C_{7}+C_{7}^{\prime}\right)f_{T}(q^{2})}_{\mathclap{\rm SM\,electromagnetic\,dipole\,contribution}}\quad\mbox{+}\quad\underbrace{h_{K}(q^{2})}_{\mathclap{\rm non-factorizable\,term}}~.

(19)

It is possible to neglect the SM contributions from the electromagnetic dipole operator (encoded in the coefficients
C_{7}^{(\prime)}
) because of the experimental cut
q_{\rm min}^{2}=1.1
GeV
2
.

It is important to establish which part of the non-factorizable contribution, stemming from
h_{K}(q^{2})
, affects the scaling dependence of
C_{9}^{SM}
and which enters the hadronic contribution in
C_{9}^{H}
. In fact, one can even define an effective
C_{9}^{\rm eff}
that eliminates the overall scale-dependence due to its contribution as shown in
[
19
]
. This happens because there is a part of
h_{K}(q^{2})
which is proportional to
f_{+}(q^{2})

[
19
]
. There is, however, still an undetermined hadronic long-distance contribution estimated, for example in
[
66
]
and discussed also in
[
19
]
. Here, we use a pragmatic approach according to which
C_{9}^{SM}
is taken to be the result of the RG analysis of
Flavio

[
65
]
. We relegate the undetermined hadronic contribution in
C_{9}^{H}
. Once precise non-perturbative techniques will be able to determine the hadronic contributions they can be compared with our fitted results.

We will return to the non-factorizable hadronic contributions in the later sections but we neglect them here since they do not affect the LFV processes. One finally arrives at the following expression for
R_{K}
in terms of the relevant Wilson coefficients:

R_{K}=\frac{|C_{b_{L+R}\mu_{L-R}}|^{2}+|C_{b_{L+R}\mu_{L+R}}|^{2}}{|C_{b_{L+R}e_{L-R}}|^{2}+|C_{b_{L+R}e_{L+R}}|^{2}}\ .

(20)

In the SM it is expected to be
R_{K}=1
with QED corrections in the relevant
q^{2}
bin of the order of few percents
[
67
]
.

To learn more about the
\Delta C_{I}
structure we switch on muon and electron coefficients’ deviations separately. We also assume
C_{I}^{H}=0
since here we are interested in understanding the contribution to LFVs.
We expand (
20
) by splitting the Wilson coefficient in SM and BSM contributions. Focusing, at first, on assuming NP in the muon sector we have:

\displaystyle R_{K}=1+\frac{2C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{SM}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{{b_{L}e_{L}}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{{b_{L}e_{R}}}^{SM})^{2}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[Re(C_{{b_{L}\mu_{L}}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{{b_{R}\mu_{L}}}^{BSM})]}+

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}^{2}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}+

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{2C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{SM}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]}+

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]}^{2}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}+

\displaystyle\frac{{[Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})+Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}^{2}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}+

\displaystyle\frac{{[Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})+Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]}^{2}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}},

where we have separated real and imaginary parts for BSM coefficients in order to gain further insight on which NP operators might be responsible for the observed discrepancies.

We can immediately make the following preliminary observations:

•

There is the
b_{L}
into
b_{R}
exchange symmetry in the BSM corrections.

•

Terms linear in the BSM coefficients involve only their real parts and are the only ones that can reduce the SM value of
R_{K}
.

•

Contributions from the imaginary parts of the coefficients push
R_{K}
towards higher values than the SM one unless they cancel each other as follows

Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})\simeq-Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})\ ,\quad Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})\simeq-Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})\ .

(22)

By inserting the numerical values for the SM contributions, equation (
21
) specializes to

\displaystyle R_{K}=1+0.2314{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}-0.0049{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]}+

(23)

\displaystyle 0.0134\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}^{2}+{[Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})+Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}^{2}+

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}[Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})+Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]}^{2}+{[Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})+Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]}^{2}\}\ .

We can now obtain some interesting constraints on the NP coefficients by first considering one coefficient at a time while setting the remaining to zero. Requiring, for example, the final value of
R_{K}
to be within one standard deviation from the experimental value in (
15
) we deduce the following analytic constraint on the real parts of the left-handed muon coefficients

\displaystyle 0.807\leq 1+

\displaystyle 0.2314{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{X}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}+0.0134{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{X}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})]}^{2}\leq 0.888

(24)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\displaystyle-0.8786\leq{Re(C_{b_{X}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})}\leq-0.4983}

where
X
is either
L
or
R
. Because of the quadratic nature of the equation we typically find two solutions. The physically acceptable one is the one for which
|C_{b_{X}\mu_{L}}|
is less or of the order unity for the effective approach to be sensible. Still insisting on the single non-vanishing coefficient approximation no other solution is possible either real or complex.

If we assume NP in the electron sector only, we obtain an expression for
1/R_{K}
analogous to (
21
). Within one standard deviation from the experimental value we deduce

\displaystyle 1.126\leq 1+

\displaystyle 0.2314{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{X}e_{L}}^{BSM})]}+0.0134{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[Re(C_{b_{X}e_{L}}^{BSM})]}^{2}\leq 1.238

(25)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\displaystyle 0.5283\leq{Re(C_{b_{X}e_{L}}^{BSM})}\leq 0.9736}\ .

We clearly experience a mirror like behaviour for the electron coefficient with respect to the muon one in trying to explain the SM deviation. In these two extreme limits the values of the coefficients are not very small. In fact, it is the large value of
C^{\rm SM}_{b_{L}\ell_{L}}=8.64
that justifies the chiral-linear approximation

{R_{K}\simeq 1+2\frac{{\rm Re}\,C^{\rm BSM}_{b_{L+R}(\mu-e)_{L}}}{C^{\rm SM}_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}}}\ .

(26)

The overall picture is consistent with earlier findings according to which the dominant effect stems from couplings to left-handed leptons.

,
69
,

]
, that is defined as

p=\frac{g_{0}+g_{\parallel}}{g_{0}+g_{\parallel}+g_{\perp}}\,.

(31)

The

R_{K^{*}}-R_{K}
for which the analytic expression under the assumption that NP hides in the muon sector reads:

\displaystyle\frac{R_{K^{*}}-R_{K}}{4p}=-\frac{C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{SM}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})}-

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}+

(32)

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{SM}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})}-

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}+

\displaystyle-\frac{Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}-

\displaystyle\frac{Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(C_{b_{L}e_{L}}^{SM})^{2}+(C_{b_{L}e_{R}}^{SM})^{2}}}\ .

Given that the left-hand side is negative we have that:

•

For the terms linear in
Re(C^{BSM}_{b_{R}\mu_{X}})
the product
C_{b_{L}\mu_{X}}^{SM}Re(C^{BSM}_{b_{R}\mu_{X}})
should be positive if all the other coefficients are set to zero. This is, however, in conflict with considerations made in the previous section for
R_{K}
.

•

To resolve the conflict above, one more coefficient must be turned on. The minimal set of non-vanishing coefficients are
Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})
and
Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})
. One can also imagine to turn on
Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})
, but as we shall soon see this contribution is highly suppressed via the SM coefficients. Additionally, note also that now we can determine the chirality of the
b
quark while it is not possible by the solely analysis on
R_{K}
, as seen in the previous section.

•

Interestingly we also observe that the imaginary parts must be suppressed in absolute value since the
R_{K}
solution in (
22
) would provide positive corrections here.

Substituting the SM values we have

\displaystyle R_{K^{*}}-R_{K}=

\displaystyle-0.3981{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})}+0.0084{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})}

(33)

\displaystyle-0.0461[{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})}+Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}^{BSM})+

\displaystyle+{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0}Re(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})}+Im(C_{b_{L}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})Im(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})]\ .

A clear SM induced hierarchy emerges, according to which we can neglect the NP contribution associated to
Re(C_{b_{R}\mu_{R}}^{BSM})
as it will also be clear from the final numerical analysis.

Deviations coming from the electron sector can be studied in a fashion similar to the muon case using
1/R_{K^{*}}-1/R_{K}
, which will be of the same form as (
33
), but with electronic BSM coefficients instead of muonic ones.

All in all, only real coefficients involving left-handed leptons can be used to agree with data within a one sigma range with the current analytic considerations.

2.4
Summary of theoretical findings

From the pedagogical theoretical investigations above we have extracted a number of relevant information that we summarise below.

1.

NP is needed to account for the deficit in
R_{K}
and
R_{K^{\ast}}
with respect to the SM value.

2.

If NP appears in either the muon or electron sector it privileges real Wilson coefficients.

3.

In agreement with the results of
[
17
]
one can explain the experimental result for
R_{K}
with NP in the muon sector by turning on the real part of
C^{\rm BSM}_{b_{L}\mu_{L}}\sim-1
. However, because of the simultaneous deviation of
R_{K^{*}}
from the SM and from
R_{K}
itself, another coefficient is needed which is the real part of
C^{\rm BSM}_{b_{R}\mu_{L}}
.

4.

To the extent that one focuses only on the HI observables, one can explain the deviations also with NP in the electron sector with order unity values for the real parts of
C^{\rm BSM}_{b_{L}e_{L}}
and
C^{\rm BSM}_{b_{R}e_{L}}
.

5.

A more precise measure of
B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
will select either the muon or the electron solution within the HI observables. As we shall see in the following sections, HS (angular observables) results suggest NP in the muon sector.

The time is ripe to provide a numerical analysis that takes into account both the HS and HI observables.

3
Comparison with experimental data

In the previous section we have assessed the main properties of the ‘Hadronic Insensitive’ observables in order to guide our expectations when comparing with the bulk of experimental results.

Here we use the
Flavio

[
65
]
toolkit to compare theory and experiments. This as an open source code aimed for flavour physics analyses. In the basic
Flavio
package NP contributions are encoded in deviations from the SM Wilson coefficients introduced in section
2
.

The statistical method employed is that of the ‘Fast Likelihood’
[
65
]
, which is based on the following form of the likelihood function

-2\ln L(\mathbf{C})=\sum_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{C})[C_{\text{exp}}+C_{\text{th}}(\mathbf{\theta}_{0})]^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{i}(\mathbf{C})\qquad\mathbf{x}_{i}(\mathbf{C})=\mathbf{O}_{i}^{\text{exp}}-\mathbf{O}_{i}^{\text{th}}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{\theta}_{0})

(38)

where

1.

L(\mathbf{C})
is the likelihood function.

2.

\mathbf{C}
is the set of Wilson Coefficients.

3.

\mathbf{\theta}_{0}
is the set of the SM parameters’ central values.

4.

\mathbf{O}^{\text{th}}
are the theory predictions for the observables.

5.

\mathbf{O}^{\text{exp}}
are the experimental measurements for the observables.

6.

C_{\text{exp}}
is the covariance matrix of the experimental measurements.

7.

C_{\text{th}}
is the covariance matrix of theory predictions in the SM for the central values
\mathbf{\theta_{0}}
of the theory parameters.

In particular,
C_{\text{th}}
is obtained from randomly sampling the observables in the theory parameter space. Finally,
C_{\text{exp}}
is obtained via a Gaussian approximation of the experimental Probability Distribution Functions (PDFs).

Following the overall strategy first outlined in
[
17
]
, we divide the flavour sensitive experimental data in two, hadronic insensitive and hadronic sensitive. The HI are
R_{K}
,
R_{K^{*}}
and
\hbox{BR}(B_{s}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
. The HS list of variables counts 257 observables (see Appendix
A
). We report in Table

the differential branching ratios of semi-leptonic
B
-meson decays and in Table
6
the physical quantities extracted by the angular analysis of
B
-mesons decay products. Specifically, observables deviating more than
2.5\sigma
from the SM prediction are highlighted in blue, while those deviating more than
3.5\sigma
are highlighted in red. Some of the observables, such as

and
P_{5}^{\prime}
, provide similar information as discussed in
[
239

240

and therefore are expected to be correlated. To compensate for the lack of knowledge about the experimental correlation we have redone the fit switching off
S_{5}
and find identical results. Furthermore, if we consider only one set of observables,
S_{i}
rather than
P_{i}
or vice-versa, we find results compatible with each other within one sigma.

In the first step of our analysis we use only the HI set of observables while in the second and third step we estimate the effects of the HS observables and combine them all in a global fit.
Finally, we propose an approach to obtain some insight on possible long distance hadronic effects based on the available data.

6
Anomalies driven theoretical models

New physics is needed to explain the violation of LFU and the measured deviation from the SM of the lepton anomalous magnetic moments. It is therefore natural to imagine this physics to appear at some energy scales
\Lambda
that are higher than the electroweak (EW) scale taken to be around
250
GeV. Because the observed deviations occur at a much lower energy scale, i.e. either
B
-meson scale or the muon mass scale one has to take into account SM and BSM radiative corrections appearing over several order of magnitudes. This investigation can be performed, model by model, and requires matching the NP theory first with effective operators around the EW scale and then running them down to the desired scale relevant for the experimental measures taking into account SM corrections. Although this procedure is well defined we prefer to concentrate on the leading BSM contributions to the desired Wilson coefficients responsible for the observed anomalies.

We discuss now the most compelling theoretical scenarios which can account for the over-mentioned anomalies.
The two first models include a new heavy particle, a
Z^{\prime}
or a leptoquark, which mediate at tree level the relevant interaction generating the effective operator
(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{X}b)(\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}P_{Y}\ell)
. The latter can also be generated radiatively via loops of fermions and scalars. In general, related interactions also induce the operators

c_{b_{L}b_{L}}(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}b)^{2}+c_{\mu_{L}\nu_{\mu}}(\bar{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\mu)(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{\mu})\ ,

(55)

which can affect other observables. In particular, the first operator contributes to
B_{s}
mass mixing and the relative coefficient is consequently bounded as
c^{\rm BSM}_{b_{L}b_{L}}=(-0.09\pm 0.08)/(110\,{\rm TeV})^{2}

[
74
,
75
]
. The second operator affects the neutrino trident cross section. CCFR data yields the bound
|c^{\rm BSM}_{\mu_{L}\nu_{\mu}}|<1/(490\,{\rm GeV})^{2}
at 95% C.L. by
[
76
]
. The scenarios we will examine, considering NP contributions in muon interactions, will also affect the
(g-2)_{\mu}
observable.

8
Conclusions

In this work we have analyzed the LHCb experimental results for the
\mu/e
ratios
R_{K}
and
R_{K^{*}}
, the
g-2
lepton magnetic moments results including the most recent data as well as the CDF result for the
W
mass. These measures substantially deviate from the expected values predicted by the SM. We have, however, also included in the analysis all the other observables related to this physics.

We started with a deep theoretical investigation of the
\mu/e
ratios
R_{K}
and
R_{K^{*}}
as well as the process
B_{s}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
. We went beyond the state-of-the-art by studying the impact of complex Wilson coefficients and derived constraints on both their imaginary and real parts. This analysis has been then followed by a comprehensive comparison with experimental results. We find that:

1.

The deviations from the Standard Model are at the
4.7\sigma
level when including only the hadronic insensitive observables.

2.

Including the hadronic sensitive observables the deviation from the SM increases to a
6.1\sigma
level.

3.

When considering simultaneously all relevant Wilson coefficients and combining both hadronic sensitive and insensitive data into the fit, the deviation from the SM peaks at 7.2
\sigma
and decreases at the
4.9\sigma
level if we assume that the central values of
R_{K}
and
R_{K}^{\ast}
are taken to be unity.

4.

By an independent method we estimated the SM contributions unaccounted for in
Flavio

[
65
]
which can stem, for an example, from hadronic physics and found that the result still supports the presence of NP.

In the future we plan to perform a similar analysis in terms of SM effective field theories also for the
g-2
and
W
-mass anomalies.

We then moved to review different theoretical models apt at explaining the deviations from the Standard Model with the following conclusions:

1.

A minimal model featuring a
Z^{\prime}
is unable to explain all the anomalies simultaneously. Additionally, even when trying to address only the LFU violation, one finds that this is challenged by the
B_{s}
mass mixing and LHC direct bounds. More involved models can ameliorate the situation.

2.

Scalar leptoquarks, either a singlet or a doublet of weak interactions, can explain all the anomalies. In the first case one has to forbid di-quark couplings and in the latter the contribution to flavour anomalies is radiatively generated.

3.

Technicolor-like models, with a scale of new physics around
2
TeV, can explain LFU violations, the
g-2
of the muon as well as the weighted average of the
W
-mass result.

4.

Composite Goldstone Higgs scenarios are challenged by the low energy composite scale required by the
g-2
anomaly, and therefore are disfavoured.

5.

Radiative models typically require very large muon left-handed Yukawa couplings and unnaturally large new fields multiplicity, thus are also challenged by the data.

6.

It is possible to accommodate dark matter and anomalies by either freeze-in or freeze-out type of constructions. The first mechanism can be realized in leptoquark type of models.

Last but not least we have estimated the impact of the forthcoming data from LHCb Belle II in few years from now, when LHC Run3 will be completed and Belle II will have accumulated
5ab^{-1}
.

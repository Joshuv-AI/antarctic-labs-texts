Quantum Linear Optics via String Diagrams
Giovanni de Felice and Bob Coecke

Abstract
We establish a formal bridge between qubit-based and photonic quantum computing. We do this by defining a functor from the ZX calculus to linear optical circuits. In the process we provide a compositional theory of quantum linear optics which allows to reason about events involving multiple photons such as those required to perform linear-optical and fusion-based quantum computing.

Fock space and permanents

Processing bosonic particles, such as photons, with linear optical devices
gives rise to statistics that are hard to simulate classically
[
6
]
.
In this section we give an interpretation of linear optical circuits, derived
from
[
30
]

\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}:\bf{Mat}_{\oplus}\to\bf{Vect}_{\otimes}

We show that this characterisation is equivalent to the model introduced in
[

Consider a box containing particles. Assume that the space of states of a single particle

H

free

\mathcal{F}(H)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{\otimes n}

where
\otimes

\oplus

\mathcal{F}(H)

distinguishable

n

Given a basis

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}X)\simeq l^{2}(X^{\ast})

where

X

X
and

is the canonical Hilbert space construction defined in
[
51
]

n

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{m})=(\mathbb{C}^{m})^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathbb{C}([m]^{n})

the basis states

Proof.

This follows by functoriality of tensor

Theorem 3.1
.

The functorial model of linear optics is equivalent to the permanent model.
Explicitly, for any

\bra{J}\mathcal{B}(U)\ket{I}=\frac{\mathtt{Perm}(U_{I,J})}{\sqrt{N_{I}N_{J}}}

Proof.

We start by expanding the left-hand side:

\displaystyle\bra{J}\mathcal{B}(U)\ket{I}

\displaystyle=\bra{J}U^{\hat{\otimes}n}\ket{I}=\bra{J}\alpha U^{\otimes n}\alpha^{\dagger}\ket{I}=(\alpha^{\dagger}\ket{J})^{\dagger}U^{\otimes n}(\alpha^{\dagger}\ket{I})

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\frac{N_{J}}{n!}}\sum_{Y\in a^{-1}(J)}\bra{Y}\right)U^{\otimes n}\,\left(\sqrt{\frac{N_{I}}{n!}}\sum_{X\in a^{-1}(I)}\ket{X}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N_{J}N_{I}}}{n!}\left|{a^{-1}(I)}\right|\sum_{Y\in a^{-1}(J)}\bra{Y}U^{\otimes n}\ket{\hat{X}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{N_{J}N_{I}}}{n!}\frac{n!}{N_{I}}\frac{1}{N_{J}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{\hat{X}_{i},\hat{Y}_{\sigma(i)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{I}N_{J}}}\mathtt{Perm}(U_{I,J})

where
\hat{X}\in a^{-1}(I)
and
\hat{Y}\in a^{-1}(J)
are any chosen representatives.
Note that this choice is irrelevant since we sum over all permutations, and
so in particular we can set
(U_{I,J})_{ij}=U_{\hat{X}_{i},\hat{Y}_{j}}
, yielding the last step.
∎

Example 3.1

(Hong-Ou-Mandel)
.

Consider the matrix of the beam splitter:

U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}i&1\\
1&i\end{pmatrix}

Suppose we input one boson in each port

.
There are three possible outcomes
J=\,(2,0),\,(1,1),\,(0,2)

We may determine the amplitudes of the different outcomes by computing permanents:

\mathtt{Perm}\begin{pmatrix}i&i\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}=2i\quad\mathtt{Perm}\begin{pmatrix}i&1\\
1&i\end{pmatrix}=0\quad\mathtt{Perm}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
i&i\end{pmatrix}=2i

The component for outcome
(1,1)
is
0

probability of observing one boson in each output port is
0

interference ensures that the bosons bunch together at the output of the device,
a phenomenon known as the Hong-Ou-Mandel effect.

Quantum paths and matchings

In the previous section we have shown that bosonic linear optics can be
formulated equivalently in terms of Fock space and permanents.
Aaronson and Arkhipov

that sampling from a linear

computer can compute an additive approximation of matrix permanents then the

results, we want to develop a diagrammatic syntax for programming linear

We do this by developing a quantised calculus
\bf{QPath}
which allows us to
compute the amplitudes of linear optical events involving multiple photons,
using simple rewrite rules.

Fusion measurements.

Fusion measurements are Bell measurements on dual-rail qubits.
They correspond to the linear map:

\ket{H,H}\,\mapsto\,\ket{H}\,,\,\ket{V,V}\,\mapsto\,\ket{V}\,,\,\ket{H,V},\ket{V,H}\,\mapsto\,0

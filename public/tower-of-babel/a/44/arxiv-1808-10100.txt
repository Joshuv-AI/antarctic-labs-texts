Optimality conditions for approximate Pareto solutions of a nonsmooth vector optimization problem with an infinite number of constraints
Ta Quang Son 1, Nguyen Van Tuyen 2, Ching-Feng Wen 3

Abstract
In this paper, we present some new necessary and sufficient optimality conditions in terms of the Clarke subdifferentials for approximate Pareto solutions of a nonsmooth vector optimization problem which has an infinite number of constraints. As a consequence, we obtain optimality conditions for the particular cases of cone-constrained convex vector optimization problems and semidefinite vector optimization problems. Examples are given to illustrate the obtained results.

Introduction

This paper mainly deals with constrained vector optimization problems formulated as follows:

\begin{split}&\text{\rm Min}_{\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\\
&\text{s.t.}\ \ x\in C:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g_{t}(x)\leqq 0,\ \ t\in T\},\end{split}

(VP)

where
f_{i}
,
i\in I:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and
g_{t}
,
t\in T
, are locally Lipschitz functions from a Banach space
X
to
\mathbb{R}
,
\Omega
is a nonempty and closed subset of
X
, and
T
is an arbitrary (possibly infinite) index set. Optimization problems of this type relate to

semi-infinite vector optimization problems
, provided that the space
X
is finite-dimensional, and to
infinite vector optimization problems
if
X
is infinite-dimensional; see
Bonnans
;
Gorberna
. The modeling of problems as (
VP
) naturally arises in a wide range of applications in various fields of mathematics, economics and engineering; we refer the readers to the books
Gorberna
;
Reemtsen
and to the papers
Canovas
;
Caristi
;
Chuong09
;
Chuong092
;
Chuong10
;
chuong14
;
Dinh07
;
Dinh09
;
Hettich
;
Kanzi10
with the references therein.

Our main concern is to study optimality conditions for approximate Pareto solutions of problem (
VP
). It should be noted here that the study of approximate solutions is very significant because, from the computational point of view, numerical algorithms usually generate only approximate solutions if we stop them after a finite number of steps. Furthermore, the solution set may be empty in the general noncompact case (see
Huy17
;
Jahn
;
Kim18
;
KMPT
;
Luc
;
Tuyen16
) whereas approximate solutions exist under very weak assumptions (see Propositions
1
and
2
in Section
2
below).

In the literature, there are many publications devoted to optimality conditions for approximate solutions of semi-infinite/infinite scalar optimization problems; see, for example,
Dinh07
;
Kanzi10
;
Kanzi14
;
L114
;
Long18
;
Loridan1982
;
Mishra12
;
Son09
. However, in contrast to the scalar case, there are a few works dealing optimality conditions for approximate Pareto solutions of semi-infinite/infinite vector optimization problems; see
Lee12
;
Son18
;
Shitkovskaya18
. In
Lee12
;
Shitkovskaya18
, the authors obtained necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for approximate Pareto solutions of a convex semi-infinite/infinite vector optimization problem under various kind of Farkas–Minkowski constraint qualifications. By using the Chankong–Haimes scalarization method, Kim and Son
Son18
established some necessary optimality conditions for approximate quasi Pareto solutions of a locally Lipschitz semi-infinite vector optimization problem.

In this paper, we present some necessary conditions of Karush–Kuhn–Tucker type for approximate (quasi) Pareto solutions of problem (
VP
) under a Slater-type constraint qualification hypothesis. Sufficient optimality conditions for approximate (quasi) Pareto solutions of problem (
VP
) are also provided by means of introducing the concepts of (strictly) generalized convex functions, defined in terms of the Clarke subdifferential of locally Lipschitz functions. The obtained results improve the corresponding ones in
Lee12
;
Son18
;
Shitkovskaya18
. As an application, we establish optimality conditions for cone-constrained convex vector optimization problems and semidefinite vector optimization problems. In addition, some examples are also given for illustrating the obtained results.

In Section
2
, we recall some basic definitions and preliminaries from the theory of vector optimization and variational analysis. Section
3
presents the main results. The application of the obtained results in Section
3
to cone-constrained convex vector optimization problems and semidefinite vector optimization problems is addressed in Section
4
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Approximate Pareto solutions

Let
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}:=\{y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\,|\,\,y_{i}\geqq 0,\,\,i\in I\}
be the nonnegative orthant in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, by
a\leqq b
, we mean
b-a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
; by
a\leq b
, we mean
b-a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
; and by
a<b
, we mean
b-a\in\text{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
.

Definition 1
(see
Loridan1984
)

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
and
\bar{x}\in C
. We say that

(i)

\bar{x}
is a
weakly Pareto solution
(resp., a
Pareto solution
) of (
VP
) if there is no
x\in C
such that

f(x)<f(\bar{x})\ \ (\text{resp.},\,\,f(x)\leq f(\bar{x})).

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-weakly Pareto solution
(resp., a
\xi
-Pareto solution
) of (
VP
) if there is no
x\in C
such that

f(x)+\xi<f(\bar{x})\ \ (\text{resp.},\,\,f(x)+\xi\leq f(\bar{x})).

(iii)

\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution
(resp., a
\xi
-quasi Pareto solution
) of (
VP
) if there is no
x\in C
such that

f(x)+\|x-\bar{x}\|\xi<f(\bar{x})\ \ (\text{resp.},\,\,f(x)+\|x-\bar{x}\|\xi\leq f(\bar{x})).

Remark 1

If
\xi=0
, then the concepts of a
\xi
-Pareto solution and a
\xi
-quasi-Pareto solution (resp., a
\xi
-weakly Pareto solution and a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution) coincide with the concept of a Pareto solution (resp., a weakly Pareto solution). Hence when dealing with approximate Pareto solutions we only consider the case that
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
.

Definition 2

Let
A
be a subset in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
{\bar{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The set
A\cap({\bar{y}}-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+})
is called a
section
of
A
at
{\bar{y}}
and denoted by
[A]_{\bar{y}}.
The section
[A]_{\bar{y}}
is said to be
bounded
if, and only if, there is
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

[A]_{\bar{y}}\subset a+\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}.

Remark 2

Let
\bar{y}
be an arbitrary element in
f(C)
. It is easily seen that every
\xi
-Pareto solution (resp.,
\xi
-quasi Pareto solution) of (
VP
) on
C\cap f^{-1}\left([f(C)]_{\bar{y}}\right)
is also a
\xi
-Pareto one (resp.,
\xi
-quasi Pareto one) of (
VP
) on
C
.

The following results give some sufficient conditions for the existence of approximate Pareto solutions of problem (
VP
).

Proposition 1
(Existence of approximate Pareto solutions)

Assume that
f(C)
has a nonempty bounded section. Then, for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\},
the problem (
VP
) admits at least one
\xi
-Pareto solution.

Proof

The assertion follows directly from Remark
2
and
(
ha06
, Lemma 3.1)
, so is omitted.
\hfill\Box

Proposition 2
(Existence of approximate quasi Pareto solutions)

If
f(C)
has a nonempty bounded section, then, for every
\xi\in{\rm int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},
problem (
VP
) admits at least one
\xi
-quasi Pareto solution.

Proof

Let
x^{0}\in C
be such that the section of
f(C)
at
f(x^{0})
is bounded. By Proposition
1
, there exists
\bar{x}\in C
such that
f(\bar{x})\leqq f(x^{0})
and

\displaystyle f(C)\ \cap\ [f(\bar{x})-\xi-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\emptyset.

Consequently,

f(C)\cap[f(\bar{x})-\xi-\mathrm{int}\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}]\ =\ \emptyset.

By the continuity of
f
and the closedness of
C,
for each
x\in C,
the set

\{u\in C\;|\;f(u)+\|u-x\|\xi\ \leqq\ f(x)\}

is closed. By
(
araya
, Theorem 3.1)
, there exists
x^{*}\in C
such that
f(x^{*})<f(\bar{x})
and

\displaystyle f(x)+\|x-x^{*}\|\xi-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\notin

\displaystyle-\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\quad\textrm{for all}\quad x\in C,x\neq x^{*}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle f(x)+\|x-x^{*}\|\xi

\displaystyle\nleq

\displaystyle f(x^{*})\quad\textrm{ for all }\quad x\in C,x\neq x^{*}.

Thus,
x^{*}
is a
\xi
-quasi Pareto solution of (
VP
). The proof is complete.
\hfill\qed

2.2
Normals and subdifferentials

For the Banach space
X
, the bracket
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
stands for the canonical pairing between space
X
and its dual
X^{*}
. The closed unit ball of
X
is denoted by
B_{X}.
The closed ball with center
x
and radius
\delta
is denoted by
B(x,\delta)
. Let
A
be a nonempty subset of
X
. The topological interior, the topological closure, and the convex hull of A are denoted, respectively, by
\mathrm{int}\,A
,
\mathrm{cl}\,A
, and
\mathrm{cone}\,A
. The symbol
A^{\circ}
stands for the polar cone of a given set
A\subset X
, i.e.,

A^{\circ}=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\;|\;\langle x^{*},x\rangle\leqq 0,\ \ \forall x\in A\}.

Let
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz function. The Clarke generalized directional derivative of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in X
in the direction
d\in X
, denoted by
\varphi^{\circ}(\bar{x};d)
, is defined by

\varphi^{\circ}(\bar{x};d):=\mathop{\limsup\limits_{x\to\bar{x}}}\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\varphi(x+td)-\varphi(x)}{t}.

The Clarke subdifferential of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\,|\,\langle x^{*},d\rangle\leqq\varphi^{\circ}(\bar{x};d),\ \ \forall d\in X\}.

Let
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
X
. The Clarke tangent cone to
S
at
\bar{x}\in S
is defined by

T(\bar{x};S):=\{v\in X\;|\;d_{S}^{\circ}(\bar{x};v)=0\},

where
d_{S}
denotes the distance function to
S
. The Clarke normal cone to
S
at
\bar{x}\in S
is defined by

N(\bar{x};S):=[T(\bar{x};S)]^{\circ}.

The following lemmas will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 1
(see
(
Clakre83
, p. 52)
)

Let
\varphi
be a locally Lipschitz function from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
and
S
be a nonempty subset of
X
. If
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
on
S
, then

0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})+N(\bar{x};S).

Lemma 2
(see
(
Clakre83
, Proposition 2.3.3)
)

Let
\varphi_{l}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
l=1,\ldots,p
,
p\geqq 2
, be locally Lipschitz around
\bar{x}\in X
. Then we have the following inclusion

\partial(\varphi_{1}+\ldots+\varphi_{p})(\bar{x})\subset\partial\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})+\ldots+\partial\varphi_{p}(\bar{x}).

Lemma 3
(see
(
Clakre83
, Proposition 2.3.12)
)

Let
\varphi_{l}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
l=1,\ldots,p
, be locally Lipschitz around
\bar{x}\in X
. Then the function

\phi(\cdot):=\max\{\varphi_{l}(\cdot)\;|\;l=1,\ldots,p\}

is also locally Lipschitz around
\bar{x}
and one has

\partial\phi(\bar{x})\subset\bigcup\bigg\{\sum_{l=1}^{p}\lambda_{l}\partial\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})\;|\;(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+},\sum_{l=1}^{p}\lambda_{l}=1,\lambda_{l}[\varphi_{l}(\bar{x})-\phi(\bar{x})]=0\bigg\}.

3
Optimality conditions

Hereafter we assume that the following assumptions are satisfied:

(i)

T
is a compact topological space;

(ii)

X
is separable; or
T
is metrizable and
\partial g_{t}(x)
is upper semicontinuous
(w^{*})
in
t
for each
x\in X
.

Denote by
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|T|}
the set of all functions
\mu\colon T\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\mu_{t}:=\mu(t)=0
for all
t\in T
except for finitely many points. The active constraint multipliers set of problem (
VP
) at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
is defined by

A(\bar{x}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|T|}\;|\;\mu_{t}g_{t}(\bar{x})=0,\ \ \forall t\in T\right\}.

For each
x\in X
, put
G(x):=\max_{t\in T}g_{t}(x)
and

T(x):=\left\{t\in T\;|\;g_{t}(x)=G(x)\right\}.

Fix
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. The following theorem gives a necessary optimality condition of fuzzy Karush–Kuhn–Tucker type for
\xi
-weakly Pareto solutions of problem (
VP
).

Theorem 3.1

Let
\bar{x}
be a
\xi
-weakly Pareto solution of problem (
VP
). If the following constraint qualification condition holds

\exists\bar{d}\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)\ \ \text{such that}\ \ G^{\circ}(\bar{x};\bar{d})<0,

(
\mathcal{U}
)

then, for any
\delta>0
small enough, there exist
x_{\delta}\in C\cap B(\bar{x},\delta)
and
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
with
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(x_{\delta})+\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathrm{cl\,conv}\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{t}(x_{\delta})\,|\,t\in T(x_{\delta})\right\}+N(x_{\delta};\Omega)+\frac{1}{\delta}\max_{i\in I}\{\xi_{i}\}B_{X^{*}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\big[f_{i}(x_{\delta})-f_{i}(\bar{x})+\xi_{i}-\max_{i\in I}\{f_{i}(x_{\delta})-f_{i}(\bar{x})+\xi_{i}\}\big]=0,\ \ i\in I,

where
\mathrm{cl\,conv}(\cdot)
denotes the closed convex hull with the closure taken in the weak
∗
-topology of the dual space
X^{\ast}
.

Proof

For each
x\in X
, put
\psi(x):=\max_{i\in I}\{f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(\bar{x})+\xi_{i}\}.
Then, we have
\psi(\bar{x})=\max_{i\in I}\{\xi_{i}\}
. Since
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-weakly Pareto solution of problem (
VP
), one has

\psi(x)\geqq 0,\ \ \forall x\in C.

(1)

Clearly,
\psi
is locally Lipschitz and bounded from below on
C
. By (
1
), we have

\psi(\bar{x})\leqq\inf_{x\in C}\psi(x)+\max_{i\in I}\{\xi_{i}\}.

By the Ekeland variational principle
(
Ekeland74
, Theorem 1.1)
, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
x_{\delta}\in C
such that
\|x_{\delta}-\bar{x}\|<\delta
and

\psi(x_{\delta})\leqq\psi(x)+\frac{1}{\delta}\max_{i\in I}\{\xi_{i}\}\|x-x_{\delta}\|,\ \ \forall x\in C.

For each
x\in C
, put

\varphi(x):=\psi(x)+\frac{1}{\delta}\max_{i\in I}\{\xi_{i}\}\|x-x_{\delta}\|.

Then
x_{\delta}
is a minimizer of
\varphi
on
C
. By Lemma
1
, we have

0\in\partial\varphi(x_{\delta})+N(x_{\delta};C).

(2)

By Lemma
2
and
(
Ioffe79
, Example 4, p. 198)
, one has

\partial\varphi(x_{\delta})\subset\partial\psi(x_{\delta})+\frac{1}{\delta}\max_{i\in I}\{\xi_{i}\}B_{X^{*}}.

(3)

Thanks to Lemma
3
, we have

\partial\psi(x_{\delta})\subset\bigg\{\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(x_{\delta})|\lambda_{i}\geqq 0,i\in I,\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}[f_{i}(x_{\delta})-f_{i}(\bar{x})+\xi_{i}-\psi(x_{\delta})]=0\bigg\}.

(4)

We claim that there exists
\bar{\delta}>0
such that for all
x\in B(\bar{x},\bar{\delta})
there is
d\in T(x;\Omega)
satisfying
G^{\circ}(x;d)<0
. Indeed, if otherwise, then there exists a sequence
\{x^{k}\}
converging to
\bar{x}
such that
G^{\circ}(x^{k};d)\geqq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in T(x;\Omega)
. Hence,
G^{\circ}(x^{k};\bar{d})\geqq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By the upper semicontinuity of
G^{\circ}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we have

G^{\circ}(\bar{x};\bar{d})\geqq\limsup_{k\to\infty}G^{\circ}(x^{k};\bar{d})\geqq 0,

contrary to condition (

U

).

By
(
Hiriart-Urruty78
, Theorem 6)
and
(
Son09
, Theorem 2.1)
, for each
\delta\in(0,\bar{\delta})
, we have

\displaystyle N(x_{\delta};C)

\displaystyle\subset N(x_{\delta};\Omega)+\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial G(x_{\delta})

\displaystyle\subset N(x_{\delta};\Omega)+\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathrm{cl\,conv}\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{t}(x_{\delta})\;|\;t\in T(x_{\delta})\right\}.

Combining this with (
2
)–(
4
), we obtain the desired assertion.
\hfill\qed

Remark 3

By using approximate subdifferentials, Lee
et al.

(
Lee12
, Theorem 8.3)
derived some necessary optimality conditions for
\xi
-weakly Pareto solutions of a convex infinite vector optimization problem. However, we are not familiar with any results on optimality conditions for
\xi
-weakly Pareto solutions of a nonconvex problem of type (
VP
). Theorem
3.1
may be the first result of this type. We also note here that when
T
is a finite set, the result in Theorem
3.1
is a corresponding result of Theorem 3.4 of
chuong16
, where the optimality condition was given in terms of the limiting subdifferential.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\bar{x}
be a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of problem (
VP
). If condition (

U

) holds at
\bar{x}
and the convex hull of
\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})\;|\;t\in T(\bar{x})\right\}
is weak
∗
-closed, then, there exist
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
with
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
, and
\mu\in A(\bar{x})
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}B_{X^{*}}+N(\bar{x};\Omega).

(5)

Proof

For each
x\in X
, put
\Phi(x):=\max_{i\in I}\{f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(\bar{x})+\xi_{i}\|x-\bar{x}\|\}.
Then,
\Phi(\bar{x})=0
. Since
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of problem (
VP
), we have

\Phi(x)\geqq 0,\ \ \forall x\in C.

This means that
\bar{x}
is a minimizer of
\Phi
on
C
. By Lemma
1
, one has

0\in\partial\Phi(\bar{x})+N(\bar{x};C).

(6)

By Lemma
3
, we have

\partial\Phi(\bar{x})\subset\left\{\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\left[\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})+\xi_{i}B_{X^{*}}\right]\;|\;\lambda_{i}\geqq 0,\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1\right\}.

(7)

Since condition (

U

) holds at
\bar{x}
and the convex hull of
\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})\;|\;t\in T(\bar{x})\right\}
is weak
∗
-closed, we obtain

\begin{split}N(\bar{x};C)&\subset N(\bar{x};\Omega)+\mathbb{R}_{+}\partial G(\bar{x})\\
&\subset N(\bar{x};\Omega)+\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathrm{conv}\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})\;|\;t\in T(\bar{x})\right\}.\end{split}

(8)

To finish the proof of the theorem, it remains to combine (
6
), (
7
), and (
8
).
\hfill\Box

Remark 4

(i)

When
X
is a finite-dimensional space and the constraint functions
g_{t}\colon X\to\mathbb{R},

t\in T
, are locally Lipschitz with respect to
x
uniformly in
t\in T
, i.e., for each
x\in X
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
and a constant
K>0
such that

|g_{t}(u)-g_{t}(v)|\leqq K\|u-v\|,\ \ \forall u,v\in U\ \ \text{and}\ \ \forall t\in T,

then the set
\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{t}(x)\;|\;t\in T(x)\right\}
is compact. Consequently, its convex hull is always closed.

(ii)

Recently, by using the Chankong–Haimes scalarization scheme (see
Chankong83
), Kim and Son
(
Son18
, Theorem 3.3)
obtained some necessary optimality conditions for
\xi
-quasi Pareto solutions of a locally Lipschitz semi-infinite vector optimization problem. We note here that condition (

U

) is weaker than the following condition (

A
i

) used in
(
Son18
, Theorem 3.3)
:

\exists\bar{d}\in T(\bar{x};\Omega):g_{t}^{\circ}(\bar{x};\bar{d})<0\ \ \forall t\in T(\bar{x})\ \ \text{and}\ \ f_{k}^{\circ}(\bar{x};\bar{d})<0,\ \ \forall k\in I\setminus\{i\}.

(
{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
)

Thus, Theorem
3.2
improves
(
Son18
, Theorem 3.3)
. To illustrate, we consider the following example

Example 1

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be defined by
f(x):=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))
, where

f_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}x^{2}\cos\frac{1}{x},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ x\neq 0,\\
0,\ \ &\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and
f_{2}(x):=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
,
T=[1,2]
, and
g_{t}(x)=tx
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the feasible set of problem (
VP
) is
C=(-\infty,0]
. Let
\bar{x}:=0\in C
. Clearly, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
,
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of (
VP
). It is easy to check that

\partial f_{1}(\bar{x})=[-1,1],\partial f_{2}(\bar{x})=\{0\},\ \ \text{and}\ \ \partial g_{t}(\bar{x})=\{t\},\ \ \ \forall t\in T.

Hence, for each
d\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

f_{1}^{\circ}(\bar{x};d)=|d|,f_{2}^{\circ}(\bar{x};d)=0,\ \ \text{and}\ \ g_{t}^{\circ}(\bar{x};d)=td,\ \ \ \forall t\in T.

Clearly, every
d<0
satisfies condition (

U

). However, for every
i=1,2
, condition (

A
i

) does not hold. Thus Theorem
3.2
can be applied for this example, but not
(
Son18
, Theorem 3.3)
.

The following concept is inspired from
chuong14
.

Definition 3

Let
f:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
and
g_{T}:=(g_{t})_{t\in T}
.

(i)

We say that
(f,g_{T})
is
generalized convex
on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
if, for any
x\in\Omega
,
z_{i}^{*}\in\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, and
x^{*}_{t}\in\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})
,
t\in T
, there exists
\nu\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)
satisfying

\displaystyle f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(\bar{x})\geqq\langle z^{*}_{i},\nu\rangle,\ \ i=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(\bar{x})\geqq\langle x^{*}_{t},\nu\rangle,\ \ t\in T,

and

\langle b^{*},\nu\rangle\leqq\|x-\bar{x}\|,\ \ \forall b^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}.

(ii)

We say that
(f,g_{T})
is
strictly generalized convex
on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
if, for any
x\in\Omega\setminus\{\bar{x}\}
,
z_{i}^{*}\in\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, and
x^{*}_{t}\in\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})
,
t\in T
, there exists
\nu\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)
satisfying

\displaystyle f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(\bar{x})>\langle z^{*}_{i},\nu\rangle,\ \ i=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(\bar{x})\geqq\langle x^{*}_{t},\nu\rangle,\ \ t\in T,

and

\langle b^{*},\nu\rangle\leqq\|x-\bar{x}\|,\ \ \forall b^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}.

Remark 5

Clearly, if
\Omega
is convex and
f_{i}
,
i\in I
, and
g_{t}
,
t\in T
are convex (resp., strictly convex), then
(f,g_{T})
is generalized convex (resp., strictly generalized convex) on
\Omega
at any
\bar{x}\in\Omega
with
\nu:=x-\bar{x}
for each
x\in\Omega
. Furthermore, by a similar argument in
(
chuong14
, Example 3.2)
, we can show that the class of generalized convex functions is
properly larger than the one of convex functions.

Theorem 3.3

Let
\bar{x}\in C
and assume that there exist
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
with
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
, and
\mu\in A(\bar{x})
satisfying (
5
).

(i)

If
(f,g_{T})
is generalized convex on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, then
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of (
VP
).

(ii)

If
(f,g_{T})
is strictly generalized convex on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, then
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi Pareto solution of (
VP
).

Proof

We will follow the proof scheme of
(
chuong16
, Theorem 3.13)
. By (
5
), there exist
z_{i}^{*}\in\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
,
x^{*}_{t}\in\partial g_{t}(\bar{x})
,
t\in T
,
b^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
, and
\omega^{*}\in N(\bar{x};\Omega)
such that

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}z^{*}_{i}+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}x^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}b^{*}+\omega^{*}=0,

or, equivalent,

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}z^{*}_{i}+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}x^{*}_{t}+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}b^{*}=-\omega^{*}.

We first prove (i). On the contrary, if
\bar{x}
is not a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of (
VP
), then there is
x\in C
such that

f(x)+\|x-\bar{x}\|\xi<f(\bar{x}).

From this and the fact that
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
, we obtain

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|x-\bar{x}\|<\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x}).

(9)

Since
(f,g_{T})
is generalized convex on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, for such
x
, there exists
\nu\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle 0\leqq-\langle\omega^{*},\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\langle z^{*}_{i},\nu\rangle+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}\langle x^{*}_{t},\nu\rangle+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\langle b^{*},\nu\rangle

\displaystyle\leqq\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}[f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}[g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|x-\bar{x}\|.

Hence,

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x})\leqq\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}[g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|x-\bar{x}\|.

Combining this with the facts that
x,\bar{x}\in C
and
\mu\in A(\bar{x})
, we conclude that

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x})\leqq\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(x)+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|x-\bar{x}\|,

(10)

contrary to (
9
).

We now prove (ii). Assume on the contrary that
\bar{x}
is not a
\xi
-quasi Pareto solution of (
VP
), i.e., there exists
y\in C
satisfying

f(y)+\|y-\bar{x}\|\xi\leq f(\bar{x}).

This implies that
y\neq\bar{x}
and

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(y)+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|y-\bar{x}\|\leqq\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x}).

(11)

Since
(f,g_{T})
is strictly generalized convex on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, for such
y
, there exists
\vartheta\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle 0\leqq-\langle\omega^{*},\vartheta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\langle z^{*}_{i},\vartheta\rangle+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}\langle x^{*}_{t},\vartheta\rangle+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\langle b^{*},\vartheta\rangle

\displaystyle<\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}[f_{i}(y)-f_{i}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{t\in T}\mu_{t}[g_{t}(y)-g_{t}(\bar{x})]+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|y-\bar{x}\|.

An analysis similar to that in the proof of (
10
) shows that

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x})<\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}f_{i}(y)+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}\|y-\bar{x}\|,

contrary to (
11
).
\hfill\Box

Remark 6

The conclusions of Theorem
3.3
are still valid if
(f,g_{T})
is generalized convex in the sense of Chuong and Kim
(
chuong14
, Definition 3.3)
.

We now present an example which demonstrates the importance
of the generalized convexity of
(f,g_{T})
in Theorem
3.3
. In particular, condition (
5
) alone is not sufficient to guarantee that
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of (
VP
) if the generalized convexity of
(f,g_{T})
on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is violated.

Example 2

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be defined by
f(x):=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))
, where

f_{i}(x):=\begin{cases}x^{2}\cos\frac{1}{x},\ \ &\text{if}\ \ x\neq 0,\\
0,\ \ &\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
i=1,2
. Assume that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
,
T=[0,1]
, and
g_{t}(x)=x-t
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\in[0,1]
. Then, the feasible set of (
VP
) is
C=(-\infty,0]
. Let
\bar{x}:=0\in C
. Clearly,
f_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and
g_{t}
,
t\in T
, are locally Lipschitz at
\bar{x}
. An easy computation shows that

\partial f_{1}(\bar{x})=\partial f_{2}(\bar{x})=[-1,1],\ \ \text{and}\ \ \partial g_{t}(\bar{x})=\{1\},\ \ \ \forall t\in T.

Take arbitrarily
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
\xi_{i}<\frac{1}{\pi}
for all
i=1,2
. Then we see that
\bar{x}
satisfies condition (
5
) for
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
, and
\mu_{t}=0
for all
t\in T
. However,
\bar{x}
is not a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of (
VP
). Indeed, let
\hat{x}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\in C
. Then,

f_{i}(\hat{x})+\xi_{i}\|\hat{x}-\bar{x}\|=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\xi_{i}-\frac{1}{\pi}\right)<f_{i}(\bar{x}),\ \ \ \forall i=1,2,

as required. We now show that
(f,g_{T})
is not generalized convex on
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
. Indeed, by choosing
z_{i}^{*}=0\in\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})
for
i=1,2
, we have

f_{i}(\hat{x})-f_{i}(\bar{x})=-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}<\langle z^{*}_{i},\nu\rangle,\ \ \ \forall\nu\in\mathbb{R}.

4
Applications

4.1
Cone-constrained convex vector optimization problems

In this subsection, we consider the following cone-constrained convex vector optimization problem:

\begin{split}&\text{\rm Min}_{\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\\
&\text{s.t.}\ \ x\in C:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g(x)\in-K\},\end{split}

(CCVP)

where the function
f
and the set
\Omega
are as in the previous sections,
K
is a closed convex cone in a normed space
Y
, and
g
is a continuous and
K
-convex mapping from
X
to
Y
. Recall that the mapping
g
is said to be
K
-convex if

g[\theta x+(1-\theta)y]-\theta g(x)-(1-\theta)g(y)\in-K,\ \ \forall x,y\in X,\ \ \forall\theta\in[0,1].

Let
Y^{\ast}
be the dual space of
Y
and
K^{+}
be the positive polar cone of
K
, i.e.,

K^{+}:=\{y^{\ast}\in Y^{\ast}\mid\langle y^{\ast},k\rangle\geqq 0,\ \ \forall k\in K\}.

Then,
K^{+}
is weak
∗
-closed. Moreover, it is easily seen that

g(x)\in-K\Leftrightarrow g_{s}(x)\leqq 0,\ \ \forall s\in K^{+},

where
g_{s}(x):=\langle s,g(x)\rangle
.
Hence, problem (
CCVP
) is equivalent to the following vector optimization problem:

\begin{split}&\text{\rm Min}_{\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\\
&\text{s.t.}\ \ x\in C:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g_{s}(x)\leqq 0,\ \ s\in K^{+}\}.\end{split}

(12)

In order to apply the results in Section
3
to problem (
12
), we need to have a compact set of indices, which is not the case with the cone
K^{+}
. However, since
Y
is a normed space, it is easily seen that the set
K^{+}\cap B_{Y^{\ast}}
is weak
∗
-compact and

g(x)\in-K\Leftrightarrow g_{s}(x)\leqq 0,\ \ \forall s\in K^{+}\cap B_{Y^{\ast}}.

Hence we can rewrite problem (
12
) as

\begin{split}&\text{\rm Min}_{\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\\
&\text{s.t.}\ \ x\in C:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g_{s}(x)\leqq 0,\ \ s\in{T}\},\end{split}

where
{T}:=K^{+}\cap B_{Y^{\ast}}.

Proposition 3

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and
\bar{x}
be a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of problem (
CCVP
). If condition (

U

) holds at
\bar{x}
and the convex hull
{\rm co}\left\{\bigcup\partial g_{s}(\bar{x})\;|\;s\in{I}(\bar{x})\right\}
is weak
∗
-closed, then, there exist
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
with
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
, and
\zeta\in K^{+}
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})+\partial g_{\zeta}(\bar{x})+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}B_{X^{*}}+N(\bar{x};\Omega).

(13)

Proof

By Theorem
3.2
, there exist
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
with
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
, and
\mu\in{A}(\bar{x})
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{T}}\mu_{s}\partial g_{s}(\bar{x})+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}B_{X^{*}}+N(\bar{x};\Omega).

Note that for each
s\in{T}
, the function
g_{s}
is continuous and convex on
X
. Moreover, since
\mu\in{A}(\bar{x})
, there exists only finitely many
\mu_{s},s\in{T},
differ from zero. Hence,

\sum_{s\in{T}}\mu_{s}\partial g_{s}(\bar{x})=\partial\left(\left\langle\sum_{s\in{T}}\mu_{s}s,g\right\rangle\right)(\bar{x})=\partial g_{\zeta}(\bar{x}),

where
\zeta:=\sum_{s\in{T}}\mu_{s}s
. Clearly,
\zeta\in K^{+}
. The proof is completed.
\hfill\Box

Remark 7

Assume that
\Omega
is convex. Since
g
is continuous and
K
-convex, we see that if
f_{i},i\in I
, are convex (resp., strictly convex), then
(f,g_{T})
is generalized convex (resp., strictly generalized convex) on
\Omega
at any
\bar{x}\in\Omega
with
\nu:=x-\bar{x}
for each
x\in\Omega
. Thus, by Theorem
3.3
, (
13
) is a sufficient condition for a point
\bar{x}\in C
to be a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto (resp.,
\xi
-quasi Pareto) solution of (
CCVP
) provided that
f_{i},i\in I
, are convex (resp., strictly convex).

4.2
Semidefinite vector optimization problem

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to S^{p}
be a continuous mapping, where
S^{p}
denotes the set of
p\times p
symmetric matrices. For a
p\times p
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
, the notion
{\rm trace}(A)
is defined by

{\rm trace}(A):=\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{ii}.

We suppose that
S^{p}
is equipped with a scalar product
A\bullet B:={\rm trace}(AB)
, where
AB
is the matrix product of
A
and
B
. A matrix
A\in S^{p}
is said to be a negative semidefinite (resp., positive semidefinite) matrix if
\langle v,Av\rangle\leqq 0
(resp.,
\langle v,Av\rangle\geqq 0
) for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If matrix
A
is negative semidefinite (resp., positive semidefinite matrix), it is denoted by
A\preceq 0
(resp.,
A\succeq 0
).

We now consider the following semidefinite vector optimization problem:

\begin{split}&\text{\rm Min}_{\,\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}}\,f(x):=(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{m}(x))\\
&\text{s.t.}\ \ x\in C:=\{x\in\Omega\;|\;g(x)\preceq 0\}.\end{split}

(SDVP)

Let us denote by
S^{p}_{+}
the set of all positive semidefinite matrices of
S^{p}
. It is well known that
S^{p}_{+}
is a proper convex cone, i.e., it is closed, convex, pointed, and solid (see
Gri
) and that
S^{p}_{+}
is a self-dual cone, i.e.,
(S^{p}_{+})^{+}=S^{p}_{+}
. Hence, problem (
SDVP
) can be rewritten under the form of problem (
CCVP
), where
K:=S^{p}_{+}
. For
\Lambda\in K
, the function
g_{\Lambda}
becomes a function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
defined by

g_{\Lambda}(x)=\Lambda\bullet g(x),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
g
is affine, i.e.,
g(x):=F_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}x_{i}
, where
F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}\in S^{p}
are given matrices, then the subdifferential of the function
g_{\Lambda}(x)
is equal to

\partial(g_{\Lambda})(x)=(\Lambda\bullet F_{1},\ldots,\Lambda\bullet F_{n})=:(\Lambda\bullet F).

In that case, by Remark
4
, the second assumption of Proposition
3
can be removed. Thus we obtain the following result.

Proposition 4

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. If
\bar{x}
is a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto solution of problem (
SDVP
), then, there exist
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
with
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
, and
\Lambda\in S^{p}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})+\Lambda\bullet F+\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\xi_{i}B_{X^{*}}+N(\bar{x};\Omega).

(14)

Remark 8

Since
g
is an affine mapping, by Theorem
3.3
, (
14
) is a sufficient condition for a point
\bar{x}\in C
to be a
\xi
-quasi-weakly Pareto (resp.,
\xi
-quasi Pareto) solution of (
SDVP
) provided that
f_{i},i\in I
, are convex (resp., strictly convex).

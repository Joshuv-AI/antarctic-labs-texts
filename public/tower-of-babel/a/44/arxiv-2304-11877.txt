A note on representation-finite symmetric algebras
Karin Erdmann

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Tame symmetric algebras of period four occur as 2-blocks of group algebras with
quaternion defect groups, and algebras of quaternion type
[
4
]
, and in much more generality, as weighted surface algebras and their
virtual mutations and some other related algebras (
[
6
]
(and
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
10
]
).
In
[
7
]
the main result is the classification of tame symmetric algebras of period four whose Gabriel quiver is 2-regular, and
there is work in progress towards a more general classification.

When the Gabriel quiver has few vertices, representation-finite algebras can occur and it is necessary to clarify when this happens.
This note gives a characterisation, which will be used in
[
5
]
. Furthermore we describe the basic algebras explicitly,
this is based on existing literature on representation-finite selfinjective algebras.

Recall that the syzygy
\Omega(M)
of a module
M
is the kernel
of a minimal projective cover of
M
. The module
M
is periodic if

\Omega^{n}(M)\cong M
for some
n\geq 1
, and if so, the minimal such
n

is the period of
M
. The algebra
\Lambda
is periodic if
\Lambda
is periodic as
a bimodule, or equivalently as a module for the enveloping algebra.
It was proved in
[
3
]
that all selfinjective representation-finite algebras
are periodic. For general background we refer to
[
2
]
.

2.
A characterisation

We fix an algebraically closed field
K
. Since the properties we will study are invariant under Morita equivalence,
we can work with basic algebras, of the form
KQ/I
where
Q
is a finite quiver which we can take to be connected, and
I
is an admissible ideal in
the path algebra
KQ
.

For a vertex
i
of
Q
, we write
i^{-}
for the set of arrows ending at
i
,
and
i^{+}
for the set of arrows starting at
i
. We work with right
modules. We denote by
P_{i}
the indecomposable projective module corresponding
to the vertex
i
, and by
S_{i}
its simple quotient.

We assume each simple module
S_{i}
has

\Omega
-period diving
4
. Then
there is an exact sequence

0\to S_{i}\to P_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{3}}}{{\to}}P_{i}^{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{2}}}{{\to}}P_{i}^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{1}}}{{\to}}P_{i}\to S_{i}\to 0

with
{\rm Im}(d_{k})\cong\Omega^{k}(S_{i})
for
1\leq k\leq 3
.
Here
P_{i}^{+}=\oplus_{\alpha:i\to j}P_{j}
and
P_{i}^{-}=\oplus_{\gamma:k\to i}P_{k}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be an indecomposable basic symmetric algebra such that all indecomposable non-projective modules have period dividing
4
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a) There is a simple module
S
such that
\Omega^{2}(S)
is simple.

(b) The quiver of
\Lambda
has an arrow
\alpha:i\to j
such that
i^{+}=\{\alpha\}=j^{-}
.

(c) There is a vertex
i
of the quiver with
|P_{i}|=|P_{i}^{+}|=|P_{i}^{-}|
where
|-|
denotes dimension.

If these hold then
\Lambda
has finite type.

Proof

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b)
Assume
\Omega^{2}(S_{i})\cong S_{j}.
Then in the exact sequence for
S_{i}
we have
P_{j}\cong P_{i}^{+}
, so that there is a unique arrow
\alpha
say, starting at
i
and it ends at
j
.
Since
S_{i}
has period four,
\Omega^{2}(S_{j})\cong\Omega^{4}(S_{i})\cong S_{i}
. Consider the exact sequence for
S_{j}
, the module
P_{j}^{-}
of this sequence is isomorphic to
P_{i}
. That is
there is a unique arrow in
Q
ending at
j
, and this must be
\alpha
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a) If (b) holds then
\Omega(S_{i})\cong\alpha\Lambda\cong\Omega^{-1}(S_{j})
and
\Omega^{2}(S_{i})\cong S_{j}
.

(c)
\Leftrightarrow
(a) Taking dimensions in the exact sequence of
S_{i}
we have

|\Omega^{2}(S_{i})|+|P_{i}|=|P_{i}^{+}|+|S_{i}|=|P_{i}^{-}|+|S_{i}|

Hence
|\Omega^{2}(S_{i})|=1
if and only if
|P_{i}^{+}|=|P_{i}^{-}|=|P_{i}|
.

Any of the conditions above implies
\Lambda
has finite type:
Suppose
\Omega^{2}(S_{i})\cong S_{j}
for
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
simple, then also
\Omega^{2}(S_{j})\cong S_{i}
. By part (b), the quiver has a connected component
either a vertex with a loop, or two vertices with a pair of arrows. This must
be all of
Q
since the algebra is indecomposable. Any finite-dimensional algebra with such a quiver is a Nakayama algebra and is of finite type (see for example

[
11
]
Vol I, Ch I.10.3, or see in the proof of the following Lemma).

\Box

3.
The basic algebras

We describe now which symmetric algebras
of finite type have only indecomposable
modules of
\Omega
-period dividing four.
The algebras appearing in the previous Lemma appear in (i) and (ii), and there
is only one other family of algebras.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\Lambda
is symmetric indecomposable of finite type such that
all indecomposable non-projective
modules have
\Omega
-period dividing
4
. Then
\Lambda
is one of the following.

(i)
\Lambda\cong K[T]/(T^{n})
for some
n\geq 1
.

(ii)
\Lambda\cong KQ/I
where
Q
is the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}

and
I=\langle(\alpha\beta)^{n}\alpha,(\beta\alpha)^{n}\beta\rangle
for some
n\geq 1
.

(iii)
\Lambda=KQ/I
where
Q

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}

and

I=\langle\rho\alpha,\beta\rho,\alpha\beta-\rho^{t}\rangle
for some

t\geq 2
.

Proof
If
\Lambda
is a local algebra of finite type over an algebraically closed field then
\Lambda
is as in part (i), by elementary algebra.
We assume now that
\Lambda
is not local.

Let
\Gamma_{s}
be the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\Lambda
. By work of Riedtmann, this is isomorphic to
a quotient of
\mathbb{Z}\Delta
where
\Delta
is a Dynkin diagram
of type
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{D},\mathbb{E}
. We start by excluding
types
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{E}
.

By
[
3
]
, all selfinjective algebras of finite type are
periodic as algebras; the table 5.2 of
[
3
]
lists the (possible) periods, in terms of the types as introduced in
[
1
]
:

The algebra has
type

(\Delta,f,t)
if
the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma_{s}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\Delta/\langle g\rangle

where
g
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}\Delta
of the form
g=\zeta\tau^{-r}
, with
\zeta
an automorphism of order
t
, and
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Furthermore,

f=\frac{r}{m_{\Delta}}
where
m_{\Delta}
is
n,2n-3,11,17
or
29
when
\Delta
is
\mathbb{A}_{n},\mathbb{D}_{n}
,
\mathbb{E}_{6},\mathbb{E}_{7}
or
\mathbb{E}_{8}
respectively.
The algebra has
s
simple modules where
s=nf
.

Consider the types when
\Delta=\mathbb{D}_{n}
. By inspecting the list in 5.2 of
[
3
]

one sees that the period of the algebra
always has a factor

2n-3
. To have period
4
this must be equal to
1
.
But
2n-3=1
means
n=2
which is excluded since
n\geq 4
for
\mathbb{D}_{n}
.
The same arguments shows that algebras for types
\mathbb{D}_{3m},\mathbb{E}_{r}
with

r=6,7,8
and also algebras of type

(\mathbb{A}_{2m+1},s,2)

cannot have period four.

This leaves only the type
(\mathbb{A}_{n},s/n,1)

where the algebra can have period
4=\frac{2s}{(s,n+1)}.
. In this case,

f=r/m_{\Delta}=r/n
but also
f=s/n
and therefore
r=s
.
We assume that the
algebra is symmetric, therefore
\tau\cong\Omega^{2}
. We have period
4
, therefore
r=2
and hence
s=2
.
Hence the quiver of the algebra has only two vertices and is connected.

To finish the proof, we will use the following properties for a finite-dimensional symmetric algebra
\Lambda
.

(a) The quiver does not have a sink or a source.

(b) For vertices
i\neq j
of
Q
, we have
{\rm soc}(e_{i}\Lambda)\cap e_{i}\Lambda e_{j}=0
.

(c) If
0\neq\omega
is a monomial in
KQ
and belongs to the socle of
\Lambda
then it corresponds to
a cyclic path, and all rotations of
\omega
are non-zero and belong to the socle of
\Lambda
.

By part (a) the quiver
Q
must contain
the quiver given in (ii).
The only other possibility is the quiver given in (iii).Namely
Q
cannot have double arrows (this would lead to the a quotient
which is a Kronecker algebra of infinite type). There cannot be another loop
since then the separated quiver of the algebra would have infinite type.

We will now determine the ideal
I
in the two cases.

In case (ii), using property (b) we see that the socle of
e_{1}\Lambda
is spanned by some
(\alpha\beta)^{n}
and then by property (c),
the monomial
(\beta\alpha)^{n}
spans the socle of
e_{2}\Lambda
. It follows that the ideal
I
is as stated (see also for example

[
11
]
vol I Ch I.10.3).

Consider now
Q
as in (iii).
First observe that in this case the simple modules have
\Omega
-period
4
, to see this we can apply the Lemma
(which shows that the algebra is as in (ii)).

Let
H:={\rm rad}(e_{2}\Lambda)/{\rm soc}(e_{2}\Lambda)=\beta\Lambda/{\rm soc}(e_{2}\Lambda)

We will show using the Auslander-Reiten quiver that
H
is simple, isomorphic to
S_{1}
.
We label the rows ot
\Gamma_{s}
by
1,2,\ldots,n
. Note that
M
is in row
k
if and only if
\Omega(M)
is in row
n-k+1
.

Recall that there is an almost split sequence

0\to\Omega(S_{2})\to H\oplus P_{2}\to\Omega^{-1}(S_{2})\to 0

Since
H
is indecomposable,
\Omega(S_{2})
has a unique successor in
\Gamma_{s}
, and then also
S_{2}

has a unique successor and therefore belongs to row
1
or to row
n
.
Say
S_{2}
is in row
1
and then
\Omega(S_{2})
is in row

n
.

We know
\Omega^{2}(S_{2})
is not simple, therefore is no other projective
attached to row
n
.
Assume (for a contradiction) that
H
is not simple. The modules in row
n-1
are

H
and
\Omega^{2}(H)
and
\Omega^{2}(H)
is the middle term of the other almost
split sequence with end terms
\Omega^{\pm}(S_{2})
and hence is not simple.

With the assumption, both modules in row
n-1
are not simple.
Then the modules in row
2
, ie
\Omega^{\pm}(H)
, are not
isomorphic to
\Omega^{\pm}(S_{1})
and there is no projective
attached to row
2
.

Now it follows
by using the exactness of almost split sequences, and also the fact that irreducible maps are injective or surjective, that the lengths of modules
in rows
2,3,\ldots
are strictly increasing. But then there is no space for the simple module
S_{1}
, a contradiction.

Therefore
H
must be simple isomorphic to
S_{1}
. The socle of
e_{2}\Lambda
is then spanned by
\beta\alpha
, and by property (b) we have that
\beta\rho=0
..
Property (c) shows that the socle of
e_{1}\Lambda
is spanned by
\alpha\beta
. It is also spanned by
\rho^{t}
for some
t\geq 2
, and
we may assume
\alpha\beta=\rho^{t}
, after possibly rescaling an arrow.
Furthermore
\rho\alpha\beta=\rho^{t+1}=0
and therefore
\rho\alpha\in{\rm soc}(e_{1}\Lambda)\cap e_{1}\Lambda e_{2}=0
.
These imply that
I
is of the stated form.

\Box

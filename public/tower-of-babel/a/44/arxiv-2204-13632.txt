Self-dual C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) vortex-like solitons in the presence of magnetic impurities
V. Almeida, R. Casana, E. da Hora, S. Krusch

Abstract
We investigate the existence of vortex configurations in two gauged- C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) models extended via the inclusion of magnetic impurities. In particular, we consider both the Maxwell- C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) and the Chern-Simons- C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) enlarged scenarios, separately. We choose a C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) -field configuration with a null topological charge not only in the simplest (free) case, but also when coupled to an Abelian gauge field. The implementation of the Bogomol’nyi-Prasad-Sommerfield (BPS) formalism shows that the effective models for such a configuration possess a self-dual structure which looks like those inherent to the gauged sigma models. Therefore, when the C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) field is coupled to the Maxwell term, the corresponding total energy possesses both a well-defined Bogomol’nyi bound and a quantized magnetic flux. Further, when the C ​ P ​ ( 2 ) CP(2) scenario is gauged with the Chern-Simons action, the total electric charge is verified to be proportional to the quantized magnetic flux. In addition, the analysis verifies that the magnetic impurity contributes to the BPS potentials and appears in both the models’ BPS equations. Next, we introduce a Gaussian type impurity and solve the self-dual equations via a finite-difference scheme. The resulting solutions present a nonmonotonic behavior that flips both the magnetic and electric fields. Finally, we discuss the topologically trivial solutions in the limit for which the impurity becomes a Dirac δ \delta -function.

Introduction

Configurations with nontrivial topology are usually achieved as the
solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equations which appear in the context of
nonlinear field theories
n5
. In this case, the nonlinearity originates from a potential term that promotes the spontaneous breaking
of the original model’s symmetry. However, the resulting second-order
Euler-Lagrange equations are highly nonlinear and usually quite hard to
solve.

Under exceptional circumstances, topological solutions can also be
obtained via a particular set of first-order differential equations, the
so-called Bogomol’nyi-Prasad-Sommerfield (BPS) ones. It is interesting to
note that these equations emerge as a result of the implementation of the
Bogomol’nyi technique, which stands for the minimization of the total energy
inherent to the field model
n4
. Among the algorithms which lead to
the BPS equations, we can include the study of the conservation of the
corresponding energy-momentum tensor
ano
and the On-Shell method
onshell
. In such a scenario, the simplest BPS gauged vortices occur in the
Maxwell-Higgs
n1
, Chern-Simons-Higgs
cshv
, and
Maxwell-Chern-Simons-Higgs
mcshv
theories.

More recently, it was shown that first-order or BPS vortices also exist
in a gauged scenario that describes the interaction between the Maxwell and
the
CP(2)
fields
loginov
;
casana
. Besides that, some of us have
also studied the existence of BPS vortices in the Chern-Simons-

where the upper (lower) sign holds for negative (positive) values of the
winding number
m
.

Therefore, from the Eq. (
48
), it is possible to note that the total
energy of the system satisfies the inequality

\mathcal{E}\geq\mathcal{E}_{bps}\text{,}

(50)

with the lower bound attained when the fields satisfy the so called BPS
equations, i.e.

\displaystyle\displaystyle B=\pm gh\left(\cos\alpha\pm\frac{\Delta}{gh}\right)\text{,}

(51)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dr}=\mp\frac{(2m-A)}{2r}\sin\alpha\text{,}

(52)

whose solutions are rotationally symmetric structures with total energy
equal to

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{bps}=4\pi h\left|m\right|\text{.}

(53)

Note that the value in (
53
) is quantized according to the winding
number
m
, such as expected for topological configurations. We also
highlight that the Bogomol’nyi bound is not affected by the presence of the
magnetic impurity.

In the BPS limit, the energy density (
43
) can be rewritten in the form

\varepsilon_{bps}=\left(\sqrt{2U}\mp\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}+2h\left(\frac{d\alpha}{dr}\right)^{2}\text{,}

(54)

where
U
stands for the BPS potential in the Eq. (
47
).

The potential can also be written as a function of
\phi_{3}
and
\Delta
,

U\left(\phi_{3},\Delta\right)=\frac{g^{2}h}{2}\left(\phi_{3}\pm\frac{\Delta}{g\sqrt{h}}\right)^{2}\text{,}

(55)

which spontaneously breaks the
SU(3)
symmetry inherent to the model (
36
), as expected. Also, the expression in (
55
) reveals that the
presence of the magnetic impurity in the original Lagrangian density (
36
) requires an adjustment on the potential (in comparison to the model

without
the impurity) to support the existence of first-order
configurations.

In the next Section, we consider a localized magnetic impurity of the
Gaussian-type, from which we solve the BPS equations (
51
) and (
52
) numerically by means of a finite-difference scheme according the
boundary conditions (
41
) and (
42
).

The effective Chern-Simons-

We now present the model which describes the interaction between the
CP(2)
field and the Chern-Simons Abelian gauge one. The model is
defined by the Lagrange density

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{\kappa}{4}\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}A_{\alpha}F_{\mu\nu}+\left|\nabla_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}-V_{0}\left(\phi_{3}\right)\text{,}

(68)

where the Chern-Simons term controls the gauge field’s dynamics, while
the parameter
\kappa
stands for the respective coupling constant. The
basic definitions, conventions and correlated discussions introduced in the
previous Sec.
II
remain the same.

For the configuration (
1

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{4}\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}A_{\alpha}F_{\mu\nu}+\left|D_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}

(69)

\displaystyle-V_{0}\left(\phi_{3}\right)-\lambda(h-\phi^{{\dagger}}\phi)\text{,}

We are interested in the BPS structure which arises from the model above. In view of the Gauss law

\kappa B=-g^{2}A_{0}\left|\psi\right|^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{2}A_{0}\left(h-\phi_{3}^{2}\right)\text{,}

(70)

the energy density takes the form

\varepsilon=\frac{\kappa^{2}B^{2}}{g^{2}\left(h-\phi_{3}^{2}\right)}+\left(D_{k}\phi\right)^{{\dagger}}D_{k}\phi+V_{0}\text{.}

(71)

After some algebraic manipulations, the total energy of the effective
system becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\mathbf{x}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left|D_{j}\phi\pm i\epsilon_{jk}h^{-1/2}\left(\phi\times D_{k}\phi\right)^{\ast}\right|^{2}\right.

(72)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\left(\frac{\kappa B}{g\left(h-\phi_{3}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\mp\sqrt{V_{0}}\right)^{2}\pm 2\pi h\bar{q}_{0}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left.\pm B\left(\frac{2\kappa\sqrt{V_{0}}}{g\left(h-\phi_{3}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}-h^{1/2}g\phi_{3}\right)\right\}\text{,}\quad\quad

where
\bar{q}_{0}
is the topological charge density defined in the
previous Eq. (
27
). Again in this case, whether we choose the factor which multiplies the magnetic field as being equal to zero,
we determine the BPS potential of the model (
69
), i.e.

V_{0}=\frac{hg^{2}}{4\kappa^{2}}\phi_{3}^{2}(h-\phi_{3}^{2})\text{.}

(73)

via which we complete the implementation of the BPS formalism for the
model (
69
) by writing the total energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{bps}+\int d^{2}\mathbf{x}\left(\frac{\kappa B}{g\left(h-\phi_{3}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\mp\sqrt{V_{0}}\right)^{2}

(74)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}\mathbf{x}\left|D_{j}\phi\pm ih^{-1/2}\epsilon_{jk}\left(\phi\times D_{k}\phi\right)^{\ast}\right|^{2}\!\!\text{,}\quad

where
\mathcal{E}_{bps}
is the same one already defined in the Eq. (

We see that total energy becomes equal to
\mathcal{E}_{bps}

We now consider a second enlarged model which describes the interaction

{\mathcal{L}}=-\frac{\kappa}{4}\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu}A_{\alpha}F_{\mu\nu}+\left|D_{\mu}\phi\right|^{2}-V\left(\phi_{3},\Delta\right)+\Delta B.

Also in this Section, our study focuses on those time-independent

38
) and (
39

and

Behavior of the solutions near the origin

We now investigate the way the profile fields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha(r)\approx\mathcal{C}_{0}r^{m}\text{,}

\displaystyle\displaystyle A(r)\approx\frac{\mathcal{C}_{0}^{2}g^{3}h\left(hg-c\right)}{4\kappa^{2}\left(m+1\right)}r^{2(m+1)}\text{,}

VI
Final comments and perspectives

We have performed the construction of BPS vortices in the context of two
different gauged-
CP(2)
scenarios that were enlarged via an additional
term which represents a magnetic impurity. With such an aim in mind, we
have chosen a specific
CP(2)
-configuration (
1
) as being coupled
to both the Maxwell’s as the Chern-Simons fields, separately. Here, it is
worthwhile to highlight that such a configuration presents a
CP(2)

topological charge which equals zero not only in the simplest (free)
scenario, but also when the
CP(2)
field is coupled to an Abelian
gauge one. However, the BPS formalism shows that the effective models for (
1
) possess a self-dual structure which looks like that of the
gauged sigma models. Moreover, the full implementation of the BPS
technique allows us to fix the self-dual potentials in both the Maxwell
and the Chern-Simons cases, see the eqs. (
47
) and (
83
),
respectively. In this sense, we have verified that the magnetic impurity
contributes explicitly to the self-dual potentials and appears in both
models’ BPS equations. The interesting point is that the impurity does not
change the Bogomol’nyi bounds saturated by the BPS configurations, i.e. the
corresponding self-dual energies remain quantized according to the winding
number
m
, as expected for topological structures.

In order to study the effects caused by the magnetic impurity on the
solutions of the BPS systems, we have particularized our analysis by
choosing a Gaussian (localized) impurity controlled by two real parameters,
c
and
d
(which control the “height” and the
“width” of the impurity, respectively). For a fixed value
of
d
and different values of
c
, the numerical analysis has demonstrated
how the parameter
c
induces not only the loss of monotonicity of the profile functions
\alpha(r)
and
A(r)
, but also the flipping of both
the magnetic and electric fields. The analysis of the behavior near the
origin has explained both peculiarities. In addition, we have verified
that the impurity does not change the manner these fields behave in the
asymptotic region (i.e.
r\rightarrow\infty
).

Based on the results which we have introduced in this work, an interesting
issue to be studied in the future is the effect eventually caused by a
localized impurity on the shape of nontopological BPS Chern-Simons-
CP(2)

vortices. Another point which claims for a future analysis includes the
study of the interaction between a moving gauged-
CP(2)
vortex and a static
magnetic impurity. The results of these topics, currently under
investigation, will be reported elsewhere.

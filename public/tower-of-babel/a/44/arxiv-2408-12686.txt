Analytic amplitudes for a pair of Higgs bosons in association with three partons
John M. Campbell, Giuseppe De Laurentis, R. Keith Ellis

Abstract
The pair production of Higgs bosons at the LHC can give information about the triple Higgs boson coupling. We perform an analytic one-loop calculation of the amplitudes for a pair of Higgs bosons in association with three partons, retaining the exact dependence on the quark mass circulating in the loop. These amplitudes constitute the real radiation corrections in the calculation of Higgs boson pair production at next-to-leading order in the strong coupling. The results of an analytic generalised-unitarity computation are simplified via analytic reconstruction in spinor variables. Compact ansätze for kinematic pole residues are iteratively fitted via p p -adic evaluations near said poles and subtracted until no pole remains. A new ansatz construction is introduced to minimally parametrise coefficients of amplitudes with multiple massive external legs. The simplified expressions are faster to evaluate than automatic codes and can lead to more stable results near singular regions.

3.1
Spinor variables subject to additional constraints

In the first instance, the massive five-point process under
consideration can be embedded into a seven-point massless

S_{7}=\mathbb{F}\big[|1\rangle,[1|,\dots|7\rangle,[7|\big]\,.

(18)

Legs

To account for equivalence relations, such as momentum conservation

3.2
Minimal spinor ansatz for an arbitrary number of massive scalars

Minimal ansätze for the analytic reconstruction of an
n

Higgs production in association with jets

, can be
constructed by considering an
(n-1)
-point process without momentum
conservation. This amounts to replacing every occurrence of the
massive four-momentum with a sum over all the massless ones. However,
this is no longer possible in the presence of multiple massive lines,
since removing a massive four-momentum causes the introduction of
another one.

Since the rings of eq. (
18
) and eq. (
19
)
over-parametrise the space of the
pp\rightarrow HHj
coefficients,
to construct a minimal ansatz, we need to consider the covariant ring
without fictitious decays,

\mathbb{S}_{5}=\mathbb{F}\big[|1\rangle,[1|,|2\rangle,[2|,|3\rangle,[3|,{\bm{4}},{\bm{5}}\big]\,,

(29)

where the bold numbers denote rank two spinors,
{\bm{4}}={\bm{4}}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
and
{\bm{5}}={\bm{5}}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
. The
relation between the seven massless legs of eq. (
18
) and
three massless plus two massive ones of
eq. (
29
) is,

1\rightarrow 1,\,2\rightarrow 2,\,3\rightarrow 3,\,\mathbb{4}\rightarrow 4+5,\,\mathbb{5}\rightarrow 6+7\,.

(30)

Because the external massive particles are scalars, the rank-two
spinors never appear decomposed as sums over two pairs of rank-one
spinors,
\mathbb{p}=\sum_{I}|\mathbb{p}^{I}\rangle[\mathbb{p}_{I}|
. In fact,
this would be equivalent to the seven-point space of
eqs. (
18
) and (
19
). It introduces 8 spinor
components in lieu of 4.

The four, four-momentum conservation equations read

J_{\text{mom.~cons.}}=\Big\langle|1\rangle[1|+|2\rangle[2|+|3\rangle[3|+{\bm{4}}+{\bm{5}}\Big\rangle\,.

(31)

We further impose the constraint that
m_{{\bm{4}}}=m_{{\bm{5}}}
. In terms
of the ring variables, the masses can be written as

\operatorname*{tr}({\bm{4}}|{\bm{4}})=2m^{2}_{{\bm{4}}}
, or
\text{det}({\bm{4}})=m^{2}_{{\bm{4}}}
,
and equivalently for
{\bm{5}}
. These are to be understood as
polynomials in the four components of the rank-two spinors.

The quotient ring is then,

\mathbb{R}_{5}=\mathbb{S}_{5}/(J_{\text{mom.~cons.}}+\left\langle\text{det}({\bm{4}})-\text{det}({\bm{5}})\right\rangle)\,.

(32)

Since amplitudes are Lorentz invariant, we now need to convert to an
invariant ring following the elimination algorithm of ref.
(
DeLaurentis:2022otd
, Section
2)
. However, the standard spinor brackets,

\langle ij\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad[ij]\,,

(33)

are no longer sufficient, since

can only be in
\{1,2,3\}
. In
addition, we must consider contractions involving the rank-two spinors

{\bm{4}}
and
{\bm{5}}
, both with the rank-one spinors and among
themselves. The former case reads,

\langle i|\mathbb{k}|i]\quad\text{and}\quad\langle i|\mathbb{k}|j]\,,

(34)

while the latter case
requires the introduction of traces, now considered as variables in
their own right (i.e. no longer as polynomials in the components),

\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{l})\,.

(35)

with
\mathbb{k},\mathbb{l}
in
\{{\bm{4}},{\bm{5}}\}
. Since we work with
2-component spinors, these are understood as traces of
2\times 2

matrices, explicitly

\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{l})=\mathbb{k}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{l}^{\dot{\alpha}\alpha}

In terms of Mandelstam invariants and

\operatorname*{tr}({\bm{4}}|{\bm{4}})=2\,M_{H}^{2}
,
\operatorname*{tr}({\bm{4}}|{\bm{5}})=s_{123}-2\,M_{H}^{2}

The invariants of eq. (

\mathcal{S}\kern-7.05626pt\mathcal{S}_{5}=\mathbb{F}\big[\langle ij\rangle,[ij],\langle i|\mathbb{k}|i],\langle i|\mathbb{k}|j],\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{k}),\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{l})\big]\,.

Once again these variables are subject to equivalence relations, thus

\mathcal{R}\kern-8.25128pt\mathcal{R}_{5}

We can obtain all these relations among Lorentz invariant spinor

\mathbb{S}_{5}
and

\mathcal{S}\kern-7.05626pt\mathcal{S}_{5}

\bm{\Sigma}_{5}=\mathbb{F}\big[|1\rangle,[1|,|2\rangle,[2|,|3\rangle,[3|,{\bm{4}},{\bm{5}},\langle ij\rangle,[ij],\langle i|\mathbb{k}|i],\langle i|\mathbb{k}|j],\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{k}),\operatorname*{tr}(\mathbb{k}|\mathbb{l})\big]\,.

(37)

In this extended ring, we
consider the ideal

\displaystyle\bm{\kappa}[J_{\text{mom.~cons.}}+\left\langle\text{det}({\bm{4}})-\text{det}({\bm{5}})\right\rangle]=

\displaystyle\;\Big\langle|1\rangle[1|+|2\rangle[2|+|3\rangle[3|+{\bm{4}}+{\bm{5}},

\displaystyle\qquad\text{det}({\bm{4}})-\text{det}({\bm{5}}),\langle 12\rangle-(\lambda_{1,0}\lambda_{2,1}-\lambda_{2,0}\lambda_{1,1},\,\dots)\Big\rangle\,,

(38)

\langle 12\rangle
bracket in terms of the components of the spinors

|1\rangle=(\lambda_{1,0},\lambda_{1,1})
and
|2\rangle=(\lambda_{2,0},\lambda_{2,1})

The invariant quotient ring,
\mathcal{R}\kern-8.25128pt\mathcal{R}_{5}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\kern-8.25128pt\mathcal{R}_{5}=\mathcal{S}\kern-7.05626pt\mathcal{S}_{5}/\big(\bm{\kappa}[J_{\text{mom.~cons.}}+\left\langle\text{det}({\bm{4}})-\text{det}({\bm{5}})\right\rangle]\cap\mathcal{S}\kern-7.05626pt\mathcal{S}_{5}\big)\,,

(39)

\bm{\kappa}
of

\bm{\Sigma}_{5}

\langle ij\rangle\langle kl\rangle+\langle ik\rangle\langle lj\rangle+\langle il\rangle\langle jk\rangle=0\,,

\left\langle j\,k\right\rangle\left\langle i|\mathbb{l}|k\right]-\left\langle i\,k\right\rangle\left\langle j|\mathbb{l}|k\right]+\left\langle i\,j\right\rangle\left\langle k|\mathbb{l}|k\right]=0\,.

3.3
Projective space in
\bm{m}

Besides the kinematic variables discussed so far, the mass of the
quark running in the loop also appears in the amplitude. The
polynomial ring is thus,

\mathbb{S}_{5}[m]=\mathbb{F}\big[|1\rangle,[1|,|2\rangle,[2|,|3\rangle,[3|,{\bm{4}},{\bm{5}},m\big]\,.

(42)

The quotient ring construction is unchanged, since no equivalence
relation involves
m
, so we simply have the covariant quotient ring

\mathbb{R}_{5}[m]
and the invariant one

\mathcal{R}\kern-8.25128pt\mathcal{R}_{5}[m]
.

It is clear that
m
has a special role in
\mathbb{R}_{5}[m]
and

\mathcal{R}\kern-8.25128pt\mathcal{R}_{5}[m]
: it is the only variable not
subject to equivalence relations. It is then straightforward to
consider this space as being projective in
m
, since
m
can be taken
to infinity independently. On the other hand, we remain in an affine
space for the spinors, since taking a spinor to infinity would require
another one also being either large (if additive in the equivalence
relation) or small (if multiplicative). That is, we include the point

m\rightarrow\infty
, but not any point at infinity for the spinor
variables. In a projective space at the point at infinity the role of
poles and zeros is inverted, a numerator factor is a pole, while a
denominator factor is a zero.

The box coefficients in the
gggHH
amplitude have a rich analytic
structure in
m
. Since performing four unitarity cuts can result in a
fifth propagator being evaluated on the cut, the kinematic and
m

dependence mix in the denominator. The large and small
m
limits
provide useful information on the general
m
expression, but do not
directly translate to individual terms of a Taylor expansion as in the
case of the triangle coefficients. In Appendix

we investigate how the loop quark mass
dependence affects various rewritings of a box coefficient, especially
in relation to the location of spurious singularities. In particular,
we make the interesting observation that there can be a spurious pole
at
m\rightarrow\infty
. This could provide an even more numerically
efficient way to write the box coefficients, if the size of the
expressions in that form can be contained. In fact, the mass
m
, when
interpreted as the top-quark mass, is large, but never truly
approaches infinity—unlike some kinematic variables that may need to
approach zero. The compact form of the box coefficients that we
present in section
is instead based on an effective
pentagon decomposition, as discussed in section
2.1
.

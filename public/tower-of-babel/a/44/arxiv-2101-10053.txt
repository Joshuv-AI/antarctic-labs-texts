Abstract
Trading frictions are stochastic. They are, moreover, in many instances fast-mean reverting. Here, we study how to optimally trade in a market with stochastic price impact and study approximations to the resulting optimal control problem using singular perturbation methods. We prove, by constructing sub- and super-solutions, that the approximations are accurate to the specified order. Finally, we perform some numerical experiments to illustrate the effect that stochastic trading frictions have on optimal trading.

Introduction

Trading frictions exist across all electronic markets and stem from a multitude of factors. In quote driven markets, liquidity providers place limit orders to buy and sell assets, while liquidity takers use market orders to extract this liquidity, sometimes by walking the limit order book (LOB), but often by picking off available volume at the touch (the volume at the best bid/ask prices). In either case, trading quickly (or with large volume) induces a trading cost while trading slowly introduces price risk. The seminal work of

2.4
Approximation for the optimal feedback control

Under the deterministic temporary price impact, there are two classical trading strategies. Firstly, the approach outlined by Almgren-Chriss (AC), where no trading signal is considered, in our setting becomes

5
Accuracy of the Approximation

In this section we prove the accuracy of the approximation of
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}
by
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. As noted earlier, once the accuracy for
h^{(2),\varepsilon}
is proved, the accuracy for the approximations for
h^{(1),\varepsilon}
and
h^{(0),\varepsilon}
, which are the solutions of the
linear
PDEs (
22
) and (
23
), follow from the usual arguments from
[
17
]
. The proof of accuracy for non-linear PDEs was first developed in
[
13
]
under the different context of portfolio optimization.

Recall that
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the non-linear PDE (
25
):

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}-\phi+\frac{(\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{k(t,y)}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}&=0,\\
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(T,y)&=-\varphi+b/2.\end{array}\right.

The zero-order approximation
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is the solution of Ricatti ODE

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}(t)-\phi+\frac{1}{\kappa(t)}\mathcal{X}^{2}_{0}(t)&=0,\\
\mathcal{X}_{0}(T)&=-\varphi+b/2.\end{array}\right.

To provide precise bounds, we make the following standing assumption for the remainder of this section.

Assumptions 5
.

We assume that

1.

\kappa
is bounded and bounded away from zero with bounded derivative; and

2.

\eta
is bounded, implying that the solutions to all Poisson equations considered below and their derivatives are bounded.
3
3

3

For the proof of Theorem
6
, it is only required that the solutions to all Poisson equations considered below and their derivatives are bounded. To this effect, we would additionally need the source of these Poisson equations to decay at the boundary of the domain so we can ensure this behavior for its solution. For instance, this would be ensured if the source has compact support.

Theorem 6
.

With Assumptions
5
enforced, there exists
C>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon<1
,

|\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(t,y)-\mathcal{X}_{0}(t)|\leq C\,\varepsilon,

for any
t\in[0,T]
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The proof involves three main steps: (i) recast the PDE (
25
) for
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}
in terms of an auxiliary control problem, (ii) construct a sub-solution and a super-solution of (
25
), and (iii) use the sub/super-solutions to bound the error.

First, the PDE for
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}
may be recast as the following HJB equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}-\phi+\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}\left\{-2\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}\xi-k(t,y)\xi^{2}\right\}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}&=0,\\
\\
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(T,y)&=-\varphi+\tfrac{b}{2},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, non-linear Feynman-Kac (see
[
30
]
) implies that
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}
admits the representation in terms of the auxiliary control problem

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(t,y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{B}}Z(t,y,\xi),

(70)

\displaystyle Z(t,y,\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[\;(-\varphi+\tfrac{b}{2})\,e^{-2\int_{t}^{T}\xi_{u}du}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}\,(-\phi-k(s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\xi_{s}^{2})\,ds\;\right],

(71)

where
\mathbb{B}
is a set of admissible controls taking values in
\mathcal{B}
that consists of
\mathcal{F}
-predictable processes in
\mathbb{L}^{2}([0,T],\Omega)
.
We next proceed along the ideas developed in
[
13
]
for the proof of accuracy of non-linear PDEs stemming from portfolio optimisation problems.

To this end, define
\mathcal{X}^{\pm}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\pm}(t,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}_{0}(t)+\varepsilon\,\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(t,y)\pm\varepsilon\,(2T-t)\,C_{1}\,N(t)\pm\varepsilon^{2}\,M(t,y),

(72)

for constant
C_{1}>0
and functions
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}
,
N
and
M
to be defined shortly.

By the Poisson equation (
35b
), we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}(t,y)=-\psi(y)\frac{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{2}(t)}{\kappa(t)}+c(t),

(73)

for some function
c
independent of
y
and
\psi
is the solution of the Poisson equation
\mathcal{L}_{0}\psi=\eta
which is defined only up to a deterministic function of time (constant in
y
). By choosing this function, we may therefore assume
\langle\psi\rangle=0
without loss of generality. We define the
y
-dependent part of
\mathcal{X}_{1}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(t,y):=-\psi(y)\,\frac{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{2}(t)}{\kappa(t)},

(74)

which sets one of the three free functions in the definition of
\mathcal{X}^{\pm}
.

Next, define the differential operator

\displaystyle R^{\xi}[\mathcal{X}]=\partial_{t}\mathcal{X}-\phi-2\xi\mathcal{X}-k(t,y)\xi^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{X},

(75)

for any
\xi\in\mathcal{B}
. The HJB equation (
5
) may be written as
\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}R^{\xi}[\mathcal{X}]=0
.
For any control
\xi\in\mathbb{B}
, Itô’s lemma implies

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\mathcal{X}^{\pm}(T,Y_{T}^{\varepsilon})\,e^{-2\int_{t}^{T}\xi_{u}du}-\int_{t}^{T}e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}\,(\phi+k(s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\xi_{s}^{2})\,ds

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{X}^{\pm}(t,y)+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}\,R^{\xi_{s}}[\mathcal{X}^{\pm}](s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,ds

(77)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}\,\beta(Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{y}\mathcal{X}^{\pm}(s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,dW_{s}^{*}.

(78)

Taking expectation, since the Itô integral above is a true martingale by Assumptions
5
, we find

\displaystyle Z(t,y,\xi)+\varepsilon\,\mathcal{H}^{\pm}(t,y,\xi)=\mathcal{X}^{\pm}(t,y)+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\,e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}\,R^{\xi_{s}}[\,\mathcal{X}^{\pm}](s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\right]\,ds,

(79)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\pm}(t,y,\xi):=\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[\,e^{-2\int_{t}^{T}\xi_{u}du}\,\left(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(T,Y_{T}^{\varepsilon})\pm T\,C_{1}N(T)\pm\varepsilon\,M(T,Y_{T}^{\varepsilon})\right)\,\right]\,.

(80)

Next, we construct particularly choices for the two remaining free functions of
\mathcal{X}^{\pm}
, defined in (
72
), that provide sub and super solutions.

Sub-solution

Let us first analyze
\mathcal{X}^{-}
. Acting on it with the
R^{\xi}
operator, we have

\begin{split}R^{\xi}[\mathcal{X}^{-}]=&\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}-\phi-2\xi\mathcal{X}_{0}-k(t,y)\xi^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\cancelto{0}{\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{X}_{0}}\\
&-\varepsilon\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}-2\varepsilon\xi\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}-\mathcal{L}_{0}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\\
&+\varepsilon C_{1}N(t)-\varepsilon C_{1}(2T-t)\left(\partial_{t}N-2\xi N\right)\\
&-\varepsilon^{2}(\partial_{t}M-2\xi M)-\varepsilon\mathcal{L}_{0}M.\end{split}

(81)

Define the particular auxiliary control

\xi_{0}(t,y):=-\frac{\mathcal{X}_{0}(t)}{k(t,y)}=-(1+\eta(y))\frac{\mathcal{X}_{0}(t)}{\kappa(t)}.

(82)

With this control, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}-\phi-2\xi_{0}\mathcal{X}_{0}-k(t,y)\xi_{0}^{2}=\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}-\phi+\tfrac{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{2}}{k(t,y)}\,,

(83)

hence, by (
35b
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{0}^{\prime}-\phi+\tfrac{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{2}}{k(t,y)}+\mathcal{L}_{0}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}=0.

(84)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}R^{\xi_{0}}[\mathcal{X}^{-}]=&\,\varepsilon\,\big\{\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}-2\xi_{0}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}+C_{1}N(t)-C_{1}(2T-t)\left(N^{\prime}(t)-2\xi_{0}N\right)-\mathcal{L}_{0}M\big\}\\
&-\varepsilon^{2}\,\left\{\partial_{t}M-2\xi_{0}M\right\}\end{split}

(85)

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&\,\varepsilon\,\Big\{-\psi(y)\left(\partial_{t}(\mathcal{X}^{2}/\kappa)+2\mathcal{X}^{3}/\kappa^{2}\right)-2\eta(y)\psi(y)\mathcal{X}^{3}/\kappa^{2}+C_{1}N(t)\\
&\quad-C_{1}(2T-t)\left(N^{\prime}(t)+2\mathcal{X}_{0}N/\kappa\right)-C_{1}(2T-t)\eta(y)\mathcal{X}_{0}N/\kappa-\mathcal{L}_{0}M\Big\}\\
&-\varepsilon^{2}\,\left\{\partial_{t}M-2\xi_{0}M\right\}.\end{split}

(86)

We next choose
M
such that the
\varepsilon
term is centered with respect to the invariant measure, that is

\displaystyle M(t,y)

\displaystyle=-\Psi_{1}(y)\left(\partial_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{2}/\kappa)+2\mathcal{X}_{0}^{3}/\kappa^{2}\right)-2\Psi_{2}(y)\mathcal{X}^{3}/\kappa^{2}-\psi(y)C_{1}(2T-t)\mathcal{X}_{0}N/\kappa,

(87)

where we use that
\langle\eta\rangle=\langle\psi\rangle=0
, and where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}\Psi_{1}=\psi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{0}\Psi_{2}=\eta\psi-\langle\eta\psi\rangle.

(88)

Therefore, we find

\begin{split}R^{\xi_{0}}[\mathcal{X}^{-}]=&\,\varepsilon\left\{-2\langle\psi\eta\rangle\frac{\mathcal{X}^{3}}{\kappa^{2}}+C_{1}N(t)-C_{1}(2T-t)\left(N^{\prime}(t)+2\frac{\mathcal{X}_{0}\,N}{\kappa}\right)\right\}\\
&-\varepsilon^{2}\left\{\partial_{t}M-2\xi_{0}M\right\}.\end{split}

(89)

Next, we choose
N
such that the term proportional to
(2T-t)
vanishes, which is given by

\displaystyle N(t)=\exp\left\{-2\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathcal{X}_{0}(s)}{\kappa(s)}ds\right\}.

(90)

For the above choices of
N
and
M
, we therefore obtain

R^{\xi_{0}}[\mathcal{X}^{-}]=\varepsilon\left(-2\langle\psi\eta\rangle\mathcal{X}^{3}/\kappa^{2}+C_{1}N(t)\right)-\varepsilon^{2}(\partial_{t}M-2\xi_{0}M).

(91)

By Assumptions
5
, the first and last terms of the expression above are bounded. Hence, there exists
C_{1}
large enough such that

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{0}(u,Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})du}\;R^{\xi_{0}}[\mathcal{X}^{-}](s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds\geq 0,

(92)

for any
t
and
y
. Moreover, notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-}(t,y,\xi_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[e^{-2\int_{t}^{T}\xi_{0}(u,Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})du}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(T,Y_{T}^{\varepsilon})-T\,C_{1}\,N(T)-\varepsilon\,M(T,Y_{T}^{\varepsilon})\right)\right].

Again by Assumptions
5
, if necessary, we increase
C_{1}
such that the term inside the expectation that defines
\mathcal{H}^{-}(t,y,\xi_{0})
is negative everywhere. Hence, by the definition of
\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}
we find that, for any
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(t,y)

\displaystyle\geq Z(t,y,\xi_{0})

(93)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}^{-}(t,y)-\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{-}(t,y,\xi_{0})+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{0}(u,Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})du}R^{\xi_{0}}[\mathcal{X}^{+}](s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds

(94)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{X}^{-}(t,y).

(95)

Super-solution

Let us now analyze
\mathcal{X}^{+}
. We start by computing

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}R^{\xi}[\mathcal{X}^{+}]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\mathcal{X}^{+}-\phi+\frac{(\mathcal{X}^{+})^{2}}{k(t,y)}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathcal{X}^{+}

(96)

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&\varepsilon\,\Big\{\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}+\frac{2}{k(t,y)}\mathcal{X}_{0}(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}+(2T-t)C_{1}N+\varepsilon M)\\
&-C_{1}N+(2T-t)C_{1}\partial_{t}N+\mathcal{L}_{0}M\Big\}\\
&+\varepsilon^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{k(t,y)}(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}+(2T-t)C_{1}N+\varepsilon M)^{2}+\partial_{t}M\right\}.\end{split}

(97)

The term of order
\varepsilon
is then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{k(t,y)}\mathcal{X}_{0}(\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}+(2T-t)C_{1}N)-C_{1}N

(98)

\displaystyle+(2T-t)C_{1}\partial_{t}N+\mathcal{L}_{0}M.

Similarly to what was done before, we choose
M
to center (
98
) with respect to the invariant distribution. Since
\langle\psi\rangle=\langle\eta\rangle=0
, the term of order
\varepsilon
becomes

\displaystyle-2\langle\eta\psi\rangle\mathcal{X}_{0}^{3}/\kappa^{2}-C_{1}N+(2T-t)C_{1}(\partial_{t}N+2\mathcal{X}_{0}N/\kappa).

(99)

Choosing the same function
N
as for the sub-solution, the term of order
\varepsilon
is given by

\displaystyle-2\langle\eta\psi\rangle\mathcal{X}_{0}^{3}/\kappa^{2}-C_{1}N.

(100)

By Assumptions
5
, the term of order
\varepsilon^{2}
is bounded. We can then choose
C_{1}
large enough so that
\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}R^{\xi}[\mathcal{X}^{+}]\leq 0
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{+}(T,y)=(-\varphi+b/2)+\varepsilon\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{1}(T)+\varepsilon TC_{1}N(T)+\varepsilon^{2}M(T,y).

(101)

Possibly increasing
C_{1}
even more, we conclude that, say for
\varepsilon\leq 1
,
\mathcal{X}^{+}(T,y)\geq-A+b/2
uniformly in
y
. Then, by Itô’s formula (
76
),

\displaystyle Z(t,y,\xi)\leq\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[\mathcal{X}^{+}(T,Y_{T}^{\varepsilon})e^{-2\int_{t}^{T}\xi_{u}du}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}(-\phi-k(s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\xi_{s}^{2})d\right]

(102)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}^{+}(t,y)+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}R^{\xi_{s}}[\mathcal{X}^{+}](s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds

(103)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{X}^{+}(t,y)+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t,y}\left[e^{-2\int_{t}^{s}\xi_{u}du}\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}R^{\xi}[\mathcal{X}^{+}](s,Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds\leq\mathcal{X}^{+}(t,y).

(104)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(t,y)\leq\mathcal{X}^{+}(t,y).

(105)

We have finally concluded that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}^{\varepsilon}(t,y)-\mathcal{X}_{0}(t)|\leq C\varepsilon.

(106)

\displaystyle h^{(1)}(t,\boldsymbol{\mu})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{4\mathsf{k}(s)}(h^{(1)})(s,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{s})ds\ \Big|\ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}=\boldsymbol{\mu}\right]

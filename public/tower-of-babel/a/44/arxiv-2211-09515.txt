Generalised Synchronisations, Embeddings, and Approximations for Continuous Time Reservoir Computers Journal: Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena
Allen G Hart

Abstract
We establish conditions under which a continuous time reservoir computer, such as a leaky integrator echo state network, admits a generalised synchronisation f f between between the source dynamics and reservoir dynamics. We show that multiple generalised synchronisations can exist simultaneously, and connect this to the multi-Echo-State-Property (multi-ESP). In the special case of a linear reservoir computer, we derive a closed form expression for the generalised synchronisation f f . Furthermore, we establish conditions under which f f is of class C 1 C^{1} , and conditions under which f f is a topological embedding on the fixed points of the source system. This embedding result is closely related to Takens’ embedding Theorem. We also prove that the embedding of fixed points occurs almost surely for randomly generated linear reservoir systems. With an embedding achieved, we discuss how the universal approximation theorem makes it possible to forecast the future dynamics of the source system and replicate its topological properties. We illustrate the theory by embedding a fixed point of the Lorenz-63 system into the reservoir space using numerical methods. Finally, we show that if the observations are perturbed by white noise, the GS is preserved up to a perturbation by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

GS for Nonlinear Reservoir Systems

We will begin with the definition of a
V
-invariant ODE. Roughly speaking, a solution to the ODE which originates in the set

Definition 2.1
.

(
V
-invariant) Let

Definition 2.2

(Uniform

-asymptotic stability)
Let

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),z_{m}(t))

-asymptotically stable with respect to

The notion of uniform

Definition 2.4
.

(Reservoir system) Suppose that

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\omega\phi^{t}(m)).

(3)

Associated to the manifold

Definition 2.5
.

(Lie derivative) Suppose that
\mathcal{V}
is a smooth vector field on a smooth manifold

with associated evolution operators
\{\phi^{t}\in\text{Diff}^{1}(M)\ |\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then the Lie derivative of a map

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V}}u(m)=\frac{d}{dt}u\phi^{t}(m)\rvert_{t=0}

We are now ready to introduce the concept of

-generalised synchronisation studied by

4
GS for Linear Reservoir Systems

4.1
Differentiable generalised synchronisation

In this paper, we have so far considered reservoir maps
F
that are in general nonlinear. In this section we will explore in detail the special case where
F
is a linear map. In particular, we define the linear reservoir system by the ODE

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=F(x(t),\omega\phi^{t}(m)):=-Ax(t)+C\omega\phi^{t}(m)

(8)

where
A\in\mathbb{M}_{N\times N}(\mathbb{R})
is symmetric positive definite, which ensures that condition (
4
) in Theorem

is satisfied, and

The linear reservoir system has been called a
next-generation reservoir computer
in a recent influential paper
[
6
]
, and is much more amenable to mathematical analysis than the generally nonlinear case studied so far. Despite its simplicity, the linear reservoir computer has interesting properties we will explore in the remainder of this paper. In the following theorem, we will obtain a closed form expression for the reservoir states
x(t)
and associated generalised synchronisation
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
for the linear system.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\omega\in C^{0}(M,\mathbb{R})
is bounded. Then for any
m\in M
and initial state

reservoir system (

defined by

\displaystyle x(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)\ d\tau+e^{-At}\bigg(x_{0}-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)\bigg),

(9)

and associated generalised synchronisation

\displaystyle f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)\ d\tau.

(10)

Proof.

We start by expressing (

\displaystyle\dot{x}+Ax=C\omega\phi^{t}(m)

and consider first the homogeneous equation

\displaystyle\dot{x}+Ax=0.

We solve this using separation of variables, and obtain the general solution
x_{h}(t)=e^{-At}v
where
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a constant of integration. We will now show that

\displaystyle x_{i}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)\ d\tau

is an inhomogeneous solution to (

) by taking the derivative of
x_{i}
and verifying that the result satisfies (
3
).

\displaystyle x_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)\ d\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{-t}^{\infty}e^{-A(t-u)}C\omega\phi^{u}(m)\ du\qquad(u=-\tau+t)

(11)

hence

\displaystyle\dot{x_{i}}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-t}^{\infty}e^{-A(t-u)}C\omega\phi^{u}(m)\ du

\displaystyle=A\int^{\infty}_{-t}e^{-A(t-u)}C\omega\phi^{u}(m)\ du+C\omega\phi^{t}(m)\qquad\text{(Leibniz Integral Rule)}

\displaystyle=-Ax_{i}(t)+C\omega\phi^{t}(m)\qquad\text{by \eqref{xt}}.

Then the full solution

\displaystyle x(t)=e^{-At}v+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)\ d\tau

must satisfy the initial condition

\displaystyle x_{0}=x(0)=v+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)\ d\tau

\displaystyle v=x_{0}-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)\ d\tau.

\displaystyle f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)\ d\tau

(12)

for each

and observe that for any initial reservoir state

the solution

converges to

is a generalised synchronisation.

∎

In the following proposition, we will state conditions under which

. This is especially interesting because a differentiable synchronisation preserves geometric information including the eigenvalues of fixed points and Lyapunov exponents of the underlying system. This results in a higher quality reconstruction

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})

denote the differential of

. Let

denote the smallest eigenvalue of

. Suppose there exists

then the

-generalised synchronisation

Proof.

Our goal is to prove that both

and the derivative

exist and are continuous. First of all,

exists and is continuous if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)\rVert\ d\tau

is finite, by lemma

. To prove the integral is finite we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert e^{-A\tau}C(\omega\phi^{-\tau})(m)\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lVert C\rVert}{\sigma[A]_{\min}}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega(m)\rVert.

The derivative

exists and is continuous if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert e^{-A\tau}CD(\omega\phi^{-\tau})(m)\rVert\ d\tau

is finite, using lemma

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert e^{-A\tau}CD(\omega\phi^{-\tau})\rVert\ d\tau(m)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert e^{-A\tau}\rVert\lVert C\rVert\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D(\omega\phi^{-\tau})(m)\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega(m)\rVert\sup_{m\in M}\lVert T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}Ke^{-(1-c)\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\ d\tau\ \sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega(m)\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K\lVert C\rVert}{(1-c)\sigma[A]_{\min}}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega(m)\rVert.

∎

4.2
Embedding the fixed points

In a highly influential paper written in 1981, Floris Takens
[
37
]
proved that the delay observation map is an embedding for a generic pair
(\omega,\phi)
of observation functions
\omega
and discrete time source system
\phi
. This launched the field of embedology
[
34
]
which explores novel and useful ways to embed a source system into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
using only a time series of scalar (or low dimensional) observations of the source system. Many authors
[
14
]

[
11
]

[
40
]

[
41
]

[
35
]
have observed that reservoir computing with observations from a dynamical system is a special type of embedology; as long as the generalised synchronisation
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding.

It is especially desirable that the generalised synchronisation
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding because this property allows a topologically faithful reconstruction of the source dynamics, which allows for future forecasting and other forms of learning. In fact an embedding creates a vector field in the reservoir space that is diffeomorphic to the vector field of the source system. It is very challenging to prove that a generalised synchronisation is embedding globally on
M
, so in this paper we prove a simpler result: that for generic observation functions
\omega
the generalised synchronisation
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding on the isolated fixed points of
\mathcal{V}
. First of all this simpler result is a necessary first step in the quest to prove a global embedding. Moreover, isolated fixed points are of particular interest in their own right because they are each contained by a small neighbourhood of
M
on which the vector field
\mathcal{V}
is approximately linear. Furthermore, when a trajectory of the source dynamics is at a point
m
close to a fixed point
m^{*}
, a nonlinear reservoir system converges to the linear reservoir system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=D_{x}F(f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m^{*}),\omega(m^{*}))x(t)+D_{z}F(f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m^{*}),\omega(m^{*}))\omega\phi^{t}(m)

\displaystyle=-Ax(t)+C\omega\phi^{t}(m)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=D_{x}F(f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m^{*}),\omega(m^{*})),

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=D_{z}F(f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m^{*}),\omega(m^{*})).

We can easily evaluate the GS evaluated at a fixed point
m^{*}

\displaystyle f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}(m^{*})

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-A\tau}C\omega(m^{*})d\tau=A^{-1}C\omega(m^{*}).

Now, before we prove that
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding of the fixed points for generic
\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
, we will define an immersion, an embedding, and the word generic.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
T_{m}M
denote the tangent space of
M
at
m
. A map

is an immersion at

if the derivative
Df(m):T_{m}M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
exists and is full rank.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
X
be a compact subset of
M
. Then a map
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an embedding on
X
if
f
is an immersion for each
m\in X
and the restriction
f\rvert_{X}
is injective.

Remark 4.5
.

If
X
is a compact subset of
M
and
f:M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an embedding on
X
then there is an open subset
\Omega\subset M
such that
X\subset\Omega\subset M
and the restriction
f\rvert_{\Omega}
is an embedding. Furthermore,
f\rvert_{\Omega}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
X
be compact subset of
M
. A property that holds on a dense open subset of
X
is a generic property of
X
.

Now to prove that
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding on the fixed points for generic

we will prove:

1.

In lemma

that

2.

In theorem

that

is an embedding on the fixed points for observation functions in a dense subset of

completes the proof.

Our strategy is very similar to Huke’s
[

]
strategy to prove Takens’ Theorem, and the strategy appearing in

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
,
\phi\in\text{Diff}^{1}(M)
, and
T_{m}\phi
denote the differential of
\phi
at
m\in M

\sigma[A]_{\min}
denote the smallest eigenvalue of
A
. Suppose there exists
c\in(0,1)
and

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in M}\lVert T_{m}\phi^{-t}\rVert\leq Ke^{c\sigma[A]_{\min}t},\qquad\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega(m)\rVert<\infty,\qquad\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega(m)\rVert<\infty

Let
X
be a compact subset of
M
. Then for an open (possibly empty) subset of

the GS

is an embedding on

Proof.

Embeddings on

are an open set in

\Psi:C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})\to C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle\Psi(\omega)=f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}

is continuous, and this will complete the proof. To this end, consider a sequence

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega_{n}(m)-D\omega_{*}(m)\rVert<\frac{\epsilon(1-c)\sigma[A]_{\min}}{2K\lVert C\rVert}.

Then our goal is to establish that
\Psi(\omega_{n})
converges to

Now

\displaystyle\lVert\Psi(\omega_{n})-\Psi(\omega_{*})\rVert_{C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lVert f_{(\omega_{n},\mathcal{V},F)}-f_{(\omega^{*},\mathcal{V},F)}\rVert_{C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C(\omega_{n}\phi^{-\tau}-\omega^{*}\phi^{-\tau})\ d\tau\bigg\rVert_{C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert e^{-A\tau}C(\omega_{n}\phi^{-\tau}-\omega^{*}\phi^{-\tau})\rVert_{C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert e^{-A\tau}C(\omega_{n}(m)-\omega^{*}(m))\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert e^{-A\tau}C(D(\omega_{n}\phi^{-\tau})(m)-D(\omega^{*}\phi^{-\tau})(m))\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert e^{-A\tau}\rVert\ d\tau\ \lVert C\rVert\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega_{n}(m)-\omega^{*}(m)\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert e^{-A\tau}\rVert\sup_{m\in M}\lVert(D(\omega_{n}\phi^{-\tau})(m)-D(\omega^{*}\phi^{-\tau})(m))\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\ d\tau\ \sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega_{n}(m)-\omega^{*}(m)\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert(D(\omega_{n}\phi^{-\tau})(m)-D(\omega^{*}\phi^{-\tau})(m))\rVert\ d\tau

by lemma
8.4

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lVert C\rVert}{\sigma[A]_{\min}}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega_{n}(m)-\omega^{*}(m)\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert(D\omega_{n}\phi^{-\tau}(m)-D\omega^{*}\phi^{-\tau}(m))T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\rVert\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lVert C\rVert}{\sigma[A]_{\min}}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega_{n}(m)-\omega^{*}(m)\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\rVert\ d\tau\ \sup_{m\in M}\lVert(D\omega_{n}(m)-D\omega^{*}(m))

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert C\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}Ke^{-(1-c)\sigma[A]_{\min}\tau}\ d\tau\ \sup_{m\in M}\lVert(D\omega_{n}(m)-D\omega^{*}(m))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lVert C\rVert}{\sigma[A]_{\min}}\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega_{n}(m)-\omega^{*}(m)\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{K\lVert C\rVert}{(1-c)\sigma[A]_{\min}}\ \sup_{m\in M}\lVert(D\omega_{n}(m)-D\omega^{*}(m))

\displaystyle<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
,
\phi\in\text{Diff}^{1}(M)
, and
T_{m}\phi
denote the differential of
\phi
at
m\in M
. Let
\sigma[A]_{\min}
denote the smallest eigenvalue of
A
. Suppose there exists
c\in(0,1)
and
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in M}\lVert T_{m}\phi^{-t}\rVert\leq Ke^{c\sigma[A]_{\min}t},\qquad\sup_{m\in M}\lVert D\omega(m)\rVert<\infty,\qquad\sup_{m\in M}\lVert\omega(m)\rVert<\infty

Let the dimension of
M
be
q
and dimension of the reservoir space
N\geq q
. Suppose that the smooth vector field
\mathcal{V}
on
M
admits a finite number of fixed points. For each fixed point
m\in M
let
J_{m}:T_{m}M\to T_{m}M
denote the Jacobian at
m
. Suppose that for each fixed point
m

1.

The eigenvalues of
J_{m}
denoted
\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{q}
are distinct.

2.

The vectors

\displaystyle\{(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})^{-1}C\}_{j=1,\ldots,q}

are linearly independent.

Then for generic
\omega\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
the GS
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding on the fixed points.

Proof.

Let
m\in M
be a fixed point. Suppose that
v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}
denote the eigenvectors of
J_{m}
associated to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q}
. Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a smooth bump function with support that contains
m
and no other fixed point. Furthermore suppose that

\displaystyle D\psi(m)=w^{\top}

where
w
is the unique solution to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}^{\top}\\
v_{2}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
v_{q}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}w=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{bmatrix}

Now define for
\epsilon>0
the perturbed observation function

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(m)=\omega+\epsilon\psi(m).

Then the perturbed GS has the form

\displaystyle f_{(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{V},F)}(m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega^{\prime}\phi^{-\tau}(m)\ d\tau

so

\displaystyle Df_{(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{V},F)}(m)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CD(\omega^{\prime}\phi^{-\tau})(m)\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CD\omega^{\prime}(m)T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CD\omega(m)T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\ d\tau+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}Cw^{\top}T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}\ d\tau.

Now
m
is a fixed point so

\displaystyle T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}=e^{-J_{m}\tau}.

Now the eignevectors
v_{j}
of the Jacobian

are linearly independent so it suffices to find arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\{Df_{(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{V},F)}v_{j}\}_{j=1,\ldots,q}

are linearly independent. Now

\displaystyle Df_{(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{V},F)}(m)v_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CD\omega(m)T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}Cw^{\top}T_{m}\phi^{-\tau}v_{j}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CD\omega(m)e^{-J_{m}\tau}v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}Cw^{\top}e^{-J_{m}\tau}v_{j}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CD\omega(m)e^{-\lambda_{j}\tau}v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}Cw^{\top}e^{-\lambda_{j}\tau}v_{j}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})\tau}CD\omega(m)v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})\tau}Cw^{\top}v_{j}\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})\tau}CD\omega(m)v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})\tau}C\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})\tau}CD\omega(m)v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})^{-1}C.

Now the vectors

\displaystyle\{(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})^{-1}C\}_{j=1,\ldots q}

are linearly independent by assumption. Hence we can choose a sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
so that

\displaystyle\bigg\{\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})\tau}CD\omega(m)v_{j}\ d\tau+\epsilon(A+\lambda_{j}\mathbb{I})^{-1}C\bigg\}_{j=1,\ldots,q}

are linearly independent, by lemma
8.5
. Thus we have an immersion of the point
m
. This immersion has no effect on the other fixed points, we so can repeat this procedure on each of the finitely many fixed points in turn without spoiling the immersion on any of the previous points. By Theorem 1.1 in
[
15
]
the immersions of the fixed points form an open set, so any sufficiently small perturbation of the observation function will preserve the immersion on the fixed points. Thus, we will construct an arbitrarily small perturbation
\omega^{\prime}
of an arbitrary observation function
\omega
which ensures that
f_{(\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{V},F)}
restricted to the fixed points is injective.

For each fixed point
m_{i}\in M
define a smooth bump function
\varphi_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that
\varphi_{i}(m_{i})=1
and
\text{supp}(\varphi_{i})
are disjoint. Then define the perturbed observation function

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(m)=\omega(m)+\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\varphi_{i}(m)

for
\epsilon_{i}>0
. Now
\omega^{\prime}\phi^{-\tau}(m_{i})=\omega^{\prime}(m_{i})
for all
\tau
because
m_{i}
is a fixed point so

\displaystyle f_{(\omega^{\prime},\varphi,F)}(m_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega^{\prime}\phi^{-\tau}(m_{i})\ d\tau

\displaystyle=\omega^{\prime}(m_{i})\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}\ d\tau C

\displaystyle=\omega^{\prime}(m_{i})A^{-1}C

\displaystyle=(\omega(m_{i})+\epsilon_{i})A^{-1}C.

Then we construct arbitrarily small
\{\epsilon_{i}\}
such that

\displaystyle\omega(m_{i})+\epsilon_{i}=\omega(m_{j})+\epsilon_{j}\iff i=j

and this proves that
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding of the fixed points for a dense set of observation functions. Then lemma
4.7
gives an embedding of the fixed points for generic
\omega
and the proof is complete.

5
A Central Limit Theorem for Reservoir Computing

If
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is injective, we can define the autonomous reservoir system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=F(x,\omega f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}^{-1}(x))

(13)

which in the linear case is

\displaystyle\dot{x}=-Ax+C\omega f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}^{-1}(x).

If
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is an embedding on some open subset
\Omega\subset M
then the autonomous system (
13
) defines a vector field on
f(\Omega)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is diffeomorphic to the source vector field
\mathcal{V}
on
\Omega
.

We cannot evaluate the map
\omega f^{-1}_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
directly, but we can approximate it with a random neural network
h:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\Theta
is a parameter space from which we draw random weights and biases. A common choice of ‘neuron’
h
comprising a random neural network is

\displaystyle h(x,\theta)=h(x,\alpha,\beta)=\tanh(\alpha^{\top}x+\beta)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are random weights and biases. Instead of using
\tanh
we could use other activation functions including the
relu
activation or radial basis activations. To improve generality we will approximate an arbitrary target function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, motivated by the special case
u=\omega f^{-1}_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
. The strategy is to first randomly generate weights and biases
\{\theta_{i}\}_{1,\ldots,D}
, then take samples
x_{j}=f(m_{j})
from a trajectory in the reservoir space. Then minimise over
\{w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\}_{i,\ldots,D}
the loss

Generalised Synchronisations with Noisy Observations

Our analysis has so far has made the rather ideal assumption that the observations are unperturbed by any noise. Reservoir computers appear to be fairly resilient to noise in time series forecasting problems
[
29
]
, which suggests that under noisy observations an (injective) GS is preserved in some form. With this inspiration we will assume that the observations are perturbed by Gaussian white noise with an amplitude
\sigma\in C^{0}(M,\mathbb{R}^{+})
. The reservoir states associated to a linear reservoir system are no longer deterministic, but instead an
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued stochastic process
X_{t}
that satisfies the SDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}=-AX_{t}dt+C\omega\phi^{t}(m)dt+C\sigma\phi^{t}(m)dW_{t}.

(16)

We will show in the following theorem, that for any initial reservoir state
X_{0}
and point
m\in M
of the source system, the reservoir states converge to a the image of a deterministic GS
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
perturbed by an error process with explicit form.

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose that
\omega,\sigma\in C^{0}(M,\mathbb{R})
are bounded. Then for any
m\in M
and initial state
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
the stochastic reservoir system (
16
) admits the unique solution
X_{t}

which converges in distribution to

\displaystyle X_{t}=f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}\phi^{t}(m)+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)\ dW_{\tau}

as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

We can solve SDE (
16
) exactly. First, let

\displaystyle Z_{t}=e^{At}X_{t}

then

\displaystyle dZ_{t}

\displaystyle=e^{At}dX_{t}+e^{At}AX_{t}dt

\displaystyle=e^{At}[-AX_{t}dt+C\omega\phi^{t}(m)dt+C\sigma\phi^{t}(m)dW_{t}]+e^{At}AX_{t}dt

\displaystyle=e^{At}C\omega\phi^{t}(m)dt+e^{At}C\sigma\phi^{t}(m)dW_{t}

hence

\displaystyle Z_{t}-Z_{-s}=\int_{-s}^{t}e^{Au}C\omega\phi^{u}(m)du+\int_{-s}^{t}e^{Au}C\sigma\phi^{u}(m)dW_{u}.

Now we substitute
X_{t}
back in

\displaystyle e^{At}X_{t}-e^{-As}X_{-s}=\int_{-s}^{0}e^{Au}C\omega\phi^{u}(m)du+\int_{-s}^{t}e^{Au}C\sigma\phi^{u}(m)dW_{u}

so

\displaystyle X_{t}-e^{-A(t+s)}X_{-s}=\int_{-s}^{t}e^{A(u-t)}C\omega\phi^{u}(m)du+\int_{-s}^{t}e^{A(u-t)}C\sigma\phi^{u}(m)dW_{u}

Then making the substitution

\tau=t-u

\displaystyle X_{t}-e^{-A(t+s)}X_{-s}=\int_{0}^{t+s}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)d\tau+\int_{0}^{t+s}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}

(17)

Now choosing

\displaystyle X_{0}-e^{-As}X_{-s}=\int_{0}^{s}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)d\tau+\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\alpha\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau}(m)dW_{\tau}

so

\displaystyle e^{-As}X_{-s}=X_{0}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)d\tau-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau}(m)dW_{\tau}

can now be substituted into equation (
17
)

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t+s}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)d\tau+\int_{0}^{t+s}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{-At}\bigg[X_{0}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)d\tau-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau}(m)dW_{\tau}\bigg].

Now let
s\to\infty

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)d\tau+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{-At}\bigg[X_{0}-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\omega\phi^{-\tau}(m)d\tau-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau}(m)dW_{\tau}\bigg].

The process
X_{t}
converges in distribution to

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)d\tau+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}

\displaystyle=f_{\omega,\mathcal{V},F}\phi^{t}(m)+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}

as
t\to\infty
.
∎

The stochastic reservoir states converge to the image of a GS, perturbed by the stochastic error term

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}.

In the special case that
\sigma(m)=\sigma>0
is a constant function the stochastic error term

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}C\sigma\phi^{-\tau+t}(m)dW_{\tau}=\sigma\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-A\tau}CdW_{\tau}=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma A^{-1})

is a multivariate normal with mean
0
and covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}A^{-1}

[
39
]
. This is the stationary distribution of the error process, which is a multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
[
39
]

\displaystyle dY_{t}=-AY_{t}dt+\sigma CdW_{t}.

The errors
Y_{t}
given by this process are normal and identically distributed, but they are not independent. This explicit form of the error may be important when analysing the error or uncertainty on the reconstruction of the source system.

8
Conclusions

In the paper, we established conditions under which a continuous time reservoir system admits a generalised synchronisation
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
, and showed that
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
solves a PDE involving the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V}}
. We discuss how it is possible for a reservoir system to admit several distinct generalised synchronisations simultaneously, and how this is related to the multi-ESP studied by
[
4
]
and the discrete time results in
[
10
]
. In the special case of a linear reservoir system, we derived a closed form expression for
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
and establish conditions that ensure that
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}
is continuously differentiable, i.e
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Furthermore, it was shown that for randomly generated weights the linear reservoir system is almost surely an embedding of the fixed points of the source system. Having achieved an embedding, we discuss how the central limit theorem applied to reservoir systems makes it possible to use the reservoir states to learn a vector field that is diffeomorphic to the source dynamics. We demonstrated this by embedding the fixed points of the Lorenz-63 system into the reservoir space, and training the reservoir computer to learn the eigenvalues of the linearisation about one of the fixed points belonging to the Lorenz system. Finally, it was shown that when the observations are perturbed by noise the GS is preserved, but the reservoir states are perturbed by an error process. When the errors on the observations are IID Brownian increments, the error process on the reservoir states is an Ornstein-Uhlumbeck process.

A possible direction of future work is to establish conditions under which the GS
f_{(\omega,\mathcal{V},F)}\in C^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a global embedding. Very similar results have been proved in discrete time by
[
11
]
, using the same tools and methods as the celebrated Takens embedding theorem. It has been conjectured
[
14
]
that for a larger class of nonlinear discrete time reservoir systems the associated GS is an embedding, and this may also hold in continuous time.

There may also be other ways to extend the results for linear systems to nonlinear systems. The best empirical results in forecasting and classification problems are typically achieved with a nonlinear reservoir map
F
, but the mathematical analysis is much more challenging. Furthermore, real data is often better modelled as a noisy trajectory of a deterministic source system, or a trajectory of a stochastic differential equation (SDE), which further complicates the analysis. A study of reservoir systems in these contexts may be shed more light on the performance of reservoir computing in practical applications.

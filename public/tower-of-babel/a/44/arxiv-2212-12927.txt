Normal structure spaces of groups
A. Goswami

Abstract
. Can we do a topological study of various classes of normal subgroups endowed with a hull-kernel-type topology? In this paper, we have provided an answer to this question. We have introduced as well a new class of normal subgroups called primitive subgroups. Separation axioms, compactness, connectedness, and continuities of these spaces have been studied. We have concluded with the question of determining spectral spaces among them.

20E28, 20E15, 20D25

1.
Introduction

In
[
26
]
, the striking similarity has been pointed out between the properties of ideals of a commutative Noetherian ring and normal subgroups of a group with the maximal condition. This analogy has been achieved by the following correspondences:
sum of ideals corresponds to the product of normal subgroups;
product of ideals corresponds to the commutator of the normal subgroups; and the residual quotient of ideals has an analogue introduced in
[
26
]
. Following the lead of
[
26
]
, the author in
[
21
]
has asked what else can be said in the presence of a finiteness condition, and has introduced notions of prime and primary ideals, the radical of an ideal,
m
-systems, isolated components and residual quotients. The main result in
[
21
]
is to obtain existence and uniqueness theorems for the decomposition of a given normal subgroup as an intersection of primary subgroups. Further studies have been done in
[
31
,
25
,
30
,
23
]
by considering radical properties as a tool in investigating the structure of a group. In
[
27
,
28
]
, prime subgroups are investigated in the
context of groups satisfying the maximal condition on normal subgroups. A study
of prime subgroups of groups have also recently been done in
[
11
]
.

Since the fundamental paper
[
29
]
on topologized (known as Stone topology) maximal ideals of Boolean algebras, intensive study of hull-kernel topologies have been done on various classes of ideals. Primitive ideals (of a noncommutative ring) endowed with a Jacobson topology (known as structure space) has been considered in
[
17
]
(see also
[
18
]
). The same Jacobson topology on primitive ideals of an enveloping algebra plays a crucial role in representations of complex finite-dimensional Lie algebras (see
[
5
,
19
,
20
]
).
For the construction of an affine scheme, in
[
13
]
, it (called the Zariski topology or the spectral topology) has been considered on prime ideals of a commutative ring. In
[
16
]
(see also
[
15
]
), the hull-kernel topologies have been endowed on minimal prime ideals of a commutative ring. In a more recent paper
[
2
]
strongly irreducible ideals of a commutative ring endowed with the hull-kernel topology have been studied. In
[
6
]
, a study has been made on various classes of ideals of a (commutative) ring endowed with coarse lower topologies. Since we can not impose hull-kernel topologies on an arbitrary class of ideals, we need to classify such classes, where coarse lower topologies also coincide with the hull-kernel ones, and all these have been obtained in
[
6
]
.

We shall here consider prime, minimal prime, maximal, primary, regular, principal, and finitely generated normal subgroups of a group. We shall introduce primitive normal subgroups (see Definition
2.14
) which best in author’s knowledge has never been considered before. Following the strategies from
[
6
]
, we shall endow coarse lower topologies on all these classes of normal subgroups and shall study various topological properties of these “normal structure spaces” (see Definition
3.1
).

3.
Normal structure spaces

The coarse lower topology (also known as lower topology
[
12
]
) on a spectrum
\sigma_{G}
is the topology for which the sets of the type

\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})=\{\mathfrak{n}\in\sigma_{G}\mid\mathfrak{x}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}\}\qquad(\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G})

form a subbasis of closed sets. Moreover, we define an (algebraic)
closure
operator on a spectrum
\sigma_{G}
as follows:

\mathrm{Cl}(X)=\{\mathfrak{n}\in\sigma_{G}\mid\cap X\subseteq\mathfrak{n}\}\qquad(X\subseteq\mathrm{N}_{G}).

Definition 3.1
.

A spectrum
\sigma_{G}
endowed with a coarse lower topology will be called a
normal structure space

We use the same notation
\sigma_{G}
to denote the space. In general, the collection of subsets
\{\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\}_{\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G}}
is not closed under finite unions. However the following result characterizes a spectrum
\sigma_{G}
on which each member of the collection is indeed a closed set,
i.e.
,
\{\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\}_{\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G}}
induces a hull-kernel topology on such a
\sigma_{G}
. A ring-theoretic version of this result was first proved in
[
24
, p. 11]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The collection of sets
\{\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\}_{\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G}}
induces a hull-kernel topology on a spectrum
\sigma_{G}
if and only if
\sigma_{G}
has the following property:

\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}\quad\text{implies}\quad\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}\;\;\text{or}\;\;\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}

(3.1)

for all
\mathfrak{n},

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{N}_{G}
and for all
\mathfrak{s}\in\sigma_{G}.

The ‘largest’ spectrum that satisfies the condition (
3.1
) is nothing but
\mathrm{Irr}^{+}_{G},
the spectrum of strongly irreducible normal subgroups. From (
2.1
) it follows that
\mathrm{Spec}_{G}
and
\mathrm{MinSpec}_{G}
also satisfy the condition (
3.1
), and hence these are the examples of spectra on which we can define hull-kernel topology. These facts summarizes in the following

Corollary 3.3
.

If
\sigma_{G}
is
\mathrm{Irr}^{+}_{G}
or its subspectra, then the coarse lower topology on it coincide with the corresponding hull-kernel topology.

Here are some examples of spectra that do not satisfy the condition (
3.1
) and hence one can not impose hull-kernel topologies on them.

Example 3.4
.

Suppose that
G=(\mathbb{Z},+)
and consider
\mathfrak{n}=2\mathbb{Z},

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=3\mathbb{Z},
and
\mathfrak{s}=6\mathbb{Z}.
Now
2\mathbb{Z}\cap 3\mathbb{Z}=6\mathbb{Z}\subseteq 6\mathbb{Z},
but
2\mathbb{Z}\nsubseteq 6\mathbb{Z}
and
3\mathbb{Z}\nsubseteq 6\mathbb{Z}.
This shows that the spectra
\mathrm{Prop}_{G}
,
\mathrm{Prin}_{G}
and
\mathrm{Fgen}_{G}
do not satisfy the condition (
3.1
).

We now study various topological properties of normal structure spaces. Some of the proofs of these results are similar to the corresponding results of rings, whereas some require additional assumptions or even do not hold at all in the context of groups. As we pass on we will highlight on this comparison with rings.
We start with continuous maps between normal structure spaces. Observe that although inverse image of a normal subgroup under a group homomorphism is normal, but the same may not hold for an arbitrary spectra
\sigma_{G}
. To resolve this problem, we need to impose that property on a spectrum. Hence we have the following

Definition 3.5
.

We say a spectrum
\sigma_{G}
satisfies the
contraction
property if for any group homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to G^{\prime},
the inverse image
\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
is in
\sigma_{G}
, whenever
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is in
\sigma_{G^{\prime}}.

It has been shown in
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
that
\mathrm{Spec}_{G}
has the contraction property.
Since the sets
\{\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\}_{\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G}}
only form a (closed) subbasis, all our arguments need to be at this level rather than just closed sets.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\sigma_{G}
be a spectrum satisfying the contraction property. Let
\phi\colon G\to G^{\prime}
be a group homomorphism and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G^{\prime}}.

(1)

The induced map
\phi_{*}\colon\sigma_{G^{\prime}}\to\sigma_{G}
defined by
\phi_{*}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
is continuous.

(2)

If
\phi
is surjective, then the normal structure space
\sigma_{G^{\prime}}
is homeomorphic to the closed subspace
\mathcal{V}(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi))
of the normal structure space
\sigma_{G}.

(3)

The subset
\phi_{*}(\sigma_{G^{\prime}})
is dense in
\sigma_{G}
if and only if
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{s}\in\sigma_{G}}\mathfrak{s}.

Proof.

To show (i), let
\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G}
and
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})
be a subbasic closed set of the normal structure space
\sigma_{G}.
Then

\displaystyle(\phi_{*})^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}))

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G^{\prime}}\mid\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G^{\prime}}\mid\phi(\mathfrak{x})\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}(\langle\phi(\mathfrak{x})\rangle),

and hence the map
\phi_{*}
continuous.
For (ii), observe that
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)\subseteq\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{e}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
for all
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G^{\prime}}.
It can thus been seen that
\phi_{*}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{V}(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)),
and hence
\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{*})=\mathcal{V}(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)).

If
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G^{\prime}},
then

\displaystyle\phi(\phi_{*}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\phi(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}))=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap G^{\prime}=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}.

Thus
\phi_{*}
is injective. To show that
\phi_{*}
is closed, first we observe that for any subbasic closed subset
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{a})
of
\sigma_{G^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{*}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}))

\displaystyle=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}))

\displaystyle=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G^{\prime}}\mid\mathfrak{x}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{x})).

Now if
K
is a closed subset of
\sigma_{G^{\prime}}
and
K=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}(\bigcup_{i\,=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}_{i\lambda})),
then

\displaystyle\phi_{*}(K)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}\left(\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}_{i\lambda})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}\bigcup_{i=1}^{x_{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{x}_{i\lambda}))

a closed subset of
\sigma_{G}.
Since by (
1
),
\phi_{*}
is continuous, we have the desired claim.
Finally to prove (iii), first we wish to show:
\mathrm{Cl}(\phi_{*}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})))=\mathcal{V}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}))
for all
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{N}_{G^{\prime}}.
For that, let
\mathfrak{s}\in\phi_{*}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})).
This implies
\phi(\mathfrak{s})\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}),
and that means
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\phi(\mathfrak{s}).
Therefore,
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{V}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})).
Since
\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}))=\mathcal{V}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}))
, the other inclusion follows. If we take
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
as the trivial normal subgroup
\mathfrak{e}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
, the above identity becomes

\mathrm{Cl}(\phi_{*}(\sigma_{G^{\prime}}))=\phi_{*}(\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{e}))=\mathcal{V}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(\mathfrak{e}))=\mathcal{V}(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)),

and hence
\mathcal{V}(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi))
to be equal to
\sigma_{G}
if and only if
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{s}\in\sigma_{G}}\mathfrak{s}.

∎

For
\mathrm{Spec}_{G}
, the inclusion condition in (

\phi
is the quotient map

Corollary 3.7

If
\sigma_{G}

\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})

irreducible

K\neq K_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}\cup K_{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
for any two proper closed subsets
K_{\scriptscriptstyle 1},K_{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
of

generic point
of

Theorem 3.8
.

In every normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
, the subbasic closed sets of the form:

\{\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})\mid\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})\}

are irreducible.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})
whenever
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
. Since
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})
is the smallest closed set containing
\mathfrak{n}
, and since
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
is a closed set containing
\mathfrak{n}
, obviously then
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
.
For the reverse inclusion, if
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})=\sigma_{G}
, then

\sigma_{G}=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})\subseteq\sigma_{G}.

This proves that
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})
. Suppose that
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})\neq\sigma_{G}
. Since
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})
is a closed set, there exists an index set,
\Omega
, such that, for each
\lambda\in\Omega
, there is a positive integer
x_{\lambda}
and normal subgroups
\mathfrak{x}_{\lambda 1},\dots,\mathfrak{x}_{\lambda n_{\lambda}}
of
G
such that

\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})={\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}}\left({\bigcup_{i\,=1}^{x_{\lambda}}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}_{\lambda i})\right).

Since

\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})\neq\sigma_{G},
we can assume that
{\bigcup_{i\,=1}^{x_{\lambda}}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}_{\lambda i})
is non-empty for each
\lambda
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{n}\in{\bigcup_{i\,=1}^{x_{\lambda}}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}_{\lambda i})
for each
\lambda
, and hence
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})\subseteq{\bigcup_{i=1}^{x_{\lambda}}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}_{\lambda i})
, that is
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})\subseteq\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})
as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Every non-empty subbasic closed subset of
\mathrm{Prp}_{G}
is irreducible.

Proof.

Since for every non-empty subbasic closed subset
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
of
\mathrm{Prp}_{G}
, the normal subgroup
\mathfrak{n}
is also in
\mathrm{Prp}_{G},
the proof now follows from Theorem
3.8
.
∎

As hull-kernel spaces are sparse with closed sets, so are normal structure spaces. Nevertheless, we have the following separation axiom for all of them.

Theorem 3.10
.

Every normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
is
T_{0}.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
be two distinct elements of
\sigma_{G}
. Then, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathfrak{n}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. Therefore
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
is a closed set containing
\mathfrak{n}
and missing
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. Therefore
\sigma_{G}
is a
T_{0}
-space.
∎

Let
R
be a ring. It is well known that prime spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
endowed with with a Zariski topology is a
T_{1}
-space if and only if
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
coincides with the spectrum
\mathrm{Max}(R)
of maximal ideals. For a normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
to be
T_{1}
, the condition is slightly different.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose that
G
is a group in which
\mathrm{Max}_{G}
exists. Then a normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
is a
T_{1}
-space if and only if
\sigma_{G}\subseteq\mathrm{Max}_{G}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{n}\in\sigma_{G}
. Then
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
, and so, by Theorem
3.8
,
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal normal subgroup with
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. Then
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n})=\{\mathfrak{n}\}
, the last part because
\sigma_{G}
is
T_{1}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}
, proving
\sigma_{G}\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(G)
.
Conversely, in
\mathrm{Max}(G)
,
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{m})=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
for every
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Max}(G)
, so that
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{m})
, and hence, by Theorem
3.8
,
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{m})=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
, showing that the structure space
\sigma_{G}
is
T_{1}
.
∎

\sigma_{G}

G

The next definition is going to play an important role in proving existence of generic points of nonempty irreducible closed sets of a normal structure space.

Definition 3.15
.

For a spectrum
\sigma_{G}
and for
\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G},
we define the normal subgroup

\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{x}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}\\
\mathfrak{n}\in\sigma_{G}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x}).

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\sigma_{G}
is
a spectrum of
G
. Then

(1)

for every
\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G},

\mathfrak{x}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{n}\in\sigma_{G},
then
\mathfrak{n}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{n}}^{\omega}
.

(3)

For all
\mathfrak{x}\in\mathrm{N}_{G},

\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})=\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}).

(4)

For every
\mathfrak{x},\mathfrak{y}\in\mathrm{N}_{G},
the inclusion
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{y})
holds if and only if
\sqrt{\mathfrak{y}}^{\omega}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}
.

Proof.

The inclusion in (i) follows from Definition
3.15
.
When
\mathfrak{n}\in\sigma_{G},
then we have

\sqrt{\mathfrak{n}}^{\omega}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}}\left\{\mathfrak{s}\in\sigma_{G}\right\}=\mathfrak{n},

and that proves (ii).
For (iii), by (
1
),
\mathfrak{x}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega},
and hence we have
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\supseteq\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}).
The other inclusion follows from Definition
3.15
.
The necessity part of (iv) is obvious. Now suppose
\sqrt[\mathrm{X}]{\mathfrak{y}}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}
, and
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})
. Then
\mathfrak{s}\in\sigma_{G}
and
\mathfrak{x}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}
, so that
\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{s}
. Therefore,

\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{s})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{x}}^{\omega})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\sqrt[\mathrm{X}]{\mathfrak{y}})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{y}),

showing that
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{x})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{y})
.
∎

Theorem 3.17
.

Every nonempty irreducible closed subset
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
of a normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
has a unique generic point if and only if
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
contains
\sqrt{\mathfrak{n}}^{\omega}.

Proof.

Suppose that every nonempty irreducible closed subset of
\sigma_{G}
has a unique generic point and
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
is one such. Then there exists
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in\sigma_{G}
such that

\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})=\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}).
Then by Lemma
3.16
(
2
) we have
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{n}}^{\omega}\in\sigma_{G}
.
Conversely, let every non-empty irreducible subbasic closed set
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
contains
\sqrt{\mathfrak{n}}^{\omega}.
and let
K
be an irreducible closed subset of
\sigma_{G}
. By definition,

K=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}E_{\lambda},

where
E_{\lambda}
is a finite union of sets of the type
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
, for suitable normal subgroups
\mathfrak{n}
of
G
. Since
K
is irreducible, for every
{\lambda}\in\Omega
there exists an normal subgroup
\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}
of
G
such that
K\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda})\subseteq E_{\lambda}
and thus, if
\mathfrak{t}=\sum_{{\lambda}\in\Omega}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}
, then we have

K=\bigcap_{{\lambda}\in\Omega}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda})=\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{t})=\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{t}}^{\omega}).

By assumption,
\sqrt{\mathfrak{t}}^{\omega}\in\sigma_{G}
and thus
K=\mathrm{Cl}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{t}}^{\omega})
.
∎

Proposition 3.18
.

If
G
has maximal normal subgroups, then every nonempty irreducible closed subset of
\mathrm{Irr^{+}}_{G}
has a unique generic point.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{N}_{G}
is a multiplicative lattice with product as intersection, every strongly irreducible normal subgroup becomes a prime element of this lattice and hence by
[
9
, Lemma 2.6]
, every nonempty irreducible closed subset of
\mathrm{Irr^{+}}_{G}
has a unique generic point.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

Since every non-empty irreducible closed subset
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})
of
\mathrm{Prp}_{G}
contains
\mathfrak{n},
every such set has a unique generic point.

For a commutative ring
R
, it is well known that spectrum of prime ideals
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is compact with respect to Zariski topology. The same is also true for any class of ideals that contains all the maximal ideals of
R
. For groups the situation is more subtle because of possible nonexistence of maximal normal subgroups in a group. However, having the following property guarantees compactness of certain normal structure spaces.

Definition 3.20
.

We say a normal structure space has the
partition of unity
property if for every
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathrm{N}_{G},

\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n})=\emptyset
implies
\mathfrak{n}=G.

Proposition 3.21
.

If a structure space
\sigma_{G}
have the partition of unity property then
\sigma_{G}
contains all maximal normal subgroups of
G
whenever they exist.

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma_{G}
has the partition of unity property. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal normal subgroup of
G
. If
\mathfrak{m}
were not in
\sigma_{G}
, then we would have
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{m})=\emptyset
, and since
\mathfrak{m}\neq G
, we would have a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.22
.

For rings the converse also holds, whereas in case of groups, maximal normal subgroups may not exist (see Remark
2.11
), and hence application’s of Zorn’s lemma fails.

Theorem 3.23
.

If a normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
has the partition of unity property, then
\sigma_{G}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\{K_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Omega}
be a family of subbasic closed sets of a normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
such that
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}K_{\lambda}=\emptyset.
Let
\{\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Omega}
be a family of normal subgroups of
\mathrm{N}_{G}
such that
\forall\lambda\in\Omega,

K_{\lambda}=\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}).
Since
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda})=\mathcal{V}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}\right),
we get
\mathcal{V}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}\right)=\emptyset,
and that by Definition
3.20
gives
\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}=G.
Then, in particular, we obtain
e=\sum_{\lambda_{i}\in\Omega}a_{\lambda_{i}},
where
a_{\lambda_{i}}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda_{i}}
and
a_{\lambda_{i}}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. This implies
G=\sum_{i\,=1}^{n}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda_{i}}.
Hence,
\bigcap_{i\,=1}^{n}K_{\lambda_{i}}=\emptyset,
and
\sigma_{G}
is compact by Alexander subbasis Theorem.
∎

If
G
is a Noetherian group, then we have the following result which may considered as a group-theoretic version of the corresponding result proved in
[
10
]
for rings.

Theorem 3.24
.

If
G
is a Noetherian group then every normal structure space
\sigma_{G}
is compact.

Proof.

Consider a collection
\{\sigma_{G}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Omega}
of subbasic closed sets of
\sigma_{G}
with the finite intersection property. By assumption, the normal subgroup
\mathfrak{s}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}
is finitely generated, say
\mathfrak{s}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
. For every
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
, there exists a finite subset
\Lambda_{j}
of
\Omega
such that
\alpha_{j}\in\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}
. Thus, if
\Lambda:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda_{j}
, it immediately follows that
\mathfrak{s}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Omega}(\sigma_{G}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{G}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{G}\cap\mathcal{V}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\sigma_{G}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda}))\neq\emptyset,

since
\Lambda
is finite and
\{\sigma_{G}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{n}_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Omega}
has the finite intersection property. Then the conclusion follows by the Alexander’s subbasis Theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.25
.

If
G
is a Noetherian group then every normal structure space is Noetherian.

Remark 3.26
.

It is well known that every Noetherian topological
space can be written as a finite union of non-empty irreducible closed subsets.
The spectrum
\mathrm{Prp}(G)
of a group
G
always has this property irrespective of
G
being a Noetherian group, and hence
\mathrm{Prp}(G)
is always a Noetherian normal structure space. This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{e})
is an irreducible closed subset in
\mathrm{Prp}(G)
(see Corollary
3.9
).

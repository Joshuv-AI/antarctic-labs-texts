Abstract
. Minkowski’s First Theorem and Dirichlet’s Approximation Theorem provide upper bounds on certain minima taken over lattice points contained in domains of Euclidean spaces. We study the distribution of such minima and show, under some technical conditions, that they exhibit Weibull asymptotics with respect to different natural measures on the space of unimodular lattices in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . This follows from very general Poisson approximation results for shrinking targets which should be of independent interest. Furthermore, we show in the appendix that the logarithm laws of Kleinbock-Margulis, Khinchin and Gallagher can be deduced from our distributional results.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider several extremal problems in Geometry of Numbers such as the existence of lattice points in convex bodies, minimal values of polynomial maps and
Diophantine approximation. Our first results deal with generic unimodular lattices, for which we use the following notation:

\displaystyle X_{d}

\displaystyle=\textrm{the space of lattices in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with covolume one},

\displaystyle\mu_{d}

\displaystyle=\textrm{the unique $\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})$-invariant probability measure on $X_{d}$. }

We write
\Vol_{d}
for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, normalized so that
\Vol_{d}\big([0,1]^{d}\big)=1
.

1.1.
Application I: Minkowski’s Theorem

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact, convex, and symmetric set with non-empty interior and let

\Lambda
be a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of covolume one. The Minkowski’s First Theorem (
[
20
, Lecture II, Theorem 11]
) says that

\Vol_{d}(B)\geq 2^{d}\implies\Lambda\cap B\neq 0.

In order to discuss whether this result can be improved, it is natural to introduce the function

c_{B}(\Lambda):=\inf\{c>0\,:\,\Lambda\cap cB\neq 0\big\},\quad\textrm{for $\Lambda\in X_{d}$}.

(1.1)

Let

\mathscr{T}_{d}:=\big\{(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:\,T_{1}\cdots T_{d}=1\big\},

(1.2)

and for
T\in\mathscr{T}_{d}
,
we consider the boxes

B_{T}:=\prod_{p=1}^{d}[-T_{p},T_{p}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
\hbox{vol}(B_{T})=2^{d}
, it follows from Minkowski’s First Theorem that

c_{B_{T}}(\Lambda)\leq 1.

Furthermore, it is easy to see that

\sup\big\{c_{B_{T}}(\Lambda)\,:\,\Lambda\in X_{d}\big\}=1,

(1.3)

whence Minkowski’s First Theorem is in some sense optimal. However, we can significantly improve it
for generic lattices:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\mu_{d}
-almost every
\Lambda\in X_{d}
,

\limsup_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\;-\frac{\log c_{B_{T}}(\Lambda)}{\log\log\|T\|_{\infty}}\geq\frac{d-1}{d}.

(1.4)

This result can be proved using, for instance, the method introduced in
[
16
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is an example of a
logarithm law
. Such laws have been
extensively studied in Number Theory and Dynamics.
Perhaps the first occurrence of a logarithm law is Khinchin’s Theorem (cf. (
1.8
)). It was realized by Sullivan
[
24
]
that Khinchin’s Theorem is intimately connected with geodesic excursions
to shrinking cuspidal neighborhoods in finite volume hyperbolic orbifolds. Later this idea was generalized
and refined in the influential work of Kleinbock and Margulis
[
16
]
.

It turns out that the logarithm law (
1.4
), as well as other
logarithm laws that we will discuss later, are very much related to
distributional
convergence of
extremal values of arithmetic functions. We show in the appendix (see Proposition
A.1
) that
Theorem
1.1
is a direct corollary of the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

For a sequence of finite subsets
\Delta_{n}
of
\mathscr{T}_{d}

such that

|\Delta_{n}|\to\infty\quad\quad\hbox{and}\quad\quad\frac{\displaystyle\min_{T\neq T^{\prime}\in\Delta_{n}}\max_{p}|\log T_{p}-\log T^{\prime}_{p}|}{\log|\Delta_{n}|}\to\infty,

(1.5)

the minima

\mathfrak{C}_{\Delta_{n}}(\Lambda):=\min_{T\in\Delta_{n}}c_{B_{T}}(\Lambda)

satisfy

|\Delta_{n}|^{1/d}\cdot\mathfrak{C}_{\Delta_{n}}{\mathrel{\to}_{\mu_{d}}}\Wei\big(2^{-(d-1)/d}\zeta(d)^{1/d},d\big),

where
\Wei
denotes the Weibull distribution.

Remark 1.4
.

Here and throughout this paper,
\zeta
denotes Riemann’s
\zeta
-function.
We also recall that the
Weibull distribution

\Wei(\lambda,a)

with scale
\lambda
and shape
a

is defined by the distribution function
1-\exp({-(\lambda t)^{a}})
.

1.2.
Application II: Minima of polynomial maps

We now turn to the very classical study of minimal values of homogeneous forms
restricted to lattice points (see
[
12
, Ch. 6]
and
[
20
, Chapter IX]
).
Unlike Theorem
1.3
, this involves finding lattice points in
non-convex
sets.
We consider the product form

\Pi(x):=\prod_{p=1}^{d}|x_{p}|,\quad\textrm{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

The following result was proved by Kleinbock and Margulis (
[
16
, Subsection 1.11]
):

Theorem 1.5

(Kleinbock–Margulis)
.

For
\mu_{d}
-almost every
\Lambda\in X_{d}
,

\limsup_{v\in\Lambda:\,\|v\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\;-\frac{\log\Pi(v)}{\log\log\|v\|_{\infty}}\geq d-1.

(1.6)

We establish a distributional version of this result. For boxes
B_{T}
with
T\in\mathscr{T}_{d}
and a lattice
\Lambda
, we define

m_{B_{T}}(\Lambda):=\inf\big\{\Pi(v):\,0\neq v\in\Lambda\cap B_{T}\big\}.

With this notation, we show:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Delta_{n}
be a sequence of finite subsets of
\mathscr{T}_{d}

satisfying (
1.5
), and

\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(\Lambda):=\min_{T\in\Delta_{n}}m_{B_{T}}(\Lambda).

Then

|\Delta_{n}|(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{d-1}\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}{\mathrel{\to}_{\mu_{d}}}\Wei\big(2^{-(d-1)}\zeta(d)/c_{d},1\big),

where
c_{d}
is the volume of the simplex
\{u\in[0,1]^{d-1}\,:\,u_{1}+\cdots+u_{d-1}\leq 1\big\}
.

Remark 1.7
.

We stress that Theorem
1.5
is in fact a direct consequence of Theorem
1.6
(see Proposition
A.1
in the Appendix).

This result can be generalized as follows.
Let
F
be a nontrivial homogeneous polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For a lattice
\Lambda\in X_{d}
, we consider the family of lattices

\Lambda_{T}:=\{(T_{1}\lambda_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\lambda_{d}):\,\lambda\in\Lambda\},\quad T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})\in\mathscr{T}_{d}.

Extremal behaviour for this family of lattices was studied in
[
12
, Ch. 3]
.
We investigate the asymptotics of the minima

m_{T}(F,\Lambda):=\min\left\{|F(v)|:\,0\neq v\in\Lambda_{T}\cap[-1,1]^{d}\right\}.

Theorem 1.8
.

There exist
c>0
,
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
for any sequence of finite subsets
\Delta_{n}
of
\mathscr{T}_{d}
satisfying (
1.5
), and

\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(F,\Lambda):=\min_{T\in\Delta_{n}}m_{T}(F,\Lambda),

one has

|\Delta_{n}|^{a}(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{b}\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(F,\cdot){\mathrel{\to}_{\mu_{d}}}\Wei\big(c,a\big).

1.3.
Application III: Khinchin’s Theorem

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we define

k_{T}(\alpha):=\min\big\{T^{1/(d-1)}\|q\alpha-p\|_{\infty}\,:\,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1},\enskip 1\leq q\leq T\big\},\quad\textrm{for $T\geq 1$}.

By Dirichlet’s Approximation Theorem, we have
k_{T}(\alpha)\leq 1
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
Furthermore, according to Davenport and Schmidt
[
8
, Theorem 1]
,
for Lebesgue almost every
\alpha\in[0,1]^{d-1}
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}k_{T}(\alpha)=1.

(1.7)

On the other hand,

Theorem 1.9

(Khinchin)
.

For Lebesgue almost every
\alpha\in[0,1]^{d-1}
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\,-\frac{\log k_{T}(\alpha)}{\log\log T}\geq-\frac{1}{d-1},

(1.8)

The limits (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) indicate that the function
T\mapsto k_{T}(\alpha)
fluctuates quite wildly, and we wish to study its extremal behavior.
More precisely, we show that minima of the function
T\mapsto k_{T}(\cdot)
on sufficiently lacunary subsets of
[1,\infty)
exhibit non-trivial Weibull asymptotics with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]^{d-1}
.

More generally, we can deal with approximation of systems of linear forms.
Let
d\geq 3
and write
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
with
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 1
. We set

\mathscr{T}_{\underline{d}}^{+}:=\big\{T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})\in\mathscr{T}_{d}\,:\,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d_{1}}\geq 1,\,T_{d_{1}+1},\ldots,T_{d}\leq 1\big\}.

(1.9)

For
T\in\mathscr{T}_{\underline{d}}^{+}
, we define

\lfloor T\rfloor_{\underline{d}}:=\min\big(\log T_{1},\ldots,\log T_{d_{1}},-\log T_{d_{1}+1},\ldots,-\log T_{d}\big).

(1.10)

Given
T\in\mathscr{T}_{\underline{d}}^{+}
,
\alpha\in\hbox{Mat}_{d_{1},d_{2}}([0,1])
and
k>0
,
we consider the inequalities

\Big|p_{l}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}\alpha_{lj}q_{j}\Big|\leq k\,T_{l}^{-1},\,l=1\ldots,d_{1},\quad|q_{j}|\leq T_{d_{1}+j},\,j=1,\ldots,d_{2},

with
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{1}}
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{2}}
and define

k_{T}(\alpha):=\min\Big\{{\max}_{l}\;T_{l}\Big|p_{l}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}\alpha_{lj}q_{j}\Big|\,:\,\,\,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{1}},\;\;q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{2}},|q_{j}|\leq T_{{d_{1}}+j}\Big\}.

Theorem 1.10
.

For a sequence of finite subsets
\Delta_{n}
of
\mathscr{T}_{\underline{d}}^{+}
such that

|\Delta_{n}|\to\infty,\quad\frac{\displaystyle\min_{T,T^{\prime}\in\Delta_{n}}\max_{p}|\log T_{p}-\log T^{\prime}_{p}|}{\log|\Delta_{n}|}\to\infty,\quad\frac{\displaystyle\min_{T\in\Delta_{n}}\lfloor T\rfloor_{\underline{d}}}{\log|\Delta_{n}|}\to\infty,

(1.11)

the minima

\mathfrak{K}_{\Delta_{n}}(\alpha):=\min_{T\in\Delta_{n}}k_{T}(\alpha),\quad\textrm{for $\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Mat}_{d_{1},d_{2}}([0,1])$},

satisfy

|\Delta_{n}|^{1/d_{1}}\cdot\mathfrak{K}_{\Delta_{n}}{\mathrel{\to}_{\hbox{\tiny\rm Leb}}}\Wei\big(2^{-(d-1)/d_{1}}\zeta(d)^{1/d_{1}},d_{1}\big).

Remark 1.11
.

We again stress that Theorem
1.9

follows directly from Theorem
1.10
(see Proposition
A.2
in the Appendix).

1.4.
Application IV: Gallagher’s Theorem

Now we consider a multiplicative version of Khinchin’s Theorem. Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we define

g_{T}(\alpha):=\min\left\{T\cdot{\prod}_{l=1}^{d-1}|q\alpha_{l}-p_{l}|\,:\,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1},\enskip 1\leq q\leq T\right\},\quad\textrm{for $T\geq 1$}.

The following logarithm law was proved by Gallagher
[
10
]
:

Theorem 1.12

(Gallagher)
.

For a.e.
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\,-\frac{\log g_{T}(\alpha)}{\log\log T}\geq d-1,

(1.12)

More generally, given
T\in\mathscr{T}_{\underline{d}}^{+}
,
\alpha\in\hbox{Mat}_{d_{1},d_{2}}([0,1])
and
g>0
,
we consider the inequalities

\prod_{l=1}^{d_{1}}\Big|p_{l}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}\alpha_{lj}q_{j}\Big|\leq g\,\left({\prod}_{l=1}^{d_{1}}T_{l}\right)^{-1},\quad|q_{j}|\leq T_{d_{1}+j},\,j=1,\ldots,d_{2},

with
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{1}}
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{2}}
and define

g_{T}(\alpha):=\min\left\{\prod_{l=1}^{d_{1}}T_{l}\Big|p_{l}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}\alpha_{lj}q_{j}\Big|\,:\,\,\,\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{2}},\;|q_{j}|\leq T_{{d_{1}}+j}$,\\
$p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{1}},\;\Big|p_{l}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}\alpha_{lj}q_{j}\Big|\leq T_{l}^{-1}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Theorem 1.13
.

For a sequence of finite subsets
\Delta_{n}
of
\mathscr{T}_{\underline{d}}^{+}
satisfying
(
1.11
), the minima

\mathfrak{G}_{\Delta_{n}}(\alpha):=\min_{T\in\Delta_{n}}g_{T}(\alpha),\quad\textrm{for $\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Mat}_{d_{1},d_{2}}([0,1])$},

satisfy

|\Delta_{n}|(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{d_{1}-1}\cdot\mathfrak{G}_{\Delta_{n}}{\mathrel{\to}_{\hbox{\tiny\rm Leb}}}\Wei\big(2^{-(d_{1}-1)}\zeta(d)/c_{d_{1}},1\big).

Remark 1.14
.

Just as before, it can be shown (see Proposition
A.2
in the appendix) that Theorem
1.13
implies
Gallagher’s logarithm law (Theorem
1.12
).

2.7
encompasses Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.6

1.8

2.9

.

2.
General framework

Let
X_{d}

denote the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given a pair
(\eta,C)
,
where
\eta
is a non-negative continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
C
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the function
\widetilde{\eta}:X_{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

\widetilde{\eta}(\Lambda):=\inf\big\{\eta(\lambda)\,:\,\lambda\in\Lambda\cap C,\enskip\lambda\neq 0\big\},\quad\textrm{for $\Lambda\in X_{d}$}.

(2.1)

Motivated by the problems introduced in the previous section,
we want to investigate the asymptotic behaviour of
minima
of
\widetilde{\eta}
along
sparse
samples taken from group orbits in
X_{d}
. More precisely, let
H
be a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
. Given a finite subset
F\subset H
, we define

\widetilde{\eta}_{F}(\Lambda):=\min_{h\in F}\,\widetilde{\eta}(h.\Lambda),\quad\textrm{for $\Lambda\in X_{d}$},

(2.2)

and consider the following problem

Problem 2.1
.

Let
\theta
be a Borel probability measure on
X_{d}
and let
(F_{n})
be a sequence of finite subsets of
H
such that
|F_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
.
Can we find a sequence
(\delta_{n})
of positive real numbers such that
(\widetilde{\eta}_{F_{n}}/\delta_{n})
has a non-trivial
\theta
-distributional limit?

Let us make a few remarks before we state our main theorems concerning this question (see Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
2.10
below). Note that
for all
t\geq 0
,

\big\{\widetilde{\eta}_{F_{n}}<t\big\}=\bigcup_{h\in F_{n}}h^{-1}.\big\{\Lambda\in X_{d}\,:\,\Lambda\cap C(t)\neq\{0\}\big\},\vskip-5.69046pt

(2.3)

where

C(t):=\big\{x\in C\,:\,\eta(x)<t\big\}.

Hence, to study the
\theta
-distributional asymptotics of the sequence
(\eta_{F_{n}}/\delta_{n})
, we need to understand

\theta\Big(\big\{\Lambda\in X_{d}\,:\,N_{n}(\Lambda;\delta_{n}t)>0\big\}\Big),\quad\textrm{as $n\rightarrow\infty$},

where

N_{n}(\Lambda;t):=\#\big\{h\in F_{n}\,:\,h.\Lambda\cap C(t)\neq\{0\}\big\}.

(2.4)

In what follows, we want to isolate conditions on
(\eta,C)
and
(F_{n})

which ensure that we can find a positive sequence
(\delta_{n})
such that, for some constants
a,m_{o}>0
,

N_{n}(\,\cdot\,;\delta_{n}t){\mathrel{\to}_{\theta}}\Poi\big(m_{o}t^{a}\big),\quad\textrm{for all $t\geq 0$},\vskip-5.69046pt

(2.5)

where
\Poi(m)
denotes the standard Poisson distribution with mean
m
, and the arrow denotes convergence in distribution (with respect to
\theta
).
We recall that (
2.5
) just means that for all
t\geq 0
and for all non-negative integers
k
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta\Big(\{\Lambda\in X_{d}\,:\,N_{n}(\Lambda;\delta_{n}t)=k\}\Big)=\frac{(m_{o}t^{a})^{k}}{k!}e^{-m_{o}t^{a}}.

Let us suppose that (
2.5
) holds. Then, by summing over all
k>0
, we see that

\theta\Big(\big\{\widetilde{\eta}_{F_{n}}/\delta_{n}\leq t\big\}\Big)=\theta\Big(\{\Lambda\in X_{d}\,:\,N_{n}(\Lambda;\delta_{n}t)>0\}\Big)\rightarrow 1-e^{-m_{o}t^{a}},

for all
t\geq 0
. In other words, if (
2.5
) holds, then

\widetilde{\eta}_{F_{n}}/\delta_{n}{\mathrel{\to}_{\theta}}\Wei\big(m_{o}^{-1/a},a\big),\quad\textrm{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.\vskip-2.84544pt

(2.6)

This shows that the Weibull asymptotics of the form (
2.6
) can be deduced from the Poisson approximation of the form (
2.5
).

2.1.
Volume regularity

In order to establish Poisson approximation of the form (
2.5
) (and consequently also Weibull asymptotics of the form (
2.6
)), we need to assume that the growth of the volumes of the sets
C(t)
is stable under small perturbations. The following definition captures a convenient form of stability which is sufficient for our purposes.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
a,c>0
and
b\geq 0
. The pair
(\eta,C)
is
(a,b,c)
-regular
if

(i)

\eta
is locally Lipschitz, and
\gamma
-homogeneous for some
\gamma\geq 0
.

(ii)

C(t)
is a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every
t\geq 0
and there exists a semi-norm
\tau
such that
C=\{\tau\leq 1\}
.

(iii)

The limit

c=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\Vol_{d}(C(t))}{t^{a}(-\log t)^{b}}

exists.

We give several examples of
(a,b,c)
-regular pairs
(\eta,C)
.

Example 2.3

(Norm balls)
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let

\eta=\tau=\|\cdot\|\quad\textrm{and}\quad C=\{\tau\leq 1\}.

Then
\eta
is clearly Lipschitz and
1
-homogeneous, and (iii) holds with

a=d,\enskip b=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad c=\Vol_{d}\big(\{\|\cdot\|\leq 1\}\big).

Example 2.4

(Products of linear forms)
.

Let

\eta(x)=|x_{1}\cdots x_{d}|,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau(x)={\max}_{k}\,|x_{k}|,\quad\textrm{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$},

and
C=\{\tau\leq 1\}
. Then
\eta
is locally Lipschitz and
d
-homogeneous, and
\tau
is a norm. To verify (iii), one can use the coordinate system and the integration formula from
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
. Using the coordinate system from
[
4
]
,
the set

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,0<x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\leq 1,\,x_{1}\cdots x_{d}\leq t\}

corresponds to

\{(u,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}:\,\,u_{1},\ldots,u_{d-1}\leq 0,\,u_{1}+\cdots+u_{d-1}\geq s,\,s\leq\log t\},

so that

\Vol_{d}(C(t))=2^{d}c_{d}\int_{-\infty}^{\log t}e^{s}s^{d-1}\,ds,

where
c_{d}
is the volume of the simplex
\{u\in[0,1]^{d-1}\,:\,u_{1}+\ldots+u_{d-1}\leq 1\big\}
.
Hence, (iii) holds with

a=1\quad\textrm{and}\quad b=d-1\quad\textrm{and}\quad c=2^{d}c_{d}.

Example 2.5

(Polynomial maps)
.

Let
F
be a nontrivial homogeneous polynomial map on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let

\eta(x)=|F(x)|,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau(x)={\max}_{k}\,|x_{k}|,\quad\textrm{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$},

and
C=\{\tau\leq 1\}
. Then
\eta
is locally Lipschitz and
d
-homogeneous, and
\tau
is a norm.
It remains to verify that the volume of the sets

C(t)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\,|F(x)|\leq t,\,|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{d}|\leq 1\}

has the stated asymptotics as
t\to 0^{+}
.
This can be deduce using the resolution of singularities that there exist
c>0
,

a\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\Vol_{d}(C(t))\sim c\,t^{a}(-\log t)^{b}\quad\hbox{as $t\to 0^{+}$.}

We refer, for instance, to
[
11
, Th. 1]
for a self-contained treatment.

Example 2.6

(Normed strips)
.

Let
\underline{d}=(d_{1},d_{2})
with
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
. Suppose that
\|\cdot\|_{1}

and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
are norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
respectively. Define

\eta(x)=\|x^{(1)}\|_{1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tau(x)=\|x^{(2)}\|_{2},\quad\textrm{for
$x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}$},

and
C=\{\tau\leq 1\}
. Then
\eta
is
1
-homogeneous,
\tau
is a semi-norm on

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and (iii) holds with

a=d_{1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad b=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad c=\Vol_{d_{1}}\big(\{\|\cdot\|_{1}\leq 1\}\big)\cdot\Vol_{d_{2}}\big(\{\|\cdot\|_{2}\leq 1\}\big).

2.2.
Poisson asymptotics with respect to
\mu_{d}

For
T\in\mathscr{T}_{d}
, we set

h(T):=\Diag\big(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\big)\in\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R}).

Then

H_{d}:=\big\{h(T)\,:\,T\in\mathscr{T}_{d}\big\}

is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
.
We will be interested in analyzing the minima

\widetilde{\eta}_{F}(\Lambda)=\min_{h\in F}\,\widetilde{\eta}(h.\Lambda)

as above, where
F
runs over sparse finite subset of
H_{d}

and
\Lambda
is generic with respect to
\mu_{d}
.
To formulate this sparseness condition precisely, we define the distance

\dist_{d}(h(T),h(T^{\prime})):=\max\big\{\big|\log T_{k}-\log T_{k}^{\prime}\big|\,:\,k=1,\ldots,d\big\},\quad T,T^{\prime}\in\mathscr{T}_{d}.

If
F
is a finite subset of
H_{d}
, we define the
spread

v_{d}(F)
by

v_{d}(F):=\min\big\{\dist_{d}(h,h^{\prime})\,:\,h,h^{\prime}\in F,\enskip h\neq h^{\prime}\big\}.

(2.7)

With this notation, we state our first main theorem:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
(F_{n})
be a sequence of finite subsets of
H_{d}
such that

|F_{n}|\rightarrow\infty\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{v_{d}(F_{n})}{\log|F_{n}|}\rightarrow\infty,\quad\textrm{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.

Suppose that the pair
(\eta,C)
is
(a,b,c)
-regular and set

2.4.
Connections to earlier works

In Section

and Section

, we explain how the results stated above can be recast in the language of dynamical systems and shrinking targets (possibly with respect to a non-invariant measure). There is a vast literature on Poisson asymptotics (and thus on extremal laws) for shrinking targets, and we will not attempt to make a comprehensive summary. The investigations in this paper could be said to originate from some surprising observations concerning Poisson approximation in the gap distribution of Laplacian eigenvalues by Sinai
[

,
23
]
(see also the subsequent work by Hirata
[

]
for a more dynamical framework, closer to the spirit of this paper). In the setting of homogeneous spaces,
the distribution of extreme values of excursions for partially hyperbolic one-parameter subgroups was studied by Pollicott
[
17
]
and Kirsebom
[
15
]
.

Recently, Dologopyat, Fayad and Liu
[
9
]
have conceptualized a lot of the early developments by emphasizing the role of quantitative multiple mixing (
\mathcal{W}
-mixing in our paper). Their setup and arguments in
[
9
, Section 3]
are close to the ones presented in Section
4
below. In particular, their notion of
simple admissible targets

[

, Definition 3.2]
captures a similar form of volume growth stability under perturbations as our notion of

(a,b,c)
-regularity, and their
[
9
, Proposition 3.9]
is close to our Theorem
4.4
(specialized to
\mathbb{R}
-flows).

An overview of the paper

In Section

we state a common generalization (Theorem

) of Theorem

and Theorem

, and
deduce these theorems from it, highlighting the role of volume regularity. In Section

Lemma 3.2

There exists a constant

W_{\varepsilon}.C(t)\subseteq(1+M\varepsilon)\cdot C(t+M\varepsilon),

Proof.

Fix

\displaystyle\subseteq\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,\exists\,y\in C(t)\enskip\textrm{such that}\enskip\|x-y\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\|y\|_{\infty}\big\}

is a bounded subset of

such that

. Hence, for every

4.
Poisson approximation for shrinking targets

In this section we show how equidistribution (or mixing) of all orders can be used to establish Poisson asymptotics in some situations which involve shrinking targets. We begin by proving a very general criterion for Poisson approximation (Lemma
4.1
). Then we introduce the notion of
\mathcal{W}
-equidistribution which is a simultaneous generalization of both (quantitative) equidistribution (of all orders) and (quantitative) mixing (of all orders). Our main result in this section is Theorem
4.4
, which provides criteria for when the hitting times to shrinking targets in a
\mathcal{W}
-equidistributing setting are asymptotically Poissonian.

4.1.
A general criterion for Poisson approximation

Let
(Z,\nu)
be a probability space and let
H
be an infinite set. Let
(F_{n})
be a sequence of finite subsets of
H
such that
|F_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
. Suppose that for every

n
and
h\in F_{n}
, we are given a measurable subset
A_{n,h}\subset Z
. Define

N_{n}(z):=\#\big\{h\in F_{n}\,:\,z\in A_{n,h}\big\},\quad\textrm{for $z\in Z$}.

(4.1)

We will prove that the sequence
(N_{n})
is
\nu
-asymptotically Poissonian with mean
m
, provided that the sets
A_{n,h}
, for
h\in F_{n}
, are ”almost independent” for large
n
. To make this more precise, we define

\Delta_{n,r}(m):=\max\Big\{\big|\,|F_{n}|^{r}\cdot\nu\Big(\bigcap_{h\in F^{\prime}}A_{n,h}\Big)-m^{r}\big|\,:\,F^{\prime}\subset F_{n},\enskip|F^{\prime}|=r\Big\},

(4.2)

for positive integers
r,n
and a real number
m\geq 0
. If
|F_{n}|<r
, we set
\Delta_{n,r}(m)=0
for all
m
. Note that if the sets
A_{n,h}
for
h\in F_{n}
are

\nu
-independent, and if
\nu(A_{n,h})=m
for all
h
, then
\Delta_{n,r}(m)=0
.

The following lemma will be useful in the next subsection.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that there exists
m\geq 0
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Delta_{n,r}(m)=0,\quad\textrm{for all $r\geq 1$}.

Then

N_{n}{\mathrel{\to}_{\nu}}\Poi(m)\quad\hbox{as $n\rightarrow\infty$. }

Proof.

By the classical method of moments (see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 30.1]
), it
suffices to show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{Z}\binom{N_{n}}{r}\,d\nu=\frac{m^{r}}{r!},\quad\textrm{for all $r\geq 1$}.

(4.3)

By
[
19
, Section 7.3, Eq. (3.4)]
,

\int_{Z}\binom{N_{n}}{r}\,d\nu=\sum_{F^{\prime}}\nu\Big(\bigcap_{h\in F^{\prime}}A_{n,h}\Big),\quad\textrm{for all $r\geq 1$},

where the sum is taken over all finite subsets
F^{\prime}\subset F_{n}
with
|F^{\prime}|=r
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{Z}\binom{N_{n}}{r}\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\sum_{F^{\prime}}\Big(\nu\Big(\bigcap_{h\in F^{\prime}}A_{n,h}\Big)-\frac{m^{r}}{|F_{n}|^{r}}\Big)+\binom{|F_{n}|}{r}\frac{m^{r}}{|F_{n}|^{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|F_{n}|^{r}}\cdot\sum_{F^{\prime}}\Big(|F_{n}|^{r}\nu\Big(\bigcap_{h\in F^{\prime}}A_{n,h}\Big)-m^{r}\Big)+\binom{|F_{n}|}{r}\frac{m^{r}}{|F_{n}|^{r}}.

Since
|F_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Delta_{n,r}(m)=0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big|\int_{Z}\binom{N_{n}}{r}\,d\nu-\frac{m^{r}}{r!}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\,\frac{1}{|F_{n}|^{r}}\binom{|F_{n}|}{r}\,\Delta_{n,r}(t)+\Big(\frac{1}{|F_{n}|^{r}}\binom{|F_{n}|}{r}-\frac{1}{r!}\Big)\cdot m^{r}\,\Big)

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta_{n,r}(m)}{r!}=0.

Since
r
is arbitrary, this proves (
4.3
), and we are done.
∎

4.2.
Quantitative multiple equidistribution

The main aim of this subsection is to define the notion of
\mathcal{W}
-equidistribution for actions of real Lie groups on locally compact spaces. To be able to do this, we first need to discuss
C^{q}
-norms and good pairs.

4.2.1.
C^{q}
-norms

Let
G
be a real Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Given
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
we denote by

D_{Y}
the differential operator defined by

(D_{Y}\rho)(g)=\frac{d}{dt}\rho(e^{tY}g)\mid_{t=0},\quad\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G).

Let
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}\}
be a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
, where
s=\dim\mathfrak{g}
. Given

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
, we define the differential operator
D_{\alpha}
by

D_{\alpha}:=D_{Y_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{Y_{s}}^{\alpha_{s}}.

We refer to
|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{s}
as the
order
of
D_{\alpha}
, and define, for
q\geq 1
,

\|\rho\|_{C^{q}}:=\max\{\|D_{\alpha}\rho\|_{\infty}\,:\,|\alpha|\leq q\big\},\quad\textrm{for $\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)$}.

(4.4)

4.2.2.
Good pairs and
\mathcal{W}
-equidistribution

Let
Z
be a locally compact and second countable metrizable space, endowed with a jointly continuous action of
G
. We assume that there exists a
G
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
on
Z
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
G
-invariant sub-algebra of

\mathbb{C}\cdot 1+C_{c}(Z)
, and let
\mathcal{S}=(\mathcal{S}_{q})
be a family of semi-norms on
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 4.2

(Good pairs)
.

We say that the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
is

if for every Borel set
A\subset Z
such that either
A
or
A^{c}
is pre-compact, and for every
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
, we have

\rho*\chi_{A}\in\mathcal{A}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{q}(\rho*\chi_{A})\ll_{q}\|\rho\|_{C^{q}},

for all
q\geq 1
, where the implicit constants only depend on
q
, and not on the set
A
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of
G
. For every
r\geq 1
, let
w_{r}
be a non-negative function on the space of
r
-element subsets of
H
, and set
\mathcal{W}=(w_{r})
.

Definition 4.3

(
\mathcal{W}
-equidistribution and
\mathcal{W}
-mixing)
.

Let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure on
Z
. We say that
\nu

\mathcal{W}
-equidistributes to
\mu
along

with respect to the pair

if for every

, there exist
\delta_{r}>0
and

such that for every

and

with

\Big|\int_{Z}\prod_{h\in F}\varphi\circ h\,d\nu-\Big(\int_{Z}\varphi\,d\mu\Big)^{r}\Big|\ll_{r}e^{-\delta_{r}w_{r}(F)}\,\mathcal{S}_{q_{r}}(\varphi)^{r},

where the implicit constants only depend on
r
. In the case when
\nu=\mu
, we
say that
\mu
is
\mathcal{W}
-mixing with respect to
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
.

In Section
6
below, we consider the case

and

and

, and provide examples of good pairs
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
and

such that
\nu
equidistributes to
\mu
along
H
with respect to the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
.

4.3.
\mathcal{W}
-equidistribution and Poisson approximation

Our aim is now to connect

-equidistribution with Poisson approximation. This is done in Theorem

In what follows, let

\mathcal{W}

(4.5)

The next theorem is the main result of this section. It provides a criterion for when

is

and the behaviour of the sequence

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widetilde{w}_{r}(F_{n})}{\log|F_{n}|}=\infty,\quad\textrm{for all $r\geq 1$}.

and that there exist

(i)

a sequence
(V_{n})
of open identity neighbourhoods in

and a sequence

of non-negative smooth functions on

for all

for all

, where the implicit constants only depend on

.

(ii)

two sequences
(A_{n}^{-})
and
(A_{n}^{+})
of Borel sets in

and such that

Then,
N_{n}{\mathrel{\to}_{\nu}}\Poi(m)
, as

.

Proof of Theorem
4.4

We define
\varphi_{n}^{\pm}=\rho_{n}*\chi_{A_{n}^{\pm}}

(4.6)

Furthermore, since

(4.7)

, and

\mathcal{S}_{q}(\varphi_{n}^{\pm})\ll_{q}\|\rho_{n}\|_{C^{q}(G)}\ll_{q}|F_{n}|^{\sigma_{q}},\quad\textrm{for all $q\geq 1$},

(4.8)

where we in the last inequality used our assumption (i). We stress that the implicit constants are independent of

.

By assumption,
\nu

\mathcal{W}
-equidistributes to
\mu
along
H
with respect to

, so it follows from (

) and (

) that

\Big|\int_{X}\prod_{h\in F^{\prime}}\varphi_{n}^{\pm}\circ h\,d\nu-\mu(A_{n}^{\pm})^{r}\Big|\ll_{r}|F_{n}|^{r\sigma_{q_{r}}}\cdot e^{-\delta_{r}w_{r}(F^{\prime})}

(4.9)

for every subset

with

) and (

\displaystyle|F_{n}|^{r}\nu\Big(\bigcap_{h\in F^{\prime}}h^{-1}.A_{n})-m^{r}

\displaystyle\leq|F_{n}|^{r}\Big(\int_{Z}\prod_{h\in F^{\prime}}\varphi_{n}^{+}\circ h-\mu(A_{n}^{+})^{r}\Big)+\big(|F_{n}|\mu(A_{n}^{+})\big)^{r}-m^{r}

\displaystyle\ll_{r}\big(|F_{n}|\mu(A_{n}^{+})\big)^{r}-m^{r}+|F_{n}|^{r(1+\sigma_{q_{r}})}\cdot e^{-\delta_{r}w_{r}(F^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\ll_{r}\big(|F_{n}|\mu(A_{n}^{+})\big)^{r}-m^{r}+|F_{n}|^{r(1+\sigma_{q_{r}})}\cdot e^{-\delta_{r}\widetilde{w}_{r}(F_{n})},

(4.10)

for every subset

of

with

for every subset

of

Since we assume that

for every

, this shows that

5.
Siegel transforms

We recall that
X_{d}
denotes the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, equipped with the Chabauty topology. The standard
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
-action on
X_{d}
is jointly
continuous and there is a unique
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant Borel probability measure
on
X_{d}
, which we denote by
\mu_{d}
.

5.1.
Hitting sets

Given a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the
hitting set

\Omega_{B}\subset X_{d}
by

\Omega_{B}=\big\{\Lambda\in X_{d}\,:\,\Lambda\cap B\neq\{0\}\big\}.

(5.1)

We note that
\Omega_{B}
is a Borel set in
X_{d}
. By Mahler’s Compactness Criterion
[
7
, Chapter V, Theorem IV]
, the complement
\Omega_{B}^{c}
is compact in
X_{d}
if
B
contains a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

5.2.
Siegel transforms

Given
\Lambda\in X_{d}
, we denote by
\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}
the set of primitive vectors in
\Lambda
. If
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a Borel function with bounded support, we define its
Siegel transform

\widehat{f}:X_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\widehat{f}(\Lambda)=\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{\hskip 5.69046pt\lambda\in\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}}\hskip-2.84544ptf(\lambda),\quad\textrm{for $\Lambda\in X_{d}$}.

(5.2)

In particular, if
B
is a bounded Borel set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\widehat{\chi}_{B}(\Lambda)=|B\cap\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}|
.

The following theorem summarizes important special cases of two fundamental formulas of Siegel and Rogers.

Theorem 5.1

(Siegel’s and Rogers’ formulas)
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and symmetric Borel set.

(i)

Siegel,
[
21
, Section 2]
If
d\geq 2
, then
\widehat{\chi}_{B}\in L^{1}(\mu_{d})
and

\int_{X_{d}}\widehat{\chi}_{B}(\Lambda)\,d\mu_{d}(\Lambda)=\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)},

(ii)

Rogers,
[
18
, Theorem 5]
If
d\geq 3
, then
\widehat{\chi}_{B}\in L^{2}(\mu_{d})
, and

\int_{X_{d}}\widehat{\chi}_{B}(\Lambda)^{2}\,d\mu_{d}(\Lambda)=2\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)}+\Big(\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)}\Big)^{2}.

5.3.
Volume asymptotics

We recall that a subset
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
balanced
if
tB\subset B
for all
|t|\leq 1
. Note that every balanced set is symmetric.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
. For every bounded and balanced Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\Omega_{B}=\{\widehat{\chi}_{B}>0\}=\{\widehat{\chi}_{B}\geq 2\}.

and

\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})\leq\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}.

Proof.

Since
B
is balanced, we have
\frac{1}{k}B\subseteq B
for every integer
k\geq 1
, and thus

\displaystyle\Omega_{B}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\Lambda\,:\,\Lambda\cap B\neq\{0\}\Big\}=\Big\{\Lambda\,:\,\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\Big(k.\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}\cap B\Big)\neq\{0\}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\Lambda\,:\,\bigcup_{k\geq 1}k.\Big(\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}\cap\frac{1}{k}B\Big)\neq\{0\}\Big\}=\big\{\Lambda\,:\,\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}\cap B\neq\{0\}\big\}.

This shows that
\Omega_{B}=\{\widehat{\chi}_{B}>0\}
. Furthermore, since
B

is symmetric,
|\Lambda_{\textrm{pr}}\cap B|
must be an even integer for every
\Lambda\in X_{d}
, which proves that we also have
\Omega_{B}=\{\widehat{\chi}_{B}\geq 2\}
. Finally, we note that by Siegel’s formula (Theorem
5.1
(i)),

\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)}=\int_{X_{d}}\widehat{\chi}_{B}\,d\mu_{d}\geq\int_{\{\widehat{\chi}_{B}\geq 2\}}\widehat{\chi}_{B}\,d\mu_{d}\geq 2\cdot\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B}).

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
. For every bounded and balanced Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we have

\Big|\,\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})-\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}\,\Big|\leq\Big(\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}\Big)^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, we have
\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})\leq\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}
, so we only need to show that

\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})\geq\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}-\Big(\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}\Big)^{2}.

(5.3)

We note that

\int_{X_{d}}\widehat{\chi}_{B}\,d\mu_{d}=\int_{X_{d}}\chi_{\{\widehat{\chi}_{B}>0\}}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{B}\,d\mu_{d}\leq\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})^{1/2}\cdot\Big(\int_{X_{d}}\widehat{\chi}_{B}^{2}\,d\mu_{d}\Big)^{1/2},

and thus, by Theorem

(i) and (ii).

\Big(\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)}\Big)^{2}\leq\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})\cdot\Big(2\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)}+\Big(\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{\zeta(d)}\Big)^{2}\Big).

From this we conclude that

\mu_{d}(\Omega_{B})\geq\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}}\geq\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}-\Big(\frac{\Vol_{d}(B)}{2\zeta(d)}\Big)^{2},

where we in the last inequality used the trivial inequality
\frac{1}{1+t}\geq 1-t

for all
t\geq 0
.
∎

Appendix A
Weibull asymptotics implies a logarithm Law

Let
(X,\mu)
be a probability space and let
f_{T}
, with
T\in\mathscr{T}_{d}
, be positive measurable functions on
X
. We
assume
that the minima of these functions are Weibull distributed, i.e. we suppose that
there exist
a,c>0
,
b\geq 0
such that
given any sequence of finite subsets
\Delta_{n}
of
\mathscr{T}_{d}

satisfying

|\Delta_{n}|\to\infty\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\displaystyle\min_{T\neq T^{\prime}\in\Delta_{n}}\max_{p}|\log T_{p}-\log T^{\prime}_{p}|}{\log|\Delta_{n}|}\to\infty,

(A.1)

the minima

\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}:=\min\big\{f_{T}:\,T\in\Delta_{n}\big\}

satisfy

|\Delta_{n}|^{a}(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{b}\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}{\mathrel{\to}_{\mu}}\Wei\big(c,a\big).

Proposition A.1
.

Under the above assumption, for every
\delta<d-1
and
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,

\liminf_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x)=0.

In particular, it follows from Theorem
1.3
that the Minkowski constants (
1.1
) satisfy

\liminf_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{\frac{d-1}{d}-\varepsilon}c_{B_{T}}(\Lambda)=0\quad\hbox{for all $\varepsilon>0$ and $\mu_{d}$-a.e. $\Lambda\in X_{d}$}

(cf. (
1.4
)). Also from Theorem
1.6
we obtain that

\liminf_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{d-1-\varepsilon}m_{B_{T}}(\Lambda)=0\quad\hbox{for all $\varepsilon>0$ and $\mu_{d}$-a.e. $\Lambda\in X_{d}$}.

In particular, there exist
v_{i}\in\Lambda
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\,(\log\|v_{i}\|_{\infty})^{d-1-\varepsilon}\Pi(v_{i})=0

(cf. (
1.6
)).

Proof of Proposition
A.1
.

For the sake of a contradiction, we assume that there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\mu\big(\{x\in X:\,{\liminf}_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x)>c_{1}\}\big)>c_{2}.

For a finite subset
\Delta\subset\mathscr{T}_{d}
, we write

\displaystyle\rho(\Delta):=\max\{(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}:\,T\in\Delta\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}(x):=\min\{f_{T}(x):\,T\in\Delta\}.

We observe that

\liminf_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x)>c_{1}

means that there exists
T_{0}(x)
such that

(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x)>c_{1}\quad\hbox{when $\|T\|_{\infty}\geq T_{0}(x)$.}

In particular, when
\|T\|_{\infty}\geq T_{0}(x)
for all
T\in\Delta
,

\rho(\Delta)\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}(x)\geq\min\{(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x):\,T\in\Delta\}>c_{1}.

Let us pick a sequence of finite subsets
\Delta_{n}
such that

\min\{\|T\|_{\infty}:\,T\in\Delta_{n}\}\to\infty\quad\hbox{ as $n\to\infty$.}

(A.2)

Then the set

\{x\in X:\,\exists T_{0}(x):\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x)>c_{1}\hbox{ when $\|T\|_{\infty}\geq T_{0}(x)$}\}

is contained in

\{x\in X:\,\exists n_{0}(x):\,\rho(\Delta_{n})\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(x)>c_{1}\hbox{ for all $n\geq n_{0}(x)$}\}.

In particular, we conclude that

\mu\big(\{x\in X:\,\exists n_{0}(x):\,\rho(\Delta_{n})\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(x)>c_{1}\hbox{ for all $n\geq n_{0}(x)$}\}\big)>c_{2}.

Setting

\Omega_{n}:=\{x\in X:\,\rho(\Delta_{n})\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(x)>c_{1}\},

we conclude that
\mu(\liminf\Omega_{n})>c_{2}
. This implies that

\mu(\Omega_{n})>c_{2}\quad\hbox{ for all sufficiently large $n$.}

(A.3)

Let us for now additionally assume that the sets
\Delta_{n}
satisfy (
A.1
) and

\frac{|\Delta_{n}|^{a}(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{b}}{\rho(\Delta_{n})}\to\infty.

(A.4)

Below we show that such sequences of sets really do exist.

We choose
u\in\mathbb{R}
so that
e^{-(\lambda u)^{a}}<c_{2}
.
Since
c_{1}\frac{|\Delta_{n}|^{a}(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{b}}{\rho(\Delta_{n})}>u

for sufficiently large
n
, we deduce that

\mu(\Omega_{n})\leq\mu\big(\{x\in X:\,|\Delta_{n}|^{a}(\log|\Delta_{n}|)^{b}\,\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta_{n}}(x)>u\}\big)\to e^{-(\lambda u)^{a}}<c_{2}.

However, this contradicts (
A.3
). Therefore, we conclude that
for all
c_{1}>0
,

\mu\big(\{x\in X:\,{\liminf}_{\|T\|_{\infty}\to\infty}\,(\log\|T\|_{\infty})^{a\delta}f_{T}(x)>c_{1}\}\big)=0.

This implies the proposition, provided that we can show that we can construct
(\Delta_{n})
satisfying
the conditions (
A.1
), (
A.2
), (
A.4
). For this we use parameters
\ell_{n},\theta_{n}\to\infty
. Let

\Delta_{n}:=\big\{T(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d-1}):\,1\leq s_{1},\ldots,s_{d-1}\leq\ell_{n}\big\},

where

T(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d-1}):=\big(\theta_{n}^{s_{1}},\ldots,\theta_{n}^{s_{d-1}},\theta_{n}^{-\sum_{k}s_{k}}\big).

Then
|\Delta_{n}|=\ell_{n}^{d-1}\to\infty
, and the condition (
A.1
) is equivalent
to
\frac{\log\theta_{n}}{\log\ell_{n}}\to\infty
, so that

\theta_{n}=\ell_{n}^{\omega_{n}}
with
\omega_{n}\to\infty
.
The condition (
A.2
) is obviously satisfied,
and the condition (
A.4
) holds when

\frac{\ell_{n}^{a(d-1)}}{\log(\theta_{n}^{\ell_{n}})^{a\delta}}=\frac{\ell_{n}^{a(d-1)-a\delta}}{(\omega_{n}\log\ell_{n})^{a\delta}}\to\infty.

Since
\delta<d-1
, this can be arranged by taking for instance
\omega_{n}=\log\ell_{n}
.
∎

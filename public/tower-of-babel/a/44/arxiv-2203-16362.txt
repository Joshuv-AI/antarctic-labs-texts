The scaled-invariant Planckian metal and quantum criticality in Ce 1-x Nd x CoIn 5
Yung-Yeh Chang, Hechang Lei, C. Petrovic, Chung-Hou Chung

Abstract
Perfect T T -linear resistivity associated with universal scattering rate: 1 / τ = α ​ k B ​ T / ( h / 2 ​ π ) 1/\tau=\alpha k_{B}T/(h/2\pi) with α ∼ 1 \alpha\sim 1 , so-called Planckian metal state, has been observed in the normal state of a variety of strongly correlated superconductors close to a quantum critical point. However, the microscopic origin of this intriguing phenomena and its link to quantum criticality still remains an outstanding open problem. In this work, we observe the quantum-critical T / B T/B -scaling of the Planckian metal state in the resistivity and heat capacity of heavy-electron superconductor Ce 1-x Nd x CoIn 5 in magnetic fields near the edge of antiferromagnetism, driven by critical Kondo hybridization at the critical doping x c ∼ 0.03 x_{c}\sim 0.03 . We further provide the first microscopic mechanism to account for the Planckian state in a quantum critical system based on the critical charge fluctuations near Kondo breakdown transition at x c x_{c} within the quasi-two-dimensional Kondo-Heisenberg lattice model. This mechanism simultaneously captures the observed universal Planckian scattering rate as well as the quantum-critical scaling and power-law divergence in thermodynamic observables near criticality. Our mechanism is generic to Planckian metal states in a variety of quantum critical superconductors near Kondo destruction.



Metallic behavior that goes against the Landau’s Fermi liquid paradigm for ordinary metals has commonly been observed in a wide variety of strongly interacting quantum materials and yet the emergence of such metals is poorly understood. This unconventional metallic or non-Fermi liquid (NFL) behavior often exists near a magnetic quantum phase transition, and shows “strange metal (SM)” phenomena with (quasi-)linear-in-temperature resistivity and singular logarithmic-in-temperature specific heat coefficient
[
1
,
2
]
.

The Planckian metal state constitutes a particularly intriguing class of SM states and has been observed in the normal state of unconventional superconductors, including cuprate superconductors
[
3
]
, iron pnictides and chalcogenides
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
, organic
[

[

12

13

[

T

\alpha\sim 1

[
15

10

16

17

\alpha

[
18
]

[
17

\alpha

T

\alpha\sim 1

F(h\omega/2\pi k_{B}T)=F(\omega\tau_{Pl})
at criticality
[
17
]

Here,we propose a microscopic mechanism to account for the above phenomena based on the interplay between Kondo screening
of a local
f
-electron (
f_{i\sigma}
on site
i
with spin
\sigma
), by mobile electrons (
c_{i\sigma}
), and the AF correlations between nearest-neighbor spins of local
f
-electrons in the form of quasi-
2d
(
d=2+\eta
with
0<\eta<1
) Kondo-Heisenberg (KH) model (see
Methods
). This mechanism has been used to qualitatively describe the AF QCP at
x_{c}
inside the superconducting dome of CeRhIn
5
under pressure
[
31
,
32
]
, which bears a striking similarity to that for Ce
1-x
Nd
x
CoIn
5
at zero field under Nd doping.
The Anderson’s resonating-valence-bond (RVB) spin-liquid state
[
33
]
, defined by the spatially homogeneous bosonic spin-singlet pair,
\Delta_{RVB}\equiv J_{H}\sum_{\sigma}\langle\text{sgn}(\sigma)f^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}f^{\dagger}_{j,-\sigma}\rangle
, on nearest-neighbor sites
i,\,j
, is introduced here for the AF Heisenberg term with the exchange coupling
J_{H}
. The Kondo hybridization is described by the spatially homogeneous bosonic
\chi
field where, at the mean-field level,
\chi\equiv J_{K}\sum_{\sigma}\langle f^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}\rangle
with Kondo coupling
J_{K}
.
At the edge of antiferromagnetism, the Kondo effect not only stablizes the RVB spin liquid against the magnetic long-ranged order by partially sharing the
f
-electron spins
[
34
]
, but also introduces hoping of the RVB bonds to the conduction band to form charged Cooper pairs. This leads to a Kondo-RVB coexisting heavy-electron superconducting state with estimated transition temperature
T_{c}\sim\chi^{2}\Delta_{RVB}
, in quantitatively good agreement with the experimental observation
[
35
]
.

Via the competition between Kondo and RVB physics, an AF-KB QCP was predicted inside the superconducting dome, qualitatively describing the phase transition between AF-superconducting coexisting phase and a pure superconducting phase observed in CeRhIn
5
[
35
]
, similar to our case here. By analyzing the amplitude fluctuations of the Kondo and RVB correlations beyond mean-field level via renormalization group (RG) analysis
[
36
,
35
]
(see Supplementary Notes, section
S.IV
), this mechanism provides a qualitative and semi-quantitative understanding of the SM properties in CeCoIn
5
in fields and pressure near the AF-KB QCP
[
21
,
26
,
25
]
. In particular, it captures the observed
T
-linear resistivity in terms of critical Kondo (charge) fluctuations via the electron-phonon-like interaction,
\hat{H}_{K}\sim J_{K}\sum_{i\sigma}\left(c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}\hat{\chi}_{i}+H.c.\right)
(see Eq. (
1
) below and Eq. (
7
) in
Methods
), and a power-law-in-
T
divergence in
\gamma(T)
via both fluctuations. Meanwhile, the critical Kondo coupling therein depends on the anomalous dimension
\eta
of our quasi-
2d
model (see below). This quasi-
2d
nature of our theoretical framework, essential to the KB transition, is consistent with the quasi-
2d
nature of the Fermi surface in CeCoIn
5

[
37
]
as well as the dimensional crossover in Fermi surface evolution of the
\alpha
-band from
2d
-like to
3d
-like in our system
[
27
]
with increasing Nd concentrations observed in quantum oscillations. By analyzing the data of quantum oscillation in Ref.
[
27
]
, we observed a rapid change in charge carrier

across
x_{c}
as
T\to 0
(see Table

), a possible signature of a jump in Fermi surface volume at ground state. This signature is consistent with our KB QCP scenario. Interestingly, evidence of a delocalization (KB) transition has been observed in a closely related compound CeCo(In

)

To account for the Planckian scattering rate, we first computed the electrical resistivity
\rho(T)
near the KB QCP via electron scattering rate
1/\tau
, proportional to imaginary part of conduction electron self energy, contributed from the critical Kondo fluctuations within the Boltzmann transport theory and renormalized perturbation.
We find the perfect
T
-linear resistivity with precise form of Kondo-coupling-dependent
A_{1}
coefficient, given by,

\displaystyle\rho(T)=\rho_{0}+A_{1}T,

\displaystyle\rho_{0}=\frac{m^{\star}}{ne^{2}}\left(\frac{\pi}{N_{0}(h/2\pi)}\right),\,A_{1}=\frac{m^{\star}}{ne^{2}}\left(\frac{Y}{\pi j_{K}^{2}}\right)\frac{k_{B}}{(h/2\pi)}

(1)

where
j_{K}=J_{K}N_{0}
is the dimensionless Kondo coupling with
N_{0}
being the conduction electron density of states at the Fermi energy,
j^{\star}_{K}=\sqrt{\eta}
at the KB QCP, and the constant
Y\approx 1.39
(see Ref.
[
36
]
and Supplementary Notes, sections
S.V
and
S.V.1
). From Eq. (
1
), we have
\alpha=Y/\pi j_{K}^{2}

[
35
]
, consistent with the relation
\alpha\propto 1/T_{\text{coh}}\propto 1/j_{K}^{2}

[
39
]
(see Supplementary Notes, section
S.II
). At criticality, we have

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{Y}{\pi\eta}.

(2)

Surprisingly, though
\alpha
depends on microscopic coupling constant, at QCP it reduces to depend only on the anomalous spatial dimension
\eta
, a macroscopic constant. By properly choosing the fitting parameter
\eta\approx 0.45
, corresponding to a critical Kondo coupling
j_{K}^{\star}\approx 0.7
, the Planckian limit
\alpha\approx 1
is recovered. The value of
j_{K}^{\star}
agrees reasonably well with the ARPES measurement for CeCoIn
5
:
j_{K}=-\left[\ln(k_{B}T^{*}_{\text{onset}}/D)\right]^{-1}\approx 0.62
with
D\sim 600

K being the onset temperature for Kondo correlation
[

,
40
]
.
The approximated constant value of
\alpha\sim O(1)
seen for
0\leq x\leq 0.05
(with an error bar, see Table
1
) can therefore be understood as the Kondo hybridization being close to quantum critical. This is also in good agreement with the same slope observed in linear-in-
T

(or Kondo correlation) with increasing Nd doping (see Fig.

and Supplementary Notes, section

). The

-linear resistivity we theoretically obtain here can be understood as a generic feature of gapless real-valued bosons (quadratically dispersed bosonic Kondo hybridization

. However, unlike the high-temperature electron-phonon effect, the linear-in-

and the Kondo coupling

\alpha\propto A_{1}\propto 1/j_{K}^{2}
, as well as
\alpha\sim 1

-linear scattering rate and critical Kondo hybridization in our system.

A few remarks are made before we conclude. Firstly, since
m^{\star}
is likely temperature dependent in the SM region, Planckian scattering rate is reached only near the lower end of the linear-in-
T
resistivity region where
m^{\star}
is well-approximated by a temperature-independent constant extracted from quantum oscillation measurement in the Fermi-liquid region
[
17
]
. Secondly, though the Drude formula has been widely used to relate the transport and scattering rate in the Fermi-liquid region and near the lower end of the
T
-linear region, whether it is valid deep in the non-Fermi liquid SM region is an open question
[
17
,
41
]
. Finally, while quantum oscillation measurement is widely used to estimate
n
and
m^{\star}
, different approach by Hall coefficient and
A
coefficient associated with the Fermi-liquid behavior with
T^{2}
resistivity has been used
[
41
]
.

In summary, we observe the quantum-critical scaling of the Planckian metal state in the resistivity and specific heat coefficient of heavy-electron superconductor Ce
1-x
Nd
x
CoIn
5
under fields.
Clear experimental evidences are shown to support the notion of Kondo hybridization being quantum critical at
x_{c}\sim 0.03
.
Our proposed mechanism based on the critical Kondo fluctuations simultaneously describes remarkably well the observed universal
T
-linear Planckian scattering rate with
\alpha\sim 1
, the quantum critical scaling in resistivity and specific heat coefficient as well as the power-law divergence in specific heat coefficient. The
\alpha
coefficient is found to in general depend microscopically on the Kondo hybridization. Surprisingly, however at criticality, it depends only on the quasi-
2d
nature of the system and reaches the universal Planckian limit. Our observation and proposed mechanism offers the first microscopic understanding of the Planckian dissipation limit in a quantum critical system.
This generic mechanism is relevant for other heavy-fermion quantum critical superconductors showing Planckian metal states, such as CeRhIn
5
and related compounds. Our results motivate further experimental study, such as the Hall coefficient measurement on the signatures of Kondo breakdown near the critical Nd doping as well as the fundamental issue on whether quantum critical systems with
T
-linear resistivity, in general, implies the Planckain metals.

Methods

A microscopic model.
Our start point is the microscopic large-
N
(Sp(
N
)) Kondo-Heisenberg Hamiltonian
H=H_{0}+H_{K}+H_{J}
, where

\displaystyle H_{0}=\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle;\sigma}\Big[t_{ij}c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}+H.c.\Big]-\sum_{i\sigma}\,\mu\,c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{i\sigma},

\displaystyle H_{J}=\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\frac{J_{ij}}{N}\mathbold{S}_{i}^{f}\cdot\mathbold{S}_{j}^{f},\hskip 11.49994ptH_{K}=\frac{J_{K}}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbold{S}_{i}^{f}\cdot\mathbold{s}^{c}.

(4)

In Eq. (
4
),
H_{0}
describes hopping of conduction (
c_{i\sigma}
) electrons. The antiferromagnetic Heisenberg interaction between two impurity-spin (
\mathbold{S}^{f}
) of the electrons occupying the localized
4f
orbitals of neighboring sites is described by
H_{J}
and the Kondo screening of impurity spins by conduction electrons is captured by
H_{K}
. Via Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation,
H_{K}
and
H_{J}
can be factorized as

\displaystyle H_{J}\to\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle;\alpha,\beta}\left[\Phi_{ij}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}f_{i\alpha}f_{j\beta}+H.c.\right]+\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}N\frac{|\Phi_{ij}|^{2}}{J_{H}}\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle H_{K}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i,\penalty\ \sigma}\left[\left(c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}\right)\chi_{i}+H.c.\right]+\sum_{i}\,\frac{|\chi_{i}|^{2}}{J_{K}}.

(5)

Here, the local spin operator
\mathbold{S}^{f}_{i}
is represented in terms of the constraint fermionic Sp(
N
) fields,
f_{i\alpha}
, which needs to be subjected to the constraint,
\langle\sum_{\sigma}f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}\rangle=N\kappa
, to ensure its local nature. The constant
\kappa<1
allows us to capture the valence fluctuations. Thus, to capture the local constraint, an additional term has to be included in the Hamiltonian,
H_{\lambda}=\sum_{i,\sigma}\lambda\Big[f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}-Q\Big]
with
\lambda
being the Lagrange multiplier.
In the
H_{J}
term, we assume an uniform antiferromagnetic RKKY coupling
J_{ij}=J_{H}
on a lattice where
i,\penalty\ j
are nearest-neighbor site indices,
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}=\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\beta}=-\mathcal{J}^{\beta\alpha}
denotes the anti-symmetric tensor,
\sigma,\alpha,\beta\in\{-\frac{N}{2},\cdots,\frac{N}{2}\}
label the spin indices. Here,
\chi_{i}
and
\Phi_{ij}
are the spatially dependent Hubbard-Stratonovich fields, where their (spatially uniform) mean-field values,
\chi\equiv\langle\frac{J_{K}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\sigma}f^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}\rangle
and
\Delta_{RVB}\equiv\langle\Phi_{ij}\rangle=\langle\frac{J_{H}}{N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\beta}f^{\alpha\dagger}_{i}f^{\beta\dagger}_{j}\rangle
, can be used as order parameters to describe the Kondo-screened heavy Fermi liquid state and the RVB spin-liquid state.

While considering the fluctuations of
\chi_{i}
and
\Phi_{ij}
beyond mean-field level, we can express
\chi_{i}\to\chi+J_{K}\hat{\chi}_{i}
and
\Phi_{ij}\to\Delta_{RVB}+J_{H}\hat{\Phi}_{ij}
, where
\hat{\chi}_{i}
and
\hat{\Phi}_{ij}
denote the fluctuation of
\chi
and
\Delta_{RVB}
. Both
H_{K}
and
H_{J}
can be divided into the mean-field and fluctuating parts, namely
H_{K}\to H^{MF}_{K}+\hat{H}_{K}
and
H_{J}\to H^{MF}_{J}+\hat{H}_{J}
, with

\displaystyle H_{J}^{MF}=\Delta_{RVB}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle;\alpha\beta}\left[\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}f_{i\alpha}f_{j\beta}+H.c.\right]+4NN_{s}\frac{\Delta_{RVB}^{2}}{J_{H}}\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle H_{K}^{MF}=\frac{\chi}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i\sigma}\left[c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}+H.c.\right]+\frac{N\chi^{2}}{J_{K}},

(6)

at mean-field, and

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{J}=J_{H}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle;\alpha\beta}\left(\hat{\Phi}_{ij}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}f_{i\alpha}f_{j\beta}+H.c.\right)+\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}NJ_{H}|\hat{\Phi}_{ij}|^{2}\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{K}=\frac{J_{K}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i\sigma}\left(c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}\hat{\chi}_{i}+H.c.\right)+\sum_{i}\,J_{K}|\hat{\chi}_{i}|^{2}

(7)

beyond mean-field level.

The microscopic model relevant for the heavy-fermion materials therefore becomes
H\to H_{0}+H_{\lambda}+H_{J}^{MF}+H_{K}^{MF}+\hat{H}_{K}+\hat{H}_{J}
.

In this section, we provide derivation of the relevant equations for carrier concentration

We start from the formula of quantum oscillation frequency

, given by

\displaystyle F=\frac{(h/2\pi)}{2\pi e}S_{F}

(S4)

with

being the extremal cross-sectional area of the Fermi surface. For simplicity, we assume a circular cross-section of Fermi surface here, hence

with

being the “averaged” Fermi wave vector of the circular Fermi surface. This links the dHvA frequency and the “average” Fermi wave vector of the Fermi surface by

F
and the carrier concentration

through

. While considering the effective dimensionality of critical modes, the carrier concentration takes the following form

]

\displaystyle n=\frac{2k_{F}}{\pi d_{b}d_{c}}=\frac{2}{\pi d_{b}d_{c}}\sqrt{\frac{2eF}{h/2\pi}}\quad(\text{for 1d}),

\displaystyle n=\frac{k_{F}^{2}}{2\pi d_{c}}=\frac{1}{2\pi d_{c}}\left(\frac{2eF}{h/2\pi}\right)\quad(\text{for 2d}),

\displaystyle n=\frac{k_{F}^{3}}{3\pi^{2}}=\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{2eF}{h/2\pi}\right)^{3/2}\quad(\text{for 3d}),

(S5)

where

and

and

2d

As an example, we provide detailed derivation of carrier concentration for the
2d

S5
) and then generalize this derivation to the case with fractional quasi-

Assume the critical modes occur on the isotropic

\displaystyle\to n=\frac{N}{\mathcal{V}}=\frac{N}{AL_{c}}=\frac{k_{F}^{2}}{2\pi d_{c}},

(S6)

for that along the
c

represents the unit volume in

denotes the sample site along

\displaystyle V_{d}(k_{F})=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(\text{$\frac{d}{2}+1$})}k_{F}^{d}.

For a general

From Eq. (

In this section, we summarize the results of the diagrammatic renormalization group (RG) analysis of the quasi-2D Kondo-Heisenberg lattice model with the inclusion of superconducting fluctuations based on Ref.

The superconducting fluctuations can be effectively captured by adding the following terms in Eq. (

)

The transition of near

and

\hat{{\chi}}_{i}
field with the RVB correlation being treated at the mean-field level, that is to neglect the RVB fluctuations. Near
g_{c1}
, we included the approximate

of Eq. (
S27
) shown below, with an estimated bare superconducting fluctuation coupling constant

being

\displaystyle H_{sc}\to v_{sc}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\hat{\chi}_{i}\hat{\chi}_{j}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}c_{i\alpha}c_{j\beta}+H.c.).

(S28)

The RG
\beta
functions for weak coupling
J_{K}
and
v_{sc}
near

read (see
Methods
and Ref.
[
35
]
for details)

\displaystyle j_{K}^{\prime}=\left(-\frac{\eta}{2}\right)j_{K}+\frac{1}{2}j_{K}^{3},

\displaystyle v_{sc}^{\prime}=-\eta v_{sc}+j_{K}^{2}v_{sc}+12v_{sc}^{3},

(S29)

where

with

and

being the running energy cutoff. In Eq. (

\left(j^{*^{2}}_{K},\penalty\ v^{*^{2}}_{sc}\right)=\left(0,\penalty\ 0\right),\penalty\ \left(\eta,\penalty\ 0\right)

\left(0,\penalty\ \eta/12\right)

[

Close to

S.V
Supplementary Notes: Derivation of the electrical resistivity

In this section, we summarize the derivation of the
T
-linear electrical resistivity based on Ref.
[
36
]
via perturbation with respect to the fluctuating Kondo Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{K}
.

The electrical resistivity
\rho(T)
is obtained via the Boltzmann transport theory, taking the following form:

\displaystyle\sigma(T)=\rho^{-1}(T)=\left(-\frac{ne^{2}}{m^{\star}}\right)\int\tau(\omega)\frac{\partial n_{F}(\omega)}{\partial\omega}d\omega,

(S32)

where the relaxation time
\tau
is directly related to the imaginary part of the conduction electron self-energy
\text{Im}\Sigma_{c}
, defined as

\displaystyle\tau(\omega)=-\frac{(h/2\pi)}{2\text{Im}\Sigma_{c}(\omega)}.

(S33)

The imaginary part of conduction electron self-energy up to a one-loop order perturbation in
\hat{H}_{K}
is given by

\displaystyle\text{Im}\Sigma_{c}(\omega)\approx\text{sgn}(\omega)\left(\Omega+\varsigma\omega\right)

(S34)

with
\omega
being measured relative to Fermi energy,
\Omega\equiv\frac{\Lambda\pi}{2N}
and
\varsigma\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi Nj_{K}^{2}}
.
While deriving Eq. (
S34
), we further assume that
\Lambda/\Gamma\gg 1
and
\omega/\Gamma\ll 1
(or equivalently,
j_{K}^{2}\ll 1
and
\omega/\Lambda\ll j_{K}^{2}
). See Ref.
[
36
]
for details.

S.V.1
Derivation of electrical resistivity

Plugging the result of
\text{Im}\Sigma_{c}(\omega)
into Eq. (
S32
), electrical conductivity due to fluctuating Kondo scattering can be obtained as

\displaystyle\sigma(T)=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{ne^{2}(h/2\pi)}{2m^{\star}}\right)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\Omega+\varsigma\omega}\right)\frac{\partial n_{F}(\omega)}{\partial\omega}d\omega\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.+\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\frac{-1}{\Omega+\varsigma\omega}\right)\frac{\partial n_{F}(\omega)}{\partial\omega}d\omega\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ne^{2}(h/2\pi)}{2m^{\star}\Omega}-\left(\frac{ne^{2}(h/2\pi)\varsigma}{2m^{\star}\Omega^{2}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\beta\left|\omega\right|e^{\beta\omega}}{\left(1+e^{\beta\omega}\right)^{2}}d\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ne^{2}(h/2\pi)}{2m^{\star}\Omega}-\left(\frac{ne^{2}(h/2\pi)\varsigma Y}{2m^{\star}\Omega^{2}}\right)k_{B}T.

(S35)

Here,
Y=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\left|x\right|e^{x}}{\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{2}}dx\approx 1.39
is a constant.

Substituting for the constants
\Omega
and
\varsigma
, the linear-in-
T
part of the resistivity, denoted as
\rho_{L}(T)
, at sufficiently low temperatures can be proved to be inversely proportional to square of the Kondo coupling:

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(T)=\frac{m^{\star}}{ne^{2}}\left(\frac{Y}{\pi Nj_{K}^{2}}\right)\frac{k_{B}T}{(h/2\pi)}\propto\frac{1}{j_{K}^{2}}.

(S36)

In Eq. (
S36
), we have replaced the density of states with the inverse of the conduction-electron bandwidth, namely
N_{0}=\Lambda^{-1}
.

At the QCP, we fix the dimensionless Kondo coupling
j_{K}
in Eq. (
S36
) at its fixed-point value, which is obtained via the RG analysis (see Supplementary Notes, section
S.IV
and Ref.
[
36
,
35
]
):
j_{K}^{2}\to\left(j_{K}^{\star}\right)^{2}=\eta\equiv d-z
with
d
beig the spatial dimension and
z
being the dynamical exponent, which can be regarded as the effective dimension of time. This leads to

\displaystyle\alpha\equiv\frac{Y}{\pi N\eta}.

(S37)

Note that the value of
\eta
is treated as a fitting parameter.

Taking the physical SU(2) limit (namely
N=1
), our result gives
\alpha\approx 0.98
with
\eta\approx 0.45
for the Nd-doped CeCoIn
5
[
35
]
.

S.VI
Supplementary Notes: The electronic specific heat coefficient

The electronic specific heat coefficient in the strange metal state is mainly contributed from the quadratic Gaussian fluctuation of the
\hat{{\chi}}{k}
field, the Fourier component of the fluctuation of
\chi_{i}
in Eq. (
5
), whose dynamics is described by the Hamiltonian
H_{G}
, given by

\displaystyle H_{G}=\sum_{\mathbold{k}}\hat{{\chi}}{k}\left[\varepsilon_{\chi}(\mathbold{k})+m_{\chi}\right]\hat{{\chi}}{k}

(S38)

with
\varepsilon_{\chi}(\mathbold{k})
being the dispersion of the
\hat{\chi}{k}
field which is obtained via calculating its leading-order self-energy correction and
m_{\chi}
being the effective mass of
\hat{\chi}
. Note that, at the bare level, the
\hat{\chi}
field does not acquire dynamics.

To proceed, we evaluate the ensemble average of internal energy
\bar{E}_{G}
for
\hat{{\chi}}{k}
, given by

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{G}=\sum{k}\frac{\varepsilon_{\chi}(\mathbold{k})}{e^{\beta\left[\varepsilon_{\chi}(\mathbold{k})+m_{\chi}\right]}-1}=\,W_{\chi}\,\int_{m_{\chi}}^{\Lambda}\,d\varepsilon\,\frac{\varepsilon^{1+\eta/2}}{e^{\beta\varepsilon}-1}.

(S39)

Here,
V
denotes the system volume,
\beta\equiv 1/T
,
\Lambda
is the upper energy cutoff scale and

\displaystyle W_{\chi}\equiv\frac{V\Omega_{d}}{2}\left(\frac{m^{\star}\lambda}{\pi N_{0}J_{K}^{2}}\right)^{1+\eta/2}

(S40)

denotes the density of states for the
\hat{{\chi}}{k}
field with
\Omega_{d}\equiv 2\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2)
being the solid angle of a
d
-dimensional sphere and
\Gamma(n)
being the gamma function with argument
n
. The specific heat coefficient can be obtained as

\displaystyle\gamma(T)\equiv\frac{1}{T}\frac{\partial\bar{E}_{G}}{\partial T}=\frac{W_{\chi}}{4T^{3}}\int^{\Lambda}_{m_{\chi}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2+\eta/2}}{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2T}\right)}d\varepsilon.

(S41)

However, the parameters shown in Eqs. (
S40
) and (
S41
) such as the effective mass
m_{\chi}
,
\lambda
or
J_{K}
are at the bare level. Reasonable results of specific heat coefficient are obtained after all the bare parameters are replaced with the rescaled ones:
J_{K}\to J_{K}e^{-\eta l/2}
,
m_{\chi}\to m_{\chi}e^{2l}
and
\lambda\to\lambda e^{\eta l/2}
. Note that, we also need to rescale temperature, given by
T(l)\equiv T_{l}=Te^{zl}
with
l
being the scaling parameter and
z
being the dynamical exponent
[
43
,
44
]
. Since the conduction band does not get renormalized, thus
m^{\star}
remains un-rescaled. Here, the density of states for the conduction electrons is expressed as
N_{0}\sim m^{\star}k_{F}^{d-2}
, and is thus scale invariant at two dimension. Details of the rescaling of the parameters and temperature near the QCP are given in Ref.
[
36
]
.

The specific heat coefficient contributed from the fluctuations of the Kondo hybridization sector should be evaluated near the critical fixed point at which
m_{\chi}
is renormalized to of order of one,
m_{\chi}(l=l_{0})\sim O(1)
where
l_{0}
denotes the scaling factor of renormalization at which the above relation is satisfied, i.e.
m_{\chi}(l_{0})\sim O(1)\Rightarrow 1=m_{\chi}e^{z\,l_{0}}=m_{\chi}\xi^{z}
with
m_{\chi}
here being the bare mass of the
\hat{{\chi}}
-field,
\xi
being the correlation length, and
T(l=l_{0})=Te^{zl_{0}}\sim T\xi^{z}\sim T/T_{LFL}
with
T_{LFL}
being the Fermi-liquid crossover. Taking the renormalized parameters into accounting, the (renormalized) specific heat coefficient
\gamma(T)\equiv C_{V}/T
is obtained by the definition

\displaystyle\frac{C_{V}}{T}

\displaystyle=\,e^{-\eta l_{0}}\,W_{\chi}\,\int^{\Lambda}_{m_{\chi}^{l}}\,d\varepsilon_{l}\,\,\frac{\beta_{l}^{2}\,\varepsilon_{l}^{2+\eta/2}\,e^{\beta_{l}\varepsilon_{l}}}{\left(e^{\beta_{l}\varepsilon_{l}}-1\right)^{2}}\Bigg|_{l=l_{0}}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{4}\,A(l_{0})\,\left(\frac{T}{T_{LFL}}\right)^{\eta/2}\,\int^{m_{\chi}\Lambda/T}_{m_{\chi}/T}\,dx\,\frac{x^{2+\eta/2}}{\sinh^{2}(x/2)}

(S42)

with
x\equiv\varepsilon_{l}/T_{l}
,
m_{\chi}^{l}\equiv m_{\chi}(l)
,
\varepsilon_{l}\equiv\varepsilon(l)
. In Eq. (
S42
),
A(l_{0})=e^{-\eta l_{0}}W_{\chi}
is a non-universal parameter while the integral part is a universal scaling function.
A(l_{0})
and
m_{\chi}
in Eq. (
S42
) are treated as numerical fitting parameters. In the above derivations, the relations
e^{l_{0}}=\xi
and
\xi^{z}\sim T_{LFL}^{-1}\sim(g-g_{c})^{-1}\sim(J_{K}-J_{K}^{\ast})^{-1}
have been used since the RG flows along the direction of
J_{K}
with
J_{H}
fixed at
J_{H}^{\ast}
dominates the critical properties.

We now examine the scaling behavior of the prefactor
A(l_{0})
, which will be shown to exhibit a power-law behavior of the specific heat coefficient in
g-g_{c}
near the quantum critical point. Replacing the bare
\lambda
and
J_{K}
in Eq. (
S40
) with the resclaed ones shown above and using the relation
e^{l_{0}}\sim(g-g_{c})^{-1/2}
yields

\displaystyle A(l_{0})=

\displaystyle e^{l_{0}}W_{\chi}(l=l_{0})\propto e^{l_{0}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{J_{K}^{2}}\Bigg|_{l=l_{0}}\right)^{1+\eta/2}

\displaystyle\propto(g-g_{c})^{\eta/2}\left[\frac{(g-g_{c})^{-\eta/4}}{(g-g_{c})^{\eta/2}}\right]^{1+\eta/2}

\displaystyle=(g-g_{c})^{-\beta}

(S43)

with
\beta=3\eta^{2}/8+\eta/4
. This shows that the specific heat coefficient exhibits a power-law behavior in
g-g_{c}
with fixed temperature near the QCP.

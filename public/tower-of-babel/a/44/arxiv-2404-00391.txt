Robust time-discretisation and linearisation schemes for singular and degenerate evolution systems modelling biofilm growth
R.K.H. Smeets, K. Mitra, I.S. Pop, S. Sonner

Abstract
We propose and analyse numerical schemes for a system of quasilinear, degenerate evolution equations modelling biofilm growth as well as other processes such as flow through porous media and the spreading of wildfires. The first equation in the system is parabolic and exhibits degenerate and singular diffusion, while the second is either uniformly parabolic or an ordinary differential equation. First, we introduce a semi-implicit time discretisation that has the benefit of decoupling the equations. We prove the positivity, boundedness, and convergence of the time-discrete solutions to the time-continuous solution. Then, we introduce an iterative linearisation scheme to solve the resulting nonlinear time-discrete problems. Under weak assumptions on the time-step size, we prove that the scheme converges irrespective of the space discretisation and mesh. Moreover, if the problem is non-degenerate, the convergence becomes faster as the time-step size decreases. Finally, employing the finite element method for the spatial discretisation, we study the behaviour of the scheme, and compare its performance to other commonly used schemes. These tests confirm that the proposed scheme is robust and fast. Keywords: degenerate diffusion; time discretisation; linearisation; unconditional convergence; stability; biofilm models; porous medium equation MSC: 65M12, 65M22, 35K51, 35K65



1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Let
T>0
be a maximal time and
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
(
d\in\mathbb{N}
) be a bounded Lipschitz domain. With
Q=\Omega\times(0,T]
denoting the parabolic space-time cylinder, we consider
the following class of degenerate quasilinear parabolic systems

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\Delta\Phi(u)+f(v)u,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v=\mu\nabla\cdot\left(D(u)\nabla v\right)+g(u,v)

(1.1b)

in
Q
.
The first equation (
1.1a
) describes evolution of a population density
u
and exhibits degenerate and possibly also singular diffusion leading to the formation of free boundaries propagating at a finite speed. More specifically, the monotone function
\Phi
vanishes as
u
tends to zero and can in addition have a singularity as
u
tends to its maximum value. The diffusion coefficient

D
in the second equation which describes evolution of a substrate concentration
v
is assumed non-degenerate, i.e. it is bounded above and below by positive constants.
However, the mobility coefficient
\mu

appearing in (
1.1b
) may be either 0 or 1, leading
to either a coupled system of a parabolic equation and an ordinary differential equation (if
\mu=0
), or two parabolic equations (if
\mu=1
). The growth and spreading of the population
u
might depend on multiple substrates. Nevertheless, for simplicity, we consider only one substrate
v
in (
1.1b
), as
an extension to multiple substrates is straightforward.
The system is completed by the initial conditions
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}
and
v(\cdot,0)=v_{0}
for given functions
u_{0},v_{0}
, and by homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for
u
and, if
\mu\neq 0
, also for
v
.
Analysing system (
1.1
) analytically and numerically is challenging due to the
degenerate and singular diffusion in the first equation which leads to free boundaries and steep gradients, and the nonlinear coupling with the second equation.

The main motivation for this work comes from the biofilm growth models in
[
13
,
15
]
, where
the solution component
u
in (
1.1a
) describes the (normalized) biomass density whose evolution is dictated by the diffusion operator

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi(u)=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{u^{\alpha}}{(1-u)^{\beta}}\nabla u\right),\qquad\text{ for some }\qquad\alpha,\beta\geq 1.

(1.2)

With this, (
1.1a
)
is coupled to a reaction-diffusion partial differential equation (PDE) or an ordinary differential equation (ODE) modelling the evolution of
the growth-limiting nutrient concentration
v
.
In the resulting model, the biofilm occupies the region where

\{u>0\}
.
Observe that the
biomass diffusion in
(
1.2
) shows a degeneracy of porous-medium type as
u
approaches
0
, ensuring a finite speed of
propagation of the interface between the biofilm and the surrounding region, as well as a singularity as
u
approaches
1
. The latter implies that the solution
u
remains bounded by a constant strictly less than 1 despite the growth term
f
in the equation. The second equation, (
1.1b
), describes the evolution of the nutrient concentration.
The case
\mu=0
corresponds to immobile substrates (e.g., in the case of cellulolytic biofilms,
[
13
]
), while
\mu=1
corresponds to diffusive substrates (e.g., whenever biofilms grow in an aqueous medium,
[
15
]
).
The biofilm growth model was also extended to take multiple substrates into account, both mobile and immobile, like in
[
17
,
27
]
. The results of our paper generalise directly to these cases.
More complex multi-species biofilm models including cross-diffusion have been studied in
[
11
,
37
]
.

Systems of the form (
1.1
) are not limited to models for biofilm growth. For instance, coupled systems of parabolic and ordinary differential equations appear in the modelling of two-phase or unsaturated flow through porous media when effects like hysteresis or dynamic capillarity are taken into account

[
27
,
30
]
(degenerate, but non-singular diffusion). They also appear in wildfire models
[
38
]
(nonlinear but non-degenerate diffusion), and reaction, diffusion, and adsorption/desorption models in a porous medium
[
21
]
, to name a few other applications.

The aim of this paper is to develop robust, efficient, and structure-preserving time discretisation and linearisation methods for System (
1.1
) relying on minimal regularity assumptions, and converging even for degenerate and singular diffusion. In what follows, the time discretisation is introduced, as well as the linear iterative schemes. Furthermore, the main results concerning the stability and convergence of these schemes are stated, their proofs being given in the subsequent sections.

1.2
Time discretisation

To define the time discretisation, we take
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\tau=\frac{T}{N}
be the time-step size, which is chosen uniform for the ease of presentation. With
n\in\{0,\dots,N\}
, let
t_{n}=n\tau
be the time-step and denote by
u_{n}
the approximation of
u
at
t=t_{n}
, and similarly for
v_{n}
. Then, we use an Euler semi-implicit approach for the time discretisation. All terms in (
1.1
) are discretised implicitly except for the
reaction functions
f
and
g
, which are discretised semi-implicitly.

Problem 1.1

(Semi-implicit time discretisation)
.

Given the approximate solutions
u_{n-1}
and
v_{n-1}
at time
t_{n-1}
, find the approximate solution pair
(u_{n},v_{n})
at the next time step
t_{n}
by solving the following system

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}(u_{n}-u_{n-1})=\Delta\Phi(u_{n})+f(v_{n-1})u_{n},

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}(v_{n}-v_{n-1})=\mu\nabla\cdot\left(D(u_{n})\nabla v_{n}\right)+g(u_{n},v_{n-1}).

(1.3b)

This approach has several advantages over explicit and implicit discretisations. Explicit methods lead to a loss of regularity of the time-discrete solutions, which results in instability due to the already low regularity triggered by degenerate diffusion. On the other hand, implicit schemes have the advantage that only a
weak restriction on the time-step size
, independent of the space-discretisation, is needed to guarantee stability.
Fully implicit time-discretisation schemes for the mentioned biofilm models were analysed in
[
4
,
12
,
19
,
20
]
. However, for fully implicit schemes, the two time-discrete equations originating from (
1.1
) are coupled which makes them challenging to solve especially using an iterative method. In the semi-implicit approach (
1.3
) the two equations are decoupled. This allows us to solve them sequentially instead of iteratively, i.e. we first solve for
u_{n}
and then update
v_{n}
using the known
u_{n}
. In fact, given
u_{n}
, (
1.3b
) is a linear problem for
v_{n}

which can be solved rather easily. Moreover, with the proposed semi-implicit discretisation we retain the same accuracy and stability one would expect from fully implicit discretisations. Generalising the results in
[
12
]
for the scalar equation (
1.1a
), we show that
under weak assumptions on
\tau
, the discretisation (
1.3
)
is well-posed, the time-discrete solutions preserve positivity, remain bounded, and converge to the time-continuous solutions as
\tau\to 0
.
The exact results are stated in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.
Below, we summarise them omitting technical details.

Theorem

(Well-posedness, boundedness, and convergence of the time-discrete solutions)
.

For all sufficiently small time steps
\tau
, there exists a unique weak solution of (
1.3
).
Moreover, the time-discrete solutions
u_{n},v_{n}
are positive and
u_{n}
is bounded
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. In particular, if
\Phi
is singular,
u_{n}
is bounded by a constant strictly less than the singularity.
Lastly, the time-discrete solutions converge to the time-continuous solutions as
\tau\to 0
.

Concerning space discretisations, we also restrict ourselves to mentioning works addressing specifically System (
1.1
). In this respect, a finite difference method was used in
[
14
]
. The finite volume method was considered in
[
20
]
, and the convergence of the space-time dicretisation scheme was proven using an entropy formulation.
Convergence results of mixed finite elements for a variation of the PDE-PDE model of biofilms were shown in
[
1
]
, and for a PDE-ODE model in the context of porous media flow in
[
8
]
. For the numerical results presented here, we use
finite elements. However, the numerical analysis is done in a time-discrete, but continuous-in-space setting. Therefore, the results are independent of the chosen spatial discretisation.

1.3
Linearisation

The time-discrete system (
1.3
)
is nonlinear, degenerate and singular. For approximating its numerical solution, stable iterative linearisation schemes are needed. Most of the works addressing such type of problems are focusing on a
direct approach
. More precisely, for solving (
1.3a
),
w=\Phi(u)
is considered as the primary unknown yielding
u=\Phi^{-1}(w)
which is then linearised by expanding in terms of the last iterate. With reference to (
1.3a
), this approach is convenient because there are no spatial derivatives applied to the nonlinear terms. However, this requires that
\Phi
is an invertible function; if this is not the case, then a regularisation step is required. Alternatively, one can use
u
as the primary variable, and avoid inverting the function
\Phi
. In this case, the nonlinearity appears under the Laplace operator, which makes the construction of robust linearisation schemes and, in particular, proving the convergence a complex task.

Following
[
7
]
here we consider instead a
split formulation
involving two primary unknowns,
u
and
w
, which are related through the algebraic relationship
w=\Phi(u)
. We reformulate the time-discrete version of (
1.1a
) as a system of a linear elliptic equation and an algebraic one. For this reformulated system, we construct linear iterative schemes having a stable and robust behaviour. They all fit in the general framework given below.

Problem 1.2

(Linearisation scheme)
.

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u^{i}_{n}
be the
i^{\rm th}
iterate of the
n^{\rm th}
time-step, and let
u^{0}_{n}:=u_{n-1}
be given. To compute
u^{i}_{n}
from
u^{i-1}_{n}
, first solve for
(\tilde{u}^{i}_{n},w^{i}_{n})
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}(\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}-u_{n-1})=\Delta w^{i}_{n}+f(v_{n-1})\tilde{u}^{i}_{n},

(1.4a)

\displaystyle L^{i}_{n}(\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}-u^{i-1}_{n})=w^{i}_{n}-\Phi(u^{i-1}_{n}).

(1.4b)

The factors
L^{i}_{n}
will be specified below. The way they are chosen is defining the different schemes used here. Finally, we take the positive part of
\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}
,

\displaystyle u^{i}_{n}=\left[\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}\right]_{+}.

(1.4c)

If
w_{n}^{i}
and
\tilde{u}_{n}^{i}
(and, consequently, also
u^{i}_{n}
) converge to
w_{n}
and
u_{n}
respectively, then the limits satisfy
w_{n}=\Phi(u_{n})
, and
u_{n}
is a (weak) solution of (
1.3a
). Observe that the formulation used in
Problem

1.2
, where (
1.3a
) is split into a linear elliptic equation and a nonlinear algebraic one, is well suited for degenerate problems. In particular, no regularisation is needed in the slow diffusion case, i.e. when
u^{i}_{n}
takes values for which
\Phi^{\prime}
vanishes.

As mentioned, the choice of the factors
L^{i}_{n}
in (
1.4b
) leads to different linearisation schemes. With
L^{i}_{n}=\Phi^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})
, one obtains the Newton Scheme (NS) in the context of the splitting formulation (
1.4
). The convergence is guaranteed rigorously in the fully discrete case, but this depends strongly on the spatial discretisation and mesh size
[
6
,
32
,
36
]
. For time-dependent problems, since the initial guess is often the solution at the previous time step, this implies that the time-step size should be sufficiently small, which may cancel the advantages brought by the implicit discretisation. In the same category, we mention the modified Picard scheme
[
9
]
and the Jäger–Kačur scheme
[
24
,
25
]
, for which the linear convergence can be proven rigorously under similar conditions as for the NS.

Ideally, one works with a scheme that is unconditionally convergent, i.e. the time-step
\tau
does not depend on the spatial discretisation and the mesh-size
h
, which was one of the main reasons for choosing an implicit time discretisation. This can be achieved by using the L-scheme (LS)
[
26
,
33
]
, which is nothing but choosing
L^{i}_{n}=L
(a suficiently large constant) in (
1.4b
). This scheme has a guaranteed but linear convergence, irrespective of the initial guess and the spatial discretisation. These results were extended in
[
35
]
to doubly-degenerate problems, where a Hölder continuous, not necessarily strictly increasing nonlinearity appears under the time derivative. The drawback of this scheme is its significantly slower convergence when compared to the NS (whenever the latter converges)
[
26
,
41
]
. Improvements can be made by choosing
L
adaptively in each step, or by performing first a number of LS steps, and then switching to the NS entirely when the iterations are close enough to the solution
[
26
,
44
]
.

Such observations have lead to the Modified L-scheme, or M-scheme (MS) for short, as introduced in
[
28
]
, and on which we mainly focus here. We define
L^{i}_{n}\coloneqq\max\{\Phi^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})\\
+M\tau^{\gamma},\,2M\tau^{\gamma}\}
, for some
\gamma\in(0,1]
and sufficiently large
M>0
. Hence, the MS can be viewed as a combination of the NS and the LS where the LS is a first order global method ensuring stability, while the NS is a first order local method speeding up convergence. In particular, the MS has a first order local term to speed up convergence but regularizes it with a second order global term which captures the evolution of
u_{n}
to ensure stability. In an earlier work
[
28
]
, it has been shown for the direct formulation that the MS is indeed unconditionally stable while achieving much better convergence rates than the LS.

In this work, we apply the LS and MS to the splitting formulation in
Problem

1.2
, allowing us to handle systems with porous medium type degeneracies, as well as singular diffusion. Similarly to
[
28
]
, we prove that the MS converges unconditionally and that, in the non-degenerate case, it has a contraction rate that scales with
\tau
. The two main results for the LS and MS in
Section

4
are summarised in the following theorems.

Theorem

(Convergence of the L-scheme)
.

For a sufficiently small time step size
\tau
independent of the mesh size
h
, the L-scheme converges to a function
u_{n}
that is the weak solution of our time-discretised
eq.

1.3a
. In the non-degenerate case, the L-scheme converges with a contraction rate
\alpha<1
.

Theorem

(Convergence of the M-scheme)
.

For a sufficiently small time step size
\tau
independent of the mesh size
h
and under certain boundedness conditions on the iterates, the M-scheme converges to a function
u_{n}
that is the weak solution of our time-discretised solution
eq.

1.3a
. In the non-degenerate case, the M-scheme converges with a contraction rate
\alpha<1
, that scales with
\tau^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in(0,1]
.

The outline of our paper is as follows: In
Section

2
we provide the required functional setting and background and state the structural assumptions. In
Section

3
we prove the results for the time discretisation and in
Section

4
we show the convergence results for both the LS and the MS. In
Section

5
we perform numerical simulations and compare the performances of the NS and MS for a porous medium equation and both cases of the biofilm model (PDE-PDE system and PDE-ODE system).
Finally, in
Section

6
we summarize our results and discuss potential future research.

\norm{u}_{H^{-1}\Omega}\coloneqq\sup_{\phi\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)}\frac{\langle u,\phi\rangle}{\norm{\nabla\phi}},

(2.2)

where

denotes the duality paring.
Since we consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, we will mainly use

. Lastly, we consider the Bochner spaces
L^{p}(0,T;V)
, with

a Banach space, equipped with the norm

\norm{u}_{L^{p}(0,T;V)}\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\norm{u(t)}_{V}^{p}dt\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

(2.3)

We will frequently use Young’s inequality

uv\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}u^{2}+\frac{\rho}{2}v^{2},\quad\text{for }\rho>0\text{ and }u,v\in\mathds{R},

(2.4)

the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\left|\int_{\Omega}uv\right|\leq\norm{u}\norm{v},\qquad u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(2.5)

and the discrete Gronwall Lemma: Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be non-negative sequences such that
u_{n}\leq a_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_{k}u_{k}
, then

u_{n}\leq a_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}b_{k}\exp\left(\sum_{k<j<n}b_{j}\right).

(2.6)

Lastly, for convex functions
\Psi\in C\left(\mathds{R}^{+}\right)
with
\Psi(0)=0
, we have the following inequalities

\displaystyle\text{Jensen's inequality:}\quad\Psi\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}|f|\right)\leq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\Psi(|f|)\quad\text{for }f\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(2.7a)

\displaystyle\text{Super-additivity:}\quad\Psi(a)+\Psi(b)\leq\Psi(a+b)\quad\text{for all }a,b\geq 0.

(2.7b)

To denote the positive and negative part we write
\left[u\right]_{+}\coloneqq\max\{0,u\}
and

.
Moreover,
\mathds{R}^{+}=\{x\in\mathds{R}\;\colon\;x\geq 0\}
and
\mathds{R}^{+}_{\ast}=\{x\in\mathds{R}\;\colon\;x>0\}
. In some proofs we will also use the notation

I(a,b)=\{x\;\colon\;\min\{a,b\}\leq x\leq\max\{a,b\}\},\qquad a,b\in\mathds{R}.

(2.8)

If

are two given functions then

should be considered pointwise a.e.

Finally, throughout this work,
C>0
will denote a generic constant that might change in each occurrence and from line to line but will always be independent of

.

2.2
Structural assumptions

Dependng on the function
\Phi
in (
1.2
), the problems may be singular-degenerate, or have a structure resembling the porous medium equations. To cover both cases, we introduce a maximum density/concentration value
b
, where
b=1
in the former case (the biofilm model) and
b=\infty
for porous medium type equations.

With
b
as above, We make the following structural assumptions for
Equation

1.1

(P1)

\Phi\colon[0,b)\to\mathds{R}^{+}
is an increasing function with locally Lipschitz continuous derivatives, satisfying

\displaystyle\quad\Phi(0)=0,\quad\lim_{u\nearrow b}\Phi(u)=\infty,\text{ and }\Phi^{\prime}(u)>0\text{ for }u\in(0,b),

with
\inf_{[0,b]}\Phi^{\prime}=:\phi_{\mathrm{m}}\in[0,\infty)
, and
\sup_{[0,b]}\Phi^{\prime}=:\phi_{\mathrm{M}}\in(0,\infty]
. We furthermore require that
\Phi^{\prime}
is strictly increasing in
[0,\varepsilon_{0})
for some
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,b)
.

(P2)

f\colon\mathds{R}^{+}\to\mathds{R}
is Lipschitz continuous and bounded, with
\|f\|_{\infty}=f_{\mathrm{M}}
for some constant
f_{\mathrm{M}}\geq 0
.
g\colon[0,b)\times\mathds{R}^{+}\to\mathds{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
g_{\mathrm{M}}>0
. Moreover, we assume that
g(\cdot,0)\geq 0
.

(P3)

D\colon[0,b)\to\mathds{R}
is a continuous function. There exist constants
D_{\mathrm{m}},D_{\mathrm{M}}
s.t.
0<D_{\mathrm{m}}\leq D(u)\leq D_{\mathrm{M}}<\infty
for all
u\in[0,b)
.

The functions
\Phi
,
f
,
g
,
D
are extended for arguments
u\in\mathds{R}^{-}
by their values at
u=0
. If
b=1
, the functions that are bounded at
u=b
are also extended for
u\in[b,\infty)
by their values at
u=b
.

Remark 2.0.1

(Validity of the assumptions
(P1)
-
(P3)
)
.

The biofilm models
[
13
,
15
]
(see Equation (
5.8
) in Section

) and the porous medium equation satisfy the assumptions
(P1)
-
(P3)
, with
b=1
and

respectively. Note that we only consider non-negative solutions as

and

denote densities and/or concentrations.

For the initial data we assume the following.

(P4)

The initial conditions
u_{0},v_{0}\colon\Omega\to[0,\infty)
are s.t.
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}<b
.

Finally, for the ease of presentation we consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for

, and also for

if

. The results here can be the extended to mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions, following the ideas in
[
23
,
29
]
.

2.3
Weak formulation of the time continuous problem

We consider weak solutions of
Equation

1.1
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and initial data satisfying
(P4)
.

Definition 2.1

(Weak formulation)
.

A weak solution of (
1.1
) is a pair
(u,v)\in\\
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}\allowbreak\cap\,H^{1}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))^{2}
s.t.
(\Phi(u),\mu v)\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right))^{2}
,
(u,v)(0)=(u_{0},v_{0})
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\partial_{t}u,\phi\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla\Phi(u),\nabla\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(f(v)u,\phi),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\partial_{t}v,\eta\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\mu(D(u)\nabla v,\nabla\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(g(u,v),\eta)

hold for all
\phi,\eta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right))
.

For the well-posedness of Problem (
1.1
) with
b=\infty
and
\mu=1
, we refer to
[
3
,
31
]
. If
\mu=0
, the existence and uniqueness of solutions for the coupled system follow by
L^{1}
-contraction similarly as in
[
29
]
. Well-posedness results for the system with
b=1
and either Dirichlet or mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions were obtained in
[
23
,
29
]
. In particular, uniqueness can be shown if
D
in
(P3)
is independent of
u

[
23
]
or if
\mu=0

[
29
]
. Under these assumptions, local well-posedness was also shown for homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions in
[
29
]
.
Furthermore, it was shown that solutions are non-negative, and if
(P4)
holds, then the solution
u
is bounded by a constant strictly less than
1
, i.e. the singularity in the diffusion coefficient is not attained.
The local Hölder continuity of solutions of such systems was studied in
[
22
]
.
In this direction, we also mention
[
16
]

where the specific PDE-PDE biofilm model
[
15
]
(corresponding to
\mu=1
with diffusion coefficient (
1.2
)) was analyzed,

[
27
]
where the existence of solutions for a similar degenerate PDE-ODE system was studied, and
[
5
]
where a doubly degenerate PDE-PDE system was analysed.

Lastly, we remark that, for simplicity, we assume homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Extending the results to mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions is possible following the arguments in

.

3
Time discretisation

In this section we analyse the following weak form of the time discretised system (
1.3
). We first state it in a weak form.

Problem

(Weak formulation of the time-discretised system)
.

Let
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
u_{n-1},\,v_{n-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
given. Find

(u_{n},v_{n})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
such that
\Phi(u_{n}),\,\mu v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
, and for all
\phi,\eta\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
it holds

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(u_{n}-u_{n-1}),\phi\right)+(\nabla\Phi(u_{n}),\nabla\phi)

\displaystyle=(f(v_{n-1})u_{n},\phi),

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(v_{n}-v_{n-1}),\eta\right)+\mu(D(u_{n})\nabla v_{n},\nabla\eta)

\displaystyle=(g(u_{n},v_{n-1}),\eta).

(3.1b)

Throughout this paper, we write
w_{n}=\Phi(u_{n})
and use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle h_{n-1}\coloneqq 1-\tau f(v_{n-1}).

(3.2)

Note that
h_{n-1}
is positive if
\tau<1/f_{\mathrm{M}}
.

Remark 3.0.1

(The decoupling of the equations)
.

Observe that the solution
u_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of (
3.1a
) does not depend on the solution
v_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of (
3.1b
). Hence, the system (
3.1
) can be solved sequentially, i.e. we first solve the nonlinear problem (
3.1a
) and subsequently the linear problem (
3.1b
).

In this section we prove the following results, already briefly mentioned in
Section

1.2
.

Theorem 3.1

(Well-posedness and boundedness of the time-discrete solutions)
.

For
\tau<1/\penalty f_{\mathrm{M}}
, there exists a unique weak solution
(u_{n},v_{n})
of (
3.1
).
Moreover, there exist
\tau_{\rm disc}\coloneqq\min\{1/f_{\mathrm{M}},1/g_{\mathrm{M}}\}>0
and
\breve{u}\in[0,b)
independent of
n
, such that

\displaystyle 0\leq u_{n}\leq\breve{u},\quad\text{ and }\quad 0\leq v_{n}\quad\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\quad\text{ for all }1\leq n\leq N\text{ and }\tau<\tau_{\rm disc}.

(3.3)

In fact,
\breve{u}
is given by

\displaystyle\breve{u}=\begin{cases}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\exp\left(\frac{Tf_{\mathrm{M}}}{1-\tau f_{\mathrm{M}}}\right)&\text{ if }b=\infty,\\[5.0pt]
\Phi^{-1}\left(\|\Phi(u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{{\rm diam}(\Omega)^{2}}{2d}f_{\mathrm{M}}\right)<1&\text{ if }b=1,\end{cases}

(3.4)

Remark 3.1.1

(Computable upper bound for
u_{n}
)
.

Observe that (
3.4
)–(
3.3
) provides a uniform upper bound for
u_{n}
that is a priori computable. This will be used in
Section

4
to show the convergence of the iterative linearisation scheme.

Theorem 3.2

(Convergence of the time-discrete solutions)
.

Let
(u,v)\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}
be the unique weak solution of
Equation

1.1
. For a time-step size
\tau=\frac{T}{N_{\tau}}>0
,
N_{\tau}\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}
be the time-discrete solution of (
3.1
) with
\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
. Then, in addition to
(P4)
, if
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then along any sequence of
\tau
converging to 0 we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\tau}}\int_{(n-1)\tau}^{n\tau}\left[\|u_{n}-u(t)\|^{2}+\|w_{n}-\Phi(u(t))\|^{2}+\|v_{n}-v(t)\|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t\to 0.

(3.5)

Moreover, if
u_{0}\not\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then consider an approximation
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of the initial data such that
\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}-u_{0}\|\leq\varepsilon
, for fixed
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\{(u_{n}^{\varepsilon},v_{n}^{\varepsilon})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}
be the corresponding time-discrete solutions with
\{w^{\varepsilon}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
. Then, along any sequence of
(\varepsilon,\tau)
converging to
(0,0)
one has

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\tau}}\int_{(n-1)\tau}^{n\tau}\left[\|u^{\varepsilon}_{n}-u(t)\|^{2}+\|w_{n}^{\varepsilon}-\Phi(u(t))\|^{2}+\|v^{\varepsilon}_{n}-v(t)\|^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}t\to 0.

(3.6)

The proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
are based on several lemmas.

3.1
Proof of
Theorem

3.1
: well-posedness, positivity, and boundedness

3.1.1
Existence and uniqueness

We first prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions of the system of equations (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.3

(Well-posedness for (
3.1
))
.

For
\tau<1/\penalty f_{\mathrm{M}}
, there exists a unique weak solution of the time discretised system (
3.1
).

Proof.

(Step 1) Existence of
u_{n}
:

As the equations are decoupled, we can first prove the existence of the solution
u_{n}
of (
3.1a
). To this end, we use arguments in
[
34
]
. We consider the function
\Psi\coloneqq\Phi^{-1}:\mathds{R}^{+}\to[0,b)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\Psi(0)=0,\quad\Psi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\Phi^{\prime}\circ\Psi}\geq 0

(3.7)

by
(P1)
.
Then, the energy
J\colon H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)\to\mathds{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle J(w)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\left[h_{n-1}\int_{0}^{w}\Psi+\frac{\tau}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}-u_{n-1}w\right]

(3.8)

is convex and coercive for
\tau<1/f_{\mathrm{M}}
. Hence, a minimizer
w_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
of
J
exists, and
u_{n}=\Psi(w_{n})
solves the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation (
3.1a
). Then using
(P1)
, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon\in(0,b)
, we have
0\leq\Psi\leq\Psi(\varepsilon)<\infty
in
[0,\varepsilon]
and
\Psi
is Lipschitz in
(\varepsilon,b)
. Hence, by (
3.7
) it follows that, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
we have

0\leq u_{n}^{2}(x)=\Psi^{2}(w_{n})(x)
, so
u_{n}
is measurable. Integrating over
\Omega
, one gets

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Psi^{2}(w_{n})=\int_{\{0\leq w_{n}\leq\varepsilon\}}\Psi^{2}(w_{n})+\int_{\{w_{n}>\varepsilon\}}\Psi^{2}(w_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\Psi(\varepsilon)^{2}|\Omega|+C\left(1+\norm{w_n}^{2}\right)\leq C+C\norm{\del w_n}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

for some constant
C>0
, where, in the last estimate, we used the Poincaré inequality (
2.1
). Since
w_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
, this shows that the integral is finite, so
u_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

(Step 2) Uniqueness of
u_{n}
:

Assume that for a given
v_{n-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there are two solutions
u_{n},\,\tilde{u}_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of (
3.1a
) with
w_{n}=\Phi(u_{n})
and
\tilde{w}_{n}=\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. For their difference we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}(h_{n-1}(u_{n}-\tilde{u}_{n}),\varphi)+(\nabla(\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})),\nabla\varphi)=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.9)

Note that
\phi=\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})\right]_{+}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
, see e.g.
[
10
]
. Choosing
\phi
as a test function in (
3.9
)
leads to

\frac{1}{\tau}\left(h_{n-1}\left(u_{n}-\tilde{u}_{n}\right),\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})\right]_{+}\right)+\norm{\nabla\posi{\Phi(u_n) - \Phi(\Tilde{u}_n)}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

(3.10)

As
\Phi
is an increasing function, we note that
\left(u_{n}-\tilde{u}_{n}\right)\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})\right]_{+}\geq 0
. Hence, both terms in (
3.10
) are non-negative and therefore, have to be equal to 0. We conclude that
\norm{\nabla\posi{\Phi(u_n) - \Phi(\Tilde{u}_n)}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0
, which results in
\norm{\posi{\Phi(u_n) - \Phi(\Tilde{u}_n)}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0
by the Poincaré inequality (
2.1
). This implies that
\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})\geq\Phi(u_{n})
a.e. in
\Omega
, but as
\Phi
is an increasing function, we also find that
\tilde{u}_{n}\geq u_{n}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Due to the symmetry in the arguments, it follows in the same way that
u_{n}\geq\tilde{u}_{n}
a.e. which implies that
u_{n}=\tilde{u}_{n}
a.e. in
\Omega
.
The uniqueness of
u_{n}
can also be shown via the
L^{1}
-contraction principle
[
46
]
.

(Step 3) Existence-uniqueness of
v_{n}
:

We now prove the existence and uniqueness for the solution
v_{n}
of (
3.1b
). In the PDE-ODE case, i.e.
\mu=0
, we have an explicit expression for
v_{n}
,

v_{n}=v_{n-1}+\tau g(u_{n},v_{n-1}).

(3.11)

Hence, the existence and uniqueness of
v_{n}
follows from the existence and uniqueness of
u_{n}
.

In the PDE-PDE case, i.e.
\mu=1
, existence and uniqueness follows from the Lax-Milgram theorem
[
18
]
. Indeed, the weak form can be rewritten as

a(v_{n},\eta)=l(\eta)\quad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right),

(3.12)

where the bilinear form
a\colon H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)\times H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)\to\mathds{R}
is given by
a(v_{n},\eta)=(v_{n},\eta)+\tau\mu(D(u_{n})\nabla v_{n},\nabla\eta)
. It is bounded and coercive since
0<D_{\mathrm{m}}\leq D\leq D_{\mathrm{M}}<\infty
by
(P3)
. Moreover,
l(\eta)=(\tau g(v_{n-1},u_{n})+v_{n-1},\eta)
is a bounded linear functional on
H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
. Consequently, there exists a unique solution
v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

3.1.2
Positivity and boundedness in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)

For the time-continuous biofilm models it was shown in
[
23
,
29
]
that the solutions
u
and
v
are non-negative, and that
u<1
. We aim to prove that these properties also hold for the time-discrete solutions. First, we derive bounds for
u_{n}
in the general case, i.e. including porous medium type diffusion.

Lemma 3.4

(Positivity and boundedness of
u_{n}
)
.

Let
u_{n-1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be positive a.e. in
\Omega
. Then for
\tau<1/\penalty f_{M}axi
, the solution
u_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of (
3.1a
) is positive and bounded a.e. in
\Omega
. More precisely, we have

0\leq u_{n}\leq\sup\left\{\frac{u_{n-1}}{1-\tau f(v_{n-1})}\right\}\quad\text{ a.e. in }\Omega

(3.13)

for all
1\leq n\leq N.

Proof.

To prove that
u_{n}
is bounded from above, we use the test function
\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}
, for some
a\in\mathds{R}^{+}
in (
3.1a
). Note that

[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)]_{\pm}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\left[\Phi(u_{n})\mp\Phi(a)\right]_{\pm}
= 0 if
u_{n}=0
as
\Phi(0)=0
, and thus
\left[\Phi(u_{n})\mp\Phi(a)\right]_{\pm}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. We find

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}\left(u_{n}-a\right)\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}+\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}\left(a-\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}u_{n-1}\right)\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=-\tau\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi(u_{n})\cdot\nabla\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}=-\tau\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}^{2}\leq 0.

(3.14)

Let
\tau<1/f_{\mathrm{M}}
and
a\coloneqq\sup\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}u_{n-1}
, which implies that
a\geq 0
as
h_{n-1},u_{n-1}\geq 0
.
Then, the second term on the left hand side is positive. The first term is also positive as
\left(u_{n}-a\right)\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}\geq 0
since
\Phi
is increasing.
We conclude that the inequality in (
3.14
) must be an equality. This is only possible if
\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\Phi(a)\right]_{+}=0
, and thus
\Phi(u_{n})\leq\Phi(a)
. As
\Phi
is an increasing function, this implies that
u_{n}\leq a
and hence,

u_{n}\leq a=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}u_{n-1}\right\}=\sup\left\{\frac{u_{n-1}}{1-\tau f(v_{n-1})}\right\}.

(3.15)

We use the same arguments to prove that
u_{n}\geq 0
, but this time with
a=0
i.e.
\Phi(a)=\Phi(0)=0
. Using the test function
\phi=\left[\Phi(u_{n})\right]_{-}
we conclude that
\left[\Phi(u_{n})\right]_{-}=0
, and thus
u_{n}\geq 0
.
∎

An explicit upper bound for
u_{n}
can be given in terms of the initial conditions and
f_{\mathrm{M}}
, which
we provide in the following result.

Lemma 3.5

(Explicit upper bound
u_{n}
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy assumption
(P4)
. Then, for the sequence
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
solving (
3.1
), one has

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\exp\left(\frac{n\tau f_{\mathrm{M}}}{1-\tau f_{\mathrm{M}}}\right).

(3.16)

Remark 3.5.1

(Upper bound as
\tau\to 0
)
.

Assuming that
u_{n}\to u(t)
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
when
\tau=t/n\to 0
, the upper bound (
3.16
) implies that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\exp\left(tf_{M}\right).

(3.17)

Proof.

By (
3.15
) in the proof of
Lemma

3.4
, we have using
\frac{1}{(1-x)}\leq\exp(\frac{x}{1-x})
for
|x|<1
that,

\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq\sup\left\{\frac{u_{n-1}}{1-\tau f(v_{n-1})}\right\}\leq\frac{\|u_{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{1-\tau f_{\mathrm{M}}}\leq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\left(1-\tau f_{\mathrm{M}}\right)^{n}}\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\exp\left(\frac{n\tau f_{\mathrm{M}}}{1-\tau f_{M}}\right).\end{split}

∎

In the biofilm case, i.e.
b=1
, we can improve the upper bound. As shown e.g. in
[
23
,
29
]
, the solution
u
of the time continuous system is strictly less than 1, so we aim to prove this also for the approximate solutions
u_{n}
.

Lemma 3.6

(Upperbound
u_{n}
if
b=1
)
.

Consider the biofilm case, i.e.
b=1
, and let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy assumption
(P4)
and
\tau<1/\penalty f_{\mathrm{M}}
. Then for the sequence
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}\\
\subset L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
solving (
3.1
), one has

0\leq u_{n}\leq 1-\delta\qquad\text{a.e. in}\ \Omega,

(3.18)

for all
1\leq n\leq N
, and some
\delta>0
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.4
we have
0\leq u_{n}\leq C
, for some constant
C>0
.
Let
\tilde{\omega}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)+\|\Phi(u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
be the solution of

\begin{split}\left(\nabla\tilde{\omega},\nabla\phi\right)=\left(Cf_{\mathrm{M}},\phi\right)\quad\text{for all }\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{split}

(3.19)

As
Cf_{\mathrm{M}}\in\mathds{R}^{+}_{\ast}
, by properties of the Poisson equation, we know that
\tilde{\omega}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Further, since
\tilde{\omega}
is superharmonic, the maximum principle implies that
\Phi(u_{0})\leq\tilde{\omega}
. We will prove that
\Phi(u_{n})\leq\tilde{\omega}
for all
1\leq n\leq N
by induction. Assuming it holds for
n-1
, we subtract (
3.19
) from (
3.1a
) and multiply both sides by
\tau
. We then choose the test function
\phi=\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right]_{+}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
to find

\begin{split}&\left(u_{n}-\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{\omega})+\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{\omega})-u_{n-1},\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right]_{+}\right)+\tau\left(\nabla\left(\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right),\nabla\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right]_{+}\right)\\
&+\tau\left(f_{M}C-f(v_{n-1})u_{n},\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right]_{+}\right)=0.\end{split}

(3.20)

By the induction hypothesis, we have
\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{\omega})-u_{n-1}\geq 0
, while
{\left(u_{n}-\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{\omega})\right)\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right]_{+}}

{\geq 0}
as
\Phi
is an increasing function. The other terms are also positive as

\tau\left(\nabla\left(\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right),\nabla\left[\Phi(u_{n})-\tilde{\omega}\right]_{+}\right)=\tau\norm{\nabla\posi{\Phi(u_n) - \Tilde{\om}}}^{2}\text{ and }f_{\mathrm{M}}C-f(v_{n-1})u_{n}\geq 0

by definition of
f_{\mathrm{M}}
and
C
. Hence, the Poincare inequality implies that

\norm{\posi{\Phi(u_n) - \Tilde{\om}}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{\Omega}\norm{\nabla\posi{\Phi(u_n) - \Tilde{\om}}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0,

(3.21)

and thus
\Phi(u_{n})\leq\tilde{\omega}
. To conclude the proof, we recall that
\tilde{\omega}
is bounded and hence,

\begin{split}0\leq u_{n}\leq\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{\omega})=1-\delta,\quad\delta>0.\end{split}

(3.22)

∎

Remark 3.6.1

(Effective Lipschitz continuity of
\Phi
)
.

By
Lemma

3.6
, in the biofilm case, i.e.
b=1
, we can effectively restrict the domain of
\Phi
to
[0,1-\delta]\subset[0,1)
. Within this interval,
\Phi^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous as stated in assumption
(P1)
.

We have shown that
u_{n}\leq 1-\delta
for some
\delta>0
, but we aim to derive an explicit bound. Such a bound will be useful in Section
4
when we propose the linearisation scheme and is provided in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7

(Explicit upper bound
u_{n}
if
b=1
)
.

Consider the biofilm case, i.e.
b=1
, and let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy assumption
(P4)
and
\tau<1/\penalty f_{\mathrm{M}}
. Then for the sequence
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
solving (
3.1
), one has

0\leq u_{n}\leq\Phi^{-1}(\tilde{C})<1\qquad\text{a.e. in}\ \Omega,

(3.23)

for all
1\leq n\leq N
, where

\tilde{C}=\norm{\Phi(u_0)}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\frac{{\rm diam}(\Omega)^{2}}{2d}f_{\mathrm{M}},

(3.24)

and
d
is the spatial dimension of
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\omega}
be the solution of
Equation

3.19
as in the proof of
Lemma

3.6
. Then,
\tilde{\omega}
is a classical solution to the elliptic problem

\begin{split}\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{\omega}=f_{\mathrm{M}}&\text{on $\Omega$},\\
\tilde{\omega}=\norm{\Phi(u_0)}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\text{on $\partial\Omega$},\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.25)

as we can take
C=1
by
Lemma

3.6
.

As
f_{\mathrm{M}}>0
,
\tilde{\omega}
is superharmonic, and we conclude that
\tilde{\omega}\geq\norm{\Phi(u_0)}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq 0

by the maximum principle. We then define the function
w=f_{\mathrm{M}}\norm{x - \Bar{x}}^{2}/(2d)\geq 0
, where
d
is the spatial dimension and
\bar{x}
is given by
\bar{x}_{i}=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}x_{i}
. It is straightforward to see that
\Delta w=f_{\mathrm{M}}
.

If we consider
z=\tilde{\omega}+w
, it satisfies
-\Delta z=-\Delta\tilde{\omega}-\Delta w=0
. Hence, by the maximum principle it follows that

\begin{split}\norm{\Tilde{\om}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\norm{z}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\norm{z}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}&=\norm{\Tilde{\om}}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}+\frac{\norm{x-\Bar{x}}^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}}{2d}f_{\mathrm{M}}\\
&=\norm{\Phi(u_0)}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\frac{{\rm diam}(\Omega)^{2}}{2d}f_{\mathrm{M}},\end{split}

(3.26)

where we used that
w,\tilde{w},z\geq 0
, which implies that
0\leq\tilde{w}\leq z
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We now show that
v_{n}
is positive, similarly as in
Lemma

3.4
for
u_{n}
, for both cases

and
b=\infty
.

Lemma 3.8

(Positivity of
v_{n}
)
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma 3.4 hold.
Let
v_{n-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be positive a.e. in
\Omega
. Then for
\tau<1/\penalty g_{\mathrm{M}}
the solution
v_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mu v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
of (
3.1b
) is positive a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By
Lemmas

3.4
,
3.5
and
3.6
,
u_{n}
is positive and bounded in
[0,b)
implying that
g(u,\cdot)
is well-defined. For the positivity of

, first observe that
G(u,t):=t+\tau g(u,t)
is an increasing function in
t
for
\tau<1/g_{\mathrm{M}}

(An important convergence result)

\varphi_{1}

with the constant

independent of

. Hence, similar to (

), along a subsequence of

. Moreover, noting that

a.e. in

\displaystyle\|(u^{\varepsilon}-u)(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(v^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}-f(v)u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}-u_{0}\|+\int_{0}^{t}\|(f(v^{\varepsilon})-f(v))u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(v)(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\overset{\ref{ass: 2}}{\leq}\varepsilon|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\overset{|u^{\varepsilon}|<C}{+}C\int_{0}^{t}\|v^{\varepsilon}-v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+f_{\mathrm{M}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

(3.44)

Applying Gronwall’s lemma (

we get that

for all

∎

Proof of (

) in

.

We choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that along the subsequence in
Lemma

3.12
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[\|u^{\varepsilon}-u\|^{2}+\|w^{\varepsilon}-w\|^{2}+\|v^{\varepsilon}-v\|^{2}\right]\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\delta.

for some arbitrary

For this fixed

4
Linearisation

We have shown that the time-discretised system (
3.1
) is well-posed and that its solutions possess the qualitative behaviour we expect from the time-continuous system. In this section, we propose linearisation schemes and prove their well-posedness and convergence.
Recall that
\Phi
is possibly not Lipschitz continuous if
b=1
.
However,
u_{n}
takes values in
[0,\breve{u}]
where
\breve{u}<b
is a uniform a priori computable upper bound (see
Remark

3.1.1
), and
\Phi
is Lipschitz in
[0,\breve{u}]
. Hence, we can regularize
\Phi
as follows.

Definition 4.1

(Regularization of
\Phi
)
.

If
b=1
and with
\breve{u}>0
given in (
3.4
), let the function
\breve{\Phi}\colon\mathds{R}^{+}\to\mathds{R}^{+}
be defined as

\begin{split}\breve{\Phi}(u)=\begin{cases}\Phi(u),&\text{if }u\leq\breve{u},\\
\Phi^{\prime}(\breve{u})(u-\breve{u})+\Phi(\breve{u}),&\text{if }u\geq\breve{u}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.1)

If
b=\infty
, we set
\breve{\Phi}=\Phi
.

Recalling that
\Phi(u)=w
posseses space regularity,
we propose an iterative linearisation scheme to solve (
3.1a
) which splits the equation into two coupled equations. The iterations are obtained by solving the following.

Problem

(The splitting linearisation)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be fixed, and assume
u_{n-1},v_{n-1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
(u^{i-1}_{n},w^{i-1}_{n})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
be given, satisfying
u_{n-1},u^{i-1}_{n}\leq\breve{u}
. Find the pair
(u^{i}_{n},w^{i}_{n})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
such that, for all
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
and
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}-u_{n-1}\right),\phi\right)+\left(\nabla w^{i}_{n},\nabla\phi\right)=\left(f(v_{n-1})\tilde{u}^{i}_{n},\phi\right),

(4.2a)

\displaystyle\left(L^{i}_{n}(\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}-u^{i-1}_{n}),\xi\right)=\left(w^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}\left(u^{i-1}_{n}\right),\xi\right),

(4.2b)

\displaystyle\ u^{i}_{n}=\left[\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}\right]_{+}\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,

(4.2c)

for some specific choice of a bounded function
L^{i}_{n}:\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{+}
, which depends only on iterates up to
u^{i-1}_{n}
but not on
u^{i}_{n}
. The iteration starts with the initial guess
u^{0}_{n}=u_{n-1}
.

Such a splitting method was first proposed in
[
7
, Section 4.2]
for the
L
-scheme assuming that
\Phi
is Lipschitz. Here, we generalize the results.

Remark 4.1.1

(Positivity of
u^{i}_{n}
)
.

To shorten the proofs, throughout this section, we will simplify
(
4.2
) (where we first determine
\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}
and then set
u^{i}_{n}=\left[\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}\right]_{+}
) by referring to
\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}
interchangeably as
u^{i}_{n}
. All inequalities and results in this section remain valid since

\norm{u^i_n - u_n}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\norm{\posi{\Tilde{u}^i_n} - u_n}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\norm{\Tilde{u}^i_n - u_n}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

(4.3)

for all
p\geq 1
.
Indeed, as

u_{n}\geq 0
which gives
\left[\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}\right]_{+}-u_{n}=\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}-u_{n}
if
\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}\geq 0
and
\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}-u_{n}<\left[\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}\right]_{+}-u_{n}\leq 0
if
\tilde{u}^{i}_{n}<0
.
The reason we introduce the formulation (
4.2
) is that it guarantees that
u^{i}_{n}\geq 0
, which is important for the numerical implementation.

Before proving results for particular linearisation schemes, we show that the regularization
\breve{\Phi}
does not alter the solution
u_{n}
. This is obvious for
b=\infty
, while for
b=1
it follows from the proposition below.

Proposition 4.2

(Consistency of the regularized
\breve{\Phi}
)
.

Let
b=1
, and
\breve{\Phi}
the regularized approximation of
\Phi
given in
Definition

4.1
. Then, the solution of (
4.2
) coincides with the solution to (
3.1
).

Proof.

Suppose
u_{n}
and
\tilde{u}_{n}
are the weak solution of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}(\tilde{u}_{n}-u_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\Delta\breve{\Phi}(\tilde{u}_{n})+f(v_{n-1})\tilde{u}_{n},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}(u_{n}-u_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\Delta\Phi(u_{n})+f(v_{n-1})u_{n}.

(4.5)

Since
u_{n}\leq\breve{u}
by
Theorem

3.1
, one has
\breve{\Phi}(u_{n})=\Phi(u_{n})
, i.e.

u_{n}
is a solution of Equation (
4.4
). However, this solution is unique due to

Theorem

3.1
which implies that
\tilde{u}_{n}=u_{n}
.
∎

To show that the linearisation scheme is well-defined, we prove that if
it converges, the limit is indeed a solution of the time-discretised equation (
3.1a
).

Proposition 4.3

(Consistency of the linearisation scheme)
.

Let
u^{i}_{n}
be uniformly bounded with respect to
i\in\mathbb{N}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
u^{i}_{n}\to\tilde{u}_{n}
strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, and
w^{i}_{n}\to\tilde{w}_{n}
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then
\tilde{u}_{n}
is the weak solution to the time-discretised equation (
3.1a
) and
\tilde{w}_{n}=\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Theorems

4.4
and
4.5
will show that the hypotheses of
Proposition

4.3
are indeed satisfied for the L- and M-schemes. Hence, the iterates
(u^{i}_{n},w^{i}_{n})
converge to the time-discrete solutions.

Proof.

First we observe that
(u_{n}^{i},\phi)\to(\tilde{u}_{n},\phi)
for all
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, since
u^{i}_{n}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
u^{i}_{n}\to\tilde{u}_{n}
strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence, taking the limit in (
4.2a
) implies that

\left(h_{n-1}\tilde{u}_{n}-u_{n-1},\phi\right)+\tau\left(\nabla\tilde{w}_{n},\nabla\phi\right)=0\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right),

(4.6)

where
h_{n-1}
is defined in (
3.2
). Similarly, taking the limit
i\to\infty
in (
4.2b
) we get

\left(\tilde{w}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}(\tilde{u_{n}}),\xi\right)=0\qquad\forall\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(4.7)

Here, we used that
\breve{\Phi}
is Lipschitz continuous implying that
(\breve{\Phi}(u^{i}_{n}),\xi)\to(\breve{\Phi}(\tilde{u}_{n}),\xi)
, and that
L^{i}_{n}
is bounded which yields

\left(L^{i}_{n}\left(u^{i}_{n}-u^{i-1}_{n}\right),\xi\right)\to 0
for all
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We conclude that
\tilde{w}_{n}=\breve{\Phi}(\tilde{u}_{n})
a.e., which allows us to substitute it back into Equation (

) and hence,

\left(h_{n-1}\tilde{u}_{n}-u_{n-1},\phi\right)+\tau\left(\nabla\breve{\Phi}\left(\tilde{u}_{n}\right),\nabla\phi\right)=0\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right).

(4.8)

This coincides with the time-discretised equation for

, and thus
\tilde{u}_{n}=u_{n}
, as solutions are unique.
∎

We can identify the linearisation schemes mentioned in
Section

1

as special cases of (

\displaystyle\textbf{Newton scheme}:L^{i}_{n}:=\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n}),

(4.9a)

\displaystyle\textbf{L-scheme}:\qquad\quad L^{i}_{n}:=L,\quad\text{for a constant }L>0,

(4.9b)

\displaystyle\textbf{M-scheme}:\qquad\;\;L^{i}_{n}:=\max\{\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})+M\tau^{\gamma},\,2M\tau^{\gamma}\},\text{ for constants }M>0,\;\gamma\in(0,1].

(4.9c)

In the sequel, we consider the L- and M-schemes as our main focus will be on degenerate problems.
We denote the errors of the iterates at the
n^{\text{th}}
time step by

\displaystyle e^{i}_{u}=u^{i}_{n}-u_{n},\quad e^{i}_{w}=w^{i}_{n}-w_{n},

where

and

.
The results for both schemes are similar, but the proofs for the L-scheme are more straightforward.

Theorem 4.4

(Convergence of the L-scheme)
.

For
\tau<1/\penalty f_{\mathrm{M}}
there exist unique solutions
\{(u^{i}_{n},w^{i}_{n})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
of (

. Furthermore, if

in

and

in

For non-degenerate problems, i.e. if

, the error-norm is a strict contraction

(4.11)

For the M-scheme, we need to impose an additional assumption.

(A1)

For a given

Remark 4.4.1

(Assumption

)
.

Assumption

was used in
[

with

Theorem 4.5

(Convergence of the M-scheme)
.

For
\tau<1/\penalty f_{\mathrm{M}}

For the convergence of

Remark 4.5.2

(Comparison L- and M-scheme)
.

Note that the extra assumptions

and (

We first prove the existence and uniqueness results stated in

and

is constant for the L-scheme and

Lemma 4.6

(Existence-uniqueness)
.

For
\tau<1/\penalty f_{M}
, the system of equations (

) with

L^{i}_{n}\coloneqq L>\sup\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\qquad\text{or}\qquad L^{i}_{n}\coloneqq\max\{\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})+M\tau^{\gamma},\,2M\tau^{\gamma}\}

We eliminate

in (

where

Consider the bilinear form

. We observe that

First, we show that the solutions of the L-scheme converge to the time-discrete solutions

and

Lemma 4.7

(Convergence of the L-scheme)
.

Under the assumptions of

, the stated convergence results hold.

Proof.

We use ideas from the proof of Lemma 2.6 in
[

]
. Subtracting (

) from Equation (

\left(h_{n-1}e^{i}_{u},\phi\right)+\tau\left(\nabla e^{i}_{w},\nabla\phi\right)=0,

where
e^{i}_{u}=u^{i}_{n}-u_{n}
and
e^{i}_{w}=w^{i}_{n}-w_{n}
, see (
4.10
). By adding and subtracting
Lu_{n}
, and adding and subtracting

on the right hand side of (

\left(L\left(e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}\right),\xi\right)=\left(e^{i}_{w}-\delta\breve{\Phi}^{i-1},\xi\right)

(4.16)

where
\delta\breve{\Phi}^{i-1}\coloneqq\breve{\Phi}\left(u^{i-1}_{n}\right)-\breve{\Phi}(u_{n})
. Choosing
\phi=e^{i}_{w}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
and

\left(h_{n-1}\left(L-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\right)\left(e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}\right),e^{i}_{u}\right)+\left(h_{n-1}\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}e^{i}_{u},e^{i}_{u}\right)+\tau\norm{\nabla e^i_w}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0,

(a-b)a=\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}-b^{2}+\left(a-b\right)^{2}\right)

and

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}\left(L+\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\right)|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}\left(L-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\right)|e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}\\

(4.20)

Note that

and assumption
(P1)
imply that

0\leq\phi_{\mathrm{m}}\leq\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}<L.

(4.21)

Combining this with equation (

) we find that

\frac{L+\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}+\tau\norm{\nabla e^i_w}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{L}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2},

(4.22)

where

\varepsilon\coloneqq L-\sup\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\leq\left(L-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\right).

(4.23)

Note that the norm
\sqrt{\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}}
is equivalent to
\norm{e^i_u}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, since

for

.

In the non-degenerate case, i.e. if
\phi_{\mathrm{m}}>0
, we obtain a contraction as the second term in (
4.22
) is positive,
\norm{\brac{e^i_u, e^i_w}}_{L}\leq\sqrt{\frac{L}{L+\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}}\norm{\brac{e^{i-1}_u, e^{i-1}_w}}_{L},
with the norm defined in (
4.11
).
Consequently,
u^{i}_{n}\to u_{n}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
w^{i}_{n}\to w_{n}
in
H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
by Banach’s fixed-point theorem.

In the degenerate case, we can sum up both sides of Equation (
4.22
) to find

\begin{split}0&\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}\right)+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\norm{\nabla e^i_w}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{L}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{0}_{u}|^{2}-\frac{L}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{N}_{u}|^{2}<\infty.\end{split}

(4.24)

Hence, taking the limit
N\to\infty
we conclude that of both sums must go to 0, yielding

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla e^i_w}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,

(4.25a)

\displaystyle\norm{e^{i}_u - e^{i-1}_u}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0.

(4.25b)

From (
4.25a
) it follows that
w^{i}_{n}\to w_{n}
in
H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
. Moreover, we can rewrite (
4.25b
) and use the strong form of (
4.2b
) to find

\norm{\frac{1}{L}\brac{w^i_n - \Phib(u^{i-1}_n)}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\norm{u^i_n - u^{i-1}_n}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\norm{e^i_u - e^{i-1}_u}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0.

(4.26)

Hence,
\breve{\Phi}(u^{i}_{n})\to w_{n}=\breve{\Phi}(u_{n})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, as
L>0
. Finally, to prove that
u^{i}_{n}\to u_{n}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
we use
Lemma

3.10
replacing the function
\psi
with
\breve{\Phi}
.

The result for the positive part of
u^{i}_{n}
follows from
Remark

4.1.1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.4
.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions
\{(u^{i}_{n},w^{i}_{n})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
follows from
Lemma

4.6
and the convergence from
Lemma

4.7
.
∎

We have shown that the L-scheme converges and that the error is a strict contraction in the non-degenerate case. Unfortunately, the contraction rate is very close to 1 if
\phi_{\mathrm{m}}>0
is small compared to
L
, as reported in
[
26
,
28
,
44
]
.
A closer inspection indicates that setting
L^{i}_{n}>\sup\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}
everywhere in the domain is superfluous and that this is the main reason for the slow convergence rate.
To overcome this drawback
we aim to modify the L-scheme such that it is stable but converges fast. This leads us to the M-scheme, first introduced in
[
28
]
.

4.2
M-scheme

Note that in contrast to the L-scheme, now
L^{i}_{n}
is a function of the previous iterate
u^{i-1}_{n}
.
We first derive two useful estimates that are needed to prove the main convergence results.

Lemma 4.8

(Some useful inequalities)
.

Let
(P1)
and (
4.12
) hold and
M\geq M_{0}=\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\Lambda
.
Then, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle L^{i}_{n}\geq 2M\tau^{\gamma},

(4.27a)

\displaystyle 0\leq(M-M_{0})\tau^{\gamma}\leq L^{i}_{n}-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma},

(4.27b)

where
\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}
was defined in (
4.19
).

Proof.

Note that (
4.27a
) is an immediate consequence of the definition of
L^{i}_{n}
.
To prove (
4.27b
), we first note that

\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}=\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta),

(4.28)

for some
\zeta\in I(u^{i-1}_{n},u_{n})
by the mean-value theorem. Moreover, for any
\zeta\in I(u^{i-1}_{n},u_{n})
, we have

|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)|\leq\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|u^{i-1}_{n}-\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\Lambda\tau=M_{0}\tau^{\gamma},

(4.29)

where we used (
4.12
) in the last inequality.
If
L^{i}_{n}=\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})+M\tau^{\gamma}
, then
L^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)\geq(M-M_{0})\tau^{\gamma}\geq 0
. On the other hand, if
L^{i}_{n}=2M\tau^{\gamma}
, then
\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})\leq M\tau^{\gamma}
by the definition of
L^{i}_{n}
. Together with (
4.29
) we conclude that
\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)\leq\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})+M_{0}\tau^{\gamma}\leq(M+M_{0})\tau^{\gamma}
, and thus again
L^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)\geq(M-M_{0})\tau^{\gamma}\geq 0
.

To derive the upper bound we argue analogously. If
L^{i}_{n}=\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(u^{i-1}_{n})+M\tau^{\gamma}
, we find

L^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)\leq\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}(u^{i-1}_{n}-\zeta)+M\tau^{\gamma}\leq\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\Lambda\tau^{\gamma}+M\tau^{\gamma}\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}.

(4.30)

If
L^{i}_{n}=2M\tau^{\gamma}
, then we have
L^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)\leq L^{i}_{n}-\phi_{\mathrm{m}}\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}
. Hence, combining all estimates we find

0\leq(M-M_{0})\tau^{\gamma}\leq L^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta)\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}.

(4.31)

As the estimates hold for any
\zeta\in I(u^{i-1}_{n},u_{n})
, the statement follows from (
4.28
).

∎

Next, we prove the first convergence result in
L^{\infty}
for non-degenerate problems.

Proposition 4.9

(
L^{\infty}
convergence of
u^{i}_{n}
)
.

Assume that
\inf\Phi^{\prime}=\phi_{\mathrm{m}}>0
and
(A1)
holds. Then, for
M>M_{0}\coloneqq\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\Lambda
, one has

\|w^{i}_{n}-w_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}\|u^{i-1}_{n}-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(4.32)

Moreover, if
\tau<(\phi_{\mathrm{m}}/M)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
, then

\|u^{i}_{n}-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{4M\tau^{\gamma}}{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}}\|u^{i-1}_{n}-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(4.33)

Therefore, if
\tau<(\phi_{\mathrm{m}}/(3M))^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
, then
u^{i}_{n}
converges linearly in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to
u_{n}
and the uniform boundedness of the iterates
\|u^{i}_{n}-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda\tau^{\gamma}
in (
4.12
) holds.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction in
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For
i=1
it is satisfied by assumption
(A1)
and
Remark

4.1.1
.
Remark

4.1.1
will also be used in the following estimates, i.e. taking the positive part of
u_{n}
does not alter the inequalities.
For the induction step we assume
\|u^{i-1}_{n}-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda\tau^{\gamma}
, which allows us to use
Lemma

4.8
. We split the proof into two parts. First, we show that (
4.32
) holds and subsequently, we deduce from it (
4.33
).

(Step 1:)
Note that (
4.32
) is equivalent to showing that
[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+}=0
and
[e^{i}_{w}+a]_{-}=0
for a specific
a>0
.
We subtract (
3.1a
) from Equation (
4.2a
) and rewrite (
4.2b
) as in the proof of
Lemma

4.7
, which yields

\displaystyle(h_{n-1}e^{i}_{u},\phi)+\tau(\nabla e^{i}_{w},\nabla\phi)

\displaystyle=0,

(4.34a)

\displaystyle\left(L^{i}_{n}\left(e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}\right),\xi\right)

\displaystyle=\left(e^{i}_{w}-\delta\breve{\Phi}^{i-1},\xi\right).

(4.34b)

Choosing
\phi=[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
in (
4.34a
) yields

(h_{n-1}e^{i}_{u},[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+})+\tau(\nabla e^{i}_{w},\nabla[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+})=0,

(4.35)

and since the second term is positive, we find that

(h_{n-1}e^{i}_{u},[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+})\leq 0.

(4.36)

To eliminate
e^{i}_{u}
we observe that equation (
4.34b
) implies that

e^{i}_{u}=\frac{L^{i}_{n}-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}}{L^{i}_{n}}e^{i-1}_{u}+\frac{e^{i}_{w}}{L^{i}_{n}},

(4.37)

almost everywhere. Combining (
4.36
) and (
4.37
) yields

\int_{\Omega}\frac{h_{n-1}}{L^{i}_{n}}[e^{i}_{w}-a][e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{h_{n-1}}{L^{i}_{n}}\left(a+\left(L^{i}_{n}-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\right)e^{i-1}_{u}\right)[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+}\leq 0.

(4.38)

The first term is positive and the second term can be made positive by choosing

a=2M\tau^{\gamma}\norm{u^{i-1}_n - u_n}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

(4.39)

as
0\leq L^{i}_{n}-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}
by
Lemma

4.8
. Hence, we find that
[e^{i}_{w}-a]_{+}=0
. The proof for
\phi=[e^{i}_{w}+a]_{-}\in H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
is analogous which proves (
4.32
).

(Step 2:)
To show (
4.33
) we
again note that
L^{i}_{n}-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}
by
Lemma

4.8
and that in the non-degenerate case, we have

\frac{1}{L^{i}_{n}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2M\tau^{\gamma}},\frac{1}{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}}\right\}.

(4.40)

Hence, using (
4.32
) in
Equation (
4.37
) implies that

\begin{split}\|e^{i}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}&\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2M\tau^{\gamma}},\frac{1}{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}}\right\}(2M\tau^{\gamma}\|e^{i-1}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2M\tau^{\gamma}\|e^{i-1}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})\\
&=\min\left\{2,\frac{4M\tau^{\gamma}}{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}}\right\}\|e^{i-1}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(4.41)

Consequently, if
\tau<(\frac{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}{M})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
, we get (
4.33
), and

Finally, note that the linear convergence and uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound of the iterates (
4.12
) follows if
\tau<(\frac{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}{3M})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
. Indeed, with the contraction rate
\bar{\alpha}={4M\tau^{\gamma}}/({\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}})<1
one has

\|e^{i}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\bar{\alpha}\|e^{i-1}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\dots\leq\bar{\alpha}^{i}\|e^{0}_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\bar{\alpha}^{i}\|u_{n-1}-u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda\tau^{\gamma},

the last inequality resulting from
(A1)
.
∎

Finally, we prove the convergence result for the M-scheme similar to
Lemma

4.7
for the L-scheme. The proof is analogous, but we obtain a better contraction rate in the non-degenerate case as the time step
\tau
is made smaller.

Lemma 4.10

(Convergence of the M-scheme)
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

4.5
with
M>M_{0}:=\|\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\Lambda
, the stated convergence results hold.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

4.9
, we subtract (
3.1a
) from Equation (
4.2a
) and rewrite (
4.2b
) which yields

\displaystyle(h_{n-1}e^{i}_{u},\phi)+\tau(\nabla e^{i}_{w},\nabla\phi)=0,

(4.42a)

\displaystyle(L^{i}_{n}e^{i}_{u},\xi)=(e^{i}_{w},\xi)+((L^{i}_{n}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta))e^{i-1}_{u},\xi),

(4.42b)

where
\zeta\in I(u^{i-1}_{n},u_{n})
.
Choosing
\phi=e^{i}_{w}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\xi=h_{n-1}e_{u}^{i}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we combine the equations and obtain

(h_{n-1}L_{n}^{i}e^{i}_{u},e^{i}_{u})+\tau(\nabla e_{w}^{i},\nabla e^{i}_{w})=(h_{n-1}(L_{n}^{i}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta))e^{i-1}_{u},e^{i}_{u}).

(4.43)

We estimate the right hand side using Young’s inequality (
2.4
) and
0\leq L^{i}_{n}-\Phi^{\prime}(\zeta)\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}
, as proven in
Lemma

4.8
, to find that for any
\rho>0
,

\begin{split}(h_{n-1}(L_{n}^{i}-\breve{\Phi}^{\prime}(\zeta))e^{i-1}_{u},e^{i}_{u})&\leq 2M\tau^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{h_{n-1}}e^{i-1}_{u}\sqrt{h_{n-1}}e^{i}_{u},\\
&\leq\frac{M\tau^{\gamma}}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}+\rho M\tau^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}.\end{split}

(4.44)

In the non-degenerate case, i.e.
\phi_{m}>0
, we estimate the left hand of (
4.43
) similarly using that
L^{i}_{n}\geq\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}
and obtain

(\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma})\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}+\tau\|\nabla e^{i}_{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq(h_{n-1}L_{n}^{i}e^{i}_{u},e^{i}_{u})+\tau\|\nabla e^{i}_{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(4.45)

Combining (
4.44
) and (
4.45
) it follows that

(\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+(1-\rho)M\tau^{\gamma})\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}(e^{i}_{u})^{2}+\tau\|\nabla e^{i}_{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{M\tau^{\gamma}}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}(e^{i-1}_{u})^{2}.

(4.46)

which implies that

\norm{(e^i_u, e^i_w)}_{M,\rho}\leq\sqrt{\frac{M\tau^{\gamma}}{\rho(\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+(1-\rho)M\tau^{\gamma})}}\norm{(e^{i-1}_u, e^{i-1}_w)}_{M,\rho},

(4.47)

where

\norm{(e^i_u, e^i_w)}_{M,\rho}^{2}\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}+\frac{\tau}{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+(1-\rho)M\tau^{\gamma}}\|\nabla e^{i}_{w}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(4.48)

Equation (
4.48
) defines a norm if
0<\rho<1+\frac{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}{M\tau^{\gamma}}
, and
choosing
0<\rho=\rho^{\ast}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}{M\tau^{\gamma}})<1+\frac{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}{M\tau^{\gamma}}
, minimizes the contraction rate. Hence,

\norm{(e^i_u, e^i_w)}_{M,\rho^{\ast}}\eqqcolon\norm{(e^i_u, e^i_w)}_{M}\leq\frac{2M\tau^{\gamma}}{\phi_{\mathrm{m}}+M\tau^{\gamma}}\norm{(e^{i-1}_u, e^{i-1}_w)}_{M},

(4.49)

which is a contraction if
\tau<({\phi_{\mathrm{m}}}/{M})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}
, and the contraction rate scales with
\tau^{\gamma}
.
As in the proof of
Lemma

4.7

we conclude that
u^{i}_{n}\to u_{n}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
w^{i}_{n}\to w_{n}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
by Banach’s fixed-point theorem.

The degenerate case is dealt with in the same manner as in the proof of
Lemma

4.7
. Analogous to (
4.22
), we find using
L^{i}_{n}\geq 2M\tau^{\gamma}
and
\varepsilon:=\inf\left(L^{i}_{n}-\left(\frac{\delta\breve{\Phi}}{\delta u}\right)^{i-1}\right)\overset{\eqref{ineq: 2}}{\geq}(M-M_{0})\tau>0
that

M\tau^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}+\tau\norm{\nabla e^i_w}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M\tau^{\gamma}\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}.

(4.50)

Summing both sides of (
4.50
) yields

\begin{split}0&\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{i}_{u}-e^{i-1}_{u}|^{2}\right)+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{N}\norm{\nabla e^i_w}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&\leq M\tau^{\gamma}\left(\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{0}_{u}|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}h_{n-1}|e^{N}_{u}|^{2}\right)<\infty.\end{split}

(4.51)

This implies that
w^{i}_{n}\to w_{n}
in
H^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
,
\breve{\Phi}(u^{i}_{n})\to w_{n}=\breve{\Phi}(u_{n})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
u^{i}_{n}\to u_{n}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. The result for the positive part of
u^{i}_{n}
follows from
Remark

4.1.1
which completes the proof.
∎

.

.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions

Problem 5.1

We test our scheme for 3 different problems: a porous medium equation, the biofilm PDE-PDE model and the biofilm PDE-ODE model. The goal is to get numerical convergence results, as well as to compare the performance of the M-scheme to the Newton.

Remark 5.1.1

(Newton scheme)
.

The ‘true’ Newton scheme with
M=0
may not converge in the degenerate case without regularization. To overcome this problem we use the M-scheme with a very small
M
(e.g.
M=10^{-7}\ll\text{tol}
) which is still large enough so that the scheme converges in most cases. This is a form of a regularized Newton scheme (
4.9
).

Remark 5.1.2

(L-scheme)
.

For the porous medium equation, the L-scheme is at least an order of magnitude slower than the M-scheme, while for the biofilm models the L required for convergence is so large, that it becomes multiple orders of magnitude slower. Therefore, we will not show the results for the L-scheme in our comparison.

Remark 5.1.3

(Adaptive M-scheme)
.

When using the M scheme, in practice,
it is beneficial to choose
M
adaptively in each step using a posteriori estimators.
In the biofilm case, with an adaptive scheme, the required
M
increases if
u_{n}
approaches 1 which ensures convergence, while
M
is small and therefore the scheme is fast when
u_{n}
is bounded away from 1.
Similar work for the L-scheme has been done in
[
44
]
. This is however beyond the scope of our current work.

6
Conclusion

We introduced a semi-implicit time-discretisation scheme for solving a class of degenerate quasi-linear parabolic problems of porous medium type with diffusion coefficients that can also be singular. Such systems model biofilm growth and other nonlinear diffusion processes with sharp interfaces that propagate at a finite speed. The well-posedness of the time-discrete solutions was shown, as well as explicit upper bounds were proved for both the biofilm model (
b=1
) and porous medium equations (
b=\infty
).
We then introduced the L/M-scheme as an iterative linearisation method for solving the quasi-linear elliptic PDEs that resulted from the time discretisation. It was shown that these schemes converge irrespective of the spatial discretisation. In the non-degenerate case, these schemes will even show a contraction with a contraction rate that scales with some power of
\tau
for the M-scheme provided
\tau
is small. Finally, the schemes were implemented numerically using a finite element method and it was shown that for larger time steps
\tau
and finer mesh sizes
h
, the M-scheme outperforms the Newton scheme.

The schemes can be generalised to systems that allow for additional substrates and admit terms for an advective flow field in these equations, see
[
29
]
. In a future work, we are considering including a nonlinearity in the time derivative as well which makes the problem doubly degenerate. Such problems are commonly found in multiphase flow through porous medium. Furthermore, one can consider multi-species biofilm models with or without cross-diffusion that comprise multiple degenerate equations that are strongly coupled through the diffusion operator, e.g. see
[
19
,
43
]
.

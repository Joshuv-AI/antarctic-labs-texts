Gravitational Faraday effect from on-shell amplitudes
Wei-Ming Chen, Ming-Zhi Chung, Yu-tin Huang, Jung-Wook Kim

Abstract
Effects of massive object’s spin on massive-massless 2 → 2 2\to 2 classical scattering is studied. Focus is set on the less-considered dimensionless expansion parameter λ / b \lambda/b , where λ \lambda is the massless particle’s wavelength and b b is the impact parameter. Corrections in λ / b \lambda/b start to appear from 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 2 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{2}) , with leading correction terms tied to the gravitational Faraday effect, which is a special case of the Lense-Thirring effect. We compute the eikonal phase up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 2 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{2}) and extract spin effect on the scattering angle and time delay up to 14th order in spin. The gravitational Faraday effect at linear order in spin Ishihara:1987dv is reproduced by λ / b \lambda/b correction terms, which we compute to higher orders in spin. We find that the equivalence principle, or universality, holds up to NLO for general spinning bodies, i.e. away from geometric optics limit. Furthermore, in the black hole limit, we confirm the absence of particular spin structure observed Bern:2020buy ; Aoude:2020ygw ; Kosmopoulos:2021zoq ; Chen:2021qkk ; Aoude:2022trd ; Bern:2022kto ; Aoude:2022thd , along with the associated shift symmetry Bern:2022kto , and argue that it holds to arbitrary spin order at 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 2 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{2}) in the massless probe limit.

In this paper, we revisit the classical limit of the gravitational Compton amplitude with spin effects included. The goal is twofold: on the one hand a rotating object will induce Lense–Thirring (or gravitomagnetic frame-dragging) effects, which can rotate the polarization plane of propagating electromagnetic or gravitational waves as in fig.

\displaystyle\left[\frac{m\sqrt{-t}}{s-m^{2}}\right]\sim\frac{\lambda}{b}

The introduction of the spin vector brings three new invariants due to the transverse condition
p\cdot S=0

as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (q\cdot S)&\sim\frac{ma}{b}&&\Rightarrow&&&\left[\frac{q\cdot S}{m}\right]&\sim\frac{a}{b}\\
(n\cdot S)&\sim\frac{m^{2}a}{\lambda b}&&\Rightarrow&&&\left[\frac{n\cdot S}{m(s-m^{2})}\right]&\sim\frac{a}{b}\\
(k_{2}\cdot S)&\sim\frac{ma}{\lambda}&&\Rightarrow&&&\left[\frac{\sqrt{-t}(k_{2}\cdot S)}{s-m^{2}}\right]&\sim\frac{a}{b}\,.\end{aligned}

(20)

The orthogonal vector

. These invariants form a basis for

\displaystyle F_{(L,i,j)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha+\beta+\gamma=j\\
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0\end{subarray}}c_{i,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\left[\frac{m\sqrt{-t}}{s-m^{2}}\right]^{i}\left[\frac{q\cdot S}{m}\right]^{\alpha}\left[\frac{n\cdot S}{m(s-m^{2})}\right]^{\beta}\left[\frac{\sqrt{-t}(k_{2}\cdot S)}{s-m^{2}}\right]^{\gamma}\,,

is a rational function, the powers of

must add up to an even number and we have the constraint

1.

There are no
\lambda/b
corrections at
{\cal O}(G^{1})
. The scalar term
M_{4}^{(0,0,0)}
contains a
t^{-1}
pole prefactor. As any expansion in
\lambda/b
must be combined to form integer powers of
-t=|\vec{q}|^{2}
, the corrections will cancel the
t^{-1}
pole and result in derivatives of delta functions in impact parameter space, which do not contribute to long range effects.

2.

At
{\cal O}(G^{2})
, the scalar term
M_{4}^{(1,0,0)}
contains a
1/|\vec{q}|
prefactor from the scalar triangle integral. The non-analytic structure of the
1/|\vec{q}|
prefactor yields long range contributions which cannot be cancelled by integer powers of
-t
, allowing arbitrary order
\lambda/b
corrections. The condition
i+\gamma\in 2\mathbb{Z}
forces odd powers of
\lambda/b
corrections to accompany odd powers of
(k_{2}\cdot S)
, and the first
\lambda/b
correction to be at
(\lambda/b)^{2}
order for spinless case (
j=0
). The corrections at this order are dependent on conventions for the eikonal phase as explained in appendix
A
.

As we will argue in the following, the polarisation plane rotation is an
\mathcal{O}(\lambda/b)
correction from the leading eikonal approximation, thus it is first observed at one-loop order. his is consistent with the conclusion in ref.
Asada:2000vn
that gravitational Faraday effect cannot be observed at
{\cal O}(G^{1})
order.

2.2
Subleading
\lambda/b
corrections and rotations of polarization planes

Previous studies on eikonal phases of gravitational Compton scattering mostly ignored subleading effects in the
\lambda/b
expansion. These subleading corrections are related to the wave-like nature of the massless particle since they depend on the wavelength
\lambda
of the particle, but which physical properties are these terms reflecting?

To answer this question, we consider geometric optics. The propagation of light (or gravitational wave) is treated as a classical curve where the tangent vector at each point of the curve is normal to the wavefront. For an introduction, see e.g. §53 of ref.
landau1975classical
. We consider the propagation of waves as a classical trajectory in the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
or
\lambda/b\ll 1
. Selecting a component of the wave (e.g. any component of
\vec{E}
or
\vec{B}
for a electromagnetic wave) and denoting it as
f
, we can write an ansatz
7
7

7

The terminology
WKB approximation
is also used in the literature for this ansatz, e.g. ref.
Isaacson:1968hbi
.

\displaystyle f=ae^{i\psi}\,,

(22)

where
a=a(x,t)
is the amplitude of the particular component and
\psi=\psi(x,t)
is the phase of the wave.
\psi
is called the
eikonal
and its derivative
\bar{k}_{\mu}=-\partial_{\mu}\psi
is regarded as the local wavenumber four-vector of the ray, which is normal to the wavefront and tangent to the propagation direction
landau1975classical
. Since
\psi
changes by
2\pi
as one transverses one
\lambda
, the limit
\lambda\to 0
implies large gradient of
\psi
. Now recall that the wave equation is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{\mu}\partial x^{\mu}}=\left[\frac{\partial^{2}a}{\partial x_{\mu}\partial x^{\mu}}e^{i\psi}+2i\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{\mu}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x^{\mu}}e^{i\psi}+\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x_{\mu}\partial x^{\mu}}f\right]-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{\mu}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x^{\mu}}f=0\,.

(23)

Since
\partial\psi\sim\frac{1}{\lambda}
is large, we expect the first three terms in square brackets to be subleading compared to the last by a factor of
\lambda/b
. Amongst the three terms in the square brackets, since
\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{\mu}}
is accompanied by a factor of
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x^{\mu}}
, the subleading corrections at this order will be dominated by the change in the wave amplitude denoted as
a
, which also encodes polarisation data. Thus if we can identify
\psi
with
\delta(b)
, the
\lambda/b
corrections reflect polarisation dependence. We provide an argument in appendix
B
on why we can identify the eikonal
\psi
in eq.(
22
) as the eikonal phase
\delta(b)
in eq.(
13
). Indeed in section
4
, our explicit results show that

\delta_{{+},{+}}=\delta_{{-},{-}}\times\left[1+{\cal O}\left(\lambda/b\right)\right]\,.

(24)

that is, the polarization dependence occurs at subleading order in
\lambda/b
.

The difference
2\alpha_{3}\equiv(\delta_{+,+}-\delta_{-,-})
, which characterises the first subleading order in
\lambda/b
, has an interpretation as
the rotation angle of the polarisation plane
.
To see this, consider positive and negative helicity one-particle states propagating along the
z
direction. The two states will acquire opposite phases under a rotation of angle
\alpha
around the
z
-axes, which is simply a little group rotation.

|{+}\rangle^{\prime}\rightarrow e^{-i\alpha J^{12}}|{+}\rangle=e^{-ih\alpha}|{+}\rangle,\quad|{-}\rangle^{\prime}\rightarrow e^{-i\alpha J^{12}}|{-}\rangle=e^{+ih\alpha}|{-}\rangle\,.

(25)

The difference of the phase gained by positive and negative helicity photons (
|h|=1
) is tied to the rotation angle of the polarisation plane
\alpha
by

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta_{\gamma,{+},{+}}-\delta_{\gamma,{-},{-}}}{2}\,.

(26)

There is an additional factor of 2 for gravitons (
|h|=2
),

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta_{g,{+},{+}}-\delta_{g,{-},{-}}}{4}\,,

(27)

which compensates for the spin of the graviton. We show that indeed the difference in eikonal phase reproduces the result of ref.
Ishihara:1987dv
, where the polarisation plan rotation angle was computed by considering a parallel transport along the light ray’s null geodesic. Finally, we see that the angle can be identified with the
\sigma_{3}
component of the eikonal phase matrix:
\alpha=-\alpha_{3}/|h|
.

4.1.1
Extraction of box and crossed box coefficients

The box coefficient can be computed from the quadruple cut using unitarity methods
Bern:1994zx
;
Bern:1994cg
;
Bern:1997sc
;
Forde:2007mi
;
Kilgore:2007qr
. We can impose one more cut condition (see fig.
4
)
\ell\cdot k_{2}=0
to get the box cut or
\ell\cdot k_{3}=0
to get the crossed box cut. For each quadruple cut, there are two solutions to loop momentum which solve the cut conditions. We use the loop momentum parametrisation of ref.
Bern:2020buy
to compute the quadruple cut, with small modifications to account for masslessness of
k_{2}^{\mu}
.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \ell^{\mu}&=\alpha p_{1}^{\mu}+\beta k_{2}^{\mu}+\gamma q^{\mu}+\delta\eta^{\mu}\,,\\
\eta^{\mu}&=\langle{p_{1}^{\flat}}|\sigma^{\mu}|{k_{2}}]\,,\,p_{1}^{\flat}=p_{1}-\frac{m}{2\omega}k_{2}\,.\end{aligned}

(59)

p_{1}^{\flat}
is a null vector computed using Mandelstam parametrisations eq.(
14
). The solutions to the cut conditions,

\displaystyle\ell^{2}=p_{1}\cdot\ell=k_{2}\cdot\ell=0\,,\,q\cdot\ell=-\frac{q^{2}}{2}\,,

(60)

are given as

\displaystyle\ell_{-}^{\mu}=-\frac{q^{2}}{2q\cdot\eta}\eta^{\mu}\,,\,\ell_{+}^{\mu}=\frac{-(2q^{2}\omega)p_{1}^{\mu}+2q^{2}(m+\omega)k_{2}^{\mu}-4m\omega^{2}q^{\mu}}{mq^{2}+2q^{2}\omega+4m\omega^{2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{2q\cdot\eta}\eta^{\mu}\,.

(61)

The coefficient of the box integral can be computed as an average over the two loop momentum solutions
\ell_{\pm}
,

\displaystyle c_{\square,+\pm}^{\{h\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=\ell_{\pm}}\Big[C_{\square}^{+\pm}(\ell,-\ell_{1})\mathcal{S}_{\{+\pm\}}(1,\ell,-\ell_{1},4)+C_{\square}^{+\pm}(\ell_{1},-\ell)\mathcal{S}_{\{+\pm\}}(1,\ell_{1},-\ell,4)\Big]_{\ell\cdot k_{2}=0}\,,

(62)

where
C_{\square}^{\pm+}
is the part of
[C_{\{\pm+\}}(-2\ell\cdot k_{2})]_{\ell\cdot k_{2}=0}
without the spin factor
\mathcal{S}_{\{\pm+\}}
. The (helicity factor stripped) crossed box coefficient can be obtained from that of the box by the substitution
k_{2}\to-k_{3}
,
k_{3}\to-k_{2}
,
h_{2}\to-h_{3}
, and
h_{3}\to-h_{2}
. For example,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{c_{\square,++}^{\{h\}}}{(\langle 3|p_{1}|2])^{h}}\right|_{\pm k_{2}\leftrightarrow\mp k_{3}}=\frac{c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}},--}^{\{h\}}}{(\langle 2|p_{1}|3])^{h}}\,.

(63)

Similar relations hold for other helicity sectors such as
c_{\square,+-}^{\{h\}}
and
c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}},++}^{\{h\}}
.

4.1.2
Extraction of triangle coefficients

For the triple-cut as shown in fig.
4
, the cut-satisfied loop momentum can be parametrized as

\displaystyle\ell^{\mu}_{\pm}=-\left(\frac{t}{t-4m^{2}}\right)p_{1}^{\mu}+\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{t-4m^{2}}\right)q^{\mu}+zP_{\pm}^{\mu}+\left[\frac{m^{2}t}{4\gamma_{\pm}(t-4m^{2})}\right]\frac{Q_{\pm}^{\mu}}{z}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-t\pm\sqrt{t(t-4m^{2})}\right)\,.

(65)

The explicit forms of

are not important but they satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle P_{\pm}^{2}=Q_{\pm}^{2}=P_{\pm}\cdot p_{1}=P_{\pm}\cdot q=Q_{\pm}\cdot p_{1}=Q_{\pm}\cdot q=0\,,~~~P_{\pm}\cdot Q_{\pm}=2\gamma_{\pm}\,,

(66)

which imply a useful formula for the loop computation,

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544ptP_{\pm}^{\mu}Q_{\pm}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}\eta^{\mu\nu}+\frac{4\gamma_{\pm}}{t-4m^{2}}\left[p_{1}^{\mu}p_{1}^{\nu}+\frac{m^{2}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{t}+\frac{p_{1}^{\mu}q^{\nu}+q^{\mu}p_{1}^{\nu}}{2}-\frac{i(2m^{2}+\gamma_{\pm})}{2\gamma_{\pm}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(p_{1})_{\rho}q_{\sigma}\right]\,.

By averaging the residues of the triple-cut with loop momentum solutions eq.(
64
) at

, we can obtain the triangle coefficients

, formally as,

\displaystyle c_{\{+,\pm\}}^{\{h\}}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Res}_{z=\infty}\left[\sum_{\ell=\ell_{\pm}}C_{\{+\pm\}}^{\{h\}}(\ell)\right]\,.

(68)

Note that for the helicity preserving case, we can rewrite eq.(
56
) as

\displaystyle C_{\{+,+\}}^{\{h\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{[2\ell]}{[3\ell]}\right)^{2h}\frac{\mathrm{tr}\left[(1-\gamma_{5})k_{3}\ell p_{1}\ell_{1}\right]^{4}S_{\{++\}}(1,\ell,-\ell_{1},4)}{t^{3}(2\ell\cdot k_{3})(2\ell_{1}\cdot k_{3})}\bigg|_{\mathrm{eq.}\eqref{triplec}}+(\ell\longleftrightarrow\ell_{1})\,.

In the leading order in
(q/\omega)
-expansion,
[2\ell]\simeq[3\ell]
, so

is independent of
|h|
and this can be understood as a manifestation of the equivalence principle. However, in the subleading order
C_{\{+,+\}}^{\{h\}}
is no longer
|h|
independent and the equivalence principle ceases to apply.

4.2
Eikonal phase and its exponentiation

The main contribution to tree order eikonal phase matrix comes from helicity preserving processes, therefore studying
c_{\square}^{++}
is enough for checking exponentiation. The box coefficient for the helicity non-preserving case
c_{\square}^{+-}
either vanishes (
|h|<2
) or is suppressed by
{\cal O}((q/\omega)^{4})
compared to
c_{\square}^{++}
(
|h|=2
) which is beyond the regime where exponentiation holds.

An interesting behaviour of the quadruple cut eq.(

) is that only one helicity configuration contributes to each loop momentum solution
\ell_{+}
and
\ell_{-}
, thus the sum over all cut solutions and intermediate massless states becomes a sum over two terms

\displaystyle c_{\square,++}^{\{h\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[C_{\square}^{++}(\ell_{+},-\ell_{1+})\mathcal{S}_{\{++\}}(1,\ell_{+},-\ell_{1+},4)

(70)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+C_{\square}^{++}(\ell_{1-},-\ell_{-})\mathcal{S}_{\{++\}}(1,\ell_{1-},-\ell_{-},4)\Big]_{\ell\cdot k_{2}=0}\,,~~~

where the spin factors are

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\{++\}}(\ell_{\pm})

\displaystyle=\oint\prod_{k=1,2}\left[\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi iz_{k}}\left(\sum_{j}C_{\text{S}^{j}}z_{k}^{j}\right)\right]e^{ib_{\pm}^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}}\,,

(71)

where
{\cal S}_{\{++\}}(\ell_{+})
and
{\cal S}_{\{++\}}(\ell_{-})
refer to the spin factors appearing in the first and second line of (
70
) respectively.
Note that the

{\cal S}_{\{++\}}(\ell_{\pm})
are distinct

By using the loop momentum solutions (
61
) and the explicit form of spin factor (
53
) subjected to the cut conditions
the exponent of the spin factor can be reorganised as

\displaystyle ib_{\pm}^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}}+\frac{1}{z_{2}}\right)\frac{i(n\cdot S)}{2m^{2}\omega}\mp\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{2}}\right)\frac{(q\cdot S)}{2m}+{\cal O}(|\vec{q}|^{2},\hbar^{1})\,,

(72)

where the subleading classical terms of order
{\cal O}(|\vec{q}|^{2}\hbar^{0})
have been omitted, since they do not contribute to exponentiation. However, the distinct spin factors can be reorganised into a common factor that can be pulled out from the sum eq.(
70
), due to the symmetry of the auxiliary integrals in
z_{k}
;
b_{+}^{\mu\nu}
becomes
b_{-}^{\mu\nu}
to the order considered in eq.(
72

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\square,++}^{\{h\}}&=\oint\prod_{k=1,2}\left[\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi iz_{k}}\left(\sum_{j}C_{\text{S}^{j}}z_{k}^{j}\right)\right]e^{ib^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}}\times c_{\square\,,\,(s=0)}^{++}\left[1+{\cal O}(|\vec{q}|^{2},\hbar^{1})\right]\,,\\

(73)

where we have omitted the subscript in
b_{-}^{\mu\nu}=b^{\mu\nu}
for brevity. Up to the considered order, the spin factor
e^{ib^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}}
is the same spin factor eq.(
124

Applying the rules (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}^{++}&=\oint\prod_{k=1,2}\left[\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi iz_{k}}\left(\sum_{j}C_{\text{S}^{j}}z_{k}^{j}\right)\right]e^{ib^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}}\times c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}\,,\,(s=0)}^{++}\left[1+{\cal O}(|\vec{q}|^{2},\hbar^{1})\right]\,.\end{aligned}

The box coefficient and the crossed box coefficient for the massive scalar are equal in the classical limit, i.e.

\displaystyle c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}\,,\,(s=0)}^{++}=c_{\square\,,\,(s=0)}^{++}\left[1+{\cal O}(\hbar^{1})\right]\,,\,c_{\square\,,\,(s=0)}^{++}=16(\kappa/2)^{4}m^{4}\omega^{4}\left[1+{\cal O}(|\vec{q}|^{2},\hbar^{1})\right]\,,

\displaystyle c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}^{++}

\displaystyle c_{\square}^{--}

(78)

The difference term should be added as corrections to one-loop eikonal phase, corresponding to NLO for
\alpha

In impact parameter space, eq.(

\displaystyle c_{\square\,,\,(s=0)}^{++}\left[{\cal I}_{\square}+{\cal I}_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}\right]\Leftrightarrow-\frac{[\delta_{\text{S}^{0}}^{(0)}(b)]^{2}}{2}\,.

(79)

The full spinning contribution in impact parameter space can be written as

\displaystyle\left[c_{\square}^{++}{\cal I}_{\square}+c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}^{++}{\cal I}_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}\right]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\oint\prod_{k=1,2}\left[\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi iz_{k}}\left(\sum_{j}C_{\text{S}^{j}}z_{k}^{j}\right)\right]e^{ib^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}}\times\left[-\frac{[\delta_{\text{S}^{0}}^{(0)}(b)]^{2}}{2}\right]\,,

(80)

where the spin factor exponent is a derivative operator

\displaystyle ib^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}}+\frac{1}{z_{2}}\right)\frac{\vec{d}\cdot\nabla_{b}}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}}-\frac{1}{z_{2}}\right)\frac{i(\vec{a}_{\perp}\cdot\nabla_{b})}{2}\,,

(81)

{\cal O}(S^{n})
, the

Note that for general Wilson coefficients the resulting expression will be different from “squaring” the tree eikonal phase eq.(

\displaystyle\left[c_{\square}^{++}{\cal I}_{\square}+c_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}^{++}{\cal I}_{\,\rotatebox{90.0}{\scalebox{0.6}[0.9]{$\bowtie$}}}+\frac{[\delta^{(0)}(b)]^{2}}{2}\right]_{{\cal O}(S^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{4G^{2}m^{2}\omega^{2}((\vec{a}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b})^{2}-(\vec{d}\cdot\vec{b})^{2})}{b^{4}}(C_{\text{S}^{2}}-1)\,.

where for the LHS we’ve taken the quadratic in spin part of the difference between the box contribution and the square of tree eikonal phase.

Such mismatch similarly occurs for higher spin orders. In the Kerr limit
C_{\text{S}^{j}}=1
the spin factor simplifies to

\displaystyle\oint\prod_{k=1,2}\left[\frac{dz_{k}}{2\pi iz_{k}}\left(\sum_{j}C_{\text{S}^{j}}z_{k}^{j}\right)\right]e^{ib^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C_{\text{S}^{j}}\to 1}}{{\rightarrow}}\exp\left(-\frac{i(n\cdot S)}{m^{2}\omega}\right)\Leftrightarrow\exp\left(\vec{d}\cdot\nabla_{b}\right)\,,

(83)

which is a constant shift by
\vec{d}=\hat{k}_{2}\times\vec{a}
in impact parameter space. This is consistent with the fact that the tree eikonal phase for Kerr is obtained from the spinless case by a constant shift in impact parameter space

\displaystyle\delta^{(0)}_{\text{Kerr}}(\vec{b})

\displaystyle=\delta^{(0)}_{\text{Scalar}}(\vec{b}+\vec{d})\,,

(84)

and exponentiation becomes manifest. The fact that the exponentiation of the eikonal phase correctly captures the eikonal limit of the amplitude only for black hole, or equivalently minimal coupling, is reminiscent of similar statement for

;

4.3
Results

We are interested in the helicity-preserving parts of the eikonal phase in the classical limit, which we decompose as

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\delta_{+,+}+\delta_{-,-}}{2}\,,\,\alpha:=\frac{\delta_{-,-}-\delta_{+,+}}{2|h|}\,.

(85)

The superscripts and subscripts for
\delta(b)

\bar{\delta}
and
\alpha

As explained in section

,

is identified as the polarisation plane rotation angle. For all spin sectors we find

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\bar{\delta}}

\displaystyle\sim{\cal O}\left(\frac{1}{\omega b}\right)\,,

(86)

confirming the expectation eq.(
24

The leading order terms of
\bar{\delta}
and

This means the observables do not depend on the details of the particle, which can be understood as a manifestation of the equivalence principle. For the computations considered in this paper, the equivalence principle is realised as the independence of
\bar{\delta}
and
\alpha
from helicity
|h|
of the massless particle. The term equivalence principle will be used to denote this helicity independence when referring to eikonal phase variables.

Since
\lambda/b
is the classical expansion parameter that parametrises deviations from the geometric optics limit, we expect the equivalence principle to be obeyed by
\bar{\delta}
and
\alpha
at leading order in
\lambda/b
. Indeed this is the case. The LO
\bar{\delta}
and
\alpha
obeys the equivalence principle, at least up to one-loop order. The general expectation is that the equivalence principle for
\bar{\delta}
and
\alpha
is broken at sufficiently high orders in
\lambda/b
, since the corrections in
\lambda/b
can be interpreted as wave property corrections to the point particle approximation; the equivalence principle only applies to the strict point particle limit where tidal effects can be neglected, while a characteristic property of waves is that they cannot be localised to a point. Interestingly, the equivalence principle is still obeyed at NLO for both
\bar{\delta}
and
\alpha
.

Spin structures

As mentioned in the introduction, the product of the two three point amplitudes transform nicely under the spin-shift eq.(
1
) in the black hole limit. In particular, this suggests that the 2PM classical Compton amplitude is invariant. Let us verify this explicitly.

All LO triangle coefficients
c_{\bar{\delta},\mathrm{LO}}
and
c_{\alpha,\mathrm{LO}}
have the special property that their dependence on
(q\cdot S)
vanish in the Kerr limit
C_{\text{S}^{j}}\to 1
. That is, projecting the spin-vector on the basis of

\{(q\cdot S)\,,\,(k_{2}\cdot S)\,,\,(n\cdot S)\},

(98)

only the latter two appears. In the all massive setup relevant for the 2PM potential, similar phenomenon was observed, which lead to the conclusion that the result is invariant under the spin-shift. Indeed while
(n\cdot S)
is manifestly invariant under the shift, the change in
(p_{2}\cdot S_{1})
is a
quantum
effect:
(p_{2}\cdot S_{1})\sim{\cal O}(\hbar^{-1})
is shifted by
\frac{p_{2}\cdot q}{q^{2}}\sim{\cal O}(\hbar^{0})
where we’ve used momentum conservation
(p_{j}\cdot q)=\pm q^{2}/2
;
\frac{(p_{j}\cdot q)/q^{2}}{(p_{j}\cdot S_{i})}={\cal O}(\hbar^{1})
, thus the shift is a quantum effect and the classical result is invariant. In our massless-massive setup one naively encounters a violation of this symmetry. This is because the term
(k_{2}\cdot S)\sim{\cal O}(\hbar^{0})
shifts as
\frac{k_{2}\cdot q}{q^{2}}\sim{\cal O}(\hbar^{0})
, i.e. it is shifted by a classical effect. This observation poses a puzzle as the LO triangle coefficient is not shift invariant, but the integrand used to compute it was manifestly shift invariant.

The apparant paradox is resolved when NLO triangle coefficients are also included in the analysis. The NLO triangle coefficients all contain a factor of
(q\cdot S)
, which is manifestly not shift invariant. However, the terms arising from the shift
(q\cdot S)\to(q\cdot S)+\xi
is
no longer subleading
in the
\lambda/b
expansion, and the shift-generated terms of both the LO and NLO triangle coefficients are of the same order in the
\lambda/b
expansion. Explicit calculations show that the two terms cancel, and the shift symmetry is restored by the interplay of LO and NLO triangle coefficients.

As a concrete example, consider triangle coefficients at linear order in spin.

\displaystyle c_{\bar{\delta},\mathrm{LO}}^{\mathrm{S}^{1}}=20im^{2}\omega(n\cdot S)\,,\,c_{\alpha,\mathrm{LO}}^{\mathrm{S}^{1}}=5m^{3}q^{2}(k_{2}\cdot S)\,,\,c^{\,\text{S}^{1}}_{\alpha,\mathrm{NLO}}=\frac{5m^{3}q^{2}(q\cdot S)}{2}\,.

(99)

Under the shift eq.(
1
) each coefficient transforms as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\bar{\delta},\mathrm{LO}}^{\mathrm{S}^{1}}&\to c_{\bar{\delta},\mathrm{LO}}^{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\,,\\
c_{\alpha,\mathrm{LO}}^{\mathrm{S}^{1}}&\to c_{\alpha,\mathrm{LO}}^{\mathrm{S}^{1}}-\frac{5m^{3}q^{2}}{2}\xi_{1}\,,\\
c^{\,\text{S}^{1}}_{\alpha,\mathrm{NLO}}&\to c^{\,\text{S}^{1}}_{\alpha,\mathrm{NLO}}+\frac{5m^{3}q^{2}}{2}\xi_{1}\,,\end{aligned}

(100)

and shift-generated terms cancel due to the interference between LO and NLO contributions. Similar behaviour can be observed for terms of higher order in spin or
\lambda/b
expansion.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we derive the
\mathcal{O}(G^{2})
eikonal phase of graviton and photons scattering of general spinning objects. This corresponds to the classical limit of the one-loop amplitude, which in terms of scalar integral basis is given by the scalar triangle integral. We extract its coefficient by computing the triple cut, which is the product of massless four-point amplitude and two massive spinning three-point amplitudes. From the result we extract the leading order gravitational Faraday effect,
\alpha:=-\alpha_{3}/|h|
for polarized electromagnetic and gravitational waves, which is given as an expansion in the ratio of spin-length and impact parameter,
a/b
. While the linear term for Kerr black hole matches with result in
Ishihara:1987dv
, we provide the result for general spinning body up to 14th order in spin.

In computing the 2PM eikonal phase, the contribution from the box and cross-box nicely reproduces the square of the 1 PM eikonal phase in the Kerr-limit, indicating that the exponentiation correctly captures the eikonal limit amplitude. This is no longer true for general Wilson coefficients. Such observation appears to be consistent with long known result that for electromagnetic scattering
Cheng:1971gf
;
Meng:1972xt
;
Weinberg:1971cdi
;
Czyz:1975bf
, the amplitude only exponentiates for
g=2
. However this appears to render the extraction of classical observables for neutron stars from eikonal amplitude challenging. We leave the resolution of this issue to future work.

In black hole limit, the
\mathcal{O}(G^{2})
eikonal phase is invariant under the shift transformation of the spin vector eq.(
1
)
Aoude:2022trd
;
Bern:2022kto
;
Aoude:2022thd
, whose origin can be traced back to the exponentiated form of the product of spinning three-point amplitudes. As discussed in the introduction, for
{\cal O}(G^{2})
calculation the spin factor gets multiplied by a factor of

\exp\left(-\xi\frac{(\sum_{i{\in}{+}}k_{i}\cdot q){-}(\sum_{j{\in}{-}}k_{j}\cdot q)}{q^{2}}\right)

(104)

which is in general non-unity. Beyond
\mathcal{O}(G^{2})
, one would have to consider the product of
n
three-point amplitudes in the cut, where
n\geq 3
. We expect the simple form of eq.(
104
) to be generalised for arbitrary multiple products of three-point amplitudes, when the calculations are reformulated in the framework such as HEFT
Brandhuber:2021kpo
;
Brandhuber:2021eyq
/HPET
Damgaard:2019lfh
;
Aoude:2020onz
where massive-massless coupling vertices are treated in a homogeneous manner. If the expectation is indeed the case, then the simplicity of the violation brings hope that one can write down the explicit form of the “anomaly”, by inserting this factor into the unitarity cuts. We leave this for future exploration.

Finally we find that at LO in
\lambda/b
the eikonal phase is universal between photons and gravitons, while deviation occurs at NNLO. Given that the expansion in
\lambda/b
is an expansion around the geometric optics approximation, the violation can be interpreted as the breakdown of eikonal approximation. Indeed we see that at this order, the overall box topology contribution no longer matches the square of the tree eikonal phase as required from exponentiation. It would be interesting nonetheless to explore whether sensible observables can be extracted from these terms.

The Reverse-log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality Keywords: log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture, reverse-to-forward principle.
Dongmeng Xi

Abstract
Firstly, we propose our conjectured Reverse-log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality (RLBM). Secondly, we show that the (RLBM) conjecture is equivalent to the log-Brunn-Minkowski (LBM) conjecture proposed by Böröczky-Lutwak-Yang-Zhang. We name this as “reverse-to-forward” (RTF) principle. Using this principle, we give a very simple new proof of the log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality in dimension two. Finally, we establish the (RTF) principle for the conjectured log-Minkowski inequality (LM). Using this principle, we prove the log-Minkowski inequality in the case that one convex body is a zonoid (the inequality part was first proved by van Handel). Via a study of the lemma of relations, the full equality conditions (the bodies have “dilated direct summands”) are also characterized, which turns to be new.

Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer.
In their seminal work
[
1
,
2
]
, Böröczky-Lutwak-Yang-Zhang (Böröczky-LYZ) proposed the following

Log-Brunn-Minkowski (LBM) conjecture
: For all origin-symmetric convex bodies
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
, it holds that

V(K_{t})\geq V(K_{0})^{1-t}V(K_{1})^{t},\quad\forall t\in(0,1),

(1.1)

where

K_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot v\leq h_{K_{0}}(v)^{1-t}h_{K_{1}}(v)^{t},\forall v\in S^{n-1}\}.

(1.2)

Equality is achieved if and only if

K_{0}
and
K_{1}
have dilated direct summands.

They verified the conjecture in dimension 2.
They also proved that (LBM) conjecture is equivalent to the following

Log-Minkowski (LM) conjecture.
For all origin-symmetric convex bodies
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
, it holds that

\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\frac{h_{K_{1}}}{h_{K_{0}}}dV_{K_{0}}\geq\frac{V(K_{0})}{n}\log\frac{V(K_{1})}{V(K_{0})},

(1.3)

with equality if and only if

K_{0}
and
K_{1}
have dilated direct summands. Here
V_{K}
is called the
cone-volume measure
, defined for Borel subsets of
S^{n-1}
, as the volume of the convex hull of the origin and the pre-image of the Borel subset under the generalized Gauss map
\nu_{K}
(where almost all boundary points have unique outer normal vectors),

V_{K}(\omega)=V({\rm conv}\{o,\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)\}),\quad{\rm for~Borel~}\omega\subset S^{n-1}.

Their work
[
1
,
2
]
also suggested that these conjectures are
equivalent to

Uniqueness conjecture of the logarithmic Minkowski problem.
If
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are origin-symmetric convex bodies such that

V_{K_{0}}=V_{K_{1}},

then
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
have dilated direct summands.

In recent years, a significant breakthrough was obtained by
Kolesnikov-Milman
[
5
]
(local version) and Chen-Huang-Li-Liu
[
3
]
(local-to-global), in which they proved the
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture for
p
slightly smaller than 1 and
showed that the (LM) conjectured inequality is equivalent to a spectral-estimate of the so-called ‘Hilbert-Brunn-Minkowski operator’.
For the case that the ‘base body’
K
is a zonoid,
van Handel
[
4
]
proved the (LM) inequality, by estimating the spectral-gap of the Hilbert-Brunn-Minkowski operator
K
. But the conjectured full equality conditions seem to be open.

A quick review of the equivalence of the conjectures by Böröczky-LYZ.
The (LBM) conjecture says that the function

f(t)=\log V(K_{t})

should be concave on
(0,1)
. If (LBM) is affirmative, then by concavity,

f^{\prime}(0)\geq f(1)-f(0)\geq f^{\prime}(1).

Meanwhile, Aleksandrov’s variation formula implies

f^{\prime}(0)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\frac{h_{K_{1}}}{h_{K_{0}}}dV_{K_{0}},\quad f^{\prime}(1)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\frac{h_{K_{1}}}{h_{K_{0}}}dV_{K_{1}}.

(1.4)

Then, (LM) follows.
The equality characterization follows from the strict concavity of
f
(if
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
do not have dilated direct summands).
For the uniqueness problem, if
V_{K_{0}}=V_{K_{1}}
, then

K_{0}
and
K_{1}
should have dilated direct summands because
f^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime}(1)
.
Here, we omit the review of the part concerning the ‘uniqueness conjecture implying (LM) conjecture and (LBM) conjecture’.

If we view the ‘uniqueness conjecture’ as the ultimate goal (actually equivalent to (LBM)), then the insight from the above review can be extracted as follows. Along with the ‘path’
K_{t}
given by (
1.2
), one can take differential to obtain (
1.4
). And since
K_{1}
can be very arbitrary, (
1.4
) actually describes all the information of the measure
V_{K_{0}}
. This makes us to consider the following nature question:

Question.

Is there the other ‘path’
\widetilde{K}_{t}
, such that
t\mapsto V(\widetilde{K}_{t})
has a certain kind of convexity, and such that one can differentiate
V(\widetilde{K}_{t})
and the differential also describes all the information of the cone-volume measure
V_{K_{0}}
?

The idea can be illustrated as follows. Studying the log-concavity of the volume functional along the ’old path’ (the (LBM) conjecture) seems too difficult to approach directly. However, one might attempt to identify a ’new path’ as suggested in the above question, along which it may be easier to investigate a certain convexity of the volume functional. This approach could then lead to achieving the ultimate goal-uniqueness conjecture.

The new path in the current paper is defined as follows.

Definition
.

For
K_{0},K_{1}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
,
define
\widetilde{K}_{t}
, for each
t\in(0,1)
, to be the unique origin-symmetric convex body that solves the following Minkowski problem

dS_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}(v)=\left[\frac{dS_{K_{0}}}{d(S_{K_{0}}+S_{K_{1}})}(v)\right]^{1-t}\left[\frac{dS_{K_{1}}}{d(S_{K_{0}}+S_{K_{1}})}(v)\right]^{t}d(S_{K_{0}}+S_{K_{1}})(v),

(1.5)

where
{dS_{K_{i}}}/{d(S_{K_{0}}+S_{K_{1}})}
, for
i=0,1
,
is the Radon-Nikodym differential. Here the Radon-Nikodym differential exists
because that
S_{K_{i}}
is always absolutely continuous with respect to
d(S_{K_{0}}+S_{K_{1}})
. We also denote it by

\widetilde{K}_{t}=(1-t)\cdot K_{0}\#_{0}t\cdot K_{1},

and name it as
log-Blaschke combination
.

The general expression in (
1.5
) may seem a little complicated, but there are two other special cases that are useful and easy to understand. If
S_{K_{0}}
and
S_{K_{1}}
are absolutely continuous w.r.t. each other, then

dS_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}=\left(\frac{dS_{K_{1}}}{dS_{K_{0}}}\right)^{t}dS_{K_{0}}

(1.6)

If both of
S_{K_{0}}
and
S_{K_{1}}
have density functions
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
, then
\widetilde{K}_{t}
is the convex body whose support function is the unique even solution to the following Monge-Amperé equation on
S^{n-1}

\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)=\varphi_{0}^{1-t}\varphi_{1}^{t}.

In general,
\widetilde{K}_{t}
will converge to a body contained in
K_{0}
as
t\to 0
, and similarly as
t\to 1
. Under the assumption that
S_{K_{0}}
and
S_{K_{1}}
are absolutely continuous w.r.t. each other, we can prove (Lemma
2.4
)

\lim_{t\to 0}\widetilde{K}_{t}=K_{0}\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{t\to 1}\widetilde{K}_{t}=K_{1}.

The convergence is in Hausdorff metric.

Proposed here is,

Reverse-log-Brunn-Minkowski (RLBM) conjecture.
Let
K_{0},K_{1}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
. Then,

V(\widetilde{K}_{t})\leq V(K_{0})^{1-t}V(K_{1})^{t},\quad\forall t\in(0,1).

(1.7)

Equality is achieved if and only if

K_{0}
and
K_{1}
have dilated direct summands.

In the subsequent context, we will proceed with the actions outlined in the Abstract.
In Section 2, we show that the (RLBM) conjecture is equivalent to the log-Brunn-Minkowski (LBM) conjecture.

The equivalence of the inequalities was also noticed independently by Emanule Milman by using the “local-to-global” principle.

Indeed, we prove in Theorem 2.6 that the (LBM) inequality is equivalent to the (RLBM) inequality (without assuming equality characterization were proved), and is equivalent to a “seemingly weaker” inequality

V(\widetilde{K}_{t})\leq V(K_{t}).

(1.8)

We prove in Corollary 2.7 that if one of the inequalities is verified, then the equality characterizations of (LBM) and (RLBM) are also equivalent, and is equivalent to the equality characterization of (
1.8
) . We refer to these results as “reverse-to-forward”
(RTF)
principle.

In Section 3, as an application of the (RTF) principle, we give a very simple new proof of the log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality in dimension two, by showing
\widetilde{K}_{t}\subset K_{t}
. In higher dimension, (
1.8
) also provides a new strategy: if one can construct a middle convex body
\bar{K}_{t}
, such that

V(\bar{K}_{t})\geq V(\widetilde{K}_{t})\quad{\rm and}\quad\bar{K}_{t}\subset K_{t},

then one can verify the (LBM) conjecture.

In Section 4, we establish the (RTF) principle for the (LM) inequality.
Based on this principle, we prove the (LM) inequality in the case that one convex body is a zonoid (the inequality part was first proved by van Handel). The conjectured full equality conditions are also characterized, which turns to be new.

2
The equivalence of (LBM) and (RLBM)

We will prove that (RLBM) conjecture is equivalent to (LBM) conjecture in this section. Before this, we need some preparations.

The following is a variation formula with respect to a pertubation of the surface area measure. The idea of its proof is in spirit of Aleksandrov’s approach to prove his well-known variation formula.

Lemma 2.1

(One-sided variation formula for the perturbation of surface area measure)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
. Let
\varphi\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K})
be an even function, such that
\varphi
is positive
S_{K}
-a.e. Define
\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}
to be the unique origin-symmetric convex body satisfying

dS_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}=\varphi^{t}dS_{K},\quad t\in[0,1].

Then,

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0^{+}}V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})=\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}.

Note that the right hand side is bounded from above, but might be
-\infty
.

Proof.

By elementary calculus, we see that

g(t)=\frac{\varphi^{t}-1}{t},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

is increasing in
t
. In particular,

\varphi^{t}-1\leq t(\varphi-1),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(2.1)

As
t\to 0^{+},

\frac{\varphi^{t}-1}{t}\quad{\rm converges~to}\quad\log\varphi\quad{\rm decreasingly}.

When
t=1
, the function
\varphi-1
is integrable w.r.t the measure
h_{K}dS_{K}
. Thus, by the monotone convergence theorem, we have

\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi^{t}-1}{t}h_{K}dS_{K}\to\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}\quad{\rm decreasingly},\quad{\rm as}~t\to 0^{+}.

(2.2)

Note that, we also have

\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}\leq\int_{S^{n-1}}(\varphi-1)h_{K}dS_{K}<\infty,

but the left hand side might be
-\infty
.

Next, we proceed our proof and split it into three steps.

Step 1.
We prove that
S_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}\to S_{K}
weakly, and hence
\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}\to K
in Hausdorff metric.

Let
f\in C(S^{n-1})
. By (
2.1
), we have

|f(\varphi^{t}-1)|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\max\{1,\varphi-1\}\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K}).

By this and the fact that
\varphi^{t}\to 1
a.e. as
t\to 0
, and by the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\int_{S^{n-1}}f(\varphi^{t}-1)dS_{K}\to 0,\quad{\rm as}~t\to 0,

the weak convergence of the surface area measure
S_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}
.

Step 2.
Note that Step 1 implies that
V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})\to V(K)
. Then, it is simple to see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V(K)}{t}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{1/n}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{(n-1)/n}-V(K)^{(n-1)/n}}{t}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}V(K)^{1/n}\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{(n-1)/n}-V(K)^{(n-1)/n}}{t},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V(K)}{t}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{1/n}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{(n-1)/n}-V(K)^{(n-1)/n}}{t}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}V(K)^{1/n}\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{(n-1)/n}-V(K)^{(n-1)/n}}{t}.

By Minkowski’s inequality for the mixed volume, we derive that

\displaystyle V(K)^{1/n}\cdot\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{(n-1)/n}-V(K)^{(n-1)/n}}{t}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{V_{1}(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t},K)-V(K)}{t}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi^{t}-1}{t}h_{K}dS_{K},

and

\displaystyle V(K_{\varphi,t})^{1/n}\cdot\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})^{(n-1)/n}-V(K)^{(n-1)/n}}{t}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V_{1}(K,\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})}{t}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi^{t}-1}{t}h_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}dS_{K}.

By (
2.5
), (
2.3
), and (
2.2
), we have

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V(K)}{t}\leq\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}.

(2.7)

If
\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}=-\infty
, then it follows from (
2.5
) and (
2.3
) that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V(K)}{t}=\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V(K)}{t}=\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}=-\infty,

and we complete the proof.

Now we suppose
\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}
is finite.
Since
h_{K}
is bounded from below, we also have

\left|\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi dS_{K}\right|<\infty.

Since Step 1 implies that
h_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}\to h_{K}
uniformly, we have

\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{\varphi^{t}-1}{t}h_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}dS_{K}\to\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K},\quad{\rm as}~t\to 0.

Then, by (
2.6
), (
2.4
), and (
2.2
),
we have

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})-V(K)}{t}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}.

As a result, we see that the limit exists, and

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0^{+}}V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})=\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}.

∎

Lemma 2.2

(Two-sided variation formula for the perturbation of surface area measure)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
. Let
\varphi
be an even function, such that both
\varphi
and
\varphi^{-1}
belong to
L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K})
, and such that
\varphi
is positive
S_{K}
-a.e. Define
\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}
to be the unique origin-symmetric convex body satisfying

dS_{\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t}}=\varphi^{t}dS_{K},\quad t\in[-1,1].

Then,

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})=\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
t<0
.
Notice that

\varphi^{t}=\left(\varphi^{-1}\right)^{-t},

with
-t>0
. By Lemma
2.1
, if we denote
s=-t
, and denote
\widetilde{L}_{s}=\widetilde{K}_{t}
, then we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0^{-}}V(\widetilde{K}_{\varphi,t})=-\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{s=0^{+}}V(\widetilde{L}_{\varphi,s})=\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi h_{K}dS_{K}.

Moreover, we know that
\log\varphi\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K})
in this case.
∎

The following lemma will be used.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
.
If
S_{K}
and
S_{L}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other,
then

\varphi(v)=\frac{dS_{L}}{dS_{K}}(v)

is positive and finite
S_{K}
-a.e. Moreover,
\varphi\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K})
and
\varphi^{-1}\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{L})
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\varphi={dS_{L}}/{dS_{K}}
is positive
S_{K}
-a.e.
Otherwise, there is
\eta\subset S^{n-1}
with
S_{K}(\eta)>0
, and

\varphi(v)=0,\quad\forall v\in\eta.

Then,
S_{L}(\eta)=0
but
S_{K}(\eta)>0
, which contradicts the fact that
S_{L}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
S_{K}
. Meanwhile, since

S_{L}(S^{n-1})=\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi dS_{K},

it is clear that
\varphi\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K})
.

Likewise,
\varphi^{-1}={dS_{K}}/{dS_{L}}
belongs to
L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{:})
, and it is positive
S_{L}
-a.e. Since an
S_{L}
-null set must be an
S_{K}
-null set, we know that
\varphi
is finite
S_{L}
-a.e.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
.
If
S_{K}
and
S_{L}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other,
then

\lim_{t\to 0}\widetilde{K}_{t}=K_{0},\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{t\to 1}\widetilde{K}_{t}=K_{1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
,

\varphi=\frac{dS_{K_{1}}}{dS_{K_{0}}}

is positive and finite
S_{K_{0}}
-a.e. Therefore,

\varphi^{t}\to 1
as
t\to 0
, and
\varphi^{t}\to\varphi
as
t\to 1
,
S_{K_{0}}
-a.e. Similar to Step 1 of the proof of Lemma
2.1
, using the dominated convergence theorem, we will complete the proof of this lemma.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
K_{0},K_{1}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
.
If
S_{K_{0}}
and
S_{K_{1}}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other,
then
F(t)=V(\widetilde{K}_{t})
is differentiable in
t\in[0,1]
, and

F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}V(\widetilde{K}_{t})=\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\left(\frac{dS_{K_{1}}}{dS_{K_{0}}}\right)dV_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}.

Here the derivatives at endpoints of
[0,1]
should be understood as one-sided derivatives.

Proof.

Denote

\varphi=\frac{dS_{K_{1}}}{dS_{K_{0}}}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\varphi\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K_{0}})
and
\varphi^{-1}\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};S_{K_{1}})
. Then,
for
t=0,1
, the differential of
F(t)
is already given by Lemma
2.1
.

Now, suppose
t\in(0,1)
. Choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
[t-\varepsilon_{0},t+\varepsilon_{0}]\subset(0,1).
Define

\widetilde{L}_{s}=\widetilde{K}_{t+s\varepsilon_{0}},\quad s\in[-1,1].

Then,
S_{\widetilde{L}_{-1}}
,
S_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}
, and
S_{\widetilde{L}_{1}}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other, and

\varphi^{-\varepsilon_{0}}=\frac{dS_{\widetilde{L}_{-1}}}{dS_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}}\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};{S_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}}),\quad{\rm and}\quad\varphi^{\varepsilon_{0}}=\frac{dS_{\widetilde{L}_{1}}}{dS_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}}\in L^{1}(S^{n-1};{S_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}}).

By the two-sided variation formula (Lemma
2.2
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}V(\widetilde{L}_{s})\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\left(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)h_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}dS_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}=\frac{n\varepsilon_{0}}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi~dV_{\widetilde{L}_{0}}.

Since
V(\widetilde{L}_{s})=V(\widetilde{K}_{t+s\varepsilon_{0}})
, the above formula implies that
F(t)=V(\widetilde{K}_{t})
is differentiable and

F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi~dV_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}.

∎

Now we give the main result in this section. In the following two results, we split the statements of the conjectures into two parts: inequalities and equality cases. For example, (LBM) inequality means the inequality (
1.1
) without the equality cases.

Theorem 2.6

(RTF Principle for the inequalities)
.

The following three items are equivalent.

(i)

(LBM) inequality is true in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(ii)

(RLBM) inequality is true in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(iii)

For all
K_{0},K_{1}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
, it follows that

V(\widetilde{K}_{t})\leq V(K_{t}).

(2.8)

Proof.

Denote
\mu=S_{K_{0}}+S_{K_{1}}
,
\varphi_{0}=dS_{K_{0}}/d\mu
,
\varphi_{1}=dS_{K_{1}}/d\mu
. Then

dS_{K_{0}}=\varphi_{0}d\mu,\quad dS_{K_{1}}=\varphi_{1}d\mu,

and

dS_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}=\varphi_{0}^{1-t}\varphi_{1}^{t}d\mu=\left(\frac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{0}}\right)^{t}dS_{K_{0}}.

Step 1: (i) implies (ii) and (iii).
Assume (LBM) inequality holds.

On one hand, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{t}dS_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}\left({h_{0}}\varphi_{0}\right)^{1-t}\left({h_{1}}\varphi_{1}\right)^{t}d\mu

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{0}dS_{K_{0}}\right)^{1-t}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{1}dS_{K_{1}}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle=V(K_{0})^{1-t}V(K_{1})^{t}.

On the other hand, since
h_{t}\leq h_{0}^{1-t}h_{1}^{t}
, by Minkowski’s inequality for the mixed volume, we have

\frac{1}{n}\int_{S^{n-1}}h_{t}dS_{\widetilde{K}_{t}}\geq V_{1}(\widetilde{K}_{t},K_{t})\geq V(\widetilde{K}_{t})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}V(K_{t})^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(2.10)

Since we have assumed that (LBM) inequality holds, combining (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we obtain (ii):

V(\widetilde{K}_{t})\leq V(K_{0})^{1-t}V(K_{1})^{t}.

It is clear that (iii) follows immediately from (i) and (ii).

Step 2.
We prove that (iii) implies (ii).
By (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), if (iii) holds, we have

, and using (
4.8
), we obtain

\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\varphi(v)dV_{K}(v)\leq\int_{S^{n-1}}\log\frac{h_{L}(v)}{h_{K}(v)}dV_{K}(v).

(4.14)

By Lemma
4.1
, (
4.10
) follows immediately for all
L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
.

Part 2: Characterization of equality conditions.

Since the sufficient part is clear, we only need to prove the necessary part.

Suppose
L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{e}
achieves equality in (
4.10
). Then, a dilation of
L
, say
aL
, will satisfy
V_{aL}=V_{K}
. Hence,
equality also holds in (
4.14
) for
K
and
L
. As a result, there is a
\rho
-full measure set
\omega
, such that
equality holds in (
4.4
) for all
u\in\omega
.
By Lemma
4.2
, there is an
S_{K}
-full measure set
\Omega
(may depend on
u
presently), such that

\varphi(v)=\frac{dS_{L}}{dS_{K}}(v)=c(u),\quad\forall v\in\Omega\setminus u^{\perp},

where
c(u)
is a constant depending on
u\in\omega
. Since
\varphi=a^{-n}h_{K}/h_{L}
is continuous, the set
\Omega
can be chosen to be
{\rm supp}(S_{K})
.

Now we recall the notations
\bowtie
,
\sim
and
\simeq
in Lemma
4.3
.
By Lemma
4.3
,

\mathbb{R}^{n}
is decomposed into the direct sum of linear spans of the equivalence classes,
V_{1},...,V_{i_{0}}
.
By the construction of
V_{i}
, there is

\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{i_{0}}(V_{i}\cap\Omega).

Notice the facts that

•

If
v_{1}\simeq v_{2}
in
\Omega
, then
\varphi(v_{1})=\varphi(v_{2})
;

•

If
u_{1}\simeq u_{2}
in
\omega
, then
c(u_{1})=c(u_{2})
.

Therefore, there are positvie numbers
c_{1},...,c_{i_{0}}
, such that

\varphi(v)=h_{K}(v)/h_{L}(v)=c_{i},\quad\forall v\in\Omega\cap V_{i}.

Equivalently to say,
K
and
L
have dilated direct summands.
∎

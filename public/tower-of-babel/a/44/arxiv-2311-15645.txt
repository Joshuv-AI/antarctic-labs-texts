1
Introduction

\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{VY}

Next he deduced an essentially projective generalization of this result:

Theorem 2

Let a triangle
ABC
be given in the Euclidean plane. Let
D
,
E
,
F
be non-collinear points, neither of which lies on a side line of the triangle. If a conic
\Sigma
passes through the points
D
,
E
,
F
, and
\Sigma\backslash\left\{D\right\}
meets the conics
BCDEF
,
ACDEF
,
ABDEF
at the points
U
,
V
,
W
, and meets the lines
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{AD}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{BD}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{CD}
at the points
X
,
Y
,
Z
, then the lines
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{UX}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{VY}

\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{WZ}
are concurrent.

Theorem 2 indeed reduces to Theorem 1 if
E
and
F
are the ’circular points at infinity’. In this note we present synthetic, elementary proofs for both of these theorems. Our proof for Theorem 2 does not rely on Theorem 1 (so we may immediately deduce the first theorem from the second one). The reasoning applied in the proof of Theorem 2 is a substantial refinement of that in the proof of Theorem 1. In fact we show that Theorem 2 is valid in any Pappian projective plane satisfying Fano’s axiom.

In both proofs we need the following basic facts from projective geometry.

Let a Pappian plane be given, satisfying Fano’s axiom. Then we have

(A)

The three pairs of opposite sides of a complete quadrangle meet any line (not passing through a vertex) in the three pairs of an involution.

(B)

If U, V, W, X, Y, Z are six points on a conic, then the three lines

\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{UX}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{VY}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{WZ}

are concurrent, if and only if, (UX), (VY), (WZ) are pairs of an involution on the conic.

For a proof we refer to Coxeter’s book
[
3
]
.

2
An elementary proof of Theorem 1

We may interpret the Euclidean plane as a part of its projective closure. The latter is a Pappian plane satisfying Fano’s axiom (in fact, it is isomorphic to the real projective plane).

Apply an inversion of pole
D
, denoting the images of points and sets by a prime. Then the sets

\left\{D,A^{\prime},X^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{D,B^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{D,C^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{A^{\prime},B^{\prime},W^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{A^{\prime},C^{\prime},V^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{B^{\prime},C^{\prime},U^{\prime}\right\}

are collinear, therefore the opposite sides of the complete quadrangle
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}D
meet the line
\Sigma^{\prime}
at the pairs
(U^{\prime}X^{\prime})
,
(V^{\prime}Y^{\prime})
,
(W^{\prime}Z^{\prime})
. By
(A)
, these are the three pairs of an involution. On the other hand, inversion preserves cross ratio cr, and pairs
(P_{1}P^{\prime}_{1})
,
(P_{2}P^{\prime}_{2})
,
(P_{3}P^{\prime}_{3})
are pairs of an involution, if and only if,
\textrm{cr}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P^{\prime}_{3})=\textrm{cr}(P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime}_{3},P_{3})
. Thus the involution sends the pairs
(U^{\prime}X^{\prime})
,
(V^{\prime}Y^{\prime})
,
(W^{\prime}Z^{\prime})
to the pairs of an involution on the circle
\Sigma=(\Sigma^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Hence, by
(B)
, the lines

\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{UX}\textrm{ , }\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{VY}\textrm{ , }\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{WZ}

are concurrent.

3
An elementary proof of Theorem 2

In this section we consider a Pappian projective plane
\mathbf{P}
satisfying Fano’s axiom. First we collect some basic facts concerning the so-called
Steiner-correspondence
. These suggest that it is a good candidate for a purely projective generalization of inversion. Indeed, Steiner correspondence will play the same role in the proof of Theorem 2 as inversion in the proof of Theorem 1.

Let
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
be two fixed conics in
\mathbf{P}
.

(1) It is known (see e.g.
[
5
]
) that
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
have a common self-polar triangle
\Delta=\left\{D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}\right\}
. For every point
P\in\mathbf{P}\backslash\Delta
let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be the polars of
P
with respect to
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, respectively. Then the mapping

\mathcal{S}:\mathbf{P}\backslash\Delta\rightarrow\mathbf{P}\textrm{ , }P\mapsto P^{\prime}:=p_{1}\cap p_{2}

is said to be the
Steiner correspondence
with respect to
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
. If
P^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}(P)
, then we say that the points
P
and
P^{\prime}
are in
Steiner-correspondence
. Notice that if
P
is a vertex of
\Delta
, e.g.,
P=D_{1}
, then
p_{1}=p_{2}=\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{D_{2}D_{3}}
, so the Steiner correspondence cannot be defined. Over

\mathbf{P}\backslash\left\{\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{D_{1}D_{2}}\cup\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{D_{2}D_{3}}\cup\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{D_{3}D_{1}}\right\}

\mathcal{S}
is involutive, and hence invertible.

(2) Let
l\subset\mathbf{P}
be a line,
not
passing through any vertices of
\Delta
. We show that the set

\mathcal{S}(l)=\left\{\mathcal{S}(P)\in\mathbf{P}\textrm{ }|\textrm{ }P\in l\right\}

is a
conic
. Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be the poles of
l
with respect to
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, and consider the pencils
\mathcal{L}_{i}
with centres
L_{i}
,
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
. Then for each point
P\in l
the point
P^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}(P)
can be obtained as the intersection of two corresponding lines in
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. Since there is a projectivity between
\mathcal{L}_{i}
and the range of all points on
l
for
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, it follows that we also have a projectivity
f:\mathcal{L}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}
. Then, by Steiner’s characterization of conics, the locus of points
m\cap f(m)\textrm{ , }m\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
is a conic. Clearly, this conic is just the set
\mathcal{S}(l)
.

(3) Observe that the conic
\mathcal{S}(l)
contains the vertices of
\Delta
, since
\mathcal{S}
sends every side line of
\Delta
into the vertex opposite to the side. From the involutiveness of
\mathcal{S}
it follows that
the image of a conic passing through a vertex of
\Delta
is a line
.

(4) By the reasoning applied in (2) we can also see that the Steiner correspondence sends the pairs of an involution of points on
l
to the pairs of an involuion of points on the conic
\mathcal{S}(l)
.

(5) Suppose, finally, that the line
l\subset\mathbf{P}

passes through
a vertex
D\in\Delta
. We claim that in this case the image of
l\backslash\left\{D\right\}
under
\mathcal{S}
is a range of points on a line. Indeed, using the same notation as in (2), the poles
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are on the side line
d
opposite to
D
.
d
is the polar of
D
, so the projectivity
f:\mathcal{L}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}
sends
d
into itself, therefore
f
is a perspectivity, and the points
m\cap f(m)

(m\in\mathcal{L}_{1})
are collinear. Again, this point set is just
\mathcal{S}(l\backslash\left\{D\right\})
.

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem 2 in the given Pappian plane
\mathbf{P}
. Consider two conics
\Sigma_{1}
,
\Sigma_{2}
with the same self-polar triangle
DEF
. Let

\mathcal{S}:\mathbf{P}\backslash\left\{D,E,F\right\}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}\textrm{ , }P\mapsto\mathcal{S}(P)=P^{\prime}

be the Steiner correspondence with respect to
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
. Then the sets

\left\{U^{\prime},V^{\prime},W^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{U^{\prime},A^{\prime},C^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{V^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime}\right\}\textrm{ , }\left\{W^{\prime},A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\right\}

are collinear. So by observation (5),

X^{\prime}=\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{DA^{\prime}}\cap\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\textrm{ , }Y^{\prime}=\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{DB^{\prime}}\cap\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\textrm{ , }Z^{\prime}=\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{DC^{\prime}}\cap\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}.

Thus the opposite side lines of the complete quadrangle
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}D
meet the line
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}
at the pairs of points
(U^{\prime}X^{\prime})
,
(V^{\prime}Y^{\prime})
,
(W^{\prime}Z^{\prime})
. These are the pairs of an involution on the line
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{U^{\prime}V^{\prime}}
, therefore, in view of (4), their images
(UX)
,
(VY)
,
(WZ)
under
\mathcal{S}
are the pairs of an involution on the conic
\Sigma
. By our construction this is equivalent to the property that the lines
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{UX}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{VY}
,
\overset{\hbox{$\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\mathord{\leftarrow}\!$}}\mkern-5.0mu\leaders{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{-}\!$}}}{\hfill}\mkern-5.0mu\mathord{\hbox{\raisebox{-1.5pt}[1.5pt][0.0pt]{$\!\mathord{\rightarrow}$}}}$}}{WZ}
are concurrent.

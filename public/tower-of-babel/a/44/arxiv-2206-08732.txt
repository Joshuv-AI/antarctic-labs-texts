Meromorphic Solutions of Homogeneous and Non-homogeneous Higher Order Linear Difference Equations in Terms of (p,q)-Order
Subhadip Khan, Chinmay Ghosh, Sanjib Kumar Datta

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the growth of meromorphic solutions of homogeneous and non-homogeneous linear difference equations with entire or meromorphic coefficients. We further extend and improve few results on the order of meromorphic solutions by using ( p , q ) − (p,q)- lower order and ( p , q ) − (p,q)- lower type followed by the investigation of Luo and Zheng ( 2016 ) , \left(2016\right), Belaidi and Bellaama ( 2020 ) . \left(2020\right). AMS Subject Classification (2010) : 30D35, 39B32, 39A10 Keywords and phrases : entire function, meromorphic function, homogeneous difference equation, non-homogeneous difference equation, ( p , q ) − (p,q)- order, ( p , q ) − (p,q)- lower order, ( p , q ) − (p,q)- type, ( p , q ) − (p,q)- lower type.



1
Introduction

The most common form of homogeneous and nonhomogeneous difference
equations are given by

A_{k}(z)f(z+c_{k})+A_{k-1}(z)f(z+c_{k-1})+\cdots+A_{1}(z)f(z+c_{1})+A_{0}(z)f(z)=0

(1)

and

A_{k}(z)f(z+c_{k})+A_{k-1}(z)f(z+c_{k-1})+\cdots+A_{1}(z)f(z+c_{1})+A_{0}(z)f(z)=A_{k+1}(z),

(2)

where the coefficients
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{k},A_{k+1}\neq 0

(k\geq 2)

in (
1
) or (
2
) are entire or meromorphic functions and
c_{k},c_{k-1},\ldots,c_{1}
are distinct nonzero complex numbers. Study of
the growth properties of meromorphic solutions of such equations from the
view point of Nevanlinna’s theory is a matter of great interest for many
researchers in recent time.

In
2007
, Laine and Yang
[
12
]
considered the homogeneous difference
equation of which more than one dominant coefficients exist but exactly one
has its type strictly greater than the others. Chiang and Feng
[
5
]
in
2008
established a theorem in which exactly one coefficient of (
1
)
has maximal order. In
2013
, Liu and Mao
[
14
]
investigated meromorphic
solutions of (
1
) or (
2
) by using hyper order when one or more
coefficients of (
1
) or (
2
) have infinite order. Also in
2017
, Zhou and Zheng and in
2019
, Belaïdi and Benkarouba
[
1
]

investigated the solutions of (
1
) or (
2
) using iterated order
and iterated type which generalized and improved previous results. Very
recently, Luo and Zheng
[
11
]
have studied the growth of meromorphic
solutions of (
1
) when more than one coeffcient has maximal lower
order and the lower type strictly greater than the type of other
coeffcients, and obtained two very important Theorems and later in
2020,

Belaïdi and Bellaama
[
2
]
improved those two theorems of Luo and
Zheng
[
11
]
for nonhomogeneous case.

In
2010,
Liu, Tu and Shi
[
15
]
modified the definition of the
(p,q)-
order, which was first introduced by Juneja, Kapoor and Bajpai
[
10
]
. In
this article we investigate meromorphic solutions of (
1
) and (
2
) using the concept of
(p,q)-
lower order and
\left(p,q\right)-
lower type given in
[
15
]
. We also extend and improve results of Luo and
Zheng
[
11
]
, Belaïdi and Bellaama
[
2
]
. Here we consider the
case, when (
1
) and (
2
) have meromorphic coefficients.

We assume that the reader is familiar with the fundamental results and the
standard notations of Nevanlinna’s value distribution theory
[
9
]
.

2
Definitions and Lemmas

In this section some basic definitions and lemmas are discussed for the
improvement of the main section.

Let
p\geq q\geq 1
or
2\leq q=p+1
be integers, then first recall the
following definitions :

Definition 1

[
13
]
For a transcendental meromorphic function
f,
the
(p,q)-
order
is defined by

\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}T(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

And if
f
is a transcendental entire function, then

\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p+1}M(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

Note that
0\leq\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)\leq\infty.
Also for a
rational function
\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)=0.

Definition 2

[
13
]
For a transcendental meromorphic function
f,
the
(p,q)-
lower
order is defined by

\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}T(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

And if
f
is a transcendental entire function, then

\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p+1}M(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

Definition 3

[
13
]
A transcendental meromorphic function is said to have index pair
\left[p,q\right]
if
0\leq\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)\leq\infty
and
\rho_{f}\left(p-1,q-1\right)
is not a non-zero finite number.

Definition 4

[
13
]
For a meromorphic function
f
having non-zero finite
(p,q)-
order
\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right),
the
(p,q)-
type is defined by

\tau_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p-1}T(r,f)}{\left(\log_{q-1}r\right)^{\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)}}.

And if
f
is a transcendental entire function, then

\tau_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}M(r,f)}{\left(\log_{q-1}r\right)^{\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right)}}.

Definition 5

[
13
]
For a meromorphic function
f
having non-zero finite
(p,q)-
lower order
\rho_{f}\left(p,q\right),
the
(p,q)-
lower type is defined
by

\underline{\tau}_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p-1}T(r,f)}{\left(\log_{q-1}r\right)^{\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)}}.

And if
f
is a transcendental entire function, then

\underline{\tau}_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}M(r,f)}{\left(\log_{q-1}r\right)^{\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)}}.

Definition 6

[
13
]
For a meromorphic function
f
, the
(p,q)-
exponent of
convergence of the sequence of poles is defined by

\lambda_{\frac{1}{f}}\left(p,q\right)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{p}N(r,f)}{\log_{q}r}.

Where
N(r,f)
denotes integrated counting function of poles of
f
in the
domain
\left\{z:\left|z\right|\leq r\right\}.

We set
\exp_{1}r=e^{r}
and
\exp_{k+1}r=\exp\left(\exp_{k}r\right),

k\in\mathbb{N}
and consider
\exp_{0}r=\log_{0}r=r,

\exp_{-1}r=\log_{1}r,

\exp_{1}r=\log_{-1}r,
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}.
Again for all
sufficiently large values of
r,

\log_{1}r=\log r
and
\log_{k+1}r=\log\left(\log_{k}r\right),

k\in\mathbb{N}.

Next we recall some further definitions which are used throughout the main
section.

Definition 7

[
1
]
The linear measure of a set
E\subset\left(0,+\infty\right)
is
defined by

m\left(E\right)=\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\limits_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{E}\left(t\right)dt

and the logarithmic measure of a set
E\subset\left(1,+\infty\right)
is
defined by

lm\left(E\right)=\mathop{\displaystyle\int}\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{E}\left(t\right)}{t}dt,

where
\chi_{E}\left(t\right)
is the characteristic function of a set
E.

Lemma 12

Let
f
be an entire function with
\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)<\infty.
Then for any given
\varepsilon>0,
there exists a
subset
S_{3}\subset\left(1,+\infty\right)

\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\rightarrow+\infty\\

M\left(r,f\right)<\exp_{p}\left[\left\{\log_{q-1}\left(r\right)\right\}^{\underline{\rho}_{f}\left(p,q\right)+\varepsilon}\right].

Proof.
For the proof we use similar logic described in
[

By the definition of (
p,q)-

exists a sequence

Let

Second part of the lemma follows from the definition stated in the lemma.

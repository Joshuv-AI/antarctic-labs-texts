Abstract
We perform a study of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) lineshape using the data samples of e + ​ e − → γ ​ X ​ ( 3872 ) e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872) , X ⁡ ( 3872 ) → D 0 ​ D ¯ 0 ​ π 0 X(3872)\to D^{0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0} and π + ​ π − ​ J / ψ \pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi collected with the BESIII detector. The effects of the coupled-channels and the off-shell D ∗ 0 D^{*0} are included in the parameterization of the lineshape. The lineshape mass parameter is obtained to be M X = ( 3871.63 ± 0.13 − 0.05 + 0.06 ) M_{X}=(3871.63\pm 0.13^{+0.06}_{-0.05}) MeV. Two poles are found on the first and second Riemann sheets corresponding to the D ∗ 0 ​ D ¯ 0 D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0} branch cut. The pole location on the first sheet is much closer to the D ∗ 0 ​ D ¯ 0 D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0} threshold than the other, and is determined to be 7.04 ± 0.15 − 0.08 + 0.07 7.04\pm 0.15^{+0.07}_{-0.08} MeV above the D 0 ​ D ¯ 0 ​ π 0 D^{0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0} threshold with an imaginary part − 0.19 ± 0.08 − 0.19 + 0.14 -0.19\pm 0.08^{+0.14}_{-0.19} MeV.



The
X(3872)
was discovered in
B^{\pm}\to[X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi]K^{\pm}
decay processes by the Belle experiment
Choi et al. 2003
, and confirmed by CDF
Acosta et al. 2004
, D0
Abazov et al. 2004
and BaBar
Aubert et al. 2005
. As the first candidate of an exotic charmonium-like state, it has been studied in numerous experimental analyses over the past two decades.
Along with a well-established quantum number
J^{PC}=1^{++}

Aaij et al. 2015
, many remarkable features of the
X(3872)
have been observed, including a mass almost exactly at the
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}
threshold, an extremely narrow width
Workman et al. 2022
, and an isospin-violating decay pattern
Abe et al.
;
del Amo Sanchez et al. 2010
;
Ablikim et al. 2019
. For the nature of the
X(3872)
, many theoretical interpretations have been proposed, including a hadronic molecule
Swanson 2004
;
Zhao et al. 2014
, a compact tetraquark state
Maiani et al. 2005
, a conventional charmonium state
\chi_{c1}(2P)

Achasov and Rogozina 2015
, a mixture of a molecule and an excited charmonium state
Suzuki 2005
;
Kalashnikova 2005
;
Takizawa and Takeuchi 2013
and so on. However, none of these explanations has been universally accepted.

The lineshape of the
X(3872)
contains essential information, since from its parameters it is possible to extract the pole locations, the effective range of the particle interaction and the scattering length. Here, the pole of a physical state refers to the corresponding single pole of the off-shell T-matrix in the complex energy plane, where the amplitude becomes infinite.
Recently, the LHCb experiment performed a lineshape study based on a high-statistics
X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
data sample, with both Breit-Wigner and
{\rm Flatt\acute{e}}
models
Aaij et al. 2020
. However, the lineshapes based on these two models can not be distinguished once the mass resolution is considered. Due to the proximity to the
D^{*}\bar{D}
threshold, the lineshape of the
X(3872)
in the
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}
channel is significantly distorted, making this channel more sensitive to the behavior of the T-matrix.
A study of
X(3872)\to D^{*}\bar{D}
has previously been performed by Belle
Aushev et al. 2010
; however, the off-shell effect of the
D^{*0}
was not taken into account due to a mass constraint applied to the
D^{0}\pi^{0}
and
D^{0}\gamma
systems coming from the
D^{*0}
, which forced the distribution to start from the
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}
threshold. In the meantime, data samples of both
X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
and

In this Letter, we present a study of the
X(3872)
lineshape using
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation data collected with the BESIII detector at center-of-mass energies ranging from 4.178 to 4.278 GeV, already used in previous
X(3872)

Ablikim et al. 2020

\rm fb^{-1}

Ablikim et al. 2015
;
Ablikim et al. 2022

Y(4230)
mass peak, since these energies correspond to a maximum in the
X(3872)
production cross section.
The pole locations of the
X(3872)
are determined based on a simultaneous fit to the data samples of
X(3872)\to D^{0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0}
and
X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi

X(3872)
produced in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872)
process. Throughout this Letter, the charge conjugations are always included and the notations
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}
and
D^{*+}D^{-}
denote both themselves and their charge conjugations.

The parameterization scheme in this analysis is developed based on the framework described in Ref.
Hanhart et al. 2010
, taking into account the effects of the
D^{*0}
width. In this framework, the
X(3872)
decays into the three-body final state
D^{0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0}
via intermediate
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}
. The differential decay rate is written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d{\rm Br}(D^{0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0})}{dE}={\mathcal{B}}\frac{{\rm Br}(D^{*0}\to D^{0}\pi^{0})\times g\times k_{\rm eff}(E)}{|D(E)|^{2}},\\
&\frac{d{\rm Br}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}{J}/\penalty\psi)}{dE}={\mathcal{B}}\frac{\Gamma_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}{J}/\penalty\psi}}{|D(E)|^{2}},\end{split}

(1)

where the denominator is

\displaystyle D(E)=E

\displaystyle-E_{X}+\frac{1}{2}g\left[\left(\kappa_{\rm eff}(E)+ik^{\phantom{a}}_{\rm eff}(E)\right)\right.

(2)

\displaystyle+\left.\left(\kappa^{c}_{\rm eff}(E)+ik^{c}_{\rm eff}(E)\right)\right]+\frac{i}{2}\Gamma_{0}.

In the above equations, all the details of the
X(3872)
production are assumed to be absorbed in a global constant factor
{\mathcal{B}}
, while
{\rm Br}
denotes the branching fractions, and
g
denotes the effective coupling constant of the
X(3872)
to neutral and charged
D^{*}\bar{D}
. The energy
E
(
E_{X}

D^{0}\bar{D}^{0}\pi^{0}
threshold, and is related to the invariant mass of the final states (the mass of the
X(3872)

M_{(X)}=m_{D^{0}}+m_{\bar{D}^{0}}+m_{\pi^{0}}+E_{(X)}

m_{D^{0}}
,
m_{\bar{D}^{0}}
and
m_{\pi^{0}}
are the masses of
D^{0},~\bar{D^{0}}
and
\pi^{0}
quoted from the Particle Data Group (PDG)

\Gamma_{0}
includes the width of all channels except
D^{*}\bar{D}
, and is comprised of three parts:
\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}{J}/\penalty\psi}+\Gamma_{\rm known}+\Gamma_{\rm unknown}=(1+\beta+\alpha)\Gamma_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}{J}/\penalty\psi}

\Gamma_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}{J}/\penalty\psi}
,

and

(3)

Based on the obtained results, we can do a comparison between the
X(3872)
and the deuteron.
The ERE parameters are related to the field renormalization
constant
Z
by:

\displaystyle a=-\frac{2(1-Z)}{(2-Z)}\frac{1}{\gamma}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{-1}),

(6)

\displaystyle r_{e}=-\frac{Z}{1-Z}\frac{1}{\gamma}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{-1}).

(7)

where
\gamma=\sqrt{2\mu_{p}E_{B}}
, with
E_{B}
the binding energy; the scale
\beta
measures the momentum scale of the binding interaction, which cannot be calculated without knowing the details of the interaction, but can be estimated to be of the order of the pion mass
m_{\pi}
for both neutron-proton and
D^{*}\bar{D}

interactions, i.e.
1/\penalty\beta\sim 1/\penalty m_{\pi}\simeq 1.4~{\rm fm}
.

In the limit of
Z\to 0
, the effective range should be positive and dominated by the range correction
Weinberg 1965
;
Baru et al. 2022
;
Matuschek et al. 2021
;
Esposito et al. 2022
. It was found that this case is compatible with the measured ERE parameters of the deuteron, which is known to be a predominantly molecular state
Weinberg 1965
. However, in the case of the
X(3872)
, we see that a negative effective range can fit the data well, which is different from the deuteron case (
+1.75~{\rm fm}
) and also
suggests an elementary component in the
X(3872)

Esposito et al. 2022
. Given that the range correction is less
important in the case of the
X(3872)
, one can solve Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) for
Z
by neglecting
\mathcal{O}(\beta^{-1})
, and obtain
Z=0.18
. However, in the case of the deuteron, it would be impossible to solve Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) for

in a model independent
way since the range correction term is non-negligible for

. Despite this, using the

\widetilde{X}_{A}
proposed in Ref.
Matuschek et al. 2021
, we find both the
X(3872)
and the deuteron have similar compositeness. Nevertheless, there are
still large uncertainties in the ERE parameters, which prevent us from drawing strong conclusions on the
nature of the
X(3872)
. More statistics would be helpful.

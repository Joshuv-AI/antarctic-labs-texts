Graded Betti numbers of powers of path ideals of paths
Silviu Bălănescu 1 , Mircea Cimpoeaş 2 and Thanh Vu 3

Abstract
. Let I n , m = ( x 1 ⋯ x m , x 2 ⋯ x m + 1 , … , x n + 1 x n + 2 ⋯ x n + m ) I_{n,m}=(x_{1}\cdots x_{m},x_{2}\cdots x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}x_{n+2}\cdots x_{n+m}) be the m m -path ideal of a path of length n + m − 1 n+m-1 over a polynomial ring S = k ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n + m ] S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m}] . We compute all the graded Betti numbers of all powers of I n , m I_{n,m} .

1.
Introduction

Let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
and
M
a finitely generated graded
S
-module. The Hilbert Syzygy theorem states that
M
has a finite graded free resolution

0\longleftarrow M\longleftarrow F_{0}\longleftarrow\cdots\longleftarrow F_{r}\longleftarrow 0,

where
F_{i}=\oplus_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}(M)}
. The numbers
\beta_{i,j}(M)
are called the graded Betti numbers of
M
. From the resolution, Hilbert deduced that the Hilbert polynomial of
M
is

p_{M}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\sum_{j}\beta_{i,j}(M)\binom{z+n-1-j}{n-1}.

Hence, from the graded Betti numbers of
M
, one can deduce the dimension, projective dimension, regularity, and multiplicity of
M
.

In
[
BC1
,
SL
,
SWL
]
, the authors compute the projective dimension, the regularity, and the multiplicity of powers of path ideals of paths, respectively. In this paper, we compute all the graded Betti numbers of powers of path ideals of paths, giving a unified approach to the mentioned work. We first introduce some notation. Let
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 0
be integers. We denote by

I_{n,m}=(x_{1}\cdots x_{m},x_{2}\cdots x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}x_{n+2}\cdots x_{n+m})\subseteq S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m}]

the
m
-path ideal of a path on
n+m
-vertices. By convention, for integers
a,b
, if
a<0
,
b<0
or
a<b
then
\binom{a}{b}=0
. Our main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
n\geq 0
,
t\geq 1
be integers. The Betti number
\beta_{i,j}(I_{n,m}^{t})
is
0
unless
j=i+tm+(m-1)\ell
for some integer
\ell
such that
0\leq\ell\leq i
and

\beta_{i,i+tm+(m-1)\ell}(I_{n,m}^{t})=\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell m}{i-\ell}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell}.

Our result also covers the result of Alilooee and Faridi
[
AF
]
for the case of path ideals of paths. As in
[
BCV
]
, where we compute all the graded Betti numbers of
n-1
and
n-2
path ideals of
n
-cycles, we employ the following procedure.

(1)

We use the following general idea from
[
NV
]
. To study the ideal
I^{n}
, we break
I=J+K
in a nice way, then we study the family
J^{s}I^{t}
as this family has the natural decomposition
J^{s}I^{t}=J^{s+1}I^{t-1}+J^{s}K^{t}
. We prove that these decompositions are Betti splittings.

(2)

We then compute the intersection
J^{s+1}I^{t-1}\cap J^{s}K^{t}
and exhibit further Betti splitting for the intersection.

(3)

To establish the Betti splittings, we use the
\Tor
-vanishing criteria in
[
NV
]
.

The main difficulties in the procedure are finding the intersection
J^{s+1}I^{t-1}\cap J^{s}K^{t}
and finding further Betti splittings for the intersection. The family of path ideals of paths have a natural decomposition
I_{n,m}=I_{n-1,m}+(f_{n+1})
where
f_{n+1}=x_{n+1}\cdots x_{n+m}
. We then follow the steps outlined above to find relevant Betti splittings. From that, we deduce recursive equations for the graded Betti numbers. We then deduce the generating function for the graded Betti numbers and arrive at the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Consequently, we deduce the results of
[
BC1
,
SL
]
.

We structure the paper as follows. In section
2
, we provide background and recall the results on Betti splittings. In section
3
, we establish various Betti splittings for the powers of path ideals of paths. We then deduce Theorem
1.1
. Finally, we derive the formula for the projective dimension and regularity of
I_{n,m}^{t}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this section, we let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring over an arbitrary field
\mathrm{k}
, with the standard grading.

Projective dimension and regularity

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. For integers
i,j
with
i\geq 0
, the
(i,j)
-graded Betti number of
M
is defined by

\beta_{i,j}(M)=\dim_{\mathrm{k}}\Tor_{i}^{S}(\mathrm{k},M)_{j}.

The projective dimension of
M
, denoted by
\pd(M)
, and the Castfelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{reg}(M)
, are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\pd_{S}(M)

\displaystyle=\sup\{i:\;\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\text{ for some }j\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}_{S}(M)

\displaystyle=\sup\{j-i:\;\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\}.

The following result is well-known.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x
be a variable and
I
a nonzero homogeneous ideal of
S
. Then

\beta_{i,j}(xI)=\beta_{i,j-1}(I)\text{ for all }i\geq 0.

Betti splittings

Betti splittings of monomial ideals were first introduced by Francisco, Ha, and Van Tuyl in
[
FHV
]
, motivated by the work of Eliahou and Kervaire
[
EK
]
. Betti splittings have been proven useful in studying the path ideals of graphs and their powers
[
BCV
,
HV
]
. We recall the definition and the following results about Betti splittings, following
[
NV
]
:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
P,I,J
be proper nonzero homogeneous ideals of
S
with
P=I+J
. The decomposition
P=I+J
is called a
Betti splitting
if for all
i\geq 0
we have

\beta_{i}(P)=\beta_{i}(I)+\beta_{i}(J)+\beta_{i-1}(I\cap J).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi:M\to N
be a morphism of finitely generated graded
S
-modules. We say that
\varphi
is
\Tor
-vanishing if for all
i\geq 0
, we have
\Tor_{i}^{S}(\mathrm{k},\varphi)=0
.

We have the following criterion of Nguyen and Vu
[
NV
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
I,J
be nonzero homogeneous ideals of
S
and
P=I+J
. The decomposition
P=I+J
is a Betti splitting if and only if the inclusion maps
I\cap J\to I
and
I\cap J\to J
are
\Tor
-vanishing.

Remark 2.5
.

By
[
FHV
, Proposition 2.1]
, once we have
P=I+J
is a Betti splitting, then the mapping cone construction for the map
I\cap J\to I\oplus J
yields a minimal free resolution of
P
. In particular, we have

\beta_{i,j}(P)=\beta_{i,j}(I)+\beta_{i,j}(J)+\beta_{i-1,j}(I\cap J)

for all non-negative integers
i,j
.

If
u\in S
is a monomial, the
support
of
u
, denoted by
\operatorname{supp}(u)
is the set of variables
x_{i}
such that
x_{i}|u
. Also, if
J\subset S
is a monomial ideal with the minimal generating set
G(J)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
,
the
support
of
J
is
\operatorname{supp}(J)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})
. The following results are well-known, see e.g.
[
HH
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
I,J,L
be monomial ideals and
f
a monomial of
S
. Then

(1)

(I+J):f=(I:f)+(J:f)
.

(2)

(I+J)\cap L=(I\cap L)+(J\cap L)
.

(3)

If
\operatorname{supp}(I)\cap\operatorname{supp}(J)=\emptyset
then
I\cap J=IJ
.

(4)

If
f=xg
where
x
is a variable and
x\notin\operatorname{supp}(I)
then
I:f=I:g
.

3.
Graded Betti numbers of powers of
m
-path ideals of
n+m
-paths

In this section, we compute all the graded Betti numbers of powers of
m
-path ideals of
n+m
-paths. We first introduce some notations. Let
n\geq 0
,
m\geq 2
be integers and
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m}]
. We denote by
f_{1}=x_{1}\cdots x_{m},\ldots,f_{n+1}=x_{n+1}\cdots x_{n+m}
. Then the
m
-path ideal of the
n+m
-path is

I_{n,m}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1}).

First, we have

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t>s\geq 0
be natural numbers. Then
I_{n,m}^{t}:f_{n+1}^{s}=I_{n,m}^{t-s}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
BC1
, Lemma 2.1]
. For completeness, we give an argument here. Since
f_{n+1}\in I_{n,m}
, the left-hand side contains the right-hand side. Let
u
be a monomial in
I_{n,m}^{t}:f_{n+1}^{s}
. We prove by induction on
s
that
u\in I_{n,m}^{t-s}
. The base case
s=0
is obvious. Now assume that the statement holds for
s-1
. Since
u\in I_{n,m}^{t}:f_{n+1}^{s}
, there exists a minimal generator
w
of
I_{n,m}^{t}
such that
w|uf_{n+1}^{s}
. Let
k=\max\{j\mid x_{j}\in\operatorname{supp}(w)\}
. Since
w
is a minimal generator of
I_{n,m}^{t}
, it follows that
f_{k-m+1}|w
,
w=f_{k-m+1}w^{\prime}
where
w^{\prime}
is a minimal generator of
I_{n,m}^{t-1}
, and
\operatorname{supp}(w^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(w)
. In particular, we have

w^{\prime}|f_{n+1}^{s-1}\cdot\frac{f_{n+1}}{\gcd(f_{n+1},f_{k-m+1})}u.

Since
\operatorname{supp}(w^{\prime})\cap\operatorname{supp}\left(\frac{f_{n+1}}{\gcd(f_{n+1},f_{k-m+1})}\right)=\emptyset
, we deduce that
w^{\prime}|f_{n+1}^{s-1}u
. By induction, the conclusion follows.
∎

For simplicity of notations, in the next four lemmas, we set

I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})
,
J=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
,
A=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-m})
and
B=(g_{n-m+1},\ldots,g_{n})
where
g_{i}=f_{i}/x_{n}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=J+(f_{n+1})

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=A+x_{n}B

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
t\geq s\geq 1
be natural numbers. Then

J^{t}:f_{n+1}^{s}=A^{t-s+1}(A+(x_{n}))^{s-1}+x_{n}^{s}J^{t-s}.

Proof.

Since
f_{n+1}=x_{n+m}g
where
g=g_{n}=x_{n+1}\cdots x_{n+m-1}
and
x_{n+m}\notin\operatorname{supp}(J)
, by Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that
J^{t}:f_{n+1}^{s}=J^{t}:g^{s}
. Now, we have

J^{t}=A^{t}+A^{t-1}(x_{n}B)+\cdots+(x_{n}B)^{t}.

Since
\operatorname{supp}(g)\cap\operatorname{supp}(A)=\emptyset
,
g
is a minimal generator of
B
, and
B
is isomorphic to the
m-1
-path ideal of the
2m-2
-path, by Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that

A^{i}(x_{n}B)^{t-i}:g^{s}=\begin{cases}A^{i}x_{n}^{t-i}B^{t-i-s}&\text{ if }i\leq t-s,\\
A^{i}x_{n}^{t-i}&\text{ if }i>t-s.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle J^{t}:g^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{t}(A^{i}(x_{n}B)^{t-i}:g^{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{t-s}A^{i}x_{n}^{t-i}B^{t-i-s}+\sum_{i=t-s+1}^{t}A^{i}x_{n}^{t-i}

\displaystyle=x_{n}^{s}(A+x_{n}B)^{t-s}+A^{t-s+1}(A+(x_{n}))^{s-1}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
t\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
are integers. Then

J^{s}f_{n+1}^{t}\cap J^{s+1}I^{t-1}=f_{n+1}^{t}J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))

and the decomposition
J^{s}I^{t}=J^{s}f_{n+1}^{t}+J^{s+1}I^{t-1}
is a Betti splitting.

Proof.

We have
I^{t}=f_{n+1}^{t}+JI^{t-1}
. Hence,
J^{s}I^{t}=J^{s}f_{n+1}^{t}+J^{s+1}I^{t-1}
. We now prove the equality for the intersection.

First, we have
f_{n+1}x_{n}\in J
and
A\subseteq J
, hence

f_{n+1}(A+(x_{n}))\subseteq J
. In particular, the left-hand side contains the right-hand side.

It remains to show that

(3.1)

J^{s}\cap(J^{s+1}I^{t-1}:f_{n+1}^{t})\subseteq J^{s}(A+(x_{n})).

By Lemma
2.6
, we have

(3.2)

J^{s+1}I^{t-1}:f_{n+1}^{t}=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}(J^{s+1+j}f_{n+1}^{t-1-j}):f_{n+1}^{t}=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}(J^{s+1+j}:f_{n+1}^{j+1}).

By Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle J^{s}\cap(J^{s+j+1}:f_{n+1}^{j+1})

\displaystyle=J^{s}\cap(x_{n}^{j+1}J^{s}+A^{s+1}(A+x_{n})^{j})

\displaystyle\subseteq x_{n}J^{s}+A^{s+1}\subseteq J^{s}(A+(x_{n})).

Together with Eq. (
3.2
) and Lemma
2.6
, Eq. (
3.1
) follows.

It remains to prove that the decomposition
J^{s}I^{t}=J^{s}f^{t}+J^{s+1}I^{t-1}
is a Betti splitting. First, we have any non-zero syzygy of
f_{n+1}^{t}J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))
has
x_{n+m}
-degree
t
, while any non-zero syzygy of
J^{s+1}I^{t-1}
has
x_{n+m}
-degree at most
t-1
. Hence, the inclusion map
f_{n+1}^{t}J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))\to J^{s+1}I^{t-1}
is
\Tor
-vanishing. By Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
2.4
, it suffices to prove that the inclusion maps
J^{s}x_{n}\to J^{s}
and
J^{s}A\to J^{s}
are
\Tor
-vanishing. The first one is clear, the second one follows from
[
NV
, Theorem 4.5]
and the fact that the map
J^{s}A\to J^{s}
factors through
J^{s}A\to J^{s+1}\to J^{s}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

We have
J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))=A^{s+1}+x_{n}J^{s}
,
A^{s+1}\cap x_{n}J^{s}=x_{n}A^{s+1}
, and the decomposition
J^{s}(A+(x_{n})=A^{s+1}+x_{n}J^{s}
is a Betti splitting.

Proof.

First, we prove that
J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))=A^{s+1}+x_{n}J^{s}
. It suffices to prove that
J^{s}A\subseteq A^{s+1}+x_{n}J^{s}
. Indeed, we have
J=A+x_{n}B
. Hence,
J^{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}A^{s-i}(x_{n}B)^{i}
. Now if
i=0
, then we have the term
A^{s+1}
. Thus, we may assume that
i>0
. Then, we have

AA^{s-i}(x_{n}B)^{i}=x_{n}(A^{s-i+1}(x_{n}B)^{i-1}B\subseteq x_{n}(A^{s-i+1}(x_{n}B)^{i-1}\subseteq x_{n}J^{s}.

Now, we prove that
x_{n}J^{s}\cap A^{s+1}=x_{n}A^{s+1}
. Indeed, we have

x_{n}A^{s+1}\subseteq x_{n}J^{s}\cap A^{s+1}\subseteq x_{n}\cap A^{s+1}=x_{n}A^{s+1}.

Since the map
A^{s+1}\to J^{s}
factors through
A^{s+1}\to J^{s+1}\to J^{s}
, it is
\Tor
-vanishing by
[
NV
, Theorem 4.5]
. Hence, the decomposition
J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))=A^{s+1}+x_{n}J^{s}
is a Betti splitting by Lemma
2.4
. The conclusion follows.
∎

We now deduce recursive equations for the Betti numbers of powers of
I_{n,m}
. Let
a
be a function on the tuple
(n,s,t,i,j)
defined as follows. If either
n,s,t,i,j<0
then
a(n,s,t,i,j)=0
. If
n,s,t,i,j\geq 0
then

\displaystyle a(n,s,t,i,j)

\displaystyle=\beta_{i,j}(I_{n-1,m}^{s}I_{n,m}^{t}).

Furthermore, we set

\tilde{a}(n,s,t,i,j)=a(n,s,t,i,j)+a(n,s,t,i-1,j-1).

With these notations, we have

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
t\geq 1
and
n,s,i,j\geq 0
are integers. We have

\displaystyle a(n,s,t,i,j)

\displaystyle=a(n,s+1,t-1,i,j)+\tilde{a}(n-1,0,s,i,j-tm)

\displaystyle+\tilde{a}(n-m-1,0,s+1,i-1,j-tm).

Proof.

Note that
I=I_{n,m}
,
J=I_{n-1,m}
, and
A=I_{n-m-1,m}
. By Lemma
3.3
, the decomposition
J^{s}I^{t}=J^{s}f_{n+1}^{t}+J^{s+1}I^{t-1}
is a Betti splitting. Hence, we have

(3.3)

a(n,s,t,i,j)=a(n,s+1,t-1,i,j)+\beta_{i,j}(J^{s}f_{n+1}^{t})+\beta_{i-1,j}(P)

where
P=f_{n+1}^{t}J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))
. By definition and Lemma
2.1
,
\beta_{i,j}(J^{s}f_{n+1}^{t})=\beta_{i,j-tm}(J^{s})=a(n-1,0,s,i,j-tm)
and
\beta_{i-1,j}(P)=\beta_{i,j-tm}(Q)
where
Q=J^{s}(A+(x_{n}))
. By Lemma
3.4
, the decomposition
Q=A^{s+1}+x_{n}J^{s}
is a Betti splitting. Hence,

\beta_{i-1,j-tm}(Q)=\beta_{i-1,j-tm}(x_{n}J^{s})+\beta_{i-1,j-tm}(A^{s+1})+\beta_{i-2,j-tm}(x_{n}A^{s+1}).

The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

We now denote by

b(n,t,i,j)=a(n,0,t,i,j)=\beta_{i,j}(I_{n,m}^{t}).

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
n,t,i,j\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle b(n,t+1,i,j+m)

\displaystyle=b(n,t,i,j)+b(n-1,t+1,i,j+m)+b(n-1,t,i-1,j-1)

\displaystyle+b(n-m-1,t+1,i-1,j)+b(n-m-1,t+1,i-2,j-1).

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle a(n,0,t,i,j)

\displaystyle=a(n,t,0,i,j)+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\tilde{a}(n-1,0,s,i,j-(t-s)m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tilde{a}(n-m-1,0,s,i-1,j-(t-s+1)m).

We also let
\tilde{b}(n,t,i,j)=b(n,t,i,j)+b(n,t,i-1,j-1)
. Then we have

\displaystyle b(n,t,i,j)=b(n-1,t,i,j)+

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\left[\tilde{b}(n-1,s,i,j-(t-s)m)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\tilde{b}(n-m-1,s+1,i-1,j-(t-s)m)\right].

Getting the equation for
b(n,t+1,i,j+m)
then subtracting that to the equation for
b(n,t,i,j)
we deduce that

\displaystyle b(n,t+1,i,j+m)

\displaystyle=b(n-1,t+1,i,j+m)+b(n,t,i,j)-b(n-1,t,i,j)

\displaystyle+\tilde{b}(n-1,t,i,j)+\tilde{b}(n-m-1,t+1,i-1,j).

The conclusion follows.
∎

Since
I_{n,m}^{t}
is generated in degree
tm
, for all
i\geq 0
, we have
\beta_{i,j}(I_{m}(P_{n+m})^{t})=0
if
j<i+tm
. For convenient in deriving these Betti numbers, we let
c(n,t,i,j)=b(n,t,i,i+tm+j)
and
\Psi(x,y,z,w)
be the generating function

\displaystyle\Psi(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,t,i,j\geq 0}c(n,t,i,j)x^{n}y^{t}z^{i}w^{j}.

We have

Theorem 3.7
.

The generating function of
c(n,t,i,j)
is

\Psi(x,y,z,w)=\frac{1-x(1+x^{m}zw^{m-1}(1+z)}{(1-x)(1-(x(1+x^{m}zw^{m-1}(1+z))+y(1+xz)))}.

.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
, for all
t\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle c(n,t

\displaystyle+1,i,j)=c(n,t,i,j)+c(n-1,t+1,i,j)+c(n-1,t,i-1,j)

\displaystyle+c(n-m-1,t+1,i-1,j-m+1)+c(n-m-1,t+1,i-2,j-m+1).

Clearly,

c(n,0,i,j)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i=j=0\text{ and }n\geq 0,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\Psi_{0}=\sum_{n,i,j\geq 0}c(n,0,i,j)x^{n}z^{i}w^{j}
. Then we have

\Psi_{0}=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}=\frac{1}{1-x}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x}+\sum_{n,i,j\geq 0,t\geq 1}c(n,t,i,j)x^{n}y^{t}z^{i}w^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x}+\sum_{n,i,j,t\geq 1}\left[c(n,t-1,i,j)+c(n-1,t,i,j)+c(n-1,t-1,i-1,j)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.c(n-m-1,t,i-1,j-m+1)+c(n-m-1,t,i-2,j-m+1)\right]x^{n}y^{t}z^{i}w^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x}+y\Psi+x(\Psi-\frac{1}{1-x})+xyz\Psi+(x^{m+1}zw^{m-1}+x^{m+1}z^{2}w^{m-1})(\Psi-\frac{1}{1-x}).

Hence,

(1-x(1+x^{m}zw^{m-1}(1+z))-y(1+xz))\Psi=\frac{1-x(1+x^{m}zw^{m-1}(1+z))}{1-x}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

We are now ready for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
I_{n,m}^{t}
is generated in degree
tm
, we have
\beta_{i,j}(I_{n,m}^{t})=0\text{ if }j<i+tm.
By definition,
b(n,t,i,i+tm+j)=c(n,t,i,j)
is the coefficient of
x^{n}y^{t}z^{i}w^{j}
of
\Psi(x,y,z,w)
. Furthermore, since
w
only appears in
\Psi
with exponent
m-1
, we deduce that
c(n,t,i,j)=0
unless
j=(m-1)\ell
for some
\ell\geq 0
.

Now assume that
j=(m-1)\ell
for some integer
\ell\geq 0
. We prove by induction on
t
the formula for
c(n,t,i,(m-1)\ell)
. The base case
t=0
is clear. Let

d(n,t,i,\ell)=\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell m}{i-\ell}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell}.

Since
c(n,0,t,i,(m-1)\ell)=d(n,0,i,\ell)
for all
n,i,\ell
, for the induction step, we need to prove the following equation for all
t\geq 0
.

\displaystyle d(n,t

\displaystyle+1,i,\ell)=d(n,t,i,\ell)+d(n-1,t+1,i,\ell)+d(n-1,t,i-1,\ell)

\displaystyle+d(n-m-1,t+1,i-1,\ell-1)+d(n-m-1,t+1,i-2,\ell-1).

We have

\displaystyle d(n,t+1,i,\ell)

\displaystyle=\binom{t+\ell}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell m}{i-\ell}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell+1}{t-i+2\ell+1}

\displaystyle d(n-1,t+1,i,\ell)

\displaystyle=\binom{t+\ell}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell m-1}{i-\ell}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell+1}

\displaystyle d(n-1,t,i-1,\ell)

\displaystyle=\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell m-1}{i-\ell-1}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell+1}

\displaystyle d(n-m-1,t+1,i-1,\ell-1)

\displaystyle=\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-\ell m-1}{i-\ell}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell}

\displaystyle d(n-m-1,t+1,i-2,\ell-1)

\displaystyle=\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-\ell m-1}{i-\ell-1}\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell+1}{t-i+2\ell+1}

We have the following binomial identities

\displaystyle\binom{t+\ell}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell}+\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}

\displaystyle\binom{n-\ell m}{i-\ell}

\displaystyle=\binom{n-\ell m-1}{i-\ell}+\binom{n-\ell m-1}{i-\ell-1}

\displaystyle\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell+1}{t-i+2\ell+1}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell+1}+\binom{n+t-\ell m-i+\ell}{t-i+2\ell}.

The conclusion follows from a routine check.
∎

We now have some applications of our main result.

Corollary 3.8
.

The Betti number
\beta_{i,j}(I_{n,m})
is equal to
0
unless
j=i+m+(m-1)\ell
for some
\ell\geq 0
and

\beta_{i,i+m+(m-1)\ell}(I_{n,m})=\binom{n-\ell m}{i-\ell}\binom{n+1-\ell m-i+\ell}{2\ell+1-i}.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
1.1
with
t=1
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For all integers
i\geq 0
and
t\geq 1
, we have

\beta_{i,i+tm}(I_{n,m}^{t})=\binom{n}{i}\binom{n+t-i}{t-i}.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
1.1
with
\ell=0
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Assume that
n\geq 0
and
t\geq 1
. Then

\operatorname{reg}(I_{n,m}^{t})=tm+(m-1)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
, the Betti numbers
\beta_{i,i+tm+(m-1)\ell}(I_{n,m}^{t})
is non-zero if and only if the following system of inequalities have non-negative integer solutions

(3.4)

\begin{split}n-\ell m&\geq\max\{\ell,i-\ell\},\\
\min\{i-\ell,2\ell+t-i\}&\geq 0.\end{split}

In particular,
\ell\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\right\rfloor
. Furthermore, when
\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\right\rfloor
, the system Eq.(
3.4
) has a non-negative integer solution
i=\ell
. Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(I_{n,m}^{t})

\displaystyle=\max\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}(I_{n,m}^{t})\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=tm+(m-1)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\right\rfloor.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Assume that
n\geq 0
and
t\geq 1
. Then

\pd(I_{n,m}^{t})=\min\left\{n,t-1+\left\lfloor\frac{n-t+1}{m+1}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{m+1}\right\rceil\right\}.

Proof.

By definition
p(n,t):=\pd(I_{n,m}^{t})=\max\{i\mid\beta_{i,j}(I_{n,m}^{t})\neq 0\}
. By Eq. (
3.4
), we deduce that

(3.5)

p(n,t)=\max\left\{\min\{t+2\ell,n-(m-1)\ell\}\mid\ell=0,\ldots\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\right\rfloor\right\}.

If
t\geq n
, then clearly,
p(n,t)=n
. Now, assume that
t\leq n
. Let
q=\left\lfloor\frac{n-t}{m+1}\right\rfloor
. Then Eq. (
3.5
) becomes

\displaystyle p(n,t)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\max\{t+2\ell\mid\ell\leq q\},\max\left\{n-(m-1)\ell\mid q+1\leq\ell\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\right\rfloor\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\{t+2q,n-(m-1)(q+1)\}

\displaystyle=t-1+\left\lfloor\frac{n-t+1}{m+1}\right\rfloor+\left\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{m+1}\right\rceil.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Exceptional pairs on del Pezzo surfaces and spaces of compatible Feigin-Odesskii brackets
Alexander Polishchuk, Eric Rains

Abstract
. We prove that for every relatively prime pair of integers ( d , r ) (d,r) with r > 0 r>0 , there exists an exceptional pair ( 𝒪 , V ) ({\mathcal{O}},V) on any del Pezzo surface of degree 4 4 , such that V V is a bundle of rank r r and degree d d . As an application, we prove that every Feigin-Odesskii Poisson bracket on a projective space can be included into a 5 5 -dimensional linear space of compatible Poisson brackets. We also construct new examples of linear spaces of compatible Feigin-Odesskii Poisson brackets of dimension > 5 >5 , coming from del Pezzo surfaces of degree > 4 >4 .

2.3.
Transformations of exceptional pairs
(V,{\mathcal{O}})

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
k
.
We have the following three natural operation on exceptional pairs

X

(1) duality
(V_{1},V_{2})\mapsto(V_{2}^{\vee},V_{1}^{\vee})
;

(2) mutation
(V_{1},V_{2})\mapsto(L_{V_{1}}V_{2},V_{1})
, where the
left mutation

L_{V_{1}}V_{2}
of
V_{2}
fits into an exact triangle

L_{V_{1}}V_{2}\to R\operatorname{Hom}(V_{1},V_{2})\otimes V_{1}\to V_{2}\ldots;

(3) rotation
(V_{1},V_{2})\mapsto(V_{2}(-Q),V_{1})
.

Combining these operations we get the following two operations on exceptional pairs of the form

:

\displaystyle(V,{\mathcal{O}})\mapsto(M(V):=L_{{\mathcal{O}}}(V^{\vee}),{\mathcal{O}}),

\displaystyle(V,{\mathcal{O}})\mapsto(R(V):=V^{\vee}(-Q),{\mathcal{O}}).

(2.6)

We can calculate the effect of these operations on the slope of

(and on other invariants of

Lemma 2.3
.

One has

\displaystyle\mu(M(V))=-\frac{\mu}{\mu+1},

\displaystyle\mu(R(V))=-k-\mu,

where
\mu=\mu(V)
.
Furthermore,

\operatorname{rk}(M(V))=-\deg(V)-\operatorname{rk}(V),\ \ \operatorname{rk}(R(V))=\operatorname{rk}(V),

c_{1}(M(V))=c_{1}(V),\ \ c_{1}(R(V))=-c_{1}(V)-\operatorname{rk}(V)Q.

. Then

\pi[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}]=\pi[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1)]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i},\ \ \pi[S_{i}]=\epsilon_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,n.

In particular,
\pi(K_{0}({\mathcal{C}}))
is contained in
P(D_{n})
, the weight lattice of
D_{n}
.

(iii) We have

\chi([V],[V^{\prime}])=(1-\frac{n}{4})r(V)r(V^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}(d(V^{\prime})r(V)-d(V)r(V^{\prime}))+\chi(\pi[V],\pi[V^{\prime}]).

(3.3)

Proof.

(i) Since the classes of objects of the exceptional collection (
3.1
) freely generate
K_{0}
, the Euler form
\chi

is determined by the condition that
\chi(E,E)=1
for each object
E
of (
3.1
) and by

\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1),{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}})=2,\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1),S_{i})=\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}},S_{i})=1,

with other values on objects of (
3.1

Thus, for
\chi^{-}(A,B)=\chi(A,B)-\chi(B,A)
we have

\displaystyle\chi^{-}(a_{1}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1)]+a_{0}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}]+\sum_{i}b_{i}S_{i},a^{\prime}_{1}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1)]+a^{\prime}_{0}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}]+\sum_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}S_{i})

\displaystyle=2(a_{1}a^{\prime}_{0}-a^{\prime}_{1}a_{0})+\sum_{i}(a_{0}+a_{1})b^{\prime}_{i}-\sum_{i}(a^{\prime}_{0}+a^{\prime}_{1})b_{i}.

On the other hand,

\operatorname{rk}(a_{1}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1)]+a_{0}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}]+\sum_{i}b_{i}S_{i})=a_{0}+a_{1},\ \ \deg(a_{1}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1)]+a_{0}[{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}]+\sum_{i}b_{i}S_{i})=-2a_{1}+\sum_{i}b_{i}.

This immediately shows that the kernel

. The above formula also shows that

\chi(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Furthermore,
\Lambda_{n}=\ker(\operatorname{rk},\deg)

(ii) One immediately checks that the classes

(iii) This formula can be checked on the generating classes

(\epsilon_{i})

Definition 3.2

For a pair of integers

•

More precisely,

There is a unique element of

in the fundamental chamber of

y_{1}\geq y_{2}\geq y_{3}\geq|y_{4}|
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{4})
.
For every coprime
(r,d)
, a class

with
\chi(v,v)=1
,
\operatorname{rk}(v)=r
and

which lies in

.
For every coprime
(r,d)
with

(resp., for

), there are
8
exceptional sheaves
F
with
\operatorname{rk}(F)=r
and

, so that

realize all
8

.

Proof.

From formula (

) with

, we immediately see that

if and only if

.
Furthermore, since

, by the same formula,

On the other hand, this formula gives

3.2.
Omnipresent exceptional bundles on
{\mathcal{C}}_{5}

Recall (see
[
11
, Sec. 6.3]
) that an exceptional bundle
V
on a weighted projective line
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
is called
omnipresent
if
\operatorname{Hom}(V,S_{i})\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Hom}(V,S^{\prime}_{i})=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Since the corresponding
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
-groups vanish, this is equivalent to the numerical condition
\chi(V,S_{i})\neq 0
,
\chi(V,S^{\prime}_{i})\neq 0
.
By Serre duality, this is equivalent to
\chi(S_{i},V)\neq 0
,
\chi(S^{\prime}_{i},V)\neq 0
for all
i
.

Our goal is to show the absence of omnipresent bundles on
{\mathcal{C}}_{5}={\mathcal{C}}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{5})
in a certain range of
(\operatorname{rk}(V),\deg(V))
(see Proposition
3.5
below).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r
be a positive integer. If
v=\sum_{i=1}^{5}y_{i}\epsilon_{i}\in P(D_{5})
is in the fundamental chamber of
D_{5}
(i.e.,
y_{1}\geq\ldots\geq y_{4}\geq|y_{5}|
) and satisfies

|v|^{2}=r^{2}/4+1
and
\sum_{i}y_{i}\leq r/2+\sqrt{r}-1
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

r
is odd and
v=\frac{r}{2}\epsilon_{1}+\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4})\pm\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{5}
;

(2)

r
is even and
v=\frac{r}{2}\epsilon_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{2}
;

(3)

r
is an odd square and
v=(\frac{r}{2}-1)\epsilon_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{r}}{2}(\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{5})
;

(4)

r
is an even square and
v=(\frac{r}{2}-1)\epsilon_{1}+\sqrt{r}\epsilon_{2}
;

(5)

r=11
and
v=\frac{5}{2}(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{5})
;

(6)

r=16
and
v=4(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4})-\epsilon_{5}
.

Proof.

Since
v\in P(D_{5})
, we either have
y_{i}\in\Z
for all
i
, in which case
|v|^{2}\in\Z
, so
r
is even, or we have
y_{i}\in 1/2+\Z
for all
i
, in which case

|v|^{2}\in 1/4+\Z
, so
r
is odd.

If
y_{1}>r/2
then
y_{1}\geq r/2+1
, so that
|v|^{2}\geq y_{1}^{2}>r^{2}/4+1
, which is a contradiction. We thus have
y_{1}\leq r/2
. Set
\delta:=r/2-y_{1}
.
As we have seen above,
\delta
is a nonnegative integer. Our condition on the sum of
y_{i}
can be rewritten as

y_{2}+\ldots+y_{5}\leq\sqrt{r}-1+\delta.

Furthermore, since
y_{i}\geq|y_{5}|
for
i\leq 4
, this implies the inequalities

|y_{2}+y_{3}+y_{4}+y_{5}|,\ |y_{2}+y_{3}-y_{4}-y_{5}|,\ |y_{2}-y_{3}+y_{4}-y_{5}|,\ |y_{2}-y_{3}-y_{4}+y_{5}|\leq\sqrt{r}-1+\delta

Furthermore, since the left-hand sides are integers, we can replace
\sqrt{r}
by
\lfloor\sqrt{r}\rfloor
.
The above inequalities define a hypercube with the side
\lfloor\sqrt{r}\rfloor-1+\delta
in the Euclidean space
\R^{4}
with coordinates
y_{2},\ldots,y_{5}
.
On the other hand, we have

|y_{2}|,\ |y_{3}|,\ |y_{4}|,\ |y_{5}|\leq r/2-\delta,

which is a hypercube with the side
r-2\delta
.
Since for a point in a hypercube in
\R^{4}
with side
a
, the distance from the center is bounded by
a
, we deduce that

y_{2}^{2}+y_{3}^{2}+y_{4}^{2}+y_{5}^{2}\leq\min(\lfloor\sqrt{r}\rfloor-1+\delta,r-2\delta)^{2},

and thus

r^{2}/4+1=|v|^{2}\leq(r/2-\delta)^{2}+\min(\lfloor\sqrt{r}\rfloor-1+\delta,r-2\delta)^{2}.

(3.4)

Hence,

r^{2}/4+1\leq\min(f(\delta),g(\delta)),

where

f(x)=(r/2-x)^{2}+(\sqrt{r}-1+x)^{2},\ \ g(x)=(r/2-x)^{2}+(r-2x)^{2}=\frac{5}{4}(r-2x)^{2}.

The roots of the quadratic equation

f(x)=r^{2}/4+1

are
x=1
and
x=r/2-\sqrt{r}
. Hence, we have

r^{2}/4+1>f(\delta)\ \text{ for }1<\delta<r/2-\sqrt{r}.

(3.5)

Note also that
f(x_{0})=g(x_{0})
for
x_{0}=(r-\sqrt{r}+1)/3
and

\min(f(x),g(x))=\begin{cases}f(x),&x\leq x_{0},\\
g(x),&x\geq x_{0}.\end{cases}

Assume
r>20
and
\delta>1
. Then

x_{0}=(r-\sqrt{r}+1)/3<r/2-\sqrt{r}.

In particular, by (
3.5
),
r^{2}/4+1>f(x_{0})
.
If
\delta<x_{0}
then using (
3.5
) again we get

r^{2}/4+1>f(\delta)=\min(f(\delta),g(\delta)),

which is a contradiction. Hence,
\delta\geq x_{0}
. But the function
g(x)
is decreasing, so we get

r^{2}/4+1>f(x_{0})=g(x_{0})\geq g(\delta)=\min(f(\delta),g(\delta)),

which is again a contradiction.

It remains to enumerate the cases with
\delta=0
and
\delta=1
, as well as
the finitely many cases with
r\leq 19
and
\delta>1
.
Assume first that
r\leq 19
and
\delta>1
. As before, we only need to consider
\delta\geq r/2-\sqrt{r}
(otherwise,
r^{2}/4+1>f(\delta)
).
Together with the inequality
r^{2}/4+1\leq g(\delta)
this gives

\frac{r}{2}-\sqrt{r}\leq\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl(r-\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}+4}{5}}\bigr).

This gives the following possibilities: 1)
\delta=5
,
r=18
; 2)
\delta=4
,
r=16,15
; 3)
\delta=3
,
r=13,12,11
; 4)
\delta=2
,
r=10,9,8
.
Of these, only the pairs

(r,\delta)=(16,4),\ (11,3),\ (9,2)

satisfy the original inequality (
3.4
) involving the floor function.
In each case, we can easily
enumerate all lattice vectors in the relevant simplex, and find that there
are no solutions for
r=9
, while for
r=11
and
r=16
the only solutions are
given in (5) and (6).

For
\delta=0
, we must have
y_{2}^{2}+y_{3}^{2}+y_{4}^{2}+y_{5}^{2}=1
, and one can easily check that this leads to
the solutions given in (1) and (2).

Finally, for
\delta=1
, we must have

|y_{2}+y_{3}+y_{4}+y_{5}|,\ |y_{2}+y_{3}-y_{4}-y_{5}|,\ |y_{2}-y_{3}+y_{4}-y_{5}|,\ |y_{2}-y_{3}-y_{4}+y_{5}|\leq\sqrt{r}

and

y_{2}^{2}+y_{3}^{2}+y_{4}^{2}+y_{5}^{2}=r.

This forces
(y_{2},y_{3},y_{4},y_{5})
to be a vertex of the hypercube, and thus,

(y_{2},\ldots,y_{5})
is either
(\sqrt{r},0,0,0)
or
\sqrt{r}(1/2,1/2,1/2,-1/2)
, giving cases (3)
and (4) and finishing the classification.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
V
be an omnipresent exceptional bundle of rank
r
and degree
d<0
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{5}
, and suppose that
r>(-d+1)^{2}
. Then
(r,d)=(11,-2)
, and

\pi[V]
is in the
W(D_{5})
-orbit of
-\frac{5}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\epsilon_{i}
.

Proof.

We will use the notations and the results of Lemma
3.1
.
Let
v:=\pi[V]\in P(D_{5})
. Using formula (
3.3
),
the condition
\chi(V,V)=1
can be rewritten as

|v|^{2}=r^{2}/4+1.

On the other hand, stability of
V
implies that for any line bundle
L
of degree 0 one has

\operatorname{Hom}(L,V)=0
, hence,
\chi(L,V)\leq 0
.
Taking
L
to be either
{\mathcal{O}}
or of the form
{\mathcal{O}}(x_{i}-x_{j})
or
{\mathcal{O}}(x_{i}+x_{j}-x_{k}-x_{l})
and
applying formula (
3.3
), we get the inequality

\omega\cdot v\leq\frac{r}{4}-\frac{d}{2}

for every
\omega
in the
W(D_{5})
-orbit of
\sum_{i=1}^{5}\epsilon_{i}/2
.
Let
w\in W(D_{5})
be an element such that
v_{0}:=w\cdot v
is in the fundamental chamber of
D_{5}
.
Then since the same inequalities hold for
v_{0}=\sum_{i}y_{i}\epsilon_{i}
, we get that

\sum_{i=1}^{5}y_{i}\leq\frac{r}{2}-d<\frac{r}{2}+\sqrt{r}-1.

Since the inequality here is strict, cases (3), (4), and (6) do not arise, and thus we are left with cases (1), (2), and (5). In the first two cases, the fact that
y_{1}=r/2
immediately translates to the existence of a point sheaf with
\chi(V,x)=0
, so only (5) remains, in which case we find that
r=11
and

y_{1}+y_{2}+y_{3}+y_{4}+y_{5}=15/2\leq 11/2-d<9/2+\sqrt{11},

forcing
-d=2
.
∎

4.1.
Connection with the derived category of

\det

Proposition 4.1
.

There is an equivalence

\Phi:D^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}{\mathcal{C}})\rTo{\sim}\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\rangle^{\perp}

for a weighted projective line

\Phi({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-3)(\sum x_{i}))={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+\sum e_{i}),\ \ \Phi({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{C}}(-2)(\sum x_{i}))={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)[1],

One has

\operatorname{rk}_{X}(\Phi(V))=-\deg_{\mathcal{C}}(V),\ \ \deg_{X}(\Phi(V))=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)+2\deg_{\mathcal{C}}(V).

(4.1)

Furthermore, the isomorphism induced by
\Phi
,

Q(D_{5})\simeq\ker(\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{C}},\deg_{\mathcal{C}})\rTo{\Phi}\ker(\operatorname{rk}_{X},\deg_{X})\simeq\langle Q\rangle^{\perp}

coincides with the one coming from the geometric marking (see Sec.
2.2
).

Proof.

The full exceptional collection on
X
(see
[
13
]

({\mathcal{O}}_{e_{i}}(-1)[-1])_{i=1,\ldots,5},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h),{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h),{\mathcal{O}}_{X})

gives a full exceptional collection in
\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\rangle^{\perp}
(by deleting the last object).
The exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+\sum_{i}e_{i})\to\bigoplus_{i}{\mathcal{O}}_{e_{i}}(-1)\to 0

(4.2)

shows that by left mutating
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h)
through all
{\mathcal{O}}_{e_{i}}(-1)[-1]
, we get a full exceptional collection

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+\sum_{i}e_{i})[-1],({\mathcal{O}}_{e_{i}}(-1)[-1])_{i=1,\ldots,5},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)

in
\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\rangle^{\perp}
.
Now the exact sequences

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(e_{i}-h)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{e_{i}}(-1)\to 0

show that by right mutating all
{\mathcal{O}}_{e_{i}}(-1)[-1]

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+\sum_{i}e_{i})[-1],{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h),({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(e_{i}-h))_{i=1,\ldots,5}.

(4.3)

Let us show that this collection is strong and compute the corresponding algebra.

Applying the functors
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}(?,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h))
to exact sequence (
4.2
), we get that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+\sum_{i}e_{i}),{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h))=0
for

,

\operatorname{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+e_{i}+e_{j}+e_{k}),{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h))=0

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}(?,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h))

\operatorname{Ext}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-2h+\sum_{j}e_{j}),?)

Since the action of

It is natural to ask whether our divisor classes

A class descends to a del Pezzo surface of degree

u=u_{f}^{4}
, where
u_{f}=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is the fundamental unit in
O
.
We are looking for elements of
O_{9}
of norm
-9
. Since
3
does not split in
O
, they are of the form
\pm 3u_{f}^{2n+1}
.
Thus, up to the action of the operations (
5.4
), we can reduce to the case
\xi(V)=\pm 3u_{f}
, in which case

V
is a shift of
{\mathcal{O}}(-2)
.

Case
k=8
.

In this case
O_{8}=\Z+\Z\cdot 2\sqrt{2}
has conductor
2
in
O=\Z[\sqrt{2}]
, and

u=u_{f}^{2}
, where
u_{f}=1+\sqrt{2}
is the fundamental unit in
O
. We are looking for elements of
O_{8}
with norm in
[-8,-1]
.
Since a norm of an element in
O_{8}
is a square modulo
8
, the norm is actually either
-8
,
-7
, or
-4
.

If
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-8=-(\sqrt{2})^{6}
for
a\in O
, then
a=(\sqrt{2})^{3}v
, where
v\in O
has
\operatorname{Nm}(v)=1
, so
v=\pm u_{f}^{2n}
. Hence, our operations (
5.4
)
(together with the shift) reduce to the case
\xi(V)=2\sqrt{2}
, i.e.,
\operatorname{rk}(V)=1
,
\deg(V)=-4
.
Similarly, if
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-7
, then
a=(-1+2\sqrt{2})v
, where
\operatorname{Nm}(v)=1
, so we can reduce to
\xi(V)=-1+2\sqrt{2}
, i.e.,
\operatorname{rk}(V)=1
,
\deg(V)=-3
.
Finally, if
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-4
then
a=2v
, where
v=\pm u_{f}^{2n+1}
, so we can reduce to
\xi(V)=2(-1+\sqrt{2})
, i.e.,
\operatorname{rk}(V)=1
,
\deg(V)=-2
.

Thus, the question reduces to which of these degrees can occur for a line bundle
L
forming an exceptional pair
(L,{\mathcal{O}})
. For
X_{8}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, the degree
is always even, and both degrees
-2
and
-4
occur (for
L={\mathcal{O}}(-1,0)
and
L={\mathcal{O}}(-1,-1)
). On the other hand, it is easy to see that for
X_{8}=F_{1}
, the exceptional
pair
(L,{\mathcal{O}})
should have
L={\mathcal{O}}(-s-nf)
or
L={\mathcal{O}}(-f)
, where
f
is the class of a fiber of
F_{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and
s
is the class of a section, with
s^{2}=-1
. Since
Q=2s+3f
,
in the former case we have
\deg(L)=2n-1
, while in the latter case
\deg(L)=-2
. Thus, for
X_{8}=F_{1}
, all three degrees occur.

Case
k=7
.

In this case
O_{8}=O=\Z+\Z\frac{\sqrt{21}-1}{2}
is the ring of integers in
\Q(\sqrt{21})
, and we are looking for elements of
O
with the norm in
[-7,-1]
.
We have
u=u_{f}=\frac{5+\sqrt{21}}{2}
.

Since the norm is a square modulo
7
, the norm has to be in
\{-7,-6,-5,-3\}
. It is easy to rule out
-7
. Also,

can be even only if both
d
and

are even.
Solutions of
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-5
are of the form
a=\pm\frac{-1+\sqrt{21}}{2}\cdot u_{f}^{n}
, so we can reduce the problem to
\xi(V)=\frac{-1+\sqrt{21}}{2}
, i.e.,
\operatorname{rk}(V)=1
,
\deg(V)=-3
.
Similarly, solutions of
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-3
are of the form
a=\pm\frac{-3+\sqrt{21}}{2}\cdot u_{f}^{n}
, so we can reduce to
\xi(V)=\frac{-3+\sqrt{21}}{2}
, i.e.,
\operatorname{rk}(V)=1
,
\deg(V)=-2
.

In both cases we can take
(V,{\mathcal{O}})
to be the pull-back of the similar pair on
F_{1}
under the blow up map
X_{7}\to X_{8}\simeq F_{1}
.

Case
k=6
.

In this case
O_{6}=\Z[\sqrt{3}]
, we have
u=u_{f}=2+\sqrt{3}
, and we are looking for elements with the norm in
[-6,-1]
.
It is easy to see that the only two possibilities are
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-3
and
\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-2
. The solutions are of the form
\pm\sqrt{3}u_{f}^{n}
and
\pm(-1+\sqrt{3})u_{f}^{n}
,
so we reduce to the cases
(r,d)=(1,-3)
and
(r,d)=(1,-2)
. Both cases are realized as pull-backs under the blow up map
X_{6}\to X_{7}
.

Case
k=5
.

In this case
O_{5}=O=\Z+\Z\frac{-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
, and
u=u_{f}^{2}
, where
u_{f}=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
. The possible norms in
[-5,-1]
(with
(r,d)
relatively prime)
are
-5
and
-1
. The norm
-5
is realized by elements of the form
\pm\sqrt{5}u_{f}^{2n}
, while the norm
-1
is realized by elements of the form
\pm u_{f}^{2n+1}
.
Thus, we reduce to the cases
(r,d)=(2,-5)
and
(r,d)=(1,-2)
. The latter case is realized as the pull-back under the blow up map
X_{5}\to X_{6}
.

It remains to find an exceptional pair
(V,{\mathcal{O}})
with
\operatorname{rk}(V)=2
and
\deg(V)=-5
. For this we can realize
X=X_{5}
as the linear section of
G(2,5)
and take the restriction to
X

of the exceptional pair
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{O}})
on
G(2,5)
, where
{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}^{5}
is the universal subbundle (the needed cohomology on

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

∎

6.
Application to bihamiltonian structures

6.1.
Nodal anticanonical divisors on del Pezzo surfaces

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
k\geq 3
.
Then there exists a nonempty open subset
U\subset X
, such that for every
q\in U
, there exists an irreducible anticanonical curve
C\subset X
, such that
q

is a singular point of
C
.

Proof.

In the case
k=3
,
X
is a cubic surface in its anticanonical embedding into
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. Then we can take
U\subset X
to be the complement to the union of
27
lines on
X
.
For every
q\in U
, let
H_{q}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
be the tangent plane to
X
.
The intersection
C_{q}:=H_{q}\cap X
is an anticanonical divisor, singular at
q
. Since
C_{q}
is a cubic in
H_{q}
, and
q
does not lie on a line contained in
X
,
C_{q}
is irreducible.

In the case
k>3
, let
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
be the blow up map, where
X^{\prime}
is a smooth cubic surface. Then we can take
U\subset X
to be the complement to the image of the union of the lines on
X^{\prime}
.
∎

The following lemma describes a special configuration of
6
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
that appears in characteristic
2
.
Let us denote by
X_{3}^{F}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
the Fermat cubic
x_{0}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}=0
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume the characteristic is
2
.
Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}
be an unordered configuration of points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, such that no
3
are collinear.
For
i=1,\ldots,6
, let
C_{i}
denote the unique conic passing through
S_{i}:=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}\}\setminus\{p_{i}\}
,
Assume that for every
i
,
C_{i}
is tangent to every line through
p_{i}
. Then
p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}
is projectively equivalent
to the unique (up to
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{4})
) unordered configuration of
6
points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{4})
,
such that no
3
are collinear. The blow up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
at these
6
points is isomorphic to
X_{3}^{F}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that the first four points are

p_{1}=(1:0:0),\ \ p_{2}=(0:1:0),\ \ p_{3}=(0:0:1),\ \ p_{4}=(1:1:1).

(6.1)

Let
p_{5}=(x_{0}:y_{0}:1)
,
p_{6}=(x_{1}:y_{1}:1)
. Then the equation of
C_{6}
is
q_{6}(x,y,z)=az(x+y)+y(x+z)=0
, where

a=y_{0}(x_{0}+1)/(x_{0}+y_{0})
. Hence,
p_{6}
is the point where all derivatives of
q_{6}
vanish,
which gives
x_{1}=a+1
,
y_{1}=a
. In particular,
x_{1}+y_{1}+1=0
. Exchanging the roles of
p_{5}
and
p_{6}
we see that
x_{0}+y_{0}+1=0
.
Hence,
a=x_{0}^{2}+1
, so

p_{5}=(x_{0}:x_{0}+1:1),\ \ p_{6}=(x_{0}^{2}:x_{0}^{2}+1:1).

Exchanging the roles of
p_{5}
and
p_{6}
we deduce that
x_{0}^{4}=x_{0}
, hence all points are defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
.
Since no three points are collinear, we see that
x_{0}\neq 0,1
. Hence,
x_{0}
is one of two roots of
x_{0}^{2}+x_{0}+1=0
.
Hence, coordinates of all points are in
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
.

Conversely, suppose we have
6
points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{4})
, such that no three are collinear, with the first
4
points given by (
6.1
),
and
p_{5}=(x_{0}:y_{0}:1)
,
p_{6}=(x_{1}:y_{1}:1)
. Then
x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{4}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
x_{i}\neq y_{i}
for
i=1,2
,
x_{1}\neq x_{0}
. Hence, the points
p_{5}
and

p_{6}
are uniquely determined up to permutation.

On the other hand, all
27
lines on the Fermat cubic
X_{3}^{F}
are defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
. Thus, for any choice of the blow up morphism

\pi:X_{3}^{F}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
the corresponding
6
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
are defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
. Hence,
X_{3}^{F}
is isomorphic to the blow up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
at the above configuration
of
6
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{4})
.
∎

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
k\geq 3
,
P:=|Q|\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{k}
the anticanonical linear system.
Let
\widetilde{P}\subset P\times X
denote the incidence variety of
(C,p)
such that
p\in C
.
We consider the following loci in
P
and
\widetilde{P}
:

•

P_{sing}
(resp.,
\widetilde{P}_{sing}
) is the locus of singular divisors (resp., of pairs
(C,p)
such that
p
is a singular point of
C
);

•

P_{red}\subset P_{sing}
is the locus of reducible divisors;

•

P_{cusp}\subset P_{sing}
is the locus of non-nodal divisors.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that
k\geq 3
.

(i) The loci
P_{sing}
(resp.,
\widetilde{P}_{sing}
),
P_{red}
and
P_{cusp}
are closed. The varieties

\widetilde{P}_{sing}
and
P_{sing}
are irreducible of dimension
k-1
.

(ii) One has
P_{red}\neq P_{sing}
. Assume that
X\not\cong X^{F}_{3}/\bar{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
.
Then
P_{cusp}\neq P_{sing}
.

Proof.

(i) Since
-K_{X}
is very ample, for a fixed point
p\in X
, the condition that an anticanonical divisor
C
is singular at
p
is given by three independent linear conditions
on a point in
P
. Hence,
\widetilde{P}_{sing}
is closed in
P\times X
and is a projective bundle over
X
,
with fibers
{\mathbb{P}}^{k-3}
, so
\widetilde{P}_{sing}
is irreducible of dimension
k-1
. The subset
P_{sing}\subset P
is the image of
\widetilde{P}_{sing}
, so it is closed.

Reducible anticanonical divisors of the form
C_{1}+C_{2}
, with fixed rational equivalence classes of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, are in the image of the map

|C_{1}|\times|C_{2}|\to P
, so they form a closed subset. We claim that there is finitely many possibilities for rational equivalence classes of
C_{i}
.
Indeed, we can assume that neither
C_{1}
or
C_{2}
is a
(-1)
-curve. Then representing
X
as a blow up of a set of points
S\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
,
we get that
C_{1}+C_{2}
is the proper transform of a reducible cubic passing through
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, so the class of one of the components is either
h-e_{i}
or
h-e_{i}-e_{j}
.
This implies the claim and proves that
P_{red}
is closed.
It is well known that the locus of curves in
P
with at most nodal singularities is open, hence,
P_{cusp}
is closed.

(ii) Lemma
6.1
shows that
P_{red}\neq P_{sing}
.
It remains to prove existence of a nodal anticanonical divisor
C\subset X
under our assumptions.
For
k\geq 4
,
X
can be realized as the blow up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
in a set
S
of
\leq 5
points (in general linear position), so we can take as
C
the proper transform of

\overline{C}=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup L_{3}
, the nodal union of three lines in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, so that
S\subset\overline{C}
but none of the nodes of
\overline{C}
is in
S
. For example, if

S=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{5}\}
, we can take
L_{1}
to be the line through
p_{1},p_{2}
,
L_{2}
the line through
p_{3},p_{4}
, and
L_{3}
a generic line through
p_{5}
.

Now assume
k=3
and characteristic is
\neq 2
. Let
X
be the blow up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
at points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}
.
Consider the (smooth) conic
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
through
p_{1},\ldots,p_{5}
, and let
\ell_{i}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, for
i=1,\ldots,5
, be the line through
p_{i}
and
p_{6}
.
Note that the linear projection of
C
from
p_{6}
is a degree
2
map
C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, so it has at most two ramification points, which correspond to tangent lines to
C

passing through
p_{6}
. Since the five lines
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{5}
all pass through
p_{6}
and are distinct, one of them is not tangent to
C

Say,
\ell_{1}
is not tangent to
C
. Therefore,
C\cap\ell_{1}
consists of two points,
p_{1}
and
q
, where
q
is distinct from
p_{i}
.
Consider the proper transforms
\widetilde{C}
and
\widetilde{\ell}_{1}
of
C
and
\ell_{1}
in
X
. Then
e_{1}\cup\widetilde{C}\cup\widetilde{\ell}_{1}
is the nodal anticanonical curve.

In the case of
k=3
and characteristic
2
, the only case when the above argument does not go through is when the linear projection of
C
from each point
p_{6}
is purely
inseparable, i.e., every line through
p_{6}
is tangent to
C
, and similarly for other points
p_{i}
instead of
p_{6}
. By Lemma
6.2
, this implies
that
X\simeq X_{F}^{3}
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
X
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
k\geq 3
, such that
X\not\cong X^{F}_{3}/\bar{{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}
.
Then there exists a nonempty open subset
U\subset X
, such that for every
q\in U

there exists an integral nodal anticanonical curve
C\subset X
such that
q
is the node of
C
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.3
, integral nodal curves are dense in
P_{sing}
.
By Lemma
6.1
, the projection
\widetilde{P}_{sing}\to X
is dominant.
Hence, the restriction to the non-empty open subset of
(C,p)\in\widetilde{P}_{sing}
with
C
integral nodal is also dominant.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
X
be a weak del Pezzo surface with
K_{X}^{2}>1
,
C\subset X
an irreducible anticanonical divisor,
p\in C
a smooth point. Then the blow up
\widetilde{X}
of
X
at
p

is still a weak del Pezzo surface, the proper transform
\widetilde{C}\subset\widetilde{X}
of
C
is irreducible, and the projection
\widetilde{C}\to C
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Prop. 8.1.23]
, it is enough to check that
p
does not lie on any
(-2)
-curve
C^{\prime}
. But this immediately follows from
C\cdot C^{\prime}=0
.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
6.4
,
every anticanonical curve
C_{0}\subset X
is contained in a non-isotrivial pencil of anticanonical curves.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.4
, there exists an integral nodal anticanonical curve
C\subset X
. In addition,
we can assume that the node of
C
is not contained in
C_{0}
. We claim that then the pencil
\langle C_{0},C\rangle
is non-isotrivial.

Indeed, applying Lemma
6.5
to blowing up points
C\cap C_{0}
, and replacing
C_{0}
and
C
by their proper transforms,
we reduce to the case when
X
is a weak del Pezzo surface of degree
1
. Then blowing up the unique point in
C_{0}\cap C

gives a minimal elliptic surface,
such that the proper transform of
C
is still integral nodal. By Tate’s Algorithm (see e.g.
[
17
, Sec. IV.9]
), this implies non-isotriviality.
∎

6.2.
Compatible Poisson brackets

Proof of Theorem B.

Start with an elliptic curve
C
. It is easy to see that for every
k\geq 4
,
C
can be realized as an anticanonical divisor on a del Pezzo surface
X_{k}
of degree
k

(where in the case
k=8
we can take
X_{8}=F_{1}
). Namely, start with an embedding
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
5
generic points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{5}\in C
, so that no three are collinear.
Then the blow up
X_{4}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
at these
5
points is a del Pezzo surface of degree
4
, and
C
lifts to an anticanonical curve of
X_{4}
. By blowing up a subset of these
5

points, we can embed
C
into
X_{k}
with
k\geq 4
.

Let
({\mathcal{O}},V)
be an exceptional pair of vector bundles on
X_{k}
, where
\operatorname{rk}(V)=r
and
\deg(V)=d>r+1
.
Applying
[
6
, Thm. 4.4(i)]
, we get a linear map

\kappa:H^{0}(X_{k},Q)\to H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{d-1},{\bigwedge}^{2}T),

so that every element corresponding to a smooth anticanonical divisor
D
maps to the corresponding Poisson bracket of type
q_{d,r}(D)
, and all the brackets
in the image of
\kappa
are compatible.
Furthermore, by
[
6
, Thm. 4.4(ii)]
,
\kappa
is injective provided every singular anticanonical divisor on
X_{k}
extends to a non-isotrivial anticanonical pencil.
By Corollary
6.6
the latter condition is always satisfied since
k\geq 4
.

Now part (i) follows from Theorem A. Namely, we can find
({\mathcal{O}},V)
on
X_{4}
with given
(d,r)
. Then
\kappa(C)
is exactly
q_{d,r}(C)
, and the image of
\kappa

is the required linear subspace of dimension
\dim H^{0}(X_{4},Q)=5
. In the case
r=2
or
r=d-2
, we first descend
({\mathcal{O}},V)
to a degree
5
del Pezzo
X_{5}
, see
Remark
4.6
.

Similarly, part (ii) follows from Proposition
5.2
(applied to
(-d,r)
since this Proposition is formulated in terms of the dual pair
(V^{\vee},{\mathcal{O}})
).
Note that in the case
k=8
we take
X_{8}=F_{1}
, so that indeed any
(d,r)
in the given range can be realized on
X_{8}
.

For part (iii), we use the well known linear map

s:H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{d-1},{\bigwedge}^{2}T)\to H^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{d},{\bigwedge}^{2}T)^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}

sending Poisson brackets on
{\mathbb{P}}^{d-1}
to quadratic Poisson brackets on
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
(see
[
1
]
,
[
14
, Sec. 12]
).
This gives a lift of a linear subspace of compatible Poisson brackets on
{\mathbb{P}}^{d-1}
to a similar subspace on
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
.
Recall also that by
[
7
, Thm. 6.12]
, a nonzero FO bracket
\pi
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{d-1}
admits no nonzero Poisson vector fields.
Hence by
[
14
, Thm. 12.1]
, a nonzero FO bracket admits a unique lifting to a quadratic Poisson bracket on
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
, which proves the uniqueness statement.
∎

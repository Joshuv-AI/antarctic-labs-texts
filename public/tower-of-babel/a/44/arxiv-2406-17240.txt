Pareto Fronts for Compositionally Solving String Diagrams of Parity Games
Kazuki Watanabe

Abstract
Open parity games are proposed as a compositional extension of parity games with algebraic operations, forming string diagrams of parity games. A potential application of string diagrams of parity games is to describe a large parity game with a given compositional structure and solve it efficiently as a divide-and-conquer algorithm by exploiting its compositional structure. Building on our recent progress in open Markov decision processes , we introduce Pareto fronts of open parity games, offering a framework for multi-objective solutions. We establish the positional determinacy of open parity games with respect to their Pareto fronts through a novel translation method. Our translation converts an open parity game into a parity game tailored to a given single-objective. Furthermore, we present a simple algorithm for solving open parity games, derived from this translation that allows the application of existing efficient algorithms for parity games. Expanding on this foundation, we develop a compositional algorithm for string diagrams of parity games.

Parity Games

Definition 1

(parity game)

A

We assume that every node has a successor in PGs.

\exists
)

We also call the winning condition as the

An

16

3.1
Denotation of Open Parity Games

Strategies

in oPGs are defined in exactly the same way as in PGs; note that oPGs are PGs with open ends.
Unlike PGs, where the winning condition is
qualitative

due to the openness of oPGs, which induces

between two extremes of winning and losing.
Specifically, we show denotations of oPGs

]

Given a play

\llparenthesis p\rrparenthesis
of the play

(
\top
),
losing
(
\bot

m

Importantly, all exits are sinks, meaning that once we enter an exit, we stay there forever.

Let
\mathcal{A}

.

Definition 8

.

\displaystyle\llparenthesis p\rrparenthesis:=\begin{cases}\top&\text{ if $v_{j}\not\in O^{\mathcal{A}}$ for any $j\in{\rm Nature}$, and $p$ satisfies the parity condition,}\\
(o,m)&\text{ if $v_{j}=o\in O^{\mathcal{A}}$ for some $j\in{\rm Nature}$,}\\
&\text{ and $m:=\max\big\{\Omega\big((v_{k},v_{k+1})\big)\ \big|\ k\in\{1,\dots,j-1\}\big\}$, }\\
\bot&\text{ if $v_{j}\not\in O^{\mathcal{A}}$ for any $j\in{\rm Nature}$, and $p$ does not satisfy the parity condition.}\end{cases}

Note that the condition in the second case distinction is well-defined
because we assume every exit is a sink state (

The denotation

of an entrance

that are induced by
\exists
- and

Specifically, for each

Definition 9

\displaystyle\llparenthesis i\rrparenthesis:=\Big\{\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \text{$\sigma_{\forall}$ is a $\forall$-strategy}\big\}\ \Big|\ \text{$\sigma_{\exists}$ is an $\exists$-strategy}\Big\}.

Definition 10

(denotation of oPGs

)

Let

3.2
Optimal Strategies of Open Parity Games

For deciding the winning region of the entrances in the operational semantics
\llbracket\mathbb{D}\rrbracket
,
as stated in
Section

2.2
, the denotation
\llparenthesis\mathcal{A}\rrparenthesis
defined in
Def.

10
may contain redundant information:
for example, suppose we fix an
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
.
If there is a
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
such that
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis:=\bot
, that is,
the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is losing for Player
\exists
, then we can exclude other
\forall
-strategies
\tau_{\forall}
because choosing
\sigma_{\forall}
is optimal for Player
\forall
when Player
\exists
chooses
\sigma_{\exists}
.
We generalize this elimination of sub-optimal strategies by introducing orders on plays and strategies.

Specifically, we use the
sub-priority order

[
22
,
4
]
on priorities: the intuition is that Player
\exists
favors larger even priorities, and Player
\forall
favors larger odd priorities.

Definition 13

(sub-priority order
[
22
,
4
]
)
.

The
sub-priority order

\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}
on the set
\mathbb{P}
of priorities is the total order defined by
M-1\prec_{\mathbb{P}}M-3\prec_{\mathbb{P}}\dots\prec_{\mathbb{P}}1\prec_{\mathbb{P}}0\prec_{\mathbb{P}}2\prec_{\mathbb{P}}\dots\prec_{\mathbb{P}}M-2\prec_{\mathbb{P}}M
.
Recall that we assume that the maximum priority
M
is even.

Remark 14
.

The existing semantics
[
24
,
43
]
uses the trivial discrete order on the set
\mathbb{P}
, that is,
m_{1}\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}m_{2}
iff
m_{1}=m_{2}
.
This difference is crucial for our development.

The operation
\max\colon\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}
that returns a bigger number with respect to the standard order on natural numbers is (also) monotone
with respect to the sub-priority order.

Lemma 15

(monotonicity
[
40
]
)
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\in\mathbb{P}
.
If the inequality
m_{1}\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}m_{2}
holds, then the inequality
\max(m_{1},m_{3})\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}\max(m_{2},m_{3})
holds.

We extend the sub-priority order to a partial order on the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
considering exits.

Definition 16

(domain order)
.

The
domain order

\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}
on the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
(defined in
Def.

7
) is the partial order such that
d_{1}\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}d_{2}
iff (i)
d_{1}=\bot
, (ii)
d_{2}=\top
, or (iii)
d_{1}:=(o_{1},m_{1})
and
d_{2}:=(o_{2},m_{2})
such that
o_{1}=o_{2}
and
m_{1}\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}m_{2}
.

Given an
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
, we define
optimal
\forall
-strategies
w.r.t.
\sigma_{\exists}
as
\forall
-strategies that induce plays whose denotations are minimal.

Definition 17

(minimal)
.

For a set
T\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
, the set
T^{\mathrm{min}}
is the set of minimal elements in
T
w.r.t. the domain order
\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}
.

Definition 18

(optimal
\forall
-strategy)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an oPG,
i
be an entrance, and
\sigma_{\exists}
be an
\exists
-strategy.
A
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
is
optimal
on
i
w.r.t.
\sigma_{\exists}
if the following condition holds:

\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\in\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \tau_{\forall}\text{ is a $\forall$-strategy}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}.

We further define optimal
\exists
-strategies; here we do not fix a
\forall
-strategy.
Specifically, we use the
upper preorder

[
1
]
.

Definition 19

(upper preorder
[
1
]
)
.

The
upper preorder

\preceq_{\mathbf{U}}
on the powerset
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}})
of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
is given by

T_{1}\preceq_{\mathbf{U}}T_{2}
if for any
d_{2}\in T_{2}
, there is
d_{1}\in T_{1}
such that
d_{1}\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}d_{2}
.

The intuition is the following: we fix an entrance
i
and suppose that there are two
\exists
-strategies
\sigma_{\exists}
and
\tau_{\exists}
.
The
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
is “better” than
\tau_{\exists}

if for any
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
, there is a
\forall
-strategy
\tau_{\forall}
such that

p_{\tau_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\preceq_{\mathbf{P}}p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
.

Definition 20

(maximal)
.

For a set
S\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}})
, the set

S^{\mathrm{max}}
is the set of maximal elements in
S
w.r.t. the upper preorder
\preceq_{\mathbf{U}}
.

Definition 21

(optimal
\exists
-strategy)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an oPG, and
i
be an entrance.
An
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
is
optimal
on
i
if the following condition holds:

\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \text{a $\forall$-strategy }\sigma_{\forall}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}\in\Big\{\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\tau_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \text{a $\forall$-strategy }\tau_{\forall}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}\ \Big|\ \text{an $\exists$-strategy }\tau_{\exists}\Big\}^{\mathrm{max}}.

We show an example of optimal
\exists
-strategies in
Example

25
later.

3.3
Pareto Fronts of Open Parity Games

We conclude this section by introducing
Pareto fronts
of oPGs.
Roughly speaking, Pareto fronts are the set of
results
that are induced by optimal strategies.

Definition 22

(results, result order
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an oPG. The set
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
of
results
is the set of non-empty antichains
\mathbf{r}
on the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
, that is,
\mathbf{r}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{min}}=\mathbf{r}
.
The
result order

\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}}
on
\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the partial order that is given by the upper preorder
\preceq_{\mathbf{U}}
.

The result order is indeed a partial order because of the minimality of results.

Proposition 23
.

The result order is a partial order.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbf{r}\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}}\mathbf{r}
.
It suffices to prove that
\mathbf{r}\subseteq\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
due to the symmetry of
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
.
For any
d_{1}\in\mathbf{r}
, there is
d_{2}\in\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
such that
d_{2}\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}d_{1}
.
Similarly, there is
d_{3}\in\mathbf{r}
such that
d_{3}\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}d_{2}
.
Thus, we have
d_{3}\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}d_{1}
and we see
d_{3}=d_{1}
due to the
minimality of
\mathbf{r}
. Thus,
d_{1}=d_{2}=d_{3}
holds and
\mathbf{r}\subseteq\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
holds.
∎

We finally define
Pareto fronts
of oPGs by optimal
\exists
- and
\forall
-strategies.

Definition 24

(Pareto front, Pareto-optimal)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an oPG, and
i
be an entrance. The
Pareto front

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)
is the set of denotations of plays that are induced by optimal
\exists
-strategies and optimal
\forall
-strategies on
i
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i):=\Big\{\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \sigma_{\forall}\text{ is a $\forall$-strategy}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}\ \Big|\ \sigma_{\exists}\text{ is an $\exists$-strategy}\Big\}^{\mathrm{max}}\subseteq\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}.

We call results
\mathbf{r}
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)

Pareto-optimal
.
We write
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
for the indexed family
\big(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)\big)_{i\in I^{\mathcal{A}}}
of Pareto fronts. Note that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)\not=\emptyset
and
\emptyset\not\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)
hold by definition.

Example 25
.

Consider the oPG
\mathcal{C}\colon(1,1)\rightarrow(1,0)
presented in
Fig.

3
.
The denotation
\llparenthesis i_{\mathbf{r},1}\rrparenthesis
contains
\big\{(o_{\mathbf{r},1},2),(o_{\mathbf{r},1},3),(o_{\mathbf{l},1},1)\big\}
.
Since
(o_{\mathbf{r},1},3)\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}(o_{\mathbf{r},1},2)

.
This result is not Pareto-optimal because the inequality
\big\{(o_{\mathbf{r},1},3),(o_{\mathbf{l},1},1)\big\}\preceq_{\mathbf{U}}\big\{(o_{\mathbf{r},1},1)\big\}
holds,
where
\big\{(o_{\mathbf{r},1},1)\big\}\in\llparenthesis i_{\mathbf{r},1}\rrparenthesis

By continuing similar arguments, we can show that the Pareto front
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{C},i_{\mathbf{r},1})
is
\Big\{\big\{(o_{\mathbf{r},1},1)\big\}\Big\}.

Thus, an optimal
\exists
-strategy on

.

Remark 26

(meager semantics)
.

In
[
45
]
, we introduce
meager semantics
to eliminate sub-optimal strategies.
Meager semantics is defined
globally
, meaning that optimal strategies are uniformly defined over entrances.
Pareto fronts are defined
locally
, with optimal strategies tailored specifically for each entrance. This locality is essential for our technical development in
Sections

4
and
5
.

Pareto fronts of oPGs characterise the winning condition of oPGs defined in
Def.

9
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an oPG, and
i
be an entrance.
The Pareto front
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)
satisfies the following properties:

1.

the entrance
i
is winning iff
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)=\big\{\{\top\}\big\}
,

2.

the entrance
i
is losing iff
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)=\big\{\{\bot\}\big\}
, and

3.

the entrance
i
is pending iff for any
T\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)
,
\top,\bot\not\in T
, and for any
(o_{1},m_{1}),(o_{2},m_{2})\in T
, if
o_{1}=o_{2}
, then
m_{1}=m_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that the entrance
i
is winning. By definition,
\{\top\}\in\llparenthesis i\rrparenthesis
holds.
Since
\{\top\}
is the greatest element,
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)=\big\{\{\top\}\big\}
.

Suppose that the entrance
i
is losing.
By definition,
\bot\in T
holds for any
T\in\llparenthesis i\rrparenthesis
.
This means that for any optimal
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
of a given
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
, the denotation
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis

is the least element in the domain, which proves

.

Suppose that the entrance

For any

,
\top\not\in T
because
\top
is the greatest element.
Similarly,
\bot\not\in T
because
T=\{\bot\}
leads to the contradiction. If
(o,m_{1}),(o,m_{2})\in T

m_{1}=m_{2}
holds because of the minimality of

.
∎

By the characterisation shown in

it suffices to compute the Pareto fronts of operational semantics of string diagrams of parity games for solving our target problem shown in

be a string diagram of PGs, and

of the operational semantics

4
Solving Open Parity Games

In this section, we prove the positional determinacy of Pareto fronts through a novel translation from an oPG to a PG specified by a given
query
, a method we call the
loop construction
.
Utilizing the loop construction, we provide a simple algorithm for computing Pareto fronts.
Additionally, we present an analysis of the upper-bound of its time complexity.

4.1
Positional Determinacy of Pareto Fronts

We begin by stating the positional determinacy of Pareto fronts: positional strategies suffice to determine Pareto fronts.

Theorem 28

(positional determinacy)
.

Pareto fronts are positionally determined, that is,
for any entrance
i
, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},i)=\Big\{\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \text{a positional $\forall$-strategy }\sigma_{\forall}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}\ \Big|\ \text{a positional $\exists$-strategy }\sigma_{\exists}\Big\}^{\mathrm{max}}.

Once we establish the positional determinacy of Pareto fronts,
it becomes clear that solving Pareto fronts is decidable through the enumeration of all positional strategies.

We use the following weaker property for proving
Thm.

28
.

Lemma 29
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an oPG,
i
be an entrance, and
\sigma_{\exists}
be a positional
\exists
-strategy.
Then, positional
\forall
-strategies suffice, that is,
the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \sigma_{\forall}\text{ is a $\forall$-strategy}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}=\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \sigma_{\forall}\text{ is a positional $\forall$-strategy}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}.

Proof.

We take a (finite) oPG
\mathcal{A}[\sigma_{\exists}]
that is induced by the positional
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
;
the nodes and edges of
\mathcal{A}[\sigma_{\exists}]
are the same of
\mathcal{A}
, except the edges from nodes of Player
\exists
are determined by
\sigma_{\exists}
.
Take an optimal
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
on
\mathcal{A}[\sigma_{\exists}]
from
i
, which is also optimal on
\mathcal{A}
w.r.t.
\sigma_{\exists}
.
If the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is losing, then there is a positional optimal
\forall
strategy by the positional determinacy of PGs.

Suppose that the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
reaches an exit
o
.
We write the sequence
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
of nodes for the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
,
where
n
is the smallest index such that
v_{n}=o
.
If the equality
v_{j}=v_{j}
implies
i=j
,
then we can easily construct a positional
\forall
-strategy
\tau_{\forall}
such that
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}=p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}
.

The remaining case is that there are
v_{i}=v_{j}
such that
i\not=j
.
Since
\sigma_{\exists}
is positional, we can assume that
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are owned by Player
\forall
:
if every
v_{i}=v_{j}
such that
i\not=j
is owned by Player
\exists
, it will not reach an exit, which leads to the contradiction.
We assume that
i<j
without loss of generality.
If the maximum priority
m
occurs in
v_{i},\dots,v_{j}
is odd,
then it contradicts to the fact that
\sigma_{\forall}
is optimal.
If the maximum priority
m
occurs in
v_{i},\dots,v_{j}
is even,
then the
shortcut
play
p:=v_{1},\dots,v_{i},v_{j+1},\dots,v_{n}
has the same denotation.
In fact, if the maximum priority in
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is
m
,
then the maximum priority in
p
is also
m
; otherwise, it contradicts to the fact that
\sigma_{\forall}
is optimal.
If the maximum priority in
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is strictly greater than
m
, then
the denotation of
p
does not change because removing
v_{i+1},\dots,v_{j}
does not matter.
By iterating this shortcut construction, we can finally obtain a play
p^{\prime}=v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{l}
such that
v^{\prime}_{i}=v^{\prime}_{j}
implies
i=j
and the equality
\llparenthesis p^{\prime}\rrparenthesis=\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
holds in finite steps.
Thus, we can construct a positional
\forall
-strategy
\tau_{\forall}
such that
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis=\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
.
∎

Remark 30
.

The assumption that the
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
in
Lem.

29
is positional is indispensable.
Consider the oPG shown in
Fig.

3
, and an
\exists
-strategy such that the Player

repeatedly moves from

to

until she reaches

twice, and then moves to

.
Then there is an optimal
\forall
-strategy that moves first from

to

, and later from
b
to
o_{\mathbf{l},1}
, which is not positional.

6
Conclusion

We introduce Pareto fronts of oPGs as a multi-objective solution.
Positional determinacy holds for our multi-objective solution of oPGs,
which leads to our novel algorithm for solving open parity games with the loop construction.
We propose the shortcut construction for open parity games, and we provide a compositional algorithm for string diagrams of parity games by composing Pareto fronts.

A future work is to support the mean-payoff objective, where we have to consider the sum of weights along plays.
Although we expect that positional determinacy also holds for a similar semantics for open mean-payoff (or energy) games,
the number of objectives may become huge because we believe that we have to consider the sum of weights along plays.
This is challenging to achieve an efficient compositional algorithm for string diagrams of mean-payoff games.
For this, we believe that an efficient approximation algorithm for Pareto fronts of open mean-payoff games is needed.

It would be interesting to investigate whether strategy improvement algorithms for parity games (e.g.
[
42
,
33
]
) can be lifted to the setting of string diagrams of parity games.
In this context, a strategy improvement algorithm for string diagrams would maintain a strategy throughout the iteration process—one that is not yet winning due to the openness of the system, but still encodes essential information for obtaining the compositional solution.
Pursuing this direction and comparing our algorithm with such strategy improvement approaches is an exciting future direction.

Extending the diagrammatic language for parity games
[
37
]
to our semantics is another interesting future direction.
In our work we use the sub-priority order on priorities, while they use the trivial discrete order on priorities.
This difference is essential, since positional strategies are sufficient for our semantics, while they are not sufficient for their semantics (of oPGs).

is not essential.
Firstly, we introduce
entrance-dependent
strategies on
\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}
:
intuitively, for each play, they behave positionally depending on the last entrance that appears in the play.

Definition 44

(entrance-dependent strategy)
.

Let
\sigma_{\exists}
be an
\exists
-strategy on
\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}
.
We write
\Sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,\mathrm{p}}
and
\Sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\exists,\mathrm{p}}
for the set of positional
\exists
-strategies on
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, respectively.
The
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
is
entrance-dependent
if
there are assignments
\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,\_}\colon I^{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\Sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,\mathrm{p}}

and
\sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\exists,\_}\colon I^{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow\Sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\exists,\mathrm{p}}

such that for any play
p=p_{1}\cdots p_{n}\in V^{\ast}\times V_{\exists}
, if the last entrance
i^{\prime}
that appears in
p
is
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{A}}
,
then the
\exists
-strategy
\sigma_{\exists}
is given by
\sigma_{\exists}(p)=\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}}(p_{n})
,
and same for the case of
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{B}}
.

We also define
entrance-dependent

\forall
-strategies in the same manner.

Entrance-dependent strategies consist of positional strategies and
monotonicity
of positional strategies holds.
Intuitively, if we use better (or worse) positional strategies for entrance-dependent strategies, then their entrance-dependent strategies are also better (or worse).

Lemma 45

(
\forall
-monotonicity)
.

Let
\sigma_{\exists}
be an entrance-dependent
\exists
-strategy and
\sigma_{\forall},\tau_{\forall}
be entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategies.
Suppose that for each
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{A}}
,
the inequality

\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
holds, and same for
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{B}}
.
Then, the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
holds.

Proof.

We prove the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
.
If the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is winning, then obviously the inequality holds.

Suppose that the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
reaches an exit
o
(of
\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}
) with the maximum priority
m
, that is,
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis=(o,m)
.
By the assumption and the monotonicity of priorities (
Lem.

15
), the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}
is either losing, or reaching the same exit with a potentially different maximum priority
m^{\prime}
such that
m^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}m
.
Thus, the desired inequality also holds.

Suppose that the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is losing.
By collecting the denotations of
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
and
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
,
we obtain an infinte sequence
m_{1}\cdot m_{2}\cdots
of priorities for
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
such that
the maximum priorities that occurs infinitely often in this sequence is odd.
By the assumption, the corresponding infinite sequence
m^{\prime}_{1}\cdot m^{\prime}_{2}\cdots
of priorities for
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}
satisfies that

m^{\prime}_{j}\preceq_{\mathbb{P}}m_{j}
for each
j\in{\rm Nature}
.
For each
j\in{\rm Nature}
, if
m_{j}
is the maximum priority
m
that occurs infinitely often in the sequence
m_{1}\cdot m_{2}\cdots
, then
m^{\prime}_{j}
is a bigger or the same odd number, and at least
one of the such odd number occurs infinitely often in the sequence
m^{\prime}_{1}\cdot m^{\prime}_{2}\cdots
because the size of priorities is finite.
If
m_{j}
is even, then
m^{\prime}_{j}
is a smaller or the same even number, therefore, the maximum even
number that occurs infinitely often in the sequence
m^{\prime}_{1}\cdot m^{\prime}_{2}\cdots
is strictly smaller than
m
.
Therefore, the maximum number that occurs infinitely often in
m^{\prime}_{1}\cdot m^{\prime}_{2}\cdots
is again odd, which means that the desired inequality holds.
∎

Lemma 46

(
\exists
-monotonicity)
.

Let
\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\exists}
be entrance-dependent
\exists
-strategies.
Suppose that for each
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{A}}
,
the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\tau_{\forall};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall}\text{ is a $\forall$-strategy}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}}}\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma_{\forall};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall}\text{ is a $\forall$-strategy}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}},

and same for
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{B}}
.
Then, for any entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
, there is an entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategy
\tau_{\forall}
such that

\llparenthesis p_{\tau_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
.

Proof.

For any entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategy
\sigma_{\forall}
,
we construct an entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategy
\tau_{\forall}
such that
the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\tau_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
holds.
For each entrance
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{A}}
, there is a positional optimal
\forall
-strategy
\sigma^{\prime\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}}
such that
the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\prime\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
holds.
By the assumption, we can take a positional optimal
\forall
-strategy
\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}}
such that
\llparenthesis p_{\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\prime\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
,
thus the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
holds.
Similarly, we can take a positional optimal
\forall
-strategy
\tau^{\mathcal{B}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}}
for each entrance
i^{\prime}\in I^{\mathcal{B}}

such that the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\tau^{\mathcal{B}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\tau^{\mathcal{B}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\exists,i^{\prime}},\sigma^{\mathcal{B}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}};i^{\prime}}\rrparenthesis
holds.
By the almost same argument used in the proof of
Lem.

45
,
we can conclude that the inequality
\llparenthesis p_{\tau_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
holds,
where
\tau_{\forall}
is the entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategy that consists of these positional optimal
\forall
-strategies
\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}}
and
\tau^{\mathcal{B}}_{\forall,i^{\prime}}
.
∎

The last ingredient for proving
Prop.

43
is the fact that entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategies suffice for entrance-dependent
\exists
-strategies.

Lemma 47
.

Let
\sigma_{\exists}
be an entrance-dependent
\exists
-strategy.
Then, entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategies suffice, that is,
the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \text{a $\forall$-strategy }\sigma_{\forall}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}=\big\{\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\ \big|\ \text{an entrance-dependent $\forall$-strategy }\tau_{\forall}\big\}^{\mathrm{min}}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{\forall}
be an optimal
\forall
-strategy.
If the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
is losing, it is straightforward
to construct a losing entrance-dependent
\forall
-strategy by distinguishing last entrances, and positional determinacy of PGs.

Suppose that the play
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
reaches an exit
o
on
\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}
.
Let
i_{1}\cdots i_{n}
be the (finite) sequence of entrances in
I^{\mathcal{A}}\uplus I^{\mathcal{B}}
that occurs in
p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}
, where the ordering is the order of their appearance.
Then, we can take a positioal
\forall
-strategy
\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,j}
for each entrances
i_{j}
such that
the constructed
\forall
-strategy
\tau_{\forall}
from these
\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,j}
satisfies that
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis\preceq_{\mathbf{D}}\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
.
Since
\sigma_{\forall}
is optimal, the equality
\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\tau_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis=\llparenthesis p_{\sigma_{\exists},\sigma_{\forall};i}\rrparenthesis
holds.

Here,
\tau_{\forall}
may not be an entrance-dependent, that is, there are
j\not=j^{\prime}
such that
i_{j}=i_{j^{\prime}}
and
\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,j}\not=\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{\forall,j^{\prime}}
in general.
If
\tau_{\forall}
is not an entrance-dependent, we construct an
\forall
-entrance-dependent strategy by a similar argument of the proof of
Lem.

29
; note that we assume
\sigma_{\exists}
is entrance-dependent.
∎

Definition 48

(lower preorder)
.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a set such that
\mathbf{T}:=\{T^{\mathrm{max}}\mid T\subseteq\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}},\text{and $T\not=\emptyset$}\}
.
Then, the
lower preorder

\preceq_{\mathbf{L}}
on
\mathbf{T}
is defined as follows:
T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}\preceq_{\mathbf{L}}T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}
if for any result
\mathbf{r}_{1}\in T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}
, there is a result
\mathbf{r}_{2}\in T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}

such that
\mathbf{r}_{1}\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}}}\mathbf{r}_{2}
.

Lemma 49
.

The preorder
\preceq_{\mathbf{L}}
is a partial order.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}\preceq_{\mathbf{L}}T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}
and
T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}\preceq_{\mathbf{L}}T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}
imply that
T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}\subseteq T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}
.
For any result
\mathbf{r}_{1}\in T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}
, there is a result
\mathbf{r}_{2}\in T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}
such that
\mathbf{r}_{1}\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}}}\mathbf{r}_{2}
.
Similarly, there is a
\mathbf{r}_{3}\in T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}
such that
\mathbf{r}_{2}\preceq_{\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{A}\fatsemi\mathcal{B}}}\mathbf{r}_{3}
.
Thus, we can conclude that
\mathbf{r}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{3}
because of the maximality of
T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}
.
Since the result order is a partial order, we can conclude that
\mathbf{r}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{2}
, thus the desired
T_{1}^{\mathrm{max}}\subseteq T_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}
holds.

∎

Strong Movable Categories and Strong Movability of Topological Spaces
Pavel S. Gevorgyan, T. A. Avakyan

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to one of the important notions of the shape theory: that of strong movability, which was primarily introduced by K. Borsuk for metrizable compacts. A strong movability criterion is proved for topological spaces, which in particular reveals a new, categorical approach to the strong movability.

1.
Introduction

Strong movability is one of the important notions of the shape theory. It had a significant role first of all in the study of stable topological spaces, i.e. spaces having a shape of some CW-complex (see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
). The strong movability notion primarily was introduced by K. Borsuk
[
3
]
for metrizable compacts by means of the neighborhoods of the given compact, which is embedded with its closure in some AR-space. For arbitrary topological space, the notion of strong movability was introduced by Mardešić
[
7
]
by means of the inverse spectrums associated with the given space.

In particular, the results of this paper permit to apply a new, more general approach to the notion of strong movability. This approach is based on some ideas applied in
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
to the movability of topological spaces. As it shown in
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
, the same ideas are applicable also for studying the uniform movability of topological spaces.

The criterion of strong movability of topological spaces, which is obtained in the present paper, leads also to some improvements and remarks on the statements of Theorem 4.1 in
[
2
]
and Theorem 3 in
[
5
]
.

3.
Strongly Movable Categories

Let
K
and
L
be any categories and let
\Phi:K\to L
be any covariant functor.

Definition 5
.

We say that the category
K
is movable with respect to the category
L
and the functor
\Phi:K\to L
, if for any object
X\in ob(K)
there exist another object
M(X)\in ob(K)
and a morphism
m_{X}\in Mor_{K}(M(X),X)
, such that for any object
Y\in ob(K)
and any morphism
p\in Mor_{K}(Y,X)
there is another morphism
u\in Mor_{L}(\Phi(M(X)),\Phi(Y))
for which
\Phi(p)\circ u=\Phi(m_{X})
.

If
K
is a complete subcategory of the category
L
and
\Phi:K\hookrightarrow L
is an embedding functor of Definition
5
, then the subcategory
K
is said to be
movable with respect to the category L
(see Definition 1 in
[
2
]
).

Supposing that
X
is a topological space with a comma category
W^{X}
, we introduce a covariant functor
\Omega:W^{X}\to HCW
, which puts into correspondence to each object
f:X\to Q
an object
Q\in HCW
and to each morphism
\eta:(f:X\to Q)\to(f^{\prime}:X\to Q^{\prime})
,
(\eta\circ f=f^{\prime})
a morphism
\eta:Q\to Q^{\prime}
. We call
\Omega

forgetful functor
.

Theorem 1
.

A topological space
X
is movable if and only if the comma category
W^{X}
is movable with respect to the category
HCW
and the forgetful functor
\Omega:W^{X}\to HCW
.

The proof of this theorem follows from Theorem 4 in
[
2
]
(see also Theorem 4.2 in
[
5
]
). Note that the above Theorem
1
corrects an inaccuracy which we found in the statement of Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
(see also Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
).

Proposition 1
.

Let a category
K
be movable with respect to the category
L
and the functor
\Phi:K\to L
, and let
F:L\to L^{\prime}
be an arbitrary functor. Then the category
K
is movable with respect to the category
L^{\prime}
and the functor
F\circ\Phi:K\to L^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Definition
5
, for any object
X\in ob(K)
there exist another object
M(X)\in ob(K)
and
a morphism
m_{X}\in Mor_{K}(M(X),X)
, such that for any object
Y\in ob(K)
and any morphism
p\in Mor_{K}(Y,X)
there is a morphism
u\in Mor_{L}(\Phi(M(X)),\Phi(Y))
for which
\Phi(p)\circ u=\Phi(m_{X})
. Hence,
F(\Phi(p))\circ F(u)=F(\Phi(m_{X}))
, since
F:L\to L^{\prime}
is a covariant functor. This means that
F(u)\in Mor_{L^{\prime}}((F\circ\Phi)(M(X)),(F\circ\Phi)(Y))
is the required morphism, i.e. the category
K
is movable with respect to the category
L^{\prime}
and the functor
F\circ\Phi:K\to L^{\prime}
.
∎

Definition 6
.

We call a category
K

strongly movable
, if it is movable with respect to itself and the identical functor
1_{K}
. In other words, if for any object
X\in ob(K)
there exist an object
M(X)\in ob(K)
and a morphism
m_{X}\in Mor_{K}(M(X),X)
, such that for any object
Y\in ob(K)
and any morphism
p\in Mor_{K}(Y,X)
there is a morphism
u_{p}\in Mor_{K}(M(X),Y)
for which
p\circ u_{p}=m_{X}
.

The naturalness of this definition will become evident later, after the proof of Theorem
6
.

Example 1
.

Let
S_{0}
be a category of sets with a tagged element, whose objects are all possible pairs
(A,a_{0})
, where
A
is a nonempty set and let
a_{0}
be some tagged element of
A
. Further, let the morphisms of
S_{0}
be all possible mappings
(A,a_{0})\to(B,b_{0})
transforming
a_{0}
to a tagged element
b_{0}
. Then it is not difficult to verify that the category
S_{0}
is strongly movable.

Example 2
.

Let
Gr
be a category of groups whose objects are all possible groups and morphisms are all possible homomorphisms between groups. Then, one can easily verify that the category
Gr
is strongly movable.

The next theorem gives many interesting, non trivial examples of strongly movable categories.

Let

Proposition 2
.

If
K
is a strong movable category, then it is movable with respect to any category
L
and any functor
\Phi:K\to L
.

Proof.

By Definition

is movable with respect to itself and the identical functor

is movable with respect to the category
L

\Phi\circ 1_{K}=\Phi:K\to L

Proposition 3
.

Let a category
K
be movable with respect to the category
L
and the functor
\Phi:K\to L
. If
\Phi:K\to L
is functorial domination, then

is a strongly movable category.

Proof.

In our conditions, there exists a functor

such that
\Psi\circ\Phi=1_{K}
. Hence, by Proposition

K
is movable with respect to itself and the functor
\Psi\circ\Phi=1_{K}:K\to K

Theorem 3
.

The product
\prod\limits_{i\in I}K_{i}
of categories

,

,

Let the product

Definition 7
.

We say that a category
K
is functorially weak dominated by a category
L
, or briefly write
K\lesssim L
, if there exist some functors
F:K\to L
and
G:L\to K
, such that
G\circ F\rightsquigarrow 1_{K}
(see Definition
2
).

Remark 1
.

It is obvious that functorial domination implies weak functorial domination, i.e.
K\leqslant L
implies
K\lesssim L
.

Theorem 4
.

If
K\lesssim L
, then the strong movability of the category
L
implies the strong movability of the category
K
.

Proof.

We suppose that
F:K\to L
and
G:L\to K
are functors, such that
G\circ F\rightsquigarrow 1_{K}
, and
\varphi:G\circ F\to 1_{K}
is some natural transformation. Then, for an arbitrary object
X
of the category
K
we consider the object
M(F(X))
and the morphism
m_{F(X)}:M(F(X))\to F(X)
of the category
L
, whose existence follows from the definition of strong movability for
L
. Besides, for
X\in K
the object
M(X)=G(M(F(X)))\in K
and the morphism
m_{X}=\varphi(X)\circ G(m_{F(X)}):M(X)\to X
satisfies the conditions of strong movability of the category
K
. Indeed, let
Y
be an object and let
p:Y\to X
be a morphism of the category
K
. By movability of the category
L
for the morphism
F(p):F(Y)\to F(X)
of
L
there is a morphism
v:M(F(X))\to F(Y)
such that
F(p)\circ v=m_{F(X)}
. It remains to see that the morphism
u=\varphi(Y)\circ G(v):M(X)\to Y
satisfies the equality
p\circ u=m_{X}
. Indeed,

p\circ u=(p\circ\varphi(Y))\circ G(v))=(\varphi(X)\circ G(F(p)))\circ G(v))=\varphi(X)\circ G(m_{F(X)})=m_{X}.

Thus,
K
is a strongly movable category, and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem
4
and Remark

immediately imply the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

If a category

is functorially dominating the category
L

4.
Strong Movability Of Topological Spaces

The notion of strong movability of topological spaces was introduced by Mardešić
[
7
]
(see Definition
4
). For the aims of this paper, we need to restate this definition in a somewhat different form.

Proposition 4
.

Let the inverse system
\{X_{\alpha},p_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}},A\}
of a category
HCW
be associated with a topological space
X
. Then
X
is strongly movable if and only if the following condition is fulfilled:

(SM2) for any
\alpha\in A
, there is
\alpha^{\prime}\in A
,
\alpha^{\prime}\geqslant\alpha
, such that for any
\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in A
,

\alpha^{\prime\prime}\geqslant\alpha
, there exists a homotopy class
r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}:X_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to X_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}
for which the following equalities simultaneously are true:

(4)

p_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime\prime}}r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}=p_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}},

(5)

r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\circ p_{\alpha^{\prime}}=p_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

Let
X
be a strongly movable space. Then, in accordance with Definition
4
it suffices to see that the equality (
5
) is true. This equality follows from formula (
2
) and condition
1^{\circ}
of Definition
3
, i.e.

r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\circ p_{\alpha^{\prime}}=r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\circ p_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{*}}\circ p_{\alpha^{*}}=p_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{*}}\circ p_{\alpha^{*}}=p_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}.

To prove that, conversely, the condition (SM2) provides the strong movability of the topological space
X
, i.e. the condition (SM1) of Definition
4
, we prove the existence of an index
\alpha^{*}\in A
,
\alpha^{*}\geqslant\alpha^{\prime}
,
\alpha^{*}\geqslant\alpha^{\prime\prime}
, for which formula (
2
) is true. To this end, we observe that for any index
\alpha^{0}\in A
,
\alpha^{0}\geqslant\alpha^{\prime}
,
\alpha^{0}\geqslant\alpha^{\prime\prime}
, the equality (
5
) can be rewritten in the form

r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\circ p_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{0}}\circ p_{\alpha^{0}}=p_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{0}}\circ p_{\alpha^{0}}.

By the condition
3^{\circ}
of Definition
3
there is an index
\alpha^{*}\in A
,
\alpha^{*}\geqslant\alpha^{0}
, such that

r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\circ p_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{0}}\circ p_{\alpha^{0}\alpha^{*}}=p_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{0}}\circ p_{\alpha^{0}\alpha^{*}}

or

r^{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\circ p_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{*}}=p_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{*}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5
.

A topological space
X
is strongly movable if and only if the following condition is fulfilled:

(*)
for any CW-complex
Q
and any homotopy class
f:X\to Q
, there are a CW-complex
Q^{\prime}
and homotopy classes
f^{\prime}:X\to Q^{\prime}
and
\eta:Q^{\prime}\to Q
which satisfy the equality
f=\eta\circ f^{\prime}
, so that for any CW-complex
Q^{\prime\prime}
and any homotopy classes
f^{\prime\prime}:X\to Q^{\prime\prime}
and
\eta^{\prime}:Q^{\prime\prime}\to Q
, for which
f=\eta^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime\prime}
, there is a homotopy class
\eta^{\prime\prime}:Q^{\prime}\to Q^{\prime\prime}
such that

(6)

\eta^{\prime}\circ\eta^{\prime\prime}=\eta,

(7)

\eta^{\prime\prime}\circ f^{\prime}=f^{\prime\prime}.

Proof.

Supposing that the condition
(*)
is fulfilled, we consider the spectrum
\{X_{\alpha},p_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}},A\}
associated with
X
and prove its strong movability, i.e. that it satisfies the condition (SM2) of Proposition
4
. To this end, we assume that
\alpha\in A
is any element and
p_{\alpha}:X\to X_{\alpha}
is the natural projection. Then, for the homotopy class
p_{\alpha}:X\to X_{\alpha}
we consider a suitable CW-complex
Q^{\prime}
and the homotopy classes
f^{\prime}:X\to Q^{\prime}
and
\eta:Q^{\prime}\to X_{\alpha}
satisfying the condition
\eta\circ f^{\prime}=p_{\alpha}
, whose existence follows from
(*)
. Since
assX=\{X_{\alpha},p_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}},A\}
, for
f^{\prime}:X\to Q^{\prime}
there are
\tilde{a}\in A
,
\tilde{a}\geqslant\alpha
and
\tilde{f^{\prime}}:X_{\tilde{\alpha}}\to Q^{\prime}
, such that

(8)

f^{\prime}=\tilde{f^{\prime}}\circ p_{\tilde{\alpha}}.

It is not difficult to verify that

(9)

p_{\alpha\tilde{\alpha}}\circ p_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\eta\circ\tilde{f^{\prime}}\circ p_{\tilde{\alpha}}.

Indeed,

\eta\circ\tilde{f^{\prime}}\circ p_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\eta\circ f^{\prime}=p_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha\tilde{\alpha}}\circ p_{\tilde{\alpha}},

and by the equality (
9
) and Definition

, there is an index
\alpha^{\prime}\in A
,
\alpha^{\prime}\geqslant\tilde{\alpha}

Optimal universal quantum circuits for unitary complex conjugation
Daniel Ebler, Michał Horodecki, Marcin Marciniak, Tomasz Młynik, Marco Túlio Quintino, Michał Studziński

Abstract
Let U d U_{d} be a unitary operator representing an arbitrary d d -dimensional unitary quantum operation. This work presents optimal quantum circuits for transforming a number k k of calls of U d U_{d} into its complex conjugate U d ¯ \overline{U_{d}} . Our circuits admit a parallel implementation and are proven to be optimal for any k k and d d with an average fidelity of ⟨ F ⟩ = k + 1 d ⁡ ( d − k ) \left\langle{F}\right\rangle=\frac{k+1}{d(d-k)} . Optimality is shown for average fidelity, robustness to noise, and other standard figures of merit. This extends previous works which considered the scenario of a single call ( k = 1 k=1 ) of the operation U d U_{d} , and the special case of k = d − 1 k=d-1 calls. We then show that our results encompass optimal transformations from k k calls of U d U_{d} to f ⁡ ( U d ) f(U_{d}) for any arbitrary homomorphism f f from the group of d d -dimensional unitary operators to itself, since complex conjugation is the only non-trivial automorphism on the group of unitary operators. Finally, we apply our optimal complex conjugation implementation to design a probabilistic circuit for reversing arbitrary quantum evolutions.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{d^{2}}\frac{d\binom{d}{k+1}}{\binom{d}{k+1}\binom{d}{k+1}}\tr\Big(A(d,k+1)\,\big(\mathds{1}_{d}^{\otimes k}-A(d,k)\big)\otimes\sigma\Big).

Since
\mathds{1}_{d}^{\otimes k}-A(d,k)
is positive semidefinite, we have that

\displaystyle\tr(\Omega\,S)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}}\frac{d\binom{d}{k}\binom{d}{k+1}\binom{d}{k+1}}{\binom{d}{k+1}\binom{d}{k+1}\binom{d}{k+1}}

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{d}{k}}{d\binom{d}{k+1}}

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d!}{k!(d-k)(d-k-1)!}\frac{(k+1)k!(d-k-1)!}{d\,d!}

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k+1}{d(d-k)}.

(87)

This finishes the proof.
∎

As we are going to show next, this protocol cannot be improved. Its average fidelity is exactly
\left\langle F\right\rangle=\frac{k+1}{d(d-k)}
and we have that
\tr\Big(A(d,k+1)\,\big(\mathds{1}_{d}^{\otimes k}-A(d,k)\big)\otimes\sigma\Big)=0
for any quantum state
\sigma
.

We start from the celebrated Schur-Weyl duality relating irreps of the general linear group
GL(d)
and symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Namely, it is known that the diagonal action of the general linear group
GL(d)
of invertible complex matrices and of the symmetric group on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
commute:

\displaystyle V(\pi)(X\otimes\cdots\otimes X)=(X\otimes\cdots\otimes X)V(\pi),

(94)

where
\pi\in S_{n}
and
X\in GL(d)
. Due to the above relation, there exists a basis called the Schur basis, producing a decomposition into irreps of
GL(d)
and
S_{n}
simultaneously. Hence, for the whole space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
we have the following

Theorem 2

(Schur-Weyl duality)
.

The tensor product space
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\vdash n\\
H(\lambda)\leq d\end{subarray}}\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{S}_{\lambda},

(95)

where the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on the space
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
and the general linear group
GL(d)
acts on the space
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
, labelled by the same Young diagram
\lambda\vdash n
.

In fact, for our purposes we need the Schur-Weyl duality not for
GL(d)
but rather its subgroup
SU(d)
, see
[
28
]
. However, it turns out that
any irreducible representation of
GL(d)
on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
is an irreducible representation of
SU(d)
on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}
and vice versa. In particular, it means that that also the linear invariants of
SU(d)
are
those of
GL(d)
, and they belong to the group algebra of
S_{n}
denoted by
\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
. This however means that we can write the same statement in Theorem
2
for group
SU(d)

[
37
]
.
From the decomposition given in Theorem
2
we deduce that for a given irrep
\lambda
of
S_{n}
, the space
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
is the multiplicity space of dimension
m_{\lambda}
(multiplicity of irrep
\lambda
), while the space
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
is the representation space of dimension
d_{\lambda}
(dimension of irrep
\lambda
). Every operator commuting with the diagonal action of
U^{\otimes n}
, due to the Schur’s lemma must be proportional to identity on spaces
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
and admits non-triviall parts on spaces
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
. Conversely, any operator commuting with action of the permutation group
S_{n}
is non-trivially supported on irreducible spaces
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
. In the former case, i.e. in very irreducible space
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
one can construct an orthonormal irreducible basis
\{|\lambda,i\rangle\}
, where
i=1,\ldots,d_{\lambda}
, for example by exploiting Young-Yamanouchi construction
[
38
]
.
With this vectors we associate an irreducible basis operators
E^{\lambda}_{ij}
, for

\displaystyle E_{ij}^{\lambda}:=\mathds{1}^{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}}\otimes|\lambda,i\rangle\langle\lambda,j|_{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}.

(96)

Sine the basis

\displaystyle E_{ij}^{\lambda}E_{kl}^{\lambda^{\prime}}=\delta^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\delta_{jk}E^{\lambda}_{il},\qquad\tr E_{ij}^{\lambda}=\delta_{ij}m_{\lambda}.

(97)

From the operators
E_{ij}^{\lambda}

\sum_{i}|\lambda,i\rangle\langle\lambda,i|_{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}}=\mathds{1}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda}
, we can construct so-called Young projectors

[
38
]
on the irreducible components
\lambda

\displaystyle P^{\lambda}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\lambda}}E_{ii}^{\lambda}=\mathds{1}^{\mathcal{U}}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathds{1}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda},

(98)

\displaystyle P^{\lambda}P^{\lambda^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\delta^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}P^{\lambda},

(99)

\displaystyle\tr P^{\lambda}=

\displaystyle m_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}.

(100)

\alpha,\beta\in\lambda

\displaystyle\phi^{\lambda}(\pi)=\left(\phi^{\lambda}_{i_{\lambda}j_{\lambda}}(\pi)\right)=\left(\phi^{\alpha\beta}_{i_{\alpha}j_{\beta}}(\pi)\right),

(101)

where the indices

are the standard matrix indices running from 1 to
d_{\lambda}
. From the above we see the following connection between the indices, namely for fixed

one has
i_{\lambda}=(\alpha\in\lambda,i_{\alpha}),j_{\lambda}=(\beta\in\lambda,j_{\beta})

run from 1 to

respectively. Please see Figure

The diagonal blocks of the matrix

A similar procedure (induction) can be also done in the reverse order, i.e. starting from an irrep

.

Operator

Proof.

The operator

\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\lambda}m_{\alpha}}{m_{\gamma}}\delta_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}\delta_{j_{\alpha}l_{\gamma}}\delta_{i_{\alpha}k_{\gamma}}.

(108)

To get the third equality we use Lemma 7 from

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}\sum_{k_{\beta},l_{\beta^{\prime}}}x_{k_{\beta}l_{\beta^{\prime}}}^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}(\lambda)\tr\left(E^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}_{k_{\beta}l_{\beta^{\prime}}}(\lambda)E^{\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}_{l_{\gamma^{\prime}}k_{\gamma}}(\lambda^{\prime})\right)

The final sum runs only over

Proof.

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq c\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\vdash(k)\\

To keep the notation simple, we drop indices the
\bm{I},\bm{O},P,F
, since it will be clear from the context which systems are referred to. First, we notice that the right-hand side of (
123
) can be re-written by applying Lemma
2
with
\tau=\mathds{1}/d
, leading to

\begin{split}&\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\frac{d}{m_{\alpha}}{E_{kl}^{\alpha}}\otimes\tau\otimes{E_{kl}^{\alpha}}\otimes\tau\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{dm_{\alpha}}{E_{kl}^{\alpha}}\otimes\mathds{1}\otimes{E_{kl}^{\alpha}}\otimes\mathds{1}\\
&=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{k_{\alpha},l_{\alpha}=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\ni\alpha}\frac{1}{dm_{\alpha}}E^{\alpha\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}l_{\alpha}}(\lambda)\otimes E^{\alpha\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}l_{\alpha}}(\lambda^{\prime})\\
&=\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\in\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{k_{\alpha},l_{\alpha}=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{dm_{\alpha}}E^{\alpha\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}l_{\alpha}}(\lambda)\otimes E^{\alpha\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}l_{\alpha}}(\lambda^{\prime}).\end{split}

(124)

Here, we introduced symbol
\alpha\in\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\prime}
, indicating that simultaneously
\alpha\in\lambda
and
\alpha\in\lambda^{\prime}
.
Next, by Notation
1
every irreducible operator basis labelled by
\lambda\vdash k+1
on the left-hand side of equation (
123
) can be written as

\displaystyle E_{pq}^{\lambda}=E^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}_{p_{\beta}q_{\beta^{\prime}}}(\lambda).

(125)

The above expression and final result of (
124
) allows us to re-write inequality (
123
) in the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}\sum_{p_{\beta},q_{\beta^{\prime}}}\frac{E^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}_{p_{\beta}q_{\beta^{\prime}}}(\lambda)\otimes E^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}_{p_{\beta}q_{\beta^{\prime}}}(\lambda)}{m_{\lambda}}

(126)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq c\cdot\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha\in\lambda\wedge\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{k_{\alpha},l_{\alpha}=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{dm_{\alpha}}E^{\alpha\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}l_{\alpha}}(\lambda)\otimes E^{\alpha\alpha}_{k_{\alpha}l_{\alpha}}(\lambda^{\prime}).

Now, notice that every
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\vdash(k+1)
label a different nonequivalent irrep. This means that we deal with different orthogonal blocks. From the above inequality it is clear that non-trivial solutions for the constant
c
can be obtained only for
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
– for
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
we do not have cross terms on the left-hand side of (
126
). Consequently, it is sufficient to restrict ourselves to comparing terms for every
\lambda
separately:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}\sum_{p_{\beta},q_{\beta^{\prime}}}\frac{E^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}_{p_{\beta}q_{\beta^{\prime}}}(\lambda)\otimes E^{\beta\beta^{\prime}}_{p_{\beta}q_{\beta^{\prime}}}(\lambda)}{m_{\lambda}}

(127)

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\sum_{\alpha\in\lambda}\sum_{i_{\alpha},j_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{dm_{\alpha}}E^{\alpha\alpha}_{i_{\alpha}j_{\alpha}}(\lambda)\otimes E^{\alpha\alpha}_{i_{\alpha}j_{\alpha}}(\lambda).

All the operators in the above expression are the irreducible basis operators for the symmetric groups
S_{k+1}
and
S_{k}
. Hence, by Schur-Weyl duality, they act non-trivially only on the symmetric component of the Schur-Weyl decomposition (
95
).
Now, using the explicit forms of the irreducible basis operators in the Schur basis from (
96
) we rewrite (
127
) in the following way (ignoring the multiplicity space):

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}m_{\lambda}}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}\sum_{p_{\beta},q_{\beta^{\prime}}}|\beta,p_{\beta}\rangle\langle\beta^{\prime},q_{\beta^{\prime}}|\otimes|\beta,p_{\beta}\rangle\langle\beta^{\prime},q_{\beta^{\prime}}|

(128)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\in\lambda}\sum_{i_{\alpha},j_{\alpha}}\frac{c}{dm_{\alpha}}|\alpha,i_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\alpha,j_{\alpha}|\otimes|\alpha,i_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\alpha,j_{\alpha}|.

Using the definition
|\Phi^{+}_{\alpha}\rangle=\sum_{i_{\alpha}=1}^{d_{\alpha}}|i_{\alpha}\rangle|i_{\alpha}\rangle
for an unnormalised maximally entangled state on irrep
\alpha
and rearranging the irreps, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}m_{\lambda}}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}|\beta\beta\rangle\langle\beta^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}|\otimes|\Phi^{+}_{\beta}\rangle\langle\Phi^{+}_{\beta^{\prime}}|

(129)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\in\lambda}\frac{c}{dm_{\alpha}}|\alpha\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha\alpha|\otimes|\Phi^{+}_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\Phi^{+}_{\alpha}|.

Now, we notice that by applying an isometry
|\beta\beta\rangle\mapsto|\beta\rangle
we can rewrite the above inequality as:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}m_{\lambda}}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}|\beta\rangle\langle\beta^{\prime}|\otimes|\Phi^{+}_{\beta}\rangle\langle\Phi^{+}_{\beta^{\prime}}|

(130)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}\frac{c}{dm_{\beta}}|\beta\rangle\langle\beta|\otimes|\Phi^{+}_{\beta}\rangle\langle\Phi^{+}_{\beta}|.

(131)

Defining a new set of normalised vectors
|\psi_{\beta}\rangle:=\frac{|\widetilde{\psi}_{\beta}\rangle}{||\widetilde{\psi}_{\beta}||}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{\beta}}}|\widetilde{\psi}_{\beta}\rangle
, where
|\widetilde{\psi}_{\beta}\rangle:=|\beta\rangle\otimes|\Phi^{+}_{\beta}\rangle
, we rewrite the above equation as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}m_{\lambda}}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}\sqrt{d_{\beta}d_{\beta^{\prime}}}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\beta^{\prime}}|\leq\frac{c}{d}\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}\frac{d_{\beta}}{m_{\beta}}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\beta}|.

(132)

Notice that the matrix
A:=\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}\frac{d_{\beta}}{m_{\beta}}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\beta}|
is diagonal and of the full rank. These properties allow us to compute easily
A^{-1/2}
, which is
A^{-1/2}=\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\beta}}{d_{\beta}}}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\beta}|
, since blocks labelled by different
\beta
are orthogonal. Sandwiching inequality (
132
) by
A^{-1/2}
and using fact that
\langle\psi_{\beta}|\psi_{\beta^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}
, gives the following after a few simple steps

\displaystyle\frac{1}{dm_{\lambda}}\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\lambda}\sqrt{m_{\beta}m_{\beta^{\prime}}}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle\langle\psi_{\beta^{\prime}}|\leq c\cdot\mathds{1},

(133)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{dm_{\lambda}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\leq c\cdot\mathds{1},

(134)

where we defined
|\psi\rangle:=\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}\sqrt{m_{\beta}}|\psi_{\beta}\rangle
, with
||\psi||^{2}=\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}m_{\beta}
. Expression (
134
) implies that

\displaystyle\forall\ \lambda\vdash k+1\quad\frac{1}{d}\frac{\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}m_{\beta}}{m_{\lambda}}\leq c.

(135)

To satisfy the above inequality, one has to choose the constant
c
at least equal to the maximal possible value of the ratio
(1/d)\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}m_{\beta}/m_{\lambda}
calculated for all possible
\lambda^{\prime}
s. This finally leads to

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{d}\max_{\lambda\vdash(k+1)}\frac{\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}m_{\beta}}{m_{\lambda}}.

(136)

∎

Lemma
3
connects the minimal value of the constant
c
from (
55
) with the specific choice of the operator
\hat{S}
in (
116
) and group theoretical parameters describing irreps of the symmetric groups
S_{k}
and
S_{k+1}
. However, the final expression (
122
) still involves a maximisation problem.
To solve this maximisation explicitly, let us define an auxiliary function of Young diagrams of
n
boxes:

c(\lambda):=\frac{\sum_{\beta\in\lambda}m_{\beta}}{m_{\lambda}}.

(137)

For the above defined function we can formulate the following:

Proposition 1
.

For a Young diagram
\lambda\vdash n
with its step representation
(k_{1},p_{1},k_{2},p_{2},\ldots,k_{s},p_{s})
, the function from (
137
) is given by

\displaystyle c(\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{k_{j}p_{j}}{d-\sum_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}+\sum_{i=j}^{s}p_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\frac{k_{i}}{d(c_{i},c_{j})}\right)

(138)

\displaystyle\ \hskip 25.60747pt\prod_{i=j+1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{p_{i}}{d(c_{j},c_{i})}\right),

where we assume that the product is equal to
1
when the lower range of the products is larger than the upper one.

Proof.

Let
(k_{1},p_{1},\ldots,k_{s},p_{s})
be a step representation of
\lambda\vdash n
.
Note that removing a one box from the Young diagram
\lambda
produces a new Young diagram if and only if the box removed is a corner box. Diagrams
\beta\in\lambda
can therefore be labelled by
\mu=1,2,\ldots,s
. We use the convention that
\beta_{\mu}
denotes the diagram obtained by removing the box
c_{\mu}
. According to formula (
137
)

c(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}\frac{m_{\beta_{\mu}}}{m_{\lambda}}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}\frac{\prod_{b\in\beta_{\mu}}\dfrac{d-i_{b}+j_{b}}{h_{\mu}(b)}}{\prod_{b\in\lambda}\dfrac{d-i_{b}+j_{b}}{h(b)}},

(139)

where
i_{b},j_{b}
denote the coordinates of a box
b
,
h_{\mu}(b)
and
h(b)
is the length of the hook of
b
in
\beta_{\mu}
and
\lambda
respectively. Here we used a relation between multiplicity of a given irrep with the number of semisimple Young diagrams
[
35
]
.

For
\mu=1,2,\ldots,s
we define the following subsets
R_{\mu}
,
C_{\mu}
of
\lambda

\displaystyle R_{\mu}:=\{b\in\lambda:\,i_{b}=i_{c_{\mu}},\;j_{b}<j_{c_{\mu}}\},

(140)

\displaystyle C_{\mu}:=\{b\in\lambda:\,i_{b}<i_{c_{\mu}},\;j_{b}=j_{c_{\mu}}\}.

(141)

These subsets contain respectively boxes which are in the same row/column as the corner box
c_{\mu}
not taking into account
c_{\mu}
itself.
Let
G_{\mu}:=R_{\mu}\sqcup C_{\mu}\sqcup\{c_{\mu}\}
. Observe that
h_{\mu}(b)=h(b)
if
b\not\in G_{\mu}
. Thus, (
139
) simplifies to

\displaystyle c(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}\frac{\prod_{b\in G_{\mu}\setminus\{c_{\mu}\}}\dfrac{d-i_{b}+j_{b}}{h_{\mu}(b)}}{\prod_{b\in G_{\mu}}\dfrac{d-i_{b}+j_{b}}{h(b)}}

(142)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}\frac{1}{d-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}}\prod_{b\in C_{\mu}}\frac{h(b)}{h(b)-1}\prod_{b\in R_{\mu}}\frac{h(b)}{h(b)-1},

(143)

where we used the fact that
h(c_{\mu})=1
, and
h_{\mu}(b)=h(b)-1
for
b\in G_{\mu}\setminus\{c_{\mu}\}
. We define

C_{\mu,\nu}:=\{b\in C_{\mu}:\,i_{c_{\nu-1}}<i_{b}\leq i_{c_{\nu}}\}

(144)

for
\nu=1,\ldots,\mu
, and

R_{\mu,\nu}:=\{b\in R_{\mu}:\,j_{c_{\nu+1}}<j_{b}\leq j_{c_{\nu}}\}

(145)

for
\nu=\mu,\ldots,s
, with the convention that
i_{c_{0}}=0=j_{c_{s+1}}
. Observe that
C_{\mu}=\bigsqcup_{\nu=1}^{\mu}C_{\mu,\nu}
and
R_{\mu}=\bigsqcup_{\nu=\mu}^{s}R_{\mu,\nu}
, and for
b\in C_{\mu}\sqcup R_{\mu}
,

h(b)=\begin{cases}i_{c_{\mu}}-i_{b}+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}+1,&\mbox{if $b\in C_{\mu,\nu}$,}\\
i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j_{b}+1,&\mbox{if $b\in R_{\mu,\nu}$.}\end{cases}

(146)

Hence, exploiting (
92
), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{b\in C_{\mu}}\frac{h(b)}{h(b)-1}=\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu}\prod_{b\in C_{\mu,\nu}}\frac{h(b)}{h(b)-1}

(147)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu}\prod_{b\in C_{\mu,\nu}}\frac{i_{c_{\mu}}-i_{b}+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}+1}{i_{c_{\mu}}-i_{b}+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}}

(148)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=i_{c_{\mu-1}}+1}^{i_{c_{\mu}}-1}\frac{i_{c_{\mu}}-i+1}{i_{c_{\mu}}-i}\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu-1}\prod_{i=i_{c_{\nu-1}}+1}^{i_{c_{\nu}}}\frac{i_{c_{\mu}}-i+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}+1}{i_{c_{\mu}}-i+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}}

(149)

\displaystyle=(i_{c_{\mu}}-i_{c_{\mu-1}})\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu-1}\frac{i_{c_{\mu}}-i_{c_{\nu-1}}+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}}{i_{c_{\mu}}-i_{c_{\nu}}+j_{c_{\nu}}-j_{c_{\mu}}}

(150)

\displaystyle=k_{\mu}\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu-1}\frac{k_{\nu}+p_{\nu}+\ldots+k_{\mu}}{p_{\nu}+\ldots+k_{\mu}}

(151)

\displaystyle=k_{\mu}\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu-1}\left(1+\frac{k_{\nu}}{d(c_{\nu},c_{\mu})}\right).

(152)

Analogously, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{b\in R_{\mu}}\frac{h(b)}{h(b)-1}=\prod_{\nu=\mu}^{s}\prod_{b\in R_{\mu,\nu}}\frac{h(b)}{h(b)-1}

(153)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\nu=\mu}^{s}\prod_{b\in R_{\mu,\nu}}\frac{i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j_{b}+1}{i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j_{b}}

(154)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=j_{c_{\mu+1}}+1}^{j_{c_{\mu}}-1}\frac{j_{c_{\mu}}-j+1}{j_{c_{\mu}}-j}\prod_{\nu=\mu+1}^{s}\prod_{j=j_{c_{\nu+1}}+1}^{j_{c_{\nu}}}\frac{i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j+1}{i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j}

(155)

\displaystyle=(j_{c_{\mu}}-j_{c_{\mu+1}})\prod_{\nu=\mu+1}^{s}\frac{i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j_{c_{\nu+1}}}{i_{c_{\nu}}-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}-j_{c_{\nu}}}

(156)

\displaystyle=p_{\mu}\prod_{\nu=\mu+1}^{s}\frac{p_{\mu}+\ldots+k_{\nu}+p_{\nu}}{p_{\mu}+\ldots+k_{\nu}}

(157)

\displaystyle=p_{\mu}\prod_{\nu=\mu+1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{p_{\nu}}{d(c_{\mu},c_{\nu})}\right).

(158)

Coming back to (
143
), we get

c(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}\frac{k_{\mu}p_{\mu}}{d-i_{c_{\mu}}+j_{c_{\mu}}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{\mu-1}\left(1+\frac{k_{\nu}}{d(c_{\nu},c_{\mu})}\right)\prod_{\nu=\mu+1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{p_{\nu}}{d(c_{\mu},c_{\nu})}\right)

(159)

what, after taking (
92
) into account, ends the proof.
∎

As an example, let us consider the diagram
\lambda=[1,1,\ldots,1]=[1^{\times n}]
. Its step representation is
s=1
,
(k_{1},p_{1})=(n,1)
. Direct application of the formula (
137
) shows that

c([1^{\times n}])=\frac{n}{d-n+1}.

(160)

Now, we are ready to prove the main theorem of this subsection

Theorem 3
.

For every diagram
\lambda
with
n
boxes,

c(\lambda)\leq c([1^{\times n}]).

(161)

Proof.

It was shown in
[
36
]
that

\sum_{j=1}^{s}k_{j}p_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\frac{k_{i}}{d(c_{i},c_{j})}\right)\prod_{i=j+1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{p_{i}}{d(c_{j},c_{i})}\right)=n.

(162)

Observe that
\sum_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}\leq n
and
\sum_{i=j}^{s}p_{i}\geq 1
for every
j=1,2,\ldots,s
. Hence,
d-\sum_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}+\sum_{i=j}^{n}p_{i}\geq d-n+1
. From (
138
) and (
162
) we get

\displaystyle c(\lambda)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d-n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{s}k_{j}p_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\frac{k_{i}}{d(c_{i},c_{j})}\right)\prod_{i=j+1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{p_{i}}{d(c_{j},c_{i})}\right)

(163)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{d-n+1}.

(164)

According to (
160
) the latter number is equal to
c([1^{\times n}])
.
∎

The above theorem clearly shows that the maximum possible value of the constant from (
136
) is attained for antisymmetric partitions with
n=k+1
boxes:

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{d}\max_{\lambda\vdash(k+1)}c(\lambda)=\frac{k+1}{d(d-k)}.

(165)

This exactly reproduces the lower bound obtained from the primal problem and proves optimally of chosen ansatz.

V
Discussions

We have presented the optimal quantum circuits for transforming
k
calls of a
d
-dimensional unitary operation
U_{d}
into its complex conjugate
\overline{U_{d}}
in a deterministic non-exact manner.
When considering probabilistic exact transformations, Ref.
[
29
]
shows that transforming
k
uses of
U_{d}
into its complex conjugation
\overline{U_{d}}
necessarily has probability one or probability zero, and shows that probability one can only be attained when
k\geq d-1
– completely solving the probabilistic exact complex conjugation. Hence, the problem of designing circuits for unitary complex conjugation is solved on the probabilistic exact paradigm and following our work now also on the deterministic non-exact one.

A direct application of our circuit for unitary complex conjugation is to design a quantum circuit for reversing quantum operations.
In Ref.
[
29
]
, the authors present a simple probabilistic circuit which performs exact unitary transposition
U_{d}\mapsto U_{d}^{T}
with probability
1/d^{2}
.
Since
U_{d}^{-1}=\overline{U_{d}}^{T}
, if we perform a unitary transposition protocol after the complex conjugation circuit presented here, we can transform
k
uses of
U_{d}
into its inverse
U_{d}^{-1}
with probability
1/d^{2}
and an average fidelity of
\left\langle F\right\rangle=\frac{k+1}{d(d-k)}
.

As stated earlier, since
\overline{(U_{d}V_{d})}=(\overline{U_{d}})\,(\overline{V_{d}})
, the complex conjugation function
f(U_{d})=\overline{U_{d}}
is a homomorphism. Also, complex conjugation is the only non-trivial homomorphism
f
between
d
-dimensional special unitary operators
f:\mathcal{SU}(d)\to\mathcal{SU}(d)
. This follows from the fact that there exist only three
d-
dimensional representations for the group
\mathcal{SU}(d)

[
34
]
: the trivial representation
f_{\text{trivial}}(U_{d})=\mathds{1}
, the defining representation,
f_{\text{def}}(U_{d})=U_{d}
, and the conjugate representation
f_{\text{conj}}(U_{d})=\overline{U_{d}}
. Hence, we have obtained optimal circuits for transforming
k
uses of
U_{d}
into
f(U_{d})
for all homomorphic functions
f:\mathcal{SU}(d)\to\mathcal{SU}(d)
.

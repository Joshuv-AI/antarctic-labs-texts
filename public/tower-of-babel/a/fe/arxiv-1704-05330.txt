Basile HERLEMONT † and Oleg OGIEVETSKY †‡§

Abstract
We construct the rings of generalized differential operators on the 𝐡 {\bf h} -deformed vector space of 𝐠𝐥 {\bf gl} -type. In contrast to the q q -deformed vector space, where the ring of differential operators is unique up to an isomorphism, the general ring of 𝐡 {\bf h} -deformed differential operators Diff 𝐡 , σ ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\bf h},\sigma}(n) is labeled by a rational function σ \sigma in n n variables, satisfying an over-determined system of finite-difference equations. We obtain the general solution of the system and describe some properties of the rings Diff 𝐡 , σ ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\bf h},\sigma}(n) .

Introduction

As the coordinate rings of
q
-deformed vector spaces, the coordinate rings of
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces are defined with the help of a solution of the dynamical Yang–Baxter equation. The coordinate rings of
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces appeared in several contexts. In
[
4
]
it was observed that such coordinate rings generate the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for
{\rm GL}(2)
. These coordinate rings appear in the study of the cotangent bundle to a quantum group
[
1
]
and in the study of zero-modes in the WZNW model
[
1
,
5
,
7
]
.

The coordinate rings of
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces appear naturally in the theory of reduction algebras. The reduction algebras
[
9
,
14
,
17
,
22
]
are designed to study the decompositions of representations of an associative algebra
\mathcal{B}
with respect to its subalgebra
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be the universal enveloping algebra of a reductive Lie algebra
\mathbf{g}
. Let
M
be a
\mathbf{g}
-module and
\mathcal{B}
the universal enveloping algebra of the semi-direct product of
\mathbf{g}
with the abelian Lie algebra formed by
N
copies of
M
. Then the corresponding reduction algebra is precisely the coordinate ring of
N
copies of
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces.

We restrict our attention to the case
\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{gl}(n)
. Let
V
be the tautological
\mathbf{gl}(n)
-module and
V^{*}
its dual. We denote by
V(n,N)
the reduction algebra related to
N
copies of
V
and by
V^{*}(n,N)
the reduction algebra related to
N
copies of
V^{*}
.

In this article we develop the differential calculus on the
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces of
\mathbf{gl}
-type as it is done in
[
19
]
for the

q
-deformed spaces. Formulated differently, we study the consistent, in the sense, explained in Section
3.2.1
, pairings between the rings
V(n,N)
and
V^{*}(n,N^{\prime})
. A consistent pairing allows to construct a flat deformation of the reduction algebra, related to
N
copies of
V
and
N^{\prime}
copies of
V^{*}
. We show that for
N>1
or
N^{\prime}>1
the pairing is essentially unique. However it turns out that for
N=N^{\prime}=1
the result is surprisingly different from that for
q
-deformed vector spaces. The consistency leads to an over-determined system of finite-difference equations for a certain rational function
\sigma
, which we call “potential”, in
n
variables. The solution space
\mathcal{W}
can be described as follows. Let
\mathbb{K}
be the ground ring of characteristic 0 and
\mathbb{K}[t]
the space of univariate polynomials over
\mathbb{K}
. Then
\mathcal{W}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{K}[t]^{n}
modulo the
(n-1)
-dimensional subspace spanned by
n
-tuples
(t^{j},\dots,t^{j})
for
j=0,1,\dots,n-2
. Thus for each
\sigma\in\mathcal{W}
we have a ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}},\sigma}(n)
of generalized
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed differential operators. The polynomial solutions
\sigma
are linear combinations of complete symmetric polynomials; they correspond to the diagonal of
\mathbb{K}[t]^{n}
.
The ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}},\sigma}(n)
admits the action of the so-called Zhelobenko automorphisms if and only if the potential
\sigma
is polynomial.

In Section
2
we give the definition of the coordinate rings of
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces of
\mathbf{gl}
-type.

Section
3
starts with the description of two different known pairings between
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector spaces, that is, two different flat deformations of the reduction algebra related to
V\oplus V^{*}
. The first deformation is the ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(n)
which is the reduction algebra, with respect to
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
, of the classical ring of polynomial differential operators. The second ring is related to the reduction algebra, with respect to
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
, of the algebra
\operatorname{U}(\mathbf{gl}_{n+1})
. These two examples motivate our study. Then, in Section
3
, we formulate the main question and results. We present the system of the finite-difference equations resulting from the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property of the ring of generalized
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed differential operators. We obtain the general solution of the system and establish the existence of the potential. We give a characterization of polynomial potentials. We describe the centers of the rings
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}},\sigma}(n)
and construct an isomorphism between a certain ring of fractions of the ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}},\sigma}(n)
and a certain ring of fractions of the Weyl algebra. We describe a family of the lowest weight representations and calculate the values of central elements on them. We establish the uniqueness of the deformation in the situation when we have several copies of
V
or
V^{*}
.

Section
4
contains the proofs of the statements from Section
3
.

(1.1)

Let
\varepsilon_{j}
,

\operatorname{U}(n)

\varepsilon_{j}\colon\tilde{h}_{i}\mapsto\tilde{h}_{i}+\delta_{i}^{j}

\varepsilon_{j}(p)

. We shall use the finite-difference operators
\Delta_{j}

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}f:=f-f[-\varepsilon_{j}].

We denote by
e_{L}
,
L=0,\dots,n
, the elementary symmetric polynomials in the variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
, and by
e(t)
the generating function of the polynomials
e_{L}
,

\displaystyle e_{L}=\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{L}}\tilde{h}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tilde{h}_{i_{L}},\qquad e(t)=\sum_{L=0}^{n}e_{L}t^{L}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(1+\tilde{h}_{i}t\big).

We denote by
\operatorname{R}\in\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)}\big(\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)^{n}\otimes_{\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)^{n}\big)
the standard solution of the dynamical Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b,u}{\operatorname{R}}^{ij}_{ab}\operatorname{R}^{bk}_{ur}[-\varepsilon_{a}]\operatorname{R}^{au}_{mn}=\sum_{a,b,u}\operatorname{R}^{jk}_{ab}[-\varepsilon_{i}]\operatorname{R}^{ia}_{mu}\operatorname{R}^{ub}_{nr}[-\varepsilon_{m}]

of type A. The nonzero components of the operator
\operatorname{R}
are

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}_{ij}^{ij}=\frac{1}{\tilde{h}_{ij}},\qquad i\not=j,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{R}_{ji}^{ij}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\tilde{h}_{ij}^{2}-1}{\tilde{h}_{ij}^{2}},&i<j,\\
1,&i\geq j.\end{cases}

(1.2)

We shall need the following properties of
\operatorname{R}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}^{ij}_{kl}[\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}]=\operatorname{R}^{ij}_{kl},\qquad i,j,k,l=1,\dots,n,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}^{ij}_{kl}=0\qquad\text{if}\quad(i,j)\neq(k,l)\ \text{or}\ (l,k),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}^{2}=\text{Id}.

(1.5)

We denote by
\Psi\in\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)}\big(\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)^{n}\otimes_{\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)^{n}\big)
the dynamical version of the skew inverse of the operator
\operatorname{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}\Psi_{jl}^{ik}\operatorname{R}_{nk}^{ml}[\varepsilon_{m}]=\delta_{n}^{i}\delta_{j}^{m}.

(1.6)

The nonzero components of the operator
\Psi
are, see
[
13
]
,

\displaystyle\Psi^{ij}_{ij}=\operatorname{Q}^{+}_{i}\operatorname{Q}^{-}_{j}\frac{1}{\tilde{h}_{ij}+1},\qquad\Psi^{ij}_{ji}=\begin{cases}1,&i<j,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-1\big)^{2}}{\tilde{h}_{ij}\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-2\big)}},&i>j,\end{cases}

(1.7)

where

\displaystyle\operatorname{Q}^{\pm}_{i}=\frac{\chi_{i}[\pm\varepsilon_{i}]}{\chi_{i}}.

2
Coordinate rings of h-deformed vector spaces

Let
\operatorname{F}(n,N)
be the ring with the generators
x^{i\alpha}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,
\alpha=1,\dots,N
, and
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, with the defining relations

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{i}\tilde{h}_{j}=\tilde{h}_{j}\tilde{h}_{i},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{i}x^{j\alpha}=x^{j\alpha}\big(\tilde{h}_{i}+\delta_{i}^{j}\big),\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,N.

(2.2)

We shall say that an element
f\in\operatorname{F}(n,N)
has an
{\mathbf{h}}(n)
-weight
\omega\in{\mathbf{h}}(n)^{*}
if

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{i}f=f\big(\tilde{h}_{i}+\omega\big(\tilde{h}_{i}\big)\big),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

(2.3)

The ring
\operatorname{U}(n)
is naturally the subring of
\operatorname{F}(n,N)
. Let
\bar{\operatorname{F}}(n,N):=\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)\otimes_{\operatorname{U}(n)}\operatorname{F}(n,N)
.
The coordinate ring
\operatorname{V}(n,N)
of
N
copies of the
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector space is the factor-ring of
\bar{\operatorname{F}}(n,N)
by the relations

\displaystyle x^{i\alpha}x^{j\beta}=\sum_{k,l}\operatorname{R}^{ij}_{kl}x^{k\beta}x^{l\alpha},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n,\quad\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,N.

(2.4)

The ring
\operatorname{V}(n,N)
is the reduction algebra, with respect to
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
, of the semi-direct product of
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
and the abelian Lie algebra
V\oplus V\oplus\cdots\oplus V
(
N
times) where
V
is the (tautological)
n
-dimensional
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
-module. According to the general theory of reduction algebras
[
9
,
12
,
22
]
,
\operatorname{V}(n,N)
is a free left (or right)
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
-module; the ring
\operatorname{V}(n,N)
has the following Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property:

given an arbitrary order on the set
x^{i\alpha}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,
\alpha=1,\dots,N
, the set

of all ordered monomials in
x^{i\alpha}
is a basis of the left
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
-module
\operatorname{V}(n,N)
.

(2.5)

Moreover, if
\{\operatorname{R}_{ij}^{kl}\}_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}
is an arbitrary array of functions in
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, then the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property of the algebra defined by the relations (
2.4
), together with the weight prescriptions (
2.2
), implies that
\operatorname{R}
satisfies the dynamical Yang–Baxter equation when
N\geq 3
.

Similarly, let
\operatorname{F}^{*}(n,N)
be the ring with the generators
\bar{\partial}_{i\alpha}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,
\alpha=1,\dots,N
, and
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, with the defining relations (
2.1
) and

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j\alpha}=\bar{\partial}_{j\alpha}\big(\tilde{h}_{i}-\delta_{i}^{j}\big),\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,N.

(2.6)

Let
\bar{\operatorname{F}}^{*}(n,N):=\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)\otimes_{\operatorname{U}(n)}\operatorname{F}^{*}(n,N)
. The
{\mathbf{h}}(n)
-weights are defined by the same equation (
2.3
).
The coordinate ring
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n,N)
of
N
copies of the “dual”
{\mathbf{h}}
-deformed vector space is the factor-ring of
\bar{\operatorname{F}}^{*}(n,N)
by the relations

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{l\alpha}\bar{\partial}_{k\beta}=\sum_{i,j}\bar{\partial}_{j\beta}\bar{\partial}_{i\alpha}\operatorname{R}^{ij}_{kl},\qquad k,l=1,\dots,n,\quad\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,N.

(2.7)

Again, the ring
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n,N)
is the reduction algebra, with respect to
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
, of the semi-direct product of
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
and the abelian Lie algebra
V^{*}\oplus V^{*}\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{*}
(
N
times) where
V^{*}
is the
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
-module, dual to
V
. The ring
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n,N)
is a free left (or right)
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
-module; it has a similar to
\operatorname{V}(n,N)
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property:

given an arbitrary order on the set
\bar{\partial}_{i\alpha}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,
\alpha=1,\dots,N
, the set

of all ordered monomials in
\bar{\partial}_{i\alpha}
is a basis of the left
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
-module
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n,N)
.

(2.8)

Again, the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property of the algebra defined by the relations (
2.7
), together with the weight prescriptions (
2.6
), implies that
\operatorname{R}
satisfies the dynamical Yang–Baxter equation when
N\geq 3
.

For
N=1
we shall write
\operatorname{V}(n)
and
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n)
instead of
\operatorname{V}(n,1)
and
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n,1)
.

Generalized rings of h-deformed differential operators

3.1
Two examples

Before presenting the main question we consider two examples.

1.
We denote by
\text{W}_{n}
the algebra of polynomial differential operators in
n

X^{j}
,

,

, and the defining relations

\displaystyle X^{i}X^{j}=X^{j}X^{i},\qquad D_{i}D_{j}=D_{j}D_{i},\qquad D_{i}X^{j}=\delta_{i}^{j}+X^{j}D_{i},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n.

The map, defined on the set
\{e_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{n}
of the standard generators of
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
by

\displaystyle e_{ij}\mapsto X^{i}D_{j},

extends to a homomorphism
\operatorname{U}({\mathbf{gl}}_{n})\to\text{W}_{n}
. The reduction algebra of
\text{W}_{n}\otimes\operatorname{U}({\mathbf{gl}}_{n})
with respect to the diagonal embedding
of
\operatorname{U}({\mathbf{gl}}_{n})
was denoted by
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(n)
in
[

]
. It is generated, over
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
, by the images

and
\partial_{i}
,

i=1,\dots,n
, of the generators

and

. Let

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{i}:=\partial_{i}\frac{\psi_{i}}{\psi_{i}[-\varepsilon_{i}]},

where the elements

). Then

\displaystyle x^{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}=\sum_{k,l}\bar{\partial}_{k}\operatorname{R}_{lj}^{ki}x^{l}-\delta_{j}^{i}\sigma_{i}^{(\text{Dif\/f})},

(3.1)

where
\sigma_{i}^{(\text{Dif\/f})}=1
,

. The
{\mathbf{h}}(n)
-weights of the generators are given by (
2.2
) and (

). Moreover, the set of the defining relations, over
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
, for the generators

and

,

, consists of (
2.4
), (
2.7
) (with

) and (
3.1
) (see
[

The algebra
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(n,N)

2.
Identifying each

matrix

3.2
Main question and results

3.2.1
Main question

Both rings,
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(n)
and
\overline{{\rm AZ}}_{n}
satisfy the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property. The only difference between these rings is in the form of the zero-order terms
\sigma_{i}^{(\text{Dif\/f})}
and
\sigma_{i}^{(\overline{{\rm AZ}})}
in the cross-commutation relations (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) (compare to the ring of
q
-differential operators
[
19
]
where the zero-order term is essentially – up to redefinitions – unique). It is therefore natural to investigate possible generalizations of the rings
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(n)
and
\overline{{\rm AZ}}_{n}
. More precisely, given
n
elements
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}
of
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
, we let
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be the ring, over
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
, with the generators
x^{i}
and
\bar{\partial}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, subject to the defining relations (
2.4
), (
2.7
) (with
N=1
) and the oscillator-like relations

\displaystyle x^{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}=\sum_{k,l}\bar{\partial}_{k}\operatorname{R}_{lj}^{ki}x^{l}-\delta_{j}^{i}\sigma_{i}.

(3.5)

The weight prescriptions for the generators are given by (
2.2
) and (
2.6
). The diagonal form of the zero-order term (the Kronecker symbol
\delta_{j}^{i}
in the right hand side of (
3.5
)) is dictated by the
{\mathbf{h}}(n)
-weight considerations.

We shall study conditions under which the ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
satisfies the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property. More specifically, since the rings
\operatorname{V}(n)
and
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n)
both satisfy the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property, our aim is to study conditions under which
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
is isomorphic, as a
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
-module, to
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n)\otimes_{\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)}\operatorname{V}(n)
.

The assignment

\displaystyle\deg\big(x^{i}\big)=\deg\big(\bar{\partial}_{i}\big)=1,\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

(3.6)

defines the structure of a filtered algebra on
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
. The associated graded algebra is the homogeneous algebra
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(0,\dots,0)
. This homogeneous algebra has the desired Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property because it is the reduction algebra, with respect to
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
, of the semi-direct product of
{\mathbf{gl}}_{n}
and the abelian Lie algebra
V\oplus V^{*}
.

The standard argument shows that the ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
can be viewed as a deformation of the homogeneous ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(0,\dots,0)
: for the generating set
\big\{x^{\prime i},\bar{\partial}_{i}\big\}
, where
x^{\prime i}=\hbar x^{i}
, all defining relations are the same except (
3.5
) in which
\sigma_{i}
gets replaced by
\hbar\sigma_{i}
; one can consider
\hbar
as the deformation parameter. Thus our aim is to study the conditions under which this deformation is flat.

3.2.2
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property

It turns out that the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property is equivalent to the system of finite-difference equations for the elements
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
satisfies the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property if and only if the elements
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
satisfy the following linear system of finite-difference equations

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{ij}\Delta_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n.

(3.7)

We postpone the proof to Section
4.1
.

3.2.3
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
-system

The system (
3.7
) is closely related to the following linear system of finite-difference equations for one element
\sigma\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}\sigma\big)=0,\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n.

(3.8)

We shall call it the “
\Delta
-system”. The
\Delta
-system can be written in the form

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{ij}\Delta_{j}\Delta_{i}\sigma=\Delta_{i}\sigma-\Delta_{j}\sigma,\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n.

We describe the most general solution of the system (
3.8
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, be the vector space of the elements of
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\big(\tilde{h}_{j}\big)}{\chi_{j}}\,\qquad\text{where $\pi\big(\tilde{h}_{j}\big)$ is a univariate polynomial in $\tilde{h}_{j}$},

and
\chi_{j}
is defined in (
1.1
). Let
\mathcal{W}
be the sum of the vector spaces
\mathcal{W}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
.

Theorem 3.3
.

An element
\sigma\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
satisfies the system (
3.8
) if and only if
\sigma\in\mathcal{W}
.

The proof is in Section
4.2
.

The sum
\sum\mathcal{W}_{j}
is not direct.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space formed by linear combinations of the complete symmetric polynomials
H_{L}
,
L=0,1,2,\dots
, in the variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
,

\displaystyle H_{L}=\sum_{i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{L}}\tilde{h}_{i_{1}}\cdots\tilde{h}_{i_{L}}.

Lemma 3.5
.

(i)

Let
L\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\tilde{h}_{j}^{L}}{\chi_{j}}=\begin{cases}0,&L=0,1,\dots,n-2,\\
H_{L-n+1},&L\geq n-1.\end{cases}

(3.9)

(ii)

The space
\mathcal{H}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{W}
. Moreover, an element
\sigma\in\operatorname{U}(n)
satisfies the system (
3.8
) if and only if
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{U}(n).

(3.10)

The symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{n}
acts on the ring
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
and on the space
\mathcal{W}
by permutations of the variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{W}^{\mathbb{S}_{n}},

(3.11)

where
\mathcal{W}^{\,\mathbb{S}_{n}}
denotes the subspace of
\mathbb{S}_{n}
-invariants in
\mathcal{W}
.

(iii)

Select
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then we have a direct sum decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\bigoplus_{k\colon k\neq j}\mathcal{W}_{k}\oplus\mathcal{H}.

(3.12)

The proof is in Section
4.2
.

Let
t
be an auxiliary indeterminate. We have a linear map of vector spaces
\mathbb{K}[t]^{n}\to\mathcal{W}
defined by

\displaystyle(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{n})\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\pi_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{j}\big)}{\chi_{j}}.

It follows from Lemma
3.5
that this map is surjective and its kernel is the vector subspace of
\mathbb{K}[t]^{n}
spanned by
n
-tuples
(t^{j},\dots,t^{j})
for
j=0,1,\dots,n-2
. The image of the diagonal in
\mathbb{K}[t]^{n}
, formed by
n
-tuples
(\pi,\dots,\pi)
, is the space
\mathcal{H}
.

3.2.4
Potential

We shall give a general solution of the system (
3.7
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that the elements
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
satisfy the system (
3.7
). Then there exists an element
\sigma\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}=\Delta_{i}\sigma,\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

We shall call the element
\sigma
the “potential” and write
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}},\sigma}(n)
instead of
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
if

,

.

According to Proposition

, the ring
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}},\sigma}(n)
satisfies the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt property iff the potential

satisfies the
\Delta
-system (

).

In Section
4.4
we give two proofs of Proposition

. In the first proof we directly describe the space of solutions of the system (

). As a by-product of this description we find that the potential exists and moreover belongs to the space

.

The second proof uses a partial information contained in the system (

). Given the existence of a potential, the general solution is then obtained by Theorem

.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space formed by linear combinations of the complete symmetric polynomials

,

, and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{k\colon k\neq 1}\mathcal{W}_{k}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\prime}.

(3.13)

The potential

is defined up to an additive constant, and it will be sometimes useful to uniquely define

by requiring that

The polynomial potentials

can be characterized in different terms. The rings

and

admit the action of Zhelobenko automorphisms

The ring

is a polynomial,

.

In the examples discussed in Section

corresponds to

and the ring

corresponds

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}H_{2}=\tilde{h}_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{h}_{k}-1.

The question of constructing an associative algebra which contains

and whose reduction with respect to

is

for

3.2.6

In

we have described the center of the ring

Let

where

Proposition 3.8
.

(i)

Let

are central in the ring

if and only if the polynomial

(ii)

(3.17)

The solution of the system (

of the form (

3.2.9
Several copies

The coexistence of several copies imposes much more severe restrictions on the flatness of the deformation. Namely, let
\mathcal{L}
be the ring with the generators
x^{i\alpha}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,
\alpha=1,\dots,N^{\prime}
, and
\bar{\partial}_{j\beta}
,
j=1,\dots,n
,
\beta=1,\dots,N
subject to the following defining relations. The
{\mathbf{h}}(n)
-weights of the generators are given by (
2.2
) and (

). The generators
x^{i\alpha}
satisfy the relations (
2.4
). The generators
\bar{\partial}_{j\beta}
satisfy the relations (
2.7
). We impose the general oscillator-like cross-commutation relations, compatible with the

-weights, between the generators

and

\displaystyle x^{i\alpha}\bar{\partial}_{j\beta}=\sum_{k,l}\bar{\partial}_{k\beta}\operatorname{R}_{lj}^{ki}x^{l\alpha}-\delta_{j}^{i}\sigma_{i\alpha\beta},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,n,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,N^{\prime},\quad\beta=1,\dots,N,

with some
\sigma_{i\alpha\beta}\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Assume that at least one of the numbers

and

is bigger than

. Then the ring
\mathcal{L}
has the

\displaystyle\sigma_{i\alpha\beta}=\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\qquad\text{for some}\quad\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb{K}.

(3.20)

The proof is in Section
4.9
.

Making the redefinitions of the generators,
x^{i\alpha}\rightsquigarrow A^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}x^{i\alpha^{\prime}}
and
\bar{\partial}_{i\beta}\rightsquigarrow B_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}\bar{\partial}_{i\beta^{\prime}}
with some

and

we can transform the matrix

to the diagonal form, with the diagonal
(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)
. Therefore, the ring
\mathcal{L}
is formed by several copies of the rings
\operatorname{Dif\/f}_{{\mathbf{h}}}(n)
,
\operatorname{V}(n)
and
\operatorname{V}^{*}(n)
.

R_{{\sf M}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
denotes the subring of
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
consisting of functions with no poles on hyperplanes
\tilde{h}_{ij}+a=0
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
i,j\in{\sf M}
,
j\neq i
. The symbol
N_{{\sf M}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
denotes the subring of
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
consisting of functions which do not depend on variables
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
i\in{\sf M}
. We shall say that an element
f\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
is regular in
\tilde{h}_{j}
if it has no poles on hyperplanes
\tilde{h}_{jm}+a=0
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
m=1,\dots,n
,
m\neq j
.

1. Partial fraction decompositions.
We will use partial fraction decompositions of an element
f\in\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
with respect to a variable
\tilde{h}_{j}
for some given
j
. The partial fraction decomposition of
f
with respect to
\tilde{h}_{j}
is the expression for
f
of the form

\displaystyle f=\mathcal{P}_{j}(f)+\operatorname{reg}_{j}(f),

where the elements
\mathcal{P}_{j}(f)
and
\operatorname{reg}_{j}(f)
have the following meaning. The “regular” part
\operatorname{reg}_{j}(f)
is an element, regular in
\tilde{h}_{j}
. The “principal” in
\tilde{h}_{j}
part
\mathcal{P}_{j}(f)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{j}(f)=\sum_{k\colon k\neq j}\mathcal{P}_{j;k}(f),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{j;k}(f)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\nu_{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}\frac{u_{ka\nu_{a}}}{\big(\tilde{h}_{jk}-a\big)^{\nu_{a}}},

(4.11)

with some elements
u_{ka\nu_{a}}\in N_{j}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
; the sums are finite.

The fact that the ring
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
admits partial fraction decompositions (that is, that the elements
u_{ka\nu_{a}}
and
\operatorname{reg}_{j}(f)
belong to
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
) is a consequence of the formula

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\big(\tilde{h}_{jk}-a\big)\big(\tilde{h}_{jl}-b\big)}=\frac{1}{\big(\tilde{h}_{kl}+a-b\big)}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{h}_{jk}-a}-\frac{1}{\tilde{h}_{jl}-b}\right).

2.
Let
D
be a domain (a commutative algebra without zero divisors) over
\mathbb{K}
. Let
f
be an element of
\text{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
. Set

\displaystyle\text{Y}_{ij}(f):=\Delta_{i}\Delta_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}f\big).

(4.12)

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\text{\rm Y}_{ij}(f)=0
for some
i
and
j
,
i\neq j
, then
f
can be written in the form

\displaystyle f=\frac{A}{\tilde{h}_{ij}}+B,

(4.13)

with some
A,B\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}R_{i,j}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
.

Proof.

We write
f
in the form

\displaystyle f=\frac{A}{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{1}\big)^{\nu_{1}}\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{2}\big)^{\nu_{2}}\cdots\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{M}\big)^{\nu_{M}}}+B,

where
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{M}
,
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{M}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
A,B\in\text{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}R_{i,j}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
and the element
A
is
not divisible by any factor in the denominator. There is nothing to prove if
A=0
. Assume that
A\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle 0=\text{Y}_{ij}(f)=\frac{\tilde{h}_{ij}A}{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{1}\big)^{\nu_{1}}\cdots\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{M}\big)^{\nu_{M}}}-\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-1\big)A[-\varepsilon_{i}]}{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{1}-1\big)^{\nu_{1}}\cdots\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{M}-1\big)^{\nu_{M}}}\!\!\!\!

(4.14)

\displaystyle\hphantom{0 =}{}-\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}+1\big)A[-\varepsilon_{j}]}{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{1}+1\big)^{\nu_{1}}\cdots\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{M}+1\big)^{\nu_{M}}}+\frac{\tilde{h}_{ij}A[-\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}]}{\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{1}\big)^{\nu_{1}}\cdots\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{M}\big)^{\nu_{M}}}+\text{Y}_{ij}(B).

The denominator
\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{M}-1\big)
appears only in the second term in the right hand side of (
4.14
). It has therefore to be compensated by
\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-1\big)
in the numerator. Hence the only allowed value of
a_{M}
is
a_{M}=0
and moreover we have
\nu_{M}=1
. Similarly, the denominator
\big(\tilde{h}_{ij}-a_{1}+1\big)
appears only in the third term in the right hand side of (
4.14
) and has to be compensated by
(\tilde{h}_{ij}+1)
in the numerator. Hence the only allowed value of
a_{1}
is
a_{1}=0
and we have
\nu_{1}=1
. The inequalities
a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{M}
imply that
M=1
and
we obtain the form (
4.13
) of
f
.
∎

3.
Let
f\in\text{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
. We shall analyze the linear system of finite-difference equations

\displaystyle\text{Y}_{ij}(f)=0\qquad\text{for all}\quad i,j=1,\dots,n,

(4.15)

where
\text{Y}_{ij}
are defined in (
4.12
).

First we prove a preliminary result. We recall Definition
3.2
of the vector spaces
\mathcal{W}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
. We select one of the variables
\tilde{h}_{i}
, say,
\tilde{h}_{1}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that an element
f\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
satisfies the system (
4.15
). Then

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j=2}^{n}F_{j}+\vartheta,

(4.16)

where
\vartheta\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\operatorname{U}(n)
and

\displaystyle F_{j}=\frac{u_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{j}\big)}{\chi_{j}}\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{W}_{j}

(4.17)

with some univariate polynomials
u_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{j}\big)
,
j=2,\dots,n
, with coefficients in
D
.

Proof.

Since
\text{Y}_{1m}(f)=0
,
m=2,\dots,n
, Lemma
4.1
implies that the partial fraction decomposition of
f
with respect to
\tilde{h}_{1}
has the form

\displaystyle f=\sum_{m=2}^{n}\;\frac{\beta_{m}}{\tilde{h}_{m1}}+\vartheta,

(4.18)

where
\beta_{m}\in\text{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}N_{1}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
,

m=2,\dots,n
, and
\vartheta\in\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{1}\big]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}N_{1}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
.
Substituting the expression (
4.18
) for
f
into the equation
\text{Y}_{1j}(f)=0
,
j=2,\dots,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\text{Y}_{1j}(f)=\Delta_{1}\Delta_{j}\left(\sum_{m\colon m\neq 1,j}\frac{\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}}{\tilde{h}_{m1}}-\beta_{j}+\tilde{h}_{1j}\vartheta\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{0}{}=\Delta_{1}\Delta_{j}\left(\sum_{m\colon m\neq 1,j}\frac{\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}}{\tilde{h}_{m1}}+\tilde{h}_{1j}\vartheta\right)

(4.19)

\displaystyle\hphantom{0}{}=\sum_{m\colon m\neq 1,j}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}}{\tilde{h}_{m1}}-\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}+1\big)\beta_{m}[-\varepsilon_{j}]}{\tilde{h}_{m1}}-\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}-1\big)\beta_{m}}{\tilde{h}_{m1}+1}+\frac{\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}[-\varepsilon_{j}]}{\tilde{h}_{m1}+1}\right)+\Delta_{1}\Delta_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}\vartheta\big).

We used that
\beta_{m}\in\text{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}N_{1}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
in the third and fourth equalities. For any
m\neq 1,j
, the terms containing the denominator
\tilde{h}_{m1}
in the expression (
4.19
) for
\text{Y}_{1j}(f)
read

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{h}_{m1}}\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}+1\big)\beta_{m}[-\varepsilon_{j}]\big).

Therefore,
\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}+1\big)\beta_{m}[-\varepsilon_{j}]
is divisible, as a polynomial in
\tilde{h}_{1}
, by
\tilde{h}_{m1}
, or, what is the same, the
value of
\tilde{h}_{1j}\beta_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{1j}+1\big)\beta_{m}[-\varepsilon_{j}]
at
\tilde{h}_{1}=\tilde{h}_{m}
is zero. This means that

\displaystyle 0=\tilde{h}_{mj}\beta_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{mj}+1\big)\beta_{m}[-\varepsilon_{j}]=\Delta_{j}\big(\tilde{h}_{mj}\beta_{m}\big).

Therefore, the element
\tilde{h}_{mj}\beta_{m}
does not depend on
\tilde{h}_{j}
for any
j>1
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\beta_{m}=\frac{u_{m}\big(\tilde{h}_{m}\big)}{\prod\limits_{k\colon k\neq 1,m}\tilde{h}_{mk}}

with some univariate polynomial
u_{m}
.

We have proved that the element
f
has the form (
4.16
) where
F_{j}
,
j=2,\dots,n
, are given by (
4.17
) and the element
\vartheta
is regular in
\tilde{h}_{1}
.

A direct calculation shows that for any
j=2,\dots,n
, the element
F_{j}
, given by (
4.17
), is a solution of the linear system (
4.15
). Therefore the regular in
\tilde{h}_{1}
part
\vartheta
by itself satisfies the system
\text{Y}_{ij}(\vartheta)=0
. It is left to analyze the regular part
\vartheta
.

We use induction in
n
. For
n=2
, the element
\vartheta
is, by construction, a polynomial in
\tilde{h}_{1}
and
\tilde{h}_{2}
. This is the induction base. We shall now prove that
\vartheta
is a polynomial, with coefficients in
D
, in all
n
variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
.

For arbitrary
n>2
we have
\vartheta\in\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{1}\big]\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}^{\prime}(n-1)
where we have denoted by
\bar{\operatorname{U}}^{\prime}(n-1)
the subring
N_{1}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
of
\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
consisting of functions not depending on
\tilde{h}_{1}
. Since
\text{Y}_{ij}(\vartheta)=0
for
i,j=2,\dots,n
, we can use the induction hypothesis with
n-1
variables
\tilde{h}_{2},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
over the ring
\text{D}^{\prime}=\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{1}\big]
.

We now select the variable
\tilde{h}_{2}
. It follows from the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\vartheta=\sum_{m\colon m\neq 1,2}\frac{\gamma_{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{h}_{m})}{\prod\limits_{l\colon l\neq 1,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}+\vartheta^{\prime},

(4.20)

where
\gamma_{m}^{\prime}\big(\tilde{h}_{m}\big)
,
m=3,\dots,n
, are univariate polynomials, with coefficients in
\text{D}^{\prime}
, and the element
\vartheta^{\prime}
is a polynomial, with coefficients in
\text{D}^{\prime}
, in the variables
\tilde{h}_{2},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
. We rewrite the equality (
4.20
) in the form

\displaystyle\vartheta=\sum_{m\colon m\neq 1,2}\frac{\gamma_{m}\big(\tilde{h}_{m},\tilde{h}_{1}\big)}{\prod\limits_{l\colon l\neq 1,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}+\vartheta^{\prime},

(4.21)

with some polynomials
\gamma_{m}
,
m=3,\dots,n
, in two variables, with coefficients in
D
; the element
\vartheta^{\prime}
is a polynomial in all variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
with coefficients in
D
.

The equation
\text{Y}_{12}(\vartheta)=0
for
\vartheta
given by (
4.21
) reads

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{m\colon m\neq 1,2}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{12}\gamma_{m}}{\tilde{h}_{m2}\prod\limits_{l\colon l\neq 1,2,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}-\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{12}-1\big)\gamma_{m}[-\varepsilon_{1}]}{\tilde{h}_{m2}\prod\limits_{l\colon l\neq 1,2,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}-\frac{\big(\tilde{h}_{12}+1\big)\gamma_{m}}{\big(\tilde{h}_{m2}+1\big)\prod\limits_{l\colon l\neq 1,2,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hphantom{0=}{}+\frac{\tilde{h}_{12}\gamma_{m}[-\varepsilon_{1}]}{\big(\tilde{h}_{m2}+1\big)\prod\limits_{l\colon l\neq 1,2,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}\right)+\text{Y}_{12}(\vartheta^{\prime}).

(4.22)

The terms containing the denominator
\tilde{h}_{m2}
in (
4.22
) read

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{h}_{m2}\prod_{l\colon l\neq 1,2,m}\tilde{h}_{ml}}\big(\tilde{h}_{12}\gamma_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{12}-1\big)\gamma_{m}[-\varepsilon_{1}]\big).

Therefore, the expression
\tilde{h}_{12}\gamma_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{12}-1\big)\gamma_{m}[-\varepsilon_{1}]
is divisible, as a polynomial in
\tilde{h}_{2}
, by
\tilde{h}_{2m}=\tilde{h}_{2}-\tilde{h}_{m}
, so

\displaystyle 0=\tilde{h}_{1m}\gamma_{m}-\big(\tilde{h}_{1m}-1\big)\gamma_{m}[-\varepsilon_{1}]=\Delta_{1}\big(\tilde{h}_{1m}\gamma_{m}\big).

Thus the product
\tilde{h}_{1m}\gamma_{m}
,
m=3,\dots,n
, does not depend on
\tilde{h}_{1}
. Since
\gamma_{m}
,
m=3,\dots,n
, is a polynomial, this implies that
\gamma_{m}=0
. We conclude that
\vartheta=\vartheta^{\prime}
and is therefore a polynomial in all variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
.
∎

4.
Now we refine the assertion of Lemma
4.2
. We shall, at this stage, obtain the general solution of the system (
4.15
)
in a form which does not exhibit the symmetry with respect to the permutations of the variables
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
.

We recall Definition
3.4
of the vector space
\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

(i)

The general solution of the linear system (
4.15
) for an element
f\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\bar{\operatorname{U}}(n)
has the form

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j=2}^{n}F_{j}+\vartheta,

(4.23)

where
F_{j}\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{W}_{j}
and

\displaystyle\vartheta\in\text{\rm D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{H}.

(4.24)

(ii)

The elements
F_{j}
,
j=2,\dots,n
, and
\vartheta
are uniquely defined.

Proof.

(i) In Lemma
4.2
we have established the decomposition (
4.23
) with
\vartheta\in\text{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\operatorname{U}(n)
.
We now prove the assertion (
4.24
). We first study the case
n=2
. Let
p\in\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{1},\tilde{h}_{2}\big]
be a polynomial such that
\text{Y}_{12}(p)=0
. Since
\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}\big(\tilde{h}_{12}p\big)=0
we have
\Delta_{2}(\tilde{h}_{12}p)\in\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{2}\big]
.

It is a standard fact that the operator
\Delta_{2}
is surjective on
\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{2}\big]
. This can be seen, for example, by noticing that the set

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2}^{\uparrow m}:=\tilde{h}_{2}\big(\tilde{h}_{2}+1\big)\cdots\big(\tilde{h}_{2}+m-1\big),\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

is a basis of
\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{2}\big]
over
D
, and

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}\big(\tilde{h}_{2}^{\uparrow m}\big)=m\tilde{h}_{2}^{\uparrow m-1}.

The surjectivity of
\Delta_{2}
implies that
\Delta_{2}\big(\tilde{h}_{12}p\big)=\Delta_{2}\big(w\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)\big)
for some polynomial
w\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)\in\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{2}\big]
. Then
\Delta_{2}\big(\tilde{h}_{12}p-w\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)\big)=0
so
\tilde{h}_{12}p-w\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)=v\big(\tilde{h}_{1}\big)
for some polynomial
v\big(\tilde{h}_{1}\big)\in\text{D}\big[\tilde{h}_{1}\big]
. Therefore

\displaystyle p=\frac{v\big(\tilde{h}_{1}\big)+w\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)}{\tilde{h}_{12}}=\frac{v\big(\tilde{h}_{1}\big)-v\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)}{\tilde{h}_{12}}+\frac{v\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)+w\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)}{\tilde{h}_{12}}.

Since
p
is a polynomial we must have
w=-v
. Thus

\displaystyle p=\frac{v\big(\tilde{h}_{1}\big)-v\big(\tilde{h}_{2}\big)}{\tilde{h}_{12}},

that is,
p
is a
D
-linear combination of complete symmetric polynomials in
\tilde{h}_{1}
,
\tilde{h}_{2}
.

For arbitrary
n
, our polynomial
\vartheta
is symmetric since, by the above argument, it is symmetric in every pair
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
\tilde{h}_{j}
of variables. Moreover, considered as a polynomial in a pair
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
\tilde{h}_{j}
, it is a
D
-linear combination of complete symmetric polynomials in
\tilde{h}_{i}
,
\tilde{h}_{j}
. It is then immediate that

is a
D
-linear combination of complete symmetric polynomials in
\tilde{h}_{1},\dots,\tilde{h}_{n}
.

To finish the proof of the statement that the formula (
4.23
) gives the general solution of the system (
4.15

,

, in the variables

satisfy the system (
4.15
). Let

be an auxiliary variable and

\displaystyle H(s)=\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}H_{L}s^{L}=\prod_{k}\frac{1}{1-s\tilde{h}_{k}}

(4.25)

be the generating function of the elements
H_{L}
,
L=0,1,\dots
It is sufficient to show that the formal power series (

4.15
). Fix

,

, and let
\zeta_{ij}=\frac{1}{(1-\tilde{h}_{i}s)(1-\tilde{h}_{j}s)}

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{ij}}{\big(1-\tilde{h}_{i}s\big)\big(1-\tilde{h}_{j}s\big)}\right)=\frac{1}{1-\tilde{h}_{j}s}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}_{ij}}{1-\tilde{h}_{i}s}-\frac{\tilde{h}_{ij}-1}{1-\big(\tilde{h}_{i}-1\big)s}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Delta_i \left( \frac{\tilde{h}_{ij}}{\big(1-\tilde{h}_{i} s\big)\big(1-\tilde{h}_{j} s\big)} \right)}{}=\frac{1}{\big(1-\tilde{h}_{i}\tau\big)\big(1-\big(\tilde{h}_{i}-1\big)\tau\big)}

does not depend on

so

\text{Y}_{ij}(H(s))=0
since the factors other than

\tilde{h}_{j}
.

(ii) Finally, the summands in (

in
\tilde{h}_{1}
.
∎

(ii) Assume that

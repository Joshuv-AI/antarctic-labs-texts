Abstract
. In this paper, we study forms of the uncertainty principle suggested by problems in control theory. We obtain a version of the classical Paneah-Logvinenko-Sereda theorem for the annulus. More precisely, we show that a function with spectrum in an annulus of a given thickness can be bounded, in L 2 L^{2} -norm, from above by its restriction to a neighborhood of a GCC set, with constant independent of the radius of the annulus. We apply this result to obtain energy decay rates for damped fractional wave equations, extending the work of Malhi and Stanislavova to both the higher-dimensional and non-periodic setting.

The goal of this paper is to investigate versions of the uncertainty principle suggested by control theory for PDEs. 1.1.
Application to the decay of damped wave equations

Our results above were inspired by the following energy decay rate problem for damped fractional wave equation.

Fix
s>0
and a damping function
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Consider the fractional damped wave equation recently introduced by Malhi and Stanislavova in
[
28
]
.

For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, let
w
satisfy

(1.3)

w_{tt}(x,t)+\gamma(x)w_{t}(x,t)+(-\Delta+1)^{s/2}w(x,t)=0.

The damping force is represented by
\gamma w_{t}
and the fractional Laplacian is defined, for
r\in\mathbb{R}
by

(-\Delta+1)^{r}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{r}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\,d\xi.

Herein, we study the decay rate of the energy of
w
, defined by

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=\|(w(t),w_{t}(t))\|_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(-\Delta+1)^{s/4}w(x,t)|^{2}+|w_{t}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

Standard analysis shows that if
\gamma=0
, then the energy is conserved, i.e. there is no decay. On the other hand, for constant damping
\gamma=c>0
, it can be shown that
E(t)
decays exponentially.

The classical case of
s=2
has been well-studied on bounded domains in the pioneering works of Bardos, Lebeau, Rauch, Taylor, and Phillips
[
3
,
33
]
. Recently, Burq and Joly have extended these results to
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
6
]
, and in particular showed that if
\gamma
is uniformly continuous and satisfies the GCC condition (
1.4
) below, then
E(t)
decays exponentially in
t
. The methods in these works are that of microlocal and semiclassical analysis for which we refer to the book of Zworski
[
37
]
. These techniques, which allow one to handle very general Laplacians, impose regularity constraints on the damping coefficient
\gamma
.

We note two recent works which have, in one dimension, utilized Fourier analysis to prove exponential decay for rough damping
[
12
,
27
]
. Fourier analytic methods have also proved useful in understanding (polynomial, or logarithmic) decay rates of the semi-group under weaker conditions than the GCC
[
1
,
36
]
.

Using Theorem
1
, we will prove a resolvent estimate (Proposition
11
below) for the fractional Laplacian, which then, using the strategy in
[
12
]
, yields a new proof of the Burq-Joly theorem
[
6
]

as a special case, and extends the results of
[
28
]
to higher dimensions and to non-periodic damping.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
\gamma
is a non-negative, bounded, uniformly continuous function. There exists
L>0
and
c>0
such that

(1.4)

\int_{\ell}\gamma(x)dm_{1}(x)\geq c>0

for all line segments

of length

for all

We reiterate that, in the case

, Theorem

was proved by Burq and Joly
[

using semiclassical analysis. Our main goal here was to show how such results follow directly from uncertainty principles. This yields a particularly elementary proof. The compactness methods of

enable one to prove Theorem

Burq and Joly also pose the question of whether the result can be proved without the assumption of uniform continuity. This would follow from a positive answer to Question

\beta>0
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\ell>0
, and
\delta\in(0,\ell)
. There exists
R>0
and
C>0
such that if

(1)

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies that for any ball
B
of radius
R
centred on
\Gamma
,

\beta_{d-k}(B\cap\Gamma)<\beta,\text{ and}

(2)

E
satisfies the
k
-dimensional
(\ell,\gamma)
-GCC,

then for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\operatorname{spec}(f)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\beta}(\Gamma)
,

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}.

In contrast with Theorem
5
, observe that in the conclusion of Theorem
8
, we only control the
L^{2}
-norm of
f
by its norm on a
\delta
-neighborhood of a
k
-GCC set. In the generality that Theorem
8
is stated, one cannot expect
R
and
C
to be bounded independently of
\delta
. This can be seen as a direct consequence of the sharpness of the classical Ingham inequality for non-harmonic trigonometric series
[
17
,
19
]
. The assumption that
\delta\in(0,\ell)
in the statement of Theorem
8
is not restrictive since
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E)\subset\mathcal{U}_{\delta^{\prime}}(E)
for
\delta^{\prime}>\delta
.

Inspecting the proof, one can see that Theorem
8
holds with
C
and
R
both taking the form

(3.1)

C_{1}\Bigl(\frac{\ell}{\delta}\Bigl)^{d+1}\Bigl(\frac{C_{0}}{\gamma}\Bigl)^{C_{0}\ell\beta}

where
C_{0}=C_{0}(k)
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(k,d)
.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

Fix
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, and
\beta\geq 1
. As in the proof of Proposition
6
, we may suppose that
\ell=1
(this means replacing
\delta
by
\delta/\ell\in(0,1)
).

Choose
\xi_{\ell}
to be a maximal
1/2
-separated subset of
\Gamma
. Then we may cover
\mathcal{U}_{\beta}(\Gamma)
by cubes
Q_{\ell}=Q(\xi_{\ell},2)
of sidelength
2
centered on
\xi_{\ell}\in\Gamma
.

Fix
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,1))
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi dm_{d}=1
and
|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|\gtrsim 1
on
Q(0,2)
.

Set
\varphi=\delta^{-d}\psi\bigl(\delta^{-1}\cdot)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,\delta))
so that
\widehat{\varphi}=\widehat{\psi}(\delta\,\cdot\,)
. Then, since
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi dm_{d}=1
,
|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\gtrsim 1
on
Q(0,2)
, and for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\lesssim_{m}\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta^{m}}(1+|\xi|)^{m}}.

Fix
\ell
fixed, consider the function

g_{\ell}=f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{\varphi}(\cdot-\xi_{\ell}),

so that
\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{\ell})}\lesssim\|\widehat{g_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Now split
g_{\ell}=g_{\ell}^{(1)}+g_{\ell}^{(2)}
where
g_{\ell}^{(1)}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\widehat{g_{\ell}}\chi_{\{\xi:|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|\leq R\}}]
. Observe that, for fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.2)

\begin{split}\|g_{\ell}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}&=\int_{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|>R}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\xi_{\ell})|^{2}dm_{d}(\xi)\\
&\lesssim_{m}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta^{-m}}\int_{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|>R}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{1}{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{m}}dm_{d}(\xi).\end{split}

By assumption
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{g_{\ell}^{(1)}}\,)
is contained in the
\beta
-neighborhood of a
(d-k)
-plane, and so Theorem
5
, with
A=\bigl(\frac{C}{\gamma}\bigl)^{C\beta}
,

\|g_{\ell}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim A\|g_{\ell}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(E)}.

Whence, applying (
3.2
) twice, we infer that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\|\widehat{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{\ell})}&\lesssim\|g_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\\
&\lesssim_{m}A\|g_{\ell}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta^{-m}}\int_{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|>R}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{1}{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{m}}dm_{d}(\xi)\\
&\lesssim_{m}A\|g_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}+A{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta^{-m}}\int_{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|>R}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\frac{1}{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{m}}dm_{d}(\xi).\end{split}

We next would like to sum this inequality over
\ell
, using the fact that
\mathcal{U}_{\beta}(\Gamma)\subset\bigcup_{\ell}Q_{\ell}
.

Due to the support property of
\varphi
, we have that
g_{\ell}=(f\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E)})*\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{\varphi}(\cdot-\xi_{\ell})
on
E
. Whence,

\begin{split}\|g_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E)}}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\xi_{\ell})|^{2}dm_{d}(\xi)\\
&\lesssim\delta^{-d-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\widehat{f\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E)}}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{d+1}}dm_{d}(\xi),\end{split}

but,

\sum_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\widehat{f\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E)}}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{d+1}}dm_{d}(\xi)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E)}}|^{2}dm_{d},

and so

(3.3)

\sum_{\ell}\|g_{\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(E)}\lesssim\delta^{-d-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}^{2}.

On the other hand, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for any
k\geq 1
, there can be at most
C2^{kd}R^{d}
of the
1/2
-separated points
\xi_{\ell}
in an annulus
A_{k}(\xi):=B(\xi,2^{k}R)\backslash B(\xi,2^{k-1}R)
. Therefore,

(3.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{\ell}\frac{\chi_{\{\ell:|\xi_{\ell}-\xi|>R\}}(\ell)}{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{m}}&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell\,:\,\xi_{\ell}\in A_{k}(\xi)}\frac{1}{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{m}}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{kd}R^{d}}{2^{(k-1)m}R^{m}}\\
&\lesssim_{m}\frac{1}{R^{m-d}}\text{ provided that }m>d.\end{split}

Consequently, if we set
m=d+1
, then

(3.5)

\sum_{\ell}\int_{\{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|>R\}}\frac{|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}}{|\xi-\xi_{\ell}|^{m}}dm_{d}(\xi)\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Combining (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) results in

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lesssim\frac{A}{\delta^{d+1}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}^{2}+\frac{A}{\delta^{d+1}R}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

If
R
is sufficiently large multiple of
A\delta^{-d-1}
, then the second term on the right hand side can be hidden in the left hand side, and we get

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lesssim\frac{A}{\delta^{d+1}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}^{2}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Fix
\delta>0
. Choose
S
large enough to be able to apply Theorem
8
with
R
replaced by
S
. Since
\Sigma
is a compact
(d-k)
-dimensional

-manifold embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can find a function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with

as

, such that if

, then

. Fix

. Then for any

,

\beta_{d-k}(R\Sigma\cap B(x,S))=R\beta_{d-k}(\Sigma\cap B(x/R,S/R))\leq R\sigma(S/R)\leq\beta.

provided that

to conclude that the desired statement holds for sufficiently large

by covering

.
∎

Finally, Theorem

is an immediate consequence of Theorem

obtained by taking

. Moreover, in this case

, and inserting the explicit form of the parameters in Theorem

.

4.
Decay Rates for Damped Wave Semigroups

We now use Theorem
1
to prove energy decay rates for damped fractional wave equation (Theorem
4
).
Throughout this section, for a function
f
we set
\|f\|:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. For an operator
A
between two normed spaces,
\|A\|
denotes the operator norm.

We view the equation (
1.3
) as the following semigroup. Setting
W(t)=(w(t),w_{t}(t))
,

\dfrac{d}{dt}W(t)=\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}W(t)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}:H^{s}\times H^{s/2}\to{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}
is densely defined by
A_{\gamma}(u_{1},u_{2})=(u_{2},-(-\Delta+1)^{s/2}u_{1}-\gamma u_{2})
.
The Sobolev space
H^{r}
for
r\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by the decay of the Fourier transform:

H^{r}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}:\|u\|_{H^{r}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{r}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<\infty\right\}.

It is easy to verify that
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is skew adjoint. That
W(t)=e^{tA_{\gamma}}
is a strong semigroup of contractions follows by the standard theory
[
31
]
since
A_{\gamma}
is closed with dense range, and for any
U=(u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{s}\times H^{s/2}
,

\re\langle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{*}U,U\rangle_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}=\re\langle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}U,U\rangle_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}

=\re\langle\mathcal{A}_{0}U,U\rangle_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}-\langle\gamma u_{2},u_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\langle\gamma u_{2},u_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq 0.

Notice that
E(t)=\|e^{t\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}}(w(0),w_{t}(0))\|_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}
, so the energy decay rates in Theorem
4
can be rewritten as

\|e^{t\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}^{-1}\|=O(t^{\tfrac{-s}{4-2s}})\quad 0<s<2,

\|e^{t\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}}\|\leq Ce^{-\omega t}\quad s\geq 2.

Once we can establish

(4.1)

\|(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-i\lambda)^{-1}\|\leq C\max\{(|\lambda|+1)^{\tfrac{4}{s}-2},1\},

the decay rates will follow from the following two results from semigroup theory.

Theorem 9

(Gearhart-Prüss Test
[
32
]
)
.

Let
e^{tA}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup in a Hilbert space and assume there exists
M>0
such that
\|e^{tA}\|\leq M
for all
t\geq 0
. Then, there exists
C,\omega>0
such that

\|e^{tA}\|\leq Ce^{-\omega t}

if and only if
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A)
and
\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\|(A-i\lambda)^{-1}\|<\infty
.

For the polynomial decay, we use the following result from
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
:

Theorem 10

(Borichev-Tomilov
[
4
]
)
.

Let
e^{tA}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Hilbert space. Assume there exists
M>0
such that
\|e^{tA}\|\leq M
for all
t\geq 0
and
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A)
. Then for a fixed
\alpha>0
,

\|e^{tA}A^{-1}\|=O(t^{-1/\alpha})\mbox{ as }t\to\infty

if and only if
\|(A-i\lambda)^{-1}\|=O(\lambda^{\alpha})
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

The main step in establishing (
4.1
) is the following resolvent estimate for the fractional Laplacian from sets which satisfy the Geometric Control Condition.

Proposition 11
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy the GCC and
\delta>0
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\|f\|^{2}\leq C(1+\lambda)^{\tfrac{2}{s}-2}\|((-\Delta+1)^{s/2}-\lambda)f\|^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}^{2}

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\lambda\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, consider the annulus
A_{\mu}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bigr||\xi|-\mu\bigr|\leq 1\}
for
\mu\geq 0
. Appealing to Corollary
1
, we find a constant
C>0
(independent of
\mu
) such that

(4.2)

\|f\|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}\mbox{ whenever }\operatorname{spec}f\subset A_{\mu}.

Now, define the Fourier restriction
\widehat{P_{\lambda}f}:=\chi_{\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}}\widehat{f}
where

\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{1/2}-\lambda^{1/s}|\leq 1/2\}.

Then, for
\lambda\geq 3^{s}
,
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}\subset A_{\lambda^{1/s}}
so for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\begin{split}\|f\|^{2}&\leq\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|^{2}+C\|P_{\lambda}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}\\
&\leq(2C+1)\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|^{2}+2C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}.\end{split}

Finally, using the fact that
|\tau^{s}-\lambda|\geq c(1+\lambda)^{1-\tfrac{1}{s}}
if
|\tau-\lambda^{1/s}|\geq 1
(see Lemma 1 in
[
12
]
), we have

\begin{split}\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|^{2}&=\int_{\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}^{c}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\\
&\leq C(1+\lambda)^{\tfrac{2}{s}-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{s/2}-\lambda\right]^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

To prove (
4.1
) one can follow the strategy from
[
12
]
which we briefly outline. First, the parallelogram identity and the positivity of
(-\Delta+1)^{s/2}
yields

(4.3)

c\|U\|^{2}_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}\leq(1+|\lambda|)^{\tfrac{4}{s}-2}\|(\mathcal{A}_{0}-i\lambda)U\|^{2}_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}^{2}

from Proposition
11
with
s
replaced by
s/2
. To get the desired estimate (
4.1
), notice first that since
\gamma
is bounded, for any
\varepsilon>0

\int_{\ell}\gamma(x)dm_{1}(x)\leq\|\gamma\|_{\infty}m_{1}(\{\gamma\geq\varepsilon\}\cap\ell)+\varepsilon L

all line segments
\ell
of length
L
. Thus, if
\gamma
satisfies (
1.4
), then for
\varepsilon
small enough,
\{\gamma\geq\varepsilon\}
satisfies the GCC. Moreover, since
\gamma
is uniformly continuous, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\gamma(y)|\geq\varepsilon/2
for
y
in a
\delta
-neighborhood of
\{\gamma\geq\varepsilon\}
. Thus, taking
E=\{\gamma\geq\varepsilon\}
,

\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{U}_{\delta}(E))}\leq 2{\varepsilon}^{-1}\|\gamma u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Finally, using the triangle inequality and the fact that

\|\gamma u_{2}\|^{2}\leq C\langle\gamma u,u\rangle=-C\re\langle(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-i\lambda U,U\rangle_{{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}}

\leq\eta^{-1}\|(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-i\lambda)U\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\|U\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}^{2}

for any
\eta>0
, we have

\displaystyle c\|U

\displaystyle\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}^{2}\leq(1+|\lambda|)^{\tfrac{4}{s}-2}\|(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-i\lambda)U\|^{2}_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}+((1+|\lambda|)^{\tfrac{4}{s}-2}+4\varepsilon^{-2})\|\gamma u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\{(1+|\lambda|)^{\tfrac{4}{s}-2},(1+|\lambda|)^{\tfrac{8}{s}-4},1\}\|(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-i\lambda)U\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}^{2}+c/2\|U\|^{2}_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}

by choosing
\eta
appropriately. This gives the desired estimate (
4.1
).

The converse is a consequence of only the exponential decay case, so we fix
s=2
. By the Gearhart-Pruss Test, one has the resolvent estimate

\|U\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}\leq C\|(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}-i\lambda)U\|_{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
U=(u_{1},u_{2})\in{H^{s/2}\times L^{2}}
. Taking
U=((-\Delta+1)^{-s/4}u,iu)
for some
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, this implies

c\|u\|^{2}\leq\|((-\Delta+1)^{s/4}-\lambda)u\|^{2}+\|\gamma u\|^{2}.

Now fix
\varkappa>0
to be small, and set
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\bigr|(|\xi|^{2}+1)-\lambda\bigr|\leq\varkappa\}
, if
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{u}\subset\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}
, then

\|((-\Delta+1)^{1/2}-\lambda)u\|\leq\varkappa\|u\|.

Consequently, if
\varkappa
is small enough, then there exists
c>0
such that for every
\lambda>0
,
c\|u\|\leq\|\gamma u\|
whenever
\operatorname{spec}(u)\subset\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}
. Since this inequality does not see modulation, the conclusion also holds with
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}
replaced by
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}+\xi
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Next, we notice that inside
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}
, one can fit a rectangle with side lengths
c_{0}\varkappa\times c_{0}\lambda^{1/2}\times\ldots\times c_{0}\lambda^{1/2}
for some constant
c_{0}>0
. Letting
\lambda\to\infty
, we therefore see that
c\|u\|\leq\|\gamma u\|
whenever
\beta_{d-1}(\operatorname{spec}(u))\leq c_{0}\varkappa
. But now, insofar as
\gamma
is bounded, we find
\varepsilon>0
such that
c\|u\|\leq\|\chi_{\{|\gamma|>2\varepsilon\}}u\|
whenever
\beta_{d-1}(\operatorname{spec}(u))\leq c_{0}\varkappa
. Employing Remark
7
(with
\beta=c_{0}\varkappa
), we see that the set
\{\gamma>\varepsilon\}
satisfies the
1
-GCC, from which we conclude that
\gamma
satisfies (
1.4
).

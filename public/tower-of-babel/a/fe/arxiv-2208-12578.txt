Surfaces of coordinate finite type in the Lorentz-Minkowski 3-space
Hassan Al-Zoubi, Alev Kelleci, Tareq Hamadneh

Abstract
. In this article, we study the class of surfaces of revolution in the 3-dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski space with nonvanishing Gauss curvature whose position vector 𝒙 \boldsymbol{x} satisfies the condition Δ I ​ I ​ I ​ 𝒙 = A ​ 𝒙 \Delta^{III}\boldsymbol{x}=A\boldsymbol{x} , where A A is a square matrix of order 3 and Δ I ​ I ​ I \Delta^{III} denotes the Laplace operator of the second fundamental form I ​ I ​ I III of the surface. We show that such surfaces are either minimal or pseudospheres of a real or imaginary radius.

2.
Basic concepts

Let

be a curve in a plane

-axis as the axis of revolution. If the axis is null, then we may assume that this axis is the line spanned by the vector (0,1,1) of the

We consider the axis of revolution is the
x
-axis (spacelike) and the curve
C
is lying in the
xy
-plane. Then a parameterization of
C
with respect to its arclength is
\boldsymbol{r}(s)=(f(s),g(s),0)
where

are smooth functions. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f(s)>0,s\in(a,b)
. A surface of revolution
M^{2}
in
E_{1}^{3}
in a system of local curvilinear coordinates
(s,\theta)
is given by:

\boldsymbol{x}(s,\theta)=\big(f(s)\cosh\theta,g(s),f(s)\sinh\theta\big).

(2.1)

or

\boldsymbol{x}(s,\theta)=\big(f(s)\sinh\theta,g(s),f(s)\cosh\theta\big).

In the case that the axis of revolution is the
z
-axis (timelike) and the curve
C
is given
\boldsymbol{r}(s)=(f(s),0,g(s))
and lies in the
xz
-plane, the surface of revolution
M^{2}
is given by:

\boldsymbol{x}(s,\theta)=\big(f(s)\cos\theta,f(s)\sin\theta,g(s)\big).

(2.2)

Finally, if the axis of revolution is the line spanned by the vector (0, 1, 1) and the curve
C
lies in the
yz
-plane, then the surface of revolution
M^{2}
can be parametrized as

\boldsymbol{x}(s,\theta)=\big(\theta h(s),g(s)+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}h(s),f(s)+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}h(s)\big),

(2.3)

where
h(s)=f(s)-g(s)\neq 0
.

We denote by
g=g_{km},b=b_{km}
and

the first, second and third fundamental forms of
M^{2}
respectively, where we put

g_{11}=E=<\boldsymbol{x_{s}},\boldsymbol{x_{s}}>,\ \ \ g_{12}=F=<\boldsymbol{x_{s}},\boldsymbol{x_{\theta}}>,\ \ \ g_{22}=G=<\boldsymbol{x_{\theta}},\boldsymbol{x_{\theta}}>,

b_{11}=L=<\boldsymbol{x_{ss}},\boldsymbol{n}>,\ \ \ b_{12}=M=<\boldsymbol{x_{s\theta}},\boldsymbol{n}>,\ \ \ b_{22}=N=<\boldsymbol{x_{\theta\theta}},\boldsymbol{n}>,

e_{11}=\frac{EM^{2}-2FLM+GL^{2}}{EG-F^{2}}=<\boldsymbol{n_{s}},\boldsymbol{n_{s}}>,

e_{12}=\frac{EMN-FLN+GLM-FM^{2}}{EG-F^{2}}=<\boldsymbol{n_{s}},\boldsymbol{n_{\theta}}>,

e_{22}=\frac{GM^{2}-2FNM+EN^{2}}{EG-F^{2}}=<\boldsymbol{n_{\theta}},\boldsymbol{n_{\theta}}>,

which are the coefficients of the first, second, third fundamental form respectively, and
<,>
is the Lorentzian metric.

For a sufficient differentiable function

on
M^{2}
The second Laplace operator according to the fundamental form

4
]

\Delta^{III}p=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}(\sqrt{e}e^{km}p_{/k})_{/m}.

where

denote the components of the inverse tensor of

After a long computation, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Delta^{III}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{\mid EG-F^{2}\mid}}{LN-M^{2}}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{(GM^{2}-2FNM+EN^{2})\frac{\partial p}{\partial s}}{(LN-M^{2})\sqrt{\mid EG-F^{2}\mid}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\frac{(EMN-FLN+GLM-FM^{2})\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}}{(LN-M^{2})\sqrt{\mid EG-F^{2}\mid}}\bigg)_{s}

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{(EMN-FLN+GLM-FM^{2})\frac{\partial p}{\partial s}}{(LN-M^{2})\sqrt{\mid EG-F^{2}\mid}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(EM^{2}-2FLM+GL^{2})\frac{\partial p}{\partial\theta}}{(LN-M^{2})\sqrt{\mid EG-F^{2}\mid}}\bigg)_{\theta}\bigg).

Here we have

Dbar-approach to coupled nonlocal NLS equation and general nonlocal Reduction
Xueru Wang, Junyi Zhu

Abstract
The coupled nonlocal NLS equation is studied by virtue of the 2 × 2 2\times 2 Dbar-problem. Two spectral transform matrices are introduced to define two associated Dbar-problems. The relations between the coupled nonlocal NLS potential and the solution of the Dbar-problem are constructed. The spatial transform method is extended to obtain the coupled nonlocal NLS equation and its conservation laws. The general nonlocal reduction of the coupled nonlocal NLS equation to the nonlocal NLS equation is discussed in detail. The explicit solutions are derived. Keywords: nonlocal NLS equation; Dbar-problem; dressing method; general nonlocal reduction.

2
Double Dbar-problems

Consider the first local Dbar-problem

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\psi(k)=\psi(k)R(k),

(2.1)

with the normalization condition

\displaystyle\psi(k)\rightarrow I,\quad k\rightarrow\infty,

(2.2)

where

The Dbar-problem (

2.2

\displaystyle\psi(k)=I+\psi(k)R(k)C_{k},

\displaystyle\psi(k)R(k)C_{k}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\int\frac{dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{z-k}\psi(k)R(k).

(2.4)

The aim of dressing method is construct the relation between the cnNLS potential and the solution of the Dbar-problem. To this end, a good way is to construct the cnNLS equation and its Lax pair from the Dbar-problem.
It is noted that the Dbar-problem is defined in the spectral space, while the cnNLS equation is in the physical space. Thus we need to introduce the physical variables

\displaystyle R_{x}(k;x,t)=-ik[\sigma_{3},R(k;x,t)],

(2.5)

\displaystyle R_{t}(k;x,t)=2ik^{2}[\sigma_{3},R(k;x,t)].

(2.6)

We note that the solution of the system (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) is not unique.

Under the dressing procedure

36
]

\displaystyle\psi_{x}(k;x,t)=-ik[\sigma_{3},\psi(k;x,t)]+Q(x,t)\psi(k;x,t),

\displaystyle Q(x,t)=-i[\sigma_{3},\langle\psi(k;x,t)R(k;x,t)\rangle],

and

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(k;x,t)=

\displaystyle 2ik^{2}[\sigma_{3},\psi(k;x,t)]-2kQ(x,t)\psi(k;x,t)

(2.8)

\displaystyle+i\sigma_{3}[Q^{2}(x,t)-Q_{x}(x,t)]\psi(k;x,t).

where

\displaystyle\langle\psi(k;x,t)R(k;x,t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\int\psi(k;x,t)R(k;x,t)dk\wedge d\bar{k}.

For the cnNLS equation, we need to consider the second local Dbar problem

\displaystyle\hat{Q}(x,t)=-i[\sigma_{3},\langle\hat{\psi}(k;x,t)\hat{R}(k;x,t)\rangle],

(2.12)

and the another linear spectral system

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{x}(k;x,t)=-ik[\sigma_{3},\hat{\psi}(k;x,t)]+\hat{Q}(x,t)\hat{\psi}(k;x,t),

(2.13)

\displaystyle 2ik^{2}[\sigma_{3},\hat{\psi}(k;x,t)]-2k\hat{Q}(x,t)\hat{\psi}(k;x,t)

Conclusions

In this paper, we extended the Dbar-problem to discuss the cnNLS equation. In this approach, we introduced two spectral transform matrices to define two Dbar-problems. The general nonlocal relations between the cnNLS potential and the solutions of the Dbar-problems were established by Dbar-dressing method. Two sets of Dbar data were used to construct the special explicit solution of the focusing cnNLS equation. By using the Cauchy-Binet formula, We expanded the determinant solution, which was used to discuss nonlocal reductions of the cnNLS equation in detail.

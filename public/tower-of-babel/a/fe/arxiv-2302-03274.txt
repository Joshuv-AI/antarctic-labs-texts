1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background & literature review

In nature, most living cells or organisms (e.g.
Dictyostelium
,
Bacillus subtilis
,
Escherichia coli
, etc.) are endowed with the ability to sense certain stimulating chemical signals (e.g. nutrients) in the environment (mostly the viscous fluid) and adapt their movements accordingly. A significant number of experiments and analytical studies have revealed noticeable facts that organisms and the complex surrounding fluid may substantially affect the motion of them each other through e.g. chemotactic forces and gravitational forces by organisms, buoyant forces by fluid, etc. For example, to describe this kind of cell-fluid interactions mathematically, Tuval et al.
[
21
]
conducted a highly influential experiment in a water drop suspended with swimming
Bacillus subtilis
, and proposed the following chemotaxis-fluid model

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}n+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla n&=\lambda\Delta n-\nabla\cdot\big(\chi(c)n\nabla c\big),\\
\partial_{t}c+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c&=\nu\Delta c-nf(c),\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\kappa(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla P&=\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}-n\nabla\phi,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=0,\qquad\qquad x\in\Omega,\,\,\,t>0.\end{split}\right.

Here the time evolution of cell density
n=n(x,t)
is described by (
1.1
)
1
, in which the chemotaxis-induced aggregation with chemotactic sensitivity
\chi
, diffusion with strength
\lambda
caused by random Brownian motion, and transportation by ambient fluid
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}(x,t)
subjected to incompressible (Navier-)Stokes equation with pressure
P=P(x,t)
are considered. The signal with concentration
c=c(x,t)
also diffuses with strength
\nu
, is transported by ambient fluid and is consumed by cells with consumption rate
f
. The external force
-n\nabla\phi
exerted on the fluid by cells can be produced by various physical mechanisms, e.g. gravity, centrifugal, electric forces, magnetic forces, etc. We refer to
[
17
,
21
]
for more details concerning its physical background and
[
2
]
for a different derivation (under the framework of the kinetic theory) of chemotaxis-fluid models.

The fundamental mathematical challenges arising in the analysis of (
1.1
) appear to consist in two parts, i.e. one is the nonlinear cross-diffusion term
-\nabla\cdot\big(\chi(c)n\nabla c\big)
which may enforce blow-up solutions (see
[
3
]
and references therein), and the other one arises from the well-known incompleteness theory of existence and regularities with large initial data for the Navier-Stokes system. Since the seminal analytical work
[
17
]
proved the local existence of weak solutions, there have been plenty of scholars devoting themselves to the well-posedness and long-time behavior of solutions to (
1.1
). For instance, Duan et al.
[
9
]
proved the global-in-time existence and explicit decay rates of classical solutions for the chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes system near constant state
(n_{\infty},0,\mathbf{0})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and also established global-in-time existence of weak solutions for the chemotaxis-Stokes system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
provided that the external forcing
\phi
is weak or the signal concentration
c
is small, i.e.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\phi\geq 0,\,\,\nabla\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\omega(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|+\omega^{2}(x)|\nabla^{2}\phi(x)|\right)\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\|c_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\,\,\textrm{are small},

or

(1.3)

\phi\geq 0,\,\nabla\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\omega(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|+\omega^{2}(x)|\nabla^{2}\phi(x)|\right)<\infty,\,\,\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\,\,\textrm{is small},

where
\omega(x)=(1+|x|)(1+\ln(1+|x|))
. In
[
16
]
, Liu-Lorz got rid of the decay of the potential
\phi
at infinity and the smallness of
c_{0}
in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) by making technical assumptions on
\chi
and
f
:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\nabla\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\chi,\,\chi^{\prime},\,f,\,f^{\prime}\geq 0,\quad\frac{d^{2}}{dc^{2}}\left(\frac{f(c)}{\chi(c)}\right)<0,\quad\frac{\chi^{\prime}f+\chi f^{\prime}}{\chi}>0,

and obtained the global existence of weak solutions even for the full chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes system. Then Chae et al.
[
7
]
presented some blow-up criteria for the local solutions to the chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes system in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d=2,3)
, and proved the global existence of classical solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
on quite different assumption from (
1.4
) that for some constant
\mu

(1.5)

\displaystyle\phi,\,\chi,\,\chi^{\prime},\,f,\,f^{\prime}\geq 0,\quad\sup_{c}|\chi(c)-\mu f(c)|<\varepsilon\,\,\,\,\textrm{for a sufficiently small}\,\,\varepsilon>0.

In particular, if the last condition on
\chi
and
f
in (
1.5
) is replaced by
\chi(c)-\mu f(c)=0
, then the global existence of weak solutions was also showed in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in
[
7
]
. In
[
8
]
, they further established the global existence of classical solutions and explicit temporal decay rates under the smallness assumptions on initial data. For more rigorous analytical results for the Cauchy problems of the coupled chemotaxis-fluid system (
1.1
), we refer to
[
11
,
15
,
29
,
10
]
and references therein. Additionally, as far as the physical domain is concerned, various types of initial-boundary value problems (in bounded domains or unbounded domains with finite depth) for system (
1.1
) are extensively studied (see e.g.
[
25
,
26
,
23
,
18
,
19
]
and references therein). We also remark that apart from the model (
1.1
) itself, a large amount of significant variants acting as an additional regularizing mechanism for preventing possible blow-up solutions have been widely studied as well. For instance, taking the nonlinearly enhanced cell diffusion at large densities into consideration by replacing
\Delta n
in (
1.1
)
1
with porous medium-type
\Delta n^{m}
for
m>1
, we refer to
[
12
,
11
,
20
,
16
]
for related Cauchy problems and
[
13

20
,
28
]
for initial-boundary value problems.

While system (
1.1
) and its relative variants presented above have been well studied and understood numerically (
[
21
,
5

14
]
) and analytically, Lorz
[
17
]
and Di Francesco et al.
[

pointed out that it could be more realistic to include both the effect of gravity (potential force) on cells and the effect of the chemotactic force on fluid, i.e. they extended the system (
1.1
) to the following self-consistent version

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}n+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla n&=\Delta n^{m}-\nabla\cdot\big(\chi(c)n\nabla c\big)+\nabla\cdot(n\nabla\phi),\\
\partial_{t}c+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c&=\Delta c-nf(c),\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\kappa(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla P&=\Delta\mathbf{u}-n\nabla\phi+\chi(c)n\nabla c,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=0,\qquad\qquad x\in\Omega,\,\,\,t>0.\end{split}\right.

The reasoning behind the coupling
\chi(c)n\nabla c
in equation (
1.6
)
3
is that the fluid will exert frictional force on the moving cells to make the cells move without acceleration and thus that the reaction forces act on the fluid, which also matches the nonlinear cross-diffusion term in the cell density in equation (
1.6
)
1
. We would like to remark that the similar forcing
n\nabla c
was also appeared in the coupling Nernst-Planck-Navier-Stokes system (see
[
6
]
and references therein).

In contrast to the large amount of existing works on system (
1.1
) and its variants, the researches on the well-posedness of system (
1.6
) are far fewer. When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain, Di Francesco et al.
[
12
]
proved the existence of global weak solution to the no-flux/no-flux/no-slip boundary value problem of system (
1.6
) with
\kappa=0
and
\frac{3}{2}<m\leq 2
, which was extended to
m>1
by Yu
[
27
]
for
\kappa=0
again and by Wang
[
22
]
for general
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, respectively. In the three-dimensional setting, the similar global existence was also obtained in case
\kappa=0
and
m>\frac{4}{3}
by
[
24
]
. To the best of our knowledge, the only available result for the linear diffusion case
m=1
is due to Lorz
[
17
]
, where the local existence of weak solutions to system (
1.6
) with
\kappa=0
in a planar bounded domain was established.

1.2.
Main results

Motivated by above works, we concern in this paper with the Cauchy problem for the self-consistent chemotaxis-(Navier-)Stokes model with linear cell diffusion

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}n+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla n&=\Delta n-\nabla\cdot\left(\chi(c)n\nabla c\right)+\nabla\cdot(n\nabla\phi),\\
\partial_{t}c+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c&=\Delta c-nf(c),\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}+\kappa(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla P&=\Delta\mathbf{u}-n\nabla\phi+\chi(c)n\nabla c,\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}&=0\end{split}\right.

in the space-time region
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)
with
d=2,3
, which will be supplemented with the initial conditions

(1.8)

\displaystyle(n,c,\mathbf{u})\big|_{t=0}=(n_{0},c_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0})\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}.

To state our results precisely, we assume basically that

(
A
):

\chi,\,f\in C^{1}\big([0,+\infty)\big)
with
f(0)=0
and
f(s)\geq 0
for all
s\geq 0
;

(
B
):

\nabla\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(
C
):

The initial data
(n_{0},c_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0})
satisfy that

\displaystyle n_{0}\geq 0,\qquad c_{0}\geq 0,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{0}=0\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}

and that

n_{0}(1+|x|+|\ln n_{0}|)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad c_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mathbf{u}_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Our first main result is concerning the Serrin-type extensibility criteria in three dimensional case.

Theorem 1.1

(Extensibility criteria in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
)
.

Let the initial data
(n_{0},c_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0})\in H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3}:\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
m\geq 3
satisfy the assumptions
(\bf{A})
,
(\bf{B})
and
(\bf{C})
with
d=3
. Suppose that
\chi,\,f\in C^{m}\big([0,+\infty)\big)
and
\phi\in W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. If the maximal existence time
T^{*}
of system (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) is finite, then in the case
\kappa\neq 0
, it holds that

(1.9)

\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\nabla c\|^{s_{1}}_{L^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\mathbf{u}\|^{s_{2}}_{L^{r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt=+\infty

for any
(r_{i},s_{i})
satisfying
\frac{3}{r_{i}}+\frac{2}{s_{i}}\leq 1
and

, while in the case

(1.10)

\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\nabla c\|^{s_{1}}_{L^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt=+\infty

satisfying

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we would like to present some preliminaries. We begin with recalling the well-known tame estimate for the product of two functions and Moser estimate.

Lemma 2.1

(Corollary 2.54 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any functions

,

, there exists a positive constant

such that

\big\|\nabla^{k}(fg)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\|\nabla^{k}g\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\big\|\nabla^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).

Furthermore, if
\nabla f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\big\|\nabla^{k}(fg)-f\nabla^{k}g\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\|\nabla^{k-1}g\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\big\|\nabla^{k}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).

(Theorem 2.61 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

and

Proof.

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|n\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\big)dt+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{r_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{s_{3}}dt\Big)\leq C_{2}

for some

depending only on initial data and

, which together with (

in

(3.39)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Big(\|\nabla n_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\Big(1+\|\nabla c\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{4}\Big)dt\Big)\leq C_{4},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla^{3}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla^{3}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+Cc_{\infty}^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\frac{4}{r_{3}}}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\frac{2(r_{3}-2)}{r_{3}}}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{r_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\Big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\nabla^{3}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}dt\Big\}\Big(\|\nabla^{2}c_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\Big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\Big)dt

(3.40)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{r_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{s_{3}}dt\Big)\leq C_{3},

where

As for the estimate of

in the current setting, we can use Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, Young’s inequality, (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\Big(\kappa^{2}\|\nabla(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla(n\nabla\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla\big(\chi(c)n\nabla c\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C\Big(\kappa^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{4}+\kappa^{2}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|n\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\|\nabla c\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{4}\|n\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\Big\{\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}dt\Big\}\Big(\|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|n\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\qquad+T^{*}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\|\nabla c\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{4}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}\|n\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\Big)\leq C_{5},

where

is a positive constant depending only on initial data and

. Combining (

), (
3.2
) and (

), we obtain the estimate of

in

4.
Global existence of classical solution

In this section, we shall explore the global existence of unique classical solution to the Cauchy problem of chemotaxis-Stokes system (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes system (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The key is to derive an entropy functional inequality.

As is showing in the equation (
1.7
)
3
, the appearance of the force term
\chi(c)n\nabla c
leads to stronger nonlinearity. It is for this reason that we need the bound estimate in the following Lemma to control this bad term when we establish an entropy inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<T<\infty
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
and
d=2,3
. Suppose that the assumptions
(\bf{A})
,
(\bf{B})
and
(\bf{C})
hold. There exists
\delta=\delta(p)
such that if
\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta
, then
n(t)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\in[0,T)
and

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|n(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C=C(p,T,\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\|n_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}).

Remark 4.1
.

As we said, Lemma
4.1
is used to control the bad term
\chi(c)n\nabla c
appeared in (
1.7
)
3
. However, the boundedness of
n
in space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in[1,+\infty)
seems relatively strong from the perspective of the proof of Lemma
4.2
below.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

The proof of this Lemma is much similar as the one in
[
8
, Proposition 2]
. We give a sketch of the main steps below for completeness.

Firstly, let
g
be a positive function satisfying
g^{\prime}\geq 0
, which is to be determined later. Then one can easily deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-1}g(c)\partial_{t}n+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\partial_{t}c

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-1}g(c)\big(-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla n+\Delta n-\nabla\cdot(\chi(c)n\nabla c)+\nabla\cdot(n\nabla\phi)\big)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\big(-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c+\Delta c-nf(c)\big).

Due to the divergence-free property of
\mathbf{u}
, we have that

(4.3)

\displaystyle-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-1}g(c)\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla n-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(c)\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla n^{p}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla g(c)=0.

Substituting (
4.3
) into (
4
) and using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)

\displaystyle=-p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}-2p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-1}g^{\prime}(c)\nabla c\cdot\nabla n

\displaystyle\qquad+p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-1}g(c)\chi(c)\nabla n\cdot\nabla c+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\chi(c)|\nabla c|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-1}g(c)\nabla n\cdot\nabla\phi-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi

(4.4)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime\prime}(c)|\nabla c|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p+1}g^{\prime}(c)f(c),

which implied by the nonnegativity of
n
,
g^{\prime}
and
f
and the application of Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime\prime}(c)|\nabla c|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p+1}g^{\prime}(c)f(c)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6p}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g(c)}|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{3p(p-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\chi^{2}(c)g(c)|\nabla c|^{2}+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\chi(c)|\nabla c|^{2}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{3p(p-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{p^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g^{\prime\prime}(c)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

Setting
g(c)=e^{(\beta c)^{2}}
, and we aim at looking for
g(c)
fulfilling

\frac{6p}{p-1}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g(c)}+\frac{3p(p-1)}{2}\chi^{2}(c)g(c)+pg^{\prime}(c)\chi(c)\leq\frac{1}{4}g^{\prime\prime}(c),

which is equivalent to

(4.6)

\displaystyle\frac{24p}{p-1}\beta^{4}c^{2}+\frac{3p(p-1)}{2}\chi^{2}(c)+2p\beta^{2}c\,\chi(c)\leq\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}+\beta^{4}c^{2}.

To make (
4.6
) be satisfied, we can firstly choose
\beta
such that

(4.7)

\displaystyle 9p(p-1)\mathcal{C}_{\chi}^{2}\leq\beta^{2}.

Then, due to
\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is sufficiently small, we can thus find
\delta=\delta(p)
satisfying that for all
\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\chi}\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{12p}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq\frac{p-1}{144p\beta^{2}}.

One can easily check that (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) are enough to ensure the validity of (
4.6
). Thus, we infer from (
4
) and (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime\prime}(c)|\nabla c|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\frac{3p(p-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{p^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g^{\prime\prime}(c)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{3p(p-1)}{2}+\frac{p^{2}}{2}\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g^{\prime\prime}(c)g(c)}\Big)\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c).

Finally, the proof of (
4.1
) is immediately implied by Gronwall’s inequality and
e^{(\beta c)^{2}}>1
.
∎

With (
4.1
) at hand, we now present the entropy functional inequality as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<T<\infty
,
d=2,3
. Suppose that the assumptions
(\bf{A})
,
(\bf{B})
and
(\bf{C})
hold. If
\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

to the Cauchy problem (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n|\ln n|+\|\nabla c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}+\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Big)ds\leq C

for all
t\in(0,T)
, where
C
depends on
T
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n_{0}|\ln n_{0}|
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n_{0}\langle x\rangle
,
\|\nabla c_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}
and
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}
.

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.7
)
1
by

, using the integration by parts, and applying Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\ln n+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(c)\nabla c\cdot\nabla n-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla n

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}+\mathcal{C}_{\chi}^{2}\|\sqrt{n}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\sqrt{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}+C\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|n\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C\|n\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which implies that for suitably small
c_{0}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\ln n+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C,

where we have used Lemma
2.4
and the boundedness of
\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
in (
4.1
) with
p=2
.

Similarly, taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
1.7
)
2
with
-\Delta c
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta c\,\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta c\,n\,f(c)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla^{2}c:\nabla\mathbf{u})\,c+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta c\,n\,f(c)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\mathcal{C}_{f}^{2}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Putting (
4.10
) and (
4
) together, it shows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\ln n+\|\nabla c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla n|^{2}}{n}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+C,

where we have used Lemma
4.1
with
p=2
again.

In order to deal with the first term on the right-hand-side of (
4
), we test (
1.7
)
3
against
\mathbf{u}
and apply Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{u}\cdot n\nabla\phi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbf{u}\cdot\chi(c)n\nabla c

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mathcal{C}_{\chi}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|n\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Proof of Theorem

We will split this proof into three steps.

Step 1

In case of

and

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime\prime}(c)|\nabla c|^{2}+C\mathcal{M}_{\omega\phi}\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}\left(\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g^{\prime\prime}(c)}\right)\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{g(c)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime\prime}(c)|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{1}{8}p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}

whenever
\mathcal{M}_{\omega\phi}
is suitably small. Substituting (
5.9
) and (
5
) into (
5
) and using Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime\prime}(c)|\nabla c|^{2}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8p}{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g(c)}|\nabla c|^{2}+2p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}\chi^{2}(c)g(c)|\nabla c|^{2}+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g^{\prime}(c)\chi(c)|\nabla c|^{2}.

Similar as in (

)-(

\displaystyle\frac{8p}{p-1}\frac{(g^{\prime}(c))^{2}}{g(c)}+2p(p-1)\chi^{2}(c)g(c)+pg^{\prime}(c)\chi(c)\leq\frac{1}{4}g^{\prime\prime}(c),

which is equivalent to

(5.12)

\displaystyle\frac{32p}{p-1}\beta^{4}c^{2}+2p(p-1)\chi^{2}(c)+2p\beta^{2}c\,\chi(c)\leq\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}+\beta^{4}c^{2}.

\displaystyle 12p(p-1)\mathcal{C}_{\chi}^{2}\leq\beta^{2},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\chi}\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{12p}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq\frac{p-1}{192p\beta^{2}},

which entail the validation of (

(5.13)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}\leq 0.

Then multiplying (

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\gamma_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)+\frac{1}{2}p(p-1)\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\gamma_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n_{0}^{p}\,g(c_{0})+\gamma_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\gamma_{2}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)\,ds.

Noticing that

\displaystyle\|\nabla n^{\frac{p}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\frac{p^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}|\nabla n|^{2}\leq\frac{p^{2}}{4}\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{g(c)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}

and thus that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}g(c)\|n\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}g(c)\left\|\nabla n^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\cdot\frac{d(p-1)}{d(p-1)+2}}\left\|n^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2\cdot\frac{2}{d(p-1)+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}g(c)\left(\max_{0\leq c\leq c_{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{g(c)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d(p-1)}{d(p-1)+2}}\left\|n\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2p}{d(p-1)+2}},

we can apply Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\gamma_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p}g(c)+\frac{1}{4}p(p-1)\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\gamma_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{p-2}g(c)|\nabla n|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n_{0}^{p}\,g(c_{0})+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{\gamma_{2}-\frac{d}{2}(p-1)-1}\|n\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}ds

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n_{0}^{p}\,g(c_{0})+C\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}(1+s)^{\gamma_{2}-\frac{d}{2}(p-1)}.

Taking
\gamma_{2}=\frac{d}{2}(p-1)
and using the facts that

and

Step 3
:
L^{\infty}
-decay of

.

For simplicity, we set

\xi(t):=(1+t)^{-1},\qquad\eta(t):=(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{4}}.

Letting
(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}:=\max\{c-\tau\eta(t),0\}
, differentiating
\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}^{2}
with respect to time variable
t
, then using the nonnegativity of
n
and
f
and the divergence-free property of
\mathbf{u}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\partial_{t}c-\tau\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\big(-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla c+\Delta c-nf(c)\big)-\tau\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}|^{2}-\tau\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}

for any
\tau>0
, which implied by a direct integration with respect to time that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}|^{2}

(5.15)

\displaystyle\,\,\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c_{0}-\tau)_{+}^{2}-\tau\int_{0}^{T}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}

for any fixed
T>0
. For notational simplicity, we will also denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\tau):=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\tau):=\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}^{2},\qquad\mathcal{A}(\tau):=\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)^{\frac{d+4}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}.

For any

, a direct calculation shows that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\tau)=-2\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}=-2\mathcal{A}(\tau)

due to

, which together with (

) and

implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\tau)\leq\tau\int_{0}^{T}\big|\eta^{\prime}(t)\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}=\frac{d\tau}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)\eta(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\leq\frac{d\tau}{8}\big|\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\tau)\big|.

On the other hand, an application of the interpolation and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality yields that

\displaystyle\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d+4}}\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d+4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d+4}}\|\nabla(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2d}{d+4}}\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d+4}}

for some constant

, which together with Hölder’s inequality entails that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d+4}}\|\nabla(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2d}{d+4}}\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d+4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)^{\frac{d+4}{4}}\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)^{\frac{4}{d+4}}\|\nabla(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{\frac{2d}{d+4}}\|(c-\tau\eta(t))_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{d+4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{A}(\tau)^{\frac{4}{d+4}}\mathcal{E}(\tau)^{\frac{d+2}{d+4}}.

Now we can deduce from (

5
) and (

|\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\tau)|=2\mathcal{A}(\tau)\geq C\mathcal{U}(\tau)^{\frac{d+4}{4}}\mathcal{E}(\tau)^{-\frac{d+2}{4}}\geq C\tau^{-\frac{d+2}{4}}\mathcal{U}(\tau)^{\frac{d+4}{4}}|\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\tau)|^{-\frac{d+2}{4}}

and thus from the nonpositivity of
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\tau)
that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\tau)\leq-C\tau^{-\frac{d+2}{d+6}}\,\mathcal{U}(\tau)^{\frac{d+4}{d+6}}.

Noticing that

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\tau_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)\int_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}(c-\tau_{0}\eta(t))_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)\int_{\mathcal{S}_{t}}\left(c-\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}\eta(t)\right)_{+}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}\eta(t)\right)^{-\frac{4}{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{2}\right)^{-\frac{4}{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\xi(t)}{\eta(t)^{\frac{4}{d}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau_{0}^{-\frac{4}{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau_{0}^{-\frac{4}{d}}\|c_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}},

where we used a direct result from (
3.7

\mathcal{U}(\tau)^{\frac{2}{d+6}}\leq\mathcal{U}(\tau_{0})^{\frac{2}{d+6}}-C\Big(\tau^{\frac{4}{d+6}}-\tau_{0}^{\frac{4}{d+6}}\Big),

which implies that

must vanish at some finite point
\tau=\tau(\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\|c_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})
and thus that

\|c(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{4}}

for any
t\in(0,T)
. By the arbitrariness of

, we obtained the desired

-decay (
1.15
). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

, where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
T
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}|\ln n_{0}|
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}\langle x\rangle
,
\|\nabla c_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}
and
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
4.2
. We only remark that the definitions of
n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}
and
c_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}
, and the convexity of
x\ln x
imply that

\|\nabla c_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\|\nabla c_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}(\ln n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon})_{+}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}|\ln n_{0}|,

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}\langle x\rangle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}\langle x\rangle,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}(\ln n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon})_{-}\leq C\Big(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{0}\langle x\rangle\Big),

which entail the dependence on the initial data of constant
C
.
∎

With the energy estimate (
6.2
) at hand, we can now extend the obtained local solution of (
6.2
)-(
6.3
) to a global one.

Proposition 6.3
.

The regularized system (
6.2
)-(
6.3
) admits a unique global-in-time classical solution
(n^{l,\varepsilon},c^{l,\varepsilon},\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

Noticing that the same regularity criterion (
1.9
) in Theorem
1.1
can be established for system (
6.2
)-(
6.3
) by repeating the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we just need to apply this regularity criterion to the local solution
(n^{l,\varepsilon},c^{l,\varepsilon},\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon})
.

On the one hannd, since
\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
is uniformly bounded in
T\in(0,T^{*})
by (
6.2
), we can infer from the inequality

\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\nabla P_{l}\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\sqrt{l}\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

due to (
6.1
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{5}\big(0,T;L^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{5}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{1}{5}}\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T^{*};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{1}{5}}\big(\sqrt{l}+1\big)\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)},

which is uniformly bounded in
T\in(0,T^{*})
if the maximal time of existence
T^{*}<+\infty
, which will contradict to the Serrin condition in (
1.9
) of
\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}
by choosing
r_{2}=s_{2}=5
.

On the other hand, for
c^{l,\varepsilon}
, we can further deduce from the uniform boundedness of
\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}
due to (
6.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{2}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla^{3}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C\Big(\|\nabla(\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|\nabla\big((n^{l,\varepsilon}f(c^{l,\varepsilon}))\ast\eta^{\varepsilon}\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla^{3}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C\Big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla^{3}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C\Big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|\nabla^{3}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla^{2}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C\Big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{4}\|\nabla^{2}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|n_{0}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\Big).

In particular, a direct application of Gronwall’s inequality can yield the uniform boundedness of
\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}
in
T\in(0,T^{*})
if
T^{*}<+\infty
, which contradicts to the Serrin condition in (
1.9
) of
c^{l,\varepsilon}
provided that we take
r_{1}=6
and
s_{1}=+\infty
.

Thus we have completed the proof of Proposition
6.3
.
∎

6.3.
Passing to the limit

In this subsection, we shall extract a suitable subsequence from
(n^{l,\varepsilon},c^{l,\varepsilon},\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon})
with the help of
a priori
energy estimates such that it is convergent and the corresponding limit triple
(n,c,\mathbf{u})
will be a global weak solution of system (
1.7
)-(
1.8
).

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Firstly, for any
T\in(0,+\infty)
, we can use the energy inequality (
6.2
) to conclude the following uniform bounds:

(
a
).

\|\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}+\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\big|\ln n^{l,\varepsilon}\big|\big\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}+\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C
, which together with the interpolation inequality

\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{2}}

implies that

\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C

and thus that

\|\nabla n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{7}}\big(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C

by the interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\|2\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})};

(
b
).

\|c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}+\|c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C
;

(
c
).

\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C
;

(
d
).

\|\partial_{t}n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(0,T;H^{-3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C
, which follows from property (
a
) and the estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\left\langle\partial_{t}n^{l,\varepsilon},\varphi\right\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\left\langle n^{l,\varepsilon},\Delta\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}n^{l,\varepsilon}+n^{l,\varepsilon}\big(\chi(c^{\l,\varepsilon})\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\big)\ast\eta^{\varepsilon}-\big(n^{l,\varepsilon}\nabla\phi\big)\ast\eta^{\varepsilon},\nabla\varphi\right\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big(1+\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big)\|\varphi\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\varphi\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

for any
\varphi\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
;

(
e
).

\|\partial_{t}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C
, which can be deduced from properties (
a
), (
b
) and (
c
) together with the estimate

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\Delta c^{\l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla^{2}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\Delta c^{\l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla^{2}c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}c^{\l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})};

(
f
).

\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(0,T;H^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)}\leq C
, which can be seen from the fact that for any
\mathbf{w}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it holds that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}P_{l}(n^{l,\varepsilon}\nabla\phi)\cdot\mathbf{w}dx\Big|\leq C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

and

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}P_{l}(n^{l,\varepsilon}\chi(c^{l,\varepsilon})\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon})\cdot\mathbf{w}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla c^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Thus for any
T\in(0,+\infty)
, the above uniform estimates entail us to find a subsequence still denoted by
(n^{l,\varepsilon},c^{l,\varepsilon},\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon})
for simplicity and some triple
(n,c,\mathbf{u})
with the regularities enquired in Definition
6.1
(i) such that when
l\to+\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
, it holds that

(
\widetilde{a}
).

\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\sqrt{n}
in
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
,
n^{l,\varepsilon}\big|\ln n^{l,\varepsilon}\big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}n|\ln n|
in
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
,    and
\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\rightharpoonup\sqrt{n}
in
L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
due to the property (
a
);

(
\widetilde{b}
).

n^{l,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup n
in
L^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
and
\nabla n^{l,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\nabla n
in
L^{\frac{8}{7}}\big(0,T;L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
due to the property (
a
);

(
\widetilde{c}
).

n^{l,\varepsilon}\to n
in
L^{\frac{8}{7}}\big(0,T;L^{\frac{12}{5}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
by properties (
a
) and (
d
), and the Aubin–Lions Lemma with
W^{1,\frac{4}{3}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\subset L^{\frac{12}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset H^{-3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
;

(
\widetilde{d}
).

c^{l,\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}c
in
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
and
c^{l,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup c
in
L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
due to the property (
b
);

(
\widetilde{e}
).

c^{l,\varepsilon}\to c
in
L^{2}\big(0,T;W^{1,6}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
by using properties (
b
) and (
e
), and the Aubin–Lions Lemma;

(
\widetilde{f}
).

\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\star}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathbf{u}
in
L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
and
\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
due to the property (
c
);

(
\widetilde{g}
).

\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{6}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)
by properties (
c
) and (
f
), and the Aubin–Lions Lemma.

Hence the above convergence properties together with Proposition
6.1
entail us to pass to the limit for all terms in the distributional form of system (
6.2
) and thus the triple
(n,c,\mathbf{u})
satisfies Definition
6.1
(ii). We take the convergence of the term

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n^{l,\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt

for any given
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T]\big)
satisfying
\varphi(\cdot,T)=0
as an example: supposing that
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi(\cdot,t)\subset K
for each
t\in[0,T]
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n^{l,\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n^{l,\varepsilon}\big(\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}\big)\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(n^{l,\varepsilon}-n\big)\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}\big\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(K)}\big\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}\big\|_{L^{6}(K)}+C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}-n\big\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(K)}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\frac{12}{7}}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\big\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(K)}^{2}\big\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}\big\|_{H^{1}(K)}+C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}-n\big\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(K)}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\big\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\big\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\big\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}\big\|_{H^{1}(K)}+C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}-n\big\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(K)}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\big\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}\big\|_{H^{1}(K)}+C\int_{0}^{T}\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}-n\big\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big\|\nabla\sqrt{n^{l,\varepsilon}}\big\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big\|\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}-\mathbf{u}\big\|_{H^{1}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+CT^{\frac{1}{8}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\big\|n^{l,\varepsilon}-n\big\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(K)}^{\frac{8}{7}}\Big)^{\frac{7}{8}}

\displaystyle\to 0\qquad\mathrm{as}\quad l\to+\infty\,\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\,\varepsilon\to 0

due to the uniform estimates (
a
) and (
c
), and the convergence properties (
\widetilde{c}
) and (
\widetilde{g}
), which implies that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n^{l,\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{l,\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt\qquad\mathrm{as}\quad l\to+\infty\,\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\,\varepsilon\to 0.

The other terms can be similarly dealt with. This completes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
∎

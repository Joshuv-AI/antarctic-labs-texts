Random vortex dynamics and Monte-Carlo simulations for wall-bounded viscous flows
By V. Cherepanov, S. W. Ertel, Z. Qian and J. Wu

Abstract
Functional integral representations for solutions of the motion equations for wall-bounded incompressible viscous flows, expressed (implicitly) in terms of distributions of solutions to stochastic differential equations of McKean-Vlasov type, are established by using a perturbation technique. These representations are used to obtain exact random vortex dynamics for wall-bounded viscous flows. Numerical schemes therefore are proposed and the convergence of the numerical schemes for random vortex dynamics with an additional force term is established. Several numerical experiments are carried out for demonstrating the motion of a viscous flow within a thin layer next to the fluid boundary. Key words : diffusion processes, incompressible fluid flow, numerical simulation, random vortex method MSC classifications : 76M35, 76M23, 60H30, 65C05, 68Q10.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we shall establish Feynman-Kac type formulas for solutions to the motion equations of an incompressible viscous fluid flow past a solid wall. These representations are useful tools for implementing (Monte-Carlo) direct numerical simulations (DNS) for wall-bounded incompressible viscous flows. In order to explain our method, the paper is devoted to two-dimensional (2D) flows past flat plates only, the technology in the paper however can be applied to three-dimensional (3D) flows with appropriate (while substantial) modifications. However the proof of the convergence of 3D random vortex dynamics for flows past solid walls requires substantial new techniques which is beyond the scope of the present paper.

The numerical method for calculating wall-bounded flows to be established in the paper is different from the traditional DNS (cf.
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
8
]
for example), and is based on the exact stochastic formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations satisfying the non-slip condition. The approach, which may be called a random vortex method, is in spirit very similar to the mean field approach, while here we deal with a much more complicated boundary value problem of the fluid dynamical equations. The present work therefore aims to provide new tools for the study of incompressible viscous flows within thin layers next to the boundaries. Numerical experiments are carried out to demonstrate this point.

The random vortex method, initiated in Chorin
[
2
]
, has been carried out for 2D incompressible viscous flows without boundary. The exact random vortex model for 2D viscous flows has been put forward by Goodman
[
5
]
and Long
[
6
]
(cf. also

[
3
]
,
[
7
]
and the literature cited therein).

One of the goals of the present work is to prove the convergence of the numerical schemes for solving 2D Navier-Stokes equations with an external force applied to the fluid based on their corresponding random vortex dynamics. The convergence of the 2D random vortex dynamics was proved in Goodman
[
5
]
and Long
[
6
]
, and their proof was refined in Majda and Bertozzi
[
7
]
and by other researchers. To our best knowledge, the convergence of random vortex dynamics treated in the literature does not contain any external vorticity in the vorticity transport equation. As we shall demonstrate in the present work, in order to deal with the boundary vorticity, we shall deform the vorticity transport equation by adding a suitable "external vorticity" term. Therefore it is important to establish the convergence result for numerical schemes built upon the vorticity transport equation with an applying vorticity force. Although our proof of the convergence follows the same line as those in the literature, the techniques used in the key steps of the proof are quite different. As a by-product, we are able to provide a transparent proof of the convergence result for 2D random vortex dynamics and to clarify several technical issues in the existing proofs of the convergence of 2D random vortex dynamics.

The second goal of the present work is to establish a useful numerical method based on the stochastic formulation of incompressible viscous flows past a solid wall, which may be called Monte-Carlo DNS. The random vortex formulation is established by using
a new functional integral representation of the solutions to certain parabolic equations. We demonstrate our method by several numerical
experiments based on Monte-Carlo DNS we put forward.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we state the stochastic integral representation for unconstrained flows with external force and propose random vortex schemes based on the representation. We analyse the corresponding mean-field equation in Section
3
. In particular, we show the existence and uniqueness and prove that the velocity is indeed approximated by corresponding dynamics with regularised kernels and localised forcing which is used in simulations. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the convergence of the discretised random vortex schemes proposed before. In Section
5

6

2
Random vortex method – flows without constraint

In this section we derive a random vortex formulation for two-dimensional
incompressible viscous flows without space constraint. The mathematical
model for such a flow may be described by its velocity
u(x,t)=(u^{1}(x,t),u^{2}(x,t))

and by the pressure
P(x,t)
where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
.
Let
\nu>0
be the viscosity of the fluid flow. Then
u
and
P

solve the Navier-Stokes equations

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u+\nabla P-F=0

(2.1)

and

\nabla\cdot u=0

(2.2)

in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,\infty)
, where
F(x,t)=(F^{1}(x,t),F^{2}(x,t))

is an external force applying to the fluid flow.

The vorticity
\omega=\nabla\wedge u
, which is a time dependent
scalar field for a 2D flow, evolves according to the vorticity equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-\nu\Delta\omega-G=0

(2.3)

where
G=\nabla\wedge F
.

2.1
Stochastic integral representation

In literature, the random vortex method is built upon two facts. Firstly,
since
u
is divergence-free, the transition probability
density function
p_{u}(s,x;t,y)
(for
t>s\geq 0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
)
of the diffusion process with infinitesimal generator
\nu\Delta+u\cdot\nabla

is the fundamental solution to the forward problem of the parabolic
equation

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta\right)w=0

which allows to represent the vorticity
\omega
in terms of
p_{u}(s,x;t,y)
.
Indeed,

\omega(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(0,\xi;t,x)\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(s,\xi;t,x)G(\xi,s)\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s

(2.4)

where
\omega_{0}=\nabla\wedge u_{0}
is the initial vorticity and

u_{0}
is the initial velocity.

Secondly, the Biot-Savart law which allows, under certain assumptions
on the decay of the velocity at infinity, one recovers the velocity from the vorticity by

u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)\omega(y,t)\textrm{d}y

(2.5)

where

K(\xi)=\frac{\xi^{\bot}}{2\pi|\xi|^{2}},\quad\textrm{ for }\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\textrm{ }

(2.6)

is called the Biot-Savart kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

As a matter of fact, numerical schemes based on (
2.4
) are
computationally expensive, therefore we develop an alternative representation
of the vorticity
\omega
, that leads to a feasible numerical scheme.

Let
X(\xi,t)
(also denoted later by
X_{t}^{\xi}
) for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

be the Taylor diffusion family defined by the SDE

\textrm{d}X(\xi,t)=u\left(X(\xi,t),t\right)\textrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B_{t},\quad X(\xi,0)=\xi

(2.7)

where
B
is a Brownian motion on some probability space.

Theorem 2.1
.

The vorticity
\omega

possesses the following representation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(0,\xi,t,x)\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.G(X(\xi,s),s)\right|X(\xi,t)=x\right]p_{u}(0,\xi,t,x)\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s\end{split}

(2.8)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We make use of the duality of conditional laws of diffusions, established
in
[
11
]
. Take arbitrary fixed
T>0
. Let
\tilde{X}(\xi,\cdot)

be the unique (weak) solution of the following SDE:

\textrm{d}\tilde{X}(\xi,t)=-u(\tilde{X}(\xi,t),T-t)\textrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B_{t},\quad\tilde{X}(\xi,0)=\xi

(2.9)

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
u(x,t)=0
for
t<0
. Let

Y_{t}=\omega(\tilde{X}(\xi,t),T-t)
. By Itô’s formula and the vorticity
transport equation to deduce that

Y_{t}=Y_{0}+\sqrt{2\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\omega(\tilde{X}(\xi,s),T-s)\cdot\textrm{d}B_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}G(\tilde{X}(\xi,s),T-s)\textrm{d}s

(2.10)

for every
t\in[0,T]
. Taking expectation on both sides we obtain
that

\omega(\xi,T)=\mathbb{E}\left[\omega_{0}(\tilde{X}(\xi,T))\right]+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[G(\tilde{X}(\xi,t),T-t)\right]\textrm{d}t.

The last term in the previous equation can be rewritten using the
conditional expectation, to obtain that

\displaystyle\omega(\xi,T)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega_{0}(\eta)p_{-u_{T}}(0,\xi,T,\eta)\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.G(\tilde{X}(\xi,t),T-t)\right|\tilde{X}(\xi,T)=\eta\right]p_{-u_{T}}(0,\xi,T,\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}t,

where
p_{-u_{T}}(\tau,\xi,t,\eta)
is the transition probability
density function of the diffusion
\tilde{X}(\xi,\cdot)
. Since
\nabla\cdot u=0
,

p_{-u_{T}}(0,\xi,T,\eta)=p_{u}(0,\eta,T,\xi),

which yields that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega(\xi,T)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega_{0}(\eta)p_{u}(0,\eta,T,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.G(\tilde{X}(\xi,t),T-t)\right|\tilde{X}(\xi,T)=\eta\right]p_{u}(0,\eta,T,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}t\end{split}

(2.11)

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},T>0
. Thanks to the duality of conditional
laws from
[
11
]
, the conditional expectation
can be written in terms of the diffusion process with infinitesimal
generator
\nu\Delta+u\cdot\nabla
, which therefore leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega(\xi,T)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega_{0}(\eta)p_{u}(0,\eta,T,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.G(X(\eta,t),t)\right|X(\eta,T)=\xi\right]p_{u}(0,\eta,T,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}t.\end{split}

(2.12)

The proof is complete.
∎

As a consequence of this representation of the vorticity, we have
the random vortex representation for the velocity.

Theorem 2.2
.

With the notations above, the velocity

u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K\left(x-X(\xi,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}G(X(\xi,s),s)\textrm{d}s\right)\right]\textrm{d}\xi

(2.13)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
, where
\omega_{0}=\nabla\wedge u_{0}

is the initial vorticity.

Proof.

The proof follows from the Biot-Savart law and the stochastic integral representation for the vorticity:

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)p_{u}(0,\xi,t,y)\textrm{d}y\right)\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)\mathbb{E}\left[\left.G(X(\xi,s),s)\right|X(\xi,t)=y\right]p_{u}(0,\xi,t,y)\textrm{d}y\right)\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)p_{u}(0,\xi,t,y)\textrm{d}y\right)\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.K\left(x-X(\xi,t)\right)G(X(\xi,s),s)\right|X(\xi,t)=y\right]p_{u}(0,\xi,t,y)\textrm{d}y\right)\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K\left(x-X(\xi,t)\right)\right]\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K\left(x-X(\xi,t)\right)G(X(\xi,s),s)\right]\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that even though the SDE used in the representation (
2.13
) involves finite-dimensional noise, in fact it lives in an infinite-dimensional function space. One should thus rather view (
2.7
) together with (
2.13
) as a field-valued McKean–Vlasov equation.

2.2
Random vortex schemes

Based on the random vortex formulation (
2.7
), (
2.13
)
one can derive numerical schemes for simulating solutions of viscous
fluid flows.

A standard method to approximate (field-valued) McKean–Vlasov type
of stochastic differential equations like (
2.7
), (
2.13
) is to use a system of interacting SDEs, which are field valued in our case. With the strong law of large numbers in mind, we consider the following system.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the system

\displaystyle u^{N}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K\left(x-X^{i;N}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X^{i;N}(\eta,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\right)\textrm{d}\eta

(2.14)

and

\displaystyle\textrm{d}X^{j;N}(\eta,t)=u^{N}\left(X^{j;N}(\eta,t),t\right)\textrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B_{t}^{j},\quad X^{j;N}(\eta,0)=\eta

(2.15)

where
j=1,\ldots,N
and
\left(B^{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

are independent copies of the 2D Brownian motion
B
. The above
system is obtained formally by replacing the expectation appearing
in (
2.13
) by the sample mean of ‘independent’ copies of the
Taylor diffusion. To turn (
2.15
) into
a viable numerical approximation, one needs to consider a discretisation of the spatial integral involved. For flows without space constraint, a uniform grid of size
h>0

may be applied.

To ensure the well-posedness of the induced particle approximation,
it is easy to have the Biot–Savart kernel
K
regularised into
a function denoted by
K_{\delta}
(where
\delta>0
is a parameter),
so that
K_{\delta}
is smooth up to the origin
x=0
.

Besides the singularity of the Biot–Savart kernel, another problem
of naive discretisation of (
2.14
) is that even for external
vorticity
G
with compact support, the function
\eta\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[G(X(\eta,t),t)\right]

will not have compact support in general. Thus, in the inhomogeneous setting,
the well-posedness of the integrals in (
2.14
) becomes an issue
and in naive numerical discretisation every node would interact with
infinitely many other nodes. As in practice this can not be implemented in numerical schemes, we need a cut-off function
\chi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
that vanishes outside of the ball centered at
0
with radius
R>0
and thus avoids infinite numerical stencils. There are various possible regularisations
K_{\delta}
and cut-off
\chi_{R}
and our analysis can deal with many of the reasonable choices. The assumptions we require are made precise in the beginning of the next section.

This then gives us the following Monte Carlo approximation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(x,t\right):=&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h^{2}K_{\delta}\left(x-\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)\right)\Bigl(\omega_{0}(jh)\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(jh)\Bigl),\end{split}

(2.16)

where
\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,\cdot)
(for
k=1,\ldots,N
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) are determined by the SDE:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)=\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t),t\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B_{t}^{k},\quad\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,0)=jh.

(2.17)

Since
\chi_{R}
has a compact support, the second sum in (
2.16
) is indeed a finite sum.

We will refer to the combined system (
2.16
),
(
2.17
) as the
Field Monte Carlo Random
Vortex (FMCRV)
approximation. Due to the localisation there are only
a finite number of
\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)
that determine
the velocity
\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(\cdot,t\right)
, and due
to the regularisation the velocity is Lipschitz. Thus one easily verifies that FMCRV approximation is well-posed.

The FMCRV is based on the law of large numbers for i.i.d. random variables. One can also derive approximations based on concentration inequalities for non identically distributed random variables.

One known way to implement a Monte-Carlo method is based on the following modified system:

\mathrm{d}X_{h,\delta,R}(i,t)=u_{h,\delta,R}\left(X_{h,\delta,R}(i,t),t\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B^{i}(t),\penalty\ X_{h,\delta,R}(i,0)=ih,

(2.18)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{h,\delta,R}(x,t)&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h^{2}K_{\delta}\left(x-X_{h,\delta,R}(j,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(jh)\right.\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X_{h,\delta,R}(j,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\left.\chi_{R}(jh)\right),\end{split}

(2.19)

where
B^{i}
are independent Brownian motions, which will be referred to as the
Particle Monte Carlo Random Vortex (PMCRV)
approximation.

3
Some facts about the mean-field equation

In this section, we prove the well-posedness of the mean-field random
vortex dynamics in the form of McKean–Vlasov type dynamics

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}X(\eta,t)&=u\left(X(\eta,t),t\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad X(\eta,0)=\eta,\\
u(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K(x-X(\eta,t))\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G(X(\eta,s),s)\textrm{d}s\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\end{split}

(3.1)

where
B
is a two-dimensional Brownian motion. Furthermore we show
that on the mean-field level the velocity can be robustly approximated
by dynamics with regularised kernels and localised forcing. To this
end, we make the following assumptions that shall hold throughout this
and the next section (i.e. Sections
3

and
4
).

Assumption 1.

We assume that the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}\right)

with a compact support. Similarly, we assume that the external vorticity

G
is smooth, and
G(x,t)=0
for
|x|>R_{0}
and for all
t
.

Assumption 2.
We consider
a family of vector kernels
\left\{K_{\delta}:\delta>0\right\}

which has the following properties:

•

K_{\delta}\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)

with bounded first and second derivatives, although in general the
bounds of the derivatives might depend on
\delta>0
.

•

There exists a constant
C_{0}
,
such that

\displaystyle\left|K_{\delta}(z)\right|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{|z|}\quad\textrm{ for all }\delta>0\textrm{ and }z\neq 0.

•

K_{\delta}
approximates
the Biot-Savart kernel
K
in the following sense that, there is

\epsilon_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
for each
\delta>0

such that

\displaystyle\left|K_{\delta}(z)-K(z)\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon_{\delta}(z)}{|z|}\quad\text{ for all }z\neq 0

(3.2)

for any
\delta>0
, where the family of functions
\epsilon_{\delta}

has to satisfy the following condition.

•

It holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{|z|\leq 1\right\}}\frac{\epsilon_{\delta}(z)}{|z|}\mathrm{d}z+\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon_{\delta}(z)}{|z|}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\delta

for some positive constant
C_{\mathrm{reg}}>0
.

Remark
.

A canonical example used throughout this work is the following

\displaystyle K_{\delta}(z):=\left(1-e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}}\right)K(z)

for
\delta>0
. Then the assumptions listed above hold with
\epsilon_{\delta}(z):=\exp\left(-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}\right)
.
Moreover
K_{\delta}
is globally Lipschitz continuous.

Furthermore we make the following assumption regarding the cutoff
\chi_{R}
.

Assumption

3
. The cut-off
function
\chi_{R}\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},[0,1]\right)
, for

R>0
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi_{R}(z)&=1\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ |z|<R,\\
\chi_{R}(z)&=0\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ |z|>R+1\end{split}

(3.3)

and the first two derivatives of
\chi_{R}
are bounded.

For simplicity, we set
K_{0}=K
and
\chi_{\infty}=1
. It is understood that the mean-field random vortex dynamics (
3.1
) are defined for
\delta=0
or/and
R=\infty
.

We consider the (field-valued) McKean–Vlasov equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{\delta,R}(x,t)=u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}(x,t),t\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\penalty\ X_{\delta,R}(x,0)=x

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\delta,R}(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X_{\delta,R}(\eta,t)\right)\right]\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X_{\delta,R}(\eta,t)\right)G(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,s),s)\right]\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s.\end{split}

(3.5)

Firstly we show that the system (
3.4
), (
3.5
) has a unique solution. To this end, for any given bounded drift function
b:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
we denote by
X^{b}
the (weak) solution to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X^{b}(x,t)=b\left(X^{b}(x,t),t\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad X^{b}(x,0)=x

(3.6)

and
p_{b}\left(\tau,x,t,y\right)
(for
t>\tau\geq 0
) denotes its
transition probability density function, which is continuous.

With this in mind, we define for any drift
b
the operation
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b
by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b\right)(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\right]\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\penalty\ t\geq 0
. Therefore, the fixed points of the mapping

b\mapsto\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b

are solutions of (
3.4
).

Before investigating the limit
\delta\to 0
and
R\to\infty
, we aim to prove the existence and uniqueness of such a fixed point, similar to Qian and Yao
[
12
]
, using a Banach fixed point argument. To this end we first generalise some bounds that were proven in
[
12
, Lemma 5 and 6]
. A key tool here is the upper Aronson estimates. In their generic form they state there exists a constant
M(C_{K},T,\nu)>0
, such that on the time interval
[0,T]
for all drift functions
b
with
\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|b(x,t)\right|\leq C_{K}
, it holds that

\displaystyle p_{b}\left(\tau,z,t,y\right)\leq\frac{M(C_{K},T,\nu)}{2\pi(t-\tau)}e^{-\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{M(C_{K},T,\nu)(t-\tau)}},\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\penalty\ 0\leq\tau\leq t\leq T.

(3.7)

This bound can be sharpened into the following more precise form

\displaystyle p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)}\left(1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\sqrt{t-\tau}+\left|z-y\right|\right)e^{\frac{q-1}{2q(t-\tau)}|z-y|^{2}+\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\right),

(3.8)

for any
q\in(1,2)
. Hereby
\kappa(q)>0
is a universal constant only depending on
q
. In what follows, we call (
3.8
) the improved Aronson estimate. Based on these estimates, we introduce

\displaystyle\bar{\kappa}(q,r)

\displaystyle:=1+\kappa(q)re^{\frac{r^{2}}{2(q-1)}}\penalty\ q\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{q\pi}{2}}\right),\quad\text{for any}\penalty\ q\in(1,2),r\geq 0.

(3.9)

The following corollary will be useful later on.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
b:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

be a bounded function with
\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|b(x,t)\right|\leq C_{K}
.
Then for any
0\leq\tau\leq t\leq T
and any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)}\left(1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\sqrt{t-\tau}+\left|z-y\right|\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\times e^{\frac{q-1}{2q(t-\tau)}|z-y|^{2}+\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\right)\mathrm{d}z=\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t-\tau}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right).

Proof.

The first inequality is just the improved Aronson estimate (
3.8
). The rest then follows immediately, as
\frac{e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)}
is the density of the 2D Normal distribution
\mathcal{N}\left(y,(t-\tau)\mathrm{I}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)}\left(1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\sqrt{t-\tau}+\left|z-y\right|\right)e^{\frac{q-1}{2q(t-\tau)}|z-y|^{2}+\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\qquad=1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}e^{\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\sqrt{t-\tau}\penalty\ q\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\left|z-y\right|}{\sqrt{t-\tau}}\right)\frac{e^{-\frac{\left|z-y\right|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)q}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)q}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\qquad=1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}e^{\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\sqrt{t-\tau}\penalty\ q\left(1+\sqrt{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|z\right|\frac{e^{-\frac{\left|z\right|^{2}}{2}}}{2\pi}\mathrm{d}z\right).

Since the the norm
|Z|
of a 2D standard Normal distribution
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{I})

is
\chi(2)
-distributed, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|z\right|\frac{e^{-\frac{\left|z\right|^{2}}{2}}}{2\pi}\mathrm{d}z=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z\right|\right]=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}},

which concludes our proof.
∎

Furthermore we make a remark with the following change of variables formula for
the transition kernel.

Remark
.

For any drift function
c:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, define

\displaystyle c_{z-}(x,t):=-c(z-x,t)\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \hat{X}^{c}(\tau,\eta,t):=z-X^{c}(\tau,\eta,t).

(3.10)

Then for
0\leq\tau\leq t
, the process
\hat{X}^{c}
solves

\displaystyle\hat{X}^{c}(\tau,\eta,t)

\displaystyle=z-X^{c}(\tau,\eta,t)=z-\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}(-c)(X^{c}(\tau,\eta,s),s)\mathrm{d}s-\sqrt{2\nu}(B_{t}-B_{\tau})

\displaystyle=(z-\eta)+\int_{\tau}^{t}c_{z-}(\hat{X}^{c}(\tau,\eta,s),s)\mathrm{d}s-\sqrt{2\nu}(B_{t}-B_{\tau}).

Since
-(B_{t}-B_{\tau})
is also a standard Brownian motion on
[\tau,+\infty)
,
\hat{X}^{c}
is a (weak) solution to the SDE with drift
c_{z-}
, and therefore

\displaystyle p_{c}(\tau,\eta,t,z-y)=p_{c_{z-}}(\tau,z-\eta,t,y).

(3.11)

Note that
\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|c_{z-}(x,t)\right|=\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|c(x,t)\right|
— in particular, if
p_{c}
satisfies the Aronson estimates (
3.7
) or (
3.8
), then so does
p_{c_{z-}}
!

This lets us prove the following key estimate.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
b:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded function with
\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|b(x,t)\right|\leq C_{K}
, and
\gamma\in(1,2)
. Furthermore let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function and define the seminorm

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}:=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(z-a,z)\right|\mathrm{d}z.

Let
\Theta:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

be such that

\displaystyle\left|\Theta(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon(x-y)}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}

for some function
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
. Then for
any
q\in(1,2)
,

•

the following inequality holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
0\leq\tau\leq t\leq T

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\Theta(x,y)\right|\penalty\ \left|f(y,z)\right|p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\\
&\leq\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t-\tau}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{\epsilon(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}z\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\left(\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\text{,}\end{split}

(3.12)

•

consequently, when
\left|\Theta(x,y)\right|\leq|x-y|^{-\gamma}
,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left|f(y,z)\right|}{\left|y-x\right|^{\gamma}}p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y\leq&\left(\frac{2\pi}{2-\gamma}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\\
&\qquad\times\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t-\tau}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right).\end{split}

(3.13)

Proof.

To carry out the estimate, we split up the inner integral into a part close to the singularity and a part that is bounded away from the singular points
\left\{y=x\right\}
. The integral about the singularity can be bounded as in Qian and Yao
[
12
]
in the following way

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\left\{|y-x|<1\right\}}\left|f(y,z)\right|\left|\Theta(x,y)\right|p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\left\{|y-x|<1\right\}}\frac{\left|f(y,z)\right|\epsilon(y-x)}{\left|y-x\right|^{\gamma}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\sqrt{t-\tau}+\left|z-y\right|\right)e^{\frac{q-1}{2q(t-\tau)}|z-y|^{2}+\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\right)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ \mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\left\{|y|<1\right\}}\frac{\left|f(y+x,y+x-z)\right|\epsilon(y)}{\left|y\right|^{\gamma}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(1+\kappa(q)\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\sqrt{t-\tau}+\left|z\right|\right)e^{\frac{q-1}{2q(t-\tau)}|z|^{2}+\frac{C_{K}^{2}(t-\tau)}{4(q-1)\nu}}\right)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\penalty\ \int_{\left\{|y|<1\right\}}\frac{\epsilon(y)}{\left|y\right|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ \bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t-\tau}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right).

To estimate the integral away from the singular points, we note that by the change of variables mentioned above and the improved Aronson estimate (
3.8
) one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\left\{|y-x|\geq 1\right\}}\left|f(y,z)\right|\left|\Theta(x,y)\right|p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(y,z)\right|p_{b}(\tau,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\left(\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(z-y,z)\right|p_{b_{z-}}(\tau,0,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|f(z-y,z)\right|\mathrm{d}z\frac{e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{2(t-\tau)}}}{2\pi t}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(1+\kappa\frac{C_{K}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\sqrt{t-\tau}+\left|y\right|\right)e^{\frac{q-1}{2q(t-\tau)}|y|^{2}+\frac{C_{K}^{2}t}{4(q-1)\nu}}\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t-\tau}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right),

which proves (
3.12
). The inequality (
3.13
) is then just a trivial consequence of (
3.12
) where we take
\epsilon\equiv 1
.
∎

Next we show a key Lipschitz estimate for the operator
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume we are given
some time frame
T_{K}\geq 0
and some upper bound for the drift
C_{K}\geq 0
.
Let the kernel function
\Theta:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

be such that for some
\gamma\in[0,2)
and some constant
C_{\Theta}>0
, one has

\displaystyle\left|\Theta(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\Theta}}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}.

(3.14)

Let furthermore
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded, such
that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{T_{K}}:=\sup_{t\leq T_{K}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|^{2}\right]}\mathrm{d}z<\infty\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}<+\infty.

(3.15)

Finally we set for
\alpha,\beta>1
with
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1

and
\alpha\gamma<2

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{L}(R,T,f)&:=C_{\Theta}\bar{\kappa}\left(q,R\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{2-\gamma}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\frac{R\sqrt{T}}{4\nu}+C_{\Theta}e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4\nu}R^{2}}\\
&\qquad\times\left(\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\alpha R\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{2-\alpha\gamma}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\right)^{1/\alpha}\frac{\sqrt{T}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{T}^{1/\beta}}{\sqrt{\nu}}.\end{split}

(3.16)

Then for all times
T\leq T_{K}
and any drift functions
b,\tilde{b}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T_{K}]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

with
\left\|b\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{K}
,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},t\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,y)\left(p_{b}\left(0,z,t,y\right)-p_{\tilde{b}}\left(0,z,t,y\right)\right)f(y,z)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right|\\
&\qquad\leq C_{L}\left(\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\nu}},T,f\right)\left(\sqrt{T}+T\right)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},t\leq T}\left|b(x,t)-\tilde{b}(x,t)\right|.\end{split}

(3.17)

Remark
.

Note that for integrable
f
, only depending on the second variable
z
, i.e.
f(y,z)=f(z)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left\|f\right\|_{1}<\infty,

thus, Theorem
3.3
is a true
generalisation of the result of Qian and Yao
[
12
]
. For
f
only
depending on the first variable, i.e.
f(y,z)=f(y)
, we also have

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}=\left\|f\right\|_{1}
.
Assume furthermore that
f\in C_{0}^{0}\left(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}\right)

has compact support. Let
R:=\sup_{z\in\mathrm{spt}f}|z|
, then for
all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with
\left|z\right|\geq R

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f\left(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t}\right)\right|^{2}\right]\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t}\right|\geq\left|z\right|-R\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\sqrt{2\nu}\left|B_{t}\right|}\right]\penalty\ e^{-\left|z\right|+R}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\frac{e^{8\nu t+R}}{2}e^{-|z|},

where the last inequality can be derived by using Young’s inequality
and the moment generating function of the
\chi^{2}
-distribution.
Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-|z|/2}\mathrm{d}z=8\pi
, we thus
derive

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{T_{K}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\leq T_{K}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|^{2}\right]}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\left(R^{2}\pi+\sup_{t\leq T_{K}}\frac{e^{4\nu t+R/2}}{\sqrt{2}}8\pi\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\left(\sup_{z\in\mathrm{spt}f}|z|^{2}\pi+\frac{8\pi e^{4\nu T_{k}+\frac{\sup_{z\in\mathrm{spt}f}|z|}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

Thus for any function
f
only depending on the the first variable,
the conditions of Theorem
3.3
are also satisfied if it has compact support. In particular both
\omega_{0}
and
G(\cdot,t)
for every finite
t\geq 0
are appropriate choices for
f
.

For
f
only depending on
y
, but not on
z
, a version of Theorem

3.3
was proven by Qian and
Yao in
[
12
]
. A key tool for their analysis, that we also use in our generalization, is the Cameron-Martin theorem,
which, for the convenience of the reader, we summarize in the following
remark.

Remark
.

The Cameron–Martin theorem determines
the Radon–Nikodym derivative of a diffusion measure w.r.t. the Wiener
measure on a finite horizon path space, i.e. for any
t\geq 0
and
any drift function
c
, the law of
\left(X^{c}(z,s)\right)_{0\leq s\leq t}
, denoted by
Q^{z,t}
, is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the law
\mathbb{P}^{B}
of
(B_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t}
(the Wiener measure) and its Radon–Nikodym derivative is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}Q^{z,t}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{B}}=R_{c}(z,t):=\exp\left(N_{c}(z,t)\right),

where the semimartingale
N_{c}(z,t)
is of the form

\displaystyle N_{c}(z,t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\int_{0}^{t}c(z+\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\mathrm{d}B_{r}-\frac{1}{4\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\left|c(z+\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

Therefore we have for any function
g

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[g(X^{c}(t,z))\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(y)p_{c}(0,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y=\mathbb{E}\left[g(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})R_{c}(z,t)\right].

With this in mind let us now prove Theorem
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We can not directly use the approach of
[
12
, Lemma 8]
,
since
f
may not be integrable with respect to
z
. Therefore we
make a change of variables to note that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,y)\left(p_{b}\left(0,z,t,y\right)-p_{\tilde{b}}\left(0,z,t,y\right)\right)f(y,z)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,z-y)p_{b}\left(0,z,t,z-y\right)f(z-y,z)\mathrm{d}y\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,z-y)p_{\tilde{b}}\left(0,z,t,z-y\right)f(z-y,z)\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,z-y)p_{b_{z-}}\left(0,0,t,y\right)f(z-y,z)\mathrm{d}y\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,z-y)p_{\tilde{b_{z-}}}\left(0,0,t,y\right)f(z-y,z)\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}z\right|

where the last identity is a consequence of (
3.11
). Therefore the Cameron–Martin theorem gives us

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,y)\left(p_{b}\left(0,z,t,y\right)-p_{\tilde{b}}\left(0,z,t,y\right)\right)f(y,z)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\left(R_{b_{z-}}(0,t)-R_{\tilde{b}_{z-}}(0,t)\right)f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right]\mathrm{d}z\right|.\end{split}

(3.18)

We define

\displaystyle M(z,t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(b_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)-\tilde{b}_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right)\mathrm{d}B_{r}

\displaystyle A(z,t)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{4\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\left|b_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right|^{2}-\left|\tilde{b}_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}r,

and note that the following holds for the finite variation
part
A

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2},s\leq t}\left|A(z,s)\right|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2},s\leq t}\left|\frac{1}{4\nu}\int_{0}^{s}\left|b_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right|^{2}-\left|\tilde{b}_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\right|

which implies

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2},s\leq t}\left|A(z,s)\right|\leq\frac{C_{K}t}{4\nu}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty},

(3.19)

and for the local martingale
M

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\langle M(z,t)\right\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t}{2\nu}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

(3.20)

This then gives us the following estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\left(R_{b_{z-}}(0,t)-R_{\tilde{b}_{z-}}(0,t)\right)f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right]\mathrm{d}z\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|\int_{0}^{1}R_{\lambda}(z,t)\mathrm{d}\lambda\left(\left|A(z,t)\right|+\left|M(z,t)\right|\right)\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z,

where we set
R_{\lambda}(z,t):=\exp\left(\lambda N_{b_{z-}}(0,t)+(1-\lambda)N_{\tilde{b}_{z-}}(0,t)\right)
.
Next we note that if we define
b_{\lambda}:=\lambda b+(1-\lambda)\tilde{b}

and
b_{\lambda,z-}:=\lambda b_{z-}+(1-\lambda)\tilde{b}_{z-}
, then

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(z,t)=R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)\penalty\ e^{-\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{4\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\left|b_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)-b_{z-}(\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r},r)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}\leq R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t),

(3.21)

and therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Theta(x,y)\left(p_{b}\left(0,z,t,y\right)-p_{\tilde{b}}\left(0,z,t,y\right)\right)f(y,z)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)\right.\\
&\qquad\left.\times\left(\left|A(z,t)\right|+\left|M(z,t)\right|\right)\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\lambda.\end{split}

(3.22)

We estimate the first term, involving the finite variation part
A

of the differences in the Girsanov-log-likelihoods, using the Cameron–Martin
theorem and Lemma
3.2

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)\left|A(z,t)\right|\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\\
&\leq\frac{C_{K}t}{4\nu}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\\
&=\frac{C_{K}t}{4\nu}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\Theta(x,z-y)\right|p_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,0,t,y)\left|f(z-y,z)\right|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\\
&\leq C_{\theta}\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{2-\gamma}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\frac{C_{K}t}{4\nu}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

(3.23)

Now we use the basic Holder inequality for
\alpha,\beta>1
, such
that
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1
and
\alpha\gamma<2
,
to derive

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)\left|M(z,t)\right|\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|^{\alpha}R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)^{\alpha}\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|M(z,t)\right|^{\beta}\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\beta}.

The first term can be estimated as follows. Note that

\displaystyle R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)^{\alpha}=R_{\alpha\penalty\ b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)e^{\frac{\left(\alpha-1\right)\alpha}{4\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\left|b_{\lambda,z-}(r,z+\sqrt{2\nu}B_{r})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}r}\leq R_{\alpha\penalty\ b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)e^{\frac{\left(\alpha-1\right)\alpha}{4\nu}C_{K}^{2}t},

which gives us

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|^{\alpha}R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)^{\alpha}\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4\nu}C_{K}^{2}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t})\right|^{\alpha}R_{\alpha b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4\nu}C_{K}^{2}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\Theta(x,z-y)\right|^{\alpha}p_{\alpha b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,0,t,y)\left|f(z-y,z)\right|\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4\nu}C_{K}^{2}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\Theta(x,z-y)\right|^{\alpha}p_{\alpha b_{\lambda}}(0,z,t,z-y)\left|f(z-y,z)\right|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}.

A change of variables and Lemma
3.2
,
which is admissible as
\alpha\gamma<2
, gives us

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Theta(x,z-B_{t})\right|^{\alpha}R_{b_{\lambda,z-}}(0,t)^{\alpha}\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}\\
&\leq C_{\theta}e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4\nu}C_{K}^{2}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|x-y\right|^{-\alpha\gamma}p_{\alpha b_{\lambda}}(0,z,t,y)\left|f(y,z)\right|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\alpha}\\
&\leq C_{\theta}e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4\nu}C_{K}^{2}t}\left(\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{\alpha C_{K}\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{2-\alpha\gamma}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\right)^{1/\alpha}.\end{split}

(3.24)

For the second term we again use Cauchy-Schwarz in combination with the BDG inequality to note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|M(z,t)\right|^{\beta}\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\beta}\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle M(z,t)\right\rangle^{\beta}\right]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|^{2}\right]}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\beta}\\
&\leq\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|^{2}\right]}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\beta},\end{split}

which, due to the definition of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{T_{K}}
, means that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|M(z,t)\right|^{\beta}\left|f(z-\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},z)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\beta}\leq\frac{\sqrt{t}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{T_{K}}^{1/\beta}}{\sqrt{\nu}}\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}.

(3.25)

Combining (
3.22
) with
(
3.23
), (
3.24
)
and (
3.25
), we can conclude
our proof.
∎

As Lemma

gives

,

\displaystyle C_{K}:=C_{0}\penalty\ \bar{\kappa}\left(3/2,1\right)\left(2\pi\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)+C_{0}\penalty\ \bar{\kappa}\left(3/2,1\right)^{2}

.

Proof.

Note that for

we generalise this result using Lemma
3.2
.
Since

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\right]&\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\bigg|\leq C_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|y-x\right|}p_{b}(0,z,t,y)\left|\omega_{0}(z)\right|\textrm{d}y\textrm{d}z\\

(3.26)

where the last inequality is a consequence of

only depending on the initial condition.

Next we note that by the Markov property of the transition kernel

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\left|G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\right]|\chi_{R}(\eta)|\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

Then we note that only the term

Thus we finally obtain by Lemma
3.2

and the fact that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\right|

, this lets us conclude that
\left|\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b\right)(x,t)\right|\leq C_{K}
.
∎

Remark
.

Note that the a priori bound

,

Next we show a contraction of

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
b,\tilde{b}
be two
drift functions such that on the time-interval

\left\|b\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{K}

,
T_{K}
,

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r\leq t}\left|\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b\right)(r,x)\penalty\ -\penalty\ \left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond\tilde{b}\right)(r,x)\right|\\
&\leq\left(C_{L}(1,T_{K},\omega_{0})+t\penalty\ C_{D}(T_{K},C_{K},\nu,G)\right)\left(t+\sqrt{t}\right)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r\leq t}\left|b(x,r)-\tilde{b}(x,r)\right|\end{split}

(3.27)

for any

.

Proof.

For the homogeneous part we note that by Lemma
3.2

and Remark
Remark
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\right]\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{\tilde{b}}(\eta,t)\right)\right]\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{L}(1,T_{K},\omega_{0})\left(t+\sqrt{t}\right)\left\|b-\tilde{b}\right\|_{\infty}

,

with
C_{\theta}=C_{0}
.

Next we need to investigate the error in the inhomogeneity

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\biggl(\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{\tilde{b}}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{\tilde{b}}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\biggl)\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\right|

Here the key difference compared to the homogeneous term is the quadratic
dependence on the diffusion process (i.e. dependence on time correlations).

Now we note that by the Markov property

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\biggl(\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{\tilde{b}}(\eta,t)\right)G\left(X^{\tilde{b}}(\eta,s),s\right)\right]\biggl)\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)p_{b}(s,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ G(z,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}z\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)p_{\tilde{b}}(s,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ G(z,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{\tilde{b}}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}z\right|

which we bound above by the following two terms

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)\left(p_{b}(s,z,t,y)-p_{\tilde{b}}(s,z,t,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y\right.\left.G(z,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}z\right|,

and

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)p_{\tilde{b}}(s,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ G(z,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)-p_{\tilde{b}}(0,\eta,s,z)\right)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}z\right|,

3.2
.

To estimate the first term we define

\displaystyle w_{s}(z):=G(z,s)\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta.

The supremum norm of

Next we note that by

(
3.8

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|G(z,s)\right|\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}z

,

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)\left(p_{b}(s,z,t,y)-p_{\tilde{b}}(s,z,t,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ G(z,s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\penalty\ \mathrm{d}z\right|&\\

.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)p_{\tilde{b}}(s,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ G(z,s)\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)-p_{\tilde{b}}(0,\eta,s,z)\right)\chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\penalty\ \mathrm{d}z\right|

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
T\leq T_{K}

be such that

\displaystyle\left(C_{L}(1,T_{K},\omega_{0})+TC_{D}(T_{K},C_{K},\nu,G)\right)\left(T+\sqrt{T}\right)<1,

then there exists a unique bounded vector field
u_{\delta,R}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)

such that
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}=u_{\delta,R}
.
Furthermore on
[0,T]
, there exists a unique strong solution
X_{\delta,R}

to the McKean–Vlasov equation (
3.4
).

Proof.

By Veretennikov
[
15
]
, for any
b\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
,
there is a unique strong solution of the SDE

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X=b(X(t),t)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B_{t},

thus
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)
is
well defined on
L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
.
Combining Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
,
shows that
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)

is even a contraction and thus by the Banach fixed point theorem,
there is a unique fixed point
u_{\delta,R}
.
∎

Remark
.

Note that the bound

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},t\leq T_{K}}\left|u_{\delta}(x,t)\right|\leq C_{K}

holds for all
\delta\geq 0
and all
R\geq 0
. This is in particular
true for the fluid velocity
u
!

Now that we have proven the well-posedness of (
3.4
), it remains to show that the true velocity
u
can be approximated by the the regularised velocity
u_{\delta,R}
, which we do in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists some sufficiently
small timeframe
T>0
and a constant
\tilde{C}(T)>0
, only depending
on
\omega_{0},G
and
T
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|u_{\delta,R}(x,t)-u(x,t)\right|\leq 2\tilde{C}(T)\left(\delta+\frac{1}{R}\right).

(3.29)

Proof.

As noted in Remark
Remark
,
both
T_{K}
and
C_{K}
do not depend on
\delta
or
R
. Let
the regularisation parameter
\delta\geq 0
and the cutoff threshold

R\in[0,+\infty]
be arbitrary, but fixed. Then we note that

\displaystyle\left|u_{\delta,R}(x,t)-u(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,\chi}(x,t)-\mathcal{K}\left(K_{0},\chi_{\infty},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}(x,t)-\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\;+\left|\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u(x,t)-\mathcal{K}\left(K_{0},\chi_{\infty},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u(x,t)\right|.

and, by Theorem
3.5
, the first term is bounded by

\left(C_{L}(1,T_{K},\omega_{0})+tC_{D}(T_{K},C_{K},\nu,G)\right)\left(t+\sqrt{t}\right)\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r\leq t}\left|u_{\delta,R}(y,r)-u(y,r)\right|.

For estimating the last term, we proceed as follows

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\biggr[\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)-K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\left|\omega_{0}(\eta)\right|+\int_{0}^{t}\left|G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\biggr]\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\left|G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\right]\left(1-\chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s.

First we note that by Assumption
•
‣
3
, as well as Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)-K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\right]\left|\omega_{0}(\eta)\right|\textrm{d}\eta\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\epsilon_{\delta}(y-x)}{|y-x|}p_{u}(0,\eta,t,y)\left|\omega_{0}(\eta)\right|\textrm{d}y\textrm{d}\eta\\
&\leq\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{\epsilon_{\delta}(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}z\penalty\ \left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left(\sup_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon_{\delta}(z)}{|z|^{\gamma}}\right)\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{diag}_{1}}\right)\\
&\leq\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)\penalty\ C_{\mathrm{reg}}\penalty\ \delta.\end{split}

(3.30)

Next we note that by the Markov property

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)-K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\right]\int_{0}^{t}\left|G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)-K\left(x-y\right)\right|p_{u}(s,z,t,y)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y\textrm{d}s.

Setting
w_{s}(z):=\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(0,\eta,s,z)\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta
,
then, just as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
,
one derives the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{1}\leq\left(\left\|G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\right)\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{s}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right).

Thus, just as we did for the homogeneous part before, we obtain by
Assumption
•
‣
3
and Lemma
3.2

the following estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)-K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\right]\int_{0}^{t}\left|G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\penalty\ \delta\int_{0}^{t}\penalty\ \bar{\kappa}\left(q,C_{K}\sqrt{s}\right)\penalty\ C_{G}(s)\penalty\ \bar{\kappa}(q,C_{K}\sqrt{t-s})\penalty\ \mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(3.31)

Finally we estimate the localisation error. Using the Markov property,
we note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\left|G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\right]\left(1-\chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|K\left(x-y\right)\right|p_{u}(s,z,t,y)\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(0,\eta,s,z)\left(1-\chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}y\textrm{d}z\textrm{d}s.

We define
v_{s,R}(z):=\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}p_{u}(0,\eta,s,z)\left(1-\chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\textrm{d}\eta

and note that by the basic Aronson estimate (
3.7
)
we have

\displaystyle v_{s,R}(z)\leq M(C_{K},s,\nu)^{2}\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\int_{\left\{|\eta|>R\right\}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|\eta-z|^{2}}{2M(C_{K},s,\nu)s}}}{2\pi s\penalty\ M(C_{K},s)}\textrm{d}\eta.

Let
\mathrm{H}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,M(C_{K},s,\nu)s\mathrm{I}\right)
be a normally distributed random variable, then we use the Markov
inequality to imply from the previous inequality

\displaystyle v_{s,R}(z)

\displaystyle\leq M(C_{K},s,\nu)^{2}\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathrm{H}-z\right|>R\right)

\displaystyle\leq M(C_{K},s,\nu)^{2}\left|G\left(z,s\right)\right|\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi M(C_{K},s,\nu)s}{2}}+|z|}{R},

and therefore if we set

\displaystyle C_{G}(t):=\sup_{s\leq t}\left(\left\|G(\cdot,s)(1+|\cdot|)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)(1+|\cdot|)\right\|_{1}\right),

(3.32)

then Lemma
3.2
gives
us

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|K\left(x-X^{u}(\eta,t)\right)\right|\left|G\left(X^{u}(\eta,s),s\right)\right|\right]\left(1-\chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{s}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(2\pi\left\|v_{s,R}(z)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|v_{s,R}(z)\right\|_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}2\pi\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{s}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)M(C_{K},s,\nu)^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\pi M(C_{K},s,\nu)s}{2}}+1\right)C_{G}(s)\mathrm{d}s\penalty\ \frac{1}{R}.\end{split}

(3.33)

Note that by Assumption
3

we can guarantee the boundedness of
C_{G}
on finite time intervals,
and thus the integral above can be bounded.

Combining (
3.30
), (
3.31
) and (
3.33
), we therefore derive that for any
T>0

there exists a constant
\tilde{C}(T)
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u(x,t)-\mathcal{K}\left(K_{0},\chi_{\infty},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u(x,x)\right|\leq\tilde{C}(T)\left(\delta+\frac{1}{R}\right).

Thus for
T\leq T_{K}
, such that

\displaystyle\left(C_{L}(1,T_{K},\omega_{0})+TC_{D}(T_{K},C_{K},\nu,G)\right)\left(T+\sqrt{T}\right)\leq 1/2,

it follows that

\displaystyle\left|u_{\delta,R}(x,t)-u(x,t)\right|\leq 2\tilde{C}(T)\left(\delta+\frac{1}{R}\right),

which concludes our proof.
∎

In the same manner one can derive the robustness of
u_{\delta,R}

with respect to the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}
and the force

G
, as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let two initial
vorticities
\omega_{0},\tilde{\omega}_{0}
and two external forces

G,\tilde{G}
be given. Then for any drift function
b
with
\sup_{t\leq T_{K},x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|b(x,t)\|\leq C_{K}
,
we have the following property

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T_{K},x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b\right)(x,t)-\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\tilde{\omega}_{0},\tilde{G}\right)\diamond b\right)(x,t)\right|\leq C(T_{K})&\\
\times\sup_{s\leq T_{K}}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}-\tilde{\omega}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\omega_{0}-\tilde{\omega}_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)-\tilde{G}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)-\tilde{G}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\right)&.\end{split}

To emphasize the dependence on the initial vorticity and forcing,
let us denote by
u_{\delta,R}\left[\omega_{0},G\right]
the mean
field velocity
u_{\delta,R}\left[\omega_{0},G\right]=\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}\left[\omega_{0},G\right]

as found in Corollary
3.6
.
Then for some sufficiently small timeframe
0<T\leq T_{K}
, there
exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|u_{\delta,R}\left[\omega_{0},G\right](x,t)-u_{\delta,R}\left[\tilde{\omega}_{0},\tilde{G}\right](x,t)\right|\\
&\leq C\sup_{s\leq T}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}-\tilde{\omega}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\omega_{0}-\tilde{\omega}_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)-\tilde{G}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)-\tilde{G}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

First we decompose the error into two parts

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b(x,t)-\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\tilde{\omega}_{0},\tilde{G}\right)\diamond b(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\tilde{\omega}_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{G}\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\textrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)-\tilde{\omega}_{0}(\eta)\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\left(G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)-\tilde{G}\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right)\right]\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\right|\mathrm{d}s.

The first term can be estimated by Lemma
3.2
,
as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)-\tilde{\omega}_{0}(\eta)\right)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|K_{\delta}\left(x-y\right)\right|\left|\omega_{0}(\eta)-\tilde{\omega}_{0}(\eta)\right|\textrm{d}y\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)\left(2\pi\left\|\omega_{0}-\tilde{\omega}_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\omega_{0}-\tilde{\omega}_{0}\right\|_{1}\right).

For the second term we note that for
\Theta(x,z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}(y-x)p_{b}(s,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y
, defined as in (
3.28
) in Theorem
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Theta(x,z)\right|\leq\frac{M(C_{K},T,\nu)^{2}C_{G}+1}{2|x-z|},

as proved in Theorem
3.5
. Therefore
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(x-X^{b}(\eta,t)\right)\left(G\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)-\tilde{G}\left(X^{b}(\eta,s),s\right)\right)\right]\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\right|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}(x-y)p_{b}(s,z,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ p_{b}(0,\eta,s,z)G(z,s)\mathrm{d}z\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M(C_{K},T,\nu)^{2}C_{G}+1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\kappa}\left(q,\frac{C_{K}\sqrt{s}}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(2\pi\left\|G(\cdot,s)-\tilde{G}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|G(\cdot,s)-\tilde{G}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Thus our first claim follows immediately. The second claim follows as in Lemma
3.7
.
∎

The lemma we just proved does not just give us that
u_{\delta,R}
depends continuously on the initial vorticity and the external force. As the following result shows, the robustness estimate from Lemma
3.8
allows us to show the Lipschitz continuity of
u_{\delta,R}
for all
\delta\geq 0
and
R\in[0,+\infty]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

There exists a time frame
0<T\leq T_{K}

and a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\left|u_{\delta,R}(x+\xi,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|}{\left|\xi\right|}\leq C\sup_{s\leq T}\left(\left\|\nabla\omega_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\nabla\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\nabla G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\nabla G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\right),

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\xi\neq 0
,
\delta\geq 0
and
R\in[0,\infty]
.
Thus, for small time frames,
u_{\delta,R}
is Lipschitz continuous
with a Lipschitz constant independent of
\delta
and
R
.

Proof.

First we note that by a simple change of variables we have for any

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any drift function
b

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond b\right)(x+\xi,t)=\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\left(\omega_{0}\right)_{\xi-},G_{\xi-}\right)\diamond b_{\xi-}\right)(x,t),

due to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}(x+\xi-y)p_{b}(0,\eta,t,y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ \omega_{0}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\delta}(x-y)p_{b}(0,\xi-\eta,t,\xi-y)\mathrm{d}y\penalty\ \omega_{0}(\xi-\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta,

and a similar equality for the inhomogeneous parts which follows from the identity
p_{b}(\tau,\xi-z,t,\xi-y)=p_{b_{\xi-}}(\tau,z,t,y)
mentioned earlier in (
3.11
).

Since
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}=u_{\delta,R}
, the difference

\displaystyle\left|u_{\delta,R}(x+\xi,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|

is equal to

\displaystyle\left|\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}\right)(x+\xi,t)-\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}\right)(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\left(\omega_{0}\right)_{\xi-},G_{\xi-}\right)\diamond\left(u_{\delta,R}\right)_{\xi-}\right)(x,t)-\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond\left(u_{\delta,R}\right)_{\xi-}\right)(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond\left(u_{\delta,R}\right)_{\xi-}\right)(x,t)-\left(\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}\right)(x,t)\right|.

Using Theorem
3.5
we conclude that the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(C_{L}(\omega_{0})+tC_{L}(G)\right)\left(t+\sqrt{t}\right)\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},r\leq t}\left|u_{\delta,R}(y+\xi,r)-u_{\delta,R}(y,r)\right|.

Lemma
3.8
implies that for some constant
C(T_{K})>0
the first term is bounded by

\displaystyle C(T_{K})\sup_{s\leq T_{K}}\Big(\left\|\omega_{0}(\xi-\cdot)-\omega_{0}(\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\omega_{0}(\xi-\cdot)-\omega_{0}(\cdot)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|G(\xi-\cdot,s)-G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|G(\xi-\cdot,s)-G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{K})\sup_{s\leq T_{K}}\left(\left\|\nabla\omega_{0}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\nabla\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\nabla G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\nabla G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{1}\right)\left|\xi\right|.

Thus for some small
T>0
such that
\left(C_{L}(\omega_{0})+TC_{L}(G)\right)\left(T+\sqrt{T}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, our claim immediately follows.
∎

Lemma
3.9
in particular also holds
for
\delta=0
and
R=+\infty
and thus it proves the Lipschitz
continuity for the true fluid velocity
u
as well. As Lipschitz
continuity also implies differentiability (almost everywhere) with
bounded derivatives, Lemma
3.9
proves
a certain regularity of
u
.

4
Convergence of Random Vortex Schemes

In this section we prove the convergence of the FMCRV scheme, an analogous proof shows the convergence of the other schemes (PMCRV, PiCRV) described in Section
2
.

To state our main result in this section, we shall repeat some definitions to establish the notation which will be used in this section.

Let
\delta>0
and
R>0
be two parameters. Then the (field-valued)
McKean–Vlasov stochastic differential equations

\mathrm{d}X_{\delta,R}(x,t)=u_{\delta,R}(X_{\delta,R}(x,t),t)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\penalty\ X_{\delta,R}(x,0)=x

and

\displaystyle u_{\delta,R}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,t)-x)\right]\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\;+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,t)-x)G(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,s),s)\right]\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
, where
B
is a two-dimensional
Brownian motion on some probability space.

Let
h>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
are given. The Monte Carlo approximation

\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}
is defined via the following system

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h^{2}\chi_{R}(jh)K_{\delta}(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)-x)

\displaystyle\times\left(\omega_{0}(jh)+\int_{0}^{t}G(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,s),s)\textrm{d}s\right)

and

\mathrm{d}\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)=\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t),t)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B_{t}^{k},\quad\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,0)=jh

where
k=1,\ldots,N
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

and
t\geq 0
. Here
B^{k}
(
k=1,2,\ldots
) are independent copies
of two-dimensional Brownian motion.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and
R>0
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\rightarrow u_{\delta,R}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}\rightarrow X_{\delta,R}

(4.1)

as
N\rightarrow\infty
and
h\rightarrow 0
.

We divide the proof into several steps. The proof uses a standard
synchronous coupling approach. First we may run independent copies

X_{\delta,R}^{k}
(for
k=1,2,\ldots)
of
X_{\delta,R}
by solving the SDE:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{\delta,R}^{k}(x,t)=u_{\delta,R}(X_{\delta,R}^{k},t)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{k},\penalty\ \penalty\ X_{\delta,R}^{k}(x,0)=x

(4.2)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
, where
u_{\delta,R}
is
the unique fixed point to
\mathcal{K}\left(K_{\delta},\chi_{R},\omega_{0},G\right)\diamond u_{\delta,R}=u_{\delta,R}

found in Corollary
3.6
in
Section
3
. Indeed
u_{\delta,R}

is the unique solution to (
4.2
) together with

\displaystyle u_{\delta,R}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(\eta,t)-x)\right]\omega_{0}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\;+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(\eta,t)-x)G(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(\eta,s),s)\right]\chi_{R}(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

for every
k
. The solution
u_{\delta,R}
is independent of
k
.

Remark
.

Note that since we are only dealing with additive noise, we can transform
the SDEs that we consider into random ODEs, where the randomness comes
from the Brownian motions
\left(B^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Thus the random field
\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}
,
\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

and
\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}
are obtained by deterministic transformations
of
\left(B^{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Next we define the Monte-Carlo velocity computed from (
4.2
) by

\begin{split}u_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)&:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}K_{\delta}(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x)\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\\
&\;+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}K_{\delta}(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x)G(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,s),s)\chi_{R}(jh)h^{2}\textrm{d}s.\end{split}

(4.3)

With this notation we note that the error in the approximation of
the vortex dynamics can be decomposed into two sources, the stability
of the evolution under empirical dynamics and the inconsistency in
the empirical approximation of the true velocity, as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(i,t)&-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(i,s)-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s)\right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\underbrace{\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(i,s),s\right)-u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s),s\right)}_{(\mathrm{I})\penalty\ \text{stability error}}\mathrm{d}s\\
&\;+\int_{0}^{t}\underbrace{u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s),s\right)-u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s),s\right)}_{(\mathrm{II})\penalty\ \text{consistency error}}\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(4.4)

4.1
Stability error

First we bound the stability error
\left(\mathrm{I}\right)
, i.e.
the difference between the empirical velocity of the particle approximation and the empirical velocity computed from independent samples of the mean-field dynamics

\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{l}(i,t),t\right)-u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t),t\right)\right|.

We note that this can be bounded above by the following three terms

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigl|K_{\delta}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)-\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{l}(i,t)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)\right)\Bigr|\left|\omega_{0}(jh)\right|h^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigl|K_{\delta}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,t)-\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{l}(i,t)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)\right)\Bigr|G\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,s),s\right)\chi_{R}(jh)h^{2}\textrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,s)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left|G\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,s),s\right)-G\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,s),s\right)\right|\chi_{R}(jh)h^{2}\textrm{d}s.

Since
K_{\delta}
is Lipschitz continuous and bounded for fixed

\delta>0
, this implies that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{l}(i,t),t\right)-u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t),t\right)\right|\right]

is bounded by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(2\mathrm{Lip}\left(K_{\delta}\right)\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left|\omega_{0}(jh)\right|h^{2}+t\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\chi(jh)h^{2}\right)+2t\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{Lip}\left(G\right)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\chi(jh)h^{2}\right)&\\
\times\sup_{s\leq t,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,s)-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,s)\right|\right]&\end{split}

for
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
t\in[0,T]
.

This estimate is still dependent on the spatial discretisation parameter
h
, due to the sums over the discrete grid. To eliminate this dependence we make use of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
f\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}\right)

with compact support, the following estimate

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}f(jh)h^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}\max_{i=1,2}\left\|\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}f\right\|_{1}h^{2}

holds.

Proof.

See
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

As an immediate consequence we derive for
\omega_{0}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left|\omega_{0}(jh)\right|h^{2}\leq\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}h^{2}\left\|D^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}.\end{split}

(4.5)

Furthermore, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\chi_{R}(jh)h^{2}\leq\pi(R+1+h)^{2}

since
\chi_{R}(z)=0
for all
|z|>R+1
.

Thus, if we define the constant
C_{\mathrm{stab}}=C_{\mathrm{stab}}(\delta,R,T,\omega_{0},G)
by the following

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\mathrm{Lip}\left(K_{\delta}\right)\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}h^{2}\left\|D^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)+2\mathrm{Lip}\left(K_{\delta}\right)T\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}\pi(R+1+h)^{2}&\\
+2T\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{Lip}\left(G\right)\pi(R+1+h)^{2}&,\end{split}

(4.6)

we derive the stability estimate for
t\leq T

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{s\leq t,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta}^{k}(j,s),s\right)-u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta}^{k}(jh,s),s\right)\right|\right]\\
&\leq C_{\mathrm{stab}}\sup_{s\leq t,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta}^{k}(j,s)-X_{\delta}^{k}(jh,s)\right|\right].\end{split}

(4.7)

As an immediate consequence we derive from (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t,i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(i,s)-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s)\right|\right]

is bounded above by

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{stab}}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{r\leq s,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(j,r)-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,r)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s),s\right)-u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,s),s\right)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}s,

and therefore by

\displaystyle e^{TC_{\mathrm{stab}}}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,t),t\right)-u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,t),t\right)\right|\right]\mathrm{d}t,

(4.8)

as follows from the Grönwall Lemma.

Thus it only remains to bound the inconsistency of the empirical velocity approximation.

4.2
Consistency error

The consistency error can be split up into two parts: an error in the law of large numbers (i.e. the difference between the real and the empirical mean) and a discretisation error. First we investigate the law of large numbers for the homogeneous part of the empirical velocity. Note that by the tower property of the conditional expectation and the boundedness of
K_{\delta}
, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)\right)\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\right]_{x=X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)}\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\right.\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\left.\left.\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\right]\right|\penalty\ \right|\penalty\ X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)=x\right]\right]

and bound it above by

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Biggr[\mathbb{E}\Biggr[\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\neq l}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)\right]\Bigg|\penalty\ \Bigg|\penalty\ X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)=x\Biggr]\Biggr]\left|\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}h^{2}\left\|D^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)}{N}.

Next we note that by the independence of the samples
\left(X^{k}\right)_{k=1,\ldots,N}
,
this can be estimated by

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Biggr[\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\neq l}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-x\right)\right]\Bigg|\Biggr]\left|\omega_{0}(jh)h^{2}\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}h^{2}\left\|D^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{N-1}}{N}\right)\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}h^{2}\left\|D^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)}{\sqrt{N-1}}.

Hereby the last inequality follows from the standard estimation of
the law of large numbers using the variance, together with the boundedness of
K_{\delta}
.

Similarly we can bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)\right)G\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,s),s\right)\chi_{R}(jh)h^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\left.\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}(jh,t)-x\right)G\left(X_{\delta,R}(jh,s),s\right)\chi_{R}(jh)h^{2}\right]\right|_{x=X_{\delta,R}^{l}(ih,t)}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(1+1/\sqrt{N}\right)\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}(R+1)^{2}\pi}{\sqrt{N-1}}

.

With this in mind we define the constant
C_{\mathrm{LLN}}=C_{\mathrm{LLN}}(\delta,R,T,\omega_{0},G)
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}4&\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}h^{2}\left\|D^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+T\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}(R+1)^{2}\pi\right)\end{split}

and derive that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{l}(jh,t)\right)\\
&\qquad\times\left(\omega_{0}(jh)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X_{\delta,R}^{k}(jh,s),s\right)\mathrm{d}s\chi_{R}(jh)\right)h^{2}\\
&\quad-\mathbb{E}\Biggr[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}(jh,t)-x\right)\biggl(\omega_{0}(jh)\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X_{\delta,R}(jh,s),s\right)\mathrm{d}s\chi_{R}(jh)\biggl)h^{2}\Biggr]\Bigg|_{x=X_{\delta,R}^{l}(jh,t)}\Bigg|\Biggr]\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{LLN}}}{\sqrt{N}}.\end{split}

Next we need to estimate the consistency error of the spatial discretisation using Lemma
4.2
. To
this end, we first note that for every
i=1,2
and
k=1,2

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}^{k}u_{\delta,R}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{x_{i}}^{k}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,t)-x\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,s),s\right)\mathrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}\eta,

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\partial_{x_{i}}^{k}u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|\leq\left\|\partial_{x_{i}}^{k}K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+T\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}(R+1)^{2}\pi\right),

(4.9)

for
i=1,2
and
k=1,2
.

Next we note that since

\displaystyle X_{\delta,R}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}(x,s),s\right)\mathrm{d}s+\sqrt{2\nu}B_{t},

we derive, using the Einstein sum convention,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}X_{\delta,R}(x,t)=e_{i}+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}(x,s),s\right)\partial_{x_{i}}X_{\delta,R}^{k}(x,s)\mathrm{d}s

and

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}X_{\delta,R}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x_{k}x_{l}}^{2}u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}(x,s),s\right)\partial_{x_{i}}X_{\delta,R}^{k}(x,s)\partial_{x_{j}}X_{\delta,R}^{l}(x,s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\;+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x_{k}}u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta,R}(x,s),s\right)\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}X_{\delta,R}(x,s)\mathrm{d}s.

As a consequence we note that by the Grönwall Lemma

\displaystyle\left\|DX_{\delta,R}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|Du_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right),

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|D^{2}X_{\delta,R}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\exp\left(3\int_{0}^{t}\left\|Du_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right)\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D^{2}u_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{\eta_{i}}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}(\cdot,t)-x\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|DK_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|Du_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{\eta_{i}\eta_{i}}^{2}K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}(\cdot,t)-x\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|DK_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|DK_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D^{2}u_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left(3\int_{0}^{t}\left\|Du_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right).

Then, setting

\displaystyle f(\eta):=\mathbb{E}\left[K_{\delta}\left(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,t)-x\right)\left(\omega_{0}(\eta)+\int_{0}^{t}G\left(X_{\delta,R}(\eta,s),s\right)\mathrm{d}s\penalty\ \chi_{R}(\eta)\right)\right],

its second derivative
\partial_{\eta_{i}\eta_{i}}^{2}f(\eta)
is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|DK_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|D^{2}K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|D^{2}u_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;\exp\left(6\int_{0}^{t}\left\|Du_{\delta,R}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right)\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|DG(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|D^{2}G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\;\left(\left|\omega_{0}(\eta)\right|+\left|\partial_{\eta_{i}}\omega_{0}(\eta)\right|+\left|\partial_{\eta_{i}\eta_{i}}^{2}\omega_{0}(\eta)\right|+\left|\chi_{R}(\eta)\right|+\left|\partial_{\eta_{i}}\chi_{R}(\eta)\right|+\left|\partial_{\eta_{i}\eta_{i}}^{2}\chi_{R}(\eta)\right|\right).

Using Lemma
4.2
and the bound
(
4.9
) for the derivatives of
u_{\delta,R}
, we therefore can bound the discretisation error

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}f(jh)h^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\right|\leq\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}\max_{i=1,2}\left\|\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}f\right\|_{1}h^{2}\leq C_{\mathrm{dis}}h^{2},

(4.10)

where the constant
C_{\mathrm{dis}}=C_{\mathrm{dis}}(\delta,R,t,\omega_{0},G)
is given by the following expression

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{52}{(2\pi)^{2}}\max_{i=1,2}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\partial_{\eta_{i}}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\partial_{\eta_{i}\eta_{i}}^{2}\omega_{0}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\chi_{R}(\eta)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\partial_{\eta_{i}}\chi_{R}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\partial_{\eta_{i}\eta_{i}}^{2}\chi_{R}\right\|_{1}\right)\\
&\exp\left(6t\left\|DK_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+t\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}(R+1)^{2}\pi\right)\right)\biggl(1+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(\left\|G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|DG(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\\
&+\left\|D^{2}G(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\Bigl)\mathrm{d}s\biggl)\left(\left\|K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|DK_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|D^{2}K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\bigl(1+t\left\|D^{2}K_{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\\
&\left(\left\|\omega_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+t\left\|G\right\|_{\infty}(R+1)^{2}\pi\right)\bigl)^{2}.\end{split}

Note that this bound is independent of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
! Thus
for the consistency error we derive the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T,i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta}^{l}(ih,t),t\right)-u_{\delta,R}\left(X_{\delta}^{l}(ih,t),t\right)\right|\right]\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{LLN}}}{\sqrt{N}}+C_{\mathrm{dis}}h^{2}.\end{split}

(4.11)

4.3
Total error of interacting vortex dynamics and their empirical velocities

Combining the inconsistency (
4.11
) with (
4.8
), we thus derive the following error estimate for the Monte-Carlo approximation
of the (regularised and localised) vorticity dynamics

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T,i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},k\leq N}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(i,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,t)\right|\right]\leq Te^{TC_{\mathrm{stab}}}\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{LLN}}}{\sqrt{N}}+C_{\mathrm{dis}}h^{2}\right).\end{split}

(4.12)

Remark
.

Note that (
4.12
) only gives us pointwise convergence of
\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}
to
X_{\delta,R}^{k}
and not the usual pathwise convergence that is often investigated in Propagation of Chaos results. However, pathwise convergence of the particles is actually not needed to show the convergence of the computed Monte Carlo velocity.

The estimate (
4.12
) can in turn
be used to bound the error of the Monte Carlo approximation to the
(regularised and localised) velocity
u_{\delta,R}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|\right],

as the same calculations that were used to derive the key stability estimate (
4.7
) give us the following upper bound

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)-u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(x,t\right)\right|\right]+\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(x,t\right)-u_{\delta,R}\left(x,t\right)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{stab}}\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{X}_{N,h,\delta,R}^{k}(i,t)-X_{\delta,R}^{k}(ih,t)\right|\right]+\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{N,h,\delta,R}\left(x,t\right)-u_{\delta,R}\left(x,t\right)\right|\right].

The second term quantifies the inconsistency of the empirical velocity,
it can be estimated just as in Subsection
4.2
. Together with the bounds for the error of the dynamics (
4.12
) we thus derive the error bound

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|\right]\leq\left(TC_{\mathrm{stab}}e^{TC_{\mathrm{stab}}}+1\right)\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{LLN}}}{\sqrt{N}}+C_{\mathrm{dis}}h^{2}\right).\end{split}

(4.13)

By the Markov inequality, this indeed gives uniform stochastic guarantees,
in the sense that the following tail bound holds for all
t\leq T

and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|>\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(TC_{\mathrm{stab}}e^{TC_{\mathrm{stab}}}+1\right)\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{LLN}}}{\sqrt{N}}+C_{\mathrm{dis}}h^{2}\right).

Combining (
4.13
) with
the robustness result in Lemma
3.7

shows that
\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}
approximates
u
pointwise.
Let us formally state this fact in the following Theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

For all
\delta,R>0
, there
exist appropriately chosen
N(\delta,R)\in\mathbb{N}
and
h(\delta,R)>0
,
with

\displaystyle N(\delta,R)\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0,R\to\infty}\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad h(\delta,R)\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0,R\to\infty}0

such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\geq N(\delta,R)\\
h\leq h(\delta,R)\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leq T\\
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\end{subarray}}\penalty\ \mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|\right]\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0,R\to\infty}0.

(4.14)

The proof of Theorem
4.3
is a trivial
combination of the estimate (
4.13
) and Lemma
3.7
. It shows that if the number of particles
N
and the grid size
h
are appropriately large/small in relation to the regularisation and cutoff parameters
\delta
and
R
, then our Monte-Carlo velocity
\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}
converges to the true velocity. Using the concrete form of
C_{\mathrm{stab}},C_{\mathrm{LLN}}
and
C_{\mathrm{dis}}
as defined in this section also would allow us to bound
N(h,\delta)
and
h(\delta,R)
and give concrete convergence rates in (
4.14
). However these bounds are far from optimal and for the sake of (notational) simplicity we refrain from computing them.

Remark
.

The estimate (
4.13
) only gives us a pointwise convergence of the Monte Carlo approximation
\hat{u}_{N,h,\delta,R}
to
u_{\delta,R}
. To derive uniform convergence one would need a suitable law of large numbers that is uniform in time and space, i.e. one would need a suitable estimate of

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|u_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)-\mathbb{E}\left[u_{N,h,\delta,R}(x,t)\right]\right|

that converges to zero for
N\to\infty
in a suitable probabilistic
sense. Such uniform laws of large numbers have been achieved in various
works in the context of kernel density estimation and statistics on
separable Banach spaces. In the same manner one proves the convergence
of the PiCRV scheme. The only difference is that in the consistency
estimate one uses a law of large numbers for non identically but independent
random variables, just as in the derivation of the scheme in Section

2
. This then gives the error estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tilde{u}_{h,\delta,R}(x,t)-u_{\delta,R}(x,t)\right|\right]\leq C\left(Ce^{TC}\penalty\ T+1\right)\left(h+h^{2}\right),\end{split}

(4.15)

for some constant
C=C(\delta,R,T,\omega_{0},G)>0
. Note that the term
of order
h
comes from the estimate of the standard deviation of
the discretised velocity!

5
Incompressible viscous flows past a wall

In the next sections we consider incompressible viscous flows past
a solid wall. We aim to develop Monte-Carlo method for such flows
based on a random vortex formulation which will be established in
this section.

Suppose the flow with viscosity constant
\nu>0
is constrained in
the upper half space
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
where
x_{d}>0
, so
that the solid wall is modelled by the hyperspace
\partial D
where

x_{d}=0
. Let
V(x,t)
, for
x\in D
and
t\geq 0
, be a time-dependent
vector field, which is bounded and differentiable up to the boundary

\partial D
, satisfying
V(x,t)=0
for
x\in\partial D
and
for
t\geq 0
.
V(x,t)
is extended to the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}

via reflection, so that

V^{i}(x,t)=V^{i}(\bar{x},t)\quad\textrm{ for }i=1,\ldots,d-1\textrm{ and }V^{d}(x,t)=-V^{d}(\bar{x},t).

(5.1)

Let
L_{V}=\nu\Delta+V\cdot\nabla
which is an elliptic operator
of second order on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
p_{V}^{D}(\tau,\xi;t,x)

denote the transition probability density function of the
L_{V}
-diffusion
killed on leaving the domain
D
, which is the Green function of

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L_{V}
on
D
satisfying the Dirichlet
condition, cf.
[
4
]
and
[
14
]

for example.

From now on we assume that
\nabla\cdot V(x,t)=0
for
x_{d}>0
.
Then the extension of
V(x,t)
via the reflection about

x_{d}=0
is divergence-free in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
\nabla\cdot V(\cdot,t)=0

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the distribution sense. Since
L_{V}^{\star}=L_{-V}
,

p_{V}^{D}(\tau,\xi;t,x)
is the Green function of
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-L_{-V}

on
D
subject to the Dirichlet boundary condition. Since
V(\bar{x},t)=\overline{V(x,t)}

for all
x
and
t\geq 0
,
p_{V}(\tau,\xi;t,x)=p_{V}(\tau,\bar{\xi};t,\bar{x})

for
\xi,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\textrm{ and }t>\tau\geq 0
, and

p_{V}^{D}(\tau,\xi;t,x)=p_{V}(\tau,\xi;t,x)-p_{V}(\tau,\xi;t,\bar{x})

(5.2)

for
\xi,x\in D\textrm{ and }t>\tau\geq 0
.

Let
f(x,t)
be a solution to the initial and boundary value problem
of the parabolic equation

\left(L_{-V}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)f+g=0\textrm{ in }D\times[0,\infty),\quad f(\cdot,0)=\varphi,

(5.3)

subject to the Dirichlet boundary condition that

\left.f(x,t)\right|_{\partial D}=0\quad\textrm{ for }x\in\partial D\;\textrm{ and }t>0,

(5.4)

where
g(x,t)
is a continuous and bounded function on
D\times[0,\infty)
.

We next derive a stochastic integral representation theorem for solutions to the problem (
5.3
), (
5.4
). Let
X^{\xi}=(X_{t}^{\xi})_{t\geq 0}
(for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
be the solution to SDE:

\textrm{d}X_{t}=V(X_{t},t)\textrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B_{t},\quad X_{0}=\xi,

(5.5)

where
(B_{t})
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on
some probability space.

Theorem 5.1
.

The solution
f(x,t)
to the problem (
5.3
), (
5.4
) possesses the following stochastic integral representation

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(x,t)&=\int_{D}\left(p_{V}(0,\eta;t,x)-p_{V}(0,\bar{\eta};t,x)\right)\varphi(\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})\}}g(X_{s}^{\eta},s)\right|X_{t}^{\eta}=x\right]p_{V}(0,\eta;t,x)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\end{split}

(5.6)

for
t>0
and
x\in D
, where
\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})=\sup\{s\in(0,t):X_{s}^{\eta}\in D\}
respectively.

Proof.

The proof relies on the duality of conditional laws of diffusions,
established in
[
11
]
. Take arbitrary fixed
t>0
.
For simplicity
\tilde{X}_{s}^{\xi}
denotes the unique (weak) solution

\tilde{X}(t,\xi;s)
to SDE

\textrm{d}\tilde{X}=-V^{t}(\tilde{X},s)\textrm{d}s+\sqrt{2\nu}\textrm{d}B,\quad\tilde{X}(t,x;0)=x.

(5.7)

Let
Y_{s}=f(\tilde{X}_{s\wedge T_{\xi}}^{\xi},t-s)=1_{\{s<T_{\xi}\}}f(\tilde{X}_{s}^{\xi},t-s)

for
s\leq t
, where
T_{\xi}=\inf\left\{s:\tilde{X}_{s}^{\xi}\notin D\right\}

and the second equality follows from the assumption that
f(x,s)
vanishes
at the boundary
\partial D
. By Itô’s formula and (
5.3
), one has

Y_{s}=Y_{0}+\sqrt{2\nu}\int_{0}^{s}1_{\{r<T_{\xi}\}}\nabla f(\tilde{X}_{r}^{\xi},t-r)\cdot\textrm{d}B_{r}-\int_{0}^{s}1_{\{r<T_{\xi}\}}g(\tilde{X}_{r}^{\xi},t-r)\textrm{d}r

(5.8)

for every
s\in[0,t]
. After taking expectation both sides we may
conclude that

f(\xi,t)=\mathbb{E}\left[f(\tilde{X}_{t}^{\xi},0)1_{\{t<T_{\xi}\}}\right]+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\{s<T_{\xi}\}}g(\tilde{X}_{s}^{\xi},t-s)\right]\textrm{d}s.

Note that
T_{\xi}
is a stopping time with respect to the filtration
generated by
\tilde{X}
, so that
1_{\{s<T_{\xi}\}}
is therefore
measurable with respect to
\tilde{X}
running up to time
s\leq t
.
Therefore we may take conditional expectation on
\tilde{X}_{t}=\eta
,
to obtain that

\displaystyle f(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{t<T_{\xi}\}}\right|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\xi}=\eta\right]\varphi(\eta)p_{-V^{t}}(0,\xi;t,\eta)\textrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{s<T_{\xi}\}}g(\tilde{X}_{s}^{\xi},t-s)\right|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\xi}=\eta\right]p_{-V^{t}}(0,\xi;t,\eta)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

for every
\xi\in D
, where
p_{-V^{t}}(\tau,\xi;s,\eta)
is the
transition probability density function of the diffusion
\tilde{X}^{\xi}
.
Since
\nabla\cdot V=0
, we can replace
p_{-V^{t}}(0,\xi;t,\eta)

by
p_{V}(0,\eta;t,\xi)
. Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\xi,t)&=\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{t<T_{\xi}\}}\right|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\xi}=\eta\right]\varphi(\eta)p_{V}(0,\eta;t,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{s<T_{\xi}\}}g(\tilde{X}_{s}^{\xi},t-s)\right|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\xi}=\eta\right]p_{V}(0,\eta;t,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\end{split}

(5.9)

for every
\xi\in D
and
t>0
. Next we utilise the duality of conditional
laws established in
[
11
]
and rewrite the conditional
expectations in terms of the diffusion process
X^{\xi}
(starting
from
\xi
at
\tau=0
) whose infinitesimal generator is
\nu\Delta+V\cdot\nabla
.
Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\xi,t)&=\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{t<\zeta(X^{\eta})\}}\right|X_{t}^{\eta}=\xi\right]\varphi(\eta)p_{V}(0,\eta;t,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})\}}g_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{\eta},s)\right|X_{t}^{\eta}=\xi\right]p_{V}(0,\eta;t,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\end{split}

(5.10)

where
\zeta(X^{\eta})=\inf\{s:X^{\eta}\notin D\}
and
\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})=\sup\{s\in(0,t):X_{s}^{\eta}\in D\}
, which completes
the proof.
∎

The Biot-Savart law for
D
may be derived from the Green formula
and integration by parts. While the Green function for
D
, satisfying
the Dirichlet condition at
\partial D
, can be identified as

\varGamma_{D}(y,x)=\varGamma(y,x)-\varGamma(y,\overline{x})\quad\textrm{ for }y\neq x\textrm{ or }\overline{x},

(5.11)

where
\varGamma
is the Green function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For the case where
d=2
, the Biot-Savart law can be stated as
follows. Suppose
u\in C^{2}(D)\cap C^{1}(\overline{D})
,
\left.u\right|_{\partial D}=0
,

u\rightarrow 0
and
|\nabla u|\rightarrow 0
at the infinity, and

\nabla\cdot u=0
in
D
. Let
\omega=\nabla\wedge u
and assume
that both
\omega,\nabla\omega\in L^{1}(D)
. Then

u(x)=\int_{D}K(x,y)\omega(y)\textrm{d}y\quad\forall x\in D,

(5.12)

where

K(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{(x-y)^{\bot}}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{(\bar{x}-y)^{\bot}}{|\overline{x}-y|^{2}}\right)

(5.13)

(for
y\neq x
or
\overline{x}
) is the Biot-Savart kernel.

We are now in a position to establish the random vortex formulation
for two-dimensional flows past a solid wall. Therefore from now on
we assume that
d=2
. The fluid flow is described by its velocity
vector field
u(x,t)
, which satisfies the Navier-Stokes equations:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\nu\Delta u+\nabla P-F=0\quad\textrm{ in }D

(5.14)

and

\nabla\cdot u=0\quad\textrm{ in }D,

(5.15)

subject to the non-slip condition
u(x,t)=0
for
x\in\partial D
,
where
P(x,t)
is the pressure,
F(x,t)
is an external force, and

\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity constant of the fluid. Recall
that
u(x,t)
is extended to the whole space so that
u^{1}(x,t)=u^{1}(\overline{x},t)

and
u^{2}(x,t)=-u^{2}(\overline{x},t)
.

The vorticity
\omega=\nabla\wedge u
evolves according to the transport
equation:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-\nu\Delta\omega-G=0\quad\textrm{ in }D,

(5.16)

where
G=\nabla\wedge F
. Since
u(x,t)
is subject to the non-slip
condition, according to the Biot-Savart law,

u(x,t)=\int_{D}K(x,y)\omega(y,t)\textrm{d}y\quad\forall x\in D,

(5.17)

where the singular integral kernel
K(y,x)
is given by (
5.13
). The vorticity
\omega(x,t)
vanishes at the infinity, while
\omega(x,t)
in general may assume non-trivial values at the rigid wall
\partial D
, and boundary vorticity is given by

\theta(x_{1},t):=-\frac{\partial u^{1}}{\partial x_{2}}(x_{1},0,t)\quad\textrm{ for }x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\textrm{ and }t\geq 0.

Let us introduce a family of perturbations of the vorticity
\omega

modified near the boundary using a cut-off function, so that the
modified vorticity vanishes along the boundary
\partial D
. More
precisely
\theta
is extended to the interior of
D
as

\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2},t)=\theta(x_{1},t)\phi(x_{2}/\varepsilon),

(5.18)

for each
\varepsilon>0
, where
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]

is a proper cut-off function to be chosen later, and
\phi
is smooth,
such that
\phi(r)=1
for
r\in[0,1/3)
and
\phi(r)=0
for
r\geq 2/3
,
so that
\left.\omega\right|_{\partial D}=\left.\sigma_{\varepsilon}\right|_{\partial D}

for every
\varepsilon>0
. Let
W^{\varepsilon}=\omega-\sigma_{\varepsilon}
, then

\left(L_{-u}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)W_{\varepsilon}+g_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\textrm{ in }D\quad\textrm{ and }\left.W_{\varepsilon}\right|_{\partial D}=0,

(5.19)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{\varepsilon}(x,t)&=G(x,t)+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2}/\varepsilon)\theta(x_{1},t)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\phi^{\prime}(x_{2}/\varepsilon)u^{2}(x,t)\theta(x_{1},t)\\
&+\phi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)\left(\nu\frac{\partial^{2}\theta}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}(x_{1},t)-\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}(x_{1},t)\right)-\phi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)u^{1}(x,t)\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x_{1}}(x_{1},t)\end{split}

(5.20)

for any
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
x_{2}\geq 0
. The initial data for
W^{\varepsilon}

is given by

W_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\omega_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})-\omega_{0}(x_{1},0)\phi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)\quad\textrm{ for }x\in D

where
\omega_{0}=\nabla\wedge u_{0}
is the initial vorticity.

Theorem 5.2
.

The following representation holds:

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(x,t)&=\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{D}(X_{t}^{\xi})K(x,X_{t}^{\xi})-1_{D}(X_{t}^{\overline{\xi}})K(x,X_{t}^{\overline{\xi}})\right]\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})\}}1_{D}(X_{t}^{\xi})K(x,X_{t}^{\xi})G(X_{s}^{\xi},s)\right]\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s\\
&+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})\}}1_{D}(X_{t}^{\xi})K(x,X_{t}^{\xi},x)\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{\xi},s)\right]\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s\end{split}

(5.21)

for every
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in D
,
t>0
, and

u^{1}(x,t)=u^{1}(\overline{x},t),\quad u^{2}(x,t)=-u^{2}(\overline{x},t)

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
with
x_{2}>0
,
t>0
, where

\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\chi(x_{2}/\varepsilon)\theta(x_{1},t)\quad\textrm{ for }x=(x_{1},x_{2})

\gamma_{t}(\psi)
denotes
\sup\left\{s\in(0,t):\psi(s)\in D\right\}

and

\chi(r)=\begin{cases}162\left(2r-1\right)&\textrm{ for }r\in[1/3,2/3],\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(5.22)

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, the deformed vorticity
W_{\varepsilon}

satisfies the parabolic equation (
5.19
):

\left(L_{-u}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)W_{\varepsilon}+g_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\textrm{ in }D

(5.23)

subject to the Dirichlet boundary condition
\left.W_{\varepsilon}\right|_{\partial D}=0.

According to Theorem
5.1

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{\varepsilon}(\xi,t)&=\int_{D}\left(p_{u}(0,\eta,t,\xi)-p_{u}(0,\bar{\eta},t,\xi)\right)W_{\varepsilon}(\eta,0)\textrm{d}\eta\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})\}}g_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{\eta},s)\right|X_{t}^{\eta}=\xi\right]p_{u}(0,\eta;t,\xi)\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s\end{split}

(5.24)

where
\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})=\sup\{s\in(0,t):X_{s}^{\eta}\in D\}
, so by using the Biot-Savart law we obtain that

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}K(x,y)\sigma_{\varepsilon}(y,t)\textrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{D}(X_{t}^{\xi})K(x,X_{t}^{\xi})-K(x,X_{t}^{\overline{\xi}})1_{D}(X_{t}^{\overline{\xi}})\right]W_{\varepsilon}(\xi,0)\textrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\eta})\}}1_{D}(X_{t}^{\eta})K(x,X_{t}^{\eta})\wedge g_{\varepsilon}(X_{s}^{\eta},s)\right]\textrm{d}\eta\textrm{d}s

for every
x\in D
,
t>0
and for every
\varepsilon>0
. The result
follows by setting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.
∎

6
Numerical simulations of wall-bounded flows

Let us make several comments on the simulations of fluid flows passing a wall based on the functional integral representations we have established in previous sections. Then we describe several numerical schemes. Our numerical experiment is carried out with a simplified scheme to demonstrate the usefulness of the methods advocated in the present
work.

Let us recall the important notations.
D
represents the upper semi-plane where
x_{2}>0
. The reflection
x\mapsto\overline{x}
with
\overline{x}=(x_{1},-x_{2})
for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
, and
u(\overline{x},t)=(u^{1}(x,t),-u^{2}(x,t))
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

For a two-dimensional vector
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
and a scalar
c
,
a\wedge c=(a_{2}c,-a_{1}c)
.

Let us reserve two parameters
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
which will be used to realise a practical approximation based on the functional integral representation in Theorem
5.2
. Define

K_{D,\delta}(y,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}1_{D}(y)\left(1-e^{-|y-x|^{2}/\delta^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{(y-x)^{\bot}}{|y-x|^{2}}-\frac{(y-\overline{x})^{\bot}}{|y-\overline{x}|^{2}}\right),

the regularised Biot-Savart kernel for the half-plane. The regularisation
is required in order to update the boundary stress. We may introduce
the boundary regularised function

\chi(r)=\begin{cases}162(2r-1)&\textrm{ for }r\in[1/3,2/3],\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(6.1)

Then, according to Theorem
5.2
, the velocity will be computed by the following formula:

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{D,\delta}(x,X_{t}^{\xi})-K_{D,\delta}(x,X_{t}^{\overline{\xi}})\right]\omega_{0}(\xi)\textrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\xi})\}}K_{D,\delta}(x,X_{t}^{\xi})G(X_{s}^{\xi},s)\right]\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\frac{X_{2;s}^{\xi}}{\varepsilon}\right)1_{\{s>\gamma_{t}(X^{\xi})\}}K_{D,\delta}(x,X_{t}^{\xi})\theta(X_{1;s}^{\xi},s)\right]\textrm{d}\xi\textrm{d}s,

for every
x\in D
and
t>0
, where
\gamma_{t}(\psi)=\sup\left\{s\in(0,t):\psi(s)\in D\right\}
. In the above formula
X_{1;s}^{\xi}
and
X_{2;s}^{\xi}
denote the first and second coordinate of
X_{s}^{\xi}
respectively. In what follows, we will omit the coordinate index by denoting
\chi(x)=\chi(x_{2})
and
\theta(x,t)=\theta(x_{1},t)
.

Let us describe the numerical schemes based on the last model. We divide the schemes into two steps. During the first step we set up the discretisation procedure for dealing with (finite-dimensional) integrals in time and in the space variables. The discretisation of this type appears in any numerical methods, but a bit care is needed due to the appearance of boundary layer phenomena for wall-bounded flows. According to Prandtl
[
10
]
, there is a thin layer near the boundary within which the main stream velocity decreases to zero sharply, hence there is substantial stress at the wall, which in turn generates significant boundary vorticity. Turbulence may be generated near the solid wall if the Reynolds number is large. The boundary layer thickness
\delta>0
of a fluid flow with small viscosity is given by
\frac{\delta}{L}\sim\sqrt{\frac{1}{\textrm{Re}}}
, where
Re
is the Reynolds number
\textrm{Re}=\frac{VL}{\nu}
,

V
and
L
are the typical velocity magnitude and typical length.

In numerical simulations, we can always first make a reduction, to make the fluid dynamics equations dimensionless. That is, the typical velocity and length may be fixed to the good size for printing the results. While we still prefer to use the Reynolds number
Re
,
so that
\nu\sim\frac{1}{\textrm{Re}}
. Suppose
\nu
is small, then near the boundary, the mesh
h_{2}
for the
x_{2}
-coordinate has to be far smaller than the boundary layer thickness
\delta\sim\sqrt{\frac{1}{\textrm{Re}}}
, which leads to the first constraint:

h_{2}\ll L\sqrt{\frac{1}{\textrm{Re}}}.

(6.2)

The mesh size
h_{1}
for
x_{1}
-coordinate should be comparable to
h_{2}
but not need to be much larger than
h_{2}
. For the region outside the boundary layer, we can use reasonable size of the mesh
h_{0}
, and in general we choose
h_{0}\geq h_{1}\geq h_{2}
. We
may choose integers
N_{1}
,
N_{2}
and
N_{0}
according to the following constraints:

N_{1}h_{1}\sim L,\ \,L\geq\delta\quad\textrm{ and }N_{0}h_{0}\sim L.

(6.3)

However in order to capture the flow in the boundary layer,
N_{2}
has to be chosen such that
h_{2}N_{2}>\delta
.

We introduce regular rectangular lattices for the boundary and the outer layer, and calculate the values at the lattice points

\displaystyle x_{b}^{i_{1},i_{2}}=(i_{1}h_{1},i_{2}h_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\textrm{for }|i_{1}|\leq N_{1}\textrm{ and }|i_{2}|\leq N_{2};

\displaystyle x_{o}^{i_{1},i_{2}}=(i_{1}h_{0},i_{2}h_{0})

\displaystyle\quad\textrm{for }|i_{1}|\leq N_{0}\textrm{ and }|i_{2}|\leq N_{0},

which in the following will be written as
x^{i_{1},i_{2}}
to denote both the boundary and outer lattices.

Notice that the lattices are symmetric with respect to the second coordinate as we need the corresponding values for
X_{t}^{\overline{\xi}}
in the first integral in representation (
5.21
).
The total number of the lattice points needed for the lattices equals

N=(2N_{1}+1)(2N_{2}+1)+(2N_{0}+1)^{2},

which largely determines the computational cost.

Thus far we have described the discretisation of space variables. For time
t
, we can use a unified scheme, at each step we use time duration
h>0
to be chosen properly, with
t_{k}=kh
for
k=0,1,2,\ldots.
The Taylor diffusions (
5.5
) are initialised at the lattice points in the numerical scheme at time
t_{0}=0
. Then we propose a simple scheme to compute them at further times
t_{k}=kh
for
k=1,2,\ldots
— it is given by the discretisation of the SDE which can be set up as follows:

X_{0}^{i_{1},i_{2}}=x^{i_{1},i_{2}},

X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}=X_{t_{k}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}+hu(X_{t_{k}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{k})+\sqrt{2\nu}(B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}),

for
k=0,1,\ldots
, where
u(x,t)
are updated according to

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(x,t_{k+1})&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)-K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},-i_{2}}\right)\right]\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)G\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)\right]\\
&+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\mathbb{E}\Biggr[1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\chi\left(\frac{X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\theta\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)\Biggr],\end{split}

(6.4)

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
with
x_{2}>0
, and

(u^{1}(x,t),u^{2}(x,t))=(u^{1}(\overline{x},t),-u^{2}(\bar{x},t))\quad\textrm{ if }x_{2}<0.

In the above formula, the summation over indices
i_{1},i_{2}
is taken over both the boundary and outer lattice, and therefore

A_{i_{1},i_{2}}=\begin{cases}h_{1}h_{2}&\textrm{ for the sum over the boundary lattice},\\
h_{0}h_{0}&\textrm{ for the sum over the outer lattice},\end{cases}

(6.5)

and

\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}=\omega_{0}(x^{i_{1},i_{2}}),

(6.6)

where
\omega_{0}(x)=\omega(x,0)
.

The representation (
6.4
) depends on the boundary vorticity

\theta(x_{1},t)=-\left.\frac{\partial u^{1}(x,t)}{\partial x_{2}}\right|_{x_{2}=0+}

which can be, for instance, approximated by a finite difference. However, as the values of the velocity can be unstable within the boundary layer, in practice it is better to update the boundary stress by differentiating the iteration (
6.4
), so that the boundary stress is updated according to the following procedure:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta(x_{1},t_{k+1})&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)-\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},-i_{2}}\right)\right]\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\mathbb{E}\left[1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)G\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)\right]\\
&+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\mathbb{E}\Biggr[1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\chi\left(\frac{X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\theta\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)\Biggr],\end{split}

(6.7)

for
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, where

\varTheta_{D,\delta}(y,x_{1})=-\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right|_{x_{2}=0+}K_{D,\delta}^{1}(y,x).

We compute the derivative explicitly as follows

\displaystyle\varTheta_{D,\delta}(y,x_{1})=1_{\{y_{2}>0\}}(y)\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\left(1-\textrm{e}^{-\frac{|y_{1}-x_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}\right)\frac{|y_{1}-x_{1}|^{2}-|y_{2}|^{2}}{\left(|y_{1}-x_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}\right)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{|y_{1}-x_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}\frac{|y_{2}|^{2}}{|y_{1}-x_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}}\right).

In the second step, one has to handle the mathematical expectations in representations in (
6.4
) and (
6.7
), which is the core of the Monte-Carlo schemes. We propose two slightly different approaches which lead to the following schemes.

6.1
Numerical scheme 1

We are now in a position to formulate our first Monte-Carlo scheme in which we drop the expectation by running independent (two-dimensional) Brownian motions. More precisely, we run the following stochastic differential equations:

X_{0}^{i_{1},i_{2}}=x^{i_{1},i_{2}},

X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}=X_{t_{k}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}+hu(X_{t_{k}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{k})+\sqrt{2\nu}\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}\Phi_{k}

where

\Phi_{k}=\left(\Phi_{k}^{1},\Phi_{k}^{2}\right)\equiv\frac{B_{t_{k+1}}-B_{t_{k}}}{\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}},

with
\Phi_{k}^{1}
,
\Phi_{l}^{2}
(for
k,l=0,1,\ldots
) being
independent standard Gaussian random variables.
u(x,t)
is updated
according to the following:

\displaystyle u(x,t_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\left(K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)-K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},-i_{2}}\right)\right)\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)G\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\chi\left(\frac{X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\theta\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right),

and

\displaystyle\theta(x_{1},t_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\left(\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)-\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},-i_{2}}\right)\right)\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)G\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\chi\left(\frac{X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\theta\left(X_{t_{l}}^{i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right),

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
with
x_{2}>0
, and

(u^{1}(x,t),u^{2}(x,t))=(u^{1}(\overline{x},t),-u^{2}(\bar{x},t))\quad\textrm{ when }x_{2}<0.

6.2
Numerical scheme 2

In this scheme we appeal to the strong law of large numbers, that is we replace the expectations by averages. Therefore we run the
following stochastic differential equations:

X_{0}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}=x^{i_{1},i_{2}},

X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}=X_{t_{k}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}+hu(X_{t_{k}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}},t_{k})+\sqrt{2\nu}\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}\Phi_{k}^{m}

where

\Phi_{k}^{m}=\left(\Phi_{k}^{m,1},\Phi_{k}^{m,2}\right)\equiv\frac{B_{t_{k+1}}^{m}-B_{t_{k}}^{m}}{\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}},

and
\Phi_{k}^{m,1}
,
\Phi_{l}^{n,2}
(for
m,n=1,2,\ldots
and

k,l=0,1,\ldots
) are independent standard Gaussian random variables.
Here
u(x,t)
is updated according to the following:

\displaystyle u(x,t_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\left(K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)-K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},-i_{2}}\right)\right)\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)G\left(X_{t_{l}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\chi\left(\frac{X_{t_{l}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times K_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\theta\left(X_{t_{l}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right),

and

\displaystyle\theta(x_{1},t_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\left(\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)-\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},-i_{2}}\right)\right)\omega_{i_{1},i_{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)G\left(X_{t_{l}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\\
i_{2}>0\end{subarray}}A_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}h\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}1_{\left\{t_{l}>\gamma_{t_{k}}\left(X^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\right\}}\chi\left(\frac{X_{t_{l}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\varTheta_{D,\delta}\left(x,X_{t_{k+1}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}}\right)\theta\left(X_{t_{l}}^{m;i_{1},i_{2}},t_{l}\right),

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
with
x_{2}>0
, and

(u^{1}(x,t),u^{2}(x,t))=(u^{1}(\overline{x},t),-u^{2}(\bar{x},t))\quad\textrm{ when }x_{2}<0.

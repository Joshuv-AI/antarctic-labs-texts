Homotopy types of diffeomorphism groups of polar Morse-Bott foliations on lens spaces, 1
Oleksandra Khokhliuk, Sergiy Maksymenko

Abstract
. Let T = S 1 × D 2 T=S^{1}\times D^{2} be the solid torus, ℱ \mathcal{F} the Morse-Bott foliation on T T into 2 2 -tori parallel to the boundary and one singular circle S 1 × 0 S^{1}\times 0 , which is the central circle of the torus T T , and 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ , ∂ T ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F},\partial T) the group of diffeomorphisms of T T fixed on ∂ T \partial T and leaving each leaf of the foliation ℱ \mathcal{F} invariant. We prove that 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ , ∂ T ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F},\partial T) is contractible. Gluing two copies of T T by some diffeomorphism between their boundaries, we will get a lens space L p , q L_{p,q} with a Morse-Bott foliation ℱ p , q \mathcal{F}_{p,q} obtained from ℱ \mathcal{F} on each copy of T T . We also compute the homotopy type of the group 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ p , q ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q}) of diffeomorphisms of L p , q L_{p,q} leaving invariant each leaf of ℱ p , q \mathcal{F}_{p,q} .

1.
Introduction

One of the main features and applications of algebraic topology and, especially, homotopy theory, to real world problems is that very often homotopy invariants of ‘‘good’’ spaces are discrete and can be described as certain properties ‘‘stable under small perturbations’’.

On the other hand, the concrete computation of homotopy types can be done in not so many cases.
Actually, the homotopy type of a CW-complex
X
is, in principle, determined by Postnikov tower: one can associate to
X
a certain ‘‘canonical’’ representative
\bar{X}
being homotopy equivalent to
X
, see
[
25
, §4.3, p. 410]
.
However, that construction is more theoretical, since
\bar{X}
is usually infinite dimensional, and to construct
\bar{X}
one need to know all the homotopy groups of
X
, which we currently do not completely know even for spheres.
Real computations of homotopy types usually are done by other methods, like theory of coverings or fibre bundles described in standard textbooks, e.g.
[
25
]
.

For infinite dimensional spaces, e.g. spaces of maps (infinite dimensional manifolds), the situation is more complicated.
It is known that for a smooth compact manifold
X
its group of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms
\mathcal{D}(X)
endowed with
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
Whitney topology is a Fréchet manifold, and therefore has the homotopy type of a CW-complex, see
[
64
]
for discussion of homotopy types of infinite dimensional manifolds.
Homotopy types of path components of
\mathcal{D}(X)
are completely computed for all compact manifolds with
\dim X\leq 2
,
[
76
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
20
]
.
There is also a lot of results about the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}(X)
for
\dim X=3
, see e.g.
[
26
,
18
,
28
]
and references therein.
In higher dimensions it is known very little (just because that every finitely presented group is the fundamental group of some manifold, so the classification of compact manifolds contains classification of finitely presented groups), and the results are mostly concern with identifying certain non-trivial elements of homotopy groups, e.g.
[

,

,

,

,

,

]
, see also good reviews by N. Smolentsev

and J. Milnor

Another important class of infinite dimensional spaces are groups of homeomorphisms and diffeomorphisms preserving leaves of a certain foliation.
Their homotopy types are studied even worse.
The most relevant results are obtained in the papers by and K. Fukui and S. Ushiki
[
17
]
and K. Fukui
[

They studied regular foliations on
3
-manifolds with finitely many Reeb components and proved that the identity path component of the group of foliated (sending leaves to leaves) diffeomorphisms has the homotopy type of a product of circles, see Remark
1.1.4

Most of other results related with the structure of diffeomorphism groups of foliations concern with extensions of results by M. Herman
[

Recall that a group is perfect, if it coincides with its commutator subgroup
G=[G,G]

Then perfectness means triviality of the first homology group

The technique developed in the latter result by Epstein

1.1.
Main result

Let
S_{{r}}=\{{z}\in\mathbb{C}\mid|{z}|={r}\}_{{r}\in[0;1]}
be the family of concentric circles in
\mathbb{C}
together with the origin
S_{0}=0
,

\mathbf{T}=\{({w},{z})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid|{z}|=1,\ |{w}|\leq 1\}\cong S^{1}\times D^{2}

be the
solid torus
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
T^{2}:=S^{1}\times S^{1}
be its boundary, and
\mathcal{F}=\{S^{1}\times S_{{r}}\}_{{r}\in[0,1]}
be the partition of
\mathbf{T}
into
2
-tori parallel to the boundary and the central circle
S^{1}\times 0
.

Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}
is a partition into the level sets of the following Morse-Bott function
f\colon\mathbf{T}\to\mathbb{R}
,
f({w},{z})=|{z}|^{2}
, for which the central circle
S^{1}\times 0
is a non-degenerate critical submanifold.

Say that a diffeomorphism
h:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{T}
is
\mathcal{F}
-foliated
if for each leaf
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
its image
h(\omega)
is a (possibly distinct from
\omega
) leaf of
\mathcal{F}
as well.
Also
h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving
, if
h(\omega)=\omega
for each leaf
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.
Define the following groups:

•

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
is the group of diffeomorphisms of
\mathbf{T}
;

•

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
is the group of
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphisms of
\mathbf{T}
;

•

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathbf{T},T^{2})
is the subgroup of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
consisting of diffeomorphisms fixed on
T^{2}
;

•

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},T^{2}):=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathbf{T},T^{2})
.

Endow all those groups with the corresponding
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
Whitney topologies.
Our main result is the following theorem proved in Section
5
:

Theorem 1.1.1
.

The group
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},T^{2})
is weakly contractible, that is weakly homotopy equivalent to a point (i.e. all its homotopy groups vanish).

Notice further that the homotopy types of the groups
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathbf{T},T^{2})
are well known.
In fact,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathbf{T},T^{2})
is contractible, see
[
29
]
, while the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
can be described as follows, see Section
6
for details.
Consider the following subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
:

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
m\ &\delta\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)\mid m\in\mathbb{Z},\,\varepsilon,\delta\in\{\pm 1\}\right\}.

It naturally acts on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by linear automorphisms and preserves the integral lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Hence it yields an action on
2
-torus
T^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by its automorphisms as a Lie group, and so one can define the semidirect product
\mathcal{T}:=(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2})\rtimes\mathcal{G}
corresponding to that action.
Moreover, every element of
\mathcal{T}
naturally extends to a certain
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphism of
\mathbf{T}
, so we get an inclusion
\mathsf{e}:\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
.
It is well known that the inclusion
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T})
is a homotopy equivalence, see Lemma
6.3.1
below.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.1.1
we get the following statement which will be proved in Section
6.4
:

Theorem 1.1.2
.

Each of the following inclusions

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\ \lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \mathsf{e}\penalty\ }\ \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{T}),

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbf{T}}\}\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},T^{2})\ \subset\ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathbf{T},T^{2})

is a weak homotopy equivalence.
In particular, every path component of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
is weakly homotopy equivalent to
2
-torus
\mathcal{R}
,
\pi_{0}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{G}
, while
\pi_{k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},T^{2})=0
for all
k\geq 0
.

Furthermore, gluing two copies of
\mathbf{T}
(each equipped with the same foliation
\mathcal{F}
) via some diffeomorphism of
T^{2}
, we will obtain a lens space
L_{p,q}
and a foliation
\mathcal{F}_{p,q}
on it into two singular circles and parallel
2
-tori.
We will call such a foliation
polar
.
Then Theorem
1.1.1
will also allow us to compute the homotopy type of the group
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})
of
\mathcal{F}_{p,q}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphisms.
Namely, the following statement holds:

Theorem 1.1.3
.

The identity path component of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})
is weakly homotopy equivalent to
2
-torus
T^{2}
, and

\displaystyle\pi_{0}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{G},&\text{for }L_{0,1}=S^{1}\times S^{2},\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2},&\text{for }L_{1,0}=S^{3},L_{2,1}=\mathbb{R}{P}^{3},\\
\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\text{for }p>2.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
p>2
and
q^{2}\not=\pm 1(\bmod\ p)
, then the inclusion
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})\subset\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

In fact, in Section
5
we will obtain a more explicit description of the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})
, see Theorem
7.1.2
.

Remark 1.1.4
.

Notice that the above foliations
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{p,q}
are in some sense <<dual>> to the ones considered by K. Fukui and S. Ushiki
[
17
]
and K. Fukui
[
15
]
.
Namely, a solid torus
\mathbf{T}
admits a Reeb foliation
\mathcal{R}
having
\partial\mathbf{T}
as a leaf and being transversal to
\mathcal{F}
in the interior of
\mathbf{T}
.
Further, let
L_{p,q}
be a lens space obtained by gluing two copies
\mathbf{T}_{0}
and
\mathbf{T}_{1}
of
\mathbf{T}
via some diffeomorphism between their boundaries.
Then that Reeb foliation
\mathcal{R}
of each
\mathbf{T}_{i}
gives a foliation
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}
of the corresponding lens space

which has a joint leaf

\mathcal{F}_{p,q}
and is transversal to other leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{p,q}
.

2.1.
Deformations

Let
X

be a homotopy.
A subset

Then is

It is well known and is easy to see that in this case the corresponding inclusion map

Moreover, if

into the pair

(A,A\cap B)\subset(X,B)
is a homotopy equivalence of pairs.
We will use this observation several times.

2.3.
Partitions

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a partition of a manifold
M
.
The elements of
\mathcal{F}
will also be called
leaves
.
Given a subset
U\subset M
, we will denote by
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
the partition of
U
into the intersections
\omega\cap U
of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
with
U
.
Also
U
is called
\mathcal{F}
-saturated
, if
U
is a union of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.
A map
h\colon U\to M
will be called

•

\mathcal{F}
-foliated
whenever for each
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
the image
h(\omega\cap U)
is contained in some (possibly distinct from
\omega
) leaf of
\mathcal{F}
;

•

\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving
if
h(\omega\cap U)\subset\omega
for all
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
be the group of
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphisms of
M
.

If now
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\bullet}(M,B,\star)
is one of the above groups, then we denote by
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},B,\star)
the intersection
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\bullet}(M,B,\star)
, i.e. just replace
M
with
\mathcal{F}
in the notations.
For instance,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},B):=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(M,B)
is the group of
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving diffeomorphisms fixed on
B
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},B,{p}):=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(M,B,{p})
, and so on.

Notice that if
U\subset M
is an
\mathcal{F}
-saturated neighborhood of
B
, then
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F},B,{p},U)
is a
group
.

2.4.
Tangent maps along fibers

Let
{p}\colon E\to B
be a smooth vector bundle of rank
n
over a compact manifold
B
.
We will identify
B
with the image of zero section of
{p}
in
E
.
For every
t>0
it will be convenient to consider the following
homothety
map
\delta_{t}\colon E\to E
,
\delta_{t}(x)=tx
.
A subset
U\subset E
will be called
star-like
if
\delta_{t}(U)\subset U
for all
t\in[0;1]
.

Let
h\colon E\to E
be a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
map,
r\geq 1
, such that
h(B)\subset B
.
Then there is a well-defined
\mathcal{C}^{r-1}
vector bundle morphism
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h\colon E\to E
given by

(2.2)

\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h(x)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\delta_{t}^{-1}\circ h\circ\delta_{t}(x)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\tfrac{1}{t}h(tx),\ x\in E.

We will call it the
tangent map of
h
along
B
in the direction of fibers of
{p}
, see e.g.
[
37
]
for discussion.

For instance, if
B
is a point, so
E=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
h\colon E\to E
is a map such that
h(0)=0
, then
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the linear map given by the Jacobi matrix of
h
at
0
.

It is easy to show that if
h
is a diffeomorphism, then
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h
is a vector bundle isomorphism.
Moreover, we actually have a
\mathcal{C}^{r-1}
-homotopy
H\colon[0;1]\times E\to E
,

(2.3)

H(t,x)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{t}h(tx),&t>0,\\
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h(x),&t=0.\end{cases}

which is
\mathcal{C}^{r}
on
(0;1]\times E
.

2.5.
Homogeneous functions on vector bundles

A continuous function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is
homogeneous
of (possibly fractional) degree
k>0
if
f(tx)=t^{k}x
for all
t>0
and
x\in E
, which can be rephrased so that
f\circ\delta_{t}=\delta_{t^{k}}\circ f
.
Evidently, for every homogeneous function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
we have that
B\subset f^{-1}(0)
, however, in general,
B\not=f^{-1}(0)
.
A homogeneous function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
will be called
definite
, whenever
f(x)>0
for all
x\in E\setminus B
.

For instance, if the vector bundle
{p}\colon E\to B
is endowed with an orthogonal structure, i.e. its structure group is orthogonal, then the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|\colon E\to[0;+\infty)
is definite homogeneous of degree
1
.
Let
S:=\{x\in E\mid\|x\|=1\}
.
Then the projection
{p}|_{S}\colon S\to B
is called the
spherical bundle
associated with (the orthogonal structure of)
{p}
.

The following lemma extends
[
52
, Lemma 36]
and is also a variation of
[
37
, Lemma 5.1.3]
.

Lemma 2.5.1

(c.f.
[
52
, Lemma 36]
,
[
37
, Lemma 5.1.3]
)
.

Let
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be a homogeneous function of some degree
k
, and
\mathcal{F}
be the partition of
E
into connected components of level sets of
f
.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

For every
t>0
the homothety map
\delta_{t}\colon E\to E
is
\mathcal{F}
-foliated.

(2)

Let
h\colon E\to E
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
map such that
h(B)\subset B
.
For each
t>0
define the map
h_{t}\colon E\to E
by
h_{t}(x)=\delta_{t}^{-1}\circ h\circ\delta_{t}(x)=\tfrac{1}{t}h(tx)
, so
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h(x)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}h_{t}(x)
.

(a)

If
f\circ h=f
, so
h
preserves partition of
E
into level sets of
f
, then
f\circ h_{t}=f
for all
t>0
, and therefore
f\circ\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h=f
as well.

(b)

If
h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving, then so is
h_{t}
for
t>0
.
If, in addition, the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are closed, then
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h
is also
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving.

(3)

If
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is definite, then
f
has the following additional properties.

(a)

f^{-1}(0)=B
.

(b)

For every
t>0
the projection
{p}:f^{-1}(t)\to B
is isomorphic to the spherical bundle
{p}:S\to B
.
In other words, there exists a homeomorphism
\zeta:f^{-1}(t)\to S
such that
{p}\circ\zeta={p}
.
In particular, the path components of
f^{-1}(t)
, i.e. the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, are closed.
Moreover, if
n=\mathsf{rank}E=1
and
{p}
is a trivial vector bundle, then
f^{-1}(t)
consists of two path components.
In all other cases,
f^{-1}(t)
is path connected.

(c)

If
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
, then
B
coincides with the set of critical points of
f
.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose points
x,y\in E
belong to the same leaf
\omega
of
\mathcal{F}
being by definition a path component of a level set
f^{-1}(c)
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
We need to show that
\delta_{t}(x)=tx
and
\delta_{t}(y)=ty
belong to the same path component of some level set of
f
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0;1]\to\omega
be a path such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.
Then
f\circ\gamma(s)=c
for all
c\in[0;1]
.
Hence
f\circ\delta_{t}\circ\gamma(s)=f(t\gamma(s))=t^{k}f(\gamma(s))=t^{k}c
.
In other words,
\delta_{t}\circ\gamma\colon[0;1]\to E
is a path in the level set
f^{-1}(t^{k}c)
between
tx
and
ty
.
Therefore, those points belong to the same path component of the level set
f^{-1}(t^{k}c)
, i.e. to the same leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.

(2)(2a)

If
f\circ h=f
, then

f\circ h_{t}(x)=f\bigl(\tfrac{1}{t}h(tx)\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{t^{k}}f\bigl(h(tx)\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{t^{k}}f(tx)=\tfrac{t^{k}}{t^{k}}f(x)=f(x),

and therefore
f\circ\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h=f
as well.

(2)(2b)

Suppose
h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving, and
\omega
be any leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.
Then by
(1)
,
\delta_{t}(\omega)\in\mathcal{F}
for each
t>0
.
Hence
h(\delta_{t}(\omega))=\delta_{t}(\omega)
, and therefore
h_{t}(\omega)=\delta^{-1}_{t}(h(\delta_{t}(\omega)))=\delta^{-1}_{t}(\delta_{t}(\omega))=\omega
.

Finally, suppose
\omega
is a closed leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.
Let
H\colon[0;1]\times E\to E
be the homotopy defined by (
2.3
).
Then
H\bigl((0;1]\times\omega\bigr)\subset\omega
, whence, by continuity of
H
,

H\bigl([0;1]\times\omega\bigr)=H\bigl(\overline{(0;1]\times\omega}\bigr)\subset\overline{H\bigl((0;1]\times\omega\bigr)}\subset\overline{\omega}=\omega.

Hence
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h(\omega)=H_{0}(\omega)\subset\omega
, and thus
\mathsf{T}_{\!\mathsf{fib}}h
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving.

(3)

Suppose now that
f
is definite.

(3)(3a)

By assumption
f>0
on
E\setminus B
.
On the other hand, if
x\in B
, then
tx=x
for all
t>0
.
Hence
t^{k}f(x)=f(tx)=f(x)
, which is possible only when
f(x)=0
.

(3)(3b)

Notice that for every
a\in(0;+\infty)
there exists a retraction

r_{f,a}\colon E\setminus B\to f^{-1}(a),\qquad r_{f,a}(x)=(a/f(x))^{1/k}x,

satisfying
{p}\circ r_{f,a}={p}
and a homeomorphism

\phi\colon E\setminus B\to f^{-1}(a)\times(0;+\infty),\qquad\phi(x)=\bigl(r_{f,a}(x),f(x)\bigr).

Hence if
g\colon E\setminus B
is another definite homogeneous function, and
b>0
, then the composition
\zeta:(r_{g,b}\circ r_{f,a})|_{f^{-1}(a)}\colon f^{-1}(a)\to g^{-1}(b)
is a homeomorphism also satisfying
{p}\circ\zeta={p}
.
In particular, if
g
is the norm of some orthogonal structure on
{p}
, and
b=1
, then
g^{-1}(1)=S
is the total space of the spherical bundle.

The latter statement about path components of
S
is a well-known property of sphere bundles.
We will briefly recall its proof.
Note that
{p}:S\to B
is a locally trivial
S^{n-1}
-bundle, and we get the corresponding exact sequence of homotopy groups and sets

\pi_{1}S^{n-1}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ i_{1}\penalty\ }\pi_{1}S\xrightarrow{\penalty\ {p}_{1}\penalty\ }\pi_{1}B\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \partial_{1}\penalty\ }\pi_{0}S^{n-1}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ i_{0}\penalty\ }\pi_{0}S\xrightarrow{\penalty\ {p}_{0}\penalty\ }\pi_{0}B,

where the latter term
\pi_{0}B
is zero, since
B
is assumed to be path connected.
Hence if
n>1
,
\pi_{0}S^{n-1}=0
, whence
\pi_{0}S=0
as well, i.e.
S
is path connected.

Suppose
n=1
.
Then
{p}|_{S}:S\to B
is an
S^{0}
-bundle, i.e. a double cover of
B
, and the above exact sequence reduces to the following one:

0\to\pi_{1}S\xrightarrow{\penalty\ {p}_{1}\penalty\ }\pi_{1}B\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \partial_{1}\penalty\ }\mathbb{Z}_{2}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ i_{0}\penalty\ }\pi_{0}S\xrightarrow{\penalty\ {p}_{0}\penalty\ }0,

Evidently, if
{p}
is trivial, then
{p}|_{S}
is trivial as well.
Conversely, if
{p}|_{S}
is trivial, then
S
is a disjoint union of two copies
B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime}
of
B
.
Hence the inverse map
({p}|_{B^{\prime}})^{-1}:B\to B^{\prime}
is a section of
{p}
, whence
{p}
is trivial as a one-dimensional vector bundle admitting a section.

Now, if
{p}|_{S}
(and therefore
{p}
) is non-trivial, then
{p}_{1}
is not surjective, whence the image of
\partial_{1}
consists of more than one element, and therefore
\partial_{1}
is surjective.
Hence,
i_{0}
is a constant map, and thus
{p}_{0}
is a bijection, i.e.
\pi_{0}S=0
.

(3)(3c)

Since
f
takes a minimum on all of
B
, we see that
B
consists of critical points of
f
.
On the other hand, let
x\in E\setminus B
and
\gamma\colon(-1;1)\to E
be given by
\gamma(t)=tx
.
Then
\gamma(1)=x
and

\tfrac{d}{dt}f(\gamma(t))|_{t=1}=\tfrac{d}{dt}f(tx)|_{t=1}=\tfrac{d}{dt}(t^{k}f(x))|_{t=1}=f(x)\cdot(kt^{k-1})|_{t=1}=kf(x)>0.

Hence
x
is a regular point of
f
.
∎

2.6.
Shifts along orbits of flows

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and
\mathbf{F}\colon M\times\mathbb{R}\to M
be a smooth flow generated by some vector field
F
.
Then for an open subset
U\subset M
and a smooth function
\alpha\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
one can define the following smooth map

\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}\colon U\to M,\qquad\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,\alpha(x)),\ x\in U,

We will call
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
the
shift
along orbits of
\mathbf{F}
via the function
\alpha
, and
\alpha
will be called in turn a
shift function
for
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the partition of
M
into the orbits of
\mathbf{F}
.
Then the map
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
is
\mathcal{F}
-leaf preserving.
Moreover, the following family of map
\mathbf{F}_{t\alpha}(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,\alpha(x))
,
t\in[0;1]
, is a homotopy of
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
to the identity inclusion
U\subset M
in the set of
\mathcal{F}|_{U}
-leaf preserving maps
U\to M
.

The following lemma will be used several times.
Let
{F(\alpha)}\colon U\to M
be derivative of
\alpha
along
F
.

Lemma 2.6.1

(
[
52
, Eq. (13)]
)
.

Let
x\in U
.
Then the map
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}\colon U\to M
is a local diffeomorphism at
x
iff
{F(\alpha)}(x)\not=-1
.
In this case, for every non-constant orbit
\omega
of
\mathbf{F}
, the restriction of
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}\colon\omega\cap U\to\omega
is locally injective.
Moreover,
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
preserves directions of orbits of
\mathbf{F}
iff
{F(\alpha)}(x)>-1
.
∎

3.
Linearization theorem

Let
M
be a manifold,
B
its proper submanifold, and
{p}\colon E\to B
a regular neighborhood of
B
, i.e. a retraction of an open neighborhood
E
of
B
endowed with a structure of a vector bundle.
In
[
37
]
the authors proved that for compact
B
the inclusion of pairs

\bigl(\mathcal{D}(M,B,{p}),\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(M,B,{p})\bigr)\subset\bigl(\mathcal{D}(M,B),\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(M,B)\bigr)

is a homotopy equivalence with respect to weak and strong Whitney topologies
\mathsf{W}^{\infty}
and
\mathsf{S}^{\infty}
.
More precisely, it is shown that for any neighborhood
V
of
B
in
E
there exists a homotopy
H:\mathcal{D}(M,B)\times[0;1]\to\mathcal{D}(M,B)
and a continuous function
\phi:\mathcal{D}(M,B)\times[0;1]\times M\to[0;1]
such that

\displaystyle H_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}(M,B)},

\displaystyle H_{1}(\mathcal{D}(M,B))\subset\mathcal{D}(M,B,{p}),

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{D}(M,B,{p})\times[0;1])\subset\mathcal{D}(M,B,{p}),

\displaystyle H(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(M,B)\times[0;1])\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(M,B),

•

for each
h\in\mathcal{D}(M,B)
the map
M\times[0;1]\to M
,
(t,x)\mapsto\phi(h,t,x)
, is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
;

•

\phi(h,t,x)>0
for
t>0
and

(3.1)

4.2.
Main result

Let
{p}\colon E\to S^{1}
be a vector bundle of rank
n
over the circle,
{q}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to E
be the universal covering of
E
,

\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the diffeomorphism generating the group
\mathbb{Z}
of covering transformations, and
\tilde{{p}}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the trivial vector bundle.

As noted in Section
4.1
, one can assume that either
E=S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\gamma=\alpha
or
E=S^{1}\tilde{\times}\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\gamma=\beta
.

Let
\tilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0;+\infty)
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function having the following properties.

(F1)

\tilde{f}^{-1}(0)=\mathbb{R}\times 0
and this set coincides with the set of critical points of
\tilde{f}
.

(F2)

\tilde{f}\circ\gamma=\tilde{f}
, so
\tilde{f}
induces a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

such that
\tilde{f}=f\circ{q}
.

(F3)

is bounded in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

(F4)

There exists an everywhere non-zero
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

on

such that
{F(\tilde{f})}\equiv 0
and

on all of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For

\displaystyle T_{t}

\displaystyle:=f^{-1}\bigl([0;t]\bigr),

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\tilde{f}^{-1}\bigl([0;t]\bigr)={q}^{-1}(T_{t}).

Let also
\mathcal{F}
be the partition of

into connected components of level sets
f^{-1}(t)
,
t\in[0;1]
.
It follows that each
T_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}
-saturated.
Finally, let

•

Q\subset\overline{T_{1}\setminus T_{0.8}}\equiv f^{-1}\bigl([0.8,1]\bigr)
be a non-empty closed subset;

•

\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q,\eta)
be the group of
\mathcal{F}

and fixed on

;

•

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q\cup S^{1},\eta)
be the subgroup of
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q,\eta)

S^{1}
.

Fix any path
\eta\colon[0;1]\to T_{1}
such that
\eta(0)\in Q
and
\eta(1)\in S^{1}
and denote by
\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q\cup S^{1},\eta)

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q\cup S^{1},\eta)
consisting of diffeomorphisms

for which the paths

and

are homotopic relative to their ends.

Theorem 4.2.1
.

\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q\cup S^{1},\eta)
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q,\eta)
and the inclusion

\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q\cup S^{1},\eta)\,\subset\,\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},Q,\eta)

Lemma 4.3.4

-

′
)

{F(\tilde{f})}\equiv 0
.

(1)

\tilde{{p}}\circ\mathbf{F}(x,v,t)=t+x
for all

(2)

\mathbf{F}_{t}\circ\gamma=\gamma\circ\mathbf{F}_{t}
;

(3)

In particular,
(1)

Proof.

Fix any
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

\nu\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0;1]

\nu=1

[0;1]
and
\nu=0

F^{\prime}=\nu F
is supported in

F

Recall that by definition
\gamma^{*}F=\mathsf{T}\gamma\circ F\circ\gamma^{-1}
.
Hence
\gamma^{*}F^{\prime}=\mathsf{T}\gamma\circ(\nu F)\circ\gamma^{-1}=(\nu\circ\gamma^{-1})\gamma^{*}F

Then the following formula

F^{\prime\prime}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\gamma^{i})^{*}F^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\nu\circ\gamma^{-i})\cdot(\gamma^{i})^{*}F

Moreover, if
a_{0}
is the component of

G
at
\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}
is

This function is strictly positive, since

We claim that the vector field

satisfies
(F4′)

Indeed, its component at

is identically

It also directly follows from the formulas for

\gamma^{*}F^{\prime\prime}=F^{\prime\prime}

Hence
\gamma^{*}G=\tfrac{1}{b_{0}\circ\gamma^{-1}}\gamma^{*}F^{\prime\prime}=\tfrac{1}{b_{0}}F^{\prime\prime}=G
.
To check that
{G(\tilde{f})}\equiv 0

{F(\tilde{f})}\circ\gamma^{-1}={(\gamma^{*}F)(\tilde{f}\circ\gamma^{-1})}

Since
\tilde{f}\circ\gamma=\tilde{f}

7.

7.1.

Consider the product

Let also

It will be convenient to denote the images of

and

Therefore, by Lemma
6.1.1

and is given by formula (
6.1
) for some

with its diffeomorphic copy, which, due to Lemma

with

for any matrices

Also, one can regard

0\ &1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)=\lambda\in\mathcal{G}
.
Then
\mathcal{G}=\xi^{-1}\mathcal{G}\xi
, and
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\mathcal{T}
.

2) Assume that
p\geq 1
.
First let us make one observation which will simplify computations.
Let
P\in\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
m\ &\delta\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)\in\mathcal{G}
.
Then the vector
x=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is an eigen vector of
P
with eigen value
\delta=\pm 1
.
Conversely,
\mathcal{G}
is exactly the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
consisting of matrices for which
x
is an eigen vector with eigen value
\pm 1
.

Hence if
Q=A^{-1}PA\in\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon^{\prime}\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
m^{\prime}\ &\delta^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)\in\mathcal{G}
, then
x
is still an eigen vector for
Q
, i.e.
\delta^{\prime}x=Qx=A^{-1}PAx
.
Therefore,
\delta^{\prime}Ax=PAx
, that is
Ax=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}r\ &p\\[1.42262pt]
s\ &q\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p\\
q\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is an eigen vector for
P
with eigen value
\delta^{\prime}
.
This effect is, of course, a consequence of the geometric situation that the diffeomorphism
\eta(Q)
should preserve isotopy classes of meridians of both
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
.

Thus,
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\delta^{\prime}p\\
\delta^{\prime}q\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
m\ &\delta\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p\\
q\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon p\\
mp+\delta q\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Since
p\not=0
, we obtain that
\delta^{\prime}=\varepsilon
and

(7.2)

\displaystyle q(\varepsilon-\delta)=mp.

Consider several cases.

2.1)
Let
(p,q)=(1,0)
, so
L_{1,0}=S^{3}
, and
A=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}0\ &1\\[1.42262pt]
1\ &0\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)
.
Then (
7.2
) implies that
m=0
.
Hence
Q=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
0\ &\delta\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)
, so
Q
can be either of the following matrices:
E
,
\tau=-E
,
\lambda
,
\mu
.
One easily checks that
A^{-1}\lambda A^{-1}=\mu\in\mathcal{G}
and
A^{-1}\mu A^{-1}=\lambda
.
Hence
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\langle\mathcal{R},\lambda,\mu\rangle
.

2.2)
Let
(p,q)=(2,1)
, so
L_{2,1}=\mathbb{R}{P}^{3}
, and
A=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}1\ &2\\[1.42262pt]
1\ &1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)
.
Then (
7.2
) implies that
\varepsilon-\delta=2m
.
Hence
Q
can be either of the following matrices:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}1\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
0\ &1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right),

\displaystyle\tau=-E=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}-1\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
0\ &-1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right).

\displaystyle\lambda d=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}1\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
-1\ &-1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right),

\displaystyle\tau\lambda d=-\lambda d=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}-1\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
1\ &1\end{smallmatrix}\!\right),

which implies that
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\langle\mathcal{R},\lambda d,\tau\rangle
.

2.3)
Suppose
p>2
.
Then
q\not=0
as well and is relatively prime with
p
.
Hence there exists
a\not=0
such that
m=aq
and
\varepsilon-\delta=ap
.
If
a=0
, then
m=0
and
\varepsilon=\delta
so
Q=\pm E
and it commutes with
A
.
On the other hand, if
a\not=0
, then
\varepsilon-\delta\in\{-2,0,2\}
implies that
|ap|=|\varepsilon-\delta|=2
which contradicts to the assumption that

.
Hence in this case
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}=\langle\mathcal{R},\tau\rangle
.

C)
Suppose
p>2
and
q^{2}\not=1(\bmod\ p)
.
In this case, due to
[
33
, Theorem 2.3]
, the group
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}
coincides with the isometry group
\mathrm{Isom}(L_{p,q})
of the natural elliptic metric on

, see below.
Now by the Smale conjecture for

, the inclusion

is a homotopy equivalence,
[
28
]
.
Hence the inclusions
\mathcal{L}_{p,q}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})\subset\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
are weak homotopy equivalences.
Theorem

is completed.
∎

7.2.
Concluding remarks

Let us briefly recall the known description of the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
and compare them with the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_{p,q})
given in Theorem
7.1.2
.

1)

For
p=0
we have the subgroup
\mathcal{Q}=\bigl(\Omega(\mathrm{O}(3))\rtimes\mathrm{O}(3)\bigr)\rtimes\mathrm{O}(2)\subset\mathcal{D}(S^{1}\times S^{2})
being a certain iterated semidirect product and consisting of diffeomorphisms
g_{\alpha,A}:S^{1}\times S^{2}\to S^{1}\times S^{2}
of the form

g_{\alpha,A}({w},u)=(\alpha{w},A({w})u),\qquad({w},u)\in S^{1}\times S^{2},

where
\alpha\in S^{1}
, and
A:S^{1}\to\mathrm{O}(3)
is a continuous map.
Topologically
\mathcal{Q}
is a topological product
\Omega(\mathrm{O}(3))\times\mathrm{O}(3)\times\mathrm{O}(2)
and A. Hatcher proved in
[
23
]
that the above inclusion
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{D}(S^{1}\times S^{2})
is a homotopy equivalence.

Then the inclusion
\mathsf{e}:\mathcal{L}_{0,1}=\mathcal{R}\rtimes\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{Q}
is given by the following formula.
Let
{w}\in S^{1}
and
u=({z},v)\in S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
be the point on
S^{2}
.
Then for
b=(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{R}
and
A=\left(\!\begin{smallmatrix}\varepsilon\ &0\\[1.42262pt]
m\ &\delta\end{smallmatrix}\!\right)\in\mathcal{G}

(7.3)

\mathsf{e}(\widetilde{h}_{b,A})({w},{z},v)=\bigl(\alpha{w}^{\varepsilon},\,\beta{w}^{m}{z}^{\delta},v\bigr).

2)

For
p=1
we have the inclusion
\mathrm{O}(4)\subset\mathcal{D}(S^{3})
which is also proved by A. Hatcher to be a homotopy equivalence, see
[
26
]
.
In this case we have a canonical embedding
\mathcal{L}_{1,0}=\mathrm{O}(2)\times\mathrm{O}(2)\subset\mathrm{O}(4)
independently acting on the first and second coordinate.

3)

Suppose
p\geq 2
.
Let
\tau\in\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
be given by
\tau({w},{z})=(\bar{{w}},\bar{{z}})
on each
\mathbf{T}\times\{i\}
,
i=0,1
, so formally it is the image
{\mathsf{e}^{\prime\prime}}(\eta(\tau))
of the matrix
\tau
, however to simplify notation we denote it here by the same letter.
If
q^{2}\equiv 1(\bmod\,p)
, then one can define a diffeomorphism
\sigma_{+}\in\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
exchanging
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
and defined by

\textstyle{\sigma_{+}:\mathbf{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({w},{z})\mapsto({w},{z})}
\scriptstyle{({w},{z})\leftarrow\!\shortmid({w},{z})}
\textstyle{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}.}

Also, if
q^{2}\equiv-1(\bmod\,p)
, then there exists another diffeomorphism
\sigma_{-}\in\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
also exchanging
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
and given by

\textstyle{\sigma_{-}:\mathbf{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({w},{z})\mapsto(\bar{{w}},{z})}
\scriptstyle{({w},\bar{{z}})\leftarrow\!\shortmid({w},{z})}
\textstyle{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}.}

It easily follows that
\sigma_{+}
preserves orientation and
\sigma_{+}^{2}=\mathrm{id}_{L_{p,q}}
, while
\sigma_{-}
reverses orientation and
\sigma_{-}^{2}=\tau
.

F. Bonahon
[
5
, Theorem 1]
proved that every diffeomorphism
h
of
L_{p,q}
is isotopic to a diffeomorphism which leaves the common boundary
T^{2}
of
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
invariant, and moreover,
\pi_{0}\mathcal{D}(L_{p,q})
is generated by the isotopy classes of
\tau
,
\sigma_{+}
, and
\sigma_{-}
, (more precisely by those
\sigma_{\pm}
which are defined for the given
(p,q)
).

4)

Notice that for
p\geq 2
the universal cover of
L_{p,q}
is
S^{3}
, whence
L_{p,q}
admits a Riemannian metric locally isomorphic with the standard metric on
S^{3}
, and for that reason such

are also called
elliptic

3
-manifolds, see

33
, Theorem 2.3]
for the description of the isometry groups

.
In the book
[
28
]

Such a statement is called

, and as mentioned above it was proved by A. Hatcher for

.

Moreover, in a recent unpublished yet preprint by R. Bamler and B. Kleiner
[
3

the Smale conjecture is proved for all elliptic
3
-manifolds including

and

In particular, it implies that

Finally, note that in
[
33
]
there were studied groups of diffeomorphisms of

,

, fixed on

, while in
[
34
]

Non-symmetric GHZ states: weighted hypergraph and controlled-unitary graph representations
Hrachya Zakaryan, Konstantinos-Rafail Revis, and Zahra Raissi

Abstract
Non-symmetric GHZ states ( n n -GHZ α ), defined by unequal superpositions of | 00 ​ … ​ 0 ⟩ \ket{00…0} and | 11 ​ … ​ 1 ⟩ \ket{11…1} , naturally emerge in experiments due to decoherence, control errors, and state preparation imperfections. Despite their relevance in quantum communication, relativistic quantum information, and quantum teleportation, these states lack a stabilizer formalism and a graph representation, hindering their theoretical and experimental analysis. We establish a graph-theoretic framework for non-symmetric GHZ states, proving their local unitary (LU) equivalence to two structures: fully connected weighted hypergraphs with controlled-phase interactions and star-shaped controlled-unitary (CU) graphs. While weighted hypergraphs generally lack stabilizer descriptions, we demonstrate that non-symmetric GHZ states can be efficiently stabilized using local operations and a single ancilla, independent of system size. We extend this framework to qudit systems, constructing LU-equivalent weighted qudit hypergraphs and showing that general non-symmetric qudit GHZ states can be described as star-shaped CU graphs. Our results provide a systematic approach to characterizing and stabilizing non-symmetric multipartite entanglement in both qubit and qudit systems, with implications for quantum error correction and networked quantum protocols.

Hypergraph States.
— Hypergraph states extend the concept of graph states by allowing multi-qubit interactions through multi-controlled-Z (
CZ
) operations. A
k

G(V,E)

V

k

E\subseteq\bigoplus_{k^{\prime}=2}^{k}V^{k^{\prime}}

A hypergraph state associated with a given hypergraph
G(V,E)
is defined as:

\displaystyle\ket{G}=\prod_{\textbf{e}\in E}CZ_{\textbf{e}}^{\Gamma_{\textbf{e}}}\ket{+}^{\otimes n},

(1)

where
\ket{+}=H\ket{0}
and each hyperedge
e
contributes a multi-controlled phase gate parameterized by the adjacency matrix element
\Gamma_{\textbf{e}}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\textbf{e}}=\begin{cases}r&\text{if }(\textbf{e}=\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{k}\},r)\in E\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

(2)

where for hyperedges involving fewer than
k
vertices, we fill the corresponding index vector with zeros to maintain a uniform representation.
The multi-controlled-Z (
CZ_{\textbf{e}}
) operation on qudits in the set
e
is given by,

\displaystyle CZ_{\textbf{e}}=\displaystyle\sum_{k_{e_{1}},\dots,k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\bar{k}_{\textbf{e}}}\ket{k_{e_{1}}\dots k_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}\dots k_{e_{m}}},

(3)

where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/d}
,
m=|\textbf{e}|
is the size of the set
e
and
\bar{k}_{\textbf{e}}
represents the collective phase contribution of the qudit states associated with the hyperedge interaction i.e.,
\bar{k}_{\textbf{e}}=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}k_{e_{j}}

The special case of
k=2
corresponds to graph states, where each edge represents a standard controlled-Z operation, whereas for
k>2
, hypergraph states capture a broader class of multipartite entanglement structures. This extended framework not only provides a richer mathematical foundation for quantum networks

Non-symmetric GHZ and Weighted Hypergraphs.
—The symmetric GHZ state is local unitary (LU) equivalent to both a star-shaped graph state, where a central qubit is entangled with all others, and a fully connected graph state, where each qubit is directly connected to every other qubit. This equivalence provides an alternative graph-based description of GHZ states, widely used in entanglement studies (see Supplementary Material for details and visual representation).

We extend this graph-based approach to non-symmetric GHZ states,

Generalization to Qudits.
—For qubits, the GHZ state contains only two elements in the superposition, limiting the possible ways to introduce asymmetry. However, in qudits, the GHZ state extends over
d
levels,

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}^{d}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\ket{kk...k},

(10)

allowing for a richer class of non-symmetric generalizations. For simplicity, we omit normalization factors in our expressions where they are not essential.

To define a qudit non-symmetric GHZ state that naturally connects to weighted hypergraphs, we first generalize the
X^{\alpha}
operator from qubits to qudits,

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}=H^{\dagger}Z^{\alpha}H=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}X^{k}.

(11)

Using this, we construct the following qudit generalization:

Proposition 3.
—(Qudit weighted hypergraph representation of non-symmetric GHZ states)
Given a qudit
X^{\alpha}
, the non-symmetric qudit GHZ state is defined as

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ\,}^{d}_{\alpha}}\coloneqq\prod_{k=2}^{n}CX_{1k}\,X^{\alpha}_{1}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}\,,

(12)

which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ\,}^{d}_{\alpha}}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}\ket{kk....k}.

(13)

This state is LU equivalent to a qudit-weighted hypergraph with controlled-phase interactions, given by

\displaystyle CZ_{G}^{-\alpha}=(Z_{1}Z_{2}...Z_{n})^{-\alpha}.

(14)

where

\displaystyle CZ_{G}=\displaystyle\prod_{\textbf{e}\in E}CZ_{\textbf{e}}\text{ for }G=(V,E)

(15)

A detailed proof of this proposition is provided in the Supplementary Material. Unlike in the qubit case, where weighted hypergraphs emerge naturally, the qudit case introduces additional complexity. This arises from the fact that,

\displaystyle(Z_{2}Z_{3}...Z_{n+1})^{-\alpha}\neq Z_{2}^{-\alpha}Z_{3}^{-\alpha}...Z_{n+1}^{-\alpha},

(16)

requiring correction terms to expand the brackets. This is present for qubits as well, however while for qubits the correction terms are of the form
CZ^{\alpha}
, for qudits more general non-local phase interactions are required. These are given by,

\displaystyle CP_{e}(\vec{\alpha})=\sum_{k_{e_{1}},...,k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\vec{\alpha}_{k_{e}}}\ket{k_{e_{1}}...k_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}...k_{e_{m}}},

(17)

where
\vec{\alpha}_{k_{e}}
denotes the phase factor associated with the decimal encoding
k_{e}
, of the computational basis states. Therefore,
CP_{e}(\vec{\alpha})
is a diagonal matrix of phases determined by the weight vector
\vec{\alpha}
. Unlike in the qubit case, where the interaction weights follow a simple recursive pattern, the qudit case requires explicit computation of the interaction structure. This necessitates an algorithmic approach, which we detail in the Supplementary Material.

Despite this structural complexity, the stabilizer formulation remains highly analogous to the qubit case. The stabilizers for the qudit non-symmetric GHZ state take the form

\displaystyle\hskip-6.544ptK_{G^{\prime}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=X\otimes Z\otimes Z\otimes...\otimes Z

\displaystyle\hskip-6.544ptK_{G^{\prime}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=X^{-\alpha}ZX^{\alpha}\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)\otimes I\otimes...\otimes I

\displaystyle\hskip-6.544ptK_{G^{\prime}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=X^{-\alpha}ZX^{\alpha}\otimes I\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)\otimes...\otimes I

(18)

\displaystyle\hskip 4.26773pt\vdots

\displaystyle\hskip-6.544ptK_{G^{\prime}}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=X^{-\alpha}ZX^{\alpha}\otimes I\otimes I\otimes...\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi).

As in the qubit case, only a single ancilla is required, independent of system size
n
. This guarantees that non-symmetric qudit GHZ states can be efficiently verified and manipulated with minimal resource overhead, making them scalable for quantum information applications.

Controlled-Unitary Graph States.
—We extend the graph state formalism by incorporating controlled-unitary (
CU
) operations as entangling gates, in contrast to conventional diagonal gates such as controlled-Z (
CZ
) for standard graph and hypergraph states or controlled-phase (
CZ^{\alpha}
) gates for weighted graph states. This broader framework naturally extends hypergraph-based representations of non-symmetric GHZ states in qudit systems.

Specifically, we show that the most general non-symmetric qudit GHZ state,

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\vec{a}}}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{jj\dots j},

(19)

where
\vec{a}=\{a_{0},a_{1},...,a_{d-1}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, can be represented as a
star-shaped graph
with controlled-
U
gates.

Proposition 4.
—(Controlled-unitary graph representation of general non-symmetric qudit GHZ states)
For any general non-symmetric qudit GHZ state, there exists a local unitary (LU) transformation that maps
\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\vec{a}}}
to a star-shaped controlled-unitary graph state, where
U
is given by,

\displaystyle U=HA^{\dagger}ZAH^{\dagger},

(20)

with the operator
A
defined as the single-qudit transformation matrix:

\displaystyle A\ket{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{j}.

(21)

The proof follows by explicitly constructing
\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\vec{a}}}
using controlled-
X
operations and applying a sequence of local Hadamard and phase corrections to obtain the controlled-unitary graph form (see Supplementary Material). This formulation provides a structured way to describe arbitrary non-symmetric GHZ states within a graph-based framework. The key feature of this approach is that while the control qudits remain unchanged, the target qudit undergoes the transformation
U
. Although general
CU
operations may not commute, in the star-shaped graph configuration, commutativity is preserved since all operations act on a shared target.

As an illustrative example, consider the qubit case where
A=XX^{\alpha}
, which naturally leads to the weighted hypergraph structure described in Eq. (
5
). In this case, the corresponding unitary transformation is

\displaystyle U=\begin{bmatrix}0&e^{i\alpha\pi}\\
e^{-i\alpha\pi}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(22)

This demonstrates how different choices of
A
yield distinct
CU
interactions.

), is local unitary (LU) equivalent to this CU graph.

Summary and Outlook.
—
We established a graph-based framework for non-symmetric GHZ states, proving their LU equivalence to two structures: fully connected weighted hypergraphs with controlled-phase interactions and star-shaped controlled-unitary (
CU
) graphs. This dual representation bridges weighted hypergraphs and controlled-unitary entanglement structures, offering new insights into non-symmetric multipartite quantum states.

In experiments, decoherence, control errors, and imperfections in high-dimensional quantum systems can lead to non-symmetric states rather than ideal GHZ states. Our framework provides a systematic method to construct stabilizers for such states using a single ancilla, enabling their detection through local operations regardless of system size. Beyond hypergraph-based stabilizers, the CU formalism offers a structured approach to synthesizing non-symmetric quantum states and analyzing controlled quantum operations.

The ability to construct stabilizers for non-symmetric GHZ states may have implications for error correction, as stabilizer based techniques are a cornerstone of fault-tolerant quantum computation. Additionally, the CU framework extends graph-based methods to controlled-unitary interactions, relevant to quantum simulation and networked quantum protocols. Future directions include applying this approach to other resource states, investigating experimental implementations in noisy quantum devices, and exploring potential advantages of non-symmetric entanglement in quantum networks and multipartite cryptography.

Proof of Proposition 2

Proposition 2
.

Given the weighted hypergraph state of
n=|V|
qubits,

\displaystyle\ket{G}=\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\prod_{\textbf{e}\in E_{k+1}}CZ_{\textbf{e}}^{(-2)^{k}\alpha}\ket{+}^{\otimes V},

and a single ancilla, it is possible to stabilize the
n+1
qubit state, if the ancilla is connected to each of the
n
qubits through a
CZ
operation.
The stabilizers are given by,

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=X\otimes Z\otimes Z\otimes...\otimes Z

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=X^{\alpha}ZX^{-\alpha}\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)\otimes I\otimes...\otimes I

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=X^{\alpha}ZX^{-\alpha}\otimes I\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)\otimes...\otimes I

(37)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\vdots

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=X^{\alpha}ZX^{-\alpha}\otimes I\otimes I\otimes...\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi),

where

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{2}((1-e^{i\pi\alpha})I+(1+e^{i\pi\alpha})X),

(38)

the first qubit is the ancilla and
G^{\prime}
is the graph with the ancilla.

Proof.

We employ a technique introduced by Tsimakuridze and Gühne
[
43
]
, which establishes LU equivalence between graph states and specific weighted hypergraph states. This technique allows us to introduce a single ancilla qubit, enabling a stabilizer representation for the fully connected weighted hypergraph state.

Consider a hypergraph
G^{\prime}
and define the real power
\alpha
of the Pauli-X gate as:

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{2}((1-e^{i\pi\alpha})I+(1+e^{i\pi\alpha})X).

(39)

The application of
X^{\alpha}
upon any vertex
v_{i}
of
G^{\prime}
leads to a weighted hypergraph given by the following rules,

1.

For each
e
in
A_{i}=\{\textbf{e}|\textbf{e}/{v_{i}}\in E\}
add a weight
\alpha
to
e
.

2.

For each
k\leq n
,
\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{e}_{2},..\textbf{e}_{k}\in A_{i}
,
\textbf{e}_{1}\neq\textbf{e}_{2}...\neq\textbf{e}_{k}
add weight
(-2)^{k}\alpha
to the edge
\textbf{e}_{1}\cup\textbf{e}_{2}...\cup\textbf{e}_{k}
.

We now introduce a star-shaped graph with
n+1
qubits, where the ancilla qubit (labeled 1) is connected to all
n
system qubits via CZ operations:

\displaystyle E=\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},...,\{1,n+1\}\}.

(40)

Now we consider the application of
X^{\alpha}
on the ancilla. In accordance with the two rules given above we obtain,

\displaystyle A_{1}=\{\{2\},\{3\},...,\{n+1\}\}.

(41)

By Rule 1, all qubits except the ancilla receive local phase gates. The second rule tells us that for each
k
combinations of the
n
qubits in
A_{1}
, we add a hyperedge of weight
(-2)^{k}\alpha
between those
k
particles. However, if we ignore the ancilla qubit this is exactly the construction of the weighted hypergraph in Eq. (
24
).

To construct the stabilizers, we use the graph state stabilizer formalism:

\displaystyle K_{G}^{(j)}=X_{j}\prod_{k\in V}Z_{k}^{\Gamma_{jk}}.

(42)

Now we simply conjugate these with the operator,

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)\otimes\dots\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi).

(43)

where
X^{\alpha}
is due to the original application and the phase gates are due to rule 1.
This leads to a new set of local stabilizers:

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=X\otimes Z\otimes Z...\otimes Z

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=X^{\alpha}ZX^{-\alpha}\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)\otimes I...\otimes I

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=X^{\alpha}ZX^{-\alpha}\otimes I\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)...\otimes I

(44)

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=X^{\alpha}ZX^{-\alpha}\otimes I\otimes I...\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi).

∎

This construction demonstrates that a single ancilla qubit, connected to all system qubits via controlled-Z operations, is sufficient to stabilize the fully connected weighted hypergraph state. The local nature of these stabilizers allows for direct experimental verification and manipulation, overcoming the primary challenge of multi-qubit entanglement in weighted hypergraphs. Thus, we have established a novel method for stabilizing non-symmetric GHZ states using a fixed number of ancillae—independent of system size
n
—which is crucial for practical quantum information processing.

Proof of Proposition 3

Proposition 3
.

Given a qudit
X^{\alpha}

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=H^{\dagger}Z^{\alpha}H=\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}X^{k},

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}^{d}_{\alpha}}

(47)

We begin by considering the general multi-controlled-Z operation acting on a hyperedge
\mathbf{e}
in a qudit system:

\displaystyle CZ_{\mathbf{e}}=\sum_{k_{e_{1}},...,k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\bar{k}_{\mathbf{e}}}\ket{k_{e_{1}}...k_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}...k_{e_{m}}},

(48)

where the total phase contribution for a given basis state is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{k}_{\mathbf{e}}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}k_{e_{j}}.

(49)

A key observation is how this operator commutes with the generalized
X
gate at some vertex
e_{1}
. We have

\displaystyle X_{e_{1}}CZ_{\mathbf{e}}X_{e_{1}}^{\dagger}=\sum_{k_{e_{1}},...k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\bar{k}_{\mathbf{e}}}\ket{k_{e_{1}}+1,...,k_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}+1,...,k_{e_{m}}},

(50)

and by multiplying on the left by
CZ_{\mathbf{e}}^{\dagger}
, we obtain

\displaystyle CZ_{\textbf{e}}^{\dagger}X_{e_{1}}CZ_{\textbf{e}}X_{e_{1}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{l_{e_{1}},...l_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\sum_{k_{e_{1}},...k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\bar{k}_{\textbf{e}}-\bar{l}_{\textbf{e}}}\ket{l_{e_{1}},...l_{e_{m}}}\braket{l_{e_{1}},...l_{e_{m}}|k_{e_{1}}+1,...k_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}+1,...k_{e_{m}}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{l_{e_{1}},...l_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\sum_{k_{e_{1}},...k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\bar{k}_{\textbf{e}}-\bar{l}_{\textbf{e}}}\delta_{l_{e_{1}}-1,k_{e_{1}}}\prod_{j=2}^{m}\delta_{l_{e_{j}},k_{e_{j}}}\ket{l_{e_{1}},...l_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}+1,...k_{e_{m}}}

(51)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{l_{e_{1}}=1}^{d-1}\sum_{l_{e_{2}},...l_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-\bar{l}_{\textbf{e}/\{e_{1}\}}}\ket{l_{e_{1}}...l_{e_{m}}}\bra{l_{e_{1}}...l_{e_{m}}}+\sum_{l_{e_{2}},...l_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{(d-1)\bar{l}_{\textbf{e}/\{e_{1}\}}}\ket{0...l_{e_{m}}}\bra{0...l_{e_{m}}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{l_{e_{1}}=0}^{d-1}\ket{l_{e_{1}}}\bra{l_{e_{1}}}\otimes\sum_{l_{e_{2}},...l_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-\bar{l}_{\textbf{e}/\{e_{1}\}}}\ket{l_{e_{2}}...l_{e_{m}}}\bra{l_{e_{2}}...l_{e_{m}}}=\mathbb{I}_{e_{1}}\otimes CZ_{\textbf{e}/\{e_{1}\}},

where to get the last line we have used
\omega^{d}=1
. Therefore, we have,

\displaystyle X_{e_{1}}CZ_{\textbf{e}}=CZ_{\textbf{e}}(\mathbb{I}_{e_{1}}\otimes CZ_{\textbf{e}/\{e_{1}\}})X_{e_{1}}\,.

(52)

Next, we derive the explicit form of
X^{\alpha}
using the following qudit operators and relations,

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}\ket{j}\bra{k}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{j}\ket{j}\bra{j}

(53)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\ket{j+1}\bra{j}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=H^{\dagger}ZH\,.

Using the above equations we can write,

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=H^{\dagger}Z^{\alpha}H=\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime},j,k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}\omega^{-j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\omega^{\alpha l}\ket{j^{\prime}}\braket{k^{\prime}|l}\braket{l|j}\bra{k}=\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime},j,k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}\omega^{-j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\omega^{\alpha l}\delta_{k^{\prime},l}\delta_{j,l}\ket{j^{\prime}}\bra{k}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime},k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{lk}\omega^{-j^{\prime}l}\omega^{\alpha l}\ket{j^{\prime}}\bra{k}=\displaystyle\sum_{j,k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(j-(k+j)+\alpha)}\ket{j+k}\bra{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{j,k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}\ket{j+k}\bra{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}X^{k}.

(54)

Similar to the qubit case we consider a qudit hypergraph
G(V,E)
with

\displaystyle\ket{G}=\displaystyle\prod_{\textbf{e}\in E}CZ_{\textbf{e}}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=CZ_{G}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}.

(55)

Additionally, we define the graph
\Delta_{i}G(V,\Delta_{i}E)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}E=\{\textbf{e}/{i}|i\in\textbf{e},\textbf{e}\in E\}.

(56)

Using the commutation relation,

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}_{i}\ket{G}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}X_{i}^{k}CZ_{G}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=CZ_{G}\sum_{k,l}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}CZ_{\Delta_{i}G}^{\dagger k}X_{i}^{k}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}CZ_{\Delta_{i}G}^{\dagger k}CZ_{G}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=CZ_{\Delta_{i}G}^{-\alpha}\ket{G}

Therefore, like in the qubit case the action of

has introduced new weighted hyperedges to our hypergraph.

Similar to the qubit case we consider the qudit

,

\displaystyle E=\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},...,\{1,n+1\}\}.

(58)

We apply

on the ancilla qudit, i.e. qudit 1, which gives us,

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}E=\{\{2\},\{3\},...,\{n+1\}\}.

(59)

Therefore, the additional weighted hypergraph added is,

\displaystyle CZ_{\Delta_{1}G}^{-\alpha}=(Z_{2}Z_{3}...\,Z_{n+1})^{-\alpha},

(60)

as an edge with a single vertex is simply a local operation. This does not look like a weighted hypergraph as

is a local operation,

\displaystyle(Z_{2}Z_{3}...Z_{n+1})^{-\alpha}\neq Z_{2}^{-\alpha}Z_{3}^{-\alpha}...\,Z_{n+1}^{-\alpha}.

(61)

To expand the brackets we need to include non-local operations. For example in the qubit case those non-local operations are what give rise to the fully connected weighted hypergraph. However, in the case of qudits, we find that

but are of the form,

\displaystyle CP_{e}(\vec{\alpha})=\displaystyle\sum_{k_{e_{1}},...k_{e_{m}}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{\vec{\alpha}_{k_{e}}}\ket{k_{e_{1}}...k_{e_{m}}}\bra{k_{e_{1}}...k_{e_{m}}},

(62)

where

is the decimal representation of

is a diagonal matrix of phases decided by the vector

. These don’t follow a pattern like the qubit case, and therefore we need to use an algorithm to find the exact operations
to apply (see next section).

For our choice of the specific form of the non-symmetric qudit GHZ, we note that for qubits,

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\alpha}}=\displaystyle\omega^{\alpha/2}\prod_{k=2}^{n}CX_{1k}X_{1}X^{\alpha}_{1}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}.

(63)

\ket{n\text{-GHZ}^{d}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}^{d}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{n}CX_{1k}X^{\alpha}_{1}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}\ket{kk....k}.

(64)

Now let us show that this state is indeed local unitary equivalent to the weighted hypergraph from Eq. (
60
). We apply Hadamards on all the qudits,

\displaystyle H^{\otimes n}\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\alpha}^{d}}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}H^{\otimes n}\ket{kk....k}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l(\alpha-k)}\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\omega^{kw_{\bm{x}}}\ket{\bm{x}}.

(65)

Writing

for some integers

and

the state can be written as,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l\alpha}\omega^{k(r_{\bm{x}}-l)}\ket{\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{l\alpha}\delta_{r_{\bm{x}},l}\ket{\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\omega^{r_{\bm{x}}\alpha}\ket{\bm{x}}.

We can show that this is exactly the state for the weighted hypergraph
\Delta_{1}G

\displaystyle Z_{1}Z_{2}...\,Z_{n}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}=\sum_{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}=0}^{d-1}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\omega^{x_{j}}\ket{x_{j}}=\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\omega^{w_{\bm{x}}}\ket{\bm{x}}=\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\omega^{r_{\bm{x}}}\ket{\bm{x}}.

(67)

Therefore, as
Z_{1}Z_{2}...\,Z_{n}
is diagonal we get,

\displaystyle(Z_{1}Z_{2}...\,Z_{n})^{\alpha}=\sum_{\bm{x}=0}^{2^{d}-1}\omega^{\alpha r_{\bm{x}}}\ket{\bm{x}}.

(68)

And hence the weighted hypergraph obtained from applying
X^{-\alpha}
on the central qudit of a
star-shaped graph
and then tracing out the ancilla qudit, is equivalent to
\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\alpha}^{d}}
.

∎

The stabilizers are given similarly to the qubit case,

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=X\otimes Z\otimes Z...\otimes Z

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=X^{-\alpha}ZX^{\alpha}\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)\otimes I...\otimes I

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=X^{-\alpha}ZX^{\alpha}\otimes I\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi)...\otimes I

(69)

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle K_{G^{\prime}}^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=X^{-\alpha}ZX^{\alpha}\otimes I\otimes I...\otimes P^{\dagger}(\alpha\pi)XP(\alpha\pi).

The stabilizer formalism for non-symmetric GHZ states in qudit systems is highly analogous to that in qubit systems. The key difference between the qudit and qubit cases is the sign of the parameter
\alpha
in the power of

X
operator and the

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\vec{a}}}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{jj\dots j},

\displaystyle U=HA^{\dagger}ZAH^{\dagger},

(76)

\displaystyle A\ket{0}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{j}.

\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\vec{a}}}

n-1

\displaystyle\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{\vec{a}}}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{jj\dots j}=\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{n}CX_{1k}A_{1}\ket{00\dots 0}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\prod_{l=2}^{n}H_{l}\ket{n\text{-GHZ}_{ns}}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{j}\sum_{k=0}^{d^{n-1}-1}\omega^{j(k_{1}+\dots+k_{n-1})}\ket{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}\ket{j}\sum_{k=0}^{d^{n-1}-1}\omega^{jw_{k}}\ket{k}.

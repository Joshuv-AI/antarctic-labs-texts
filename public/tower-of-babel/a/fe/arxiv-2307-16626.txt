Perturbative quasinormal mode frequencies Preprint: RUP-23-13
Yasuyuki Hatsuda, Masashi Kimura

Abstract
We often encounter a situation where linear wave equations around a black hole solution can be regarded as continuous deformations of simpler ones, or modifications from the general relativity case by continuous parameters. We develop a general framework to compute high-order perturbative corrections to quasinormal mode frequencies in such deformed problems. Our method has many applications, and allows to compute numerical values of the high-order corrections very accurately. For several examples, we perform this computation explicitly, and discuss analytic properties of the quasinormal mode frequencies for deformation parameters.



I
Introduction

Perturbation theory is one of the most powerful tools in physics.
We have a typical situation that a system cannot be solved analytically but its special limit can be.
Perturbation around the special limit provides us a good approximation method and more importantly a clue to get global information on the total system by combining with the analytic continuation in complex analysis or asymptotic analysis.
The application range of perturbation theory is extremely wide. It is important to clarify what we can learn about from perturbation theory.

In this work, we propose a systematic way to compute high-order perturbative corrections to quasinormal mode (QNM) frequencies of black holes.
QNMs are solutions to linearized field equations, which
satisfy purely ingoing (outgoing) boundary conditions at the horizon (infinity),
around a background black hole spacetime.
It is known that QNMs are related to the late time behavior of the field dynamics around black holes
Nakamura:1987zz
;
Kokkotas:1999bd
;
Nollert:1999ji
;
berti2009
;
konoplya2011
;
Ferrari:2007dd
;
Leaver:1986gd
;
Andersson:1996cm
;
Andersson:1995zk
;
Nollert:1992ifk
;
Berti:2006wq
.
In many cases, one can regard some parameters of black hole solutions as smooth deformation parameters of simpler black holes.
We apply perturbation theory for such deformation parameters.
Similar situations also happen if one considers possibilities of effective field theories or modified gravity theories beyond general relativity.
Since such modification parameters are expected to be small, it is natural to expand physical quantities perturbatively.
It is desirable to develop a general framework widely applicable for such cases.

There are two obstacles to achieve it.
One is that we cannot solve the QNM spectral problem analytically even in spherically symmetric black holes.
Therefore we have only numerical or semi-analytic eigenvalues and eigenfunctions in this simplest case.
The other point is more serious. In the QNM problem, a set of the eigenfunctions is not complete in the usual sense.
This means that we cannot apply the well-known formula in quantum mechanics to the computation of perturbative corrections to the QNM spectrum.
There is already an extended formula to compute perturbative corrections to QNM frequencies
leung1997
;
leung1998
;
leung1999
.
However it is not clear for us how to use this formulation for our interested examples systematically and practically. For this reason, we revisit the similar problem in this work, and propose another way to get high-order perturbative corrections to QNM frequencies.

A possible resolution for this problem is simply to use numerical fittings.
1
1

1

There is another resolution. One can analytically continue eigenvalue problems from the real line to the complex domain. This is well-known as the complex scaling method for resonance problems in quantum mechanics.

However it is hard to predict high-order corrections accurately in this way.
Recently, a smart way to compute perturbative quadratic order corrections to the QNM frequencies was proposed in
cardoso2019
;
mcmanus2019
.
We are strongly motivated by these works.
We extend them to more general setups.
Our approach is based on the first principle of perturbation theory.
We do not use any numerical fittings to determine the perturbative coefficients though we need numerical solutions at each order in perturbation.
Our method is quite general and applicable to various situations with smoothly continuous deformations.
In fact, we give, for the first time, the high-precision perturbative expansion of the QNM frequencies around the extremal Reissner-Nordström black holes.
Combining a recently proposed method
hatsuda2020
, our approach allows to compute numerical values of high-order corrections very accurately.
Once we get the high-order perturbative data, we can discuss analytic properties (convergence, singularity, analytic continuation, non-perturbative effect etc.) of the QNM frequencies in principle.

General framework

We first illustrate our idea.
In this section, we set up a problem, and explain a conceptual way to obtain perturbative series of QNM frequencies systematically. We will show a technical method to achieve it in the next section.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{0}-V_{0}(x)\right)\Phi_{1}(x)=(V_{1}(x)-\mathcal{E}_{1})\Phi_{0}(x).

III
Technical remark: the Bender-Wu approach

In the previous section, we proposed a general idea to compute the perturbative corrections
\mathcal{E}_{k}
systematically.
The main problem is of course how we solve the differential equation (
12
) for our interested QNM problems.
In this section, we see that this is done by the so-called Bender-Wu approach
bender1969
that is recently extended to the QNM computation in
hatsuda2020
;
eniceicu2020
, based on
blome1984
;
ferrari1984
;
Ferrari:1984zz
.
The main advantage of this approach is that it is widely applicable to many models, as in the WKB approach
mashhoon1983
;
schutz1985
.
The Bender-Wu approach itself also highly depends on perturbation theory.
Since we need eigenfunctions as well as eigenvalues, we review the Bender-Wu approach for our problem.
We follow the notation in
sulejmanpasic2018
as much as possible.

III.1
Leading order solution

Let us solve the zeroth order equation (
8
).
We first introduce a formal parameter
g
by hand,

\displaystyle\left(-g^{4}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{0}-V_{0}(x)\right)\Phi_{0}(x)=0,\qquad\mathcal{E}_{0}=\omega_{0}^{2}.

(19)

It is clear to see that
g^{2}
plays the role of a Planck parameter. Setting
g=e^{\pi i/4}
, the original equation (
8
) is reproduced.
10
10

10

Note that there is another possibility:
g=e^{-\pi i/4}
. This ambiguity reflects the fact that the QNM frequencies have two branches for the real part
hatsuda2020
.

The basic idea is the following. We first consider the eigenvalue problem for
g\in\mathbb{R}
.
In this case, we have the Schrödinger-type equation with the
inverted
potential
-V_{0}(x)
, which admit bound states, and we can apply the standard perturbative method in quantum mechanics near the minimum of
-V_{0}(x)
. The important observation in
hatsuda2020
is that the boundary conditions for bound states and QNMs are simply related by the analytic continuation of
g
. This implies that if we know the bound state energy for
g\in\mathbb{R}
, we can obtain the QNM eigenvalue by the analytic continuation
g=e^{\pi i/4}
.

Let
\bar{x}
be the value of
x
at which
-V_{0}(x)
takes the minimal value.
We expand the inverted potential
-V_{0}(x)
around
x=\bar{x}
:

\displaystyle-V_{0}(x)=V_{00}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}V_{0j}(x-\bar{x})^{j}.

(20)

We introduce a new variable by
x-\bar{x}=gq
.
This change means that as
g
decreases, we zoom in on the neighborhood of the minimum at
x=\bar{x}
.
Then Eq. (
19
) leads to

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dq^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{2}q^{2}+v_{0}(q)-\epsilon_{0}\right)\psi_{0}(q)=0,

(21)

where
\Omega:=\sqrt{V_{02}}
,
\epsilon_{0}:=-(\mathcal{E}_{0}+V_{00})/(2g^{2})
and

\displaystyle v_{0}(q)=\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}V_{0j}(gq)^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g^{j}v_{0j}q^{j+2},\quad v_{0j}:=\frac{V_{0,j+2}}{2}.

(22)

We denoted
\psi_{0}(q)=\Phi_{0}(\bar{x}+gq)
to avoid confusion.
In this picture, the Planck constant is unity, and
g
now plays the role of a coupling constant in the potential.

We solve Eq. (
21
) perturbatively in
g
order by order.
At the leading order, we can regard it as the harmonic oscillator with frequency
\Omega
.
To eliminate the exponential factor of the eigenfunction, we rescale
\psi_{0}(q)=e^{-\Omega q^{2}/2}u_{0}(q)
:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{0}^{\prime\prime}(q)+\Omega qu_{0}^{\prime}(q)+\left(\frac{\Omega}{2}+v_{0}(q)-\epsilon_{0}\right)u_{0}(q)=0.

(23)

We have the following expansions:

\displaystyle u_{0}(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}u_{0n}(q),\qquad\epsilon_{0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}\epsilon_{0n}.

(24)

Plugging these expansions into Eq. (
21
), we get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{0n}^{\prime\prime}+\Omega qu_{0n}^{\prime}+\frac{\Omega}{2}u_{0n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{0j}q^{j+2}u_{0,n-j}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\epsilon_{0j}u_{0,n-j}=0.

(25)

Let us focus on the ground state for simplicity. The ground state corresponds to the lowest (or fundamental) overtone mode in the QNM problem.
For
n=0
, we have the trivial solution
u_{00}(q)=1
and
\epsilon_{00}=\Omega/2
.
Using it, we get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{0n}^{\prime\prime}+\Omega qu_{0n}^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(v_{0j}q^{j+2}-\epsilon_{0j})u_{0,n-j}=0,\quad n\geq 1.

(26)

The very important fact is that
u_{0n}
is a
polynomial of
q
whose degree is at most
3n

bender1969
;
sulejmanpasic2018
:

\displaystyle u_{0n}=\sum_{m=1}^{3n}A_{0n}^{m}q^{m},\quad n\geq 1.

(27)

As shown in
bender1969
, the differential equation (
26
) determines all the coefficients
A_{0n}^{m}
and
\epsilon_{0n}
recursively.
This is what the
Mathematica
program in
sulejmanpasic2018
is doing.
One has to keep in mind that the above result is valid only for the ground state. For the excited states, we need to modify it slightly.
See
sulejmanpasic2018
for these cases.

We finally want to set
g=e^{\pi i/4}
in the perturbative series. However, in general, the formal power series in Eq. (
24
) are not convergent for any
g\neq 0
.
The substitution of
g=e^{\pi i/4}
merely gives a meaningless answer.
To avoid it, one needs to truncate all the high-order corrections beyond a certain optimal order or to use summation methods. Note that the former turns out to be equivalent to the WKB series in the literature
mashhoon1983
;
schutz1985
.
We use the latter, called the Borel summation method, to decode a meaningful result for finite
g
from formal divergent series.
11
11

11

An alternative way is to use Padé approximants

;
konoplya2019
;
matyjasek2019
.

The conclusion in
hatsuda2020
is that the Borel summation of Eq. (
24

We emphasize that the above method allows us to construct not only the eigenvalue
\mathcal{E}_{0}
but also the eigenfunction
\psi_{0}(q)
. In summary, for the ground state, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=-V_{00}-2g^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}\epsilon_{0n},

(28)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(q)

\displaystyle=e^{-\Omega q^{2}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}u_{0n}(q),\qquad u_{0n}(q)=\sum_{m=1}^{3n}A_{0n}^{m}q^{m},

where
\epsilon_{00}=\Omega/2
and
u_{00}(q)=1
.

III.2
First order correction

Let us proceed to the first order correction.
We need to solve

\displaystyle\left(-g^{4}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{0}-V_{0}(x)\right)\Phi_{1}(x)=(V_{1}(x)-\mathcal{E}_{1})\Phi_{0}(x).

(29)

Note that we already know the zeroth order eigenfunction
\Phi_{0}(x)
and eigenvalue
\mathcal{E}_{0}
in the previous subsection.
As in the computation above, we can rewrite it as

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dq^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{2}q^{2}+v_{0}(q)-\epsilon_{0}\right)\psi_{1}(q)=\frac{V_{1}(x)-\mathcal{E}_{1}}{2g^{2}}\psi_{0}(q),

(30)

We also expand
-V_{1}(x)
around
x=\bar{x}
as

\displaystyle-V_{1}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}V_{1j}(gq)^{j}.

(31)

Note that
x=\bar{x}
does not extremize

in general.
As mentioned in the previous subsection, we have to impose the same boundary conditions for
\psi_{0}(q)
and
\psi_{1}(q)

Therefore we set
\psi_{1}(q)=e^{-\Omega q^{2}/2}u_{1}(q)
as well as
\psi_{0}(q)=e^{-\Omega q^{2}/2}u_{0}(q)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\Omega qu_{1}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{2}+v_{0}-\epsilon_{0}\right)u_{1}+\left(\frac{V_{11}}{2g}q+v_{1}-\epsilon_{1}\right)u_{0}=0,

(32)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{1}+V_{10}}{2g^{2}},

(33)

\displaystyle v_{1}(q)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}V_{1j}(gq)^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g^{j}v_{1j}q^{j+2},\qquad v_{1j}=\frac{V_{1,j+2}}{2}.

We use the zeroth order perturbative solution in Eq. (
24
).
From the consistency at the orders
1/g^{2}
and
1/g
, we should take

\displaystyle u_{1}(q)=-\frac{V_{11}}{2\Omega g}q+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}u_{1n}(q),\qquad\epsilon_{1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}\epsilon_{1n}.

(34)

It is observed that for the ground state,
u_{1n}(q)
is a polynomial of at most degree
3n+4
.
After putting an ansatz for the polynomial
u_{1n}(q)
, we can determine all the coefficients of
u_{1n}(q)
and
\epsilon_{1n}
from the perturbative equations.
The remaining computation is the same as the zeroth order one.
By performing the Borel summation of
\epsilon_{1}
, we obtain the first correction
\mathcal{E}_{1}
.

III.3
On higher order corrections

The computations for higher orders are straightforward.
At the
k
-th order, we have

\displaystyle\left(-g^{4}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\mathcal{E}_{0}-V_{0}(x)\right)\Phi_{k}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(V_{\ell}(x)-\mathcal{E}_{\ell})\Phi_{k-\ell}(x).

(35)

It leads to

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}u_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\Omega qu_{k}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\Omega}{2}+v_{0}-\epsilon_{0}\right)u_{k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(\frac{V_{\ell 1}}{2g}q+v_{\ell}-\epsilon_{\ell}\right)u_{k-\ell}=0,

(36)

where
\Phi_{k}(x)=e^{-\Omega q^{2}/2}u_{k}(q)
and

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\ell}:=-\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\ell}+V_{\ell 0}}{2g^{2}},\qquad v_{\ell}(q):=\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}V_{\ell j}(gq)^{j}.

(37)

We observe that the ground state solution in general behaves as

\displaystyle u_{k}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{k,-k}(q)}{g^{k}}+\cdots=\sum_{n=-k}^{\infty}g^{n}u_{kn}(q),

(38)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{k,-2}}{g^{2}}+\cdots=\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}g^{2n}\epsilon_{k,2n},

where
u_{kn}(q)
is a polynomial of at most degree
3n+4k
.
Under this assumption, we can easily compute
\epsilon_{k}
perturbatively in
g
.

IV.1
A toy model: the Rosen-Morse potential

We demonstrate that the idea in Section
II
actually works in the QNM problem for a simple exactly solvable toy model.
What we consider is the so-called Rosen-Morse potential, which is regarded as an integrable deformation of the Pöschl-Teller potential.
The Rosen-Morse potential was studied in the context of the quasinormal modes in massive scalar perturbations
ohashi2004
.
We revisit the same model to validate our framework.
This model is given by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\omega^{2}-V_{\text{RM}}(x)\right)\phi(x)=0,

(39)

\displaystyle V_{\text{RM}}(x)=\frac{1}{2\cosh^{2}x}+\mu^{2}\frac{1+\tanh x}{2}.

where
\mu
is a deformation parameter. If
\mu=0
, the potential reduces to the well-known Pöschl-Teller potential.
The Rosen-Morse potential in Eq. (
39
) for

is very similar to the potential for the spherically symmetric black hole in the massive scalar perturbation
ohashi2004
. We will see it in the next subsection.
We treat this system as a perturbation in the parameter
\mu

We first show that this system is in fact exactly solvable. To do so, we perform a change of variables and a transformation of the wave function by

\displaystyle z=\frac{1}{2}(1+\tanh x),\qquad\phi(x)=z^{-i\omega/2}(1-z)^{-i\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}/2}y(z).

(40)

Then, the new function

satisfies the standard hypergeometric equation:

\displaystyle z(1-z)y^{\prime\prime}(z)+[c-(a+b+1)z]y^{\prime}(z)-aby(z)=0,

(41)

where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}(\omega+\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}+1),

(42)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i}{2}(\omega+\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}-1),

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=1-i\omega.

For a given
\mu

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\phi(x)\sim e^{-i\omega x},\qquad\lim_{x\to+\infty}\phi(x)\sim e^{+i\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\mu^{2}}\,x},

(43)

where we have to choose a branch of the square root so that

In terms of

The regular solution at

\displaystyle y(z)=F(a,b;c;z).

Using the well-known analytic connection formula of the hypergeometric function:

\displaystyle F(a,b,c;z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}F(a,b,a+b-c+1;1-z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(a+b-c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}(1-z)^{c-a-b}F(c-a,c-b,c-a-b+1;1-z),

\displaystyle\omega^{(n,\pm)}=\pm\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu^{2}\frac{1}{4(2n^{2}+2n+1)}\right)-i\left(n+\frac{1}{2}-\mu^{2}\frac{2n+1}{4(2n^{2}+2n+1)}\right).

We have two symmetric branches of the spectra.

\displaystyle\phi^{(n,\pm)}(x)=\left(\frac{1+\tanh x}{2}\right)^{-i\omega^{(n,\pm)}}\left(\frac{1-\tanh x}{2}\right)^{-i\sqrt{\omega^{(n,\pm)2}-\mu^{2}}/2}

\displaystyle{}_{s}A_{\ell m}(c)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c^{k}{}_{s}A_{\ell m}^{(k)},\qquad{}_{s}S_{\ell m}(\xi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c^{k}{}_{s}S_{\ell m}^{(k)}(\xi).

The crucial step is to find the following general structure of the regular function
{}_{s}S_{\ell m}^{(k)}(\xi)
:

\displaystyle{}_{s}S_{\ell m}^{(k)}(\xi)=(1-\xi)^{-\frac{m+s}{2}}(1+\xi)^{\frac{m-s}{2}}{}_{s}Q_{\ell m}^{(k)}(\xi),

(83)

where
{}_{s}Q_{\ell m}^{(k)}(\xi)
is a polynomial of degree
\ell+s+k
in
\xi
. From the differential equation (
79
), we can fix all the coefficients in the polynomial
{}_{s}Q_{\ell m}^{(k)}(\xi)
and
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}^{(k)}
order by order.
This method allows us to compute the exact value of
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}^{(k)}
up to very high orders for given
s
,
\ell
and
m
. We have confirmed that the first few coefficients indeed agree with the results in
berti2006
;
berti2006a
.

Once we know the small
c
expansion of
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}(c)
, we obtain the perturbative expansion of the potential
V(x)
. Then we can apply the method in Section
II
. The result is given by the following small
c
expansion:

\displaystyle M{}_{s}\omega_{\ell m}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c^{k}{}_{s}v_{\ell m}^{(k)}.

(84)

However, we are interested in the perturbative expansion in terms of the rotation parameter
a
rather than
c=a\omega
.
This expansion is easily obtained by plugging Eq. (
84
) into
c=a\omega
and by inversely expanding
c
in
a/M
.
We finally obtain the following perturbative series

\displaystyle M{}_{s}\omega_{\ell m}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a}{M}\right)^{k}{}_{s}w_{\ell m}^{(k)},

(85)

where the explicit values of
{}_{s}w_{\ell m}^{(k)}
for
(s,\ell,m)=(-2,2,0),(-2,2,1),(-2,2,2)
up to
k=12
are found in Table 1 in
hatsuda2021
.

IV.4
Almost asymptotically flat black holes

We can also apply our formalism to asymptitically non-flat geometries.
We focus on the Schwarzschild de Sitter black holes.
In this case, the functions in the minimally coupled massless scalar/vector/odd-parity gravitational perturbations are all given by

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}}{3},

(86)

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=f(r)\left(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+(1-s^{2})\left(\frac{2M}{r^{3}}-\frac{4-s^{2}}{6}\Lambda\right)\right),

where
s=0,1,2
denotes the spin-weight of the perturbation fields, and
\Lambda
is the cosmological costant.
We regard
\Lambda
as a deformation parameter.
In contrast to the previous examples, the function
f(r)
depends on
\Lambda
.
The explicit relation between
r
and
x
is now quite complicated.
As discussed in Section
II
, we have to use the relation in Eq. (
14
) to eliminate
r
.
This can be done at least perturbatively with respect to
\Lambda
.
After this prescription, the potential in terms of
x
receives an infinite number of perturbative corrections.
We apply the Bender-Wu approach for such a perturbative series of the potential.
In the Bender-Wu approach, we need the Taylor series of the perturbative corrections to the potential around the extremal point
x=\bar{x}
of the zeroth potential.
This can be done systematically.

We expand the frequency as

\displaystyle M\omega=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(9M^{2}\Lambda)^{k}w_{k}.

(87)

The numerical values of
w_{k}
for the fundamental mode with
\ell=2
in the gravitational perturbation (
s=2
) up to

IV.5
Reissner-Nordström black holes

The spectrum for the Reissner-Nordström black holes are more involved.
The master equation in the odd-parity gravitational perturbation consists of

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}},

(92)

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=f(r)\left(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{q}{r^{3}}+\frac{4Q^{2}}{r^{4}}\right),

where

\displaystyle q=3M+\sqrt{9M^{2}+4Q^{2}(\ell-1)(\ell+2)}.

(93)

We have two characteristic regimes:
Q=0
and
Q=M
.
We discuss perturbative series around these two points.

\displaystyle M\omega=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{Q}{M}\right)^{2k}w_{k}.

The potential receives an infinite number of corrections.

IV.5.3
An interpolating function

We have the two perturbative expansions of the same spectrum in the different regimes.
In each regime, we determine its Padé approximant, and can extrapolate it to the other regime.
However, to know the global behavior, there is a better approximation, called multi-point Padé approximants
bender1978
;
bakerjr.1996
.
Let us consider a rational function

\displaystyle M\omega^{[p/q]}=\frac{a_{0}+a_{1}Q/M+\cdots+a_{p}(Q/M)^{p}}{1+b_{1}Q/M+\cdots+b_{q}(Q/M)^{q}}.

(97)

We fix the coefficients
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
so that the rational function reproduces the
both
perturbative expansions around
Q/M=0
and
Q/M=1
.
For instance to get the rational function
M\omega^{[4/4]}
we totally need nine data in Eq. (
94
) and in Eq. (
95
).
A balanced choice is to take
w_{k}
(
0\leq k\leq 2
) in Eq. (
94
) and
w_{k}^{\text{ext}}
(
0\leq k\leq 3
) in Eq. (
95
).
Recall the expansion Eq. (
94
) has no odd-order terms. We can use this information to fix
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
.
The explicit values of
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
in this case are shown in Table
4
.
The interpolating function remarkably reproduces the numerical values in the whole regime
0\leq Q/M\leq 1
, as shown in Figure

.

Interpolating functions will be improved if one considers further perturbative expansions around other points in the middle region.
For instance, a perturbative expansion around
Q/M\sim 0.8
will provide us an important information on the global structure of the imaginary part of the QNM frequency for
\ell=2
.
We do not compute it in this work, but expect that our method is still applicable in such situations.

IV.6
Parameterized black hole QNMs

Recently, a simple and effective way to compute perturbative corrections was proposed in
cardoso2019
;
mcmanus2019
;
kimura2020
.
We refer to it as the
parameterized QNM approach
.
As one can see in the previous examples, most deformation terms in the potential take the form as linear combinations of
1/r^{j}
with integral
j
.
At the first order in the perturbation, corrections to the QNM frequencies are the same linear combinations of the potential. See Eqs. (
17
)
and (
18
). The main idea of the parameterized QNM approach is the following. We make a list of corrections generated by only the
1/r^{j}
-deformations beforehand, and use it for a more complicated potential to which corrections are linear combinations of the
1/r^{j}
-deformations. The extension to high-order corrections is straightforward
mcmanus2019
.
Physical applications of the parameterized QNM approach have been shown in
Tattersall:2019nmh
;
hatsuda2020b
;
deRham:2020ejn
;
Volkel:2022aca
;
Volkel:2022khh
;
Franchini:2022axs
;
Lahoz:2023csk
;
Ghosh:2023etd
;
Mukohyama:2023xyf
;
Franchini:2023eda
.
(See also Appendix.
A
for complementary discussion.)

At the technical level, it is not so easy to compute the precise values of the quadratic corrections.
In
cardoso2019
;
mcmanus2019
, the authors used numerical fittings.
Since our formalism is easily applied to the setup of the parameterized QNM approach, we re-evaluate the corrections up to the quadratic order.
This re-evaluation played an important role in the computation of perturbative corrections for slowly rotating black holes
hatsuda2020b
.
We keep at least ten-digit precision for all the corrections listed in this section.
We focus on deformation of the odd-parity gravitational perturbation of the Schwarzschild black holes.
The computations for the other cases are straightforward.
The potential is

(98)

where

is the location of the event horizon and

IV.7
Series expansion method

As an application of our perturbative framework based on a method other than the Bender-Wu approach,
we study the series expansion method known as Leaver’s method
leaver1985
;
konoplya2011
.
We consider the system with the parameterized QNM potential in Eqs (
123
)-(
124
)
with a single correction term

\displaystyle\delta V=\alpha\frac{f}{r_{H}^{2}}\left(\frac{r_{H}}{r}\right)^{j},

(102)

where
f
is given by
f=1-r_{H}/r
.
We assume the following series expansion of the wave function as

\displaystyle\Phi=e^{i\omega r_{*}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}f^{k+n},

(103)

where the characteristic exponent
n
is given by

\displaystyle n=-2ir_{H}\omega,

(104)

so that the QNM boundary condition at
r=r_{H}
is satisfied.
After some calculations, we obtain recursion relations for
a_{k}

\displaystyle A_{k}a_{k-1}+B_{k}a_{k}+C_{k}a_{k+1}+\alpha\sum_{m=0}^{j-2}D_{m}a_{k-m}=0,

(105)

where coefficients
A_{k},B_{k},C_{k}
and
D_{m}
are given by

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=(k-2-2ir_{H}\omega)(k+2-2ir_{H}\omega),

(106)

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=3-2k(1+k)-\ell(\ell+1)+4ir_{H}\omega(1+2k)+8r_{H}^{2}\omega^{2},

(107)

\displaystyle C_{k}

\displaystyle=(1+k)(1+k-2ir_{H}\omega),

(108)

\displaystyle D_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}(j-2)!}{m!(j-2-m)!}.

(109)

The coefficients
a_{k}
with large
k
take exponentially small value
only for the wave function with the appropriate QNM boundary condition at
r\to\infty
.
Thus, we can calculate the approximate QNM frequency by setting

\displaystyle a_{k_{\rm max}}=0,

(110)

with a large integer
k_{\rm max}
.
However, directly solving Eq. (
110
) numerically is
very difficult, and then we usually use
Leaver’s continued fraction method
leaver1985
;
konoplya2011
whose basic equation
is mathematically same as Eq. (
110
).
In this section, we study this problem based on our perturbative approach.

Expanding the coefficients
a_{k}
and the QNM frequency
\omega
as

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=a_{k}^{(0)}+\alpha a_{k}^{(1)}+\alpha^{2}a_{k}^{(2)}+\cdots,

(111)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}+\alpha\omega_{1}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{2}+\cdots,

(112)

the coefficients
A_{k},B_{k},C_{k}
become

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}^{(0)}+\alpha\omega_{1}A_{k}^{(1)}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{1}^{2}A_{k}^{(2,0)}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{2}A_{k}^{(0,1)}+\cdots,

(113)

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=B_{k}^{(0)}+\alpha\omega_{1}B_{k}^{(1)}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{1}^{2}B_{k}^{(2,0)}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{2}B_{k}^{(0,1)}+\cdots,

(114)

\displaystyle C_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}^{(0)}+\alpha\omega_{1}C_{k}^{(1)}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{1}^{2}C_{k}^{(2,0)}+\alpha^{2}\omega_{2}C_{k}^{(0,1)}+\cdots,

(115)

where the coefficients in RHS depend only on
\omega_{0}
.
The recursion relations in Eq. (
105
) at each order become

\displaystyle{\cal O}(\alpha^{0}):\quad

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(0)}a_{k-1}^{(0)}+B_{k}^{(0)}a_{k}^{(0)}+C_{k}^{(0)}a_{k+1}^{(0)}=0,

(116)

\displaystyle{\cal O}(\alpha^{1}):\quad

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(0)}a_{k-1}^{(1)}+B_{k}^{(0)}a_{k}^{(1)}+C_{k}^{(0)}a_{k+1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle+\omega_{1}\Big[A_{k}^{(1)}a_{k-1}^{(0)}+B_{k}^{(1)}a_{k}^{(0)}+C_{k}^{(1)}a_{k+1}^{(0)}\Big]+\sum_{m=0}^{j-2}D_{m}a_{k-m}^{(0)}=0,

(117)

\displaystyle{\cal O}(\alpha^{2}):\quad

\displaystyle A_{k}^{(0)}a_{k-1}^{(2)}+B_{k}^{(0)}a_{k}^{(2)}+C_{k}^{(0)}a_{k+1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+\omega_{1}\Big[A_{k}^{(1)}a_{k-1}^{(1)}+B_{k}^{(1)}a_{k}^{(1)}+C_{k}^{(1)}a_{k+1}^{(1)}\Big]

\displaystyle+\omega_{1}^{2}\Big[A_{k}^{(2,0)}a_{k-1}^{(0)}+B_{k}^{(2,0)}a_{k}^{(0)}+C_{k}^{(2,0)}a_{k+1}^{(0)}\Big]

V
Outlook

In this paper, we proposed a systematic way to compute high-order perturbative corrections to black hole quasinormal mode frequencies with continuous deformation parameters. Our method is widely applicable to many situations, and allows to compute the high-order corrections very accurately.
We showed various explicit examples. In particular, for the Reissner-Nordström black holes, we can expand the quasinormal mode frequency not only around the chargeless limit but also around the extremal limit.

There are several future directions. It is interesting to consider the near extremal expansion of the Kerr black holes.
It was argued that the QNM frequencies in the extremal Kerr geometry have an interesting behavior in
Sasaki:1989ca
.
It is also interesting to develop the perturbative expansion of rotating black holes in modified gravity theories
Srivastava:2021imr
;
Pierini:2021jxd
;
Pierini:2022eim
;
Wagle:2021tam
;
Li:2022pcy
;
Cano:2023tmv
;
Cano:2020cao
;
Cano:2023jbk
;
Cano:2021myl
. In this case, the full analytic solution with the general rotating parameter
is not yet known.
We inevitably have to restrict ourselves to the perturbative treatment in terms of the rotating parameter.
We would like to extend our framework to coupled master equations.
Typically, the master equations in general relativity are decoupled, but in modified gravity theories, they are sometimes coupled
Molina:2010fb
;
Sarbach:2001mc
;
Cardoso:2018ptl
;
McManus:2019ulj
;
Nomura:2021efi
;
Cano:2021myl
;
Hui:2022vov
. Therefore if we consider perturbative expansions of modified parameters, it is desirable to generalize our formalism to such a situation.

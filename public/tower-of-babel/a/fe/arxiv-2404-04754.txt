Abstract
. We revisit certain localised variants of the Bennett–Carbery–Tao multilinear restriction theorem, recently proved by Bejenaru. We give a new proof of Bejenaru’s theorem, relating the estimates to the theory of Kakeya–Brascamp–Lieb inequalities. Moreover, the new proof allows for a substantial generalisation, exploiting the full power of the Kakeya–Brascamp–Lieb theory.

1.2.
Regularised Brascamp–Lieb inequalities

In order to state the main result, we briefly recall some elements of Brascamp–Lieb theory. For
2\leq k\leq n
and
1\leq n_{j}\leq n
for
1\leq j\leq k
, a
Brascamp–Lieb datum
is a pair
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}):=((L_{j})_{j=1}^{k},(p_{j})_{j=1}^{k})
where
L_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}
are linear surjective maps and
p_{j}\in(0,1]
. We let
\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
denote the best constant
C>0
for which the inequality

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(f_{j}\circ L_{j})^{p_{j}}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}}f_{j}\Big)^{p_{j}}

holds for all non-negative functions
f_{j}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}})
that are constant on cubes in the unit cube lattice
\mathcal{Q}^{n_{j}}:=[0,1)^{n_{j}}+\mathbb{Z}^{n_{j}}
. The inequalities (
1.6
) are typically referred to as
regularised
Brascamp–Lieb inequalities and were first introduced in
[
8
]
. Moreover, in
[
8
]
it was shown that
3
3

3

Here the notation
V\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes that
V
is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(1.7)

\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\dim(V)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\dim(L_{j}V)\quad\text{for all $V\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

An advantage of regularised Brascamp–Lieb inequalities is that the characterisation (
1.7
) for the finiteness of
\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
does not involve the scaling condition
\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}n_{j}=n
, which is necessary in the standard Brascamp–Lieb theory
[
7
]
.

1.3.
Main result

Our main theorem generalises Theorem
1.4
in three directions. First, rather than just considering hypersurfaces
S_{j}
, we allow the
S_{j}
to be of arbitrary dimension. Secondly, rather than considering a single submanifold
S_{j}^{\prime}\subseteq S_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq k

[
10

12
,
13
,
18
]
. Finally, rather than work with the transversality condition (
1.4

We first explain the nested setup. Suppose
S_{1}
is a
d_{1}
-dimensional submanifold of a

\mathbb{R}^{n}

is also a submanifold of

\gamma_{1}\colon U_{1}\to U_{0}

To describe the setup precisely,
we say

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Remark 1.7
.

To contextualise the result, we make the following remarks.

a) Let
2\leq k<n
. A prototypical application of our theorem is a
k
-linear estimate for the
n

U:=(-1,1)^{n-1}
and
S:=\Sigma(U)
, where
\Sigma(u):=(u,|u|^{2})

. Let

(1.10)

\Big|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{(2u_{j},-1)}{|(2u_{j},-1)|}\wedge e_{k}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}\Big|\geq 1/2.

If

are sufficiently small neighbourhoods of the points

is a sufficiently small neighbourhood of the origin, then for all

there exists a constant

\int_{Q_{R}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|E_{S}f_{j}|^{\frac{2}{k-1}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\delta^{\frac{(n-k)(n-k+1)}{2(k-1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(U_{j})}^{\frac{2}{k-1}}

for all

.

\mathcal{S}_{k}=(S,(S_{k,\ell})_{\ell=1}^{n-k})

2.
Kakeya–Brascamp–Lieb theory

2.1.
Regularised Brascamp–Lieb revisited

Let
2\leq k\leq n
,
1\leq n_{j}\leq n
for
1\leq j\leq k
and
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
a Brascamp–Lieb datum as in §
1.2
. Given
R\geq 1
and
0\leq\lambda<R
dyadic, we let
\mathrm{BL}_{\lambda,R}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
denote the best constant
C>0
for which the associated generalised Brascamp–Lieb inequality

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{R}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(f_{j}\circ L_{j})^{p_{j}}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}}f_{j}\Big)^{p_{j}}

holds for all non-negative functions

that are constant on cubes of sidelength
\lambda
in the fixed dyadic grid
[0,\lambda)^{n_{j}}+\lambda\mathbb{Z}^{n_{j}}
. Inequalities of this form were first introduced in
[
7
]
and can be seen as a generalisation of the classical scale-invariant Brascamp–Lieb inequalities, which correspond to taking
\lambda\to 0
,
R\to\infty
. Regarding the latter, we let
\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
denote the classical Brascamp–Lieb constant, which is the best constant
C>0
for which (
2.1
) holds for all
R\geq 1
and all non-negative functions
f_{j}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}})
(without any local constancy hypothesis).

The case
\lambda=1
of the above is often referred to as
regularised
.
4
4

4

Note that if
0<\lambda\leq R<\infty
, one can always reduce to the case
\lambda=1
by the scaling relation

\mathrm{BL}_{\lambda,R}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})=\lambda^{n-\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}n_{j}}\mathrm{BL}_{1,R\lambda^{-1}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}).

Maldague
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
recently showed that

(2.2)

\mathrm{BL}_{1,R}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})\sim_{\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}}R^{\alpha(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})}\quad\textrm{for}\quad\alpha(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}):=\sup_{V\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\dim(V)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\dim(L_{j}V)\Big).

Note that the constant
\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
introduced in §
1.2
satisfies

\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})=\sup_{R>1}\mathrm{BL}_{1,R}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p});

in particular, one can recover (
1.7
) from the characterisation (
2.2
) for
\mathrm{BL}_{1,R}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
. We remark that the classical Brascamp–Lieb constant
\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
is finite if and only if
\alpha(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})=0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}n_{j}=n
: see
[
7
]
. In this case, we in fact have

\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})=\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})

as a consequence of a simple limiting argument.

2.2.
Regularised Brascamp–Lieb and transversality conditions

Here we compare the hypotheses of Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.4
and, in particular, address the comments made in Remark
1.7
b).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{p}_{k}=(p_{k})_{j=1}^{k}
where
p_{k}:=q_{k}/2=\frac{1}{k-1}
and suppose that
L_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}
,
1\leq j\leq k
, are linear surjections. Then the following are equivalent:

a)

\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}_{k})<\infty
;

b)

\displaystyle\Big|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{k}\ker(L_{j})\Big|>0
.

Proof.

For
V\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we first note that

(2.3)

\dim(L_{j}(V))=\dim(V)-\dim(V\cap\ker(L_{j})),

by the rank–nullity theorem applied to the restriction
L_{j}|_{V}
,
1\leq j\leq k
. We define the subspace
W:=\ker(L_{1})+\cdots+\ker(L_{k})
.

a)
\Rightarrow
b)
. Assume b) fails. Applying (
2.3
) to
W
as defined above, we deduce that

(2.4)

\dim(L_{j}(W))=\dim(W)-\dim(\ker(L_{j})),

since
\ker(L_{j})\leq W
for all
1\leq j\leq k
. The failure of b) implies that
\dim(W)<\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dim(\ker(L_{j}))
and applying this to (
2.4
) yields

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dim(L_{j}(W))=k\dim(W)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dim(\ker(L_{j}))<(k-1)\dim(W).

This means that
W
violates the finiteness characterisation (
1.7
) for the datum
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}_{k})
, implying that a) fails.

b)
\Rightarrow
a)
. Assume that b) holds, so that
W=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}\ker(L_{j})
. Given
V\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}(V\cap\ker(L_{j}))\leq V\cap W
and therefore

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dim(V\cap\ker(L_{j}))=\dim\bigg(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}V\cap\ker(L_{j})\bigg)\leq\dim(V\cap W).

Combining the above inequality with (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\dim(L_{j}V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\dim(V)-\dim(V\cap\ker(L_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq k\dim(V)-\dim(V\cap W)

\displaystyle\geq(k-1)\dim(V).

Since
V\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
was arbitrary, the characterisation (
1.7
) implies that a) holds.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose the maps
L_{j}
are orthogonal projections. By invoking
[
5
, Proposition 1.2]
, one may deduce that

\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p}_{k})\sim\Big|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{k}\ker(L_{j})\Big|^{-\frac{1}{k-1}},

which is a quantitative variant of Proposition

. Since in this paper we only need finiteness of such transversality quantites, and not uniform boundedness, we leave the proof of this more quantitative result to the interested reader.

.

Let

\mathrm{BL}_{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathbf{L}(\mathscr{E}),\mathbf{p}_{k})<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Big|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{k}N_{\xi_{j}^{\prime}}S_{j}^{\prime}\Big|>0.

1.6

Proof.

For each

, let

be the parametrisation of

, and let

be a submanifold of

2.3.

Fix
2\leq k\leq n
and let
\mathscr{E}=((\mathcal{S}_{j})_{j=1}^{k},\mathbf{q})

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a transverse ensemble in

. Then any subensemble

is also a transverse ensemble.

Proof.

It suffices to verify that

\mathbf{L}(\mathscr{F})
as in (
1.9
). In view of (

), for each

we have that

\Sigma_{j,r_{j}}=\Sigma_{j,\tilde{r}_{j}}\circ\gamma_{j,\tilde{r}_{j},r_{j}}\quad\text{ for }\,\,\gamma_{j,\tilde{r}_{j},r_{j}}:=\gamma_{j,\tilde{r}_{j}+1}\circ\cdots\circ\gamma_{j,r_{j}};

here, for the case

, we simply have

. Thus,

. It is then clear that

Remark 2.5
.

\mathbf{w}_{k-1,\ell}\end{pmatrix}=D\gamma_{k}|_{\gamma_{k,\ell}(s)}D\Phi_{k,\ell}|_{(s;\mathbf{0}_{k,\ell})}\begin{pmatrix}v_{\ell}\\
\mathbf{w}_{k,\ell}\end{pmatrix}+G_{k}\big(\gamma_{k,\ell}(s)\big)w_{k}

for all
1\leq k\leq\ell\leq r
,
s\in U_{\ell}
,
v_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\ell}}
and
\mathbf{w}_{k-1,\ell}=(w_{k},\mathbf{w}_{k,\ell})\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{c_{\ell}-c_{k}}
. Here we have used (
3.2
).

Let
\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell}(s)
denote the Jacobian matrix of
\sigma_{\ell}
at
s
and
B_{k,\ell}(s)\in\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbb{R},d\times m_{k})
denote the composition of matrices

\displaystyle B_{k,\ell}(s):

\displaystyle=D\gamma_{1}|_{\gamma_{1,\ell}(s)}\circ\cdots\circ D\gamma_{k-1}|_{\gamma_{k-1,\ell}(s)}\circ G_{k}\big(\gamma_{k,\ell}(s)\big),

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\sigma_{k-1}\big(\gamma_{k-1,\ell}(s)\big)G_{k}\big(\gamma_{k,\ell}(s)\big),

for
1\leq k\leq\ell
, where here
B_{1,\ell}(s):=G_{1}\circ\gamma_{1,\ell}(s)
and the second equality is due to the chain rule. Furthermore, let
\mathbf{B}_{\ell}(s)\in\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbb{R},d\times c_{\ell})
denote the block matrix

(3.25)

\mathbf{B}_{\ell}(s):=\begin{bmatrix}B_{1,\ell}(s)&B_{2,\ell}(s)&\cdots&B_{\ell,\ell}(s)\end{bmatrix}.

With these definitions, repeated application of (
3.24
) yields

(3.26)

D\Phi_{\ell}|_{(s;\mathbf{0})}\begin{pmatrix}v_{\ell}\\
\mathbf{w}_{\ell}\end{pmatrix}=\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell}(s)v_{\ell}+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}B_{k,\ell}(s)w_{k}=\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell}(s)v_{\ell}+\mathbf{B}_{\ell}(s)\mathbf{w}_{\ell}

for
s\in U_{\ell}
,
v_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\ell}}
and
\mathbf{w}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-d_{\ell}}
. Let
b_{j,\ell}(s)
denote the
j
th column of
\mathbf{B}_{\ell}(s)
and
V_{k,\ell}(s):=\mathrm{span}\,\{b_{1,\ell}(s),\dots,b_{c_{k},\ell}(s)\}
for
1\leq k\leq\ell
. By the non-degeneracy of the parametrisations,

(3.27)

\bigg|\bigwedge_{j=1}^{c_{\ell}}b_{j,\ell}(s)\bigg|\gtrsim 1,\qquad|b_{j,\ell}(s)|\lesssim 1,\qquad\big|V_{k,\ell}(s)\wedge T_{\sigma_{\ell}(s)}M_{k}\big|\gtrsim 1

for all
1\leq k\leq\ell\leq r
,
1\leq j\leq c_{\ell}
, and
s\in U_{\ell}
.

Anisotropic decomposition.
We continue with the nested family
\mathcal{S}=(S,(S_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{r})
from §
3.1
. Let
R\geq 1
and suppose
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
with
R^{-1}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{r}\leq\mu_{\circ}
. Note that the upper bound is slightly stronger than that appearing in the setup in previous subsections, whilst the lower bound corresponds to the regime in which we do not have access to the local constancy property (
3.23
). Set

\mathcal{L}_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}:=\big\{1\leq\ell\leq r:\mu_{\ell}>R^{-1/2}\big\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\ell_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}:=\begin{cases}\min\mathcal{L}_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}-1&\textrm{if $\mathcal{L}_{R^{1/2}}\neq\emptyset$,}\\
r&\textrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

so that if
\mathcal{L}_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{L}_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\{\ell_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}+1,\dots,r\}
. For the ease of notation in this section, we set
\ell_{\star}:=\ell_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
. We define a
d\times d
diagonal matrix

D_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{1}I_{m_{1}}&\cdots&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\mu_{\ell_{\star}}I_{m_{\ell_{\star}}}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\mathbf{0}&R^{-1/2}I_{d_{\star}}\end{bmatrix},

where
d_{\star}:=d-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{\star}}m_{\ell}=\dim(S_{\ell_{\star}})
.
Finally, for
\mathbf{B}_{\ell}(s)
as in (
3.25
), define the
d\times d
matrices

(3.28)

\Lambda_{\ell}(s):=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}_{\ell}(s)&\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma_{\ell}}{\partial s}(s)\end{bmatrix},\qquad s\in W_{\ell},

which, by (
3.27
), are invertible for
1\leq\ell\leq r
, and let

(3.29)

\Lambda_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(s):=\begin{cases}C_{\circ}^{1/2}\Lambda_{\ell_{\star}}(s)\circ D_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}&\textrm{if $1\leq\ell_{\star}\leq r$,}\\
C_{\circ}^{1/2}R^{-1/2}I_{d}&\textrm{if $\ell_{\star}=0$,}\end{cases}

noting that
D_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}=R^{-1/2}I_{d}
in the
\ell_{\star}=0
case.

Define a grid of points

\Gamma_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S}):=R^{-1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{d_{\star}}\cap\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star},r}(\boldsymbol{\mu};2C_{\circ})

where
\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star},r}(\boldsymbol{\mu};2C_{\circ})
is as defined in (
3.12
) and
C_{\circ}\geq 1
is an admissible constant, chosen large enough so that the conclusion of Lemma
3.2
holds. We form a covering of

\mathfrak{N}_{r}(\boldsymbol{\mu}):=\mathfrak{N}_{0,r}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\mathcal{N}_{\mu_{1}}M_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mu_{r}}M_{r}\cap W

by parallelepipeds. Let
\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
denote the family of all parallelepipeds of the form

(3.30)

\theta=u_{\theta}+\Lambda_{\theta}([-1,1]^{d})\quad\textrm{where $u_{\theta}:=\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})$ and $\Lambda_{\theta}:=\Lambda_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(s_{\theta})$}

for
s_{\theta}\in\Gamma_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
. Here
\sigma_{0}:=\mathrm{Id}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Provided
C_{\circ}\geq 1
is sufficiently large, the following properties hold:

i)

The collection
\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
forms a cover of
\mathfrak{N}_{r}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.

ii)

The scaled parallelepipeds
\{4\cdot\theta:\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})\}
are finitely-overlapping.

iii)

4\cdot\theta\cap W\subseteq\mathfrak{N}_{r}(C_{\circ}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mu})
for all
\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
.

Proof.

If
\ell_{\star}=0
, then the
\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
is a standard covering by
R^{1/2}
cubes, and the desired properties are immediate. We therefore assume
1\leq\ell_{\star}\leq r
.

i) By Corollary
3.3
, it suffices to show that
\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
forms a cover of
\Omega_{0,\ell_{\star},r}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Fix
x\in\Omega_{0,\ell_{\star},r}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
so that

x=\Phi_{0,\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})\quad\textrm{for some $s\in\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star},r}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\mu})$ and $\boldsymbol{\eta}\in P_{0,\ell_{\star}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=P_{\ell_{\star}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})$.}

Since
\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star},r}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star},r}(\boldsymbol{\mu};C_{\circ})
, there exists some
s_{\theta}\in\Gamma_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
such that
|s-s_{\theta}|_{\infty}<R^{-1/2}
. The problem is therefore reduced to showing
\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})\in\theta
, provided
C_{\circ}\geq 1
is sufficiently large.

For
u_{\theta}:=\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta};\mathbf{0})
, write

\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})-u_{\theta}=\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})\boldsymbol{\eta}+\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})(s-s_{\theta})+\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})

where

(3.31)

\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta}):=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})-\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})-\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})(s-s_{\theta})-\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})\boldsymbol{\eta}.

By combining the definitions (
3.28
), (
3.29
) and (
3.30
), we have

\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})-u_{\theta}=\Lambda_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\zeta})\qquad\textrm{where}\qquad\boldsymbol{\zeta}:=\big(\Lambda_{\theta}\big)^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})

and

\boldsymbol{\xi}:=C_{\circ}^{-1/2}\big(D_{R^{1/2},\boldsymbol{\mu}}\big)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\eta}\\
s-s_{\theta}\end{bmatrix}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{d};

here we are assuming that
C_{\circ}^{3/8}\geq 2
and using the definition of
P_{\ell_{\star}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
Since
\mu_{k}\leq R^{-1/2}
for
1\leq k\leq\ell_{\star}
, by (
3.26
), (
3.2
) and Taylor’s theorem, we have

(3.32)

|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})|\lesssim|s-s_{\theta}|_{\infty}^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim C_{\circ}^{1/4}R^{-1}.

Thus, since we also have
\mu_{k}\geq R^{-1}
for
1\leq k\leq\ell_{\star}
, we can ensure that
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
provided that
C_{\circ}\geq 1
is sufficiently large.

Combining the above observations,
\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})=u_{\theta}+\Lambda_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\zeta})
where
\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in[-1,1]^{d}
and so
\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})\in\theta
, as required.

ii) The finite overlap property is immediate. Indeed, if
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
are such that
4\cdot\theta_{1}\cap 4\cdot\theta_{2}\neq\emptyset
, then it follows that the corresponding centres
u_{\theta_{i}}=\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta_{i}})
satisfy
|u_{\theta_{1}}-u_{\theta_{2}}|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{-1/2}
. Since
\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}
maps diffeomorphically onto its image, we conclude that
|s_{\theta_{1}}-s_{\theta_{2}}|_{\infty}\lesssim R^{-1/2}
. But for
\theta_{1}
fixed, this is only possible for
O(1)
choices of
\theta_{2}
since the
s_{\theta_{2}}
lie on
R^{-1/2}
separated points.

iii) Fix
\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
with centre
u_{\theta}=\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})
for some
s_{\theta}\in\Gamma_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
.

Let
\ell_{\star}+1\leq t\leq r
so that
s_{\theta}\in\mathcal{N}_{2C_{\circ}\mu_{t}}M_{\ell_{\star},t}
. Thus there exists some
s_{\theta,t}\in M_{\ell_{\star},t}
with
|s_{\theta}-s_{\theta,t}|<2C_{\circ}\mu_{t}
. Consequently,
|u_{\theta}-u_{\theta,t}|\lesssim C_{\circ}\mu_{t}
where
u_{\theta,t}:=\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta,t})\in M_{t}
and so
\mathrm{dist}(u_{\theta},M_{t})\lesssim C_{\circ}\mu_{t}
. On the other hand, it is clear from the definition that
4\cdot\theta
lies in a ball of radius
O(C_{\circ}^{1/2}R^{-1/2})
centred at
u_{\theta}
. Since
\mu_{t}>R^{-1/2}
, we conclude that

(3.33)

4\cdot\theta\subseteq\bigcap_{t=\ell_{\star}+1}^{r}\mathcal{N}_{C_{\circ}^{2}\mu_{t}}M_{t},

provided
C_{\circ}\geq 1
is chosen sufficiently large.

We now consider
1\leq k\leq\ell_{\star}
. Suppose
u\in 4\cdot\theta\cap W
, so that, by the definitions (
3.28
), (
3.29
) and (
3.30
), we can write

u=\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})\boldsymbol{\eta}+\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})(s-s_{\theta})+\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(s_{\theta})

for some
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in P_{\ell_{\star}}(4C_{\circ}^{3/8}\boldsymbol{\mu})
and
s-s_{\theta}\in[-4C_{\circ}^{1/2}R^{-1/2},4C_{\circ}^{1/2}R^{-1/2}]^{d_{\star}}
. Thus,

u=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})-\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta}),

where
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})
is as defined in (
3.31
). Arguing as in (
3.32
), we have
|\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})|\lesssim C_{\circ}R^{-1}
.

Since
\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}\colon W_{\ell_{\star}}\times V_{\ell_{\star}}\to W
is a bijection, we can write
u=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(\tilde{s};\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})
for some
\tilde{s}\in W_{\ell_{\star}}
and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in V_{\ell_{\star}}:=V_{0,\ell_{\star}}
. Taking inverses,

(\tilde{s}-s;\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}-\boldsymbol{\eta})=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}^{-1}\big(\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})-\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})\big)-\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}^{-1}\big(\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(s;\boldsymbol{\eta})\big),

and therefore, by the mean value theorem,
|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}-\boldsymbol{\eta}|_{\infty}\lesssim C_{\circ}R^{-1}
. Since
\mu_{k}\geq R^{-1}
for
1\leq k\leq r
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in P_{\ell_{\star}}(4C_{\circ}^{3/8}\boldsymbol{\mu})
, we have
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in P_{\ell_{\star}}(C_{\circ}\boldsymbol{\mu})
, provided
C_{\circ}\geq 1
is chosen sufficiently large. By the definition (
3.10
), we have
u=\Phi_{\ell_{\star}}(\tilde{s},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\in\Omega_{0,\ell_{\star}}(C_{\circ}\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Our hypotheses ensure that
C_{\circ}\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq C_{\circ}\mu_{r}\leq C_{\circ}\mu_{\circ}
, and so we may apply Lemma
3.2
to deduce that
u\in\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star}}^{+}(C_{\circ}\boldsymbol{\mu})=\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star}}(C_{\circ}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Thus,

(3.34)

4\cdot\theta\cap W\subseteq\mathfrak{N}_{\ell_{\star}}(C_{\circ}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mu})

and combining (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) concludes the proof.
∎

Definition of the wave packets.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
0\leq\psi\leq 1
;
\psi(u)=1
if
|u|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
\psi(u)=0
if
|u|_{\infty}\geq 2
. Define
\tilde{\psi}(u):=\psi(u/2)
, so that
\psi\tilde{\psi}=\psi
. Given
\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
, let

\psi_{\theta}(u):=\psi\big(\Lambda_{\theta}^{-1}(u-u_{\theta})\big)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}(u):=\tilde{\psi}\big(\Lambda_{\theta}^{-1}(u-u_{\theta})\big).

Thus,
\psi_{\theta}(u)=1
if
u\in\theta
,
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{\theta}\subseteq 2\cdot\theta
,
\mathrm{supp}\,\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}\subseteq 4\cdot\theta
and
\psi_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}=\psi_{\theta}
. By Lemma
3.4
i) and ii), we have

1\leq\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})}\psi_{\theta}(u)\lesssim 1\qquad\textrm{for all $u\in\mathfrak{N}_{r}(\boldsymbol{\mu})$.}

Now suppose
f\in L^{2}(S)
is smooth and satisfies
\mathrm{supp}\,f\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mathcal{S}
. This abuse of notation translates as
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
with
\mathrm{supp}\,f\subseteq\mathfrak{N}_{r}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, provided we also assume
\mathrm{supp}\,f\subseteq W
. Under these conditions, we may write

f=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})}f_{\theta}\psi_{\theta}\qquad\textrm{where $f_{\theta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)$ satisfy $|f_{\theta}(u)|\lesssim|f(u)|$ for all $u\in U$.}

Letting
\Lambda_{\theta}^{-\top}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}):=\{\Lambda_{\theta}^{-\top}m:m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
, define

\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S}):=\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})\times\Lambda_{\theta}^{-\top}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S};\theta):=\{\theta\}\times\Lambda_{\theta}^{-\top}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})

for all
\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
. By rescaling and applying a Fourier series decomposition,

f(u)=\sum_{(\theta,v)\in\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})}f_{\theta,v}(u)\quad\textrm{and}\quad(f_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})(u)=\sum_{(\theta,v)\in\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S};\theta)}f_{\theta,v}(u)

for each
\theta\in\Theta_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
, where

f_{\theta,v}(u):=(2\pi)^{-d}|\det\Lambda_{\theta}|^{-1}(f_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})\;\widehat{}\;(v)e^{iv\cdot u}\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}(u).

Here
(f_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})\;\widehat{}\;(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-iv\cdot u}(f_{\theta}\psi_{\theta})(u)\,\mathrm{d}u
is the Fourier transform of
f_{\theta}\psi_{\theta}
evaluated at
v
.

Basic properties of the wave packets.
We identify three key properties of the above decomposition:

Orthogonality.
Parseval’s identity for Fourier series and Lemma
3.4
ii) give

\big\|\sum_{(\theta,v)\in\mathcal{T}}f_{\theta,v}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{(\theta,v)\in\mathcal{T}}\|f_{\theta,v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

for any collection
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
.

Spatial localisation.
Recall
\Sigma_{\ell_{\star}}:=\Sigma\circ\sigma_{\ell_{\star}}
and
S_{\ell_{\star}}=\Sigma_{\ell_{\star}}(U_{\ell_{\star}})
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
\varepsilon_{\circ}:=\varepsilon/(100n)
and define the slabs

(3.35)

T_{\theta,v}:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\big|(\partial_{s}\Sigma_{\ell_{\star}})(s_{\theta})^{\top}x+v_{\theta}^{\star}\big|_{\infty}<R^{1/2+\varepsilon_{\circ}}\big\},\quad v_{\theta}^{\star}:=(\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell_{\star}})(s_{\theta})^{\top}v

for all
(\theta,v)\in\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
. Let
u_{\theta}
be as defined in (
3.30
). Observe that, by the chain rule formula

(\partial_{s}\Sigma_{\ell_{\star}})(s_{\theta})=(\partial_{u}\Sigma)(u_{\theta})\circ(\partial_{s}\sigma_{\ell_{\star}})(s_{\theta})

and the definition of
\Lambda_{\theta}
, we have
\widetilde{T}_{\theta,v}\subseteq T_{\theta,v}
where

(3.36)

\widetilde{T}_{\theta,v}:=\big\{x\in Q_{R}:\big|\Lambda_{\theta}^{\top}\big((\partial_{u}\Sigma)(u_{\theta})^{\top}x+v\big)\big|_{\infty}<R^{\varepsilon_{\circ}}\big\}.

Let
\mathbf{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S}):=\{T_{\theta,v}:(\theta,v)\in\mathcal{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})\}
denote the collection of all slabs of the form (
3.35
) and, for each
T=T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbf{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})
, write
f_{T}:=f_{\theta,v}
. A standard integration-by-parts argument then shows that

|E_{S}f_{T}(x)|\lesssim_{N,\varepsilon}|E_{S}f_{T}(x)|\chi_{T}(x)+R^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(U)}

for all
x\in Q_{R}
and all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
E_{S}
has amplitude
a
supported in the open set
W
. Indeed, we have a stronger estimate with
\chi_{T}
replaced with
\chi_{\widetilde{T}}
for
\widetilde{T}
as defined in (
3.36
), but, for our purposes, we only require localisation to the larger slab
T
.

Preservation of support.
With the above setup, for
C_{\circ}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mu}=(C_{\circ}^{2}\mu_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{r}
, we have

(3.37)

\mathrm{supp}\,f_{T}\cap W\subseteq\mathfrak{N}_{r}(C_{\circ}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mu})\qquad\textrm{for all $T\in\mathbf{T}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathcal{S})$.}

Indeed, this follows from Lemma
3.4
iii) after unravelling the notation and noting that
\mathrm{supp}\,\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}\subseteq 4\cdot\theta
. The localisation (
3.37
) is the key advantage of the above
anisotropic
wave packet decomposition, as opposed to the regular wave packet decomposition used, for instance, in
[
9
,
6
]
, and is crucial for the forthcoming induction-on-scales argument.

4.
The recursive scheme

Throughout this section, we fix
2\leq k\leq n
and

\mathscr{E}=((\mathcal{S}_{j})_{j=1}^{k},\mathbf{q})\quad\textrm{ with }\quad\mathcal{S}_{j}=(S_{j},(S_{j,\ell})_{\ell=1}^{r_{j}})

a transverse ensemble in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of codimension
(m(j,\ell))_{j,\ell}
. We apply the observations of §
3
to each of the nested families of submanifolds
\mathcal{S}_{j}
. In particular, for
1\leq j\leq k
we let
\Phi_{k,\ell}^{(j)}\colon W_{\ell}^{(j)}\times V_{k,\ell}^{(j)}\to W_{k}^{(j)}
denote the diffeomorphism chain and
\mu_{\circ}^{(j)}>0
be the threshold width for
\mathcal{S}_{j}
, as defined in (
3.9
) and on page
3.1
, respectively. We let
\mu_{\circ}:=\min\{\mu_{\circ}^{(1)},\dots,\mu_{\circ}^{(k)}\}>0
.

4.1.
A reformulation

Given
R\geq 1
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j})_{j=1}^{k}
with
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j}:=(\mu_{j,\ell})_{\ell=1}^{r_{j}}
a compatible family of scales for
\mathscr{E}
, set

\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j):=\big\{1\leq\ell\leq r_{j}:\mu_{j,\ell}>R^{-1}\big\}

and

\ell_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j):=\begin{cases}\min\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)-1&\textrm{if $\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)\neq\emptyset$,}\\
r_{j}&\textrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

so that if
\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)=\{\ell_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)+1,\dots,r_{j}\}
. We then set

(4.1)

S_{j,R}:=S_{j,\ell_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)}\quad\textrm{and}\quad U_{j,R}:=U_{j,\ell_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)},\quad 1\leq j\leq k,

where
S_{j,0}:=S_{j}
and
U_{j,0}:=U_{j}
; note that
S_{j,R}
and
U_{j,R}
also depend on
\boldsymbol{\mu}
, but we suppress this for notational simplicity. Consider also the restricted nested family of submanifolds
\mathcal{S}_{j,R}:=(S_{j,R},(S_{j,\ell})_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)})
for
1\leq j\leq k
.

We let
\rho_{\circ}>0
be a constant, chosen sufficiently small for the purposes of the forthcoming argument. In particular, we assume
B(0,\rho_{\circ})\subseteq W_{0}^{(j)}
for
1\leq j\leq k
. For all
R\geq 1
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}
as above, let
\mathfrak{R}(R;\boldsymbol{\mu})
denote the smallest constant
C\geq 1
for which the inequality

(4.2)

\int_{Q_{R}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|E_{S_{j,R}}f_{j}|^{q_{j}}\leq C\mathbf{C}_{R}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(U_{j,R})}^{q_{j}}

holds for

(4.3)

\mathbf{C}_{R}(\boldsymbol{\mu}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)}(\mu_{j,\ell})^{m(j,\ell)q_{j}/2}

whenever
\rho(E_{S_{j,R}})<\rho_{\circ}^{n-\ell_{R,\boldsymbol{\mu}}(j)}
for
1\leq j\leq k

Appendix A
A multilinear Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund theorem

Let
H
be a separable complex Hilbert space and
\{e_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
be a choice of orthonormal basis for
H
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a countable set. Given any
g\in H
, we may write

g=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\langle g,e_{a}\rangle\quad\textrm{where}\quad\|g\|_{H}=\big(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}|\langle g,e_{a}\rangle|^{2}\big)^{1/2}.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
and
(\Omega^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
be measure spaces. We say that
\mathbf{f}\colon\Omega^{\prime}\to H
is
\Sigma^{\prime}
-measurable if
\langle\mathbf{f},g\rangle
is a
\Sigma^{\prime}
-measurable function for all
g\in H
.
Let
T
be a linear mapping sending
\Sigma^{\prime}
-measurable functions on
\Omega^{\prime}
to
\Sigma
-measurable functions on
\Omega
. We define
the vector-valued extension

\mathbf{T}
to be the operator mapping
H
-valued measurable functions on
\Omega^{\prime}
to
H
-valued measurable functions on
\Omega
given by

\langle\mathbf{T}\mathbf{f}(x),e_{a}\rangle:=T(\langle\mathbf{f},e_{a}\rangle)(x)\qquad\textrm{for all $\mathbf{f}\colon\Omega^{\prime}\to H$ measurable and all $a\in\mathcal{A}$.}

This uniquely defines the operator
\mathbf{T}
, which is linear.

The classical Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality
[
15
]
for separable Hilbert spaces has the following multilinear counterpart.

Proposition A.1

(Multilinear Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund Theorem)
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
and
(\Omega_{j},\Sigma_{j},\mu_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq m
be
\sigma
-finite measure spaces. For
1\leq j\leq m
, let
T_{j}
be linear mappings sending
\Sigma_{j}
-measurable functions on
\Omega_{j}
to
\Sigma
-measurable functions on
\Omega
. Let and
0<p_{j},q_{j}<\infty
for
1\leq j\leq k
and further suppose that there exists some
M\geq 0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|T_{j}f_{j}(x)|^{q_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq M\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega_{j},\mu_{j})}^{q_{j}}

holds for all
\Sigma_{j}
-measurable functions
f_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
.

Let
H_{j}
be separable complex Hilbert spaces and let
\mathbf{T}_{j}
denote the vector-valued extensions of
T_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Then the inequality

\int_{\Omega}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathbf{T}_{j}\mathbf{f}_{j}(x)\|_{H_{j}}^{q_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\lesssim_{p,q}M\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|\|\mathbf{f}_{j}\|_{H_{j}}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega_{j},\mu_{j})}^{q_{j}}

holds for all
\Sigma_{j}
-measurable functions
\mathbf{f}_{j}\colon\Omega_{j}\to H_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq k
.

If the Hilbert spaces
H_{j}
are finite dimensional, the result follows from a standard Khintchine’s inequality argument and the Fubini–Tonelli theorem. The extension to separable spaces follows from Fatou’s lemma. We omit the details.

On an inhomogeneous slip-inflow boundary value problems for a steady viscous compressible channel flows
Wengang Yang

Abstract
. We prove the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the steady isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations with inflow boundary conditions for density and mixed boundary conditions for the velocity around a shear flow. In particular, the Dirichlet boundary condition on inflow and outflow part of the boundary while the full Navier boundary conditions on the wall Γ 0 \Gamma_{0} for the velocity filed are considered. For our result, there are no restrictions on the amplitude of friction coefficients α \alpha , and the appropriately large hypothesis for the viscous coefficients μ \mu is enough. One of the substantial ingredients of our proof is an elegant transformation induced by the flow field. With the help of this transformation, we can overcome the difficulties caused by the hyperbolicity of continuity equation, establish the a priori estimates for a linearized system and apply the fixed point arguments.



1.
introduction and main results

In this paper, we consider the steady isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes
equations with inflow boundary condition in a two-dimensional tube
\Omega=(0,1)\times(0,1)
near a shear flow. It is well known that the Navier–Stokes
equations for a steady isentropic compressible viscous flow is a mixed
system of hyperbolic-elliptic type. The momentum equations are an elliptic system in
the velocity, while the continuity equation is hyperbolic in the density. Therefore,
it is necessary to prescribe the density on the part of inflow boundary
(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}<0)
where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal to the part of
inflow boundary. The inflow boundary value problem considered in this article reads
as follows:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\rho\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})-\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}-(\mu+\nu)\nabla\operatorname{div}{\bf u}+\nabla P

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{u}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{in }}\cup\Gamma_{\text{out }},

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\rho_{\text{in }}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{in }},

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0},

\displaystyle 2\mu\mathbf{n}\cdot D({\bf u})\cdot\tau+\alpha{\bf u}\cdot\tau

\displaystyle=b

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0},

where
{\bf u}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the unknown velocity filed of the fluid and
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the unknown density. The
D(\bf{u})
denotes the symmetric part of the velocity gradient, more precisely,

D({\bf u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla{\bf u}+(\nabla{\bf u})^{\mathrm{T}}).

The constant viscosity coefficients
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy
\mu>0,\nu+\mu\geq 0
and
\alpha\geq 0
is a friction coefficient. The pressure P is usually determined through the equation of states. In the case of isentropic flows, the pressure P is given by
P(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
being the heat ratio. Next,
{\bf n}
and
\tau
are outer normal and tangent vectors to
\partial\Omega
. The boundary
\partial\Omega
is naturally divided into the inflow part
\Gamma_{in}
, the outflow part
\Gamma_{out}
, and the impermeable wall
\Gamma_{0}
, more precisely,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{in}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1}=0,0\leq x_{2}\leq 1\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{out}

\displaystyle=\{x_{1}=1,0\leq x_{2}\leq 1\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=\{0\leq x_{1}\leq 0,x_{2}=0\}\cup\{0\leq x_{1}\leq 0,x_{2}=1\}.

The flow of a viscous, compressible fluid were governed by the compressible
Navier-Stokes system, which has been extensively studied. The general existence
results for weak solution to stationary compressible Navier-Stokes system are given
by the work of Lions
[
15
]
under the
assumption that
\gamma>1
in two dimensions and
\gamma\geq\frac{5}{3}
in three
dimensions. Later, this results was improved with the weaker assumptions on the
specific heat ration
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
in three dimensions by
Feireisl
[
4
]
for nonsteady equations. By adapting Feireisl’s nonsteady
approach, the authors in
[
19
]
show that
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
is
also valid to the case of steady system. An overview of these results is
given in the monograph
[
20
,
5
]
. Several refined results for steady
compressible flow were given in
[
7
,
27
,
25
]
. Let us remark that all
those results were obtained under various homogeneous boundary condition.

The strong solutions of steady compressible Navier-Stokes equations with homogeneous
boundary condition has been studied in

[
2
,
10
,
18
,
28
,
29
]
. As to the existence of strong
solutions to the stationary Navier-Stokes system with the inhomogeneous boundary

[
11
]
proved the existence of strong solutions to
the stationary problems with an inflow boundary conditions for the density and
Dirichlet boundary condition for the velocity in a smooth two dimension domain

\Omega
under the assumption that the Reynolds number is small. A mass of
researches showed that the regularity of the strong solutions is restricted by the
geometry of the boundary
[
14
]
. In
[
12
,
13
,
21
]
, the authors
studied the existence and regularity of solutions to an inflow boundary value
problem under the assumption that the viscosity coefficient
\mu
is large enough on
a polygon domain. In
[
22
]
, T. Piasecki proved the existence of strong
solution around a constant equilibrium with an inflow boundary condition for the

the full Navier boundary conditions for the velocity filed under the assumption
that the friction coefficient
\alpha
is large enough. Later, the authors obtained
similar results in a cylinder domains
[
23
]
. It is worth mentioning that the
validity for compressible perturbation of a Poiseuille type flow under the same
boundary conditions as before is also obtained in
[
17
]
. In contrast to the
boundary conditions prescribed above, the authors established the existence of
strong solutions near the constant state
{\bf u}=0,\rho=1
with Dirichlet boundary
condition on
\Gamma_{in}
and

while slip without friction boundary
conditions on the wall
\Gamma_{0}
in
[

[

studied the

large boundary data under additional physical hypotheses called molecular hypothesis

[
3

26
]
for more results concerning the existence of strong

The goal of this paper is to investigate the existence of strong solutions near the
shear flow to the steady isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes system with inflow
boundary conditions in a square. We impose the Dirichlet boundary condition on
inflow and outflow part of the boundary, and the full Navier boundary conditions on
the wall
\Gamma_{0}
for the velocity. Due to the hyperbolicity of the density in
the continuity equation, it is natural to prescribe the density on the inflow part
of the boundary. Let us remark here that we do not need any restrictions on the
amplitude of friction coefficients
\alpha
, and an assumption that
\mu>\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}
is enough. It is worth noting that most of the results discussed above
investigated the structural stability around a constant equilibrium, while our
background solution is a shear flow.

Let us introduce the perturbed flow. Consider the shear flow
(\rho_{0}=1,U_{0})
, with
U_{0}=(1+x_{2},0)=(\bar{U},0)
. It is obviously that the shear flow
(\rho,U_{0})
satisfy the following system:

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\rho_{0}{U_{0}})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\rho_{0}{U_{0}}\otimes{U_{0}})-\mu\Delta{U_{0}}-(\mu+\nu)\nabla\operatorname{div}U_{0}+\nabla P_{0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle{U_{0}}

\displaystyle={U_{0}}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{in }}\cup\Gamma_{\text{out }},

\displaystyle\rho_{0}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{in }},

\displaystyle{U_{0}}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0},

\displaystyle 2\mu\mathbf{n}\cdot D({U_{0}})\cdot\tau+\alpha U_{0}\cdot\tau

\displaystyle=\tilde{b}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0},

where
\tilde{b}
is determined when
U_{0}
and
\alpha
are given. Our aim here is to study the existence and uniqueness of strong solution in a square around the shear flow
(\rho_{0},U_{0})
.
To formulate our main results, it’s convenient to define the quantity
D_{0}
as

(1.2)

D_{0}:=\|{\bf u}_{0}-U_{0}\|_{{2-\frac{1}{p},p};\Gamma_{in}\cap\Gamma_{out}}+\|b-\tilde{b}\|_{1-\frac{1}{p},p;\Gamma_{0}}+\|\rho_{in}-1\|_{{1,p};\Gamma_{in}}

which measure the distance of the initial data away from the shear flow
U_{0}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
2<p<\infty
, if
D_{0}
given by (
1.2
) is small enough and the viscosity coefficients
\mu>\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}
, then there exists a unique solution
({\bf u},\rho)\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to the system
(\ref{S-NS})
such that

(1.3)

\|{\bf u}-U_{0}\|_{{2,p};\Omega}+\|\rho-1\|_{{1,p};\Omega}\leq E(D_{0}),

where
E(D_{0})
can be arbitrarily small provided that the quantity
D_{0}
is small enough.

This paper will be organized as follows. In Section 2, we linearized the original problem (
1.1
) around the shear flow
(\rho_{0},U_{0})
. Next, we introduce an elegant transformation to overcome the difficulties caused by the hyperbolicity of the continuity equation. In Section 3, we establish the
basic and higher order energy estimates to the linearized equations. In order to
overcome the difficulties caused by the nonlinearity for the
Navier–Stokes system, we derive the uniform

W^{2,p}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p}(\Omega),(2<p<\infty)
estimate for the solution
of the linearized system. An iteration scheme is also developed to study the
existence and uniqueness of strong solution. In Section 4, we construct approximated
solutions by a Galerkin method and obtain the existence and regularity of weak
solution to the linearized system. In the Section 5, we show that the iteration
scheme designed in the Section 3 is a contraction and prove our main result Theorem
1.1 finally.

2.
Reformulation of problem

It is convenient to convert the inhomogeneous boundary conditions of the original problems into homogeneous one. To this end, introduce a function
\tilde{\bf u}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{u}_{0}-U_{0}

\displaystyle on\,\Gamma_{in}\cup\Gamma_{out},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0

\displaystyle on\,\Gamma_{0},

\displaystyle 2\mu\mathbf{n}\cdot D(\tilde{\bf u})\cdot\tau+\alpha\tilde{\bf u}\cdot\tau=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0}.

Indeed, one can construct
\tilde{\bf u}
as a solution of the following Lam
\acute{e}
system

-\mu\Delta\tilde{\bf u}-(\mu+\nu)\nabla\operatorname{div}\tilde{\bf u}=0\quad\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\\

with boundary condition (
2.1
).Consequently,

(2.2)

\|\tilde{\bf u}\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C|{\bf u}_{0}-U_{0}|_{W^{2-\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma_{in}\cap{\Gamma_{out}})}.

We denotes the differences

{\mathbf{v}}=\mathbf{u}-\tilde{\mathbf{u}}-U_{0},\,w=\rho-1.

Then, one can yields
(\bar{\mathbf{u}},\bar{\rho})
satisfy the following system
by straightforward computation,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\ \bar{U}\partial_{1}w+({\bf v}+\tilde{\mathbf{u}})\cdot\nabla w+\operatorname{div}{\bf v}

\displaystyle=F({\bf v},w)

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\ \bar{U}\partial_{1}{\bf v}+{\bf{v}}\cdot\nabla U_{0}+\gamma\nabla w-\mu\Delta{\bf v}-(\mu+\nu)\nabla\operatorname{div}{\bf v}

\displaystyle=G({\bf v},w)

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\ {\bf v}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{in }}\cup\Gamma_{\text{out }},

\displaystyle\ w

\displaystyle=\rho_{\text{in }}-1

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{in }},

\displaystyle\ {\bf v}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0},

\displaystyle 2\mu\mathbf{n}\cdot D({\bf v})\cdot\tau+\alpha{\bf v}\cdot\tau

\displaystyle=B

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0},

where

(2.4)

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle b-\tilde{b},

\displaystyle F({\bf v},w)=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}\tilde{\mathbf{u}}-w\operatorname{div}({\bf v}+\tilde{\mathbf{u}}),

\displaystyle G({\bf v},w)=

\displaystyle-(w+1)\left({\bf v}+\tilde{\mathbf{u}}+U_{0}\right)\cdot\nabla\tilde{\mathbf{u}}-(w+1)({\bf v}+\tilde{\mathbf{u}})\cdot\nabla{\bf v}

\displaystyle-wU_{0}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}-\tilde{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla U_{0}-w({\bf v}+\tilde{\mathbf{u}})\cdot\nabla U_{0}

\displaystyle+\mu\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{u}}+(\mu+\nu)\nabla\operatorname{div}\tilde{\mathbf{u}}+\gamma\left[(w+1)^{\gamma-1}-1\right]\nabla w.

A straightforward computation gives the estimates of
F
and
G
as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F({\bf v},w)
and
G({\bf v},w)
be defined by (
2.4
).Then we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|F({\bf v},w)\|_{W^{1,p}}+\|G({\bf v},w)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C[(\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|w\|_{W^{1,p}})^{3}+(\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|w\|_{W^{1,p}})^{2}]

\displaystyle+E(D_{0})(\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|w\|_{W^{1,p}})+\|\tilde{\bf u}\|_{W^{2,p}}.

Proof.

The estimates is almost obviously by the embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, one can easily check it term by term. In fact, the term
w\divg{\bf v}
in
F({\bf v},w)
is estimated as

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|w\divg{\bf v}\|_{1;p}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{\infty}\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{p}+\|\nabla w\|_{p}\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{\infty}+\|w\|_{\infty}\|\nabla^{2}{\bf v}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|w\|_{W^{1,p}})^{2}.

The most subtle term in
G({\bf v},w)
is controlled by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|w{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{\infty}\|{\bf v}\|_{p}\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\bf v}\|_{2;p}^{2}\|w\|_{1;p}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|w\|_{W^{1,p}})^{3},

hence, the results follows.
∎

We want to prove the existence of solution by fixed point theorem. However, a direct application of fixed point arguments may fail due to the term
{\bf v}\cdot\nabla w
on the left hand side of (
2.3
) which would cause the loss of regularity on the right hand side of the system. Fortunately, we can introduce a good enough transformation such that the problematic term disappear in the new coordinates. Indeed, consider the following transformation
\psi:(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2})
satisfy

(2.8)

\begin{cases}x_{1}=\psi^{1}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1},\\
x_{2}=\psi^{2}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}+\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\tilde{v}(t,\psi^{2}(t;z_{2}))dt,\\
\end{cases}

where
\tilde{v}=\frac{({\bf v+\tilde{\bf u}})^{(2)}}{\bar{U}+({\bf v+\tilde{\bf u}})^{(1)}}
. Thanks to the smallness assumption, the transformation
\psi:\tilde{\Omega}\rightarrow\Omega
is a diffeomorphism. Furthermore, if we denote

(2.9)

{\bf E}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0\\
\tilde{v}(\psi(z))&\partial_{z_{2}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\tilde{v}(\psi(t,z_{2}))dt\\
\end{array}\right),

with
\nabla\psi={\bf Id}+{\bf E}
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\psi
is defined in (
2.8
), then
\psi
is a diffeomorphism such that
\psi(\tilde{\Omega})=\Omega
, moreover

(2.10)

\|{\bf E}\|_{W^{1,p}(\tilde{\Omega})}\leq E,

can be arbitrary small provided
\|{\bf v}\|_{2,p},\|\tilde{\bf u}\|_{2,p}

Since
\bar{U}
is strictly positive and
\|{\bf v}\|_{2,p},\|\tilde{\bf u}\|_{2,p}
is small, there exists a positive constant
c

\bar{U}+({\bf v}+\tilde{\bf u})^{(1)}\geq c>0,

it follows that
\tilde{v}\in W^{2,p}

(2.11)

\|\tilde{v}\|_{2,p}\leq E(\|{\bf v}\|_{2,p},\|\tilde{\bf u}\|_{2,p}).

At this stage,the first part is standard, one can refer to
[

2.10

\displaystyle|E_{22}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq|\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\nabla\tilde{v}(\psi(t,z_{2}))\cdot\partial_{z_{2}}\psi(t,z_{2})dt|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq|z_{1}|^{p-1}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}|\nabla\tilde{v}(\psi(t,z_{2}))\cdot\partial_{z_{2}}\psi(t,z_{2})|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{v}\|_{\infty}^{p}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}|1+E_{22}|^{p}dt,

Integrating it over

\|E_{22}\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{v}\|_{\infty}\|1+E_{22}\|_{p}.

Differentiate

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{1}}E_{22}=\nabla\tilde{v}(\psi(z_{1},z_{2}))\cdot\partial_{z_{2}}\psi(z_{1},z_{2}),

similarly, differentiate

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{2}}E_{22}

\displaystyle=\partial_{z_{2}}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{v}(\psi(t,z_{2}))\partial_{z_{2}}\psi^{i}(t,z_{2})dt

\displaystyle|I_{1}|^{p}

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\partial_{x_{i},x_{j}}\tilde{v}(\psi(t,z_{2}))\partial_{z_{2}}\psi^{j}(t,z_{2})\partial_{z_{2}}\psi^{i}(t,z_{2})dt|^{p}

finally, combine all those inequality together the estimate (

3.
a priori estimates

In this section, we construct a sequence that will converge to a solution of the nonlinear system (

(3.1)

\displaystyle((\bar{U}+({{\bf v}^{n}+\tilde{\bf u}})^{(1)})\circ\psi_{{\bf v}^{n}+\tilde{\bf u}})\partial_{z_{1}}w^{n+1}+\operatorname{div}_{z}{\bf v}^{n+1}=\tilde{F}({\bf v}^{n},w^{n})\quad\text{ in }\tilde{\Omega},

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle v}^{n+1}=0\quad\text{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{\text{in }}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{\text{out }},

\displaystyle w^{n+1}=\rho_{in}-1\quad\text{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{in}

\displaystyle 2\mu\mathbf{n}\cdot D_{z}({\bf v}^{n+1})\cdot\tau+\alpha{\bf v}^{n+1}\cdot\tau=\tilde{B}({\bf v}^{n})\quad\text{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{0},

To show the existence of the solutions to (

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle v}=0\quad\text{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{\text{in }}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{\text{out }},

\displaystyle w=w_{in}\quad\text{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{\text{in }},

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\quad\text{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{\text{0 }},

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}w\operatorname{div}{\bf v}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\ [\mu\pi^{2}

Next, we want to show higher regularity up to boundary by the method of difference quotient.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
({\bf v},w)\in H^{1}\times L^{\infty}(L^{2})

3.2
) with

(3.15)

\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|{\bf v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}}+|\tilde{B}|_{H^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\Gamma_{0})}+|w_{in}|_{H^{1}({\Gamma_{in}})}).

Let us firstly focus on the interior estimates. By using the standard

z_{2}

(3.16)

where

. Hence, in order to find a bound of

\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}

\|\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

({\bf v},w)\in H^{1}\times L^{\infty}(L^{2})
be a solution of the system (
3.2
), which means

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bar{U}\partial_{z_{1}}{\bf v}

\displaystyle\cdot\phi dz+\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}\cdot\nabla U_{0})\cdot\phi dz-\gamma\int_{\Omega}w\operatorname{div}\phi dz+\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla{\bf v}:\nabla\phi dz

\displaystyle+(\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\operatorname{div}\phi dz=\int_{\Omega}g\cdot\phi dz

\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. There is no risk of confusing, we may remove the superposition of
\psi
.
Replace
\phi

3.17

\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi
, then we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}w\operatorname{div}(\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi)dz+\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla{\bf v}:\nabla(\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi)dz+(\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\operatorname{div}(\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{2}^{h}w)\operatorname{div}\phi dz-\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v}):\nabla\phi dz-(\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})\operatorname{div}\phi dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\bar{U}\partial_{z_{1}}{\bf v}\cdot(\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi)dz-\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}\cdot\nabla U_{0})\cdot(\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi)dz+\int_{\Omega}g\cdot\Delta_{2}^{-h}\phi.

For any interval

, selecting a cut-off function

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{2}^{h}w)\operatorname{div}\phi dz

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{2}^{h}w)[2\eta(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})\cdot\nabla\eta+\eta^{2}\operatorname{div}\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v}]dz

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{2}^{h}w)[2\eta(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})\cdot\nabla\eta-\eta^{2}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})\partial_{z_{1}}(\Delta_{2}^{h}w)]dz

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{\Omega}(\Delta_{2}^{h}w)[\eta^{2}\Delta_{2}^{h}f-\eta^{2}\partial_{z_{1}}w^{h}\Delta_{2}^{h}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})]dz

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{in}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})|\eta\Delta_{2}^{h}w|^{2}ds

and

(3.20)

\displaystyle-\mu

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v}):\nabla\phi dz-(\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})\operatorname{div}\phi dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|\nabla(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})|^{2}dz-(\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|\operatorname{div}(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})|^{2}dz

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\mu\int_{\Omega}\eta\nabla(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v}):(\nabla\eta\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})dz+2(\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\eta\operatorname{div}(\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v})\Delta_{2}^{h}{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\eta dz,

here

in

On the other hands, (

(3.23)

\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(\|\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}\|_{{H^{1}}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|f\|_{{H^{1}}(\Omega)}+|\partial_{z_{2}}w(0,z_{2})|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{in})}),

.

As to the boundary estimates near

3.3.
W^{2,p}
estimates of linear system

The crucial point to establish the
W^{2,p}
estimates is to find a bound of
\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|_{L^{p}}
, a key observation shows that it can be bounded by
\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}}
. To this end, we introduce the following lemma concerning the so called Bogovskii operator which is proved by Bogovskii in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is starlike with respect to some ball contained in it, and that
1<p<\infty
and
n\geq 2
, Then, the exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
n,p
and
\Omega
, such that for any
h\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
\int_{\Omega}h(x)dx=0
, there is a vector field
\omega\in W_{0}^{1,p}
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\divg\omega=h\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\omega=0\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

and

\|\omega\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where the constant
C
depends only on
p
and
\Omega
.

By constructing a proper test function defined by Bogovskii operator, we can establish the following results.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
({\bf v},w)
be a solution of (
3.2
) with
(f,g,\tilde{B},w_{in})\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times H^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\Gamma_{0})\times W^{1,p}(\Gamma_{in})
, then for any
p\in(2,\infty)
we have

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{2}}w\|_{L^{p}}+|\partial_{z_{2}}w|_{L^{p}(\Gamma_{out})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}}+\|f\|_{W^{1,p}}+\|g\|_{L^{p}}+|\tilde{B}|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\Gamma_{0})}+|w_{in}|_{W^{1,p}({\Gamma_{in}})})

Proof.

Setting
\phi=\mathfrak{B}(\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}-M)
, then by the properties of Bogovskii operator we have

(3.25)

\|\phi\|_{W^{1,\frac{p}{p-1}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
M=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}dz
.  Next,
differentiate the momentum equation in (
3.2
) with respect to
z_{2}
and
multiply the resulting equations by
\phi
we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p}dz+\gamma M\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{z_{2}}w|dz+(2\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}\operatorname{div}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}dz

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}(\operatorname{curl}{\bf v}):\nabla\phi dz+(\mu+\nu)M\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}dz+\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{z_{2}}\bar{U})\partial_{z_{1}}{\bf v}\cdot\phi dz

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bar{U}(\partial_{z_{1},z_{2}}{\bf v})\cdot\phi dz+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}\nabla U_{0}\phi dz+\int_{\Omega}{\bf v}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{z_{2}}U_{0})\phi dz=-\int_{\Omega}g\partial_{z_{2}}\phi dz

Consequently,

(3.27)

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p}dz-(2\mu+\nu)\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}\operatorname{div}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}dz

\displaystyle\leq(\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}\|_{L^{p}}+\|g\|_{L^{p}}+\|{\bf v}\|_{W^{1,p}})\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+(\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|_{L^{1}}+\|\nabla^{2}{\bf v}\|_{L^{1}})\|\partial_{z_{2}}w\|^{p-1}_{L^{p-1}}.

To control the second term of the l.h.s of (
3.27
), we differentiate the mass equation in (
3.2
) to obtain

(3.28)

\partial_{z_{2}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})\partial_{z_{1}}w+(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})\partial_{z_{1},z_{2}}w+\operatorname{div}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}=\partial_{z_{2}}f

and hence

(3.29)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}\operatorname{div}\partial_{z_{2}}{\bf v}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}[\partial_{z_{2}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})\partial_{z_{1}}w+(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})\partial_{z_{1},z_{2}}w-\partial_{z_{2}}f]dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}\partial_{z_{1}}wdz-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{2}}w|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p-2}\partial_{z_{2}}f

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{z_{1}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p}+\int_{\Gamma_{out}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p}ds-\int_{\Gamma_{in}}(\bar{U}+\bar{\bf v}^{(1)})|\partial_{z_{2}}w|^{p}ds.

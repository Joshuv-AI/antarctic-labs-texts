Undecidability in Finite Transducers, Defense Systems and Finite Substitutions
Vesa Halava

Abstract
In this manuscript we present a detailed proof for undecidability of the equivalence of finite substitutions on regular language b ​ { 0 , 1 } ∗ ​ c b\{0,1\}^{*}c . The proof is based on the works of Leonid P. Lisovik.

Finite transducers

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and denote by
\epsilon
the empty word.
The star operation on
\Sigma
,
\Sigma^{*}
, is as usual the set of
all word over
\Sigma
. Denote by
\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma^{*}\setminus\{\epsilon\}
.

We begin with a definition of
finite transducer
, FT for short,
which is a 6-tuple
(Q,\Sigma,\Delta,E,q_{0},F)
, where

•

Q
is a finite set of states,

•

\Sigma
and
\Delta
are input and output alphabets,

•

E\subseteq Q\times\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*}\times Q
is a finite
set of transitions,

•

q_{0}\in Q
is the initial state and
F\subseteq Q
is the set of
final states.

FT is a finite automaton with output. If the underlying
automaton is nondeterministic, then FT is called
generalized
sequential machine
, GSM for short, or
sequential transducer
.

Let
T
be a finite transducer. Define the set

\displaystyle O(T)=

\displaystyle\{(w,y)\mid w=a_{0}\dots a_{n},\quad y=b_{0}\dots b_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad a_{i}\in\Sigma^{*},

\displaystyle b_{i}\in\Delta^{*},\quad 0\leq i\leq n,\text{ and there exists states }q_{i}\in Q,\text{ such that }

\displaystyle(q_{i},a_{i},b_{i},q_{i+1})\in E\text{ and }q_{n+1}\in F\}

If
(w,y)\in O(T)
, then we say that
(w,y)\in\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*}
is
recognized by
T
.

Let

L(T)=\{w\mid(w,y)\in O(T)\text{ for some }y\}

be the language accepted by the finite transducer
T
.

A subset
O(T)
of
\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta
, which is recognized by
a FT
T
is called
rational relation
. We denote the family of rational
relations of
\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*}
by
\rat(\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*})
.

It is clear that if
A,B\in\rat(\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*})
, then

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\cup B\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle A\cdot B=AB

\displaystyle=\{(w_{1}w_{2},y_{1}y_{2})\mid(w_{1},y_{1})\in O(A),(w_{2},y_{2})\in O(B)\}

are in
\rat(\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*})
. The union is clear, since
we may connected the FT’s that recognize
A
and
B
, by merging
their initial states of FT’s recognizing
A
and
B
. The product

AB
is recognized, by an FT, where we define every final state of
FT recognizing
A
to be a initial state of the FT recognizing
B
.

The star operation for subset
U
of
\Sigma^{*}\times\Delta^{*}
is
defined naturally by

U^{*}=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}U^{i},

where
U^{i}
is the
i
’th power of
U
defined using the product by initial values
U^{0}=\{\epsilon\}\times\{\epsilon\}
,

U^{1}=U
, and
U^{i+1}=UU^{i}
for all
i\geq 1
.

We shall next prove that the equivalence and inclusion of two
rational relations is an undecidable problem in the case where

\Delta
is unary. This result has many proofs, for example cf.

[
4
]
,
[
8
]
. We shall here present the construction
from
[
8
]
.

Before the theorem, recall that the
Post Correspondence Problem
, PCP
for short, which asks for a given pairs of non-empty words over
alphabet
\Gamma
,

(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2}),\dots,(u_{n},v_{n})
, whether there exists a sequence

1\leq\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{s}\leq n

such that

u_{\alpha_{1}}u_{\alpha_{2}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}=v_{\alpha_{1}}v_{\alpha_{2}}\dots v_{\alpha_{2}},

is known to be an undecidable problem. For more details about the PCP, cf.
[
11
]
,
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be two rational relations from
\rat(\Sigma^{*}\times c^{*})
.
Then it is undecidable, whether

\displaystyle 1)\quad A

\displaystyle\subseteq B,

\displaystyle 2)\quad A

\displaystyle=B.

Proof.

Assume that
(u_{1},v_{1}),\dots,(u_{n},v_{n})
is a sequence of pairs of non-empty
words over

\{a,b\}
. Define alphabet
\Sigma=\{a,b,i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\}
, and

k_{\alpha}=|u_{\alpha}|
for all
\alpha=1,2,\dots,n
.

Next we define needed subsets of
\Sigma^{+}\times c^{+}
:

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(i_{\alpha},c^{k_{\alpha}+1})\mid 1\leq\alpha\leq n\}^{*},

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\beta=1}^{n}\bigcup_{j=1}^{k_{\beta}}L_{\beta j},

where

L_{\beta j}=L_{1}\cdot(i_{\beta},c^{j})\{(i_{\alpha},c)\mid 1\leq\alpha\leq n\}^{*}
,

\displaystyle L_{3}

\displaystyle=L_{2}\{(a,c),(b,c)\}^{*},

\displaystyle L_{4}

\displaystyle=L_{1}\{(a,c),(b,c)\}^{*}\{(a,c^{2}),(b,c^{2})\}^{+}.

Finally, for
\beta\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, let

\displaystyle S_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\{\mu\mid\mu\in\{a,b\}^{*},|\mu|=|u_{\beta}|,\mu\neq u_{\beta}\},

and set

\displaystyle L_{5}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\beta=1}^{n}\bigcup_{\mu\in S_{\beta}}M_{\beta\mu},

where

\displaystyle M_{\beta\mu}

\displaystyle=L_{1}(i_{\beta},c)\{(i_{\alpha},c)\mid 1\leq\alpha\leq n\}^{*}\{(a,c),(b,c)\}^{*}(\mu,c^{2k_{\beta}})\{(a,c^{2}),b,c^{2})\}^{*}.

Now we define

L_{u}=L_{3}\cup L_{4}\cup L_{5}.

Similarly, let
L_{v}
be defined for the second components of the pairs
(u_{\alpha},v_{\alpha})
in the sequence. Note that
L_{u}
and
L_{v}
are in
\rat(\Sigma^{*}\times c^{*})
, since we can define nondeterministic
FT’s to recognize
L_{1}
,
L_{\beta j}
’s,
M_{\beta\mu}
’s and
therefore also
L_{2}
,
L_{3}
,
L_{4}
and
L_{5}
are rational
relations.

Next define

L_{0}=\{(i_{\alpha},c)\mid 1\leq\alpha\leq n\}^{+}\{(a,c^{2}),(b,c^{2})\}^{+}
.
It is easy to construct a FT, that recognizes
L_{0}
.

Claim.

L_{0}\subseteq L_{u}\cup L_{v}
if and only if
there does not exist sequence of
\alpha_{i}
’s, such that
1\leq\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\leq n

and

u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}=v_{\alpha_{1}}\dots v_{\alpha_{s}}
.

Proof of the Claim.
Assume that there exists such sequence
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}
, that PCP has solution and let

w=(x,y)=(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}}u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}},c^{s+2(k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}})})\in L_{0}.

(i) If
w\in L_{3}
, then for some

w_{1}=(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{alpha_{s}},c^{m})\in L_{2}
,

w=w_{1}(u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}},c^{k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}}).

Therefore
w_{1}\in L_{\beta j}
, for some
\beta\in\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\}
and
1\leq j\leq k_{\beta}
, and so in
path recognizing
w_{1}
,
i_{beta}
has outputs
c^{j}
and

j<k_{\beta}+1
, so
m<k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}+s
. Therefore

w\notin L_{3}
.

(ii) Let
\beta_{i}
’s,
i\in\{1,r\}
be a sequence such that

1\leq\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}\leq n
, and let

w_{1}=(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}}u_{\beta_{1}}\dots u_{\beta_{r}},c^{m})\in L_{4}.

In the recognizing paths of
w_{1}
, for each
i_{\alpha_{j}}
the
output is
k_{\alpha_{j}}+1
and for
u_{j}
,

j\in\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}\}
, the output is
c^{\ell_{j}}
, where

\ell_{j}\geq k_{j}
and at least for one
j

\ell_{j}>k_{j}
, because of

\{(a,c^{2}),(b,c^{2})\}^{+}
. So we have that

m>k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}+s+k_{\beta_{1}}+\dots+k_{\beta_{r}}
,
and therefore
w\notin L_{4}
.

(iii) Assume that
w\in L_{5}
. Then there exists integer
\beta
,

1\leq\beta\leq s
, and
\gamma_{1},\mu,\gamma_{2}\in\{a,b\}^{*}
such that

w\in M_{\alpha_{\beta}\mu}
,
u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}=\gamma_{1}\mu\gamma_{2}
,

|\mu|=u_{\alpha_{\beta}}
and
\mu\neq u_{\alpha_{\beta}}
. If

(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{\beta}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}}\gamma_{1}\mu\gamma_{2},c^{m})\in M_{\alpha_{\beta}\mu},

then

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=(k_{\alpha_{1}}+1)+\dots+(k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}+1)+(s-\beta+1)+|\gamma_{1}|+2|\mu|+2|\gamma_{2}|

\displaystyle=k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}+s+|\gamma_{1}|+2|\mu|+2|\gamma_{2}|.

Now since
w\in L_{5}
and
|\gamma_{1}\mu\gamma_{2}|=k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}
, we get that

|\mu|+|\gamma_{2}|=k_{\alpha_{\beta}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}},

and since
|\mu|=k_{\alpha_{\beta}}
, finally

|\gamma_{2}|=k_{\alpha_{\beta+1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}\text{ and }|\gamma_{1}|=k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}.

It follows that
\mu=u_{\alpha_{\beta}}
and we have a contradiction.
Therefore
w\notin L_{5}
.

So
w\notin L_{u}
and by similarly it can shown that
w\notin L_{v}
,
and we have proved one direction of the claim.

Assume now that there is no sequence
1\leq\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s}\leq n
such that the instance of PCP has
solution. Let
w_{1}\in\{a,b\}^{+}
and
w=(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}}w_{1},c^{s+2|w_{1}|})\in L_{0}
.

By assumption,
w_{1}\neq u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}
or

w_{1}\neq v_{\alpha_{1}}\dots v_{\alpha_{s}}
.
We shall
show that if
w_{1}\neq u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}
, then

w\in L_{u}
. Of course then similarly, if

w_{1}\neq v_{\alpha_{1}}\dots v_{\alpha_{s}}
, then
w\in L_{v}
.

(i) If
|w_{1}|>|u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}|
, i.e.

|w_{1}|>k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}
, then
for some

x,y\in\{a,b\}^{+}
,
|x|=k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}
,
w_{1}=xy
and

w=(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}},c^{k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}+s})(x,c^{k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}})(y,c^{2|y|})\in L_{4}.

(ii) If
|w_{1}|<|u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}|
, i.e.

|w_{1}|<k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}
, then there exists

\beta\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,k_{\alpha_{\beta}}\}

such that

|w_{1}|=k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}+j-1,

and so

\displaystyle w=

\displaystyle(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{\beta-1}},c^{(k_{\alpha_{1}}+1)+\dots+(k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}+1)})(i_{\alpha_{\beta}},c^{j})

\displaystyle\cdot(i_{\alpha_{\beta+1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}},c^{s-\beta})(w_{1},c^{k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}+j-1})\in L_{3}.

(iii) If
|w_{1}|=k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{s}}
, then since

w_{1}\neq u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}
, there exists

\beta\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and
\mu,\gamma\in\{a,b\}^{*}
such that

w_{1}=u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}\mu\gamma\text{ and }|\mu|=|u_{\alpha_{\beta}}|\text{ but }\mu\neq u_{\alpha_{\beta}},

and so

\displaystyle w=

\displaystyle(i_{\alpha_{1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{\beta-1}},c^{(k_{\alpha_{1}}+1)+\dots+(k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}+1)})(i_{\alpha_{\beta}},c)

\displaystyle\cdot(i_{\alpha_{\beta+1}}\dots i_{\alpha_{s}},c^{s-\beta})(u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{\beta-1}},c^{k_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+k_{\alpha_{\beta-1}}})(\mu,c^{2|u_{\alpha_{\beta}}|})(\gamma,c^{2|\gamma|})\in L_{5}.

So
w\in L_{u}
, if
w_{1}\neq u_{\alpha_{1}}\dots u_{\alpha_{s}}
and so
the claim is proved.

Now by the undecidability of PCP, it is undecidable whether

L_{0}\subseteq L_{u}\cup L_{v}
and whether
L_{0}\cup L_{u}\cup L_{v}=L_{u}\cup L_{v}
. This proves the theorem.
∎

\displaystyle\subseteq B,

We shall next define a special type of finite transducer, so called

-transducer

-transducer is called

Let

\displaystyle O(T)=

\displaystyle\{(w,y)\mid w=a_{0}\dots a_{n},\quad y=b_{0}\dots b_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad a_{i}\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle b_{i}\in\{c,cc\},\quad 0\leq i\leq n,\text{ and there exists states }q_{i}\in Q,\text{ such that }

\displaystyle(q_{i},a_{i},b_{i},q_{i+1})\in E\text{ and }q_{n+1}=q_{f}\}

Note that

-transducer

Defense Systems

In this section we shall consider so called
defense systems
, DS for short.
Result in this section is from
[
9
]
.
A DS system is intended to defense some elements of the set integers
\mathbb{Z}
. The
elements of
\mathbb{Z}
are also called defense
nodes
.
Any DS is a triple
V=(K,H,\Gamma)
, where
K
is set of
lines
,

K=\{i\mid 1\leq i\leq s,\quad i,s\in\mathbb{Z}\},

H
is the set of instructions and
\Gamma
is the set of attacking symbols.

Each node can be defended by lines from
K
. In other words, each node
can be defended by
s
different lines. The initial situation in our case
is that
only node 0 is defended by line 1,
and the other nodes don’t have defence at all.

The attacking system is supposed to ‘send’ symbols from the set
\Gamma
to
the defending system. This means that attacks can be thought as a words from

\Gamma^{*}
.

Each rule of the set
H
is of the form
(k,a,j,z,p)
, where
1\leq k,j\leq s
,

a\in\Gamma
,
z\in\{-1,0,1\}
and
p
is the real number
0\leq p\leq 1
.
Each rule means that when attacking symbol
a
is send, defense of node
i

by line
k
is transferred with
probability
p
to defense of node
i+z
by line
j
. We shall denote the
probability above also
p_{a,k,j}^{z}
. Naturally for all
a\in\Gamma

\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{z=-1}^{1}p_{a,k,j}^{z}=1,

i.e. on each attacking symbol something necessarily happens.
Note that the underlying system in defense systems is nondeterministic and
therefore the model of defense systems we defined is sometimes called

nondeterministic DS
, NDS for short.

Figure 1:
A picture illustrating a defense system in the initial configuration
defending the node 0 by the line 1.

We fix the attacking symbol set
\Gamma=\{0,1\}
in this paper.

A NDS can also be viewed as a countable Markov system. To simplify notations
we denote each configuration of a NDS by an integer. If node
i
is defended
by line
j
, we denote this configuration by integers
i\cdot s+(j-1)
.
Recall that the initial configuration is that node 0 is defended by line
1, which is represented as an integer 0.

Let
w\in\{0,1\}^{*}
. We shall denote the probability that the NDS is in the
configuration
k\in\mathbb{Z}
in response to a finite sequence of attacking signals

w

p_{w}(k)
.

Let
D=(K,H,\Gamma)
be a defense system.
D
is called
unreliable
if,
for some
w\in\Gamma^{*}
,
after attacking sequence
w
the probability that node 0 is defended
by some line is 0, i.e.
p_{w}(j)=0
for all
0\leq j\leq s-1
. The word
w
here
is called
critical
. If there is no
critical words
w\in\Gamma^{*}
that
D
,
then
D
is called
reliable
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The unreliability of NDS is undecidable, i.e. it is undecidable for a given
NDS
B=(K,H,\{0,1\})
, to determine whether there exist
w\in\{0,1\}^{*}
such that

p_{w}(j)=0
for all
0\leq j\leq s-1
.

Proof.

In this proof we shall use the undecidability result of Corollary

.

Let

be a deterministic

-transducer and

Z
-transducer,

Define a nondeterministic complete
Z

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=K_{1}\cup K_{2},

\displaystyle H_{3}

\displaystyle=H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup\{(g_{0},a,b,q)\mid(q_{0},a,b,q)\in H_{1})\}.

-transducer
D^{\prime}
satisfies
O(D^{\prime})=O(D)
, but the transducer also has
paths of
C
in it, although they are not accepting paths.

Let

Let

((q_{k},g_{\ell})_{i},a,z,(q_{r},g_{t})_{j})\in H

(q_{k},a,b_{1},q_{r})\in H_{1}\text{ and }(g_{\ell},a,b_{2},g_{t})\in H_{3},

and
z

(b_{1},b_{2}\in\{c,cc\})

z=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }b_{1}=b_{2}c,\\
0&\text{if }b_{1}=b_{2},\\
1&\text{if }b_{2}=b_{1}c.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Moreover
H
contains elements

((q_{f},g_{f}),a,0,(q_{f},g_{f})),

(3.2)

((q,f),a,1,(q_{f},q_{f})),\text{ where }\{q,f\}\cap\{q_{f},g_{f}\}\neq\emptyset,\quad a=0,1.

(3.3)

.
We shall refer the elements of

Claim.
The existence of finite sequence

such

By (

),

gives the difference of lengths

and

and

are equal. If the node is negative, the length of

by the absolute value of the node. If it is positive,

Now we are ready to proof the equivalence mentioned above.

Assume first that
O(C)\not\subseteq O(D)
. This means that there

such that for unique
y\in c^{*}
,

. Then

.

, and defense of node moves to one larger by every symbol. The simulation

does not change from beginning, since no

-transducer.

2) If the simulation of

before than the

ii) If

, then the 1-3 above cases are possible, and again

,

, such that NDS

is unreliable.

is unreliable.

Assume next that NDS

is unreliable. It means that
there exists sequence
w\in\{0,1\}^{*}
such that

p_{w}(j)=0
for all

. By the fact that

is deterministic
and therefore complete, it means that some subword of

must be in

, since otherwise there is a path in

for a input word

and therefore in

node 0 has positive defense probability for

,

.

Now assume that

is the subword of

such that
v\in L(C)
and
let

be the unique element of
\{0,1\}^{*}
, such that
(v,y)\in O(C)
.
Now
(v,y)\notin O(D)
, since otherwise there would be a possible defense
in node 0 after attacking sequence

and after

the instruction used
would be the corresponded to rule (

) which does not move the
defense anywhere. Therefore for the attacking sequence

there would be
a defense in the node 0 with positive probability,

Now we have finally proved the Claim.

By Corollary
2.3
it is undecidable whether

O(C)\not\subseteq O(D)
and therefore the unreliability of NDS is
also undecidable.
∎

Note that since unreliability is a complement of reliability, this also
means that reliability is undecidable.

Let
\Sigma

\Delta
two alphabets. For a set

A mapping

1)
\varphi(\epsilon)=\{\epsilon\}
and

2)
\varphi(xy)=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)

Because of condition 2, a substitution is usually defined by giving
the images of all letters in
\Sigma

Let
\varphi
be as above and
L
be a language over
\Sigma^{*}
, i.e.
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
. We denote

\varphi(L)=\bigcup_{w\in L}\varphi(w).

Two substitutions
\varphi,\xi:\Sigma^{*}\to 2^{\Delta^{*}}
are equivalent on language

L
if

\varphi(L)=\xi(L).

A substitution
\varphi
is called
\epsilon
-free

\epsilon\notin\varphi(a)

for all
a\in\Sigma
.
And it is called a
finite substitution
if, for all
a\in\Sigma
,
the set
\varphi(a)
is finite.

A language
L
is called
regular
, if it is accepted by a
finite automaton. It is known that regular languages are closed under finite
substitutions, which means that if
L
is regular , so is
\varphi(L)
for finite
substitution

v_{1}=\beta_{\ell}\beta_{k}w^{z+1}\alpha_{j}
, then
v_{0}v_{1}\notin P
.

3) If
v_{1}\in C_{a}N
, i.e. for some
1\leq\ell\leq s
and
(k,z,j)\in D_{a}

v_{1}=\beta_{k}w^{z+2}\beta_{\ell}
, then
v_{0}v_{1}\notin P
.

4) If
v_{1}\in NC_{a}N
, i.e. for some
1\leq\ell,t\leq s
and
(k,z,j)\in D_{a}

v_{1}=\beta_{\ell}\beta_{k}w^{z+2}\beta_{t}
, then
v_{0}v_{1}\notin P
.

5) If
v_{1}\in T_{a}
, then let
t=\min\{i\mid v_{i}\notin T_{x_{i}},1\leq i\leq n\}
.
Now if
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{t-1}\in P
, then
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{t-1}=w^{r}\alpha_{j}
for some integers
r
and
j
,
where
1\leq j\leq s
.

Assume now that
t=n
. If now
v_{n+1}=w
, then
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{n}v_{n+1}\notin P
,
and if
v_{n+1}=\beta_{j}w\in NM
, by Claim above
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{n}v_{n+1}\neq w^{2n+2}
and so necessarily
t<n
.

Now we have four cases on whether
v_{t}\in B
,
v_{t}\in NT_{x_{t}}
,

v_{t}\in C_{x_{t}}
or
v_{t}\in NC_{x_{t}}N
, but like cases 1-4 above, these
cases lead to contradiction, since
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{t}\notin P
.

So we have proved that
w^{2n+2}\notin\xi(x)
and therefore the prove
of the theorem is completed.

∎

A homotopy classification of Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures with applications to exceptional Riemannian holonomy
Raúl Alvarez-Patiño

Abstract
We use classical obstruction theory à la Eilenberg-Steenrod to obtain a homotopy classification of Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures on compact 8 8 -manifolds with abelian fundamental group. As an application we show that a compact, connected Riemannian 8 8 -manifold with holonomy contained inside the group Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) has exactly two Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) \mathrm{Spin}(7) -structures extending the induced G 2 G_{2} -structure on the boundary.



1
Introduction

Bonan
[
4
]
, Gray and Green
[
13
]
, and Marisa Fernández
[
11
]
were among the first authors to consider manifolds of dimension eight endowed with
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures. Since then, the study of geometric and topological properties of manifolds with
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures of different types has become a central topic in Riemannian geometry. This paper aims to complete the circle of ideas initiated in
[
13
]
by establishing two results.
A
below gives a somewhat general classification of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures from the point of view of homotopy theory. Our main result,
B
at the end of this section, establishes the existence of exactly two
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures on a compact and connected
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifold.

Theorem A
.

Let
W
be a compact
8
-manifold with perfect fundamental group. Suppose there is a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
W
. Then the set of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures on
W
that extend the induced
G_{2}
-structure on the boundary
\partial W
has a free and transitive action of the group
H^{8}(W,\partial W;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.

Crowley and Nordström showed that any compact
7
-manifold
M
with a
G_{2}
-structure admits a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-coboundary, that is,
M
bounds a spin
8
-manifold endowed with a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
[
9
, Lemma 3.4]
.
A
complements this result by giving an explicit description of the set of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures extending a
G_{2}
-structure specified on the boundary. On the other hand, the hypothesis for the fundamental group in
A
is motivated by an early result of Joyce stating that a Riemannian
8
-manifold with holonomy inside the group
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
is necessarily simply-connected
[
18
, Theorem C]
. We provide a deeper reason for our assumption on
\pi_{1}W
in
Remark

3.4
.

1.1
Preliminaries and the statement of the main Theorem

The combined works of Hitchin
[
15
]
, Wang
[
29
,
30
]
, McInnes
[
21
]
, and Moroianu and Semmelmann
[
25
]
, imply that a compact Riemannian manifold with special holonomy is characterized by the existence of non-trivial parallel spinors. Moreover, a compact Riemannian manifold carrying a non-trivial parallel spinor must be Ricci-flat (see Hitchin
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
or Friedrich
[
12
, Folgerung 1]
) and non-symmetric, because symmetric manifolds have Einstein metrics with non-zero scalar curvature by the results of Besse
[
3
, Chapter 7 Section F]
.

Let
H\subset\mathrm{SO}(n)
be the holonomy group of an irreducible, non-symmetric, Ricci-flat manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. Berger’s classification
[
2
]
implies that
H
is simply-connected. In particular,
H
admits a lifting into the universal covering of
\mathrm{SO}(n)
, the spin group
\mathrm{Spin}(n)
, with image contained inside the set of all elements that fix a unit spinor in the spin representation of
\mathrm{Spin}(n)
(see
Remark

5.3
below). On the other hand, a metric with special holonomy reduces the structure group of the tangent bundle of the underlying manifold from
\mathrm{SO}(n)
down to
H
. Therefore, the existence of a spin structure supporting a non-zero spinor field is a necessary condition for a compact manifold to admit Ricci-flat metrics of special holonomy.

There are two exceptional cases in the classification of holonomy representations corresponding to Ricci-flat manifolds. The group
G_{2}\subset\mathrm{SO}(7)
with a representation in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
as the automorphism group of the imaginary octonions, and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{8}
via the (real) spin representation
\Delta_{7}
. The copy of
G_{2}
inside
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
coincides with the stabilizer of a unit spinor in the spin representation
\Delta_{7}
. Meanwhile,
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
has two liftings into
\mathrm{Spin}(8)
corresponding to isotropy subgroups of unit spinors of positive or negative chirality, respectively. A third
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Spin}(8)
is obtained as the stabilizer of a unit element in the vector representation
\mathrm{Ad}_{8}
. Varadarajan
[
28
, Section 1 Theorem 5]
showed that these subgroups exhaust all possibilities, as they represent every conjugacy class of
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-subgroups of
\mathrm{Spin}(8)
. Moreover, the intersection of any two such
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-subgroups gives the copy of
G_{2}
inside
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
associated with the holonomy representation
[
28
, Section 3 Theorem 5]
. It is worth mentioning that the two chiral representations
\Delta_{8}^{+}
,
\Delta_{8}^{-}
and the vector representation
\mathrm{Ad}_{8}
are permuted by the elements of the outer automorphism group of
\mathrm{Spin}(8)
. This phenomenon, commonly known as principle of triality, was discovered by Èlie Cartan and can be read from the
S_{3}
-symmetry of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathrm{SO}(8)
(see Michelson-Lawson
[
14
, Chapter 8]
, De Sapio
[
10
]
or Baez
[
1
, Section 2.4]
).

The positive spinor bundle of a spin
8
-manifold
W
is an orientable
8
-plane bundle associated with a spin structure on
W
via the right-handed spin representation
\Delta_{8}^{+}
. We say that
W
has a geometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure if there is a positive spinor bundle
S^{+}_{W}
over
W
carrying a right-handed spinor field
\psi\in\Gamma(S^{+}_{W})
with norm
\left\lvert\,\psi\,\right\rvert=1
everywhere on
W
. A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure is the homotopy class represented by a path
\psi_{t}\in\Gamma(S^{+}_{W})
with
\left\lvert\,\psi_{t}\,\right\rvert=1
for all
t\in[0,1]
and at every point of
W
(cf
Definition

2.1
). Then
W
has a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure if and only if the Euler class
e(S^{+}_{W})=0
. Gray and Green
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
found that
e(S^{+}_{W})
can be expressed in terms of Pontrjagin classes
p_{1}(TW),p_{2}(TW)
and the Euler class
e(TW)
of the tangent bundle of
W
according to

16\,e(S^{+}_{W})=4p_{2}(TW)-p_{1}^{2}(TW)+8e(TW).

This means that the existence of a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
W
is a purely topological condition on the tangential information of
W
that does not depend on a specific choice of spin structure. Accordingly, a geometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
W
is defined by a pair
(\Omega,g)
, where
\Omega
is a
4
-form on
W
depending quadratically on the unit spinor field
\psi
(see Lucia Martín-Merchán
[
20
, Proposition 2.4]
), and
g
is a Riemannian metric determined by
\Omega
in a highly non-linear fashion (see Karigiannis
[
19
, Theorem 4.3.3]
). Note that
\Omega^{2}
is non-vanishing and consequently determines an orientation for
W
. A well-known result of Marisa Fernández
[
11
, Theorem 5.3]
implies that the metric
g
has
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-holonomy if and only if
\Omega
is closed, and this happens precisely when
\psi
is parallel. In such case, one says that the corresponding
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure is torsion-free.

The first non-compact examples of Riemannian manifolds with
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-holonomy were discovered by Bryant
[
5
]
. Later on, Bryant and Salamon
[
6
]
extended the catalogue to include complete but still non-compact manifolds. The compact case was finally settled by Joyce in
[
18
,
17
]
. Those manifolds constructed by Joyce were the first examples of Ricci-flat
8
-manifolds of non-Kähler type. Another key result implies that the holonomy of a compact, simply-connected,
8
-manifold
W
with a torsion-free
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure is controlled by the index of the Dirac operator. More precisely, the
\hat{A}
-genus
\hat{A}(W)
determines the holonomy
H
by the following criteria;
H=\mathrm{Spin}(8-k)
if and only if
\hat{A}(W)=k
, for
k=1,2,3,4

[
18
, Theorem C]
. This together with the Cheeger-Gromoll splitting Theorem
[
7
]
implies that
W
must be simply-connected. This result by Joyce is the strongest obstruction to the existence of Riemannian metrics with
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-holonomy known to this day. Combining
A
with
[
18
, Theorem C]
we get our main result.

Theorem B
.

Let
W
be a compact, connected
8
-manifold with boundary
\partial W
. Suppose that
W
has a Riemannian metric with holonomy contained inside the group
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
. Then there are exactly two
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures on
W
that extend the induced
G_{2}
-structure on the boundary.

8

.

2
Reductions of the structure group of a vector bundle

Let us consider a pair of compact Lie groups
H
and
G
related by a smooth homomorphism
\iota:H\rightarrow G
with kernel
\kr\iota
. The induced map
\mathrm{B}{\iota}:\mathrm{B}{H}\rightarrow\mathrm{B}{G}
between classifying spaces defines a Hurewicz fibration

F\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{B}{\iota}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{B}{G}.

(2.1)

The homotopy fibre
F=\mathrm{Hofib}(\mathrm{B}{\iota})
is homotopy equivalent to the quotient space
G/H
if
\kr\iota
is trivial, and is given by the classifying space
\mathrm{B}{\kr\iota}
when
\iota
is surjective. Milnor’s construction
[
22
, Section 3]
together with our compactness assumption ensures that
\mathrm{B}{H}
and
\mathrm{B}{G}
are countable CW complexes. The same is true for the corresponding universal spaces
\mathrm{E}H
and
\mathrm{E}G
. From now on,
X
will denote a finite CW complex.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S:X\rightarrow\mathrm{B}{G}
be the classifying map of a vector bundle
S_{X}
over
X
with structure group
G
. A geometric
H
-reduction for
S_{X}
is a homotopy lifting
\hat{S}:X\rightarrow\mathrm{B}{H}
of the classifying map
S
along the projection
\mathrm{B}{\iota}
. An
H
-structure for
S_{X}
is the homotopy class represented by a continuous path of geometric
H
-reductions for
S_{X}
. We write
SX
to denote the total space of the bundle
S_{X}
.

Remark 2.2
.

A homotopy lifting of
S
along a projection
\mathrm{B}{\iota}
is a map
\hat{S}
such that the composition
\mathrm{B}{\iota}\circ\hat{S}
is homotopic to
S
. From now on we will use the term lifting in place of homotopy lifting.

Remark 2.3
.

Denote by
S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H}\rightarrow X
the pullback of
Equation

2.1
along
S:X\rightarrow\mathrm{B}{G}
. Note that a lifting for
S
and a section of
S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H}\rightarrow X
define each other. For this reason, we identify the set of all geometric
H
-reductions of
S_{X}
with the set of sections
\Gamma(S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H})
of the fibration
S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H}\rightarrow X
. Moreover, two liftings for
S
are homotopic if and only if the corresponding sections of
S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H}
are homotopic. In general, two sections of a given fibration are homotopic if and only if they are vertically homotopic
[
16
, Lemma 1.1]
; that is, they are homotopic through a path of sections. Therefore, if we denote by
H(S_{X})
the set of all
H
-reductions of
S_{X}
, then there is a natural bijection

H(S_{X})\simeq\pi_{0}\Gamma(S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H}),

(2.2)

where
\pi_{0}\Gamma(S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H})
denotes the set of connected components of the space of sections
\Gamma(S^{\ast}\mathrm{B}{H})
. From now on we make no distinction between homotopies and vertical homotopies when dealing with liftings.

The following example serves as the basic model for geometric
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures and
G_{2}
-structures, respectively.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose that
G
is a compact and connected Lie group, and let
\rho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{SO}(m+1)
be a smooth and faithful representation of
G
into the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. Define
H\subset G
as the isotropy subgroup of the first coordinate vector
e_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. Consider the associated
G
-vector bundle
S_{X}
corresponding to
\rho
. Then
S_{X}
has an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and an orientation induced by
\rho
. A geometric
H
-reduction for
S_{X}
is completely determined by a choice of nowhere vanishing section
s:X\rightarrow SX
normalized so that
\langle s,s\rangle=\left\lvert\,s\,\right\rvert^{2}=1
everywhere. Such a section exists precisely when the Euler class
e(S_{X})
equals zero. Moreover, any two such sections induce the same
H
-structure for
S_{X}
if and only if they are homotopic through a path of sections of the associated
m
-sphere bundle formed by all unit vectors of
S_{X}
. If the group
G
acts transitively on the sphere
S^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, then there is a diffeomorphism identifying the quotient space
G/H
with
S^{m}
. In this case, the associated fibration
Equation

2.1
is spherical and takes the form

S^{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{B}{H}\rightarrow\mathrm{B}{G}.

(2.3)

Montgomery and Samelson
[
24
]
showed that the classical groups
\mathrm{SO}(n)
,
\mathrm{U}(n)
,
\mathrm{SU}(n)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\cdot\mathrm{SO}(2)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\cdot\mathrm{Sp}(1)
, together with the exceptional groups;
G_{2},\mathrm{Spin}(7)
, and
\mathrm{Spin}(9)
are the only compact Lie groups acting effectively and transitively on a finite-dimensional sphere.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be an abelian group and

a)

For every triple

b)

if and only if

.

c)

and

Assume we have an action

\mathfrak{a}(h,x)=hx
. If
\mathfrak{a}
is free and transitive, then every pair

defines a unique element

such that

. This gives us the affine difference function

. Now we show that

satisfies properties

a)
,
b)
and

The uniqueness of

satisfying

. Now we verify property

. Since

is free,

is the only group element fixing a given

if and only if

. Finally property
Item

c)
.
Let

and

and suppose that

has two solutions

and

. Then

. After straightforward manipulations using

one gets

. Hence

by property
Item

b)
.

Conversely, if an affine difference

,
b)
and
c)
is given, we define a function

, where

provided by
Item

. Again we denote the value of

by

. We claim that

is an action. The trivial element

, then

The Clifford algebra

\mathbb{R}
-algebra with unit

(4.1)

If

The spin group

generated by all monomials

Remark 6.2
.

The spinor bundle of a compact spin
8
-manifold
W
splits as the sum of positive and negative spinor bundles
S_{W}^{+}
,
S_{W}^{-}
. Clifford multiplication by a vector field
v\in\Gamma(TW)
establishes an isomorphism
c_{v}:S_{W}^{+}\rightarrow S_{W}^{-}
that reverts chirality of spinors. On the other hand, a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure on
W
is characterized by a unit positive spinor field
\psi\in\Gamma(S_{W}^{+})
. In particular,
c_{v}(\psi)\in\Gamma(S_{W}^{-})
vanishes over the zero-locus of
v
. This means that Clifford multiplication
c_{v}
induces a natural pairing between
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structures on
W
when the Euler characteristic of
W
equals zero (eg if
\partial W=\emptyset
). By
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
, the Euler classes
e(S_{W}^{+})
,
e(S_{W}^{-})
differ by
e(TW)
. Hence, the vanishing of any two
e(S_{W}^{+})
,
e(S_{W}^{+})
,
e(TW)
implies the vanishing of the third. Therefore,
B
is non-trivial only when
W
is closed.

A well known result by Milnor
[
23
]
says that the spin cobordism group in dimension seven is trivial (see Stong
[
27
, Chapter XI]
for details). This means that any compact spin
7
-manifold
M
is the spin boundary of a compact spin
8
-manifold

. Crowley and Nordström improved this result by showing that there is always some

with a

-structure
[

, Lemma 3.4]
. Using this fact, the authors introduced a pair of invariants that completely determine the deformation class of a
G_{2}
-structure on

; that is, the class of a geometric

-structure identified under homotopy equivalence and diffeomorphism. This program ultimately shows that
M
posses at least
24
deformation classes of
G_{2}
-structures
[
9

. We expect to complete the classification of

-structures up to diffeomorphism in a future publication.

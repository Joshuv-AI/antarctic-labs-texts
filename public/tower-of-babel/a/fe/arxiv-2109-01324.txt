Forest formulas of discrete Green’s functions
Fan Chung, Ji Zeng

Abstract
The discrete Green’s functions are the pseudoinverse (or the inverse) of the Laplacian (or its variations) of a graph. In this paper, we will give combinatorial interpretations of Green’s functions in terms of enumerating trees and forests in a graph that will be used to derive further formulas for several graph invariants. For example, we show that the trace of the Green’s function 𝐆 \mathbf{G} associated with the combinatorial Laplacian of a connected simple graph Γ \Gamma on n n vertices satisfies Tr ​ ( 𝐆 ) = ∑ λ i ≠ 0 1 λ i = 1 n ​ τ ​ | 𝔽 2 ∗ | \text{Tr}(\mathbf{G})=\sum_{\lambda_{i}\neq 0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}=\frac{1}{n\tau}|\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}| where λ i \lambda_{i} denotes the eigenvalues of the combinatorial Laplacian, τ \tau denotes the number of spanning trees, and 𝔽 2 ∗ \mathbb{F}^{*}_{2} denotes the set of rooted spanning 2 2 -forests in Γ \Gamma . We will prove forest formulas for discrete Green’s functions for directed and weighted graphs and apply them to study random walks on graphs and digraphs. We derive a forest expression of the hitting time for digraphs, which gives combinatorial proofs to old and new results about hitting times, traces of discrete Green’s functions, and other related quantities.

Introduction

The classical matrix-tree theorem of Kirchhoff
[
18
]
states that the number of spanning trees in a graph is equal to the determinant of a principle minor of the combinatorial Laplacian. In this paper, we consider the Green’s functions which are the pseudoinverses of the Laplacians of graphs. It is our goal to follow the spirit of Kirchhoff to find the inherent combinatorial meanings for the Green’s functions. We will show that Green’s functions can be expressed as specific enumerations of trees and forests in the graph.

Since discrete Green’s functions for graphs were introduced in 2000
[
13
]
, there have been numerous related articles most of which are in applications while relatively few are graph-theoretical. Previously, Chebotarev and Agaev
[

[

]
Section 9). In
[

, Chebotarev and Agaev derived the group inverse

of the Laplacian
L
for a weighted digraph as an enumeration of spanning rooted forests. For an undirected graph
\Gamma
, the two types of inverses coincide and are called the Green’s function of
\Gamma
in
[
13
]
. Xu and Yau
[

[

[

We list three types of Laplacians with their associated Green’s functions for graphs and digraphs, weighted or unweighted (while the detailed definitions will be given in Section

•

The combinatorial Laplacian

The generalized Laplacian

We remark that the combinatorial Laplacian

This paper is organized as follows: Section

2
Preliminaries

In this paper, we use
\Gamma=(V,E)
to denote a strongly-connected weighted digraph, where
V
and
E
are its vertex set and edge set respectively. For each edge
e=(u,v)
we denote its weight by
\omega(e)=\omega(u,v)
. It’s required that
\omega(u,v)=0
for
(u,v)\not\in E
.

For simplicity, we do not allow loops in all (di-)graphs in this paper. We also require the weights to be non-negative, although some of our forest formulas still hold if negative weights are allowed.

In this paper, each digraph shall come with a total order on its vertex set for the purpose of its matrix representation. The adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
is defined by

A(u,v):=\begin{cases}\omega(u,v)\quad\text{, if $(u,v)\in E$};&\\
0\quad\text{, otherwise.}\end{cases}

The transpose of a matrix
M
is denoted as
M^{*}
.

For each vertex
u
, we define its (out-)degree to be
d_{u}:=\sum_{v}\omega(u,v)
and denote the matrix
D:=\text{Diag}[d_{u}]
. The volume of a subset
U\subset V
refers to the quantity
\text{vol}(U):=\sum_{u\in U}d_{u}
.

A typical random walk on
\Gamma
is defined by the probability transition matrix
P:=D^{-1}A
. If
\Gamma
is strongly-connected, there’s a unique vector
(\pi_{u})_{u\in V}
, satisfying
\pi_{u}>0
for all
u
,
\sum_{u}\pi_{u}=1
, and
\pi^{*}P=\pi^{*}
(
[
15
]
Section 1.3). We will call this vector the stationary distribution. (Although aperiodicity is a necessary condition for the convergence of a random walk, we note that a lazy walk with transition probability matrix
\frac{I+P}{2}
always converges to
\pi
.) We write
\Pi:=\text{Diag}[\pi_{u}]
.

For a strongly-connected digraph
\Gamma
, the normalized Laplacian matrix is defined by
\mathcal{L}:=\Pi^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-P)\Pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}
(see
[
3
]
). For an undirected weighted graph
\Gamma
, the identity
\Pi=\text{vol}(\Gamma)^{-1}D
is easy to verify and consequently we have
\mathcal{L}=D^{\frac{1}{2}}(I-P)D^{-\frac{1}{2}}
as in
[
11
]
.

The combinatorial Green’s function
\mathbf{G}
(resp. normalized Green’s function
\mathcal{G}
) is defined to be the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the combinatorial Laplacian
L
(resp. normalized Laplacian
\mathcal{L}
). There are many equivalent definitions of Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, we refer our readers to
[
16
]
7.3.P7: Let
M
be an
m
-by-
n
matrix with singular value decomposition
M=V\Sigma W^{*}
, then its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse
M^{+}
is
W\Sigma^{+}V^{*}
where
\Sigma^{+}
is obtained from
\Sigma

Next, we turn to definitions and notations of rooted or unrooted forests. A
k

k

1
-forest is a tree. A rooted
k
-forest is a digraph, whose underlying graph structure is a
k

1
-forest is called an in-tree or a rooted tree. A sub-digraph is said to be spanning if it contains all vertices of the ambient digraph.
We denote by
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{k}
the set of spanning rooted
k
-forests of the considered graph or digraph. When we are considering an undirected graph, we use
\mathbb{F}_{k}
to denote the set of spanning unrooted
k
-forests.

For a rooted or unrooted
k
-forest
F
of
\Gamma
, we define its weight as

\omega(F)=\prod_{e\in E(F)}\omega(e)

and for a vertex
u
in
\Gamma
, we define

\tau_{u}=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{F}_{1}^{*};r(T)=u}\omega(T).

We remark that when

is undirected, each vertex in a tree can be chosen as the root, so we have

\tau_{u}=\tau,\forall u\in V

(2.1)

where
\tau
is the total weight of all spanning (unrooted) trees of
\Gamma
.

We shall use the following identity in our proof, known as the Markov chain tree theorem

\pi_{u}=\frac{d_{u}\tau_{u}}{\sum_{w}d_{w}\tau_{w}}.

(2.2)

4
Forest formula for hitting times and applications

In this section, we will use the forest formula of the Green’s function to examine several invariants arising in random walks on graphs or digraphs. First, we will derive the combinatorial expression for the hitting time
H(u,v)
, that is, the expected length of the random walk on
\Gamma
starting at
u
and ending at
v
. Using the forest formula of hitting times, we can give combinatorial proofs of old and new results related. For example, we prove the Kemeny’s constant of a digraph with
n
vertices is at least
\frac{n-1}{2}
, which is a tight lower bound achieved by directed cycles.

We state the connection between the Green’s function
\mathcal{G}
and the hitting time
H(u,v)
. The following identity was first established for undirected graphs in
[
13
]
and was generalized to strongly-connected digraphs in
[
3
]
.

H(u,v)=\frac{\mathcal{G}(v,v)}{\pi(v)}-\frac{\mathcal{G}(u,v)}{\sqrt{\pi(u)\pi(v)}}.

(4.1)

The following hitting time formula is also obtained in
[
10
]
using group inverse techniques.

Theorem 4.1
.

In a graph
\Gamma
, we have the following formula for the hitting time
H(u,v)
.

(i)

If
\Gamma
is a weighted strongly-connected digraph, then

H(u,v)=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}).

(ii)

If additional to (i),
e=(v,u)
is an edge, then

H(u,v)=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}w(e)}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}^{*}_{e}}d_{r(T)}\omega(T),

where
\mathbb{T}^{*}_{e}
denotes the set of all in-trees containing the edge
e
.

(iii)

If
\Gamma
is a weighted connected undirected graph, then

H(u,v)=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}\text{vol}(T_{2})\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}).

Proof.

From Theorem
1.4
, we have

\frac{\mathcal{G}(v,v)}{\pi_{v}}=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}\pi(T_{2})d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}).

We also have

\frac{\mathcal{G}(u,v)}{\sqrt{\pi_{u}\pi_{v}}}=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\Big(\hskip-8.67204pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{1},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.67204pt\pi(T_{2})d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})-\hskip-8.67204pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.67204pt\pi(T_{1})d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})\Big).

Since
\pi(T_{1})+\pi(T_{2})=1
when
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
is spanning, we have

\displaystyle H(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{G}(v,v)}{\pi_{v}}-\frac{\mathcal{G}(u,v)}{\sqrt{\pi_{u}\pi_{v}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}\pi(T_{2})d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}\pi(T_{1})d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}),

proving (i).

If
e=(v,u)
is an edge, the above expression can be further simplified since

T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup e
forms an in-tree. We have

H(u,v)=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}d_{r(T_{2})}\frac{\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup e)}{\omega(e)}=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}\omega(e)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}^{*}_{e}\end{subarray}}d_{r(T)}w(T),

proving (ii).

(iii) follows from (i) by (
2.1
) and free choices of roots.
∎

The commute time
C(u,v)
is the expected time for a random walk starting from
u
, reaching
v
, and then returning to
u
. Namely,

C(u,v)=H(u,v)+H(v,u).

The following simple expression of the commute time is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
. A forest expression for commute times of digraphs is still complicated.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\Gamma
is a connected weighted undirected graph, then

C(u,v)=\frac{\text{vol}(\Gamma)}{\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{1},v\in T_{2}\end{subarray}}w(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

If
e=\{u,v\}
is an edge of
\Gamma
, then

C(u,v)=\frac{\tau_{e}}{\tau\omega(e)}\text{vol}(\Gamma)

where
\tau_{e}
denotes the total weight of all spanning trees containing
e
.

The return time is the expected length of a random walk to return to its starting vertex. The following is a classical result about return time. We present a new proof similar to but simpler than
[
21
]
Theorem 4.5.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
v
is a vertex of a strongly-connected weighted digraph
\Gamma
, then the expected return time
R(v)
, with starting vertex
v
, is
\frac{\sum_{w}d_{w}\tau_{w}}{d_{v}\tau_{v}}=\pi_{v}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Write
N^{+}_{v}:=\{u\in V;(v,u)\in E\}
. Using Theorem
4.1
, the return time is

\displaystyle R(v)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{u\in N^{+}_{v}}\frac{\omega(v,u)}{d_{v}}H(u,v)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{d_{v}\tau_{v}}\sum_{b\neq v}d_{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
v=r(T_{1}),b=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\sum_{u\in N^{+}_{v}\cap T_{2}}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup(v,u)).

It suffices to note that
T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup(v,u)
forms an in-tree with root
b
and each such in-tree appears in the summation of RHS exactly once.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle R(v)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\sum_{b\not=v}d_{b}\tau_{b}}{d_{v}\tau_{v}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{b}d_{b}\tau_{b}}{d_{v}\tau_{v}}=\pi_{v}^{-1},

by identity (
2.2
) as wanted.
∎

Kemeny’s constant is the expected number of steps required for a random walk starting from a specified vertex to reach a random vertex sampled from the stationary distribution. Surprisingly, this quantity does not depend on which starting state is chosen (
[
17
]
, see also
[

Corollary 4.4
.

In a graph
\Gamma
, Kemeny’s constant
\kappa=\sum_{v}H(u,v)\pi_{v}

satisfies the following:

(i)

If
\Gamma
is a weighted strongly-connected digraph, then

\kappa=\frac{1}{\sum_{w}d_{w}\tau_{w}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\end{subarray}}d_{r(T_{1})}d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=\text{Tr}(\mathcal{G}).

In particular this quantity is independent of the choice of
u
.

(ii)

If
\Gamma
is a weighted connected undirected graph, then

\kappa=\frac{1}{\text{vol}(\Gamma)\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\end{subarray}}\text{vol}(T_{1})\text{vol}(T_{2})\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=\text{Tr}(\mathcal{G}).

Proof.

We have, for any vertex
u
,

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}H(u,v)\pi_{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}\frac{\pi_{v}}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}\frac{1}{\sum_{w}d_{w}\tau_{w}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}d_{v}d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sum_{w}d_{w}\tau_{w}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\end{subarray}}d_{r(T_{1})}d_{r(T_{2})}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})=\text{Tr}(\mathcal{G}),

where the last line is based on Corollary
1.6
.

(ii) follows from (i) by (
2.1
) and free choices of roots.
∎

We can use the forest formula of the hitting time to establish the following tight lower bound for Kememy’s constant.

Theorem 4.6
.

For a strongly-connected weighted digraph
\Gamma

\frac{n-1}{2}
with equality if and only if

Proof.

We rewrite

\sum_{v}H(u,v)\pi(v)=\frac{1}{2\sum_{w}d_{w}\tau_{w}}\sum_{w}\sum_{v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
w=r(T_{1}),v=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}d_{w}d_{v}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}),

where the factor
\frac{1}{2}
is because of the latter summation running over each rooted
2
-forest twice.

It suffices to show that for any

,

\sum_{v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
w=r(T_{1}),v=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}d_{w}d_{v}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})\geq(n-1)d_{w}\tau_{w}.

For a
2
-forest
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
, let

denote the set of edges

(r(T_{2}),z)
with

. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
w=r(T_{1}),v=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}d_{w}d_{v}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
w=r(T_{1}),v=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\sum_{e\in E(r(T_{2}),T_{1})}d_{w}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)d_{w}\tau_{w},

since
T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup e
forms an in-tree with root
w
and each in-tree
T
with root
w

appears in the last summation of above inequality exactly
n-1
times based on the fact that deleting each of the

results a rooted

-forest with a unique edge

.

From the above proof, the equality holds if and only if for any

-forest

rooted at any pair

, all edges leaving

both present in
\Gamma
. Take an in-tree

with root

and let

be the one in

having a longer distance to

in

and

the other. Delete the edge

starting at

in

rooted at pair

with

1.2
:

\mathbf{G}(u,v)=\frac{1}{n^{2}\tau}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{1},r(T_{1})=v\end{subarray}}|T_{2}|^{2}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\end{subarray}}|T_{1}||T_{2}|\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})\Big).

Thus, we we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(v,v)-\mathbf{G}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}|T_{2}|^{2}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{1}{n^{2}\tau}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u,v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}|T_{2}|^{2}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}|T_{1}||T_{2}|\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}|T_{2}|(|T_{1}|+|T_{2}|)\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}|T_{2}|\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}).

Similarly,

\mathbf{G}(u,u)-\mathbf{G}(v,u)=\frac{1}{n\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}|T_{1}|\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}).

Together, we have

\displaystyle R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\tau}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v\in T_{1}\end{subarray}}(|T_{1}|+|T_{2}|)\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau\omega(e)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}_{e}\end{subarray}}\omega(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{e}}{\tau\omega(e)},

where
\mathbb{T}_{e}
is the set of all (unrooted) trees containing the edge
e
.
∎

For digraphs, the expression for
R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}
is not as elegant. Nevertheless,
we use the forest formula to derive several general upper bounds for hitting time.

Here are generalizations of Corollary 3.4 and Corollary 3.5 in
[
8
]
, originally obtained for undirected graphs using Chung-Yau invariants.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a strongly-connected weighted digraph.

(i)

For an edge
e=(v,u)
, we have

H(u,v)\leq\frac{d_{v}}{\omega(v,u)}(\pi_{v}^{-1}-1).

(ii)

For distinct vertices
u,v
, let
R
denote the set of vertices
b
such that there exists a direct path from
u
to
b
without passing
v
. Then

H(u,v)\leq\max_{b\in R}\frac{d_{b}}{\omega(b,v)}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle H(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{b\neq v}d_{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\\
b=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}\omega(v,u)}\sum_{b\neq v}d_{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\\
b=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup(v,u))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}\omega(v,u)}\sum_{b\not=v}d_{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}^{*}_{e},b=r(T)\end{subarray}}\omega(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{v}\omega(v,u)}\Big(\sum_{b}d_{b}\tau_{b}-d_{v}\tau_{v}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{v}}{\omega(v,u)}\Big(\frac{\sum_{b}d_{b}\tau_{b}}{d_{v}\tau_{v}}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{v}}{\omega(v,u)}\Big(\pi_{v}^{-1}-1\Big),

proving (i).

For (ii) we assume
(b,v)\in E
for each
b\in R
, otherwise RHS of the inequality is infinity and there’s nothing to prove. Note that any
2
-forest
T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{*}
with
u\in T_{2}
,
r(T_{1})=v
and
r(T_{2})=b
guarantees
b\in R
, hence Theorem
4.1
gives,

\displaystyle H(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{b\neq v}d_{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\\
b=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{b\in P}\frac{d_{b}}{\omega(b,v)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\\
b=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup(b,v))

\displaystyle\leq\max_{b\in P}\{\frac{d_{b}}{\omega(b,v)}\}\frac{1}{\tau_{v}}\sum_{b\in R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1}\cup T_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{2}\\
u\in T_{2},v=r(T_{1})\\
b=r(T_{2})\end{subarray}}\omega(T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup(b,v)).

It suffices to note that
T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup(b,v)
is an in-tree rooted at
v
and each such an in-tree appears in the summand of RHS at most once.
∎

For the case of undirected graphs, the upper bound for the hitting time can be further improved.

Theorem 4.9
.

In a connected weighted undirected graph
\Gamma
, if
\{u,v\}
is an edge, then

H(u,v)=(\text{vol}(\Gamma)-d_{v})R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(u,v).

If
u
and
v
are not adjacent, let
P
denote a path connecting
u
and
v
with length
l
. Then we have

H(u,v)\leq l\text{vol}(\Gamma)\max_{e\in E(P)}R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(e).

Proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem
4.8
. If
\{u,v\}
is an edge, we have

\displaystyle H(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau~\omega(v,u)}\sum_{b\not=v}d_{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathbb{T}^{*}_{e},b=r(T)\end{subarray}}\omega(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\not=v}d_{b}\frac{\tau_{e}}{\tau~\omega(v,u)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{b}d_{b}-d_{v}\Big)R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\Big(\text{vol}(\Gamma)-d_{v}\Big)R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(u,v),

by using Theorem
4.7
.

If
u
and
v
are not adjacent and are connected by a path
P
, from the subadditivity of the hitting time, we have

H(u,v)\leq\sum_{e\in E(P)}H(e)\leq l~\text{vol}(\Gamma)\max_{e\in E(P)}R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(e).

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.9

∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.9
, we have the following upper bound for the maximal hitting time of an undirected graph.

Corollary 4.10
.

For a weighted connected undirected graph
\Gamma
, the maximal hitting time satisfies

\max_{u,v}H(u,v)\leq\text{vol}(\Gamma){\rm diam}(\Gamma)R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}

where
{\rm diam}(\Gamma)
denotes the diameter of
\Gamma
and
R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}
is the maximum
R_{e\hskip-1.4457ptf\hskip-1.4457ptf}(e)
among all edges
e
in
\Gamma
.

For connected simple graphs on
n
vertices, the maximum hitting time is defined by

H^{*}(n)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Gamma\\
|V(\Gamma)|=n\end{subarray}}\max_{u,v}H(u,v),

The upper bound of hitting time given in Theorem
4.9
gives an immediate upper bound of order
\Omega(n^{3})
for the maximum hitting time. In
[

, Winkler and Brightwell showed that
H^{*}(n)
is of order
\frac{4}{27}n^{3}
and determined the extremal graphs

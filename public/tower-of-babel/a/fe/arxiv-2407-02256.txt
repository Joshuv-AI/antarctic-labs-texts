Mixed Hodge modules on stacks
Swann Tubach

Abstract
Using the ∞ \infty -categorical enhancement of mixed Hodge modules constructed by the author in a previous paper, we explain how mixed Hodge modules canonically extend to algebraic stacks, together with all the 6 6 operations and weights. We also prove that Drew’s approach to motivic Hodge modules gives an ∞ \infty -category that embeds fully faithfully in mixed Hodge modules, and we identify the image as mixed Hodge modules of geometric origin.



Introduction

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be an integer. Deligne’s work in
[
Del74
]
gives a polarisable mixed Hodge structure on the singular cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{sing}}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
of the complex points of
X
, seen as an analytic variety. M. Saito’s category of algebraic mixed Hodge modules (
[
Sai90b
]
) on
X
is an Abelian category
\mathrm{MHM}(X)
modelled on perverse sheaves which is a relative version of polarisable mixed Hodge structures. Its derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
is endowed with the
6
sheaf operations and any complex of mixed Hodge modules
K
has an underlying complex
\mathrm{rat}(K)
of perverse sheaves. The mixed Hodge structure on
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{sing}}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
can be recovered as
\mathrm{H}^{n}(f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X})
where

f_{*}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})))\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}})

is the pushforward of the map
f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathbb{Q}_{X}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
is the unit for the tensor product, whose underlying sheaf of perverse sheaves is the constant sheaf of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces
\mathbb{Q}_{X}\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Sh}}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
under Beilinson’s (
[
Bei87b
]
) isomorphism
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Perv}(X,\mathbb{Q}))\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})
. This point of view is very powerful as the formalism of the 6 operations is useful to make computations and reductions.

If
\mathfrak{X}
is a reasonable algebraic stack (say a global quotient stack or a stack exhausted étale locally by such), one can construct a mixed Hodge structure on
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
either by hyperdescent as in
[
Del74
, Section 6.1]
or by using exhaustions as in
[
Dav24
, Section 5.2]
. One purpose of this article (see
Section

3
) is to give an extension of M. Saito’s derived category, together with the
6
operations, to algebraic stacks so that the above mixed Hodge structures can be recovered as
\mathrm{H}^{n}(f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathfrak{X}})
with
f:\mathfrak{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
the structural map:

Theorem

(
Theorem

3.1
,
Corollary

3.24
and
Theorem

3.36
)
.

There exists a canonical extension of the derived category of mixed Hodge modules to algebraic stacks over the complex numbers. It has the
6
-operations, nearby cycles, and a notion of weights. Over stacks with affine stabilisers this notion of weights gives rise to a weight structure
à la
Bondarko.

The proof relies on the
\infty
-categorical enhancement of mixed Hodge modules obtained in
[
Tub25
]
, and then on Liu and Zheng’s work (
[
LZ17
]
) on extension of formalisms of
6
operations to stacks. The construction of nearby cycles is based on a motivic construction of the unipotent nearby cycles functor considered in
[
CHS24
]
, who give a natural setting in which Ayoub’s construction of nearby cycles (
[
Ayo07b
]
) works. We hope that this paper will be useful as a toolbox for studying the Hodge cohomology of stacks. It has been used by T. Kinjo in
[
Kin24
]
to prove purity statements and decomposition theorems for the homology of stacks having a good moduli space.

There is another approach to giving a relative version of mixed Hodge structures. In
[
Dre18
]
, Drew constructs an
\infty
-category of
motivic Hodge modules

\mathrm{DH}(X)
which is endowed with the
6
operations as well as with a notion of weights. If
X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
this category embeds fully faithfully in
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{MHM}^{p}_{\mathbb{C}})
the derived category of the indization of mixed Hodge modules. This construction has the advantage of being quite straightforward: one consider the commutative algebra
\mathcal{H}
in the
\infty
-category of Voevodsky étale motives that represents Hodge cohomology, and then one takes modules over this algebra. In comparison, M. Saito’s construction is very delicate and requires a lot of attention in order to work. The major drawback of Drew’s construction is that there was no easy construction of a t-structure, hence one looses access to an abelian category. The second purpose of this article is to prove that Drew’s category gives the right thing: it is endowed with a t-structure and it embeds fully faithfully in the derived category of mixed Hodge modules. We also identify its image.

Theorem

(
Theorem

2.7
)
.

Let
X
be a finite type scheme over the complex numbers. The
\infty
-category of motivic Hodge modules on
X
of Drew embeds in the derived category of ind-mixed Hodge modules on
X
. Its image is the category generated under shifts, and colimits by objects of the form
f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}
with
f:Y\to X
a proper morphism.

There is also an improved version of this theorem with enriched motivic Hodge modules, that reach the
\infty
-category generated under shifts and colimits by objects of the form
f_{*}g^{*}H
with
f:Y\to X
proper,
g:Y\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
the structural morphism and
H\in\mathrm{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}}
a graded polarisable rational mixed Hodge structure over
\mathbb{C}
. We refer the reader to
Section

2
for more details.
To prove this result, we use that by the work of Drew and Gallauer (
[
DG22
]
) the
\infty
-categorical enhancement of mixed Hodge modules provides a realisation functor

\rho_{\mathrm{H}}\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\to\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(-))

from the presentable
\infty
-category of rational étale Voevodsky motives to the indization of the derived category of mixed Hodge modules, that commutes with the operations and is colimit preserving. By abstract nonsense this functor will factor through Drew’s category of mixed Hodge modules, and gives a fully faithful functor
\mathrm{DH}\to\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM})
. The identification of the image is inspired by the proof of Ayoub in the Betti case (
[
Ayo22
, Theorem 1.98]
), and relies on the semi-simplicity of smooth and proper pushforwards of pure mixed Hodge modules.

Organisation of the paper

In the first
Section

1
of this article we recall how to construct the
\infty
-categorical enhancement of the
6
operations for mixed Hodge modules, and why this gives a Hodge realisation of étale motives. In the second
Section

2
we show that Drew’s construction embeds fully faithfully in mixed Hodge modules. In the last
Section

3
we explain how to extend mixed Hodge modules on stacks, finishing with a comparison to existing constructions.

1
Recollections on
\infty
-categorical enhancements of mixed Hodge modules

1.1
Construction of the enhancement

In
[
Tub25
]
, we proved that Saito’s construction of the triangulated bounded derived category of algebraic mixed Hodge modules, together with the
6
operations, can be enhanced to the world of
\infty
-categories. Let us recall how this works:

For every separated finite type
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
X
, the bounded derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
of mixed Hodge modules carries a natural
standard
t-structure (called the
constructible
t-structure in
loc. cit.
but we prefer the name standard because all complexes on
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))
are constructible) which is characterised by the fact that

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))^{t_{\mathrm{std}}\in[a,b]}=\{K\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))\mid\forall x\in X\text{ closed},\ x^{*}K\in\mathrm{D}^{[a,b]}(\mathrm{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}})\}

for every
-\infty\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant+\infty
. If we endow
\mathrm{D}_{c}^{b}(X(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})
with its canonical t-structure induced by the inclusion

\mathrm{D}_{c}^{b}(X(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})\subset\mathrm{D}(\operatorname{\mathrm{Sh}}(X(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q}))

then the "underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-structure functor"

\mathrm{rat}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})

is t-exact if the left-hand side is endowed with the standard t-structure. Denote by
\mathrm{MHM}_{\mathrm{std}}(X)
the heart of the standard t-structure. The crucial result in
[
Tub25
]
is the following, whose proof is adapted from Nori’s proof (
[
Nor02
]
) of the analogous result for
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X(\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})
:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Tub25
, Corollary 2.19]
)
.

The canonical functor

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}_{\mathrm{std}}(X))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(X))

is an equivalence.

Using this, because pullbacks
f^{*}
are t-exact for the standard t-structure, it is not hard to see that they are derived functors, hence that they canonically have a
\infty
-categorical enhancement which is functorial in
f
, and the same can be said for the tensor product. The enhancements of the other operations arises by adjunction: if a
\infty
-functor between stable
\infty
-categories has an adjoint on the homotopy triangulated category, it has an
\infty
-categorical adjoint by
[
NRS20
, Theorem 3.3.1]
.

In particular, we have a functor

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(-)):\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{St})

taking values in the
\infty
-category of stably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories and symmetric monoidal exact functors. It sends a map
f:Y\to X
of finite type
\mathbb{C}
-schemes (say, separated) to an
\infty
-functor
f^{*}
lifting Saito’s pullback functor on the homotopy category. For smooth
f
, the functor
f^{*}
admits a left adjoint
f_{\sharp}
, and moreover
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(-))
satisfies the usual axioms considered in
motivic context:
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance,
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stability, smooth base change, proper base change
etc
. In particular, the underlying homotopy functor is a
motivic triangulated category
in the sense of
[
CD19
, Definition 2.4.45]
, it satisfies moreover
h
-hyperdescent. We now consider the functor

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(-):=\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(-)):\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{St}})

taking values in
presentable

\infty
-categories. By
[
Tub25
, Appendix A]
, we see that both
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}
and
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(-))
extend to non necessarily separated schemes using Zariski descent, together with all the operations.

Remark 1.2
.

The above functor extends tautologically to a functor from diagram of schemes to diagram of symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories, hence we can evaluate
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}
on a simplicial scheme to obtain a cosimplicial diagram of
\infty
-categories. Using the fact that it is easy (see
[
Sai90b
, Remark before 4.6]
) to compare the mixed Hodge structures on
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{sing}}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Q})
constructed by Deligne and Saito (under the equivalence
\mathrm{MHM}(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}))\simeq\mathrm{MHS}^{p}_{\mathbb{C}}
) when
X
is a closed subset of a smooth variety,
h
-hyperdescent of the derived category of mixed Hodge modules gives (using a resolution of singularities, that provides a
h
-hypercovering made out of smooth varieties) a simple proof that in fact the two mixed Hodge structures are the same for a general complex variety
X
. This result was known, but the proof is quite involved (see
[
Sai00
]
).

Remark 1.3
.

Everything we do in this article would probably hold more generally for arithmetic mixed Hodge modules over varieties defined over a subfield
k
of
\mathbb{C}
. They are considered in
[
Sai06
, Examples 1.8 (ii)]
, and our work in
[
Tub25
]
, thus the proofs of this article, would probably work
verbatim
in this slightly more general context.

1.2
Hodge realisation of étale motives

The main motivation for the
\infty
-categorical lifting of mixed Hodge modules was that this was the only obstruction for the existence of a realisation functor from Voevodsky motives that commutes with all the operations. We will deal here with the étale version, with rational coefficients.
Recall that the
\infty
-category of étale motives with rational coefficients over a scheme
X
is defined (see
[
Rob15
]
) as a formula by

\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X):=\operatorname{\mathrm{Sh}}_{{\mathrm{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{\wedge}(\mathrm{Sm}_{X},\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}})[\mathbb{Q}(1)^{\otimes-1}].

This means that to construct
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)
, one consider étale hypersheaves on smooth
X
-schemes with values in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\mathrm{D}(\mathbb{Q})
that are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant, and then one invert the object
\mathbb{Q}(1)=(M(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X})/M(\infty_{X}))[-2]
for the tensor product, with
M
the Yoneda embedding. The formula gives an universal property of the presentable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)
, as proven by Robalo in
[
Rob15
]
: any symmetric monoidal functor
F
on smooth
X
-schemes with values in a rational presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathrm{D}
that satisfies étale hyperdescent,
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance, and such that
F(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{X})/F(\infty_{X})
is a tensor invertible object factors uniquely through the functor
M\colon\mathrm{Sm}_{X}\to\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)
. By the work of Drew and Gallauer
[
DG22
]
in fact this universality of
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)
induces a universal property of the functor
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}
on schemes of finite type over some base
S
. Together with
[
CD19
, Theorem 4.4.25]
, taking
S=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
one obtains:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
Tub25
, Theorem 4.4]
)
.

There exists a Hodge realisation

\rho_{\mathrm{H}}\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}

on finite type
\mathbb{C}
-schemes that commutes with the
6
operations. Moreover, the composition with the functor

\mathrm{rat}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}\to\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(-,\mathbb{Q})

gives the Betti realisation.

We will use this functor in the next section to compare mixed Hodge modules with Drew’s approach, and at the end of this article to obtain the computation of the cohomology of a quotient stack with exhaustions in a easy way.

2
Mixed Hodge modules of geometric origin

In this section, we prove that Drew’s approach in
[
Dre18
]
to motivic Hodge modules gives a full subcategory of the derived category of mixed Hodge modules, stable under truncation, and thus has a t-structure.

2.1
Motivic Hodge modules

As recalled above, we have a Hodge realisation

\rho_{\mathrm{H}}\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}

compatible with all the operations. This functor is
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear. The target
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}
is naturally valued in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHS}^{p}_{\mathbb{C}})
-linear presentable
\infty
-categories, where
\mathrm{MHS}^{p}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the abelian category of polarisable mixed Hodge structures over
\mathbb{C}
(with rational coefficients). Thus the above realisation has a natural enrichment

\mathbf{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\mathbf{{DM}}\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}

where
\mathbf{DM}:=\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})
is the
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})
-linearisation of
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}
. It can be computed as

\mathbf{DM}(X)=\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C}),

but can also be put inside the definition:

\mathbf{DM}(X)\simeq\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathbb{A}^{1},{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}^{\wedge}(\mathrm{Sm}_{X},\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C}))[\mathbb{Q}(1)^{\otimes-1}]

(this follows from
[
Vol23
, Corollary 2.24]
which proves that tensoring can go inside sheaves, but also works for localisations such as
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localisation. The commutation of tensoring with
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})
with inverting the Tate twist follows from the expression of the
\mathbb{Q}(1)
-tensor inverted category as a colimit (see
[
AI23
, 1.4.12, 1.3.13, 1.6.3]
), and the preservation of colimits by the functor
-\otimes\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})
). Thus the
\infty
-category
\mathbf{DM}
is the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stabilisation of the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant étale hypersheaves on
\mathrm{Sm}_{X}
with values in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, it also affords the
6
operations and the canonical functor
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\to\mathbf{DM}
commutes with them.
For each finite type
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
X
, the functors

{\rho_{H,X}}\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(X)

and

{\rho_{\mathbf{H},X}}\colon\mathbf{{DM}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(X)

are colimit preserving symmetric monoidal functors, hence they have lax symmetric monoidal right adjoints
\rho^{H,X}_{*}
and
\rho^{\mathbf{H},X}_{*}
that create commutative algebras

\mathcal{H}_{X}:=\rho_{*}^{H,X}\mathbb{Q}_{X}\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X))

and

\bm{\mathbf{\mathcal{H}}}_{X}:=\rho_{*}^{\mathbf{H},X}\mathbb{Q}_{X}\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathbf{DM}(X))

in étale motives. Because the right adjoints commute with pushforwards, the counit maps induces algebra maps

\mathcal{H}_{X/\mathbb{C}}:=\pi_{X}^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathcal{H}_{X}

and

\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{X/\mathbb{C}}:=\pi_{X}^{*}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{X},

where
\pi_{X}:X\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
is the structural map. We will show below that those maps are in fact equivalences.

Definition 2.1

(Drew)
.

The
\infty
-category of motivic Hodge modules over
X
is

\mathrm{DH}(X):=\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{H}_{X/\mathbb{C}}}(\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(X)).

There is an enriched version, that we will call the
\infty
-category of enriched mixed Hodge modules over
X
, defined as

\mathbf{DH}(X):=\mathrm{Mod}_{\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{X/\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbf{DM}(X)).

An advantage of
\mathbf{DH}(X)
when compared to
\mathrm{DH}(X)
is that over a point, the
\infty
-category
\mathbf{DH}(X)
is the whole derived category of graded polarisable mixed Hodge structures, whereas
\mathrm{DH}(X)
only consists of mixed Hodge structures of geometric origin. However:

Notation 2.2
.

As the reader begins to guess, both situations, motivic Hodge modules and enriched motivic Hodge modules are parallel. Thus from now on except for the important results we will only deal with motivic Hodge modules, the proofs in the enriched case being the same. This is only to avoid doubling the size of this article, and the extensive use of bold.

The functor
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}
is in fact valued in the
\infty
-category of
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
-linear presentable
\infty
-categories, and the construction
\mathrm{DH}=\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{H}_{-/\mathbb{C}}}(\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}})
can be rewritten

\mathrm{DH}=\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{DH}(\mathbb{C}).

As
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{DH}(\mathbb{C})
are rigid (this means that they are indization of small symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories in which every object is dualisable: by
[
Ayo14
, Proposition 3.19]
they are compactly generated, by
[
Ayo07a
, Proposition 2.2.27]
the motives of smooth projective varieties generate the compact objects, and by
[
Rio05
, Théorème 2.2]
the motives of smooth projective varieties are strongly dualisable), the
\infty
-functor

-\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{DH}(\mathbb{C})\colon\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})}\to\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{DH}(\mathbb{C})}

(2.2.1)

sending a
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
-linear presentable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{DH}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{C})
has an
(\infty
-2)-categorical enhancement thanks to
[
HSS17
, Section 4.4]
. In particular any adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bot}

between
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
-linear presentable
\infty
-categories, such that the right adjoint
G
itself commutes with colimits and is
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
-linear (this is automatic if

preserves compact objects by
[

because
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
and

are rigid), the image under

is again an internal

is the right adjoint to

. Now it turns out that all properties of coefficients systems (

\infty

being an equivalence, where

is naturally a coefficient system in the sense of Drew and Gallauer, and therefore affords the 6 operations in a way that is compatible to the functor
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\to\mathrm{DH}
(by
[
Ayo07a
]
and
[
CD19
]

-descent (by

\infty
-

[
Dre18
, Theorem 8.10]
.

2.2
Embedding in mixed Hodge modules

The Hodge realisation naturally factors as

\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\xrightarrow{\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}\mathrm{DH}\xrightarrow{\underline{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}

where all functors commute with the operations and all categories are compactly generated on schemes. Indeed one can see it in the following way:

\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}\to\mathrm{DH}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{H}_{(-)/\mathbb{C}}}(\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}})\xrightarrow{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{(-)/\mathbb{C}})}(\mathrm{DH})\to\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{DH})\xleftarrow{\sim}\mathrm{DH}

where the map
\rho_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{H}_{(-)/\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{Q}
is induced by the co-unit of the adjunction
(\rho_{\mathrm{H}},\rho^{H}_{*})
.

The first observation is a consequence of the commutation with the operations:

Lemma 2.3
.

For each finite type
\mathbb{C}
-scheme, the functor

\underline{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\mathrm{DH}\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}

is fully faithful. Moreover the map

\mathcal{H}_{X/\mathbb{C}}\to\mathcal{H}_{X}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of
[
Tub25
, Lemma 4.14]
and is originally due to Cisinski and Déglise. We recall it here. First over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
the functor is fully faithful by
[
Dre18
, Lemma 4.11]
, because
\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\mathbb{C})
is rigid and
\rho_{\mathrm{H}}
preserves compact objects. Over a general base it suffices to prove that the functor is fully faithful on compact objects
\mathrm{DH}^{\omega}
. Using that

the cohomology functor for the standard t-structure.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\eta
be the generic point of an irreducible finite type

-scheme

, and consider the functor

\mathrm{colim}_{\eta\in U}\mathrm{DH}(U)\to\mathrm{colim}_{\eta\in U}(\mathrm{D}_{H,\mathrm{geo}}(U))

(2.6.1)

induced by
\underline{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}}
, where the colimit (taken in
\mathrm{Pr}^{L}
) runs over all the smooth open subsets
U
of
X
that contain

. It is an equivalence.

Proof.

Note that by
[
Lur09
, 4.4.5.21, 5.5.7.8]
, the colimit is in fact computed in
\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\omega}
the
\infty
-category of compactly generated
\infty
-categories, and we can rewrite it as

\mathrm{Ind}\Big(\mathrm{colim}_{\eta\in U}\mathrm{DH}(U)^{\omega}\to\mathrm{colim}_{\eta\in U}(\mathrm{D}_{H,\mathrm{geo}}(U))^{\omega}\Big)

where now the colimit inside the
\mathrm{Ind}(-)
is taken in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
. By
[
HRS25
, Proposition 2.1]
the functor between compact objects is fully faithful, using
Lemma

2.3
. It remains to show that it is essentially surjective. It suffices to reach compact objects.

Denote by

\mathcal{D}:=\mathrm{colim}_{\eta\in U}(\mathrm{D}_{H,\mathrm{geo}}(U)^{\omega})

the colimit of the compact objects, computed in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
.

For each

, the compact objects of
\mathrm{D}_{H,\mathrm{geo}}(U)
have a standard t-structure and its heart is the category defined in the same way as
\mathrm{MHM}_{\mathrm{geo}}^{\mathrm{std}}(U)
, but allowing only direct factors instead of all filtered colimits. As all transitions in the diagram are t-exact, the colimit
\mathcal{D}
of the compact objects (which are the compact objects of the colimit) has a bounded t-structure. Moreover,
the category
\mathcal{D}
is generated under finite colimits, finite limits, extensions and truncations by images in the colimit of objects of the form
g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n)
where
g\colon Z\to U
is a proper morphism and

is an integer.

By continuity of motives
[
EHIK21
, Lemma 5.1 (ii)]
and
[
LurHA
, Theorem 4.8.5.11]
, the left hand side of
Equation

2.6.1
is canonically equivalent to
\mathrm{DH}(\eta):=\mathrm{Mod}_{u^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\operatorname{\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}}(\eta))
, where
u:\eta\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
is the structural morphism. Moreover, by
[
Ayo07a
, Lemma 2.2.27]
, the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{DH}(\eta)
is generated under colimits by the
f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K(n)
where
f:Y\to\eta
is a smooth and projective morphism,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
K\in\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. By spreading out (
[
Gro66
, 8.10.5(xiii)]
,
[
Gro67
, 17.7.8(ii)]
) and proper base change, such an object is the restriction to
\eta
of a
g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n)
with
g:Z\to U
a smooth projective morphism, and
U
an open subset of
X
.

Now consider an object of the form
g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n)
in
\mathcal{D}
, where
g\colon Z\to U
is a proper morphism and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer. This object is the image by
Equation

2.6.1
of
(g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n))_{\eta}\in\mathrm{DH}(\eta)
. By the above paragraph, there exists an open subset
V
of
U
, a finite category
I
and a functor
F\colon I\to\mathrm{DH}(V)
such that for each
i\in I
, the object
F(i)
is of the form
(f_{i})_{*}\pi_{Z_{i}}^{*}K(n_{i})
for
f_{i}\colon Z_{i}\to U
a
smooth and projective
morphism and

, and such that in
\mathrm{DH}(\eta)
, we have

(g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n))_{\eta}\simeq(\mathrm{colim}_{I}F(i))_{\eta}.

In particular, in
\mathcal{D}
, the object
g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n)
is isomorphic to the image in the colimit over all open subsets, of the finite colimit
\mathrm{colim}_{I}F^{\prime}(i)
, where
F^{\prime}
is the composition of the functor
F
with
Equation

2.6.1
, which has the same formula for
F^{\prime}(i)
.

This gives that the category
\mathcal{D}
is generated under finite colimits, finite limits, direct factors, extensions and truncations by images in the colimit of objects of the form
g_{*}\pi_{Z}^{*}K(n)
with
g:Z\to U
smooth and projective. We may also assume

to be smooth.

It turns out that to prove that the functor of the proposition is essentially surjective, it suffices to check that it reaches all objects of the heart of the compact objects. Thanks to
[
Ayo22
, Lemma 1.6.22]
, we see that it suffices to show that all subquotients of the image in the colimit of all
{\ {}^{\mathrm{std}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
, for
K\in\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
compact and
f:Y\to U
projective and smooth, are in the image.
Now by dévissage we can assume
K
to be pure, so that by the conservation of weights under pushforwards by proper maps, and because
U
is smooth hence
Y
is also smooth so that
\pi_{Y}^{*}K
is pure, the object
f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K
is pure.
Using the decomposition theorem for pure complexes, we see that there is a decomposition

f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K\simeq\bigoplus_{n}{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)[-n].

In particular, each
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
lies in the image.
As
f
is smooth and proper and
\pi_{Y}^{*}K
is a dualisable object coming from
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})
, we see that (as in
[
Rio05
, Theorem 2.2]
, the dual is
f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K^{\vee}(d)[2d]
where
d
is the relative dimension of
f
) the complex
f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K
is dualisable, so that
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
are local systems. Because duality datum consists of finitely many maps, and because compact objects in the colimit are dualisable, we see that any subquotient of the image of
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
in the colimit will lift as a subquotient in the category of local systems, of the restriction of
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
to a smaller open subset. Moreover, as
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
is lisse we have
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K[d])[-d]\simeq{\ {}^{\mathrm{std}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
where
d=\dim U
(this is because over a smooth scheme, and for lisse complexes, the standard t-structure is the perverse t-structure, shifted by the dimension
[
BBD82
, proof of Proposition 2.1.3]
). This proves that any subquotient of the image of
{\ {}^{\mathrm{std}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K)
in the colimit is in fact a subquotient in perverse sheaves of a suitable restriction of
{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(f_{*}\pi_{Y}^{*}K[d])
to an open subset of
U
. As this object is a pure perverse sheaf, any subquotient is in fact a direct factor, thus is in the image, and the proof is finished.
∎

This theorem is the same as Ayoub’s theorem for Betti sheaves, and is proved the same way.

Remark 2.8
.

In a forthcoming work with Raphaël Ruimy, we use this point of view of motivic Hodge modules to construct categories of mixed Hodge modules of geometric origin that have
\mathbb{Z}
-linear coefficients.

Although what we have done ensures that the goals of Drew in
[
Dre18
, Desiderata 1.1]
are achieved, it may still seem unsatisfactory: another motivation for Drew’s work was probably to give an alternative construction of mixed Hodge modules. Here our proof uses mixed Hodge modules hence it is not quite right. It should be possible to prove that motivic Hodge modules afford a t-structure without comparing them to M. Saito’s mixed Hodge modules.

We give here a weak reason for which one could possibly define the t-structure only by geometric means (a t-structure on the generic points is enough to have a t-structure for every variety by gluing, see

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be an Artin stack locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. The
\infty
-category
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})
admits a t-structure such that for any smooth presentation
\pi:X\to\mathfrak{X}
the conservative functor
\pi^{*}
is t-exact up to a shift. Moreover, the t-structure is right complete and compatible with filtered colimits. It restricts to a t-structure on
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(-)
.

Proof.

We have to first deal with the case
\mathfrak{X}
is an algebraic space. In this case, the map
\pi
can be chosen étale, and as the diagonal of
\mathfrak{X}
is representable by schemes, the limit diagram

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})\to\lim_{\Delta}(X^{n/X})

has t-exact transitions whence the limit
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})
has a perverse t-structure. The left separation and compatibility with colimits can be checked after applying
\pi^{*}
, hence hold. The right completeness is checked exactly as in the case of stacks, that we deal with below.

We can assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is a connected algebraic stack. Choose a presentation
\pi:X\to\mathfrak{X}
which is smooth of relative dimension
d
for some integer
d
. Then all the projections

p_{n}:X\times_{\mathfrak{X}}X\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\cdots\times_{\mathfrak{X}}X\to X\times_{\mathfrak{X}}\cdots\times_{\mathfrak{X}}X

are also smooth of relative dimension
d
, so that each
p_{n}^{*}[d]
are t-exact for the perverse t-structure. As
3.0.1
can be done with the shifts
p_{n}^{*}[d]
, this creates a t-structure on the limit of the
\check{\text{C}}
ech nerve of
\pi
such that
\pi^{*}
is t-exact. It does not depend on
\pi
as one can see by taking another presentation
p:Y\to\mathfrak{X}
and then
Y\times_{\mathfrak{X}}X\to\mathfrak{X}
.
The t-structure obviously restricts to
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(\mathfrak{X})
. For the right completeness, we want to show that the natural functor

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})\to\lim\Big(\cdots\xrightarrow{\tau^{\geqslant 1}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\geqslant 1}(\mathfrak{X})\xrightarrow{\tau^{\geqslant 2}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\geqslant 2}(\mathfrak{X})\xrightarrow{\tau^{\geqslant 3}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\geqslant 3}(\mathfrak{X})\xrightarrow{\tau^{\geqslant 4}}\cdots\Big)

is an equivalence. As (shifts of) pullbacks by smooth maps are t-exact, this can be checked locally on
\mathfrak{X}
, hence holds.
∎

3.2
Duality

We want a working Verdier duality on algebraic stacks locally of finite type over the complex numbers.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be an algebraic stack locally of finite type over the complex numbers. The dualising object on
\mathfrak{X}
is the object
\omega_{\mathfrak{X}}:=\pi_{\mathfrak{X}}^{!}\mathbb{Q}(0)\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})
with
\pi_{\mathfrak{X}}\colon\mathfrak{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
. The Verdier duality functor is

\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{X}}:=\sHom(-,\omega_{\mathfrak{X}}).

If follows directly from the projection formula between
g_{!}
and
g^{*}
, for

a morphism of algebraic stacks, that there are natural equivalences

g^{!}\circ\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{X}}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\circ g^{*}

and

g_{*}\circ\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{X}}\circ g_{!}

for any morphism
g:\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
between algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
g:\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
be a
smooth
morphism between algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Then there is also a natural equivalence

g^{*}\circ\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{X}}\simeq\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{Y}}\circ g^{!}

of functors

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{Y})
.

Proof.

The smooth projection formula implies that the canonical map

g^{*}\sHom(-,\omega_{X})\to\sHom(g^{*}(-),g^{*}\omega_{\mathfrak{X}})

is an equivalence. Now, using the purity isomorphism twice
g^{*}\simeq g^{!}(-d)[-2d]
we see that the right-hand side is equivalent to
\sHom(g^{!}(-),g^{!}\omega_{\mathfrak{X}})
, and because
g^{!}\omega_{\mathfrak{X}}\simeq\omega_{\mathfrak{Y}}
, the proof is finished.
∎

Proposition 3.13

and

The remaining assertions follow from the autoduality over schemes (

).

3.3
Weights

In this subsection we introduce weights for mixed Hodge modules on stacks. We will work only with stack that are of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
in this section. The abelian category of mixed Hodge modules over a scheme has a functorial and exact
weight filtration
. Using ideas of the PhD of Sophie Morel, for each integer
w\in\mathbb{Z}
we can define a t-structure “
\omega\leqslant w
” on
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H,c}}(X)
, that we call the
weight-t-structure
(
Construction

3.17
). It has the particularity that both its right and left sides are stable subcategories of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H,c}}(X)
, so that the heart is zero, but the truncations functors are exact functors. This provides the canonical extension of the weight filtration to the bounded derived category: we obtain exact functors
\tau^{(\omega\leqslant w)\leqslant 0}=:\omega_{\leqslant w}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H,c}}(X)\to\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H,c}}(X)
that are perverse t-exact, and whose restriction to the perverse heart gives back the weight filtration of mixed Hodge modules. By indization we obtain the weight filtration on the Ind-category. For this t-structure, all pullbacks by smooth morphisms are t-exact, so that we can extend the weight filtration to any algebraic stack by descent (
Proposition

3.18
). In particular, the perverse heart of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})
is endowed with an exact weight filtration. This gives the possibility to define
punctual weights
(we chose this name because this is the correct analogue of punctual weights in the
\ell
-adic setting) in
Definition

3.19
.

However, this does not quite exactly gives a weight structure
à la Bondarko
. Indeed, the weight filtration may fail to have sufficiently many orthogonality properties, so that the property that a pure perverse object is semi-simple may not be satisfied (see
Remark

3.25
). However, if
\mathfrak{X}
is an algebraic stack
with affine stabilisers
, then the objects that are of punctual weight
0
are indeed the heart of a weight structure, as we show in
Corollary

3.24
.

Construction 3.17
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X
be a finite type
\mathbb{C}
-scheme. Recall that following
[
Mor08
]
we can construct a weight-t-structure on
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)
by setting

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)^{\omega\leqslant w}:=\{K\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)\mid\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\forall m>w,\ \mathrm{gr}_{m}^{W}({\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(K))=0\}

and

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)^{\omega\geqslant w}:=\{K\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)\mid\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\forall m<w,\ \mathrm{gr}_{m}^{W}({\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{n}(K))=0\},

where the
\mathrm{gr}_{m}^{W}
are the graded pieces of the weight filtration on mixed Hodge modules (
[
Sai89
, Proposition 1.5]
).
The pair
(\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)^{\omega\leqslant w},\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)^{\omega\geqslant w+1})
form a t-structure (of trivial heart) on
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(X)
, such that the truncations functors
\omega_{\leqslant w}
and
\omega_{\geqslant w+1}
are exact functors, t-exact for the perverse t-structure (
[
Mor08
, Proposition 3.1.1.]
). They give a filtration
(\omega_{\leqslant w}K)_{w}
on each complex
K
, that gives back the weight filtration on the heart. For any smooth map
f:Y\to X
of finite type
\mathbb{C}
-schemes, and any integer
d
, the functor
f^{*}[d]
is t-exact for the weight t-structures (indeed, if
d
is the relative dimension of
f
we see that
f^{*}[d]
is perverse t-exact and preserves weights, thus is t-exact for the weight-t-structure. The shift functor being weight-t-exact this proves the claim). This induces a weight t-structure on
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(X)
by indization and it has a similar description.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be an Artin stack of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Then the
\infty
-categories
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})
and
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H},c}(\mathfrak{X})
admit weight-t-structures such that for every smooth map
f:X\to\mathfrak{X}
with
X
a finite type
\mathbb{C}
-scheme and every integer
d
, the functor
f^{*}
is weight-t-exact. Moreover, the weight truncations functors are perverse t-exact. The inclusion functor
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H},c}(\mathfrak{X})\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{X})
is weight-t-exact.

Proof.

Once again assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is connected, choose
\pi:X\to\mathfrak{X}
a presentation of
\mathfrak{X}
, of relative dimension
d
. Then if
f_{n}
is a part of the
\check{\text{C}}
ech nerve of
\pi
, the functors

are t-exact for the weight t-structures, thus this induces a weight t-structure on the limit as in
3.0.1
(as in the proof of

we have to first deal with algebraic spaces but everything works the same). Of course, it restricts to
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{H},c}(\mathfrak{X})
, and then to
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H},c}(\mathfrak{X})

∎

Definition 3.19
.

Let

.
A object

is of

\leqslant w
if for each

{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{i}(\omega_{\leqslant w+i}K)\to{\ {}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{H}}^{i}(K)

.

Remark 3.20
.

The above definition

from the weight t-structure we used to define weights.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let

be a morphism of algebraic stacks of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and let

(3.25.1)

We wish to construct a nearby cycle functor

\Psi_{f}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{U})\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{Z})

with good properties. We begin with the unipotent part of the nearby cycle following
[
CHS24
]
, whose authors give a wonderful interpretation of Ayoub’s unipotent nearby cycle functor (as in
[
Ayo07b
]
).

In
[
CHS24
, Definition 2.22]
, Cass, van den Hove and Scholbach define a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear stable
\infty
-category
\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}
(denoted by
\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
in
loc. cit.
) whose objects can be interpreted as pairs
(K,N)
with
K\in\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
a complex of graded
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces and
N:K(1)\to K
is a locally nilpotent map of graded complexes, where
K(1):=K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(1)
and
\mathbb{Q}(1)
is the graded
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space placed in degree
-1
. This
\infty
-category is compactly generated by objects
(\mathbb{Q}(k),\mathbb{Q}(k+1)\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb{Q}(k))
, and if
(K,N)\in\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}
is compact, its underlying complex
K
is compact and the operator
N
is nilpotent. Now for any stably presentable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
with and action of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, on can define

\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}},

which has a similar description as pairs
(c,N)
with
c\in\mathcal{C}
and
N:c(1)\to c
, which is nilpotent if
c
is compact.

The relation of this construction with unipotent nearby cycles
\Upsilon_{f}
comes from the desire to have a monodromy operator on
\Upsilon_{f}
, thus a lift of
\Upsilon
as a functor

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{U})\to\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{Z}).

This is made possible thanks to the following observation.

Proposition 3.26
.

Let
p:{\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
be the projection. Then the pushforward

p_{*}\colon\mathrm{D}_{HT}({\mathbb{G}_{m}})\to\mathrm{D}_{HT}(\mathbb{C})

exhibits the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{D}_{HT}({\mathbb{G}_{m}})
as
\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}\mathrm{D}_{HT}(\mathbb{C})
. Here we have denoted by
\mathrm{D}_{HT}(X)
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(X)
of mixed Hodge Tate modules over
X
, that is the
\infty
-category generated under colimits and shifts by the
\mathbb{Q}(i)
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

This is the combination of
[
CHS24
, Corollary 2.20 and Lemma 2.17]
, that we reproduce for the reader’s convenience. First we remark that the functor
p_{*}
indeed preserves Hodge Tate objects because
p_{*}\mathbb{Q}(n)\simeq\mathbb{Q}(n)\oplus\mathbb{Q}(n+1)[1]
. Now, it is clear that
p_{*}
preserves colimits as its left adjoint
p^{*}
preserves compact objects, and it is also conservative because if
p_{*}M=0
then for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the mapping spectra
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{D}_{HT}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{\mathbb{C}})}(\mathbb{Q}(n),M)\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{D}_{HT}(\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{Q}(n),p_{*}M)
vanishes, so that
M=0
. Thus, by Barr-Beck’s theorem, the functor
p_{*}
upgrades to an equivalence

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{\mathbb{C}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Mod}_{p_{*}\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbb{C})).

There is a unique colimit preserving symmetric monoidal functor

i\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathrm{D}_{HT}(\mathbb{C})

that sends
\mathbb{Q}(1)
to
\mathbb{Q}(1)
.
The image under the Hodge realisation of
[
CHS24
, Lemma 2.18]
tells us that
p_{*}\mathbb{Q}
is, as a commutative algebra object, the object
i(\Lambda)
where
\Lambda
is the split square zero extension
\Lambda:=\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}(-1)[-1]
. Thus we have that

\mathrm{D}_{HT}({\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{p_{*}\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{D}_{HT}(\mathbb{C}))\simeq\mathrm{D}_{HT}(\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{Mod}_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}}).

and the equivalence
\mathrm{Mod}_{\Lambda}(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}
of
[
CHS24
, Lemma 2.9]
finishes the proof.
∎

We come back to the setup
3.25.1
, and define, as
[
CHS24
, Definition 3.1]
:

Definition 3.27
.

The unipotent nearby cycle functor is the composite

\Upsilon_{f}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{U})\to\mathrm{Mod}_{f_{\eta}^{*}p^{*}p_{*}\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{U}))\simeq\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{U})\xrightarrow{i^{*}j_{*}}\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{Z})

where the first functor is the way one can see any object
K\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathfrak{U})
as a
f_{\eta}^{*}p^{*}p_{*}\mathbb{Q}
-module with the counit
f_{\eta}^{*}p^{*}p_{*}\mathbb{Q}\otimes K\to K
, and the second is expressing that
i^{*}j_{*}
preserves the nilpotent structure.

In fact as they show in
[
CHS24
, Section 3.1]
, there is a better way to look at the above definition.
Indeed, the construction of the operations for stacks in
Theorem

3.1
goes by extending the functor
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}
on schemes to a lax monoidal functor

\mathrm{D}_{H}\colon\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{St}},

where
\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the
\infty
-category of correspondences of stacks over
\mathbb{C}
: its objects are derived algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and its morphisms are informally diagrams

Y\xleftarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{f}X

of algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Composition is given by forming pullback squares. The functor sends
X
to
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(X)
, and the above morphism to

g_{!}f^{*}\colon\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(Y)\to\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(X).

In fact, the projection formulae even allow us to have a functor with values in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{D}_{H}(\mathbb{C})}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{St}})
.

Now, unipotent nearby cycles can be upgraded to a highly coherent natural transformation
\Upsilon\colon\mathrm{D}_{H\eta}\to\operatorname{\mathrm{Nilp}}\mathrm{D}_{Hs}
of lax monoidal functors

\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}}^{\mathrm{pr,sm}}\to\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{gr}}^{L},

where
\mathrm{Cor}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}}^{\mathrm{pr,sm}}
is the wide subcategory (so it has the same objects but fewer morphisms) of
\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}}=(\mathrm{Corr}_{\mathbb{C}})_{/\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}}
whose morphisms are the roofs
Y\xleftarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{f}X
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
with
g
proper and
f
smooth, and
\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{gr}}^{L}:=\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{St}})
is the
\infty
-category of presentable
\infty
-categories with an action of graded complexes of vector spaces.

Here,

Corollary 3.41
.

In
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{Ind}\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MHM}(\mathcal{M}))

Proof.

The Hodge realisation

The minimal primes of localizations of rings
V. V. Bavula

Abstract
The set of minimal primes of a ring is a very important set as far the spectrum of a ring is concerned as every prime contains a minimal prime. So, knowing the minimal primes is the first (important and difficult) step in describing the spectrum. In the algebraic geometry, the minimal primes of the algebra of regular functions on an algebraic variety determine/correspond to the irreducible components of the variety. The aim of the paper is to obtain several descriptions of the set of minimal prime ideals of localizations of rings under several natural assumptions. In particular, the following case are considered: a localization of a semiprime ring with finite set of minimal primes; a localization of a prime rich ring where the localization respects the ideal structure of primes and primeness of certain minimal primes; a localization of a ring at a left denominator set generated by normal elements, and others. As an application, for a semiprime ring with finitely many minimal primes, a description of the minimal primes of it’s largest left/right quotient ring is obtained. For a semiprime ring R R with finitely many minimal primes min ⁡ ( R ) \min(R) , criteria are given for the map ρ R , min : min ⁡ ( R ) → min ⁡ ( Z ⁡ ( R ) ) , 𝔭 ↦ 𝔭 ∩ Z ⁡ ( R ) \rho_{R,\min}:\min(R)\rightarrow\min(Z(R)),\;\;\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}\cap Z(R) being a well-defined map or surjective where Z ⁡ ( R ) Z(R) is the centre of R R . Key words: semiprime ring, prime ring, localization, classical left quotient ring, largest left quotient ring, Ore set, denominator set, minimal primes, prime rich ring, denominator set that respects the ideal structure of a ring. Semiprime ideals and minimal primes.
Prime and semiprime rings are large and important classes of rings both in commutative and non-commutative algebra. They also have geometric flavour. The algebra of regular functions on an affine algebraic variety is a semiprime algebra and the corresponding ideal of definition of the variety is a semiprime ideal. If, in addition, the variety is irreducible then the algebra of regular functions on it is a prime algebra and the defining ideal is a prime ideal. In the algebraic geometry, the minimal primes of the algebra of regular functions correspond to the irreducible components of the algebraic variety and generators of minimal primes are the equations of the corresponding irreducible component of the variety. In the commutative situation,
localizations respect many operations and concepts. This is not the case in the noncommutative setting (in general, a localization of an ideal is not an ideal, a localization of a prime ideal is not a prime ideal, there is little connection between prime ideals of a ring and its localization, etc).

(2\Rightarrow 3)

\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
.
Notice that

\mathfrak{q}:=Z(R)\cap\mathfrak{p}\in{\rm Spec}(Z(R)).

Then
\mathfrak{q}\not\in\min(Z(R))
since otherwise
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq{\cal Z}(R)
where
{\cal Z}(R):=\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{l}}\in\min(Z(R))}{\mathfrak{l}}
is the set of zero divisors of the semiprime commutative ring
Z(R)
with
\min(Z(R)|<\infty
but the non-empty subset
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\cap\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{q}
has zero intersection with
{\cal Z}(R)
, a contradiction.

(3\Rightarrow 2)
Suppose that the restriction map
\min(R)\rightarrow\min(Z(R))
,
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}\cap Z(R)
is not a well-defined map. So,
\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{p}\cap Z(R)\not\in\min(Z(R))
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
. Notice that
\mathfrak{q}\in{\rm Spec}(Z(R))
. Therefore,

\mathfrak{q}\not\subseteq{\cal Z}(R)=\bigcup_{{\mathfrak{l}}\in\min(Z(R))}{\mathfrak{l}}

since otherwise
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq{\mathfrak{l}}
for some
{\mathfrak{l}}\in\min(Z(R))
, i.e.
\mathfrak{q}={\mathfrak{l}}\in\min(Z(R))
, a contradiction. Then

\mathfrak{p}\cap{\cal C}_{Z(R)}=\mathfrak{q}\cap{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\neq\emptyset

since
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}=Z(R)\backslash{\cal Z}(R)
.

(2\Rightarrow 1)
Suppose that
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\cap\mathfrak{p}\neq\emptyset
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
. Fix an element
c\in{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\cap\mathfrak{p}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{p}^{c}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R)\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}}\mathfrak{q}\neq 0
and
\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}^{c}=0
. Then

c\mathfrak{p}^{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}^{c}=0
, and so
c\in{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\backslash{\cal C}_{R}
.

Q(Z(R))={\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}Z(R)

\min(Q(Z(R)))=\{{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}\mathfrak{q}\,|\,\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R)\}.

Q(Z(R))
is a semisimple Artinian ring with

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
R
be a semiprime ring such that
|\min(R)|<\infty
,
|\min(Z(R))|<\infty
and
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
. Then

1.

R\subseteq{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}R_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

2.

Z({\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R)={\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}Z(R)\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a finite direct product of fields
Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

3.

For every
\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))
,
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a semiprime ring,
\min(R_{\mathfrak{q}})=\{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{q}}\,|\,\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)\}
and
|\min(R_{\mathfrak{q}})|\leq|\min(R)|<\infty
.

4.

For every
\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))
,
Z(R_{\mathfrak{q}})\simeq Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
, i.e. the
Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
-algebra
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a central algebra.

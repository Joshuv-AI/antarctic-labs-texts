Cyclic forms on DG-Lie algebroids and semiregularity
Emma Lepri

Abstract
. Given a transitive DG-Lie algebroid ( 𝒜 , ρ ) (\mathcal{A},\rho) over a smooth separated scheme X X of finite type over a field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} of characteristic 0 we define a notion of connection ∇ : 𝐑 ​ Γ ​ ( X , Ker ​ ρ ) → 𝐑 ​ Γ ​ ( X , Ω X 1 ​ [ − 1 ] ⊗ Ker ​ ρ ) {\nabla\colon\mathbf{R}\Gamma(X,\mathrm{Ker}\rho)\to\mathbf{R}\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1]\otimes\mathrm{Ker}\rho)} and construct an L ∞ L_{\infty} morphism between DG-Lie algebras f : 𝐑 ​ Γ ​ ( X , Ker ​ ρ ) ↝ 𝐑 ​ Γ ​ ( X , Ω X ≤ 1 ​ [ 2 ] ) f\colon\mathbf{R}\Gamma(X,\mathrm{Ker}\rho)\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{R}\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2]) associated to a connection and to a cyclic form on the DG-Lie algebroid. In this way, we obtain a lifting of the first component of the modified Buchweitz-Flenner semiregularity map in the algebraic context, which has an application to the deformation theory of coherent sheaves on X X admitting a finite locally free resolution. Another application is to the deformations of (Zariski) principal bundles on X X .



Introduction

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf admitting a finite locally free resolution on a smooth variety
X
over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.
The Buchweitz-Flenner semiregularity map, introduced in
[
8
]

5

\sigma\colon\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to\prod_{q\geq 0}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q}),\qquad\sigma(c)=\operatorname{Tr}(\exp(-\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F}))\circ c),

where
\operatorname{Tr}
denotes the trace maps

\operatorname{Tr}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{i}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{j}_{X})\to H^{i}(X,\Omega^{j}_{X})
for
i,j\geq 0
, and the exponential of the opposite of the Atiyah class
\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F})\in\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1})
is defined via the Yoneda pairing

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{i}_{X})\times\operatorname{Ext}^{j}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{j}_{X})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{i+j}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{i+j}_{X}),\qquad(a,b)\mapsto a\circ b,

\exp(-\operatorname{At}(\mathcal{F}))\in\prod_{q\geq 0}\operatorname{Ext}^{q}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega^{q}_{X}).

We refer to
[
20
]
for a discussion of the role of the Buchweitz-Flenner semiregularity map in deformation theory and of the reason it is more convenient in this setting to consider the modified Buchweitz-Flenner semiregularity map, obtained as follows.
Denote by
\sigma_{q}\colon\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\to H^{q+2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q})
the components of the semiregularity map

for every

denote by

A lifting to an

annihilates all obstructions to deformations of a coherent sheaf admitting a finite locally free resolution. We refer again to

1.
DG-Lie algebroids, connections and extension cocycles

The goal of this section is to define
\mathbb{K}
-linear operators

\nabla\colon\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{L})\to\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1]\otimes\mathcal{L})

connections on the kernel

of the anchor map of a transitive DG-Lie algebroid. A short review of the Thom-Whitney totalisation functor
\operatorname{Tot}
is given. In order to construct a connection, we introduce the notion of simplicial lifting of the identity. The section ends with the definition of the extension cocycle associated to a simplicial lifting of the identity, which generalises the notion of Atiyah cocycle. Different notions of Atiyah classes for DG-Lie algebroids have been considered elsewhere in the literature, see e.g.
[

Let

be a smooth separated scheme of finite type over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero, and let
\Theta_{X},\Omega^{1}_{X}
denote its tangent and cotangent sheaves respectively. Often it will be useful to consider the cotangent sheaf as a trivial complex of sheaves concentrated in degree one, so as to have an inclusion

\Omega^{1}_{X}[-1]\to\Omega_{X}^{*}

\Omega_{X}^{*}=\oplus_{p}\Omega_{X}^{p}[-p]

Definition 1.1
.

A DG-Lie algebroid over
X
is a complex of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

\mathcal{A}
equipped with a
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear bracket

, which defines a
DG-Lie algebra structure on the spaces of sections, and with a morphism
of complexes of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\to\Theta_{X}
, called the anchor map, such that the induced
map on the spaces of sections is a homomorphism of DG-Lie
algebras. Moreover for any sections
a_{1},a_{2}
of
\mathcal{A}
and
f
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the following
Leibniz identity holds:

[a_{1},fa_{2}]=f[a_{1},a_{2}]+\rho(a_{1})(f)a_{2}.

Example 1.2
.

The sheaf
\Theta_{X}
is a trivial example of a DG-Lie algebroid concentrated in degree zero, with anchor map given by the identity.

\mathbb{K}
. Every sheaf of DG-Lie algebras over

can be considered as a DG-Lie algebroid over

Example 1.6
.

2.

L_{\infty}
morphisms

This section describes cyclic forms on DG-Lie algebroids and illustrates how DG-Lie algebroid representations give rise to cyclic forms. We then discuss induced
cyclic forms on the Thom-Whitney totalisation and the property of
d_{\operatorname{Tot}}
-closure.
The central result is the construction of a
L_{\infty}
morphism associated to a connection and to a
d_{\operatorname{Tot}}
-closed cyclic form for a transitive DG-Lie algebroid.
This allows us to state the results of
[
20
]
for a coherent sheaf admitting a finite locally free resolution on a smooth separated scheme of finite type over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a DG-Lie algebroid over a smooth separated scheme
X
of finite type over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero, with anchor map
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\to\Theta_{X}
. Assume that the kernel of the anchor map
\mathcal{L}
is a finite complex of locally free sheaves. Notice that for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in\mathcal{L}
, the bracket

[a,x]
belongs to
\mathcal{L}
:

\rho([a,x])=[\rho(a),\rho(x)]=0.

Definition 2.1
.

A cyclic bilinear form on a DG-Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},\rho)
is a graded symmetric
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-bilinear product of degree zero on
\mathcal{L}=\operatorname{Ker}\rho

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

such that for all sections
x,y
of
\mathcal{L}
and
a
of
\mathcal{A}

\langle[a,x],y\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\langle x,[a,y]\rangle=\rho(a)(\langle x,y\rangle).

Notice that the definition implies that for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{L}

\langle x,[y,z]\rangle=\langle[x,y],z\rangle.

In the following two examples, the cyclicity of the forms will follow from Lemma
2.5
.

Example 2.2
.

An example of cyclic form on
(\mathcal{A},\rho)
is induced by the Killing form. Consider the adjoint representation as a morphism of sheaves of DG-Lie algebras

\operatorname{ad}\colon\mathcal{L}\to{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}),\quad a\mapsto[a,-]

and consider the trace map
\operatorname{Tr}\colon{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
, which is morphism of sheaves of DG-Lie algebras (when considering
\mathcal{O}_{X}
as a trivial sheaf of DG-Lie algebras). Then one can define the form

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{O}_{X},\quad(x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}x\operatorname{ad}y).

.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any DG-Lie algebroid representation
\theta\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{E})
the induced form
\langle-,-\rangle_{\theta}\colon\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is cyclic.

Proof.

For
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
x,y\in\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\langle[a,x],y\rangle_{\theta}+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\langle x,[a,y]\rangle_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\theta([a,x])\theta(y)+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\theta(x)\theta([a,y]))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}([\theta(a),\theta(x)]\theta(y)+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\theta(x)[\theta(a),\theta(y)])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}(\theta(a)\theta(x)\theta(y)-(-1)^{\overline{a}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}\theta(x)\theta(y)\theta(a))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}([\theta(a),\theta(x)\theta(y)]).

Notice that if
\overline{a}\neq 0
then
a
belongs to
\mathcal{L}
, so that
\theta(a)
belongs to
{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
and it is clear that
\operatorname{Tr}([\theta(a),\theta(x)\theta(y)])=0
, by the properties of the trace map.

The only remaining non-trivial case is when
\overline{a}=\overline{x}+\overline{y}=0
. Let
\{e_{i}^{k}\}
with
i=1,\cdots,n_{k}
be a local basis of
\mathcal{L}^{k}
and let

\theta(a)(e_{i}^{k})=\sum_{j}A^{k}_{ij}e_{j}^{k},\quad\theta(x)\theta(y)(e_{i}^{k})=\sum_{j}B^{k}_{ij}e_{j}^{k},\quad A^{k}_{ij},B^{k}_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}_{X},

then

\displaystyle[\theta(a),\theta(x)\theta(y)](e_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle=\theta(a)\theta(x)\theta(y)(e_{i}^{k})-\theta(x)\theta(y)\theta(a)(e_{i}^{k})

\displaystyle=\theta(a)\big(\sum_{j}B^{k}_{ij}e_{j}^{k}\big)-\theta(x)\theta(y)\big(\sum_{j}A^{k}_{ij}e_{j}^{k}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}B^{k}_{ij}\theta(a)(e_{j}^{k})+\sum_{j}(\alpha\circ\theta)(a)(B^{k}_{ij})e_{j}^{k}-\sum_{j,s}A^{k}_{ij}B^{k}_{js}e_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,s}B^{k}_{ij}A^{k}_{js}e_{s}^{k}+\sum_{j}\rho(a)(B^{k}_{ij})e_{j}^{k}-\sum_{j,s}A^{k}_{ij}B^{k}_{js}e_{s}^{k}.

The trace of
[\theta(a),\theta(x)\theta(y)]
is hence equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\big(\sum_{j,i}B^{k}_{ij}A^{k}_{ji}+\sum_{i}\rho(a)(B^{k}_{ii})-\sum_{j,i}A^{k}_{ij}B^{k}_{ji}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,i}(-1)^{k}\rho(a)(B^{k}_{ii})=

\displaystyle\rho(a)\big(\sum_{k,i}(-1)^{k}B_{ii}^{k}\big)=\rho(a)\operatorname{Tr}(\theta(x)\theta(y))

\displaystyle=\rho(a)(\langle x,y\rangle_{\theta}).

∎

For every

, let
\Omega_{X}^{i}[-i]
denote the sheaf

seen as a trivial complex concentrated in degree

Any cyclic form

can be extended to a collection of

-bilinear forms

according to the Koszul sign rule, by setting for

,

, an

\langle\omega\otimes x,\eta\otimes y\rangle=(-1)^{\overline{x}j}\omega\wedge\eta\langle x,y\rangle.

It is immediate to see that this form is cyclic in the sense that

\langle[b,x],y\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{b}\overline{x}}\langle x,[b,y]\rangle=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\rho)(b)(\langle x,y\rangle)\quad\forall b\in\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1]\otimes\mathcal{A},\ \ \forall x,y\in\mathcal{L},

where the bracket is the one of Lemma

, and the anchor map has been extended to

by setting

Definition 2.6
.

A cyclic form
\langle-,-\rangle\colon\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

-closed
if for all

\langle dz,w\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{z}}\langle z,dw\rangle=0.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any DG-Lie algebroid representation

the induced cyclic form

-closed.

Proof.

Since

\theta(dx)=d_{{{\mathcal{H}}om}^{*}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})}(\theta(x))=[d_{\mathcal{E}},\theta(x)].

For

\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\langle dx,y\rangle_{\theta}+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\langle x,dy\rangle_{\theta}=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\theta(dx)\theta(y)+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\theta(x)\theta(dy))=\operatorname{Tr}([d_{\mathcal{E}},\theta(x)]\theta(y)+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\theta(x)[d_{\mathcal{E}},\theta(y)])=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(d_{\mathcal{E}}\theta(x)\theta(y)-(-1)^{\overline{x}+\overline{y}}\theta(x)\theta(y)d_{\mathcal{E}})=\operatorname{Tr}([d_{\mathcal{E}},\theta(x)\theta(y)])=0.

∎

Lemma 2.8

The form induced on the totalisation by a cyclic form

Moreover if the form

d
-closed (Definition

), then for the induced form, for

Proof.

For the first item, since everything is defined componentwise, it suffices to prove that for every

\displaystyle\langle[a,x],y\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\langle x,[a,y]\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle[\omega_{n}\otimes z_{n},\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n}],\phi_{n}\otimes y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\langle\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n},[\omega_{n}\otimes z_{n},\phi_{n}\otimes y_{n}]\rangle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\overline{\eta_{n}}\ \overline{z_{n}}}\langle\omega_{n}\eta_{n}\otimes[z_{n},x_{n}],\phi_{n}\otimes y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a}\ \overline{x}}\langle\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n},(-1)^{\overline{\phi_{n}}\overline{z_{n}}}\omega_{n}\phi_{n}\otimes[z_{n},y_{n}]\rangle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\overline{\phi_{n}}(\overline{z_{n}}+\overline{x_{n}})+\overline{\eta_{n}}\ \overline{z_{n}}}\big(\omega_{n}\eta_{n}\phi_{n}\langle[z_{n},x_{n}],y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{\omega_{n}}\ \overline{\eta_{n}}+\overline{z_{n}}\ \overline{x_{n}}}\eta_{n}\omega_{n}\phi_{n}\langle x_{n},[z_{n},y_{n}]\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\overline{\phi_{n}}(\overline{z_{n}}+\overline{x_{n}})+\overline{\eta_{n}}\ \overline{z_{n}}}\omega_{n}\eta_{n}\phi_{n}(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\rho)(z_{n})(\langle x_{n},y_{n}\rangle)=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\rho)(a)(\langle x,y\rangle).

For the second item, recall that

is the differential on

when considering

for

and

\displaystyle\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}(\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n}),\omega_{n}\otimes y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x_{n}}+\overline{\eta_{n}}}\langle\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n},d_{\operatorname{Tot}}(\omega_{n}\otimes y_{n})\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle d_{A_{n}}\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n}+(-1)^{\overline{\eta_{n}}}\eta_{n}\otimes dx_{n},\omega_{n}\otimes y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x_{n}}+\overline{\eta_{n}}}\langle\eta_{n}\otimes x_{n},d_{A_{n}}\omega_{n}\otimes y_{n}+(-1)^{\overline{\omega_{n}}}\omega_{n}\otimes dy_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\overline{\omega_{n}}\ \overline{x_{n}}}d_{A_{n}}(\eta_{n})\omega_{n}\langle x_{n},y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{\eta_{n}}+\overline{\omega_{n}}(\overline{x_{n}}+1)}\eta_{n}\omega_{n}\langle dx_{n},y_{n}\rangle+

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\overline{x_{n}}+\overline{\eta_{n}}+(\overline{\omega_{n}}+1)\overline{x_{n}}}\eta_{n}d_{A_{n}}(\omega_{n})\langle x_{n},y_{n}\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x_{n}}+\overline{\eta_{n}}+\overline{\omega_{n}}+\overline{\omega_{n}}\ \overline{x_{n}}}\eta_{n}\omega_{n}\langle x_{n},dy_{n}\rangle

where

denotes the differential on

, the differential graded algebra of polynomial differential forms on the affine

The next part is dedicated to defining an

morphism associated to a connection and to a
d_{\operatorname{Tot}}
-closed cyclic form on a transitive DG-Lie algebroid.
We assume that the reader is familiar with the notions and basic properties of DG-Lie algebras and

L_{\infty}
morphisms between them; details can be found in
[
14
,
19
,
23
,
25
]
and in the references therein. The definition of an

L_{\infty}
morphism between a DG-Lie algebra and an abelian DG-Lie algebra, i.e., a DG-Lie algebra with trivial bracket is recalled here, because it will be
needed for explicit calculations.

Let
V
be a graded vector space over a field of characteristic 0. Let
\sigma
be a permutation of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
be
homogeneous vectors of
V
: denote by

\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=\pm 1
the antisymmetric Koszul sign, defined by the relation

v_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\sigma(n)}=\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\,v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\,

in the
n
th exterior power
V^{\wedge n}
. If the vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
are clear from the context we will write
\chi(\sigma)
instead of

\chi(\sigma;v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
. Given two non-negative integers
p
and
q
,
S(p,q)
denotes the set of
(p,q)
-shuffles, the permutations
\sigma
of the set
\{1,\cdots,p+q\}
such that

\sigma(1)<\sigma(2)<\cdots<\sigma(p);\quad\sigma(p+1)<\cdots<\sigma(p+q).

Recall that the cardinality of
S(p,q)
is
\binom{p+q}{p}
.
Because of the universal property of wedge powers, every linear map

V^{\wedge p}\to W
will be interpreted as a graded skew-symmetric
p
-linear map
V\times\cdots\times V\to W
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(V,\delta,[-,-])
be a DG-Lie algebra and
(M,d)
an abelian DG-Lie algebra. An
L_{\infty}
morphism
g\colon V\to M
is a sequence of maps

g_{n}\colon V^{\wedge n}\to M
,
n\geq 1
, with
g_{n}
of degree
1-n
such that, for every
n
and every

v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V
homogeneous, the following conditions
C_{i}
are satisfied for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
:

(C_{1}):\quad dg_{1}(v_{1})=g_{1}(\delta v_{1}),

\begin{split}(C_{n}):\quad dg_{n}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})&=(-1)^{1-n}\sum_{\sigma\in S(1,n-1)}\chi(\sigma)g_{n}(\delta v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)})\\
&\quad+(-1)^{2-n}\sum_{\sigma\in S(2,n-2)}\chi(\sigma)g_{n-1}([v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)}],v_{\sigma(3)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}).\end{split}

Remark 2.11
.

Notice that condition
C_{1}
entails that the linear component
g_{1}
induces a map in cohomology
g_{1}\colon H^{*}(V)\to H^{*}(M)
. It is clear that the cohomology
H^{*}(M)
of an abelian DG-Lie algebra
M
is an abelian graded Lie algebra. Condition
C_{2}
can be written as

g_{1}([v_{1},v_{2}])=dg_{2}(v_{1},v_{2})+g_{2}(\delta v_{1},v_{2})+(-1)^{\overline{v_{1}}}g_{2}(v_{1},\delta v_{2}),

which implies that the map induced by
g_{1}
in cohomology is a morphism of graded Lie algebras.

Recall that since the functor
\operatorname{Tot}
sends semicosimplicial DG-Lie algebras to DG-Lie algebras, the complex
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{L})
is a DG-Lie algebra. The complex of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{d_{dR}}\Omega_{X}^{1}
can be considered as a sheaf of abelian DG-Lie algebras, and hence it gives rise to a semicosimplicial abelian DG-Lie algebra; therefore the complex
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])
is an abelian DG-Lie algebra.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\rho)
be a transitive DG-Lie algebroid over a smooth separated scheme

of finite type over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. Let
\mathcal{L}=\operatorname{Ker}\rho

,
i,j\geq 0
, be a cyclic form which is
d_{\operatorname{Tot}}
-closed.
For every simplicial lifting of the identity

there exists a

morphism between DG-Lie algebras on the field

f\colon\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{L})\rightsquigarrow\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])

with components

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle u,x\rangle,

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle\nabla(x),y\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle\nabla(y),x\rangle\big),

\displaystyle f_{3}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle x,[y,z]\rangle,

\displaystyle f_{n}

\displaystyle=0\ \forall n\geq 4,

denotes the connection associated to the simplicial lifting of the identity
D
, and
u=d_{\operatorname{Tot}}D
its extension cocycle.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof it to check that the conditions
C_{i}
of Definition
2.10
hold for

f_{n}=0
for

Denote by
d_{\operatorname{Tot}}
the differential on
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{L})

the differential on
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])

Condition
C_{1}
requires that

notice however that since

\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1]\otimes\mathcal{L})
,
(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}+d_{dR})f_{1}=d_{\operatorname{Tot}}f_{1}
in
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{U},\Omega_{X}^{\leq 1}[2])

d_{\operatorname{Tot}}
-closure of the cyclic form and by the fact that

f_{1}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x)=\langle u,d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x\rangle=(-1)^{\overline{u}}d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\langle u,x\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{u}}\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}u,x\rangle=d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\langle u,x\rangle=d_{\operatorname{Tot}}f_{1}(x).

For
n=2
the condition is

(C_{2}):\quad f_{2}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,y)+(-1)^{\overline{x}}f_{2}(x,d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y)=f_{1}([x,y])-(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}+d_{dR})f_{2}(x,y).

By definition of
f_{2}
we have again that
(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}+d_{dR})f_{2}=d_{\operatorname{Tot}}f_{2}
, and then, using Lemma
1.14
,

\displaystyle f_{2}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,y)+(-1)^{\overline{x}}f_{2}(x,d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle\nabla(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x),y\rangle-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+1)\overline{y}}\langle\nabla(y),d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\langle\nabla(x),d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle\nabla(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y),x\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(-\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\nabla(x),y\rangle+\langle[u,x],y\rangle-(-1)^{(\overline{x}+1)\overline{y}}\langle\nabla(y),d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\langle\nabla(x),d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y\rangle+

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\nabla(y),x\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle[u,y],x\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle u,[x,y]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle u,[y,x]\rangle-d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\langle\nabla(x),y\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\langle\nabla(y),x\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle\langle u,[x,y]\rangle-\frac{1}{2}d_{\operatorname{Tot}}(\langle\nabla(x),y\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle\nabla(y),x\rangle)=f_{1}([x,y])-d_{\operatorname{Tot}}f_{2}(x,y).

is the following

\displaystyle(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}+d_{dR})f_{3}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=f_{3}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,y,z)-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}f_{3}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y,x,z)+(-1)^{\overline{z}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}f_{3}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}z,x,y)+

\displaystyle-f_{2}([x,y],z)+(-1)^{\overline{y}\ \overline{z}}f_{2}([x,z],y)-(-1)^{\overline{x}(\overline{y}+\overline{z})}f_{2}([y,z],x),

and we begin by noting that by the

-closure

\displaystyle f_{3}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,y,z)-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}f_{3}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y,x,z)+(-1)^{\overline{z}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}f_{3}(d_{\operatorname{Tot}}z,x,y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,[y,z]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y,[x,z]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{z}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}z,[x,y]\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,[y,z]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle[d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y,x],z\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}+\overline{y}}\langle[x,y],d_{\operatorname{Tot}}z\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,[y,z]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\langle[x,d_{\operatorname{Tot}}y],z\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}+\overline{y}}\langle x,[y,d_{\operatorname{Tot}}z]\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle d_{\operatorname{Tot}}x,[y,z]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\langle x,d_{\operatorname{Tot}}[y,z]\rangle\big)=-\frac{1}{2}d_{\operatorname{Tot}}\langle x,[y,z]\rangle=d_{\operatorname{Tot}}f_{3}(x,y,z).

On the other hand, by Corollary
2.9

\displaystyle-f_{2}([x,y],z)+(-1)^{\overline{y}\ \overline{z}}f_{2}([x,z],y)-(-1)^{\overline{x}(\overline{y}+\overline{z})}f_{2}([y,z],x)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle\nabla([x,y]),z\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{z}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}\langle\nabla(z),[x,y]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{y}\ \overline{z}}\langle\nabla([x,z]),y\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle\nabla(y),[x,z]\rangle+

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{\overline{x}(\overline{y}+\overline{z})}\langle\nabla([y,z]),x\rangle-\langle\nabla(x),[y,z]\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle[\nabla(x),y],z\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}}\langle[x,\nabla(y)],z\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{z}(\overline{x}+\overline{y})}\langle\nabla(z),[x,y]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{y}\ \overline{z}}\langle[\nabla(x),z],y\rangle+

\displaystyle\qquad-(-1)^{\overline{y}\ \overline{z}+\overline{x}}\langle[x,\nabla(z)],y\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}\ \overline{y}}\langle\nabla(y),[x,z]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{x}(\overline{y}+\overline{z})}\langle[\nabla(y),z],x\rangle+

Lastly, condition

is

\displaystyle f_{3}([a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3},a_{4})-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}f_{3}([a_{1},a_{3}],a_{2},a_{4})+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}f_{3}([a_{1},a_{4}],a_{2},a_{3})

By the graded Jacobi identity we have that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle[a_{1},a_{2}],[a_{3},a_{4}]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\langle[a_{1},a_{3}],[a_{2},a_{4}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle[a_{1},a_{4}],[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle+

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{\overline{a_{1}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle[a_{2},a_{3}],[a_{1},a_{4}]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{3}}\ \overline{a_{4}}+\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{1}}\ \overline{a_{4}}}\langle[a_{2},a_{4}],[a_{1},a_{3}]\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{(\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{a_{2}})(\overline{a_{3}}+\overline{a_{4}})}\langle[a_{3},a_{4}],[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle\big)=

\displaystyle-(\langle[a_{1},a_{2}],[a_{3},a_{4}]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\langle[a_{1},a_{3}],[a_{2},a_{4}]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle[a_{1},a_{4}],[a_{2},a_{3}]\rangle)=

\displaystyle-(\langle a_{1},[a_{2},[a_{3},a_{4}]]\rangle-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}\langle a_{1},[a_{3},[a_{2},a_{4}]]\rangle+(-1)^{\overline{a_{4}}(\overline{a_{2}}+\overline{a_{3}})}\langle a_{1},[a_{4},[a_{2},a_{3}]]\rangle)=

\displaystyle-\langle a_{1},[a_{2},[a_{3},a_{4}]]-(-1)^{\overline{a_{2}}\ \overline{a_{3}}}[a_{3},[a_{2},a_{4}]]-[[a_{2},a_{3}],a_{4}]\rangle=0\,.

\{\eta_{k,i}\colon U_{i}\to\mathfrak{g}\ |\ \eta_{k,i}(p)=\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{ij}(p)}\eta_{k,j}(p)\ \ \forall p\in U_{ij}\}.

Then, setting
(\eta_{k,i}\otimes t_{k})(p)=\eta_{k,i}(p)\otimes t_{k}
for every
p\in U_{i}
, the data

\{\eta_{k,i}\otimes t_{k}\colon U_{i}\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\}
is exactly a section of
P\times^{G}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}
there is an isomorphism of groups

\exp(\Gamma(\mathscr{ad}(P))\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})\cong\{\text{automorphisms of the trivial deformation }P\times\operatorname{Spec}A\}.

Proof.

Denote by
G^{0}(A)
the group of morphisms
f\colon\operatorname{Spec}A\to G
such that
f(0)=\operatorname{Id}_{G}
, and recall that there is an isomorphism of groups
\exp(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})\cong G^{0}(A)
(see e.g.
[
30
, Section 10]
). The group structure on
G^{0}(A)
is induced by the group structure on
G
, while
\exp(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
is a group with the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff product.
By Lemma
3.3

\Gamma(\mathscr{ad}(P))\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\cong\Gamma(P\times^{G}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})),

so that we can work with
\exp(\Gamma(P\times^{G}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}))).

Consider the associated bundle
P\times^{G}G^{0}(A)
, induced by the adjoint action of
G
on
G^{0}(A)
; the isomorphism
\exp(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})\cong G^{0}(A)
induces an isomorphism

\exp(\Gamma(P\times^{G}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})))\cong\Gamma(P\times^{G}G^{0}(A))
.
In fact, a section of
P\times^{G}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
is the data of

\{\eta_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\ |\ \eta_{i}(p)=(\operatorname{Ad}_{g_{ij}(p)}\otimes\operatorname{Id})\eta_{j}(p)\ \ \forall p\in U_{ij}\},

and composing with the exponential
\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A}\to G^{0}(A)
we obtain

\{\exp\circ\eta_{i}\colon U_{i}\to G^{0}(A)\ |\ \exp\circ\eta_{i}(p)={g_{ij}(p)}\exp\circ\eta_{j}(p){g_{ij}(p)}^{-1}\ \ \forall p\in U_{ij}\}.

Notice that this data is equivalent to

\{\lambda_{i}\colon U_{i}\times\operatorname{Spec}A\to G\ |\ \lambda_{i}(p,0)=\operatorname{Id}_{G}\ \ \forall p\in U_{i},\ \ \lambda_{i}(p)=g_{ij}(p)\lambda_{j}(p)g_{ij}(p)^{-1}\ \ \forall p\in U_{ij}\},

which is a section of the associated bundle
\operatorname{Ad}(P\times\operatorname{Spec}A)=(P\times\operatorname{Spec}A)\times^{G}G
, where
G
acts on itself by conjugation.

For any
G
-principal bundle
Q
the global sections of the associated bundle
\operatorname{Ad}(Q)=Q\times^{G}G
correspond to bundle automorphisms of
Q
.
Therefore the
\{\lambda_{i}\}
give an element
F\in\operatorname{Gauge}(P\times\operatorname{Spec}A)
, and the condition
\lambda_{i}(p,0)=\operatorname{Id}_{G}
for all
p\in U_{i}
is equivalent to the fact that the automorphism
F
induces the identity when restricted to
P
, so that
F
is an automorphism of the trivial deformation.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}
be an affine open cover of
X
and let
\mathscr{ad}(P)(\mathcal{U})
be the semicosimplicial Lie algebra of Čech cochains:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i}\mathscr{ad}(P)(U_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\prod_{i,j}\mathscr{ad}(P)(U_{ij})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\prod_{i,j,k}\mathscr{ad}(P)(U_{ijk})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots.}

There is a natural isomorphism of functors
H^{1}_{\mathscr{ad}(P)(\mathcal{U})}\to\operatorname{Def}_{P}
.

Proof.

Recall that all deformations of
P
on an affine open set are trivial, as mentioned above.
Fix
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}
; by Lemma
3.4
an element
f
of

is the data for every
U_{ij}
of isomorphisms
f_{ij}\colon P|_{U_{ij}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A\to P|_{U_{ij}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A
, which restrict to the identity
P|_{U_{ij}}\to P|_{U_{ij}}
and such that
f_{ik}=f_{ij}f_{jk}
for all
i,j,k
.

The last condition means that the
\{f_{ij}\}
glue to obtain a principal
G
-bundle
P_{A}\to X\times\operatorname{Spec}A
and isomorphisms
f_{i}\colon P_{A}|_{U_{i}\times\operatorname{Spec}A}\to P|_{U_{i}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A
such that
f_{ij}=f_{i}f_{j}^{-1}
. Such isomorphisms coincide when restricted to

\overline{f_{i}}\colon i^{*}(P_{A}|_{U_{i}\times\operatorname{Spec}A})\to P|_{U_{i}}
and hence glue to an isomorphism of principal bundles
i^{*}(P_{A})\to P
. This means that an element of
Z^{1}_{\mathscr{ad}(P)(\mathcal{U})}(A)
gives a locally trivial deformation of
P
over
A\in\mathbf{Art}_{\mathbb{K}}
.

An element of
\exp(\prod_{i}\mathscr{ad}(P)(U_{i})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
is again by Lemma
3.4
the data, for every
U_{i}
, of automorphisms
\lambda_{i}\colon P|_{U_{i}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A\to P|_{U_{i}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A
which restrict to the identity
P|_{U_{i}}\to P|_{U_{i}}
.
Two elements

of

are equivalent under the action of
\lambda\in\exp(\prod_{i}\mathscr{ad}(P)(U_{i})\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{A})
if and only if
h_{ij}=\lambda_{i}f_{ij}\lambda_{j}^{-1}
for all
i,j
.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{A}|_{U_{i}\times\operatorname{Spec}A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P|_{U_{i}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{A}^{\prime}|_{U_{i}\times\operatorname{Spec}A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P|_{U_{i}}\times\operatorname{Spec}A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{i}}

This can be expressed as
h_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{i}f_{i}=h_{j}^{-1}\lambda_{j}f_{j}
, which means that the
\{\lambda_{i}\}
glue to a bundle isomorphism
\lambda\colon P_{A}\to P^{\prime}_{A}
, where
P_{A}
is the deformation corresponding to
\{f_{ij}\}
, and
P^{\prime}_{A}
to
\{h_{ij}\}
. Since each

restricts to the identity on

is an isomorphism of deformations.
∎

Corollary 3.12

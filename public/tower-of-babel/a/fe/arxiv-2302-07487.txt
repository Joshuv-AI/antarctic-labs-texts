Abstract
. We completely characterize Δ \Delta - and local subexponentialities of positive-half compound Poisson distributions and extend the characterization on two-sided distributions. Moreover, Δ \Delta -subexponentiality of infinitely divisible distributions is characterized with new conditions, and local subexponentiality is newly characterized in the two-sided case. In the process closedness properties of these subexponentialities are derived, particularly for distributions on ℝ \mathbb{R} . Most results are obtained by exploiting monotonic-type assumptions. We apply our results to distributions of supremum of a random work and a randomly stopped iid sum.

Introduction

Studies on the subexponentiality
of infinitely divisible distributions (IDs for abbreviation and ID in the singular form) have been initiated by Embrecht et al.

[
5
]
, where the subexponentiality of one-sided distributions was
completely characterized. Pakes
[
12
]
extended the result into distributions on the real line.
Here the “characterization of subexponentiality” means
the equivalence of three assertions:

(\mathrm{i})
the truncated Lévy measure is subexponential,
(\mathrm{ii})
the corresponding
ID is subexponential, and
(\mathrm{iii})
Lévy measure is long-tailed
and its tail is asymptotically equivalent to that of the corresponding ID.
Since then, under various assumptions and
with several different versions, subexponential properties of IDs
have been investigated for forty years. However, not a few issues remain unsolved
(see the detailed explanation below).

Recently, two monotonic-type conditions: asymptotic to a non-increasing function (a.n.i. for short) and almost decreasing
(al.d. for short) are
found to be useful for characterizing subexponential densities of IDs, particularly in the two-sided case,
see Matsui
[
11
]
. The latter assumption is known to be crucial for analyzing two-sided
subexponential densities, see Foss et al.
[
9
, Section 4.3]
and
Finkelshtein and Tkachov

[
8
]
.

In this paper, we will exploit the two conditions in different versions of subexponentiality and
extend the boundaries of the subexponential characterization on IDs significantly. Firstly
we adopt four different definitions of subexponentiality and characterize their relations (see Section
2
).
In particular we show that these definitions are equivalent under the a.n.i. condition.

Secondly, we focus on
\Delta
- and local subexponentialities and derive properties such as
the closedness in convolution, asymptotic equivalence, factorization and convolution root.
The closedness property has been one of central problems in the literature
(see Section
3
together with references their in).

\Delta
-subexponentiality has been introduced by Asmussen et al.
[
2
]
and is
now applied in a rather wide area, while local subexponentiality by Watanabe and Yamamuro

[
21
]
is of theoretical importance, i.e. it has a close inseparable relation with
\gamma
-
subexponentiality with
\gamma>0
.

Based on the obtained properties in Section
3
,
we characterize
\Delta
- and local
subexponentialities of IDs on the whole real line.

\Delta
-subexponentiality was only partially characterized, and local subexponentiality
was completed only on positive-half IDs and was
not investigated at all in the two-sided case (see the table and explanation below).
We completely characterize
\Delta
- and local subexponentialities on positive-half compound Poisson distributions,
where we found that no additional assumptions are needed. Moreover, with new and more simple assumptions
we characterize these subexponentialities on IDs on
\mathbb{R}
. Specifically, we extend the boundaries of

\Delta
-subexponential characterization from that of the self-decomposable distribution to that of

s
-self-decomposable distribution (Jurek class
[
10
]
). These main results are
stated in Section
4
.

As applications, we characterize
\Delta
-subexponentiality of
the distribution of supremum of a random walk and that of randomly stopped iid sum in Section
5
,
which are closely related with classical ruin theory and queuing theory (
[
9
, Section 5]
).

This gives a good perspective of the results in this paper, and
one could see remaining open problems also.

Suppose that

The last two results are about the closedness under factorization

The negligible distribution includes a compound Poisson distribution.

Needless to say, a random walk and a randomly stopped sum

5.1.
Supremum of a random walk

We are starting with
\Delta
-subexponentiality of
compound negative binomial distributions, which include compound geometric distributions
as a subclass.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
0<\lambda<1
,

\mathbb{R}_{+}

,

(\mathrm{ii})

G\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}
,

(\mathrm{iii})

G\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
and
F_{a}(x+\Delta)\sim a\lambda/(1-\lambda)G(x+\Delta)
.

Next, we define the distribution
\pi
of the supremum of a random walk. Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be iid random variables with the common distribution

\mathbb{R}

Denote by

Define the measure

(0,\infty)
and a quantity

\nu(dx):={\bf 1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1}G^{*n}(dx)

It is well known that

, then

is a compound Poisson distribution on

.
Here,

It is also well known that

(a=1)
in

[

[
1

The distribution

[

]
). We say that
F
is

Proof of Lemma

Obviously the a.n.i. properties imply (

1\leq\frac{\sup_{t\geq x}\alpha(t)}{\alpha(x)}=\sup_{t\geq x}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\beta(x)}\frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x)}\leq\sup_{t\geq x}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\beta(t)}\frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x)}\to 1

as

\alpha(x)
is positive on
[x_{0},\infty)
for some
x_{0}>0
, and

\alpha(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
, there exists
y_{x}\leq x
such that

1\geq\frac{\inf_{x_{0}\leq t\leq x}\alpha(t)}{\alpha(x)}=\inf_{y_{x}\leq t\leq x}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\beta(x)}\frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x)}\geq\inf_{y_{0}\leq t\leq x}\frac{\alpha(t)}{\beta(t)}\frac{\beta(x)}{\alpha(x)},

where
y_{0}\in(x_{0},y_{x}]
is arbitrary.
Letting
x\to\infty
, so that one may let
y_{x}\to\infty
and then
y_{0}\to\infty
, the right converges to
1
and we obtain the result.
It is known that
f\in{\mathcal{L}}
implies that
f(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
(see
[
9
, p.76]
).
Next we see that
F(x+\Delta)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
. But this follows from

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}F(x+nc+\Delta)=1,\quad x>0

together with the Cauchy criterion for summability.
The final result is trivial if we take arbitrary
\Delta
in the previous argument.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Take a sufficiently large
x_{0}>0
such that
\inf_{t\in[x_{0},x]}f(t)\sim f(x)
.
For any

\displaystyle\frac{f(x+y)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sup_{t\geq x+y}f(t)}{\inf_{s\in[x_{0},x]}f(s)}\frac{f(x+y)}{\sup_{t\geq x+y}f(t)}\frac{\inf_{s\in[x_{0},x]}f(s)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{F(x+y+\Delta)}{F(x-c+\Delta)}\frac{f(x+y)}{\sup_{t\geq x+y}f(t)}\frac{\inf_{s\in[x_{0},x]}f(s)}{f(x)}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{f(x+y)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\inf_{t\in[x_{0},x+y]}f(t)}{\sup_{s\geq x}f(s)}\frac{f(x+y)}{\inf_{t\in[x_{0},x+y]}f(t)}\frac{\sup_{s\geq x}f(s)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{F(x+y-c+\Delta)}{F(x+\Delta)}\frac{f(x+y)}{\inf_{t\in[x_{0},x+y]}f(t)}\frac{\sup_{s\geq x}f(s)}{f(x)}.

Now since
F\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
and

, by taking

in both inequalities, we have

1=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{F(x+y+\Delta)}{F(x-c+\Delta)}\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x+y)}{f(x)}\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{F(x+y-c+\Delta)}{F(x+\Delta)}=1.

Thus we have

.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Since the assertion of
{\mathcal{S}}_{loc}
follows from that of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}
, we only prove the result for
{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}
.
It suffices to see that
F_{+}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}\Rightarrow F\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}
. Although
we can use
F_{+}(x+\Delta)\asymp F^{+}(x+\Delta)
together with
[
9
, Lemma 4.20]
for the proof,
we give a direct proof for the latter use. Notice that the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 4.20]
was omitted
by similarity.

We observe that

F_{+}(x)=(F(x)-F(0-))/\overline{F}(0-),

where
F(x-)=\lim_{y\uparrow x}F(y)
, so that
\overline{F}(0-)=1-F(0-)
and

(6.12)

\displaystyle F_{+}^{\ast 2}(x+\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\overline{F}(0-)^{2}}\Big(\int_{[0,x]}F(x+\Delta-y)F(dy)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\int_{(x,x+c]}(F(x+c-y)-F(0-))F(dy)\Big).

The condition
F_{+}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\Delta}
implies that

(6.13)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{+}^{\ast 2}(x+\Delta)}{F_{+}(x+\Delta)}=\frac{F_{+}^{\ast 2}(x+\Delta)\overline{F}(0-)}{F(x+\Delta)}\to 2

as
x\to\infty
. Noticing (
6.12
) we write

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\ast 2}(x+\Delta)}{F(x+\Delta)}

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\infty,\infty)}\frac{F(x+\Delta-y)}{F(x+\Delta)}F(dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{F_{+}^{\ast 2}(x+\Delta)\overline{F}(0-)^{2}}{F(x+\Delta)}+2\int_{(-\infty,0)}\frac{F(x+\Delta-y)}{F(x+\Delta)}F(dy),

where we exploit the identity by Fubini:

(6.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{(x,\infty)}F(x-y)F(dy)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{\{x<y\leq x-s\}}(F\times F)(d(s,y))\\
&=\int_{(-\infty,0)}F(x-s)F(ds)-F(x)F(0-).\end{split}

Since we have (
6.13
), it suffices to show that the second integral term
converges to
2F(0-)
. Since
F\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
,
there exists a function

such that

,

\alpha(x)\to\infty
as

and

is

2\int_{(-\alpha(x),0)}\frac{F(x+\Delta-y)}{F(x+\Delta)}F(dy)\to 2F(0-),\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty,

while by

,

2\int_{(-\infty,-\alpha(x)]}\frac{F(x+\Delta-y)}{F(x+\Delta)}F(dy)\leq 2K\int_{(-\infty,-\alpha(x)]}F(dy)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad x\to\infty.

Now we obtain the desired result.
∎

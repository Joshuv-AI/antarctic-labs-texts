Differential Contracting Homotopy in Higher-Spin
Theory

Abstract
A new efficient approach to the analysis of nonlinear higher-spin equations, that treats democratically auxiliary spinor variables Z A Z_{A} and integration homotopy parameters in the non-linear vertices of the higher-spin theory, is developed. Being most general, the proposed approach is the same time far simpler than those available so far. In particular, it is free from the necessity to use the Schouten identity. Remarkably, the problem of reconstruction of higher-spin vertices is mapped to certain polyhedra cohomology in terms of homotopy parameters themselves. The new scheme provides a powerful tool for the study of higher-order corrections in higher-spin theory and, in particular, its spin-locality. It is illustrated by the analysis of the lower order vertices, reproducing not only the results obtained previously by the shifted homotopy approach but also projectively-compact vertices with the minimal number of derivatives, that were so far unreachable within that scheme.

2.3
Fundamental Ansatz

Being originally designed to capture a most general class of solutions of the HS equations, the
differential homotopy approach turns out to be useful in many more respects as we explain now
starting from the simplest case of the lowest order holomorphic deformation linear in
\eta

As shown in Section

yields expressions of the following remarkable form

2.4
Homology map

Formula (
2.11

{\Omega}
-dependent part is
{\rm d}
-closed,

are anticommuting and hence,

(2.25)

since

This maps the original homological problem in terms of

on the space of the measure factors
\mu(t_{i})
with no reference
to the spinor variables. The great advantage of this formalism is that there is no
need
to use the Schouten identity in practical computations: the only formula where
Schouten identity in the form (
2.25

2.26
).
(The necessity of accounting the Schouten identity in the formalisms available so far was

\mu_{f}=h_{g}\,.

(2.31)

Being analogous to the approach of
[
55

resolved explicitly in terms of the preexponent factors that however resulted in a rather involved

be illustrated in the examples of Section

4
Special properties

Written in terms of differential forms, formula (
2.11
) is invariant under diffeomorphisms
in the homotopy variables
t^{a}\to t^{\prime}{}^{a}(t)
. In most cases such diffeomorphisms affect
the form of
{\Omega}_{\alpha}
(
2.13
) and, hence, the exponent (
2.16
) in (
2.11
). These
can be applied of course, but it does not help just affecting the form of the expression
as a whole.
However, remarkably, the dependence on
all coordinates
\sigma_{i},\beta,\rho
disappears from
{\Omega}^{\alpha}
at
\tau=1
and the dependence
on
\rho

disappears from the exponent in (
2.11
) since

(y_{\alpha}+u_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha})(y^{\alpha}+u^{\alpha}+p^{\alpha}_{+})=0\,.

This has two consequences allowing to perform any change of variables in
t^{a}
at
\tau=1

and in
\rho
at any
\tau
without
affecting the exponent (
2.16
). Let us consider these important cases in more detail.

4.1
\tau=1

Let
t^{a}
denote all homotopy coordinates except for
\tau
. (For simplicity we
discard the sector of antiholomorphic variables.)
Let the measure factor
\mu(\tau,t)
be of the form

\mu(\tau,t,d\tau,dt)=\mathbb{D}(1-\tau)\tilde{\mu}(t,dt)\,.

(4.1)

In that
case, the
t^{a}
-dependent terms drop out from the exponent in (
2.11
) while the

t^{a}
,
dt^{a}
-dependent terms drop out from
{\rm d}{\Omega}^{\alpha}{\rm d}{\Omega}_{\alpha}

with
{\Omega}_{\alpha}
(
2.13
),

\mathbb{D}(1-\tau){\Omega}^{\alpha}=\mathbb{D}(1-\tau)z^{\alpha}\,,\qquad\mathbb{D}(1-\tau){\rm d}{\Omega}^{\alpha}=\mathbb{D}(1-\tau)\theta^{\alpha}\,.

(4.2)

This allows us to make any change of variables
t^{a}
which only affect the
measure
\mu
. Such changes of variables can be done
freely at any stage of the computation.

4.2
\rho

That the dependence on
\rho
drops out from the exponent in (
2.11
)
is the manifestation and further extension of the phenomenon
found in
[
51
,
63
]
at
\beta=0
of the independence
of vertices bilinear in
C
on the uniform shifts

\sigma_{i}\to\sigma_{i}+\lambda\,,\qquad\rho\to\rho+\lambda

(4.3)

in the variables of those references which is equivalent to

\sigma_{i}\to\sigma_{i}\,,\qquad\rho\to\rho+\lambda

(4.4)

in the variables of this paper. In
[
51
,
63
]

the parameter
\rho
was a constant while in this paper it is a variable,
i.e.,
a
coordinate in
{\cal M}
.
Though the dependence on
\rho
drops out from the exponent in (
2.11
),
it contributes to
{\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}
in a very special way.

Indeed, let us set

{\Omega}_{\alpha}(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho)=w_{\alpha}(\tau,\sigma,\beta)-(1-\tau)\rho(y+u+p_{+})_{\alpha}\,,

(4.5)

w_{\alpha}(\tau,\sigma,\beta):=\tau z_{\alpha}-(1-\tau)(p_{\alpha}(\sigma)-\beta v_{\alpha})\,.

(4.6)

Using the differential realization of
p_{j\alpha}
(
2.23
), this yields

f_{\mu}=\!\int_{u^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}{{\cal E}(\Omega)}g_{1}(r_{1})\ldots g_{k}(r_{k})\big|_{r_{l}=0}k\,

(4.7)

with

{{\cal E}(\Omega)}:=\exp i\big(w_{\beta}(y^{\beta}+p^{\beta}_{+}+u^{\beta})+u_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}-\!\sum_{k\geq j>i\geq 1}p_{i\beta}p_{j}^{\beta}\big)\,.

(4.8)

From (
4.5
) it follows that

{\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}={\rm d}w^{2}-2{\rm d}((1-\tau)\rho)(y^{\alpha}+u^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha}){\rm d}w_{\alpha}\,

(4.9)

and, hence,

f_{\mu}=\!\int_{u^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v)\big({\rm d}w^{\alpha}{\rm d}w_{\alpha}+2i{\rm d}((1-\tau)\rho){\rm d}\big){{\cal E}(\Omega)}g_{1}(r_{1})\ldots g_{k}(r_{k})\big|_{r_{l}=0}k\,.

(4.10)

Since any change of variables of the form

\rho\to\rho^{\prime}(\rho,\tilde{t}^{a})\,,\qquad\tilde{t}^{\prime}{}^{a}=\tilde{t}^{a}\,,\qquad\tilde{t}^{a}=(\tau,\sigma_{i},\beta)\,

(4.11)

does not affect the exponent, it can be freely performed only
affecting the form of the factor
\mu{\rm d}{\Omega}^{\alpha}{\rm d}{\Omega}_{\alpha}

to relate seemingly different expressions.
This property will play instrumental role in the analysis of Section
6.3.1

of the form of field equations guaranteeing equivalence of the spinor
and space-time spin-locality according to the
criterion of
[
58
]
.

4.3
\beta

An interesting feature of the
\beta
-dependence of the shifted homotopy procedure
is that it does not affect the lower-order analysis as was
explained in
[
52
]
in terms of certain star-product re-ordering inspired by the analysis of
[
64
,
65
]
. Here we explain it in a somewhat different way
within the approach of this paper.

Consider the
\beta
-dependent part of the Ansatz (
2.11
),

X=\int_{u^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[u_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}+{\Omega}_{\alpha}(y^{\alpha}+p^{\alpha}_{+}+u^{\alpha})]\,,

(4.12)

with
{\Omega}_{\alpha}
(
2.13
) and

\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v)=\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho)d^{2}ud^{2}v\,.

This yields

\displaystyle X=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{u^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[u_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}+((1-\tau)\beta v_{\alpha}+{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha})(u^{\alpha}+y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha})]

(4.13)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\int_{u^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[\lambda(\tau,\beta)u_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}+(1-\tau)\beta v_{\alpha}(y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha})+{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}(u^{\alpha}+y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha})]\,,

where

{\Omega}_{\alpha}=(1-\tau)\beta v_{\alpha}+{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}\,,

(4.14)

{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}:={\Omega}_{\alpha}\big|_{\beta=0}=\tau z_{\alpha}-(1-\tau)(p_{\alpha}(\sigma)+\rho(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha}+u_{\alpha}))\,,

(4.15)

\lambda(\tau,\beta):=1-(1-\tau)\beta\,.

(4.16)

Note that

\lambda>0
in the allowed domain of the variables
\tau
and
\beta
:

\tau\in[0,1]\,,\qquad-\infty<\beta<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda(\tau,\beta)>0\,.

(4.17)

The term linear in
v_{\alpha}
in the exponent can be removed by a change of integration variables

u_{\alpha}\to\tilde{u}_{\alpha}=u_{\alpha}-(1-\lambda^{-1}(\tau,\beta))(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha})\,,

(4.18)

du_{\alpha}\to d\tilde{u}_{\alpha}=du_{\alpha}+d(\lambda^{-1}(\tau,\beta))(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha})\,.

(4.19)

This yields

X=\int_{\tilde{u}{}^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[u_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}\lambda(\tau,\beta)+{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}(u^{\alpha}+\lambda^{-1}(\tau,\beta)(y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha}))]

(4.20)

with

{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}:=\tau z_{\alpha}-(1-\tau)\big(p_{\alpha}(\sigma)+\rho\big(\lambda(\tau,\beta)^{-1}(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha})+u_{\alpha}\big)\big)\,

(4.21)

or, upon the redefinition,

u_{\alpha}=\lambda(\tau,\beta)^{-1}u^{\prime}_{\alpha},

(4.22)

X=\int_{u^{\prime}{}^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho,u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[u^{\prime}_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}+\lambda(\tau,\beta)^{-1}{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}(u^{\prime\alpha}+y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha})]

(4.23)

with

{\Omega}^{0}_{\alpha}:=\tau z_{\alpha}-(1-\tau)\big(p_{\alpha}(\sigma)+\rho\lambda(\tau,\beta)^{-1}(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha}+u^{\prime}_{\alpha})\big)\,,

(4.24)

d\tilde{u}_{\alpha}\to\lambda^{-1}(\tau,\beta)du^{\prime}_{\alpha}+d(\lambda(\tau,\beta)^{-1})u^{\prime}_{\alpha}\,.

(4.25)

Finally, making the rescaling

z_{\alpha}=z_{\alpha}^{\prime}\lambda(\tau,\beta)\,,\qquad\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}{}^{\prime}\lambda(\tau,\beta)\,,\qquad\rho=\rho^{\prime}\lambda(\tau,\beta)\,,

(4.26)

we recast
X
into the form

X=\int_{u^{\prime}{}^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\mu(\tau,\lambda\sigma^{\prime},\beta,\lambda\rho^{\prime},u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[u^{\prime}_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}+{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{0}{}^{\prime}(u^{\prime\alpha}+y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha})]\,,

(4.27)

{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{0}{}^{\prime}:=\tau z_{\alpha}^{\prime}-(1-\tau)\big(p_{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime})+\lambda(\tau,\beta)^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha}+u^{\prime}_{\alpha})\big)\,.

(4.28)

Equivalently, taking into account that the
\rho
-dependent term does not contribute to the
exponent,

X=\int_{u^{\prime}{}^{2}v^{2}\tau\sigma\beta\rho}\mu(\tau,\lambda\sigma^{\prime},\beta,\lambda\rho^{\prime},u,v){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\exp i[u^{\prime}_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}+{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{0}{}^{\prime\prime}(u^{\prime\alpha}+y^{\alpha}+p_{+}^{\alpha})]\,,

(4.29)

{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{0}{}^{\prime\prime}:=\tau z_{\alpha}^{\prime}-(1-\tau)\big(p_{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime})+\rho^{\prime}(u^{\prime}_{\alpha}+y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha})\big)\,.

(4.30)

Since

{\Omega}_{\alpha}^{0}{}^{\prime\prime}
(
4.30
) has the same form as

{\Omega}_{\alpha}
at
\beta=0
,

\beta
can only affect the vertex via the preexponential factor,

{\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}\mu(\tau,\lambda\sigma^{\prime},\beta,\lambda\rho^{\prime},u,v)

with

{\Omega}_{\alpha}=\lambda(\tau,\beta){\Omega}_{\alpha}^{0}{}^{\prime\prime}+(1-\tau)\beta v_{\alpha}-(1-\tau)(1-\lambda(\tau,\beta))\rho^{\prime}(y_{\alpha}+p_{+\alpha}+u^{\prime}_{\alpha})\,.

(4.31)

Let us stress that the exterior algebra formalism underlying the proposed
approach allows us to make any
changes of variables
\tau,\gamma_{i},\rho,\beta
and
z^{\alpha}
.
This is different from the analysis
of
[
52
]
where the rescaling analogous to (
4.26
) was performed with
the factor of
1-\beta
treated as a constant parameter
rather than
1-(1-\tau)\beta
as in this paper. These
rescalings coincide at
\tau=0
and hence give rise to the same physical vertices
at
\tau=0
. However, the approach of this paper allows one to
trace the
\beta
-dependence effects at the intermediate steps as well.

5
Differential homotopy form of HS equations

A remarkable novel feature of the proposed approach is that it treats
democratically space-time coordinates
x^{n}
, auxiliary spinor variables
Z^{A}
,
homotopy parameters
t^{a}
and even coordinates of the non-commutative
star-product space like
U^{A}
and
V^{A}
. This allows us to rewrite the
field equations in the
differential homotopy
form stripping away the integrations
over homotopy parameters
t^{a}
and non-commutative coordinates
U^{A}
,
V^{A}
,
extending the differential in the

Z^{A}
space to that in the
Z^{A},\,t^{a}
,
U^{A}
,
V^{A}
, where
t^{a}
and
U^{A}
,
V^{A}

denote the whole set of the homotopy parameters and noncommutative coordinates, respectively.
For brevity, the dependence on the noncommutative coordinates is implicit
in the rest of this section.

Thus, in addition to the space-time differential

{\rm d}_{x}:=dx^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}\,,

we introduce
total exterior differential

{\rm d}
(
2.1
).

5.1
Differential homotopy HS equations

The transition to the differential homotopy setup with space-time coordinates
being on equal footing with the homotopy parameters demands slight
modification of the form of the HS equations.
The idea is that upon integration
over homotopy parameters one has to recover the original system (
1.4
)-(
1.8
).
For that purpose we let the field
B
contain the homotopy differentials

dt^{a}
and
Z^{A}
-differentials
\theta^{A}
as well as
space-time differentials that enter via the one-form
\omega
. Hence
we set

{\cal B}=\sum_{n\geq 0}B^{(n)}

(5.1)

with
B^{(n)}
containing
n
one-forms
\omega

B^{(n)}=\tilde{B}^{n}\omega^{n}\,.

Analogously
W
is extended by the components being space-time
differential forms of higher degrees.

It should be stressed that the new added fields will enter under
the
{\rm d}
differential and hence will not contribute to the field
equations at
Z^{A}=\theta^{A}=0
upon integration over the homotopy parameters.
Thus, the differential homotopy system that we introduce now is equivalent
to the original integral one (
1.4
)-(
1.8
).

Namely, introducing notation

\qquad\qquad\theta^{A}Z_{A}+{\cal W}:=W+S\,,

(5.2)

where the term
\theta^{A}Z_{A}
is explicitly introduced to incorporate

{\rm d}_{Z}
into
{\rm d}
,
the differential homotopy system of HS equations takes the form

({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d}){\cal B}+[{\cal W}\,,{\cal B}]_{*}\cong 0\,,

(5.3)

({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d}){\cal W}+{\cal W}*{\cal W}\cong i{\cal B}*(\eta\gamma+\bar{\eta}\bar{\gamma})\,,

(5.4)

where the sign
\cong
implies
weak equality
up to terms that do not contribute under
integration.

There are three types of weak terms, that appear in the practical analysis.

Firstly, these are the terms in the space
{\cal V}^{<}
with the number
of the homotopy differentials
dt^{a}
smaller than that of homotopy
parameters to be integrated.

Secondly, these are the terms in the space
{\cal V}^{>}
with the form degree greater
than the number of homotopy
parameters to be integrated. Formally, such terms vanish because their
form degree exceeds that of the volume form. However, it is useful to keep them
because they may become relevant at higher orders after the number of homotopy
parameters (
i.e.,
the dimension of the homotopy space) is increased within the Ansatz of Section
2.3
.

Thirdly, these are
{\rm d}
-exact terms in
{\cal V}^{E}

v={\rm d}\phi\,.

(5.5)

Note that this is equivalent to the
space of
{\rm d}_{t}
-exact terms since the difference
{\rm d}\phi-{\rm d}_{t}\phi={\rm d}_{Z}\phi
is in
{\cal V}^{<}

for
\phi\in{\cal V}^{<}
.

The
\omega
-dependent part of
{\cal B}
now contributes into the

{\rm d}_{x}
sector of the equation on the zero-form
C
that acquires
the form

{\rm d}_{x}C+[\omega\,,C]_{*}-2i{\rm d}(\tilde{B}^{1}\omega)+\ldots=0\,.

(5.6)

Here the additional term with
\tilde{B}^{1}
enters either under the
exterior differential in the homotopy parameters sector, that does
not contribute upon integration, or under

{\rm d}_{Z}
differential which terms must cancel out in the
Z
-independent
(
i.e.,

{\rm d}_{Z}
-cohomological) sector of the dynamical equations.
Thus the modification of the
{\cal B}
sector (
5.1
) does not affect the form
of integrated equations. Nevertheless, it still plays an important role in the
analysis of HS equations within the differential homotopy approach, being
demanded by the formal compatibility of the equations (
5.3
) and
(
5.4
).

5.2
Bulk and boundary terms in the homotopy space

In the practical analysis it is convenient to distinguish between the bulk and boundary
terms in the space of homotopy parameters. The bulk terms are those that are integrated
over the interior of the square formed by the homotopy parameters
0<\tau<1
and

0<\bar{\tau}<1
that stand in front of
z_{\alpha}
or
\bar{z}_{\dot{\alpha}}
in
\Omega
(
2.13
)
or its conjugate.
The boundary terms are those that are
supported by the boundary of the square,
i.e.,

\tau
and/or
\bar{\tau}

equals to 0 or 1 containing a factor of
\mathbb{D}(\tau)
or

\mathbb{D}(\tau-1)
and/or
\mathbb{D}(\bar{\tau})
or

\mathbb{D}(\bar{\tau}-1)
. Note that the cohomological contribution to the dynamical
HS equations is located at
\tau=\bar{\tau}=0
.

For the degenerate square with no homotopy parameters
\tau
and
\bar{\tau}

the corresponding fields are
treated as bulk. These are the HS fields
\omega(Y;K|x)
and

C(Y;K|x)
.

A useful form of the homotopy-differential equations is

[({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d}){\cal B}+[{\cal W}\,,{\cal B}]_{*}]\Big|_{bulk}={\cal V}_{\cal B}\,,

(5.7)

[({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d}){\cal W}+{\cal W}*{\cal W}]\Big|_{bulk}={\cal V}_{{\cal W}}\,,

(5.8)

where
{\cal V}_{\cal B}
and
{\cal V}_{{\cal W}}
are homotopy boundary terms. For instance, the
r.h.s.
of
(
5.4
) is a boundary term because
\gamma
and
\bar{\gamma}
(
1.12
)
are supported at
\tau=1
and
\bar{\tau}=1
, respectively.
In practice we set

{\cal V}_{\cal W}\cong i{\cal B}*(\eta\gamma+\bar{\eta}\bar{\gamma})\,,\qquad{\cal V}_{\cal B}\cong 0\,.

(5.9)

Now we observe that

\gamma
can be represented in the form
(
2.11
) with
p_{i\alpha}=0

\mu=({\rm d}(l(\tau))-\mathbb{D}(\tau))\mathbb{D}(\beta)\mathbb{D}(\rho)\,,

(5.10)

where the first term is
{\rm d}
-exact, while the second one yields a weakly zero result
upon multiplication by
{\rm d}\Omega^{2}
(
2.13
) since
there are no
\sigma_{i}
in that case. Thus, the boundary terms with
\gamma
and
\bar{\gamma}

can be represented in the manifestly
{\rm d}
-exact form modulo weakly zero terms.

As a consequence of the compatibility conditions for the equations
(
5.7
), (
5.8
),

({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d}){\cal V}_{\cal W}+[{\cal W}\,,{\cal V}_{\cal W}]_{*}=0\,,

(5.11)

({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d}){\cal V}_{\cal B}+[{\cal W}\,,{\cal V}_{\cal B}]_{*}=[{\cal V}_{\cal W}\,,{\cal B}]_{*}\,.

(5.12)

Note that
{\cal V}_{\cal W}
(
5.9
) obeys these conditions as a consequence of (
5.7
) and
since
\gamma
and
\bar{\gamma}
are central.

The gauge transformations, that leave invariant equations
(
5.7
), (
5.8
), (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) are

\delta{\cal W}=({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d})\epsilon_{\cal W}+[{\cal W}\,,\epsilon_{\cal W}]_{*}\,,\qquad\delta{\cal B}=[{\cal B}\,,\epsilon_{\cal W}]_{*}\,,

(5.13)

\delta{\cal V}_{\cal W}=[{\cal V}_{\cal W}\,,\epsilon_{\cal W}]_{*}\,,\qquad\delta{\cal V}_{\cal B}=[{\cal V}_{\cal B}\,,\epsilon_{\cal W}]_{*}\,,

(5.14)

\delta{\cal B}=({\rm d}_{x}-2i{\rm d})\epsilon_{\cal B}+[{\cal W}\,,\epsilon_{\cal B}]_{*}\,,\qquad\delta{\cal V}_{B}=[{\cal V}_{\cal W}\,,\epsilon_{\cal B}]\,,

(5.15)

where the
gauge parameters
\epsilon_{\cal W}
and
\epsilon_{\cal B}
are associated
with the fields
{\cal W}
and
{\cal B}
, respectively.
Note that the gauge transformations with the parameters
\epsilon_{\cal B}
are not
present in the original integral formulation of
[
50
]
(
1.4
)-(
1.8
)
where the field
B
is a zero-form. In the differential formulation of this paper with
{\cal B}

being a differential form both in
{\cal M}
and in space-time, the gauge symmetry
parameters
\epsilon_{\cal B}
are responsible for the addition of exact forms, that do not
affect the final integrated result.

6
Lower-order computations

In this section we illustrate the differential homotopy machinery by the lower-order
analysis of the HS equations. Most of the results obtained in this section were reached before
within the shifted homotopy analysis of
[
51
]
with the only important
exception of the reconstruction of
the projectively-compact spin-local form of the HS equations with the minimal number
of space-time derivatives, obtained originally “by hand”
in
[
59
]
. The aim of this section is to illustrate
how the proposed approach works in practice. The higher-order analysis within the
differential homotopy scheme will be presented elsewhere.
Note that in this section we will use the differential version (
2.23
) for the
variables
p_{\alpha}
and
r_{\alpha}
.

6.1
Vacuum

As a vacuum solution to equations (
5.7
), (
5.8
) we choose as usual

[
50
]
(see
[
66
]
for a review)

{\cal B}_{0}=0\,,\qquad{\cal W}_{0}=\omega(Y;K|x)\,,\qquad{\cal V}_{0{\cal B}}=0\,,\qquad{\cal V}_{0{\cal W}}=0\,,

(6.1)

where
\omega(Y;K|x)
describes a flat HS connection that verifies

{\rm d}_{x}\omega(Y;K|x)+\omega(Y;K|x)*\omega(Y;K|x)=0\,.

(6.2)

For spin-two fields
\omega(Y;K|x)
bilinear in
Y^{A}

(
\omega(Y;K|x)=\omega^{AB}(x)Y_{A}Y_{B}
)
these equations describe
AdS_{4}
geometry
in terms of the flat
sp(4)
connection
\omega^{AB}(x)
.

6.2
First order

6.2.1
{\cal B}_{1}

In the lowest order,
{\cal B}_{1}
is a bulk field on the degenerate homotopy square.
Since the latter has no boundary,
equation (
5.7
) on
{\cal B}_{1}
yields

{\cal D}_{x}{\cal B}_{1}-2i{\rm d}{\cal B}_{1}=0\,,

(6.3)

where
{\cal D}_{x}
is the covariant derivative in the adjoint representation of the

{\cal D}_{x}:={\rm d}_{x}+[\omega(Y;K|x)\,,\ldots]_{*}\,.

(6.4)

The part of equation (
6.3
) of degree zero as a space-time form implies that the
dx
-independent
part of
{\cal B}_{1}
is
{\rm d}
-closed. The gauge transformations (
5.15
) allow one to gauge
away the
{\rm d}
-exact part. Choosing
{\cal B}_{1}=C(Y;K|x)
in
{\rm d}
cohomology

H^{0}({\rm d})
the
dx
-dependent part of equation (
6.3
) yields the standard form of the linearized HS equations

[
62
]
,

{\cal D}_{x}C(Y;K|x)=0\,.

(6.5)

6.2.2
S_{1}

S_{1}
determines the first-order corrections to HS equations.
It is a sum of the terms linear in
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}
,

S_{1}=S_{1}^{\eta}+S_{1}^{\bar{\eta}}\,.

(6.6)

Equations (
5.8
), (
5.9
) yield in the lowest order

-2i{\rm d}S_{1}\cong iC*(\eta\gamma+\bar{\eta}\bar{\gamma})\,.

(6.7)

To reproduce the standard linearized
HS equations of
[
62
]
, where the
\eta
deformation terms
contain the zero-form
C(y,\bar{y};K|x)
at
y=0
,
i.e.,

C(0,\bar{y};K|x)
,

has to be free from the
terms

in the exponent at
\tau=0
. (This is because the physical sector of the HS

-independent terms from
{\rm d}_{Z}

\tau=0
, which is
the content of the
Z
-dominance Lemma
[

]
(see also
[
67

Taking into account the form of
\Omega_{\alpha}
(
2.13
)

{\Omega}_{\alpha}(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho):=\tau z_{\alpha}-(1-\tau)(p_{\alpha}\sigma-\beta v_{\alpha}+\rho(y_{\alpha}+p_{\alpha}+u_{\alpha}))\,

(6.8)

(note that

for the first order with a single

{\Omega}_{\alpha}(\tau,\sigma,\beta,\rho)\Big|_{\tau=0}:=-(p_{\alpha}\sigma-\beta v_{\alpha}+\rho(y_{\alpha}+p_{\alpha}+u_{\alpha}))\,.

(6.9)

Hence, the relevant part of the exponent at
\tau=0
takes the form

7
General lessons

Let us highlight some general features of the developed formalism illustrated by the
analysis of the previous section.

7.1
Cancellation of the bulk homotopy terms

As we have seen, the bulk terms in the homotopy space with
\tau\in(0,1)

must cancel out in the final result leaving only the boundary terms located
at
\tau=\bar{\tau}=0
. This fact was known before as the
Z
-dominance Lemma

[
51
]
(see also
[
67
]
).) The novel feature of the
proposed formalism is that now the bulk homotopy terms cancel out pairwise without detailed
computations that in the previously available formalisms demanded essential use
of the Schouten identity. This remarkable simplification is due to the
fundamental Ansatz (
2.11
), (
2.13
) that trivializes the role of the
Schouten identity.

Let us stress that the final result for the dynamical field equations must
belong to
{\rm d}_{Z}
-cohomology in the original integral formulation of Section

1.2
. Indeed, one can see that
{\rm d}_{Z}
of the
l.h.s.
of equations (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) yields zero once
equations (
1.5
), (
1.7
), (
1.8
) have been solved.
This gives a perturbative proof that equations
(
1.4
) and (
1.6
) are
Z
-independent.

In the differential homotopy approach of this paper the analogous statement is that
the
l.h.s.
of equations (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) in the physical sector must be
Z,\theta

independent upon integration over the homotopy space
{\cal M}
, which in turn implies that such terms
must be
{\rm d}
-exact. These however are cancelled by the terms like
W_{1,2}
of Section

6.2.4
or
B_{2\omega}
of Section
6.3.2
. Thus all bulk terms in physical HS equations
have to cancel out while the nontrivial contribution is concentrated at
\tau=\bar{\tau}=0
.
(The terms at
\tau=1
and/or
\bar{\tau}=1
are weakly zero in physical equations.)

7.2
Projectively-compact spin-locality

Generally, spin-locality in the spinor space of
Y
variables does not automatically imply
space-time spin-locality at higher orders. As was explained recently in
[
58
]
, there exists however
a class of projectively-compact spin-local vertices for which this is true.
The characteristic feature of such vertices, which, in fact, contain a minimal possible
number of derivatives
[
59
]
, is that they contain contraction between
HS connection
\omega(Y)
and the
Y
-variables. In terms of vertices this
demands them to contain the contraction
y_{\alpha}p_{\omega}^{\alpha}
or
{\bar{y}}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{p}_{\omega}^{{\dot{\alpha}}}
.
The approach of this paper gives hints how to control the projective compactness
of the resulting spin-local vertices.
To illustrate this point let us show
that the vertices (
6.91
) do indeed share this property.

For simplicity, consider the case of
\beta=0
(
i.e.,

\mu(\beta)=\mathbb{D}(\beta)
) where
the
u,v
integration trivializes. (The analysis for general
\beta

can be done analogously.)
We observe that the factor of
\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{1}+\rho)\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{2}-(1-\rho))

implies that the combinations of the differentials
{\rm d}\sigma_{1}+{\rm d}\rho
and

{\rm d}\sigma_{2}+{\rm d}\rho
drops out from
{\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}
. As a result,
the terms from
{\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}
that survive at
\tau=0
are

\mathbb{D}(\tau)\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{1}+\rho)\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{2}-(1-\rho)){\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}=2\mathbb{D}(\tau)\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{1}+\rho)\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{2}-(1-\rho)){\rm d}\rho{\rm d}\sigma_{\omega}y^{\alpha}p_{\omega\alpha}\,,

(7.1)

which just implies the projective compactness
[
58
]
of the resulting
vertex.
As explained in
[
58
]
, this guarantees equivalence of
space-time and spinor spin-locality in the analysis of the higher-order
interactions, that makes this choice distinguished
for the higher-order analysis.

Thus, the form of

B_{2}
(
6.80
) leads to the field equations containing an overall factor of

y^{\alpha}p_{\omega\alpha}
. Our analysis shows that, more generally, to reach
projective compactness of the vertex of this type at higher orders
the prexponent factors have to be of the form

\prod_{i}\mathbb{D}(\sigma_{C_{i}}+\rho+\ldots)\,

in which case
d\rho
and
d\sigma_{\omega}
will contribute via
{\rm d}{\Omega}^{2}
along with
the factor of
y^{\alpha}p_{\omega\alpha}

what is a proper form of the general projectively-compact vertices in the
proposed formalism. It would be interesting to understand better its relation with the
shift symmetry condition of
[
67
]
.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, a new approach to the analysis of HS equations of
[
50
]

is proposed with the homotopy parameters, that result from the Poincaré resolution of
the differential equations, treated democratically with the space-time coordinates
and non-commutative coordinates of the auxiliary spinor space. The geometric form of the
approach is very suggestive: extra coordinates associated with the homotopy parameters
parameterize compact polyhedra embedded into a multidimensional hypercube.
From that perspective, they are reminiscent of the coordinates of the extra compact spaces like
Calabi-Yau in String Theory,
etc
. Since the original
unfolded approach to HS theory of
[
62
,
50
]
is formulated
entirely in terms of the exterior algebra machinery, the formulation is coordinate independent, allowing
in particular to reformulate the theory in terms of smooth manifolds rather than polyhedra.

The
differential homotopy

approach shares two seemingly conflicting features: it is both far more general and far simpler
than the standard shifted homotopy approach of
[
51
]
. One of its great advantages is that it trivializes the complicated issue of accounting the Schouten identity. The same time,
the proposed approach allowed us to derive HS current interactions in the projectively-compact
spin-local form, that, as shown in
[
58
]
, implies equivalence of the spinor
and space-time spin-locality. (Though this form of HS vertices was obtained by hand
field redefinitions in
[
59
]
, so far it was unreachable by systematic
homotopy approaches.) Moreover, the differential homotopy approach will be used in
[
68
]
to derive
moderately non-local
\eta\bar{\eta}
vertices, establishing some bound on
non-locality of HS gauge theory. This raises two related interesting problems. One is
to compare the level of non-locality of HS theory found in
[
68
]
with that derived by
Sleight and Taronna
[
28
]
from the Klebanov-Polyakov holographic conjecture

[
20
]
. Another one is to
look for better schemes that may further reduce the level of non-locality of HS theory at higher orders.

Another remarkable output of the analysis of this paper is that, as explained in
Section
7.1
, in the process of computation of the terms that contribute to field
equations most of them (so-called homotopy bulk terms) cancel out by using general
HS equations without going into their detailed form.
To apply this mechanism to higher-order computations, where it is anticipated to be
particularly efficient, it is desirable to work out its extension to multilinear
combinations of the elementary fields. We hope to come back to this interesting
problem in the future.

We believe that the results of this paper highlight the beauty and deepness of HS gauge theory
to the extent making its study extremely interesting and exciting. The generality of the proposed approach gives a hope to uncover its relationship with
other powerful tools in field theory like, for instance, the harmonic superspace approach to supersymmetric models
[
70
]

extended recently to HS multiplets

[
71
]
-
[
73
]
.

Let us stress that the differential homotopy approach
tested in this paper within the
AdS_{4}
HS
theory is applicable to other HS models like
3d
HS gauge theory of
[
8
]

and HS gauge theory in any dimension of
[
69
]
. The form of the
equations is still (
5.3
), (
5.4
)
while the procedure is exactly
the same as in the
4d
theory considered in this paper though the master fields
may depend on somewhat different auxiliary variables. Namely, one introduces the homotopy
space
{\cal M}
, strips out integrations and adds additional components of fields that contribute
under the total differential hence not affecting the final integrated results. Since the
extension to other HS models is straightforward we will not discuss it in more detail here.
Analogously, the differential homotopy approach can be applied to the multi-particle Coxeter HS theory
[
14
]
anticipated to shed light on the relation between
HS theory and String Theory, one of the favorite ideas of Lars Brink.

Traveling phase interfaces in viscous forward-backward diffusion equations
Carina Geldhauser, Michael Herrmann, Dirk Janßen

Abstract
The viscous regularization of an ill-posed diffusion equation with bistable nonlinearity predicts a hysteretic behavior of dynamical phase transitions but a complete mathematical understanding of the intricate multiscale evolution is still missing. We shed light on the fine structure of propagating phase boundaries by carefully examining traveling wave solutions in a special case. Assuming a trilinear constitutive relation we characterize all waves that possess a monotone profile and connect the two phases by a single interface of positive width. We further study the two sharp-interface regimes related to either vanishing viscosity or the bilinear limit.



Keywords:

hysteretic phase interfaces, ill-posed diffusion equations,

viscous regularization, traveling waves in piecewise linear systems

Introduction

This work concerns special solutions to the viscous diffusion equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}-\nu^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{t}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}=\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi^{\prime}{\big(u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\big)}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi^{\prime}{\big(u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\big)}\,.

(2)

We are solely interested in the bistable case, in which
\Phi^{\prime}
is the derivative of a double-well potential and admits two increasing (or
stable
) branches that are separated by a decreasing (or
unstable
) one. Such nonlinearities arise in the context of phase transitions while the monostable case has applications in image processing and population dynamics, see for instance
[
Ell85
,
PM90
,
HPO04
,
Pad98
]
. It has been shown in
[
NCP91
]
that the initial value problem to (

and that there exists a plethora of stable and unstable steady states. The limit PDE (
2

forward-backward parabolic equation
. Besides of (
1

13
) and (
14
) below.

From a mathematical point of view it is very natural to study the vanishing viscosity limit
\nu\to 0
of solutions to (
1
), to characterize all possible limit functions, and to understand in which sense they satisfy the nonlinear equation (
2
). Of particular interest is the sharp-interface limit in which there exist finitely many interface curves which separate regions in which
u
takes values in either one of the convex components of
\Phi
. The effective dynamics for
\nu=0
is then governed by a free boundary problem, that complements the bulk diffusion (
2

(
11
)
+
(
12
)
for the nonlinearity (
1.1
).

For the viscous approximation we still have no complete theory for the passage to the limit
\nu\to 0
although there exist important partial results as explained below in greater detail. It has already been observed in
[

,
EP04
]
that the sharp-interface limit of the viscous regularization (

) is quite involved since the interface value of
\Phi^{\prime}{\left({u}\right)}
depends in a hysteretic manner on both the instantaneous state of the system and the current propagation direction of the interface. In particular, in the viscous case we find both standing and moving phase interfaces but numerical simulations as well as heuristic arguments indicate that the dynamics of the latter is rather complicated and related to several time and space scales.

In this paper we prove the existence and uniqueness of monotone traveling waves solutions to (
1

\Phi^{\prime}
is a trilinear function. These special solutions can be viewed as particular instances of moving phase interfaces for

Moreover, numerical simulations as presented below indicate that the moving interfaces that emerge from certain classes of initial data can be approximated by traveling waves.

1.1
Setting and overview of previous results

Simplified setting

To obtain explicit formulas we restrict our analysis to the continuous and piecewise linear constitutive relation

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}{\left({u}\right)}=u-\sgn_{\kappa}{\left({u}\right)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}u+1&\text{for $u\in(-\infty,\,-{\kappa}]$\,,}\\
\displaystyle-\frac{\,1-{\kappa}\,}{{\kappa}}\,u&\text{for $u\in[-{\kappa},\,+{\kappa}]$\,,}\\
u-1&\text{for $u\in[+{\kappa},\,+\infty)$\,,}\\
\end{array}\right.

which involves the additional parameter
0<{\kappa}<1
and is illustrated in Figure
1
. The spinodal region, in which the backward character of the equation manifests, is given by the interval
[-{\kappa},\,-{\kappa}]
, while the regions
(-\infty,\,-{\kappa}]
and
[+{\kappa},\,+\infty)
represent the two phases and are related to forward diffusion. In the limit as
\kappa\to 0
, the spinodal region contracts to the point
u=0
, so
\sgn_{\kappa}
coincides with the usual sign function and
\Phi^{\prime}
becomes bilinear and discontinuous. Piecewise linear functions
\Phi^{\prime}
have already been studied previously in the context of ill-posed diffusion equations since they simplify the nonlinear problem considerably.

Of particular interest are

\displaystyle u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(-\infty,\,-{\kappa}]&\text{for $x\in(-\infty,\,\xi_{-}{\left({t}\right)}]$}\\

for two time-dependent positions

On the sharp-interface limit

Due to numerical simulations and heuristic arguments we expect that smooth single-interface solutions to (
1
)
+
(
1.1
) converge as
\nu\to 0
to a function

which do not enter the spinodal region but possess a single phase interface of vanishing width. This means

\displaystyle\xi{\left({t}\right)}=\xi_{-}{\left({t}\right)}=\xi_{+}{\left({t}\right)}\,,\qquad u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}<-{\kappa}\quad\text{for}\quad x<\xi{\left({t}\right)}\,,\qquad u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}>+{\kappa}\quad\text{for}\quad x>\xi{\left({t}\right)}

and simplifies the bilinear constitutive relation (

\displaystyle p{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}:=\Phi^{\prime}{\big(u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\big)}=u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}-\sgn{\big(x-\xi{\left({t}\right)}\big)}\,.

(9)

Moreover, the formal asymptotic analysis in
[

are discontinuous and continuous at the interface

, respectively, and that the limit dynamics are governed by a free boundary problem with strong hysteresis that permits both standing and moving phase interfaces. The first two ingredients to the limit model are the
bulk diffusion

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}=\partial_{x}^{2}p{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\qquad\text{for}\quad x\neq\xi{\left({t}\right)}

(10)

and the

\displaystyle{\big|\!\big[p{\left({t},\,{\cdot}\right)}\big]\!\big|}_{x=\xi{\left({t}\right)}}=0\,,\qquad\quad{\big|\!\big[u{\left({t},\,{\cdot}\right)}\big]\!\big|}_{x=\xi{\left({t}\right)}}=2\,,\qquad\quad 2\,\dot{\xi}{\left({t}\right)}+{\big|\!\big[\partial_{x}p{\left({t},\,{\cdot}\right)}\big]\!\big|}_{x=\xi{\left({t}\right)}}=0\,,

(11)

where we denote spatial jumps as

\displaystyle{\big|\!\big[p{\left({t},\,{\cdot}\right)}\big]\!\big|}_{x=\xi{\left({t}\right)}}=\lim_{x\searrow\xi{\left({t}\right)}}p{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}-\lim_{x\nearrow\xi{\left({t}\right)}}p{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}.

The bulk diffusion is actually linear since (
9
) ensures
\partial_{x}^{2}p=\partial_{x}^{2}u
outside the interface and the Stefan condition (
11
) ensures that the parabolic PDE (
10
) holds across the interface
x=\xi{\left({t}\right)}
in a distributional sense. The last part of the limiting free boundary problem is the
hysteretic flow rule

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{array}[]{lcccc}\text{(left moving)}&&\dot{\xi}{\left({t}\right)}<0&\quad\Longrightarrow&p{\big({t},\,{\xi{\left({t}\right)}}\big)}=+1-{\kappa}\,,\\
\text{(right moving)}&&\dot{\xi}{\left({t}\right)}>0&\quad\Longrightarrow&p{\big({t},\,{\xi{\left({t}\right)}}\big)}=-1+{\kappa}\,,\\
\text{(standing)}&&p{\big({t},\,{\xi{\left({t}\right)}}\big)}\in(-1+{\kappa},\,+1-{\kappa})&\quad\Longrightarrow&\dot{\xi}{\left({t}\right)}=0\,,\end{array}\end{split}

(12)

which combines three dynamical relations between the current interface value of

and the current interface speed
\dot{\xi}{\left({t}\right)}
. It stipulates that the interface can only move to the left or to the right when
p{\big({t},\,{\xi{\left({t}\right)}}\big)}
attains the corresponding critical value. Moreover, it is equivalent to a certain family of entropy inequalities as discussed in
[
Plo94
,
EP04
]
.

Hysteresis and other models

The dynamics of sharp interfaces depend on the
regularization
of the ill-posed diffusion equation (
2
). Any reasonable limit model combines the bulk diffusion (
10
) with the Stefan condition (
11
) but these equations do not determine the evolution of the free boundary completely and must be complemented by an extra condition. The latter takes into account microscopic details in the vicinity of the interface and differs for distinct regularization.

For the one-dimensional Cahn-Hilliard equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}=\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi^{\prime}{\big(u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\big)}-\nu^{2}\partial_{x}^{4}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}

(14)

which is discussed, among others, in
[
BBMN12
,
BFG06
]
, there is not hysteresis in the limit
\nu\to 0
since the interface value of
p
is uniquely determined by the properties of
\Phi
. More precisely, for even double-well potentials
\Phi
we find
p{\big({t},\,{\xi{\left({t}\right)}}\big)}=0
according to the classical Maxwell construction.

The sharp-interface limits of both the viscous regularization (
1
) and the lattice approximation (
13
), however, are more complicated as they involve the flow rule (
12
). In particular, we expect a strong hysteretic behavior of the phase interface since
p{\left({t},\,{\xi{\left({t}\right)}}\right)}
is not fixed anymore but can alter in time and affect the propagation mode of the interface. More details concerning the interface hysteresis can be found in
[
EP04
,
HH13
,
Jan23
,
HJ23a
]
and the thermodynamical properties of both (
1
) and (
14
) are discussed in
[
DG17
]
.

Discretization

We transform (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}=\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,}\,{\Big(w{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}-\Phi^{\prime}{\big(u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\big)}\Big)}\,,\qquad w{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}-\nu^{2}\,\partial_{x}^{2}w{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}=\Phi^{\prime}{\big(u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}\big)}

1.3
Main results and plan of paper

It seems that traveling wave solutions to the viscous forward-backward diffusion equation (
1
) have not yet been studied. In this paper we characterize all waves that are strictly monotone and connect the two phases by a single phase interface. In particular, each wave enters and leaves the spinodal region exactly once. We restrict our considerations to the trilinear function
\Phi^{\prime}
from (
1.1
) because this allows us to compose the nonlinear wave of the solutions to linear auxiliary ODEs with constant coefficients. Similar traveling waves can be expected to exist for more general bistable nonlinearities but the corresponding analysis is more involved. It requires to characterize the monotonicity of solutions to certain nonlinear differential equations and does not provide explicit formulas.

Our main findings can be summarized as follows.

Main result
.

Let
0<{\kappa}<1
and a negative wave speed be fixed.

1.

For any given
\nu>0
there exists a unique two-parameter family of left-moving and strictly increasing traveling wave solutions to (
1
)
+
(
1.1
) but most of these waves grow for small
\nu
rapidly at
-\infty
.

2.

There exists a subfamily that converges for
\nu\to 0
to a one-parameter family of traveling wave solutions to the hysteretic limit model (
10
)
+
(
11
)
+
(
12
).

Moreover, symmetry arguments imply three similar results for traveling waves that are strictly
decreasing
and/or move with prescribed positive speed.

2
Monotone traveling waves

In this section we study special solutions to the viscous PDE (
1
)
+
(
1.1
) that penetrate both phases and satisfy the traveling wave ansatz

\displaystyle u{\left({t},\,{x}\right)}=U{\left({X}\right)}\,,\qquad X=x+S\,t\,.

(16)

Here, the parameter
S
quantifies the negative speed and the wave profile
U
is supposed to be both continuously differentiable and monotonic. In what follows we assume
S>0
and characterize all solutions with increasing profile
U
, see Figures
5
and
6
for an schematic illustration. However, similar arguments can be applied to
S<0
and/or decreasing functions
U
and provide traveling waves as depicted in Figure
7

Preliminaries and further notations

Each traveling wave with increasing
U
possesses a single phase interface and without loss of generality we can assume that it is centered around
X=0
. We denote the width of this interface by
2\,\Xi
and introduce the three disjoint intervals

\displaystyle I_{-}:=(-\infty,\,-\,\Xi]\,,\qquad I_{0}:=[-\,\Xi,\,+\,\Xi]\,,\qquad I_{+}:=[+\,\Xi,\,+\infty)\,,

(17)

which cover
{\mathbb{R}}
. The intervals
I_{-}
and
I_{+}
represent the regions in which
U
attains values in either one of the two phases
while it is confined to the spinodal interval inside the interface
I_{0}
. Notice, however, that
\Xi
is not known a priori but must be found as part of the solution. Using
\Xi
, the constraints on
U
can be written as

\displaystyle U^{\prime}{\left({X}\right)}\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad X\in I_{-}\cup I_{0}\cup I_{+}\,,\qquad\qquad U{\left({X}\right)}\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-\infty,\,-{\kappa}]&&\text{for $X\in I_{-}$}\\
[-{\kappa},\,+{\kappa}]&&\text{for $X\in I_{0}$}\\
[+{\kappa},\,+\infty)&&\text{for $X\in I_{+}$}\end{array}\right.

and imply
U{\left({\pm\Xi}\right)}=\pm{\kappa}
.

ODE for traveling waves

Thanks to (
1
) and (
1.1
), the traveling wave ansatz (
16
) yields

\displaystyle S\,\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}X}U{\left({X}\right)}-\nu^{2}\,S\,\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\,\mathrm{d}X^{3}}U{\left({X}\right)}=\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}X^{2}}{\Big(U{\left({X}\right)}-\sgn_{\,{\kappa}}{\big(U{\left({X}\right)}\big)}\Big)}.

(21)

Writing
V=\,\mathrm{d}U/\,\mathrm{d}X
, we see that traveling wave solutions to (
1
) fulfill

\displaystyle S\ V{\left({X}\right)}-\nu^{2}\,S\,\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}X^{2}}V{\left({X}\right)}=\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}X}{\Big(V{\left({X}\right)}-\sgn_{\,{\kappa}}^{\prime}{\big(U{\left({X}\right)}\big)}V{\left({X}\right)}\Big)}

(22)

thanks to the chain rule. Here, the coefficient function on the right hand side is piecewise constant in view of (
1.1
) and compactly supported with

\displaystyle\sgn_{\,{\kappa}}^{\prime}{\big(U{\left({X}\right)}\big)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&&\text{for $X\in I_{-}$}\,,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,{\kappa}\,}&&\text{for $X\in I_{0}$}\,,\\
0&&\text{for $X\in I_{+}$}\,.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the derivative of the wave profile must satisfy the consistency relation

\displaystyle V{\left({X}\right)}\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad X\in I_{-}\cup I_{0}\cup I_{+}\,,\qquad\qquad\int\limits_{-\Xi}^{+\Xi}V{\left({X}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}X=2\,{\kappa}\,,

(26)

which ensures the existence of a primitive
U
as in (
2

Exponential rates of the determining ODE

The ODE (
22
) is linear and autonomous on each of the three intervals defined in (
17
) and the general solutions involve the exponential rates

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{\pm}=-\frac{1}{\,2\,\nu^{2}\,S\,}\pm\sqrt{{\left({\frac{1}{\,2\,\nu^{2}\,S\,}}\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,}\,}\,,\qquad\mu_{\pm}=+\frac{1-{\kappa}}{2\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}}\pm\sqrt{{\left({\frac{\,1-{\kappa}\,}{\,2\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}}\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,}\,}\,.

(27)

Here,
{\lambda}_{\pm}
corresponds to both
I_{-}
and
I_{+}
while
\eta_{\pm}
characterize the fundamental solutions inside of the intervals
I_{0}
. These rates satisfy the order relations

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{-}<0<{\lambda}_{+}\,,\qquad\mu_{-}<0<\mu_{+}

(28)

as well as the asymptotic expansions

\displaystyle{\lambda}_{-}=-\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,}-S+O{\left({\nu^{2}}\right)}\,,\qquad{\lambda}_{+}=S+O{\left({\nu^{2}}\right)}

(29)

and

\displaystyle\mu_{-}=-\frac{\,S\,{\kappa}\,}{\,1-{\kappa}\,}+O{\left({\nu^{2}}\right)}\,,\qquad\mu_{+}=\frac{\,1-{\kappa}\,}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}+\frac{S\,{\kappa}}{\,1-{\kappa}\,}+O{\left({\nu^{2}}\right)}\,.

(30)

The subsequent analysis also relies on the following results.

Lemma 1

(sign of auxiliary quantities)
.

The estimates

\displaystyle 0>\tau_{--}:=-{\lambda}_{-}+\mu_{-}-\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}\,,\qquad 0<\tau_{-+}:=-{\lambda}_{-}+\mu_{+}-\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}

(31)

and

\displaystyle 0>\tau_{+-}:=-{\lambda}_{+}+\mu_{-}-\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}\,,\qquad 0>\tau_{++}:=-{\lambda}_{+}+\mu_{+}-\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}

(32)

hold for all
\nu>0
,
S>0
,
0<{\kappa}<1
.

Proof.

Setting
\theta:=1/{\left({2\,\nu\,S\,{\kappa}}\right)}>0
we compute

\displaystyle\nu\,\tau_{--}

\displaystyle=-\,\theta+\sqrt{{\kappa}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}-\sqrt{{\left({1-{\kappa}}\right)}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}\,,

\displaystyle\nu\,\tau_{-+}

\displaystyle=-\,\theta+\sqrt{{\kappa}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}+\sqrt{{\left({1-{\kappa}}\right)}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}\,,

\displaystyle\nu\,\tau_{+-}

\displaystyle=-\,\theta-\sqrt{{\kappa}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}-\sqrt{{\left({1-{\kappa}}\right)}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}\,,

\displaystyle\nu\,\tau_{++}

\displaystyle=-\,\theta-\sqrt{{\kappa}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}+\sqrt{{\left({1-{\kappa}}\right)}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}\,.

The assertion concerning
\tau_{+-}
is trivial while the estimate for
\tau_{-+}
is a consequence of

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\kappa}^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}>{\kappa}\,\theta\,,\qquad\sqrt{(1-{\kappa})^{2}\,\theta^{2}+1\,}>{\left({1-{\kappa}}\right)}\,\theta\,.

The claims on
\tau_{--}
and
\tau_{++}
follow from
\sqrt{\theta^{2}+1\,}<\theta+1
due to monotonicity with respect to
{\kappa}
.
∎

Matching conditions

In order to solve the ODE for the traveling waves, see equation (
22
), we make the ansatz

\displaystyle V{\left({X}\right)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lccr}A_{-}\,\exp{\left({{\lambda}_{-}\,X}\right)}+A_{+}\,\exp{\left({{\lambda}_{+}\,X}\right)}&&\text{for $X\in I_{-}$}\,,\\
B_{-}\,\exp{\left({\mu_{-}\,X}\right)}+B_{+}\,\exp{\left({\mu_{+}\,X}\right)}&&\text{for $X\in I_{0}$}\,,\\
C_{-}\,\exp{\left({{\lambda}_{-}\,X}\right)}+C_{+}\,\exp{\left({{\lambda}_{+}\,X}\right)}&&\text{for $X\in I_{+}$}\,,\end{array}\right.

which combines on each interval the respective independent solutions. Due to the discontinuities in the coefficient function (
2
), traveling waves cannot be smooth on
{\mathbb{R}}
and both sides of (
22
) involve Dirac-type terms located at
X=\pm\Xi
. All singular contributions have to balance and using elementary arguments we deduce that

must be continuous everywhere while the jumps of

and
\sgn_{{\kappa}}^{\prime}{\left({U}\right)}\,V
must equal each other. More specifically, any weak solution to the traveling waves ODE (

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}V{\left({-\Xi-0}\right)}&=&V{\left({-\Xi+0}\right)}\,,\qquad\displaystyle\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}X}V{\left({-\Xi-0}\right)}&=&\displaystyle\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}X}V{\left({-\Xi+0}\right)}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}V{\left({-\Xi+0}\right)}\,,\\
V{\left({+\Xi+0}\right)}&=&V{\left({+\Xi-0}\right)}\,,\qquad\displaystyle\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}X}V{\left({+\Xi+0}\right)}&=&\displaystyle\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}X}V{\left({+\Xi-0}\right)}-\frac{1}{\,\nu^{2}\,S\,{\kappa}\,}V{\left({+\Xi-0}\right)}\,,\end{array}

where the notation
\pm 0
indicates one-sided limits.

Isolating a subfamily of relevant traveling waves

In view of the expansion (

Theorem 3

(family of relevant traveling waves)
.

For any choice of

,

Using the abbreviations

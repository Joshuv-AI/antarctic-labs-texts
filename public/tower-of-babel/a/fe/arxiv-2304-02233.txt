Ericson: An Interactive Open-Domain Conversational Search Agent
Zihao Wang Ali Ahmadvand Jason Ingyu Choi Payam Karisani Eugene Agichtein

Abstract
Open-domain conversational search (ODCS) aims to provide valuable, up-to-date information, while maintaining natural conversations to help users refine and ultimately answer information needs. However, creating an effective and robust ODCS agent is challenging. In this paper, we present a fully functional ODCS system, Ericson , which includes state-of-the-art question answering and information retrieval components, as well as intent inference and dialogue management models for proactive question refinement and recommendations. Our system was stress-tested in the Amazon Alexa Prize, by engaging in live conversations with thousands of Alexa users, thus providing empirical basis for the analysis of the ODCS system in real settings. Our interaction data analysis revealed that accurate intent classification, encouraging user engagement, and careful proactive recommendations contribute most to the users’ satisfaction. Our study further identifies limitations of the existing search techniques, and can serve as a building block for the next generation of ODCS agents.

Introduction

Conversational search is a crucial step towards the dream of truly conversational AI agents. Unlike in traditional Web search or automatic question answering, which typically consists of one-sided interactions of a user making a request/question and the system responding with one or more answers, a conversation could allow the user to better formulate and understand their information needs, and even help them ask the right questions by proactively recommending information relevant to the conversation context.

More specifically, a conversational search system should include at least two capabilities: being able to retrieve information instantly upon users’ requests, and proactively recommend information if the user does not know what to ask; and to maintain the state, and flow, of conversation to allow the user to naturally refine or participate in the search process.

To guide our design of an effective conversational search system, we formulate the following research questions:

•

RQ1: What is the system functionality needed for a satisfactory conversational search agent?

•

RQ2: How is the user satisfaction and engagement affected by the proactiveness and perfomance of individual conversational search system components?

To answer these questions, we developed and incrementally deployed our conversational agent,
Ericson
, which allows us to observe the benefits and drawbacks of our design decisions on real user conversations. Our implementation combined state-of-the-art question-answering and web search components with conversational functionalities to enable both traditional and interactive access to relevant and interesting information.

One benefit of having a system interact with users in the real world is that a number of limitations and directions for improvement are revealed.
Ericson
was deployed as one of the participants in the Amazon Alexa Prize challenge

While our focus was on investigating conversational search, Alexa Prize provided a venue to test our ideas in a realistic setting. Through the challenge, a large amount of interaction data, as well as explicit ratings and free-form feedback from users were collected. Using these data, we report our analysis of the key factors that affect user experience with our system, such as the accuracy of intent classification, and the “aggressiveness” of the system proactivity and information recommendation. To our knowledge, this paper presents the first large-scale study of a fully functional open-domain conversational search system with real users, utilizing state-of-the-art search and question answering components. The system goes beyond previous published attempts at conversational search, and presents extensive analysis of the effects of the system design and component implementation choices on user satisfaction and engagement. Next, we present related work on conversational (search) agents to put our contribution in context.

3.2
Ericson
System Overview

The
Ericson
system is comprised of five main parts.

•

Hierarchical Intent classifier
: infers the intent of the users’ utterance.

•

Dialogue State Manager (DSM)
: controls conversation flow and invokes system components.

•

Information retrieval components
: general and domain-specific question answering and search components.

•

Conversational transition component
: generates and suggests transitions to next topics to discuss.

•

Dialogue logger
: saves detailed interaction information.

Figure 2:
System overview.

An overview of the system is illustrated in Figure
2
. The intent classifier interacts with the dialogue state manager to direct the flow of the conversation. Specifically, the intent classifier tries to classify the users’ intent into corresponding general or specific classes, described in detail below. The Dialogue State Manager (DSM) controls the conversational flow based on conversational logic. The DSM, based on the input from the intent classifier and the current conversational state, guides the conversation to the next topic, or continues the discussion within the current topic. When invoked by the DSM, the information retrieval components retrieve the requested information as well as potential follow-up information, as described in detail below. Similarly, based on the intent classification, the DSM may invokes a conversational transitions component provide the appropriate follow-up related topic recommendations, or offer to transition the conversation to different components or topics. Finally, dialogue logging stores all the information generated throughout the conversation.

Figure 3:
Conversation flow example

An example conversation is illustrated in Figure
3
. When the system is activated by a user, the intent classifier first categories the utterance, "hello", with a class label "Small-Talk". This information is checked once again by dialogue state manager based on the current dialogue state "New Topic". After this process, dialogue state manager finally determines the direction of the conversational flow and sends the utterance to the corresponding retrieval module "Small-Talk". At the end, conversational transition will incorporate the collected information, and generate a response "Hi, how are you?" from the "Small-Talk" component, and suggest a popular follow-up topic, "Celebrity", and compose a final response. As this process continues throughout the conversation, dialogue logging stores all the processed information to maintain the full convesarsation context, for use both during the conversation and for future analysis.

3.3
Hierarchical Intent Classifier

A hierarchical intent classification structure is formed to classify users’ intent, both semantically and grammatically, to categories corresponding to sentiment (yes/no) and specific topics of interests. The structure of the classifier is shown in
4
.

Figure 4:
Hierarchical classifier.

The top level of the classifier determines which retrieval module would best fit to return the relevant information, based on the predicted topic of the utterance and the conversation context, the classifier If the user utterance is determined to contain a specific entity, a second-level, topic-specific intent classifier is invoked, to further refine the intent category of the user. For example, utterances such as “let’s talk about Bruno Mars” may not be classified as “Music”, and related to Music-search component by the general classifier, but may be labeled as a “conversation transition” class. However, by using the entity classifier (using Google Knowledge Graph API), the entity “Bruno Mars” is detected and labeled as “American singer-songwriter”. Using this information, the intent classifier can then re-classify the utterance correctly as a music related request.

Through preliminary log analysis of available conversations and popular historical Alexa requests, 14 intent classes were identified. Of those, two are sentiment classes (Positive and Negative) and 12 are the ones pertinent to topics of interest. For the classifier algorithm itself, the performances of Naive Bayes (NB), GBDT, Maximum Entropy (ME)
[
11
]
, and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
[
6
]
, were compared as candidates. Ultimatately, we chose the Gradient Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT)
[
4
]
as the classifier of choice, due to both high performance and known robustness and interpretability. As features, the classifier used both surface (lexical) features, as well as seminatic features derived from the word2vec
[
10
]
representation of the utterances. Additionally, syntactic patterns corresponding to phrases such as “how to cook X”, and “what is the news about Y” are also used. The lexical, semantic, and syntactic features were combined into a single vector to represent each utterance. As the results in Section
5.1
show, the general top-level intent classifier performs well in most, but not all cases.

A particularly important type of utterances is one involving entities, but is not always correctly categorized by the general classifier, as illustrated in the “Bruno Mars” example above. To address this problem, we defined a second-level classifier that takes advantage of the Google Knwoledge graph to incorporate domain knowledge about important entities. Specifically, the Google Knowledge Graph API is integrated to build the entity classifier. The label returned by the Knowledge Graph is matched against the available domain topics (using text-based cosine similarity). If the match of the entity type and a topic domain is above the minimum threshold, the entity is classified as the coressponding topic. 3.4
Dialogue State Manager(DSM)

The Dialogue State Manager (DSM) plays a critical role in determining the system response. As input, the DSM uses both the intent classifier prediction as well as the conversation context, to better interpret the intent, and can interact with the user to confirm the intent if unclear. The information flow of the dialogue state manager is illustrated in Figure
5
.

Figure 5:
Dialogue State Manager.

There are 14 top-level predefined dialogue states, such as "Greetings", "Info-request", "Movies", "Music" and so on. Within each of the dialogue state, there may be other sub-states within the top level state. As each utterance is categorized by the intent classifier to the appropriate state, the DSM maintans a stack of recent conversation states to enable back-tracking seemlessly.

When users start on a new topic, the dialogue state is only determined by the intent classifier and the state is updated in accordance with the classification result. Once users intend to discuss more details about a topic, pre-defined states predominate and the intent classifier stands by. Details of the discussion will be handled based on specific scenarios in each state, such as available information in a moview reviw site such as “Rotten Tomatoes”. The general classifier is utilized if a user’s intent is out of scope of pre-defined scnarios, and is passed to one of the (general) information retrieval components, described next.

As an example, consider the following (common) scenario. When a user starts to discuss movies, a state variable is set as "Movies", and a context variable stores all the utterances and responses from that user and the system. A user can continue talking about recent popular movies, and information would be retrieved from the cache variable, and at the same time, any pronouns can be translated by co-reference based on information in context variables. At any point, the user can switch to another topic by saying an utterance about something else.

3.5
Information Retrieval Components

Providing relevant and up-to-date information is a top priority of our ODCS system. Our system
Ericson
integrates both real-time, and static sources in the information retrieval process. There are 12 information retrieval components in total, as described below.

•

Small-Talk. The Small-Talk module retrieves information from a static source. It is based on modifications of AIML language templates from Alicebot
[
9
]
. This module supports dialogues in greetings, some factoid questions and simple transitional dialogues.

•

News. The News module is a real-time information retrieval module. It retrieves up-to-date news from variety of mainstream media sources such as Washington Post, CNN and ESPN.

•

Wiki-info. The Wiki-info module is a real-time information retrieval module. It retrieves summarized information from Wikipedia by the Wikipedia API. It is real-time since Wikipedia is a source that always keeps information updated.

•

Weather. The Weather module is real-time information retrieval module. It provides temperature and weather information for a particular city from Yahoo! Weather API.

•

Jokes. The Jokes module is static. It selects random jokes from a database. This component is designed to cheer people up based on users’ negative responses to greetings and entertain users upon requests.

•

LiveQA. The LiveQA module is both static and real-time. The static database is the Yahoo! Webscope Comprehensive Questions and Answers up to 2006. By using similarity matrix,the most similar question is selected and the corresponding answer is returned. Answers for How-To questions are retrieved from the Wikihow source, which is constantly updated.

•

Movies. The Movies module is both static and real-time. It retrieves relevant movie information such as actors, plots and reviews from IMDB database for relatively old movies. Recent box-office movie information is retrieved from Rotten Tomatoes.

•

Music. The Music module is real-time. It retrieves relevant music information such as ranking charts, artists information and music news from Billboard.

•

Opinions. The Opinions module is currently unavailable due to issues of providing personal opinions on sensitive fields such as politics or religion.

•

Food. The Food module is real-time. It retrieves recipe information, such as directions, ingredients and nutrition, from Epicurious.com.

•

Transition. The Transition module is special. This component is not designed as an information retrieval component, but rather a function that directs conversation to a desired component or mechanism, when an utterance is entity related, or a recommendation is proposed. This component is crucial to drive the conversation fluently and consistently.

•

Unrecognized. The Unrecognized module is a state that a given utterance cannot be classified due to speech recognition or severe grammatical errors. A repetition request will be sent to the user.

3.6
Conversational Transition

Conversational transition aims to smooth the conversation flow, as well as progress through the conversation to new topics. It is composed of two functions, “hot topic recommendation” and “reminders” of available topics.

Hot topic recommendation is based on two mechanisms. First, when users mention entities, which are identified by the entity classifier, the system will try to recommend a related topic. For example, after a user finishes talking about Bruno Mars, details about other celebrity information will be suggested if the user agrees to this topic suggestion. A topics reminder is called when a user continuously refuses the recommended topics. System will repeat the available topics and let users choose their desired topic. The "Suggestion" state is designed to indicate this transition state. In this state, recommendation mechanism works together with the intent classifier. The system is expecting positive or negative responses to the recommendations. If the classifier categorizes the utterance to another component rather than positive or negative, conversation transitions to the (other) the identified component.

3.7
Dialogue Logger

The dialogue logger stores all the information generated through the conversation. It includes three functions, action, context, and utility.

•

Action. Action is the function that tracks and stores responses from designated information retrieval components, and eventually returns the best possible answer to users.

•

Context. Context stores all dialogue state related information in variables, required for tracking the progress of conversations. There are three types of variables, state variables, cache variables, and context variables.

State variables are predefined variables to indicate different states of a conversation. Based on different information retrieval components and their capabilities of extending discussions, different states are correspondingly planned. State variables are checked and updated per turn of conversation.
Cache variables store detailed and categorized information of a topic and can be retrieved later for deeper topic discussion.
Context variables store all the utterances and responses in sequence. Context variables can also be retrieved, mainly for the use of context understanding, such as co-reference resolution function to resolve ambiguous pronouns.

Experimental Setup

In this section, details about performance evaluation metrics, users, experimental period, and data collection are provided.

The user-
Ericson
interactions were conducted during the Alexa Prize 2017, with the system as a deployed Alexa skill . Alexa Prize 2017 is a competition, initiated by Amazon to university students, with the objective of developing a socialbot to conduct natural and engaging conversations with humans, in popular topics, such as entertainment, fashion, politics, sports, and technology, while providing direct feedback from users to participants to further improve the socialbot
2
2

2

https://developer.amazon.com/alexaprize/faq
. All socialbots in the competition are promoted to Alexa users, who are encouraged to interact with these socialbots, and at the same time, provide feedback. All socialbots and users are anonymized during the contest, and socialbots are assigned to users randomly for interactions, to ensure the objectivity of ratings. In addition, socialbots are only allowed for topic-wise conversations, and prohibited from collecting personal information to avoid confidentiality violations.

4.1
Performance Evaluation Metrics

4.1.1
Explicit User Ratings

The primary performance metrics are the explicit user ratings to the system. Each user was asked to give a numeric rating score, ranging from 1 to 5 (with 5 meaning “excellent” and 1 meaning “poor”), based on their satisfaction of the conversation, after they finished their interactions with Ericson. The rating scores were collected by the Alexa organizers.

4.1.2
Number of Conversation Turns

Each conversation contains one or more turns. A conversation turn is defined as one utterance from a user and one response from the system. The number of conversation turns was considered a secondary factor in the Alexa Prize competition, but can be a useful tool to analyze the amount of effort, interest, and engagement a user is willing to spend with the system overall, and specific components.

4.1.3
User Engagement

Engagement is defined as the ability to conduct coherent conversations on a specific topic. In Ericson, several components are well developed focusing on deeper discussions about news, foods, and news. Therefore, it is measured by the number of conversation turns conducted within the specific component continuously in a conversation.

4.1.4
System Proactivity

Proactivity is defined as the ability to drive the conversation forward by proposing or reminding of related topics to the current conversation. It is measured from two aspects, the overall ratings and the number of conversation turns with respect to the overall system.

4.2
Users

The users came from two populations: the regular group of Alexa users in the States, and staff members of the Amazon Alexa team. No demographic information about users are provided due to privacy protection.

4.3
Experimental Period

The reported data were collected from June 15th, when the system was relatively stable, to August 30th,

Ericson
was operating live to interact with real users. 46 days of interaction data were collected, as well as user ratings and free-form feedback provided by Amazon.

4.4
Data Collection

The results in this paper are based on over 22,000 sessions, 230,000 turns of conversations. Users were encouraged to interact with the system and provide ratings and free-form feedback once the session ended. The comparison is conducted between before and after 7/8 for the Movies component, 6/23 for both the Food and News component, of which the dates are the time we added engagement respectively. The average number of conversation turns for the Movies component has increased by about 71.0% (from 1.554 to 2.667) after adding the engagement technique, 45.0% (from 1.280 to 1.850) for the Food component, and 50.0% (from 1.830 to 2.745) for the News component. The same tests were conducted for Food and News components, with p-values both <0.001, indicating significant differences. 5.1.3
Proactivity

Whenever an ODCS system conducts a conversation with users, handling situations when users are unsure of the next topic is significant in terms of maintaining the natural conversational flow. In other words, proactive behavior is a reflection of how intelligent an ODCS system is, since smart suggestions can directly influence the interaction quality by helping users to focus on and continue with the overall conversation.

6
Limitations

The main limitation of the reported study was that it was performed in a real-time setting. Thus, some of the system component changes may have potential effects on other components. Unfortunately, due to the limited time frame available for the user study, some of the changes made to classifiers, engagement, and proactivity components were completed within a short time period, thus making it challenging to exclusively attribute ratings to individual component updates. Nevertheless, the effects of major improvements of the intent classifier, engagement, and proactive topic recommendation were significant enough to be credited for the improvements to the overall user experience.

7
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we described an open-domain conversational search agent prototype that has been interacting with a sufficient number of real users. Three significant factors are studied on their effects on the interactions.

Based on our conversation analysis, our initial answer to RQ1 is that an open-domain conversational search system requires two essential functions, information retrieval/question answering, and also careful conversational transition modeling. These two functions meet users’ needs for obtaining desired information while naturally support progressing through a conversation. These two essential functions are organically incorporated featuring three factors, intent classification, engagement, and proactivity.

Our analyses also show that, as an answer to RQ2, the most crucial part of an ODCS system is to have a very accurate user intent detection component and a comprehensive dialogue manager to control the conversation flow. Since our information retrieval components have different mechanisms as well as varying topic coverages and depths, interactions with users can be jeopardized if the classifier or dialogue management fails. We spent substantial efforts on improving our classification system, starting from a simple classifier and leading to a hierarchical classification system, as well as developing carefully designed logical flows for the suggested conversation transitions.

We believe that modeling proactive behavior and enhancing the engagement will further increase the quality of interaction between the user and agent.

To summarize our findings, we have shown that a conversational search system such as the
Ericson
, presented in this paper, could allow users to retrieve information more effectively, and with higher satisfaction, compared to using traditional Web search and recommendations, especially in restricted communication settings such as voice or text. The main reason, we confirmed experimentally, is providing practivity and interaction, which allows the user to more naturally explore and retrieve content of interest through the dialogue with the system instead of putting the burden solely on the user to formulate and query the system.

Our current and future work will focus on improving the conversational engagement and proactivity of
Ericson
to enhance the conversation interactions, as well as adding more in-depth support for additional topical domains.

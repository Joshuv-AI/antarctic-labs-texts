1.
Introduction

Throughout we assume the Riemann Hypothesis (RH).
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)=\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{\log T}+it\right)\right|^{2K}dt.

In an important paper of Goldston, Gonek and Montgomery
[
8
]
, they studied
\mathcal{I}_{1}(a,T)

and find its close connections to Montgomery’s Pair Correlation Conjecture (see Montgomery
[
11
]
) and to the distribution of prime numbers in short intervals (see also Selberg
[
15

) when
a
is of constant size. Generalizations of
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
for larger

have also been studied by Farmer, Gonek, Lee and Lester in
[
5

, in which they related these integrals to higher correlation functions of zeta zeros.

In
[
8
]
Goldston et al. also proved that

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(a,T)\sim\frac{1}{2a}T(\log T)^{2}\quad\text{ as }a=a(T)\to 0,

assuming RH and the Essential Simplicity hypothesis (ES). To state (ES), let
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
be a generic zero of zeta, and let

\displaystyle N^{*}(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma<T}m_{\gamma}

where
m_{\gamma}
is the multiplicity of
\rho
and the sum counts multiplicity of zeros. We also define

\displaystyle N(\beta,T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}<T\\
0<\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}<2\pi\beta/\log T\end{subarray}}1.

Recall that

\displaystyle N(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma<T}1\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T.

Then, the (ES) states that

(ES)

\displaystyle N^{*}(T)\sim\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T,\quad\text{ and }\quad N(\beta,T)=o(T\log T)\text{ for any }\beta=\beta(T)\to 0.

It is widely believed that the circular unitary ensemble (CUE) models the Riemann zeta-function regarding zero statistics, value distribution, and more (for example, see Montgomery
[
11
]
, Keating and Snaith
[
9
]
). Bailey, Bettin, Blower, Conrey, Prokhorov, Rubinstein and Snaith
[
1
]
proved asymptotics for the analogue of
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
when
a\to 0
in the setting of CUE for all positive integers
K
. The expressions in these asymptotics lead them to conjecture that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{(2a)^{2K-1}}{T(\log T)^{2K}}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)=\binom{2K-2}{K-1}.

When
K=1
, this conjecture agrees with the result of Goldston et al (
1
) apart from a small difference in how the limits are taken.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the above conjecture of Bailey et al., assuming RH and a hypothesis which we call the
2K
-tuple Essential Simplicity hypothesis (ES
2K
). To state (ES
2K
), let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},...
be the list of zeta zeros on the upper half plane ordered with heights and counted with multiplicity, and define

N(K,v,T)
to be the number of
2K
-tuples
(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{2K})
of zeta zeros satisfying

•

\lambda_{j}\in[T,2T]
for all

;

•

|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|\leq\frac{v}{\log T}
for all
i,j=1,...,2K
;

•

not all of
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{2K}
have the same label
\rho_{i}
in the list
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},...
of zeta zeros.

(If all the zeros of zeta are simple, then the last condition is the same as saying that not all of
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{2K}
are the same complex number.)
Then, our (ES
2K
) states that

(ES
2K
)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N(K,v,T)}{N(T)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }v\to 0.

Therefore, roughly speaking, this hypothesis states that there are not too many small gaps between zeta zeros. For
K=1
, this assumption is essentially the same as the (ES) assumed in the result of Goldston et al. (
1
), apart from a small difference in how we take the limits. (If we let
v=v(T)
depend on
T
in our assumption as in (ES), our result below modifies accordingly to a generalization of (
1
).) Note also that our (
ES

⁢
2
K

) is consistent with, but much weaker than, the more general GUE hypothesis for
n

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a positive integer, and

. Assume RH and (ES

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{(2a)^{2K-1}}{T(\log T)^{2K}}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{\log T}+it\right)\right|^{2K}dt=\binom{2K-2}{K-1}.

We remark that the method used in proving (
1
) (i.e., the case when
K=1
) in Goldston et al.
[
8
]
does not seem to generalize to
K\geq 2
.
The method in the work of Farmer et al.
[
5
]
could possibly be used to obtain estimates for
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
(and for more general integrals), assuming certain knowledge on the correlation functions of zeta zeros; however, according to
[
5
]
, the calculation seems quite difficult in general. Thus, it is currently not clear whether we could obtain Theorem
1.1
using this method. Another possible approach to studying
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
is via the ratios conjectures for zeta (see Conrey, Farmer and Zirnbauer
[
3
]
, Conrey and Snaith
[
4
]
). Assuming the ratios conjectures, it is possible to obtain estimates (also involving complicated calculation) for
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
for each

is currently unknown; see
[
1
]
for relevant discussion in the CUE case.
On the contrary, our approach here is to directly calculate the leading asymptotics of the integral when
a\to 0
, instead of working on the asymptotics of
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
first for
a
of constant size (which is more important but more difficult) by appealing to its relations to the correlation functions or ratios conjectures. Moreover, our proof reveals transparently where the binomial coefficient in the result comes from.

We also remark that our result shows the limiting asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
when
a\to 0
(as expected in Theorem
1.1
) only requires that there are not too many very small gaps between zeta zeros, but
does not require finer information on the distribution of typical sizes of zero gaps
. Thus, in particular, we expect that the leading term of
\mathcal{I}_{K}(a,T)
as
a\to 0
cannot distinguish the believed distribution of zeta zeros from the Alternative Hypothesis. (See Baluyot
[
2
]
for a discussion of
\mathcal{I}_{1}(a,T)
under the Alternative Hypothesis; see also Ki
[
10
]
for an estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{1}(a,T)
under an assumption on zeros of

[

[

[

Propositions and Proof of Theorem
1.1

Our first proposition, which is Lemma 5 in Radziwiłł

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume RH. Let

. Uniformly in

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)=\sum_{|s-\rho|<\frac{c}{\log T}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+O\left(\frac{\log T}{c}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{T,N}(s)\right)

Thus, roughly speaking, for

From this point on, we let

\displaystyle s=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{\log T}+it

With such

Assume RH and (ES

We use Proposition

Proof of Proposition

The treatment in this section is similar to some moments computations in Radziwiłł

Let

be real numbers, and suppose that

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}e(x_{n}t)\right|^{2}dt\leq 3\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{n}e(x_{n}t)\right|^{2}dt.

Here, as usual,

We also need a familiar result of Soundararajan (Lemma 3 in

Lemma 3.2

Let

be large, and let

. Let

be a natural number such that

. For any complex numbers

where

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{a(p)}{p^{1/2+it}}\right|^{2k}dt\ll k!T\left(\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{|a(p)|^{2}}{p}\right)^{k}.

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
2.2
.

Expanding the
2K
-th power and using Hölder’s inequality repeatedly, we see that it suffices to prove the following bounds:

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\frac{\log T}{\log N}\right|^{2K}dt\ll_{K}T,\quad\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\frac{1}{\log N}\cdot A_{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{\log N}+it\right)\right|^{2K}dt\ll_{K}T,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{ and }\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\frac{1}{\log N}\cdot B_{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2K}dt\ll_{K}T.

The first inequality is clear, since
\frac{\log T}{\log N}\ll_{K}1
. For the second inequality, we first apply the Majorant Principle to the Dirichlet polynomial

and obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left|A_{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{\log N}+it\right)\right|^{2K}dt

\displaystyle\ll\int_{-2T}^{2T}\left|A_{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{\log N}+it\right)\right|^{2K}dt

\displaystyle\ll\int_{-2T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\right|^{2K}dt.

This last integral is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{-2T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{\log p}{p^{1/2+it}}+\sum_{p^{2}\leq N}\frac{\log p}{p^{1+i2t}}+O(1)\right|^{2K}dt,

where we can again expand the power, use Hölder’s inequality, and then apply Lemma

to bound the integral of the

The third inequality is proved in a similar way.
∎

Upper bounds for the relaxed area of 𝕊 1 {\mathbb{S}}^{1} -valued Sobolev maps and its countably subadditive interior envelope
Giovanni Bellettini, Riccardo Scala, Giuseppe Scianna

Abstract
Given a bounded open connected Lipschitz set Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} , we show that the relaxed Cartesian area functional 𝒜 ¯ ​ ( u , Ω ) \overline{\mathcal{A}}(u,\Omega) of a map u ∈ W 1 , 1 ​ ( Ω , 𝕊 1 ) u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1}) is finite, and provide a useful upper bound for its value. Using this estimate, we prove a modified version of a De Giorgi conjecture [ 17 ] adapted to W 1 , 1 ​ ( Ω , 𝕊 1 ) W^{1,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1}) , on the largest countably subadditive set function 𝒜 ¯ ¯ ​ ( u , ⋅ ) \overline{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}(u,\cdot) smaller than or equal to 𝒜 ¯ ​ ( u , ⋅ ) \overline{\mathcal{A}}(u,\cdot) .



Key words:
Plateau problem, relaxation, Cartesian currents, area functional, minimal surfaces,
countably subadditive interior envelope.

49J45, 49Q05, 49Q15, 28A75.

u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
.
Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a finite
set
C_{\epsilon}
of points of

\Omega

such that

\overline{\mathcal{A}}(u,\Omega\setminus C_{\epsilon})\leq\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}dx+\epsilon.

(7.20)

Proof.

We know that
\frac{1}{\pi}\,{\rm Det\ }(\nabla u)=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}(\delta_{x_{i}}-\delta_{y_{i}})

with
\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}|{x_{i}}-{y_{i}}|<+\infty
. Take
N_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}

so that
\sum_{i=N_{\epsilon}+1}^{+\infty}|x_{i}-y_{i}|<\frac{\epsilon}{2\pi}
, and
let
C_{\epsilon}:=\{x_{k}\in\Omega:1\leq k\leq N_{\epsilon}\}\cup\{y_{k}\in\Omega:1\leq k\leq N_{\epsilon}\}
.
Set
T:=\,{\rm Det\ }(\nabla u)\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus C_{\epsilon})
,
so that
\|T\|_{{\rm flat},\alpha,\Omega\setminus C_{\epsilon}}\leq\epsilon
.
Then Theorem
7.4
implies (
7.20
).
∎

Using Theorem
7.5
we can positively answer
to a modification of a
conjecture by De Giorgi
[
17
]
, adapted to the context of

-valued Sobolev maps.

Corollary 7.6
.

Let

.
Then

\overline{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}(u,\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{0}(C)<+\infty\end{subarray}}\overline{\mathcal{A}}(u,\Omega\setminus C).

Proof.

From Theorem

we get

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\subset\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{0}(C)<+\infty\end{subarray}}\overline{\mathcal{A}}(u,\Omega\setminus C)\leq\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}~dx=\overline{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}(u,\Omega),

where the last equality follows from Corollary

On the other hand, for any finite set

we know
[

∎

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\Lambda\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(\Omega)^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)\\
\|\varphi\|_{{\rm Lip}_{0},\alpha}\leq 1\end{subarray}}}\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle=

\displaystyle\inf\Big\{|R|_{\Omega}+\alpha^{-1}|S|_{\Omega}:(R,S)\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{D}_{1}(\Omega),\;\Lambda=R+\partial S\Big\}

(8.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in{\rm Lip}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\varphi\|_{{\rm Lip}_{0},\alpha}\leq 1\end{subarray}}}\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle.

Proof.

We adapt the arguments
of
[
19
, page 367]
.
Let
R\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)
and
S\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(\Omega)

be such that
\Lambda=R+\partial S
in
\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Then, as
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
,

\left|\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle\right|\leq|R(\varphi)|+|S(d\varphi)|\leq(|R|_{\Omega}+2|S|_{\Omega})\|\varphi\|_{{\rm Lip}_{0},\alpha}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),

so the inequality

\leq
holds in the first line of
(
8.1
).
To prove the converse inequality, set

Y:=\left\{(\varphi,\psi)\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)\times C^{0}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}

endowed with the norm
\|(\varphi,\psi)\|_{Y}:=\max\{\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}},\frac{1}{2}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\}
,
and define the linear injective operator

Q:C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)\rightarrow Y,\qquad Q(\varphi):=(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega).

Since

Q(C^{1}_{c}(\Omega))\subset Y
, we have

\langle\Lambda,Q^{-1}(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\rangle\leq\|\Lambda\|_{{\rm flat},\alpha}\|(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\|_{Y}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),

and therefore
we can extend the linear functional
\Lambda\circ Q^{-1}:Q(C^{1}_{c}(\Omega))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to some linear functional
L:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with

L(\varphi,\psi)\leq\|\Lambda\|_{{\rm flat},\alpha}\|(\varphi,\psi)\|_{Y}\qquad\forall(\varphi,\psi)\in Y.

(8.2)

Now we define

\displaystyle R(\varphi):=L(\varphi,0)\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega),

\displaystyle S(\psi):=L(0,\psi)\qquad\forall\psi\in C^{0}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),

so that, from (
8.2
),

\|(\varphi,\psi)\|_{Y}\leq 1
implies
R(\varphi)+S(\psi)=L(\varphi,\psi)\leq\|\Lambda\|_{{\rm flat},\alpha}
.
In particular,
R\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)
,
S\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(\Omega)
, and passing to the supremum,

|R|_{\Omega}+2|S|_{\Omega}\leq\|\Lambda\|_{{\rm flat},\alpha}.

Since
R(\varphi)+S(d\varphi)=L(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)=\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle
for all
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
,
it follows
\Lambda=R+\partial S
, and
the first equality in (
8.1
) follows.

To show the second equality
in (
8.1
), we first observe that, from
the first equality,

\sup_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)\\
\|\varphi\|_{{\rm Lip_{0}},\alpha}\leq 1\end{subarray}}}\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle\leq\|\Lambda\|_{{\rm flat},\alpha},

and so
\leq
holds.
On the other hand, if
R\in\mathcal{\mathcal{}}R_{f}
and
S\in\mathcal{S}

are such that
\Lambda=R+\partial S
in
\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)
, then

\left|\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle\right|=|R(\varphi)|+|S(d\varphi)|\leq(|R|_{\Omega}+2|S|_{\Omega})\|\varphi\|_{{\rm Lip}_{0},\alpha}\qquad\forall\varphi\in\text{{\rm Lip}}_{0}(\Omega),

so also the inequality

\leq
holds, thanks to Corollary
3.4
.
∎

x_{j}\in\partial\Omega
is such that
|x_{j}-y_{k_{1}}|=d(y_{k_{1}},\partial\Omega)
.

Proof of (c): To show this, fix
x\in\partial\Omega
; if
\Psi_{m}(x)=\Psi_{0}(x)=0
there is nothing to prove. If not, we can find a set of
r>0
indices
0=m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}\leq m
, and indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\in I_{D}
such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{m}(x)=\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i}}|-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i+1}}|,

where
y_{k_{r+1}}:=x
. If
\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})=-2d(y_{k_{1}},\partial\Omega)
, we show that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i}}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i+1}}|+d(y_{k_{1}},\partial\Omega).

As usual we might assume that the indices
k_{i}
are distinct; being
x\in\partial\Omega
, we have
|x_{k_{r}}-x|\geq d(x_{r_{k}},\partial\Omega)
, and so the previous inequality is obtained by minimality of
R_{{\rm min}}
and
S_{{\rm min}}
, arguing similarly as in the preceding cases.

If instead
\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})=1-2|x_{h}-y_{k_{1}}|
for some
h\in P^{+}
, we reduce ourselves to prove that

1+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i}}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i+1}}|+|x_{h}-y_{k_{1}}|,

which is again implied by the minimality of
R_{{\rm min}}
and
S_{{\rm min}}
.

Proof of (d):
The first condition in (
8.8
) is a consequence of point (c)
and the fact that
\Psi_{m}\in\text{{\rm Lip}}_{0}(\Omega)
,
with

\|\Psi_{m}\|_{{\rm Lip}_{0},\alpha}\leq 1
. Let us prove the second condition. If
\Psi_{m}(y_{k})=\Psi_{0}(y_{k})
then the thesis follows from (
8.5
); if not, we can find a sequence of
r>0
indices
0=m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}\leq m
, and indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\in I_{D}
such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{m}(y_{k})=\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i}}|-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i+1}}|,

where
k_{r+1}:=k
. Now, either
\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})=-2d(\partial\Omega,y_{k_{1}})
or
\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})=1-2|x_{h}-y_{k_{1}}|
for some
h\in P^{+}
.
Again assuming that the indices
k_{i}
are distinct, in the first case it is
sufficient to observe that

d(\partial\Omega,y_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i}}|\leq d(\partial\Omega,n_{k_{1}})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i+1}}|,

which follows once more by the minimality of
R_{{\rm min}}
and
S_{{\rm min}}
, since
d(\partial\Omega,y_{k})=|x_{k}-y_{k}|
, and
x_{k}\in\partial\Omega
.

Assuming instead that
\Psi_{0}(y_{k_{1}})=1-2|x_{h}-y_{k_{1}}|
for some
h\in P^{+}
, we can show that

1+d(\partial\Omega,y_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i}}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|x_{k_{i}}-y_{k_{i+1}}|+|x_{h}-y_{k_{1}}|,

using once again the minimality of
R_{{\rm min}}
and
S_{{\rm min}}
.

Before passing to the next step, observe that
since
\Psi_{m}\leq\Psi_{m+1}
for all
m\geq 0
, we can take the limit

\varphi:=\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\Psi_{m},

and thanks to the properties of
\Psi_{m}
we easily infer that conditions (a), (b), (c), and (d) are still valid for
\varphi
. Furthermore, since
\Psi_{m}
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
2
, we also have that
\Psi_{m}\rightarrow\varphi
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
.

The next step concludes the proof of the lemma.

Step 3.
The function
\varphi
satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{k})=\varphi(n_{k})+2|x_{k}-y_{k}|\qquad\forall k\in I_{D}.

(8.15)

To see this we define

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}:=\max_{k\in I_{D}}\{\phi_{k}\},\qquad\phi_{k}(x):=\varphi(y_{k})+2|x_{k}-y_{k}|-2|x_{k}-x|,\qquad\forall k\in I_{D},\;\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}.

In order to prove
(
8.15
) we show that
\varphi\geq\overline{\varphi}
;
this implies
that
\varphi(x_{k})\geq\phi_{k}(x_{k})=\varphi(y_{k})+2|x_{k}-y_{k}|

for all
k\in I_{D}
, and since the opposite inequality is guaranteed
due to the fact that
\varphi
is
2
-Lipschitz, (
8.15
) follows.
Now, for all
\epsilon>0
we can find
m_{\epsilon}
so that
\Psi_{m}(x)+\epsilon\geq\varphi(x)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
m\geq m_{\epsilon}
.
We compute

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi(y_{k})+2|x_{k}-y_{k}|-2|x_{k}-x|\leq\epsilon+\Psi_{m}(x)+2|x_{k}-y_{k}|-2|x_{k}-x|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\Psi_{m+1}(x)\leq\epsilon+\varphi(x),

where the last but one inequality follows from the definition of
\Psi_{m+1}
. This implies
\overline{\varphi}(x)\leq\epsilon+\varphi(x)
which, by the arbitrariness of
\epsilon>0
, implies the claim.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

If one knows in advance the regularity

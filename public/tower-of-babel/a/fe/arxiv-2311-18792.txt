Abstract
We analyze the overall benefits of an energy community cooperative game under which distributed energy resources (DER) are shared behind a regulated distribution utility meter under a general net energy metering (NEM) tariff. Two community DER scheduling algorithms are examined. The first is a community with centrally controlled DER, whereas the second is decentralized letting its members schedule their own DER locally. For both communities, we prove that the cooperative game’s value function is superadditive, hence the grand coalition achieves the highest welfare. We also prove the balancedness of the cooperative game under the two DER scheduling algorithms, which means that there is a welfare re-distribution scheme that de-incentivizes players from leaving the grand coalition to form smaller ones. Lastly, we present five ex-post and an ex-ante welfare re-distribution mechanisms and evaluate them in simulation, in addition to investigating the performance of various community sizes under the two DER scheduling algorithms.

III-B

Payoff Allocation, Imputation, and Core

We showed that both the community under both scheduling schemes has a superadditive value function, which raises the normative question of how we should assign the coalition value to each player of the cooperative game.

Definition 1

(Payoff allocation and imputation)
.

Let
\bm{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{H}
denote the payoff allocation vector for coalition
{\cal N}\subseteq{\cal H}
, with
\psi_{i}
being the payoff to prosumer
i\in{\cal H}
. No payoff is assigned to prosumers outside the coalition, i.e.,
\psi_{i}=0,~\forall i\notin{\cal N}
.

The allocation
\bm{\psi}
is an imputation if: 1) the grand coalition welfare is wholly distributed to the members (efficiency), i.e.,
\sum_{i\in{\cal H}}\psi_{i}=\nu({\cal H})
, and 2) individuals are better off joining the coalition (individual rationality), i.e.,
\psi_{i}\geq\nu(i),~\forall i\in{\cal H}
. Therefore, the set of all imputations is defined as

{\cal I}:=\big\{\bm{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{H}:\sum_{i\in{\cal H}}\psi_{i}=\nu({\cal H}),~\psi_{i}\geq\nu(i),\forall i\in{\cal H}\big\}.

(6)

It is not hard to show that for any superadditive game,
{\cal I}
is nonempty. Despite the favorable properties of a payoff allocation that is an imputation
\bm{\psi}\in{\cal I}
, it does not guarantee
group rationality
, which ensures that no subset of prosumers would be better-off if they jointly withdraw from the
grand coalition
to form their own coalition. Therefore, we further require the payoff allocation to be in the
core
, which is the fundamental solution concept for cooperative games.

Definition 2

(Core and coalition stability)
.

The core
{\cal C}
for the cooperative game
({\cal H},\nu)
is defined as the set of all payoffs that are imputations and ensures that no coalition can have a value greater than the sum of its members’ payoffs

{\cal C}:=\big\{\bm{\psi}\in{\cal I}:\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}\psi_{i}\geq\nu({\cal N}),~\forall{\cal N}\in 2^{\cal H}\big\}.

(7)

The coalition is stable if
\bm{\psi}\in{\cal C}
.

III-C

Balanced Games

Balanced games
, which gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a stabilizing allocation (nonempty core) in a cooperative game are defined next.

Definition 3

A map

\sum_{{\cal N}\in 2^{\cal H}}\alpha({\cal N})\bm{1}_{{\cal N}}(i)=1

\bm{1}_{{\cal N}}(i)=0

The cooperative game (

\sum_{{\cal N}\in 2^{\cal H}}\alpha({\cal N})\nu({\cal N})\leq\nu({\cal H})

V
Conclusion

In this paper, a cooperative game theoretic approach is employed to analyze resource sharing and welfare distribution in energy community coalitions. We showed that when the community DER is centrally controlled to maximize the community welfare, and when the DER of each player are locally controlled to maximize each player’s surplus, the cooperation value function is superadditive and the game is balanced. The payoff of the game is then allocated using five different
ex-post
payoff allocation mechanisms, which are compared to an
ex-ante
and welfare maximizing mechanism. In simulations, we evaluated the welfare gains of the energy coalition under both scheduling algorithms and the performance of the
ex-post
and
ex-ante
allocation mechanisms.

Lemma
3
and Proof of Lemma
3

Recall the welfare maximization problem under centralized DER scheduling

\displaystyle W^{\ast}(\bm{d}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}},\bm{z}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}}):=\underset{\{\bm{d}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|{\cal N}|},\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|{\cal N}|}}{\text{ maximize}}

\displaystyle~~W({\cal N}):=\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}S_{i}^{\pi}(\bm{d}_{i},z_{i})

subject to

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}P^{\pi}(z_{i})=P^{\pi}(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}z_{i})

\displaystyle\text{for all }~i=1,\ldots,N

\displaystyle z_{i}=\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{d}_{i}-r_{i}

\displaystyle\underline{z}_{i}\leq z_{i}\leq\overline{z}_{i}

\displaystyle\underline{\bm{d}}_{i}\preceq\bm{d}_{i}\preceq\overline{\bm{d}}_{i},

which can be more compactly written as

\displaystyle W^{\ast}(\bm{d}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}},\bm{z}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}}):=\underset{(\bm{d},\bm{z})\in\Omega^{{\cal N}}}{\text{ maximize}}\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}U_{i}(\bm{d}_{i})-P^{\pi}(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}z_{i}),

where we used the constraint (
2b
), the notation
\bm{d}^{{\cal M}^{\ast}}:=\{\bm{d}^{{\cal M}^{\ast}}_{i}\},\bm{z}^{{\cal M}^{\ast}}:=\{z^{{\cal M}^{\ast}}_{i}\},\forall i\in{\cal M}\in 2^{{\cal H}}
, and the feasible set notation

\displaystyle\Omega^{{\cal N}}:=\{

\displaystyle(\bm{d}_{1},\ldots,\bm{d}_{N}),(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}):z_{i}=\bm{1}^{\top}\bm{d}_{i}-r_{i},

\displaystyle\underline{z}_{i}\leq z_{i}\leq\overline{z}_{i},\underline{\bm{d}}_{i}\preceq\bm{d}_{i}\preceq\overline{\bm{d}}_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,N\}.

Given that the constraints are linear/affine, we can scale the decision variables by
\beta
without changing the feasible set
\Omega^{{\cal N}}
.

Now, we solve

\displaystyle W^{\ast}(\beta\bm{d}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}}\hskip-2.84544pt,\beta\bm{z}

{}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}})\hskip-2.84544pt=\hskip-2.84544pt\underset{(\bm{d},\bm{z})\in\Omega^{{\cal N}}}{\text{maximize}}\bigg(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}U_{i}(\beta\bm{d}_{i})-P^{\pi}(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}\beta z_{i})\bigg)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(A)}}}{{=}}\underset{(\bm{d},\bm{z})\in\Omega^{{\cal N}}}{\text{maximize}}\bigg(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}U_{i}(\beta\bm{d}_{i})-\beta P^{\pi}(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}z_{i})\bigg)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(B)}}}{{\geq}}\underset{(\bm{d},\bm{z})\in\Omega^{{\cal N}}}{\text{maximize}}\bigg(\beta\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}U_{i}(\bm{d}_{i})-\beta P^{\pi}(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}z_{i})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\beta\cdot\underset{(\bm{d},\bm{z})\in\Omega^{{\cal N}}}{\text{maximize}}\bigg(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}U_{i}(\bm{d}_{i})-P^{\pi}(\sum_{i\in{\cal N}}z_{i})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\beta W^{\ast}(\bm{d}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}},\bm{z}^{{\cal N}^{\ast}}),

where step (A) is because, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle P^{\pi}(\beta x)

\displaystyle=\pi^{+}[\beta x]^{+}-\pi^{-}[\beta x]^{-}

\displaystyle=\beta\pi^{+}[x]^{+}-\beta\pi^{-}[x]^{-}=\beta P^{\pi}(x),

and step (B) is due to the concavity, additivity and
U_{i}(\bm{0})=0

U_{i}(\cdot)

i\in{\cal N},k\in{\cal K}
,

\displaystyle U_{ik}(\beta d_{ik})

\displaystyle=U_{ik}(0+\beta d_{ik})

\displaystyle\geq(1-\beta)U_{ik}(0)+\beta U_{ik}(d_{ik})=\beta U_{ik}(d_{ik}),

which completes the proof.

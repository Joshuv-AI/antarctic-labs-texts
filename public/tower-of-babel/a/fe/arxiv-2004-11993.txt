Exterior powers and pointwise creation operators Thanks: N.J. Young is the corresponding author.
Dimitrios Chiotis, Zinaida A. Lykova, N. J. Young

Abstract
. We develop a theory of pointwise wedge products of vector-valued functions on the circle and the disc, and obtain results which give rise to a new approach to the analysis of the matricial Nehari problem. We investigate properties of pointwise creation operators and pointwise orthogonal complements in the context of operator theory and the study of vector-valued analytic functions on the unit disc.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
15A75, 47B35, 30J99

1.
Introduction

The wedge product of Hilbert spaces, though a long established theory (see, for example
[
11
,
12
,
3
,
5
]
), deserves in our view to be better exploited in operator theory than it has been hitherto. In this paper we put forward a new approach to some aspects of the analysis of
E
-valued functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
and on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, where
E
is a separable Hilbert space. Our first application of this idea was to the problem of the superoptimal analytic approximation of a continuous matrix-valued function on the unit circle
[
2
]
. The computation of such approximants arises naturally in the context of the classical “Nehari problem”, and also in its application to the “robust stabilization problem” in control engineering. In
[
2
]
we give a new algorithm for the construction of the unique superoptimal analytic approximant of a continuous
matrix-valued function on the unit circle, making use of exterior powers of operators in preference to spectral or Wiener-Masani factorizations.
This algorithm is parallel to the construction of
[
10
]
, but has the advantage that it
requires only the spectral factorisation of
scalar
functions on the circle, together with the calculation of singular-value decompositions, and otherwise requires only rational arithmetic. In the algorithm we make use of pointwise creation operators and pointwise orthogonal complements.

We show how the Hilbert space geometry of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
, where
E
is a separable Hilbert space, interacts with the pointwise geometry of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
, that is, the geometry of
E
over each point of
\mathbb{T}
or
\mathbb{D}
. We work with the completion of the algebraic tensor product of Hilbert spaces, namely the Hilbert tensor product (see
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
), and, in particular, with its closed linear subspace of antisymmetric tensors. The space of all antisymmetric tensors
\wedge^{p}E
, also called a
wedge
or
exterior product
, is a closed linear subspace of the
p
-fold Hilbert tensor product
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
, see Section
2
.

In Section
3
, we define the pointwise wedge product of maps that are defined either on the unit disc or on the unit circle and take values in Hilbert spaces. We illustrate the fact that some classical results from function theory extend to the pointwise wedge product. An exemplary result is Proposition
3.7
which asserts that, for any separable Hilbert space
E
, the pointwise wedge product of two functions in the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is an element of
H^{1}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).
We study pointwise creation operators, which are of the form

C_{\xi}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E),f\mapsto\xi\dot{\wedge}f,

where
\xi\colon\mathbb{D}\to E
is a bounded analytic function, and the symbol
\xi\dot{\wedge}f
denotes the function

(\xi\dot{\wedge}f)(z)=\xi(z)\wedge f(z)\mbox{ for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

Analogous notation applies when
\xi,f
are
E
-valued functions defined almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.
We connect the kernel of
C_{\xi}
to the pointwise linear span and pointwise orthogonal complement of
\xi.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.

H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
denotes the space of bounded analytic
E
-valued functions
Q
on the unit disk with supremum norm:

\|Q\|_{H^{\infty}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\emph{def}}}{{=}}\|Q\|_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\emph{def}}}{{=}}\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|Q(z)\|_{E}.

In Section 4 we prove, among other things, the following two statements.

Theorem

4.7
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space, let
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{j}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
. Suppose that the set
\{\xi_{i}(z)\}_{i=0}^{j}
is orthonormal in
E
for almost every
z\in\mathbb{T}.
Then

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)

is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E).

Let
F
and
E
be Hilbert spaces. The Banach space of bounded linear operators from
F
to
E
with the operator norm is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(F,E)
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space and let
\xi,\eta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},E)
.
We define
\xi\eta^{*}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(E,E))
by

(\xi\eta^{*})(z)x=\langle x,\eta(z)\rangle\ \xi(z)\mbox{ for all }x\in E\ \mbox{and for almost every }\ z\ \mbox{on}\ \mathbb{T}.

Thus
\xi\eta^{*}(z)
is the operator of rank one on
E
that is sometimes denoted by
\xi(z)\otimes\eta(z)
(for example, in
[
1
, equation (1.17)]
).

Theorem

4.18
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space. Let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
be an inner function. Then, for any
h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}h=P_{+}\alpha_{\xi},

where
\alpha_{\xi}=h-\xi\xi^{*}h
and
P_{+}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is the orthogonal projection. Moreover

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}h=h-T_{\xi\xi^{*}}h,

where
T_{\xi\xi^{*}}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is the Toeplitz operator with symbol
\xi\xi^{*}
.

Thus
C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}
is the Toeplitz operator with symbol
1-\xi\xi^{*}.
Finally, we show that although
C_{\xi}
is an isometry from the pointwise orthogonal complement of
\xi
to
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E),
a simple example proves that
C_{\xi}
fails to be a partial isometry.

2.
Exterior powers of Hilbert spaces and operators

In this Section we recall the established notion of the exterior power, or wedge product, of Hilbert spaces. One can find definitions and properties of wedge products in
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
,
12
]

and
[
13
]
.
Here we present a concise version of this theory which we need for the development of pointwise wedge products of vector-valued functions on the circle and the disc.
We shall assume the notion of the algebraic tensor product of linear spaces as given, for example, in
[
6
]
.

For
E
a Hilbert space, we consider the completion of the p-fold algebraic tensor product, denoted by
\otimes_{H}^{p}E,
with respect to the norm induced by the inner product (
2.1
). We define an action of permutation operators on tensor products and examine various properties.
These permutation operators generate two different types of tensors, symmetric and antisymmetric, and we focus on the properties of the antisymmetric tensors. The space of all antisymmetric tensors, also called wedge or exterior products, in
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
is denoted by
\wedge^{p}E
and in Theorem
2.9
we prove it is a closed linear subspace of
\otimes_{H}^{p}E.

In the following
E
denotes a Hilbert space.

Definition 2.1
.

\otimes^{p}E
is the
p
-fold algebraic
tensor product
of
E,
spanned by tensors of the form
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p},
where
x_{j}\in E
for

j=1,\dots,p.

Definition 2.2
.

An inner product on
\otimes^{p}E

is given on elementary tensors by

\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p},y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{p}\rangle_{\otimes^{p}E}=p!\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle_{E}\cdots\langle x_{p},y_{p}\rangle_{E}

(2.1)

for any
x_{1},\dots,x_{p},y_{1},\dots,y_{p}\in E
,
and is extended to

\otimes^{p}E
by sesqui-linearity.

Definition 2.3
.

\otimes_{H}^{p}E
is the completion of
\otimes^{p}E
with respect to the norm
\|u\|=\langle u,u\rangle^{1/2}_{\otimes^{p}E},
for
u\in\otimes^{p}E.

Observe that the inner product (
2.1
), in contrast to the majority of the ones included in the bibliography, invokes a multiple of
p!
. The reason for this choice will be apparent in Theorem
2.11
.

In order to introduce antisymmetric tensors, we need to consider the action of the following permutation operators on tensors.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
denote the symmetric group on
\{1,\dots,p\},
with the operation of composition.
For
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
, we define

S_{\sigma}\colon\otimes^{p}E\to\otimes^{p}E

on elementary tensors by

\displaystyle S_{\sigma}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p})=x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes x_{\sigma(2)}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{\sigma(p)},

and we extend
S_{\sigma}
to
\otimes^{p}E
by linearity, that is, for
u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{1}^{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{p}^{i},
we define

S_{\sigma}(u)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}S_{\sigma}(x_{1}^{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{p}^{i}).

for any
x_{j}^{i}\in E
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 2.5
.

(\mathfrak{S}_{p},\circ)
is a group, and so, for every permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p},
there exists
\sigma^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
such that

\sigma\circ\sigma^{-1}={\mathrm{id}}=\sigma^{-1}\circ\sigma,

where
id\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
is the identity map on
\{1,\dots,p\}.

Elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
induce unitary operators on
\otimes^{p}_{H}E
in an obvious way.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space, and let
p
be a positive integer. Then, for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
,

S_{\sigma}
is a linear operator on the normed space
(\otimes^{p}E,\|\cdot\|)
, which extends to an isometry
\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
on
(\otimes^{p}_{H}E,\|\cdot\|)
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
is a unitary operator on
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
S_{\sigma}
is linear.
For any elementary tensors
w=x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p},
v=y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\cdots y_{p}
by the definition of the inner product on
\otimes^{p}E,

\displaystyle\left\langle S_{\sigma}^{*}w,v\right\rangle_{\otimes^{p}E}

\displaystyle=\left\langle w,y_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{\sigma(p)}\right\rangle_{\otimes^{p}E}

\displaystyle=p!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\left\langle x_{j},y_{\sigma(j)}\right\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=p!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\left\langle x_{\sigma^{-1}(j)},y_{j}\right\rangle_{E}

\displaystyle=\left\langle S_{\sigma^{-1}}w,v\right\rangle_{\otimes^{p}E}.

Hence
S_{\sigma}^{*}=S_{\sigma^{-1}}
, thus

S_{\sigma}^{*}S_{\sigma}=S_{\sigma^{-1}}S_{\sigma}=I,

the identity operator on
\otimes^{p}E
,
and therefore
S_{\sigma}
is an isometric linear self-map of
\otimes^{p}E
. Likewise,
S_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}^{*}=I
, and so
S_{\sigma}
is also a surjective self-map of
\otimes^{p}E
.

Thus one can extend
S_{\sigma}
by continuity to an isometric linear self-map
\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
of the completion
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
of
\otimes^{p}E
. Since

\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
is isometric, its range is complete, hence closed in
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
. Since the range of
\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
contains that of
S_{\sigma}
,
\ran\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}=\otimes_{H}^{p}E
. Being both surjective and isometric,

\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
is a unitary operator on
\otimes^{p}_{H}E
.
∎

Henceforth we shall denote the extended operator
\mathbf{S}_{\sigma}
by
S_{\sigma}
.

Definition 2.7
.

A tensor
u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E
is said to be
symmetric
if
S_{\sigma}(u)=u
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}.

A tensor
u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E
is said to be
antisymmetric
if
u=\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}u
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p},

where
\epsilon_{\sigma}
is the signature of
\sigma.

Note that
\epsilon_{\sigma\circ\sigma^{-1}}=\epsilon_{\sigma}\epsilon_{\sigma^{-1}}=1,
and hence
\epsilon_{\sigma}=\epsilon_{\sigma^{-1}}.

Definition 2.8
.

The space of all antisymmetric tensors in
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
will be denoted by
\wedge^{p}E
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space. Then
\wedge^{p}E
is a closed linear subspace of the Hilbert space
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
for any
p\geq 2.

Proof.

For
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
define the operator

f_{\sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\emph{def}}}{{=}}S_{\sigma}-\epsilon_{\sigma}I\;\colon\otimes_{H}^{p}E\to\otimes_{H}^{p}E,

where
I
denotes the identity operator on
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
. Since
S_{\sigma}
is a continuous linear operator on
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
,
f_{\sigma}
is a continuous linear operator.
The kernel of the operator
f_{\sigma}
is

\begin{array}[]{cllllllllll}\ker f_{\sigma}&=\{u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E\colon(S_{\sigma}-\epsilon_{\sigma}I)(u)=0\}\\
&=\{u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E\colon S_{\sigma}(u)=\epsilon_{\sigma}u\}\\
&=\{u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E\colon\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)=u\}.\end{array}

Since
f_{\sigma}
is a continuous linear operator on
\otimes_{H}^{p}E,

\ker f_{\sigma}
is a closed linear subspace of
\otimes_{H}^{p}E.
Thus
\wedge^{p}E
is a closed linear subspace of
\otimes_{H}^{p}E,

since

\wedge^{p}E=\{u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E\;\colon\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)=u\mbox{ for all }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}\}=\bigcap\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\ker f_{\sigma}.\qed

Theorem
2.9
implies that the orthogonal projection from
\otimes_{H}^{p}E

onto
\wedge^{p}E
is well-defined.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space. For
x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\in E,
define
x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p}
to be the orthogonal
projection of the elementary tensor
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p}
onto
\wedge^{p}E
, that is

x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p}=P_{\wedge^{p}E}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{p}).

Theorem 2.11
.

For all
u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E,

P_{\wedge^{p}E}(u)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u).

Proof.

Let
u\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E
. Then, for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p},

u=\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)+(u-\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)),
and so

p!u=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)+\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}(u-\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)).

It suffices to show that

\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)\in\wedge^{p}E
and

\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}(u-\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u))

is
orthogonal to the set of antisymmetric tensors, in other words, that if
v\in\wedge^{p}E
then

\langle v,\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}(u-\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u))\rangle_{\otimes^{p}_{H}E}=0.

Let
w=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)\in\otimes_{H}^{p}E
. For every
\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{p},
we have

\begin{array}[]{clll}\epsilon_{\tau}S_{\tau}(w)&=\displaystyle\epsilon_{\tau}S_{\tau}\left(\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)\right)\vskip 8.61108pt=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau\circ\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\tau\circ\sigma}S_{\tau\circ\sigma}(u)\\
&=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma^{\prime}}S_{\sigma^{\prime}}(u)\vskip 8.61108pt=w,\end{array}

where
\tau\circ\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}.

Hence
\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u)\in\wedge^{p}E.

For every
v\in\wedge^{p}E,
we have
v=\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}v
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p},
and

\begin{array}[]{cllllllllllll}\langle v,\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}(u-\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(u))\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}&=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\langle v,u\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}-\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}\langle v,S_{\sigma}(u)\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\langle v,u\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}-\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{{\sigma}}\langle{S_{\sigma}}^{*}v,u\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\langle v-\epsilon_{{\sigma}^{-1}}{S_{\sigma^{-1}}}v,u\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\vskip 8.61108pt=0.\end{array}

∎

Remark 2.12
.

If
p>1,
then
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
contains a transposition, for instance
\sigma=(1\;2),
and
\epsilon_{\sigma}=-1.
If
p=1,
then
\wedge^{1}E=E.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space and let
p\geq 2
. The set of antisymmetric tensors and the set of symmetric tensors are orthogonal in
\otimes_{H}^{p}E.

Proof.

Suppose that
u
is a symmetric tensor, that is
S_{\sigma}u=u
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
, and that
v
is an antisymmetric tensor, that is,
S_{\sigma}v=\epsilon_{\sigma}v
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
.
Since
p\geq 2
there exists a transposition
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}
, so that
\epsilon_{\sigma}=-1
, and therefore
S_{\sigma}v=-v
. By Proposition
2.6
,
S_{\sigma}
is a unitary operator on
\otimes_{H}^{p}E
. Thus

\langle u,v\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}=\langle S_{\sigma}u,S_{\sigma}v\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}=\langle u,-v\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}.

Thus

\langle u,v\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}=-\langle u,v\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E},
and so

\langle u,v\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}=0.

∎

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space.
The inner product in
\wedge^{p}E
is given by

\langle x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p},y_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge y_{p}\rangle_{\wedge^{p}E}=\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\langle x_{1},y_{p}\rangle_{E}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle x_{p},y_{1}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\langle x_{p},y_{p}\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}

for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{p},y_{1},\dots,y_{p}\in E
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.11
, we have

\begin{array}[]{cllllllllllllllllll}&\langle x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p},y_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge y_{p}\rangle_{\wedge^{p}E}\\[21.52771pt]
&=\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p}),\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\tau}S_{\tau}(y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{p})\right\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!^{2}}\sum\limits_{\sigma,\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\langle\epsilon_{\sigma}S_{\sigma}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p}),\epsilon_{\tau}S_{\tau}(y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{p})\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\;\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!^{2}}\sum\limits_{\sigma,\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma}\epsilon_{\tau}\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p},S_{\sigma}^{*}S_{\tau}(y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{p})\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\;\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!^{2}}\sum\limits_{\sigma,\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma^{-1}}\epsilon_{\tau}\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p},S_{\sigma^{-1}}S_{\tau}(y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{p})\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\;\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma^{\prime}}\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{p},S_{\sigma^{\prime}}(y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes y_{p})\rangle_{\otimes_{H}^{p}E}\\
&=\;\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{p}}\epsilon_{\sigma^{\prime}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p}\langle x_{i},y_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}\rangle_{E}\\
&=\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\langle x_{1},y_{p}\rangle_{E}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle x_{p},y_{1}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\langle x_{p},y_{p}\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}\end{array}

by Leibniz’ formula.∎

Since we have already shown that
\wedge^{p}E
is a closed linear subspace of the Hilbert space
\otimes_{H}^{p}E,
the space
(\wedge^{p}E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\wedge^{p}E})
with inner product given by Proposition
2.14
is itself a Hilbert space.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{j}\}
is an orthonormal set in
E.
Then,
for every
x\in E,

\|u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j}\wedge x\|_{\wedge^{j+1}E}=\|x-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\langle x,u_{i}\rangle u_{i}\|_{E}.

Proof.

For
x\in E
we may write

x=x-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\langle x,u_{i}\rangle u_{i}+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\langle x,u_{i}\rangle u_{i}.

By Proposition
2.14
,

\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}&\|u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j}\wedge x\|_{\wedge^{j+1}E}^{2}\\
&=\langle u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j}\wedge x,u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j}\wedge x\rangle_{\wedge^{j+1}E}\\
&=\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle u_{1},u_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\cdots&\langle u_{1},x\rangle_{E}\\
\langle u_{2},u_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle u_{2},u_{2}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\cdots&\langle u_{2},x\rangle_{E}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\ddots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\langle u_{j},u_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle u_{j},u_{2}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\langle u_{j},u_{j}\rangle_{E}&\langle u_{j},x\rangle_{E}\\
\langle x,u_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x,u_{2}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\cdots&\langle x,x\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}.\end{array}

By assumption,

\langle u_{i},u_{k}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,\quad\mbox{ if }i\neq k\\
1,\quad\mbox{ if }i=k\end{array},\right.

and hence

\begin{array}[]{lllllllll}&\|u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j}\wedge x\|_{\wedge^{j+1}E}^{2}=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&\langle u_{1},x\rangle_{E}\\
0&1&\cdots&\langle u_{2},x\rangle_{E}\\
\vdots&~&\hskip-38.74988pt\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&1&\langle u_{j},x\rangle_{E}\\
\langle x,u_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x,u_{2}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\langle x,x\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}.\end{array}

If, for
k=1,\cdots,j
we multiply the
k
-th column of the determinant by
\langle u_{k},x\rangle_{E}
and subtract it from the
(j+1)
-th column, we find that

\begin{array}[]{clll}\|u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j}\wedge x\|_{\wedge^{j+1}E}^{2}&=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&~&\cdots&0\\
0&1&~&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&~&\ddots&~&\vdots\\
0&~&\cdots&1&0\\
\langle x,u_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x,u_{2}\rangle_{E}&\cdots&\cdots&\langle x,x\rangle_{E}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle_{E}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\|x\|_{E}^{2}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle_{E}|^{2}\\
&=\|x-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\langle x,u_{i}\rangle_{E}u_{i}\|_{E}^{2},\end{array}

the latter equality by Pythagoras’ theorem. ∎

Definition 2.16
.

Let
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
be a Hilbert space. The
p
-fold Cartesian product of
E
is defined to be the set

\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{p-times}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}):x_{i}\in E\}.

Moreover, we define a norm on
\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{p-times}
by

\|(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\|=\{\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|x_{i}\|_{E}^{2}\}^{\tfrac{1}{2}}.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space. We define the multilinear operator

\Lambda\colon\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{p-times}\to\wedge^{p}E

by

\Lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})=x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p}\quad\text{for all}\quad x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\in E.

Proposition 2.18
.

[Hadamard’s inequality,
[
7
]
, p. 477]

For any matrix

A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},

|\det(A)|\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad|\det(A)|\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space. Then the multilinear mapping

\Lambda\colon\underbrace{E\times\dots\times E}_{p-times}\to\wedge^{p}E

is bounded.

Proof.

Let
x_{i}\in E
for all
i=1,\dots,p.
Then
\Lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})=x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p}
and

\begin{array}[]{clll}\|\Lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\|_{\wedge^{p}E}^{2}&=\|x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p}\|_{\wedge^{p}E}^{2}\\
&=\langle x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p},x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{p}\rangle_{\wedge^{p}E}\\
&=\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle x_{1},x_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\langle x_{1},x_{p}\rangle_{E}\\
\langle x_{2},x_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x_{2},x_{2}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\langle x_{2},x_{p}\rangle_{E}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\dots\\
\langle x_{p},x_{1}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\dots&\langle x_{p},x_{p}\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.\end{array}

Let

X=\begin{pmatrix}\langle x_{1},x_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\langle x_{1},x_{p}\rangle_{E}\\
\langle x_{2},x_{1}\rangle_{E}&\langle x_{2},x_{2}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\langle x_{2},x_{p}\rangle_{E}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\dots\\
\langle x_{p},x_{1}\rangle_{E}&\dots&\dots&\langle x_{p},x_{p}\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}.

By Hadamard’s inequality,

|\det(X)|\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}|\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle_{E}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Moreover, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

|\det(X)|\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\|x_{j}\|_{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\|x_{i}\|_{E}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Therefore

\|\Lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\|_{\wedge^{p}E}^{2}\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\|x_{j}\|_{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\|x_{i}\|_{E}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.2)

Let
\|(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\|_{E^{p}}\leq 1
. Since
\|x_{j}\|_{E}\leq\|(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\|_{E^{p}}\leq 1
for each
j
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\|^{2}_{\wedge^{p}E}

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Hence the
p
-linear operator
\Lambda
is bounded.
∎

3.
Pointwise wedge products

In this section we introduce the notion of pointwise wedge product of vector-valued functions on the unit circle or in the unit disk and explore its features.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space and let
f,g\colon\mathbb{D}\to E

\mathrm{(}f,g\colon\mathbb{T}\to E\mathrm{)}
be
E
-valued maps. We define the
pointwise wedge product of
f
and
g,

f\dot{\wedge}g\colon\mathbb{D}\to\wedge^{2}E\quad\mathrm{(}f\dot{\wedge}g\colon\mathbb{T}\to\wedge^{2}E\mathrm{)}

by

(f\dot{\wedge}g)(z)=f(z)\wedge g(z)\quad\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\quad\mathrm{(}\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\mathrm{)}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space and let
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to E

\mathrm{(}\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}\colon\mathbb{T}\to E\mathrm{)}
be
E
-valued maps. We call
\chi_{1},\dots\chi_{n}

pointwise linearly dependent
on
\mathbb{D}

(
or on
\mathbb{T}
)
if for all
z\in\mathbb{D}

(for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}
respectively) the vectors
\chi_{1}(z),\dots,\chi_{n}(z)
are linearly dependent in
E
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are pointwise linearly dependent on
\mathbb{T}
, then

(x_{1}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}x_{n})(z)=0

for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}.

3.1.
Pointwise wedge products on function spaces

For vector-valued
L^{p}
spaces we use the terminology of
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space and let
1\leq p<\infty.
Define

(i)

L^{p}(\mathbb{T},E)
to be the normed space of measurable
(weakly or strongly, which amounts to the same thing, in view of the separability of
E
)

E
-valued maps
f\colon\mathbb{T}\to E
such that

\|f\|_{p}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|f(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{p}d\theta\right)^{1/p}<\infty;

(ii)

H^{p}(\mathbb{D},E)
to be the normed space of analytic
E
-valued maps
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to E
such that

\|f\|_{p}=\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|f(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{p}d\theta\right)^{1/p}<\infty;

(iii)

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},E)
is the space of essentially bounded measurable
E
-valued functions on the unit circle with essential supremum norm

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=\mathrm{ess}\sup\limits_{|z|=1}\|f(z)\|_{E}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space and let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,
where
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Suppose that

x\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T},E),

y\in L^{q}(\mathbb{T},E).
Then

x\dot{\wedge}y\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E).

and

\|x\dot{\wedge}y\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E)}\leq\|x\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T},E)}\|y\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T},E)}.

(3.1)

Proof.

By Proposition
2.14
, for all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\begin{array}[]{cllllllll}\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(z)\|_{\wedge^{2}E}^{2}&=\langle x(z)\wedge y(z),x(z)\wedge y(z)\rangle_{\wedge^{2}E}\\
&=\langle x(z),x(z)\rangle_{E}\cdot\langle y(z),y(z)\rangle_{E}-|\langle x(z),y(z)\rangle_{E}|^{2}\\
&\leq\|x(z)\|_{E}^{2}\|y(z)\|_{E}^{2}.\end{array}

Thus, for all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(z)\|_{\wedge^{2}E}\leq\|x(z)\|_{E}\|y(z)\|_{E}.

By Definition
3.4
,

\|x\dot{\wedge}y\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E)}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(e^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{2}E}\;d\theta\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}\|y(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}\;d\theta.

(3.2)

Now by Hölder’s inequality,

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}\|y(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}\;d\theta\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{p}\;d\theta\right)^{1/p}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|y(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{q}\;d\theta\right)^{1/q}.

(3.3)

By inequalities (
3.2
) and (
3.3
),
x\dot{\wedge}y\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E)
and the inequality (
3.1
) holds.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space and
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to E
be analytic
E
-valued maps on
\mathbb{D}.

Then,

x_{1}\dot{\wedge}x_{2}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}x_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\wedge^{n}E

is also analytic on
\mathbb{D}
and

(x_{1}\dot{\wedge}x_{2}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}x_{n})^{\prime}(z)=x_{1}^{\prime}(z)\wedge x_{2}(z)\wedge\dots\wedge x_{n}(z)+\dots+x_{1}(z)\wedge x_{2}(z)\wedge\dots\wedge x_{n}^{\prime}(z)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}.

The proof is straightforward. It follows from Proposition
2.14
and the continuity of
\Lambda
, see Hadamard’s inequalities (
2.2
).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space. Suppose
x,y\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).
Then

x\dot{\wedge}y\in H^{1}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
,
x\dot{\wedge}y
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}.
By Proposition
2.14
, for
0<r<1
and
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi,

\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(re^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{2}E}\leq\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}\|y(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}.

By Proposition
2.14
and by Definition
3.4
,

\begin{array}[]{cllll}\|x\dot{\wedge}y\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)}&=\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(re^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{2}E}\;d\theta\right)\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}\|y(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}\;d\theta\right),\end{array}

for
0<r<1
and
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi.

Also, by Hölder’s inequality, for
0<r<1
and
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi,

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}\|y(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}\;d\theta\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|y(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2},

hence

\|x\dot{\wedge}y\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)}\leq\|x\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}\|y\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}.

Consequently,
x\dot{\wedge}y\in H^{1}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space, let
x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and let
y\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E).
Then

x\dot{\wedge}y\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
,
x\dot{\wedge}y
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}.
By Proposition
3.5
, for

0<r<1,\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi,

we have

\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(re^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{2}E}\leq\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}\|y(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}.

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{cllllllll}\|x\dot{\wedge}y\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)}&=\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|(x\dot{\wedge}y)(re^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{2}E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\|y(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}d\theta\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\|y\|_{\infty}\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(re^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}d\theta\right)^{1/2}<\infty.\end{array}

∎

Definition 3.9
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space.
We say that a family of
\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

of maps from
\mathbb{T}
to
E
is pointwise orthonormal on
\mathbb{T}
, if for all
z
in a set of full measure in
\mathbb{T}
,
the set of vectors
\{f_{\lambda}(z)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is
orthonormal in
E
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
Let
F
be a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
and let
X
be a subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).

We define the
pointwise orthogonal complement
of
X
in
F
to be the set

\Poc(X,F)=\{f\in F:f(z)\perp\{x(z):x\in X\}\;\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\}.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space, and let

\;\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},E)
be a pointwise orthonormal set on
\mathbb{T}
, and let
x\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
. Then

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{j+2}E),

and

\|\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{j+2}E)}\leq\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}.

Furthemore,

\|\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{j+2}E)}=\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}

if and only if
x\in\Poc(\{\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{j}\},L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.15
, for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\|\xi_{0}(z)\wedge\xi_{1}(z)\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{j}(z)\wedge x(z)\|^{2}_{\wedge^{j+2}E}=\|x(z)\|^{2}_{E}-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}|\langle x(z),\xi_{i}(z)\rangle_{E}|^{2}\leq\|x(z)\|^{2}_{E}.

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{cllllllll}&\|\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{j+2}E)}\\
&=\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\xi_{0}(e^{i\theta})\wedge\xi_{1}(e^{i\theta})\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{j}(e^{i\theta})\wedge x(e^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{j+2}E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}d\theta\right)^{1/2}=\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}<\infty.\end{array}

∎

4.
Pointwise creation operators, orthogonal complements and linear spans

Definition 4.1
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
Let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
. We define the
pointwise creation operator

C_{\xi}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)

by

C_{\xi}f=\xi\dot{\wedge}f,\;\text{for}\;f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).

Remark 4.2
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space. Let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
and let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
.
By the generalized Fatou’s Theorem
[
8
, Chapter V]
, the radial limits

\lim_{r\to 1}\xi(re^{i\theta})\underset{\|\cdot\|_{E}}{=}\tilde{\xi}(e^{i\theta}),\quad\lim_{r\to 1}f(re^{i\theta})\underset{\|\cdot\|_{E}}{=}\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})\;\;(0<r<1)

exist almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and define functions
\tilde{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},E)
and
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
respectively, which satisfy the relations

\lim_{r\to 1}\|\xi(re^{i\theta})-\tilde{\xi}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}=0,\quad\lim_{r\to 1}\|f(re^{i\theta})-\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}=0\;\;(0<r<1)

for almost all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
Let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
and let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).
Then the radial limits
\lim_{r\to 1}(\xi(re^{i\theta})\wedge f(re^{i\theta}))
exist for almost all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}
and define functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.19
, the bilinear operator
\Lambda\colon E\times E\to\wedge^{2}E
is a continuous operator for the norms of
E
and
\wedge^{2}E.
By Remark
4.2
, the functions
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
and

f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
have radial limit functions
\tilde{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},E)
and
\tilde{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
. Also, by Proposition
3.8
,
\xi\dot{\wedge}f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).
Hence

\lim_{r\to 1}\|\xi(re^{i\theta})\wedge f(re^{i\theta})-\tilde{\xi}(e^{i\theta})\wedge\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{2}E}=0\quad\text{ almost everywhere on}\quad\mathbb{T}

and we conclude that

\lim_{r\to 1}(\xi(re^{i\theta})\wedge f(re^{i\theta}))\underset{\|\cdot\|_{\wedge^{2}E}}{=}\tilde{\xi}(e^{i\theta})\wedge\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})\;\text{almost everywhere on}\;\mathbb{T}.

This shows that the radial limits

\lim_{r\to 1}(\xi(re^{i\theta})\wedge f(re^{i\theta}))

exist almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and, by Lemma
3.5
, define functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E).

Hence one can consider
(C_{\xi}f)(z)=(\xi\dot{\wedge}f)(z)
to be defined for either all
z\in\mathbb{D}
or for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
By
[
8
, Chapter 5, Section 1]
, for any separable Hilbert space
E
, the map

f\mapsto\tilde{f}
is an isometric embedding of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
, where

\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})=\lim_{r\to 1}f(re^{i\theta}).
Since
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is complete and the embedding is isometric, the image of the embedding is complete, and therefore is closed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).
Therefore, the space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is identified isometrically with a closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).

In future we shall use the same notation for
f
and
\tilde{f}.

Our next aim is to show that
\Poc(X,F)
is a closed subspace of
F.
We are going to need the following results.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E
be a Hilbert space and let
x\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).
The function
\varphi\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\varphi(g)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\;d\theta

is continuous.

Proof.

Consider
g_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).
For any
\epsilon>0,
we are looking for a
\delta>0
such that

\|g-g_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|g(e^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}<\delta

implies

|\varphi(g)-\varphi(g_{0})|<\epsilon.

Note that

\begin{array}[]{cllllll}|\varphi(g)-\varphi(g_{0})|&=\left|\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|d\theta-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|d\theta\right|\\
&=\left|\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left(|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|-|\langle g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\right)d\theta\right|.\end{array}

For each
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T},
by the reverse triangle inequality, the integrand satisfies

\begin{array}[]{cllll}|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|-|\langle g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|&\leq\left|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}-\langle g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\right|\\
&=|\langle(ge^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|,\end{array}

|\varphi(g)-\varphi(g_{0})|\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g(e^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|d\theta.

(4.1)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\begin{array}[]{cll}\begin{array}[]{cllll}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g(e^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\;d\theta\\
&\leq\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|g(e^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}.\end{array}\end{array}

(4.2)

For the given
\epsilon>0,
let
\delta
be equal to
\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}+1},
and let

\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|g(e^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}<\delta.

By equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),

\begin{array}[]{clll}|\varphi(g)-\varphi(g_{0})|&\leq\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\|g(e^{i\theta})-g_{0}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right)^{1/2}\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}\\
&<\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}+1}\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}<\epsilon.\end{array}

Hence
\varphi
is a continuous function. ∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
Let
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
. Then

(i)

The space
V=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\langle f(z),\eta(z)\rangle_{E}=0\;\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).

(ii)

The space
V=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E):\langle f(z),\eta(z)\rangle_{E}=0\;\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).

Proof.

(i).

V
is a linear subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
since for
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C},

\psi,k\in V
and for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\langle\lambda\psi(z)+\mu k(z),\eta(z)\rangle_{E}=\lambda\langle\psi(z),\eta(z)\rangle_{E}+\mu\langle k(z),\eta(z)\rangle_{E}=0,

hence
\lambda\psi+\mu k\in V.

Now suppose that the sequence of functions
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
V
converges to a function
g.
We need to show that
g\in V.

Since
g_{n}\in V
for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\langle g_{n}(z),\eta(z)\rangle_{E}=0\;\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}.

(4.3)

Consider the function

\varphi\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\varphi(f)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\eta(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\;d\theta.

Then, by equation (
4.3
), we have

\varphi(g_{n})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g_{n}(e^{i\theta}),\eta(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\;d\theta=0.

By Remark
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
,
\varphi
is a continuous function on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),
thus

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\varphi(g_{n})=\varphi(g),

and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\eta(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\;d\theta=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle g_{n}(e^{i\theta}),\eta(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|\;d\theta=0.

Thus
|\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\eta(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|=0
for almost all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T},
and, hence,
g\in V.

We have proved that
V
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).

(ii).
The proof is similar to
(i)
. ∎

Our motivation for the next theorem is the following.
In
[
2
]
spaces of the form

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)

play a crucial role in the superoptimal Nehari problem. They are the domains of Hankel-type operators whose norms are “superoptimal singular values” of error functions
G-Q
, where
G
is a given continuous approximand and
Q
is its analytic approximation.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space, let
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{j}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
. Suppose that the set
\{\xi_{i}(z)\}_{i=0}^{j}
is orthonormal in
E
for almost every
z\in\mathbb{T}.
Then

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)

is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
, for every
x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x

is analytic on
\mathbb{D}.
By Proposition
3.11
, since
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{j}
are pointwise orthogonal on
\mathbb{T},

\|\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{j+2}E)}<\infty.

Thus, for every
x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E).

Let us first show that
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).
Observe that, by Proposition
4.3
,
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).

Let

\Xi_{0}=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\langle f(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}=0\quad\text{almost everywhere on}\;\mathbb{T}\}.

Consider a vector-valued function
w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).

For almost every
z\in\mathbb{T},
we may write
w
as

w(z)=w(z)-\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z)+\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z).

Then, for all
w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and for almost every
z\in\mathbb{T},

\begin{array}[]{clllll}(\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}w)(z)&=\xi_{0}(z)\wedge\big(w(z)-\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z)+\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z)\big)\\
&=\xi_{0}(z)\wedge\big(w(z)-\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z)\big)\end{array}

due to the pointwise linear dependence of
\xi_{0}
and

z\mapsto\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z)
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}.

Note that

w(z)-\langle w(z),\xi_{0}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{0}(z)\in\Xi_{0},

thus

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\subset\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}.

By Proposition
4.6
,
\Xi_{0}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),
hence

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\supset\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0},

and so,

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)=\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}.

Consider the mapping

C_{\xi_{0}}\colon\Xi_{0}\to\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}

given by

C_{\xi_{0}}w=\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}w

for all
w\in\Xi_{0}.
Notice that, by assumption,

\|\xi_{0}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}=1
for almost every
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}.
Therefore, for any
w\in\Xi_{0},
we have

\begin{array}[]{cllllll}\|\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E)}^{2}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\langle\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}w,\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}w\rangle(e^{i\theta})d\theta\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\|\xi_{0}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\|w(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}-|\langle w(e^{i\theta}),\xi_{0}(e^{i\theta})\rangle|^{2}\right)\;d\theta\\
&=\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}^{2},\end{array}

since

w
is pointwise orthogonal to
\xi_{0}
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}.
Thus the mapping

C_{\xi_{0}}\colon\Xi_{0}\to\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}

is an isometry. Furthermore,

C_{\xi_{0}}\colon\Xi_{0}\to\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}

is a surjective mapping,
thus
\Xi_{0}
and
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}
are isometrically isomorphic. Therefore, since
\Xi_{0}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),
the space
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{0}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)
. Hence
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).

To prove that

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\dots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E)
, let us consider

\Xi_{j}=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\langle f(z),\xi_{i}(z)\rangle_{E}=0,\;\text{for}\;i=0,\cdots,j\}

to be the pointwise orthogonal complement of
\xi_{0},\dots,\xi_{j}
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).
Let
\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).
We may write
\psi
as

\psi(z)=\psi(z)-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}\langle\psi(z),\xi_{i}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{i}(z)+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}\langle\psi(z),\xi_{i}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{i}(z).

Then, for all
\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and for almost all
z\in\mathbb{T},

(\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\psi)(z)=\xi_{0}(z)\wedge\cdots\wedge\left(\psi(z)-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}\langle\psi(z),\xi_{i}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{i}(z)\right)

due to the pointwise linear dependence of
\xi_{k}
and

z\mapsto\langle\psi,\xi_{k}\rangle_{E}\xi_{k}
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}.

Notice that
\left(\psi(z)-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}\langle\psi(z),\xi_{i}(z)\rangle_{E}\xi_{i}(z)\right)
is in
\Xi_{j},
thus

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\subset\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}.

The reverse inclusion holds by the definition of
\Xi_{j},
hence

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)=\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}.

Consequently, in order to prove the proposition it suffices to show that
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E).

By Proposition
4.6
,
\Xi_{j}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),
being a finite intersection of closed subspaces.

By Lemma
2.15
, for any
f\in\Xi_{j},
we get

\begin{array}[]{clllll}\|\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{j+2}E)}^{2}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|\xi_{0}(e^{i\theta})\wedge\xi_{1}(e^{i\theta})\wedge\cdots\wedge\xi_{j}(e^{i\theta})\wedge f(e^{i\theta})\|_{\wedge^{j+2}E}^{2}d\theta\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|f(e^{i\theta})-\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\xi_{i}(e^{i\theta})\rangle\xi_{i}(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}d\theta\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|f(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}d\theta=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}^{2}.\end{array}

Thus

(\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\cdot)\colon\Xi_{j}\to\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}

is an isometry.
Furthermore

(\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\cdot)\colon\Xi_{j}\to\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\xi_{1}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}

is a surjective mapping, thus
\Xi_{j}
and
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}
are isometrically isomorphic. Therefore, since
\Xi_{j}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),
the space
\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}\Xi_{j}
is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E).
Hence

\xi_{0}\dot{\wedge}\cdots\dot{\wedge}\xi_{j}\dot{\wedge}H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)

is a closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{j+2}E).

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space,
let
F
be a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
and let
X
be a subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).
The space

\Poc(X,F)=\{f\in F\;:\;f(z)\perp\{x(z):x\in X\}\;\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\}

is
a closed subspace of
F.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
4.6
, since
\Poc(X,F)
is
an intersection of closed subspaces

V_{x}=\{f\in F:\langle f(z),x(z)\rangle_{E}=0\;\;\text{for almost all }\;\;z\in\mathbb{T}\}
over

.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

By the generalized Fatou’s Theorem, for
E
a separable Hilbert space, the space
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
can be identified with a closed subspace of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},E).

Definition 4.10
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space. Let
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D},E),
for
1\leq p\leq\infty.

By the generalized Fatou’s Theorem (see
[
8
]
, p. 186), the radial limit

\lim_{r\to 1}f(re^{i\theta})\underset{\|\cdot\|_{E}}{=}\tilde{f}(e^{i\theta})\;\;(0<r<1)

exists almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and defines a function
\tilde{f}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T},E).
The set of points on
\mathbb{T}
at which the above limit does not exist, will be called the
singular set
of the function
f
and will be denoted by
N_{f}.

Note that the singular sets of functions in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D},E)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
are null sets with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
Let
F
be a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
and let
X
be a subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E).
We define the
pointwise linear span
of
X
in
F
to be the set

\Pls(X,F)=\{f\in F:f(z)\in\spn\{x(z):x\in X\}\;\text{for almost all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\}.

Recall Definition
4.1
, for a separable Hilbert space
E
and for
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
, the pointwise creation operator
C_{\xi}
is defined by

C_{\xi}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E),\ f\mapsto\xi\dot{\wedge}f,

where

(\xi\dot{\wedge}f)(z)=\xi(z)\wedge f(z)\mbox{ for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
For
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E),

\ker C_{\xi}\subset\Pls(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[]{clll}\ker C_{\xi}&=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):(\xi\dot{\wedge}f)(z)=0\;\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}\\
&=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\xi(z)\wedge f(z)=0\;\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}\\
&=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\xi(z),f(z)\;\text{are pointwise linearly dependent for all}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}\\
&\subset\Pls(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).\end{array}

∎

Example 4.13
.

Let
E=\mathbb{C}^{2}.
We can find functions
f,g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
such that
f\in\Poc(\{g\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E))
but it is false that
\langle f(z),g(z)\rangle_{E}=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}.

Choose

g(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z\\
z^{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad f(z)=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(z)\\
f_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then

f\in\Poc(\{g\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E))

is equivalent to

\langle{f}(z),{g}(z)\rangle_{E}=0\quad\text{for almost all}\quad z\in\mathbb{T}.

The later is equivalent to

\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}z\\
z^{2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}{f_{1}}(z)\\
{f_{2}}(z)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{E}=0\quad\text{for almost all}\quad z\in\mathbb{T},

which holds if and only if

\bar{z}{f_{1}}(z)+\bar{z}^{2}{f_{2}}(z)=0\quad\text{for almost all}\quad z\in\mathbb{T}.

Equivalently

{f_{1}}(z)=-\bar{z}{f_{2}}(z)\quad\text{for almost all}\quad z\in\mathbb{T},

which in turn is equivalent to

f(z)=\begin{pmatrix}{f_{1}}(z)\\
-z{f_{1}}(z).\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for almost all}\quad z\in\mathbb{T}.

Now, for
z\in\mathbb{D},

\begin{array}[]{clll}\langle f(z),g(z)\rangle_{E}&=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(z)\\
-zf_{1}(z)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}z\\
z^{2}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{E}\\
&=\bar{z}f_{1}(z)-\bar{z}|z|^{2}f_{1}(z)\\
&=\bar{z}(1-|z|^{2})f_{1}(z).\end{array}

So if we take
f_{1}(z)=1,f(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-z\end{pmatrix}

for
z\in\mathbb{D},
then
f\in\Poc(\{g\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E))
, but
\langle f(z),g(z)\rangle_{E}\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}.

Thus it is not true in general that
\Poc(\{g\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E))\subset\{g\}^{\perp}
.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space.
For
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E),

\Poc(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E))\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\ominus\Pls(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).

Proof.

Let
f\in\Poc(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).
This is equivalent to
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and

{f}(z)\perp{\xi}(z)\;\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\setminus(N_{f}\cup N_{\xi}),

where
N_{f},N_{\xi}
are the singular sets for the functions
f,\xi
respectively. This in turn is equivalent to
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and

\langle{f}(z),{\xi}(z)\rangle_{E}=0\;\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{T}\setminus(N_{f}\cup N_{\xi}).

The latter implies
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and

\langle{f}(z),{g}(z)\rangle_{E}=0\;\text{for almost all}\;\;z\in\mathbb{T}\;\text{for all}\;g\in\Pls(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).

Thus

f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\ominus\Pls(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).\qed

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
E
and
F
be separable Hilbert spaces, and let
G\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(F,E))
. For every
x\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
, the function
Gx
, defined almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
by

(Gx)(z)=G(z)(x(z)),

belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
.

Proof.

For almost all
z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\|(Gx)(z)\|_{E}=\|G(z)x(z)\|_{E}\leq\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(F,E))}\|x(z)\|_{F}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|Gx\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|Gx(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\ d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(F,E))}^{2}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{F}^{2}\ d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(F,E))}^{2}\|x\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},F)}<\infty.

∎

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space, and let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E).

Consider the adjoint operator

C_{\xi}^{*}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E).

(i)
For every
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
and
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
,

C_{\xi}^{*}(f\dot{\wedge}g)=P_{+}\alpha_{f},

where
\alpha_{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)
is defined by

\alpha_{f}(e^{i\theta})=\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}g(e^{i\theta})-\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}f(e^{i\theta})\quad

for all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}\setminus(N_{f}\cup N_{\xi}\cup N_{g}),
and

P_{+}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is the orthogonal projection.
Here
N_{f},N_{g},N_{\xi}
are the singular sets of the functions
f,g
and
\xi
respectively.

(ii)
Let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E)
be an inner function. Then, for any
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}g=P_{+}\alpha_{\xi},

where
\alpha_{\xi}=g-\xi\xi^{*}g
.
Moreover

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}g=g-T_{\xi\xi^{*}}g,

where
T_{\xi\xi^{*}}\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
is
the Toeplitz operator with symbol
\xi\xi^{*}
.

Proof.

(i) By Proposition
3.8
,
f\dot{\wedge}g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E).
Now, for all
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E),
and all
g,h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{cllll}\langle C_{\xi}^{*}(f\dot{\wedge}g),h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}&=\langle f\dot{\wedge}g,C_{\xi}h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)}\\
&=\langle f\dot{\wedge}g,\xi\dot{\wedge}h\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E)}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle f(e^{i\theta})\wedge g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\wedge h(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{\wedge^{2}E}\;d\theta,\end{array}

which, by Proposition
2.14
, is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}&\langle f(e^{i\theta}),h(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\\
\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}&\langle g(e^{i\theta}),h(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}\;d\theta.

The latter in turn is equal to

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\langle g(e^{i\theta}),h(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}-\langle f(e^{i\theta}),h(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\right)\;d\theta\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}g(e^{i\theta})-\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}f(e^{i\theta}),h(e^{i\theta})\right\rangle_{E}\;d\theta,\end{array}

which equals

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\langle\alpha_{f}(e^{i\theta}),h(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\;d\theta&=\langle\alpha_{f},h\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}\\
&=\langle P_{+}(\alpha_{f}),h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)},\end{array}

where

\alpha_{f}(e^{i\theta})=\langle f(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}g(e^{i\theta})-\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}f(e^{i\theta})

for all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}\setminus(N_{\xi}\cup N_{f}\cup N_{g}).
Hence
C_{\xi}^{*}(f\dot{\wedge}g)=P_{+}\alpha_{f}
as required.

Let us show (ii). By Part (i), for all
g,h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)
,

\begin{array}[]{cllll}\langle C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}g,h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}&=\langle C_{\xi}^{*}(\xi\dot{\wedge}g),h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}\\
&=\langle P_{+}(\alpha_{\xi}),h\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)},\end{array}

where

\alpha_{\xi}(e^{i\theta})=\langle\xi(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}g(e^{i\theta})-\langle g(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\xi(e^{i\theta})

for all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}\setminus(N_{\xi}\cup N_{g}\cup N_{h}).

Since
\xi
is inner,
\|\xi(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T},
and so,

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}g=P_{+}\alpha_{\xi},
where
\alpha_{\xi}=g-\xi\xi^{*}g.
Hence

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}g=P_{+}(g-\xi\xi^{*}g)=g-T_{\xi\xi^{*}}g,

where
T_{\xi\xi^{*}}g=P_{+}(\xi\xi^{*}g)
is the Toeplitz operator with symbol
\xi\xi^{*}
.
∎

Example 4.19
.

There exists an inner function
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
such that, for some

h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2}),

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}h
is not in the pointwise orthogonal complement of
\xi
in
E.

Let
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
be an inner function and let
h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2}).
Let
\xi,h
be given by

\xi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix},\quad h(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}\;\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

By Theorem
4.18
,

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}h=P_{+}\alpha,

where,
for all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\alpha(z)=\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}-\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix}.

Calculations yield, for all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\begin{array}[]{clllll}\alpha(z)&=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+\bar{z})\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+\bar{z}\\
z(1+\bar{z})\end{pmatrix}\end{array}\vskip 8.61108pt

\begin{array}[]{clll}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1-\bar{z}\\
1-z\end{pmatrix}.\end{array}

Thus

(P_{+}\alpha)(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1-z\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for all}\quad z\in\mathbb{T}.

The latter expression is not in the pointwise orthogonal complement of
\xi
in
\mathbb{C}^{2},
since for all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\begin{array}[]{clllll}\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1-z\end{pmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar{z}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1-z\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(1+\bar{z}-|z|^{2})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\bar{z}\neq 0.\par\end{array}

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
E
be a separable Hilbert space. For every inner function
\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},E),

\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\|C_{\xi}x\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)}=\|x\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}\}=\Poc(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).

Proof.

By Remark
4.4
, for every
x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E),

\|x\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}=\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}.
Hence

\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\|C_{\xi}x\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\wedge^{2}E)}^{2}=\|x\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)}^{2}\}=\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\|C_{\xi}x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\wedge^{2}E)}^{2}=\|x\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T},E)}^{2}\}.

By Proposition
2.14
, the latter set is equal to

\begin{array}[]{llll}&\left\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle\xi(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}&\langle\xi(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\\
\langle x(e^{i\theta}),\xi(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}&\langle x(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}\end{pmatrix}\;d\theta\right.\\
&\hskip 86.11084pt\left.=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right\}.\end{array}

Since
\xi
is inner,
\|\xi(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}=1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T},
hence the last set is equal to

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\left\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}-|\langle\xi(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|^{2})\;d\theta\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 86.11084pt=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|x(e^{i\theta})\|_{E}^{2}\;d\theta\right\}\\
&=\left\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle\xi(e^{i\theta}),x(e^{i\theta})\rangle_{E}|^{2}\;d\theta=0\right\}\\
&=\{x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E):{\xi}(e^{i\theta})\perp{x}(e^{i\theta})\;\text{almost everywhere on}\;\mathbb{T}\}\\
&=\Poc(\{\xi\},H^{2}(\mathbb{D},E)).\end{array}

∎

The following example illustrates the fact that, although
C_{\xi}
is an isometry on
\Poc\{\xi,H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})\},
it is not a partial isometry on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Example 4.21
.

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}
fails to be a projection for some inner function

\xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Let us calculate
C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}
for

\xi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix},\;z\in\mathbb{D}
.
By Theorem
4.18
, for
h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}h=P_{+}\alpha,

where, for all
z\in\mathbb{T},

\alpha(z)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{clll}\alpha(z)&=h(z)-\langle h(z),\xi(z)\rangle_{E}\xi(z)\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}(z)\\
h_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(h_{1}(z)+\bar{z}h_{2}(z))\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z\end{pmatrix}\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}&=\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}(z)-\frac{1}{2}(h_{1}(z)+\bar{z}h_{2}(z))\\
h_{2}(z)-\frac{1}{2}(zh_{1}(z)+h_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}(z)-\bar{z}h_{2}(z)\\
h_{2}(z)-zh_{1}(z)\end{pmatrix}.\par\end{array}

Thus

P_{+}\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}-S^{*}h_{2}\\
-Sh_{1}+h_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
S,S^{*}
denote the shift and the backward shift operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
respectively. Hence

C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-S^{*}\\
-S&1\end{pmatrix}.

(4.4)

By equation (
4.4
),

\begin{array}[]{cllllll}(C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi})^{2}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}1+S^{*}S&-2S^{*}\\
-2S&SS^{*}+1\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}2&-2S^{*}\\
-2S&2-P_{0}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-S^{*}\\
-S&1-\frac{1}{2}P_{0}\end{pmatrix}\neq C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi},\par\end{array}

since
SS^{*}=1-P_{0},
where

P_{0}(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n})=a_{0}.

Consequently
C_{\xi}^{*}C_{\xi}
is not a projection and hence
C_{\xi}
is not a partial isometry on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Toward a generalization of Lehmer’s problem to adelic curves
Mounir Hajli

Abstract
. In this short note, we investigate the generalization of Lehmer’s problem to finitely generated fields over ℚ \mathbb{Q} .



Keywords: Lehmer’s problem; adelic curves; heights

MSC: 11G50, 14G40.

1.
Introduction

Moriwaki
[
24
]

has developed an Arakelov height theory for varieties over a finitely generated extension of a number field. He applied his theory to the study of Bogomolov problem over finitely generated fields over
\mathbb{Q}
and obtained generalizations of results due to Ullmo
[
27
]
and Zhang
[
29
]
. As a corollary, he recovered the original Raynaud’s theorem (Manin-Mumford’s conjecture).

Using the height machinery developed in
[
24
]
, Vojta
[
28
]
extended the classic Roth’s
theorem to finitely generated fields. Burgos, Philippon and Sombra
[
9
]
have showed that the height of a variety over a finitely generated extension of
\mathbb{Q}
can be written as an integral of local heights over the set of places of the field. Using their previous work on toric varieties in

[
8
]
, they gave a combinatorial formula for the height of toric varieties over finitely generated fields over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Chen and Moriwaki
[
12
]
have introduced the theory of adelic curves. For example, a number field can be viewed as an example of adelic curve. They developed an arithmetic intersection theory on these curves. For instance, one can define a height function on the set of points of adelic curves. The Arakelov height theory of
[
24
]
is then seen as an arithmetic intersection theory in the sense of
[
12
]
.

We briefly recall the classical Lehmer’s problem (for details, see §
2
). It can be formulated as follows: There exists a constant
c>0
such that for every non zero algebraic number
\alpha
of degree
d
over
\mathbb{Q}
that is not a root of unity, we have

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{d}.

Lehmer’s question has not yet been settled. The best result in the direction of solving Lehmer’s problem is Dobrowolski’s result
[
18
]
(see (
2
)).

Our work is an attempt to generalize Lehmer’s problem to adelic curves. Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve and
K^{ac}
an algebraic
closure of
K
(see §
4
). A natural question arises: Is there a constant
c>0
such for every
\alpha\in K^{\mathrm{ac}}
with
h_{S}(\alpha)>0
, we have

h_{S}(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{[K(\alpha):K]}?

where

h_{S}
is the standard height function (see Paragraph
4.1
).

In this paper, we show that Lehmer’s problem generalizes to a natural adelic curve defined on the field of rational functions with coefficients in

\mathbb{Q}
. Let us denote by
S
the adelic curve on
\mathbb{Q}(T)
defined in Paragraph
4.2
. Our first main result is a generalization of Kronecker’s theorem to

S
. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1

(A Kronecker’s theorem)
.

\{\alpha\in(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)})^{\ast}\mid h_{S}(\alpha)=0\}=\{\text{roots of unity in}\ \overline{\mathbb{Q}}\}.

Our second main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assuming Lehmer’s conjecture (see Conjecture
2.1
below), then for every
\alpha
in an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}(T)
which is not a root of unity, we have

h_{S}(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{\deg(\alpha)},

where
\deg(\alpha)=[\mathbb{Q}(T)(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}(T)]
and
c
is an absolute positive constant.

(see Theorem
5.3
).

An interesting question is to explore the possibility of adapting Dobrowolski’s methods in our setting, which will be addressed in forthcoming papers.

Let us note that generalizations of Lehmer’s problem to higher-dimensional varieties have been explored by Amoroso and David (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
). As a continuation of this work, we propose two conjectures, namely Conjectures
1.3
and
1.4
below, which we plan to investigate in a forthcoming paper. Let
n
and
d
be two positive integers.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})}\right)
, such that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
are multiplicatively independent. We have

h_{S}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\geq\frac{c(n)}{\delta_{{\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})},

where
h_{S}
is the height associated with the adelic curve
\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
, and
\delta_{\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})}
is, by definition,
\deg F_{0}
where
F_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a non-zero polynomial that vanishes at
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and has minimal degree.

By Remark
5.2
, it is clear that this constitutes a natural generalization of Conjecture
2.4
due to Amoroso and David. Observe that more refined versions have been developed, taking into account the possibility that
\alpha
lies in a proper subgroup, see for instance,
[
16
,
10
,
11
]
.

Let
V
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
. Let us consider the normalized height
\hat{h}_{S}(V)
of
V
, which can be defined using methods of
[
12
]
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Assume that
V
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
and that it is not an union of torsion subvarieties. Then,

\hat{h}_{S}(V)\geq c(n)\,.

where
c(n)
is a positive constant which does not depend on
V
.

Note that if
V
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then one can show that
\widehat{h}_{S}(V)=m(f)
, where
m(f)
is the Mahler measure of an equation
f
defining
V
(cf. §
2
for details). Conjecture
1.4
then reduces to Conjecture
2.3
.

Organization of the paper
: In §
2
, we provide the definition of the usual Weil height function on
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and recall the classical Lehmer’s problem along with some results concerning this problem. In §
3
, we review the definition of the canonical height on Drinfel’d modules and briefly summarize some results concerning Lehmer’s problem in this context. In §
4
, we introduce the theory of adelic curves and discuss the extension of Lehmer’s problem to this setting. Finally, in §
5
, we prove our main results on Lehmer’s problem on the adelic curve
\mathbb{Q}(T)
.

2.
Lehmer’s problem on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

We denote by
\mathbb{Q}
the field of rational numbers,
\mathbb{Z}
the ring of rational integers,
K
a number field,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its ring of integers, and
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
. We denote by
\mathbb{R}
the field of real numbers and
\mathbb{C}
the field of complex numbers. We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of
natural numbers including
0
.

For each rational prime
p
, we denote by
|\cdot|_{p}
the
p
-adic absolute value on
\mathbb{Q}
such that
|p|_{p}=p^{-1}
; we also denote by
|\cdot|_{\infty}
or simply
|\cdot|
the standard absolute value. These form a complete set of absolute values on
\mathbb{Q}
: we identify the set
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
of these absolute values with the set
\{\infty,p\mid p\text{ prime}\}
. More generally, we denote by
M_{K}
the set of places of
K
extending those of
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
x\in K
, we define the absolute value
|x|_{v}=|N_{K_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}}(x)|_{v}^{1/[K_{\nu}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}
for each
v\in M_{K}
, where

[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]
is the local degree at
v
. We have the product formula

\prod_{v\in M_{K}}|x|_{v}^{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}=1,

for all
x\in K\setminus\{0\}
(cf.
[
22
, Chapter V]
).

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers, and
M_{K}
be the set of places. Let
N
be a positive integer. Let
\mathbb{P}^{n}
be the projective space of dimension
N
. Let

P=[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{N}(K)
. We define the absolute logarithmic height of the point
P
as

h(P)=\sum_{v\in M_{K}}\frac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\log\max(|x_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|x_{n}|_{v}),

which is independent of the choice of the projective coordinates by the product formula. The height
h(P)
is independent of the choice of the base field
K
(cf.
[
21
, Chapter 3]
). This defines the usual Weil height on

\mathbb{P}^{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. We define its Mahler measure by setting
m(0)=0
and

m(f)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|f(e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{n}})|\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{n},

if
f\neq 0
. Let
f
be a nonzero polynomial with complex coefficients in one variable such that
f(x)=a\prod_{i}(x-\alpha_{i})
. By Jensen’s formula, the Mahler measure of
f
can be expressed as:

(1)

m(f)=\log|a|+\sum_{i}\log\max(1,|\alpha_{i}|).

If
f
has integer coefficients, is irreducible, and has a positive degree
d
, then (
1
) shows that
m(f)
is nonnegative, and

h(\alpha)=\frac{m(f)}{d},

where
\alpha
is a root of
f
.
A classical theorem of Kronecker asserts that when
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, we have
m(f)=0
if and only if
|a|=1
and

\alpha_{i}
are zero or roots of the unity. Boyd, Lawton, and Smyth independently showed (cf.
[
7
,
23
,
26
]
) that the Mahler measure of an irreducible polynomial

f\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],

with
f\neq\pm x_{j}
, is equal to
0
if and only if the zero set of
f
is a torsion variety.

Let us point out that the case of hypersurfaces is particularly interesting. The notion of normalized height is linked to the Mahler measure of one of its equations. More precisely, let
V
be a hypersurface of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
f=0
be one of its equations with integer coefficients of content 1. The
normalized height
of
V
for the projective embedding that we have fixed,
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
, is nothing other than the Mahler measure of the polynomial
f
:

\widehat{h}(V)=m(f).

(cf.
[
15
, Proposition 2.1(vii)]
).

Lehmer asked whether there exists a constant
c>0
such that for every polynomial

f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
with
m(f)>0
, then
m(f)\geq c
.

Lehmer’s question can thus be translated into the following two equivalent conjectures:

Conjecture 2.1
.

There exists a real number
c>0
such that for every algebraic number
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
of degree
d
over
\mathbb{Q}
that is not a root of unity, we have

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{d}.

Conjecture 2.2
.

There exists a real number
c>0
such that for every irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
f\neq\pm x
, which is not a cyclotomic polynomial, we have

m(f)\geq c.

Within the framework of Conjectures
2.1
and
2.2
, the best known result to date is Dobrowolski’s lower bound (see
[
18
]
): There exists a constant
c
such that for every
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
, an algebraic number of degree
d
, and is not a root of unity, we have

(2)

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{d}\left(\frac{\log(\log(3d))}{\log(3d)}\right)^{3}.

This inequality can obviously be reformulated in polynomial language:

m(f)\geq c\left(\frac{\log(\log(3d))}{\log(3d)}\right)^{3}

for any irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
f\neq\pm x
, of degree
d
, which is not a cyclotomic polynomial.

Analogues of Conjectures
2.1
and
2.2
have been considered in the context of elliptic curves (cf.
[
13
,
20
]
).

Let us note that generalizations of Lehmer’s problem to higher-dimensional varieties have been explored by Amoroso and David (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
). For instance, they proposed the following conjectures.

Conjecture 2.3
.

For every positive integer
n
, there exists a constant
c(n)>0
such that for any irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
f\neq\pm x_{j}
, which is not a generalized cyclotomic polynomial, we have

m(f)\geq c(n).

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{G}_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{n}
. Let
F_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial that vanishes at
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and has minimal degree. Amoroso and David
[
2
]
defined the
obstruction index
of the point
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
as the quantity
\deg F_{0}
, which they denote by
\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. Amoroso and David noted that

1\leq\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\leq nD^{1/n},

where
D=[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}):\mathbb{Q}]
.

Conjecture 2.4
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. There then exists a real number
c(n)>0
such that for all
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, such that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
are multiplicatively independent, we have

h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\geq\frac{c(n)}{\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}.

Amoroso and David
[
1
]
adapted the method of Dobrowolski in order to address Conjecture
2.4
. Their work yielded the following result:

h(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\geqslant\frac{c(n)}{\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\log(3\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha}))^{-\kappa(n)},

where
\kappa(n)=(n+1)(n+1)!^{n}-n
(cf.
[
1
, Théorème 1.5]
).

3.
Lehmer’s problem for a Drinfel’d module

Let
p
be a prime number and
n
a positive integer. We put
q:=p^{n}
, and we denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field of cardinal
q
.
We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
the ring of polynomials in one variable with coefficients in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, by
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
its field of fractions, and by
K_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}((1/T))
the completion of
K
with respect to the
(1/T)
-adic valuation
v
. This valuation
v
is extended to an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
(respectively,
\overline{K}_{\infty}
) of
K
(respectively,
K_{\infty}
). We also denote by
\iota
the canonical homomorphism from
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
to
\overline{K}
.

We have the usual Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\overline{K})
. If
L
is a finite extension of
K
of degree
m
, the height of a point
P=[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{N}]
of
\mathbb{P}^{N}(L)
is given by:

h(P)=\sum_{m}\frac{d(w)}{m}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\left(-w(x_{i})\right),

where the sum is extended over all places of
L
,
d(w)
denotes the residual degree at the place
w
, normalized by
w(L)=\mathbb{Z}\cup\{+\infty\}
. This height is independent of the field
L
chosen as shown by the usual properties of valuations (cf.
[
21
]
).

We define the height
h(\alpha)
of an element
\alpha
of
\overline{K}
as the height
h([1:\alpha])
of the point
[1:\alpha]
. That is, we embed
\overline{K}
into
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and consider the Weil height relative to this embedding.

Let
\overline{K}\{\tau\}
be the ring of twisted polynomials over
\overline{K}
. For every
f:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}\in\overline{K}\{\tau\}
, we define its derivation
D
at
0
by
D(f):=a_{0}
. An homomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\to\overline{K}\{\tau\}
is called a
Drinfel’d
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-module
defined over
\overline{K}
if
D\circ\phi=\iota
and there exists
P\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
such that
\phi(P)\neq\iota(P)\tau^{0}
. According to
[
19
, Chap. 4, Lemma 4.5.1]
, there exists an integer
r>0
such that
\deg(\phi(P))=r\deg(P)
, for all
P\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. The integer
r
is called the
rank
of
\phi
.

Denis
[
17
]
defined the global height of the Drinfel’d module
\phi
at
\alpha\in\overline{K}
as follows:

\hat{h}_{\phi}(\alpha)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(\phi(P^{n})(\alpha))}{q^{nr}},

where
P
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
of positive degree
r
. This limit does not depend on the choice of
P
(cf.
[
17
, Théorème 1]
). Furthermore, Denis
[
17
, Théorème 2]
established an analogue of Dobrowolski’s result
[
18
]
when
\phi
is a Carlitz module.

David and Pacheco
[
14
]
solved the so-called abelian version of the Lehmer’s problem in the context of Drinfel’d modules. It is worth noting that the classical version of this problem, concerning non-torsion points in
\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{Q}^{\text{ab}})
, was solved by Amoroso and Dvornicich
[
4
]
. The analogue of this result for abelian varieties was given by Baker and
Silverman, see
[
5
,
6
,
25
]
.

4.
Adelic curves

In this section, we briefly recall the theory of adelic curves. Let
K
be a commutative ring and
M_{K}
be the set of all absolute values on
K
.
Following
[
12
]
, an adelic structure on
K
consists of a measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu)
equipped with a map
\phi:\omega\mapsto|\cdot|_{\omega}
from

\Omega
to
M_{K}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{A}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega
and
\nu
is a measure on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
;

(2)

for any
a\in K^{\ast}:=K\setminus\{0\}
, the function

\omega\mapsto\log|a|_{\omega}
is
\mathcal{A}
-measurable, integrable with respect to
the measure
\nu
.

This data is denoted by
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\varphi)
.

S
is said to be proper if the equality

\int_{\Omega}\log|a|_{\omega}d\nu(\omega)=0,

holds for each
a\in K^{\ast}
.

Number fields and function fields are fundamental examples of proper adelic curves
(cf.
[
12
, p. 170]
). Note that the properness of these curves corresponds to the product formula.

From now on,
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
is a proper adelic curve and
K^{ac}
an algebraic
closure of
K
. Let
S\otimes_{K}K^{ac}=({K^{\mathrm{ac}}},(\Omega_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}},\mathcal{A}_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}},\nu_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}}),\phi_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}})
be the algebraic extension of
S
by
{K^{\mathrm{ac}}}
(see
[
12
, Paragraph 3.4.2]
for the construction).

4.1.
Height of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(K^{\mathrm{ac}})

For
[a_{0}:\ldots:a_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}({K^{\mathrm{ac}}})
,
the height of
[a_{0}:\ldots:a_{n}]
with respect to the adelic curve
S
is given as follows.

h_{S}([a_{0}:\ldots:a_{n}])=\int_{\Omega^{ac}}\log\max(|a_{0}|_{\chi},\ldots,|a_{n}|_{\chi})\nu_{K^{\mathrm{ac}}}(d\chi),

see
[
12
, Paragraph 3.5]
for more details about the construction.

For every
\alpha\in K^{\textrm{ac}}
, we set

(3)

h_{S}(\alpha):=h_{S}([1:\alpha]).

Lemma 4.1
.

The following set

\{\alpha\in(K^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\ast}\mid h_{S}(\alpha)=0\},

is a subgroup of
(K^{\mathrm{ac}})^{\ast}
.

Proof.

This follows from the following inequality

\max(1,xy)\leq\max(1,x)\max(1,y),\quad\forall x,y\geq 0.

and the product formula (i.e. the properness of
S
).

∎

4.2.
The adelic structure on
\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be the field of rational functions of
d
variables
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
and with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.

\mathbb{Q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
can be endowed with an interesting adelic structure, see
[
12
, Paragraph 3.2.6]
, or
[
24
, Section 3]

and
[
9
, Example 2.5]
for a more explicit construction. Let us review in detail
the case when
d=1
.

Let
\Omega=\Omega_{h}\coprod\mathcal{P}\coprod[0,1]_{\ast}
, where
\Omega_{h}
is the set of all closed points of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
, and
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set all prime numbers, and

[0,1]_{\ast}
the set of
t\in[0,1]
such that
e^{2\pi it}
is transcendental.

Let
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow M_{K}
be the map sending
\omega\in\Omega
to
|\cdot|_{\omega}
, defined as follows. Let
x\in\Omega_{h}
. Then,
x
defines a discrete valuation on
K
which is denoted by
\mathrm{ord}_{x}(\cdot)
. Let
F_{x}
be an irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[T]
with
F_{x}(x)=0
. Let
H(x)
be the Mahler measure of the polynomial
F_{x}
. It is defined as follows.

H(x):=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{1}\log|F_{x}(e^{2\pi it})|dt\right).

Let
|\cdot|_{x}
be the absolute value on
\mathbb{Q}(T)
such that

|\varphi|_{x}=H(x)^{-\mathrm{ord}_{x}(\varphi)},\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathbb{Q}(T).

For
p
a prime number, let
|\cdot|_{p}
be
p
-adic value on
\mathbb{Q}
. It extends to
\mathbb{Q}[T]
as follows.

|\varphi|_{p}:=\max_{j=0,\ldots,d}|a_{j}|_{p},\quad\forall\varphi=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}T^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[T],

and then, by multiplicativity, to
\mathbb{Q}(T)
.

For any
t\in[0,1]_{\ast}
, let
|\cdot|_{t}
be the absolute value on
\mathbb{Q}(T)
such that

|\varphi|_{t}:=|\varphi(e^{2\pi it})|,\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathbb{Q}(T),

where
|\cdot|
denotes the usual absolute value of
\mathbb{C}
.

\Omega_{h}
and
\mathcal{P}
are equipped with the discrete
\sigma
-algebras, and
[0,1]_{\ast}

with the restriction of the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
[0,1]
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega
generated by the above
\sigma
-algebras on
\Omega_{h},\mathcal{P}
and

[0,1]_{\ast}
respectively. Let
\nu
be the measure on
\Omega
such that

\nu(\{x\})=1
for
x\in\Omega_{h}
, that
\nu(\{p\})=1
for any prime number
p
and that the restriction of
\nu
on
[0,1]_{\ast}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure.

This data defines an adelic structure on
\mathbb{Q}(T)
. Moreover,
(\mathbb{Q}(T),(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)

is proper (cf.
[
12
, Paragraph 3.2.5]
).

4.3.
On Lehmer’s problem on adelic curves

Motivated by the previous work on Lehmer’s problem, it is natural to consider the following question:

Question 4.2
.

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve and
K^{ac}
an algebraic
closure of
K
. Is there a constant
c:=c_{S}>0
such for every
\alpha\in K^{\mathrm{ac}}
with
h_{S}(\alpha)>0
, we have

h_{S}(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{[K(\alpha):K]}?

When
K
is
\mathbb{Q}
and
S
is the classical adelic curve associated with
\mathbb{Q}
, then this is the classical Lehmer’s problem.

5.
Lehmer’s problem on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)}

In this section, we show that the classical Lehmer’s problem generalizes naturally to the adelic curve
\mathbb{Q}(T)
.

Let
S
be the adelic curve on
\mathbb{Q}(T)
recalled in Paragraph
4.2
. Let

[\varphi:\psi]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)}\right).

We may assume that
\varphi
and
\psi
are integral over
\mathbb{Z}[T]
. Then, there exist

d,e\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{1}(T),\ldots f_{d}(T)
and
g_{1}(T),\ldots,g_{e}(T)
in
\mathbb{Z}[T]
such that

\varphi^{d}+f_{1}(T)\varphi^{d-1}+\ldots+f_{d-1}(T)\varphi+f_{d}(T)=0,

and

\psi^{e}+g_{1}(T)\varphi^{e-1}+\ldots+g_{e-1}(T)\psi+g_{e}(T)=0.

Let

c_{\varphi,\psi}:=\gcd(c(f_{d})^{e},c(g_{e})^{d}).

where
c(f_{d})
(resp.
c(g_{e})
) is the common divisor of the coefficients of
f_{d}
(resp.
g_{e}
).

If
\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\mid g_{e}(x)=f_{d}(x)=0\}
is not empty, we let

F(T):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\\
g_{e}(x)=f_{d}(x)=0\end{subarray}}F_{x}(T)^{\min(\mathrm{ord}_{x}(f_{d}^{e}),\mathrm{ord}_{x}(g_{e}^{d}))}\,,

with
F_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
with coprime coefficients, irreducible in
\mathbb{Q}[T]
and
F_{x}(x)=0
for
x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Otherwise, we let

F(T)=1.

By the product formula, we have

h_{S}([\varphi:\psi])=h_{S}\Bigl(\Bigl[\tfrac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}:\tfrac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr]\Bigr).

Let
K^{\prime}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}(T)
such that
\varphi
and
\psi
are in
K^{\prime}
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega_{K^{\prime}}
with
\omega\mid x
where
x\in\Omega_{h}
. If
x
is a common zero of
f_{e}
and
g_{d}
, then

\begin{split}\log\left|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega}=&\frac{1}{d}\log|f_{d}|_{x}-\frac{1}{de}\log|F|_{x}\\
=&-\frac{\mathrm{ord}_{x}(f_{d}(T))}{d}\log H(x)+\frac{\min(\mathrm{ord}_{x}(f_{d}(T)^{e}),\mathrm{ord}_{x}(g_{e}(T)^{d}))}{de}\log H(x).\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\log\left|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{d}}}\right|_{\omega}=&\frac{1}{e}\log|g_{e}|_{x}-\frac{1}{de}\log|F|_{x}\\
=&-\frac{\mathrm{ord}_{x}(g_{e}(T))}{e}\log H(x)+\frac{\min(\mathrm{ord}_{x}(f_{d}(T)^{e}),\mathrm{ord}_{x}(g_{e}(T)^{d}))}{de}\log H(x).\end{split}

We conclude that

\max\left(\left|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega},\left|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega}\right)=1

This equation holds if
x
is not a common zero of
f_{e}
and
g_{d}
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
with
\omega\mid p
and
p\in\mathcal{P}
. We have

\left|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega}=\frac{|f_{d}(T)|_{p}^{\frac{1}{d}}}{|(c_{\varphi,\psi})^{\frac{1}{de}}|_{p}}=\left(\frac{|c(f_{d})^{e}|_{p}}{|c_{\varphi,\psi}|_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{de}},

and

\left|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega}=\frac{|g_{e}(T)|_{p}^{\frac{1}{e}}}{|(c_{\varphi,\psi})^{\frac{1}{de}}|_{p}}=\left(\frac{|c(g_{e})^{d}|_{p}}{|c_{\varphi,\psi}|_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{de}}.

We conclude that

\max\left(\left|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega},\left|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right|_{\omega}\right)=1.

Case of
w\in\Omega
with
w\mid t
and
t\in[0,1]_{\ast}
.
Note that
K^{\prime}_{\omega}=\mathbb{Q}(T)_{t}=\mathbb{C}
. We have then

\begin{split}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\Omega\\
w\mid t,\ t\in[0,1]_{\ast}\end{subarray}}\log\max\Bigl(&\Bigl|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr|_{\omega},\Bigl|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr|_{\omega}\Bigr)d\nu(\omega)\\
=&\int_{t\in[0,1]_{\ast}}\frac{1}{[K^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}(T)]}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in K^{\prime}\\
w\mid t\end{subarray}}\log\max\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr|_{\omega},\Bigl|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr|_{\omega}\Bigr)\right)dt.\end{split}

Gathering the above computations, we obtain that

\frac{1}{[K^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}(T)]}\max\Bigl(\int_{t\in[0,1]_{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in K^{\prime}\\
w\mid t\end{subarray}}\log\Bigl|\frac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr|_{\omega}dt,\int_{t\in[0,1]_{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in K^{\prime}\\
w\mid t\end{subarray}}\log\Bigl|\frac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\Bigr|_{\omega}dt\Bigr)\leq h_{S}([\varphi:\psi]).

Using
[
12
, Lemma 3.5.4]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{[K^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}(T)]}\max\Bigl(\int_{t\in[0,1]_{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in K^{\prime}\\
w\mid t\end{subarray}}\log\Bigl|\mathrm{N}_{K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(T)}\left(\tfrac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right)\Bigr|_{\omega}dt,\int_{t\in[0,1]_{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in K^{\prime}\\
w\mid t\end{subarray}}&\log\Bigl|\mathrm{N}_{K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(T)}\left(\tfrac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right)\Bigr|_{\omega}dt\Bigr)\\
&\leq[K^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}(T)]h_{S}([\varphi:\psi]).\end{split}

Hence

(4)

\begin{split}\max\Bigl(\int_{t\in[0,1]}\log\Bigl|\mathrm{N}_{K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(T)}\left(\tfrac{\varphi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right)\Bigr|_{t}dt,\int_{t\in[0,1]}&\log\Bigl|\mathrm{N}_{K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(T)}\left(\tfrac{\psi}{(c_{\varphi,\psi}F)^{\frac{1}{de}}}\right)\Bigr|_{t}dt\Bigr)\\
&\leq[K^{\prime}:\mathbb{Q}(T)]h_{S}([\varphi:\psi]).\end{split}

Theorem 5.1

(A Kronecker’s theorem)
.

\{\alpha\in(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)})^{\ast}\mid h_{S}(\alpha)=0\}=\{\text{roots of unity in}\ \overline{\mathbb{Q}}\}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be two coprime polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[T]
. We have

h_{S}([\varphi:\psi])=\int_{0}^{1}\log\max(|\varphi(e^{2\pi it})|,|\psi(e^{2\pi it})|)dt.

Let us assume that
h_{S}([\varphi:\psi])=0
. This implies that

\varphi(z),\psi(z),\ \text{and}\ \varphi(z)+y\psi(z)

are generalized cyclotomic polynomials in the variables
y,z
(see for instance
[
7
]
). We have used the following fact:

\int_{0}^{1}\log\max(|\varphi(e^{2\pi it})|,|\psi(e^{2\pi it})|)dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\log|\varphi(e^{2\pi it})+e^{2\pi is}\psi(e^{2\pi it})|dtds.

It is not difficult to show that

\phi
and
\psi
are, in fact, roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)}
with
h_{S}(\alpha)=0
. Using (
4
), we can conclude that
\alpha
is a root of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

∎

Let
\varphi\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}(T)}
. Let
d:=[\mathbb{Q}(T)(\varphi):\mathbb{Q}(T)]
. We can assume

[1:\varphi]=[\varphi_{1}:\varphi_{2}],

with
\varphi_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
and
\varphi_{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}[T]}
(integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}[T]
).

From (
4
) and assuming that Lehmer’s problem is true, we get

h_{S}(\varphi)(=h_{S}([\varphi_{1}:\varphi_{2}]))\geq\frac{c}{\deg(\varphi)},

where
\deg\varphi=[\mathbb{Q}(T)(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}(T)]
and
c
is the constant in Lehmer’s problem.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. It is clear that

h_{S}(\alpha)=h(\alpha),

where
h
is the standard Weil height of
\alpha
.

We conclude that

Theorem 5.3
.

Lehmer’s problem is equivalent to Lehmer’s problem on
\mathbb{Q}(T)
.

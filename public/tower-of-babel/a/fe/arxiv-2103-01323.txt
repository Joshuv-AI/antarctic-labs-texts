Estimate of Heat Kernel for Euler-Maruyama Scheme of SDEs Driven by α \alpha -Stable Noise and Applications Supported in part by NNSFC (11801406).
Xing Huang, Yongqiang Suo, Chenggui Yuan

Abstract
In this paper, the discrete parametrix method is adopted to investigate the estimation of heat kernel for Euler-Maruyama scheme of SDEs driven by α \alpha -stable noise, which implies Krylov’s estimate and Khasminskii’s estimate. As an application, the convergence rate of Euler-Maruyama scheme for a class of multidimensional SDEs with singular drift (in aid of Zvonkin’s transformation) is obtained.



AMS subject Classification: 60H10, 34K26, 39B72.

Keywords: Zvonkin’s transformation, Euler-Maruyama scheme, Heat kernel, Krylov’s estimate.

Introduction

We consider the following
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued stochastic differential equation (SDE for short)

(1.1)

\displaystyle X_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\text{\rm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s-})\text{\rm{d}}L_{s},

where
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are measurable functions, and
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a general
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Lévy process defined on a complete filtration probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
.

If coefficients
b
and
f
are Lipschitz continuous, the existence and uniqueness of strong solution to (
1.1
) is established by the Picard iteration. Moreover, SDE (
1.1
) can also be numerically solved with the Euler-Maruyama (EM for short) scheme, see
[
18
]
and references therein.

When the coefficients
b
and
f
are irregular, there is a great interest in investigating pathwise uniqueness for SDE (
1.1
) in the past decades. A useful method in this direction is Zvonkin’s transformation which was introduced in
[
32
]
. This method has been applied to various SDEs, see e.g.
[
8
,
10
,
14
,
19
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
and references therein. Furthermore, Zvonkin’s transformation has also been applied to investigate the convergence rate of EM scheme for SDEs, we refer to
[
2
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
for more details. There are also other transformation methods applied to EM scheme, see
[
20
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

In the continuous case,
L_{s}=as+\sigma W_{s}
, where
(W_{s})_{\{s\geq 0\}}
is a Brownin motion.
[
22
]
establishes the existence and uniqueness result and numerical scheme for (
1.1
) in the one-dimensional case, the drift therein is piecewise Lipschitz. Their proof is based on a transformation, which globally transform the piecewise Lipschitz drifts into Lipschitz ones.
[
23
,
24
]
present a transformation for the multidimensional case which allows to prove an existence and uniqueness result for
d
-dimensional SDEs with discontinuous drift and degenerate diffusion coefficients. Compared with the Zvonkin’s transformation, the transformation in
[
23
,
24
]
does not have to solve a system of parabolic partial differential equations in each step. Recently, the strong convergence rate of EM scheme for SDEs with integrable drift is obtained by the first author with his co-authors in
[
3
]
, and the proof is based on “parametrix method”, which was introduced to obtain existence and estimate on the fundamental solutions of PDEs, see
[
16
,
21
]
.

For a general Lévy process,
[
12
]
shows that the EM scheme converges strongly with convergence rate
\frac{1}{2}
for (
1.1
) with additive noise and a one-sided Lipschitz continuous drift
b
.
[
25
]
establishes the convergence rate for SDEs with Hölder continuous coefficients driven by Brownian motion and by truncated
\alpha
-stable processes with index
\alpha>1
.
Moreover,
[
20
]
studies the strong convergence of the EM scheme for a large class of SDEs driven by Lévy processes such as isotropic
\alpha
-stable, relative stable, layered stable processes, whose proofs rely on the so-called Itô-Tanaka trick which relates to the time average
\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\text{\rm{d}}s
. Under the assumption that the drift is Hölder continuous, the EM scheme for stochastic functional differential equations with
\alpha
-stable noise is shown in
[
13
]
. It is worth noting that the drifts of SDEs in these literature are assumed to be Hölder(-Dini) continuous or piecewise Lipschitz continuous.
However, essential difficulty comes up when the drifts only belong to some Sobolev space. More precisely, it is not easy to obtain the estimate like

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{T}b(X^{(\delta)}_{t})-b(X^{(\delta)}_{t_{\delta}})\text{\rm{d}}t\right|^{q}\leq\Phi(\delta)

for some function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with
\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\Phi(\delta)=0
, here,
X^{(\delta)}_{t}
stands for the solution to the numerical SDEs and
b
is the singular drift,
t_{\delta}:=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\delta
, and
\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor

denotes the integer part of
t/\delta
.

In this work, we consider the case where

(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion subordinated by a subordinator, i.e.

(1.2)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}W_{S_{t}},~~~t\geq 0,\ \ X_{0}=x.

Herein,
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(W_{S_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
is a rotationally invariant
d

\alpha
-stable process, with the Lévy measure
\nu(\text{\rm{d}}z)=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\text{\rm{d}}z
for some constant
c_{\alpha}>0
. Note that
W_{S_{t}}
is composed of processes
W_{t}
and
S_{t}
, where

(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on some complete

\alpha/2

has the same law as

2
Assumptions and Main results

To state our main result, we first introduce some notation and facts of Sobolev space, which can be found in
[
26
]
.

For
(p,\gamma)\in[1,\infty]\times[0,2]
, let
H_{p}^{\gamma}:=(I-\Delta)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be the usual Bessel potential space with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\gamma,p}:=\|(I-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f\|_{p}\asymp\|f\|_{p}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f\|_{p},

where
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is the usual
L^{p}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
(I-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f
and
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f
are defined by the Fourier transformation

(I-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\mathcal{F}f),\ \ (-\Delta)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{\gamma}\mathcal{F}f).

For
p=\infty,\gamma=1
, we define
H_{\infty}^{1}
as the space of Lipschitz functions with norm

\|f\|_{1,\infty}:=\|f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}.

In the sequel, we introduce the Sobolev embedding and an important inequality on the norm of elements in
H_{p}^{\gamma}
, which will be used to construct the Zvonkin transformation and obtain the priori estimate of solution to the associated elliptic equation.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\gamma\in[0,2]
,

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &H_{p}^{\gamma}\subset L^{q},\>\>q\in\left[p,\frac{dp}{d-\gamma p}\right],\>&\gamma p<d;\\
&H_{p}^{\gamma}\subset H_{\infty}^{\gamma-\frac{d}{p}}\subset C_{b}^{\gamma-\frac{d}{p}},\>&\gamma p>d,\end{aligned}\right.

where
C_{b}^{\beta}
is the usual Hölder space.
Moreover, for
\gamma\in[0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty]
, there exists a constant
c
such that for all
f\in H_{p}^{\gamma}
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot+z)-f(\cdot)\|_{p}\leq c(|z|^{\gamma}\wedge 1)\|f\|_{\gamma,p}.

Throughout the paper, we impose the following assumptions on the drift
b
.

(A1)

\|b\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|b(x)|<\infty
.

(A2)

There exist constants
\beta\in(1-\frac{\alpha}{2},1)
and
p>(\frac{2d}{\alpha}\vee 2)

such that
b\in H_{p}^{\beta}
.

Under
{\bf(A2)}
, (
1.2
) has a unique strong
solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, (see, for instance,
[
28
, Theorem 2.4]
).

The EM scheme corresponding to (
1.2
) is defined as follows:
for any
\delta\in(0,1),

(2.3)

\text{\rm{d}}X^{(\delta)}_{t}=b(X^{(\delta)}_{t_{\delta}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}W_{S_{t}},\ \ t\geq 0,~~~X^{(\delta)}_{0}=X_{0}

with
t_{\delta}:=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\delta
, where
\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor

denotes the integer part of
t/\delta
. We emphasize that

(X^{(\delta)}_{k\delta})_{k\geq 0}
is a homogeneous Markov process. For
t>s
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

p^{(\delta)}(s,x;t,\cdot)
denotes the transition density of
X^{(\delta)}_{t}
with the starting point
X_{s}^{(\delta)}=x
.

Let
p_{\alpha}(t,x)
be the density of
W_{S_{t}}
.
Our first main result gives an explicit upper bound of the transition
kernel
p^{(\delta)}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under
{\bf(A1)}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.4)

p^{(\delta)}(j\delta,x;t,y)\leq Cp_{\alpha}(t-j\delta,{y-x}),~~~x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~~t>j\delta,~~\delta\in(0,1).

As an application of Theorem
2.1
, the rate of strong convergence for EM Scheme (
2.3
) can be obtained as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
(A1)
-
(A2)
. Then, for

\eta\in(0,2)
, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)

(2.5)

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}-X_{t}^{(\delta)}|^{\eta}\Big)\leq C_{1}2^{C_{2}(1+\|b\|^{\frac{2\alpha p}{\alpha p-2d}}_{\beta,p})}\left(\delta^{\frac{\eta\beta}{\alpha}}1_{\{2\beta<\alpha\}}+\mathbb{E}(S_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\epsilon})\delta^{\frac{\eta}{2}\epsilon}1_{\{2\beta\geq\alpha\}}\right).

Remark 2.3
.

(1)

Under certain balance condition, and that the drift

is a bounded function and is Hölder continuous with respect to space, time variable, respectively, the rate of strong convergence of EM scheme (

) (inhomogenous case) is established in

Notation and Preliminaries

Through the paper we use the following notation:

The letter
C,c
with or without subscripts will denote positive constants, whose value may change in different places. We write
f(x)\asymp g(x)
to mean that there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that
C_{1}g(x)\leq f(x)\leq C_{2}g(x)
,
f(x)\vee g(x)=\max\{f(x),g(x)\}
, and
f(x)\wedge g(x)=\min\{f(x),g(x)\}
.

Let
\eta_{t}
be the density of
S_{t}
for
t>0
. It follows from
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
that the density of
W_{S_{t}}
has the following expression

(3.1)

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2s}}\eta_{t}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s\asymp t(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{-d-\alpha},~(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The following inequality will be used frequently:

(3.2)

\displaystyle(t^{1/\alpha}+|x+z|)^{-\gamma}\leq 4^{\gamma}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{-\gamma},~~\gamma\geq 0,~~|z|\leq(2t^{1/\alpha})\vee(|x|/2).

Moreover, according to
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
, it is clear that

(3.3)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}p_{\alpha}(t,x)|\leq Ct(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{-d-\alpha-k}\leq Ct^{-k/\alpha}p_{\alpha}(t,x),\ \ k\in\mathbb{N},

where
\nabla^{k}
stands for the
k
th-order gradient with respect to the spatial variable
x
.

According to the Markov property of
W_{S_{t}}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\alpha}(t-r,x^{\prime}-y)p_{\alpha}(r-s,y-x)\text{\rm{d}}y=p_{\alpha}(t-s,x^{\prime}-x).

In addition, for any
p\geq 1
, (
3.1
) implies that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.5)

\begin{split}\|p_{\alpha}(t,\cdot)\|_{p}&\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Ct^{p}(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{-pd-p\alpha}\text{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Ct^{p}t^{-pd/\alpha-p\alpha/\alpha}(1+|x|/t^{1/\alpha})^{-pd-p\alpha}\text{\rm{d}}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Ct^{p}t^{-pd/\alpha-p\alpha/\alpha}t^{d/\alpha}(1+|y|)^{-pd-p\alpha}\text{\rm{d}}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq Ct^{-d/\alpha+d/(\alpha p)}.\end{split}

Recall that
\eta_{t}
is the density of
S_{t}
. Let

\Theta(r)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left((2\pi S_{1})^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{r}{2S_{1}}}\right)}{\mathbb{E}(2\pi S_{1})^{-\frac{d}{2}}}=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{r}{2s}}\eta_{1}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\eta_{1}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s},\ \ r\geq 0,

which is well defined due to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\text{\rm{e}}^{\gamma S_{t}^{-1}}\leq\exp{\left[\frac{c\gamma}{t^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}+\frac{c\gamma^{\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}}}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}\right]}<\infty,\ \ \gamma,t>0,

for some constant
c>0
, see
[
27
, Proof of Corollary 2.2]
.
It is not difficult to see that the function
\Theta
is increasing and continuous on
[0,\infty)
with
\Theta(0)=1
.

Next, we give a lemma which will be used frequently in the sequel sections.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
x,M\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
,

(3.6)

\begin{split}p_{\alpha}(r,x+M)\leq C4^{d+\alpha}p_{\alpha}(r,x)\Theta(|M|^{2}r^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}).\end{split}

Proof.

It follows from the elemental inequality
|a-\overline{a}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|a|^{2}-|\overline{a}|^{2},a,\overline{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the FKG inequality and the scaling property of
S_{t}
that

(3.7)

\begin{split}p_{\alpha}(r,x+M)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{|x+M|^{2}}{2s}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4s}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{|M|^{2}}{2s}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s\\
&\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4s}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{|M|^{2}}{2s}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}\\
&\leq p_{\alpha}\left(r,\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{|M|^{2}}{2s}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\eta_{r}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}\\
&=p_{\alpha}\left(r,\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{|M|^{2}r^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{2s}}\eta_{1}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(2\pi s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\eta_{1}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}\\
&=p_{\alpha}\left(r,\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\Theta(|M|^{2}r^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}).\end{split}

This together with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for

and

yields (

4
Heat Kernel of EM scheme (
2.3
)

In this section, we first express the heat kernel of solution to discrete-time EM scheme in terms of a sum of convolutional terms with iterated kernels
H^{(\delta),(k)}
and the density of the frozen homogeneous scheme defined (
4.1
) below, and reveal its explicit upper bounds.
Following this result, we then finish the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
j\geq 0
, let us begin with the “frozen” homogeneous scheme
(\widetilde{X}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}}_{i\delta})_{i\geq j}
, which is defined by

(4.1)

\widetilde{X}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}}_{(i+1)\delta}=\widetilde{X}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}}_{i\delta}+b(x^{\prime})\delta+(W_{S_{(i+1)\delta}}-W_{S_{i\delta}}),~~~i\geq j,~~~\widetilde{X}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}}_{j\delta}=x.

Note that, the drift
b
is frozen at
x^{\prime}
in the above definition. In what follows,
p^{(\delta)}(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,\cdot)
and

\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,\cdot)
denote the transition densities between times
j\delta
and
j^{\prime}\delta
of the discretization scheme (
2.3
) and the above “frozen” scheme, respectively.

To derive the kernel of the discrete parametrix representation, we introduce discrete and homogeneous infinitesimal generators as follows:

For
\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and
j\geq 0
, we define the family of operators
\mathscr{L}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}
and
\hat{\mathscr{L}}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}
by

\displaystyle(\mathscr{L}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}\psi)(x):=\delta^{-1}\left\{\mathbb{E}(\psi(X_{(j+1)\delta}^{(\delta)})|X_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}=x)-\psi(x)\right\},

\displaystyle(\hat{\mathscr{L}}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}\psi)(x):=\delta^{-1}\left\{\mathbb{E}\psi(\widetilde{X}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}}_{(j+1)\delta})-\psi(x)\right\},

and the discrete kernel
H^{(\delta)}
as:

(4.2)

\begin{split}H^{(\delta)}(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime}):&=(\mathscr{L}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}-\hat{\mathscr{L}}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)})\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}((j+1)\delta,\cdot;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})(x),~j^{\prime}\geq j+1,\end{split}

here we use the convention
\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}((j+1)\delta,\cdot;(j+1)\delta,x^{\prime})=\delta_{\{x^{\prime}\}}(\cdot)
, where
\delta_{\{x^{\prime}\}}(\cdot)
is the Delta function at the point
x^{\prime}
.
In what follows, let
0\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor.
According
to the parametrix method in
[
21
, Proposition 4.1]
, the transition density of EM scheme (
2.3
) and the transition density of the frozen scheme (
4.1
) have the following relationships.

(4.3)

p^{(\delta)}(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})=\sum_{k=0}^{j^{\prime}-j}(\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}\otimes_{\delta}H^{(\delta),(k)})(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime}),

where
\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}\otimes_{\delta}H^{(\delta),(0)}=\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}},

\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}\otimes_{\delta}H^{(\delta),(k)}=(\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}\otimes_{\delta}H^{(\delta),(k-1)})\otimes_{\delta}H^{(\delta)}
with

\otimes_{\delta}
being the convolution type binary operation defined by

(f\otimes_{\delta}g)(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})=\delta\sum_{k=j}^{j^{\prime}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(j\delta,x;k\delta,z)g(k\delta,z;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})\text{\rm{d}}z.

The following lemma gives the smoothing properties of the discrete convolution kernel and the estimate of
p^{(\delta)}(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
(A1)
. Then there exists a constant
\hat{C}_{T}>0
independent of
\delta
such that for any
0\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor
,

(4.4)

\begin{split}&|(\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}\otimes_{\delta}H^{(\delta),(m)})|(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})\\
&\leq\hat{C}_{T}^{m}\|b\|_{\infty}^{m}((j^{\prime}-j)\delta)^{m(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\frac{\Gamma(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})^{m}}{\Gamma(1+m(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}))}p_{\alpha}((j^{\prime}-j)\delta,x^{\prime}-x),~~~~~m\geq 0,\end{split}

where
\Gamma(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-t}\text{\rm{d}}t
is the gamma function. Consequently, it holds

(4.5)

p^{(\delta)}(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})\leq\sum_{m=0}^{j^{\prime}-j}\frac{[\hat{C}_{T}\|b\|_{\infty}T^{(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})}\Gamma(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})]^{m}}{\Gamma(1+m(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}))}p_{\alpha}((j^{\prime}-j)\delta,x^{\prime}-x).

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We claim that

(4.6)

|H^{(\delta)}|(j\delta,x;j^{\prime}\delta,x^{\prime})\leq\hat{C}_{T}\|b\|_{\infty}((j^{\prime}-j)\delta)^{\frac{-1}{\alpha}}p_{\alpha}((j^{\prime}-j)\delta,x^{\prime}-x),\ \ j^{\prime}>j.

Firstly, we prove (
4.6
) for
j^{\prime}=j+1.
It follows from (
3.1
), (
3.3
), (
3.6
) and (
4.2
) that

\begin{split}&|H^{(\delta)}|(j\delta,x;(j+1)\delta,x^{\prime})\\
&=\left|(\mathscr{L}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}-\hat{\mathscr{L}}_{j\delta}^{(\delta)})\widetilde{p}^{(\delta),x^{\prime}}((j+1)\delta,\cdot;(j+1)\delta,x^{\prime})(x)\right|\\
&=\left|\frac{1}{\delta}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\left(\delta_{\{x^{\prime}\}}(X_{(j+1)\delta}^{(\delta)})|X_{j\delta}^{(\delta)}=x\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(\delta_{\{x^{\prime}\}}(\widetilde{X}_{(j+1)\delta}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}})|\widetilde{X}_{j\delta}^{(\delta),j,x,x^{\prime}}=x\right)\Big\}\right|\\

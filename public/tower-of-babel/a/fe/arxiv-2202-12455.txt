Cauchy problem for general time fractional diffusion equation
Chung-Sik Sin

Abstract
In the present work, we consider the Cauchy problem for the time fractional diffusion equation involving the general Caputo-type differential operator proposed by Kochubei. First, the existence, the positivity and the long time behavior of solutions of the equation without source term are established by using the Fourier analysis technique. Then, based on the representation of the solution of the inhomogenous linear ordinary differential equation with the general operator, the similar problems for the diffusion equation with source term are studied.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we study the general time fractional diffusion equation of the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{(k)}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\triangle u(t,x)+h(t,x),t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.2)

where
n\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\mathscr{D}_{(k)}
denotes the general Caputo-type fractional differential operator defined by
[
11
]

\mathscr{D}_{(k)}v(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}k(t-\tau)v(\tau)d\tau-k(t)v(0).

Here
k\in L_{1}^{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
The operator
\mathscr{D}_{(k)}
stands for the Caputo fractional differential operator when
k(t)=t^{-\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
. Moreover, it generalizes multi-term and distributed order fractional differential operator.

The symbol
\triangle
means the Laplacian defined by

\triangle w(x)=-F^{-1}(|\xi|^{2}Fw(\xi))(x),w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
F,F^{-1}
are respectively Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform defined by

\displaystyle Fw(\xi)=\tilde{w}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx,

\displaystyle F^{-1}w(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi.

Here
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the Sobolev space endowed with the norm defined by
[
2
, Chapter 4]

\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})\tilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The regularities of the source term
h
and the initial value
u_{0}
will be discussed later.

The equation (
1.1
) is derived from the continuous time random walk theory which is one of the powerful tools for describing the anomalous diffusion process of complex materials
[
20
]
. The anomalous diffusion phenomena have attracted many researchers’ attention
[
4
,
28
,
27
,
9
,
26
]
.
Due to the continuous time random walk theory,
u(t,x)
means the probability density function to find a walker at position
x
at time
t
.
If the waiting time probability density function of the walker follows the power law asymptotically, we obtain the Caputo time fractional diffusion equation, which is a special type of the equation (
1.1
).
In
[
1
]
, the ultraslow diffusion phenomena which can not be described by employing the usual fractional derivatives were successfully captured by using the distributed order fractional derivative including single-term and multi-term fractional derivatives.
Since the equation (
1.1
) is a generalization of the diffusion equation involving the distributed order fractional derivative, it has the ability to model more complex anomalous diffusion processes.

The mathematical aspects for the anomalous diffusion equation have been extensively investigated.
In
[
17
]
, the fundamental solutions of Cauchy problem and Signalling problem for the Caputo time fractional diffusion equation without source term were expressed in terms of special functions by using the Laplace transform.
Eidelman and Kochubei investigated the fundamental solution of the Cauchy problem for the diffusion equation with the Caputo fractional derivative in time and a uniformly elliptic operator with variable coefficients in space
[
3
]
.
Kochubei considered the fundamental solution and the classical solution of the ultraslow diffusion equation with the distributed order derivative
[
10
]
.
In
[
11
]
, Kochubei proposed the general Caputo-type differential operator
\mathscr{D}_{(k)}
and investigated the LT-solution of the homogenous diffusion equation involving the general operator. In order that a function becomes a LT-solution, its Laplace transform should be twice continuously differentiable with respect to
x
. The kernel function of the operator
\mathscr{D}_{(k)}
considered in
[
11
]
satisfies the following conditions.

(C1) The Laplace transform
\hat{k}
of
k
,

Lk(s)=\hat{k}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}k(t)e^{-ts}dt,

exists for all
s>0
,

(C2)
\hat{k}(s)
is a Stieltjes function,

(C3)
\hat{k}(s)\rightarrow 0
and
s\hat{k}(s)\rightarrow\infty
as
s\rightarrow\infty
,

(C4)
\hat{k}(s)\rightarrow\infty
and
s\hat{k}(s)\rightarrow 0
as
s\rightarrow 0
.

In
[
14
,
5
,
6
,
12
,
15
,
13
,
16
]
, by using the eigenfunction expansion of the symmetric uniformly elliptic operator and the properties of Mittag-Leffler type functions, the initial boundary value problems for single-term, multi-term and distributed order time fractional diffusion equations were studied. In particular, in
[
15
]
, the authors pointed out that the asymptotic behavior of solutions of the Cauchy problem for the general fractional diffusion equation (
1.1
) remains open.

In this work, existence and analytical properties of solutions of the Cauchy problem are investigated.
In particular, we find a result for the asymptotic behavior of solutions related to the open problem presented in
[
15
]
.
In the present paper, we consider the solution which is Fourier transformable with respect to the spatial variable.
In fact, when the equation (
1.1
) is obtained from continuous time random walk theory, it is supposed that
u(t,x)
is not only Laplace transformable with respect to
t
but also Fourier transformable with respect to
x
.
From the physical view point, it is not neccessary for the solution to be twice continuously differentiable with respect to
x
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, the general fractional diffusion equation without source term is studied. The existence, the nonnegativity and the long time behavior of the solution are proved. In Section 3, we disuss the equation with source term subjected to zero initial condition.

By using the Plancherel theorem, for

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\!=\!\|\tilde{u}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\!=\!\|\tilde{u}_{0}Y(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\!\leq\!\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\!=\!\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

For

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})(\tilde{u}(t,\cdot)-\tilde{u}_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})\tilde{u}_{0}(1-Y(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\|u(t,\cdot)-u_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lim_{t\to 0}\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})(\tilde{u}(t,\cdot)-\tilde{u}_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|(1+|\cdot|^{2})\tilde{u}_{0}\lim_{t\to 0}(1-Y(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0.

By the L’Hôpital Rule and the property (C4), we can easily see that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\|k\|_{L^{1}(0,t)}}{t}=0.

(2.10)

Using the Young inequality for convolution, for

\displaystyle|\xi|^{2}tY(t,\xi)\leq\||\xi|^{2}Y(\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^{1}(0,t)}=\|\mathscr{D}_{(k)}Y(\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^{1}(0,t)}

3
General time fractional diffusion equation with source term

In this section, the general diffusion equation (

) with the initial condition

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda>0,T>0
and
f\in C[0,T]
.
Then the fractional differential equation

\mathscr{D}_{(k)}w(t)+\lambda w(t)=f(t),t>0

(3.1)

with the initial condition
w(0)=0

has a unique solution in
C[0,T]
. In particular, the solution has the form

w(t)=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}v^{\prime}(t-s)f(s)ds,

(3.2)

where
v(t)
is the solution of the following initial value problem

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{(k)}v(t)+\lambda v(t)=0,t>0,

\displaystyle v(0)=1.

Proof.

First, First, we prove that the function of the form (
3.2
) satisfies (
3.1
). For
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{(k)}w(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}k(t-s)w(s)ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}k(t-s)v^{\prime}(s-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\tau}^{t}k(t-s)v^{\prime}(s-\tau)dsf(\tau)d\tau

(3.3)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-\tau}k(t-\tau-r)v^{\prime}(r)drf(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{D}_{(k)}v(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}(-\lambda)v(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}v^{\prime}(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau+f(t)

\displaystyle=-\lambda w(t)+f(t).

For
t>0
, we estimate

|w(t)|\leq-\frac{1}{\lambda}\max_{s\in[0,t]}|f(s)|\int_{0}^{t}v^{\prime}(s)ds=\frac{1}{\lambda}\max_{s\in[0,t]}|f(s)|(1-v(t)),

which implies that
\lim\limits_{t\to 0+}w(t)=0
. Thus,
w\in C[0,T]
.
The uniqueness of the solution follows from Theorem 4.1 in
[
23
]
.
∎

Conic bundles that are not birational to numerical Calabi–Yau pairs
János Kollár

Abstract
Let X X be a general conic bundle over ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} with branch curve of degree at least 19. We prove that there is no normal projective variety Y Y that is birational to X X and such that some multiple of its anticanonical divisor is effective.



Keywords.
Rationally connected variety, conic bundle, Calabi–Yau variety, birational equivalence.

Understanding the difference between rationally connected varieties and Fano varieties has long been a goal of birational geometry.
In any dimension, smooth Fano varieties come in finitely many families
but rationally connected varieties form infinitely many families.
Thus one expects that not every rationally connected variety is birational to a Fano variety, but actual proofs of this fact have been quite subtle; see

[
Sar80
]
or
[
Cor95
]
.

A more general form of this problem asks if every rationally connected variety
X
is birational to the underlying variety of a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Fano pair
(Y,\Delta)
.
Since
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Fano pairs form infinitely many families up-to birational equivalence
[
Oka09
]
, this is a harder variant. This form of the question was posed in
[
CG13
]

and a negative answer is established in
[
Kry14
]
.
Closely related results describing
Calabi–Yau fiber-space structures on certain Fano 3-folds are
proved in
[
Che04
,
Ryd06
,
CK10
]
.

Probably the most general question in this direction is whether
every rationally connected variety is birational to the underlying variety of a
numerical Calabi–Yau
pair
(Y,\Delta)
. Here we allow the most general
definition of Calabi–Yau pairs, that is,
Y
is normal, proper,

\Delta
is a pseudo-effective
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor,

K_{Y}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and
K_{Y}+\Delta\equiv 0
, but we impose no restrictions on the singularities of
Y
.
(For most purposes the more restrictive definition of
Calabi–Yau pairs adopted in
[
KX16
]
is the best; but the above numerical version also seems natural.)
It turns out that the singularities of
Y
are not very important
and the difference between effective and pseudo-effective divisors also
may not be significant,
but
allowing divisors with coefficient
>1
in
\Delta
leads to many more cases; see Example
Conic bundles that are not birational to

numerical Calabi–Yau pairs
and Definition
Conic bundles that are not birational to

numerical Calabi–Yau pairs
.
If
X
is birational to a numerical Calabi–Yau pair
(Y,\Delta)

then the birational transform of
\Delta
on
X
is frequently a
quite interesting divisor. Understanding such divisors was a key to proving unirationality of degree 1 conic bundle surfaces
[
KM17
]
.

We discuss several methods to show that certain conic bundles
(see Definition

Typical results are the following.

There are conic bundles

S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
that are
not birational to any numerical Calabi–Yau surface.

This property seems to depend very subtly on the coefficients involved in the definition of

. The following are some concrete special cases.

Let

be a prime such that
p\equiv-1\mod 4
and choose

not divisible by

Then the conic bundle

\Bigl(z^{2}=\Bigl(\tfrac{s^{p-1}-1}{s^{2}-1}\Bigr)x^{2}+\bigl(s^{p-1}-1+mp\bigr)y^{2}\Bigr)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{xyz}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{s}

is not birational—over
{\mathbb{Q}}
—to any numerical Calabi–Yau surface.

In dimension 3 we get the following.

Let

be a general hypersurface of bidegree

over a field of characteristic

Then

I could not write down explicit examples with smooth branch curve,
but it is easy to get many with reducible branch curve using Corollary

It is quite likely that the theorem also holds over any infinite field. The main arguments in our paper work whenever the characteristic is
\neq 2

is stated in the literature only for characteristic

The most important numerical invariant of a conic bundle

\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is the degree of its branch divisor
B_{X}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
.
Typical results say that for smooth branch divisors the degree is the only important invariant and
the higher the degree of

This is, however, not the case for our question. We see

(resp.

with many singular fibers)
that are birational to numerical Calabi–Yau varieties.

The following example shows that many varieties are birational to the underlying variety of a smooth numerical Calabi–Yau pair.

If
(Y,\Delta)
is a numerical Calabi–Yau pair and

then

is uniruled by
[

]

In fact, for any smooth, projective variety

the product

is birational to a numerical Calabi–Yau pair.
To see this, let

be an ample divisor on

A
conic bundle

is a flat, proper morphism
\pi:X\to Z
such that every fiber of
\pi
is isomorphic to a plane conic.
We are mainly interested in the cases when
Z
is regular, but for the basic definition it is enough to assume that
Z
is normal.
(For many purposes one should allow non-flat morphisms and more singular fibers, but for us the restrictive version is more convenient.)

A conic bundle is called
extremal

if for every codimension 1 point
z\in Z
the fiber
X_{z}
is irreducible over
k(z)
. Equivalently, the relative Picard number is 1.

A conic bundle is called
minimal
if it is extremal and
has no rational sections. (This is the “right” definition for conic bundles but note that
a product
Z\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to Z
is extremal but not minimal in our sense.)
If
X
is regular then
\pi:X\to Z
is minimal
iff
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\pi^{*}\operatorname{Pic}(Z)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}[\omega_{X/Z}^{-1}]
; this is the key property that we are interested in.
This is equivalent to saying that if
L
is any line bundle on
X
and

C\subset X
is a fiber then
\deg(L|_{C})
is even and if

C
is reducible then
L|_{C}
has the same degree on both
irreducible components.

The
branch locus,
denoted by
B_{X}\subset Z
, is the
subscheme parametrizing singular fibers of
\pi
. Set theoretically
it consists of points
z\in Z
such that

X_{z}:=\pi^{-1}(z)
is singular. If
X
is regular then
B_{X}
is reduced.
In general, the scheme structure is given by the formula
(
25.3
) which also shows that

B_{X}
has pure codimension 1 in
Z
,
except possibly when
\operatorname{char}k(Z)=2
.
In order to avoid various complications, we
assume from now on that all residue characteristics are
\neq 2
.
Our main interest is in conic bundles over
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{Q}}
, but we will
use some examples that are defined over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

Assume that
Z
is regular and let
z\in B_{X}
be a regular point. Then

X_{z}
is a pair of lines and
X
is regular along
X_{z}
; see Paragraph
Birational maps of conic bundles
.
This defines a
double cover
\tilde{B}_{X}\to B_{X}
that is étale over the regular locus of
B_{X}
. Then
X\to Z
is minimal
iff, for every irreducible component
B_{i}\subset B_{X}
, the corresponding

\tilde{B}_{i}
is also irreducible.

A divisor class

Birational transformation of divisors does not preserve linear equivalence,

However, the birational transform of a mobile linear system is well defined

Let

For now our main interest is in the case

Assume that

Surface conic bundles

Fix an arbitrary field
k
. Assume for simplicity that
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
.
Let
S
be a smooth surface over
k
and

S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
a minimal conic bundle. The number of singular geometric fibers, which is the degree of the branch locus
B_{S}
, is denoted by
\delta(S)
.
A typical fiber is denoted by
F
.
Thus
(K_{S}^{2})=8-\delta(S)
and
(K_{S}\cdot F)=-2
.

Let
k
be a field and
\pi:S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
a minimal conic bundle.
Fix a natural number
m
such that
\delta(S)>12+6m
.
Assume that
-K_{S}+mF
is pseudo-effective.

Then there is a unique irreducible curve
C\subset S
such that

(C^{2})<0
. Furthermore,
\pi:C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
has degree 2 and
|-K_{S}+mF|=C+|bF|
for some
b\geq 0
.
In particular,

-K_{S}+mF
is effective.

By assumption there is a sequence of effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors

D_{t}
converging to
-K_{S}+mF
.
Note that
(-K_{S}+mF)^{2}=8-\delta(S)+4m<0
, hence
(D_{t}^{2})<0
for some
t
.
So there is a
k
-irreducible component
C\subset\operatorname{Supp}D_{t}
with
C^{2}<0
. Thus the cone of curves is generated by
C
and a fiber
F
.

Write
C\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}a(-K_{S}+m^{\prime}F)
. Here
a
is an integer but
m^{\prime}
could be rational.
The degree of the dualizing sheaf of
C
is

\deg\omega_{C}=2p_{a}(C)-2=C(C+K_{S})=a(a-1)(K_{S}^{2})+2a(2a-1)m^{\prime}.

Since
C_{\bar{k}}
has at most
2a
irreducible components,
\deg\omega_{C}\geq-4a
, hence

a(a-1)(K_{S}^{2})+2a(2a-1)m^{\prime}\geq-4a.

If
a\geq 2
then this rearranges to

\delta(S)-8=-(K_{S}^{2})\leq\bigl(4+\tfrac{2}{a-1}\bigr)m^{\prime}+\tfrac{4}{a-1}\leq 6m^{\prime}+4.

Note that
-K_{S}+mF\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\frac{1}{a}C+(m-m^{\prime})F
, hence

m^{\prime}\leq m
since
-K_{S}+mF
is pseudo-effective.
Thus, if
\delta(S)>12+6m
then
a=1
.

Note that we also could have used Bend-and-break
(as stated in
[
Kol96
, II.5.5.3]
) to show that

.

There are minimal conic bundles
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

with
\delta(S)=12
for which
-2K_{S}
is effective but

-K_{S}
is not effective.

To construct such examples,
let

be an irreducible degree 4 point. Then

is a minimal conic bundle with with
\delta=3
.
The exceptional curves give a conjugate set of 4 sections, each with
self-intersection
-1
.

Pull it back by a general degree

map

to get

. We get a minimal conic bundle

with

and a conjugate set of 4 sections, each with

-r
. The sum of these 4 sections is in

.
Thus the bound

in Lemma

(It is interesting to note that for

.)

Let
k
be a field and
S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
a minimal conic bundle. Assume that
\delta(S)>12+6m
. Then
-K_{S}+mF
is birationally pseudo-effective

contains a double section

whose normalization has genus
\leq m+1
.

Assume that

is birational and

is pseudo-effective.
By

(see also Theorem

is another minimal conic bundle with the same

and

.
Thus

.
Its normalization has genus

.

Conversely, let

be a double section.
We can resolve the singularities of

.
(Clearly, these points are on smooth fibers.) At the end we have

Let

Threefold conic bundles

We use Corollary
Surface conic bundles
to prove a similar result for 3-dimensional conic bundles.

Let
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a
smooth, minimal conic bundle over a field of characteristic
\neq 2,3,5

whose branch curve
B_{X}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

has degree
\geq 19
. The following are equivalent.

1.

-K_{X}
is birationally pseudo-effective.

2.

There is a generically finite double section
D\subset X
with normalization
\tau:\bar{D}\to D
such that the branch curve
B_{D}
of
\pi\circ\tau:\bar{D}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
has degree
\leq 6
.

3.

X
is birational to a smooth, minimal conic bundle
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

(with the same branch curve) such that
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|\neq\emptyset
.

Assume that
\phi:X\dasharrow X^{\prime}
shows that
-K_{X}
is birationally pseudo-effective. By Complement
Birational maps of conic bundles
, we may choose
X^{\prime}
such that

\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is also a conic bundle with the same branch curve.
Write
-K_{X^{\prime}}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta^{\prime}
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is pseudo-effective.

Let
L\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be the generic line. By restriction
we get
\pi^{\prime}_{S}:S^{\prime}\to L
.
Since
-K_{S^{\prime}}+F=-K_{X^{\prime}}|_{S^{\prime}}
, we see that

-K_{S^{\prime}}+F\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}
is pseudo-effective.
Moreover, if
\Delta^{\prime}_{t}
is a sequence of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-effective divisors converging to

\Delta^{\prime}
then
\Delta^{\prime}_{t}|_{S^{\prime}}
is a sequence of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-effective divisors converging to
\Delta^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}
.

Thus, by Lemma
Surface conic bundles
,

\Delta^{\prime}_{t}|_{S^{\prime}}
has an irreducible component
C^{\prime}\subset S^{\prime}

with negative self-intersection and its normalization
has genus
\leq 2
by Lemma
Surface conic bundles
. Since
L
is the generic line,
C^{\prime}

is the restriction of an irreducible component
D^{\prime}
of
\Delta^{\prime}_{t}
.
Thus
D^{\prime}
is a
double section and its birational transform on
X
is a double section

D\subset X
. Furthermore,
we know that
the preimage of
L
in the
normalization
\tau:\bar{D}\to D
has genus
\leq 2
.
Thus
L
intersects the
branch curve of
\pi\circ\tau:\bar{D}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
in
\leq 6
points.
This shows that (1)
\Rightarrow
(2).

Assume next that
\pi
has a double section
D\subset X
as in (2).
By Lemma
Threefold conic bundles
, there is a sequence of elementary transformations

such that the branch curve of

has degree
\leq 6
. By Lemma
Threefold conic bundles
this implies that

is a sub-divisor of
|-K_{X^{\prime}}|
. Thus (2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
and (3)
\Rightarrow
(1) holds by definition.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be a
conic bundle. Assume that

X
and
Z
are smooth
and
\dim Z=2
. Let
D\subset X
be a generically finite double section.
Then there is a sequence of elementary transformations

X\dasharrow X^{\prime}
such that the resulting
D^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
is normal, except possibly along finitely many fibers.

Let
C\subset\operatorname{Sing}D
be a curve not contained in a fiber of
\pi
. Let
F^{\prime}\subset F\subset X
be an irreducible component of a fiber that is not contained in
D
but meets

at a point

.
Since

is singular at
p
, the local intersection number
(D\cdot F^{\prime})_{p}
is at least 2. Since
D
is a double section,
(D\cdot F)\leq 2
. Thus

F
is irreducible and

, hence
\pi_{C}:C\to\pi(C)
is birational.
As we discuss in Paragraph
Birational maps of conic bundles
,
the elementary transformation centered at

decreases the degree of the branch curve of
D\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. After finitely many such steps
we get
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Z
such that
D^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
is normal, except possibly along finitely many fibers.

Let
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a
minimal conic bundle and

a generically finite double section. Then

D
is a sub-divisor of
|-K_{X}|
iff the branch curve

of
\pi_{D}:D\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
has degree
\leq 6
.

Since

is minimal, we know that

for some

where

is the class of a line in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Thus

Let

denote the Stein factorization. Then

By the Hurwitz formula

K3 surfaces on conic bundles

In order to prove Theorem
Conic bundles that are not birational to

numerical Calabi–Yau pairs
, it remains to show that
(
2.
) does not hold for suitable
branch curves
B\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Example
K3 surfaces on conic bundles
shows that there are conic bundles with high degree smooth branch curve for which
|-K_{X}|
does contain a K3 surface.
Thus we need to focus on more subtle properties of
B
.
We present 2 approaches. The first uses branch curves with many nodes;
we prove that all the nodes have to lie on a sextic curve.
This leads to quite explicit examples starting with
\deg B=12
; see Corollary
K3 surfaces on conic bundles
.

Note also that, in a flat family of conic bundles, double sections are parametrized by countably many components of the Chow variety. Thus if we find
one conic bundle without certain type of double sections then the very general conic bundle also has no double sections of the same type.

The other approach proves that there has to be a
sextic curve that is everywhere tangent to
B_{X}
.
This is easily seen to be a non-empty Zariski open condition for

\deg B\geq 15
, though I could not write down explicit examples.
As pointed out by
[
Sar82
, Sec.5]
, the results of

[
AM72
, Sec.3]
imply that every smooth plane curve of
degree
\geq 3
is the branch curve of a minimal conic bundle.

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be a minimal, smooth conic bundle with branch curve

B_{X}
. Let
D\subset X
be a double section
with normalization
\tau:\bar{D}\to D

and
B_{D}\subset Z
the branch curve of
\pi|_{D}\circ\tau:\bar{D}\to Z
.
Then
\operatorname{Sing}(B_{X})\subset B_{D}
.

If
p\notin B_{D}
then both of the étale local branches of

\bar{D}\to Z
give étale local sections of
\pi
.
As we discuss in Claim
Birational maps of conic bundles
, there are no étale local
sections over the nodes of
B_{X}
for
X
smooth.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbf{y}}
be given by an equation of bidegree
(d,2)

\textstyle{\sum}_{i}g_{i}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})y_{i}^{2}=0.

Assume that the curves

B_{i}:=\bigl(g_{i}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})=0\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}

are smooth and they intersect each other transversally in
3d^{2}
distinct points.

Let
D\subset X
be a double section
and
B_{D}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\mathbf{x}}
the branch curve of
\pi|_{D}\circ\tau:\bar{D}\to Z
.
Then
\deg B_{D}\geq 2d
.

The assumptions imply that
X
is smooth.
The branch curve of
X
is
B_{X}=B_{0}+B_{1}+B_{2}
.
By Lemma
K3 surfaces on conic bundles
,
B_{D}
passes through all the nodes of
B
.
In particular,
B_{D}
intersects
B_{0}
in the
2d^{2}
points

B_{0}\cap(B_{1}\cup B_{2})
. Thus
\deg B_{D}\geq 2d
unless
B_{0}\subset B_{D}
.
We can repeat the argument for
B_{1}
to get that

\deg B_{D}\geq 2d
unless
B_{0}+B_{1}\subset B_{D}
. The letter case also
implies that
\deg B_{D}\geq 2d
.

For conic bundles with smooth branch curves we have a less precise condition.
(The 2 results are closely related
since passing through a node guarantees that the
intersection multiplicity is
\geq 2
.)

Let
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a
smooth, minimal conic bundle with branch locus
B_{X}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Assume that there is no reduced curve
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
\leq 6

such that all points of
C\cap B_{X}

have intersection multiplicity
\geq 2
.
Then
-K_{X}
is not effective.

Assume to the contrary that
-K_{X}\sim D
where
D
is an effective
{\mathbb{Z}}
-divisor. Since
X
is minimal, there are no rational sections.
Thus there is a unique irreducible component
D_{0}\subset D
that is a double section of
\pi
.
Let
\tau:\bar{D}_{0}\to D_{0}
denote the normalization and
let
B_{D}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be the branch curve of

\tau\circ\pi|_{D_{0}}:\bar{D}_{0}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
Note that
D_{0}\sim-K_{X}-mH
for some
m\geq 0
and
\deg B_{D}\leq 6
by
Lemma
Threefold conic bundles
.

We claim that all points of
B_{D}\cap B_{X}

have intersection multiplicity
\geq 2
. To see this, assume to the contrary that there is a
point
p\in B_{D}\cap B_{X}
where
B_{D},B_{X}
are smooth and intersect transversally.
In a neighborhood of
p
the surface
D_{0}
is a smooth double cover
ramified along
B_{D}
.
Then
(\pi|_{D_{0}})^{-1}(B_{X})
is a smooth double cover of
B_{X}

ramified at
p
. On the other hand,
(\pi|_{D_{0}})^{-1}(B_{X})=D_{0}\cap\pi^{-1}(B_{X})

hence its normalization factors through the étale cover

\tilde{B}_{X}\to B_{X}
. This is a contradiction.
(This argument in fact shows that these intersection multiplicities are even.)

Next we check that the assumptions of Proposition
K3 surfaces on conic bundles

hold for a general branch curve of degree
\geq 15
.

Let
B\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a general curve of degree
d
.
Then there is no reduced curve
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
<\tfrac{1}{2}d-1

such that all points of
C\cap B

have intersection multiplicity
\geq 2
.

Fix a reduced curve
C
and let
L
be a line bundle of degree
m
. Let
W(L)\subset H^{0}(C,L)
denote the subvariety consisting of sections without simple zeros. Note that any
s\in W(L)
has at most
\frac{1}{2}m
zeros.
The map that sends a section of
L
to its set of zeros has 1-dimensional fibers. Thus
\dim W(L)\leq\frac{1}{2}m+1
.

Fix a reduced plane curve
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
c
. Let
W(C,d)
denote the set of degree
d
curves
B

such that all points of
C\cap B

have intersection multiplicity
\geq 2
. Applying the above estimate to

L={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(d)|_{C}
we get that

\dim W(C,d)\leq\frac{1}{2}cd+\binom{d-c+2}{2}
.
Next let
W(d)
denote the set of degree
d
curves
B

such that all points of
C\cap B

have intersection multiplicity
\geq 2
for some curve
C
of degree
c
.
Then

\dim W(d)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}cd+\tbinom{d-c+2}{2}+\tbinom{c+2}{2}-1.

After expanding the binomials we see that

\dim W(d)<\tbinom{d+2}{2}-1\quad\mbox{for $d>2c+2$.}\quad

(Note that the bound
\tfrac{1}{2}d-1
is sharp for
d=4
but
one should be able to prove slightly better bounds for larger values of
d
.)

Let
Z
be a smooth variety and
E
a rank 3 vector bundle one
Z
.
Set
P:={\mathbb{P}}_{Z}(E)
with projection
\pi:P\to Z
and
note that
\omega_{P/Z}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(-3)\otimes\pi^{*}\det E
.
Let
L
be a line bundle on
Z
and
X\subset P
the zero set of a section of
{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(2)\otimes\pi^{*}L
.
Then
\omega_{X}\cong\bigl({\mathcal{O}}_{P}(-1)\otimes\pi^{*}(L\otimes\det E)\bigr)|_{X}
and

\pi_{*}\bigl(\omega_{X}^{-1}\bigr)\cong(\omega_{Z}\otimes L\otimes\det E)^{-1}\otimes E
.

Note that
H^{0}\bigl(P,{\mathcal{O}}_{P}(2)\otimes\pi^{*}L\bigr)=H^{0}\bigl(Z,L\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{2}E\bigr)
and

H^{0}\bigl(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{X})\bigr)=H^{0}\bigl(Z,\bigl(L(K_{Z})\otimes\det E\bigr)^{-1}\otimes E\bigr)
.
We would like
X
to be smooth, this suggests that

L\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{2}E
should be generated by global sections,
hence
L
should be positive. By contrast, the condition

|-K_{X}|\neq\emptyset
suggests that
L
should be negative.
It seems that both of these can be satisfied only if
E
is rather unstable.

For example, take
Z={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, set
E={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(c)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(3)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
where
c>3
and
L={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
.
Then
L\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{2}E
is generated by global sections, so a general section gives a smooth conic bundle
X
. Furthermore,

\pi_{*}\bigl(\omega_{X}^{-1}\bigr)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(3-3-c)\otimes E\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(3-c)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(-c)

has a unique section. Thus
X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is a conic bundle such that

|-K_{X}|
contains a unique K3 surface.
It is obtained by intersecting
X
with
the divisor in
P
corresponding to the
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(3)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
summand of
E
.
The branch curve of
X
is given by the equation

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}g_{2c}&g_{c+3}&g_{c}\\
g_{c+3}&g_{6}&g_{3}\\
g_{c}&g_{3}&g_{0}\end{array}\right)\ =0,

where
g_{i}:=g_{i}(x,y,z)
denotes a homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
.
(We can thus assume that
g_{0}=1
.) Eliminating
g_{c},g_{3}
in the last row
writes
g_{0}
times the determinant, hence the equation of
B_{X}
, in the form

(g_{6}g_{0}-g_{3}^{2})(g_{2c}g_{0}-g_{c}^{2})-(g_{c+3}g_{0}-g_{c}g_{3})^{2}=0.

Thus the degree 6 curve
g_{6}g_{0}-g_{3}^{2}=0
intersects
B_{X}
at the points where

g_{c+3}g_{0}-g_{c}g_{3}=0
and all intersection multiplicities are even;
as needed in Lemma
K3 surfaces on conic bundles
.

Working backwards from Theorem

{\mathbb{Q}}

{\mathbb{Q}}

is birationally effective
for every conic bundle over

has a section.
The situation over finite fields is unclear to me.

Birational maps of conic bundles

We summarize the results on birational maps of conic bundles that we used.
As before, all residue characteristics are assumed to be
\neq 2
.

We will need to understand the following

Problem 25.1.

Let
Z
be a regular surface,
W\subset Z
a finite subset and

\pi^{0}:X^{0}\to Z^{0}:=Z\setminus W
a conic bundle with branch locus

B^{0}_{X}
. We would like to extend
\pi^{0}:X^{0}\to Z^{0}
to a conic bundle

\pi:X\to Z
and control the singularities of
X
in terms of
B_{X}
.
(This is also interesting if
\dim Z>2
but the 2-dimensional case is simpler.)

We may assume that
W=\{p\}
is a single point and
Z
is local.
The push-forward
E^{0}:=\pi^{0}_{*}\omega_{x^{0}/Z^{0}}^{-1}
is a locally free sheaf of rank 3. Set
E:=j_{*}E^{0}
where
j:Z^{0}\lhook\joinrel\to Z
is the natural injection.
Then
E
is a reflexive sheaf but, since
Z
is regular and 2-dimensional,

E
is locally free, hence free. Set
P:={\mathbb{P}}_{Z}(E)
and let
X\subset P
be the closure of
X^{0}\subset P^{0}
. Choose an isomorphism

P\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{Z}
, then

X
is given by an equation

\textstyle{\sum}_{ij}g_{ij}(z_{1},z_{2})x_{i}x_{j}=0,

(25.2)

where
g_{ij}(z_{1},z_{2})\in{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
. The scheme structure of the branch locus is defined by

\det\bigl(g_{ij}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr)=0.

(25.3)

The worst case is when the central fiber
X_{p}
equals
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
; thus
\pi:X\to Z
is not even equidimensional. If this happens then all the
g_{ij}
vanish at
p

so
\det\bigl(g_{ij}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr)\in m_{p}^{3}
. Thus
B_{X}
has a triple
(or higher) point at
p
.
Otherwise the central fiber
X_{p}
is conic.

X_{p}
is smooth iff
p\not\in B_{X}
.

Next assume that
X_{p}
is a pair if lines.
Possibly after a quadratic residue field extension, in suitable formal coordinates
we can diagonalize the equation of
X
as

x_{0}^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+f(z_{1},z_{2})x_{2}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{X}=\bigl(f(z_{1},z_{2})=0\bigr)

where
f(0,0)=0
. We see that
X
is regular along
X_{p}
iff

f\not\in(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}
; that is, iff
B_{X}
is regular at
p
.
Furthermore,
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(1{:}1{:}0;z_{1},z_{2})
is a formal section.

Finally consider the case when
X_{p}

is a double line. Then
\det\bigl(g_{ij}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr)\in m_{p}^{2}
and
B_{X}
has a double (or higher) point
at
p
. If the branch curve has a node then, after a quadratic residue field extension, in suitable formal coordinates
we can write
X
as

X=\bigl(x_{0}^{2}=z_{1}x_{1}^{2}+z_{2}x_{2}^{2}\bigr).

The fiber of the projection
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\mathbf{z}}
is a
double line over the origin,
a pair of lines over the coordinate axes
(z_{1}z_{2}=0)
and smooth otherwise.
In contrast with the previous nodal case, the equation has no solutions in the quotient field of the completion of
{\mathcal{O}}_{z,Z}
; see Claim
Birational maps of conic bundles
.

We have thus proved the following.

Claim 25.4.

Let
Z
be a regular surface whose residue characteristics are
\neq 2
,
B\subset Z
a curve
with only nodal singularities and
W\in Z
a finite subset containing the nodes of

. Let

\pi^{0}:X^{0}\to Z\setminus W
be a conic bundle with (scheme theoretic) branch locus

.
Then
X^{0}
extends to a unique
conic bundle

\pi:X\to Z
.

Furthermore,
X
is regular iff the following holds:
for every
p\in W
that is a node of

\pi:X\to Z
has no sections over the quotient field of the strict henselization
{{\mathcal{O}}}^{\rm sh}_{p,Z}

of the local ring

.

We also used the following well known result.

Claim 25.5.

Let
\bigl(R,m=(s,t)\bigr)
be a regular local ring of dimension 2.
Then
x_{0}^{2}=sx_{1}^{2}+tx_{2}^{2}
has no nonzero solutions in the quotient field of
R
.

After clearing denominators, we may assume that
the
x_{i}
are in
R
. Let
c
be the largest such that the
x_{i}
are in
m^{c}
.
We can thus write
x_{i}=p_{i}(s,t)+r_{i}
where
p_{i}
is a
homogeneous polynomial of degree
c
and
r_{i}\in m^{c+1}
.
Then
x_{0}^{2}\in m^{2c}
but
sx_{1}^{2}+tx_{2}^{2}\in m^{2c+1}
. Thus
in fact
p_{0}\equiv 0
and so
sp_{1}^{2}+tp_{2}^{2}\in m^{2c+2}
.
Thus
sp_{1}^{2}+tp_{2}^{2}\equiv 0
hence
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are both identically 0.
This is a contradiction.

Claim 26.2.

Assume in addition that

Let
\pi:X\to Z
be a
conic bundle over a field of characteristic 0 such that
X,Z
are smooth.
Pick a point
z\in Z
and let
p:Z_{1}:=B_{z}Z\to Z
denote the blow-up.
The pull-back
X\times_{Z}Z_{1}
is birational to a minimal conic bundle
\pi_{1}:X_{1}\to Z_{1}
.
We want to describe its branch locus
B_{X_{1}}
.
It is clear that
B_{X_{1}}\supset p^{-1}_{*}B_{X}
, the only question is what happens with the exceptional curve
E_{z}
. The following possibilities are listed in
[
Sar80
, 2.4–2.5]
.

If
z\not\in B_{x}
then
X_{1}=X\times_{Z}Z_{1}
hence
B_{X_{1}}=p^{-1}_{*}B_{X}
.

If
z\in B_{x}
is a smooth point then
E_{z}\subset B_{X_{1}}
iff
the fiber
X_{z}
irreducible. (Thus again
B_{X_{1}}=p^{-1}_{*}B_{X}
if the base field is algebraically closed.)

If
z\in B_{x}
is a singular point then
X_{1}=X\times_{Z}Z_{1}
is singular.
Using the local equation
x_{0}^{2}=z_{1}x_{1}^{2}+z_{2}x_{2}^{2}
we get

x_{0}^{2}=z^{\prime}_{1}x_{1}^{2}+z^{\prime}_{1}z^{\prime}_{2}x_{2}^{2}
. We can rewrite this as

z^{\prime}_{1}(x^{\prime}_{0})^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+z^{\prime}_{2}x_{2}^{2}
where
x^{\prime}_{0}=x_{0}/z^{\prime}_{1}
. Thus

B_{X^{\prime}}=p^{-1}_{*}B_{X}+E_{z}
.
In all these cases we see that for any sequence of blow ups

p_{r}:Z_{r}\to Z
we have

K_{Z_{r}}+B_{X_{r}}=p_{r}^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{X})+(\mbox{effective exceptional divisor}).

(28.2)

Applying this to a common resolution
Z^{\prime}\leftarrow Z_{r}\to Z

we conclude that if
K_{Z}+B_{X}
is ample then
(Z,B_{X})
is the unique log-canonical model of
(Z_{r},B_{X_{r}})
. In particular, the rational map

\phi_{Z}:Z^{\prime}\dasharrow Z
in (
27.1
) is a morphism.
This establishes the bottom row of (
28.1
).

In order to get the top row, let
A^{\prime}
be an ample divisor on
Z^{\prime}
and choose
m
such that
|H^{\prime}|:=|-K_{Z^{\prime}}+m(p^{\prime})^{*}A^{\prime}|
is a very ample linear system on
Z^{\prime}
.
Its push-forward
\phi_{*}|H^{\prime}|
is a mobile linear system consisting of rational double sections of
\pi
. Its base locus consists of some horizontal curves
(that is curves
C_{i}\subset X
such that
C_{i}\to\pi(C_{i})
is birational) and some vertical curves
(that is curves that are contracted by
\pi
).
By a small variation of Claim
Birational maps of conic bundles
,
a sequence of elementary transformations along the horizontal curves leads to a factorization

\phi:X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\dasharrow}}X^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\dasharrow}}X

(28.3)

where
\tau
is a composite of elementary transformations and
the base locus
\operatorname{Bs}\bigl(\rho_{*}|H^{\prime}|\bigr)
is vertical.
Thus
\rho^{-1}
gives an injection
X^{\prime\prime}\setminus\operatorname{Bs}\bigl(\rho_{*}|H^{\prime}|\bigr)\lhook\joinrel\to X^{\prime}
, hence
it has no exceptional divisors. Thus
\rho
is a birational contraction and therefore
-K_{X^{\prime\prime}}=\rho_{*}\bigl(-K_{X^{\prime}}\bigr)
.

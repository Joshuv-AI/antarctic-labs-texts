Cesàro operators associated with Borel measures acting on weighted spaces of holomorphic functions with sup-norms
María J. Beltrán-Meneu, José Bonet, Enrique Jordá

Abstract
Let μ \mu be a positive finite Borel measure on [ 0 , 1 ) . [0,1). Cesàro-type operators C μ C_{\mu} when acting on weighted spaces of holomorphic functions are investigated. In the case of bounded holomorphic functions on the unit disc we prove that C μ C_{\mu} is continuous if and only if it is compact. In the case of weighted Banach spaces of holomorphic function defined by general weights, we give sufficient and necessary conditions for the continuity and compactness. For standard weights, we characterize the continuity and compactness on classical growth Banach spaces of holomorphic functions. We also study the point spectrum and the spectrum of C μ C_{\mu} on the space of holomorphic functions on the disc, on the space of bounded holomorphic functions on the disc, and on the classical growth Banach spaces of holomorphic functions. All characterizations are given in terms of the sequence of moments ( μ n ) n ∈ ℕ 0 (\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}} . The continuity, compactness and spectrum of C μ C_{\mu} acting on Fréchet and (LB) Korenblum type spaces are also considered.



Keywords:
Cesàro-type operators, Weighted Banach spaces of analytic functions, Korenblum type spaces, Compactness, Spectrum.

Introduction

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be the space of holomorphic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on the compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. If
\mu
is a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
f\in H(\mathbb{D}),

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

z\in\mathbb{D},
the Cesàro-type operator associated with
\mu
is defined by

C_{\mu}(f)(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n},\ z\in\mathbb{D},

where
\mu_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}d\mu(t)
is the
n-th moment of
\mu
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. This operator was studied by Galanopoulos et al. in
[
18
]
. They proved, in particular, that
C_{\mu}:\ H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H(\mathbb{D})
is continuous and has the integral representation (
[
18
, Proposition 1]
):

C_{\mu}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}d\mu(t),\ f\in H(\mathbb{D}).

It is a generalization of the classical Cesàro operator
C
. Indeed, if
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure, then
C_{\mu}=C,
with
\mu_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}dt=\frac{1}{n+1},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Several authors have analyzed the continuity and compactness of Cesàro-type operators on different Banach spaces of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}.
By the closed graph theorem, when dealing with Fréchet or (LB)-spaces
X
continuously embedded in
H(\mathbb{D}),
to prove the continuity of
C_{\mu}
it suffices to show that the operator is well defined on
X

[
25
, Theorem 24.31]
. Galanopoulos et al.
[
18
]
characterized the boundedness and compactness on the Hardy spaces
H^{p},

1\leq p\leq\infty,
on the weighted Bergman spaces
A_{\alpha}^{p},

1<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
, on
BMOA
, and on the Bloch space
\mathcal{B},
obtaining conditions on the positive finite Borel measure
\mu.
Measures of Carleson type played a basic role, relating boundedness to Carleson measures in the case of
H^{p}
and
A_{\alpha}^{p},
and to logarithmic Carleson measures in the case of
BMOA
, and
\mathcal{B}.
Compactness was related to vanishing and vanishing logarithmic Carleson measures, respectively. In our study we deal with
s
-Carleson and vanishing
s
-Carleson measures.

Galanopoulos et al.
[
18
]
also characterized the continuity of
C_{\mu}
on the Banach space of bounded holomorphic functions on the disc
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, space on which the Cesàro operator is not well defined
[
15
]
, and asked whether
C_{\mu}(H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq BMOA
. Blasco
[
10
]
solved this question in the afirmative whenever
\mu
is a positive Carleson measure and studied the boundedness of

on Hardy,
BMOA
and Bloch spaces for complex Borel measures
\mu.
This question has also been solved by Bao et al.
[
6
]
. Recently, Galanopoulos et al. continue their study of the Cesàro-type operator on different Hilbert spaces of holomorphic functions
[
19
]
, on the Dirichlet space and on analytic Besov spaces
[
17
]
.

In our work, we study the action of Cesàro-like operators on weigthed spaces of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
defined by sup-norms. A

\mathbb{D}
is a positive, radial (

), continuous and decreasing function with respect to

Both are Banach spaces under the norm

They appear in the study of growth conditions of analytic functions and have been investigated by several authors since the work of Shields and Williams (see eg.
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
30
]

coincides with the closure of the polynomials in

and

is canonically isomorphic to the bidual
(H_{v}^{0})^{**}

[
9
]
. Observe that the constant weight

is the classical Banach space

of bounded holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, and

reduces to

standard weights
are defined by

\gamma>0,
the spaces

Given two functions

2
Boundedness and compactness of
C_{\mu}
on spaces of holomorphic functions

2.1
Weighted Banach spaces of holomorphic functions

In this section we study the Cesàro-type operator
C_{\mu}
on weighted Banach spaces of holomorphic functions defined by general weights as well as for
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

In the proof of next Lemma we use the following notation: for a weight
v
,
B^{\infty}_{v}
and
B^{0}_{v}
denote the closed unit ball of
H_{v}^{\infty}
and
H_{v}^{0}
, respectively. It is easy to see by Montel’s theorem that
B^{\infty}_{v}
is a compact subset of
H(\mathbb{D})
for the topology
\tau_{0}
of uniform convergence on the compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover,
[
8
, Lemma 1.1]
implies that
B^{0}_{v}
is
\tau_{0}
-dense in
B^{\infty}_{v}
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.1 and Example 2.1]
yields that
H^{\infty}_{v}
and
(H^{0}_{v})^{**}
are isometrically isomorphic. In fact, the compact open topology
\tau_{0}
, the pointwise topology and the weak* topology
\sigma(H^{\infty}_{v},(H^{0}_{v})^{*})
coincide on the closed unit ball
B^{\infty}_{v}
, by Alaoglu-Bourbaki’s theorem, and consequently on all the bounded subsets.

Theorem 1

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
v,w
be weights on
\mathbb{D}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is continuous and
C_{\mu}(1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}z^{n}\in H_{w}^{0}
.

(ii)

C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
is continuous.

If these equivalent conditions hold, then the bitranspose
C_{\mu}^{**}
of
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
coincides with
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty},
and
\|C_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H_{v}^{\infty},H_{v}^{\infty})}=\|C_{\mu}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H_{v}^{0},H_{v}^{0})}.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) If
C_{\mu}(1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}z^{n}\in H_{w}^{0},
then
C_{\mu}(z^{N})\in H_{w}^{0}
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}.
Indeed,
C_{\mu}(z^{N})=C_{\mu}(1)-\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu_{n}z^{n}\in H_{w}^{0}
, as the polynomials
\mathcal{P}
are contained in
H_{w}^{0}.
Moreover, since the set of polynomials
\mathcal{P}
is densely contained in
H_{w}^{0},

C_{\mu}(H_{w}^{0})=C_{\mu}(\overline{\mathcal{P}})\subseteq\overline{C_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})}\subseteq H_{w}^{0},
where the closure is taken with respect to
\|\ \|_{w}.
So, the closed graph theorem yields the continuity.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) If
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
is continuous, then it is obvious that
C_{\mu}(1)\in H_{w}^{0}.
Moreover, there is
M>0
such that
C_{\mu}(B^{0}_{v})\subseteq MB^{0}_{w}
. The continuity of
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H(\mathbb{D})
implies (with closures taken in
(H(\mathbb{D}),\tau_{0})
)

C_{\mu}(B^{\infty}_{v})=C_{\mu}(\overline{B^{0}_{v}})\subseteq\overline{C_{\mu}(B^{0}_{v})}\subseteq M\overline{B^{0}_{w}}\subseteq MB^{\infty}_{w}.

This implies that
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is well defined and continuous.

Now assume that these two equivalent conditions are satisfied. By our comments before the Lemma (see
[
9
]
),
C_{\mu}^{**}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
and
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
are two continuous linear operators which coincide on
H^{0}_{v}
. To see that they also coincide on
H^{\infty}_{v}
, take
f\in B^{\infty}_{v}
. There is a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\in B^{0}_{v}
which converges to
f
for the topology
\tau_{0}
. Since
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H(\mathbb{D})
is continuous,
(C_{\mu}(f_{n}))_{n}
converges to
C_{\mu}(f)
in
(H(\mathbb{D}),\tau_{0})
. On the other hand,
C_{\mu}^{**}
is weak*-weak* continuous and
(f_{n})_{n}
converges to
f
for the topology
\sigma(H^{\infty}_{v},(H^{0}_{v})^{*})
. Hence, the sequence
C_{\mu}(f_{n})=C^{**}_{\mu}(f_{n}),n=1,2,...
converges to
C^{**}_{\mu}(f)
in
H^{\infty}_{w}
for the topology
\sigma(H^{\infty}_{w},(H^{0}_{w})^{*})
. Since this topology coincides with
\tau_{0}
on the bounded subsets of
H^{\infty}_{w}
, we get
C^{**}_{\mu}(f)=C_{\mu}(f)
. The coincidence that we have just proved implies the assertion about the operator norms.
∎

Proposition 2

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1).
The following hold:

(i)

If
\mu
is a Carleson measure and
w
a weight such that
w(r)|\log(1-r)|\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
, then
C_{\mu}(1)\in H_{w}^{0}.
This condition is satisfied, for instance, by the standard weights.

(ii)

If
\mu
is an
s
-Carleson measure for
0<s<1
, then
C_{\mu}(1)\in H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0}
for each
\gamma>1-s
.

Proof.

(i) By assumption, there exists
C>0
such that
\mu_{n}\leq\frac{C}{n+1}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. So, for
z\in\mathbb{D},z\neq 0,

w(z)|C_{\mu}(1)(z)|\leq w(z)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}|z|^{n}\leq Cw(z)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{n}}{n+1}=-Cw(z)\frac{\log(1-|z|)}{|z|}.

Now, the hypothesis on
w
implies
\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1^{-}}w(z)|C_{\mu}(1)(z)|=0.

(ii) Fix
\gamma>1-s
and take
p\in\mathbb{R}
such that
p>1/s
and
(p-1)/p<\gamma
. This choice is possible since
\lim_{p\to\frac{1}{s}}\frac{p-1}{p}=1-s
. Let
q:=p/(p-1)
. Then
(\mu_{n})_{n}\in l_{p}
,
(z^{n})_{n}\in l_{q}
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\|(z_{n})_{n}\|_{q}=\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}}
. We apply Hölder inequality to get

|C_{\mu}(1)(z)|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}|z|^{n}\leq\|(\mu_{n})_{n}\|_{p}\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

Since
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{p-1}{p}<\gamma
, this yields
|C_{\mu}(1)(z)|=o\left(\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{\gamma}}\right)
as
|z|\rightarrow 1^{-}
.

∎

Example 1

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
with
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}=\infty

Proposition 3

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
v,w
be weights on
\mathbb{D}
such that
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
is continuous. Then

C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
and
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
are compact if and only if
C_{\mu}(H_{v}^{\infty})\subseteq H_{w}^{0}.

Proof.

By Theorem
1
, the operators
C_{\mu}^{**}
and
C_{\mu}
coincide on
H_{v}^{\infty}
. Therefore
C_{\mu}(H_{v}^{\infty})\subseteq H_{w}^{0}
if and only if
C^{**}_{\mu}((H_{v}^{0})^{**})\subseteq H_{w}^{0}
. This fact holds if and only if
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
is weakly compact by

[
23
, Proposition 17.2.7]
. Now
[
26
, Corollary 2.5]
implies that this is equivalent to the compactness of
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
. Finally, Theorem
1
also yield that
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is compact if and only if
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
is compact.
∎

Proposition 4

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
v,w
be weights on
\mathbb{D}.

(i)

If

C(v,w):=\sup_{0\leq r<1}w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(tr)(1-tr)}<\infty,

then
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
is continuous.

(ii)

If
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(tr)(1-tr)}=0,

then
C_{\mu}(H_{v}^{\infty})\subseteq H_{w}^{0}
. In particular, the condition implies that
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{0}
and
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
are compact.

The conditions are also necessary if the function
f:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
r\mapsto 1/v(r)
admits holomorphic extension
\tilde{f}
to
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
|\tilde{f}(z)|\leq f(|z|)
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}.
In this case,
\|C_{\mu}\|=C(v,w).

Proof.

Let
f\in H_{v}^{\infty}
with
\|f\|_{v}\leq 1.
For
0\leq r<1,

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|=r}w(z)\left|C_{\mu}f(z)\right|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|=r}w(|z|)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|f(tz)|}{(1-t|z|)}d\mu(t)\leq w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(tr)(1-tr)},

(2.1)

so the condition in (i) yields the boundedness of
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{\infty}
with
\|C_{\mu}\|\leq C(v,w),
and the condition in (ii), that
C_{\mu}(H_{v}^{\infty})\subseteq H_{w}^{0}
, in particular, that
C_{\mu}(1)\in H_{w}^{0}.
This fact, together with (i) and Theorem
1
implies the continuity of
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}.
The compactness of
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{0}\to H_{w}^{0}
and
C_{\mu}:H_{v}^{\infty}\to H_{w}^{0}
follows by Proposition
3
.

For the converses, assume the function
f:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
r\mapsto 1/v(r)
admits holomorphic extension
\tilde{f}
to
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
|\tilde{f}(z)|\leq f(|z|)
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}.
As
v(|z|)|\tilde{f}(z)|\leq v(|z|)f(|z|)
and
v(r)f(r)=1,

0\leq r<1,
we get
\|\tilde{f}\|_{v}=1.
Moreover,

w(r)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\mu(t)}{v(tr)(1-tr)}\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|=r}w(z)\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{f}(tz)}{(1-tz)}d\mu(t)\right|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D},|z|=r}w(z)|C_{\mu}(\tilde{f})|.

(2.2)

So, if we assume
C_{\mu}(\tilde{f})\in H_{v}^{\infty},
we get the condition in (i) is also necessary, and analogously for (ii) if we assume
C_{\mu}(\tilde{f})\in H_{v}^{0}.
The conclusion about the norm of the operator follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

Corollary 5

Let
\mu

[0,1)
and

\sigma(T):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ \lambda I-T\text{ is not invertible}\}
. When the space
E
is Fréchet, Banach or regular (LB), i.e. a countable inductive limit of Banach spaces in which each bounded set is contained and bounded in some step, the closed graph theorem
[
25
, Theorem 24.31]
implies that
\sigma(T):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ \lambda I-T\text{ is not bijective}\}
. The
point spectrum
of
T
is defined as
\sigma_{p}(T):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ \lambda I-T\text{ is not injective}\}
. When
E
is Banach and
T
is compact, the Riesz theorem ensures that
\sigma_{p}(T)
is countable,
\sigma(T)=\overline{\sigma_{p}(T)}
and
\overline{\sigma_{p}(T)}\setminus\sigma_{p}(T)\subseteq\{0\}
. The set
\rho(T)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(T)
is called the
resolvent
of
T.
If we want to distinguish the space, we write
\sigma(T,E),

\sigma_{p}(T,E)
and
\rho(T,E).

3.1
The spectrum of
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})

Proposition 13

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
such that
\mu((0,1))>0
and let
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
be well defined (and then continuous). The point spectrum satisfies
\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D}))=\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
and the eigenspace associated to
\mu_{n}
is generated by the function

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k},\ a_{k}=\frac{\mu_{k}\mu_{n}^{k-n-1}}{\prod_{j=n+1}^{k}(\mu_{n}-\mu_{j})},\ k\geq n+1,\ a_{n}=1.

(3.1)

Proof.

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is an eigenvalue, then there exists
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}),

f\neq 0,
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k})z^{n}=C_{\mu}(f)=\lambda f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda a_{n}z^{n}.

Thus, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we get
\lambda a_{n}=\mu_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k},
or equivalently,
a_{0}(\lambda-\mu_{0})=0,

a_{n}(\lambda-\mu_{n})=\mu_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k},

n\geq 1.

If
a_{n}=0
for every
n\leq n_{0}-1
and
a_{n_{0}}\neq 0,
then we get
a_{n_{0}}(\lambda-\mu_{n_{0}})=0,
and thus,
\lambda=\mu_{n_{0}}.
This yields
\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.
Moreover, as
(\mu_{n})_{n}
is strictly decreasing by Lemma
30
(iii),

a_{n}=\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n_{0}}-\mu_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k},\ n\geq n_{0}+1,

(3.2)

which implies
\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}=a_{n}\frac{\mu_{n_{0}}-\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n}}+a_{n}=\frac{\mu_{n_{0}}}{\mu_{n}}a_{n}.

Using this fact and (
3.2
), we get by induction that if
f_{n_{0}}
is an eigenfunction associated to
\mu_{n_{0}},
it must be of the form

f_{n_{0}}=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\text{ where }a_{n}=\frac{\mu_{n}\mu_{n_{0}}^{n-n_{0}-1}}{\prod_{j=n_{0}+1}^{n}(\mu_{n_{0}}-\mu_{j})}a_{n_{0}},\ n\geq n_{0}+1,\ a_{n_{0}}\in\mathbb{C}.

Now, observe that

\frac{a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}=\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}\frac{\mu_{n_{0}}-\mu_{n+1}}{\mu_{n_{0}}}>0,

hence, by Lemma
30
(i) and (ii) the radius of convergence of
f_{n_{0}}
is

R=\lim_{n}\frac{a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}=\lim_{n}\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}\geq 1,

which implies
f_{n_{0}}\in H(\mathbb{D})
for every
a_{n_{0}}\in\mathbb{C}
. As this is satisfied for every
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
we get
\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D}))=\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

∎

In the case of the Cesàro operator
C:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D}),
we get that the eigenspace associated to the eigenvalue
\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}
is generated by the eigenfunction
\frac{z^{n}}{(1-z)^{n+1}},
as obtained in
[
27
]
.

Proposition 14

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
such that
\mu((0,1))>0
and consider
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
well defined (and then continuous). The following are equivalent:

(i)

0\notin\sigma(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D})).

(ii)

C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is surjective.

(iii)

M_{(1/\mu_{n})_{n}}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D}),

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\mapsto\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\mu_{n}}z^{n}
is continuous.

(iv)

For every
r\in(0,1)
there exist
s\in(r,1)
and
C>0
such that
\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\leq C\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.

(v)

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists
C>0
such that
\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\leq C(1+\varepsilon)^{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

(i) is equivalent to (ii) because
0\notin\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D}))
by Proposition
13
.

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) Given
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}),
consider the problem of finding
g_{f}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k})z^{n}=C_{\mu}(g_{f})(z)=f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

This yields
\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}=\frac{b_{n}}{\mu_{n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.
As we can assume without loss of generality that
\mu_{0}=1,
we obtain

a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}=\frac{b_{n}}{\mu_{n}}-\frac{b_{n-1}}{\mu_{n-1}},\ n\in\mathbb{N},\ a_{0}=b_{0}.

As a consequence,
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is surjective if and only if

g_{f}(z)=b_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{b_{n}}{\mu_{n}}-\frac{b_{n-1}}{\mu_{n-1}}\right)z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})

(3.3)

for every
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}).
And this condition is equivalent to saying that the operator

M_{(1/\mu_{n})_{n}}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D}),\ f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\mapsto h_{f}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\mu_{n}}z^{n}

(3.4)

is continuous. Indeed,
g_{f}(z)=(1-z)h_{f}(z)
and then
h_{f}(z)=\frac{1}{1-z}g_{f}(z)
. Hence
g_{f}
is well defined and holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}
if and only if
h_{f}
is so.

(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv) First, observe that
M_{(1/\mu_{n})_{n}}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is continuous if and only if for every
r\in(0,1)
there exist
s\in(r,1)
and
C>0
such that
\sup_{n}\frac{|b_{n}|}{\mu_{n}}r^{n}\leq C\sup_{n}|b_{n}|s^{n}
for every
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D}).
And this condition is equivalent to (iv) (by evaluation on the monomials).

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(v) follows easily if we take
r=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}.
For the converse, given
0<r<1,
consider
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1-r}{r},

\epsilon<1,
and
s\in[r(1+\varepsilon),1).
Now, the hypothesis yields
C>0
such that
\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\leq C(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\leq C\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.

∎

Proposition 15

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
such that
\mu((0,1))>0
and consider
C_{\mu}:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D}).

(a)

For
\lambda\in\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},
the operator
C_{\mu}-\lambda I:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is not injective and not surjective. In fact,
z^{k}\notin(C_{\mu}-\mu_{k}I)(H(\mathbb{D})),

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(b)

For
\lambda\notin\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\cup\{0\},
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\lambda\notin\sigma(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D})).

(ii)

C_{\mu}-\lambda I:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D})
is surjective.

(iii)

For every
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D}),

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{b_{n}}{\prod_{j=n}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\lambda}\right)}\right)z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D})

(3.5)

Proof.

(a) First, take
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and consider
C_{\mu}-\mu_{k}I:H(\mathbb{D})\to H(\mathbb{D}).
By Proposition
13
we know that
C_{\mu}-\mu_{k}I
is not injective. Let us see that it is not surjective either by showing that
z^{k}
does not belong to the image of
C_{\mu}-\mu_{k}I.
Indeed, if
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in H(\mathbb{D})
is such that
(C_{\mu}-\mu_{k}I)(f)(z)=z^{k},
then

\displaystyle z^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\mu_{n}(\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j})-\mu_{k}a_{n}\right)z^{n}.

Since
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a sequence of strictly positive numbers, there is no
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
such that
\mu_{n}(\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j})-\mu_{k}a_{n}=0
for
n\neq k
and
\mu_{k}(\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j})-\mu_{k}a_{k}=1
. Hence
C_{\mu}-\mu_{k}I
is not surjective.

(b) We take
\lambda\notin\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\cup\{0\},

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D}),
and consider the problem of finding
g_{f}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
such that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}(\sum_{n=0}^{k}a_{n})z^{k}-\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}=(C_{\mu}-\lambda I)(g_{f})(z)=f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}.

This implies
\mu_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k}a_{n}-\lambda a_{k}=b_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
that is,
a_{k}(\mu_{k}-\lambda)=b_{k}-\mu_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}a_{n}.
Assuming without loss of generality that
\mu_{0}=1
we get

a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}}{\mu_{k}-\lambda}-\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{k}-\lambda}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}a_{n},\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ a_{0}=\frac{b_{0}}{\mu_{0}-\lambda}.

Inductively, for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
we get

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{k}}{\mu_{k}-\lambda}+\mu_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{k-n}\frac{\lambda^{k-n-1}b_{n}}{\prod_{j=n}^{k}(\mu_{j}-\lambda)}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{k}}{\mu_{k}-\lambda}-\frac{\mu_{k}}{\lambda^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{b_{n}}{\prod_{j=n}^{k}(1-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\lambda})}.

Now, observe that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{\mu_{k}-\lambda}z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D})
since
|\mu_{k}-\lambda|>\delta
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
for some
\delta>0
. Therefore,
g_{f}(z)\in H(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{b_{n}}{\prod_{j=n}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\lambda}\right)}\right)z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D})
.
This gives the equivalence (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii). Observe that (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) is trivial, since
\lambda\notin\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H(\mathbb{D})).

∎

Remark 3

Condition (
3.5
) is satisfied, for instance, if
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}<\infty.
Indeed, in this case the infinite product converges, and thus the radius of convergence of the power series is that of
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}(\sum_{n=0}^{k}|b_{n}|)z^{k}
, which belongs to
H(\mathbb{D})
because
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|b_{k}|z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D})
and

is well defined, and then continuous. In the next proposition we prove that this assumption can be relaxed.

Lemma 16

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1).
The following assertions are satisfied:

and

(3.7)

. Here, the constant giving the lower estimate can depend on

and on

satisfies that there exists

D
and on

(i) Fix

\lambda,

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\geq

(ii) Fix

,

and

and

∎

\{\mu_{n}:\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil\leq\gamma\}=\{\mu_{n}:\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil}}\in H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty})\subseteq\{\mu_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

(ii)

\{\mu_{n}:\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil<\gamma\}=\{\mu_{n}:\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil}}\in H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0})\subseteq\{\mu_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

Moreover, if there exists
D>0
(consider the maximum) such that
\mu_{n}\geq\frac{D}{n}
eventually, then we even get

(iii)

\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty})\subseteq\{\mu_{n}:\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor\leq\gamma\}=\{\mu_{n}:\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor}}\in H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty}\}

(iv)

\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0})\subseteq\{\mu_{n}:\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor<\gamma\}=\{\mu_{n}:\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor}}\in H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0}\}

Here,
\lceil x\rceil

(\text{resp.}\lfloor x\rfloor)
denotes the least (greatest) integer greater (smaller) than or equal to
x.

Proof.

As
H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq H(\mathbb{D})
, Proposition
18
(i) yields
\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0})\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty})\subseteq\{\mu_{n}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},
and if
\mu_{n}
is an eigenvalue, by Proposition
13
the corresponding eigenspace is generated by the function
f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k},
with
a_{n}=1
and

a_{k}=\frac{\mu_{k}\mu_{n}^{-1}}{\prod_{j=n+1}^{k}(1-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{n}})},\ k\geq n+1.

By Lemma
16
(i) we get

\prod_{j=n+1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{n}}\right)\gtrsim\frac{1}{k^{\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil}}.
Thus, as
a_{k}\lesssim k^{\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil-1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
and
g_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\alpha}},\ z\in\mathbb{D},
satisfies
g_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(\alpha)z^{k},\ z\in\mathbb{D},
with
c_{k}(\alpha)\cong k^{\alpha-1}
, we have

\sup_{|z|=r}|f_{n}(z)|v_{\gamma}(z)\lesssim\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}k^{\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil-1}r^{k}v_{\gamma}(r)\lesssim\sup_{|z|=r}\left|\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\lceil\frac{C}{\mu_{n}}\rceil}}\right|v_{\gamma}(z).

This yields (i) and (ii). Analogously, for
n
fixed and
D
as in the hypothesis, Lemma
16
(ii) yields

\prod_{j=n+1}^{k}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{n}}\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\lfloor D/\mu_{n}\rfloor}.

Now, as
c_{k}(\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor)\lesssim k^{\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor-1}\lesssim a_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
we have

\sup_{|z|=r}|f_{n}(z)|v_{\gamma}(z)\gtrsim\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}k^{\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor-1}r^{k}v_{\gamma}(r)\gtrsim\sup_{|z|=r}\left|\frac{1}{(1-z)^{\lfloor\frac{D}{\mu_{n}}\rfloor}}\right|v_{\gamma}(z)

which concludes (iii) and (iv).
∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
21
, we get the following:

Corollary 22

Let

be a Carleson measure. For every
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\{\mu_{n}:\ n\leq n_{0},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{0})\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\mu},H_{v_{\gamma}}^{\infty})
.

Remark 5

4
The operator
C_{\mu}
on Korenblum Fréchet and (LB) spaces

In this section we study the Cesàro-type operator
C_{\mu}
when it acts on Korenblum Fréchet and (LB) spaces. We refer to
[
25
, Chapter 25]
for all the definitions related to the locally convex spaces considered in this section.

For
0<\alpha<\beta,
then
H_{v_{\alpha}}^{\infty}\subseteq H_{v_{\beta}}^{\infty}
with continuous inclusions. So, given
\gamma\geq 0
we can consider the projective limit

A_{+}^{-\gamma}:=\cap_{\mu>\gamma}H_{v_{\mu}}^{\infty}=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|)^{\gamma+\frac{1}{k}}|f(z)|<\infty\text{ for every }k\in\mathbb{N}\},

which is a Fréchet Schwartz space under the norms
\|\ \|_{v_{\gamma}+\frac{1}{k}},

k\in\mathbb{N}.

Analogously, given
\gamma>0,
and
k\geq k_{0}
with
\gamma-\frac{1}{k_{0}}>0,
we can consider the inductive limit

A_{-}^{-\gamma}:=\cup_{\mu<\gamma}H_{v_{\mu}}=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|)^{\gamma-\frac{1}{k}}|f(z)|<\infty\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}\},

endowed with the finest locally convex topology such that all inclusions
H_{v_{\gamma-\frac{1}{k}}}^{\infty}\subseteq A_{-}^{-\gamma}
are continuous. With this topology,
A_{-}^{-\gamma}
is a complete regular (DFS)-space. If
\gamma=\infty,
then
A_{-}^{-\gamma}
is the Korenblum space
[
22
]
denoted simply by

A^{-\infty}:=\ind_{n\in\mathbb{N}}H_{v_{n}}^{\infty}=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|)^{n}|f(z)|<\infty\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Albanese et al.
[
2
]
determined the spectrum of the Cesàro operator
C
acting on the Korenblum type spaces
A_{+}^{-\gamma},

A_{-}^{-\gamma}
and
A^{-\infty}
and used it to prove the operator is never compact on them.

Proposition 23

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1).
The operator
C_{\mu}:A^{-\infty}\rightarrow A^{-\infty}
is continuous.

Proof.

For the weights
v_{n}(z)=(1-|z|)^{n},

z\in\mathbb{D},

n\in\mathbb{N},
the operator
C_{\mu}:H_{v_{n}}\rightarrow H_{v_{n+1}}
is continuous for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
by Corollary
5
. So, the continuity follows from the continuity of operators on inductive limits.
∎

Proposition 24

Assume
\mu
is a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1).
The following are equivalent:

(i)

\mu
is an
\varepsilon
-Carleson measure for every
0<\varepsilon<1,

(ii)

C_{\mu}:A_{+}^{-\gamma}\rightarrow A_{+}^{-\gamma}
is continuous for every
\gamma\geq 0,

(iii)

C_{\mu}:A_{-}^{-\gamma}\rightarrow A_{-}^{-\gamma}
is continuous for every
\gamma>0.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) It is known that
C_{\mu}
is continuous on
A_{+}^{-\gamma}
if and only if for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
there exists
l\in\mathbb{N},

l\geq k

is continuous (see e.g.
[
3
, Lemma 2.5]
). So, a necessary condition for the continuity is that
\mu
is a
(1-(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{l}))
-Carleson measure. As this must be satisfied for every

we get
\mu
must be an
\varepsilon
-Carleson measure for every
0<\varepsilon<1.
Let us see the suficiency.
Given
k\in\mathbb{N},
select
\delta>0
with
\delta<\frac{1}{k}.
For

as

is a
(1-\delta)
-Carleson measure, Theorem
10
yields

C_{\mu}:H_{v_{\gamma+\frac{1}{l}}}^{\infty}\rightarrow H_{v_{\gamma+\frac{1}{l}+\delta}}^{\infty}

is continuous.
Since

the continuity follows.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) By the Grothendieck factorization theorem
[
25
, Theorem 24.33]
,
C_{\mu}
is continuous on
A_{-}^{-\gamma}
if and only if for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
there exists
l\in\mathbb{N},

l\geq k
such that
C_{\mu}:H_{v_{\gamma-\frac{1}{k}}}^{\infty}\rightarrow H_{v_{\gamma-\frac{1}{l}}}^{\infty}
is continuous. Therefore,
\mu
must be a
(1-(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{l}))
-Carleson measure for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
i.e. (i) must be satisfied. Assume now (i) holds. Given
k\in\mathbb{N},
select
\delta>0
with
\delta<\frac{1}{k}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that

As
\mu
is a

10
yields

C_{\mu}:H_{v_{\gamma-\frac{1}{k}}}^{\infty}\rightarrow H_{v_{\gamma-\frac{1}{k}+\delta}}^{\infty}

is continuous.
Since

we get the continuity. ∎

Appendix A
Hausdorff moment problem for Borel probabilities on
[0,1)

This appendix collects a few classical results about Hausdorff moments for Borel probabilities on
[0,1)
. They provide us with examples which are relevant in connection with the theorems in the article.

We start with the following result, which is known to specialists. We state it and give its short proof, since it is very useful in the article.

Lemma 30

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
(\mu_{n})_{n}
its moments.Then the following hold:

(i)

(\mu_{n})_{n}
is a decreasing sequence satisfying
\lim_{n}\mu_{n}=0.

(ii)

\left(\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}\right)_{n}
is a decreasing sequence bounded below by
1
, so the limit
L=\lim_{n}\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}
exists, with
L\geq 1.

(iii)

If
\mu((0,1))>0
, then
(\mu_{n})_{n}
is a strictly decreasing sequence.

Proof.

(i)
(\mu_{n})_{n}
is clearly decreasing, so
\lim_{n}\mu_{n}=0
by the Monotone Convergence Theorem.

(ii) By Hölder’s inequality, we get

\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}d\mu\leq\left(\int_{0}^{1}(t^{\frac{n-1}{2}})^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(t^{\frac{n+1}{2}})^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2},

therefore
\mu_{n}^{2}\leq\mu_{n-1}\mu_{n+1}
and so,
\left(\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}\right)_{n}
is a decreasing sequence. As
\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}\geq 1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N},
then
\lim_{n}\frac{\mu_{n}}{\mu_{n+1}}
exists.

(iii) By assumption, there are
a,b\in(0,1)
such that
\mu([a,b])>0.
So, we can find
c_{n}>0
such that
\int_{0}^{1}(t^{n}-t^{n+1})d\mu\geq\int_{a}^{b}(t^{n}-t^{n+1})d\mu\geq c_{n}\mu([a,b])>0,
and we get
(\mu_{n})_{n}
is a strictly decreasing sequence.
∎

For a Borel probability measure on
[0,1)
, the condition
\mu((0,1))>0
in Lemma
30
is equivalent to
\mu\neq\delta_{0},
where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac measure on
[0,1)
.

The
difference sequences
of a given sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of real numbers are defined by
\Delta^{0}(a_{n})=a_{n}
,
\Delta^{1}(a_{n})=a_{n+1}-a_{n}
,
\Delta^{j}(a_{n})=\Delta^{1}(\Delta^{j-1}(a_{n}))
for
j>1
. A sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
is said to be
completely monotonic
whenever
((-1)^{j}\Delta^{j}(a_{n}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a sequence of positive numbers for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of positive numbers is said to be
a solution of the Hausdorff moment problem
if there exists a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
such that
\mu_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}d\mu(t)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

A proof of the following characterization due to Hausdorff can be seen in
[
28
]
Theorem 3.15.

Theorem 31

(Hausdorff)

A sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of positive numbers is a sequence of moments of a Borel probability
\mu
in
[0,1)
if and only
\mu_{0}=1
,
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is completely monotonic and
\lim_{n}\mu_{n}=0
.

Hausdorff theorem is stated for measures on
[0,1]
without the condition
\lim_{n}\mu_{n}=0
. If
\mu
is a probability on
[0,1]
, the condition
\mu(\{1\})=0
is easily seen to be equivalent to the condition
\lim_{n}\mu_{n}=0
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. Observe that
\int_{\{1\}}t^{n}d\mu=\mu(\{1\})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Example 5

If
0<a<1,
then
(a^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is the sequence of moments of a probability on
[0,1)
, as follows immediately from the Hausdorff theorem and the equality
(\Delta^{j}(a^{n}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}=((a-1)^{j}a^{n})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

A function
f\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
is said to be
completely monotonic
whenever
(-1)^{j}f^{(j)}(x)\geq 0
whenever
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The following theorem is a consequence of the Hausdorff theorem together with
[
31
, Theorem 11d, p.158]
.

Theorem 32

If
f\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
is a completely monotonic function such that
f(0)=1
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=0,
then
(f(n))_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of moment problems for a Borel probability on
[0,1)
.

Example 6

Let
\mu_{n}=(n+1)^{-p}
,
p>0
. The sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a sequence of moments for a Borel probability
\mu
on
[0,1)
.

Lemma 33

Assume that
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}[0,\infty)
and
(-1)^{j}f_{i}^{(j)}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[0,\infty)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
i=1,2
,
f_{1}(0)f_{2}(0)=1
,
f_{1}
bounded and
\lim_{x\to\infty}f_{2}(x)=0
. Then
((f_{1}(n)f_{2}(n))_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of moments for a Borel probability on
[0,1)
.

Proof.

Under these hypothesis,
\lim_{x\to\infty}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)=0
. By applying the Leibniz rule, we get

(-1)^{n}(f_{1}f_{2})^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
j\end{array}\right)(-1)^{j}f_{1}^{(j)}(x)(-1)^{n-j}f_{2}^{(n-j)}(x)\geq 0.

The result follows now immediately from Theorem
32
.
∎

The following example shows that, beyond multiples of the Cesàro operator, there are more examples satisfying the conditions of Proposition
21
.

Example 7

Let
\mu_{n}=\frac{n+2}{2(n+1)^{2}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.
There exists a probability
\mu
on
[0,1)
which is the solution of the Hausdorff moment problem for
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Indeed, it is enough to observe that, if one takes
f_{1}(x)=1+\frac{1}{x+1}
and
f_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{2(1+x)}
, then
\mu_{n}=f_{1}(n)f_{2}(n)
. The conclusion holds by Lemma
33
.

Abstract
. In this article, we study the limit behavior of solutions to an energy-critical complex Ginzburg-Landau equation. Via energy method, we establish a rigorous theory of the zero-dispersion limit from energy-critical complex Ginzburg-Landau equation to energy-critical nonlinear heat equation in dimensions three and four for both the defocusing and focusing cases. Furthermore, we derive the invisicid limit of energy-critical complex Ginzburg-Landau equation to energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation in dimension four for the focusing case. Keywords : Complex Ginzburg-Landau equation, nonlinear heat equation, nonlinear Schrödinger equation, zero-dispersion limit, inviscid limit Mathematics Subject Classification (2020) Primary: 35B40; Secondary: 35Q35, 35Q56



1.
Introduction

In this article, we will consider the Cauchy problem for the energy-critical complex Ginzburg-Landau equation (CGL) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=3,4
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}e^{-i\theta}v^{\theta}_{t}-\Delta v^{\theta}+\mu f(v^{\theta})=0,\\
v^{\theta}(0,x)=v^{\theta}_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
v^{\theta}
is a complex-valued function of
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
-\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
,
\mu=\pm 1
, and

f\left(v^{\theta}\right)=\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}v^{\theta}
.
When
\mu=1
, the equation (
1.1
) is called
defocusing
; while when
\mu=-1
, it is
focusing
. Equation (
1.1
) is the
L^{2}
gradient flow of the energy functional

\displaystyle E\left(v^{\theta}\right):=\int\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla v^{\theta}\right|^{2}+\mu\frac{d-2}{2d}\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The CGL equation can be derived from the Navier-Stokes equation via multiple scaling methods in convection (see for instance
[
14
]
), and solutions to CGL equation can be derived as an amplitude of some good unknowns in the asymptotic expansion near some equilibria such as in Taylor-Coutte flow and plane Poiseuille flow (see
[
1
,
8
,
7
]
). Compactness property of the Ginzburg-Landau type equation in dimension four was studied in
[
19
]
.

The global well-posedness (GWP) of (
1.1
) in the defocusing case was proved by C. Huang and B. Wang
[
10
]
, where they used the energy induction method developed by J. Bourgain
[
3
]
for the defocusing energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation. In the focusing case
\mu=-1
, (
1.1
) admits a stationary solution
W
, where

\displaystyle W(t,x)=W(x):=\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{d(d-2)}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}.

(1.2)

Motived by global well-posedness theory for energ-critical focusing Schrödinger equations (NLS), very recently, the authors
[
4
]
studied (
1.1
) in the focusing case, and established the global well-posedness theory when the energy of the initial data is less than the energy of
W
and the kinetic energy of the initial data is less than that of
W
, via the concentration-compactness/rigidity method.

In (
1.1
), the parameter
\theta\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
is a measure of the relative strength of the dispersive and dissipative effects in the equation: when
\theta\to 0,
(
1.1
) tends to the energy-critical nonlinear heat equation (NLH)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u+\mu f(u)=0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.3)

When
|\theta|\to\frac{\pi}{2}
,
(
1.1
) tends to the energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}iv_{t}+\Delta v+\mu f(v)=0,\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.4)

Before presenting the limit theory of solutions to energy-critical CGL, we would like to discuss some known results about the global well-posedness of the limit equations to energy-critical CGL, that is energy-critical NLS and energy-critical NLH.

(i)

The energy-critical nonlinear heat equation is the
L^{2}
gradient flow of the energy

\displaystyle E(u):=\int\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\mu\frac{d-2}{2d}|u|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The basic theory for the nonlinear heat equation can be found in
[
15
,
13
]
. Recently, the global well-posedness theroy for energy-critical focusing nonlinear heat equations has been studied by S. Gustafson and D. Roxanas
[
9
]
.

(ii)

The global well-posedness theory for energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation has been studied intensively in the last two decades. The global well-posedness theory of energy-critical NLS in three and four dimensions is almost complete (see for instance
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
17
]
), except the GWP of non-radial focusing case in dimension three.

Our main motivation of this article is to establish a rigorous theory for the limit
v^{\theta}\to u
in an appropriate sense as
\theta\to 0
in dimensions three and four. Meanwhile, we also consider the behavior of
v^{\theta}\to v
as
|\theta|\to\pi/2
for the focusing case in dimension four. In our first main result, we consider the case when
\theta\to 0
, which corresponds to the zero-dispersion limit of (
1.1
). Based on the global well-posedness theory of CGL and NLH, we derive the following result on zero-dispersion limit.

Theorem 1.1

(Zero-dispersion limit of energy-critical CGL)
.

Let
d=3,4
. Suppose that the initial data
v^{\theta}_{0}\in H^{1}
for (
1.1
) and
u_{0}\in H^{2}
for (
1.3
) satisfy
\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}-u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon
for a sufficiently small universal
\varepsilon>0
. In the focusing case, we additionally assume that

\displaystyle\max\left\{E\left(v_{0}^{\theta}\right),E(u_{0})\right\}<E(W)\text{ and }\max\left\{\left\|\nabla v_{0}^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}},\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\right\}<\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}}.

Then for any
T\in\left(0,\epsilon^{2}|e^{i\theta}-1|^{-2}\right)
, the solutions
v^{\theta}
of (
1.1
) and
u
of (
1.3
) satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\theta}-u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{0}\left(\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}-u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}+\left|e^{i\theta}-1\right|\left(T^{1/2}+1\right)\right),

where
C_{0}
depends only on
v^{\theta}_{0}
and
u_{0}
.

As far as we know, this seems to be the first result in the literature concerning the zero-dispersion of (variant of) the energy-critical nonlinear heat equation. As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we infer that on any interval
[0,T]\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, if the initial datum satisfies the conditions in
Theorem
1.1
, then

v^{\theta}\to u
in
L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}
as
\theta\to 0
.
The argument is based on energy method together with the known results of the strong space-time bound of energy-critical CGL and NLH, which is similar to the argument of the proof of the inviscid limit of CGL. But we need less regularity than the assmption in
[
10
]
for their inviscid limit of energy-critical NLS.

We now consider the case when
|\theta|\to\frac{\pi}{2}
, which is the inviscid limit of (
1.1
). In this case, the dissipative effect becomes weaker, and it is interesting to compare the solutions to energy-critical NLS and CGL. The inviscid limit of CGL has been studied in
[
2
,
10
,
18
,
20
]
. In particular, the inviscid limit of (
1.1
) in the defocusing case was proved in
[
10
,
18
]
. In our second main result, we complete the picture by establishing the inviscid limit of (
1.1
) in the focusing case, based on the global well-posedness and scattering of four dimensional focusing energy-critical NLS in
[
6
]
,

Theorem 1.2

(Inviscid limit of energy-critical CGL)
.

Let
d=4
and
\mu=-1
. Suppose that the initial data
v^{\theta}_{0}\in H^{1}
for (
1.1
) and
v_{0}\in H^{3}
for (
1.4
) satisfy
\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}-v_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon
for a universal
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. We also assume that

\displaystyle\max\left\{E\left(v_{0}^{\theta}\right),E(v_{0})\right\}<E(W)\text{ and }\max\left\{\left\|\nabla v_{0}^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}},\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\right\}<\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}}.

(1.5)

Then for any
T\in\left(0,\epsilon|e^{i\theta}-i|^{-1}\right)
, the solutions
v^{\theta}
to (
1.1
) and
v
to (
1.4
) satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\theta}-v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}_{x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{1}\left(\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}-v_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}+\left|e^{i\theta}-i\right|(T+1)\right),

where
C_{1}
depends only on
v^{\theta}_{0}
and
v_{0}
.

Remark 1.3
.

As one may observe from our later proofs, this result can be easily extended to dimension three if the GWP and scattering theory of focusing 3d energy-critical NLS in non-radial case were proven.

This result reveals that on any
[0,T]\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
, if the initial datum satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1.2
, then we have
v^{\theta}\to v\text{ in }L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\text{ as }|\theta|\to\frac{\pi}{2}
.
In the proof, the assumption (
1.5
) is necessary to make sure solutions of the energy-critical CGL and NLS in the focusing case are global and have finite space-time norm. The argument is similar to the argument in
[
2
,
10
,
18
,
20
]
.

Our notations are standard. We use
X\lesssim Y
when
X\leq CY
for some constant
C>0
and
X\sim Y
when
X\lesssim Y\lesssim X
. We write
C=C(a,b,\cdots)
meaning that
C
is a constant depending on
a,b,\cdots
.

For any space-time slab
I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to denote the space of functions
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, whose norm satisfies

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left(\int_{I}\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty
,
with the usual modification when
q
or
r
are equal to infinity. When
q=r
, we abbreviate
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{q}
as
L_{t,x}^{q}
.

We define the
S(I)-
norm of
u
on the time slab
I
to be

\|u\|_{S(I)}:=\left(\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2(d+2)}}.

2.
Energy-dissipation estimates

In this section, we will first recall some basic theory for the CGL. Then we establish the energy-dissipation estimates for energy-critical CGL and NLH.

2.1.
Basic results for CGL

In this subsection, we recall the Strichartz estimate of CGL, the variational estimates, as well as the global well-posedness of energy-critical CGL in
[
4
,
10
]
, which will be our start point for the limit theory.

Lemma 2.1

(Strichartz estimate,
[
4
]
)
.

If
\tilde{v}^{\theta}
satisfies

\displaystyle e^{-i\theta}\partial_{t}\tilde{v}^{\theta}-\Delta\tilde{v}^{\theta}=F,

on some interval
[0,T_{0}]
, then for
t_{0}\in[0,T_{0}]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{v}^{\theta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\leq\left\|\tilde{v}^{\theta}(t_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\|F\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}},

where
2\leq q,r,\tilde{q},\tilde{r}\leq\infty
,
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{2}{\tilde{q}}+\frac{d}{\tilde{r}}=\frac{d}{2}
, with
(q,r,d),\left(\tilde{q},\tilde{r},\tilde{d}\right)\neq(2,\infty,2)
, and
C
is a constant depending only on
q,r,\tilde{q},\tilde{r},d
.

Lemma 2.2

(Variational estimates,
[
4
]
)
.

For
\mu=-1
, if

\displaystyle E\left(v_{0}^{\theta}\right)\leq(1-\delta_{0})E(W),\left\|\nabla v_{0}^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\|\nabla W\|_{L^{2}},\delta_{0}>0,

then the solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle E\left(v^{\theta}(t)\right)\sim\|\nabla v^{\theta}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Theorem 2.3

(GWP of energy-critical CGL
[
4
,
10
]
)
.

Fix
d=3,4
and
v_{0}^{\theta}\in\dot{H}^{1}
. Assume one of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1)

\mu=1
,

(2)

\mu=-1
,
\left\|v_{0}^{\theta}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
and
E\left(v_{0}^{\theta}\right)<E(W)
.

Then the solution
v^{\theta}
to (
1.1
) is global and satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{S(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C=C\left(\left\|v_{0}^{\theta}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right).

2.2.
Energy-dissipation estimates for energy-critical CGL and NLH

In this subsection, we first give a global energy-dissipation control of (
1.1
), based on Theorem
2.3
, in both defocusing and focusing cases. Then we present the similar results for NLH.

Proposition 2.4

(Energy-dissipation estimates for (
1.1
) in the defocusing case)
.

Fix
d=3,4
and
v^{\theta}_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
. Then the solution
v^{\theta}
of (
1.1
) satisfies
v^{\theta}\in C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{W}_{x}^{1,\frac{2d}{d-2}}
and

\displaystyle E\left(v^{\theta}(t)\right)+\cos\theta\int\left|\Delta v^{\theta}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\cos\theta\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{S(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}\lesssim E\left(v^{\theta}_{0}\right).

If
v^{\theta}_{0}\in L^{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\theta}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\cos\theta\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla v^{\theta}(\tau)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\lesssim\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.1
) by
\overline{v^{\theta}}
, then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and integrating by parts, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+2\cos\theta\left\|\nabla v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\int\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(2.2)

Integrating (
2.2
) from
0
to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\cos\theta\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\lesssim\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}.

We next turn to the energy formulation. Multiplying (
1.1
) by
\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}
and integrating spatially, the real part of resulted equation is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\frac{d-2}{2d}\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\right)\\
&+\cos\theta\left(\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&-\cos\theta\left(\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)=0.\end{aligned}

(2.3)

Multiplying the complex conjugate of (
1.1
) by
\Delta v^{\theta}
, and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and taking the real part of the resulted equation, we find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\\
-\int\text{Re}\left(\overline{f\left(v^{\theta}\right)}\Delta v^{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{aligned}

(2.4)

Similarly, multiplying the complex conjugate of (
1.1
) by
f\left(v^{\theta}\right)
,
then integrating the resulted equation over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and taking the real part, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
-\int\text{Re}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{\Delta v^{\theta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}=0.\end{aligned}

(2.5)

Now we may substitute (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) into (
2.3
) to deduce

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E\left(v^{\theta}\right)+\cos\theta\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\cos\theta\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}-2\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(\overline{f\left(v^{\theta}\right)}\Delta v^{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{aligned}

Furthermore, there holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int\overline{f\left(v^{\theta}\right)}\Delta v^{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x&=-\frac{d}{d-2}\int\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left|\nabla v^{\theta}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{d-2}\int\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{4}{d-2}-2}\left(\nabla v^{\theta}\cdot\nabla v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{aligned}

which implies

\displaystyle\int\text{Re}\left(\overline{f\left(v^{\theta}\right)}\Delta v^{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq-\int\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left|\nabla v^{\theta}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

Hence

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E\left(v^{\theta}\right)+\cos\theta\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\cos\theta\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}\leq 0,\end{aligned}

whence for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle E\left(v^{\theta}(t)\right)+\cos\theta\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\cos\theta\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{S([0,t])}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}\leq E\left(v^{\theta}_{0}\right).

∎

Remark 2.5
.

The result in Proposition
2.4
partly has been shown in
[

]
. We include this and provide a proof to make the proposition as self-contained as possible.

We now establish the global energy-dissipation estimate of (

) in the focusing case.

Proposition 2.6

(Energy-dissipation estimates for
(
1.1
)
in the focusing case)
.

Let
d=3,4
. Suppose that
v^{\theta}_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
,
E\left(v_{0}^{\theta}\right)<E(W)
, and
\left\|v_{0}^{\theta}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
. Then for a solution
v^{\theta}
to (
1.1
), we have
v^{\theta}\in C_{t}^{0}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{W}_{x}^{1,\frac{2d}{d-2}}
and

\displaystyle E\left(v^{\theta}(t)\right)+\cos\theta\int\left|\Delta v^{\theta}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\cos\theta\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{S(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}\leq C=C\left(\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right).

If
v^{\theta}_{0}\in L^{2}
, then we also have

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\theta}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C=C\left(\left\|v^{\theta}_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right).

Proof.

We first derive the energy formulation. Multiplying (
1.1
) by
\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}
and integrating spatially, we find that the real part of the resulted equation is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}-\frac{d-2}{2d}\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\right)\\
&+\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{aligned}

(2.6)

Multiplying the complex conjugate of (
1.1
) by
\Delta u
, and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and taking the real part of the resulted equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\int\text{Re}\left(\overline{f\left(v^{\theta}\right)}\Delta v^{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{aligned}

(2.7)

Similarly, multiplying the complex conjugate of (
1.1
) by
f\left(v^{\theta}\right)
, integrating the resulted equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and taking the real part, we find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \cos\theta\int\text{Re}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\sin\theta\int\text{Im}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{v^{\theta}_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
-\int\text{Re}\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)\overline{\Delta v^{\theta}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}.\end{aligned}

(2.8)

Now we substitute (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) into (
2.6
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}E\left(v^{\theta}\right)+\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}+2\int\text{Re}\left(\overline{f\left(v^{\theta}\right)}\Delta v^{\theta}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{aligned}

(2.9)

Furthermore, the inequality

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int\left|v^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\Delta v^{\theta}\overline{v^{\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\Delta v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+4\left\|v^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{x}}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}\end{aligned}

together with (
2.9
) implies

3.1.
Higher regularity for energy-critical nonlinear heat equation

In this subsection, we show the solution of energy-critical NLH has higher regularity when the initial data lies in

Proposition 3.1

(Higher regularity for nonlinear heat equation)
.

Suppose that
u
is a solution to (
1.3
) and that
u_{0}\in H^{2}
. When
\mu=-1
, we additionally assume

\displaystyle E(u_{0})<E(W)\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}}+\|u\|_{L_{t}^{2}\dot{H}^{3}_{x}}\leq C=C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}\right).

(3.1)

Proof.

From Theorem
2.7
, we know that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}).

For any sufficiently small
\delta_{0}>0
, we can divide
\mathbb{R_{+}}
into a finite number of disjoint intervals,
\mathbb{R_{+}}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}I_{j}
, where
I_{j}=[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and
N=C(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}})
, so that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S(I_{j})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{0},\quad j=1,\ldots,N.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\left\|\nabla^{2}u(t_{j-1})\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\||u|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d+4}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+C\left\||u|^{\frac{6-d}{d-2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(3.2)

Then Hölder’s inequality allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\||u|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d+4}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\|u\|_{S(I_{j})}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(3.3)

By the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\||u|^{\frac{6-d}{d-2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{6-d}{d-2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(3.4)

Hence, combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left\|\nabla^{2}u(t_{j-1})\right\|_{L^{2}}+2C\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.5)

When
j=1
,
u(t_{j-1})=u_{0}
and so (

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}},

which in particular implies
\left\|\nabla^{2}u(t_{1})\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}
.

Therefore, repeating this procedure finitely many times, we may deduce

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2^{j}\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}},\quad j=1,\ldots,N.

(3.10)

Thus, repeating this procedure from

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}})\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{3}\right).\end{aligned}

(3.11)

(3.26)

implies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq 2\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C|e^{i\theta}-1|\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+2C\left|e^{i\theta}-1\right||I_{j}|^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(3.27)

Hence, by iteration, for
j=1,\ldots,N
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq 2^{j}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C\left|e^{i\theta}-1\right|\sum_{k=1}^{j}2^{k}|I_{j-k+1}|^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I_{j-k+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+C|e^{i\theta}-1|\sum_{k=1}^{j}2^{k-1}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d}}_{t,x}(I_{j-k+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(3.28)

Summing over
j
and using the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{k\leq j}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d}}_{t,x}(I_{j-k+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(E(u_{0}))\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}},

(3.29)

which follows from the Strichartz estimate and (
3.24
),
we conclude

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C2^{N+2}\big(\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+|e^{i\theta}-1|T^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+|e^{i\theta}-1|\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}\big),\end{aligned}

(3.30)

where
N
is independent of
\theta
.
This together with (
3.6
) implies the result for
L^{2}
. The approximated result for
\dot{H}^{1}
is similar to the
L^{2}
case and can be essentially derived by Lemma
3.2
, so we omit the details here.
∎

4.
Proof of the invisicid limit theory

In this section, we show the inviscid limit of (
1.1
), that is Theorem
1.2
. The proof relies on the following global well-posedness and scattering of energy critical NLS.

Theorem 4.1

(GWP & scattering of 4 dimensional focusing energy-critical NLS,
[
6
]
)
.

Fix
d=4
,
\mu=-1
and
v_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}.
If

\displaystyle E(v_{0})<E(W)\quad\text{and}\quad\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

then the solution
v
to (
1.4
) is global and satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{S(\mathbb{R})}\leq C=C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right).

We now give the higher regularity of NLS.

4.1.
Higher regularity of NLS

Proposition 4.2

(Higher regularity for NLS)
.

Suppose that
v
is a solution to (
1.4
) and that
v_{0}\in H^{3}
.
In addition, we assume

\displaystyle E(v_{0})<E(W)\quad\text{and}\quad\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Then for
k=2,3
, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{k}_{x}}\leq C=C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{H^{k}}\right).

(4.1)

Proof.

The proof for the case
k=2
is the same as that used in Proposition
3.1
and thus we only consider the case when
v_{0}\in\dot{H}^{3}
.
Divide
[0,T]
into a finite number
N
of disjoint intervals
I_{j}
such that the solution
v
of (
1.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{S(I_{j})}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{0}.

(4.2)

From the Duhamel formula

\displaystyle v(t)=e^{it\Delta}v_{0}-\mu\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}f(v(s))\,\mathrm{d}s,

(4.3)

we know

\displaystyle\nabla v_{t}=e^{it\Delta}\nabla\Delta v_{0}-e^{it\Delta}\nabla f(v_{0})-\mu\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\partial_{s}f(v(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

Applying the Strichartz estimate on each
I_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\left\|v(t_{j-1})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}+C\left\|\nabla f(u(t_{j-1}))\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+C\left\||v|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d+4}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+C\left\||v|^{\frac{6-d}{d-2}}\nabla vv_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(4.4)

By Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev’s inequality and (
4.2
), we deduce

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\||v|^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d+4}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\|v\|_{S(I_{j})}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d}}_{t,x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})},\end{aligned}

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\||v|^{\frac{6-d}{d-2}}\nabla vv_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\|v\|_{S(I_{j})}^{\frac{6-d}{d-2}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad\times\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(4.6)

For the second term on the right hand side of (
4.4
), we may apply Sobolev’s inequality and interpolation to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|\nabla f\left(v\left(t_{j-1}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|v(t_{j-1})\|_{L^{\frac{4d}{d-2}}}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\|u(t_{j-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}\lesssim\|v(t_{j-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\|v(t_{j-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}.\end{aligned}

(4.7)

Substituting
(
4.5
) – (
4.7
) into (
4.4
), with the smallness of
\delta
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v(t_{j-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}\left(1+\|v(t_{j-1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\right).

(4.8)

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\bigcap L^{2}_{t}L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}\left(1+\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\right).

Since

\displaystyle-\nabla\Delta v=-\nabla u_{t}-\mu\nabla f(v),

it follows from (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\nabla\Delta v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\lesssim\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\|\nabla f(v)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}},\end{aligned}

which implies

\displaystyle\|v(t_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}\leq C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}.

(4.9)

Combining (
4.7
), (
4.8
) with (
4.9
) leads to the estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Delta v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}.

Hence, by a finite induction argument for
j=1,\ldots,N
, we find

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Delta v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}},

which is (
4.1
) when
k=3
.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Set
\tilde{w}=v^{\theta}-v
. Then
\tilde{w}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}e^{-i\theta}\tilde{w}_{t}-\Delta\tilde{w}=\mu\left(f(v^{\theta})-f(v)\right)-\left(e^{-i\theta}-i\right)\left(\Delta v-\mu f(v)\right),\\
\tilde{w}(0,x)=\tilde{w}_{0}(x)=v^{\theta}_{0}(x)-v_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(4.10)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d=3,4
. Suppose that
\tilde{w}
satisfies (
4.10
). There exists a small
\varepsilon>0
such that if
\|\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon
, then for each
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{w}\|_{S([0,T])}\leq C=C\left(T,\left\|\tilde{w}_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}\right).

(4.11)

Proof.

Following the argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we may divide
[0,T]
into a finite number
N
of disjoint intervals
I_{j}

such that the solution
v
of (
1.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{S(I_{j})}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\delta_{0}.

(4.12)

By the Strichartz estimate, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\|\nabla\tilde{w}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\|\nabla\tilde{w}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\tilde{w}(t_{j-1})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+|e^{i\theta}-i|\left(\left\|\nabla\Delta v\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\|\nabla f(v)\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\\
&\quad+\left\|\nabla\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)-f(v)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(4.13)

The terms on the right-hand side of (
4.13
) can be estimated as follows. First of all, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\Delta v\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq|I_{j}|\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{3}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(4.14)

Secondly, we may follow the same argument as (
3.18
)–(
3.20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|\nabla f(v)\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\delta_{0}^{\frac{d+2}{d-2}},\end{aligned}

(4.15)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\|\nabla\left(f\left(v^{\theta}\right)-f(v)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\nabla\tilde{w}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{d+2}{d-2}}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left\|\nabla\tilde{w}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+\delta_{0}\left\|\nabla\tilde{w}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}.\end{aligned}

(4.16)

When
j=1
,
\tilde{w}(t_{0})=\tilde{w}_{0}
. Combining (
4.13
)–(
4.16
) with Proposition
4.2
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq C\|\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+C|e^{i\theta}-i|\left(TC\left(\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)\|v_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{3}}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{d+2}{d-2}}\right)\\
&\quad+C\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{d+2}{d-2}}+C\delta_{0}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+C\delta_{0}\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\frac{2d+4}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\end{aligned}

with a universal constant

.

such that

4.17

\displaystyle\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla\tilde{w}\|_{L^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-2}}_{t}L^{\frac{2d(d+2)}{d^{2}+4}}_{x}(I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2C\varepsilon,

On the Reducedness of Quiver Schemes
Yehao Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper we prove that a quiver scheme in characteristic zero is reduced if the moment map is flat. We use the reducedness result to show that the equivariant integration formula computes the K-theoretic Nekrasov partition function of five dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 quiver gauge theories when the moment map is flat. We also give an explicit characterization of flatness of moment map for finite and affine type A Dynkin quivers with framings. As an application, we give a refinement of a theorem of Ivan Losev on the relation between quantized Nakajima quiver variety in type A and parabolic finite W-algebra in type A.



1.
Introduction

Nakajima’s quiver varieties play important roles in representation theory, algebraic geometry, mathematical physics and other fields. For example they geometrically realize representations of Kac-Moody algebras
[
1
]
, Yangians
[
2
]
, quantum affine algebras
[
3
]
, and they are the key objects in the study of Bethe/gauge correspondences
[
4
]
. They also show up as Higgs branches of three dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
quiver gauge theories
[
5
]
.

Quiver varieties can be defined analytically as hyper-Kähler reductions of ADHM data of a quiver
[
6
]
, and also algebraically as a GIT quotient
[
6
]
. The algebraic construction gives rise to a
quiver scheme
, whose underlying reduced scheme structure is the quiver variety. The formal definition is as follows.

Let
(Q,\mathbf{v})
be a quiver, where
Q
is a finite directed graph with vertex set
Q_{0}
and arrow set
Q_{1}
, and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
is called the dimension vector. Define the representation space of
(Q,\mathbf{v})

(1.1)

\displaystyle R(Q,\mathbf{v})=\bigoplus_{a\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{t(a)}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{h(a)}}),

where
h(a)
and
t(a)
are head and tail of an arrow
a\in Q_{1}
. The reductive group
G(\mathbf{v})=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{GL}(\mathbf{v}_{i})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}
naturally acts on
R(Q,\mathbf{v})
, where
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
embeds as the diagonal center and it acts trivially. There is a moment map
\mu:T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}
associated to the
\GL(\mathbf{v})
-action. Let
Z
be the subspace of
\prod_{\mathbf{v}_{i}\neq 0}\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{C}^{Q_{0}}
that is annihilated by
\mathbf{v}
. Note that
Z
can be identified with
(\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})/[\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v}),\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})])^{*}
, which is a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}
. Similarly let
\Theta
be the subspace of
\prod_{\mathbf{v}_{i}\neq 0}\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{Q_{0}}
that is annihilated by
\mathbf{v}
. For
\theta\in\Theta
, we denote the
\theta
-semistable (respectively
\theta
-stable) locus of
\mu^{-1}(Z)
by
\mu^{-1}(Z)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
(respectively
\mu^{-1}(Z)^{\theta\mathrm{-s}}
). For the definition of
\theta
-(semi)stability, see
[
7
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Define the universal quiver scheme with stability condition
\theta\in\Theta
by the GIT quotient

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v}):=\mu^{-1}(Z)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}/G(\mathbf{v}).

And for
\lambda\in Z
, define the quiver scheme with stability condition
\theta
by the GIT quotient

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v}):=\mu^{-1}({\lambda})^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}/G(\mathbf{v}).

Here we do not take reduced scheme structure.

In the study of using the cohomology of quiver schemes to geometrize representations of quantum algebras, it is fine to take the underlying reduced scheme structure, since this does not make a difference to the cohomology. However in other circumstances the potentially non-reduced scheme structure does raise an issue.

One example comes from the computation of K-theoretic Nekrasov partition function of 5d
\mathcal{N}=2
quiver gauge theories, which is the equivariant K-theory class of
\Gamma(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})})
for a generic stability parameter
\theta
(see Definition
2.9
). Physicists proposed a way to compute it using the equivariant integration
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, which effectively computes the equivariant K-theory class of the differential graded algebra

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{C}[T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})]\overset{L}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}]}\mathbb{C}\right)^{G(\mathbf{v})},

via the Koszul resolution of
\mathbb{C}
as a
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}]
-module. There are two potential issues in this proposal. The first is that (
1.4
) might have non-zero homological degree components, which maps to zero in
\Gamma(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})})
via the natural projection
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
. One may resolve this issue by requiring that the moment map
\mu:T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}
is flat, then (
1.4
) is concentrated in homological degree zero, and this is in fact the case for many examples computed in
[
10
]
. Another issue is that the
H^{0}
part of (
1.4
) is
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})]
, which might not be isomorphic to
\Gamma(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})})
:
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
can have different dimensions, and even if they have the same dimension, they are not isomorphic if there are nilpotent elements in
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})]
. This is the place where reducedness becomes important. Nevertheless, we will see that if the moment map is flat, then both of the potential issues are resolved, namely the differential graded algebra (
1.4
) is quasi-isomorphic to
\Gamma(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})})
(see Corollary
2.16
), in other words the equivariant integration formula holds under the assumption on the flatness of the moment map.

It seems to be unknown whether
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is in general reduced or not, though in some cases the reducedness have been shown. For example Gan and Ginzburg
[
11
]
showed that when
Q
is a framed Jordan quiver,
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced for all
(\theta,\lambda)\in\Theta\times Z
; Crawley-Boevey
[
12
]
showed that if
\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
then
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced, later Bellamy and Schedler
[
13
]
extended Crawley-Boevey’s result to that if
\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma_{\lambda,\theta},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}\cap Z
, then
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced.

The main goal of this paper is to simultaneously generalize previous results on reducedness of quiver schemes of Gan and Ginzburg
[
11
]
, Crawley-Boevey
[
12
]
, Bellamy and Schedler
[
13
]
. The formal statement is as follows.

Theorem A
.

If the moment map
\mu
is flat along
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
, then the scheme
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced.

When
Q
is a framed Jordan quiver then
\mu
is flat (cf. Proposition
4.2
). When
\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma_{\lambda,\theta}
then
\mu
is flat along
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}

[
13
, Proposition 3.28]
. Therefore Theorem
A
indeed covers previous results on reducedness of quiver schemes in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. A special case of the above theorem is the following.

Theorem B
.

If the moment map
\mu
is flat, then
\forall(\theta,\lambda)\in\Theta\times Z
, the scheme
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced.

In fact Theorem
A
can be deduced from Theorem
B
, see Proposition
2.7
.

Remark 1.2
.

If
\mu
is flat along
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
(assume it is non-empty), then the support of the dimension vector
\mathbf{v}
must be connected. In fact, if
\Supp(\mathbf{v})=\Supp(\mathbf{v}^{(1)})\sqcup\Supp(\mathbf{v}^{(2)})
such that
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}+\mathbf{v}^{(2)}
, then the image of
\mu
lies in
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v}^{(1)})^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v}^{(2)})^{*}
, which is a proper linear subspace of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}
, but the flatness of
\mu
along
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
implies that the image of
\mu
is Zariski-dense in
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}
, we get a contradiction. However, this is just a feature of our definition of the group action that it does not take the possibility of multiple connected components of
\Supp(\mathbf{v})
into account, a more careful definition could separate the components and discuss flatness locally on each component. This is a straightforward generalization of the discussion in this paper, and in order to simplify notation we will not invoke this generalization throughout this paper.

Remark 1.3
.

One might wonder if Theorem
B
generalizes to other Hamiltonian reductions, namely if
M
is a complex symplectic representation of complex algebraic group
G
with a flat moment map
\mu:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, then is
M\sslash_{0}G=\mu^{-1}(0)/G
reduced or not? The answer is that
M\sslash_{0}G
is not reduced in general, for example the algebraic Uhlenbeck partial compactification of moduli space of framed
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-instantons on
S^{4}
with instanton number
4
is isomorphic to a Hamiltonian reduction with flat moment map, but it is not reduced, see
[
14
,
15
]
for more detail.

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
we present preliminaries on quiver schemes that will be useful in the proof of Theorem
A
, and we also derive some corollaries of Theorem
A
. Specifically we recall a criterion on the flatness of moment map in
2.1
, and recall a theorem on étale transversal slice in
2.3
, then reduces the proof of Theorem
A
to that of Theorem
B
. In
2.8
and
2.11
we discuss generic stability parameters and deduce Lemma
2.15
which is a key to induction step in the proof of Theorem
B
, then prove Corollary
2.16
which is useful in applications (for example the computation of K-theoretic Nekrasov partition functions). In
2.17
we apply the Theorem
B
to construct the
(\pm 1)
-reflection isomorphism for all
(\theta,\lambda)\in\Theta\times Z
, assuming that the moment map is flat.

In Section
3
we present the proof of Theorem
B
. The idea is to use the induction on
|\mathbf{v}|=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{v}_{i}
, combined with Lemma
2.15
and a result of Crawley-Boevey recalled in Lemma
3.6
. Then it boils down to some simple cases which are discussed in
3.1
,
3.2
, and the end of Section
3
.

In Section
4
we discuss the application of Theorem
B
to the study of quantization of quiver schemes. In
4.1
we focus on affine type A Dynkin quivers and give an explicit description of the set of
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
such that the moment map for the framed quiver
(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
is flat (Proposition
4.2
). In
4.4
we recall the definition of quantum Hamiltonian reduction and also its sheafified version, and show that in the quiver scheme case, if the moment map is flat and
\mathbf{v}
is indivisible then the quantized quiver scheme is isomorphic to certain sub-algebra of global sections of quantized structure sheaf on a resolution (Proposition
4.9
). Finally in
4.10
we present a refinement of a theorem of Ivan Losev on the relation between quantized Nakajima quiver schemes in type A and parabolic finite W-algebras in type A (Proposition
4.11
).

2.
Preliminaries on Quiver Schemes

2.1.
Criterion of flatness of the moment map

We recall a theorem of Crawley-Boevey here.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The moment map
\mu
is flat if and only if

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})\geq\sum_{t=1}^{r}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(t)}),

for all decomposition
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}+\cdots+\mathbf{v}^{(r)}
into non-zero elements in
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. Here
\mathbf{p}
is the function

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})=1-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{v},

where
C_{Q}
is the Cartan matrix of
Q
.

It is worth mentioning that
[
16
]
gives an explicit description of the set of
\mathbf{v}
such that the moment map is flat in terms of
(-1)
-reflections (see
loc. cit.
for the definition). We will come back to this point at this end of this section.

2.3.
Transverse slice

One important tool of our proof of Theorem
A
is the following result essentially due to Bellamy and Schedler
[
13
]
of which the
\theta=0
case goes back to Crawley-Boevey
[
17
]
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Take
\lambda\in Z
, then for every point
x\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
such that its orbit
G(\mathbf{v})\cdot x
is closed in
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
,
G(\mathbf{v})\cdot x
has an étale transverse slice in
x\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
such that it is étale locally isomorphic to an open neighborhood
U
of
0\in\hat{\mu}^{-1}(0)
, where
\hat{\mu}
is the moment map for another quiver
(\hat{Q},\hat{\mathbf{v}})
such that
G(\hat{\mathbf{v}})\cong G(\mathbf{v})_{x}
(stabilizer of
x
). In addition, this étale transverse slice gives rise to an isomorphism:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{x}\cong\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{0}.

Here
(-)^{\wedge}_{x}
means formal completion at
x
. Moreover if
\mu
is flat at
x
, then
\hat{\mu}
is flat.

Proof.

The statement of étale transverse slice and local isomorphism are essentially
[
13
, Theorem 3.8]
, and the argument in
loc. cit.
works at the full scheme-theoretic level, i.e. not just for underlying reduced scheme structure. And we also emphasize that their argument works for general
\lambda\in Z
, not just for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}\cap Z
. We only need to show that if
\mu
is flat at
x
, then
\hat{\mu}
is flat.

Recall some of the ingredients of the construction of étale transverse slice. Since the stabilizer
G(\mathbf{v})_{x}
is reductive by Matsushima’s theorem
[
18
]
, there is
G(\mathbf{v})_{x}
-stable complement
L
of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})_{x}
in
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})
. By
[
17
, Lemma 4.1]
, the
G(\mathbf{v})_{x}
-submodule
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})\cdot x\subset T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})
is isotropic, and by
[
17
, Corollary 2.3]
, there exists a coisotropic
G(\mathbf{v})_{x}
-module complement
C
to
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})\cdot x
in
T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})
. Let
W=(\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})\cdot x)^{\perp}\cap C
. The composition of
\mu:T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}
with the restriction map
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\mathbf{v})^{*}_{x}
is denoted by
\mu_{x}
, and the restriction of
\mu_{x}
to
W
is denoted by
\hat{\mu}
. Then there is an identification
W\cong T^{*}R(\hat{Q},\hat{\mathbf{v}})
, with the moment map being
\hat{\mu}
.

Note that the natural map
\eta:G(\mathbf{v})\times^{G(\mathbf{v})_{x}}C\rightarrow T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v})
is étale at
(1,0)\in G(\mathbf{v})\times^{G(\mathbf{v})_{x}}C
, therefore the composition
\mu\circ\eta
is flat at
(1,0)
. Since
\mu|_{C}
agrees with the composition
\mu\circ\eta\circ\iota
, where
\iota:C=G(\mathbf{v})_{x}\times^{G(\mathbf{v})_{x}}C\hookrightarrow G(\mathbf{v})\times^{G(\mathbf{v})_{x}}C
is the natural embedding, we see that
\mu|_{C}
is flat at
0\in C
, thus
\mu_{x}|_{C}
is flat at
0\in C
. According to
[
17
, Lemma 4.7]
we have
C=C^{\perp}\oplus W
so there is a projection map
p:C\rightarrow W
, and it is easy to see that
\hat{\mu}\circ p=\mu_{x}|_{C}
, thus
\hat{\mu}
is flat at
0\in W
. Then it follows that
\hat{\mu}
is flat by the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariance of
\hat{\mu}
, here the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acts on the doubling of
\hat{Q}
by scaling all arrows with weight one.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

(\hat{Q},\hat{\mathbf{v}})
is determined as following. Suppose that
x
decomposes as

x=x_{1}^{\oplus k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus x_{r}^{\oplus k_{r}}

where
x_{i}
are non-isomorphic
\theta
-stable representations, and let
\mathbf{v}^{(t)}
be the dimension vector of

. Then the vertex set of
\hat{Q}
is
\{1,\cdots,r\}
with dimension vector
\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}=k_{t},t\in\hat{Q}_{0}

The adjacency matrix of
\hat{Q}
is determined from its Euler form
(-,-)_{\hat{Q}}
, i.e. the sum of adjacency matrix and its transpose, which is

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\hat{}\mathbf{e}_{t},\hat{}\mathbf{e}_{u})_{\hat{Q}}=2\delta_{tu}-\mathbf{v}^{(t)}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{v}^{(u)}.

Here
\hat{}\mathbf{e}_{t}
is the dimension vector on

such that it is

(2.5)

\displaystyle\hat{}\mathbf{p}\left(\sum_{t}k_{t}\hat{}\mathbf{e}_{t}\right)=\mathbf{p}\left(\sum_{t}k_{t}\mathbf{v}^{(t)}\right).

Lemma 2.6
.

For a quiver
(Q,\mathbf{v})
, the reduced scheme
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\mathrm{red}}
is normal for all
(\theta,\lambda)
. Moreover, following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The moment map
\mu
is flat along
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
.

(2)

For every decomposition
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\mathbf{v}_{t}
such that there exists mutually non-isomorphic
\theta
-stable representations
x_{t}
of dimension
\mathbf{v}_{t}
and
\lambda\cdot\mathbf{v}_{t}=0
, then

\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})\geq\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}_{t}).

(3)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})=2\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}).

Proof.

In the view of Lemma
2.4
, for every point
x\in\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
, the formal completion
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{x}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{0}
of some quiver
(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})
, then the normality of
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\mathrm{red}}
follows from
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
.

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
: For every point
x\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
with closed
G(\mathbf{v})
-orbit, we attach the quiver
(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})
to it, and by Lemma
2.4

\hat{\mu}
is flat, so we have

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{x}=\dim\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{0}=2\hat{}\mathbf{p}(\hat{}\mathbf{v})=2\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}).

Here we use the equation (
2.5
).

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
: Suppose there is a decomposition
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\mathbf{v}_{t}
such that there exists mutually non-isomorphic
\theta
-stable representations
x_{t}
of dimension
\mathbf{v}_{t}
and
\lambda\cdot\mathbf{v}_{t}=0
, then let
x=x_{1}^{\oplus k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus x_{r}^{\oplus k_{r}}
be a point in
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
with closed

-orbit, and we attach the quiver
(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})
to it, then we have
\dim\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{0}=2\hat{}\mathbf{p}(\hat{}\mathbf{v})
, therefore
\dim\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})=2\hat{}\mathbf{p}(\hat{}\mathbf{v})
. By
[

, Theorem 1.3]
we see that
\hat{\mu}
is flat, thus by

12

we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})=\hat{}\mathbf{p}(\hat{}\mathbf{v})\geq\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\hat{}\mathbf{p}(\hat{}\mathbf{e}_{t})=\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}_{t}).

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: Generalizing
[
17
, Corollary 6.4]
to
\theta
-stable representations, of which the proof follows verbatim as
loc. cit.
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\xi^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\tau})\leq\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}-1+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})+\sum_{t=1}^{r}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}_{i}),

Here
\tau=(k_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1};\cdots;k_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r})
is a representation type such that
\mathbf{v}_{i}\cdot\theta=\mathbf{v}_{i}\cdot\lambda=0
, and
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\tau}
is the locus of representation type
\tau
, and
\xi:\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is the quotient map. Therefore we have
\dim\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}\leq\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}-1+2\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})
. On the other hand
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)
is a fiber of morphism between two smooth schemes of relative dimension

, every irreducible component of
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)
has dimension
\geq\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}-1+2\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})
. Since
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
is open in
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)
, this forces
\dim\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}-1+2\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})
, and therefore
\mu
is flat along
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
by Miracle flatness theorem.

An immediate consequence of Lemma
2.4
is the following.

Proposition 2.7
.

Theorem
B
implies Theorem
A
.

Proof.

Assume that Theorem
B
holds. By Lemma
2.4
there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{x}\cong\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})^{\wedge}_{0}

\hat{\mu}
is flat for the quiver
(\hat{Q},\hat{\mathbf{v}})
so
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\hat{Q},\hat{}\mathbf{v})

\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced at

. Let
x
runs through the set of points in
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
with closed

-orbits, this set maps surjectively to

\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})

2.8.
Generic parameters

Definition 2.9
.

We show that
(\theta,\lambda)\in\Theta\times Z
is
generic
if

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\theta,\lambda)\in(\Theta\times Z)\bigg\backslash\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\\
\mathbf{v}\not\propto\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{0<\mathbf{v}^{\prime}<\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{v}^{{}^{\prime}\perp}.

Here the partial order on
\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
is such that
\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\leq\mathbf{v}^{(2)}
if
\mathbf{v}^{(2)}-\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. The set of generic
(\theta,\lambda)
is denoted by
(\Theta\times Z)_{0}
, and we use the notation
\Theta_{0}
(resp.
Z_{0}
) to denote the intersection of

Lemma 2.13
.

If the moment map
\mu
is flat, and
(\theta,\lambda)
is generic, and moreover assume that either
\lambda\in Z_{0}
or
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced and irreducible.

Proof.

If
\lambda\in Z_{0}
then we can set
\theta
equals to zero since
p^{\theta}:\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is isomorphism. Therefore we are in the setups of Bellamy and Schedler
[
13
, 1.1]
. We claim that
\mathbf{v}\in\Sigma_{\lambda,\theta}
(see
[
13
, Section 2]
for notation), this means that for all decomposition
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}+\cdots+\mathbf{v}^{(r)}
into non-zero elements in
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
such that
\forall t\in\{1,\cdots,r\},\mathbf{v}^{(t)}\cdot\lambda=\mathbf{v}^{(t)}\cdot\theta=0
, the inequality

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})>\sum_{t=1}^{r}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(t)})

holds. Since
(\theta,\lambda)
is generic by assumption, we only need to show the inequality when
\mathbf{v}
is divisible and
\mathbf{v}^{(t)}=k_{t}\mathbf{w}
for
k_{t}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we already have

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})\geq\sum_{t=1}^{r}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(t)}),

and it remains to show that the inequality must be strict, i.e. equality never holds. In effect, we expand two sides the above inequality as functions of
\mathbf{w}
:

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\right)^{2}\mathbf{w}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{w}\geq r-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{w}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{w},

and conclude that
\mathbf{w}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{w}<0
. Since
\mathbf{w}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{w}
is an even number, so
\mathbf{w}\cdot C_{Q}\mathbf{w}\leq-2
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})-\sum_{t=1}^{r}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(t)})

\displaystyle\geq 1-r+\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}\right)^{2}-\left(\sum_{t=1}^{r}k_{t}^{2}\right)=1-r+2\sum_{1\leq t<u\leq r}k_{t}k_{u}

\displaystyle\geq 1-r+2{{r}\choose{2}}=(r-1)^{2}>0,

and our claim is justified. Then
[
13
, Corollary 3.22]
shows that the stable locus of
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})^{s}
, is dense. And by
[
13
, Theorem 3.27]

\displaystyle\dim\xi^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})\backslash\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})^{s})<\dim\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}.

Here
\xi:\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is the quotient map. Since
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
is Cohen-Macaulay hence equidimensional, this implies that the set of
\theta
-stable points in
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
is dense. Since
\mu
is smooth at
\theta
-stable points, we conclude that
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
is generically smooth. Combined with the Cohen-Macaulay property, we see that
\mu^{-1}(\lambda)^{\theta\mathrm{-ss}}
is reduced, thus
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced.
∎

It is easy to see that
\forall\theta\in\Theta

and it is compatible with (

Lemma 2.15
.

If the moment map
\mu
is flat, and moreover assume that
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is normal, then
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced if
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced, and if this is the case then the morphism
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta}:\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is birational.

Proof.

Note that the normality of
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
implies that
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta}
has connected fibers since it is an isomorphism over the open subset
Z_{0}
. In particular, the restriction of
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta}
to any fiber over
\lambda\in Z
gives rise to a birational morphism
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\mathrm{red}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\mathrm{red}}
since both sides have the same dimension and
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta}
has connected fibers.
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced by Lemma
2.13
, and it remains to show that
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced.

We claim that
Rp^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
is concentrated in degree zero and
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
is flat over
Z
. In effect, since both
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
and
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
are flat over
Z
and
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta}
is proper,
Rp^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
is is a relative perfect complex, and its formation commutes with base change
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
. In particular, we base change to
i:0\hookrightarrow Z
, and get

\displaystyle Li^{*}Rp^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}\cong Rp^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}({\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})}).

The RHS is concentrated in degree zero, because both
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\mathrm{red}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
(which is reduced by Lemma
2.13
) have symplectic singularities
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
, in particular they have rational singularities, moreover the induced morphism between them is birational, so we conclude that
R^{k}p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}({\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})})
for
k>0
(using the fact that any desingularization of
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is automatically a desingularization of
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})_{\mathrm{red}}
). Hence
Rp^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
is concentrated in degree zero in an open neighborhood of
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
. Next we observe that there is a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action on the quiver which scales every arrow with weight one, this action commutes with
\GL(\mathbf{v})
-action and descends to actions on
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
and
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
and
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta}
is equivariant. Since
Z
has positive weights under this
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action,
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
contracts to
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v})
under this
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action, henceforth
Rp^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
is concentrated in degree zero on the whole
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
.
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
is flat over
Z
because it’s relatively perfect over
Z
.

The punchline of above discussions is that we have a homomorphism between sheaves of rings

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})},

and it is an isomorphism on the open locus
Z_{0}
. By the assumption that
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is normal, the homomorphism (
2.10
) is isomorphism. Since
p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v})}
has base change property, specialization to arbitrary
\lambda\in Z
gives us isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})}\cong p^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\theta*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})}.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced if
\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v})
is reduced.
∎

is connected), and we assume that
a\leq c
(otherwise we just reverse the direction of arrows).

(1)

c\geq a\geq 1
. We claim that (a) is true in this case. In effect,

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})-N\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})-K\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2})

\displaystyle=(a-1)(N-1)N+(c-1)(K-1)K+bNK+1-N-K

\displaystyle\geq NK-N-K+1>0.

(2)

a=0,c>1
. We claim that (a) is true in this case. Consider
\mathbf{w}=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2})=(1,K)
, then it is easy to see that
\mathbf{w}\in\Sigma_{0}
, thus
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{w})>\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})+K\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2})
, therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})\geq(N-1)\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{w})>N\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})+K\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2}).

(3)

a=0,b>1
. We claim that (a) is true in this case. Consider
\mathbf{w}=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2})=(1,1)
, then it is easy to see that
\mathbf{w}\in\Sigma_{0}
, thus
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{w})>\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2})
, therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})\geq(N-1)\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})+(K-1)\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2})+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{w})>N\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})+K\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2}).

(4)

(a,b,c)=(0,1,0)
. We claim that this case can not happen. In fact

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})=-N^{2}-K^{2}+NK+1=1-(N-K)^{2}-NK\leq 1-NK<0,

by our assumption that
N\geq 2,K\geq 2
. This contradicts with the flatness assumption.

(5)

(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)
and
K>N+1
. We claim that (a) is true in this case. In fact

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})-N\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})-K\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2})=NK-N^{2}+1-K=(N-1)(K-N-1)>0.

(6)

(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)
and
K<N+1
. This case can not happen, because

\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})-N\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})-K\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{2})=(N-1)(K-N-1)<0,

this ccontradicts with the flatness assumption.

(7)

(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)
and
K=N+1\geq 4
. This case can not happen, because

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})-(N-2)\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{e}_{1})-\mathbf{p}(2\mathbf{e}_{1}+K\mathbf{e}_{2})=NK-N^{2}+1-(2K-3)=3-K<0,

this contradicts with the flatness assumption.

(8)

(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)
and
N=2,K=3
. We claim that (b) is true in this case. By a straightforward computation we see that the only representation type
\tau=(k_{1},\mathbf{v}^{(1)};\cdots;k_{r},\mathbf{v}^{(r)})
such that
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v})=\sum_{t=1}^{r}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(t)})
is

\displaystyle\tau=(2,\mathbf{e}_{1};1,\mathbf{e}_{2};1,\mathbf{e}_{2};1,\mathbf{e}_{2}),

therefore
r=4
and (b) holds.

All cases have been covered, and this finishes the proof of Theorem
B
.
∎

4.
Type A Dynkin Quivers and Finite W-Algebras

4.1.
Flatness of moment maps for affine type A Dynkin quivers with framings

Let
(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
be a framed quiver with framing vector
\mathbf{d}
(assume
\mathbf{d}\neq 0
). Following Crawley-Boevey, we define the associated unframed quiver
(Q^{\mathbf{d}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}})
as
Q^{\mathbf{d}}_{0}=Q_{0}\sqcup\{\infty\}
(union of vertices of
Q
with an extra vertex denoted by
\infty
), and arrows in
Q^{\mathbf{d}}
are those from
Q
and for each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
attach
\mathbf{d}_{i}
-copies of arrows from
\infty
to
i
, and set
\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}_{i}=\mathbf{v}_{i}
if
i\in Q_{0}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}_{\infty}=1
. From the construction we see that the group

(4.1)

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}})=\prod_{i\in Q^{\mathbf{d}}_{0}}\GL(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}_{i})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL(\mathbf{v}_{i})=:\GL(\mathbf{v})

acts on
R(Q^{\mathbf{d}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}})
. Note that the deformation space
Z
can be identified with
\prod_{\mathbf{v}_{i}\neq 0}\mathbb{C}
, the identification is by
\lambda\mapstochar\rightarrow\lambda^{\mathbf{d}}
such that
\lambda^{\mathbf{d}}_{i}=\lambda_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
and
\lambda^{\mathbf{d}}_{\infty}=-\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
. Similarly the stability space
\Theta
can be identified with
\prod_{\mathbf{v}_{i}\neq 0}\mathbb{Q}
via
\theta\mapstochar\rightarrow\theta^{\mathbf{d}}
such that
\theta^{\mathbf{d}}_{i}=\theta_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
and
\theta^{\mathbf{d}}_{\infty}=-\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\theta_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
. We define the Nakajima quiver schemes associated to
(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
as:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{Z}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d}):=\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\mathbf{d}}}_{Z}(Q^{\mathbf{d}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}),\;\mathcal{M}^{\theta}_{\lambda}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d}):=\mathcal{M}^{\theta^{\mathbf{d}}}_{\lambda^{\mathbf{d}}}(Q^{\mathbf{d}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}).

Proposition 4.2
.

If
Q
is an affine type A Dynkin quiver, let
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{d}
be dimension vector and framing vector (assume
\mathbf{d}\neq 0
), then the moment map
\mu
is flat if and only if the following condition is satisfied:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{I}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})\geq-1,

for arbitrary subset
I\subset Q_{0}
such that nodes in
I
are connected by arrows in
Q
, and
\mathbf{e}_{I}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, the flatness of
\mu
is equivalent to that for every decomposition
\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}=\mathbf{v}^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{(1)}+\cdots+\mathbf{v}^{(r)}
with
\mathbf{v}^{(1)},\cdots,\mathbf{v}^{(r)}
being roots of
Q
, the inequality
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}})\geq\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(1)})+\cdots+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(r)})
holds.

Let
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
, then it is easy to see that
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(1)})+\cdots+\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{(r)})\leq\min_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{u}_{i}
, and the equality can be achieved. On the other hand
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{d}})-\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})_{Q}
, so the flatness of moment map is equivalent to that
\forall\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
such that
0\leq\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{v}
, the inequality

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})_{Q}\geq\min_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{u}_{i}

holds. Let us discuss two situations separately: (1)
\Supp(\mathbf{v})\neq Q
; (2)
\Supp(\mathbf{v})=Q
.

In the case (1), the right hand side of (
4.4
) is zero and
Q
can be regarded as a type A quiver by removing one node with zero dimension vector. Moreover (
4.4
) holds if and only if it holds on each connected component of
\Supp(\mathbf{v})
, so let us assume that
Q^{\prime}=\Supp(\mathbf{v})
is connected. Let
I
be a subset of
Q^{\prime}_{0}
such that nodes in
I
are connected by arrows in
Q^{\prime}
, and set
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{e}_{I}
, then (
4.4
) implies that
\mathbf{e}_{I}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})\geq-1
. On the other hand if
\mathbf{e}_{I}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})\geq-1
holds for all
I\subset Q^{\prime}_{0}
such that nodes in
I
are connected by arrows in
Q^{\prime}
, then Lemma
4.3
implies that
\forall\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q^{\prime}_{0}}
such that
0\leq\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{v}
we have a decomposition
\mathbf{u}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}}
, such that
(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}},\mathbf{e}_{I_{\beta}})_{Q}\geq 0
for any pair
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
, therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})_{Q}\geq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}},\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}})_{Q}\right)\geq 0

This shows that (
4.4
) is equivalent to (
4.3
) being true for all
I\subset Q_{0}
such that
I
is a contained in a connected component of
\Supp(\mathbf{v})
. Observe that if
\mathbf{v}_{i}=0
then
\mathbf{e}_{i}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})\geq 0
, it follows that if (
4.3
) is true for all
I\subset Q_{0}
such that
I
is a contained in a connected component of
\Supp(\mathbf{v})
, then it is true for all
I
. This completes the proof in the case (1).

In the case (2), by taking
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{e}_{I}
we see that (
4.4
) implies that (
4.3
) holds for all
I
. Note that the case
I=Q_{0}
is automatically true, since we assume that
\mathbf{d}\neq 0
. Conversely, assume that (
4.3
) holds for all
I
, then let us write
\mathbf{u}=m\delta+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}}
(
\delta
is the imaginary root of
Q
) such that
I_{\alpha}\neq Q_{0}
for all
1\leq\alpha\leq n
, and
(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}},\mathbf{e}_{I_{\beta}})_{Q}\geq 0
for any pair
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
, the existence of such decomposition follows from Lemma
4.3
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})_{Q}\geq m\delta\cdot\mathbf{d}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-C_{Q}\mathbf{v})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}},\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}})_{Q}\right)\geq m=\min_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{u}_{i}.

This completes the proof in the case (2).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
Q
is an affine type A Dynkin quiver, and
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, then there exists subsets
I_{1},\cdots,I_{n}
of
Q_{0}
such that nodes in
I_{\alpha}
are connected by arrows in
Q
for all
\alpha=1,\cdots,n
, and
\mathbf{u}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}}
, and
(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}},\mathbf{e}_{I_{\beta}})_{Q}\geq 0
for any pair
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that
\Supp(\mathbf{u})
is connected, otherwise we can restrict to connected components. Let us take
I_{1}
to be the set of nodes in
\Supp(\mathbf{u})
, note that for any
J\subset I_{1}
, we have
(\mathbf{e}_{J},\mathbf{e}_{I_{1}})_{Q}\geq 0
. By induction on
\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{u}_{i}
, we have
I_{2},\cdots,I_{n}
such that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{I_{1}}=\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}}
and
(\mathbf{e}_{I_{\alpha}},\mathbf{e}_{I_{\beta}})_{Q}\geq 0
for any pair
2\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
. Now
I_{\alpha}\subset I_{1}
for all
\alpha
, thus it suffices to take
I_{1},\cdots,I_{n}
.
∎

4.4.
Quantization of quiver schemes

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
be a flat
\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
algebra such that
A:=\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}/(\hbar)
is commutative. Suppose that
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acts on
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
by automorphisms such that
\hbar
has weight
2
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra with an action on
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
, i.e. a Lie homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar})
. Suppose that
\Phi_{\hbar}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\hbar]
-linear map such that image of
\Phi_{\hbar}
is in the weight
2
subspace of
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
. We furthermore assume that
\Phi_{\hbar}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, where the Lie bracket
[-,-]_{\hbar}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
is
[a,b]_{\hbar}=\frac{1}{\hbar}[a,b]
.

Definition 4.5
.

A Lie algebra homomorphism
\Phi_{\hbar}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
as above is called a
quantum moment map
if
\forall a\in\mathfrak{g},b\in\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle[\Phi_{\hbar}(a),b]_{\hbar}=a\cdot b.

Here
a\cdot b
means the action of
a
on
b
.

Let
\chi\in(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])^{*}
and form the shift
\mathfrak{g}_{\hbar\chi}=\{a-\hbar\langle\chi,a\rangle\}\subset\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{C}\hbar
, then it is easy to see that
(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\Phi_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{\hbar\chi}))^{\mathfrak{g}}
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{\mathfrak{g}}
. More generally, let
U
be a linear space and
\varphi:U\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])^{*}
be a linear map, and form the shift
\mathfrak{g}_{U}=\{a-\varphi^{*}(a)\}\subset\mathfrak{g}\oplus U^{*}
, then
(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}[U^{*}]\Phi_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{U}))^{\mathfrak{g}}
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{\mathfrak{g}}[U^{*}]
.

Definition 4.6
.

Define the quantum Hamiltonian reductions
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\sslash_{\hbar\chi}\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{\mathfrak{g}}/(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\Phi_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{\hbar\chi}))^{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\sslash_{U}\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}^{\mathfrak{g}}[U^{*}]/(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}[U^{*}]\Phi_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{U}))^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Note that we also have classical Hamiltonian reduction
A\sslash_{U}\mathfrak{g}=A^{\mathfrak{g}}[U^{*}]/(A[U^{*}]\Phi(\mathfrak{g}_{U}))^{\mathfrak{g}}
, and it is easy to see that there is a natural algebra homomorphism
(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\sslash_{U}\mathfrak{g})/(\hbar)\rightarrow A\sslash_{U}\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is reductive, and
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
is a basis of
\ker(\varphi^{*})\subset\mathfrak{g}
such that
\{\Phi(e_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a regular sequence in
A=\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}/(\hbar)
, then there is an isomorphism

(4.6)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\sslash_{U}\mathfrak{g})/(\hbar)\cong A\sslash_{U}\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

See
[
20
, Lemma 3.3.1]
.
∎

Apply the above construction to the quiver representation of
(Q,\mathbf{v})
, and we set
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}=W_{\hbar}(T^{*}R(Q,\mathbf{v}))
(Weyl algebra of the symplectic vector space

,

4.10.
Finite W-algebras

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group,
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. Pick a nilpotent element
e\in\mathfrak{g}
and choose an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
e,f,h
. Set
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be
(e,-)
. Consider the grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{g}(i)
by the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}(h)
. It is easy to see that the skew-symmetric form
\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\chi([\xi,\eta])
is non-degenerate on
\mathfrak{g}(-1)
. Pick a Lagrangian
l\subset\mathfrak{g}(-1)
and set
\mathfrak{m}:=l\oplus\bigoplus_{i\leq-2}\mathfrak{g}(-i)
. Note that
\chi\in(\mathfrak{m}/[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}])^{*}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}
is nilpotent, it exponentiates to an algebraic subgroup
M\subset G
. The finite W-algebra
\mathcal{W}_{\hbar}
is defined as the quantum Hamiltonian reduction
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\sslash_{\chi}M
, where
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})=T(\mathfrak{g})/(xy-yx-\hbar[x,y]\>|\>x,y\in\mathfrak{g})
.

Let
P\subset G
be a parabolic subgroup and
P_{0}=[P,P]
, and let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}
. Consider the
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}^{*}][\hbar]
-algbera
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
defined as
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-finite elements in
\Gamma(G/P,D_{\hbar}(G/P_{0})^{P/P_{0}})
, where
D_{\hbar}(G/P_{0})
is the sheaf of
\hbar
-adic differential operators and the action of
P/P_{0}
on
D_{\hbar}(G/P_{0})
is induced from the right action on
G/P_{0}
, and the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
D_{\hbar}(G/P_{0})
is inherited from
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
T^{*}(G/P_{0})
by scaling cotangent fibers of weight
-2
. Note that the
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}^{*}]
-algebra structure comes from a modification of the moment map
\Phi_{\hbar}:\mathfrak{a}\subset U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}
, which is defined as
a\mapstochar\rightarrow\Phi_{\hbar}(a)-\langle\rho,a\rangle\hbar
, where
\rho
is the half sum of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
. The parabolic finite W-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{P}_{\hbar,\mathfrak{a}}
is defined as the quantum Hamiltonian reduction
\mathcal{A}_{\hbar}\sslash_{\chi}M
.

We focus on the type A case. Explicitly,
G=\SL_{N}
, and fix
r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}=N
, then
r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}
defines a parabolic subgroup
P\subset\SL_{N}
as the stabilizer of a partial flag
\mathcal{F}=(0\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{N})
with
\dim F_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}r_{i}
. Also pick
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}_{\geq 0}
with

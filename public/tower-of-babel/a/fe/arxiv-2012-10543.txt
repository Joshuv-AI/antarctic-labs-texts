Scattering from infinity of the Maxwell Klein Gordon Equations in Lorenz Gauge
Lili He

Abstract
. We prove global existence backwards from the scattering data posed at infinity for the Maxwell Klein Gordon equations in Lorenz gauge satisfying the weak null condition. The asymptotics of the solutions to the Maxwell Klein Gordon equations in Lorenz gauge were shown to be wave like at null infinity and homogeneous towards timelike infinity in [ 3 ] and expressed in terms of radiation fields, and thus our scattering data will be given in the form of radiation fields in the backward problem. We give a refinement of the asymptotics results in [ 3 ] , and then making use of this refinement, we find a global solution which attains the prescribed scattering data at infinity. Our result corresponds to “existence of scattering states” in the scattering theory. Our work starts from the approach in [ 22 ] and is more delicate since it involves nonlinearities with fewer derivatives. The method of proof relies on a suitable construction of the approximate solution from the scattering data, a weighted conformal Morawetz energy estimate and a spacetime version of Hardy inequality.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the backward problem for the Maxwell Klein Gordon (MKG) equations on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
. The MKG equations describe the interaction between an electromagnetic field represented by a two form
F_{\alpha\beta}
and a complex-valued scalar field
\phi
, and read

(1.1)

\begin{split}D^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\phi&=0\\
\partial^{\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}&=J_{\alpha}\end{split}

where
D_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}+iA_{\alpha}
is the covariant derivative corresponding to a real-valued gauge potential
A_{\alpha}
which represents the electromagnetic field
F_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha}
. To complete the coupling of
F
and
\phi
, the current is defined as
J_{\alpha}=\Im(\phi\overline{D_{\alpha}\phi})
where
\Im(z)
denotes the imaginary part of
z
, and satisfies the conservation law
\partial^{\alpha}J_{\alpha}=0
.
Here we write
\partial_{\alpha}=\partial/\partial x^{\alpha}
,
x^{0}=t
,
\partial_{0}=\partial_{t}
and use the Einstein summation convention with Greek indices
\mu,\nu,\dots
summed from
0
to
3
, and lower case Roman indices
j,k,\dots
summed from
1
to
3
. Indices are raised and lowered using the Minkowski metric
m=\text{diag }(-1,1,1,1)
.

The charge of these equations is given by

(1.2)

\mathbf{q}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!J_{0}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\Im(\phi\overline{D_{0}\phi})\,dx.

It is easy to verify that the charge is conserved.

We can rewrite the equations (
1.1
) in the unknowns
\phi
and
A_{\alpha}
as

(1.3)

\begin{split}\Box\phi&=-2iA^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi+A^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}\phi-i\lambda\phi\\
\Box A_{\alpha}&=-J_{\alpha}+\partial_{\alpha}\lambda\end{split}

where
\Box=\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}=-\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta
and
\lambda=\partial^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}
.
The system of equations (
1.3
) does not uniquely determine
\phi
and
A
. In fact, MKG system is invariant under the gauge transformation

\phi\to\tilde{\phi}=e^{i\psi}\phi,\quad A_{\alpha}\to\tilde{A}_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}\psi

for any real-valued function
\psi
. In other words, if
(\phi,A_{\alpha})
satisfies (
1.3
), then so does
(\tilde{\phi},\tilde{A}_{\alpha})
. Thus, we have gauge freedom, that is, we may stipulate a condition that the gauge potential should satisfy. In this work we fix the gauge by imposing the Lorenz gauge condition

(1.4)

\lambda=\partial^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}=0.

Now we define the reduced MKG equations to be

(1.5)

\begin{split}\Box\phi&=-2iA^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi+A^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}\phi\\
\Box A_{\alpha}&=-J_{\alpha}.\end{split}

Then we note that if the Lorenz gauge condition (
1.4
) holds, the MKG equations (
1.3
) become the reduced MKG equations (
1.5
) which is a system of nonlinear wave equations. A key observation is that if
(\phi,A)
solves (
1.5
), then
\lambda
satisfies a wave equation

(1.6)

\Box\lambda=|\phi|^{2}\lambda.

In view of (
1.6
), we see that if the solution to (
1.5
) satisfies the Lorenz gauge condition (
1.4
) at
t=0
, then (
1.4
) holds for all times.

For the study of global existence for MKG equations, first the pioneering works
[
8
,
9
]
of Eardly-Moncrief established the global existence result for the Yang-Mills-Higgs equations with sufficiently smooth initial data. Later the result was refined by Klainerman-Machedon in
[
14
]
for the MKG equations with finite energy.

In this paper, we focus on the scattering theory for MKG equations in Lorenz gauge (
1.5
). In scattering theory for (
1.5
), once we formulate a suitable notion of scattering data, we shall ask the following questions

(i)

Existence of scattering states
: for every scattering data, does there exist a global solution to (
1.5
) which has the given scattering data?

(ii)

Uniqueness of scattering states
: must two global solutions corresponding to the same scattering data be the same?

(iii)

Asymptotic completeness
: do the above solutions determined by scattering data include all the global solutions to (
1.5
)?

We refer to
[
29
,
36
,
37
]
for a general introduction to scattering theory.

In
[
3
]
Candy-Kauffman-Lindblad formulated a suitable notion of scattering data in terms of radiation field and gave an affirmative answer to question (iii) for small initial data solution. They showed that (
1.5
) satisfies the weak null condition of Lindblad-Rodnianski
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
and established the detailed asymptotics of the field and the gauge potential. These asymptotics have two parts and can be expressed by the scattering data, one wave like along outgoing light cones at null infinity, and one homogeneous inside the light cone at timelike infinity, and the charge imposes an oscillating factor in the asymptotic behavior for the scalar field.

In this work, we first establish a refinement of the asymptotics obtained in
[
3
]
. Then making use of the asymptotics of the new form, we answer question (i) in the affirmative for small scattering data. One inspiration for our work is the work of Lindblad-Schlue
[
22
]
where they studied the backward problem for models of Einstein equations by finding a solution that has the asymptotics of the form given in
[
18
]
.

1.1.
Statement of the main results

To state our main theorem, we first define some necessary notations and review the asymptotics results in
[
3
]
. We use the Cartesian coordinates
\{x^{0}=t,x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
of Minkowski space as well as the null coordinates

q=r-t,\quad p=r+t.

We define a null frame
\{L,\underline{L},e_{1},e_{2}\}
as

(1.7)

L=\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}=\partial_{t}+\omega^{i}\partial_{i}=L^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},\quad\underline{L}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}=\partial_{t}-\omega^{i}\partial_{i}=\underline{L}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},\quad e_{B}=\omega_{B}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}

where
r=|x|
,
w^{i}=x^{i}/r
and
\{e_{B}\}_{B=1,2}
is an orthonormal basis on each sphere in
\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}
for constant
t
. The null decomposition of the gauge potential
A_{\alpha}
can be expressed as follows

A_{L}=L^{\mu}A_{\mu},\quad A_{\underline{L}}=\underline{L}^{\mu}A_{\mu},\quad A_{e_{B}}=\omega_{B}^{\mu}A_{\mu}.

1.1.1.
Asymptotics Results

In
[
3
]
the asymptotic system, which was introduced by Hörmander in
[
12
]
to study finite time blow-up and later used by Lindblad in
[
17
]
to study the global solutions of nonlinear wave equations, was obtained by plugging the expansion

\phi(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{\varepsilon\Phi_{0}(q,s,\omega)}{r},\quad A_{\alpha}(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{\varepsilon\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,s,\omega)}{r}\quad\text{where}\quad s=\varepsilon\ln r,\quad\omega=x/r.

into (
1.5
) and equating the powers of order
\varepsilon^{2}/r^{2}

(1.8)

\begin{split}\partial_{s}\partial_{q}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}&=-\frac{L_{\alpha}}{2}\Im(\Phi_{0}\overline{\partial_{q}\Phi_{0}})\\
\partial_{s}\partial_{q}\Phi_{0}&=-i\mathcal{A}_{L}\partial_{q}\Phi_{0}.\end{split}

It was shown in
[
3
]
that the equations (
1.5
) satisfy the weak null condition since the corresponding asymptotic system given above has a global solution and the solution together with its derivatives grows at most exponentially in
s
.
It was then proved that in the Lorenz gauge if the initial data
(\phi,D_{t}\phi,A_{j},\partial_{t}A_{j})|_{t=0}
lies in a weighted Sobolev space, then for each
q\in\mathbb{R},\omega=x/r\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the scalar field is asymptotically

(1.9)

\phi(t,r\omega)\sim e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln(1+r)}\frac{\Phi(q,\omega)}{r}\quad\text{where}\quad|\Phi|\leq\varepsilon\langle q\rangle^{-\gamma}

for some
1/2<\gamma<1
,
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and is concentrated close to the light cone
t-r
constant. And asymptotically the null decomposition of the gauge potential is

\displaystyle A_{L}(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r},\quad A_{e_{B}}(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{e_{B}}(q,\omega)}{r},

\displaystyle A_{\underline{L}}(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{q}^{\infty}\!\mathcal{J}_{\underline{L}}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\underline{L}}(q,\omega)}{r}.

where

|\mathcal{A}_{e_{B}}|,|\mathcal{A}_{\underline{L}}|\leq\varepsilon\langle q_{+}\rangle^{-\gamma},\quad\mathcal{J}_{\underline{L}}=-2\Im\bigl(\Phi(q,\omega)\overline{\partial_{q}\Phi(q,\omega)}\bigr)

with
\langle q_{+}\rangle=(1+q_{+}^{2})^{1/2},\,q_{+}=\max\{q,0\}
.
Here we notice that the radiation field for the gauge potential does not decay in the interior of the light cone. To obtain more precise asymptotic behavior, Candy-Kauffman-Lindblad in
[
3
]
subtracted a better approximation using the formulas from
[
16
]
and thus concluded that asymptotically the gauge potential is

(1.10)

A_{\alpha}(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)}{\langle t+r\rangle}+\frac{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(q,\omega,\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle})}{\langle t+r\rangle}.

Here,
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is concentrated close to the light cones
r-t
constant, and
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is homogeneous of degree
0
with a logarithmic loss close to the light cone. More precisely, we have

(1.11)

\begin{split}|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)|&\leq\varepsilon\langle q\rangle^{-\gamma},\\
|\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(q,\omega,s)|&\leq\varepsilon^{2}\ln|s|.\end{split}

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is the radiation field for the free wave operator.
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
has two parts, the backscattering of the wave operator with the quadratic source term
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}
, and a term coming from the long range effect of the charge
\mathbf{q}
.
In particular, in the wave zone
|t-r|\ll t+r

(1.12)

A_{\alpha}(t,r\omega)\sim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)}{\langle t+r\rangle}+\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{q}^{\infty}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\chi_{ex}(q)\delta_{\alpha 0}

where
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)=L_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im\bigl(\Phi(q,\omega)\overline{\partial_{q}\Phi(q,\omega)}\bigr)
,
\chi_{ex}
is a smooth cutoff such that
\chi_{ex}(q)=1
if
q\geq 1
, and
\chi_{ex}(q)=0
if
q\leq 1/2
. We notice that the integral in the second term of the right-hand side in (
1.12
) does not decay in the interior and we cannot use the weighted conformal Morawetz energy estimate to estimate it. So we have to subtract off a leading term in the interior.

In
[
3
]
, it was also shown that at timelike infinity in the interior, say
|x|<t
, we have given
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|y|<1

(1.13)

A_{\alpha}(t,ty)\sim\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{1-\langle y,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta.

Then by re-examining the proof of asymptotics of
A_{\alpha}
(
1.13
) at timelike infinity in
[
3
]
, we refine the asymptotics for
A_{\alpha}
in the interior wave zone, see Proposition
3.2
. In fact, combining (
1.13
) and our refinement, we conclude that the asymptotics of
A_{\alpha}
take the form

(1.14)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(t,r\omega)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{r}+\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{q}^{\infty}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\chi_{ex}(q)\delta_{\alpha 0}\quad\text{at null infinity with }q\geq-1,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{\alpha}(t,r\omega)&-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\\
&\sim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{-\infty}^{q}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\quad\text{at interior null infinity }q\leq-1,\end{split}

(1.16)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(t,ty)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{1-\langle y,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\quad\text{if}\quad t\gg r.

Combining (
1.14
) and (
1.15
), we find that after subtracting a leading term in the interior,
A_{\alpha}
has asymptotics at null infinity in the sense that the radiation field and the logarithm term have decay in
q
both in the exterior and interior. Then we may express the asymptotics of
A_{\alpha}
after subtracting a leading term in the interior in a smoothed out version in the wave zone

(1.17)

\begin{split}A_{\alpha}(t,r\omega)&-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,dq\chi_{in}(q)\\
&\sim\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)}{r}-\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{-\infty}^{q}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\chi_{in}(q)\\
&\quad+\Bigl(\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{q}^{\infty}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\Bigr)\bigl(1-\chi_{in}(q)\bigr)+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\chi_{ex}(q)\delta_{\alpha 0}.\end{split}

where
\chi_{in}
is a smooth cutoff such that
\chi_{in}(q)=1
if
q\leq-1
, and
\chi_{in}(q)=0
if
q\geq-1/2
. Here the smoothed version of the asymptotics (
1.17
) at null infinity depends on the cutoff
\chi_{in}
. This is not a big deal as this ambiguity just amounts to a radiation field with sufficient decay in
q
.

1.1.2.
Asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition

Another key conclusion in
[
3
]
is that asymptotically the Lorenz gauge condition can be written as

\partial_{q}(rA_{L})\sim 0

at null infinity. More rigorously, in view of (
1.9
) and (
1.17
) we see that in the wave zone every set of asymptotics
(\phi_{asy},A_{\alpha,asy})
of the solution to the MKG equations (
1.3
) is expressed in terms of a radiation filed set
(\Phi,\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
, which is exactly the scattering data we impose in the backward problem, as follows

(1.18)

\begin{split}\phi_{asy}&=e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\Phi(q,\omega)}{r},\\
A_{\alpha,asy}&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,dq\chi_{in}(q)+\text{the right-hand side of ~\eqref{eq:unified_K}}.\end{split}

Since we discuss the asymptotic form of the Lorenz condition, we focus on the region where
r
is large and do not need to worry about the singularity of
1/r
at
r=0
. In fact, we say that a radiation filed set
(\Phi,\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
satisfies the
asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition
if modulo the terms of order
\mathcal{O}(r^{-2}\ln r)
, it holds true for the corresponding asymptotics
(\phi_{asy},A_{\alpha,asy})
that

(1.19)

\partial^{\alpha}A_{\alpha,asy}(r-q,r\omega)=0.

for each
q
and sufficient large
r
.
Thus the radiation fields cannot be specified freely and they must satisfy the
asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition
.

To capture the decay of the scattering data which is given in terms of radiation fields, we define the following norm for any radiation field
F(q,\omega)

(1.20)

\|F\|_{N,\infty,\gamma}:=\sum_{|\alpha|+k\leq N}\sup_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(\langle q\rangle\partial_{q})^{k}\partial_{\omega}^{\alpha}F(q,\omega)|\langle q\rangle^{\gamma}

for some positive integer
N
and constant
1/2<\gamma<1
.

1.1.3.
Statement of the main theorem

For the backward problem we simply give a scattering data in terms of a radiation field set
(\Phi,\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
satisfying the
asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition
and a smallness condition in the sense of norm defined in (
1.20
). Then we construct an approximate solution
(\phi_{app},A_{\alpha,app})
(see Subsubsection
1.3.3
) associated to
(\Phi,\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
such that
(\phi_{app},A_{\alpha,app})
coincides with the given asymptotics (
1.18
) in the wave zone and with
(
1.16
)
in the far interior for sufficiently large time
t
. We then show that there is an exact solution
(\phi,A_{\alpha})
to the equations (
1.3
) which is asymptotically the same as
(\phi_{app},A_{\alpha,app})
when
t\to\infty
. In our analysis, we apply the energy estimates to the remainders

u=\phi-\phi_{app},\quad v_{\alpha}=A_{\alpha}-A_{\alpha,app}.

We are ready to state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem)
.

Let
N\geq 7
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\gamma,\mu
be given such that
1/2<\gamma<1
,
\mu>0
and
\mu<\gamma-1/2
. Let
(\Phi(q,\omega),\mathcal{A}_{0}(q,\omega),\mathcal{A}_{1}(q,\omega),\mathcal{A}_{2}(q,\omega),\mathcal{A}_{3}(q,\omega))
be a radiation field set (scattering data) and define the charge to be the value

(1.21)

\mathbf{q}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\mathcal{J}_{0}(q,\omega)\,dS(\omega)\,dq=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!-\Im\bigl(\Phi(q,\omega)\overline{\partial_{q}\Phi(q,\omega)}\bigr)\,dS(\omega)\,dq.

Then there exists a small absolute constant
\varepsilon_{0}
, which depends on
N,\gamma,\mu
, such that for any radiation field set
(\Phi,\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
given above which satisfies the asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition (see the detail in Proposition
3.7
) and smallness condition

\varepsilon:=\|\Phi\|_{N,\infty,\gamma}+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}\|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\|_{N,\infty,\gamma}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

there exists a global solution
(\phi,A_{\alpha})
to the equations (
1.3
) with asymptotics (
1.18
) at null infinity and (
1.16
) at far interior timelike infinity and satisfying the Lorenz gauge condition, and the following properties of the solution hold:

(i)

The remainders satisfy the energy bounds

(1.22)

\|w^{\frac{1}{2}}Z^{I}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}+\|w^{\frac{1}{2}}Z^{I}v_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\leq C\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}\quad\text{for}\quad|I|\leq N-4

where

w(q)=\begin{cases}1+(1+|q|)^{\mu},\quad&q=r-t<0\\
1+(1+|q|)^{-\gamma+\frac{1}{2}},\quad&q=r-t\geq 0\end{cases}

(ii)

The remainders satisfy the pointwise estimates

(1.23)

|w^{\frac{1}{2}}Z^{I}u|+|w^{\frac{1}{2}}Z^{I}v_{\alpha}|\leq C\varepsilon\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}\quad\text{for}\quad|I|\leq N-6

where
Z\in\{\partial_{\mu},\,x_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-x_{\nu}\partial_{\mu},\,t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}\}
and the constant
C
depends only on
N,\gamma,\mu
.

]

[

. A simpler proof was later given by Bieri-Miao-Shahshahani in
[

]
. Recently, the decay of the solutions to (

) with large initial data was studied in
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
. Regarding the long time dynamics of solutions to the massive MKG equations, we refer the reader to
[

,

,

,

]

The scattering problem for MKG equations was recently studied by Taujanskas. He showed in
[
33
]
the existence of a bounded invertible scattering operator for the MKG equations in de Sitter spacetime by exploiting the conformal compactification method. However, this work cannot be extended to the Minkowski space as the charge will cause a long rang effect on the asymptotic behavior of the solutions.

There are some previous results on the scattering problem for some other nonlinear dispersive and hyperbolic equations. In
[
11
]
, Flato-Simon-Taflin proved the existence of the modified wave operators for Maxwell-Dirac equations in
1+3
dimensions. In
[

,

[

1.3.
Sketch of the proof

1.3.1.
Discussion of the difficulties

First we discuss the main difficulty in our analysis. In view of (
1.8
), we consider the following simplified model

(1.24)

\begin{split}\Box\psi&=0,\\
\Box\phi&=2i\psi\partial_{t}\phi,\\
\Box\varphi&=-2\Im(\phi\overline{\partial_{t}\phi}).\end{split}

which has a similar weak null structure as MKG equations in Lorenz gauge.

This model is different from that studied in
[
22
]
whose form is
\Box\psi=Q(\partial\psi,\partial\phi),\Box\phi=\tilde{Q}(\partial\phi,\partial\varphi),\Box\varphi=(\partial_{t}\psi)^{2}
, where
Q
and
\tilde{Q}
are two null forms. First, since the quadratic nonlinearities in the model considered in
[
22
]
only depend on the derivatives, they can be put in the standard energy norm
\int\!|\partial\psi|^{2}+|\partial\phi|^{2}+|\partial\varphi|^{2}
. However, our model (
1.24
) contains the dangerous terms
2i\psi\partial_{t}\phi
and
-2\Im(\phi\overline{\partial_{t}\phi})
where
\psi
and
\phi
cannot be placed in the standard energy norm due to the lack a derivative
\partial
. Second, as we observe in the forward problem, we have
|\psi|,|\phi|,|\varphi|\sim\langle t\rangle^{-1}
in the far interior region
t>2r
. Then when we consider these two models in the region
t>2r
, for our model (
1.24
), both the left-hand side and the right-hand side decay at the rate of
\langle t\rangle^{-3}
because the derivative decreases the order of decay rate. However, for the model in
[
22
]
, the left-hand side decays like
\langle t\rangle^{-3}
while the right-hand side has the decay rate
\langle t\rangle^{-4}
. Therefore, the nonlinearities in the model in
[
22
]
give us more room as far as decay in the interior.

1.3.2.
The charge
\mathbf{q}

In the forward problem, it was observed that the asymptotics of
rA_{L}
close to the light cone is
\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}
. On the other hand it can also be deduced from the asymptotics for the wave equation with a quadratic source term given by
\Im(\Phi\overline{\partial_{q}\Phi})
. Therefore we expect that there is a relation between
\mathbf{q}
and
\Im(\Phi\overline{\partial_{q}\Phi})
. In fact, this relation is given by (
1.21
) which we will prove rigorously in Proposition
3.1
.

1.3.3.
Construction of the approximate solution

First, we need to take into account the
asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition
. In fact, we derive an ODE that the radiation field set
(\Phi,\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
should satisfy

(1.25)

\partial_{q}\Bigl(L^{\alpha}(\omega)\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\chi_{in}(q)+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\chi_{ex}(q)\Bigr)=0.

See Proposition
3.7
for the derivation of this ODE.

Next, in view of (
1.18
), and (
1.16
), we construct
(\phi_{app},A_{\alpha,app})
as

(1.26)

\begin{split}\phi_{app}(t,r\omega)&=e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\Phi(q,\omega)}{r}\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr)\\
A_{\alpha,app}(t,r\omega)&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\chi_{ex}(\frac{t}{12})\chi_{in}(q)\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{2r}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{-\infty}^{q}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr)\chi_{in}(q)\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{2r}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{q}^{\infty}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\chi_{in}(q)\Bigr)\\
&\qquad+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\delta_{\alpha 0}\chi_{ex}(q)+\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)}{r}\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr).\end{split}

Here
\chi(s)=1
when
s\leq 1/2
and
\chi(s)=0
when
s\geq 3/4
, which is used to localize in the wave zone, away from the origin. Indeed this is a “good approximate solution” in the sense that module terms of order
\mathcal{O}(r^{-3}\ln r)
,
(\phi_{app},A_{\alpha,app})
solves the reduced MKG equations (
1.5
), i.e.

(1.27)

\begin{split}\Box\phi_{app}&\sim-2iA^{\alpha,app}\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}+A^{\alpha,app}A_{\alpha,app}\phi_{app}\\
\Box A_{\alpha,app}&\sim-\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}})+|\phi_{app}|^{2}A_{\alpha,app}.\end{split}

Remark 1.4
.

The equation (
1.25
) for
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
implies that
A_{L,app}=\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-2}\ln r)
, which coincides with the asymptotics of
A_{L}
in
[
3
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

Observing the first term in the construction of
A_{\alpha,app}
, we find that there is no singularity at
r=0
when the derivative falls on the integral term, however, if the derivative falls on the cutoff
\chi_{in}(q)
,
\frac{1}{r}
is generated which leads to a singularity at
r=0
. Therefore, we use the cutoff
\chi_{ex}(\frac{t}{12})
to remove the singularity at
r=0
.

1.3.4.
Reduced MKG equations

Next, we expect to find a sequence of exact solutions
(\phi_{T},A_{\alpha T})
that take the form
(\phi_{T},A_{\alpha T})=(\phi_{app}+u_{T},A_{\alpha,app}+v_{\alpha T})
to the reduced MKG equations (
1.5
). To this end we consider the following equations for
(u_{T},v_{\alpha T})
with trivial data at
t=2T

(1.28)

\begin{split}\Box u&=\widetilde{\chi}\bigl(\frac{t}{T}\bigr)\Bigl(-2iA^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi+A^{\alpha}A_{\alpha}\phi-\Box\phi_{app}\Bigr)\\
\Box v_{\alpha}&=\widetilde{\chi}\big(\frac{t}{T}\big)\Bigl(-J_{\alpha}-\Box A_{\alpha,app}\Bigr),\quad J_{\alpha}=\Im(\phi\overline{(\partial_{\alpha}+iA_{\alpha})\phi})\end{split}

where
\widetilde{\chi}
is a smooth cutoff such that
\widetilde{\chi}(s)=1
when
s\leq 1
, and
\widetilde{\chi}(s)=0
when
s\geq 2
. Clearly,
(\phi_{T},A_{\alpha T})=(\phi_{app}+u_{T},A_{\alpha,app}+v_{\alpha T})
is an exact solution to the reduced MKG equations (
1.5
) when
t\leq T
. In Section
4
,
5
, we show that (
1.28
) has a solution
(u_{T},v_{\alpha T})
for
t\leq 2T
and that the limit
(u,v_{\alpha})=\lim_{T\to\infty}(u_{T},v_{\alpha T})
exists in the energy norm used in this work. In this way we obtain a global solution
(\phi,A_{\alpha})=(\phi_{app}+u,A_{\alpha,app}+v_{\alpha})
to the reduced MKG equations (
1.5
) which is asymptotically the same as
(\phi_{app},A_{\alpha,app})
when
t\to\infty
, in the sense that the energy norm of
(u,v_{\alpha})
approaches 0 as
t\to\infty
.

Remark 1.6
.

If we solve the backward problem for the reduced MKG equations (
1.5
) directly, we face the obstacle that while
(u_{T},v_{\alpha T})
has trivial data at
t=2T
, this does not hold for
(Z^{I}u_{T},Z^{I}v_{\alpha T})
for
|I|\geq 1
. Here we introduce the cutoff
\widetilde{\chi}
to overcome this problem.

1.3.5.
Weighted conformal Morawetz energy estimate in backward direction

We establish a backward version of weighted conformal Morawetz energy estimate in Proposition
2.2

(1.29)

E^{w}[\varphi](t_{1})+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}L(r\varphi)|^{2}|w^{\prime}|\,dx\,dt\leq E^{w}[\varphi](t_{2})+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\Re{\Bigl(\frac{2}{r}\overline{K}_{0}(r\varphi)\overline{\Box\varphi}\Bigr)}w\,dx\,dt

where
\overline{K}_{0}=\bigl(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}L+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}\underline{L}\bigr)/2
,

E^{w}[\varphi](t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\Bigl(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}L(r\varphi)|^{2}+\left(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}\right)|\not{\partial}\varphi|^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}(r\varphi)|^{2}\Bigr)w\,dx

with the weight
w=w(q)
defined such that
w^{\prime}\leq 0
. This energy estimate (
1.29
) has two advantages: first since
\sum_{|I|\leq 1}\int\!|Z^{I}\varphi|w\,dx\lesssim E^{w}[\varphi](t)
(see Proposition
2.4
),
\varphi
can be put in
E^{w}[\varphi](t)
; second, due to the weight, an additional spacetime integral is generated in the left-hand side in (
1.29
), which plays a crucial role in our analysis.

The energy estimate (
1.29
) is a generalization of the classical conformal Morawetz estimate in
[
26
]
. We remark that there were other variants of the classical conformal Morawetz estimate. Lindblad-Sterbenz introduced in
[
25
]
a weighted fractional Morawetz estimate in the forward direction. In
[
22
]
, Lindblad-Schlue established the fractional Morawetz estimate in the backward direction.

1.3.6.
Estimates for the main error terms

Now we explain how we deal with the difficulty we discussed in Subsubsection
1.3.1
. Here we only display the treatment of the most delicate error terms in the energy estimate. For simplicity, we consider the equation for
v_{\alpha}
as an example and
u
can be handled in an analogous way. In view of (
1.27
) and (
1.28
), we think of the equation for
v_{\alpha}
as

(1.30)

\displaystyle\Box v_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\Im(u\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}})-\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}u})+\cdots

Then we commutate the vector fields
Z
through the equation (
1.30
) and apply (
1.29
) to the following equations

(1.31)

\Box Z^{I}v_{\alpha}=-\sum_{|J|\leq|I|}c_{J}Z^{J}\bigl(\Im(u\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}})\bigr)-\sum_{|J|\leq|I|}c_{J}Z^{J}\bigl(\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}u})\bigr)+\cdots=B+C+\cdots

where
c_{J}
are constants for all
|I|\leq N-3
. Owing to
|\partial\varphi|\lesssim\langle t-r\rangle^{-1}\sum_{|I|\leq 1}|Z^{I}\varphi|
and
|Z^{J}\phi_{app}|\lesssim\varepsilon\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\gamma}
for all
J
which is from (
1.26
)
, we see that

|B|+|C|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t-r\rangle}\sum_{|J|+|K|\leq|I|+1}|Z^{J}\phi_{app}||Z^{K}u|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle t+r\rangle\langle t-r\rangle^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{|K|\leq|I|+1}|Z^{K}u|.

First we deal with the component
(\langle t-r\rangle^{2}\underline{L})/2
in the multiplier
\overline{K}_{0}
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\Re{\Bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r}\underline{L}(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})\overline{B+C}\Bigr)}w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r}|\underline{L}(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})|\sum_{|K|\leq|I|+1}\frac{|Z^{K}u|}{\langle t+r\rangle}w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{|K|\leq|I|+1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\Bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r^{2}}|\underline{L}(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})|^{2}+|Z^{K}u|^{2}\Bigr)\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle t+r\rangle}w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\frac{\varepsilon\Bigl(E^{w}[Z^{I}v_{\alpha}](t)+E^{w}[Z^{I}u](t)\Bigr)}{\langle t\rangle}\,dt

where we use
\sum_{|I|\leq 1}\int\!|Z^{I}\varphi|w\,dx\lesssim E^{w}[\varphi](t)
(see Proposition
2.4
) in the last step.

The treatment of
(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}L)/2
component needs more delicate analysis. We rewrite the term
C
in the right-hand side of (
1.31
) as

C=-\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}Z^{I}u})+\Bigl(\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}Z^{I}u})-\sum_{|J|\leq|I|}c_{J}Z^{J}\bigl(\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}u})\bigr)\Bigr)=C_{1}+C_{2}.

Therefore, the highest order term
\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}Z^{I}u})
only occurs in
C_{1}
. For the terms
B
and
C_{2}
, we have the following estimate

(1.32)

\begin{split}|B|+|C_{2}|&\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t-r\rangle}\sum_{|K|\leq|I|,|J|+|K|\leq|I|+1}|Z^{J}\phi_{app}||Z^{K}u|\\
&\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle t+r\rangle\langle t-r\rangle^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{|K|\leq|I|}|Z^{K}u|.\end{split}

Since
Z^{J}u=\partial_{t}Z^{J}u=0
for all
J
, we are able to use a spacetime version of Hardy inequality (see Lemma
2.7
): if
\varphi=\partial_{t}\varphi=0
,

(1.33)

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!|\varphi|^{2}|w^{\prime}|\,dx\,dt\lesssim\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}L(r\varphi)|^{2}|w^{\prime}|\,dx\,dt

to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\Re{\Bigl(\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r}L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})\overline{B+C_{2}}\Bigr)}w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{r}|L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})|\sum_{|K|\leq|I|}\frac{|Z^{K}u|}{\langle t-r\rangle^{1+\gamma}}w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\sum_{|K|\leq|I|}\Bigl(\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r^{2}}|L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})|^{2}+|Z^{K}u|^{2}\Bigr)|w^{\prime}|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\varepsilon\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\sum_{|K|\leq|I|}\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r^{2}}\Bigl(|L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})|^{2}+|L(rZ^{K}u)|^{2}\Bigr)|w^{\prime}|\,dx\,dt.

where we use
|w\langle t-r\rangle^{-1-\gamma}|\lesssim|w^{\prime}|
in the second step and (
1.33
) in the last step.

It remains to consider the term
C_{1}
. We rewrite

\partial_{\alpha}Z^{I}u=L_{\alpha}(\omega)\partial_{q}Z^{I}u-\frac{\underline{L}_{\alpha}(\omega)}{2}LZ^{I}u+\omega_{\alpha}^{B}e_{B}(Z^{I}u).

We are only concerned about the principal term
\partial_{q}Z^{I}\varphi
because the other terms have good derivatives
L,e_{B}
and we have
|L\varphi|+|e_{B}(\varphi)|\lesssim\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\sum_{|I|\leq 1}|Z^{I}\varphi|
. Then we are left with estimating

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!-\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r}L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})L_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{q}Z^{I}u})\,dx\,dt.

In order to handle this, we integrate by parts with respect to the
q
direction to transfer
\partial_{q}
from
\partial_{q}Z^{I}u
to
L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})
. Since
\Box\varphi=2r^{-1}\partial_{q}L(r\varphi)+r^{-2}\Delta_{\omega}\varphi=2r^{-1}\partial_{q}L(r\varphi)+(r^{-2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\Omega_{ij}^{2}\varphi)/2
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!-\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r}L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})L_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{q}Z^{I}u})w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}\Box(Z^{I}v_{\alpha})L_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{Z^{I}u})w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}\frac{1}{4r^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\Omega_{ij}^{2}Z^{I}v_{\alpha}\Bigr)L_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{Z^{I}u})w\,dx\,dt+\cdots

For the second integral in the right-hand side above, we do integration by parts with respect to the spatial variables to move one angular rotation vector field
\Omega_{ij}
from
\Omega_{ij}^{2}Z^{I}v_{\alpha}
to the other terms and then we are able to control it by
\varepsilon\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\langle t\rangle^{-1}(E^{w}[Z^{I}v_{\alpha}](t)+E^{w}[Z^{I}u](t))\,dt
. It remains to estimate the first integral in the right-hand side above. We insert the equation for
\Box Z^{I}v_{\alpha}
into the integral and again we are only concerned about the principal term

-L_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{q}Z^{I}u})
in
\Box Z^{I}v_{\alpha}
because the other terms can be controlled by using the spacetime version of Hardy inequality (
1.33
) as before. In this way we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}L^{2}_{\alpha}(\omega)\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{q}Z^{I}u})\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{Z^{I}u})w\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{8}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}L^{2}_{\alpha}(\omega)\Bigl(\frac{\phi_{app}^{2}}{2}\partial_{q}(\overline{Z^{I}u})^{2}-|\phi_{app}|^{2}\partial_{q}|Z^{I}u|^{2}+\frac{\overline{\phi_{app}}^{2}}{2}\partial_{q}(Z^{I}u)^{2}\Bigr)w\,dx\,dt.

Integrating by parts in
q
direction again yields

(1.34)

-\frac{1}{8}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\langle t+r\rangle^{2}L^{2}_{\alpha}(\omega)\Bigl((\overline{Z^{I}u})^{2}\partial_{q}(\frac{\phi_{app}^{2}}{2})-|Z^{I}u|^{2}\partial_{q}|\phi_{app}|^{2}+(Z^{I}u)^{2}\partial_{q}(\frac{\overline{\phi_{app}}^{2}}{2})\Bigr)w\,dx\,dt+\cdots.

Then we are in the position to use the spacetime version of Hardy inequality (
1.33
) again to estimate the leading term in (
1.34
).
Our conclusion then follows by using a standard bootstrap argument.

1.3.7.
MKG equations

Finally in Section
5
we prove that the global solution to the backward problem for the reduced MKG equations (

In this section we first introduce the basic notations and identities in Subsection

2.1.
Notation and conventions

For nonnegative
A
,
B
, we write
A\lesssim B
or
A=\mathcal{O}(B)
if
A\leq CB
for some universal positive constant
C
. We write
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
. We use the notation
\lesssim_{\nu}
or
C_{\nu}
to indicate that the constant depends on a parameter
\nu
. The exact values of all constants in this paper may vary from line to line. Moreover, we use the Japanese brackets notation
\langle a\rangle=(1+a^{2})^{1/2}
and we denote by
\not{\partial}_{i}
the spatial angular components of
\partial_{i}
.

We can write the usual translation invariant frame
\{\partial_{\alpha}\}
in terms of the null frame defined in (
1.7
) as follows

(2.1)

\partial_{\alpha}=-\frac{\underline{L}_{\alpha}}{2}L-\frac{L_{\alpha}}{2}\underline{L}+\omega_{\alpha}^{B}e_{B}.

where
\omega_{\alpha}^{B}=e_{B}(x^{\alpha})=\omega^{\alpha}_{B}
, which follows from the formula

\omega_{\alpha}^{B}=\langle\partial_{\alpha},e_{B}\rangle=dx^{\alpha}(e_{B})=e_{B}(x^{\alpha}).

Also we note that

(2.2)

\omega^{i}\omega_{i}^{B}=\omega_{i}\omega^{i}_{B}=0,\quad\omega_{B}^{i}\omega_{i}^{D}=\delta_{B}^{D},\quad\omega_{i}^{B}\omega_{B}^{j}=\delta_{i}^{j}-\omega_{i}\omega^{j}.

Similarly, we can write the gauge potential
A_{\alpha}
in terms of its null decomposition

(2.3)

A_{\alpha}=-\frac{\underline{L}_{\alpha}}{2}A_{L}-\frac{L_{\alpha}}{2}A_{\underline{L}}+\omega_{\alpha}^{B}A_{e_{B}}.

We use the following vector fields in this work

(2.4)

\begin{split}\partial_{\alpha},\quad\Omega_{ij}=x_{i}\partial_{j}&-x_{j}\partial_{i},\quad\Omega_{0i}=t\partial_{i}+x_{i}\partial_{t},\quad S=t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r},\\
\overline{K}_{0}&=\frac{1+(t+r)^{2}}{2}L+\frac{1+(t-r)^{2}}{2}\underline{L}.\end{split}

\partial_{\alpha}
,
\Omega_{ij}
,
\Omega_{0i}
and
S
will be used as commutators, while
\overline{K}_{0}
will be served as our multiplier. We let
Z
be any of the commutator vector fields here and in the sequel. For any multiindex
I
with length
|I|
, let
Z^{I}
denote the product of
|I|
such commutator vector fields. Moreover, by a sum over
I=J+K
we mean a sum over all possible order preserving partitions of the ordered multiindex into two ordered multiindex
J
and
K
, that is, if
I=(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{k})
, then
J=(\iota_{i_{1}},\ldots,\iota_{i_{n}})
and
K=(\iota_{i_{n+1}},\ldots,\iota_{i_{k}})
, where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
is any reordering of the integers
1,\ldots,k
such that
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{n}
and
i_{n+1}<\ldots<i_{k}
. Then we have Leibniz’s rule

(2.5)

Z^{I}(fg)=\sum_{J+K=I}Z^{J}fZ^{K}g.

We define the tangential derivatives
\overline{\partial}
to be either the
\partial_{p}
or the angular derivatives
\not{\partial}_{i}
, and we have the following expressions for the tangential derivatives in terms of the commutator vector fields

(2.6)

\partial_{p}=\frac{1}{2}L=\frac{S+\omega^{i}\Omega_{0i}}{2(t+r)},\quad\not{\partial}_{i}=\partial_{i}-\omega_{i}\partial_{r}=\frac{\omega^{j}\Omega_{ji}}{r}=\frac{-\omega_{i}\omega^{j}\Omega_{0j}+\Omega_{0i}}{t}.

We have the following identities

(2.7)

\begin{split}\partial_{\alpha}&=-\frac{\underline{L}_{\alpha}}{2}L-\frac{L_{\alpha}}{2}\underline{L}+\omega_{\alpha}^{B}e_{B},\quad\Omega_{ij}=(x_{i}\omega_{j}^{B}-x_{j}\omega_{i}^{B})e_{B},\\
\Omega_{0i}&=\frac{\omega_{i}}{2}((t+r)L-(t-r)\underline{L})+t\omega_{i}^{B}e_{B},\quad S=\frac{1}{2}((t+r)L+(t-r)\underline{L}).\end{split}

In this paper, we repeatedly use the following commutation identities

(2.8)

\begin{split}[\Box,Z]&=0,\quad\text{if}\quad Z=\partial_{\alpha},\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{0i},\quad[\Box,S]=2\Box,\\
[\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta}]&=0,\quad[S,\partial_{\alpha}]=-\partial_{\alpha},\quad[L,\partial_{0}]=[\underline{L},\partial_{0}]=[e_{B},\partial_{0}]=0,\\
[\Omega_{0i},\partial_{\alpha}]&=-\delta_{0\alpha}\partial_{i}-\delta_{i\alpha}\partial_{0},\quad[\Omega_{ij},\partial_{\alpha}]=\delta_{j\alpha}\partial_{i}-\delta_{i\alpha}\partial_{j},\\
[L,\partial_{i}]&=\omega_{i}^{B}[L,e_{B}]=-\frac{\omega_{i}^{B}}{r}e_{B},\\
[\underline{L},\partial_{i}]&=\omega_{i}^{B}[\underline{L},e_{B}]=\frac{\omega_{i}^{B}}{r}e_{B},\\
[e_{B},\partial_{i}]&=\frac{\omega_{Bi}}{2r}(L-\underline{L})-\omega_{i}^{E}\overline{\Gamma}_{EB}^{D}e_{D},\\
[L,\Omega_{ij}]&=-[\underline{L},\Omega_{ij}]=0,\\
[e_{B},\Omega_{ij}]&=e_{B}(\Omega_{ij}^{D})e_{D}+\Omega_{ij}^{E}[e_{B},e_{E}]^{D}e_{D},\\
[L,\Omega_{0i}]&=\omega_{i}L+\frac{r-t}{r}\omega_{i}^{D}e_{D},\\
[\underline{L},\Omega_{0i}]&=-\omega_{i}\underline{L}+\frac{t+r}{r}\omega_{i}^{D}e_{D},\\
[e_{B},\Omega_{0i}]&=\frac{\omega_{i}^{B}}{2r}((t+r)L+(r-t)\underline{L})-t\omega_{i}^{E}\overline{\Gamma}_{EB}^{D}e_{D},\\
[L,S]&=L,\quad[\underline{L},S]=\underline{L},\quad[e_{A},S]=0.\end{split}

Here,
\overline{\Gamma}_{BE}^{D}
denotes the Christoffel symbol of the sphere.

Finally, we record a pointwise estimate on partial derivatives

Lemma 2.1
.

For any function
f
, we have the estimate

(2.9)

(1+t+r)|\overline{\partial}f|+(1+|t-r|)|\partial f|\leq C\sum_{|I|=1}|Z^{I}f|.

2.2.

Proposition 2.2

Proof.

Let
Q_{\alpha\beta}
be the energy momentum tensor for the linear wave equation (

We now form a momentum density associated with the vector field

w

Now, calculating the spacetime divergence of the quantity (
2.14

(2.15)

\begin{split}\partial^{\alpha}P_{\alpha}&=\bigl(\partial^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha\beta}\overline{K}_{0}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\overline{K}_{0}}\pi^{\alpha\beta}+2t\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\varphi}+2t\Re(\varphi\overline{F})\bigr)w(q)\\

Integrating both sides of (

In order to proceed, we calculate each term in (
2.16

For the the flux term
P_{0}=\bigl(Q(\overline{K}_{0},\partial_{t})+2t\Re(\varphi\overline{\partial_{t}\varphi})-|\varphi|^{2}\bigr)w(q)

Q(\overline{K}_{0},\partial_{t})=\frac{1}{4}\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|L\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2})|\not{\partial}\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\langle t-r\rangle^{2}|\underline{L}\varphi|^{2}.

We also note that

\displaystyle 2t\Re(\varphi\partial_{t}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}-\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{\partial_{t}\varphi})

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})-\frac{\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{\underline{L}\varphi})-\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{\partial_{r}\varphi}).

Integrating by parts in
r

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!-\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{\partial_{r}\varphi})w(q)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2})r\partial_{r}(\varphi\overline{\varphi})w(q)\,dS(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r^{2}}|\varphi|^{2}w(q)\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\varphi|^{2}w(q)\,dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r}|\varphi|^{2}w^{\prime}(q)\,dx.

(2.17)

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\!P_{0}\,dx&=\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|L\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2})|\not{\partial}\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\langle t-r\rangle^{2}|\underline{L}\varphi|^{2}\Bigr)w(q)\,dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\!\Bigl(\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})-\frac{\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{\underline{L}\varphi})+\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{4r^{2}}|\varphi|^{2}\Bigr)w(q)\,dx\\

&=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\!\Bigl(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}L(r\varphi)|^{2}+\left(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}\right)|\not{\partial}\varphi|^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}(r\varphi)|^{2}\Bigr)w(q)\,dx\\

It remains to calculate the last term on the right-hand side of (

Q(\overline{K}_{0},L)=\frac{1}{2}\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|L\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle t-r\rangle^{2}|\not{\partial}\varphi|^{2}.

Then we write

2t\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})=\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}-\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})=\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r}\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})-\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi}).

Integrating by parts in
p=t+r

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!-\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{2r}\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})w^{\prime}(q)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r}|\varphi|^{2}w^{\prime}(q)\,dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r}|\varphi|^{2}w^{\prime}(q)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{4}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r^{2}}|\varphi|^{2}w^{\prime}(q)\,dx\,dt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\frac{(t+r)}{r}|\varphi|^{2}w^{\prime}(q)\,dx\,dt.

(2.18)

\begin{split}&\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\!\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\!\bigl(Q(\overline{K}_{0},L)+2t\Re(\varphi\overline{L\varphi})-|\varphi|^{2}\bigr)w^{\prime}(q)\,dx\,dt\\

Proposition 3.1

We first analyze the error terms generated by the approximate solutions, namely

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert Z^{I}\Bigl(\Box\phi_{app}+ie^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial_{q}\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr),

(4.11)

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert Z^{I}\Bigl(\Box A_{\alpha,app}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{D^{2}_{|I|+1}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{D_{|I|+3}^{2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{2\gamma}}\Bigl(1+\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\Bigr)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

for any
m>0
.

Proof.

We first consider
\phi_{app}
. To this end we calculate

\displaystyle\Box\phi_{app}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}L\Bigl(e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\Phi(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)+\frac{1}{r^{3}}e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\Delta_{\omega}\Phi(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-ie^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial_{q}\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+ie^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}+\frac{i\mathbf{q}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\right)\frac{\Phi}{r^{3}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{1}{r^{3}}e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\Delta_{\omega}\Phi(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+ie^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\frac{\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r^{2}}+\frac{2(t-r)}{\langle t-r\rangle r}\Bigr)+ie^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\frac{\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{2\partial_{q}\Phi}{r}\chi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r^{2}}+e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\Phi}{r}\underline{L}\Bigl(\chi^{\prime}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r^{2}}\Bigr).

Then the first estimate (
4.10
) follows from the formula above.

Next let us turn to
A_{\alpha,app}
. We rewrite
\Box A_{\alpha,app}
as

\Box A_{\alpha,app}\equiv{I}+{II}

where

\displaystyle{I}

\displaystyle=\Box\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{n(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\chi_{ex}(\frac{t}{12})\chi_{in}(q)

\displaystyle\qquad-\Box\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{2r}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{-\infty}^{q}\!n(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr)\chi_{in}(q)+\frac{1}{2r}\ln\frac{2r}{\langle t-r\rangle}\int_{q}^{\infty}\!n(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr)\Bigl(\chi_{in}(q)-1\Bigr)

and

{II}=\Box\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\delta_{\alpha 0}\chi_{ex}(q)+\Box\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)}{r}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr).

By the calculation and estimates we did in the proof of Proposition (
3.2
) we obtain for any
m>0
that

\biggl\lvert Z^{J}\Bigl({I}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggl\rvert\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+1}^{2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{m}}+\frac{D_{|I|+3}^{2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{2\gamma}}\Bigl(1+\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\Bigr)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

So we are left with calculating
{II}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle{II}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{r}\underline{L}L\Bigl(\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\delta_{\alpha 0}\chi_{ex}(q)+\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\Delta_{\omega}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{r}\underline{L}L\Bigl(\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)+\frac{2\partial_{q}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}{r}L\Bigl(\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\Delta_{\omega}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr).

Then we find that

|Z^{J}{II}|\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}.

Putting things together yields the second estimate (
4.11
).
∎

for any

(4.13)

(4.14)

For

(4.15)

\begin{split}|Z^{I}A_{e_{B},app}|&\lesssim\frac{D^{2}_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle}\chi_{in}(q)+\frac{D_{|I|}}{\langle t+r\rangle\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr),\\
|Z^{I}A_{\underline{L},app}|&\sim|Z^{I}A_{\alpha,app}|.\end{split}

Proof.

Observing the formula (
4.1
) for
A_{\alpha,app}

with

4.12

The last two terms are controlled by
D_{|I|}\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\gamma}

(4.16)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{4\pi r}\ln\frac{t+r}{t-r}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)\,d\eta+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\sigma)-\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(\eta,\omega)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta,\end{split}

by the estimate (

Next, we turn to the estimates for the null decomposition of

\displaystyle A_{L,app}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{(1-\langle\sigma,\omega\rangle)\mathcal{J}_{0}(\eta,\sigma)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\chi_{ex}(\frac{t}{12})\chi_{in}(q)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{r-t}{r}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\!\frac{\mathcal{J}_{0}(\eta,\sigma)-\mathcal{J}_{0}(\eta,\omega)}{t-r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle}\,dS(\sigma)\,d\eta\chi_{ex}(\frac{t}{12})\chi_{in}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\chi_{in}(q)+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}\delta_{\alpha 0}\chi_{ex}(q)+\frac{L^{\alpha}(\omega)\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(q,\omega)}{r}\chi\Bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\Bigr)+\mathcal{R}

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

(4.17)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{V}_{\alpha 1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+3}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{D^{2}_{|I|+1}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{D_{|I|+3}^{2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{2\gamma}}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

(4.18)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{D^{3}_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

for any
m>0
.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha 1}
, in view of Lemma
4.2
we are left with dealing with
-\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}})+|\phi_{app}|^{2}A_{\alpha,app}
. Direct calculation implies

\displaystyle-\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}})+|\phi_{app}|^{2}A_{\alpha,app}

\displaystyle\qquad=-L_{\alpha}\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{q}\phi_{app}})+\frac{\underline{L}_{\alpha}}{2}\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{L\phi_{app}})-\omega_{\alpha}^{B}\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{e_{B}(\phi_{app})})+|\phi_{app}|^{2}A_{\alpha,app}.

Since

\displaystyle-L_{\alpha}\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{q}\phi_{app}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}}{r^{2}}\chi^{2}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)-L_{\alpha}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{8\pi}\frac{|\Phi|^{2}}{r^{3}}\chi^{2}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr),

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{L}_{\alpha}}{2}\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{L\phi_{app}})

\displaystyle=\underline{L}_{\alpha}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{8\pi}\frac{|\Phi|^{2}}{r^{3}}\chi^{2}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr),

\displaystyle-\omega_{\alpha}^{B}\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{e_{B}(\phi_{app})})

\displaystyle=-\omega_{\alpha}^{B}\frac{\Im(\Phi\overline{e_{B}(\Phi)})}{r^{2}}\chi^{2}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr),

\displaystyle|\phi_{app}|^{2}A_{\alpha,app}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Phi|^{2}}{r^{2}}\chi^{2}\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)A_{\alpha,app},

by (
3.14
) and the estimate (
4.12
) from Lemma
4.3
, we find that

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert Z^{I}\Bigl(-\Im(\phi_{app}\overline{\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}})+|\phi_{app}|^{2}A_{\alpha,app}+\frac{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{2\gamma}}\Bigl(D^{2}_{|I|+1}+\bigl(D^{4}_{|I|+2}\chi_{in}(q)+\frac{D^{4}_{|I|+1}}{\langle t-r\rangle^{2\gamma}}\bigr)\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\Bigr)\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr).

Combining the above estimate with (
4.11
) and the fact
Z^{I}\widetilde{\chi}(t/T)\sim 1
in the support of
\chi_{in}
or
\chi
, we prove (
4.17
).

Moving on, we consider
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and express

\displaystyle-2iA^{\alpha}_{app}\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}+A^{\alpha}_{app}A_{\alpha,app}\phi_{app}

\displaystyle\qquad=-2iA_{L,app}\partial_{q}\phi_{app}+iA_{\underline{L},app}L(\phi_{app})-2iA^{e_{B}}_{app}e_{B}(\phi_{app})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-A_{L,app}A_{\underline{L},app}+A^{e_{B}}_{app}A_{e_{B},app})\phi_{app}.

Then we see that

\displaystyle A_{L,app}\partial_{q}\phi_{app}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}+\mathcal{R}_{L})\partial_{q}\phi_{app}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\frac{\partial_{q}\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\mathcal{R}_{L}\frac{\partial_{q}\Phi}{r}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}(\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi r}+\mathcal{R}_{L})\biggl(-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{8\pi}\frac{\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)-\frac{\Phi}{2r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{\Phi}{r}\partial_{q}\Bigl(\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr),

\displaystyle A_{\underline{L},app}L(\phi_{app})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}A_{\underline{L},app}\biggl(-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\frac{\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)-\frac{\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{\Phi}{r}L\Bigl(\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle A^{e_{B}}_{app}e_{B}(\phi_{app})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}A_{e_{B},app}\frac{e_{B}(\Phi)}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr).

By the estimates for the null decomposition of

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert Z^{I}\Bigl(-2iA^{\alpha}_{app}\partial_{\alpha}\phi_{app}+A^{\alpha}_{app}A_{\alpha,app}\phi_{app}+ie^{-i\frac{\mathbf{q}}{4\pi}\ln r}\frac{\mathbf{q}}{2\pi}\frac{\partial_{q}\Phi}{r^{2}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\frac{D^{2}_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)+\frac{D^{3}_{|I|+2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{3}\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma}}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle q\rangle}{r}\bigr)

4.10

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

(4.19)

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\biggl\lvert\Re\bigl(\frac{1}{r}\overline{K}_{0}(rZ^{I}u)\overline{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\bigr)w\biggr\rvert\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+2}}{\langle t\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}S_{|I|}(t)^{1/2}+\frac{D_{|I|+2}}{\langle t\rangle^{\gamma-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{E}_{|I|}(t)^{1/2},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{r}\overline{K}_{0}(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})\mathcal{V}_{\alpha 1}w\biggr\rvert\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+3}}{\langle t\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}S_{|I|}(t)^{1/2}+\frac{D_{|I|+3}}{\langle t\rangle^{\gamma-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{E}_{|I|}(t)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Recall that
\overline{K}_{0}=(\langle t+r\rangle^{2}+\langle t-r\rangle^{2})/2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\biggl\lvert\Re\bigl(\frac{1}{r}\overline{K}_{0}(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})\overline{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\bigr)w\biggr\rvert\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\biggl\lvert\frac{\langle t-r\rangle^{2}}{r}\underline{L}(rZ^{I}u)\mathcal{U}_{1}w\biggr\rvert\,dx\,d\tau+\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\biggl\lvert\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}{r}L(rZ^{I}u)\mathcal{U}_{1}w\biggr\rvert\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv A+B.

By Lemma
4.4
we can bound
A
by Cauchy-Schwarz in spatial variables

A\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{|I|}(t)^{1/2}\int_{t}^{2T}\!\Bigl(\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\langle t-r\rangle^{2}|\mathcal{U}_{1}|^{2}w\,dx\Bigr)^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+3}}{\langle t\rangle^{\gamma-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{E}_{|I|}(t)^{1/2}.

For the term
B
, we apply Cauchy-Schwarz to the whole spacetime integral to obtain that

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\lesssim\biggl(\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\frac{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|\frac{1}{r}L(rZ^{I}v_{\alpha})|^{2}}{\langle q\rangle^{\gamma+1/2}}w\,dx\,d\tau\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\biggl(\int_{t}^{2T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\!\langle t-r\rangle^{\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}\langle t+r\rangle^{2}|Z^{J}\mathcal{V}_{\alpha 1}|^{2}w\,dx\,d\tau\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{D_{|I|+3}}{\langle t\rangle^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}S_{|I|}(t)^{1/2}.

Here we use
\ln x\leq 4x^{1/4}
for
x\geq 1
to deal with the logarithm terms in the above two estimates. This proves the first estimate (
4.19

4.20

∎

1.1

) if the

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Once we obtain a sequence of solutions
(\phi_{T},A_{\alpha T})
to the equations (
4.2
), we consider the term
\lambda_{T}=\partial^{\alpha}A_{\alpha T}
.
Since
\lambda_{T}
solves the equation (
1.6
)

\Box\lambda_{T}=|\phi_{T}|^{2}\lambda_{T},

we apply Proposition
2.2
with

to conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq 2}E[Z^{I}\lambda_{T}](t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|I|\leq 2}E[Z^{I}\lambda_{T}](T)+\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\int_{t}^{T}\!\int_{\Sigma_{\tau}}\bigl\lvert\frac{2}{r}\overline{K}_{0}(r\lambda_{T})\Box Z^{I}\lambda_{T}\bigr\rvert\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|I|\leq 2}E[Z^{I}\lambda_{T}](T)+C\int_{t}^{T}\!\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{1+\tau}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}E[Z^{I}\lambda_{T}](\tau)\,d\tau

from which we can infer by Grönwall that

\sum_{|I|\leq 2}E[Z^{I}\lambda_{T}](t)\leq\sum_{|I|\leq 2}CE[Z^{I}\lambda_{T}](T)\bigl(\frac{1+T}{1+t}\bigr)^{C\varepsilon^{2}}

. Therefore, it remains to determine

asymptotic Lorenz gauge condition

3.7
, we find that

\sum_{|I|\leq 3}Z^{I}\partial^{\alpha}A_{\alpha,app}=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{\langle t+r\rangle^{2}}\ln\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}\chi\bigl(\frac{\langle t-r\rangle}{r}\bigr)\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\langle t+r\rangle^{m}})

Moment map for coupled equations of Kähler forms and curvature
King Leung Lee

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce two new systems of equations in Kähler geometry: The coupled p equation and the generalized coupled cscK equation. We motivate the equations from the moment map pictures, prove the uniqueness of solutions and find out the obstructions to the solutions for the second equation. We also point out the connections between the coupled cscK equation, the coupled Kähler Yang-Mills equations and the deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills equation. Moreover, using this moment map, we can show the Mabuchi functional for the generalized coupled cscK equation, and a special case of the coupled Kähler Yang-Mills equations and the deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills equation are convex along the smooth geodesic, which is different from the one using the moment map picture from the gauge group. In our case, the geodesic is given by the natural metric on the product of smooth Kähler potential 𝒦 ( X , ω 0 ) × ⋯ × 𝒦 ( X , ω k ) \mathcal{K}(X,\omega_{0})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{K}(X,\omega_{k}) .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Over the years, many important equations in complex geometry have been given moment map interpretations. A few examples of equations with moment map interpretations are the cscK equation (
[
Don00
]
and
[
Fuj92
]
), the coupled Kähler Yang-Mills equation (
[
AGG13
]
) and the coupled constant scalar curvature (
[
DaPi19
]
). In this paper, we combine the moment maps for the latter two together with some new ideas to define a new type of canonical metrics. We begin by recalling the definition of the coupled Kähler-Yang-Mills equation.

Definition 1.1

(
[
AGG13
]
)
.

Let
P
be a principal
U(k)
bundle on a Kähler manifold
(X,\omega_{X})
,
A
be a connection on
P
, and
F_{A}
be the curvature which is an
Lie(G)
-valued 2 form. Then the coupled Kähler equation is given by

\begin{matrix}\alpha_{0}S_{g}+\alpha_{1}\bigwedge^{2}F_{A}\wedge F_{A}&=&c\\
\bigwedge F_{A}&=&z\end{matrix}

where
z\in Lie(G)
is invariant under the adjoint
U(k)
action and
c
is a constant, which depended on the topological constraint on
P
and
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},[\omega]
. Also, we need the integrability condition

F_{A}^{0,2}=0.

If
P=U(1)^{k}
, then the Lie algebra is
u(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus u(1)
, and the
F_{A}
can be represented as

F_{A}=\omega_{1}+\cdots+\omega_{k},

where
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
are
L
-valued Kähler forms on
X
, which can be realized as Kähler forms on
X
. In this special case, the moment map equation is given by

\begin{matrix}\displaystyle\alpha_{0}S_{g}+\alpha_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dfrac{\omega_{i}^{2}\wedge\omega_{0}^{n-2}}{\omega_{0}^{n}}&=&c_{0}\\
\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega_{0}}(\omega_{1})&=&c_{1}\\
\vdots&=&\vdots\\
\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega_{0}}(\omega_{k})&=&c_{k}.\end{matrix}.

In
[
HuNy16
]
, Hultgren and Witt Nyström introduced another type of canonical metrics: the coupled Kähler Einstein equation. This equation was later generalized by Datar and Pingali (
[
DaPi19
]
) to the coupled cscK equation:

Definition 1.2

(
[
DaPi19
]
)
.

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold and
\omega_{0},...,\omega_{k}
be Kähler forms on
X
, and let
\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\omega_{i}
. Then the coupled cscK equation is given by

\begin{matrix}\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n}}{\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{0})}=\cdots=\dfrac{\omega_{k}^{n}}{\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{k})}\\
S_{\omega_{0}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega_{0}}\omega+c,\end{matrix}

here
c
is the topological constant depending on the class of
\omega_{i}
and
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)
. If
c=0
, then this reduces to the coupled Kähler-Einstein equation.

Both the cKYM equation and the ccscK equation are moment map equations. For both setups, the domains are in a subspace of
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{J}\times\mathcal{A}
, which for each
(J,A)
,
A\in\Omega_{J}^{1,1}(ad(P))
(See
[
AGG13
]
,
[
DaPi19
]
). Notice that in order to get the topological constraint, the setups are in the complexifed orbit. But we can study deformation of ths solutions if we consider the bigger subspace
\mathcal{Y}
. When
P
is a principal
U(1)
bundle, the moment map for the coupled Kähler Yang-Mills equation is

\displaystyle\mu_{cKYM}(J,A)(\varphi,\xi)=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\varphi\left(c-S(J)-\alpha_{2}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-2}\wedge\omega_{A}^{2}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}+\alpha_{1}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{A}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right)\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\theta_{A}\xi\wedge\left(\alpha_{1}z-\alpha_{2}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{A}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right)\omega_{X}^{n};

and the moment map for coupled cscK equation is

\mu_{ccscK}(J,A)(H_{\xi,0},H_{\xi,A})=\int_{X}H_{\xi,0}\left(c-S(J)+\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-1}\wedge\omega_{A}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right)+\int_{X}H_{\xi,A}\left(\dfrac{\omega_{A}^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}-z\right)

We will now explain how both these moment maps can be constructed using the moment map for the cscK metrics together with a new construction involving maps between two symplectic manifolds
(X,\omega_{X})
and
(Y,\omega_{Y})
which are diffeomorphic to each others.

Definition 1.3

(Defintion
2.2
)
.

We denote the map

\mu_{p}:\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}\rightarrow Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y}))^{*}

by

(1)

\mu_{p,\omega_{X}\omega_{Y}}(f)=\dfrac{n}{n-p}\left(c_{1}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n}}{n!}-\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p+1}}{(n-p-1)!(p+1)!},c_{2}\dfrac{\omega_{Y}^{n}}{n!}-\dfrac{f_{*}\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}\right),

where
\displaystyle c_{1}=\dfrac{\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p+1}}{\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}}
,
\displaystyle c_{2}=\dfrac{\int_{Y}f_{*}\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{Y}^{p}}{\int_{Y}\omega_{Y}^{n}}
.

We also have the classical moment map: Denote
\mathcal{J}_{int}(X)
be the space of all integrable almost complex structure, and let

\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{0}):=\{J\in\mathcal{J}_{int}(X)|\omega_{0}(\bullet,\bullet)=\omega_{0}(J\bullet,J\bullet),\omega_{0}(\bullet,J\bullet)>0\}

be the space of integrable almost complex structure compactible with
\omega_{0}
. The metric
g_{J}:=\omega(\bullet,J\bullet)
induces a pairing on
T_{J}\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{0})
, and

\Omega_{\mathcal{J}}(\delta_{1}J,\delta_{2}J):=\int_{X}g_{J}(\delta_{1}J,\delta_{2}J)\dfrac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

Then the map

\mu_{\mathcal{J}}(J)=(S_{J}-\underline{S_{J}})\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n}}{n!}=\mathrm{Ric}(X,J)\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}-S\dfrac{\omega_{n}}{n!}

is a moment map corresponding to
(\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{0}),\Omega_{\mathcal{J}})
(see
[
Don97
]
,
[
Fuj92
]
), where

\underline{S_{J}}=\dfrac{1}{Vol(X,\omega^{n})}\int_{X}S_{J}\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n}}{n!}

is the average of
S_{J}
.

As
X
is diffeomorphic to
Y
, if we take
\omega_{A}=f^{*}\omega_{Y}
, then under suitable domain,

(1)

\mu_{cKYM}(J,A)=0
iff
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}(J)+c_{1}\mu_{1}^{*}(f)-c_{2}\mu_{0}^{*}(f)=0
for some suitable constant
c_{1},c_{2}
;

(2)

\mu_{ccscK}(J,A)=0
iff
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}(J)+c\mu_{0}(f)=0
for some suitable constant
c
.

Notice that with a suitable choice of domain and symplectic form, the sum of two moment maps can also be a moment map. Therefore, we unify the ccscK equations and coupled Kähler Yang Mills equation into one general moment map setup, namely, the sum of different moment maps
\mu_{p}
with the standard moment map
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}
. Moreover, using the same idea, we reconstruct the moment map for deformed Hermitian Yang Mills equation (dHYM) (see
[
CXY17
]
) and the coupled dHYM (
[
ScSt19
]
) in section 3.4.

1.2.
Construction

We will now explain how to choose the symplectic form, the domain and the range to make the sum of two moment maps a moment map in general by considering the construction for the moment map
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}+\mu_{0}
, i.e, the moment map for ccscK, as an example.

Step 1

Define

\mu_{\mathcal{J},0}:\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Map}(X,X)\rightarrow Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0}))^{*}\oplus Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{1}))^{*}

by

\mu_{\mathcal{J},0}(J,f):=(\mu_{\mathcal{J}},\mu_{0}).

We need to show that this is a moment map for the Kähler form

\Omega_{\mathcal{J},0}:=\Omega_{\mathcal{J}}+\Omega_{0}

For this moment map, the range contains more equations than we want, and the domain
J
and
f
has no relation. We will fix this issue by the following steps.

Step 2

Consider the subgroup
H\cong\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{1})
, and the embedding map

\iota:H\rightarrow\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{1})
by

\iota(\sigma,\eta)=(\sigma^{-1},\sigma,\eta)

It induces a map

\iota^{*}:Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{1}))^{*}\rightarrow Lie(H)^{*}
, and the map

\iota^{*}\circ\mu_{\mathcal{J},0}=\left(\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-1}\wedge(-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{0},J)+f^{*}\omega_{1}-c_{1}\omega_{0})}{(n-1)!},\dfrac{f_{*}\omega_{0}^{n}-c_{2}\omega_{1}^{n}}{n!}\right)

is also a moment map.

Step 3

In order to make sure the solution indeed is Kähler, we consider the subspace

\mathcal{Y}_{0}:=\{(J,f)|DfJDf^{-1}\in\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{1})\},

and we need to show that this space has the following properties:

(a)

It is closed under the action of
H
;

(b)

It is a smooth manifold. If we want the solutions to be Kähler, we need this space to be a Kähler manifold.

Then
f^{*}\omega_{1}
is
J
invariant and hence it is a Kähler form. But our theory also need the domain to be the complexified orbit space
H^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot(J_{0},f_{0})
. Notice that this space is equivalent to

\{(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})\in\mathrm{Map}(X,X)|\varphi_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}=\omega_{i}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}h_{i}\text{ for some }h_{i}\in PSH(X,\omega_{i}),i=0,1\}.

We will show that the solution of
\iota^{*}\circ\mu_{\mathcal{J},0}|_{H^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot(J_{0},f_{0})}=0
is equivalent to the solution of ccscK equation in the Kähler class
[\omega_{0}],[\omega_{1}]
.

Remark 1.4
.

(1)

we may also choose
\Omega_{\mathcal{J},0;a_{1},a_{2}}:=a_{1}\Omega_{\mathcal{J}}+a_{2}\Omega_{0}
in step 1 for some positive number
a_{1},a_{2}
to affect the constant of the outcome moment map, that is,

\iota^{*}\circ\mu_{\mathcal{J},0}=\left(\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-1}\wedge(-a_{1}\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{0},J)+a_{2}f^{*}\omega_{1}+c_{1}^{\prime}\omega_{0})}{(n-1)!},\dfrac{a_{2}f_{*}\omega_{0}^{n}-c_{2}^{\prime}\omega_{1}^{n}}{n!}\right)

but
a_{1},a_{2}
need to be positive so that
\Omega_{\mathcal{J},0}
is still a symplectic (or Kähler form if it is
J
invariant).

(2)

Notice that the embedding is not unique. For example ,it may also be
(\sigma,\sigma,\eta)
,
(\sigma,\sigma^{-1},\eta)
or
(\sigma^{-1},\sigma,\eta^{-1})
. These embedding change part of the sign of the moment map. For example, if we change the embedding to be
(\sigma,\sigma,\eta)
, then the moment map becomes

\iota^{*}\circ\mu_{\mathcal{J},0}=\left(\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-1}\wedge(-a_{1}\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{0},J)-a_{2}f^{*}\omega_{1}-c_{1}^{\prime}\omega_{0})}{(n-1)!},\dfrac{a_{2}f_{*}\omega_{0}^{n}-c_{2}^{\prime}\omega_{1}^{n}}{n!}\right).

(3)

Notice that if
(J,f)\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
, then
(J^{-1},f)=(-J,f)\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. This implies the above choices of embedding won’t affect the space
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. However, the corresponding Mabuchi functional will be the same.

In general, the main technical part for this set up is to find the correct domain space (which is
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
here). We need a space that is closed under the action and is a Kähler submanifold. In section
5
, we will discuss the difficulties of finding the suitable complex submanifold of
\mathrm{Map}(X,X)
for the moment map
\mu_{p}
.

Similarly, for coupled Kähler Yang-Mills equation, we first construct

\mu_{01}=(\mu_{\mathcal{J}}+a_{1}\mu_{0}^{*}-a_{2}\mu_{1}^{*})|_{cKYM_{01}}=\iota^{*}\circ(\mu_{\mathcal{J}},\mu_{0}^{*},\mu_{1}^{*})|_{\mathcal{YM}_{01}}

using step 1 and step 2 with a suitable embedding restricted in a suitable subspace
\mathcal{YM}_{01}^{+}
. The subspace we take in step 3 should be

\mathcal{YM}_{01}\subset\{(J,f,g)\in\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{0})\times\mathrm{Map}(X_{1},X_{0};n-2)^{+}\times\mathrm{Map}(X_{1},X_{0};n-1)^{+}|g=f^{-1},DfJDf^{-1}\in\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{1})\},

such that

\Omega_{\mathcal{J},01;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}=\alpha_{0}\Omega_{\mathcal{J}}-\alpha_{1}\Omega_{0}^{*}+\alpha_{2}\Omega_{1}^{*}>0\}.

Here
\mu^{*}
and
\Omega^{*}
are defined in Definition
2.9
, as we need

\int_{X}(\alpha_{1}\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{0}^{[n-1]}-\alpha_{2}\omega_{0}^{[n]})=0,

where
\omega^{[k]}=\dfrac{\omega^{k}}{k!}
. If we take the undual one,
\alpha_{0}\Omega_{\mathcal{J}}-\alpha_{1}\Omega_{0}+\alpha_{2}\Omega_{1}
must not be positive.

1.3.
Main result

To discuss the main result, We first define the coupled p equation.

Definition 1.5

(coupled p equation)
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X}),(Y,\omega_{Y})
be symplectic manifolds which are diffeomorphic to each other,
0\leq p\leq n-1
and let
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}
be the space of diffeomorphism such that
\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p}
is a volume form (see definition (
2.1
)). Then the couple equation p is given by

\mu_{p}=0,

where
\mu_{p}:\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}\rightarrow Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y}))^{*}
is defined by

\mu_{p}(f):=\left(c_{1}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n}}{n!}-\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p+1}}{(n-p-1)!(p+1)!},\quad c_{2}\dfrac{\omega_{Y}^{n}}{n!}-\dfrac{f_{*}\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}\right),

with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{matrix}\displaystyle\int_{X}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p+1}}{(n-p-1)!(p+1)!}&=&\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{X}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n}}{n!};\\
\displaystyle\int_{Y}\dfrac{f_{*}\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}&=&\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{Y}\dfrac{\omega_{Y}^{n}}{n!}.\end{matrix}

After that, we will study the procedure of combining the moment maps
\mu_{p}
and
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}
by
a special case which we call the generalized ccscK equation:

Definition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with Kähler forms
\omega_{0},...,\omega_{k}
. Then we define the generalised ccscK equation to be the following:

\left\{\begin{matrix}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\dfrac{\omega_{i,\varphi_{i}}^{p_{i}+1}}{(p_{i}+1)!}\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}^{n-p_{i}-1}}{(n-p_{i}-1)}\right)-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{0,\varphi_{0}},J_{0})\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}-c_{0}\dfrac{\omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}^{n}}{n!}&=&0\\
\dfrac{\omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}^{n-p_{1}}}{(n-p_{1})!}\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{1,\varphi_{1}}^{p_{1}}}{p_{1}!}-c_{1}\dfrac{\omega_{1,\varphi_{1}}^{n}}{n!}&=&0\\
\vdots\\
\dfrac{\omega_{0,\varphi_{0}}^{n-p_{k}}}{(n-p_{k})!}\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{k,\varphi_{k}}^{p_{k}}}{p_{k}!}-c_{k}\dfrac{\omega_{k,\varphi_{k}}^{n}}{n!}&=&0.\end{matrix}\right.

We will show that this system of equations has a moment map setup. Moreover, there exists a underlying space which has a Kähler structure and is compatible with the action. Hence, by considering the orbit space

\mathcal{O}_{J,\vec{f}}:=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{k}\mathrm{Ham}_{J_{i}}^{\mathbb{C}}(X_{i},\omega_{i})\right)\cdot(J,f_{1},...,f_{k}).

Then the moment map equation is given by theorem
3.11
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Consider the moment map
\mu_{\vec{p}}:\mathcal{O}_{J,\vec{f}}\rightarrow Lie(H_{0}\times...\times H_{k})^{*}
defined by theorem
3.9
restricted on
\mathcal{O}_{J,\vec{f}}
. Then
\mu_{\vec{p}}=0
has a solution iff the generalized ccscK equation has a solution
(\varphi_{0},\cdots,\varphi_{k})
.

In particular, if
X_{0}=\cdots=X_{k}
,
f_{1}=f_{2}=\cdots=f_{k}=id
,
\vec{p}=(0,...,0)
, then this is the ccscK equation with the classes fixed. Also, using similar idea, we can get an alternate setup for the coupled Kähler Yang-Mills equation (see
[
AGG13
]
) for the case
G=U(1)^{k}
.

2.
moment map for coupled equation p

In this section, we will define a class of moment map
\mu_{p}
on a open subset of
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}\subset\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)
, with a sympectic form
\Omega_{p}
on
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}
.
To do so, first, we define the domain
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}
and the symplectic form
\Omega_{p}
on
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}
:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X}),(Y,\omega_{Y})
be two compact symplectic manifolds which are diffeomorphic to each other. We define
(\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+},\Omega_{p})
to be the space

\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}:=\{f\in\mathrm{Diffeo}(X,Y)|\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p}>0\}.

that is,
\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p}
is a volume form, with the symplectic form

\displaystyle\Omega_{p}(\delta_{1}f,\delta_{2}f):=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(n-p)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{Y}(\delta_{1}f,\delta_{2}f)\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p},

where
\delta_{1}f,\delta_{2}f\in T_{f}(\mathrm{Map}(X,Y))=f^{*}(TY):=\{s_{f}:X\rightarrow TY||s_{f}|_{x}\in T_{f(x)}Y\}
.

Notice that
\Omega_{p}
is a symplectic form on
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)

defined by

Moreover, by considering the group action

which is given by

{\mu}_{\mathcal{J},\vec{p}}(J,f_{1},...,f_{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle c_{0}\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\dfrac{f_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}^{p_{i}+1}}{(p_{i}+1)!}\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-p_{i}-1}}{(n-p_{i}-1)!}\right)+\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{0},J)\wedge\dfrac{\omega_{0}^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\\

Remark 3.5
.

We can consider the action on

to be

Notice that it is not the equation we aim to obtain yet. In the next section, we will define a suitable submanifold as the domain of the moment map, and discuss how to transform this moment map equation into the moment map equation we want.

3.2.
Kähler structure on generalized ccscK

We now define the domain of the generalized ccscK
\mathcal{Y}_{\vec{p}}
, which hope to be the largest Kähler manifold which is closed in the group action, and

\mathcal{Y}_{\vec{p}}\subset\{(\omega_{0},\cdots\omega_{k})\in\Omega^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})\times\cdots\times\Omega^{2}(X_{k},\mathbb{R})|\omega_{i}\text{ is K\"{a}hler}\}.

This space is important as it is useful to study the deformation of solutions. Also, with this Kähler manifold, any complex orbit is a Kähler manifold.

Definition 3.6
.

Denote

\mathcal{Y}_{\vec{p}}\subset\mathcal{J}(X_{0},\omega_{0})\times\mathcal{Z}_{\vec{p}}

Our goal is to show that

is Kähler with respect to the symplectic form

\Omega_{\mathcal{J},p}:=\Omega_{J}+\Omega_{\vec{p}}.

As a remark, in
[
DaSm02
]

J

Notice that we have a natural almost complex structure on

On the other hand, let
(X_{i},\omega_{i})
be Kähler manifolds diffeomorphic to each other.

For fix

Lemma 4.1
.

G_{j}^{\vec{f}}
are subgroup of

\displaystyle\iota:G_{0}^{\vec{f}}\rightarrow\prod_{i=0}^{k}Aut(X_{i},L_{i})

Proof.

Let

Then

hence

For the second part, first,

\pi_{i}(\iota(\varphi)\iota(\psi)^{-1})=(f_{i}\circ\varphi\circ f_{i}^{-1})\circ(f_{i}\circ\psi\circ f_{i}^{-1})^{-1}=f_{i}\circ\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}\circ f_{i}^{-1}=\pi_{i}(\iota(\varphi\circ\psi^{-1})).

It is well known that we can identify

with

((f_{i})_{*}\varphi)\cdot f_{i}=f_{i}\circ\varphi\circ f_{i}^{-1}\circ f_{i}\circ\varphi^{-1}=f_{i}.

is unique (if exists).

5.
Kähler construction for coupled equation p

In this section, we will try to find a suitable space for the coupled equation p which is a Kähler manifold. However, the case is much more subtle then the pervious case. The problem is, unlike
\mathcal{J}\times\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}
, it is not easy to find a good complex submanifold inside
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+}
such that both
f^{*}\omega_{Y}
and
\omega_{X}
form of
X
and
f_{*}\omega_{X}
and
\omega_{Y}
are Kahler forms of
Y
. Before we go on our main disscusion, notice that if we restrict the group to be either
\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{X})
or
\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y})
we do have a good complex submanifold. For the first subgroup, the coupled moment map equation become

\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p+1}=c_{1}\omega_{X}^{n}.

The second subgroup gives

f_{*}\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{Y}^{p}=c_{2}\omega_{Y}^{n}.

We have seen this trick when we re-construct the deformed HYM.

The method we suggest is the following: we consider
F_{f}:\mathrm{Map}(X,X)\times\mathrm{Map}(Y,Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)
by

F_{f}(\sigma,\eta)=\eta\circ f\circ\sigma^{-1},

and consider the pull back image
F_{f}^{-1}(\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+})
. Notice that
F_{f}^{-1}(\Omega_{p})
is not a symplectic form as it may be degenerated. Then we can find a ”lagrest complex submanifold”
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{+}
, and the orbit space
\mathrm{Ham}^{\mathbb{C}}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}^{\mathbb{C}}(Y,\omega_{Y})
inside
F^{-1}(\mathrm{Map}(X,Y;p)^{+})
. And we will show that if
F_{f}(\mathcal{X}_{p}^{+})
and
F_{f}(\mathrm{Ham}^{\mathbb{C}}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}^{\mathbb{C}}(Y,\omega_{Y}))
are complex manifold, then these are the spaces for the moment map picture for moment map
p
.

Let
J_{X}
to be an integrable almost complex structure of
X
. Then for any diffeomorphism
g:X\rightarrow Y
, we can define an almost complex structure of
Y
by

J_{Y}:=J_{X}^{g}:=DgJ_{X}Dg^{-1}.

This is integrable as the complex local coordinate of
Y
can be defined by
X
and
g
, namely, if

\{U_{i},\varphi_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

are complex local coordinate of
X
, then
\{g(U_{i}),\psi_{i}:=\varphi_{i}\circ g^{-1}:g(U_{i})\rightarrow\Omega_{i}\}
with transition map

\psi_{j}\circ\psi_{i}^{-1}|_{\psi_{i}(g^{-1}(U_{i}\cap U_{j}))}=\varphi_{j}\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}|_{\varphi_{i}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})}

defines the complex local coordinate of
Y
.

However, let

(X,\omega_{X},J_{X}),(Y,\omega_{Y},J_{Y})
be two Kähler manifold. Notice that
J_{X}
is compatible with
\omega_{X}
doesn’t implies
J_{X}^{g}
is compatible with
\omega_{Y}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X},J_{X})
,
(Y,\omega_{Y},J_{Y})
be compact Kähler manifolds.
Define

\mathcal{J}(X,\omega_{X}):=\{J\in\mathcal{J}_{int}(X)|\omega_{X}(J\cdot,J\cdot)=\omega_{X}(\cdot,\cdot),\omega_{X}(J\cdot,\cdot)>0\}.

Define
F_{f}:\mathrm{Map}(X,X)\times\mathrm{Map}(Y,Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)
to be

F_{f}(\varphi,\psi):=\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}.

We also denote
J_{X}^{\varphi}:=D\varphi\circ J_{X}D\varphi^{-1}
for any
\varphi\in\mathrm{Map}(X,X)
(and similarly for
J_{Y}^{\psi}
.) Notice that

D\varphi^{-1}J_{X}^{\varphi}=J_{X}D\varphi^{-1}.

Then we define the following:

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X},J_{X})
,
(Y,\omega_{Y},J_{Y})
be compact Kähler manifolds. Then we define

\mathrm{KMap}_{\omega_{Y}}(X,Y;J_{X}):=\{f\in\mathrm{Diffeo}(X,Y)|J_{X}^{f}=DfJ_{X}Df^{-1}\in\mathcal{J}(Y,\omega_{Y})\}.

As a remark, we can also define

by fixing

Lemma 5.3
.

The manifold
(\mathrm{Map}(X,Y),J)
, where
J\delta f:=DfJ_{X}Df^{-1}\delta f=J_{X}^{f}
, is a complex manifold.

Proof.

It is obvious that
(\mathrm{Map}(Y,X),J_{X})
is a complex manifold as
J_{X}
is integrable. Notice that the map
inv:\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{Map}(Y,X)
defined by

inv(f)=f^{-1}

is a diffeomorphism, hence we can consider
J
on
\mathrm{KMap}(X,Y)
by

inv_{*}J_{X}=D\,inv^{-1}J_{X}D\,inv.

For any

. Therefore, for any

inv_{*}J(v)=Df(J_{X}Df^{-1}v\circ f^{-1})\circ f=DfJ_{X}Df^{-1}v.

Therfore,
(\mathrm{KMap}(X,Y),J)
is a complex manifold with integrable almost complex structure
J
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

\mathrm{KMap}(X,Y)
and
\mathrm{KMap}(X,Y;p)^{+}
is a complex submanifold.

Proof.

Consider
\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{J}(Y,\omega_{Y})
with the product complex structure
J(\sigma,A)=(J_{X}^{f}\sigma,J_{Y}A)
for all
(\sigma,A)\in T_{f,J_{Y}}\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{J}(Y,\omega_{Y})
. Then we have a subvariety

\mathcal{W}:=\{(f,J_{Y})|Df(J_{X}^{0})Df^{-1}=J_{Y}\},

here
J_{X}^{0}
is fixed. We can rewrite the relation as
f_{*}J_{Y}-J_{X}=0
as an endmorphism. By this, we can consider the map

by

F(f,J_{Y})=f_{*}J_{Y}-J_{X}.

Then
\mathcal{W}=\{F(f,J_{Y})=0\}.
When we differenate with respect to
J_{Y}
direction, say
A
, then

\delta_{A}F(f,J_{Y})=\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(Df^{-1}J_{Y}^{t}Df)=Df^{-1}ADf

which is bounded and indeed
c|A|<|\delta_{A}F(f,J_{Y})|<C|A|
for some

, and for any norm. So
\mathcal{W}
is locally a graph, which gives the smooth structure of
\mathcal{W}
.

We now show
\mathcal{W}
is a complex subvariety. Let
\dfrac{d}{dt}f_{t}=\sigma
and
\dfrac{d}{dt}J_{Y}^{t}=A
at

. Then the condition on tangent space is given by

-Df^{-1}D\sigma Df^{-1}J_{Y}Df+Df^{-1}ADf+Df^{-1}AD\sigma=0.

We now see if the vector
(J_{X}^{f}\sigma,J_{Y}A)
with

satisfies this relation. Notice that

implies

J_{X}Df^{-1}=Df^{-1}J_{Y}.

So

\displaystyle-Df^{-1}DJ_{X}^{f}\sigma Df^{-1}J_{Y}Df+Df^{-1}J_{Y}ADf+Df^{-1}J_{Y}DJ_{X}^{f}\sigma

\displaystyle=-J_{X}Df^{-1}D\sigma Df^{-1}J_{Y}Df+J_{X}Df^{-1}ADf+Df^{-1}J_{X}^{f}J_{Y}D\sigma

\displaystyle=-J_{X}Df^{-1}D\sigma Df^{-1}J_{Y}Df+J_{X}Df^{-1}ADf+J_{X}Df^{-1}J_{Y}D\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{X}(-Df^{-1}D\sigma Df^{-1}J_{Y}Df+Df^{-1}ADf+Df^{-1}AD\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Also, we need to show that the map

is injective, holomorphic and the image is

. The injectivity is obvious as if

Notice that

∎

Remark 5.5

Definition 5.6
.

Let

Proposition 5.7
.

Let

is a biholomorphism, and define an action
\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y})
on
\mathrm{Map}(X,X)\times\mathrm{Map}(Y,Y)

Then

commutes with the group action.

(2)

\mathcal{X}_{f,p}^{+}

(3)

The action
\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y})

.

(4)

Proof.

(1)

F_{f}(\sigma\circ\varphi,\eta\circ\psi)=\eta\circ\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}\sigma^{-1}=(\sigma,\eta)\cdot F_{f}(\varphi,\psi)
.

(2)

To show

is continuous, so it implies

Then

Also, let

Therefore,

gives a local coordinate, which implies

is smooth. Hence
\mathrm{KMap}(Y,Y)
is also smooth, and thus

We now show
\mathcal{X}_{f,p}^{+}
is

invariant. Again, as

is open, and
\mathrm{KMap}(X,X)
and

invariant. Suppose

. The equation we have is the following: for all

Differentiating it along

Now

\displaystyle\omega_{X}(J_{X}^{\varphi}D\sigma J_{X}v,D\varphi J_{X}w)+\omega_{X}(D\varphi J_{X}v,J_{X}^{\varphi}D\sigma J_{X}w)-\omega_{X}(J_{X}^{\varphi}D\sigma v,D\varphi w)-\omega_{X}(D\varphi v,J_{X}^{\varphi}D\sigma w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{X}(D\sigma J_{X}v,J_{X}^{\varphi}D\varphi J_{X}w)+\omega_{X}(J_{X}^{\varphi}D\varphi J_{X}v,D\sigma J_{X}w)-\omega_{X}(D\sigma v,J_{X}^{\varphi}D\varphi w)-\omega_{X}(J_{X}^{\varphi}D\varphi v,D\sigma w)

\displaystyle\omega_{X}(D\sigma J_{X}v,D\varphi J_{X}J_{X}w)+\omega_{X}(D\varphi J_{X}J_{X}v,D\sigma J_{X}w)-\omega_{X}(D\sigma v,D\varphi J_{X}w)-\omega_{X}(D\varphi J_{X}v,D\sigma w)

(3)

As

preserves the group action, we only need to show

is closed under the action. Let

As

,

Example 5.8
.

Consider
(X,\omega_{0},\omega_{1})
with

\mu_{p}:\mathrm{EMap}(X,Y;p)^{+}\rightarrow Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(X,\omega_{X})\times\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y}))^{*}
is given by

\mu_{p}(f)=\left(\dfrac{n}{n-p}\left(c_{1}\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n}}{n!}-\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p-1}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p+1}}{(n-p-1)!(p+1)!}\right),\dfrac{m}{m-p}\left(c_{2}\dfrac{\omega_{Y}^{m}}{m!}-\delta_{f(X)}\wedge\left(f_{*}(\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p})\right)\right)\right).

Proof.

The proof is basically the same as the proof of theorem
2.6
. The main difference is that
\mathrm{EMap}(X,Y)
and
\mathrm{Map}(Y,Y)
is not a one-one correspondence. However, given
v^{\prime}\in T_{f}\mathrm{EMap}(X,Y)
,
v^{\prime}|_{x}\in T_{f(x)}Y
. Hence, we can still identify it as
v\in T_{g}\mathrm{Map}(f(X),Y)
, where
g_{t}(y):=f_{t}\circ f^{-1}(y)
. After that, we extend this
g_{t}
to
\hat{g_{t}}:Y\rightarrow Y
. Then the same proof can be applied.

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\int_{f_{t}(X)}\psi(y)\dfrac{{f_{t}}_{*}(\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\omega_{Y}^{p})}{(n-p)!p!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\int_{X}\psi(f_{t}(x))\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}d\psi(v\circ f)\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}+p\int_{X}\psi(f(x))\dfrac{df^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}^{p-1}}{(n-p)!(p)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\omega_{Y}(\xi_{\psi},(v\circ f))\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}-\dfrac{p}{m}\int_{f(X)}\omega_{Y}(\xi_{\psi},(v))\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{m-p}{m}\int_{X}\omega_{Y}(\xi_{\psi},(v\circ f))\dfrac{\omega_{X}^{n-p}\wedge f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}^{p}}{(n-p)!p!}.

Notice that this is independent of the choice of extension of
g
as the term
f_{t}^{*}\iota_{v}\omega_{Y}
only depends on
v
and
f_{t}
, but not the extension
\hat{v}:=\dot{\hat{g_{t}}}|_{t=0}
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Notice that as
p
is fixed, we can take
\Omega_{X}^{\prime}:=\dfrac{n}{(n-p)}\Omega_{X}
,
\Omega_{Y}^{\prime}=\dfrac{m}{m-p}\Omega_{Y}
to remove the leading coefficient. We will denote this moment map as
\mu_{p}
from now on.

Remark 6.3
.

When
Y
is compact, given any function
\psi
,

\psi-\dfrac{1}{Vol(Y)}\int_{Y}\psi\dfrac{\omega_{Y}^{m}}{m!}\in Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y})).

However, for the case where
Y
is non compact, and
\displaystyle\int_{Y}\dfrac{\omega_{Y}^{m}}{m!}=\infty
, we cannot normalized
\psi
. So we need to assume
\displaystyle\int_{Y}\psi\omega_{Y}^{m}=0
.

Remark 6.4
.

For
p=n
, the map
\mu_{n}(f):\mathrm{EMap}(X,Y;p)^{+}\rightarrow Lie(\mathrm{Ham}(Y,\omega_{Y});n)^{*}
is a moment map. Therefore, we can still get a non-trivial moment map for
p=n
if this is an embedding.

Appendix A
Analytic compuation of convexity of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{J},p}

In this section, we will show that the Mabuchi functional
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{J},p}
is stictly convex along the smooth geodesic
(h_{0,t},\cdots,h_{k,t})
, where the geodesic equation is given by

\ddot{h}_{i,t}-|\nabla\dot{h}_{i,t}|_{\omega_{i,h_{i}}}^{2}=0

for all
0\leq i\leq k
.
As the standard Mabuchi functional
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{J}_{p}}
is well known to be convex, it suffices to consider
\mathcal{M}_{p}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{J},p}-\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{J}}
. For simplicity, we will only consider the case
k=1
. We also denote
\varphi_{i,t}=\dot{h_{i,t}}
, and

\omega^{[k]}:=\dfrac{\omega^{k}}{k!}
. As

|\nabla\varphi_{i}|_{\omega_{i}}^{2}=\dfrac{\omega_{i}(X_{\varphi_{i}},JX_{\varphi_{i}})}{\omega_{i}^{[n]}},

The geodesic equation with Lemma
2.5
implies that

|\nabla\varphi_{i}|_{\omega_{i}}^{2}\omega_{i}^{n-p}\wedge\alpha^{p}=\sqrt{-1}n\partial\varphi\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{i}\wedge\omega_{i}^{n-p-1}\wedge\alpha^{p}.

Hence

\dfrac{n-p}{n}\int_{X}|\nabla\varphi_{i}|_{\omega_{i}}^{2}\omega_{i}^{[n-p]}\wedge\alpha^{[p]}=-\int_{X}\varphi\wedge\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{i}\wedge\omega_{i}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\alpha^{[p]}.

As

d\mathcal{M}_{p}(\varphi_{0,t},\varphi_{1,t}):=\int_{X}\varphi_{0,t}(\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]}-c_{1}\omega_{0,t}^{[n]})+\int_{X}\varphi_{1,t}(\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]}-c_{2}\omega_{1,t}^{[n]}),

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{p}(h_{0,t},h_{1,t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{0,t}(\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]}-c_{1}\omega_{0,t}^{[n]})+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{1,t}(\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]}-c_{2}\omega_{1,t}^{[n]})

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\varphi_{0,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-2]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]}-c_{1}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-1]})

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\varphi_{1,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})+\int_{X}\varphi_{0,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\varphi_{1,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p-1]}-c_{2}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[n-1]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{0,t}(\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]}-c_{1}\omega_{0,t}^{[n]})+\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{1,t}(\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]}-c_{2}\omega_{1,t}^{[n]})

\displaystyle-\int_{X}|\nabla\varphi_{0,t}|_{\omega_{0,t}}^{2}(c_{1}\omega_{0,t}^{[n]}-\dfrac{n-p-1}{n}\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]})

\displaystyle-\int_{X}|\nabla\varphi_{1,t}|_{\omega_{1,t}}^{2}(\dfrac{p}{n}\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]}-c_{2}\omega_{1,t}^{[n]})

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\varphi_{1,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})+\int_{X}\varphi_{0,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{p+1}{n}\int_{X}|\nabla\varphi_{0,t}|_{\omega_{0,t}}^{2}\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]}+\dfrac{n-p}{n}\int_{X}|\nabla\varphi_{1,t}|_{\omega_{1,t}}^{2}\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\varphi_{1,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})+\int_{X}\varphi_{0,t}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(p+1)\sqrt{-1}}{n-p-1}\int_{X}\partial\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-2]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p+1]}+\dfrac{\sqrt{-1}(n-p)}{p}\int_{X}\partial\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p-1]}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{-1}(\int_{X}\partial\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]}+\int_{X}\partial\varphi_{0,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1,t}\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{[n-p-1]}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{[p]})

Using the same proof as in Lemma
2.5
, and

\sqrt{-1}\partial\varphi_{i,t}\wedge\bar{\partial}\varphi_{j,t}=d\varphi_{i,t}\wedge d^{c}\varphi_{j,t}=\omega_{i,t}(X_{\varphi_{i,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{j,t}(-JX_{\varphi_{j,t}},\bullet),

the expression becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{0,t}(X_{\varphi_{0,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}(-JX_{\varphi_{0,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-2}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p+1}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{1,t}(-JX_{\varphi_{1,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p-1}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}(-JX_{\varphi_{0,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-1}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{0,t}(X_{\varphi_{0,t}},JX_{\varphi_{0,t}})\wedge\omega_{1,t}\wedge\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-1}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},JX_{\varphi_{1,t}})\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-1}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},JX_{\varphi_{0,t}})\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}.

We claim that if
\alpha,\beta
are two forms, then

\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\alpha\wedge\beta=\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\beta=\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\beta\wedge\alpha.

With this claim, and

\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},JX_{\varphi_{0,t}})=\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{0,t}},JX_{\varphi_{1,t}}),

the expression becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{0,t}},JX_{\varphi_{0,t}})\wedge\omega_{0,t}\wedge\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-1}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},JX_{\varphi_{1,t}})\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-1}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{1,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}(-JX_{\varphi_{0,t}},\bullet)\wedge\omega_{0,t}^{n-p-1}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(n-p-1)!p!}\int_{X}\omega_{1,t}(X_{\varphi_{0,t}}-X_{\varphi_{1,t}},J(X_{\varphi_{0,t}}-X_{\varphi_{1,t}}))\omega_{0,t}^{n-p}\wedge\omega_{1,t}^{p}

We finally show the claim.
To show that, observe that

(\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta=\iota_{v}(\iota_{w}\alpha\wedge\beta)+\iota_{w}\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\beta=\iota_{v}((\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta)+\iota_{w}(\alpha\wedge(\iota_{v}\beta))-\alpha\wedge\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\beta.

Therefore,

(\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta-\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\beta=\iota_{v}((\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta)+\iota_{w}(\alpha\wedge(\iota_{v}\beta)).

Also,

(\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta-\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\beta=(\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\beta)\wedge\alpha-\beta\wedge\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\alpha=\iota_{w}((\iota_{v}\beta)\wedge\alpha)+\iota_{v}(\beta\wedge(\iota_{w}\alpha)).

as

\iota_{w}((\iota_{v}\beta)\wedge\alpha)+\iota_{v}(\beta\wedge(\iota_{w}\alpha))=-\iota_{w}(\alpha\wedge(\iota_{v}\beta))-\iota_{v}((\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta),

2((\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta-\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\beta)=((\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\alpha)\wedge\beta-\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\beta)+(\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\beta)\wedge\alpha-\beta\wedge\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\alpha=0.

Remark A.1
.

Multi-Channel Convolutional Analysis Operator Learning for Dual-Energy CT Reconstruction
Alessandro Perelli, Suxer Alfonso Garcia, Alexandre Bousse, Jean-Pierre Tasu, Nikolaos Efthimiadis, Dimitris Visvikis

Abstract
Objective. Dual-energy computed tomography has the potential to improve contrast, reduce artifacts and the ability to perform material decomposition in advanced imaging applications. The increased number or measurements results with a higher radiation dose and it is therefore essential to reduce either number of projections per energy or the source X-ray intensity, but this makes tomographic reconstruction more ill-posed. Approach. We developed the multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning method to exploit common spatial features within attenuation images at different energies and we propose an optimization method which jointly reconstructs the attenuation images at low and high energies with a mixed norm regularization on the sparse features obtained by pre-trained convolutional filters through the convolutional analysis operator learning algorithm. Main results. Extensive experiments with simulated and real computed tomography data were performed to validate the effectiveness of the proposed methods and we reported increased reconstruction accuracy compared to CAOL and iterative methods with single and joint total-variation regularization. Significance. Qualitative and quantitative results on sparse-views and low-dose dual-energy computed tomography demonstrate that the proposed multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning method outperforms both convolutional analysis operator learning applied on each energy independently and several existing state-of-the-art model-based iterative reconstruction techniques, thus paving the way for dose reduction.

Introduction

Dual-energy computed tomography (DECT) is an energy-resolved X-ray imaging technique that offers two sets of attenuation measurements utilizing two different energy spectra acquired over the same anatomical region CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) Since its invention, the technique has progressively been used for material decomposition and energy-selective imaging CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )
Applications of dual-energy computed tomography include differentiation and quantification of materials, tissue characterization, virtual monoenergetic imaging, automated bone removal in computed tomography angiography, cardiovascular imaging, multiple contrast agent imaging and mapping of effective atomic number, and radiotherapy CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

Dual-energy computed tomography acquisition techniques require two helical scans at two different tube voltages; therefore, two sets of projection data at different energy levels are collected and further reconstructed.
However, as the number of incident photons increases when irradiating with two sources the same anatomical region, the radiation dose increases proportionally CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

A reduction in radiation exposure as well as in acquisition time can be achieved by decreasing the number of projection angles and/or reducing the X-ray source dose.
However, aliasing artifacts can appear in the reconstructed images if the number of projection angles does not follow the Nyquist sampling theorem. Moreover, it is more challenging to achieve high-resolution, high-contrast image reconstruction due to the low signal-to-noise ratio CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

In the literature, most of the development on low-dose computed tomography reconstruction has focused on single image. Among the main techniques, model-based iterative reconstruction methods are the most popular. These techniques exploit models of the imaging system’s physics (forward models) along with statistical models of the measurements and noise and often simple object priors. They iteratively optimize model-based cost functions to estimate the underlying unknown image CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) Typically, such cost functions consist of a data-fidelity term, e.g., least squares or negative log-likelihood, capturing the imaging forward model and the measurement/noise statistical model and a regularizer term promoting smoothness, low-rank or sparsity CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )
Total-variation CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )as been proposed to solve incomplete projection data reconstruction problems and achieved good performance. However, total-variation assumes that the signal is piecewise constant, resulting in undesired patchy effects on the reconstructed images CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )
Recent developments and interests within spectral computed tomography refer to self-supervised methods using only noisy data CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )nd dynamic dual-energy computed tomography, which refers to randomly changing the energy threshold of the detectors to obtain the spectral information CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

Data-driven and learning-based approaches have gained much interest in recent years for biomedical image reconstruction. These methods learn representations of images and are used in combination with model-based iterative reconstruction techniques to perform complex mappings between limited or corrupted measurements and high-quality images. Among those algorithms, data-driven sparse transforms such as dictionary learning CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )se a training dataset of high-resolution and denoised images to learn features, in an unsupervised manner, that can be used to reconstruct new images. These features take the form of “atoms”, which are regrouped into dictionaries and are used to sparsely represent the image CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) Dictionary learning-based image reconstruction integrates the learned atoms with the raw scanner data within a regularized model-based iterative reconstruction context CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) Other closely related methods include sparsifying transform learning CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )nd the connection between data-adaptive models and convolutional deep learning algorithms CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )ith an increase of interest in methods that leverage both learning-based and model-based iterative reconstruction tools.

However, most dictionary learning methods are patch-based, and the learned features often contain shifted versions of the same features. The resulting learned dictionary may be redundant and therefore are memory demanding, which makes it difficult to utilize in 3-dimensional multimodal imaging.
To address these problems, convolutional dictionary learning techniques utilize shift-invariant filters, providing a convenient and memory-efficient alternative to conventional dictionary learning techniques CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) Convolutional dictionary learning approaches can be combined with model-based iterative reconstruction by providing unsupervised prior knowledge of the target image.
The convolutional dictionary learning approach can also be formulated from an analysis point of view CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )sparse convolution) and is known as convolutional analysis operator learning.

Despite the rapidly expanding research, the application of convolutional dictionary learning to multi-channel images has received little attention CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )
However, image reconstruction from dual-energy computed tomography sparse-views or low-dose requires algorithms more advanced than the standard approach where attenuation at each measured energy is reconstructed independently. Notable models in the literature designed to promote structural similarity of images are joint total variation CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) spectral patch-based penalty for the maximum-likelihood method CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) tensor-based and coupled dictionary learning CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) parallel level sets CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )nd the prior rank, intensity and sparsity model (PRISM) CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

1.1
Main Contribution

We extend the CAOL approach to multichannel settings and we develop a multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning framework that can exploit direct joint reconstruction, given the low-dose dual-energy computed tomography measurements, where all the unknown images are reconstructed simultaneously by solving one combined optimization problem. This is the first time that MCAOL is applied to DECT image reconstruction and we demonstrate its superiority respect to CAOL. Furthermore, MCAOL requires considerably less memory compared to alternative DL approaches.
The joint reconstruction approach is developed for a low-dose data acquisition protocol which consists of collecting data using a sparse angular sampling, using a different X-ray energy in consecutive steps and low X-ray photon counts.

In dual-energy computed tomography, a reasonable prior assumption is that attenuation images at different energies can be expected to be
structurally
similar in the sense that an edge (e.g., an organ boundary) that is present at one energy, is likely to be at same location and alignment with the other energies as well, even though the contrast between materials will be different at each energy.

MCAOL technique reconstructs attenuation images from the projection data combined with multi-channel filters trained on a dataset of reconstructed images.

The central idea of multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning is to learn unsupervised dual-energy computed tomography muti-channel convolutional dictionaries that can provide a joint sparse representation of the underlined images by jointly learning filters for the different energies: each atom not only carries individual information for each energy individually but also inter-energy information.

By reconstructing dual-energy computed tomography images using model-based iterative reconstruction techniques in conjunction with multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning, the multi-energy information can be optimally used by allowing the images to “talk to each other” during the reconstruction process through the learned joint dictionaries, reducing noise while preserving image resolution. In order to deal with the extreme low-dose scenario, we model the Poisson model exactly and we solve the image optimization problem by using approximated quasi-Newton method with constrained memory to achieve accurate joint reconstruction with limited computational complexity.

4.1
X-ray computed tomography Discrete Model

In this section, we describe the computed tomography discrete physical measurement process with the spectrum of the X-ray source beams composed of two different energies. We consider the case of 2-dimensional slice-by-slice imaging systems.

For image reconstruction we assume that the continuous attenuation image
\mu_{e}(\bm{r})
which denotes the linear attenuation coefficient at position
\bm{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the energy level
e=1,2
, can be represented by a linear combination of basis functions
\{b_{j}\}
associated to a discrete sampling on a
\sqrt{J}\times\sqrt{J}
Cartesian grid,

\mu_{e}(\bm{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{J}x_{e,j}b_{j}(\bm{r})\,,

(19)

where
x_{e,j}>0
for all
j=1,\dots,J
and all
e=1,2
.
The line integral becomes a summation:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{e}(\bm{\nu}_{i}(l))\,\mathrm{d}l=\sum_{j=1}^{J}x_{e,j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}b_{j}(\bm{\nu}_{i}(l))\,\mathrm{d}l=\sum_{j=1}^{J}a_{i,j}x_{e,j}

(20)

where
\bm{\nu}_{i}(l)=\bm{s}_{i}+l\vec{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a parametrisation of the
i
-th ray emitted from the source
\bm{s}_{i}
with direction
\vec{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}
,
a_{i,j}\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}}b_{j}(\bm{\nu}_{i}(l))\,\mathrm{d}l
is the contribution of the
j
-th pixel to the
i
-th ray.
The system matrix
\bm{A}
is constructed as an under-determined matrix of dimensions
I\times J
where
I=N_{\mathrm{d}}\times N_{\theta}
with
N_{\mathrm{d}}
and
N_{\theta}
being respectively the number of detectors and
N_{\theta}
and the number of angles (projections), and is defined as
[\bm{A}]_{i,j}=a_{i,j}
,
\forall\;i=1,\dots,I,\;\forall\;j=1,\dots,J
.
The spectral X-ray mathematical discrete model is based on the Beer’s law which provides the X-ray intensity after transmission. The expected number of detected photons
\bar{y}_{i,e}
is then redefined as a function of the discrete image
\bm{x}_{e}
as

\bar{y}_{i,e}(\bm{x}_{e})=S_{e}\mathrm{e}^{-[\bm{A}\bm{x}_{e}]_{i}}+\eta_{e,i}

(21)

where
\bm{x}_{e}=[x_{e,1},\ldots,x_{e,J}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{J}
is the vector of attenuation coefficients at source energy
e
,
S_{e}
is the mean photons flux at the
e
-th energy bin, as we assume a mono-energetic intensity, and
\eta_{e,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a known additive term representing the expected number of background events (primarily from
scatter). In the case of normal exposure, the number of detected photons follows a Poisson distribution, i.e.,

y_{i,e}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\bar{y}_{i,e}(\bm{x}_{e}))

(22)

and the measurements at each energy bin
e=1,2
are stored in a vector
\bm{y}_{e}=[y_{e,1},\dots,y_{e,N_{\mathrm{d}}\cdot N_{\theta}}]^{\top}
.

Although monochromatic X-ray source does not usually hold for scanners in clinical practice, a common effective strategy consists of applying a polychromatic-to-monochromatic source correction pre-processing step CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) ) and in the rest of the paper we will therefore assume that we have a monoenergetic source or that it has already been appropriately corrected.

4.2
Low-Dose CT Reconstruction

In case of low X-ray dose, since the photons counts can be very limited, the Gaussian approximation is no longer applicable as the logarithm of the data cannot be computed. We therefore chose to perform sparse view computed tomography reconstruction from the raw measurements
(\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2})
by solving the minimization problem (
\etb@resrvda
eq:MPML), with positivity constraints on
(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})
, using the Poisson negative log-likelihood functions
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
defined as

-L_{e}(\bm{x}_{e},\bm{y}_{e})=\sum_{i=1}^{I}y_{e,i}\log\bar{y}_{i,e}(\bm{x}_{e})-\bar{y}_{i,e}(\bm{x}_{e}),\quad e=1,2

(23)

and the trained regularizer

derived from the learned filters
(\bm{D}_{1}^{\star},\bm{D}_{2}^{\star})
as in (
\etb@resrvda
eq:pen_MCAOL).

\etb@resrvda

5
Validation

We validated the proposed methods on two different dual-energy computed tomography low-dose acquisition setup. In particular, we analyzed the case of sparse-view dual-energy computed tomography reconstruction with normal photon dose and the case of extreme low-photon counts with increased number of views. By approximating the dose as the product of the number of views and photon counts, the latter case represents a more challenging scenario since the overall dose considered is lower than the sparse-view case. Our implementation was based on CONVolutional Operator Learning Toolbox CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

5.1
Methods Used for Comparison

The objective of the simulations with sparse views and normal X-ray source intensity is to demonstrate that multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning achieves improved accuracy compared reconstructing each energy separately by solving (
\etb@resrvda
eq:PML) with the convolutional analysis operator learning regularizer defined in (
\etb@resrvda
eq:pen_CAOL) and with the edge-preserving total-variation regularizers, as well as simultaneously by solving (
\etb@resrvda
eq:MPML) with the joint total variation regularizer, respectfully defined as

R_{\mathrm{tv}}(\bm{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\omega_{j,k}\sqrt{(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}+\varepsilon}

(27)

and

R_{\mathrm{jtv}}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\omega_{j,k}\sqrt{(x_{1,j}-x_{1,k})^{2}+(x_{2,j}-x_{2,k})^{2}+\varepsilon}

(28)

where
\mathcal{N}_{j}
denotes the 8 nearest neighboring pixels of pixel
j
and
\omega_{j,k}
are weights (
\omega_{j,k}=1
for axial neighbors and
\omega_{j,k}=1/\sqrt{2}
for diagonal neighbors), and
\varepsilon>0
is a small real value to ensure differentiability. For each method, we used the limited-memory Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno solver to estimate
\bm{x}_{1}
and
\bm{x}_{2}
.

The experiment with extreme low-counts aims at demonstrating that considering a weighted least-squares approximation of the log-likelihood function no longer guarantees effective reconstruction results, instead the exact Poisson statistics should be accounted. This results in a degradation of the performance of convolutional analysis operator learning when optimized through the penalized weighted least-squares solver while using the quasi-Newton solver limited-memory Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno leads to improved qualitative and quantitative results.

6
Discussion

The bias-variance trade-off analysis of the estimation results over the regularization parameters confirms that multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning allows to achieve the minimum absolute bias compared to convolutional analysis operator learning and other model-based iterative reconstruction state-of-the-art methods and also reduce standard deviation.
Furthermore, multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning has the benefit of requiring less memory respect to dictionary learning methods because of the convolutional structure of the trained filters.

The multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning algorithm can be potentially extended to multi-energies
e=1,\ldots,E
since the
l_{1,0}
semi-norm in Eq. (
\etb@resrvda
eq:l_1_0) can be defined for a set of
E
-vectorised feature maps
\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{E}
. Each
\bm{z}_{e},e=1,\ldots,E
is a column vector of dimension
J\times 1
. Then the joint
l_{1,0}
semi-norm is defined as

\|(\bm{z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{E})\|_{1,0}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\bm{1}_{]0,+\infty[}\left(|z_{1,j}|+\ldots+|z_{E,j}|\right)

(31)

While the statistical noise tends to be higher in the multi-energy case, on each sub-band the contribute of the noise is reduced since the noise is split on more energy bands. Therefore, we believe that evaluating the joint norm, i.e., non-zeros elements in the feature vectors in overlapping positions for all energies at the same time, will reduce the degradation due to the increased overall noise.

The multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning framework allows to utilize any mixed norms for the jointly sparse regularization and other norms, such as the
l_{2,1}
-norm which as proposed by
?
for convolutional synthesis operator learning, may also be considered.

In our experiments we have considered the product between the X-ray source intensity and the number of projection angles as an empirical measure for the total transmitted X-ray dose. While this metric gives a good approximation of the dose, we consider the analysis of the standardized measure of radiation dose, i.e., CT dose index (CTDI), as well as the absorbed dose as a follow-up study.

We account the open problems of how to optimally select both the regularization norm and regularization parameter according to the dataset for future algorithm development.

Although this work focuses on the multi-channel imaging reconstruction problem, we believe that our proposed method can be utilized in conjunction to dual-energy computed tomography to task-oriented material decomposition problems. In particular, while an approach would be to design a material decomposition module in the image space which takes as input the multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning reconstructed images, a more compelling strategy would be designing a direct approach from sinograms to material images through multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning.

Furthermore, multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning method can be exploited for other multimodal imaging application such as PET/CT and PET/MRI. In the multimodal case, given the different intensity range on each channel, a further analysis on how to choose the negative log-likelihood weights
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
in (
\etb@resrvda
eq:MPML) should be conducted to properly balancing the information coming from the different modalities.

Finally, from a learning point of view, multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning training can be seen as a multi-channel single layer unsupervised convolutional autoencoder CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )ppendix A]Chun2019 which paves the way to extend this approach to deeper autoencoder architectures to capture more complex features such as textures.
The analysis and comparison of the proposed multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning approach with other supervised deep learning approaches is planned as a follow-up study. It is important to stress that multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning inherits a precise mathematical derivation and therefore it should not be susceptible of instabilities in the reconstruction which have been proven to occur with deep learning methods CODE(Pointer addr: 0x441a2adcdf0, metadata: DynMetadata(0x6214f1e68558) )

We consider these problems as future development of the proposed algorithm.

7
Conclusion

In this work, we have extended the convolutional analysis operator framework to multi-channel imaging and we have applied and extensively analyzed the proposed method to the dual-energy computed tomography application. The presented results show that by using the information coming from both energies and allowing the channels to “talk to each other” a more accurate solution of the reconstruction problem can be achieved together with a reduction of the noise in the estimate. The coupling between energies is encapsulated by using an
l_{1,0}
sparse mixed norm in the multi-channel convolutional analysis operator learning optimization problems both for training and reconstruction. We obtain consistently better performances across different dual-energy computed tomography acquisition scenarios from sparse-views to low-dose photon counts.

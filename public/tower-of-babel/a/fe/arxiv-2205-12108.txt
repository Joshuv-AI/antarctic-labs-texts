Variance of Squarefull Numbers in Short Intervals
Tsz Ho Chan

Abstract
In this paper, we study the variance of the number of squarefull numbers in short intervals. As a result, we are able to prove that, for any 0 < θ < 1 / 2 0<\theta<1/2 , almost all short intervals ( x , x + x 1 / 2 + θ ] (x,x+x^{1/2+\theta}] contain about ζ ⁡ ( 3 / 2 ) 2 ​ ζ ​ ( 3 ) ​ x θ \frac{\zeta(3/2)}{2\zeta(3)}x^{\theta} squarefull numbers.



1
Introduction and Main Results

A number
n
is
squarefree
if
p^{2}\nmid\;n
for every
p\mid n
, i.e. all the exponents in its prime factorization are exactly one. In contrast, a number
n
is
squarefull
if
p^{2}\mid n
for every prime
p\mid n
, i.e. all the exponents in its prime factorization are at least two. It is well-known that every squarefull number
n
can be factored uniquely as
n=a^{2}b^{3}
for some integer
a
and squarefree number
b
. Let
Q(x)
be the number of squarefull numbers which are less than or equal to
x
.
Then

Q(x)=\sum_{a^{2}b^{3}\leq x}\mu^{2}(b)

(1)

where
\mu(n)
is the Möbius function. Bateman and Grosswald
[
1
]
proved that

Q(x)=\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}x^{1/2}+\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}x^{1/3}+O\bigl(x^{1/6}e^{-C_{1}(\log x)^{4/7}(\log\log x)^{-3/7}}\bigr)

(2)

for some absolute constant
C_{1}>0
where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function. We are interested in squarefull numbers in short intervals, namely

Q(x+y)-Q(x)\;\;\text{ with }\;\;y=o(x).

Bateman and Grosswald’s result (
2
) gives

Q\bigl(x+x^{1/2+\theta}\bigr)-Q(x)\sim\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{2\zeta(3)}x^{\theta}

(3)

for
1/6=0.1666...\leq\theta<1/2
. Subsequently, exponential sum, elementary, and differencing methods were used to expand the range of validity for (
3
). The current best result was obtained by Trifonov
[
9
]
, showing that the asymptotic formula (
3
) is true for
19/154=0.1233...<\theta<1/2
(please refer to the same paper for earlier developments and relevant references). As a consequence, for
x^{\epsilon}\ll H\ll x^{1/2-\epsilon}
, we have

Q\bigl(x+2H\sqrt{x}+H^{2}\bigr)-Q(x)\ll_{\epsilon}(1_{H\leq x^{19/154}}\cdot x^{19/154}+H)H^{\epsilon}

(4)

with the indicator function
1_{H\leq T}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{ if }H\leq T,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.
. We will use this crude upper bound later.

Inspired by the recent breakthrough work of Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers
[
3
]
on variance of squarefree numbers in short intervals and arithmetic progressions, we are interested in studying the variance of squarefull numbers in short intervals:

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x})\Big|^{2}dx.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1

Given
\epsilon>0
,
X>1
and
X^{3\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/2-\epsilon}
. With
y=2\sqrt{x}H+H^{2}
,

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\ll_{\epsilon}H^{16/11+\epsilon}.

Note that
16/11=1.454545\ldots
and
\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x}=H
(which explains our choice of
y
).

Define

S:=\Bigl\{X\leq x\leq 2X:\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|>H^{8/11+\epsilon}\Bigr\}.

Then Theorem
1
implies
|S|\cdot(H^{8/11+\epsilon})^{2}\ll_{\epsilon}X\cdot H^{16/11+\epsilon}
where
|S|
stands for the measure of
S
. Hence,
|S|\ll_{\epsilon}X/H^{\epsilon}
which gives the following.

Corollary 1

Given
\epsilon>0
,
X>1
and
X^{3\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/2-\epsilon}
. With
y=2\sqrt{x}H+H^{2}
,

Q(x+y)-Q(x)=\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H+O_{\epsilon}(H^{8/11+\epsilon})

for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
.

Since
\epsilon
can be arbitrarily small, Corollary
1
immediately implies

Corollary 2

For any
0<\theta<1/2
, the asymptotic formula (
3
) is true for almost all
x\in[X,2X]
.

In both corollaries, almost all means all
x\in[X,2X]
apart from a set of measure
o(X)
. Hence, we confirm the expected count (
3
) of squarefull numbers in almost all short intervals. Based on Theorem
1
, we suspect the following.

Conjecture 1

Given
\epsilon>0
,
X>1
and
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/4-\epsilon}
. With
y=2\sqrt{x}H+H^{2}
,

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\sim\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{\sin\pi y}{\pi y}\Bigr)^{2}y^{1/3}dy\cdot H^{2/3}.

The reason for the upper exponent
1/4-\epsilon
is that the secondary main term from (
2
) gives

\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}(\sqrt[3]{x+y}-\sqrt[3]{x})\gg\frac{y}{X^{2/3}}\gg\frac{H}{X^{1/6}}\gg H^{1/3}

when
H\geq X^{1/4}
. Unfortunately, we were unable to establish the above conjecture unconditionally for any range of
H
at the moment. To do that, one needs to improve the error terms in Proposition
1
below. However, assuming the Riemann Hypothesis or the Lindelöf Hypothesis
\zeta(1/2+it)\ll_{\epsilon}t^{\epsilon}
, one has the following conditional result.

Theorem 2

Given
\epsilon>0
and
X>1
. Conjecture
1
is true for
X^{\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{3/14-\epsilon}
under the Lindelöf Hypothesis. Note that
3/14=0.2142857\ldots
.

One future direction of research is to lower the exponent
16/11
in Theorem
1
to something below
1
. To do that, one may need to include the secondary main term from (
2
) and consider

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt{x})-\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}(\sqrt[3]{x+y}-\sqrt[3]{x})\Big|^{2}dx

instead. It would also be interesting to see if one can prove the full range for Conjecture
1
under the Lindelöf Hypothesis. Another direction of inquiry is to study the variance of squarefull numbers over arithmetic progressions. However, the method of Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers
[
3
]
cannot be applied directly as they made use of Poisson summation which does not translate easily into the “non-linear” situation involving squarefull numbers. It is worth mentioning that Roditty-Gershon
[
8
]
recently studied the variance of the number of squarefull polynomials over finite fields in both short intervals and arithmetic progressions settings, and obtained interesting asymptotic results.

Recently, Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
6
]
made an important discovery on multiplicative function
f(n)
with
-1\leq f(n)\leq 1
by showing that the local average of
f
on (almost all) short interval
[x,x+h]
is close to its global average between
[X,2X]
. It would be interesting to see if similar behavior holds for multiplicative functions supported on squarefull numbers or other sparse sequences.

Our proof of Theorem
1
uses different strategies based on various sizes of
H
. For large
H
, one simply applies Bateman and Grosswald’s asymptotic formula (
2
). For small
H
, we establish the following two propositions by means of smooth weights as in
[
3
]
and some counting arguments.

Proposition 1

Given
0<\epsilon<0.1
. For
X^{3\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{1/2-\epsilon}
and
y=2\sqrt{x}H+H^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle(1+O(X^{-\epsilon/2}))2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq X^{1/4}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle+O_{\epsilon}\Bigl(H^{4/3}+\frac{H^{5/3+\epsilon}}{X^{1/16}}+\frac{H^{2+\epsilon}}{X^{1/8}}\Bigr).

Note: We keep the expected main term even though Proposition
2
below renders it much smaller than the error term.

Proposition 2

Given
0<\epsilon<0.1
. For
1\leq H\leq X^{1/2}
, we have

2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq X^{1/4}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}=H^{2/3}\cdot\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}S(y)^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}\Bigl(\frac{H^{1+\epsilon/4}}{X^{1/8}}+H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}\Bigr)

where
S(x)=\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}
. Note: This is truly an asymptotic formula when
H\leq X^{3/8-\epsilon}
.

This paper is organized as follows. First, we will give explicit construction of certain smooth weights and prove a related lemma on oscillatory integral. We also recall the Kusmin-Landau inequality on exponential sums and a strong form of the Erdős-Turán inequality on uniform distribution. Then we will prove Proposition
1
by splitting the sum into two major parts. The first part is estimated by means of gaps of fractions while the second part requires a discretization and switching of roles between two variables. This provides another way to approach the problem other than the usage of Dirichlet series and their mean-value theorems. Next, we will prove Proposition
2
. Then we will combine everything together to derive Theorem
1
. At the end, we will give a sketch of the proof of Theorem
2
by highlighting modifications of our arguments through combination with Gorodetsky, Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Rodgers’ method.

,

Construction of smooth functions and some lemmas

Let

u(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{ if }x\geq 0,\\
0,&\text{ if }x<0,\end{array}\right.\;\;\text{ and }\;\;r_{L}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{L},&\text{ if }0\leq x\leq L,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for some parameter
0<L\leq X
. Recall the definition of the convolution of two integrable real-valued functions
f
and
g
as

(f*g)(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-u)g(u)du.

We define
u_{k}(x)
recursively as follows:

u_{0}(x)=u(x),\;\;\text{ and }\;\;u_{k+1}(x)=(u_{k}*r_{L})(x)\text{ for integer }k\geq 0.

One can show that
0\leq u_{k}(x)\leq 1
on
[0,\infty)
,
u_{k}(x)=0
when
x\in(-\infty,0]
and
u_{k}(x)=1
when
x\in[kL,\infty)
. Also, one can prove inductively that
u_{k}(x)
is
k-1
times continuously differentiable for
k\geq 2
and satisfies the derivative bound
u_{k}^{(i)}(x)\ll_{k}\frac{1}{L^{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
by using
(f*g)^{\prime}=f^{\prime}*g
repeatedly. In fact, one can derive an explicit formula for
u_{k}(x)
via Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}
and its properties as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\{u_{k}(x)\}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\{u(x)\}\cdot\mathcal{L}\{r_{L}(x)\}^{k}=\frac{1}{s}\cdot\Bigl[\frac{1}{L}\cdot\frac{1-e^{-Ls}}{s}\Bigr]^{k}=\frac{1}{L^{k}}\cdot\frac{(1-e^{-Ls})^{k}}{s^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!L^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{k!e^{-iLs}}{s^{k+1}},

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\Bigl\{\frac{1}{k!L^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\frac{k!e^{-iLs}}{s^{k+1}}\Bigr\}=\frac{1}{k!L^{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}u(x-iL)(x-iL)^{k}.

For
0<L<X/(2k)
, we define the smooth weight functions

\sigma_{k,X,L}^{-}(x):=u_{k}(x-X)\cdot u_{k}(2X-x)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\sigma_{k,X,L}^{+}(x):=u_{k}(x-X+kL)\cdot u_{k}(2X+kL-x).

One can verify that
0\leq\sigma_{k,X,L}^{-}(x)\leq 1
and
0\leq\sigma_{k,X,L}^{+}(x)\leq 1
are supported on
[X,2X]
and
[X-kL,2X+kL]
respectively. Moreover,
\sigma_{k,X,L}^{-}(x)=1
for
x\in[X+kL,2X-kL]
and
\sigma_{k,X,L}^{+}(x)=1
for
x\in[X,2X]
. One also has the derivative conditions:

\frac{d^{i}\sigma_{k,X,L}^{\pm}(x)}{dx^{i}}\ll_{k}\frac{1}{L^{i}}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq k-1.

We will need the following lemma to show that oscillatory integral of certain smooth function is small.

Lemma 1

Suppose
K>1
is some large integer. Let
H\geq 1
,
W,Q,L,R>0
satisfying
QR/\sqrt{H}>1
and
RL>1
. Suppose that
w(t)
is a smooth function with support on
[\alpha,\beta]
satisfying

w^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}WL^{-j}\;\;\text{ for }j=0,1,2,...,2K-1.

Suppose
h(t)
is a smooth function on
[\alpha,\beta]
such that
|h^{\prime}(t)|\geq R
and

h^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}HQ^{-j},\;\;\text{ for }j=2,3,...,2K-1.

Then the integral

I=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}w(t)e^{ih(t)}dt

satisfies

I\ll_{K}(\beta-\alpha)W[(QR/\sqrt{H})^{-K}+(RL)^{-K}].

Proof: This is basically Lemma 8.1 in
[
2
]
but we give the proof here for completeness. Note that we do not require
w
to be infinitely differentiable or in Schwartz class. Since
w
has compact support and
h^{\prime}
is non-zero, repeated integration by parts gives

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}w(t)d\frac{e^{ih(t)}}{ih^{\prime}(t)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{w^{\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)}e^{ih(t)}dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{w^{\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)}d\frac{e^{ih(t)}}{ih^{\prime}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{ih^{\prime}(t)}\Bigl(\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)}-\frac{w^{\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)^{2}}\Bigr)e^{ih(t)}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Bigl(-\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{2}}+\frac{w^{\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{3}}\Bigr)d\frac{e^{ih(t)}}{ih^{\prime}(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{ih^{\prime}(t)}\Bigl(-\frac{w^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{2}}+\frac{2w^{\prime\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{3}}+\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{3}}+\frac{w^{\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{3}}-\frac{3w^{\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)^{2}}{h^{\prime}(t)^{4}}\Bigr)e^{ih(t)}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Bigl(-\frac{w^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)^{3}}+\frac{3w^{\prime\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)^{4}}+\frac{w^{\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)}{ih^{\prime}(t)^{4}}-\frac{3w^{\prime}(t)h^{\prime\prime}(t)^{2}}{ih^{\prime}(t)^{5}}\Bigr)d\frac{e^{ih(t)}}{ih^{\prime}(t)}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{\nu=K}^{2K-1}\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\frac{w^{(\mu)}(t)}{h^{\prime}(t)^{\nu}}\sum_{2\gamma_{2}+3\gamma_{3}+\cdots+\nu\gamma_{\nu}=\nu-\mu}c_{\nu,\mu,\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\cdots,\gamma_{\nu}}h^{(2)}(t)^{\gamma_{2}}h^{(3)}(t)^{\gamma_{3}}\cdots h^{(\nu)}(t)^{\gamma_{\nu}}e^{ih(t)}dt

for some absolute coefficients
c_{\nu,\mu,\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\cdots,\gamma_{\nu}}\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence,

|I|\ll_{K}(\beta-\alpha)W\sum_{\nu=K}^{2K-1}R^{-\nu}\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}L^{-\mu}\frac{H^{(\nu-\mu)/2}}{Q^{\nu-\mu}}

which gives the lemma.

Lemma 2

\sum_{a<n\leq b}e(f(n))\ll\frac{1}{\lambda}

. Here

.

Proof: See Theorem 2.1 in

4
]

Lemma 3
(Strong form of Erdős-Turán inequality)

Let

\hat{g}
and
\zeta(1/2+it)\ll t^{1/6}\log^{2}t
, we can push the contour integral above to the left to an integral over
\Re(s)=-2/3
. Picking out a residue at
s=-1/3
from the singularity of
\zeta(\frac{3}{2}(1+s))
, the above simplifies to

\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\hat{g}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6\pi i}\Bigr)H^{2/3}+O(H^{1/3+2\epsilon})=\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy\cdot H^{2/3}+O(H^{1/3+2\epsilon}).

This and (
34
) finish the proof of the claim.

To deal with (
31
), we introduce the function

w(y):=S(y)h\Bigl(\frac{y}{H^{\epsilon/4}}\Bigr)

where
h
is a smooth bump function such that
h(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
h(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
(for example, one can take
h(x)=\sigma_{10,2,0.1}^{+}(x+3)
). Then one can check that
w(y)
satisfies the hypothesis (
32
). For such
w
,

\displaystyle 2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq X^{1/4}}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\biggl(S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}-w\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr)\ll H^{2}\mathop{\sum_{b\leq X^{1/4},n\geq 1}}_{nH^{1-\epsilon/4}\geq b^{3/2}}\frac{1}{b^{3}}\frac{1}{(nH/b^{3/2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\sum_{b>H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}}\sum_{n\geq b^{3/2}/H^{1-\epsilon/4}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\ll_{\epsilon}H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}.

(35)

On the other hand,

\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy-\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy\ll\int_{H^{\epsilon/4}}^{\infty}y^{-5/3}dy\ll_{\epsilon}H^{-\epsilon/6}.

(36)

Combining (
33
), (
7
) and (
36
), we have

2H^{2}\sum_{b\leq X^{1/4}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big|S\Bigl(\frac{nH}{b^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=H^{2/3}\frac{4\zeta(4/3)}{3\zeta(2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|S(y)|^{2}y^{1/3}dy+O_{\epsilon}\Bigl(\frac{H^{1+\epsilon/4}}{X^{1/8}}+H^{2/3-\epsilon/6}\Bigr)

which gives Proposition
2
.

8
Proof of Theorem
1

From Propositions
1
and
2
, we have

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\ll_{\epsilon}H^{4/3}+\frac{H^{5/3+\epsilon}}{X^{1/16}}+\frac{H^{2+\epsilon}}{X^{1/8}}\ll H^{16/11+\epsilon}

for
X^{3\epsilon}\leq H\leq X^{11/48}
. For
X^{11/48}<H\leq X^{1/2-\epsilon}
, we simply apply (
2
) and get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|Q(x+y)-Q(x)-\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\zeta(3)}H\Big|^{2}dx\ll

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\Big|\frac{\zeta(2/3)}{\zeta(2)}(\sqrt[3]{x+y}-\sqrt[3]{x})+x^{1/6}\Big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{H^{2}}{X^{1/3}}+X^{1/3}\ll H^{16/11}.

Combining the above, we have Theorem
1
.

9
A sketch of proof of Theorem
2

Similar to our proof of Proposition
1
, one starts by splitting the integral (
5
) into

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}+\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{b>H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{b>H^{2/3-\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\bigg|^{2}dx.

It can be rewritten as
I_{1}+I_{2}+O(I_{1}^{1/2}I_{2}^{1/2})
where

I_{1}=\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{b\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\bigg|^{2}dx

and

I_{2}=\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{b>H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{b>H^{2/3+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\bigg|^{2}dx.

Then, one can imitate the proof of Proposition 2 in
[
3
]
by splitting
I_{2}
into dyadic ranges and trying to show that

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{x<a^{2}b^{3}\leq x+y}}_{B<b\leq 2B}\mu^{2}(b)-H\sum_{B<b\leq 2B}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{3/2}}\bigg|^{2}dx\ll_{\epsilon}H^{2/3-\epsilon/4}

(37)

for
B\in[H^{2/3+\epsilon},(2X)^{1/3}]
. Following
[
3
]
, one can use contour integral of Dirichlet series and Plancherel’s identity to bound the left hand side of (
37
) by

\ll\sqrt{X}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\min\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{H}{\sqrt{X}}\Bigr)^{2},\frac{1}{|t|^{2}}\Bigr)\,|\zeta(1/2+2it)M(3/4+3it)|^{2}dt\;\;\text{ with }\;\;M(s):=\sum_{B<b\leq 2B}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{s}}.

One can show that the integral with
|t|\geq X
contributes
O(1)
. For the remaining range of
t
, one can split the range and show that it is

\ll H\Bigl(\sup_{\sqrt{X}/H\leq T\leq X}\frac{1}{T}\int_{|t|\leq T}|\zeta(1/2+2it)M(3/4+3it)|^{2}dt\Bigr)+O(1).

Applying Lindelöf Hypothesis and standard mean-value theorem on Dirichlet polynomial, the above can be shown to be

\ll\frac{HT^{\delta}}{T}(T+B)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}\ll\frac{HT^{\delta}}{\sqrt{B}}+\frac{HT^{\delta}\sqrt{B}}{T}

for some
\delta>0
. As
\sqrt{X}/H\leq T\leq X
and
B\gg H^{2/3+\epsilon}
, the above is
O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/4})
when
H\ll X^{1/4-\epsilon}
.

It remains to deal with
I_{1}
. This can be done similarly as our estimation of
J_{1}
using smooth weight. The only difference is in some of the details in our counting argument for the non-diagonal terms. We have
B_{1},B_{2}\leq H^{2/3+\epsilon}
. Instead of (
4
), the non-diagonal contribution is

\ll\sum_{b}\sum_{n}\sum_{b_{1}^{\prime}}\sum_{b_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N_{1}N_{2}}\cdot\frac{B_{1}^{3/2}N_{2}}{n^{2}X^{1/2-\epsilon}}+\sum_{b}\sum_{n}\sum_{b_{1}^{\prime}}\sum_{b_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N_{1}N_{2}}\ll\frac{B_{1}^{5/2}B_{2}}{X^{1/2-\epsilon}}+\sum_{n}\frac{B_{1}B_{2}}{N_{1}N_{2}}

which is
O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon})
as long as
H\leq X^{3/14-5\epsilon}
and
n\leq N_{1}N_{2}/H^{2/3+3\epsilon}
. It remains to deal with
n>N_{1}N_{2}/H^{2/3+3\epsilon}
. One can show that
b\leq(B_{1}B_{2})^{1/2-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
when
H\ll X^{3/14-2\delta-5\epsilon}
. Then, instead of (
4
), the non-diagonal contribution is

\sum_{b}\sum_{n}\sum_{n_{1}^{\prime}}\sum_{n_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N_{1}N_{2}}\cdot\frac{B_{1}B_{2}^{5/2}}{b^{2}N_{2}X^{1/2-\epsilon}}+\sum_{b\leq(B_{1}B_{2})^{1/2-\delta}}\sum_{n}\sum_{n_{1}^{\prime}}\sum_{n_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{N_{1}N_{2}}\ll\frac{B_{1}B_{2}^{5/2}}{X^{1/2-\epsilon}}+(B_{1}B_{2})^{1/2-\delta}

which is
O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon})
as long as
H\ll X^{3/14-2\delta-5\epsilon}
and
\delta\geq\epsilon
. So, we have an acceptable error.

Finally, the main term of
I_{1}
can be studied in the same way as the proof of Proposition
2
with an acceptable error
O_{\epsilon}(H^{2/3-\epsilon/6})
.

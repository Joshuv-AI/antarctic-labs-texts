A multiparameter integral inequality for the dyadic maximal operator and applications to the corresponding Bellman function of three integral variables - lower bounds
Eleftherios N. Nikolidakis

Abstract
We prove a multiparameter integral inequality for the dyadic maximal operator which refines the one-parameter inequality that is given by A. Melas in [ 10 ] which in turn is applied for the evaluation of the Bellman function of two integral variables for this maximal operator. Moreover we find the exact domain of definition of the related Bellman function of three integral variables and by using the results connected with the sharpness of this new multiparameter inequality we give lower bounds of this Belman function.



1
Introduction

The dyadic maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a useful tool in analysis and is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}|\phi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u:x\in S,\ S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{is a dyadic cube}\right\},

(1.1)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the dyadic cubes are those formed by the grids
2^{-N}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for
N=0,1,2,\ldots
.

It is well known that it satisfies the following weak type (1,1) inequality

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi(x)>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\left\{\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi>\lambda\right\}}|\phi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u,

(1.2)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every
\lambda>0
,
from which it is easy to get the following
L^{p}
-inequality

\|\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi\|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|\phi\|_{p},

(1.3)

for every
p>1
, and every
\phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is easy to see that the weak type inequality (
1.2
) is the best possible. For refinements of this inequality one can consult
[
18
]
.

It has also been proved that (
1.3
) is best possible (see
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
for general martingales and
[
39
]
for dyadic ones).
An approach for studying the behaviour of this maximal operator in more depth is the introduction of the so-called Bellman functions which play the role of generalized norms of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Such functions related to the
L^{p}
-inequality (
1.3
) have been precisely identified in
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
23
]
. For the study of the Bellman functions of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, we use the notation
\Av_{E}(\psi)=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\psi
, whenever
E
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive measure and
\psi
is a real valued measurable function defined on
E
. We fix a dyadic cube
Q
and define the localized maximal operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\phi
as in (
1.1
) but with the dyadic cubes
S
being assumed to be contained in
Q
. Then for every
p>1
we let

B_{p}(f,F)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\phi)^{p}:\Av_{Q}(\phi)=f,\ \Av_{Q}(\phi^{p})=F\right\},

(1.4)

where
\phi
is nonnegative in
L^{p}(Q)
and the variables
f,F
satisfy
0<f^{p}\leq F
. By a scaling argument it is easy to see that (
1.4
) is independent of the choice of
Q
(so we may choose

Q
to be the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
).
In
[
10
]
, the function (
1.4
) has been precisely identified for the first time. The proof has been given in a much more general setting of tree-like structures on probability spaces.

More precisely we consider a non-atomic probability space
(X,\mu)
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of measurable subsets of
X
, that has a tree-like structure similar to the one in the dyadic case (the exact definition will be given in Section
2
).
Then we define the dyadic maximal operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
, by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|\phi|\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu:x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(1.5)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
,
x\in X
.

This operator is related to the theory of martingales and satisfies essentially the same inequalities as
\mathcal{M}_{d}
does. Now we define the corresponding Bellman function of four variables (two of which are integral) of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
, by

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}\left[\max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)\right]^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\phi\geq 0,\int_{X}\phi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\phi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F,\ K\subseteq X\ \text{measurable with}\ \mu(K)=k\right\},

(1.6)

the variables
f,F,L,k
satisfying
0<f^{p}\leq F
,
L\geq f
,
k\in(0,1]
.
The exact evaluation of (
1.6
) is given in
[
10
]
, for the cases where
k=1
or
L=f
. In the first case the author (in
[
10
]
) precisely identifies the function
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by evaluating it in a first stage for the case where
L=f
. That is he precisely identifies
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
(in fact
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)=F\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})^{p}
, where
\omega_{p}:[0,1]\to[1,\frac{p}{p-1}]
is the inverse function
H^{-1}_{p}
, of
H_{p}(z)=-(p-1)z^{p}+pz^{p-1}
).

The proof of the above mentioned evaluation relies on a one-parameter integral inequality which is proved by arguments based on a linearization of the dyadic maximal operator. More precisely the author in
[
10
]
proves that the inequality

F\geq\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}f^{p}+\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\;d\mu,

(1.7)

is true for every non-negative value of the parameter
\beta
and sharp for one that depends on
f
,
F
and
p
, namely for
\beta=\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})-1
. This gives as a consequence an upper bound for
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
, which after several technical considerations is proved to be best possible.Then by using several calculus arguments the author in
[
10
]
provides the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
for every
L\geq f
.

Now in
[
23
]
the authors give a direct proof of the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by using alternative methods. In fact they prove a sharp symmetrization principle that holds for the dyadic maximal operator, which is stated as Theorem 2.1 (see Section
2
).

In the second case, where
L=f
, the author (in
[
10
]
) uses the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
and provides the evaluation of the more general
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,k)
,
k\in(0,1]
. Moreover in
[
17
]
we give a determine the value of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)
, for all posible variables
f,F,L,k
.

In this paper, our intention is to prove a three-parameter inequality (two of the parameters are nonnegative real numbers ordered in a specific way and the third parameter varies as an arbitrary measurable subset of the non-atomic probability space
(X,\mu)
) which generalizes and strengthens (
1.7
). Our aim is to use this inequality in the future in order to study the following Bellman function problem (of three integral variables)

B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\phi\geq 0,\int_{X}\phi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\phi^{q}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=A,\ \int_{X}\phi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F\right\},

(1.8)

where
1<q<p
, and the variables
f,A,F
lie in the domain of definition of the above problem. We believe that the inequality that is presented in Theorem 1.1 below may be applied by an effective way in order to give an answer for the Bellman function described in (
1.8
), and this belief relies on the fact of the appearance of two additional variables which may be handled to provide the evaluation of (
1.8
). Moreover in Section 6, we prove one form of sharpness of the inequality stated in Theorem 1.1, which provides us information for the Bellman function defined in (
1.8
).

More precisely we prove an inequality that connects the
L^{p}-
integral of
\phi
on
X
and
K
, and also the
L^{p}-
integral of
M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi
, on
X
and
K
, where
K
is an arbitrary measurable subset of
X
. That is we prove the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\beta\geq\gamma\geq 0
, and
K
an arbitrary measurable subset of
X
, with measure
k\in(0,1]
. Then for every
\phi\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
such that
\int_{X}\phi\;d\mu=f
and
\int_{X}\phi^{p}\;d\mu=F
the following inequality is true

F\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right]\int_{K}\phi^{p}\;d\mu+\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\;d\mu\\
-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{K}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\;d\mu+\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}.

(1.9)

Note that if we set
\gamma=0
in (
1.9
) we get (
1.7
). Obviously, since (
1.9
) refines (
1.7
), we obtain that it is sharp. It is interesting to search for other forms of sharpness in (
1.9
), connected with the additional variables
\gamma
and
K
and by this way we wish to provide connections with the problem (
1.8
). Such results are given in Section 6. We also note in Section 5 we describe the domain of definition of
B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)
, while in Sections 6 and 7 we give lower bounds for this quantity for all possible values of
f,A,F
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
(X,\mu)
be a nonatomic probability space. We give the following

Definition 2.1
.

A set
\mathcal{T}
of measurable subsets of
X
will be called a tree if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

X\in\mathcal{T}
and for every
I\in\mathcal{T}
we have that
\mu(I)>0
.

2.

For every
I\in\mathcal{T}
there corresponds a finite or countable subset
C(I)\subseteq\mathcal{T}
containing at least two elements such that

(a)

the elements of
C(I)
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
I
.

(b)

I=\cup\,C(I)
.

3.

\mathcal{T}=\cup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{(m)}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{(0)}=\left\{X\right\}
and
\mathcal{T}_{(m+1)}=\cup_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{(m)}}C(I)
.

4.

We have
\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\sup_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{(m)}}\mu(I)\right)=0

5.

The tree
\mathcal{T}
differentiates
L^{1}(X,\mu)
. That is for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
it is true that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\\
\mu(I)\to 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\phi\;d\mu=\phi(x),

for
\mu-
almost every
x\in X
.

Then we define the dyadic maximal operator corresponding to
\mathcal{T}
by

M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)=sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\mid\phi\mid\;d\mu\;:x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(2.1)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
,
x\in X
.

We now give the following

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi:(X,\mu)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then
\phi^{*}:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined as the unique non-increasing, left continuous and equimeasurable to
\phi
function on
(0,1]
.

There are several formulas that express
\phi^{*}
, in terms of
\phi
.
One of them is as follows:

\phi^{*}(t)=\inf\left(\left\{y>0:\mu\left(\left\{x\in X:\phi(x)>y\right\}\right)<t\right\}\right),

for every
t\in(0,1]
. An equivalent formulation of the non increasing rearrangement can be given by

\phi^{*}(t)=\sup_{e\subseteq X,\\
\mu(e)\geq t}\left[\inf_{x\in e}\phi(x)\right],

for any
t\in(0,1]
.

In
[
23
]
one can see the following symmetrization principle for the dyadic maximal operator
M_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
g:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be non-increasing and
G_{1},G_{2}
be non-decreasing and non-negative functions defined on
[0,+\infty)
. Then the following identity is true, for any
k\in(0,1]

\sup\left\{\int_{K}G_{1}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)\,G_{2}(\phi)\;d\mu:\phi^{*}=g\text{ and }\mu(K)=k\right\}=\\
=\int_{0}^{k}G_{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g\right)G_{2}\left(g(t)\right)\;dt.

By using Theorem 2.1 one immediately can see that for the evaluation of
B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)
, it is enough to find the following function (related to the Hardy transform) of three integral variables :

C_{p,q}(f,A,F)=\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}:h\geq 0,\int_{0}^{1}h=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{0}^{1}h^{q}=A,\ \int_{0}^{1}h^{p}=F\right\},

(2.2)

where the function
h
varies on decreasing functions on
(0.1]
satisfying the above mentioned conditions.

3
A multiparameter inequality for
M_{\mathcal{T}}

We begin by describing a linearization of the dyadic maximal operator, as it was introduced in
[
10
]
. First we give the notion of the
\mathcal{T}
-good function. Let
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
be a non-negative function and for any
I\in\mathcal{T}
, set
\Av_{I}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu
. We will say that
\phi
is
\mathcal{T}
-good, if the set

\mathcal{A}_{\phi}=\left\{x\in X:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)>\Av_{I}(\phi)\ \text{for all}\ I\in\mathcal{T}\ \text{such that}\ x\in I\right\}

has
\mu
-measure zero.

For example one can define, for any
m\geq 0
, and
\lambda_{I}\geq 0
for each
I\in\mathcal{T}_{(m)}
(the
m
-level of the tree
\mathcal{T}
), the following function

\phi=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{(m)}}\lambda_{I}\chi_{I},

where
\chi_{I}
denotes the characteristic function of
I
. It is an easy matter to show that
\phi
is
\mathcal{T}
-good.

Suppose that we are given a
\mathcal{T}
-good function
\phi
. For any
x\in X\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
(that is for
\mu
-almost all
x\in X
), we denote by
I_{\phi}(x)
the largest element in the non empty set

\big\{I\in\mathcal{T}:x\in I\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)=\Av_{I}(\phi)\big\}.

We also define for any
I\in\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle A(\phi,I)

\displaystyle=\big\{x\in X\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\phi}:I_{\phi}(x)=I\big\},\ \text{and we set}

\displaystyle S_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\big\{I\in\mathcal{T}:\mu\left(A(\phi,I)\right)>0\big\}\cup\big\{X\big\}.

It is obvious that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi=\sum_{I\in S_{\phi}}\Av_{I}(\phi)\chi_{A(\phi,I)}
,
\mu
-almost everywhere.

We also define the following correspondence
I\to I^{\star}
with respect to
S_{\phi}
:
I^{\star}
is the smallest element of
\{J\in S_{\phi}:I\subsetneq J\}
.
This is defined for every
I\in S_{\phi}
except
X
. It is clear that the family of sets
\left\{A(\phi,I):I\in S_{\phi}\right\}
consists of pairwise disjoint sets and it’s union has full measure on
X
, since
\mu\!\left(\cup_{J\notin S_{\phi}}A(\phi,J)\right)=0
.

We give without proof a lemma (appearing in
[
10
]
) which describes the properties of the class
S_{\phi}
, and those of the sets
A(\phi,I)
,
I\in S_{\phi}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

1.

If
I,J\in S_{\phi}
then either
A(\phi,J)\cap I=\emptyset
or
J\subseteq I
.

2.

If
I\in S_{\phi}
, then there exists
J\in C(I)
such that
J\notin S_{\phi}
.

3.

For every
I\in S_{\phi}
we have that

I\approx\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}S_{\phi}\ni J\subseteq I\ \,\end{subarray}}{\bigcup}A(\phi,J).

4.

For every
I\in S_{\phi}
we have that

\displaystyle A(\phi,I)=I\setminus\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}:J^{\star}=I\ \end{subarray}}{\bigcup}J,\ \ \text{and thus}

\displaystyle\mu(A(\phi,I))=\mu(I)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}:J^{\star}=I\ \end{subarray}}\mu(J).

Here by writing
A\approx B
, we mean that
A,B
are measurable subsets of
X
such that
\mu(A\setminus B)=\mu(B\setminus A)=0
.

From the above lemma we immediately get that

\Av_{I}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}:J\subseteq I\ \end{subarray}}\int_{A(\phi,J)}\phi\;d\mu,

for any
I\in S_{\phi}
. We are now in position to prove Theorem 1.1, that is the validity of (
1.9
).

Proof.

We begin by considering a
\mathcal{T}
-good function
\phi
, satisfying
\int_{X}\phi\;d\mu=f
and
\int_{X}\phi^{p}\;d\mu=F
. Let
K
be a measurable subset of
X
, with
\mu(K)=k\in(0,1]
and
\beta,\gamma
such that
\beta>\gamma>0
.

By Lemma
3.1
we get that
F=\int_{X}\phi^{p}\;d\mu=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\int_{A_{I}}\phi^{p}
, where we write
A_{I}
for the set
A(\phi,I),I\in S_{\phi}
. We split the set
A_{I}
in two measurable subsets
B_{I},\Gamma_{I}
for any
I\in S_{\phi}
, where
\mu\left(B_{I}\right),\mu\left(\Gamma_{I}\right)>0
. The choice of
B_{I},\Gamma_{I}
will be given in the sequel. Write
\mu(A_{I})=a_{I}
, for
I\in S_{\phi}
. For any
I\in S_{\phi}
we search for a constant
\tau_{I}>0
for which

\mu(I)\,\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}\\
J^{\star}=I\ \end{subarray}}\mu(J)-(\gamma+1)\mu(B_{I})=\mu(\Gamma_{I}),

(3.1)

Then (
3.1
) in view of Lemma
3.1
is equivalent to

\mu(I)\,\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)\,\left(\mu(I)-\mu(A_{I})\right)-(\gamma+1)\mu(B_{I})=\mu(\Gamma_{I})\Leftrightarrow\\
\left[\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)\right]\mu(I)+(\beta+1)\mu(B_{I})+(\beta+1)\mu(\Gamma_{I})-(\gamma+1)\mu(B_{I})=\mu(\Gamma_{I})\Leftrightarrow\\
\left[\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)\right]\mu(I)+\beta\mu(\Gamma_{I})=(\gamma-\beta)\mu(B_{I}),

(3.2)

We let
\mu(\Gamma_{I})=k_{I}\,a_{I}
, for some
k_{I}\in(0,1)
, so
\mu(B_{I})=(1-k_{I})a_{I}
. Thus (
3.2
) becomes

\left[\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)\right]\mu(I)=(\gamma-\beta)(1-k_{I})a_{I}-\beta k_{I}\,a_{I}\Leftrightarrow\\
\left[\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)\right]\mu(I)=\gamma(1-k_{I})a_{I}-\beta a_{I},

(3.3)

We now set
p_{I}=\frac{a_{I}}{\mu(I)}
, for any
I\in S_{\phi}
.
Thus (
3.3
) gives

\tau_{I}-(\beta+1)=\gamma(1-k_{I})p_{I}-\beta p_{I}\Leftrightarrow\\
\tau_{I}=\left((\beta+1)-\beta p_{I}\right)+(1-k_{I})\gamma p_{I},

(3.4)

Note that this choice of
\tau_{I},I\in S_{\phi}
, immediately gives
\tau_{I}>0
, since
\beta>\gamma>0
and
0<p_{I}\leq 1
for any
I\in S_{\phi}
.

We write now

F=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\int_{A_{I}}\phi^{p}\;d\mu=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\int_{B_{I}}\phi^{p}\;d\mu+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\phi^{p}\;d\mu\geq\\
\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\int_{B_{I}}\phi\;d\mu\right)^{p}}{\mu(B_{I})^{p-1}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\phi\;d\mu\right)^{p}}{\mu(\Gamma_{I})^{p-1}},

(3.5)

in view of Hölder’s inequality. We denote the first and the second sum on the right of (
3.5
) by
\Sigma_{1}
,
\Sigma_{2}
respectively.
Then by (
3.1
) and Lemma 3.1 iv) we obtain the following

\Sigma_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mu(\Gamma_{I})^{p-1}}\left(\int_{I}\phi\;d\mu-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}\\
J^{\star}=I\ \end{subarray}}\int_{J}\phi\;d\mu-\int_{B_{I}}\phi\;d\mu\right)^{p}=\\

J^{\star}=I\end{subarray}}\mu(J)y_{J}-\int_{B_{I}}\phi\;d\mu\right)^{p}}{\left(\tau_{I}\mu(I)-(\beta+1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}\\
J^{\star}=I\end{subarray}}\mu(J)-(\gamma+1)\mu(B_{I})\right)^{p-1}},

(3.6)

where

, for every

.
Now because of Hölder’s inequality in the form

\frac{\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+...+\lambda_{\nu}\right)^{p}}{\left(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+....+\mu_{\nu}\right)^{p-1}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}^{p}}{\mu_{1}^{p-1}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}^{p}}{\mu_{2}^{p-1}}+...+\frac{\lambda_{\nu}^{p}}{\mu_{\nu}^{p-1}},

(3.7)

where
p>1,\mu_{i}>0

\lambda_{i}\geq 0
, for

, we have in view of
3.6
that:

\Sigma_{2}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\mu(I)y_{I}\right)^{p}}{\left(\tau_{I}\mu(I)\right)^{p-1}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in S_{\phi}\\
J^{\star}=I\ \end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\mu(J)y_{J}\right)^{p}}{\left((\beta+1)\mu(J)\right)^{p-1}}-\\
-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(\gamma+1)^{p-1}}\frac{\left(\int_{B_{I}}\phi\;d\mu\right)^{p}}{\mu(B_{I})^{p-1}}.

(3.8)

By
3.8

\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\mu(I)\frac{y_{I}^{p}}{\tau_{I}^{p-1}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\\
I\neq X\end{subarray}}\mu(I)\frac{y_{I}^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}=\\
=\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{1}+\frac{y_{X}^{p}}{\tau_{X}^{p-1}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\\
I\neq X\end{subarray}}\mu(I)y_{I}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{\tau_{I}^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}\right)=\\
=\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{1}+\frac{f^{p}}{\tau_{X}^{p-1}}+\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\\
I\neq X\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{I}}{p_{I}}\left(\frac{1}{\left((\beta+1-\beta p_{I})+(1-k_{I})\gamma p_{I}\right)^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}\right)y_{I}^{p}.

(3.9)

Note that in (
3.9
) we have used the properties of the correspondence
I\longrightarrow I^{\star}
, on
S_{\phi}
.

We denote now
\Sigma_{3}
the sum on the right of
3.9
.
Then

\Sigma_{3}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\\
I\neq X\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p_{I}}\left[\frac{\beta p_{I}-(1-k_{I})\gamma p_{I}}{(\beta+1)^{p}}(p-1)\right]a_{I}y_{I}^{p},

(3.10)

because of the inequality

\frac{1}{\left((\beta+1)-s\right)^{p-1}}-\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}\geq\frac{(p-1)s}{(\beta+1)^{p}},

(3.11)

which is true for any
\beta>0
, and
s\in[0,\beta]
, by the mean value theorem on derivatives. Note that since
\beta>\gamma>0
, we have that the quantity

\beta
so (
3.11
) applies in
\Sigma_{3}
, and gives (
3.10
). Thus

\Sigma_{3}\geq(p-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\\
I\neq X\end{subarray}}\frac{\beta-\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}a_{I}y_{I}^{p}+(p-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\\
I\neq X\end{subarray}}\frac{k_{I}\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}a_{I}y_{I}^{p}=\\
=(p-1)\frac{\beta-\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}a_{I}y_{I}^{p}-(p-1)\frac{\beta-\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}a_{X}y_{X}^{p}+\\
+(p-1)\frac{\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}k_{I}a_{I}y_{I}^{p}-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}k_{X}a_{X}y_{X}^{p}=\\
=(p-1)\frac{\beta-\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu+\\
+(p-1)\frac{\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{\Gamma}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left((\beta-\gamma)a_{X}+\gamma k_{X}a_{X}\right)f^{p},

(3.12)

where we have set
\Gamma=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{I}
.

By (
3.9
) and (
3.12
) we get

\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{1}+(p-1)\frac{\beta-\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu+\\
+(p-1)\frac{\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{\Gamma}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu+\lambda_{4},

(3.13)

where

\lambda_{4}=\frac{f^{p}}{\tau_{X}^{p-1}}-\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left((\beta-\gamma)a_{X}+\gamma k_{X}a_{X}\right)f^{p}.

(3.14)

By definition of
\tau_{X}
,
3.14
gives

\lambda_{4}=f^{p}\left[\frac{1}{\left((\beta+1)-\beta p_{X}+(1-k_{X})\gamma p_{X}\right)^{p-1}}-(p-1)\frac{(\beta-\gamma)a_{X}+\gamma a_{X}k_{X}}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\right]=\\
=f^{p}\left(\frac{1}{\left((\beta+1)-\delta\right)^{p-1}}-(p-1)\frac{\delta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\right),

(3.15)

where
\delta=(\beta-\gamma)a_{X}+\gamma a_{X}k_{X}
(note that we used that
p_{X}=a_{X}
).

Now because of inequality (
3.11
) we have that

\frac{1}{\left((\beta+1)-s\right)^{p-1}}-(p-1)\frac{s}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}},\forall s\in[0,\beta]

and note that
\delta\in(0,\beta)
, by the definition of
\delta
.

So (
3.15
) gives
\lambda_{4}\geq\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}
. Then, by (
3.13
) we have

\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{1}+(p-1)\frac{\beta-\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu+\\
+(p-1)\frac{\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{\Gamma}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu+\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}=\\
=\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{1}+\\
+\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left[\beta\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\gamma\int_{B}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu\right]+\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}},

(3.16)

where
B=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}B_{I}=X\setminus\Gamma
.

Now (
3.16
) gives

\Sigma_{2}+\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\Sigma_{1}\geq\\
\geq\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left[\beta\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\gamma\int_{B}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu\right]\Rightarrow\\
\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\left(\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}\right)+\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\Sigma_{2}\geq\\
\geq\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left[\beta\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\gamma\int_{B}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu\right].

(3.17)

But
F\geq\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}
, and
\Sigma_{2}\leq\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{p}\;d\mu
, so that we conclude from
3.17
that

\frac{F}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{p}\;d\mu\geq\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\\
\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left[\beta\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\gamma\int_{B}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu\right]

(3.18)

Now from (
3.18
) we immediately get

\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\int_{B}\phi^{p}\;d\mu+\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{p}\;d\mu\geq\\
\geq\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\left[\beta\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\gamma\int_{B}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu\right].

(3.19)

But the left side of (
3.19
) equals
F-\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\int_{B}\phi^{p}\;d\mu
, so that (
3.19
) becomes

F\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\int_{B}\phi^{p}\;d\mu+\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\\
+\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{B}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\right)^{p}\;d\mu.

(3.20)

Inequality (
3.20
) is in fact true for every choice of
B
, since every measurable subset
B
, of
X
can be written as
B=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in S_{\phi}\end{subarray}}B_{I}
, where
B_{I}=B\cap A_{I}
. Then setting
\Gamma_{I}=A_{I}\setminus B_{I}
and following the above proof, we obtain the validity of (
3.20
). Theorem
1.1
is thus proved for any
\phi
which is
\mathcal{T}
-good function (replace
B
by
K
). Note that in the above proof we have used the fact that
\mu(B_{I})>0
, for every
I\in S_{\phi}
, but this can be applied (by using the fact that
(X,\mu)

3.20
) even if
\mu(B_{I})=0
, for some
I\in S_{\phi}
. Now if
\phi\in L^{p}(X,\mu)

(\phi_{m})_{m}
, where

Then since

Obviously

Additionally
\Phi_{m}

\phi_{m}
is
\mathcal{T}
-good for any

measurable. Since

4
Applications of Theorem 1.1

Let
h:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be an arbitrary non-increasing function such that
\int_{0}^{1}h=f
and
\int_{0}^{1}h^{p}=F
, where the variables
f
,
F
satisfy
f^{p}<F
. Let
k\in(0,1]
and fix a non atomic probability space
(X,\mu)
, equipped with a tree structure
\mathcal{T}
, such that
\mathcal{T}
differentiates
L^{p}(X,\mu)
. By the proof of Theorem
2.1
(see
[
23
]
), we can construct a family
(\phi_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,1]}
, of non-negative measurable functions defined on
(X,\mu)
, and a family
(K_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,1]}
of measurable subsets of
X
, such that the following hold:
\phi_{\alpha}^{\star}=h,\forall\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{K_{\alpha}}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\alpha}\right)^{p}\;d\mu=\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt
,
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{K_{\alpha}}\phi_{\alpha}^{p}\;d\mu=\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}
and
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mu(K_{\alpha})=k
.
If we apply the inequality (
1.9
), for
\phi_{\alpha}
and
K_{\alpha}
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we get:

F\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\right)\int_{K_{\alpha}}\phi_{\alpha}^{p}\;d\mu+\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\\
+\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{X}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\alpha}\right)^{p}\;d\mu-\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{K_{\alpha}}\left(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\alpha}\right)^{p}\;d\mu,

(4.1)

for any
\beta\geq\gamma>0
.

Obviously
\int_{X}\phi_{\alpha}\;d\mu=f
and
\int_{X}\phi_{\alpha}^{p}\;d\mu=F
, since
\phi_{\alpha}^{\star}=h,\forall\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Letting
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we immediately see by (
4.1
) that

\frac{(p-1)\beta}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt\leq\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt+F-\frac{f^{p}}{(\beta+1)^{p-1}}+\\
+\left(\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}-1\right)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}.

(4.2)

Set now
\delta=\delta_{k}=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt}{\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. Obviously
1\leq\delta<\frac{p}{p-1}
and
\delta=1\Leftrightarrow h
is constant on
(0,k]
. We assume that
\beta>\delta-1>0
.
We wish, for any such
\beta
, to minimize the right side of (
4.2
), with respect to
\gamma\in(0,\beta)
.

For this purpose we define

G_{\beta}(\gamma)=\frac{(p-1)\gamma}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt+\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^{p-1}}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p},

for
\gamma\in(0,\beta]
. Note that

G^{\prime}_{\beta}(\gamma)=\frac{(p-1)}{(\beta+1)^{p}}\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt-\frac{(p-1)}{(\gamma+1)^{p}}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}.

We note at this point that if we fix
0<\beta\leq\delta-1
, then by the calculations that follow, the right side of (
4.2
) is minimized when
\gamma=0
, thus producing the respective inequality to (
1.7
) for the Hardy transform operator (by applying Theorem 2.1), and this does not give us further information for the integral properties of the Hardy transform of
h
. Thus we choose to work on the range
\beta>\delta-1>0
.

By the definition of
G_{\beta}
we get:
G^{\prime}_{\beta}(\gamma)=0\Leftrightarrow\frac{\beta+1}{\gamma+1}=\delta\Leftrightarrow\gamma=\frac{\beta+1}{\delta}-1
.
Since
\beta>\delta-1
, if we set
\gamma_{0}=\frac{\beta+1}{\delta}-1
we have that
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\beta)
. We easily get now that
\min\left\{G_{\beta}(\gamma):\gamma\in(0,\beta]\right\}=G_{\beta}(\gamma_{0})
. Replacing the value
\gamma_{0}
into (
4.2
) for any
\beta>\delta-1
, and using the definition of
\delta
we get

\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\delta}-1\right)\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}+\frac{(\beta+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta}F-\frac{(\beta+1)}{(p-1)\beta}f^{p}+\\
+\frac{(\beta+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta}\left(-1+\left(\frac{\delta}{\beta+1}\right)^{p-1}\right)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p},

(4.3)

\forall\beta>\delta-1
.

Now the right side of (
4.3
), equals

\frac{(\beta+1)}{\beta(p-1)}\left(p\delta^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-f^{p}\right)-\\
-\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}-1\right)\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}+\frac{(\beta+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta}\left(F-\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}\right)=\\
=\frac{(\beta+1)}{\beta(p-1)}\left(p\delta^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-(p-1)\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-f^{p}\right)+\\
+\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}+\frac{(\beta+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta}\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}=\\
=\frac{(\beta+1)}{\beta(p-1)}\left(H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-f^{p}\right)+\delta^{p}\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}+\frac{(\beta+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta}\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}=\\
=\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt+\Lambda(\beta)

where

\Lambda(\beta)=\frac{(\beta+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta}\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}+\frac{(\beta+1)}{(p-1)\beta}\left(H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-f^{p}\right)

(4.4)

Assume at this point that
\delta
satisfies

\delta=\delta_{k}\leq\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right),

(4.5)

while we also assume that
\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}<F
, that is
\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}>0
.
We wish to find the infimum value of
\Lambda(\beta)
, for
\beta>\delta-1
, when
\delta
satisfies (
4.5
).

It is a simple matter to show that

\Lambda^{\prime}(\beta)=-\frac{H_{p}(\beta+1)}{(p-1)\beta^{2}}\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}-\frac{1}{(p-1)\beta^{2}}\left(H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-f^{p}\right).

We solve now the equation
\Lambda^{\prime}(\beta)=0\Leftrightarrow

H_{p}(\beta+1)=\frac{f^{p}-H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}}{\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}}.

(4.6)

Note that the right side of (
4.6
) is less or equal than
H_{p}(\delta)
, that is

H_{p}(\delta)\geq\frac{f^{p}-H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}}{\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}}

(4.7)

Indeed (
4.7
) is equivalent to
H_{p}(\delta)F\geq f^{p}\Leftrightarrow\delta\leq\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right)
, which is true in view of the assumption that we made on
\delta
.

Now
H_{p}
, defined on
[1,+\infty)
satisfies the following:

H_{p}(1)=1
,
H_{p}
is strictly decreasing, and
\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\rightarrow+\infty\end{subarray}}H_{p}(x)=-\infty
.

Thus there exists a unique value
\beta_{0}\geq\delta-1
for which, we have equality in (
4.6
). That is

H_{p}(\beta_{0}+1)=\frac{f^{p}-H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}}{\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}}.

(4.8)

As is easily seen for this value of
\beta_{0}
we have that
\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta-1<\beta<+\infty\end{subarray}}\Lambda(\beta)=\Lambda(\beta_{0})
, thus (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) give in view of the above calculations that

\int_{k}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt\leq\frac{(\beta_{0}+1)^{p}}{(p-1)\beta_{0}}\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}+\frac{\beta_{0}+1}{(p-1)\beta_{0}}\left(H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}-f^{p}\right).

(4.9)

It is not difficult now to show, that the right side of (
4.9
) equals

\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}-H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}}{\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}}\right)^{p},\ \ \text{where }\omega_{p}:(-\infty,1]\longrightarrow[1,+\infty)

is the inverse of
H_{p}:H_{p}^{-1}
. Thus (
4.9
) states that for any
h:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
non-increasing, with
\int_{0}^{1}h=f,\int_{0}^{1}h^{p}=F
and any
k\in(0,1]
for which
1<\delta_{k}\leq\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right)
and
\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}>0
, we have:

\int_{k}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h\right)^{p}\;dt\leq\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}-H_{p}(\delta)\int_{0}^{k}h^{p}}{\int_{k}^{1}h^{p}}\right)^{p}.

(4.10)

It is now easily seen that there exists a function
g_{1}:(0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying the following properties:

\int_{0}^{1}g_{1}=f,\int_{0}^{1}g_{1}^{p}=F

5
The range of
(f,A,F)

Let
(X,\mu)
be a probability space. We prove the following

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
be non-negative and
(f,A,F)
be such that

\int_{X}\phi\;d\mu=f
,
\int_{X}\phi^{q}\;d\mu=A
and
\int_{X}\phi^{p}\;d\mu=F
where the indices
1,q,p
satisfy
1<q<p
. Then the following inequalities are satisfied

f^{q}\leq A\leq f^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}F^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}

(5.1)

Proof.

Since
(X,\mu)
is a probability space the first inequality that is stated in (
5.1
) is an immediate consequence of Holder’s inequality. For the proof of the second one we write

\int_{X}\phi^{q}\;d\mu=\int_{X}\phi^{q\lambda}\phi^{q(1-\lambda)}\;d\mu

(5.2)

where
\lambda
is defined by
\lambda=\frac{p-q}{q(p-1)}
. Obviously the following inequality is true:
0<\lambda<\frac{1}{q}
. Then if we define
a=\frac{1}{q\lambda}
and
b=\frac{1}{1-q\lambda}
we get

a=\frac{p-1}{p-q}>1
,
b=\frac{p-1}{q-1}>1
and
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}=1
. Then, applying Holder’s inequality in (
5.2
) with exponents
a
and
b
we get

A\leq\big(\int_{X}(\phi^{q\lambda})^{a}\big)^{\frac{1}{a}}\big(\int_{X}(\phi^{q(1-\lambda)})^{b}\big)^{\frac{1}{b}}.

The last stated inequality immediately now gives the right side of (
5.1
).
∎

Remark 5.1
.

It is well known that Holder’s inequality:

\int_{X}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\;d\mu\leq\big(\int_{X}\phi_{1}^{a}\;d\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{a}}\big(\int_{X}\phi_{2}^{b}\;d\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{b}}

(5.3)

for the non-negative measurable functions
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
and exponents
a,b>1
satisfying
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}=1
, becomes equality if and only if there exist non-negative constants
c,d
with
|c|+|d|>0

for which the equality

c\phi_{1}^{a}(x)=d\phi_{2}^{b}(x)

(5.4)

holds for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
. Thus by the proof of Lemma 2.1, that is given above we conclude that the equation

A=f^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}F^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}

(5.5)

is true if and only if there exist
c,d
as above such that
c\phi(x)=d\phi^{p}(x)
, for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
. So if we consider the set

C_{\phi}=\{x\in X:\phi(x)\neq 0\}
, we must have that
\phi
should be constant on
C_{\phi}
. We conclude that (
5.5
) is true if and only if there exist a non-negative real number
c
and a measurable subset
C
of
X
for which the equality
\phi=c\chi_{C}
is almost everywhere true, where
\chi_{C}
denotes the characteristic function of
C
.

Finally we investigate when we do have equality on the first inequality that is stated in (
5.1
). If
\int_{X}\phi\;d\mu=f
,
\int_{X}\phi^{q}\;d\mu=A
then,
f^{q}=A
if and only if the function
\phi
is constant almost everywhere on
X
, and this is a consequence of the condition (
5.4
), assuming that
\phi_{1}=\phi
,
\phi_{2}=1
and
a=q
.

We now work in the opposite direction. We state the following

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a non-atomic probability space and suppose that
f,A,F
are constants satisfying
f^{q}<A<f^{\frac{p-q}{p-1}}F^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}
, where
f>0
and
1<q<p
. Then there exist
a,b>0
and pairwise disjoint measurable subsets
C_{1},C_{2}
of
X
, each one of them of positive measure, such that the function defined by
\phi=a\chi_{C_{1}}+b\chi_{C_{2}}
, satisfies
\int_{X}\phi\;d\mu=f
,
\int_{X}\phi^{q}\;d\mu=A
and
\int_{X}\phi^{p}\;d\mu=F
.

Proof.

By considering non-increasing equimeasurable rearrangements of functions, with the domain of their definition being the interval
(0,1]
, it is enough to prove that, under the conditions that are stated for
f,A,F
in the statement of the theorem, there exist
a,b>0
and
k_{1},k_{2}>0
with
k_{1}<1
and
k_{2}\leq 1-k_{1}
for which the function
\phi:(0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
\phi=a\chi_{(0,k_{1}]}+b\chi_{(k_{1},k_{1}+k_{2}]}
, satisfies the integral conditions that are stated in the theorem.

Thus we search for
a,b,k_{1},k_{2}
as above such that the following equations are satisfied:

k_{1}a+k_{2}b=f,

(5.6)

k_{1}a^{q}+k_{2}b^{q}=A,

(5.7)

k_{1}a^{p}+k_{2}b^{p}=F,

(5.8)

and the inequality
k_{2}\leq 1-k_{1}
holds also true.

We work on this system of equations in the following manner.
First, (
5.6
) is equivalent to
b=\frac{f-k_{1}a}{k_{2}}
, thus giving us that
a
should satisfy
a<\frac{f}{k_{1}}
. Then (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) respectively become

k_{1}a^{q}+\frac{(f-k_{1}a)^{q}}{k_{2}^{q-1}}=A,

(5.9)

k_{1}a^{p}+\frac{(f-k_{1}a)^{p}}{k_{2}^{p-1}}=F.

(5.10)

In view of (
5.9
) and (
5.10
),
a
should in fact satisfy

0<a<min\{\frac{f}{k_{1}},(\frac{A}{k_{1}})^{\frac{1}{q}},(\frac{F}{k_{1}})^{\frac{1}{p}}\}.

Now (
5.9
) gives

k_{2}=\frac{(f-k_{1}a)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}{(A-k_{1}a^{q})^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}

(5.11)

and in view of (
5.11
), (
5.10
) becomes

k_{1}a^{p}+\frac{(A-k_{1}a^{q})^{\frac{p-1}{q-1}}}{(f-k_{1}a)^{\frac{p-q}{q-1}}}=F.

(5.12)

Since
k_{1},k_{2}
should satisfy
k_{2}\leq 1-k_{1}
, we must have in view of
(
5.11
) that

k_{1}a^{q}+\frac{(f-k_{1}a)^{q}}{(1-k_{1})^{q-1}}\leq A.

(5.13)

From the above we conclude that if we find
k_{1}\in(0,1)
and
a>0
such that (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) are satisfied, then by defining
k_{2}
by (
5.11
) and

, we will have that the initial system of equations (
5.6

5.7
) and (
5.8

being also true.

For this purpose we define the following functions

(5.14)

G_{k_{1}}(a)=k_{1}a^{q}+\frac{(f-k_{1}a)^{q}}{(1-k_{1})^{q-1}},

(5.15)

in the following range of values of

Then (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) are equivalent to

F_{k_{1}}(a)=F,

(5.16)

G_{k_{1}}(a)\leq A,

(5.17)

and thus we search for
a
in the above range and
k_{1}\in(0,1)
, so that (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) hold true.
We now study the behaviour of the function
G_{k_{1}}
, where the value
a

. It’s derivative equals

B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)=F\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})^{p}.

Now let
A
and
k
be as in (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) respectively. We define the following non-increasing function on the interval
(0,1]
:
\lambda(t)=ct^{-1+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}}
, for
t\in(0,k]
, and
\lambda(t)=0
for
t\in(k,1]
, where the constant c is such that the integral inequality
\int_{0}^{1}\lambda=f
holds. Solving this last equation on
c
we deduce
c=\frac{f}{\epsilon_{0}k^{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}}}
. Substituting this value to the definition of the function
\lambda
, and using (
6.3
) we easily get that the integral conditions
\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{q}=A
and
\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{p}=F
are true. Additionally we easily see that the equality

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda=\epsilon_{0}\lambda(t),t\in(0,k],

(6.4)

is true.
Note also that if we define

\delta=\delta_{k^{\prime}}=\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{k^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\right)^{p}\;dt}{\int_{0}^{k^{\prime}}\lambda^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, for
0<k^{\prime}<k
, we see by (
6.4
), that
\delta=\epsilon_{0}=\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{k^{p-1}F})\leq\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})
, that is (
4.5
) is true for this choice of the function
\lambda
, for every
k^{\prime}
such that
0<k^{\prime}<k
, while also
\int_{k^{\prime}}^{1}\lambda>0
for every such
k^{\prime}
.

We prove now that we have equality on inequality (
4.10
) in the limit, as
k^{\prime}\rightarrow k^{-}
, where we replace
k
by
k^{\prime}
and
h
by
\lambda
. More precisely the inequality

\int_{k^{\prime}}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\right)^{p}\;dt\leq\int_{k^{\prime}}^{1}h^{p}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}-H_{p}(\delta_{k^{\prime}})\int_{0}^{k^{\prime}}\lambda^{p}}{\int_{k^{\prime}}^{1}\lambda^{p}}\right)^{p},

(6.5)

is true for every
k^{\prime}\in(0,k)
by the conditions that
\lambda
satisfies, as mentioned above and by the results of Section 4.

Now if we let
k^{\prime}\rightarrow k^{-}
, the left side of (
6.5
) tends to

\int_{k}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\right)^{p}\;dt=\int_{k}^{1}f^{p}(\frac{1}{t})^{p}\;dt=\frac{1}{p-1}(\frac{f^{p}}{k^{p-1}}-f^{p})=:\alpha(k).

Letting
k^{\prime}\rightarrow k^{-}
on the right side of (
6.5
) we immediately see that it tends to the limit

\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}y\omega_{p}(\frac{-(p-1)\alpha(k)}{y})^{p}=\alpha(k).

Thus by using the results of Section 4 we get the following

Corollary 6.1
.

If the variables
f,F,k
satisfy
f^{p}<F
and

k\in[(\frac{f^{p}}{F})^{\frac{1}{p-1}},1)
, then there exists a sequence of non-negative functions
(\phi_{n})
defined on
(X,\mu)
and satisfying

\int_{X}\phi_{n}d\mu=f
,
\int_{X}\phi^{p}_{n}d\mu=F
, a sequence of measurable sets
(K_{n})
with
\mu(K_{n})\rightarrow k
, and two sequences
(\beta_{n})
,
(\gamma_{n})
such that
\beta_{n}\geq\gamma_{n}>0
, in a way that if we replace
\phi_{n},K_{n},\beta_{n},\gamma_{n}
in (
1.9
) we get equality in the limit. Moreover we can achieve every
\phi_{n}
to satisfy
\int_{X}\phi^{q}_{n}d\mu=A
, where
A
is given by (
6.3
).

Corollary 6.2
.

B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)\geq\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{k^{p-1}F})^{p}F+\frac{1}{p-1}(\frac{f^{p}}{k^{p-1}}-f^{p})
, for every
(f,A,F)
satisfying (
6.2
) where
k
is given by (
6.3
).

Proof.

Immediate, since the non-increasing function
\lambda
satisfies the integral conditions on
(0,1]
corresponding to (
1.8
) and thus by Theorem 2.1 in Section 2, we get

B_{p,q}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,A,F)\geq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\right)^{p}\;dt,

(6.6)

while the right side of (
6.6
) equals

\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\right)^{p}\;dt+\int_{k}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\right)^{p}\;dt=\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{k^{p-1}F})^{p}F+\frac{1}{p-1}(\frac{f^{p}}{k^{p-1}}-f^{p})

∎

, when

satisfy
\omega_{q}(\frac{f^{q}}{A})<\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})
. For more recent results related to this conjecture see
[

]
and

]
.

7
A lower bound for
B_{p,q}(f,A,F)
when
(f,A,F)
satisfies
\omega_{q}\left(\frac{f^{q}}{A}\right)<\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right)

In this section we study the rest of the domain of the definition of
B_{p,q}(f,A,F)
,so that we assume that
f,A,F
satisfy

f^{q}<A<\frac{f^{q}}{H_{q}\left(\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right)\right)}

(7.1)

Now for an arbitrary
k\in(0,1)
we define the function

g_{k}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
by the following way:

g_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}A_{1}t^{-1+\frac{1}{a}}\ ,\ t\in(0,k]\\
c\ ,\ t\in(k,1]\end{cases}

where the constants
A_{1},a,c
obey the following rules:

\displaystyle c=\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}\ ,

(7.2)

\displaystyle A_{1}=\frac{B_{0}k^{-1/a}}{a}\ ,

(7.3)

\displaystyle a=\omega_{p}(z_{0})\ ,

(7.4)

where
z_{0}
is given by

z_{0}=\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)}

(7.5)

and
B_{0}
satisfies

\omega_{p}(z_{0})=\frac{B_{0}}{k}\frac{1-k}{f-B_{0}}.

(7.6)

Such a choice of
B_{0}
is possible (for details see
[
10
]
, page 321-322, Lemma 7). Then according to the results in
[
10
]
,
B_{0}
should also satisfy

\frac{f(1-k)}{f-B_{0}}=\omega_{p,k}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{p}\right)

(7.7)

For the definition of
\omega_{p,k}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{p}\right)
,see Lemma
7(i)
, page 321 in
[
10
]
.

Now in
[
17
]
it is proved that the function
g_{k}
that is constructed above satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}=f\ ,\ \int_{0}^{1}g_{k}^{p}=F\ ,\ \forall k\in(0,1)

(7.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}=B_{0}\ ,

(7.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g_{k}=ag_{k}(t)\ ,\ \forall t\in(0,1)\ ,\ \forall k\in(0,1)\ ,

(7.10)

\displaystyle g_{k}(k)=c=\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}\ ,

(7.11)

Note that
B_{0}
depends on
f,F,k
and that (
7.10
) implies that
g_{k}
is continuous and non-increasing in
(0,1]
.

We now evaluate the
L^{q}
-integral of
g_{k},
for any
k\in(0,1),
as follows:

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}^{q}=\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}^{q}+\int_{k}^{1}g_{k}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}^{q}+(1-k)c^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}+\int_{0}^{k}A_{1}^{q}t^{-q+\frac{q}{a}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}+\frac{B_{0}^{q}k^{-q/a}}{a^{q}}\cdot\frac{k^{-q+q/a+1}}{(-q+q/a+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}+\frac{B_{0}^{q}}{k^{q-1}}\frac{1}{qa^{q-1}-(q-1)a^{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}+\frac{B_{0}^{q}}{k^{q-1}}\frac{1}{H_{q}(a)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}+\frac{B_{0}^{q}}{k^{q-1}}\frac{1}{H_{q}(\omega_{p}(z_{0}))}

\displaystyle=(1-k)\left(\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}\right)^{q}+\frac{B_{0}^{q}}{k^{q-1}}\frac{1}{H_{q}(\omega_{p}(z_{0}))}

\displaystyle=(1-k)\left(\frac{B_{0}}{k}\right)^{q}\frac{1}{\omega_{p}(z_{o})^{q}}+k\left(\frac{B_{0}}{k}\right)^{q}\frac{1}{H_{q}(\omega_{p}(z_{0}))}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{B_{0}}{k}\right)^{q}\left[(1-k)\frac{1}{\omega_{p}(z_{0})^{q}}+k\frac{1}{H_{q}(\omega_{p}(z_{0}))}\right]\ ,

(7.12)

where we have used the definition of
g_{k}
, and relations (
7.2
), (
7.3
), (
7.4
) and (
7.6
).

We prove the following

Claim
1
:

\quad\lim\limits_{k\to 1^{-}}A_{k}=\frac{f^{q}}{H_{q}\left(\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right)\right)}

Proof of claim 1
:

Note that since
g_{k}
is non-increasing on
(0,1]
, we have

\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}=\frac{1}{1-k}\int_{k}^{1}g_{k}\leq\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}=f\ ,

which gives
f-B_{0}\leq f(1-k)
, thus letting
k\to 1^{-}
we get

\lim\limits_{k\to 1^{-}}B_{0}=f.

Additionally

\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}=(1-k)\left(\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}\right)^{q}\leq(1-k)f^{q},

so that

\lim\limits_{k\to 1^{-}}\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}=0,

and analogously

\lim\limits_{k\to 1^{-}}\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}=0.

Thus we get

z_{0}:=\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)}\rightarrow\frac{f^{p}}{F},\quad\text{as}\quad k\to 1^{-}

and by the chain of equalities in (
7.12
) we get that

\lim\limits_{k\to 1^{-}}A_{k}=\frac{f^{q}}{H_{q}\left(\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right)\right)}\ ,

so that claim
1
is proved.

Claim
2
:

\lim\limits_{k\to 0^{+}}A_{k}=f^{q}

Proof of claim 2
:

By (
7.6
) we get

B_{0}=k\ \frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}\ \omega_{p}(z_{0})\ ,

so that
B_{0}\to 0
, as
k\to 0^{+}
, since the factor
\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}
, remains bounded as
k\to 0^{+}
and
\omega_{p}(z_{0})\in\left[1,\frac{p}{p-1}\right]
, for any
k\in(0,1)
.

Moreover, for any
k\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p}}=\left(\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}\right)^{p}\leq\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}^{p}=\frac{1}{k}F

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}}\leq F\ ,\ \forall k\in(0,1).

Thus
B_{0}
satisfies
B_{0}\leq k^{1-\frac{1}{p}}F^{1/p}\ ,
and consequently we have

\frac{B_{0}^{q}}{k^{q-1}}\leq\frac{k^{q-\frac{q}{p}}F^{q/p}}{k^{q-1}}=k^{1-\frac{q}{p}}F^{q/p}\to 0\ ,

as
k\to 0^{+}
, since
q\in(1,p)
.

Moreover, since
1\leq\omega_{p}(z_{0})\leq\frac{p}{p-1},
for any
k\in(0,1),
we have

\frac{1}{H_{q}(\omega_{p}(z_{0}))}\leq\frac{1}{H_{q}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)}.

By the comments above we immediately get that

\frac{B_{0}^{q}}{k^{q-1}}\ \frac{1}{H_{q}(\omega_{p}(z_{0}))}\to 0,

as
k\to 0^{+}.
Thus by (
7.12
) we have that

\lim\limits_{k\to 0^{+}}A_{k}=\lim\limits_{k\to 0^{+}}\frac{(f-B_{0})^{q}}{(1-k)^{q-1}}=f^{q}\ ,

and claim 2 is proved.

Claim
3
:

\text{The function}\ k\mapsto A_{k}\ ,\text{defined on}\ k\in(0,1)\ \text{is continuous}.

Proof of claim 3
:

We write
B_{0}=B_{0}(k)
and
Z_{0}=Z_{0}(k)
, denoting the dependence of
B_{0}
and
z_{0}
on
k\in(0,1)
. If we prove that
B_{0}
is a continuous function on
k\in(0,1)
, then by (
7.5
) and (
7.12
) , the claim follows.

For this purpose, it is enough to prove that, for any fixed
k\in(0,1)
and every sequence
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq(0,1)
for whick
k_{n}\to k
, there exists a subsequence:
(k_{n_{m}})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
for which

\lim\limits_{n}B_{0}(k_{n_{m}})=B_{0}(k).

(7.13)

But for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
B_{0}(k_{n})
satisfies (using (
7.6
)) the following equality:

\displaystyle\omega_{p}(z_{0}(k_{n}))

\displaystyle=\omega_{p}\left(\frac{B_{0}(k_{n})^{p}}{k_{n}^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(1-B_{0}(k_{n}))^{p}}{(1-k_{n})^{p-1}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{0}(k_{n})}{k_{n}}\cdot\frac{1-k_{n}}{1-B_{0}(k_{n})}.

(7.14)

Also,
(B_{0}(k_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence since

0\leq B_{0}(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\int_{0}^{k_{n}}g_{k_{n}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}g_{k_{n}}=f,

so that there exists a subsequence:
(B_{0}(k_{n_{m}}))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
for which

B_{0}(k_{n_{m}})\to\lambda,\quad\text{for some}\ \lambda\in[0,f].

Note also that
\lambda
should satisfy
\lambda\neq f
, since otherwise, if we let
n=n_{m}\to\infty
in (
7.14
) we would get that the right hand side will tend to infinity, while the left hand side is bounded. Thus
\lambda\in[0,f).

Then, taking limits in (
7.14
) (replacing first
n
by
n_{m}
) we get

\omega_{p}(z)=\frac{\lambda}{k}\ \frac{1-k}{f-\lambda},

(7.15)

where

z=\frac{\lambda^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(f-\frac{(f-\lambda)^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)},

(7.16)

Then by the proof of Lemma
7
, in
[
10
]
(pages 321-322) we see that
\lambda
should satisfy:

\frac{f(1-k)}{f-\lambda}=\omega_{p,k}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right),

(7.17)

while the definition of
B_{0}
implies also that

\frac{f(1-k)}{f-B_{0}}=\omega_{p.k}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{F}\right),

(7.18)

By (
7.17
) and (
7.18
) we immediately get that
\lambda=B_{0}(k)
, thus yielding

\lim\limits_{n}B_{0}(k_{n_{m}})=B_{0}(k)

and (
7.13
) follows, thus giving the proof of claim 3.

By claims 1, 2, 3 above we get that, for any

A\in\left(f^{q},\frac{f^{q}}{H_{q}\left(\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{p}\right)\right)}\right),

there exists
k\in(0,1)
for which:
A_{k}=A
. In fact, after some tedious calculations on (
7.12
) and by using (
7.6
) we can see that the function
k\mapsto A_{k}
is strictly increasing on
(0,1)
thus giving us, for any

A\in\left(f^{q},\frac{f^{q}}{H_{q}\left(\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{p}\right)\right)}\right),

a unique
k\in(0,1)
for which
A_{k}=A.
As a consequence we have proved the following

Corollary 7.1
.

If the variables
f,A,F
satisfy

f^{q}<A<\frac{f^{q}}{H_{q}\left(\omega_{p}\left(\frac{f^{p}}{p}\right)\right)},

then there exists unique
k=k_{A}\in(0,1)
for which the function
g_{k}
satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}=f\ ,\quad\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}^{q}=A\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}^{p}=F\ ,

so that

B_{p,q}(f,A,F)\geq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g_{k}\right)^{p}dt.\quad\qed

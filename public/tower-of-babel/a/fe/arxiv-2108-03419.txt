Spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations for wave-like decaying null infinity
Chuxiao Liu †, Xiao Zhang ♭

Abstract
. We show that the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations for wave-like decaying initial data at null infinity have unique local solutions and unique global solutions for small initial data. We also generalize Christodoulou’s global generalized solutions to the wave-like decaying initial data. We emphasize that this decaying condition is sharp.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations

Let
g
be a 4-dimensional Lorentzian metric,
\phi
be a real function. The Einstein-scalar-field equations are the following systems

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} &R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}\\
&T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}\Longleftrightarrow R_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi.

(1.1)

The spherically symmetric solutions of (
1.1
) were studied extensively by Christodoulou
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]

using slight different notation of the following spherically symmetric Bondi-Sachs metrics
[
1
,
12
]

\displaystyle g=-e^{2\beta(u,r)}\frac{V(u,r)}{r}du^{2}-2e^{2\beta(u,r)}dudr+r^{2}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\big),

(1.2)

where
u
is referred as the retarded time,

V(u,r)>0,

and
\beta
,
V
satisfy the following boundary conditions

\displaystyle\beta(u,\infty)=0,\quad\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(u,r)}{r}=1.

(1.3)

Using the expressions of the Ricci curvature (c.f. Appendix), we obtain the Einstein-scalar-field equations

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} -&\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial u}+\frac{1}{2r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u}=8\pi\Big[\Big(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial u}\Big)^{2}-\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial u}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\Big],\\
-&2\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial u\partial r}+\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{V}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}=8\pi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial u}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r},\\
&\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}=2\pi r\Big(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\Big)^{2},\quad\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}=e^{2\beta}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}

(1.4)

The function
\phi
satisfies the wave equation by the twice contracted Bianchi identity

\displaystyle-2\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial u\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial u}\Big)+\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial r^{2}}+\Big(\frac{V}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\Big)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=0.

(1.5)

Given function
f(u,r)
, denote
\bar{f}(u,r)
its average

\displaystyle\bar{f}(u,r)=\dfrac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{f(u,r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}}.

Denote
D
the derivative along the incoming light rays parameterized by
u

\displaystyle D=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}-\dfrac{V}{2r}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r}.

In
[
2
]
, Christodoulou introduced

\displaystyle h=\frac{\partial(r\phi)}{\partial r}

and showed that the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations (
1.4
), (
1.5
) under boundary
conditions (
1.3
) are equivalent to the system
[
2
]

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} \beta&=-2\pi\int_{r}^{\infty}{(h-\bar{h})^{2}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}},\\
g&=e^{2\beta},\quad\bar{g}=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{gdr^{\prime}}=\frac{V}{r},\\
Dh&=\frac{1}{2r}(g-\bar{g})(h-\bar{h}).\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}

(1.6)

Let
r=\chi(u)
be the integral curves of
D
. They are the incoming light rays called characteristics and satisfy the ordinary differential equation
[
2
]

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{du}=\frac{d\chi(u)}{du}=-\frac{1}{2}\bar{g}(u,r).

(1.7)

1.2.
The Bondi mass and the Bondi-Christodoulou mass

Denote

\displaystyle m_{B}(u)=\frac{r}{2}\Big(1-\frac{V}{r}\Big)=\frac{r}{2}(1-\bar{g}).

(1.8)

Following from Bondi
[
1
]
, the Bondi mass and the final Bondi mass are defined as

\displaystyle M_{B}(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}m_{B}(u),\quad M_{B1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}M_{B}(u).

In
[
2
]
, Christodoulou defined a mass function

\displaystyle m(u,r)=\frac{r}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\bar{g}}{g}\Big)

(1.9)

and proved that it satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial m}{\partial r}&=2\pi\frac{\bar{g}}{g}(h-\bar{h})^{2}\geq 0,\\
Dm(u,r)&=-\frac{\pi}{g}\Big(\int_{0}^{r}\bar{g}(h-\bar{h})^{2}\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}

(1.10)

He also defined

\displaystyle M(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}m(u,r),\quad M_{1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}M(u).

In general,
M(u)
may not be equal to
M_{B}(u)
. So it is reasonable to call
M(u)
the Bondi-Christodoulou mass and
M_{1}
the final Bondi-Christodoulou mass.

If the metric
g
is regular in
[0,\infty)
at each
u
, then (
1.6
), (
1.10
) give

\displaystyle g\leq 1,\quad m(u,r)\geq m(u,0)=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle m_{B}(u)\geq m(u),\quad M_{B}(u)\geq M(u),\quad M_{B1}\geq M_{1}.

1.3.
Existence and uniqueness

Throughout the paper, we always assume

\displaystyle 0<\epsilon\leq 2.

We summarize the main results proved by Christodoulou in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
as the following three theorems.
(They are proved for
\epsilon=2
in the papers. But the proofs can be extended to the case
0<\epsilon\leq 2
, see
[
9
]
.)

Theorem 1.1
.

For any initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\breve{h}(r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{1+\epsilon}}\Big),\quad\frac{\partial\breve{h}}{\partial r}(r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2+\epsilon}}\Big)

as
r\rightarrow\infty
, there exists
u_{0}>0
and a unique classical solution

\displaystyle h(u,r)\in C^{1}\Big([0,u_{0}]\times[0,\infty)\Big)

of (
1.6
) which satisfies the initial condition
h(0,r)=\breve{h}(r)
and

\displaystyle h(u,r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{1+\epsilon}}\Big),\quad\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2+\epsilon}}\Big)

uniformly in
u\in[0,u_{0}]
as
r\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\breve{h}(r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{1+\epsilon}}\Big),\quad\frac{\partial\breve{h}}{\partial r}(r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2+\epsilon}}\Big)

as
r\rightarrow\infty
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that if

\displaystyle\inf_{b>0}\sup_{r\geq 0}\Big\{\Big(1+\frac{r}{b}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}|h(r)|+\Big(1+\frac{r}{b}\Big)^{2+\epsilon}\Big|b\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(r)\Big|\Big\}\leq\delta_{0},

there exists a global classical solution

\displaystyle h(u,r)\in C^{1}\Big([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\Big)

of (
1.6
) which satisfies the initial condition
h(0,r)=\breve{h}(r)
and

\displaystyle\big|h(u,r)\big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}},\quad\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{2+\epsilon}}.

The corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete with vanishing final Bondi-Christodoulou mass
M_{1}
.
Furthermore, if the final Bondi-Christodoulou mass
M_{1}
is positive, a black hole forms and, in the region exterior to the Schwarzschild sphere
r=2M_{1}
, the spacetime metric tends to the Schwarzschild metric.

In
[
3
,
4
]
, Christodoulou introduced the following definition of global generalized solutions and proved their existence and uniqueness.

Definition 1.1
.

Denote
I=[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
the complement of the central line. A generalized solution to the equation (
1.6
) is a function

\displaystyle h(u,r)\in C^{1}(I)

such that, for each
u\in[0,\infty)
,
h(u,r)\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
and the quantity
\int_{0}^{\infty}{h^{2}dr}
is bounded by a continuous function of
u
. Moreover,
h
satisfies the following properties.

(1)

h
satisfies the equation (
1.6
) in
I
and takes
h(0,r)=\breve{h}
at
u=0
as the initial data,
\bar{h}
,
g
,
\bar{g}
are continuous functions in
I
.

(2)

At each
u
, for arbitrary
r_{1}
,

\dfrac{g}{\bar{g}}\in L^{1}(0,r_{1}).

(3)

For almost all
u
, and any
(u_{1},r_{1})\in I
,

\displaystyle\xi=\lim\limits_{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\int_{\delta}^{r}{\bar{g}(h-\bar{h})\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}}}

exists and

\dfrac{g^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi}{\bar{g}r^{\frac{1}{2}}}\in L^{2}\Big((0,u_{1})\times(0,r_{1})\Big).

(4)

\bar{h}
,

are weakly differentiable in

, and

\displaystyle D\bar{h}=\dfrac{\xi}{2r},\quad Dm=-\dfrac{\pi}{g}\xi^{2}.

(5)

For each
(u_{1},r_{1})\in I
, denote
r_{0}=\chi_{u_{1}}(0;r_{1})
,

I(u_{1},r_{1})=\Big\{(u,r)|0<r<\chi_{u_{1}}(u;r),\,\,0<u<u_{1}\Big\}.

Then the following integral identity holds

\int_{0}^{r_{1}}{\frac{g}{\bar{g}}(u_{1},r)dr}+2\pi\iint\limits_{I(u_{1},r_{1})}{\frac{g\xi^{2}}{\bar{g}^{2}r}drdu}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}g(u,0)du=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}{\frac{g}{\bar{g}}(0,r)dr}.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\breve{h}(r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{1+\epsilon}}\Big),\quad\frac{\partial\breve{h}}{\partial r}(r)=O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2+\epsilon}}\Big)

as
r\rightarrow\infty
, there exists at least one global generalized solution which has the same data as a classical solution coincides with it in the domain of existence of the latter.

On the other hand, by using the double null coordinate system

\displaystyle g=-\Omega^{2}dudv+r^{2}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\big),

(1.11)

Christodoulou solved the characteristic (lightlike) initial value problem for small bounded variation norm and proved the following theorem

]
.

Theorem 1.4

If there exists universal constant
\delta_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial v}(u_{0},v)\Big|dv\leq\delta_{1},

then the unique spherically symmetric global solution exists for metric (

This result was extended to more general cases by Luk-Oh and Luk-Oh-Yang

Theorem 1.5

for any

, where

We remark that the

and

1.4.
Wave-like decaying null infinity

Gravitational waves can be described by the Bondi-Sachs metrics

\displaystyle-\Big(e^{2\beta}\frac{V}{r}-r^{2}h_{AB}U^{A}U^{B}\Big)du^{2}-2e^{2\beta}dudr-2r^{2}h_{AB}U^{B}dudx^{A}+r^{2}g_{2}

where

\displaystyle g_{2}=h_{AB}(u,x^{A},x^{B})dx^{A}dx^{B}

is certain Riemannian metrics on unit 2-sphere. Intuitively, when the waves arrive at infinity, the metrics should decay to the Minkowski metric wave-likely. For instance, as
r\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle g_{Bondi-Sachs}=g_{Minkowski}+O\Big(\frac{\sin(r)}{(1+r)^{1+\delta}}\Big),\quad\delta>-1.

Note that this kind of error terms satisfy

\displaystyle(1+r)^{1+\delta}\Big|\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big)^{k}\Big(\frac{\sin(r)}{(1+r)^{1+\delta}}\Big)\Big|<C

for any nonnegative integer
k\geq 0
. Motivated by this, the second author posed the wave-like decaying spatial infinity and found that, for the Bondi-Sachs metrics, the past limit of the Bondi energy-momentum are not equal to the ADM total energy-momentum
[
13
,
8
]

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}M_{Bondi,0}\neq E_{ADM},\qquad\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}M_{Bondi,k}\neq P_{ADM,k},

where
1\leq k\leq 3
. This violates physicist’s expectation that they should be equal to each other, see, e.g.
[
11
]
.

Therefore it is important to investigate wave-like boundary conditions and study new physical properties for gravitational waves. In particular,
for the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations, it is interesting to study existence and uniqueness of (
1.6
) for wave-like decaying initial condition, e.g.

\breve{h}(r)=\frac{\sin(r)}{(1+r)^{1+\epsilon}}.

1.5.
Main results

Let
h(u,r)
be a continuous function and possess a continuous partial derivative with respect to
r
defined on
[0,u_{1}]\times[0,\infty)
if
u_{1}<\infty
, or, on
[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)
if
u_{1}=\infty
. We call that
h(u,r)
satisfies the wave-like decaying condition at a retarded time
u
if

\displaystyle\sup_{r\geq 0}\Big\{(1+r)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(|h(u,r)|+\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\Big)\Big\}<\infty.

(1.12)

Here
h(u,r)
is replaced by
\breve{h}(r)
for initial data.

In this paper we first prove the following local existence and uniqueness of classical solutions.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
which satisfies (
1.12
), then there exists
u_{0}>0
and a unique classical solution

\displaystyle h(u,r)\in C^{1}\Big([0,u_{0}]\times[0,\infty)\Big)

of (
1.6
) which satisfies the initial condition
h(0,r)=\breve{h}(r)
and the decay property (
1.12
) uniformly in
u\in[0,u_{0}]
.

We then prove the following global existence and uniqueness for small initial data.

Theorem 1.7
.

Consider initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
which satisfies (
1.12
). Denote

\displaystyle d_{0}:=\inf_{b>0}\sup_{r\geq 0}{\Big\{\Big(1+\frac{r}{b}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(|h_{0}(r)|+\Big|b\frac{\partial h_{0}}{\partial r}(r)\Big|\Big)\Big\}}.

Then there exists
\delta>0
such that if
d_{0}<\delta
, there exists a unique global classical solution

\displaystyle h(u,r)\in C^{1}\Big([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\Big)

of (
1.6
) which satisfies the initial condition
h(0,r)=\breve{h}(r)
and the decay property

\displaystyle|h(u,r)|\leq\frac{C}{\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)^{1+\epsilon}},\quad\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)^{1+\epsilon}}

for some constant
C
depending on
\epsilon
only. Moreover, the corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete with vanishing final Bondi mass
M_{B1}
.

Finally, we extend Christodoulou’s global generalized solutions to the case of wave-like decaying initial data.

Theorem 1.8
.

Given initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
which satisfies (
1.12
), there exists at least one global generalized solution which has the same data as a classical solution coincides with it in the domain of existence of the latter.

We remark that the wave-like decaying condition is sharp for the well-posedness of Einstein-scalar-field equations and we prove our main theorems by adopting Christodoulou’s idea. Due to the slowly wave-like decaying condition, we need to derive some key estimates in more delicate way.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove the three lemmas which contain the main estimates for the solutions of the Einstein-scalar-field equations. In Section 3, we prove Theorem
1.6
. In Section 4, we prove Theorem
1.7
. In Section 5, we prove Theorem
1.8
.

2.
Main estimates

In this section, we follow from Christodoulou’s argument in
[
2
]
to prove three lemmas for the wave-like decaying solutions. As the solutions have a slowly decaying partial derivative with respect to
r
, we need to derive much more fine estimates.

Let
t\in[0,1]
,
u_{1}\in[0,\infty)
or
u_{1}=\infty
. Denote
X_{u_{1},t}
,
Y_{u_{1},t}
the spaces of continuous functions which possess continuous partial derivatives with respect to
r
defined on
[0,u_{1}]\times[0,\infty)
if
u_{1}<\infty
, or, on
[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)
if
u_{1}=\infty
such that the following norms are finite

\|f\|_{X_{u_{1},t}}=\sup_{u\in[0,u_{1}]\,\text{or}\,\,[0,\infty)}\sup_{r\geq 0}\Big\{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(|f(u,r)|+\Big|\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\Big)\Big\},

\|f\|_{Y_{u_{1},t}}=\sup_{u\in[0,u_{1}]\,\text{or}\,\,[0,\infty)}\sup_{r\geq 0}\Big\{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{1+\epsilon}|f(u,r)|\Big\}.

It is obvious that
X_{u_{1},t}
,
Y_{u_{1},t}
are Banach spaces equipped with the corresponding norms.

For any initial data
h(0,r)=\breve{h}(r)\in X_{0,t}
, denote

\displaystyle\|h\|_{X_{0,t}}=\sup_{r\geq 0}\Big\{(1+r)^{1+\epsilon}\Big(|h(0,r)|+\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(0,r)\Big|\Big)\Big\}=d>0.

(2.1)

We construct a sequence of approximate solutions by setting

\displaystyle h_{0}(u,r)=h\Big(0,\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big).

(2.2)

For
h_{n}\in X_{u_{1},t}
, define
h_{n+1}
to be the solution of the equation

\displaystyle D_{n}h_{n+1}-\frac{1}{2r}(g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n})h_{n+1}=-\frac{1}{2r}(g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n})\bar{h}_{n}

(2.3)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle h_{n+1}(0,r)=h(0,r),

where
g_{n}
is the
g
-function corresponding to
h_{n}
and
D_{n}
is the
D
-operator corresponding to
g_{n}
.

Denote

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,\,\,c=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{6}{\epsilon|1-\epsilon|},&\qquad\qquad\qquad\epsilon\neq 1,\\
&24,&\qquad\qquad\qquad\epsilon=1,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,c_{t}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,\,\,t=0,\\
&\exp\Big(\frac{2(1+\epsilon)\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{3}\Big),\quad 0<t\leq 1,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

\displaystyle\,\,a(u)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u,\,\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,u<\infty,\\
&\frac{1}{t\epsilon},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,u=\infty,\,\ t\neq 0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

and

F_{t}(u,x)=c_{t}^{2}\Big(2+8\pi c^{2}x^{2}a(u)\Big)\Big(d+\frac{24+6\epsilon+4\pi}{3\epsilon}c^{3}x^{3}a(u)\Big)\exp{\Big(\frac{8\pi c^{2}x^{2}a(u)}{3}\Big)},

C_{t}(u,x)=\frac{32\pi(\epsilon+1)}{\epsilon^{2}}c_{t}c^{3}x^{2}a(u)(3+4\pi c^{2}x^{2})\exp\Big({\frac{4\pi c^{2}x^{2}a(u)}{3}}\Big).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let initial data
\breve{h}(r)
satisfy (
2.1
). If there exists some constant
x>0
such that

\displaystyle\|h_{n}(u,r)\|_{X_{u_{1},t}}\leq x,

then it satisfies that

\displaystyle\|h_{n+1}(u,r)\|_{X_{u_{1},t}}\leq F_{t}(u_{1},x).

(2.7)

Proof:
First we prove the following claim case by case.

Claim I
: For
0<\epsilon\leq 2
,

\displaystyle|(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|\leq\frac{cxr\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{1-\epsilon}}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{2}},

(2.8)

where
c
is given by (
2.4
).

Indeed, it is easy to find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\bar{h}_{n}(u,r)|&\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{|h_{n}(u,r)|dr}\\
&\leq\frac{x}{\epsilon r}\Big[\frac{1}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{\epsilon}}\Big]\\
&\leq\frac{x}{\epsilon r\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}}\Big[1-\frac{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{2}}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{2}}\Big]\\
&\leq\frac{2x}{\epsilon\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)}.\end{aligned}

(2.9)

Case I
:
0<\epsilon<1
.

(i) For
r\geq 1+\dfrac{tu}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|&\leq|h_{n}|+|\bar{h}_{n}|\\
&\leq\frac{x}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{1+\epsilon}}+\frac{2x}{\epsilon\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)}\\
&\leq\frac{6xr}{\epsilon\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{6xr\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{1-\epsilon}}{\epsilon\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{2}}.\end{aligned}

(2.10)

(ii) For
0\leq r\leq 1+\dfrac{tu}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(h_{n}&-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{\int_{r^{\prime}}^{r}{\Big|\frac{\partial h_{n}}{\partial s}\Big|ds}dr^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\frac{x}{\epsilon r}\int_{0}^{r}{\Big[\frac{1}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r^{\prime}\Big)^{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{\epsilon}}\Big]dr^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\frac{x}{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)r}\Big[\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{1-\epsilon}-\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{1-\epsilon}-\frac{(1-\epsilon)r}{\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{\epsilon}}\Big]\\
&\leq\frac{x\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}\Big)^{1-\epsilon}}{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)r\Big(1+\frac{tu}{2}+r\Big)^{2}}\Big[F(r)+5r^{2}\Big],\end{aligned}

\displaystyle 0\leq 1-g(u,r)\leq\frac{2\pi C_{1}^{2}}{\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{2\epsilon-2}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)^{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} r(1-g)\leq&\frac{2\pi C_{1}^{2}r}{\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{2\epsilon-2}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)^{2}}\\
\leq&\frac{2\pi C_{1}^{2}}{\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{2\epsilon-2}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)}.\end{aligned}

This implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}{r(1-g)}=0.

(4.2)

Therefore

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}{g}=1,\quad\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}{g^{-1}}=1.

From (
1.8
), (
1.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle m(u,r)=\frac{1}{g}\Big[\frac{r}{2}(1-\bar{g})-\frac{r}{2}(1-g)\Big]=\frac{1}{g}\Big[m_{B}(u)-\frac{r}{2}(1-g)\Big].

In terms of (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle M(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}{m(u,r)}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}{m_{B}(u,r)}=M_{B}(u).

Thus, for wave-like decaying solutions, the Bondi-Christodoulou mass is equal to the Bondi mass.

Now similar to the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we can show

\displaystyle|\bar{h}|\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{hdr^{\prime}}\leq\frac{2C}{\epsilon\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)}.

Then

\displaystyle|h-\bar{h}|\leq|h|+|\bar{h}|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\epsilon\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{\epsilon}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)},

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0\leq m(u,r)\leq&\frac{2\pi{C^{\prime}}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{2\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{r}{\frac{dr^{\prime}}{\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r^{\prime}\Big)^{2}}}\\
=&\frac{2\pi{C^{\prime}}^{2}r}{\epsilon^{2}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}\Big)^{2\epsilon+1}\Big(1+\frac{u}{2}+r\Big)}.\end{aligned}

Let

This implies that the final Bondi mass

\displaystyle M_{B1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}{M_{B}(u)}=0.

The uniqueness can be proved as same as Theorem
1.6

Let

(5.2)

Let

\displaystyle x(u)=\sup\limits_{r\geq 4M_{0,\alpha}}{\Big[\Big(\frac{r}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}|h_{\alpha}(u,r)|\Big]}.

As pointed out in

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} &f,\,\,r\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial r}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)\\

Taking

in the inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 4M_{0,\alpha}\bar{h}^{2}_{\alpha}(4M_{0,\alpha})\leq&\int_{4M_{0,\alpha}}^{\infty}{(r+\alpha)^{2}\Big(\frac{\partial\bar{h}_{\alpha}}{\partial r}\Big)^{2}dr}\\
=&\int_{4M_{0,\alpha}}^{\infty}(h_{\alpha}-\bar{h}_{\alpha})^{2}dr\leq\frac{M_{0,\alpha}}{\pi}.\end{aligned}

This gives

\displaystyle\bar{h}^{2}_{\alpha}(4M_{0,\alpha})\leq\frac{1}{2\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Thus, for
r\geq 4M_{0,\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\bar{h}_{\alpha}(r)|&=\frac{1}{r+\alpha}\Big|(4M_{0,\alpha}+\alpha)\bar{h}_{\alpha}(4M_{0,\alpha})+\int_{4M_{0,\alpha}}^{r}{h_{\alpha}(r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}}\Big|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{r+\alpha}\Big[\frac{4M_{0,\alpha}+\alpha}{2\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\int_{4M_{0,\alpha}}^{r}{x\Big(\frac{4M_{0,\alpha}}{r^{\prime}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}dr^{\prime}}\Big]\\
&\leq\Big[\Big(1+\frac{\alpha}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big)\frac{1}{2\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{x}{\epsilon}\Big]\frac{4M_{0,\alpha}}{r}.\end{aligned}

(5.3)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{g_{\alpha}-\bar{g}_{\alpha}}{2(r+\alpha)}=\frac{m_{\alpha}}{(r+\alpha)^{2}}g_{\alpha}\leq\frac{M_{0,\alpha}}{r^{2}}.

(5.4)

Thus
(\ref{503})

(\ref{504})
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x(u)\leq&\sup\limits_{r\geq 4M_{0,\alpha}}{\Big[\Big(\frac{r}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}|h_{0}(r)|\Big]}\\
&+\frac{1}{8\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(1+\frac{\alpha}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big)\frac{u}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\\
&+\frac{\epsilon+1}{16\epsilon M_{0,\alpha}}\int_{0}^{u}{x(u^{\prime})du^{\prime}}\\
\leq&e^{\tau}\sup\limits_{r\geq 4M_{0,\alpha}}{\Big[\Big(\frac{r}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big)^{1+\epsilon}|h_{0}(r)|\Big]}\\
&+\frac{e^{\tau}}{8\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(1+\frac{\alpha}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big)\frac{u}{4M_{0,\alpha}}\Big],\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{(1+\epsilon)u}{16\epsilon M_{0,\alpha}}.

This implies the wave-like decaying for
h_{\alpha}
.

Next we show the wave-like decaying for

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial h_{\alpha}}{\partial r}(u_{1},r_{1})=\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(0,r_{0,\alpha})+\int_{0}^{u_{1}}{\Big[\frac{g_{\alpha}-\bar{g}_{\alpha}}{r+\alpha}\frac{\partial h_{\alpha}}{\partial r}\Big]_{\chi_{\alpha}}du}\\
&+\int_{0}^{u_{1}}{\Big\{\frac{1}{2(r+\alpha)^{2}}\Big[-3(g_{\alpha}-\bar{g}_{\alpha})+4\pi g_{\alpha}(h_{\alpha}-\bar{h}_{\alpha})^{2}\Big](h_{\alpha}-\bar{h}_{\alpha})\Big\}_{\chi_{\alpha}}du}.\end{aligned}

Therefore, the proof of the lemma is complete. Q.E.D.

Appendix: Spherically symmetric Bondi-Sachs metrics

The nontrivial metric components

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &g_{uu}=-e^{2\beta}\frac{V}{r},\quad\,\,g_{ur}=-e^{2\beta},\quad\,\,\,\,\,g_{\theta\theta}=r^{2},\quad\,\,\,\,g_{\psi\psi}=r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,\\
&g^{ur}=-e^{-2\beta},\quad\,\,\,\,g^{rr}=e^{-2\beta}\frac{V}{r},\quad g^{\theta\theta}=r^{-2},\quad\,g^{\psi\psi}=r^{-2}\sin^{-2}\theta.\end{aligned}

The nontrivial Christoffel symbols

\displaystyle\Gamma_{uu}^{u}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial u}-\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{2r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{V}{2r^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\theta\theta}^{u}

\displaystyle=re^{-2\beta},\quad\Gamma_{\psi\psi}^{u}=r\sin^{2}\theta e^{-2\beta},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{uu}^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{V^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}+\frac{V}{2r^{2}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}-\frac{V^{2}}{2r^{3}}-\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial u}+\frac{1}{2r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ru}^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{2r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}-\frac{V}{2r^{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{rr}^{r}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r},\quad\Gamma_{\psi\psi}^{r}=-Ve^{-2\beta}\sin^{2}\theta,\quad\Gamma_{\theta\theta}^{r}=-Ve^{-2\beta}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\theta r}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r},\quad\Gamma_{\psi\psi}^{\theta}=-\sin\theta\cos\theta,\quad\Gamma_{\psi r}^{\psi}=\frac{1}{r},\quad\Gamma_{\theta\psi}^{\psi}=\cot\theta.

The nontrivial components of the Riemann curvature tensors

\displaystyle R_{\theta r\theta}^{u}

\displaystyle=-2re^{-2\beta}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r},

\displaystyle R_{\theta r\theta}^{r}

\displaystyle=e^{-2\beta}\Big(\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{V}{2r}\Big),

\displaystyle R_{uru}^{u}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial r\partial u}-\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial r^{2}}-\frac{V}{r^{2}}-\frac{V}{2r^{3}}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{2rV}(\frac{\partial V}{\partial r})^{2},

\displaystyle R_{uru}^{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2V}{r}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial r\partial u}+\frac{V^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{V}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}-\frac{V^{2}}{r^{3}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}-\frac{V}{r^{3}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{V^{2}}{r^{4}},

\displaystyle R_{u\theta u}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=R_{u\psi u}^{\psi}=\frac{V^{2}}{r^{3}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}+\frac{V}{2r^{3}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}-\frac{V}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial u}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u}-\frac{V^{2}}{2r^{4}},

\displaystyle R_{r\theta r}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=R_{r\psi r}^{\psi}=\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r},

\displaystyle R_{u\theta r}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=R_{u\psi r}^{\psi}=\frac{V}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}-\frac{V}{2r^{3}},

\displaystyle R_{\psi\theta\psi}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\theta\Big(1-\frac{V}{r}e^{-2\beta}\Big).

The nontrivial components of the Ricci curvature tensors

\displaystyle R_{uu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2V}{r}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial u\partial r}+\frac{V^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{V}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}+\frac{V^{2}}{r^{3}}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}-\frac{V}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial u}+\frac{1}{2r}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u},

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{r}\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r},

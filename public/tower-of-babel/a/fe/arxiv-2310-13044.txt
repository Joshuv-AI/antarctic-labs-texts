Global Symmetries, Code Ensembles, and Sums Over Geometries
Ahmed Barbar, Anatoly Dymarsky, Alfred D. Shapere

Abstract
We consider abelian topological quantum field theories (TQFTs) in 3d and show that possible gaugings of invertible global symmetries naturally give rise to additive codes. These codes emerge as non-anomalous subgroups of the 1-form symmetry group, parameterizing fusion rules of condensable TQFT anyons, and the boundary theories dual to TQFTs with the anyons condensed (i.e. with the corresponding symmetry subgroup gauged) are “code CFTs”. This observation bridges together, in the holographic sense, results on 1-form symmetries in 3d TQFTs with developments connecting codes to 2d CFTs. Building on this relationship, we proceed to consider an ensemble of maximal gaugings (gaugings of Lagrangian subgroups) for a given 3d TQFT and propose that it has a holographic interpretation as an ensemble of boundary code CFTs dual to a gravitational theory – the bulk TQFT summed over geometries.



Introduction.

Error-correcting codes and related concepts have deeply penetrated many areas of physics, from condensed matter
Kitaev 2003
;
Nayak et al. 2008
, to field theory
Gaiotto and Johnson-Freyd 2022
;
Harvey and Moore 2020
, to quantum gravity
Almheiri et al. 2015
;
Pastawski et al. 2015

In the context of quantum field theory, it has been known for several decades that additive codes are related to 2d abelian CFTs
Dolan et al. 1996
;
Dong et al. 1998
, an idea that has gained traction recently
Dymarsky and Shapere 2020
. The relation to codes has led to applications to the modular bootstrap
Dymarsky and Shapere 2021a
;
Dymarsky and Kalloor 2023

Kawabata et al. 2023a
;
Kawabata and Yahagi 2023a

One of the questions raised by these results is to understand the physical nature of the relation between codes and CFTs, and to explore its holographic counterpart. In this letter we address this question and identify how codes emerge in the context of 3d topological quantum field theories, which are holographically dual to the 2d CFTs in question. We find that classical additive codes describe subgroups of invertible global symmetries, with even self-dual codes corresponding to maximal non-anomalous (Lagrangian) subgroups. The code-based CFTs emerge as boundary duals to 3d TQFTs after non-anomalous symmetries have been gauged. The connection between codes and gaugings of 1-form symmetries opens the door to a new interpretation of many results pertaining to higher-form symmetries
Belov and Moore 2005
;
Kapustin and Saulina 2011
;
Gaiotto et al. 2015
;
Hsin et al. 2019
;
Buican and Radhakrishnan 2022
;
Yu 2021
;
Benini et al. 2023
;
Roumpedakis et al. 2023
;
Buican and Radhakrishnan 2023
, connecting them with classic problems in information theory and discrete mathematics.

In what follows we first review the relation between codes and abelian 2d CFTs
Dymarsky and Shapere 2020
. Then we discuss 3d abelian TQFTs – abelian Chern-Simons (CS) theories – and make an explicit connection between their invertible global symmetries and additive codes. Inspired by
Aharnoy et al. 2023
we then proceed to consider the ensemble of all maximal gaugings of the 1-form symmetry group of such a TQFT, and propose that
it has an equivalent description as a “gravitational” theory summing over inequivalent classes of 3d topologies. We conclude with a discussion of generalizations beyond abelian CS theories.

Codes and CFTs.

The code construction of
Dymarsky and Shapere 2020
;
Angelinos et al. 2022
;
Aharnoy et al. 2023
is defined with help of a lattice
{\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n,\bar{n}}
, which is even with respect to a Lorentzian bilinear form
\eta
of signature
(n,{\bar{n}})
. In what follows we assume
n={\bar{n}}
, but our results can be easily extended to general case.
The abelian group
G={\Lambda}^{*}/{\Lambda}
is defined in terms of the dual lattice

{\Lambda}^{*}=\{\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{n,\bar{n}}\,|\,\eta(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z},\,\,\forall\,\ell^{\prime}\in{\Lambda}\}.

(1)

It inherits its scalar product
\eta
from
{\Lambda}
.
The group
G
is the “dictionary” – the full set of all possible “words.” A code
\mathcal{C}\subset G
is a collection of words
c\in G
closed under addition. A code is even when all its codewords
c\in\mathcal{C}
are even with respect to
\eta
.
A code is self-dual with respect to
\eta
when only the codewords of
\mathcal{C}
have integer scalar product with all codewords of
\mathcal{C}
.

The crucial step relating codes to lattices is provided by Construction A of Leech and Sloane

;
Conway and Sloane 2013
. The code-based lattice is defined by

\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}=\{v\,|\,v\in{\Lambda}^{*},\,v\,{\rm mod}\,{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{C}\},

(2)

or, equivalently as a lattice satisfying
{\Lambda}\subset\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\Lambda}^{*}
, and
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}/{\Lambda}=\mathcal{C}
.
When a code is even and/or self-dual, the lattice
{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}
is even and/or self-dual correspondingly. Thus starting from an even self-dual code, through (
2
), we obtain the so-called Narain lattice, an even self-dual lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n,{\bar{n}}}
. A Narain lattice, in turn, defines a Narain CFT, a 2d theory describing e.g. a string propagating on an
n
-dimensional torus
Polchinski 2007
. We call Narain theories defined this way “code CFTs.”

Narain theories depend on both Euclidean and Lorentzian structures on
\mathbb{R}^{n,\bar{n}}
.
The group
G
, and the associated codes, remain the same if
\Lambda
is changed by an
\eta
-preserving transformation from
O(n,\bar{n},\mathbb{R})
, but the Euclidean structure can change. Hence the relation between codes and CFTs depends on the choice of a Euclidean embedding of
\Lambda
into
\mathbb{R}^{n,\bar{n}}
while keeping
\eta

A key property of code CFTs is the representation of the partition function in terms of the underlying code. Consider a CFT based on a code lattice
{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}
, on a Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus
g
. Its partition function
Z_{{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}}
will be a sum over “word blocks”
\Psi_{c_{1}\dots c_{g}}
– symmetric functions of
g
-tuples of codewords
c_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Focusing for simplicity on
\Sigma
of genus one we find

Z_{\mathcal{C}}=W_{\mathcal{C}}(\{\Psi\}),\quad Z_{\mathcal{C}}\equiv Z_{{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}},\quad W_{\mathcal{C}}=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\Psi_{c},

(3)

where
W_{\mathcal{C}}
is the so-called code enumerator polynomial
Dymarsky and Shapere 2020

The codeword blocks form a finite-dimensional representation of the modular group of
\Sigma
, providing through (
3
) a block decomposition of the partition function of
any
Narain CFT.

It is illustrative to consider an example where
\Lambda={\sf\Lambda}\oplus\dots\oplus{\sf\Lambda}
with a two-dimensional “glue” lattice

\displaystyle{\sf\Lambda}=\sqrt{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),\quad\eta=\bigoplus^{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

In this case a code is a collection of strings of length
n+\bar{n}
with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}

form a finite-dimensional representation of the modular group and

becomes a polynomial in

variables, satisfying certain algebraic identities

;
Yahagi 2022

Delsarte 1973

leading to a number of recent developments
Dymarsky and Shapere 2021a
;
Dymarsky and Kalloor 2023
;
Dymarsky and Shapere 2021b
;
Dymarsky and Sharon 2021

“Symmetry codes.”

Now we would like to consider gauging a subgroup of the 1-form symmetry group. In TQFT language this is described as anyon condensation.
In general, 1-form
symmetries can be t’Hooft anomalous
Gaiotto et al. 2015
;
Hsin et al. 2019
, in which case they cannot be gauged. Starting with a subgroup
\mathcal{C}\subset G
,
we demand it to be non-anomalous, i.e. with trivial spin and mutual braiding of all of its lines
Kaidi et al. 2022
;
Benini et al. 2023
.
We readily recognize this as the condition that
\mathcal{C}
is an even additive code.
Gauging the subgroup
\mathcal{C}
(condensing the anyons of
\mathcal{C}
) is equivalent to summing over insertions of all Wilson line operators with charges
c\in\mathcal{C}

Gaiotto et al. 2015
.
The resulting theory is a
U(1)^{n+\bar{n}}
CS theory with lattice

\displaystyle{\Lambda}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\Lambda_{c},\quad\Lambda_{\delta}\equiv\{v+\delta\,|\,v\in\Lambda\};

(14)

which we readily identify with
{\Lambda}^{\prime}=\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
as given by Construction A (
2
).

A particularly interesting question is to consider gauging a maximal non-anomalous subgroup
\mathcal{C}
of
G
.
In this case the resulting CS theory would be topologically trivial. Indeed, the maximal non-anomalous subgroups are the Lagrangian subgroups, those subgroups of anyons with trivial mutual braidings, such that all anyons not in the subgroup have nontrivial braiding with at least one anyon inside. This is equivalent to the condition for
\mathcal{C}
, understood as a code, to be even and self-dual; also see
Kawabata et al. 2023b
.
Accordingly,
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
will be a self-dual lattice, with only the trivial line
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}/{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}=0
appearing
in the resulting theory’s spectrum. In that theory there is a unique boundary wavefunction (
13

1

Z_{\mathcal{C}}=\Psi_{0}[{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}],

(15)

which gives the partition function of the modular-invariant CFT on the boundary
\Sigma

{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{C}}
. The relation (

\Sigma

The holographic duality (

Second, the construction of

The boundary counterpart of the picture above is straightforward. One starts with a non-modular-invariant lattice-based CFT of

Conclusions.

In this letter we have developed a picture connecting the code CFTs of
Dolan et al. 1996
;
Dymarsky and Shapere 2021a
;
Yahagi 2022
;
Kawabata et al. 2023c
;
Alam et al. 2023
to gaugings of 1-form symmetries in 3d abelian Chern-Simons theories. The codes are subgroups of the 1-form symmetry group, even codes are non-anomalous subgroups, and even self-dual codes are Lagrangian (maximal non-anomalous) subgroups.
Code CFTs arise on the 2d boundary after gauging these subgroups in the bulk.
This picture readily leads to a representation of the code CFT partition function (
3
) in terms of non-holomorphic/word blocks.
The definition of codes can be naturally generalized beyond the abelian case to other TQFTs; to emphasize that we refer to them as symmetry codes.

We have further proposed that the
ensemble of (maximal) gaugings
, understood as an ensemble of boundary code CFTs, is holographically dual to the TQFT in the bulk coupled to topological gravity (i.e., summed over topologies). This proposal encompasses several recent examples of ensemble holographic duality
Afkhami-Jeddi et al. 2021
;
Maloney and Witten 2020
;
Henriksson and McPeak 2022
;
Aharnoy et al. 2023
;
Meruliya et al. 2021
;
Meruliya and Mukhi 2021
, and calls for a deeper understanding. One question is whether
(
16
) admits a generalization for an ensemble of non-maximal gaugings, potentially providing a symmetry code interpretation to the results of
Ashwinkumar et al. 2021b
.
Another question is whether an ensemble of gaugings, understood as the boundary ensemble, can give rise to gravitational theories in higher or smaller number of dimensions.
In
2d
, a natural question is posed by
JT gravity
Saad et al. 2019
, to understand it in terms of symmetry codes, given that it can be formulated as a
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
BF-theory
Iliesiu et al. 2019
. Ultimately, it would be interesting to see if the proposed picture of global symmetry gaugings can include the higher-dimensional examples of holography, which so far have evaded an ensemble interpretation.

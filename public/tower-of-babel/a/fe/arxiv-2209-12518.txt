Classification of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over dual Radford algebras Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Rongchuan Xiong, Naihong Hu ∗

Abstract
. We determine and classify all finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero whose Hopf coradicals are isomorphic to dual Radford algebras of dimension 4 ​ p 4p for a prime p > 5 p>5 . In particular, we obtain families of new examples of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras without the dual Chevalley property. Keywords: Nichols algebra; Hopf algebra; Dual Chevalley property; Dual Radford algebra.

Introduction

Let
\mathds{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. This work is to classify those finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\mathds{k}
without the dual Chevalley property, that is, the coradical is not a subalgebra. To date there are few classification results on such Hopf algebras without pointed duals, with some exceptions in
[
19
,
25
,
33
,
34

[

As a generalization of the
lifting method
introduced by Andruskiewitsch-Schneider in
[
7
]
, the generalized lifting method works because of the following observation. Let
A
be a Hopf algebra over
\mathds{k}
and
A_{[0]}
the subalgebra generated by the coradical
A_{0}
of
A
. We will say
A_{[0]}
is the
Hopf coradical
of
A
. Assume that
S_{A}(A_{[0]})\subseteq A_{[0]}
, the standard filtration
\{A_{[n]}\}_{n\geq 0}
, defined recursively by
A_{[n]}=A_{[n-1]}\bigwedge A_{[0]}
, is a Hopf algebra filtration, which implies that the associated graded coalgebra

\operatorname{gr}A=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{[n]}/A_{[n-1]}
with
A_{[-1]}=0
is a Hopf algebra. It follows from
[
29
, Theorem 2]
that there exists uniquely a connected graded braided Hopf algebra
R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R(n)
in
{}^{A_{[0]}}_{A_{[0]}}\mathcal{YD}
such that
\operatorname{gr}A\cong R\sharp A_{[0]}
. We call
R
and
R(1)
the
diagram
and
infinitesimal braiding
of
A
, respectively. If the coradical
A_{0}
is a Hopf subalgebra, then the generalized lifting method coincides with the lifting method.

In order to obtain new classification results, according to the principle of the generalized lifting method due to
[

]

3

•

Question 1.
Let

•

Question 3.
Given

. We call

2.2.
Braided vector spaces and Nichols algebras

For any

\displaystyle\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\tau_{i},\quad\tau_{i}\tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}=\tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}\tau_{i+1},\quad\text{ for }i\in\mathds{I}_{1,n-2},~j\not\in\{i-1,i,i+1\}.

Let

The

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{n}}\varrho_{n}(M_{n}(\tau)).

[

3.
Dual Radford algebra
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
and the category
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}\mathcal{YD}

Let
p
denote a natural number. We shall describe the dual Radford algebra
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
and the category
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}\mathcal{YD}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume that
\xi
is a primitive
2p
-th root of unity and
\lambda=\frac{\xi-1}{\xi+1}
. The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
is the algebra generated by elements
a
and
b
satisfying the relations

\displaystyle a^{2p}=1,\quad b^{2}=0,\quad ba=\xi ab,

with the coalgebra structure defined by

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta(a)=a\otimes a+\lambda^{-1}b\otimes ba^{p},\quad\epsilon(a)=1,\quad\Delta(b)=b\otimes a^{p+1}+a\otimes b,\quad\epsilon(b)=0.

and the antipode given by

S(a)=a^{2p-1},\quad S(b)=\xi^{p+1}ba^{p-2}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
is well-defined and generated by a simple subcoalgebra
C:=\mathds{k}.a\bigoplus\mathds{k}.b\bigoplus\mathds{k}.a^{p+1}\bigoplus\mathds{k}.ba^{p}
. In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
has no the dual Chevalley property.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{H}_{p,-1})=\{1,a^{p}\}
,
\mathcal{P}_{1,a^{p}}(\mathcal{H}_{p,-1})=\mathds{k}.(1{-}a^{p})\bigoplus\mathds{k}.ba^{p-1}
.

(2)

\{a^{i},ba^{i}\mid i\in\mathds{I}_{0,2p-1}\}
is a linear basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
.

(3)

\{(a^{i})^{\ast},(ba^{i})^{\ast}\mid i\in\mathds{I}_{0,2p-1}\}
is the dual basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}^{\ast}
associated to the basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
given in
(2)
.
From the multiplication table induced by the relations of
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{x}\otimes\epsilon+\widetilde{g}\otimes\widetilde{x},\quad\Delta(\widetilde{g})=\widetilde{g}\otimes\widetilde{g},

where
\widetilde{x}=\sum_{i=0}^{2p-1}(ba^{i})^{\ast}
,
\widetilde{g}=\sum_{i=0}^{2p-1}\xi^{-i}(a^{i})^{\ast}
.

(4)

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}^{\ast})
. Since
a^{2p}=1,b^{2}=0,ba=\xi ab
, it follows that
\alpha(a)
is a
2p
th root of unity and
\alpha(b)=0
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}^{\ast})=\left\{\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{2p-1}\xi^{-ij}(a^{j})^{\ast}\right\}.

Note that
\alpha_{0}=\epsilon
,
\alpha_{i}=(\alpha_{1})^{i}
, and
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}^{\ast})\simeq C_{2p}
with the generator
\alpha_{1}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{p,-1}
denote a Radford algebra of dimension

Lemma 3.6

Let

\chi^{i}

\mathcal{D}

\mathds{k}_{\chi^{i}}

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{D}^{\ast})
. Since

th roots of unity.

,

and

, we have
\lambda(b)=0=\lambda(x)
. Since

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Set
\Lambda_{p}=\{(i,j)\in\mathds{I}_{0,2p-1}\times\mathds{I}_{0,2p-1}\mid pi-j\not\equiv 0\mod 2p\}
. For any
(i,j)\in\Lambda_{p}
, there exists a simple
\mathcal{D}
-module

of dimension

whose matrices defining the
\mathcal{D}
-action with respect to a fixed basis are given by

\displaystyle[a]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\xi^{i}&0\\
0&\xi^{i+1}\end{array}\right),\quad[b]_{i,j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1\\
0&0\end{array}\right),\quad[g]_{i,j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\xi^{j}&0\\
0&-\xi^{j}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle[x]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\theta^{-1}\xi^{p-1-i}((-1)^{i}+\xi^{j})\\
\theta\xi^{p+1+i}((-1)^{i}-\xi^{j})&0\end{array}\right).

Moreover, any simple
\mathcal{D}
-module of dimension
2
is isomorphic to
V_{i,j}
for some
(i,j)\in\Lambda_{p}
;
V_{i,j}\cong V_{r,s}
if and only if
(i,j)=(r,s)
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that
V_{i,j}
is well-defined. Now we claim that any simple
\mathcal{D}
-module
V
of dimension
2
is isomorphic to
V_{i,j}
for some
(i,j)\in\Lambda_{p}
.
Since
a^{2p}=g^{2p}=1
and
ga=ag
, we can choose a basis of
V
such that the matrices defining the
\mathcal{D}
-action on
V
are of the form

\displaystyle[a]

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{array}\right),\quad[b]=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}b_{1}&b_{2}\\
b_{3}&b_{4}\end{array}\right),\quad[g]=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}g_{1}&0\\
0&g_{2}\end{array}\right),\quad[x]=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{1}&x_{2}\\
x_{3}&x_{4}\end{array}\right),

where

and

. From the relation

, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}g_{1}b_{1}&g_{1}b_{2}\\

b_{3}g_{1}&b_{4}g_{2}\end{array}\right).

Hence

(g_{1}+g_{2})b_{3}=0=(g_{1}+g_{2})b_{2}
, which implies that

since
g_{1},g_{2}\neq 0
. Similarly, from the relation

, we have
x_{1}=0=x_{4}
and
(g_{1}+g_{2})x_{3}=0=(g_{1}+g_{2})x_{2}
. If
g_{1}+g_{2}\neq 0
, then
b_{3}=0=b_{2},\,x_{3}=0=x_{2}
, that is,
[x],[b]
are zero matrices, which implies
V
is not simple
\mathcal{D}
-module, a contradiction. Hence
g_{1}=-g_{2}
.

From the relation
b^{2}=0
, we have
b_{2}b_{3}=0
. By permuting the elements of the basis, we may assume
b_{3}=0
. If
b_{2}=0
, it is clear that
V
is simple if and only if
x_{2}x_{3}\neq 0
. From the relation

\displaystyle(a_{2}-\xi a_{1})x_{3}=0,\quad(a_{1}-\xi a_{2})x_{2}=\lambda^{-1}\theta\xi^{p+1}(a_{2}^{p}-g_{1})b_{2},

which implies

b_{2}\neq 0

The relation

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{3}&0\\

0&a_{2}^{p+1}-g_{2}a_{2}\end{array}\right),

x_{3}=\theta(a_{2}^{p+1}-g_{2}a_{2})\Leftrightarrow x_{3}=\theta\xi^{p+1}(a_{1}^{p}-g_{1})a_{1}

a_{2}=\xi a_{1}

\displaystyle[a]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\lambda_{1}&0\\

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\theta^{-1}\xi^{p-1}\lambda_{1}^{-1}(\lambda_{1}^{p}+\lambda_{2})\\

Now we claim that

5.
On Hopf algebras over
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}

In this section, we shall study finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
for a natural number
p
. By computing the liftings of Nichols algebras, we construct families of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras without the dual Chevalley property and pointed duals, which constitute new examples of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras. In particular, we give a complete classification of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}
when
p>5
is a prime number.

5.1.

{}_{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}\mathcal{YD}

It is a hard question to present Nichols algebras in a suitable category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules by generators and relations, for lack of standard methods and effective tools. After tedious computations, we solve the question for some Nichols algebras in

for

, which are necessary to obtain our classification results. We leave the remaining cases to future work.

Definition 5.13

Proposition 5.18
.

(-1)^{i}=-1

\displaystyle\Delta(\sum_{i=0}^{2p-1}\alpha_{i,1}^{1}ba^{i})=(\sum_{i=0}^{2p-1}\alpha_{i,1}^{1}ba^{i})\otimes 1+1\otimes(\sum_{i=0}^{2p-1}\alpha_{i,1}^{1}ba^{i})-2\mu_{2}ba^{-1}\otimes ba^{p-1}.

in

Proposition 5.19

\operatorname{gr}A\cong\mathcal{B}(V_{i,j})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}

\displaystyle\Delta(v_{2}v_{3}+v_{3}v_{2})=(v_{2}v_{3}+v_{3}v_{2})\otimes 1+a^{-j}\otimes(v_{2}v_{3}+v_{3}v_{2}).

Similar to the proof of Proposition
5.18
, a tedious computation on
A_{[1]}
shows that
v_{2}v_{3}+v_{3}v_{2}=0
in
A
. Then similar to the proof of Proposition
5.30
or
5.32
, the defining relations of
\mathcal{B}(V_{p,j})
hold in
A
. Therefore,
A\cong\operatorname{gr}A
.

Assume that
W
is isomorphic to one of the Hopf algebras described in
(2)
–
(4)
, the proof follows from the same lines.
∎

Theorem 5.35
.

[
35
, Theorem B]

Suppose
A
is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
such that the infinitesimal braiding is an indecomposable object in
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}}\mathcal{YD}
. Then
A
is isomorphic to

(a)

\bigwedge\mathds{k}_{\chi^{k}}\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
, for
k\in\{1,3,5\}
;

(b)

\mathcal{B}(V_{3,1})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{3,5})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{2,2})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{2,4})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;

(c)

\mathcal{B}(V_{4,1})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{4,5})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;

(d)

\mathcal{B}(V_{1,1})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{1,5})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{4,2})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;
\mathcal{B}(V_{4,4})\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
;

(e)

\mathfrak{A}_{1,2}(\mu)
, for some
\mu\in\mathds{k}
;

(f)

\mathfrak{A}_{1,4}(\mu)
, for some
\mu\in\mathds{k}
.

Proof.

By
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
and Proposition 4.2, the diagram of
A
is a Nichols algebra over a simple object in
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}}\mathcal{YD}
. Let
V
be the infinitesimal braiding of
A
. Then by Theorem
4.13
,
V
is isomorphic to
\mathds{k}_{\chi^{k}}
for
k\in\{1,3,5\}
,
V_{1,1}
,
V_{4,2}
,
V_{3,1}
,
V_{2,2}
,
V_{1,4}
,
V_{4,5}
,
V_{2,4}
,
V_{3,5}
,
V_{4,4}
,
V_{1,5}
,
V_{4,1}
or
V_{1,2}
.

If
V\cong\mathds{k}_{\chi^{k}}
for
k\in\{1,3,5\}
, then by Proposition
5.24
,
A\cong\bigwedge\mathds{k}_{\chi^{k}}\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
.

If
V\cong V_{3,j}
or
V_{2,j+5}
for

, then by Proposition
5.25
,
A\cong\mathcal{B}(V)\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
.

If
V\cong V_{1,j}
or
V_{4,j+5}
for

, then by Proposition
5.33
,
A\cong\mathcal{B}(V)\sharp\mathcal{H}_{3,-1}
.

If
V\cong V_{4,j}
, then by Proposition
5.19
,

. If

for

5.19
,

∎

Theorem 5.36
.

Assume
A
is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\mathcal{H}_{4,-1}
. If the diagram of
A
admits non-trivial quadratic relations, then
A
is a basic Hopf algebra.

Proof.

Let
V
be the infinitesimal braiding of
A
. By
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
and Proposition 4.2, the diagram of
A
is a Nichols algebra over a semisimple object in
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{p,-1}}\mathcal{YD}
.

Assume that
V
is a simple object in
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{4,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{4,-1}}\mathcal{YD}
such that
\mathcal{B}(V)
admits non-trivial quadratic relations. Then by Proposition
4.5
and Remark
5.3
,
V
is isomorphic to one of the following objects

•

\mathds{k}_{\chi^{i_{k}}}
with
i_{k}\in\{1,3,5,7\}
;

•

V_{i,j}
, for
(i,j)\in\{(2,2),(2,6),(6,2),(6,6),(4,1),(4,3),(4,5),(4,7)\}
, i.e.,
(i,j)\in\Lambda_{4}^{1}
;

•

V_{i,j}
, for
(i,j)\in\{(5,2),(5,6),(1,2),(1,6),(3,3),(3,1),(3,7),(3,5)\}
, i.e.,
(i,j)\in\Lambda_{4}^{2}-\Lambda_{4}^{3}
.

Then by Propositions
5.24
and
5.25
,
A\cong\operatorname{gr}A
.

Assume that
V
is a non-simple semisimple object in
{}_{\mathcal{H}_{4,-1}}^{\mathcal{H}_{4,-1}}\mathcal{YD}
. Then by Theorem
4.14
,
\mathcal{B}(V)
admits non-trivial quadratic relations. By Propositions
5.24
,
5.28
,
5.29
and
5.32
, it follows that
A\cong\operatorname{gr}A
.
∎

Smooth Selection for Infinite Sets
Fushuai Jiang, Garving K. Luli, Kevin O’Neill

Abstract
. Whitney’s extension problem asks the following: Given a compact set E ⊂ ℝ n E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} and a function f : E → ℝ f:E\to\mathbb{R} , how can we tell whether there exists F ∈ C m ​ ( ℝ n ) F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) such that F = f F=f on E E ? A 2006 theorem of Charles Fefferman [ 6 ] answers this question in its full generality. In this paper, we establish a version of this theorem adapted for variants of the Whitney extension problem, including nonnegative extensions and the smooth selection problems. Among other things, we generalize the Finiteness Principle for smooth selection by Fefferman-Israel-Luli [ 9 ] to the setting of infinite sets. Our main result is stated in terms of the iterated Glaeser refinement of a bundle formed by taking potential Taylor polynomials at each point of E E . In particular, we show that such bundles (and any bundles with closed, convex fibers) stabilize after a bounded number of Glaeser refinements, thus strengthening the previous results of Glaeser, Bierstone-Milman-Pawłucki, and Fefferman which only hold for bundles with affine fibers.



1.
Introduction

Let
m,n,d
be positive integers. We write
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
to denote the space of all functions
\vec{F}:=(F_{1},\cdots,F_{d}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}\vec{F}=(\partial^{\alpha}F_{1},\cdots,\partial^{\alpha}F_{d})
(for all
|\alpha|\leq m
) are continuous and
bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We equip
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})

with the usual norm

\|{\vec{F}}\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq m,\\
1\leq j\leq d\end{subarray}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}F_{j}(x)|

and seminorm

\|{\vec{F}}\|_{\dot{C}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|=m,\\
1\leq j\leq d\end{subarray}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}F_{j}(x)|.

When
d=1
, we write
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
instead of
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Whitney’s extension problem
[
33
]
asks, given a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a function
f:E\to\mathbb{R}
, how can we tell whether there exists
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F|_{E}=f
? The following nonnegative variation of Whitney’s extension problem has attracted some recent attention (see
[
9
,
10
,
21
,
20
,
22
]
).

Problem 1.1

(Nonnegative extension)
.

Given integers
m\geq 0,n\geq 1
, an arbitrary subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f:E\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, how can we tell if there exists
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F\geq 0
and
F|_{E}=f
?

Simple examples, such as
E=\{0\}\cup\{\frac{1}{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
f(x)=x
and
m\geq 1
, demonstrate that the existence of a nonnegative
C^{m}
extension does not follow merely from nonnegative
f
and the existence of a (not necessarily nonnegative)
C^{m}
extension. Thus, a solution to Problem
1.1
must address an obstacle absent from the classical Whitney extension problem. Naturally, this obstacle extends to the further generalizations explored in this paper.

Problem
1.1
can be viewed as a special case of the following generalized problem (see
[
11
]
for recent progress on the analogous problem for finite sets):

Problem 1.2

(Extension with restricted range)
.

Given extended real numbers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
with
-\infty\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\infty
,
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
f:E\to[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\cap\mathbb{R}
, how can we tell if there exists
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq F\leq\lambda_{2}
and
F|_{E}=f
?

Our solution to Problems
1.1
and
1.2
will result from the solution to an even more generalized problem. In order to present our solution to this problem, we need to introduce some background definitions and recall some facts.

If
\vec{F}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d}),
we write
J_{x}\vec{F}=(J_{x}F_{1},\cdots,J_{x}F_{d})
(the “
m
-th
jet”) to denote the
m
-th degree Taylor polynomial of
\vec{F}
at
x
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the vector space of polynomials in
n
real variables and of degree at most
M
.
Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we give
\mathcal{P}
the structure of a ring, denoted
\mathcal{R}_{x}
, by defining multiplication
\odot_{x}
as
R\odot_{x}R^{\prime}=J_{x}(RR^{\prime})
. We write
\vec{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
for the
\mathcal{R}_{x}
-module of
m
-jets of functions
\vec{F}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As a vector space,
\vec{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
can be identified with
\vec{\mathcal{P}}:=\underbrace{\mathcal{P}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{P}}_{d\text{ copies}}
, the
space of
d
-tuples of real
m
-th degree polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
\mathcal{R}_{x}
-multiplication on
\vec{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
is given by
R\odot_{x}\vec{P}=\left(R\odot_{x}P_{1},\cdots,R\odot_{x}P_{d}\right)
, where
R\in\mathcal{R}_{x}
and
\vec{P}=(P_{1},...,P_{d})
.

We write
c(m,n,d),C(m,n,d),
etc., to denote constants that depend on
m,n,d
; they may denote different constants in different appearances.

If
S
is a finite set, we write
\#(S)
to denote the number of elements in

S
.

A problem that is closely related to Problems
1.1
and
1.2
is the following

Problem 1.3

(Smooth Selection)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary set. For
each
x\in E
, let
\mathcal{K}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be closed and

\vec{F}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\vec{F}(x)\in\mathcal{K}(x)
for all
x\in E
?

The pioneer papers
[
28
,
9
,
5
]
studied the analogous version of Problem
1.3
for finite sets
E
. In particular, for finite sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the authors in
[
9
]
gave an answer to the finite set version of the problem by means of proving a finiteness principle. See also
[
16
,
30
,
29
,
25
,
31
,
26
,
27
]
for related Lipschitz selection problems and
[
10
,
21
,
22
,
20
]
for related problems of nonnegative interpolation.

The
C^{1}
case of nonnegative extension (Problem
1.1
) was addressed in
[

]
, though little other work has directly addressed the infinite set versions of the constrained extension problems addressed here.

In this paper, we modify the approach of
[
6
]
to answer Problem
1.3
for infinite sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Before we present our solution to the above problems, we would like to point out another closely related problem for which our result can provide an answer:

Problem 1.4

(Generalized Brenner-Epstein-Hochster-Kollár Problem)
.

Suppose we are
given real-valued functions
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{d}
, an
\mathbb{R}^{s}
-valued
function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and closed convex subsets
K_{1}(x),\cdots,K_{d}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
for each
x\in E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. How can we decide whether there
exist
f_{1},\cdots,f_{d}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{s})
such that

(1.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{i}f_{i}\leq\phi\text{ on }E

and

(1.2)

f_{i}(x)\in K_{i}(x),\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d,x\in E?

The pioneer papers
[
2
,
14
,
15
]
studied Problem
1.4
where the inequalities in (
1.1
) are replaced by equations and
there are no
convex constraints (
1.2
). Note that the inequalities in (
1.1
) may be used to restrict the output of the
f_{i}
to convex sets depending on
x
, provided said convex sets are the intersection of a bounded number of half-spaces.

In order to present our solutions to Problems
1.2
(
a fortiori

1.1
),
1.3
, and
1.4
, we introduce some basic definitions.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set. A
bundle
over
E

is a family
\mathcal{H}=(H(x))_{x\in E}
of (possibly empty) subsets
H(x)\subset\vec{\mathcal{P}}
, parameterized by the points
x\in E
. We refer to each
H(x)
,
x\in E
, as a
fiber
. A
C^{m}
section
(or “section” for short) of a bundle
\mathcal{H}=(H(x))_{x\in E}
is a
C^{m}
function
\vec{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
J_{x}\vec{F}\in H(x)
for each
x\in E
. The problem of finding a
C^{m}
extension satisfying certain conditions reduces to the problem of finding a section of a given bundle (see below).

We say a bundle
(H(x))_{x\in E}
is
convex
if
H(x)
is convex for each
x\in E
. (We note that prior literature has required each fiber of a bundle to be an affine space; we drop the requirement to handle nonlinear problems.)

Fix integers
m\geq 0,n,d\geq 1
. Let
(H(x))_{x\in E}

be a bundle and let
{k^{\sharp}}
be a positive integer depending only on
m,n,d
.
For each
x_{0}\in E
, we define the
Glaeser refinement
of
H(x_{0})
, denoted by
\tilde{H}(x_{0})
, according to the following rule:

(1.3)

Let
\vec{P}_{0}\in\vec{\mathcal{P}}
. We say that
\vec{P}_{0}\in\tilde{H}(x_{0})
if and only if given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
x_{1},\cdots,x_{{k^{\sharp}}}\in E\cap B(x_{0},\delta)
, there exist
\vec{P}_{1},\cdots,\vec{P}_{{k^{\sharp}}}\in\vec{\mathcal{P}}
, with
\vec{P}_{j}\in H(x_{j})
for
j=0,1,\cdots,{k^{\sharp}}
and
\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(x_{j})\right|\leq\epsilon\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{{m-\left|\alpha\right|}}
for
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
,
0\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}},1\leq l\leq d
.

The Glaeser refinement is used, essentially, to toss polynomials which cannot satisfy the conditions of Taylor’s theorem with the polynomials remaining at nearby fibers.

We say that a bundle
(H(x))_{x\in E}
is
Glaeser stable
if
H(x)
is its own Glaeser refinement for each
x\in E
.

We will often produce repeated Glaeser refinements of the same bundle, in
which case
(H_{0}(x))_{x\in E}
will be used to denote the original bundle and
(H_{k+1}(x))_{x\in E}
will be used to denote
(\tilde{H_{k}}(x))_{x\in E}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The Glaeser refinement is a procedure first suggested in 1958 by G. Glaeser
[
18
]
for determining whether a function
f
defined on an arbitrary subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be extended to a
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function. In their studies of extension problems in
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for subanalytic sets, Bierstone, Milman and Pawłucki
[
1
]
introduced the notion of iterated paratangent bundles, which is analogous to the Glaeser refinement. Our version of the Glaeser refinement is a vector-valued version of the one used by C. Fefferman for extending a function
f
defined on an arbitrary subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function. We also apply our Glaeser refinement to a more general class of bundles than in
[
6
]
.

Here is an outline for the rest of the introduction. First, we briefly
recall Fefferman’s solution
[
6
]
to the Whitney’s extension problem for
C^{m}
and then we will point out the major hurdles in adapting the machinery to prove our main theorems (see Theorems
1.5
,
1.6
below). We will then state our main results and explain our proof in broad strokes.

Given a function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
on an arbitrary subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, to determine whether
f
can be extended to a function
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
– This is called the “Whitney Extension Problem for
C^{m}
”– C. Fefferman
[
6
]

associated an affine subspace
H(x)\subset\mathcal{P}

for each
x\in E
, with the following crucial property:

•

If
F\in C^{m}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
F=f
on
E,
then
J_{x}\left(F\right)\in H(x)
for all
x\in E
.

Evidently, if
H(x)=\emptyset
for any
x\in E
, then
f

cannot be extended to a
C^{m}
function
F
. Fefferman’s solution
[
6
]
to the
Whitney’s extension problem for
C^{m}
can be summarized as follows

(1)

We start with the trivial holding space
H_{0}(x)=\left\{P\in\mathcal{P}:P(x)=f(x)\right\}
for all
x\in E
.

(2)

We produce a list of holding spaces
H_{0}(x)\supset H_{1}(x)\supset H_{2}(x)\supset\cdots
by
repeatedly taking the Glaeser refinement (see Definition
1
).

(3)

After at most
L=2\dim\mathcal{P}+1
, we have
H_{L}(x)=H_{l}(x)
for all
l\geq L
and all
x\in E
. (In other words,
(H_{L}(x))_{x\in E}
is Glaeser stable.)

(4)

f
extends to a
C^{m}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if
H_{L}(x)
is non-empty for all
x\in E
.

Fefferman’s proof of the solution relies on the affine space structure of the
H(x)
(and breaks down for more general sets) in three crucial ways:

(1)

The proof that iterated Glaeser refinements terminate in a Glaeser stable bundle after a finite number of steps (Step 3) in
[
6
]
(adapted from
[
1
]
) relies on the affine space structure of
H_{l}
and an induction on the dimensions. It uses the fact that if
W\subset V
is a subspace with
\dim W=\dim V
, then
W=V
, which is false for the natural notion of dimension for convex sets.

(2)

The proof that the
\delta
in the definition of Glaeser stability may be chosen uniformly in
x
and
P
(with some adjustment) relies on the finite representation of
H(x)
via a linear basis. General convex sets may not be represented in terms of a finite basis.

(3)

The set
E
is partitioned according to the dimension of the
H(x)
as affine spaces. If one does this by dimension of a convex set, it must be checked this dimension does not change under natural set operations.

Our circumstances do not guarantee the presence of affine spaces. For instance, considering Problem
1.1
we see that
x^{2}
and
2x^{2}
are potential Taylor polynomials at
x=0
, while
-x^{2}
is not.

To establish the termination of iterated Glaeser refinements of bundles
(H(x))_{x\in E}
whose fibers
H(x)
are allowed to be general convex sets, we recognize the following: The Glaeser refinement depends on a local procedure (see Definition
1
). More precisely, in order to check if
\vec{P}_{0}\in\tilde{H}(x_{0})
, we need to consider only polynomials
\vec{P}_{i}
that are close to
\vec{P}_{0}
. Furthermore, convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
have a notion of dimension which allows us to view them as affine spaces locally. To formalize this idea, we introduce the
local dimension
in Definition
3.1
.

To find uniform
\delta
as dictated by (2) above, we pass from the infinite to the finite via compactness of
E
and the
H(x)
, truncating the latter as necessary. An open cover of neighborhoods in
E\times\mathcal{P}
must be carefully constructed so that each of these neighborhoods allows for uniform choice of
\delta
(for given
\epsilon
) and for the necessary quantitative analysis to be conducted. This delicate construction is done in Section
9
. Sorting the elements
x
of
E
by
\dim H(x)
is initially easy; any convex set naturally caries a dimension. However, we must show that the truncations described above do not change the dimension and ruin the partition. This is done in Section
7
.

To state our main theorems, we need to introduce a few terms.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary set. For each
x\in E
and
M\geq 0
, let
\Gamma\left(x,M\right)\subset\mathcal{\vec{P}}
be a (possibly empty) convex set. We say
\Gamma=(\Gamma\left(x,M\right))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
is a
shape field
if for all
x\in E
and
0\leq M^{\prime}\leq M<\infty
, we have

\Gamma(x,M^{\prime})\subset\Gamma\left(x,M\right)
.
We say a shape field
\Gamma=(\Gamma\left(x,M\right))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}

is
closed
if
\Gamma(x,M)
is closed for each
x\in E
and
M\geq 0
.

Any shape field
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
on a compact set
E

gives rise to a convex bundle
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
if we set

\Gamma(x)=\bigcup_{M\geq 0}\Gamma(x,M)\text{ for each }x\in E.

We may then define the Glaeser refinement of a shape field
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
to be
(\tilde{\Gamma}(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
, where
\tilde{\Gamma}(x,M)=\tilde{\Gamma}(x)\cap\Gamma(x,M)
with
\tilde{\Gamma}(x)
being the Glaeser refinement of
\Gamma(x)
.

We will also define what it means for a shape field to be regular (see Definition
2.1
) or
(C,\delta_{\text{max}})
-convex for
\delta_{\text{max}}>0
(see Definition
2.2
), though in the interest of postponing technicalities this will be presented in Section
2
. For now, we simply note that regularity ensures
\Gamma(x,M)
“varies continuously” in

and
M
, while
(C,\delta_{\text{max}})
convexity allows us to combine functions via partition of unity as in
[
9
]
. Both properties will be satisfied by reasonable examples of shape fields, in particular, the examples arising from Problems
1.1
-
1.4
.

We are now ready to state our main theorems.

Theorem 1.5

(Qualitative Main Theorem)
.

Let
m\geq 0,n,d\geq 1
be integers. There exists

k^{\sharp}=k^{\sharp}(m,n,d)
in (
(1.3)
) such that the following holds.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set. Let
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
be a bundle arising from a closed regular
(C,1)
-convex shape field. Then the iterated Glaeser refinement of
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
terminates in a Glaeser stable bundle
(\Gamma^{*}(x))_{x\in E}
in at most
2\dim\vec{\mathcal{P}}+1
steps and
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
has a section if and if every fiber of
(\Gamma^{*}(x))_{x\in E}
is nonempty.

We can further strengthen Theorem
1.5
to have better control
on the derivatives. In fact, this step is required for our proof strategy. We need the following definition for this purpose.

Given a shape field
\Gamma=(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
with nonempty fibers, we define

(1.5)

\|\Gamma\|:=\inf\left\{M\geq 0:\,\begin{matrix}[l]\text{For all $x_{1},\cdots,x_{k^{\sharp}}\in E$, there exist }\\
\text{$\vec{P}_{i}\in\Gamma(x_{i},M)$ for $1\leq i\leq{k^{\sharp}}$ such that }\\
\text{$\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(x_{i})\right|\leq M\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}$, $1\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}}$.}\end{matrix}\right\}\,.

The finiteness of
\|\Gamma\|
will be established in Lemma
6.1
. (Specifically, we prove this for the scalar-valued case in Section
6
and reduce to this case in Section
4
.)

Theorem 1.6

(Quantitative Main Theorem)
.

Let
m\geq 0,n,d\geq 1
be integers. There exists

k^{\sharp}=k^{\sharp}(m,n,d)
in (
(1.3)
) and (
1.5
) such that the following holds:

Fix
C_{w}>0
. Let

be a cube of

and

(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}

(C_{w},1)
-convex shape field. Write

(1.6)

\Gamma(x)=\bigcup_{M\geq 0}\Gamma(x,M).

Then the following hold.

(1)

The repeated Glaeser refinement of
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
terminates
in a Glaeser stable bundle
(\Gamma^{\ast}(x))_{x\in E}
after at most
2D+1
steps, where

.

(2)

If

, then there

\vec{F}\in C^{m}(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

such that

(1.7)

\|\vec{F}\|_{\dot{C}^{m}(\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|\Gamma^{\ast}\|

and

(1.8)

J_{x}\vec{F}\in\Gamma(x,C\|\Gamma^{\ast}\|)\text{ for all }x\in E.

By specializing to

\mathcal{K}(x)=\begin{cases}\left\{f(x)\right\}\text{ for }x\in E_{0}\\
[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]\cap\mathbb{R}\text{ for }x\in E\setminus E_{0}\end{cases}\,,

3.

Let

. The purpose of this section is to prove the following lemma, which serves as part 1 of Theorem

Lemma 3.1

(Termination of Glaser Refinement)
.

If
(H(x))_{x\in E}
is a convex bundle, then
H_{l}(x)=H_{2D+1}(x)
for all

The origin of Lemma

goes back to Glaeser

[
1
]
and
[
4
]

Before beginning the proof of Lemma

Lemma 3.2
.

If
(H_{0}(x))_{x\in E}
is a convex bundle, then
(H_{l}(x))_{x\in E}
is a convex bundle for all
l\geq 1
.

Proof.

We induct on
l\geq 0
. The case
l=0
is trivial by assumption.

Let
l\geq 1
. Suppose
H_{l-1}(x)
is convex for each
x\in E
. We want to show that
H_{l}(x)
is convex for each
x\in E
.

Fix
x_{0}\in E
. Let
\vec{P}_{0},\vec{Q}_{0}\in H_{l}(x_{0})
. Let
\theta\in[0,1]
, and set

\vec{R}_{0}:=(1-\theta)\vec{P}_{0}+\theta\vec{Q}_{0}.

We want to show that
\vec{R}_{0}\in H_{l}(x_{0})
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Since
\vec{P}_{0},\vec{Q}_{0}\in H_{l}(x_{0})
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
x_{1},\cdots,x_{{k^{\sharp}}}\in E\cap B(x_{0},\delta)
, there exist
\vec{P}_{1},...,\vec{P}_{{k^{\sharp}}},\vec{Q}_{1},...,\vec{Q}_{k^{\sharp}}
, with

(3.1)

\vec{P}_{j},\vec{Q}_{j}\in H_{l-1}(x_{j})\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,{k^{\sharp}};

(3.2)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(x_{j})\right|\leq\epsilon\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}};

and

(3.3)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{Q}_{i}-\vec{Q}_{j})(x_{j})\right|\leq\epsilon\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{for }\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}}.

We set

(3.4)

\vec{R}_{j}:=(1-\theta)\vec{P}_{j}+\theta\vec{Q}_{j}\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,{k^{\sharp}}.

By the induction hypothesis that
H_{l-1}(x)
is convex for each

, (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) imply
\vec{R}_{j}\in H_{l-1}(x_{j})
for each

. Moreover, by (
3.4
) and the triangle inequality,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{R}_{i}-\vec{R}_{j})(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\theta)\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(x_{j})\right|+\theta\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{Q}_{i}-\vec{Q}_{j})(x_{j})\right|

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}

for
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
and
0\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}}
.

Hence,
H_{l}(x_{0})
is convex. Since
x_{0},\vec{P}_{0},
and
\vec{Q}_{0}
were arbitrary, the lemma is proved.
∎

Definition 3.1

Given

(3.8)

For an example illustrating the notion of local dimension, consider the half-ball

. Here, we use Remark
3.2
No.
1
.

By compactness of the Grassmannian of
(D-k-1)
-planes in
\vec{\mathcal{P}}
with intersection in
\overline{B}_{\eta}(\vec{P})
and the closure of
\overline{B}_{\eta}(\vec{P})
, we may assume

converges to a subspace

by passing to a subsequence.

Since
H_{l+1}(x)\cap B_{\eta}(\vec{P})=W\cap B_{\eta}(\vec{P})
,

must equal
W
. Else, there exists
\vec{P}^{\prime}\in H_{l+1}(x)
which is not the limit of
\vec{P}_{n}\in H_{l}(x_{n})
, contradicting our prior observation on refinements.

Since (
3.15
) fails, there exists
\vec{P}^{\prime}_{n}\in V_{n}\cap B_{\eta/2}(\vec{P})
such that
\vec{P}^{\prime}_{n}\notin H_{l}(x_{n})
. Furthermore, by the convexity of

, there exists
\vec{Q}_{n}\in B_{\eta}(\vec{P})\cap V_{n}
such that
\vec{Q}_{n}\notin H_{l}(x_{n})
and
B_{\eta/4}(\vec{Q}_{n})\subset B_{\eta}(\vec{P})

.

By passing to another subsequence, we may assume
\vec{Q}_{n}
converges to some

. However, this contradicts the fact that
\vec{Q}\in H_{l+1}(x)
, establishing our claim for
\vec{P}\in\text{int}H_{l+1}(x)
.

If
\vec{P}
is in the relative boundary of
H_{l+1}(x)
, then
\dim H_{l+1}(x)>D-k-1
. Taking
\vec{Q}\in\text{int}H_{l+1}(x)
such that

, we may repeat the above argument to deduce, at the very least, that for

sufficiently close to

. This completes the proof of Claim

.
∎

Consider
y
sufficiently close to
x
. There are now distinct cases based on which of (
3.14
) or (

and

First, there is the case that (

and

Else, (

) holds for both

, meaning

for some
(D-k-1)
-dimensional affine subspace

of
\vec{\mathcal{P}}
and

for some

-dimensional affine subspace

of
\vec{\mathcal{P}}
. We see that

since
H_{2k+2}(y)\subset H_{2k+1}(y)
and
\dim V_{y}=\dim V_{y}^{\prime}
.

In any case, we see that for
y
sufficiently close to

,
H_{2k+1}(y)\cap B_{\eta}(\vec{P})
and
H_{2k+2}(y)\cap B_{\eta}(\vec{P})
have equal relative interiors. Ideally, one would like to show that these sets are completely equal; however, Lemma

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(H(x))_{x\in E}
be a bundle and fix
y_{0}\in E
. Fix
\delta_{0}>0
. Let
\left(\hat{H}(x)\right)_{x\in E}
denote the bundle made from taking
(H(x))_{x\in E}
and replacing
H(y)
by
\overline{H}(y)
for all
y\in B(y_{0},\delta_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\}
. Then
H_{1}(y_{0})=\hat{H}_{1}(y_{0})
.

In particular, if two bundles have the same relative interiors of their fibers at every
y\in B(y_{0},\delta_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\}
and
\vec{P}
is in both their fibers at
y_{0}
, then
\vec{P}
is either in the fiber at
y_{0}
of both Glaeser refinements or neither.

We recall that given
r>0
,
y
in a relatively open subset of
E
,
H_{l+1}(y)\cap B_{r}(\vec{P})
is determined solely by
H_{l}(z)\cap B_{r}(\vec{P})
for other
z
in that relatively open subset. Applying Lemma
3.4
with
y_{0}
ranging over
y
sufficiently close to
x
, we find

(3.16)

\text{int}H_{l}(y)=\text{int}H_{2k+1}(y)\text{ for }l\geq 2k+1.

Another application of Lemma
3.4
shows that
H_{l}(x)\cap B_{\eta/2}(\vec{P})
remains constant in
l
for
l\geq 2k+1
as well, completing the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

At this point, it merely remains to prove Lemma
3.4
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Since
H(x)\subseteq\hat{H}(x)
for all
x\in E
, we have
H_{1}(y_{0})\subseteq\hat{H}_{1}(y_{0})
.

Suppose
\vec{P}_{0}\in\hat{H}_{1}(y_{0})
. Let
\epsilon>0
and pick
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
such that for every
y_{1},...,y_{k^{\sharp}}\in B(y_{0},\delta)
, there exists
\vec{P}_{j}\in\hat{H}(y_{j})
for
j=0,,1,...,k^{\sharp}
such that

(3.17)

|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(y_{j})|\leq(\epsilon/2)|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq k^{\sharp}.

Now fix a particular choice of
y_{1},...,y_{k^{\sharp}}
and make a choice of
\vec{P}_{j}\in\hat{H}(y_{j})
satisfying (
3.17
). If all the
\vec{P}_{j}
lie in

, then we are done. It is possible to choose
\vec{P}_{j}\notin H(y_{j})
; however, we may always choose
\vec{P}_{j}^{\prime}\in H(y_{j})
such that
|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{j}-\vec{P}_{j}^{\prime})(x)|\leq\eta_{j}
for some
\eta_{j}>0
and all

.

By the triangle inequality, for any

,

(3.18)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}^{\prime}-\vec{P}_{j}^{\prime})(y_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}^{\prime}-\vec{P}_{i})(y_{j})|+|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(y_{j})|+|\partial^{\alpha}(\vec{P}_{j}-\vec{P}_{j}^{\prime})(y_{j})|

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{i}+(\epsilon/2)|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}+\eta_{j}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}

by taking
\eta_{j}
(

) sufficiently small relative to
\epsilon
and
\min_{i\neq j}|y_{i}-y_{j}|
.

Since the choices of
\epsilon
and
y_{j}
were arbitrary, this shows
\vec{P}_{0}\in H_{1}(y_{0})
.

The second conclusion follows from the fact that if two fibers have the same relative interior, then they have the same closure and by considering the holding space
(H^{\prime}(x))_{x\in E}
, where

for
x\neq y_{0}
and
H(y_{0})=\{\vec{P}\}
.
∎

4.
Reduction to the scalar-valued case

In this section, we show that the validity of Theorem
1.6
for
C^{m+d}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
implies the same for
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thus reducing all future analysis to scalar-valued functions. The main lemma of the section is the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m,n,d
be positive integers. Let
{k^{\sharp}}
be a sufficiently large constant depending only on
m,n,d
. If Theorem
1.6
holds for
C^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+d},\mathbb{R})
and shape fields of type
(m+1,n+d,1)
, then it also holds for
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and shape fields of type
(m,n,d)
.

We use a gradient trick inspired by
[
15
]
.

To begin with, we put more emphasis on the degree of regularity and dimension: For positive integers
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
, we write
\mathcal{P}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{s_{2}},\mathbb{R}^{s_{3}})
to denote the vector space of
s_{3}
-tuples of polynomials with degree no greater than
s_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{s_{2}}
. We write
\odot_{x}^{s_{1},s_{2}}
to denote the ring multiplication of
s_{1}
-jets at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{s_{2}}
, and denote this ring by
\mathcal{R}_{x}^{s_{1},s_{2}}
. As before, we use the same notation to denote the action of
\mathcal{R}_{x}^{s_{1},s_{2}}
on the module
\mathcal{P}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{s_{2}},\mathbb{R}^{s_{3}})
.

For the rest of the section, we use
(x,v)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},v_{1},\cdots,v_{d})
to denote a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
. We write
\nabla_{v}
to denote the operator
(\partial_{v_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{v_{d}})
. Note that if
P
is an
(m+1)
-jet (of a real-valued function) on
\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
, then
P(\,\cdot\,,0)
is an
(m+1)
-jet (of a real-valued function) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}P
is an
m
-jet (of an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued function) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We say a shape field
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
is
of type
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
if
\Gamma(x,M)\subset\mathcal{P}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{s_{2}},\mathbb{R}^{s_{3}})
for all
x\in E
and
M\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set. Let
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
be a regular
(C_{w},\delta_{\text{max}})
-convex shape field of type
(m,n,d)
. For each
x\in E
and
M\geq 0
, define

(4.1)

\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M)=\left\{\widehat{P}\in\mathcal{P}^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+d},\mathbb{R}):\,\begin{matrix}[l]\left|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{P}(x,0)\right|\leq M\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1,\\
\widehat{P}(\,\cdot\,,0)\equiv 0\text{, and }\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}\in\Gamma(x,M)\end{matrix}\right\}.

There exists
C=C(m,n,d,C_{w})
such that

(1)

(\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M))_{(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\},M\geq 0}
is a regular
(C,\delta_{\text{max}})
-convex shape field of type
(m+1,n+d,1)
on
E\times\left\{0\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
.

(2)

C^{-1}\|\Gamma\|\leq\|\widehat{\Gamma}\|\leq C\|\Gamma\|
, with
\|\cdot\|
as in Definition
1.5
.

As a corollary to part 2 of the above lemma, the Finiteness Lemma (Lemma
6.1
) for vector-valued functions immediately follows from the lemma for scalar-valued functions.

Proof.

We begin with the first statement. It is clear that
(\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M))_{(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\},M\geq 0}
is a shape field of type
(m+1,n+d,1)
.

We now prove that
(\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M))_{(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\},M\geq 0}
is regular.

Condition (
(2.1)
) is clearly satisfied, thanks to the first constraint in (
4.1
).

We prove condition (
(2.3)
). Let
\epsilon>0
and
M\geq 0
. Let
\eta=\eta(\epsilon,M)>0
be a small number to be chosen. Let
(x,0),(x^{\prime},0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\}
with
\left|(x,0)-(x^{\prime},0)\right|\leq\eta
. Let
\widehat{P}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M)
and
\widehat{P}\in\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime},0)=\bigcup_{M^{\prime}\geq 0}\widehat{\Gamma}((x^{\prime},0),M^{\prime})
, with

(4.2)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\widehat{P}-\widehat{P}^{\prime})(x,0)\right|\,,\,\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\widehat{P}-\widehat{P}^{\prime})(x^{\prime},0)\right|\leq\delta\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|^{m+1-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1.

Set
\vec{P}=\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}
and
\vec{P}^{\prime}=\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}^{\prime}
. By the definition of
\widehat{\Gamma}
,
\vec{P}\in\Gamma(x,M)
and
\vec{P}^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x^{\prime})
. In view of (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

\left|\partial^{\beta}(\vec{P}-\vec{P}^{\prime})(x)\right|\,,\,\left|\partial^{\beta}(\vec{P}-\vec{P}^{\prime})(x^{\prime})\right|\leq C\eta\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\left|\alpha\right|\leq m.

Since
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
is regular, for sufficiently small
\eta
, (
4.3
) implies

(4.4)

\vec{P}^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x,M+\tilde{\epsilon}(\eta))\text{, i.e.,}\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}\in\Gamma(x,M+\tilde{\epsilon}(\eta)).

Here
\tilde{\epsilon}(\cdot)
is a decreasing function of
\eta
, depending only on
M
.

We choose
\eta
to be sufficiently small (depending only on
\epsilon
and
M
), so that (
4.2
), (
4.4
), and a similar argument as in Lemma
2.1
altogether imply
\widehat{P}^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x^{\prime},0),M+\epsilon)
. Condition (
(2.3)
) is satisfied.

For condition (
(2.5)
), let
\epsilon>0
,
M\geq 0
, and let
\eta>0
be a sufficiently small number to be determined. Let
(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\}
,
\widehat{P}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M)
,
\widehat{P}^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}(x,0)
with

(4.5)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\widehat{P}-\widehat{P}^{\prime})(x,0)\right|\leq\eta\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\,\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1.

Set
\vec{P}=\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}
and
\vec{P}^{\prime}=\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}^{\prime}
. By the definition of
\widehat{\Gamma}
,
\vec{P}\in\Gamma(x,M)
and
\vec{P}^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x^{\prime})
. In view of (
4.5
), we have

(4.6)

\left|\partial^{\beta}(\vec{P}-\vec{P}^{\prime})(x)\right|\leq C\eta\text{ for }\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\,\left|\beta\right|\leq m.

Since
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
is regular, for sufficiently small
\eta
(depending only on
\epsilon
and
M
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and a similar argument as in Lemma
2.1
imply
\widehat{P}^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M+\epsilon)
. Condition (
(2.5)
) is satisfied.

Thus, we have shown that
(\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M))_{(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\},M\geq 0}
is regular.

We now show that
(\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M))_{(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\},M\geq 0}
is
(C,\delta_{\text{max}})
-convex for some
C
depending only on
m,n,d,C_{w}
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\text{max}}]
,
(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\}
,
M\geq 0
,
\widehat{P}_{1},\widehat{P}_{2},\widehat{Q}_{1},\widehat{Q}_{2}\in\mathcal{P}^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+d},\mathbb{R})
satisfy

(4.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{1},\widehat{P}_{2}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M)

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\widehat{P}_{1}-\widehat{P}_{2})(x,0)\right|\leq M\delta^{m+1-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\,\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1;

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{Q}_{i}(x,0)\right|\leq\delta^{-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\,\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1,\,i=1,2;\text{ and}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{1}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\widehat{Q}_{1}+\widehat{Q}_{2}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\widehat{Q}_{2}=1.

We want to show that

(4.11)

\widehat{P}=\sum_{i=1,2}\widehat{Q}_{i}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\widehat{Q}_{i}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\widehat{P}_{i}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),CM),

or equivalently,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{P}(x,0)\right|\leq M\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\,\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}(\,\cdot\,,0)\equiv 0\text{, and }

(4.14)

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}\in\Gamma(x,CM).

Thanks to (
4.7

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{P}_{i}(x,0)\right|\leq M\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d},\,\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{i}(\,\cdot\,,0)\equiv 0\text{ for }i=1,2,\text{ and }

(4.17)

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}_{i}\in\Gamma(x,M)\text{ for }i=1,2..

In view of (
4.10
) and the definition of
\widehat{P}
in (

(4.18)

\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{P}(x,0)=\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\cdot\partial^{\beta}\widehat{Q}_{1}(x,0)\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\widehat{Q}_{1}(x,0)\cdot\partial^{\alpha-\beta-\gamma}(\widehat{P}_{1}-\widehat{P}_{2})(x,0).

Using (
4.8
) and (
4.9

4.12

Thanks to (

\widehat{P}(\,\cdot\,,0)=\sum_{i=1,2}\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\widehat{P}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\equiv 0.

Thus, (
4.13
) follows.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{R}_{(x,0)}^{m+1,n+d}\to\mathcal{R}_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}
be the natural projection. Thanks to (

(4.19)

\begin{split}\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}&=\sum_{i=1,2}\pi\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}\pi\widehat{P}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}_{i}\\
&\quad\quad\quad+2\pi\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}\pi\widehat{P}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{Q}_{i}\\
&=\sum_{i=1,2}\pi\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}\pi\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\odot_{(x,0)}^{m,n+d}\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}.\end{split}

We write
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v}^{\xi}
, for

,

,

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\widehat{P}_{1}-\widehat{P}_{2})(x,0)\right|=\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\partial_{v_{s}}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}_{1}-\partial_{v_{s}}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}_{2})(x)\right|\leq M\delta^{m-\left|\beta\right|}\text{ for }\left|\beta\right|\leq m.

Therefore,

(4.20)

\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\left[\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}(\widehat{P}_{1}-\widehat{P}_{2})\right](x)\right|\leq CM\delta^{m-\left|\beta\right|}\text{ for }\left|\beta\right|\leq m.

On the other hand, we see from (

(4.21)

\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\left[\pi\widehat{Q}_{i}\big|_{v=0}\right]\right|=\left|\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{Q}(x,0)\right|\leq\delta^{-\left|\beta\right|}\text{ for }\left|\beta\right|\leq m.\right|

In view of (

)–(

-convexity of

, we can conclude that
\widehat{P}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),CM)
, so (

(\widehat{\Gamma}((x,0),M))_{(x,0)\in E\times\left\{0\right\},M\geq 0}
is

-convex.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
4.2
(1).

We turn to the second statement.

Let
M=2\|\widehat{\Gamma}\|
. We will show that
\|\Gamma\|\leq CM
.

Let
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k^{\sharp}}\in E
be given. There exist
\widehat{P}_{1}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x_{1},0),M),\cdots,\widehat{P}_{k^{\sharp}}\in\widehat{\Gamma}((x_{k^{\sharp}},0),M)

(4.22)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(\widehat{P}_{i}-\widehat{P}_{j})(x_{i},0)\right|\leq M\left|(x_{i},0)-(x_{j},0)\right|^{m+1-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+d}$, $\left|\alpha\right|\leq m+1$}.

We set

\vec{P}_{i}:=\nabla_{v}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ for }1\leq i\leq{k^{\sharp}}.

It follows immediately from (
4.22
) that

\left|\partial^{\beta}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(x_{i})\right|\leq CM\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\beta\right|}\text{ for $\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}$, $\left|\beta\right|\leq m$}.

Therefore,
\|\Gamma\|\leq\|\widehat{\Gamma}\|
.

Now we show the reverse direction. Let
M=2\|\Gamma\|
. We will show that
\|\widehat{\Gamma}\|\leq M
.

Let
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k^{\sharp}}\in E
be given. There exist
\vec{P}_{1}\in\Gamma(x_{1},M),\cdots,\vec{P}_{k^{\sharp}}\in\Gamma(x_{k^{\sharp}},M)
such that

(4.23)

\left|\partial^{\beta}(\vec{P}_{i}-\vec{P}_{j})(x_{i})\right|\leq M\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for $\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}$, $\left|\beta\right|\leq m$}.

We define

\widehat{P}_{i}(\,\cdot\,,v)=v\cdot\vec{P}_{i}(\,\cdot\,)\in\mathcal{P}^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+d},\mathbb{R})\text{ for }1\leq i\leq{k^{\sharp}}.

Observe that for
1\leq i\leq{k^{\sharp}}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,

(4.24)

\partial_{v}^{\xi}\big|_{v=0}\widehat{P}_{i}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\left|\xi\right|=0\\
P_{i,j}\text{ for some $1\leq j\leq d$}&\text{ if }\left|\xi\right|=1\\
0&\text{ if }\left|\xi\right|\geq 2\end{cases}\,.

Therefore, for
\left|\xi\right|\neq 1
, we have

(4.25)

\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v}^{\xi}(\widehat{P}_{i}-\widehat{P}_{j})(x_{i},0)\right|=0\text{ for }\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\,\xi\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\,\left|\beta\right|+\left|\xi\right|\leq m+1.

Suppose
\left|\xi\right|=1
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\partial_{v}^{\xi}=\partial_{v_{s}}
for some
s\in\left\{1,\cdots,d\right\}
. Thanks to (
4.23
), we have

(4.26)

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{v_{s}}(\widehat{P}_{i}-\widehat{P}_{j})(x_{i},0)\right|=\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(P_{i,s}-P_{j,s})(x_{i})\right|\leq M\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}=M\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{m+1-\left|\gamma\right|}.\end{split}

We see from (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) that
\|\widehat{\Gamma}\|\leq M
. This proves the second statement.

∎

With Lemma

(4.33)

In view of (

5.
Finiteness principle for shape fields

Recall from the previous section that we will be working with real-valued functions for the rest of the paper.

Definition 5.1
.

A function
\omega:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
is called a
regular modulus of continuity
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(
\omega
-1)

\omega(0)=\lim\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\omega(t)=0
and
\omega(1)=1
;

(
\omega
-1)

\omega(t)
is increasing on
[0,1]
; and

(
\omega
-1)

\omega(t)/t
is decreasing on
(0,1]
.

Note that in (
\omega
-2) and (
\omega
-3), we do not demand that
\omega
be strictly increasing, or that
\omega(t)/t
be strictly decreasing.

Let
\omega
be a regular modulus of continuity. We write
C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to denote the space of all
C^{m}
functions
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which the norm

\|F\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m\end{subarray}}\left|\partial^{\alpha}F(x)\right|+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|\alpha\right|=m\\
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
0<\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\partial^{\alpha}F(x)-\partial^{\alpha}F(x^{\prime})\right|}{\omega(\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|)}

is finite.

Similarly, we write
\dot{C}^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to denote the space of all
C^{m}
functions
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which the norm

\|F\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|\alpha\right|=m\\
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
0<\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\partial^{\alpha}F(x)-\partial^{\alpha}F(x^{\prime})\right|}{\omega(\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|)}

is finite.

The following lemma was proven in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
be a
(C_{w},\delta_{\max})
-convex shape field. Let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{\max})
,
x\in E
,
M\geq 0
,
A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime}>0
,
P_{1},\cdots,P_{k},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
. Assume that

•

P_{i}\in\Gamma(x,A^{\prime}M)
for
i=1,\cdots,k
;

•

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{i}-P_{j})(x)\right|\leq A^{\prime}M\delta^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}
for
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
,
1\leq i,j\leq k
;

•

\left|\partial^{\alpha}Q_{i}(x)\right|\leq A^{\prime\prime}\delta^{-\left|\alpha\right|}
for
\left|\beta\right|\leq m
and
1\leq i\leq k
;

•

\sum_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}\odot_{x}Q_{i}=1
.

Then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}\odot_{x}Q_{i}\odot_{x}P_{i}\in\Gamma(x,CM)
, with
C=C(A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime},C_{w},m,n,k)
.

By adapting the proof of the Finiteness Principle for shape fields from
[
9
]
(see also
[
3
,
5
]
), we obtain the following result.

Theorem 5.2

(
\dot{C}^{m,\omega}
-Finiteness Principle for Shape Fields)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. For sufficiently large
k^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{SF}}=k(m,n)
, the following holds.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary subset and
\omega
be a regular modulus of continuity. Let
\Gamma(x,M)_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
be a
(C_{w},\delta_{\max})
-convex shape field. Let
M_{0}<\infty
and
Q_{0}
be a cube of sidelength
\delta_{Q_{0}}\leq\delta_{\max}
. Assume that for each
S\subset E
with
\#(S)\leq{k^{\sharp}}_{SF}
, there exists
(P^{x})_{x\in S}
such that

(5.1)

P^{x}\in\Gamma(x,M_{0})\text{ for }x\in S

and

(5.2)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P^{x}-P^{y})(x)|\leq M_{0}\omega(|x-y|)|x-y|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,x,y\in S,x\neq y.

Then there exists

F\in C^{m,\omega}(Q_{0})
,
with

(5.3)

J_{x}F\in\Gamma(x,C^{\sharp}M_{0})\text{ for }x\in E\cap Q_{0}

and

(5.4)

\|F\|_{\dot{C}^{m,\omega}(Q_{0})}\leq C^{\sharp}M_{0}.

Here,
C^{\sharp}=C^{\sharp}(m,n,C_{w})
.

Other Preliminaries

Lemma 6.1

(Finiteness Lemma)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set. Let
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
be a regular Glaeser stable shape field. Then there exists a finite constant
A^{\sharp}
such that:

Given
x_{1},...,x_{k^{\sharp}}\in E
, there exist
P_{i}\in\Gamma(x_{i},A^{\sharp})
such that

(6.1)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{i}-P_{j})(x_{j})|\leq A^{\sharp}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,1\leq i,j\leq k^{\sharp}.

The
A^{\sharp}
from Lemma
6.1
will be fixed and referenced throughout the remainder of the paper.

Proof.

We slightly modify the proof given in
[
6
]
.

Suppose towards a contradiction, that for each
\nu=1,2,\cdots
we can find

•

x^{(\nu)}_{1},\cdots,x^{(\nu)}_{k^{\sharp}}\in E
, and

•

A^{(\nu)}>0
, with
\lim\limits_{\nu\to\infty}A^{(\nu)}=\infty
,

such that for each
\nu
,

(6.2)

there do
not
exist polynomials
P_{1},\cdots,P_{k^{\sharp}}\in\mathcal{P}
such that

(6.4)

P_{j}\in\Gamma(x_{j}^{(\nu)},A^{(\nu)})\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,{k^{\sharp}}\text{; and}

(6.5)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{i}-P_{j})(x^{(\nu)}_{j})\right|\leq A^{(\nu)}\left|x^{(\nu)}_{i}-x^{(\nu)}_{j}\right|^{{m-\left|\alpha\right|}}\text{ for }\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}}.

Since
E
is compact, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

x^{(\nu)}_{j}\to x^{(\infty)}_{j}\in E\text{ as }\nu\to\infty,\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,{k^{\sharp}}.

Let
z_{1},\cdots,z_{\mu_{\max}}
be an enumeration of the distinct elements of
\left\{x^{(\infty)}_{1},\cdots,x^{(\infty)}_{k^{\sharp}}\right\}
. For each
\mu=1,\cdots,{\mu_{\max}}
, let
S(\mu):=\left\{j:x^{(\infty)}_{j}=z_{\mu},\,1\leq j\leq{k^{\sharp}}\right\}
. Therefore, for sufficiently large
\nu

(6.6)

\left|x^{(\nu)}_{j}-z_{\mu}\right|\leq\eta_{1}\text{ for all }j\in S(\mu),

and

(6.7)

\left|x^{(\nu)}_{j}-x^{(\nu)}_{j^{\prime}}\right|\geq\eta_{2}\text{ for }j\in S(\mu),\,j^{\prime}\in S(\mu^{\prime})\text{ with }\mu\neq\mu^{\prime},

where
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}>0
are chosen to be independent of
\nu
. For the rest of the proof, we use
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
, etc., to denote constants independent of
\nu
.

We now apply the hypothesis that
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
is Glaeser-stable.

Fix
\mu\in\left\{1,\cdots,{\mu_{\max}}\right\}
. We set
x_{0}=z_{\mu}
,
P_{0}\in\Gamma(x_{0},A_{0})
, and
\epsilon=1
in the definition of Glaeser refinement. Let
\nu
be sufficiently large. We set
x_{j}:=x^{(\nu)}_{j}
for
j\in S(\mu)
,
x_{j}:=z_{\mu}
for
j\notin S(\mu)
(
1\leq j\leq{k^{\sharp}}
). Since
\Gamma(x)
is its own Glaeser refinement, it follows from (
6.6
) that there exist

(6.8)

P^{(\nu)}_{j}\in\Gamma(x^{(\nu)}_{j})\text{, for }j\in S(\mu)

\nu
sufficiently large, such that

(6.9)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(P^{(\nu)}_{i}-P^{(\nu)}_{j})(x^{(\nu)}_{j})\right|\leq\left|x^{(\nu)}_{i}-x^{(\nu)}_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }i,j\in S(\mu),\left|\alpha\right|\leq m.

Furthermore, it follows from (
6.6
), (
6.8
), (
6.9
), and (
(2.3)
) (upon taking
\eta_{1}
sufficiently small) that

(6.10)

P^{(\nu)}_{j}\in\Gamma(x^{(\nu)}_{j},A_{1})\text{, for }j\in S(\mu).

We repeat the argument above for
\mu=1,\cdots,{\mu_{\max}}
. Thus, for sufficiently large
\nu
, we can find polynomials
P^{(\nu)}_{1},\cdots,P^{(\nu)}_{k^{\sharp}}
that satisfy the following:

(6.11)

P^{(\nu)}_{j}\in\Gamma(x^{(\nu)}_{j},A_{2})\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,{k^{\sharp}};\text{ and }

(6.12)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(P^{(\nu)}_{i}-P^{(\nu)}_{j})(x^{(\nu)}_{j})\right|\leq\left|x^{(\nu)}_{i}-x^{(\nu)}_{j}\right|^{{m-\left|\alpha\right|}}\text{ for }\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,\,i,j\in S(\mu),\,\mu=1,\cdots,{\mu_{\max}}.

Observe that by
(2.1)
, (

) implies

|\partial^{\alpha}P_{j}^{(\nu)}(x_{j}^{(\nu)}|\leq A_{2}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m.

Combining (

) and (

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(P^{(\nu)}_{i}-P^{(\nu)}_{j})(x^{(\nu)}_{j})\right|\leq A_{3}\left|x^{(\nu)}_{i}-x^{(\nu)}_{j}\right|^{{m-\left|\alpha\right|}}\text{ for }\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,\,i\in S(\mu),\,j\in S(\mu^{\prime}),\,\mu\neq\mu^{\prime}.

Together with (

\left|\partial^{\alpha}(P^{(\nu)}_{i}-P^{(\nu)}_{j})(x^{(\nu)}_{j})\right|\leq A_{4}\left|x^{(\nu)}_{i}-x^{(\nu)}_{j}\right|^{m-\left|\alpha\right|}\text{ for }\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,\,1\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}}.

For sufficiently large

, we have

and

, with

and

) (

). Then, (

)

The lemma is proved.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
r
-dimensional subspace,
w\in W
, and
w_{0},...,w_{r}
form a nondegenerate affine
r
-simplex containing
B(w,\delta)\cap W
for some
\delta>0
. Then, there exists
\eta>0
for which the following holds:

Let
W^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be another
r
-dimensional subspace. If
w_{0}^{\prime},...,w_{r}^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
satisfy

(6.16)

|w_{j}-w_{j}^{\prime}|<\eta\text{ for }j=0,1,\cdots,r,

and

satisfies

|w-w^{\prime}|<\eta,

is contained in the convex hull of
w_{0}^{\prime},...,w_{r}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since

. Let

be an orthonormal basis for

satisfy the following constrained linear system.

(6.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}(w_{i}\cdot\xi_{j})=w\cdot\xi_{j}\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,r;

(6.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}=1;

(6.20)

\displaystyle 0\leq\lambda_{i}\leq 1\text{ for }i=0,1,\cdots,r.

Note that the system (
6.18
)–(

) is nondegenerate, since

form a nondegenerate affine

-simplex.

Moreover, since

If

) implies that
w_{0}^{\prime},\cdots,w_{r}^{\prime}
also form a nondegenerate

-simplex in

(6.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}(w_{i}^{\prime}\cdot\xi_{j})=w^{\prime}\cdot\xi_{j}\text{ for }j=1,\cdots,r;

(6.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=1.

Note that

if and only if

satisfy the system (

) and (

) coupled with the constraint

By choosing

) and (

)–(
6.19

to be sufficiently close to to

for

In this section, we write

.

Definition 7.1
.

For
x_{0}\in E
,

, and

, we let
\Gamma(x_{0},k,A)
be the set of

, with

Note that by the Finiteness Lemma, there exists

such that

is nonempty for all
x\in E
and

.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
denote the ring of
(m-1)
-jets of functions at
x
and let

be the natural projection. We identify
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{x}
with the space

of polynomials with degree no greater than

\overline{\Gamma}(x,A):=\pi_{x}\Gamma(x,A).

7.1.
Strata

The following three lemmas will assist in defining the dimension of a Glaeser stable bundle.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
A\geq 2A^{\sharp}
,
x_{0}\in E
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

(1)

\dim\Gamma(x_{0})=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},A)=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},2A^{\sharp})
.

(2)

\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,A)=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,2A^{\sharp})
.

Proof.

Suppose the contrary to the first conclusion, that there exists
A>2A^{\sharp}
such that
\dim\Gamma(x_{0},A)>\dim\Gamma(x_{0},2A^{\sharp})=d
and pick
P\in\Gamma(x_{0},A)
such that
P
is not contained in the
d
-dimensional hyperplane in
\mathcal{P}
containing
\Gamma(x_{0},2A^{\sharp})
.

By Lemma
6.1
,
\Gamma(x_{0},A^{\sharp})
is nonempty, so take
P^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x_{0},A^{\sharp})
.

Now consider the sequence
P_{n}=(1/n)P^{\prime}+(1-1/n)P
. By convexity,
P_{n}\in\Gamma(x_{0},A)
for all
n
. But by supposition,
P_{n}\notin\Gamma(x_{0},2A^{\sharp})
for all
n
, contradicting (
(2.5)
). Thus,
\dim\Gamma(x_{0},A)=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},2A^{\sharp})
.
\dim\Gamma(x_{0})=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},2A^{\sharp})
follows by taking unions.

Now suppose the contrary to the second conclusion. Analogous to the previous situation, there exists
A>2A^{\sharp}
such that
\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,A)>\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,2A^{\sharp})=d^{\prime}
and pick
P\in\Gamma(x_{0},k,A)
such that
P
is not contained in the
d^{\prime}
-dimensional hyperplane in
\mathcal{P}
containing
\Gamma(x_{0},k,2A^{\sharp})
.

Let
P^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x_{0},k,A^{\sharp})
and again consider the sequence
P_{n}=(1/n)P^{\prime}+(1-1/n)P
. By our prior reasoning, there exists
N
such that
n\geq N
implies
P_{n}\in\Gamma(x_{0},3/2A^{\sharp})
.

Now, let
x_{1},...,x_{{\overline{k}}}\in E
. Choose
Q_{j}\in\Gamma(x_{j},1)
such that

(7.3)

|\partial^{\alpha}(Q_{i}-Q_{j})(x_{i})|\leq|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq k

with
Q_{0}=P
. Similarly, choose
Q_{j}^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x_{j},A)
such that

(7.4)

|\partial^{\alpha}(Q^{\prime}_{i}-Q^{\prime}_{j})(x_{i})|\leq A|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq k

with
Q_{0}^{\prime}=P^{\prime}
.

Form sequences
Q_{j}^{(n)}=(1/n)Q_{j}^{\prime}+(1-1/n)Q_{j}
. By (
(2.5)
) (and the independence of
\delta
of
x
in this condition), we may take
N
independent of
x_{1},...,x_{k}
such that
n\geq N
implies
Q_{j}^{(n)}\in\Gamma(x_{j},2A^{\sharp})
. Thus, for
n\in N
,
P^{\prime}_{n}\in\Gamma(x_{0},k,2A^{\sharp})
. This is a contradiction, so
\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,A)=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,2A^{\sharp})
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
A\geq 2A^{\sharp}
,
x_{0}\in E
, and
k\leq{k^{\sharp}}
. Then,

(7.5)

\dim\Gamma(x_{0},A)=\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,A).

Proof.

Suppose the contrary. Then by Lemma
7.1
,
\dim\Gamma(x_{0},k,A^{\prime})\leq\dim\Gamma(x_{0},A)-1
for all
A^{\prime}>A
. In particular, there exists
P\in\Gamma(x_{0},A)
such that for all
A^{\prime}>0

P\notin\Gamma(x_{0},k,A^{\prime})
.

Define the convex bundle
(\Gamma^{\prime}(x))_{x\in E}
, where

(7.6)

\Gamma^{\prime}(x,M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\Gamma(x,M)&\text{if }M\geq 0,x\neq x_{0}\\
\{P\}&\text{if }x=x_{0},M\geq A\\
\emptyset&\text{if }x=x_{0},M<A.\end{array}\right.

Observe that

By Lemma
6.1
and the fact

, there exists
\tilde{A}>0
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}(x_{0},k,\tilde{A})
is nonempty (in particular,

), where the

are defined analogously to the original

in place of

Since

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let

. Then,

∎

Definition 7.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set. Let

be a convex bundle over

. For each

, we define the

For given integers

We define the stratum

where

.

7.2.
Lowest stratum

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(H(x))_{x\in E}
be a convex, Glaeser stable bundle. Let
E_{1}
be the lowest stratum as in Definition
7.2
. Then
E_{1}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
x\in E
. Suppose
\dim H(x)=d
. Let
P_{1},\cdots,P_{d}
be the vertices of a nondegenerate affine
d
-simplex in
\text{int}H(x)
. Thus, small perturbation of
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{d}\}
remain the vertices of a nondegenerate affine
d
-simplex.

By the Glaeser stability of
(H(x))_{x\in E}
, for any
\tilde{x}\in E
sufficiently close to
x
, we may find
\tilde{P}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{P}_{d}\in H(\tilde{x})
, such that they are the vertices of a nondegenerate affine
d
-simplex. Hence,

\dim(H(\tilde{x}))\geq d\text{ for any $\tilde{x}$ sufficiently close to $x$.}

It follows that

\{x\in E:\dim H(x)<d\}

is a closed set for any
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In particular, for
k_{1}=\min_{y\in E}\dim H(y)
, the set

(7.9)

E(k_{1}):=\bigcup_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}E(k_{1},k_{2})\text{ is closed.}

By hypothesis of Theorem
1.6
again, we see that the map

x\mapsto W_{H}(x)

is continuous from
E(k_{1})
to
\mathcal{G}
, the Grassmannian of
k_{1}
-planes in
\mathcal{P}
.

Set
k_{2}=\max_{y\in E(k_{1})}\dim(\ker\pi_{y}\cap W_{H}(y))
, noting that
\dim(\ker\pi_{y}\cap W_{H}(y))=\dim(\ker\pi_{y}\cap H(y))
. By Definition
7.2
, we see that

E_{1}=\{x\in E(k_{1},k_{2}):\dim(\ker\pi_{x}\cap H(x))=k_{2}\}.

We will show that
E_{1}
is closed.

Let
(x_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
E_{1}
converging to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By (
7.9
), we know that
x\in E(k_{1})
, and thus,
W_{H}(x_{\nu})
converges to
W_{H}(x)
in
\mathcal{G}
. Passing onto a subsequence, we can further assume that
\ker\pi_{x_{\nu}}\cap W_{H}(x_{\nu})
converges to some (not necessarily unique)
W\in Conv(\mathcal{P})
with
\dim W=k_{2}
.

Now,
W\in H(x)
and
\pi_{x}|_{W}\equiv 0
. Therefore,

\dim(\ker\pi_{x}\cap W_{H}(x))\geq k_{2}.

By our choice of
k_{2}
, we must have

\dim(\ker\pi_{x}\cap W_{H}(x))=k_{2}.

Hence,
x\in E_{1}
as claimed.

Since
E
is compact and
E_{1}\subset E
is closed, the lemma follows.

∎

Recall from Definition
7.2
that
E_{1}
denotes the lowest stratum of
E
. By Lemma
7.4
,
E_{1}
is compact. Furthermore, for a convex bundle

,

7.3.
Glaeser Stability

Given a shape field
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0},x_{0}\in E,
and
A>0
, define

(7.10)

\Gamma^{o}(x,A):=\bigcup_{M<A}\Gamma(x,M).

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E}
be a regular, convex, Glaeser-stable, shape field and fix
A>0
. The bundle
(\Gamma^{o}(x,A))_{x\in E}
is Glaeser stable.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in E
and
P_{0}\in\Gamma^{o}(x,A)
. Then, there exists
M<A
such that
P_{0}\in\Gamma(x,M)
. By
(2.3)
, there exists
\eta>0
such that for any
x^{\prime}\in E\cap B(x,\eta)
, if
P^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x^{\prime})
with

(7.11)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(x)|,|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(x^{\prime})|\leq\eta|x-x^{\prime}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for all }|\alpha|\leq m,

then
P^{\prime}\in\Gamma(x^{\prime},\frac{M+A}{2})\subset\Gamma^{o}(x^{\prime},A)
.

Let
0<\epsilon<\eta
. By the Glaeser stability of
(\Gamma(x))_{x\in E}
, there exists
0<\delta<\eta
such that if
x_{1},...,x_{{k^{\sharp}}}\in E\cap(x,\delta)
, then there exist
P_{1},...,P_{{k^{\sharp}}}\in\mathcal{P}
such that

(7.12)

P_{j}\in\Gamma(x_{j})\text{ for }j=1,...,{k^{\sharp}}

and

(7.13)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{i}-P_{j})(x_{i})|<\eta|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq{k^{\sharp}}.

Since (
7.13
) implies (
7.11
), we have
P_{j}\in\Gamma^{o}(x_{j},A)
for
j=1,...,{k^{\sharp}}
, which is precisely what we wanted to show.

∎

In the sequel, we use
\Gamma(x,A)
to denote

, simplifying notation. Note that
\Gamma^{o}(x)
may not be a closed subset of
\mathcal{P}
, but since
\Gamma^{o}(x,A)\subset\Gamma(x,A)\subset\Gamma^{o}(x,A^{\prime})
for

, we see

(7.14)

and

will share most relevant properties with
(\Gamma(x,M))_{x\in E}
, in particular,
(C,\delta_{\text{max}})
-convexity and regularity.

More on Convex Sets

Lemma 8.1

Let

. By the fact that

, there exist

(

) such that

(8.1)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{i}-P_{j})(x_{j})|\leq A|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j,\leq{\overline{k}}.

The conclusion will follow from establishing

(8.2)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{0}^{\prime}-P_{j})(x_{0})|\leq A^{\prime}|x_{0}-x_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,1\leq j\leq{\overline{k}}

9.
Uniform delta and modulus of continuity

The purpose of this section is to show that, given
\epsilon>0
, we may take the
\delta
in the definition of Glaeser stable to be uniform in
x
and
P_{0}
(with some change in
{k^{\sharp}}
), then use this fact and the results of Section
8
to construct a modulus of continuity that will let us apply Theorem
5.2
.

Given
(x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime})\in E\times\mathcal{P}
, we let

(9.1)

\text{dist}\left((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime})\right):=|x-x^{\prime}|+\max_{\alpha}|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(x)|+\max_{\alpha}|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(x^{\prime})|.

Note that the above turns
E\times\mathcal{P}
into a metric space. While the above description of
\text{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
will be most useful, the metric defined by

(9.2)

\rho((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime})):=|x-x^{\prime}|+\max_{\alpha}|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(0)|.

will be useful for establishing a crucial property of
\text{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 9.1
.

The metrics
\text{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
are equivalent, that is, given compact
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
C>0
such that

(9.3)

C^{-1}\rho((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime}))\leq\text{dist}((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime}))\leq C\rho((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime}))

whenever
x,x^{\prime}\in E,P,P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
.

In particular, a set is compact in the
dist
metric if and only if it is closed and bounded in the
dist
metric.

Proof.

Write

(9.4)

P-P^{\prime}(y)=\sum_{\beta}c_{\beta}x^{\beta},

so that

(9.5)

\rho((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime}))=|x-x^{\prime}|+\max_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|

and

(9.6)

\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|\leq m\\
\beta\geq\alpha\end{subarray}}c_{\beta}\beta!y^{\beta-\alpha}.

Then, letting
M=\sup_{y\in E}|y|<\infty
,

\displaystyle\text{dist}((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=|x-x^{\prime}|+\max_{\alpha}|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(x)|+\max_{\alpha}|\partial^{\alpha}(P-P^{\prime})(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=|x-x^{\prime}|+\max_{\alpha}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|\leq m\\
\beta\geq\alpha\end{subarray}}c_{\beta}\beta!x^{\beta-\alpha}\right|+\max_{\alpha}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|\leq m\\
\beta\geq\alpha\end{subarray}}c_{\beta}\beta!(x^{\prime})^{\beta-\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|x-x^{\prime}|+2DM^{m}\max_{|\beta|\leq m}\beta!\max_{|\beta|\leq m}|c_{\beta}|

\displaystyle\leq\left[2DM^{m}\max_{|\beta|\leq m}\beta!\right]\rho((x,P),(x^{\prime},P^{\prime})).

The reverse direction may be proven analogously.

The metric
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
is by definition equivalent to a Euclidean metric on
E\times\mathcal{P}
identified in the natural way with
E\times\mathbb{R}^{D}
. By the above, so is the metric
\text{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
; thus, the Heine-Borel theorem applies, which is the form of our conclusion.
∎

Theorem
1.6
holds with
k^{\sharp}=2^{\dim\vec{P}}
in (
(1.3)
) and (
1.5
).

Given a holding space
H(x)
, we will now let
\tilde{H}(x)
denote its Glaeser refinement when
k^{\sharp}
is taken to be the value in (
(1.3)
) initially used to prove Theorem
1.5
. Call this value
k_{1}
. We will use
H^{\prime}(x)
to denote the Glaeser refinement when
k^{\sharp}
is taken to be
k_{0}:=2^{\dim\vec{P}}
. Theorem
11.1
is easily seen to be implied by the following proposition.

Proposition 11.2
.

Let
H(x)
be a holding space. Then
\tilde{H}(x)=H^{\prime}(x)
.

The containment
\tilde{H}(x)\subset H^{\prime}(x)
follows trivially from the definitions, as increasing
k^{\sharp}
increases the number of
y_{j}
near
x
for which we must find polynomials in
H(y_{j})
. Thus, the essence of the proof will be showing that
\tilde{H}(x)\supset H^{\prime}(x)
.

A key to proving Proposition
11.2
is the following result proven in
[
23
]
.

Theorem 11.3
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set of diameter at most 1. For each
x\in S
, let
\vec{G}(x)\subset\vec{\mathcal{P}}
be convex. Suppose that for every subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
with
\left|S\right|\leq 2^{\dim\vec{\mathcal{P}}}
, there exists
F^{S^{\prime}}\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\|F^{S^{\prime}}\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1
and
J_{x}F^{S^{\prime}}\in\vec{G}(x)
for all
x\in S^{\prime}
.

Then, there exists
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\|F\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\gamma
and
J_{x}F\in\vec{G}(x)
for all
x\in S
.

Here,
\gamma
depends only on
m,n,d
, and
|S|
.

A scalar-valued version of Theorem
11.3
was originally proven in
[
28
]
. However, we state the vector-valued version for full generality, as this is particularly useful for the selection problem.

Proof of Proposition
11.2
.

Pick
y_{0}\in E
and let
P_{0}\in H^{\prime}(y_{0})
. Replacing each
H(y)
with
H(y)-P_{0}
, we may assume
P_{0}
is the zero polynomial.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
M\geq 0
be a constant depending only on
m,n,d
and to be determined later. Choose
0<\delta<1/2
so that for any
y_{1},...,y_{k_{0}}\in B(y_{0},\delta)\cap E
there exist
P_{1},...,P_{k_{0}}\in\vec{P}
such that

(11.1)

P_{j}\in H(y_{j})

and

(11.2)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{i}-P_{j})(y_{j})|\leq(\epsilon/M)|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j\leq k_{0}.

Let
y_{1},...,y_{k_{1}}\in B(x,\delta)\cap E
and write
S=\{y_{0},y_{1},...,y_{k_{1}}\}
. For
y_{j}\in S
, define
\vec{G}(y_{j})
to be
H(y_{j})
if
j\geq 1
and
\{P\}
if
j=0
. Since
P\in H^{\prime}(y_{0})
, for any
S^{\prime}\subset S
satisfying
|S^{\prime}|=k_{0}+1
and
y_{0}\in S
, there exist polynomials
(P_{z})_{z\in S^{\prime}}
such that

(11.3)

|\partial^{\alpha}(P_{z}-P_{z^{\prime}})(z)|\leq(\epsilon/M)|z-z^{\prime}|^{m-\alpha}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,z,z^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}.

In particular, taking
z^{\prime}=y_{0}
in (
11.3
), we see that

(11.4)

|\partial^{\alpha}P_{z}(z)|\leq(\epsilon/M)\delta^{m-|\alpha|}\leq(\epsilon/M).

Therefore, by the classical Whitney extension theorem for finite sets (Lemma
6.5
), there exists
F_{S^{\prime}}
such that

(11.5)

J_{y_{j}}F=P_{j}\in\vec{G}(y_{j})\text{ for }y_{j}\in S^{\prime}

and

(11.6)

\|F_{S^{\prime}}\|_{C^{m}}\leq C(\epsilon/M),

where
C
depends only on
m,n,d
.

By adding
y_{0}
if necessary, we see that for
any

S^{\prime}\subset S
with
|S^{\prime}|=k_{0}+1
there exists
F_{S^{\prime}}
such that

(11.7)

J_{y_{j}}F_{S^{\prime}}=P_{j}\in\vec{G}(y_{j})\text{ for all }y_{j}\in S^{\prime}

and

(11.8)

\|F_{S^{\prime}}\|_{C^{m}}\leq C(\epsilon/M).

Taking
\gamma=\gamma(m,n,d,k_{1})
as in Theorem
11.3
, there exists
F
such that

(11.9)

J_{y_{j}}F\in\vec{G}(y_{j})\subset H(y_{j})

and

(11.10)

\|F\|_{C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\gamma C(\epsilon/M).

Setting
Q_{j}=J_{y_{j}}F
and
Q_{0}=P_{0}
, we see that for all
0\leq i,j\leq k_{1}
, there exist
Q_{1},...,Q_{k_{1}}\in\vec{P}
with

(11.11)

Q_{j}\in H(y_{j}),1\leq j\leq k_{1}

and

(11.12)

|\partial^{\alpha}(Q_{i}-Q_{j})(y_{j})|\leq\gamma C(\epsilon/M)|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m.

Picking
M=C\gamma
, we have

(11.13)

|\partial^{\alpha}(Q_{i}-Q_{j})(y_{j})|\leq\epsilon|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{m-|\alpha|}\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq m,0\leq i,j,\leq k_{1}.

Since
y_{1},...,y_{k_{1}}\in B(x,\delta)\cap E
were arbitrary,
Q_{0}=P_{0}\in\tilde{H}(y_{0})
. Thus,
\tilde{H}(x)\supset H^{\prime}(x)
.

∎

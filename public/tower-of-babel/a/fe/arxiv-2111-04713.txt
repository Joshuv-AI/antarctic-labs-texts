Quantum variance for holomorphic Hecke cusp forms on the vertical geodesic
Peter Zenz

Abstract
. We compute the quantum variance of holomorphic cusp forms on the vertical geodesic for smooth, compactly supported test functions. The variance is related to an averaged shifted-convolution problem that we evaluate asymptotically. We encounter an off-diagonal term that matches exactly with a certain diagonal term, a feature reminiscent of moments of L L -functions.



1.
Introduction

The
Quantum Unique Ergodicity
(QUE) conjecture, introduced by Rudnik and Sarnak
[
RS94
]
, is concerned about the equidistribution of Hecke eigenforms as the Laplace eigenvalue tends to infinity. More precisely, consider a smooth compactly supported function
\psi
on
X:=SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
and let
f(z)
be a
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke Maass cusp form with Laplace eigenvalue
\lambda
. Then QUE predicts that

\int_{X}\psi(z)|f(z)|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\to\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{X}\psi(z)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

as
\lambda\to\infty
. This conjecture is now a known theorem by the work of Lindenstrauss
[
Lin06
]
and Soundararajan
[
Sou10
]
. We can formulate an analogous conjecture for holomorphic Hecke cusp forms, where in the relation above we replace
f(z)
with
f(z)y^{k/2}
and the limit is the same, though this time it is
k
that tends to infinity. This problem was solved by Holowinsky and Soundararajan in
[
HS10
]
.

Going further, we can raise questions about the distribution of eigenforms in certain subsets of the fundamental domain. In
[
You16
]
, Young considers several problems in that direction, such as QUE restricted to geodesics, horocycles or shrinking discs. Among other results, he showed that Eisenstein series satisfy QUE restricted to the vertical geodesic. In this paper we study the distribution of holomorphic Hecke cusp forms on the vertical geodesic. In this case, Young puts forward the following QUE conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
You16
, Conjecture 1.1]

Suppose that
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth, compactly-supported function. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k}|f(iy)|^{2}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y},

where
f(z)
runs over weight
k
holomorphic Hecke cusp forms that are
L^{2}
-normalized.

Young provides evidence for Conjecture
1.1
by translating the problem to a (shifted) moment of
L
-functions and invoking the random matrix theory conjectures (
[
CFK+05
]
). Conjecture
1.1
is out of reach of current technology. As Young mentions, already a sharp upper bound
\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(iy)|^{2}y^{k}\frac{dy}{y}\ll k^{\varepsilon}
, would imply a subconvexity bound for
L(f,1/2)\ll C^{1/8+\varepsilon}
, where
C\asymp k^{2}
is the analytic conductor of
L(s,f)
. A bound of this strength is currently not even known for the simpler Riemann zeta function.

As a first step toward Conjecture
1.1
, it is therefore natural to ask whether equidistribution holds for
almost all
eigenforms, similar to the initial papers on equidistribution results for Hecke cusp forms by Luo and Sarnak (
[
LS95
]
and
[
LS03
]
). The equidistribution problem for almost all eigenforms is often termed
Quantum Ergodicity
(QE) in the literature. The Quantum Ergodicity problem restricted to certain hypersurfaces was studied in the case of Maass forms, or Laplacian eigenfunctions in general, by Christianson-Toth-Zelditch in
[
CTZ13
]
, Dyatlov-Zworski in
[
DZ13
]
and Toth-Zelditch in
[
TZ13
]
. We will show such a QE result for holomorphic Hecke cusp forms on the vertical geodesic. In fact, it will be a direct consequence of the stronger
quantum variance
result we will describe now:

Let
f
be a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of weight
k
for the full modular group
\Gamma=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
. We normalize
f
such that
||f||_{2}^{2}=\langle f,f\rangle=1
, where

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{X}f(z)\overline{g(z)}y^{k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

is the standard Petersson inner product for two holomorphic cusp forms
f,g
of weight
k
. For a smooth, compactly supported test function
\psi\colon\mathbb{R^{+}}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

(1.1)

\mu_{f}(\psi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(iy)|^{2}y^{k}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}(\psi)=\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y}.

To measure how far
\mu_{f}(\psi)
deviates from its expected value
\mathbb{E}(\psi)
we introduce the so-called
quantum variance
(for the vertical geodesic), which is (up to suitable normalization) given by

\sum_{K<k\leq 2K}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\big|\mu_{f}(\psi)-\mathbb{E}(\psi)\big|^{2},

where
H_{k}
is a basis of Hecke cusp forms. Note, that we will work with even weight functions, i.e.
\psi(y)=\psi(1/y)
, since we can decompose a test function into its even and odd part and the quantum variance for an odd function is identically
0
. In particular, we also have
\widetilde{\psi}(s)=\widetilde{\psi}(-s)
, where

\widetilde{\psi}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y^{-1})y^{s-1}dy.

The quantum variance problem was first investigated in a different setting by Zelditch in
[
Zel94
]
. Luo and Sarnak were the first to compute the asymptotic of the quantum variance for holomorphic Hecke cusp forms on the full fundamental domain (see
[
LS04
]
). Since then, several variants of this problems were explored. Zhao
[
Zha10
]
considered the problem for Hecke Mass cusp forms and Sarnak-Zhao
[
SZ19
]
further explored it for the modular surface. Closed geodesics on the modular surface were investigated my Luo, Rudnick and Sarnak in
[
LRS09
]
. In the compact setting of quaternion algebras Nelson
[
Nel16
]
,
[
Nel17
]
,
[
Nel19
]
evaluated the quantum variance using the theta correspondence. Further papers indicating the active field of research are for example given by work of Huang
[
Hua21
]
for the variance of Eisenstein series, Huang-Lester
[
HL20
]
for the variance of dihedral Maass cusp forms and the work of Nordentoft, Petridis and Risager
[
NPR21
]
on small scale equidistribution at infinity.

We explore for the first time the one-dimensional case of the vertical geodesic for holomorphic cusp forms and show

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
and
h
be smooth compactly-supported functions on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Moreover, suppose that
\psi_{i}(y)=\psi_{i}(1/y)
for
i=1,2
. Then

(1.2)

\sum_{k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)}h\Big(\frac{k-1}{K}\Big)\sum_{f\in H_{k}}L(1,\sym^{2}f)\Big(\mu_{f}(\psi_{1})-\mathbb{E}(\psi_{1})\Big)\cdot\Big(\mu_{f}(\psi_{2})-\mathbb{E}(\psi_{2})\Big)=V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})

with

\displaystyle V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=

\displaystyle K^{3/2}\log K\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{32}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du+

\displaystyle+K^{3/2}\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{64}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\log(u)du+

\displaystyle+K^{3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du\cdot\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{16}\Big(\frac{3}{2}\gamma-\log(4\pi)\Big)\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\Big)+

\displaystyle+K^{3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{8}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(-s_{2})\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2})\zeta(1+s_{2})ds_{2}+

\displaystyle+O_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(K^{5/4+\varepsilon})

as
K\to\infty
.

In the computation of quantum variances one usually chooses a test function
\psi
that is on average
0
, i.e.
\mathbb{E}(\psi)=0
. As a corollary of Theorem
1.2
we then get

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
and
h
be smooth compactly-supported functions on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Moreover, suppose that
\psi_{i}(y)=\psi_{i}(1/y)
and
\mathbb{E}(\psi_{i})=0
for
i=1,2
, then

(1.3)

\sum_{k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)}h\Big(\frac{k-1}{K}\Big)\sum_{f\in H_{k}}L(1,\sym^{2}f)\mu_{f}(\psi_{1})\mu_{f}(\psi_{2})=V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})

with

(1.4)

\displaystyle V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=

\displaystyle K^{3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{8}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(-s_{2})\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2})\zeta(1+s_{2})ds_{2}

\displaystyle+O_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(K^{5/4+\varepsilon}).

as
K\to\infty
.

As mentioned before, a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
is the following equidistribution result:

Corollary 1.4

(Quantum Ergodicity)
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of even weight
k
and
\psi
a real-valued, smooth, compactly-supported on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. For a fixed
\epsilon>0
we have

\#\big\{f\in\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K<k\leq 2K\\
k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}H_{k}:|\mu_{f}(\psi)-\mathbb{E}(\psi)|>K^{-1/4+\epsilon}\big\}=o(K^{2}),

i.e. almost all eigenforms
f\in\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K<k\leq 2K\\
k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}H_{k}
are equidistributed.

In subsequent work we will also address an application of Theorem
1.2
to count real zeros (for almost all) holomorphic Hecke cusp forms low in the fundamental domain (say with
y
in the interval
[1,2]
). Gosh and Sarnak
[
GS12
]
considered this problem for all Hecke cusp forms high in the fundamental domain (close to the cusp).

3.
Comparing the full fundamental domain with the vertical geodesic

We now highlight some of the differences between the variance computation for the full fundamental domain (denoted by
X
) and the vertical geodesic. To do so we revisit the work of Luo and Sarnak, who compute the quantum variance for the full fundamental domain in
[
LS04
]
. Set

\mu_{f}^{X}(\psi):=\int_{X}\psi(z)|f(z)|^{2}y^{k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\quad\mathbb{E}^{X}(\psi):=\int_{X}\psi(z)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}},

\psi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth, compactly supported function and
z=x+iy
. We further restrict our attention to test functions
\psi
that have mean value
0
, i.e.
\mathbb{E}^{X}(\psi)=0
. As in
[
LS04
]
we denote by

V^{X}(\psi)=\sum_{K<k\leq 2K}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\big|\mu_{f}^{X}(\psi)\big|^{2}

the quantum variance for the full fundamental domain. Computing the variance
V^{X}(\psi)
reduces to computing an averaged shifted convolution problem of the form

\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})=\sum_{K<k\leq 2K}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\Big|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell)h_{\psi}\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)\Big|^{2},

in the sense that an asymptotic (or bound) of
\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})
will lead to an asymptotic (or bound) of
V^{X}(\psi)
(see
[
LS04
, Theorem 2 and Section 4]
). In the definition of
\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})
the shift
\ell
is a fixed non-zero integer,
\lambda_{f}(n)
denotes the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue of
f
, and
h_{\psi}
is a smooth, compactly supported test function (that arises after decomposing
\psi
into Poincaré series) which forces
n
to be of size
k
.
We expect
\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})=O(K^{1+\epsilon})
, assuming square root cancellation in the shifted convolution problem. Indeed, Luo and Sarnak
[
LS04
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]
compute the asymptotic for
\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})
, and show that
\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})
and
V^{X}(\psi)
are of size
K
. More precisely, they obtain the asymptotic

\mathcal{M}^{X}(h_{\psi})\sim B(h_{\psi})K

with

\displaystyle B(h_{\psi})=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}\sum_{d_{1}|\ell_{1},d_{2}|\ell_{2};|\ell_{1}|/d_{1}=|\ell_{2}|/d_{2}=|\ell_{2}|/d_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{\psi}(d_{2}\eta)\overline{h_{\psi}}(d_{1}\eta)\frac{d\eta}{\eta^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\sum_{d_{1}|\ell_{1},d_{2}|\ell_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}\sum_{c\geq 1}\frac{S_{c,|\ell_{1}|/d_{1},\ell_{2}/d_{2}}}{c^{5/2}}e\Big(\frac{1}{2c}\frac{|\ell_{1}|}{d_{1}}\frac{|\ell_{2}|}{d_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sin\Big(-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{\pi}{2c}\Big(\frac{\ell_{1}}{d_{1}}\Big)^{2}\frac{\xi}{\eta}-\frac{\pi}{2c}\Big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{d_{2}}\Big)^{2}\frac{\eta}{\xi}+2\pi(d_{1}d_{2})^{2}\xi\eta c\Big)

\displaystyle\times\frac{h_{\psi}(d_{2}\xi)}{\sqrt{\xi}}\frac{h_{\psi}(d_{1}\eta)}{\sqrt{\eta}}\frac{d\xi d\eta}{\xi\eta},

where

(3.1)

S_{c,\ell_{1}/d_{1},\ell_{2}/d_{2}}:=\sum_{a,b\,({\rm mod}\,c)}S(a(a+\ell_{1}/d_{1}),b(b+\ell_{2}/d_{2});c)e_{c}\Big(2ab+\Big(\frac{\ell_{2}}{d_{2}}\Big)a+\Big(\frac{\ell_{1}}{d_{1}}\Big)b\Big)

and
S(m,m;c)
denotes the classical Kloosterman sum and
e_{c}(x)=e^{2\pi ix/c}
.

The quantum variance for the vertical geodesic (with
\mathbb{E}(\psi)=0
) is given by

V(\psi)=\sum_{K<k\leq 2K}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\big|\mu_{f}(\psi)\big|^{2}.

After a Fourier expansion of the Hecke cusp form
f
and opening the square we are facing a different shifted convolution problem:

\mathcal{M}(\psi)=\sum_{K<k\leq 2K}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\Big|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0<|\ell|\leq k^{1/2+\epsilon}}\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell)\psi\Big(\frac{n}{k}\Big)\Big|^{2}.

We notice an additional summation over the shifts
\ell
up to
\sqrt{k}
, compared to the full fundamental domain where shifts are fixed. Again, assuming square root cancellation in the summation over
n
and
\ell
leads us to believe that
\mathcal{M}(\psi)=O(K^{3/2})
.

Although, the additional summation over
\ell
makes the problem of computing the variance more difficult (as we need cancellation over the summation of
n
and
\ell
), it also gives rise to a surprising feature of the vertical geodesic, namely the aesthetically pleasing formula

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\psi)\sim

\displaystyle B(\psi)K^{3/2}

with

B(\psi)=\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{8}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\widetilde{\psi}(-s_{2})\widetilde{\psi}(s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2})\zeta(1+s_{2})ds_{2}.

In the following section we quickly sketch the proof of the asymptotic formula (
3.2
) (and hence Theorem
1.2
).

4.
High Level Sketch

As mentioned before, to compute the quantum variance it suffices to consider the shifted convolution problem

(4.1)

\mathcal{M}(\psi)=\sum_{K<k\leq 2K}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\Big|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0<|\ell|\leq k^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell)\psi\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)\Big|^{2}.

Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{M}(\psi)
is of size
K^{3/2}
. If we can show that
\mathcal{M}(\psi)=o(K^{2})
then we could deduce an equidistribution result, i.e. that
\mu_{f}(\psi)=\mathbb{E}(\psi)+o(1)
for almost all Hecke cusp forms in
\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K<k\leq 2K\\
k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}H_{k}
. Observe that the trivial bound for
\mathcal{M}(\psi)
is
K^{3+\epsilon}
, since the Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
are divisor bounded. On the other hand, assuming square root cancellation only in the summation over
n
yields the bound
\mathcal{M}(\psi)=O(K^{2+\epsilon})
. We therefore need to detect further cancellation over the shifts
\ell
to achieve our desired equidistribution result.

We now explain how to compute the asymptotic formula for
\mathcal{M}(\psi)
. First, we open the square in (
4.1
) and apply the Petersson trace formula. We obtain a diagonal term that is easy to evaluate and a more complicated off-diagonal expression involving Kloosterman sums. Let
\mathcal{OD}
denote this off-diagonal term that is roughly given by

(4.2)

\frac{1}{K}\sum_{0<|\ell_{1}|,|\ell_{2}|\leq K^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\sum_{K<n_{1},n_{2}\leq 2K}\sum_{c\ll K^{\varepsilon}}\frac{S(n_{1}(n_{1}+\ell_{1}),n_{2}(n_{2}+\ell_{2});c)}{\sqrt{c}}e_{c}(2\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+\ell_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+\ell_{2})}),

where
S(n,m;c)
represents the classical Kloosterman sum and
e_{c}(n)=e^{2\pi in/c}
. At this point, a trivial bound (using the Weil bound for Kloosterman sums) shows that
\mathcal{OD}=O(K^{2+\epsilon})
. In particular, we need to exploit a little bit more cancellation over the shifts to break the barrier for equidistribution.

As the summation over the variable
c
is short, it is instructive to consider the special case when
c=1
so that

\mathcal{OD}\approx\frac{1}{K}\sum_{0<|\ell_{1}|,|\ell_{2}|\leq K^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\sum_{K<n_{1},n_{2}\leq 2K}e_{1}(2\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+\ell_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+\ell_{2})}).

The difference to the full fundamental domain, as noted before, is given by the size of the shifts. When the shifts are fixed, the exponential
e_{c}(2\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+\ell_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+\ell_{2})})
is essentially smooth. For the vertical geodesic this is not the case and we expect cancellation when summing over the various variables
n_{i},\ell_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We will sum at first over the variable
n_{1}
and detect square root cancellation in

\sum_{n_{1}\sim K}e_{c}(2\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+\ell_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+\ell_{2})})

with a stationary phase argument. The new phase in the exponential collapses to zero and so there is no further cancellation to be found. This will show that
\mathcal{OD}=O(K^{3/2+\epsilon})
, as desired. To compute the exact asymptotic of
\mathcal{OD}
we use the long shifts
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
to our advantage. We split the various variables into residue classes modulo
c
, namely
n_{i}\equiv a_{i}\,({\rm mod}\,c)
and
\ell_{i}\equiv b_{i}\,({\rm mod}\,c)
for
i=1,2
. After the stationary phase argument from before we need to evaluate

(4.3)

S:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}S_{c}(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2});c)e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}).

The additional summations over the parameters
b_{1},b_{2}
arising from the long shifts
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
are crucial to show that
S=c^{3}\varphi(c)
. Here
\varphi(c)
denotes as usual Euler’s totient function. Let us compare quantity
S
with expression (
3.1
) for the full fundamental domain. Expression (
3.1
) does not simplify significantly, but is related to Salié sums instead. With the formula
S=c^{3}\varphi(c)
in hand and some complex contour integrals we can evaluate the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
asymptotically. The final computations remind us of computing moments of
L
-functions, in particular, the paper
[
Kha10
]
of Khan and the computation of the second moment of the symmetric square
L
-function therein. We also observe that the off-diagonal term agrees with a certain diagonal term, which enables us to write down the rather clean formula (
1.4
) for the quantum variance.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

5.1.
Setup

Recall from the introduction that
\psi
,
h
are smooth compactly-supported functions on
\mathbb{R^{+}}
and
H_{k}
denotes a basis of Hecke cusp forms of weight
k
. We want to compute the asymptotic of

V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\sum_{k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)}h\Big(\frac{k-1}{K}\Big)\sum_{f\in H_{k}}L(1,\sym^{2}f)\Big(\mu_{f}(\psi_{1})-\mathbb{E}(\psi_{1})\Big)\cdot\Big(\mu_{f}(\psi_{2})-\mathbb{E}(\psi_{2})\Big)

with

\mu_{f}(\psi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(iy)|^{2}y^{k/2}\psi(y)dy\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}(\psi)=\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y}.

Here the symmetric square L-function is given by

L(s,\sym^{2}f)=\zeta(2s)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n^{2})}{n^{s}},

for
{\rm Re}(s)>1
. The Fourier expansion of a normalized Hecke cusp form is given by

f(z)=a_{f}(1)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)(4\pi n)^{(k-1)/2}e(nz),

with
|a_{f}(1)|^{2}=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\Gamma(k)L(1,\sym^{2}f)}
arising from the normalization
||f||_{2}^{2}=1
.
Similar to Luo and Sarnak in
[
LS03
, p. 877]
we define the function
\widetilde{\psi}(s)
by

\widetilde{\psi}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y^{-1})y^{s-1}dy.

Then
\widetilde{\psi}
is entire, and for any integer
j>0
and any vertical strip
a\leq{\rm Re}(s)\leq b
, it satisfies

\widetilde{\psi}\ll_{a,b,j}(|s|+1)^{-j}
. Mellin inversion yields

\psi(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}\widetilde{\psi}(s)y^{s}ds,\quad\text{for }\sigma>0,y>0.

5.2.
Reduction to a Shifted Convolution Problem

To evaluate the variance
V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
we first use the Fourier expansion of
f(iy)
and write

|f(iy)|^{2}=\big|a_{f}(1)\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)(4\pi n)^{(k-1)/2}e^{-2\pi ny}\big|^{2}.

We then expand the square, seperating the terms with
m=n
from those with
m\neq n
. The terms with
m=n
agree up to a small error term (which we will call
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}
below) with the expected main term
\mathbb{E}(\psi)
. The terms with
m\neq n
lead to a shifted convolution problem and this quantity will be denoted by
S_{\psi}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\psi}:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)y^{k}|a_{f}(1)|^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}(4\pi n)^{k-1}e^{-4\pi ny}\frac{dy}{y}-\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\cdot{\frac{1}{2\pi i}}\int_{(1/2)}\widetilde{\psi}(s-1)\frac{\zeta(s)L(s,\sym^{2}f)}{(4\pi)^{s}\zeta(2s)}\frac{\Gamma(k+s-1)}{\Gamma(k)}ds.

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)y^{k}|a_{f}(1)|^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}(4\pi n)^{k-1}e^{-4\pi ny}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\Gamma(k)L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\widetilde{\psi}(s)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}(4\pi n)^{k-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k+s}e^{-4\pi ny}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\cdot{\frac{1}{2\pi i}}\int_{(2)}\widetilde{\psi}(s)\frac{\zeta(1+s)L(1+s,\sym^{2}f)}{(4\pi)^{1+s}\zeta(2(1+s))}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}ds

We used that we can write the Rankin-Selberg L-function in terms of the symmetric square as

L(s,f\otimes f)=\frac{\zeta(s)L(s,\sym^{2}f)}{\zeta(2s)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}}{n^{s}}.

We then shift the contour from
{\rm Re}(s)=2
to
{\rm Re}(s)=-1/2
and pick up a pole at
s=0
with residue

\frac{2\pi}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\cdot\widetilde{\psi}(0)\frac{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}{4\pi\zeta(2)}=\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)\frac{dy}{y}.

The lemma follows by making the change of variables
s\to s-1
for the new line integral.
∎

The term
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}
should be seen as an error term that is of size
K^{-1/2}
(compare for example with
[
LS03
, Section 5]
). The off-diagonal term on the other hand is given by a shifted convolution (which we denote by
S_{\psi}
) of size
K^{-1/4}
as the following lemma indicates:

Lemma 5.2
.

We have

(5.2)

\displaystyle S_{\psi}:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)y^{k}|a_{f}(1)|^{2}\sum_{n\neq m}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(m)(16\pi^{2}nm)^{(k-1)/2}e^{-2\pi(n+m)y}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{\ell\neq 0}\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell)}{\sqrt{n(n+\ell)}}\exp\Big(-\frac{k\ell^{2}}{2(2n+\ell)^{2}}\Big)\psi\Big(\frac{k}{2\pi(2n+\ell)}\Big)+O_{\psi}(k^{-1/2+\varepsilon}).

Remark 5.1
.

Since
\psi
is smooth compactly-supported on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we see that
(2n+\ell)\asymp k
. The exponential factor limits the size of
\ell
as otherwise we have rapid decay. It follows that
\ell\ll k^{1/2+\varepsilon}
and consequently
n\asymp k
. These observations show that the off-diagonal term is related to a shifted convolution problem of the form

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0<|\ell|\ll k^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\sum_{n\sim k}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell).

Assuming square root cancellation in
n
and the shifts
\ell
the expected size of
S_{\psi}
is
k^{-1/4+\varepsilon}
. The main part of the paper is attributed to showing this statement on average.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

First, we show by elementary means that only the terms satisfying
n+m\asymp k
contribute to the main term of
S_{\psi}
.
Since
\psi(y)
is compactly-supported, there exist real numbers
0<a<b
such that
\psi(y)
is supported in
[a,b]
. We write

L(y)=-2\pi(n+m)y+k\log y
and set
y_{0}=k/2\pi(n+m)
so that
L(y)
attains its maximum at
y_{0}
. Suppose that
y_{0}\leq a/2
. Then for all
y
in the support of
\psi
we have

L^{\prime}(y)=-2\pi(n+m)+k/y\leq-2\pi(n+m)+k/(2y_{0})\leq-\pi(n+m).

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)e^{L(y)}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{\infty}\psi(y)e^{L(y_{0})}e^{-(y-y_{0})\pi(n+m)}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\ll_{\psi}e^{L(y_{0})}e^{-(a-y_{0})\pi(m+n)}

\displaystyle\ll_{\psi}e^{L(y_{0})}e^{-a\pi(m+n)/2}

It follows that the contribution from
n,m
such that
k/(2\pi(n+m))\leq a/2
to
S_{\psi}
is bounded by

(5.3)

\frac{2\pi^{2}}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\\
(n+m)\geq k/(a\pi)\end{subarray}}\frac{d(n)d(m)}{\sqrt{nm}}\cdot\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{nm}}{n+m}\Big)^{k}e^{-a\pi(n+m)/2}.

Here we used the Deligne bound
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)
, where
d(n)
is the divisor function.
Expression (
5.3
) decays exponentially in
k
and is thus negligible.
Similarly, we treat the case when
y_{0}\geq 2b
. Note that
L^{\prime\prime}(y)=-k/y^{2}\leq-k/b^{2}
for every
y
in the support of
\psi
. Moreover,
(y-y_{0})^{2}\geq b^{2}
. We then have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)e^{L(y)}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{L(y_{0})-(y-y_{0})^{2}\frac{k}{b^{2}}}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\ll_{\psi}e^{L(y_{0})}e^{-k}.

The contribution from
n,m
such that
k/(2\pi(n+m))\geq 2b
to
S_{\psi}
is thus bounded by

(5.4)

\frac{2\pi^{2}}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\\
(n+m)\leq k/(4\pi b)\end{subarray}}\frac{d(n)d(m)}{\sqrt{nm}}\cdot\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{nm}}{n+m}\Big)^{k}e^{-k},

which decays exponentially in
k
.

Subsequently, we restrict our attention to the case
a/2\leq y_{0}\leq 2b
and in particular,
n+m\asymp k
.
We start by performing an inverse Mellin transform on
\psi
and evaluating the integral over
y
as a Gamma function:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)y^{k}|a_{f}(1)|^{2}\sum_{n\neq m}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(m)(16\pi^{2}nm)^{(k-1)/2}e^{-2\pi(n+m)y}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\Gamma(k)L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{n\neq m}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(m)(16\pi^{2}nm)^{(k-1)/2}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\widetilde{\psi}(s)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k+s}e^{-2\pi(n+m)y}\frac{dy}{y}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{n\neq m}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(m)(16\pi^{2}nm)^{(k-1)/2}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\widetilde{\psi}(s)\frac{1}{\big(2\pi(n+m)\big)^{k+s}}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}ds

Similar to
[
LS03
, Eq. 2.3]
Stirlings formula yields that for any vertical strip
0<a\leq{\rm Re}(s)\leq b
,

(5.5)

\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}=k^{s}\cdot(1+O_{a,b}((1+|s|)^{2}k^{-1})).

Using (
5.5
) we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle S_{\psi}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(m)}{\sqrt{nm}}\cdot\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{nm}}{n+m}\Big)^{k}\psi\Big(\frac{k}{2\pi(n+m)}\Big)

\displaystyle+O_{\psi}\Big(\frac{1}{k\cdot L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{d(n)d(m)}{\sqrt{nm}}\cdot\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{nm}}{n+m}\Big)^{k}\Big)

The factor
\big(\frac{2\sqrt{mn}}{n+m}\big)^{k}
is forcing
m
and
n
to be close (roughly
|m-n|\ll k^{1/2+\varepsilon}
). More precisely, note that

\displaystyle\frac{m+n}{2\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{(m+n)^{2}}{4mn}}\geq\sqrt{1+\frac{(m-n)^{2}}{4(m+n)^{2}}}

In particular,

\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{mn}}{n+m}\Big)^{k}\leq e^{-O(|m-n|^{2}/k)}

and the contribution from
m,n
with
|m-n|\geq k^{1/2+\varepsilon}
is exponentially small in
k
. When
|m-n|\ll k^{1/2+\varepsilon}
we have as in
[
BKY13
, p. 9]

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{mn}}{m+n}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{|m-n|^{2}}{2(m+n)^{2}}+O\Big(\frac{|m-n|^{4}}{(m+n)^{4}}\Big)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(k\log\Big(1-\frac{|m-n|^{2}}{2(m+n)^{2}}+O\Big(\frac{|m-n|^{4}}{(m+n)^{4}}\Big)\Big)

By a Taylor expansion it follows that

S_{\psi}=\frac{\pi}{2L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\exp\Big(-\frac{k|m-n|^{2}}{2(m+n)^{2}}\Big)\psi\Big(\frac{k}{2\pi(n+m)}\Big)+O_{\psi}(k^{-1/2+\varepsilon}).

The lemma follows upon writing
m=n+\ell
.
∎

Subsequently, we only consider the case when
m>n
and thus
\ell>0
as the case with
m<n
is exactly the same upon relabelling the variables.

5.3.
Cancellation in the Shifted Convolution Problem

Our goal now is to detect cancellation in the shifted convolution sum
S_{\psi}
(in an
L^{2}
sense, when averaged over
k
and
f\in H_{k}
). To do this we will use an averaged Petersson trace formula:

Lemma 5.3
.

[
ILS00
, Iwaniec, Luo, Sarnak, Lemma 10.1]

For any positive numbers
m,n
we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)}

\displaystyle 2h\Big(\frac{k-1}{K}\Big)\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k-1}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{h}(0)K1_{m=n}-\pi^{1/2}(mn)^{-1/4}K{\rm Im}\Big(e^{-2\pi i/8}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{S(m,n;c)}{\sqrt{c}}e_{c}(2\sqrt{mn})\hbar\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{mn}}\Big)\Big)+

\displaystyle+O\Big(\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{K^{4}}\cdot\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}v^{4}|\hat{h}(v)|dv+1_{m=n}\Big),

where
\hat{h}
denotes the Fourier transform of
h
and
\hbar(v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}e^{iuv}du
.

Remark 5.2
.

We kept the dependency on
h
explicit in the error term, as our weight function will depend on
n,m
and
K
. Similar computations are also done by Khan in
[
Kha10
]
(see for example Lemma 2.6 and expression (2.29) therein).

Remark 5.3
.

Integrating by parts several times shows that
\hbar(v)\ll_{A}v^{-A}
for any
A>0
. In particular, the second term on the right hand side of (
5.7
) is absorbed in the error term if
cK^{2}/\sqrt{mn}>K^{\varepsilon}
. In our case
mn
will be of size
K^{4}
and so this effectively restricts the range of
c
to
c\ll K^{\varepsilon}
.

5.4.
Variance Computation

Now we compute the main term of the variance
V(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
, which is given by the averaged shifted convolutioin problem

(5.8)

\mathcal{M}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\sum_{k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)}h\Big(\frac{k-1}{K}\Big)\sum_{f\in H_{k}}L(1,\sym^{2}f)S_{\psi_{1}}S_{\psi_{2}}

with

\displaystyle S_{\psi}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{\ell\neq 0}\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell)}{\sqrt{n(n+\ell)}}\exp\Big(\frac{-k\ell^{2}}{2(2n+\ell)}\Big)\psi\Big(\frac{k}{2\pi(2n+\ell)}\Big)+O_{\psi}(k^{-1/2+\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,d\\
d|\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n(n+\ell/d))}{\sqrt{d^{2}n(n+\ell/d)}}\exp\Big(\frac{-k\ell^{2}}{2(2nd+\ell)^{2}}\Big)\psi\Big(\frac{k}{2\pi(2nd+\ell)}\Big)+O_{\psi}(k^{-1/2+\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\sum_{d}\sum_{m}\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n(n+m))}{d\big(n(n+m)\big)^{1/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{km^{2}}{2(2n+m)^{2}}\Big)\psi\Big(\frac{k}{2\pi d(n+m)}\Big)+O_{\psi}(k^{-1/2+\varepsilon})

For the second equality we used the Hecke relations

\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+\ell)=\sum_{d|n,d|\ell}\lambda_{f}\Big(\frac{n(n+\ell)}{d^{2}}\Big)

and replaced
n
by
nd
. For the third equality we wrote
\ell_{i}/d_{i}=m_{i}
.
After expanding
S_{\psi_{1}},S_{\psi_{2}}
we see that the main term of
\mathcal{M}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
is equal to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\\
d_{1},d_{2}\\
m_{1},m_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{k\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,2)}h^{*}\Big(\frac{k-1}{K}\Big)\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k-1}\sum_{f\in H_{k}}\frac{1}{L(1,\sym^{2}f)}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1}))\lambda_{f}(n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2}))}{d_{1}d_{2}\big(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{1/2}}

with

\displaystyle h^{*}(t)

\displaystyle=h^{*}_{n_{1},n_{2},d_{1},d_{2},m_{1},m_{2},K}(t)

\displaystyle=h(t)\frac{tK}{8}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{tK}{2\pi d_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})}\Big)\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{tK}{2\pi d_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{tKm_{1}^{2}}{2(2n_{1}+m_{1})^{2}}-\frac{tKm_{2}^{2}}{2(2n_{2}+m_{2})^{2}}\Big).

We can now apply the averaged Petersson trace formula (Lemma
5.7
) so that
\mathcal{M}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{OD}
with the diagonal

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=K\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\\
d_{1},d_{2}\\
m_{1},m_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})=n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}{d_{1}d_{2}\big(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{1/2}}\cdot\widehat{h^{*}}(0)

and the off-diagonal

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=-\sqrt{\pi}K{\rm Im}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi i/8}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\\
d_{1},d_{2}\\
m_{1},m_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{S(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1}),n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2});c)}{\sqrt{c}}e_{c}(2\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})})\cdot

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}\big(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{3/4}}\cdot\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)\bigg),

where
\hbar^{*}(v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h^{*}(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}e^{iuv}du
.
Now that we have established a formula

(5.11)

\mathcal{M}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{OD},

we start by evaluating the diagonal term.

5.5.
Evaluating the Diagonal

(5.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}K^{2}}{16}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})\sqrt{u}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}u^{(-1/2+s_{2}/2)/2}K^{-1/2+s_{2}/2}(4\pi)^{-s_{2}}4^{1/2+s_{2}/2}

\displaystyle\cdot\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\Gamma(1/2+s_{2}/2)\zeta(1+s_{2})^{2}\frac{1}{2}duds_{2}

We follow up with the shift from
{\rm Re}(s_{2})=1
to
{\rm Re}(s_{2})=-1+\varepsilon
and pick up a double pole at
s_{2}=0
. The error term from the line at
{\rm Re}(s_{2})=-1+\varepsilon
is again
O_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(K^{1+\varepsilon})
. To compute the residue at the double pole we use the expansion

\zeta(1+s_{2})^{2}=\frac{1}{s_{2}^{2}}+\frac{2\gamma}{s}+\cdots
for
s_{2}
close to
0
, where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. The residue is then given by

\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}K^{3/2}}{16}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\cdot\lim_{s_{2}\to 0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big(s^{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{K\sqrt{u}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\Gamma(1/2+s_{1}/2)\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{s_{2}^{2}}+\frac{2\gamma}{s}+\cdots\Big)\Big)du

The limit is equal to

\displaystyle\lim_{s_{2}\to 0}\Big(\frac{K\sqrt{u}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}\cdot\Big(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\Big(\frac{K\sqrt{u}}{4\pi^{2}}\Big)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\Gamma(1/2+s_{2}/2)+

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\psi_{2}}^{\prime}(s_{2})\Gamma(1/2+s_{2}/2)+\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\Gamma^{\prime}(1/2+s_{2}/2)+2\gamma\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\Gamma(1/2+s_{2}/2)\Big)

We evaluate the limit, using
\Gamma^{\prime}(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}(-\gamma-\log 4)
, to

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\log K+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\log u+\sqrt{\pi}\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\Big(\frac{3}{2}\gamma-\log(4\pi)\Big)+\sqrt{\pi}\widetilde{\psi_{2}}^{\prime}(0)

Thus (
5.13
) is equal to

\displaystyle K^{3/2}

\displaystyle\log K\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{32}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle K^{3/2}\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{64}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\log(u)du+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle K^{3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du\cdot\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{16}\Big(\frac{3}{2}\gamma-\log(4\pi)\Big)\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(0)+\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{16}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(0)\widetilde{\psi_{2}}^{\prime}(0)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle O_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(K^{1+\varepsilon})

This is our main term. There is another term of size
K^{3/2}
coming from the residue of expression (
5.12
) at
s_{1}=-s_{2}
. This residue is given by

(5.14)

\displaystyle K^{3/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})u^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}du\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{16}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\widetilde{\psi}_{1}(-s_{2})\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\zeta(1-s_{2})\zeta(1+s_{2})ds_{2}

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.6.
Auxiliary Lemmas

In the following section we record some lemmas that we use to compute the off-diagonal term asymptotically. We start with some observations regarding the function
\hbar(v)
, appearing in the off-diagonal term. For any complex number
w
define the function

\hbar^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}u^{w/2}\cos(uv)du.

For
w=0
this is the real part of
\hbar(v)
. The Mellin transform of this function and its properties were evaluated by Khan
[
Kha10
, Lemma 3.5]
(and also Das-Khan
[
DK18
, sec. 2.6]
). Note that Khan and Das-Khan treat
\hbar_{w}(v)
but the observations also go through for the real part that we consider. As in
[
DK18
, sec. 2.6]
we have by repeated integration by parts the bound

(5.15)

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial v^{j}}\hbar^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(v)\ll(1+|w|)^{A}|v|^{-A}

for any non-negative integer
j,A
and the implied constant depending on
{\rm Re}(w),j,A
.
We denote the Mellin transform of
\hbar^{{\rm Re}}_{w}
by

\widetilde{\hbar}^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\hbar^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(v)v^{s}\frac{dv}{v}.

The bound (
5.15
) implies that the Mellin transform is absolutely convergent and holomorphic for
{\rm Re}(s)>0
. Integrating by parts several times and using again the bound (
5.15
) shows that the Mellin transform deacys rapidly. More precisiely we have

\widetilde{\hbar}^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(s)\ll(1+|w|)^{A+{\rm Re}(s)+1}(1+|s|)^{-A},

with the implied constants depending on
{\rm Re}(w)
and
A
.
By Mellin inversion we have for
c>0

(5.16)

\hbar^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(v)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\widetilde{\hbar}^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(s)v^{-s}ds.

As in
[
Kha10
, Lemma 3.5]
we can explicitly evaluate the Mellin transform of
\hbar^{\rm Re}_{w}
within the range
0<{\rm Re}(s)<1
. There we get

\widetilde{\hbar}^{{\rm Re}}_{w}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}u^{w/2}\Gamma(s)\cos(\pi s/2)du.

The next two lemmas will be useful to treat the exponential sum in the off-diagonal term.

Lemma 5.5

(Poisson summation)
.

Let
f
be a rapidly decaying, smooth function, then

\sum_{n\equiv a\,({\rm mod}\,c)}f(n)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n}\hat{f}\Big(\frac{n}{c}\Big)e_{c}(an),

where
\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e(-x\xi)dx
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the classical Poisson summation formula and noting that
n\equiv a\,({\rm mod}\,c)
is a shifted lattice of
\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

We detect cancellation in the off-diagonal term with the stationary phase method. We use the following version of Blomer, Khan and Young, which is a special case of their Proposition 8.2 in
[
BKY13
]

Lemma 5.6

(Stationary phase)
.

Let
X,Y,V,V_{1},Q>0
and
Z:=Q+X+Y+V_{1}+1
, and assume that

Y\geq Z^{3/20},\quad V_{1}\geq V\geq\frac{QZ^{1/40}}{Y^{1/2}}.

Suppose that
h
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}
with support on an interval
J
of length
V_{1}
, satisfying

h^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}XV^{-j}

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Suppose
f
is a smooth function on
J
such that there exists a unique point
t_{0}\in J
such that
f^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
, and furthermore

f^{\prime\prime}(t)\gg YQ^{-2},\quad f^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}YQ^{-j},\quad\text{ for }j\geq 1\text{ and }t\in J.

Then

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t)e^{2\pi if(t)}dt=e^{\sgn(f^{\prime\prime}(t_{0}))\cdot\pi i/4}\frac{e^{2\pi if(t_{0})}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})|}}h(t_{0})+O\Big(\frac{Q^{3/2}X}{Y^{3/2}}\cdot\big(V^{-2}+(Y^{2/3}/Q^{2})\big)\Big).

In particular, we also have the trivial bound

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t)e^{2\pi if(t)}dt\ll\frac{XQ}{\sqrt{Y}}+1.

Proof.

See Proposition 8.2 in
[
BKY13
]
, with
\delta=1/20
and
A
sufficiently large. We bounded the contribution of the non-leading terms in the asymptotic expansion of
[
BKY13
, Eq. 8.9]
) trivially by
O\Big(\frac{Q^{3/2}X}{Y^{3/2}}\cdot\big(V^{-2}+(Y^{2/3}/Q^{2})\big)\Big)
.
∎

The last lemma we need concerns the Kloosterman sum over arithmetic progressions. We have

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
S(a,b;c)
denote the classical Kloosterman sum, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2});c)e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})=c^{3}\varphi(c),

where
\varphi(c)
is Euler’s totient function.

Proof.

Let us call
\mathcal{T}
the sum we must calculate. First we open the Kloosterman sum and get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
(x,c)=1\end{subarray}}e_{c}\big(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1})\overline{x}+a_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2})x+2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}\big).

Here
x\overline{x}\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,c)
. Since
(x,c)=1
we can substitute
a_{1}
with
a_{1}x
and
b_{1}
with
b_{1}x
. We get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
(x,c)=1\end{subarray}}e_{c}\big(a_{1}^{2}x+a_{1}b_{1}x+a_{2}^{2}x+a_{2}b_{2}x+2a_{1}a_{2}x+a_{1}b_{2}x+a_{2}b_{1}x\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
(x,c)=1\end{subarray}}e_{c}\big((a_{1}+a_{2})^{2}x+(a_{1}+a_{2})(b_{1}+b_{2})x\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~c^{3}\varphi(c)

To obtain the last equality we used orthogonality when summing over
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, i.e.

\sum_{b\,({\rm mod}\,c)}e_{c}\big(b(a_{1}+a_{2})x\big)=\begin{cases}c&\text{ if }(a_{1}+a_{2})x\equiv 0\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and so
a_{1}\equiv-a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

We highlight here that the additional summation over
b_{1},b_{2}
(that originally stems from averaging over the shifts) is crucial here. In contrast to that, Luo and Sarnak (
[
LS04
, Appendix A.2]
) work for the full fundamental domain with fixed shifts. The corresponding summation over the Kloosterman sum then reduces “only” to an expression involving Salié sums. Lemma
5.7
might be seen as the reason why we can obtain a comparably clean formula for the quantum variance of the vertical geodesic.

5.7.
Evaluating the Off-Diagonal

Our goal is to obtain an asymptotic formula for the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
given by

(5.17)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\pi}{\rm Im}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi i/8}K\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}\sum_{c}\frac{S(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1}),n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2});c)}{\sqrt{c}}e_{c}(2n_{1}n_{2}+n_{1}m_{2}+n_{2}m_{1})\cdot

\displaystyle\times\frac{e_{c}\big(f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\big)}{d_{1}d_{2}\big(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{3/4}}\cdot\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)\bigg)

where

f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})=2\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}-2n_{1}n_{2}-n_{1}m_{2}-n_{2}m_{1}

and

(5.18)

\displaystyle\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(

\displaystyle\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)=

\displaystyle\frac{K}{8}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})\sqrt{u}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}K}{2\pi d_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})}\Big)\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}K}{2\pi d_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}\Big)\cdot

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{1}^{2}}{(2n_{1}+m_{1})^{2}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}^{2}}{(2n_{2}+m_{2})^{2}}\Big)e^{iu\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2}))^{1/2}}}du.

This task requires several intermediate steps.

First we will detect square-root cancellation in the exponential sum

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\asymp K}e_{c}\big(f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\big)

when
m_{1},m_{2}
are large. To see that this is possible, it is helpful to keep the critical ranges
m_{i}\asymp\sqrt{K}
and
n_{i}\asymp K
for
i=1,2
in mind. We then have the following heuristic that guides our further analysis:

f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})=-\frac{m_{1}^{2}n_{2}}{2n_{1}}-\frac{m_{2}^{2}n_{1}}{2n_{2}}+\ldots

\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{1}}f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\approx\frac{m_{1}^{2}n_{2}}{n_{1}^{2}}-\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{2n_{2}}\asymp O(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial n_{1}^{2}}f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\approx-\frac{3m_{1}^{2}n_{2}}{n_{1}^{3}}\asymp\frac{1}{K}.

From these bounds on the derivatives we can see that we expect square root cancellation in the summation over
n_{1}
(see
[
IK04
, Corollary 8.12]
). To make this heuristic precise and to compute an asymptotic formula for the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
, we will use the Poisson summation formula and the stationary phase method, which is the subject of the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{OD}
be defined as in (
5.17
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=-\sqrt{\pi}K{\rm Im}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi i/8}\sum_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2});c)}{c^{3/2}}e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\cdot

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\equiv b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
m_{2}\equiv b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)\bigg)

with

(5.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{e_{c}\big(f(x,n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})-v(x+a_{1})\big)}{d_{1}d_{2}\big(x(x+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{3/4}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{x(x+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)dx

Proof.

We work with expression (
5.17
) and split the variables
n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2}
into residue classes modulo
c
. The off-diagonal
OD
is then given by

(5.20)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\pi}K{\rm Im}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi i/8}\sum_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2});c)}{\sqrt{c}}e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\cdot

(5.21)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\equiv b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
m_{2}\equiv b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\equiv a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{e_{c}\big(f(n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\big)}{d_{1}d_{2}\big(n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{3/4}}\cdot

(5.22)

\displaystyle\times\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)\bigg).

Here we used the fact that the Kloosterman sum only depends on the residue classes modulo
c
. The lemma follows now upon applying the Poisson summation formula (see Lemma
5.5
) to the summation over
n_{1}
.
∎

Next we will analyze the quantity

(5.23)

\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)

with the stationary phase method.

We record here some restrictions on the variables that will be useful for the further analysis. Since
h,\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
we have that
n_{i}d_{i}\asymp K
for
i=1/2
. Additionally,
d_{i}\leq K^{\delta}
for some
\delta>0
, as noted in Remark
5.3
. Indeed, if
d_{i}>K^{\delta}
then
n_{i}\leq K^{1-\delta}
and
cK^{2}/\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}\geq K^{2\delta}
and the off-diagonal term can be absorbed in the error term. We will choose
\delta=1/32
for convenience. From the observations in Remark
5.3
we also have the condition
\frac{cK^{2}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\ll K^{\epsilon}
. The exponential functions ensure that
m_{i}\leq n_{i}/K^{1/2-\varepsilon}\leq K^{1/2+\varepsilon}
, as otherwise we have exponential decay. For technical purposes we also impose a lower bound on
m_{i}
. If both variables
m_{1},m_{2}
are small than the exponential
e_{c}(\sqrt{n_{1}(n_{1}+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})})
is essentially smooth and the analysis is similar as in the case of full fundamental domain (see
[
LS04
]
). If
m_{i}\leq K^{1/8}
for
i=1,2
, then we can bound
OD
trivially by
K^{5/4+\varepsilon}
, which is smaller than the expected main term of size
K^{3/2}
. In our stationary phase analysis we will only need one of the variables
m_{i}
to be large. Since
OD
is symmetric in
m_{i}/n_{i}
we can assume without loss of generality that
m_{1}\geq K^{1/8}
. The most important case is of course when both
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are of size
\sqrt{K}
and so one should view this restriction only as a technical convenience. To summarize, we will work subsequently under the following conditions:

(5.24)

n_{i}d_{i}\asymp K\quad\text{and}\quad d_{i}\leq K^{1/32}\quad\text{for }i=1,2,

(5.25)

K^{1/8}\leq m_{1}\leq K^{1/2+\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq m_{2}\leq K^{1/2+\varepsilon},

(5.26)

\frac{cK^{2}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\ll K^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and in particular }\quad c\ll K^{\varepsilon}.

A stationary phase analysis leads to the following lemma:

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)
be defined as in (
5.19
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{c}\Big(a_{1}v+\frac{m_{1}}{2}\big(v+m_{2}-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}\big)\Big)e^{-\pi i/4}\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2}))|}\cdot x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})^{3/2}\cdot n_{2}^{3/2}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi n_{2}x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle+O(K^{-2+\varepsilon})

with
x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})=\frac{m_{1}}{2}\Big(-1+\frac{v+m_{2}+2n_{2}}{\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}}\Big)
.

Proof.

We now need to check the conditions of the stationary phase Lemma
5.6
so that we can apply it to the quantity
\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)
. The stationary points
x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})
for fixed
v
are the solutions to the equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(f(x,n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})-v(x-a_{1})\big)=0

and are given by

(5.27)

x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})=\frac{m_{1}\big(m_{2}+2n_{2}+v-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}\big)}{2\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}}=\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}}\cdot\big(1+O(K^{-1/2+1/32+\varepsilon})\big).

From a Taylor expansion of
f(x,n_{1},m_{1},m_{2})
we see that

f(x,n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{n_{2}m_{1}^{2}}{x}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{xm_{2}^{2}}{n_{2}}+\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{8}\frac{m_{1}^{2}m_{2}}{x}-\frac{1}{8}\frac{m_{2}^{2}m_{1}}{n_{2}}+\ldots

and thus

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,n_{1},m_{1},m_{2})=\frac{1}{4}\frac{n_{2}m_{1}^{2}}{x^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{8}\frac{m_{1}^{2}m_{2}}{x^{2}}+\ldots\ll K^{\varepsilon}.

From the bound on the first derivative of
f
it follows also that
v\ll K^{\varepsilon}
.
The second derivate of the phase function
f
is given by

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}f(x,n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})=-\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}{(x(x+m_{1}))^{3}}}.

We note here that

(5.28)

\Big|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}f(x,n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\Big|\asymp\frac{m_{1}^{2}n_{2}}{x^{3}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Big|\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}f(x,n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\Big|\ll_{j}\frac{m_{1}^{2}n_{2}}{x^{1+j}}

for any integer
j\geq 2
.
If we evaluate the second derivative at the stationary point
x_{v}^{*}
we get

(5.29)

f^{\prime\prime}(x_{v}^{*})=-\frac{((v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2})^{3/2}}{2m_{1}n_{2}(m_{2}+n_{2})}=-\frac{((v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2})^{3/2}}{2m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\cdot(1+O(K^{-1/2+\epsilon})).

After a tedious computation we see that the exponential evaluated at the stationary point equals

e_{c}\big(f(x_{v}^{*},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})-v(x_{v}^{*}-a_{1})\big)=e_{c}\Big(a_{1}v+\frac{m_{1}}{2}\big(v+m_{2}-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}\big)\Big).

For the stationary phase analysis we also require bounds on the derivatives of the weight function
\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi(x(x+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2}))^{1/2}}\big)
, defined in (
5.18
).
Conditions (
5.24
), (
5.25
) and (
5.26
) yield

(5.30)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\big(x(x+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{3/4}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{x(x+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{x^{3/2}n_{2}^{3/2}}K\Big(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{Km_{1}^{2}}{x^{3}}+\frac{cK^{2}}{n_{2}x^{2}}\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{x^{3/2}n_{2}^{3/2}}K\Big(\frac{K^{\epsilon}}{x}\Big)^{j}

for any fixed integer
j\geq 0
.

We are now in the position to determine the required parameters
X,Y,V,V_{1},Q
and
Z
of Lemma
5.6
.
From the bounds on the derivatives (
5.28
) and the fact that
x\asymp K/d_{1}
we see that
Y=\frac{m_{1}^{2}n_{2}d_{1}}{cK}
and
Q=\frac{K}{d_{1}}
. From (
5.30
) we see that
X=d_{1}^{3/2}n_{2}^{-3/2}K^{-1/2}
,
V_{1}=V=\frac{K^{1-\varepsilon}}{d_{1}}
and
Z\ll K^{1+\varepsilon+1/32}
. With these choices we also see that the condition
V_{1}\geq\frac{QZ^{1/40}}{Y^{1/2}}
is satisfied, as long as
d_{2}\leq K^{1/32}
, say. Indeed, with the lower bound on
m_{1}\geq K^{1/8}
we have

\frac{QZ^{1/40}}{Y^{1/2}}\ll\frac{K\sqrt{cK}Z^{1/40}}{d_{1}m_{1}\sqrt{n_{2}}}\ll\frac{K}{d_{1}}\cdot\frac{K^{\varepsilon}\sqrt{d_{2}}K^{1/30}}{m_{1}}\ll\frac{K}{d_{1}}\cdot K^{1/64+1/30+\varepsilon-1/8}\ll VK^{-1/16}.

We can now apply the stationary phase lemma (Lemma
5.6
) and see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{c}\big(f(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2}),n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})-v(x_{v}^{*}-a_{1})\big)e^{-\pi i/4}\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2}))|}\cdot\big(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})\big)^{3/4}}

\displaystyle\times\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})+m_{1})n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}}\Big)+O(K^{-2}).

Since
x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})\asymp n_{1}
we can simplify the above result slightly by using a Taylor expansion and conditions (
5.24
), (
5.25
). We get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)=

\displaystyle\frac{e_{c}\Big(a_{1}v+\frac{m_{1}}{2}\big(v+m_{2}-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vn_{2}}\big)\Big)e^{-\pi i/4}\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}(x_{v}^{*}(n_{2}))|}\cdot x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})^{3/2}\cdot n_{2}^{3/2}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi n_{2}x_{v}^{*}(n_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle+O(K^{-2+\varepsilon}).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We expect the main term of (
5.23
) to come from the
0
-frequency, i.e. when
v=0
. We have

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)
be defined as in (
5.19
). Then

\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\mathcal{I}_{0}(n_{1},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)=e^{-\pi i/4}\sqrt{2c}\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\frac{1}{m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi n_{2}^{2}m_{1}}\Big)+O(K^{-1+\varepsilon}).

Proof.

This lemma is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.9
, specialized to the case
v=0
. For
v=0
the stationary point is given by
x_{0}^{*}(n_{2})=\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{m_{2}}
. From (
5.29
) we see that

\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}\big(x_{0}^{*}(n_{2})\big)|}=\frac{m_{2}^{3}}{2m_{1}n_{2}(n_{2}+m_{2})}=\frac{m_{2}^{3}}{2m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\cdot\Big(1+O(K^{-1/2+\varepsilon})\Big).

Moreover, a direct computation gives

e_{c}\big(f(x_{0}^{*}(n_{2}),n_{2},m_{1},m_{2})\big)=e_{c}(0)=1

and thus the claimed result.

∎

On the other hand, the contribution of the non-zero frequencies, i.e. when
v\neq 0
, is negligible. To show this we will need to detect further cancellation in the summation over
n_{2}
. We have

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)
be defined as in (
5.19
). Then

(5.31)

\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\sum_{v\neq 0}\mathcal{I}_{v}(n_{2},m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2},c)\ll\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{\sqrt{c}K^{1-\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

Let
ET
(for error term) denote the left-hand side of (
5.31
). First we use the Poisson summation formula for the summation over
n_{2}
. Together with Lemma
5.9
it follows that

(5.32)

\displaystyle ET=\frac{e^{-\pi i/4}}{c}\sum_{w}\sum_{v\neq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle e_{c}\Big(a_{1}v+\frac{m_{1}}{2}\big(v+m_{2}-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy}\big)-w(y-a_{2})\Big)

(5.33)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}\big(x_{v}^{*}(y))|}}\frac{1}{x_{v}^{*}(y)^{3/2}y^{3/2}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi x_{v}^{*}(y)y}\Big)dy+O(K^{-2+\varepsilon})

with

(5.34)

x_{v}^{*}(y)=\frac{m_{1}\big(m_{2}+2y+v-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy}\big)}{2\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy}}\asymp\frac{K}{d_{1}}.

As in Lemma (
5.9
) we perform a stationary phase analysis on the integral

\mathcal{J}_{w}(m_{1},m_{2},v):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e_{c}\big(g_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)-w(y-a_{2})\big)\frac{\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}\big(x_{v}^{*}(y))|}}\frac{1}{x_{v}^{*}(y)^{3/2}y^{3/2}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi x_{v}^{*}(y)y}\Big)dy

with

g_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)=a_{1}v+\frac{m_{1}}{2}\big(v+m_{2}-\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy}\big).

It is again useful to have the critical cases in mind when
y\sim K
and
m_{1},m_{2}\sim\sqrt{K}
. The first derivative
g^{\prime}_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)
is then again roughly bounded, while
g^{\prime\prime}_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)
is of size
1/K
. Consequently, we again expect square root cancellation in
n_{2}
(see for example
[
IK04
, Corollary 8.12]
). We now perform the stationary phase method on the integral over
y
of expression
ET
. The stationary points are given by the solutions to the equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big(g_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)-(w-a_{2})\Big)=-\frac{m_{1}v}{\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy}}-w=0.

Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}g_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)\ll K^{\varepsilon}
, we also see that
w\ll K^{\epsilon}
. Moreover, we have the following bounds on the higher derivatives:

(5.35)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}g_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)=\frac{4m_{1}v^{2}}{\big((v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy\big)^{3/2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial y^{j}}g_{m_{1},m_{2},v}(y)\ll_{j}\frac{m_{1}v^{j}}{\big((v+m_{2})^{2}+4vy\big)^{j-1/2}},

for
j\geq 3
.
From the computations in the proof of Lemma
5.9
, in particular equation (
5.27
) and (
5.29
) we have

\frac{\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{|f^{\prime\prime}\big(x_{v}^{*}(y)\big)|}}\frac{1}{x_{v}^{*}(y)^{3/2}y^{3/2}}=\frac{\sqrt{2c}}{m_{1}y^{2}}\cdot\big(1+O(K^{-1/2+1/32+\varepsilon})\big).

Similarly to (
5.30
) we will need bounds on the derivatives of the involved weight function with respect to
y
. It is useful to first compute

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial{y^{j}}}\big(x_{v}^{*}(y)\big)\ll_{j}\frac{m_{1}v^{j-1}(m_{2}^{2}+vy)}{(4vy+(m_{2}+v)^{2})^{1/2+j}}\ll_{j}\frac{K}{d_{1}}\Big(\frac{1}{y}\Big)^{j}.

Using the chain rule, a similar computation as in (
5.30
) yields

(5.36)

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial y^{j}}\frac{\sqrt{2c}}{m_{1}y^{2}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}}{8\pi x_{v}^{*}(y)\cdot y}\Big)\ll_{j}\frac{\sqrt{c}}{m_{1}y^{2}}\cdot K\Big(\frac{K^{\epsilon}}{y}\Big)^{j}\ll_{j}\frac{\sqrt{c}}{m_{1}}\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{K}\cdot\Big(\frac{d_{2}}{K^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big)^{j}.

We can now again establish the various required quantities for Lemma
5.6
. From the computation (
5.36
) we can see that
X=\sqrt{c}d_{2}^{2}/(m_{1}K)
and
V=K^{1-\varepsilon}/d_{2}
. From the bounds (
5.35
) we find
Y=m_{1}\cdot\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vK/d_{2}}
and
Q=((v+m_{2})^{2}+4vK/d_{2})/v
. The trivial bound of Lemma
5.6
yields

(5.37)

\mathcal{J}_{w}(m_{1},m_{2},v)\ll\frac{XQ}{\sqrt{Y}}\ll\frac{\sqrt{c}d_{2}^{2}}{vK}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vK/d_{2}}}{m_{1}}\bigg)^{3/2}\ll\frac{\sqrt{c}d_{2}^{2}}{vK}.

Here we used that
\frac{\sqrt{(v+m_{2})^{2}+4vK/d_{2}}}{m_{1}}\asymp 1
, which can be deduced for example from the size of the stationary point (see (
5.34
)).

From the bounds
w\ll K^{\varepsilon}
,
v\ll K^{\varepsilon}
and (
5.37
) it follows that

ET\ll\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{\sqrt{c}K^{1-\varepsilon}}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Using the previous lemmas we will obtain the following formula for the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
:

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\mathcal{OD}
be defined as in (
5.17
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=

\displaystyle\frac{-\sqrt{2\pi}K^{2}}{8}\sum_{c}\frac{\varphi(c)}{c}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\cdot

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})\sqrt{u}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}}{4\pi m_{1}d_{1}n_{2}}\Big)\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}K}{4\pi d_{2}n_{2}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}^{2}}{4n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\cos\Big(u\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)du

\displaystyle+O(K^{5/4})

Proof.

Combining Lemma
5.8
and Lemma
5.10
we obtain the main term of the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
given by

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2\pi}K{\rm Im}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{\pi i/2}\sum_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),n_{2}(n_{2}+b_{2});c)}{c}e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\cdot

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\equiv b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
m_{2}\equiv b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\bigg)

with

\displaystyle\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)=

\displaystyle\frac{K}{8}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})\sqrt{u}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}}{2\pi m_{1}d_{1}(2n_{2}+m_{2})}\Big)\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}K}{2\pi d_{2}(2n_{2}+m_{2})}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}^{2}}{(2n_{2}+m_{2})^{2}}\Big)e^{iu\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}}du.

On the other hand, by Lemma
5.8
and Lemma
5.11
, the error term is bounded by

\displaystyle K^{\varepsilon}\sum_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{|S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),n_{2}(n_{2}+b_{2});c)|}{c^{2}}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\equiv b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
m_{2}\equiv b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{2}}{d_{1}}\ll K^{5/4}.

We thus have

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=-\sqrt{2\pi}K{\rm Im}\bigg(

\displaystyle e^{\pi i/2}\sum_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),n_{2}(n_{2}+b_{2});c)}{c}e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\cdot

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\equiv b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\\
m_{2}\equiv b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\bigg)+O(K^{5/4}).

Note that
\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\ll K^{\varepsilon}
as otherwise we have rapid decay (using integration by parts). Additionally, we have the derivative bounds

(5.38)

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial n_{2}^{j}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\ll K\cdot\Big(\frac{K^{\epsilon}}{n_{2}}\Big)^{j}\ll K\cdot K^{-j/2},

(5.39)

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial m_{1}^{j}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\ll K\cdot\Big(\frac{K^{\epsilon}}{m_{1}}\Big)^{j}\ll K\cdot K^{-j/16}

and

(5.40)

\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial m_{2}^{j}}\hbar^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\ll K\cdot\Big(\frac{K}{m_{1}d_{1}n_{2}}+\frac{Km_{2}}{n_{2}^{2}}+\frac{cK^{2}}{m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{j}\ll K\cdot K^{-j/16}.

With the Poisson summation formula we see that

\sum_{n_{2}\equiv a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar^{*}_{d_{1},d_{2}}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{u}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}x^{2}}\hbar^{*}_{d_{1},d_{2}}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}x^{2}}\Big)e_{c}\big(-u(x-a_{2})\big)dx.

Repeated integration by parts and the bounds (
5.38
) show that the non-zero frequencies are bounded by
O_{j}(K^{-10})
. For the
0
-frequency we have

\frac{1}{c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}x^{2}}\hbar^{*}_{d_{1},d_{2}}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}x^{2}}\Big)dx=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar^{*}_{d_{1},d_{2}}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n^{2}}\Big)+O_{j}(K^{-10})

again by the Poisson summation formula. We proceed in the same way for the summation over
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, using the derivative bounds (
5.39
), (
5.40
) respectively, to bound the non-zero frequencies. It follows that the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
is up to a negligible error term given by

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2\pi}K{\rm Im}\bigg(e^{-\pi i/2}

\displaystyle\sum_{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
a_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1}\,({\rm mod}\,c),\\
b_{2}\,({\rm mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}\frac{S(a_{1}(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2});c)}{c^{4}}e_{c}(2a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\cdot

\displaystyle\times\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\bigg).

We now use Lemma
5.7
to simplify the summation of the Kloosterman sum over arithmetic progressions and see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=\frac{-\sqrt{2\pi}K^{2}}{8}{\rm Im}\bigg(e^{-\pi i/2}\sum_{c}\frac{\varphi(c)}{c}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\bigg)+O(K^{5/4})

By a Taylor expansion, using (
5.24
) and (
5.25
), we see that

\displaystyle\hbar_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{*}\Big(\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})\sqrt{u}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}}{4\pi m_{1}d_{1}n_{2}}\Big)\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}K}{4\pi d_{2}n_{2}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}^{2}}{4n_{2}^{2}}\Big)e^{iu\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}}du

\displaystyle+O(K^{1/2+\varepsilon}),

so that the lemma follows upon evaluating the imaginary part of
\mathcal{OD}
.
∎

Finally, we are in the position to evaluate the off-diagonal
\mathcal{OD}
asymptotically. To do so we will relate
\mathcal{OD}
to a complex contour integral over several variables. We then evaluate this contour integral with the residue theorem.

.

Let
\mathcal{OD}
be given by expression (

From Lemma
5.12
we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=

\displaystyle\frac{-\sqrt{2\pi}K^{2}}{8}\sum_{c}\frac{\varphi(c)}{c}\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}}\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\cdot

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})\sqrt{u}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}}{4\pi m_{1}d_{1}n_{2}}\Big)\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{u}K}{4\pi d_{2}n_{2}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{u}Km_{2}^{2}}{4n_{2}^{2}}\Big)\cos\Big(u\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)du.

To evaluate this expression asymptotically we perform an inverse Mellin transform on
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
and the exponential function.
Then
\mathcal{OD}
is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}K^{2}}{8}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{3}}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}\int_{(2)}\int_{(1+\varepsilon)}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}(s_{1})\widetilde{\psi_{2}}(s_{2})\Gamma(s_{3})\Big(\frac{Km_{2}}{4\pi m_{1}d_{1}n_{2}}\Big)^{s_{1}}\Big(\frac{K}{4\pi d_{2}n_{2}}\Big)^{s_{2}}\Big(\frac{4n_{2}^{2}}{Km_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{s_{3}}\cdot

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}u^{(1+s_{1}+s_{2}-s_{3})/2}\cos\Big(u\frac{cK^{2}m_{2}}{8\pi m_{1}n_{2}^{2}}\Big)duds_{1}ds_{2}ds_{3}

Finally, we also perform an inverse Mellin transform on

\hbar_{1+s_{1}+s_{2}-s_{3}}^{{\rm Re}}(v):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h(\sqrt{u})}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}u^{(1+s_{1}+s_{2}-s_{3})/2}\cos(uv)du

as indicated in equation (
5.16

\displaystyle\mathcal{OD}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}K^{2}}{8}

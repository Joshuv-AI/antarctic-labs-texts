On twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity and finite generation of generalized canonical rings
Yoshinori Gongyo, Shigeharu Takayama

Abstract
. We give a twisted version of the Kawamata semi-positivity theorem by the ℚ \mathbb{Q} -line bundle with a vanishing Lelong number at every point. Moreover, we apply the result to the finite generation problem for canonical rings of Birkar’s generalized pairs.

14D06, 14E30, 32J25

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over
\mathbb{C}
, the complex number field.

In this paper, we prove the following theorem, which we call ”twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem”:

Theorem 1.1

(Twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a surjective morphism of projective varieties with connected fibers.
Suppose
X
is quasi-smooth and
Y
is smooth.
Let
P=\sum_{j}P_{j}
and
Q=\sum_{l}Q_{l}
be reduced effective divisors on
X
and
Y
, respectively,
such that
f^{-1}(Q)\subseteq P
. Suppose that
f
is a smooth morphism over
Y\setminus\mathrm{Supp}\,Q

Let
M
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor on
X
, which, as a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle, admits a singular Hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature and whose local weight functions have vanishing Lelong numbers at any points in
X
(see
2.2.4
for the definition).
In particular,
M
is nef.

Let
D=\sum_{j}d_{j}P_{j}
be
a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor (
d_{j}
’s may
be negative or zero), which satisfies
the following conditions (1)–(4):

(1)

f:(X,P)\to(Y,Q)
is a well prepared model (
f:(X,P)\to(Y,Q)
is toroidal and
f
is equi-dimensional, in fact
f
is flat),

(2)

d_{j}<1
,

(3)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}(\eta)}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ulcorner-D\urcorner)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathbb{C}(\eta)=1
,
where
\eta
is the generic point of
Y
, and

(4)

K_{X}+D+M\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}(K_{Y}+L)

for some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L
on
Y
.

Let
(5)

\displaystyle f^{*}Q_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}w_{lj}P_{j},\ \text{where}\ w_{lj}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{j}+w_{lj}-1}{w_{lj}}(\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{<1})\ \text{if}\ f(P_{j})=Q_{l},

\displaystyle\delta_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{max}\{\bar{d_{j}}|f(P_{j})=Q_{l}\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\delta_{l}Q_{l},\ \text{and}

\displaystyle M_{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L-\Delta_{Y}.

By construction, the pair
(Y,{\Delta}_{Y})
is sub-klt and
K_{X}+D+M\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}(K_{Y}+{\Delta}_{Y}+M_{Y})
.
We sometimes call
M_{Y}
(resp.
\Delta_{Y}
)
the moduli part (resp. discriminant
part).

Then, the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
M_{Y}
admits, as a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundle, a singular Hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature and whose local weight functions have vanishing Lelong numbers at any points in
Y
.
In particular,
M_{Y}
is nef.

Remark 1.2
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the toroidal conditions and the smoothness of
f
over
Y\setminus\mathrm{Supp}\,Q
imply that
\mathrm{Supp}\,D^{h}
is relative normal crossing over
Y\setminus\mathrm{Supp}\,Q
since the torus invariant boundaries of smooth toric varieties are simple normal crossings.

The property (3) is, in fact, to say that
f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ulcorner{-D}\urcorner)
is a line bundle. When

and

is smooth, the above theorem follows from the traditional Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem in
[
K98
]
. Compared with
[
K98
]
, we add the extra assumption (1). The condition is required by analysis of the singularities of the metric on

. However, applying the toroidalization theorem in
[
AK
]
, we can always assume (1) with the birational modification. Then, we need to generalize the theorem for quasi-smooth and toroidal. Therefore, we state the above situation. The proof follows from the idea of the second author’s paper
[
Ta
]
. However, by the contribution of

[

]
.

Next, using twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem
1.1
, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3

(=Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective generalized klt pair such that a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
M^{\prime}
on a smooth higher birational model

has a semi-positive singular Hermitian metric

with a vanishing Lelong number for the potential at every point of

Then the ring

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,ml(K_{X}+B+M))

is a finite generated

We collect the notion of generalized pairs by Birkar in Section

since we perturb

Finally, we note the interesting difference between the usual and generalized pair in the Kawamata semi-positivity, the canonical bundle formula, and the finite generation.
For generalized pairs, we do not know the functorial behavior of the moduli part of

For the case of (generalized) log Calabi–Yau fibrations, it is known by
[

.
However, in general, it is unknown. Fortunately, this does not matter for our application. Moreover, one may ask the case of log canonical in Theorem
1.3

2.2.
Analytic preliminary

We recall a few basic facts on the Lelong number, which measures the “analytic” singularities of plurisubharmonic (psh for short) functions.
We refer to
[
D12
, 2.B, 5.B]
for some properties of psh functions related to algebraic geometry.
We also refer to
[
D12
]
for a general theory of singular Hermitian metrics, multiplier ideal sheaves, and so on.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a (quasi-)psh function on an open set
B
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The
Lelong number

\nu(\varphi,x)
of
\varphi
at
x\in B
is defined by

\nu(\varphi,x)=\liminf_{z\to x}\frac{\varphi(z)}{\mathrm{log}|z-x|}.

The Lelong number does not depend on the choice of local coordinates and can, hence, be defined on complex manifolds.

A singular Hermitian metric
h
on a line bundle
L
can be written as
h=e^{-{\varphi}}
with
{\varphi}\in L^{1}_{loc}(U,{\mathbb{R}})
on every local trivialization
L|_{U}\cong U\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Every such
{\varphi}
is said to be a weight function of
h
.
If the curvature current of
h
is semi-positive, then
{\varphi}
is psh on
U
.
The curvature positivity of
h
relates to positivity notions in algebraic geometry.
The following result is particularly related to this paper, which is derived from Nadel’s vanishing theorem (
[
FF
, Proposition 3.5]
).
See also
[
D92
, Proposition 3.7]
for a stronger statement.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

A line bundle on a smooth projective variety admitting a singular Hermitian metric
h
with semi-positive curvature and whose local weight functions have vanishing Lelong numbers everywhere is nef. Namely, the intersection number with any curve is semi-positive.

The following is a particular case of
[
Fa
, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
f:U\to V
be a surjective holomorphic map of complex manifolds, and let
{\varphi}
be a psh function on
V
.
Let
p\in U
.
Then,
\nu({\varphi},f(p))=0
if and only if
\nu(f^{*}{\varphi},p)=0
.

Although we do not try to define the Lelong number on singular spaces, we can introduce the zero Lelong number property.
Refer to
[
Pe
, §1]
for psh functions on possibly singular complex spaces.

Definition 2.2.4
.

Let
V
be a normal complex space, and let
{\varphi}
be a psh function on
V
.
Let
q\in V
.
The function
{\varphi}
has zero (or vanishing) Lelong number at
q
, if there exist a complex manifold
U
, a surjective holomorphic map
f:U\to V
and a point
p\in f^{-1}(q)
such that
\nu(f^{*}{\varphi},p)=0
.

This property is preserved by an arbitrary pull-back.

Lemma 2.2.5
.

Let
V
be a normal complex space, and let
{\varphi}
be a psh function on
V
.
Let
q\in V
.
Suppose
{\varphi}
has zero Lelong number at
q
.
Let
g:W\to V
be a surjective holomorphic map from a normal complex space
W
and take a point
r\in g^{-1}(q)
.
Then,
g^{*}{\varphi}
has zero Lelong number at
r
.
In particular, if
W
is smooth,
\nu(g^{*}{\varphi},r)=0
.

Proof.

Let
f:U\to V
and
p\in f^{-1}(q)
be as in
2.2.4
such that
\nu(f^{*}{\varphi},p)=0
.
(We can restrict everything over a relatively compact open neighborhood of
q
.)
By taking the fiber product
W\times_{V}U
and a resolution of singularities of it, we can find a complex manifold
X
with surjective holomorphic maps
f^{\prime}:X\to W
and
g^{\prime}:X\to U
such that
g\circ f^{\prime}=f\circ g^{\prime}
.
We can also take a point
x\in X
such that
f^{\prime}(x)=r
and
g^{\prime}(x)=p
.
By
2.2.3
, the property
\nu(f^{*}{\varphi},p)=0
is equivalent to
\nu((g\circ f^{\prime})^{*}{\varphi},x)=0
, which is saying that
g^{*}{\varphi}
has zero Lelong number at
r
.
∎

We will use the following criterion to check the vanishing of a Lelong number.

Lemma 2.2.6
.

Let
U
be a unit ball in
({\mathbb{C}}^{n},z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}))
,
and let
{\varphi}
and
u
be quasi-psh functions on
U
with
\nu(u,0)=n
.
Let
k\in(0,2]
.
Suppose
e^{-{\varphi}}e^{-cu}\in L^{1}_{loc}(U)
for any
c\in(0,k)
.
Then
\nu({\varphi},0)\leq(2-k)n
.
For example, we can conclude
\nu({\varphi},0)=0
, if we can take
k=2
and
u=\log|\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}|
.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
\nu({\varphi},0)>(2-k)n
.
We let
a>0
so that
\nu({\varphi},0)=(2-k)n+a
, and take
b>0
such that
b<\min\{k,a/n\}
.
Then
{\varphi}+(k-b)u
is quasi-psh on
U

\nu({\varphi}+(k-b)u,0)\geq\nu({\varphi},0)+(k-b)\nu(u,0)=2n+a-bn>2n
.
Then
e^{-{\varphi}}e^{-(k-b)u}\not\in L^{1}_{loc}(U)
(
[
D12
, 5.6]
).
This is a contradiction.
∎

Psh functions with vanishing Lelong numbers do not affect multiplier ideals.

Lemma 2.2.7
.

Let
U
be a unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
and let
{\varphi}
and
u
be quasi-psh functions on
U
.
Suppose
\nu({\varphi},0)=0
.
Then
{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-{\varphi}}e^{-u})={\mathcal{I}}(e^{-u})
on a neighborhood of
0
.

Here,
{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-u})\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
is a multiplier ideal sheaf.
It is the sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions

which is
L^{2}
with respect to
e^{-u}dv
, where
dv
is the standard Lebesgue measure on
U
, namely
|f|^{2}e^{-u}\in L^{1}_{loc}(U)
.
The multiplier ideal sheaf is coherent if
u
is quasi-psh (
[
D12
, 5.7]
).

Proof.

{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-{\varphi}}e^{-u})\subset{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-u})
is clear.
We take
f\in{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-u})_{0}
.
We can take a relatively compact open neighborhood
V
of
0
in
U
such that
\int_{V}|f|^{2}e^{-u}<\infty
.
Here, we take the Lebesgue measure as a volume form on
U
.
We take
p,q\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>1}
with
1/p+1/q=1
such that
{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-qu})={\mathcal{I}}(e^{-u})
on
V
.
The last property is the so-called strong openness property of multiplier ideals
[
GZ
]
(see also
[
Be
]
).
As
\nu(p{\varphi},0)=0
, we can take a neighborhood
W\subset V
of
0
such that
e^{-p{\varphi}}\in L^{1}(W)
by Skoda’s lemma (
[
D12
, 5.6]
).
Then, by Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{W}|f|^{2}e^{-{\varphi}}e^{-u}\leq\Big(\int_{W}|f|^{2}e^{-p{\varphi}}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{W}|f|^{2}e^{-qu}\Big)^{1/q}.

Thus, we have
f\in{\mathcal{I}}(e^{-{\varphi}}e^{-u})_{0}
.
If
u
has only analytic singularities, the openness property is an elementary property.
∎

3.1.
Basic estimate: Smooth total space

Let everything be as in
1.1
, and suppose
X
is smooth.
We let
\dim X=n+m
and
\dim Y=m
.
We explain a key object: a fiber integral, which will appear repeatedly in this paper (and can be formulated in various other situations).

Let us recall a convention, which is commonly used in complex algebraic geometry.
Let
{\Delta}
be an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor, say on
X
in general.
We take a positive integer
k
such that
k{\Delta}
is a divisor with integral coefficients, a section
s_{k{\Delta}}\in H^{0}(X,k{\Delta})
whose divisor is
k{\Delta}
, and a Hermitian metric
h_{k{\Delta}}
on the line bundle
k{\Delta}
.
Then,
s_{{\Delta}}:=(s_{k{\Delta}})^{1/k}
is a root of section (or a multi-valued holomorphic section), and
h_{{\Delta}}:=(h_{k{\Delta}})^{1/k}
is a Hermitian metric on the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundle
{\Delta}
in a somewhat formal sense.
But,
|s_{{\Delta}}|_{h_{{\Delta}}}:=(|s_{k{\Delta}}|_{h_{k{\Delta}}})^{1/k}\in C^{0}(X,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})
is a function in the usual sense.

3.1.1
.

We let

E=\ulcorner{D_{-}}\urcorner-K_{X/Y},

which is a line bundle on
X
.
We take any Hermitian metric
h
on
E
.
We take a root of section
s_{{\Delta}_{D}}
whose divisor is
{\Delta}_{D}
, and take a Hermitian metric
h_{{\Delta}_{D}}
on the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundle
{\Delta}_{D}
.
Let
{\varphi}\in L^{1}_{loc}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
be a quasi-psh function.
We finally let

g=h\ \frac{e^{-{\varphi}}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|_{h_{{\Delta}_{D}}}^{2}}

be a singular Hermitian metric on
E
.
We take a section

{\sigma}=s_{\ulcorner{D_{-}}\urcorner}\in H^{0}(X,\ulcorner{D_{-}}\urcorner)=H^{0}(X,K_{X/Y}+E)

whose divisor is
\ulcorner{D_{-}}\urcorner=\ulcorner{-D}\urcorner
.
We regard
{\sigma}
as an
E
-valued relative holomorphic
n
-form.
We then consider the associated relative (possibly singular) volume form
\omega
and the fiber (
L^{2}
-)integral of
{\sigma}
with respect to
g
:

\omega=g\ c_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}},\ {\rm and}\ F_{\omega}(y)=\int_{X_{y}}\omega\ \in[0,+\infty]

for
y\in Y\setminus Q
.
Here
c_{n}=\sqrt{-1}^{n^{2}}
so that the pairing becomes semi-positive on each fiber.
∎

For
y\in Y\setminus Q
,
F_{\omega}(y)<+\infty
if and only if

{\sigma}|_{X_{y}}\in H^{0}(X_{y},K_{X_{y}}\otimes E|_{X_{y}}\otimes{\mathcal{I}}(g|_{X_{y}}))
,
where
{\mathcal{I}}(g|_{X_{y}})
is the multiplier ideal sheaf of
g|_{X_{y}}
.
We understand
F_{\omega}(y)=+\infty
if
{\varphi}|_{X_{y}}\equiv-\infty
on a fiber for example.
We know, in general,
F_{\omega}(y)
is lower-semi-continuous on which
f
is smooth,
[
HPS
, 22.5]
.

We are interested in the behavior of
F_{\omega}(y)
as
y
approaches to
Q
.
Here, we are only concerned about the singularity type of
g
, not about the curvature property of
g
, e.g. semi-positivity.
The choices of smooth metrics
h,h_{{\Delta}_{D}}
also do not affect the following conclusion.

Theorem 3.1.2
.

Suppose
X
is smooth and
{\Delta}_{Y}=0
in
1.1
, and use the notations in
3.1.1
.
Suppose the Lelong number
\nu({\varphi},x)=0
for every
x\in X
.
Let
y_{0}\in Y
and let
V\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{m},t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}))
be a local coordinate centered at a point
y_{0}\in Y
.
Then

(1) Upper estimate.

F_{\omega}/|\prod_{j=1}^{m}t_{j}|^{c}\in L^{1}_{loc}(V,0)
for any
c\in(0,2)
.
In particular, if
-\log F_{\omega}
is quasi-psh on a neighborhood of
y_{0}
,
one has
\nu(-\log F_{\omega},y_{0})=0
.

(2) Lower estimate.
There exist positive constants
{\delta}(<1)
and
c_{1}
such that, for any
t\in V\setminus Q
with
|t|<{\delta}
,
F_{\omega}(t)\geq c_{1}
holds.

Proof.

(1)

Step 1. Preliminary discussion.

We take
c\in(0,2)
.
We put
T=\prod_{j=}^{m}t_{j}
, and
|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|=|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|_{h_{{\Delta}_{D}}}
for short.
We note
hc_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}}|dt|^{2}
is a
C^{\infty}
semi-positive volume form on
X_{V}=f^{-1}(V)
, where
dt=dt_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dt_{m}
and
|dt|^{2}=c_{m}dt\wedge d\overline{t}
.
Then

\int_{V}F_{\omega}\ \frac{|dt|^{2}}{|T|^{c}}=\int_{X_{V}}\frac{e^{-{\varphi}}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}|T|^{c}}hc_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}}|dt|^{2}.

We take an open covering
{\mathcal{U}}=\{U_{{\lambda}}\}_{{\lambda}}
of
X
, which is fine enough and locally finite, and take a partition of unity
\{\rho_{{\lambda}}\}_{{\lambda}}
subordinate to
{\mathcal{U}}
.
We can suppose
\overline{X_{V}}\cap U_{{\lambda}}\neq\emptyset
for a finite number of
{\lambda}
.
Then

\int_{V}F_{\omega}\ \frac{|dt|^{2}}{|T|^{c}}=\sum_{{\lambda}}\int_{U_{{\lambda}}\cap X_{V}}\rho_{{\lambda}}\frac{e^{-{\varphi}}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}|T|^{c}}hc_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}}|dt|^{2}.

Thus, it is enough to evaluate an integral on each local coordinate
U
of
X
.
We let
X_{t}=f^{-1}(t)
for
t\in V
.

Step 2. Local coordinate.

At each
x_{0}\in X_{0}
, we can find local coordinates
U\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{n+m},x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m})),V\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{m},t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}))

(we suppose
|x_{i}|\leq 1
for all
i
,
|t_{j}|\leq 1
for all
j
),
such that
f|_{U}:U\to V
is given by
t=f(x),t_{j}=f_{j}(x),j=1,\ldots,m
, with

t_{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{1}}x_{j}^{a_{j}},\ t_{2}=\prod_{j=\ell_{1}+1}^{\ell_{2}}x_{j}^{a_{j}},\ \ldots,\ t_{m}=\prod_{j=\ell_{m-1}+1}^{\ell_{m}}x_{j}^{a_{j}}

for some
0=\ell_{0}<\ell_{1}<\ldots<\ell_{m}\leq n+m
and
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
(The coordinates
x,t
depend on
x_{0}
, but)
Moreover
Q\cap V\subset\{t_{1}\cdots t_{m}=0\}
and
P\cap U\subset\{x_{1}\cdots x_{n+m}=0\}
.
This
V\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{m},t)
may be different from the one we took in the statement of
3.1.2
.
However, it does not matter what our purpose is.
We can write as
\ell_{i}=\ell_{i-1}+n_{i}+1
with
n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}n_{i}=n
, where
n_{m+1}:=n+m-\ell_{m}
.
We denote by
y_{j}=x_{\ell_{j}},b_{j}=a_{\ell_{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
, and by

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{j}=(x_{\ell_{j-1}+1},\ldots,x_{\ell_{j-1}+n_{j}}),\ \mathbf{a}_{j}=(a_{\ell_{j-1}+1},\ldots,a_{\ell_{j-1}+n_{j}}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{m+1}=(x_{\ell_{m}+1},\ldots,x_{n+m}).

We will use a multi-index convention.
For example
t_{j}=\mathbf{x}_{j}^{\mathbf{a}_{j}}y_{j}^{b_{j}}
.

Step 3. Integrand.

We may suppose that every (
{\mathbb{Q}}
-)line bundle appearing in our situation is trivialized on a neighborhood of
\overline{U}
or of
\overline{V}
.
We take a local frame
dx=\wedge_{i=1}^{n+m}dx_{i}
of
K_{X}|_{U}
and
dt=\wedge_{j=1}^{m}dt_{j}
(as before) of
K_{Y}|_{V}
respectively.
Using relations
\frac{dt_{j}}{t_{j}}=\sum_{i=\ell_{j-1}+1}^{\ell_{j-1}+n_{j}}a_{i}\frac{dx_{i}}{x_{i}}+b_{j}\frac{dy_{j}}{y_{j}}
, we have

\wedge_{i=\ell_{j-1}+1}^{\ell_{j}}dx_{i}=\frac{y_{j}}{b_{j}t_{j}}\big(\wedge_{i=\ell_{j-1}+1}^{\ell_{j-1}+n_{j}}dx_{i}\big)\wedge dt_{j}
, in other term
d\mathbf{x}_{j}\wedge dy_{j}=\frac{y_{j}}{b_{j}t_{j}}d\mathbf{x}_{j}\wedge dt_{j}
.
Then, a local frame
\frac{dx}{dt}
of
K_{X/Y}
on
U
can be expressed as

\frac{dx}{dt}=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{y_{j}}{b_{j}t_{j}}d\mathbf{x}_{j}\Big)\wedge d\mathbf{x}_{m+1},

which satisfies
\frac{dx}{dt}\wedge f^{*}dt=\pm dx
.
We also let
|dx|^{2}=c_{n+m}dx\wedge d\overline{x}
, and

\big|\frac{dx}{dt}\big|=c_{n}\frac{dx}{dt}\wedge\overline{\frac{dx}{dt}}
.

Regarding
{\sigma}\in H^{0}(X,K_{X/Y}+E)={\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(f^{*}K_{Y},K_{X}+E)
, we have
{\sigma}(f^{*}dt)|_{U}={\sigma}_{U}dx=\pm{\sigma}_{U}\frac{dx}{dt}\wedge f^{*}dt
for some
{\sigma}_{U}\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})(\cong H^{0}(U,E))
, and hence

{\sigma}|_{X_{t}}=\pm{\sigma}_{U}\frac{dx}{dt}\Big|_{X_{t}}

for
t\in V\setminus Q
.
The metric
h
can be seen as a function in
C^{\infty}(\overline{U},{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
.
Thus we have

hc_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}}|dt|^{2}=A_{U}|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2}|dx|^{2}

on
U
for some
A_{U}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{U},{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
.
We finally take a cut-off function
\rho_{U}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})
.
Then, in view of Step 1, we need to estimate

I_{U}=\int_{U}\rho_{U}e^{-{\varphi}}\frac{A_{U}|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2}}{|T|^{c}|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}}|dx|^{2}.

Step 4. Reduction to an un-twisted case.

We introduce auxiliary quantities.
Recall
f^{*}Q_{j}=\sum_{i}a_{ji}P_{i}
for each
j
.
We set
c_{j}=\max_{i}\{d_{i}/a_{ji}\ {\rm if}\ f(P_{i})=Q_{j}\}
, and

\widetilde{c}=\max_{j}c_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We take
\varepsilon>0
such that
c(1+\varepsilon)+2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon<2
.
A simpler condition
c(1+\varepsilon)+2d_{i}\varepsilon<2
for any coefficient
d_{i}
of
P_{i}
also works (which in fact implies
c(1+\varepsilon)+2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon<2
).
We also require that
d_{i}(1+\varepsilon)<1
holds for any
i
with
d_{i}<1
.
We then take
q=1+\varepsilon
and
p\gg 1
such that

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

I_{U}\leq\big\|\rho_{U}^{1/p}e^{-{\varphi}}\big\|_{L^{p}(U,|dx|^{2})}\cdot\Big\|\rho_{U}^{1/q}\frac{A_{U}|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2}}{|T|^{c}|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}}\Big\|_{L^{q}(U,|dx|^{2})}.

The first term on the right hand side is finite:

(J_{p}:=)\ \big\|\rho_{U}^{1/p}e^{-{\varphi}}\big\|_{L^{p}(U,|dx|^{2})}^{p}=\int_{U}\rho_{U}e^{-p{\varphi}}|dx|^{2}<\infty,

by virtue of our assumption
\nu({\varphi},x)=0
for any
x\in X
.
We next consider

J_{q}:=\int_{U}\rho_{U}\frac{A_{U}^{q}|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2q}}{|T|^{cq}|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2q}}|dx|^{2}.

By Fubini, we have

J_{q}=\int_{t\in V}\frac{|dt|^{2}}{|T|^{cq}}\int_{X_{t}\cap U}\rho_{U}A_{U}^{q}\frac{|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2q}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2q}}\Big|\frac{dx}{dt}\Big|^{2}.

We will show in the next step that the fiber integral is estimated as

\int_{X_{t}\cap U}\rho_{U}A_{U}^{q}\frac{|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2q}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2q}}\Big|\frac{dx}{dt}\Big|^{2}\leq\frac{A}{|T|^{2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(-\log|t_{j}|)^{n}

for any
t\in V\setminus Q
, where
A>0
is a constant independent of
t
.
This estimate deduces that

J_{q}\leq A\int_{t\in V}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(-\log|t_{j}|)^{n}}{|T|^{cq+2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon}}\ |dt|^{2}<\infty.

The last integrability follows from
cq+2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon<2
and
2.2.8
.
We have
I_{U}<\infty
as a conclusion.

The statement
\nu(-\log F_{\omega},y)=0
is a consequence of this integrability combined with
2.2.6
.

Step 5. Estimate of fiber integral.

We can suppose
V\cap Q=\{t_{1}\cdots t_{k}=0\}
for some
k
with
0\leq k\leq m
, and
f
is smooth over
V\setminus Q
and singular over
V\cap Q
(cf.
[
Ta
, 2.2 (2)]
).
We can let
Q_{j}\cap V=\{t_{j}=0\}
for
1\leq j\leq k
, and
P_{i}\cap U=\{x_{i}=0\}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell_{k}
(such that
P_{i}
is
f
-vertical and appeared in
D
).
For a notational simplicity, we suppose
k=m
(and hence
\ell_{k}=\ell_{m}
).
Other cases
0\leq k\leq m-1
can be deduced from the case
k=m
.
In particular
P_{i}
is
f
-vertical if and only if
1\leq i\leq\ell_{m}
.
We write as

D^{h}={\rm div}\Big(\prod_{i>\ell_{m},d_{i}<1}x_{i}^{d_{i}}\Big),\ D^{v}_{-}={\rm div}\Big(\prod_{i\leq\ell_{m},-d_{i}>0}x_{i}^{-d_{i}}\Big),\ D^{v}_{+}={\rm div}\,(1)=0.

Here
D_{+}^{v}=0
by our assumption:
f
is equi-dimensional and
{\Delta}_{Y}=0
(cf.
[
Ta
, 2.2 (3)]
).

We estimate the integrand of the fiber integral in
J_{q}
.
We have

\displaystyle|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2}

\displaystyle=A_{1}\cdot\prod_{i,-d_{i}>0}|x_{i}|^{2\ulcorner{-d_{i}}\urcorner},

\displaystyle|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=A_{2}\cdot\prod_{i>\ell_{m},0<d_{i}<1}|x_{i}|^{2d_{i}}\prod_{i,-d_{i}>0}|x_{i}|^{2(\ulcorner{-d_{i}}\urcorner+d_{i})}

on
U
, where
A_{1},A_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{U},{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
.
Then

\frac{|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2q}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2q}}\Big|\frac{dx}{dt}\Big|^{2}=A_{3}\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m+1}W_{j}

holds with
A_{3}=A_{1}^{q}A_{2}^{-q}\prod_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}^{-2}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{U},{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
, and

\displaystyle W_{j}=(|y_{j}|^{2})^{-d_{\ell_{j}}q+1-a_{\ell_{j}}}\prod_{i=\ell_{j-1}+1}^{\ell_{j-1}+n_{j}}(|x_{i}|^{2})^{-d_{i}q-a_{i}}\ c_{n_{j}}d\mathbf{x}_{j}\wedge d\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\ \ (j=1,\ldots,m),

\displaystyle W_{m+1}=\frac{\prod_{i>\ell_{m},d_{i}<0}|x_{i}|^{-2d_{i}q}}{\prod_{i>\ell_{m},0<d_{i}<1}|x_{i}|^{2d_{i}q}}\ c_{n_{m+1}}d\mathbf{x}_{m+1}\wedge d\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{m+1}.

We now start to estimate.
Let us look at
W_{1}
for example, and show

W_{1}\leq\frac{1}{|t_{1}|^{2c_{1}\varepsilon}}\frac{c_{n_{1}}d\mathbf{x}_{1}\wedge d\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|^{2}}

as
(n_{1},n_{1})
-form on
U
, where
|\mathbf{x}_{1}|^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}|x_{i}|^{2}
(and
|\mathbf{x}_{j}|^{2}=\prod_{i=\ell_{j-1}+1}^{\ell_{j-1}+n_{j}}|x_{i}|^{2}
in general).

(i)
For
t_{1}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{1}}x_{j}^{a_{j}}
, we have

|t_{1}|^{c_{1}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{1}}|x_{j}^{a_{j}}|^{c_{1}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{1}}|x_{j}^{a_{j}}|^{d_{j}/a_{j}}
(by definition of
c_{1}
and as
|x_{j}|\leq 1
).
Thus,
|t_{1}|^{c_{1}\varepsilon}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{1}}|x_{j}|^{d_{j}\varepsilon}
.

(ii)
We note, as a consequence of
{\Delta}_{Y}=0
, that for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
i\in\{\ell_{j-1}+1,\ldots,\ell_{j}\},-d_{i}-a_{i}\geq-1
holds, in particular

-d_{\ell_{j}}+1-a_{\ell_{j}}\geq 0
holds.
Recalling
q=1+\varepsilon
, we use it as
-d_{\ell_{j}}q+1-a_{\ell_{j}}\geq-d_{\ell_{j}}\varepsilon
and
-d_{i}q-a_{i}\geq-1-d_{i}\varepsilon
.

(iii)
The “coefficient function” of
W_{1}
can be estimated as

(|y_{1}|^{2})^{-d_{\ell_{1}}q+1-a_{\ell_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}(|x_{i}|^{2})^{-d_{i}q-a_{i}}

\leq_{{\rm(ii)}}

(|y_{1}|^{2})^{-d_{\ell_{1}}\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}|x_{i}|^{-2}(|x_{i}|^{2})^{-d_{i}\varepsilon}\leq_{{\rm(i)}}|t_{1}|^{-2c_{1}\varepsilon}|\mathbf{x}_{1}|^{-2}
.
This gives the desired estimate of
W_{1}
.

We have a similar estimate for
W_{j}\ (j=2,\ldots,m)
.
We note
\prod_{j=1}^{m}|t_{j}|^{-2c_{j}\varepsilon}\leq|T|^{-2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon}
.
For
W_{m+1}
, we simply estimate it as

W_{m+1}\leq\frac{c_{n_{m+1}}d\mathbf{x}_{m+1}\wedge d\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{m+1}}{\prod_{i>\ell_{m},0<d_{i}<1}|x_{i}|^{2d_{i}q}}.

We introduce a (possibly singular) semi-positive
(n,n)
-form

\psi=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\frac{c_{n_{j}}d\mathbf{x}_{j}\wedge d\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{j}}{|\mathbf{x}_{j}|^{2}}\wedge\frac{c_{n_{m+1}}d\mathbf{x}_{m+1}\wedge d\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{m+1}}{\prod_{i>\ell_{m},0<d_{i}<1}|x_{i}|^{2d_{i}q}}

on
({\mathbb{C}}^{n+m},x)
.
We then have a constant
A_{4}>0
(independent of
t
) such that

\rho_{U}A_{U}^{q}\frac{|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2q}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2q}}\Big|\frac{dx}{dt}\Big|^{2}=\rho_{U}A_{U}^{q}A_{3}\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m+1}W_{j}\leq\frac{A_{4}}{|T|^{2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon}}\ \psi

holds on
U
.

As
d_{i}q<1
in the expression of
\psi
,
we can directly apply the way of computation in
[
Ta
, p.1733, Step 4]
to conclude that

\int_{X_{t}\cap U}\psi\leq A_{5}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(-\log|t_{j}|)^{n}

holds for any
t\in V\setminus Q
, where
A_{5}>0
is a constant independent of
t
.
Thus we have

\int_{X_{t}\cap U}\rho_{U}A_{U}^{q}\frac{|{\sigma}_{U}|^{2q}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2q}}\Big|\frac{dx}{dt}\Big|^{2}\leq\frac{A_{4}A_{5}}{|T|^{2\widetilde{c}\varepsilon}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(-\log|t_{j}|)^{n}

for any
t\in V\setminus Q
.
This is what we wanted.

(2)
Lower estimate.
As
{\varphi}
is quasi-psh, the function
e^{-{\varphi}}
has a positive lower bound on every compact set of
X
.
In particular there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that
e^{-{\varphi}}>c_{0}
on a neighborhood of
X_{y_{0}}
.
(We merely use the existence of a positive lower bound of
e^{-{\varphi}}
, and we do not use the Lelong number condition of
{\varphi}
.)
The integrand is

\omega=h\frac{e^{-{\varphi}}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}}c_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}}\geq h\frac{c_{0}}{|s_{{\Delta}_{D}}|^{2}}c_{n}{\sigma}\wedge\overline{{\sigma}}.

The last term is “an un-twisted case”.
We then just follow the argument in
[
Ta
, p.1735, Step 5]
.
∎

Remark 3.1.3
.

3.1.2
is local over

.
It can be generalized as follows.

3.2.
Quasi-smooth total space

We explain fiber integrals in the orbi-fold setting.
We refer to
[
CM
]
for a basic discussions on orbi-folds;

[
CM
, §2, §3]
gives a nice and concise account on (complex) orbi-folds, orbi-line bundles and so on.

3.2.1
Fiber integral.

Let
X
be a complex orbi-fold,
Y
be a complex manifold, and let
f:X\to Y
be a proper surjective holomorphic map with connected fibers.
We let
\dim X=n+m
and
\dim Y=m
.
We regard
\omega_{X}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{X})
as a reflexive sheaf of rank 1 and an orbi-line bundle.

(1)
Let
E
be an orbi-line bundle on
X
, and suppose
E
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundle.
Suppose there is a section

{\sigma}\in H^{0}(X,K_{X/Y}+E).

To be more precise,

5.
On finite generation problem of log canonical rings for generalized pairs

In this section, we apply twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem (Theorem
1.1
) for the finite generation problem for some versions of canonical rings.

Definition 5.1
.

A
generalized pair

(X,B+M)
consists of

•

a normal projective variety
X
,

•

an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
on
X
, and

•

a b-
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier b-divisor over
X
represented by some projective birational morphism
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
and
a nef
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
M^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}

such that
M=\varphi_{*}M^{\prime}
and
K_{X}+B+M
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a generalized pair.
Let
Y\rightarrow X
be a projective birational morphism.
We can write

K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M),

for some divisor
B_{Y}
on
Y
.
The
generalized log discrepancy

of
(X,B+M)
at a prime divisor
E\subset Y
, denoted by
a_{E}(X,B+M)

is defined to be
1-{\rm coeff}_{E}(B_{Y})
.

A generalized pair
(X,B+M)
is said to be
generalized log canonical
(or glc for short) if all its generalized log discrepancies are non-negative.
A generalized pair
(X,B+M)
is said to be
generalized Kawamata log terminal
(or gklt for short) if all its generalized log discrepancies are positive.
In the previous definitions, if the analogous statement holds for
M=0
, then we drop the word “generalized”.

First, we see the following lemma by
[
BCHM
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective generalized klt pair such that
K_{X}+B+M
is big. Assume that
l(K_{X}+B+M)
is a Cartier divisor for an
l>0
.

Then the ring

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,ml(K_{X}+B+M))

is a finite generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

Proof.

Note that by
[
FM
, Proposition 5.1]
, it is enough to show the lemma for a sufficiently large and divisible
l
. Taking a log resolution, we may assume that
(X,B)
is simple normal crossing klt pair and
M
is nef. Since
K_{X}+B+M
is big, we take an effective divisor
E
and an ample divisor
A
such that
l(K_{X}+B+M)\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}E+A
. Since
M+\frac{1}{l}A
is ample, we can choose an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
A^{\prime}
such that
(X,B+\frac{1}{l}(E+A^{\prime}))
is klt and
A^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}lM+A
for a sufficiently large and divisible positive integer
l
. Since

2l(K_{X}+B+M)\sim_{\mathbb{Z}}l(K_{X}+B+\frac{1}{l}(E+A^{\prime})),

it is enough to show the finite generation of

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,ml(K_{X}+B+\frac{1}{l}(E+A^{\prime}))),

which follows from
[
BCHM
, Corollary 1.1.2]
.
∎

Using twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem
1.1
, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective generalized klt pair such that a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
M^{\prime}
on a smooth higher birational model
X^{\prime}\to X
has a semi-positive singular Hermitian metric
h_{M^{\prime}}
with a vanishing Lelong number for the potential at every point of
X^{\prime}
. Assume that
l(K_{X}+B+M)
is a Cartier divisor for an
l>0
.

Then the ring

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,ml(K_{X}+B+M))

is a finite generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

Proof.

When
\kappa(K_{X}+B+M)=0
or
\kappa(K_{X}+B_{M})<0
,

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,ml(K_{X}+B+M))\simeq\mathbb{C}[t]\text{ or }\mathbb{C},

respectively, where
\mathbb{C}[t]
is the polynomial ring with one variable.
Thus we may assume that
\kappa(X,l(K_{X}+B+M))>0
. We consider the Iitaka fibration
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
of
l(K_{X}+B+M)
and take a higher birational model
X^{\prime}\to X
induces a holomorphic morphism
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
which is birational equivalent to
f
. Thus, by replacing, we may assume that
(X,B)
is a simple normal crossing klt pair admitting the Iitaka fibration
f:X\to Y
of
K_{X}+B+M
, and that
M
has a semi-positive singular Hermitian metric with a vanishing Lelong number for the potential at every point of
X
.

Now, we apply
2.1.3
: the toroidalization theorem due to Abramovich and Karu
[
AK
]
for
f
.
We have a birational morphisms
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
and
\psi:Y^{\prime}\to Y
, and a well prepared birational model
f^{\prime}:(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to Y^{\prime}
of
f
such that
M^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}M
and

\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}.

We use the notation in twisted Kawamata’s semi-positivity theorem
1.1
. Consider the divisor
\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}^{-}
. Since
B
is effective, any components of
f^{\prime*}\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}^{-}
are
\varphi
-exceptional divisors. Thus,
f^{\prime}
is also the Iitaka fibarion of
K_{X^{\prime}}+f^{\prime*}\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}^{-}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}
. Therefore the finite generation of
R(K_{X}+B+M)
is reduced to that of
R(K_{X^{\prime}}+f^{\prime*}\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}^{-}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}).

And that is equivalent to the finite generation of
R(K_{Y^{\prime}}+M_{Y^{\prime}}+\Delta^{+}_{Y^{\prime}})
.
Now
(Y^{\prime},\Delta_{Y^{\prime}}^{+})
is klt and
M_{Y^{\prime}}
is nef by Theorem
1.1
. Since
K_{Y^{\prime}}+M_{Y^{\prime}}+\Delta^{+}_{Y^{\prime}}
is big, the desired finite generation follows from Lemma
5.3
.
∎

The following is an interesting corollary for even smooth projective varieties.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety such that anti-canonical bundle
\omega_{X}^{-1}
has a semipositive singular metrics
h
with a vanishing Lelong number for the potential at every point of
X
.
Then, the anti-canonical ring

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{-m})

is a finite generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

In the last, we discuss the finite generation of generalized canonical rings for generalized klt pairs in a general situation. Indeed we prove that for lower dimensional cases as follows:

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a projective generalized klt pair such that
\mathrm{dim}\,X\leq 3
.
Assume that
l(K_{X}+B+M)
is a Cartier divisor for an
l>0
.

Then the ring

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,ml(K_{X}+B+M))

is a finite generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

Proof.

If
\kappa(X,l(K_{X}+B+M))\leq 0
, the finite generation is clear as the proof of Theorem
5.4
. We may assume that
1\leq\kappa(K_{X}+B+M)\leq 2
by Lemma
5.3
.
The case of
\kappa(K_{X}+B+M)=1
is known since the finite generation is always true for a section ring of a Cartier divisor with Iitaka dimension one (cf.
[
W
, Lemma 4.4]
).
When
\mathrm{dim}\,X=2
, we complete the proof by Lemma
5.3
again. Thus we may assume that
\mathrm{dim}\,X=3
and
\kappa(K_{X}+B+M)=2
.
As the proof of Theorem
5.4
, by taking the Iitaka fibration and log resolution, we may assume that there exists a projective surjective morphism
f:X\to Y
with connected fibers such that
(X,B+M)
is generalized klt and
\mathrm{dim}\,Y=2
. For a general fiber
F
of
f
, it holds that
\kappa((K_{X}+B+M)|_{F})=0
since the property of Iitaka fibrations.
Since
F
is a smooth projective curve, we see that
(K_{X}+B+M)|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0.
Thus we can apply
[
FiS
, Theorem 2.20]
.

Then there exists
B_{Y}
and
M_{Y}
such that
(Y,B_{Y}+M_{Y})
is generalized klt and the finite generation for
K_{X}+B+M
is equivalent for that for a big divisor
K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}
as the proof of Theorem
5.4
. Since
K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}
is big, the desired finite generation follows from Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Serrin-type Overdetermined problems for Hessian quotient equations
Zhenghuan Gao, Xiaohan Jia, Dekai Zhang

Abstract
. We prove the symmetry of solutions to overdetermined problems for a class of fully nonlinear equations, namely Hessian quotient equations and Hessian quotient curvature equations. Our approach is based on establishing a Rellich-Pohozaev type identity for Hessian quotient equations and using a P function. Our result generalizes the overdetermined problems for k k -Hessian equations and k k -curvature equations.



1.
Introduction

In the seminal paper
[
19
]
, Serrin established the symmetry of the solution to

(1.1)

\Delta u=n

in a bounded
C^{2}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

(1.2)

u=0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial\gamma}=1\quad\text{on }\ \partial\Omega.

where

\partial\Omega

If

is a solution to (

) and (

[
19
]
proved that upto a translation
u=\frac{|x|^{2}-1}{2}
and
\Omega
is the unit ball centered at the origin. The proof is based on the method of
moving planes
and it can be applied to more general uniformly elliptic equations. In
[
22
]
, Weinberger provided an alternative proof by using maximum principle for P function and a Rellich-Pohozaev type identity.

There have been many generalizations of Serrin and Weinberger’s work to quasilinear elliptic equations (see e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
9
]
and reference therein ) and fully nonlinear equations such as Hessian equation and Weingarten curvature equation (see e.g.
[
1
,
12
,
21
]
).
In the Euclidean space, the overdetermined problem for the

-Hessian equation i.e.

[
1

by using a Rellich-Pohozaev type identity and Newton inequalities and was also dealt in

P functions have been extensively investigated and inspired several effective works in the context of elliptic partial differential equations. For fully nonlinear equations, the P function for 2-dimensional Monge-Ampère equation was given by Ma
[

]
, and the P functions for

In the third part, we consider Hessian quotient curvature equations in the Euclidean space

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Elementary symmetric functions

Let
\mathcal{S}^{n}
be the space of real symmetric
n\times n
matrices. We denote by
A=(a_{ij})
a matrix in
\mathcal{S}^{n}
, and by
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
the eigenvalues of
A
. For
1\leq k\leq n
, we recall the definition of
k
-th elementary symmetric functions of
A
,

(2.1)

S_{k}(A)=S_{k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

Denote by

(2.2)

S_{k}^{ij}(A):=\frac{\partial S_{k}(A)}{\partial a_{ij}},

then it is easy to see from the definition above that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}S_{k}^{ij}(A)a_{ij}=

\displaystyle kS_{k}(A),\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{k}^{ii}(A)=

\displaystyle(n-k+1)S_{k-1}(A).

For
1\leq k\leq n-1
, Newton’s inequalities say that

(2.4)

(n-k+1)(k+1)S_{k-1}(A)S_{k+1}(A)\leq k(n-k)S_{k}^{2}(A).

For
1\leq k\leq n
, recall that the Garding cone is defined as

(2.5)

\Gamma_{k}=\{A\in\mathcal{S}^{n}:S_{1}(A)>0,\cdots,S_{k}(A)>0\}.

For
A\in\Gamma_{k}
and
k>l\geq 0
,
r>s\geq 0
,
k\geq r
,
l\geq s
, we have the following Mclaurin inequalities

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{S_{k}(A)/C_{n}^{k}}{S_{l}(A)/C_{n}^{l}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\leq\bigg(\frac{S_{r}(A)/C_{n}^{r}}{S_{s}(A)/C_{n}^{s}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r-s}}.

In particular,

(2.7)

\bigg(\frac{S_{k}(A)}{C_{n}^{k}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq\bigg(\frac{S_{l}(A)}{C_{n}^{l}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{l}},\quad\forall\ k\geq l\geq 1,

and

(2.8)

\frac{{S_{k}(A)}/{C_{n}^{k}}}{{S_{k-1}(A)}/{C_{n}^{k-1}}}\leq\frac{{S_{l}(A)}/{C_{n}^{l}}}{{S_{l-1}(A)}/{C_{n}^{l-1}}},\quad\forall\ k\geq l\geq 1.

By (
2.6
), we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{k+1}(A)/C_{n}^{k+1}}{S_{k}(A)/C_{n}^{k}}\leq\bigg(\frac{S_{k}(A)/C_{n}^{k}}{S_{l}(A)/C_{n}^{l}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}.

The equalities in (
2.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) hold if the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
of
A
are equal to each other.
We need the following useful proposition which can be found in
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
n\times n
matrix
A
, we have

(2.10)

S_{k}^{ij}(A)=S_{k-1}(A)\delta_{ij}-\sum_{l=1}^{n}S_{k-1}^{il}(A)a_{jl}.

In the following we write
D,D^{2}
and
\Delta
for the gradient, Hessian and Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We also follow Einstein’s summation convention.

2.2.
Hessian operators

2.2.1.
Hessian operators in Euclidean space

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. The
k
-Hessian operator
S_{k}(D^{2}u)
is defined as the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of
D^{2}u
. Notice that

(2.11)

S_{1}(D^{2}u)=\Delta u\quad\text{and}\quad S_{n}(D^{2}u)=\mathrm{det}D^{2}u.

A function
u
is called
k
-convex in
\Omega
, if
D^{2}u(x)\in\Gamma_{k}
for any
x\in\Omega
. A direct computation yields that
(S_{k}^{1j}(D^{2}u),\cdots,S_{k}^{nj}(D^{2}u))
is divergence free, that is

(2.12)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)=0.

By using (
2.10
), it is easy to see that

(2.13)

S_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u)u_{il}=S_{k}^{il}(D^{2}u)u_{ij}.

2.2.2.
Hessian operators in hyperbolic space

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. The
k
-Hessian operator
S_{k}[u]
is defined as the
k
-th elementary symmetric function
\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u-uI)
of
D^{2}u-uI
. Notice that

(2.14)

S_{1}[u]=\Delta u-nu\quad\text{and}\quad S_{n}[u]=\mathrm{det}(D^{2}u-uI).

A function
u
is called
k
-admissible
in
\Omega
if
D^{2}u(x)-u(x)I\in\Gamma_{k}
for any
x\in\Omega
. A function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is called an
admissible solution
of
S_{k}[u]=f
in
\Omega
, if
u
solves the equation and
u
is an
k
-admissible function.

We list the following propositions proven in
[
8
]
, which will be used in Section
4
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
u\in C^{3}(\Omega)
, then

(2.15)

D_{i}(\sigma_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u-uI))=0.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, then

(2.16)

\sigma_{k}^{ij}(D^{2}u-uI)u_{il}=\sigma_{k}^{il}(D^{2}u-uI)u_{ij}.

2.3.
Weingarten hypersurfaces

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which is locally represented as a graph
x_{n+1}=u(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
,
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\Omega
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote
u_{i}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}
,
u_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}
, and
Du=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
. Then
\nu=\frac{(-Du,1)}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}
is the outer unit normal of
\mathcal{M}
. Denote
w=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}
. The furst and secondfundamental forms can be respectively expressed as

(2.17)

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+u_{i}u_{j},\quad\text{and}\quad b_{ij}=\frac{u_{ij}}{w}.

Then the principal curvatures
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
of
\mathcal{M}
are the eigenvalues of the second fundamental form relative to the first fundamental form, i.e. the eigenvalues of
g^{ik}b_{kj}=:a_{ij}
, where
g^{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\frac{u_{i}}{u_{j}}{w^{2}}
is the inverse mateix to
g_{ij}
.
Hence

(2.18)

\displaystyle a_{ij}(u)=g^{ik}b_{kj}=\frac{u_{ij}}{w}-\frac{u_{i}u_{k}u_{kj}}{w^{3}}=\big(\frac{u_{i}}{w}\big)_{j},

where
\big(\frac{u_{i}}{w}\big)_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\big(\frac{u_{i}}{w}\big)
. We say
u
is
k
-admissible if
a_{ij}(u)\in\Gamma_{k}
.

Reilly [Reilly1973] gave the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
u\in C^{3}(\Omega)
,
A_{ij}=(\frac{u_{i}}{w})_{j}
. Then

(2.19)

\displaystyle D_{j}S_{k}^{ij}(A)=0.

For general (non-symmeric) matrices, Pietra, Gavitone and Xia proved the following result in
[
5
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
, we have

(2.20)

S_{k}^{ij}(A)=S_{k-1}(A)\delta_{ij}-\sum_{l=1}^{n}S_{k-1}^{il}(A)a_{jl}.

The following proposition can be inferred from above proposition, we omit the proof.

Proposition 2.6
.

For any
n\times n

A=(a_{ij})
, we have

(2.21)

S_{k}^{il}(A)a_{jl}=S_{k}^{lj}(A)a_{li}.

2.4.
Minkowskian integral formulas

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{2}
bounded domain, and
\partial\Omega
is the boundary of
\Omega
. Denote the principle curvatures of
\partial\Omega

\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})

-th curvature of

(2.22)

H_{k}:=S_{k}(\kappa).

\Omega
is called
k
-convex

H_{i}>0
for all
1\leq i\leq k

(n-1)
-convex is strictly convex, 1-convex is also called mean convex.

In the theory of convex bodies and differential geometry, Minkowskian integral formula (see

\Omega

(2.23)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H_{k}}{C_{n-1}^{k}}x\cdot\gamma\mathrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H_{k-1}}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Suppose
\Omega
is a domain of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Let
p\in\Omega
,
r
be the distance from
p
. Let
V(x)=\cosh(r(x))
. Then the Minkowskian integral formula says

(2.24)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H_{k}}{C_{n-1}^{k}}V_{\gamma}\mathrm{d}\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{H_{k-1}}{C_{n-1}^{k-1}}V\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Remark 3.2

The proof is amost the same as that in

\displaystyle 0<\frac{1}{S_{l}}(u_{nn}H_{k-1}+H_{k}).

So we need

Based on Lemma

Lemma 3.3

(
[

Let
u\in C^{3}(\Omega)
be an admissible (i.e.

is

(3.1)

\displaystyle{S_{k}(D^{2}u)}=\frac{C_{n}^{k}}{C_{n}^{l}}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}\quad\text{in }\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

with
0\leq l<k\leq n

P

(3.2)

P:=|Du|^{2}-2u

are obtained only on the boundary

Proof.

Denote by

(3.3)

\displaystyle F^{ij}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{ij}}\frac{S_{k}(D^{2}u)}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}=\frac{1}{S_{l}^{2}}(S_{k}^{ij}S_{l}(D^{2}u)-S_{k}(D^{2}u)S_{l}^{ij}).

By (

(3.4)

\displaystyle F^{ij}u_{ij}=

\displaystyle(k-l)\frac{S_{k}(D^{2}u)}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)},

(3.5)

\displaystyle F^{ij}u_{si}u_{sj}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{S_{l}^{2}}\big((l+1)S_{l+1}S_{k}-(k+1)S_{k+1}S_{l}\big).

Differenting the equation (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle F^{ij}u_{ijs}=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle F^{ij}P_{ij}=

\displaystyle 2F^{ij}\big(u_{si}u_{sj}+u_{s}u_{sij}-u_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2F^{ij}u_{si}u_{sj}-2F^{ij}u_{ij}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2S_{k}(D^{2}u)}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}\bigl((l+1)\frac{S_{l+1}(D^{2}u)}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}-(k+1)\frac{S_{k+1}(D^{2}u)}{S_{k}(D^{2}u)}-(k-l)\bigr).

By (
2.6
) and (

), we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{l+1}(D^{2}u)/C_{n}^{l+1}}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)/C_{n}^{l}}\geq\bigg(\frac{S_{k}(D^{2}u)/C_{n}^{k}}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)/C_{n}^{l}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}=1.

That is

\displaystyle(l+1)\frac{S_{l+1}(D^{2}u)}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}\geq n-l.

Plugging (

3

\displaystyle F^{ij}P_{ij}\geq\frac{2S_{k}(D^{2}u)}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}\bigl(n-k-(k+1)\frac{S_{k+1}(D^{2}u)}{S_{k}(D^{2}u)}\bigr)

(k+1)\frac{S_{k+1}}{S_{k}}\leq n-k.

Plugging (
3.8
) and (
3.9

(3.10)

\displaystyle F^{ij}P_{ij}\geq 0.

By maximum principle, the maximum of

The Rellich-Pohozaev type identity for

-Hessian equation has already been found
[
1
,
20

(3.11)

\frac{n-2k}{k(k+1)}\int_{\Omega}S_{k}^{ij}u_{i}u_{j}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{\partial\Omega}x\cdot\gamma|Du|^{k+1}H_{k-1}\mathrm{d}\sigma=n\int_{\Omega}F(u)\mathrm{d}x,

For Hessian quotient equations, we first prove identities for

\int_{\Omega}S_{l}(D^{2}u)u

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain,

is a solution to the problem

(3.12)

\begin{cases}{S_{k}(D^{2}u)}=\frac{C_{n}^{k}}{C_{n}^{l}}{S_{l}(D^{2}u)}&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Suppose
S_{l}(D^{2}u)>0
in
\overline{\Omega}
, then

Dynamics of strongly I I -regular hyperbolic elements on affine buildings
Corina Ciobotaru

Abstract
The first goal of this article is to investigate a refinement of previously-introduced strongly regular hyperbolic automorphisms of locally finite thick Euclidean buildings Δ \Delta of finite Coxeter system ( W , S ) (W,S) . The new ones are defined for each proper subset I ⊊ S I\subsetneq S and called strongly I I -regular hyperbolic automorphisms of Δ \Delta . Generalizing previous results, we show that such elements exist in any group G G acting cocompactly and by automorphisms on Δ \Delta . Although the dynamics of strongly I I -regular hyperbolic elements γ \gamma on the spherical building ∂ ∞ Δ \partial_{\infty}\Delta of Δ \Delta is much more complicated than for the strongly regular ones, the lim n → ∞ γ n ​ ( ξ ) \lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{n}(\xi) still exists in ∂ ∞ Δ \partial_{\infty}\Delta for ideal points ξ ∈ ∂ ∞ Δ \xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta that satisfy certain assumptions. An important role in this business is played by the cone topology on Δ ∪ ∂ ∞ Δ \Delta\cup\partial_{\infty}\Delta and the projection of specific residues of ∂ ∞ Δ \partial_{\infty}\Delta on the ideal boundary of Min ⁡ ( γ ) \mathrm{Min}(\gamma) . All the above research is performed in order to achieve the second, and main, goal of the article. Namely, we prove that for closed groups G G with a type-preserving and strongly transitive action by automorphisms on Δ \Delta , the Chabauty limits of certain closed subgroups of G G contain as a normal subgroup the entire unipotent radical of concrete parabolic subgroups of G G .

2
Background material on groups acting on buildings

In this article we fix
\Delta
to be a locally finite thick Euclidean building of dimension
\geq 1
, thus irreducible. We consider
\Delta
, and all other buildings in this paper, to be endowed with their complete system of apartments. With respect to that, the ideal boundary (also called the visual boundary) of
\Delta
is denoted by
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, and this is endowed with a structure of a spherical building. Points of
\Delta
, respectively
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, will be considered with respect to a geometric realisation of that affine, respectively spherical, building. By
[
1
, Theorem 11.79]
the apartments of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
are in one to one correspondence with those of
\Delta
. We denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
the full group of automorphisms of
\Delta
, and by
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
the subgroup of all
type-preserving
automorphisms, meaning those automorphisms that preserve a chosen coloring of the vertices of the model chamber and thus of the vertices of the chambers of
\Delta
.

By a simplex
\sigma
of
\Delta
, or of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, we mean the interior of it, thus
\sigma
is considered without its faces, so
\sigma
is open. Let
\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\Delta)
, respectively
\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
, be the set of all chambers of the spherical building
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, respectively spherical apartment
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
.

For a subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
, and a subset

of
\Delta
, or of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, we denote

\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(Y):=\{g\in G\;|\;g(z)=z,\forall z\in Y\}\text{\; and \; }\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(Y):=\{g\in G\;|\;g(Y)=Y\text{ setwise}\}.

Let us fix for what follows a special vertex
x_{0}\in\Delta
(see
[
12
, Sec. 16.1]
) and an apartment
\mathcal{A}\subset\Delta
such that
x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
. In the apartment
\mathcal{A}
we fix a Weyl chamber
Q(x_{0},c_{+})\subset\mathcal{A}
, with base point
x_{0}
. This
Q(x_{0},c_{+})
is a sector in
\mathcal{A}
with base point
x_{0}
and having at its infinity the ideal chamber in
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
that will be denoted by
c_{+}
. The ideal chamber opposite
c_{+}
in the apartment
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
c_{-}
. The two opposite ideal chambers

of
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
are also fixed for what follows.

(Positive, negative, simple roots)

For the fixed apartment

, denote by

, and by

Definition 2.3

(Type of an ideal simplex)
.

Let

Notice, any two ideal simplices

Let

2.1
General facts on strong transitivity

In this section we consider
G
to be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
.

For an ideal simplex
\sigma
of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
we take

G_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(\sigma)=\{g\in G\;|\;g(\sigma)=\sigma\text{ pointwise}\}

and call it the
parabolic subgroup of

associated with
\sigma
. Notice

is closed in

, and since

is type-preserving we have that
G_{\sigma}=\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(\sigma)=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\sigma)
.

The subgroup

is maximal when

is an ideal vertex in the spherical building
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, and minimal when
\sigma
is an ideal chamber in the spherical building
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
.

When
\sigma=c
we call
B(G):=G_{c}
to be a
Borel subgroup
of
G
. We also take

B^{0}(G):=\{g\in G\;|\;g(c)=c,\;g\text{ elliptic}\}.

A
good maximal compact
subgroup of
G
is the stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x):=\{g\in G\;|\;g(x)=x\}
of a special vertex
x
of
\Delta
.

Definition 2.5

(Strong transitivity)
.

We say that a closed subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
acts
strongly transitively
on
\Delta
(respectively
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
), if for any two pairs
(\mathcal{A}_{1},c_{1})
and
(\mathcal{A}_{2},c_{2})
, consisting each of an apartment
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of
\Delta
(respectively
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
), and a chamber

, there exists
g\in G
such that
g(\mathcal{A}_{1})=\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
g(c_{1})=c_{2}
.

It is well known that any semi-simple algebraic group over a non-Archimedean local field acts by type-preserving automorphisms and strongly transitively on its associated Bruhat–Tits building.

When the closed subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
acts on
\Delta
strongly transitively, it is a known, easy-to-see, fact that
G
induces a strongly transitively action on the visual boundary

, see
[
12
, 17.1]
. Then in this case a Borel subgroup

of

is unique, up to conjugation in

.

For the fixed apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
\Delta
, the special vertex
x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
, and the ideal chamber

, let

K(G):=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x_{0})\;\text{ and }\;B(G)^{+}:=G_{c_{+}},

and

be a lift in
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{A})
of the maximal abelian normal subgroup

of the affine Weyl group

of
\Delta
. Also, since

acts by translations on the apartment

, one can see that it fixes pointwise the ideal apartment

, and so

. Notice that all those closed subgroup of

depend on the choice of the apartment

, the special vertex

and the ideal chamber

. In particular, the decompositions below also depend on the choice of

and

. For simplicity, in the next two results we just take

.

(

when

2.3
Short on Chabauty topology

For a locally compact topological space
X
, the set
\mathcal{F}(X)
of all closed subsets of
X
is a compact topological space with respect to the Chabauty topology (
[
11
, Proposition 1.7, p. 58]
). Given a family
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{F}(X)
it is natural to ask what is the closure
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{Ch}
of
\mathcal{T}
with respect to the Chabauty topology, and whether or not elements of
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{Ch}\setminus\mathcal{T}
satisfy the same properties as the ones of
\mathcal{T}
. We call elements of
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{Ch}
the
Chabauty limits of
\mathcal{T}
, and
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{Ch}
the
Chabauty compactification
of
\mathcal{T}
(see
[
7
]
).

For a locally compact group
G
we denote by
\mathcal{S}(G)
the set of all closed subgroups of
G
. By
[
11
, Proposition 1.7, p. 58]
the space
\mathcal{S}(G)
is closed in
\mathcal{F}(G)
, with respect to the Chabauty topology, and thus compact.

Proposition 2.20
.

(
[
11
, Proposition 1.8, p. 60]
,
[
5
, Proposition I.3.1.3]
)

Suppose
X
is a locally compact metric space.
A sequence of closed subsets
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}(X)
converges to
F\in\mathcal{F}(X)
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

1)

For every
f\in F
there is a sequence
\{f_{n}\in F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
f
;

2)

For every sequence
\{f_{n}\in F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, if there is a strictly increasing subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\{f_{n_{k}}\in F_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f
, then
f\in F
.

Proposition
2.20
can be applied to a sequence of closed subgroups
\{H_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{S}(G)
converging to
H\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, obtaining a similar characterisation of convergence in
\mathcal{S}(G)
, when
G
is a locally compact group that is endowed with a metric inducing its topology.

\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and dynamical properties

3.1
Apartments and stars of ideal simplices

In this subsection we define and give some properties of the ‘stars’ and the opposites of ideal simplices in the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
of a locally finite thick Euclidean building
\Delta
. We show that an apartment of
\Delta
determines uniquely, respectively, is uniquely determined by, the stars of two opposite ideal simplices.

Let
\alpha:=\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
G
that escapes every compact subset of
G
. Then, as the space
\Delta\cup\partial_{\infty}\Delta
is compact with respect to the cone topology, the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
admits a subsequence
\beta:=\{a_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
with the property that
\{a_{n_{k}}(x_{0})\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to an ideal point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
. Notice, there is a unique minimal ideal simplex
\sigma\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta
such that
\xi
is in the interior of
\sigma
. One can have anything in-between
\sigma
being an ideal chamber or
\sigma
being an ideal vertex of the spherical building
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
.

Definition 3.1

(Stars in apartments and
\sigma
-cones)
.

Let
\sigma
be an ideal simplex in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and choose an apartment
\mathcal{B}
of
\Delta
such that
\sigma\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{B}
be a point. We set

\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}):=\{c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})\;|\;\sigma\text{ in the closure of }c\},\text{ resp.,}

\operatorname{St}(x,\mathcal{B}):=\{c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\mathcal{B})\;|\;x\text{ in the closure of }c\},

and call them the
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}
-star of
\sigma
, respectively, the
\mathcal{B}
-star of
x
. As well, the associated
\sigma
-cone in
\mathcal{B}
with base-point
x\in\mathcal{B}
is defined by
Q(x,\sigma,\mathcal{B}):=\bigcup\limits_{c\in\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})}Q(x,c)
, where
Q(x,c)\subset\mathcal{B}
is the open Weyl sector emanating from the point
x
and having
c
as ideal chamber. A
\sigma
-cone in
\mathcal{B}
is

for some
x\in\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 3.2
.

1.

Notice,
\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
, respectively,
\operatorname{St}(x,\mathcal{B})

2.

As well, the intersection of two
\sigma
-cones in
\mathcal{B}
contains a
\sigma
-cone in
\mathcal{B}
.

3.

Moreover, if we take
\sigma_{-}
to be the unique simplex in
\partial\mathcal{B}
opposite
\sigma

\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
is opposite
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
in the sense that any ideal chamber

has a unique opposite in
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
, and vice-versa.

The following remark recalls some facts related to residues and projections (see
[
1
, Section 5.3]
).

Remark 3.3
.

1.

Given an ideal simplex
\sigma_{+}
in

of

\partial_{\infty}\Delta

For the following lemma we will consider the star of a simplex as a set of chambers.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick Euclidean building,
\sigma_{-}
and
\sigma_{+}
two opposite ideal simplices of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, and
\mathcal{B}
an apartment of
\Delta

\sigma_{-},\sigma_{+}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}

is the unique one with this property that
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
and
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B^{\prime}})
can be part of the same ideal apartment in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
. In particular,
\mathcal{B}
is uniquely determined by, respectively, determines uniquely,
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
and
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})

Proof.

Let
c\in\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be an apartment of
\Delta
such that
c
and
\sigma_{-}
both belong to
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Then there is a unique ideal chamber
c^{\prime}_{-}
of
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
which contains
\sigma_{-}
in its closure, and it is opposite

. It is a well know fact that any two opposite ideal chambers uniquely determine, respectively, are uniquely determined by, an apartment of
\Delta
.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be an apartment of
\Delta
with
\sigma_{-}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Suppose
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B^{\prime}})
and
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
are part of the same ideal apartment
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}
, for some apartment
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
of
\Delta
. We claim that
\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}=\mathcal{B}
. Indeed, let

. Consider the projection

. Then since

are all in
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}
and
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
, we have that
\mathrm{proj}_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\Delta)}(c)
is in both
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}
and
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
; apply
[
1

and the fact that in each of the apartments

and
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}
there is a unique chamber at distance

w_{1}:=\min\{\delta(\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}),\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\prime}))\}\in W\text{ from }c.

Moreover, by the same
[

we also have that

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick Euclidean building,

\Delta
, and

Proof.

Let us consider another choice

Then, considering the cone topology with respect to the family of baricenters

, for some

large enough, then

is a convex set, it is a “large” portion of

.
In particular,

contains some large

By the last axiom of a building, there is a Weyl isomorphism of apartments

and fixes

Then

So

and

We have proved that the cone topology associated with the family

\eta

. Since

, we are dealing with a simplicial complex, and
V_{c}=U_{x,r,c}
is open, then all of the cones
Q(x,c^{\prime})
must have in common at least the same chamber
C
of
Q(x,c)\subset\Delta
having
x
as a special vertex.

Consider now the
\sigma^{\prime}
-cone
Q(x,\sigma^{\prime})\subset Q(x,c^{\prime})\subset\Delta
with base point
x
and ideal simplex
\sigma^{\prime}\subset c^{\prime}
of type
I
, for each of the ideal chambers
c^{\prime}\in U_{x,r,c}
. Then by the above, the
\sigma^{\prime}
-cones
Q(x,\sigma^{\prime})
will also have in common at least a face of
C
having
x
as a vertex. This shows that
V_{\sigma}
is indeed
U(x,r^{\prime},\sigma)
, for some
0<r^{\prime}\leq r
, and thus
V_{\sigma}
is a standard open neighborhood of
\sigma
in
I(\partial_{\infty}\Delta)
.
∎

3.3
Strongly
I
–regular hyperbolic elements

In this section we generalize the notions of regular and strongly regular hyperbolic elements used in
[
6
, Section 2.1]
(and the references therein) when the
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space is a Euclidean building
X
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
X
be a Euclidean building of dimension
n\geq 1
,
\gamma
a hyperbolic automorphism of
X
,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible finite Coxeter system, and
I\subsetneq S
. Let

\mathrm{Min}(\gamma):=\{x\in X\;|\;d(x,\gamma(x))=|\gamma|\},

where
|\gamma|
denotes the translation length of
\gamma
, i.e.
|\gamma|:=\inf\{d(x,\gamma(x))\;|\;x\in X\}
. Since
X
is a simplicial complex the infimum is always attained. We say
\gamma
is an
I
-regular hyperbolic
if
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
contains a flat of dimension
n-|I|
. We call
\gamma

strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic
if moreover the two endpoints of some (and hence all) of its translation axes lie, respectively, in the interior of two opposite ideal simplices of type
I
of the spherical building at infinity
\partial_{\infty}X
. Here we abuse the notation, and use the type
I
with respect to the walls in the affine Weyl group
(W^{\text{\tiny{aff}}},S^{\text{\tiny{aff}}})
of
X
.

Notice, when
I=\{\emptyset\}
, a flat of dimension
n-|\emptyset|
is an apartment of
X
. Still, for
I=\{\emptyset\}
, our Definition
3.10
of
\emptyset
-regular does not imply regular in the sense of
[
6
, Section 2.1, Definition 2.1]
. This is because in the latter definition, the set
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
contains a unique apartment. Yet, the set
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
from Definition
3.10
might contain more apartments. In any case,
[
6
, Section 2.1, Definition 2.1]
implies Definition
3.10
. Thus, strongly regular hyperbolic in the sense of
[
6
, Section 2.1]
is strongly
\emptyset
-regular hyperbolic in the sense of Definition
3.10
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
X
be a Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system, and
I\subsetneq S
. A geodesic line
\ell
is called
strongly
I
-regular
if its endpoints lie, respectively, in the interior of two opposite ideal simplices of type
I
of the spherical building at infinity
\partial_{\infty}X
. (Here we abuse the notation and by the type
I
we mean the walls corresponding to the affine Weyl group
(W^{\text{\tiny{aff}}},S^{\text{\tiny{aff}}})
.)

Remark 3.12
.

By
[
3
, Part II, Prop. 6.2(3)]
we know that
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is a closed and convex subset of
X
(i.e. convex means that if two points are in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
then also is the geodesic between them). Moreover,
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is
\gamma
-invariant, i.e.
\gamma(\mathrm{Min}(\gamma))=\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. The structure of
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, for
\gamma
hyperbolic, is given in
[
3
, Part II, Prop. 6.8]
.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
X
be a Euclidean building of dimension
n\geq 1
, and
\gamma
a hyperbolic automorphism of
X
. We define the ideal boundary of the set
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, that is denoted by
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, to be the set of all equivalence classes of geodesic rays that are contained in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. Two geodesic rays are in the same equivalent class (i.e. they determine the same point in
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
) if they are asymptotic (see
[
3
, Part II, Def. 8.1]
).

Notice that
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is a subset of
\partial_{\infty}X
. Moreover, from
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
being convex we have that for any fixed point
p\in\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, there is a geodesic ray with basepoint
p
and endpoint
\xi
that is entirely contained in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, for every
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
(see
[
3
, Part II, Prop. 8.2, Lem. 8.3]
). Using just the definitions, and the fact that
\gamma
acts like a translation, thus sending the geodesic rays in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
to parallel (i.e. asymptotic) geodesic rays, one can deduce that
\gamma
fixes pointwise the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
X
be a Euclidean building of dimension
n\geq 1
, and
\gamma
a hyperbolic automorphism of
X
. Then for every
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
we have that
\gamma(\xi)=\xi
.

Proof.

First notice that the translation length
|\gamma|
of
\gamma
is strictly positive,
|\gamma|>0
.
Fix a point
p\in\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. Take
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
and consider the geodesic ray
\sigma:[0,\infty)\to\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
from
p
to
\xi
, that is also denoted by
[p,\xi)
. This exists by the remark made just above. Then, since
\gamma
leaves invariant
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, we have that
\gamma([p,\xi))=[\gamma(p),\gamma(\xi))
is still a geodesic ray in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, this is exaclty
\gamma\circ\sigma:[0,\infty)\to\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. But the two geodesic rays
\sigma
and
\gamma\circ\sigma
are asymptotic, as
d(\sigma(t),\gamma(\sigma(t)))=|\gamma|
for every
t\in[0,\infty)
. The conclusion follows since
\xi=\gamma(\xi)
.
∎

As in
[
6
, Section 2.1]
we also record in the next lemmas some easy description of strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic automorphisms.

Lemma 3.15
.

(see
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
)

Let
X
be a Euclidean building of dimension
n\geq 1
,
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
a type-preserving hyperbolic automorphism, and
I\subsetneq S
. If the two endpoints of some (and hence all) of the translation axes of
\gamma
lie, respectively, in the interior of two opposite ideal simplices of type
I
of the spherical building at infinity
\partial_{\infty}X
, then
\gamma
is strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic. Moreover, if
\gamma
is strongly
\emptyset
-regular hyperbolic, then
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is an apartment of
X
.

When
\gamma
is strongly
\emptyset
-regular hyperbolic, the apartment
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
will henceforth be called the
translation apartment
of
\gamma
; this apartment is uniquely determined.

Proof of Lemma
3.15
.

Since
\gamma
is hyperbolic, and since the endpoints of some
\gamma
-axis, say
\ell
, lie respectively in the interior of two opposite simplices
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\subset\partial_{\infty}X
, then the same holds for all
\gamma
-axes, because of asymptoticity. As
\gamma
is type-preserving, we get that
\gamma
fixes pointwise both simplices
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
(see
[
3
, Part II, Prop. 6.8]
).

By considering a point in
\ell
, and say
\sigma_{+}
, from the axioms of a building, one can construct a flat
F
of dimension
n-|I|
of
X
containing
\ell
,
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
in its boundary. The flat
F
is part of an apartment of
X
. Now, because
\gamma
is an automorphism of an affine building, and the translation axis
\ell
is strongly
I
-regular, the flat
F
is uniquely determined by
\ell
and
\sigma_{\pm}
. Thus,
F
must be invariant under
\gamma
. In particular,
\gamma
preserves
\partial_{\infty}F
, but fixes pointwise
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
in
\partial_{\infty}F
. Therefore,
\gamma
must act trivially on
\partial_{\infty}F
(since
\gamma
is type-preserving); in other words
\gamma
acts by translations on
F
. This proves that
F\subseteq\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, and so
\gamma
is
I
-regular hyperbolic, and by hypothesis,
\gamma
is strongly
I
-regular.

Suppose now
\gamma
is moreover strongly
\emptyset
-regular. Then
F
is an apartment
A
of
X
. Since any translation axis
\ell^{\prime}
of
\gamma
has the same endpoints as
\ell
, and those lie in the interior of the
c_{+},c_{-}
, respectively, then
\ell^{\prime}
must be entirely contained in
A
. Thus
A=\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

Given a locally finite thick Euclidean building
X
, notice that any of the type-preserving hyperbolic elements of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is strongly
I
-regular, for some
I\subsetneq S
, where
(W,S)
is the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system with
X
.

Lemma 3.17
.

(see
[
6
, Lemma 2.5]
)
Let
X
be a Euclidean building,
I\subsetneq S
, and
\ell
be a bi-infinite geodesic line in
X
. If
\ell
contains two special vertices that are contained in the respective interiors of two and opposite
I
-sectors in a common apartment of
X
, then
\ell
is strongly
I
-regular. Conversely, if
\ell
is strongly
I
-regular and contains three special vertices, then at least two of them must be contained in the interiors of two opposite
I
-sectors of a common apartment.

Proof.

Let
v,v^{\prime}\in\ell
be special vertices, respectively contained in the interiors of two opposite
I
-sectors
s,s^{\prime}
with the same base point, and which are contained in some apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
X
. Then
\mathcal{A}
contains the geodesic segment
[v,v^{\prime}]
, which cannot be parallel to any of the boundary-walls of the closure of the
I
-sectors
s,s^{\prime}
. Extend this geodesic segment
[v,v^{\prime}]
to a bi-infinite geodesic line
\ell^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\ell^{\prime}
is contained in the union of the open sectors
s,s^{\prime}
, and
\ell^{\prime}
is strongly
I
-regular. Moreover,
[v,v^{\prime}]\subset\ell\cap\ell^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an apartment of
X
containing
\ell
. Then both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
contain the special vertices
v,v^{\prime}
, that are in particular simplices. Thus, by the last axiom of the definition of a building, there exists an isomorphism between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
which fixes
v,v^{\prime}
pointwise, thus also the unique geodesic
[v,v^{\prime}]
between them. In particular,
\ell^{\prime}
is sent to
\ell
, which makes
\ell
strongly
I
-regular as well.

The converse statement is straightforward.
∎

3.4
Existence of strongly
I
-regular elements

In this section we generelize the existence of strongly regular hyperbolic elements from
[
6
, Section 2.2]
to the strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic ones. But first we need some preparation. Although the results and the proofs in this section about strongly
I
-regular elements are very similar with the ones in
[
6
]
for strongly regular elements, we rewrite them for the reader convenience. The convergence in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
is to be understood with respect to the cone topology, which turns
\Delta\cup\partial_{\infty}\Delta
into a compact space (see
[
3
, Chap.II.8]
).

Lemma 3.18
.

(See also
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
)

Let
(W_{aff},S_{aff})
be an irreducible Euclidean Coxeter system,
\mathcal{A}
be the associated Euclidean Coxeter complex,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system, and
I\subsetneq S
. Then
W_{aff}
contains strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic elements.

Proof.

Recall that
W_{aff}
acts transitively on the set of special vertices of
(W_{aff},S_{aff})
that are in the same equivalence class (i.e. of the same type). In particular, the translation group of
W_{aff}
acts simple-transitively on the above-mentioned set of special vertices. Now let a special vertex
x
in
\mathcal{A}
and consider two opposite
I
-sectors of
\mathcal{A}
with the same base point
x
. By the construction of the corresponding Coxeter complex associated with
(W_{aff},S_{aff})
, there are two special vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
in the same equivalence class which are contained in the interiors of the two chosen opposite
I
-sectors, respectively. Then the unique geodesic line
\ell
through
v
and
v^{\prime}
is contained in the flat generated by the two chosen opposite
I
-sectors, and so
\ell
is strongly
I
-regular. Then the unique translation
t
mapping
v
to
v^{\prime}
is an element of
W_{aff}
having
\ell
as a translation axis. Thus
t
is strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic by Lemma
3.15
.
∎

The following basic construction is a key step in proving the existence of strongly
I
-regular elements in groups acting on Euclidean buildings.

Lemma 3.19
.

(See also
[
6
, Lemma 2.7]
)

Let
X
be a locally finite thick Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system,
I\subsetneq S
, and
G
a type-preserving subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, with cocompact action on
X
. Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic map such that
\ell:=\rho(\mathbb{R})
is a strongly
I
-regular geodesic line, and
C>0
be such that
\rho(nC)
is a special vertex of a given type, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then there exist a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
in
G
and an increasing sequence
f(n)
of positive integers such that for every
m\geq 0
and every
r\in[-m,m]
, the sequence
n\mapsto g_{n}\circ\rho({f(n)C}+r)
is constant on
\{n\;|\;n\geq m\}
. Moreover, the map

\rho^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X:r\mapsto\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}\circ\rho({f(n)}C+r)

is a geodesic line, and its image
\rho^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is a strongly
I
-regular geodesic line.

Proof.

Set
t_{n}=nC
and
x_{n}=\rho(nC)
.
Since
G
is type-preserving and with cocompact action on
X
, up to replacing
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
by a subsequence, we can find a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subset G
such that for every
n\geq 0
,
g_{n}(x_{n})
is the special vertex
v_{0}
in a fixed fundamental domain for the
G
-action on
X
.

Since
X
is locally finite, any ball around
v_{0}
contains finitely many special vertices. On the other hand, for any
n\geq m\geq 0
, the geodesic segment
g_{n}\circ\rho([t_{n}-m,t_{n}+m])
, which is of length
2m
, contains
\lfloor\frac{2m}{C}\rfloor
special vertices, of the same type, including
v_{0}
. Therefore, we may extract subsequences to ensure that for every
m\geq 0
and every
r\in[-m,m]
, the sequence
n\mapsto g_{n}\circ\rho(t_{n}+r)
is constant on
\{n\;|\;n\geq m\}
. By construction, the map
\rho^{\prime}\colon r\mapsto\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}\circ\rho(t_{n}+r)
is well defined. Moreover it is a limit of a sequence of geodesic maps converging uniformly on compact sets, and thus
\rho^{\prime}
is a geodesic. That
\ell^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
is strongly
I
-regular follows from Lemma
3.17
.
∎

Lemma 2.8 from
[
6
]
remains the same.

The next proposition is again a technical version of Theorem
1.1
, and it is stated separately to ease future references.

Proposition 3.20
.

(See also
[
6
, Prop. 2.9]
)

Let
X
be a locally finite thick Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system,
I\subsetneq S
, and
G
a type-preserving subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, with cocompact action on
X
. Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic map such that
\ell:=\rho(\mathbb{R})
is a strongly
I
-regular geodesic line, and let
C>0
be such that
\rho(nC)
is a special vertex of a given type for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then there is an increasing sequence
f(n)
of positive integers such that, for any
n>m>0
, there is a strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic element
h_{m,n}\in G
which has a translation axis containing the geodesic segment
[\rho(f(m)C),\rho(f(n)C)]
.

Proof.

Let
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subset G
and
f(n)\in\mathbb{N}
be the sequences afforded by Lemma
3.19
. Fix
m>0
. Then by Lemma
3.19
, we know that for every
r\in[-m,m]
, the map
n\mapsto g_{n}\circ\rho({f(n)C}+r)
is constant on
\{n\;|\;n\geq m\}
. In particular, for every
n>m
, the element
h_{m,n}=g_{m}^{-1}g_{n}\in G
has the property that

h_{m,n}\circ\rho(f(n)C+r)=\rho(f(m)C+r)

for any
r\in[-m,m]
. Therefore
[
6
, Lemma 2.8]
ensures that
h_{m,n}
is hyperbolic and has a translation axis passing through
\rho(f(m)C)
and
\rho(f(n)C)
. This axis must therefore be strongly
I
-regular by Lemma
3.17
, and we conclude that
h_{m,n}
is strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic in view of Lemma
3.15
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
X
is thick, the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
consisting of type-preserving automorphisms is of finite index. In particular,
G
has a finite index subgroup acting by type-preserving automorphisms, and whose action remains cocompact. There is thus no loss of generality in assuming that the
G
-action is type-preserving.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an apartment in
X
. By Lemma
3.18
, the affine Weyl group of
X
acting on
\mathcal{A}
contains strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic elements. Any point, and so also any special vertex, of the apartment
\mathcal{A}
belongs to a translation axis of such a fixed hyperbolic element. It follows there exist a geodesic map
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{A}
and some

such that
\ell=\rho(\mathbb{R})
is strongly
I
-regular and that
\rho(nC)
is a special vertex for all

. The desired conclusion now follows from Proposition
3.20
.
∎

3.5
Dynamics of strongly
I
-regular elements

Since the visual boundary of
\mathrm{Min}(g)
of a strongly
I
-regular element
g
of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
is generally not reduced to the visual boundary of a flat, or an affine apartment, the dynamics of such element
g
on the ideal points outside
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(g)
is not very clear, and therefore, the same result as
[
6
, Prop. 2.10]
cannot be expected. Still, if we consider points in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
with specific properties, then
[
6
, Prop. 2.10]
will become true.

We recall the following proposition which is a key ingredient in this section.

Proposition 3.21
.

(See
[
1
, Prop. 4.106]
)
Let
c
and
d
be chambers in a spherical building. If
c
and
d
are not opposite, then there is a panel (i.e. facet)
p<c
such that
c=\mathrm{proj}_{p}(d)
.

Recall that a residue is a set of chambers of a spherical/affine building with specific properties (see
[
1
, Section 5.3.1.]
). In this section the residues are seen from the perspective of the finite Weyl group
(W,S)
, and not with respect to the walls of the affine Weyl group. Thus, two opposite simplices in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
might have different types.

Remark 3.22
.

Let
X
be an irreducible spherical building of associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system
(W,S)
. Let
I,J\subsetneq S
, and
\mathcal{R}
, respectively
\mathcal{S}
, residue of type
I
, respectively
J
, in
X
. Then, by
[
1
, Lemma 5.29]
, the set
\delta(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S})
is a double coset
W_{I}wW_{J}
, for some
w\in W
. Also, by
[
1
, Prop. 5.36(2)]
, there is a unique element
w_{1}
in
W
such that
w_{1}=\min(\delta(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S}))
. Moreover,
w_{1}
has minimal length in the double coset
W_{I}wW_{J}
(see
[
1
, Section 5.3.2.]
). Then
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S})
is a residue of type
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}
, where
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}
should be seen as the intersection of the associated reflections from
W
in the corresponding Euclidean space associated with the finite Coxeter system
(W,S)
. Notice the following cases:

1.

If
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}=I
, then
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{R}
.

2.

If
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S})
will be a residue that consists of more than one chamber.

3.

If
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}=\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S})
will just be a chamber.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
X
be an irreducible spherical building of associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system
(W,S)
. Let
I,J\subsetneq S
, and
\mathcal{R}
, respectively
\mathcal{S}
, residue of type
I
, respectively
J
, in
X
such that
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
, seen as the intersection of two sets of chambers. Then for any chamber
c\in\mathcal{S}
, the set of all walls of the chamber
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(c)\in\mathcal{R}
sperating it from
c
does not contain any wall in
\mathcal{R}
corresponding to the elements of
W_{I}
.

Proof.

Take some
c\in\mathcal{S}
, and denote
f:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(c)\in\mathcal{R}
. Consider
c^{\prime}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{S}}(f)\in\mathcal{S}
, and by
[
1
, Prop. 5.34]
we have
d(c,f)=d(f,c^{\prime})+d(c^{\prime},c)
. Moreover, by a simple computation using the same
[
1
, Prop. 5.34]
, one has that
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(c^{\prime})=f\in\mathcal{R}
, as otherwise
f^{\prime}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(c^{\prime})\neq f
would have the property that
d(c,f^{\prime})<d(c,f)
, giving a contradiction. Then, by
[
1
, Prop. 5.36]
we know that

\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S})=\{f\in\mathcal{R}\;|\;\text{there is }c\in\mathcal{S}\text{ with }d(f,c)=d(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S})\},

(3)

where by definition
[
1
, Def. 5.35]

\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{S}):=\{\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(c)\;|\;c\in\mathcal{S}\}
.
Since
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}}(c^{\prime})=f
and
c^{\prime}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{S}}(f)
, equality (
3
) guarantees that
d(f,c^{\prime})=d(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S})
. Moreover, there is a unique element
w_{1}\in W
such that
w_{1}=\min(\delta(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S}))
, and moreover
w_{1}
has minimal length in the double coset
W_{I}wW_{J}=\delta(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S})
. In particular, since
\mathcal{R}\cap\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
, we have
w_{1}
is not the trivial element of
W
. This means that the set of all walls separating
f
from
c^{\prime}
, and thus from
c
, cannot contain any wall in
\mathcal{R}
corresponding to the elements of
W_{I}
, as otherwise
w_{1}=\delta(f,c^{\prime})
would not be minimal in
W_{I}w_{1}W_{J}
.
∎

In the next proposition, we use
\rho_{\mathcal{A},c_{-}}
to denote the retraction from the ideal chamber
c_{-}\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
to the apartment
\mathcal{A}
. We consider residues as sets of chambers.

Proposition 3.24
.

Let
\Delta
be a Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system,
I\subsetneq S
, and
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
a type-preserving, strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic element. Denote by
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
the minimal ideal simplices containing the attracting and repelling endpoints of
\gamma
in their interior, respectively. Let
\mathcal{R}_{-}
be the
I
-residue corresponding to
\sigma_{-}
. Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta\setminus\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
,
\beta
the minimal ideal simplex containing
\xi
in its interior whose type is denoted by
J
, and
\mathcal{S}
the
J
-residue corresponding to
\beta
.

Then, under the assumption that
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
contains at least one ideal chamber, the limit
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{n}(\xi)
exists (in the cone topology), and coincides with
\rho_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime},c_{-}}(\xi)
, for any
c_{-}\in\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
and any apartment
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with
c_{-},\sigma_{+}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta\setminus\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. We are under the assumption that
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
contains at least one ideal chamber. Let
\mathcal{R}_{+}
be the
I
-residue corresponding to
\sigma_{+}
, thus
\mathcal{R}_{+}
and
\mathcal{R}_{-}
are opposite.

We have two cases to treat, when
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})
is just a chamber, and when
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})
is a residue with more than one chamber. The latter case is possible only when
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}\neq\emptyset
.

We also make the following remark. Let
f\in\mathcal{R}_{-}\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, and take
c:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(f)
, which is uniquely determined by
f
. Since
\gamma
fixes pointwise
f
, by uniqueness of
c
, the same is true for
c
, i.e.
\gamma(c)=c
pointwise. Consider now the combinatorial convex hull
\Gamma(f,c)
in the spherical building
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
.
\Gamma(f,c)
is also unique, contains every minimal galery between
c
and
f
, and is contained in any apartment of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
having both
c,f
as chambers (see
[
1
, Section 4.11.1]
). Then again by uniqueness, we have that
\gamma(\Gamma(f,c))=\Gamma(f,c)
pointwise.

Case 1:
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})
is just a chamber.
Take
c_{-}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, and consider
c:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(c_{-})
. Notice that
\sigma_{+}\subset c
, but
c
is not opposite
c_{-}
. Fix for what follows an apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
\Delta
such that
c_{-},c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
, and such that
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)\cap\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
(which is always true). Then
\Gamma(c_{-},c)\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
, but it might be the case that
\gamma
does not leave invariant
\mathcal{A}
, maybe just a convex part of it.

Consider an apartment
\mathcal{B}
of
\Delta
whose boundary contains
\xi
and
c_{-}
. Since
\xi\notin\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
we have
\xi\not\in c_{-}
. Moreover, notice that
\mathcal{B}
contains an ideal chamber
b
in its boundary such that
\xi\in b
and
d(c_{-},b)=d(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S})
.

Let
Q
be a closed sector in
\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}
with base point
x\in\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
and pointing towards
c_{-}
. Consider the combinatorial convex hull
\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)
in
\Delta
, that in entirely contained in
\mathcal{A}
. In particular,
\Gamma(c_{-},c)\subseteq\partial_{\infty}\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)
.

Since
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is convex and
c_{-}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, we have that
Q\subset\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)\subset\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. Let moreover
p\in Q
be a base point contained in the interior of
Q
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is convex, the geodesic ray
[p,\xi)
is entirely contained in
\mathcal{B}
. In particular, the ray
[p,\xi)
is not entirely contained in
Q
and hence there is some
q\in Q
such that
[p,\xi)\cap Q=[p,q]
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.23
, the set of all walls of
c_{-}
separating it from
b
does not contain any of the walls correspondig to the elements in
W_{I}
. So the point
q
lies in at least one of the walls of
Q
that are different from the ones corresponding to elements of
W_{I}
. But
q
is not contained in any of the walls corresponding to elements of
W_{I}
.

Now, because
p
lies in the interior of
Q
, it follows that the segment
[p,q]
is of positive length. Therefore, in the apartment
\mathcal{A}
, the geodesic segment
[p,q]
can be extended uniquely to a geodesic ray emanating from
p
; in other words, there is a unique boundary point
\eta\in\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
such that the segment
[p,q]
is contained in the ray
[p,\eta)
.

We claim that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{n}(\xi)=\eta
, and
\eta=\rho_{\mathcal{A},c_{-}}(\xi)
, and that
\eta\in\Gamma(c_{-},c)\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
.

Indeed, that
\eta\in\Gamma(c_{-},c)\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
follows easily from the fact that

[p,q]\subset Q\subset\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)\subset\mathrm{Min}(\gamma),

convexity of
\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)\subset\mathcal{A}
, and the fact that
q
lies in at least one of the walls of
Q
that are different from the ones corresponding to elements of
W_{I}
.

For the rest of the claim we proceed as follows. Since
\gamma
is strongly
I
-regular with repelling simplex
\sigma_{-}
, and
\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)\subset\mathcal{A}\cap\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, we get that
\gamma(\Gamma(c_{-},c,x))=\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)
setwise. Then
Q\subset\gamma^{n}(Q)\subset\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)
for all
n>0
. Moreover
[\gamma^{n}(p),\gamma^{n}(q)]=[\gamma^{n}(p),\gamma^{n}(\xi))\cap\gamma^{n}(Q)
. Because
\gamma^{n}
acts on
\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)
as a Euclidean translation, it follows that
[\gamma^{n}(p),\gamma^{n}(q)]=\gamma^{n}([p,q])
is parallel to
[p,q]
(in the Euclidean sense), or even more, it can be the case that they both lie on a translation axis of
\gamma
in
\Gamma(c_{-},c,x)
. On the other hand, the apartment
\gamma^{n}(\mathcal{B})
contains both the ray
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))
and the ray
[\gamma^{n}(p),\gamma^{n}(\xi))
, which are parallel since they have the same endpoint
\gamma^{n}(\xi)
. It follows that
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))
contains
[p,q]
.

Our next important observation is the following. Recall that
\gamma
is strongly
I
-regular and
q
does not lie in any of the walls of
Q
parallel with the ones corresponding to the elements of
W_{I}
. Then, for any boundary wall
M
of
Q
that is not parallel with same wall from
W_{I}
, the distance between
M
and
\gamma^{n}(M)
grows linearly with
n
. This implies that the length of the geodesic segment
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))\cap\gamma^{n}(Q)
tends to infinity with
n
. In particular, so does the length of
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))\cap\mathcal{A}
. Notice that if
q
had lied in one of the walls corresponding to elements in
W_{I}
, then the segment
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))\cap\gamma^{n}(Q)
would have had constant distance.

We have thus shown that the geodesic ray
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))
contains
[p,q]
for all
n\geq 0
and also a subsegment of
\mathcal{A}
whose length tends to infinity with
n
. By the definition of
\eta
, this implies that
[p,\gamma^{n}(\xi))\cap[p,\eta)
is a segment whose length also tends to infinity with
n
. In particular we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{n}(\xi)=\eta
. Also, by the definition of the retraction
\rho_{\mathcal{A},c_{-}}
and by the construction above we have
\rho_{A,c_{-}}(\xi)=\eta
. The claim stands proven.

To finish the proof of the proposition in the considered case, we notice that the choice of the apartment
\mathcal{A}
does not matter, because for any other apartment
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
of
\Delta
such that
c_{-},c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, we have that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cap\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
and
\Gamma(c_{-},c)\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Case 2:
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})
is a residue with more than one chamber.
By Remark
3.22
, this will be the case only when
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}\neq\emptyset
.

Take
c_{-}\in\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, and consider
c:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(c_{-})
. Proceed as in Case 1, for
c_{-},c
and any apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
\Delta
such that
c_{-},c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
. As
I\cap w_{1}Jw_{1}^{-1}\neq\emptyset
represent reflections with respect to the walls of the affine building
\Delta
, we have that
\mathcal{S}
contains a sub-residue laying in the same set of walls in
\Delta
as a sub-residue of
\mathcal{R}_{-}
. Then we get that the segment
[p,q]
is parallel with at least one of the walls corresponding to the elements of
W_{I}
. Still,
q
does not lie in any of the walls of
Q
corresponding to the elements of
W_{I}
. Then at the end of the day, one can notice that
\eta
is a precise point in the closure of the combinatorial convex hull
\Gamma(c_{-},c)
in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, and
\eta
does not depend on the choice of
c_{-}
in
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})
or of the apartment
\mathcal{A}
.

If one takes
c_{-}\in\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})
such that
c_{-}\notin\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, then for
c:=\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(c_{-})
we have that
\{\gamma^{n}(c)\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is still a set of chambers in
\mathcal{R}_{+}
, but the their dynamics is chaotic with no clear convergent pattern.
∎

Remark 3.25
.

For a
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta\setminus\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
that does not satisfy the conditions of Proposition
3.24
, i.e.
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{R}_{-}}(\mathcal{S})\cap\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma))=\emptyset
, it might be the case that the set
\{\gamma^{n}(\xi)\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
will rotate around either the repelling endpoint of
\gamma
, or some translation axis of
\gamma
, without a clear convergent pattern.

Remark 3.26
.

Since two disjoint, opposite open sectors (not necessarily with a common base point) that are translated along an axis that is parallel to some of the boundary walls of both of the sectors might still have an empty intersection after applying that translation, the proof of
[
6
, Prop. 2.11]
cannot work for strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic elements.

Remark 3.27
.

For split semi-simple algebraic groups
G
over non-Archimedean local fields, if one considers strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic elements
\gamma
from a maximal split torus of
G
, the dynamical behaviour of such
\gamma
, in the sense of Propositon
3.24
, is much nicer as both of the residues
\mathcal{R}_{-},\mathcal{R}_{+}
will be contained in
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
.

4
Applications to strongly
I
-regular elements

4.1
Some intermediate results

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system,
I\subsetneq S
, and
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
a type-preserving, strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic element. Consider the attracting and repelling endpoints
\xi_{+},\xi_{-}
of
\gamma
, and the minimal ideal simplices
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta
that contain those in their interior, respectively. Notice
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is contained in the combinatorial convex hull
Conv_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\operatorname{St}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\sigma_{+}),\operatorname{St}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\sigma_{-}))
. In general, that inclusion might be strict.

Fix a geodesic line
\ell
in
\mathcal{A}
such that
\ell
is a translation axis of
\gamma
. Thus, the endpoints
\xi_{+},\xi_{-}
of
\ell
are in the interior of
\sigma_{+}
,
\sigma_{-}
, repsectively. We can write
\ell=[\xi_{-},\xi_{+}]
. We want to understand the topology on

\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+}):=\{\sigma\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta\;|\;\sigma\text{ opposite }\sigma_{+}\}.

Notice that
\sigma_{-}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})
, also
\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})
is in natural bijection with
\mathrm{Opp}(\xi_{+})
, and
\xi_{-}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\xi_{+})
.

Remark 4.1
.

We claim
\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})\setminus\sigma_{-}
is not a subset of
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. Recall the convexity properties of
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
from
[
3
, Chapter II, Prop. 6.2, 6.8]
:
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is a closed convex set in
\Delta
, and
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is the union of all translation axes of
\gamma
. From those properties one can see that
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
is contained in
Conv_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\operatorname{St}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\sigma_{+}),\operatorname{St}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\sigma_{-}))
. In general, that inclusion might be strict. Thus, if there is a geodesic line
\ell
in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
with one endpoint
\xi_{+}
and which intersects, or it is parallel to, a translation axis of
\gamma
in a geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi_{+}
, then the other endpoint of the geodesic line
\ell
can only be
\xi_{-}
. Using that, one can deduce that appart from
\sigma_{-}
no element from
\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})\setminus\sigma_{-}
will be fixed by
\gamma
, and so
\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})\setminus\sigma_{-}\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)=\emptyset
.

Remark 4.2
.

Because the boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
is a compact space with respect to the cone topology on
\Delta\cup\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, and since the Grassmannian
G/P_{I_{\sigma_{+}}}
is also compact, one can deduce that
I_{\sigma_{+}}(\partial_{\infty}\Delta)
, which is isomorphic to
G/P_{I_{\sigma_{+}}}
, is also compact, and thus closed subset of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\sigma
be an ideal simplex in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and consider two apartments
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}
of
\Delta
such that
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{1}\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{2}
contains
\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{1})
. Then there is a common
\sigma
-cone in both
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
.

Proof.

For any ideal chamber
c
in
\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{1})
, the apartments
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}
have a sector in common corresponding to
c
; denote it by
Q_{x_{c}}(c)
, with
x_{c}
its base point. Now, since all
Q_{x_{c}}(c)
are in
\mathcal{B}_{1}\cap\mathcal{B}_{2}
, for any
c\in\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{1})
, by working in a Euclidean space, it is then easy to pick a point
x\in\mathcal{B}_{1}\cap\mathcal{B}_{2}
and for each
c\in\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial\mathcal{B}_{1})
to draw a sector with base point
x
and pointing towards
c
. Then we get our
\sigma
-cone in both
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
having base point
x
. The lemma is proven.
∎

For the rest of the section, and for simplicity,
we will suppose that the apartment
\mathcal{A}
is a subset of
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
. This is because for the applications that we have in mind this will be the case, and as mentioned before it is easier to handle the dynamics of such strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic elements
\gamma
.

In Lemma
3.4
we have proved that an apartment
\mathcal{B}
of
\Delta
, containing
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
in its ideal boundary, uniquely determines and is uniquely determined by
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
and
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})
. Then take
\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})\setminus\sigma_{-}
. To
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
there is associated a unique
\operatorname{St}(\sigma^{\prime}_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{St}_{\partial\Delta}(\sigma_{-})
such that
\operatorname{St}(\sigma^{\prime}_{-},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{St}(\sigma_{+},\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
are in the same apartment; this apartment is uniquely determined and denote it by
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma_{+},\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
. Now we apply Lemma
4.3
to the apartments
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma_{+},\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
. And so we deduce that
\ell\cap\mathcal{A}_{\sigma_{+},\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
is a ray, say
[y_{\sigma^{\prime}_{-}},\xi_{+})
, and denote by
\ell_{\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
the geodesic line in
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma_{+},\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
having endpoints
\xi^{\prime}_{-},\xi_{+}
and containing
[y_{\sigma^{\prime}_{-}},\xi_{+})
. Notice,
\xi^{\prime}_{-}
is opposite
\xi_{+}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{-}\in\sigma^{\prime}_{-}
. Since
\xi^{\prime}_{-}
and
\sigma^{\prime}_{-}
are not in
\partial_{\infty}\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
, when we apply
\gamma
to the line
\ell_{\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
, we get
\xi^{\prime}_{-}\neq\gamma(\xi^{\prime}_{-})
and
\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\neq\gamma(\sigma^{\prime}_{-})
. But still,
\gamma^{n}(\sigma^{\prime}_{-})\in\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})
and
\gamma^{n}(y_{\sigma^{\prime}_{-}})\in\ell
for every
n\in\mathbf{Z}
.

Remark 4.4
.

For
\gamma
a strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic element such that there is an apartment
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
we always have
\gamma(\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+}))=\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})
. This is also true without the assumption on the existence of the apartment
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
.

Thus, for every
\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\xi_{+})
, we can view some of the open neighborhoods of
\xi^{\prime}_{-}
with respect to the cone topology from the points of the fixed
\gamma
-translation axis
\ell
. This is possible because the cone topology does not depend on the chosen base point (see Proposition
3.8
). Pick a point
x\in\ell
, and
-r\in(x,\xi_{-})
. Then we take

V_{x,-r}:=\{\xi^{\prime}_{-}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\xi_{+})\;|\;[-r,x]\subset[x,\xi^{\prime}_{-})\cap\ell\}.

Lemma 4.5
.

For every
x\in\ell
and
-r_{1}\in(x,\xi_{-})
we have
\gamma(V_{x,-r_{1}})=V_{\gamma(x),\gamma(-r_{1})}
, and
V_{x,-r_{2}}\subset V_{\gamma(x),\gamma(-r_{1})}
for every
-r_{2}\in(\gamma(-r_{1}),\xi_{-})\cap(x,\xi_{-})
.

Proof.

The first part just follows from the definition.
The second part goes as follows. Since
\gamma(x)\in(x,\xi_{+})
and
\gamma(-r_{1})\in(-r_{2},\xi_{+})
, then the distance between
\gamma(x)
and
-r_{2}
is larger than the distance from
\gamma(x)
to
\gamma(-r_{1})
, and
[-r_{2},x]\subset(\xi_{-},\gamma(x))
. Thus for any
\xi^{\prime}_{-}\in V_{x,-r_{2}}
, the geodesic line
\ell_{\xi^{\prime}_{-}}
will contain the ray
(-r_{2},\xi_{+})
and thus by the definitions,
\xi^{\prime}_{-}
will be in
V_{\gamma(x),\gamma(-r_{1})}
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
x\in\ell
and
-r_{1}\in(x,\xi_{-})
. Take
\xi^{\prime}_{-}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\xi_{+})
. Then there is
N>0
such that
\xi^{\prime}_{-}\in\gamma^{n}(V_{x,-r_{1}})
for every
n>N
.

Proof.

We use the same notation as just before Remark
4.4
.
Pick some
y\in\ell\cap\ell_{\sigma^{\prime}_{-}}
, where
\xi^{\prime}_{-}\in\sigma^{\prime}_{-}
.
Since
\gamma^{n}(V_{x,-r_{1}})=V_{\gamma^{n}(x),\gamma^{n}(-r_{1})}
, and
\gamma^{n}([-r_{1},x])
converges to
\xi_{+}
with respect to the cone topology of
\Delta\cup\partial\Delta
when
n\to\infty
, one concludes there is indeed
N>0
such that
\gamma^{n}([-r_{1},x])\subset(y,\xi_{+})
, for every
n\geq N
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system,
I\subsetneq S
, and
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
a type-preserving, strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic element with attracting and repelling endpoints
\xi_{+},\xi_{-}
contained, respectively, in the interior of the minimal ideal opposite simplices
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
. Suppose there is an apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
\Delta
such that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathrm{Min}(\gamma)
and with
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
.

If
V_{\sigma_{-}}\subset\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})
is an open neighborhood of
\sigma_{-}
with respect to the cone topology on

, then
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{n}(V_{\sigma_{-}})=\mathrm{Opp}(\sigma_{+})
, where

is the type of
\sigma_{-}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
V_{\sigma_{-}}
is of the form
V_{x,-r}
, for some
x\in\ell
, and
-r\in(x,\xi_{-})
. Notice that such

is an open neighborhood of
\xi_{-}
, and thus of
\sigma_{-}
, with respect to the cone topology on

. Then the conclusion follows from Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite Euclidean building,
(W,S)
the associated irreducible spherical Coxeter system, and
I\subsetneq S
. Let
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
be two opposite ideal simplices with
\sigma_{+}
of type
I
. Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of strongly
I
-regular hyperbolic elements of
\Delta
such that
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
are the attracting and repelling minimal ideal simplices of
\gamma_{n}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose there is an apartment
\mathcal{A}
of
\Delta
such that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathrm{Min}(\gamma_{n})
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
. Assume that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(x,\gamma_{n}(x))=\infty

Characterizations of weighted Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable smoothness
Jae-Hwan Choi, Jin Bong Lee, Jinsol Seo, Kwan Woo

Abstract
. In this paper, we study different types of weighted Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable smoothness. The function spaces can be defined by means of the Littlewood-Paley theory in the field of Fourier analysis, while there are other norms arising in the theory of partial differential equations such as Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces. It is known that two norms are equivalent when one considers constant regularity function spaces without weights. We show that the equivalence still holds for variable smoothness and weights, which is accomplished by using shifted maximal functions, Peetre’s maximal functions, and the reverse Hölder inequality. Moreover, we obtain a weighted regularity estimate for time-fractional evolution equations and a generalized Sobolev embedding theorem without weights.

Introduction and main results

Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces have been crucial tools for measuring the smoothness of functions in the theory of partial differential equations(PDEs) and approximation theory.
Due to their importance, there are several equivalent norms of the spaces well-known in the literature.
Among those equivalent norms, two of them are frequently and separately used in Fourier analysis and fields of PDEs, which are given by the Littlewood-Paley theory and difference operators, respectively.

For instance, let us consider norms of Besov spaces
B_{2,2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is actually a Sobolev space.
Then, it follows from the Littlewood-Paley theory that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.1)

S_{0}
and

1.6

It is well-known the quantity (

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W_{2}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left(\int_{|h|\leq 1}\left|\frac{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{2}\left(f^{(n)}\right)\right\|_{L_{2}}}{|h|^{\alpha}}\right|^{2}~\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d}}\right)^{1/2},

\|f_{0}(\cdot+h)-f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{p}\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f_{0}(x+h)|^{p}|x|^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{-1}^{1}|x-h|^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\geq Nh^{\alpha}

we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|f_{0}(\cdot+h)-f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}}{|h|^{1+q\theta}}\mathrm{d}h\geq\int_{4}^{\infty}h^{q(\frac{\alpha}{p}-\theta)-1}\mathrm{d}h=+\infty,

and this implies (

).

To demonstrate that

\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\subsetneq B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),

we need to prove that the inclusion mapping

is not surjective.
To establish this, we assume, for the sake of contradiction, that
I
is surjective.
Then, by Proposition
2.9
and Lemma
3.1
,
I
is bounded linear and bijective.
Hence
[
20
, Theorem 5.10]
, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\lesssim\|I(g)\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}=\|g\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x).

(1.15)

However, under the assumption of Theorem
1.5
-
(i)
, (
1.15
) fails.
To verify this, put
f_{n}(x):=2^{n/p}f_{0}(2^{n}x)
.
Then for

\displaystyle\int_{|h|\leq 1}\frac{\|f_{n}(\cdot+h)-f_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}}{|h|^{1+q\theta}}\mathrm{d}h=2^{qn\left(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)}\int_{|h|\leq 2^{n}}\frac{\|f_{0}(\cdot+h)-f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}}{|h|^{1+q\theta}}\mathrm{d}h.

(1.16)

Moreover,

\begin{gathered}\|f_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}=2^{-\frac{n\alpha}{p}}\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\\
\|f_{n}\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|\Delta_{j}f_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}=2^{n\left(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)}\|f_{0}\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.\end{gathered}

(1.17)

Therefore, by (
1.15
), (
1.16
) and (
1.17
)

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{n\alpha}{p}}\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+2^{n\left(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)}\left(\int_{4}^{2^{n}}|h|^{q(\frac{\alpha}{p}-\theta)-1}\mathrm{d}h\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{n\alpha}{p}}\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+2^{n\left(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)}\left(\int_{|h|\leq 2^{n}}\frac{\|f_{0}(\cdot+h)-f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}}{|h|^{1+q\theta}}\mathrm{d}h\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{n}\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\simeq 2^{-\frac{n\alpha}{p}}\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+2^{n\left(\theta-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)}\|f_{0}\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

The last equivalence follows from Proposition
2.9
.
This certainly implies

+\infty=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{4}^{2^{n}}|h|^{q(\frac{\alpha}{p}-\theta)-1}\mathrm{d}h\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

which contradicts the fact that
f_{0}
is in
\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

For mathematical completeness in function space theory, one might be curious about some analogous version of the equivalence results in Theorem

•

Lemma 2.4

,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(w)\min(|\rho|,1)^{L}\big(1+|\rho|\big)^{\frac{d}{p}\left(\frac{p}{r}+\frac{1}{s}-1\right)}\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(w)\min(2^{j}|\rho|,1)^{L}\big(1+2^{j}|\rho|\big)^{\frac{d}{p}\left(\frac{p}{r}+\frac{1}{s}-1\right)}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)},

and

Proof.

The proof of the first inequality is nothing but taking

Hence we only consider

For a smooth function

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\rho}F(x)

\displaystyle=F(x+\rho)-F(x)

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{1}D_{x_{1}}F+\cdots\rho_{d}D_{x_{d}}F\big)(x+t\rho)\,\mathrm{d}t

By using (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f(x)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,L}|\rho|^{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}=L\end{subarray}}\int_{[0,1]^{L}}\big|\big(D_{x}^{\alpha}\Delta_{j}f\big)\big(x+(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{L})\rho\big)\big|\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}t_{L}\,,

Take a Schwartz function

Since

\big|D_{x}^{\alpha}\phi\big(x)|\lesssim_{\alpha,d}(1+|x|)^{-100d}

\displaystyle|D_{x}^{\alpha}\Delta_{j}f(x+\rho)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,d}\,

\displaystyle 2^{jL}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Delta_{j}f(y)|\frac{2^{jd}}{(1+2^{j}|x-y+\rho|)^{100d}}~\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}

\displaystyle 2^{jL}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|\vec{n}|)^{-100d}2^{jd}\int_{(x+\rho+2^{-j}\vec{n})+I_{j}}|f(y)|~\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{jL}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|\vec{n}|)^{-100d}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{d}}2^{jd}\int_{(\rho+2^{-j}\vec{n})+I_{j,k}}|f(y)|~\mathrm{d}y,

where

and
\{I_{j,k}\}_{k=1,\dots,N_{d}}
is a collection of all dyadic cubes such that
l(I_{j,k})=l(I_{j})
and
(x+I_{j})\cap I_{j,k}\neq\emptyset
.
Thus we have

|D_{x}^{\alpha}\Delta_{j}f(x+\rho)|\lesssim_{\alpha,d}2^{jL}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|\vec{n}|)^{-100d}\mathcal{M}^{2^{j}\rho+\vec{n}}\left(\Delta_{j}f\right)(x)

and by Proposition
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|D_{x}^{\alpha}\Delta_{j}f(\,\cdot+\rho)\|_{L_{p}(w)}\lesssim N(w)2^{jL}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|\vec{n}|)^{-100d}(1+2^{j}|\rho|+|\vec{n}|)^{\frac{d}{r}+\frac{d}{ps}-\frac{d}{p}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\,.

(2.12)

With help of (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(w)\big(2^{j}|\rho|\big)^{L}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{(1+|\vec{n}|+2^{j}|L\rho|)^{\frac{d}{r}+\frac{d}{ps}-\frac{d}{p}}}{(1+|\vec{n}|)^{100d}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim N(w)\big(2^{j}|\rho|\big)^{L}\big(1+2^{j}|\rho|\big)^{\frac{d}{r}+\frac{d}{ps}-\frac{d}{p}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)},

and note that
\frac{d}{r}+\frac{d}{ps}-\frac{d}{p}\leq d
.

On the other hand, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{L}\binom{L}{k}(-1)^{L-k}\Delta_{j}f(x+k\rho).

For each term in the above, one can check from (
2.12
) that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}f(x+k\rho)\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(w)(1+2^{j}k|\rho|)^{\frac{d}{r}+\frac{d}{ps}-\frac{d}{p}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\,,

which implies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\lesssim N(w)(1+2^{j}|\rho|)^{\frac{d}{r}+\frac{d}{ps}-\frac{d}{p}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

(2.14)

The lemma is proved by a combination of (
2.13
) and (
2.14
).

2.4.
Peetre’s maximal functions

Peetre’s maximal functions are defined by

M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\Delta_{j}f(x-y)|}{(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\lambda}},\quad M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|S_{0}f(x-y)|}{(1+|y|)^{\lambda}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(i)

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{\varphi})\subseteq B_{2}(0)
, there exists
N=N(d,r)>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\varphi(x-z)|}{(1+|z|)^{d/r}}\lesssim_{d,r}(\mathcal{M}(|\varphi|^{r})(x))^{1/r}.

(ii)

For any
\lambda>0
and multi-index
\alpha
,

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}\Delta_{j}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\lambda,\alpha}2^{j|\alpha|}M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x),

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}S_{0}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\lambda,\alpha}M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x).

(iii)

For any
r\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle M_{j,d/r}^{*}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,r}(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(x))^{1/r},

\displaystyle M_{d/r}^{0,*}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,r}(\mathcal{M}(|S_{0}f|^{r})(x))^{1/r}

(iv)

For any
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
L\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,L}\min(1,|\rho|2^{j})^{L}(1+|\rho|2^{j})^{\lambda}M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}S_{0}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,L}\min(1,|\rho|)^{L}(1+|\rho|)^{\lambda}M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x)

(v)

Suppose that
f
is also a locally integrable function.
For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle|S_{0}f(x)|+|\Delta_{j}f(x)|\lesssim_{d}\mathcal{M}f(x).

Proof.

(i)

This is an easy consequence of
[
22
, Theorem 1.3.1-1]
with the fact that
(1+|z|)^{d/r}\simeq 1+|z|^{d/r}
.

(ii)

By an almost orthogonality of
\Delta_{j}
,

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{x}\Delta_{j}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Delta_{j}f(x-y)|\sum_{k=-1}^{1}|D^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{2^{j+k}}(y)|~\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\lambda}\sum_{k=-1}^{1}|D^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{2^{j+k}}(y)|~\mathrm{d}y.

Clearly,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+2^{j}|y|)^{\lambda}\sum_{k=-1}^{1}|D^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{2^{j+k}}(y)|~\mathrm{d}y=:c2^{j|\alpha|},

and we remark that
c
is independent of
j
.
Similarly,

|D^{\alpha}_{x}S_{0}f(x)|\leq M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x).

(iii)

Let
g_{j}(x):=\Delta_{j}f(2^{-j}x)
. Then

\widehat{g_{j}}(\xi):=2^{jd}\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\widehat{f}(2^{j}\xi),\quad\widehat{S_{0}f}(\xi):=\widehat{\Phi}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

Thus
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{g_{j}}),\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{S_{0}f})\subseteq B_{2}(0)
.
Then by the first assertion,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|g_{j}(x-z)|}{(1+|z|)^{d/r}}

\displaystyle\leq N(\mathcal{M}(|g_{j}|^{r})(x))^{1/r},

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|S_{0}f(x-z)|}{(1+|z|)^{d/r}}

\displaystyle=M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x)\leq N(\mathcal{M}(|S_{0}f|^{r})(x))^{1/r}.

This implies that

\displaystyle M_{j,d/r}^{*}f(2^{-j}x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\Delta_{j}f(2^{-j}x-z)|}{(1+2^{j}|z|)^{d/r}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\Delta_{j}f(2^{-j}x-2^{-j}z)|}{(1+|z|)^{d/r}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|g_{j}(x-z)|}{(1+|z|)^{d/r}}

\displaystyle\leq N(\mathcal{M}(|g_{j}|^{r})(x))^{1/r}=N(\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})(2^{-j}x))^{1/r}.

(iv)

Due to (
2.11
) and the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}
, we have

|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f(x)|\leq\min\Big(|\rho|^{L}\sup_{|z|\leq L|\rho|}\sum_{|\alpha|=L}|D^{\alpha}_{x}\Delta_{j}f(x-z)|,\sup_{|z|\leq L|\rho|}|\Delta_{j}f(x-z)|\Big).

By the second assertion of the lemma,

|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}\Delta_{j}f(x)|\leq N\min(1,|\rho|2^{j})^{L}\sup_{|z|\leq L|\rho|}M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x-z).

Since

\sup_{|z|\leq L|\rho|}M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x-z)\leq N(1+2^{j}|\rho|)^{\lambda}M_{j,\lambda}^{*}f(x),

we obtain the result.
Similarly,

|\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{L}S_{0}f(x)|\leq N\min(1,|\rho|)^{L}\sup_{|z|\leq L|\rho|}M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x-z)

and

\sup_{|z|\leq L|\rho|}M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x-z)\leq N(1+|\rho|)^{\lambda}M_{\lambda}^{0,*}f(x).

(v)

Note that
\Phi
from (
1.6
) is in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence it follows that

\displaystyle|S_{0}f(x)|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}|f(x-y)\Phi(y)|~\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{2^{k}}(0)\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}(0)}}|f(x-y)\Phi(y)|~\mathrm{d}y

and

|\Phi(y)|+|y|^{d+1}|\Phi(y)|\leq N.

This certainly implies that
|S_{0}f|\leq N\mathcal{M}f
. For
\Delta_{j}
, the change of variable formula yields

\Delta_{j}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-y)\psi_{2^{j}}(y)~\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-2^{-j}y)\psi(y)~\mathrm{d}y.

Using the similar arguments, we have
|\Delta_{j}f(x)|\leq N\mathcal{M}f(x)
. The lemma is proved.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

The spaces

and

are continuously embedded into

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\min(\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}).

Proof.

One can observe that

\displaystyle f=S_{0}f+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{j}f.

By Minkowski’s inequality,

If
q=1
or
q=\infty
, then it is clear.
For
q\in(1,\infty)
we apply Hölder’s inequality and obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{q\delta j}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}

\delta>0
.
Then it is clear from (
2.4
) that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

2^{\varepsilon j}\lesssim\frac{\phi(2^{j})}{\phi(1)},

(2.16)

and therefore

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

For

, if
q\in(1,\infty)
, then by Hölder’s inequality,

|f|\leq N\Big(|S_{0}f|+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{q\delta j}|\Delta_{j}f|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big)

for any
\delta>0
. By (
2.16

The lemma is proved.

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\{2^{j(M-\varepsilon)}\}_{j\leq 1}\|_{\ell^{q}}\sup_{j\leq 0}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\|\Delta_{1}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

This certainly implies that

X_{0}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

When
X=F
, we apply Lemma
2.7
.
Let
T_{j}:=\Delta_{j}
and
f_{j}:=\phi(2^{j})f
in Lemma
2.7
.
Since
|\Delta_{j}f(x)|\leq N\mathcal{M}(f)(x)
, one verify that
\{T_{j}\}_{j\leq 0}
is linearizable and satisfies (
2.15
).
Then by Lemma
2.7
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\leq 0}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|\Delta_{j}f\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\leq 0}\left|T_{j}f_{j}\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\leq 0}\left|f_{j}\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)},

where the last inequality follows from
\phi(2^{j})\lesssim 2^{j(M-\varepsilon)}
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\simeq X_{1}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\inf\Big(\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|f-f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big),

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\inf\Big(\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|f-f_{j}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)},

where the infimum runs over all sequences of functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(w)
such that

f=\lim_{j\to\infty}f_{j}\,\,\text{in}\,\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}_{j})\subset B_{2^{j+1}}(0).

Step 1.

X_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}
.

In this step, by the similarity, we only prove for
X=B
.
From the support condition of
\widehat{f_{j}}
, we have

f_{j}:=S_{0}f+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\Delta_{k}f,\quad f_{0}:=S_{0}f,

and

f-f_{j}=\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}f,

Then by Minkowski’s inequality, it follows that

\|f-f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

For
q\in[1,\infty)
, we apply Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|f-f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left(\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}2^{q\delta(k-j-1)}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{k})^{q}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(k-j-1)}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q},

where
\delta=0
if
q=1
and
\delta>0
if
q\in(1,\infty)
. If we choose
\varepsilon>\delta\geq 0
and use Fubini’s theorem, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{k})^{q}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(k-j-1)}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\phi(2^{k})^{q}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(k-j-1)}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{k})^{q}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\leq N\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}^{q}.

For
q=\infty
, we simply have

\begin{gathered}\phi(2^{j})\|f-f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}2^{\varepsilon(j-k)}\phi(2^{k})\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\phi(2^{k})\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}.\end{gathered}

This implies that

B_{1}\leq N\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

Step 2.

\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim X_{1}
.

First we consider
X=B
. Similar to the previous case, for
k\geq 2
one can observe that

\Delta_{k}f=\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}(f_{k+j}-f_{k+j-1}).

Since
\Delta_{j}f
is less than a constant multiple of
\mathcal{M}f
, we use the weighted
L_{p}
boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function so that

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

Take
\delta=0
if
q=1
and
\delta>0
if
q\in(1,\infty)
.
For
q\in[1,\infty)
, we apply Hölder’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\phi(2^{k})^{q}\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\phi(2^{k})^{q}\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}2^{q\delta(j-k)}\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(j-k)}\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle=N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2}^{j+1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(j-k)}\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle=N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q},

where
\varepsilon>\delta\geq 0
.
For
q=\infty
, we simply have

\phi(2^{k})\|\Delta_{k}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}2^{\varepsilon(k-j)}\phi(2^{j})\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\phi(2^{j})\|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

It is left to control
\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}
and
\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}
.
One can observe that

\displaystyle\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\|\Delta_{1}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\|f-f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|f-f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|f-f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for all sequence of functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
and this certainly implies that

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim X_{1}.

Now, we suppose
X=F
.
Similar to (
4.9
), for
q\in(1,\infty)
we have

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\phi(2^{k})^{q}|\Delta_{k}f|^{q}\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\sum_{k=2}^{j+1}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(j-k)}|\Delta_{k}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})|^{q}.

Then
|\Delta_{k}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})|\leq N\mathcal{M}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\sum_{k=2}^{j+1}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(j-k)}|\Delta_{k}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\mathcal{M}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})|^{q}\sum_{k=2}^{j+1}2^{q(\delta-\varepsilon)(j-k)}

\displaystyle=N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\mathcal{M}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})|^{q}.

Let
T_{j}:=\mathcal{M}
and
G_{j}:=\phi(2^{j})(f_{j}-f_{j-1})
in Lemma
2.7
.
Note that
\{T_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is linearizable and satisfies (
2.15
).
Thus by Lemma
2.7
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|\mathcal{M}(f_{j}-f_{j-1})\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|T_{j}G_{j}\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\left|G_{j}\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

Hence

\displaystyle\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|f_{j}-f_{j-1}\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)},

for all sequence of functions
\{f_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
and this certainly implies that

\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim F_{1}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\simeq X_{2}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

Step 1.

\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim X_{2}
.

Since
|S_{0}f|\leq N\mathcal{M}(f)
, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|\Delta_{j}f\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

First we prove for
X=B
.
To do so, we define

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}:=\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\psi_{2^{i}t}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\quad\text{so that}\quad\Delta_{j}f=N\Delta_{j}(\Psi_{j}\ast f).

(4.10)

Indeed, (
4.10
) follows from
\Delta_{j}f=\Delta_{j}\left(\left(\psi_{t/2}+\psi_{t}+\psi_{2t}+\psi_{4t}\right)\ast f\right)
for all
t\in[2^{j-1},2^{j})
and
\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}t^{-1}~\mathrm{d}t=N
.
By making use of (
4.10
), we have for
p\in(1,\infty],q\in[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|\Psi_{j}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\|\psi_{2^{i}t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{i}t)^{q}\|\psi_{2^{i}t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq 4N\int_{1}^{\infty}\phi(t)^{q}\|\psi_{t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Note that the last line equals to

\displaystyle 4N\int_{0}^{1}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\|\psi_{1/t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

(4.11)

If
q=\infty
, then

\phi(2^{j})\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}=\phi(2^{j})\|\psi_{2^{j}}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq\sup_{t>0}\phi(t^{-1})\|\psi_{1/t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)},

and this implies that

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim B_{2},\quad\forall p\in(1,\infty],q\in[1,\infty].

For
X=F
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\Delta_{j}f|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{j}\ast f)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

By Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{j}\ast f)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\Phi_{j}\ast f|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\Phi_{j}\ast f|^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\psi_{2^{i}t}\ast f|^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\phi(2^{i}t)^{q}|\psi_{2^{i}t}\ast f|^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t)^{q}|\psi_{t}\ast f|^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Hence we have

\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim F_{2}.

Step 2.

X_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}
.

By Lemma
2.8
, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)},

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

We begin with
X=B
.
If
t<1/4
, then
\psi_{t}\ast f=\psi_{t}\ast S_{0}f
, hence we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/4}\phi(t)^{q}\left\|\psi_{t}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq N\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\int_{0}^{1/4}\phi(t)^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=N\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q},\quad\forall q\in[1,\infty)

and

\sup_{t\in(0,1/4)}\phi(t)\|\psi_{t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq\sup_{t\in(0,1/4)}\phi(t)\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}\leq N\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

Then, we make use of the following equality

\psi_{t}\ast f=\psi_{t}\ast(\Delta_{j-1}f+\Delta_{j}f+\Delta_{j+1}f+\Delta_{j+2}f),\quad\forall t\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}),j\geq-1

to obtain that for
q\in[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\int_{1/4}^{\infty}\phi(t)^{q}\left\|\psi_{t}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\phi(t)^{q}\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\|\Delta_{j+i}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}.

For
q=\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(t)\|\psi_{t}\ast f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq\phi(t)\sum_{i=-1}^{2}\|\Delta_{j+i}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{B_{p,\infty}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w,\mathrm{d}x)},\quad\forall t\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}),j\geq-1.

This certainly implies that

B_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)},\quad\forall p\in(1,\infty],q\in[1,\infty].

For
X=F
, we proceed in a similar manner.
Note that

\begin{gathered}|\psi_{t}\ast f|\leq N\mathcal{M}(S_{0}f),\quad\forall t\in(0,1/4),\\
|\psi_{t}\ast f|\leq N\mathcal{M}((\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1}+\Delta_{j+2})f),\quad\forall t\in[2^{j-1},2^{j}),j\geq-1,\end{gathered}

Then for
q\in(1,\infty)
we have

\int_{0}^{1/4}\phi(t)^{q}\left|\psi_{t}\ast f\right|^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\leq N\mathcal{M}(S_{0}f)

and

\displaystyle\int_{1/4}^{\infty}\phi(t)^{q}\left|\psi_{t}\ast f\right|^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\phi(t)^{q}\sum_{i=-1}^{2}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta_{j+i}f)|^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=-2}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta_{j}f)|^{q}.

With the help of Lemma
2.7
,

F_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

X_{2}\simeq X_{3}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}\ast f\right|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)},

where

\displaystyle\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\binom{L}{j}(-1)^{L-j}\psi_{\frac{1}{jt}}(y).

(4.12)

B_{2}\lesssim B_{3}
:

Observe that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\psi}_{t}^{L}(2^{k}\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\binom{L}{j}(-1)^{L-j}=(-1)^{L+1},

and

\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{\psi}_{t})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{\psi}^{L}_{t})=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(2L)^{-1}t\leq|\xi|\leq 2t\}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f=(-1)^{L+1}\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast(\psi_{\frac{2}{t}}^{L}+\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}+\psi_{\frac{1}{2t}}^{L})\ast f.

(4.13)

By Lemma
2.5
, we also have

\displaystyle|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f|\lesssim\left(\left|\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\frac{2}{t}}^{L}\ast f)\right|+\left|\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}\ast f)\right|+\left|\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\frac{1}{2t}}^{L}\ast f)\right|\right).

(4.14)

Using (
4.14
), one can check that for
p\in(1,\infty]
and
q\in[1,\infty]

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This proves

B_{2}\lesssim B_{3}.

F_{2}\lesssim F_{3}
:

Note that for
2^{j-1}<t<2^{j}

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{\frac{1}{t}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{L+1}\widehat{\psi}_{\frac{1}{t}}\left(\widehat{\psi}_{2^{-j-2}}^{L}+\widehat{\psi}_{2^{-j-1}}^{L}+\widehat{\psi}_{2^{-j}}^{L}+\widehat{\psi}_{2^{-j+1}}^{L}+\widehat{\psi}_{2^{-j+2}}^{L}\right),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\psi_{2^{j}}^{L}

\displaystyle=N\psi_{2^{j}}^{L}\ast\left(\sum_{i=-2}^{2}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\psi_{2^{i}t}^{L}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right).

(4.16)

The inequality (
4.15
) is nothing but (
4.13
),
and the inequality (
4.16
) is a variant of (
4.10
).
Then by making use of (
4.15
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-j})^{q}\left(\mathcal{M}(\psi_{-j}^{L}\ast f)(x)\right)^{q}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L_{p}(w)
, we apply Lemma
2.7
and obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-j})^{q}\left|\psi_{2^{-j}}^{L}\ast f\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|\psi_{2^{j}}^{L}\ast f\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

Then we apply (
4.16
) to the last line of (
4.17
).

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\left|\psi_{2^{j}}^{L}\ast f\right|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

(4.18)

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\Big|\psi_{2^{j}}^{L}\ast\sum_{-2\leq i\leq 2}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\psi_{2^{i}t}^{L}\ast f~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\Big|\mathcal{M}\Big(\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\psi_{t}^{L}\ast f~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)\Big|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

By Lemma
2.7
again to the last line of (
4.18
), it follows that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\Big|\mathcal{M}\Big(\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\psi_{t}^{L}\ast f~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)\Big|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\Big|\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\psi_{t}^{L}\ast f~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{j-1}}^{2^{j}}\phi(t)^{q}\left|\psi_{t}^{L}\ast f\right|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}\ast f\right|^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)},

where the last equality follows from summation over
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and the change of variable
t\to t^{-1}
.
Combining (
4.17
), (
4.18
), (
4.19
), we conclude

F_{2}\lesssim F_{3}.

The converse direction, verifying
X_{3}\lesssim X_{2}
, is rather simpler than showing
X_{2}\lesssim X_{3}
.
Let
|f|_{X}
denote
|f|_{B}
,
|f|_{F}
with
|f|_{B}=\|f\|_{L_{p}(w)}
and
|f|_{F}=|f(x)|
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}^{L}\ast f|_{X}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{L}\binom{L}{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}|\psi_{\frac{1}{jt}}\ast f|_{X}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f|_{X}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

Hence, we easily obtain
X_{3}\lesssim X_{2}
.
The lemma is proved.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\simeq X_{4},X_{5}
.

Proof.

Due to similarity, we only prove for
X_{4}
.
Recall that

\displaystyle B_{4}

\displaystyle=\|M_{d/r}^{0,*}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|M_{j,d/r}^{*}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(4.20)

\displaystyle F_{4}

\displaystyle=\|M_{d/r}^{0,*}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|M_{j,d/r}^{*}f|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)}.

(4.21)

By Lemma
2.5
(
2
),

|\Delta_{j}f(x)|\leq M_{j,d/r}^{*}f(x),\quad|S_{0}f(x)|\leq M_{d/r}^{0,*}f(x)\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}

and this certainly implies that

\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\lesssim X_{4}.

For the converse inequality, note that
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for
r\in(0,R_{w,p})
, the weight
w\in A_{p/r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then we apply Lemma
2.5
(
3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X_{p,q}^{\phi,4}(w)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}(|S_{0}f|^{r})^{1/r}\|_{L_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})^{\frac{1}{r}}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},&X=B\\
\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\mathcal{M}(|\Delta_{j}f|^{r})^{\frac{1}{r}}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(w)},&X=F.\end{array}\right.

Thus we have

The lemma is proved.
∎

Finally, we prove Theorem

and Proposition

.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By similarity, we only provide proof for the homogeneous Besov case.
It directly follows from the proof of Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
that

\|f\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi^{L}_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,,

where
\psi_{1/t}^{L}
is the function defined in (
4.12
).
Due to the proof of (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}\left\|\psi^{L}_{\frac{1}{t}}\ast f\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}~\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(t^{-1})^{q}|\psi_{1/t}(y)|\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\bigg)\big\|\mathcal{D}_{y}^{L}f\big\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y

is obtained.
Due to (

\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{0}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathring{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\,,

where

\|f\|_{\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{0}}:=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(|h|^{-1})^{q}\sup_{|\rho|\leq|h|}\|\mathcal{D}^{L}_{\rho}f\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d}}\right)^{1/q},\quad&q<\infty,\\

∎

We end this section with the proof of Proposition

Proof of Proposition
1.8
.

We first observe that for any multi-index
\alpha\in\big(\mathbb{N}_{0}\big)^{d}
and
x,\,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{L}_{h}(x^{\alpha}):=\sum_{k=0}^{L}\binom{L}{k}(-1)^{L-k}(x+kh)^{\alpha}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\quad|\alpha|<L\\[5.69054pt]
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta:L\leq|\beta|,\,\beta\leq\alpha\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha,\beta}x^{\alpha-\beta}h^{\beta}&\text{if}\quad|\alpha|\geq L\,,\end{cases}

(4.22)

for some nonzero constants
c_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Here, for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d})
,
\beta\leq\alpha
denotes that
\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}
for all
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
.
Indeed,

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}(x^{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta:\beta\leq\alpha}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{L}\binom{L}{k}(-1)^{L-k}k^{|\beta|}\right)\binom{\alpha}{\beta}x^{\alpha-\beta}h^{\beta},\quad\text{where}\,\,\,\,\binom{\alpha}{\beta}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\binom{\alpha_{i}}{\beta_{i}}\,,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{L}\binom{L}{k}(-1)^{L-k}k^{n}=\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{n}\left(\mathrm{e}^{t}-1\right)^{L}\right]_{t=0}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\quad n<L\\[5.69054pt]
\text{positive constant}&\text{if}\quad n\geq L\,.\end{cases}

It follows from (
4.22
) that if
P
is a polynomial with
\mathrm{deg}\,P<L
, then
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}P\equiv 0
for all
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which implies the first assertion in (
1.18
).
For the second assertion in (
1.18
), due to similarity, we consider only the case
\mathring{\mathscr{X}}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathring{\mathscr{B}}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
\mathrm{deg}\,P=:M\geq L
, then (
4.22
) implies that

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}P(x)=\sum_{i=L}^{M}Q_{i}(x,h)

where
Q_{i}(x,h)
is a polynomial satisfying that

Q_{i}(x,h)=|h|^{i}Q_{i}\big(x,h/|h|\big)\quad\forall\,\,x,\,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\qquad;\qquad Q_{M}\not\equiv 0\,.

Take
(x_{0},\sigma_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
\delta>0
such that

\inf_{(x,\sigma)\in E_{1}\times E_{2}}|Q_{M}(x,\sigma)|=:2\epsilon_{0}>0\,,

where
E_{1}=B_{\delta}(x_{0})
and
E_{2}=B_{\delta}(\sigma_{0})\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Put

N_{0}=\sup_{(x,\sigma)\in E_{1}\times E_{2}}\sum_{i=L}^{M-1}|Q_{i}(x,\sigma)|\quad\text{and}\quad R=:\frac{N_{0}}{\epsilon_{0}}\vee 1\,.

Then for any
x\in E_{1}
,
\sigma\in E_{2}
, and
r\geq R
,

|\mathcal{D}_{r\sigma}^{L}P(x)|\geq r^{M}|Q_{M}(x,\sigma)|-\sum_{i=L}^{M-1}r^{i}|Q_{i}(x,\sigma)|\geq r^{M}\bigg(2\epsilon_{0}-\frac{N_{0}}{R}\bigg)\geq\epsilon_{0}r^{M}\,.

Consequently, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|P\|_{\mathring{\mathscr{B}}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle:=\left\|\phi(|h|^{-1})\left\|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}P(x)\right\|_{L_{p}(w)}\right\|_{L_{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d};\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d}}\right)}

(4.23)

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\phi(|h|^{-1})\left\|\mathcal{D}_{r\sigma}^{L}P(x)\right\|_{L_{p}(E_{1};w\mathrm{d}x)}\right\|_{L_{q}\left(E_{2}\times[R,\infty);\frac{\mathrm{d}h}{|h|^{d}}\right)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(w(E_{1})\right)^{1/p}\left\|\phi(r^{-1})r^{M}\right\|_{L_{q}\left([R,\infty);\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\right)}\,.

Since
\phi\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,L)
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for any
r\in[R,\infty)
,
r^{M}\phi(r^{-1})\geq r^{L}\phi(r^{-1})\gtrsim r^{\epsilon}
(see (
2.4
)).
In addition,
w(E_{1})>0
because
w>0
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Therefore, the last term in (
4.23
) is infinite.
The proof is completed.
∎

In this section, we explore the applications of our main results.
We begin by introducing a notation.
The space

5.1.
Regularity theory for evolution equations

In this subsection, we offer some remarks into the weighted
L_{p}
-regularity theory for non-divergence form equations with initial data, as discussed in the research by
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
8
]
.
Specifically, we focus on the equation:

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=a^{ij}(t,x)u_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(5.1)

Here,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
.
When
\alpha=1
and
a^{ij}(t,x)=a^{ij}(t)
, J.-H. Choi, I. Kim and J. B. Lee
[
5
]
studied the regularity theory for Equation (
5.1
).
With help of Theorem
1.5
, one can rewrite the works of
[
5
]
as following:

Theorem 5.1
.

If
\gamma\in[0,\infty)
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\tilde{w}\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
, and

\phi(\lambda):=\lambda^{\gamma+2}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-2}}\tilde{w}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}\left(\frac{d}{q}\left(R_{w}+\frac{1}{\Gamma_{w}}-1\right),M\right)

for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
, then for a given initial data
u_{0}\in\mathscr{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{\gamma+2}(w))
to the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

with the estimate

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}+\|u_{xx}(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}\right)^{q}\tilde{w}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathscr{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

Moreover,

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|u_{xx}(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}(w)}\right)^{q}\tilde{w}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathring{\mathscr{B}}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

For
\alpha<1
, H. Dong and D. Kim
[
7
]
proved that under certain conditions (where
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
\mu\in(-1,q-1)
,
\alpha\in(\frac{1+\mu}{q},1]
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
2-2(1+\mu)/(\alpha q)
is not an integer), if the initial data
u_{0}
satisfies

\sum_{|\beta|\leq k}\|D^{\beta}u_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w)}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\|D^{k}u_{0}(\cdot+h)-D^{k}u_{0}\|_{L_{p}(w)}^{q}}{|h|^{d+q(2-2(1+\mu)/(\alpha q)-k)}}\mathrm{d}h\right)^{1/q}<\infty,

(5.2)

where
k:=\left\lfloor 2-2(1+\mu)/(\alpha q)\right\rfloor
,
then there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{q}((0,T),t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{2}(w))
with
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\in L_{q}((0,T),t^{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))
.
Later, H. Dong and Y. Liu
[
8
]
expanded upon the findings of
[
7
]
, extending them to
\alpha\in(1+\frac{1+\mu}{q},2)
.

Theorem
1.5
and Remark
1.7
, together with results in
[
16
]
, indicate that the initial data spaces contemplated in
[
7
,
8
]
usually do not align with the trace spaces of
[
16
]
.
D. Kim and K. Woo
[
16
]
established the trace theorem for Equation (
5.1
), where they defined the trace space as the weighted Besov space:

\text{Trace space of \eqref{eq:non_divergence}}=B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),\quad\phi(\lambda):=\lambda^{2-\frac{2(1+\mu)}{\alpha q}}.

If

2-\frac{2(1+\mu)}{\alpha q}\leq\frac{d}{p}\left(R_{w}+\frac{1}{\Gamma_{w}}-1\right)<1,

then, according to Remark
1.7
, the space that induced by (
5.2
) is
\mathscr{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
, which is a proper subset of
B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
for certain weights
w
.

Combining
[
6
,
7
]
and Theorem
1.5
, we obtain a more general weighted regularity theory as considered in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. and
\tilde{w}\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
. Suppose that

\tilde{W}_{\alpha}(\lambda):=\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\tilde{w}(t^{1/\alpha})\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}\left(0,1\right),

\phi(\lambda):=\lambda^{2}\tilde{W}_{\alpha}(\lambda^{-2})\in\mathcal{I}_{o}\left(\frac{d}{q}\left(R_{w}+\frac{1}{\Gamma_{w}}-1\right),M\right),

for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then for a given initial data
u_{0}\in\mathscr{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{2}(w))
to the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(5.3)

with the estimate

\||u|+|u_{xx}|+|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathscr{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}.

(5.4)

Proof.

By
[
6
, Proposition A.3]
,

(H_{p}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))_{\tilde{W}_{\alpha},q}=B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),

where
(H_{p}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))_{\tilde{W}_{\alpha},q}
is the generalized interpolation space (for definition, see
[
6
, Section 3]
).
First, we choose

v\in L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{2}(w))\quad\text{and}\quad g\in L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))

such that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v(t,x)=g(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
v(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

with the estimate

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{2}(w))}+\|g\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

(5.5)

The existence of
v
and
g
is established in
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
. By
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists a unique solution
\tilde{v}\in L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{2}(w))
such that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\tilde{v}(t,x)=\Delta\tilde{v}(t,x)+(\Delta v(t,x)-g(t,x)),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\tilde{v}(0,x)=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

with the estimate

\displaystyle\||\tilde{v}|+|\tilde{v}_{xx}|+|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\tilde{v}|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}\lesssim\||\Delta v|+|g|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}.

(5.6)

If we put
u:=v+\tilde{v}
, then
u
is a solution of (
5.3
) satisfying (
5.4
).
Therefore, (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and Theorem
1.5
yield that

\displaystyle\||u|+|u_{xx}|+|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\tilde{v}|+|\tilde{v}_{xx}|+|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\tilde{v}|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|v|+|v_{xx}|+|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||v|+|v_{xx}|+|g|\|_{L_{q}((0,T),\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;H_{p}^{2}(w))}+\|g\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\tilde{w}\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(w))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}\simeq\|u_{0}\|_{\mathscr{B}_{p,q}^{\phi}(w)}

The uniqueness follows from

5.2.
Generalized Sobolev embedding theorem without weights

It is well-known that if
p\gamma>d
and
\gamma-d/p
is not an integer, then the classical Sobolev-Hölder embedding theorem (see
e.g.
,
[
18
, Chapter 13, Section 8, Theorem 1]
) states that

H_{p}^{\gamma}\hookrightarrow C^{\gamma-\frac{d}{p}}.

(5.7)

In this subsection, we derive the generalized version of (
5.7
) using Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty)
. Suppose that

\tilde{\phi}_{p}(\lambda):=\frac{\phi(\lambda)}{\lambda^{\frac{d}{p}}}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,M),

for some
M>0
. Then

\|f\|_{C^{\tilde{\phi}_{p}}_{L}}\lesssim\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{\tilde{\phi}_{p}}_{L}}:=\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}+\sup_{|h|\leq 1}\tilde{\phi}_{p}(|h|^{-1})\|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}f\|_{L_{\infty}},\quad L\geq M.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\Delta_{j}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi_{2^{j-1}}(x-y)+\psi_{2^{j}}(x-y)+\psi_{2^{j+1}}(x-y)||\Delta_{j}f(y)|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=-1}^{1}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}}\|\psi_{2^{j+i}}\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}=C2^{jd/p}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}},\quad\forall p\in[1,\infty],

where
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. This implies that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\phi(2^{j})2^{-jd/p}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\phi(2^{j})^{\tilde{q}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}}^{\tilde{q}}\right)^{1/\tilde{q}},\quad\forall p,\tilde{q}\in[1,\infty).

Similarly, we have

\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}},\quad\forall p\in[1,\infty].

Therefore

\|f\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\tilde{\phi}_{p}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p,\tilde{q}}^{\phi}}.

Since
\tilde{\phi}_{p}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,M)
, by Theorem
1.5
, we have

\|f\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{\tilde{\phi}_{p}}}\simeq\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}+\sup_{|h|\leq 1}\tilde{\phi}_{p}(|h|^{-1})\|\mathcal{D}_{h}^{L}f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|f\|_{C^{\tilde{\phi}_{p}}_{L}}.

The remaining part of the proof focuses on proving

\|f\|_{B_{p,\tilde{q}}^{\phi}}\lesssim\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}}

for an appropriate
\tilde{q}
.
We divide the proof into two cases.

\bullet
Case 1.
p\geq q
.

By taking
\tilde{q}=p
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{p,p}^{\phi}}=\|f\|_{F_{p,p}^{\phi}}.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{p}|\Delta_{j}f(x)|^{p}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\Delta_{j}f(x)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}},

it is clear that

\|f\|_{F_{p,p}^{\phi}}\leq\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}}.

\bullet
Case 2.
1\leq p<q
.

By taking
\tilde{q}=q
and using Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq\left\|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{j})^{q}|\Delta_{j}f|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\|_{L_{p}},

which implies

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\phi}}\leq\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{\phi}}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

(i)
In Theorem
5.3
, if

\tilde{\phi}_{p}\in\mathcal{I}_{o}(0,M)

\gamma-d/p>0
. Since
F_{p,2}^{\phi}=H_{p}^{\gamma}

C^{\tilde{\phi}_{p}}_{1}=C^{\gamma-\frac{d}{p}},\quad 0<\gamma-\frac{d}{p}<1,

Therefore, Theorem
5.3
includes (
5.7
).

(ii)
If

is a Bernstein function satisfying

\displaystyle c_{0}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\delta_{0}}\leq\frac{\phi(R)}{\phi(r)}\quad\forall 0<r\leq R<\infty,

(5.8)

for some
c_{0},\delta_{0}>0
, then
\phi\in\mathcal{I}(\delta_{0},1)
.
Hence, by Proposition
2.2
,

\zeta(\lambda):=\frac{\phi(\lambda^{2})^{\gamma/2}}{\lambda^{d/p}}\in\mathcal{I}(\delta_{0}\gamma-d/p,\gamma-d/p),\quad\gamma>0.

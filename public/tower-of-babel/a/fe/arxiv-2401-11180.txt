Abstract
Perfect code in Cayley graphs and Cayley sum graphs is studied extensively in recent years. In this paper, we consider perfect code in generalized Cayley graphs.



1
Introduction

Given a graph
\Gamma
, a subset
C_{1}
of
V(\Gamma)
is called a perfect code in
\Gamma
if
C_{1}
is an independent set and every vertex in
(\Gamma)\setminus C_{1}
is adjacent to exactly one vertex in
C_{1}
.
A subset
C_{2}
of
V(\Gamma)
is said to be a total perfect code in
\Gamma
if every vertex of
\Gamma
has exactly one neighbor in
C_{2}
.
(In particular,
C_{2}
induces a matching in
\Gamma
and so
|C_{2}|
is even.)

Given a finite group
G
and an automorphism
\alpha
of
G
with
\alpha^{2}={\rm\textsf{id}}
, define subsets
\omega_{\alpha}(G)=\{\alpha(g^{-1})g\mid g\in G\}
,
\Omega_{\alpha}(G)=\{g\in G\setminus\omega_{\alpha}(G)\mid\alpha(g)=g^{-1}\}
and
\mho_{\alpha}(G)=\{g\in G\mid\alpha(g)\neq g^{-1}\}
.
A subset
S
of
G
is called a generalzied Cayley subset induced by
\alpha
if it satisfies
S\cap\omega_{\alpha}(G)=\emptyset
and
\alpha(S)=S^{-1}
.
The graph with vertices
G
and edges
\{\{g,h\}\mid\alpha(g^{-1})h\in S\}
is called the generalized Cayley graph of
G
with respect to the ordered pair
(S,\alpha)
, and denoted by
GC(G,S,\alpha)
.
In particular, if
\alpha={\rm\textsf{id}}
, the corresponding graph is Cayley graph.
To differ from the Cayley graph, we only discuss the case of
\alpha
is involution of
{\rm\textsf{Aut}}(G)
throughout this paper.

We call a subgroup is a (total) perfect code of
G
with respect to
\alpha
if
H
is subgroup (total) perfect code of some generalized Cayley graph of
G
induced by
\alpha
.

In this paper, we study perfect code in generalized Cayley graphs.
All groups appeared among this paper is finite.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S=\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{r}\}
be a generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by the involutory automorphism
\alpha
and
X
a subset of
G
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

each vertex in
GC(G,S,\alpha)
is adjacent to at most one vertex in
X
;

(ii)

for each two distinct element
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
in
S
,
\alpha(X)s_{1}\cap\alpha(X)s_{2}=\emptyset
;

(iii)

(\alpha(X^{-1})\alpha(X))\cap(SS^{-1})=\{e\}
.

Proof.

(\rm i)\Rightarrow(\rm ii)
:
Assume that
\alpha(X)s_{1}\cap\alpha(X)s_{2}\neq\emptyset
for some different elements
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
in
S
.
Then there exists elements
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
X
such that
\alpha(x_{1})s_{1}=\alpha(x_{2})s_{2}
.
It implies

\alpha(\alpha(x_{1})s_{1})^{-1}x_{2}=\alpha(s_{1}^{-1})x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}=\alpha(s_{2}^{-1})\in S.

Thus, the vertex
\alpha(x_{1})s_{1}
is adjacent to
x_{2}
.
Observe that
\alpha(x_{1})s_{1}
is also adjacent to
x_{1}
as
\alpha(x_{1}^{-1})\alpha(x_{1})s_{1}=s_{1}\in S
.
From the assumption, we have
x_{1}=x_{2}
and this yields that
s_{1}=s_{2}
, which is a contradiction.

(\rm ii)\Rightarrow(\rm iii)
:
This is clear from the fact that,
\alpha(x_{1})s_{1}=\alpha(x_{2})s_{2}
is equivalent to
\alpha(x_{1}^{-1})\alpha(x_{2})=s_{1}s_{2}^{-1}
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
.

(\rm iii)\Rightarrow(\rm i)
:
Assume that there exists element
g
such that
g
is adjacent to both
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, where
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are different elements in
X
.
Then
\alpha(x_{1}^{-1})g
and
\alpha(x_{2}^{-1})g
are belong to
S
.
As a result,

\alpha(x_{1}^{-1})g(\alpha(x_{2}^{-1})g)^{-1}=\alpha(x_{1}^{-1})\alpha(x_{2})\in\alpha(X^{-1})\alpha(X)\cap SS^{-1},

which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
S
be a generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by the involutory automorphism
\alpha
and
X
a subset of
G
.
In
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, each vertex in
G\setminus X
is adjacent to at least one vertex in
X
if and only if
G\setminus X\subseteq\cup_{s\in S}(\alpha(X)s)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S
be a generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by the involutory automorphism
\alpha
and
X
a subset of
G
.

X
is an independent set of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
if and only if
X\cap\alpha(X)s=\emptyset
for each
s\in S
, that is
\alpha(X^{-1})X\cap S=\emptyset
.

Based on the statements above, we give the equivalent condition of a subset to be perfect code.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S=\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{r}\}
be a generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by the involutory automorphism
\alpha
and
X
a subset of
G
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
;

(ii)

\{X,\alpha(X)s_{1},\cdots,\alpha(X)s_{r}\}
is a partition of
G
;

(iii)

|G|=|X|(r+1)
,
\alpha(X^{-1})X\cap S=\emptyset
and
(\alpha(X^{-1})\alpha(X))\cap(SS^{-1})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
.
Since each element in
G\setminus X
is adjacent to exactly one vertex in
X
and
X
is an independent set, by Lemmas
2.1
to
2.4
, we obtain
\rm{(i)}
is equivalent to
\rm{(iii)}
.

\rm{(ii)}\Leftrightarrow\rm{(iii)}
:
\{X,\alpha(X)s_{1},\cdots,\alpha(X)s_{r}\}
construct a partition of
G
is equivalent to
|G|=|X|(r+1)
, and for different integers
i
and
j
,

X\cap\alpha(X)s_{i}=\emptyset
and
\alpha(X)s_{i}\cap\alpha(X)s_{j}=\emptyset
, where
1\leq i,j\leq r
.
By Lemma
2.1
and
2.3
,
X\cap\alpha(X)s_{i}=\emptyset
is equivalent to
\alpha(X^{-1})X\cap S=\emptyset
, and

\alpha(X)s_{i}\cap\alpha(X)s_{j}=\emptyset
is equivalent to
(\alpha(X^{-1})\alpha(X))\cap(SS^{-1})=\emptyset
.
∎

Especially, we find that if a subgroup
H
of
G
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, then it is fixed by
\alpha
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
.
If
H
is subgroup perfect code of graph
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, then
S\cap\alpha(H)=\emptyset
.
Further, we have
\alpha(H)=H
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{r}\}
.
Firstly, by lemma
2.4
,
H\cap\alpha(H)s_{i}=\emptyset
for each
s_{i}\in S
.
We prove the result by negation.
Assume
S\cap\alpha(H)\neq\emptyset
and let
s\in S\cap\alpha(H)
.
Then
\alpha(s^{-1})\in H\cap\alpha(H)\alpha(s^{-1})
, which is a contradiction as
\alpha(s^{-1})\in S
.
Hence,
S\cap\alpha(H)=\emptyset
, which indicates that
\alpha(H)\cap\alpha(H)s_{i}=\emptyset
for each
s_{i}\in S
.
Recall that
G=H\cup\alpha(H)s_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\alpha(H)s_{r}
, we have
\alpha(H)\subseteq H
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
S
be a generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by the involutory automorphism
\alpha
.
Then the subgroup
H
of
G
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
if and only if
\alpha(H)=H
and
\{e\}\cup S
is a set of coset representatives of
H
.

Remark 2.7
.

Recall that
\alpha(S)=S^{-1}
.
If
H
is subgroup perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, then

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\cup Hs_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Hs_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(H)\cup\alpha(Hs_{1})\cup\cdots\cup\alpha(Hs_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\cup H\alpha(s_{1})\cup\cdots\cup H\alpha(s_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\cup Hs_{1}^{-1}\cup\cdots\cup Hs_{r}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\cup s_{1}H\cup\cdots\cup s_{r}H.

Thus, in Corollary
2.6
,
\{e\}\cup S
is not only a set of right coset representatives of
H
, but also a set of left coset representatives of
H
.

Definition 2.8
.

Given a group
G
, a subgroup
H
of
G
, and an involution
\alpha\in{\rm\textsf{Aut}}(G)
, we call a subset
T
of
G
a generalized Cayley transversal of
H
in
G
if
T
containing identity element
e
,
T\setminus\{e\}
is a generalized Cayley subset induced by
\alpha
and
T
is a set of coset representatives of
H
in
G
.

Remark 2.9
.

Note that for
s\in S
with
\alpha(s)\neq s^{-1}
,
\{s,e\}
and
\{s,\alpha(s)s\}
are edges of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
.
Thus if
H
is a subgroup perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, then
\alpha(s)s\notin H
.
Since
\alpha(H)=H
, we have
s\alpha(s)\notin H
, which is equivalent to
H\alpha(s^{-1})\neq Hs
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
,
\alpha
an involution in
{\rm\textsf{Aut}}(G)
.
For any element
g\in{\rm\textsf{fix}}_{\alpha}(G)
, if
H
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
for some generalized Cayley subset induced by
\alpha
, then
g^{-1}Hg
is perfect code of
GC(G,g^{-1}Sg,\alpha)
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{r}\}
.
Note that

g^{-1}\omega_{\alpha}(G)g=\alpha(g^{-1})\{\alpha(x^{-1})x\mid x\in G\}g=\{\alpha(xg)^{-1}xg\mid x\in G\}=\omega_{\alpha}(G).

In view of
S\cap\omega_{\alpha}(G)=\emptyset
, we obtain that
g^{-1}Sg\cap g^{-1}\omega_{\alpha}(G)g=g^{-1}Sg\cap\omega_{\alpha}(G)=\emptyset
.
Combining the fact that

\alpha(g^{-1}Sg)=g^{-1}\alpha(S)g=g^{-1}S^{-1}g=(g^{-1}Sg)^{-1},

it follows that
g^{-1}Sg
is a generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by
\alpha
.
Since
H
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
,
\alpha(H)=H
and
G=H\cup Hs_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Hs_{r}
.
Further, we have

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=g^{-1}Hg\cup g^{-1}Hs_{1}g\cup\cdots\cup g^{-1}Hs_{r}g

\displaystyle=g^{-1}Hg\cup(g^{-1}Hg)(g^{-1}s_{1}g)\cup\cdots\cup(g^{-1}Hg)(g^{-1}s_{r}g).

By Corollary
2.6
,
g^{-1}Hg
is perfect code of
GC(G,g^{-1}Sg,\alpha)
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
and
\alpha
an involutory automorphism of
G
.
For any
\beta\in{\rm\textsf{Aut}}(G)
, if
H
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
for some generalized Cayley subset induced by
\alpha
, then
H^{\beta}
is perfect code of
GC(G,S^{\beta},\alpha^{\beta})
, where
\alpha^{\beta}=\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{r}\}
.
Observe that
\alpha^{\beta}
is an involutory automorphism of
G
and

\displaystyle\beta^{-1}(\omega_{\alpha^{\beta}}(G))

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\{\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}(g^{-1})g\mid g\in G\}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\beta^{-1}(g^{-1})\beta^{-1}(g)\mid g\in G\}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha(\beta^{-1}(g))^{-1}(\beta^{-1}(g))\mid g\in G\}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\alpha}(G),

we have

S^{\beta}\cap\omega_{\alpha^{\beta}}(G)=\beta(S\cap\beta^{-1}(\omega_{\alpha^{\beta}}(G)))=\beta(S\cap\omega_{\alpha}(G))=\emptyset.

Since
\alpha^{\beta}(S^{\beta})=\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}\beta(S)=\beta\alpha(S)=\beta(S^{-1})=(S^{\beta})^{-1}
,
S^{\beta}
is generalized Cayley subset of
G
induced by
\alpha^{\beta}
.
As
H
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, by Corollary
2.6
,
\alpha(H)=H
, and
G=H\cup Hs_{1}\cup\cdots\cup Hs_{r}
.
Thus,
\alpha^{\beta}(H^{\beta})=\beta\alpha(H)=\beta(H)=H^{\beta}
and

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\beta(H)\cup\beta(Hs_{1})\cup\cdots\cup\beta(Hs_{r})

\displaystyle=H^{\beta}\cup H^{\beta}s_{1}^{\beta}\cup\cdots\cup H^{\beta}s_{r}^{\beta}.

Applying Corollary
2.6
again,
H^{\beta}
is subgroup perfect code of
GC(G,S^{\beta},\alpha^{\beta})
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and
H
a subgroup of
G
with odd order.
If there exists an involutory automorphism
\alpha
such that
H\leq\omega_{\alpha}(G)
, then
H
is perfect code of generalized Cayley graph induced by
\alpha
if and only if
H=\omega_{\alpha}(G)
.

Proof.

Necessity.
Assume
H\subset\omega_{\alpha}(G)
and choose
g\in\omega_{\alpha}(G)\setminus H
.
Then each element in
Hg
belongs to
\omega_{\alpha}(G)
.
By Corollary
2.6
,
H
is not perfect code of any generalized Cayley graph
induced by
\alpha
.

Sufficiency.
Let
H=\omega_{\alpha}(G)
.
For any nontrivial coset
Hg
,
g\in\Omega_{\alpha}(G)\cup\mho_{\alpha}(G)
.
In fact, if
g\in\mho_{\alpha}(G)
, then
Hg\neq H\alpha(g^{-1})
.
Otherwise,
\alpha(g)g\in H
, this yields that
\alpha(\alpha(g)g)=\alpha(g)g=(\alpha(g)g)^{-1}
.
Thus,
\alpha(g)g
is an involution in
H
, which is contrary to the order of
H
is odd.
In addition, for any
g_{1}\in\Omega_{\alpha}(G)
and
g_{2}\in\mho_{\alpha}(G)
,
since
Hg_{1}=H\alpha(g_{1})^{-1}
and
Hg_{2}\neq H\alpha(g_{2}^{-1})
, we have
Hg_{1}\neq Hg_{2}
.
Therefore, there exists a set of coset representative of
H
with form
\{e\}\cup\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\}\cup\{t_{1},\alpha(t_{1}^{-1}),\cdots,t_{\ell},\alpha(t_{\ell}^{-1})\}
, where
s_{i}\in\Omega_{\alpha}(G)
for

and
t_{j}\in\mho_{\alpha}(G)
for

.

S=\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\}\cup\{t_{1},\alpha(t_{1}^{-1}),\cdots,t_{\ell},\alpha(t_{\ell}^{-1})\}
.
As
\alpha(H)=H
and
\{e\}\cup S
is generalized Cayley coset representative of
H
in
G
,
H
is perfect code of
GC(G,S,\alpha)
.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and
H
a subgroup of
G
.
Let
\alpha
be an involutory automorphism of
G
.
Then
H
is perfect code of generalized Cayley graph induced by
\alpha
if and only if
\alpha(H)=H
and for any
g\in G\setminus H
,
\alpha(g)g\in H
implies there exists an element
h\in H
such that
hg\in\Omega_{\alpha}(G)
.

Proof.

Assume
H
is perfect code of graph
GC(G,S,\alpha)
, where
S=\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{r}\}
.
By Proposition
2.5
, we have
\alpha(H)=H
directly.
Now assume
\alpha(g)g\in H
for
g\in G\setminus H
and
g\in Hs_{i}
.
Then we obtain
Hs_{i}=Hg=H\alpha(g^{-1})=H\alpha(s_{i}^{-1})
.
Recall that both
s_{i}
and
\alpha(s_{i}^{-1})
belong to
S
and
\{e\}\cup S
is a set of right coset representatives of
H
, it follows that

s_{i}=\alpha(s_{i}^{-1})
.
Since
Hg=Hs_{i}
, there exists
h\in H
such that
hg=s_{i}
and then
\alpha(hg)hg=e
.

Conversely, assume that
\alpha(H)=H
and for any
g\in G\setminus H
,
\alpha(g)g\in H
implies there exists an element
h\in H
such that
hg\notin\omega_{\alpha}(G)
and
\alpha(hg)hg=e
.
Let
\Phi
denote the set of elements of
G/H
not including
H
, and define two subsets of
\Phi
as follows:

I=\{Hx_{i}\mid Hx_{i}\in\Phi,Hx_{i}=H\alpha(x_{i}^{-1})\}
,
J=\Phi\setminus I
.
Assume
I=\{Hx_{1},\cdots,Hx_{\ell}\}
.
For any
Hx_{i}\in I
,
\alpha(x_{i})x_{i}\in H
, which gives that there exists
h_{i}\in H
such that
h_{i}x_{i}\notin\omega_{\alpha}(G)
and

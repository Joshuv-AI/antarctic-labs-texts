Moduli spaces of slope-semistable sheaves with reflexive Seshadri graduations
Mihai Pavel, Matei Toma

Abstract
. We study the moduli stacks of slope-semistable torsion-free coherent sheaves that admit reflexive, respectively locally free, Seshadri graduations on a smooth projective variety. We show that they are open in the stack of coherent sheaves and that they admit good moduli spaces when the field characteristic is zero. In addition, in the locally free case we prove that the resulting moduli space is a quasi-projective scheme.

1.
Introduction

Apart from its usefulness in studying geometrical properties of a given projective variety, e.g.
[
Miy87
,
CP19
]
, the concept of slope-stability of torsion-free coherent sheaves is important in algebraic geometry for offering a restrictive condition under which the construction of moduli spaces of coherent sheaves on a fixed projective variety becomes possible. It was first used in the classification of locally free sheaves over algebraic curves
[
Mum63
,
NS65
,
Ses67
]
and later extended to higher dimensional bases polarized by some ample divisor class
[
Tak72
]
. However it turned out that in order to obtain reasonable compactifications of the obtained moduli spaces one needed to allow at the boundary
S
-equivalence classes
of torsion-free sheaves which are Gieseker-Maruyama-semistable,
[
Gie77
,
Mar78
,
Sim94
]
. One feature of the Gieseker-Maruyama compactifications is that they corepresent a suitable moduli functor. For the known constructions of compact moduli spaces of slope-semistable torsion-free sheaves this feature is lost,
[
HL10
,
GRT16
,
GSTW21
]
. Here we exhibit an open subfunctor of the moduli functor of coherent sheaves, which is corepresented by a moduli space and which contains all slope-polystable reflexive sheaves.

The problem of constructing moduli spaces of slope-polystable locally free sheaves was recently addressed by Buchdahl and Schumacher in a complex geometrical setting. Specifically they constructed
a coarse moduli space of slope-polystable locally free sheaves which enjoys a universal property over the category of weakly normal analytic spaces
[
BS22
, Theorem 1]
. The techniques employed rely on the Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence and on analytic Geometric Invariant Theory. In the process the authors proved that small deformations of polystable locally free sheaves enjoy a special property, namely that they have locally free Seshadri graduations, see Definition
2.2
.

The above property, which we will call property
(SLF)
, is the object of study of this paper in an algebraic geometrical context. We will also consider the weaker property
(SR)
of slope-semistable torsion-free sheaves to admit a reflexive Seshadri graduation. Our main result asserts that these properties are open in flat families of coherent sheaves and that they give rise to corresponding moduli spaces which corepresent natural functors on the category of (noetherian) schemes. More precisely we show:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.6
, Corollary
4.6
, Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
(X,H)
be a polarized smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of zero characteristic, and fix a numerical class
\gamma\in\Knum(X)
. Then the substacks
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
and
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
of slope-semistable torsion-free sheaves of class
\gamma
on
X
with the property
(SLF)
, respectively
(SR)
, are open in the stack
\Cohs_{X}
of all coherent sheaves and admit good moduli spaces
M_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
, respectively
M_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
, in the sense of Alper
[
Alp13
]
.

In addition,
M_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
is a quasi-projective scheme over
k
.

The main technical result that we use in the proof is the recent existence theorem of Alper, Halpern-Leistner and Heinloth for good moduli spaces,
[
AHLH23
]
.

Here are some further properties of the moduli space
M_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
that we obtain.

Firstly, it contains the moduli space of locally free stable sheaves as an open subscheme, which is also an open subscheme of the moduli space of Gieseker-Maruyama (GM) semistable sheaves. All these spaces are good moduli spaces of appropriate open substacks of
\Cohs_{X}
. Note however that in general they are mutually distinct, cf. Example
2.2
.

Secondly,
M_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
is not proper in general. In the surface case we compactify it in Theorem
5.6
via a certain good moduli space of Bridgeland semistable objects, which was recently shown to be projective by Tajakka
[
Taj23
]
. In higher dimensions there is a natural open embedding

M^{(SLF),wn}_{X,\gamma}\to M^{\mu ss}_{X,\gamma},

where
M^{(SLF),wn}_{X,\gamma}
is the weak normalization of
M^{(SLF)}_{X,\gamma}
, and
M^{\mu ss}_{X,\gamma}
is the (weakly normal) moduli space of slope-semistable sheaves constructed in
[
GT17
]
. Note that
M^{\mu ss}_{X,\gamma}
is not a good moduli space in general.

2.
Properties of sheaves with reflexive Seshadri filtrations

We will denote by
X
a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
H
be an integral ample class on
X
.

For a torsion-free sheaf
E\in\Coh(X)
, we define the slope of
E
(with respect to
H
) by

\mu(E)\coloneqq\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H^{n-1}}{\rk(E)}.

Definition 2.1

(Slope-semistability)
.

A sheaf
E\in\Coh(X)
is
slope-semistable
(resp.
slope-stable
) if

(1)

E
is torsion-free,

(2)

for any subsheaf
F\subset E
with
0<\rk(F)<\rk(E)
we have

\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)\quad(\text{resp. }<).

We also will say
\mu
-(semi)stable instead of slope-(semi)stable.

Any slope-semistable sheaf
E
on
X
is known to admit a
Seshadri filtration

E^{\bullet}:\quad 0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{m}=E

such that its factors
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
are slope-stable with
\mu(E_{i}/E_{i-1})=\mu(E)
for all
i
,
[
HL10
, Theorem 1.6.7]
. In general neither the filtration nor its associated graded module
\gr(E^{\bullet})\coloneqq\oplus_{i}E_{i}/E_{i-1}
are unique, see
[
BTT17
, Example 3.1]
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a torsion-free coherent sheaf
E
on
X
has property
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
, if
E
is
\mu
-semistable and for some Seshadri filtration of
E
, its corresponding graded module is reflexive, resp. locally free.

In this section and the next one all statements will concern coherent sheaves satisfying property
(SR)
or property
(SLF)
, but the proofs will only treat the case of property
(SR)
, the same arguments applying for property
(SLF)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
E
is a
\mu
-semistable sheaf on
X
which admits some Seshadri filtration whose corresponding graded module is reflexive, resp. locally free, then this property holds for any of its Seshadri filtrations. In this case all Seshadri graduations of
E
are reflexive, resp. locally free, and isomorphic to one another.

Proof.

Let
G
,
\tilde{G}

be Seshadri graduations of a
\mu
-semistable sheaf
E
on
X
such that
G
is reflexive. It is known that the double dual sheaves of
G
and
\tilde{G}
are isomorphic, see
[
HL10
, Corollary 1.6.10]
,
[
BTT17
, Proposition A.2]
. Thus
\tilde{G}^{\vee\vee}\cong G^{\vee\vee}\cong G
. In particular
\tilde{G}^{\vee\vee}
has the same topological type as
E
and as
\tilde{G}
and therefore the natural injective morphism
\tilde{G}\to\tilde{G}^{\vee\vee}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Consider a short exact sequence

0\to E\to F\to G\to 0

of coherent sheaves on
X
. By using
[
Sta20
, Tag 00LX]
one can easily check:

(1)

If
E
and
G
are reflexive, resp. locally free, then
F
is reflexive, resp. locally free.

(2)

If
F
is reflexive and
G
is torsion-free, then
E
is reflexive.

Note that if
E
satisfies
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
, then
E
is in particular reflexive, resp. locally free.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
E
be a slope-semistable torsion-free sheaf. Then
E
satisfies
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
, if and only if any torsion-free quotient
F
of
E
of slope
\mu(F)=\mu(E)
is reflexive, resp. locally free.

Proof.

We first assume that
E
satisfies
(SR)
, and let
q:E\to F
be a torsion-free quotient with
\mu(F)=\mu(E)
and
G
its kernel. It is easy to check that
G
and
F
are
\mu
-semistable with the same slope. Then
G
and
F
admit Seshadri filtrations, say
G^{\bullet}:0=G_{0}\subset G_{1}\subset\ldots\subset G_{l}=G
, and respectively
F^{\bullet}:0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\ldots\subset F_{s}=F
. We set
m=l+s
and

E_{j}=\begin{cases}G_{j},&\quad\text{if }0\leq j\leq l\\
q^{-1}(F_{j-l}),&\quad\text{if }l+1\leq j\leq m.\\
\end{cases}

Then
0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{m}=E
is a Seshadri filtration of
E
. As
E
satisfies
(SR)
, we deduce that the graded factors
F_{j}/F_{j-1}
of
F^{\bullet}
are reflexive, and so
F
is reflexive by Remark
2.1
.

The converse follows similarly.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E
and
F
be torsion-free sheaves on
X
with
\mu=\mu(E)=\mu(F)
that satisfy
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
. Then any extension of
F
by
E
also satisfies
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
.

Proof.

Consider an extension

0\to E\xrightarrow{\iota}G\xrightarrow{p}F\to 0,

and let
G\to Q
be a torsion-free quotient of slope
\mu
. By Proposition
2.2
, it is enough to show that
Q
is reflexive. Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Q_{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Q_{F}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

where
\iota
and
p
are the canonical morphisms, and the vertical maps are surjective. As
E
and
F
satisfy
(SR)
, it follows by Proposition
2.2
that
Q_{E}
and
Q_{F}
are reflexive. Hence
Q
is also reflexive.
∎

The following properties are now immediate.

Proposition 2.4
.

The full subcategory of
\Coh(X)
whose objects are the zero sheaf and the torsion-free sheaves with the property
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
, and fixed slope equal to
\mu
is abelian, noetherian, artinian and closed under extensions.

Remark 2.2
.

Recall that in an abelian, noetherian and artinian category, any object admits a Jordan-Hölder (JH) filtration and the associated graded object is unique,
[
Sta20
, Tag 0FCK]
. Therefore, when working with torsion-free sheaves enjoying property
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
, the notion of Seshadri filtration coincides with the notion of JH filtration in the corresponding categories.

Corollary 2.5
.

Any reflexive, resp. locally free,
\mu
-polystable sheaf on
X
satisfies
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
X
be an abelian surface,
x
be a closed point on
X
and
L
be a non-trivial element in
\Pic^{0}(X)
. A standard computation using Serre’s locally freeness criterion shows that the middle term
E
of any non-trivial extension

0\to L\to E\to\mathcal{I}_{x}\to 0

is locally free, cf.
[
OSS11
, Section I.5]
,
[
Tom92
, Section I.2]
. In this case
E
is reflexive, it is slope semistable with respect to any ample polarization on
X
but it is not reflexively Seshadri filtered.

Semistable sheaves in the sense of Gieseker and Maruyama are automatically slope-semistable and it is known that the converse is not true in general. The following example shows that slope-semistable sheaves need not be GM-semistable even when they are moreover supposed to have property
(SLF)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(X,H)
be a polarized
K3
surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Then its tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is slope-stable of slope zero, see
[
Huy16
, Proposition 9.4.5]
, and so is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
too. Their direct sum
\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{X}
is slope-polystable, has property
(SLF)
, but is not GM-semistable, since the Hilbert polynomials
P_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(m)=\frac{m^{2}}{2}h^{2}+2
and
P_{\mathcal{T}_{X}}(m)=m^{2}h^{2}-20
are not proportional. Note that over complex non-algebraic
K3
surfaces the same example works; in this case one defines an analogue of the Hilbert polynomial with respect to some Kähler class.

Similar examples may be constructed in rank two over polarized
K3
surfaces with more special properties.

3.
Openness of the properties (SR) and (SLF)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring over
k
with quotient field
K
and residue field
k
, let
E
be a coherent sheaf on
X\times\Spec(R)
with slope
\mu
, flat over
R
and such that
E_{k}
has property
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
. Let further
F
be a quotient of
E
flat over
R
such that
F_{K}
is torsion-free and has slope
\mu
. Then
F_{k}
is torsion-free and is therefore even reflexive, resp. locally free.

Proof.

We have
\mu(F_{k})=\mu(F_{K})=\mu
by flatness. We write
B^{0}:=\Tors(F_{k})
for the torsion of
F_{k}
and we suppose by contradiction that
B^{0}\neq 0
. Set
G^{0}:=F_{k}/B^{0}
, the torsion-free part of
F_{k}
. Then
\mu(G^{0})\leq\mu(F_{k})=\mu=\mu(E_{k})
on one hand, and on the other,
\mu(E_{k})\leq\mu(G^{0})
by the semistability of
E_{k}
. Thus
\mu(G^{0})=\mu(E_{k})
and so,
G^{0}
must be reflexive by Proposition
2.2
and
\codim_{X}(B^{0})\geq 2
.

We will next apply to
F
the first step of Langton’s method aimed at producing a subsheaf
F^{\prime}
of
F
, flat over
R
, whith
F^{\prime}_{K}=F_{K}
and
F^{\prime}_{k}
torsion-free, cf.
[
HL10
, Exercise 2.B.2]
. This goes as follows, cf.
[
HL10
, Proof of Theorem 2.B.1]
.
Put
F^{1}:=\Ker(F\to G^{0})
.
We get two exact sequences

0\to B^{0}\to F_{k}\to G^{0}\to 0

and

0\to G^{0}\to F_{k}^{1}\to B^{0}\to 0.

Set
B^{1}:=\Tors(F^{1}_{k})
. Then we get an injective morphism

G^{0}\to F_{k}^{1}/B^{1}

whose bidual

G^{0}\to(F_{k}^{1}/B^{1})^{\vee\vee}

is an isomorphism by the normality property of reflexive sheaves
and factorizes through
F_{k}^{1}/B^{1}
. Thus
G^{0}\to F_{k}^{1}/B^{1}
is an isomorphism and it follows that the exact sequence
0\to G^{0}\to F_{k}^{1}\to B^{0}\to 0
splits and therefore

F^{1}_{k}\cong G^{0}\oplus B^{0}
and
B^{1}\cong B^{0}
.

If we continue the process by setting

G^{1}:=F^{1}_{k}/B^{1}
,
F^{2}:=\Ker(F^{1}\to G^{1})
,
B^{2}:=\Tors(F^{2}_{k})
, and so on, we see as above that the sequences
(G^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(B^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
remain stationary and the argument in
[
HL10
, Proof of Theorem 2.B.1]
or in
[
Lan75
]
leads to a contradiction, see also
[
Tom20
, Proposition 3.3]
. This proves the Lemma.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

An analogue of Lemma
3.1
holds in the complex analytic setup, following the proof of
[
Tom20
, Theorem 3.1]
. That is, for a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
, a family
E
flat over the unit disk
\Delta
with fibers of slope
\mu
on
X
such that
E_{0}
has property
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
, and a quotient of
E\to F
flat over
\Delta
such that
F_{t}
is torsion-free of slope
\mu
for all
t\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
, one has that
F_{0}
is torsion-free and therefore reflexive, resp. locally free.

Proposition 3.2
.

The properties
(SR)
and
(SLF)
are open in flat families of sheaves.

Proof.

Let
S
be a scheme of finite type over
k
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be an
S
-flat family of sheaves on
X
. Since the property of being slope-semistable is open, we may assume that all fibers
\mathcal{F}_{s}
over
S
are slope-semistable with slope
\mu
. Consider the set

\displaystyle H=\{P(F^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Q}[T]:{}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s}\to F^{\prime}\text{ is a torsion-free quotient for a }

\displaystyle\text{geometric point $s\in S$ such that $\mu(F^{\prime})=\mu$}\}.

By Grothendieck’s Lemma (see
[
HL10
, Lemma 1.7.9]
), the set
H
is finite. Consider the relative Quot scheme
\varphi:Q:=\Quot(\mathcal{F},H)\to S
of quotients
[\mathcal{F}_{s}\to F^{\prime}]
with
P(F^{\prime})\in H
, and let
Z\subset Q
be the
subset of non-reflexive quotients in
Q
. As the property of being reflexive is open in flat families, we see that
Z
is closed in
Q
, and so
Z
is proper over
S
. Therefore the scheme-theoretic image
\varphi(Z)
is closed in
S
.

Let
Z^{\prime}
be the closed subscheme of
Z
corresponding to quotients of
\mathcal{F}
which are not torsion-free. By Proposition
2.2
the subset of
S
parameterizing fibers not satisfying property
(SR)
is exactly
\varphi(Z\setminus Z^{\prime})
. Thus it suffices to show that
Z\setminus Z^{\prime}
is proper over
S
. To do this we will use the valuative criterion of properness and Lemma
3.1
. Let
R
be a DVR over
k
with quotient field
K
, and consider a commutative diagram such as follows

\textstyle{\Spec(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{Z\setminus Z^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{\Spec(R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S}

where
\iota
is given by the inclusion
R\subset K
. As
Z
is proper over
S
, there exists a unique morphism
f:\Spec(R)\to Z
such that the above diagram with
f
added remains commutative. As
Z
is a subset of the relative Quot scheme,
f
corresponds to an
R
-flat family
\mathcal{F}_{R}\to\mathcal{E}
of quotients with slope
\mu
. Let us assume by contradiction that
f
does not factorize through
Z\setminus Z^{\prime}
, which implies that
\mathcal{F}_{k}
satisfies
(SR)
. Furthermore we see that
\mathcal{E}_{K}=\mathcal{E}\otimes_{R}K
is torsion-free, since
\mathcal{E}_{K}
corresponds to the morphism
\Spec(K)\to Z\setminus Z^{\prime}
. Thus, by Lemma
3.1
, we obtain that
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is reflexive, which gives a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The Zariski-openness of the
(SR)
and
(SLF)
properties also holds in the Kähler analytic setup.

Sketch of proof.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold,
S
a complex space and
\mathcal{F}
an
S
-flat family of sheaves of slope
\mu
on
X
. We consider similarly to the proof of Proposition
3.2
the union
Q
of the irreducible components of the relative Douady space over
S
of quotients of
\mathcal{F}
such that each such irreducible component contains a torsion-free quotient of slope
\mu
. By
[
Tom21
, Corollary 6.3]
the natural map
\varphi:Q\to S
is proper. Let
Z\subset Q
be the closed subset of non-reflexive quotients, and
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
the closed subset of quotients with torsion. As before, it is enough to show that
\varphi(Z\setminus Z^{\prime})
is closed in
S
.

Let
s\in S\setminus\varphi(Z\setminus Z^{\prime})
be a point. If
s\notin\varphi(Z)
then
S\setminus\varphi(Z)
is a Zariski-neighbourhood of
s
. Suppose now that
s
is in
\varphi(Z)
. By restricting
S
to a Zariski-open neighbourhood of
s
, we may assume that all irreducible components of
Z
intersect the fiber
\varphi^{-1}(s)
non-trivially. Now let
T\subset Z
be an irreducible component of
Z
and let
z
be a point in
T\cap\varphi^{-1}(s)
. Since
s\notin\varphi(Z\setminus Z^{\prime})
, we have
\varphi^{-1}(s)\cap Z=\varphi^{-1}(s)\cap Z^{\prime}
. Let
T^{\prime}\subset Z^{\prime}\cap T
be an irreducible component of
Z^{\prime}
passing through
z
. We will show that
T^{\prime}=T
, which will imply that
Z^{\prime}=Z
, thus finishing the proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
T^{\prime}\neq T
. Then there exists a holomorphic curve
f:\Delta\to T
such that
f(0)=z
and
f(\Delta\setminus\{0\})\subset T\setminus T^{\prime}
, where
\Delta
denotes the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
. This contradicts Remark
3.1
.
∎

4.
Existence of good moduli spaces

Here and in the next section we assume that the characteristic of the base field
k
is zero.

We have denoted by
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)},\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}\subset\Cohs_{X}
the substacks of slope-semistable torsion-free sheaves of fixed numerical class
\gamma
on
X
which satisfy property
(SR)
, resp.
(SLF)
. In this section we shall first show that
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
admits a separated good moduli space (in the sense of Alper
[
Alp13
]
), then as a consequence we will obtain the existence of a good moduli space for
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
as well.

We will use the following criterion for the existence of good moduli spaces:

Theorem 4.1

(see Theorem A in
[
AHLH23
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an algebraic stack of finite presentation, with affine automorphism groups and separated diagonal over a noetherian algebraic space
S
of
characteristic 0. Then
\mathfrak{M}
admits a separated good moduli space if and only if is
\mathfrak{M}
is
\Theta
-reductive and
S
-complete.

We note that
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}\subset\Cohs_{X}
is an open substack by Proposition
3.2
. Thus
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
is an algebraic stack locally of finite presentation and with affine diagonal over
k
, since
\Cohs_{X}
is so by
[
Sta20
, TAG 09DS,Tag 0DLY]
. In particular,
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
has affine automorphism groups and separated diagonal. We also know that
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
is quasi-compact, since the set of

-semistable sheaves of class

on

is bounded (see

). According to Theorem

, in order to show that
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
admits a separated good moduli space, it is enough to check that
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
is both
\Theta
-reductive and

-complete. We shall see this below, after we recall the definition of
\Theta
-reductivity and

-completeness (following
[

]

4.1.
\Theta
-reductivity

Let
\Theta=[\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
be the quotient stack corresponding to the standard contracting action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{m}
on the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
over
k
.

Definition 4.1

(see Definition 3.10 in
[
AHLH23
]
)
.

A noetherian algebraic stack
\mathfrak{M}
is
\Theta
-reductive over

if for every
R
DVR over
k
, any commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Theta_{R}\setminus\{0\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Theta_{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

of solid arrows can be uniquelly filled in, where

is the unique closed point.

Let

be a DVR over

with quotient field

. Then the closed point of
\Theta_{R}
is defined by

, where
\BG_{m}=[\text{pt}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
is the classifying stack of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
over

. Moreover, note that
\Theta_{R}\setminus 0
is described by the following union

\Theta_{R}\setminus\{0\}=([(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus 0)/\mathbb{G}_{m}]\times\Spec R)\cup_{\Spec K}\Theta_{K},

with

and

. Thus, a morphism

corresponds to a commutative diagram as follows:

\textstyle{\Spec R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}}
\textstyle{\Spec K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Theta_{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We can think of a morphism

as an object
f(1)=E_{K}\in\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}(K)
together with a filtration

such that

(see
[

, Section 2.3]
for more details).

Putting together the above considerations, we obtain the following characterization of
\Theta
-reductivity (see also
[

, Proposition 3.6]

\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
is
\Theta
-reductive if and only if for

(1)

every DVR

over
k
with quotient field
K
and residue field
k
,

(2)

every family

, and

(3)

every filtration
E_{K}^{\bullet}
of
E_{K}
with
\gr(E_{K}^{\bullet})\in\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}(K)
,

the (uniquely) induced filtration

of

satisfies
\gr(E_{k}^{\bullet})\in\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}(k)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The stack
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SR)}
is
\Theta
-reductive.

Proof.

Let
R
be a DVR over
k
with quotient field
K
and residue field
k
. Let

be an
R
-flat family of sheaves with slope

and consider a filtration

of

∎

Let

such that

and

. Then

on

. We recall the equivalence relation introduced by Langton on such submodules

, which declares

Recall also that Langton defines two equivalence classes

to be

. In this case, as

for some

induces an inclusion of associated

-modules

. Let

denote its restriction to

. One gets two exact sequences

(4.2)

\displaystyle 0\to\Ker(\alpha)\to E^{\prime}_{k}\to\im(\alpha)\to 0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle 0\to\im(\alpha)\to E_{k}\to\Coker(\alpha)\to 0,

and it is shown in

that

is torsion-free and that there is a natural isomorphism

. One says in this case that

is an

of

. In Langton’s terminology and notation the passage from

The next result is analogous to

Lemma 4.3

Let
E_{K}
be a torsion-free sheaf of rank

on

Set

For integers

Proposition 5.1
.

The line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(u)
on
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
descends to a line bundle

on
M^{(SLF)}_{X,\gamma}
.

Proof.

By
[
Alp13
, Theorem 10.3]
we know that
\mathcal{L}
descends to a line bundle on the good moduli space
M^{(SLF)}_{X,\gamma}
if and only if it has trivial stabilizer action at the closed points of
\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
. So consider a closed point
t\in\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}
corresponding to some polystable sheaf

E=E_{1}^{\oplus r_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus E_{m}^{\oplus r_{m}}

such that
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
are pairwise non-isomorphic stable locally free sheaves of slope
\mu=\mu(E)
. Then

\Aut(E)\cong\GL_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\GL_{r_{m}},

and as shown in
[
Taj23
, Section 4.3]
a group element
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\in\Aut(E)
acts on the fiber of
\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(u)
at

by multiplication by

\det(g_{1})^{\chi(E_{1}\cdot u)}\cdots\det(g_{m})^{\chi(E_{m}\cdot u)}.

Note that
\mu(E_{i})=\mu(E)
for each
i
, which gives

\chi(E_{i}\cdot h^{n-1})\chi(E\cdot h^{n})=\chi(E\cdot h^{n-1})\chi(E_{i}\cdot h^{n}).

By a straightforward computation we get

\chi(E_{i}\cdot u)=\frac{\chi(E_{i}\cdot h^{n})}{\chi(E\cdot h^{n})}\chi(E\cdot u)=0,

thus the stabilizer action on
\mathcal{L}
is trivial at the closed point
t\in\Cohs_{X,\gamma}^{(SLF)}

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

There is an integer

Proof.

The proof follows the same strategy as in
[

[

we can find a smooth complete intersection

H_{1},\ldots,H_{n-1}\in|aH|

, such that for any family

is an
S
-flat family of Gieseker-semistable sheaves of class

on

. Thus we obtain a natural morphism of stacks

where

is the algebraic stack of Gieseker semistable sheaves of class

on
C
. Moreover, if

is the universal family on

Note that

Theorem 5.6

If
X

Proof.

There is a natural open embedding of algebraic stacks

Indeed, an
S

\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}_{X,\gamma}(S)
is by definition an
S
-perfect complex
\mathcal{E}\in\Db(S\times X)
such that for any closed point

the derived fiber
\mathcal{E}|^{\textbf{L}}_{\{s\}\times X}
is a
\sigma
-semistable object of class
\gamma
in
\mathcal{A}
, and by

Lie06

of points

\mathcal{E}

\Cohs^{\mu ss}_{X,\gamma}

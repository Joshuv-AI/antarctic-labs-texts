A Fubini–type limit theorem for the integrated hyperuniform infinitely divisible moving averages
Evgeny Spodarev

Abstract
This short note shows a limiting behavior of integrals of some centered antipersistent stationary infinitely divisible moving averages as the compact integration domain in d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 dimensions extends to the whole positive quadrant ℝ + d \mathbb{R}^{d}_{+} . Namely, the weak limit of their finite dimensional distributions is again a moving average with the same infinitely divisible purely jump integrator measure (i.e., possessing no Gaussian component), but with an integrated kernel function. The results apply equally to time series ( d = 1 d=1 ) as well as to random fields ( d > 1 d>1 ). Apart from the existence of the expectation, no moment assumptions on the moving average are imposed allowing it to have an infinite variance as e.g. in the case of α \alpha –stable moving averages with α ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) \alpha\in(1,2) . If the field is additionally square integrable, its covariance integrates to zero (hyperuniformity).



Keywords
: Stationary random field, moving average, infinitely divisible, ambit random field, integral spectral representation,
\alpha
–stable, time series, heavy tails, hyperuniformity, antipersistence.

Introduction

The major object of interest of this note is a real-valued stationary measurable infinitely divisible (ID, for short) moving average random field
X=\{X(t),t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
on a
d
–dimensional Euclidean index space defined by its spectral representation

X(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t-x)\ \Lambda(dx),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;d\geq 1,

(1)

where
\Lambda
is a
centered stationary ID independently scattered purely jump random measure and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a suitable
\Lambda
–integrable kernel. Such random functions are often used as mathematical models in many application contexts varying from turbulent liquid flows
[
4
]
(where they form a special case of

, see
[
29
,
3
]
for a review) to radial growth of trees and tumors
[
20
,
21
]
. An important special case of (
1
) constitute continuous–time (
d=1
) stationary moving averages governed by Lévy processes (e.g. the so–called CARMA
[
13
]
) which are widely used in mathematical insurance and finance
[
5
,
12
,
9
,
27
,
39
]
. They often do not have a finite variance describing high volatility of financial risks. Whereas the limiting behaviour of some classes of functionals (both linear and non–linear) of random fields (
1
) is relatively well understood if the integrator
\Lambda
is Gaussian (see e.g.
[
23
,
25
]
and references therein) or has at least finite second or higher moments
[
1
,
35
,
14
,
16
]
,
the case of a heavy–tailed
\Lambda
is far less investigated. Few central limit theorems are available
[
19
,
28
,
22
,
33
,
8
,
2
]
in the case of short range dependent
X
as well as some results of non–central type
[
6
,
7
,
34
]
for
X
with long memory. The novel contribution of this note is a weak limit of finite dimensional distributions (FDDs, for short) of the sample integral of
X
over a cubic infinitely growing sliding domain when we only assume the existence of the mean
\mathbf{E}X(0)=0
. The kernel function
f
has to be integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to zero and factorize as

f(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}^{\prime}(x_{j}),\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),

where differentiable functions
g_{j}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
belong to the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})

In case of square integrable

[

[

36
,

]

[

38
]

We show that all FDDs of sample averages

\int_{[0,T]^{d}+l}X(t)\,dt,\qquad l\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad T>0,

converge as
T\to+\infty
to the FDDs of the random field

(-1)^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(l_{j}-x_{j})\,\Lambda(dx_{1},\ldots,dx_{d}),\quad l=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For simplicity, this result is first proven for the case of stochastic time series (
d=1
, See Theorem
1
) and is then generalized to all

Interestingly enough, the limiting process belongs to the same family of ID moving averages with integrator
\Lambda

(valid due to the linearity of integration operator combined with the convergence of the corresponding characteristic functions) can be used to understand the behaviour of spatial averages of hyperuniform structures. To some extent, it can be seen as a generalization of the Fubini theorem
[

\alpha

[

3
Preliminaries on ID moving averages

Let
X=\{X(t),t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be a real-valued measurable ID moving average random field defined on a probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
by the integral representation

X(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t-x)\ \Lambda(dx),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2)

where
\Lambda
is a
centered stationary ID independently scattered purely jump random measure with Lebesgue control measure. The absence of Gaussian part means that the characteristic function
\varphi_{\Lambda(B)}
for bounded Borel subsets
B
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has the Lévy-Khintchin representation

\log\varphi_{\Lambda(B)}(z)=|B|_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{izy}-1-izy\chi(y)\right)\,\nu(dy),\quad z\in\mathbb{R},

(3)

where

\chi(y)=\mathbbm{1}(|y|\leq 1)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
and

\nu(\cdot)
is the Lévy measure on
\mathbb{R}
:
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\{y^{2},1\}\nu(dy)<\infty
.
The kernel function
f
above is
\Lambda
–integrable, i.e. satisfies the following two conditions (cf.
[
30
, Theorem 2.7, p. 461]
):

(i)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s)|\left|\int\limits_{\{1\leq|x|\leq|f(s)|^{-1}\}}x\,\nu(dx)-\int\limits_{\{|f(s)|^{-1}\leq|x|\leq 1\}}x\,\nu(dx)\right|\,ds<\infty,

(ii)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int\limits_{\{|x|\geq|f(s)|^{-1}\}}\nu(dx)\,ds<\infty,

(iii)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(s)|^{2}\int\limits_{\{|x|<|f(s)|^{-1}\}}x^{2}\,\nu(dx)\,ds<\infty.

Example 1

(
[
8
]
, p.3)
.

Let
\Lambda
be symmetric with Lévy measure
\nu(\cdot)
having a density

\psi(x)\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{1+\beta}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\quad\beta\in(0,2),

where
C>0
is a constant. The conditions (i) –(iii) are satisfied whenever
f\in L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, e.g. for

|f(x)|\leq K\left(|x|^{\gamma}\mathbbm{1}(|x|<1)+|x|^{-\alpha}\mathbbm{1}(|x|\geq 1)\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad K,\alpha>0,\quad\gamma\in\mathbb{R},

with
\alpha\beta>d
,
\gamma\beta>-d
.

By
[
31
, Proposition 2.10]
, it holds

X(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}yf(t-s)\left[\eta(ds,dy)-\chi(yf(t-s))ds\,\nu(dy)\right]+b,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4)

where
\eta(\cdot,\cdot)
is a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
with intensity measure
ds\,\nu(dy)
, and

b=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}yf(s)\left[\chi(yf(s))-\chi(y)\right]ds\,\nu(dy).

The log characteristic function of
X(t)
writes (
[
30
, p. 462]
)

\begin{split}\log\mathbf{E}e^{izX(t)}&=iz\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}yf(t-s)\left[\chi(yf(t-s))-\chi(y)\right]ds\,\nu(dy)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left[e^{izyf(t-s)}-1-izyf(t-s)\chi(yf(t-s))\right]ds\,\nu(dy),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.\end{split}

Now assume

f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y|\,\nu(dy)<\infty,

(6)

which evidently imply that conditions (i), (ii) hold true and also
yield the integrability of
X(t)
.
Under these assumptions, the above characteristic function simplifies to

\log\mathbf{E}e^{izX(t)}=-iza+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\left[e^{izyf(t-s)}-1\right]ds\,\nu(dy),\quad z\in\mathbb{R},

(7)

where

a=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(s)\,ds\int_{-1}^{1}y\,\nu(dy).

Interpreting (
7
), we may write

X(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}yf(t-s)\eta(ds,dy)-a,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(8)

as a stationary shot noise random field.
If we (additionally to conditions (
5
), (
6
)) require

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}\,\nu(dy)<\infty

(9)

the field
X
becomes square integrable with covariance function
C(t)=\mathbf{E}[X(0)X(t)]
equal to

C(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}\,\nu(dy)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(-x)f(t-x)\,dx,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(10)

compare
[
22
, Relation 2.44]
.

4
Main results

First, consider the case
d=1
of stationary time series moving averages (
2
). Introduce the sample path integral of
X
by

S_{T,l}:=\int_{l}^{T+l}X(t)\,dt,\qquad l\in\mathbb{R},\quad T>0.

If
\mathbf{E}|X(0)|<\infty
,
S_{T,l}
exists a.s. by measurability of
X
and and is integrable by stationarity and Fubini’s theorem:

\mathbf{E}|S_{T,l}|\leq\int_{l}^{T+l}\mathbf{E}|X(t)|\,dt=T\mathbf{E}|X(0)|<\infty.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X=\{X(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
be the stationary measurable ID moving average with integral representation (
2
) and kernel function
f(x)=g^{\prime}(x)
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
, where

g\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
a.e. Assuming that conditions (
6
) and (iii) hold, one gets the weak convergence of all FDDs of

\{S_{T,l},\;l\in\mathbb{R}\}
as
T\to+\infty
to those of
\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(l-s)\Lambda(ds),\quad l\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Remark 1
.

1.

Since
f(x)=g^{\prime}(x)
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, it holds
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dg(x)=0
and thus
a=0
in representation (
8
). This yields that
X
is centered:
\mathbf{E}X(t)=0
.

2.

As the kernel function
f
reflects the dependence structure of the process
X
, the case
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,dx=0
may be called antipersistence, similar to the case
\int_{\mathbb{R}}C(t)\,dt=0
for stationary square integrable moving averages
X
with covariance function
C
. Indeed, assuming the conditions (
5
), (
6
), (
9
) the relation (
10
) for
C(t)
yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}}C(t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}\,\nu(dy)\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(-x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t-x)\,dt\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}\,\nu(dy)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,dx\right)^{2}=0

by Fubini theorem.
Hence, the condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,dx=0
indicates a phenomenon called in physics hyperuniformity which is characterized by very regular shapes of sample paths of a time series
X
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N},

z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
, write

\log\mathbf{E}e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}S_{T,l_{j}}}=\log\mathbf{E}e^{i\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}\mathbbm{1}(t\in[l_{j},T+l_{j}])X(t)\,dt}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(e^{iyJ_{T}(s)}-1\right)ds\,\nu(dy),

where

\begin{split}J_{T}(s):=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}\mathbbm{1}(t\in[l_{j},T+l_{j}])f(t-s)\,dt=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}\int_{l_{j}-s}^{T+l_{j}-s}f(x)\,dx\\
&\longrightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}\int_{l_{j}-s}^{+\infty}f(x)\,dx=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}g(l_{j}-s)\quad\mbox{as }T\to+\infty.\end{split}

Hence,

\log\mathbf{E}e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}S_{T,l_{j}}}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(e^{-iy\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}g(l_{j}-s)}-1\right)ds\,\nu(dy),\quad T\to+\infty,

and finally

\left(S_{T,l_{1}},\ldots,S_{T,l_{m}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}yg(l_{1}-s)\,\eta(ds,dy),\ldots,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}yg(l_{m}-s)\,\eta(ds,dy)\right)\quad\mbox{as }T\to+\infty.

This together with (
8
) finishes the proof.

∎

Automorphisms and real structures for a Π \Pi -symmetric super-Grassmannian
Elizaveta Vishnyakova appendix by Mikhail Borovoi

Abstract
. Any complex-analytic vector bundle 𝔼 \mathds{E} admits naturally defined homotheties ϕ α \phi_{\alpha} , α ∈ ℂ ∗ \alpha\in\mathds{C}^{*} , i.e. ϕ α \phi_{\alpha} is the multiplication of a local section by a complex number α \alpha . We investigate the question when such automorphisms can be lifted to a non-split supermanifold corresponding to 𝔼 \mathds{E} . Further, we compute the automorphism supergroup of a Π \Pi -symmetric super-Grassmannian Π ​ Gr n , k \Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k} , and, using Galois cohomology, we classify the real structures on Π ​ Gr n , k \Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k} and compute the corresponding supermanifolds of real points.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Supermanifolds

This paper is devoted to the study of the automorphism supergroup of a
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, and to a classification of real structures on
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
. Details about the theory of supermanifolds can be found in
[
Ber
,
L
,
BLMS
]
. As usual, the superspace
\mathds{C}^{n|m}:=\mathds{C}^{n}\oplus\mathds{C}^{m}
is a
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space over
\mathds{C}
of dimension
n|m
. A
superdomain in
\mathds{C}^{n|m}
is a ringed space
\mathcal{U}:=(U,\mathcal{F}_{U}\otimes\bigwedge(\mathds{C}^{m})^{*})
, where
U\subset\mathds{C}^{n}
is an open set and
\mathcal{F}_{U}
is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
U
. If
(x_{a})
is a system of coordinates in
U
and
(\xi_{b})
is a basis in
(\mathds{C}^{m})^{*}
we call
(x_{a},\xi_{b})
a system of coordinates in
\mathcal{U}
. Here
(x_{a})
are called even coordinates of
\mathcal{U}
, while
(\xi_{b})
are called odd ones.
A
supermanifold

\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
of dimension
n|m
is a
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-graded
ringed space that is locally isomorphic to a superdomain in

\mathds{C}^{n|m}
. Here the underlying space
M
is a complex-analytic manifold. A

morphism

F:(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})\to(N,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})
of two
supermanifolds is, by definition, a morphism of the corresponding
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-graded locally ringed spaces.
In more details,
F=(F_{0},F^{*})
is a pair, where
F_{0}:M\to N
is a holomorphic map and

F^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}\to(F_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})

is a homomorphism of sheaves of
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-graded local superalgebras.
We see that the morphism
F
is even, that is,
F
preserves the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-gradings of the sheaves.
A morphism
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is called an
automorphism of
\mathcal{M}
if
F
is an automorphism of the corresponding
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-graded ringed spaces. The automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
form a group, which we denote by
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}
. Note that in this paper we also consider the automorphism supergroup, see a definition below.

A supermanifold
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
is called
split
, if its structure sheaf is isomorphic to
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}
is a sheaf of sections of a holomorphic vector bundle
\mathds{E}
over
M
. In this case the structure sheaf of
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathds{Z}
-graded, not only
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-graded. There is a functor assigning to any supermanifold a split supermanifold. Let us briefly remind this construction. Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
be a supermanifold. Consider the following filtration in
\mathcal{O}

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{J}^{0}\supset\mathcal{J}\supset\mathcal{J}^{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{J}^{p}\supset\cdots,

where
\mathcal{J}
is the subsheaf of ideals in
\mathcal{O}
locally generated by odd elements of
\mathcal{O}
. We define

\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}:=(M,\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}:=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\mathcal{J}^{p}/\mathcal{J}^{p+1}.

The supermanifold
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}
is split and it is called the
retract
of
\mathcal{M}
. The underlying space of
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}
is the complex-analytic manifold
(M,\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J})
, which coincides with
M
. The structure sheaf
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}
is isomorphic to
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{J}/\mathcal{J}^{2}
is a locally free sheaf of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J}
-modules on
M
.

Further let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
and
\mathcal{N}=(N,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})
be two supermanifolds,
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}}
be the subsheaves of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}
, which are locally generated by odd elements in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
and in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}
, respectively. Any morphism
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
preserves these shaves of ideals, that is
F^{*}(\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{N}})\subset(F_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{M}})
, and more generally
F^{*}(\mathcal{J}^{p}_{\mathcal{N}})\subset(F_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{J}^{p}_{\mathcal{M}})
for any
p
. Therefore
F
induces naturally a morphism
\mathrm{gr}(F):\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{N}
. Summing up, the functor
\mathrm{gr}
is defined.

2.2.
A classification theorem for supermanifolds

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
be a (non-split) supermanifold. Recall that we denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}
the group of all (even) automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O}
the sheaf of automorphisms of
\mathcal{O}
. Consider the following subsheaf of
\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O}:=\{F\in\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O}\,\,|\,\,\,\mathrm{gr}(F)=id\}.

This sheaf plays an important role in the classification of supermanifolds, see below. The sheaf

\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O}
has the following filtration

\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{A}ut_{(0)}\mathcal{O}\supset\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O}\supset\ldots\supset\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O}\supset\ldots,

where

\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O}=\{a\in\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O}\mid a(u)\equiv u\mod\mathcal{J}^{2p}\,\,\text{for any}\,\,\,u\in\mathcal{O}\}.

Recall that
\mathcal{J}
is the subsheaf of ideals generated by odd elements in
\mathcal{O}
. Let
\mathds{E}
be the bundle corresponding to the locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{J}/\mathcal{J}^{2}
and let
\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
be the group of all automorphisms of
\mathds{E}
. Clearly, any automorphism of
\mathds{E}
gives rise to an automorphism of
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}
, and thus we get a natural action of the group
\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
on the sheaf
\mathcal{A}ut(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
by
Int:(a,\delta)\mapsto a\circ\delta\circ a^{-1}
, where

\delta\in\mathcal{A}ut(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
and
a\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
. Clearly, the group

\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
leaves invariant the
subsheaves
\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}
. Hence

\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
acts on the cohomology sets

H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
. The unit element

\epsilon\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
is fixed under this action. We denote by
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})/\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
the set of orbits of the action in
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
induced by
Int
.

Denote by
[\mathcal{M}]
the class of supermanifolds which are isomorphic to
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
.
(Here we consider complex-analytic supermanifolds up to isomorphisms inducing the identical isomorphism of the base spaces.) The following theorem was proved in
[
Gr
]
.

Theorem 1

(
Green).

Let
(M,\bigwedge\mathcal{E})
be a fixed split supermanifold. Then

\begin{array}[]{c}\{[\mathcal{M}]\mid\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}\simeq\bigwedge\mathcal{E}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1:1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})/\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}.\end{array}

The split supermanifold
(M,\bigwedge\mathcal{E})
corresponds to the fixed point
\epsilon
.

2.3.
Tangent sheaf of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}

Let again
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
be a (non-split) supermanifold. The
tangent sheaf
of a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
is by definition the sheaf
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{D}er\mathcal{O}
of
derivations of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
. Sections of the sheaf
\mathcal{T}
are called
holomorphic vector fields
on
\mathcal{M}
.
The vector superspace
\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M})=H^{0}(M,\mathcal{T})
of all holomorphic vector fields is a complex Lie superalgebra
with the bracket

[X,Y]=X\circ Y-(-1)^{\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}}Y\circ X,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M}),

where
\tilde{Z}
is the parity of an element
Z\in\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M})
.
The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M})
is finite dimensional if
M
is compact.

Let
\dim\mathcal{M}=n|m
. The tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}
possesses the following filtration:

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{(-1)}\supset\mathcal{T}_{(0)}\supset\mathcal{T}_{(1)}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{T}_{(m)}\supset\mathcal{T}_{(m+1)}=0,

where

\mathcal{T}_{(p)}=\{v\in\mathcal{T}\,\,|\,\,v(\mathcal{O})\subset\mathcal{J}^{p},\,\,v(\mathcal{J})\subset\mathcal{J}^{p+1}\},\quad p\geq 0.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}}
the tangent sheaf of the retract
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}
. Since the structure sheaf
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}
of
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}
is
\mathds{Z}
-graded, the sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}}
has the following induced
\mathds{Z}
-grading

\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}}=\bigoplus_{p\geq-1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{p},

where

(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{p}=\{\,v\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}}\,\,|\,\,v(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}_{q})\subset\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}_{q+p}\,\,\text{for any}\,\,q\in\mathds{Z}\}.

We have the following exact sequence the sheaves of groups

(1)

e\to\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p+2)}\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O}\to(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{2p}\to 0

for any
p\geq 1
, see
[
Roth
]
. More details about this sequence can be also found in
[
Oni1
, Proposition 3.1]

2.4.
Order of a supermanifold

Let again
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
be a (non-split) supermanifold. According to Theorem
1
a supermanifold corresponds to an element
[\gamma]\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})/\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
. Furthermore for any
p\geq 1
we have the following natural embedding of sheaves

\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O},

that induces the map of
1
-cohomology sets

H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O})\to H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O}).

(Note that our sheaves are not abelian.) Denote by
H_{2p}
the image of
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O})
in
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O})
. We get the following
\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}
-invariant filtration

\displaystyle H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O})=H_{2}\supset H_{4}\supset H_{6}\supset\cdots.

Let
\gamma\in[\gamma]
be any representative.
As in
[
Roth
]
we define the order
o(\gamma)
of the cohomology class
\gamma\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O})
to be equal to the
maximal number between the numbers
2p
such that
\gamma\in H_{2p}
. The order of the supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
is by definition the order of the corresponding cohomology class
\gamma
. We put
o(\mathcal{M}):=\infty
, if
\mathcal{M}
is a split supermanifold.

2.5.
The automorphism supergroup of a complex-analytic compact supermanifold

Let us remind a description of a Lie supergroup in terms of a super-Harish-Chandra pair. A
Lie supergroup

\mathcal{G}
is a group object in the category of supermanifolds, see for example
[
Vi1
,
Vi3
]
for details. Any Lie supergroup can be described using a super-Harish-Chandra pair, see

[
Ber
]
and also
[
BCC
,
Vi3
]
, due to the following theorem, see
[
Vi3
]
for the complex-analytic case.

Theorem 2
.

The category of complex Lie supergroups is
equivalent to the category of complex super Harish-Chandra pairs.

A
complex super Harish-Chandra pair
is a pair

(G,\mathfrak{g})
that consists of a complex-analytic Lie group
G
and a Lie
superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
over
\mathds{C}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}={\rm Lie}(G)
, endowed with a representation
\operatorname{Ad}:G\to\operatorname{Aut}\mathfrak{g}
of
G
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that

•

\operatorname{Ad}
preserves the parity and induces the adjoint representation of
G
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
,

•

the differential
(\operatorname{d}\operatorname{Ad})_{e}
at the identity
e\in G
coincides with
the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Super Harish-Chandra pairs form a category. (A definition of a morphism is natural, see in
[
Ber
]
or in
[
Vi3
]
.)

A supermanifold
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
is called compact if its base space
M
is compact. If
\mathcal{M}
is a compact complex-analytic supermanifold, the Lie superalgebra of vector fields
\mathfrak{o}(\mathcal{M})
is finite dimensional. For a compact complex-analytic supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
we define the
automorphism supergroup
as the super-Harish-Chandra pair

(2)

(\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{o}(\mathcal{M})).

3.
Super-Grassmannians and
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannians

3.1.
Complex-analytic super-Grassmannians and complex-analytic

\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannians

A super-Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
is the supermanifold that parameterizes all
k|l
-dimensional linear subsuperspaces in
\mathds{C}^{m|n}
. Here
k\leq m
,
l\leq n
and
k+l<m+n
. The underlying space of
\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
is the product of two usual Grassmannians
\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}\times\operatorname{Gr}_{n,l}
.
The structure of a supermanifold on
\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
can be defined in the following way. Consider the following
(m+n)\times(k+l)
-matrix

\mathcal{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{array}\right).

Here
A=(a_{ij})
is a
(m\times k)
-matrix, whose entries
a_{ij}
can be regarded as (even) coordinates in the domain of all complex
(m\times k)
-matrices of rank
k
. Similarly
D=(d_{sr})
is a
(n\times l)
-matrix, whose entries
d_{sr}
can be regarded as (even) coordinates in the domain of all complex
(n\times l)
-matrices of rank
l
. Further,
B=(b_{pq})
and
C=(c_{uv})
are
(m\times l)
and
(n\times k)
-matrices, respectively, whose entries
b_{pq}
and
c_{uv}
can be regarded as generators of a Grassmann algebra. The matrix
\mathcal{L}
determines the following open subsuperdomain in
\mathds{C}^{mk+nl|ml+nk}

\mathcal{V}=(V,\mathcal{F}_{V}\otimes\bigwedge(b_{pq},c_{uv})),

where
V
is the product of the domain of complex
(m\times k)
-matrices of rank
k
and the domain of complex
(n\times l)
-matrices of rank
l
,
\mathcal{F}_{V}
is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
V
and
\bigwedge(b_{pq},c_{uv})
is the Grassmann algebra with generators
(b_{pq},c_{uv})
.
Let us define an action
\mu:\mathcal{V}\times\operatorname{GL}_{k|l}(\mathds{C})\to\mathcal{V}
of the Lie supergroup
\operatorname{GL}_{k|l}(\mathds{C})
on
\mathcal{V}
on the right in the natural way, that is by matrix multiplication.
The quotient space under this action is called the
super-Grassmannian

\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
.
Now consider the case
m=n
. A
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian

\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is a subsupermanifold in
\operatorname{Gr}_{n|n,k|k}
, which is invariant under odd involution
\Pi:\mathds{C}^{n|n}\to\mathds{C}^{n|n}
, see below.

Let us describe
\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
and
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
using charts and local coordinates
[
Man
]
. First of all let as recall a construction of an atlas for the usual Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}
. Let
e_{1},\ldots e_{m}
be the standard basis in
\mathds{C}^{m}
. Consider a complex
(m\times k)
-matrix
C=(c_{ij})
, where
i=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,k
, of rank
k
.
Such a matrix determines a
k
-dimensional subspace
W
in
\mathds{C}^{m}
with basis
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}c_{i1}e_{i},\ldots,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}c_{ik}e_{i}
. Let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
be a subset of cardinality
k
such that the square submatrix
L=(c_{ij})
,
i\in I
and
j=1,\ldots,k
, of
C
is non-degenerate. (There exists such a subset since
C
is of rank
k
.) Then the matrix
C^{\prime}:=C\cdot L^{-1}
determines the same subspace
W
and contains the identity submatrix
E_{k}
in the lines with numbers
i\in I
. Let
U_{I}
denote the set of all
(m\times k)
-complex matrices
C^{\prime}
with the identity submatrix
E_{k}
in the lines with numbers
i\in I
. Any point

determines a
k
-dimensional subspace
W_{x}
in
\mathds{C}^{n}
as above, moreover if
x_{1},x_{2}\in U_{I}
,
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, then
W_{x_{1}}\neq W_{x_{2}}
. Therefore, the set
U_{I}
is a subset in
\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}
. We can verify that
U_{I}
is open in a natural topology in
\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}
and it is homeomorphic to
\mathds{C}^{(m-k)k}
. Therefore
U_{I}
can be regarded as a chart on
\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}
. Further any
k
-dimensional vector subspace in
\mathds{C}^{n}
is contained in some

for a subset

of cardinality

\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}
.

Now we are ready to describe an atlas
\mathcal{A}
on
\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
. Let
I=(I_{\bar{0}},I_{\bar{1}})
be a pair of sets, where

I_{\bar{0}}\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{\bar{1}}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},

with
|I_{\bar{0}}|=k,
and
|I_{\bar{1}}|=l
. As above to such an

we can assign a chart
U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}}
on
\operatorname{Gr}_{m,k}\times\operatorname{Gr}_{n,l}
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{U}_{I}\}
be a family of superdomains parametrized by
I=(I_{\bar{0}},I_{\bar{1}})
, where

\mathcal{U}_{I}:=(U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}},\mathcal{F}_{U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}}}\otimes\bigwedge((m-k)l+(n-l)k)).

Here
\bigwedge(r)
is a Grassmann algebra with
r
generators and
\mathcal{F}_{U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}}}
is the sheaf of holomorphic function on
U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}}
. Let us describe the superdomain
\mathcal{U}_{I}
in a different way. First of all assume for simplicity that
I_{\bar{0}}=\{m-k+1,\ldots,m\}
,

I_{\bar{1}}=\{n-l+1,\ldots,n\}
. Consider the following matrix

\mathcal{Z}_{I}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\Xi\\
E_{k}&0\\
H&Y\\
0&E_{l}\end{array}\right),

where
E_{s}
is the identity matrix of size
s
. We assume that the entries of
X=(x_{ij})
and
Y=(y_{rs})
are coordinates in the domain
U_{I_{\bar{0}}}
and the domain
U_{I_{\bar{1}}}
, respectively. We also assume that the entries of
\Xi=(\xi_{ab})
and of
H=(\eta_{cd})
are generators of the Grassmann algebra
\bigwedge((m-k)l+(n-l)k)
. We see that the matrix
\mathcal{Z}_{I}
determines a superdomain

\mathcal{U}_{I}:=(U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}},\mathcal{F}_{U_{I_{\bar{0}}}\times U_{I_{\bar{1}}}}\otimes\bigwedge(\xi_{ab},\eta_{cd}))

with even coordinates
x_{ij}
and
y_{rs}
, and odd coordinates
\xi_{ab}
and
\eta_{cd}
.

Let us describe
\mathcal{U}_{I}
for any
I=(I_{\bar{0}},I_{\bar{1}})
. Consider the following
(m+n)\times(k+l)
-matrix

\mathcal{Z}_{I}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X^{\prime}&\Xi^{\prime}\\
H^{\prime}&Y^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\right).

Here the blokes
X^{\prime}
,
Y^{\prime}
,
\Xi^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
are of size
m\times k
,
n\times l
,
m\times l
and
n\times k
, respectively.
We assume that this matrix contains the identity submatrix in the lines with numbers
i\in I_{\bar{0}}
and
i\in\{m+j\,\,|\,\,j\in I_{\bar{1}}\}
. Further, non-trivial entries of
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
can be regarded as coordinates in
U_{I_{\bar{0}}}
and
U_{I_{\bar{1}}}
, respectively, and non-trivial entries of
\Xi^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
are identified with generators of the Grassmann algebra
\bigwedge((m-k)l+(n-l)k)
, see definition of
\mathcal{U}_{I}
. Summing up, we have obtained another description of
\mathcal{U}_{I}
.

The last step is to define the transition functions in
\mathcal{U}_{I}\cap\mathcal{U}_{J}
. To do this we need the matrices
\mathcal{Z}_{I}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{J}
. We put
\mathcal{Z}_{J}=\mathcal{Z}_{I}C_{IJ}^{-1}
,
where
C_{IJ}
is an invertible submatrix in
\mathcal{Z}_{I}
that consists of the lines with numbers
i\in J_{\bar{0}}
and
m+i,
where
i\in J_{\bar{1}}
. This equation gives us a relation between coordinates of
\mathcal{U}_{I}
and
\mathcal{U}_{I}
, in other words the transition functions in
\mathcal{U}_{I}\cap\mathcal{U}_{J}
. The supermanifold obtained by gluing these charts together is called the super-Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{m|n,k|l}
. The supermanifold
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is defined as a subsupermanifold in
\operatorname{Gr}_{n|n,k|k}
defined in
\mathcal{Z}_{I}
by the equations
X^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}
and
\Xi^{\prime}=H^{\prime}
. We can define the
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
as all fixed points of an automorphism of
\operatorname{Gr}_{n|n,k|k}
induced by an odd linear involution
\Pi:\mathds{C}^{n|n}\to\mathds{C}^{n|n}
, given by

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&E_{n}\\
E_{n}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}V\\
W\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}W\\
V\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}V\\
W\\
\end{array}\right)
is the column of right coordinates of a vector in
\mathds{C}^{n|n}
. In our charts
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is defined by the following equation

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&E_{n}\\
E_{n}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\Xi\\
H&Y\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&E_{k}\\
E_{k}&0\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\Xi\\
H&Y\\
\end{array}\right),

or equivalently,

X=Y,\quad H=\Xi.

An atlas
\mathcal{A}^{\Pi}
on
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
contains local charts
\mathcal{U}_{I}^{\Pi}
parameterized by
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|I|=k
. The retract
\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is isomorphic to
(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k},\bigwedge\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is the sheaf of
1
-forms on
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
.
More information about super-Grassmannians and
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannians can be found in
[
Man
]
, see also
[
Oni1
,
Vi2
]
.

3.2.
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannians over
\mathds{R}
and
\mathds{H}

We will also consider
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannians
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}(\mathds{R})
and
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}(\mathds{H})
over
\mathds{R}
and
\mathds{H}
. These supermanifolds are defined in a similar way as
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
assuming that all coordinates are real or quaternion. In more details, to define
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}(\mathds{R})
we just repeat the construction of local charts and transition functions above assuming that we work over
\mathds{R}
. The case of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}(\mathds{H})
is slightly more complicated. Indeed, we consider charts
\mathcal{Z}_{I}
as above with even and odd coordinates
X=(x_{ij})
and
\Xi=(\xi_{ij})
, respectively, where by definition

x_{ij}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x^{ij}_{11}&x^{ij}_{12}\\
-\bar{x}^{ij}_{12}&\bar{x}^{ij}_{11}\end{array}\right),\quad\xi_{ij}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi_{11}^{ij}&\xi^{ij}_{12}\\
-\bar{\xi}^{ij}_{12}&\bar{\xi}^{ij}_{11}\end{array}\right).

Here
x^{ij}_{ab}
are even complex variables and
\bar{x}^{ij}_{ab}
is the complex conjugation of
x^{ij}_{ab}
. Further, any
\xi_{ab}^{ij}
is an odd complex variable and
\bar{\xi}_{ab}^{ij}
is its complex conjugation. (Recall that a complex conjugation of a complex odd variable
\eta=\eta_{1}+i\eta_{2}
is
\bar{\eta}:=\eta_{1}-i\eta_{2}
, where
\eta_{i}
is a real odd variable.) To obtain
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}(\mathds{H})
we repeat step by step the construction above.

3.3.
The order of a
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian

We start this subsection with the following theorem proved in
[
Oni1
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 3
.

A
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is split if and only if
(n,k)=(2,1)
.

From
[
Oni1
, Theorem 4.4]
it follows that for the
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
we have

H^{1}(M,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{p})=\{0\},

p\geq 3.

This implies the following statement.

Proposition 4
.

A
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is a supermanifold of order
2
for
(n,k)\neq(2,1)
. The order of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2,1}
is
\infty
, since this supermanifold is split.

Proof.

To show the statement consider the exact sequence (
1
) for
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
and the corresponding exact sequence of cohomology sets

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p+2)}\mathcal{O})\to H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathcal{O})\to H^{1}(M,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{2p})\to.

Since
H^{1}(M,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{p})=\{0\}
for
p\geq 3
, see
[
Oni1
, Theorem 4.4]
, and
\mathcal{A}ut_{(2q)}\mathcal{O}={\rm id}
for sufficiently large
p
, we have by induction

H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2q)}\mathcal{O})=\{\epsilon\},\,\,\,q\geq 2.

Therefore
H_{2p}=\{\epsilon\}
for
p\geq 2
. Since by Theorem
3
, the
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is not split for
(n,k)\neq(2,1)
, the corresponding to
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, where
(n,k)\neq(2,1)
, cohomology class
\gamma
is not trivial. Therefore,
\gamma\in H_{2}\setminus H_{4}=H_{2}\setminus\{\epsilon\}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Lifting of homotheties on a non-split supermanifold

4.1.
Lifting of an automorphism in terms of Green’s cohomology

On any vector bundle
\mathds{E}
over
M
we can define a natural automorphism
\phi_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\mathds{C}^{*}=\mathds{C}\setminus\{0\}
. In more details,
\phi_{\alpha}
multiplies any local section by the complex number
\alpha
. Let
r
be the minimum between positive integers
k
such that
\alpha^{k}=1
. The number
r
is called the
order

\textsf{ord}(\phi_{\alpha})
of the automorphism
\phi_{\alpha}
. If such a number does not exist we put
\textsf{ord}(\phi_{\alpha})=\infty
. In this section we study a possibility of lifting of
\phi_{\alpha}
on a non-split supermanifold corresponding to
\mathds{E}
.

A possibility of lifting of an automorphism (or an action of a Lie group) to a non-split supermanifold was studied in
[
Oni2
]
, see also
[
BO
, Proposition 3.1]
for a proof of a particular case. In particular the following result was obtained there. Denote by
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}\mathds{E}
the group of automorphisms of
\mathds{E}
, which are not necessary identical on
M
. Clearly, we have
\operatorname{Aut}\mathds{E}\subset\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}\mathds{E}
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\gamma\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
be a Green cohomology class of
\mathcal{M}
. Then
{\sf B}\in\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}\mathds{E}
lifts to
\mathcal{M}
if and only if for the induced map in the cohomology group we have
{\sf B}(\gamma)=\gamma
.

Consider the case
{\sf B}=\phi_{\alpha}
in details. Let us choose an acyclic covering
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{a}\}_{a\in I}
of
M
. Then by the Leray theorem, we have an isomorphism
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})\simeq H^{1}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
, where
H^{1}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O})
is the C̆ech 1-cohomology set corresponding to
\mathcal{U}
. Let
(\gamma_{ab})
be a C̆ech cocycle representing
\gamma
with respect to this isomorphism. Then

\displaystyle\gamma=\phi_{\alpha}(\gamma)\,\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\,\gamma_{ab}=u_{a}\circ\phi_{\alpha}(\gamma_{ab})\circ u_{b}^{-1}=u_{a}\circ\phi_{\alpha}\circ\gamma_{ab}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}\circ u_{b}^{-1},

where
u_{c}\in\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}(U_{c})
. In Theorem
7
we will show that we always can find a Čech cocycle
(\gamma_{ab})
representing the cohomology class
\gamma
such that

(3)

\gamma_{ab}=\phi_{\alpha}(\gamma_{ab})=\phi_{\alpha}\circ\gamma_{ab}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}.

4.2.
Natural gradings in a superdomain

Let us consider a superdomain
\mathcal{U}:=(U,\mathcal{O})
, where
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{F}\otimes\bigwedge(\xi_{1},\ldots\xi_{m})
and
\mathcal{F}
is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
U
, with local coordinates
(x_{a},\xi_{b})
. For any
\alpha\in\mathds{C}^{*}
we define an automorphism
\theta_{\alpha}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}
of order
r=\textsf{ord}(\theta_{\alpha})
given by
\theta_{\alpha}(x_{a})=x_{a}
and
\theta_{\alpha}(\xi_{b})=\alpha\xi_{b}
. Clearly
\theta_{\alpha}
defines the following
\mathds{Z}_{r}
-grading (or
\mathds{Z}
-grading if
r=\infty
) in
\mathcal{O}
:

(4)

\mathcal{O}=\bigoplus_{\tilde{k}\in\mathds{Z}_{r}}\mathcal{O}^{\tilde{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{O}^{\tilde{k}}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}\,\,|\,\,\theta_{\alpha}(f)=\alpha^{\tilde{k}}f\}.

If
r=2
, the decomposition (
4
) coincides with the standard decomposition of
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\bar{1}}
into even and odd parts

\mathcal{O}_{\bar{0}}=\mathcal{O}^{\tilde{0}},\quad\mathcal{O}_{\bar{1}}=\mathcal{O}^{\tilde{1}}.

4.3.
Lifting of an automorphism
\phi_{\alpha}
, local picture

Let
\mathds{E}
be a vector bundle,
\mathcal{E}
be the sheaf of section of
\mathds{E}
,
(M,\bigwedge\mathcal{E})
be the corresponding split supermanifold, and
\mathcal{M}=(M,\mathcal{O})
be a (non-split) supermanifold with the retract
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}\simeq(M,\bigwedge\mathcal{E})
. Recall that the automorphism
\phi_{\alpha}
of
\mathds{E}
multiplies any local section of
\mathds{E}
by the complex number
\alpha
. We say that
\psi_{\alpha}\in H^{0}(M,\mathcal{A}ut\mathcal{O})
is a
lift
of
\phi_{\alpha}
if
\mathrm{gr}(id,\psi_{\alpha})=(id,\wedge\phi_{\alpha})
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathcal{V}_{a}\}
be any atlas on
\mathcal{M}
and let
\mathcal{V}_{a}\in\mathcal{B}
be a chart with even and odd coordinates
(x_{i},\xi_{j})
, respectively. In any such
\mathcal{V}_{a}\in\mathcal{B}
we can define an automorphism
\theta_{\alpha}^{a}=\theta_{\alpha}^{a}(\mathcal{V}_{a})
as in Section
4.2
depending on
\mathcal{V}_{a}
. This is
\theta^{a}_{\alpha}(x_{i})=x_{i}
and
\theta^{a}_{\alpha}(\xi_{j})=\xi_{j}
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\psi_{\alpha}
be a lift of the automorphism
\phi_{\alpha}
of order
r=\textsf{ord}(\phi_{\alpha})
.

(1)

If
r
is even, then there exists an atlas
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{U}_{a}\}
on
\mathcal{M}
with local coordinates
(x^{a}_{i},\xi^{a}_{j})
in
\mathcal{U}_{a}=(U_{a},\mathcal{O}|_{U_{a}})
such that

\theta_{\alpha}^{a}(\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i}^{a}))=\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i}^{a}),\quad\theta_{\alpha}^{a}(\psi_{\alpha}(\xi_{k}^{a}))=\alpha\psi_{\alpha}(\xi_{k}^{a}).

(2)

If
r>1
is odd or if
r=\infty
, then there exists an atlas
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{U}_{a}\}
on
\mathcal{M}
with local coordinates
(x^{a}_{i},\xi^{a}_{j})
in
\mathcal{U}_{a}=(U_{a},\mathcal{O}|_{U_{a}})
such that

\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i}^{a})=x_{i}^{a},\quad\psi_{\alpha}(\xi_{j}^{a})=\alpha\xi_{j}^{a}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be any atlas on
\mathcal{M}
and let us fix a chart
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{A}
with coordinates
(x_{i},\xi_{j})
.
In local coordinates any lift
\psi_{\alpha}
of
\phi_{\alpha}
can be written in the following form

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i})=x_{i}+F_{2}+F_{4}+\cdots;\quad\psi_{\alpha}(\xi_{j})=\alpha(\xi_{j}+G_{3}+G_{5}\cdots),

where
F_{s}=F_{s}(x_{i},\xi_{j})
is a homogeneous polynomial in variables
\{\xi_{j}\}
of degree
s
, and the same for
G_{q}=G_{q}(x_{i},\xi_{j})
for odd
q
.
We note that

\psi_{\alpha}(F_{s})=\alpha^{s}F_{s}+\mathcal{J}^{s+1},\quad\psi_{\alpha}(G_{q})=\alpha^{q}G_{q}+\mathcal{J}^{q+1}

for any even
s
and odd
q
.
The idea of the proof is to use successively the following coordinate change

(5)

\begin{split}&(I)\quad x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2p}}F_{2p}(x_{i},\xi_{j}),\quad\xi^{\prime}_{j}=\xi_{j};\\
&(II)\quad x^{\prime\prime}_{i}=x^{\prime}_{i},\quad\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j}=\xi^{\prime}_{j}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2p}}G_{2p+1}(x^{\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime}_{j}),\end{split}

where
p=1,2,3\ldots
in the following way.

If
r=2
there is nothing to check. If
r>2
, first of all we apply (
5
)(I) and (
5
)(II) successively for
p=1
. After coordinate changes (
5
)(I) we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(x^{\prime}_{i})=\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2}}F_{2})=x_{i}+F_{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha^{2}}F_{2}+\cdots=

\displaystyle x_{i}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2}}F_{2}+\cdots=x^{\prime}_{i}+\cdots\in x^{\prime}_{i}+\mathcal{J}^{3};\quad\psi_{\alpha}(\xi^{\prime}_{j})\in\alpha\xi^{\prime}_{j}+\mathcal{J}^{3}.

After coordinate changes (
5
)(II) similarly we will have

(6)

\psi_{\alpha}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i})\in x^{\prime\prime}_{i}+\mathcal{J}^{4},\quad\psi_{\alpha}(\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j})\in\alpha\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j}+\mathcal{J}^{4}.

Now we change notations
x_{i}:=x^{\prime\prime}_{i}
and
\xi_{j}:=\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j}
. Further, since (
6
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i})=x_{i}+F_{4}+F_{6}+\cdots;\quad\psi_{\alpha}(\xi_{j})=\alpha(\xi_{j}+G_{5}+G_{7}+\cdots).

Here we used the same notations for monomials
F_{s}
and
G_{q}
as above, however after the first step these functions may change.
Now we continue to change coordinates consequentially in this way.
If
\alpha^{2p}\neq 1
for any
p\in\mathds{N}
, that is the order
r=\textsf{ord}(\phi_{\alpha})
is odd or infinite, we can continue this procedure and obtain the required coordinates. This proves the second statement.

If
r
is even we continue our procedure for
p<r/2
. Now in our new coordinates
\psi_{\alpha}
has the following form

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i})=x_{i}+F_{r}+F_{r+2}\cdots;\quad

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(\xi_{j})=\alpha\xi_{j}+\alpha G_{r+1}+\alpha G_{r+3}+\cdots.

For any
p
such that
\alpha^{2p}\neq 1
, the changes of variables inverse to (
5
)(I) and (
5
)(II) have the following form

(7)

\begin{split}&(I)\quad x_{a}=x^{\prime}_{a}+F^{\prime}(x^{\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime}_{j})_{(2p)},\quad\xi_{b}=\xi^{\prime}_{b};\\
&(II)\quad x^{\prime}_{a}=x^{\prime\prime}_{a},\quad\xi^{\prime}_{b}=\xi^{\prime\prime}_{b}+G^{\prime}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j})_{(2p+1)},\end{split}

where
F^{\prime}(x^{\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime}_{j})_{(2p)}\in\mathcal{J}^{2p}
and
G^{\prime}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j})_{(2p+1)}\in\mathcal{J}^{2p+1}
.

Now we use again the coordinate change (
5
)(I) and (
5
)(II) for
p=r+2
, successively. Explicitly after coordinate changes (
5
)(I) using (
7
) for
p=r+2
we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(x^{\prime}_{i})=\psi_{\alpha}(x_{i}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{r+2}}F_{r+2}(x_{i},\xi_{j}))=x_{i}+F_{r}(x_{i},\xi_{j})+F_{r+2}(x_{i},\xi_{j})+

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{r+2}}{1-\alpha^{r+2}}F_{r+2}(x_{i},\xi_{j})+\cdots=x_{i}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{r+2}}F_{r+2}(x_{i},\xi_{j})+F_{r}(x_{i},\xi_{j})+\cdots=

\displaystyle x^{\prime}_{i}+F_{r}(x_{i},\xi_{j})+\cdots\in x^{\prime}_{i}+F_{r}(x^{\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime}_{j})+\mathcal{J}^{r+3};

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(\xi^{\prime}_{j})\in\alpha\xi^{\prime}_{j}+\alpha G_{r+1}(x^{\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime}_{j})+\mathcal{J}^{r+3}.

After the coordinate change (
5
)(II), we will have

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i})\in x^{\prime\prime}_{i}+F_{r}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j})+\mathcal{J}^{r+4},\quad\psi_{\alpha}(\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j})\in\alpha\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j}+\alpha G_{r+1}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i},\xi^{\prime\prime}_{j})+\mathcal{J}^{r+4}.

5.
Automorphisms of the structure sheaf of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}

Let
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
be a
\Pi
-symmetric super-Grassmannian. Recall that the retract
\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is isomorphic to
(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k},\bigwedge\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is the sheaf of
1
-forms on the usual Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
. The sheaf
\Omega
is the sheaf of sections of the cotangent bundle
\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
over

. In the next subsection we recover a well-known result about the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
of
\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
.

5.1.
Automorphisms of the cotangent bundle over a Grassmannian

Let
M=\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
be the usual Grassmannian, i.e. the complex manifold that parameterizes all
k
-dimensional linear subspaces in
\mathds{C}^{n}
and let
\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
be its cotangent bundle. It is well-known result that
\operatorname{End}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)\simeq\mathds{C}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Aut}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)\simeq\mathds{C}^{*}
. For completeness we will prove this fact using use the Borel-Weil-Bott Theorem, see for example
[
A
]
for details.

Let
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathds{C})
be the general linear group,
P
be a parabolic subgroup in
G
,
R
be the reductive part of
P
and let
\mathds{E}_{\chi}\to G/P
be the homogeneous vector bundle corresponding to a representation
\chi
of

P
in the fiber
E=(\mathds{E}_{\chi})_{P}
. Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\chi}
the sheaf of holomorphic section of
\mathds{E}_{\chi}
.
In the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathds{C})=\operatorname{Lie}(G)

we fix the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}=\{\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})\}
,
the following system of positive roots

\Delta^{+}=\{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\,\,|\,\,\,\,1\leq i<j\leq n\},

and the following system of simple roots

\Phi=\{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-1}\},\,\,\,\alpha_{i}=\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}
, where
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.
Denote by
\mathfrak{t}^{*}(\mathds{R})

a real subspace in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}

spanned by
\mu_{j}
. Consider the scalar product
(\,,\,)
in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}(\mathds{R})
such that the vectors
\mu_{j}
form an orthonormal basis. An element
\gamma\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}(\mathds{R})
is called
dominant
if
(\gamma,\alpha)\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
. We assume that
B^{-}\subset P
, where
B^{-}
is the Borel subgroup corresponding to
\Delta^{-}
.

Theorem 14

(Borel-Weil-Bott)
.

Assume that the representation
\chi:P\to\operatorname{GL}(E)
is completely reducible and
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{s}
are highest weights of
\chi|R
. Then the
G
-module
H^{0}(G/P,\mathcal{E}_{\chi})
is isomorphic to the sum of irreducible
G
-modules with highest weights
\lambda_{i_{1}},...,\lambda_{i_{t}}
, where

\lambda_{i_{a}}
are dominant highest weights of
\chi|R
.

Now we apply this theorem to the case of the usual Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
. We have
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}\simeq G/P
, where
G=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathds{C})
and
P\subset G
is given by

P=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&0\\
B&C\end{array}\right)\right\},

where
A
is a complex
k\times k
-matrix. We see that
R=\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathds{C})\times\operatorname{GL}_{n-k}(\mathds{C})
. The isotropy representation
\chi
of
P
can be computed in a standard way, see for instance
[
Oni1
, Proposition 5.2]
. The representation
\chi
is completely reducible and it is equal to
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho^{*}_{2}
, where
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are standard representations of the Lie groups
\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathds{C})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{n-k}(\mathds{C})
, respectively.

Proposition 15
.

For usual Grassmannian
M=\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, where
n-k,k>0
, we have

\operatorname{End}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)\simeq\mathds{C},\quad\operatorname{Aut}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)\simeq\mathds{C}^{*}.

Proof.

The cotangent bundle
\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
over
M
is homogeneous and the corresponding representation is the dual to isotropy representation
\chi
. Let us compute the representation
\omega
of
P
corresponding to the homogeneous bundle

\operatorname{End}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)\simeq\textsf{T}(M)\otimes\textsf{T}^{*}(M).

The representation
\omega
is completely reducible and we have

\omega|R=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho^{*}_{2}\otimes\rho_{1}^{*}\otimes\rho_{2}\simeq\rho_{1}\otimes\rho^{*}_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\rho^{*}_{2}.

Therefore, we have

(1)

\omega|R=1+ad_{1}+ad_{2}+ad_{1}\otimes ad_{2}
for
k>1
and
n-k>1
;

(2)

1+ad_{2}
for
k=1
and
n-k>1
;

(3)

1+ad_{1}
for
k>1
and
n-k=1
;

(4)

1
for
k=n-k=1
,

where
1
is the trivial one dimensional representation,
ad_{1}
and
ad_{2}
are adjoint representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathds{C})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{n-k}(\mathds{C})
, respectively. Then the heights weights of the representation
\omega|R
are

(1)

0,

\mu_{1}-\mu_{k}
,
\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{n}
,
\mu_{1}-\mu_{k}+\mu_{k+1}-\mu_{n}
for
k>1
and
n-k>1
;

(2)

0,

\mu_{2}-\mu_{n}
for
k=1
and
n-k>1
;

(3)

0,

\mu_{1}-\mu_{n-1}
for
k>1
and
n-k=1
;

(4)

0
for
k=n-k=1
,

respectively.
We see that the unique dominant weight is
0
in any case. By Borel-Weil-Bott Theorem we obtain the result.
∎

The following theorem can be found for example in

Theorem 18
.

The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})
if

and if
(n,k)=(2,1)
; and
\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})
is a normal subgroup of index

in

for

,

More precisely in the case

we have

\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k})=\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})\rtimes\{{\rm id},\Phi\},

where
\Phi^{2}={\rm id}
and
\Phi\circ g\circ\Phi^{-1}=(g^{t})^{-1}
for

An additional automorphism
\Phi
can be described geometrically. (Note that an additional automorphism is not unique.)
It is well-known that
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}\simeq\operatorname{Gr}_{n,n-k}
and this isomorphism is given by
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}\ni V\mapsto V^{\perp}\in\operatorname{Gr}_{n,n-k}

is the orthogonal complement of

with respect to a bilinear form

\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}=\operatorname{Gr}_{n,n-k}
, hence the map

induces an automorphism of
\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}
, which we denote by

. This automorphism is not an element of
\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})
for
(n,k)\neq(2,1)
.

Assume that
B
is the symmetric bilinear form, given in the standard basis of
\mathds{C}^{n}
by the identity matrix. Denote the corresponding automorphism by
\Phi
.
Let us describe
\Phi
in the standard coordinates on
\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}
, given in Section

. Recall that the chart
U_{I}
on
\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X\\

\end{array}\right),

where

-matrix of local coordinates and

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X\\
E\\
\end{array}\right)\xrightarrow{\Phi}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E\\

\end{array}\right),

since

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E\\

\end{array}\right)^{t}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X\\

More general, let

, where

Remark 19
.

In case

the automorphism

described above is defined as well, however it coincides with the following automorphism from
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathds{C})

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x\\

\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\

The same in another chart.

Let us discuss properties of
\Phi
mentioned in Theorem

.
Clearly
\Phi^{2}={\rm id}
. Further, for

\displaystyle\left[(g^{t})^{-1}\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E\\

6.2.1.
Lifting of the automorphism
\Phi
to
\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}

Recall that we have

\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}\simeq(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k},\bigwedge\Omega),

where
\Omega
is the sheaf of
1
-forms on
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
. Therefore any automorphism of
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
can be naturally lifted to
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
. Indeed, the lift of an automorphism
F
of
\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
is the automorphism
(F,\wedge\operatorname{d}(F))
of
(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k},\bigwedge\Omega)
.
Further, by Theorem
17
we have

\{e\}\to H^{0}(M,\mathcal{A}ut(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}))\simeq\mathds{C}^{*}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}).

Hence,

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathds{C}^{*}\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}).

Now we see that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M})
is isomorphic to the group of all automorphisms
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
of
\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
. An automorphism
\phi_{\alpha}\in\mathds{C}^{*}
commutes with any
(F,\wedge\operatorname{d}(F))\in\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k})
. Hence we obtain the following result.

Theorem 20
.

If
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, then

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M})\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}\textsf{T}^{*}(M)\simeq\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k})\times\mathds{C}^{*}.

In other words, any automorphism of
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}
is induced by an automorphism of
\textsf{T}^{*}(M)
.

More precisely,

(1)
If
\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}=\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}
, where
k\geq 2
, then

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M})\simeq(\operatorname{PGL}_{2k}(\mathds{C})\rtimes\{{\rm id},(\Phi,\wedge d(\Phi))\})\times\mathds{C}^{*},

where
(\Phi,\wedge d(\Phi))\circ g\circ(\Phi,\wedge d(\Phi))^{-1}=(g^{t})^{-1}
for
g\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2k}(\mathds{C})
.

(2)
For other
(n,k)
, we have

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M})\simeq\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})\times\mathds{C}^{*}.

Corollary 21
.

We see, Theorem
20
, that any lift
of the automorphism
\Phi
to
\mathrm{gr}\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}

has the following form

\phi_{\alpha}\circ(\Phi,\wedge d(\Phi)),\quad\alpha\in\mathds{C}^{*}.

About lifting of the automorphism

In this subsection we use results obtained in

Oni1
]
. Recall that
\Omega

\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}

1

\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}

e\to\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p+2)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{A}ut_{(2p)}\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}\to(\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gr}})_{2p}\to 0.

The automorphism

6.2.4.
A geometric construction of a lift of
\Phi
to
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}

Together with the supermanifold
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
we can consider a
\Pi^{L}
-symmetric super-Grassmannian
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}^{L}_{n,k}
. A construction of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}^{L}_{n,k}
is similar to the construction of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
given in Section
3.1
. The difference is that we write even and odd coordinates in rows, not columns. For example a coordinate matrix
\mathcal{Z}_{I}
, where
I=\{1,\ldots,k\}
, of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}^{L}_{n,k}
has the following form

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}E&X&0&\Xi\\
0&\Xi&E&X\\
\end{array}\right)

The supermanifold
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}^{L}_{n,k}
is a super-Grassmannian of
\Pi^{L}
-symmetric
k
-dimensional superspaces in
\mathds{C}^{n|n}
, where
\Pi^{L}
is an odd involution, which is linear with respect to left coordinates in
\mathds{C}^{n|n}
. (If we write our involution
\Pi
in left coordinates, it will be superlinear, not linear, see
[
L
]
.)

In
\mathds{C}^{2k|2k}
we can consider a bilinear form given in the standard basis by the identity matrix of size
4k
. This form is not super-symmetric. The form induces a map
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}\to\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}^{L}_{2k,k}
given in charts by

(22)

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\Xi\\
E&0\\
\Xi&X\\
0&E\end{array}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}E&-X&0&-\Xi\\
0&-\Xi&E&-X\\
\end{array}\right)

For other charts, let
\mathcal{U}_{I}
, where
|I|=k
, be another chart on
\Pi\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}
with coordinates
(x_{ij},\xi_{st})
,
i,j,s,t=1,\ldots,k
, as described in Section
3.1
. Denote
J:=\{1,\ldots,2k\}\setminus I
. Then
\mathcal{U}^{L}_{J}
is a chart on
\Pi\operatorname{Gr}^{L}_{2k,k}
with coordinates
(y_{ij},\eta_{st})
,
i,j,s,t=1,\ldots,k
. Then our map is given by
y_{ij}=-x_{ij}
,
\eta_{st}=-\xi_{st}
. Since,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}E&-X&0&-\Xi\\
0&-\Xi&E&-X\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\Xi\\
E&0\\
\Xi&X\\
0&E\end{array}\right)=0.

and the same for coordinate matrices of charts
\mathcal{U}_{I}
and
\mathcal{U}^{L}_{J}
,
J:=\{1,\ldots,2k\}\setminus I
, we see that the map is independent on the choice of a chart.

Further, for
\Pi
-symmetric even matrices an
i
-transposition is defined, see (
25
),

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
B&A\\
\end{array}\right)^{t_{i}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{t}&iB^{t}\\
iB^{t}&A^{t}\\
\end{array}\right),

which satisfies
(C_{1}\cdot C_{2})^{t_{i}}=C_{2}^{t_{i}}\cdot C_{1}^{t_{i}}
. (Here
iB^{t}
is the matrix
B^{t}
, transpose to the matrix
B
, multiplied by the complex number
i=\sqrt{-1}
.) Now we can define an automorphism of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
by

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&\Xi\\
E&0\\
\Xi&X\\
0&E\end{array}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}E&-X&0&-\Xi\\
0&-\Xi&E&-X\\
\end{array}\right)\xrightarrow{{t_{i}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E&0\\
-X^{t}&-i\Xi^{t}\\
0&E\\
-i\Xi^{t}&-X^{t}\\
\end{array}\right).

In charts
\mathcal{Z}_{I}\to\mathcal{Z}_{J}
on
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, where
J:=\{1,\ldots,2k\}\setminus I
, this map is given by
y_{ij}=-x_{ji}
,
\eta_{st}=-i\xi_{ts}
. In notations of Theorem
22
, the obtained automorphism is the (unique) lift of
\phi_{i}\circ(\Phi,\wedge d(\Phi))
for
(n,k)\neq(2,1)
and obtained automorphism is equal to
\phi_{i}\circ(\Phi,\wedge d(\Phi))
for
(n,k)=(2,1)
.

We denote the automorphism described in this section by
\Theta
.

6.3.
The automorphism supergroup of
\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}

As above we denote by
\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M}):=H^{0}(M,\mathcal{T})
the Lie superalgebra of vector fields on a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
. In this section
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{C})
is a strange Lie superalgebra, see
[
Kac
]
for details. This Lie superalgebra contains all matrices over
\mathds{C}
in the following form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
B&A\\
\end{array}\right),

where
A,B
are complex square matrix of size
n
. For instance the identity matrix
E_{2n}
of size
2n
is an element of
\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{C})
. The corresponding Lie supergroup we denote by
\operatorname{Q}_{n}(\mathds{C})
. This is a subsupergroup in
\operatorname{GL}_{n|n}(\mathds{C})
which is invariant with respect to the odd involution
\Pi
. This Lie supergroup can be seen as a subseperdomain of the following form in
\operatorname{GL}_{n|n}(\mathds{C})
.

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}L&M\\
M&L\\
\end{array}\right),

where
L
is an
n\times n
-matrix of even coordinates, while
M
is an
n\times n
-matrix of odd coordinates. The corresponding to
\operatorname{Q}_{n}(\mathds{C})
Harish-Chandra pair is
(\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathds{C}),\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{C}))
.

In
[
Oni1
]
the following theorem was proved, see
[
Oni1
, Theorem 5.2]
,
[
Oni1
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
Vi2
, Section 5]
for an explicit description of
\mathfrak{v}(\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2,1})
.

Theorem 23
.

(1)
If
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, where
(n,k)\neq(2,1)
, then

\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{C})/\langle E_{2n}\rangle.

(2)
If
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2,1}
, then

\mathfrak{v}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathfrak{g}\rtimes\langle z\rangle\simeq\mathfrak{q}_{2}(\mathds{C})/\langle E_{4}\rangle\rtimes\langle z\rangle.

Here
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is a
\mathds{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra defined in the following way.

\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=V,\quad\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathds{C}),\quad\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\langle d\rangle,

where
V=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathds{C})
is the adjoint
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathds{C})
-module,
[\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\{0\}
,
[d,-]
maps identically
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and
z
is the grading operator of the
\mathds{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the element
d
corresponds to the matrix
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&E\\
E&0\\
\end{array}\right)\in\mathfrak{q}_{2}(\mathds{C})
.

Recall that the automorphism
\psi^{st}_{-1}
of the structure sheaf was defined in Corollary
11
. Denote by
\Psi^{st}_{-1}
the corresponding automorphism of the supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
. Note that for any supermanifold
(M,\mathcal{O})
the action of
{\Psi^{\rm st}_{-1}}
of
H^{0}(M,\mathcal{T})
is given by
v\mapsto\psi^{st}_{-1}\circ v\circ(\psi^{st}_{-1})^{*}=(-1)^{\tilde{v}}v
. If our supermanifold is split and
v
is a vector field of degree
k
, we have
\phi_{\alpha}\circ v\circ\phi_{\alpha^{-1}}=\alpha^{k}v
. In particular for the graded operator
z
from Theorem
23
we have
\phi_{\alpha}\circ z\circ\phi_{\alpha^{-1}}=z
.

Now everything is ready to prove the following theorem. Recall that the automorphism supergroup for a compact complex supermanifold is defined in terms of super-Harish-Chandra pairs by formula (
2
).

Theorem 24
.

(1)
If
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{n,k}
, where
n\neq 2k
, then

{\sf A}\coloneqq\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}\simeq\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})\times\{{\rm id},\Psi^{st}_{-1}\}.

The automorphism supergroup is given by the Harish-Chandra pair

(\operatorname{PGL}_{n}(\mathds{C})\times\{{\rm id},\Psi^{st}_{-1}\},\mathfrak{q}_{n}(\mathds{C})/\langle E_{2n}\rangle).

(2)
If
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2k,k}
, where
k\geq 2
, then

{\sf A}\coloneqq\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}\simeq\operatorname{PGL}_{2k}(\mathds{C})\rtimes\{{\rm id},\Theta,\Psi^{st}_{-1},\Psi^{st}_{-1}\circ\Theta\},

where
\Theta^{2}=\Psi^{st}_{-1}
,
\Psi^{st}_{-1}
is a central element of
{\sf A}
,
and
\Theta\circ g\circ\Theta^{-1}=(g^{t})^{-1}
for
g\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2k}(\mathds{C})
.

The automorphism supergroup is given by the Harish-Chandra pair

(\operatorname{PGL}_{2k}(\mathds{C})\rtimes\{{\rm id},\Psi^{st}_{-1},\Theta,\Psi^{st}_{-1}\circ\Theta\},\mathfrak{q}_{2k}(\mathds{C})/\langle E_{4k}\rangle),

where
\Theta\circ C\circ\Theta^{-1}=-C^{t_{i}}
for
C\in\mathfrak{q}_{2k}(\mathds{C})/\langle E_{4k}\rangle
and
\Psi^{st}_{-1}\circ C\circ(\Psi^{st}_{-1})^{-1}=(-1)^{\tilde{C}}C
.

(3)
If
\mathcal{M}=\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2,1}
, then

{\sf A}\coloneqq\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}\simeq\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathds{C})\times\mathds{C}^{*}.

The automorphism supergroup is given by the Harish-Chandra pair

(\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathds{C})\times\mathds{C}^{*},\mathfrak{g}\rtimes\langle z\rangle).

Here
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathds{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra described in Theorem
23
, the action of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathds{C})\times\mathds{C}^{*}
on
z
is trivial, and
\phi_{\alpha}\in\mathds{C}^{*}
multiplies
X\in\mathfrak{v}(\Pi\!\operatorname{Gr}_{2,1})_{k}
by
\alpha^{k}
.

Proof.

We use the following exact sequence of groups:

e\to H^{0}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O})\to\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}).

By (
16
) we have
H^{0}(M,\mathcal{A}ut_{(2)}\mathcal{O})=e
. Therefore,
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}
is a subgroup in

and
M

For

is a subgroup and

\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{M}

7.1.
Real structures on commutative superalgebras

Let us fix
\epsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
for
i=1,2,3
. Following Manin
[
Man
, Definition 3.6.2]
a

real structure of type
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})
on a
\mathds{C}
-superalgebra
A

is an
\mathds{R}
-linear (even) automorphism
\rho
of
A
such that

\kern 0.8pt{}^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(a))=\epsilon_{1}^{\tilde{a}}a,\qquad\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(ab)=\epsilon_{3}\epsilon_{2}^{\tilde{a}\kern 0.8pt\tilde{b}}\kern 0.8pt\kern 0.8pt{}^{\rho}\kern-0.8ptb\,\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt\kern-0.8pta,\qquad\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(\lambda a)=\bar{\lambda}\kern 0.8pt\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt\kern-0.8pta,

for
\lambda\in\mathds{C}
and for homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
, where
\tilde{a}
and
\tilde{b}
denote the parities of
a
and
b
, respectively. To any real structure
\rho
of type
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})
on
A
, we can assign a real structure
\rho^{\prime}
of type
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},-\epsilon_{3})
as follows: we put
\rho^{\prime}=-\rho
.
Moreover, we can assign a real structure
\rho^{\prime\prime}
of type
(\epsilon_{1},-\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})
as follows:
we put
\rho^{\prime\prime}(a)=\rho(a)
if
a
is even, and
\rho^{\prime\prime}(a)=i\rho(a)
when
a
is odd,
where
i=\sqrt{-1}
. See
[
BLMS
, Section 1.11.2]
.
Thus in order to classify real structures on
A
of all types
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})
,
it suffices to classify real structures of the types
(1,-1,1)
and
(-1,-1,1)
.
In this paper we consider real structures of the type
(1,-1,1)
.
Then for a commutative superalgebra
A
and for a real structure
\rho
on
A
we have

\kern 0.8pt{}^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(a))=a,\qquad\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(ab)=\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt\kern-0.8pta\,\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8ptb,\qquad\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt(\lambda a)=\bar{\lambda}\kern 0.8pt^{\rho}\kern-0.8pt\kern-0.8pta.

For a definition of a real structure on a complex Lie superalgebra see for instance
[
S
]
.

7.2.
Real structures on supermanifolds

Recall that a
real structure
on a complex-analytic manifold
M
is an anti-holomorphic involution
\mu:M\to M
, see for instance
[
ACF
]
. This definition is equivalent to the following one. Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a complex-analytic manifold, that is
\mathcal{F}
is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
M
. A homomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
of sheaves of real local algebras is called a
real structure
on
M
if

\rho^{2}=id,\quad\rho(\lambda f)=\overline{\lambda}\rho(f),

where
\lambda\in\mathds{C}
and
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
. These definitions are equivalent. Indeed, if
\mu
is a complex involution, we put

\rho(f)=\overline{\mu^{*}(f)}\in\mathcal{F}.

Conversely, if
\rho
satisfies the second definition of a real structure, we put

\mu^{*}(f)=\overline{\rho(f)},\quad\mu^{*}(\overline{f})=\rho(f)\quad\text{for}\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{F}.

Let us define a real structure on a supermanifold. A
real structure
of type
(1,-1,1)
on a complex-analytic supermanfold
{\mathcal{M}}=(M,{\mathcal{O}})
is a homomorphism
\rho:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}
of sheaves of real local superalgebras such that

\rho^{2}=id,\quad\rho(\lambda f)=\overline{\lambda}\rho(f)\qquad\text{for}\ \ \lambda\in\mathds{C},\ f\in{\mathcal{O}}.

Since
\rho
preserves the parity of elements in
\mathcal{O}
, it preserves the sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{O}
generated by odd elements. Clearly the induced sheaves homomorphism
\rho^{\prime}:\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J}\to\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J}
is a real structure on the underlying manifold
M
.

As in the case of complex-analytic manifolds let us give another definition of a real structure. To any complex-analytic supermanfold
{\mathcal{M}}=(M,{\mathcal{O}})
of dimension
(n|m)
, we can assign a real supermanifold
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathds{R}}
of dimension
(2n|2m)
. This procedure is described for instance in
[
Kal
, Section. From complex to real]
. There a definition of a complex-conjugation of a function was given. This definition can be regarded as follows. Any super-function
f\in\mathcal{O}
is at the same time a morphism from
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathds{C}^{1|1}=\mathds{C}^{1|0}\oplus\mathds{C}^{0|1}
. More precisely, an even function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\bar{0}}
can be regarded as a morphism
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathds{C}^{1|0}
and an odd element
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\bar{1}}
can be regarded as a morphism
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathds{C}^{0|1}
.
Further, let
(z,\eta)
be the standard complex coordinates in
\mathds{C}^{1|1}
, where
z=z_{1}+iz_{2}
and
\eta=\eta_{1}+i\eta_{2}
and
z_{i}
,
\eta_{j}
are standard real even and odd coordinates in
\mathds{R}^{2|2}
, respectively. In
\mathds{C}^{1|1}
we define a complex conjugation by the following formula

\overline{z}=z_{1}-iz_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\eta}=\eta_{1}-i\eta_{2}.

Then for any
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathds{C}^{1|1}
its complex conjugation
\overline{f}
can be regarded as a composition of the morphism
f
and the complex conjugation in
\mathds{C}^{1|1}
.
We also have

\overline{f_{1}\cdot f_{2}}=\overline{f_{1}}\cdot\overline{f_{2}},\quad f_{1},\,f_{2}\in\mathcal{O}.

If
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathds{C}^{1|1}
is a morphism (or a super-function), we have for any morphism
F=(F_{0},F^{*}):\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
the following formula

(23)

F^{*}(f)=f\circ F.

Now a
real structure
\mu
on a supermanifold
{\mathcal{M}}
is an anti-holomorphic involutive automorphism
\mu=(\mu_{0},\mu^{*})
of the supermanifold
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathds{R}}
. That is
\mu^{*}
maps
\mathcal{O}
to
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
and
\mu^{2}={\rm id}
.

Proposition 25
.

Two definitions of a real structure on
\mathcal{M}
coincide.

Proof.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{0},\mu^{*})
be an anti-holomorphic involutive automorphism of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathds{R}}
. Then we define

\rho(f):=\overline{\mu^{*}(f)}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{O}.

Let us check that
\rho\circ\rho={\rm id}
. Using (
23
) we have

\displaystyle\rho(\rho(f))=\rho(\overline{f\circ\mu})=\overline{\mu^{*}(\overline{f\circ\mu})}=\overline{\overline{f\circ\mu\circ\mu}}=f.

Other properties of
\rho
are clear.

On the other hand, let
\rho:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}
be a real structure according to the first definition. By definition we put

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(f)=\overline{\rho(f)}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{O};\quad\mu^{*}(f)=\rho(\overline{f})\quad\text{for}\,\,\,f\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}.

Clearly
\mu
is anti-holomorphic. Further, we have

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\mu^{*}(f))=\mu^{*}(\overline{\rho(f)})=\rho({\rho(f)})=f\quad\text{for}\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{O};

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(\mu^{*}(f))=\mu^{*}({\rho(\overline{f})})=\overline{\rho({\rho(\overline{f})})}=f\quad\text{for}\,\,\,f\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}.

The proof is complete. ∎

Two real structures
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
on
{\mathcal{M}}

are called
equivalent
if the pairs
({\mathcal{M}},\mu)
and
({\mathcal{M}},\mu^{\prime})
are isomorphic,
that is, if there exists a (complex-analytic) isomorphism of supermanifolds
\beta\colon{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
such that
\mu^{\prime}\circ\beta=\beta\circ\mu
.

.

.

Lemma A.18
.

For a cocycle
c=(F,\Theta)
consider the twisted group
{}_{c}G
with

.
Then

(\kern 0.8pt_{c}G)({\mathds{R}})=\{g\in\operatorname{PGL}(n,\mathds{C})\ |\ g\kern 0.8ptF\kern 0.8pt\bar{g}\kern 0.8pt^{t}=F\}.

\displaystyle(\kern 0.8pt_{c}G)({\mathds{R}})

\displaystyle=\big\{g\ |\ c({\bar{g}},1)c^{-1}=(g,1)\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{g\ |\ (F,\Theta)\cdot({\bar{g}},1)\cdot(F^{t},\Theta^{-1})=(g,1)\big\}.

Lemma A.19
.

Let
c=(F,\Theta)
,
\overline{F}\kern 0.8pt^{t}=F
(then
F,\Theta)
is a 1-cocycle).
Then

(\kern 0.8pt_{c}X)({\mathds{R}})=\{W\subset V\ |\ \ \dim W=k\ \ \text{and}\ \ \bar{x}\kern 0.8pt^{t}\kern 0.8ptF^{-1}y=0\ \text{for all}\ \,x,y\in W\}.

Proof.

We write

(\kern 0.8pt_{c}X)({\mathds{R}})=\{W\in\operatorname{Gr}_{k}(V)\ \,|\ \,(F,\Theta)\cdot\overline{W}=W\}.

We have

(F,\Theta)\cdot\overline{W}=F\cdot\overline{W}^{\bot B_{0}}.

Thus the condition for

to lie in
(\kern 0.8pt_{c}X)({\mathds{R}})
is

(39)

F\cdot\overline{W}^{\bot B_{0}}=W,

or, equivalently,

(40)

\overline{W}^{\bot B_{0}}=F^{-1}\cdot W.

This implies

B_{0}(\bar{x},F^{-1}\cdot y)=0\quad\ \text{for all}\ \,x,y\in W,

(41)

\bar{x}\kern 0.8pt^{t}F^{-1}y=0\quad\ \text{for all}\ \,x,y\in W.

) holds,

and comparing the dimensions, we obtain in turn the equalities (

) and (

),
which gives

, as required.
∎

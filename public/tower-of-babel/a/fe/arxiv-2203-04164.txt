Weakly Flux-Tunable Superconducting Qubit
José M. Chávez-Garcia, Firat Solgun, Jared B. Hertzberg, Oblesh Jinka, Markus Brink, Baleegh Abdo

Abstract
Flux-tunable qubits are a useful resource for superconducting quantum processors. They can be used to perform cPhase gates, facilitate fast reset protocols, avoid qubit-frequency collisions in large processors, and enable certain fast readout schemes. However, flux-tunable qubits suffer from a trade-off between their tunability range and sensitivity to flux noise. Optimizing this trade-off is particularly important for enabling fast, high-fidelity, all-microwave cross-resonance gates in large, high-coherence processors. This is mainly because cross-resonance gates set stringent conditions on the frequency landscape of neighboring qubits, which are difficult to satisfy with non-tunable transmons due to their relatively large fabrication imprecision. To solve this problem, we realize a coherent, flux-tunable, transmon-like qubit, which exhibits a frequency tunability range as small as 43 43 MHz, and whose frequency, anharmonicity and tunability range are set by a few experimentally achievable design parameters. Such a weakly tunable qubit is useful for avoiding frequency collisions in a large lattice while limiting its susceptibility to flux noise.

Here, we realize a superconducting qubit, named weakly tunable qubit (WTQ), that retains the desirable properties of single-JJ transmons in multi-qubit architectures and whose frequency tunes weakly with applied magnetic flux. Such a limited tunability could solve the frequency-collision problem in multi-qubit architectures while maintaining high coherence. This tunability could also improve qubit relaxation times by evading two-level systems (TLSs) in frequency space. According to Ref.

;

Prior to introducing the WTQ circuit, we briefly highlight the drawbacks of existing tunable transmons, namely, the symmetric dc-SQUID transmon and the asymmetric dc-SQUID transmon, whose circuits are shown in Fig.
1

, results in a significant dephasing away from the sweet spot

. In the asymmetric case on the other hand, a smaller tunability and sensitivity to flux noise can be achieved with large JJ-area ratio. But even with a ratio as high as
15
to
1
, the smallest achievable tunability is about
330
MHz, which is much larger than what is needed to avoid frequency collisions. Furthermore, since the Josephson energy of the large-size JJ in the asymmetric SQUID (i.e.,

C_{1}^{\prime}=C_{1}+C_{J_{1}}
and
C_{2}^{\prime}=C_{2}+C_{J_{2}}+C_{J_{3}}
. Next we do a capacitance
re-scaling as required by the Born-Oppenheimer treatment of
Brito

\mathbf{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{C_{a}}&0&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{C_{11}}&-\beta\sqrt{C_{22}}&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{C_{22}}&0\\
0&0&0&\sqrt{C_{b}}\end{pmatrix}

(40)

with

\displaystyle C_{11}

\displaystyle=C_{J_{2}}\frac{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}{C_{a}C_{b}},

(41)

\displaystyle C_{22}

\displaystyle=C_{J_{3}}\frac{C_{A}C_{B}}{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}},

(42)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{C_{J_{2}}C_{J_{3}}}{C_{a}C_{b}}}\frac{\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)}{\sqrt{C_{A}C_{B}}},

(43)

and

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,3}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\prime}+\frac{C_{c}\left(C_{2}+C_{J_{3}}\right)}{C_{2}+C_{c}+C_{J_{3}}},

(44)

\displaystyle C_{\beta,3}

\displaystyle=C_{2}+C_{c}+C_{J_{3}}.

(45)

All four coordinate transformations can be combined into one, i.e.,
\mathbf{R}_{t}
, such that

\mathbf{R}_{t}=\mathbf{R}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{1}\mathbf{R}_{2}\mathbf{A}^{-1}.

(46)

The initial capacitance matrix
\mathbf{C}_{0}
is transformed into
the identity matrix by the total transformation
\mathbf{R}_{t}

as

\mathbf{C}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{t}^{T}\mathbf{C}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{t}=\mathbf{1}.

(47)

This ensures the choice of the irrotational gauge that removes the
inconsistencies in the decoherence rate calculations as discussed
in
You-Koch
.

Initial flux coordinate vector
\mathbf{\Phi}
is transformed into
the final capacitance re-scaled coordinate vector
\mathbf{f}
by

\mathbf{\Phi}=\mathbf{R}_{t}\mathbf{f}.

(48)

Phases across the junctions can be written in terms of the final coordinates

\mathbf{f}=\left(f_{1,}f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\right)^{T}
as

\displaystyle\varphi_{J_{1}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{11}f_{1}+\alpha_{12}f_{2}+\alpha_{13}f_{3}+\alpha_{14}f_{4},

(49)

\displaystyle\varphi_{J_{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{21}f_{1}+\alpha_{22}f_{2}+\alpha_{23}f_{3}+\alpha_{24}f_{4},

(50)

\displaystyle\varphi_{J_{3}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{31}f_{1}+\alpha_{32}f_{2}+\alpha_{33}f_{3}+\alpha_{34}f_{4},

(51)

with

\alpha_{11}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{a}}},\quad\alpha_{21}=\left(\frac{C_{c}}{C_{b}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{a}}},\quad\alpha_{31}=\left(\frac{C_{c}}{C_{b}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{a}}},

(52)

\alpha_{14}=0,\quad\alpha_{24}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{b}}},\quad\alpha_{34}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{b}}},

(53)

\alpha_{12}=-\frac{C_{c}\sqrt{C_{J_{2}}}}{\sqrt{C_{a}C_{b}C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}},\quad\alpha_{13}=-\frac{C_{c}\sqrt{C_{J_{3}}}}{\sqrt{C_{A}C_{B}C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}},

(54)

\alpha_{22}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{J_{2}}}}\sqrt{\frac{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}{C_{a}C_{b}}},\quad\alpha_{23}=0,

(55)

\alpha_{32}=-\frac{\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)\sqrt{C_{J_{2}}}}{\sqrt{C_{a}C_{b}C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}},\quad\alpha_{33}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{J_{3}}}}\sqrt{\frac{C_{A}C_{B}}{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}}.

(56)

Hence, we can write the system Hamiltonian in the capacitance re-scaled
final coordinates
\mathbf{f}
as

\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}_{f}^{T}\mathbf{q}_{f}+U(\mathbf{f}),

(57)

where
\mathbf{q}_{f}
is the vector of momenta canonically conjugate
to the final coordinates
\mathbf{f}
and the potential
U(\mathbf{f})

is given by

U(\mathbf{f})=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{f}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{t}^{T}\mathbf{M}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{t}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{f}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{t}^{T}\mathbf{S}_{0}I_{B}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}E_{J_{i}}\mathrm{cos}(\varphi_{J_{i}}).

(58)

Born-Oppenheimer Approximation

We perform the Born-Oppenheimer approximation following
Brito
.
The coordinates
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
are fast coordinates denoted
by the vector
\mathrm{\mathbf{f}_{\bot}}=(f_{2},\,f_{3})
and will
be eliminated. Since the potential seen is very steep along the direction
of the fast coordinates it can be approximated with a harmonic potential
as given by the Eq. (14) in
Brito

U(\mathbf{f})\approx V(\mathbf{f}_{\|})+\underset{i}{\sum}a_{i}(\mathbf{f}_{\bot})_{i}+\underset{i,j}{\sum}b_{ij}(\mathbf{f}_{\bot})_{i}(\mathbf{f}_{\bot})_{j},

(59)

where
\mathrm{\mathbf{f}_{\|}}=(f_{1},\,f_{4})
is the vector
holding the slow coordinates and the matrix
\mathbf{b}=[b_{ij}]

is the 2x2 sector (corresponding to the coordinates
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
)
of the transformed
\mathbf{M}_{0}
matrix in the final frame; i.e.
of the matrix
\mathbf{R}_{t}^{T}\mathbf{M}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{t}
:

\mathbf{b}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\alpha_{22}^{2}C_{J_{2}}^{2}L_{1}}&\frac{1}{C_{23}L_{1}}\\
\frac{1}{C_{23}L_{1}}&\frac{1}{\alpha_{33}^{2}C_{J_{3}}^{2}L_{2}}+\frac{(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c})^{2}C_{J_{3}}}{C_{A}C_{B}C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}L_{1}}\end{array}\right),

(60)

where we defined
C_{23}^{-1}=\sqrt{\frac{C_{J_{3}}C_{a}C_{b}}{C_{J_{2}}C_{A}C_{B}}}\frac{(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c})}{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}L_{1}}
.
The vector
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\,a_{2})
in Eq. (
59
)
holds the entries (corresponding to the fast coordinates
f_{2}

and
f_{3})
of the transformed
\mathbf{S}_{0}
vector in the final
frame; i.e. of the vector
\mathbf{R}_{t}^{T}\mathbf{S}_{0}
(with
the scale factor
I_{B}/\varphi_{0}
):

\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{M_{1}}{\alpha_{22}C_{J_{2}}L_{1}}\\
-\frac{(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c})\sqrt{C_{J_{3}}}M_{1}}{\sqrt{C_{A}C_{B}C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}L_{1}}+\frac{M_{2}}{\alpha_{33}C_{J_{3}}L_{2}}\end{array}\right)\left(\frac{I_{B}}{\varphi_{0}}\right),

(61)

where
\varphi_{0}\equiv\Phi_{0}/2\pi
is the reduced flux quantum.

The potential is centered around

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{f}_{\perp}^{\rm{min}}\right)_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{22}C_{J_{2}}\left(M_{1}+\frac{\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)C_{J_{3}}}{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}M_{2}\right)\\
-\alpha_{33}C_{J_{3}}M_{2}\end{pmatrix}\left(\frac{I_{B}}{\varphi_{0}}\right)

(62)

for the fast coordinates (here, we dropped terms quadratic in the small
inductive coupling coefficients
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
). This is the DC
component of the fast coordinates that does not depend on the slow
coordinates. We used the Eq. (16) of
Brito
to calculate
\left(\mathbf{f}_{\perp}^{min}\right)_{0}
:

\left(\mathbf{f}_{\perp}^{\rm{min}}\right)_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{b}^{-1}\mathbf{a}.

(63)

Hence, the reduced DC flux bias in the phases of the junctions is

\displaystyle\mathbf{\varphi}_{x}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi_{x,1}^{(0)}\\
\varphi_{x,2}^{(0)}\\
\varphi_{x,3}^{(0)}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{12}&\alpha_{13}\\
\alpha_{22}&\alpha_{23}\\
\alpha_{32}&\alpha_{33}\end{array}\right)\left(\mathbf{f}_{\perp}^{min}\right)_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{C_{c}\left(C_{J_{3}}M_{2}-C_{J_{2}}M_{1}\right)}{C_{a}C_{b}}\\
\frac{C_{\alpha,3}C_{\beta,3}}{C_{a}C_{b}}M_{1}+\frac{\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)C_{J_{3}}}{C_{a}C_{b}}M_{2}\\
-\frac{\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)C_{J_{2}}}{C_{a}C_{b}}M_{1}-\frac{C_{\alpha,2}C_{\beta,2}}{C_{a}C_{b}}M_{2}\end{pmatrix}\left(\frac{I_{B}}{\varphi_{0}}\right),

(70)

where we defined

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,2}

\displaystyle=C_{1}^{\prime}+\frac{C_{c}(C_{2}+C_{J_{2}})}{C_{2}+C_{c}+C_{J_{2}}},

(71)

\displaystyle C_{\beta,2}

\displaystyle=C_{2}+C_{c}+C_{J_{2}}.

(72)

We note here that
\varphi_{x}=\varphi_{x,2}^{(0)}-\varphi_{x,3}^{(0)}=\left(M_{1}+M_{2}\right)\left(\frac{I_{B}}{\varphi_{0}}\right)
, as expected.

We perform the Born-Oppenheimer approximation by expanding the cosine
potentials around the DC flux biases in Eq. (
62
) for
the fast coordinates to obtain the following effective Hamiltonian:

\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathcal{C}^{-1}\mathbf{q}-E_{J_{1}}\mathrm{cos}\left(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{x,1}^{(0)}\right)-E_{2}\mathrm{cos}\left(\varphi_{2}+\varphi_{x}^{(2)}\right),

(73)

where

\mathcal{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}&-C_{c}\\
-C_{c}&C_{2}^{\prime}+C_{c}\end{array}\right),

(74)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(E_{J_{2}}+E_{J_{3}}\right)\mathrm{cos}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{x}}{2}\right)\sqrt{1+d^{2}\mathrm{tan}^{2}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{x}}{2}\right)},

(75)

\displaystyle\varphi_{x}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\varphi_{x,2}^{(0)}+\varphi_{x,3}^{(0)}\right)}{2}-\mathrm{tan}^{-1}\left(d\mathrm{tan}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{x}}{2}\right)\right),

(76)

with
d=\left(E_{J_{3}}-E_{J_{2}}\right)/\left(E_{J_{2}}+E_{J_{3}}\right)
,

\varphi_{1}=f_{1}/\sqrt{C_{a}}
,
\varphi_{2}=f_{2}/\sqrt{C_{b}}
,
E_{J_{1}}=\varphi_{0}I_{J1}
,
E_{J_{2}}=\varphi_{0}I_{J2}
, and
E_{J_{3}}=\varphi_{0}I_{J3}
.

Eq. (
73
) is the Hamiltonian corresponding to two
transmon qubits with Josephson energies
E_{J_{1}}
and
E_{2}

and shunting capacitances
C_{1}^{\prime}
and
C_{2}^{\prime}

coupled electrostatically with capacitance
C_{c}
.

IV
WTQ frequency and anharmonicity

At this point we can calculate the qubit frequency
\omega_{q}
using
formulas derived in
Z-paper

\omega_{q}=\omega_{1}\sqrt{1-\frac{r^{2}\omega_{1}^{2}}{\omega_{2}^{2}-(1-r^{2})\omega_{1}^{2}}},

(77)

where
\omega_{1}
is the bare frequency of the qubit mode given
by
Z-paper

\omega_{1}=\omega_{J_{1}}-\frac{E_{C_{1}}/\hbar}{1-E_{C_{1}}/(\hbar\omega_{J_{1}})},

(78)

with

E_{C_{1}}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)},

(79)

and

\omega_{J_{1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{J_{1}}\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)}}.

(80)

The coupling coefficient
r
in Eq. (
77
) between the modes
of the WTQ is defined by

r=\frac{C_{c}}{\sqrt{C_{1}^{\prime}C_{2}^{\prime}+C_{c}\left(C_{1}^{\prime}+C_{2}^{\prime}\right)}}.

(81)

The charging energy
E_{C_{2}}
for the high-frequency SQUID mode is

E_{C_{2}}=\frac{e^{2}\left(1+r^{2}\right)}{2\left(C_{2}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)}.

(82)

Bare frequency
\omega_{2}
of the SQUID mode is given by

\omega_{2}=\omega_{J_{2}}-\frac{E_{C_{2}}/\hbar}{1-E_{C_{2}}/\left(\hbar\omega_{J_{2}}\right)},

(83)

with

\omega_{J_{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{J_{S}}\frac{\left(C_{2}^{\prime}+C_{c}\right)}{\left(1+r^{2}\right)}}},

(84)

where we defined the effective SQUID inductance
L_{J_{S}}

L_{J_{S}}=\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{L_{J_{2}}}+\dfrac{1}{L_{J_{3}}}\right)\left|\mathrm{cos}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{x}}{2}\right)\right|\sqrt{1+d^{2}\mathrm{tan^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{x}}{2}\right)}\right)^{-1}.

(85)

Anharmonicity
\alpha
of the qubit mode can be estimated by

Z-paper

\alpha=-E_{C_{1}}\left(\frac{\omega_{J_{1}}}{\omega_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{r^{2}\omega_{1}^{2}}{\omega_{2}^{2}-(1-r^{2})\omega_{1}^{2}}\right)^{3}.

(86)

In Fig.
5
(a),(b) we plot the
transition frequency
f_{01}=f_{q}=\omega_{q}/2\pi
and the anharmonicity
\alpha
of a WTQ as a function of the normalized applied flux. The device parameters in this example are chosen to yield a WTQ with

and
\alpha
of about
5
GHz and
300
MHz, respectively, and frequency tunability
\delta=50
MHz. In the calculation, we use the analytical
formulas of Eqs. (
77
) and (
86
), which we plot as red dashed curves and compare them to the results obtained using the exact diagonalization
of the qubit Hamiltonian specified in Eq. (
73
) in the charge basis
(Bloch-wave basis), which we plot as blue solid curves. As seen in Fig.
5
(b), the WTQ anharmonicity varies with the applied flux. But the variation is relatively small of about
17
MHz in this example, between the minimum and maximum sweet spots (based on the exact calculation). Similarly, we plot in Fig.
5
(c), using blue solid and red dashed curves, the exact and analytical solutions for the high-frequency mode of the qubit
f_{10}
versus the normalized applied flux. The slight bending in the exact diagonalization curve (the solid blue), seen around
14
GHz, is due to the crossing of
f_{10}
, the first excited level of the SQUID oscillator, and the third level of the qubit.

VIII
Conclusion

We introduce weakly tunable superconducting qubits whose frequency can be tuned with external magnetic flux. The qubits comprise capacitively shunted JJ and asymmetric dc-SQUID, sharing one electrode and capacitively coupled via the other two.

We develop a theoretical model that captures the device physics and its coupling to the flux biasing circuit. By solving the full Hamilitonian of the system, we calculate the various qubit properties as a function of the circuit parameters and applied flux. We also calculate the qubit relaxation and dephasing times associated with the flux-biasing circuit. Furthermore, we derive analytical expressions that yield approximate values for the various device parameters and coherence times.

Moreover, we fabricate and test two superconducting chips containing several variations of these qubits. We show that they can achieve frequency tunablity ranges as low as
43-287
MHz with only a small asymmetry in the size of their SQUID JJs (
\alpha_{J}=2-3.5

350

15

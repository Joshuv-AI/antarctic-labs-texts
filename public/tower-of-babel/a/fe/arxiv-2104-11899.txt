On Abelian subvarieties of bounded degree in a polarized Abelian variety
Lucio Guerra

Abstract
If A A is an Abelian variety, endowed with a polarization L L , we study the function N A ​ ( t ) N_{A}(t) which counts the number of Abelian subvarieties S S in A A such that for the induced polarization L | S L|_{S} the Euler characteristic χ ⁡ ( L | S ) \chi(L|_{S}) is bounded above by t t . We give an estimate for the asymptotic order of growth of this function.



1
Introduction

Let
A
be an Abelian variety, endowed with a polarization
L
. (We view polarizations as divisor classes, rather than line bundles.) Let
S
be a subvariety of
A
, of dimension
n
. The intersection number

S\cdot L^{n}=(L|_{S})^{n}

can be considered as a geometric degree of
S
, at least when
L
is a very ample divisor class. If
S
is an Abelian subvariety of
A
, the geometric form of the Riemann Roch theorem for Abelian varieties says that

\frac{(L|_{S})^{n}}{n!}=\chi(L|_{S})

is the Euler characteristic of the line bundle associated to the restricted polarization.
So, for an Abelian subvariety, the Euler characteristic
\chi(L|_{S})
can be considered as a ‘reduced degree’. Denote with the symbol
{\sf AS}_{A}
the collection of all Abelian subvarieties of
A
. For every positive integer or real number
t
, define

N_{A}(t):=\#\big\{S\in{\sf AS}_{A}\mbox{ s.t. }\chi(L|_{S})\leq t\big\}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt.

That this is a finite number was already known to Poincaré, at least for the subcollection of subvarieties of dimension
n=1
. The general statement follows from a result of Birkenhake and Lange
[
2
]
, to the effect that the collection of all Abelian subvarieties with bounded exponent in
A
is finite. In a couple of papers
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
we studied the problem of giving an asymptotic estimate for the finite number of elliptic curves with bounded degree in
A
. The aim of the present work is to show that the method of the previous papers can be adjusted to the general situation. The argument leads to make explicit the relation between the rate of growth of the counting function
N_{A}(t)
and the complete decomposition of
A
up to isogeny into a product of simple Abelian varieties.

Assume that the complete decomposition of
A
is given by an isogeny

A\approx B_{1}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times B_{q}^{k_{q}}

where
B_{1},\ldots,B_{q}
are simple Abelian varieties, each other non-isogenous,
and where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}
are positive integers. Define the integers:
k
, the maximum among the multiplicities
k_{j}
, and
h
, the maximum rank of an endomorphism group
End(B_{j})
.
In terms of these data, we prove the following

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an asymptotic estimate

N_{A}(t)=O\left(t^{\,q(kh+2)(k-1)}\right).

The main tools for the argument are: a thorough use of the complete reducibility theorem in the relative version involving a given polarization (cf. §
2
, especially Proposition
2.1
), and a classical result in Number Theory concerning the number of lattice points in a bounded region of Euclidean space (cf. §
3.3
). The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in the final §
6
.

2
Some remarks about reducibility

2.1
Reducibility with respect to a polarization

Let
A
be an Abelian variety, with a polarization
L
. Let
S
be an Abelian subvariety of
A
. There is in
A
an Abelian subvariety
S^{\prime}
such that

-

S\cap S^{\prime}
is a finite set and
S+S^{\prime}=A
, so that
the sum map

s:S\times S^{\prime}\rightarrow A

is an isogeny, and

-

the pullback polarization from
A
splits as

s^{\ast}(L)=p^{\ast}(L|_{S})+(p^{\prime})^{\ast}(L|_{S^{\prime}})

where
p,p^{\prime}
denote the natural projections of the product
S\times S^{\prime}
to the factors
S,S^{\prime}
, respectively.
The complementary Abelian subvariety
S^{\prime}
is uniquely determined.

2.2
On the degree of the sum isogeny

In the setting of the preceding section, the following holds:

\deg(s)\;\chi(L)=\chi(L|_{S})\;\chi(L|_{S^{\prime}}).

In fact, one has
\chi(s^{\ast}(L))=\deg(s)\,\chi(L)
(the behavior of the Euler characteristic under an etale finite morphism) and on the other hand
\chi(s^{\ast}(L))=\chi(p^{\ast}(L|_{S})+(p^{\prime})^{\ast}(L|_{S^{\prime}}))=\chi(L|_{S})\,\chi(L|_{S^{\prime}})
(by the Künneth formula for the cohomology of shaves). Furthermore, concerning the degree
\deg(s)=\#(S\cap S^{\prime})
, we point out the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

For the degree of the sum isogeny in the reduciblity theorem, one has the upper bound

\deg(s)\leq\chi(L|_{S})^{2}.

Proof.

When
L
is a principal polarization on
A
, the relation between the types of the induced polarizations
L|_{S}
and
L|_{S^{\prime}}
is completely understood (see
[
1
]
, 12.1.5, p. 366). If the type for the smaller dimension is
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
then for the higher dimension the type is
(1,\ldots,1,d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
. It follows that
\chi(L|_{S})=\chi(L|_{S^{\prime}})
, both equal to the product
d_{1}\cdots d_{m}
, and therefore
\deg(s)=\chi(L|_{S})^{2}
follows from the equation above, with
\chi(L)=1
.

If
L
is an arbitrary polarization, there is an isogeny
g:A\rightarrow B
onto an Abelian variety
B
having a principal polarization
M
such that
L=g^{\ast}(M)
(see
[
1
]
, 4.1.2, p. 71).
If
S
is an Abelian subvariety of
A
, define
T:=g(A)
and let
d_{S}
be the degree of the restriction
g_{S}:S\rightarrow T
.
One has
\chi(L|_{S})=d_{S}\,\chi(M|_{T})
.

Let
T^{\prime}
be the complement of
T
in
B
with respect to
M
. Then
S^{\prime}
, the complement of
S
in
A
with respect to
L
, coincides with
g^{-1}_{\,0}(T^{\prime})
, the connected component of
0
in the pre-image
g^{-1}(T^{\prime})
. In order to check this, it is enough to work with the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}S\times g^{-1}_{\,0}(T^{\prime})@>{s}>{}>A\\
@V{\overline{g\times g}}V{}V@V{}V{g}V\\
T\times T^{\prime}@>{}>{t}>B\end{CD}

where
s,t
are the sum homomorphisms,
and check that the pullback of
M

splits on
S\times g^{-1}_{\,0}(T^{\prime})
.
We have just seen that

\#(T\cap T^{\prime})=\chi(M|_{T})^{2}
holds since
M
is a principal polarization.
Then

\#(S\cap S^{\prime})\leq\#(S\cap g^{-1}(T^{\prime}))\leq d_{S}\,\#(T\cap T^{\prime})\leq{d_{S}}^{2}\chi(M|_{T})^{2}=\chi(L|_{S})^{2}
.
∎

Finally, here is an immediate consequence:

\chi(L|_{S^{\prime}})\leq\chi(L)\,\chi(L|_{S}).

2.3
Complete reducibility with respect to a polarization

As a consequence of the reducibility theorem quoted in §
2.1
one has the following theorem of complete reducibility into simple factors.

Let
A
be an arbitrary Abelian variety, endowed with a polarization
L
. There exist:

-

a sequence of simple Abelian varieties
B_{1},\ldots,B_{q}
, each other non-isogenous,

-

a corresponding sequence of positive integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}
,

-

and an isogeny

s:B_{1}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times B_{q}^{k_{q}}\rightarrow A

such that the pullback polarization
s^{\ast}(L)
splits.

In general we say that a polarization on a product variety
S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{k}
is a split polarization if it is of the form

p_{1}^{\ast}(L_{1})+\cdots+p_{k}^{\ast}(L_{k})

where
L_{i}
is a polarization on
S_{i}
and
p_{i}
is the projection to the
i
th factor.

In the theorem of complete reducibility,
the collection of pairs
(B_{j},k_{j})
is determined from
A
alone, up to isogeny, however the isogeny
s
that is described above also depends on the polarization
L
.

2.4
Behavior under isogenies

Let
A
and
B
be Abelian varieties, and let

g:B\rightarrow A
be an isogeny, of degree
d
.
Let
L
be a polarization on
A
and consider on
B
the pullback polarization
g^{\ast}(L)
.
There is a one to one correspondence

{\sf AS}_{A}\overset{{}_{\sim}}{\longrightarrow}{\sf AS}_{B}.

Given
S\subset A
the corresponding
S^{\ast}
in
B
is
the connected component of
0
in the pre-image
\varphi^{-1}(S)
.
The restricted isogeny
S^{\ast}\rightarrow S
has degree
d_{S}\leq d

(in fact a divisor of
d
). With respect to the polarizations one has

\chi((g^{\ast}L)|_{S^{\ast}})=d_{S}\,\chi(L|_{S})
.
So there is the chain of inequalities

\chi(L|_{S})\leq\chi((g^{\ast}L)|_{S^{\ast}})\leq d\,\chi(L|_{S})
.
It follows that the functions counting Abelian subvarieties in
A
and in
B

are related by the inequalities

N_{A}(t)\leq N_{B}(dt){\rm\ \ \ and\ \ \ }N_{B}(t)\leq N_{A}(t)

and therefore one has

N_{A}(t)=O(t^{e})\mbox{ \; if and only if \; }N_{B}(t)=O(t^{e}).

Remark 2.2
.

Because of the invariance under isogeny of the asymptotic estimate for the counting function, and because of the complete reducibility theorem with respect to a polarization, in order to prove Theorem
1.1
it is enough to confine oneself to the case of a product of simple Abelian varieties endowed with a split polarization. This is what we do in the following.

3
Bounding homomorphisms

3.1
A quadratic form

Let
A,A^{\prime}
be Abelian varities, of dimensions
n,n^{\prime}
, endowed with polarizations
\Theta,\Theta^{\prime}
. Consider the function

Hom(A,A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

which is defined by the assignment

f\longmapsto\dfrac{\Theta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}.

It is known that this is
a quadratic form,
always non-negative.
positive definite if
A
is simple.
The second and the third assertion are almost apparent, by some intersection theory.
The first assertion for the special case
A=A^{\prime}
, i.e. for an endomorphism group
End(A)
, can be found for instance in
[
1
]
, §5.1, p. 117; and the general case can be reduced to this special one by embedding the homomorphism group into a suitable endomorphism group in such a way that the function defined above for homomorphisms can be recovered, up to some scalar factor, as the restriction of the analogous function defined for endomorphisms.

3.2
Homomorphisms in a hyperellipsoid

Assume now that
A
is a simple Abelian variety.
Define

\Phi(t):=\#\left\{f\in Hom(A,A^{\prime})\mbox{ s.t. }\dfrac{\Theta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}\leq t^{2}\right\}.

Let
H
be the rank of the homomorphism group
Hom(A,A^{\prime})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is an asymptotic estimate

\Phi(t)=O(t^{H}).

Proof.

Choose an isomorphism
Hom(A,A^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{H}
. The quadratic form on
Hom(A,A^{\prime})
corresponds to a quadratic form
Q:\mathbb{Z}^{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, and this is naturally extended to
\widehat{Q}:\mathbb{R}^{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. As the original form is positive definite, then
Q
is positive definite and so is
\widehat{Q}
too. And so we have

\Phi(t)=\#\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{H}\mbox{ s.t. }Q(x)\leq t^{2}\}.

Now observe that

\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{H}\mbox{ s.t. }Q(x)\leq t^{2}\}

can be written as

t\cdot\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{H}\mbox{ s.t. }\widehat{Q}(y)\leq 1\}\cap\,\mathbb{Z}^{H}

and that

\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{H}\mbox{ s.t. }\widehat{Q}(y)\leq 1\}
,
a hyperellipsoid, is compact and convex.
So we are lead to a classical topic in Number Theory, the problem of estimating the number of lattice points belonging to a bounded region in the Euclidean space.
Thus the conclusion follows from the general result quoted below.
∎

3.3
Lattice points in a bounded region

Let
K
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, compact convex and with Lipschitz frontier. The function
\#(tK\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})
counts the number of points of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
which lie in a deformed region
tK
. There is an asymptotic estimate in the form

\#(tK\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})={\rm Vol}(K)\,t^{n}+O(t^{n-1})
(see
[
5
]
, Ch. 6, Lemma 2, p. 165),
that we only use in the coarse version

\#(tK\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})=O(t^{n}).

4
Subvarieties from homomorphisms

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be Abelian varieties, and consider the product
A\times A^{\prime}
with the natural projections
p,p^{\prime}
. Let
n
be the dimension of
A
. If
S
is an Abelian subvariety in
A\times A^{\prime}
, we denote by
p_{S},p^{\prime}_{S}
the two projections restricted to
S
.

4.1
Parametrization

We describe the Abelian subvarieties
S
such that the projection

p_{S}:S\rightarrow A
is surjective and finite, i.e. an isogeny. Define

{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}:=\Big\{S\in{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}}\mbox{ s.t. }p{}_{S}:S\rightarrow A\mbox{ is an isogeny}\Big\}.

So assume that
p_{S}:S\rightarrow A
is an isogeny.
Take the inverse, or dual, isogeny
\widehat{p_{S}}:A\rightarrow S
and
define the homomorphism
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
as
f:={p^{\prime}_{S}}\circ\widehat{p_{S}}
.
Let
a:=\deg(p_{S})
. Then
\deg(\widehat{p_{S}})=a^{2n-1}

(follows from
p_{S}\circ\widehat{p_{S}}=a_{A}
).
If
\widehat{p_{S}}
is viewed as a map
A\rightarrow A\times A^{\prime}
,
it is given by
x\mapsto(ax,f(x))
. So we can describe

S
as the image of some homomorphism

A\rightarrow A\times A^{\prime}
of the form
(a,f)
.

For every integer
a>0
and every homomorphism
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}

we denote by
C_{a,f}
the image of the homomorphism

A\rightarrow A\times A^{\prime}
given by the pair
(a,f)
, and denote by the same symbol
(a,f)
the induced homomorphism
A\rightarrow C_{a,f}
.
The condition that

\deg(a,f)=a^{2n-1}

is necessary and sufficient for
the projection
C_{a,f}\rightarrow A
to be of degree
a
, and
in this case the inverse isogeny
A\rightarrow C_{a,f}\hookrightarrow A\times A^{\prime}
is naturally identified to
(a,f)
.
So we have a correspondence

\Bigg\{\mbox{\begin{tabular}[]{c}$(a,f)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times Hom(A,A^{\prime})$\\
s.t. $\deg(a,f)=a^{2n-1}$\end{tabular}}\Bigg\}\longrightarrow{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}\,.

This is a bijective correspondence.
The surjectivity is clear from the discussion above.
Assume that
(a,f)
and
(a^{\prime},f^{\prime})
determine the same subvariety
C_{a,f}=C_{a^{\prime},f^{\prime}}
.
(Taking the degree of the first projection we already have
a=a^{\prime}
.)
Taking the inverse of the first projection we have
(a,f)=(a^{\prime},f^{\prime})
.

4.2
Degree

Assume that on
A\times A^{\prime}
is given a split polarization
L={p}^{\ast}\Theta+{p^{\prime}}{}^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}
.
For a subvariety
S=C_{a,f}

we now study the degree
S\cdot L^{n}=(L|_{S})^{n}
, for which we give some estimate from below.

Pulling back to
A
, by means of
\widehat{p_{S}}:A\rightarrow S
, we obtain
\widehat{p_{S}}^{\ast}(L|_{S})^{n}=a^{2n-1}(L|_{S})^{n}
. On the other hand, since
\widehat{p_{S}}^{\ast}(L|_{S})=a^{\ast}\Theta+f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}
, we have
\widehat{p_{S}}^{\ast}(L|_{S})^{n}=(a^{2}\Theta+f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime})^{n}
. Here we replaced
a^{\ast}\Theta
with
a^{2}\Theta
, in the computation of the intersection number, because
a^{\ast}(\Theta)\equiv\frac{a^{2}+a}{2}\,\Theta+\frac{a^{2}-a}{2}\,(-1)^{\ast}\Theta
in
Pic(A)
(see
[
1
]
, Proposition 2.3.5, p. 33) and because
\Theta\equiv(-1)^{\ast}\Theta
in
H_{2n-2}(A,\mathbb{Z})
. It follows that

a^{2n-1}(L|_{S})^{n}=(a^{2}\Theta+f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime})^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}a^{2i}\,(\Theta^{i}\cdot f^{\ast}{\Theta^{\prime}}^{n-i}).

Furthermore, due to the ampleness of
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
, one has

\Theta^{i}\cdot f^{\ast}{\Theta^{\prime}}^{n-i}>0

for
0\leq i\leq n
(see
[
1
]
, Lemma 4.3.2, p. 76).
As a consequence, if we drop from the right hand side all terms except the two with
i=n,n-1
, and then divide by
n!
, we obtain the inequality

\chi(L|_{S})=\frac{(L|_{S})^{n}}{n!}\geq a\;\dfrac{\Theta^{n}}{n!}+\dfrac{1}{a}\;\dfrac{\Theta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}.

4.3
On subvarieties of bounded degree
-
I

Assume now that
A
is a simple Abelian variety.
We study the set

{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}:=\Big\{S\in{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}}\mbox{ s.t. }p{}_{S}:S\rightarrow A\mbox{ is an isogeny}\Big\}

and give an asymptotic estimate for the counting function

N_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}(t):=\#\Big\{S\in{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}\mbox{ s.t. }\chi(L|_{S})\leq t\Big\}.

The estimate is going to be in terms of the quantity

H:=\mbox{the rank of the group }Hom(A,A^{\prime}).

Theorem 4.1
.

In the present setting, with
A
simple, one has

N_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}(t)=O\left(t^{H+1}\right).

Proof.

We apply the description in §
4.1
.
The set
{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}
is bijective to the set of parameter data

\Big\{(a,f)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times Hom(A,A^{\prime})\mbox{ s.t. }\deg(a,f)=a^{2n-1}\Big\}.

Thus
N_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}(t)
is the number of elements of the set

\Big\{(a,f)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times Hom(A,A^{\prime})\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \deg(a,f)=a^{2n-1}\ \mbox{and}\ \dfrac{\deg C(a,f)}{n!}\leq t\Big\}.

Then we consider the description in §
4.2
.
We have the inequality

\frac{\deg C_{a,f}}{n!}\geq a\;\dfrac{\Theta^{n}}{n!}+\dfrac{1}{a}\;\dfrac{\Theta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}.

Hence the codnition
\frac{\deg C_{a,f}}{n!}\leq t
implies

a\leq\dfrac{t}{M}\ \mbox{ and }\ \dfrac{\Theta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}\leq a\,t,

where
M:=\Theta^{n}/n!
.
It follows that the set of parameter data above is a subset of the product

\Big\{a\in\mathbb{Z}\mbox{ s.t. }1\leq a\leq\frac{t}{M}\Big\}\times\Big\{f\in Hom(A,A^{\prime})\ \mbox{s.t.}\ \dfrac{\Theta^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\cdot f^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{M}\Big\}.

Since from Proposition
3.1
we know that the cardinality of the set on the right hand side of the product is a function of order
O(t^{H})
,
it follows that
N_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}(t)

is bounded above by a function of order
O(t)\,O(t^{H})

as in the statement.
∎

5
Subvarieties of a product variety

Keeping the notation introduced in the preceding section, we extend the description of subvarieties in the product variety
A\times A^{\prime}
.

5.1
Parametrization

We describe the Abelian subvarieties
S
of
A\times A^{\prime}
such that the projection

p{}_{S}:S\rightarrow A
is surjective.

Define
F:=Ker_{0}(p_{S})
and view it as an Abelian subvariety of
A^{\prime}
.
Let
T
in
S
be an Abelian subvariety such that

F+T=S
and
F\cap T
is finite. Then the sum map

s:F\times T\rightarrow S
is an isogeny, and
the restricted projection
p_{T}:T\rightarrow A

is an isogeny too.

Assume that a polarization
L
is given on
A\times A^{\prime}
.
Because of the reducibility theorem relative to a given polarization (cfr. §
2.1
),
we can choose
T
in such a way that

s^{\ast}(L|_{S})={q}^{\ast}(L|_{F})+{q^{\prime}}{}^{\ast}(L|_{T})

where
q
and
q^{\prime}
denote the projections
from
F\times T
to
F
and
T
, respectively.

We say that two subvarieties
F
and
T
in
A\times A^{\prime}
are complementary with respect to
L
when the sum map
s:F\times T\rightarrow F+T
is an isogeny and the pullback polarization
s^{\ast}(L|_{F+T})
splits as
{q}^{\ast}(L|_{F})+{q^{\prime}}^{\ast}(L|_{T})
.

There is a correspondence

\left\{\mbox{\begin{tabular}[]{c}$(F,T)\in{\sf AS}_{A^{\prime}}\times{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}$\\
s.t. complementary w.r.t. $L$\end{tabular}}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\mbox{\begin{tabular}[]{c}$S\in{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}}$ s.t.\\
$p_{S}:S\rightarrow A$ surjective\end{tabular}}\right\}

\hskip 38.0pt(F,T)\hskip 10.0pt\longmapsto\hskip 10.0ptF+T

and this is a bijective correspondence.
That the correspondence is surjective is clear from the construction.
Given
S
, the choice of
F
is unique, because
Ker(p_{S})

must be equal to
(T\cap F)+F
and therefore
Ker_{0}(p_{S})=F
.
That the choice of
T
is then unique is part of the reducibility theorem.

5.2
Degree

Concerning the (reduced) degree of
S=F+T
with respect to the polarization
L=p^{\ast}\Theta+{p^{\prime}}{}^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}
, i.e. the Euler characteristic
\chi(L|_{S})
, we here recall what we know. Define

a:=\deg(s:F\times T\rightarrow S)=\#(F\cap T)=\deg(p{}_{T}:T\rightarrow A).

From §
2.2
we have the basic facts

a\,\chi(L|_{S})=\chi(\Theta^{\prime}|_{F})\,\chi(L|_{T})\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \chi(L|_{T})\leq\chi(L|_{S})\,\chi(\Theta^{\prime}|_{F})\hskip 20.0pt

and from §
4.2
we take the inequality

aM\leq\chi(L|_{T})

where
M=\chi(\Theta)
, holding because
T
is of the form
C_{a,f}
with
a=\deg(p{}_{T})
as above.

5.3
On subvarieties of bounded degree
-
II

Assume now that
A
is a simple Abelian variety.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
A
is simple and if
N_{A^{\prime}}(t)=O(t^{E})
then
N_{A\times A^{\prime}}(t)=O\left(t^{E+2(H+1)}\right)
.

Proof.

Since
A
is simple, the set
{\sf AS}_{A\times A^{\prime}}
is the disjoint union of
{\sf AS}_{A^{\prime}}

and the complementary subset which, according to §
5.1
,
is bijective to the set of parameter data

\Big\{(F,T)\in{\sf AS}_{A^{\prime}}\times{\sf AS}_{A/\,p}\mbox{ \ s.t. $F$ and $T$ are complementary w.r.t. $L$}\Big\}.

The basic facts concerning the degree of
S=F+T
expressed in terms of the degree
a
of the sum isogeny
F\times T\rightarrow S
and the degrees of
F
and
T
are collected in §
5.2
. In the present setting we can show that

\chi(L|_{S})\leq t\ \ \ \mbox{ implies }\ \ \ \chi(\Theta^{\prime}|_{F})\leq t\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \chi(L|_{T})\leq t^{2}.

We have
a\,\chi(L|_{S})=\chi(\Theta^{\prime}|_{F})\chi(L|_{T})
. So from
\chi(L|_{T})\geq aM
we obtain
\chi(\Theta^{\prime}|_{F})\leq t/M
and from
\chi(L|_{T})\leq\chi(L|_{S})\chi(\Theta^{\prime}|_{F})
we obtain
\chi(L|_{T})\leq t^{2}/M
.
Note that in the weaker inequalities stated above we dropped the constant
M
, just because it will not affect the exponent in the estimate of the counting function
N_{A\times A^{\prime}}(t)
.

Summing everything up, we have

N_{A\times A^{\prime}}(t)\leq N_{A^{\prime}}(t)+N_{A^{\prime}}(t)\,{N}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}(t^{2}).

From Theorem
4.1
we know that

{N}_{A\times A^{\prime}/\,p}(t^{2})=O(t^{2(H+1)}).

So, with the hypothesis that
N_{A^{\prime}}(t)=O(t^{E})
, the statement follows.
∎

6
Subvarieties of a completely reduced product

Let
A=B^{k}
be the
k
th self product of a simple Abelian variety
B
, of dimension
n
, endowed with a split polarization
L=p_{1}^{\ast}\Theta_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}^{\ast}\Theta_{k}
, where each
\Theta_{i}
is a polarization on
B
.
The estimate for the counting function
N_{B^{k}}(t)
is in terms of

h
, the rank of the endomorphism group
End(B)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

There is an asymptotic estimate

N_{B^{k}}(t)=O\left(t^{(kh+2)(k-1)}\right).

Proof.

By induction on
k
. The initial case
k=1
being obvious, assume
k\geq 2
.
Write
B^{k}=B\times B^{k-1}
and let
p:B^{k}\rightarrow B
and
p^{\prime}:B^{k}\rightarrow B^{k-1}
be the natural projections, with
p:=p_{1}
and
p^{\prime}:=(p_{2},\ldots,p_{k})
. The given polarization on
B^{k}
splits as
L=p^{\ast}\Theta+p^{\prime}{}^{\ast}\Theta^{\prime}
where
\Theta:=\Theta_{1}
and where
\Theta^{\prime}
is the split polarization on
B^{k-1}
obtained from
\Theta_{2},\ldots,\Theta_{k}
.

The group
Hom(B,B^{k-1})
is of rank
H=(k-1)h
.
By the induction hypothesis we have
N_{B^{k-1}}(t)=O(t^{E})

with
E=\left((k-1)h+2\right)(k-2)
.
From Theorem
5.1

we have
N_{B^{k}}(t)=(t^{E+2(H+1)})
. In the present setting

E+2(H+1)=(kh+2)(k-1)
, and the statement is proven.
∎

Let
A=B_{1}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times B_{q}^{k_{q}}
be a product of simple Abelian varieties, each occurring with some multiplicity, and assume that
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
are not isogenous for
i\neq j
.

Each simple Abelian subvariety of
A
is contained in a unique
B_{j}^{k_{j}}
and is then isogenous to
B_{j}
.
An arbitrary Abelian subvariety of
A
may be written as
S=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{q}
where

S_{j}

=

the sum of all simple Abelian subvarieties of
S
contained in
B_{j}^{k_{j}}

(and hence belonging to the isogeny class of
B_{j}
)

=

S\cap B_{j}^{k_{j}}\,.

Thus we have a correspondence

{\sf AS}_{B_{1}^{k_{1}}}\times\cdots\times{\sf AS}_{B_{q}^{k_{q}}}\longrightarrow{\sf AS}_{A}

(S_{1},\ldots,S_{q})\longmapsto S_{1}\times\ldots\times S_{q}

and clearly this is a bijective correspondence.

Let
L
be a split polarization on
A
. It may be written as
L=p_{1}^{\ast}(\Theta_{1})+\cdots+p_{q}^{\ast}(\Theta_{q})
where each
\Theta_{j}
is a split polarization on
B_{j}^{k_{j}}
. If
S=S_{1}\times\ldots\times S_{q}
is an Abelian subvariety of
A
, as above, then
(the Künneth formula again)

\chi(L|_{S})=\chi(\Theta_{1}|_{S_{1}})\cdots\chi(\Theta_{q}|_{S_{q}}).

In the present setting, the estimate for the function
N_{A}(t)
, and its proof, involve the following data:

h_{j}

the rank of the endomorphism group
End(B_{j})
,

h

the maximum among the ranks
h_{1},\ldots,h_{q}
,

k

the maximum among the multiplicities
k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

There is an asymptotic estimate

N_{B_{1}^{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times B_{q}^{k_{q}}}(t)=O\left(t^{\,q(kh+2)(k-1)}\right).

Proof.

We have seen that
the set
{\sf AS}_{A}
is bijective to
{\sf AS}_{B_{1}^{k_{1}}}\times\cdots\times{\sf AS}_{B_{q}^{k_{q}}}
and from the expression for
\chi(L|_{S})
above it follows that

N_{A}(t)\leq N_{B_{1}^{k_{1}}}(t)\cdots N_{B_{q}^{k_{q}}}(t)
.
From Theorem
6.1
we have

N_{B_{j}^{k_{j}}}(t)=O\left(t^{(k_{j}h_{j}+2)(k_{j}-1)}\right)
and therefore

N_{B_{1}^{k_{1}}}(t)\cdots N_{B_{q}^{k_{q}}}(t)=O\left(t^{(k_{1}h_{1}+2)(k_{1}-1)+\cdots+(k_{q}h_{q}+2)(k_{q}-1)}\right)
,
and so the statement follows.
∎

2.2
the proof of Theorem
1.1
is now complete.

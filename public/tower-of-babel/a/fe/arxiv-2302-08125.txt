Abstract
. It is shown in Ferrari [ 14 ] that if [ 0 , T ∗ ) [0,T^{*}) is the maximal time interval of existence of a smooth solution of the incompressible Euler equations in a bounded, simply-connected domain in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , then ∫ 0 T ∗ ‖ ω ⁡ ( t , ⋅ ) ‖ L ∞ ​ 𝑑 t = + ∞ \int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\omega(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}t=+\infty , where ω \omega is the vorticity of the flow. Ferrari’s result generalizes the classical Beale-Kato-Majda [ 3 ] ’s breakdown criterion in the case of a bounded fluid domain. In this manuscript, we show a breakdown criterion for a smooth solution of the Euler equations describing the motion of an incompressible fluid in a bounded domain in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with a free surface boundary. The fluid is under the influence of surface tension. In addition, we show that our breakdown criterion reduces to the one proved by Ferrari [ 14 ] when the free surface boundary is fixed. Specifically, the additional control norms on the moving boundary will either become trivial or stop showing up if the kinematic boundary condition on the moving boundary reduces to the slip boundary condition. Keywords. Breakdown Criterion, Incompressible Euler Equations, Surface Tension, Free-boundary Problem. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q35, 35R35, 76B03, 76B45



1.
Introduction

We consider the Euler equations modeling the motion of an incompressible fluid in a domain with a moving boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=0,&\quad\mathrm{in}\;\mathcal{D}_{t},\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,&\quad\mathrm{in}\;\mathcal{D}_{t},\end{cases}

where
u:=u(t,y)
,
p:=p(t,y)
represent the velocity and pressure of fluid, respectively. Also, for each fixed
t
,

\mathcal{D}_{t}=\left\{(y^{\prime},y_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\big|\,y^{\prime}:=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\,-b<y_{3}\leq\psi(t,y^{\prime})\right\}

denotes the moving fluid domain.
The boundary of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is given by
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}=\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}\cup\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{btm}}
, where the moving boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}
is determined by a graph

\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}=\left\{(y^{\prime},y_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\big|\,y_{3}=\psi(t,y^{\prime})\right\},

and

\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{btm}}=\left\{(y^{\prime},y_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\big|\,y_{3}=-b\right\}

is the fixed finite bottom.

The initial and boundary conditions of the system (
1.1
) are

(1.2)

\displaystyle\text{(IC)}

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot):=u_{0},\quad\psi(0,\cdot):=\psi_{0};

\displaystyle\text{(BC)}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\psi=u\cdot N,\quad N:=(-\partial_{y_{1}}\psi,-\partial_{y_{2}}\psi,1)^{T},&\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}},\\
&p=\sigma\mathcal{H},&\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}},\\
&u\cdot n=0,\quad n:=(0,0,1)^{T},&\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{btm}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here, we denote by

\mathcal{H}
is the mean curvature of the free boundary of the fluid domain, while
\sigma>0
is the surface tension coefficient.
Finally, we point out that the local existence theory requires that
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}\cap\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{btm}}=\emptyset
within the interval of existence
[0,T_{0}]
. To achieve this, we may set
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq 1
and
b>10
. Then the continuity of
\psi(t,\cdot)
guarantees that
\|\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq 10
holds for all
t\in[0,T_{0}]
.

1.1.
Fixing the Fluid Domain

Let

\Omega:=\big\{(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\big|\,x^{\prime}:=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\;-b<x_{3}\leq 0\big\},

with
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}
, where

\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}:=\{x_{3}=0\},\quad\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}:=\{x_{3}=-b\}.

For each fixed
t\geq 0
, we consider a family of mappings
\Phi(t,\cdot):\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{t}
given by

(1.3)

\Phi(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})=(x^{\prime},\varphi(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})),

with

(1.4)

\varphi(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})=x_{3}+\chi(x_{3})\psi(t,x^{\prime}).

Here,
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(-b,0]
a cut-off function verifying

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\chi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-b,0]}\leq\frac{1}{\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+1},\quad&\|\chi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-b,0]}+\|\chi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-b,0]}\leq C,\quad\text{for some generic}\,\,C>0,\\
&\text{and}\,\,\chi=1\quad\text{on}\,\,\,\,(-\delta_{0},0],\end{aligned}

holds for some
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small. Note that the first condition in (
1.5
) yields that

(1.6)

\partial_{3}\varphi(0,x^{\prime},x_{3})=1+\chi^{\prime}(x_{3})\psi(0,x^{\prime})\geq 2c_{0},

for some
c_{0}>0
, and thus we infer from the local existence theory that

(1.7)

\partial_{3}\varphi(t,x^{\prime},x_{3})\geq c_{0},\quad\forall t\in[0,T_{0}],

which guarantees that
\Phi(t,\cdot)
is a diffeomophism (see Subsection
2.1
). It can be seen that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}
respectively correspond to the moving surface boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}
and the fixed finite bottom
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{btm}}
through
\Phi(t,\cdot)
.

We denote respectively by

(1.8)

v(t,x):=u(t,\Phi(t,x)),\qquad q(t,x):=p(t,\Phi(t,x)),

the velocity and pressure defined on the fixed domain
\Omega
.

Notation 1.1

(Coordinates and Derivatives)
.

The following notations will be used throughout this manuscript.

i.

We denote by
\partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},i=1,2,3
the spatial derivatives with respect to the
x
-coordinates.

ii.

We denote by
y_{i}=\Phi_{i}(t,x)
,
i=1,2,3
the Eulerian spatial coordinates, and by
\nabla_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}
the Eulerian spatial derivatives.

iii.

We use
\overline{\partial}:=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
to indicate tangential spatial derivatives.

Then, we see that

(1.9)

\nabla_{\alpha}u\circ\Phi=\partial_{\alpha}^{\varphi}v,\quad\nabla_{\alpha}p\circ\Phi=\partial_{\alpha}^{\varphi}q,\;\alpha=t,1,2,3.

where

(1.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\varphi}=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}-\frac{\partial_{t}\varphi}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\partial_{3},

\displaystyle\partial_{a}^{\varphi}=

\displaystyle\partial_{a}-\frac{\partial_{a}\varphi}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\partial_{3},\quad a=1,\,2,

\displaystyle\partial_{3}^{\varphi}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\partial_{3}.

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{H}=-\overline{\partial}\cdot\left(\frac{\overline{\partial}\psi}{\sqrt{1+|\overline{\partial}\psi|^{2}}}\right)
, the boundary condition in (
1.2
) is turned into

(1.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\psi=v\cdot N,\quad N:=(-\partial_{1}\psi,-\partial_{2}\psi,1)^{T},&\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}},\\
&q=-\sigma\overline{\partial}\cdot\left(\frac{\overline{\partial}\psi}{\sqrt{1+|\overline{\partial}\psi|^{2}}}\right),&\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}},\\
&v\cdot n=0,&\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Let

D_{t}^{\varphi}=\partial_{t}^{\varphi}+v\cdot\partial^{\varphi}

be the material derivative. Then the incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
) with initial-boundary conditions (
1.2
) is converted into

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D_{t}^{\varphi}v+\partial^{\varphi}q=0,\quad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
&\partial^{\varphi}\cdot v=0,\quad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
&\partial_{t}\psi=v\cdot N,\quad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}},\\
&q=-\sigma\overline{\partial}\cdot\left(\tfrac{\overline{\partial}\psi}{\sqrt{1+|\overline{\partial}\psi|^{2}}}\right),\quad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}},\\
&v\cdot n=0,\quad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}},\\
&(v,\psi)\big|_{t=0}=(v_{0},\psi_{0}).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Also, note that we can express

(1.13)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\varphi}=\partial_{t}+\overline{v}\cdot\overline{\partial}+\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi}(v\cdot\mathbf{N}-\partial_{t}\varphi)\partial_{3}

after invoking (
1.10
),
where
\overline{v}:=(v_{1},v_{2})
and
\mathbf{N}:=(-\partial_{1}\varphi,-\partial_{2}\varphi,1)^{T}
. It can be seen that the kinematic boundary condition
\partial_{t}\psi=v\cdot N
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}
indicates that

D_{t}^{\varphi}|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}=\partial_{t}+\overline{v}\cdot\overline{\partial}.

Moreover, since
v\cdot n=0
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}
,
\partial_{3}\varphi|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=1
, and
\partial_{t}\varphi|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=0
, we have

D_{t}^{\varphi}|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=\partial_{t}+\overline{v}\cdot\overline{\partial}.

In other words,
D_{t}^{\varphi}|_{\partial\Omega}\in\mathcal{T}(\partial\Omega)
, where
\mathcal{T}(\partial\Omega)
is the tangential bundle of
\partial\Omega
. Also, by restricting the momentum equation (the first equation of (
1.12
)) on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}
and taking the normal component, one has

(1.14)

\displaystyle n\cdot\partial q\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=0.

Notation 1.2

(Norms)
.

We adopt the following norms in the sequel of this manuscript.

i.

(
H^{s}
-Sobolev norms)
\|\cdot\|_{s}:=\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
,
|\cdot|_{s}:=\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})}
.

ii.

(
L^{\infty}
-based Sobolev norms)
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
,
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,\infty}}:=\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}
,
|\cdot|_{\infty}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})}
,
|\cdot|_{W^{1,\infty}}:=\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})}
.

iii.

(Hölder norms)
|\cdot|_{C^{k}}:=\|\cdot\|_{C^{k}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})}
.

1.2.
Main Results

The local existence theorem for the free-boundary incompressible Euler equations can be stated as follows: Let
(v_{0},\psi_{0})\in H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s+1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})
for some fixed
s\geq 3
. Then there exists a
T_{0}>0
, depends on
\|v_{0}\|_{s}
and
|\psi_{0}|_{s+1}
, such that the equations (
1.12
) have a unique solution in

C([0,T_{0}];H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s+1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})).

We refer to
[
11
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
for the local well-posedness of the system (
1.12
). Also, we can retrieve the local existence from
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
after taking the incompressible limit (with fixed
\sigma>0
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(v(t),\psi(t))\in H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s+1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})
,
s>\frac{9}{2}
, be the solution of (
1.12
) described above. Let

(1.15)

T^{*}=\sup\left\{T>0\,\big|\,(v(t),\psi(t))\text{ can be continued in the class}\;C([0,T];H^{3}(\Omega)\times H^{4}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}))\right\}.

If
T^{*}<+\infty
, then at least one of the following three statements hold:

a.

(1.16)

\lim_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\mathcal{K}(t)=+\infty,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(t):=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{K}_{2}(t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(t):=|\psi(t)|_{C^{3}}+|\psi_{t}(t)|_{C^{3}}+|\psi_{tt}(t)|_{1.5},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{v}(\tau)|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}\,d\tau+|\overline{v}(t)|_{\infty},

b.

(1.17)

\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|v(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\mathrm{d}t=+\infty,

c.

(1.18)

\lim_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\left(\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi(t)}+\frac{1}{b-|\psi(t)|_{\infty}}\right)=+\infty,

or turning occurs on the moving surface boundary.

Moreover, if
\overline{v}(t)
,
\partial_{1}\overline{v}(t)
, and
\partial_{2}\overline{v}(t)
are continuous on
\Omega
, then
\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{v}(\tau)|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}\,d\tau
in
\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)
can be dropped.

Remark 1.4
.

The last sentence in Theorem
1.3
indicates that, if
v(t)
is a smooth solution (as opposed to a
H^{s}(\Omega)
-solution), then
\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)
is reduced to
|\overline{v}(t)|_{\infty}
.

Remark 1.5
.

The first term in
\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)
, i.e.,
|\psi(t)|_{C^{3}}
controls the second fundamental form
\Theta
of the moving boundary in
C^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})
, where
\Theta:=\overline{\partial}(\frac{N(t)}{|N(t)|})
which contributes to
\overline{\partial}^{2}\psi(t)
in the leading order. Moreover, the second and third terms in
\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)
, i.e.,
|\psi_{t}(t)|_{C^{3}}
and
|\psi_{tt}(t)|_{1.5}
, control respectively the velocity and acceleration of the moving boundary.

Remark 1.6
.

The quantities on the LHS of (
1.18
) are required to be finite to continue the solution. Recall that we need
\partial_{3}\varphi>0
to ensure the mapping
\Phi(t,\cdot):\Omega\to\mathcal{D}_{t}
is invertible. In addition,
b-|\psi(t)|_{\infty}>0
ensures that the upper moving boundary is strictly above the fixed bottom.

Next, we show that (
1.17
) can be relaxed to
\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\omega^{\varphi}(t)\|_{\infty}\mathrm{d}t=+\infty
, where
\omega^{\varphi}:=\partial^{\varphi}\times v
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
T^{*}
and
\mathcal{K}(t)
be as in Theorem
1.3
. If
T^{*}<+\infty
, then at least one of the following three statements hold:

a.

(1.19)

\lim_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\mathcal{K}(t)=+\infty,

b’.

(1.20)

\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\omega^{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}t=+\infty,

c.

(1.21)

\lim_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\left(\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi(t)}+\frac{1}{b-|\psi(t)|_{\infty}}\right)=+\infty,

or turning occurs on the moving surface boundary.

Moreover, if
\overline{v}(t)
,
\partial_{1}\overline{v}(t)
, and
\partial_{2}\overline{v}(t)
are continuous on
\Omega
, then
\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{v}(\tau)|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}}\,d\tau
in
\mathcal{K}_{2}(t)
can be dropped.

Remark 1.8
.

Theorem
1.7
can be regarded as a generalization of the classical results of Beale-Kato-Majda
[
3
]
to the free-boundary Euler equations. Specifically, if
\psi_{t}=v\cdot N=0
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}
, then the moving surface boundary becomes fixed; in other words,
\psi=\psi(x^{\prime})
becomes time-independent.
As a consequence,
the control norm
|\psi|_{C^{3}}
in
\mathcal{K}_{1}
reduces to a non-negative constant, whereas
|\psi_{t}|_{C^{3}}=|\psi_{tt}|_{1.5}=0
. Moreover, if
\psi_{t}=0
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}
, the control norms in
\mathcal{K}_{2}
would not even appear.
Lastly, both
\partial_{3}\varphi
and
b-|\psi|_{\infty}
are automatically bounded from below by a positive constant.

Remark 1.9
.

We require
(v(t),\psi(t))\in H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s+1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})
,
s>\frac{9}{2}
in Theorems
1.3
and
1.7
, but the space of continuation is merely
H^{3}(\Omega)\times H^{4}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})
. The loss of regularity is owing to
|\psi_{t}|_{C^{3}}=|v\cdot N|_{C^{3}}
in
\mathcal{K}(t)
, which cannot be controlled by
E(t)
. We can prove an alternative breakdown criterion in which
|\psi_{t}|_{C^{3}}
is replaced by
|\psi_{t}|_{C^{2}}+|\psi_{t}|_{3}
, and the latter can be bounded by the energy ties to the local existence in
H^{3}(\Omega)\times H^{4}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}})
. We devote Section
5
to discuss the details.

1.3.
History and Background

The study of the free-boundary problems in Euler equations has blossomed over the past three decades. In the case without surface tension (i.e.,
\sigma=0
), the first breakthrough came in Wu
[
35
,
36
]
, where the local well-posedness (LWP) is established assuming the flow is
irrotational
, under the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition

(1.22)

-\nabla_{N}p\geq c>0,\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}.

It is known that the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition serves as an essential stability condition on the moving surface boundary to ensure the LWP when
\sigma=0
. Otherwise, Ebin
[
13
]
showed that (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) is
ill-posed
when
\sigma=0
if (
1.22
) is violated. We further remark that there are numerous results concerning the long-term well-posedness for the free-boundary incompressible Euler equations with small and
irrotational
data, see, e.g.,
[
1
,
7
,
10
,
16
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
34
,
37
,
38
]
.
In the rotational case, Christodoulou–Lindblad
[
6
]
established the
a priori energy estimate
for (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with
\sigma=0
, and the LWP was proved by Lindblad
[
25
]
using the Nash-Moser iteration and by Zhang–Zhang
[
39
]
using the classical energy approach. On the other hand, when
\sigma>0
, the LWP (as well as the a priori estimate that ties to LWP) for this model was proved independently by Coutand–Shkoller
[
8
,
9
]
, Disconzi–Kukavica
[
11
]
, Disconzi–Kukavica–Tuffaha
[
12
]
, Kukavica–Tuffaha–Vicol
[
23
]
, and Shatah–Zeng
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
. Also, Kukavica–Ozanski
[
24
]
studies the LWP with localized
H^{2+\delta}
-vorticity near the free boundary.

Moreover, there are available results (e.g.,
[
17
,
32
,
33
]
) concerning the breakdown criterion for the free-boundary Euler equations when
\sigma=0
. Particularly, using paradifferential calculus, the authors of
[
32
,
33
]
proved that, for
T<T^{*}
,

(1.23)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{L^{p}\cap L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}})}+\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}\right)<+\infty,\quad p\geq 6,

(1.24)

\displaystyle\inf_{(t,y^{\prime})\in\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}}-\nabla_{N}p(t,y^{\prime})\geq c>0,

together with a condition analogous to (
1.18
).
Note that (
1.23
) depends on the boundedness of
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}
, which is stronger than
\int_{0}^{T}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}\mathrm{d}t<\infty
, where
\omega:=\nabla\times u
. Apart from this, (
1.24
) is imposed to avoid the Rayleigh-Taylor breakdown described in
[
5
]
.
Recently, Ginsberg
[
17
]
proved an alternative breakdown criterion by adapting the method of
[
6
]
, which states that
if
T<T^{*}
, then

(1.25)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}})}+\|\mathcal{N}(u|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}})}+\|\nabla_{N}D_{t}p(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}})}\Big)\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Here,
\mathcal{N}
denotes the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator. On the other hand, Julin–La Manna
[
22
]
studied the a priori estimates for the motion of a charged liquid droplet in Eulerian coordinates with
\sigma>0
. As a by-product, they show if
T<T^{*}
, then

(1.26)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(|\psi(t)|_{C^{1,\gamma}}+|\mathcal{H}(t)|_{L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}})}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}+|u_{N}(t)|_{H^{2}(\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}})}\right)<+\infty,

where the
C^{1,\gamma}
-norm of
\psi
is expected to be sharp. Nevertheless, compared with Remark
1.8
, it appears to be hard to further reduce either of the aforementioned breakdown criteria to
\int_{0}^{T}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}\mathrm{d}t<\infty
if
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t,\mathrm{top}}
becomes fixed.

1.4.
What is New?

In this manuscript, we demonstrate a new breakdown criterion for the free-boundary Euler equations when
\sigma>0
. Specifically, with the help of some carefully chosen control norms with explicit physical background on the moving boundary, we can reduce our breakdown criterion to
the classical Beale-Kato-Majda criterion in a bounded, simply-connected domain, which was shown in
[
14
]

if the kinematic boundary condition on the moving surface boundary is reduced to the slip boundary condition (Remark
1.8
). Moreover,
if
T^{*}<+\infty
and conditions (a) and (c) in Theorem
1.7
do not occur, then
\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{t})}\,dt=+\infty
.
On the other hand, this implies that if
\omega(0)=\mathbf{0}
, then the 3D free-boundary Euler equations with surface tension can blow up only on the moving surface boundary caused by either condition (a) or (c) in Theorem
1.7
.

2.1.
The Change of Coordinates
\Phi(t,\cdot)

Remark 2.3

3.6.1.

We first bound

Lemma 3.8
.

If
q
verifies (
3.69
), then for each sufficiently small

, we have

(3.70)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\varphi}q\|_{0}^{2}\leq\epsilon P(|\Omega|,c_{0}^{-1},|\psi|_{C^{1}})\|\partial q\|_{1}^{2}+C(\epsilon^{-1})P(c_{0}^{-1},|\psi|_{C^{1}})\left(\|\partial v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\partial v\|_{0}^{2}+|\sqrt{\sigma}\psi|_{4}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Invoking Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{\varphi}q|^{2}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}q(\triangle^{\varphi}q)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}q(N\cdot\partial^{\varphi}q)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

where

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}q(\triangle^{\varphi}q)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\leq P(|\psi|_{C^{1}})\|\triangle^{\varphi}q\|_{0}\|q\|_{0}\leq P(|\psi|_{C^{1}})\Big(\epsilon\|q\|_{0}^{2}+C(\epsilon^{-1})\|\triangle^{\varphi}q\|_{0}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon P(|\psi|_{C^{1}})\|q\|_{0}^{2}+C(\epsilon^{-1})P(c_{0}^{-1},|\psi|_{C^{1}})\|\partial v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\partial v\|_{0}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}q(N\cdot\partial^{\varphi}q)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\leq P(|\psi|_{C^{1}})\Big(\epsilon\|\partial^{\varphi}q\|_{1}^{2}+C(\epsilon^{-1})|q|_{0}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon P(c_{0}^{-1},|\psi|_{C^{1}})\|\partial q\|_{1}^{2}+C(\epsilon^{-1})P(|\psi|_{C^{1}})\sum_{k=0}^{1}|\overline{\partial}^{k+1}\psi|_{0}^{2}.

Summing these up, we obtain

(3.71)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\varphi}q\|_{0}^{2}\leq P(c_{0}^{-1},|\psi|_{C^{1}})\left(\epsilon\|q\|_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\|\partial q\|_{1}^{2}\right)+C(\epsilon^{-1})P(c_{0}^{-1},|\psi|_{C^{1}})\left(\|\partial v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\partial v\|_{0}^{2}+|\sqrt{\sigma}\psi|_{4}^{2}\right).

On the other hand, using Poincaré’s inequality, we get

\|q\|_{0}^{2}\leq C(|\Omega|)\Big(\|\partial q\|_{0}^{2}+\left(\int_{\Omega}q\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\Big).

Let
X=(x^{1},0,0)^{T}
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}q\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}X^{i}q\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}=\left(\int_{\Omega}x^{1}\partial_{1}q\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\leq C\|\partial q\|_{0}^{2}.

Thus,

(3.72)

\displaystyle\|q\|_{0}^{2}\leq C(|\Omega|)\|\partial q\|_{0}^{2}.

Finally, (
3.70
) follows from (
3.71
) and (
3.72
).
∎

3.6.2.

We next bound

To achieve this, we take the dot product of the momentum equation
with

Since

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}v)\cdot\mathbf{N}\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\psi+(\overline{v}\cdot\overline{\partial})\partial_{t}\psi,

c.

or turning occurs on the moving surface boundary.

Also, parallel to Theorem

Appendix A
The Reynold Transport Theorems

Lemma A.1
.

Let
f,\,g
be smooth functions defined on
[0,T]\times\Omega
. Then there holds that

(A.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{\varphi}f)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f(\partial_{t}^{\varphi}g)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}fg\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Proof.

We exchange
\partial_{t}
and the integral in
\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x
and use the definition (
1.10
) of
\partial_{t}^{\varphi}
to get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}f)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f(\partial_{t}g)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{t}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}^{\varphi}f)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f(\partial_{t}^{\varphi}g)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{t}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi\partial_{3}fg\mathrm{d}x}_{A}+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi f\partial_{3}g\mathrm{d}x}_{B}.

Since
\partial_{t}\varphi|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=0
,
we integrate
\partial_{3}
in B by parts to give us

b=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}fg\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}-\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{t}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x-A,

which concludes the proof of (
A.1
).
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f,\,g
be defined as in Lemma
A.1
. Then there holds that for
i=1,2
:

(A.2)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{i}^{\varphi}f\right)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}f\left(\partial_{i}^{\varphi}g\right)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}fgN_{i}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Furthermore, if
g|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=0
, then

(A.3)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{3}^{\varphi}f\right)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}f\left(\partial_{3}^{\varphi}g\right)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}fgN_{3}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Proof.

We consider the cases when
i=1,\,2
and
i=3
respectively. We have

\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{i}^{\varphi}f\right)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}^{\varphi}(fg)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x}_{C}-\int_{\Omega}f\left(\partial_{i}^{\varphi}g\right)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

Let
i=1,2
. Note that
\partial_{i}\varphi\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=0
and
\partial_{i}\varphi\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}=\partial_{i}\psi
. We expand C as

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}(fg)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{3}(fg)\partial_{i}\varphi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(fg)\partial_{i}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x-\left\{\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}(fg)\partial_{i}\varphi\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}-\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}(fg)\partial_{i}\varphi\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}-\int_{\Omega}(fg)\partial_{3}\partial_{i}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}fgN_{i}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

On the other hand, in the case when
i=3
, since
g|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{btm}}}=0
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{3}^{\varphi}f\right)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{3}f)g\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}fg\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}-\int_{\Omega}f\left(\partial_{3}^{\varphi}g\right)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

∎

Theorem A.3
.

Let
f
be described as in Lemma
A.1
. Then we have

(A.4)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}^{\varphi}f\right)f\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

We expand the RHS of (

Invoking Lemma
A.1
,

\displaystyle i=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}f\left(\partial_{t}^{\varphi}f\right)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}|f|^{2}\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}}|f|^{2}\partial_{t}\psi\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Also, since

Since
\partial_{t}\psi=v\cdot N
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}

and
ii
.

∎

Corollary A.4
.

Let
f,\,g
be defined as in Lemma
A.1
. Then it holds that

(A.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}fg\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}^{\varphi}f\right)g\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}f\left(D_{t}^{\varphi}g\right)\partial_{3}\varphi\mathrm{d}x.

Lemma B.1

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let

. There exists a constant

(1)

Lemma B.2
.

Let
f
be a continuous function defined on
\Omega
. Then

(B.3)

\displaystyle|f|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

We proceed with the proof by contradiction.
Let
\|f\|_{\infty}=L
. We assume that (
B.3
) is false, then there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that

(B.4)

\displaystyle|f|_{\infty}\geq L+4\epsilon.

Since
\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}
is compact, and so there exists a point
\bar{x}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{top}}
such that
|f|_{\infty}=|f(\bar{x})|
. Let
\{x_{n}\}\subset\text{int }\Omega
be the sequence that converges to
\bar{x}
, where
\text{int }\Omega
is the interior of
\Omega
. Since
f
is continuous on
\Omega
, there exists an
N>0
such that
|f(x_{N})-f(\bar{x})|<2\epsilon
. On the other hand, the continuity of
f
also implies that there exists an
\delta>0
such that
|f(x)-f(x_{N})|<\epsilon
holds for all
x\in B_{\delta}(x_{N})
, i.e., the ball centered at
x_{N}
with radius
\delta
. Thus, it holds that
|f(x)-f(\bar{x})|<3\epsilon
for all
x\in B_{\delta}(x_{N})
. Together with (
B.4
), this implies that
\|f\|_{\infty}\geq L+\epsilon
, which contradicts the definition of
L
.
∎

Remark B.3
.

This theorem is false if

\Omega
. For instance, we consider

\displaystyle f(x^{\prime},x_{3})=\begin{cases}1,\quad|x^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{2},\,\,x_{3}=0,\\

Then

Appendix C
The Hodge-type elliptic estimate

Theorem C.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth vector field. Let
s\geq 1
be an integer. Then

(C.1)

\|X\|_{s}^{2}\leq C_{0}(|\psi|_{C^{s}})\left(\|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot X\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\partial^{\varphi}\times X\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{s}X\|_{0}^{2}+\|X\|_{0}^{2}\right).

Proof.

This theorem is essentially Lemma B.2 of
[
18
]
, whose proof is built on the following Hodge-type decomposition,

(C.2)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\varphi}X|\leq C(|\psi|_{C^{s}})\left(|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot X|+|\partial^{\varphi}\times X|+|\overline{\partial}X|\right),

which is Lemma B.1 of
[
18
]
. We recall that
\partial^{\varphi}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}
, and
\partial_{i}=(\mathcal{A}^{-1})_{i}^{j}\partial^{\varphi}_{j}
, where

\mathcal{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-\frac{\partial_{1}\varphi}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\\
0&1&-\frac{\partial_{2}\varphi}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\\
0&0&\frac{1}{\partial_{3}\varphi}\end{pmatrix}^{T},\quad\,\,\mathcal{A}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\partial_{1}\varphi\\
0&1&\partial_{2}\varphi\\
0&0&\partial_{3}\varphi\end{pmatrix}^{T}.

We prove (
C.1
) by induction. When
s=1
, we derive (
C.1
) from (
C.2
) after squaring and integrating in space. We next assume
s>1
, and (
C.1
) holds for all
m\leq s-1
. Let
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
be a multi-index with
|\beta|=s-1
. We write

(C.3)

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\partial^{\beta}X|=|(\mathcal{A}^{-1})^{j}_{i}\partial_{j}\partial^{\beta}X|\leq C(|\psi|_{C^{s}})|\partial^{\varphi}_{i}\partial^{\beta}X|,

and then invoke (
C.2
) to arrive at

(C.4)

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\partial^{\beta}X|^{2}\leq C(|\psi|_{C^{s}})\left(|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot(\partial^{\beta}X)|^{2}+|\partial^{\varphi}\times(\partial^{\beta}X)|^{2}+|\overline{\partial}\partial^{\beta}X|^{2}\right).

This leads to

(C.5)

\displaystyle\|X\|_{s}^{2}\leq C(|\psi|_{C^{s}})\left(\|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot(\partial^{\beta}X)\|_{0}^{2}+\|\partial^{\varphi}\times(\partial^{\beta}X)\|_{0}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}\partial^{\beta}X\|_{0}^{2}\right)

after integrating in space.
For the first term on the RHS of (
C.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot(\partial^{\beta}X)\|_{0}^{2}\leq\|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot X\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|[\partial^{\beta},\partial^{\varphi}\cdot]X\|_{0}^{2}\leq C(|\psi|_{C^{s}})\left(\|\partial^{\varphi}\cdot X\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|X\|_{s}^{2}\right),

where
\|X\|_{s}^{2}
is covered by the inductive hypothesis. In addition, the second term on the RHS of (
C.5
) is treated similarly. Finally, since
\overline{\partial}
commutes with
\partial
, the last term in (
C.5
) is just
\|\partial^{\beta}(\overline{\partial}X)\|_{0}^{2}
, which can be further reduced by repeating the steps above.
∎

Berry-Esseen theorems for the asymptotic normality of incomplete U-statistics with Bernoulli sampling
Dennis Leung

Abstract
There has been a resurgence of interest in incomplete U-statistics that only sum over a subset of kernel evaluations, due to their computational efficiency and asymptotic normality which can be leveraged to quantify the uncertainty of ensemble predictions in machine learning. In this paper, we study the weak convergences to normality of one such construction, the incomplete U-statistic with Bernoulli sampling, under three different regimes on the relative sizes of the raw sample and the computational budget. Under minimalistic moment assumptions, we establish accompanying Berry-Esseen bounds with the natural rates that characterize the accuracy of these normal approximations. The key ingredients in our proofs include a variable censoring technique and a methodology for establishing Berry-Esseen bounds for the so-called Studentized nonlinear statistics recently formalized in the Stein’s method literature, as well as an exponential lower tail bound for non-negative kernel U-statistics.

U-statistics; Berry-Esseen theorems; exponential tail bounds; self-normalized limit theory; Stein’s method; variable censoring

Introduction

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\in M
are independent and identically distributed random variables
taking values in a given measurable space
(M,\mathcal{M})
.
Hoeffding (1948)
first introduced U-statistics, which, for a given degree
m\geq 2
, are statistics of the form

U_{n}=\frac{1}{{n\choose m}}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\in I_{n,m}}h(X_{i_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{m}}),

(1.1)

where
{I_{n,m}}\equiv\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{m}):1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n\}
and
h:M^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given symmetric function in
m
arguments, i.e.

h(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=h(x_{\pi_{1}},\dots,x_{\pi_{m}})

for any permutation
(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{m})
of the indices
(1,\dots,m)
. It generalizes the sample mean and encompasses many examples such as the sample variance; see
Koroljuk and Borovskich (1994)
for a comprehensive treatment of the classical theory on U-statistics.
To simplify notation, for each
m
-tuple
{\bf i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\in I_{n,m}
, we will henceforth adopt the shorthands
{\bf X}_{\bf i}\equiv(X_{i_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{m}})
and
h({\bf X}_{\bf i})\equiv h(X_{i_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{m}})
.

U_{n}
is
complete
, in a sense that it is an average of the kernel
h
evaluated at
all possible
ordered subsamples of size
m
from
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Blom (1976)
suggested alternative constructions dubbed “incomplete U-statistics” which only average over an appropriate subset of
{I_{n,m}}
, with the intuition that the strong dependence among the summands in (
1.1
) should allow one to only use a well-chosen subset of them without losing too much statistical efficiency. In this work, we study the asymptotic normality of one such construction, known as the
incomplete U-statistic with Bernoulli sampling
, which has the form

U_{n,N}^{\prime}\equiv\frac{1}{\hat{N}}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}Z_{{\bf i}}h({\bf X}_{\bf i}),

(1.2)

where the
Z_{\bf i}
’s are
{n\choose m}
independent and identically distributed Bernoulli random variables with success probability

p=p_{n,N,m}\equiv N/{n\choose m}\text{ for a positive integer }N<{n\choose m},

(1.3)

i.e.,
P(Z_{\bf i}=1)=p=N{n\choose m}^{-1}
and
P(Z_{\bf i}=0)=1-p=1-N{n\choose m}^{-1}
,
and
\hat{N}
is defined as

\hat{N}\equiv\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}Z_{\bf i}.

The Bernoulli samplers
(Z_{\bf i})_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}
are also assumed to be independent of the raw data
(X_{i})_{i\in[n]}
. We call
N
the
computational budget
because it is the expected number of kernel evaluations
\hat{N}
that
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
winds up summing over.
In the sequel, we will use
\mathcal{X}\equiv(X_{i})_{i\in[n]}
and
\mathcal{Z}\equiv(Z_{\bf i})_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}
to represent the entirety of the raw data and Bernoulli samplers, respectively.

Let
\sigma_{h}^{2}\equiv\text{var}[h(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})]
. To describe the asymptotic normality of
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
, throughout this paper, we will assume

\mathbb{E}[h(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})]=0\text{ and }0<\sigma_{h}^{2}<\infty,

(1.4)

without loss of generality; see Section
1.1
below. For each
r=1,\dots,m
, we will define the
r
-argument function
h_{r}:M^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated with the kernel
h
by

h_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\equiv\mathbb{E}[h(x_{1},\dots,x_{r},X_{r+1},\dots,X_{m})],

and let

d\equiv\min\{r:\text{var}[h_{r}(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})]>0\}

be the
rank
of
h
. Moreover, for simplicity, we rename the single-argument function
h_{1}
as

g(x)\equiv h_{1}(x),

and the kernel
h
is said to be non-degenerate when

\sigma_{g}^{2}\equiv\text{var}[g(X_{1})]>0,

(1.5)

or equivalently, when
d=1
.
In the sequel, we will also let

\alpha=\alpha_{n,N}\equiv\frac{n}{N}.

and

\sigma^{2}\equiv m^{2}\sigma_{g}^{2}+\alpha\sigma_{h}^{2}.

The following weak convergences of
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
to normality under different regimes on the relative sizes between
n
and
N

; also see Section

Theorem 1.1

(Normal convergence of
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
for a given degree
m
)
.

Fix
m\geq 2
. Suppose both
n
and
N
tend to
\infty
and the assumptions in (
1.4
) holds.

(i)

If
\alpha\longrightarrow 0
, and (
1.5
) is true, then
\frac{\sqrt{n}U_{n,N}^{\prime}}{m\sigma_{g}}
converges weakly to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

(ii)

If

, then
\frac{\sqrt{N}U_{n,N}^{\prime}}{\sigma_{h}}
converges weakly to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

\mathcal{N}(0,1)

Note that Theorem

(ii)
does not require the non-degeneracy condition (

(B-E) bounds that characterize their normal approximation accuracy have been missing from the literature. In recent years,
these normal convergences have found a resurgence of interest among the machine learning community, since

following up on this line of work by proving B-E bounds for the convergences in Theorem

(i)

; however, their bounds contain artefacts that clearly suggest they are not of the optimal rate, as we will explain in Section

In this work, we precisely offers B-E bounds with the natural rates for the convergences in Theorem
1.1
to fill this literature gap. Broadly speaking, our refined results are enabled by three technical advances:

(i)

a framework of using Stein’s method to prove B-E bounds for the so-called “Studentized nonlinear statistics” formalized in
Leung et al. (2024)
,

(ii)

the handy “variable censoring” technique introduced in the same paper, and

(iii)

an exponential lower tail bound for the non-negative U-statistic proven in
Leung and Shao (2024)
.

In passing, we mention that the statistical community has also been developing B-E bounds for
high-dimensional
incomplete U-statistics with vector-valued kernels
(
Song et al., 2019
,
Chen and Kato, 2019
)
, but our focus here is to provide bounds of the best rate for the one-dimensional convergent results in Theorem
1.1
.

1.1
The random normalizer
\hat{N}
of
U^{\prime}_{n,N}

Suppose we do not assume
\mathbb{E}[h]=0
as in (
1.4
). Because
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
is normalized by

, one can write

U^{\prime}_{n,N}-\mathbb{E}[h]=\frac{1}{\hat{N}}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}Z_{{\bf i}}(h({\bf X}_{\bf i})-\mathbb{E}[h]);

which is the same incomplete U-statistic construction in (

) based on the centered kernel

. Therefore, the three convergences in Theorem
1.1
can be restated simply by replacing all the occurrences of

by
(U^{\prime}_{n,N}-\mathbb{E}[h])

1.1
(ii)
can be stated as the weak convergence of
\sqrt{N}(U_{n,N}^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}[h])/\sigma_{h}
to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. This also explains why
assuming
\mathbb{E}[h]=0
does not forgo any generality of the Berry-Esseen-type results developed for these convergences.

The random
\hat{N}
is also preferred to the deterministic

as a normalizer for statistical power. In fact,
Janson (1984, Corollary 1)
is originally stated for the weak convergence of the deterministically normalized
\breve{U}_{n,N}^{\prime}\equiv N^{-1}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}Z_{{\bf i}}h({\bf X}_{\bf i})
: Under exactly the same asymptotic regimes and assumptions as Theorem
1.1

,
Janson (1984, Corollary 1)
states the three weak convergences in Theorem

and

and

become the weak convergences of

\frac{\sqrt{N}(\breve{U}_{n,N}^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}[h])}{\sqrt{\sigma_{h}^{2}+(\mathbb{E}[h])^{2}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\sqrt{n}(\breve{U}_{n,N}^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}[h])}{\sqrt{m^{2}\sigma_{g}^{2}+\alpha(\sigma_{h}^{2}+(\mathbb{E}[h])^{2})}}

to
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
1
1

1

Therefore,

implies Theorem

as

.
. Since

is asymptotically less powerful than

1.2
Random kernel incomplete U-statistics

Our present work is partly stimulated by the applications of incomplete U-statistics in uncertainty quantification for ensemble predictions, where each base learner is built on a subsample of the original data. Some of these applications may employ base learners of a randomized nature, such as the trees in
Breiman (2001)
’s random forests, which randomly select the input covariates to split each node. To accommodate such applications, the kernels in (
1.2
) have to be embellished by additional random elements, to result in a
random kernel
incomplete U-statistic of the form

U_{n,N,\omega}^{\prime}\equiv\frac{1}{\hat{N}}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}Z_{{\bf i}}h({\bf X}_{\bf i};\omega_{\bf i}),

(1.6)

where
\omega\equiv(\omega_{\bf i})_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}
are independent and identically distributed random parameters that are also independent of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Z}

(
Mentch and Hooker, 2016
,
Peng et al., 2022
)
. Each
h({\bf X}_{\bf i};\omega_{\bf i})
, while still symmetric in its arguments
X_{i_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{m}}
, also depends on the realized value of its random parameter
\omega_{\bf i}
. The methodology in this paper can also be readily extended to prove B-E bounds for (
1.6
); see Section
6
.

The normal convergences of
U_{n,N}^{\prime}

In what follows, we let

U_{h^{2}}\equiv{n\choose m}^{-1}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}h^{2}({\bf X}_{\bf i})\text{ and }U_{|h|^{3}}\equiv{n\choose m}^{-1}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}|h|^{3}({\bf X}_{\bf i}),

(2.1)

be the U-statistics whose
non-negative
kernels

and
|h|^{3}
are induced by taking the square and cube of the absolute value of the original kernel
h
. Moreover, as in many recent works on B-E bounds for incomplete U-statistics
(
Chen and Kato, 2019

Song et al., 2019
,
Peng et al., 2022
,
Sturma et al., 2024
)
, much of the development in this paper revolves around a basic decomposition of
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
, which is

U_{n,N}^{\prime}=\frac{N}{\hat{N}}(\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n}+U_{n})\text{ when }\hat{N}\neq 0,

(2.2)

for

B_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}\frac{Z_{\bf i}-p}{\sqrt{1-p}}h({\bf X}_{\bf i}).

(2.3)

Therefore, up the multiplicative scaling factor
N/\hat{N}
,

can be understand as a sum of the complete U-statistic

and
\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n}
.

We first expand on the nature of the normal convergences in Theorem
1.1

3

U_{n}
and

. Given (

\sqrt{n}U_{n}\text{ converges weakly to }\mathcal{N}(0,m^{2}\sigma_{g}^{2})\text{ as }n\longrightarrow\infty\\

(2.4)

see
Koroljuk and Borovskich (1994, Ch.4)
. On the other hand, when

,
\frac{\sqrt{N}B_{n}}{\sqrt{U_{h^{2}}}}=\sum_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}\zeta_{\bf i}
is a sum
of the conditionally independent random variables

\zeta_{\bf i}\equiv\frac{(Z_{\bf i}-p)h({\bf X}_{\bf i})}{\sqrt{U_{h^{2}}N(1-p)}}

(2.5)

indexed by
{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}
, each having the conditional mean

\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{\bf i}\mid\mathcal{X}]=0,

(2.6)

and the conditional second and third absolute moments

\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{\bf i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{X}]=\frac{h^{2}({\bf X}_{\bf i})}{U_{h^{2}}{n\choose m}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}[|\zeta_{\bf i}|^{3}\mid\mathcal{X}]=\frac{(1-2p+2p^{2})|h|^{3}({\bf X}_{\bf i})}{U_{h^{2}}^{3/2}{n\choose m}\sqrt{N(1-p)}};

in the latter.
Since
\sqrt{N}B_{n}/\sqrt{U_{h^{2}}}
has conditional variance
1

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{NB_{n}^{2}}{U_{h^{2}}}\mid\mathcal{X}\bigg]=\sum_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{\bf i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{X}]=\sum_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}\frac{h^{2}({\bf X}_{\bf i})}{U_{h^{2}}{n\choose m}}=1,

(2.7)

and the conditional absolute third moments of its summands add up to

\sum_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}\mathbb{E}\Big[|\zeta_{\bf i}|^{3}\mid\mathcal{X}\Big]=\frac{U_{|h|^{3}}(1-2p+2p^{2})}{U_{h^{2}}^{3/2}\sqrt{N(1-p)}},

the Lyapunov’s central limit theorem
(

, p.362)

suggests that

\text{
$\sqrt{N}B_{n}$ converges weakly to $\mathcal{N}(0,U_{h^{2}})$
conditional on $\mathcal{X}$
}\\
\text{under the asymptotic regimes of Theorem~\ref{thm:weak_convergences}$(ii)$ and $(iii)$,}

(2.9)

because
\frac{1-2p+2p^{2}}{\sqrt{N(1-p)}}\longrightarrow 0

The observations in (

2.1
The “

” regime,
\alpha\longrightarrow 0

By (

), write

\sqrt{n}U_{n,N}^{\prime}=\frac{N}{\hat{N}}(\sqrt{(1-p)n}B_{n}+\sqrt{n}U_{n}).

Because (

) implies

,

is bounded in probability, which further implies

), it is easy to see that

2.2
The “

” regime,

From the decomposition (

\frac{\sqrt{N}U_{n,N}^{\prime}}{\sigma_{h}}=\frac{N}{\hat{N}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{(1-p)N}B_{n}+\sqrt{N}U_{n}}{\sigma_{h}}.

5

5

The “

\frac{\sqrt{n}U_{n,N}^{\prime}}{\sigma}=\frac{N}{\hat{N}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{(1-p)n}B_{n}}{\sigma}+\frac{\sqrt{n}U_{n}}{\sigma}\bigg)\approx\frac{\alpha^{1/2}\sqrt{N}B_{n}+\sqrt{n}U_{n}}{\sigma},

is expected to be approximately normally distributed with mean

and

3
The main results

Since the normal convergences of
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
under the three regimes in Theorem
1.1
are driven by either or both of the distributions of
U_{n}
and
B_{n}
as discussed in Section
2
, one would intuitively expect their corresponding B-E bounds to capture features of the existing Berry-Esseen-type results for the convergences of
U_{n}
and
B_{n}
in (
2.4
) and (
2.9
). Firstly, under the non-degeneracy condition (
1.5
), the complete U-statistic
U_{n}
has an established B-E bound
(
Chen and Shao, 2007
,
Peng et al., 2022
)
that reads as

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\bigg|P\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{n}U_{n}}{m\sigma_{g}}\leq z\bigg)-\Phi(z)\bigg|\leq\frac{6.1\mathbb{E}[|g|^{3}]}{\sqrt{n}\sigma_{g}^{3}}+(1+\sqrt{2})\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\bigg)\bigg)^{1/2}.

(3.1)

3.1
Preparation for the proofs

The proofs of Theorems
3.1
-
3.3
all adopt a handy technique called
variable censoring
, recently introduced in
Leung et al. (2024)
as a refinement of the variable truncation technique for proving B-E bounds with Stein’s method. For a given real-valued random variable
Y
, its censored version with respect to two constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
, where
a\leq b
, is defined as

\bar{Y}\equiv aI(Y<a)+YI(a\leq Y\leq b)+bI(Y>b),

i.e. once
Y
goes beyond the range
[a,b]
, its censored version
\bar{Y}
can only be either
a
or
b
depending on which direction the value of
Y
goes; that
a
or/and
b
are allowed to be infinity means that
Y
may only be censored in one direction, or not be censored at all.
This technique has the following apparent but useful properties for establishing bounds:

Property 3.4

(Properties of variable censoring)
.

Let
Y
and
Z
be any two real-value variables. The following facts hold:

(i)

Suppose, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
with
a\leq b
, we define

\bar{Y}\equiv aI(Y<a)+YI(a\leq Y\leq b)+bI(Y>b)

and

\bar{Z}\equiv aI(Z<a)+ZI(a\leq Z\leq b)+bI(Z>b);

then it must be that

|\bar{Y}-\bar{Z}|\leq|Y-Z|.

(ii)

If
Y
is a non-negative random variable, then it must also be true that

YI(0\leq Y\leq b)+bI(Y>b)\leq Y\text{ for any }b\in(0,\infty),

i.e., the upper-censored version of
Y
is always no larger than
Y
itself.

Throughout, we will use
\Omega
to denote the entire underlying probability space. Moreover, we will define
the (upper-and-lower) censored version of
\hat{N}

\hat{N}_{c}\equiv\hat{N}I\Big(\hat{N}\in\Big[\frac{N}{2},\frac{3N}{2}\Big]\Big)+\frac{3N}{2}I\Big(\hat{N}>\frac{3N}{2}\Big)+\frac{N}{2}I\Big(\hat{N}<\frac{N}{2}\Big),

(3.12)

and
the event

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Z}}\equiv\bigg\{\hat{N}\in\Big[\frac{N}{2},\frac{3N}{2}\Big]\bigg\}

\mathcal{Z}

\hat{N}_{c}=\hat{N}\text{ on }\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Z}}.

(3.13)

\hat{N}

|\hat{N}_{c}-N|\leq|\hat{N}-N|,

(3.14)

\frac{n\mathbb{E}[(U_{\bar{h}_{m}}-U_{\bar{h}_{m}}^{(i)})^{2}]}{m^{2}\sigma_{g}^{2}}\leq\frac{2(m-1)^{2}}{nm(n-m+1)}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\bigg)\leq\frac{4m}{n^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\bigg).

(4.2)

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{E}[B_{n}^{2}]=\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{N}

(4.3)

from (
2.7
) and

\mathbb{E}[(B_{n}-B_{n}^{(i)})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bf j}\in I_{n,m},i\in{\bf j}}\frac{Z_{\bf j}-p}{\sqrt{1-p}}h({\bf X}_{\bf j})\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\\
=\frac{1}{N^{2}(1-p)}\sum_{{\bf j}\in I_{n,m},i\in{\bf j}}\mathbb{E}[(Z_{\bf j}-p)^{2}h^{2}({\bf X}_{\bf j})]=\frac{{n-1\choose m-1}\sigma_{h}^{2}}{N{n\choose m}}=\frac{m\sigma_{h}^{2}}{Nn}.

(4.4)

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we write

\bigg|P\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{n}U_{n,N}^{\prime}}{m\sigma_{g}}\leq z\bigg)-\Phi(z)\bigg|\leq\bigg|P\bigg(\frac{N}{\hat{N}_{c}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}(U_{n}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n})}{m\sigma_{g}}\leq z\bigg)-\Phi(z)\bigg|+P(\Omega\backslash\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Z}})

by virtue of the decomposition in (
2.2
).
So, it suffice to prove the B-E bound

\bigg|P\bigg(\frac{N}{\hat{N}_{c}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}(U_{n}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n})}{m\sigma_{g}}\leq z\bigg)-\Phi(z)\bigg|\leq\\
C\bigg\{\frac{\mathbb{E}[|g|^{3}]}{\sqrt{n}\sigma_{g}^{3}}+\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\bigg)\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\frac{n(1-p)\sigma_{h}^{2}}{Nm\sigma_{g}^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg\}

(4.5)

in light of (
3.16
), as
\exp(-3N/28)\leq C/\sqrt{N}
. To that end, we will invoke an existing result on Berry-Esseen bounds for so-called nonlinear statistics from
Chen et al. (2011, Theorem 10.1)
:

Lemma 4.1

(B-E bound for nonlinear statistics)
.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be independent random variables satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}]=0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}^{2}]=1
, and define
S\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}
. Moreover, let
\Delta
be any random variable on the same probability space as
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
. Then

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}|P(S+\Delta\leq z)-P(S\leq z)|\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}^{3}]+\mathbb{E}[|S\Delta|]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{i}(\Delta-\Delta^{(i)})|],

where
\Delta^{(i)}
is any random variable on the same probability space such that
\xi_{i}
and
(S-\xi_{i},\Delta^{(i)})
are independent, for each
i=1,\dots n
.

Now, we will define

\Delta_{1}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{n}(U_{\bar{h}_{m}}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n})}{m\sigma_{g}}\text{ and }\\
\Delta_{2}\equiv\bigg(\frac{N-\hat{N}_{c}}{\hat{N}_{c}}\bigg)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}(U_{n}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n})}{m\sigma_{g}},\\
\text{ and }\Delta\equiv\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}

(4.6)

as well as writing

\frac{N}{\hat{N}_{c}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}(U_{n}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n})}{m\sigma_{g}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(X_{i})}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}.

(4.7)

via the decomposition

U_{n}=\frac{m\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(X_{i})}{n}+U_{\bar{h}_{m}}.

(4.8)

For each
i\in[n]
, we also define the leave-one-out versions of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

\Delta_{1}^{(i)}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{n}(U_{\bar{h}_{m}}^{(i)}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n}^{(i)})}{m\sigma_{g}}\text{ and }\\
\Delta_{2}^{(i)}\equiv\bigg(\frac{N-\hat{N}_{c}}{\hat{N}_{c}}\bigg)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}(\frac{m\sum_{j\neq i}g(X_{j})}{n}+U_{\bar{h}_{m}}^{(i)}+\sqrt{(1-p)}B_{n}^{(i)})}{m\sigma_{g}}

that leave out any terms involving
X_{i}
,
where we have used (
4.8
) in defining
\Delta_{2}^{(i)}
.
To prove a bound on (
4.5
), under the non-degeneracy condition (
1.5
), we will now apply Lemma
4.1
by taking

\xi_{i}=\frac{g(X_{i})}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}},\quad\Delta=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},\quad\Delta^{(i)}=\Delta^{(i)}_{1}+\Delta^{(i)}_{2},

(4.9)

as well as using the following estimates regarding the remainder terms proven in Appendix
B
:

Lemma 4.2

(Estimates regarding
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
)
.

The following estimates are true:

(i)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(X_{i})}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\cdot\Delta_{1}\Big|\Big]\leq\Big(\frac{2m}{n}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\Big)\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\frac{n(1-p)\sigma_{h}^{2}}{Nm\sigma_{g}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}
;

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{g(X_{i})}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\cdot(\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{1}^{(i)})\Big|\Big]\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{m}}{n}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\Big)^{1/2}+\frac{\sigma_{h}}{\sigma_{g}}\sqrt{\frac{(1-p)}{Nm}}\Big)
;

(iii)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(X_{i})}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\cdot\Delta_{2}\Big|\Big]\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(1+\Big(\frac{2m}{n}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\Big)\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\frac{n(1-p)\sigma_{h}^{2}}{Nm\sigma_{g}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big)
;

(iv)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{g(X_{i})}{\sigma_{g}\sqrt{n}}\cdot(\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{2}^{(i)})\Big|\Big]\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{nN}}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{2\sqrt{m}}{n}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{m\sigma_{g}^{2}}-1\Big)^{1/2}+\frac{\sigma_{h}}{\sigma_{g}}\sqrt{\frac{(1-p)}{Nm}}\Big)
.

Combining (
4.9
) and Lemmas
4.1
-
4.2
proves (
4.5
), and hence Theorem
3.1
.

5
Proofs of Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
, the cases of “
N\ll n^{d}
” and “
N\asymp n
”

The proofs of Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
are placed under the same umbrella, because both employ two crucial ingredients:

(i)

an exponential lower tail bound for the non-negative U-statistic
U_{h^{2}}
in (
2.1
), and

(ii)

a framework of using Stein’s method to prove B-E bounds for the so-called “Studentized nonlinear statistics”.

Our first ingredient is the lower tail probability bound

P(U_{h^{2}}\leq\sigma^{2}/2)\leq 1.05\exp\Big(\frac{-n\sigma_{h}^{6}}{24m(\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}])^{2}}\Big)\text{ when }2m<n,

(5.1)

which can be established by Lemma
5.1
below.

Lemma 5.1

(Exponential lower tail bound for U-statistics with non-negative kernels,
Leung and Shao (2024)
)
.

Suppose
U_{\kappa}={n\choose m}^{-1}\sum_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}\kappa(X_{\bf i})
is a U-statistic, and
\kappa:M^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a symmetric kernel of degree
m
that can only take non-negative values, with the property that
0<\mathbb{E}[\kappa^{\ell}]<\infty
for some
\ell\in(1,2]
. Then for
0<t\leq\mathbb{E}[\kappa]
,

P(U_{\kappa}\leq t)\leq\exp\left(\frac{-[n/m](\ell-1)(\mathbb{E}[\kappa]-t)^{\ell/(\ell-1)}}{\ell(\mathbb{E}[\kappa^{\ell}])^{1/(\ell-1)}}\right),

where
[n/m]
is defined as the greatest integer no larger than
n/m
.

Note that Lemma
5.1
immediately gives
P(U_{h^{2}}\leq\sigma^{2}/2)\leq\exp\Big(\frac{-[n/m]\sigma_{h}^{6}}{24(\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}])^{2}}\Big)
by taking
\ell=3/2
and
t=\sigma_{h}^{2}/2
, but when
2m<n
, one can further simplify it with

\exp\Big(\frac{-[n/m]\sigma_{h}^{6}}{24(\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}])^{2}}\Big)\leq\exp\Big(\frac{-(n/m-1)\sigma_{h}^{6}}{24(\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}])^{2}}\Big)<1.05\exp\Big(\frac{-n\sigma_{h}^{6}}{24m(\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}])^{2}}\Big),

to give (
5.1
), where the last inequality uses
\sigma_{h}^{6}\leq(\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}])^{2}
and
\exp(1/24)<1.05
.
The exponential decay in
n
of the bound (
5.1
) implies that the event

\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}\equiv\bigg\{U_{h^{2}}>\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{2}\bigg\}

(5.2)

has a dominating probability, a fact we will leverage. For the rest of this paper, with a slight abuse of notation we will use
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}
to indicate the realized value of
\mathcal{X}
is such that
U_{h^{2}}>\sigma_{h}^{2}/2
. Moreover, we will lower censor
U_{h^{2}}
as

\bar{U}_{h^{2}}\equiv\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{2}I\Big(U_{h^{2}}\leq\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{2}\Big)+U_{h^{2}}I\Big(U_{h^{2}}>\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{2}\Big),

(5.3)

which has the property that

\bar{U}_{h^{2}}=U_{h^{2}}\text{ on }\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}.

(5.4)

Now we describe the second ingredient. Let

\xi_{\bf i}\equiv\frac{(Z_{\bf i}-p)h({\bf X}_{\bf i})}{(\bar{U}_{h^{2}}N(1-p))^{1/2}},

(5.5)

whose first moments conditional on the raw data
\mathcal{X}
can be computed as

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\bf i}\mid\mathcal{X}]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\bf i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{X}]=\frac{h^{2}({\bf X}_{\bf i})}{\bar{U}_{h^{2}}{n\choose m}}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{\bf i}|^{3}\mid\mathcal{X}]=\frac{(1-2p+2p^{2})|h({\bf X}_{\bf i})|^{3}}{{n\choose m}\bar{U}_{h^{2}}^{3/2}\sqrt{N(1-p)}}.

(5.6)

Note the similarity between the forms of
\xi_{\bf i}
and
\zeta_{\bf i}
in (
2.5
); however,
\xi_{\bf i}
is always well-defined because, unlike
U_{h^{2}}
, the
\bar{U}_{h^{2}}
in its denominator is always greater than
1/2
, so division by zero is not an issue. Now, for any
arbitrary
random variable

D_{1}=D_{1}(\mathcal{Z};\mathcal{X})

(5.7)

that is a function of both
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Z}

and

D_{2}\equiv-\frac{\sigma_{h}^{2}}{\bar{U}_{h^{2}}}\Big(\frac{U_{h^{2}}}{\sigma_{h}^{2}}-1\Big),

(5.8)

we
define
the generic random variable

T_{SN}\equiv\frac{\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}\xi_{\bf i}+D_{1}}{\sqrt{1+D_{2}}},

(5.9)

which will serve as a proof device for establishing Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
. In particular, for a generic threshold

\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{X})\in\mathbb{R}

(5.10)

which may possibly depend on the data
\mathcal{X}
, we will
leverage the following general Berry-Esseen bound for the absolute difference
\big|P(T_{SN}\leq\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})-\mathbb{E}[\Phi(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})]\big|
.

Lemma 5.2

(B-E bound for
|P(T_{SN}\leq\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})-\mathbb{E}\mbox{[}\Phi(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})\mbox{]}|
)
.

Suppose that (
1.4
),
\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}]<\infty
and
2\leq m<n/2
hold. Then for any
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathbb{R}
that possibly depends on the data
\mathcal{X}
, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

|P(T_{SN}\leq\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})-\mathbb{E}[\Phi(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})]|\leq C\bigg(T(D_{1})+T(D_{2})+P(\Omega\backslash\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}})+\frac{\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}](1-2p+2p^{2})}{\sigma_{h}^{3}\sqrt{N(1-p)}}\bigg),

for

T(D_{1})\equiv P(|D_{1}|>1/2)+\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{1}^{2}|\mathcal{X}]}+\frac{\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}|h({\bf X}_{\bf i})|\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[(\bar{D}_{1}-\bar{D}_{1}^{({\bf i})})^{2}|\mathcal{X}]}}{\sigma_{h}\sqrt{{n\choose m}}}\Bigg]

and

T(D_{2})\equiv P\Big(|D_{2}|>1/2\Big)+\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_{2}^{2}]+\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\bar{D}_{2}f_{\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Big(\sum_{{\bf i}\in I_{n,m}}{\bar{\xi}}_{\bf i}\Big)\Big]\Big|,

where we have defined

\bar{D}_{k}\equiv D_{k}I\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\leq D_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}I\Big(D_{k}<-\frac{1}{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}I\Big(D_{k}>\frac{1}{2}\Big)\text{ for }k=1,2,

{\bar{\xi}}_{\bf i}\equiv\xi_{\bf i}I(|\xi_{\bf i}|\leq 1)+1I(\xi_{\bf i}>1)-1I(\xi_{\bf i}<-1),

(5.11)

and

f_{\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}}(w)\equiv\begin{cases}\sqrt{2\pi}e^{w^{2}/2}\Phi(w)\bar{\Phi}(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})&w\leq\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\\
\sqrt{2\pi}e^{w^{2}/2}\Phi(\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}})\bar{\Phi}(w)&w>\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\end{cases};

(5.12)

moreover,

D_{1}^{({\bf i})}=D_{1}^{({\bf i})}(\mathcal{Z}\backslash\{Z_{\bf i}\};\mathcal{X})\text{ is any function in }\mathcal{X}\text{ and }\mathcal{Z}\text{ except }Z_{\bf i}

(5.13)

and

\bar{D}_{1}^{({\bf i})}\equiv D_{1}^{({\bf i})}I\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\leq D_{1}^{({\bf i})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}I\Big(D_{1}^{({\bf i})}<-\frac{1}{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}I\Big(D_{1}^{({\bf i})}>\frac{1}{2}\Big),

(5.14)

is its censored version.

We remark that (
5.12
) is simply the typical solution
(
Chen et al., 2011
, p.14)

f_{z}(w)\equiv\begin{cases}\sqrt{2\pi}e^{w^{2}/2}\Phi(w)\bar{\Phi}(z)&w\leq z\\
\sqrt{2\pi}e^{w^{2}/2}\Phi(z)\bar{\Phi}(w)&w>z\end{cases}

(5.15)

to the normal Stein equation
(
Stein, 1972
)

I(w\leq z)-\Phi(z)=\frac{\partial f_{z}}{\partial w}(w)-wf_{z}(w),

but with the fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
replaced by the possibly random
\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}
to accommodate our proof later.
Lemma
5.2
is established at some length in Appendix
C
via adapting the arguments from
Leung et al. (2024)
while conditioning on a given data realization
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}
. Importantly,
Leung et al. (2024)
fully formalizes a paradigm of using Stein’s method to prove B-E bounds for
Studentized nonlinear statistics
: For
n
real-valued deterministic functions
t_{1}(\cdot),\dots,t_{n}(\cdot)
and a sample of independent random variables
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{E}[t_{i}(G_{i})]=0
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[t^{2}(G_{i})]=1
, a Studentized nonlinear statistic is a statistic that can be written into the generic form

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}(G_{i})+\mathcal{D}_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\mathcal{D}_{2}}},

(5.16)

where
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathcal{D}_{1}(G_{1},\dots,G_{n})
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}=\mathcal{D}_{2}(G_{1},\dots,G_{n})
are (usually small) remainder terms that may depend on the data
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
, with
\mathcal{D}_{2}>-1
almost surely; in particular, the generic statistic in (
5.16
) is “Studentized” by the data-driven variance estimate
1+\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Indeed,

\text{$T_{SN}$ can be understood as an instance of \eqref{sn_stat_generic_form} }\\
\text{ when conditioning on a given data realization $\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}$}:

(5.17)

From the conditional moment calculations in (

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\bf i}\mid\mathcal{X}]=0\text{ and }\sum_{{\bf i}\in{I_{n,m}}}\mathbb{E}[\xi_{\bf i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{X}]=\frac{U_{h^{2}}}{\bar{U}_{h^{2}}}=1\text{ for }\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}},

where the last equality comes from the fact in (

).
Moreover, only

\mathcal{Z}

(5.19)

These verify the claim in (
5.17

6
Discussion

With more careful book keeping, it is possible to produce numerical estimates of the absolute constants
C
appearing in Theorems
3.1
-
3.3
. The most important contribution of this work nevertheless lies in our methodology of adapting the latest techniques in the Stein’s method literature to establish B-E bounds of natural rates in
N
and
n
for
U_{n,N}^{\prime}
, which we believe to be optimal. That being said, we don’t exclude the possibility that comparable bounds with simpler forms could be proved, potentially with alternative methods. At present, it is unclear to us how the terms in our bounds can be further simplified, such as by the “absorption trick” illustrated in (
5.33
). We also speculate that some techniques might be combined with the recent advances in the Berry-Esseen-type theory for sums of independent random vectors
(
Chernozhuokov et al., 2022
,
Chernozhukov et al., 2023
)
to improve upon the existing Berry-Esseen-type theory for vector-valued incomplete U-statistics
(
Song et al., 2019
,
Chen and Kato, 2019
)
.

It takes only slight adjustments to our present methodology to establish B-E bounds for the random kernel incomplete U-statistic
U_{n,N,\omega}^{\prime}
in (
1.6
) as well. For instance, the Hoeffding decomposition is also available to random kernel U-statistics
(
Peng et al., 2022
, Definition 2)
, and the conditioning on
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}
in (
5.17
) simply has to be changed to conditioning on both
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{E}_{1,\mathcal{X}}
and
\omega
altogether. To keep our presentation the most streamlined possible, we leave these details to the reader. Moreover, in the real applications for uncertainty quantification of ensemble predictions mentioned in Section
1.2
, one almost always has to
studentize
their incomplete U-statistics by data-driven estimates of the standardizing factors such as
\sigma_{g}
and
\sigma_{h}
in Theorem
1.1
; we refer the reader to
Zhou et al. (2021)
and
Peng et al. (2021)
for related work in this vein. Therefore, the more imperative and challenging task is to develop a Berry-Esseen theory for
studentized
incomplete U-statistics that is directly applicable; we are currently pursuing a deep investigation into this.

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{1}\|_{3/2}\Big(\mathbb{E}[P(\Omega\backslash{E}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z}}\mid\mathcal{X})]\Big)^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{1}\|_{3/2}\Bigg(\frac{2^{3/2}\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}](1-2p+2p^{2})}{\sigma_{h}^{3}\sqrt{N(1-p)}}\Bigg)^{1/3}\text{ by \eqref{cE_3_bdd} }

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\|\Pi_{1}\|_{3/2}\|h\|_{3}(1-2p+2p^{2})^{1/3}}{\sigma_{h}(N(1-p))^{1/6}}.

(F.15)

Putting (
F.13
)-(
F.15
) together gives

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\Pi_{1}f_{\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Big(\sum_{{\bf j}\in I_{n,m}}{\bar{\xi}}_{\bf j}\Big)\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}\|\Pi_{1}\|_{3/2}\|h\|_{3}(1-2p+2p^{2})^{1/3}}{\sigma_{h}(N(1-p))^{1/6}}+\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\Pi_{1}f_{\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Big(\sum_{{\bf j}\in I_{n,m}}\xi_{\bf j}\Big)\bigg]\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}\bigg(\mathbb{E}[|\Pi_{1}|^{3/2}]+\frac{\mathbb{E}[|h|^{3}](1-2p+2p^{2})}{\sigma_{h}^{3}(N(1-p))^{1/2}}\bigg)+\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}\Pi_{1}f_{\mathfrak{z}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Big(\sum_{{\bf j}\in I_{n,m}}\xi_{\bf j}\Big)\bigg]\bigg|.

(F.16)

Combining (
F.11
) and (
F.16
) gives (
E.9
).

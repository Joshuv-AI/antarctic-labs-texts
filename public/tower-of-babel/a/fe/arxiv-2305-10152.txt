On the extremal families for the Kruskal–Katona theorem
Oriol Serra, Lluís Vena

Abstract
In [ 9 , Serra, Vena, Extremal families for the Kruskal-Katona theorem] , the authors have shown a characterization of the extremal families for the Kruskal-Katona Theorem. We further develop some of the arguments given in [ 9 ] and give additional properties of these extremal families. Füredi-Griggs/Mörs theorem from 1986/85 [ 2 , 7 ] claims that, for some cardinalities, the initial segment of the colexicographical is the unique extremal family; we extend their result as follows: the number of (non-isomorphic) extremal families strictly grows with the gap between the last two coefficients of the k k -binomial decomposition. We also show that every family is an induced subfamily of an extremal family, and that, somewhat going in the opposite direction, every extremal family is close to being the inital segment of the colex order; namely, if the family is extremal, then after performing t t lower shadows, with t = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) ) t=O(\log(\log n)) , we obtain the initial segment of the colexicographical order. We also give a “fast” algorithm to determine whether, for a given t t and m m , there exists an extremal family of size m m for which its t t -th lower shadow is not yet the initial segment in the colexicographical order. As a byproduct of these arguments, we give yet another characterization of the families of k k -sets satisfying equality in the Kruskal–Katona theorem. Such characterization is, at first glance, less appealing than the one in [ 9 ] , since the additional information that it provides is indirect. However, the arguments used to prove such characterization provide additional insight on the structure of the extremal families themselves.

The hyptenusal process.

Given a bin-balls-wall configuration
(\mathcal{B}_{0},w_{0},0)
, we can uniquely find a sequence of bin-balls-walls configurations indexed by the iteration counter,
\{(\mathcal{B}_{t},\allowbreak w,t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}

.

At the beginning of each iteration step

(the first iteration being

as all the balls with delay counter
s\leq t

. Let

If the set of balls to be processed is not empty and none of them can be legally processed, then we say that the
process terminates abruptly

The legally processable ball

is processed as follows.

•

Add balls
\{(j,s+1),\ldots,(w-2,s+1),(w-1,s+1)\}

•

•

move the wall from the position
w
to the position
(w+1)
.

After all the balls from

are processed, we increment the iteration counter

and repeat the process with

is the last position of the wall obtained during the iteration

5.4
Growth of the hypotenusal numbers

For a detailed account of the growth of the hypothenusal numbers see

[

, Page 71, second equation from the bottom]
where the authors show that, asymptotically,

\displaystyle\frac{a[i]}{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{11}{18}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)+\frac{10}{81}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{11}{45}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\frac{2^{3}}{3^{3}}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{8}}+\frac{2^{4}}{3^{4}}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{16}}+\ldots+\frac{2^{j}}{3^{j}}\left(\frac{a[i-1]}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{j}}}+\ldots\right]

(31)

Note that (
5.4
) corrects some imprecisions with respect to the asymptotic expression appearing in

, Page 311, third equation from the top]
.

In the remaining of this section we use the ideas of correspondence between the hypothenusal process and the hypothenusal numbers given in Lemma

to provide some simple recurrences between

and

For a lower bound for

.

a[i]\geq\binom{a[i-1]+1}{2}+a[i-1],\text{ for }i\geq 2

which implies that

Now, for

,

and

, a straight inductive argument shows that, if

. Hence, we obtain that:

which is a double exponential increase. On the other hand, for an upper bound:

.

Maximal chains

Given a family
S

|E|
should we add to
S

(Q1)

S\cup E
is an extremal family, or

(Q2)

S\cup E

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
with

(Q2)(1)

all
\{S\cup E\cup\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}\}\}_{i\in[m]}
are extremal, and

(Q2)(2)

S\cup E\cup\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}=\binom{n}{k}

We do not give a comprehensive and precise answer to the
(Q1)
.
However, one can give an upper bound on the minimal size of
E
.
If
H
is the family of sets of
S
, we can find all the
H^{\prime}\subset H
such that
H^{\prime}
is the hypergraph of an extremal family
S^{\prime}
, then
S\subset S^{\prime}
and we have

|E|\leq\min_{|S^{\prime}|}\{S^{\prime} \text{ is extremal with hypergraph }H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\subset H\}-|S|

that is, we consider all the possible hypergraphs that are subhypergraphs of
H
and that give an extremal family, and then consider the smallest of such families (which is a superfamily of
S
).

For
(Q2)

32

.

1.
Introduction

The study of sign patterns of real-valued multiplicative functions at consecutive integers
has received a lot of attention in recent years, as a means of investigating the apparently random behaviour of multiplicative functions in interactions with additive patterns of integers.
Of classical, as well as modern, interest is the particular collection of multiplicative functions that arise from the sequence of Fourier coefficients of a normalized Hecke eigencusp form
f
of some weight and level. Here, we study the case of forms with level
1
and without complex multiplication. In the sequel, for such a cusp form
f
of weight
k
and level 1 we write

f(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\tfrac{k-1}{2}}e(nz),\quad\text{Im}(z)>0,

where, as usual
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. In this case the sequence
\{\lambda_{f}(n)\}_{n}
is real, and it is natural to study its
sign changes
, i.e., the set of
n
for which
\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n+1)<0
.

A number of works (see e.g.,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
) have explored the relationship between the distribution of signs and sign changes of Fourier coefficients of cusp forms and the least quadratic non-residue problem modulo primes
p
. Moreover, in the spirit of exploring the mass distribution of Hecke-Maass cusp forms in the weight aspect, Ghosh and Sarnak
[
2
]
exhibited a relationship between the distribution of “real zeros” of such forms and counts for sign changes of their coefficients.

Improving on work of Lau and Wu
[
6
]
, Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
10
]
obtained the optimal result that there are
\gg_{f}X
sign changes for the sequence
\{\lambda_{f}(n)\}_{n\leq X}
.
Shortly thereafter, they managed to reprove this result in a much more general context introduced in the breakthrough work
[
11
]
on general bounded multiplicative functions
g
. This new proof is based on relating short and long partial sums of
\text{sign}(g(n))
(using the convention
\text{sign}(0)=0
), discovering that sufficient cancellation in the long partial sums of
g
imply that in
typical
short intervals of bounded but large length
g
must change sign at least once.

More generally, given a set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{N}
and a map
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, by a
sign change of
g
on
\mathcal{S}
we mean a pair of elements
n<n^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{S}
such that

g(n)g(n^{\prime})<0
, and such that any
n<m<n^{\prime}
belonging to
\mathcal{S}
satisfies
g(m)=0
.
One can naturally ask what, if anything, can be said about the sign changes that arise along
sparse
subsequences of positive integers of arithmetic interest. As an example, in
[
12
, Thm. 5]
, M.R. Murty unconditionally estimates the number of sign changes among the prime values
\{\lambda_{f}(p)\}_{p}
. Using a Hoheisel-type argument, based on zero-density estimates for the corresponding Hecke
L
-function, he obtains cancellation in the short interval prime sums

\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(p)\log p,

for
h<x^{1-\delta}
and some small
\delta=\delta(f)>0
, enabling him to obtain
\gg_{f}x^{\delta}
such sign changes among prime values. We might expect this to be rather far from the truth, and that perhaps there should even be
\gg_{f}\pi(x)
such sign changes
p\leq x
.

In this paper, instead of the sequence of primes we will consider the model set of sums of two squares,

\mathcal{N}:=\{a^{2}+b^{2}:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\},

as a setting in which strong lower bounds on the number of sign changes can be obtained. This set also reveals itself as a natural choice given the relevance of the generating function for the set of perfect squares, the Jacobi theta function, in the theory of modular forms.

In
[
1
]
, the authors consider the problem of counting sign changes in the sequence
\{\lambda_{f}(n)\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
. They showed that there are
\gg x^{1/8-\varepsilon}
such sign changes in
(x,2x]
.
Their proof ultimately relies on comparing the partial sums

\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)r(n)\quad\text{ and }\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}r(n),

where
r(n)
denotes the number of representations of
n
as a sum of two squares, and with
h=h(x)\geq x^{7/8+\varepsilon}
. Using a contour integration argument involving Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions, they derive a contradiction to the assertion that
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq 0
(say) for all
x<n\leq x+h
. The structure of
\mathcal{N}
(in particular the convolution formula
\tfrac{1}{4}r=1\ast\chi_{4}
, where
\chi_{4}
is the non-principal character modulo
4
) seems to play a crucial role.

In view of the result of Matomäki and Radziwiłł mentioned above, even accounting for the sparseness of
\mathcal{N}
it seems that one ought to do better, with the optimal result expected to be
\gg\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}\asymp|\mathcal{N}\cap[1,x]|
sign changes in
[1,x]
.
It should be noted that this cannot be achieved by their method, which relies on
Deligne’s bound
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)
as

\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}r(n)\leq\left(\max_{x<m\leq x+h}d(m)\right)\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)r(n)

to obtain a contradiction to the purported non-negativity of
\lambda_{f}(n)
on
[x,x+h]
. This forces any admissible choice of length
h=h(x)
to satisfy
h\geq\exp\left((1+o(1))\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right)
by invoking pointwise bounds on the divisor function, and the number of sign changes obtained with this argument (as discussed later) is
\gg x/h
.

In this paper, we improve upon the main theorem in
[
1
]
by making extensive use of the techniques in the more recent paper
[
9
]
of Matomäki and Radziwiłł, which among other things are applicable to sparsely-supported multiplicative functions (of which
f(n)1_{\mathcal{N}}(n)
is an example). By additionally incorporating some refinements to these methods from the author’s paper
[
8
]
(which render their results slightly more amenable to the study Fourier coefficients of cusp forms), we in fact obtain the optimal result.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
f
be a primitive Hecke eigencusp form without complex multiplication of weight
k\geq 2
for the full modular group, and let
X
be large.
Then
\mathcal{N}\cap[1,X]
contains
\gg_{f}\frac{X}{\sqrt{\log X}}
sign changes for
\lambda_{f}
.

We will actually prove a more general result that provides the optimal number of sign changes in
\{\lambda_{f}(n)\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{N}_{K}\\
n\leq X\end{subarray}}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{K}
is the sequence of positive integers arising as norms of algebraic integers in a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Following Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
9
, Sec. 1.3]
, we refer to these as
norm forms
. An integer
n
is thus a norm form of
K
if there is an algebraic integer
x
in the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of
K
such that
N_{K}(x)=n
, where
N_{K}
is the norm map on
K
. As the norm on
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
is simply
N_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}(a+ib)=a^{2}+b^{2}
, we have in the above notation
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}(i)}
. Define now

\delta_{K}(X):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\neq N_{K}(\mathfrak{a})\text{ for }\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

It is known (see e.g.,
[
15
]
) that
|\mathcal{N}_{K}\cap[1,X]|\asymp_{K}X\delta_{K}(X)
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field, and let
f
be a primitive Hecke eigencusp form without complex multiplication of weight
k\geq 2
for the full modular group. As
X\rightarrow\infty
, the number of sign changes of
\lambda_{f}
in
\mathcal{N}_{K}\cap[1,X]
is
\gg_{K,f}X\delta_{K}(X)
.

Returning to the setting of sums of two squares, we may modify the problem slightly by asking about sign changes among other patterns of integers, such as shifted sums
a+\mathcal{N}=\{a+m^{2}+n^{2}:m,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, for
a\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
. In this case, it is more challenging to directly apply tools from multiplicative number theory. Nevertheless, using shifted convolution sum estimates among other techniques, we obtain a lower bound on the number of sign changes of
\lambda_{f}
along
a+\mathcal{N}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
a\neq 0
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
and
X
sufficiently large there are
\gg_{\varepsilon}X^{1/2-\varepsilon}
sign changes for
\lambda_{f}
in
(a+\mathcal{N})\cap[1,X]
.

1.1.
Proof Ideas

1.1.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
f
be a non-CM eigencusp form for
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field. Denote by
\sigma_{f}(n)
the sign of
\lambda_{f}(n)
, using the convention
\sigma_{f}(n)=0
whenever
\lambda_{f}(n)=0
.

Let
1\leq h\leq X
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
follows the strategy of
[
11
, Cor. 3]
, the objective of which is to show that for all but
o(X)
points
x\in[X,2X]
, for
X
large, the two averages

\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)(|\sigma_{f}(n)|+\sigma_{f}(n)),\quad\quad\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)(|\sigma_{f}(n)|-\sigma_{f}(n))

are simultaneously
>0
. The positivity of the left-hand sum implies the existence of
x<n_{1}\leq x+h
such that
\sigma_{f}(n_{1})>0
, and similarly that of the right-hand sum implies that
\sigma_{f}(n_{2})<0
for some
x<n_{2}\leq x+h
. This gives rise to a sign change in most short intervals
[x,x+h]
, and by dissecting
[X,2X]
into disjoint such short intervals, yields
\gg X/h
distinct sign changes.

Of course, as
\mathcal{N}_{K}
is a sparse subset of positive integers containing
\asymp\delta_{K}(X)X
integers
n\in[X,2X]
, it is not even guaranteed that the above sums have non-empty support unless
h
is sufficiently large. To avoid this issue we require, in particular, that
h\geq C\delta_{K}(X)^{-1}
, for
C>0
a large constant (depending at most on
K
and
f
).

Depending on the class number of
K
, the argument must be modified. Consider first when
K
has class number 1 (this being in particular the case for
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
). Then (by Dedekind’s ideal factorization theorem) the indicator function
1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}
is multiplicative.
Now, naturally if we had a means of making the comparison

\frac{1}{h}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)(|\sigma_{f}(n)|+\eta\sigma_{f}(n))=\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)(|\sigma_{f}(n)|+\eta\sigma_{f}(n))+o(\delta_{K}(X)),

for
typical

x\in[X,2X]
and each
\eta\in\{-1,+1\}
then our problem becomes substantially easier. The key issue, that of the sparseness of support, is a main obstacle in this comparison. Fortunately, the recent work of Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
9
]
is dedicated to addressing exactly such complications, and may be employed to give such a comparison theorem. Our particular application of their methods is worked out in Section
3
.

The problem is thus reduced to to one involving mean values of multiplicative functions with sparse support. As we show using work of Wirsing
[
24
]
and of Tenenbaum
[
20
]
, respectively, we may deduce that

\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}|\sigma_{f}(n)|1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)\asymp\delta_{K}(X),\quad\quad\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X<n\leq 2X}\sigma_{f}(n)1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)=o(\delta_{K}(X)).

Whereas the former bound is of a more classical nature, the latter relies on an understanding (roughly speaking) of the prime sums

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\in\mathcal{I}_{K}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\text{Re}(\sigma_{f}(p)p^{-it})}{p},\quad|t|\leq\log X,

where
\mathcal{I}_{K}
is a class of ideals of the ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of algebraic integers of
K
.
As a concrete example, when
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
and
\mathcal{N}_{K}
is the sequence of sums of two squares,
\mathcal{I}_{K}
is the set of primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
(the collection of primes where
1_{\mathcal{N}}
is supported). By employing a (generalization of a) hybrid Chebotarev-Sato-Tate type estimate due to R.M. Murty and V.K. Murty
[
12
]
, we are able to condition on both the sign of
\lambda_{f}(p)
as well as the ideal class of
p
in order to prove that these sums tend to
\infty
uniformly in
|t|\leq\log X
as
X\rightarrow\infty
.

When
K
has class number
>1
the problem is rendered more complicated by the fact that the indicator
1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}
is no longer a multiplicative function. Luckily, a result of Odoni (discussed in some detail in
[
9
]
) allows one to express
1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}
as a linear combination of structured multiplicative functions, and (with some work) similar techniques may then be applied to the individual terms of these linear combinations.

1.1.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Fix a non-zero integer
a
. To prove Theorem
1.3
, we make use of more classical arguments about sign changes. Namely, we find a lower bound on the least
h
such that
\lambda_{f}(n)
must change sign in an interval
[x,x+h]
for
typical

x\in[X,2X]
. Dually, we show by way of contradiction that if
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq 0
, say, for all
x<n\leq x+h
then we obtain contradictory upper and lower bounds for the quantity

\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)r(n-a)

for typical
x\in[X,2X]
, provided
h\gg X^{1/2+\varepsilon}
. This results in
\gg X/h\gg X^{1/2-\varepsilon}
distinct sign changes. Here, as above,
r(n)
denotes the number of representations of
n
as a sum of two squares.

The upper bound we need is furnished by estimates for shifted convolution sums arising from the spectral theory of automorphic forms. In this context, one obtains the square-root cancelling bound
O_{f}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})
using the work of Ravindran
[
16
]
.

The lower bound requires more work, and principally involves restricting the sum to those
n
for which
|\lambda_{f}(n)|>X^{-\delta}
. Assuming
\lambda_{f}(n)\geq 0
for all
x<n\leq x+h
, it follows that then

(1)

\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)r(n-a)\geq X^{-\delta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\lambda_{f}(n)>X^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}r(n-a)=X^{-\delta}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\lambda_{f}(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}r(n-a)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
0<\lambda_{f}(n)\leq X^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}r(n-a)\right).

Since, as Serre
[
17
]
showed, the set of primes
p
such that
\lambda_{f}(p)=0
is quite sparse, the conditon
\lambda_{f}(n)\neq 0
is easily dealt with using sieve theoretical arguments, and the first expression in brackets in (
1
) is shown to be of size
\gg h
for typical
x\in[X,2X]
.

On the other hand, the support of the second sum in (
1
) is shown to be sparse for typical
x
. Indeed, by exploiting the multiplicativity of
\lambda_{f}
together with some Diophantine information about coefficients of cusp forms, we show that any
n
for which
0<|\lambda_{f}(n)|<X^{-\delta}
has a prime power divisor
p^{\nu}>(\log X)^{c_{1}}
for which
|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log X)^{-c_{2}}
, for
c_{1},c_{2}>0
constants depending at most on
\delta
and
f
. Using a recent version of the Sato-Tate theorem with a quantitative error term due to Thorner
[
21
]
, we show that the set of such multiples
n
is a sparse set, and therefore typical length
h
short intervals have few such multiples. This is essentially enough to conclude that the second sum in brackets in (
1
) is
o(h)
for most
x\in[X,2X]
, giving rise to the conflicting bounds

hX^{-\delta}\ll_{f}\sum_{x<n\leq x+h}\lambda_{f}(n)r(n-a)\ll_{f}X^{1/2+\varepsilon},

(taking
\delta=\varepsilon
small) whenever
h\gg_{f}X^{1/2+3\varepsilon}
.

2.
Background Results

2.1.
Background on norm forms

Fix
K/\mathbb{Q}
a number field, and let
\mathcal{N}_{K}
be the sequence of norm forms of
K
. In general, the indicator function
g_{K}(n):=1_{\mathcal{N}_{K}}(n)
of
\mathcal{N}_{K}
is not a multiplicative function, and thus an analysis of sign changes of
\lambda_{f}(n)g_{K}(n)
purely on the basis of multiplicative techniques seems a priori difficult. However, Odoni
[
15
]
showed that
g_{K}
can be written as a linear combination of certain multiplicative functions that have fairly predictable values, and this will be sufficient for the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The material required to these ends is drawn essentially from
[
9
, Sec. 13]
, which leverages Odoni’s ideas. We describe the salient points in brief detail here.

Let
\overline{K}
denote the normal closure of
K
, and let
\mathcal{H}(\overline{K})
denote the narrow class field of
\overline{K}
. Thus,
G_{K}:=\text{Gal}(\mathcal{H}(\overline{K})/\overline{K})
is canonically isomorphic to the narrow class group
H(\overline{K})
of
\overline{K}
, via the map
\mathcal{C}\in H(\overline{K})\mapsto\sigma_{\mathcal{C}}
, the Frobenius conjugacy class of the ideal class
\mathcal{C}
.

Consider first the case of prime norm forms. Since the set of rational primes that ramify in
K
is finite (they all must divide the discriminant
\text{disc}(K/\mathbb{Q})
) we will be able to ignore them in the sequel, and therefore focus mainly on the unramified primes. Any unramified rational prime
p
factors as

p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r},

where, as
N_{K}(p)=p^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}
, we have
N_{K}\mathfrak{p}_{l}=p^{m}
for some
1\leq m\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. Each of the prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{l}
lying above
p
belongs to some class in the narrow class group
H(\overline{K})
. Let
\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{h(K)}\}
be an enumeration of these classes,
h(K)=|H(\overline{K})|
denoting the narrow class number of
\overline{K}
. For each
1\leq i\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]
,
1\leq j\leq h(K)
and rational prime
p
let us write

b_{i,j}(p):=|\{\mathfrak{p}|p:N_{K}(\mathfrak{p})=p^{i},\mathfrak{p}\in C_{j}\}|,\quad B(p):=\{b_{i,j}(p)\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]\\
1\leq j\leq h(K)\end{subarray}}.

Following Odoni, the matrix
B(p)
is called the
pattern of

p
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B(p)\}_{p}
be the (finite) collection of all pattern matrices that occur.

It will be profitable to have access to asymptotic formulae for the number of rational primes with a given pattern, which can be achieved by an application of the Chebotarev density theorem. Indeed, Odoni
[
15
, Thm. 4.1]
showed that for a given
B\in\mathcal{B}
there is a collection
\mathcal{C}_{B}
of conjugacy classes of
G_{K}
such that

p\text{ is unramified and }B(p)=B\text{ if, and only if, the Frobenius class }\sigma_{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{B}.

By the Chebotarev density theorem (see
[
22
]
for an unconditional result that is state-of-the-art), it thus follows that for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
there is a positive constant
c(B):=\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{B}|}{h(K)}>0
such that as
X\rightarrow\infty
(keeping
K
fixed),

(2)

|\{p\leq X:\text{ unramified, }B(p)=B\}|=c(B)\int_{2}^{X}\frac{dt}{\log t}+O_{K}\left(Xe^{-c_{K}\sqrt{\log X}}\right),

for some constant
c_{K}>0
depending only on
K
.

We highlight the following consequence of this. Define

\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\text{ with }N_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=n\}.

Since
N_{K}(\alpha)=N_{K}(\alpha\mathcal{O}_{K})
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
we have
\mathcal{N}_{K}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}
, though in general these sets differ. By Dedekind’s theorem on factorization of integral ideals,
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}
is a multiplicative set, i.e.,
m,n\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}
iff
mn\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}
.

As discussed by Odoni
[
15
, p. 71]
, there is a collection
\mathfrak{C}
of ideal classes such that an unramified prime
p\in\mathcal{N}_{K}
if, and only if,
R(p)\cap\mathfrak{C}\neq\emptyset
, where
R(p)
is the collection of integral ideals
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
N_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=p
. By necessity,
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}
must then be a prime ideal, lying in some ideal class
C_{j}
containing
\mathfrak{p}
, and since
N_{K}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathfrak{p}
we must have
\mathfrak{p}|p
. Thus, there is
1\leq j\leq h(K)
such that
\mathcal{C}_{j}\in\mathfrak{C}
and
b_{1,j}(p)>0
. In other terms, among unramified primes,

p\in\mathcal{N}_{K}\text{ if, and only if, }\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq h(K)\\
\mathcal{C}_{j}\in\mathfrak{C}\end{subarray}}b_{1,j}(p)>0.

Define
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
the subcollections of patterns
B=\{b_{i,j}\}
such that

\displaystyle B\in\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }\sum_{j:\mathcal{C}_{j}\in\mathfrak{C}}b_{1,j}>0,

\displaystyle B\in\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }\sum_{j}b_{1,j}>0.

Combined with the Chebotarev density theorem, these remarks imply the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta_{K}:=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{U}}c(B)
and
\tau_{K}:=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{V}}c(B)
. Then

(3)

\displaystyle|\{p\leq X:p\in\mathcal{N}_{K}\}|=\beta_{K}\int_{2}^{X}\frac{dt}{\log t}+O_{K}\left(Xe^{-c_{K}\sqrt{\log X}}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle|\{p\leq X:p\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}\}|=\tau_{K}\int_{2}^{X}\frac{dt}{\log t}+O_{K}\left(Xe^{-c_{K}\sqrt{\log X}}\right).

With the above preliminaries in hand, we can now proceed towards Odoni’s decomposition theorem, as presented in
[
9
, Sec. 13]
. In the sequel, define the multiplicative function
\Delta_{K}
at prime powers
p^{\nu}
via

\Delta_{K}(p^{\nu}):=\begin{cases}1\text{ if $p\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}$}\\
0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}\supseteq\mathcal{N}_{K}
we have
\Delta_{K}(n)\geq g_{K}(n)
for all integers

.

Proposition 2.2

(Matomäki-Radziwiłł, Lemma 13.3 of
[
9
]
; Odoni
[
15
]
)
.

There are positive real constants
\alpha=\alpha(K)
,
\rho=\rho(K)
, non-negative integers
M=M(K)
,
R=R(K)
with
R>M
, an integer
D=D(K)\geq 1
and complex numbers
c_{i}=c_{i}(K)\in\mathbb{C}
for
0\leq i\leq R
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

g_{K}(n)=\sum_{0\leq l\leq M}c_{i}f_{i}(n)+\sum_{M+1\leq l\leq R}c_{i}f_{i}(n),

where the functions
f_{i}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are multiplicative with
f_{i}(p)^{D}=\Delta_{K}(p)
for all
p
and
0\leq i\leq R
, and satisfy the following properties for sufficiently large
X
:

(i)

For each
0\leq l\leq R
and
2\leq w\leq z\leq X
,

\sum_{w\leq p\leq z}\frac{|f_{i}(p)|}{p}=\tau_{K}\sum_{w\leq p\leq z}\frac{1}{p}+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{\log w}\right);

(ii)

If
p\nmid\text{disc}(K/\mathbb{Q})
then
f_{l}(p)=\Delta_{K}(p)
for all
0\leq l\leq M
;

(iii)

\sum_{0\leq l\leq M}c_{l}>0
; and

(iv)

For each
M+1\leq l\leq R

\min_{|t|\leq 2X}\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\Delta_{K}(p)-\text{Re}(f_{l}(p)p^{-it})}{p}\geq\rho\log\log X.

The utility of (iv) will become evident in Section
2.3
, where we will appeal to some notions from pretentious number theory.

Proof.

Claims (ii),(iii) and (iv) are explicitly stated in
[
9
, Lem 13.3]
. Claim (i) follows immediately from the claim
f_{l}(p)^{D}=\Delta_{K}(p)
,
which implies that
|f_{l}(p)|=\Delta_{K}(p)\in\{0,1\}
for all
l
and
p
. Thus, (i) follows from Lemma
2.1
and partial summation.

Otherwise, the only statement mentioned here that does not explicitly appear in
[
9
, Lem. 13.3]
is the existence of
D
. However, the functions
f_{i}
are constructed in such a way that for each
B\in\mathcal{B}
there is a root of unity
\zeta_{i,B}\in\mu_{d_{B}}
of some order
d_{B}\geq 1
such that whenever
B(p)=B
we have
f_{i}(p)=\zeta_{i,B}

[
9
, p. 77]
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is a finite set, this constitutes a finite set of roots of unity, and thus taking

, we obtain the required number

. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Chebotarev-Sato-Tate for modular forms of weight

Let

Proof.

The argument is based on

, but is simplified since we work with Abelian

be the set of characters of

2.3.
Results about Pretentious Number Theory

In the sequel, our arguments dealing with multiplicative functions will, implicitly and explicitly, use notions from pretentious number theory. A key role in that theory is played by the pretentious distance functions. Write
\mathbb{U}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
. Given
y\geq 2
and sequences
a:=\{a(p)\}_{p},b:=\{b(p)\}_{p}\subset\mathbb{U}
we define the pretentious distance between
a
and
b
up to
y
to be

\mathbb{D}(a,b;y):=\left(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{1-\text{Re}(a(p)\overline{b}(p))}{p}\right)^{1/2}.

This distance satisfies the
pretentious triangle inequality
, i.e., if
c=\{c(p)\}_{p}
is any such third sequence then

\mathbb{D}(a,c;y)\leq\mathbb{D}(a,b;y)+\mathbb{D}(b,c;y)

(see e.g.
[
3
, Lem. 3.1]
). Since
\eta(u,v):=(1-\text{Re}(u\overline{w}))^{1/2}
, well-defined for
u,v\in\mathbb{U}
, satisfies
\eta(u,v)=\eta(u\overline{v},1)=\eta(1,\overline{u}v)
, one can consequently show the useful inequality

\mathbb{D}(a_{1}a_{2},b_{1}b_{2};y)\leq\mathbb{D}(a_{1},b_{1};y)+\mathbb{D}(a_{2},b_{2};y),

using the notation
(ab)(p):=a(p)b(p)
for all
p
for prime-indexed sequences
a
and
b
. Among other things, iterating this leads to the result that

(5)

\mathbb{D}(a^{d},b^{d};y)\leq d\mathbb{D}(a,b;y)\text{ for any }d\in\mathbb{N}.

Perhaps less well-known is the following general, weighted variant of the pretentious triangle inequality, also due to Granville and Soundararajan
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{r(p)\}_{p}\subset[0,\infty)
, and let
a=\{a(p)\}_{p},b=\{b(p)\}_{p}
and
c=\{c(p)\}_{p}
be as above. Then for any
y\geq 2
,

\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)(1-\text{Re}(a(p)\overline{c}(p)))\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)(1-\text{Re}(a(p)\overline{b}(p)))\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)(1-\text{Re}(b(p)\overline{c}(p)))\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

This is alluded to below the proof of Lem 3.1 in
[
3
]
. Since the proof is not given, we give the details here, elaborating slightly on the arguments in
[
3
]
for the sake of clarity.

Observe first of all that if
z\in\mathbb{U}
then
|z|\leq 1
and thus

\text{Im}(z)^{2}\leq 1-\text{Re}(z)^{2}=(1+\text{Re}(z))(1-\text{Re}(z))\leq 2(1-\text{Re}(z)).

It follows, therefore, that for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{U}
,
2\eta(1,z_{1})\eta(1,z_{2})\geq|\text{Im}(z_{1})||\text{Im}(z_{2})|
. Next, note the property

\displaystyle 0\leq(1-\text{Re}(z_{1}))(1-\text{Re}(z_{2}))=1-\text{Re}(z_{1})-\text{Re}(z_{2})+\text{Re}(z_{1})\text{Re}(z_{2}),

whence we may deduce that

\displaystyle\text{Re}(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})=\text{Re}(z_{1})\text{Re}(z_{2})+\text{Im}(z_{1})\text{Im}(z_{2})\geq\text{Re}(z_{1})+\text{Re}(z_{2})-1+\text{Im}(z_{1})\text{Im}(z_{2}).

We therefore obtain that

\displaystyle\eta(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\text{Re}(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})\leq 2-\text{Re}(z_{1})-\text{Re}(z_{2})-\text{Im}(z_{1})\text{Im}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\eta(1,z_{1})^{2}+\eta(1,z_{2})^{2}+|\text{Im}(z_{1})||\text{Im}(z_{2})|\leq\eta(1,z_{1})^{2}+\eta(1,z_{2})^{2}+2\eta(1,z_{1})\eta(1,z_{2})

\displaystyle=(\eta(1,z_{1})+\eta(1,z_{2}))^{2}.

Specializing to the case
z_{1}=u\overline{v}
and
z_{2}=v\overline{w}
, we obtain the preliminary inequality

(6)

\eta(u,w)\leq\eta(u,v)+\eta(v,w),

for any
u,v,w\in\mathbb{U}
.

Next, note that by Cauchy-Schwarz we have

\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)\eta(a(p),b(p))\eta(b(p),c(p))\leq\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)\eta(a(p),b(p))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)\eta(b(p),c(p))^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and from this and (
6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)\eta(a(p),b(p))^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)\eta(b(p),c(p))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)(\eta(a(p),b(p))+\eta(b(p),c(p)))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p\leq y}r(p)\eta(a(p),c(p))^{2}.

This implies the claim.
∎

We specialize the above lemma as follows. Given
y\geq 2
, a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
and multiplicative functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, let us write

\mathbb{D}_{K}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2};y):=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{K}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\text{Re}(\phi_{1}(p)\overline{\phi_{2}}(p))}{p}\right)^{1/2},

i.e., we set
r(p):=\Delta_{K}(p)/p
in the notation of the previous lemma. Trivially,
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\cdot,\cdot;y)=\mathbb{D}(\cdot,\cdot;y)
for all
y\geq 2
. Note in particular that item (iv) of Proposition
2.2
is precisely the statement that
\min_{|t|\leq 2X}\mathbb{D}_{K}(f_{l},n^{it};X)^{2}\geq\rho\log\log X
for all
M+1\leq l\leq R
. The above lemma allows us to conclude, analogously to (
5
), that for any
d\geq 1
,

(7)

\mathbb{D}_{K}(f^{d},g^{d};X)\leq d\mathbb{D}_{K}(f,g;X),

for
f,g
multiplicative functions taking values in
\mathbb{U}
.

In the sequel, we take the convention that
\text{sign}(0):=0
, and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the multiplicative function
\sigma_{f}(n):=\text{sign}(\lambda_{f}(n))
, which takes values in
\{-1,0,1\}
. By Lemma
2.4
we know that
\sigma_{f}(p)\neq 0
for all but a zero density set of primes, and in fact by the Sato-Tate theorem we have

|\{p\leq X:\sigma_{f}(p)=+1\}|\sim|\{p\leq X:\sigma_{f}(p)=-1\}|\sim\frac{1}{2}\pi(X).

At several junctures of our argument we will require precise information about, in particular quantitative lower bounds for,
\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f},n^{it};X)
, where
|t|\leq 2X
, among other such distances. In this direction, we prove the following.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0\leq l\leq R
, and let
f_{l}
be one of the functions mentioned in Proposition
2.2
. Then there is a
\sigma=\sigma(K)>0
such that whenever
|t|\leq 2X
we have

\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f}f_{l},n^{it};X)^{2}\geq\sigma\min\{\log\log X,\log(1+|t|\log X)\}-O_{K,f}(1).

Moreover, as
X\rightarrow\infty
we have

\min_{|t|\leq 2X}\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f}f_{l},n^{it};X)^{2}\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

We begin with the first claim.
Recall from Proposition
2.2
that there is a
D=D(K)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{l}^{D}=\Delta_{K}
, and thus
(f_{l}(p)\sigma_{f}(p))^{2D}=1
for all
p
such that
\Delta_{K}(p)\lambda_{f}(p)\neq 0
. It follows from Lemma
2.5
and (
7

\displaystyle(2D)\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f}f_{l},n^{it};X)\geq\mathbb{D}_{K}((\sigma_{f}f_{l})^{2D},n^{2iDt};X)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
\Delta_{K}(p)\lambda_{f}(p)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\text{Re}(p^{-2Dit})}{p}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{D}_{K}(1,n^{2iDt};X)-O_{K,f}(1).

If
D|t|\leq 10/\log X
then the claim is trivial, so we may assume otherwise that
|t|>10/\log X
.
Now, for
\eta\in(0,1/3)
set
N_{X,\eta}:=\exp((\log X)^{2/3+\eta})
and let
Y:=\max\{N_{X,\eta},e^{1/|Dt|}\}\leq X
. By
[
9
, Lem. 13.3(ii)]
we have

\mathbb{D}_{K}(1,n^{2iDt};X)^{2}\geq\sum_{B\in\mathcal{V}}\text{Re}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<p\leq X\\
B(p)=B\end{subarray}}\frac{1-p^{2iDt}}{p}\right)+O_{K}(1)=\tau_{K}\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{1-\text{Re}(p^{2iDt})}{p}+O_{K}(1).

We now consider two cases. If
1\leq D|t|\leq 2X
then it follows from standard arguments employing the Vinogradov-Korobov zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function that the latter prime sum is

\tau_{K}(\tfrac{1}{3}-\eta)\log\log X-O_{K}(1).

Thus, for
1\leq|t|\leq 2X
we have

\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f}f_{l},n^{it};X)^{2}\geq\frac{(1-3\eta)\tau_{K}}{12D^{2}}\log\log X-O_{K}(1).

On the other hand, if
10/\log X<D|t|\leq 1
then by partial summation and the prime number theorem we have

\displaystyle\sum_{Y<p\leq X}\frac{1-\text{Re}(p^{2iDt})}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}(1-\cos\alpha)d\alpha\right)\cdot\log\left(\frac{\log(2D|t|\log X)}{\max\{1,D|t|\log N_{X,\eta}\}}\right)-O_{K}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{3}-\eta\right)\min\{\log\log X,\log(1+|t|\log X)\}-O_{K}(1).

This completes the proof of the first claim with any
0<\sigma<\tau_{K}/(12D^{2})
(choosing
\eta
appropriately small).

For the second, let
Z=Z(X)\geq 10
be a parameter to be chosen later and observe that if
|t|\geq Z/\log X
then by the first claim we obtain

\min_{Z/\log X\leq|t|\leq 2X}\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f}f_{l},n^{it};X)^{2}\geq\sigma\log Z.

Next, suppose
|t|\leq Z/\log X
.
As mentioned in the proof of Proposition
2.2
, Odoni showed that
f_{l}
is constant on the set of unramified primes

with a fixed pattern

B

B\in\mathcal{V}
. We know that
f_{j}(p)=\zeta_{B}
for some root of unity (possibly equal to 1) for all
p

B(p)=B
.
As discussed in Section
2.1
, there is a collection of conjugacy classes
\mathcal{C}_{B}
of

for which the Frobenius
\sigma_{p}
of

belongs to some class

.
Applying (
2

|t|\leq Z/\log X

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{K}(\sigma_{f}f_{j},n^{it};X)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e^{\sqrt{\log X}}<p\leq X\\
B(p)=B\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\sigma_{f}(p)\text{Re}(\zeta_{B}p^{-it})}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{c(B)}{2}\log\log X+O_{K}(1)-\text{Re}\left(\zeta_{B}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{B}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e^{\sqrt{\log X}}<p\leq X\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(p)p^{-it}}{p}\right),

where the contribution from ramified primes is absorbed in the

term.
Applying partial summation, the estimate
|t|\leq Z/\log X
and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain that there is a function
\varepsilon(t)\rightarrow 0
as

such that for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{B}

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e^{\sqrt{\log X}}<p\leq X\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(p)p^{-it}}{p}\right|\ll(1+|t|)\int_{e^{\sqrt{\log X}}}^{X}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y\\
\sigma_{p}\in C\end{subarray}}\sigma_{f}(p)\right|\frac{du}{u^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log X}}

\displaystyle\leq(Z\log\log X)\max_{e^{\sqrt{\log X}}\leq Y\leq X}\frac{1}{\pi(Y)}\left||\{p\leq Y:\sigma_{p}\in C,\sigma_{f}(p)\in[0,1]\}|-|\{p\leq Y:\sigma_{p}\in C,\sigma_{f}(p)\in[-1,0]\}|\right|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log X}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\min_{e^{\sqrt{\log X}}\leq Y\leq X}\varepsilon(Y)\right)Z\log\log X.

If we therefore define
Z=Z(X)
via

Z(X)^{2}:=\left(\min_{e^{\sqrt{\log X}}\leq Y\leq X}\varepsilon(Y)\right)^{-1},

we obtain that, uniformly over
|t|\leq 2X

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\Delta_{K}(p)-\sigma_{f}(p)\text{Re}(f_{j}(p)p^{-it})}{p}\geq\min\{\sigma\log Z,\left(\frac{1}{2}\max_{B\in\mathcal{V}}c(B)-O(\tfrac{1}{Z})\right)\log\log X\}-O_{K,f}(1),

and as
Z(X)\rightarrow\infty
with

∎

2.4.
Background on multiplicative functions

In the proof of our main theorems, we will need to estimate, both asymptotically and/or with non-trivial upper bounds, the averages of sparsely-supported multiplicative functions. The following two lemmas will be key in this endeavour.
The first of these results, due to Wirsing, allows us to asymptotically estimate partial sums of non-negative multiplicative functions that are slowly growing and suitably regular on the primes; it applies immediately to any such function that is everywhere bounded by 1.

Lemma 2.8

(Wirsing
[
24
]
)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a multiplicative function for which there are constants
A,B>0
such that

\sup_{p}\phi(p)\leq B,\quad\quad\sum_{p^{\nu},\nu\geq 2}\frac{\phi(p^{\nu})\log p^{\nu}}{p^{\nu}}\leq A.

Assume furthermore that there is
\tau>0
, such that

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\phi(p)\log p}{p}=(\tau+o(1))\log X.

Then, as
X\rightarrow\infty
,

\frac{1}{X}\sum_{n\leq X}\phi(n)=\left(\frac{e^{-\gamma\tau}}{\Gamma(\tau)}+o(1)\right)\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\phi(p^{k})}{p^{k}}.

We would also like a result complementing Wirsing’s theorem that allows us to say that if
g
is a multiplicative function such that
|g(n)|
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
2.8
then, provided
g
oscillates sufficiently, we have
\sum_{n\leq X}g(n)=o(\sum_{n\leq X}|g(n)|)
. The following strong result of this kind is due to Tenenbaum.

Lemma 2.9

(Tenenbaum, Cor. 2.1 of
[
20
]
)
.

Let
T\geq 1
. Let
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a multiplicative function such that

\sup_{p}|g(p)|\leq A,\quad\sum_{p^{\nu},\nu\geq 2}\frac{|g(p^{\nu})|(\log p^{\nu})^{2}}{p^{\nu}}\leq B.

Assume furthermore that there is a constant
\beta>0
such that for any
2\leq y\leq x
we have

\sum_{y\leq p\leq x}\frac{|g(p)|}{p}\geq\beta\log\left(\frac{\log x}{\log y}\right)+O(1).

Then as
X\rightarrow\infty
,

choices of
x\in[X,2X]

\lambda_{f}
changes sign on
(a+\mathcal{N})\cap[x,x+h]
. By the greedy selection argument mentioned at the beginning of Section
3
, it follows that there are
\gg X/h\gg_{\varepsilon,f}X^{1/2-3\varepsilon}
distinct such
n
, all of which produce a sign change, and the theorem is proved.
∎

4.1.
Proof of Proposition
4.1

In order to prove Proposition
4.1
we will deduce from the condition
0<|\lambda_{f}(n)|<X^{-\delta}
that
n
is divisible by a
large
prime power
p^{k}
with a
small
Fourier coefficient
|\lambda_{f}(p^{k})|
. The following lemma plays an important role in showing that the number of multiples of all such prime powers is quite small.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
c\in(0,1/4)
be fixed and let
2\leq Z\leq X
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\leq p^{\nu}\leq X\\
0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|\leq(\log X)^{-c}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll_{f}\frac{\log\log X}{(\log X)^{c}}+\frac{1}{(\log(Z+\log X))^{1/5}}.

Similarly, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\leq p^{\nu}\leq X\\
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll_{f}\frac{1}{(\log Z)^{1/5}}.

Proof.

By Thorner’s quantitative version of the Sato-Tate theorem
[
21
, Thm. 1.1]
, for any
\Delta>0
we have

|\{p^{\nu}\leq X:|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<\Delta\}|=\pi(X)\left(\mu_{ST}([-\Delta,\Delta])+O\left(\frac{\log(k\log X)}{\sqrt{\log X}}\right)\right).

By Lemma
2.4
, we also have

|\{p^{\nu}\leq X:\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0\}|\ll_{f}\frac{1}{(\log X)^{1/4-\varepsilon}}.

Applying these two results with
\Delta=(\log X)^{-c}
,
0<c<1/4
, then using partial summation, for any
2\leq Y\leq X
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y<p^{\nu}\leq X\\
0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log X)^{-c}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{ST}([-\Delta,\Delta])\log\left(\log X/\log Y\right)+O\left(\int_{Y}^{X}\frac{du}{u(\log u)^{6/5}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{ST}([-\Delta,\Delta])\log\left(\log X/\log Y\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log Y)^{1/5}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\log\log X}{(\log X)^{c}}+\frac{1}{(\log Y)^{1/5}}.

Now, it is known that there is a constant
C_{f}>0
such that if
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})\neq 0
then
|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|\geq p^{-C_{f}\nu}
. Indeed, as argued in
[
7
]
, writing
a_{f}(n):=\lambda_{f}(n)n^{(k-1)/2}
for all
n
we have that
a_{f}(n)
is an algebraic integer in some number field
K_{f}
of (finite) degree
d_{f}\geq 1
, and so for each
n=p^{\nu}
with
a_{f}(n)\neq 0
its norm
N_{K_{f}}(a_{f}(p^{\nu}))\geq 1
. On the other hand, for each automorphism
\sigma
of
K_{f}
it is well-known that
\{a_{f}(n)^{\sigma}\}_{n}
is the sequence of coefficients of an eigencusp form of weight
k
and level
1
, and we obtain

1\leq N_{K_{f}}(a_{f}(p^{\nu}))=|a_{f}(p^{\nu})|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\text{Aut}(K_{f})\\
\sigma\neq 1\end{subarray}}|a_{f}(p^{\nu})^{\sigma}|\leq|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|d(p^{\nu})^{d_{f}-1}p^{\nu d_{f}(k-1)/2},

using Deligne’s bound
|a_{f}(p^{\nu})^{\sigma}|\leq d(p^{\nu})p^{\nu(k-1)/2}
for each
\sigma\neq 1
in the last inequality. From this, the claim
|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|\geq p^{-C_{f}\nu}
follows with any
C_{f}>d_{f}(k-1)/2
.

Therefore, if
0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log X)^{-c}
then
p^{\nu}>(\log X)^{\eta}
with
\eta:=c/C_{f}
, and so we immediately have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z<p^{\nu}\leq X\\
0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log X)^{-c}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max\{(\log X)^{\eta},Z\}<p^{\nu}\leq X\\
0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log X)^{-c}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll\frac{1}{(\log(Z+(\log X)^{\eta}))^{1/5}},

and the first claim follows.

The second claim is proven more directly by appealing to Lemma
2.4
and partial summation.
∎

To leverage the bound in Lemma
4.3
we require the following upper bound for correlations of
r(n)
supported on multiples of prime powers
p^{k}
, provided they are not too close to
h
in size.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X^{\varepsilon}\leq h\leq X
. Then for any
p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{h}{p^{\nu}}.

Proof.

Write
r^{\prime}(n)=r(n)/4
, which is multiplicative and divisor-bounded. By Shiu’s theorem
[
18
]
, the sum in question is bounded above by

4\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x+a<m\leq x+h+a\\
m\equiv a\pmod{p^{\nu}}\end{subarray}}r^{\prime}(m)\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{h}{p^{\nu}}\prod_{p^{\prime}\leq X}\left(1+\frac{r^{\prime}(p^{\prime})-1}{p^{\prime}}\right)\ll h/p^{\nu},

as claimed.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Suppose
n\leq X
satisfies
0<|\lambda_{f}(n)|<X^{-\delta}
.
Writing out the prime factorization of
n
and using multiplicativity, we observe that

X^{-\delta}>\prod_{p^{k}||n}|\lambda_{f}(p^{k})|\geq\left(\min_{p^{k}||n}|\lambda_{f}(p^{k})|\right)^{\omega(n)}>0,

where
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
n
. Since
\omega(n)\leq C\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}
for some
C>0
absolute, we deduce that if
n
satisfies
0<|\lambda_{f}(n)|<X^{-\delta}
then there is a prime power
p^{k}||n
for which

0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{k})|<\exp\left(-\delta\frac{\log X}{\omega(n)}\right)\leq(\log X)^{-\delta/C}.

Set
c:=\min\{\delta/C,1/5\}
, and call

\mathcal{P}_{c}:=\{p^{\nu}\leq 2X:0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log X)^{-c}\}.

Then it suffices to bound, for all but
O(X/(\log X)^{1/10})
choices of
x\in[X,2X]
, the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\exists p^{\nu}||n,p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}\end{subarray}}r(n+a).

We split the set
\mathcal{P}_{c}
into three ranges:

i)
p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}
, ii)
h^{1-\varepsilon}<p^{\nu}\leq X^{1-\varepsilon}
and iii)
X^{1-\varepsilon}<p^{\nu}\leq 2X
.

We immediately note that since
h<X^{1-\varepsilon}
there is at most one multiple of any prime power
p^{\nu}
from range iii) in any interval
(x,x+h]
, and since (by arguing as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
) there are
\ll X/(\log X)^{1+c^{\prime}}
such prime powers in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
, for some
c^{\prime}>0
depending on
c
, we may ignore the range
p^{\nu}>X^{1-\varepsilon}
by excluding at most
O(X/(\log X)^{1+c^{\prime}})
intervals
[x,x+h]
.

Next, consider range i). Combining Lemmas
4.4
and
4.3
we obtain that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\exists p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}\cap[2,h^{1-\varepsilon}]\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll_{\varepsilon}h\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}\\
0<|\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})|<(\log x)^{-c}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll_{\delta,f}\frac{h}{(\log\log X)^{1/5}}.

Note that this holds for every
x\in[X,2X]
.

Finally, consider range ii). Observe that by the same argument as in Lemma
4.4
(splitting into dyadic intervals
[X,2X]
and
[2X,4X]
and taking
h(y)=y
for
y\in\{X,2X\}
),

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 4X\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll\frac{X}{p^{\nu}}.

Thus, on combining this with Lemma
4.3
we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 4X\\
\exists p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}\cap(h^{1-\varepsilon},X^{1-\varepsilon}]\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{X}{(\log\log X)^{1/5}},

in light of the condition
h\geq X^{\varepsilon}
.
By positivity, we deduce from this that

\sum_{X<m\leq 2X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<n\leq m+h\\
\exists p^{\nu}\in(h^{1-\varepsilon},X^{1-\varepsilon}]\cap\mathcal{P}_{c}\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\leq h\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<n\leq 2X+h\\
\exists p^{\nu}\in(h^{1-\varepsilon},X^{1-\varepsilon}]\cap\mathcal{P}_{c}\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll\frac{hX}{(\log\log X)^{1/5}}.

By Markov’s inequality, we thus obtain that for all but
O(X/(\log\log X)^{1/10})
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<n\leq m+h\\
\exists p^{\nu}\in(h^{1-\varepsilon},X^{1-\varepsilon}]\cap\mathcal{P}_{c}\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll\frac{h}{(\log\log X)^{1/10}}.

Combined with the bound for the primes
p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}
and
p^{\nu}>X^{1-\varepsilon}
, the claim follows.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
4.2

To prove Proposition
4.2
we sieve out short intervals
[x,x+h]
by the sparse set of prime powers
p^{\nu}
satisfying
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0
. To accomplish this we make use of an estimate for
r(n)
in arithmetic progressions
n\leq y
,
n\equiv a\pmod{q}
with
q\leq y^{1/10}
, say. Asymptotic formulae of this kind, with power saving error term for the fairly wide range
q\leq y^{2/3-\varepsilon}
, were given by R.A. Smith
[
19
, Thm. 9]
. The following improvement of these results is due to Tolev
[
23
]
.

Lemma 4.5

(Tolev
[
23
]
)
.

Let
y
be large,
q\leq y^{2/3}
be squarefree and let
a
be a residue class modulo
q
with
(a,q)=1
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}r(n)=\pi\prod_{p|q}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p}\right)\frac{y}{q}+O\left(y^{\varepsilon}\left(q^{1/2}+y^{1/3}\right)\right),

where
\chi_{4}
is the non-principal character mod
4
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Let
z:=(\log X)^{1/2}
and let

P=P(z):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq p\leq z\\
\exists\nu\geq 1\text{ with }\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0\end{subarray}}p.

By the prime number theorem, we have
P\leq\exp(2z)\ll_{\varepsilon}X^{\varepsilon}
.

We may lower bound the sum in question by

(12)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
2\nmid n\\
\lambda_{f}(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
2\nmid n\\
(n,P(z))=1\end{subarray}}r(n+a)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\exists p>z,k\geq 1\\
\lambda_{f}(p^{k})=0\\
p^{k}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a).

To estimate the first sum we use Möbius inversion together with Lemma
4.5
, getting

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
2\nmid n\\
(n,P(z))=1\end{subarray}}r(n+a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|P\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
2\nmid n\\
d|n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e|(a,P)}\mu(e)r^{\prime}(e)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{\prime}|P/e\\
(d^{\prime},a/e)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(d^{\prime})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/e<m\leq(x+h)/e\\
m\equiv 1\pmod{2}\\
m\equiv 0\pmod{d^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}r(m+a/e)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi h}{2}\sum_{e|(a,P)}\frac{\mu(e)r^{\prime}(e)}{e}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d^{\prime}|P/e\\
(d^{\prime},a/e)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d^{\prime})}{d^{\prime}}\prod_{p|d^{\prime}}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p}\right)+O\left(X^{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{d|P}d^{\tfrac{1}{2}}+X^{\tfrac{1}{3}+\varepsilon}\tau(P)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi h}{2}\prod_{p|(a,P)}\left(1-\frac{r^{\prime}(p)}{p-1+\chi_{4}(p)/p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq p\leq z\\
\lambda_{f}(p)=0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1+\chi_{4}(p)}{p}+\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p^{2}}\right)+O\left(X^{1/3+\varepsilon}\right).

Note that as
P
is odd,
2\nmid(a,P)
and thus the products above are
>0
. Furthermore, since
\sum_{p:\lambda_{f}(p)=0}\frac{1}{p}<\infty
, for
h>X^{1/3+2\varepsilon}
the product over
3\leq p\leq z
with
\lambda_{f}(p)=0
converges, and thus we deduce the existence of a constant
c_{f,a}>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
2\nmid n\\
(n,P(z))=1\end{subarray}}r(n+a)=(c_{f,a}+o(1))h.

Next, we upper bound the second sum in (
12
) similarly as in Proposition
4.1
. For
p^{k}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}
we use Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
to get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\exists p>z,\nu\geq 1\\
p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}\\
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll h\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<p^{\nu}\leq h^{1-\varepsilon}\\
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll_{f}\frac{h}{(\log z)^{1/5}}\ll\frac{h}{(\log\log X)^{1/5}},

and, if
x\in[X,2X]
is chosen outside of a set of size
\ll X/(\log\log X)^{1/10}
then we also have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\exists p>z,\nu\geq 1\\
p^{\nu}>h^{1-\varepsilon}\\
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0,p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll_{f}\frac{h}{(\log\log X)^{1/10}}.

Adding up the two contributions, we get that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\exists p>z,\nu\geq 1\\
\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=0,p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\ll_{f}\frac{h}{(\log\log X)^{1/10}}.

To summarize, we have shown the existence of a constant
c_{f}>0
such that if
h>X^{1/3+2\varepsilon}
then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq x+h\\
\lambda_{f}(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}r(n+a)\geq(c_{f,a}+o(1))h+O_{f}\left(\frac{h}{(\log\log X)^{1/10}}\right)\gg_{f}h

for all but
O(X/(\log\log X)^{1/10})
choices of
x\in[X,2X]
, and the claim follows.
∎

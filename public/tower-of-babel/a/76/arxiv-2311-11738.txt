Linearity and Local Correctness in Weighted Colourings of Random Graphs
Ghurumuruhan Ganesan

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a weighted generalization of the chromatic number of a Binomial random graph G . G. We equip each edge with a random weight and then colour the vertices in such a way that the absolute colour difference between any two adjacent vertices is at least as large as their edge weight. We show that with high probability, the weighted colouring number grows linearly with the maximum vertex degree if the edge weights have sufficiently large moments. Conversely, if the edge weight moments are unbounded then the weighted chromatic number is much larger than the maximum vertex degree, with high probability. We also obtain a sharp threshold result for locally correct weighted colourings for balanced subgraphs of G . G. Key words: Weighted colouring; random graphs; edge weight moments, locally correct colourings, sharp threshold. AMS 2020 Subject Classification: Primary: 60C05.



1
Introduction

The chromatic number
\chi(G)
of a graph
G
is the smallest number of colours needed for a proper colouring of
G
and many upper and lower bounds for
\chi(G)
exist in terms of various graph parameters like maximum vertex degree, independence number, clique number etc. (see Chapter
5,
(West (2000)). For random graphs, concentration and estimates for the actual value of the chromatic number in terms of the edge probability
p
has been well-studied (see for example Bollobás (2001)).

In this paper, we study weighted colouring in random graphs whose edge probabilities are not necessarily homogenous. Generalizing radio labelling of graphs (Chartrand et al. (2001), Korze et al. (2021)) which we call as weighted colouring number, we use the probabilistic method to estimate the weighted colouring number when the edge weights themselves are random. Such situations arise frequently in applications and we show that if the weights have sufficiently large moments, then the “cost” incurred due to the unboundedness of weights is small.

We also consider Ramsey type properties of weighted colourings in the sense of locally correct colourings. The study of Ramsey property of random graphs is important from theoretical and application perspectives. Luczak et al. (1992) initiated the study by determining the threshold for the edge probability exponent that results in the presence of a monochromatic copy of a given graph for both vertex and edge colouring. Since then many variants and extensions have been studied. For example, Rödl et al. (2017) extend the results of Luczak et al. (1992) and determine a double exponential upper bound for the
2-
colour Folkmann numbers. Concurrently Dudek and Pralat (2017) studied monochromatic long paths in
3-
colourings of random graphs and obtain optimal bounds on the length exponent. Recently Liebenau et al. (2022) established threshold properties for asymmetric Ramsey properties of
G
where every colouring contains either a
1-
coloured copy of
F
or a
2-
coloured copy of
H,
where the pair
(F,H)
constitutes a clique and a cycle.

In this paper, we consider weighted random graphs and obtain sharp threshold for the occurrence of properly coloured copies of balanced subgraphs. We demonstrate a “sharp threshold” for the number of colours needed for such properly coloured copies.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we study weighted colourings and obtain estimates for the weighted chromatic number of random graphs when the edge weights have bounded moments. In Section
3
, we consider locally correct weighted colourings where we fix the number of colours and seek properly coloured subgraphs of the overall random graph.

2
Weighted Colourings

Let
K_{n}
be the complete graph on
n
vertices and let
X_{h},h\in K_{n}
be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) Bernoulli random variables indexed by the edge set of
K_{n}
and satisfying

\mathbb{P}(X_{f}=1)=p=1-\mathbb{P}(X_{f}=0)

(2.1)

where
0\leq p\leq 1.
Equip the
{n\choose 2}
edges of
K_{n}
with i.i.d. weights
\{w(h)\}_{h\in K_{n}}
(that are also independent of
\{X_{h}\}
), satisfying
w(h)\geq 1
a.s. and let
\mathbb{P}
denote the probability measure associated with
\{X(h)\}
and
\{w(h)\}.

Let
G
be the random subgraph of
K_{n}
formed by the set of all edges satisfying
X_{f}=1.
For an integer
k\geq 1,
a proper
k-
colouring of
G
is a map
g:V\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
such that
g(u)\neq g(v)
for all edges
(u,v)\in E.
The chromatic number
\chi(G)
is the smallest integer
k
such that
G
admits a proper
k-
colouring.

Let
f:V\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots\}
be any map from the vertex set of
G
to the set of positive integers. We say that
f
is a proper weighted colouring of
G
if for each edge
h=(u,v)\in E
with endvertices
u
and
v
we have

|f(u)-f(v)|\geq w(u,v).

(2.2)

We define the weighted colouring number of
G
as

\chi_{w}=\chi_{w}(G):=\inf_{f}\max_{1\leq u\leq n}f(u),

(2.3)

where the infimum is over all proper weighted colourings of
G.
Roughly speaking
\chi_{w}(G)
could be thought of as a measure of the effect of weights on a proper colouring of
G.
Indeed if
w(u,v)=1
for all edges, then the weighted colouring number
\chi_{w}(G)=\chi(G),
the chromatic number.

If
p\geq\frac{M\log{n}}{n}
for some constant
M>0,
then it is well-known (see for example Theorem
2,
Ganesan (2020)) that for every constant
\epsilon>0
there are constants
M_{0},D_{1},D_{2}>0
such that if
M>M_{0},
then

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{D_{1}np}{\log{n}}\leq\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1\leq np(1+\epsilon)\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{D_{2}}},

(2.4)

where constants do not depend on
n,
throughout and
\Delta(G)
is the maximum vertex degree in
G.
By definition, the weighted colouring number
\chi_{w}(G)\geq\chi(G)
and we have the following result regarding the linearity for
\chi_{w}(G).

Theorem 2.1

We have:

(a)
Suppose
p\geq\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}
and
\mathbb{E}w^{2s+c}(h)\leq C
for some constants

0<\beta<1,C,c>0
and integer constant
s\geq 2.
For every
\beta<1-\frac{1}{s+1/2-1/2s}
and
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
D>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\chi_{w}(G)\leq 2\mu np(1+\epsilon)\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{D}}

(2.5)

where
\mu:=\mathbb{E}w(h)
is the expected edge weight.

(b)
Suppose
p=\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}
and
\mathbb{P}(w(h)\geq x)\geq\frac{A}{x^{2s}}
for some constants
0<\beta<1,A>0
and
s>1.

For every
\beta>1-\frac{1}{2s-1},
there are constants
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\chi_{w}(G)\geq n^{\theta_{1}}\cdot np\right)\geq 1-\exp\left(-n^{\theta_{2}}\right).

(2.6)

Part
(a)
of Theorem
2.1
says that if the edge weights have bounded
(2s)^{th}
moments and the edge probability exponent is small enough (or equivalently if
G
is sufficiently dense), then the weighted chromatic number grows linearly with the maximum vertex degree. In part
(b),
we obtain a partial converse of part
(a):
if the
(2s)^{th}
moment is infinite and the graph
G
is sparse enough, then
\chi_{w}(G)
is “much” larger than the maximum degree in the sense of (
2.6
).

We prove Theorem
2.1
(a)
using a greedy colouring strategy analogous to the maximum degree bound for unweighted chromatic number and prove part
(b)
by estimating the maximum weight of an edge in
G.
Throughout, we use the following standard deviation estimate regarding sums of independent Bernoulli random variables. Let
Z_{i},1\leq i\leq t
be independent Bernoulli random variables satisfying

\mathbb{P}(Z_{i}=1)=p_{i}=1-\mathbb{P}(Z_{i}=0).

If
W_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}Z_{i}
and
\mu_{t}=\mathbb{E}W_{t},
then for any
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
we have that

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|W_{t}-\mu_{t}\right|\geq\epsilon\mu_{t}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\mu_{t}\right).

(2.7)

For a proof of (
2.7
), we refer to Corollary
A.1.14,
pp.
312,
Alon and Spencer (2008).

Proof of Theorem
2.1
: We prove
(a)
first and begin with a deterministic estimate that obtains a bound for the weighted chromatic number in terms of the locally averaged weights. Specifically, we show that

\chi_{w}(G)\leq 1+\max_{v\in G}\sum_{u\sim v}(2w(u,v)-1)

(2.8)

where
u\sim v
denotes that
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G.
This is a generalization of the maximum degree bound
\chi(G)\leq\Delta+1,
where
\Delta
is the maximum vertex degree in
G.
In fact if
w(u,v)=1
for all edges, then the right side of (
2.8
) reduces to
\Delta+1.
As in the proof of the maximum degree bound, we use a greedy colouring procedure to obtain (
2.8
).

Let
L+1:=1+\max_{v}\sum_{u\sim v}(2w(u,v)-1),
the right hand side of (
2.8
). We iteratively colour the vertices of
H
from the set
\{1,2,\ldots,L+1\}.
Pick a vertex
u_{1}
and assign the colour
l(u_{1})=1.
For the
i^{th}
iteration,
i\geq 2,
we pick an uncoloured vertex
u_{i}
and let
l(v_{1}),\ldots,l(v_{t})
be the colours of its coloured neighbours. Let
I(v_{j})
be the set of all integers
l
satisfying
|l-l(v_{j})|\leq w(u_{i},v_{j})-1.
There are
2w(u_{i},v_{j})-1
integers in
I(v_{j})
and so the number of integers in
\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq t}I(v_{j})
is at most
L.
We therefore assign a colour
l(u_{i})\in\{1,2,\ldots,L+1\}\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq t}I(v_{j})
to the vertex
u_{i}.
Continuing this process we obtain a proper weighted colouring of
G
and this proves (
2.8
).

We use (
2.8
) to prove (
2.5
) as follows. For
1\leq i\leq n
let
N_{i}
be the number of neighbours of the vertex
i
in the graph
G.
The expected number of neighbours for vertex
i
is at least
(n-1)p.
Therefore using the concentration estimate (
2.7
) with
\epsilon>0,
we have that

\mathbb{P}\left(np(1-\epsilon)\leq N_{i}\leq np(1+\epsilon)\right)\geq 1-e^{-Dnp},

(2.9)

for some positive constant
D=D(\epsilon).
Letting

E_{nei}:=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\{np(1-\epsilon)\leq N_{i}\leq np(1+\epsilon)\right\},

we have by the union bound that

\mathbb{P}(E_{nei})\geq 1-ne^{-Dnp}.

(2.10)

Let
\omega\in E_{nei}
be a realization of
G
and let
\mathbb{P}_{\omega}
be the conditional probability measure given
\omega.
To find an upper bound for
\chi_{w}(\omega),
we use the locally averaged bound (
2.8
). For
1\leq i\leq n
let
J_{i}:=\sum_{v\sim i}w(i,v)=\sum_{v=1}^{N_{i}}Z_{v}
be the sum of the weights of edges containing
i
as an endvertex, where
N_{i}
is the degree of vertex
i
in
\omega.
The expected value
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(J_{i})=N_{i}\mu
and so by the Markov inequality, we have for constant
\epsilon>0
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\omega}\left(\left|J_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i}\right|\geq\epsilon\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(J_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i}\right)^{2s}}{(\epsilon\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i})^{2s}}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}}{N_{i}^{2s}}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(J_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i}\right)^{2s}

for some constant
D_{1}>0.

Expanding
(J_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i})^{2s}=\left(\sum_{v=1}^{N_{i}}(Z_{v}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}Z_{v})\right)^{2s},
we see that any term of the form
\prod_{j}(Z_{i_{j}}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}Z_{i_{j}})^{b_{j}}
has expectation zero, unless each
b_{j}
is even. This implies that the total number of terms with non-zero expectation is

\sum_{l=1}^{s}{N_{i}\choose l}\leq s{N_{i}\choose s}\leq sN_{i}^{s}

by the monotonicity of the binomial coefficient for
N_{i}\geq 2s.
Thus

\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left(\sum_{v=1}^{N_{i}}(Z_{v}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}Z_{v})\right)^{2s}\leq D_{2}N_{i}^{s}

for some constant
D_{2}>0
and plugging this into (
2.11
), we get that

\mathbb{P}_{\omega}\left(\left|J_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i}\right|\geq\epsilon\mathbb{E}_{\omega}J_{i}\right)\leq\frac{D_{3}}{N_{i}^{s}}

for some constant
D_{3}>0.
Recalling that
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}(J_{i})=N_{i}\mu
and
np(1-\epsilon)\leq N_{i}\leq np(1+\epsilon)
since
\omega\in E_{nei},
we get

\mathbb{P}_{\omega}\left(J_{i}\geq\mu(1+\epsilon)^{2}np\right)\leq\frac{D_{3}}{N_{i}^{s}}\leq\frac{D_{4}}{(np)^{s}}

(2.12)

for some constant
D_{4}>0.

Define vertex
v
to be
bad
if
J_{v}>\mu np(1+\epsilon)^{2}
and let
G_{good}\subset G
be the graph induced by set of all vertices that are
not
bad. Arguing as in the first paragraph of the proof, we obtain a proper colouring of
G_{good}
using at most
L:=2\mu np(1+\epsilon)^{2}+1

v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
is the set of all bad vertices and

is the maximum weight of an edge in

containing

as an endvertex, then we assign colour

The overall resulting colouring is proper and uses at most
L+2\sum_{i=1}^{t}M_{v_{i}}

and so

is bounded above by

\displaystyle\zeta_{w}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 2\mu np(1+\epsilon)^{2}+1+2\sum_{i=1}^{t}M_{v_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mu np(1+\epsilon)^{2}+1+2M_{tot},

where
M_{tot}:=\sum_{v}M_{v}1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}\left(v\text{ is bad}\right).

We estimate

as follows. By the moment condition in the Theorem statement and the Markov inequality, we have for

that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\omega}(M_{v}>x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\omega}\left(\bigcup_{u\sim v}\{w(u,v)>x\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{u\sim v}\mathbb{P}_{\omega}(w(u,v)>x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}N_{v}}{x^{2s+c}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2C_{1}np}{x^{2s+c}}

for some constant

where we recall that

is the number of neighbours of

and the final relation in (

) is true since

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}M_{v}^{2s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int x^{2s-1}\mathbb{P}(M_{v}>x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C_{1}np\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{1+c}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}np

for some constant
C_{2}>0.
By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and (
2.12
), we therefore have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}M_{v}1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}(v\text{ is bad})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}M_{v}^{2s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}\cdot\left(\mathbb{P}(v\text{ is bad })\right)^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C_{2}np)^{\frac{1}{2s}}\cdot\left(\frac{D_{5}}{(np)^{s}}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}}{(np)^{r}}

where

and

is a constant.

Summing over

in (

) and recalling that

we get that

\mathbb{E}M_{tot}\leq\frac{C_{3}n}{(np)^{r}}

and so by Markov inequality we have for
\epsilon>0
that

\mathbb{P}\left(M_{tot}\geq\frac{n^{1+\epsilon}}{(np)^{r}}\right)\leq\frac{C_{4}}{n^{\epsilon}}

for some constant
C_{4}>0.
Plugging this into (

), we get that

\mathbb{P}\left(\chi_{w}\leq 2\mu np(1+\epsilon)^{2}+1+\frac{n^{1+\epsilon}}{(np)^{r}}\right)\geq 1-\frac{C_{4}}{n^{\epsilon}}.

If we ensure that

\frac{n^{1+\epsilon}}{(np)^{r}}\leq\epsilon np

or equivalently that

then we get (

). But by choice

To prove part

we suppose that the event

defined above occurs, so that the number of edges in

is at least

and each edge has degree at most

The probability that an edge

has weight less than

is at most

and so the probability that the maximum edge weight is at most

\left(1-\frac{A}{n^{2s\epsilon}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}n^{2-\beta}}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{A}{4}n^{2-\beta-2s\epsilon}\right)\longrightarrow 0

). This completes the proof of the Theorem.

3
Locally Correct Colourings

In this section, we consider a dual problem where the number of colours is fixed and we seek properly coloured subgraphs of random graphs. As before, let
G
be the random graph as in (
2.1
) and let
\{w(h)\}_{h\in K_{n}}
be independent and identically distributed edge weights that are also independent of
G.

Let
\Gamma
be a labelled deterministic graph on
v_{0}
vertices and containing
e_{0}
edges and let
V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma)
respectively denote the vertex and edge sets of
\Gamma.
We say that
\Gamma
is
balanced
if for any subgraph
H\subset\Gamma
we have that
\frac{e(H)}{v(H)}\leq\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}}
where
e(H)=\#E(H)
and
v(H)=\#V(H)
respectively denote the number of edges and vertices in
H.
Throughout
\#A
denotes the cardinality of
A
and we assume henceforth that
\Gamma
is a balanced graph.

We say that a subgraph
T\subset G
containing
v_{0}
vertices and
e_{0}
edges is a
copy
of
\Gamma
if
T
is isomorphic to
\Gamma:
i.e. there exists a bijection
g:V(T)\rightarrow V(\Gamma)
such that
(u,v)\in E(T)
if and only if
(g(u),g(v))\in E(\Gamma).
For constant
M>0,
we say that a subgraph
T\subset G
is
M-
good
or simply good if

w(u,v)\leq|\theta(u)-\theta(v)|\leq M

(3.1)

for each edge
(u,v)\in E(T).
Say that the colouring
\theta
is
good
if there is at least one good copy of
\Gamma
in
G.

Letting
N_{tot}
and
N_{good}=N_{good}(M)
respectively denote the total number of colourings and the number of good colourings of
G,
the following result obtains a “sharp threshold” for the occurrence of good colourings. As before, constants do not depend on
n.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\Gamma
be a balanced graph containing
v_{0}
vertices and
e_{0}
edges and suppose
r=n^{\theta}\text{ and }p=\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}
for some constants
0<\theta<1
and
\frac{1}{e_{0}}<\beta<\frac{v_{0}}{e_{0}}
so that
0<\theta_{th}:=\frac{v_{0}-e_{0}\beta}{v_{0}-1}<1
strictly. There exists
M_{0}>0
large such that for all
M>M_{0},
the following hold:

(a)
If
\theta<\theta_{th},
then

\mathbb{P}\left(N_{good}\geq N_{tot}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{C_{1}}}\right)\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{C_{2}}}

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0.

(b)
If
\theta>\theta_{th},
then

\mathbb{P}\left(N_{good}\leq\frac{N_{tot}}{n^{D_{1}}}\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{D_{2}}}

for some constants
D_{1},D_{2}>0.

In words, we see that if
\theta<\theta_{th},
then nearly all colourings are good with high probability and if
\theta>\theta_{th}
then nearly all colourings are bad with high probability. Thus
\theta_{th}
could be interpreted as the threshold value of
\theta
for the occurrence of good colourings.

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{|N_{\Gamma}(G)-\mathbb{E}N_{\Gamma}(G)|\leq\epsilon N_{\Gamma}(G)\}

good copy of
\Gamma
with
M=v_{0}(K+1)
and so

Y_{\Gamma}:=\sum_{T}1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}(A_{T})1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}(B_{T})

is a lower bound on the number of good copies of
\Gamma,
where
1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}(.)
refers to the indicator function and the summation is over all copies of
\Gamma
in the
complete graph

K_{n}.

We now use the second moment method show that
Y_{\Gamma}
is concentrated around its mean with high
\mathbb{P}_{X}-
probability for nearly all realizations of the random subgraph
G.
Indeed,

\mathbb{E}_{X}Y^{2}_{\Gamma}=\sum_{T}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}(B_{T})+\sum_{T_{1}\neq T_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{1}}\cap A_{T_{2}})1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}\left(B_{T_{1}}\cap B_{T_{2}}\right).

Taking expectations with respect to
G
and the edge weights, we then get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{X}Y^{2}_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})\mathbb{P}(B_{T})+\sum_{T_{1}\neq T_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{1}}\cap A_{T_{2}})\mathbb{P}\left(B_{T_{1}}\cap B_{T_{2}}\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T_{1}}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{1}})\mathbb{P}(B_{T_{1}})\left(1+\Delta\right),

where

\Delta:=\sum_{T_{1}\neq T_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{2}}\mid A_{T_{1}})\mathbb{P}\left(B_{T_{2}}\mid B_{T_{1}}\right).

Let
T
be any copy of
\Gamma
and let
u
be any vertex of
T.
Conditioning on the colour
X_{u},
we see that
\mathbb{P}(A_{T}\mid X_{u})
lies between
\frac{C_{1}}{r^{v_{0}-1}}
and
\frac{C_{2}}{r^{v_{0}-1}}
for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
and so taking averages we get

\frac{C_{1}}{r^{v_{0}-1}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})\leq\frac{C_{2}}{r^{v_{0}-1}}.

(3.6)

If
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are distinct copies of
\Gamma
that do not share any vertex, then

\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{2}}\mid A_{T_{1}})=\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{2}})\text{ and }\mathbb{P}\left(B_{T_{2}}\mid B_{T_{1}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(B_{T_{2}}\right).

(3.7)

On the other hand if
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
share exactly
1\leq i\leq v_{0}-1
common vertices, then the total number of vertices in
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
is
2(v_{0}-i)+i=2v_{0}-i
and arguing as in (
3.6
), we have that

\frac{C_{3}}{r^{2v_{0}-i-1}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{1}}\cap A_{T_{2}})\leq\frac{C_{4}}{r^{2v_{0}-i-1}}

(3.8)

for some constants
C_{3},C_{4}>0.
Combining with (
3.6
) we get

\frac{C_{5}}{r^{v_{0}-i}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{2}}\mid A_{T_{1}})\leq\frac{C_{6}}{r^{v_{0}-i}}

(3.9)

for some constants
C_{5},C_{6}>0.

Next, the graph
\Gamma
is balanced and so there are at most
\frac{ie_{0}}{v_{0}}
edges containing both end vertices within
T_{1}\cap T_{2}.
Consequently

\mathbb{P}(B_{T_{2}}\mid B_{T_{1}})\leq(pz_{K})^{e_{0}-\frac{ie_{0}}{v_{0}}}

where we recall that
z_{K}=\mathbb{P}(w(h)\leq K)
is the (constant) probability that edge weight is at most
K.
Since there at most
n^{v_{0}-i}
choices for the vertices in
T_{2}\setminus T_{1},
we get from the above discussion that

\Delta\leq\sum_{T_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T_{2}})\mathbb{P}(B_{T_{2}})+D_{1}\sum_{1\leq i\leq v_{0}-1}\frac{n^{v_{0}-i}}{r^{v_{0}-i}}p^{e_{0}-\frac{ie_{0}}{v_{0}}}

for some constant
D_{1}>0.
Plugging this into (
3.5
), we get that

\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{X}Y^{2}_{\Gamma}\leq\alpha+\alpha^{2}+D_{1}\alpha\sum_{1\leq i\leq v_{0}-1}\frac{n^{v_{0}-i}}{r^{v_{0}-i}}p^{e_{0}-\frac{ie_{0}}{v_{0}}},

(3.10)

where

\alpha:=\sum_{T}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})\mathbb{P}(B_{T})=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma}.

(3.11)

By Jensen’s inequality we have that
\alpha^{2}=(\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma})^{2}
and so from (
3.10
) we get that

\mathbb{E}var_{X}(Y_{\Gamma})\leq\alpha\left(1+D_{1}\sum_{1\leq i\leq v_{0}-1}\frac{n^{v_{0}-i}}{r^{v_{0}-i}}p^{e_{0}-\frac{ie_{0}}{v_{0}}}\right).

(3.12)

Using the lower bounds in (
3.6
) and (
3.2
), we get that

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{T}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})\mathbb{P}(T\in G_{K})

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}z_{K}^{e_{0}}}{r^{v_{0}-1}}\mathbb{E}N_{\Gamma}(G_{K})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{3}n^{v_{0}}p^{e_{0}}}{r^{v_{0}-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{3}n^{|\zeta|},

for some constants
D_{2},D_{3}>0,
since
\theta<\frac{v_{0}-e_{0}\beta}{v_{0}-1}
strictly. Similarly using the upper bounds in (
3.6
) and (
3.2
), we get

\alpha\leq\sum_{T}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})\mathbb{P}(T\in G)\leq D_{4}n^{|\zeta|}

(3.14)

for some constant
D_{4}>0.

Next, for
1\leq i\leq v_{0}-1
we have that

\frac{n^{v_{0}-i}}{r^{v_{0}-i}}p^{e_{0}-\frac{ie_{0}}{v_{0}}}=\left(\frac{n^{v_{0}}p^{e_{0}}}{r^{v_{0}}}\right)^{1-\frac{i}{v_{0}}}\leq D_{5}\left(\frac{\alpha}{r}\right)^{1-i/v_{0}}\leq D_{5}\alpha^{1-1/v_{0}}=D_{5}\frac{\alpha}{n^{2\delta}}

for some positive constants

not depending on the choice of

Plugging this into (

) we get that
\mathbb{E}var_{X}(Y_{\Gamma})\leq\frac{D_{6}\alpha^{2}}{n^{2\delta}}

for some constant
D_{6}>0
and so letting

we get from the Markov inequality that

\mathbb{P}\left(E^{c}_{var}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n^{\delta}}.

(3.15)

Suppose
E_{var}\cap E_{\Gamma}
occurs where
E_{\Gamma}
is as defined prior to (
3.4

we then get from the Chebychev inequality that

\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(|Y_{\Gamma}-\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma}|\geq\epsilon\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma}\right)\leq\frac{var_{X}(Y_{\Gamma})}{\epsilon^{2}(\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma})^{2}}\leq\frac{D_{7}\alpha^{2}}{n^{\delta}(\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma})^{2}}

(3.16)

for some constant

From (
3.11
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}Y_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T}\mathbb{P}_{X}(A_{T})1\hskip-6.54413pt{1}(T\in G_{K})

(3.17)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle D_{8}\frac{N_{\Gamma}(G_{K})}{r^{v_{0}-1}}

\displaystyle D_{8}\frac{(1-\epsilon)\mathbb{E}N_{\Gamma}(G_{K})}{r^{v_{0}-1}}

\displaystyle D_{9}n^{|\zeta|}

for some constants

For part

we proceed in a similar manner as above with minor modifications. Let

As before, let

G

\frac{1}{r^{v_{0}-1}}\leq\mathbb{P}_{X}(S_{T})\leq\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{v_{0}-1}.

Therefore if the event

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}Z_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle N_{\Gamma}(G)\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{v_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}N_{\Gamma}(G)(1+\epsilon)\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{v_{0}-1}

\displaystyle D_{2}(1+\epsilon)(2M)^{v_{0}-1}n^{v_{0}-e_{0}\beta-\theta(v_{0}-1)}

\displaystyle\frac{D_{3}}{n^{|\zeta|}}

for some constant

Abstract
. We consider a PDE-ODE model of a flexible satellite that is composed of two identical flexible solar panels and a center rigid body. We prove that the satellite model is exponentially stable in the sense that the energy of the solutions decays to zero exponentially. In addition, we construct two internal model based controllers, a passive controller and an observer based controller, such that the linear and angular velocities of the center rigid body converge to the given sinusoidal signals asymptotically. A numerical simulation is presented to compare the performances of the two controllers.

As the first main contribution of this paper, we present a detailed proof of uniform exponential stability of the satellite model in the sense that the energy of the solutions decay exponentially to zero.
The stability proof is based on the results from
C_{0}
-semigroup theory.
We write the satellite system as a coupled system of a PDE (two beams are combined into a single system) and an ODE (rigid body) via a power preserving interconnection. The main proof is divided naturally into two steps. In the first step, we show that the imaginary axis is included in the resolvent set of the satellite system operator. In the second step, we derive an explicit expression for the resolvent operator
and show that it is uniformly bounded on the imaginary axis. The stability proof is challenging because the input and the output operators of the PDE are not admissible and its transfer function is not well-posed (in the sense that the input-output map of the PDE is unbounded).

Stability of coupled PDE-ODE systems can often be obtained using controllability and observability results. In

3.
Stability of the Satellite Model

In this section, we will show the exponential stability of the satellite system
in the sense that the operator
A
defined in Section
2.3
generates an exponentially stable semigroup
T(t)
. Let us recall the operator
A

(12)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{b}&0\\
-B_{c}\mathcal{C}_{b}&0\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle D(A)

\displaystyle=\{(x_{b},x_{c})\in D(\mathcal{A}_{b})\times X_{c}\ :\ \mathcal{B}_{b}x_{b}=C_{c}x_{c}\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

The semigroup
T(t)
generated by
A
in (
12
) is exponentially stable.

We prove the theorem by using frequency domain criteria
[
16
, Cor. 3.36]
which states that the semigroup
T(t)
generated by
A
is exponentially stable if and only if
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A)
and
\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}{\lVert R(i\omega,A)\rVert}<\infty
. We complete the proof in the following steps. Since the satellite system is a coupled system of the beam system and the center rigid body, we will first show that
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A_{b})
and
\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}{\lVert R(i\omega,A_{b})\rVert}<\infty
where
A_{b}=\mathcal{A}_{b}|_{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{b})}
. As the second step, we will show that
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A)
. In this step, we will obtain an expression for the resolvent operator
R(i\omega,A)
. Next, we will estimate upper bounds of the operators which appear in the resolvent expression. Finally, we will show that
R(i\omega,A)
is uniformly bounded.

Lemma 3.2
.

The operator
A_{b}
defined in Remark
2.5
satisfies
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A_{b})
and
\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}{\lVert R(i\omega,A_{b})\rVert}<\infty
.

Proof.

We show that the semigroup
T_{b}(t)
generated by
A_{b}
is exponentially stable
which guarantees
i\mathbb{R}\subset\rho(A_{b})
and uniform boundedness of the resolvent
R(i\omega,A_{b})
.
First we claim that the operator
A_{r}=\mathcal{A}_{r}|_{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}_{r})}
corresponding to the right beam system (
7
) generates an exponentially stable semigroup
T_{r}(t),t\geq 0
. We use
[
7
, Main Theorem 1]
.
We write
A_{r}
as
A_{r}=A_{0}+B_{0}
where

\displaystyle A_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-EI\partial_{\xi\xi}\\
{(\rho a)}^{-1}\partial_{\xi\xi}&0\end{bmatrix},\quad B_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}-\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

and
D(A_{0})=D(A_{r})
. We will show that the operators
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
satisfy the following conditions.

(c1)

The operator
A_{0}
is skew-adjoint and it has compact resolvent.

(c2)

The spectrum of
A_{0}
satisfies the gap property

\displaystyle\inf{\{|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}|\ |j,k=1,2,3,\cdots,j\neq k\}}>0.

(c3)

The operator
B_{0}
is dissipative.

(c4)

If any sequence
\{(x_{r_{n}})\in X_{r},\ n=1,2,\cdots\}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\text{Re}\left\langle B_{0}x_{r_{n}},x_{r_{n}}\right\rangle_{X_{r}}=0,

then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert B_{0}x_{r_{n}}\rVert_{X_{r}}=0
.

(c5)

There exists
\delta>0
such that
\lVert B_{0}\phi_{k}\rVert_{X_{r}}\geq\delta
, where
\phi_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}
is an orthonormal eigenvector of
A_{0}
.

We have
\text{Re}\left\langle A_{0}x_{r},x_{r}\right\rangle=0,x_{r}\in D(A_{0})

[
5
, Thm. 2.3]
,

has compact resolvent. This implies that the operator
A_{0}
is skew-adjoint.

By a direct computation, we can obtain eigenvalues

of
A_{0}
and orthonormal basis
\phi_{k}=(f_{k},g_{k})^{T},k\in\mathbb{Z}
consisting of eigenvectors of
A_{0}
. The eigenvalues and the eigenvectors are given by

(13)

\displaystyle i\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{EI}{\rho a}}[\pi(k-\frac{1}{2})+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\pi(k-\frac{1}{2})})]^{2},

\displaystyle f_{k}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}[(\cosh{(\mu_{k})}+\cos{(\mu_{k})})(\cosh{(\mu_{k}\xi)}-\cos{(\mu_{k}\xi)})

\displaystyle-(\sinh{(\mu_{k})}-\sin{(\mu_{k})})(\sinh{(\mu_{k}\xi)}-\sin{(\mu_{k}\xi)})],

\displaystyle g_{k}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{k}}{i\sqrt{\rho aEI}}[(\cosh{(\mu_{k})}+\cos{(\mu_{k})})(\cosh{(\mu_{k}\xi)}+\cos{(\mu_{k}\xi)})

\displaystyle-(\sinh{(\mu_{k})}-\sin{(\mu_{k})})(\sinh{(\mu_{k}\xi)}+\sin{(\mu_{k}\xi)})],

where
\mu_{k}=(\frac{\rho a}{EI})^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}
are the
solutions of
\cosh{(\mu_{k})}\cos{(\mu_{k})}+1=0
and
\beta_{k}>0
are chosen such that
\lVert\phi_{k}\rVert_{X_{r}}=1
.
It is clear that
the condition (c2) is satisfied since the gap between two successive eigenvalues satisfies
|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1}|\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
The operator
B_{0}
is dissipative since

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left\langle B_{0}x_{r},x_{r}\right\rangle_{X_{r}}=-\gamma(\rho a)^{-2}\lVert x_{r_{1}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0.

Also,
-\text{Re}\left\langle B_{0}x_{r},x_{r}\right\rangle_{X_{r}}=\gamma^{-1}\rho a\lVert B_{0}x_{r}\rVert^{2}_{X_{r}}
holds. This implies that the conditions (c3) and (c4) are satisfied.

Next, we show that the condition (c5) is satisfied.
The formulas for
f_{k}
and
g_{k}
in (
13
) can be used to show that

(14)

\lim_{|k|\to\infty}\frac{\lVert g_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho aEI}}.

Here we note that
f_{k}\neq 0,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
(f_{k},g_{k})^{T}
are eigenvectors and
f_{k}=0
would imply
g_{k}=\frac{(\rho a)^{-1}}{i\lambda_{k}}f_{k}^{\prime\prime}=0
. The equation (

\epsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

with

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\lVert g_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}\bigg|\leq\epsilon+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho aEI}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert g_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\rho aEI}},\ \forall\ k\in\mathbb{Z},

where
C^{\prime}=\max\{1+\epsilon\sqrt{\rho aEI},\sqrt{\rho aEI}\max_{|k|<N}\frac{\lVert g_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}}\}

\displaystyle\lVert B_{0}\phi_{k}\rVert_{X_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2C^{\prime 2}}\gamma^{2}(\rho a)^{-2}\lVert\phi_{k}\rVert_{X_{r}}^{2},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2C^{\prime 2}}\gamma^{2}(\rho a)^{-2}\geq\delta^{2}>0,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Analogously, we have that

generates an exponentially stable semigroup

which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\cos{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}u_{r1}+\frac{\sin{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}}{\alpha(\omega)}u_{r2}\bigg],

\displaystyle g_{r}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\alpha(\omega)^{2}}}{i\omega}\bigg[(C_{1,\omega}u_{r1}-\frac{C_{3,\omega}}{\alpha(\omega)}u_{r2})g_{1}(\xi)+(C_{2,\omega}u_{r1}+\frac{C_{4,\omega}}{\alpha(\omega)}u_{r2})g_{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\cos{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}u_{r1}-\frac{\sin{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}}{\alpha(\omega)}u_{r2}\bigg],

where

(23)

\displaystyle C_{1,\omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{1}(\omega)}{C_{2}(\omega)},\ C_{2,\omega}=\frac{C_{2}(\omega)\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}-C_{1}(\omega)C_{5}(\omega)}{C_{2}(\omega)C_{3}(\omega)},

\displaystyle C_{3,\omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{4}(\omega)}{C_{2}(\omega)},\ C_{4,\omega}=\frac{C_{2}(\omega)\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}+C_{4}(\omega)C_{5}(\omega)}{C_{2}(\omega)C_{3}(\omega)},

\displaystyle f_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\cosh{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}-\cos{(\alpha(\omega)\xi}),\ f_{2}(\xi)=\sinh{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}-\sin{(\alpha(\omega)\xi}),

\displaystyle g_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\cosh{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}+\cos{(\alpha(\omega)\xi}),\ g_{2}(\xi)=\sinh{(\alpha(\omega)\xi)}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega)\xi})

and

\displaystyle C_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=1+\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))},

\displaystyle C_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=2+2\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}\cos{(\alpha(\omega))},

\displaystyle C_{3}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))},

\displaystyle C_{4}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}-\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))},

\displaystyle C_{5}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\cos{(\alpha(\omega))},

\displaystyle\alpha(\omega)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\rho a}{EI}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\omega^{2}-i\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}\omega)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Therefore, the transfer function of the right beam can be written as,

\displaystyle P_{r}(i\omega)u_{r}=EI\begin{bmatrix}g_{r}^{\prime}(0)\\
-g_{r}(0)\end{bmatrix},\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}

where

\displaystyle g_{r}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=2\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}^{3}}{i\omega}C_{2,\omega}u_{r1}+\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}^{2}}{i\omega}(2C_{4,\omega}-1)u_{r2},

\displaystyle g_{r}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}^{2}}{i\omega}(2C_{1,\omega}-1)u_{r1}-2\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}}{i\omega}C_{3,\omega}u_{r2}.

In the same way, we can obtain the transfer function of the left beam which is given by,

\displaystyle P_{l}(i\omega)u_{l}=EI\begin{bmatrix}-g_{l}^{\prime}(0)\\
g_{l}(0)\end{bmatrix},\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}

where

\displaystyle g_{l}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=-2\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}^{3}}{i\omega}C_{2,\omega}u_{l1}+\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}^{2}}{i\omega}(2C_{4,\omega}-1)u_{l2},

\displaystyle g_{l}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}^{2}}{i\omega}(2C_{1,\omega}-1)u_{l1}+2\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}}{i\omega}C_{3,\omega}u_{l2}.

Thus, the transfer function of the combined beam system is given by,

(24)

\displaystyle P_{b}(i\omega)u_{b}=4EI\frac{{\alpha(\omega)}}{i\omega}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha(\omega)^{2}C_{2,\omega}&0\\
0&C_{3,\omega}\end{bmatrix}u_{b},\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

Now, let us estimate the absolute values of
C_{2,\omega}
and
C_{3,\omega}
which contain trigonometric and hyperbolic terms.
Writing
\alpha(\omega)
in terms of its real and imaginary parts, we obtain

(25)

\displaystyle\alpha(\omega)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\rho a}{EI}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\omega^{2}-i\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}\omega)^{\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle=|\alpha(\omega)|\bigg(\cos{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)+2\pi k}{4}\bigg)}+i\sin{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)+2\pi k}{4}\bigg)}\bigg),\ k=0,1,2,3,

where

\displaystyle|\alpha(\omega)|

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\rho a}{EI}|\omega|\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\gamma^{2}(\rho a)^{-2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle\theta(\omega)

\displaystyle=\tan^{-1}\bigg(\frac{-\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}}{\omega}\bigg).

We have

\displaystyle\text{Re}(\alpha(\omega))

\displaystyle=\pm|\alpha(\omega)|\cos{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)}{4}\bigg)}\ \text{or}\ \text{Re}(\alpha(\omega))=\mp|\alpha(\omega)|\sin{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)}{4}\bigg)},

\displaystyle\text{Im}(\alpha(\omega))

\displaystyle=\pm|\alpha(\omega)|\sin{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)}{4}\bigg)}\ \text{or}\ \text{Im}(\alpha(\omega))=\pm|\alpha(\omega)|\cos{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)}{4}\bigg)}.

In addition, there exists
\omega_{1}\geq\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}>0

\tan^{-1}(\frac{\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}}{|\omega|})\leq\frac{\gamma(\rho a)^{-1}}{|\omega|}
for all
|\omega|>\omega_{1}
.
Therefore, there exist
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{4}>0
and
\omega_{2}>\omega_{1}

\displaystyle M_{1}\sqrt{|\omega|}\leq|\alpha(\omega)|\bigg|\cos{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)}{4}\bigg)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq M_{2}\sqrt{|\omega|}

\displaystyle M_{3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\omega|}}\leq|\alpha(\omega)|\bigg|\sin{\bigg(\frac{\theta(\omega)}{4}\bigg)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq M_{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\omega|}}

for all
|\omega|\geq\omega_{2}
. Denoting
x_{\omega}=\re(\alpha(\omega))
and

|x_{\omega}|
grows at a rate of
\sqrt{|\omega|}

|y_{\omega}|
decays at a rate of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\omega|}}

Since
\lvert C_{2,\omega}\rvert
and
\lvert C_{3,\omega}\rvert
have similar terms for all the four roots of
\alpha(\omega)
, we restrict our analysis to the principal branch of the fourth root of
\alpha(\omega)

The definition of
\alpha(\omega)
and straightforward estimates can be used to verify that
\lvert\cosh(\alpha(\omega))\cos(\alpha(\omega))\rvert\to\infty
as
\lvert\omega\rvert\to\infty
. Therefore, there exists
\omega_{0}>\omega_{2}
such that

(26)

|\cosh{\alpha(\omega)}\cos{\alpha(\omega)}|\geq 2

for all
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}

(27)

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}}{1+\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|&\leq\frac{\lvert\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}\rvert}{\lvert\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}\rvert-1}\\
&\leq 2\lvert\tan{(\alpha(\omega))}\rvert\lvert\tanh{(\alpha(\omega))}\rvert\\
&\leq 2(\lvert\coth{(y_{\omega})}\rvert+\lvert\tanh{(y_{\omega})}\rvert)\\
&\qquad(\lvert\tanh{(x_{\omega})}\rvert+\lvert\coth{(x_{\omega})}\rvert)\end{split}

where
the last inequality is obtained by separating real and imaginary parts of the second inequality and using straightforward estimates. Here we note that
\lvert\tanh{(x_{\omega})}\rvert
,
\lvert\coth{(x_{\omega})}\rvert
and
\lvert\tanh{(y_{\omega})}\rvert
are all uniformly bounded for

and since
|y_{\omega}|
decays at a rate of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\omega|}}
, using Taylor series, we can estimate, there exists
M_{0}^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\coth{(y_{\omega})}|

\displaystyle=\bigg|y_{\omega}^{-1}+\frac{y_{\omega}}{3}-\frac{y_{\omega}^{3}}{45}+\cdots\bigg|\leq M_{0}^{\prime}\sqrt{|\omega|}

for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
.
Therefore, from (

), we obtain

(28)

\bigg|\frac{\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}}{1+\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|\lesssim\sqrt{|\omega|}

for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
.
Moreover,
\lvert\frac{\sin{(x_{\omega})}\cosh{(y_{\omega})}}{\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}}\rvert\to 0
as
|\omega|\to\infty
. This implies that there exists
M_{0}^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\lvert\re(\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))})\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}+\sin{(x_{\omega})}\cosh{(y_{\omega})}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}\rvert\bigg|1+\frac{\sin{(x_{\omega})}\cosh{(y_{\omega})}}{\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq M_{0}^{\prime\prime}\lvert\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}\rvert

for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
.
Since
|\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}|
and
|\tan{(y_{\omega})}|
are uniformly bounded for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
, the above estimate implies that there exist
M_{1}^{\prime}>0
and
M_{1}^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

(29)

\bigg|\frac{\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}{\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|\leq M_{1}^{\prime}\qquad\text{and}

(30)

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}}{\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|&\leq\frac{1}{M_{0}^{\prime\prime}}\bigg|\frac{\cosh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}+i\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\sin{(y_{\omega})}}{\sinh{(x_{\omega})}\cos{(y_{\omega})}}\bigg|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{M_{0}^{\prime\prime}}[|\coth{(x_{\omega})}|+|\tan{(y_{\omega})}|]\leq M_{1}^{\prime\prime}\end{split}

for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
. We note that
\lvert\coth{(x_{\omega})}\rvert
is uniformly bounded for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
.
Using the estimates (
28
), (
29
) and (
30
), from (
23
), we obtain

(31)

\begin{split}\lvert C_{2,\omega}\rvert&\leq\bigg|\frac{\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}{\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|\\
&\qquad+\bigg|\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}}{2+2\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|\bigg|\frac{\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}{\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}+\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\sqrt{|\omega|}\end{split}

for
|\omega|\geq\omega_{0}
.
Again using (
26
), from (
23
), we can estimate

\displaystyle\lvert C_{3,\omega}\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}-\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}}{2+2\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}\sinh{(\alpha(\omega))}-\sin{(\alpha(\omega))}\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}}{\cosh{(\alpha(\omega))}\cos{(\alpha(\omega))}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq|\tanh{(\alpha(\omega))}|+|\tan{(\alpha(\omega))}|

(32)

\lvert C_{3,\omega}\rvert\lesssim\sqrt{|\omega|}

for

.
Finally, from the estimates (

), (
32
) and from equation (

\displaystyle\|P_{b}(i\omega)u_{b}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 16(EI)^{2}\bigg[\frac{{|\alpha(\omega)|}^{6}}{|\omega|^{2}}|\omega||u_{b1}|^{2}+\frac{{|\alpha(\omega)|^{2}}}{|\omega|^{2}}|\omega||u_{b2}|^{2}\bigg],

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that
\lVert R(i\omega,A_{b_{-1}})B_{b}\rVert\leq C^{\prime}\sqrt{|\omega|+1}
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
I+P_{b}(i\omega)P_{c}(i\omega)
is nonsingular for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

By using
[
21
, Rem. 10.1.5]
, we have that for every
u_{b}\in U_{b}
,
i\omega\in\rho(A_{b_{-1}})
,

\displaystyle x_{b}=R(i\omega,A_{b_{-1}})B_{b}u_{b}=\begin{bmatrix}R(i\omega,A_{l_{-1}})B_{l}\\
R(i\omega,A_{r_{-1}})B_{r}\end{bmatrix}u_{b}\in D(\mathcal{A}_{b})

where
B_{l}
,
B_{r}
and
B_{b}
are defined in Remark
2.5
, is the unique solution of the abstract elliptic problem

\displaystyle(i\omega-\mathcal{A}_{b})x_{b}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{b}x_{b}=u_{b}.

Assume that
|\omega|\geq 1
. Let us start by estimating the norm of
x_{r}=R(i\omega,A_{r_{-1}})B_{r}u_{b}
which is the unique solution of
(i\omega-\mathcal{A}_{r})x_{r}=0,\ \mathcal{B}_{r}x_{r}=u_{b}.

If
x_{r}=(f_{r},g_{r})^{T}
, then using the expression for
\mathcal{A}_{r}
, we have

(33)

\displaystyle(i\omega+\gamma(\rho a)^{-1})f_{r}+EIg_{r}^{\prime\prime}=0,

(34)

\displaystyle-(\rho a)^{-1}f_{r}^{\prime\prime}+i\omega g_{r}=0,

(35)

\displaystyle f_{r}(0)=\rho a\ u_{b1},\ f_{r}^{\prime}(0)=\rho a\ u_{b2},

(36)

\displaystyle EIg_{r}(1)=0,\ EIg_{r}^{\prime}(1)=0.

Taking
L^{2}
inner product of (
33
) with
(\rho a)^{-1}f_{r}
and
L^{2}
inner product of (
34
) with
EIg_{r}
, respectively, we obtain

(37)

\displaystyle(\rho a)^{-1}(i\omega+\gamma(\rho a)^{-1})\lVert f_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}-EI(\rho a)^{-1}(\rho a)g_{r}^{\prime}(0)u_{b1}

\displaystyle-EI(\rho a)^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}{g_{r}^{\prime}\bar{f_{r}}^{\prime}}d\xi=0.

(38)

\displaystyle EI\bar{g_{r}}(0)u_{b2}+EI(\rho a)^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}{f_{r}^{\prime}\bar{g_{r}}^{\prime}}d\xi+iEI\omega\lVert g_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}=0.

Adding complex conjugate of (
38
) to (
37
), we obtain

(39)

(\rho a)^{-1}(i\omega+\gamma(\rho a)^{-1})\lVert f_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}-iEI\omega\lVert g_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}=\left\langle y_{r},u_{b}\right\rangle.

Equating real and imaginary parts and using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma(\rho a)^{-2}\lVert f_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert P_{r}(i\omega)u_{b}\rVert\lVert u_{b}\rVert

\displaystyle EI\lVert g_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{\rho a}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{|\omega|}\bigg)\lVert P_{r}(i\omega)u_{b}\rVert\lVert u_{b}\rVert,

where
P_{r}(\cdot)
is the transfer function of the right beam system. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert x_{r}\rVert_{X_{r}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\rho a)^{-1}\lVert f_{r}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+EI\lVert g_{r}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho a}{\gamma}\lVert P_{r}(i\omega)u_{b}\rVert\lVert u_{b}\rVert+\bigg(\frac{\rho a}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{|\omega|}\bigg)\lVert P_{r}(i\omega)u_{b}\rVert\lVert u_{b}\rVert,

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{2\rho a}{\gamma}+1\bigg)\lVert P_{r}(i\omega)u_{b}\rVert\lVert u_{b}\rVert.

Since we have from Lemma
3.5
that
\lVert P_{r}(i\omega)\rVert
can grow at most linearly, the above estimate implies that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that
\lVert x_{r}\rVert=\lVert R(i\omega,A_{r_{-1}})B_{r}u_{b}\rVert\leq C_{1}\sqrt{|\omega|+1}\lVert u_{b}\rVert,\ |\omega|\geq 1
. We can analogously show that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that
\lVert R(i\omega,A_{l_{-1}})B_{l}u_{b}\rVert\leq C_{2}\sqrt{|\omega|+1}\lVert u_{b}\rVert,\ |\omega|\geq 1
. Combining these estimates, we can see that
\lVert R(i\omega,A_{b_{-1}})B_{b}\rVert\lesssim\sqrt{|\omega|+1}
for all
|\omega|\geq 1
. Finally, by continuity of
R(i\omega,A_{b_{-1}})B_{b}
with respect to
i\omega
on
i\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\lVert R(i\omega,A_{b_{-1}})B_{b}\rVert\lesssim\sqrt{|\omega|+1}
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.

From equation (
39
), we observe that
\re{P_{r}(i\omega)}>0,\ \omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, from (
39
) we have

\displaystyle\re\left\langle y_{r},u_{b}\right\rangle=\re\left\langle P_{r}(i\omega)u_{b},u_{b}\right\rangle=\gamma(\rho a)^{-2}\lVert f_{r}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Analogously, we have that
\re{P_{l}(i\omega)}>0,\ \omega\in\mathbb{R}.
This implies that
\re{P_{b}(i\omega)}>0,\ \omega\in\mathbb{R}
. In addition, from the transfer function

(40)

P_{c}(i\omega)=\frac{1}{i\omega}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{m}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{I_{m}}\end{bmatrix},\ \omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}

of the rigid body, we see that
\re{P_{c}(i\omega)}=0,\ \omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
.
Consequently, we have that
I+P_{b}(i\omega)P_{c}(i\omega)
is nonsingular for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that
\lVert C_{b}R(i\omega,A_{b})\rVert\leq C^{\prime\prime}\sqrt{|\omega|+1}
for all

Proof.

First let us prove that
\lVert C_{r}R(i\omega,A_{r})\rVert
, where

,

[

.
Therefore, for all

,

Since

and

are arbitrary, we have

(41)

C_{r}R(i\omega,A_{r})=-(R(i\omega,A_{r_{-1}})B_{r})^{*}(I+2B_{0}R(i\omega,A_{r})),\ i\omega\in\rho(A_{0})\cap i\mathbb{R}

3.2
, we have that

,

.
Now, using Lemma
3.6
, we have that there exists

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
P_{b}(\cdot)
and

(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathcal{B}_{b},\mathcal{C}_{b})
and the rigid body

4.2.
Robustness of Closed-loop Stability

In the case of the passive controller, the controller parameters
\mathcal{G}_{2}
,
K
and
\kappa
depend on the parameters
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and therefore the closed-loop stability margin
\alpha_{1}
depends on the choice of the parameters
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.
On the other hand, for the observer based controller the closed-loop stability margin is determined by the minimum of stability margins of
A
and
G_{1}+B_{1}K_{1}
, respectively, see the proof of
[
17
, Thm. 15]
for more details. The stability margin of
G_{1}+B_{1}K_{1}
can be affected by adjusting the gain parameter
K_{1}
. This can be done for example by linear quadratic regulator design or pole placement.

From Section
4.1
we have that both controllers with suitable choices of parameters solve the robust output regulation problem. Therefore,
A_{e}
generates an exponentially stable semigroup
T_{e}(t)
and there exist
\alpha_{1}>0
and
M_{e}\geq 1
depending on the controller and the chosen parameters such that
\lVert T_{e}(t)\rVert\leq M_{e}e^{-\alpha_{1}t}
. If
\Delta\in\mathcal{L}(X_{e})
is a perturbation of
A_{e}
, where the perturbation is generated by the perturbations in
(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathcal{B}_{b},\mathcal{C}_{b},A_{c},B_{c},C_{c})
, such that
\lVert\Delta\rVert<\alpha_{1}/M_{e}
, then
A_{e}+\Delta
generates an exponentially stable semigroup
\tilde{T}_{e}(t)
on
X_{e}
and
\lVert\tilde{T}_{e}(t)\rVert\leq M_{e}e^{(-\alpha_{1}+M_{e}\lVert\Delta\rVert)t}
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore the stability margin
\tilde{\alpha}_{1}
of the perturbed semigroup
\tilde{T}_{e}(t)
satisfies
\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\geq\alpha_{1}-M_{e}\lVert\Delta\rVert
. In addition, the semigroup
T_{e}(t)
may remain exponentially stable under perturbations with large norms in which cases the decay rates cannot be estimated explicitly by using the perturbation formula.

5.
Conclusion

We investigated robust output tracking problem of a flexible satellite composed of two identical flexible solar panels and a center rigid body. A detailed proof of exponential stability of the model was presented. We constructed two robust controllers for the robust output tracking of the satellite model.
Moreover, simulation results showing the performances of the controllers were presented.

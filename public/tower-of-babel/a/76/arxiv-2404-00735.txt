Two-Stage Nuisance Function Estimation for Causal Mediation Analysis
AmirEmad Ghassami

Abstract
When estimating the direct and indirect causal effects using the influence function-based estimator of the mediation functional, it is crucial to understand what aspects of the treatment, the mediator, and the outcome mean mechanisms should be focused on. Specifically, considering them as nuisance functions and attempting to fit these nuisance functions as accurate as possible is not necessarily the best approach to take. In this work, we propose a two-stage estimation strategy for the nuisance functions that estimates the nuisance functions based on the role they play in the structure of the bias of the influence function-based estimator of the mediation functional. We provide robustness analysis of the proposed method, as well as sufficient conditions for consistency and asymptotic normality of the estimator of the parameter of interest. Keywords: Causal Mediation Analysis; Influence Function-Based Estimation; Multiple Robustness; Balancing Estimators.

Introduction

In contrast to simply assessing the overall treatment effect of a treatment variable on an outcome variable of interest, causal mediation analysis delves deeper by posing a “how” inquiry, investigating the underlying mechanisms through which the treatment influences the outcome. Due to this conceptually invaluable focus, it has gained significant popularity in many fields of science including social science, economics, and epidemiology
(
VanderWeele, 2015
;
MacKinnon, 2012
;
Hayes, 2017
;
Richiardi et al., 2013
;
Ten Have and Joffe, 2012
)
.

The idea of causal mediation analysis is to partition the total causal effect of the treatment on the outcome into a direct part and an indirect part, where the indirect part is mediated by post-treatment mediator variables.
A fundamental result in the literature of causal inference demonstrates that under certain conditions on the statistical model for the data generating process, the direct and indirect effects can be identified by a functional known as the mediation formula or mediation functional
(
Robins and Greenland, 1992
;
Pearl, 2001
)
. If the researcher, based on expert and background knowledge, believes those assumptions hold in the system under study and the parameter is identified, the next question is how to estimate the mediation functional.

There has been several estimation strategies proposed in the literature based on regression and weighting techniques. In their seminal paper,
Tchetgen Tchetgen and Shpitser, 2012
proposed an estimator for the mediation functional which is locally semiparametric effeicint and has the desired property that under certain regularity conditions, it remains consistent even if the working models for all the nuisance functions used in the estimator are not correctly specified. More specifically, they utilize one nuisance function describing the treatment mechanism, one describing the mediator mechanism, and one describing the outcome mean mechanism, and the model for two out of three should be correctly specified. Among the aforementioned nuisance functions, the mediator mechanism, that is, the conditional distribution of the mediator variable given covariates and the treatment, is usually a difficult object to estimate. Trying to naively fit this conditional distribution as good as possible will most likely lead to a final estimator of the mediation functional that is sensitive to even small errors in the estimator of this nuisance function. More importantly, in the process of estimation, it is not clear what aspects of this object should be focused on to obtain a better estimate of the mediation functional.

In this paper, we propose a two-stage nuisance function estimation approach for mediation analysis, which avoids direct estimation of the mediator mechanism. In the first stage, we estimate two basic nuisance functions, namely the treatment mechanism and the outcome mean mechanism. In the second stage, using the basic nuisance functions, we estimate two additional nuisance functions that contain the needed information regarding the mediator mechanism. We use the weighted balancing approach of
Imai and Ratkovic, 2014
to design the estimator of the treatment mechanism in Stage 1 and one of the nuisance functions in Stage 2. The weights in these balancing estimators are designed directly based on the bias of the final estimator of the parameter of interest. We provide parametric and nonparametric versions of the balancing estimators. The other two nuisance functions are obtained using standard parametric or nonparametric regressions. After obtaining all four nuisance functions, they are used in the influence function-based estimator of
Tchetgen Tchetgen and Shpitser, 2012
. We provide a robustness analysis of the resulting estimator and demonstrate that our approach retains the multiple robustness property in the sense that not all the nuisance functions are needed to be correctly specified to obtain a consistent estimator of the parameter of interest. We also provide requirements on the nuisance function estimators sufficient to obtain a consistent and asymptotically normal estimator for the parameter of interest.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we describe the model and review conditions sufficient for identification of the direct and indirect causal effects. In Section
3
, we review the influence function-based estimator of
Tchetgen Tchetgen and Shpitser, 2012
and its multiple-robustness property. We propose our two-stage nuisance function estimation approach in Section
4
, and we provide the robustness and asymptotic analyses of the proposed estimator in Section
5
. We conclude in Section
6
.

3
Influence Function-Based Estimator

Tchetgen Tchetgen and Shpitser, 2012
showed that the efficienet influence function of the mediation functional is given by

\displaystyle IF_{\psi_{0}}=

\displaystyle\frac{I(A=1)p(M\mid A=0,X)}{p(A=1\mid X)p(M\mid A=1,X)}\{Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]\}

\displaystyle+\frac{I(A=0)}{p(A=0\mid X)}\{\mathbb{E}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]-\eta(1,0,X)\}+\eta(1,0,X)-\psi_{0},

where

\eta(a^{\prime},a,X)=\int\mathbb{E}[Y\mid A=a^{\prime},M,X]p(M\mid A=a,X)dM.

Based on
IF_{\psi_{0}}
, they proposed to first estimate the following nuisance functions.

•

p(A=1\mid X)
,
\forall X\in\mathcal{X}
,

•

\mathbb{E}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]
,
\forall M\in\mathcal{M},X\in\mathcal{X}
,

•

p(M\mid A=a,X)
,
\forall M\in\mathcal{M},X\in\mathcal{X}
.

Then, estimate the mediation functional by

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}^{TTS}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}\bigg[\frac{I(A=1)\hat{p}(M\mid A=0,X)}{\hat{p}(A=1\mid X)\hat{p}(M\mid A=1,X)}\{Y-\hat{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]\}

\displaystyle+\frac{I(A=0)}{1-\hat{p}(A=1\mid X)}\{\hat{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]-\hat{\eta}(1,0,X)\}+\hat{\eta}(1,0,X)\bigg],

where
\mathbb{E}_{n}
is the empirical expectation operator (treating nuisance functions as non-random), and

\hat{\eta}(1,0,X)=\int\hat{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]\hat{p}(M\mid A=0,X)dM.

The authors showed that this estimator is multiply robust in the following sense.

Proposition 1
.

\hat{\psi}^{TTS}
is unbiased if at least one of the following pairs of nuisance function estimators is correctly specified.

•

\{\hat{p}(A=1\mid X),\hat{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid A=1,M,X]\}
,

•

\{\hat{p}(A=1\mid X),\hat{p}(M\mid A=a,X)\}
,

•

\{\hat{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid A=1,M,X],\hat{p}(M\mid A=a,X)\}
.

4
Proposed Estimation Approach

We define the following nuisance functions.

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(X)=p(A=0\mid X),

\displaystyle\forall X\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(M,X)=\frac{p(M\mid A=0,X)}{p(M\mid A=1,X)},

\displaystyle\forall M\in\mathcal{M},X\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(M,X)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid A=1,M,X],

\displaystyle\forall M\in\mathcal{M},X\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(X)=\mathbb{E}[\mu_{1}(M,X)\mid A=0,X],

\displaystyle\forall X\in\mathcal{X}.

Note that the parameter of interest can be written as

\psi_{0}=\mathbb{E}[\mu_{2}(X)].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\psi}^{mr}]-\psi_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\frac{(1-A)}{\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}-1\right\}\{\mu_{2}(X)-\hat{\mu}_{2}(X)\}\right]

\displaystyle~~~+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\frac{A\hat{\pi}_{2}(M,X)}{1-\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}-\frac{(1-A)}{\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}\right\}\{\mu_{1}(M,X)-\hat{\mu}_{1}(M,X)\}\right].

5.1
Asymptotic Normality

We next provide sufficient conditions for consistency and asymptotic normality of the estimator
\hat{\psi}^{mr}
.

Assumption 2
.

(a)

\big\|\frac{p(A=0\mid X)}{\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}-1\big\|_{2}=o_{p}(1)
,

\big\|\frac{p(A=1\mid M,X)\hat{\pi}_{2}(M,X)}{1-\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}-\frac{p(A=0\mid M,X)}{\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}\big\|_{2}=o_{p}(1)
,

\|\mu_{2}(X)-\hat{\mu}_{2}(X)\|_{2}=o_{p}(1)
, and

\|\mu_{1}(M,X)-\hat{\mu}_{1}(M,X)\|_{2}=o_{p}(1)
.

(b)

\big\|\frac{p(A=0\mid X)}{\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}-1\big\|_{2}\|\mu_{2}(X)-\hat{\mu}_{2}(X)\|_{2}=o_{p}(n^{-1/2})
, and

\big\|\frac{p(A=1\mid M,X)\hat{\pi}_{2}(M,X)}{1-\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}-\frac{p(A=0\mid M,X)}{\hat{\pi}_{1}(X)}\big\|_{2}\|\mu_{1}(M,X)-\hat{\mu}_{1}(M,X)\|_{2}=o_{p}(n^{-1/2})
.

Define
O:=(X,A,M,Y)
, and for any estimates of nuisance functions
\{\tilde{\pi}_{1},\tilde{\pi}_{2},\tilde{\mu}_{1},\tilde{\mu}_{2}\}
, let

\displaystyle m(O;\tilde{\pi}_{1}

\displaystyle,\tilde{\pi}_{2},\tilde{\mu}_{1},\tilde{\mu}_{2}):=\tilde{\mu}_{2}(X)+\frac{(1-A)}{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(X)}\{\tilde{\mu}_{1}(M,X)-\tilde{\mu}_{2}(X)\}+\frac{A\tilde{\pi}_{2}(M,X)}{1-\tilde{\pi}_{1}(X)}\{Y-\tilde{\mu}_{1}(M,X)\}.

We have the following result regarding the asymptotic normality of the estimator
\hat{\psi}^{mr}
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose nuisance functions and the parameter of interest are estimated on disjoint samples with sample sizes of the same order as the total sample size. Under Assumption
2
, the estimator
\hat{\psi}^{mr}
is asymptotically normal and satisfies

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\psi}^{mr}-\psi_{0})=\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}_{n}\left[m(O;\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\right]+o_{p}(1).

As a corollary of Theorem
2
, one can use the influence function of the proposed estimator to obtain confidence intervals for the parameter of interest.

6
Conclusion

In estimating a functional of interest that involves nuisance functions, if one naively aims to perfectly fit the nuisance functions, the resulting estimator of the parameter of interest can be sensitive to even small errors in the nuisance function estimators. Therefore, in order to estimate a functional, it is important to investigate the role of nuisance functions involved in that functional in the structure of the bias. Based on this vision, we proposed a new approach for estimating the nuisance functions of the mediation functional when the influence function-based estimator is used for the parameter of interest. Our proposed estimators were based on the approach of balancing that can target the bias of the parameter of interest more directly and also can lead to an estimator which is less sensitive to error. We demonstrated that this approach retains the multiple-robustness property. Moreover, we provided conditions on our nuisance function estimators sufficient for asymptotic normality of the influence function-based estimator of the mediation functional.

Harmonic metrics of generically regular semisimple Higgs bundles on non-compact Riemann surfaces
Qiongling Li, Takuro Mochizuki

Abstract
We prove that a generically regular semisimple Higgs bundle equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing on any Riemann surface always has a harmonic metric compatible with the pairing. We also study the classification of such compatible harmonic metrics in the case where the Riemann surface is the complement of a finite set D D in a compact Riemann surface. In particular, we prove the uniqueness of a compatible harmonic metric if the Higgs bundle is wild and regular semisimple at each point of D D . MSC: 53C07, 58E15, 14D21, 81T13. Keywords: harmonic bundle, non-degenerate symmetric product, real structure

Introduction

1.1
Harmonic bundles

Let
X
be a Riemann surface.
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
be a Higgs bundle on
X
.
Let
h
be a Hermitian metric of
E
.
We obtain the Chern connection
\nabla_{h}=\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E,h}

and the adjoint
\theta^{\dagger}_{h}
of
\theta
.
The metric
h
is called a harmonic metric of
the Higgs bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

if
\nabla_{h}+\theta+\theta^{\dagger}_{h}
is flat,
i.e.,

\nabla_{h}\circ\nabla_{h}+[\theta,\theta^{\dagger}_{h}]=0
,
and
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,h)
is called a harmonic bundle.
It was introduced by Hitchin
[
5
]
,
and it has been one of the most important
and interesting mathematical objects.

A starting point is the study
of the existence and the classification of harmonic metrics.
If
X
is compact,
the following definitive theorem is most fundamental,
which is due to Hitchin
[
5
]

and Simpson
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.1
(
[
5
,
17
]
)

If
X
is compact,

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
has a harmonic metric
if and only if

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is polystable of degree
0
.
If
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are two harmonic metrics of

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
,
there exists a decomposition

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}(E_{i},\overline{\partial}_{E_{i}},\theta_{i})

such that (i) the decomposition is orthogonal
with respect to both
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
,
(ii)
h_{2|E_{i}}=a_{i}h_{1|E_{i}}
for some
a_{i}>0
.

The study in the non-compact case was pioneered by Simpson
[
17
,
18
]
,
and pursued by Biquard-Boalch
[
2
]

and the second author
[
14
]
.
Let
X
be the complement of a finite subset
D

in a compact Riemann surface
\overline{X}
.
A Higgs bundle

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
on
X

induces a coherent sheaf on the cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}X

whose support
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
is called the spectral curve of
the Higgs bundle.
The natural morphism
\Sigma_{E,\theta}\to X
is finite and flat.
The Higgs bundle is called tame
if the closure of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}

in
T^{\ast}\overline{X}(\log D)
is proper over
\overline{X}
.
The Higgs bundle is called wild
if the closure of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}

in the projective completion of

T^{\ast}\overline{X}
is complex analytic.
If a tame (resp. wild) Higgs bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

is equipped with a harmonic metric
h
,

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,h)

is called a tame (resp. wild) harmonic bundle.
From a tame (resp. wild) harmonic bundle

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,h)
,
we obtain a regular (resp. good) filtered Higgs bundle

(\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E),\theta)
on
(\overline{X},D)
.
(See §
4.2.2

for the notions of regular and good filtered Higgs bundles.
See
[
14
, §2.5]

for the notation
\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E)
.)
The following theorem was proved by Simpson
[
18
]

in the tame case
and generalized to the wild case
in
[
2
,
14
]
.

Theorem 1.2

For a wild harmonic bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,h)
on
(\overline{X},D)
,

(\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E),\theta)
is polystable of degree
0
.
Conversely, for any polystable good filtered Higgs bundle

(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)
of degree
0
on
(X,D)

such that
(\mathcal{V},\theta)_{|X\setminus D}=(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
,
there exists a harmonic metric
h
of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

such that
\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E)=\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V}
.
Moreover, if
h_{i}

(i=1,2)
be two harmonic metrics of

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
such that

\mathcal{P}^{h_{1}}_{\ast}(E)=\mathcal{P}^{h_{2}}_{\ast}(E)
,
then there exists a decomposition

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}(E_{i},\overline{\partial}_{E_{i}},\theta_{i})

as in Theorem
1.1
.

More recently,
in
[
9
,
10
]
,
we studied a different type of existence
and classification results.
Let
X
be any Riemann surface,
which is not necessarily the complement of
a finite subset in a compact Riemann surface.
Let
K_{X}
denote the canonical bundle of
X
.
Let
r\geq 2
be an integer.
We set

\mathbb{K}_{X,r}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}K_{X}^{(r+1-2i)/2}
.
For any
r
-differential
q_{r}
on
X
,
we obtain the cyclic Higgs field

\theta(q_{r})
of
\mathbb{K}_{X,r}

from
q_{r}:K_{X}^{-(r-1)/2}\to K_{X}^{(r-1)/2}\otimes K_{X}

and the identity morphisms

K_{X}^{(r+1-2i)/2}\simeq K_{X}^{(r+1-2(i+1)/2)}\otimes K_{X}
.

Theorem 1.3
(
[
9
]
)

If
q_{r}\neq 0
,

(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q_{r}))
has a harmonic metric.
More precisely,
there exists a unique harmonic metric
h^{c}

such that
(i)
\det(h^{c})=1
,
(ii) the decomposition

\mathbb{K}_{X,r}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}K_{X}^{(r+1-2i)/2}

is orthogonal,
(iii) the metrics

h^{c}_{|K_{X}^{(r+1-2i)/2}}\otimes(h^{c}_{|K_{X}^{(r+1-2(i-1))/2}})^{-1}

of
TX=K_{X}^{-1}
are complete.

Note that in general there are many other harmonic metrics

h
of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q_{r}))

satisfying the conditions (i) and (ii).
In
[
10
]
,
we studied a classification of such harmonic metrics
under the additional assumption that

X
is the complement of a finite subset
D

in a compact Riemann surface
\overline{X}
,
and that
q_{r}
has at worst multi-growth orders
at each point of
D
.
See the introductions of
[
9
,
10
]

for a brief review of previous studies on this type of harmonic bundles
and related subjects.

After
[
9
,
10
]
,
it seems reasonable to study
whether a Higgs bundle with an appropriate additional symmetry
has a harmonic metric compatible with the symmetry.
Note that such a harmonic metric is interesting
in relation with the theory of minimal surfaces.
If the base space is compact, it should be
a consequence of the existence and uniqueness theorem
due to Hitchin and Simpson
(see Theorem
1.1
).
Namely, we know the existence of a harmonic metric
of a polystable Higgs bundle of degree
0
,
and the uniqueness should imply the compatibility
of the harmonic metric with the symmetry.
(For example, see §
2.3.2
.)
If the base space is the complement of
a finite subset in a compact Riemann surface,
and if the Higgs bundle is wild,
we may still apply a similar argument
to obtain the classification of harmonic metrics.
However, in a more general situation,
we do not know a useful general result
for neither existence nor uniqueness.
We are motivated to find many examples.
Even in the wild case,
it would be helpful
if we could simplify the assumption on Higgs bundles.
We also note that the analysis of the non-compact case
would be useful for the compact case.
In this paper, we shall study Higgs bundles
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
under the assumption that
the Higgs field is generically regular semisimple.

1.2
Higgs bundles with non-degenerate symmetric product

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
be a Higgs bundle
on any Riemann surface
X
.
A non-degenerate symmetric pairing of
the Higgs bundle
is a holomorphic symmetric bilinear form

C:E\otimes E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

such that

C(\theta\otimes\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits)=C(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits\otimes\theta)
.
When
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is equipped with
a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
,
we say that a harmonic metric
h
is compatible with
C

if the induced morphism

\Psi_{C}:E\to E^{\lor}

is isometric with respect to
h
and
h^{\lor}
.
In that case,
the harmonic map
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is induced
by a
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits(r,{\mathbb{R}})
-harmonic bundle,
where
r=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits E
.

We shall also impose the following generic regular semisimplicity
condition.

•

There exists
Q\in X

such that the fiber of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}\to X

over
Q
consists of exactly
r
points.

Remark 1.4

From the definition,
it is clear that a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
is
generically regular semisimple
if one of the following holds:
(i)
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(\theta^{r})\neq 0

and
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(\theta^{k})=0

(k=1,\ldots,r-1)
;
(ii)
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(\theta^{r-1})\neq 0

and
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(\theta^{k})=0

(k=1,\ldots,r-2,r)
,
where
r=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits E
.
We also remark that
most Higgs bundles
are generically regular semisimple.
For example, a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
is
generically regular semisimple
if one of the following holds:
(i) the characteristic polynomial of
(E,\theta)
is irreducible;
(ii)
E
has no proper subbundle preserved by
\theta
.
See
§
2.4

(Proposition
2.42
and
Proposition
2.43
)
for more details.

We shall prove the following general theorem.

Theorem 1.5
(Theorem

2.34
)

Suppose that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
generically regular semisimple
and equipped
with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
.
Then,
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

has a harmonic metric compatible with
C
.

In the compact case,
it follows from Theorem
1.1
,
but note that we do not have to assume
that the Higgs bundle is polystable of degree
0
.
For the proof of Theorem
1.5

in the non-compact case,
we use a method developed in
[
10
]

together with a key estimate for harmonic metrics
compatible with a non-degenerate symmetric product
(Proposition
2.37
).
It follows from Simpson’s main estimate (
[
18
,
19
]
)
on the norm of the Higgs field
with the aid of
a linear algebraic argument (Proposition
2.15
).

Remark 1.6

We can obtain another a priori estimate
for harmonic metrics compatible with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
from a variant of Simpson’s main estimate

[
18
,
13
]

and a linear algebraic argument.
It is useful even in the study of Higgs bundles
on compact Riemann surfaces
which are not necessarily equipped with
a global non-degenerate symmetric pairing.
In
[
16
]
,
the estimate is applied to study
large-scale solutions of Hitchin equations.
Non-degenerate symmetric pairings are also
useful in the study of Hitchin metric
of the moduli space of Higgs bundles

[
15
]
.

In particular,
suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is cyclic,
that is,
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is
a direct sum of holomorphic line bundles
L_{k}

(k=1,\ldots,r)

and
\theta
takes
L_{k}
to

L_{k+1}\otimes K_{X}

(k=1,\ldots,r)
where
L_{r+1}:=L_{1}
.
Then, if
\det(\theta)\neq 0
,

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
obviously generically regular semisimple.
We obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.7

Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
a cyclic Higgs bundle on
X
satisfying
\det\theta\neq 0

and equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
.
Then,

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
has a harmonic metric compatible with
C
.

If
X
is compact,
the assumptions imply that

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is polystable of degree
0
,
and we can easily deduce Theorem
1.5

from Theorem
1.1
.
(See §
2.3.2
.)
We can also obtain the uniqueness of
such compatible harmonic metrics in the compact case.
In the non-compact case,
the uniqueness does not hold, in general.

We also study more detailed classification
in the case where
X
is the complement of
a finite subset
D
in a compact Riemann surface
\overline{X}
.
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
be
a generically regular semisimple Higgs bundle on
X

which is wild at each point of
D
.
Suppose that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is equipped with
a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
.

Theorem 1.8
(Theorem

4.15
)

If
h
is a harmonic metric of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

compatible with
C
,
then the induced symmetric pairing

C
of
\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E)
is perfect.
Conversely,
for any good filtered Higgs bundle

(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)
on
(\overline{X},D)

equipped with a perfect pairing
C_{\mathcal{V}}

such that
(\mathcal{V},\theta,C_{\mathcal{V}})_{|X}=(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta,C)
,
there exists a unique harmonic metric
h

of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
compatible with
C

satisfying
the boundary condition
\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E)=\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V}
.

We remark that in Theorem
1.8
,
we do not have to assume that

(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)
is polystable of degree
0

because it follows from the existence of a perfect pairing.

Let us mention a condition under which
we can prove the uniqueness of a compatible harmonic metric
without the boundary condition.
For each
P\in D
,
let
(U_{P},z)
be a holomorphic coordinate neighbourhood around
P

such that
z(P)=0
.
We can regard
U_{P}
as an open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
.
For
e\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
let
\varphi_{e}:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the map
defined by
\varphi_{e}(\zeta)=\zeta^{e}
.
We set
U^{(e)}_{P}:=\varphi_{e}^{-1}(U_{P})
.
We obtain the endomorphism

f^{(e)}_{P}
of

\varphi_{e}^{\ast}(E)_{|U^{(e)}_{P}\setminus\{0\}}

defined by

f^{(e)}_{P}\,d\zeta/\zeta=\varphi_{e}^{\ast}(\theta)
.
If
U
is sufficiently small,
and if
e=r!
,
there exist holomorphic functions

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
on
U^{(e)}\setminus\{0\}

and the eigen decomposition

\varphi_{e}^{\ast}(E)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}

such that
(i)
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}

(i\neq j)
are nowhere vanishing
on
U^{(e)}\setminus\{0\}
,
(ii)
f^{(e)}-\alpha_{i}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E_{i}}

is
0
on
E_{\alpha_{i}}
,
(iii)
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits E_{i}=1
.
We say that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is regular semisimple at
P

if
(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}

(i\neq j)

are holomorphic at
0
.

Theorem 1.9
(Theorem
4.16
)

Suppose
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is generically regular semisimple on
X
which is wild at each point of
D
and equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
. If
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
regular semisimple at each point of
D
, there exists a unique
harmonic metric on

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
compatible with
C
.

1.3
Hitchin section

Our main examples are Higgs bundles
contained in the Hitchin section
[
7
]
,
which we recall by following
[
8
]
.
Let
q_{j}

(j=2,\ldots,r)
be
holomorphic
j
-differentials on
X
.
The multiplication of
q_{j}
induces the following morphisms:

K_{X}^{(r-2i+1)/2}\to K_{X}^{(r-2i+2(j-1)+1)/2}\otimes K_{X}\quad(j\leq i\leq r).

We also have the multiplications of
i(r-i)/2

for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
:

K_{X}^{(r-2i+1)/2}\to K_{X}^{(r-2(i+1)+1)/2}\otimes K_{X}.

They define a Higgs field

\theta(\boldsymbol{q})
of
\mathbb{K}_{X,r}
.
The natural pairings

K_{X}^{(r-2i+1)/2}\otimes K_{X}^{-(r-2i+1)/2}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

induce a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

C_{\mathbb{K},X,r}
of

\mathbb{K}_{X,r}
.
It is a non-degenerate symmetric pairing of

(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(\boldsymbol{q}))
.
The following result is a partial affirmative answer
to
[
20
, Question A]
.

Corollary 1.10
(Corollary

4.18
)

If the Higgs bundle
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(\boldsymbol{q}))

is generically regular semisimple,
then there exists a harmonic metric
of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(\boldsymbol{q}))

which is compatible with
C_{\mathbb{K},X,r}
.
It is induced by an
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits(r,{\mathbb{R}})
-harmonic bundle.

In the other extreme case
q_{i}=0

(i=2,\ldots,r)
,

(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(\boldsymbol{q}))

has a harmonic metric
if and only if
X
is hyperbolic.
For example,

(\mathbb{K}_{{\mathbb{C}},r},\theta(0,\ldots,0))

does not have a harmonic metric.
(See
[
9
, Lemma 3.13]
.)

See §
4.3.1
–§
4.3.3

for more concrete examples.
In §
4.3.1
,
we explain an example for which
Theorem
1.9
ensures the uniqueness of harmonic metrics.
In §
4.3.2
,
we explain an example for which
the uniqueness does not hold.

Remark 1.11

In
[
11
]
,
by using different techniques,
we shall establish that the Higgs bundle
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(\boldsymbol{q}))

has a harmonic metric for any
\boldsymbol{q}
if
X
is hyperbolic.
We shall also study the existence of harmonic metrics
of Higgs bundles in

[

]

1.4
Higher dimensional case

Though we do not study the higher dimensional case in this paper,
some part of the theory can be easily generalized.
We give only a brief sketch of the statements without proof.
More details will be explained elsewhere.

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
be a Higgs bundle of rank
r
on
X
.
It naturally induces a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{T^{\ast}X}
-module
whose support
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
is called the spectral variety.
The Higgs bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is called
generically regular semisimple
if there exists
Q\in X
such that
the fiber of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}\to X
over
Q

consists of exactly
r
points.
A non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
of
E

is called a non-degenerate symmetric pairing of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

if
\theta
is self-adjoint with respect to
C
.
Note that the existence of
C

implies that
c_{1}(E)=0
in
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Let us consider
a generically regular semisimple Higgs bundle

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
on
X

equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
.
If
X
is compact Kähler,
we can prove that

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is polystable.
By the theory of Simpson
[
17
]
,
we can prove that there exists
a unique Hermitian-Einstein metric
h
of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

which is compatible with
C
.
If moreover
\mathop{\rm ch}\nolimits_{2}(E)=0
,
h
is pluri-harmonic.
In this sense,
Theorem
1.5

can be generalized if
X
is compact Kähler.

Suppose that there exists a complex projective manifold
\overline{X}

with a simple normal crossing hypersurface
D

such that
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
.
If
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is good wild along
D

and equipped with a pluri-harmonic metric compatible with
C
,
then
C
induces a perfect pairing of
the associated good filtered Higgs bundle

(\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}(E),\theta)
.
Conversely, suppose that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
and
C

extends to
a good filtered Higgs bundle

(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)
with a perfect pairing
C_{\mathcal{V}}

such that
\mathop{\rm ch}\nolimits_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V})=0
,
then there exists a unique pluri-harmonic metric

h
of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
compatible with
C

such that
\mathcal{P}^{h}_{\ast}E=\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V}
.
In this sense, Theorem
1.8

can be generalized.

We can generalize the regular semisimplicity condition
for a good wild Higgs bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
on
(X,D)

at
P\in D
.
If it is satisfied at any point of
D
,
there exists a unique good filtered extension

(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)
of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)

such that
C
induces a perfect pairing of

(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)
.
If
\mathop{\rm ch}\nolimits_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V})=0
,
then
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
has a unique pluri-harmonic
metric compatible with
C
.
If
\mathop{\rm ch}\nolimits_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V})\neq 0
,
then
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
does not have
a pluri-harmonic metric compatible with
C
.
In this sense, Theorem
1.9
can be generalized.

2
Harmonic bundles with real structure

2.1
Real structures and symmetric pairings of vector spaces

2.1.1
Real structures of vector spaces

For complex vector spaces
V_{i}

(i=1,2)
,
an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear morphism
f:V_{1}\to V_{2}

is called sesqui-linear
if
f(\alpha v)=\overline{\alpha}f(v)

for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
v\in V_{1}
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space.
A real structure of
V
is
a sesqui-linear morphism
\kappa:V\to V

such that
\kappa\circ\kappa=\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{V}
.
When a real structure
\kappa
is provided with
V
,
we set

V_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}:=\{v\in V\,|\,\kappa(v)=v\}
.
It is naturally an
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space.
The naturally induced morphism

{\mathbb{C}}\otimes V_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}\to V
is an isomorphism
of complex vector spaces.
We have

\kappa(\alpha\otimes v_{{\mathbb{R}}})=\overline{\alpha}\otimes v_{{\mathbb{R}}}

under the identification.

Let
C_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}
be a positive definite
symmetric bilinear form on
V_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}
.
It extends to a symmetric bilinear form
C

and a Hermitian metric
h

on
V

in the standard ways:

C(\alpha\otimes u_{{\mathbb{R}}},\beta\otimes v_{{\mathbb{R}}})=\alpha\beta C_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}(u_{{\mathbb{R}}},v_{{\mathbb{R}}}),\quad\quad h(\alpha\otimes u_{{\mathbb{R}}},\beta\otimes v_{{\mathbb{R}}})=\alpha\overline{\beta}C_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}(u_{{\mathbb{R}}},v_{{\mathbb{R}}}).

(1)

Here,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}

and
u_{{\mathbb{R}}},v_{{\mathbb{R}}}\in V_{{\mathbb{R}},\kappa}
.
We have

C(\kappa(u),\kappa(v))=\overline{C(u,v)}

and

h(\kappa(u),\kappa(v))=\overline{h(u,v)}=h(v,u)
.

2.1.2
Compatibility of symmetric pairings and Hermitian metrics

Let us recall the notion of compatibility of
a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
and a Hermitian metric
on a complex vector space
V
.
Let
V^{\lor}
denote the dual space of
V
.
Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V^{\lor}\times V\to{\mathbb{C}}

denote the canonical pairing.

Let

be a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form.
We obtain the linear isomorphism

\Psi_{C}:V\simeq V^{\lor}

by

\langle\Psi_{C}(u),v\rangle=C(u,v)
.
We obtain the symmetric bilinear form

C^{\lor}:V^{\lor}\times V^{\lor}\to{\mathbb{C}}

by

C^{\lor}(u^{\lor},v^{\lor})=C(\Psi_{C}^{-1}(u^{\lor}),\Psi_{C}^{-1}(v^{\lor})).

We have
\Psi_{C^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{C}=\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{V}
.

Let
h
be a Hermitian metric of
V
.
We obtain the sesqui-linear isomorphism

\Psi_{h}:V\simeq V^{\lor}

by

\langle\Psi_{h}(u),v\rangle=h(v,u)
.
We obtain the Hermitian metric
h^{\lor}
of
V^{\lor}

by

h^{\lor}(u^{\lor},v^{\lor})=h\bigl(\Psi_{h}^{-1}(v^{\lor}),\Psi_{h}^{-1}(u^{\lor})\bigr).

It is easy to see that

\Psi_{h^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{h}=\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{V}
.

Definition 2.1

We say that
h
is compatible with
C

if
\Psi_{C}
is isometric with respect to
h
and
h^{\lor}
.
Let
\mathop{\rm Herm}\nolimits(V,C)
denote the space of Hermitian metrics
of

which are compatible with
C
.

Lemma 2.2

The following conditions are equivalent.

•

h
is compatible with
C
.

•

C(u,v)=\overline{C^{\lor}(\Psi_{h}(u),\Psi_{h}(v))}
holds
for any
u,v\in V
.

•

\Psi_{C^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{h}=\Psi_{h^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{C}
holds.
It is also equivalent to

\Psi_{C}\circ\Psi_{h^{\lor}}=\Psi_{h}\circ\Psi_{C^{\lor}}
.

Proof
Let
f\in V^{\lor}
and
u\in V
.
We have

\langle f,\Psi_{C^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{h}(u)\rangle=C^{\lor}(f,\Psi_{h}(u))=\langle\Psi_{h}(u),\Psi_{C}^{\lor}(f)\rangle=h(\Psi_{C}^{-1}(f),u).

We also have

\langle f,\Psi_{h^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{C}(u)\rangle=h^{\lor}(f,\Psi_{C}(u))=\overline{h^{\lor}(\Psi_{C}(u),f)}=\overline{\langle\Psi_{h^{\lor}}(f),\Psi_{C}(u)\rangle}=\overline{C(u,\Psi_{h}^{-1}(f))}.

Hence, we obtain the claim of Lemma
2.2
.

From a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
C

and a Hermitian metric

,
we obtain a sesqui-linear isomorphism

\kappa=\Psi_{C^{\lor}}\circ\Psi_{h}
.

Lemma 2.3

h

.

Proof

.
Hence, the claim follows from Lemma

Lemma 2.4

Suppose that

is compatible with

.

•

•

2.4
Some sufficient conditions
for generically regular semisimplicity

2.4.1
Spectral curves

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
be a Higgs bundle
of rank
r
on a Riemann surface
X
.
We may regard
E
as a module over
the sheaf of algebras
\mathop{\rm Sym}\nolimits\Theta_{X}
,
where
\Theta_{X}
denote the tangent sheaf of
X
.
There exists the coherent
\mathcal{O}_{T^{\ast}X}
-module

\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)

with an isomorphism of
\mathop{\rm Sym}\nolimits\Theta_{X}
-modules

\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)\simeq E

where
\pi:T^{\ast}X\to X
denote the projection.
The support
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
of

\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)
is called the spectral curve of
(E,\theta)
.
(See
[
1
,
6
]
.)
Let us recall some related notions in a way convenient to us.

For any
P\in X
,
let
\iota_{P}:T^{\ast}_{P}X\to T^{\ast}X
denote the inclusion.
We obtain the
\mathcal{O}_{T^{\ast}_{P}X}
-module

\iota_{P}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)
.
We have the decomposition by the supports

\iota_{P}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)=\bigoplus_{Q\in T^{\ast}_{P}X\cap\Sigma_{E,\theta}}\iota_{P}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)_{Q},

where the support of
\iota_{P}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)_{Q}

is
\{Q\}
.
Each
\iota_{P}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)_{Q}

is naturally a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space.
We set
\mathfrak{m}(Q):=\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\iota_{P}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(E,\theta)_{Q}
.
We obtain a map

\mathfrak{m}:\Sigma_{E,\theta}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
We have
\sum_{\pi(Q)=P}\mathfrak{m}(Q)=r
for any
P\in X
.
In particular,
we obtain

|\Sigma_{E,\theta}\cap T_{P}^{\ast}X|\leq r
.
We set

n(E,\theta)=\max_{P\in X}|\Sigma(E,\theta)\cap T_{P}^{\ast}X|\leq r
.
The generically regular semisimplicity condition
for
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is equivalent to
n(E,\theta)=r
.

We set

D(E,\theta):=\bigl\{P\in X\,\big|\,|\Sigma_{E,\theta}\cap T_{P}^{\ast}X|<n(E,\theta)\bigr\}
.
It is easy to see that
D(E,\theta)

is discrete in
X
.
We set

\Sigma_{E,\theta}^{\circ}:=\Sigma_{E,\theta}\setminus\pi^{-1}(D(E,\theta))
.
It is a closed complex submanifold of

T^{\ast}(X\setminus D(E,\theta))
,
and the projection

\Sigma_{E,\theta}^{\circ}\to X\setminus D(E,\theta)

is a local homeomorphism.
The function
\mathfrak{m}
is locally constant
on
\Sigma_{E,\theta}^{\circ}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
We set

(\Sigma_{E,\theta}^{\circ})_{k}:=\Sigma_{E,\theta}^{\circ}\cap\mathfrak{m}^{-1}(k)
.
Let
(\Sigma_{E,\theta})_{k}

denote the closure of
(\Sigma_{E,\theta}^{\circ})_{k}

in
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
.
We have

\Sigma_{E,\theta}=\bigcup_{k}(\Sigma_{E,\theta})_{k}
.

A union of
some irreducible components of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}

is called a spectral subcurve of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
.
For example,
(\Sigma_{E,\theta})_{k}

are spectral subcurves.
A spectral subcurve of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}

is a purely
1
-dimensional complex analytic closed subset
of
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
.
For a spectral subcurve
\Sigma_{1}
,
we set

\Sigma_{1}^{\circ}:=\Sigma_{1}\setminus\pi^{-1}(D(E,\theta))
.
It is easy to see that
(i)
\Sigma_{1}^{\circ}
is a smooth complex submanifold of

T^{\ast}(X\setminus D(E,\theta))
,
(ii)
\Sigma_{1}^{\circ}\to X\setminus D(E,\theta)
is a local homeomorphism,
(iii)
\Sigma_{1}
is the closure of
\Sigma_{1}^{\circ}
in
T^{\ast}X
.
For any
P\in X\setminus D(E,\theta)
,
we set
n(\Sigma_{1}):=\bigl|\Sigma_{1}\cap T_{P}^{\ast}X\bigr|

and
r(\Sigma_{1}):=\sum_{Q\in T_{P}^{\ast}X\cap\Sigma_{1}}\mathfrak{m}(Q)
,
which are independent of the choice of
P
.
In particular,
we set

n(k):=n((\Sigma_{E,\theta})_{k})

and

r(k):=r((\Sigma_{E,\theta})_{k})
.
Note that
r(k)=k\cdot n(k)
.

2.4.2
Characteristic polynomials and
some Higgs subsheaves in the local case

Let
(U,z)
be a connected holomorphic chart of
X
,
i.e.,
U
is a connected open subset of
X
,
and
z
is a holomorphic coordinate on
U
.
Let
f_{U}
be the endomorphism of
E_{|U}

defined by
\theta=f_{U}\,dz
.
We obtain the characteristic polynomial

\det(T\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E_{|U}}-f_{U})=\sum_{j=0}^{r}a_{j}(z)T^{j}

which is a monic.
Then, the polynomial

P_{E,\theta,U}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}a_{j}(z)(dz)^{r-j}T^{j}\in\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r}H^{0}(U,K_{U}^{r-j})T^{j}

is independent of the choice of a coordinate
z
on
U
.
It is called the characteristic polynomial of

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)_{|U}
.
Under the isomorphism

U\times{\mathbb{C}}\simeq T^{\ast}U
induced by

(z,T)\longmapsto(z,T\,dz)
,
the spectral curve

\Sigma_{E,\theta}\cap T^{\ast}U

of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)_{|U}

is equal to

\{(z,T)\in U\times{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\sum a_{j}(z)T^{j}=0\}
.
For
(z_{0},\alpha\,dz)\in\Sigma_{E,\theta}\cap T^{\ast}U
,

\mathfrak{m}(z_{0},\alpha\,dz)
is equal to
the multiplicity of
the root
\alpha
of the polynomial
\sum a_{j}(z_{0})T^{j}
.

Let
\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{E,\theta}\cap T^{\ast}U

be a spectral subcurve.
For
z_{0}\in U\setminus D(E,\theta)
, we obtain the complex numbers

c_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}(z_{0})

(0\leq j\leq n(\Sigma_{1}))

and

\widetilde{c}_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}(z_{0})

(0\leq j\leq r(\Sigma_{1}))

by setting

\sum_{j=0}^{n(\Sigma_{1})}c_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}(z_{0})T^{j}=\prod_{(z_{0},\alpha\,dz)\in\Sigma_{1}}(T-\alpha),\quad\quad\sum_{j=0}^{r(\Sigma_{1})}\widetilde{c}_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}(z_{0})T^{j}=\prod_{(z_{0},\alpha\,dz)\in\Sigma_{1}}(T-\alpha)^{\mathfrak{m}(z_{0},\alpha\,dz)}.

We obtain holomorphic functions

c_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}
and
\widetilde{c}_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}

on
U\setminus D(E,\theta)
.
Because the eigenvalues of
f_{U}
are locally bounded,
they extend to holomorphic functions on
U
,
which are also denoted by

c_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}
and
\widetilde{c}_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}
.
We obtain the following endomorphisms
of the
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-module
E_{|U}
:

F^{\Sigma_{1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n(\Sigma_{1})}c^{\Sigma_{1}}_{j}f_{U}^{j},\quad\quad\widetilde{F}^{\Sigma_{1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{r(\Sigma_{1})}\widetilde{c}^{\Sigma_{1}}_{j}f_{U}^{j}.

We obtain the
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-submodules

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},0)=\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits F^{\Sigma_{1}}

and

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1)=\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits\widetilde{F}^{\Sigma_{1}}
.
Note that
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)\neq 0

(i=0,1)
.

Lemma 2.39

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)

(i=0,1)

are holomorphic subbundles of
E_{|U}
,
i.e.,

E_{|U}\big/\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)

(i=0,1)

are torsion-free
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-modules.
We also have

f_{U}(\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i))\subset\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)
.

Proof
We obtain the first claim from

E_{|U}\big/\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},0)\simeq\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits F^{\Sigma_{1}}\subset E_{|U}

and

E_{|U}\big/\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1)\simeq\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\widetilde{F}^{\Sigma_{1}}\subset E_{|U}
.
We obtain the second claim from the commutativity

[f,F^{\Sigma_{1}}]=[f,\widetilde{F}^{\Sigma_{1}}]=0
.

In particular,

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)
are Higgs subbundles
of
(E,\theta)_{|U}
.
Let
\theta(\Sigma_{1},i)
denote the Higgs field
of
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)
induced by
\theta_{|U}
.

Lemma 2.40

We have

\det(T\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1)}-f_{U|\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1)})=\sum_{j=0}^{r(\Sigma_{1})}\widetilde{c}^{\Sigma_{1}}_{j}T^{j}
.
As a result, we obtain

P_{\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1),\theta(\Sigma_{1},1)}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{r(\Sigma_{1})}\widetilde{c}^{\Sigma_{1}}_{j}(dz)^{r(\Sigma_{1})-j}T^{j}
.

Proof
It is easy to see

\det(T\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1)}-f_{U|\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},1)})_{|U\setminus D(E,\theta)}=\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{r(\Sigma_{1})}\widetilde{c}^{\Sigma_{1}}_{j}T^{j}\Bigr)_{|U\setminus D(E,\theta)}
.
Then, we obtain the claim of the lemma.

Remark 2.41

The rank of
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},0)
is not necessarily
equal to
n(\Sigma_{1})
.
The characteristic polynomial of

f_{U|\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},0)}
is not necessarily equal to

\sum_{j=0}^{n(\Sigma_{1})}c_{j}^{\Sigma_{1}}T^{j}
.

We set
\Sigma_{E,\theta,k,U}:=(\Sigma_{E,\theta})_{k}\cap T^{\ast}U

and

P_{E,\theta,k,U}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{n(k)}c^{\Sigma_{E,\theta,k,U}}_{j}(z)(dz)^{n(k)-j}T^{j}.

Note that by definition,

P_{\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{E,\theta,k,U},1),\theta(\Sigma_{E,\theta,k,U},1),U}(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{r(k)}\widetilde{c}^{\Sigma_{E,\theta,k,U}}_{j}(z)(dz)^{r(k)-j}T^{j}=P_{E,\theta,k,U}(T)^{k}.

We then have the factorization

P_{E,\theta,U}(T)=\prod_{k\geq 1}P_{E,\theta,k,U}(T)^{k}
.

2.4.3
Some sufficient conditions for
generically regular semisimplicity

There uniquely exists

P_{E,\theta}(T)\in\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{r-j})T^{j}\quad\left(\mbox{{r}esp. }P_{E,\theta,k}(T)\in\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n(k)}H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{n(k)-j})T^{j}\right)

such that the restriction of
P_{E,\theta}(T)

(resp.
P_{E,\theta,k}(T)
) to
U

is
P_{E,\theta,U}(T)

(resp.
P_{E,\theta,k,U}(T)
)
for any holomorphic chart
(U,z)
.
We have the factorization

P_{E,\theta}(T)=\prod_{k\geq 1}P_{E,\theta,k}(T)^{k}
.
The polynomial
P_{E,\theta}(T)
is called
the characteristic polynomial of
the Higgs bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
.

Proposition 2.42

The Higgs bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is generically regular semisimple
if the characteristic polynomial is irreducible.

Proof
The irreducibility of

P_{E,\theta}(T)
implies that

P_{E,\theta}(T)=P_{E,\theta,1}(T)
.
Hence,
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
generically regular semisimple.

Proposition 2.43

Suppose that

(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
has no proper Higgs subbundle.
Then, the characteristic polynomial

P_{E,\theta}
is irreducible.
In particular,
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},\theta)
is
generically regular semisimple.

Proof
Let

\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{E,\theta}

be a non-empty spectral subcurve.
We obtain non-zero Higgs subbundles

(\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i),\theta(\Sigma_{1},i))

of
(E,\theta)

such that

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1},i)_{|U}=\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1}\cap\pi^{-1}(U),i)

for any holomorphic chart
(U,z)
,
where
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{1}\cap\pi^{-1}(U),i)

are as in Lemma
2.39
.
Because

3.1
Pairings of filtered bundles

3.1.1
Pairings of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-modules

Let
X
be a Riemann surface.
Let
D\subset X
be a discrete subset.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
denote the sheaf of
meromorphic functions on
X
whose poles are contained in
D
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-module
of finite rank.
Let
\mathcal{V}^{\lor}
denote the dual of
\mathcal{V}
,
i.e.,

\mathcal{V}^{\lor}={\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)).

The determinant bundle of
\mathcal{V}
is denoted by

\det(\mathcal{V})
,
i.e.,

\det(\mathcal{V})=\bigwedge^{\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{V}
.
There exists a natural isomorphism

\det(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})\simeq\det(\mathcal{V})^{\lor}
.
For a morphism
f:\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{2}

of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-modules,

f^{\lor}:\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\lor}\to\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\lor}

denotes the dual of
f
.
If
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{V}_{1}=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{V}_{2}
,

\det(f):\det(\mathcal{V}_{1})\to\det(\mathcal{V}_{2})

denotes the induced morphism.

For locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-modules
\mathcal{V}_{i}

(i=1,2)
,
a pairing
C
of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}

is a morphism

C:\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)}\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
.
It induces a morphism

\Psi_{C}:\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\lor}

by
\Psi_{C}(u)(v)=C(u\otimes v)
.
Let

\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits:\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{2}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{1}

denote the natural morphism defined by

\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})=v_{2}\otimes v_{1}
.
We obtain the pairing

C\circ\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits:\mathcal{V}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
.
We have

\Psi_{C\circ\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits}=\Psi_{C}^{\lor}
.
If
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{V}_{1}=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits\mathcal{V}_{2}
,
we obtain the induced pairing

\det(C):\det(\mathcal{V}_{1})\otimes\det(\mathcal{V}_{2})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
.
We have
\det(\Psi_{C})=\Psi_{\det(C)}
.

A pairing
C
is called non-degenerate if
\Psi_{C}
is an isomorphism.
It is equivalent to the condition that

C\circ\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits
is non-degenerate.
It is also equivalent to the condition that

\det(C)
is non-degenerate.
If
C
is non-degenerate,
we obtain the pairing

C^{\lor}
of
\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\lor}
and
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\lor}

defined by

C^{\lor}=C\circ\bigl(\Psi_{C}^{-1}\otimes\Psi_{C\circ\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits}^{-1}\bigr)
,
i.e., the composition of the following morphism:

\begin{CD}\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\lor}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\lor}@>{\Psi_{C}^{-1}\otimes\Psi_{C\circ\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits}^{-1}}>{}>\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{2}@>{C}>{}>\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D).\end{CD}

A pairing
C
of a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-module
\mathcal{V}

is a morphism
C:\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
,
i.e., a pairing of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}
.
It is called symmetric if
C\circ\mathop{\rm ex}\nolimits=C
,
i.e.,

C(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})=C(v_{2}\otimes v_{1})

for any local sections
v_{i}
of
\mathcal{V}
.
We obtain the induced pairing

\det(C)
of
\det(\mathcal{V})
.
If
C
is non-degenerate,
we obtain the induced pairing

C^{\lor}
of
\mathcal{V}^{\lor}
.

Filtered bundles

Let us recall the notion of filtered bundles
by following

17
,
18
]

For any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
and

let
\mathcal{F}_{Q}
denote the stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at

.
In this subsection,

\mathcal{V}
and

(i=1,2)

For

,

over
\mathcal{V}_{P}

(iii) for any

\mathcal{P}_{a+n}(\mathcal{V}_{P})=\mathcal{P}_{a}(\mathcal{V}_{P})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X,P}(n\cdot P)

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}_{P})

(P\in D)

is provided,

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules

.
We also say that

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})
is a filtered bundle on
(X,D)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{V}
be a locally free

\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-submodule of
\mathcal{V}
.

over
\mathcal{V}

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
over
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
.

When filtered bundles
\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}_{i})

(i=1,2)

over
\mathcal{V}_{i}
are provided,
a morphism

\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)
-homomorphism

and

.

When filtered bundles
\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
over
\mathcal{V}_{i}

are provided,
for any

and

\mathcal{P}_{a}\bigl((\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2})_{P}\bigr)=\mathcal{P}_{a}\mathcal{V}_{1,P}\oplus\mathcal{P}_{a}\mathcal{V}_{2,P},\quad\mathcal{P}_{a}\bigl((\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{2})_{P}\bigr)=\sum_{c_{1}+c_{2}\leq a}\mathcal{P}_{c_{1}}(\mathcal{V}_{1,P})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{c_{2}}(\mathcal{V}_{2,P}).

\mathcal{P}_{a}\bigl({\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2})_{P}\bigr)=\bigl\{f\in{\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(\ast D)_{P}}(\mathcal{V}_{1,P},\mathcal{V}_{2,P})\,\big|\,f(\mathcal{P}_{b}(\mathcal{V}_{1,P}))\subset\mathcal{P}_{b+a}(\mathcal{V}_{2,P})\,\,\forall b\in{\mathbb{R}}\bigr\}.

we obtain filtered bundles

over
\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2}

{\mathcal{H}om}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2})

We obtain the numbers

Let

-submodules

By the definition, we obtain

Because
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathcal{V}_{2}^{\prime}(-\sum m(Q)Q)

and

Proof

(15)

\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}

for some

({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\times\bigl(({\mathbb{C}}^{2})\setminus\{(0,0)\}\bigr)
.

Proof
Let
L_{i}

(i=1,2)
be the image of

\mathcal{U}
by the projection to
\mathcal{O}_{U}(\ast 0)\cdot e_{i}
.
There exists
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that

L_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{U}\cdot z^{-n_{i}}e_{i}
.
There exists
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}

and a holomorphic function
\beta
on
U

such that
(i)
\beta(0)\neq 0
,
(ii)
\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}}\beta e_{2}

is contained in
\mathcal{U}
.
Because
\theta
is logarithmic with respect to
\mathcal{U}
,

\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+1}e_{1}-\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+1}\beta e_{2}

is also contained in
\mathcal{U}
.
We obtain
z\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}\subset\mathcal{U}
,
and hence

\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}\subset\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}
.
It implies

\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})},\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}
.

Lemma 4.23

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a logarithmic lattice.
If
z\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}\subsetneq\mathcal{U}\subsetneq\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}

for some
{\boldsymbol{n}}
,
then there exists
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}

such that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}
.
If
z\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}\subsetneq\mathcal{U}\subsetneq\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}

for some
({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}
,
then we have

\mathcal{U}=z\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}
.

Proof
The first claim is clear.
Let us study the second claim.
Let
L\subset\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}_{|0}

be the image of
\mathcal{U}
.
Because
\mathcal{U}
is a logarithmic lattice,

L
is preserved by
\mathop{\rm Res}\nolimits_{\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}}(\theta)
.
Then, the second claim follows.

4.4.2
Regular filtered Higgs bundles

Let
({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\})
.
For
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
let
\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

and
\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

be the filtered bundles over
\mathcal{V}

defined as follows
(c\in{\mathbb{R}})
:

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{c}(\mathcal{V})=z^{-[c-b]}\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})},\quad\quad\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{c}(\mathcal{V})=z^{-[c-b]}\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}.

Here,
[d]:=\max\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,|\,n\leq d\}
.
For
{\boldsymbol{b}}=(b_{1},b_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

such that
b_{1}-1<b_{2}<b_{1}
,
let
\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

be the filtered bundle defined as follows
(
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
):

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{c}(\mathcal{V})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z^{-m}\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}&(m+b_{2}\leq c<m+b_{1})\\
\\
z^{-m}\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}&(m+b_{1}\leq c<m+1+b_{2}).\end{array}\right.

We set
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}=(1,1)
.
We can check the following lemma directly from the definitions.

Lemma 4.24

The following holds for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-m{\boldsymbol{\delta}};b-m)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}),\quad\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-m{\boldsymbol{\delta}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b-m)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}),\quad\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-m{\boldsymbol{\delta}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}}-m{\boldsymbol{\delta}})}.

The following holds:

\mathcal{P}^{((n_{1},n_{2}),(\alpha_{1},0);b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{((n_{1},n_{2}-1);b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}),\quad\mathcal{P}^{((n_{1},n_{2}),(0,\alpha_{2});b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{((n_{1}-1,n_{2});b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}).

The following holds:

\mathcal{P}^{((n_{1},n_{2}),(\alpha_{1},0);(b_{1},b_{2}))}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{((n_{1},n_{2}-1),(0,\alpha_{2});(b_{2},b_{1}-1))}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}).

We also have

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},\gamma{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

and

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},\gamma{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}
.

Proposition 4.25

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})
be a filtered bundle
such that
(\mathcal{P}_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\theta)

is a regular filtered Higgs bundle.
Then,
\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

equals one of

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})
,

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

or

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})
.

Proof
If there exists
-1<b\leq 0

such that

\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits^{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\mathcal{V})=0

unless
c-b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

equals either

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

or
\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})
.
If not,
there exists
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

such that (i)
b_{1}-1<b_{2}<b_{1}
,
(ii)

\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits^{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(\mathcal{V})=0

if
c-b_{i}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(i=1,2)
.
By Lemma
4.23
,
one of
\mathcal{P}_{b_{i}}(\mathcal{V})

is of the form
\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}
for some
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
By exchanging
(b_{1},b_{2})
with
(b_{2},b_{1}-1)
if necessary,
we may assume that

\mathcal{P}_{b_{1}}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}
.
We obtain
\mathcal{P}_{b_{2}}({\boldsymbol{v}})=\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}

for some
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
.
Then, we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})
.

4.4.3
Compatibility with the symmetric form

Proposition 4.26

Let
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(I)

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if

n_{1}-\frac{m_{1}}{2}=n_{2}-\frac{m_{2}}{2}=b
.

(II)

If
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}
,

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if

n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}=b+\frac{1}{2}

and

\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=0
.

(III-1)

If
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}
,

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if

n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{2})

and

\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=0
.

(III-2)

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},(0,\alpha_{2});{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if

n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=b_{1}
and

n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}=b_{2}
.

Proof
As a preliminary, let us study the dual lattices.
Let
e_{1}^{\lor},e_{2}^{\lor}

be the dual frame of
\mathcal{V}^{\lor}
.
For
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

and
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,
we set

(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}=\mathcal{O}_{U}\cdot z^{-n_{1}}e_{1}^{\lor}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{U}\cdot z^{-n_{2}}e_{2}^{\lor},\quad\quad(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}=z(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{({\boldsymbol{n}})}+\mathcal{O}_{U}\cdot\bigl(\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}}e_{1}^{\lor}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}}e_{2}^{\lor}\bigr).

For any lattice
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{V}
,
we set

\mathcal{U}^{\lor}={\mathcal{H}om}_{\mathcal{O}_{U}}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{O}_{U})
.
We have

(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})})^{\lor}=(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{(-{\boldsymbol{n}})}
.

Lemma 4.27

For
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,
let
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}

such that
\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}=0
.
Then, we have

(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})})^{\lor}=(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{(-{\boldsymbol{n}}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}},{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}.

Proof
Because

\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-{\boldsymbol{\delta}})}\subset\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}
,
we obtain

(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})})^{\lor}\subset(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-{\boldsymbol{\delta}})})^{\lor}=(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{(-{\boldsymbol{n}}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}})}
.
Because

\bigl\langle\beta_{1}z^{n_{1}-1}e_{1}^{\lor}+\beta_{2}z^{n_{2}-1}e_{2}^{\lor},\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}}e_{2}\bigr\rangle=z^{-1}\bigl(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}\bigr),

\beta_{1}z^{n_{1}-1}e_{1}^{\lor}+\beta_{2}z^{n_{2}-1}e_{2}^{\lor}

is contained in

(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})})^{\lor}

if and only if

\beta_{1}\alpha_{1}+\beta_{2}\alpha_{2}=0
.

Let us return to the proof of Proposition
4.26
.
Because

\Psi_{C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}})})=(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-{\boldsymbol{m}})}
,

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}};b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if
2n_{1}-m_{1}=2n_{2}-m_{2}=2b
,
i.e.,

n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}=b
.

Let us consider the case of

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};b)}(\mathcal{V})

for
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}
.
Note that

\Psi_{C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}(\mathcal{V}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})})=(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{({\boldsymbol{n}}-{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}
.
Because it cannot be

(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{({\boldsymbol{p}})}
for any
{\boldsymbol{p}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
,
it equals to

(\mathcal{V}^{\lor})^{(-{\boldsymbol{n}}+\ell{\boldsymbol{\delta}},{\boldsymbol{\beta}})}

for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and

{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}
such that

\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}=0

by Lemma
4.27
.
Hence,
we obtain
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=0

and
n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}
.
Note the following equalities:

\bigl\langle\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}}e_{2},\,\,\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+m_{1}}e_{1}^{\lor}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+m_{2}}e_{2}^{\lor}\bigr\rangle=0,

\bigl\langle\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}}e_{2},\,\,\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+1+m_{1}}e_{1}^{\lor}-\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+1+m_{2}}e_{2}^{\lor}\bigr\rangle=z^{-2n_{1}+1+m_{1}}(\alpha_{1}^{2}-\alpha_{2}^{2}),

\bigl\langle\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+1}e_{1}-\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+1}e_{2},\,\,\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+m_{1}}e_{1}^{\lor}+\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+m_{2}}e_{2}^{\lor}\bigr\rangle=z^{-2n_{1}+1+m_{1}}(\alpha_{1}^{2}-\alpha_{2}^{2}),

\bigl\langle\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+1}e_{1}-\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+1}e_{2},\,\,\alpha_{1}z^{-n_{1}+1+m_{1}}e_{1}^{\lor}-\alpha_{2}z^{-n_{2}+1+m_{2}}e_{2}^{\lor}\bigr\rangle=0.

Hence,
under the condition
n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}

and
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=0
,

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},\alpha;b)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if
2b=2n_{1}-1-m_{1}
.
Similarly,
if
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})^{2}
,
and if
\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}
,
we obtain
n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}

and
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=0
,
and under these conditions,
the compatibility condition is

(b_{1}-1)+b_{2}=2n_{1}-1-m_{1}
,
i.e.,

n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{2})
.
If
\alpha_{1}=0
,
then the filtration

\mathcal{P}^{({\boldsymbol{n}},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};{\boldsymbol{b}})}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

is compatible with the decomposition

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{O}_{U}(\ast 0)e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{U}(\ast 0)e_{2}
.
Hence,
it is compatible with
C_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}

if and only if it equals to the canonical filtered extension
in §
4.1.3

by Lemma
4.4
.
It is equivalent to

n_{i}-\frac{1}{2}m_{i}=b_{i}
.

Suppose
m_{1}=m_{2}=:m
.
Then, the filtered bundle

\mathcal{P}^{((0,0);-m/2)}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

of type I is unique
by Lemma
4.24
,
which equals the canonical filtered extension.
There are two filtered bundles

\mathcal{P}_{\ast}^{((0,0),{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};-(1+m)/2)}(\mathcal{V})

of type II corresponding to

{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=(1,\pm\sqrt{-1})
.
The filtered bundles

\mathcal{P}^{((0,0),{\boldsymbol{\alpha}};(b_{1},-b_{1}-m))}_{\ast}(\mathcal{V})

of type (III-1)
are parameterized by

{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=(1,\pm\sqrt{-1})

and

-\frac{m+1}{2}<b_{1}<-\frac{m}{2}
,
where we set
{\boldsymbol{n}}=(0,0)
.
The filtered bundle of type (III-2) does not exist.

Suppose
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
.
Then, there does not exist
the filtered bundle of type I, II nor (III-1).
There is a unique filtered bundle of type (III-2),
which is equal to the canonical filtered extension.

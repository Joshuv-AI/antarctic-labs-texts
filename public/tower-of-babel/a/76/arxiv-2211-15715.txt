Upper bounds for Heilbronn’s triangle problem in higher dimensions
Dmitrii Zakharov

Abstract
We develop a new simple approach to prove upper bounds for generalizations of the Heilbronn’s triangle problem in higher dimensions. Among other things, we show the following: for fixed d ⩾ 1 d\geqslant 1 , any subset of [ 0 , 1 ] d [0,1]^{d} of size n n contains • d + 1 d+1 points which span a simplex of volume at most C d ​ n − log ⁡ d + 6 C_{d}n^{-\log d+6} , • 1.1 ​ d 1.1d points whose convex hull has volume at most C d ​ n − 1.1 C_{d}n^{-1.1} , • k ⩾ 4 ​ d k\geqslant 4\sqrt{d} points which span a ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -dimensional simplex of volume at most C d ​ n − k − 1 d − k 2 8 ​ d 2 C_{d}n^{-\frac{k-1}{d}-\frac{k^{2}}{8d^{2}}} .

Introduction

The Heilbronn’s triangle problem asks for the smallest number
\Delta=\Delta(n)
such that among any
n
points in the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
one can find 3 which form a triangle of area at most
\Delta
. After a series of developments by Roth
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
and Schmidt
[
14
]
, in 1981-82 Komlos–Pintz–Szemerédi
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
obtained the following bounds:

n^{-2}\log n\lesssim\Delta(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{8}{7}+\varepsilon}

(1)

where
\varepsilon>0
can be taken arbitrarily small. Very recently, Cohen, Pohoata and the author
[
5
]
improved the upper bound in (
1
) to
\Delta(n)\ll n^{-\frac{8}{7}-\frac{1}{2000}}
.

In this note, we consider a higher dimensional analogue of this question, namely, given
1\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
define
\Delta_{k,d}(n)
to be the minimum
\Delta>0
such that among any
n
points in
[0,1]^{d}
there are
k
points which form a simplex with
(k-1)
-dimensional volume at most
\Delta
.
Setting
d=2,~k=3
recovers the original Heilbronn’s triangle problem.

Simple probabilistic and packing arguments imply that

n^{-\frac{k-1}{d-k+2}}\lesssim\Delta_{k,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{k-1}{d}},

(2)

for any fixed
1\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
(see also
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
).
The lower bound can be improved by a logarithmic factor using results on independence numbers of sparse hypergraphs, see
[
8
]
for details. Lefmann
[
8
]
improved the upper bound by a polynomial factor for all
even

k\geqslant 2
:

\Delta_{k,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{k-1}{d}-\frac{k-2}{2d(d-1)}},

(3)

earlier, Brass
[
4
]
proved this in the special case
k=d+1
. For
d\geqslant 3
and odd
k
, no improvements over (
2
) were known and the first interesting special case is
d=3
,
k=3
.

In this note we establish the following inequality between functions
\Delta_{k,d}
:

Theorem 1.1

Fix
2\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
and
1\leqslant\ell<k
. Then for some constant
C=C(d)
and all
n^{\prime}>Cn
we have:

\Delta_{k,d}(n^{\prime})\leqslant C\Delta_{\ell,d}(n)\Delta_{k-\ell,d-\ell}(n).

(4)

Combining (
4
) with known upper bounds on
\Delta_{k,d}
leads to new inequalities.
The most notable improvement appears in the case when
k=d+1
. Recall that known estimates give
n^{-d}\lessapprox\Delta_{d+1,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-1}
for all
d
and
\Delta_{d+1,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-1-\frac{1}{2d}}
when
d
is odd. We show:

Corollary 1.2

We have the following bounds:

\displaystyle\Delta_{4,3}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{4}{3}},~~\Delta_{5,4}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{3}{2}},~~\Delta_{6,5}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{33}{20}},

\displaystyle\Delta_{7,6}(n)\lesssim n^{-1.676},~~\Delta_{8,7}(n)\lesssim n^{-1.792},~~\Delta_{9,8}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{19}{10}}.

Note that these bounds significantly improve the previously known estimates on
\Delta_{d+1,d}
. Corollary
1.2
follows from a sequence of repeated applications of Theorem
1.1
in some optimized way. One can continue this process indefinitely to write down the best possible upper bound on
\Delta_{d+1,d}(n)
for each fixed value of
d
. The exponents quickly become rather messy so we will not write them down for
d\geqslant 9
. Instead, we have the following general result:

Corollary 1.3

For any fixed
d\geqslant 3
we have
\Delta_{d+1,d}(n)\ll n^{-\log d+6}
.

We did not attempt to optimize the constant
6
in the exponent above. It can be done by a more careful analysis of the recursion from Theorem
1.1
but the resulting bound is likely very far from optimal anyway. For a given
d
it is more efficient to apply Theorem
1.1
directly to obtain the best possible exponent.

For arbitrary
k\leqslant d
we get results of the following form:

Corollary 1.4

For all
d\geqslant 3
and
2\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
we have
\Delta_{k,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{d+2}{d-k+2}}
.

The factor of
\frac{1}{2}
in the bound can be improved to 1 at the expense of an additive constant error in the exponent, similar to Corollary
1.2
. We decided to keep a slightly worse bound for simplicity.

Note that the bound on
\Delta_{k,d}(n)
in Corollary
1.4
improves the trivial upper bound (
2
) only if
k\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)d
for a small constant
\varepsilon\approx 0.1
. For smaller values of
k
, Theorem
1.1
gives the following improvement to (
2
):

Corollary 1.5

For any
d\geqslant 9
and
3\sqrt{d}\leqslant k\leqslant d
we have
\Delta_{k,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{k-1}{d}-\frac{k^{2}}{8d^{2}}}
.

Note that if
2\leqslant k\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)d
, then by (
2
) we have
\Delta_{k,d}(n)\gtrsim n^{-\frac{k-1}{d-k+2}}\geqslant n^{-\frac{k-1}{d}-\frac{2k^{2}}{\varepsilon d^{2}}}
. So, in a way, the improvement in Corollary
1.5
is optimal up to a constant factor of
16\varepsilon^{-1}
for all
3\sqrt{d}\leqslant k\leqslant(1-\varepsilon)d
.

For
k\leqslant c\sqrt{d}
our ideas do not lead to any improvements, so the Lefmann’s bound (
3
) which holds for even
k
is still the best known upper bound on
\Delta_{k,n}(n)
.

Another natural generalization of the Heilbronn’s triangle problem is to ask for more points with convex hull of the smallest volume.
Namely, for each
k\geqslant d+1
, we can define a Heilbronn-type function
\Delta_{k,d}(n)
as the minimum number
\Delta
such that among any
n
points in
[0,1]^{d}
there are
k
points whose convex hull has
d
-dimensional volume at most
\Delta
.

By a packing argument, for arbitrary
k>d\geqslant 2
we have a trivial upper bound
\Delta_{k,d}\ll n^{-1}
and no asymptotic improvements of this inequality were known prior to this work. For
d=2
the best lower bound is due to Lefmann
[
9
]
:

\Delta_{k,2}(n)\gtrsim(\log n)^{\frac{1}{k-2}}n^{-\frac{k-1}{k-2}},

(5)

which is a logarithmic improvement over a bound by Bertram-Kretzberg, Hofmeister and Lefmann
[
3
]
. A simple probabilistic argument gives the following lower bound for arbitrary
k>d\geqslant 2
:

\Delta_{k,d}(n)\gtrsim n^{-\frac{k-1}{k-d}},

(6)

see (
[
10
]
) for a proof. It seems likely that one should be able to improve (
6
) by a logarithmic factor similarly to (
5
) but we do not pursue this direction here.

Our method allows us to improve the trivial upper bound on
\Delta_{k,d}(n)
when
k
is a bit larger than
d
.

Theorem 1.6

For any
d\geqslant 2
and
d+3\leqslant k\leqslant 2d
we have
\Delta_{k,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-(1-\frac{1}{k-d-1})(\log\frac{d}{k-d-1}-1.1)}
.

Note that
\Delta_{k,d}(n)
is monotone increasing in
k
, so Theorem
1.6
can also be used for
k=d+2
, to say:

\Delta_{d+2,d}(n)\lesssim\min_{k\geqslant d+3}n^{-(1-\frac{1}{k-d})(\log\frac{d}{k-d}-1.1)}\lesssim n^{-\log d+\log\log d+10},

where the right hand side is achieved at
k\approx d+\log d
. The
\log\log d
term can be removed, in principle, by a more careful analysis like we do in Section
2
. We decided to only include a simpler bound in Theorem
1.6
which applies to many pairs
k
and
d
and still gives essentially the strongest possible bounds.

Next, note that if
d
is large enough then the bound in Theorem
1.6
is less than the trivial upper bound
n^{-1}
for all
k\leqslant 1.1d
. That is, our result allows us to find up to
\approx 1.1d
points with convex hull of volume
\lesssim n^{-1-c}
for some constant
c>0
in an arbitrary
n
-element set in
[0,1]^{d}
. The actual constant in place of
1.1
can be improved somewhat by a more careful analysis in the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

It is also interesting to determine the smallest dimension
d
, in which one can show
\Delta_{d+2,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-1-c}
for some
c>0
(i.e. any set of
n
points in
[0,1]^{d}
contains
d+2
points with convex hull volume at most
n^{-1-c}
). Our approach gives

Proposition 1.7

We have
\Delta_{9,7}(n)\lesssim n^{-\frac{23}{21}}
.

It would be interesting to lower the dimension
d=7
further (with the ultimate goal being showing that
\Delta_{4,2}(n)\lesssim n^{-1-c}
).

It would be interesting to combine the approach introduced in this paper with the analytic method of Roth
[
13
]
, which was developed further by Komlós–Pintz–Szemerédi
[
7
]
and then by Cohen, Pohoata and the author
[
5
]
.
Note however that all these techniques applied to the ‘main’ problem of estimating
\Delta_{d+1,d}
have a natural barrier at the exponent
n^{-1-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{3}-\ldots-\frac{1}{d}}=n^{-\log d+O(1)}
. So improving the main term in Corollary
1.3
likely requires new ideas.
On the other hand, our argument seems rather wasteful for small values of
d
so perhaps the bounds on
\Delta_{k,d}
for small values of
d
and

can be improved further.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

For our argument it will be convenient to restate the problem in the following, essentially equivalent form.
Fix
1\leqslant k\leqslant d
. For a collection of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let
\operatorname{Vol}_{k}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
be the
k
-dimensional volume of the parallelepided spanned by vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
. For brevity, we call
\operatorname{Vol}_{k}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
the determinant of the collection
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
. Note that
\operatorname{Vol}_{k}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
equals to the square root of the determinant of the Gram matrix
(\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle)_{i,j=1}^{k}
of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
.

We will use the following basic formula to estimate
\operatorname{Vol}_{k}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
. We include a proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.1

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be arbitrary vectors, then for any
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k
we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\operatorname{Vol}_{\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\operatorname{Vol}_{k-\ell}(x_{\ell+1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime}),

(7)

where for
j=\ell+1,\ldots,k
, we denote by
x_{j}^{\prime}
the projection of
x_{j}
on the orthogonal complement to the space
V=\operatorname{span}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
.

Proof:

View
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
as
d\times 1
column vectors and consider the matrices
A=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and
A^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},x_{\ell+1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime})
. Note that
A^{\prime}=AU
for some upper triangular
k\times k
matrix
U
with all ones on the diagonal. The Gram matrices of the collections
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},x_{\ell+1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime}
are given by
G=A^{T}A
and
G^{\prime}=A^{\prime T}A^{\prime}
, respectively. It follows that
\det G^{\prime}=\det U^{T}GU=\det G
.
The Gram matrix
G^{\prime}
splits into
\ell\times\ell
and
k-\ell\times k-\ell
blocks which are the Gram matrices of the collections
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
and
x^{\prime}_{\ell+1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{k}
, respectively. This implies (
7
).

\Box

Now for any
2\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
define
\Delta^{\prime}_{k,d}(n)
to be the minimum
\Delta^{\prime}
such that any collection
X
of
n
unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
contains
k
vectors with determinant at most
\Delta^{\prime}
.

Proposition 2.2

For any
2\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
there are constants
c,c^{\prime},C
such that for all
n>k
:

c\Delta_{k,d}(n)\leqslant\Delta^{\prime}_{k,d}(n)\leqslant C\Delta_{k,d}(c^{\prime}n).

Proof:

Let
X\subset[0,1]^{d}
be an
n
-element set of points. Recall that for any points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the volume of the simplex spanned by them is equal to
\frac{1}{k!}\operatorname{Vol}_{k}((x_{1},1),\ldots,(x_{k},1))
.
Define a set
Y\subset S^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
as follows:

Y=\left\{\frac{(x,1)}{\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}},~x\in X\right\}.

Note that the volumes of
(k-1)
-dimensional simplices in
X
now precisely correspond to determinants of
k
-tuples of vectors
(x,1)
,
x\in X
. Since for any
x\in[0,1]^{d}
we have
\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}\in[1,\sqrt{d+1}]
, renormalization by these factors changes the determinants only by a constant factor. Thus, if
X
does not contain
k
points with volume at most
\Delta
then
Y
does not contain
k
points with determinant at most
c\Delta
for some
c>0
depending only on
d
and
k
. This shows the first inequality.

Similarly, suppose that
Y\subset S^{d}
has size
n
and any
k
points of
Y
have determinant at least
\Delta^{\prime}
. Consider the central projection of the sphere
S^{d}
on the hyperplane
W=\{x~|~x_{d+1}=1\}
. Then for some
c^{\prime}>0
and a random rotation of
Y
we get that at least
c^{\prime}n
points of
Y
are projected in the cube
[0,1]^{d}\times\{1\}
. Define
X
to be the set of these points and note that
X
does not contain
(k-1)
-simplices of volume
\Delta^{\prime}/C
. This completes the proof of the second inequality.

\Box

In light of Proposition
2.2
, Theorem
1.1
follows from an analogous statement about
\Delta^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.3

For any
2\leqslant k\leqslant d+1
,
1\leqslant\ell<k
and
n>k
we have

\Delta^{\prime}_{k,d}(n)\leqslant\Delta^{\prime}_{\ell,d}(n)\Delta^{\prime}_{k-\ell,d-\ell}(n-\ell).

Proof:

Let
X\subset S^{d}
be an
n
-element set, we need to show that
X
contains
k
points with determinant at most
\Delta^{\prime}_{\ell,d}(n)\Delta^{\prime}_{k-\ell,d-\ell}(n-\ell)
. A small perturbation of points changes the determinants by a small amount, so we may assume that
X\subset S^{d}
is a set of points in general position.

By the definition of
\Delta^{\prime}_{\ell,d}
there are points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\in X
such that
\operatorname{Vol}_{\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\leqslant\Delta^{\prime}_{\ell,d}(n)
.
Let
V=\operatorname{span}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
and let
\pi
be the projection on the orthogonal complement of
V
.
Consider the set
X^{\prime}=\pi(X\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\})
and define
Y=\{x/|x|,~x\in X^{\prime}\}
. By the general position assumption,
Y
is a well-defined
n-\ell
-element set of points lying on a
d-\ell
-dimensional unit sphere
S^{d-\ell}\subset V^{\perp}
.

So, by the definition of
\Delta_{k-\ell,d-\ell}(n-\ell)
, there exists a collection of points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k-\ell}\in Y
such that
\operatorname{Vol}_{k-\ell}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k-\ell})\leqslant\Delta_{k-\ell,d-\ell}(n-\ell)
. Consider the preimages
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-\ell}\in X
of these points. By Proposition
2.1
we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k-\ell})=\operatorname{Vol}_{\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\operatorname{Vol}_{k-\ell}(\pi(z_{1}),\ldots,\pi(z_{k-\ell}))\leqslant

\leqslant\operatorname{Vol}_{\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\operatorname{Vol}_{k-\ell}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k-\ell})\leqslant\Delta_{\ell,d}(n)\Delta_{k-\ell,d-\ell}(n-\ell),

where in the first inequality we replaced vectors
\pi(z_{j})
with longer vectors
y_{j}=\pi(z_{j})/|\pi(z_{j})|
which increases the determinant. This completes the proof of the lemma, and by Proposition
2.2
, of Theorem
1.1
.

\Box

Simplices with faces of small volume

To prove bounds on the Heilbronn functions
\Delta_{k,d}(n)
with
k>d
we will need the following lemma. Roughly speaking, the lemma states that any set of size
n
in
[0,1]^{d}
contains a simplex on
d+1
vertices
S
, such that the volume of
S
is small and, moreover, for all not too large
\ell

\ell

S
also have small volume.

Lemma 4.1

Let
a,t\geqslant 0
and let
d=d_{0}>d_{1}>\ldots>d_{t}>0
be a sequence of positive integers with
d_{i}-d_{i+1}\geqslant a+1
for all
i=0,\ldots,t-1
and
d_{t}\geqslant a
.
Let
X\subset[0,1]^{d}
be a set of
n
points in general position. Then there exists a subset
Y\subset X
of
d+1
points with the following property. For any
0\leqslant\ell\leqslant a
and any subset
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
of size
d+1-\ell
we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{d-\ell}(\operatorname{conv}(Y^{\prime}))\leqslant Cn^{-\gamma_{\ell}},

(10)

where

\gamma_{\ell}=\frac{d_{0}-d_{1}-1}{d_{0}}+\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{d_{t-1}-d_{t}-1}{d_{t-1}}+\frac{d_{t}-\ell}{d_{t}}.

(11)

Proof:

The proof is by induction on
t
. First, let us reformulate the statement a little bit to make the induction cleaner. Instead of a subset
X\subset[0,1]^{d}
we consider an
n
-element set on the sphere
X\subset S^{d}
. We want to find a subset
Y\subset X
of size
d+1
such that
\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell}(Y^{\prime})\lesssim n^{-\gamma_{\ell}}
holds for any
\ell\leqslant a
and any
(d+1-\ell)
-element subset
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
(where by
\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell}
we mean the function defined in Section
2
). This is an equivalent problem (up to a constant factor) as any subset
X\subset[0,1]^{d}
can be mapped to a subset on the sphere given by:

X^{\prime}=\left\{\frac{(x,1)}{|(x,1)|},~x\in S^{d}\right\},

and any set on the sphere
X^{\prime}\subset S^{d}
contains a linearly sized subset which can be projected back into
[0,1]^{d}
. These operations preserve volumes of the convex hulls up to a constant factor, so it is sufficient to work with the spherical formulation of the problem.

Let
t=0
, and consider some
d=d_{0}>0
. Let
X\subset S^{d}
be an
n
-element set. By pigeonhole principle, there exists a
(d+1)
-element subset
Y\subset X
of diameter at most
cn^{-1/d}
for some constant
c
depending on
d
. Then for any
0\leqslant\ell\leqslant d
any
(d+1-\ell)
-element subset
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
has volume at most
Cn^{-\frac{d-\ell}{d}}
. Note that
d_{0}\geqslant a
by assumption so we get the desired bound for all
0\leqslant\ell\leqslant a
.

Now let
t\geqslant 1
and consider a sequence
d=d_{0}>\ldots>d_{t}>0
such that
d_{i}-d_{i+1}\geqslant a+1
and
d_{t}\geqslant a
. Let
X\subset S^{d}
be an
n
-element set in general position. By pigeonhole principle, there exists a
(d_{0}-d_{1})
-element subset
Z\subset X
of diameter at most
cn^{-1/d}
. Let
V=\operatorname{span}(Z)
and let
\pi
be the projection on the orthogonal complement of
V
.
Consider the set
X^{\prime}=\pi(X\setminus Z)
and define
X^{\prime\prime}=\{x/|x|,~x\in X^{\prime}\}
. By the general position assumption,
X^{\prime\prime}
is an
n-(d_{0}-d_{1})
-element set of points lying on a
d_{1}
-dimensional unit sphere
S^{d_{1}}\subset V^{\perp}
. Apply the induction assumption to the set
X^{\prime\prime}
with parameters
a,t-1
and the sequence
d_{1}>\ldots>d_{t}
. We obtain a
(d_{1}+1)
-element set
Y^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime\prime}\subset S^{d_{1}}
such that for any
0\leqslant\ell\leqslant a
and any subset
Y^{\prime\prime}\subset Y^{\prime}
of size
d_{1}+1-\ell
satisfies

\operatorname{Vol}_{d_{1}+1-\ell}(Y^{\prime\prime})\leqslant Cn^{-\gamma^{\prime}_{\ell}},

(12)

where

\gamma^{\prime}_{\ell}=\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}+\frac{d_{2}-d_{3}-1}{d_{2}}+\ldots+\frac{d_{t-1}-d_{t}-1}{d_{t-1}}+\frac{d_{t}-\ell}{d_{t}}.

Let
Z^{\prime}
be the preimage of
Y^{\prime}
in
X
(which is well-defined thanks to the general position assumption).
Now define
Y=Z\cup Z^{\prime}
, we claim that
Y
satisfies the desired property. Indeed, let
0\leqslant\ell\leqslant a
be arbitrary and let
W\subset Y
be an
(d+1-\ell)
-element subset. Write
W_{1}=W\cap Z
and
W_{2}=W\cap Z^{\prime}
. Note that by assumption
d_{0}-d_{1}\geqslant a+1
and so

|W_{1}|=|W\cap Z|\geqslant|Z|-|Y\setminus W|\geqslant(d_{0}-d_{1})-\ell\geqslant(a+1)-a>0,

i.e.
W_{1}
is non-empty. By a similar computation,
W_{2}
is non-empty as well. Denote
m=|W_{1}|
.
Thus, by Proposition
2.1
applied with
\ell=m
we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell}(W)=\operatorname{Vol}_{m}(W_{1})\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell-m}(W^{\prime}_{2}),

where
W_{2}^{\prime}=\pi(W_{2})
. Since
W_{1}\subset Z
, the diameter of
W_{1}
is at most
Cn^{-1/d}
and so
\operatorname{Vol}_{m}(W_{1})\lesssim n^{-\frac{m-1}{d}}
.
Let
W_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\{w/|w|,~w\in W_{2}^{\prime}\}
then
W_{2}^{\prime\prime}
is a subset in
Y^{\prime}
of size
d+1-\ell-m
. Let

\ell^{\prime}=|Y^{\prime}|-|W_{2}^{\prime\prime}|=(d_{1}+1)-(d+1-\ell-m)=\ell+m+d_{1}-d_{0}.

Note that we have
m\leqslant d_{0}-d_{1}
and so
\ell^{\prime}\leqslant\ell\leqslant a
holds.
So by (
12
) applied to
Y^{\prime\prime}=W_{2}^{\prime\prime}
we get

\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell-m}(W^{\prime}_{2})\leqslant\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell-m}(W^{\prime\prime}_{2})\leqslant Cn^{-\gamma^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}}.

Combining the bounds above leads to an upper bound

\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell}(W)\leqslant Cn^{-\frac{m-1}{d_{0}}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}}.

Expanding the number in the exponent gives

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{d_{0}}+\gamma^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}=\frac{m-1}{d_{0}}+\gamma^{\prime}_{\ell+m+d_{1}-d_{0}}=

\displaystyle=\frac{m-1}{d_{0}}+\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{d_{t-1}-d_{t}-1}{d_{t-1}}+\frac{d_{t}-\ell-m-d_{1}+d_{0}}{d_{t}}=

\displaystyle=\frac{m+d_{1}-d_{0}}{d_{0}}+\frac{d_{0}-d_{1}-1}{d_{0}}+\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{d_{t-1}-d_{t}-1}{d_{t-1}}+\frac{d_{t}-\ell-m}{d_{t}}-\frac{m+d_{1}-d_{0}}{d_{t}}=

\displaystyle=\frac{m+d_{1}-d_{0}}{d_{0}}+\gamma_{\ell}-\frac{m+d_{1}-d_{0}}{d_{t}}\geqslant\gamma_{\ell}

where in the last line we used
m+d_{1}-d_{0}\leqslant 0
and
d_{0}>d_{t}
. We conclude that
\operatorname{Vol}_{d+1-\ell}(W)\leqslant Cn^{-\gamma_{\ell}}
holds with
\gamma_{\ell}
as in (
11
). This shows that the constructed set
Y
satisfies the desired property and the induction step is complete.

\Box

5
Proof of Theorem
1.6
and Proposition
1.7

Let
a=k-d-1
,
t=[\frac{d}{a+1}]-1
(note that
t\geqslant 0
since
k\leqslant 2d
) and consider the sequence of integers
d_{i}=d-i(a+1)
, for
i=0,\ldots,t
. Let
X\subset[0,1]^{d}
be a set of size
n
.

Apply Lemma
4.1
to
X
with parameters
a
,
t
and the sequence
d_{0}>\ldots>d_{t}
. We obtain a subset
Y\subset X
of size
d+1
such that for any
\ell\leqslant a
and any
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
of size
d+1-\ell
we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{d-\ell}(\operatorname{conv}(Y^{\prime}))\lesssim n^{-\gamma_{\ell}},

where

\gamma_{\ell}=\frac{d_{0}-d_{1}-1}{d_{0}}+\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}-1}{d_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{d_{t-1}-d_{t}-1}{d_{t-1}}+\frac{d_{t}-\ell}{d_{t}}=

=\frac{a}{d_{0}}+\frac{a}{d_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{a}{d_{t-1}}+\frac{d_{t}-\ell}{d_{t}}.

Using the bounds
d_{i}\leqslant(t-i+2)(a+1)
and
d_{t}\geqslant a+1
, leads to an estimate

\gamma_{\ell}\geqslant\frac{a}{(t+2)(a+1)}+\frac{a}{(t+1)(a+1)}+\ldots+\frac{a}{3(a+1)}+1-\frac{\ell}{a+1}\geqslant\frac{a}{a+1}\log\frac{t+3}{3}+1-\frac{\ell}{a+1}.

Let
Z\subset X\setminus Y
be a set of size
k-d-1=a
and diameter
\lesssim n^{-1/d}
. We claim that the set
S=Y\cup Z
works. We will use the following simple volume estimate:

Proposition 5.1

Given finite sets
A,B\subset[0,1]^{d}
we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(A\cup B))\lesssim\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(A))+\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(B))+

+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A^{\prime}\subset A,B^{\prime}\subset B\\
|A^{\prime}|=i,|B^{\prime}|=d+1-i\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Vol}_{i-1}(\operatorname{conv}(A^{\prime}))\operatorname{Vol}_{d-i}(\operatorname{conv}(B^{\prime})).

(13)

Proof:

By the Caratheodory’s theorem, the convex hull of the set
A\cup B
is covered by the convex hulls of all its
(d+1)
-element subsets. So it is enough to prove (
13
) when
|A|+|B|=d+1
. If one of the two sets is empty then the first two terms in (
13
) upper bound the left hand side.
In the remaining case we need to show that

\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(A\cup B))\lesssim\operatorname{Vol}_{|A|-1}(\operatorname{conv}(A))\operatorname{Vol}_{|B|-1}(\operatorname{conv}(B)).

This quickly follows from Proposition
2.1
after projecting the sets on the sphere
S^{d}
and noting that the volumes change only by a constant factor.

\Box

We can now upper bound the volume of the convex hull of
S
as follows:

\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(S))\lesssim\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(Y))+\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y^{\prime}\subset Y,Z^{\prime}\subset Z,\\
|Y^{\prime}|=d-i+1,|Z^{\prime}|=i\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Vol}_{d-i}(\operatorname{conv}(Y^{\prime}))\operatorname{Vol}_{i-1}(\operatorname{conv}(Z^{\prime}))\lesssim

\lesssim n^{-\gamma_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{a}n^{-\gamma_{i}-\frac{i-1}{d}}\lesssim n^{-\gamma_{a}-\frac{a-1}{d}}\lesssim n^{-\frac{a}{a+1}\log\frac{t+3}{3}-1+\frac{a}{a+1}-\frac{a-1}{d}}\lesssim n^{-(1-\frac{1}{a+1})(\log\frac{d}{a}-1.1)},

(where we bounded
\log 3\leqslant 1.1
).
Note that
|Z|=a\leqslant d_{0}-d_{1}-1<d+1
, so we do not have the term
\operatorname{Vol}_{d}(\operatorname{conv}(Z))
in the above expression.
Since
a=k-d-1
, this implies that
\Delta_{k,d}(n)\lesssim n^{-(1-\frac{1}{k-d-1})(\log\frac{d}{k-d-1}-1.1)}
holds. This proves Theorem
1.6
.

Now we prove Proposition
1.7
. Let
a=1,t=1
and consider the sequence
d_{0}=7>d_{1}=3
. Repeating the argument above with these parameters produces a
9
-element set
S\subset[0,1]^{7}
whose convex hull has volume at most
n^{-\gamma_{1}}=n^{-\frac{7-3-1}{7}-\frac{3-1}{3}}=n^{-\frac{23}{21}}.

Proposal for spin squeezing in rare-earth ion-doped crystals with a four-color scheme
T. Kriváchy, K. T. Kaczmarek, M. Afzelius, J. Etesse, G. Haack

Abstract
Achieving spin squeezing within solid-state devices is a long standing research goal, due to the promise of their particularities, for instance their long coherence times, the possibility of low-temperature experiments or integration of entanglement-assisted sensors on-chip. In this work, we investigate an interferometer-free four-color scheme to achieve spin squeezing of rare-earth ion-doped crystals. The proposal relies on an analytic derivation that starts from a Tavis-Cummings model for light-matter interaction, providing microscopic insights onto spin-squeezing generation. We evidence spin squeezing signature in the light intensity variance. We consider the two particular cases of europium- and praseodymium-doped yttrium orthosilicates, workhorses of quantum technology developments. We show that up to 8 dB of spin squeezing can be obtained with readily accessible experimental resources, including noise due to photon scattering. Our results for rare-earth ion-doped crystals add to promising properties of these platforms for manipulating many-body entangled states and for high-precision measurements.



I
Introduction

Spin squeezing provides a way of entangling a large ensemble of spins, which can also be readily verified with current experimental techniques. Correlations among the spins are typically built via their collective interaction with an optical field, as opposed to individually addressing the quantum mechanical degrees of freedom of the spins
Pezzè et al. 2018
;
Hammerer et al. 2004
. Spin squeezing has numerous applications towards entanglement detection
Gühne and Tóth 2009
, quantum sensing and quantum metrology, see the above reviews. A natural starting point for achieving spin squeezing of an ensemble of real or artificial atoms is the coherent spin state (CSS) of
N
two-level quantum systems in a fully quantum coherent superposition between ground and excited states. Light-matter collective interactions are typically implemented by transferring quantum states of light to the atoms or through non-destructive measurements via interferometric schemes, leading to successful experiments and promising proposals with various platforms, including trapped ions
Bohnet et al. 2016
, Bose–Einstein condensates
Estève et al. 2008
;
Gross et al. 2010
;
Riedel et al. 2010
, cold thermal atoms
Hald et al. 1999
;
Pezzè et al. 2018
;
Gühne and Tóth 2009
;
Tóth et al. 2009
, nuclear spin squeezing
Serafin et al. 2020
, optical spin squeezing
Ono et al. 2017
, and magnons
Kamra et al. 2020
.

The goal of achieving spin squeezing within solid-state devices is motivated by several key advantages specific to these platforms. In particular, spin squeezing in solid-state devices could benefit from their long coherence times, would allow for low-temperature operation and provide the possibility to integrate entanglement-assisted sensors on-chip. Whereas very recent experiments have demonstrated spin-squeezing with NV centers opening the way towards spin squeezing at room temperature
Song et al. 2017
, no experiments have yet been realized with rare-earth ion-doped crystals (REIDCs). REIDCs are solid-state devices of particular interest for implementing a broad diversity of quantum information processing protocols such as quantum memories
Jobez et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2022
;
Rakonjac et al. 2021
;
Ortu et al. 2022a
, photonic processors in the spectro-temporal domain
Saglamyurek et al. 2014
;
Craiciu et al. 2021
and quantum repeaters
Laplane et al. 2017
;
Kutluer et al. 2019
. They are particularly appealing materials thanks to their record coherence times
Zhong et al. 2015
;
Rančić et al. 2018
;
Ortu et al. 2022a
;
Askarani et al. 2021
, and their solid-state nature allows them to be integrated via a large range of available techniques
Saglamyurek et al. 2011
;
Marzban et al. 2015
;
Zhong et al. 2017
;
Rakonjac et al. 2022
;
Dutta et al. 2021
.

In this work, we propose to achieve spin squeezing in a REIDC using a four-color scheme
Saffman et al. 2009
as illustrated in Fig.
1
. A single spatial mode containing two pairs of optical modes (labelled
\alpha
and
\beta
) probe the lower two spin states of the ground manifold of the ensemble, initially prepared in a CSS. Quantum non demolition (QND) measurements of the intensity variance will constitute a signature of spin squeezing within the medium. In contrast to Ramsey-type experiments, we avoid interferometric-stability issues as quantum interferences take place within each pair of optical probes as shown in the next sections. Other known methods to implement spin squeezing in the previously mentioned platforms also remain valid for REIDCs, but the goal of our approach is to combine the simplicity of an experimental scheme with our solid-state ensembles.

From a theoretical perspective, we provide a complementary approach with respect to Ref.
Saffman et al. 2009
, starting from the generalized Jaynes-Cummings Hamiltonian in the rotating wave approximation for light-matter interaction. We then work in the dispersive regime, valid for a large detuning between the light and atomic transitions. In this regime, the light-matter interaction is QND, inducing a frequency-shift of the probes that depends linearly on the atomic populations of the CSS. Such a QND interaction is known to be a valid way to achieve spin squeezing
Hammerer et al. 2004
;
Pezzè et al. 2018
. We show that the amount of squeezing (i.e. the squeezing parameter) depends directly on the dispersive coupling strength, which we estimate from state-of-the-art experiments with rare-earth ions. We derive the variance of the light’s intensity, the atomic post-measured state in terms of this dispersive shift, and discuss the role of photon scattering in future experiments with two different types of REIDCs, europium-doped yttrium orthosilicate (Eu
3+
:Y
2
SiO
5
) and praseodymium-doped yttrium orthosilicate (Pr
3+
:Y
2
SiO
5
). Our model predicts that up to 8 dB spin squeezing can be achieved in these. This value would place REIDCs as serious counterparts to gaseous systems, while retaining all the aforementioned strengths of solid-state systems.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we briefly introduce spin squeezing. In Sec.
III
, we derive the light-atoms entangled state created by our experimental scheme. These analytical results are based on a microscopic description of light-matter interaction in the dispersive regime. In Sec.
IV
, we compute the first two moments of light intensity without and with the presence of atoms, and show how the intensity variance depends on the number of atoms and on the dispersive phase shift per atom. In Sec.
V
, we derive the atomic state and show that it is modified upon the measurement outcomes for light detection. In Sec.
VI
, we provide an analytical expression of the spin squeezing parameter
\xi^{2}
specific to our experimental scheme and discuss its dependence on the measurement outcomes taking state-of-the-art experimental values for the two REIDCs under consideration. In the last section, Sec.
VII
, we discuss squeezing in presence of photon scattering considering for these systems. We compare our predictions to past experiments with rubidium and cesium cold atomic gases.

II
Our proposal for solid-state based spin squeezing

We use a simplified model where each atom has two spin states
|\!\!\downarrow\rangle
and
|\!\!\uparrow\rangle
in the electronic ground state, and another two spin states
|e_{\downarrow}\rangle
and
|e_{\uparrow}\rangle
in an excited electronic state. The transitions between the ground and excited spin states
|\!\!\downarrow\rangle\leftrightarrow|e_{\downarrow}\rangle
and
|\!\!\uparrow\rangle\leftrightarrow|e_{\uparrow}\rangle
are probed with optical modes, labelled
\alpha
and
\beta
, see Fig.
1
. The
N
spins are initially put in a coherent superposition
{(|\!\!\downarrow\rangle+|\!\!\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt{2}}
, forming a CSS. In the Dicke state basis
\{|m\rangle\}
where
|m\rangle
is an eigenstate of the collective operator
\hat{J}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{z}
with eigenvalue
m
, the CSS takes the form:

\displaystyle|\text{CSS}\rangle=\frac{1}{2^{N/2}}\left(|\!\!\downarrow\rangle+|\!\!\uparrow\rangle\right)^{\otimes N}=\sum_{m=-N/2}^{N/2}c_{m}|m\rangle\,.

(1)

The Dicke state
|m\rangle
is a superposition of spin states with
N/2+m
(
N/2-m
) spins in their excited (ground) state and
c_{m}
the normalized binomial coefficients such that
\sum_{m}|c_{m}|^{2}=1
:

c_{m}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\frac{1}{2^{N/2}}}}\sqrt{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\frac{N}{2}+m\end{array}\right)}\,.

(2)

The CSS state is characterized by the expectation value of the collective operator
\hat{J}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{x}
and
\hat{J}_{y}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{y}
, with their expected values being
\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle=N/2
,
\langle\hat{J}_{y}\rangle=0
. The variance of a collective spin operator in the orthogonal direction
\hat{z}
, is simply given by
\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle=N/4
, whereas its average reduces to 0 as easily seen from the above definitions. The CSS is the optimal separable state for metrology, saturating the standard quantum limit. However, the standard quantum limit can be surpassed when the spins become entangled
Pezzè et al. 2018
, and the metrological gain can then be quantified through the squeezing parameter
\xi^{2}
:

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=N\frac{\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle}{\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle^{2}}\,.

(3)

This parameter takes the value
\xi^{2}=1
when expectation values of the orthogonal collective operators
\hat{J}_{z}
and
\hat{J}_{x}
are evaluated for a CSS. In contrast, when evaluated for a spin-squeezed state, values for
\xi^{2}
below 1 can be achieved, evidencing a reduced variance in the
z
-direction while maintaining the mean value in the
x
-direction, giving rise to an enhanced sensitivity.

The experimental setup is illustrated in Fig.
2
. Assuming the atoms and the light probes being detuned from the atomic transition resonance, their interaction effectively shifts the phase of each frequency mode according to the spin population. By measuring the intensity of each probe,
\bar{I}_{\alpha,\beta}
, the state of the atoms is modified according to quantum measurement theory, and we will show that it enables non-interferometric spin squeezing with REIDCs.

III
Entangling light and atoms

As sketched in Fig.
2
, the two pairs of modes required in the four-color scheme are generated by sending two coherent states with frequencies
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\omega_{\beta}
into a modulator that produces two pairs of coherent states detuned by
\pm\delta\omega/2
from their central frequency :

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha,\beta}^{\pm}=\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\pm\delta\omega/2\,.

(4)

The photonic states to be manipulated in this proposal hence take the form :

\displaystyle|\alpha\rangle\rightarrow|\alpha(e^{i\omega_{\alpha}^{+}t}+e^{i\omega_{\alpha}^{-}t})\rangle=|\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}t}(e^{i\delta\omega t/2}+e^{-i\delta\omega t/2})\rangle

\displaystyle|\beta\rangle\rightarrow|\beta(e^{i\omega_{\beta}^{+}t}+e^{i\omega_{\beta}^{-}t})\rangle=|\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}t}(e^{i\delta\omega t/2}+e^{-i\delta\omega_{\beta}t/2})\rangle

The total Hamiltonian for spins and optical modes, accounting for light-matter interaction through a Tavis-Cummings Hamiltonian, takes the form:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{tot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{\alpha}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\omega_{\beta}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}\hat{a}_{\beta}\right)+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}}\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{z_{\alpha}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\frac{\omega_{\beta}}{2}}}\hat{\sigma}_{z_{\beta}}^{i}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}}g_{\alpha}^{(i)}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{+_{\alpha}}^{i}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{\sigma}_{-_{\alpha}}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}}g_{\beta}^{(i)}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{+_{\beta}}^{i}\hat{a}_{\beta}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}\hat{\sigma}_{-_{\beta}}^{i}\right)\,,

with
\hat{a}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha,\beta}
the bosonic annihilation and creation operators for the two photonic probes, and
\sigma_{\pm_{\alpha,\beta}}^{i}
the raising and lowering operators for the optical transitions
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}
for the atom
i
. The Tavis-Cummings interaction Hamiltonian is valid under the rotating-wave approximation (RWA),
i.e.
considering only near-resonant interactions between the photonic modes and the spin degrees of freedom
Scully and Zubairy 1997
;
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
. Notice that excitation of crossed transition (
|\!\!\downarrow\rangle\leftrightarrow|e_{\uparrow}\rangle
and
|\!\!\uparrow\rangle\leftrightarrow|e_{\downarrow}\rangle
) are neglected here, which might not be the case in actual experimental implementations. The interaction strengths
g_{\mu}^{i}
will be made explicit as a function of experimental parameters below.

III.1
Atomic QND-measurement

To achieve a QND measurement of the CSS state by the photonic probes, we consider the dispersive regime of the Tavis-Cummings Hamiltonian in the RWA, valid when the detuning
\delta\omega/2
between atomic and photonic frequencies is much larger than the interaction strengths
g_{\mu}^{(i)}
.

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}^{(i)},g_{\beta}^{(i)}\ll\delta\omega/2\,.

(7)

In this situation, the total Hamiltonian takes the effective form of the dispersive Hamiltonian in the RWA
Boissonneault et al. 2009
;
Zueco et al. 2009
;
Fink et al. 2009
;
Bishop et al. 2010
:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\omega_{\alpha}+\frac{(g_{\alpha}^{(i)})^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\right)\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{z_{\alpha}}}{2}

(8)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\omega_{\alpha}^{\pm}\pm\frac{(g_{\alpha}^{(i)})^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{z_{\alpha}}\right)\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\omega_{\beta}+\frac{(g_{\beta}^{(i)})^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\right)\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{z_{\beta}}}{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\omega_{\beta}^{\pm}\pm\frac{(g_{\beta}^{(i)})^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\hat{\sigma}^{i}_{z_{\beta}}\right)\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}

Further assuming that the light-matter interaction strength is the same for all atoms and does not depend on the optical frequencies,
g_{\alpha}^{(i)}=g_{\beta}^{(i)}\equiv g
(as discussed below), the RWA Hamiltonian in the dispersive regime that applies to our scheme is

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}}}=

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{\alpha}+\frac{g^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\right)\hat{J}_{z_{\alpha}}+\left(\omega_{\alpha}^{\pm}\pm\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{2}}g^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\hat{J}_{z_{\alpha}}\right)\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\left(\omega_{\beta}+\frac{g^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\right)\hat{J}_{z_{\beta}}+\left(\omega_{\beta}^{\pm}\pm\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{2}}g^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\hat{J}_{z_{\beta}}\right)\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}\,.

(9)

Here we have introduced the collective operators
\hat{J}_{z_{\mu}}
(
\mu=\alpha,\beta
), the spin operators for the optical transitions
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\omega_{\beta}
:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{z_{\alpha}}^{i}

(10a)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{z_{\beta}}^{i}\,.

(10b)

probing respectively the ground and excited spin states of the atomic CSS
|\!\!\downarrow\rangle
and
|\!\!\uparrow\rangle
:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z_{\alpha}}|m\rangle={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\left(\frac{N}{2}-m\right)|m\rangle

(11a)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z_{\beta}}|m\rangle={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\left(\frac{N}{2}+m\right)|m\rangle\,.

(11b)

The minus sign originates in the definition of the operators
\hat{J}_{z_{\alpha,\beta}}
(probing the ground and excited states of the CSS correspond respectively to probing the ground state of the
\alpha
-optical transition and of the
\beta
-optical transition). Recall that
\hat{J}_{z}
without a subscript represents the operator acting on the
\{|\!\!\downarrow\rangle,|\!\!\uparrow\rangle\}
subspace, cf. Fig. 1. Equation (
III.1
) clearly shows that ground spin states and probes acquire a frequency-shift due to their interaction in the RWA in the dispersive regime. This frequency shift
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{2}}g^{2}/(\delta\omega/2)
will be denoted as
\Omega_{0}
in the rest of this work:

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{2}}g^{2}}{\delta\omega/2}\,.

(12)

This dispersive regime allows for a QND-measurement with no population transfer to the excited states
|e_{\downarrow}\rangle
and
|e_{\uparrow}\rangle
through the shift in frequency of the photonic probes:

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}^{\pm}\rightarrow\omega_{\alpha}^{\pm}\pm\Omega_{0}\hat{J}_{z_{\alpha}}

(13a)

\displaystyle\omega_{\beta}^{\pm}\rightarrow\omega_{\beta}^{\pm}\pm\Omega_{0}\hat{J}_{z_{\beta}}\,.

(13b)

Below, we derive the entangled state for atoms and photonic modes and demonstrate squeezing upon measurements of light intensities.

III.2
Light-atoms entangled state

For simplicity, we start from the state
|\psi_{t-t_{p}}\rangle
at time
t-t_{p}
before the light-matter interaction of duration
t_{p}
took place. It is therefore the tensor product of the CSS written as a superposition of Dicke states
|m\rangle
(see Eq. (
1

III

:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{|\psi_{t-t_{p}}\rangle}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}|m\rangle\otimes|\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(t-t_{p})}}}(e^{i\delta\omega{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(t-t_{p})}}/2}+e^{-i\delta\omega{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(t-t_{p})}}/2})\rangle\otimes|\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(t-t_{p})}}}(e^{i(\delta\omega{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(t-t_{p})}}/2+\theta/2)}+e^{-i(\delta\omega{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(t-t_{p})}}/2-\theta/2)})\rangle\,.

Here, a relative phase
\theta
is introduced as an additional parameter, that can be adjusted via the independent modulations of the optical fields
\alpha
and
\beta
.
As a consequence of the QND scheme, all coherent states pick up a phase factor that depends on the
m
-th Dicke state through
\hat{J}_{z_{\mu}}
according to Eqs. (
13

t_{p}
during which the coherent states interacted with the atoms. Light and atoms become entangled and the state evolves to :

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{|\psi_{t}\rangle}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}|m\rangle\otimes|\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}t}\big(e^{i\delta\omega t/2}e^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}i\Omega_{0}(N/2-m)t_{p}}+e^{-i\delta\omega t/2}e^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{+}}i\Omega_{0}(N/2-m)t_{p}}\big)\rangle

(15)

\displaystyle\otimes|\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}t}\big(e^{i\delta\omega t/2}e^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}i\Omega_{0}(N/2+m)t_{p}}e^{i\theta/2}+e^{-i\delta\omega t/2}e^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{+}}i\Omega_{0}(N/2+m)t_{p}}e^{-i\theta/2}\big)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}|m\rangle\otimes|2\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}t}\cos\left(\frac{\Omega_{0}t_{p}N{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\delta\omega t}{2}-m\Omega_{0}t_{p}\right)\rangle\otimes|2\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}t}\cos\left(\frac{\Omega_{0}t_{p}N{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\delta\omega t{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\theta}{2}+m\Omega_{0}t_{p}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}|m\rangle\otimes|2\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}t}\cos\left(X_{t}-m\varphi\right)\rangle\otimes|2\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}t}\cos\left(X_{t}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\theta/2+m\varphi\right)\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}|m\rangle\otimes|\alpha_{m}\rangle\otimes|\beta_{m}\rangle\,.

(16)

After interaction, photonic and atomic degrees of freedom can no longer be written as a separable state. Each photonic state now depends explicitly on the atomic quantum number
m
through the population of the spin ground state (
N/2-m
) or the population of the spin excited state (
N/2+m
). We have introduced the notations

\displaystyle X_{t}=(\varphi N{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\delta\omega t)/2

(17)

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}t_{p}=\varphi\,.

(18)

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}=2\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}t}\cos\left(X_{t}-m\varphi\right)

\displaystyle\beta_{m}=2\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}t}\cos\left(X_{t}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{-}}\theta/2+m\varphi\right)\,.

The amplitudes
\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{m}
exhibit a time dependence through

and depend on the atomic quantum number
m
. The relative phase
\theta
between the two coherent states modulations plays the role of a control parameter which influences both the light intensity and variance. In the following, we will fix it to
\theta={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{+}}\pi

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}

\displaystyle=2\alpha e^{i\omega_{\alpha}t}\cos\left(X_{t}-m\varphi\right)

(19a)

\displaystyle\beta_{m}

\displaystyle=2\beta e^{i\omega_{\beta}t}\sin\left(X_{t}+m\varphi\right)\,.

(19b)

The probe time
t_{p}
entering the QND-measurement induced phase factors in Eq. (
15

III.1
) introduces an additional phase term in Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
) in front of

, given by
e^{i(\omega_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha})t_{p}m}
. This additional phase changes the reference direction in the

(\omega_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha})t_{p}
. It implies a squeezed state with a given orientation set by the pulse time
t_{p}
and the beating frequency
\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}
. Without loss of generality and for the sake of clarity, we work in the rotating frame of the atoms in the following, hence omitting this phase factor in the main text. In App.

, we explain how this term exactly enters the squeezing parameter.

Towards implementation in a solid-state experiment, several aspects should be addressed. First, the Eu- and Pr-doped crystals usually display inhomogeneously broadened optical lines, with typical widths (
\sim
GHz) much broader than the frequency spacing of the spin transitions (
\sim
10 MHz, see Fig.
1
b). Therefore, specific preparation procedures are required in order to select only the atoms resonant with the proper transition at a given wavelength (also known as ‘class cleaning procedure’
Lauritzen et al. 2012
). This can be performed by using well-known optical pumping techniques, which also come with a decrease of the optical depth of the ensemble. A detailed analysis of these aspects is given in App.
A
. Second, it is important to explicit the form of the phase shift
\varphi=\Omega_{0}t_{p}=2\frac{g^{2}}{\Delta}t_{p}
with the detuning
\Delta=\delta\omega/2
in terms of relevant parameters for a REIDC, in order to assess the feasibility of spin-squeezing experiments. As mentioned above, the coupling strengths
g_{\alpha}
and
g_{\beta}
of both transitions at frequencies
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\omega_{\beta}
can be made equal independently by adjusting the relative transition cross sections. To this extent, one can act on an additional degree of freedom for the two coherent states
\alpha
and
\beta
such as polarization for instance. Then, the probe time
t_{p}
will typically be set by the length of the sample
L_{s}
and the group velocity
v_{g}
of the light pulses. We then have
g=\mu|\mathcal{E}^{(1)}|/\hbar
with
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}=\sqrt{\hbar\omega/(2\epsilon_{0}V)}\sqrt{v_{g}/(nc)}
the single photon electric field,
\mu=\sqrt{n\epsilon_{0}c\hbar\sigma\Gamma/\omega}
the electric dipole of the optical transition,
\sigma
the transition resonant cross-section,
\Gamma
the homogeneous linewidth,
\omega
the transition frequency,
V=A\cdot L_{s}
the quantization volume,
A
the area of the addressed ensemble and
n
the refractive index of the medium
Sipe 2009
. Using these expressions, the phase shift per atom
\varphi
can be expressed in terms of the optical depth of the sample,
d=\sigma N/A
with
N
the total number of atoms in the crystal and the photon scattering rate
\eta=I_{ph}\sigma/A\cdot(\Gamma/\Delta)^{2}
. Here,
I_{ph}
corresponds to the number of photons probing one optical transition,
I_{ph}=2I_{0}
with the definition
|\alpha|^{2}=|\beta|^{2}=I_{0}
. This gives:

\varphi=\sqrt{\frac{\eta d}{NI_{ph}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\eta d}{N(2I_{0})}}\,,

(20)

a relation that agrees with the literature
\eta d=\varphi^{2}NI_{ph}

Hammerer et al. 2004
;
Appel et al. 2009
;
Saffman et al. 2009
;
Pezzè et al. 2018
and references therein.

The beatnotes at frequency
\delta\omega
will be subsequently measured and the components at frequencies
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\omega_{\beta}
will each produce photocurrents that can be accessed individually or together in basic operations such as their sum or differences. The case of difference in the photocurrents is presented in App.
B.2
; this scheme may be advantageous to facilitate the measurement of the light variance, as the mean value can be made zero through the relative phase
\theta
between the two coherent states in Eq. (

). In the following sections, we focus on the case where both intensities can be measured independently.

IV
Moments of light in presence of atoms

We now calculate the light intensity and its variance, assuming the photonic modes have interacted with the atomic cloud and assuming they can be measured independently through the photon number operators
\hat{n}_{\alpha}
and
\hat{n}_{\beta}
. The total intensity operator is then simply the sum of the two operators:

\displaystyle\hat{I}=\hat{n}_{\alpha}+\hat{n}_{\beta}=\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}\,,

(21)

The average intensity
\langle\hat{I}\rangle
is calculated with respect to the photonic state, obtained by taking the partial trace with respect to the atomic state. The light intensity variance is defined as
\text{Var}(\hat{I})=\langle(\hat{I}-\langle\hat{I}\rangle)^{2}\rangle=\langle\hat{I}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{I}\rangle^{2}
. Before interaction with the atoms, one simply has

\displaystyle\langle\hat{I}\rangle=\text{Var}(\hat{I})=4I_{0}\,.

(22)

After interaction with the atoms, the situation changes radically. The coherent states acquire a phase shift, their amplitude now depend on the atomic quantum number
m
, see Eqs. (
19
) for
|\alpha_{m}\rangle
and
|\beta_{m}\rangle
. When examining observables of the optical modes, such as intensity and variance, one must start from Eq. (
16
) and trace out the atomic degrees of freedom. The resulting density matrix describing the optical degrees of freedom is
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}|\alpha_{m}\rangle|\beta_{m}\rangle\langle\beta_{m}|\langle\alpha_{m}|
, and the average intensity reads

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{I}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\langle\alpha_{m}|\langle\beta_{m}|\hat{I}|\beta_{m}\rangle|\alpha_{m}\rangle

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\left(\langle\alpha_{m}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}|\alpha_{m}\rangle+\langle\beta_{m}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\beta}\hat{a}_{\beta}|\beta_{m}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\left(|\alpha_{m}|^{2}+|\beta_{m}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4I_{0}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\left[1+\sin(2X_{t})\sin(m{\varphi})\right]\,.

(24)

The sum over
m
is performed by determining a valid support for the binomial coefficients
c_{m}
. Initially, the binomial distribution
c_{m}^{2}
is centered around 0 and its support is primarily in
[-\sqrt{N/2},\sqrt{N/2}]
, where
N
is the total number of atoms. Comparing
m\varphi
(of order
\sim 1/\sqrt{N}
) to
N\varphi
(of order
\sim
1, total phase shift for the atomic ensemble), we can Taylor expand
\sin(m\varphi)
and keep the lowest-order terms in
\varphi
in the support of the binomial distribution. Using then
\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}=1
and
\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}m=0
, we find that the average light intensity remains unaffected by the presence of the atoms to first-order in
\varphi
:

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{I}}\rangle=4I_{0}\,.

(25)

In contrast, a change in the intensity variance is observed from
4I_{0}
when interaction with the atoms is taken into account. Keeping only lowest-order terms in
\varphi
,
\sin^{2}(m\varphi)\approx m^{2}\varphi^{2}
, and using
\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}m^{2}=N/4
, we find (see details in App.
B.1
):

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{I}^{2}}\rangle\sim 4I_{0}\left(1+4I_{0}\left(1+\frac{N\varphi^{2}}{4}\sin^{2}(2X_{t})\right)\right),

(26)

Subtracting
\langle\hat{I}\rangle^{2}=16I_{0}^{2}
, we get the intensity variance of light after interaction with the atoms up to second order in
\varphi
:

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{I})=4I_{0}\left(1+I_{0}N\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}(2X_{t})\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varphi^{3}).

(27)

Equation (
27
) shows that a change in the variance of light in a QND scheme depends on the total number of atoms
N
, the phase shift per atom
\varphi
and the average light intensity
I_{0}
in each optical mode. Let us note that this change will only be present if
I_{0}N\varphi^{2}\gtrapprox 1
. Note that there is an explicit time dependence in the intensity variance through the parameter
X_{t}:=\delta\omega t/2+N/2
in the above expression. All these specificities will show up in the final form of the squeezing parameter as discussed in Sec.
VI
. For clarity for the reader, we emphasize that similar expressions for the optical intensity variance were obtained in previous works by identifying the phase shift
\varphi^{2}
with the angular phase shift typically labelled as
\tilde{\kappa}^{2}
, see for instance
Saffman et al. 2009
.

V
Post-measurement atomic state

Having shown in the previous section that the optical intensity variance is modified by the interaction with the atoms, we now turn to determine how the average and the variance of the atomic CSS collective spin operators are modified by the interaction with the photonic modes. Before interaction, the atoms are in a coherent spin state (CSS) with density operator
\rho_{A}

\displaystyle\rho_{A}=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}c_{m}c_{m^{\prime}}|m\rangle\langle m^{\prime}|\,,

(28)

where we have assumed, without loss of generality, that the binomial coefficients
c_{m},c_{m^{\prime}}
are real. When calculating the average of the collective operators with respect to
\rho_{A}
, one finds
\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle=N/4
and
\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle=N/2
, such that
\xi^{2}=1
for the CSS. After interaction and measurement on the optical modes, the state of the atoms is modified according to the measurement outcomes. In this work, we assume the measurement outcomes to be the intensity of the two photonic modes
\bar{I}_{\alpha}
and
\bar{I}_{\beta}
, corresponding to each photonic mode to be detected by its own classical detector, see Fig.
2
.

Measurement theory tells us that the atomic state given a certain set of outcomes
(\bar{I}_{\alpha},\bar{I}_{\beta})
is modified into
Jacobs 2014
:

\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{\prime}=\frac{\text{Tr}_{\alpha,\beta}[K_{\alpha}K_{\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{|\psi_{t}\rangle\langle\psi_{t}|}}K_{\beta}^{\dagger}K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}]}{{\rm Tr}\left\{K_{\alpha}K_{\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{|\psi_{t}\rangle\langle\psi_{t}|}}K_{\beta}^{\dagger}K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\right\}},

(29)

with the Kraus operators

\displaystyle K_{\gamma}=e^{-\bar{I}_{\gamma}/2}\sum_{n_{\gamma}}\frac{\bar{I}_{\gamma}^{n_{\gamma}/2}}{\sqrt{n_{\gamma}!}}|n_{\gamma}\rangle\langle n_{\gamma}|\,\quad\gamma=\alpha,\beta

(30)

corresponding to the POVM (Positive Operator Valued Measure)
M_{\gamma}=K_{\gamma}^{\dagger}K_{\gamma}
:

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}=e^{-\bar{I}_{\gamma}}\sum_{n_{\gamma}}\frac{(\bar{I}_{\gamma})^{n_{\gamma}}}{n_{\gamma}!}|n_{\gamma}\rangle\langle n_{\gamma}|=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\Psi}{2\pi}|\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma|\,.

(31)

The POVM
M_{\gamma}
corresponds to an intensity measurement of the coherent state
|\gamma\rangle
with no information about its phase. Its form is demonstrated in App.
C
. Equation (
29
) can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\rho_{A}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\text{Tr}_{\alpha,\beta}\{K_{\alpha}K_{\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{|\psi_{t}\rangle\langle\psi_{t}|}}\,K_{\beta}^{\dagger}K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\}

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}c_{m}c_{m^{\prime}}|m\rangle\langle m^{\prime}|\cdot

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\alpha}\{K_{\alpha}|\alpha_{m}\rangle\langle\alpha_{m^{\prime}}|K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\}\text{Tr}_{\beta}\{K_{\beta}|\beta_{m}\rangle\langle\beta_{m^{\prime}}|K_{\beta}^{\dagger}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}c_{m}c_{m^{\prime}}\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}|m\rangle\langle m^{\prime}|\,,

with

\displaystyle\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\alpha}\{K_{\alpha}|\alpha_{m}\rangle\langle\alpha_{m^{\prime}}|K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\}

(33a)

\displaystyle\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\beta}\{K_{\beta}|\beta_{m}\rangle\langle\beta_{m^{\prime}}|K_{\beta}^{\dagger}\}

(33b)

and the normalization constant
\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\alpha,\beta,m}\{K_{\alpha}K_{\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{|\psi_{t}\rangle\langle\psi_{t}|}}\,K_{\beta}^{\dagger}K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\}

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m}\,.

Equation (
32
) explicitly shows that the coefficients
\langle M_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}
will modify the initial binomial distribution of the atoms given a certain set of measurement outcomes
\bar{I}_{\alpha},\bar{I}_{\beta}
. The form of these coefficients can be derived analytically up to second-order in
\varphi
, as detailed in App.
D
. Here, we only provide their final expressions, highlighting their dependence on
\varphi
and on the atomic degrees of freedom
m,m^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}=\exp(-\bar{I}_{\alpha}-\bar{I}_{\beta})\exp(V_{\alpha\beta})\exp(W_{\alpha\beta}(m+m^{\prime})\varphi)\exp((Y_{\alpha\beta}(m^{2}+m^{\prime 2})+Z_{\alpha\beta}m,m^{\prime})\varphi^{2})\,,

(35)

with :

\displaystyle V_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=4I_{0}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\alpha}}{I_{0}}}|\cos X_{t}|+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\beta}}{I_{0}}}|\sin X_{t}|-1\right)\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle W_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=2I_{0}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\alpha}}{I_{0}}}\frac{\sin X_{t}\cos X_{t}}{|\cos X_{t}|}+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\beta}}{I_{0}}}\frac{\cos X_{t}\sin X_{t}}{|\sin X_{t}|}-2\sin 2X_{t}\right)\,,

(36b)

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{I_{0}}{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\alpha}}{I_{0}}}\frac{1+\cos^{2}X_{t}}{|\cos X_{t}|}+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\beta}}{I_{0}}}\frac{1+\sin^{2}X_{t}}{|\sin X_{t}|}\right)\,,

(36c)

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=I_{0}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\alpha}}{I_{0}}}\frac{\sin^{2}X_{t}}{|\cos X_{t}|}+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\beta}}{I_{0}}}\frac{\cos^{2}X_{t}}{|\sin X_{t}|}\right)\,.

(36d)

Equations (
35
)-(
36
) are key analytical results of our derivation. In the above equations, any terms independent of
m,m^{\prime}
(namely
\exp(-\bar{I}_{\alpha}-\bar{I}_{\beta})\exp(V_{\alpha\beta})
) will cancel out and are physically meaningless. We can now provide exact expressions for the variance and average of the atomic collective spin operators relevant for demonstrating spin squeezing.

To quantify spin squeezing, we need to calculate
\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle
and
\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left\{m^{2}\rho_{A}^{\prime}\right\}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}m^{2}\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m}\,,

In contrast, the average of

\hat{J}_{x}

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}-m\right)\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m+1}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m+1}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dm}{\sqrt{2\pi(N/4)}}e^{-m^{2}/(2(N/4))}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dm}{\sqrt{\pi N/2}}e^{-m^{2}/(N/2)}\,.

(39)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle\!=\!\frac{N}{4}\left(\frac{1}{1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2}+\frac{N\varphi^{2}W^{2}_{\alpha\beta}}{(1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2)^{2}}\right)\!,

(40)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle\approx\frac{N}{2}\quad\text{for}\,\,N\gg 1

(41)

\displaystyle\lambda=-2Y_{\alpha\beta}-Z_{\alpha\beta}=2I_{0}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\alpha}}{I_{0}}}|\cos X_{t}|+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\beta}}{I_{0}}}|\sin X_{t}|\right)\,.

\displaystyle\xi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\frac{\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle}{\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle^{2}}=\frac{1}{1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2}+\frac{N\varphi^{2}W^{2}_{\alpha\beta}}{(1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2)^{2}},

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2}\left(1+\frac{N\varphi^{2}W^{2}_{\alpha\beta}}{1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2}\right)\,.

\bar{I}_{\alpha},\bar{I}_{\beta}

X_{t}

\bar{I}_{\alpha}=\langle I_{\alpha}\rangle=4I_{0}\cos^{2}(X_{t})

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=\frac{1}{1+2I_{0}N\varphi^{2}}.

(44)

\xi^{2}=\frac{1}{1+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\eta d}}}\,.

(45)

This difference originates from the fact that we did not use local oscillators to isolate the phase directly, our scheme is based on a ’self-heterodyne’ detection. This also means that we maintain a time dependence in the intensity measurement’s variance. Note, that

(

after measurement. Additionally, the authors in

When discussing experimental implementations, additional key parameters need to be taken into account, in particular the noise coming from the spontaneous decay (decoherence) of the atoms, characterized by the average scattering rate per atom
\eta
. Depending on the atomic system and the experimental platform, noise arising from scattered photons enters in different ways in the final expression of the squeezing parameter

;

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime 2}=\frac{1}{(1-\eta)^{2}}\,\xi^{2}=\frac{1}{(1-\eta)^{2}}\frac{1}{1+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\eta d}}}\,.

Hence, increasing the light intensity in order to achieve more squeezing induces atomic scattering, which will in turn preclude the increase in squeezing. Choosing an experimental sample characterized by a large optical depth is therefore one of the main criteria for achieving strong spin squeezing. In Fig.

The key experimental aspects that should be considered for particular implementations are listed in App.
A
, but several points require a particular attention. Importantly, our proposal relies on the capability to shape narrow spectral structures with large (
d\sim 10-40
) optical depths: it should be explicitly assessed which combination of optical depth/spectral width are experimentally accessible via usual optical pumping techniques. Linked with this, in our analysis we considered a single detuning
\delta\omega
for all the atoms, while a non-zero spectral width will be obtained experimentally. Given that the required detuning can be comparable with the structure width, a refined analysis might be needed to take into account such imperfection. Another aspect concerns the structure of the atomic system: here we considered that the spin transition is only composed of two levels,
|\!\!\uparrow\rangle
and
|\!\!\downarrow\rangle
. However, in Eu
3+
:Y
2
SiO
5
and Pr
3+
:Y
2
SiO
5
, these levels actually consist in two degenerate states, whose degeneracy can be lifted by applying a magnetic field
Cruzeiro et al. 2018
;
Lovrić et al. 2012
. Spin and optical manipulations therefore involve complex evolution within four-level systems, whose dynamics require specific considerations
Etesse et al. 2021
. Further studies would allow determining the influence of such peculiarities, which go beyond the derivations treated here. Finally, the scattering model (
46
) that we chose to adopt relies on the hypothesis that transitions are essentially cycling transitions (see
Saffman et al. 2009
, Eq.(12)). This is motivated by the fact that the branching ratios of the considered systems are mainly polarized
Lauritzen et al. 2012
;
Cruzeiro et al. 2018
;
Nilsson et al. 2004
;
Lovrić et al. 2012
, but does not reflect the strict reality of these systems. This means that the actual squeezing parameter could be lower than predicted. The results in this work motivate future investigations with more precise models to confirm REIDCs as promising platforms for achieving spin squeezing.

VIII
Conclusion

With this work, we provide a complementary theoretical approach to existing works for demonstrating spin squeezing. We employed this approach for predicting spin squeezing with rare-earth ion-doped crystals, a solid-state platform which is highly promising for quantum technologies thanks to their very long coherence times and their straightforward integration. Despite their lower light-matter coupling than alkali ensembles, the particularity of their atomic structure allows reaching squeezing parameter ranging from 3 to 8 dB with Eu
3+
:Y
2
SiO
5
and Pr
3+
:Y
2
SiO
5
, at the state-of-the-art of current realizations. These values would place
rare-earth ion-doped crystals as serious counterparts to
gaseous systems, while retaining all
strengths of solid-state systems.

Appendix A
Experimental implementation

This proposal relies on the coherent manipulation of spin transitions to initialize the atomic ensemble in a coherent spin state, and then to optically address these transitions independently for the readout. Within rare-earth experimental platforms, atomic nuclear as well as electronic spins can be used for this purpose, such that both Kramers and non-Kramers rare-earths can in principle be used to implement such experiments. Given that such materials are usually strongly inhomogeneously broadened, optical pumping of the atomic population must be performed to select a given class of atoms: this corresponds to performing the so-called class-cleaning procedure
Lauritzen et al. 2012
. As stated in the body of the paper, we focus on the case of europium- and praseodymium-doped Y
2
SiO
5
, workhorses for the implementation of quantum information protocols
Jobez et al. 2015
;
Ma et al. 2021

Appendix B
Variance of light

For pedagogical purposes, we detail in this Appendix the calculation of the light variance when the photocurrents are measured independently from each other and when the difference of photocurrents is measured. These two cases are relevant experimentally as presented in the main text.

B.1
Photocurrents measured separately

In case the photocurrents associated to the two photonic modes
\alpha,\beta
are measured independently from each other, the light intensity operator is simply the sum of the two photon number operators:

\displaystyle\hat{I}=\hat{n}_{\alpha}+\hat{n}_{\beta}=\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}\,.

(52)

The variance is calculated from
\langle\hat{I}^{2}\rangle
with

\displaystyle\hat{I}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}+2\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger})^{2}\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{2}+\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+(\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger})^{2}\hat{a}_{\beta}^{2}+\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}+2\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}\,,

(54)

where we have used the commutation relations
[\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]=\delta_{ij}
. When taking the average with respect to the atomic + photonic state
|\psi_{m}\rangle
, see Eq. (
16
), and tracing out the atomic degrees of freedom, we get:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{I}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\{|\alpha_{m}|^{4}+|\alpha_{m}|^{2}+|\beta_{m}|^{4}+|\beta_{m}|^{2}+2|\alpha_{m}|^{2}|\beta_{m}|^{2}\}.

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\{\big(|\alpha_{m}|^{2}+|\beta_{m}|^{2}\big)^{2}+|\alpha_{m}|^{2}+|\beta_{m}|^{2}\}\,.

(56)

Inserting the exact expressions for the amplitudes
\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{m}
, see Eqs. (
19
) with
|\alpha|^{2}=|\beta|^{2}=I_{0}
, we use

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\left(X-mk/2\right)+\sin^{2}\left(X+mk/2\right)=1+\sin(2X)\sin(mk)

(57)

to obtain

\displaystyle\langle\hat{I}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\left\{(4I_{0}^{2}\cos^{2}(X_{t}-m\varphi/2)+4I_{0}\sin^{2}(X_{t}+m\varphi/2))^{2}+4I_{0}(\cos^{2}(X_{t}-m\varphi/2)+\sin^{2}(X_{t}+m\varphi/2))\right\}

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16I_{0}^{2}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}(1+\sin(2X_{t})\sin(m\varphi))+4I_{0}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}(1+\sin(2X_{t})\sin(m\varphi)).

(59)

Keeping terms up to second order in
\varphi
, the sum over
m
is performed exploiting the binomial distribution of the coefficients
c_{m}
,
\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}=1
,
\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}m=0
and
\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}m^{2}=N/4
. This leads to Eq. (
26
) in the main text.

In a similar manner, one can also calculate
\langle\hat{I}_{\alpha}\rangle
,
\langle\hat{I}_{\alpha}^{2}\rangle
,
\langle\hat{I}_{\beta}\rangle
,
\langle\hat{I}_{\beta}^{2}\rangle
. The mean values of the intensity operators for
\alpha
and
\beta
modes are, up to second order in
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{I}_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=4I_{0}\cos^{2}(X_{t})-8I_{0}N\varphi^{2}\cos^{2}(X_{t})\cos(2X_{t}),

(60)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{I}_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=4I_{0}\sin^{2}(X_{t})+8I_{0}N\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}(X_{t})\cos(2X_{t}).

(61)

and the variances are, to the same order,

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{I}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=4I_{0}\left(\cos^{2}(X_{t})+2I_{0}N\varphi^{2}\cos^{2}(X_{t})\cos(2X_{t})-I_{0}N\varphi^{2}(\cos(2X_{t})+\cos(4X_{t}))\right),

(62)

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{I}_{\beta})

\displaystyle=4I_{0}\left(\sin^{2}(X_{t})-2I_{0}N\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}(X_{t})\cos(2X_{t})+I_{0}N\varphi^{2}(\cos(2X_{t})-\cos(4X_{t}))\right).

(63)

These expressions are used when showing the dependence of the squeezing parameter on the measurement outcomes in Fig.
3
.

B.2
Difference of photocurrent

The advantage of measuring the difference in photocurrent is to cancel out the two intensities of the photocurrents
\hat{I}_{\alpha}
and
\hat{I}_{\beta}
such that it becomes easier to access experimentally the fluctuations of this operator that depends on the atomic state via
m\varphi
. To cancel out the difference in photocurrents, one needs to exploit the phase
\theta
between the two coherent states, such that the amplituds
\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{m}
become:

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\cos(X_{t}-m\varphi/2)

(64a)

\displaystyle\beta_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\beta\cos(X_{t}+m\varphi/2)\,.

(64b)

The operator for the difference of photocurrents is

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{I}=\hat{n}_{\alpha}-\hat{n}_{\beta}\,.

(65)

and its average with respect to
|\psi_{m}\rangle
with the coherent states being given by Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle\Delta\hat{I}\rangle=4I_{0}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\sin(2X_{t})\sin(m\varphi)=\mathcal{O}(\varphi^{3})\,.

(66)

The variance is calculated from
\Delta\hat{I}^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{I}^{2}=(\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger})^{2}\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{2}+\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+(\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger})^{2}\hat{a}_{\beta}^{2}+\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}-2\hat{a}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\beta}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\beta}\,.

(67)

Evaluating this operator with respect to the amplitudes Eq. (
64a
), we get up to second order in
\varphi
:

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\Delta\hat{I}^{2})=4I_{0}\left(1+\cos(2X_{t})+\frac{N\varphi^{2}}{4}(4I_{0}\sin^{2}(2X_{t})-\cos(2X_{t})/2)\right)\,.

(68)

Appendix C
POVM and Kraus operator associated with measuring the intensity of coherent states

Measuring experimentally the intensity of a coherent state
|\gamma\rangle=||\gamma|e^{i\Psi}\rangle
without any information about its phase
\Psi
corresponds in measurement theory to the POVM

defined as

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\Psi}{2\pi}|\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma|,

(69)

This POVM satisfies
\int d|\gamma|^{2}M_{\gamma}=\mathbb{I}
and that
M_{\gamma}>0
. One can express

in the Fock basis by inserting the definition of a coherent state in the Fock basis

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\Psi}{2\pi}e^{-|\gamma|^{2}}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{|\gamma|^{n+n^{\prime}}e^{i\psi(n-n^{\prime})}}{\sqrt{n!n^{\prime}!}}|n\rangle\langle n^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=e^{-|\gamma|^{2}}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{|\gamma|^{n+n^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n!n^{\prime}!}}|n\rangle\langle n^{\prime}|\underbrace{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\,\frac{d\Psi}{2\pi}e^{i\psi(n-n^{\prime})}}_{\delta(n-n^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=e^{-|\gamma|^{2}}\sum_{n}\frac{|\gamma|^{2n}}{n!}|n\rangle\langle n|\,.

(70)

The corresponding Kraus operators are choosen to be
K_{\gamma}=\sqrt{M_{\gamma}}
:

\displaystyle K_{\gamma}=e^{-|\gamma|^{2}/2}\sum_{n}\frac{|\gamma|^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle\langle n|\,.

(71)

and
m=-N/2
, which evaluate to
0
anyway), so we can write

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m}

\displaystyle c_{m}c_{m+1}\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m+1}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m+1}\sqrt{(N/2-m)(N/2+1+m)},

where we explicitly denoted that the coefficients from the optical contribution should be evaluated for
(m,m^{\prime})=(m,m+1)
.
Using the recursive binomial relation

\displaystyle c_{m+1}=c_{m}\sqrt{\frac{\frac{N}{2}-m}{\frac{N}{2}+m+1}},

(100)

it is easy to show that

\displaystyle c_{m}c_{m+1}\sqrt{(N/2-m)(N/2+1+m)}=c_{m}^{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}-m\right).

(101)

Putting it all together, we get Eq. (
VI
) in the main text:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}(N/2-m)\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m+1}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m+1}.

(102)

At this point let us refer back to the effect of the additional phase which appears in front of
|m\rangle
, which we neglected in the main text for clarity. The phase factor reads as
\exp(i(\omega_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha})t_{p}m)
(cf. the effective Hamiltonian Eq. (
III.1
)). In any observable where
\langle m|m^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}
appears, e.g.
\langle I\rangle,\langle\hat{J}_{z}\rangle
, this phase term will cancel with its conjugate. The only observable where this phase term will appear is
\hat{J}_{x}
. There, the phase will appear in the two terms of (
99
) as
\exp(i(\omega_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha})t_{p}(m-m^{\prime}))
. Due to the Kronecker deltas, this results in
\exp(-i(\omega_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha})t_{p})
in the first term and
\exp(+i(\omega_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha})t_{p})
in the second. As a consequence, a simple factorization is not possible, and an additional cosine factor will appear
\cos((\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta})t_{p})
factor for
\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle
. Notice that for
\langle\hat{J}_{y}\rangle
the same happens, but with
\sin((\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta})t_{p})
. This implies that, depending on the beating frequency and pulse duration values, the mean direction of the spins in the
x
–
y
plane will end up rotated. Hence, the squeezing value as defined in (
3
), considering a given rotated
x
direction, will remain unaffected by the phase factor appearing in front of
|m\rangle
. Experimentally, it means that one would not be able to assess exactly the mean direction of the spin squeezed state. Whether it constitutes a drawback or not depends on how one exploits this squeezed state for specific applications, which goes beyond the scope of this work.

Appendix F
Gaussian integrals

For evaluating the squeezing parameter
\xi^{2}
, we need to compute the following quantities:

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left\{m^{2}\rho_{A}^{\prime}\right\}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}m^{2}\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m}\,,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}-m\right)\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m+1}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m+1}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left\{\rho_{A}^{\prime}\right\}=\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m}\,.

(103)

As explained in the main text, for large coherent spin states, the Dicke states approximately follow a normal distribution as the limit of the binomial distribution. As a consequence, the sum over
m
can be replaced by a Gaussian integral:

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}^{2}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dm}{\sqrt{2\pi(N/4)}}e^{-m^{2}/(2(N/4))}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dm}{\sqrt{\pi N/2}}e^{-m^{2}/(N/2)}\,.

(104)

With the expression of
\langle M_{\alpha}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}\langle M_{\beta}\rangle_{m,m^{\prime}}
given by Eqs. (
35
)-(
36
), the different averages then amount to calculate the following integrals:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\bar{I}_{\alpha}-\bar{I}_{\beta}}e^{V_{\alpha\beta}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dm}{\sqrt{\pi N/2}}e^{-m^{2}\varphi^{2}\lambda}e^{2m\varphi W_{\alpha\beta}}\,,

(105)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\bar{I}_{\alpha}-\bar{I}_{\beta}}e^{V_{\alpha\beta}}}{\mathcal{N}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dm\,m^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi N/2}}e^{-m^{2}\varphi^{2}\lambda}e^{2m\varphi W_{\alpha\beta}}\,,

(106)

and

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\bar{I}_{\alpha}-\bar{I}_{\beta}}e^{V_{\alpha\beta}}}{\mathcal{N}}e^{Y_{\alpha\beta}\varphi^{2}}e^{W_{\alpha\beta}\varphi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dm\left(\frac{N}{2}-m\right)e^{-\lambda m^{2}\varphi^{2}}e^{(2W_{\alpha\beta}\varphi-\lambda\varphi^{2})m}\,,

(107)

with the notation (see Eq. (
VI
))

\displaystyle\lambda:=-(2Y_{\alpha\beta}+Z_{\alpha\beta})=2I_{0}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\alpha}}{I_{0}}}|\cos X_{t}|+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{I}_{\beta}}{I_{0}}}|\sin X_{t}|\right)\,.

(108)

The convergence of the Gaussian integrals is ensured when
\lambda\geq 0
. The different integrals appearing above are Gaussian integrals of the following type:

\displaystyle G_{1}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dxe^{-ax^{2}+bx+c}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}e^{b^{2}/(4a)+c}\quad a\geq 0\,,

(109)

\displaystyle G_{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,x^{2}e^{-ax^{2}+bx+c}=\frac{2a+b^{2}}{4a^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}e^{b^{2}/(4a)+c}=\frac{2a+b^{2}}{4a^{2}}G_{1}\quad a\geq 0\,,

(110)

\displaystyle G_{3}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\left(\frac{N}{2}-x\right)e^{-ax^{2}+bx+c}=\frac{-b+aN}{2a}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}e^{b^{2}/(4a)+c}=\frac{-b+aN}{2a}G_{1}\quad a\geq 0\,.

(111)

Using these results, we get :

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\bar{I}_{\alpha}-\bar{I}_{\beta}}e^{V_{\alpha\beta}}\frac{e^{N\varphi^{2}W_{\alpha\beta}^{2}/(2(1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2))}}{\sqrt{1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2}}\,,

(112)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{4}\left[\frac{1}{1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2}+\frac{NW_{\alpha\beta}^{2}\varphi^{2}}{(1+N\varphi^{2}\lambda/2)^{2}}\right]\,,

(113)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{J}_{x}\rangle=\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{W_{\alpha\beta}\varphi}{1+N\lambda\varphi^{2}/2}\right)e^{-N\varphi^{4}\lambda(W_{\alpha\beta}+\lambda/4)/(2+N\lambda\varphi^{2})}\,

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}\quad\text{for}\,\,N\gg 1,\,I_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(N).

(114)

The last approximation is obtained by using that
\bar{I}_{\alpha}
(
\bar{I}_{\beta}
) typically only deviates from its mean value
4I_{0}\cos^{2}(X_{t})
(
4I_{0}\sin^{2}(X_{t})
) by at most
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{I_{0}})
(see Eq. (
62
)). Under such circumstances
W_{\alpha\beta}
is at most of the order of
\sqrt{N}
, such that
W_{\alpha\beta}\,\varphi
is of the order of
1/\sqrt{N}
, which vanishes for
N\gg 1
. These expressions correspond to Eqs. (
40
) and (
41
) in the main text.

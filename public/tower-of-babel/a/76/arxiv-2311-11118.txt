Introduction

The celebrated Ratner’s measure classification theorem and orbit closure theorem, stand on homogeneous spaces with finite volume over a local field of characteristic zero
[
6
,
7
,
8
,

,

,

For positive characteristics, we refer to
[

Thanks to her theorems,
unipotent dynamics have been an important tool for classifying the closures of unipotent orbits.
As in her paper
[
6
]
, a direct consequence of her theorem is the classification of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbits in a homogeneous space
\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, where
\Gamma
is a lattice in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
In this case, every
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit is either closed or dense in the homogeneous space.
Since
\Gamma\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
can be viewed as a frame bundle of the 3-manifold
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{3}
of finite volume, it is an example of geometric rigidity.
We remark that this is a 3-dimensional case of a more general result of Shah
[
12
]
.
Shah proved his theorem in a purely topological way while Ratner used her measure classification theorem.

On the other hand, developments in geometric rigidity within homogeneous spaces of infinite volume have recently emerged
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
23
]
.
The technical difficulty of the infinite volume case arises from the fact that,
unlike the finite volume case, we cannot directly use the recurrence of unipotent orbits into the given compact region as almost every unipotent orbit (with respect to Haar measure) escapes any compact region (see Remark
1.6
, and also
[
1
]
).
Nevertheless, Mohammadi, McMullen, and Oh introduced the concept of
K
-thickness to capture scarce recurrence of unipotent orbits into a compact region
[
1
]
.
Among 3-manifolds of infinite volume,
convex cocompact acylindrical 3-manifolds provide scarce recurrence allowing for the application of their method, known as the unipotent blowup.
Thanks to their insights, unipotent dynamics remains a crucial tool for the classification of orbit closures.

In this article,
we investigate the classification of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-orbit closures on a non-Archimedean homogeneous space

Main theorems

Identify the visual boundaries

Remark 1.6
.

Now let us explain the necessity of the conditions in Definition
1.1
.
Condition (1) restricts the degree of vertices in the invariant subtree
S_{\Gamma}
.
We will use the unipotent blowup lemma analogous to that of
[
1
]
.
Denote the subtree
g.T_{H}\cap S_{\Gamma}
by
S_{g}
.
Let
\cal{U}
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-unipotent subgroup of
G
.
To obtain the unipotent blowup lemma, we desire a certain recurrence property that the
(-\infty)
-point of a geodesic in
S_{g}
returns infinitely many times into
\partial S_{g}
by
\cal{U}
-action within a bounded time gap.
More precisely,

(1.3)

if
\ell=(g_{\ell}.0,g_{\ell}.\infty)
is a given geodesic in
S_{g}
and
u_{i}\in\cal{U}
such that
g_{\ell}u_{i}.0\in S_{g}
, then we want to show the existence of
u_{j}\in\cal{U}-\{u_{i}\}
such that
g_{\ell}u_{j}.0\in S_{g}
and

\lvert\log_{p}\lVert u_{i}-e\rVert-\log_{p}\lVert u_{j}-e\rVert\rvert

is bounded.

To ensure the recurrence property described in (1.3),
\partial g.T_{H}\cap\partial S_{\Gamma}
must be infinite unless it is empty.
Suppose that there exists some constant
K

such that for any vertex
{\mathbf{v}}\in S_{g}
with
\operatorname{deg}_{S_{g}}({\mathbf{v}})\geq 3
,
there exists
{\mathbf{w}}\in S_{g}
such that
\operatorname{deg}_{S_{g}}({\mathbf{w}})\geq 3
and the number of edges between
{\mathbf{v}}
and
{\mathbf{w}}
is less than
K
.
The existence of such
K
implies that

\partial g.T_{H}\cap\partial S_{\Gamma}
is infinite
(see Proposition
3.15
for details).
Then, we can prove that for every
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
x\in(p^{-(K+l)}\mathbb{Z}_{p}-p^{-l}\mathbb{Z}_{p})
so that
g_{\ell}u_{x}.0\in\partial S_{g}
, where
u_{x}
is a unipotent element defined by

(see Proposition
3.2
).

In
[
1
]
, the limit set of a convex cocompact acylindrical subgroup is a Sierpiński carpet.
The limit set being a Sierpiński carpet implies
an important property called
K
-thickness (see Definition
3.1
for
K
-thickness in our setting), which ensures the recurrence property in (1.3).
However, a direct analog of Sierpiński carpet in
\partial T_{G}
is not obvious since
\partial T_{G}
is fractal and totally discrete.
Thus, instead of dealing with the boundary
\partial T_{G}
, we use the tree

to give a condition, which is condition (1).

Without condition (1) in Definition

, the boundary

may be a finite set.
If
\partial S_{g}
is finite, we cannot ensure the unipotent blowup lemma.
We provided such non-example in Example

Condition (2) in Definition

follows from Zariski density of

in the real case.
However, in our case, Zariski density does not ensure the density of

in
\mathbb{\mu}_{p^{2}-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{p}^{1}
(see Section

In this regard, we need condition (2), a condition stronger than the Zariski density of

We will use this condition to prove Proposition

In this section, we gather preliminary facts about
p

-adic analog of a symmetric space, frames, axes of hyperbolic elements, and examples of convex cocompact highly-branched subgroups.

2.1.
p
-adic field and its unramified quadratic extension

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the
p
-adic field for an odd prime
p
, i.e.,

\mathbb{Q}_{p}=\left\{\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}a_{i}p^{i}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Its residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Let
\nu_{p}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{p}
be the
p
-adic norm.
If
a=\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}a_{i}p^{i}
, then

\lvert a\rvert_{p}=p^{-\nu_{p}(a)}=p^{-n}.

Using the
p
-adic norm, the ring of integers and the unit ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are characterized as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\{a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:\lvert a\rvert_{p}\leq 1\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}=\{a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:\lvert a\rvert_{p}=1\}.

As in the introduction, let
\mathbb{K}
be a degree
2
unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{K}}
be the ring of integers of
\mathbb{K}
.
We remark that an unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of each degree is unique (see Section 6.5 of
[
37
]
).
Since
\mathbb{K}
is unramified, we can choose the uniformizer of
\mathbb{K}
to be
p
.
Hence we can use
\nu_{p}
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{p}
on
\mathbb{K}
as well.
The residue field of
\mathbb{K}
is
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{K}}/p{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{K}}
.
Moreover, there is a degree
2
irreducible polynomial
f(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
and its root
\omega\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}
so that we can express

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)=\left\{\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}(a_{i}+\omega b_{i})p^{i}:a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{K}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\omega)=\{x\in\mathbb{K}:\lvert x\rvert_{p}\leq 1\},\hskip 3.0pt{\rm{and}}\hskip 5.0pt{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\omega)^{\times}=\{x\in\mathbb{K}:\lvert x\rvert_{p}=1\}.

Here,
for
x=a+\omega b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)
,

\lvert x\rvert_{p}=\max\{\left|a\right|_{p},\left|b\right|_{p}\}.

2.1.1.
p
-adic exponential and trigonometric functions

In this subsection, we state properties of
p
-adic exponential and trigonometric functions (see
[
34
, Chapter II, Section 4]
for more details).

Remark that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
contains
i=\sqrt{-1}
if and only if
p^{f}\not\equiv 3\text{(mod 4)}
for
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
For all odd prime

, we obtain

.
Since our residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, we have
i\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)
for all

There are 2 cases for
\omega
.
The first case is
\omega=\alpha i
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
so that
\omega i\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and the second case is
\omega i\notin\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Let
\lambda=\omega_{p}i
, where
\omega_{p}=\omega
if
\omega i\notin\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and
\omega_{p}=1
if
\omega=\alpha i
.
If we let
\lambda=\omega_{p}i
, then
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\omega)
.
Moreover, there is an isomorphism between
\mathbb{\mu}_{p^{2}-1}\cdot\mathbb{S}_{p}^{1}
defined on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\lambda)
and
\mathbb{\mu}_{p^{2}-1}\cdot\mathbb{S}_{p}^{1}
defined on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)
.
Thus, for computational convenience, let us use
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\lambda)
instead of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)
until Subsection
2.1.2

For

Let

\mathbb{\mu}_{p^{2}-1}\cdot\mathbb{S}_{p}^{1}

We start with the following statement in

, define elements

and

\displaystyle\gamma=g\operatorname{diag}(p^{n_{1}}\Theta_{1},p^{n_{2}}\Theta_{2})g^{-1},{\text{ and }}\gamma^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\operatorname{diag}(p^{n^{\prime}_{1}}\Theta^{\prime}_{1},p^{n^{\prime}_{2}}\Theta^{\prime}_{2})g^{\prime-1},

Lemma 3.3

Some new inequalities for univalent functions
M. M. Motamedinezhad and R. Kargar

Abstract
. By use of a modified Nunokawa’s lemma, we obtain some new conditions for univalence. Also, some sharp inequalities concerning univalent functions are presented.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
30C45

1.
Introduction

Let
\Delta
be the open unit disc on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{B}
the class of functions
w(z)
analytic in
\Delta
with
w(0)=0
and
|w(z)|<1
, and denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of all functions that are analytic and normalized in
\Delta
. A function belongs to the class
\mathcal{A}
has the following form

(1.1)

f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}+\cdots\quad(z\in\Delta).

The subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of univalent functions in
\Delta
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
.
Also we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}
the class of starlike functions and denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mu)
the class of all starlike functions of order
\mu
,
0\leq\mu<1
. Put
\mathcal{S}^{*}(0)\equiv\mathcal{S}^{*}
.
Analytically
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mu)
if, and only if
{\rm Re}\left\{zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\right\}>\mu
.
Also, we say that the function
f
is convex of order
\mu
in
\Delta
, if and only if
zf^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mu)
. The class of convex functions of order
\mu
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}(\mu)
and the class of convex functions is denoted by
\mathcal{K}\equiv\mathcal{K}(0)
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
be the subclass
of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of functions
f
which satisfy

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}<\delta\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
\delta>1
. Also, we say that
f\in\mathcal{N}(\delta)
if and only if
zf^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{M}(\delta)
. The class
\mathcal{M}(\delta)
and
\mathcal{N}(\delta)
for
1<\delta\leq 4/3
were introduced by Uralegaddi
et

al.

[
14
]
. Also, the class
\mathcal{N}(\delta)
including locally univalent functions was studied extensively by Kargar
et

al
.,
[
4
]
(see also,
[
7
]
) and they proved that
\mathcal{N}(3/2)\subset\mathcal{S}
. Thus,
\mathcal{M}(3/2)
and
\mathcal{N}(3/2)
are a subclass of univalent functions.
A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be close–to–convex, if there exists
a convex function
g
such that

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>0\quad(z\in\Delta).

This class was introduced by Kaplan in 1952
[
5
]
and we denote by
\mathcal{CK}
.
If we take
g(z)\equiv z
, then the class
\mathcal{CK}
becomes the Noshiro–Warschawski class as follows

\mathcal{C}:=\{f\in\mathcal{A}:{\rm Re}\{f^{\prime}(z)\}>0,\,z\in\Delta\}.

Note that
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{S}
, by the basic Noshiro–Warschawski lemma
[
1
, §2.6]
.

In 1977, Chichra
[
2
]
studied the class of all functions whose derivative has positive real part in the unit disc
\Delta
. Indeed, he denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
which satisfying the following inequality

{\rm Re}\left\{f^{\prime}(z)+\gamma zf^{\prime\prime}(z)\right\}>0\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
\gamma\geq 0
, and showed that
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}\subset\mathcal{S}
. Also, he proved that if
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
and
{\rm Re}\{\gamma\}\geq 0
, then
{\rm Re}\{f^{\prime}(z)\}>0
in
\Delta
. Recent result, also was obtained by Lewandowski
et

al.
, see
[
6
]
.
On the other hand, Gao and Zhou
[
3
]
considered the class
R(\beta,\gamma)
as follows:

R(\beta,\gamma)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:{\rm Re}\left\{f^{\prime}(z)+\gamma zf^{\prime\prime}(z)\right\}>\beta,\ \ \gamma>0,\,\beta<1,\,z\in\Delta\right\}.

They found the extreme points of
R(\beta,\gamma)
, some sharp bounds of certain
linear problems, the sharp bounds for
{\rm Re}\{f^{\prime}(z)\}
and
{\rm Re}\{f(z)/z\}
and determined the number

\beta(\gamma)
such that
R(\beta,\gamma)\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}
, where
\gamma
is certain fixed number in
[1,\infty)
. Note that a generalization of the class
R(\beta,\gamma)
was studied by Srivastava et al. in
[
12
]
.

To prove of our main results we need the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

(
Simple generalization of Nunokawa’s lemma
[
9
]
)
Let
p(z)
be an analytic function in
|z|<1
of the form

p(z)=1+\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}\quad(c_{m}\neq 0),

with
p(z)\neq 0
in
|z|<1
. If there exists a point
z_{0}
,
|z_{0}|<1
, such that

{\rm Re}\{p(z)\}>0\quad for\quad|z|<|z_{0}|

and

{\rm Re}\{p(z)\}=0,\quad a=|p(z_{0})|\neq 0,

then we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z)}=ik,

where
k
is real number and

k\geq\frac{m}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)\geq m\geq 1\ \ when\ \ p(z_{0})=ia

and

k\leq-\frac{m}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{a}\right)\leq-m\leq-1\ \ when\ \ p(z_{0})=-ia.

In the present paper, motivated by the works
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
we shall determine some new sufficient conditions for close–to–convexity. Also, some another applications of Nunokawa’s lemma concerning the class
\mathcal{M}(3/2)
,
\mathcal{N}(3/2)
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}(1/2)
are presented.

2.
Application of Nunokawa’s lemma

In this section, we present some applications of Nunokawa’s lemma. The first result is as follows which it introduces new conditions for close–to–convexity, hence univalence.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f
be of the form (
1.1
). If
f
satisfies

(2.1)

{\rm Re}\left\{f^{\prime}(z)+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)\right\}\leq 1\quad(z\in\Delta),

or

(2.2)

{\rm Re}\left\{f^{\prime}(z)+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)\right\}>1\quad(z\in\Delta),

then

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

(2.3)

|f^{\prime}(z)-1|<1\quad(z\in\Delta).

The inequality (
2.3
) means that
f
is close–to–convex (hence univalent).

Proof.

Let us define the function
p(z)
as follows

(2.4)

p(z)=\frac{2}{f^{\prime}(z)}-1\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
f\in\mathcal{A}
. Then
p
is analytic function in
\Delta
and
p(0)=1
. With a simple computation, (
2.4
) implies that

(2.5)

f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{2}{1+p(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

(2.6)

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=1-\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{1+p(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Now, from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(z)+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(z)\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{1+p(z)}\right)\left(1-\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}\frac{p(z)}{1+p(z)}\right).

Suppose that there exists a point
z_{0}\in\Delta
so that

|f^{\prime}(z)-1|<1\quad(|z|<|z_{0}|)

and

|f^{\prime}(z_{0})-1|=1.

Then applying (
2.3
), we obtain
{\rm Re}\{p(z)\}>0
for
|z|<|z_{0}|
and
{\rm Re}\{p(z_{0})\}=0
. Also
p(z_{0})\neq 0
. Now, by using of Nunokawa’s lemma, we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=ik,

where

(2.8)

k\geq\frac{1+a^{2}}{2a}\quad{\rm when}\quad p(z_{0})=ia

and

k\leq-\frac{1+a^{2}}{2a}\quad{\rm when}\quad p(z_{0})=-ia.

First, we investigate the case
p(z_{0})=ia
. From (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{f^{\prime}(z_{0})+z_{0}f^{\prime\prime}(z_{0})\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{\left(\frac{2}{1+p(z_{0})}\right)\left(1-\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\frac{p(z_{0})}{1+p(z_{0})}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{\left(\frac{2}{1+ia}\right)\left(1-ik.\frac{ia}{1+ia}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{\frac{2(1+ka)+2ia}{(1+ia)^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1+ka)(1-a^{2})+4a^{2}}{(1+a^{2})^{2}}=:k(a).

Since
a>0
, the following two cases arise.

Case 1.
Let
0<a<1
. Then by (
2.8
), we get

k(a)\geq\frac{3+2a^{2}-a^{4}}{(1+a^{2})^{2}}=:h(a).

It is easy to see that
h
is decreasing and thus

h(a)>h(1)=1.

But, this is contradictory with assumption (
2.1
). Therefore we have

{\rm Re}\{p(z)\}>0\quad(z\in\Delta)

or equivalently

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Finally, from (
2.4
) we have

|f^{\prime}(z)-1|=\left|\frac{1-p(z)}{1+p(z)}\right|<1\quad(z\in\Delta).

Case 2.
Let
a\geq 1
. Then by (
2.8
) we have
k(a)\leq h(a)
. Because
h
is decreasing function and
a\geq 1
we get
k(a)\leq h(a)\leq h(1)=1
. This contradicts the hypothesis (
2.2
) and thus we have the inequality (
2.3
). Note that the proof of the case
p(z_{0})=-ia
is similar and therefore we omit the details. The proof of this theorem here ends.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f
be of the form (
1.1
) and satisfies

(2.9)

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}<\frac{3}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Then

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{z}{f(z)}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

(2.10)

\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}-1\right|<1\quad(z\in\Delta).

The results are sharp for the function
z\mapsto z+z^{2}
.

Proof.

We define the function
p(z)
as follows

(2.11)

p(z):=\frac{2z}{f(z)}-1,

with
p(0)=1
. A simple calculation gives us

(2.12)

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=1-\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}\frac{p(z)}{1+p(z)}.

If there is a point
z_{0}\in\Delta
such that

\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}-1\right|<1\quad(|z|<|z_{0}|)

and

\left|\frac{f(z_{0})}{z_{0}}-1\right|=1,

then by (
2.10
), we get
{\rm Re}\{p(z)\}>0
when
|z|<|z_{0}|
,
{\rm Re}\{p(z_{0})\}=0
and
p(z_{0})\neq 0
. Now, by the Nunokawa lemma, we have

\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}=ik,

where

(2.13)

k\geq\frac{1+a^{2}}{2a}\quad{\rm when}\quad p(z_{0})=ia

and

(2.14)

k\leq-\frac{1+a^{2}}{2a}\quad{\rm when}\quad p(z_{0})=-ia.

We investigate the case (
2.13
) and let
a>0
. The proof of another case (
2.14
) is similar. From (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{z_{0}f^{\prime}(z_{0})}{f(z_{0})}\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{1-\frac{z_{0}p^{\prime}(z_{0})}{p(z_{0})}\frac{p(z_{0})}{1+p(z_{0})}\right\}

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\left\{1+\frac{ka}{1+ia}\right\}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{ka}{1+a^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}

and this contradicts the hypothesis (
2.9
). Ergo, we have

{\rm Re}\{p(z)\}>0\quad(z\in\Delta)

or equivalently

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{z}{f(z)}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Finally, (
2.11
) implies that

\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}-1\right|=\left|\frac{1-p(z)}{1+p(z)}\right|<1\quad(|z|<1)

and concluding the proof.
∎

It is well–known that (see
[
8
,
13
]
) if

{\rm Re}\left\{1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>0\quad(z\in\Delta),

then
{\rm Re}\{\sqrt{f^{\prime}(z)}\}>1/2
and
{\rm Re}\{f(z)/z\}>1/2
. Alos, these estimates are sharp. Now by using the Alexander theorem, we have the following.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f
be of the form (
1.1
) and satisfies

{\rm Re}\left\{1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}<\frac{3}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Then

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

\left|f^{\prime}(z)-1\right|<1\quad(z\in\Delta).

The results are sharp for the function
z\mapsto z+z^{2}/2
.

Finally, using the Nunokawa lemma we obtain the following well–known result, see
[
1
, p. 73]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
be starlike function of order
1/2
.
Then

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{f(z)}{z}\right\}>\frac{1}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

\left|\frac{z}{f(z)}-1\right|<1\quad(z\in\Delta).

The results are sharp for the function
z\mapsto\frac{z}{1+z}
.

Proof.

It is enough to define
p(z)+1=2f(z)/z
and to apply the Nunokawa lemma, then we get the desired result.
∎

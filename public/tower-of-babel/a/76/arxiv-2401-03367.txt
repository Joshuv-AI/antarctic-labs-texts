Entanglement detection length of multipartite quantum states
Fei Shi, Lin Chen, Giulio Chiribella, Qi Zhao

Abstract
Multipartite entanglement is a valuable resource for quantum technologies. However, detecting this resource can be challenging: for genuine multipartite entanglement, the detection may require global measurements that are hard to implement experimentally. Here we introduce the concept of entanglement detection length, defined as the minimum number of particles that have to be jointly measured in order to detect genuine multipartite entanglement. For symmetric states, we show that the entanglement detection length can be determined by testing separability of the marginal states. For general states, we provide an upper bound on the entanglement detection length based on semidefinite programming. We show that the entanglement detection length is generally smaller than the minimum observable length needed to uniquely determine a multipartite state, and we provide examples achieving the maximum gap between these two quantities.

EDL and SDL.
—
Consider a quantum system consisting of
n
particles, labeled by integers in the finite set
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. We denote by
{\cal H}_{i}
the Hilbert space of the
i
-th particle, and by
{\cal H}_{[n]}:=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\cal H}_{i}
the Hilbert space of the total system.
The set of all density matrices on
{\cal H}_{[n]}
will be denoted by
{\cal D}_{[n]}
. Subsystems will be identified by subsets of
[n]
. For a given subset
S\subseteq[n]
, the Hilbert space of the corresponding subsystem is
{\cal H}_{S}:=\bigotimes_{j\in S}\,{\cal H}_{j}
and the set of density matrices of
{\cal H}_{S}
is denoted by
{\cal D}_{S}
. For a given quantum state
\rho\in{\cal D}_{[n]}
, we will denote by
\rho_{S}\in{\cal D}_{S}
the marginal state of the state
\rho
on system
S
, namely
\rho_{S}:={\sf Tr}_{\overline{S}}[\rho]
, where
{\sf Tr}_{\overline{S}}
denotes the partial trace over the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\overline{S}}
associated to the complementary subsystem
\overline{S}:=[n]\setminus S
.

A pure state
|\psi\rangle\in{\cal H}
is
separable with respect to the bipartition
S|\overline{S}
if
|\psi\rangle=|\alpha\rangle_{S}\otimes|\beta\rangle_{\overline{S}}
, where
|\alpha\rangle_{S}
and
|\beta\rangle_{\overline{S}}
are pure states in
{\cal H}_{S}
and
{\cal H}_{\overline{S}}
, respectively. The state
|\psi\rangle
is called
biseparable
if there exists a proper subset
\emptyset\not=S\subsetneq[n]
such that
|\psi\rangle
is separable with respect to the bipartition
S|\overline{S}
. The state
|\psi\rangle
is called
fully separable
if it is separable with respect to all possible bipartitions. A mixed state
\rho\in{\cal D}_{[n]}
is biseparable (respectively, fully separable) if it can be written as a convex combination of biseparable (respectively, fully separable) pure states.
A state
\rho\in{\cal D}_{[n]}
is
entangled
if it is not fully separable, and
genuinely entangled
if it is not biseparable.

We now consider the problem of determining the entanglement of a state from the specification of its marginals.
Let
{\cal S}:=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{t}\}
be a collection of subsets of
[n]
. We define the
compatibility set

{\cal C}(\rho,{\cal S})
as the set of all density matrices

\sigma\in{\cal D}_{[n]}
that have the same marginals as
\rho
on the subsystems labeled by elements of
{\cal S}
: in formula,

\displaystyle{\cal C}(\rho,{\cal S}):=\big\{\sigma\in{\cal D}_{[n]}~\big|~\sigma_{S}=\rho_{S},\ \forall\,S\in{\cal S}\big\}\,.

(1)

Note that the compatibility
set is a convex set
chen2012ground
. If the set
{\cal C}(\rho,{\cal S})
contains only the state
\rho
, we say that
{\cal S}

determines
\rho
.
If the compatibility set
{\cal C}(\rho,{\cal S})
contains only genuinely entangled states, we say that
{\cal S}

detects
\rho
’s GME
. The set of all
{\cal S}
that determine
\rho
will be denoted by
{\sf SD}(\rho)
, while the set of all
{\cal S}
that detect
\rho
’s GME will be denoted by
{\sf ED}(\rho)
. For a genuinely entangled state
\rho
, one has the inclusion
{\sf SD}(\rho)\subseteq{\sf ED}(\rho)
.

The
entanglement detection length (EDL)
and the
state determination length (SDL)
of the state
\rho
are defined as

\displaystyle l(\rho):=\mathop{\rm min}_{{\cal S}\in{\sf ED}(\rho)}\mathop{\rm max}_{S\in{\cal S}}\,|S|\,,

(2)

and

\displaystyle L(\rho):=\mathop{\rm min}_{{\cal S}\in{\sf SD}(\rho)}\mathop{\rm max}_{S\in{\cal S}}\,|S|\,,

(3)

respectively. For pure states
|\psi\rangle
, we will slightly abuse the notation, writing
l(|\psi\rangle)
and
L(|\psi\rangle)
in place of
l(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
and
L(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
, respectively.

The SDL (respectively, EDL) quantifies the minimum number of particles that have to be jointly measured in order to determine a state (respectively, detect its GME). In particular, every experimental scheme that determines the state
\rho
(respectively, certifies the presence of GME in the state
\rho
) must include at least one
k
-body observable with
k\geq L(\rho)
(respectively,
k\geq l(\rho)
). In Section I of the Supplemental Material (SM), we show that the EDL and SDL are both invariant under local unitary (LU) operations, but may change under SLOCC. Moreover, since they are both integer-valued, they are nonconvex.

The definitions of EDL and SDL imply the inequality
l(\rho)\leq L(\rho)
for every genuinely entangled state
\rho
. This inequality is generally strict: for example, a 4-qubit pure state with non-zero gap between the EDL and SDL can be found,
e.g.,
in Ref.
miklin2016multiparticle
(see Section IX of the SM for details). Later in the paper, we will provide examples of states with maximum gap between the EDL and SDL.

Examples.
—
The notions of SDL and EDL are related to several important features of the key examples of multipartite entangled states considered in the literature. For example,
n
-qubit GHZ states

have maximum SDL, equal to
n
, since all their
k
-particle marginals with
k\leq n-1
are compatible with the separable state
\sigma=1/2(|0\rangle\langle 0|)^{\otimes n}+1/2(|1\rangle\langle 1|)^{\otimes n}
. For the same reason, they also have maximum EDL. In fact, one can show that the SDL (respectively, EDL) of an
n
-qubit (respectively, genuinely entangled) pure state is equal to
n
if and only if the state is “
\mathop{\rm GHZ}
-like”

, that is, it is LU-equivalent to an
n
-qubit generalized
\mathop{\rm GHZ}
state of the form
\alpha\,|0\rangle^{\otimes n}+\beta\,|1\rangle^{\otimes n}
, with
\alpha\beta\neq 0
.
Lower bounds on the EDL and SDL are also immediate for
k
-uniform states
scott2004multipartite
;
goyeneche2014genuinely
,
i.e.,

n
-qubit pure states for which all the
k
-body marginals are maximally mixed: since the
k
-body marginals are compatible with the maximally mixed
n
-qubit state, the SDL and EDL
k
-uniform states must be strictly larger than
k
.

Examples of entangled states with minimum SDL/EDL are provided unique ground states of 2-local Hamiltonians, such as projected pair entangled states
10.5555/2016976.2016982
: when entangled, they have SDL equal to 2, since their
2
-body marginals determine the expectation value of the Hamiltonian. When genuinely entangled, they have EDL equal to 2. Dicke states,
i.e.,

n
-qubit states of the form

|D_{n}^{i}\rangle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{i}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\\
s_{j}\in\{0,1\},\,\forall\,j\in[n]\\
\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}=i\end{subarray}}|s_{1}\rangle\otimes|s_{2}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|s_{n}\rangle\,,

(4)

are an example of this situation
chen2012correlations
. For
i\in[1,n-1]
, the Dicke state
|D_{n}^{i}\rangle
is genuinely entangled
dicke1954coherence
;
lucke2014detecting
, and its EDL is
l(|D_{n}^{i}\rangle)=2
. More generally, unique ground states of
k
-local Hamiltonians have SDL upper bounded by
k
, and the same bound applies to the EDL if they are genuinely entangled

.

Graph states
hein2004multiparty
;
guhne2005bell
;
hein2006entanglement
, including in particular cluster states
briegel2001persistent
and ring states
baccari2020scalable
, are another interesting class of multipartite states. For cluster states and ring states, we show that the SDL and EDL coincide and are equal to 3. For more general genuinely entangled graph states, we show that the SDL and EDL are between
3
and 1 plus the maximum degree of a vertex in the corresponding graph (see Section II of the SM for details).

EDL and SDL of symmetric states.
—
We now provide a complete characterization of the EDL for
n
-qubit symmetric states
i2016characterizing
, that is, states with support in the completely symmetric subspace (see Section III of the SM for a more detailed introduction). Notably, a symmetric state is either fully separable or genuinely entangled
ichikawa2008exchange
.

For entangled symmetric states, we have the following:

Proposition 1

For an
n
-qubit entangled symmetric state
\rho
,
l(\rho)=\mathop{\rm min}\{k\mid\rho_{[k]}\text{ is entangled}\}
.

The proof is provided in Section IV of the SM, where we compute the EDL in several special cases of pure and mixed symmetric states.

Proposition
1
implies that every symmetric state with EDL equal to 2 is a robust multipartite entangled state

, meaning that all its
k
-body marginals with
k\geq 2
are genuinely entangled.

Proposition
1
reduces the calculation of the EDL to the problem of determining whether a symmetric state is entangled or not. This reduction is important, because the entanglement of symmetric states is well studied
toth2009entanglement
;
eckert2002quantum
;
stockton2003characterizing
;
ichikawa2008exchange
;
tura2012four
;
augusiak2012entangled
;
wolfe2014certifying
;
yu2016separability
;
i2016characterizing
;
tura2018separability
;
imai2024collective
. For example, all symmetric states that are diagonal in the basis of the Dicke states are separable if and only if their density matrix has positive partial transpose with respect to
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
qubits

.

An explicit expression for the SDL can be provided for a family of Dicke-diagonal symmetric states:

Proposition 2

Let
\rho
be a symmetric state of the diagonal form
\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{i}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\langle D_{n}^{i}|
for some integer
k\leq n
, where
\lambda_{i}
is nonzero for all but one value of
i
, namely
\lambda_{i}\not=0,\,\forall\,i\in\{0,\dots,k\}\setminus\{i_{*}\}
for some
i_{*}\in\{0,\dots,k\}
.
Then,
L(\rho)=k+\mathop{\rm min}\{[(k-i_{*})\mod 2],|n-k|\}
.

The proof of Proposition
2
is given in Section V of the SM, where we compute the SDL for several examples of diagonal symmetric states.

For symmetric states, the collection of marginals needed to determine a state (detect its GME) have a simple graph-theoretic characterization. A hypergraph
G=(V,E)
consists of a set of vertices
V
and a set of hyperedges
E
, a hyperedge being a nonempty subset of
V
.
In this language, every collection
{\cal S}
of subsets of
[n]
defines a hypergraph
G=([n],{\cal S})
. A hypergraph is connected if, for any partition of its vertex set into two non-empty sets
X
and
Y
, there exists at least one hyperedge connecting a vertex in
X
to a vertex in
Y
.

Proposition 3

For an
n
-qubit entangled symmetric state
\rho
, a collection
{\cal S}
determines
\rho
(detects
\rho
’s GME) if and only if the hypergraph
G=([n],{\cal S})
is connected and
|S|\geq L(\rho)
(
|S|\geq l(\rho)
)
\exists\,S\in{\cal S}
.

This result, provided in Section VI of the SM, can be used to compute the minimum number of marginals needed to determine a state/guarantee GME. Without loss of generality, we can restrict the attention to
L(\rho)
-body (
l(\rho)
-body) marginals for determining (detecting the GME of) the state
\rho
. We denote by
{\cal S}_{k}
the collection of all
k
-subsets of
[n]
, that is
|{\cal S}_{k}|=\binom{n}{k}
, and
|S|=k
for every
S\in{\cal S}_{k}
. The minimum number of marginals needed to determine
\rho

is

M(\rho):=\mathop{\rm min}_{{\cal S}\in{\sf SD}(\rho)}\left\{|{\cal S}|~|~{\cal S}\subseteq{\cal S}_{L(\rho)}\right\}\,,

(5)

while the minimum number marginals needed to detect
\rho
’s GME is

m(\rho):=\mathop{\rm min}_{{\cal S}\in{\sf ED}(\rho)}\left\{|{\cal S}|~|~{\cal S}\subseteq{\cal S}_{l(\rho)}\right\}\,.

(6)

For an
n
-qubit entangled symmetric state
\rho
, Proposition
3
yields the simple expressions
M(\rho)=\lceil(n-1)/(L(\rho)-1)\rceil
and
m(\rho)=\lceil(n-1)/(l(\rho)-1)\rceil
(see Section VI of the SM for the derivation).

The graph-theoretic characterization of Proposition
3
has also an application to the problem of entanglement transitivity

. A set of marginals
\{\rho_{S}\mid S\in{\cal S}\}
exhibits
entanglement transitivity
in a target system
T\notin{\cal S}
if for all global states
\rho
compatible with these marginals, the marginal
\rho_{T}
is entangled. By definition of the EDL,
the set of all
l(\rho)
-body marginals
\{\rho_{S}\mid S\in{\cal S}_{l(\rho)}\}
exhibits entanglement transitivity in
[n]
, for every genuinely entangled state
\rho
. In the special case of symmetric states, one can prove a stronger result:

Proposition 4

For an
n
-qubit entangled symmetric state
\rho
, if the hypergraph
G=([n],{\cal S})
is connected and
|S|\geq l(\rho)

\exists\,S\in{\cal S}
, then the set of marginals
\{\rho_{S}\mid S\in{\cal S}\}
exhibits entanglement transitivity in any

with
|T|\geq l(\rho)
.

The proof of Proposition
4
is given in Section VII of the SM, where we also present examples.

General upper bound on the EDL.
—
We now provide a general upper bound on the EDL of arbitrary genuinely entangled states. The bound is based on the notion of fully decomposable witness (FDW)
jungnitsch2011taming
: an
n
-qubit observable
W
is an FDW if for every bipartition
S|\overline{S}
, there exist positive semidefinite operators
P_{S}
and
Q_{S}
such that
W=P_{S}+Q_{S}^{T_{S}}
, where
T_{S}
is the partial transpose over
{\cal H}_{S}
. This definition implies that the expectation value of
W
is non-negative for every biseparable state. Hence, a negative value of
{\sf Tr}[W\rho]
implies that
\rho
is genuinely entangled.

We now restrict the attention to FDWs that can be constructed from measurements on a given collection of subsystems, labeled by a collection
{\cal S}
of subsets of
[n]
, that is, FDWs of the form
W=\sum_{S\in{\cal S}}H^{S}\otimes I^{\overline{S}}
, where
H^{S}
is an Hermitian operator on
{\cal H}_{S}
and
I^{\overline{S}}
is the identity operator on
{\cal H}_{\overline{S}}
. For a given state
\rho
, minimizing the expectation value
{\sf Tr}[W\rho]
over all FDWs of this form is an SDP, whose optimal value is

\displaystyle\alpha(\rho,{\cal S}):=\mathop{\rm min}

\displaystyle\sf{\displaystyle Tr}(W\rho)

(7)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\sf{\displaystyle Tr}(W)=1,

\displaystyle W=\sum_{S\in{\cal S}}H^{S}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\overline{S}},

\displaystyle\text{$W$ is fully decomposable.}

For any given collection
{\cal S}
, Eq. (
7
) can be solved by using the convex optimization package in Matlab
cvx
. We then have the following:

Proposition 5

For every state
\rho
, one has
l(\rho)\leq\mathop{\rm min}\{k~|~\alpha(\rho,{\cal S}_{k})<0\}
.

We also observe that evaluation of
l(\rho)
from the SDP is robust to small amounts of white noise: for a state of the form
\rho_{p}=(1-p)\,\rho+p\,I/2^{n}
, we have
l(\rho_{p})\leq k
for all
p\in[0,\frac{2^{n}\alpha(\rho,{\cal S}_{k})}{2^{n}\alpha(\rho,{\cal S}_{k})-1})
when
\alpha(\rho,{\cal S}_{k})<0
. Moreover, we can also evaluate the SDL of pure states from SDP. See Section VIII of the SM for all details, where we also show some examples.

Maximum gap between EDL and SDL.
—
We conclude the paper by providing examples of states for which the gap is maximum.

Let us define

\mathop{\rm gap}(\rho):=L(\rho)-l(\rho)
for a genuinely entangled state
\rho
. Since for every genuinely entangled state
\rho
, one has the trivial bounds
L(\rho)\leq n
and
l(\rho)\geq 2
, the maximum value of the gap is at most
n-2
. On the other hand, this maximum value cannot be obtained for genuinely entangled pure states, because the SDL of an
n
-qubit pure state is
n
only if the state is
\mathop{\rm GHZ}
-like, which in turn implies that the EDL is
n
.
Hence, the gap between the SDL and EDL is at most
n-3
for pure states. We show that this value can be achieved by suitable superpositions of Dicke states:

Proposition 6

For an
n
-qubit genuinely entangled pure state
|\psi\rangle
, one has
\mathop{\rm gap}(|\psi\rangle)\leq\mathop{\rm max}\{0,n-3\}
. The bound is attained by every state of the form
|\psi\rangle=\alpha|D_{n}^{1}\rangle+\beta\,|D_{n}^{n}\rangle
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are two amplitudes satisfying the condition
\frac{n^{2}-2n}{n^{2}-2n+1}<|\alpha|^{2}<1
.

For mixed states, the maximum value can attained by suitable mixtures of Dicke states:

Proposition 7

For an
n
-qubit genuinely entangled mixed state
\rho
, one has
\mathop{\rm gap}(\rho)\leq\mathop{\rm max}\{0,n-2\}
. The bound is attained by every state of the form

\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{i}|
where
(\lambda_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
are probabilities satisfying the conditions
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{n}\neq 0
and
\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\,(n-i)j\,\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[(n-i-1)(j-1)-i(n-j)]<0
.

An example of mixed state with maximum gap is
\rho=\frac{1}{24}|D_{4}^{0}\rangle\!\langle D_{4}^{0}|+\frac{1}{3}|D_{4}^{1}\rangle\!\langle D_{4}^{1}|+\frac{1}{2}|D_{4}^{2}\rangle\!\langle D_{4}^{2}|+\frac{1}{12}|D_{4}^{3}\rangle\!\langle D_{4}^{3}|+\frac{1}{24}|D_{4}^{4}\rangle\!\langle D_{4}^{4}|
. The proof of Propositions
6
and
7
can be found in Section IX of the SM.

Conclusions.
—
We have introduced the notion of EDL, determining the minimum number of particles that have to be jointly measured in order to detect genuine multipartite entanglement. This quantity
can be used to analyze different aspects of multipartite entanglement, including entanglement transitivity and robustness.

A promising avenue of future research is to extend the notion of EDL from GME to other properties of multipartite quantum states. For example, it is interesting to consider the minimum number of particles needed to detect entanglement depth
guhne2009entanglement
;
liang2015family
;
tura2019optimization
;
lin2019exploring
;
aloy2019device
;
lu2018entanglement
and GME without multipartite correlations
schwemmer2015genuine
;
klobus2019higher
. Similarly, one could explore the notion of nonlocality length, defined as the minimum length of the Bell correlations needed to detect nonlocality in a multipartite quantum state
wurflinger2012nonlocal
;
vertesi2014certifying
.

Appendix D
III. Symmetric states

A symmetric (bosonic) space
{\cal H}_{Sym}
is a subspace of
{\cal H}_{[n]}
, where every pure state is invariant under the swap of any two subsystems, i.e.

{\cal H}_{Sym}:=\{|\psi\rangle\in{\cal H}_{[n]}:\ {\mathop{\rm Swap}}_{(i,j)}|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle,\forall\,1\leq i,j\leq n\},

(12)

where
\mathop{\rm Swap}_{(i,j)}
is a swap operator that swaps
i
-th and
j
-th subsystems. Every pure state of
{\cal H}_{Sym}
is called a
symmetric pure state
.
Note that
\mathop{\rm Dim}{\cal H}_{Sym}=n+1
, and
{\cal H}_{Sym}
has an orthogonal basis
\{|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\}_{i=0}^{n}
consisting of Dicke states, where

|D_{n}^{i}\rangle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{i}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\\
s_{j}\in\{0,1\},\,\forall\,j\in[n]\\
\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}=i\end{subarray}}|s_{1}\rangle\otimes|s_{2}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|s_{n}\rangle\,.

(13)

A symmetric pure state
|\psi\rangle
can be written as

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle,

(14)

where
\sum_{i=0}^{n}|a_{i}|^{2}=1
.

Next, we introduce the concept of symmetric mixed states.

Definition 1

An
n
-qubit state
\rho
is a symmetric state if
{\cal R}(\rho)\subseteq{\cal H}_{Sym}
. In other words,
\rho=\mathop{\rm Swap}_{(i,j)}\rho
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

A symmetric state can be written as
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|
, where
|\psi_{i}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{Sym}
. Furthermore, a symmetric state
\rho
can also be written as

\rho=\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}a_{i,j}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{j}|,

(15)

where
A=(a_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
is positive semidefinite and
{\sf Tr}(A)=1
. Specially, the
diagonal symmetric state
is a mixture of the Dicke states, i.e.

\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\langle D_{n}^{i}|,

(16)

where
0\leq\lambda_{i}\leq 1
for all
0\leq i\leq n
, and
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
.

A symmetric state
\rho
is either fully separable or genuinely entangled

. When
\rho
is a fully separable symmetric state, it can be written as
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i}\cdots\alpha_{i}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i}\cdots\alpha_{i}|

ichikawa2008exchange
.

We stipulate that the binomial coefficient
\binom{n}{i}=0
if
i<0
or
i>n
, and
\binom{0}{0}=1
.
According to Ref.
aloy2021quantum

\displaystyle|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{i}|_{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\frac{\binom{k}{s}\binom{n-k}{i-s}}{\binom{n}{i}}|D_{k}^{s}\rangle\!\langle D_{k}^{s}|,\quad\forall\,0\leq i\leq n;

(17)

\displaystyle|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{j}|_{[k]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\frac{\binom{n-k}{i-s}\sqrt{\binom{k}{s}\binom{k}{j-i+s}}}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{j}}}|D_{k}^{s}\rangle\!\langle D_{k}^{j-i+s}|,\quad\forall\,0\leq i<j\leq n;

The most famous entanglement detection criterion is the so-called
positivity under partial transposition (PPT) criterion

;
horodecki2001separability

An

the partial transpose of

subsystems

;
yu2016separability
;
tura2018separability
. The

-qubit symmetric states and the

;
peres1996separability
;
horodecki2001separability
;
eckert2002quantum
;
yu2016separability
. Based on PPT criterion, we can calculate the EDLs for a wide range of symmetric states.
Specially, for a diagonal symmetric state, there exists a simple method to determine whether it is PPT or NPT

Lemma 8

Lemma 9

chen2012correlations

For all

Proof.

Since

\sigma=\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}a_{i,j}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{j}|,

According to Eq. (

\displaystyle|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{i}|_{[2]}

Lemma 10

(a_{i,i}\geq 0

Note that

(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

Assume there exists a solution
(a_{0,0},a_{1,1},\ldots,a_{n,n})\neq(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
for the nonhomogeneous linear equations in Eq. (

Since

\sum_{s=0}^{k}\frac{\binom{k}{s}\binom{n-k}{i-s}}{\binom{n}{i}}=\sum_{s=0}^{i}\frac{\binom{k}{s}\binom{n-k}{i-s}}{\binom{n}{i}}=\frac{\binom{n}{i}}{\binom{n}{i}}=1.

Proposition 7

For an
n
-qubit genuinely entangled mixed state
\rho
, one has
\mathop{\rm gap}(\rho)\leq\mathop{\rm max}\{0,n-2\}
. The bound is attained by every state of the form

\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}|D_{n}^{i}\rangle\!\langle D_{n}^{i}|
where
(\lambda_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
are probabilities satisfying the conditions
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{n}\neq 0
and
\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\,(n-i)j\,\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[(n-i-1)(j-1)-i(n-j)]<0
.

Proof.

We only need to consider
n\geq 2
. If
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{n}\neq 0
, then
L(\rho)=n
according to Lemma
14
.
According to Lemma
8
, if
\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n}(n-i)(n-i-1)\lambda_{i}\right]\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n}i(i-1)\lambda_{i}\right]-\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n}i(n-i)\lambda_{i}\right]^{2}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\,(n-i)j\,\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[(n-i-1)(j-1)-i(n-j)]<0
, then
\rho_{[2]}
is NPT, and
\rho_{[2]}
is entangled. According to Proposition
1
, we have
l(\rho)=2
. Thus
\mathop{\rm gap}(\rho)=n-2
.

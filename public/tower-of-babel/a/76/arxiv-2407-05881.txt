Abstract
. A finite-dimensional Hopf algebra is called quasi-split if it is Morita equivalent to a split abelian extension of Hopf algebras. Combining results of Schauenburg and Negron, it is shown that every quasi-split finite-dimensional Hopf algebra satisfies the finite generation cohomology conjecture of Etingof and Ostrik. This is applied to a family of pointed Hopf algebras in odd characteristic introduced by Angiono, Heckenberger and the first author, proving that they satisfy the aforementioned conjecture.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The problem

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field.
We say that an augmented
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
has finitely generated cohomology (fgc for short)
when

(a)

the cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}(A,\Bbbk)=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{A}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)
is finitely generated, and

(b)

for any finitely generated

\operatorname{H}(A,M)=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{A}(\Bbbk,M)
is a finitely generated
\operatorname{H}(A,\Bbbk)
-module.

This definition was extended in
[
16
]
as follows. A finite tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}

(with unit object
\mathbb{1}
)
has finite generation of cohomology (or fgc) when

(a)

the
\Bbbk
-algebra
\operatorname{H}({\mathcal{C}})=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(\mathbb{1},\mathbb{1})

is finitely generated, and

(b)

for any object

in
{\mathcal{C}}
the
\operatorname{H}({\mathcal{C}})
-module

\operatorname{H}(V)=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(\mathbb{1},V)

1.2.
Morita equivalence

Let
H
and
U
be finite-dimensional Hopf algebras.
By
[
35
, Theorem 3.4]
, see also
[
5
, Theorem 3.2.1]
,
if the Drinfeld double
D(H)
has fgc, then
H
has fgc.
The following argument was intensively used in
[
5
]
.

We say that
H
and
U
are

Morita equivalent
, denoted
H\sim_{\text{Mor}}U
, if the finite tensor categories
\operatorname{rep}H
and
\operatorname{rep}U
are Morita equivalent as in
[
33
,
15
]
.
Equivalently,
H\sim_{\text{Mor}}U
if there exists an equivalence of braided tensor categories
between the Drinfeld centers
\mathcal{Z}(\operatorname{rep}H)
and
\mathcal{Z}(\operatorname{rep}U)

[
15
]
.
In other words,
H\sim_{\text{Mor}}U
iff
D(H)
and
D(U)
are twist equivalent quasitriangular Hopf algebras
(this does not imply that
H
and
U
are Morita equivalent as algebras).
Thus to prove that
H
has fgc
it suffices to find

such that

\displaystyle\textrm{(i)}\ D(U)

has fgc

and

(ii)

\displaystyle H\sim_{\text{Mor}}U.

1.3.
Extensions

In this paper we consider the question of fgc for a Hopf algebra
H

fitting into an exact sequence of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras

(1)

\displaystyle\Bbbk\to K\rightarrow H\rightarrow L\to\Bbbk.

If
H
has fgc, then
K
has fgc by
[
5
, Theorem 3.2.1]
while
it is unclear whether we could infer that
L
has fgc. Thus, it is natural to ask:

Question 1.1
.

Given an extension (
1
)
such that
K
and
L
have fgc, does
H
also have fgc?

For instance, if
K
is semisimple and
L
has fgc, then
H
has fgc, see
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.5]
.
The proof uses a variation of the classical Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence
but it is not clear (to us) how to proceed when the semisimplicity assumption on
K
is dropped.

The extension (
1
) is
abelian
when
K
is commutative
and
L
is cocommutative. In this case there are suitable
actions of
L
on
K
and of
K^{*}
on
L
, and a pair
(\sigma,\tau)
of compatible cocycles that determine the possible extensions
H
.
The suitable actions give rise to a double complex
\mathscr{E}
.
The abelian extension (
1
) is
split
if
the pair
(\sigma,\tau)
is trivial in the total complex associated to
\mathscr{E}
.
Furthermore, by an argument due to G. I. Kac
there is a long exact sequence (the Kac exact sequence)
in which the pair
(\sigma,\tau)
is sent to a 3-cocycle.
See
[
32
]
for details.

We shall say that an abelian extension is
quasi-split

if it is Morita equivalent to the split extension (with respect to the same suitable actions);
see Definition
3.1
.

Tautologically, split abelian extensions are quasi-split; also, abelian extensions for which the Kac 3-cocycle is trivial are quasi-split (see Corollary
3.5
). The starting
point of the paper is the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

If (
1
) is a quasi-split abelian extension, then
H
and
D(H)
have fgc.

See Theorem
3.6
for a precise formulation.
As a consequence, the dual, any twist and any cocycle deformation
of
H
have fgc. In characteristic 0, Theorem
1.2

is trivial, as a finite-dimensional abelian extension is semisimple.

Theorem
1.2
follows combining
the next two results due to Negron and Schauenburg respectively.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
34
]

If
U
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra,
\dim U<\infty
, then
D(U)
has fgc.

Notice that Theorem
1.3
is stated in
[
34
]
in the language of finite group schemes.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
39
]

Let (
1
) be a split abelian extension
of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras. Then there exists a cocommutative Hopf algebra

U
such that
H\sim_{\text{Mor}}U
.

Observe that the Drinfeld double of a finite-dimensional
cocommutative Hopf algebra is a split abelian extension.

1.4.
Pointed Hopf algebras

In the paper
[
3
]
(assuming
\operatorname{char}\Bbbk=0

Nichols algebras over abelian groups

It was observed later in

Theorem

We observe:

\circ

2.
Extensions of Hopf algebras

This Section contains a crash exposition of extensions of Hopf algebras.

2.1.
Exact sequences

Recall that a sequence of morphisms of Hopf algebras

(2)

\displaystyle\Bbbk\to A\overset{\iota}{\to}C\overset{\pi}{\to}B\to\Bbbk

is exact
[
6
,
40
,
25
]
if the following conditions holds:

(i)

\iota
is injective.

(ii)

\pi
is surjective.

(iii)

\ker\pi=C\iota(A)^{+}
.

(iv)

\iota(A)=C^{\operatorname{co}\pi}
.

The exact sequence (
2
) is
abelian
if
A
is commutative and
B
is cocommutative.
We shall also refer to
C
in (
2
) as an (abelian when it corresponds)
extension of
B
by
A
.

The exact sequence (
2
) is
cleft

(see
[
6
, Definition 3.2.13]
) if there exist

(i)

a unit preserving right
B
-colinear section
\mathpzc{s}:B\to C
of the projection
\pi
and

(ii)

a counit preserving left
A
-linear retraction
\mathpzc{r}:C\to A
of the inclusion
\iota
,

both
\mathpzc{s}
and
\mathpzc{r}
being invertible with respect to the convolution product,
such that the following equivalent conditions hold, for all
c\in C
:

(a)

\mathpzc{s}^{-1}(\pi(c))=\mathcal{S}(c_{(1)})\mathpzc{r}(c_{(2)})
.

(b)

\mathpzc{s}(\pi(c))=\mathpzc{r}^{-1}(c_{(1)})c_{(2)}
.

(c)

\mathpzc{r}^{-1}(c)=\mathpzc{s}(\pi(c_{(1)}))\mathcal{S}(c_{(2)})
.

(d)

\mathpzc{r}(c)=c_{(1)}\mathpzc{s}^{-1}(\pi(c_{(2)}))
.

(e)

\mathpzc{r}\mathpzc{s}=\epsilon_{B}1_{A}
.

The maps
\mathpzc{s}
and
\mathpzc{r}
are called
cleaving maps
of (
2
).

Remark 2.1
.

[
40
]

If

is finite dimensional, then the extension (
2
) is cleft.

Definition 2.2
.

[
39

The exact sequence (
2

if there exist cleaving maps
\mathpzc{s}
and
\mathpzc{r}
as above, called

of (

\mathpzc{s}
is an algebra map and
\mathpzc{r}
is a coalgebra map.

Cleft extensions can be described using suitable linear maps.
A

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup:B\otimes A

\displaystyle\to A,

\displaystyle\rho:B

\displaystyle\to B\otimes A,

A bicrossed product datum

\displaystyle(k\#h)(t\#g)

\displaystyle=k\left(h_{(1)}\rightharpoonup t\right)\sigma(h_{(2)},g_{(1)})\#h_{(3)}g_{(2)},

\displaystyle\Delta(k\#h)

\displaystyle=k_{(1)}\tau^{(1)}(h_{(1)})\#\rho(h_{(2)})_{i}\otimes k_{(2)}\tau^{(2)}(h_{(1)})\rho(h_{(2)})^{i}\#h_{(3)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(a\#b)

\displaystyle=((\mathcal{S}((\rho(b)_{i})_{(2)})\rightharpoonup\mathcal{S}(\rho(b)^{i})\#\mathcal{S}((\rho(b)_{i})_{(1)}))\mathcal{S}(a)\#1.

The natural maps

of Hopf algebras, with cleaving maps

\displaystyle\mathpzc{s}:B

\displaystyle\to A\#{}_{\sigma}^{\tau}B,

\displaystyle\mathpzc{s}(b)

\displaystyle=1\#b,

\displaystyle\mathpzc{r}:A\#{}_{\sigma}^{\tau}B

\displaystyle\to A,

\displaystyle\mathpzc{r}(a\#b)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(b)a,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\in A,\,b\in B.

\displaystyle b\rightharpoonup a

\displaystyle=\mathpzc{s}(b_{(1)})a\mathpzc{s}^{-1}(b_{(2)}),\quad\sigma(b\#b^{\prime})=\mathpzc{s}(b_{(1)})\mathpzc{s}(b^{\prime}_{(1)})\mathpzc{s}^{-1}(b_{(2)}b^{\prime}_{(2)}),

\displaystyle\rho(\pi(c))

\displaystyle=\pi(c_{(2)})\otimes\mathpzc{r}^{-1}(c_{(1)})\mathpzc{r}(c_{(3)}),\quad\tau(\pi(c))=\Delta(\mathpzc{r}^{-1}(c_{(1)}))\;\mathpzc{r}(c_{(2)})\otimes\mathpzc{r}(c_{(3)}),

2.2.
Matched pairs

We now present a way to produce extensions due to G. I. Kac for abelian extensions
and to S. Majid in general. We start by the definition, see
[
29
, §7.2]
.
An
exact factorization
of a Hopf algebra

consists of a pair
(G,L)
of Hopf subalgebras of
S

such that the restriction of the multiplication map

\displaystyle\operatorname{mult}:G\otimes L\to S

is a linear isomorphism. Exact factorizations are classified through the following notion.

Definition 2.3
.

[

A

of Hopf algebras is a collection
(G,L,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
where
L
and
G

-module coalgebra with action
\triangleright

(4)

\displaystyle(\ell m)\triangleleft x

\displaystyle=\left(\ell\triangleleft(m_{(1)}\triangleright x_{(1)})\right)(m_{(2)}\triangleleft x_{(2)}),

(5)

\displaystyle\ell\triangleright(xy)

\displaystyle=\left(\ell_{(1)}\triangleright x_{(1)}\right)\left((\ell_{(2)}\triangleleft x_{(2)})\triangleright y\right),

(6)

\displaystyle\ell_{(1)}\triangleleft x_{(1)}\otimes\ell_{(2)}\triangleright x_{(2)}

\displaystyle=\ell_{(2)}\triangleleft x_{(2)}\otimes\ell_{(1)}\triangleright x_{(1)}.

Proposition 2.4
.

(i)

[

Given a matched pair
(G,L,\triangleright,\triangleleft)

\displaystyle(x\otimes\ell)(y\otimes m)

\displaystyle=x\left(\ell_{(1)}\triangleright y_{(1)}\right)\otimes\left(\ell_{(2)}\triangleleft y_{(2)}\right)m,

\displaystyle\in L,\ x,y\in G,

(ii)

[

\displaystyle\simeq G\bowtie L.

The Hopf algebra

associated to the actions

in the following way.

Proposition 2.5

[

If

[

The minimal bosonization of
\mathcal{V}(1,2)
arises as follows.
Let
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/p=\langle g\rangle
.
We realize
\mathcal{V}(1,2)
in

\displaystyle g\cdot x

\displaystyle=x,

\displaystyle g\cdot y

\displaystyle=y+x,

\displaystyle\deg x=\deg y

\displaystyle=g.

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle g^{p}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle gx

\displaystyle=xg,

\displaystyle gy

\displaystyle=yg+xg,

\displaystyle yx

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

Let

Lemma 4.4
.

The Hopf algebra
H
fits
into a split abelian extension
\Bbbk\to K\overset{\iota}{\rightarrow}H\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}L\to\Bbbk
,
where
\iota
is the inclusion and
\pi

\pi(x)=0
,
\pi(g)=1

.

The defining relations of

imply that

is commutative.
Furthermore

is generated by the primitive element
\zeta
, whence cocommutative.
Clearly
\pi
is well-defined and

.
We thus obtain an abelian exact sequence

\Bbbk\to K\overset{\iota}{\rightarrow}H\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}L\to\Bbbk
.

Since

, there exists a unique algebra map
\mathpzc{s}:L\to H
such that
\mathpzc{s}(\zeta)=-\mathcal{S}(y)=g^{-1}y
.
Clearly,
\pi\mathpzc{s}=\operatorname{id}_{L}

Being an algebra map,

The

\displaystyle(\mathpzc{s}\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(\zeta)=g^{-1}y\otimes 1+1\otimes\zeta=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\pi)\Delta\mathpzc{s}(\zeta)=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\pi)(g^{-1}y\otimes g^{-1}+1\otimes g^{-1}y).

\displaystyle\mathpzc{r}(g^{i}{x}^{j}y^{\ell})

\displaystyle=g^{i}{x}^{j}\,\,\text{ if }\,\,\ell=0,

\displaystyle\mathpzc{r}(g^{i}{x}^{j}y^{\ell})

\displaystyle=0\text{ if }\,\,\ell>0.

It is clear that
\mathpzc{r}
is

and
\mathpzc{r}\mathpzc{s}=\epsilon_{L}1_{K}
.
We next show that
\mathpzc{r}
is a coalgebra map. Since

we have

for all

Let

The relation

\mathpzc{r}

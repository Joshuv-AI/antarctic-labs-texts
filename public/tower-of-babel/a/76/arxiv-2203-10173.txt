Abstract
. We provide suitable conditions under which the asymptotic limit of the Hilbert-Samuel coefficients of the Frobenius powers of an 𝔪 \displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}} -primary ideal exists in a Noetherian local ring ( R , 𝔪 ) \displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}}) with prime characteristic p > 0 . \displaystyle p>0. This, in turn, gives an expression of the Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of powers of the ideal. We also prove that for a face ring R \displaystyle R of a simplicial complex and an ideal J \displaystyle J generated by pure powers of the variables, the generalized Hilbert-Kunz function ℓ ⁡ ( R / ( J [ q ] ) k ) \displaystyle\ell(R/(J^{[q]})^{k}) is a polynomial for all q , k \displaystyle q,k and also give an expression of the generalized Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of powers of J \displaystyle J in terms of Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of J . \displaystyle J. We conclude by giving a counter-example to a conjecture proposed by I. Smirnov which connects the stability of an ideal with the asymptotic limit of the first Hilbert coefficient of the Frobenius power of the ideal.



1.
Introduction

This article is inspired by the work of I. Smirnov in
[
13
]
, where the author studies the Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of powers of an ideal. Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Noetherian local ring of prime characteristic
\displaystyle p
and let
\displaystyle I
be an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal. The
\displaystyle q^{th}
-Frobenius power of
\displaystyle I
is the ideal
\displaystyle I^{[q]}=(x^{q}\mid x\in I)
where
\displaystyle q=p^{e}
for
\displaystyle e\in{\mathbb{N}}.
The function
\displaystyle e\mapsto\ell_{R}(R/I^{[p^{e}]})
is called the
Hilbert-Kunz function
of
\displaystyle R
with respect to
\displaystyle I
and was first considered by E. Kunz in
[
8
]
. In
[
10
]
, P. Monsky showed that this function is of the form

\ell_{R}(R/I^{[q]})=e_{HK}(I,R)q^{d}+O(q^{d-1}),

where
\displaystyle e_{HK}(I,R)
is a positive real number called the
Hilbert–Kunz multiplicity
of
\displaystyle R
with respect to
\displaystyle I.
We write
\displaystyle e_{HK}(R):=e_{HK}({\mathfrak{m}},R)
and
\displaystyle e_{HK}(I):=e_{HK}(I,R).
The Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of powers of an ideal was first considered by D. Hanes in
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
. He proved that

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{[q]})^{k}}\right)=\left(\frac{e_{0}(I)}{d!}k^{d}+O(k^{d-1})\right)q^{d},

where
\displaystyle e_{0}(I)
denotes the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of
\displaystyle R
with respect to
\displaystyle I.

Recall that the
Hilbert-Samuel function

\displaystyle H_{I}(n)
of
\displaystyle R
with respect to
\displaystyle I
is defined as
\displaystyle H_{I}(n)=\ell_{R}(R/I^{n}).
It is known that
\displaystyle H_{I}(n)
is a polynomial function of
\displaystyle n
of degree
\displaystyle d
, for large
\displaystyle n.
In particular, there exists a polynomial
\displaystyle P_{I}(x)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x],
called the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial, such that
\displaystyle H_{I}(n)=P_{I}(n)
for all large
\displaystyle n.
Write

P_{I}(x)=e_{0}(I)\binom{x+d-1}{d}-e_{1}(I)\binom{x+d-2}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(I),

where
\displaystyle e_{i}(I)
for
\displaystyle i=0,1,\ldots,d
are integers, called the
Hilbert-Samuel coefficients of
\displaystyle I.
The leading coefficient
\displaystyle e_{0}(I)
is called the multiplicity of
\displaystyle I
and
\displaystyle e_{1}(I)
is called the Chern number of
\displaystyle I.

In
[
14
]
, Trivedi proved that if
\displaystyle R
is a standard graded ring of dimension
\displaystyle d
over a perfect field of characteristic
\displaystyle p>0,
and
\displaystyle I
is a homogeneous ideal of finite colength and generated in the same degree, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e_{HK}(I^{k})-e_{0}(I^{k})/d!}{k^{d-1}}=\frac{e_{0}(I)}{2(d-2)!}-\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e_{1}(I^{[q]})}{q^{d}(d-1)!}

and the last limit exists. The result was proved in full generality by Smirnov in
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
. He proved that if
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is a Noetherian local ring and
\displaystyle I
is an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal, then

e_{HK}(I^{k})=e_{0}(I)\binom{k+d-1}{d}-\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e_{1}(I^{[q]})}{q^{d}}\binom{k+d-2}{d-1}+o(k^{d-1}).

The above equation motivates one to ask if other Hilbert coefficients may also be appear in the expression.

One of the aims of this article is to provide some suitable conditions under which the following questions, proposed by Smirnov in
[
13
]
, can be answered in the affirmative. Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d

\displaystyle p>0.
Let
\displaystyle I
be an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal. Put
\displaystyle q=p^{e},

Question 1.1

Does the limit
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{i}(I^{[q]})/q^{d}
exist for all
\displaystyle i\geq 1

\displaystyle?

Question 1.2

Do we have for large
\displaystyle k,
that

e_{HK}(I^{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}\binom{k+d-1-i}{d-i}\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e_{i}(I^{[q]})}{q^{d}}\ ?

Recall that for an ideal
\displaystyle I
in a ring
\displaystyle R,
a reduction of
\displaystyle I
is an ideal
\displaystyle J\subseteq I
such that
\displaystyle JI^{n}=I^{n+1}

\displaystyle n.
A minimal reduction of
\displaystyle I
is a reduction of
\displaystyle I
minimal with respect to inclusion. For a minimal reduction
\displaystyle J
of
\displaystyle I

\displaystyle r_{J}(I)=\min\{n\mid I^{m+1}=JI^{m}\text{ for all }m\geq n\}.
The reduction number
\displaystyle r(I)

\displaystyle I

An ideal with reduction number one is called a stable ideal.

It is easy to check that the questions

and
1.2
have affirmative answers when
\displaystyle R
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
\displaystyle I
is a parameter ideal, or when
\displaystyle R

and
\displaystyle I
is a stable ideal (see
[

Recall that
\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\text{emb}(R)-\dim R+1=e_{0}(R),
where
\displaystyle\text{emb}(R)

In section 2, we prove the following results which answer the above questions in the affirmative under suitable assumptions.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{i}(I^{[q]})/q^{d}=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{HK}(I^{k})=e_{0}(I)\binom{k+d-1}{d}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
and prime characteristic
\displaystyle p.
Let
\displaystyle I
be an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}

The generalized Hilbert-Kunz function was introduced by Aldo Conca in
[
3
]
. Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Noetherian local (resp. standard graded) ring with maximal (resp. maximal homogeneous) ideal
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\displaystyle I
be an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary (resp. a graded
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary) ideal. Fix a set of generators of
\displaystyle I

\displaystyle I=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{g}).
We choose these as homogeneous elements in case
\displaystyle R
is a graded ring. Define the
\displaystyle s^{th}

\displaystyle I

The generalized Hilbert-Kunz function of
\displaystyle I

\displaystyle HK_{I}(s)=\ell(R/I^{[s]}).
The generalized Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity is defined as
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow\infty}HK_{I}(s)/s^{d}

.

In sections

and

we compute the generalized Hilbert-Kunz function of powers of certain monomial ideals in face rings of simplicial complexes. In particular, these results are characteristic independent.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\displaystyle K
be a field and
\displaystyle R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]/I_{\Delta}
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional face ring of a simplicial complex

\displaystyle J=(x_{1}^{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{v_{r}})R,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{i}(J^{[q]})/q^{d}=0
for all

the generalized Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity

e_{HK}(J^{k})=e_{0}(J)\binom{k+d-1}{d}.

Smirnov, in the same paper, also proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Then an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
\displaystyle I

This means that one side of the conjecture is always true for such ideals. In particular, the conjecture is false whenever

is not a stable ideal. The following example illustrates one such case.

Using Theorem

2.
Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of powers of ideals

In this section we answer Smirnov’s questions, mentioned in the introduction, in the affirmative in certain cases. We show that both the questions have answers in the affirmative for parameter ideals in Buchsbaum local rings. We then consider the questions for
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideals
\displaystyle I
in Cohen-Macaulay local rings
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
of dimension
\displaystyle d

subject to the condition
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G(I^{[q]})\geq d-1.
This is a strong condition. However, we show that it is satisfied in several examples (in sections 3 and 4).
We begin by considering the case of Buchsbaum local rings.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Buchsbaum ring of dimension
\displaystyle d
with prime characteristic
\displaystyle p
and let
\displaystyle I
be a parameter ideal. Then for all
\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{i}(I^{[q]})/q^{d}=0
and for all
\displaystyle k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{HK}(I^{k})=e_{0}(I)\binom{k+d-1}{d}.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle I
is a parameter ideal, then so is
\displaystyle I^{[q]}
for all
\displaystyle q.
Therefore, using
[
15
, Corollary 4.2]
, we obtain that for all
\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,d,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{i}(I^{[q]})=(-1)^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{d-i}\binom{d-i-1}{j-1}\ell(H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)),

where
\displaystyle\binom{d-i-1}{-1}=0
if
\displaystyle i\neq d
and
\displaystyle\binom{-1}{-1}=1.
Also, for all
\displaystyle k\geq 1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left(\frac{R}{I^{[q]k}}\right)=e_{0}(I^{[q]})\binom{k+d-1}{d}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}(I^{[q]})\binom{k+d-1-i}{d-i}.

From (
2.1
), it follows that for all
\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,d,

\displaystyle e_{i}(I^{[q]})
is independent of
\displaystyle q
and hence using (
2.2
), we get the required result.
∎

The next result answers Smirnov’s questions for
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideals
\displaystyle I
in Cohen-Macaulay local rings of prime characteristic
\displaystyle p
and dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
and
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G(I^{[q]})\geq d-1
for all large
\displaystyle q.
Recall that for a function
\displaystyle f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\displaystyle\Delta(f)(n)=f(n)-f(n-1).
Define
\displaystyle\Delta^{t}(f)=\Delta(\Delta^{t-1}(f))
for all
\displaystyle t\geq 2.
We shall use the following results due to S. Huckaba and T. Marley.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
5
,
6
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1.
Let
\displaystyle I
be an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal with a minimal reduction
\displaystyle J=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}).
Suppose that
\displaystyle r=r(I)
and
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G(I)\geq d-1.
Then for all
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(1)

\displaystyle\Delta^{d}[P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n)]=\ell(I^{n}/JI^{n-1})
.

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta^{d}P_{I}(n)=e_{0}(I)
and
\displaystyle\Delta^{d}(H_{I}(n))=\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d}{j}\ell(R/I^{n-j}).

(3)

\displaystyle e_{i}(I)=\sum_{n=i}^{r}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\ell(I^{n}/JI^{n-1})
for
\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,d.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
and prime characteristic
\displaystyle p.
Let
\displaystyle I
be an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal with a minimal reduction
\displaystyle J=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{d}).
Suppose that
\displaystyle r=r(I)
and
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G(I^{[q]})\geq d-1
for all large
\displaystyle q.
Then

(1)

For all
\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,d
the limit
\displaystyle L_{i}(I):=\lim_{q\to\infty}e_{i}(I^{[q]})/q^{d}
exists and

L_{i}(I)=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{e_{i}(I^{[q]})}{q^{d}}=\sum_{n=i}^{r}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\left[e_{0}(I)-\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d}{j}e_{HK}(I^{n-j})\right].

(2)

For all
\displaystyle n\geq r-d+1,

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{[q]})^{n}}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}e_{i}(I^{[q]})\binom{n+d-1-i}{d-i}

and hence for all
\displaystyle n\geq r-d+1,
we have

e_{HK}(I^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}L_{i}(I)\binom{n+d-1-i}{d-i}.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle JI^{r}=I^{r+1},
it follows that
\displaystyle J^{[q]}(I^{[q]})^{r}=(I^{[q]})^{r+1}.
Hence
\displaystyle r(I^{[q]})\leq r
for all
\displaystyle q.
Since
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G(I^{[q]})\geq d-1
for all large
\displaystyle q,

\displaystyle P_{I^{[q]}}(n)=H_{I^{[q]}}(n)
for all
\displaystyle n\geq r-d+1.
For all
\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,d,
by Theorem
2.2
(3),

e_{i}(I^{[q]})=\sum_{n=i}^{r}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\ell\left(\frac{(I^{[q]})^{n}}{J^{[q]}(I^{[q]})^{n-1}}\right).

Let us find
\displaystyle\beta_{I}(n):=\ell(I^{n}/JI^{n-1}).
By Theorem
2.2
, for all
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle\Delta^{d}(P_{I}(n)-H_{I}(n))=\ell(I^{n}/JI^{n-1}).
Therefore

\beta_{I}(n)=e_{0}(I)-\Delta^{d}(H_{I}(n))=e_{0}(I)-\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d}{j}\ell(R/I^{n-j}).

Since
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G(I^{[q]})\geq d-1
for large
\displaystyle q,
we have

\beta_{I^{[q]}}(n)=q^{d}e_{0}(I)-\Delta^{d}(H_{I^{[q]}}(n))=q^{d}e_{0}(I)-\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d}{j}\ell(R/(I^{[q]})^{n-j}).

Divide by
\displaystyle q^{d}
and then take the limit to get

\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\beta_{I^{[q]}}(n)}{q^{d}}=e_{0}(I)-\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d}{j}e_{HK}(I^{n-j}).

Therefore we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{i}(I)=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{e_{i}(I^{[q]})}{q^{d}}=\sum_{n=i}^{r}\binom{n-1}{i-1}\left[e_{0}(I)-\sum_{j=0}^{d}(-1)^{j}\binom{d}{j}e_{HK}(I^{n-j})\right].

Since
\displaystyle r(I^{[q]})\leq r,
for all
\displaystyle n\geq r-d+1,

\displaystyle H_{I^{[q]}}(n)=P_{I^{[q]}}(n).
Hence for all
\displaystyle n\geq r-d+1,

\ell(R/(I^{[q]})^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}e_{i}(I^{[q]})\binom{n+d-1-i}{d-i}.

Divide by
\displaystyle q^{d}
and take the limit to see that for all
\displaystyle n\geq r-d+1,

e_{HK}(I^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}L_{i}(I)\binom{n+d-1-i}{d-i}.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring of prime characteristic
\displaystyle p.

\displaystyle J=(a)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
\displaystyle I

\displaystyle JI^{r}=I^{r+1}.

\displaystyle L_{1}(I)=0.

Proof.

Put
\displaystyle i=1

Example 2.6

Let

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}},

Using Valabrega-Valla’s result (

\displaystyle r({\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]})\leq 2,

\displaystyle(y^{q},z^{q})\cap({\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]})^{2}=(y^{q},z^{q}){\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle(y^{q},z^{q})\cap({\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]})^{2}=(y^{q},z^{q})\cap(x^{2q},x^{q}y^{q},x^{q}z^{q},y^{2q},y^{q}z^{q},z^{2q}).

for some

Since

it follows that

Therefore,

and hence,
\displaystyle a\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]}.
Similarly,
\displaystyle b\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]}.
This proves that
\displaystyle G({\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]})
is Cohen-Macaulay for all large
\displaystyle q.

Note that

\displaystyle e_{HK}({\mathfrak{m}})=3

Lemma 3.2

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left(\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]k}}\right)=\binom{k+\beta-1}{\beta}q^{\beta}+\binom{k+\alpha-1}{\alpha}q^{\alpha}-1.

This implies that
\displaystyle e_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=q^{\beta},

\displaystyle e_{\beta-\alpha}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=(-1)^{\beta-\alpha}q^{\alpha}
,
\displaystyle e_{\beta}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=(-1)^{\beta+1}
and
\displaystyle e_{HK}({\mathfrak{n}}^{k})=\binom{k+\beta-1}{\beta}.

4.
Counter-example to Smirnov’s conjecture

Smirnov proposed that following conjecture in
[
13
]
.

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Then an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
\displaystyle I
is stable if and only if
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{1}(I^{[q]})/q^{d}=e_{0}(I)-e_{HK}(I).

From section 3, we know that if
\displaystyle R
is a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional face ring of a simplicial complex and
\displaystyle J=(x_{1}^{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{v_{r}})
is an
\displaystyle R
-ideal such that
\displaystyle v_{i}>0
for all
\displaystyle i
, then
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to\infty}e_{1}(J^{[q]})/q^{d}=0
and
\displaystyle e_{HK}(J)=e_{0}(J).
This means that
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{1}(J^{[q]})/q^{d}=e_{0}(J)-e_{HK}(J)
is always true for such ideals. In particular, the conjecture is false whenever
\displaystyle J
is not a stable ideal. The following example illustrates one such case.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\Delta
be the simplicial complex
\displaystyle x_{1}
\displaystyle x_{2}
\displaystyle x_{3}
\displaystyle x_{4}

Then
\displaystyle R=K[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]/((x_{3},x_{4})\cap(x_{1},x_{3})\cap(x_{1},x_{4})\cap(x_{1},x_{2}))
is the face ring of
\displaystyle\Delta.
Observe that
\displaystyle R
is a
\displaystyle 2
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring with
\displaystyle f
-vector
\displaystyle f(\Delta)=(1,4,4).
Let
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{n}}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
denote the maximal ideal of
\displaystyle R.
Using Theorem
3.4
, it follows that
\displaystyle 4=e_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})=e_{HK}({\mathfrak{n}})
and
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow\infty}e_{1}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})/q^{2}=0.
We prove that
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{n}}
is not stable. Consider the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle R
,

H(R,z)=1+\frac{4z}{1-z}+\frac{4z^{2}}{(1-z)^{2}}=\frac{1+2z+z^{2}}{(1-z)^{2}}.

As
\displaystyle R
is Cohen-Macaulay and degree of the numerator above is 2, we get
\displaystyle r({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=2.

We also observe that in this case,
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})\geq 1.
Set
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}=(x_{3},x_{4}),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}=(x_{1},x_{3}),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{3}=(x_{1},x_{4})
and
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{4}=(x_{1},x_{2}).
Then
\displaystyle I=\cap_{i=1}^{4}\ {\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
and
\displaystyle R=S/I,
where
\displaystyle S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{4}].
From Theorem
3.3
it follows that for all
\displaystyle q,k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left(\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]k}}\right)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{4}\ell\left(\frac{S}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}\ell\left(\frac{S}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j<u\leq 4}\ell\left(\frac{S}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{u}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 236.15796pt-\ell\left(\frac{S}{\sum_{i=1}^{4}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ 4\binom{k+1}{2}q^{2}-(5kq+1)+(kq+3)-1

(4.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ 4\binom{k+1}{2}q^{2}-4kq+1.

This implies that

e_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=4q^{2},\ e_{1}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=4q,\ e_{2}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})=1\text{ and }e_{HK}({\mathfrak{n}}^{k})=4\binom{k+1}{2}.

From equation (
4.2
), we get that for all
\displaystyle k\geq 1,

\ell\left(\frac{({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})^{k}}{{({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})^{k+1}}}\right)=4q^{2}{{k+2}\choose 2}-4q(k+1)+1-4q^{2}{{k+1}\choose 2}+4qk-1=4q^{2}(k+1)-4q.

Hence

\ell({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]}/({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})^{2})+(1-d)\ell(R/{\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})+1=8q^{2}-4q-(4q^{2}-4q+1)+1=4q^{2}=e_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]}).

Using Theorem
3.8
, we get depth
\displaystyle G({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})\geq 1
for all
\displaystyle q.

Remark 4.3
.

In the above example,
\displaystyle G({\mathfrak{n}})\simeq R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})\geq d-1
for all
\displaystyle q.
This indicates that the conjecture is false even if
\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})\geq d-1.

The above observation indicates that Conjecture
4.1
does not help in characterizing the stability of ideals in rings, as in the setup of section 3. We end the section by characterizing the stability of ideals in certain classes of Cohen-Macaulay simplicial complexes and graphs. We use Theorem
3.5
for the same.

Examples 4.4
.

(1) Let
\displaystyle R
be the face ring of the simplicial complex on
\displaystyle r
vertices,
\displaystyle r\geq 3,

3.6
.
\displaystyle x_{1}
\displaystyle x_{2}
\displaystyle x_{3}
\displaystyle x_{r-1}
\displaystyle x_{r}

By putting
\displaystyle q=1
in (

\ell(R/{\mathfrak{n}}^{k})=(r-1)\binom{k+1}{2}-(r-2)k.

Hence,

e_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})-\ell(R/{\mathfrak{n}})=(r-1)-\big(r-1-(r-2)\big)=r-2=e_{1}({\mathfrak{n}}),

implying that
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{n}}

\displaystyle J=(x_{1}^{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{v_{r}})

(2) Let
\displaystyle R
be the face ring of the simplicial complex related to an
\displaystyle n

Theorem 4.5
.

Consider a complete graph,
\displaystyle K_{r},
on
\displaystyle r
vertices, where
\displaystyle r\geq 1.
Let
\displaystyle S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
and
\displaystyle I=I(K_{r})
denote the edge ideal of
\displaystyle K_{r}.
Let
\displaystyle R=S/I
and
\displaystyle J=(x_{1}^{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{v_{r}})
be an ideal of
\displaystyle R
such that
\displaystyle v_{i}>0
for all
\displaystyle i.
Then
\displaystyle J
is a stable ideal.

Proof.

We begin by observing that any minimal vertex cover of
\displaystyle K_{r}
contains exactly
\displaystyle r-1
vertices. Hence,
\displaystyle I=\cap_{i=1}^{r}(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{r}).
Set
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{r}),
for all
\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,r.
Let
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{n}}
denote the maximal homogeneous ideal of
\displaystyle R.
Note that
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}={\mathfrak{n}}
for all
\displaystyle i\neq j.
Since
\displaystyle\ell(R/(J^{[q]})^{k})=\ell(R/({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})^{k})\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i},
we compute
\displaystyle\ell(R/({\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]})^{k}).
From Theorem
3.3
, it follows that for all
\displaystyle q,k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left(\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{n}}^{[q]k}}\right)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{r}\ell\left(\frac{S}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\ell\left(\frac{S}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)+\cdots+(-1)^{r-1}\ \ell\left(\frac{S}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{[q]k}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ rkq-\binom{r}{2}+\binom{r}{3}+\cdots+(-1)^{r-1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ rkq-(r-1).

This implies that
\displaystyle\ell(R/(J^{[q]})^{k})=rkq\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}-(r-1)\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}.
In particular,
\displaystyle\ell(R/J^{k})=rk\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}-(r-1)\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}.
Hence,

e_{0}(J)-e_{1}(J)=r\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}-(r-1)\prod_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}=\ell(R/J),

implying that
\displaystyle J
is a stable ideal.

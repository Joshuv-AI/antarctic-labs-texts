Excitation spectra of quantum matter without quasiparticles II: random t t - J J models
Maria Tikhanovskaya, Haoyu Guo, Subir Sachdev, Grigory Tarnopolsky

Abstract
We present numerical solutions of the spectral functions of t t - J J models with random and all-to-all exchange and global SU( M M ) spin rotation symmetry. The solutions are obtained from the saddle-point equations of the large volume limit, followed by the large M M limit. These saddle point equations involve Green’s functions for fractionalized spinons and holons carrying emergent U(1) gauge charges, obeying relations similar to those of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) models. The low frequency spectral functions are compared with an analytic analysis of the operator scaling dimensions, with good agreement. We also compute the low frequency and temperature behavior of gauge-invariant observables: the electron Green’s function, the local spin susceptibility and the optical conductivity; along with the temperature dependence of the d.c. resistivity. The time reparameterization soft mode (equivalent to the boundary graviton in holographically dual models of two-dimensional quantum gravity) makes important contributions to all observables, and provides a linear-in-temperature contribution to the d.c. resistivity.

II
t
-
J
model and fractionalization

II.1
Model Hamiltonian

We consider the following Hamiltonian of the
t
-
J
model

H=\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle,l,\alpha}t_{ij}c_{il\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{jl\alpha}+h.c.+\sum_{\left\langle ij\right\rangle,\alpha\beta}J_{ij}\left(S_{i\alpha\beta}S_{j\beta\alpha}-\frac{1}{M}S_{i\alpha\alpha}S_{j\beta\beta}\right).

(4)

Here
c_{il\alpha}
is electron operator, with
i
denoting site,
l
denotes an auxiliary SU
(M^{\prime})
orbital label, and
\alpha
denotes SU(
M
) spin. The
S_{i\alpha\beta}=S_{i\beta\alpha}^{\dagger}
is the spin operator on site
i
, and the
1/M
term (which will be dropped in large
M
limit) is added to ensure it transform in the adjoint of SU
(M)
.

We assume the system lives on a Bethe lattice of coordination number
z
with

t_{ij}=\frac{t}{\sqrt{Mz}}\,,

(5)

and
J_{ij}
’s are Gaussian random variables with

\overline{J_{ij}}=0,\quad\overline{J_{ij}^{2}}=\frac{J^{2}}{Mz}\,.

(6)

Implementing dynamical mean-field approximation (
z\to\infty
), and assuming a diagonal mean-field ansatz, the Hamiltonian induces the following interaction terms in the single-site action

\begin{split}S\supset&\frac{t^{2}}{M}\sum_{l\alpha}\int_{0}^{1/T}{\rm d}\tau{\rm d}\tau^{\prime}\sum_{l\alpha}c_{l\alpha}^{\dagger}G_{c}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})c_{l\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})-\frac{J^{2}}{2M}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\int_{0}^{1/T}{\rm d}\tau{\rm d}\tau^{\prime}S_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)Q(\tau,\tau^{\prime})S_{\beta\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\,.\end{split}

(7)

Here the mean-field ansatz
G_{e}
and
Q
should satisfy the following self-consistency equations

G_{e}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{-1}{MM^{\prime}}\sum_{l\alpha}\langle c_{l\alpha}(\tau)c_{l\alpha}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle\,,\quad Q(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\left\langle S_{\beta\alpha}(\tau)S_{\alpha\beta}(\tau^{\prime})\right\rangle\,.

(8)

We also assume large
M,M^{\prime}
limit with fixed

k=\frac{M^{\prime}}{M}\,.

(9)

The full action also contains WZW terms for slave particles and Langrangian multipliers, which will be detailed shortly.

II.2
Fermionic spinon + Bosonic holon

We fractionalize the model by introducing fermionic spinon and bosonic holon:

c_{il\alpha}=f_{i\alpha}b_{il}^{\dagger},\quad S_{i\alpha\beta}=f_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{i\beta}\,.

(10)

Here the boson
b_{il}
transforms in anti-fundamental of SU(
M^{\prime}
) such that the electron
c_{il\alpha}
transforms in fundamental of SU(
M^{\prime}
). This induces a U(1) gauge symmetry

f_{i\alpha}(\tau)\to f_{i\alpha}(\tau)e^{i\phi_{i}(\tau)},\quad b_{il}(\tau)\to b_{il}(\tau)e^{i\phi_{i}(\tau)}.

(11)

For the theory to be consistent, the physical Hilbert space must be U(1) gauge-symmetric, which implies that the gauge charge is conserved, and we consider the representation

\sum_{\alpha}f_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{i\alpha}+\sum_{l}b_{il}^{\dagger}b_{il}=\kappa M.

(12)

We also implement the doping density
p
by

\frac{1}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{l}\langle b_{il}^{\dagger}b_{il}\rangle=p.

(13)

The full action is thus

\displaystyle S[f,b,\lambda]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/T}d\tau\left[\sum_{\ell}b_{\ell}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+i\lambda\right)b_{\ell}+\sum_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+\epsilon_{0}+i\lambda\right)f_{\alpha}-i\lambda\frac{M}{2}\right]

(14)

\displaystyle~+\frac{t^{2}}{M}\sum_{\ell,\alpha}\int_{0}^{1/T}d\tau d\tau^{\prime}f^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\tau)b_{\ell}(\tau)G_{c}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})b^{\dagger}_{\ell}(\tau^{\prime})f_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle~-\frac{J^{2}}{2M}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\int_{0}^{1/T}d\tau d\tau^{\prime}Q(\tau-\tau^{\prime})f_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\tau)f_{\beta}(\tau)f_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})f_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\,.

Introducing bilocal fields

G_{f}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{-1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\tau)f^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime}),\quad G_{b}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{-1}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{l}b_{l}(\tau)b^{\dagger}_{l}(\tau^{\prime})

(15)

the saddle-point equations for the slave particles

and

\displaystyle G_{b}(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}+\mu_{b}-\Sigma_{b}(i\omega_{n})},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{2}G_{f}(\tau)G_{f}(-\tau)G_{b}(\tau),

(17)

\displaystyle G_{f}(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}+\mu_{f}-\Sigma_{f}(i\omega_{n})},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}(\tau)

\displaystyle-J^{2}G_{f}(\tau)^{2}G_{f}(-\tau)+kt^{2}G_{f}(\tau)G_{b}(\tau)G_{b}(-\tau).

;

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{f}}{\pi}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\Delta_{f}\right)\frac{\sin(2\theta_{f})}{\sin(2\pi\Delta_{f})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\kappa+kp,

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{b}}{\pi}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\Delta_{b}\right)\frac{\sin(2\theta_{b})}{\sin(2\pi\Delta_{b})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+p\,.

II.3
Bosonic Spinon + Fermionic holon

We also discuss the other fractionalization scheme, consisting of bosonic spinons and fermionic holons,

c_{il\alpha}=\mathfrak{b}_{i\alpha}\mathfrak{f}_{il}^{\dagger},\quad S_{i\alpha\beta}=\mathfrak{b}_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{b}_{i\beta}\,,

(35)

with the gauge charge constraint

\sum_{\alpha}\mathfrak{b}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\mathfrak{b}_{i\alpha}+\sum_{l}\mathfrak{f}_{il}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{f}_{il}=\kappa M,

(36)

and filling density

\frac{1}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{l}\langle\mathfrak{f}_{il}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{f}_{il}\rangle=p\,.

(37)

But most of our analysis will be carried out in the fractionalization scheme in Section
II.2
.
Following the similar discussion of the other scheme, we can now derive the action to be

\displaystyle S[\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{b},\lambda]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/T}{\rm d}\tau\left[\sum_{\alpha}\mathfrak{b}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+i\lambda\right)\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha}\mathfrak{f}_{l}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+\epsilon_{0}+i\lambda\right)\mathfrak{f}_{l}-i\lambda\frac{M}{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{t^{2}}{M}\sum_{l\alpha}\int_{0}^{1/T}{\rm d}\tau{\rm d}\tau^{\prime}\mathfrak{f}(\tau)\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\tau)G_{c}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\mathfrak{b}_{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\mathfrak{f}^{\dagger}_{l}(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle G_{\mathfrak{b}}(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}+\mu_{\mathfrak{b}}-\Sigma_{\mathfrak{b}}(i\omega_{n})}\,,

\displaystyle G_{\mathfrak{f}}(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}+\mu_{\mathfrak{f}}-\Sigma_{\mathfrak{f}}(i\omega_{n})}\,,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathfrak{b}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-kt^{2}G_{\mathfrak{f}}(\tau)G_{\mathfrak{f}}(-\tau)G_{\mathfrak{b}}(\tau)+J^{2}G_{\mathfrak{b}}(\tau)^{2}G_{\mathfrak{b}}(-\tau)\,,

\displaystyle t^{2}G_{\mathfrak{f}}(\tau)G_{\mathfrak{b}}(-\tau)G_{\mathfrak{b}}(\tau)\,,

Again we assume a conformal ansatz for the solution

\quad G^{c}_{a}(\tau)=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{\pi\mathscr{E}_{a}}\\
\zeta e^{-\pi\mathscr{E}_{a}}\end{pmatrix}\frac{b_{a}^{\Delta_{a}}}{|J\tau|^{2\Delta_{a}}},\quad G_{a}^{c}(i\omega)=-\frac{iC_{a}}{J}\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\theta_{a}}\\
-e^{i\theta_{a}}\end{pmatrix}|\omega/J|^{2\Delta_{a}-1}\,,

(48)

where
c
in the superscript means conformal and subscript
a=\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{f}
indexes boson and fermion respectively.
Assuming
\Delta_{\mathfrak{f}}=\Delta_{\mathfrak{b}}=1/4
, the coefficients
C_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
C_{\mathfrak{f}}
satisfy

\begin{split}&(t^{2}/J^{2})C_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}C_{\mathfrak{b}}^{2}\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})=-\pi\,,\\
&C_{\mathfrak{b}}^{4}\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})-k(t^{2}/J^{2})C_{\mathfrak{b}}^{2}C_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{f}})=-\pi\end{split}

(49)

and we find

C_{\mathfrak{b}}=\Big(\frac{\pi(1-\eta)}{-\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}\Big)^{1/4},\quad C_{\mathfrak{f}}=\frac{J}{t}\Big(\frac{\pi}{-\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})(1-\eta)}\Big)^{1/4},\quad\eta\equiv-k\frac{\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{f}})}{\cos(2\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}\,.

(50)

The unitarity constraints are
-\pi\Delta_{\mathfrak{f}}<\theta_{\mathfrak{f}}<\pi\Delta_{\mathfrak{f}}
,
\pi\Delta_{\mathfrak{b}}<\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}<\pi/2
and
the condition for real
C_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
C_{\mathfrak{f}}
is
\eta<1
as before.

The Luttinger constraints for
\theta_{\mathfrak{f}},\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}
are

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\pi}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\Delta_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)\frac{\sin(2\theta_{\mathfrak{f}})}{\sin(2\pi\Delta_{\mathfrak{f}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-p\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}{\pi}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\Delta_{\mathfrak{b}}\right)\frac{\sin(2\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}{\sin(2\pi\Delta_{\mathfrak{b}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\kappa-kp\,.

(52)

If we ignore the Luttinger constraints, the saddle point equations above are identical to that of the other fractionalization scheme by substitution

G_{f}(\tau)\leftrightarrow G_{\mathfrak{b}}(\tau),\quad G_{b}(\tau)\leftrightarrow G_{\mathfrak{f}}(\tau),\quad 0<\tau<1/T\,,

(53)

and we transform
\tau<0
into
0<\tau<\beta
using KMS relation. In particular, when
\Delta_{\mathfrak{f}}=\Delta_{b}=\Delta_{\mathfrak{b}}=\Delta_{f}=1/4
, we could identify the Green’s functions in the two schemes by

\theta_{\mathfrak{f}}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{b},\quad\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{f},\quad C_{\mathfrak{f}}=C_{b},\quad C_{\mathfrak{b}}=C_{f}.

(54)

However, we point out that (
54
) is incompatible with Luttinger constraints. For example, we can add (
34
) and (
51
) together and using (
54
) to see
\sin(2\theta_{b})=1
, which leads to
p=0
, but (
52
) has no solution for
p=0
. This reflects fact that at large
M
limit the two fractionalization schemes are two different theories, and they are identical only at
M=2,M^{\prime}=1
.

III
Operator Spectrum and Corrections to Conformality

III.1
Corrections to Conformality

The saddle point solutions
G_{f}
and
G_{b}
of the
t
-
J
model can be viewed as a correlation functions in conformal field theory, which is deformed by infinite series of irrelevant operators
Gross and Rosenhaus 2017
;
Klebanov and Tarnopolsky 2017
;
Klebanov et al. 2018
. Therefore the two-point functions receive corrections from these operators. We shall focus here on the fermionic spinon
+
bosonic holon model and the results can be easily generalized to the other case. The general expression for the two-point function at zero temperature reads

\displaystyle G(\tau)

\displaystyle=G^{c}(\tau)\left(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}v_{h}}{|J\tau|^{h-1}}-\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}-\sum_{h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}\alpha_{h^{\prime\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}-3}}-\dots\right)\,,

(55)

where
v_{h}
,
a_{hh^{\prime}}
,
a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}
, etc are four-component vectors in
b/f
and
+/-
basis, so
v_{h}=(v_{hb+},v_{hb-},v_{hf+},v_{hf-})^{\textrm{T}}
. For instance the fermionic Green’s function for
\tau>0
reads

G_{f}(\tau)=G^{c}_{f}(\tau)\left(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}v_{hf+}}{|J\tau|^{h-1}}-\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}f+}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}-\sum_{h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}f+}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}\alpha_{h^{\prime\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}-3}}-\dots\right)\,.

(56)

The real numbers
\alpha_{h}
are the same for all components
b/f
and
+/-
. We are able to find these numbers only numerically. On the other hand the vectors
v_{h}
and anomalous dimensions
h
can be found analytically from solving the equation

K_{G}(h)v_{h}=v_{h}\,,

(57)

where the
4\times 4
matix
K_{G}(h)=W_{\Sigma}(h)W_{G}(h)
is a product of two matrices
W_{\Sigma}(h)
and
W_{G}
which in the
(b/f)\times(+/-)
basis are given by formulas

W_{\Sigma}(h)=\frac{\sec(\pi h)}{(1-2h)}\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\pi h+2\theta_{b})&\sin(2\theta_{b})-1\\
-\sin(2\theta_{b})-1&\sin(\pi h-2\theta_{b})\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\pi h+2\theta_{f})&\sin(2\theta_{f})-1\\
-\sin(2\theta_{f})-1&\sin(\pi h-2\theta_{f})\end{pmatrix},

(58)

W_{G}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&1&1\\
0&1&1&1\\
\eta&\eta&2-\eta&1-\eta\\
\eta&\eta&1-\eta&2-\eta\\
\end{array}\right),

(59)

and we remind that
\eta=-k\cos(2\theta_{b})/\cos(2\theta_{f})
. We notice that the matrix
W_{\Sigma}(h)
is block-diagonal in
b/f
space and
W_{G}
does not depend on
h
. For
\eta=0
the fermionic sector is independent on the bosonic one and its Green’s function is exactly the same as in the complex SYK model.

The results described above are based on the Kitaev-Suh theory
Kitaev and Suh 2018
;
Guo et al. 2020
;
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
.
The derivation of the kernel
K_{G}
is done in
Guo et al. 2020
.
The kernel
K_{G}
for bosonic spinon + fermionic holon case is obtained by substitution
\theta_{f}\to\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
\theta_{b}\to\theta_{\mathfrak{f}}
, for details see Appendix
B
. The coefficients
a_{hh^{\prime}}
,
a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}
can be calculated using the recursion procedure described in
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. We present explicit derivation of
a_{hh^{\prime}}
for the
t
-
J
model in Appendix
B
.

The linear order zero-temperature correction (
55
) was generalized to finite temperature in
Guo et al. 2020
;
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. The result takes the form on the interval
\tau\in(0,\beta)
:

G_{a}(\tau)=G^{c}_{a}(\tau)\left(1-\frac{v_{ha+}+v_{ha-}}{2}\frac{\alpha_{h}}{(\beta J)^{h-1}}f_{h}^{\mathrm{A}}(\tau)-\frac{v_{ha+}-v_{ha-}}{2}\frac{\alpha_{h}}{(\beta J)^{h-1}}f_{h}^{\mathrm{S}}(\tau)\right)\,,

(60)

where
G^{c}_{a}(\tau)
is the finite temperature conformal Green’s function

G^{c}_{a}(\tau)=-b_{a}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{\beta J}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\tau}{\beta}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\pi\mathscr{E}_{a}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\tau}{\beta})}\,,

(61)

and the functions
f_{h}^{\textrm{A}/\textrm{S}}(\tau)
are given by the formula

\displaystyle f_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{h-1}\Gamma(h)^{2}}{\Gamma(2h-1)\cos(\frac{\pi h}{2})}\left(A_{h}(e^{i\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}})+A_{h}(e^{-i\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}})\right)\,,

\displaystyle f_{h}^{\textrm{S}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{h-1}\Gamma(h)^{2}}{\Gamma(2h-1)\sin(\frac{\pi h}{2})}\left(iA_{h}(e^{i\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}})-iA_{h}(e^{-i\frac{2\pi\tau}{\beta}})\right)\,,

(62)

where
A_{h}(u)=(1-u)^{h}\mathbf{F}(h,h,1;u)
and

\mathbf{F}
is the regularized hypergeometric function. We stress that there are no vectors in the formula (
60
) and it is correct for both bosons and fermions
a=b,f
with their corresponding conformal two-point functions
G^{c}(\tau)
and components
v_{h\pm}
.

III.2
Operator Spectrum

In this subsection we describe solutions
h
of the equation (
57
). Such values of
h
correspond to scaling dimensions of the bilinear operators
\mathcal{O}_{h}
in the
t
-
J
model
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. We can rewrite the equation for
h
in the form

\det(1-K_{G}(h))=0\,.

(63)

We label positive solutions of the above equation by

and
\mathscr{E}_{b}

and

Guo et al. 2020

and is not normalized to unity. We assume that all other vectors are normalized to unity, so
v_{i}^{\textrm{T}}v_{i}=1
for

-

IV
Gauge Invariant Observables

In this section we present results for some gauge invariant observables including electron spectral density
\rho_{e}(\omega)
, spin spectral density
\rho_{Q}(\omega)
and optical conductivity
\text{Re}~\sigma(\omega)
. We shall be focusing here on the fermionic spinon + bosonic holon fractionalization of Section
II.2
.

IV.1
Electron Spectral Density

The electron Green’s function is a product of the spinon and holon Green’s functions

G_{e}(\tau)=-G_{f}(\tau)G_{b}(-\tau)\,.

(66)

To obtain the electron spectral density, we shall first analytically continue to retarded-Green’s function from imaginary time to the real one

G_{e,R}(t)=i\theta(t)(G_{e}(it+0)-G_{e}(it-0))\,,

(67)

where
\theta(t)
is the Heaviside step function.
Then we can find
G_{e,R}(\omega)
in the frequency space and extract the spectral density as

\rho_{e}(\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}G_{e,R}(\omega)\,.

(68)

The electron spectral density can also be written in terms of the spectral densities of spinon
\rho_{f}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}G_{f,R}
and holon
\rho_{b}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}G_{b,R}
as

\rho_{e}(\Omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm d}\nu(n_{F}(\Omega+\nu)+n_{B}(\nu))\rho_{f}(\Omega+\nu)\rho_{b}(\nu)\,,

(69)

where
n_{F/B}(\omega)=1/(e^{\beta\omega}-\zeta)
is the Fermi or Bose distribution.
We will perform the calculation at both zero temperature at finite temperature. At zero temperature we include nonlinear corrections, and at finite temperature we will restrict expressions to linear order corrections.

IV.1.1
Zero temperature

Using (
55
) and (
66
), we can write the electron Green’s function as

G_{e}(\tau)=G_{e}^{c}(\tau)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}}{|J\tau|^{h-1}}(v_{hf}+\bar{v}_{hb})-\sum_{hh^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}(a_{hh^{\prime}f}+\bar{a}_{hh^{\prime}b}-v_{hf}\bar{v}_{h^{\prime}b})-\dots\bigg).

(70)

Here
v_{hf(b)}
means taking the fermionic (bosonic) component of the 4-component vector
v_{h}
, and similar meanings apply to
a_{hh^{\prime}f(b)}
.
\bar{v}_{hb}
means switching the
\pm
components of
v_{hb}
and similar for
\bar{a}_{hh^{\prime}b}
.
G_{e}^{c}(\tau)
is the conformal electron Green’s function

\quad G^{c}_{e}(\tau)=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{\pi\mathscr{E}_{e}}\\
-e^{-\pi\mathscr{E}_{e}}\end{pmatrix}\frac{C_{e}}{|J\tau|}\,,\quad C_{e}=\frac{J}{t}\Big(\frac{-\cos(2\theta_{b})}{4\pi}\Big)^{1/2}\,,

(71)

where
\mathscr{E}_{e}=\mathscr{E}_{f}-\mathscr{E}_{b}
and we have used that
\Delta_{f}=\Delta_{b}=1/4
.
Analytically continuing to real-time and extract the spectral density, we obtain

\rho_{e}(\omega)=\rho_{e}^{c}(\omega)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}(v_{hf}+\bar{v}_{hb})}{\Gamma(h)}\left|\frac{\omega}{J}\right|^{h-1}-\sum_{hh^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}(a_{hh^{\prime}f}+\bar{a}_{hh^{\prime}b}-v_{hf}\cdot\bar{v}_{h^{\prime}b})}{\Gamma(h+h^{\prime}-1)}\left|\frac{\omega}{J}\right|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}-\dots\bigg).

(72)

Here
\rho_{e}^{c}(\omega)
is the electron-spectral weight of the conformal solution

\rho_{e}^{c}(\omega)=\frac{C_{e}}{J}\begin{pmatrix}e^{\pi\mathscr{E}_{e}}\\
e^{-\pi\mathscr{E}_{e}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(73)

where
C_{e}
is defined in (
71
).

IV.1.2
Finite Temperature

We will work on the interval
0<\tau<\beta
. The electron Green’s function to linear order in
\alpha_{h}
is

\displaystyle G_{e}(\tau)=

\displaystyle G_{e}^{c}(\tau)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}}{2(\beta J)^{h-1}}\big((v_{hf+}+v_{hf-}+v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})f_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\tau)

\displaystyle+(v_{hf+}-v_{hf-}-v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})f_{h}^{\textrm{S}}(\tau)\big)-\dots\bigg)\,,

(74)

where the conformal electron Green’s function at finite temperature is

G_{e}^{c}(\tau)=-C_{e}\Big(\frac{\beta J}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\tau}{\beta}\Big)^{-1}e^{2\pi\mathscr{E}_{e}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\tau}{\beta})}\,,

(75)

and the functions
f_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\tau)
and
f_{h}^{\textrm{S}}(\tau)
are given in (
62
).

To find electron spectral density at finite temperature we analytically continue
G_{e}(\tau)
to real
time using (
67
) and then take the Fourier transform to find
G_{e,R}(\omega)
in the frequency space.
Finally using (
68
) we find for the electron spectral density

\displaystyle\rho_{e}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\rho_{e}^{c}(\omega)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}}{2(\beta J)^{h-1}}\Big((v_{hf+}+v_{hf-}+v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{A}}\big(\frac{\beta\omega}{2\pi}-\mathscr{E}_{e}\big)

\displaystyle+(v_{hf+}-v_{hf-}-v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{S}}\big(\frac{\beta\omega}{2\pi}-\mathscr{E}_{e}\big)\Big)-\dots\bigg)\,,

(76)

where the conformal electron spectral density is

\rho_{e}^{c}(\omega)=\frac{C_{e}\cosh(\frac{\beta\omega}{2})}{J\cosh(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}-\pi\mathscr{E}_{e})}\,,

(77)

and the functions
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\omega)
and
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{S}}(\omega)
are

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\omega)&=\frac{2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{h}\Gamma(h)}{\sqrt{\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi h}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\textrm{Re}\,{}_{3}\textbf{F}_{2}\biggl(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{h,1-h,\frac{1}{2}+i\omega}{1, 1};1\biggr)\,,\\
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{S}}(\omega)&=\frac{2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{h}\Gamma(h)}{\sqrt{\pi}\cos\left(\frac{\pi h}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\textrm{Im}\,{}_{3}\textbf{F}_{2}\biggl(\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{h,1-h,\frac{1}{2}+i\omega}{1, 1};1\biggr)\,,\end{split}

(78)

where
\,{}_{3}\textbf{F}_{2}
is the regularized hypergeometric function.
For derivation of similar to (
76
) results see
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
.
Retaining only
h_{0}=2
mode in (
76
) we find

\displaystyle\rho_{e}(\omega)=\frac{C_{e}\cosh(\frac{\beta\omega}{2})}{J\cosh(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}-\pi\mathscr{E}_{e})}\left(1-\frac{\pi\alpha_{0}}{\beta J}\Big(\frac{\beta\omega}{2\pi}-\mathscr{E}_{e}\Big)\Big(4\tanh(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}-\pi\mathscr{E}_{e})-3(\sin(2\theta_{f})-\sin(2\theta_{b}))\Big)\right)\,,

(79)

where we have used the explicit expression (
65
) for the eigenvector
v_{0}
.

IV.2
Conductivity

The Kubo formula for conductivity on Bethe lattice is derived in
Guo et al. 2020
:

\text{Re}~\sigma(\omega)=\frac{2\pi M^{\prime}e^{2}t^{2}a^{2-d}}{z}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm d}\Omega\rho_{e}(\Omega)\rho_{e}(\Omega+\omega)\frac{n_{F}(\Omega)-n_{F}(\Omega+\omega)}{\omega}\,,

(80)

where
a
is lattice constant and
d
is spatial dimension. We can evaluate the expression in three scenarios as below:

IV.2.1
Zero temperature Optical conductivity

At zero temperature, we can evaluate the conductivity to nonlinear order in
\alpha_{h}
using (
72
), the result is

\begin{split}&\text{Re}~\sigma(\omega)=\sigma_{0}\Bigg[1-\sum_{h}\frac{2\alpha_{h}}{\Gamma(h+1)}(v_{hf+}+v_{hf-}+v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})|\omega/J|^{h-1}-\sum_{hh^{\prime}}\frac{2\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}|\omega/J|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}{\Gamma(h+h^{\prime})}\\
&\times[a_{hh^{\prime}f+}+a_{hh^{\prime}f-}+a_{hh^{\prime}b+}+a_{hh^{\prime}b-}-v_{hf+}v_{h^{\prime}b-}-v_{hf-}v_{h^{\prime}b+}-(v_{hf+}+v_{hb-})(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}b+})/2]\Bigg].\end{split}

(81)

Here
\sigma_{0}
is the
T=0
DC conductivity given by

\sigma_{0}=-\frac{M^{\prime}e^{2}a^{2-d}\cos(2\theta_{b})}{2z}\,,

(82)

where we used (
73
) for
\rho_{e}^{c}(\Omega)
.

IV.2.2
Finite temperature DC conductivity

The finite temperature DC conductivity can be evaluated by

\sigma_{DC}=\frac{2\pi M^{\prime}e^{2}t^{2}a^{2-d}}{z}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm d}\Omega\rho_{e}(\Omega)^{2}\left(-\frac{\partial n_{F}}{\partial\Omega}\right)\,,

(83)

The conformal contribution is given by

\begin{split}\sigma_{DC}^{c}&=\frac{2\pi M^{\prime}e^{2}t^{2}a^{2-d}}{z}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm d}\Omega\rho_{e}^{c}(\Omega)^{2}\left(-\frac{\partial n_{F}}{\partial\Omega}\right)=\sigma_{0}\,,\end{split}

(84)

where for
\rho_{e}^{c}(\Omega)
we used (
77
).
We can also include conformal corrections up to linear order

\delta_{h}\sigma_{DC}=\sigma_{0}\frac{\int{\rm d}\Omega\,2\rho_{e}^{c}(\Omega)\delta_{h}\rho_{e}(\Omega)\left(-\frac{\partial n_{F}}{\partial\Omega}\right)}{\int{\rm d}\Omega\rho_{e}^{c}(\Omega)^{2}\left(-\frac{\partial n_{F}}{\partial\Omega}\right)}\,.

Here

contains two terms given in (

\frac{\delta_{h}\sigma_{DC}}{\sigma_{0}}=-\frac{\alpha_{h}}{(\beta J)^{h-1}}(v_{hf+}+v_{hf-}+v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})R(h)\,,

(86)

where

R(h)=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm d}\omega\frac{\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\omega)}{\cosh^{2}(\pi\omega)}=-\frac{2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{h-1}\cos\left(\frac{\pi h}{2}\right)\Gamma(h-1)}{\sqrt{\pi}h\Gamma\left(h-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,.

IV.3
Spin Spectral Density

In the large

\rho_{Q}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}Q_{R}(\omega)

\rho_{Q}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\rho_{f}(\nu)\rho_{f}(\nu-\omega)(n_{F}(\nu-\omega)-n_{F}(\nu))\,.

The computation of this quantity is the same as the previous paper

IV.3.1
Zero temperature

At zero temperature, the conformal spin spectral density is

\rho_{Q}^{c}(\omega)=\frac{C_{Q}}{J}{\rm sgn\,}\omega,\quad C_{Q}=\Big(\frac{\cos(2\theta_{f})}{4\pi}(1-\eta)\Big)^{1/2}\,.

(94)

Including the conformal corrections, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{Q}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\rho_{Q}^{c}(\omega)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}(v_{hf+}+v_{hf-})}{\Gamma(h)}\left|\frac{\omega}{J}\right|^{h-1}

\displaystyle-\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}(a_{hh^{\prime}f+}+a_{hh^{\prime}f-}-v_{hf+}v_{h^{\prime}f-})}{\Gamma(h+h^{\prime}-1)}\left|\frac{\omega}{J}\right|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}-\dots\bigg)\,,

(95)

At finite temperature, we write the spin spectral weight to linear order in

(96)

where the conformal contribution to spin spectral weight is

\displaystyle\rho_{Q}^{c}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{Q}}{J}\tanh(\frac{\beta\omega}{2})\,.

(97)

and the function
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{A}}(\omega)
defined in (
78

Considering only the correction from
h_{0}=2

\displaystyle\rho_{Q}(\omega)=\frac{C_{Q}}{J}\tanh\big(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\big)\left(1-\frac{2\alpha_{0}\omega}{J}\tanh\big(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\big)-\dots\right)\,.

VI
Conclusions

We have presented numerical and analytic solutions of the non-Fermi liquid phase random
t
-
J
model without quasiparticle excitations, with good agreement between the two approaches. The solutions were obtained the large
M
limit of a model with SU(
M
) spin symmetry, and follow our previous analyses of the undoped insulator in Ref.
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. The theory has the structure of the SYK model for fractionalized spinons and holons carrying gauge charges, which have random
q=4
-body interaction terms between them. Note, however, that expressed in terms of the underlying gauge-invariant electrons, the interactions are not of the
q=4
SYK form.

Our attention focused on the leading and sub-leading singularities of several gauge-invariant physical observables at low frequencies and temperatures. These are controlled by operators with scaling dimension
h
which perturb the leading conformally invariant solution. As the theory is in
0+1
spacetime dimensions, we require
h>1
for the operator to be irrelevant in the infrared, and contribute to the sub-leading singularities.

An important operator that appears in all the subleading corrections is the time-reparameterization mode, or the boundary graviton of the holographic dual. This operator is universal in SYK criticality, and has
h_{0}=2
exactly; no corrections are expected to this scaling dimension at all orders in
1/M
. It was also present in the previous analysis of the insulating model
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. This operator leads to relative corrections in observables which scale
\omega^{h_{0}-1}
or
T^{h_{0}-1}
in the IR. In particular, as the leading contribution to the resistivity is
T
-independent in the
t
-
J
, the first contribution from the graviton mode is a linear-in-
T
resistivity
Guo et al. 2020
.

We also found that the
t
-
J
model has an irrelevant operator with scaling dimension
1<h_{1}<2
over the regime where scaling solutions were found numerically (see Fig.
8
). This will therefore lead to corrections which scale as
\omega^{h_{1}-1}
or
T^{h_{1}-1}
in the IR, which are more singular than those from the graviton mode. This operator is special to the doped case, and was not found in insulator studied previously
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
.
However, it should be noted that this operator is not protected, and we do expect
1/M
corrections to the value of
h_{1}
. We have not studied these corrections, and the possibility remains that such corrections will increase the value of
h_{1}
above 2, and return the graviton mode as the dominant correction. Moreover, we found that the numerical co-efficient of the
h_{1}
correction was quite small in the spin spectral density, and significantly smaller than that of the
h_{0}
correction (see Fig.
9
).

A recent exact-diagonalization study of the random
t
-
J
model with SU(2) spin symmetry
Shackleton et al. 2020
computed the spin spectral density and found that it matched the spectral density similar to Fig.
10
a over a wide range of frequencies near the critical doping where the spin-glass order vanished (spin-glass order is present at
M=2
for low doping). The numerics show
\rho_{Q}(\omega)\sim\mbox{sgn}(\omega)\left[c_{0}-c_{1}|\omega|+\ldots\right]
at small
\omega
, consistent with the numerical results in Table
9
where the
h_{1}
contribution is small.

We note from Figs.
9
and
12
that the contributions of
h_{1}
mode are more significant for the other observables, including the d.c. resistivity, and can be comparable to the
h_{0}=2
contribution. It would be interesting to study the relative contributions of these modes at higher order in
1/M
to understand the situation for SU(2) better.

Appendix A
Zero temperature numerics for
t-J
model

We consider Dyson-Schwinger equations for the retarded Green’s function for
t-J
model, which is obtained by analytic continuation from the Matsubara frequency
i\omega_{n}\to\omega+i0
. The first pair of the Dyson-Schwinger equations reads

\displaystyle G_{f,R}(\omega)^{-1}=\omega+i0+\mu_{f}-\Sigma_{f,R}(\omega)\,;

(103)

\displaystyle G_{b,R}(\omega)^{-1}=\omega+i0+\mu_{b}-\Sigma_{b,R}(\omega)\,.

(104)

As before, we define analytic in the upper half plane Green’s functions
G(z)
and self energy
\Sigma(z)
, which are expressed through
the spectral densities
\rho(\omega)
and
\sigma(\omega)
as

\displaystyle G(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\frac{\rho(\omega)}{z-\omega}\,,\quad\Sigma(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\frac{\sigma(\omega)}{z-\omega}\,.

(105)

The Matsubara and retarded Green’s functions can be obtained from these functions by taking
z=i\omega_{n}
and
z=\omega+i0

105

\displaystyle G_{a}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}G_{a}(i\omega_{n})e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau}=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\frac{\rho(\omega)e^{-\omega\tau}}{1-\zeta e^{-\beta\omega}},\quad\tau\in(0,\beta)\,,

(106)

where

,
\zeta_{b}=1

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}(\tau)=J^{2}G_{f}^{2}(\tau)G_{f}(\beta-\tau)+kt^{2}G_{f}(\tau)G_{b}(\tau)G_{b}(\beta-\tau)\,;

(107)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b}(\tau)=t^{2}G_{f}(\tau)G_{f}(\beta-\tau)G_{b}(\tau)\,.

(108)

\Sigma_{R}(\omega)

and for bosons

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b}(i\omega_{n})

of the self-energy. For this we use Kramers-Kronig transform

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\nu}{\pi}\frac{\Sigma^{\prime\prime}(\nu)-\Sigma^{\prime\prime}(\omega)}{\nu-\omega}\,.

(117)

Defining
\Sigma^{\prime(\pm)}_{a}(\omega)
as
\Sigma_{a}^{\prime}(\omega)=\Sigma^{\prime(+)}_{a}(\omega)\theta(\omega)+\Sigma^{\prime(-)}_{a}(-\omega)\theta(-\omega)
we find

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime(\pm)}_{a}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\pm\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{d\nu}{\pi}\left(\frac{\Sigma^{\prime\prime(\pm)}_{a}(\nu)-\Sigma^{\prime\prime(\pm)}_{a}(\omega)}{\nu-\omega}-\frac{\Sigma^{\prime\prime(\mp)}_{a}(\nu)-\Sigma^{\prime\prime(\pm)}_{a}(\omega)}{\nu+\omega}\right)\,.

(118)

At zero temperature we set chemical potential
\mu_{a}=\Sigma_{a}^{\prime}(\omega=0)
, so introducing
h^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)
as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a}^{\prime}(\omega)-\Sigma_{a}^{\prime}(0)=\begin{cases}4\sqrt{\omega}h^{+}_{a}(\omega),\quad\omega>0\\
4\sqrt{-\omega}h^{-}_{a}(-\omega),\quad\omega<0\end{cases}\,.

(119)

and simplifying expressions we finally obtain

\displaystyle h^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)=\pm\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\nu\left(\frac{\sqrt{\omega}s^{\pm}_{a}(\nu)-\sqrt{\nu}s^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)}{\sqrt{\nu}(\nu-\omega)}+\frac{\sqrt{\omega}s^{\mp}_{a}(\nu)+\sqrt{\nu}s^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)}{\sqrt{\nu}(\nu+\omega)}\right)\,.

(120)

Now using the first pair of the Dyson-Schwinger equations (
103
),(
104
) we can get
g^{\pm}_{a}
from
s^{\pm}_{a}
and
h^{\pm}_{a}

\displaystyle g^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)=-\frac{4s^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)}{(4h^{\pm}_{a}(\omega)\mp\sqrt{\omega})^{2}+16\pi^{2}(s^{\pm}_{a}(\omega))^{2}}\,.

(121)

We solve Dyson-Schwinger equations iteratively using (
115
), (
116
), (
120
) and (
121

\displaystyle g^{\pm}_{a}(0)=\frac{C_{a}}{\pi J}\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}\pm\theta_{a}),\quad s_{a}^{\pm}(0)=-\frac{1}{4\pi C_{a}}\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}\pm\theta_{a}),\quad h_{a}^{\pm}(0)=\mp\frac{1}{4C_{a}}\cos(\frac{\pi}{4}\pm\theta_{a})\,.

(122)

where the constants

B.0.2
Linear order correction at zero temperature

Following derivations in
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
, the first order conformal corrections at zero temperature take the following form :

\begin{split}\delta G(\tau)&=\sum_{h}\delta_{h}G(\tau)\,,\\
\delta_{h}G(\tau)&=-\alpha_{h}v_{h}\frac{G^{c}(\tau)}{|J\tau|^{h-1}}\,,\quad\alpha_{h}=-\frac{w_{h}W_{\Sigma}(h)\vec{\sigma}_{h}}{k_{G}^{\prime}(h)}\,.\end{split}

(134)

Here the sum is over the operator spectrum of the
t
-
J
model determined by (
63
), and
v_{h}
and
w_{h}
are the corresponding right and left eigenvectors of
K_{G}(h)
respectively which have eigenvalue
k_{G}(h)=1
and normalized as
w_{h}v_{h}=1
, and
\vec{\sigma}_{h}=(\delta\sigma_{b+},\delta\sigma_{b-},\delta\sigma_{f+},\delta\sigma_{f-})^{T}
.

It can be verified that the eigenspace corresponding to the two
h=1
operators are spanned by
\delta G^{c}_{a}/\delta\theta_{b}
and
\delta G^{c}_{a}/\delta\theta_{b}
(i.e. changes of
\theta_{b}
and
\theta_{f}
) which by the Luttinger relations (
33
),(
34
) are related to
\delta\mathcal{Q}_{f}
and
\delta\mathcal{Q}_{b}
respectively. Therefore, there is no need to include these corrections if we use the renormalized value of
\theta_{f},\theta_{b}
.

B.0.3
Nonlinear order correction at zero temperature

It is also possible to obtain corrections of higher order in
\alpha
following derivations in
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. The result takes the form

\displaystyle G(\tau)

\displaystyle=G^{c}(\tau)\bigg(1-\sum_{h}\frac{\alpha_{h}v_{h}}{|J\tau|^{h-1}}-\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}-\sum_{h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}}\frac{a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}\alpha_{h^{\prime\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}-3}}-\dots\bigg)\,,

(135)

where
v_{h}
,
a_{hh^{\prime}}
,
a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}
, etc are four-component vectors. The coefficients
a_{hh^{\prime}}
,
a_{hh^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}
can be calculated using the recursion procedure described in
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
. As an example, we perform a calculation for
a_{hh^{\prime}}
, using Eq. (4.32) of
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
:

\delta^{2}G=\frac{1}{1-W_{\Sigma}W_{G}}\left[W_{\Sigma}\bar{\delta}^{2}\Sigma_{*}[G]+\bar{\delta}^{2}G_{*}[\Sigma]\right],

(136)

where
\Sigma_{*}[G]
and
G_{*}[\Sigma]
denote the RHS of Schwinger-Dyson equations (
16
)-(
19
) with
(i\omega_{n}+\mu)
dropped. The meaning of
\bar{\delta}^{2}\Sigma_{*}[G]
is that we compute the second-order variation of
\Sigma_{*}
using only first-order variation of
G
.

The second term in the bracket of (
136
) can be computed using the same procedure as
Tikhanovskaya et al. 2020
, with the result

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\delta}^{2}G_{*}(\tau)}{G^{c}(\tau)}=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}F(h+h^{\prime}-1)^{-1}\big(F(h)v_{h}\cdot F(h^{\prime})v_{h^{\prime}}\big)\frac{\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}\,,

(137)

where “
\cdot
” means multiply two vectors entry-wise and
F(h)
is a 4 by 4 matrix that acts on
v_{h}
:

\begin{split}F(h)&=f(h,\theta_{b})\oplus f(h,\theta_{f}),\\
f(h,\theta)&=-\frac{\Gamma(3/2-h)}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-e^{-\frac{1}{2}i\pi h}\left(e^{2i\theta}+i\right)&-e^{\frac{i\pi h}{2}}\left(e^{2i\theta}-i\right)\\
i\left(e^{2i\theta}+i\right)e^{\frac{1}{2}i(\pi h-4\theta)}&\left(-1-ie^{2i\theta}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}i(\pi h+4\theta)}\\
\end{array}\right).\end{split}

(138)

The first term in the bracket of (
136
) can be computed by expanding the Schwinger-Dyson equation
\Sigma=\Sigma_{*}[G]
to second order, with the result

\frac{\bar{\delta}^{2}\Sigma_{*}(\tau)}{\Sigma^{c}(\tau)}=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{h}\alpha_{h^{\prime}}}{|J\tau|^{h+h^{\prime}-2}}d_{hh^{\prime}}\,,

(139)

and
d_{hh^{\prime}}
is a four-component vector whose entries are

\displaystyle d_{hh^{\prime};b+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(v_{hb+}(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})+v_{hf-}(v_{h^{\prime}b+}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})+v_{hf+}(v_{h^{\prime}b+}+v_{h^{\prime}f-}))\,,

(140)

\displaystyle d_{hh^{\prime};b-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(v_{hb-}(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})+v_{hf-}(v_{h^{\prime}b-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})+v_{hf+}(v_{h^{\prime}b-}+v_{h^{\prime}f-}))\,,

(141)

\displaystyle d_{hh^{\prime};f+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta\Big(v_{hb-}(v_{h^{\prime}b+}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})+v_{hb+}(v_{h^{\prime}b-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})-2v_{hf-}v_{h^{\prime}f+}

(142)

\displaystyle+v_{hf+}(v_{h^{\prime}b-}+v_{h^{\prime}b+}-2(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+}))\Big)+v_{hf-}v_{h^{\prime}f+}+v_{hf+}(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})

\displaystyle d_{hh^{\prime};f-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta\Big(v_{hb-}(v_{h^{\prime}b+}+v_{h^{\prime}f-})+v_{hb+}(v_{h^{\prime}b-}+v_{h^{\prime}f-})-2v_{hf+}v_{h^{\prime}f-}\,,

(143)

\displaystyle+v_{hf-}(v_{h^{\prime}b-}+v_{h^{\prime}b+}-2(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+}))\Big)+v_{hf-}(v_{h^{\prime}f-}+v_{h^{\prime}f+})+v_{hf+}v_{h^{\prime}f-}\,.

We remind the reader that
\eta=-k\cos(2\theta_{b})/\cos(2\theta_{f})

137
) and (

\displaystyle a_{hh^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle-\big(1-W_{\Sigma}(h+h^{\prime}-1)W_{G}\big)^{-1}\Big(F(h+h^{\prime}-1)^{-1}\big(F(h)v_{h}\cdot F(h^{\prime})v_{h^{\prime}}\big)+W_{\Sigma}(h+h^{\prime}-1)d_{hh^{\prime}}\Big)\,.

Appendix C
Qualitative analysis of potentially relevant operators in the
t
-
J
model

The goal of this analysis is to determine the range of parameters regarding the existence and stability of the saddle conformal solution, and to study the relevance of time-reparameterization mode
h=2
. The outcome of the analysis is in Fig.
2
and Fig.
4
. We will mainly discuss the fermionic spinon + bosonic holon model. The analysis of the fermionic holon + bosonic spinon model is completely parallel as the kernel is related to by
\theta_{f}\to\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
\theta_{b}\to\theta_{\mathfrak{f}}
.

We will be analyzing the function

F(h)\equiv\det(K_{G}(h)-1)=0,

(145)

whose zeroes determine the scaling dimension of irrelevant operators appearing in the corrections.
The conformal saddle exists if the Eq.(
24
) has real solutions for
C_{f}
and
C_{b}
, which implies

\cos(2\theta_{f})+k\cos(2\theta_{b})>0.

(146)

Here
\theta_{f}\in[-\pi/4,\pi/4]
and
\theta_{b}\in[\pi/4,\pi/4]
are assumed to respect unitarity constraint.

By numerically inspecting the function
F(h)
, we found that there is almost always a solution with scaling dimension
\text{Re}~h_{1}<\frac{3}{2}
. Depending on parameters, this solution may be on the real axis within the range
\frac{1}{2}<h_{1}<\frac{3}{2}
, or it may move onto the imaginary axis such that
h_{1}=1/2\pm is,~s>0
.

Qualitative behaviors of the solution can be described by simple inequalities, which we describe below. First, whether the solution
h_{1}
is real or complex depends on the sign of
F(h)
near the pole
h=1/2
. When
h_{1}
moves from real to complex, the pole switches sign. Therefore the existence of a complex solution corresponds to

\begin{split}0>&16\pi^{2}\lim_{h\to 1/2}(h-\frac{1}{2})^{4}F(h)=8\left(4\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right)+\pi\right)\cos\left(2\theta_{f}\right)+16k\cos\left(4\theta_{b}\right)+16k+\pi(3\pi-4)\\
&+2(\pi-2)k\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right)\left(8\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right)+3\pi-4\right)\sec\left(2\theta_{f}\right)+4(3\pi-4)(k+1)\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right).\end{split}

(147)

Second, generically
F(h)
has a double zero at
h=1
, corresponding two gauge charges
\mathcal{Q}_{f},~\mathcal{Q}_{b}
which have scaling dimension 1. When
h_{*}
crosses 1,
F(1)
will be a triple zero such that

0=F^{\prime\prime}(1)=16\left(\pi\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right)+2\right)\left(\pi\cos\left(2\theta_{f}\right)+2\right)\sec\left(2\theta_{f}\right)\left(k\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right)+\cos\left(2\theta_{f}\right)\right).

(148)

Assuming, (
146
) holds, the only factor that could change sign is
2+\pi\cos(2\theta_{b})
, so the condition for
1/2<h_{1}<1
is

\theta_{b}>\frac{1}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{-2}{\pi}\right)\simeq 0.3598\pi.

(149)

We also remark that this is the maximum of the LHS of Luttinger constraint (
34
). When
h_{1}=\frac{3}{2}
, it will be absorbed by the pole of
F(h)
at
h=\frac{3}{2}
, so the mode disappears (it has zero OPE with the
f
and
b
). Physically, this corresponds to
\theta_{b}=\pi/4
, i.e. the holon density is zero.

Additionally, there can be a second solution near
h_{2}
near
h=2
. When this mode crosses
h=2
,
F^{\prime}(2)
changes sign. The condition that
h_{2}<2
is

0>F^{\prime}(2)=\frac{16}{81}\sec\left(2\theta_{f}\right)\left(-4k\cos\left(2\theta_{b}\right)-3\pi k\cos\left(4\theta_{b}\right)+4\cos\left(2\theta_{f}\right)+3\pi\cos\left(4\theta_{f}\right)-3\pi k+3\pi\right).

(150)

The above constraints define all the boundaries in Fig.
2
. Same inequalties with
\theta_{f}\to\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
\theta_{b}\to\theta_{\mathfrak{f}}
define the boundaries in Fig.
4
.

Appendix D
Calculation of Optical Conductivity

In this appendix we give details on the evaluation of (
90
). For convenience, we will work in units where
\beta=2\pi
.

Our starting point is the Kubo formula (
80
), recast into the following form

\text{Re}\sigma(\omega)=\frac{\sigma_{0}}{C_{e}^{2}/J^{2}}\int{\rm d}\Omega\frac{1}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{e^{2\pi\Omega}+1}-\frac{1}{e^{2\pi(\Omega+\omega)}+1}\right)\rho_{e}(\Omega)\rho_{e}(\Omega+\omega)\,,

(151)

Here the prefactors are chosen such that the conformal contribution is
\sigma_{0}
.

The electron spectral weight is given in (
76
),

\rho_{e}(\omega)=\rho_{e}^{c}(\omega)\left(1-\sum_{h}V_{h}^{\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{A}}\big(\omega-\mathscr{E}_{e}\big)-V_{h}^{\mathrm{S}}\mathcal{R}_{h}^{\textrm{S}}\big(\omega-\mathscr{E}_{e}\big)\right),

(152)

where

V_{h}^{\mathrm{A/S}}=\frac{\alpha_{h}}{2(\beta J)^{h-1}}(v_{hf+}\pm v_{hf-}\pm v_{hb+}+v_{hb-})\,.

(153)

The conformal contribution is

\text{Re}\sigma_{0}(\omega)=\sigma_{0}I(0^{+},\omega,\mathscr{E}_{e})\,,

(154)

where the integral involved is of the type

I(\epsilon,\omega,\mathscr{E})=\int{\rm d}\Omega\frac{e^{\epsilon\Omega}}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{e^{2\pi\Omega}+1}-\frac{1}{e^{2\pi(\Omega+\omega)}+1}\right)\frac{\cosh(\pi\Omega)\cosh(\pi(\Omega+\omega))}{\cosh(\pi(\Omega-\mathscr{E}))\cosh(\pi(\Omega+\omega-\mathscr{E}))}.

(155)

We have add a convergence factor
e^{\epsilon\Omega}
with
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
eventually, and then split the two terms in the parenthesis. The individual integrands are periodic under
\Omega\to\Omega+i
, so we can use an rectangular contour between
\text{Im}\Omega=0
and
\text{Im}\Omega=1
. The encircled poles are at
\Omega=\mathscr{E}+\frac{i}{2}
and
\Omega=\omega+\mathscr{E}+\frac{i}{2}
. We obtain

I(\epsilon,\omega,\mathscr{E})=\frac{1-e^{-\epsilon\omega}}{2\omega\sin\frac{\epsilon}{2}}e^{\epsilon\mathscr{E}},

(156)

\text{Re}\sigma_{0}(\omega)=\sigma_{0}I(0^{+},\omega,\mathscr{E})=\sigma_{0},

(157)

which is independent of frequency as expected from scaling analysis.

Next we look at contribution due to corrections.

\begin{split}\text{Re}\delta_{h}\sigma(\omega)&=-\sigma_{0}\int{\rm d}\Omega\frac{e^{\epsilon\Omega}}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{e^{2\pi\Omega}+1}-\frac{1}{e^{2\pi(\Omega+\omega)}+1}\right)\frac{\cosh(\pi\Omega)\cosh(\pi(\Omega+\omega))}{\cosh(\pi(\Omega-\mathscr{E}_{e}))\cosh(\pi(\Omega+\omega-\mathscr{E}_{e}))}\\

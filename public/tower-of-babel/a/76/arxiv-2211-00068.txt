Diffeomorphisms as quadratic charges in 4d BF theory and related TQFTs
Marc Geiller, Florian Girelli, Christophe Goeller, Panagiotis Tsimiklis

Abstract
We present a Sugawara-type construction for boundary charges in 4d BF theory and in a general family of related TQFTs. Starting from the underlying current Lie algebra of boundary symmetries, this gives rise to well-defined quadratic charges forming an algebra of vector fields. In the case of 3d BF theory (i.e. 3d gravity), it was shown in [ 1 ] that this construction leads to a two-dimensional family of diffeomorphism charges which satisfy a certain modular duality. Here we show that adapting this construction to 4d BF theory first requires to split the underlying gauge algebra. Surprisingly, the space of well-defined quadratic generators can then be shown to be once again two-dimensional. In the case of tangential vector fields, this canonically endows 4d BF theory with a diff ⁡ ( S 2 ) × diff ⁡ ( S 2 ) \mathrm{diff}(S^{2})\times\mathrm{diff}(S^{2}) or diff ⁡ ( S 2 ) ⋉ vect ​ ( S 2 ) ab \mathrm{diff}(S^{2})\ltimes\mathrm{vect}(S^{2})_{\mathrm{ab}} algebra of boundary symmetries depending on the gauge algebra. The prospect is to then understand how this can be reduced to a gravitational symmetry algebra by imposing Plebański simplicity constraints.



Introduction

Motivations

Recently, a vast effort has been directed towards understanding the structure of gauge theories at codimension-2 boundaries. This is the location where symmetry charges and their algebras are revealed and may be studied in order to gain insights into the fine structure of the classical and quantum theory. In the context of gravity, this is of particular importance because (asymptotic) boundary charges provide the very definition of physical quantities such as energy and momenta, and encode a wealth of information about the interior region. This is relevant for the study of classical phenomena such as (say) radiation, but also holds promise for defining quantum gravity itself, for example through AdS/CFT and celestial (or flat space, or Carrollian) holography in the case of asymptotically-AdS and asymptotically-flat spacetimes.

It has been proposed recently that a notion of holography could be meaningful (and potentially more fundamental) even at finite distance

These come indeed equipped with a quasi-local algebra of charges, whose exact form depends on the formulation of gravity being considered and whose representation theory can in principle be used to define building blocks of the quantum theory. Since classical gravity contains this rich quasi-local symmetry structure and given the availability of geometric quantization tools

This picture, if taken seriously, suggests that one should aim at understanding the most general symmetry algebra which can be supported on boundaries of gravitational systems. In the case of asymptotic boundaries in three and four spacetime dimensions, this has in part motivated for example the study of various extensions of the BMS algebra. We want to see what this entails at the level of the charges. Indeed, field-dependent gauge transformations do not generically lead to integrable charges unless one imposes extra conditions, on the dynamical fields and/or on the gauge parameters. \displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*}(\xi)\coloneqq p\mathcal{J}(\xi\lrcorner\,e)+\mathcal{T}(\xi\lrcorner\,\omega)+\frac{q}{2}\Big(\mathcal{T}(\xi\lrcorner\,e)-\mathcal{J}(\xi\lrcorner\,\omega)\Big).

We will however solve this puzzle and show that the 4d theory also admits similar field-dependent charges in addition to the usual diffeomorphisms. Another insight into the duality in the 3d case comes from evaluating

Quadratic charges in 4d BF theory

A similar structure to the one presented above in the 3d case exists for 4d TQFTs, with in particular a notion of dual diffeomorphisms which can be constructed from field-dependent internal gauge transformations. The construction is however not as straightforward as in the 3d case. In order to understand why this is so, let us first discuss the example of 4d BF theory.

The reason for considering 4d BF theory, especially with gauge group
{\mathfrak{so}}(4)

{\mathfrak{so}}(3,1)

[

It will be interesting to understand in future work how this extends to the diffeomorphism symmetries constructed from quadratic charges. Indeed, one can hope that if a holographic dual can be constructed for 4d topological BF theory, then this boundary theory could be reduced to a gravitational boundary theory by implementing the Plebański simplicity constraints reducing BF theory to gravity.

Summary of 4d BF theory

Let us consider as the basic variables a 2-form
\mathcal{B}
and a connection 1-form
\mathcal{A}
, both valued in a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We will think of this algebra as being
{\mathfrak{so}}(4)
or
{\mathfrak{so}}(3,1)
, although the details will not matter at this stage. The Lagrangian of 4d BF theory with quadratic potential is

\displaystyle L=\langle\mathcal{B}\wedge\mathcal{F}\rangle-\kappa\langle\mathcal{B}\wedge\mathcal{B}\rangle,

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{d}\mathcal{A}+{\frac{1}{2}}[\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{A}]
is the curvature of
\mathcal{A}
. The pairing
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by the Killing form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We keep it explicit because it will play an important role in what follows. The equations of motion and the symplectic potential are

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}\equiv\mathrm{d}\mathcal{B}+[\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}]\approx 0,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{F}-2\kappa\mathcal{B}\approx 0,\qquad\qquad\theta=\langle\mathcal{B}\wedge\delta\mathcal{A}\rangle.

(2.2)

The symmetries of the Lagrangian (
2.1
) are given by Lorentz transformations parametrized by
{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued 0-forms
\alpha
, and translations parametrized by
{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued 1-forms
\phi
. These internal gauge transformations act as

\displaystyle\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{B},\alpha],

\displaystyle\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{A}}\phi,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{A}}\alpha,

\displaystyle\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=2\kappa\phi.

(2.3b)

As a remark, we note that computing the repeated action of these internal gauge transformations with field-independent parameters leads to

\displaystyle\big[\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha},\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}\big]=\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{[\alpha,\phi]},\qquad\qquad\big[\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha},\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\beta}\big]=\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{[\alpha,\beta]},\qquad\qquad\big[\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi},\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\chi}\big]=0,

(2.4)

As in the previous section, we can now use the covariant phase space to compute the charges generating these transformations. Restricting once again momentarily to field-independent gauge parameters, we find that the charges are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\alpha)=\oint_{S}\langle\alpha,\mathcal{B}\rangle,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{T}(\phi)=-\oint_{S}\langle\phi\wedge\mathcal{A}\rangle,

(2.5)

and that they form the centrally-extended current algebra

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{J}(\alpha),\mathcal{T}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}([\alpha,\phi])-\oint_{S}\langle\alpha,\mathrm{d}\phi\rangle,

(2.6a)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{J}(\alpha),\mathcal{J}(\beta)\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}([\alpha,\beta]),

(2.6b)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{T}(\phi),\mathcal{T}(\chi)\}

\displaystyle=2\kappa\oint_{S}\langle\phi\wedge\chi\rangle.

(2.6c)

As expected, we recover the familiar fact that the algebra of charges is a centrally-extended current algebra based on the algebra of infinitesimal transformations (

By analogy with the 3d construction introduced above, we now want to understand how diffeomorphisms are related to these charges of internal gauge transformations. Because (

\displaystyle\Big(\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{A}}+\delta_{\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{T}}\Big)\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{A}-\xi\lrcorner\,(\mathcal{F}-2\kappa\mathcal{B})\approx\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{\xi}\mathcal{A},

(2.7a)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta^{\mathcal{T}}_{\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{B}}+\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{A}}\Big)\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{B}-\xi\lrcorner\,(\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B})\approx\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{\xi}\mathcal{B},

(2.7b)

\delta^{\mathcal{D}}_{\xi}\approx\delta^{\mathcal{J}}_{\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{A}}+\delta_{\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{B}}^{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\xi)=\mathcal{J}(\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{A})+\mathcal{T}(\xi\lrcorner\,\mathcal{B}).

Interestingly, this mechanism puts in a sense 4d BF theory on the same footing as 3d Chern–Simons (CS) theory. Indeed, if we consider 3d CS theory with

We first motivate and illustrate this construction on BF theory, and then present it in the case of a general 4d TQFT in order to mimic closely the structure of the 3d model (

Construction of quadratic charges

Let us now consider 4d BF theory with an algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
whose generators can be decomposed into

with brackets

\displaystyle[J_{i},P_{j}]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}P_{k},\qquad\qquad[J_{i},J_{j}]={\epsilon_{ij}}^{k}J_{k},\qquad\qquad[P_{i},P_{j}]=\lambda{\epsilon_{ij}}^{k}J_{k},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=A^{i}J_{i}+C^{i}P_{i},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{B}=B^{i}J_{i}+\Sigma^{i}P_{i}.

We also present in appendix

\displaystyle\langle P_{i},J_{j}\rangle_{\text{I}}=\eta_{ij},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{\text{I}}=0,\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\langle J_{i},J_{j}\rangle_{\text{I}}=0,

(2.11a)

\displaystyle\langle P_{i},J_{j}\rangle_{\text{II}}=0,

\displaystyle\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle_{\text{II}}=\lambda\eta_{ij},

\displaystyle\langle J_{i},J_{j}\rangle_{\text{II}}=\eta_{ij}.

(2.11b)

One should note that the second bilinear form is degenerate when

\displaystyle=-\lambda\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta]),

(3.23c)

with
\lambda

3.15
). This is a generalization to the case of the Lagrangian (
3.1
) of the diffeomorphism algebra (
2.17
) obtained above in 4d BF theory. One can also note that this diffeomorphism algebra is a “vector field version” of the current Lie algebra obtained above in (

). We have therefore constructed a 4d generalization of the results of
[
17
]
for the topological sector of the theory (
3.1
).

One can note that these results are of course compatible with the ones obtained in (

) in section
2
for 4d BF theory, since plugging the couplings of table (

) into

and

, in agreement with the definition (

The algebra (
3.23
) features two commuting copies of a
\text{diff}(S^{2})
algebra. As in the 3d case, this can be made more explicit with the change of generators
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\coloneqq(\mathcal{D}\pm\lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal{D}^{*})/2
. We can also take the “flat” limit

in order to obtain
\text{diff}(S^{2})\ltimes\text{vect}(S^{2})_{\text{ab}}

[

to the 4d topological case, is that we have considered the quadratic charges in the current algebra (
3.13

Perspectives

In this paper we have proposed a Sugawara-type construction for a class of 4d TQFTs including 4d BF theory. This Sugawara construction starts from the current algebra of boundary symmetries, and then constructs quadratic generators forming a diffeomorphism algebra with vector fields as the parameters. More precisely, for 4d BF theory with an algebra (
2.9
), starting from the current algebra (
2.6
), we have built the quadratic generators (
2.16
) and shown that their algebra is (
2.17
). In section
3
we have then generalized this construction to the family of 4d TQFTs described by the Lagrangian (
3.1
).

This construction extends to 4d the 3d construction presented in
[
1
,
17
]
. As we have recalled, in 3d the existence of two independent sets of quadratic charges relies on a duality between the 1-forms
e
and
\omega
. In the 4d case this duality is not available since the fields
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
of BF theory have a different form degree. However, we have argued that 4d BF theory is in fact the analogue of Chern–Simons theory in 3d, and that it is therefore natural to split its fields according to the decomposition (
2.10
) of the algebra (
2.9
). When this split is performed, there is then a notion of duality within the 1-form and 2-form sectors, which allows to define the general quadratic charge (
A.1
).

Surprisingly, as we have shown in the detailed proof of appendix
A
, for the examples of TQFTs described here the space of well-defined quadratic generators (which are integrable for tangential vector fields and which form a closed algebra) is 2-dimensional, just like in the 3d case. Although the quadratic generators
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}
are not functionally independent from the generators
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{T}
of the current algebra, they parametrize the independent symmetries of the theory. In 3d gravity in connection and triad variables, the diffeomorphisms are not the only gauge symmetries to which one can associate non-vanishing codimension-2 charges, since there are also the Lorentz transformations. Equivalently, one can replace these latter by the other set of (dual) diffeomorphisms, so as to preserve the size of the symmetry algebra. In 3d these extra quadratic generators have an interpretation in terms of a duality between the variables
(e,\omega)
, and also between the
u
and
\phi
directions in e.g. Bondi coordinates. Such an interpretation is not readily available in 4d, but should be investigated in future work, in particular to understand if the other set of quadratic charges (on top of the diffeomorphism charges) can be interpreted in terms of gravitational dual charges
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

We should stress that it is not yet clear which condition should be satisfied by the internal gauge group in order for extra quadratic charges to exist. It is already clear from the construction of appendix
B
that one can exhibit BF theories for which the space of well-defined quadratic generators is more than 2-dimensional. One could hope for an algebraic criterion relating the properties of the gauge algebra to the size of the well-defined family of quadratic generators. Going even further, one can also wonder if the construction can be extended to cubic and higher order generators, so as to obtain
w_{N}
algebras
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
. This is particularly interesting in light of the recent discussions on the
w_{1+\infty}
symmetry present in (self-dual) gravity
[
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
. We should however stress that already in the case of 3d gravity the extension of the construction
[
1
]
beyond second order has not yet been performed.

For future work, it would be interesting to investigate how the two sets of quadratic diffeomorphism charges relate to the bi-metric interpretation of BF theory
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
. It could indeed be the case that each quadratic diffeomorphism is a diffeomorphism charge for a specific metric sector of the bi-metric theory.

We also note that the construction presented here relies on a split of the internal gauge algebra, which essentially amounts to picking a preferred vector. In order to keep the internal covariance one should keep this vector as a variable and in particular include it in the covariant phase space analysis, as was done in
[
32
]
. Just like in the case of gravity
[
31
,
32
]
, which as we recall can be obtained from BF theory by imposing simplicity constraints
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, it would be interesting to perform the full covariant phase space analysis and the analysis of asymptotic symmetries for BF theory, either in terms of the bi-metric formulation or in terms of the bi-vector formulation. This would help understand the nature of the second set of quadratic charges (and in particular if they are “dual” charges as in the gravitational case
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
). This could also help develop a boundary fluid interpretation for 4d topological theories such as BF theory
[
46
,
47
,
10
]
, and once again enable to reach gravitational results by imposing the simplicity constraints via the symmetry structure.

Finally, an interesting question is that of how to include central extensions in the algebra of quadratic charges by performing a higher-dimensional analogue of the twisted Sugawara construction which exists in 3d gravity (see e.g. appendix C.4 of
[
17
]
).

Appendix A
Derivation of the dual diffeomorphism charge

In this appendix we give the detailed derivation of the set of quadratic charges built from field-dependent generators of the current algebra. We require these quadratic charges to be integrable for tangential vector fields and to form a closed algebra with themselves and with the charges of the current algebra.

In order to perform this construction, let us consider the most general quadratic field-dependent combination of the charges
(\mathcal{J}_{1},\mathcal{J}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2})
. This is given by

\displaystyle\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)=\not{\delta}\mathcal{J}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC)\big)+\not{\delta}\mathcal{J}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(bC+dA)\big)+\not{\delta}\mathcal{T}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+g\Sigma)\big)+\not{\delta}\mathcal{T}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(f\Sigma+hB)\big),

(A.1)

where the parameters
(a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h)
are to be determined and the vector field
\xi
is field-independent. We now impose the following two requirements on this general quadratic charge:

1)

The charges must be integrable when the vector fields
\xi
are tangent to the codimension-2 surface
S
.

2)

The charges must form a closed algebra (up to possible central extensions) with the gauge charges (
3.12
) of the initial current algebra.

Let us start with the condition of integrability. Using the explicit form of the gauge charges, we find that
\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)
takes the form

\displaystyle\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC)(\sigma_{1}\delta B+\sigma_{2}\delta\Sigma)+\xi\lrcorner\,(bC+dA)(\sigma_{3}\delta B+\sigma_{4}\delta\Sigma)

(A.2a)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+(\sigma_{1}\delta A+\sigma_{3}\delta C)\wedge\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+g\Sigma)+(\sigma_{2}\delta A+\sigma_{4}\delta C)\wedge\xi\lrcorner\,(f\Sigma+hB)

(A.2b)

\displaystyle=\delta\Big(\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC)\wedge(\sigma_{1}B+\sigma_{2}\Sigma)+\xi\lrcorner\,(bC+dA)\wedge(\sigma_{3}B+\sigma_{4}\Sigma)\Big)

(A.2c)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\xi\lrcorner\,\Big((a\delta A+c\delta C)\wedge(\sigma_{1}B+\sigma_{2}\Sigma)+(b\delta C+d\delta A)\wedge(\sigma_{3}B+\sigma_{4}\Sigma)\Big)

(A.2d)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-(\sigma_{1}a+\sigma_{3}d-\sigma_{2}h-\sigma_{1}e)\delta A\wedge\xi\lrcorner\,B-(\sigma_{2}a+\sigma_{4}d-\sigma_{1}g-\sigma_{2}f)\delta A\wedge\xi\lrcorner\,\Sigma

(A.2e)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-(\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b-\sigma_{3}e-\sigma_{4}h)\delta C\wedge\xi\lrcorner\,B-(\sigma_{2}c+\sigma_{4}b-\sigma_{3}g-\sigma_{4}f)\delta C\wedge\xi\lrcorner\,\Sigma.

(A.2f)

Equation (
A.2b
) is the explicit expression for
\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)
in terms of the gauge charges (
3.12
) of the current algebra. Equation (
A.2f
) is then a rewriting of
\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)
which isolates the integrable part and the part which vanishes when
\xi
is tangent to
S
. The last two lines are four independent and generically non-integrable contributions to
\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)
. To obtain (
A.2f
), we performed a
\delta
integration by part over the first line of (
A.2b
), followed by an “integration by parts” on
\xi\lrcorner\,
in the non-exact term arising from the
\delta
integration by part. Note that this procedure is of course not unique. Instead of using the first line of (
A.2b
), we could have used the second one. While this choice does affect the intermediate results, the final result of this appendix is of course independent of it.

Without additional conditions on the fields and/or on the vector field
\xi
to render the last two lines of (
A.2f
) integrable, we need to enforce the following four conditions on the parameters in order for
\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)
to be integrable:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}a+\sigma_{3}d-\sigma_{2}h-\sigma_{1}e

\displaystyle=0,

(A.3a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}a+\sigma_{4}d-\sigma_{1}g-\sigma_{2}f

\displaystyle=0,

(A.3b)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b-\sigma_{4}h-\sigma_{3}e

\displaystyle=0,

(A.3c)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}c+\sigma_{4}b-\sigma_{3}g-\sigma_{4}f

\displaystyle=0.

(A.3d)

We now assume that
(a,b,c,d,e,f,g)
are such that the constraints (
A.3
) hold, and we therefore consider the 4-dimensional space (spanned by
(a,b,c,d,e,f,g)
respecting (
A.3
)) of integrable charges of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\xi)=\mathcal{J}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC)\big)+\mathcal{J}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(bC+dA)\big)+\mathcal{T}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+g\Sigma)\big)+\mathcal{T}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(f\Sigma+hB)\big).

(A.4)

We now look at the Poisson brackets of these charges with the charges
(\mathcal{J}_{1},\mathcal{J}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2})
of the initial current algebra. We first consider the bracket with
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)
, which reads

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{1}\big([\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC),\phi]\big)-\sigma_{1}\oint_{S}\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC)\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi

(A.5)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+p_{3}\mathcal{T}_{1}\big([\xi\lrcorner\,(dA+bC),\phi]\big)+p_{4}\mathcal{T}_{2}\big([\xi\lrcorner\,(dA+bC),\phi]\big)-\sigma_{3}\oint_{S}\xi\lrcorner\,(dA+bC)\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi

(A.6)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sigma_{5}\oint_{S}\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+h\Sigma)\wedge\phi

(A.7)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sigma_{7}\oint_{S}\xi\lrcorner\,(gB+f\Sigma)\wedge\phi.

(A.8)

Here the first line corresponds to the Poisson bracket between
\mathcal{J}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(aA+cC)\big)
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)
, the second between
\mathcal{J}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(bC+dA)\big)
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)
, the third between
\mathcal{T}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+g\Sigma)\big)
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)
and the fourth line between
\mathcal{T}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(f\Sigma+hB)\big)
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)
. To go further, we now use the explicit expressions for the charges
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
to write

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\oint_{S}\big(\sigma_{1}a+(p_{3}\sigma_{1}+p_{4}\sigma_{2})d\big)[A,\xi\lrcorner\,A]\wedge\phi+\big(\sigma_{3}c+(p_{3}\sigma_{3}+p_{4}\sigma_{4})b\big)[C,\xi\lrcorner\,C]\wedge\phi

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\oint_{S}\big(\sigma_{1}c+(p_{3}\sigma_{1}+p_{4}\sigma_{2})b\big)[A,\xi\lrcorner\,C]\wedge\phi+\big(\sigma_{3}a+(p_{3}\sigma_{3}+p_{4}\sigma_{4})d)\big)[C,\xi\lrcorner\,A]\wedge\phi

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sigma_{1}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(a\mathrm{d}A+c\mathrm{d}C)\big)\wedge\phi-\sigma_{3}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(d\mathrm{d}A+b\mathrm{d}C)\big)\wedge\phi

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sigma_{5}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+h\Sigma)\big)\wedge\phi-\sigma_{7}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(gB+f\Sigma)\big)\wedge\phi,

(A.9)

where we have integrated by parts on
\mathrm{d}\phi
and used the relation
[P\wedge Q]\wedge R=(-1)^{(p+q)r}[R\wedge P]\wedge Q
in order to isolate
\phi
. Now, noting that the couplings satisfy the relations

\displaystyle p_{3}\sigma_{1}+p_{4}\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{3},\qquad\qquad p_{3}\sigma_{3}+p_{4}\sigma_{4}=\sigma_{8},

(A.10)

one can rewrite the bracket as

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\oint_{S}\big(\sigma_{1}a+\sigma_{3}d\big)[A,\xi\lrcorner\,A]\wedge\phi+\big(\sigma_{3}c+\sigma_{8}b\big)[C,\xi\lrcorner\,C]\wedge\phi

(A.11)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\oint_{S}\big(\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b\big)[A,\xi\lrcorner\,C]\wedge\phi+\big(\sigma_{3}a+\sigma_{8}d)\big)[C,\xi\lrcorner\,A]\wedge\phi

(A.12)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sigma_{1}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(a\mathrm{d}A+c\mathrm{d}C)\big)\wedge\phi-\sigma_{3}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(d\mathrm{d}A+b\mathrm{d}C)\big)\wedge\phi

(A.13)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sigma_{5}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(eB+h\Sigma)\big)\wedge\phi-\sigma_{7}\oint_{S}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(gB+f\Sigma)\big)\wedge\phi.

(A.14)

Finally, using Cartan’s magic formula, the fact that the vector field
\xi
is tangential, and massaging the various terms, the bracket becomes

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=b_{1}\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi)+b_{2}\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi)

(A.15)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\oint_{S}\xi\lrcorner\,\Big((\sigma_{1}a+\sigma_{3}d)F+\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{3}c+\sigma_{8}b)[C\wedge C]+(\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b)\mathrm{d}_{A}C

(A.16)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\qquad\qquad+(\sigma_{5}e+\sigma_{7}g)B+(\sigma_{5}h+\sigma_{7}f)\Sigma\Big)\wedge\phi

(A.17)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\oint_{S}(\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b-\sigma_{3}a-\sigma_{8}d)[\xi\lrcorner\,A,C]\wedge\phi,

(A.18)

where

\displaystyle b_{1}=\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}c+\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}b-\sigma_{1}\sigma_{4}a-\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}d}{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{4}-\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}},\qquad\qquad b_{2}=\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}a+\sigma_{3}^{2}d-\sigma_{1}^{2}c-\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}b}{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{4}-\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}}.

One can see in (

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{T}_{1}
has two types of contributions. The terms on the first line are once again charges of the current algebra. The last three lines however are neither expressible in terms of gauge charges nor central extensions. Therefore, the only possibility for the algebra to close is that somehow these terms cancel or vanish on-shell. More precisely, we would like to find a condition on the parameters such that the bracket takes the form

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}b_{1}\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi)+b_{2}\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi)-\oint_{S}\xi\lrcorner\,\Big(\alpha\text{EOM}_{B}+\beta p_{4}\text{EOM}_{\Sigma}\Big)\wedge\phi,

where
(\alpha,\beta)
are arbitrary parameters and where the rescaling by

has been introduced for later convenience. The reason for which only the equations of motion enforced by

and
\Sigma
appear here is because the terms which need to be cancelled in the bracket (
A.15
) only involve contributions from these equations of motion. It is clear that the last line in (
A.15
) never appears in the equations of motion. We therefore need to impose that this term is vanishing, which amounts to the condition

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b=\sigma_{3}a+\sigma_{8}d.

(A.21)

Then a comparison with the equations of motion (

) tells us that the remaining terms in (
A.15

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{1}+\beta p_{4}\sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}a+\sigma_{3}d,

(A.22a)

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{8}+\beta p_{4}\sigma_{9}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}c+\sigma_{8}b,

(A.22b)

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{3}+\beta p_{4}\sigma_{4}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}c+\sigma_{3}b,

(A.22c)

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{5}+\beta p_{4}\sigma_{7}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{5}e+\sigma_{7}g,

(A.22d)

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{7}+\beta p_{4}\sigma_{6}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{5}h+\sigma_{7}f.

(A.22e)

In summary, the coupling therefore have to satisfy the 10 equations (
A.3
), (
A.21
), and (
A.22
). This system is overdetermined, but luckily some of these equations are redundant. The system can be solved and the space of solutions is in fact 2-dimensional. It can be easily parametrized by two parameters
(x,y)
, in terms of which we get

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=x-yp_{3},

(A.23a)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=x+yq_{4},

(A.23b)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=yr_{1},

(A.23c)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=y,

(A.23d)

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=x,

(A.23e)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=x+y(q_{4}-p_{3}),

(A.23f)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=yq_{3},

(A.23g)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=yp_{4},

(A.23h)

provided that the
\sigma
’s satisfy

\displaystyle\sigma_{5}q_{3}+\sigma_{7}q_{4}-\sigma_{7}p_{3}-\sigma_{6}p_{4}=0,

(A.24)

which is in fact equivalent to the condition (

We have therefore found a 2-dimensional space of parameters within
(a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h)
which gives integrable charges

satisfying a closed algebra with
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{x,y}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{1}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=-x\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi)-yp_{4}\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi),

(A.25a)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{x,y}(\xi),\mathcal{T}_{2}(\tau)\}

\displaystyle=-yq_{3}\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\tau)-\big(x+y(q_{4}-p_{3})\big)\mathcal{T}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\tau),

(A.25b)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{x,y}(\xi),\mathcal{J}_{1}(\alpha)\}

\displaystyle=-\left(x-yp_{3}\right)\mathcal{J}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\alpha)-y\mathcal{J}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\alpha),

(A.25c)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{x,y}(\xi),\mathcal{J}_{2}(\chi)\}

\displaystyle=-yr_{1}\mathcal{J}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\chi)-(x+yq_{4})\mathcal{J}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\chi).

(A.25d)

We note that the brackets with
\mathcal{J}_{1}
and
\mathcal{J}_{2}
are closed regardless of the constraint on the parameters.

We can now pick two convenient representatives in the 2-parameter family
\mathcal{G}_{x,y}
of quadratic charges. The usual diffeomorphism corresponds to taking
(x,y)=(1,0)
, for which we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1,0}(\xi)=\mathcal{D}(\xi)=\mathcal{J}_{1}(\xi\lrcorner\,A)+\mathcal{J}_{2}(\xi\lrcorner\,C)+\mathcal{T}_{1}(\xi\lrcorner\,B)+\mathcal{T}_{2}(\xi\lrcorner\,\Sigma).

(A.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0,1}(\xi)=\mathcal{C}(\xi)=\mathcal{J}_{1}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(r_{1}C-p_{3}A)\big)+\mathcal{J}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(A+q_{4}C)\big)+q_{3}\mathcal{T}_{1}(\xi\lrcorner\,\Sigma)+\mathcal{T}_{2}\big(\xi\lrcorner\,(p_{4}B+q_{4}\Sigma-p_{3}\Sigma)\big).

These two charges
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
form a basis of the integrable quadratic charges for tangent vector fields, and we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{x,y}(\xi)=x\mathcal{D}(\xi)+y\mathcal{C}(\xi).

(A.28)

Finally, we want to compute the bracket between these quadratic charges themselves. To do so, we can either use the definition of the quadratic charges and the elementary brackets of the current algebra, or alternatively use the action of the quadratic generators on the fields. By construction, the diffeomorphism acts by the Lie derivative. On the other hand,
\mathcal{C}
acts as

\displaystyle\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\xi}A

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\big(r_{1}C-p_{3}A\big),

(A.29a)

\displaystyle\delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\xi}C

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\big(A+{q_{4}}C\big),

(A.29b)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{C}}B

\displaystyle={q_{3}}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Sigma,

(A.29c)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{C}}\Sigma

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\big(p_{4}B+q_{4}\Sigma-p_{3}\Sigma\big).

(A.29d)

Using these expressions in the covariant phase space formula for the Poisson brackets, it can be shown that the algebra between
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})
is closed and takes the form

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}(\xi),\mathcal{C}(\zeta)\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{C}([\xi,\zeta]),

(A.30a)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}(\xi),\mathcal{D}(\zeta)\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta]),

(A.30b)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{C}(\xi),\mathcal{C}(\zeta)\}

\displaystyle=(p_{3}-q_{4})\mathcal{C}([\xi,\zeta])-p_{4}q_{3}\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta]),

(A.30c)

which can further be rewritten as (
3.23
) upon redefining the new generator (
3.22
).

Appendix B
Self-dual basis and additional quadratic charges

In this appendix, we present another derivation of the two sets of quadratic charges in 4d BF theory. While this derivation is less general than the one presented in the main text, in the sense that it requires an additional property on the underlying algebra, it allows to understand possible generalizations to the construction of the admissible quadratic charges. We illustrate this at the end of the appendix on the example of BF theory with gauge algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{su}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
.

In the following we focus on the case of
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{so}}(4)
(the generalization to
{\mathfrak{so}}(3,1)
or
{\mathfrak{so}}(2,2)
can easily be done). Recall that the algebra then has the form (
2.9
)

\displaystyle[J_{i},P_{j}]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}P_{k},\qquad\qquad[J_{i},J_{j}]={\epsilon_{ij}}^{k}J_{k},\qquad\qquad[P_{i},P_{j}]=\lambda{\epsilon_{ij}}^{k}J_{k},

(B.1)

where
\lambda
is never vanishing. This is the Cartan decomposition. These exists however another decomposition of these algebra, the so-called self-dual decomposition, which allows to have another viewpoint on the existence of multiple quadratic charges. The self-dual basis is induced by the following change of basis
4
4

4

The notation
1,2
instead of the standard
\pm
will become clearer at the end of this appendix, when we consider the case with more subalgebras in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

\sigma^{1}_{i}=J_{i}+\sqrt{\lambda}\,P_{i},\qquad\qquad\sigma^{2}_{i}=J_{i}-\sqrt{\lambda}\,P_{i},

(B.2)

such that the brackets become

[\sigma_{i}^{a},\sigma^{b}_{j}]=2\delta^{ab}\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}\sigma^{a}_{k}.

(B.3)

The self-dual basis therefore splits the algebra into two commuting subalgebras. For example, in the case of
{\mathfrak{so}}(4)
, each subalgebra is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
. Note that this construction only works if
\lambda
is non-vanishing, which is why we are imposing this condition. The fields
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
can be decomposed in either of the above basis, and we have

\mathcal{A}=A^{i}J_{i}+C^{i}P_{i}=A_{1}^{i}\sigma^{1}_{i}+A_{2}^{i}\sigma_{i}^{2},\qquad A_{1}^{i}={\frac{1}{2}}(A^{i}+C^{i}),\qquad A_{2}^{i}=\frac{1}{2}(A^{i}-C^{i})\;,

(B.4a)

\mathcal{B}=B^{i}J_{i}+\Sigma^{i}P_{i}=B_{1}^{i}\sigma^{1}_{i}+B_{2}^{i}\sigma_{i}^{2},\qquad B_{1}^{i}={\frac{1}{2}}(B^{i}+\Sigma^{i}),\qquad B_{2}^{i}=\frac{1}{2}(B^{i}-\Sigma^{i}).

(B.4b)

The main advantage of the self-dual basis is that it allows to diagonalize the Killing forms of the algebra

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\langle P_{i},J_{j}\rangle=0\\
\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle=\lambda\eta_{ij}\\
\langle J_{i},J_{j}\rangle=\eta_{i,j}\end{array}\right)\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle P_{i},J_{j}\rangle=\eta_{ij}\\
\langle P_{i},P_{j}\rangle=0=\langle J_{i},J_{j}\rangle\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right\}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle=0\\
\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{1}_{j}\rangle=2\eta_{ij}=\langle\sigma^{2}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle\end{array}\right)\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle=0\\
\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{1}_{j}\rangle=2\sqrt{\lambda}\,\eta_{ij}=-\langle\sigma^{2}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right.

Similarly to what was done in equation (
2.12
), we can rewrite the 4d BF Lagrangian (
2.1
) in the self-dual basis. We then get the two Lagrangians

\displaystyle L^{+}

\displaystyle=2(L_{1}+L_{2}),

(B.14a)

\displaystyle L^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}(L_{1}-L_{2}),

(B.14b)

which are built from a pair of uncoupled BF Lagrangians

L_{i}=B_{i}\wedge F_{i}-\kappa B_{i}\wedge B_{i}.

(B.15)

Now, the whole construction of section
2.1
can be applied to each
L_{i}
independently of the other one. For each
L_{i}
we have two usual gauge symmetries, associated to two charges

\displaystyle\not{\delta}\mathcal{J}_{i}(\alpha_{i})=\oint_{S}\alpha_{i}\delta B_{i},\qquad\qquad\not{\delta}\mathcal{T}_{i}(\phi_{i})=\oint_{S}\delta A_{i}\wedge\phi_{i},

(B.16)

and a diffeomorphism symmetry which can be realized as a field-dependent combination of these gauge transformations. For a tangential vector field this diffeomorphism leads to the integrable charge

\mathcal{D}_{i}(\xi_{i})=\mathcal{J}_{i}(\xi_{i}\lrcorner\,A_{i})+\mathcal{T}_{i}(\xi_{i}\lrcorner\,B_{i}).

(B.17)

From the viewpoint of the initial BF Lagrangian, the algebra of quadratic charges is then naturally spanned by
\mathcal{D}_{1,2}
, which form the algebra

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}_{1}(\xi),\mathcal{D}_{1}(\zeta)\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}_{1}([\xi,\zeta]),

(B.18a)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}_{2}(\xi),\mathcal{D}_{2}(\zeta)\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}_{2}([\xi,\zeta]),

(B.18b)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}_{1}(\xi),\mathcal{D}_{2}(\zeta)\}

\displaystyle=0,

(B.18c)

corresponding to two commuting copies of
\text{diff}(S^{2})
. In order to recover the form (
2.17
) of the algebra, we need to go back to the Cartan basis by inverting the change of basis (
B.2
). This is easily done by considering the combinations

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\xi)=\mathcal{D}_{1}(\xi)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\mathcal{D}_{2}(\xi),\qquad\qquad\mathcal{D}^{*}(\xi)=\mathcal{D}_{1}(\xi)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\mathcal{D}_{2}(\xi).

(B.19)

It is then immediate to generalize this construction. Indeed, the main point was that we were able to rewrite the BF Lagrangian in terms of two independent Lagrangians (which are also of the BF type in the present case). However, it is evident that there is no reason to limit the construction to a rewriting in terms of only two independent Lagrangians. For example, let us consider 4d BF with gauge group given by
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{su}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
. The total connection
\mathcal{A}
and the 2-form
\mathcal{B}
can then be split into three components

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=A_{1}^{i}\sigma^{1}_{i}+A_{2}^{i}\sigma^{2}_{i}+A_{3}^{i}\sigma^{3}_{i},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{B}=B_{1}^{i}\sigma^{1}_{i}+B_{2}^{i}\sigma^{2}_{i}+B_{3}^{i}\sigma^{3}_{i},

(B.20)

where each
\sigma^{a}
for
a=1,2,3
generates
{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
and satisfies

[\sigma_{i}^{a},\sigma^{b}_{j}]=2\delta^{ab}\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}\sigma^{a}_{k}.

(B.21)

Using this basis, the 4d BF Lagrangian for
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{su}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
can be rewritten into three independent Lagrangians
L_{i}
for
{\mathfrak{su}}(2)
as we did previously. Each Lagrangian has its corresponding diffeomorphism charge, such that the full theory is equipped with three generators for the quadratic charges. Explicitly, the Lagrangian (
2.1
) depends, as usual, on the choice of a Killing form. In this particular case, three independent Killing forms exist, which are given by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma^{a}_{i},\sigma^{b}_{j}\rangle_{+,+}=0\;,\qquad\qquad\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{1}_{j}\rangle_{+,+}=\langle\sigma^{2}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle_{+,+}=\langle\sigma^{3}_{i},\sigma^{3}_{j}\rangle_{+,+}=\eta_{ij}\;,

(B.22a)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma^{a}_{i},\sigma^{b}_{j}\rangle_{-,+}=0\;,\qquad\qquad\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{1}_{j}\rangle_{-,+}=\langle\sigma^{2}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle_{-,+}=\eta_{ij}=-\langle\sigma^{3}_{i},\sigma^{3}_{j}\rangle_{-,+}\;,

(B.22b)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma^{a}_{i},\sigma^{b}_{j}\rangle_{+,-}=0\;,\qquad\qquad\langle\sigma^{1}_{i},\sigma^{1}_{j}\rangle_{+,-}=\langle\sigma^{3}_{i},\sigma^{3}_{j}\rangle_{+,-}=\eta_{ij}=-\langle\sigma^{2}_{i},\sigma^{2}_{j}\rangle_{+,-}\;.

(B.22c)

Note that one can naturally construct a Killing form
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle_{-,-}
where both the coupling for
\sigma_{i}^{2}
and
\sigma_{i}^{3}
have a
-1
factor in terms of the above pairings. In terms of these (four) choices of pairings, the action (
2.1
) takes the form

\displaystyle L^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{B}\wedge\mathcal{F}\rangle_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}-\alpha\langle\mathcal{B}\wedge\mathcal{B}\rangle_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}=L_{1}+\epsilon_{1}L_{2}+\epsilon_{2}L_{3},\qquad\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1,\qquad i=1,2.

(B.23)

As explained previously, each Lagrangian
L_{i}
then has its own diffeomorphism charge
\mathcal{D}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, with the standard Poisson algebra for the diffeomorphisms. Since we have now three charges, the space spanned by the diffeomorphism charges is now three-dimensional from the viewpoint of the full theory. As above, instead of considering the basis
\mathcal{D}_{i}
, we can consider the basis given in terms of the natural diffeomorphism associated to
L^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}
, which is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}(\xi)=\mathcal{D}^{1}(\xi)+\epsilon_{1}\mathcal{D}^{2}(\xi)+\epsilon_{2}\mathcal{D}^{3}(\xi).

(B.24)

Indeed, it is immediate to check that each diffeomorphism charge
\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}
is the natural one associated to the Lagrangian
L^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}
, while the other diffeomorphisms are the “dual” ones. The interpretation of these diffeomorphisms is a generalization of the previous case. In the sector 1, we always have a diffeomorphism along
\xi
, but in the sectors 2 and 3 we have diffeomorphisms directions
\epsilon_{1}\xi
and
\epsilon_{2}\xi
respectively. Indeed, looking at how
\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}
acts on the fields we find

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}}A_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A_{1},\qquad\qquad\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}}A_{2}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{1}\xi}A_{2},\qquad\qquad\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}}A_{3}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{2}\xi}A_{3},

(B.25a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}}B_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}B_{1},\qquad\qquad\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}}B_{2}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{1}\xi}B_{2},\qquad\qquad\delta_{\xi}^{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}}B_{3}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon_{2}\xi}B_{3}.

(B.25b)

These diffeomorphisms form of course a closed algebra, given by

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}(\xi),\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4}}(\zeta)\}=-\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{4}}([\xi,\zeta]),\quad\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1.

(B.26)

As we have shown in the main text, the existence of a self-dual basis is not necessary for the construction to work. It therefore begs the question of which conditions on the algebra are necessary for the existence of additional quadratic charges, and how many of them can be constructed. We are leaving this interesting question for future work.

1.
Introduction

Historically, rationality problem for Fano varieties is
one of the most important and difficult problems in the birational algebraic geometry.
It is mostly solved for smooth Fano threefolds (see
[
IP99
, Ch. 8-9]
and references therein) but it is still widely open for singular ones.
In this paper we discuss
rationality of Fano threefolds with terminal Gorenstein singularities.
The main reason for studying such objects is
that they arise
naturally in the minimal model program, in classification of finite subgroups of the space Cremona group
[
Pro21
]
, and in many other instances.

This work is a sequel to
[
Pro19
]
.
As in
[
Pro19
]
, we concentrate on the class of Fano threefolds of Picard number 1 and genus
g\geq 5

(a similar classification in the case
g\leq 4
is expected to be much longer, cf.
[
Kal12
]
).
For each value of genus
g=g(X)
and Fano index
\iota(X)
these varieties form an irreducible family.
This follows from the result
[
Nam97
]
and explicit description of smooth members
of the families
[
IP99
, §§ 3.3, 4.1, 5.1]
.

A natural invariant of a singular Fano threefold is the rank of the Weil divisor class group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
.
The case
\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Cl}(X)=1
was considered in
[
Pro19
]

(for the notation we refer to

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper we work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk

of characteristic
0
.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}=\oplus_{i=0}^{n}{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{i})
be a vector bundle of rank
n+1

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let

\mathbb{F}[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]:=\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{E}})

be its projectivization.
We denote by
M
and
F
the tautological divisor and a fiber of the projection

\mathbb{F}[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, respectively.

Typically, we consider Fano threefolds with terminal Gorenstein singularities
but when we say this, it means that the singularities are not worse than that,
in particular, the variety can be smooth.
As usual,
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
denotes the Picard group of a variety
X
and

\uprho(X)
is the rank of
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
.

\operatorname{Cl}(X)
denotes the Weil divisor class group of a normal variety
X
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with at worst Gorenstein terminal singularities. Then

{\mathrm{g}}(X):=\frac{1}{2}(-K_{X})^{3}+1
is its
genus
.
The
Fano index

X
is defined as follows

\iota(X):=\max\{t\mid-K_{X}=tA,\quad A\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)\}.

We say that
X
is a del Pezzo threefold if
\iota(X)=2
.
In this case, the integer

\mathrm{d}(X):=\frac{1}{8}(-K_{X})^{3}

is called the
degree
of
X
.

2.2 Proposition

(
[
Pro19
, Theorems 4.2 and 4.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with terminal Gorenstein singularities and
g:={\mathrm{g}}(X)\geq 5
.
Assume that
X
is not a del Pezzo threefold of degree
1
. Then the anticanonical class
-K_{X}
is very ample and defines an embedding
X=X_{2g-2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{g+1}
.
If furthermore
\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Cl}(X)=2
and
X
is not of type
1.2
nor
1.2
, then
X
is an intersection of quadrics.

2.2.1 Corollary
.

In the notation of Theorem
1.2
, assume that
X
is not a del Pezzo threefold of degree
1
. Then the contractions
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
coincide with anticanonical maps, that is, the divisor
-K_{X}
is very ample,
-K_{\hat{X}}=\tau^{*}(-K_{X})
and
-K_{\hat{X}^{\prime}}=\tau^{\prime*}(-K_{X})
.

We need some information about codimension two linear sections of the Grassmannian

\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}
.

2.3 Proposition
.

Let
G
be the Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)
and let
G\subset\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}\Bbbk^{5})=\mathbb{P}^{9}
be its Plücker
embedding. Let

W:=\mathbb{P}^{7}\cap G\subset\mathbb{P}^{7}
be a codimension two linear section.
Let
\mathscr{H}
be the pencil of hyperplane sections passing through
W
.
Suppose that
\dim\operatorname{Sing}(W)\leq 1
.
Then one of the following holds:

(i)

W
is smooth and any member
H\in\mathscr{H}

is smooth,

(ii)

\operatorname{Sing}(W)
is a line and there is exactly one singular member
H_{1}\in\mathscr{H}
and this
H_{1}
is a Schubert variety
\upsigma_{1.0}
,

(iii)

\operatorname{Sing}(W)
is a pair of skew lines and there are exactly two singular members
H_{1},\,H_{2}\in\mathscr{H}
and these
H_{i}
are Schubert varieties
\upsigma_{1.0}
.

Proof.

Any hyperplane
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}=\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}\Bbbk^{5})
is given by
a non-zero skew-symmetric bilinear form
q_{\Lambda}\in(\wedge^{2}\Bbbk^{5})^{\vee}

and the hyperplane section
G\cap\Lambda
is smooth if and only if
\operatorname{rk}(q_{\Lambda})=4
.
If
\operatorname{rk}(q_{\Lambda})=2
, then
G\cap\Lambda
is a Schubert variety
\upsigma_{1,0}
.
Thus for any singular hyperplane section
H=G\cap\Lambda
one can associate
a two dimensional linear subspace
\mathbb{P}(\ker(q_{\Lambda}))=\operatorname{Sing}(H)\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
From this, one can see that the Grassmannian
G=\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)
is self-dual, i.e. its dual variety
G^{\vee}\subset\mathbb{P}^{9\,\vee}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Gr}(3,5)\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)
. Therefore, the pencil
\mathscr{P}
of hyperplanes passing through
W
either meets
G^{\vee}
in at most
2
points or is contained in
G^{\vee}
.

Suppose that
W
is singular at
P_{1}\in W\subset G
.
Then some hyperplane
\Lambda_{1}\in\mathscr{P}

contains the tangent space
T_{P_{1},G}
.
Then the hyperplane section
H_{1}:=\Lambda_{1}\cap G
is a Schubert variety
\upsigma_{1,0}
, so it is singular
along a plane
\Pi_{1}
.
Let
\Lambda_{2}\in\mathscr{P}
be a hyperplane different from
\Lambda_{1}
.
Then
W=\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}\cap G
is singular along the line
l_{1}:=\Pi_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
.
Now assume that
W
is singular at
P_{2}\in W\setminus l_{1}
. As above, we may assume that
\Lambda_{2}\supset T_{P_{2},G}
and so
the hyperplane section
H_{2}:=\Lambda_{2}\cap G
is singular
along a plane
\Pi_{2}\ni P_{2}
. Then
W
is singular along the line
l_{2}:=\Pi_{2}\cap\Lambda_{1}
. Since
\dim\operatorname{Sing}(W)\leq 1
by our assumption, the above arguments show
that
\mathscr{P}\not\subset G^{\vee}
and
\operatorname{Sing}(W)=l_{1}\cup l_{2}
.
If
l_{1}\cap l_{2}\neq\varnothing
, then

\Pi_{1}\cap\Pi_{2}\neq\varnothing
but in this case
\Pi_{1}\subset H_{2}
. Hence,
\Pi_{1}\subset W
and
W
is singular along

\Pi_{1}
, a contradiction.
∎

4.
Nonrationality criteria

4.1.
Conic bundles

Let
V
be a threefold with terminal Gorenstein singularities.
A
conic bundle
is a flat contraction
f:V\to S
to a surface such that the anticanonical divisor

-K_{V}
is
f
-ample. In this situation any scheme fiber
V_{s}
,
s\in S
is a plane conic.
A conic bundle
f:V\to S
is said to be
standard
if both
V
and
S
are smooth and the relative Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(V/S)
has rank
1
.
The points
s\in S
such that the corresponding fiber
V_{s}
is singular
form a reduced divisor. It is called the
discriminant curve
.

4.1.1 Proposition

(
[
Isk87
, Theorem 1]
, see also
[
Pro18
, Proposition 5.6]
)
.

Let
f:V\to S
be a conic bundle over a projective rational surface
S

and let
\Delta\subset S
be the discriminant curve.
Suppose that there exists a contraction
\phi:S\to T
to a curve
such that for a fiber
S_{t}
over a closed point
t\in T
one has

\Delta\cdot S_{t}\leq 3
. Then
V
is rational.

The following result was proved in

[
Bea77
, Sect. 4]
and
[
Sho84
, Sect. 10]
.

4.1.2 Theorem
.

Let
f:V\to S
be a standard conic bundle over a projective rational surface
S
.
Assume that
S
is minimal, i. e. it does not contain
(-1)
-curves.
The variety
V
is rational if and only if its intermediate Jacobian
\operatorname{J}(V)
is
isomorphic as a principally polarized Abelian variety
to a sum of the Jacobians of nonsingular curves, i.e., if its Griffiths component

\operatorname{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(V)
is trivial.

4.1.3 Corollary

(
[
Bea77
, Sect. 4]
,
[
Sho84
, § 10]
)
.

Let
f:V\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a standard conic bundle and let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be
the discriminant curve.
If
\deg\Delta\geq 6
, then
V
is not rational.

4.1.4 Corollary
.

Let
V
be a variety with only terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities and let

be a Mori contraction.
Let

be

If the variety

is not rational, then

Moreover, if

Suppose that

Thus

4.2.

Let

A

\mathrm{c_{1}}

has a
\Bbbk(B)
-point.
If

is rational over

[
Man74

∎

Denote by

(
[

]

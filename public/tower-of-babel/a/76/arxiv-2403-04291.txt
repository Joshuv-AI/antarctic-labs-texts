Positivity preserving and mass conservative projection method for the Poisson-Nernst-Planck equation Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Fenghua Tong, Yongyong Cai

Abstract
We propose and analyze a novel approach to construct structure preserving approximations for the Poisson-Nernst-Planck equations, focusing on the positivity preserving and mass conservation properties. The strategy consists of a standard time marching step with a projection (or correction) step to satisfy the desired physical constraints (positivity and mass conservation). Based on the L 2 L^{2} projection, we construct a second order Crank-Nicolson type finite difference scheme, which is linear (exclude the very efficient L 2 L^{2} projection part), positivity preserving and mass conserving. Rigorous error estimates in L 2 L^{2} norm are established, which are both second order accurate in space and time. The other choice of projection, e.g. H 1 H^{1} projection, is discussed. Numerical examples are presented to verify the theoretical results and demonstrate the efficiency of the proposed method.



keywords

Poisson-Nernst-Planck equation, finite difference method, mass conservation, positivity preserving, projection methods, error estimates.

In this section, we introduce the finite difference scheme for solving the PNP system (

For the simplicity of presentation, we focus on the 2D case, i.e.

2.1
Finite difference discretization

Choosing
N_{x}
and
N_{y}
two positive integers, the domain
\Omega
is uniformly partitioned with mesh sizes
h_{x}=\frac{a_{r}-a_{l}}{N_{x}},h_{y}=\frac{b_{r}-b_{l}}{N_{y}}
, and the numerical domain
\Omega_{h}
is

\Omega_{h}=\left\{\left(x_{i},y_{j}\right)\mid x_{i}=a_{l}+ih_{x},y_{j}=b_{l}+jh_{y},0\leq i\leq N_{x},0\leq j\leq N_{y}\right\}.

We write the discrete grid function
u_{h}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}
(
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{h}
), which can be regarded as a vector consisting of
u_{i,j}=u_{h}(x_{i},y_{j})
(
(x_{i},y_{j})\in\Omega_{h}
). For the discrete periodic boundary conditions, we introduce the periodic discrete function space for
u_{h}
(or

X=\left\{u_{h}:\Omega_{h}\to\mathbb{R}|u_{0,j}=u_{N_{x},j},\;u_{i,0}=u_{i,N_{y}},\;\forall 0\leq i\leq N_{x},\,0\leq j\leq N_{y}\right\},

(8)

and the mean zero subspace

X_{0}=\left\{u_{h}\in X\bigg|\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}u_{i,j}=0\right\}.

(9)

For the simplicity of presentation, we assume
a_{r}-a_{l}=b_{r}-b_{l}
,
h_{x}=h_{y}=h,N_{x}=N_{y}=N
in the subsequent discussion, i.e. a uniform tensor grids on a 2D square.
For

, we denote the averaging operator
A_{x}(A_{y}):X\to X
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{x}u_{i,j}=\frac{u_{i,j}+u_{i-1,j}}{2},\quad\mathcal{A}_{y}u_{i,j}=\frac{u_{i,j}+u_{i,j-1}}{2},

and the forward/backward finite difference operators

where similar difference operators

can be introduced as

D^{-}_{y}u_{i,j}=\frac{u_{i,j}-u_{i,j-1}}{h},D^{+}_{y}u_{i,j}=\frac{u_{i,j+1}-u_{i,j}}{h}
.
To derive a second order finite difference discretization of the PNP system (
1
), for

,
we introduce the notations

\mathcal{A}v_{i,j}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{x}v_{i,j}&0\\
0&\mathcal{A}_{y}v_{i,j}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{i,j}=\begin{pmatrix}D^{-}_{x}\\
D^{-}_{y}\\
\end{pmatrix}u_{i,j},\quad\nabla_{h}^{+}u_{i,j}=\begin{pmatrix}D^{+}_{x}\\
D^{+}_{y}\\
\end{pmatrix}u_{i,j},

and the 2D discrete elliptic operator with variable coefficients can be written as

\displaystyle\nabla^{+}_{h}\cdot((\mathcal{A}v)_{i,j}\nabla^{-}_{h}u_{i,j})=\begin{pmatrix}D^{+}_{x}\quad D^{+}_{y}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{x}v_{i,j}&0\\
0&\mathcal{A}_{y}v_{i,j}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D^{-}_{x}\\
D^{-}_{y}\\
\end{pmatrix}u_{i,j},

while the standard 2D discrete Laplacian operator is

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}u_{i,j}:

\displaystyle=\nabla_{h}^{+}\cdot(\nabla^{-}_{h}u_{i,j})=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\left(u_{i+1,j}+u_{i-1,j}+u_{i,j+1}+u_{i,j-1}-4u_{i,j}\right).

For

, the discrete

inner products, the
L^{2}
norm and the

(

) norm are given by

\langle u_{h},v_{h}\rangle=h^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}u_{i,j}v_{i,j},\quad\|u_{h}\|^{2}=\langle u_{h},u_{h}\rangle,\quad\|u_{h}\|_{p}^{p}=h^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|u_{i,j}|^{p},

and the

\|u\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|u_{i,j}|
.
Without confusion, we shall use the same notation for the vector case, i.e.

and

(

The discrete semi-

norm and the

\|\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{h}\|^{2}=\langle\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{h},\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{h}\rangle,\quad\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}=\|u_{h}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{h}\|^{2}.

Lemma 1
.

(Summation by parts) For any grid functions

, the following identities are valid

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{h},\nabla_{h}^{-}v_{h}\rangle=-\langle\Delta_{h}u_{h},v_{h}\rangle,\quad\langle(\mathcal{A}w_{h})\nabla_{h}^{-}u_{h},\nabla_{h}^{-}v_{h}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{h}^{+}\cdot((\mathcal{A}w_{h})\nabla^{-}_{h}u_{h}),v_{h}\rangle.

2.2
Finite difference methods with positivity preservation and mass conservation

Now, we are ready to construct our finite difference schemes based on the prediction-correction strategy. Choose the time step size
\tau>0
, and the time steps are
t_{k}:=k\tau
for
k=0,1,\ldots
. Let
({p}^{k}_{h}(\mathbf{x}),{n}^{k}_{h}(\mathbf{x}),{\phi}^{k}_{h}(\mathbf{x}))\in X\times X\times X_{0}
(
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{h}
) be the numerical approximation of the exact solution
(p,n,\phi)
of PNP (
1
) on
\Omega_{h}
at time
t_{k}
. For any continuous periodic function
v(\mathbf{x})
on
\Omega
, we define the interpolation operator
I_{h}
on the numerical domain
\Omega_{h}
as

\displaystyle I_{h}v\in X\quad\text{with}\quad{I_{h}v}(x_{i},y_{j})=v(x_{i},y_{j}),\quad\forall\mathbf{x}_{i,j}=(x_{i},y_{j})\in\Omega_{h}.

We assume the initialization is compatible, i.e.
\langle p_{h}^{0},1\rangle=\langle n_{h}^{0},1\rangle
. For the initial data
(p_{0},n_{0})
given in (
2
), we assume
\min\left\{\langle I_{h}p_{0},1\rangle,\langle I_{h}n_{0},1\rangle\right\}>0
(valid for sufficiently small
h
) and set
M_{0}=\max\left\{\langle I_{h}p_{0},1\rangle,\langle I_{h}n_{0},1\rangle\right\}>0
,

p_{h}^{0}(x_{i},y_{j})=\varepsilon_{1}p_{0}(x_{i},y_{j}),\quad n_{h}^{0}(x_{i},y_{j})=\varepsilon_{2}n_{0}(x_{i},y_{j}),\quad-\Delta_{h}\phi_{h}^{0}=p_{h}^{0}-n_{h}^{0},

(10)

where
\varepsilon_{1}=M_{0}/\langle I_{h}p_{0},1\rangle
,
\varepsilon_{2}=M_{0}/\langle I_{h}n_{0},1\rangle
and
\phi_{h}^{0}\in X_{0}
is uniquely determined.

For
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, we follow the prediction-projection strategy as:

Step 1
: intermediate solutions
\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h},\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}\in X
are obtained from the Crank-Nicolson finite difference scheme (CNFD) with the Adams–Bashforth strategy for the nonlinear part:

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}-p^{k}_{h}}{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}(\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}+p^{k}_{h})+\nabla_{h}^{+}\cdot\left((\mathcal{A}p_{h}^{k+\frac{1}{2},*})\nabla_{h}^{-}\phi_{h}^{k+\frac{1}{2},*}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}-n^{k}_{h}}{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}(\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}+n^{k}_{h})-\nabla_{h}^{+}\cdot\left((\mathcal{A}n_{h}^{k+\frac{1}{2},*})\nabla_{h}^{-}\phi_{h}^{k+\frac{1}{2},*}\right),

(12)

where

\displaystyle p^{k+\frac{1}{2},*}_{h}=\frac{3}{2}p^{k}_{h}-\frac{1}{2}p^{k-1}_{h},\qquad n^{k+\frac{1}{2},*}_{h}=\frac{3}{2}n^{k}_{h}-\frac{1}{2}n^{k-1}_{h},\qquad{\phi}^{k+\frac{1}{2},*}_{h}=\frac{3}{2}{\phi}^{k}_{h}-\frac{1}{2}{\phi}^{k-1}_{h}.

Step 2
: the correction step.
In general, the intermediate numerical solutions
\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}
and
\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}
computed by the linear semi-implicit schemes fail the positivity preservation
[
22
]
.
Here, we treat the positivity and mass conservation at the discrete level as the constraints for the numerical solution at
t_{k+1}
as
(p_{h}^{k+1},n_{h}^{k+1})
with
\langle{p}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{p}^{0}_{h},1\rangle
,
\langle{n}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{n}^{0}_{h},1\rangle
and
p_{h}^{k+1},n_{h}^{k+1}\geq 0
.
A most natural way to obtain such
(p_{h}^{k+1},n_{h}^{k+1})
from
(\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h},\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h})
is to
project the nodal vectors
\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}
and
\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}
to the constrained manifold with mass conservation and positivity preservation.
We adopt the
L^{2}
projection here to enforce the positivity and mass conservation, which reads

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\min_{{p}^{k+1}_{h},{n}^{k+1}_{h}\in X}\quad\frac{1}{2}\left(\|{p}^{k+1}_{h}-\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}\|^{2}+\|{n}^{k+1}_{h}-\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}\|^{2}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r@{\quad}l@{}l@{\quad}l}\qquad\text{s.t.}&{p}^{k+1}_{h}\geq 0,{n}^{k+1}_{h}\geq 0,\,\langle{p}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{p}^{0}_{h},1\rangle,\quad\langle{n}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{n}^{0}_{h},1\rangle.\end{array}

This is a convex minimization problem and can be solved efficiently. In particular, a simple semi-smooth Newton solver through the following Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions is available (see Appendix):

\displaystyle{p}^{k+1}_{h}=\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}+\lambda^{k+1}_{h}-\xi^{k+1},\quad{n}^{k+1}_{h}=\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}+\eta^{k+1}_{h}-\gamma^{k+1},

(14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{k+1}{p}^{k+1}_{h}=0,\quad\eta_{h}^{k+1}{n}^{k+1}_{h}=0,\quad\lambda_{h}^{k+1}\geq 0,\quad\eta_{h}^{k+1}\geq 0,

(15)

\displaystyle\quad\langle{p}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{p}^{0}_{h},1\rangle,\quad\langle{n}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{n}^{0}_{h},1\rangle,

(16)

where
\xi^{k+1},\gamma^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}
are the Lagrange multipliers for the mass conservation, and
\lambda^{k+1}_{h},\eta^{k+1}_{h}\in X
are the Lagrange multipliers for the positivity preservation. The idea is to use the semi-smooth Newton method for solving the equations of the Lagrange multipliers
\xi^{k+1}
and
\gamma^{k+1}
, where
p_{h}^{k+1}
and
n_{h}^{k+1}
can be obtained from (
51
)-(
52
). The detail is shown in Appendix.

Step 3
: Update
\phi_{h}^{k+1}\in X_{0}
by solving

-\Delta_{h}\phi^{k+1}_{h}=p^{k+1}_{h}-n^{k+1}_{h}.

(17)

By initialization and the mass conservation property, we have
p^{k+1}_{h}-n^{k+1}_{h}\in X_{0}
and
\phi_{h}^{k+1}\in X_{0}
can be uniquely determined.

Now, (
11
)-(

) complete the

According to
lemma

4
and the KKT conditions, we find both right-hand-sides in the above equations are non-positive, and the conclusions in the lemma follow.

3.1
Main results

Let

Based on the theoretical results, we make the following assumptions,

. The following error bounds can be established.

Theorem 6
.

Under the Assumption (A), let
(p_{h}^{k},n_{h}^{k},\phi_{h}^{k})\in X\times X\times X_{0}
be obtained by CNFDP (
11
)-(
17
) with (
10
) and (
18

\tau_{0},h_{0}>0

0<\tau<\tau_{0}
and
0<h<h_{0}
satisfying a mild CFL type condition
\tau\leq C_{0}h
(
C_{0}>0

\displaystyle\|I_{h}p(t_{k})-p_{h}^{k}\|+\|I_{h}n(t_{k})-n_{h}^{k}\|+\|\nabla_{h}^{-}(I_{h}\phi(t_{k})-\phi_{h}^{k})\|\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{2}),\,0\leq k\leq T/\tau,

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
h
,
\tau
and
k
.

From initialization (
10
),
\langle p_{h}^{0},1\rangle=\langle n_{h}^{0},1\rangle=\max\{\langle I_{h}p_{0},1\rangle,\langle I_{h}n_{0},1\rangle\}=M_{0}
. It is easy to see that
I_{h}p(t)
and
I_{h}n(t)
do not preserve the discrete mass
M_{0}
in general. For the purpose of numerical analysis, we introduce the ‘biased’ error functions

e_{p}^{k},e_{n}^{k},\tilde{e}_{p}^{k},\tilde{e}_{n}^{k}\in X,e_{\phi}^{k}\in X_{0}
(
k\geq 0
):

\displaystyle e_{p}^{k}=p_{h}(t_{k})-p^{k}_{h},\quad\tilde{e}_{p}^{k}=p_{h}(t_{k})-\tilde{p}^{k}_{h},\quad e_{n}^{k}=n_{h}(t_{k})-n^{k}_{h},

(20)

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{n}^{k}=n_{h}(t_{k})-\tilde{n}^{k}_{h},\quad e_{\phi}^{k}=\phi_{h}(t_{k})-\phi^{k}_{h},

where
\tilde{p}_{h}^{0}=p_{h}^{0}
,
\tilde{n}_{h}^{0}=n_{h}^{0}
and

p_{h}(t_{k})=(1+\epsilon_{p}^{k})I_{h}p(t_{k}),\quad n_{h}(t_{k})=(1+\epsilon_{n}^{k})I_{h}n(t_{k}),\quad\phi_{h}(t_{k})=I_{h}\phi(t_{k})-\epsilon_{\phi}^{k},

(21)

with

\epsilon_{p}^{k}=\frac{M_{0}-\langle I_{h}p(t_{k}),1\rangle}{\langle I_{h}p(t_{k}),1\rangle},\quad\epsilon_{n}^{k}=\frac{M_{0}-\langle I_{h}n(t_{k}),1\rangle}{\langle I_{h}n(t_{k}),1\rangle},\quad\epsilon_{\phi}^{k}=\frac{1}{N^{2}h^{2}}\langle I_{h}\phi(t_{k}),1\rangle.

(22)

It is straightforward to check
\langle p_{h}(t_{k}),1\rangle=\langle n_{h}(t_{k}),1\rangle=M_{0}
and
\phi_{h}(t_{k})\in X_{0}
, which immediately implies
\langle e_{p}^{k},1\rangle=\langle e_{n}^{k},1\rangle=\langle\tilde{e}_{p}^{k},1\rangle=\langle\tilde{e}_{n}^{k},1\rangle=0
(
k\geq 0
). Under the assumption (A), for sufficiently small
h
, we have
p_{h}(t_{k}),n_{h}(t_{k})\geq 0
and

|\epsilon_{p}^{k}|+|\epsilon_{n}^{k}|+|\epsilon_{\phi}^{k}|\leq Ch^{2},\quad|\epsilon_{p}^{k+1}-\epsilon_{p}^{k}|+|\epsilon_{n}^{k+1}-\epsilon_{n}^{k}|\leq C\tau h^{2},\quad 0\leq k\leq T/\tau,

(23)

where
C>0
depends on
(p(t),n(t),\phi(t))
. The first part in (
23
) is a direct consequence of the second order trapezoidal rule (it is indeed of spectral accuracy for periodic functions). For the second part in (
23
), using the error formula for the trapezoidal rule, we can get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(\mathbf{x},t_{k})\,d\mathbf{x}-\langle I_{h}p(t_{k}),1\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{y_{0}}^{y_{N}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\frac{(x_{i}-x)(x_{i-1}-x)}{2}\partial_{xx}p(x,y,t_{k})\,dxdy

\displaystyle+h\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{y_{j-1}}^{y_{j}}\frac{(y_{j}-y)(y_{j-1}-y)}{2}\partial_{yy}p(x_{i},y,t_{k})\,dy.

Since

is a constant in time, we have from the above error formula that
\epsilon_{p}^{k+1}-\epsilon_{p}^{k}=M_{0}\frac{\langle I_{h}p(t_{k}),1\rangle-\langle I_{h}p(t_{k+1}),1\rangle}{\langle I_{h}p(t_{k}),1\rangle\,\langle I_{h}p(t_{k+1}),1\rangle}=O(\tau h^{2}(\partial_{xxt}p+\partial_{yyt}p))
and we omit the details here for brevity.

R_{p}^{k},R_{n}^{k},R_{\phi}^{k}\in X
(
k\geq 0

\displaystyle-\Delta_{h}\phi_{h}(t_{k})-p_{h}(t_{k})+n_{h}(t_{k}),\quad k\geq 0,

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{p_{h}(\tau)-p_{h}(0)}{\tau}-\Delta_{h}p_{h}(\tau)-\nabla_{h}^{+}\cdot((\mathcal{A}p_{h}(0))\nabla_{h}^{-}\phi_{h}(0)),

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{n_{h}(\tau)-n_{h}(0)}{\tau}-\Delta_{h}n_{h}(\tau)+\nabla_{h}^{+}\cdot((\mathcal{A}n_{h}(0))\nabla_{h}^{-}\phi_{h}(0)),

(26)

and for

\displaystyle R_{p}^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{h}(t_{k+1})-p_{h}(t_{k})}{\tau}-\frac{\Delta_{h}\left(p_{h}(t_{k+1})+p_{h}(t_{k})\right)}{2}-\nabla^{+}_{h}\cdot\left((\mathcal{A}p_{h}^{t_{k},*})\nabla^{-}_{h}\phi_{h}^{t_{k},*}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\|\tilde{e}_{n}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|e_{n}^{k}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla_{h}^{-}(\tilde{e}_{n}^{k+1}+e_{n}^{k})\|^{2}

(43)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(\|R_{n}^{k}\|^{2}+\sum\limits_{l=k,k-1}(\|e_{p}^{l}\|^{2}+\|e_{n}^{l}\|^{2}+\|R_{\phi}^{l}\|^{2}).

Denoting
S^{k}=\|\tilde{e}_{p}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{e}_{n}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{p}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{n}^{k+1}\|^{2}
(
k\geq 1
), in view of (
42
) and (
43
), recalling Lemma
8
where
\|e_{p}^{l}\|^{2}\leq\|\tilde{e}_{p}^{l}\|^{2}
and
\|e_{n}^{l}\|^{2}\leq\|\tilde{e}_{n}^{l}\|^{2}
(
l=k,k-1
), we have for
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(S^{k+1}-S^{k}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}^{-}(\tilde{e}_{p}^{k+1}+e_{p}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}^{-}(\tilde{e}_{n}^{k+1}+e_{n}^{k})\|^{2}

(44)

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{3}(\|R_{p}^{k}\|^{2}+\|R_{n}^{k}\|^{2}+\sum\limits_{l=k,k-1}(S^{l}+\|R_{\phi}^{l}\|^{2}).

Summing (
44
) together for
1,2,\cdots,k
, using the local error in Lemma
7
and the estimates (
37
) at the first step, for
1\leq k\leq m
, we arrive at

\displaystyle S^{k+1}+\frac{\tau}{2}\sum^{k-1}_{l=1}\left(\|\nabla_{h}^{-}(\tilde{e}_{p}^{l+1}+e_{p}^{l})\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}^{-}(\tilde{e}_{n}^{l+1}+e_{n}^{l})\|^{2}\right)

(45)

\displaystyle\leq S^{1}+8C_{3}\tau\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}(\|R_{p}^{k}\|^{2}+\|R_{n}^{l}\|^{2}+\|R_{\phi}^{l}\|^{2}+S^{l})+4\tau C_{3}\|R_{\phi}^{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8C_{3}\tau\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}S^{l}+C_{4}(\tau^{2}+h^{2})^{2},

where
C_{4}>0
is a constant independent of
\tau
,
h
and
k
.
Discrete Gronwall inequality
[
16
]
yields for some
0<\tau<\tau_{0}
(
\tau_{0}>0
),

S^{k+1}\leq\exp(8C_{3}(k+1)\tau)C_{4}(\tau^{2}+h^{2})^{2},\quad 1\leq k\leq m.

From (
39
), we have
\|\nabla_{h}^{-}e_{\phi}^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq C_{5}(S^{k+1}+h^{4})
(
C_{5}>0
) and

S^{k+1}+\|\nabla_{h}^{-}e_{\phi}^{k+1}\|^{2}\leq(C_{5}+1)(C_{4}+1)\exp(8C_{3}(k+1)\tau)C_{4}(\tau^{2}+h^{2})^{2},\quad 1\leq k\leq m,

and (
33
) holds at
k=m+1
, if we set
C=\max\{8C_{3},(C_{5}+1)(C_{4}+1),C_{4}\}
. It is easy to check that the constant
C
is independent of
h
,
\tau
and
k
. We are left with (

) for

, which can be derived similarly as the

case by the inverse inequality. More precisely, for

, (

) implies for
\tau\leq C_{0}h
with

(

)

\displaystyle\|e^{m+1}_{p}\|_{\infty}+\|e^{m+1}_{n}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{h}^{-}e^{m+1}_{\phi}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h^{-1}(\|e^{m+1}_{p}\|+\|e^{m+1}_{n}\|+\|\nabla_{h}^{-}e_{\phi}^{m+1}\|)\leq 3\sqrt{Ce^{CT}}(h^{-1}\tau^{2}+h)\leq 1,

Triangle inequality implies (
32
) at
k=m+1
. Choosing

, we then complete the induction process for proving Theorem

.

4
Different projections

Apart from the
L^{2}
projection employed in CNFDP (
11
)-(
17
), the correction step (
13
) can be performed in other suitable norms, e.g. the discrete
H_{1}
norm. Here, we briefly discuss the
H^{1}
projection strategy, which would easily yield optimal convergence in
H^{1}
norm. In detail, the projection step (
13
) is replaced by the following
H^{1}
projection: given
\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1},\tilde{n}_{h}^{k+1}\in X
, find
p_{h}^{k+1},n_{h}^{k+1}\in X
such that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\min_{{p}^{k+1}_{h},{n}^{k+1}_{h}}\quad\frac{1}{2}\left(\|{p}^{k+1}_{h}-\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}\|_{H_{1}}^{2}+\|{n}^{k+1}_{h}-\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}\|_{H_{1}}^{2}\right)

(46)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{r@{\quad}l@{}l@{\quad}l}\qquad\text{s.t.}&{p}^{k+1}_{h}\geq 0,{n}^{k+1}_{h}\geq 0,\,\langle{p}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{p}^{0}_{h},1\rangle,\,\langle{n}^{k+1}_{h},1\rangle=\langle{n}^{0}_{h},1\rangle.\end{array}

The convex minimization problem (
46
) can be similarly solved as (
13
).
The KKT conditions for (
46
) become

\displaystyle(I-\Delta_{h}){p}^{k+1}_{h}=(I-\Delta_{h})\tilde{p}^{k+1}_{h}+\lambda^{k+1}_{h}-\xi^{k+1},

(47)

\displaystyle(I-\Delta_{h}){n}^{k+1}_{h}=(I-\Delta_{h})\tilde{n}^{k+1}_{h}+\eta^{k+1}_{h}-\gamma^{k+1},

where the Lagrange multiplies
\xi^{k+1},\gamma^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda^{k+1}_{h},\eta^{k+1}_{h}\in X
satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{k+1}{p}^{k+1}_{h}=0,\quad\eta_{h}^{k+1}{n}^{k+1}_{h}=0,\quad\lambda_{h}^{k+1}\geq 0,\quad\eta_{h}^{k+1}\geq 0,

(48)

and
I
is the identity operator.
In particular, an efficient approach based on the semi-smooth Newton method to solve (
47
)-(
48
) is available (see Appendix). Extensive numerical results (see Section 5) indicate that the optimization problem (
46
) can be solved by the semi-smooth Newton method within several Newton iterations (serval matrix-vector products are required per iteration step). Therefore, the above
H^{1}
projection approach is less efficient than the
L^{2}
projection in terms of the computational complexity.

We shall denote the corresponding numerical scheme (
10
)-(
12
) with the
H^{1}
correction step (
46
)-(
48
) and the electric potential step (
17
) as
CNFDP2
.
Similar to CNFDP with the
L^{2}
projection, we also have the following lemmas regarding CNFDP2 with the
H^{1}
projection.

For CNFDP2 (

)-(

)-(

) and(

\displaystyle\xi^{k+1}\geq 0,\gamma^{k+1}\geq 0,\quad k\geq 0.

By testing both sides of (
47
) by
p_{h}^{k+1}
and

respectively, we can obtain the following estimates from the KKT conditions (

For the

\|p_{h}^{k+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|p_{h}^{k+1}-\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\|\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},\quad\|n_{h}^{k+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|n_{h}^{k+1}-\tilde{n}_{h}^{k+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\|\tilde{n}_{h}^{k+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

(49)

6
Conclusion

In this work, we developed efficient numerical schemes CNFDP and CNFDP2 to simulate the PNP equation (
1
), with positivity preserving and mass conservation at the discrete level. The key idea is to solve a minimization problem to project (correct) the intermediate solution to satisfy the desired physically constraints. By projection idea, either in
L^{2}
norm (CNFDP) or
H^{1}
norm (CNFDP2), the positivity and mass conservation of the numerical solutions can be guaranteed. For CNFDP, error estimates were shown to be at
O(\tau^{2}+h^{2})
for the ion densities in
L^{2}
norm and for the electric potential in
H^{1}
norm. The proposed method with projection technique is a linearized
scheme, which can be implemented very efficiently, with negligible computational cost in solving a nonlinear algebraic system. The method can be directly generalized to the case of multi-ions PNP system without any difficulty. Extension of the techniques and ideas to other more complex problems or higher order methods will be subject to future investigation.

In this Appendix, we briefly describe the process about how to determine the Lagrange multipliers
\lambda^{k+1}_{h}(\mathbf{x}),\eta^{k+1}_{h}(\mathbf{x})\in X
and
\xi^{k+1},\gamma^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}
.

L^{2}
projection (
15
)
. From the complementary relaxation conditions (
15
), we can obtain numerical solutions
p_{h}^{k+1}
and
n_{h}^{k+1}
expressed as

(p_{h}^{k+1},\lambda_{h}^{k+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}-\xi^{k+1},0\right],&\text{ if }\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}-\xi^{k+1}>0,\\
\left[0,-(\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}-\xi^{k+1})\right],&\text{ otherwise},\end{array}\right.

(51)

and

(n_{h}^{k+1},\eta_{h}^{k+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\tilde{n}_{h}^{k+1}-\gamma^{k+1},0\right],&\text{ if }\tilde{n}_{h}^{k+1}-\gamma^{k+1}>0,\\
\left[0,-(\tilde{n}_{h}^{k+1}-\gamma^{k+1})\right],&\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(52)

where the Lagrange multipliers
\xi^{k+1},\gamma^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}
are determined by the mass constraints, e.g. for
\xi^{k+1}
,

h^{2}\sum_{0<\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x})-\xi^{k+1}}\left(\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x})-\xi^{k+1}\right)=\langle{p}^{0}_{h},1\rangle.

Hence
\xi^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}
is the solution to the nonlinear algebraic equation

F(\xi^{k+1})=h^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x}_{i,j})-\xi^{k+1}\right)^{+}-\langle{p}^{0}_{h},1\rangle=0,

(53)

where
f^{+}=\max\{f,0\}
for
f\in\mathbb{R}
.
Semi-smooth Newton methods
[
21
,
10
]
can be employed to solve (
53
), i.e. for some initial guess
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, find the root of
F(\xi)=0
by updating

\xi_{s+1}=\xi_{s}-V^{-1}(\xi_{s})F\left(\xi_{s}\right),\quad s=0,1,\cdots,

(54)

where
V(\xi_{s})
is a generalized derivate in semi-smooth Newtown methods as

V(\xi_{s})=-h^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\text{sgn}((\tilde{p}_{h}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x}_{i,j})-\xi_{s})^{+}),

and
\text{sgn}(\cdot)
is the sign function with

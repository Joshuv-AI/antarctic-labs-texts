Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor and Matsuki duality
Tsao-Hsien Chen

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected complex reductive group and let K K be a symmetric subgroup of G G . We prove a formula for the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor for the dg-category Shv ⁡ ( K \ X ) \operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X) of K K -equivariant sheaves on the flag manifold X X of G G in terms of the Matsuki duality functor [ MUV ] . As byproducts, we obtain a description of the Serre functor for Shv ⁡ ( K \ X ) \operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X) , generalizing the one in [ BBM ] in the case of category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , and a formula for the Deligne-Lusztig duality for ( 𝔤 , K ) ({\mathfrak{g}},K) -modules.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

For any compactly generated dg-category
{\mathcal{C}}
, Drinfeld-Gaitsgory
[
G
]

introduced a certain
canonical endofunctor

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{\mathcal{C}}:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}

to be called the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor (in
loc. cit.
they called it the pseudo-identity functor)
with many remarkable properties.
Let
G
be a connected complex reductive group and let

K
be a symmetric subgroup of
G
.
In this paper, we study the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor for
the dg-category
{\mathcal{C}}=\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
of

\lambda
-twisted

K
-equivariant
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible sheaves on the
flag manifold
X
of
G
.
Here
\lambda
is a character of Lie algebra of the abstract Cartan group of
G
. In this geometric setting the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}}:\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}\to\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}

has a concrete description: it
is the functor given by the kernel

(\Delta_{K\backslash X})_{!}({\mathbb{C}}_{K\backslash X})\in\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X\times K\backslash X)_{-\lambda,\lambda}

where
{\mathbb{C}}_{K\backslash X}
is the constant sheaf
on the quotient stack
K\backslash X
and

\Delta_{K\backslash X}:K\backslash X\to K\backslash X\times K\backslash X

is the diagonal map (see Section
3
).

The main result of this paper is
a formula for the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor for
\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
in terms of the so called Matsuki functors in
[
MUV
]
.
To explain the formula, we first recall some notations and results in Lie theory.
Let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be the real form of
G
corresponding to

K
under the
Cartan bijection.
We write
K_{\mathbb{R}}=K\cap G_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The groups
K
and
G_{\mathbb{R}}
act naturally on
X
with finitely many orbits and the celebrated Matsuki correspondence
says that there is a natural bijection between the

K
-orbits and
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-orbits:

(1.1)

|K\backslash X|\simeq|G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X|.

We have the following functors, to be called the Matsuki functors:

\Upsilon^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{!}:=\operatorname{Av}_{!}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}/K_{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\operatorname{oblv}_{K/K_{\mathbb{R}}}:\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}

\Upsilon^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{*}:=\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}/K_{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\operatorname{oblv}_{K/K_{\mathbb{R}}}:\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}

\Upsilon^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}_{!}:=\operatorname{Av}_{!}^{K/K_{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\operatorname{oblv}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}/K_{\mathbb{R}}}:\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}

\Upsilon^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}_{*}:=\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{K/K_{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\operatorname{oblv}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}/K_{\mathbb{R}}}:\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}.

Here for any Lie group
H
and a Lie subgroup
H^{\prime}\subset H
, the functor

\operatorname{oblv}_{H/H^{\prime}}:\operatorname{Shv}(H\backslash X)_{\lambda}\to\operatorname{Shv}(H^{\prime}\backslash X)_{\lambda}
is the natural forgetful functor
and
\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{H/H^{\prime}}
(resp.
\operatorname{Av}_{!}^{H/H^{\prime}}
) is its right (resp. left) adjoint.
In
[
MUV
]
, the authors updated the Matsuki correspondence (
1.1
) to the level of sheaves:

(1.2)

The Matsuki functor

\Upsilon^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{*}
(resp.
\Upsilon_{!}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}
)
is an equivalence with inverse given by

\Upsilon_{!}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}
(resp.
\Upsilon_{*}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}
).

The following theorem is the main result of the note.
Write
\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{K\backslash X}=\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}}
for the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor for

\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
.
For simplicity, we assume
K
is connected.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of functors
\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
:

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{K\backslash X}\simeq\Upsilon_{!}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}\circ\Upsilon^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{!}[-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{\mathbb{R}})].

It was shown in
[
GY
, Theorem 2.1.5]
that

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{K\backslash X}
is
isomorphic to the inverse
of the
Serre functor
\operatorname{Se}_{K\backslash X}:\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
.
Thus, in view of (
1.2
), we obtain

Corollary 1.2

(Theorem
6.4
)
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of functors
\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}\rightarrow\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
:

\operatorname{Se}_{K\backslash X}\simeq\Upsilon_{*}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}\circ\Upsilon^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{*}[\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(X)-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{\mathbb{R}})].

In fact, Theorem
6.3
and Theorem
6.4

are in a more general setting
of
Matsuki datum
, see Section
4
.

Remark 1.1
.

If
K
(equivalently
K_{\mathbb{R}}
or
G_{\mathbb{R}}
) is disconnected, one need to modify the formulas above for
\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{K\backslash X}
and

\operatorname{Se}_{K\backslash X}
by
inserting the functor
(-)\otimes\ell_{K\backslash X}

of tensoring with a certain rank one local system on

K\backslash X
coming from the orientation sheaf
\operatorname{or}_{K_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X}
of
K_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X
, see Remark
5.2
.
When
K
is connected,
\operatorname{or}_{K_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X}
is trivial
and we do not need such modification, see Remark
6.1
.

Remark 1.2
.

The formulas above for the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor and the Serre functor
generalize the one in

[
CGY
, Theorem 3.4.2]
and
[
BBM
, Proposition 2.5]

to the setting of real groups.
Indeed, let
N
be the
unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of
G

and let
N^{-}
be its opposite unipotent radical.
In
loc. cit.
the authors showed that the
Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor
\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{N\backslash X}

and the Serre functor
\operatorname{Se}_{N\backslash X}
for the category
\operatorname{Shv}(N\backslash X)

of sheaves on the stack
N\backslash X
(equivalently, the category
\mathcal{O}
) are isomorphic to

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{N\backslash X}\simeq\Upsilon_{!}^{N^{-}\to N}\circ\Upsilon_{!}^{N\to N^{-}}[-2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)]\ \ \ \ \operatorname{Se}_{N\backslash X}\simeq\Upsilon_{*}^{N^{-}\to N}\circ\Upsilon_{*}^{N\to N^{-}}[2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)]

where the functors
\Upsilon_{!}^{N\to N^{-}},\Upsilon_{*}^{N\to N^{-}}:\operatorname{Shv}(N\backslash X)\to\operatorname{Shv}(N^{-}\backslash X)

are the so called long-intertwining functors.
Our results
suggest that the Matsuki functors are “real group” analog
of the long-intertwining functors.

1.2.
A formula for the Deligne-Lusztig duality for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules

In
[
GY
]
, Gaitsgory-Yom Din
introduced an analog of the
Deligne-Lusztig duality
for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules
as the composition of the
canonical duality
\mathbb{D}^{\text{can}}
and the contragrediant duality
\mathbb{D}^{\text{contr}}
:

(1.3)

\mathbb{D}^{\text{can}}\circ\mathbb{D}^{\text{contr}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{-mod}^{K}_{\chi}\to{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{-mod}^{K}_{\chi}.

The main result in
loc.cit.
says that
the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{-mod}^{K}_{\chi}}
for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules
is isomorphic to the Deligne-Lusztig functor

(1.4)

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{-mod}^{K}_{\chi}}\simeq\mathbb{D}^{\text{can}}\circ\mathbb{D}^{\text{contr}}

Since the category
{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{-mod}^{K}_{\chi}
is equivalent to
\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}

via the localization theorem and the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence (or rather the dg-category version, see, e.g.,
[
P
]
), Theorem
1.1
gives
a formula for the Deligne-Lusztig functor
for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules in terms of Matsuki functors:

Corollary 1.3
.

We have an isomorphism of functors on
{\mathfrak{g}}\operatorname{-mod}^{K}_{\chi}\simeq\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
:

\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname{can}}\circ\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname{contr}}\simeq\Upsilon_{!}^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}\circ\Upsilon^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}_{!}[-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{\mathbb{R}})]

Remark 1.3
.

It is interesting to note that the formula above for the Deligne-Lusztig duality
for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules
is not algebraic but only
real analytic
; as it involves the Matsuki functors
going from
\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
to
\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}
.
A similar phenomenon also appeared in the work of Barlet-Kashiwara
[
BK
]
, where the authors
described the functor on
\operatorname{Shv}(K\backslash X)_{\lambda}
which corresponds
to the contragradient duality
\mathbb{D}^{\text{contr}}
for
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules and they
also observed that the functor is not algebraic but only real analytic.
It will be interesting to compare the formula obtained here and the one in
loc. cit.
.

Remark 1.4
.

It would be nice to have an analog of Corollary
1.3

for
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-representations.
Note that we have similar formulas in the
real setting:
the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory and
the Serre functor for
\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}

are isomorphic to:

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X}\simeq\Upsilon_{!}^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\Upsilon^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}_{!}[-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{\mathbb{R}})]

\operatorname{Se}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X}\simeq\Upsilon_{*}^{K\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\Upsilon^{G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow K}_{*}[\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(X)-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{\mathbb{R}})].

In
[
KSd
]
, Kashiwara-Schmid constructed a functor from

\operatorname{Shv}(G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X)_{\lambda}
to (certain) derived category of

G_{\mathbb{R}}
-representations, known as Kashiwara-Schmid
localization. Similar to the case of
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules, we expect that the Kashiwara-Schmid
localization intertwines the
Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X}

with
the composition
\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname{coh}}\circ\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname{contr}}

where

\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname{contr}}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\operatorname{coh}}
are the
the contragredient duality
and (some version) of cohomological duality for

G_{\mathbb{R}}
-representations respectively.
At the level of virtual
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-representations (up to infinitesimal equivalence),
this follows from the
fact that the Matsuki functor intertwines the Kashiwara-Schmid
localization with the Beilinson-Bernstein localization (see, e.g.,
[
SV
, Section 2]
).

1.3.
Further directions

1.3.1.
Theory of tilting perverse sheaves

In
[
BBM
]
, the authors use
the theory of tilting perverse sheaves
on
N\backslash X
to derive the formula for Serre functor
for category
\mathcal{O}
. It would be nice if one can do the same for
(g,K)
-modules:
study tilting perverse sheaves on
K\backslash X
or
G_{\mathbb{R}}\backslash X
and explore its connection with the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor and the Serre functor.

1.3.2.
Affine Matsuki duality

In
[
CN
]
, Nadler and the author prove a affine version of the Matsuki duality, to be called the affine Matsuki duality, which relates the category
of sheaves on the moduli stack
\operatorname{Bun}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}}
of
real bundles over real projective line with the category
spherical sheaves on the loop space
X((t))
of the symmetric variety
X=G/K
.
The results of the paper suggest that there is a close relationship between
the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor for those categories
and the affine Matsuki duality. Note that
\operatorname{Bun}_{G_{\mathbb{R}}}

and
X((t))
are the main players for
the geometric Langlands for real groups (proposed by Ben-Zvi and Nadler)
and derived Satake equivalence for symmetric varieties (proposed by
Ben-Zvi, Venkatesh, and Sakellaridis), and we expect that the
affine Matsuki duality (and its relation with the Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor)
would help us to understand some “duality” phenomena in both subjects.

2.
constructible sheaves on a semi-analytic stack

2.1.

We will be working will
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear dg-categories (see
[
DG
, Section 0.6]
for a concise summary of the theory of DG categories). Unless specified otherwise, all dg-categories will be assume cocomplete, i.e., contains all small colimits,
and all functors between dg-categories will be assumed continous, i.e., preserves all small colimits.

Recall that a subset
S
of a
real analytic manifold
M

is called semi-analytic if
any point
s\in S
has a open neighbourhood
U

such that the intersection
S\cap U
is a finite union of sets of the form

\{s\in U|f_{1}(s)=\cdot\cdot\cdot=f_{r}(s)=0,g_{1}(s)>0,...,g_{l}(s)>0\},

where the
f_{i}
and
g_{j}
are real analytic functions on

U
.
A map
f:S\to S^{\prime}
between two semi-analytic sets is called semi-analytic if
it is continuous and its graph is a semi-analytic set.

We collect some facts about constructible sheaves on a semi-analytic stack following
[
AGKRRV1
, Appendix C]
.
1
1

1

In
loc. cit.
, the authors work with constructible sheaves on a
complex semi-analytic stack but the discussion works for real semi-analytic stacks.

For any semi-analytic set
S
,
we define
\operatorname{Shv}(S)=\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{Shv}(S)^{\operatorname{constr}})
to be the ind-completion
of the
bounded dg-category
\operatorname{Shv}(S)^{\operatorname{constr}}
of
{\mathbb{C}}
-constructible sheaves on

S
. For any semi-analytic stack
{\mathcal{S}}

we define
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}}):=\underset{S}{\operatorname{lim}}\ \operatorname{Shv}(S)

where the index category is that of semi-analytic sets equipped with a semi-analytic map
to
{\mathcal{S}}
, and the transition functors are given by
!
-pullback.
Since we are in the constructible context
!
-pullback admits left adjoint, given by

!
-pushforward, and it follows that

\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})=\underset{S}{\operatorname{colim}}\operatorname{Shv}(S)
. In particular,

\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})
is compactly generated.
We let
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{c}
be the full subcategory of compact objects
and
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{\text{constr}}\subset\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})
be the full subcategory
of objects that pullback to an object of
\operatorname{Shv}(S)^{\operatorname{constr}}
for any
S
mapping to
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Note that we have natural inclusion
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{c}\subset\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{\text{constr}}

but the inclusion is in general
not an equality. For example, the constant sheaf
{\mathbb{C}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}\in\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{\text{constr}}

for the classifying stack
{\mathcal{S}}=B({\operatorname{GL}}_{1}({\mathbb{C}}))
is not compact.

Let
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
be a map between semi-analytic stacks.
We have the usual six functor formalism

f_{*}
,
f^{!}
,
f_{*}
,
f_{!}
,
\otimes
,
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
.
For any
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\in\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})
we denote by

\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes^{!}\mathcal{F}_{2}:=\Delta^{!}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{2})
,
where
\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
be the diagonal map.

2.2.
Renormalized direct image

The usual direct image functor

f_{*}:\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
is in general not continuous
(see, e.g.,
[
DG
, Example 7.1.4]
).
We define the
renormalized
direct image

(2.1)

f_{*,\text{ren}}:\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})

to be the unique colimit-preserving functor such that

f_{*,\text{ren}}=f_{*}
on
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{c}
.

Following
[
AGKRRV1
, Appendix C]
, a semi-analytic stack
{\mathcal{S}}
is called

duality-adapted
if
the Verdier duality functor

\mathbb{D}:(\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{\text{constr}})^{\operatorname{op}}\simeq\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{\text{constr}}

sends
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{c}
to
\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})^{c}
.
In this paper we are mainly interested in
semi-analytic stacks
{\mathcal{S}}
of the form

{\mathcal{S}}=H\backslash S
where
H
is Lie group acting semi-analytically on a semi-analytic set

S
. According to
[
AGKRRV1
, Theorem C.2.6]
, any such stack is duality-adapted.
By
[
AGKRRV1
, C.3.6]
, given a map

f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is duality-adapted, the functor

f_{*,\operatorname{ren}}
satisfies the projection formula

f_{*,\operatorname{ren}}(\mathcal{F})\otimes^{!}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\simeq f_{*,\operatorname{ren}}(\mathcal{F}\otimes^{!}f^{!}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))

and base change: for a Cartesian diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{1}}
\scriptstyle{f_{1}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{S}}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{2}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{2}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{S}}_{2}^{\prime}}

we have

g_{2}^{!}\circ f_{2*,\operatorname{ren}}\simeq f_{1*,\operatorname{ren}}\circ g_{1}^{!}.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
the usual direct image functor

f_{*}
for a map
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
between semi-analytic stacks might not satisfy the projection formula or base change
(see
[
DG
, Example 7.7.6]
) and this is one of the reason
to introduce the renormalized version
f_{*,\operatorname{ren}}
.
However, according to
[
DG
, Corollary 10.2.5]
, if the map
f
is schematic (or more generally, the map is “safe” as defined in
loc. cit.
) then
we have
f_{*}=f_{*,\operatorname{ren}}
and hence
f_{*}
satisfies
the projection formula or base change.

2.3.
Orientation sheaf

Let
f:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
be a map between semi-analytic stacks.
We will write
d_{f}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{S}}-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
for the relative dimension of
f
.
The complex
\omega_{f}:=f^{!}{\mathbb{C}}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}}
is called the (relative) dualizing complex for
f
.
If
f
is smooth then we have
\omega_{f}\simeq\operatorname{or}_{f}[d_{f}]
where

\operatorname{or}_{f}:={\mathcal{H}}^{-d_{f}}(\omega_{f})
is a local system on

{\mathcal{S}}
called the (relative) orientation sheaf of
f
.
Note that we have
\operatorname{or}_{f}\otimes\operatorname{or}_{f}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{S}}
.
Moreover, have a canonical isomorphism of functors

(2.2)

f^{*}\otimes\omega_{f}\simeq f^{*}\otimes\operatorname{or}_{f}[d_{f}]\simeq f^{!}.

In the case when
S^{\prime}=\operatorname{pt}
we will write

d_{\mathcal{S}}=d_{f}
,

\omega_{\mathcal{S}}=\omega_{f}
,
\operatorname{or}_{\mathcal{S}}=\operatorname{or}_{f}
, etc.

2.4.
Averaging functors

Let
X
be a real analytic manifold acted on by
a Lie group
H
. Let
U\subset H
be Lie subgroup
and consider the quotient map

f:U\backslash X\to H\backslash X
.
We have the forgetful functor

\operatorname{oblv}_{H/U}=f^{*}:\operatorname{Shv}(H\backslash X)\to\operatorname{Shv}(U\backslash X)

with right adjoint

\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{H/U}=f_{*}:\operatorname{Shv}(U\backslash X)\to\operatorname{Shv}(H\backslash X)

(note that
f_{*}=f_{*,\operatorname{ren}}
as
f
is schematic) and left adjoint

\operatorname{Av}_{!}^{H/U}=f_{!}\circ((-)\otimes\operatorname{or}_{f})[d_{f}]:\operatorname{Shv}(U\backslash X)\to\operatorname{Shv}(H\backslash X)

here
(-)\otimes\operatorname{or}_{f}:\operatorname{Shv}(U\backslash X)\to\operatorname{Shv}(U\backslash X)
is the functor of tensoring with the
orientation sheaf
\operatorname{or}_{f}
.

3.
The Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor

3.1.

We recall the definition of Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor
in the setting of constructible sheaves following
[
AGKRRV2
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be semi-analytic stack.
An object
\mathcal{Q}\in\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}})
defines a functor

F_{\mathcal{Q}}:\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}}),\ \ \mathcal{F}\to(p_{2})_{*,\operatorname{ren}}(\mathcal{Q}\otimes^{!}(p_{1})^{!}(\mathcal{F}))

where

{\mathcal{S}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{1}}}{{\longleftarrow}}{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{S}}
are the projection maps.
We shall call
F_{\mathcal{Q}}
the functor given by the kernel
\mathcal{Q}
.
Consider the diagonal embedding

\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}
.
It is straightforward to check that the identity functor

\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{S}}:\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})

is given by the kernel
(\Delta_{\mathcal{S}})_{*}(\omega_{\mathcal{S}})
.
The
Drinfeld-Gaitsgory functor
:

\operatorname{Ps-Id}_{{\mathcal{S}}}:\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})\to\operatorname{Shv}({\mathcal{S}})

is by definition the functor given by the
kernel
(\Delta_{\mathcal{S}})_{!}({\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{S}})
where
{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the constant sheaf on
{\mathcal{S}}
.

4.
Matsuki duality

4.1.

We recall the definition of “Matsuki datum”
introduced in
[
MUV
, Section 5]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Matsuki datum
(G,X,H^{+},H^{-})
consists of
a linear Lie group
G
acting on a
real analytic manifold
X
and two subgroups
H
and
H^{-}
of

G
such that there is a Bott-Morse function

f:X\to\mathbb{R}
invariant under
H^{+}\cap H^{-}=U
and
a
U
-invariant metric on
X
, satisfying the following:

(1)

The gradient flow
\Phi
of
f
on

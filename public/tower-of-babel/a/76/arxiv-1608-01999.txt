Two-Loop Beam and Soft Functions for Rapidity-Dependent Jet Vetoes Preprint: MITP/16-078 DESY 16-154 NIKHEF 2016-032 August 05, 2016
Shireen Gangal, Jonathan R. Gaunt, Maximilian Stahlhofen, Frank J. Tackmann

Abstract
Jet vetoes play an important role in many analyses at the LHC. Traditionally, jet vetoes have been imposed using a restriction on the transverse momentum p T ​ j p_{Tj} of jets. Alternatively, one can also consider jet observables for which p T ​ j p_{Tj} is weighted by a smooth function of the jet rapidity y j y_{j} that vanishes as | y j | → ∞ |y_{j}|\to\infty . Such observables are useful as they provide a natural way to impose a tight veto on central jets but a looser one at forward rapidities. We consider two such rapidity-dependent jet veto observables, 𝒯 B ​ j \mathcal{T}_{Bj} and 𝒯 C ​ j \mathcal{T}_{Cj} , and compute the required beam and dijet soft functions for the jet-vetoed color-singlet production cross section at two loops. At this order, clustering effects from the jet algorithm become important. The dominant contributions are computed fully analytically while corrections that are subleading in the limit of small jet radii are expressed in terms of finite numerical integrals. Our results enable the full NNLL ′ resummation and are an important step towards N 3 LL resummation for cross sections with a 𝒯 B ​ j \mathcal{T}_{Bj} or 𝒯 C ​ j \mathcal{T}_{Cj} jet veto.

Introduction

Jet vetoes find frequent application at the LHC, e.g. in Higgs property measurements as well as in searches for physics beyond the Standard Model. They are used to cut away backgrounds, and more generally to classify the data into exclusive categories, or ‘jet bins’, based on the number of jets in the final state, in order to increase the signal sensitivity. When a generic jet veto observable
\mathcal{T}
is constrained to be much smaller than the hard scale of the process
Q
, large logarithms of
\mathcal{T}/Q
appear in the perturbative expansion of the jet-vetoed cross section, and should be resummed to obtain precise predictions.

The default jet variable by which jets are currently classified and vetoed is the transverse momentum
p_{Tj}
of a jet.
However, there are some drawbacks to using this variable. In harsh pile-up conditions, it can be difficult to identify (and veto) low-
p_{T}
jets in the forward region (beyond
|\eta|\gtrsim 2.5
) when a large part or all of the jet lies in a detector region where no tracking information is available. One way to get around this problem is by introducing a hard cut on the jet (pseudo)rapidity and only consider jets at
|\eta_{j}|<\eta^{\mathrm{cut}}
. However, such a cut also changes the logarithmic structure
Tackmann:2012bt
, and none of the extant resummations of
p_{Tj}
take account of such a rapidity cut. Another way one might try to avoid the problem is to raise the cut on
p_{Tj}
, but then one loses the potential benefits of a tight jet veto (such as its utility to identify the initial state of heavy resonances
Ebert:2016idf
).

An alternative approach is to consider a generalized jet-veto variable
Gangal:2014qda

\mathcal{T}_{fj}=p_{Tj}f(y_{j})

(1)

that is smoothly dependent on the jet rapidity
y_{j}
, where the function
f(y)
is decreasing for increasing
\lvert y\rvert
such that limiting
\mathcal{T}_{fj}
to small values tightly constrains central jets, but only loosely constrains the forward ones.
In addition to the above practical considerations, given the importance of jet binning
it is highly desirable to have several different options for performing jet vetoes, both experimentally and theoretically. This avoids having to rely exclusively on
p_{Tj}
, and provides important complementary information on the pattern of additional jets produced, for example in Higgs production
Aad:2014lwa
.

2
Calculation

The measurement function for a jet veto using a generic variable
\mathcal{T}_{fj}
is given by

\mathcal{M}_{f}^{\mathrm{jet}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})=\prod_{j\in J(R)}\theta(\mathcal{T}_{fj}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,.

(3)

This constraints all jets to have
\mathcal{T}_{fj}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}
and thus vetoes any jets with
\mathcal{T}_{fj}>\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}
.
The jets
J(R)
in eq. (
4
) are identified with a specific jet algorithm with jet radius
R
.
At NNLO, we can have at most two emissions, and the precise form of the jet algorithm is irrelevant.
Our results are valid for any jet algorithm that clusters the emissions together if they are closer in

\Delta R^{2}=\Delta\phi^{2}+\Delta y^{2}
than
R^{2}
, where
\Delta y
is the separation in rapidity,
\Delta\phi
is the separation in azimuthal angle and
R
is the jet radius. In experimental analyses at the LHC typically
R=0.4
or
0.5
.
For two real emissions in the final state with momenta
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, eq. (
3
) reduces to

\mathcal{M}_{f}^{\mathrm{jet}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})=\theta(\Delta R<R)\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{fj}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})+\theta(\Delta R>R)\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f1}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,.

(4)

That is, for
\Delta R<R
the two emissions are clustered into a jet and
\mathcal{T}_{fj}
is computed from the sum
p_{j}=k_{1}+k_{2}
. For
\Delta R>R
, each emissions forms its own jet and
\mathcal{T}_{f1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{f2}
are computed with
p_{j}=k_{1}
and
p_{j}=k_{2}
, respectively.

The measurement function
\mathcal{M}_{f}^{\mathrm{jet}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})
is inserted into the usual SCET operator matrix elements defining the beam and soft functions. In this case, the jets
J(R)
are obtained purely from the collinear or soft radiation within each sector. For details we refer to refs.
Tackmann:2012bt
;
Gangal:2014qda
. The practical implementation of such a measurement in beam and soft function calculations is discussed below.

The factorization of the cross section with a
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
or
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
veto in refs.
Tackmann:2012bt
;
Gangal:2014qda
is strictly speaking valid only to lowest order in an expansion in
R
. The possibility of clustering independent soft and collinear emissions into the same jet breaks the soft-collinear factorization of the measurement function with the corresponding corrections starting at
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{2})

Tackmann:2012bt
. Since this only affects the measurement itself but not the soft-collinear factorization of the amplitudes and SCET Lagrangian, these soft-collinear clustering corrections can be computed in the effective theory and are included in our results.
We separate out the corresponding contributions in the two-loop beam and soft functions that are associated with the clustering of independent emissions. They are denoted with the subscript ‘indep’ and together with the corrections from soft-collinear clustering reproduce the two-loop clustering behaviour of independent emissions in full QCD (in the singular limit). In section
3
, we give two prescriptions as to how this collection of terms can be treated in the NNLL
′
resummation.

In addition, at
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{2})
(potentially) factorization breaking effects due to Glauber interactions can play a role
Gaunt:2014ska
. At the perturbative level, they first appear in a nonlogarithmic
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s}^{4})
diagram, implying that the factorization breaking effects first appear at the N
4
LL order
Zeng:2015iba
;
Rothstein:2016bsq
. They will not be discussed further here.

Our calculation of the beam and soft functions is organized in an expansion in
R
as well.
We will give terms in this expansion up to orders high enough for all practical purposes.
This expansion only involves even powers of
R
(up to few exceptional terms at lower orders).
We find the
R^{2}
expansion to converge very quickly, suggesting that the relevant
expansion parameter is
(R/R_{0})^{2}
with
R_{0}\simeq 2
. (Similar observations have been made recently also in
other contexts involving small-
R
expansions, see e.g.
Dasgupta:2016bnd
;
Kolodrubetz:2016dzb
.)
As pointed out in ref.
Tackmann:2012bt
, in the small-
R
limit one should also consider resumming
the corresponding logarithms
\ln R
appearing in the jet-vetoed cross section. The dominant contribution
beyond
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
was obtained in ref.
Alioli:2013hba
. Their
resummation at the LL was obtained in refs.
Dasgupta:2014yra
;
Banfi:2015pju
, and very recently methods have been developed
Kang:2016mcy
;
Dai:2016hzf
that could allow one to systematically carry out their resummation to higher orders.

To perform the computation of the jet-algorithm dependent soft and beam functions we follow the same strategy used in refs.
Tackmann:2012bt
;
Stewart:2013faa
;
Banfi:2012yh
by computing the difference to a reference soft or beam function defined with a global (jet-algorithm independent) measurement, with the reference functions having been computed elsewhere in the literature. A key property of the global reference measurements we use is that they coincide with the jet-dependent measurements for the case of one real emission. Then when we compute the differences, we only need to consider the double-real emission amplitudes.
Since the measurement functions are different for
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
and
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
, we must perform a separate computation of the soft function for the two observables. Since
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
is equal to
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
at forward rapidities, the beam function is in fact the same for both observables and there is only computation to be performed.

2.1
Soft function

We discuss explicitly the calculation of the quark soft function, i.e., where the two partons initiating the hard process are quarks. The results for the two-loop gluon soft function are obtained in the usual way by replacing
C_{F}\to C_{A}
due to Casimir scaling.
Following ref.
Tackmann:2012bt
, the calculation is done in a different way for the ‘uncorrelated’
C_{F}^{2}
and the ‘correlated’
C_{F}C_{A}
and
C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}
color channels.
The notion of correlated and uncorrelated soft emissions is closely connected to the notion of webs in the context of non-Abelian exponentiation of eikonal matrix elements
Gatheral:1983cz
;
Frenkel:1984pz
.
Without the potentially exponentiation breaking measurement operator the soft amplitude factorizes into sums of products of webs. For example in our two-loop calculation the
C_{F}^{2}
part of the double-real soft emission amplitude can be written as the product of two identical one-loop amplitudes (webs) proportional to
C_{F}
. We therefore regard the two emissions in the
C_{F}^{2}
channel as independent or uncorrelated. In contrast, the
C_{F}C_{A}
and
C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}
parts of the total soft amplitude are nonfactorizable two-loop webs, and we refer to the corresponding emissions as correlated.
The method of calculation for the correlated and uncorrelated channels is described in the following.

2.1.1
C_{F}^{2}
piece

For the uncorrelated
C_{F}^{2}
part of the bare soft function, we conveniently compute the two-loop correction relative to the expectation from non-Abelian exponentiation for the unclustered case, i.e. half of the one-loop soft function squared. This difference is directly the two-loop clustering correction for independent emissions, so we denote it by
\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R)
.
The total
C_{F}^{2}
contribution to the bare soft function is then

S_{f}^{\mathrm{bare}(2,C_{F}^{2})}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[S^{\mathrm{bare}{(1)}}_{f}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]^{2}+\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R)\,.

(5)

The clustering correction
\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}
starts at order
R^{2}
, as discussed earlier, i.e., non-Abelian exponentiation for the jet-veto soft function works up to terms of order
R^{2}
.

The first term in eq. (
5
) corresponds to using a reference measurement
\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f1}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})
, which separately restricts each emissions irrespective of their separation. The difference to eq. (
4
) then gives the measurement function
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{f,\mathrm{indep}}
that corresponds to
\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{(2)}
,

\Delta\mathcal{M}_{f,\mathrm{indep}}=\theta(\Delta R<R)\Bigl[\theta(\mathcal{T}_{fj}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f1}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]\,,

(6)

where
\mathcal{T}_{Bi}=\min\{k_{i}^{+},k_{i}^{-}\}
and
\mathcal{T}_{Ci}=k_{i}^{+}k_{i}^{-}/(k_{i}^{+}+k_{i}^{-})
for the single parton
i=1,2
.

For the
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
veto, we split the measurement eq. (
6
) into two pieces:

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{B,\mathrm{indep}}

\displaystyle=\Delta\mathcal{M}_{B,\mathrm{indep,1}}+\Delta\mathcal{M}_{B,\mathrm{indep,2}}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{B,\mathrm{indep},1}

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R<R)\Bigl[\theta(k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(k_{1}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\theta(k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]2\,\theta(y_{t}>0)\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{B,\mathrm{indep},2}

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R<R)\Bigl\{\Bigl[\theta(k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(k_{1}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\theta(k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times 2\bigl[\theta(y_{1}>0)\,\theta(y_{2}>0)-\theta(y_{t}>0)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigl[\theta(k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(k_{1}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\theta(k_{2}^{-}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\times 4\theta(y_{1}>0)\,\theta(y_{2}<0)\,\theta(y_{j}>0)\Bigr\}\,.

(9)

The jet and total rapidities are defined by

\displaystyle y_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{k_{1}^{-}+k_{2}^{-}}{k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}}\,\qquad\text{and}\qquad y_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(y_{1}+y_{2})=\frac{1}{4}\Bigl(\ln\frac{k_{1}^{-}}{k_{1}^{+}}+\ln\frac{k_{2}^{-}}{k_{2}^{+}}\Bigr)\,.

(10)

For the
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
veto, we split eq. (
6
) as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{C,\mathrm{indep}}

\displaystyle=\Delta\mathcal{M}_{C,\mathrm{indep,1}}+\Delta\mathcal{M}_{C,\mathrm{indep,2}}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{C,\mathrm{indep},1}

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R<R)\Bigl[\theta(\mathcal{T}_{C1}+\mathcal{T}_{C2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(\mathcal{T}_{C1}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\theta(\mathcal{T}_{C2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{C,\mathrm{indep},2}

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R<R)\Bigl[\theta(\mathcal{T}_{Cj}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(\mathcal{T}_{C1}+\mathcal{T}_{C2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]\,.

(13)

In both cases, the contribution to
\Delta S
associated with the first part of the measurement,
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\rm{f,indep,1}}
, starts to contribute at order
R^{2}
and contains a
1/\epsilon
divergent piece (using dimensional regularization with
d=4-2\epsilon
), and thus produces a single logarithm of
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}
at
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{2})
.
The second part is found to start at order

and is finite in four dimensions.

S^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}=Z^{{(2)}}+Z^{{(1)}}S^{{(1)}}+S^{{(2)}}\,.

(14)

For the case of the uncorrelated soft function contribution we can directly use eq. (
5
) together with the one-loop relation
S^{\mathrm{bare}{(1)}}=Z^{(1)}+S^{(1)}
and find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(Z^{{(1)}}+S^{{(1)}}\Bigr)^{2}+\Delta S_{\rm indep}^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\mathrm{indep}}^{{(2)}}

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{\mathrm{indep}}^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(Z^{{(1)}}\big)^{2}-Z^{(2,C_{F}^{2})}\,,

\displaystyle S_{f}^{(2,C_{F}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(S_{f}^{{(1)}})^{2}+\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{{(2)}}\,.

2.1.2
C_{F}C_{A}
and
C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}
pieces

The correlated
C_{F}C_{A}
and
C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}
contributions to the soft function are split into three pieces,

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{f}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R)=S^{(2)}_{G,f}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})+\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R)+\Delta S^{(2)}_{{\rm rest},f}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R)\,.

(17)

Here,
S_{G,f}
is defined for a (known) global reference measurement.
For
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
,
S_{G,B}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})
is given by the cumulant of the single-differential thrust soft function. It has been computed in refs.
Kelley:2011ng
;
Monni:2011gb
;
Hornig:2011iu
, and the explicit
\mathcal{T}_{B}
-differential expression can be found e.g. in ref.
Gaunt:2015pea
.

S_{G,C}
is the cumulant of the
C
-parameter soft function, which is defined e.g. in eq. (28) of ref.
Hoang:2014wka
and has been obtained numerically in refs.
Hoang:2014wka
;
Bell:2015lsf
.
Since the reference measurements are chosen to coincide with the jet measurements for a single real emission,
S^{(2)}_{G,f}
already contains the correct real-virtual contributions. The measurement function
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{f}
corresponding to the total difference
\Delta S_{f}^{(2)}=S_{f}^{(2)}-S_{G,f}^{(2)}
is thus given by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{f}

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R<R)\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{fj}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})+\theta(\Delta R>R)\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f1}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle\quad-\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f1}+\mathcal{T}_{f2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,,

(18)

where the first line is the full jet measurement for two emissions in eq. (
4
) and the second line subtracts the reference measurement
\mathcal{M}_{G,f}=\theta(\mathcal{T}_{f1}+\mathcal{T}_{f2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})
.

The divergences of
S_{f}
and
S_{G,f}
differ by a
1/\epsilon
term and are the same for
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
and
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
.
The second quantity
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
in eq. (
17
) is designed to capture this divergence and is the same for both
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
and
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
. The remaining piece
\Delta S^{(2)}_{{\rm rest},f}
is then a finite correction.
The measurement function for
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
is defined as

\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}=2\theta(y_{t}>0)\,\theta(\Delta R>R)\Bigl[\theta(k_{1}^{+}\!<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,\theta(k_{2}^{+}\!<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}\!<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr].

(19)

The global reference measurement essentially amounts to always clustering the emissions, and
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}
corrects this to a constraint on the individual emissions when they are further apart than
R
. The reference measurement thus already captures most of the singularities. The remaining
1/\epsilon
divergence of
\Delta S_{f}^{(2)}

is associated with the limit of large jet rapidity
|y_{j}|\to\infty
.
The correlated amplitude has support only in a finite range of
\Delta y=y_{1}-y_{2}
around zero.
The limits
|y_{j}|\to\infty
,
|y_{t}|\to\infty
and
y_{1},\,y_{2}\to\pm\infty
simultaneously are therefore effectively equivalent.
In particular, in the latter limit
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{f}
in eq. (
2.1.2
) becomes equal to
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}
in eq. (
19
). Thus the divergence of
\Delta S_{f}^{(2)}
is equal to the one of
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
.

As we will see below, the
1/\epsilon
divergence in
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
can be isolated analytically, which makes
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
much easier to compute than the full difference
\Delta S^{(2)}_{f}
.
In this sense,
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
performs a very similar role as a subtraction term in a conventional fixed-order calculation.
In particular, the remainder
\Delta S^{(2)}_{{\rm rest},f}=\Delta S^{(2)}_{f}-\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
can be computed numerically setting
d=4
from the start.

The soft function
S^{(2)}_{f}
contains terms proportional to
\ln R
and
\ln^{2}\!R
.
These logarithms are entirely contained in
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
, such that
\Delta S^{(2)}_{{\rm rest},f}
is finite as
R\to 0
, see appendix
B
. We compute these logarithms analytically as detailed below, while all remaining contributions are computed numerically. Some details regarding the setup for the numerical calculations are given in appendix
B
.

In the remainder of this section we describe explicitly the computation of
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
. We first write it in terms of the momenta
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
of the two emitted gluons,

\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}={\tilde{\mu}}^{4\epsilon}\int\!\!\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{\rm corr}(k_{1},k_{2})\,\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}(k_{1},k_{2})\,C(k_{1})\,C(k_{2})\,.

(20)

Here
C(k_{i})=2\pi\delta(k_{i}^{2})\theta(k_{i}^{0})
denotes a cut propagator,
\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{\rm corr}(k_{1},k_{2})
is the amplitude obtained by adding all
C_{F}C_{A}
and
C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}
terms of the double-real diagrams as given explicitly in appendix B of Ref.
Hornig:2011iu
,
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}
is the measurement function in eq. (
19
), and we have included the usual
{\overline{\rm MS}}
factor with
{\tilde{\mu}}=\mu\exp\{\tfrac{1}{2}[\gamma_{E}-\ln(4\pi)]\}
.

We can rewrite the phase-space integral in terms of light-cone components in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions and arrive at

\displaystyle\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{-4-\epsilon}\,e^{\gamma_{E}2\epsilon}\mu^{4\epsilon}}{\pi^{5}\,\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk_{1}^{+}dk_{2}^{+}dk_{1}^{-}dk_{2}^{-}(k_{1}^{+}k_{1}^{-}k_{2}^{+}k_{2}^{-})^{-\epsilon}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{\pi}d\Delta\phi\,(\sin\Delta\phi)^{-2\epsilon}\,\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{\rm corr}(k_{1},k_{2})\,\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}(k_{1},k_{2})\,.

(21)

To incorporate the constraint on
\Delta R
we write
k_{1}^{\pm}
and
k_{2}^{\pm}
in terms of the variables

\Delta y
,
y_{t}
,
\Delta\phi
,
\mathcal{T}_{T}
, and
z
, as in Appendix C of ref.
Tackmann:2012bt
,

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln{\frac{k_{1}^{-}}{k_{1}^{+}}}\,,\quad y_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\ln{\frac{k_{2}^{-}}{k_{2}^{+}}}\,,\quad y_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(y_{1}+y_{2})\,,\quad\Delta y=y_{1}-y_{2}\,,

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}^{+}}{k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}}\,,\quad\mathcal{T}_{T}=k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}\,,\quad k_{1}^{+}=z\mathcal{T}_{T}\,,\quad k_{2}^{+}=(1-z)\mathcal{T}_{T}\,,

\displaystyle\cos\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}^{\perp}\!\cdot\!k_{2}^{\perp}}{|k_{1}^{\perp}||k_{2}^{\perp}|}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}(k_{1}^{+}k_{2}^{-}+k_{2}^{+}k_{1}^{-})-k_{1}\!\cdot\!k_{2}}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{+}k_{1}^{-}k_{2}^{+}k_{2}^{-}}}\,.

(22)

The measurement function in eq. (
19
) can be expressed in these variables as

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}=\theta(y_{t}>0)\,\theta(\Delta R>R)\,\theta\Bigl[\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}<\mathcal{T}_{T}<\frac{\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}{\max(z,1-z)}\Bigr]\,.

(23)

As mentioned previously, the integration of
\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{\rm corr}
with
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{base}}
gives terms proportional to
\ln^{n}R
.
We calculate these terms analytically by expanding the full integrand of the
\Delta y,\Delta\phi,z,\mathcal{T}_{T}
integration (including the amplitude and phase
space factors) for small
\Delta R\sim\Delta y\sim\Delta\phi
, keeping only the lowest order terms of order
\Delta R^{-2}
. The integral may then be performed analytically by using the relations

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta y\int_{0}^{\pi}d\Delta\phi\frac{\Delta\phi^{-2\epsilon}}{\Delta\phi^{2}+\Delta y^{2}}\,\theta(\Delta\phi^{2}+\Delta y^{2}>R^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad=-\pi\ln\frac{R}{2\pi}+\epsilon\,\pi\Bigl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}+\ln^{2}\frac{R}{2}-\ln^{2}{\pi}\Bigr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{2})\,,

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta y\int_{0}^{\pi}d\Delta\phi\frac{2\Delta y^{2}\Delta\phi^{-2\epsilon}}{(\Delta\phi^{2}+\Delta y^{2})^{2}}\,\theta(\Delta\phi^{2}+\Delta y^{2}>R^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\pi}{2}-\pi\ln\frac{R}{2\pi}+\epsilon\,\pi\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}+\ln^{2}\frac{R}{2}-\ln\frac{R}{2}-\ln^{2}{\pi}\Bigr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{2})\,.

(25)

To compute the remainder of
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
, we subtract the expanded integrand from the full one and integrate the result. The integrand has a sufficiently simple dependence on
y_{t}
and
\mathcal{T}_{T}
that we can perform the integrations over these variables first analytically. The integral over
y_{t}
simply gives a
1/(4\epsilon)
factor. The result can then be expanded in
\epsilon
up to order
\epsilon^{0}
and integrated numerically over the remaining variables
\Delta y
,
\Delta\phi
,
z
order-by-order in
\epsilon
.

The
\ln R
and
\ln^{2}\!R
terms in the soft and beam functions all arise from eqs. (
2.1.2
) and (
2.1.2
). They are remainders of the collinear divergence between particles
1
and
2
, which is regulated by
R
.
For a
p_{Tj}
veto there is only a single
\ln R
. The
\ln^{2}\!R
terms we find for the
\mathcal{T}_{fj}
veto are related to the different treatment of rapidity divergences compared to the
p_{Tj}
case. In the latter an additional rapidity regulator is required, whereas in our
\mathcal{T}_{fj}
veto calculation rapidity-type divergences are effectively regulated (along with the usual IR and UV divergences) by dimensional regularization. The
\ln^{2}\!R
terms only arise from the
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon)
terms in eqs. (
2.1.2
) and (
2.1.2
) and therefore cannot appear in the anomalous dimensions. For the same reason, their coefficient in the fixed-order terms is the same as the coefficient of the
\ln R
terms in the anomalous dimensions. Consequently, they cancel between the beam and soft functions at fixed order due to RG consistency.

To convert the bare result into a renormalized one, we again use eq. (
14
). For the correlated channels, the cross term
Z^{{(1)}}S^{{(1)}}
however vanishes, implying that
S^{{(2)}}
is just the finite part of
S^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}
here. In particular, we can obtain the
C_{F}C_{A}
and
C_{F}T_{F}n_{f}
contributions to the renormalized jet-dependent
S_{f}^{(2)}
exactly as shown in eq. (
17
), namely by adding to the corresponding reference
S_{G,f}^{(2)}
the finite part of
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\mathrm{base}}
as well as
\Delta S^{(2)}_{{\rm rest},f}
.

2.2
Beam Function

For the beam function, the calculation is done in the same way for all color and partonic channels.
The jet-veto beam functions
B_{i}
can be computed as a convolution of perturbative matching coefficients
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
with the standard parton distribution functions (PDFs)
f_{j}
as
Fleming:2006cd
;
Stewart:2009yx
;
Stewart:2010qs

\displaystyle B_{i}(t^{\mathrm{cut}}\!,x,R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\!\int^{1}_{x}\!\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\mathcal{I}_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}}\!,z,R,\mu)f_{j}\Bigl(\frac{x}{z},\mu\Bigr)\biggl[1+{\mathcal{O}}\biggl(\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}}{t^{\mathrm{cut}}}\biggr)\biggr]\,.

(26)

To obtain the matching coefficients at two loops, we compute the two-loop partonic beam functions
B_{ij}
, see refs.
Stewart:2009yx
;
Stewart:2010qs
;
Gaunt:2014xga
;
Gaunt:2014cfa
for details and related definitions in terms of SCET operator matrix elements.

The partonic jet-dependent
B_{ij}
are calculated via the difference
\Delta B_{ij}
between them and the reference beam functions as

B_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R)=B_{G,ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x)+\Delta B_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R)\,,

(27)

where
t^{\mathrm{cut}}\equiv xp^{-}\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}
with
p^{-}
being the large light-cone momentum of the incoming parton.
The reference functions
B_{G,ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x)
are defined using the global reference measurement
\theta(\mathcal{T}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})
, where
\mathcal{T}
is the total plus momentum of all real emissions. They are given by the cumulant of the virtuality-dependent beam functions calculated in refs.
Gaunt:2014xga
;
Gaunt:2014cfa
,

B_{G,ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x)=\int_{0}^{t^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}t\,B_{ij}(t,x)\,.

(28)

The measurement function corresponding to
\Delta B_{ij}
is given by the difference of eq. (
4
) and the global reference measurement,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{jet}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R<R)\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{j}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})+\theta(\Delta R>R)\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{1}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\,\theta(\mathcal{T}_{2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle\quad-\theta(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle=\theta(\Delta R>R)\,\Bigl[\theta(k_{1}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\theta(k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})-\theta(k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}<\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]\,.

(29)

In the second step we used that in the collinear sector we always have
\mathcal{T}_{i}=k_{i}^{+}
and
\mathcal{T}_{j}=k_{1}^{+}+k_{2}^{+}
for both
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
and
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
.
In addition, we have the usual measurement
\delta
-function that fixes the large light-cone momentum fraction of the parton that enters the hard process to
x
.

Although collinear matrix elements, like the beam functions, do not exponentiate, we nevertheless introduce a notion of ‘correlated’ and ‘uncorrelated’ emissions also for their calculation. We note, however, that this distinction is purely technical and there is no one-to-one correspondence to the different color factors.
It is inspired by the behavior of the amplitude in the limit where (at least) one emission becomes soft.

We start by taking the double-real emission amplitudes
\mathcal{A}
for the partonic beam function for each parton and color channel,
previously calculated in refs.
Gaunt:2014xga
;
Gaunt:2014cfa
. These amplitudes
are gauge invariant and have been calculated in both Feynman and axial (light-cone)
gauge as a cross check. We again denote the momenta of the emitted partons as
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
,
and (without loss of generality) we have symmetrized the amplitudes such that they are symmetric under interchanging

k_{1}\leftrightarrow k_{2}
. For each color channel we separate
\mathcal{A}
into two pieces,

\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2},x)=\mathcal{A}_{A}(k_{1},k_{2},x)+\mathcal{A}_{B}(k_{1},k_{2},x)\,,

(30)

where
\mathcal{A}_{A}
is defined by

\mathcal{A}_{A}(k_{1},k_{2},x)\equiv\frac{\lim_{k_{1}^{-}\to 0}\bigl[k_{1}^{-}k_{2}^{-}\cdot\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2},x)|_{k_{2}^{-}=(1-x)p^{-}-k_{1}^{-}}\bigr]}{k_{1}^{-}k_{2}^{-}}\,,

(31)

and
\mathcal{A}_{B}=\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}_{A}
is the remainder.
Labeling the parton entering the hard process with
i
and the incoming parton with
j
, the term
\mathcal{A}_{A}
always has the form

\mathcal{A}_{A,ij}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{ij}^{B(1)}(k_{2},x)\,\mathcal{A}_{i}^{S(1)}(k_{1})\,,

(32)

with

\mathcal{A}_{ij}^{B(1)}(k_{2},x)=\frac{2g^{2}\hat{P}^{(0)}_{ij}(x)(1-x)p^{-}}{k_{2}^{+}k_{2}^{-}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{i}^{S(1)}(k_{1})=\frac{4g^{2}C_{i}}{k_{1}^{+}k_{1}^{-}}\,.

(33)

The color factors are defined as

C_{i}=\begin{cases}C_{F}&\text{for}\quad i=q\\
C_{A}&\text{for}\quad i=g\end{cases}\,,

(34)

and the
\hat{P}^{(0)}_{ij}(x)
are the unregularized one-loop splitting functions

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q_{i}q_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\,\theta(x)\,\delta_{ij}\hat{P}_{qq}(x),\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{A}\,\theta(x)\,\hat{P}_{gg}(x)\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q_{i}g}(x)=\hat{P}^{(0)}_{\bar{q}_{i}g}(x)

\displaystyle=T_{F}\,\theta(x)\,\hat{P}_{qg}(x)\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{gq_{i}}(x)=\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g\bar{q}_{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\,\theta(x)\,\hat{P}_{gq}(x)\,,

(38)

where

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{qq}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+x^{2}}{1-x}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle=2\Bigl[\frac{x}{1-x}+\frac{1-x}{x}+x(1-x)\Bigr]\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{qg}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}+(1-x)^{2}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{gq}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}\,.

(42)

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{A}
is nonzero only for certain parton and color channels.

As can be seen from eq. (
31
),
\mathcal{A}_{A}
is essentially obtained by taking the soft limit for one of the partons. In this limit the amplitude, since it contains a sum over all diagrams, falls apart into a product of a one-loop soft amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{i}^{S(1)}
and a one-loop collinear amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{ij}^{B(1)}
by Ward identity arguments. As should be clear from its structure, the term
\mathcal{A}_{A}
corresponds to the uncorrelated emission part of the amplitude. This term is the part of the amplitude that when expressed in terms of the
\Delta y,\Delta\phi,z,\mathcal{T}_{T},y_{t}
variables in eq. (
2.1.2
), and when multiplied by the appropriate Jacobian becomes flat in
\Delta y
,
\Delta\phi
as well as in
y_{t}
in four dimensions. It is also the only part of the amplitude that remains in the zero-bin subtraction terms (see below).

The integration of the term
\mathcal{A}_{B}
yields only a
1/\epsilon
divergence, and is essentially identical in character to the integral of the soft correlated amplitude.
In particular there are no divergences associated with the
z,\Delta y,\Delta\phi
or
\mathcal{T}_{T}
integrations, so the same integration variables and techniques as for the correlated soft contributions can be used to integrate this piece. Integrating the
\mathcal{A}_{A}
piece yields a much deeper divergence structure, but this piece can be integrated in a straightforward way analytically.

2.2.1
‘Correlated’ piece

To integrate
\mathcal{A}_{B}

2.2.3
Renormalization

The result of the above calculation yields the difference between the bare jet-veto and reference beam functions,
\Delta B^{\mathrm{bare}}
.
To convert this to a difference between renormalized beam functions, we must
expand the relation
B^{\mathrm{bare}}=Z\otimes B
for the two beam functions to two loops.
Even though
Z^{(1)}
and
B^{(1)}
are the same for both (
B_{G}^{(1)}=B^{(1)}
), the two beam functions renormalize differently, i.e., the nature of the convolution between the counter terms
Z
and the renormalized beam functions
B
is different.
In particular, for the jet-veto beam function this convolution is just the simple product.
Taking the difference between the renormalized beam functions according to eq. (
27
) we thus obtain

\displaystyle\Delta B^{{(2)}}_{ik}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle=\Delta B_{ik}^{\mathrm{bare}{(2)}}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})-\Delta Z_{i}^{{(2)}}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})\delta_{ik}

\displaystyle\quad-\Bigl[Z_{i}^{(1)}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})B_{ik}^{(1)}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})-\left(Z_{i}^{(1)}\otimes B_{ik}^{(1)}\right)(t^{\mathrm{cut}})\Bigr]\,,

(48)

where we define

\left(A\otimes B\right)(t^{\mathrm{cut}})=\int_{0}^{t^{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}t\int_{0}^{t}\!\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,A(t-t^{\prime})\,B(t^{\prime})\,.

(49)

The second term in square brackets in eq. (
2.2.3
) accounts for the different renormalization of the global
reference beam function.

The result for
\Delta Z^{{(2)}}_{i}
can be converted to the corresponding clustering correction for the two-loop
anomalous dimensions,
\Delta\gamma^{{(2)}}_{Bi}
. The relation
needed to achieve this can be obtained by expanding
Z\otimes\gamma_{B}=-\mathrm{d}Z/\mathrm{d}\ln\mu

to two-loop order for the two beam functions and taking the difference,

\Delta\gamma_{B}^{(2)}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})=-Z^{(1)}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})\times\gamma_{B}^{(1)}(t^{\mathrm{cut}})+\left(Z^{(1)}\otimes\gamma_{B}^{(1)}\right)(t^{\mathrm{cut}})-\biggl[\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta Z(t^{\mathrm{cut}})}{\mathrm{d}\ln\mu}\biggr]^{(2)}\,.

(50)

The result we find for
\Delta\gamma_{B}^{(2)}
is precisely as was predicted in ref.
Tackmann:2012bt
.
Namely, it is
(-1/2)
times that of the soft function, plus an additional term related to soft-collinear mixing as required by RG consistency. This is an important check of our calculation.

Finally, the difference in two-loop renormalized partonic beam functions
\Delta B_{ij}^{(2)}
can be converted into a difference in two-loop matching coefficients
\Delta\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{ij}
by expanding eq. (
26
) for a partonic incoming state to two loops for the jet-veto and reference beam functions, and taking the difference.
Since
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{(0)}
and
\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{(1)}
are both zero, this immediately yields

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R,\mu)=\Delta B^{(2)}_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R,\mu)\,.

(51)

The contributions to
\Delta\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{ij}
from the integration of
\mathcal{A}_{A}
with the unclustered ‘
1
term’, as well as the terms in square brackets in eq. (
2.2.3
), are of
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{0})
. As mentioned above, their purpose is to correct the running of the beam function to be multiplicative rather than involving convolutions.
We therefore present these pieces together with the integration of the correlated amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{B}
below. On the other hand, the integration of
\mathcal{A}_{A}
with
\theta(\Delta R<R)
, together with corresponding the zero-bin terms, are of
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{2})
. They are in fact the clustering corrections for independent emissions (analogous to
\Delta S^{(2)}_{\rm indep}
), and so we separate these pieces off, and denote them using the subscript ‘indep’ in the results below.

3.2
Independent emission pieces

The corrections to the two-loop soft and beam functions associated with the clustering of independent emissions, together with the soft-collinear mixing term, are given by

\displaystyle\Delta{S}_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{(2)}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R,\mu)

\displaystyle=16C_{i}^{2}\,R^{2}\biggl[\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\ln\frac{\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}{\mu}-2\zeta_{3}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\mathbb{F}(R)+\mathcal{U}_{f}(R)\biggr]\,,

(73)

\displaystyle\Delta{\mathcal{I}}^{(2)}_{ij,\mathrm{indep}}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R,\mu)

\displaystyle=4C_{i}\,\hat{P}^{(0)}_{ij}(x)(1-x)

(74)

\displaystyle\quad\times R^{2}\biggl[\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-x)+\delta(1-x)\biggl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\ln\frac{t^{\mathrm{cut}}}{\mu^{2}}+\frac{\zeta_{3}}{4}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\mathbb{F}(R)\biggr)\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle S\hskip-1.42262ptC^{(2)}_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},z,R,\mu)

\displaystyle=-8C_{i}\,\hat{P}^{(0)}_{ij}(x)(1-x)

(75)

\displaystyle\quad\times R^{2}\biggl[\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-x)+\delta(1-x)\biggl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\ln\frac{t^{\mathrm{cut}}}{\mu^{2}}-\zeta_{3}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\mathbb{F}(R)\biggr)\biggr]\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}(R)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}+\ln R-\frac{1}{6}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{2}-\frac{1}{90}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{4}-\frac{1}{567}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{6}+\mathcal{O}(R^{8})

(76)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{B}(R)

\displaystyle=-\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{2}-\frac{64}{45\pi}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{3}-\frac{1}{9}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{4}+\frac{1}{135}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{6}-\frac{1}{945}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{8}+{\mathcal{O}}(R^{10})\,,

(77)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{C}(R)

\displaystyle=-2\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{2}-\frac{2}{9}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{4}+\frac{2}{135}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{6}-\frac{2}{945}\Bigl(\frac{R}{2}\Bigr)^{8}+{\mathcal{O}}(R^{10})\,.

(78)

\mathcal{U}_{B}(R)
and
\mathcal{U}_{C}(R)
are the terms associated with the measurements
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{B,\mathrm{indep},2}
and
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{C,\mathrm{indep},2}
in eqs. (

) and (
13
), respectively, cf. appendix
A
. Further terms in the
R
expansion may be easily computed, but have a negligible impact for
R<1.5
. From the above expansions it seems clear that the expansion parameter is
R/2
or even smaller.

As mentioned already, the factorization of the jet-veto measurement, does not hold at
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{2})
due to the soft-collinear mixing contributions. As a result, the scale dependence from the independent emission terms only cancels, when all three contributions in eqs. (
73
), (
74
), and (
75
) are correctly combined in the cross section, see eq. (
83
). (Equivalently, the
1/\epsilon
divergences in the corresponding bare contributions only cancel between all three contributions.) They therefore have to be included together in a consistent way.

There are two possibilities as to how one can treat these
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{2})
terms at NNLL
′
.
These different treatments are formally equivalent at NNLL but not necessarily beyond.
In the first, we note that at the two-loop order we work here, the soft-collinear term has the same type of logarithms as the beam function. This allows us, at this order at least, to absorb the soft-collinear mixing terms into the beam functions, such that in eqs. (
53
) and (
54
) we have

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbb{I}_{ij}^{{(2)}}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,\mu)

\displaystyle=\Delta{I}^{(2)}_{ij,\mathrm{indep}}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R,\mu)+S\hskip-1.42262ptC^{(2)}_{ij}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x,R,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbb{S}_{f}^{(2)}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{{(2)}}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},R,\mu)\,,

(79)

and the
\mu
-dependence cancels between
\Delta\mathbb{I}_{ij}^{(2)}
and
\Delta\mathbb{S}_{f}^{(2)}
.
In practice, this scheme is effectively equivalent to the one employed in ref.
Becher:2013xia
for a
p_{Tj}
veto.

Let us define the anomalous dimensions of the beam and soft functions (for either
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
or
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
) as

\displaystyle\gamma_{S}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},\mu,R)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{G,S}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}},\mu)+\Delta\gamma_{S}[\alpha_{s}(\mu),R]\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{B}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{G,B}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},\mu)+\Delta\gamma_{B}[\alpha_{s}(\mu),R]\,,

(80)

where
\gamma_{G,S}
is the cumulant of the anomalous dimension for thrust (and C-parameter), and
\gamma_{G,B}
is the cumulant of the anomalous dimension for the virtuality-dependent beam function. Then in the scheme defined by eq. (
3.2
), the jet clustering corrections to the (noncusp) anomalous dimensions are given by

\displaystyle\Delta\gamma^{i}_{S}(\alpha_{s},R)=-2\Delta\gamma^{i}_{B}(\alpha_{s},R)=\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl[\Delta\gamma_{S\,1}^{i}(R)-C_{i}^{2}\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}R^{2}\Bigr]\,,

(81)

where the last
C_{i}^{2}R^{2}
term is from the independent emission contributions.

Alternatively, we can simply exclude eqs. (
73
), (
74
), and (
75
) from the factorized cross section, in which case we have in eqs. (
53
) and (
54
)

\Delta\mathbb{I}_{ij}^{{(2)}}(t^{\mathrm{cut}},x)=0\,,\qquad\Delta\mathbb{S}_{f}^{(2)}(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{cut}})=0\,,

(82)

and in addition the last
C_{i}^{2}R^{2}
term is removed from eq. (
81
).
Instead, we combine all terms into a common independent emissions contribution and add the following correction term to the
0
-jet cross section:

\Delta\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{Rsub}}=\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}H^{(0)}\biggl[\delta(1-x)\delta_{ij}\Delta S_{f,\mathrm{indep}}^{{(2)}}+2\Delta\mathcal{I}_{ij,\mathrm{indep}}^{{(2)}}(x)+2S\hskip-1.42262ptC^{(2)}_{ij}(x)\biggr]\otimes f_{i}(x)\,f_{j}(x)\,.

(83)

This equation is somewhat schematic – the
\otimes
symbols represent the Mellin convolution in light-cone momentum fractions along with the appropriate flavour sums, and all pieces are evaluated at a common scale
\mu
. In the resummed cross section this term can then be multiplied with the overall evolution factor of the
{\mathcal{O}}(R^{0})
cross section. This corresponds to what is done in refs.
Banfi:2012jm
;
Stewart:2013faa
in the context of a
p_{Tj}
veto.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have computed the two-loop beam and dijet soft functions for the rapidity-dependent jet-veto observables
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
and
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
. Each function is computed as the difference from the known results for the corresponding global jet-independent observable. The beam and soft functions have been computed for the complete set of color and parton channels. The dominant parts of the functions in the small-
R
limit are computed fully analytically – e.g. the
\ln^{n}R
terms and the
R^{2}
terms in the non-
\delta(1-x)
part of the beam functions. The remaining parts are determined in terms of finite numerical integrals, for which we provide simple interpolation functions.

Our results enable the resummation of color-singlet production cross sections with
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
or
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
jet vetoes at full NNLL
′
order, and are a necessary ingredient for the N
3
LL resummation (with the missing ingredient being the corrections to the three-loop anomalous dimension). Our computed beam functions can also be used in the resummation of
N
-jet cross sections for which a central jet veto is imposed using
\mathcal{T}_{Bj}
or
\mathcal{T}_{Cj}
.
Such measurements will provide important information on the production pattern of additional jets in hard processes at the LHC.

Appendix D
Beam function amplitudes in the small-
R
limit

Figure 1:
The sole graph topology that, in light-cone gauge, corresponds to a divergent amplitude at small
\Delta R
. For small
\Delta R
, this decomposes into two splitting processes, as shown on the right hand side of the figure.

It is possible to write compact formulae for the small-
R
limit of the beam function amplitudes
\mathcal{A}
in terms of one-loop splitting functions. This is expected since at small
R
the amplitude factorizes into two splitting
processes. The first is a splitting
i\to j+m
, where
i
is the initial
state parton in
\mathcal{A}
, and
j
is the physical parton that goes into
the operator with momentum fraction
x
. The second is the splitting
m\to k+l
, where
k
and
l
are the partons that go into the final state and
generate the
\Delta R
.
m
is an intermediate parton whose nature
can be determined by quark number conservation from the other partons.
As discussed in the main text, there is only one diagram that contributes
in the small-
R
limit – namely, the one in figure
1

that already has the topology of a diagonal
i\to j+(m\to k+l)
process. Figure
1
also illustrates the two splitting processes that this graph decomposes into at small
R
.

There are two distinct cases, corresponding to whether the intermediate parton

m
is a gluon or a quark. We must treat the two cases differently because
if one has a quark in the initial state, the plane of the splitting is not
correlated to whether the quark has positive or negative helicity. However,
when one has a gluon, there is a well-known correlation between the plane of
splitting and the gluon polarization.

The formula for the case when
m=q
(or
\bar{q}
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{R}=

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{i\to jq}(x,\epsilon)\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q\to kl}(z,\epsilon)\frac{2g^{4}}{\Delta R^{2}(p^{-}\mathcal{T}_{T})^{2}(1-x)z(1-z)}\,,

(108)

where
\hat{P}^{(0)}_{i\to jq}(x,\epsilon)
originates from the first graph on the right hand side of figure
1
, whilst
\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q\to kl}(z,\epsilon)
originates in the second graph. The final factor in eq. (
108
) arises from converting the transverse momentum denominators (etc.) in the splitting amplitudes
relative to the initial-state parton direction (for the first amplitude on the right hand side of figure
1
), or relative to the parton
m
(for the second amplitude), to our variables. In this formula one must
always sum over all possibilities for
(k,l)
– i.e.
(q,g)
and
(g,q)
. To get
the full expression for
\mathcal{A}_{R}
in
d
dimensions, one must use
the
d
-dimensional splitting functions given, e.g. in ref.
Catani:1998nv

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q\to gq}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=C_{F}\biggl[\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}-\epsilon x\biggr]\,,

(109)

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g\to q\bar{q}}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=T_{F}\biggl[1-\frac{2x(1-x)}{1-\epsilon}\biggr]\,.

(110)

The result when
m
is an intermediate gluon is a little more complex and given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{R}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{\hat{P}^{(0)}_{i\to jg(\mathrm{in})}(x,\epsilon)\left[(\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{in})\to kl}(z)\Delta y^{2}+\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{out})\to kl}(z)\Delta\phi^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{P}^{(0)}_{i\to jg(\mathrm{out})}(x,\epsilon)\left[(\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{out})\to kl}(z)\Delta y^{2}+\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{in})\to kl}(z)\Delta\phi^{2}\right]

(111)

\displaystyle\quad-2\epsilon\hat{P}^{(0)}_{i\to jg(\mathrm{out})}(x,\epsilon)\left[(\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{out})\to kl}(z)(\Delta y^{2}+\Delta\phi^{2})\right]\Bigr\}\frac{2g^{4}}{\Delta R^{4}(p^{-}\mathcal{T}_{T})^{2}(1-x)z(1-z)}\,.

The splitting functions
\hat{P}^{(0)}_{i\to jg(\mathrm{in}/\mathrm{out})}(x,\epsilon)
are splitting functions depending on
whether the final state gluon is polarized in or out of the splitting plane. These
functions may be computed from results in ref.
Ellis:1991qj
. The two nonzero cases are given by

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q\to qg(\mathrm{in})}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}C_{F}\frac{(1+x)^{2}}{1-x}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{q\to qg(\mathrm{out})}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}C_{F}(1-x)\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g\to gg(\mathrm{in})}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{A}}{2-2\epsilon}\biggl[(2-2\epsilon)\frac{x}{1-x}+\frac{1-x}{x}+x(1-x)\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g\to gg(\mathrm{out})}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{A}}{2-2\epsilon}\biggl[x(1-x)+\frac{1-x}{x}\biggr]\,.

(112)

Here, the second outgoing parton is polarized, while the remaining spin/polarization indices are summed over or
averaged as appropriate, and
x
is the momentum fraction of the first outgoing parton relative to the incoming quark.

Similarly
\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{in}/\mathrm{out})\to jk}(x,\epsilon)
are splitting functions depending on the
polarization of the initial state gluon with respect to the splitting plane. These are given by

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{in})\to q\bar{q}}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=n_{f}T_{F}(1-2x)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{out})\to q\bar{q}}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=n_{f}T_{F}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{in})\to gg}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\biggl[\frac{1-x}{x}+\frac{x}{1-x}+(2-2\epsilon)x(1-x)\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{(0)}_{g(\mathrm{out})\to gg}(x,\epsilon)

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\biggl[\frac{1-x}{x}+\frac{x}{1-x}\biggr]\,,

(113)

where
x
is again the momentum fraction of the first outgoing parton relative to the incoming gluon.
In the case where the intermediate gluon decays into quarks one must remember to sum over the possibilities

(j,k)=(q,\bar{q})
and
(j,k)=(\bar{q},q)
.

Say, without loss of generality, that the initial parton travels along the
z
direction,
and gluon
m

\Delta\phi

Direct and Inverse Problems in Baumslag-Solitar Group B ​ S ​ ( 1 , 3 ) BS(1,3)
Sandeep Singh, Ramandeep Kaur

Abstract
Abstract. For integers m m and n n , the Baumslag-Solitar groups, denoted as B ​ S ​ ( m , n ) BS(m,n) , are groups generated by two elements with a single defining relation: B S ( m , n ) = ⟨ a , b | a m b = b a n ⟩ BS(m,n)=\langle a,b|a^{m}b=ba^{n}\rangle . The sum of dilates, denoted as r ⋅ A + s ⋅ B r\cdot A+s\cdot B for integers r r and s s , is defined as { r ​ a + s ​ b ; a ∈ A , b ∈ B } \{ra+sb;a\in A,b\in B\} . In 2014, Freiman et al. [ 10 ] derived direct and inverse results for sums of dilates and applied these findings to address specific direct and inverse problems within Baumslag-Solitar groups, assuming suitable small doubling properties. In 2015, Freiman et al. [ 11 ] tackled the general problem of small doubling types in a monoid, a subset of the Baumslag-Solitar group B ​ S ​ ( 1 , 2 ) BS(1,2) . This paper extends these investigations to solve the analogous problem for the Baumslag-Solitar group B ​ S ​ ( 1 , 3 ) BS(1,3) .

1  Introduction

Combinatorial number theory explores the sum-product problems of sets, constituting a dynamic field within additive combinatorics. The sum-product problem involves direct and inverse problems in additive number theory, where direct problems focus on describing the size of sumsets associated with a given set, and inverse problems involve determining the structure of sets based on the cardinality of sumsets obtained from direct problems. For

, where

\displaystyle(3^{m_{i}}-1)x_{0}=(3^{m_{0}}-1)x_{i}.

\displaystyle|S^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq|T^{2}|+|s_{0}T\cup Ts_{0}|+|\{{s_{0}}^{2}\}|

\displaystyle=|T^{2}|+|s_{0}T|+|Ts_{0}|+|\{{s_{0}}^{2}\}|

\displaystyle\geq(2t-1)+t+t+1=4t=4|S|-4.

∎

Lemma 2.4

.

If

\{s_{1}^{2},s_{1}s_{2},s_{1}s_{n+1},s_{n+1}s_{1}\}\subseteq S^{2}\setminus S^{\prime 2}

(2.3)

3  Proof of Main Theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be a finite non-abelian subset of
BS^{+}(1,3)
of size
k=|S|\geq 3.

(I) If
S=S_{0}\cup S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{t},
where
t\geq 1,
then
|S^{2}|\geq\frac{7}{2}|S|-6.

(II) If
S=b^{r}a^{A}
, where
A

(|A|=k)
is a finite set of integers containing
0
and
|S^{2}|=(t+2)k-2t+h
, where

(I)
If

2.1
. For the induction step, let

be an integer. Assume that the result holds for any non-abelian finite set

u

\langle a\rangle=a^{\mathbb{Z}}

or

1.1
,

\displaystyle|{S_{t}}^{2}\cup S_{t}S_{t-1}\cup S_{t-1}S_{t}|

j
,
0\leq j\leq t-1
, then by Lemma
2.1
,
S^{*}
generates a non-abelian group. By induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle|S^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq|(S^{*})^{2}|+|{S_{t}}^{2}\cup S_{t}S_{t-1}\cup S_{t-1}S_{t}|

k_{t}\geq 6-2k_{t-1}=4.

2.2
.

2.4
, in view of

, then write

,

\displaystyle|S^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq|(S^{*})^{2}|+|{S_{t}}^{2}\cup S_{t}S_{t-1}\cup S_{t-1}S_{t}|

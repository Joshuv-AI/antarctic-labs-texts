Abstract
. Let G G be a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group whose irreducible unitary representations are square-integrable modulo the center. We obtain characterization results for reproducing formulas associated with the left translation generated systems in L 2 ​ ( G ) L^{2}(G) . Unlike the previous study of discrete frames on the nilpotent Lie groups, the current research occurs within the set up of continuous frames, which means the resulting reproducing formulas are given in terms of integral representations instead of discrete sums. As a consequence of our results for the Heisenberg group, a reproducing formula associated with the orthonormal Gabor systems of L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) is obtained.

Introduction

One of the most attractive areas of research in Harmonic analysis is to reproduce a function from a given set of functions via a formula known as
reproducing formula
. Such formulas are studied in various platforms like Gabor, wavelet, and shift generated systems in the Euclidean and locally compact abelian (LCA) group set up due to its wide use in various areas: time-frequency analysis and mathematical physics, quantum mechanics, quantum optics, etc., (see,
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
).
In general, the aim is to find
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, for the given system
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, where
I
is countable such that the below reproducing formula

\quad f=\sum_{i\in I,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle f,\psi_{i}(\cdot-k)\rangle\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k),\quad\mbox{for all}\ f\in\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}

holds true provided both the systems
\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
and
\{\psi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
are Bessel. The system
\{\psi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
is known as
alternate dual
to
\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
, where
\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
is a frame sequence satisfying the above formula.
It can be noted that the above stable decomposition of
f
allows the flexibility of choosing different type of duals of a frame
\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
. When the frame sequence
\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
becomes Riesz basis, the choice of
\psi_{i}
is unique and the system
\{\psi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
is
biorthogonal dual
to
\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
. In general
,
the researchers consider
\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}=\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\psi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
but sometimes choosing
\psi_{i}
’s outside the space
\overline{\mbox{span}}\{\varphi_{i}(\cdot-k)\}_{i,k}
provides better localization properties in both the time and the frequency domains. Such kind of requirements motivate researchers to define various duals of a frame, like, oblique dual, type-I and type-II duals, etc. We refer
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
for more details.

Our main goal is to describe the reproducing formulas associated with the left translation generated continuous frame systems in
L^{2}(G)
.
In particular, we assume
G
to be a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
Z
be the center of
G
. Then
G
is an
SI/Z
group
if almost all of its irreducible representations are square-integrable (SI) modulo the center
Z
.
An irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is called
square integrable modulo the center
if it satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\int_{G/Z}|\langle\pi({g})u,v\rangle|^{2}d\dot{g}<\infty,\ \mbox{for all}\ u,v.

The motive of the current study has two folds: First it touches the non-abelian set up of the nilpotent Lie group which is considered to be the high degree of non-abelian structure, and secondly, it captures the non-discrete translation. Consequently, the theory is valid for the
d
-dimensional Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, a 2-step nilpotent Lie group. Indeed, our work is the continuation of a chain of work of Currey et al.
[
6
]
for
L^{2}(G)
, and Barbieri et al.
[
2
]
for
L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{d})
.

We briefly start by describing left translation generated systems in
L^{2}(G)

\mathscr{A}=\{\varphi_{k}:k\in I\}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}):=\{L_{\lambda}\varphi:\lambda\in\Lambda,\varphi\in\mathscr{A}\},\ \mbox{and}\ \mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}):=\overline{\mbox{span}}\ \mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}),

where for each
\lambda\in G
, the

L_{\lambda}

is defined by

for

. For an integer lattice
\Lambda_{0}

Due to the wide use of continuous frames in various areas, like harmonic analysis, mathematical physics, quantum mechanics, quantum optics, etc., we discuss the reproducing formulas for

associated with the general set

need not be discrete. The current study provides a compendious study of duals for a continuous frame

In the continuous setup, we provide point-wise characterizations of alternate duals and their subcategories like oblique, type I, and type II duals for the continuous frame

We can get results for discrete frames by sampling the continuous frames, which keep wide applications due to their computational simplicity. Further for the discrete set

2.
Plancherel transform for SI/Z nilpotent Lie group

Let
G
be a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We identify

G
with
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
due to the analytic diffeomorphism of the exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow G
, where
n=\mbox{dim}~{\mathfrak{g}}
. To choose a basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we consider the Jordon-Hölder series
(0)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{n}=\mathfrak{g}
of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
such that dim
\mathfrak{g}_{j}=j
for
j=0,1,\dots,n
satisfying ad
(X)\mathfrak{g}_{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{j-1}
for
j=1,\dots,n
, for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, where for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
, ad
(X)(Y)=[X,Y]
, the Lie bracket of
X
and
Y
. Now we pick
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}\backslash\mathfrak{g}_{j-1}
for each
j=1,2,\dots,n
, such that the collection
\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\}
is a Jordan-Hölder basis. Then the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow G
defined by
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}X_{j}\mapsto\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}X_{j})
is a diffeomorphism, and hence the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be realized as a Haar measure on
G

[
5
]
.

Note that the center
\mathfrak{z}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is non-trivial, and it maps to the center
Z:=\exp{\mathfrak{z}}
of
G
.
The Lie group
G
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the
adjoint action

\exp(Ad(x)X):=x\exp(X)x^{-1}
and
co-adjoint action

(Ad^{*}(x)\ell)(X)=\ell(Ad(x^{-1})X)
, respectively, for
x\in G,X\in\mathfrak{g},
and
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denotes the vector space of all real-valued linear functionals on
\mathfrak{g}
. For
\ell\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the
stabilizer

R_{\ell}=\{x\in G:(Ad^{*}x)\ell=\ell\}
is a Lie group with the associated Lie algebra
r_{\ell}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:\ell[Y,X]=0,\mbox{for all}\ Y\in\mathfrak{g}\}
.

Our aim is to discuss Kirilov Theory
[
5
]
to define the Plancherel transform for
SI/Z
group. Given any
\ell\in\mathfrak{g^{*}}
there exists a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}
(known as
polarizing
or
maximal subordinate subalgebra
) of
\mathfrak{g}
which is maximal with respect to the property

\ell[\mathfrak{h}_{\ell},\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}]=0
. Then the map
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}:\exp(\mathfrak{h}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
defined by

\mathcal{X}_{\ell}(\exp X)=e^{2\pi i\ell(X)}
,
X\in\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}
is a character on
\exp(\mathfrak{h}_{\ell})
, and hence the representations induced from
\mathcal{X}_{l}
,
\pi_{\ell}:=\mbox{ind}_{\exp{\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}}}^{G}
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
, have the following properties:

(i)

\pi_{\ell}
is an irreducible unitary representation of
G
.

(ii)

Suppose
\mathfrak{h^{\prime}}_{\ell}
is another subalgebra which is maximal with respect to the property
\ell[{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}_{\ell},{\mathfrak{h}}^{\prime}_{\ell}]=0
, then
\mbox{ind}_{\exp{\mathfrak{h}_{\ell}}}^{G}\mathcal{X}_{\ell}\cong\mbox{ind}_{\exp(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}_{\ell})}^{G}\mathcal{X}_{\ell^{\prime}}
.

(iii)

\pi_{\ell_{1}}\cong\pi_{\ell_{2}}
if and only if
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
lie in the same co-adjoint orbit.

(iv)

Suppose
\pi
is a irreducible unitary representation of
G
, then there exists
\ell\in\mathfrak{g^{*}}
such that
\pi\cong\pi_{\ell}
.

Therefore there exists a bijection

\mathfrak{t}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}/Ad^{*}(G)\rightarrow\widehat{G}
which is also a Borel isomorphism, where
\widehat{G}
is the collection of all irreducible unitary representations of
G
.

For an irreducible representation
\pi\in\widehat{G}
, let
O_{\pi}
denote as coadjoint orbit corresponding to the equivalence class of
\pi
. Then the orbital characterization for the SI/Z representation is:

\pi
is square integrable modulo the center if and only if for
\ell\in O_{\pi}
,
r_{\ell}=\mathfrak{z}
and
O_{\pi}=\ell+\mathfrak{z}^{\perp}
. If SI/Z
\neq\phi
, then SI/Z=
\widehat{G}_{max}
, where the Borel subset
\widehat{G}_{max}\subseteq\widehat{G}
corresponds to coadjoint orbits of maximal dimension which is co-null for Plancherel measure class. Hence when
G
is an SI/Z group,
\widehat{G}_{max}
is parameterized by a subset of
\mathfrak{z}^{*}
. If
\pi\in\widehat{G}_{\max}
, then dim
O_{\pi}=n-\mbox{dim}\ \mathfrak{z}
, since
O_{\pi}
is symplectic manifold, it is of even dimension, say,
\dim O_{\pi}=2d
. By Schurs’ Lemma the restriction of
\pi
to
Z
is a character and hence it is a unique element
\sigma=\sigma_{\pi}\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}
(say) and
\pi(z)=e^{2\pi i\langle\sigma,logz\rangle}I
, where
I
is the identity operator. It shows that
O_{\pi}=\{l\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}:l|_{\mathfrak{z}}=\sigma\}
and
\pi\mapsto\sigma_{\pi}
is injective.

Let
G
be an SI/Z group and
\mathcal{W}=\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{z^{*}}:{\bf Pf}(\sigma)\neq 0\}

be a cross section for the coadjoint orbits of maximal dimension, where the
Pfaffian determinant

{\bf Pf}:\mathfrak{z}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by
\ell\mapsto\sqrt{\big|\det(\ell[X_{i},X_{j}])_{i,j=r\dots n}\big|}
. Then for a fixed
\sigma\in\mathcal{W}
,
p(\sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathfrak{g}_{j}(\sigma\big|_{\mathfrak{g}_{j}})
is a maximal subordinate subalgebra for
\sigma
and the corresponding induced representation
\pi_{\sigma}
is realized naturally in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
n=r+2d
for some
d
. For each
\varphi\in L^{1}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
, the Fourier transform of
\varphi
given by

\widehat{\varphi}(\sigma)=\int_{G}\varphi(x)\pi_{\sigma}(x)dx,\quad\sigma\in\mathcal{W}

defines a trace-class operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with the inner product
\langle A,B\rangle_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}=tr(B^{*}A)
. This space is denoted by
{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. When
d\sigma
is suitable normalized,

\|\varphi\|^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{W}}\|\widehat{\varphi}(\sigma)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}|{\bf Pf}(\sigma)|d(\sigma).

The Fourier transform can be extended unitarily as
\mathcal{F}
- the
Plancherel transform
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(G)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathfrak{z}^{*},{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),|{\bf Pf}(\sigma)|d\sigma)),\quad\mathcal{F}f=\widehat{f}.

Note that the Plancherel transform
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the relation

\mathcal{F}(L_{\lambda}f)(\sigma)=\pi_{\sigma}(\lambda)\mathcal{F}f(\sigma),\ \mbox{for}\ \lambda\in G,\ a.e.\ \sigma\in\mathfrak{z}^{*},\ \mbox{and}\ f\in L^{2}(G),

where the left translation operator
L_{\lambda}
on
L^{2}(G)
is given by
L_{\lambda}f(x)=f(\lambda^{-1}x)
.

Applying the periodization on
\mathcal{F}
there is a unitary map (see,
[
6
,
12
]
),
\mathscr{F}:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{r};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))))

(2.1)

\mathscr{F}f(\alpha)(m)=\mathcal{F}f(\alpha+m)|{\mbox{{\bf Pf}}}(\alpha+m)|^{1/2},\ \mbox{for all }f\in L^{2}(G),\ m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r},\ \mbox{a.e.}\ \alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}.

Moreover,
\mathscr{F}
satisfies the intertwining property of left translation by the action of
\Lambda
with the representation
\tilde{\pi}
as follows:

(2.2)

\mathscr{F}(L_{\lambda}f)(\alpha)=e^{2\pi i\langle\alpha,\lambda_{0}\rangle}\tilde{\pi}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\quad\mbox{a.e.}\ \alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r},

where for
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
and
\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}
, we write
\lambda=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{0}
, and for
g\in G
and
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{r};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))))
,
\tilde{\pi}(g)h(\alpha)=\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(g)h(\alpha)
, a.e.
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
. The representation
\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(g)
is defined on
{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\mathcal{B}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))}
by
\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(g)z(m)=\pi_{\alpha+m}(g)\degree z(m)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, where
(z(m))\in{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\mathcal{B}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))}
,
\degree
denotes the composition of operators in
{\mathcal{B}_{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\pi_{\alpha+m}(g)
is the Hilbert-Schmidt operator defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3.
Reproducing formulas associated to continuous frames

Through out the section we assume
G
to be a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The Haar measure on
G
can be realised as a Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Further assume an arbitrary measurable subset

\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\exp\mathbb{R}X_{r+1}\dots\exp\mathbb{R}X_{n}\ \mbox{(not necessarily discrete)}

and the integer lattice
\Lambda_{0}=\exp\mathbb{Z}X_{1}\dots\exp\mathbb{Z}X_{r}
in
G
. For the
countable collection of functions

\mathscr{A}=\{\varphi_{k}:k\in I\}
and
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}=\{\psi_{k}:k\in I\}
in
L^{2}(G)
, we recall the
\Lambda
-translation generated systems

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})=\{L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}:\lambda\in\Lambda,k\in I\}\ \mbox{and}\ \mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})=\{L_{\lambda}\psi_{k}:\lambda\in\Lambda,k\in I\},

where
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{0}=\{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{0}:\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1},\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}\}
. The set
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{0}
is measurable.

In the present section our aim is to develop theory for the reproducing formulas in the continuous setup having multiple generators.
Two Bessel families
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
are said to be
dual frames
if

<f,g>=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda}<f,L_{\lambda}\psi_{k}><L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k},g>d\lambda,\ \mbox{for all}\ f,g\in L^{2}(G).

In this case the reproducing formula (in weak sense) is

(3.1)

f=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda}<f,L_{\lambda}\psi_{k}>L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}d\lambda,\ \mbox{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(G),

and both the systems
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
will become a continuous frame for
L^{2}(G)
in view of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. By a
Bessel family

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
, we mean there is a
B>0
such that

\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda}|<f,L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}>|^{2}d\lambda\leq B\|f\|^{2},\ \mbox{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(G).

In addition it is a
continuous frame
for
L^{2}(G)
if there exists a
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that
A\|f\|^{2}
is a lower bound of the above expression.
If both the lower and upper inequality holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
, then
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
is a frame for
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
.
In the case of countable set
\Lambda
and counting measure
\mu_{\Lambda}
, the expression (
3.1
) becomes

f=\sum_{k\in I}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}<f,L_{\lambda}\psi_{k}>L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k},\ \mbox{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(G).

The reproducing formula (
3.1
) might hold under much weaker restrictions on
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
. Assume
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
to be a continuous frame for
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
is Bessel so that the reproducing formula (
3.1
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
then
the system
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
is said to be an
alternate dual
of
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
. In this case
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
may not be a continuous frame for
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
need not be an alternate dual of
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
. If it is so, then the alternate dual
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
is an
oblique dual
of
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
. To get more flexibility for choosing of alternate duals, we further categorize it into two more categories based on the range of analysis operator viz., type-I and type-II duals.
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})
is a
type-I (type-II) dual
of
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
if it is an alternate dual of
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
and

\mbox{range}(T_{{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})}}^{*})\subset\mbox{range}(T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}^{*})
(
\mbox{range}(T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})})\subset\mbox{range}(T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})})
), where the analysis
T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}
and synthesis
T^{*}_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}
operators associated to
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
are defined as follows:

T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}f(k,\lambda)=\langle f,L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}\rangle,\ \mbox{and}\ \langle T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}^{*}g,f\rangle=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{G}g(\lambda,k)\langle L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k},f\rangle\ d{\lambda},

for all
k\in I,\lambda\in\Lambda
,

g\in L^{2}(I\times\Lambda),\,f\in L^{2}(G),
respectively. The reproducing formula (
3.1
) for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
can be expressed

T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}^{*}T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})}\big|_{\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}=I_{\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}

in the form of operators, where
I_{\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}
is the identity operator restricted on the
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
. Such duals have been investigated for the discrete frames by many authors including
[
10
,
11
]
in the Euclidean setup.

We state our main results of this section which characterize alternate (oblique) duals and type-I (type-II) duals in the nilpotent Lie group setup. Our characterization is based on the range function technique for SI/Z Lie group associated with the representation
\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}

, we calculate the following for
\{c_{k,\lambda}\}_{k\in I,\lambda\in\Lambda}=\{c_{k,\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}}\}_{k\in I,\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}
in
L^{2}(I\times\Lambda)
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda}\langle f,L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}\rangle\overline{c_{k,\lambda}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\langle\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\mathscr{F}L_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{0}}\varphi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle\overline{c_{k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{0}}}d\alpha d\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\langle\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\mathscr{F}L_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle e^{-2\pi i\langle\alpha,\lambda_{0}\rangle}\overline{c_{k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{0}}}d\alpha d\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\langle\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{F}\varphi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle\overline{p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)}d\alpha d\lambda_{1},

where
p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)=\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}{c_{k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{0}}}e^{2\pi i\langle\alpha,\lambda_{0}\rangle}
satisfies

\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}|p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)|^{2}d\alpha d\lambda_{1}=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}|c_{k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{0}}|^{2}d\lambda_{1}<\infty.

Similarly, we can obtain

\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda}\langle f,L_{\lambda}\psi_{k}\rangle\overline{c_{k,\lambda}}d\lambda=\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\langle\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{F}\psi_{k})(\alpha)\rangle\overline{p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)}d\alpha d\lambda_{1}
.

By assuming
\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda}\langle f,L_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}\rangle\overline{c_{k,\lambda}}d\lambda=0
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
, and applying Proposition
3.5
we get for a.e.
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\langle\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{F}\varphi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle\overline{p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)}d\lambda_{1}=0\implies\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\Lambda_{1}}\langle\mathscr{F}f(\alpha),\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{F}\psi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle\overline{p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)}d\lambda_{1}=0,

from (
3.6
).
Therefore, we get
\sum_{k\in I}\int_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,L_{\lambda}\psi_{k}\rangle\overline{c_{k,\lambda}}d\lambda=0
,
for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})
. Thus, range
T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})}\subseteq
range
T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}
. Conversely, we assume, range
T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A}^{\prime})}\subseteq
range
T_{\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\mathscr{A})}
. For range
T_{{\mathscr{A}^{\prime}}(\alpha)}\subseteq\ \mbox{range}\ T_{{\mathscr{A}}(\alpha)}
, a.e.
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
, we can proceed with the help of (
3.7
) and choosing
p_{\lambda_{1}}^{k}(\alpha)=c_{k,\lambda_{1}}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
. ∎

4.
Reproducing formulas by the action of discrete translations

In this section we assume a discrete subset
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\exp\mathbb{R}X_{r+1}\dots\exp\mathbb{R}X_{n}
and the integer lattice
\Lambda_{0}=\exp\mathbb{Z}X_{1}\dots\exp\mathbb{Z}X_{r}
in
G
. Our aim is to obtain results related to reproducing formula of a Bessel family in terms of the bracket map for a
\Lambda
-translation generated system having biorthogonal property, where
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{0}
. In the sequel we use the operator
[\cdot,\cdot]:L^{2}(G)\times L^{2}(G)\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{r})
, known as
bracket map
, defined as follows for a.e.

[\varphi,\psi](\alpha):=\langle\mathscr{F}\varphi(\alpha),\mathscr{F}\psi(\alpha)\rangle_{{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{r},\mathcal{B}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))}}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\langle\mathcal{F}\varphi(\alpha+m),\mathcal{F}\psi(\alpha+m)\rangle|{\bf Pf}(\alpha+m)|,

to address the results related to the reproducing formulas
[
2
]
.
Recall the
\Lambda
-translation generated system
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)=\{L_{\lambda}\varphi:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
and its associated
\Lambda
-translation invariant space
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)=\overline{\mbox{span}}\ \mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
in
L^{2}(G)
from (
1.1
), where
\varphi\in L^{2}(G)
. Then for the reproducing formulas associated to the system
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
, we proceed by considering biorthogonal systems generated by the discrete translations.
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\psi)
are
biorthogonal
if
\langle\varphi,L_{\lambda}\psi\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,0}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. We obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for the biorthogonality and orthogonality of translation generated systems in terms of the bracket map.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}(G)
be non-zero functions. Then the following hold:

(i)

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\psi)
are biorthogonal if and only if

[\varphi,L_{\lambda_{1}}\psi](\alpha)=\delta_{\lambda_{1},0},\ \mbox{for all}\ \lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1},\ \mbox{a.e.}\ \alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}.

(ii)

The subspace generated by
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\psi)
if and only if

[\varphi,L_{\lambda_{1}}\psi](\alpha)=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ \lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1},\ \mbox{ a.e.}\ \alpha\in\Omega_{\varphi}:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r}:[\varphi,\varphi](\alpha)\neq 0\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is an orthogonal system of functions if and only if the orthogonality condition

(4.1)

\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}:\quad[\varphi,L_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi](\alpha)=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ \lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}\backslash\{0\},\ \mbox{ a.e.}\ \alpha\in\Omega_{\varphi},\quad\mbox{holds}.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
can be realized on the same technique followed in
[
1
,
2
]
for the Heisenberg group. In the wake of Proposition
4.1
, we observe that the biorthogonality (or, orthogonality) of
\Lambda
-translation generated systems is equivalent to the corresponding biorthogonality (or, orthogonality) of
\Lambda_{0}
-translation generated systems.

Now we state our main result for a
\Lambda
-translation generated system in
L^{2}(G)
to form reproducing formula. Unlike the case of the Euclidean setup, we observe that a necessary condition is involved related to the orthogonality of
\Lambda
-translation generated system of functions.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}(G)
be two functions such that it satisfies the orthogonality conditions
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}
mentioned in (

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\psi)

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,L_{\lambda}\psi\rangle L_{\lambda}\varphi,\ \mbox{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi).

Next, we discuss reproducing formula for a
\Lambda
-translation generated system, and we find an easily verifiable condition to satisfy the reproducing formula.

Theorem 4.3

Let

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,L_{\lambda}\psi\rangle L_{\lambda}\varphi

(ii)
[\varphi,\psi](\alpha)=1,\ a.e.\ \alpha\in\Omega_{\varphi}.

As a consequence of Theorem
4.3
for the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{d}

For

Next for all

0\ \quad\ \mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}\hskip 56.9055pt\mbox{and}\hskip 56.9055pt\mathscr{G}_{t}(y)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{Q}_{y}\quad\mbox{for}\ y\in(t,1]\\

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

such that

To proceed for the proof of Theorems

Proposition 4.5

Let

\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{1}

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda_{0}}(L_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\varphi)

\displaystyle(\mathscr{F}f)(\alpha)(m)=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda_{1}}(\alpha)\mathscr{F}L_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi(\alpha)(m),\ \mbox{for a.e.}\ \alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{r},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r},

The above Proposition

We now a provide a characterization of Bessel family under the orthogonality condition. The sequence
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is called Bessel in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
if,
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle f,L_{\lambda}\varphi\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}\ ,\forall f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi).

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(G)
be such that it satisfies the orthogonality condition
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
. Also,

\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
being a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is equivalent to
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda_{0}}(\varphi)
being a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda_{0}}(\varphi)

Proof.

The Bessel condition of
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is followed from the Parseval equality of the orthonormal system
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
. To show the equivalence, let
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda_{0}}(\varphi)

be a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda_{0}}(\varphi)
with bound
B
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
, there exists
\varphi_{\lambda_{1}}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda_{0}}{(L_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi)}
such that
f=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\varphi_{\lambda_{1}}
, and hence by using Proposition
4.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle f,L_{\lambda}\varphi\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\eta_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}|\langle\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},L_{\eta_{1}}L_{\eta_{0}}\varphi\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\eta_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}|\langle L_{{\eta_{1}}^{-1}}\varphi_{\eta_{1}},L_{\eta_{0}}\varphi\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\|L_{{\eta_{1}}^{-1}}\varphi_{\eta_{1}}\|^{2}=B\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\|\varphi_{\eta_{1}}\|^{2}=B\|f\|^{2},

since

and
L_{{\eta_{1}}^{-1}}
is an isometry. Hence
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is also Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
.

Conversely, assume
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
is a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)

\langle\varphi,L_{\lambda_{1}}L_{\lambda_{0}}\varphi\rangle=0

\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}|\langle\varphi,L_{\lambda_{0}}\varphi\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}|\langle\varphi,L_{\lambda_{1}}L_{\lambda_{0}}\varphi\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|\varphi\|^{2}.

∎

Proposition 4.7

(i)

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\langle f,L_{\lambda_{0}}\psi\rangle\langle L_{\lambda_{0}}\varphi,g\rangle,\,\forall f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda_{0}}(\varphi).

Proof.

Firstly note that the summations used in (i) and (ii) are well defined in view of Proposition
4.6
, since
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\psi)
are Bessel, and
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy the orthogonality conditions
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}
, respectively.

Now assume (ii) holds. By choosing
\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},\psi_{\lambda_{1}}\in\mathcal{S}^{\Lambda_{0}}{(L_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi)}

f=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},g=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\psi_{\lambda_{1}}
and using Proposition (
4.5

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\langle f,L_{\lambda}\psi\right\rangle\left\langle L_{\lambda}\varphi,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\eta_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\left\langle\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},L_{\eta_{1}}L_{\eta_{0}}\psi\right\rangle\left\langle L_{\eta_{1}}L_{\eta_{0}}\varphi,\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\psi_{\lambda_{1}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{\eta_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\left\langle L_{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},L_{\eta_{0}}\psi\right\rangle\left\langle L_{\eta_{0}}\varphi,L_{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}\psi_{\lambda_{1}}\right\rangle.

\langle f,L_{\lambda_{1}}L_{\lambda_{0}}\varphi\rangle=0
and

∎

Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Observe that for each

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{F}L_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi_{t}(\alpha+m),\mathcal{F}\varphi_{t}(\alpha+m)\rangle_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}|\alpha+m|^{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\pi_{\alpha+m}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha+m),\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha+m)\rangle_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}|\alpha+m|^{d}

\displaystyle=\langle\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha),\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha)\rangle_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}|\alpha|^{d}=\langle\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})v_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\alpha|^{d},

since
\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}=(\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}v_{\alpha})\otimes v_{\alpha}
and

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha),\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha)\rangle_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{v_{\alpha}(s)}(\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})v_{\alpha}(w))\overline{v_{\alpha}(w)}v_{\alpha}(s)dsdw=\langle\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})v_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Similarly we can calculate
[L_{\lambda_{1}}\psi_{t},\psi_{t}](\alpha)=\langle\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})w_{\alpha},w_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\alpha|^{d}
.
Therefore for each
0<t<\alpha\leq 1
, the functions
\varphi_{t}
and
\psi_{t}
satisfy orthogonality conditions
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi_{t}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\psi_{t}}
(mentioned in (
4.1
) ) due to the orthonormal Gabor systems

\{\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})v_{\alpha}:\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}\}
and
\{\pi_{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})w_{\alpha}:\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}\}
.

Further observe that
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\varphi_{t})
is Bessel since
[\varphi_{t},\varphi_{t}](\alpha)
is bounded above, follows by noting

\displaystyle[\varphi_{t},\varphi_{t}](\alpha)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mathcal{F}\varphi_{t}(\alpha+m)\|_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}^{2}|\alpha+m|^{d}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha+m)\|_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}^{2}|\alpha+m|^{d}=\|\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha)\|^{2}|\alpha|^{d}=\begin{cases}\alpha^{d}\ t<\alpha\leq 1,\\
0\ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In a similar way we can obtain
\mathcal{E}^{\Lambda}(\psi_{t})
to be a Bessel system. Next we calculate
[\varphi_{t},\psi_{t}](\alpha)
as follows:

\displaystyle[\varphi_{t},\psi_{t}](\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathcal{F}\varphi_{t}(\alpha+m),\mathcal{F}\psi_{t}(\alpha+m)\rangle|\alpha+m|^{d}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha+m),\mathscr{G}_{t}(\alpha+m)\rangle|\alpha+m|^{d}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathscr{H}_{t}(\alpha),\mathscr{G}_{t}(\alpha)\rangle_{{\mathcal{B}_{2}}}|\alpha|^{d}=|\alpha|^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{v_{\alpha}(s)}v_{\alpha}(u)\overline{w_{\alpha}(u)}w_{\alpha}(s)dsdu

\displaystyle=|\langle v_{\alpha},w_{\alpha}\rangle|^{2}|\alpha|^{d}.

Hence the result follows from Theorem
4.3
provided
[\varphi_{t},\psi_{t}](\alpha)=1
, for
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
0<t<\alpha\leq 1
.
∎

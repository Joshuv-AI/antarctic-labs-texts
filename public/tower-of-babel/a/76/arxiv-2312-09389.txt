Fractional Brownian Motion ruin model with random inspection time
Grigori Jasnovidov

Abstract




Abstract:
In this contribution we study the asymptotics of

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(L(t))-cL(t)>u\right),\quad u\to\infty,

where
B_{H},H\in(0,1)
is a fractional Brownian motion,

L(t)
is a non-negative pure jumps
Lévy process independent of
B_{H}
,
c>0
.

AMS Classification:
Primary 60G15; secondary 60G70

Keywords:
fractional Brownian motion;
ruin probability; Pickands constants; random inspection time

1.
Introduction

The classical ruin probability

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:B(t)-ct>u\right)

with
B
a standard Brownian motion,
u>0
and
c>0
,
is a benchmark model in risk theory, see
[
1
]
.
This probability can be calculated explicitly, namely
it equals to
e^{-2cu}
for all
c,u>0
, see
[
2
]
.
However, this is an exceptional case in the
field of ruin probabilities for Gaussian and related processes.
Typically probabilities of such type cannot be explicitly computed
and their asymptotics as
u\to\infty
is dealt with, see, e.g.,

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]

and references therein.
One of possible extensions of the classical ruin probability is

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(t)-ct>u\right)

with
B_{H},H\in(0,1)
a standard fractional Brownian motion (fBm), i.e.,
a Gaussian process with zero mean and

\cov(B_{H}(t),B_{H}(s))=\frac{1}{2}(|t|^{2H}+|s|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

The ruin probability for fBm model and its extensions are a subject of numerous investigations,
see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
In this paper we consider the following
generalization of fBm ruin probability:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(L(t))-cL(t)>u\right),\quad u>0,

where
L(t)
is a pure jumps Lévy process independent of
B_{H}
,
\mathcal{Z}
is the non-negative distribution of jumps of
L
and
c>0
.
Observe, that this model is an extension of discrete-time ruin probabilities
introduced in
[
14
,
15
]
. Indeed,
taking
\mathcal{Z}
to be a positive constant with probability one we reduce our model
to discrete ruin problems.
For studies devoted to Lévy time subordinations
in the classical case
H=1/2
we refer to
[
16
]
and references therein.
We can rewrite (
1.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\pi_{H,\mathcal{Z}}(u)=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(t)-ct>u,\ t=Z_{1}+Z_{2}+...+Z_{n}\text{ for some }n\right),

where
Z_{i}
’s are i.i.d. with distribution
\mathcal{Z}
.
From the representation above it is easy to see that
L
does not affect on
\pi_{H,\mathcal{Z}}(u)
, but
\mathcal{Z}
may play a role. This additional randomness (compared to the classical case) brings supplementary complexities to our analysis.
It turns out that cases
H<1/2
,
H=1/2
and
H>1/2

require different approaches and we treat them separately. For case
H>1/2
the asymptotics of
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)
given in
Theorem
3.1
is as in the classical continuous case,
see
[
8
]
, and
\mathcal{Z}
does not effect on the answer.
This behavior is typical for long-range dependence case,
see also
[
14
,
10
,
17
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
.
For
H\leq 1/2
we need to control behavior of
L(t)

as
t
tends to
\infty
, and thus we impose finiteness of variance (and hence expectation) of
\mathcal{Z}
:

A
:
\mathbb{E}Z_{1}=\mu\in(0,\infty),\quad\Var(Z_{1})<\infty
.

The asymptotics in Theorem
3.1
for
H=1/2
reminds the results in
[
19
]
;
the only difference is in Pickands-type constants.
For case
H<1/2
due to short-range dependence of fBm
asymtpotics of
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)
as
u\to\infty
coincides with the
asymptotics of the classical discrete ruin probability, see

[
14
,
20
]
. The derivation of the
results for case
H\leq 1/2
relies on seminal double-sum method, see, e.g.,
[
3
]
.

We organize the rest of the paper as follows. In the next
section we present necessary tools and notation. Main
results of the paper are given in Section
3
,
while the their proofs are relegated to Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

To study the asymptotics of
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)

it is worth to start with the asymptotics of
the classical continuous and discrete ruin probabilities. Let

G(\delta)=\{0,\delta,2\delta,...\}
for
\delta>0
and
G(0)=[0,\infty)
.
Define for
u,c>0
and
\delta\geq 0

\displaystyle\psi_{\delta,H}(u)=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\in G(\delta):B_{H}(t)-ct>u\right)

and
\psi_{H}(u)=\psi_{0,H}(u)
.
Define for
\delta\geq 0
and
H\in(0,1)
the
classical and discrete Pickands constants by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{2H}^{\delta}=\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac{1}{S}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,S]\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}}e^{\sqrt{2}B_{H}(t)-t^{2H}}\right)

and
\mathbb{H}_{2H}=\mathbb{H}_{2H}^{0}
.
It is known that
\mathbb{H}_{2H}^{\delta}\in(0,\infty)

for all
\delta\geq 0
and
H\in(0,1)
, see

[
21
,
4
,
22
]
.
Let in the following

C_{H}=\frac{c^{H}}{H^{H}(1-H)^{1-H}}.

The proposition below establishes the asymptotic
behavior of
\psi_{\delta,H}(u)
for
any
\delta\geq 0
:

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
\delta\geq 0
as
u\to\infty

(2.1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \psi_{\delta,H}(u)\sim\begin{cases}\mathbb{H}_{2H}\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2H}}\sqrt{\pi}}{H^{1/2}(1-H)^{1/2}}(C_{H}u^{1-H})^{1/H-1}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H>1/2\text{ or }\delta=0,\\
\mathbb{H}^{2c^{2}\delta}_{1}e^{-2cu},&H=1/2\text{ and }\delta>0\\
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}H^{H+1/2}u^{H}}{\delta c^{H+1}(1-H)^{H+1/2}}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H<1/2\text{ and }\delta>0.\end{cases}

The statement of the proposition above is given in Corollary 3.2 in
[
10
]
(case
\delta=0
) and in Theorem 2.2 in
[
14
]

(case
\delta>0
).

3.
Main Results

Define for any positive distribution
\mathcal{X}
the following
Pickands-type constant:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}}=\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac{1}{S}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:L_{\mathcal{X}}(t)\in[0,S],\sqrt{2}B(L_{\mathcal{X}}(t))-L_{\mathcal{X}}(t)>x\right)e^{x}dx,

where
L_{\mathcal{X}}(t)
is a pure jumps Lévy process
independent of
B
with jump distribution
\mathcal{X}
.
The following lemma guaranties finiteness and positivity of this constant under

A
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any positive distribution
\mathcal{X}
it holds that

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}}\leq 1
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}
has finite variance,
then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X}}\in(0,1]
.

The next theorem gives the asymptotics of
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)
for all

H\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
A
holds if
H\leq 1/2
. Then as
u\to\infty

(3.1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)\sim\begin{cases}\mathbb{H}_{2H}\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2H}}\sqrt{\pi}}{H^{1/2}(1-H)^{1/2}}(C_{H}u^{1-H})^{1/H-1}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H>1/2,\\
\mathcal{H}_{2c^{2}\mathcal{Z}}e^{-2cu},&H=1/2,\\
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}H^{H+1/2}u^{H}}{\mu c^{H+1}(1-H)^{H+1/2}}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}),&H<1/2\end{cases}

with
\mathcal{H}_{2c^{2}\mathcal{Z}}\in(0,1]
.

Remark 3.2
.

Comparing the asymptotics of
\psi_{\delta,H}(u)
in Proposition

2.1
and
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)

providing both asymptotics hold
we observe that as
u\to\infty
:

1)
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)\sim\psi_{\delta,H}(u)
for
H>1/2
and all
\delta\geq 0
;

2)
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},\frac{1}{2}}(u)\sim C_{\delta,\mathcal{Z}}\psi_{\delta,\frac{1}{2}}(u)
for all
\delta\geq 0
with some
constant
C_{\delta,\mathcal{Z}}>0
that
depends only on
\delta
and
\mathcal{Z}
;

3.1)
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)\sim\psi_{\mu,H}(u)
for
H<1/2
;

3.2)
\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)=o(\psi_{H}(u))
for
H<1/2
.

A natural question is what happens if
condition
A
does not hold for
H\leq 1/2
.
It seems to be very difficult to derive the exact asymptotics of

\pi_{H,\mathcal{Z}}(u)
in this case.
If
H<1/2
and
\mathbb{E}\{Z_{1}\}=\infty

we give the following proposition:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{E}\{Z_{1}\}=\infty
and
H<1/2
. Then as
u\to\infty

(3.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)=o(u^{H}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}))=o(\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)).

4.
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Case
H>1/2
.
Upper bound.
Since all trajectories of
L(t),t\geq 0
lay in
[0,\infty)
we obtain
for all
u\geq 0

\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)\leq\psi_{H}(u).

Lower bound. As follows from
Corollary 3.2 in
[
10
]

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B(t)-ct)>u\right)\sim\psi_{H}(u),\ \ \ u\to\infty

if
T_{u}\leq u^{2-1/H}a_{u}
with any
a_{u}\to 0
as
u\to\infty
.
Take in the following
T_{u}=u^{2-1/H}(\ln u)^{-1},u>1
.
We consider the subset
\mathcal{A}(u)
of the general probability space
consisting of all outcomes such that

\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(t)-ct>u
, the subset

\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)
consisting of
all outcomes such that
\exists t\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s)-cs)>u
and

\mathcal{A}_{L}(u)
the subset of
\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)
consisting
of all outcomes such that
\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(L(t))-cL(t)>u
.
Denote by
G_{u}(t)
the distribution function of

\inf t\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s)-cs)>u\Big|\exists z\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s_{1}\in[z,z+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s_{1})-cs_{1})>u.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)\backslash\mathcal{A}_{L}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s)-cs)>u,\forall s_{2}\geq 0:L(s_{2})\notin[t,t+T_{u}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s)-cs)>u,\forall s_{2}\geq 0:L(s_{2})\notin[t,t+T_{u}]

\displaystyle\Big|\exists z\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s_{1}\in[z,z+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s_{1})-cs_{1})>u\Big)dG_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s)-cs)>u\Big|\exists z\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s_{1}\in[z,z+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s_{1})-cs_{1})>u\Big)

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{P}\left(\forall s_{2}\geq 0:L(s_{2})\notin[t,t+T_{u}]\right)dG_{u}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall s_{2}\geq 0:L(s_{2})\notin[t,t+T_{u}]\right)

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\inf\limits_{s\in[t,t+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s)-cs)>u\Big|\exists z\geq 0:\inf\limits_{s_{1}\in[z,z+T_{u}]}(B_{H}(s_{1})-cs_{1})>u\Big)dG_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)\right)\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall s\geq 0:L(s)\notin[t,t+T_{u}]\right).

Finally,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{L}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)\backslash\mathcal{A}_{L}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\inf}(u)\right)(1-\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall s\geq 0:L(s)\notin[t,t+T_{u}]\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\psi_{H}(u)(1-\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\forall s\geq 0:L(s)\notin[t,t+T_{u}]\right)),

where the last line above follows by (
4.1
).
Since
Z_{i}
are i.i.d. and
T_{u}\to\infty
as
u\to\infty
,
the
\sup
above tends to
0
as
u
tends to
\infty
and the claim follows.

Cases
H=1/2
and
H<1/2
.

We have by the self-similarity of fBm
with
t_{0}=\frac{H}{c(1-H)}
being the unique
maxima of
\Var(\frac{B_{H}(t)}{1+ct}),V(t)=\frac{B_{H}(t)}{1+ct},\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)=L(t)/u
and

I_{u}(t_{0})=[-u^{H-1}\ln u+t_{0},t_{0}+u^{H-1}\ln u]

(4.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{Z},H}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:B_{H}(u\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>u+uc\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\frac{B_{H}(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))}{1+c\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)}>u^{1-H}\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle p(u):=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in I_{u}(t_{0}),V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>u^{1-H}\right),\ \ \ \ u\to\infty.

The last line above follows from
Borell-TIS inequality (see
[
3
]
) and the asymptotics of

p(u)
given in (
4.7
) for case
H=1/2
and in
(
4.16
) for case

H<1/2
, see the Appendix for
the detailed proof. Now we treat cases
H=1/2
and
H<1/2
separately.

Case
H=1/2
.
We apply approach from
[
19
]
.
Define
N_{u,S}=[\frac{\sqrt{u}\ln u}{S}]
for
S>0

and

c_{u,j,S}=t_{0}+jS/u,\ \ \ \Delta_{u,j,S}=[c_{u,j,S},c_{u,j+1,S}],

for
j\in[-N_{u,S},N_{u,S}]
.
We have for
S,u>0
by Bonferroni inequality

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=-N_{u,S}}^{N_{u,S}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S}:V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>\sqrt{u}\right)-\Sigma(u,S)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=-N_{u,S}-1}^{N_{u,S}+1}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S},V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>\sqrt{u}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Sigma(u,S)=\!\!\!\sum\limits_{-N_{u,S}-1\leq i<j\leq N_{u,S}+1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t,s\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S},\widetilde{L}_{u}(s)\in\Delta_{u,i,S},V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>\sqrt{u},V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(s))>\sqrt{u}\right).

Next we have as
u\to\infty
and then
S\to\infty

(4.5)

\displaystyle\Sigma(u,S)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{-N_{u,S}-1\leq i<j\leq N_{u,S}+1}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t,s\in\Delta_{u,j,S}\times\Delta_{u,i,S}:V(t)>\sqrt{u},V(s)>\sqrt{u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(e^{-2cu}),

where the last line above follows from
[
23
]
eq. (43).
Next we approximate each probability in the sum above. We have
with

and
\phi_{u,j,S}
the density of

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S},\frac{B(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))}{1+c\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)}>\sqrt{u}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S},B(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))-vc\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)>v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S},B(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))-B(c_{u,j,S})+B(c_{u,j,S})-vc(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)-c_{u,j,S})-vcc_{u,j,S}>v

\displaystyle|B(c_{u,j,S})=v-\frac{x}{v}\Big)\phi_{u,j,S}(v-\frac{x}{v})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in\Delta_{u,j,S},B(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)-c_{u,j,S})+v-\frac{x}{v}-vc(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)-c_{u,j,S})-vcc_{u,j,S}>v\right)

\displaystyle\times\phi_{u,j,S}(v-\frac{x}{v})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:L(t)\in u\Delta_{u,j,S},B(\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t))-c\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t)>x+v^{2}cc_{u,j,S}\right)\phi_{u,j,S}(v-x/v)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t)\in[0,S],B(\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t))-c\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t)>v^{2}cc_{u,j,S}+x\right)\phi_{u,j,S}(v-x/v)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t)\in[0,S],B(\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t))-c\widetilde{L}_{u,j,S}(t)>x\right)\phi_{u,j,S}(v(1+cc_{u,j,S})-x/v)dx

\displaystyle\phantom{ghjl}\sim\frac{e^{-\frac{v^{2}(1+cc_{u,j,S})^{2}}{2c_{u,j,S}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi c}}\frac{1}{v}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:L(t)\in[0,S],B(L(t))-cL(t)>x\right)e^{2cx}dx,

as
u\to\infty
and then
S\to\infty
,
we prove the last line above in the Appendix.
The last integral above equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2c}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:2c^{2}L(t)\in[0,2c^{2}S],\sqrt{2}B(2c^{2}L(t))-2c^{2}L(t)>2cx\right)e^{2cx}d(2cx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{cS}{2c^{2}S}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:2c^{2}L(t)\in[0,2c^{2}S],\sqrt{2}B(2c^{2}L(t))-2c^{2}L(t)>x\right)e^{x}dx

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2c^{2}L}\sum\limits_{j=-N_{u}}^{j=N_{u}}\frac{e^{-\frac{v^{2}(1+cc_{j})^{2}}{2c_{j}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi c}v}Sc

\displaystyle p(u)\sim\mathcal{H}_{2c^{2}L}e^{-2cu}

Case
H<1/2
.

Let
\Omega_{u}=\#\{I_{u}(t_{0})\cap\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\}
. We show in the Appendix that
for sufficiently small

We write first with
I_{u}^{-}(t_{0})=[t_{0}-u^{H-1}\ln u,t_{0}]

I_{u}^{+}(t_{0})=[t_{0},t_{0}+u^{H-1}\ln u]

(4.9)

\displaystyle q(u)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in I_{u}^{-}(t_{0}),V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>u^{1-H}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)

\displaystyle+\ \mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t)\in I_{u}^{+}(t_{0}),V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t))>u^{1-H}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle q_{-}(u)+q_{+}(u),\ \ u\to\infty,

proof is given in the Appendix.
Next we compute the asymptotic of
q_{+}(u)

\displaystyle u^{2-1/H}(\ln u)^{1/H}u^{\max(H+\varepsilon_{1},1/H-2+\varepsilon_{2})}C\ln u=o(u^{H}),\ \ u\to\infty.\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \hfill\Box

Proof of (
4.9
).

To prove the claim in view of the final asymptotic of

p(u)
given in (
4.16
) we need to show that
as
u\to\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\exists t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0:\widetilde{L}_{u}(t_{1})\in I_{u}^{-}(t_{0}),\widetilde{L}_{u}(t_{2})\in I_{u}^{+}(t_{0}),V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t_{1}))>u^{1-H},V(\widetilde{L}_{u}(t_{2}))>u^{1-H}

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\Big)\!=o(u^{H}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})).

Define for
j<0

S_{u,j}=-\frac{1}{u}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{|j|}Z^{\prime}_{k},

where
Z_{k}^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
Z_{k}
. We have that the probability
above does not exceed

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{-w\leq j\leq 0,0\leq i\leq w}\mathbb{P}\left(V(t_{0}+S_{u,j})>u^{1-H},V(t_{0}+S_{u,i})>u^{1-H}\right).

Repeating the proof of (
4.11
) we obtain the claim,
the only difference is that in (
5.6
) the index of summation
changes to
-u^{\max(H+\varepsilon_{1},1/H-2+\varepsilon_{2})}\leq j\leq 0,0\leq i\leq u^{\max(H+\varepsilon_{1},1/H-2+\varepsilon_{2})}
.
\Box

Proof of (
4.13
).

We have with
F_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}
the df of
\widetilde{N}_{i}

for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}S_{u,i}^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{\widetilde{N}_{i}}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|z|\leq i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{|z|>i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z).

The second integral does not exceed

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(|\widetilde{N}_{i}|>i/\ln i\right)\leq C\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})i^{-1}\ln^{2}i,

where the last inequality follows from Chebyshev’s inequality.
Thus,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq w}\int\limits_{|z|>i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq w}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})i^{-1}\ln^{2}i

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\ln^{3}u\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}).

For the first integral in (
5.7
) we have for any small

\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}>0

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|z|\leq i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1+\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|z|\leq i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{|z|\leq i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1-\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z).

The upper bound in the inequality above equals

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1-\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)(1-\mathbb{P}\left(|\widetilde{N}_{i}|>i/\ln i\right))\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right).

By Chebyshev’s inequality and the proof of
(
4.8
) we have
uniformly for
i>u^{H/2}

1-u^{-H/4}\leq 1-\mathbb{P}\left(|\widetilde{N}_{i}|>i/\ln i\right)\leq 1,\ \ \ 1-u^{-H}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)\leq 1.

Thus, by the line above

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq w}\int\limits_{|z|\leq i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\sum\limits_{u^{H/4}\leq i\leq w}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1-\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)+O(u^{H/4}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq w}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1-\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)+Cu^{H/4}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}).

Similarly for the lower bound we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq w}\int\limits_{|z|\leq i/\ln i}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}(\frac{z}{u}+\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)dF_{\widetilde{N}_{i}}(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\sum\limits_{u^{H/4}\leq i\leq w}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1+\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq w}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1+\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)-Cu^{H/4}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}).

Finally by (
5.8
) we have

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq w}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1+\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)-Cu^{H/4}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}S_{u,i}^{2}\text{ and }\Omega_{u}\in[\varepsilon_{0}u^{H}\ln u,w]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\!\!\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq w}\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}>C_{H}u^{1-H}+u^{1-H}f_{-}((1-\bar{\varepsilon}_{1})\frac{\mu i}{u})^{2}\right)+Cu^{\frac{H}{4}}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})+C\ln^{3}u\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H}).

Repeating the calculations for the sums above as in the proof of Theorem

3.1
after
(
4.13
) and then letting
\varepsilon_{1}\to 0
we
obtain the claim, since

u^{H/4}\Psi(C_{H}u^{1-H})=o(p(u)),\ u\to\infty

by (
4.16
).

\Box

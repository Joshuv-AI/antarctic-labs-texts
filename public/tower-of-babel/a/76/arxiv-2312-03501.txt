2.
Generalities and strategy

First we define the property of group varieties in which we are interested.

Definition 1
.

An exact sequence of group varieties
\displaystyle e\to N\overset{\iota}{\to}G\overset{\pi}{\to}Q\to e
has
\displaystyle(*)
if there is an isomorphism of graded
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-algebras
\displaystyle s\otimes\pi^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(N)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{*}(Q)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
where
\displaystyle s
is a section to
\displaystyle\iota^{*}
.

The aim of this document is to show that any group variety has this property and hence to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

An exact sequence of group varieties
\displaystyle e\to N\to G\to Q\to e
has
\displaystyle(*)
.

We now state two known lemmas that are key for proving Theorem
Theorem
. The first one is a structure theorem on algebraic groups (see
[
11
]
).

Lemma 3
.

The following statements about group varieties are true:

•

A group variety
\displaystyle G
is an extension of an abelian variety
\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{ab}}
by a linear group variety
\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{lin}}
.

•

A linear group variety is the extension of a reductive group variety
\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{red}}
by a unipotent group variety
\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{\text{u}}(G)
.

•

A reductive group variety
\displaystyle G
is the extension of a semisimple group variety
\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{ss}}
by a torus
\displaystyle\mathrm{R}(G)

•

A group variety
\displaystyle G
has a largest normal subgroup variety
\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{ant}}
, called the anti-affine group variety of
\displaystyle G
, such that
\displaystyle G/G_{\mathrm{ant}}
is affine.

The second lemma says that an isogeny induces an isomorphism on cohomology, which is a well known fact.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\displaystyle\varphi:G\to H
be an isogeny of group varieties. Then
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(H)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for separable and purely inseparable isogenies. In the purely inseparable case,
\displaystyle\ker(\varphi)
is connected, so
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/l^{n}\mathbb{Z}\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathbb{Z}/l^{n}\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism of sheaves. Since
\displaystyle\ker(\varphi)
is zero dimensional, the Leray spectral sequence map
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{n}(H,\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathbb{Z}/l^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\varphi^{*}\mathbb{Z}/l^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism, which gives the result.

In the separable case,
\displaystyle\ker(\varphi)
may be thought of as a finite group
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle H
is identified with
\displaystyle G/M
. For any abelian sheaf
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
the map
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(H,\mathcal{F})\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(G,\varphi^{*}\mathcal{F})\cong\mathrm{H}^{*}(H,\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathcal{F})
is induced by
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathcal{F}
, which identifies
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=(\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathcal{F})^{M}
, see (
[
16
]
(5.10)). Then there exists a map
\displaystyle\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
given by
\displaystyle x\mapsto\sum_{m\in M}m\cdot\penalty\ x
, called the transfer map. Applying this to the sheaves
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/l^{n}\mathbb{Z}
and passing to
\displaystyle l
-adic cohomology gives a map
\displaystyle N:\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(H)
such that
\displaystyle N\circ\varphi^{*}
is multiplication by
\displaystyle\#M
and
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\circ N
maps
\displaystyle x\mapsto\sum_{m\in M}m\cdot x
. This first point gives that
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}
is injective and the second point gives that under
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}
,
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(H)
is identified with
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)^{M}
.

Since the action of
\displaystyle M
on
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
is induced by the one of
\displaystyle G(k)
and
\displaystyle G
is a connected algebraic group, the action is trivial on compactly supported cohomology (
[
7
]
Proposition 6.4). When combined with the functoriality of the Poincaré duality pairing, this gives that
\displaystyle M
acts trivially on
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
, which gives the result.
∎

Strategy 6
.

The strategy for proving Theorem

will be as follows:

(1)

We show that the exact sequence

\displaystyle e\to\mathrm{R}_{\text{u}}(G)\to G\to G_{\mathrm{red}}\to e

(2)

\displaystyle(*)

(3)

\displaystyle e\to N_{\mathrm{lin}}\to G_{\mathrm{lin}}\to Q_{\mathrm{lin}}\to e

We know quite a bit about
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
for
\displaystyle G
a general group variety. For
\displaystyle\mu:G\times_{k}G\to G
the multiplication,
\displaystyle\mathrm{inv}:G\to G

\displaystyle e:\mathrm{Spec}(k)\to G
the identity, the data
\displaystyle(\mathrm{H}^{*}(G),\mu^{*},\mathrm{inv}^{*},e^{*})
defines a
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-Hopf-algebra, where one uses the Künneth isomorphism
\displaystyle p_{1}^{*}\cup p_{2}^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(G\times_{k}G)
(see
[

]
for an introduction to Hopf-algebras). An important subspace of a Hopf-algebra is the primitive subspace.

Definition 7

Let
\displaystyle(\mathrm{H},\Delta,\iota,\epsilon)
be a Hopf algebra over a field
\displaystyle l
. Then the

of
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}
is defined by
\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}:=\{x\in\mathrm{H}\,|\,\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+1\otimes x\}

Note that a homomorphism of Hopf-algebras preserves the primitive subspace. Since
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
is finite dimensional over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{l}
and graded-commutative we can use the following theorem of Hopf.

Theorem 8

(Part 2.5D
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}
be a graded commutative Hopf-algebra over a field
\displaystyle l
with
\displaystyle\mathrm{char}(l)=0
such that
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{n}
is finite dimensional for all
\displaystyle n\geq 0
. Then there is a natural isomorphism of graded
\displaystyle k
-algebras
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}=\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}:=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}\bigwedge^{r}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}
, where for
\displaystyle\{z_{i_{j}}\}_{1\leq j\leq r}
homogeneous elements of
\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}
of degree
\displaystyle i_{j}
, the element
\displaystyle z_{i_{1}}\wedge...\wedge z_{i_{r}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}i_{j}

In particular we have
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)=\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
.

Using the primitive subspace allows us to formulate the property
\displaystyle(*)
in a simpler way.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\displaystyle e\to N\overset{\iota}{\to}G\overset{\pi}{\to}Q\to e
be an exact sequence of group varieties. The sequence has
\displaystyle(*)
if and only if
\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(Q)\overset{\pi^{*}}{\to}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)\overset{\iota^{*}}{\to}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(N)\to 0
is an exact sequence of graded
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-vector spaces.

Proof.

If the sequence of primitive subspaces is exact, then any section
\displaystyle s
to
\displaystyle\iota^{*}
induces an isomorphism:

\mathrm{H}^{*}(N)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{*}(Q)=\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(N)\otimes\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(Q)\overset{\pi^{*}\otimes s}{\to}\bigwedge^{*}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)=\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)

Conversely if the sequence has
\displaystyle(*)
, then note that pullback homomorphisms give Hopf-algebra homomorphisms on cohomology, which preserve primitive subspaces. It follows that
\displaystyle\iota^{*}:\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)\overset{\iota^{*}}{\to}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(N)
is surjective and that
\displaystyle\pi^{*}:\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(Q)\overset{\pi^{*}}{\to}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
is injective. Moreover
\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(Q)+\dim\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(N)=\mathrm{P}\mathrm{H}^{*}(G)
and
\displaystyle\ker(\pi^{*})\subset\mathrm{Im}(\pi^{*})
, so we obtain an exact sequence of primitive subspaces.
∎

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})=1

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n})=n
by the Künneth formula, so indeed the above generalization does not hold.

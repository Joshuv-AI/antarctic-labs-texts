Four Algorithms on the Swapped Dragonfly


Abstract
A Swapped Dragonfly with M M routers per group and K K global ports per router is denoted D ​ 3 ​ ( K , M ) D3(K,M) . A Swapped Dragonfly with K K and M M restricted is studied in this paper. There are four cases. In each case the resulting Swapped Dragonfly has a special utility: • If K = L 2 K=L^{2} , an L ​ M × L ​ M LM\times LM matrix product can be performed in L ​ M LM rounds. An n × n n\times n matrix product may be performed in n 2 / L ​ M n^{2}/LM rounds. Each round takes four router hops. • If K = k ​ s K=ks and M = m ​ s M=ms an all-to-all exchange may be performed in K ​ M 2 / s KM^{2}/s rounds. An all-to-all exchange of size n ≥ K ​ M 2 n\geq KM^{2} may be performed in n 2 / K ​ M 2 ​ s n^{2}/KM^{2}s rounds. • If K = 2 k K=2^{k} and M = 2 m M=2^{m} , a dilation three emulation of the ( k + 2 ​ m ) (k+2m) dimension Boolean hypercube exists. An ascend-descend algorithm may be performed at twice the cost on the hypercube. • D ​ 3 ​ ( K , M ) D3(K,M) contains M M adjacent depth four edge-disjoint spanning trees. Equipped with a synchronized source-vector packet header, it can perform n n broadcast in 3 ​ n / M 3n/M rounds. The rounds in these algorithms are free of link conflicts. These results are compared with algorithms on a hypercube. Comparisons with the fully populated Dragonfly are also made.These results are more applicable than the special cases because D ​ 3 ​ ( K , M ) D3(K,M) contains emulations of every Swapped Dragonfly with J ≤ K J\leq K and/or L ≤ M L\leq M . The Swapped Dragonfly is a new approach to the Dragonfly concept. It behaves much like a three-dimensional mesh. The underlying graph is defined before the network is defined. The underlying graph of a Dragonfly is not defined until ports are identified on routers and links are defined by connections with ports.

It has been shown that there are three constraints on the parameters

1.

On

On

3.

4.

Additionally, it has been shown that equipping

Result 2 is the only algorithm to do an all-to-all on

Source-vector routing is used to define the algorithms in 1, 2, and 4. It leads to algorithms devoid of interround conflicts. Source-vectors can be defined on a Maximal Dragonfly. However, a vector
(\gamma,\pi,\delta)
generally leads to a link conflict at the third hop when it is used by two routers. This produces interround conflicts which lead to hotspots in an application. On a Dragonfly, the algorithms studied here would be used in a deflective routing environment. Vectors would be converted to destinations.

Note that

is both a

and a

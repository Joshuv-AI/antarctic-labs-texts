Scaling of kinematical, global observables, energy and entropy densities in p + p \rm p\!+\!p , p + Pb \rm p\!+\!Pb and Pb + Pb \rm Pb\!+\!Pb collisions from 0.01 to 13 TeV {\rm TeV} Journal: J. Phys. G: Nucl. Part. Phys.
E. Cuautle, E. D. Rosas, M. Rodríguez-Cahuantzi

Abstract
The multiplicity and average transverse momentum of the charged and identified particles produced in different kinds of colliding systems are an example of global observables used to characterize events over a wide range of energy. Studying these observables provides insights into the collective phenomena and the geometric scaling properties of the systems created in ultra-relativistic p + Pb \rm p\!+\!Pb , Pb + Pb \rm Pb\!+\!Pb , and even in p + p \rm p\!+\!p collisions. The first part of this work presents a study of these variables using different Monte Carlo event generators. It analyzes their sensitivity to find collective phenomena at 0.01, 0.9, 2.76, 7, and 13 TeV {\rm TeV} , finding a less satisfactory description as the energy decreases. The second part analyzes the average transverse momentum of charged hadrons as a function of the multiplicity for p + p \rm p\!+\!p , p + Pb \rm p\!+\!Pb , and Pb + Pb \rm Pb\!+\!Pb data from the CMS \rm{CMS} and ALICE \rm{ALICE} experiments. Comparing with Monte Carlo event generators, we look for a possible scaling law of average transverse momentum scaled to the overlap transverse collision area. Additionally, the experimental data are used to compute thermodynamical quantities such as the energy and entropy densities in the Bjorken approach. The results are compared with predictions from EPOS \rm{EPOS} and PYTHIA \rm{PYTHIA} Monte Carlo event generators. We observe an excellent agreement for ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle from p + p \rm p\!+\!p but not for thermodynamical observables, where a sudden rise in a small ⟨ p T ⟩ \langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle range resembles the lattice QCD results for the ϵ / T 4 \epsilon/T^{4} as a function of the temperature; however, only the experimental data from p + p \rm p\!+\!p show a kind of saturation.



Keywords: proton-proton, heavy ion collision; energy density, entropy density, multiplicity, transverse momentum.

1
Introduction

The CERN proton anti-proton collider results have shown that the flattening of the average transverse momentum (
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
) as a function of the charged multiplicity (
\rm{N_{ch}}
), observed in the central rapidity region, may serve as a probe for the equation of state of hot hadronic matter. These results constitute a possible signal for a phase transition in hadronic collisions
[
1
]
.

In general, measurements in the low-momentum regime provide essential information
to describe strong interactions using the non-perturbative region of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD); QCD-inspired models are usually implemented in Monte Carlo event generators. Additionally, these kinds of measurements are used to constrain the free parameters of such models.

Furthermore,
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
studies as a function of collision energy and multiplicity distributions provide more detailed information on the kinematic processes of colliding systems. Thermodynamical quantities describing the system created during the collisions are estimated using the transverse momentum of hadrons
[
2
]
.

Jet production seems to explain the increase of
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
with multiplicity. Jets are responsible for the first rise since hard processes dominate over soft ones in this multiplicity region
[
3
]
. A second rise seems to appear due to jet production and fragmentation; this behavior is consistent with results obtained at Tevatron energies. However, this behavior is not observed
at higher energies. Moreover, the energy dependence has been studied by the CDF experiment that reported
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
as a function of multiplicity for minimum bias events at 630 and 1800
{\rm GeV}
, observing and increase with energy and multiplicity for central pseudorapidities. It has also been observed that
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
for soft events is not energy dependent whereas for hard events it increases faster as the energy of the colliding system increases
[
4
]
.

The behavior of
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
is almost linear at low collision energies
[
5
]
it grows faster for higher energies, where it can be described using a second order logarithmic polynomial as a function of the collision energy
[
6
,
7
]
. For identified charged particles the
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
exhibits also a mass dependence.
This behavior can not be explained
[
8
]
by the default tune of the
\rm{PYTHIA}
event generator which overestimates the transverse momentum of ATLAS results
[
9
]
for values larger than 40 in charged particle multiplicity, in the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<2.5
.
Alternative models such as the Color Glass Condensate (CGC)
[
10
]
, where the
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
is a universal function of the ratio of the multiplicity density and the transverse area of the collision (
S_{\rm{T}}
)
[
11
]
qualitatively reproduce the experimental data. However, this geometrical model seems to describe the
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
at larger multiplicities for
\rm p\!+\!p
and
\rm p\!+\!Pb
collisions but fails for lower multiplicities, and neither can describe
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
for
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions. A possible reason for this poor description is that collective effects may be present in
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
, and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
systems, since the CGC is just a geometric model, and it is not expected to reproduce the data. It is worth mentioning that in
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions, it has been suggested that there are flow-like
[
12
]
effects in high multiplicity events at
\rm{LHC}
energies.
The
\rm{ALICE}
collaboration at the LHC has measured the
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle

[
13
]
of charged hadrons from
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions at energies of
\sqrt{s}
= 0.9, 2.76, and 7
{\rm TeV}
in the kinematical range
0.15<p_{\rm{T}}<10.0

{\rm GeV}/c
and
|\eta|<0.3
. ALICE selected all the charged prompt particles produced in
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions including all decay products, except those from weak decays of strange hadrons. The results show a correlation of
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
with
\rm{N_{ch}}
which is stronger for
\rm p\!+\!p
than for
\rm p\!+\!Pb
and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions. The observed strong correlation has been attributed within
\rm{PYTHIA}
to Color Reconnections (CR) between hadronization strings
[
14
,
15
]
, which are directly related with multiple parton interactions.

Furthermore, the observed enhancement of
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
versus multiplicity at low
p_{\rm{T}}
is not reproduced by any model
[
7
]
.
These kinematic and global observables have been associated in simulation studies to QGP formation in proton-proton collisions
[
16
]
. The relationship between multiplicity and multiple parton interactions
[
17
]
have been studied in an attemp to explain experimental results. An incoherent superposition of such interactions would lead to a constant
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
at high multiplicities.

In high multiplicity
\rm p\!+\!p
events, a medium governed by strong interactions is produced
[
18
]
, thus, the produced matter can be described in terms of quark and gluon degree of freedom. The equation of state, relating the energy and entropy densities, pressure, and temperature of such matter, is of fundamental importance to understand its composition and the static and dynamical properties of the medium created in
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions at different energies.
In this work we address these issues. The paper is organized as follows:
A brief presentation of hadronization models used by
\rm{PYTHIA}
and
\rm{EPOS}
event generators is provided in section II. Results and their comparison with experimental data and the average transverse momentum versus multiplicity scaled to the transverse collision area are presented in section III: Subsection III A describes results on multiplicity and average transverse momentum versus multiplicity, and the multiplicity scaled to the transverse collision area obtained from event generators. Subsection III B is devoted to investigate the equation of state and a possible phase transition of QGP through the analysis of the energy and entropy densities, scaled by
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle^{4}
and
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle^{3}
, respectively, for charged and identified particles. Finally, the discussions and conclusions are drawn in section IV.

2
Modeling parameters of the hadroproduction

Hadronization processes are one of the most widely studied topics. Despite these efforts, currently we only have effective theories to describe these phenomena. Different event generators have been developed to investigate hadronization processes in
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
, and ion-ion collision systems. Each event generator approaches hadron production mechanisms in a different manner but most of them are based on string fragmentation models at high energies. Some general aspects are briefly described in the following lines:

\rm{PYTHIA}
hadronization processes include the CR mechanisms providing an alternative final interaction mechanism, modifying some kinematical variables of the hadron-hadron collisions and lepton collisions. These final state interactions allow a better description of the transverse momentum measured by the
\rm{ALICE}
experiment.
Color Reconnection is based on the production of hadrons through the interaction probability between partons of low and high
p_{T}
.
These interacting partons can come from the beam or from the remnant of the beam. They can be quantified by the probability distribution of reconnecting partons,
P_{rec}(p_{T})
, defined as:

P_{rec}(p_{T})=\frac{(R_{rec}\;p_{T0})^{2}}{(R_{rec}\;p_{T0})^{2}+p_{T}^{2}},

(1)

where the range of CR,
0\leq R_{rec}\leq 10
, is a phenomenological parameter and
p_{T0}
is an energy dependent parameter used to damp the low
p_{T}
divergence of the
2\rightarrow 2
QCD cross section.
Equation (
1
) used for the description of the color flow, is based on the Multiple Parton Interaction model. However, there are other newer approaches
[
19
]
, or even older ones such as those developed to describe
W
bosons in
e^{-}+e^{-}
processes
[
20
]
, each model contains additional parameters to handle CR effects.

Hadron-hadron collisions consider a spherically symmetric matter distribution within each hadron,
\rho({\bf x})d^{3}x=\rho(r)d^{3}x
. In collision processes, the overlap of the colliding hadrons is a function of the impact parameter (
b
) and is given by:

O(b)\propto\int\int d^{3}xdt\rho(x,y,z)\rho(x,y,z-\sqrt{b^{2}-t^{2}}),

(2)

where different
\rho
distributions have been studied previously
[
21
]
.
Taking a simple Gaussian for this
\rho
, the convolution becomes trivial, but it does not give a good enough description of the data. A better choice of this matter distribution is a double Gaussian function given by:

\rho(r)=(1-\beta)\frac{1}{r_{1}^{3}}\exp(\frac{r^{2}}{r_{1}^{3}})+\beta\frac{1}{r_{2}^{3}}\exp(-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}),

(3)

this equation corresponds to a distribution with a small core region of radius
r_{2}
and containing a fraction (
\beta
) of the total hadronic matter, embedded in a larger hadron of radius
r_{1}
. Two free parameters
\beta
and
r_{2}/r_{1}
are sufficient to give the necessary flexibility.

This way of modeling the overlap matter distribution allows the study of hadron-hadron collisions as a function of impact parameter as for heavy-ion collisions. Thus kinematical variables like
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
in hadron production can also be studied in terms of the impact parameter
[
21
]
.

\rm{EPOS}
simulates interactions among ions as binary interactions, each represented by a parton ladder; this ladder considers a longitudinal color field, conveniently treated as a relativistic string. The string decays via the production of quark anti-quark pairs creating string fragments usually identified with hadrons
[
22
]
.

Our
\rm{EPOS}

\rm p\!+\!p
simulation at the energy of
\sqrt{(}s)=10

{\rm GeV}
used the version 1.99, and for larger than 10
{\rm GeV}
,
\rm{EPOS}
-LHC was used. From now on, these choices are referred to as
\rm{EPOS}
. Both versions include a simplified treatment of final state interaction but do not include full hydro as is already possible in most recent
\rm{EPOS}
3 not released version.

\rm{EPOS-LHC}
is a minimum bias hadronic generator used for heavy-ion collisions and cosmic ray air shower development. However, the goal of this generator is to describe soft particle production (
p_{\rm{T}}

<\sim
5
{\rm GeV}/c
) for any system and energy in great detail. Moreover, high-density effects leading to collective behavior in heavy-ion collisions are taken into account
[
23
]
by the fusion model, which means that there is the probability to form a core and a corona, even in proton-proton collisions. The model considers the hadronization part of the secondary particles, and the flow intensity includes different radial flow types that depend only on the total mass of the high density core produced by the overlap of the string segments due to multiple parton interactions in proton-proton or multiple nucleon interactions. The model also includes different radial flow types in the core created in a small volume due to multiple scattering between partons in a single pair of nucleons, which is the case of
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions.

Results

Results presented in this work correspond to
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
, and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions at different energies, generated with
\rm{PYTHIA}
, and
\rm{EPOS}

\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
with respect to the
\rm p\!+\!p

\rm{CMS}
and
\rm{ALICE}
data.

3.2
Experimental equation of state in
\rm p\!+\!p
and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions

Thermodynamical quantities like energy and entropy densities, temperature, and pressure, are related by an equation of state to describe the evolution of the medium created in heavy-ion collisions and also in high multiplicity
\rm p\!+\!p
events. Hydrodynamic theory has been used to get the energy density, entropy, temperature and other quantities as a first description approach to
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions
[
35
]
. Different observables are analyzed to explore the formation of QGP-droplets in
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions
[
36
]
. The HotQCD Collaboration has developed an equation of state in (2+1) flavor QCD
[
37
]
which gives a prediction for the energy density divided by temperature
T^{4}
(
\epsilon/T^{4}
) as a function of
T
. Similar results have been obtained
[
38
]
using the Color String Percolation Model, which allow to compute hydrodynamical properties
[
2
]
in
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions.

Analyzing data from the
\rm p\!+\!p
and
\rm p\!+\!\bar{p}
collisions, the equation of state can be estimated
[
39
]
by combining
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
versus multiplicity distribution measurements.

Results from heavy-ion collisions can be well described by hydrodynamics, however, this theory does not apply to results from
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions. Nevertheless,
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions at high energy produce a high particle density, and the mean free path of particles in such a system is very small compared to its dimension. The created system expands, and a fraction of the expansion processes must have hydrodynamical characteristics. Under this consideration the initial entropy density estimated by Landau
[
40
]
is proportional to the multiplicity (
s_{0}=kdN_{ch}/d\eta
, where
k
is a constant) which allows to compute the entropy
[
41
]
for a mini-QGP system.
Statistical models can be used to analyze the multiplicity densities and then get thermodynamical limits, in
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions. In particular, the
\epsilon/T^{4}
as a function of multiplicity tends to the same value
[
42
]
when it is calculated from different statistical ensembles. The shear viscosity, isothermal compressibility and speed of sound have also been calculated
[
43
]
from
\rm{ALICE}

\rm p\!+\!p
results.

This work presents the extraction of the thermodynamical properties from
\rm{CMS}
and
\rm{ALICE}
data, starting from the average transverse momentum as a function of multiplicity. At the same time, the results are compared to those from event generators.

The Bjorken energy density (
\epsilon_{Bj}
)
[
44

[

46

\epsilon_{Bj}\simeq\frac{3}{2}\frac{\langle p_{T}\rangle\frac{dN}{d\eta}}{S_{T}\cdot\tau}.

(8)

In this equation,
S_{\rm{T}}
is the interaction area calculated as in the previous section, and
\tau
is the proper time in the Bjorken model, so the product
S_{\rm{T}}\cdot\tau
is the volume occupied by the particles. Using Eq. (
8
), we compute the energy density for charged hadrons from
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
, and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
data at different energies measured by
\rm{ALICE}
. The results as a function of the multiplicity scaled by
S_{\rm{T}}
are shown in Fig. 7 (a), where a clear scaling law is observed at low multiplicity for all colliding systems. At high multiplicity, the energy density shows differences that may come from the measured radii. Analysis of the energy density for identified particles produced in
\rm p\!+\!Pb
is shown in Fig. 7 (b), where higher values for the heaviest particles are evident. It is possible to add the energy by particle species and the total approximately corresponds to the results for all charged hadrons.

4
Conclusions and remarks

The first part of this work presents an analysis of the correlation between transverse momentum and multiplicity distributions for different colliding systems at energies of 0.01, 0.9, 2.76, 5.0, 7.0, and 13
{\rm TeV}
using two event generators:
\rm{PYTHIA}
with/without CR;
\rm{EPOS}
with/without fusion model as part of the hadronization. The second part of the work carried out a data analysis of the average transverse momentum versus multiplicity from
\rm{ALICE}
and
\rm{CMS}
experiments to extract thermodynamical properties of the system created in
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
, and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions at different collision energies. The results were compared with predictions from
\rm{EPOS}
and
\rm{PYTHIA}
event generators.

Average multiplicity distributions increase with the collision energy and can be parameterized as
\langle N_{\rm{ch}}\rangle=a+b\cdot p_{\rm{T}}^{c}
, where
a,b
, and
c
, are free parameters for each energy. The
\langle N_{\rm{ch}}\rangle
is observed to rise more rapidly with increasing the transverse momentum for higher collision energies.
The hadronization mechanism incorporated through CR in
\rm{PYTHIA}
produces a more substantial reduction in multiplicity than the
\rm{EPOS}
fusion model; this effect is more visible for transverse momentum larger than 0.5
{\rm GeV}/c
. On the other hand, the average transverse momentum as a function of the multiplicity, computed with
\rm{PYTHIA}
, shows that a differential analysis in terms of impact parameter is not enough to explain the data.
The CR with different strengths is enough to explain the behavior of data at collision energies of 0.9, 2.76, and 7
{\rm TeV}
reported by the
\rm{ALICE}
experiment. This has been tested for other energies finding good agreement. Nonetheless, in the case of the
\rm{EPOS}
fusion model with a combination of impact parameters, which is related to hadronic matter model in the proton, it allows an excellent description of the data, although
\rm{EPOS}
results without cut on impact parameter are within experimental uncertainties.

We have shown that the average transverse momentum and Bjorken energy density as a function of multiplicity, normalized to the transverse interaction area, present a scaling law. The results were computed with
\rm{ALICE}
data,
\rm{PYTHIA}
, and
\rm{EPOS}
event generators. For all cases,
the
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
scaling breaks in the lowest and highest multiplicity values, which can not be associated with flow effects, as other authors have suggested.

Related to thermodynamical properties obtained with an analysis of
\rm{CMS}
and
\rm{ALICE}
data: the results indicate an exponential growth of the energy and entropy normalized to
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle^{3}
as a function of the average transverse momentum for charged and identified particles measured in
\rm p\!+\!p
,
\rm p\!+\!Pb
, and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
collisions at the available energies. This growth is faster for light particles with respect to heavy ones.
Considering
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
as a linear function of the initial temperature of the system created in the collisions, the conclusions obtained from
\epsilon_{Bj}/\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle^{4}
are similar to those from
\epsilon_{Bj}/T^{4}
, a quantity widely studied to look for a QCD phase transition. The same conclusion can be reached from
\sigma/T^{3}
.
Analysis of experimental
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
data with
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle=3T
produces an effective temperature range of 0.17-0.2
{\rm GeV}/c
, where the energy density normalized to
T^{4}
has a linear behavior, and for effective temperatures larger than 0.2
{\rm GeV}/c
, it grows exponentially. Our results from
\rm{EPOS}
and
\rm{PYTHIA}

\rm p\!+\!p
collisions agree with
\rm{ALICE}

\rm p\!+\!p
data and have the behavior obtained by lattice (2+1) flavor QCD. They show a kind of saturation for
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
larger than 0.8
{\rm GeV}/c
, which is not observed for either
\rm p\!+\!Pb
and
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb

\rm{ALICE}
data.
The predictions from
\rm{PYTHIA}
and
\rm{EPOS}
show the data trend, but there is no complete agreement to
\rm{ALICE}
data. Particularly, the
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
in
\rm Pb\!+\!Pb
from
\rm{EPOS}
does not describe the data at the most central pseudorapidity and consequently produces two values of the entropy density for one
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
value, which is not a physical result.

Results for other thermodynamical variables require a more detailed analysis of the relation between
\langle p_{\rm{T}}\rangle
and temperature and more precision on the radii measurements to look for a quantitative possible QCD phase transition.
It is important to remember that, the results on the energy and entropy densities are calculated using simple models of Landau and Bjorken, respectively, based on hydrodynamical approaches. Consequently, our conclusions can thus be considered as qualitative results that could be improved using more realistic models in the calculations, hadronic matter in the proton, and models for the core created in
\rm p\!+\!p
collisions.

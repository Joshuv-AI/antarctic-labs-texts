1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN for short) inequality:

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b(p+1)}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\leq C_{a,b,N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle N\geq 3,\quad-\infty<a<\frac{N-2}{2},\quad a\leq b\leq a+1\quad\text{and}\quad p=\frac{N+2(1+a-b)}{N-2(1+a-b)},

(1.2)

u\in D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the Hilbert space given by

\displaystyle D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}|\nabla u|^{2}dx<+\infty\}

(1.3)

with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-2a}\nabla u\nabla vdx

and
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\dot{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
being the usual homogeneous Sobolev space (cf.
[
15
, Definition 2.1]
).

(
1.1
) is established in the celebrated paper
[
4
]
by Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg, as it is named, for a much more general version. Moreover, as pointed out in
[
5
]
, a fundamental task in understanding the CKN inequality (
1.1
) is to study the best constants, existence (and nonexistence) of extremal functions, as well as their qualitative properties for parameters
a
and
b
in the full region (
1.2
), since (
1.1
) contains the classical Sobolev inequality (
a=b=0
) and the classical Hardy inequality (
a=0,b=1
) as special cases, which have played important roles in many applications by virtue of the complete knowledge about the best constants, extremal functions, and their qualitative properties.

Under the condition (
1.2
), it is well-known (cf.
[
1
,
6
,
5
,
17
,
23
]
) that (
1.1
) has extremal functions if and only if either for
a<b<a+1
with
a<0
or for
a\leq b<a+1
with
a\geq 0
. Moreover, let

\displaystyle b_{FS}(a)=\frac{N(a_{c}-a)}{2\sqrt{(a_{c}-a)^{2}+(N-1)}}+a-a_{c}>a

(1.4)

be the Felli-Schneider curve found in
[
16
]
,
then it is also well-known (cf.
[
1
,
6
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
23
]
) that up to dilations
\tau^{a_{c}-a}u(\tau x)
and scalar multiplications
Cu(x)
(also up to translations
u(x+y)
in the special case
a=b=0
), (
1.1
) has a unique extremal function

\displaystyle W(x)=(2(p+1)(a_{c}-a)^{2})^{\frac{1}{(p-1)}}\bigg(1+|x|^{(a_{c}-a)(p-1)}\bigg)^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}

(1.5)

either for
b_{FS}(a)\leq b<a+1
with
a<0
or for
a\leq b<a+1
with
a\geq 0
while, extremal functions of (
1.1
) must be non-radial for
a<b<b_{FS}(a)
with
a<0
. Here, for the sake of simplicity, we denote
a_{c}=\frac{N-2}{2}
, as that in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
. On the other hand, it has been proved in
[

]
that extremal functions of (
1.1
) must have

symmetry for
a<b<b_{FS}(a)
with
a<0
, that is, extremal functions of (

and the angle
\theta_{N}
between the positive
x_{N}
-axis and
\overrightarrow{Ox}
for
a<b<b_{FS}(a)
with
a<0

1.1
) is unique or not for
a<b<b_{FS}(a)
with
a<0
is still unknown.

Once the extremal functions of (
1.1
) are well understood, it is natural to study quantitative stability for the CKN inequality (
1.1
) by asking whether the deviation of a given function from attaining equality in (
1.1
) controls its distance to the family of extremal functions. These studies were initialed by Brezís and Lieb in
[
3
]
by raising an open question for the classical Sobolev inequality (

[
2
]
while, quantitative stability for the Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
a=0
,

[

]

Since the extremal function of (

) is unique up to dilations
\tau^{a_{c}-a}u(\tau x)
and scalar multiplications
Cu(x)
either for
b_{FS}(a)\leq b<a+1
with
a<0
or for
a\leq b<a+1
with
a\geq 0
and
a+b>0
,
the smooth manifold

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\{cW_{\tau}(x)\mid c\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}\text{ and }\tau>0\}

is all extremal functions of (

a=b=0
) and the Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
a=0
,

) to the CKN inequality (
1.1
) in the above parameter region. Our first result in this paper, which devoted to this aspect, can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
b_{FS}(a)

be the Felli-Schneider curve given by (
1.4
) and assume that either

(1)

b_{FS}(a)\leq b<a+1
with
a<0
or

(2)

a\leq b<a+1
with
a\geq 0
and
a+b>0
.

Then

\displaystyle dist_{D^{1,2}_{a}}^{2}(u,\mathcal{Z})\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-C_{a,b,N}^{-1}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})}

for all
u\in D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the usual weighted Lebesgue space and its usual norm is given by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b(p+1)}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}.

Remark 1.1
.

The conditions
b_{FS}(a)\leq b<a+1
with
a<0
and
a\leq b<a+1
with
a\geq 0
and
a+b>0
in Theorem

with
a<0
, the extremal function of (

) is no longer

.

On the other hand, it is well-known that the Euler-Lagrange equation of the Sobolev inequality (
a=b=0
) is given by

\displaystyle-\Delta u=|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u,\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}

(1.6)

and the Aubin-Talanti bubbles, given by

\displaystyle U[z,\lambda]=[N(N-2)]^{\frac{N-2}{4}}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|x-z|^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{N-2}{2}},

are the only poaitive solutions of (
1.6
), where
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\lambda>0
. Thus, the smooth manifold (except
c=0
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\{cU[z,\lambda]\mid c\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\lambda>0\}

is all nonnagetive solutions of (
1.6
). Moreover,
Struwe proved in
[

the following well-known stability of profile decompositions to (

) for nonnegative functions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
and
\nu\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nonnegative sequence with

\displaystyle(\nu-\frac{1}{2})S^{\frac{N}{2}}<\|u_{n}\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}<(\nu+\frac{1}{2})S^{\frac{N}{2}},

where
S
is the best Sobolev constant.
Assume that
\|\Delta u_{n}+|u_{n}|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u_{n}\|_{H^{-1}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, then there exist a sequence
(z_{1}^{(n)},z_{2}^{(n)},\cdots,z_{\nu}^{(n)})
of
\nu
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a sequence of
(\lambda_{1}^{(n)},\lambda_{2}^{(n)},\cdots,\lambda_{\nu}^{(n)})
of
\nu
-tuples of positive real numbers such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\nabla U[z_{i}^{(n)},\lambda_{i}^{(n)}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

In the recent papers
[
7
,
15
]
, Figalli et al. initialed a study on the quantitative version of Theorem
1.2
by proving

(1)

(Ciraolo-Figalli-Maggi
[
7
]
) Let

and

be positive such that
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}S^{\frac{N}{2}}
and
\|\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\|_{H^{-1}}\leq\delta
for some
\delta>0
sufficiently small, then

\displaystyle dist_{D^{1,2}}(u,\mathcal{M}_{0})\lesssim\|\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\|_{H^{-1}},

where
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\{U[z,\lambda]\mid z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\lambda>0\}.

(2)

(Figalli-Glaudo
[
15
]
) Let
u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be nonnegative such that

\displaystyle(\nu-\frac{1}{2})S^{\frac{N}{2}}<\|u\|_{D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}<(\nu+\frac{1}{2})S^{\frac{N}{2}}

and
\|\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\|_{H^{-1}}\leq\delta
for some
\delta>0
sufficiently small,
then for

,

\displaystyle dist_{D^{1,2}}(u,\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\nu})\lesssim\|\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\nu}=\{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]\mid z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\lambda_{i}>0\}.

Remark 1.2
.

The stability obtained in
[
15
]
is more general than the conclusion
(2)
stated here in the sense that,

is close to the sum of

Preliminaries

Let
D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the Hilbert space given by (
1.3
) with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
By
[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
,
D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is isomorphic to the Hilbert space
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
by the transformation

\displaystyle u(x)=|x|^{-(a_{c}-a)}v(-\ln|x|,\frac{x}{|x|}),

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
is the standard cylinder, the inner product in
H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
is given by

\displaystyle\langle w,v\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\nabla w\nabla v+(a_{c}-a)^{2}uvd\mu

with
d\mu
being the volume element on
\mathcal{C}
,

u\in D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
w,v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C})
.

The CKN inequality (
1.1
) can be rewritten as the following minimizing problem:

\displaystyle C_{a,b,N}^{-1}=\inf_{u\in D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})}},

(2.2)

where
L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the usual weighted Lebesgue space and its usual norm is given by
\|u\|_{L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b(p+1)}|u|^{p+1}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}
.
The Euler-Lagrange equation of the minimizing problem (
2.2
) is given by (
1.7
).
By (
2.1
), (
2.2
) is equivalent to the following minimizing problem:

\displaystyle C_{a,b,N}^{-1}=\inf_{v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{C})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathcal{C})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{p+1}(\mathcal{C})}},

(2.3)

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p+1}(\mathcal{C})}
is the usual norm in the Lebesgue space
L^{p+1}(\mathcal{C})
. Let
t=-\ln|x|
and
\theta=\frac{x}{|x|}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
, then by
[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
, (
1.7
) is equivalent to the following equation of
v
:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\theta}v-\partial_{t}^{2}v+(a_{c}-a)^{2}v=|v|^{p-1}v,\quad\text{in }\mathcal{C}

(2.4)

where
\Delta_{\theta}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.

Clearly, minimizers of (
2.2
) are ground states of (
1.7
). It is also well-known (cf.
[
6
,
5
,
16
]
) that up to dilations
u_{\tau}(x)=\tau^{a_{c}-a}u(\tau x)
and scalar multiplications

(also up to translations

for the spacial case

), the radial function

given by (

\displaystyle C_{a,b,N,rad}^{-1}=\inf_{u\in D^{1,2}_{a,rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{p+1}(|x|^{-b(p+1)},\mathbb{R}^{N})}}

under the condition (

),
where

\displaystyle D^{1,2}_{a,rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in D^{1,2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid u\text{ is radially symmetric}\}.

Thus,

is always a solution of (

) under the condition (

). It has been proved in

,

]
that

is the unique nonnegative solution of (

) in

either for

with

or for
a\leq b<a+1
with

. Moreover, it has also been proved in

]
that

is nondegenerate in
